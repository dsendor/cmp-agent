## Library gaps

- Measurement-circuit crosstalk in multi-terminal devices: a shared line impedance makes
  a resonance on one side appear, attenuated, on the other, which can fake end-to-end
  correlation. Source: Martinez et al. 2021, https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02671, "if a
  nonlocal conductance signature is not proportional to the local conductances, then it
  cannot be attributed to voltage divider effects." Signature family: nonlocal.

**What the reader or the skill made hard.**
- `read_mtx.py` reshapes as (nz, ny, nx), "x fastest" in its docstring. These files (and
  the authors' own `qtplotter.py`, and Spyview) store y fastest: reshaping that way gives
  smooth bias traces, the other way gives jumps about ten times larger between adjacent
  points. The printed summary is unaffected because it uses only global min and max, but
  any linecut through `read_mtx()` would be scrambled. I wrote my own loader.
- The reader prints DAC labels and raw units (`dac11 (FG1)`, `Vbias` in 10 µV with and
  without the "(*10uV)" tag), so mapping files to TL, TR and S-gate and to figure axes
  took caption matching and rendered figures.
- It reports only global value ranges, so duplicates show up only as matching min and
  max by eye; it has no zero-bias summary and doesn't flag negative or non-physical
  values.
- The skill asks for "the ranges the figure shows", which needs the figures as images;
  pdftotext gives no axes. I rendered pages with pdftoppm; the skill doesn't say to.
- The skill says to use figure data to count devices; here it all comes from one device,
  so it counts scans and settings only.

## Skill issues

- Status vocabulary assumes a positive topological claim. For a paper arguing
  "non-Majorana", "Ruled out" on the paper's own control would mislead. The skill should
  say what to write when the control supports the lookalike.
- The "Scope" adaptation clause covers other kinds of measurement; it says nothing about a
  paper of the same kind whose conclusion is negative.
- Hold-out: it doesn't say what to do with an entry whose every source is held out (four
  entries here), or whether related-work papers that cite the paper under review in
  passing (Martinez et al.) count as held out. I kept both, and flagged them here.
- Library entries built from the paper under review (one-end-only, and a quote in
  fine-tuned-plateau) leak its conclusion into a test run; the hold-out rule covers
  sources only.
- Step 1 says to note figure ranges and step 6 says data beyond a figure is worth a
  question, but no step says how to treat duplicated or mislabeled files. I put them in a
  question.
- Step 6 asks "How many devices were measured" unless answered; this paper answers it,
  so I folded the answer into question 1. The template keeps that question as a fixed
  item.
- The 600 to 900 word target and the eight-question cap fit, but the table plus five
  data-derived numbers made cells longer than "one quote and one clause" in places.
