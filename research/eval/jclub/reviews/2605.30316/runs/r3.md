Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use tilted-tip nanoSQUID-on-tip magnetometry to image the magnetization of electron-doped rhombohedral graphene with 3, 4, 5, 6, 13 and 15 layers. They report three results. First, the quarter-metal magnetization peaks at finite density, which they attribute to the "Berry ring of fire". Second, an easy-axis magnetic moment persists inside the zero-resistance state of a superconducting tetralayer. Third, gate-driven valley reversal and metastable domains explain the stochastic resistance switching seen in transport. They also give an upper bound of 0.1 μB per carrier on any "condensation magnetization". This is the first local magnetic imaging of the chiral-superconductor candidate and of capacitive orbital reversal across many layer numbers, so it would interest a broad readership. How strong the superconductivity-related conclusions are depends on a few controls and analyses that are currently missing.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A survey across many layer numbers (seven devices) with consistent phase diagrams (Fig. 1, Fig. E1). This establishes the finite-density magnetization peak as a generic feature of the electron-doped quarter metal.
- Real-space images with forward-model fits (Fig. 2b–d, Fig. E2). These go well beyond transport inference about domain structure.
- Resistance and δB recorded at the same time (Fig. 3a–b). This ties the two classes of resistance traces directly to two local magnetic configurations, and the reversal is reproduced in 5L, 6L and a second 13L device (Figs. E4–E6). The ramp-rate statistics (Fig. E7) are a useful practical result.
- Careful checks of nSOT-induced heating on the superconducting state (Fig. S4) and of electric-field background (Figs. S2, S3).
- A quantitative experimental bound on magnetization changes at the superconducting transition, which is relevant to current theory (Ref. [5]).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The evidence for valley polarization inside the superconductor rests only on easy-axis anisotropy, and the paper's own numbers say the moment there is not orbital. The abstract says the tetralayer "reveals a finite orbital ferromagnetic moment", and the title and the Fig. 2 discussion call the signal "orbital magnetization in … the superconducting ground state". Yet Fig. 4f and the accompanying text find m ≈ 1 μB/carrier across the superconducting dome, interpreted as a vanishing orbital part with a spin moment locked to the valley.

   A spin-polarized, valley-balanced half metal also gives 1 μB/carrier, so the size of the moment does not separate the two scenarios. The only discriminator is that the moment points out of plane with B∥ ≫ B⊥ (Methods, "Orbital and spin magnetic moments…"). That argument assumes intrinsic Ising-type spin-orbit coupling strong enough to pin spins against 40 mT. This is asserted from Refs. [29, 44] but not shown for this unaligned, WSe₂-free 4L device.

   The authors should:
   - show that the superconducting-state image reverses sign when B⊥ is reversed (0.5 mT → −0.5 mT), with hysteresis in the magnetic signal under a B⊥ sweep at fixed (n_e, D) inside the dome. Fig. E3g–j shows such hysteresis only in transport;
   - show that the out-of-plane signal does not depend on B∥ magnitude (for example 0, 40 and 100 mT) and in-plane direction;
   - fit the superconducting-state images (Fig. 2e, Fig. 4c, Fig. E2c–d) with an in-plane-moment model, including the 12.5° tip tilt, and report the goodness of fit against the out-of-plane model, as was done for the 6L in Fig. E10a–b;
   - reword the title, abstract and Fig. 2 text so that "orbital magnetization" in the superconductor matches what Fig. 4f actually shows.

2. How the 0.1 μB/carrier "condensation magnetization" bound was obtained is not stated, and the measurement is not well matched to the claim. In Fig. 4f the bound comes from the absence of a feature in m(n_e) at fixed D as the density crosses the edges of the dome at T = 50 mK. A condensation contribution is defined relative to the normal state at the same (n_e, D). Comparing a superconducting point with a neighbouring normal point at different density mixes in the intrinsic n_e dependence of m. Fig. 4f itself shows m rising from about 0.5 to about 1.3 μB/carrier across the dome.

   The point-to-point scatter in Fig. 4f looks comparable to 0.1–0.2 μB/carrier. The m axis also inherits all the uncertainty of the ΔB→M calibration (tip height, smoothness regularization, reference choice), and there is visible bistability between reference protocols (Fig. 4e, blue versus green).

   The authors should:
   - measure ΔB, or better m, at fixed (n_e, D) inside the dome as a continuous function of temperature through T_c, with resistance taken at the same time. Fig. 2g gives only 50 and 600 mK, and the authors themselves note differences between them ("rising at the lowest temperatures", "additional structure");
   - state explicitly how 0.1 μB/carrier follows from the data (noise floor, fit residuals, calibration uncertainty);
   - say what magnitude theory (Ref. [5]) predicts, so readers can judge whether the bound is constraining.

3. Coexistence of magnetism and superconductivity is called "dispositive evidence of orbital magnetization in … the superconducting ground state" and of chiral superconductivity "under this definition" (Discussion). However, the spatial resolution is set by a 350 nm tip held about 350 nm above the graphene (Methods), and the magnetic signal in the dome is weak and partly switchy (Fig. 2e, Fig. 4d–e, Fig. E8a). With these data, phase separation on scales below about 0.5 μm cannot be ruled out: magnetized normal puddles inside a percolating, non-magnetic superconducting network. The transport dome boundaries used to draw the contours in Figs. 2a and 4a–b were measured in a different refrigerator and cooldown (Methods, Fig. E3), so their registration to the magnetic maps is also uncertain at the scale that matters near the boundaries.

   The authors should:
   - discuss explicitly the length scale on which coexistence is shown;
   - compare the fitted m(x, y) inside the dome (Fig. E2c–d) with what a filling fraction below one would give, for example a two-component fit;
   - measure the dome boundaries in the nSOT microscope (as in Fig. 2f) at the same (n_e, D) points used for the maps;
   - soften "dispositive" accordingly.

B. Should address

4. The theory is compared with experiment only qualitatively, and the quantitative comparison that can be read off the figures disagrees.
   - For 13L, Fig. 1h gives M_total ≈ 4–5 μB·10¹² cm⁻² at n_e ≈ 2.6 × 10¹² cm⁻² (about 2 μB/electron). The measurement in Fig. 1d peaks at about 25 μB·10¹² cm⁻², roughly 10 μB/electron, consistent with the fitted m in Fig. E2b.
   - The calculation is done at D = 0.9 V/nm, while the experimental trajectories in Fig. 1a–b run at D ≈ 0.7–0.83 V/nm.
   - The mean-field model (Supplement) is Hartree-only and assumes full single-flavor polarization. It uses fixed hopping parameters from Ref. [47], ε_r = 4, and a 30 nm gate distance, whereas Table I lists hBN thicknesses from 8 to 30 nm.
   - No M(n_e) calculation is shown for 6L, the device used in Fig. 1c.

   The authors should compute M(n_e) along the actual experimental (n_e, D) trajectories with device-specific gate geometry, for both 6L and 13L. They should report how sensitive the result is to D, ε_r and the hopping parameters, and discuss the factor of about 5 (for example exchange enhancement of the Berry curvature, or D calibration). Without this, "we attribute the enhanced magnetization at finite density to … the ring of fire" rests on the shape of a monotonic curve only.

5. The low-density behavior that supports the ring-of-fire picture ("the total magnetization drops at low densities … even appearing to go slightly negative", Fig. 1c–d) is vulnerable to analysis artifacts.
   - The electric-field background is fitted to "the very low density portion of the curve" and subtracted (Fig. S2d–f). That is exactly the region where the claimed near-zero or negative M sits.
   - The ΔB→M factor is taken from 2D images at the peak (Fig. E2a–b) and assumed constant "as long as the magnetic domain structure does not change". Figs. 3 and E5 show that the domain structure does change at low density.
   - Fig. 1 data use a distant reference and are "an average over many field-cooled configurations", so partial domain averaging could also suppress M at low density.

   The authors should show a spatial image with a fit at a low-density point within the quarter metal, demonstrating a uniform domain with small M. They should also show that the low-density M is robust to the choice of background-fit window.

6. The mechanism proposed for capacitive reversal, a sign change of the single-valley total magnetization M_K with density, is drawn schematically (Fig. 3d insets, Fig. E4c,f) but is not calculated, even though the same model is available. Fig. 1h shows only positive M_total, M_sr and M_cm for 13L, with no sign change at low n_e.
   - The authors should plot the calculated M_K(n_e) along the Fig. 1b trajectory and identify where it is predicted to cross zero, against the observed reversals near 0.9 and 2.75 × 10¹² cm⁻².
   - The abstract's general statement that "widely observed stochastic switching of the resistivity in both metallic and superconducting regimes arises from a density-tuned sign change" is stronger than the data support for the superconducting regime. There the authors attribute the bistability in Fig. 4e to possible "transient heating from the gate modulation", which Fig. S4b suggests is not large enough to affect superconductivity. That heating hypothesis should either be tested (for example by varying the modulation amplitude or frequency, or B⊥ as in Fig. E8) or the abstract should be narrowed.

7. All superconducting-state magnetometry comes from one device (4L), one cooldown, and essentially one D range. The 6L device, of the type in which chiral superconductivity has been reported, shows no superconductivity and spatially inhomogeneous ground states (Fig. E10, Supplement). The authors should state the single-device limitation clearly. If a second superconducting device or a thermal cycle of 4L is available, they should show that the superconducting-state moment and its anisotropy reproduce.

8. Temperature is not well determined for several key data sets.
   - Fig. 2a is labelled T ≈ 800 mK, but this is an estimate of heating by the nSOT in exchange gas ("from transport comparisons… likely around"), not a measured value.
   - T_c is given as ≈ 400 mK in the main text and Fig. E3e, but as "onsets around 500 mK" in the Methods for Fig. 2a.
   - Because the authors note that the low-density boundary of the quarter metal depends strongly on temperature, the comparison of normal and superconducting phase diagrams at fixed (n_e, D) depends on knowing the actual temperature.

   The authors should describe how the 800 mK estimate was obtained, with its uncertainty. They should also reconcile the T_c values, and ideally show the Fig. 2a phase diagram at a controlled temperature above T_c without exchange-gas heating.

9. The calibrated m in the superconducting regime is not consistent between figures. Fig. E2d (the same data as Fig. 4c, at n_e = 0.71 × 10¹² cm⁻², D = 1.05 V/nm) peaks at about 2 μB/carrier. Fig. 4f (D = 1.02 V/nm), and the text, give "indistinguishable from 1 μB/carrier across the entire superconducting regime". Fig. E2c gives about 1 μB/carrier.

   The authors should:
   - explain the difference, whether it comes from regularization, tip height or the reference point;
   - give error bars on m in Fig. 4f that include calibration uncertainty;
   - state the tip-to-graphene distance uncertainty and how it propagates into M.

   This matters directly for both the "spin-only" interpretation (concern 1) and the 0.1 μB bound (concern 2).

10. Several superconducting-state measurements use a reference point in the spin-polarized, valley-balanced half metal, which is taken to have "zero magnetic fringe field due to the in-plane applied magnetic field" (Methods, Fig. 4a–b). The tip is tilted by 12.5°, so it is partly sensitive to in-plane fields, and an in-plane-magnetized reference produces edge fringe fields (compare the simulation in Fig. E10b). The authors should quantify the reference-state contribution at the tip's sensing axis and show that it does not distort the in-dome signal, particularly near device edges and contacts.

C. Minor

11. The field values do not agree between captions and Methods.
    - Fig. 2a caption gives (B⊥, B∥) = (3 mT, 50 mT), while the Methods give (3 mT, −50 mT).
    - The Fig. E8 caption uses (B∥, B⊥) ordering in panel a but gives "(B∥, B⊥) = (50 mT, 2 mT)" in panel b, while its image label says B⊥ = 3 mT.
    - The Fig. 3 text says "sweep to n_e = 2.3 × 10¹²", while the caption says 2.2 × 10¹².

    Use a consistent (B⊥, B∥) convention throughout and correct these values.

12. In Fig. 4, the caption for panel b ("The same as panel a at a different location") should name the location. The Methods note on panels a–b (δV_BG = +2 V) should be reconciled with the text's statement that panels a–d use "a large δV_BG".

13. The Supplement section "Gate tuned hysteresis of the magnetic moment" contains only Fig. S1 and its heading, with no text. Fig. S2's text refers to Fig. S3 before it is introduced.

14. The strain explanation for the inhomogeneous 6L ground state, and the statement that "the magnetized domains in the contacts in Fig. E10 represent the superconducting parent state" (Supplement), are speculative. They should be labelled as such or backed by a strain measurement such as Raman mapping.

15. The comparison with the diamagnetic response of Ref. [24] ("considerably smaller than our noise floor of approximately 5 nT") would be more useful with an estimate of the expected Meissner signal for this device geometry, field and Pearl length.

16. The data and code are available only "upon request". Depositing the processed ΔB maps, the fitting code and the mean-field code would help readers check the calibration-dependent numbers (m, the 0.1 μB bound).

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a single decisive superconducting-state measurement: imaging (or point ΔB) inside the dome at fixed (n_e, D) under B⊥ reversal and as a continuous function of T through T_c, with transport recorded in the same microscope. This would at once strengthen the valley-polarization claim, the coexistence claim and the condensation-magnetization bound.
2. Put the calculated M(n_e) and M_K(n_e) for the actual devices and trajectories directly on top of the calibrated data (Fig. 1c–d, Fig. 3d), with the parameter sensitivity shown. This would turn the ring-of-fire and capacitive-reversal attributions into quantitative tests.
3. Provide an error budget for the ΔB→M→m conversion (tip height, tilt, regularization weight, reference state) and apply it consistently to Figs. 1c–d, 4f and E2.
4. Align the title and abstract with what is measured: valley polarization with a near spin-only moment in the superconductor, and stochastic switching explained by capacitive reversal in the normal state, with the superconducting-state mechanism still open.
