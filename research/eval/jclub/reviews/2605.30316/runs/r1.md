Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use tilted-tip nanoSQUID-on-tip magnetometry to map the orbital magnetization of electron-doped rhombohedral graphene with 3 to 15 layers. They make three claims. First, the magnetization of the quarter metal peaks at finite density (up to about 10 μB per carrier), which they assign to the "Berry ring of fire". Second, the widely reported stochastic resistance switching comes from gate-driven, capacitive reversal of macroscopic valley domains. Third, in a superconducting tetralayer device the zero-resistance state carries a finite out-of-plane moment, which they take as direct evidence of valley polarization, and they bound any "condensation magnetization" at 0.1 μB per carrier. A local magnetic measurement inside the putative chiral superconductor of rhombohedral graphene is timely and of broad interest, and the multilayer survey with real-space switching images is a useful resource. The superconducting-state conclusions, however, rest on a single device, on an indirect spin-orbit argument and on calibration steps that the manuscript does not yet support at the level the abstract claims.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First local magnetometry inside the zero-resistance region of a rhombohedral graphene superconductor (Fig. 2e–g, Fig. 4). The out-of-plane moment is seen under B∥ ≫ B⊥, and real-space images fit an out-of-plane magnetization model (Fig. E2c,d).
- A broad survey of six layer numbers in seven devices (Fig. E1, Table I). It shows a common magnetization maximum at finite n_e and a suppression of magnetization at low density.
- Clear real-space images of gate-driven, nearly uniform reversal of the magnetization (Fig. 3d–f, Fig. S1). The effect is reproduced in the 5L, 6L and a second 13L device (Figs. E4–E6). The images are correlated with transport switching (Fig. 3a–c), and the ramp-time dependence of domain formation is shown (Fig. E7). This is practically important for interpreting transport data in these systems.
- Useful technical controls: nSOT heating of the superconductor with and without exchange gas (Fig. S4a), square-wave transport (Fig. S4b), B⊥ offset calibration via ring-of-fire coercivity, and δB measurements that rule out averaging artifacts in the 6L device (Fig. E10g–i).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Spatial coexistence of the magnetization with the zero-resistance path is not shown. Fig. 4d shows that within the superconducting (n_e, D) window "only the lower end of the device appears to support a stable magnetization, while the upper half … appears to have little or no net magnetization". The text attributes this to averaging over domains of opposite sign. It is equally consistent with a nonmagnetic region that carries the supercurrent. A four-terminal Rxx = 0 (Fig. 2f, Fig. E3) requires only one percolating path between the voltage probes used. Which contact pair defines the contours in Figs. 2a, 4a,b and E3c is not stated. The claim of "direct evidence of valley polarization in the superconducting ground state" requires that the magnetized region and the zero-resistance region be the same. The authors should:
   - report Rxx for each pair of adjacent voltage probes along the Hall bar, and show that the segments imaged as magnetized in Figs. 2e and 4c are themselves at zero resistance;
   - test the domain-averaging interpretation of Fig. 4d directly. One way is to image the upper region after a slow-ramp initialization (as in Fig. E7) or after a B⊥ training field, and show that it can be stabilized into a uniform domain with the same m as the lower region.
   - discuss phase separation below the ~300 nm spatial resolution. Is the m ≈ 1 μB/carrier of Fig. 4f precise enough to exclude a mixture of nonmagnetic superconducting puddles and magnetized normal regions? (See concern 5 on calibration accuracy.)

2. The evidence for valley polarization in the superconductor is indirect, and the paper describes it inconsistently. The abstract and Fig. 2 say the superconductor has a "finite orbital ferromagnetic moment", and the text calls this "dispositive evidence of orbital magnetization in both the normal and the superconducting state". Fig. 4f and the accompanying text instead find m indistinguishable from 1 μB/carrier, "consistent with a vanishing orbital part of the magnetization". In that reading the moment is spin, held out of plane against B∥ = 40 mT by intrinsic spin-orbit coupling.
   - Valley polarization is then inferred only through the Methods argument that spin pinning out of plane requires valley imbalance. That argument assumes the Kane–Mele-type coupling is strong enough to overcome a 40 mT in-plane Zeeman energy and that no other source of out-of-plane anisotropy exists. Neither assumption is quantified for this device, which has no WSe2.
   - The authors should make the abstract, Fig. 2 discussion and Fig. 4 discussion consistent about whether the measured moment in the superconductor is orbital or spin.
   - They should provide a direct, model-light test of time-reversal breaking in the superconducting state. Options include a local ΔB hysteresis loop at a fixed position versus B⊥ inside the zero-resistance region at 50 mK, showing remanence and sign reversal. Another is the dependence of ΔB on the magnitude and direction of B∥ (rotating B∥ by 180°, and increasing it until the spin cants). The transport hysteresis in Fig. E3i,j is suggestive, but it is noisy and is not tied to the imaged region.
   - They should estimate the spin-orbit pinning field for 4L and compare it with 40 mT.

3. The 0.1 μB/carrier bound on the "condensation magnetization" is not substantiated and may not be constraining. The bound comes from the absence of a visible step in m at the edges of the superconducting window in Fig. 4f (50 mK, fixed D, sweeping n_e). That comparison sets the superconductor against normal states at different densities.
   - The scatter in the green trace of Fig. 4f is itself roughly ±0.1–0.2 μB/carrier, and no error budget is given. The calibration from ΔB to m carries additional systematic uncertainty (concern 5).
   - The matched-(n_e, D) comparison across Tc in Fig. 2g is described in the text as showing that "ΔB appears to develop additional structure in the regime of superconductivity" and that the magnetization rises at the lowest temperatures. This sits uneasily with "no detectable signature of the superconductor itself".
   - The authors should do three things:
     - Extract the bound from ΔB(T) at fixed (n_e, D) inside the superconducting dome, from well above Tc (≥800 mK, since 600 mK sits on the resistive transition in Fig. E3e) to 50 mK. Average over the superconducting window and state the statistical and calibration uncertainties separately.
     - Explain the "additional structure" in Fig. 2g.
     - Estimate the condensation magnetization that Ref. [5] or a simple estimate (for example, a fraction of order Δ/E_F of carriers changing their angular momentum) would predict. Otherwise the reader cannot tell whether 0.1 μB/carrier constrains any model.

B. Should address

4. The theory agrees with the data only qualitatively, and the comparison is made at mismatched displacement field.
   - Fig. 1h (13L, D = 0.9 V/nm) gives M_total ≈ 4–5 μB × 10^12 cm^-2 at n_e = 2.5 × 10^12 cm^-2 (about 2 μB/carrier). Fig. 1d (13L, measured along the grey line in Fig. 1b, D ≈ 0.73–0.8 V/nm) shows a peak of ~25 μB × 10^12 cm^-2 (about 10 μB/carrier, as also stated in the text and in Fig. E2). That is a factor of ~5 discrepancy, and it is not discussed.
   - The calculation also gives a monotonically increasing, positive M_total. It does not reproduce the low-density suppression or the "slightly negative" magnetization. It also does not show the sign change of the single-valley M_K with density that the paper invokes as the mechanism of capacitive reversal (Fig. 3d insets, citing [28]).
   - The authors should repeat the calculation at the D of each experimental trajectory, using the device's actual hBN thicknesses (Table I lists 12–30 nm, whereas the calculation assumes 30 nm gates and εr = 4). They should plot the theory in μB/carrier directly against Fig. 1c,d and show where M_K changes sign.
   - They should discuss what is missing: exchange (Fock) enhancement of the orbital moment, the assumption of a single flavor at all n_e, and sensitivity to the remote hoppings. Unless the comparison becomes semi-quantitative, the attribution of the peak to the ring of fire should be worded as consistent with the model rather than confirmed.

5. The conversion from ΔB to M and m has no stated uncertainty. All quantitative statements depend on fits to the fringe field: "order 10 μB per electron", m ≈ 1 μB/carrier in the superconductor, and the 0.1 μB bound. The fits use a least-squares inversion with a gradient penalty (Methods; Fig. E2).
   - The tip height is given only as "200–350 nm" and the tip has a 12.5° tilt. The regularization strength, the treatment of the reference state and the fit residuals are not given.
   - In Figs. 2 and 4 the reference state is a spin-polarized half metal under B∥ = 40 mT. Its in-plane spin magnetization is projected onto the tilted tip axis, so it contributes to ΔB at the edges (compare Fig. E10b).
   - The authors should give the free and fixed parameters of the inversion and propagate the uncertainty in tip height and regularization into m. They should quantify how much a reference state with an in-plane moment contributes, and put error bars on Fig. 4f and Fig. 1c,d.

6. The electric-field background subtraction is a possible source of artifacts.
   - For the n_e–D maps, a plane fitted at low density is subtracted (Fig. S2). The claimed slightly negative magnetization at low n_e (Fig. 1c,d) lies in or next to that fitting window, so its existence depends on the choice of window.
   - For the superconductor images (Fig. S3), the "nonmagnetic" image used for subtraction is taken at a different D (0.875 versus 1.05 V/nm) with a different reference point. A background that is linear in each gate voltage would then not cancel exactly. The raw contrast (~0.25 μT) is larger than the reported magnetic contrast (~0.1 μT).
   - The authors should show that the negative low-density magnetization and the superconductor images are robust to the choice of background window and nonmagnetic reference. Matched top-gate voltages would be preferable, or the authors could show the result with two independent background references.

7. The statements about sample temperature and heating are inconsistent.
   - Fig. S4b is used to show that square-wave gate modulation does not heat the superconductor. Yet the bistability in Fig. 4e is attributed to "transient heating from the gate modulation" large enough to drive thermal domain reversal at ~1 Hz.
   - The Fig. 2a temperature of "≈800 mK" is estimated, not measured.
   - The authors should reconcile these statements, ideally by measuring Rxx while applying the same large δV_BG square wave used in Figs. 4a–e, rather than the protocol of Fig. S4b. They should also say how the 800 mK estimate was obtained.

8. The chiral-superconductivity conclusion is circular. The Discussion adopts the definition of Ref. [33] (any superconductor that coexists with finite orbital magnetization) and then calls the observation "dispositive evidence of chiral superconductivity under this definition". Given concerns 1 and 2, coexistence on the same region and the orbital character of the moment are both not yet established. Even granting both, the conclusion follows from the definition alone and says nothing about gap winding, as the authors acknowledge. The abstract and Discussion should state plainly that the data show a superconductor coexisting with valley/spin polarization. Chirality in the conventional sense should be described as untested.

9. The switching mechanism and the novelty claim need support.
   - Fig. 3a,b correlates resistance classes with δB classes, but gives no counts. How many sweeps were taken, how often do the R class and the δB class agree, and is the 100 Ω threshold robust?
   - The mechanism (relaxation suppressed until M_K changes sign) is drawn schematically in Fig. 3d. It is not tied to calculated energetics (see concern 4).
   - The claim that this is "the first real-space imaging" of electrically controlled orbital-magnet reversal should be checked against earlier nSOT work on moiré Chern magnets, including Refs. [14, 20]. The claim should be qualified to what is new here: metallic rhombohedral multilayers, capacitive reversal of the whole device.
   - Fig. E7 shows that slower ramps favor the negative domain. A brief explanation of this trend would strengthen the domain picture.

10. Reproducibility of the superconducting result.
   - All superconducting-state magnetometry comes from one device (4L). It is not stated whether the magnetized-superconductor images survive thermal cycling or a second cooldown. The second 13L device and the 5L, 6L devices address only the switching.
   - The 6L device, in which no electron-side superconductivity is seen, is used for a speculative strain explanation (Fig. E10, SI section 4) with no strain measurement. Its inference that "the magnetized domains in the contacts … represent the superconducting parent state" is not supported by data from that device.
   - The authors should state the cooldown history for 4L and either add a strain characterization (for example Raman 2D mapping or local dHvA as in Ref. [45]) or move the strain discussion clearly into speculation.

C. Minor

11. Field labels are inconsistent. The order of (B⊥, B∥) and (B∥, B⊥) changes between captions, text and Methods. Fig. 2a gives (3 mT, 50 mT) in the caption but (3 mT, −50 mT) in the Methods, and the main text gives (B∥, B⊥) = (40 mT, 0.5 mT). Fig. E8b gives (50 mT, 2 mT), whereas the main text says B∥ = 40 mT. Please use one convention throughout and check the signs.

12. Tc is quoted as ≈400 mK in the main text and Fig. E3, but the Methods say superconductivity "onsets around 500 mK". Please define Tc (for example, 50% of normal-state Rxx) and use it consistently.

13. The density for the Fig. 3b insets is 2.3 × 10^12 cm^-2 in the text and 2.2 × 10^12 cm^-2 in the caption. Fig. 3a classifies curves at 2.25 × 10^12 cm^-2.

14. The Fig. E2 caption says "Data in panel b is the same as in Fig. 2f"; it presumably means Fig. 3f. In the same caption, the 6L reference point is at negative density (−0.058 × 10^12 cm^-2), which should be confirmed.

15. Within one figure, Fig. 3d and Figs. 3e,f use different reference points, (0, 0.7 V/nm) versus (3.97 × 10^12 cm^-2, 0.79 V/nm). The zero of ΔB therefore differs between panels, and this should be noted where the magnitudes are compared.

16. In the SI, the section "Gate tuned hysteresis of the magnetic moment" has a heading after Fig. S1 but no text. Section 2 refers to Fig. S3 but gives no recipe for the n_e–D background window.

17. Theory details: state the D and the layer-resolved potential used for Fig. E1h–l, the k-space cutoff (2.8 nm^-1 box) for the thinner layers, and convergence with respect to N_k and k_BT. Also specify the sign convention of m_sr relative to the applied field, so that "positive" ΔB can be compared with the sign of theoretical M.

18. Data and code are "available upon request". Deposition of the processed n_e–D maps, images and fitting code is encouraged.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add local ΔB versus B⊥ hysteresis loops and B∥-direction reversal measured at fixed points inside the zero-resistance region at 50 mK. Pair them with segment-resolved Rxx along the Hall bar, so that time-reversal breaking and zero resistance are shown in the same place.
2. Replace the density-sweep bound in Fig. 4f with a temperature sweep at fixed (n_e, D) through Tc that has a full error budget. Compare it with a theoretical estimate of the expected condensation magnetization.
3. Make the theory comparison quantitative: matched D, real gate geometry, μB/carrier units, and the single-valley M_K sign change that underlies the switching mechanism.
4. Add an uncertainty analysis for the fringe-field inversion (tip height, regularization, reference-state in-plane moment) and robustness checks of the background subtraction. Propagate these into every quoted value of m.
