Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use tilted-tip nanoSQUID-on-tip magnetometry to map the gate-tuned orbital magnetization of electron-doped rhombohedral graphene with 3 to 15 layers. They report three main results: (i) the quarter-metal magnetization peaks at finite density, which they attribute to the Berry-curvature "ring of fire"; (ii) a gate-driven sign change of the single-valley moment produces metastable domains and capacitive magnetization reversal, which they link to the stochastic resistance switching seen in transport; (iii) in the tetralayer chiral-superconductor device, an out-of-plane moment persists inside the zero-resistance region, and the magnetization changes by less than 0.1 μB per carrier across the superconducting boundary. No earlier work has imaged the magnetism of the proposed chiral superconductor directly, so the result is timely and of broad interest. The strongest claims, however, are "orbital magnetization in the superconducting ground state", "dispositive evidence of chiral superconductivity" and the 0.1 μB bound. In their present form these rest on interpretive steps that the data do not yet secure.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A real-space magnetic probe of a proposed chiral superconductor, a regime that transport alone cannot access because the zero resistance masks the anomalous Hall effect.
- A broad survey of seven devices and six layer numbers (Fig. E1). The phase diagrams agree qualitatively, and the finite-density magnetization peak appears in all of them.
- Gate-driven valley reversal is shown convincingly in several devices: 13L (Fig. 3, Fig. S1), 5L/WSe2 (Fig. E4), 6L (Fig. E5) and 13L-2 (Fig. E6). In 13L, simultaneous transport and δB measurements tie the two resistance classes to local magnetic states (Fig. 3a-b).
- The protocol work is careful and useful to others: control of the ramp time (Fig. E7), a heating check (Fig. S4), and fringe-field inversion fits (Fig. E2).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Orbital versus spin magnetization in the superconducting state is contradictory and not resolved. The abstract and the Fig. 2 discussion state that the superconductor carries "a finite orbital ferromagnetic moment" and that the data are "dispositive evidence of orbital magnetization in both the normal and superconducting ground state." The Fig. 4f discussion says the opposite: m is "indistinguishable from 1 μB/carrier", which "is consistent with a vanishing orbital part of the magnetization despite full valley polarization", with the moment coming from spin locked to valley by intrinsic spin-orbit coupling. The Methods ("Orbital and spin magnetic moments...") then argue only that an out-of-plane moment under B∥ ≫ B⊥ shows valley imbalance. These three statements imply different claims. Valley polarization with a spin-only moment is not orbital ferromagnetism in the superconductor. What the data support is easy-axis out-of-plane anisotropy, which the paper takes as a sign of valley polarization. The authors should:
   - Measure ΔB in the superconducting region as a function of B∥ magnitude and in-plane angle, and compare with the quarter metal and with the half-metal reference. Separate spin and orbital contributions by asking at what field the spin cants, using the known Ising spin-orbit scale.
   - For the superconducting images (Fig. 2e, Fig. 4c, Fig. E2c-d), show that an in-plane-moment model fits worse than the out-of-plane model, as was done for 6L in Fig. E10a-b.
   - Rewrite the abstract, Fig. 2 text and Discussion so they claim no more than the data show: valley polarization of the superconducting state, with an orbital contribution that is consistent with zero.

2. The paper does not show that the magnetized material and the superconducting material are the same. The evidence for magnetism "in the superconducting state" is that ΔB is nonzero at (n_e, D) points where four-terminal R_xx = 0 (Fig. 2e-g, Fig. 4). Zero resistance needs only a percolating path, and the probe averages over about 300 nm at a height of about 300-350 nm. Several observations fit sub-resolution coexistence of a magnetic normal metal and a non-magnetic (or differently polarized) superconductor equally well:
   - the region of "diminished or reversed magnetic response" inside the superconducting dome (Fig. 4a-b, 4d);
   - the comparison with Ref. [24], where a non-magnetic rhombohedral superconductor showed diamagnetism;
   - Ref. [32], which reports complex, multi-pocket fermiology in this same device;
   - the strain-driven competing ground states proposed for 6L (Fig. E10, SI section 4).

   No local superconducting signature (diamagnetic screening or vortices) is detected; the Meissner response is below the 5 nT noise floor. The authors should:
   - Measure ΔB at fixed positions inside the zero-resistance region as a continuous function of temperature through Tc, in the same cooldown and at the same contact geometry. Fig. 2g gives only two temperatures, and the text notes "additional structure" below Tc without quantifying it.
   - Map zero resistance along individual contact-pair segments of the Hall bar, and correlate each segment with the local magnetization in Fig. 2e and Fig. 4c-d.
   - Estimate the expected Pearl-length diamagnetic signal at B⊥ = 0.5 mT, or repeat at larger B⊥ (superconductivity survives to well above 0.5 mT, Fig. E3f). This would show whether the absence of a Meissner signal is informative.
   - Address the reduced-moment region of Fig. 4d explicitly as a possible non-magnetic superconducting region. At present it is interpreted only as domain instability.

3. The 0.1 μB/carrier bound on the "condensation magnetization" is not derived and rests on inconsistent conditions. The bound comes from Fig. 4f, a single density sweep at D = 1.02 V/nm. The green m(n_e) trace has no error bars, and its point-to-point scatter looks comparable to 0.1 μB. Several problems follow:
   - **Temperature.** The Fig. 4 caption gives T = 50 mK, but the Methods entry for Fig. 4(a-f) gives T = 350 mK. At 350 mK the sample is close to Tc ≈ 400 mK (Fig. E3e), and condensation effects would be strongly suppressed.
   - **Method.** A density sweep across the superconducting/normal boundary mixes any condensate contribution with the intrinsic n_e dependence of the normal-state moment. The direct measurement is m at fixed (n_e, D) as a function of T across Tc, or as a function of B⊥ through Hc2.
   - **Systematics.** The conversion to m uses the fringe-field fit (tip height, smoothness penalty, assumed single domain). Its systematic uncertainty, and how it propagates into the 0.1 μB figure, is not given.
   - **Relevance.** For the bound to constrain Ref. [5], the paper should state the theoretical scale of the expected effect, for example the condensate fraction of order Δ/E_F times the moment per pair. Without that comparison the reader cannot tell whether 0.1 μB/carrier constrains anything.

   The authors should give the statistical and systematic error budget, settle the measurement temperature, and preferably add a temperature sweep at fixed (n_e, D).

B. Should address

4. The theory agrees with the data only qualitatively, and Fig. 1 is presented as if the agreement were quantitative. In Fig. 1d, the 13L magnetization peaks near 24 μB·10^12 cm^-2 at n_e ≈ 2.6×10^12 cm^-2, about 9 μB per carrier, close to the "order 10 Bohr magnetons per electron" quoted in the text. The calculated M_total in Fig. 1h at the same density is about 4-5 in the same units, about 2 μB per carrier: a factor of 3-5 smaller. Further problems:
   - The calculation uses a single D = 0.9 V/nm, while the experimental trajectories (dashed lines in Fig. 1a-b) run at D ≈ 0.75-0.85 V/nm.
   - It assumes 30 nm gate distances, whereas Table I gives 12 nm hBN for 13L.
   - It includes Hartree screening only, with single-flavor occupancy imposed by hand.
   - The calculation has no peak; the observed decrease is attributed to a half-metal transition that is not computed.

   The authors should compute M along the actual (n_e, D) trajectories with the actual gate geometries, state the remaining discrepancy, and check how sensitive the result is to ε_r and the hopping parameters (Eq. S4). Otherwise they should describe the agreement as qualitative.

5. The paper's own calculation does not show the sign change it uses to explain switching. The domain-reversal mechanism rests on a "gate-controlled change in sign of the single valley total magnetization" (Fig. 3d insets; Refs. [18, 28]). Yet the 13L M_total in Fig. 1h is positive at all densities shown. The data place the reversal near n_e ≈ 0.9×10^12 cm^-2 (red curve, Fig. 3d) and show slightly negative ΔB at low n_e (Fig. 1c-d, Fig. S1). The authors should show the computed single-valley M_K(n_e) along the Fig. 3d trajectory and identify where it crosses zero. Ideally they should also show this for 5L and 6L, where switching is likewise observed (Figs. E4, E5).

6. The link from domains to stochastic switching in the superconducting state is asserted, not shown. The abstract says the widely observed stochastic switching "in both metallic and superconducting regimes arises from" the density-tuned sign change. The direct correlation of resistance classes with local δB (Fig. 3a-b) is shown only for the metallic 13L device. For the 4L superconductor the paper offers only line-by-line jumps (Fig. 2a inset, Fig. E3) and bistable ΔB (Fig. 4e), with no simultaneous transport. The authors should repeat the Fig. 3a-b protocol (simultaneous R and δB with resistance-class color coding) in device 4L inside the superconducting dome, or else narrow the abstract claim to the metallic regime.

7. The electric-field background is subtracted from a superconducting-state signal of similar size. The superconducting-state ΔB is about 0.1 μT. The parasitic electric-field contrast in the raw image (Fig. S3a) reaches about ±0.25 μT, and it is removed by subtracting an image taken at a different gate configuration (n_e = 0, D = 0.875 V/nm with reference 3.25×10^12 cm^-2, 0.58 V/nm). The SI says the background is "most sensitive to changes in the voltage of the top gate." The authors should:
   - state the top-gate and back-gate voltage steps for both images and show that they match;
   - show a residual map for two nonmagnetic configurations with matched steps;
   - state whether the same subtraction was used in Fig. 2e and Fig. 4a-b (a planar fit at low density, Fig. S2).

   The parasitic contrast concentrates over the contacts in Fig. S3, and so does the structure of the superconducting-state signal. A mis-scaled subtraction could therefore imprint contact-shaped features.

8. The reference state's in-plane spin moment and the tilted tip are not accounted for. With B∥ = 40-50 mT, the spin-polarized, valley-balanced reference phase has its spin moment in the plane (Methods). The tip is tilted 12.5°, so it has finite sensitivity to that in-plane fringe field. Moving from the reference to a valley-polarized state also rotates the spin moment out of plane. The resulting ΔB therefore contains an in-plane term that the out-of-plane-only fits (Fig. 2c-d, Fig. E2) omit. This matters most for the small superconducting-state signal and for the claim that m ≈ 1 μB/carrier. The authors should estimate this term, or include an in-plane component in the inversion.

9. The heating arguments contradict each other, and the temperatures for Fig. 2 are mixed.
   - SI Fig. S4b is used to argue that the square-wave gate modulation does not heat the sample enough to affect superconductivity.
   - The Fig. 4e discussion proposes that "transient heating from the gate modulation is sufficient to cause thermal domain reversal" at about 1 Hz.
   - The Fig. 2a phase diagram was taken at an estimated electron temperature of about 800 mK, inferred "from transport comparisons" that are not shown.
   - The superconducting contour overlaid on Fig. 2a comes from a separate measurement at 20 mK in a dilution refrigerator, which by the Methods appears to be a different cooldown and setup.

   The authors should reconcile the two heating statements and show the transport data behind the 800 mK estimate. They should also overlay a superconducting contour measured in the nSOT microscope in the same cooldown as the magnetometry. The superconducting region in rhombohedral graphene can shift with cooldown and strain.

10. The magnetization values in the superconductor do not agree across figures, and none has an uncertainty.
    - The text says m in the superconductor is "indistinguishable from 1 μB/carrier" (Fig. 4f).
    - The fits in Fig. E2c and E2d peak at about 1 and about 2 μB/carrier respectively.
    - The bistable ΔB values in Fig. 4e (blue) are not symmetric about zero, as two time-reversed domains of one state would be. This suggests partial domains or an offset in the reference.

    The authors should give uncertainties for each extracted m, explain the difference between E2c and E2d, and discuss the asymmetry in Fig. 4e. The 150-350 nm range of tip heights quoted in the Methods should be propagated into the calibration.

11. All of the superconducting results come from one device. Magnetism in a superconductor is shown only in device 4L, and the one other candidate (6L) shows no electron-side superconductivity and has spatially competing ground states (Fig. E10, SI section 4). The paper should state this limitation clearly. It should also say whether the result survived thermal cycling of 4L, since the transport contour and the magnetometry come from different setups. If any other superconducting device was measured, it should be included.

12. The anomalous Hall support is weak, and the language is too strong. The text cites Fig. E3 for "anomalous Hall measurements that imply a finite orbital magnetization of the normal state." In Fig. E3g-h, however, the forward and reverse B⊥ sweeps above Tc nearly overlap apart from features at zero field, with no clear hysteresis loop. The authors should quantify the anomalous Hall hysteresis, for example as the antisymmetrized R_xy difference at B = 0. More broadly, "dispositive evidence" (Fig. 2 text and Discussion) is too strong for the reasons in concerns 1-2. The Discussion also concedes that the data have "no bearing" on winding of the order parameter, so the chiral-superconductivity conclusion holds only under the expanded definition of Ref. [33]. That caveat should appear in the abstract.

13. The priority claim needs qualifying. "Our results here represent the first real-space imaging of this unique orbital magnetic phenomenon" sits alongside earlier work on gate-controlled orbital magnets, including nSOT imaging (Refs. [15-20]), and the authors' own Note added (Ref. [34]). The authors should state precisely what is new compared with those works.

C. Minor

14. The Tc of device 4L is given inconsistently: "Tc ≈ 400 mK" (main text, Fig. E3e) but "the superconducting Tc which onsets around 500 mK" (Methods, Fig. 2a entry).

15. Field values disagree between caption and Methods. For Fig. 2a the caption gives (B⊥, B∥) = (3 mT, 50 mT), while the Methods give (3 mT, -50 mT). The main text quotes fields in (B∥, B⊥) order and the captions in (B⊥, B∥) order; use one convention throughout.

16. The Fig. E2 caption says "Data in panel b is the same as in Fig. 2f"; Fig. 2f is a resistance trace, so this should read Fig. 3f.

17. The ramp time for Fig. 3e is given as "τ = 1472 μs" in the Methods, but Fig. E7 uses τ in ms (1470 ms). Please check the units.

18. The density of the Fig. 3 snapshot appears as 2.25×10^12 cm^-2 (Fig. 3a caption), 2.3×10^12 (main text) and 2.2×10^12 (Fig. 3b caption). Please harmonize.

19. Figs. 1c-d and 1h use units of μB·10^12 cm^-2 for M, while the text discusses μB per carrier. Please add a per-carrier axis or state the conversion, so the comparison in concern 4 is easy to check.

20. Section 1 of the SI ("Gate tuned hysteresis of the magnetic moment") has a heading but no text. The SI also refers to "Extended Data Fig. E10", whereas the main text uses "Fig. E10".

21. Data and code are available only "upon request." Depositing the magnetometry maps, the fit code and the mean-field code would make the 0.1 μB bound and the fits in Fig. 1 reproducible.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one decisive temperature-dependent data set for device 4L, taken in a single cooldown: R_xx(T) and ΔB(T) at the same fixed (n_e, D), and at two or three positions including the reduced-moment region of Fig. 4d, with fine steps through Tc. This would address concerns 2, 3 and 9 together.
2. Add a B∥-magnitude and in-plane-angle series in the superconducting state, analyzed with a fringe-field model that includes both in-plane and out-of-plane moments. This would settle the spin-versus-orbital question (concerns 1 and 8).
3. Extend the mean-field calculation to the experimental (n_e, D) trajectories and gate geometries, show the single-valley M_K(n_e) with its zero crossing, and state the remaining quantitative discrepancy (concerns 4-5).
4. Recast the abstract and Discussion around what the data establish: valley polarization coexisting with zero resistance, observed in one device, and a bound on the condensation magnetization with an explicit error budget. Claims of orbital magnetism of the condensate and "dispositive" chirality should wait for the measurements above.
