A. Must address

1. The paper does not show that the magnetized region and the zero-resistance region are the same. The case for magnetism "in the superconducting state" is that ΔB is nonzero at (n_e, D) points where the four-terminal R_xx = 0 (Fig. 2e–g, Fig. 4, Fig. E3). Zero resistance needs only one percolating path between the voltage probes, and the paper does not say which contact pair defines the superconducting contours in Figs. 2a, 4a,b and E3c. The probe averages over about 300 nm (a tip of about 350 nm held 200–350 nm above the graphene), so phase separation below about 0.5 μm cannot be excluded: magnetized normal puddles could sit inside a percolating non-magnetic superconducting network. Several observations fit that picture as well as they fit the authors' one. In Fig. 4d, inside the superconducting (n_e, D) window, "only the lower end of the device appears to support a stable magnetization, while the upper half … appears to have little or no net magnetization", and the text explains this only as averaging over domains of opposite sign. The magnetic signal in the dome is weak and partly switchy (Fig. 2e, Fig. 4d–e, Fig. E8a). Ref. [24] found a non-magnetic rhombohedral superconductor with a diamagnetic response. Ref. [32] reports complex multi-pocket fermiology in this same device. The 6L device shows spatially competing ground states (Fig. E10). No local superconducting signature (diamagnetic screening or vortices) is detected, and the Meissner response is said to be below the ~5 nT noise floor without an estimate of the expected size. The transport dome boundaries overlaid on Figs. 2a and 4a–b were measured at 20 mK in a different refrigerator and cooldown (Methods, Fig. E3). Their registration to the magnetic maps near the boundaries is therefore uncertain, and the superconducting region in rhombohedral graphene can shift with cooldown and strain. The claim of "direct evidence of valley polarization in the superconducting ground state" needs the two regions to coincide. The authors should: (i) report R_xx for each pair of adjacent voltage probes along the Hall bar, and show that the segments imaged as magnetized in Figs. 2e and 4c–d are themselves at zero resistance; (ii) measure the dome boundaries in the nSOT microscope, in the same cooldown, at the same (n_e, D) points used for the maps (as in Fig. 2f); (iii) measure ΔB at fixed positions inside the zero-resistance region as a continuous function of temperature through T_c, including the reduced-moment region of Fig. 4d; (iv) test the domain-averaging reading of Fig. 4d directly, for example by imaging the upper region after a slow-ramp initialization (as in Fig. E7) or a B⊥ training field, and showing that it can be stabilized into a uniform domain with the same m as the lower region, while treating that region explicitly as a possible non-magnetic superconductor; (v) compare the fitted m(x, y) inside the dome (Fig. E2c–d) with what a filling fraction below one would give, for example with a two-component fit, and say whether m ≈ 1 μB/carrier in Fig. 4f is precise enough to exclude such a mixture; (vi) estimate the expected Pearl-length diamagnetic signal at B⊥ = 0.5 mT for this geometry, or repeat at larger B⊥ (superconductivity survives well above 0.5 mT, Fig. E3f), so that the absence of a Meissner signal becomes informative; (vii) state the length scale on which coexistence is actually shown.

2. The evidence that the superconductor is valley polarized is indirect, and the paper contradicts itself on whether the moment is orbital. The abstract and the Fig. 2 discussion say the tetralayer superconductor carries "a finite orbital ferromagnetic moment", and the text calls the data "dispositive evidence of orbital magnetization in both the normal and superconducting ground state"; the title also speaks of orbital magnetization in the superconducting ground state. Fig. 4f and its text instead find m "indistinguishable from 1 μB/carrier", "consistent with a vanishing orbital part of the magnetization despite full valley polarization", with the moment carried by spin locked to valley by intrinsic spin-orbit coupling. A spin-polarized, valley-balanced half metal also gives 1 μB/carrier, so the size of the moment cannot tell the two scenarios apart. The only discriminator is the Methods argument ("Orbital and spin magnetic moments…") that an out-of-plane moment under B∥ = 40 mT ≫ B⊥ requires valley imbalance. That argument assumes Ising (Kane–Mele-type) spin-orbit coupling strong enough to pin spins against the in-plane Zeeman energy, and that no other source of out-of-plane anisotropy exists. Both are asserted from Refs. [29, 44] and neither is quantified for this unaligned, WSe₂-free 4L device. The transport hysteresis in Fig. E3g–j is suggestive but noisy and not tied to the imaged region. The authors should: (i) give a direct test of time-reversal breaking in the superconducting state, namely a local ΔB hysteresis loop versus B⊥ at fixed (n_e, D) and fixed position inside the zero-resistance region at 50 mK, and show that the image reverses sign when B⊥ is reversed (0.5 mT → −0.5 mT) and shows remanence; (ii) measure ΔB in the dome as a function of B∥ magnitude (for example 0, 40 and 100 mT, up to the field where the spin cants) and in-plane direction (including a 180° rotation), and compare with the quarter metal and the half-metal reference; (iii) estimate the spin-orbit pinning field for 4L and compare it with 40 mT; (iv) fit the superconducting-state images (Fig. 2e, Fig. 4c, Fig. E2c–d) with an in-plane-moment model that includes the 12.5° tip tilt, and report its goodness of fit against the out-of-plane model, as was done for 6L in Fig. E10a–b; (v) make the title, abstract, Fig. 2 text, Fig. 4 text and Discussion consistent and limited to what the data show: valley polarization of the superconducting state, with an orbital contribution consistent with zero.

3. The 0.1 μB/carrier bound on the "condensation magnetization" is not derived, uses a mismatched comparison and may not constrain any model. The bound comes from the absence of a visible step in m at the edges of the superconducting window in Fig. 4f, a single density sweep at D = 1.02 V/nm. That compares superconducting points with normal points at different densities and so mixes any condensate contribution with the intrinsic n_e dependence of m; Fig. 4f itself shows m rising from about 0.5 to about 1.3 μB/carrier across the dome. The green m(n_e) trace has no error bars, and its point-to-point scatter, about ±0.1–0.2 μB/carrier, is comparable to the bound. The ΔB→m conversion adds a systematic uncertainty of its own (concern 4), and the trace shows bistability between reference protocols (Fig. 4e, blue versus green). The measurement temperature is also unclear: the Fig. 4 caption gives 50 mK, but the Methods entry for Fig. 4a–f gives 350 mK, close to T_c ≈ 400 mK (Fig. E3e), where condensation effects would be strongly suppressed. The matched-(n_e, D) comparison in Fig. 2g covers only 50 and 600 mK, and 600 mK lies on the resistive transition (Fig. E3e). Even there the text reports that "ΔB appears to develop additional structure in the regime of superconductivity" and that the magnetization rises at the lowest temperatures, which sits uneasily with "no detectable signature of the superconductor itself". The authors should: (i) settle the measurement temperature for Fig. 4; (ii) extract the bound from ΔB or m at fixed (n_e, D) inside the dome as a continuous function of temperature from well above T_c (≥800 mK) to 50 mK, with resistance recorded at the same time, or as a function of B⊥ through H_c2, averaged over the superconducting window; (iii) state how 0.1 μB/carrier follows from the data, giving statistical uncertainty (noise floor, fit residuals) and calibration uncertainty separately; (iv) explain the "additional structure" in Fig. 2g; (v) state the size of the effect predicted by Ref. [5] or by a simple estimate (for example a condensate fraction of order Δ/E_F times the moment per pair), so that readers can judge whether the bound constrains anything.

B. Should address

4. The conversion from ΔB to M and m has no stated uncertainty, and the values in the superconductor disagree between figures. Every quantitative statement depends on the fringe-field inversion (least squares with a gradient penalty; Methods, Fig. E2): "order 10 μB per electron", m ≈ 1 μB/carrier in the superconductor, and the 0.1 μB bound. The tip height is given only as a range (200–350 nm in one place, 150–350 nm in another), the tip is tilted by 12.5°, and the regularization strength, the free and fixed parameters, the treatment of the reference state and the fit residuals are not given. In the superconductor the text says m is "indistinguishable from 1 μB/carrier" (Fig. 4f, D = 1.02 V/nm). The fit in Fig. E2c peaks at about 1 μB/carrier, but Fig. E2d, the same data as Fig. 4c (n_e = 0.71 × 10¹² cm⁻², D = 1.05 V/nm), peaks at about 2 μB/carrier. The bistable ΔB values in Fig. 4e (blue) are not symmetric about zero, as two time-reversed domains of one state would be, which suggests partial domains or a reference offset. The authors should state the inversion parameters, propagate the tip-height and regularization uncertainties into M and m, put error bars on Figs. 1c–d and 4f and on every quoted m, explain the E2c/E2d difference (regularization, tip height or reference point), and discuss the asymmetry in Fig. 4e. This bears directly on the spin-only reading (concern 2) and on the 0.1 μB bound (concern 3).

5. The in-plane spin moment of the reference state, seen by a tilted tip, is not accounted for. In Figs. 2 and 4 the reference point is a spin-polarized, valley-balanced half metal under B∥ = 40–50 mT, assumed to have "zero magnetic fringe field due to the in-plane applied magnetic field" (Methods, Fig. 4a–b). Its spin moment lies in the plane. The tip is tilted by 12.5°, so it is partly sensitive to in-plane fringe fields, and an in-plane-magnetized sheet produces fringe fields at its edges (compare the simulation in Fig. E10b). Going from the reference to a valley-polarized state also rotates the spin moment out of plane. ΔB therefore contains an in-plane term that the out-of-plane-only fits (Fig. 2c–d, Fig. E2) leave out. This matters most for the small superconducting-state signal, for the m ≈ 1 μB/carrier value, and near device edges and contacts. The authors should estimate the reference-state contribution along the tip's sensing axis, or include an in-plane component in the inversion, and show that it does not distort the in-dome signal.

6. The electric-field background removed from the superconducting-state images is as large as the signal and is taken from a mismatched gate configuration. The superconducting-state ΔB is about 0.1 μT. The parasitic electric-field contrast in the raw image (Fig. S3a) reaches about ±0.25 μT, and it is removed by subtracting a "nonmagnetic" image taken at n_e = 0, D = 0.875 V/nm with reference point (3.25 × 10¹² cm⁻², 0.58 V/nm), rather than at the D = 1.05 V/nm of the data. The SI says the background is "most sensitive to changes in the voltage of the top gate", so a background linear in each gate voltage would not cancel exactly. The parasitic contrast concentrates over the contacts, and so does much of the structure in the superconducting-state signal, so a mis-scaled subtraction could imprint contact-shaped features. The authors should state the top-gate and back-gate voltage steps for both images and show that they match, show residual maps for two nonmagnetic configurations with matched steps (or show the result with two independent background references), and state whether the same subtraction or a low-density planar fit (Fig. S2) was used in Figs. 2e and 4a–b.

7. The chiral-superconductivity conclusion follows from a definition, and "dispositive" is too strong. The Discussion adopts the definition of Ref. [33] (any superconductor coexisting with finite orbital magnetization) and calls the observation "dispositive evidence of chiral superconductivity under this definition"; the Fig. 2 text uses "dispositive" for orbital magnetization in the superconducting ground state. Given concerns 1 and 2, neither coexistence in the same region nor the orbital character of the moment is yet established. Even if both were, the conclusion would follow from the definition alone and say nothing about winding of the order parameter, on which the authors concede the data have "no bearing". The authors should drop "dispositive", and the abstract and Discussion should state that the data show a superconductor coexisting with valley (and spin) polarization, with chirality in the conventional sense untested. The caveat about the expanded definition should appear in the abstract.

8. Theory and experiment agree only qualitatively, and the comparison is made at mismatched parameters. For 13L, Fig. 1h gives M_total ≈ 4–5 μB·10¹² cm⁻² at n_e ≈ 2.5–2.6 × 10¹² cm⁻² (about 2 μB/carrier). The measurement in Fig. 1d peaks at about 24–25 μB·10¹² cm⁻² (about 9–10 μB/carrier, consistent with the text and with the fit in Fig. E2b). That factor of 3–5 is not discussed. The calculation uses D = 0.9 V/nm, while the experimental trajectories in Fig. 1a–b run at D ≈ 0.7–0.85 V/nm. It assumes 30 nm gate distances and ε_r = 4, whereas Table I lists hBN thicknesses of 8–30 nm (12 nm for 13L). It includes Hartree screening only, imposes single-flavor occupancy at all n_e by hand, and uses fixed hopping parameters from Ref. [47]. It gives a monotonically increasing, positive M_total with no peak, and it reproduces neither the low-density suppression nor the "slightly negative" magnetization; the observed decrease is attributed to a half-metal transition that is not computed. No calculation is shown for 6L, the device in Fig. 1c. The authors should compute M(n_e) along the actual experimental (n_e, D) trajectories with each device's gate geometry, for both 6L and 13L, plot it in μB/carrier directly against Fig. 1c–d, report sensitivity to D, ε_r and the remote hoppings (Eq. 12. Statements about sample temperature and heating are inconsistent or unsupported. Fig. S4b is used to show that square-wave gate modulation does not heat the superconductor, yet the bistability in Fig. 4e is attributed to "transient heating from the gate modulation" large enough to drive thermal domain reversal at about 1 Hz. The Fig. 2a phase diagram is labelled T ≈ 800 mK, but this is an estimate of nSOT heating in exchange gas, inferred "from transport comparisons" that are not shown. Because the low-density boundary of the quarter metal depends strongly on temperature, comparing normal and superconducting phase diagrams at fixed (n_e, D) depends on knowing the actual temperature.
