Review outline:

Note on scope: the PDF supplied for review contains the main text, four figures and references (16 pages). The Supplementary Materials it refers to repeatedly (full dataset, measurement details, error-bar procedure, DFT and Planckian analysis) were not included. Where a concern below may already be answered there, I say so, and ask that the decisive information be brought into the main text.

1. Significance and novelty

The authors measure the transport temperature exponent n(H, θ_b-c) of UTe2 in pulsed fields up to about 62 T with the field rotated in the b-c plane. They report that ρ(T) becomes linear in T (n ≈ 1) in a narrow window just above the metamagnetic transition H* near θ_b-c ≈ 35°, where the field-induced SC3 phase is strongest. A large slope there is estimated to correspond to near-Planckian scattering (α = 1.3 ± 0.6). If the result holds, it is the first report of strange-metal transport next to a putatively spin-triplet, field-induced superconducting phase inside a spin-polarized state, and it would add a new kind of setting to the strange-metal and superconductivity link. The measurements are hard and the dataset is valuable. However, the main conclusion rests on power-law fits over a narrow temperature window in pulsed fields, close to a first-order transition, and the evidence for "Planckian" and for a link to SC3 is still indirect.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A unique and technically demanding dataset: angle-resolved ρ(H, T) to above 60 T across the SC3 angular window, with zero resistance in SC3 at 0.5 K (Figs. 1c, 3c,d) showing that the sample quality and alignment reach SC3.
- A clear, reproducible qualitative contrast. At 10 T, n stays near 2 at all angles (Figs. 3e-h, 4c). At H* + 5 T, n changes strongly with angle, from about 3 at 25° to about 1 at 36° (Figs. 3, 4b), and the A coefficient is enhanced at the same angles (Fig. 4e).
- Clean, high-mobility samples (ρ0 ≈ 1 μΩ cm, Tc = 2.1 K) with Fermi-liquid T² at zero field (Fig. 1a), which gives a sound baseline.
- A thoughtful discussion of why the metamagnetic quantum critical line is unlikely to explain the angular selectivity, and a concrete call for thermodynamic probes.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The T-linearity claim rests on three-parameter fits over a factor of about 3 in temperature, and these may sample an inflection point rather than a genuine power law. Equation 1 is fitted with A, n and ρ0 all free over Tc < T < 7.5 K. In practice that is about 2.4-7.5 K (Figs. 1e, 2, 3e-h), roughly half a decade, with about 8-10 points. In every high-field panel of Fig. 2 (45, 50, 55, 60 T), the 8.9 K and 10 K points fall well below the extrapolated fit, so ρ(T) bends downward (sublinearly) just above the window. A curve that is convex at low T and concave at higher T always has a range where a free-exponent fit returns n ≈ 1, whether or not the scattering is T-linear. The window is also not justified by the zero-field data: Fig. 1a puts the deviation from T² at T_Dev ≈ 6 K, below the 7.5 K cut-off. With n and ρ0 free in such a short window, the two are strongly correlated, and the n = 1 result at 50 T depends on the extrapolated ρ0 (about 43 μΩ cm at 50 T, Fig. 2d, compared with 1 μΩ cm at zero field). The authors should:
   (i) show how sensitive n is to the upper and lower window limits (for example T_max = 5, 6, 7.5 K) at 36° and at the other angles;
   (ii) show the logarithmic derivative d ln(ρ - ρ0)/d ln T, or dρ/dT against T, at 50 T, so readers can see whether there is a T-independent slope or only an inflection;
   (iii) report the covariance between n and ρ0, and the fit with ρ0 fixed by an independent route;
   (iv) at angles where SC3 can be avoided (just outside the SC3 window, or at fields above H_c2^SC3 near 44°), extend the data below 2 K to test whether linearity continues as T → 0.
   Without this, "strange metallicity" should be described as an exponent minimum rather than an established T-linear state.

2. The sample temperature during the pulse, near a first-order metamagnetic transition, is not established. The key cuts sit 5 T above H*, where a first-order transition with a magnetization jump of about 0.5 μB/U has been crossed only milliseconds earlier. Magnetocaloric heating or cooling and latent heat at H*, together with eddy-current heating in a metallic sample in a pulsed field, change the sample temperature in a way that depends on field and temperature. This would affect the high-T and low-T points differently and directly distort the exponent. Each ρ(T) cut is also assembled from separate pulses at different starting temperatures. The main text does not say which sweep direction is used, whether up- and down-sweeps agree, what the pulse length is, or how the temperature was checked at fields above H*. The authors should:
   - compare up-sweep and down-sweep ρ(H) at the same nominal T for θ = 36°;
   - where possible, compare pulses of different duration, or a DC-field point at 36° below about 45 T as a cross-check;
   - estimate the magnetocaloric temperature change at H* from the known magnetization jump and specific heat, and show it is small compared with the 0.5-1 K spacing between temperatures used in the fits.
   If these checks are in the SM, the main text should summarize them.

3. The spatial match between T-linearity and SC3 is not shown with the resolution or independence the title and abstract imply. Four points:
   (a) In Fig. 4a,d the SC3, SC2 and PM boundaries (white lines) are the schematic boundaries from the literature, not boundaries measured on these samples, even though the 0.5 K traces in Figs. 1c and 3c,d contain that information.
   (b) The heatmaps interpolate between about seven angles (vertical lines in Fig. 4a: about 15°, 18°, 25°, 32°, 36°, 36.5°, 44°). The minimum of n rests essentially on the 32°, 36° and 36.5° points, with nothing between 37° and 44°, so the "bright yellow region centred around 35°" is mostly interpolation. The interpolation scheme is not described.
   (c) At 36°, Fig. 1d shows that n returns to about 2 by 60 T, while SC3 at this angle extends to about 65-70 T (Fig. 1b). T-linearity is therefore confined to a few tesla just above H*, not spread over the SC3 dome. That fits a link to the metamagnetic transition at least as naturally as a link to SC3, and "encompasses" in the title overstates it.
   (d) Because the red points are defined as H* + 5 T, they lie inside SC3 by construction for 30-40° and outside it elsewhere, so the angle at which n is smallest and the SC3 window are not independent tests.
   The authors should: determine the SC3 boundaries from their own 0.5 K data; add angles between 37° and 44° and between 25° and 32°; give full n(H) curves like Fig. 1d at every measured angle, rather than one field per angle; and show whether the exponent minimum tracks H* (constant H - H*) or tracks the SC3 dome (for example, maximal Tc^SC3).

4. The Planckian estimate (α = 1.3 ± 0.6) uses band parameters that probably do not apply in the polarized paramagnetic state. The scattering rate is derived from plasma frequencies of the low-field Fermi surface (refs. 31-35), with renormalization from fits to the SC3 domain and its coherence length. Above H*, UTe2 has crossed a first-order transition with a large magnetization jump and a jump in resistivity of more than 40 μΩ cm (Figs. 1c, 2). That makes a Fermi-surface reconstruction or change in mass likely, and the effective mass and carrier density in the PPM state are not independently known. The 50% uncertainty already allows α anywhere from about 0.7 to 1.9, so "occurs at the Planckian rate" is not tightly constrained. The authors should:
   - state in the main text which quantities enter α and which are taken from low-field data;
   - propagate the uncertainty from a possible Fermi-surface change across H* (for example, a range of plausible n/m* in the PPM state);
   - cross-check with any independent measure of the PPM-state mass, such as the Sommerfeld coefficient above H* from the literature or the Kadowaki-Woods ratio from the T² coefficient at 60 T.
   If this cannot be pinned down, "Planckian" should be softened in the abstract and title-level claims.

B. Should address

5. The constant-field cuts lie on the steep, temperature-dependent shoulder of the resistivity peak at H*. In Fig. 1c the peak at H* ≈ 45-46 T broadens and changes shape with temperature, and the first-order transition is expected to turn into a crossover as the temperature rises towards the critical end point. A fixed-field cut at 50 T therefore samples a different part of the peak tail at each temperature. That alone can produce an apparent T-dependence unrelated to the scattering rate of a homogeneous phase. The authors should show the peak position and width against T at each angle, and repeat the exponent analysis at H* + 3, 5, 8 and 10 T to test whether n ≈ 1 survives away from the immediate vicinity of the transition. If the critical end-point temperature at 36° is known, it should be stated relative to the 2.4-7.5 K fit window.

6. Superconducting fluctuations above Tc^SC3 may affect the lowest-temperature points. At about 35°, Tc^SC3 reaches about 2.4 K (text, p. 6), which is the lowest temperature included in the fits (Figs. 1e, 2d). Paraconductivity just above Tc lowers ρ at the low-T end, exactly where it most affects the fitted ρ0 and n, and only at the angles and fields where SC3 is strongest. That would correlate the exponent anomaly with SC3 artificially. The authors should exclude points within about 1 K of the local Tc^SC3(H, θ), measured on their own samples, and show that n is unchanged.

7. The A heatmap compares quantities with different dimensions using an extrapolated measure. Fig. 4d,e and the text (p. 8) use "the initial increment of the fitted resistivity between 0 K and 1 K", which is numerically just A, read from a region (0-1 K) below Tc where there are no normal-state data. For n = 1 this increment is the slope; for n = 2 it is small by construction. The claimed "order of magnitude" enhancement therefore partly reflects the change in n. A dimensionally consistent comparison would plot dρ/dT at a fixed measured temperature (for example 4 K), or (ρ(T1) - ρ(T2))/(T1 - T2) over the measured window, for both field sets.

8. Number of samples, angle calibration and out-of-plane misalignment are not given in the main text. The main text does not say how many crystals were measured, whether each angle is a separate sample or mounting, or whether the n ≈ 1 result at about 36° was reproduced on more than one crystal (36° and 36.5° look like separate runs). SC3 is known to be very sensitive to tilt toward the a-axis. The angle uncertainty in a pulsed-field rotator, and any a-axis component, could shift where the exponent minimum falls relative to SC3. The authors should give the sample count, the RRR of each sample, and the angle calibration (for example, H*(θ) fitted to 1/cos θ, which they already use), with uncertainty.

9. The orbital magnetoresistance contribution is not separated out. With j ∥ a and H in the b-c plane, the transverse orbital magnetoresistance at 40-60 T depends on temperature through τ(T) and can change the apparent ρ(T) exponent at fixed H. A Kohler-type analysis, or the H-dependence of ρ in the PPM state at several T, would show whether ρ(T, H = const) reflects the zero-field-like scattering rate the Planckian analysis assumes.

10. The interpretation in the Discussion goes beyond what the data show. "Strongly point to the presence of quantum criticality" (p. 11) and the suggestion of an "additional, so far unidentified, order parameter" (p. 13) rest on a transport exponent alone. There is no divergence in A as a control parameter is tuned through a point, no thermodynamic anomaly, and no scaling collapse. The link to spin-triplet pairing in SC3 is also assumed ("putatively", p. 4). The authors should present these as hypotheses. Where possible, they should test the scaling expected near a QCP, for example whether A(θ) or the width in angle of the n < 1.5 region narrows as T → 0.

C. Minor

11. The exponent values in the text and figures disagree:
   - p. 7 says n = 1.9 at 10 T, citing Fig. 1c, but Fig. 1e and Fig. 2a give n = 1.8, and Fig. 1c shows no fit.
   - p. 8 says 2.1 ≤ n ≤ 2.5 at 10 T over 18°-44°, but Fig. 4c contains a point near 1.7-1.8 at about 36° (matching Fig. 1e).
   - The Fig. 4c caption says "n = 2 within error".
   Please reconcile these.
12. The guide line in Fig. 4b dips to about 0.7-0.8 near 35°, below every data point and below n = 1. It suggests a sub-linear minimum that the data do not show. Please redraw it or remove it.
13. Fig. 1d places the minimum of n at about 48 T, while the text says the drop happens "upon crossing H* (that for this angle is at around 45 T)" and the Fig. 2 caption says μ0H* ≈ 45 T. State how H* is defined (peak in ρ, midpoint of the step, at which T), and whether the minimum of n sits at a fixed offset above it.
14. The text (p. 8) quotes the minimum at θ = 36°, while the Fig. 3 caption says linearity is closest at 37° (n = 1.2, Fig. 3g). Please use consistent angle labels, and give the uncertainty on each angle.
15. Error bars in Fig. 4b,c,e,f vary widely (for example about ±2 in n at 15° in Fig. 4c, and about ±7 in A at about 32° in Fig. 4e) with no explanation in the main text. Briefly state the procedure in the caption, including whether systematic window dependence is included.
16. Fig. 1d is built from "100 equal-length intervals" between 10 and 62 T. Give the bin width (about 0.5 T) and confirm that bins near H* do not average across the transition.
17. Units: "μΩcm" in Fig. 2 and "μΩ cmK^-n" in Fig. 4 should be written "μΩ cm" and "μΩ cm K^-n". Ref. 25 lacks a volume or page number.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a panel of d ln(ρ - ρ0)/d ln T (or dρ/dT) against T at 36°, 50 T and at a control angle (for example 25°, H* + 5 T). This would make the T-linearity claim visually robust or show its limits.
2. Measure the SC3 boundaries on the same crystals and overlay them on the exponent map, with denser angles at 37-44° and 25-32°. Show n against H - H* for all angles on one plot to separate the effect of H* from the effect of SC3.
3. Bring the essential pulsed-field controls (up/down sweep, temperature calibration, magnetocaloric estimate, sample count) and the key ingredients of the α estimate into the main text or Methods.
4. Moderate the title and abstract ("encompasses", "Planckian", "shared underlying mechanism") to match what transport alone establishes, and keep the stronger statements for after thermodynamic tests that the authors themselves propose.
