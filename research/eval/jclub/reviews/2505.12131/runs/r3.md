Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports pulsed-field (up to ~62 T) magnetotransport on UTe2 for fields rotated in the b-c plane. The authors extract an effective resistivity exponent n from fits of rho = rho0 + A T^n over T_c < T < 7.5 K. They find that n falls to ~1 and A peaks in a narrow window just above the metamagnetic field H* near theta_b-c ~ 35 deg, where the field-induced SC3 phase is strongest. They argue that SC3 emerges from a Planckian strange metal (alpha = 1.3 +/- 0.6). If it holds up, this would be the first association of T-linear, Planckian transport with a putatively spin-triplet, field-induced superconductor inside a spin-polarized state, which would interest a broad audience. The angular sharpness of the effect is also striking. As presented, however, the T-linear identification rests on three-parameter fits over a factor of about three in temperature, made at fixed field next to a temperature-dependent first-order transition. The Planckian estimate and most methodological details sit in Supplementary Materials that are not in the submitted PDF.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The data set is rare and technically demanding: angle-resolved, multi-temperature rho(H) to 62 T across the full SC3 angular window, on high-quality crystals (rho0 ~ 1 uOhm cm at zero field).
- The comparison at a fixed offset H* + 5 T across angles (Fig. 3e-h, Fig. 4b) is a useful internal control. At 18 and 25 deg the same offset from H* gives n ~ 3, so the low n at ~36 deg is not simply generic to being just above the metamagnetic transition.
- The coincidence of minimal n and maximal A (Fig. 4b,e) with the angle of maximal T_c^SC3 and H_c2^SC3 is a suggestive correlation that bears on the SC3 pairing mechanism.
- The Discussion clearly argues why the metamagnetic quantum critical line is unlikely to be the source, and it states openly that no thermodynamic anomaly is known near 35 deg.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Fixed-field cuts next to a temperature-dependent first-order metamagnetic transition can produce an apparent T-linear rho(T).
   - What the data show: in Fig. 1c, Fig. 3a-d and Fig. 2, rho(H) has a sharp peak at H* ~ 45 T (36 deg), followed by a steep negative magnetoresistance. The peak broadens and shifts with temperature.
   - Why it matters: the T-linear cut at 50 T (Fig. 1e, Fig. 2d) and the H* + 5 T cuts (Fig. 3e-h, Fig. 4b,e) sit on this tail. If H*(T), the transition width, or the peak amplitude depend on T, a fixed-H slice picks up a contribution d(rho)/dH x dH*/dT that has nothing to do with inelastic scattering.
   - Why it is specific to the key angle: at 36 deg the tail is steepest and the peak is largest, and the authors' own earlier work (ref. 28) shows the critical end point moving towards zero temperature with tilt. The critical end-point temperature, or the crossover broadening, may therefore lie inside the 2-7.5 K fit window exactly at the angles of interest.
   - Requested:
     - plot H*(T) and the transition width versus T for each angle;
     - redo the analysis at fixed H - H*(T), and at several offsets (for example H* + 2, + 5, + 10, + 15 T), at each angle;
     - show that the T-linear behaviour does not track the steepness of d(rho)/dH.
   - Fig. 1d places the minimum of n at ~48 T, close to the peak region, which reinforces this worry.

2. The "T-linear" regime may be an inflection point between a T^2 regime and a saturating regime.
   - In Fig. 1e and Fig. 2d (50 T), the points at 8.9 K and 10 K fall clearly below the linear fit. The same downward curvature above ~7.5 K appears at every field in Fig. 2.
   - In zero field (Fig. 1a), T^2 behaviour already fails above T_Dev ~ 6 K, so the 2-7.5 K window mixes regimes even without a field. This is why n = 1.8 rather than 2 is found at 10 T.
   - In the PPM state the coherence scale is presumably lowered, so a curve that goes from convex (Fermi liquid) to concave (approaching a maximum) passes through a nearly linear stretch near its inflection point. With rho0, A and n all free over a factor of ~3 in T, such a curve returns n ~ 1.
   - A narrow window with a shifting crossover scale would produce a minimum of n where that scale is lowest, without requiring a strange metal.
   - Requested:
     - show d(rho)/dT versus T, and the logarithmic derivative d ln(rho - rho0)/d ln T, at 50 T and at neighbouring fields and angles, with rho0 fixed independently (for example from the lowest-T normal-state data, or from fields where SC3 is absent);
     - show the covariance of n with rho0;
     - test whether the result survives shrinking the window (2.3-5 K) or moving it;
     - report the temperature T_max of the resistivity maximum versus angle and field. If T_max is lowest near 35 deg, the minimum in n may simply follow it.

3. Sample temperature in pulsed fields is not established at the transition where the effect is seen.
   - A first-order metamagnetic transition with a ~0.5 mu_B magnetization jump carries a large entropy change, so the magnetocaloric effect in a quasi-adiabatic pulse can shift the sample temperature by an amount comparable to the 0.5-1 K spacing of the isotherms. This happens precisely at and just above H*, where the T-linear cuts are taken. Eddy-current heating in a metal at 62 T is a further concern.
   - Because n is extracted from slices across isotherms, a T-dependent temperature error just above H* would distort n directly.
   - Requested:
     - compare up-sweep and down-sweep data, and data taken at different pulse lengths or dB/dt;
     - show that rho(H) on the falling side matches at 50 T;
     - state how the sample temperature is measured or assured during the pulse. The Supplementary Materials are said to contain the method but are not in the submitted PDF.

4. The data do not support the claim that the strange metal "encompasses" SC3 or is "confined to" its strongest region.
   - At 36 deg, SC3 extends to ~68-73 T (Fig. 1b; ref. 26). At 0.5 K, Fig. 1c shows zero resistance up to ~58 T. Yet n returns to 1.9 at 60 T (Fig. 2f) and 1.5 at 55 T (Fig. 2e). T-linearity therefore occupies only a narrow sliver just above H*, not the SC3 dome.
   - The T-linearity was effectively sampled along one line (H* + 5 T) and at 10 T. The heatmaps in Fig. 4a,d are interpolations between about eight angles (the vertical lines). The yellow region's extent and shape, including its apparent coincidence with the dome, are set by the interpolation rather than by data.
   - The white SC3 phase boundaries are described as schematic, taken from the literature (refs. 21, 22, 26-29), and not measured on these samples.
   - Requested:
     - overlay the actual n values at all measured (H, theta) points without interpolation;
     - determine the SC3 boundaries (onset of zero resistance at the lowest T) from the authors' own 0.5 K data;
     - extend the analysis over the full field range of the SC3 dome at each angle.
   - The title, abstract and Fig. 4 title should then be reworded to match: T-linearity near H* in the angular range of SC3, rather than strange metallicity "encompassing" or "underpinning" SC3.

5. The Planckian claim (alpha = 1.3 +/- 0.6) cannot be evaluated, and its inputs appear mismatched to the state studied.
   - The estimate is described only qualitatively (DFT bare and renormalized plasma frequencies, "fittings of the SC3 domain" and a coherence length), and all details are deferred to Supplementary Materials that are not in the submitted PDF.
   - The DFT is based on the low-field Fermi surface (refs. 31-35). At 50 T the system is in the spin-polarized PPM state with ~0.5 mu_B/U, and rho0 has risen from ~1 to ~40-50 uOhm cm (Fig. 2d). Any Fermi-surface reconstruction or change in mass across H* would change the plasma frequency entering alpha.
   - Extracting an effective mass from H_c2 or the coherence length of SC3 assumes an orbital limit and a clean-limit relation, which are questionable for a field-reentrant triplet state.
   - The quoted +/- 0.6 (about 50 %) is consistent both with alpha = 1 and with alpha ~ 2.
   - Requested:
     - include the full calculation in the submission;
     - justify the use of the low-field Fermi surface in the PPM state, or bound the error it introduces;
     - show how alpha depends on each input;
     - otherwise tone down "Planckian" in the title and abstract.

B. Should address

6. Superconducting fluctuations above T_c^SC3 could lower the lowest-temperature points and reduce the fitted n.
   - At 50 T and 36 deg, T_c^SC3 ~ 2-2.4 K, and the fit window starts at T_c. Paraconductivity just above T_c lowers rho at the low-T end and steepens the apparent slope there, which biases n downwards.
   - This bias would be largest where SC3 is strongest, giving exactly the observed correlation.
   - Requested: show that n is unchanged when the lower bound of the fit is raised to ~1.5-2 T_c, and compare cuts at the same field just outside the SC3 angular range.

7. Number of samples, mounting and angle calibration are not stated in the main text.
   - The main text does not say how many crystals were measured, whether the different angles come from different samples or from re-mounts, or whether the key n ~ 1 result at ~36 deg was reproduced on more than one sample. The three points at 36-37 deg in Fig. 4b suggest some repetition; this should be stated explicitly.
   - The effect is extremely sharp in angle, and the authors' own ref. 28 shows that an a-axis field component drives the metamagnetic end point towards a quantum critical end point. The uncertainty in theta_b-c, and in any out-of-plane (a-axis) misalignment, is therefore essential.
   - Requested: state the samples (dimensions, rho0, RRR), the angle-calibration method (for example H*(theta) against the 1/cos law) and the angular uncertainties, and show the n(theta) minimum for at least two samples.

8. The quantum-criticality interpretation outruns the evidence.
   - The Discussion states that the findings "strongly point to the presence of quantum criticality", and speculates about a new order parameter (SDW or hidden order).
   - Transport alone does not establish this. A T-linear stretch over 2-7.5 K, with no divergence of A as T decreases or as a control parameter is tuned, and no thermodynamic anomaly (acknowledged on p. 11), is equally compatible with a crossover.
   - Requested: either provide supporting evidence, for example scaling of A or of a crossover temperature towards a point in (H, theta), or a field-dependent thermodynamic probe, or frame these statements as hypotheses.

9. The fit procedure and error bars need to be documented and stress-tested.
   - Each fit has three free parameters (rho0, A, n) against about 8-10 temperatures.
   - The A heatmap is defined as the fitted increment between 0 and 1 K. This extrapolates below the lowest data point (~2.3 K) and, because of the n-A covariance, depends strongly on n.
   - The error bars in Fig. 4b,c,e,f are deferred to Supplementary Materials that are not included. Some are very large (for example n at ~15 deg in Fig. 4c spans ~0.5-4.5).
   - Requested:
     - give the fitting procedure, how rho0 is handled, and the parameter covariance in the main text or Methods;
     - quote A at a temperature inside the data window (for example the slope d(rho)/dT at 4 K), which avoids extrapolation and dimension issues.

10. The large field-induced residual resistivity and the magnetoresistance need to be accounted for.
   - rho0 rises from ~1 uOhm cm at zero field to ~40-55 uOhm cm in the PPM state (Fig. 2d-f). The current is along a with the field in the b-c plane, so orbital magnetoresistance and spin-disorder or domain scattering at the metamagnetic transition contribute and may themselves depend on T. Kohler-type mixing of orbital magnetoresistance with the zero-field T dependence can change the apparent exponent.
   - Requested: discuss whether rho - rho0 at 50 T can be separated from the T dependence of the magnetoresistance, for example via the field dependence at fixed T on either side of 50 T. Measuring a second current direction would strengthen the claim that the scattering is intrinsic.

C. Minor

11. Several numbers in the text and figures disagree.
   - The text (p. 7) gives n = 1.9 at 10 T "(Fig. 1c)", whereas Fig. 1e and Fig. 2a give n = 1.8, and the relevant panel is Fig. 1e, not 1c.
   - The text (p. 8) gives n = 3.0 at 25 deg and Fig. 3f gives 3.0, but the 25 deg point in Fig. 4b sits at ~2.6.
   - The text gives the minimum n = 1.0 at 36 deg, while Fig. 3 shows 37 deg with n = 1.2. Please make clear which datasets correspond to which angle.
   - The text says n drops to 1 "upon crossing H* (~45 T)", but the minimum in Fig. 1d is at ~48 T.

12. Fig. 1d is built over "T_c < T < 7.5 K", but T_c depends strongly on field across 10-62 T (SC1, gap, SC3). State the lower bound used in each field bin and the number of temperatures per bin.

13. Figure presentation needs some corrections.
   - The Fig. 1c caption says 0.5-8.9 K, but the colour bar shows 2.5-7.5 K.
   - The colour bars in Fig. 3a-d lack tick values for several curves.
   - The units of A in Fig. 4d-f ("uOhm cm K^-n") change between points; this should be explained in the caption.
   - "PM" appears in Fig. 4a but not in Fig. 1b; please use consistent labels.

14. Ref. 25 lacks volume and page numbers. The date on the title page (July 28, 2026) differs from the arXiv stamp.

15. The Introduction defines the Planckian rate as tau ~ hbar/(k_B T) and later introduces alpha. Using a single definition, 1/tau = alpha k_B T / hbar, would avoid confusion.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Re-analyse rho(T) at fixed H - H*(T) and at several offsets for every angle. Show logarithmic-derivative plots with rho0 fixed independently, and compare up and down sweeps to exclude magnetocaloric and transition-shift artifacts. This single figure would do most to establish or refute the T-linear claim.
2. Replace the interpolated heatmaps with maps of the measured points. Overlay SC3 boundaries determined from the same samples, and extend the n analysis across the full SC3 field range at each angle.
3. Include the Supplementary Materials (methods, thermometry, the full dataset, the error analysis and the alpha calculation) with the submission. Add a sentence on the number of samples and the reproducibility of the minimum in n.
4. Add a bulk thermodynamic probe in the same (H, theta) window, for example pulsed-field magnetocaloric measurements or the T dependence of the magnetization. It would test whether the transport anomaly marks an incipient order or quantum critical point, as proposed in the Discussion.
