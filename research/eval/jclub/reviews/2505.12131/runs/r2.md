Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports pulsed-field (to about 62 T) angle-dependent resistivity of UTe2 with the field rotated in the b–c plane. It finds that, just above the metamagnetic transition H* and near θ_b–c ≈ 35°, the normal-state ρ(T) between T_c and 7.5 K follows a power law with exponent n ≈ 1. The A coefficient is strongly enhanced in the same place, and a supplementary estimate gives α = ħ/(k_B T τ) = 1.3 ± 0.6. The authors argue that this "Planckian strange metal" encompasses the field-induced SC3 phase and shares its mechanism. If it holds, this is an interesting observation: T-linear transport tied to a putatively spin-triplet, field-induced superconductor inside a spin-polarized state. As presented, though, the evidence is a phenomenological power-law fit over a narrow temperature window, at fields right next to a first-order metamagnetic transition measured in pulsed fields. Several artifacts and conventional explanations have not yet been excluded.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The data are hard to obtain: systematic ρ(H) isotherms to 62 T at about eight angles across the SC3 window (Figs. 1c, 3a–d). Zero resistance at 0.5 K confirms that SC3 is reached in the same sample.
- The angle dependence is striking. At 10 T the exponent is nearly constant (n ≈ 2.1–2.5, Fig. 4c). At H* + 5 T it varies from about 3 to about 1 (Fig. 4b), and A rises by more than an order of magnitude (Fig. 4e). This is a real, angle-selective change in the normal-state scattering near the SC3 optimum.
- The constant-H cuts (Fig. 2) are cross-checked against the n(H) profile (Fig. 1d), and the exponent is non-monotonic: it returns towards 2 at 60 T.
- The discussion compares the result with the quantum critical line of the metamagnetic transition and is candid about the lack of a known order parameter near 35°.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Sample temperature during the pulse, just above a first-order metamagnetic transition. The n ≈ 1 region sits a few tesla above H* (Fig. 1d: the minimum is at about 48 T against H* ≈ 45 T), so every isotherm has just crossed a first-order transition with a magnetization jump of about 0.5 μ_B/U. In pulsed fields this transition causes magnetocaloric and dissipative (hysteretic, eddy-current) heating, which is largest at the lowest bath temperatures, where the heat capacity is smallest. If the true sample temperature at 50 T is higher than the nominal value for the 2–3 K curves, ρ is overestimated at the low-T end of every cut. That compresses the curvature and lowers the fitted n, and it does so preferentially just above H*, which is exactly where n ≈ 1 is found. The main text never says whether up-sweeps or down-sweeps were used, what the pulse duration and dB/dt were, or how T was known during the pulse. The Supplement is referenced but is not in the submitted PDF.
   What to do:
   - Show ρ(H) for rising and falling field and the n(H) extracted from each, at θ = 36°.
   - Show that n at 50 T does not change with pulse length or peak field (for example, pulses stopping below and well above H*).
   - Ideally, measure the magnetocaloric temperature change with a thermometer on the sample, or show a T-linear cut at a field where H* is approached from above on the down-sweep.

2. The T-linear field window coincides with the tail of the metamagnetic resistivity peak. In Figs. 1c and 3a–d, ρ(H) has a large peak at H* and a steep negative slope just above it. The peak height, width and position all depend on temperature: the peak sharpens, grows and shifts as T changes, and above some critical-endpoint temperature the first-order step becomes a crossover. A fixed-field cut at H* + 5 T therefore samples a different distance from the peak at each temperature. A temperature-dependent H*(T) or peak width alone can make ρ(T) at fixed H look linear over 2–7.5 K, with no change in the inelastic scattering rate. The strong angle dependence of n at H* + 5 T (3.0 at 25°, 1.0 at 36°) may simply reflect how the peak shape and the critical-endpoint temperature change with angle. Defining the red points from a single H* per angle (which temperature?) does not deal with this.
   What to do:
   - Report H*(T) and the peak width for each angle.
   - Repeat the n extraction at several offsets (H* + 2, + 5, + 10, + 15 T) and in reduced field H/H*(T).
   - Show where the first-order critical endpoint lies in temperature at each angle relative to the 2–7.5 K fit window.
   - Show that n ≈ 1 persists far enough from the peak that dρ/dH·dH*/dT cannot account for the linear term. A simple estimate of this term from the measured dρ/dH at 50 T and dH*/dT would be decisive.

3. The title claim, "strange metallicity encompasses ... superconductivity", is not supported by the data shown.
   - At θ = 36° the exponent climbs back to n ≈ 1.9 by 60 T (Figs. 1d and 2f). By the authors' own Fig. 1b, and the H_c2^SC3 ≈ 73 T quoted in the text, 60 T is still inside the SC3 dome. At 44° SC3 is clearly present (zero resistance near 55 T, Fig. 3d), yet n = 1.6 (Fig. 3h).
   - The n ≈ 1 region is therefore a narrow band just above the lower (metamagnetic) boundary of SC3, not a region encompassing the dome.
   - The SC3 boundaries overlaid in Figs. 1b and 4a, d appear to be a schematic from the literature, not boundaries determined in this sample.
   - The heatmaps interpolate between about eight measured angles, three of them clustered at 36–37°.
   What to do:
   - Determine T_c^SC3(θ, H) from the same sample (the 0.5 K and higher isotherms should allow this) and overlay the measured boundary.
   - Show the n and A maps without interpolation, or mark the measured (θ, H) grid explicitly.
   - Either restrict the claim to "T-linear resistivity at the low-field edge of SC3, near its optimal angle", or show data that support the broader statement.

4. The robustness of the exponent is not established.
   - n is obtained from a three-parameter fit (ρ0, A, n) over T_c < T < 7.5 K, roughly a factor of 3 in temperature. In this range ρ0 and n are strongly covariant.
   - At 50 T, ρ0 is an extrapolation about 12 μΩ cm below the lowest data point (Fig. 1e versus Fig. 2d).
   - Every panel of Fig. 2 and the 50 T cut in Fig. 1e shows points above about 8 K falling well below the fit, so the result depends on where the window is cut.
   - In zero field the T² regime itself ends at T_Dev ≈ 6 K (Fig. 1a), so the 7.5 K upper limit already includes non-Fermi-liquid curvature at 0 T.
   - Near T_c, superconducting fluctuations also distort the low-T end.
   What to do:
   - Show how n(50 T, 36°) changes as the upper limit moves from 5 to 9 K and the lower limit from T_c to 3 K.
   - Plot the logarithmic derivative d ln(ρ − ρ0)/d ln T, or dρ/dT against T, which shows linearity directly without a fitted ρ0.
   - Give the fit covariance between n and ρ0.
   - Where SC3 can be avoided (for example at angles just outside the dome, or with a small a-axis tilt), extend the cuts below 2 K to test whether a Fermi-liquid regime returns at a suppressed T_FL(H), as conventional metamagnetic crossovers would predict.

B. Should address

5. The Planckian estimate cannot be assessed, and its inputs look ill-suited to the polarized state.
   - The abstract and main text assert Planckian dissipation (α = 1.3 ± 0.6), but the calculation is entirely in a Supplement that is not part of the submitted document.
   - From the description, 1/τ comes from DFT plasma frequencies of the low-field Fermi surface, with a renormalization taken from SC3 fits and an implied coherence length. Above H* the magnetization jumps by about 0.5 μ_B/U, and the Fermi surface and effective masses are expected to change. The residual resistivity also rises from about 1 μΩ cm (Fig. 1a) to about 40 μΩ cm at 50 T (inferred from Figs. 1e and 2d), which signals a strong change in carriers or scattering.
   - Using low-field band parameters for the PPM state therefore needs justification.
   - The roughly 45% uncertainty covers a wide range of α, and the uncertainty in the sample geometric factor (pulsed-field samples are small) also enters the slope.
   What to do:
   - Include the full derivation, state which parameters were fitted and which were independent, and propagate the geometric-factor error.
   - Discuss Hall or quantum-oscillation evidence for the Fermi surface above H*, or present α as a conditional estimate rather than a result.

6. The A-coefficient map is biased by construction. A is defined as the fitted increment between 0 and 1 K, which is ρ(1 K) − ρ0 = A·1^n. This is an extrapolation below the lowest data point. For a fixed rise across the fit window it is mechanically larger when n is smaller. Part of the "order of magnitude" peak in Figs. 4d–e may therefore just restate the drop in n. The large error bars at 32° and 37° in Fig. 4e already hint at this. Compare scattering strength with a model-independent quantity such as ρ(5 K) − ρ(T_c⁺), or dρ/dT at a fixed temperature inside the window, mapped against θ and H.

7. Number of samples, angle calibration and misalignment.
   - The paper does not say how many crystals were measured, which angles came from which sample, or whether the n ≈ 1 result at 36° was reproduced on more than one sample or mounting.
   - SC3 and H* are both very sensitive to tilt towards the a-axis (ref. 28 of the paper), yet neither the in-plane angle uncertainty nor the out-of-plane misalignment is stated.
   - The heatmap places the minimum of n "centred around 35°", but the data have spacings of 0.5–1° at 36–37° and 7° elsewhere. The spread among 36°, 36.5° and 37° (Fig. 4b: n ≈ 1.0–1.2; Fig. 3g gives 1.2 at 37°) shows that small angle errors matter.
   - Please give the angle determination method and its uncertainty, the misalignment towards a, and a second sample at the optimal angle.

8. Conventional explanations are not discussed adequately.
   - Near a metamagnetic transition in heavy-fermion systems, the coherence scale is commonly suppressed. Over a factor-of-3 window, the incoherent-to-coherent crossover can produce apparently linear ρ(T) without a quantum critical point. The Fig. 1a zero-field deviation at about 6 K shows the coherence scale is already low.
   - The Lifshitz scenario is dismissed using the simplicity of the low-field Fermi surface (ref. 33 of the paper), which does not apply above H*.
   - The novelty claim, that T-linear resistivity has "not previously been associated with a spin-triplet superconductive state", should be weighed against field-reentrant superconductivity in URhGe and UCoGe, where an enhanced A coefficient and non-Fermi-liquid transport near the reorientation field accompany reentrant (putatively triplet) pairing.
   - What to do: address these alternatives explicitly and say what would distinguish them, for example magnetostriction or specific heat over the same (θ, H) window, or Hall data.

9. "Shared underlying mechanism" goes beyond a visual co-location. The link between the T-linear region and SC3 is argued from the position of a maximum in the interpolated maps against a literature dome. Please make the comparison quantitative, for example T_c^SC3 or H_c2^SC3 against A (or n, or the fixed-T slope) angle by angle in the same sample. Also discuss why n returns to about 2 deep inside the dome (60 T at 36°) if the same fluctuations mediate pairing there.

C. Minor

10. Internal inconsistencies.
    - The text gives n = 1.9 at 10 T, citing Fig. 1c, but Figs. 1e and 2a give n = 1.8. Fig. 1c does not show n at all (presumably 1e is meant).
    - The text quotes 2.1 ≤ n ≤ 2.5 at 10 T, whereas the Fig. 4c caption says "n = 2 (within error)".
    - The text says n reaches 1.0 at 36°, while the Fig. 3 caption calls 37° (n = 1.2) "close to linearity".
    - The Fig. 1c caption gives isotherms from 0.5 to 8.9 K, but Figs. 1e and 2 show points at about 10 K. Please state which temperatures exist and which enter the fits.

11. The grey guide line in Fig. 4b dips to about 0.75 near 35°, below every data point, which visually implies sublinear resistivity that the data do not show. Fig. 4e has the same issue with its peak. Please remove these guides or constrain them to the data.

12. The text quotes μ0H_c2^SC3 ≈ 73 T, but the domes drawn in Figs. 1b and 4a top out at about 65–68 T. Please state the source of the plotted boundaries and reconcile the two.

13. Attributing super-quadratic n ≈ 3 to spin-wave scattering (ref. 30 of the paper) is asserted without support. In a field-polarized paramagnet, magnon modes are gapped and should give activated rather than power-law behaviour. Please justify the attribution or soften it.

14. The Fig. 1d construction (100 bins between 10 and 62 T) should state the sweep direction. It should also state how the first-order hysteresis region is handled, since bins straddling H* mix the two states.

15. The main text gives no experimental details: current direction in pulsed field (presumably [100], as in Fig. 1a), contact configuration, sample dimensions, pulse profile, or thermometry. At least a short Methods section is needed, given that the Supplement was not supplied.

16. Fig. 1a quotes ρ0 ~ 1 μΩ cm as evidence of long mean free paths. The PPM-state ρ0 of about 40 μΩ cm should be commented on, since it bears on both the mean free path and the Planckian estimate.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a figure dedicated to artifacts at θ = 36°, 50 T. It should show up-sweep against down-sweep, two pulse lengths, the fit-window dependence of n, and d ln(ρ − ρ0)/d ln T. On its own this would settle most of concerns 1 and 4.
2. Recast the analysis in coordinates referenced to the metamagnetic transition, H − H*(T) or H/H*(T), at all angles. Show n and a model-independent slope as functions of this distance, together with the critical-endpoint temperature for each angle.
3. Extract T_c^SC3(θ, H) from the same data and replace the schematic domes with measured boundaries. Then correlate the superconducting and normal-state parameters angle by angle, and narrow the title and abstract claim if the T-linear region stays confined to the lower edge of the dome.
4. Put the Planckian analysis and its error budget in the paper (or a complete Supplement), and complement it with a measurement sensitive to the Fermi surface above H*, such as Hall effect or quantum oscillations. Alternatively, present α as an order-of-magnitude estimate.
