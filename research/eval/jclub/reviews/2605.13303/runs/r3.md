Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript uses scanning SQUID-on-tip (SOT) magnetometry, combined with transport, on rhombohedral pentalayer graphene (R5G). It images isospin (spin-valley) polarized domains in the quarter-metal (1/4M) phase and in the superconducting pocket next to it. The authors report four results. A positive or negative gate-modulated stray field Bz^ac, trainable by an initialization field, appears in the zero-resistance state. Resistive domain walls (DWs) explain the spike-like high-resistance (HR) hysteresis. The density n_DW(D) at which this hysteresis sets in tracks the superconducting boundary both above and below Tc. Nanoampere currents move DWs and switch the resistance. This would be the first real-space magnetic imaging inside a superconducting phase of rhombohedral graphene and the first direct visualization of isospin DWs and their current-driven motion, which makes it of broad interest. However, the headline claim, "establishing chiral superconductivity", rests partly on a definition of CSC that the data meet almost automatically, given that the parent state is already known to break time-reversal symmetry.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First spatially resolved magnetic images in the superconducting regime of RMG. The sign of Bz^ac follows the training field at a fixed measurement field (Fig. 1j,k).
- Direct connection between transport anomalies (HR spikes, LR state, negative Rxx) and specific domain configurations imaged in the same device (Figs. 2m-t, 3, 5, ED Fig. 9). This resolves the long-standing question of where the spikes in earlier RMG transport data come from.
- A quantitative estimate of the isospin DW resistivity (ρ_DW ≈ 41 kΩ·µm) from COMSOL modelling of the imaged geometry.
- Imaging of current-driven DW motion at sub-nA nominal currents, with polarity, frequency and amplitude dependences (Figs. 4, 5, ED Figs. 6-8). Reversible electrical switching of Rxx between about 0 and 2.7 kΩ at ±1.7 nA.
- The observation that the onset of spike-like hysteresis is the same at 20 mK and at 1 K (Fig. 2i-l). This points to a normal-state origin of the DW proliferation and is an interesting constraint for theory.
- Extensive supporting data on sweep rate, in-plane field and stochastic dynamics (ED Figs. 3, 5, 10).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The data establish TRS breaking coexisting with superconductivity, not chiral superconductivity in the sense the title and abstract imply. Page 2 defines CSC as "superconducting states exhibiting macroscopic TRS breaking of orbital origin, irrespective of its microscopic mechanism". Because superconductivity here emerges from a 1/4M parent that is already known to be orbitally ferromagnetic (refs 3, 14, 16), any superconductor in this pocket meets the definition automatically. The manuscript itself argues that the TRS breaking is "inherited from the parent 1/4M phase" (p. 5), and the Discussion (p. 14) concedes that "whether the superconducting order parameter itself is intrinsically chiral requires further investigation". Yet the abstract and p. 3-4 call Fig. 1h-k "unambiguous thermodynamic evidence for CSC". Page 3 also presents it as the signature "long sought but thus far absent in candidate chiral superconductors [40,41]". That comparison is not like-for-like: the Sr2RuO4 searches looked for magnetization generated by the superconducting condensate itself (edge or domain-wall supercurrents), not for a ferromagnetic normal-state background. Page 14 says the coexistence of superconductivity with oppositely polarized domains "has no analogue in any other superconductors". This overlooks ferromagnetic superconductors (for example UCoGe, URhGe and EuFe2(As1-xPx)2), where superconductivity coexists with switchable magnetic domains. The authors should do two things:
   - Measure Bz^ac (or m_z) at the same (n, D) point and training history from 20 mK through Tc to about 0.5-1 K. Any change at Tc would be a signature of the condensate itself; continuity would support "inherited" and show that the signal is a property of the normal state.
   - Revise the title, abstract and the "unambiguous" wording so that the claim is TRS breaking in the superconducting regime that is inherited from the isospin order. If they keep "chiral superconductivity", they should give evidence specific to the condensate.

2. The evidence from imaging in the zero-resistance state (Fig. 1h-k) is thin and not quantified, and alternative contributions are not excluded.
   - The maps are noisy, with signal of a few nT on a ±25 nT colour scale. The text calls the state "uniformly polarized, forming a single isospin-majority domain" (p. 8), but there are no averaged values, signal-to-noise figures, line cuts or reconstructed m_z. Sensitivity to small minority domains at a 140 nm scan height is not stated.
   - Figs. 1h and 1i flip sign together with B. That behaviour is also expected from a diamagnetic contribution, because gate modulation changes the superfluid density and hence the Meissner screening of the applied 26 mT, which gives Bz^ac proportional to B·d(λ_Pearl^-1)/dn. Gate-driven motion of field-induced or spontaneous vortices would do the same. Only Figs. 1j,k separate trained magnetization from field-odd responses, and they come from one (n, D) point in one device (D1). The authors should estimate the expected Meissner and vortex contributions, show the training dependence at several fields including B ≈ 0 and at more than one (n, D) point, and repeat it in D2.
   - It is not shown that the sample remained in the zero-resistance state during scanning. Possible problems are SOT tip heating (Tc ≈ 150 mK), the 0.1 Vrms backgate modulation, whose amplitude in n and D is not given and may move the system across the superconducting boundary or n_DW, and the spontaneous ZR-to-LR transitions shown in ED Fig. 3v,w. The authors should report Rxx measured during the scans, or immediately before and after each map, with the tip at scan height, and convert V_bg^ac into δn and δD.

3. The claim that n_DW(D) coincides with the superconducting boundary and signals a "hidden symmetry-breaking transition" is not tested quantitatively, and simpler explanations remain.
   - The coincidence is argued by overlaying a few magenta squares on Fig. 1d and ED Fig. 2a. The authors should give a plot of n_DW(D) against the superconducting onset density n_SC(D), defined by a stated criterion, with uncertainties, in both devices.
   - Spike-like HR hysteresis also appears in the high-|D| 1/4M phase where there is no superconductivity at 20 mK (yellow asterisk, Fig. 2m,n; Figs. 4a-f; ED Fig. 3a-d). This suggests that DW proliferation extends beyond the superconducting pocket, which weakens the one-to-one correspondence. The authors should show where the n_DW line runs outside the superconducting D range.
   - The differential magnetization falls steeply with decreasing n (ED Fig. 2d). An Imry-Ma-type picture, in which DWs proliferate once the Zeeman energy 2M_z·B and the line tension fall below the random-field disorder b(x), would also reduce B_co and produce domains without any secondary order. The authors should test this against the data, for example with M_z(n) and B_co(n) across n_DW.
   - No thermodynamic signature of the proposed transition at n_DW (a kink in m_z(n), compressibility or quantum-oscillation frequency) is shown. The Landau model (ED Fig. 11, λ = 0.7g) has free parameters that are not tied to any measurement.
   As written, "indicating an underlying transition in the parent state that both induces superconductivity and reduces domain-wall energy" (abstract) is a conjecture. The authors should either supply the missing evidence or present it as one hypothesis among several, as the Methods (p. 34) already does.

B. Should address

4. The extraction of the DW resistivity rests on several unquantified assumptions (Fig. 2q-t, p. 8).
   - The DW trajectory is taken from a difference map whose two images were acquired at different fields: 35 mT (LR) and 13 mT (HR), per ED Table 1.
   - The scan frame (dashed blue in Fig. 2s) does not cover the whole device, so domains outside it are unconstrained.
   - ρ_xx and ρ_xy are assumed identical in both domains, and ρ_DW is assumed uniform.
   - One parameter is fitted to two measured numbers, Rxx and Rxy, but the paper does not say whether both are reproduced or how well.
   The authors should give an uncertainty on ρ_DW from varying the DW path within the imaging resolution and from plausible unseen domains outside the frame. They should also show that the same ρ_DW (or a stated different value) reproduces the HR resistances of the imaged configurations in the superconducting phase (Figs. 3c, 5h and ED Fig. 9). Those resistances reach 2.7-25 kΩ, and the Landauer estimate of β ≈ 8×10^-3 and the momentum-transfer argument both depend on ρ_DW.

5. The quantitative support for a momentum-transfer mechanism is weak, and the currents are nominal (p. 27-29).
   - The two "independent" pinning estimates differ by about 8x (1.3×10^-15 against 1×10^-14 N/µm). The second uses the differential m_z ≈ 3 µB/e from Fig. 4g as if it were M_z/n, and uses B_co ≈ 50 mT, whereas ED Fig. 4 at D2 shows B_co ≈ 150 mT at I_dc = 0.
   - The superconducting condensation contribution is estimated at f_pin ≲ 3×10^-16 N/µm, an order of magnitude below the normal-state pinning estimate. Yet the authors invoke it to explain a fourfold increase in the required I_ac below Tc (6.55 nA against 1.63 nA).
   - ED Table 1 states that the actual ac current is "significantly lower" than nominal because the low-pass filter cutoff is at or below the drive frequency. The current that reaches the sample through these RC filters depends on the sample impedance, which differs strongly between 20 mK (superconducting bulk) and 550 mK (normal bulk), so the 20 mK against 550 mK comparison is confounded.
   The authors should calibrate the actual sample current at each drive frequency and temperature, for example with a circuit model or by measuring the voltage at the sample. They should also restate the 0.4 nA "lowest manipulation current" claim accordingly. Finally, they should give a test that separates momentum transfer from other current-driven mechanisms, such as the current-induced magnetization reversal in orbital Chern magnets (ref. 66) or electrochemical-potential and density shifts near the injecting contact. Examples are the scaling of threshold current with k_F or ρ_DW, or the dependence on which isospin is the majority (compare Fig. 4 with Fig. 5).

6. The interpretation of the in-plane field data (ED Fig. 10) is not secured.
   - The spin of the minority domains is never measured. If spin-orbit coupling is weak, an out-of-plane Zeeman field could favour K↑/K'↑ rather than K↑/K'↓ domains, and then the spin-flip argument for the large ρ_DW does not apply.
   - The peak R_xx^max comes from only four stochastic peaks per B∥, with large scatter (ED Fig. 10c). Peak resistance depends on the domain configuration, not only on ρ_DW.
   - A few-tenths-of-a-degree misalignment at B∥ = 0.5-1 T gives an out-of-plane component comparable to the coercive fields.
   - B∥ may also suppress superconductivity in the bulk.
   The authors should state the misalignment, show that the hysteresis-loop field positions are not shifted, compare with the same measurement at T > Tc, and soften "both spin and valley contribute to the interfacial barrier" unless a direct test is added.

7. The absence of a Josephson critical current across the DW is not evidence for different order parameters (p. 14). With ρ_DW ≈ 41 kΩ·µm and a wall a few µm long, the junction normal resistance is about 10 kΩ, above R_Q = h/4e² ≈ 6.5 kΩ. Using Δ ≈ 1.76 k_B·Tc (≈ 23 µeV), the Ambegaokar-Baratoff critical current is a few nA and the Josephson energy is comparable to k_B·T. Phase diffusion, or a dissipative (Schmid-type) suppression, would therefore remove any measurable supercurrent even for identical order parameters on both sides, and the 1 nA probe current is itself comparable to I_c. The authors should either remove this argument or support it with an estimate showing that a supercurrent would be expected.

8. The explanation of negative Rxx (p. 13, ED Fig. 9) uses an incorrect premise. The text says negative resistance at zero bias "would appear to violate local thermodynamic equilibrium". That holds for two-terminal resistance, but a four-terminal resistance can be negative in an inhomogeneous conductor in linear response, through current redistribution around a resistive wall or through a Hall-type contribution in this non-standard one-sided geometry. The authors already have the COMSOL model and should use it to check whether static DW configurations like ED Fig. 9g give negative Rxx. The claim that the field ramp acts as "an effective bias equivalent to a finite dc current" also needs a mechanism and an order-of-magnitude estimate.

9. "Reversible switching between states of opposite chirality" (abstract, p. 12) overstates what Figs. 4 and 5 show.
   - The current moves a single DW locally. The device is never switched between global K↑ and K'↓ states.
   - The switching in Fig. 5 is shown for about six cycles at one (n, D, B) point in D1, with visible fluctuations in the LR state. No endurance or yield statistics are given, and the effect is not shown in D2 or after thermal cycling.
   - The current density of 50 A/cm² comes from dividing a 2D sheet current by the 1.7 nm thickness and assuming w ≈ 4 µm. For a 2D system, a sheet current density in A/m is the meaningful comparison with moiré systems (refs 35, 36, 46).
   The authors should rephrase the claim, add statistics over many cycles and a second device or operating point, and quote linear current densities.

10. Transport is used as a non-invasive probe at currents that the paper shows are invasive. Standard transport uses I_ac = 1 nA rms (Methods), while DW motion is resolved at nominal 0.4-0.8 nA (ED Fig. 8a,b) and Fig. 5 switches at 1.7 nA. The phase diagrams (Fig. 1d,e), the Tc determination, the LR-state finite resistance and n_DW(D) were all measured in this regime. The authors should show that the key transport features, including the zero resistance, n_DW and the LR/HR values, do not change at lower excitation (for example 0.1-0.2 nA) or with dc I-V at small bias.

11. The m_z reconstruction method is not described. Figs. 1n,p and 4g and the quoted 9.5 µB/e and 3 µB/e rely on "numerical inversion" (refs 16, 43) that, according to the Acknowledgements, uses a neural-network model. The Methods do not describe the network, training data, regularization, edge handling or uncertainty. In addition, single-gate modulation (V_bg^ac or V_tg^ac) changes D as well as n, so Bz^ac contains a dM_z/dD term. The authors should describe the inversion, give uncertainties on the m_z values, and either correct for or bound the dM/dD contribution.

12. The one-sided contact geometry (Fig. 1a,b) makes "Rxx" and "Rxy" mixtures of longitudinal and transverse responses. The paper does not say whether the data are symmetrized or antisymmetrized in B. The "anomalous Hall" box loop in Fig. 1l and the HR/LR assignments rely on these quantities. The authors should state the procedure and, ideally, use the COMSOL model to show how σ_xx and σ_xy map onto the measured Rxx and Rxy in this geometry (partly done for Fig. 2r).

C. Minor

13. Fig. 5c and the text disagree on sign. The text and caption give Rxy ≈ +500 Ω in the LR state, but the plotted LR plateau sits at about −0.5 kΩ on an axis running from 0 to −3 kΩ.

14. The Fig. 1g colour bar is labelled "T (K)" with range 20-550; it should be mK. The Fig. 2a caption refers to the "green dashed line in (i,j)", while the Fig. 2i caption says the data were taken "along the cyan dashed line in Figs. 1d,e". The line colours and which data correspond to which line should be made consistent.

15. The LR−HR difference map in Fig. 2q subtracts images taken at different fields (35 and 13 mT) and the same scan height. SOT responsivity and any field-odd background can differ between them. Please comment, or show images taken at the same field.

16. The C3-breaking nematic order offered as the "hidden order" (p. 34) is speculative. It would help to state what measurement (for example angle-resolved transport or in-plane anomalous Hall) could confirm or rule it out in these devices.

17. The number of cooldowns and of independent ZR states imaged is not stated. Please report how often the ZR, LR and HR configurations of Figs. 1h-k and 3 were reproduced after thermal cycling above 10 K.

18. Data are "available on reasonable request". Depositing the SOT maps and transport data in a public repository would help reproducibility, given how much of the interpretation rests on noisy images.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a temperature series of gate-modulated Bz^ac maps at fixed (n, D) and training across Tc (20 mK to about 1 K) in the ZR region, together with simultaneous Rxx. This one dataset would settle whether the superconducting condensate contributes to the TRS-breaking signal, and it would show directly whether the magnetization is inherited from the parent state.
2. Reframe the central claim as "imaging of inherited orbital TRS breaking and isospin domains in the superconducting phase of RMG". Reserve "chiral superconductivity" for evidence specific to the condensate, and remove the "unambiguous" and "no analogue" statements.
3. Put the quantitative arguments on a firmer footing. Calibrate the actual sample currents through the filters, propagate the uncertainty on ρ_DW, use consistent M_z and B_co inputs in the pinning estimates, and use the COMSOL model to test the configurations behind the negative-Rxx and superconducting-phase HR states.
4. Plot n_DW(D) against n_SC(D) quantitatively for both devices, including the high-|D| region without superconductivity, and look for an independent thermodynamic signature at n_DW, such as a kink in m_z(n) from SOT or a change in quantum-oscillation frequency.
