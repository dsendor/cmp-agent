Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use scanning SQUID-on-tip (SOT) magnetometry on rhombohedral pentalayer graphene to image isospin (K-up / K'-down) domains in the quarter-metal (1/4M) phase and, for the first time, inside the zero-resistance superconducting pocket that sits at its low-density edge. They report four results. First, a sign-trainable magnetic signal in the zero-resistance state, which they present as thermodynamic evidence for chiral superconductivity (CSC). Second, highly resistive isospin domain walls (DWs) that produce the high-resistance, low-resistance and negative-resistance transport regimes. Third, a coincidence between the density at which DWs proliferate in the normal state, n_DW(D), and the boundary of the superconducting pocket. Fourth, DW motion driven by currents of a few nA, including switching between a low-resistance and a high-resistance state. Real-space imaging of this kind in a candidate chiral superconductor is new and of broad interest. The central claim, however, is that the superconducting state itself carries the trainable orbital TRS breaking. That claim needs firmer measurement controls and more careful framing than the manuscript currently gives it.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- This is the first spatially resolved magnetic imaging of the superconducting pocket in rhombohedral graphene. The opposite signals after opposite initialization at the same measurement field (Figs. 1j,k) are a clean training test.
- The 1/4M imaging is careful: the m_z reconstruction (Figs. 1m-p) gives uniform domains with large orbital magnetization, ~9.5 μB per electron.
- The imaged DW geometry is tied to specific transport states: the low-resistance (LR) and high-resistance (HR) states in Figs. 2o-q and 3c,d, and the COMSOL model in Figs. 2r-t. This gives a concrete microscopic explanation for the resistance spikes and hysteresis already reported in transport, and a plausible explanation for the negative R_xx (Extended Data Fig. 9).
- The current-driven bubble dynamics (Figs. 4, 5; Extended Data Figs. 6-8) are directly visualized, reversible and reproduced. The comparison at 20 mK and 550 mK (Extended Data Fig. 8) is an informative test of extra pinning below Tc.
- The observation that the spike-like hysteresis sets in at the same n_DW(D) above and below Tc (Figs. 2i-l; Extended Data Figs. 1, 2) is a striking empirical correlation that will motivate theory.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. It is not shown that the sample is superconducting throughout the gate-modulated imaging of Figs. 1h-k.
   - The signal B_z^ac ∝ n^ac dM_z/dn is generated by a back-gate modulation of 0.1 V rms (Extended Data Table 1). The resulting density modulation n^ac is not stated. With typical hBN dielectrics it is of order several 10^10 cm^-2.
   - The imaging point (n = 0.63×10^12 cm^-2, D = -0.961 V/nm) lies close to the high-density edge of the zero-resistance window. In Fig. 1g, at a nearby D, zero resistance ends at about 0.63-0.65×10^12 cm^-2.
   - A modulation of this size may therefore sweep the sample in and out of the superconducting pocket, or into the n > n_DW region, every cycle. The dM/dn signal would then be dominated by the normal-state part of the cycle.
   - In addition, the SOT and tuning fork sit 140 nm above the sample, and Tc is only about 150 mK. Tip-induced heating during the scans has not been excluded.
   - The authors should do the following:
     - quote n^ac (and the accompanying D modulation) for every gate-modulated map;
     - show R_xx recorded simultaneously during the scans of Figs. 1h-k, as they do in Extended Data Fig. 5, to demonstrate zero resistance while imaging;
     - repeat at least one sign-training pair (as in Figs. 1j,k) deeper inside the pocket, at a smaller modulation amplitude.
   - Without this, the statement that the maps show "the CSC phase in the ZR state" is not established.

2. The data show that the superconducting state coexists with, and inherits, the valley polarization of the parent state. They do not show a magnetic response attributable to the superconducting state itself, yet the text calls this "unambiguous thermodynamic evidence for CSC".
   - The SOT measures dM_z/dn of the electrons added by the gate. In a weak-pairing superconductor (Δ ~ 20 μeV), this is essentially the Berry-curvature orbital magnetization of the normal Fermi sea. It would look the same whether or not the pairing is chiral, and whether superconductivity and magnetization coexist microscopically or in phase-separated regions smaller than the ~200 nm resolution (220 nm SOT, 140 nm scan height).
   - The authors themselves say (Discussion) that whether the superconducting order parameter is chiral "requires further investigation". They define CSC to mean any superconductor with macroscopic orbital TRS breaking.
   - Even under that definition, the main text gives no quantitative comparison between the superconducting state and the normal state at the same (n, D). The 1/4M comparison maps (Figs. 1m-p) were taken at n = 1×10^12 cm^-2, where m_z is much larger.
   - The claim of a "ferromagnetic rather than diamagnetic" response also needs an estimate. How large is the expected diamagnetic dM/dn from Meissner or vortex screening, given the Pearl length of this low-density 2D superconductor?
   - The authors should do the following:
     - reconstruct m_z, or at least give the spatially averaged B_z^ac with uncertainties, for Figs. 1h-k;
     - measure the same (n, D) point through Tc (e.g., at 20, 100, 200 and 550 mK), showing whether the signal is continuous across the transition;
     - provide the Meissner estimate;
     - reword the abstract and main text (pp. 1, 3-4, 14). As it stands, the data support "superconductivity emerging within, and inheriting the trainable TRS breaking of, a valley-polarized state". They do not support direct evidence of a chiral superconducting order parameter.

B. Should address

3. In the current-modulated imaging, the domain configurations are inferred rather than imaged, and the "switching between states of opposite chirality" is local and volatile.
   - With I_ac modulation, B_z^ac shows only the regions that change during the ac cycle.
   - The static configurations drawn in Figs. 4j-p, 5e-k and Extended Data Figs. 9g-k are therefore schematics chosen to reproduce the difference maps. One example is the claim that the minority domain encloses contacts 3 and 4 in the HR state of Fig. 5.
   - These static configurations could be imaged directly with gate modulation at fixed I_dc, as was done in Figs. 4a-f. This should be done at least for the LR and HR states of Fig. 5.
   - In Fig. 4e the bubble collapses when I_dc returns to zero. The authors should state whether the Fig. 5 states persist at I_dc = 0 by reading them out with I_ac alone after the dc pulse is removed.
   - What is reconfigured is the position of one DW near a contact, not a global reversal of chirality. The abstract ("reversible switching between states of opposite chirality—a defining CSC property") and the device-functionality framing should be reworded accordingly.

4. The isospin labels depend on the sign of dM_z/dn, which is not shown to be fixed.
   - K-up and K'-down are assigned from the sign of B_z^ac. That sign was calibrated in Fig. 1m at n = 1×10^12 cm^-2 with top-gate modulation.
   - Extended Data Fig. 2d shows that m_z = dM_z/dn changes sign and magnitude strongly with n within the 1/4M phase. Back-gate and top-gate modulation also modulate D differently.
   - One case appears inconsistent. Fig. 4a was taken in D2 after B_int = -150 mT at B = +13 mT (Extended Data Table 1), well inside the hysteresis loop at that D. Yet the majority is labeled K-up, the state that positive initialization produces in Fig. 1.
   - The authors should do the following:
     - show m_z(n) at the D values used for Figs. 2-5;
     - explain the Fig. 4a initialization;
     - confirm the sign convention at each imaging point with a field-reversed or initialization-reversed map.

5. The link between n_DW and the superconducting boundary, and the proposed hidden order, need quantitative support.
   - The correlation rests on magenta marks overlaid on the phase diagrams (Figs. 1d,e; Extended Data Fig. 2a). These come from about four or five D values per device, and the criteria are qualitative: "onset of spike-like hysteresis" and "high-density boundary of the CSC region".
   - The authors should plot n_DW(D) and n_SC(D) together for both devices, with explicit definitions and error bars.
   - They should also track n_DW at temperatures above 1 K, to show whether and where the feature disappears and so give the energy scale of the proposed transition.
   - They should look for independent signatures of a phase transition at n_DW, for example:
     - a kink or step in m_z(n) (Extended Data Fig. 2d);
     - a change in quantum-oscillation frequency or Fermi-surface degeneracy (Extended Data Figs. 1c, 2f);
     - a feature in R_xx(n) at 1 K.
   - The Landau functional (Extended Data Fig. 11) is purely phenomenological, and the authors themselves state that a Lifshitz transition cannot be excluded. The phrase "indicating an underlying transition in the parent state that both induces superconductivity" in the abstract is therefore stronger than the evidence supports.

6. The extraction of ρ_DW = 41 kΩ·μm needs uncertainties and a version for the superconducting state.
   - The value comes from a single COMSOL fit to one HR/LR pair, in the high-D 1/4M phase at n = 0.44×10^12 cm^-2. The text on p. 27 instead quotes n ≈ 0.5×10^12 cm^-2.
   - The fit assumes the following:
     - a uniform wall resistivity;
     - the DW trajectory as read from the difference map of Fig. 2q, at 150 nm scan height with a 220 nm SOT;
     - a homogeneous LR reference state, although Fig. 3d shows small minority bubbles in the LR state;
     - no contact resistance.
   - The authors should report how sensitive ρ_DW is to the DW position near contacts and to contact resistance. They should also fit a second HR configuration, e.g., Fig. 3c or 3g, to check consistency.
   - The Discussion relies on DWs being "highly resistive" inside the superconducting phase, but no value is extracted there. Fitting Fig. 3c with a superconducting or very low-resistivity bulk would show whether the same wall resistivity accounts for the multi-kΩ spikes below Tc.

7. The evidence for linear momentum transfer as the switching mechanism is weak, and some alternatives are not addressed.
   - The two force estimates (1.3×10^-15 and 1×10^-14 N/μm, p. 27) differ by almost an order of magnitude. Both rely on uncertain inputs:
     - nominal currents, which Extended Data Table 1 says overestimate the actual sample current;
     - a single bubble circumference;
     - an assumed M_z.
   - Not discussed are current-induced orbital magnetization or chemical-potential shifts near the contacts (Ref. 66), injection of valley-unpolarized carriers from the metal contacts, which could nucleate minority domains directly, and local Joule heating at the contacts.
   - Extended Data Fig. 4 states that positive and negative I_dc reduce B_co similarly, while Fig. 4f shows no nucleation at I_dc = -45 nA. This needs reconciling with a directional momentum-transfer force.
   - Useful tests would be:
     - thermal SOT imaging of dissipation at the injecting contact (the authors' own Ref. 56 technique);
     - nucleation thresholds compared across contacts with different contact resistance;
     - the threshold current as a function of B∥, which per Extended Data Fig. 10 tunes ρ_DW and hence the reflected-electron force.

8. The in-plane-field evidence for the spin contribution to ρ_DW (Extended Data Fig. 10) comes from one (n, D) point with large scatter between repeated sweeps (open squares in panel c), and the domain configurations were not imaged.
   - A lower peak resistance could equally come from a different domain geometry, from field misalignment (1° at 0.5 T gives ~9 mT out of plane, comparable to the switching fields), or from in-plane-field effects on the superconducting bulk.
   - The authors should do the following:
     - repeat the measurement in the 1/4M normal state;
     - estimate and correct the misalignment;
     - ideally, image an HR state at finite B∥ and convert the spike heights to ρ_DW with the COMSOL model.

9. The negative R_xx is not checked against linear response, and the "effective bias" from the field ramp has no stated mechanism (Extended Data Fig. 9; Methods pp. 31-32).
   - The contact geometry is nonstandard: all contacts are on one edge, and contact 5 lies outside the current path. Together with domains of opposite σ_xy separated by resistive walls, this can give negative four-terminal resistances purely through current redistribution in linear response.
   - The authors should use their COMSOL model to show that the configuration of Extended Data Fig. 9g cannot by itself produce R_xx < 0, before invoking negative differential response.
   - The statement that a magnetic-field ramp produces "an effective bias ... equivalent to a finite dc current" needs a physical mechanism and an estimate of its magnitude. The induced EMF at 50 mT/min over a few μm² is negligible.

10. Basic superconducting characterization is missing for the claims about Josephson coupling and about DW dynamics "in the CSC phase".
    - The Discussion states that in the HR state there is "no detectable Josephson critical current" across the DW, but no dV/dI versus I_dc data are shown.
    - The superconducting critical current is not given. It is therefore unclear whether the nominal I_ac of 5-11 nA used for imaging (Figs. 4i,m; Extended Data Figs. 5, 7, 8) leaves the sample superconducting or drives it resistive. In the latter case, the "CSC-phase" dynamics would partly be normal-state dynamics.
    - The authors should show dV/dI(I_dc) in the zero-resistance, LR and HR states at the imaging points.

11. The novelty claims and comparisons should be tempered.
    - "has no analogue in any other superconductors" (p. 14) and "absent in other superconductors" (abstract) overlook ferromagnetic and magnetic superconductors in which superconductivity coexists with field-switchable magnetic domains, such as UCoGe, URhGe and EuFe2(As,P)2. The distinguishing point here is the orbital, valley origin and the electrical control. That should be stated precisely.
    - "lowest magnetic domain manipulation current reported to date" (p. 12; Extended Data Fig. 8) is quoted alongside the statement that the currents are "comparable to or lower than those in moiré systems". These currents are also nominal values of uncertain magnitude (Extended Data Table 1). Moiré Chern magnets were switched at nA currents (Refs. 35, 36, 46).
    - Phrase these as comparable to the lowest reported values, and give the estimated actual current.

C. Minor

12. "R_xy" is defined as (V5 - V3)/I with contact 5 outside the current path between contacts 4 and 1. This is not a Hall resistance. The box-like loop in Fig. 1l is described as an anomalous Hall effect, but it mixes σ_xx and σ_xy contributions. The authors should rename it, or state explicitly that it is a nonlocal combination interpreted via the COMSOL model.

13. The switching current density of 50 A/cm² (p. 12) is obtained by dividing by the graphene thickness t_g = 1.7 nm. For a 2D conductor the sheet current density, ~0.8 A/m, is the meaningful quantity. Comparisons with bulk spintronic devices in A/cm² are not like-for-like.

14. Inconsistencies between text, captions and table:
    - The caption of Extended Data Fig. 5g-i gives I_ac = 11.5 nA, but Extended Data Table 1 lists 7 nA rms for Extended Data Fig. 5g.
    - The main text (p. 6) cites Extended Data Figs. 1l,m for T = 1 K, but those panels are at 550 mK.
    - The ρ_DW discussion (p. 27) uses n ≈ 0.5×10^12 cm^-2, while the fitted state (Figs. 2m,n) is at 0.44×10^12 cm^-2.
    - Extended Data Fig. 8 is missing from Extended Data Table 1.
    - "divides" should read "devices" (Methods, p. 20).

15. The magnetization reconstruction (Figs. 1n,p; Fig. 4g; Extended Data Fig. 2d) is credited in the Acknowledgements to a "neural network model". The Methods give no description: training data, regularization, assumed tip height and point-spread function, or uncertainty of m_z. These details are needed to judge values such as 9.5 μB per electron.

16. Superconducting imaging (Figs. 1h-k, 3, 4i-p, 5) is shown for device D1 only. The authors should state which of the key results (sign training in the zero-resistance state, LR/HR domain configurations, sub-nA DW motion) have been reproduced in D2 or after thermal cycling.

17. Each pixel averages about 300 ac cycles, so the stochastic DW dynamics bias the map amplitudes (Extended Data Figs. 5, 7). A brief statement of how this affects the inferred DW excursion ranges would help readers interpret Figs. 4-5 quantitatively.

18. Data and code are available only "on request". Depositing the imaging and transport data would be appropriate for a paper whose claims rest on image interpretation.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one key figure: a sign-training pair of B_z^ac (or m_z) maps at a single (n, D) deep in the superconducting pocket, with n^ac stated, R_xx recorded simultaneously showing zero resistance, and the same maps repeated above Tc. Plot the spatially averaged signal against temperature through Tc.
2. Image the static domain configurations, using gate modulation at fixed I_dc, for the LR and HR states of Fig. 5. Show the state read out after the dc current is removed, to establish whether the switching is nonvolatile.
3. Present n_DW(D) and the superconducting boundary n_SC(D) as a single quantitative plot for both devices, with error bars and its temperature evolution. Add any independent thermodynamic or quantum-oscillation signature at n_DW.
4. Use the existing COMSOL framework more widely:
   - error bars on ρ_DW;
   - a fit of a superconducting-phase HR configuration;
   - a linear-response test of the negative-R_xx configuration;
   - conversion of the B∥ data into ρ_DW(B∥).
