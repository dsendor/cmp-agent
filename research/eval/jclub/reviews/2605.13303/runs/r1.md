Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use scanning SQUID-on-tip magnetometry, together with transport, to image isospin-polarized domains in rhombohedral pentalayer graphene across the quarter-metal (1/4M) and superconducting regimes. They report four results. First, a trainable, sign-reversible magnetization inside the zero-resistance state. Second, a density n_DW(D) at which the field hysteresis changes from box-like to spike-like; it follows the high-density edge of the superconducting pocket and appears both below and above Tc. Third, imaging that attributes the low-resistance (LR), high-resistance (HR) and negative-resistance states to domain configurations, with a domain-wall resistivity of about 41 kΩ·µm. Fourth, domain-wall motion and resistance switching driven by currents of a few nA or less. Local magnetic imaging inside the superconducting state of rhombohedral graphene is new and important. Explaining the much-discussed resistance spikes and negative R_xx as domain-wall effects would be a real advance. The strongest claims are the "chiral superconductivity" label, the hidden transition, and the switching between states of opposite chirality. These rest on weaker evidence than the imaging itself.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- This is the first real-space magnetic imaging of the superconducting regime of rhombohedral graphene. In Fig. 1j,k the sign of B_z^ac is set by the initialization field B_int while the measurement field (B = 20 mT) is the same. This is a good control against field-odd artifacts such as modulated diamagnetic screening.
- The 1/4M imaging (Fig. 1m-p) is clean. It gives a quantitative m_z of about 9.5 µB per electron, uniform over each branch of the hysteresis loop.
- In the 1/4M phase, one image ties a specific domain-wall trajectory (Fig. 2o-q) to a specific transport state (Fig. 2m,n). The COMSOL model then gives a first estimate of ρ_DW.
- The spike-like hysteresis changes at the same n_DW(D) at 20 mK, 550 mK and 1 K (Fig. 2i-l; Extended Data Figs. 1, 2). It is seen in two devices and is a striking empirical finding.
- Current-driven domain dynamics are imaged in detail: the dc bubble in Fig. 4a-f, the unipolar and bipolar ac regimes in Fig. 4i-p, and the amplitude series in Extended Data Fig. 8. Reversible LR/HR switching (Fig. 5a-c) is linked to imaged configurations (Fig. 5d-k). Together these give a concrete, testable picture of the HR, LR and negative-resistance states.
- The Methods address heating during field ramps (point 7) with several sensible arguments.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The evidence shows superconductivity coexisting with an inherited orbital magnetization. It does not show a chiral superconducting state, and the signal in the superconducting phase is not quantified.**
   - **What the paper shows.** The main evidence for "CSC" (abstract; p. 3-4, "unambiguous thermodynamic evidence for CSC in RMG") is Fig. 1h-k. These are gate-modulated B_z^ac maps on a ±25 nT scale. To the eye they show a uniform tint of a few nT on streaky noise.
   - **What is missing.** The text gives no average signal, noise floor, or reconstructed m_z for these panels.
   - **Why it matters.**
     - The authors themselves say the order parameter's chirality is not established (Discussion, last sentences of p. 14), and they define CSC as any superconducting state with orbital TRS breaking (p. 2).
     - The same data are equally consistent with an itinerant orbital ferromagnet that coexists with a superconducting condensate, where the condensate need not take part in the TRS breaking.
     - Ferromagnetic superconductors (uranium-based and Eu-based compounds) also host bistable, trainable magnetization and magnetic domains inside the superconducting state. The statements that this "has no analogue in any other superconductors" (p. 14) and is "absent in other superconductors" (abstract, p. 12) are therefore not accurate as written.
   - **What the authors should do.**
     - (i) Quantify the Fig. 1h-k signal: the area-averaged B_z^ac with uncertainty, and the reconstructed m_z in µB per electron.
     - (ii) Show how m_z at the Fig. 1h imaging point (n = 0.63×10^12 cm^-2, D = -0.961 V/nm) changes with temperature through Tc, and with n across the edge of the superconducting pocket. This would show whether superconductivity changes the magnetization at all.
     - (iii) Show R_xx at the exact imaging (n, D, B) point. Fig. 1g is at D = -0.935 V/nm, not at the imaging point.
     - (iv) Restate the headline claim as macroscopic orbital TRS breaking coexisting with, and inherited by, the superconducting state. Drop "unambiguous evidence for CSC" and "absent in other superconductors" unless (ii) shows that superconductivity itself is involved.

2. **The hidden symmetry-breaking transition at n_DW is inferred only from a change in hysteresis shape, and a simpler explanation is not ruled out.**
   - **What the paper claims.** The abstract and pp. 9-10 and 14 state that n_DW marks a transition that "both induces superconductivity and reduces domain-wall energy". The only evidence is the change from box-like to spike-like hysteresis and the falling coercive field near n_DW (Fig. 2i-l; Extended Data Figs. 1, 2).
   - **The simpler explanation.**
     - Extended Data Fig. 2d shows that m_z in the 1/4M phase falls steeply with decreasing n.
     - The Zeeman driving force 2M_zB and the bulk anisotropy that keeps single domains stable shrink with M_z. Disorder and edge nucleation could then produce multi-domain states below some density without any new order.
     - The disorder field b(x) in the Landau model (Methods) already contains this. The line tension J_DW ∝ gξ_φ falls as the condensation energy g of the isospin order weakens, with no Ψ needed.
     - The authors acknowledge a Lifshitz-transition alternative (p. 34) but do not test either alternative.
   - **What the authors should do.**
     - (i) Look for an independent thermodynamic or transport signature at n_DW(D) above Tc: a kink or step in m_z(n) from SOT (as in Extended Data Fig. 2d, but at several D crossing n_DW), a feature in R_xx(n) or its derivative, or a change in quantum-oscillation frequency or degeneracy (Extended Data Figs. 1b,c, 2e-h).
     - (ii) Map n_DW against temperature. A true phase boundary should bend and end at some T*, whereas a disorder- or M_z-controlled crossover need not. Currently n_DW is reported as the same at 20 mK, 550 mK and 1 K.
     - (iii) Compare n_DW(D) quantitatively with the measured n-dependence of M_z and B_co, to test whether one critical value of M_zB_co accounts for the boundary.
     - (iv) Plot n_DW(D) and the superconducting-pocket boundary n_SC(D) together, with error bars and a stated criterion for each, in both devices. At present, "closely tracks" rests on magenta squares placed on colour maps.
     - (v) If C3 breaking is proposed, note that all contacts lie on one edge, so anisotropy cannot be tested. A device with contacts at several angles would be the direct test.
     - Without such evidence, the abstract should say "coincides with" rather than "indicating an underlying transition".

B. Should address

3. **"Reversible switching between states of opposite chirality" (abstract, p. 12) overstates what Figs. 4 and 5 show.**
   - In Fig. 5 the majority isospin (K'↓) never changes. The dc current moves the wall of a minority K↑ bubble near contact 4, so that contacts 3 and 4 fall inside or outside it (Fig. 5e-k). The same applies to Fig. 4.
   - This is local domain-wall positioning, not a switch of the macroscopic chirality of the superconducting state.
   - The only global chirality reversal shown is done with field training (Fig. 1j,k).
   - The authors should either show current-driven reversal of the net magnetization of the whole sample, imaged before and after, or reword the claim as current-controlled positioning of chiral domain walls.

4. **The momentum-transfer force estimate counts only the transmitted current, so the "order-of-magnitude agreement" with f_pin is not robust.**
   - **The estimate as given.** In the Methods (p. 27), f_DW ≈ 2ħk_F J_⊥/e uses the net (transmitted) current density J_⊥ ≈ 4 nA/µm.
   - **Why it is too small.** For a wall with transmission β ≈ 8×10^-3, the incident and reflected fluxes exceed the transmitted flux by about 1/β. The net force on a wall is better estimated from the electrochemical potential drop across it: f ≈ n e ρ_DW J_⊥.
   - **The numbers.**
     - ρ_DW J_⊥ ≈ 41 kΩ·µm × 4 nA/µm ≈ 160 µV.
     - With n ≈ 0.5×10^12 cm^-2, f ≈ 1×10^-13 N/µm. This is about 100 times their f_DW and about 10 times the Zeeman-based f_pin ≈ 1×10^-14 N/µm.
   - **Other weak points.**
     - f_pin = 2M_zB_co uses the differential m_z ≈ 3 µB/e as if it were the magnetization per carrier, M_z/n. It should use the integrated M_z = ∫m_z dn.
     - The claim of a "first realization of DW motion dominated by linear momentum transfer" (p. 26) needs to exclude other mechanisms. These include current-induced orbital-magnetization or edge-chemical-potential mechanisms proposed for Chern magnets (refs. 46, 66) and local Joule heating at the injecting contact.
   - **What the authors should do.**
     - Redo the force estimate and state which force is meant.
     - Use the integrated M_z.
     - Test the predictions that tell the mechanisms apart: how the threshold current depends on B and on which isospin is the majority, and whether the polarity asymmetry follows the electron-flow direction when the source and drain contacts are swapped.

5. **The ac current amplitudes are nominal and the filter transfer function is unknown. This affects several quantitative claims.**
   - **The problem.** Extended Data Table 1 says the real ac current is "significantly lower" than the quoted value, because of ~1 kHz low-pass filters, and imaging is done at 0.43-2.1 kHz. The Methods say the filters are on "all electrical lines". If this includes the gate lines, the gate modulation δn at 1.18-2.13 kHz is also attenuated.
   - **Claims affected.**
     - (a) Manipulation at 0.4 nA, described as "the lowest reported to date" (Extended Data Fig. 8g; p. 12).
     - (b) The roughly fourfold larger current needed at 20 mK than at 550 mK (6.55 vs 1.63 nA, p. 27-28), used as evidence for extra superconducting pinning. Here the opposite field polarities differ (B = -20 vs +19.5 mT). More importantly, the current division into the sample depends on the sample impedance, which differs between the superconducting and normal states.
     - (c) The interpretation of Extended Data Fig. 7, where the B_z^ac amplitude falls from 633 Hz to 1533 Hz. The paper attributes this to stochastic dynamics, but it could simply be filter roll-off.
     - (d) The absolute m_z values (9.5 µB/e, Fig. 1n; Extended Data Fig. 2d), if the gate modulation is filtered.
   - **What the authors should do.**
     - Measure or model the transfer function of the current and gate lines at each frequency used.
     - Quote actual sample currents and δn.
     - Repeat the frequency series of Extended Data Fig. 7 with the current amplitude corrected for the filter.

6. **In the multi-domain gate-modulated images (Figs. 2o-q, 3c,d,g,h), reading domain polarity directly from the sign of B_z^ac needs support.**
   - **Problems.**
     - These maps have low contrast and are streaky, especially Fig. 3c,d,g,h on a ±25 nT scale. The minority domains "spanning multiple contacts" are hard to separate from scan-line noise.
     - B_z^ac measures dM_z/dn, not M_z. Its sign can differ from that of M_z where M_z(n) is non-monotonic (Extended Data Fig. 2d shows a sign change at higher n).
     - The domain walls move at sub-nA currents. A gate modulation of 0.15-0.2 V_rms may also move walls. Near a wall, the signal would then be dominated by ±2M_z switching, not by dM_z/dn.
     - Extended Data Fig. 5 shows domains reconfiguring during a single scan.
   - **What the authors should do.**
     - Provide reconstructed m_z maps for the multi-domain states.
     - Show repeated scans of the same state, or forward/backward scan pairs, to show the configuration is stable during acquisition.
     - Show line cuts with the noise level.
     - Check, by comparing gate-modulation amplitudes, that gate modulation does not drive wall motion.

7. **The ρ_DW extraction has no uncertainty, rests on one state, and is not checked in the superconducting phase.**
   - **How it is obtained now.** ρ_DW = 41 kΩ·µm comes from one fit to two numbers (R_xx and R_xy at the blue star, Fig. 2m,n). The wall path is taken from the noisy difference map in Fig. 2q, and σ_xy is assumed to reverse in the minority domain.
   - **What is missing.**
     - No uncertainty is given.
     - The sensitivity of the fit to the wall path, especially near contacts 1-4, is not explored.
     - Contact resistance is not included.
   - **The R_xy definition.** All five contacts lie on one edge, so R_xy = (V_5 - V_3)/I is not a Hall resistance. It mixes longitudinal and Hall responses, and the "AHE" interpretation of Fig. 1l and Fig. 2a should be stated with that caveat.
   - **No check below Tc.** The ρ_DW picture is never tested in the superconducting phase, where the bulk resistivity is zero. For example: does the same ρ_DW, with the wall paths in Fig. 3c or Fig. 5h-j, reproduce the HR values R_xx ≈ 2.7 kΩ (Fig. 5b) and the HR values of several kΩ up to ~25 kΩ (Extended Data Fig. 3)?
   - **What the authors should do.**
     - Give a confidence interval for ρ_DW from varying the wall path.
     - Fit at least one more state, ideally including one in the superconducting phase.
     - Explain what R_xy measures in this geometry.

8. **The absence of a Josephson coupling across the walls is stated without data, and does not distinguish pairing states.**
   - The Discussion (p. 14) says that HR walls show "no detectable Josephson critical current". It reads this as "consistent with superconducting order parameters that differ ... in phase or chirality". No I-V or dV/dI data are shown.
   - With β ≈ 10^-2 and a spin flip required at the wall (p. 8, Methods), little or no supercurrent is expected for any spin-polarized pairing state. Its absence therefore says nothing about chirality.
   - The authors should show dV/dI versus I_dc in an HR state at base temperature, and estimate the critical current expected from β for an ordinary junction. Otherwise the inference about the order parameter's internal structure should be removed.

9. **The in-plane-field reading of the spin contribution to ρ_DW (Extended Data Fig. 10) is not unique.**
   - **What is measured.** The peak R_xx^max is a stochastic quantity that depends on how long the wall is and where it lies relative to the contacts (Extended Data Figs. 3, 5).
   - **Other effects of the in-plane field.** It also changes the coercive fields and the superconductivity itself.
   - **The scale problem.** The saturation at |B_∥| ≳ 0.1 T corresponds to a Zeeman energy of about 10 µeV. With spin-valley locking, which the paper invokes on pp. 2 and 5, it is not clear such a small field can tilt the spins appreciably. The text does not discuss this.
   - **What the authors should do.**
     - Image the domain configuration at finite B_∥, to show the wall geometry is unchanged.
     - Give an estimate of the spin canting expected at 0.1 T.
     - Otherwise present the spin-mismatch interpretation as tentative.

10. **The negative-R_xx section rests on an incorrect premise.**
    - The Methods (p. 31) say a negative R_xx at zero bias "would appear to violate local thermodynamic equilibrium". They therefore invoke an "effective bias generated by the ramping magnetic field".
    - A negative four-terminal resistance is allowed in linear response. It occurs readily when non-local current paths combine with regions of opposite σ_xy, which is exactly the domain structure proposed here.
    - The authors should use their COMSOL model, with oppositely signed σ_xy domains and resistive walls, to check whether the field-sweep negative R_xx of Extended Data Fig. 9a arises in linear response from plausible wall configurations. The effective-bias explanation should be kept only if it does not.

11. **The imaging conditions in Fig. 5 differ from the transport conditions they are meant to explain.**
    - The transport in Fig. 5b,c uses I_ac = 1 nA. The images in Fig. 5d,h use a 2 nA_rms square wave at 433 Hz (Extended Data Table 1), with I_dc = ±1.7 nA.
    - For a square wave, peak equals rms, so the instantaneous current swings from -3.7 to +0.3 nA in Fig. 5d. The current changes sign during each cycle, rather than merely being "reduced in magnitude" as in the text and Fig. 5e,f.
    - Because walls respond at sub-nA currents, the imaged configuration may not be the one present during the transport measurement.
    - The authors should image with |I_ac| < |I_dc|, or show that the LR/HR configuration is the same at both probe amplitudes.

12. **The reproducibility of the central imaging results is limited.**
    - The trainable magnetization in the zero-resistance state (Fig. 1h-k) is shown only in device D1, and each sign only once.
    - The 1/4M dc-current imaging is shown only in device D2.
    - Given the stochastic behaviour the authors document (Extended Data Figs. 3v,w, 5), the following should be stated:
      - how many training cycles were imaged, and how many times the sign followed B_int;
      - whether Fig. 1j,k-type training was repeated after thermal cycling;
      - whether it was seen in D2.

C. Minor

13. **R_xy sign in Fig. 5.** The text (p. 12) and the Fig. 5 caption give R_xy ≈ +500 Ω in the LR state, but the LR plateau in Fig. 5c is at about -0.5 kΩ.

14. **Coercive field in Fig. 1l.** The text gives B_co ≈ ±150 mT, but the switching fields in the figure appear to be about -120 and +130 mT.

15. **Current-density comparison (p. 12).** The value J ≈ 50 A/cm² divides by an assumed thickness t_g = 1.7 nm to compare with bulk spintronic devices. For a 2D conductor, a sheet current density (A/m) is the more meaningful figure. Comparisons should also be made at similar device widths.

16. **Superconducting pinning estimate (p. 29).** The condensation energy density times the wall width gives a line tension, an energy per length. Turning it into a force per length needs a length scale, which is not stated. The comparison "f_pin ≲ 3×10^-16 N/µm vs 1.3×10^-15 N/µm" should be redone with explicit dimensions.

17. **Domain-wall width (p. 27).** The statement that the wall width is "a few lattice constants" because the valley order parameter must vary on the lattice scale needs justification. Domain walls between valley-polarized states can be wider and partly intervalley-coherent (cf. ref. 59), which would change the adiabaticity argument.

18. **Misalignment with hBN.** The devices are "intentionally misaligned" from hBN (p. 3). State how the alignment angle was checked, for example by the absence of moiré features at high density.

19. **Typos and wording.** "to the divides" (p. 20); "dependance" (Fig. 1g caption); "Extended Data Figs. 9l shows" (p. 31). Data and code are available "on request". Depositing the SOT maps and the COMSOL model would help others reproduce the ρ_DW and m_z extractions.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one figure following a single (n, D) point inside the superconducting pocket from 20 mK to above Tc, showing m_z from SOT, R_xx and the hysteresis shape side by side. This would show directly whether superconductivity changes the magnetization, the domain-wall pinning, or both. That is the evidence the "chiral superconductivity" label needs.
2. Measure m_z(n) by SOT along several D cuts crossing n_DW, above Tc, to look for a thermodynamic signature of the proposed hidden transition. Compare it with the alternative where M_z falls and the coercive field softens.
3. Calibrate the actual sample currents and gate modulations at the imaging frequencies. Then restate the threshold-current, frequency-dependence and 20 mK vs 550 mK comparisons in calibrated units.
4. Extend the COMSOL modelling to the superconducting HR, LR and negative-R_xx configurations, using the imaged wall paths. Getting the resistances observed in Figs. 3 and 5 and Extended Data Fig. 9 from one ρ_DW would strongly support the domain-wall picture of transport in this phase.
