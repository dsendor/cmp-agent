A. Must address

1. **The data show orbital TRS breaking that coexists with superconductivity and is inherited from the parent state. They do not show chiral superconductivity, yet the abstract and pp. 3-4 call Fig. 1h-k "unambiguous thermodynamic evidence for CSC".** Page 2 defines CSC as any superconducting state with macroscopic TRS breaking of orbital origin. Superconductivity here emerges from a 1/4M parent already known to be orbitally ferromagnetic (refs 3, 14, 16), so any superconductor in this pocket meets the definition automatically. The manuscript itself says the TRS breaking is "inherited from the parent 1/4M phase" (p. 5), and the Discussion (p. 14) concedes that whether the order parameter is chiral "requires further investigation". The SOT measures dM_z/dn of the electrons added by the gate. In a weak-pairing superconductor (Δ ~ 20 µeV) this is essentially the Berry-curvature orbital magnetization of the normal Fermi sea. It would look the same whether or not the pairing is chiral, and whether superconductivity and magnetization coexist microscopically or in phase-separated regions smaller than the ~200 nm resolution (220 nm SOT, 140 nm scan height). The data are equally consistent with an itinerant orbital ferromagnet coexisting with a condensate that takes no part in the TRS breaking. No quantitative comparison is made between the superconducting and normal states at the same (n, D); the 1/4M comparison maps (Fig. 1m-p) are at n = 1×10^12 cm^-2, where m_z is much larger. Several comparisons are also inaccurate. On p. 3 the signal is presented as "long sought but thus far absent in candidate chiral superconductors [40,41]", but the Sr2RuO4 searches looked for magnetization generated by the condensate itself (edge or domain-wall supercurrents), not for a ferromagnetic normal-state background. The statements that the coexistence of superconductivity with oppositely polarized domains "has no analogue in any other superconductors" (p. 14) and is "absent in other superconductors" (abstract, p. 12) overlook ferromagnetic superconductors such as UCoGe, URhGe and EuFe2(As1-xPx)2, which host bistable, field-switchable magnetic domains inside the superconducting state. What distinguishes this system is the orbital, valley origin and the electrical control. The authors should measure B_z^ac or m_z at the Fig. 1h point (n = 0.63×10^12 cm^-2, D = -0.961 V/nm), with fixed training history, from 20 mK through Tc to about 0.5-1 K (e.g. 20, 100, 200, 550 mK), and as a function of n across the edge of the superconducting pocket. A change at Tc would be a signature of the condensate; continuity would show the signal belongs to the normal state. Unless this shows the condensate is involved, the title, abstract and main text (pp. 1, 3-4, 12, 14) should be reworded to macroscopic orbital TRS breaking in the superconducting regime that is inherited from the isospin order, and the "unambiguous", "long sought" and "no analogue"/"absent in other superconductors" statements should be removed or made precise.

2. **The zero-resistance imaging in Fig. 1h-k is not quantified, and field-odd contributions are excluded only by one training pair at one point in one device.** The maps are gate-modulated B_z^ac on a ±25 nT scale and show a uniform tint of a few nT on streaky noise. The authors should give the area-averaged B_z^ac with uncertainty and the reconstructed m_z in µB per electron for Fig. 1h-k, provide the Meissner and vortex estimates, and show the training dependence at several fields including B ≈ 0, at more than one (n, D) point including one deeper inside the pocket, and in D2.

3. **It is not shown that the sample is superconducting throughout the gate-modulated imaging of Fig. 1h-k.** The signal is generated by a 0.1 V_rms back-gate modulation (Extended Data Table 1). The resulting n^ac and D^ac are not stated; with typical hBN dielectrics n^ac is of order several 10^10 cm^-2. The imaging point lies close to the high-density edge of the zero-resistance window: in Fig. 1g, taken at a nearby but different D (-0.935 V/nm), zero resistance ends at about 0.63-0.65×10^12 cm^-2. The modulation may therefore sweep the sample in and out of the superconducting pocket, or across n_DW, every cycle, and the dM/dn signal would then be dominated by the normal-state part of the cycle. In addition, Tc is only about 150 mK and the SOT and tuning fork sit 140 nm above the sample, so tip heating has not been excluded, and Extended Data Fig. 3v,w shows spontaneous ZR-to-LR transitions. The authors should quote n^ac and D^ac for every gate-modulated map, show R_xx at the exact imaging (n, D, B) point recorded simultaneously during the scans (as in Extended Data Fig. 5) or immediately before and after each map with the tip at scan height, and repeat at least one sign-training pair deeper inside the pocket at a smaller modulation amplitude. Without this, the statement that the maps show "the CSC phase in the ZR state" is not established.

4. **The "hidden symmetry-breaking transition" at n_DW(D), and its coincidence with the superconducting boundary, are inferred only from a change in hysteresis shape; simpler explanations are not ruled out.** The abstract and pp. 9-10, 14 state that n_DW marks a transition that "both induces superconductivity and reduces domain-wall energy". The only evidence is the change from box-like to spike-like hysteresis and the falling coercive field near n_DW (Fig. 2i-l; Extended Data Figs. 1, 2). The coincidence with the pocket rests on magenta squares overlaid on colour maps (Fig. 1d,e; Extended Data Fig. 2a) from about four or five D values per device, with qualitative criteria ("onset of spike-like hysteresis", "high-density boundary of the CSC region"). Spike-like HR hysteresis also appears in the high-|D| 1/4M phase where there is no superconductivity at 20 mK (yellow asterisk, Fig. 2m,n; Fig. 4a-f; Extended Data Fig. 3a-d), which weakens the one-to-one correspondence. A simpler, Imry-Ma-type explanation is available: Extended Data Fig. 2d shows m_z in the 1/4M phase falling steeply with decreasing n, so the Zeeman driving force 2M_zB and the anisotropy that stabilizes single domains shrink, and disorder and edge nucleation could produce multi-domain states below some density without any new order. The disorder field b(x) in the Landau model already contains this, and J_DW ∝ gξ_φ falls as the isospin condensation energy g weakens, with no Ψ needed. The Landau functional (Extended Data Fig. 11, λ = 0.7g) is phenomenological with parameters not tied to measurement, and the authors acknowledge that a Lifshitz transition cannot be excluded (p. 34) but test neither alternative. The authors should: (i) plot n_DW(D) and n_SC(D) together, with stated criteria and error bars, for both devices, including where the n_DW line runs outside the superconducting D range; (ii) look for an independent signature at n_DW above Tc, such as a kink or step in m_z(n) from SOT along several D cuts crossing n_DW, a feature in R_xx(n) or its derivative at 1 K, a compressibility feature, or a change in quantum-oscillation frequency or degeneracy (Extended Data Figs. 1b,c, 2e-h); (iii) track n_DW above 1 K to see whether and where it bends and ends, since a true phase boundary should, while a disorder- or M_z-controlled crossover need not (it is currently the same at 20 mK, 550 mK and 1 K); (iv) compare n_DW(D) quantitatively with the measured M_z(n) and B_co(n) to test whether one critical value of M_zB_co accounts for the boundary. If C3-breaking nematic order is proposed (p. 34), they should note that all contacts lie on one edge, so anisotropy cannot be tested here, and state what measurement would test it (a device with contacts at several angles, angle-resolved transport, in-plane anomalous Hall). Without such evidence, the abstract should say "coincides with" rather than "indicating an underlying transition", and present the hidden order as one hypothesis among several.

7. **The quantitative case for linear-momentum-transfer domain-wall motion is weak, and alternative mechanisms are not excluded (Methods pp. 26-29).** The force estimate f_DW ≈ 2ħk_F J_⊥/e uses only the net transmitted current density J_⊥ ≈ 4 nA/µm. For a wall with transmission β ≈ 8×10^-3, incident and reflected fluxes exceed the transmitted flux by about 1/β; the net force is better estimated from the electrochemical potential drop, f ≈ n e ρ_DW J_⊥. With ρ_DW J_⊥ ≈ 41 kΩ·µm × 4 nA/µm ≈ 160 µV and n ≈ 0.5×10^12 cm^-2, f ≈ 1×10^-13 N/µm, about 100 times their f_DW and 10 times f_pin. The two "independent" pinning estimates (1.3×10^-15 and 1×10^-14 N/µm) differ by about 8x. The second, f_pin = 2M_zB_co, uses the differential m_z ≈ 3 µB/e (Fig. 4g) as if it were M_z/n instead of the integrated M_z = ∫m_z dn, and uses B_co ≈ 50 mT, whereas Extended Data Fig. 4 at D2 shows B_co ≈ 150 mT at I_dc = 0. Both rely on nominal currents and a single bubble circumference. The superconducting condensation contribution, f_pin ≲ 3×10^-16 N/µm, is an order of magnitude below the normal-state pinning estimate, yet is invoked to explain the fourfold increase in required I_ac below Tc. Extended Data Fig. 4 states that positive and negative I_dc reduce B_co similarly, while Fig. 4f shows no nucleation at I_dc = -45 nA; this needs reconciling with a directional force. The claim of a "first realization of DW motion dominated by linear momentum transfer" (p. 26) requires excluding current-induced orbital-magnetization reversal or edge electrochemical-potential and density shifts near contacts proposed for Chern magnets (refs 46, 66), injection of valley-unpolarized carriers from metal contacts nucleating minority domains, and local Joule heating at the injecting contact. The authors should redo the force estimate stating which force is meant, use the integrated M_z and consistent B_co, and test discriminating predictions: threshold current versus B, k_F, ρ_DW (e.g. via B∥, Extended Data Fig. 10) and majority isospin (compare Fig. 4 with Fig. 5); whether the polarity asymmetry follows electron flow when source and drain are swapped; nucleation thresholds across contacts with different contact resistance; and thermal SOT imaging of dissipation at the injecting contact (ref. 56).
