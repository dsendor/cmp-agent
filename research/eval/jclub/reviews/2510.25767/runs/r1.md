Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

This Perspective argues that a disorder-free overdoped hole-doped cuprate would be a weak-coupling (BCS-like) d-wave superconductor built on a Fermi-liquid normal state, with T_theta >> T_c ≈ Delta_0/2. It attributes the main contrary observations to substitutional disorder and mesoscopic inhomogeneity: the superfluid-stiffness "boomerang", low-T T-linear resistivity, large residual C/T, gaps persisting above T_c, and Nernst/diamagnetic fluctuation signals. The paper also inverts the BCS gap equation on Bi2212 ARPES data to infer a short-range pairing interaction of magnetic magnitude, and it lists falsifiable predictions for cleaner samples. The organizing viewpoint is clear and useful, and it sharpens a long-running debate (disorder versus intrinsic two-fluid or quantum-critical physics). In its current form, however, the central attribution to disorder rests mostly on qualitative argument rather than quantitative comparison with the data.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- It gives a clear, testable alternative to two-fluid and quantum-critical scenarios for the overdoped regime. It states explicit predictions (Experimental Tests section): T_c(p) becomes rounded with an exponential tail; T_theta/T_c grows; Delta_0 → 2.14 k_B T_c; the residual C/T and residual Drude weight shrink; the T-linear resistivity term vanishes in cleaner samples. It also names a concrete test material (pressure-doped YBa2Cu3O7).
- It brings together evidence from many techniques and families (quantum oscillations in Tl2201, penetration depth in YBCO and Ca-YBCO, NQR linewidths, STM and ARPES gaps in Bi2212, specific heat in LSCO) under one organizing hypothesis.
- The observation that YBCO, the family with the least intrinsic substitutional disorder, has no boomerang in T_theta (Fig. 2) while Ca-YBCO does is a striking and well-chosen piece of evidence.
- The real-space argument (Eqs. 4–7) that the k-dependence of the gap reflects the range of the effective interaction is a useful framing for experiments.
- The order-of-magnitude consistency between the inferred pairing interaction (Ṽ ≈ 0.6 eV, J ≈ 0.15 eV) and the parent-compound exchange is suggestive.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The attribution of every non-BCS/non-Fermi-liquid feature to disorder is not quantitative, so the central conclusion cannot yet be told apart from intrinsic alternatives. The sections "Reconciling results on the Fermi-liquid ground state" (p. 7), "Evidence that large phase fluctuations are extrinsic" (p. 8) and "Concerning the non-mean-field T-dependence of the gap" (p. 9) each show that a disorder mechanism could produce the anomaly. None shows that the measured size of the anomaly follows from the measured amount of disorder. As written, the hypothesis can absorb any discrepancy.

   The paper already cites the inputs needed for a quantitative test:
   - NQR hole-density spreads (refs 71–73);
   - Dingle scattering rates from quantum oscillations (ħ/τ = 2.4 meV in Tl2201);
   - residual resistivities;
   - irradiation series (refs 87, 88).

   The authors should do at least one of the following:
   - (a) For one material with a known disorder metric, estimate with the puddle or dirty-d-wave model they favour the expected suppression of T_theta, residual C/T, and size of the T-linear coefficient A_1. Then compare with experiment.
   - (b) Compile existing data at fixed p across samples or families of different disorder, for example A_1 against residual resistivity or NQR linewidth, residual C/T against linewidth, and T_theta/T_c against Dingle temperature. Show the monotonic trends the hypothesis requires.

   Without such a comparison, the statement in the abstract that the inconsistent features "can in fact be attributed to the expected effects of material disorder" is a conjecture, not a conclusion drawn from the evidence reviewed.

2. Fig. 2, the main positive evidence that T_theta >> T_c in the cleanest overdoped material, has an unaddressed chain contribution and an inconsistent normalization.
   - Several teal points near p ≈ 0.18–0.19 (about 330–450 K) lie above the "1 + p" line. That line is the stiffness of all carriers per planar Cu with m* = 5 m_e. Taken at face value, the measured superfluid stiffness exceeds that of every carrier in the planes.
   - The likely causes are not discussed:
     - the CuO chains in YBa2Cu3O7 superconduct and contribute strongly to λ_b (the paper cites ref. 59, "Evidence for chain superconductivity", in another context);
     - m* = 5 m_e is taken from Tl2201, not measured for overdoped YBCO.
   - The 50% rise in T_theta from p = 0.16 to 0.19 (p. 7) could therefore come partly or mostly from chain filling as O7 is approached, not from planar stiffness.
   - The authors should separate planar from chain stiffness, for example by showing λ_a alone, which is chain-free, as its own data set with its own p dependence. They should justify or replace m* = 5 m_e for YBCO and explain why points exceed the 1 + p bound. If T_theta from λ_a alone also rises across p = 0.16–0.19, the argument stands. If it does not, the contrast with Ca-YBCO and LSCO weakens considerably.

3. In Tl2201, coherent quantum oscillations coexist with strong inhomogeneity, and the paper does not reconcile the two in the same material. The paper uses Tl2201 as its Fermi-liquid exemplar:
   - quantum oscillations for p > 0.27;
   - ħ/τ = 2.4 meV;
   - no deviation from Lifshitz–Kosevich behaviour.

   Yet it also invokes, for the same family:
   - strong extrinsic phase fluctuations (refs 63–65);
   - a specific-heat jump at T_c only 10% of the residual C/T (p. 8);
   - T-linear resistivity from scattering off superconducting puddles (p. 7);
   - inhomogeneity on a scale of a few hundred Å (ref. 70).

   Missing step: the paper never checks whether a Dingle rate of 2.4 meV (an upper bound on the transport rate, as the paper notes) and a quasiparticle mean free path much longer than ξ_0 fit with a large non-superconducting volume fraction and puddle-dominated transport. The puddle scenario sets the disorder correlation length at L_dis ~ p^{-1/2} lattice constants. It is not shown how a disorder landscape strong enough to leave most of the specific-heat weight uncondensed still permits coherent cyclotron orbits.

   Requested: estimate the Dingle damping expected from the hole-density variance that the puddle picture needs, and compare it with the measured damping. Discuss whether the quantum-oscillation and specific-heat samples are at comparable p and disorder. This is the one family where both sides of the argument are measured, so it is the natural quantitative test.

B. Should address

4. The inversion of the gap equation in Fig. 3 is under-specified, and it is applied where the paper itself says BCS does not hold.
   - Eq. 7 is inverted "without introducing any additional high-energy cutoff (F = 1)". With a fixed renormalized dispersion (t = 166 meV), the inferred Ṽ depends logarithmically on the effective bandwidth, so its absolute value (0.6 eV) and the derived J ≈ 0.15 eV depend on that choice.
   - The paper should state:
     - whether μ is recomputed at each p from the Luttinger count;
     - whether t, t′ and t_⊥ are held fixed with doping;
     - how the bilayer bands are averaged;
     - whether Δ_0 is the T → 0 antinodal gap or the gap at the measurement temperature.
   - For p ≲ 0.2 the measured Δ_0 is about twice 2.14 k_B T_c in Fig. 3. Using that Δ_0 in a BCS inversion gives a Ṽ whose meaning is unclear, yet these points set the slope of the "smooth but nonetheless pronounced decrease" used to argue for a magnetic origin (p. 11).
   - It is unclear which points the red line is fitted to, and why the purple Δ_extrap points track 2.14 k_B T_c (not Δ_0) below p ≈ 0.24.
   - The authors should give the procedure, show how sensitive Ṽ is to the cutoff and to the dispersion parameters, and restrict the fit to the range where Δ_0 ≈ 2.14 k_B T_c.
   - The van Hove crossing of the antibonding band near p ≈ 0.22 in Bi2212 should be discussed. It strongly changes the density of states over exactly the range where Ṽ is extrapolated to its sign change.

5. The predicted exponential T_c tail rests on an assumption. The prediction (Eq. 8, Fig. 3 inset, first bullet on p. 12) follows from assuming that Ṽ(p) is analytic and crosses zero linearly at p_max. The sign-change doping of about 0.31 comes from a linear extrapolation beyond the data. The authors should state that the tail shape is an input, not an output, of the analysis. They should give the uncertainty in p_max and α from the fit, and say what observed T_c(p) would falsify the picture, as opposed to merely leaving the extrapolation unconstrained.

6. The doping scales are not consistent across families, but cross-family doping comparisons serve as evidence.
   - The paper says it avoids T_c-based doping estimates and prefers Fermi-surface areas (p. 6). Yet:
     - YBCO dopings in Fig. 2 and LSCO dopings (Sr content) are not from Fermi-surface areas;
     - T_c persisting to p = 0.31 in Tl2201 (quantum-oscillation area) against 0.27 in LSCO (nominal x) is offered as evidence for disorder-induced rounding (p. 12).
   - Known offsets between Luttinger counts and nominal or T_c-based dopings are of order 0.02–0.04, comparable to the difference being interpreted.
   - The authors should state the doping scale used for each data set in Figs. 2 and 3, and estimate its uncertainty.

7. The argument that Δ(k) ∝ d(k) rules out quantum-critical pairing (p. 11) needs more support. Spin-fluctuation exchange peaked near (π, π) also produces a gap dominated by the lowest d-wave harmonic. A nearly pure cos k_x − cos k_y gap therefore does not by itself show that no critical mode takes part.
   - The authors acknowledge that bounds on higher harmonics are lacking. They should quote the bound the cited ARPES data actually impose; for example, the maximum cos 2k_x − cos 2k_y admixture compatible with the error bars.
   - They should also state how large a harmonic a critical mode with a given correlation length would produce.
   - Without this, the conclusion that "no quantum critical mode is the prime driver" is too strong.

8. The paper claims weak coupling while its own inferred coupling is of order one. It reports Ṽρ(E_F) ≈ 1 at optimal doping and about 0.5 at the highest doping (p. 11), and defends the analysis by analogy with Migdal–Eliashberg theory for Pb (λ ≈ 1). In Eliashberg theory, however, retardation (ω_D << E_F) is what controls λ ≈ 1, and here F = 1 explicitly removes retardation. The authors should explain what small parameter justifies mean-field treatment when Ṽρ ≈ 1 without retardation. They should also estimate the strong-coupling corrections to Δ_0/k_B T_c that would then be expected. Those corrections bear on how close to 2.14 the ratio should come in the cleanest samples (prediction 3, p. 12).

9. Several normal-state anomalies are left unaddressed, and some directly supportive evidence is unused.
   - The B-linear, scale-invariant magnetoresistance in LSCO, Tl2201 and Bi2201 (refs 18, 48) is introduced on p. 7 but never reconciled with the puddle or disorder picture. The doping dependence of the Hall coefficient in the overdoped regime is not discussed either.
   - Conversely, ref. 17 is cited on p. 7 as consistent with T-linear resistivity. Its title reports Fermi-liquid heat transport and "pure d-wave BCS" behaviour, including Wiedemann–Franz tests and universal quasiparticle thermal conductivity. These are direct, low-energy tests of the dirty-d-wave and Fermi-liquid picture, and they distinguish homogeneous pair breaking from granular puddles.
   - The authors should discuss these measurements explicitly, both as support and as a means of discriminating between their two disorder mechanisms.

10. The puddle explanation of T-linear resistivity needs a stated field dependence. The only quantitative support given is a reduction of the T-linear slope by at least a factor of two between 10 and 18 T in Tl2201 (ref. 50). If puddles cause the T-linear term, it should keep falling at fields well above the bulk H_c2. The scenario should then predict a field scale tied to the local gap distribution (e.g. the STM gaps of 120 K in Bi2212). The authors should state this prediction, and check whether existing pulsed-field data on LSCO and Tl2201 at higher fields agree with it.

11. Key arguments are deferred to a Supplementary Information and a "Box 1" that are not included. The text sends the reader to Supplementary Information Sections I–III for:
    - the crossover versus phase-transition argument (adiabatic connection between underdoped and overdoped regimes);
    - the relation to a quantum critical point at p*;
    - the distinction between homogeneous dirty d-wave and self-organized granularity.

    The Fig. 1 caption also refers to "Box 1". None of these appears in the submitted PDF. Two of these arguments carry weight for the conclusions: why mesoscopic inhomogeneity is preferred over Abrikosov–Gorkov pair breaking, and why the crossover licenses ignoring the underdoped physics. They cannot be assessed. The authors should include them.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one quantitative "disorder budget" figure. At fixed p (e.g. p ≈ 0.25), plot the main anomalies against an independent disorder metric across samples and families, or against controlled irradiation series. The anomalies are T_theta/T_c, residual C/T fraction, A_1 and the gap-above-T_c range. The disorder metric could be ρ_0, Dingle temperature or NQR linewidth. This would turn the central claim from a plausible reinterpretation into a tested one.
2. State discriminating predictions between this picture and the two-fluid and quantum-critical alternatives, and set them out with numbers. Examples: the expected A_1 for a given ρ_0; the field scale at which the T-linear term should vanish; how the Wiedemann–Franz ratio and the universal κ/T of nodal quasiparticles should behave in each picture.
3. Rebuild Fig. 2 with planar-only (λ_a) stiffness and a YBCO-appropriate m*. Show the uncertainty in p, and if possible extend it with existing overdoped Tl2201 and LSCO stiffness data on the same T_theta axis for direct comparison.
4. Document the Fig. 3 inversion fully, in an appendix or the Supplementary Information:

   - which points are fitted;
