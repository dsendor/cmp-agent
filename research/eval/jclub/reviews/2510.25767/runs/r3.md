Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

This Perspective proposes that in an ideal, disorder-free cuprate there is a crossover near optimal doping. On the underdoped side, phase-fluctuation-limited strong-coupling physics governs. On the overdoped side, a weak-coupling BCS description built on a Fermi-liquid normal state applies at energies of order the gap and below. The authors argue that the main observations that seem to contradict this picture (the superfluid-stiffness "boomerang", low-T T-linear resistivity, residual C/T and Drude weight, gaps persisting above Tc) are extrinsic consequences of dopant disorder amplified by a short coherence length. They also invert the BCS gap equation on Bi2212 ARPES data to estimate an effective nearest-neighbour pairing interaction. The viewpoint is clearly stated, timely, and framed as falsifiable, and it would interest a wide audience. As written, though, the central attribution to disorder rests mostly on qualitative arguments. The two quantitative supports (Figs. 2 and 3) have methodological problems that weaken the conclusions drawn from them.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- It states a clear, contrarian organising hypothesis for the overdoped cuprates and separates "intrinsic" from "extrinsic" behaviour more explicitly than the two-fluid and quantum-critical pictures it contrasts with (p. 2).
- It brings together evidence from many techniques into one argument: quantum oscillations and their Lifshitz–Kosevich form in Tl2201, NQR linewidths across families, superfluid stiffness in YBCO versus Ca-YBCO, the ARPES/STM gap ratio in Bi2212, residual specific heat in LSCO, and neutron spin gaps.
- It lists concrete experimental tests (p. 12): rounding of the Tc(p) tail, growth of the Tθ/Drude-weight fraction, Δ0 → ~2kBTc, a mean-field specific-heat jump, and suppression of the T-linear term with decreasing disorder. It also names candidate materials for these tests (stoichiometric YBa2Cu3O7 under pressure, inner planes of multilayer cuprates).
- Inverting the gap equation to infer the size and doping dependence of the effective interaction (Fig. 3) is an interesting and reusable idea.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The central claim that the deviations are extrinsic is not tested quantitatively against any independent measure of disorder. Throughout pp. 7–9 and in the Fig. 1 caption ("we attribute those discrepancies to the effects of disorder"), every feature that contradicts BCS/Fermi-liquid behaviour is assigned to disorder. However, no figure or table sets these features against a disorder metric measured independently on the same or comparable samples. Such metrics include residual resistivity ρ0, the Dingle scattering rate, the NQR linewidth (pp. 8, refs 71–74) and irradiation dose (refs 87, 88). Without that comparison the hypothesis can absorb any discrepancy and is hard to falsify with existing data.
   - What to do: compile the available data as a function of such a metric at fixed p (or fixed Tc), across families or within one family where ρ0 varies (Tl2201, LSCO films, irradiated LSCO/YBCO). The quantities to compile are Tθ/Tc, residual C/T (or residual Drude fraction), and the T-linear resistivity coefficient.
   - Show whether each deviation shrinks as the metric shrinks.
   - Where data already exist, say whether they support or contradict the expected trend. For example, the low-T T-linear slope in Tl2201 and LSCO has been reported to correlate with Tc, or with the superconducting dome itself, rather than with ρ0 (the paper itself raises this correspondence on p. 7). The authors should address it directly rather than only noting it.

2. Tl2201 is the authors' own benchmark for clean overdoped behaviour, yet it shows the features they attribute to disorder, and the paper does not reconcile this. On p. 6, Tl2201 is "the cleanest cuprate that can access the overdoped regime" and supplies the Fermi-liquid evidence: quantum oscillations, LK form, m* = 5me, ħ/τ = 2.4 meV. Yet on pp. 7–8 the same material shows a T-linear resistivity (ref 17), a vanishing superfluid stiffness near the dome edge with Tc/Tθ behaving like the underdoped side (refs 63, 64), and a specific-heat jump only 10% of the residual C/T (ref 65).
   - The paper's only reconciliation is a single citation (ref 70) to inhomogeneity on the scale of a few hundred Å. Samples that show quantum oscillations must have a mean free path of that order, so the authors need to explain quantitatively how the required "puddle" inhomogeneity coexists with coherent orbits around the full Fermi surface.
   - The quoted ħ/τ = 2.4 meV (ref 36) is comparable to kBTc ≈ 2.2 meV for Tc = 26 K. The authors should estimate ξ0/ℓ for these samples and state where they sit relative to the Abrikosov–Gorkov critical value. Otherwise it is unclear whether homogeneous pair breaking already accounts for the Tl2201 observations and whether mesoscopic inhomogeneity is needed at all.

3. The gap-equation inversion in Fig. 3 rests on a cutoff choice that conflicts with the effective-theory framework, and it has no uncertainty analysis. On p. 4 the effective theory is defined below an intermediate scale, and on p. 6 the analysis is restricted to energies below about 0.1 eV. On p. 10, however, Eq. 7 is inverted "without introducing any additional high-energy cutoff (taking F(ε) = 1)". The momentum integral therefore runs over the full tight-binding band of Eq. 10 (bandwidth of order 1 eV).
   - The inferred value V ≈ 0.6 eV at optimal doping, the identification with J ≈ 0.15 eV via V = 4J, and the inferred "smooth but pronounced decrease of V" as evidence for magnetic pairing (p. 11) all depend logarithmically on this cutoff. With a cutoff of 0.1 eV, consistent with the stated effective theory, V would be substantially larger, and the "reasonable agreement" with J would change.
   - What to do: show V(p) for a range of cutoffs (for example 0.05, 0.1, 0.3 eV and none), and propagate the uncertainties in the measured Δ0, the tight-binding parameters and the bilayer-splitting treatment into error bars on V.
   - At optimal doping and below, the paper itself states that Δ0/kBTc reaches 4 or more (p. 9). The inversion uses Δ0 rather than Tc, so V inferred from the gap and V inferred from Tc must differ substantially there. Please show both, so that readers can see the doping range over which the BCS inversion is self-consistent.
   - The authors acknowledge Vρ(EF) ~ 1 (p. 11). Comparing to Pb within Migdal–Eliashberg theory does not justify using the weak-coupling BCS gap equation with no frequency dependence. A brief estimate of the corrections expected at this coupling would help.

4. The YBCO superfluid-stiffness evidence in Fig. 2 is open to an alternative explanation (CuO chains) and has an internal inconsistency with its own clean-limit reference lines. The increase of Tθ by about 50% from p = 0.16 to p = 0.19 (p. 7) is a principal piece of evidence that clean overdoped cuprates avoid the boomerang. However, the teal points mix λa^-2, λb^-2 and the geometric mean √(λaλb).
   - Near YBa2Cu3O7, the b-axis (chain) direction carries a sizeable chain condensate, and ref 59, cited on this very point, is titled "Evidence for chain superconductivity". An increase of the chain contribution as the chains fill would raise Tθ along b without any change in planar physics.
   - Several points at p ≈ 0.185 (about 400–450 K) lie above the blue "1+p" clean-limit line. That line is computed with m* = 5me taken from a different material (Tl2201). Either the m* choice or the inclusion of chain carriers makes this comparison inconsistent.
   - What to do: show the planar (a-axis) stiffness separately, explain why the measured stiffness exceeds the clean-system estimate, and justify the m* used for YBCO.
   - State how p was assigned for YBCO and Ca-YBCO. On p. 6 the authors say they avoid Tc(p)-dome-based doping estimates "wherever possible". For YBCO, p is usually obtained from Tc or from c-axis lattice parameters calibrated to Tc, and Ca substitution changes that calibration.

5. Key parts of the argument are delegated to Supplementary Information that is not part of the submission. Pages 5–6 and 8 refer to Supplementary Information sections I–III: the crossover versus phase-transition argument that justifies treating the overdoped side without the underdoped physics, the relation to a quantum critical point at p*, and above all the distinction between homogeneous "dirty d-wave" pair breaking and inhomogeneous granularity. That last distinction is exactly the debate on which the extrinsic attribution rests (p. 8: "the debate ... is ongoing"). Without these sections the main conclusion cannot be evaluated. They should be supplied, or their essential content moved into the main text.

B. Should address

6. The scaling argument in Eq. 9 and the statements on p. 5 about Abrikosov–Gorkov theory need derivation and appear to conflict with standard results. The text says that in AG theory "Tc vanishes linearly as ξ0/ℓ approaches the critical value" and that κ "vanishes in proportion to Tc", giving Tθ/Tc ~ kFℓ ≫ 1 as Tc → 0.
   - In the standard AG treatment of a sign-changing gap, Tc vanishes as the square root of the distance to the critical scattering rate, and the T = 0 superfluid density in the gapless regime scales as Δ0², that is as Tc². The ρs ∝ Tc² dirty-d-wave behaviour is what refs 28–30 use to discuss overdoped LSCO. On that scaling, homogeneous pair breaking already drives Tθ/Tc downward as Tc → 0, and the case for needing mesoscopic inhomogeneity (p. 5) changes.
   - The paper also states that ξ0 is only 2–3 lattice constants (p. 5), so kFξ0 is of order a few rather than ≫ 1, and order-one prefactors decide the conclusion.
   - What to do: give the derivation, with prefactors, and state where the observed Tθ/Tc values fall relative to the homogeneous AG prediction.

7. The text overstates the agreement of the gap ratio with BCS relative to what Fig. 3 shows. Page 9 says that in overdoped Bi2212 "Δ0/Tc approaches 2 (within substantial error bars) by p = 0.24".
   - In Fig. 3, the measured Δ0 (yellow) lies clearly above 2.14kBTc (cyan) at every doping where both are plotted, by roughly a factor of 1.5–2 near p ≈ 0.22–0.23. No error bars are shown, and the yellow series ends before the cyan one.
   - What to do: add error bars, plot Δ0/kBTc directly as a function of p, mark where it reaches the d-wave weak-coupling value, and give the STM gap-distribution means quoted on p. 9 alongside the ARPES values.

8. A gap shape close to d(k) does not by itself rule out pairing by a critical or near-critical collective mode. On pp. 10–11 the authors infer from f(k) ≈ 1 that the effective interaction is nearest-neighbour only, and conclude that this "precludes any theory that identifies optimal doping with the point at which the pairing arises primarily from the exchange of any familiar quantum-critical collective mode."
   - Spin-fluctuation interactions peaked at (π,π) with correlation lengths of one to two lattice spacings, and weak-coupling calculations more generally, also produce gaps dominated by the lowest harmonic.
   - What to do: give a quantitative bound on the higher harmonic, for example the cos(2kx) − cos(2ky) coefficient with its error bar from the ARPES data in ref 83. Translate that bound into a bound on the range of the interaction, and hence on the correlation length of any mode that could be mediating pairing. Without this, the exclusion should be softened.

9. The Fermi-liquid evidence covers only a narrow edge of the doping range the claim spans. The proposal concerns the whole regime p > popt (Fig. 1 and p. 2). The direct Fermi-liquid evidence (quantum oscillations with LK form) exists only for Tl2201 at p ≥ 0.27, Tc ≤ 26 K (p. 6). In the range 0.16 < p < 0.27, where most of the overdoped dome and its high Tc lie, the paper offers no direct evidence of well-defined low-energy quasiparticles in the field-induced normal state. Please state this limitation explicitly, and discuss what evidence exists in that window: ARPES quasiparticle widths, specific-heat γ, and Wiedemann–Franz tests (ref 17 concerns Tl2201 at Tc ≈ 15 K).

10. The predictions are qualitative and lack thresholds that would count as a failure of the proposal. The tests listed on p. 12 ("should become increasingly rounded", "should approach", "should become increasingly small") are not tied to a disorder scale or to numbers. The exponential tail of Eq. 8 should give a definite prediction once V0 and pmax are fixed from Fig. 3, as the inset purports to do. For each test, state the expected magnitude for a sample with a given ρ0 or NQR linewidth, and what result would falsify the proposal. A concrete example would be the predicted T-linear coefficient or Tθ/Tc for pressure-doped YBa2Cu3O7 at a stated p.

11. The doping assignment is inconsistent across the figures. Page 6 says p is taken from the Fermi-surface area wherever possible. In practice:
   - Fig. 3 uses ARPES-derived p for Bi2212;
   - Fig. 2 uses p for YBCO from other calibrations;
   - LSCO p is taken as x;
   - the statement "Tc persists up to at least p = 0.31 in Tl2201, compared to p = 0.27 in ... LSCO" (p. 12) compares p from quantum oscillations with p = x.

   Since the rounded-tail prediction depends on comparing pmax across materials, the authors should tabulate the p calibration used for each data set and its uncertainty.

12. The proposed test using pressure-doped YBa2Cu3O7 needs a way to measure doping. On pp. 8 and 12, hydrostatic pressure or uniaxial strain on YBa2Cu3O7 is offered as the ideal test. Pressure changes the bandwidth, the chain–plane charge transfer and the c-axis spacing at the same time. The authors should say how p, and the disorder level, would be measured independently under pressure, for example by quantum oscillation frequency or Hall number, so that a Tθ(p) or ρ(T) result can be interpreted.

C. Minor

13. The Fig. 3 caption and legend disagree. The caption calls Δ0 the "teal points" and 2.14kBTc the "dark blue points", but in the legend Δ0 is yellow, 2.14kBTc is light blue, and Δextrap is purple. The inset axes are unlabeled, and it is unclear whether the inset's dashed curve is Δextrap, Tc or V. Please label them and state the extrapolated pmax.

14. In Fig. 2, the legend cites "[1]–[5]", which do not match the reference list. The first entry reads "λa^2" where λa^-2 is presumably meant. The caption should state which data sets the "50% increase" quoted on p. 7 is drawn from.

15. Notation for the scales is inconsistent:
   - Tθ in the text versus TΘ in Fig. 1;
   - TΔ ≡ Δ0/2 (Fig. 1 caption) versus |Δ0|/2 in the figure label;
   - Δ/kBTc = 2.14 on p. 9 versus "Δ0 ≈ 2Tc" on p. 12 (kB missing).

   Also, Tc ≈ |Δ0|/2 "up to 20–30%" (p. 3) is used as the BCS benchmark, while the s-wave BCS value 1.76 already sits at the edge of that range. Please make this consistent.

16. On p. 4, the 3D κ(T) given via λ has units of energy per length. Please clarify how L is chosen for the bilayer YBCO in Fig. 2: ac = c/2, or the c-axis lattice parameter. The caption mentions two planar Cu per cell, but the choice changes Tθ by a factor of 2.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add one synthesis figure that plots a deviation from BCS/Fermi-liquid behaviour (Tθ/Tc, residual C/T, or the T-linear coefficient) against an independent disorder metric, across all samples for which both exist. This would turn the central hypothesis from an interpretation into a tested claim.
- In Fig. 3, show the robustness of V(p) to the cutoff and to the input uncertainties. Also plot Tc predicted from the inferred V next to the measured Tc, so readers can see directly where the weak-coupling description is self-consistent.
- Add a short side-by-side table of the specific observations that would distinguish the present picture from two-fluid phenomenology and from a quantum-critical scenario, so that the predictions on p. 12 discriminate between the alternatives rather than only being consistent with this one.
- Move the essential content of Supplementary Information sections I–III, especially the homogeneous pair-breaking versus inhomogeneous granularity comparison with quantitative estimates for Tl2201, LSCO and Bi2212, into the main text or a Box.
