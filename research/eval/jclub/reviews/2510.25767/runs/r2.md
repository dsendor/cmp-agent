Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

This Perspective proposes that an idealized, disorder-free overdoped cuprate is described at low energies by a Fermi-liquid normal state and a weak-coupling (BCS-like) d-wave superconducting state, with Tθ >> Tc ≈ |Δ0|/2. It argues that the main observations against this picture are extrinsic effects of substitutional disorder amplified by the short coherence length: the "boomerang" in superfluid stiffness, T-linear resistivity to the edge of the dome, residual C/T and Drude weight, and gaps and puddles above Tc. It also inverts a nearest-neighbour BCS gap equation with Bi2212 ARPES data to infer a pairing interaction of magnetic magnitude (Fig. 3), and it ends with falsifiable predictions. The framing is clear and useful and the predictions are testable. The central claim, though, is an attribution argument whose strength depends on how quantitatively the disorder hypothesis is tied to data, and at present it mostly is not.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- It clearly separates "BCS as a low-energy effective description" from "phonon-mediated pairing" (pp. 3–4), and it gives a concrete energy-scale hierarchy (Fig. 1) that experiments can test.
- It gathers in one place several underused pieces of evidence: the NQR linewidth hierarchy, with YBa2Cu3O7, YBCO-248 and Ortho-II far sharper than other cuprates (p. 8); the rising Tθ in YBCO compared with Ca-YBCO (Fig. 2); quantum oscillations in Tl2201 that follow the Lifshitz–Kosevich form (pp. 6–7); and the ARPES/STM convergence of Δ0/kBTc towards about 2 at p ≈ 0.24 (p. 9).
- The "Experimental tests" section (pp. 11–12) lists concrete predictions: Tc(p) becoming convex near p_max, Tθ >> Tc, Δ0 ≈ 2Tc, a mean-field C/T jump with vanishing residual C/T, and a vanishing T-linear resistivity in cleaner samples. It also names a candidate platform, pressure-doped YBa2Cu3O7.
- The estimate J ≈ 0.15 eV extracted from the gap data is an interesting order-of-magnitude link between overdoped pairing and the parent exchange.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The disorder attribution is asserted case by case and never tested against a common, measured disorder parameter.
   - Where: p. 2 and the section "Evidence that large phase fluctuations are extrinsic" (p. 8). The paper attributes each inconsistency with the BCS picture to disorder: the boomerang, T-linear resistivity, residual C/T, the residual Drude peak, gaps above Tc and the linear end of the dome. Of the evidence offered, only Ca-YBCO versus YBCO (Fig. 2) and the irradiation studies [87, 88] vary disorder at all.
   - Why it matters: the main conclusion, that the ideal overdoped cuprate is BCS-like, follows only if the anomalies shrink as disorder shrinks. Without a quantitative link, the hypothesis can absorb any discrepancy.
   - What to do: build a table or figure across materials and samples (LSCO, Tl2201, Bi2212, Bi2201, YBCO and Ca-YBCO, MBE LSCO films). For each, list independent disorder metrics (residual resistivity per plane, Dingle temperature from quantum oscillations, NQR/NMR linewidth, the STM gap-map width) beside each anomaly at comparable p and Tc. The anomalies are Tθ/Tc, the T-linear coefficient A1 per CuO2 plane, residual γ0/γn, and the uncondensed Drude fraction.
   - Particular tension to address: Tl2201 and LSCO differ in disorder (by the paper's own account, p. 12), yet the reported T-linear slopes per plane are comparable [16, 17]. A disorder or puddle mechanism [49] would naively make A1 depend on disorder strength. The authors should say what their scenario predicts for A1 as a function of residual resistivity, and whether existing data already constrain it.
   - Also discuss the MBE LSCO films of ref. [14]. These are presented in the literature as highly homogeneous, yet they show Tθ ∝ Tc over hundreds of samples. The "Swiss cheese" description [68, 69] comes from bulk crystals. Explain why it should apply to those films.

2. The main "clean" counterexample (YBCO, Fig. 2) covers only a marginal overdoped range and may be affected by the chains.
   - Where: p. 7 and Fig. 2. The rise of Tθ in YBa2Cu3Ox is the main positive evidence that clean overdoped cuprates avoid the boomerang. But the data reach only p ≈ 0.19, where Tc has fallen only from 94 K to 88 K, so the material is barely past optimal doping.
   - The chains complicate the comparison. YBa2Cu3O7 has superconducting CuO chains (ref. [59] is cited for chain superconductivity), and the b-axis stiffness includes a chain contribution that grows as the chains fill. The caption says that λa, λb and their averages "produce different results", but the figure does not separate them.
   - Fig. 2 also shows teal points at p ≈ 0.19 of about 400–450 K, above the "clean" 1+p dashed line (about 360 K). That line is computed with m* = 5me, which comes from single-layer Tl2201 (p. 9 caption). The measured stiffness exceeding the clean estimate suggests either that the comparison mass is inappropriate for YBCO or that the chains contribute.
   - Why it matters: the claim that disorder-free overdoped cuprates realise Tθ >> Tc rests heavily on this one dataset.
   - What to do:
     - Plot λa-only (plane) stiffness separately from λb and from geometric averages.
     - Justify, or replace, the m* used for the reference lines.
     - State plainly that YBCO tests only the first few percent of the overdoped regime.
     - If any data exist on other low-disorder overdoped systems (for example YBCO-248 or the inner planes of multilayer cuprates, both mentioned on pp. 8 and 12), include them.

3. Several assumptions in the inversion of the gap equation (Fig. 3) are not justified. The quantitative conclusions drawn from it (V̄ ≈ 0.6 eV, J ≈ 0.15 eV, a magnetic origin, the extrapolated tail) depend on those assumptions.
   - Sign convention:
     - Eq. 7 defines V̄ = −[3J(1) − 4U(1)] and states that pairing requires V̄ < 0.
     - The Fig. 3 left axis plots negative values.
     - The text on p. 10, however, writes V̄ ≈ 0.6 eV and "V̄ = 3J(1) − 4U(1) = 4J". The signs need to be made consistent.
   - Cutoff: the inversion sets F(ε) = 1 (no cutoff). In a BCS gap equation the inferred coupling depends logarithmically on the cutoff, and the paper itself invokes an intermediate scale of order J (p. 4). So the agreement with J ≈ 0.15 eV is cutoff-dependent. Show how V̄ changes for cutoffs between, say, 0.1 eV and the bandwidth.
   - Inflated input gap: near optimal doping the measured Δ0 (gold points) is about twice the BCS value 2.14 kBTc (light-blue points). So V̄ at p ≈ 0.16–0.2 is fitted to a gap the paper elsewhere calls non-BCS. The J ≈ 0.15 eV estimate uses exactly this optimal-doping value. Either use V̄ only where Δ0 ≈ 2.14 kBTc, or state how much the J estimate changes.
   - Coupling strength: V̄ρ(EF) ≈ 0.5–1 (p. 11) is not weak coupling. The weak-coupling ratio 2.14 then needs strong-coupling corrections. Give at least an estimate of their size, by analogy with Eliashberg results at λ ~ 1, before Δ0/kBTc → 2 is used as evidence of BCS behaviour.
   - Extrapolation: the purple Δ_extrap points, and hence the predicted exponential tail and the crossing near p ≈ 0.31, rest on a straight-line extrapolation of V̄(p) beyond the data (red line). Give the uncertainty of that fit, and show how the predicted p_max and tail change under other plausible functional forms.

B. Should address

4. Bi2212 is used selectively.
   - Where: pp. 8–11. Bi2212 is the main source for the BCS-like gap ratio (p. 9) and for the inversion (Fig. 3). The same material is cited as showing large gap inhomogeneity, puddles up to 120 K, and an ARPES gap persisting 50% above Tc (p. 8), all attributed to disorder.
   - Why it matters: if disorder in Bi2212 is strong enough to produce those effects, it is unclear why a spatially averaged ARPES gap there should reflect the clean-limit Δ0. The ARPES-determined p in the most overdoped Bi2212 samples is also sensitive to surface doping.
   - What to do: explain why averaged gaps from a strongly inhomogeneous material are a faithful input to a clean-limit gap equation. Alternatively, show that the conclusions hold when restricted to dopings where STM gap maps are narrow.

5. The inference that the pairing interaction is nearest-neighbour, and that no quantum-critical mode drives pairing, is stronger than the data support.
   - Where: pp. 4 and 10–11.
   - The argument has three steps:
     - Δ(k) is fixed by the form of V because φ(r) is "much longer ranged" than V.
     - The measured Δ(k) ≈ Δ0 d(k).
     - Therefore V is short-ranged, which "precludes" pairing by any familiar quantum-critical collective mode.
   - Weaknesses in these steps:
     - On p. 5 the paper notes that ξ0 is only about 2–3 lattice constants in cuprates, which weakens the separation of length scales that the first step assumes.
     - The gap shape is a Fermi-surface projection. Interactions with antiferromagnetic correlation lengths of one to two lattice spacings, or peaked near (π, π), also produce gaps that are close to pure d(k).
     - The authors themselves call for bounds on higher harmonics.
   - What to do: either give quantitative bounds on the cos(2kx) − cos(2ky) harmonic from the ARPES data [83, 96, 102], and show what bound on the interaction range follows, or soften "precludes" to a statement about consistency.

6. Abrikosov–Gorkov scaling and Eq. 9 do not fit together as written.
   - Where: p. 5. The text says that in AG theory κ vanishes in proportion to Tc, and then says that in 2D (L = ac) Tθ/Tc ~ kFℓ ~ kFξ0 diverges as Tc → 0.
   - If κ ∝ Tc with a fixed prefactor, the 2D ratio tends to a constant. The divergence requires the prefactor to change, for example through ξ0 growing at the pair-breaking boundary, which should be shown.
   - The exponent is also a modelling question. Dirty-limit d-wave pair-breaking is often stated to give ρs ∝ Tc² near the critical point, whereas Lee-Hone et al. [28–30] obtain a near-linear relation only with specific scattering assumptions.
   - Why it matters: this is the argument that homogeneous disorder alone cannot make phase fluctuations important. That in turn motivates invoking mesoscopic granularity.
   - What to do: give the explicit AG result for κ(Tc) in the d-wave Born and unitary limits, and derive Eq. 9 with its assumptions stated.

7. The evidence for a Fermi-liquid ground state is confined to a narrow corner of the overdoped regime.
   - Where: pp. 6–7. Quantum oscillations are seen only in Tl2201 for p > 0.27 (Tc ≤ 26 K). The conclusion, however, is applied to "the overdoped regime" down to near p_opt (Fig. 1).
   - Why it matters: between p_opt and 0.27 the only evidence offered is indirect. That is also the range in which T-linear resistivity and incoherent transport [16, 18] are reported.
   - What to do:
     - State the doping range in which the Fermi-liquid ground state is directly established.
     - Discuss whether the quantum-oscillation samples also show the T-linear term, and with what magnitude relative to the T² term. If a single sample displays both quantum oscillations and T-linear resistivity, the puddle reconciliation [49, 50] should be made quantitative for that sample.

8. Doping is determined inconsistently.
   - Where: p. 6 states that p is taken from Fermi-surface area wherever possible, rather than from an assumed Tc(p) dome. Fig. 2, however, plots YBCO and Ca-YBCO, for which p is normally inferred from Tc or lattice parameters. Ca substitution also changes doping and disorder at the same time.
   - Why it matters: the comparison of Tθ(p) between YBCO and Ca-YBCO, and the predicted convexity of Tc(p) near p_max, depend on the p scale.
   - What to do: state the method used to determine p for each dataset in Figs. 2 and 3 and its uncertainty. Discuss whether shifting the Ca-YBCO p scale could account for part of the stiffness difference.

9. The Supplementary Information is referenced but not provided.
   - Where: pp. 5–6 point to SI sections I–III for:
     - the crossover argument that justifies ignoring underdoped physics;
     - the relation to a putative QCP at p*;
     - the distinction between homogeneous dirty d-wave and granularity.
   - Why it matters: these are load-bearing steps of the argument and cannot be evaluated from the PDF as submitted.
   - What to do: include the SI, or move its essential content into the main text.

10. The predictions are mostly qualitative.
    - Where: pp. 11–12. Most predictions are phrased as trends ("increasingly rounded", "increasingly small").
    - Why it matters: in their present form they are hard to falsify, because almost any partial improvement in cleaner samples would count as support.
    - What to do: attach numbers where the framework permits. For example:
      - the expected Tθ/Tc at p ≈ 0.25 for a given residual resistivity;
      - the predicted shape of the tail of Tc(p) from Fig. 3, including the value of α in Eq. 8;
      - an expected upper bound on A1 in pressure-doped YBa2Cu3O7 [56];
      - the expected size of the specific-heat jump relative to γn.

C. Minor

11. Fig. 3: the caption and the legend disagree.
    - The caption calls the measured Δ0 "teal points" and 2.14 kBTc "dark blue points". The legend shows Δ0 in gold, 2.14 kBTc in light blue, and Δ_extrap in purple.
    - The caption mentions "black and red lines", but the plot shows a pink line and a grey curve or dashed line.
    - The inset has no axis label, so it is unclear what quantity runs from 0 to 1.
    - The right axis is labelled "Gap, Tc (meV)"; state that Tc is converted to energy units.

12. Fig. 2:
    - The legend cites [1]–[5], which do not correspond to the bibliography numbering. Map them to refs [57, 58, 90–92].
    - The small purple dots, which appear to be Ca-YBCO Tc, are not labelled.
    - The text says Tθ rises "by 50%" from p = 0.16 to 0.19, but different symbol sets in the figure imply different ratios. Specify which dataset is meant.

13. Text and references:
    - Typos: "of of" (p. 1); "the our approach" (p. 2); "principle focus" for "principal" (Fig. 1 caption and p. 12); "Tl1201" for Tl2201 (p. 7); "none-the-less" (p. 11).
    - On p. 5 the sentence "...lattice constant, and [116]" is incomplete.
    - Duplicate references: [32] and [116] are the same paper, and so are [13] and [63].
    - Ref. [61] lacks authors and a full citation.
    - The relation "Tc ≈ |Δ0|/2" (p. 2) and Δ0/kBTc = 2.14 (p. 9) should be stated with consistent factors and units.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one summary figure plotting each BCS-violating anomaly against a measured disorder metric across materials and samples. This would turn the central attribution from an argument into a testable correlation (Concern 1).
2. Repeat the Fig. 3 inversion with explicit cutoffs and only where Δ0 ≈ 2.14 kBTc, and report the uncertainty of V̄(p), the inferred J, and the extrapolated p_max (Concern 3).
3. Separate the plane and chain contributions to the YBCO stiffness, and recompute the clean-limit reference lines with a mass appropriate to YBCO (Concern 2).
4. Turn the experimental tests into a short table of quantitative predictions, each with the material, doping, and expected value or bound (Concern 10).
