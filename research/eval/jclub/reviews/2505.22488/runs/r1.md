Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report a large cross-circular Raman intensity difference (g_ROA up to about 1 for the E_g^(1) mode at 785 nm) in centrosymmetric, non-magnetic, ferroaxial NiTiO3 (point group -3). The sign reverses between the front and back faces of a single-domain crystal, and spatial maps of a multidomain crystal show domain-like contrast. A symmetry argument and a tight-binding model show that a cross-circular asymmetry |R1| ≠ |R2| between the 1E_g and 2E_g Raman tensors is allowed within the electric-dipole approximation when the ferroaxial term t_ax is present. If the result is firmly established, it gives a simple, all-optical probe of ferroaxial domains in a bulk three-dimensional insulator, and it clarifies the "chiral CDW" Raman results in 1T-TaS2. The novelty over the TaS2 and NiCo2TeO6 work rests on two things: tying the effect to a bulk ferroaxial order parameter, and the microscopic model. Both need tightening.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The effect is large and clear in the raw spectra (Fig. 3, Fig. S6). It reverses sign between the front and back faces with nearly equal magnitude (Table S4: 0.96 vs -1.04 for E_g^(1)), which rules out a simple fixed instrumental offset.
- Several internal checks fit the proposed picture: no asymmetry for A_g modes in LL/RR (Fig. S7); the same sign in Stokes and anti-Stokes; a strong dependence on excitation wavelength (785 vs 532 vs 633 nm); and a sharp sign reversal across a domain wall in the high-resolution line scan (Fig. S10).
- The group-theory derivation (Sec. S3) and the essential-parameter analysis (Eqs. S57–S65), which ties the effect to t_ax, are useful and general.
- A Raman-based ferroaxial domain imaging method with about 1 µm resolution would be practically valuable, since electrogyration and SHG are the main alternatives.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The correlation between the Raman map and the ferroaxial domains is not shown on the same region. The main evidence that g_ROA tracks the ferroaxial domain pattern (rather than only the crystal face) is Fig. 5(c) against Fig. 5(d). Fig. 5(d) is "adapted from Ref. [23] (image inverted from the original)". The text does not say whether it shows the same crystal, the same area or the same orientation as Fig. 5(c), and the two images do not visibly match: (d) has straight stripes and large blocks, while (c) shows patchy, lower-contrast regions. Inverting the electrogyration image is also an unexplained free choice that fixes the claimed sign correspondence. As presented, the statement that the pattern "closely matches" is not supported. The authors should:
   - acquire electrogyration (or circularly polarized SHG) and Raman maps of the same region of the same multidomain crystal, with fiducial marks;
   - show them co-registered at the same scale;
   - quantify the correspondence (for example, pixel-wise correlation of sign(g_ROA) with sign(ΔT/T));
   - state the absolute relation between the sign of g_ROA and A+ or A-, including how the electrogyration sign maps onto the domain state.

   Without this, the domain-imaging claim in the abstract and conclusion rests only on the front/back reversal of one crystal.

2. A bulk origin is not separated from a near-surface or field-induced one. The front/back reversal shows that the signal follows an axial, time-even quantity along c. However, any surface breaks inversion, and the product n·A of the surface normal and the ferroaxial vector is a pseudoscalar whose sign is opposite on the two faces. A surface-localized chiral (gyrotropic) layer would therefore reproduce exactly the front/back reversal and the domain contrast. The same holds for electrogyration driven by a surface or depletion field, which NiTiO3 is known to show (Refs. [15, 23]). These would be ROA from an inversion-broken region, not from the centrosymmetric bulk that the title and abstract stress. The setup is confocal (Sec. S2) and the crystal is 60 µm thick, so the test is straightforward:
   - measure g_ROA(E_g^(1)) as a function of focal depth through the crystal, and compare the depth profile with the confocal depth resolution and the 785-nm absorption length;
   - ideally, also measure g_ROA under an applied electric field along c, to bound any electrogyration contribution.

   A depth-independent g_ROA inside the bulk would substantiate the "centrosymmetric bulk" claim.

B. Should address

3. The physical mechanism in the main text is incomplete, and its relation to earlier explanations is unclear. On p. 8 the text says the "static ferroaxial distortion ... render[s] the two rotational components inequivalent", which gives |R1| ≠ |R2|. However, 1E_g and 2E_g are complex-conjugate irreps paired by time reversal. Writing the C3i E_g Raman tensors in the Cartesian form (c, d; d, -c) and (d, -c; -c, -d) gives I_LR - I_RL ∝ Im(c* d). The asymmetry therefore needs both:
   - d ≠ 0, which is allowed only without the vertical glide or mirror, i.e. the ferroaxial part;
   - a relative phase between c and d, i.e. resonance or absorption.

   With real (non-resonant) tensors it vanishes. This matches the 633-nm null and the model, where the effect appears only above the JDOS onset and depends on η. But it means the mechanism is a ferroaxial-allowed version of the "complex Raman tensor due to absorption" explanation of Ref. [4]. It is not a separate mechanism, and the introduction says the mechanism "remains unknown".

   The end of Sec. S3 argues that interference "cannot give rise to ROA" because 1E_g and 2E_g are eigenmodes. This needs reconciling with the Cartesian picture, where the effect is precisely an interference between the (xx-yy) and xy components. The authors should:
   - state explicitly that dissipation is necessary;
   - show that g_ROA in the model goes to zero as η_J, η_X → 0 away from resonance, and as t_ax → 0 (plot g against t_ax and against η);
   - explain how their result relates to, or goes beyond, Ref. [4].

4. Instrumental and polarization controls are thin. The only stated null control is that g_ROA ≈ 0 "outside the sample or near cracks" (p. 10). Where there is no phonon peak, g_ROA is a ratio of noise and says nothing about instrumental bias. In addition:
   - A single QWP between the beam splitter and the objective defines both the incident and the analysed polarizations (Sec. S2). The beam splitter's polarization-dependent phase, any QWP retardance error at each of the three wavelengths, and high-NA depolarization in a uniaxial crystal are not characterised.
   - The A_g^(3) peak appears in the cross-circular spectrum at 633 nm (Fig. S5), which points to imperfect circular polarization at that wavelength. The 633-nm null may then partly reflect reduced polarization purity rather than a real absence of ROA.

   The authors should:
   - report the extinction ratio (A_g leakage into LR/RL, E_g leakage into LL/RR) at each wavelength and state whether the QWP is achromatic;
   - measure a non-ferroaxial reference with E_g modes, such as α-Al2O3 or another D3d (R-3c) crystal, at 785 nm under identical conditions to show g_ROA = 0;
   - preferably measure a chiral reference of known handedness, such as α-quartz, to confirm the L/R convention.

5. The resonance claim rests on three wavelengths and an absorption spectrum from the literature. The attribution to the Ni2+ 3A2g→3T1g d-d resonance (p. 9, Sec. S3) uses 785, 532 and 633 nm. The absorption is cited from NiTiO3 nanocrystallites (Ref. [25]), not measured on these crystals for light along c. The authors should:
   - show the c-axis absorption spectrum of the measured crystal;
   - ideally, add an excitation-energy profile of g_ROA(E_g^(1)) across the d-d band (for example 700–850 nm with a tunable source) and compare it with that spectrum.

   The statement that E_g^(1) "involves the largest displacement of Ni2+ ions", and that this explains why it has the largest ROA, is not quantified. The authors should tabulate the Ni displacement amplitude (or projected Ni weight) for each E_g eigenvector from the DFT calculation and plot |g_ROA| against it for the five modes.

6. The comparison between model and experiment is qualitative only. The tight-binding model (Sec. S8) uses spinless p orbitals on a trigonal lattice with arbitrary hoppings (Eq. S50), arbitrary ω units and a single phenomenological phonon vertex X_Q. NiTiO3's resonance, by contrast, is a localized Ni d-d transition. In Fig. 4 the sign of I_LR - I_RL changes several times as ω varies (LR > RL near ω ≈ 1.4, LR < RL between 1.45 and 1.75), so the model predicts neither the sign nor the magnitude for NiTiO3. It also cannot account for the different signs of E_g^(1) (+0.96) and E_g^(2) (-0.16) on the same face.

   The main text says "our calculations show that the observed NROA originates directly from the ferroaxial order" and that the calculations support "the experimental results obtained with different light sources". Both should be reworded as a symmetry-level proof of principle. Alternatively, the authors could tie the model to NiTiO3: use d orbitals with a realistic crystal field, map ω onto the measured transition energies, and give a mode-resolved electron–phonon vertex.

7. The Stokes/anti-Stokes argument for a non-magnetic origin is asserted, not derived for this geometry. The claim on p. 7 that the same sign in Stokes and anti-Stokes indicates a non-magnetic origin, "in contrast to magnetic ROA where the sign reverses", is taken from molecular ROA (Refs. [29–31]), which concerns different scattering geometries and observables. The reproduction of this result in the model (Fig. 4 inset) is uninformative, because δω = ±10^-3 is far smaller than the smearing η = 0.01, so Stokes and anti-Stokes are identical by continuity. Measuring at 295 K, well above T_N, already makes a magnetic origin unlikely. However, the time-reversal argument for cross-circular backscattering from a T-even axial order should be written out, for example in the SM, and the model should be evaluated at δω comparable to the real phonon energy relative to the resonance width.

8. The mode-dependent signs, front/back asymmetries and error bars need discussion. In Table S4:
   - E_g^(4) does not reverse between the faces (-0.01 front, -0.05 back);
   - E_g^(2) and E_g^(3) reverse with unequal magnitudes (-0.16/+0.11 and 0.21/-0.27);
   - E_g^(1) and E_g^(2) have opposite signs on the same face.

   The quoted uncertainty of ±0.02 for every peak sits poorly with the pixel-to-pixel scatter in the single-domain maps of Fig. 5(a,b), where many pixels within a nominally uniform domain range from about 0.2 to 0.8. The authors should:
   - state how g_ROA is extracted (peak fit, integrated window, background subtraction) and how the error bar is obtained (repeat spectra, positions, fit uncertainty);
   - give the spread across positions in each domain;
   - discuss what sets the sign and size of each mode's ROA, or at least state that the model does not address this.

9. There is effectively one sample. All quantitative data come from one 60-µm single-domain crystal. The multidomain crystal was made by annealing the same crystal at 1620 K, above T_C. In the multidomain map (Fig. 5c), |g_ROA| within domains appears clearly smaller (about 0.2–0.5) than in the single-domain crystal (about 0.6–0.8 in Fig. 5a). This may reflect partial Ni/Ti antisite disorder after the anneal, finite spot averaging, or domain overlap through the thickness. If the authors can show that the reduction follows a reduced degree of cation order (for example from X-ray superlattice intensity), that would be strong extra evidence that g_ROA scales with the ferroaxial order parameter. They should also show the key front/back reversal on at least one more independently grown crystal.

C. Minor

10. Values of g_ROA for E_g^(1) disagree across the paper: 0.96/-1.04 in Table S4, about 0.6–0.8 in the Fig. 5 maps (whose colour scale saturates at ±0.8), and about 0.72 in Fig. S10(a). The x = 0 spectrum in Fig. S10(b) (LR ≈ 130, RL ≈ 48 counts over a baseline of about 15) implies g ≈ 1. The authors should explain the differences, for example spectral window, spot size or fitting.

11. Several experimental parameters are missing: laser power at the sample for each wavelength, objective NA, spot size for the spectra in Fig. 3, integration times, and the QWP model. With resonant absorption at 785 nm, the authors should either estimate local heating (for example from the anti-Stokes/Stokes ratio) or show that g_ROA does not depend on power.

12. Small errors in figures and equations:
   - The caption of Fig. S8 gives δω = 10^-3, which by the convention of Eq. S26 means anti-Stokes, yet panel (a) appears identical to the Stokes curve of Fig. 4 (δω = -10^-3).
   - In Eqs. S14–S15, with e = (1, ±i)/√2 the amplitude is 2R1, so the intensity is 4|R1|², not 2|R1|². This does not affect the ratio.
   - Eq. 1 and the Fig. 5 caption write g_ROA differently; one form should be used throughout.

13. Two side claims in the SM need support or rewording. In Sec. S4, the phonons along Γ–Z are called "truly chiral phonons". But 1E and 2E stay degenerate, the net angular momentum is zero, and there is no band splitting, so it is unclear in what sense the definition of Ref. [32] is met. In Table S1, attributing the 738 cm^-1 feature A_g^(5)' to an "amorphous phase" in a flux-grown single crystal needs evidence, or an alternative assignment such as a second-order or disorder-activated mode.

14. The comparison with molecular NROA (g ~ 10^-3) on p. 7 is not like for like: the observables and the resonance conditions differ. The relevant benchmark is the cross-circular asymmetry reported for 1T-TaS2 (Refs. [3–6]) and NiCo2TeO6 (Ref. [7]), and the authors should give those values for context.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a focal-depth series of g_ROA(E_g^(1)) through the 60-µm single-domain crystal, and co-registered electrogyration and Raman maps of one multidomain region. Together these would settle both "bulk" and "domain-resolved".
2. Rewrite the mechanism paragraph on p. 8 around the explicit result I_LR - I_RL ∝ Im(c* d). Make clear that ferroaxial symmetry allows d ≠ 0 and that resonance supplies the phase, and support this with model curves of g against t_ax and against η.
3. Add a measured c-axis absorption spectrum and, if possible, an excitation-energy scan of g_ROA through the d-d band, together with per-mode Ni displacement weights from the DFT eigenvectors.
4. Add a D3d reference crystal measured under identical 785-nm conditions, and report polarization extinction ratios at each wavelength, so that instrumental asymmetry is bounded directly rather than inferred from off-sample pixels.
