Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report a large difference between LR and RL cross-circular Raman intensities of the Eg phonons in single-crystal NiTiO3 (g_ROA up to about 1 for Eg(1) at 785 nm). NiTiO3 is a centrosymmetric, non-magnetic ferroaxial crystal (point group -3). The sign of g_ROA reverses between the front and back faces of a single-domain crystal, and maps of g_ROA on a multidomain crystal show a domain-like contrast. A symmetry analysis and a generic tight-binding model support the claim that the effect is allowed within the electric-dipole approximation once the mirror symmetry is broken by ferroaxial order. If the effect is firmly tied to the ferroaxial order parameter, it would give a simple, all-optical, phase-sensitive probe of ferroaxial domains with micron resolution. The results also bear on the debated origin of "chiral" Raman signals in 1T-TaS2 and related materials.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The effect is large and clean. The sign reversal between the front and back faces of the same single-domain crystal is internally controlled (Fig. 3, Fig. S6, Table S4): for Eg(1), Eg(3) and Eg(5), the front and back values are nearly equal and opposite.
- Within the 785 nm data, the selection rules are respected. Ag modes appear only in LL/RR and show no circular difference (Fig. S7). Eg modes appear only in LR/RL (Fig. 2c).
- The high-resolution line scan across a domain wall (Fig. S10) shows a sharp sign change of g_ROA and raw LR/RL spectra on both sides. This is convincing evidence that the contrast is spatially local and intrinsic.
- The wavelength dependence (785 nm large, 532 nm small, 633 nm about zero) points clearly to resonance with the Ni2+ d-d absorption.
- The symmetry derivation of the Eg Raman tensors in the circular basis (Sec. S3) is explicit and easy to check.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The mechanism in the paper leaves out absorption (a complex Raman tensor), which is a necessary second ingredient. This changes both the main claim and its novelty relative to Ref. [4].
   - **The symmetry argument.** For the in-plane Eg Raman tensor allowed in -3, written in the standard form [[c, d],[d, -c]] and [[d, -c],[-c, -d]], a short calculation gives I_LR ∝ |c - i d|^2 and I_RL ∝ |c + i d|^2. So I_LR - I_RL ∝ Im(c d*).
     - In -3m, the mirror forces d = 0, so there is no ROA. Ferroaxial order is therefore required.
     - Even in -3, if c and d are real, as they are off resonance in a transparent crystal, there is no ROA. A relative phase between c and d, which requires dissipation or resonance, is therefore also required.
   - **The paper's own data and model show this.** The model in Fig. 4 gives exactly I_LR = I_RL = 0.5 below the JDOS threshold (omega < 1.38). The experiment gives g_ROA about 0 at 633 nm, where absorption is minimal (Table S4).
   - **How the text frames it.** The main text (p. 8-9) and the abstract present resonance as an "enhancement" of an effect that "originates from the ferroaxial order". The Introduction (p. 3) states that the mechanism is "unknown" and sets the paper against Ref. [4], which attributed ROA in 1T-TaS2 to complex Raman tensors arising from absorption. Read this way, the present results look like a confirmation of the Ref. [4] mechanism, with ferroaxial order supplying the symmetry-allowed off-diagonal element d. This is still valuable, but it is a different and narrower claim than "ROA arises from ferroaxial order".
   - **What to do.**
     - State the phenomenological condition (ferroaxial order needed for d ≠ 0; absorption needed for Im(c d*) ≠ 0) in the main text.
     - Revise the abstract and conclusion so that the probe is described as working under resonant or absorbing conditions.
     - Explain explicitly what the tight-binding calculation adds beyond this condition.
     - State clearly how the present mechanism relates to, and differs from, Refs. [4] and [7].

2. The correlation between the Raman map of the multidomain crystal and the ferroaxial domains is only qualitative, and the reference image is not from the same measurement.
   - **What the paper shows.** Fig. 5(d) is an electrogyration image "adapted from Ref. [23] (image inverted from the original)". The text says the Raman map in Fig. 5(c) "closely matches" it.
   - **Why this is not yet convincing.**
     - Inspection does not make the match obvious. Fig. 5(d) has sharp, straight stripe-like domains and large blocks, while Fig. 5(c) shows diffuse patches at 20 µm sampling.
     - It is not stated whether the two images show the same crystal, the same area and the same orientation.
     - "Inverted" is ambiguous: a colour-sign inversion and a spatial mirror flip have very different implications.
   - **Why it matters.** The abstract and conclusion rest the "correlates with the ferroaxial domain structure" claim and the domain-imaging application on this comparison.
   - **What to do.**
     - Measure electrogyration (or circularly polarized SHG, Ref. [16]) and Raman on the same region of the same crystal.
     - Co-register the two maps, report a pixel-by-pixel correlation, and state which sign of g_ROA corresponds to A+ and which to A-, as defined by an independent technique.
     - If this cannot be done, at minimum state precisely what "inverted" means and whether it is the same sample and area.
   - **A related unexplained point.** The g_ROA magnitudes in Fig. 5(c) appear to be about ±0.3-0.4. This is far below the about ±0.75 measured on the same crystal in Fig. S10(a) and the about ±1 in the single-domain crystal (Table S4). With roughly 100 µm domains and a 3.3 µm spot, lateral averaging cannot account for this. Possible causes are domains stacked through the thickness within the probed depth, a different degree of Ni/Ti order after the 1620 K anneal, or a different focal depth. Please explain the difference.

B. Should address

3. Instrumental polarization asymmetry is not quantified, and the 532 nm and 633 nm conclusions depend on it.
   - **The optical path.** In the setup of Fig. S1, a non-polarizing beam splitter sits between the polarizer/analyzer and a single QWP. Retardance in the beam splitter, and a QWP whose retardance is not exactly λ/4 at each of the three wavelengths, both produce an LR/RL efficiency imbalance of fixed sign.
   - **What the 785 nm data already show.** The front and back values in Table S4 give an estimate of this offset: their mean is -0.04 for Eg(1), -0.03 for Eg(2), -0.03 for Eg(3), -0.03 for Eg(4) and 0.00 for Eg(5). This is comparable to or larger than the quoted error of ±0.02.
   - **Why it matters at 532 nm.** There the reported values (0.10, 0.01, 0.10, 0.05, 0.09) are front-surface only, and all have the same sign. They therefore cannot be separated from an instrumental offset. This undermines the claim of "discernible ROA signals at 532 nm" (Sec. S5).
   - **A polarization-purity problem at 633 nm.** In Fig. S5 (633 nm, cross-circular), the Ag(3) mode is visible, although it should be forbidden in LR/RL. This indicates imperfect circular polarization or depolarization at that wavelength.
   - **The "outside the sample" control.** On p. 10 the authors argue that g_ROA ~ 0 outside the sample or near cracks shows the signal is not instrumental. This is not a valid control, because where there is no phonon peak the ratio is undefined or noise-dominated.
   - **What to do.**
     - Specify the QWP (zero-order or achromatic, and its design wavelength) and the beam splitter.
     - Report the circular extinction ratio at each wavelength, for example from LL/RR leakage of Eg modes and LR/RL leakage of Ag modes.
     - Measure back-surface spectra at 532 nm and 633 nm.
     - Measure a reference crystal with Eg modes and a mirror symmetry under identical conditions at 785 nm (for example α-Al2O3 or calcite, both -3m), where g_ROA must vanish.
     - Explain how the ±0.02 error was obtained: repeated acquisitions, fit uncertainty, or something else.

4. The mode dependence of g_ROA, which is the most material-specific information in the data, is not explained, and the resonance argument is not quantified.
   - **What the data show.** Table S4 gives very different values and signs for the modes: Eg(1) +0.96, Eg(2) -0.16, Eg(3) +0.21, Eg(4) about 0, Eg(5) +0.47 (front, 785 nm).
   - **What the paper offers.** The only explanation (p. 9) is that Eg(1) "involves the largest displacement of Ni2+ ions", and no numbers are given. Nothing is said about why Eg(2) has the opposite sign or why Eg(4) vanishes.
   - **What the model can and cannot do.** The tight-binding model uses a single, phenomenological phonon-electron vertex X_Q (Eqs. S47-S48), so it cannot distinguish modes. It also gives a sign of I_LR - I_RL that changes several times with ω within the resonant window (Fig. 4: the sign flips near ω = 1.4 and again near 1.75). The model therefore does not predict the sign or magnitude at the experimental excitation energy.
   - **What to do.**
     - Tabulate the Ni (and Ti, O) displacement amplitudes for each Eg eigenvector from the DFT calculation (Fig. S3) next to g_ROA, and test whether the claimed correlation holds, including the sign.
     - Ideally, compute frequency-dependent complex Raman tensors from first principles near the d-d band, or construct a local Ni-centred model of the 3A2g → 3T1g transition, to address the mode-resolved signs.
     - Otherwise, state explicitly that the calculations show symmetry allowance only and are not a quantitative account.

5. The theoretical model does not match the real electronic structure.
   - **The mismatch.** The model uses spinless p orbitals on a two-sublattice triangular lattice, with dispersive bands and a JDOS threshold, in arbitrary units (Eqs. S37-S51). The resonance invoked experimentally is a localized Ni2+ d-d crystal-field (multiplet) excitation.
   - **What this limits.** The claim that the model "supports the experimental results obtained with different light sources" (p. 9) is therefore qualitative at best. No mapping from ω in model units to 785/633/532 nm is given, and the parameters (t1-t6 = 0.1-1.0, t_ax = 0.1, μ = -1.05) are not motivated.
   - **What to do.**
     - Justify the choice of p orbitals and these parameters, or present the model explicitly as a minimal symmetry model.
     - Show how the ROA amplitude scales with t_ax, which should be linear at small t_ax according to Eqs. S64-S65. This would directly support the statement that t_ax is the essential parameter.
     - Show that the ROA vanishes when the smearing η → 0 below the threshold, and that it vanishes when t_ax = 0. This ties in with concern 1.

6. The resonance interpretation rests on three wavelengths, without an absorption spectrum of the measured crystals.
   - **What the paper relies on.** The absorption at 785, 532 and 633 nm is taken from nanocrystallite data (Ref. [25]).
   - **Why this matters.** Absorption determines the probed depth, and so the front/back comparison and the multidomain contrast (concern 2). It also determines whether a complex tensor is expected.
   - **What to do.**
     - Report the c-axis absorption spectrum of the 60 µm crystal and the resulting penetration depth at each wavelength.
     - If possible, measure g_ROA(λ) at several wavelengths across the d-d band (for example with a tunable Ti:sapphire laser, 700-900 nm). Correlating g_ROA(λ) with the absorption line shape would be the most direct test of the resonance mechanism and of the sign changes predicted in Fig. 4.

7. Sample statistics, laser heating and acquisition parameters are not given.
   - **Samples.** Only one single-domain crystal and one multidomain crystal appear to have been measured.
   - **Missing parameters.**
     - The laser power at the sample is not stated anywhere. This matters at 785 nm, where the excitation is resonantly absorbed.
     - The numerical aperture and the integration times are not stated.
     - The confocal depth of focus relative to the 60 µm thickness is not stated.
   - **What to do.**
     - Report these parameters and estimate the local temperature from the Stokes/anti-Stokes ratio of Eg(1) in Fig. 3.
     - Show that g_ROA does not depend on laser power.
     - Show the key front/back result on a second, independently grown single-domain crystal.
     - Report the distribution (histogram) of g_ROA over the single-domain maps.
   - **A magnitude inconsistency.** In Fig. 5(b) the back-surface values appear noticeably smaller in magnitude (lighter blue, about -0.4 to -0.6) than the front (about +0.6 to +0.8). This does not obviously agree with the -1.04 in Table S4.

8. The argument from identical Stokes and anti-Stokes signs is weaker than presented.
   - **Why it is weak.** At 295 K, magnetic ROA is already excluded because T >> T_N = 22.5 K and there is no net magnetization. The Stokes/anti-Stokes sign test (p. 7) therefore adds little, and its transfer from molecular ROA (Refs. [29-31]) to degenerate 1Eg/2Eg phonon pairs is not shown.
   - **The comparison with molecules.** The comparison with molecular NROA (g ~ 10^-3) mixes an orientationally averaged magnetic-dipole/electric-quadrupole interference effect with a resonant electric-dipole effect in an oriented crystal. "Several orders of magnitude larger" is therefore not a like-for-like comparison.
   - **What to do.** Either derive the Stokes/anti-Stokes relation for the 1Eg/2Eg pair in -3 under resonance, or present the test as a consistency check. Qualify the comparison with chiral molecules.

C. Minor

9. **Local symmetry (p. 9).** The text says the ferroaxial distortion lowers the Ni local symmetry "from 3m to 3". In the disordered R-3c corundum structure the cation site (12c) appears to already have site symmetry 3. Please check this statement and correct it if needed, since it is used to motivate the resonance argument.

10. **Sign convention for δω.** In Fig. S8 the caption gives δω = 10^-3, which is the anti-Stokes sign in the paper's convention. Yet panel (a) appears identical to the Stokes curve in the main Fig. 4 (δω = -10^-3). Please check which sign was used.

11. **Phonon relations (Sec. S8, Eqs. S32-S33).** The argument that "time-reversal symmetry ensures Q1(δω) = Q1(-δω)", combined with reality, to give Q1 = Q2 is unclear as written. The conclusion |Q1| = |Q2| follows simply from the 1Eg/2Eg degeneracy enforced by time reversal; please state it that way. Also, Eq. S36 omits the factor of 2 relative to Eq. (1).

12. **"Truly chiral phonons" (Sec. S4).** The authors themselves note that the 1E and 2E branches remain degenerate along Γ-Z and that the net angular momentum is zero. Please justify the term or soften it.

13. **The Ag(5)' mode (Table S1).** It is listed at 738 cm^-1 and attributed to an "amorphous phase", but no separate peak is evident in Fig. 2(c) or Fig. S7. An amorphous phase in a flux-grown single crystal also needs comment.

14. **Intensity mismatch.** The RL intensity of Eg(2) is about 58 in Fig. 2(c) and about 70 in Fig. 3(b), both for the front surface at 785 nm. Please state whether these are the same dataset and whether the intensities are normalized.

15. **Raw spectra in Fig. S10.** In Fig. S10(b), the raw peak heights at x = 0 (LR ~130, RL ~48) would give g_ROA ≈ 0.9, compared with about 0.7 in panel (a). Please state the background subtraction and peak-integration procedure used for g_ROA throughout.

16. **Fig. 4.** The figure uses arbitrary units for ω, and the main text does not state t_ax or the band-structure parameters. Please give them in the caption.

17. **Literature.** Please discuss the related ferroaxial ilmenite MnTiO3 (Refs. [19, 20]) and whether similar ROA is expected there. Ni-free MnTiO3, without the 785 nm d-d resonance, would be a natural test of the resonance requirement.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. In the main text, add a short phenomenological section expressing ROA as Im(c d*) for the -3 Eg tensor. This will make clear which part comes from ferroaxial order (d ≠ 0) and which from absorption (the relative phase), and it will frame the tight-binding results.
2. Replace the borrowed electrogyration image with a co-registered, same-sample comparison. Add a reference -3m crystal measured under identical conditions, so that the instrumental offset is known.
3. Map g_ROA(λ) across the Ni2+ d-d absorption band and compare it with the measured absorption spectrum. Supplement this with mode-resolved Ni displacement analysis, or first-principles resonant Raman tensors, to account for the mode-dependent signs.
4. Consider measuring g_ROA as a function of temperature, and on crystals with different degrees of Ni/Ti order (for example after different anneals). This would test whether g_ROA tracks the ferroaxial order parameter rather than only the resonance condition.
