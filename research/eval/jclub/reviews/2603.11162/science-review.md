A. Must address

1. The electrostatic (bulk-edge Coulomb) contribution to the jump size is not quantified, so the π/2 jump is not tied to e/4 braiding over other sources of a partial phase shift. The paper establishes in "Antidot-tuned interference" and Fig. 2a,d,f,h that adding one electron to the antidot changes the interfering area: at ν = -1 this gives a sawtooth whose amplitude is a sizeable fraction of 2π (θ/2π swinging by about 0.3 in Fig. S4a; the text gives δAB/Φ0 < π). At fractional fillings the constant-phase lines in V_ADG-V_PG are tilted (Fig. 2b,c; Fig. S4b); at ν = -1/2, θ falls by about 2π per ΔV_ADG ≈ 2.6 mV, the voltage that adds one electron, so the interference phase already follows the antidot charge continuously. The same coupling must act when a localized e/2 or e/4 enters during the time traces, so the measured jump is θ_braid plus a non-universal term proportional to the quasiparticle charge and the edge-antidot coupling (as in Nakamura et al. and Halperin et al., Ref. 31). This coupling should be strongest at ν_AD ≈ 0, where the text says a "well-defined edge state between the antidot area and the bulk" exists, so a localized e/2 partly screened, a charge moving between the antidot's own edge and the loop interior, or a fluctuator outside the antidot coupling capacitively to the edge could each produce a jump well below π, possibly near π/2. The paper nonetheless reads the jumps in Figs. 1f,g and 4c,d directly as θ_braid. The systematic excess above 0.25 (0.23-0.33 × 2π in Figs. 4e and S11, rather than 0.25) and the low |θ|/2π = 0.19 and 0.26 at ν = -1/3 (Fig. 3f; Fig. S9g,j) point to such corrections. The ν = -1/3 control (Fig. 3f) helps but was taken at 11 T rather than 8 T and in a different state, so it does not rule out renormalization at ν = -1/2 (and its staircase value is itself questionable; see concern 11). The authors should: estimate the coupling ratio from the integer sawtooth amplitude, the tilt and period of the V_ADG-V_PG pattern (dθ/dV_ADG with ΔV_ADG in Fig. 2b) and the ν = -1/3 staircase, scaled by quasiparticle charge, and show what jump a localized e/2 and e/4 would produce at ν_AD ≈ 0; state the resulting correction and its uncertainty for each assigned θ_braid and show that the residual is consistent with π and π/2; and show that the jump size does not change with V_PG window or with B along α_c inside the ν = -1/2 plateau at fixed ν_AD, since a topological phase should stay fixed while an electrostatic one would drift.

2. The interfering charge is not measured at the antidot settings where π/2 jumps appear, and the plunger period changes between settings. The e/2 interfering charge comes from the flux periodicity in Fig. 1c,d and Fig. S3c,g,k, taken with V_TG = V_ADG, i.e. with the antidot inactive. The π/2 jumps appear only at V_ADG ≈ 0.48-0.50 V (ν_AD ≈ 0.06-0.10). The Conclusions state that the antidot does not change the interfering charge, but no data are shown, and they themselves suggest the sharper potential at ν_AD ≈ 0 "could promote tunneling of e/4". This matters because an interfering e/4 encircling a localized e/2 also gives π/2 (charge sector (1/4)(1/2)·... = π/2, with a trivial Ising contribution from an even e/2), as can e/4 around e/4 in some channels. The V_PG period also visibly differs: about 0.044 V at V_ADG = 0.235 V (Fig. S12a; Fig. 4a, about 7-8 periods over 0.3 V) against about 0.06 V at V_ADG = 0.487 V (Figs. 1g, 4b, S11, about 5 periods), a change of roughly 1.4 that is not discussed and could come from either lever arm or interfering charge. The authors should measure the B-periodicity along α_c, with the same 2D-FFT analysis as in Fig. S3, at one ν_AD ≈ -1/2 setting (e.g. 0.235 V) and one ν_AD ≈ 0.06 setting (e.g. 0.487 V, the Fig. 4b conditions), report Φ0/ΔB for each including the expected small change in effective area once the 0.1 µm² island is depleted, and show that ΔΦ ≈ 2Φ0 holds there. Without this, assigning π/2 to "e/2 encircling e/4" rather than "e/4 encircling e/2" is not supported.

3. The jump sizes, especially π/2, come from two-ridge histogram fits rather than from individual events, and the fit is neither validated nor able to represent the processes claimed. The values of θ_braid in Figs. 1f,g, 3f and 4e come from the SI7 procedure: a primary ridge is found, a second ridge is taken from "additional peaks well separated from the primary ridge", and both are fitted with a common amplitude and a common polynomial phase, with 1st-99th percentile clipping and error bars from "the residual variance of this fit". Problems: (i) requiring good separation can bias the offset away from small values, and when the ridges are only π/2 apart they overlap strongly; when P_ex is 0.04-0.05 (Fig. S11a,d) the second ridge is barely populated. (ii) Only two phase states can be captured. If e/4 occupancy can change by ±1 or by 2, the expected phases relative to the ground state are 0, ±π/2 and π, and the Introduction states that at ν_AD ≈ 0 "π/2 phase jumps occur in addition to π jumps, indicating the presence of two distinct processes" (two e/4 entering would also give π). Yet Fig. 4e and Fig. S11 show only a single secondary ridge at |θ|/2π ≈ 0.23-0.33 at every V_ADG, and no π jumps are identified in that regime. (iii) The scatter (0.23, 0.27, 0.33, 0.28, 0.27, 0.28, 0.26 in Fig. S11a-g; mean about 0.27) exceeds the residual-variance error bars, which are visibly smaller than the spread between neighbouring V_ADG points in Figs. 3f and 4e; Fig. S11c gives 0.33 at V_ADG = 0.489 V, which is 2π/3 rather than π/2, yet the text describes the majority as "close to π/2". Fig. 4d shows only a single event. The authors should extract the phase of each sweep, or of each segment before and after a jump (fitting windows as in Fig. 4c,d, or a sliding-window 1D FFT as in SI4), for all sweeps in Figs. S6, S7 and S8; show histograms of individual jump sizes with event counts for each V_ADG, stating how many events support π/2 and π peaks at ν_AD ≈ 0 (a multimodal distribution would show directly how many occupancy states take part); validate the two-ridge fit on synthetic data with known π/2 and π shifts and the measured noise, testing the common-amplitude constraint and the percentile clipping; and reconcile the statement about coexisting π and π/2 jumps with the data, or remove it.

B. Should address

4. The π/2 phase does not identify a non-Abelian e/4, and the link to non-Abelian statistics is overstated. The abstract and Introduction describe the localized e/4 as "expected to be non-Abelian", and the Conclusions frame the work as a step toward non-Abelian braiding. SI10 (Eq. S4) shows that θ_braid(e/2, e/4) = ±π/2 for every ℓ, with the π/2 for even-parity e/2 coming entirely from the charge sector; the same value follows for Abelian paired orders such as Halperin 331 and the strong-pairing K = 8 state. The page-5 cancellation argument between even- and odd-parity e/2 contributions is the only Ising-specific element. The measurement therefore constrains the charge of the localized quasiparticle, not whether it is non-Abelian, and cannot distinguish Pfaffian from anti-Pfaffian. The authors should state explicitly which candidate orders (Pfaffian, anti-Pfaffian, 331, 113, K = 8) predict π/2 and which predict a ±π/2 sign structure or cancellation; temper "expected to be non-Abelian" in the abstract and Introduction; state the anti-Pfaffian assignment on page 8, which rests on daughter states from other work (Refs. 34-40), as an assumption and say whether anything in the present data favours it over the Pfaffian; and say which further measurement (visibility suppression for odd e/4 number, or e/4 interference) would discriminate.

5. It is not shown that the fluctuating anyons sit in the antidot, so the "selectivity" and "local control over the number of anyons" claims are not supported. At ν_AD ≈ ν = -1/2 (V_ADG = 0.235 V; Fig. 4a "ν_AD = ν") the antidot filling matches the bulk and the device is nearly a uniform FPI, so the π jumps (Fig. 4a, Fig. S7c-l) could come from localized e/2 anywhere in the loop or from extrinsic charge traps near the loop, as seen in other graphene FPIs. Fig. 1c shows clean AB with V_TG = V_ADG, but its time stability is not reported. As presented, "selectivity" (abstract, p. 5) amounts to jumps of different size at different V_ADG. The authors should state where the localized anyons are believed to reside; show time-resolved sweeps with V_TG = V_ADG exactly, compared with ν_AD = -0.51, and show that these have no π jumps; compare the lever arms of P_ex peaks and jump rate with respect to V_ADG versus V_PG and V_TG (an antidot-bound state should couple far more strongly to V_ADG); show how the degeneracy positions move with B; and temper "local control over the number of anyons" in the abstract to what is shown, since the gate selects which type fluctuates and how often rather than deterministically adding a single anyon.

6. The V_ADG dependence of P_ex does not match the stated N/N+1 degeneracy picture and is undersampled; a fine scan would give charge-sensitive evidence independent of the jump magnitude. At ν = -1/3 the P_ex maxima in Fig. 3f are about 5 mV apart, which the text attributes to near-degeneracy of N and N+1, but the same paper gives an electron-addition period of 2.2-2.7 mV (Fig. 2c,e; SI4; Fig. S4c), i.e. one e/3 every 0.7-0.9 mV, consistent with the ν_AD labels in Figs. S5-S6 (about 26 mV for Δν_AD ≈ 0.044 over 0.1 µm² at 11 T). A 5 mV spacing matches neither, and the text does not explain why most addition points show no fluctuations; it also notes that the sign pattern of θ around the peaks "does not occur consistently". At ν = -1/2 with ν_AD ≈ -1/2 (Fig. 4e), P_ex rises smoothly from about 0 to 0.46 over V_ADG = 0.25 → 0.18 V, roughly 25 charging periods, with no oscillation resolved, suggesting fluctuators in the bulk or with weak gate coupling. Near ν_AD ≈ 0 (Fig. 4e, Figs. S8, S11), V_ADG is sampled every 2 mV, coarser than the expected e/4 addition period of about 0.65 mV, so the peaks may be aliased. The authors should take a dense V_ADG scan (≤ 0.1-0.3 mV steps) over at least one electron period at ν = -1/3 and at ν = -1/2, ν_AD ≈ 0, mapping P_ex and the sign of θ; estimate the lever arm of the fluctuators from the P_ex peak spacing; and show whether degeneracies recur with the expected quasiparticle period with a sign flip of the jump across each. Recurrence every e/4 with sign flips would strongly support the e/4 assignment; recurrence every e/2 would point the other way.

8. The results come from a single device, field and cooldown, with no temperature or bias dependence. Only one FPI is shown (the Methods refers to FPIs in the plural), and the ν = -1/2 dynamics are only at B = 8 T. Because the fluctuating anyons are probably pinned by local disorder, reproducibility is the key test of the claimed control, and temperature dependence would show whether switching is thermally activated across the antidot-edge barrier between localized-anyon configurations (the two-level picture, page 7) rather than driven by the 50 pA excitation or extrinsic fluctuators, and would give the energy scale of the near-degeneracy. The authors should state how many devices and cooldowns were measured; show whether the π and π/2 regimes reappear after thermal cycling, at a second field inside the ν = -1/2 plateau (e.g. 7 T, as in Fig. S1c), and ideally in a second device; and report P_ex and dwell times (t_ex) at two or three temperatures and excitation levels.

10. There is no null control for extrinsic telegraph noise. A charge trap in the hBN or near the QPCs can produce random telegraph phase shifts of fixed, gate-dependent size. The consistency of the π jumps across V_ADG argues against this, but only weakly in the π/2 regime where values scatter (concern 3). The authors should show repeated sweeps at ν = -1 (and if possible ν = -2/3) at the same V_ADG and V_TG settings that give π/2 jumps at ν = -1/2, which should be free of jumps or show only trivial (2π-equivalent) jumps, and show that the π/2 jumps follow the FQH state when B and density are moved together along α_c.

11. At ν = -1/3 the staircase value Δθ/2π = 0.33 ± 0.02 appears to follow from the extraction method rather than measure the braiding phase. Fig. 2e, SI4 and Fig. S4b-d describe θ decreasing roughly linearly by 2π per ΔV_ADG ≈ 2.7 mV, with steps located as minima of dθ/dV_ADG, three per period. If the phase advances by 2π per period and three events are evenly spaced, consecutive minima differ by 2π/3 whatever the discontinuity at each step. The paper presents 0.33 as confirming θ_braid = 2π/3 (Fig. 2i, Conclusions), and uses ν = -1/3 as a reference. The authors should separate the continuous slope from the discontinuity (fit a linear baseline within plateaus and measure the jump at each step), state the total phase per ΔV_ADG period, and say so if the discontinuity alone is not 2π/3. They should quote the ±0.93 and ±1.02 × 2π/3 values in Fig. 3b,c with uncertainties and discuss the low |θ|/2π = 0.19 at V_ADG = 0.467 V (Fig. S9g), which disagrees with 1/3 well beyond its error bar.

13. The parity argument on page 5 is untested. The authors argue that even-parity e/2 anyons dominate the interference, since otherwise the two parities would cancel for an odd number of e/4, with dominance attributed to scaling dimensions at the QPCs. This would show up as different visibilities in the two branches, but SI7 imposes a common amplitude. The sign of the π/2 jumps also varies between V_ADG values (Fig. S11: -0.23, +0.27, +0.33, +0.28, -0.27, +0.26, -0.26) with no correlation with P_ex; observing both signs is equally consistent with either parity (e/4 entering or leaving) or with switches of interfering-anyon parity. The authors should fit the branch amplitudes separately and report the visibility ratio (comparing the oscillation amplitude of the two ridges in Fig. 1g would be a first test; with equal parity weights an odd e/4 number should suppress visibility), and either tie the jump sign to the direction of tunnelling, for example through a fine V_ADG scan across a degeneracy, or remove the parity claim.
