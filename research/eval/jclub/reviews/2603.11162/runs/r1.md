Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors embed a graphite-gated antidot inside a bilayer-graphene Fabry-Pérot interferometer (FPI) and use the antidot filling to change which localized anyons fluctuate inside the interference loop. At ν = -1/2 they report telegraph-like phase jumps of about π when ν_AD ≈ -1/2, which they assign to localized e/2 anyons. When ν_AD ≈ 0 the jumps are about π/2, which they assign to localized e/4 anyons. At ν = -1/3 they report jumps of about 2π/3. Resolving individual phase slips at an even-denominator filling, and selecting by gate which localized quasiparticle type braids with the interfering e/2, would be a clear advance over earlier even-denominator interference work, where no phase slips were resolved. It is a necessary step toward interferometric tests of non-Abelian statistics. Whether the paper's interpretation holds depends on three things: how much of each jump is braiding phase rather than area change, what charge interferes in the ν_AD ≈ 0 configuration, and how firmly the π/2 value is established.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new device concept: an antidot gate inside a graphene FPI with seven independently biased graphite sections (Fig. 1a,b). It gives a tuning knob that GaAs interferometers lack.
- At ν = -1/2 the phase jumps are discrete and resolved in time (Fig. 4a-d; Figs. S7, S8, S10, S11). In the ν_AD ≈ -1/2 regime the jump sizes are tightly clustered near π (θ/2π = 0.46-0.49 across nine V_ADG values, Fig. S10). This is the cleanest evidence the paper offers.
- The raw repeated sweeps for every V_ADG setting are shown in the SI (Figs. S5-S8). The analysis is therefore transparent, and readers can judge the stability of the phase.
- The integer, ν = -1/3 and ν = -1/2 cases are compared in the same device (Fig. 2). This gives a useful internal reference: sawtooth phase at the integer filling, staircase at ν = -1/3.
- SI10 gives a concise theoretical framework for the expected phases.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The jump size is not corrected for the area change caused by the added charge. Section "Antidot-tuned interference" and Fig. 2a,d,f,h establish that adding one electron to the antidot changes the interfering area. At ν = -1 this produces a sawtooth phase whose amplitude is a sizeable fraction of 2π (the text gives δAB/Φ0 < π). The same Coulomb coupling must also act when a localized e/2 or e/4 enters the antidot region during the time traces. The measured jump is then θ_braid plus a non-universal term set by the bulk-edge coupling, as in the analyses of Nakamura et al. and Halperin et al. (Ref. 31). The paper reads the jump in Figs. 1f,g and 4c,d directly as θ_braid. A non-universal shift of a few tenths of π would make a π/2 jump indistinguishable from other combinations; it could, for example, partly account for the measured values of 0.23-0.33 × 2π rather than 0.25 × 2π (Fig. S11). The authors should:
   - estimate the area-change contribution per e/4 and per e/2 quantitatively, using the integer sawtooth amplitude and the slope of the tilted baseline in Fig. 2b (dθ/dV_ADG together with ΔV_ADG), scaled by the quasiparticle charge;
   - state the resulting correction and its uncertainty for each assigned θ_braid;
   - show that the residual is consistent with π and π/2.
   The ν = -1/3 case could serve as a calibration, but see concern 5.

2. The interfering charge is not measured in the ν_AD ≈ 0 configuration where π/2 jumps appear. The e/2 interfering charge comes from the flux periodicity in Fig. 1c,d and Fig. S3c,g,k. Those data were taken with V_TG = V_ADG, that is, with the antidot inactive. The π/2 jumps, however, are seen only at V_ADG ≈ 0.48-0.50 V (ν_AD ≈ 0.06-0.10). This matters because an interfering e/4 encircling a localized e/2 also yields a phase of π/2 (charge sector only: (1/4)(1/2)·2π·... = π/2, with a trivial Ising contribution), and so does an interfering e/4 encircling an e/4 in some channels. The Conclusions themselves suggest that a sharper potential at ν_AD = 0 "could promote tunneling of e/4". The plunger-gate oscillation period also visibly differs between antidot settings: there are about 8 periods over 0.9-1.2 V in Figs. 1f/4a but about 5 in Figs. 1g/4b. The interfering configuration therefore changes with V_ADG. The authors should:
   - measure the magnetic-field (AB) periodicity along α_c at V_ADG ≈ 0.487 V, and preferably at 0.235 V, with the same 2D-FFT analysis as in Fig. S3;
   - show that ΔΦ ≈ 2Φ0 also holds there.
   Without this, assigning π/2 to "e/2 encircling e/4" rather than "e/4 encircling e/2" is not supported.

3. The π/2 value and its claimed coexistence with π jumps at ν_AD ≈ 0 are not firmly established.
   - The Introduction states that at ν_AD ≈ 0 "π/2 phase jumps occur in addition to π jumps, indicating the presence of two distinct processes". Yet Fig. 4e and Fig. S11 show only a single secondary ridge with |θ|/2π ≈ 0.23-0.33 at every V_ADG, and no π jumps in that regime are identified anywhere.
   - The extracted values scatter: 0.23, 0.27, 0.33, 0.28, 0.27, 0.28, 0.26 (Fig. S11a-g). The mean is about 0.27, and one point equals 1/3. The error bars come from "the residual variance of this fit" (SI7), with a common amplitude and a common polynomial phase imposed on both ridges. When the ridges are only π/2 apart, they overlap strongly. Neither the common-amplitude constraint nor the 1st-99th percentile clipping has been tested for bias in that case.
   - The authors should report the jump size from individual identified events, not only from histogram ridge fits. One route is to fit each sweep's phase in windows before and after t0, as in Fig. 4c,d, and give a histogram of jump sizes over all events at each V_ADG.
   - They should validate the two-ridge fit on synthetic data with known shifts of π/2 and π and the measured noise level.
   - They should reconcile the statement about coexisting π and π/2 jumps with the data, or remove it.

B. Should address

4. The π/2 phase does not identify a non-Abelian e/4, and the parity argument depends on the model. The abstract and Introduction describe the localized e/4 as "expected to be non-Abelian", and the Conclusions frame the work as a step toward non-Abelian braiding. A π/2 phase for e/2 around e/4 also follows, via the charge sector, in Abelian paired candidates such as the 331 state or the strong-pairing K = 8 state. The page-5 argument about cancellation between even- and odd-parity e/2 contributions is specific to Ising-type neutral sectors. The authors should:
   - state explicitly which candidate orders (Pfaffian, anti-Pfaffian, 331, 113, K = 8) predict π/2, and which predict a ±π/2 sign structure or cancellation;
   - make clear that the present data constrain the charge of the localized quasiparticle, not whether it is non-Abelian.
   The anti-Pfaffian assignment (page 8) rests on daughter states from other work (Refs. 34-40). The authors should say whether anything in the present data favours it over the Pfaffian.

5. At ν = -1/3, Δθ/2π = 0.33 ± 0.02 appears to follow from the extraction method rather than measure the braiding phase. Fig. S4b,c and SI4 describe θ decreasing roughly linearly by 2π per ΔV_ADG ≈ 2.7 mV. The steps are located as minima of dθ/dV_ADG, and there are three per period. If the phase advances by 2π per period and three events are equally spaced, then the phase difference between consecutive minima is 2π/3 whatever the size of the discontinuity at each step. The paper presents the 0.33 value as confirming θ_braid = 2π/3 (Fig. 2i, Conclusions). The authors should separate the continuous slope from the discontinuity, for example by fitting a linear baseline between steps and measuring the jump at each step. If the discontinuity alone is not 2π/3, they should say so. The same caution applies to the ±0.93 and ±1.02 × 2π/3 values in Fig. 3b,c, which should be quoted with uncertainties.

6. The spacing of the excitation-probability peaks does not match the charging period. At ν = -1/3, Fig. 3f shows P_ex maxima separated by about 5 mV in V_ADG, and the text says this reflects near-degeneracy of the N and N+1 states. The antidot addition period, however, is 2.7 mV per electron, or about 0.9 mV per e/3 (Fig. 2e, Fig. S4c). If the fluctuating anyon were the one added to the antidot, degeneracies should recur every 0.9 mV. Similarly, at ν = -1/2 with ν_AD ≈ -1/2 (Fig. 4e), P_ex rises smoothly from about 0 to 0.46 over V_ADG = 0.25 → 0.18 V. That range covers roughly 25 charging periods, and no oscillation is resolved. This suggests that the fluctuating anyons sit in the bulk, or at the antidot edge with weak gate coupling, rather than being charged one by one by the antidot. The authors should:
   - state where the localized anyons are believed to reside;
   - estimate their lever arm from the P_ex peak spacing;
   - temper "local control over the number of anyons" (abstract) to what is shown. The gate selects which type fluctuates and how often; it does not deterministically add a single anyon.

7. There is no null control for extrinsic telegraph noise. A charge trap in the hBN or near the QPCs can produce random telegraph phase shifts of fixed size that depend on gate voltages. The consistency of the π jumps across V_ADG argues against this, but only weakly for the π/2 regime, where the values scatter (concern 3). The authors should show repeated sweeps at ν = -1 (and, if possible, ν = -2/3) at the same V_ADG and V_TG settings that give π/2 jumps at ν = -1/2. Those sweeps should either be free of jumps or show jumps of 2π-equivalent (trivial) size. They should also show that the π/2 jumps follow the FQH state when B and density are moved together along α_c.

8. The results come from one device and one cooldown, without B or temperature dependence. Only one FPI is described (Methods), and the ν = -1/2 dynamics are shown only at B = 8 T. The authors should:
   - state how many devices and cooldowns were measured;
   - show whether the π and π/2 regimes reappear after thermal cycling and at a second field along the ν = -1/2 plateau, for example 7 T, as used in Fig. S1c;
   - report a temperature or bias dependence of the switching rate (t_ex). This would test whether the switching is thermally activated between localized-anyon configurations, as in the two-level picture (page 7), and is not driven by the 50 pA excitation.

9. The ν_AD calibration and the meaning of ν_AD > 0 are unclear. ν_AD is quoted to three decimals (Figs. S5-S11). It is apparently converted from V_ADG using C_TG = 0.647 mF/m² (from the Středa slope, Fig. S2c) and the lithographic antidot area of 0.1 µm². That calculation ignores fringing fields, the 40 nm trench, and any offset between the TG and ADG graphite sections. In the integer test, the same conversion gave ΔQ ≈ 0.89e rather than e, so the conversion carries roughly 10% systematic error. The regime labelled "ν_AD ≈ 0" in which π/2 jumps appear is in fact ν_AD = 0.06-0.10. At ν_AD = -0.016 (Fig. S7p) no jumps are seen. In this hole-doped system, ν_AD > 0 would mean electron doping, a p-n-like island. Other inconsistencies:
   - Fig. 1e is described as ν_AD ≈ 0, but V_ADG = 0.4 V corresponds to ν_AD = -0.132 in Fig. S7o.
   The authors should:
   - explain how ν_AD is defined and calibrated, including its zero;
   - give its uncertainty;
   - discuss whether the π/2 regime corresponds to a depleted island or a weakly electron-doped one, since this changes which edge separates the antidot from the bulk.

10. There are no direct time traces or dwell-time statistics at ν = -1/2. The two-level interpretation and t_ex ≈ 11 s are shown only for ν = -1/3 (Fig. 3a). For ν = -1/2, the dynamics are inferred from 90 s sweeps. The authors should:
    - show R_D(t) at fixed V_PG for both antidot regimes;
    - give dwell-time distributions for both states and test whether they are single-exponential, as expected for a two-level system;
    - examine, in the ν_AD ≈ 0 regime, whether more than two levels occur. With both e/2 and e/4 fluctuations, a four-state pattern (shifts of 0, π/2, π, 3π/2) would be expected, and its absence or presence bears directly on concern 3.

11. The QPCs do not reach full transmission at ν = -1/2. Fig. S1c shows G_D saturating near 0.38 e²/h rather than 0.5 e²/h with both QPCs open. The authors attribute this to narrow QPCs, but it may instead indicate partial reflection or non-equilibration of part of the edge, which matters for an edge with a neutral sector. The operating transmission is defined relative to G_xy (t = 0.7-0.9). The authors should:
    - clarify whether the weak-backscattering form ΔR_D ∝ cos θ (Eq. 1) still applies;
    - say whether this partial transmission is what determines which e/2 parity sector interferes, given that this is central to the argument that one parity dominates (page 5).

12. The P_ex extraction may be biased for small phase shifts. SI9 separates the populations by a horizontal threshold R_D0 and averages over "plateau regions". This method was designed for π shifts (Fig. S12), where the ridges are anti-phase. For π/2 shifts, the two ridges cross R_D0 at different V_PG, and the plateaus are short. The authors should show the Fig. S12 analysis for a π/2 case (for example V_ADG = 0.487 V), or obtain P_ex from the time traces (concern 10).

C. Minor

13. Fig. 1g shows θ/2π = -0.27, while Fig. S11b, which is the same V_ADG = 0.487 V, shows +0.27, and the text (page 8) gives θ ≈ 0.54π. Fig. 1g also gives P_e = 0.42, while Fig. S11b gives 0.41. The signs and values should be made consistent, with a statement of the sign convention.

14. The Fig. 4b label gives ν_AD = 0.06, while Fig. S8c and Fig. S11b give 0.069 for V_ADG = 0.487 V. The Fig. S10a caption says ν_AD = -0.654, while the panel and Fig. S7a give -0.645.

15. On page 6, the text says the ν = -1/2 aperiodic jumps in Fig. 2b "have not been reported before", but their sizes are not quantified. A jump-size analysis of the V_ADG-V_PG maps in Fig. 2b, as done for the time-domain data, would support this claim.

16. Page 8 reads "for which we observe distinct phase jumps, unlike the ν = -1/3 case (see SI6)". The comparison is unclear, since ν = -1/3 also shows jumps (Fig. S6). Please rephrase.

17. The Conclusions use ν_ADG; elsewhere the notation is ν_AD.

18. SI2 refers to a back-gate voltage V_BG = -0.3 V, but the main text says that "a single continuous graphite gate controls the filling". Please clarify the gate configuration. Also, Fig. S2c contains a fourth point near 1/ν = -1 that the text does not discuss.

19. SI5 says the measurements are stable "for more than 2 hours". Please also state the total acquisition time and order for each V_ADG series, so that drift in V_ADG history can be excluded as a source of the P_ex trends.

20. The data are "available upon request". For a claim of this weight, please deposit the raw time-resolved data and the analysis code.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a short section that converts each measured jump into θ_braid after correcting for the Coulomb area change, with explicit uncertainties, for ν = -1/3, -1/2 (π) and -1/2 (π/2). Use this as the main quantitative result.
2. Measure the flux periodicity in the π/2 configuration. If e/4 interference can be induced, as the Conclusions suggest, demonstrate the complementary case: an interfering e/4 with a controlled localized e/2.
3. Present event-by-event jump-size histograms and dwell-time statistics from fixed-V_PG time traces at ν = -1/2. Compare them with what a two-level or four-level model predicts.
4. Rephrase the abstract and Introduction so the claims match the evidence. The data show the charge (e/2 versus e/4) of the localized quasiparticle via a charge-sector braiding phase. They do not test non-Abelian character, and the gate selects which anyon fluctuates rather than setting the anyon number deterministically.
