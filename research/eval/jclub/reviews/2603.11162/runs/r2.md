Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors put a gate-defined graphite antidot (about 0.1 µm²) inside a bilayer-graphene Fabry-Pérot interferometer (about 1 µm²). At ν = −1/2 they report time-resolved phase jumps of about π when the antidot is near the bulk filling (ν_AD ≈ −0.5), and of about π/2 when the antidot is nearly depleted (ν_AD ≈ 0). They attribute these to an interfering e/2 quasiparticle braiding around an added localized e/2 or e/4 quasiparticle. Individual phase slips at an even-denominator filling have not been resolved before, and gate control over which type of localized anyon fluctuates would be a real step toward interferometric tests of non-Abelian statistics. The central new claim is that the π/2 jumps are e/4 quasiparticles braiding. As presented, the evidence for that claim rests mainly on the size of the jump, taken from a two-ridge fit to histograms.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The device concept is new and well executed: a single-graphite-gate FPI with an independently tunable antidot. Both the integer case (sawtooth period ΔQ ≈ 0.89e, Fig. 2a,d) and the ν = −1/3 case (three steps per period with Δθ/2π = 0.33 ± 0.02, Fig. 2e, SI4) behave as expected, which makes the setup a credible platform.
- The repeated-sweep datasets (Figs. S5–S8) are extensive and openly shown. They include ranges of ν_AD where no jumps occur (Fig. S5), which act as a stability baseline over more than 2 hours.
- At ν = −1/2 the π jumps (|θ|/2π = 0.46–0.49 in Fig. 4e and Fig. S10) are clean, stay near π while P_ex changes from 0 to 0.5, and are visible in individual sweeps (Fig. 4c). This is the first resolution of discrete e/2 braiding events at half filling.
- The ν = −1/3 data (Fig. 3) give an internal reference: the jump magnitudes are close to 2π/3, so the method returns the expected Abelian value in a known state.
- The paper says plainly that it addresses only one of the two requirements for non-Abelian braiding, and that the interfering charge stayed e/2.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The π/2 jump is not tied to e/4 braiding over other origins of a partial phase shift.** At fractional fillings the constant-phase lines in V_ADG–V_PG are tilted (Fig. 2b,c; Fig. S4b). At ν = −1/2, θ falls by about 2π per ΔV_ADG ≈ 2.6 mV, the voltage that adds one electron to the antidot. So the interference phase already follows the antidot charge continuously, at a rate of about 2π per e. In that regime, a discrete jump of size Δθ only says that a charge of roughly (Δθ/2π)·e has been redistributed in a way the interfering edge senses. A π/2 jump could then come from:
   - an e/2 rearrangement partly screened by bulk–edge coupling;
   - a charge moving between the antidot's own edge (well defined at ν_AD ≈ 0, as the Conclusions note) and the region inside the loop;
   - a fluctuator outside the antidot that couples capacitively to the interfering edge.
   The spread of the reported values (|θ|/2π from 0.23 to 0.33 in Figs. 4e and S11, against 0.25 expected) leaves room for these. The authors should show that the jump magnitude is quantized and topological rather than electrostatic:
   - (i) a histogram of jump sizes taken from individual sweeps, as in Fig. 4d, over all ν_AD ≈ 0 datasets, not only the two-ridge fit;
   - (ii) the jump magnitude at several magnetic fields inside the ν = −1/2 plateau and at several V_PG windows. A braiding phase should not depend on these; a Coulomb or partial-screening contribution generally would;
   - (iii) an estimate of the bulk–edge coupling ratio for this geometry at ν = −1/2, for example from the tilt and period of the V_ADG–V_PG pattern, with the size of the renormalization it would cause for an e/2 event.
   The ν = −1/3 control (Fig. 3f, values near 1/3) helps. However, it was taken at a different field (11 T rather than 8 T) and in a different state, so it does not on its own rule out renormalization at ν = −1/2.

2. **The histogram analysis assumes two ridges with equal amplitude, which is not justified for the π/2 case, and the P_ex extraction does not work there.** SI7 fits exactly two ridges with a common amplitude and a polynomial phase, and quotes uncertainties from the fit residuals.
   - If e/4 occupancy can change by ±1, or by 2 (equivalent to e/2), the expected phases relative to the ground state are 0, ±π/2 and π. A two-ridge fit to overlapping ridges offset by about π/2 can return a biased offset.
   - Assuming equal amplitude hides the visibility difference between branches, which concern 6 needs.
   - SI9 obtains P_ex by counting histogram weight above and below a fixed level R_D0. This separates branches only when they are anti-phased (π offset, Fig. S12). For a π/2 offset, the two cosines overlap above and below any horizontal threshold over much of each period, so the P_ex values for ν_AD ≈ 0 in Figs. 1g and 4e, and the peak near 0.42, are not defined by this method.
   - The residual-variance error bars in Figs. 3f and 4e are visibly smaller than the scatter between neighbouring V_ADG points.
   The authors should analyse the data sweep by sweep instead: fit θ for each sweep, or for each segment before and after a jump, and report the full distribution of θ for each V_ADG. A multimodal distribution would show directly how many occupancy states take part. They should then compute P_ex from those state assignments rather than from a threshold.

3. **There is no independent determination of the localized charge, and the V_ADG dependence of P_ex disagrees with the proposed degeneracy picture.**
   - The authors interpret the maxima of P_ex as near-degeneracies of N and N+1 localized anyons. At ν = −1/3, the integer and fractional antidot periods (2.2–2.7 mV per electron, SI4, which agrees with the ν_AD labels in Figs. S5–S6: about 26 mV for Δν_AD ≈ 0.044 over 0.1 µm² at 11 T) imply that one e/3 is added every 0.7–0.9 mV. Yet the P_ex maxima in Fig. 3f are about 5 mV apart, several quasiparticle additions per maximum. The text calls this a near-degeneracy of N and N+1 without explaining why most addition points show no fluctuations.
   - At ν = −1/2 near ν_AD ≈ 0 (Fig. 4e, Figs. S8 and S11), V_ADG is sampled every 2 mV. That is coarser than the expected e/4 addition period of about 0.65 mV, so the data cannot resolve the degeneracy structure.
   A fine V_ADG scan (about 0.1 mV steps) of P_ex and of the sign of θ_braid at ν = −1/2, ν_AD ≈ 0 would give charge-sensitive evidence that does not depend on the jump magnitude. If the degeneracies recur every e/4 (about 0.65 mV) and the sign of the jump flips across each one, that would support the e/4 assignment strongly. A recurrence every e/2 would point the other way.

B. Should address

4. **Single device, single field and single cooldown for the headline result.** The ν = −1/2 dynamics are all at B = 8 T in one device. The paper does not say how many cooldowns were made, whether the π/2 regime reappears after thermal cycling, or whether a second antidot device was measured. Because the fluctuating anyons are probably pinned by local disorder, reproducibility is the key test of the claimed "local control". The authors should report at least one more field within the plateau and, ideally, a second device or cooldown.

5. **The antidot as the site of the fluctuating anyons is asserted, not shown.** The π jumps appear at ν_AD ≈ −0.5, where the antidot filling matches the bulk (Fig. 4a: "ν_AD = ν") and the antidot is in effect inactive. The e/2 fluctuators could therefore sit anywhere in the loop. The "selectivity" in the abstract and on p. 5 then amounts to "jumps of different size at different V_ADG", not to control over an anyon trapped in the antidot.
   - The authors should show how P_ex and the jump rate depend on V_ADG compared with V_TG or V_PG. The ratio of the lever arms would locate the fluctuator.
   - They should also show that equivalent conditions without the antidot (V_ADG = V_TG, as in Fig. 1c, swept over time) show no π jumps.

6. **The parity argument is untested.** On p. 5 the authors argue that even-parity e/2 anyons dominate the interference, because otherwise the two parities would cancel when the number of e/4 anyons is odd. That would show up as different visibilities in the two branches. Yet the fit in SI7 imposes a common amplitude. Separately, the sign of the π/2 jumps varies between V_ADG values (Fig. S11: −0.23, +0.27, +0.33, +0.28, −0.27, +0.26, −0.26) with no correlation with P_ex. That pattern fits either parity, depending on whether e/4 enters or leaves. The authors should fit the branch amplitudes separately and report the visibility ratio, and either tie the sign of the jumps to the direction of tunnelling (for example through a fine V_ADG scan across a degeneracy) or remove the parity claim.

7. **The link to non-Abelian statistics is overstated.** SI10 (Eq. S4) shows that θ_braid(e/2, e/4) = ±π/2 for every ℓ. The same value follows for Abelian paired states such as Halperin 331 and, more generally, from charge-sector counting alone. The measurement therefore confirms that a charge of about e/4 is localized; it does not test non-Abelian character or tell Pfaffian from anti-Pfaffian. The abstract and the Introduction ("expected to be non-Abelian") should say clearly that the π/2 phase does not discriminate between these possibilities, and the role of the daughter-state argument (Refs. 34–40) should be stated as an assumption.

8. **The interfering charge under the antidot conditions is not measured.** The e/2 periodicity (Fig. 1c,d; Fig. S3) was measured with V_TG = V_ADG, that is, with the antidot inactive. The Conclusions state that the antidot did not change the interfering charge, but no B-periodicity data at ν_AD ≈ −0.5 or ν_AD ≈ 0 are shown. Because ν_AD ≈ 0 is exactly where the authors suggest e/4 tunnelling at the QPCs might be promoted, and an interfering e/4 braiding around localized e/2 would also give π/2-type shifts, the flux period at the Fig. 4b settings should be shown. Related points:
   - SI1 reports that at ν = −1/2, G_D stays below G_xy even with the QPCs fully open (about 0.38 against 0.5 e²/h, Fig. S1c), and the working transmissions of 0.7–0.9 are not in the weak-backscattering limit assumed for Eq. (1).
   - SI1 also notes that the QPC characterization is at 7 T, whereas the interference is at 8 T.
   The authors should discuss how these affect which modes interfere.

9. **The headline claim of time-domain single-event resolution at ν = −1/2 is supported only indirectly.** Fig. 3a shows a two-level telegraph trace with t_ex ≈ 11 s at ν = −1/3. No equivalent R_D(t) trace, lifetime or autocorrelation is given at ν = −1/2, for either the π or the π/2 regime, although the abstract claims "directly resolving individual anyon tunnelling events". The authors should add fixed-V_PG time traces at ν = −1/2 for both regimes, with dwell-time distributions, and check that the telegraph amplitude at several V_PG follows the predicted cos θ − cos(θ + Δθ). They should also report the temperature dependence of P_ex and the lifetimes, which would give the energy scale of the near-degeneracy.

10. **The conversion from V_ADG to ν_AD is not described, and the ν_AD labels disagree with each other.** The text and Fig. 1g give ν_AD ≈ 0 for V_ADG = 0.487 V, but Fig. 4b and Fig. S8c give 0.06–0.069. Fig. 3a (V_ADG = 0.48 V) is labelled ν_AD = 0, whereas Fig. S6 places 0.48 V near ν_AD ≈ 0.045. The ν_AD range "−1/2 ≲ ν_AD ≲ 0" for the jump-free regime has gaps where no data are shown (for example between −0.016 and 0.06 at ν = −1/2). Because "ν_AD ≈ 0" is the main control variable for the e/4 claim, the authors should give the calibration (offset, capacitance, effective antidot area including fringing) and its uncertainty, and use consistent labels.

C. Minor

11. Fig. 1g quotes θ/2π = −0.27, but Fig. S11b, which comes from the same V_ADG = 0.487 V data, shows +0.27. Given the discussion of jump sign, the authors should resolve this.
12. P_ex values differ slightly between the main text and the SI: Fig. 3d gives P_e = 0.16 and Fig. S9b gives 0.15; Fig. 1f gives 0.12 and Fig. S10c gives 0.11.
13. Methods: "alternating current ranging from 50 nA to 0.5 nA" is presumably 50 pA (as in SI5 and SI6). The authors should state the excitation used for each dataset and check that the electron temperature is not raised at the larger values.
14. Conclusions: "ν_ADG = 0" should read ν_AD. Page 8: "these sweeps leads" should read "lead". Page 8 says sweeps of 80 s with a 10 s return; the captions say one sweep every 90 s. The authors should make these consistent.
15. Figure 2c (ν = −1/3) is at 8 T, whereas the dynamics at ν = −1/3 (Fig. 3) are at 11 T. The reason for the change of field, and whether the staircase persists at 11 T, should be stated.
16. ΔQ ≈ e/3 (Fig. 2e) and ΔQ ≈ 0.89e use the lithographic antidot area. The authors should give the uncertainty from the effective area, since the integer value deviates from 1 by 11%.
17. Data are available only "upon request". Deposition of the raw repeated-sweep data would let readers repeat the per-sweep analysis requested above.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Replace or supplement the two-ridge histogram fits with per-sweep or per-segment phase extraction, and show the full θ distribution for every V_ADG at ν = −1/2. A single figure of jump-size distributions at ν_AD ≈ −0.5 and ν_AD ≈ 0, with the ν = −1/3 distribution as a reference, would carry the main claim far better than the current Fig. 4e.
2. Run a fine V_ADG scan across one or two P_ex maxima at ν = −1/2, ν_AD ≈ 0. Locating degeneracies with e/4 spacing and showing that the jump sign flips across them would give charge-sensitive evidence that does not depend on the jump magnitude.
3. Repeat the key ν = −1/2 measurements at a second magnetic field within the plateau, and if possible after thermal cycling or in a second device. Show that the π and π/2 magnitudes do not depend on B or V_PG.
4. Add a short quantitative estimate of bulk–edge Coulomb coupling for this geometry, using the measured tilt of the constant-phase lines. Use it to bound how far a non-topological renormalization could move a π jump toward π/2.
