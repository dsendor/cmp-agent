Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors put a gate-defined graphite antidot (about 0.1 µm²) inside a bilayer-graphene Fabry-Pérot interferometer (about 1 µm²). At ν = −1/2 they report time-resolved, rigid shifts of the interference pattern. These shifts are about π when the antidot filling is near the bulk filling (ν_AD ≈ −1/2) and about π/2 when ν_AD ≈ 0. The authors attribute them to e/2 or e/4 quasiparticles entering the loop, encircled by interfering e/2 anyons. At ν = −1/3 they show telegraph switching with jumps of about 2π/3 and a three-step staircase in V_ADG. If the attribution holds, this would be the first single-event braiding phase at an even-denominator state, and the first gate-selectable choice of which localized anyon species is braided. Both are significant steps toward interferometric tests of non-Abelian statistics. The weak point is the π/2 identification. It rests on histogram fits, at antidot settings where neither the interfering charge nor the electrostatic part of the jump has been established.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- New device concept: the antidot is a local, gate-tunable reservoir of localized anyons inside a graphene FPI, and the AB regime is shown clearly (Fig. 1c,d; Fig. S3).
- Large data set. Repeated V_PG sweeps (100 × 90 s) at about 25 values of V_ADG at two fillings are shown in full (Figs. S5–S8). The π jumps at ν_AD ≈ −1/2 are seen at many V_ADG values, with |θ|/2π = 0.46–0.49 (Fig. 4e, Fig. S10).
- Individual phase-slip events are resolved in single sweeps (Fig. 4c,d) and in a fixed-gate time trace (Fig. 3a). This goes beyond earlier even-denominator FPI work, where only averaged or smeared phase evolution was seen.
- The integer, ν = −1/3 and ν = −1/2 cases are compared in the same device (Fig. 2). The sawtooth at ν = −1 versus the tilted baselines and staircase at ν = −1/3 is a useful internal reference.
- The theory is laid out clearly (SI10, Eq. S4), including the parity dependence of the e/2–e/4 braiding phase.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The interfering charge is not measured at the antidot settings where the π/2 jumps appear, and the V_PG period changes between settings. The assignment "e/2 interfering around localized e/4" assumes e* = e/2 at ν_AD ≈ 0.06–0.10. The only interfering-charge measurement (Fig. 1c,d; Fig. S3c,g,k) was taken with V_TG = V_ADG, i.e. with the antidot inactive. The Conclusions say the antidot has no influence on e*, but no data are shown for this. It matters because e/4 interfering around a localized e/2 gives the same phase. The charge sector gives (1)(1/2)π = π/2, and an even e/2 contributes nothing in the Ising sector. The Conclusions themselves suggest that the ν_AD ≈ 0 configuration "could promote tunneling of e/4 particles" at the QPCs. The plunger period also differs between settings:
   - about 0.044 V at V_ADG = 0.235 V (Fig. S12a, Fig. 4a: about 7 periods in 0.3 V);
   - about 0.06 V at V_ADG = 0.487 V (Fig. 1g, Fig. 4b, Fig. S11).

   This change of roughly 1.4 is not discussed. It could come from a change in lever arm or in interfering charge.

   The authors should measure the B-periodicity along α_c (as in Fig. 1c,d) at one ν_AD ≈ −1/2 setting and one ν_AD ≈ 0.06 setting. They should report Φ0/ΔB for each, including the expected small change in effective area once the 0.1 µm² island is depleted. Without this, the π/2 jumps cannot be assigned to localized e/4 rather than to interfering e/4.

2. The jump-size values come from two-ridge histogram fits, not from the distribution of individual events. A two-state fit would miss π jumps or third states at ν_AD ≈ 0. The values of θ_braid in Figs. 1f,g, 3f and 4e come from the SI7 procedure:
   - the primary ridge is found;
   - a second ridge is taken from "additional peaks well separated from the primary ridge";
   - both ridges are fitted with a common amplitude and polynomial phase.

   This has three problems.
   - (i) Requiring good separation can bias the extracted offset away from small values. When P_ex is 0.04–0.05 (Fig. S11a,d), the second ridge is barely populated.
   - (ii) Only two phase states can be captured. The Introduction says "π/2 phase jumps occur in addition to π jumps" near ν_AD = 0. That is a mixture of processes (and two e/4 entering would also give π), which a two-ridge model cannot represent.
   - (iii) The spread at ν_AD ≈ 0 is larger than the error bars. |θ|/2π ranges from 0.26 to 0.33 in Fig. 4e, and Fig. S11c gives 0.33 at V_ADG = 0.489 V. That value is 2π/3, not π/2, yet the text describes the majority as "close to π/2".

   The authors should extract the phase of each sweep segment between jumps (or a sliding-window phase from the 1D FFT, as in SI4) for all sweeps in Figs. S7, S8 and S6. They should show histograms of individual jump sizes Δθ with counts for each V_ADG, and state how many events support the π/2 and π peaks at ν_AD ≈ 0. Fig. 4d shows a single example, which is not sufficient.

3. The electrostatic (bulk-edge) contribution to the jump size is not quantified. It is a leading alternative explanation for a reduced phase near ν_AD ≈ 0. The integer data show that adding one electron to the antidot shifts the interference phase through an area change: the sawtooth in Fig. 2a,d, with θ/2π swinging by about 0.3 in Fig. S4a. So the antidot charge is clearly coupled to the interfering edge. At fractional filling, a localized quasiparticle of charge q then gives a measured jump of θ_braid minus a Coulomb term proportional to q and to the edge-antidot coupling. This coupling should be strongest at ν_AD ≈ 0, where the text says a "well-defined edge state between the antidot area and the bulk" exists. With enough coupling, a localized e/2 could produce a jump well below π, possibly near π/2. The systematic excess above 0.25 (0.26–0.29 in Fig. 4e) and the low |θ|/2π = 0.19 and 0.26 at ν = −1/3 (Fig. 3f; Fig. S9g,j) also point to such corrections.

   The authors should do three things:
   - Use the integer sawtooth amplitude and the ν = −1/3 staircase to estimate the coupling ratio, and show what jump a localized e/2 would produce at ν_AD ≈ 0.
   - Show that the jump size does not change with V_PG position and with B along α_c at fixed ν_AD. A topological phase should stay fixed; an electrostatic one should drift.
   - Report jump sizes with this correction applied or bounded.

B. Should address

4. It is not shown that the switching anyons are located at the antidot. At ν_AD ≈ ν = −1/2 (V_ADG = 0.235 V), the antidot is close to the bulk filling, so the device is nearly a uniform FPI. The π jumps there (Fig. 4a, Fig. S7c–l) may come from localized e/2 anywhere in the bulk, or from extrinsic charge traps near the loop, as seen in other graphene FPIs. Fig. 1c shows clean AB with V_TG = V_ADG, but its time stability is not reported. The selectivity claim needs evidence that ν_AD controls where the fluctuating anyon sits. Useful tests:
   - (a) time-resolved sweeps with V_TG = V_ADG exactly, compared with ν_AD = −0.51;
   - (b) the lever arms of the P_ex degeneracy peaks with respect to V_ADG versus V_PG and V_TG, since an antidot-bound state should couple far more strongly to V_ADG;
   - (c) how the degeneracy positions move with B.

5. The P_ex(V_ADG) structure does not match the stated picture and is undersampled. The text says the three P_ex maxima at ν = −1/3, spaced by about 5 mV (Fig. 3f), mark N/N+1 degeneracies. But the same paper gives:
   - an electron-addition period of 2.7 mV at ν = −1/3 (Fig. 2c, Fig. S4c);
   - a quasiparticle step every ΔV_ADG/3 ≈ 0.9 mV (Fig. 2e).

   A 5 mV spacing matches neither. The V_ADG sampling in Figs. 3f and 4e is 1–2 mV, which is coarser than the expected quasiparticle period, so the peaks may be aliased. The text also notes that the sign pattern of θ around the peaks "does not occur consistently". The authors should take a dense V_ADG scan (≤ 0.3 mV steps) over at least one electron period. They should show that the degeneracy points recur with the expected quasiparticle periodicity, and that the sign of the jump flips across each degeneracy. Fig. 4e at ν = −1/2 needs the same treatment.

6. At ν = −1/3, the staircase step size does not test the braiding phase independently. Δθ/2π = 0.33 ± 0.02 (Fig. 2e; SI4, Fig. S4c,d) is the phase difference between consecutive minima of dθ/dV_ADG. That difference includes the continuous area-induced slope as well as the abrupt jump. If three charging events are evenly spaced within one electron period, and the phase accumulated per period is about 2π, then 1/3 per step follows automatically, whatever the braiding phase. The authors should separate the abrupt part from the linear background: fit the slope within plateaus and measure the discontinuity. They should also state the total phase per ΔV_ADG period. The text should also discuss the low |θ|/2π = 0.19 at V_ADG = 0.467 V (Fig. S9g), which disagrees with 1/3 well beyond its error bar.

7. The π/2 phase does not distinguish non-Abelian from Abelian paired states, and the parity argument is weak. In SI10 the π/2 for even-parity e/2 around e/4 comes entirely from the charge sector. Every candidate half-filled order, Abelian or not (e.g., 331, K = 8), gives π/2 for e/2 around e/4. The cancellation argument (even versus odd parity e/2) is the only Ising-specific element. Its conclusion, that one parity dominates because of scaling dimensions at the QPCs, is not tested. Also, observing both +π/2 and −π/2 shifts (Fig. S11) is equally consistent with switches of interfering-anyon parity. The authors should state explicitly that the measured phases do not discriminate the topological order, and temper "expected to be non-Abelian" in the abstract and Introduction. Alternatively, they could analyse visibility versus the number of localized e/4: with equal parity weights, an odd number should suppress the visibility. Comparing the oscillation amplitude in the two ridges of Fig. 1g would be a first test.

8. The calibration of ν_AD is not described, and quoted values are inconsistent. ν_AD is given to three decimals (Figs. S5–S11) and is central to the selectivity claim, yet the paper never says how it is computed. Missing are:
   - the zero offset;
   - whether C_TG from the Středa fit (SI2) is assumed for the island;
   - how fringe fields across a 0.1 µm² island with 40 nm trenches are treated;
   - the dependence on V_BG and displacement field.

   The π/2 regime sits at ν_AD = +0.06 to +0.10, which is the opposite carrier sign to the bulk. The physical picture of what is localized there (an n-type island and a p-n interface?) needs to be stated. The labels are also inconsistent:
   - Fig. 1e is called ν_AD ≈ 0 in the caption and text, but V_ADG = 0.4 V is labelled ν_AD = −0.132 in Fig. S7o.
   - Fig. 4b says 0.06, but Fig. S8c says 0.069.
   - Fig. S10 caption (a) gives −0.654, but the panel and Fig. S7a give −0.645.

9. Only one device and one cooldown are shown, with no temperature or bias dependence. All data appear to come from a single device; the Methods refers to FPIs in the plural, but none are shown. There is no evidence that the jump sizes or the ν_AD regimes survive thermal cycling. There is also no temperature or excitation dependence of the switching rates (t_ex, P_ex). That dependence would reveal whether switching is thermally activated across the antidot-edge tunnel barrier, and would help separate anyon tunneling from extrinsic fluctuators. The authors should report the number of devices and cooldowns, show the key π and π/2 results in a second device or cooldown if available, and give P_ex and dwell times at two or three temperatures.

10. At ν = −1/2 there is no fixed-gate time trace for either species. The abstract claims that individual tunneling events are directly resolved. At ν = −1/2, however, events are only inferred from 90 s sweeps (Fig. 4c,d), and lifetimes are given only for ν = −1/3 (Fig. 3a). Fixed-V_PG traces (as in Fig. 3a) at ν_AD ≈ −0.5 and ≈ 0.07 would give dwell-time distributions and their amplitude ratio for each process. These would show whether e/2 and e/4 switching have different kinetics, and would avoid jumps falling mid-sweep and blurring the histograms.

C. Minor

11. The excitation current is inconsistent. The Methods give "50 nA to 0.5 nA" with a 100 MΩ load, while SI1, SI5 and SI6 give 50 pA. Please state the current used for each data set, and estimate the electron temperature or heating at that current.

12. t_ex "extracted from the autocorrelations" (Fig. 3a): a telegraph autocorrelation gives (1/τ_g + 1/τ_e)^−1, not the excited-state lifetime alone. Please give dwell-time histograms for both states. Also comment on events shorter than the 300 ms time constant and 0.4 s sampling.

13. SI1 / Fig. S1c: at ν = −1/2 (B = 7 T), G_D saturates at about 0.38 e²/h with the QPC open, below ν e²/h. Please comment on what this implies for edge structure and equilibration at 8 T, where the interference data were taken. Also state how t_L and t_R were chosen at the antidot settings used.

14. The notation switches between ν_AD and "ν_ADG" (Conclusions). Fig. 3b,c are described as "100 time periods of 80 s with 10 s return", while the axes read "t = 90 s each". These are consistent, but please state it once.

15. "Its edge theory with ν = 0 is described by the anti-Pfaffian" (p. 8) is unclear. Please explain which interface is meant (−1/2 to vacuum versus the hole-conjugate description) and why it matters for the antidot boundary.

16. Data are available only on request. Given the event-level analysis requested above, please deposit the raw repeated-sweep data and analysis code.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a figure with event-resolved jump-size distributions at ν_AD ≈ −0.5 and ≈ 0.07. Put it next to the B-periodicity (interfering charge) measured at those same two settings. This would make the central π versus π/2 assignment direct.
2. Use the integer sawtooth and the ν = −1/3 staircase to calibrate the antidot-edge Coulomb coupling. Then plot the corrected braiding phases, with all fillings on one panel.
3. Map P_ex and jump sign at fine V_ADG resolution across one full electron period at each filling. This would connect the degeneracy points to the charging staircase of Fig. 2.
4. Reframe the even-denominator result as selective braiding of the localized species. State clearly that the π/2 phase is common to Abelian and non-Abelian paired states, and say which additional measurement (visibility suppression for odd e/4 number, or e/4 interference) would discriminate them.
