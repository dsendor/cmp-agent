Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper argues that large-angle twisted graphene (and TMD) multilayers in a magnetic field can host three-dimensional, non-foliated fractional quantum Hall phases. Weak interlayer tunneling would suppress the metallic spontaneous interlayer-coherent (SILC) states, while the strong interlayer Coulomb interaction would favour generalized Halperin states (mnop), such as (3110) and (3111), that couple up to three neighbouring layers. These states have rational charge and irrational braiding in the infinite-layer Chern-Simons description. The evidence is a variational Monte Carlo comparison of 862 trial wavefunctions, combined with the infinite-component K-matrix analysis of Ma et al. If the energetics hold up, this would be the first concrete materials proposal for infinite-component Chern-Simons / fracton-like FQH order. That is a timely and interesting target, given recent growth of helical and alternating twisted graphite spirals.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear and physically appealing mechanism: twist suppresses t_perp, while interlayer Coulomb at d = 3.35 Å stays strong. The authors place this mechanism in a specific, recently realized material family.
- A large and systematically built variational space. It contains generalized Halperin liquids with and without local valley polarization, multilayer composite-fermion crystals with Thomson-problem geometries, SILC states, four staging patterns, and products and particle-hole conjugates of these.
- The quasiparticle charges and self- and mutual statistics are worked out explicitly for (3100), (3110) and (3111). I checked that Eq. (3), Eq. (S48) and the red lines in Supp. Fig. 6d,e agree (π/√5 ≈ 1.405, π√(1/7 + 2/(3√21)) ≈ 1.687). The finite-layer convergence analysis (Supp. Fig. 6d–f) is a useful, experimentally minded addition.
- The authors make some effort to test generality, through other phenomenological parameters (Supp. Fig. 1) and five TMDs (Supp. Fig. 4).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The dimensionless tunneling strength seems to be too small by a factor of about ε_r, and t_perp is the parameter that drives the main conclusion. Supp. Table 5 lists "t_perp × l_B/d (e²/ε l_B)" = 0.0023, 0.00041 and 0.00025 for Bernal, ATG and HTG. These values equal t_perp/(e²/4πε_0 d) with ε_r = 1: 10 meV / 4.30 eV = 0.0023, 1.78/4300 = 0.00041 and 1.09/4300 = 0.00025. With the stated ε_r = 12.5, the ratio of t_perp to the Coulomb scale at the layer separation is t_perp ε_r d/e² ≈ 0.029, 0.0052 and 0.0032, about 12.5 times larger. Supp. Table 7 shows the same pattern. The TMD entries are consistently about 1/12.5 of t_perp ε_r d/e², for example WS2: 6.32 × 14.4 / 2312 meV ≈ 0.039, against 0.00313 listed. Perhaps an extra normalization is intended, but none is described. If tunneling really enters the energy comparison an order of magnitude too weakly relative to Coulomb, the SILC regions in Fig. 3b,c and the B ranges of Fig. 3f,g could change qualitatively. The whole message rests on the contrast between twisted and Bernal stacking, which is a contrast in t_perp. The authors should:
   - state exactly how t_perp enters the energy in units of e²/ε l_B, with the meaning of the "× l_B/d" label;
   - confirm or correct the factor;
   - if it changes, recompute Fig. 3, Supp. Figs. 1 and 4 and the quoted fields (B ≈ 3 T, B ≈ 1 T).

2. The key energy differences are not shown, and the method is not benchmarked. The phase diagrams (Fig. 3, Supp. Figs. 1 and 4) report only the winning state. The experimentally relevant window is B ≲ 20 T, which is d/l_B ≲ 0.06 (d/l_B ≈ 0.013 √(B/T)). There all layers are nearly coincident, the problem approaches a nearly SU(2N_L)-symmetric limit, and many candidates are close to degenerate. The paper gives no energies, no energy differences between the winner and the runner-up, no Monte Carlo statistical errors, and no uncertainty from the 1/N_L extrapolation (Methods; Supp. Note 6). It also does not show that the method reproduces any known result in a limit where unbiased numerics exist. The V_1–V_3 parameters are said to be "chosen to reproduce the experimentally observed phase diagrams" of twisted bilayer and trilayer [24–26], but that reproduction is never displayed. The authors should:
   - plot E/N_tot against d/l_B for the leading three to five candidates at ν_L = 1/2, 1/7 and 1/9, in ATG, HTG and Bernal stacking, with error bars that include extrapolation;
   - give the margin by which the generalized Halperin states win at, for example, 5, 10 and 20 T;
   - benchmark the variational energies against exact diagonalization or DMRG for small N_L (bilayer, trilayer, and periodic stacks of 3–4 layers with few electrons), and show that the method recovers the observed bilayer and trilayer phases with the chosen V_i.

3. The winning phase at ν_L = 1/2 needs a consistency check against the SU(2N_L) ferromagnet limit. ν_L = 1/2 is integer total filling per layer: (3111)⊕PH(3111) gives 1/9 + 8/9 = 1. As d/l_B → 0 and t_perp → 0, the projected Coulomb problem becomes SU(2N_L)-symmetric at integer total filling. Fully polarized Slater determinants, which include (1000) and the ν_L = 1/2 SILC state, are then expected to be the exact Coulomb ground states. Yet Fig. 3b,c put (3111)⊕PH(3111) at the lowest d/l_B for 1/ν_L = 2, below both (1000) and SILC, and the text cites this as a highlight ("stable even at … ν_L = 1/2, requiring … only B ≈ 3 T"). Near d = 0, symmetry-breaking corrections are of order d/l_B. A correlated product of a Laughlin-type state and its conjugate beating the ferromagnet there needs an explicit explanation. The authors should break the energy into Coulomb, tunneling, mean-field V_i and staging contributions. That would show which term stabilizes this state and how this fits the ferromagnet expectation. If the margin comes from the approximate product and PH formulas (Concern 4), from the √(N_φ ν/N̄) density correction, or from the V-term heuristic (Supp. Note 6c), the result is likely an artifact.

4. The energies of the winning states come from approximate formulas that are not validated. Every interlayer-coherent winner in Fig. 3 is a product and/or a particle-hole conjugate: (3111)⊕PH(3111), (3110)⊕(3110), (3111)⊕(3111). Their energies are not sampled directly. They are assembled from seed energies through Eq. (S36) for products of different states and Eq. (S39) for PH conjugates, both marked "≈" with no error estimate. The short-range V_i terms are treated in mean field. When the result is "unphysical", the valley texture is then picked by a heuristic that reverses the ranking of the V energy (Supp. Note 6c). The decisive energies therefore sit on stacked approximations of unknown accuracy, at energy scales where the competition is tight. The authors should:
   - evaluate at least the winning product and PH states, and their closest competitors, directly by Monte Carlo on the full wavefunction, and compare with Eqs. (S36) and (S39);
   - state which phase points relied on the Supp. Note 6c rule;
   - give the V_i mean-field energies of the winners and the runners-up, so readers can see how much these phenomenological terms decide the outcome.

B. Should address

5. The SILC comparison is biased against the metallic state. The SILC state (Eqs. S14–S31) is an uncorrelated Slater determinant whose energy is evaluated analytically. The liquids and crystals carry full intralayer Jastrow correlations and are evaluated by sampling. A metallic interlayer-coherent state can lower its energy by building in intralayer correlations: Jastrow-dressed SILC, composite fermions forming a Fermi sea along k_parallel, or interlayer-coherent CF states. Without these, the conclusion that twist "suppresses metallic states" partly reflects the variational ansatz, not the physics. The SILC energy also uses N̄ = 48 with Richardson/Shanks extrapolation in the layer sum, while the liquids use Monte Carlo with a 1/N_L fit, so the two sets of energies carry different finite-size systematics. The authors should include at least one correlated interlayer-coherent metallic ansatz, or argue quantitatively why its correlation gain cannot close the gap. They should also check that the SILC and liquid energies extrapolate consistently, for example by treating the SILC energy at finite N_L in the same way.

6. The interlayer tunneling model for twisted layers is not justified, and its sensitivity is not scanned. H_Tunnel (Eq. 1) is a local, momentum-conserving, intravalley hopping with a single amplitude.
   - Momentum mismatch: for layers rotated by θ ≳ 10°, the Dirac points of adjacent layers are displaced by ΔK ≈ 2K sin(θ/2). The zeroth-Landau-level matrix element of the moiré-modulated tunneling then carries form factors in ΔK l_B, which are strongly B-dependent. The paper should explain how the effective 1.78 meV (ATG) and 1.09 meV (HTG) values were derived; the HTG value and the HTG bandwidth W = 4.36 meV have no reference. It should also say whether they depend on B.
   - Helical case near 30°: for HTG "slightly less than 30°" (Supp. Note 3c), the K–K and K–K′ mismatches between neighbouring layers become comparable. The neglect of intervalley tunneling (Methods) is then not justified.
   - ATG: alternate layers are aligned, so next-nearest-layer tunneling should be discussed.
   - Robustness (Supp. Note 3b) is tested only by changing V_1–V_3. The authors should scan t_perp, ε_r and d continuously for graphene, as Supp. Table 8 does partially for TMDs, and give the t_perp range over which the Halperin windows survive below 20 T.

7. The spin-polarization assumption at low fields is not justified. The model assumes the spin degeneracy is "completely lifted". The headline fields are B ≈ 1–3 T, where the Zeeman energy is about 0.1–0.35 meV, much smaller than e²/ε l_B ≈ 4.5 √B meV and comparable to the energy differences between competing correlated states. Spin-unpolarized or spin-valley-entangled liquids and crystals, and spin textures, are possible competitors there. The authors should include spin in the candidate set at least for ν_L = 1/7 and 1/9 at the lowest fields, or limit the claimed field range to where spin polarization is secure.

8. Finite-size and extrapolation details are missing.
   - The N_L values used in the 1/N_L extrapolation are not given, nor the quality of the fits. A (3111) state couples third neighbours and needs N_L ≥ 7 to avoid wrapping onto itself.
   - Only N̄ = 48 electrons per layer is used, with a √(N_φ ν/N̄) density correction and no extrapolation in N̄. Different trial states have different shifts S, so their finite-size corrections differ.
   - The staging energies (S41–S47) assume d ≪ R and are added analytically.
   - The cutoff M_eff (Supp. Table 9) is calibrated only on decoupled 1/3 Laughlin stacks, not on interlayer-correlated states.

   The authors should report the N_L values used, give extrapolation plots for representative states, and show the N̄ dependence (for example N̄ = 24, 36, 48) of the energy differences between competing states.

9. The candidate set includes ill-defined states and omits relevant competitors. Several seed states in Supp. Table 1 have a K-matrix symbol K(q) = m + 2n cos q + 2o cos 2q + 2p cos 3q that vanishes for some q. Examples are (1100), (1110), (3200), (3210) [K(q) = (2cos q + 1)²] and (3300). By the criterion of Ref. [7], their infinite-layer limits are gapless or not well-defined topological phases. Their finite-N_L energies and filling assignments are therefore questionable, even though they do not appear to win. The winners (3100), (3110) and (3111) do have K(q) > 0 (the minimum is about 0.37 for (3111)). The paper should state this gap criterion and apply it. Separately:
   - Composite-Fermi-liquid states are absent at the even-denominator fillings 1/ν_L = 4, 6 and 8.
   - Only four staging patterns are included.
   - Generalized Halperin states with longer-range or non-monotonic exponents are not considered.

   The authors should discuss how these omissions could affect the phase diagram.

10. The topological claims are asserted rather than shown for the actual ground states. The abstract and Discussion claim non-foliated, fracton-related order and an exponentially large topological degeneracy. Supp. Note 5 computes only charges and braiding phases. The authors should:
   - give the finite-N_L ground-state degeneracy |det K| for the winning states, and its growth with N_L;
   - state the non-foliation criterion of Ref. [7] being used and show that the winners satisfy it;
   - confirm that the valley-product and PH-conjugated forms that actually win, such as (3111)⊕PH(3111) with its mixed-chirality K-matrix (S37), retain irrational braiding and a gapped infinite-layer limit. Supp. Note 5e,f addresses braiding only.

11. The claim of experimental realism skips the practical barriers and the signatures. The paper concludes that twisted multilayers are an "experimentally realistic platform" without discussing three points.
    - Filling control: setting a uniform ν_L across tens to hundreds of layers is not possible with surface gates in a screening 3D stack, so how is the filling to be tuned?
    - Required densities: ν_L = 1/9 at about 1 T corresponds to roughly 5 × 10^9 cm⁻² of zLL electrons per layer and per valley pair.
    - Disorder and twist-angle variation in grown spirals [27–30].

    Also, the per-layer Hall conductance of (3110)⊕(3110) (2e²/7h per layer) is the same as that of decoupled or staged states at the same filling, so σ_xy alone cannot identify the proposed phase. The authors should map ν_L to the carrier density relative to charge neutrality, discuss how the density could be controlled (intrinsic doping, intercalation), estimate the excitation gaps (needed for any claim of incompressibility relative to disorder and temperature), and propose measurements that distinguish generalized Halperin order from foliated or staged states. Examples are c-axis transport, interlayer drag, shot-noise charge e/7 or e/9, and thermal Hall response.

12. The Bernal-graphite baseline is oversimplified. The "untwisted limit" is the same two-valley zLL model with t_perp = 10 meV. The n = 0 and n = 1 manifold of Bernal graphite has orbital as well as valley degeneracy, with γ_2/γ_5-controlled k_z dispersion and layer-sublattice structure, so a per-layer, monolayer-like zLL does not describe it. The claim that conventional graphite is "dominated by metallic SILC phases" (abstract) depends on this baseline. The authors should state it as a model with weaker coupling rather than as graphite, or justify the mapping.

C. Minor

13. The magnetic-field conversion is inconsistent. Section "Phase Diagrams" says d/l_B ≳ 0.2 corresponds to B ≳ 100 T. With d = 3.35 Å and l_B = 25.66 nm/√B, d/l_B = 0.2 corresponds to about 235 T, and 100 T to about 0.13. The authors should check this sentence and the positions of the right-hand B-axis ticks in Fig. 3a–c and Supp. Figs. 1 and 4 (the 10 T and 100 T ticks look low).

14. Labelling errors in the supplement:
    - Supp. Table 6 lists "V_1" twice; the second row should presumably be V_2.
    - The Supp. Fig. 4 caption uses "f" for two different panels. It also quotes MoSe2 t_perp ≈ 17.75 meV, while Supp. Table 7 gives 17.15 meV.
    - Supp. Fig. 1 caption says "alternating/helical twisted graphite", whereas the main text says "graphene multilayer".
    - Supp. Fig. 3 says "10° ≪ θ < 30°", while Supp. Note 3c says "slightly less than 30°".

15. The seed counts do not add up as stated. Methods states 43 liquid seeds. Supp. Tables 1 and 2 list 18 + 34 = 52 by my count (the 123 crystal seeds in Supp. Tables 3–4 do add up). The authors should reconcile the counts or say which listed states were excluded.

16. The definition ν_L = N_e/[2L(N_Φ + S)] makes ν_L = 1/2 an integer total filling per layer. This should be stated explicitly. ν_L should also be related to the experimentally used filling of the graphene zLL, for example ν measured from charge neutrality.

17. In Fig. 3e–g and Supp. Fig. 1e–g, several bars run to the top of the 50 T axis. The upper boundaries of these phases should be stated, and the energy margin at the edge of the shaded region given.

18. The wording "establish" and "first realistic condensed-matter platform" (abstract, Discussion) goes beyond what a variational study can support. The authors should temper it until the benchmarks in Concerns 2–4 are available.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a main-text figure of energy against d/l_B (or B) for the top competitors at ν_L = 1/7, 1/9 and 1/2 in ATG and Bernal stacking, with Monte Carlo and extrapolation error bars, and the Coulomb, tunneling, V and staging contributions separated.
2. Benchmark against exact diagonalization or DMRG for small periodic stacks, and against the twisted bilayer and trilayer data that fix V_1–V_3. Directly sample the winning product and PH states to validate Eqs. (S36) and (S39).
3. Give a clear derivation of the effective zLL interlayer tunneling for twisted layers, including its momentum-mismatch form factors and B dependence. Present the phase diagram as a function of t_perp (correctly normalized by ε_r) so that readers can place any real sample on it.
4. Add a short section on experimental identification: how to set the filling, gap estimates, and observables that separate non-foliated generalized Halperin order from staged or decoupled states with the same σ_xy.
