Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper compares the variational energies of 862 trial states (generalized Halperin liquids, composite-fermion crystals, staged states, metallic spontaneous interlayer-coherent (SILC) states, and their products and particle-hole conjugates) for an infinite stack of lowest-Landau-level layers with interlayer tunneling t⊥. It argues that reducing t⊥ from about 10 meV (Bernal graphite) to about 1–2 meV (large-angle alternating or helical twisted multilayers) removes the metallic SILC phases at B ≲ 20 T. In their place come generalized Halperin states such as (3110)⊕(3110) at ν_L = 1/7 and (3111)⊕(3111) at ν_L = 1/9. These states have rational quasiparticle charge and irrational self- and mutual statistics, following infinite-component Chern-Simons theory. A credible materials route to non-foliated three-dimensional fractional quantum Hall order would be of wide interest. As it stands, however, the experimental-relevance claim rests on (i) the dimensionless tunneling values, which appear to contain a unit-conversion problem, (ii) a minimal model of what the twist does, and (iii) energy comparisons reported without uncertainties.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The question is timely. Helical and alternating twisted graphite has recently been grown (refs. 27–30), and the paper links that work to the long-standing theory of infinite-layer quantum Hall systems (Qiu–Joynt–MacDonald, Naud–Pryadko–Sondhi, Burnell–Bernevig–Arovas).
- The candidate space is large and systematically generated: seeds plus product, particle-hole and staging operations, with crystal geometries optimized by basin-hopping solutions of the Thomson problem (Supp. Note 2, Supp. Tables 1–4). This is much broader than earlier infinite-layer studies, which compared a handful of states.
- The quasiparticle analysis is explicit. Closed forms for θ_ii and θ_ij of (3100), (3110) and (3111) are given (Eqs. S48–S50), and the finite-N_layer convergence is shown (Supp. Fig. 6). The numbers check: π/√5 ≈ 1.405 and π√(1/7 + 2/(3√21)) ≈ 1.687 match Supp. Fig. 6d,e. The K(q) = m + 2n cos q + 2o cos 2q + 2p cos 3q of the quoted states has no zeros, so the states are gapped in the K-matrix sense.
- Untwisted Bernal graphite serves as a reference within the same framework, and TMD multilayers are added as a second family (Supp. Note 4).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The dimensionless tunneling strengths in Supp. Tables 5, 7 and 8 appear to be too small by a factor of about 4π. In Supp. Table 5 the column "t⊥ × l_B/d (e²/εl_B)" is field-independent, so it can only mean t⊥/(e²/εd). With d = 3.35 Å, ε_r = 12.5 and e²/(4πε₀) = 1.44 eV·nm, t⊥ = 10 meV gives t⊥εd/e² ≈ 0.029, whereas the table lists 0.0023. The table values follow from the meV values only after dividing by about 12.57 ≈ 4π:
   - Bernal: 0.029/4π = 0.0023;
   - alternating twisted graphene (ATG), 1.78 meV: 0.0052/4π = 0.00041;
   - helical twisted graphene (HTG), 1.09 meV: 0.00025.
   The TMD entries behave the same way. WS₂ (6.32 meV, ε = 14.4, d = 6.229 Å) gives 0.0394/4π = 0.00313, as listed, and Supp. Table 8 (0.0015 ↔ 3.026 meV for WS₂) uses the same conversion. This pattern is what one gets from writing the Coulomb scale as e²/(ε₀ε_r l_B) in SI units instead of e²/(4πε₀ε_r l_B). If so, every phase diagram was computed with tunneling about 12.6 times weaker relative to Coulomb than the physical values in meV. The plotted "Bernal" panel (Fig. 3a,e) would then correspond to t⊥ ≈ 0.8 meV, so ATG and HTG at their stated t⊥ would lie on the more tunneling-dominated side of what is now labeled Bernal graphite. That would overturn the central claim that twisting moves SILC out of the accessible field range. A sanity check: at 10 T, e²/εl_B ≈ 14 meV for ε = 12.5, so t⊥ = 10 meV is not a small perturbation for Bernal graphite. The authors should state the exact definition of the dimensionless tunneling and of the Coulomb unit used in the code. If the factor is an error, they should recompute Fig. 3, Supp. Figs. 1 and 4, and the field windows quoted in the text (B ≈ 3 T and 1 T at ν_L = 1/2, B ≲ 8 T at 1/7). If it is not an error, they should explain how the tabulated numbers follow from the meV values.

2. The "twist" enters only as a smaller, momentum-local intravalley t⊥, and the values are not derived. In Eq. (1), H_Tunnel = −t⊥ Σ ∫ ψ†_{l,σ}(r) ψ_{l+1,σ}(r) contains no momentum transfer. In a large-angle twisted pair, however, the K points of adjacent layers differ by ΔK = 2K sin(θ/2). Projected onto the n = 0 Landau level, a tunneling term carrying momentum ΔK has a matrix element of order exp(−|ΔK|² l_B²/4). That is vanishingly small for θ ≳ 10°, where ΔK l_B ~ 10² at 10 T. The residual coupling therefore comes from moiré umklapp and commensurability processes, with its own field and angle dependence, not from a reduced constant t⊥. Further problems with the values:
   - t⊥ = 1.78 and 1.09 meV equal W/4 for the listed bandwidths W = 7.12 and 4.36 meV (Supp. Table 5). The HTG bandwidth has no source.
   - Ref. 43 (Lu et al.), cited for ATG, reports magic momenta and three-dimensional Landau levels in alternating twisted graphite. That is evidence of strong, non-trivial interlayer coupling, which a uniform nearest-neighbor t⊥ does not capture.
   - In ATG, layers l and l+2 are aligned, so second-order or next-nearest-layer coupling may not be negligible.

   Because the paper's conclusion is essentially "SILC loses once t⊥ is small enough", the result is only as good as the mapping from θ to an effective zero-Landau-level tunneling. The authors should:
   - derive the effective n = 0 interlayer coupling for each geometry, or at least justify the W/4 identification and the HTG value;
   - show the phase boundary at each ν_L as a continuous function of t⊥/(e²/εd) and d/l_B, so that any system can be placed on it;
   - show the sensitivity to ε_r, which sets the same ratio and is fixed at 12.5 without discussion of interband or anisotropic screening in a graphitic stack.

3. The phase diagrams are drawn as sharp boundaries, but no energies, energy differences or statistical and extrapolation uncertainties are reported. Methods and Supp. Note 6 state that N̄ = 48 particles per layer, 10⁶ samples, and a least-squares 1/N_L → ∞ extrapolation. Missing are:
   - the N_L values used;
   - the fit quality;
   - the error bars;
   - any extrapolation in the number of particles per layer, since only one N̄ is used.

   The claimed phases sit exactly where the comparison is most delicate. At ν_L = 1/7 and 1/9, Laughlin liquids lose to Wigner or composite-fermion crystals in two dimensions by energies of order 10⁻³ e²/εl_B, and liquid–crystal competition at these fillings is known to be size-sensitive on the sphere. Three choices could bias the comparison by amounts of that size:
   - Crystal states are assigned N_Φ = N̄/ν̄ − 2p − 1 to match the liquids (Supp. Note 2.2). This is a matching convention, not a thermodynamic choice.
   - SILC energies come from a different route: an analytic series with Richardson, Shanks and Euler-Maclaurin acceleration (Eq. S32).
   - Product and particle-hole energies are approximated by Eqs. S36 and S39 rather than computed directly.

   For the states and boundaries that support the headline (the generalized Halperin windows in Fig. 3e–g and the SILC → Halperin transitions), the authors should tabulate energies per particle with uncertainties for the winning state and its two closest competitors. They should also show the 1/N_L extrapolations and repeat the key comparisons at a second N̄, for example 36 or 64, or extrapolate in 1/N̄.

4. The metallic competitor is evaluated at a less correlated level than the liquids it is compared with. The SILC energy (Eqs. S14–S31) is that of a Slater determinant, a filled one-dimensional Fermi sea, so it contains exchange but no correlation energy. The liquids and crystals, by contrast, carry Jastrow correlations. Correlation energy in a partially filled, dispersing Landau band is not small, and its absence shifts every SILC–liquid boundary toward the liquids. That boundary is precisely where the twist acts in Fig. 3. Two related omissions point the same way:
   - The density-wave instability of the partially filled one-dimensional band, which the introduction highlights (Fig. 1c, refs. 13–14), is not among the candidates. The staged states are a different, layer-commensurate construction.
   - The gapped liquids gain no second-order tunneling energy (of order −t⊥²/Δ), although SILC gains first-order tunneling energy.

   The authors should include correlated metallic or density-wave competitors: a Jastrow-correlated SILC, a Hartree-Fock k_z density wave of the one-dimensional band, or at least an RPA-level estimate of the SILC correlation energy. Without these, the claimed suppression of metallicity is an upper-bound comparison that favors the liquids.

B. Should address

5. The candidate space limits interlayer correlations to third neighbors, with the hierarchy m ≥ n ≥ o ≥ p (Eq. 2, Supp. Note 2). Yet the regime the paper emphasizes is d/l_B ≈ 0.02–0.06 (B ≲ 20 T), where the interlayer Coulomb interaction is nearly as strong as the intralayer one over about l_B/d ≈ 20–50 layers. In that limit there is little reason to expect the optimal correlations to stop at three layers. The list also has no composite-Fermi-liquid-like or other gapless correlated candidates at even per-layer total fillings (ν_L = 1/4, 1/8). Every comparison is variational, and there is no unbiased check. The authors should:
   - include longer-range seeds, at least (mnopq), or argue why they must lose;
   - benchmark the variational ranking against exact diagonalization or DMRG on a small periodic stack (N_L = 3–4, a few particles per layer) at representative d/l_B and t⊥, reporting overlaps or energy differences.

6. The phenomenological short-range interactions differ between the three systems, and the ground state is sometimes assigned by hand. Supp. Table 5 gives V₂ = 0.507, 0.406 and 0.315 and V₃ = 0.101, 0.203 and 0.294 (in units of (d/l_B) e²/εl_B) for Bernal, ATG and HTG. The Bernal and HTG values are not sourced, and the ATG values come from bilayer data (ref. 24). So the "twisted versus untwisted" comparison changes several parameters at once, not only t⊥. The V terms are also handled in mean field (Eq. S63). Supp. Note 6c states that when "the liquid ground state turns out to be unphysical", the valley texture is chosen by a heuristic. The robustness test (Supp. Table 6, Supp. Fig. 1) scales the V's down but never makes them equal across systems, and it does not vary t⊥, ε or d. The authors should:
   - present the three phase diagrams with identical V₁–V₃, so that only t⊥ differs;
   - mark which points in Fig. 3 and Supp. Fig. 1 were settled by the Note 6c rule;
   - correct the duplicated "V₁" row label in Supp. Table 6.

7. The low-field regime, where the claims are strongest, is where the model's approximations are least safe. The paper assumes full spin polarization ("sufficiently strong magnetic field", Results) and no Landau-level mixing. Yet it quotes stability at B ≈ 1–3 T, and Fig. 3f,g show the ν_L = 1/7 and 1/9 Halperin windows reaching down toward B → 0. At 1–3 T the Zeeman energy (about 0.1–0.35 meV) is far below t⊥ and below the interaction energy differences, so spin-unpolarized or spin–valley-mixed competitors become relevant. Landau-level mixing (n = ±1 levels) and disorder broadening also grow relative to the gaps. The authors should:
   - explain why the phases persist as B → 0 at fixed ν_L, given that t⊥/(e²/εl_B) grows like l_B;
   - include spin as a degree of freedom, or bound its effect, at the lowest fields claimed;
   - estimate the Landau-level-mixing parameter;
   - qualify the "B ≈ 1 T" statement.

8. The paper does not estimate gaps or give experimental signatures. The phase diagrams say which variational state is lowest but give no transport or neutral gap for the generalized Halperin states. Experimental relevance depends on such a gap exceeding disorder and twist-angle-variation broadening in multilayer stacks. In addition:
   - σ_xy = 2e²/7h per layer for (3110)⊕(3110) equals that of decoupled valley-polarized Laughlin 1/7 layers, so Hall quantization cannot identify the proposed order.
   - A uniform per-layer filling of ν_L = 1/7–1/9 across 20 or more layers is assumed. Electrostatic gating, however, is screened within a few layers of a graphitic stack, and the staging analysis itself shows that non-uniform layer densities compete.

   The authors should estimate the quasiparticle gap, for example from quasihole–quasiparticle trial states. They should name observables that separate the generalized Halperin state from staged and decoupled states (shift or thermal Hall response, interlayer drag, layer-resolved capacitance or compressibility). And they should discuss how a uniform bulk filling could be achieved and how robust the result is to a density gradient along the stack.

9. The topological claims need to be stated more precisely. With periodic N_L, the K matrix is finite and all braiding phases are rational multiples of π, so "irrational" statistics is an asymptotic statement. Supp. Fig. 6d–f shows convergence, but real samples have open boundaries in the stacking direction. Two further claims rest on ref. 7 rather than being shown here:
   - "non-foliated order closely related to fractonic order";
   - "exponentially large topological degeneracy".

   The authors should state the open-boundary behavior, show the ground-state degeneracy |det K| as a function of N_L for the claimed states, and state the criterion of ref. 7 used to conclude non-foliation.

10. The Bernal graphite reference is a strongly simplified model. The zero-energy Landau band of graphite involves the n = 0 and n = 1 orbitals with γ₁- and γ₂-dependent dispersion (ref. 45). Here it is represented by the same two-valley monolayer level with t⊥ = W/4 = 10 meV. Since the untwisted case is the paper's main control, the authors should justify this reduction or discuss how the orbital degree of freedom would change the SILC energies.

C. Minor

11. Results, "Phase Diagrams": "B ≳ 100 T, corresponding to d/l_B ≳ 0.2" is inconsistent. With d = 3.35 Å, d/l_B = 0.2 corresponds to about 230 T, and 100 T to about 0.13. The right-hand axis of Fig. 3 is consistent with the latter.

12. The text says B ≈ 3 T for ATG at ν_L = 1/2 is required, but Fig. 3f shows a bounded window, roughly 3–14 T. Please state both bounds for each window in Fig. 3e–g.

13. Methods reports 43 liquid seeds, but Supp. Tables 1 and 2 appear to list about 52 liquid seeds (18 + 34). The crystal count of 123 matches Supp. Tables 3 and 4. Please reconcile.

14. Small inconsistencies:
    - Supp. Fig. 4 caption: MoSe₂ t⊥ ≈ 17.75 meV versus 17.15 meV in Supp. Table 7; panel label "f" is used twice.
    - The Supp. Fig. 4i legend places (3111)⊕PH(3111) under "Staged", although it appears as unstaged elsewhere.
    - Supp. Fig. 3 caption ("10° ≪ θ < 30°") versus Supp. Note 3 ("θ slightly less than 30°").
    - The Methods sentence in which "each generalized Halperin state exhibits a valley polarization" needs clarifying.

15. In Fig. 3a–c the experimentally relevant region (d/l_B ≲ 0.06) is a thin strip at the bottom of each panel. A logarithmic d/l_B axis, or an inset, would make the twist-induced changes readable without relying on panels e–g.

16. "The first realistic condensed-matter platform" (Discussion) should be qualified. Multilayer quantum wells and superlattices were proposed for similar infinite-layer states, including irrational charge or statistics, in refs. 32, 36 and 10–11 and in Qiu–Joynt–MacDonald (Supp. ref. 20).

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Recheck the unit conversion for t⊥ (concern 1). Then replace the three discrete systems with a phase diagram in the (t⊥/(e²/εd), d/l_B) plane at each ν_L, with the Bernal, ATG and HTG points marked on it.
2. Add a table of energies per particle, with Monte Carlo and extrapolation uncertainties, for the leading competitors along the boundaries in Fig. 3e–g, including a second particle number per layer.
3. Strengthen the metallic and density-wave competitors (a correlated SILC and a k_z density wave of the one-dimensional Landau band), and benchmark the ranking against exact diagonalization or DMRG on a small periodic stack.
4. Add a short section on how the proposed phases could be identified in experiment: estimated gaps, a distinguishing observable beyond σ_xy, and how uniform bulk filling could be reached in a 20-layer or thicker twisted stack.
