Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript proposes large-angle twisted van der Waals multilayers (alternating and helical twisted graphene, and by extension twisted TMDs) as a platform for three-dimensional, non-foliated fractional quantum Hall phases. These would be generalized Halperin (mnop) states with interlayer correlations reaching up to third-neighbour layers, rational quasiparticle charge and irrational braiding phases. The evidence is a variational Monte Carlo comparison of 862 trial states in the zeroth Landau level. In this comparison, reducing the interlayer tunneling from about 10 meV (Bernal graphite) to about 1–2 meV (twisted stacks) suppresses metallic spontaneous-interlayer-coherent (SILC) states and opens generalized Halperin windows at fields of a few tesla. If correct, this would be the first concrete materials proposal for infinite-component Chern–Simons / fractonic-type FQH order. That makes it of broad interest. The headline numbers, however, rest on a parameter set and a variational comparison whose reliability is not yet shown.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A timely, clearly motivated question: how to escape both quasi-1D dispersive Landau bands and foliated stacks of 2D liquids. Twisted 3D stacks are now being grown (helical spirals, alternating twisted graphene spirals).
- A large, systematically generated variational space: liquids, composite-fermion crystals, staged states, SILC metals, and their products and particle-hole conjugates. A Thomson-problem optimization sets the crystal geometries.
- An appealing physical mechanism (twist suppresses coherent hopping, leaving the Coulomb coupling intact) with a clear control (untwisted Bernal graphite) in the same calculation.
- Explicit closed-form K-matrix results for charges and braiding phases of (3100), (3110) and (3111). I checked three values. Eq. (3) gives θ ≈ 1.687 for (3110), π/√5 ≈ 1.405 for (3100), and the K matrices of (3110) and (3111) are positive definite. A finite-layer convergence analysis (Supp. Fig. 6) is directly relevant to experiment.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The dimensionless interlayer tunneling appears to be too small by a factor of ε_r ≈ 12.5. If so, the "experimentally accessible field" scale moves.
   - Supplementary Table 5 lists t⊥ × l_B/d in units of e²/εl_B as 0.0023 (Bernal, 10 meV), 0.00041 (ATG, 1.78 meV) and 0.00025 (HTG, 1.09 meV), with d = 3.35 Å and ε_r = 12.5. These numbers equal t⊥/(e²/4πε₀d) with no dielectric constant: e²/4πε₀d = 4.30 eV, and 10 meV/4.30 eV = 0.0023.
   - With ε_r = 12.5, the field-independent ratio is t⊥/(e²/ε_r d) = 0.029 for Bernal graphite, which is 12.5 times larger.
   - The TMD entries (Supp. Tables 7–8) follow the same rule: tabulated value = t⊥ε_r d/e² ÷ 12.5. For WS₂ this gives 0.00313, and the ratio to t⊥d/e² tracks ε_r/12.5 for every compound.
   - A physical check also points this way. With the full 10 meV, t⊥ ≈ 0.4 e²/εl_B at d/l_B ≈ 0.07, where the Bernal SILC-to-liquid boundary sits in Fig. 3a. Overcoming a tunneling gain of order 2t⊥ would need a correlation-energy advantage of nearly 1 e²/εl_B, which is implausible for lowest-Landau-level liquids. With the tabulated value, the required advantage is a few 10⁻² e²/εl_B, which is consistent with the figure.
   - If the code used the tabulated numbers while energies are in e²/ε_r l_B, the modeled tunneling is effectively 0.8 meV (Bernal) and 0.14 meV (ATG), not 10 and 1.78 meV. The B axes of Fig. 3 and Supp. Figs. 1 and 4, and the claims of ν_L = 1/2 at about 3 T (ATG) and about 1 T (HTG), would then not correspond to the stated materials.
   - Action: state explicitly the dimensionless t⊥/(e²/ε l_B) used at each d/l_B, reconcile it with ε_r, and recompute the phase diagrams if the factor is confirmed. Check the V₁–V₃ entries, which carry the same unit label, the same way.

2. Neither the tunneling model nor its parameter values are derived for twisted stacks, yet the main conclusion turns on them.
   - The central claim is that twisting changes the phase diagram by lowering t⊥ from 10 meV to 1.78 or 1.09 meV (main text p. 3; Supp. Table 5).
   - The model keeps a single, field-independent, nearest-neighbour intravalley hopping in the zeroth Landau level. Three points are not justified:
     - (a) How a large-angle momentum mismatch, projected onto Landau-level orbitals, becomes a constant t⊥ rather than a B-dependent, form-factor-suppressed amplitude (with umklapp contributions near commensurate angles).
     - (b) The value for HTG (1.09 meV, W = 4.36 meV), which carries no reference. The ATG value is attributed to refs. [43, 44], but the mapping from those band structures to an effective zeroth-Landau-level hopping is not shown.
     - (c) Hopping between next-nearest layers. In the alternating stack, layers l and l+2 are aligned, so there is no momentum mismatch between them. In Bernal graphite the analogous next-nearest-layer hopping (γ₂ ≈ −20 meV) largely sets the zeroth-Landau-level dispersion, so it cannot simply be dropped for ATG.
   - The "untwisted control" is also not a faithful model of Bernal graphite. Its zeroth Landau level is the bilayer-like n = 0,1 manifold with γ₁ ≈ 0.4 eV, not a stack of monolayer zeroth Landau levels with a 10 meV hop.
   - Action:
     - Derive the effective Landau-level hopping for each stacking, including its B dependence and the l, l+2 terms for ATG.
     - Most usefully, present phase diagrams in the (t⊥/(e²/εd), d/l_B) plane, so that the critical tunneling separating SILC from generalized Halperin states is explicit and readers can place any material on it.

3. The variational comparison is biased between correlated liquids and uncorrelated metals, and its space is truncated where the claimed physics occurs.
   - The SILC competitor (Supp. Note 2.3) is a Slater determinant with no intralayer correlations. Its Coulomb energy contains exchange only.
   - The generalized Halperin and composite-fermion states all carry Jastrow correlations. Comparing correlated liquids with an uncorrelated metal systematically favours the liquids, and the size of this bias directly sets the critical t⊥ in concern 2.
   - No correlated metallic or density-wave competitor along the field direction is included, such as a Jastrow-dressed SILC, a composite-fermion Fermi sea with interlayer coherence, or the quasi-1D CDW the Introduction itself invokes (Fig. 1c).
   - The liquids restrict interlayer zeros to third neighbours (m ≥ n ≥ o ≥ p). At the fields where the key claims are made, d/l_B ≈ 0.013–0.025 (1–3 T). There the Coulomb interaction is almost uniform over l_B/d ≈ 40–75 layers, so states with longer-range interlayer correlations, or genuinely 3D liquids or crystals, are natural competitors and are absent.
   - Action:
     - Add correlated metallic and longer-range-correlated trial states.
     - Benchmark the variational ranking against an unbiased method on small systems: exact diagonalization or DMRG for N_L = 3–4 layers with periodic boundary conditions, and a few electrons per layer, at the relevant d/l_B and t⊥.
     - Show that the overlap with, or energetic ranking of, the (3110)/(3111) states survives.

4. No energies, uncertainties or convergence data are shown, so the phase boundaries cannot be assessed. The low-field region also appears to lie outside the computed grid.
   - The manuscript presents only colour-coded phase diagrams. No plot shows E/N against d/l_B for the leading competitors, with Monte Carlo statistical errors, the 1/N_L extrapolation fits, or the margin by which (3110)⊕(3110) and (3111)⊕(3111) win.
   - Every calculation uses N̄ = 48 per layer (Methods; Supp. Note 6). There is no extrapolation in the in-plane particle number, although competing states have different shifts (S = 1 to 9). In 2D, crystal and liquid energies at ν = 1/7–1/9 differ by roughly 10⁻³ e²/εl_B, comparable to typical single-size finite-size errors.
   - Supp. Table 9 lists cutoff parameters only for d/l_B ≥ 0.05, which is about 15 T for d = 3.35 Å. The generalized Halperin windows quoted at 1–3 T (d/l_B ≈ 0.013–0.023), and the bars in Fig. 3f,g that reach B ≈ 0, are therefore either computed on an unstated grid or extrapolated.
   - Action:
     - Provide energy-difference curves with error bars for the top three or four states at ν_L = 1/2, 1/7 and 1/9 for each stacking.
     - Show the 1/N_L and 1/N̄ extrapolations.
     - List the d/l_B grid actually computed and mark where boundaries are interpolated.

B. Should address

5. The phenomenological short-range interactions are fitted, treated in mean field, and sometimes overridden by hand.
   - V₁–V₃ are "chosen to reproduce" bilayer and trilayer phase diagrams (Methods; Supp. Table 5). The tabulated V₂ ≈ 0.4–0.5 × d/l_B (in e²/εl_B) is comparable to the liquid–liquid energy differences that decide the phase diagram. The HTG values are not referenced.
   - V₁–V₃ enter through a Hartree mean field (Eq. S63), which by construction cannot distinguish interlayer-correlated liquids from uncorrelated states with the same densities.
   - Supp. Note 6c states that when the mean-field ranking yields an "unphysical" state, the valley texture is chosen by reversing the mean-field ordering. This is an ad hoc rule applied to the very states being claimed.
   - The robustness test (Supp. Table 6, Supp. Fig. 1) changes all V's in one direction only, to values about 100 times smaller.
   - Action:
     - State at which (ν_L, d/l_B) points the exception rule was invoked.
     - Show the phase diagram with V's set to zero and with V's scaled up as well as down.
     - Give the source of the HTG parameters.

6. Spin is frozen out at 1–3 T without justification.
   - The model assumes complete spin polarization (p. 3). At 1–3 T, graphene's bare Zeeman energy is about 0.1–0.35 meV, far below e²/εl_B (about 4–8 meV) and below t⊥.
   - The zeroth Landau level of each graphene layer has four spin-valley flavours. The relevant competitors therefore include spin-unpolarized and spin-valley-entangled Halperin states, spin-singlet composite-fermion states and canted orders, none of which are in the 862-state space.
   - Action: include the spin degree of freedom, or restrict the claims to fields at which spin polarization is established (experimentally or by exchange), and update the "as low as 1 T" statements accordingly.

7. Incompressibility and gap size are not established.
   - The paper calls the winning states FQH phases and proposes them for experiment, but computes no transport or neutral gaps.
   - For infinite-layer K matrices, the gap can be small when the smallest K eigenvalue is small. For (3111), the minimum eigenvalue of m + 2n cos k + 2o cos 2k + 2p cos 3k is about 0.37.
   - The ground-state energy margins are also not shown (concern 4).
   - Action: estimate quasiparticle–quasihole gaps, for example from Monte Carlo energies of quasihole trial states. Compare them with disorder and temperature scales typical of graphene multilayers.

8. The "non-foliated" claim is asserted rather than demonstrated, and no experimental discriminator is proposed.
   - Supp. Note 5 states that exponentially decaying, nonzero θ_ij "ensures the non-foliated property". An explicit argument, or a precise citation (for example to the criteria in ref. [7]), is needed showing that these particular K matrices are not foliated.
   - Supp. Fig. 6c shows a slowly decaying, oscillating θ_ij for (3111), not a clean exponential. Please quantify the decay length.
   - Experimentally, the per-layer Hall response 2e²/7h of (3110)⊕(3110) is also produced by stacked 2D 1/7 states per valley. The paper does not say which measurement would distinguish its states from the staged or foliated competitors.
   - Action: specify observables that distinguish the claimed phase, such as thickness dependence of the ground-state degeneracy, interlayer drag or counterflow, or surface-sheath transport. Discuss how open boundaries in finite stacks (the calculation uses periodic boundary conditions along z) affect them.

9. Experimental realizability of uniform fractional filling across many layers is not discussed.
   - The proposal needs every layer of a stack of more than 20 layers held at the same ν_L ≈ 1/7–1/9 per valley, which is about 10¹⁰–10¹¹ cm⁻² per layer.
   - External gates are screened within a few layers. Gate-induced charge gradients act exactly like a staging field and would favour the staged phases already competing in Fig. 3.
   - Twist-angle variation and dislocations in grown spirals (refs. [27–30]) produce inhomogeneous t⊥.
   - Action: discuss how density is set in interior layers. Estimate the tolerance of the generalized Halperin windows to a layer-dependent potential, using the staging energies already derived in Supp. Note 2.6, and to t⊥ disorder.

10. The extension to TMDs (Supp. Note 4) neglects the relevant band physics.
    - The TMD model drops the valley index and uses K-point interlayer hoppings.
    - In multilayer and bulk 2H TMDs, the valence-band maximum is usually at Γ and the conduction-band minimum often at Q/Λ. At Γ, interlayer hopping is hundreds of meV, and a twist does not suppress it because there is no momentum mismatch at the zone centre.
    - The 180° layer alternation of 2H stacking also couples spin and valley between layers.
    - Action: either justify which valley is active for the proposed carrier type and stack, or soften the statement that the "main results extend straightforwardly" to TMDs (p. 3, p. 5).

11. The approximate energy relations used to generate most of the variational space have unquantified errors.
    - Energies of product states (Eq. S36), particle-hole conjugates (Eq. S39) and staged states (Eqs. S41–S47, capacitor-like with d ≪ R and intralayer correlations assumed unchanged) are obtained from approximate relations, not computed directly.
    - Most phases that win in Fig. 3 are products or conjugates, for example (3111)⊕PH(3111).
    - Action: for a few winning states, compare these approximations with direct Monte Carlo evaluation of the full product or conjugate wavefunction, and report the discrepancy against the energy margins.

C. Minor

12. Supp. Table 6: the second row is labelled V₁ again and should presumably be V₂.
13. Supp. Fig. 4 caption: the label "f" is used twice (the field-comparison panel and the twisted 2–3 meV panel). MoSe₂ is given t⊥ ≈ 17.75 meV in the caption but 17.15 meV in Supp. Table 7.
14. The twist-angle assumptions are inconsistent across the paper: "θ ≳ 10°" (main text), "0 ≤ θ < 30°" (Methods), "slightly less than 30°" (Supp. Note 3c) and "10° ≪ θ < 30°" (Supp. Fig. 3). For the helical stack, layer l+2 is rotated by 2θ, which for θ near 30° approaches 60°, where K and K′ interchange. State which θ the HTG results assume and how valley labels are assigned between non-adjacent layers.
15. Eq. (S22) uses t̃⊥, which is never defined.
16. Notation: ν̄ (per layer, Supp. Table 1) and ν_L (per layer per valley) are used side by side. σ_xy = 2e²/7h should be stated explicitly as per layer.
17. The Fig. 3 legend reads "Not staged", while Supp. Figs. 1–3 use "Unstaged". Several Supp. Note 4 sentences are garbled, for example "TMDs are the graphites, but without the valley degeneracy" and "magentic".
18. Fig. 3e–g draw bars down to B = 0. Please mark the lowest field actually computed and note the very low densities this implies at ν_L = 1/9.
19. "The first realistic condensed-matter platform" (Discussion) is stronger than a variational phase diagram supports. Temper it until concerns 1–4 are resolved.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Replace the material-specific bar charts with a master phase diagram in the plane of tunneling (in units of e²/εd) against d/l_B at each filling. Overlay Bernal, ATG, HTG and the TMDs as lines once the tunneling units (concern 1) are fixed. This makes the claim independent of any single parameter estimate.
2. Add a short exact-diagonalization or DMRG benchmark on small periodic stacks to validate the variational ranking between SILC, staged and generalized Halperin states.
3. Include a figure of energy differences with error bars and finite-size extrapolations for the winning states. Also add an estimate of their gaps.
4. Add a short section on experimental signatures and on how filling would be controlled in the interior layers, so that the "experimentally accessible" claim is concrete.
