A. Must address

1. The slip sizes come from hand-selected slips, the selection favours the expected answer, and the full distribution is not shown. The headline values |Δθ_ps|/2π = 0.330(72) at ν = 1+1/3, 0.206(50) at −2/5 and 0.221(30) at 1+1/2 (Fig. 3d–f) each rest on about 4–10 hand-picked steps (about 7 visible in Fig. 3e) out of a population the authors put at ~200–300. Section IV says the analysis "concentrate[s] on regions that contain the most prominent Δθ_ps to analyze the jumps closest to the statistical value", and the Ext. Fig. 3 caption adds that "for many less prominent jumps … Δθ_ps is often less than the theoretically expected value", attributing these to bulk-edge coupling. Choosing steps by closeness to the expected value builds in agreement with that value. The extraction has further free choices in Methods F: the trimmed-mean window (0.5–2 mV, trim 10%), the plateau threshold ("0.5–0.25 of the maximum derivative"), given as ranges without saying which value was used for each dataset, and the choice to average only negative Δθ to obtain ⟨θ_cnt⟩. Ext. Fig. 9 also contains slip-size analyses for ν_cav = −1/3 and −1/2 that the main text never discusses; as far as the small labels can be read, the −1/3 steps (for example in the 1 < ν_AD < 2 row) cluster around 0.10–0.25, below the Laughlin value 1/3 that the text uses to validate the method at 1+1/3, and the −1/2 values also appear to sit near 0.1–0.25 rather than 1/4 (or 1/2). If so, this is an important counterexample, and the abstract's statement that the slips agree with the expected values for abelian anyons rests on a subset of slips in a subset of states. The authors should fix the jump-detection and step-height criteria in advance (automated) and apply them to every resolved slip; show histograms of Δθ_ps, with signed values and the number of slips, for every (ν_cav, ν_AD) combination including −1/3 and −1/2; report the unselected mean, median and spread and the fraction of jumps within the quoted uncertainty of theory; tabulate the −1/3 and −1/2 results in the main text; state which threshold values were used and how Δθ_ps changes across the trimming and plateau ranges; and discuss explicitly any state that departs from theory. If the distributions are broad and centred below the statistical value, the claim must be restated as an upper envelope.

3. Coupling between the AD and the interfering edge may set the slip size, and it is never estimated. Between slips the phase winds continuously with a large negative slope, and each slip is a positive jump; the two nearly cancel, leaving an average slope "the same as the small slope observed in the slow-change regime" (Fig. 2c, Section III, Methods F). This sawtooth is the generic signature of a charging island capacitively coupled to the interfering edge: each discrete charging event partly resets a continuously built-up phase, and the jump is θ_a minus a coupling term of order 2πζ_AD(e*_loc/e)/ν̃ (Halperin–Stern–Neder–Rosenow; Ref. [63]), roughly (continuous slope) × (slip spacing), which is non-universal and can be as large as the jump itself. The AD edge sits only ~150–250 nm from the interfering edge in a cavity of effective area 0.56 µm², and the bridge gate also tunes the cavity area (Section III). The authors invoke ζ = K_IL/K_I to explain the smaller jumps (Ext. Fig. 3) but assume the analysed "prominent" jumps carry the bare θ_a (Δθ_ps = θ_a, Methods F); this is not self-consistent without an independent coupling measurement. Methods D's conclusion that ζ is small constrains the cavity's bulk-edge coupling as a whole, not the AD-to-edge coupling. With ζ_AD unknown and of either sign, steps near 1/3, 1/4 or 1/5 could arise for more than one charge assignment. The authors should estimate the AD–edge mutual capacitance relative to the edge stiffness from their COMSOL/Thomas-Fermi model and from data not involving statistics (for example the ratio of the inter-slip slope to the regime-(i) slope, or the AB phase shift per unit AD charge in the smooth regime), and propagate it into the expected jump; plot Δθ_ps against (θ_cnt slope × Δs) slip by slip across states and windows; show that Δθ_ps stays fixed while slope and spacing vary with B (7.95 T data in Ext. Fig. 8), V_BG, cavity size (plunger), AD–edge distance, position within a window and AD Landau level (Ext. Fig. 9); fit a model with AD–edge and bulk–edge coupling (ζ measured or free) and report how far Δθ_ps can be pulled from θ_a; and explain why the coupling would be negligible for the prominent jumps but not for the others.

4. The slip sign contradicts the loading direction, and with only cos θ measured the mod-2π branch, and hence the matched theoretical value, is not fixed. Section IV says the slip sign "consistently corresponds to removing quasiparticles … even though sweeping V_brg … loads additional quasiparticles" (also Ext. Fig. 10), and the Conclusion lists this and the large negative inter-slip slope as open questions. Because the phase is defined mod 2π, the sign decides the match: +1/3 is the same as −2/3, +1/4 the same as −3/4, and at −2/5 the K-matrix values −2π(2/5) and −2π(1/5) (Methods G) correspond to +3/5 and +4/5 as positive jumps. At 1+1/2, |θ| = 1/4 and 3/4 are equally possible, especially if the e/2 interfering excitation can be in the ψ fusion channel, whose braiding with σ adds π. The branch of each jump is inferred from continuity with the subtracted θ_cnt; given the large opposite-sign θ_cnt, it is unclear how a +2π/3 jump is distinguished from a −4π/3 jump. The main text compares only magnitudes. The authors should explain how the θ_cnt/Δθ_ps decomposition fixes the branch; give a signed comparison using the SI sign conventions (Eqs. 4–5); determine from the AB slope in Fig. 1d and the V_brg loading direction whether quasiparticles or quasiholes are added; state which signed theoretical phase each measurement is compared with; and resolve, or at least model, the sign inversion (treated as an open inconsistency, not a minor note) before tying the step size uniquely to θ_a.

5. Null and reference controls in which the statistical phase is trivial are missing. With the cavity at integer filling (ν_cav = 1, 2 or −1, available in the measured range, Ext. Fig. 2), electrons entering the AD give a 2π braiding phase, so any visible discrete slip would measure the pure Coulomb or bulk-edge contribution, while charge-induced area changes would still appear. Further controls are a ν_AD window where the AD is incompressible at integer filling (integer AD with fractional edge), a check that discrete slips vanish when the hole region is depleted or filled far from any fractional plateau, and the data the authors say were taken on this device "initially … without the lithographically defined AD" (Methods D), which are the natural baseline for the rate of spontaneous disorder-induced slips. Without these, a jump of about 1/3 of a period at 1+1/3 cannot be separated from a coupling-induced jump of similar size or from electrostatic artifacts at the bridge-gate overhang of the trenches, which Section III acknowledges also tunes the area. The authors should show the same V_brg–V_TG maps and step analysis at integer ν_cav under the AD conditions of Figs. 2–4, for an integer ν_AD, and from before the hole was etched, reporting whether slips appear and how large they are.

6. The charge estimates from slip spacing do not independently support e/4 and e/5, and with a common AD radius they point the other way. The spacing estimate |e*_loc/e| = πr_AD² Δs (C/eA)_brg, with (C/eA)_brg = 3.8e14 F m⁻² C⁻¹, scales with r_AD², which is not measured: Methods D gives ~100 nm for the lithographic hole, the main text 150–200 nm, and Ext. Figs. 3–5 use 100–200 nm, over which the inferred charge changes about fourfold, so the quoted range 0.2–0.4 includes 1/5, 1/4 and 1/3 and only excludes e. With r_AD = 150–200 nm as used for 1+1/3 (Δs = 8.7 ± 5.5 mV, giving 0.2–0.4), the same formula gives 0.35–0.63 at 1+1/2 (Δs = 13.2 ± 5.6 mV from only 9 slips, Ext. Fig. 5) and 0.30–0.54 at −2/5 (Δs = 11.3 ± 15.7 mV, Ext. Fig. 4); Ext. Figs. 4d and 5d reach 1/5 and 1/4 only by widening to 100–200 nm. Radius-independently, Δs(1+1/2)/Δs(1+1/3) = 1.52 should equal e_loc(1+1/2)/e_loc(1+1/3), which is 0.75 for e/4 and 1.5 for e/2; for −2/5 the ratio is 1.30, against 0.6 for e/5 and 1.2 for 2e/5. On their face the spacing data favour e/2 and 2e/5, contradicting the slip-size conclusions. The spacings are also broad (relative spreads of 63%, 140% and 42%), which does not fit "evenly spaced" (Section III) and suggests missed, merged or misidentified peaks, or a mixture of slip types, given the peak-picking in Ext. Figs. 3c–5c. In addition, (C/eA)_brg is derived in Methods E from m_TG = dV_TG/dV_brg times the bulk top-gate capacitance, while Methods H argues that the top gate has negligible effect on the AD density (f(V_TG) ≈ 0); if the top gate does not couple to the AD, m_TG cannot set the AD capacitance. The authors should show the spacing histograms; resolve the capacitance inconsistency; bound r_AD independently (Thomas-Fermi simulation at each state's actual gate voltages, or the AD Landau-level periodicity in V_brg), or justify a state-dependent r_AD; and otherwise drop the claim that the spacing analysis confirms e/4 and e/5 and is "inconsistent with larger charges", presenting it as a consistency check that excludes only e.

7. The interfering-edge charges, on which every predicted braiding phase depends, rest on one fitted area and a circular argument. In Fig. 1f and Methods D the area A = 0.56 µm² (against a lithographic 0.7 µm²) is a free parameter in a linear fit of φ0/ΔB against ν̃_cav that assumes e*_ie = ν̃_cav e; the "measured" charges (0.31, 0.49, −0.36, −0.41, −0.50) are then obtained by dividing by that A, and Methods D concludes that ζ is small "since the charges we extract are very close to the theoretical charges". The hole-like and electron-like states were measured with the Si back gate at −35 V and +35 V and plunger voltages of opposite sign (2 to 4 V versus −4 to −2 V), so they need not share one effective area, and an AD at a different filling also changes the effective area. 8. An e/4 localized charge with an e/2 interferer does not test non-abelian statistics. With e*_ie = ν̃e the expected Δθ_ps/2π reduces to e*_loc/e for every state considered, so the measurement probes the charge-sector (U(1)) phase. The π/2 braiding of a localized e/4 with the abelian e/2 interferer (up to the ψ-channel sign) is the same for the Pfaffian, anti-Pfaffian and abelian candidates such as 331 or K = 8, and no even-odd effect can appear with an e/2 interferer. The paper uses "putative", but the abstract and conclusion ("putative non-abelian anyons remain localized"; "Controlling the population of localized e/4 anyons … marks a significant milestone"; "fault tolerant topological qubit") read as evidence of non-abelian loading. The authors should state that the data fix the localized charge and abelian mutual statistics (if concerns 1–2 are resolved) but not non-abelian character, list the compatible candidate orders, temper the milestone and qubit language, and discuss or attempt the test this AD enables: a weak e/4-periodic AB component, for example in a regime where e/4 tunnelling at the QPCs is enhanced, whose visibility alternates with AD parity across successive slips.
