Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors put a gate-defined anti-dot (AD), controlled by a bridge gate over a hole in the top graphite gate, inside a bilayer-graphene Fabry-Pérot interferometer. They report long series of discrete phase slips in the interference signal, which they attribute to fractional quasiparticles entering the AD one at a time. From the slip sizes they infer braiding phases of about 1/3, 1/5 and 1/4 (in units of 2π) at ν = 1+1/3, −2/5 and 1+1/2. Deterministic, gate-controlled loading of localized anyons in an interferometer would be an important technical advance over relying on random traps, especially for even-denominator states. The main quantitative claims, above all the "direct measurement" of a localized charge e/4 at ν = 1+1/2, depend on a phase-slip extraction whose selection, statistics and systematics are not yet established well enough.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new and clearly useful device concept: an AD with independent gate control inside a high-quality BLG FPI. It gives several hundred gate-tunable slip events, compared with a handful of stochastic slips from random traps.
- Clean Aharonov-Bohm interference in five fractional states, including two even-denominator states. The interfering charges (Fig. 1d–f, Methods: 0.31, 0.49, −0.36, −0.41, −0.50) are consistent with e*_ie = ν̃_cav e, in agreement with recent BLG work.
- The slips repeat over 50 sweeps (Ext. Fig. 6), they have a consistent sign, and they group into three AD-filling windows per cavity state, with an ordering that follows ν_cav (Fig. 4b). This points to a deterministic charging process rather than telegraph noise.
- The capacitance-based charge estimates rule out charge e entering the AD (Ext. Figs. 3–5, 10g).
- Self-consistent COMSOL/Thomas-Fermi electrostatics (SI) support the parallel-plate estimate of ν_AD.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The phase-slip sizes come from hand-selected regions, and the full distribution is not shown. The headline values are |Δθ_ps|/2π = 0.330(72), 0.206(50) and 0.221(30) (Fig. 3d–f). Section IV says the analysis is restricted to "regions that contain the most prominent Δθ_ps to analyze the jumps closest to the statistical value". Ext. Fig. 3 adds that "for many less prominent jumps … Δθ_ps is often less than the theoretically expected value". Each value in Fig. 3d–f therefore rests on about 4–10 hand-picked steps out of a claimed ~200–300. Steps were also selected by closeness to the expected answer, which biases the result toward that answer.
   - Further steps add analysis freedom: the trimmed-mean window (0.5–2 mV, trim 10%), the plateau threshold ("0.5–0.25 of the maximum derivative"), and the choice to average only negative Δθ to obtain ⟨θ_cnt⟩.
   - Ext. Fig. 9 shows slip-size analyses for ν_cav = −1/2 and −1/3 that are not discussed in the text. As far as the small labels can be read, the ν_cav = −1/3 steps in the 1 < ν_AD < 2 row are about 0.10–0.24, well below the Laughlin value of 1/3 that the text uses to validate the method at ν = 1+1/3.
   - The abstract's statement that "for abelian anyons, the observed phase slips agree with the theoretically expected value" therefore rests on a subset of slips in a subset of states.
   - The authors should:
     - fix the extraction parameters in advance and apply them to every resolved slip in each region;
     - show histograms of Δθ_ps for all states and all ν_AD windows (including ν_cav = −1/3 and −1/2), with the number of slips;
     - report how the means change with the trimming and plateau thresholds;
     - discuss explicitly any state where the result departs from theory.

2. The data cannot yet tell e/4 from e/5 (or 1/4 from 1/5 braiding), which the e/4 headline needs. At ν = 1+1/2 the reported value is 0.221(30). This is 1σ from 0.25 and 0.7σ from 0.20, the value claimed at ν = −2/5 (0.206(50), itself 0.9σ from 0.25).
   - The paper does not say whether the bracketed uncertainty is the standard deviation of individual steps or the standard error of the mean, or how many steps enter Fig. 3e (about 7 are visible).
   - The independent charge estimates do not discriminate either:
     - The slip spacing is Δs = 0.0132 ± 0.0056 V at 1+1/2, 0.0113 ± 0.0157 V at −2/5 (the uncertainty exceeds the value) and 8.7 ± 5.5 mV at 1+1/3.
     - Combined with r_AD left free between 100 and 200 nm, these give |e*_loc/e| = 0.2–0.4 or wider (Ext. Figs. 3–5d). That only excludes e.
     - The counting estimate extrapolates N ≥ 9 observed slips to N_ps ≥ 200 at 1+1/2 (Ext. Fig. 5), and N ≥ 29 to ≥ 200 at −2/5, assuming equal spacing.
   - "Culminating in the direct measurement of charge |e*_loc/e| = 1/4" (Introduction, abstract) is therefore not supported at a confidence level a reader can assess.
   - The authors should:
     - state the error definition;
     - give the likelihood or goodness of fit of the step distribution for the 1/4, 1/5 and 1/3 hypotheses (and 3/4, see concern 3);
     - add the slips needed to separate them, for example from other ν_AD windows, other bridge-gate ranges and the B = 7.95 T data already taken;
     - otherwise, soften the abstract and conclusion.

3. Coupling between the AD and the interfering edge, and the sign anomaly, leave the step size open to other interpretations.
   - The step size is identified with θ_a (Δθ_ps = θ_a, Methods F). But the bridge gate also tunes the cavity area (Section III), and the AD edge sits only ~150–250 nm from the interfering edge in a cavity of effective area 0.56 µm².
   - When charge enters the AD, the interfering edge can relax capacitively, which adds a term of order −2π ζ_AD (e*_loc/e)/ν̃. Ext. Fig. 3 invokes exactly this mechanism to explain the smaller slips.
   - Methods D concludes that ζ is small from the cavity Aharonov-Bohm periodicities. That constrains the bulk-edge coupling of the cavity as a whole, not the AD-to-edge coupling.
   - The paper also reports three features it does not understand: (i) a large negative phase slope between slips (Conclusion); (ii) slip signs that correspond to removing quasiparticles while the gate loads them (Section IV); (iii) smaller slips away from the prominent ones.
   - Because only cos θ is measured, the sign determines which mod-2π branch is assigned. A step of +1/3 is −2/3. At ν = 1+1/2, |θ| = 1/4 and 3/4 are equally possible, especially if the e/2 interfering excitation can be in the ψ fusion channel, whose braiding with σ adds π.
   - With an unknown ζ_AD of either sign, the combination θ_a − 2πζ_AD e*_loc/(ν̃e) could produce steps near 1/3, 1/4 or 1/5 for more than one charge assignment.
   - The authors should:
     - estimate the AD–edge mutual capacitance relative to the edge stiffness from their own COMSOL/Thomas-Fermi model and propagate it into Δθ_ps;
     - test whether Δθ_ps changes with B, using the 7.95 T data in Ext. Fig. 8, or with cavity size (plunger) and AD–edge distance. The statistical part should not change, while a capacitive contribution should;
     - explain how the decomposition into θ_cnt and Δθ_ps fixes the branch;
     - resolve, or at least model, the sign inversion before tying the step size uniquely to θ_a.

B. Should address

4. The text and figures disagree on which AD filling the representative data belong to.
   - Fig. 2a (V_BG = 0.2425 V, V_TG ≈ 0.302–0.3025 V, slips near V_brg ≈ 0) appears in Fig. 4a and Ext. Fig. 7 as the panel centred on the ν_AD ≈ 0.6 window. Yet the text says the Fig. 2 slip region "coincides with ν_AD ≈ ν_cav". Fig. 2b (V_BG = 0.2706 V, V_TG ≈ 0.2735 V) likewise matches the 0 < ν_AD < 1 row of Ext. Fig. 9, not the ν_AD ≈ 1+1/3 data in Fig. 3a.
   - Ext. Fig. 6 (ν_cav = 1+1/3, 0.557 < ν_AD < 0.568) shows three sinusoids, that is 2π/3 jumps. Yet the FFT step heights for the same ν_AD window in Ext. Fig. 9 (middle row, ν_cav = 1+1/3) appear to be mostly about 0.15–0.23. Section V says slips with ν_AD ≠ ν_cav are "comparable to, or smaller than" the statistical phase.
   - The authors should label each dataset with its ν_AD, correct the Section III statement, and reconcile the Ext. Fig. 6 and Ext. Fig. 9 analyses of the same window. If the two methods give different step sizes on the same data, that bears directly on concern 1.

5. An e/4 localized charge does not by itself indicate non-abelian anyons. The interfering excitation at 1+1/2 is the abelian e/2. Its braiding phase with a localized e/4 (π/2 up to the ψ-channel sign) is the same for the Pfaffian, anti-Pfaffian and the abelian 331 or K = 8 candidates. No even-odd effect can appear with an e/2 interferer.
   - The abstract and conclusion ("putative non-abelian anyons remain localized"; "Controlling the population of localized e/4 anyons … significant milestone") are appropriately hedged in places, but elsewhere they read as evidence about non-abelian character.
   - The authors should:
     - state clearly that the measurement probes charge and abelian mutual statistics only;
     - list which candidate orders are compatible;
     - if possible, search the FFT for a weak e/4-periodic Aharonov-Bohm component whose visibility alternates with AD parity across successive slips. This would be the actual non-abelian test that the AD enables.

6. Null and reference controls are missing.
   - Integer cavity filling (for example ν_cav = 1 or 2) is a natural control: there, electrons entering the AD should give 2π (invisible) slips, while charge-induced area changes would still appear.
   - Another control is a ν_AD window where the AD region is incompressible at an integer filling.
   - A third check is whether discrete slips vanish when the hole region is depleted or filled far from any fractional plateau.
   - Without these, the step sizes cannot be separated from gate-induced electrostatic artifacts at the bridge-gate overhang of the trenches, which Section III acknowledges also tunes the area.
   - The authors should show the same V_brg–V_TG maps and step analysis at integer ν_cav and for an integer ν_AD.

7. All data come from one device at one temperature, and only one field was used for the slip-size analysis.
   - Fig. 1b shows "a similar device"; everything else is from a single FPI with one AD of uncertain radius.
   - Device-to-device spread, behaviour after thermal cycling, and dependence on AD size cannot be assessed. AD size is a natural lever: Δs should scale as 1/r_AD² and N_ps as r_AD², while Δθ_ps should not change.
   - The authors should add a second device, ideally with a different hole radius. At minimum, they should repeat the slip-size extraction after a thermal cycle and at 7.95 T.

8. The ν_AD calibration and the three-window interpretation rest on limited checks.
   - The f(V_TG) check (Ext. Fig. 7c) uses three scans.
   - In the field check (Ext. Fig. 8), window centres move by 0.07–0.10 in ν_AD between 7.95 and 9.95 T, with horizontal bars of about ±0.2.
   - The Thomas-Fermi model has two free parameters (r_AD and XC), and the smoothing constant κ = 1 meV is set by hand.
   - The windows lie at ν_AD ≈ −0.3, 0.6 and 1.4–1.56, not at simple fractions. Their assignment to −1/3, 2/3 and 1+1/3 (SI Fig. 1) depends on an "least steep profile" argument that is chosen after the fact.
   - The statement that the AD "fills" once per LL also needs the zero-density offset of the AD region, which is not independently measured.
   - The authors should:
     - show ν_AD from simulation and from the parallel-plate model at the actual V_BG and V_TG of each dataset, with uncertainty bands;
     - report where the slip windows sit relative to the plateau edges of the reservoir;
     - present the SI edge-profile picture as a hypothesis, and test it with a prediction, for example which ν_AD windows should appear for ν_cav = −2/5 and 1+1/2.

9. The claims about slip spacing and slip counting are stronger than the data.
   - "Evenly spaced" (Section III) does not fit relative spreads of 63% (1+1/3), 140% (−2/5) and 42% (1+1/2).
   - (C/eA)_brg is obtained from m_TG and the same slip spacing that is then used to infer charge, so the two estimates are not independent.
   - The relation |e*_loc N_ps| ≈ e ν̃ Φ_AD assumes e*_loc = 1/3 to begin with.
   - The authors should:
     - show the Δs distributions as histograms;
     - give how many slips were counted directly and how many were extrapolated;
     - present the counting relation as a consistency check, not as evidence.

10. Excitation, electron temperature and visibility are not characterized. A 20 µV excitation at 10–20 Hz (Methods B) is about ten times k_BT at 20 mK, and visibilities are only ~1–3% (ΔG_D ~ 0.015–0.035 e²/h). At ν = 1+1/2 in particular, heating and nonlinear transport can affect which excitation interferes and how localized charges equilibrate.
   - The authors should give the effective electron temperature, show that the AB amplitude and the slip pattern are linear in excitation, and add a temperature dependence of the visibility and of the slip sizes for at least one state.

11. The interfering-charge extraction and the small-ζ conclusion are partly circular. A is a free parameter in the linear fit of Fig. 1f; the result is 0.56 µm², compared with a lithographic area of 0.7 µm². Methods D then infers small ζ because the charges match theory.
   - The authors should fit A and ζ together, using the ν/ν̃ dependence of the correction, report the constraint on ζ, and state the ν_AD at which each Fig. 1d map was taken. An AD at a different filling changes the effective area.

C. Minor

12. Ext. Fig. 2 caption lists "−2/3" among the measured states instead of −1/3. Ext. Fig. 10a,b y-axes label ν_AD in units of e²/h. The Ext. Fig. 8 caption has "derivative if the conductance". The Fig. 2 caption has "indicates that that". The Introduction has "has lead".

13. Methods D gives r_AD ~ 100 nm for the hole, the main text gives 150–200 nm, and Ext. Figs. 3–5 use 100–200 nm. Please state one convention: lithographic radius against effective radius in the graphene plane.

14. Units and notation: Δs is quoted in mV in the main text and in V in Methods and the figures. ΔG and ΔG_D are both used for the same quantity (Fig. 1 caption). Fig. 3d–f quote values without uncertainties while the text gives them. The text calls Fig. 2c the "upper panel" and "lower panel" but does not name the corresponding datasets.

15. Fig. 3g–i: the V_brg values were "selected to include a sufficient number of phase slips". Please describe how the traces were chosen, and show that the number of sinusoid families comes out the same under an objective clustering, for example a fit of N equally spaced phase offsets with N free.

16. SI Fig. 1 marks "Yes/No" against "Do we see jumps experimentally" for six AD fillings. Please mark which ν_AD ranges were actually measured, and how densely, so that a "No" is distinguishable from "not measured".

17. Please state data and analysis-code availability. Given the analysis freedom in concern 1, the extraction scripts matter for reproducibility.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Fix the step-extraction pipeline before analysis and apply it to every slip. Present histograms of Δθ_ps per state and per ν_AD window, with a quantitative comparison of the hypotheses (1/3, 1/4, 1/5, and their mod-2π complements).
2. Use the B = 7.95 T data and plunger or cavity-size variation to separate the topological part of each slip from the capacitive part. The topological part should be independent of B and geometry, while the AD–edge coupling term should not be.
3. Add integer-filling null controls and a second device or AD radius. This would show that the slip size tracks the bulk topological order and not the electrostatics of the bridge-gate overhang.
4. Restate the 1+1/2 result as localized charge e/4 with abelian mutual statistics relative to the e/2 interferer. Outline, or attempt, the parity-dependent e/4 interference test that this AD geometry makes possible.
