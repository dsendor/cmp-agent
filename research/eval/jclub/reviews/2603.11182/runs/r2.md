Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors etch a hole in the top graphite gate of a bilayer-graphene Fabry-Pérot interferometer and cover it with a separate bridge gate. This makes a gate-tunable anti-dot (AD) inside the cavity. Sweeping the bridge gate produces long, reproducible sequences of discrete interference phase slips at ν = −1/2, −2/5, −1/3, 1+1/3 and 1+1/2. The authors read these as quasiparticles of charge e/3, e/5 and e/4 entering the AD one at a time, with braiding phases near 2π/3, 2π/5 and 2π/4. Controlled, gate-defined loading of localized anyons, especially in an even-denominator state, would be an important step beyond experiments that rely on random disorder traps. The interferometer is technically impressive. The quantitative link from the slips to anyon charge and statistics is where the paper needs more support.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new and useful device concept. A bridge-gated AD inside a graphite-gated bilayer-graphene interferometer gives deterministic control of the localized quasiparticle population. Fig. 2 and Extended Fig. 6 show that the slips recur at the same bridge-gate voltage on repeated sweeps.
- Very large data sets. Tens to hundreds of slips per state, measured in five fractional states, in several AD Landau-level windows (Fig. 4, Extended Fig. 9) and at two magnetic fields (Extended Fig. 8).
- For ν = 1+1/3, the mean slip size 0.330(72) agrees with the expected 1/3, and the interleaved-sinusoid plots (Fig. 3g–i) show the discreteness of the phase clearly.
- The repeated groups of slips as the AD crosses successive Landau levels, and their ordering with ν_cav (Fig. 4b), are new observations about how the AD edge is structured.
- Electrostatic modelling (COMSOL plus Thomas-Fermi, SI) supports the parallel-plate estimate of the AD filling.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The slip sizes do not separate the candidate charges, and the slips analysed were selected.
   - Where: Section IV, Fig. 3d–f, Extended Fig. 9, Methods F.
   - Selection: the text says "we concentrate on regions that contain the most prominent Δθ_ps to analyze the jumps closest to the statistical value". Smaller jumps elsewhere are attributed to bulk-edge coupling (Extended Fig. 3 caption). Selecting jumps because they lie close to the expected value builds in agreement with that value.
   - Overlap: the two key numbers are 0.206(50) at −2/5 (expected 1/5) and 0.221(30) at 1+1/2 (expected 1/4). They are statistically indistinguishable from each other, and each lies within one quoted uncertainty of both 1/5 and 1/4. The individual steps in Fig. 3e range from about 0.185 to 0.27, and in Fig. 3f from about 0.116 to 0.254. As presented, the e/4 assignment at 1+1/2 is therefore not distinguished from e/5, or from a reduced value of 1/4 or 1/3 caused by bulk-edge coupling.
   - Unanalysed states: ν_cav = −1/3 and −1/2 also show slips (Fig. 4b, Extended Fig. 9) but get no quantitative analysis in the main text. For −1/3, the step values printed in Extended Fig. 9 appear to sit mostly around 0.1–0.3, below the 1/3 expected for this Laughlin state. If so, this is an important counterexample.
   - Requested:
     - For every state and every AD window, a histogram of all detected slips, extracted with fixed criteria set before looking at the values.
     - A clear definition of the quoted uncertainty: standard deviation of individual steps, or standard error of the mean.
     - The same analysis for −1/3 and −1/2.
     - An explicit test of whether 1/4 is statistically preferred over 1/5 and 1/3 at ν = 1+1/2.

2. Electrostatics, not braiding statistics, may set the slip size.
   - Where: Fig. 2c, Fig. 3d–f, Methods F, Conclusion.
   - Observation: between slips the phase winds continuously with a large negative slope. Each slip is a positive jump. The two nearly cancel, leaving an average slope "the same as the small slope observed in the slow-change regime" (Section III).
   - Why it matters: this sawtooth is what one expects when a charging island (the AD) is capacitively coupled to the interfering edge. In that case each discrete charging event partly resets a phase built up continuously, and the jump size is roughly (continuous slope) × (slip spacing). That is a non-universal, electrostatic quantity. The authors themselves invoke ζ = K_IL/K_I to explain smaller jumps (Extended Fig. 3). AD–edge coupling can in principle shift the larger jumps by a comparable amount.
   - The sign is also unexplained. The slips correspond to removing quasiparticles even though the bridge gate adds them (Section IV, Extended Fig. 10). The authors list the sign and the large negative slope as open questions. Both bear directly on whether Δθ_ps equals θ_a.
   - Requested:
     - Plot Δθ_ps against θ_cnt slope × Δs, slip by slip, across states and windows.
     - Show that Δθ_ps stays fixed while the continuous slope and the slip spacing vary: different V_BG and B, different positions within a window, different AD Landau levels (Extended Fig. 9 data).
     - Fit a model that includes AD–edge and bulk–edge coupling, with ζ either measured independently or left free, and report how far Δθ_ps can be pulled away from θ_a.
     - Give a physical account of the slip sign that is consistent with the claimed statistical phase.

3. The interfering-edge charge is inferred circularly.
   - Where: Fig. 1f, Methods D.
   - The area A = 0.56 µm² comes from fitting φ0/ΔB against ν̃_cav, assuming e*_ie = ν̃_cav e. The "measured" charges (0.31, 0.49, −0.36, −0.41, −0.50) are then obtained by dividing by that same A. The conclusion that bulk-edge coupling ζ is small "since the charges we extract are very close to the theoretical charges" relies on the same assumption.
   - The hole-like and electron-like states were measured with the Si back gate at −35 V and +35 V and with plunger voltages of opposite sign (2 to 4 V versus −4 to −2 V). The two groups therefore need not share one effective area.
   - The e/2 at 1+1/2 and 2e/5 at −2/5 assignments are central to every braiding phase used later. They differ from the GaAs results (e/5 at 2/5), so they need an independent check.
   - Requested:
     - An independent area calibration: ΔB at integer fillings (ν_cav = ±1, ±2) under the same gate settings, separately for the hole-side and electron-side configurations.
     - Ideally, DC-bias (checkerboard) measurements of e*_ie for each fractional state.
     - The intercept and the uncertainty of the Fig. 1f fit.

B. Should address

4. The charge estimates from slip spacing and slip counting add little independent support.
   - Where: Section III, Methods E, Extended Figs. 3–5, 10.
   - The spacing estimate e*_loc = A·Δs·m_TG·(C/eA)_TG scales with r_AD², and r_AD is not measured. The paper assumes 150–200 nm, against a lithographic hole of 100 nm. Over r_AD = 100–200 nm the inferred charge changes roughly fourfold. The quoted range 0.2–0.4 includes 1/5, 1/4 and 1/3, so it cannot separate the states it is meant to support.
   - The spacings are broad: 8.7 ± 5.5 mV (1+1/3), 11.3 ± 15.7 mV (−2/5, where the standard deviation exceeds the mean) and 13.2 ± 5.6 mV from only 9 slips (1+1/2). These spreads are hard to square with "evenly spaced". They suggest missed or merged peaks, given the peak-picking in Extended Figs. 3c–5c.
   - The counting relation |e*_loc N_ps| ≈ e ν̃ Φ_AD is close to an identity. N_ps is the bridge-voltage range divided by Δs. Multiplying it by e*_loc ∝ (C/eA)_brg πr_AD² Δs gives Δν_AD Φ_AD, computed with the same capacitance and radius. In addition, N_ps ≈ 300 is extrapolated from ≥ 70 counted slips by assuming uniform spacing.
   - There is also a contradiction. Methods E derives (C/eA)_brg from m_TG = dV_TG/dV_brg times the bulk top-gate capacitance. Methods H then argues that the top gate has negligible effect on the AD density (f(V_TG) ≈ 0). If the top gate does not couple to the AD, m_TG cannot set the AD capacitance.
   - Requested:
     - Present these estimates as consistency checks rather than independent charge measurements.
     - Show the spacing histograms.
     - Resolve the capacitance inconsistency.
     - Bound r_AD independently, for example from the simulations or from the AD Landau-level periodicity in V_brg.

5. The braiding phase measured at 1+1/2 does not distinguish non-abelian from abelian states.
   - Where: abstract, Section IV, Conclusion.
   - With e*_ie = ν̃e, the expected Δθ_ps/2π reduces to e*_loc/e for every state considered. The quantity measured is the charge-sector (U(1)) phase. An e/4 quasiparticle in an abelian candidate (for example the Halperin 331 state) gives the same π/2 as the Ising σ.
   - Because the edge carries e/2, no even-odd visibility effect is available as a non-abelian signature.
   - The abstract does say "putative". The abstract and conclusion should nevertheless state that the data fix the localized charge (if concern 1 is resolved) but not the non-abelian character. The phrases "milestone" and "fault tolerant topological qubit" should be tempered.
   - The authors could note which observations, such as visibility alternation if e/4 interference were reached, would test non-abelian statistics.

6. Null and reference controls are missing, and only one device is shown.
   - Where: Methods D, Fig. 1b.
   - The device was "initially measured … without the lithographically defined AD", but no data from before the hole was etched are shown. Those data are the natural baseline for the rate of spontaneous, disorder-induced slips.
   - No integer-filling control is shown. With the cavity at ν = 1 or 2, an electron entering the AD should give a trivial 2π phase, so no visible slips.
   - Fig. 1b shows "a similar device", not the one measured. All data come from one device, apparently from one cooldown. Thermal-cycle reproducibility is not shown.
   - Requested:
     - Bridge-gate sweeps at integer cavity filling.
     - Data from before the hole was etched.
     - Ideally, a second AD device or a second cooldown that reproduces the slip sizes.

7. The AD filling calibration is not precise enough for the filling-window and ordering claims.
   - Where: Fig. 4b, Extended Fig. 8, SI B and SI Fig. 1.
   - The ordering claim (slips for −1/2 below −2/5, below −1/3) rests on peak positions that differ by 0.05–0.15 in ν_AD. Between 7.95 T and 9.95 T the same peaks move by comparable or larger amounts: for example −0.21 → −0.28 and 1.64 → 1.54, and the lowest −1/2 group is absent at 7.95 T.
   - The Extended Fig. 8 caption assigns the values to ν_cav = −1/2 and −1/3 in the reverse of the panel labels.
   - The SI picks the "least steep" density profile as a heuristic, without a calculation of edge reconstruction energetics. The account of where phase slips appear is therefore qualitative.
   - Requested:
     - State the ν_AD uncertainty (including the capacitance and f(V_TG) error) on the Fig. 4b axes.
     - Test whether the ordering survives that uncertainty.
     - Present SI B as a hypothesis.

8. In the slow-change regions, the destination of the added charge is not accounted for.
   - Where: Fig. 2a, Fig. 4a.
   - Across most of the bridge-gate range (white region in Fig. 2a; between the peaks in Fig. 4) the AD density still changes, yet no slips are seen.
   - Unless the charge goes into compressible regions that are invisible to the interference, this conflicts with the picture that each localized quasiparticle produces a slip.
   - The Thomas-Fermi profiles (Extended Fig. 10c,d) show compressible rings. The authors should use them to state where charge accumulates in these regions, and estimate how many quasiparticles enter without a detectable slip.

9. Several dependences are missing, and the electron temperature is not known.
   - All data are at the 20 mK base temperature, with a 20 µV excitation applied through a divider. The excitation amplitude at the sample and the electron temperature are not stated.
   - Requested:
     - Temperature dependence of visibility and of Δθ_ps: a statistical phase should be temperature-independent, whereas an electrostatic one may not be.
     - Excitation-amplitude dependence.
     - Bridge-gate sweep-direction (hysteresis) tests.
   - Extended Fig. 6 shows repeatability for only one narrow window at 1+1/3, and that window includes random-telegraph switching. Equivalent data for 1+1/2 would strengthen the even-denominator claim.

10. The simulations are said to "confirm" fractional charge more strongly than they can.
   - Where: SI C, Extended Fig. 10g.
   - The comparison of jump spacing with ΔQ = e/3 versus ΔQ = e depends on r_AD and on an empirical smoothing stiffness κ = 1 meV. The text says κ was chosen ad hoc ("left for future work").
   - ΔQ = e/4 or e/5, or ΔQ = e at a different radius, may match equally well.
   - Requested:
     - Show the ΔQ = e/4 and e/5 curves.
     - Show how sensitive the results are to κ.
     - Describe the simulations as consistent with fractional charge rather than as confirming e/3.

C. Minor

11. Caption and text errors:
   - The Extended Fig. 2 caption lists "−2/3" among the measured states; −1/3 is meant.
   - The Extended Fig. 8 caption labels are swapped (see concern 7).
   - The Fig. 2 caption contains "indicates that that".
   - "has lead" should be "has led".
   - Use either "Extended Fig." or "Extended Data Fig." throughout.

12. Fig. 2b and 2d were taken at V_BG = 0.2706 V and 0.3133 V, while Fig. 2a was taken at V_BG = 0.2425 V. Yet the panels are described as "around the same AD density as in (a)". State the ν_AD of each panel explicitly.

13. Fig. 3g–i: the number of sinusoid families depends on the chosen bridge-gate window and the cut angle φ_s. Give α, β and the window used, and show that the count of families does not change when the window is shifted.

14. The measurement gives only cos θ, so the sign and the modulo-2π branch of each jump are inferred from continuity with the subtracted θ_cnt. Explain how a +2π/3 jump is distinguished from a −4π/3 jump given the large opposite-sign θ_cnt. This matters directly for the sign discussion in Section IV.

15. Methods F, step 3: the plateau threshold of "0.5–0.25 of the maximum derivative" and the trimmed-mean window are tunable. Report how Δθ_ps changes across this range.

16. Show an image of the measured device, including the AD hole and the bridge, instead of only "a similar device" (Fig. 1b).

17. In Extended Figs. 3–5 the y-axis is labelled "diff(θ − ⟨θ_cnt⟩)/2π" and reaches values around 100–200. Give the units, or state that it is normalized.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Restructure Section IV around unselected slip distributions for all five states (including −1/3 and −1/2). Add a quantitative AD–edge/bulk–edge coupling model that shows the extracted Δθ_ps is universal, and state explicitly how well 1/4 is separated from 1/5 at 1+1/2.
2. Calibrate the interferometer area independently at integer fillings, separately for the hole-side and electron-side gate configurations. If possible, add DC-bias measurements of e*_ie, so that the e/2 and 2e/5 edge charges do not rest on the fitted area.
3. Add the integer-cavity bridge-gate control and the pre-AD baseline. Add temperature dependence of Δθ_ps, and, if possible, a second device or cooldown.
4. Recast the spacing, counting and simulation estimates as consistency checks, with r_AD uncertainty shown honestly. Make the abstract and conclusion clear that the measured phase constrains the localized charge, not non-abelian statistics.
