Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors put a gate-defined anti-dot (AD), tuned by an overhanging bridge gate, at the centre of a bilayer-graphene Fabry-Perot interferometer. They report many discrete phase slips in the diagonal conductance as the bridge-gate voltage changes. They read these as single localized quasiparticles entering the AD, and the slip sizes as braiding phases: about 1/3 at nu = 1+1/3, 1/5 at -2/5 and 1/4 at 1+1/2. The last value is presented as gate-controlled loading of putative non-abelian e/4 anyons. Deterministic, gate-controlled changes of the localized quasiparticle number, in place of stochastic disorder traps, would be an important step for anyon interferometry, and the even-denominator result would matter to the non-abelian community. The device concept is new and the data are of high quality. The quantitative support for the statistical-phase and charge assignments, however, is not yet strong enough for the headline claims.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear new device concept: a bridge-gated AD inside a graphite-gated BLG interferometer, independent of the plunger and global gates.
- Smooth Aharonov-Bohm (AB) oscillations in five fractional states, including two even-denominator states. The flux periods scale linearly with the fractional part of the filling (Fig. 1d-f).
- Two regimes under bridge-gate tuning: smooth phase evolution in regime (i) and discrete jumps in regime (ii) (Fig. 2). The slips are reproducible over 50 repeated sweeps, with rare telegraph switching (Ext. Fig. 6).
- Groups of phase-slip regions recur once per Landau level of the AD. Their ordering follows nu_cav (Fig. 4b), and they appear at similar nu_AD at two magnetic fields (Ext. Fig. 8).
- Electrostatic modelling that combines COMSOL and Thomas-Fermi supports the parallel-plate filling assignment (Ext. Fig. 10, SI).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Selection of the phase slips used for the statistical angles, and the full distribution of slip sizes. Section IV says the analysis "concentrate[s] on regions that contain the most prominent Delta theta_ps to analyze the jumps closest to the statistical value". It also says a wider statistical analysis "shows less prominent phase slips with smaller Delta theta_ps" (Ext. Fig. 3 caption: "Delta theta_ps is often less than the theoretically expected value"). The quoted values (0.330(72), 0.221(30), 0.206(50)) come from about 7-10 hand-chosen jumps per state, out of a population the authors say numbers in the hundreds. This choice decides which theoretical value the data appear to match. Ext. Fig. 9 makes this sharper: the plateau labels for nu_cav = -1/3 and -1/2, which the main text never discusses, appear to cluster near 0.1-0.25 rather than 1/3 and 1/4 (or 1/2). The abstract nevertheless says the slips are "consistent with" the expected statistics across the states. The authors should:
   - give a histogram of all extracted Delta theta_ps for each (nu_cav, nu_AD) combination, using a pre-defined, automated jump-detection and step-height criterion;
   - report the mean, median and spread of each distribution without selection;
   - tabulate the Ext. Fig. 9 values for -1/3 and -1/2 in the main text and compare them with theory;
   - state what fraction of jumps falls within the quoted uncertainty of the theoretical value.

   If the distribution is broad and centred below the statistical value, the statistical-angle claim must be restated as an upper envelope, which is a weaker claim.

2. The Coulomb or bulk-edge coupling explanation for the slips has not been excluded quantitatively. Between slips the phase winds rapidly with a negative slope; at each slip it jumps positive. The average reproduces the smooth regime-(i) slope (Fig. 2c, Section III, Methods F). This sawtooth is the generic signature of a charging island capacitively coupled to the interfering edge. In that picture the discrete jump is 2*pi*(e_loc/e*)-weighted statistics minus a coupling term, and the coupling term can be as large as the jump itself (Halperin-Stern-Neder-Rosenow; Ref. [63]). The authors themselves invoke zeta to explain the smaller jumps (Ext. Fig. 3 caption), but they never estimate zeta for the AD-edge coupling. The jumps they do analyse are assumed to carry the bare statistical phase, while the neighbouring jumps are assumed to be reduced by zeta. This is not self-consistent without an independent measure of the coupling. The authors should:
   - extract the AD-edge coupling from data that do not involve statistics, for example the ratio of the inter-slip phase slope to the regime-(i) slope, or the shift of the AB phase per unit change of AD charge in the smooth regime;
   - report the expected jump size theta_a - 2*pi*zeta(...) with that coupling value;
   - explain why the coupling would be negligible for the "prominent" jumps and not for the others.

3. There is no null control in which the statistical phase is trivial. The cleanest test of whether the jump size reflects braiding rather than electrostatics is to load the AD with electrons while the cavity sits at an integer filling (nu_cav = 1 or 2, or -1). There the braiding phase is 2*pi, so any visible discrete slip would measure the pure Coulomb or bulk-edge contribution. A comparable test is an integer edge with fractional AD filling. Neither is shown, although the same gates allow it and the integer states are available in the measured range (Ext. Fig. 2). Without such a control, a jump of about 1/3 of a period at 1+1/3 cannot be separated from a coupling-induced jump that happens to be of similar size. The authors should add bridge-gate sweeps at integer nu_cav, under the same AD conditions as in Figs. 2-4. They should report whether discrete slips appear and how large they are.

4. The charge extracted from the slip spacing does not independently support e/4 and e/5 once the same AD radius is used for every state. Methods E and Ext. Fig. 3 use r_AD = 150-200 nm for nu_cav = 1+1/3 (Delta s = 8.7 mV), which gives |e_loc/e| = 0.2-0.4. The formula |e_loc/e| = pi r_AD^2 Delta s (C/eA)_brg, with (C/eA)_brg = 3.8e14 F m^-2 C^-1, gives:
   - nu_cav = 1+1/2 (Delta s = 13.2 mV, Ext. Fig. 5): 0.35-0.63 over the same 150-200 nm range.
   - nu_cav = -2/5 (Delta s = 11.3 mV, Ext. Fig. 4): 0.30-0.54 over the same range.

   Ext. Figs. 4d and 5d reach consistency with 1/5 and 1/4 only by widening the radius range to 100-200 nm. A radius-independent comparison is more telling. If r_AD is the same, the ratio of spacings Delta s(1+1/2)/Delta s(1+1/3) = 1.52 should equal e_loc(1+1/2)/e_loc(1+1/3). That ratio is 0.75 for e/4 and 1.5 for e/2. The corresponding ratio for -2/5 is 1.30, against 0.6 for e/5 and 1.2 for 2e/5. On their face, the spacing data therefore favour e/2 and 2e/5, which contradicts the conclusions drawn from the phase-slip size. The spacing uncertainties are large (Ext. Fig. 4 quotes 11.3 +/- 15.7 mV), and r_AD could depend on the electrostatic configuration, as the SI notes. In that case, however, the spacing analysis cannot discriminate the candidate charges at all. The authors should either:
   - justify a state-dependent r_AD independently, for example with the Thomas-Fermi simulation at the actual gate voltages of each state; or
   - drop the claim that the spacing analysis confirms e/4 and e/5 and is "inconsistent with larger charges". At present it only excludes charge e.

5. The phase is only defined mod 2*pi, and the slip sign contradicts the loading direction. Section IV says the observed slip sign "consistently corresponds to removing quasiparticles ... even though sweeping V_brg ... loads additional quasiparticles". Because the phase is defined mod 2*pi, the sign decides which theoretical value a measured jump matches:
   - +1/3 is the same as -2/3;
   - +1/4 is the same as -3/4;
   - for -2/5, the K-matrix values -2*pi(2/5) and -2*pi(1/5) (Methods G) correspond to +3/5 and +4/5 when taken as positive jumps.

   The main text compares only |Delta theta_ps| with |theta_a|. If the sign of the braiding phase cannot be reconciled with the known direction of charge loading, matching the magnitude alone does not identify the anyon type. The authors should give a signed comparison using the SI sign conventions (Eqs. 4-5). They should determine, from the AB slope in Fig. 1d and the V_brg loading direction, whether quasiparticles or quasiholes are being added. They should then state which signed theoretical phase each measurement is compared with, and treat the unexplained sign as an open inconsistency rather than as a minor note.

B. Should address

6. The interfering-edge charge depends on one fitted area. Fig. 1f and Methods D obtain e*_ie by fitting phi_0/Delta B against nu_tilde with a single area, A = 0.56 um^2, and then dividing by that A. The linear trend across five states is informative, but the absolute scale rests on assuming the same A in every state, including the positive and negative back-gate polarities. That scale is bounded only by the lithographic 0.7 um^2. Several later steps depend on this scale:
   - the -2/5 analysis depends on e*_ie = 2/5 rather than 1/5;
   - the 1+1/2 analysis depends on e*_ie = 1/2;
   - Methods D concludes that bulk-edge coupling is small "since the charges ... are very close to the theoretical charges", which is circular.

   The authors should measure the AB period at an integer filling (nu_cav = 1 and/or -1) with the same gate geometry, to fix A independently. They should also use the slope of the AB stripes in the B-V_PG plane to estimate zeta directly.

7. The uncertainties are not defined, and they are too large to discriminate between neighbouring alternatives. For the values 0.330(72), 0.221(30) and 0.206(50), the text does not say whether the bracketed figure is the standard deviation of individual jumps or the standard error of the mean. The values listed in Fig. 3d (0.233-0.431) suggest it is the standard deviation. At the quoted precision, 0.221(30) does not separate 1/4 from 1/5, and 0.206(50) does not separate 1/5 from 1/4. The authors should state how the uncertainties are obtained. They should then give the likelihood of each candidate (1/5, 1/4, 1/3, 1/2 and the sign-flipped values) from the full distribution requested in concern 1.

8. The non-abelian claim is overstated relative to what the experiment tests. The 1+1/2 result, a jump of about pi/2 with an e/2 interfering excitation, is the charge-sector phase for an e/4 quasiparticle. It is shared by the Pfaffian, the anti-Pfaffian and abelian candidates such as the 331 state. No measurement probes the neutral sector: for example, an even-odd modulation of e/4-edge visibility as N_qp changes by one, or a comparison of interference with e/4 and e/2 edge tunnelling regimes. The paper uses "putative" in places, but the abstract and the conclusion ("Controlling the population of localized e/4 anyons ... marks a significant milestone ...") read as though non-abelian loading had been demonstrated. The authors should state explicitly that the data cannot tell abelian from non-abelian e/4 quasiparticles. They should also discuss what signature in the present device could, for example visibility alternation between successive e/4 loading events.

9. "Hundreds" of phase slips are partly extrapolated. The directly counted slips are N >= 70 (1+1/3), N >= 29 (-2/5) and N >= 9 (1+1/2) (Ext. Figs. 3-5). These are extrapolated to about 300, >=200 and >=200 by assuming uniform spacing over the whole slip region. The spacing scatter is about 60% for 1+1/3 and larger than the mean for -2/5. Extrapolating from 9 slips to more than 200 is especially weak. The abstract's "hundreds of controlled phase slips" should be restated as the number actually observed, or the full ranges should be counted. The consistency check |e_loc N_ps| ≈ e nu_tilde_AD Phi_AD inherits the same assumptions about r_AD and spacing as concern 4, so it is not independent evidence.

10. The evidence comes from one device, at one temperature and one excitation. The Methods describe a single device, and all data are at T = 20 mK and B = 9.95 T, apart from the nu_AD calibration at 7.95 T. There is no second device or second AD radius, which would test the predicted r_AD^2 scaling of the slip spacing directly and would be a strong check of concern 4. There is no thermal-cycling check, and no temperature or excitation dependence of slip size or visibility. The 20 uV source excitation is roughly ten times k_B T/e at 20 mK, and the electron temperature is not given. The authors should:
   - state the electron temperature;
   - show the temperature dependence of the visibility and of Delta theta_ps for at least one state;
   - show whether the slip size depends on excitation amplitude;
   - if possible, add a second device or AD size.

11. Slips at nu_AD different from nu_cav weaken the claim that the slip signature is unique. Fig. 4 and Ext. Fig. 9 show phase slips in three nu_AD windows per Landau level, including nu_AD ≈ -0.3 and 0.6 while nu_cav = 1+1/3. There, the AD hosts a different FQH state or a compressible ring (Ext. Fig. 10c,d). The explanation offered (SI Fig. 1, which picks the "least steep" density profile) is qualitative. Compressible rings that charge in units of e or of a different fractional charge, and that couple capacitively to the edge, could produce slips in all these windows. That would be the generic electrostatic mechanism of concern 2. The authors should:
   - report Delta theta_ps for each window with the same analysis as Fig. 3;
   - state the braiding phase predicted for each window under the SI edge-reconstruction picture;
   - show whether the predicted and measured values agree.

12. The nu_AD calibration and r_AD are uncertain. Ext. Fig. 8 is presented as showing slips "at the same fillings" at 7.95 and 9.95 T. The peak positions, however, shift by up to about 0.1 in nu_AD (for example 1.64 to 1.54, and -0.21 to -0.28). That shift is comparable to the separation between the slip windows of different nu_cav (Fig. 4b). Neglecting f(V_TG) rests on three back-gate settings (Ext. Fig. 7c). r_AD is never measured. It is inferred from the simulation and then chosen to make the charges agree (Ext. Fig. 10g), and the Thomas-Fermi model contains an ad hoc smoothing parameter kappa = 1 meV. The authors should give uncertainties on nu_AD, show how the slip-region positions scale with B over more than two fields, and state the r_AD range the simulation predicts at each state's actual gate voltages.

13. The sinusoid-family analysis (Fig. 3g-i) is visual. Reading three, four and five interleaved families depends on which V_brg traces are chosen ("selected to include a sufficient number of phase slips") and on the visual grouping. An objective version would be a histogram of the fitted AB phase (mod 2*pi) for every trace across a long series of slips, with clustering tested against n = 2, 3, 4, 5. It should also check that the phase returns to its start after n slips over many cycles. The authors should replace or supplement the visual panels with this analysis.

C. Minor

14. The Ext. Fig. 8 caption swaps the labels: the peak values listed for nu_cav = -1/2 (-0.21, 0.69, 1.64 at 7.95 T; -0.28, 0.62, 1.54 at 9.95 T) are plotted in the -1/3 row, and the reverse. Fig. 4b agrees with the figure, not with the caption.

15. The Ext. Fig. 2 caption lists the five states as "-1/2, -2/5, -2/3, 1+1/3, 1+1/2"; it should read -1/3.

16. Ext. Fig. 4 quotes Delta s = 0.0113 +/- 0.0157 V. A spread larger than the mean suggests misidentified peaks or a mixture of slip types. Please explain, and show the spacing histogram.

17. The Fig. 1 caption says all measurements are at B = 9.95 T, but Fig. 1d spans 9.95-10.05 T. The notation also switches between Phi_0 and phi_0 (Fig. 1f, Ext. Fig. 3).

18. The Ext. Fig. 10a,b axis is labelled "nu_AD (e^2/h)"; filling is dimensionless.

19. In Methods F, the trimmed-mean window (0.5-2 mV) and the plateau threshold (0.5-0.25 of the maximum derivative) are given as ranges. Please state which value was used for each dataset and how sensitive Delta theta_ps is to the choice.

20. Typographical errors: "has lead" (Intro), "the the central cavity", "statistical contrbution" (Methods F), "if the conductance" (Ext. Fig. 8), "Ithaco" (Methods B), "the center if the AD" (SI C4).

21. The statement that "the amount of charges it takes to destabilize the topological order in the AD is roughly equal to the amount forming the topological order itself" (Section III) is suggestive but not quantified. Either support it or soften it.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add an integer-filling control (concern 3) and an independent estimate of the AD-edge coupling (concern 2). Together these would turn the slip-size comparison from a consistency check into a measurement.
2. Replace the hand-selected step heights with an automated, complete analysis of all slips in each window. Include the -1/3 and -1/2 data and the nu_AD ≠ nu_cav windows, and present histograms with signed values.
3. Measure a second AD radius, or a second device. The r_AD^2 scaling of the slip spacing would test the charge assignment without assuming r_AD, and would resolve the tension in concern 4.
4. At 1+1/2, look for visibility alternation or parity effects between successive slips, for example in a regime where e/4 tunnelling at the QPCs is enhanced. This would move the even-denominator result from charge-sector consistency toward a test of non-abelian character.
