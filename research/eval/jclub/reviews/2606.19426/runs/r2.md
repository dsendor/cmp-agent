Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript studies stacks of N_l lowest-Landau-level layers at nu = 1/3 per layer with truncated Haldane pseudopotentials (intralayer V_1^(0) only; interlayer V_0^(1), V_1^(1) and, in Sec. V, V_0^(2)). It uses exact diagonalization (ED) on the sphere and torus, thin-torus perturbation theory and CFT model wavefunctions. It makes four claims: (i) decoupled Laughlin layers (a foliated Laughlin phase) survive sizable nearest-neighbour interlayer interactions up to N_l = 10 or more; (ii) stacks of 2, 3 and 4 layers host SU(3)_{N_l} non-Abelian orders, for which the authors build wavefunctions and spin-TQFT identifications; (iii) at average filling 2/9 per layer, the stack spontaneously trimerizes into a [1/3 1/3 0] layer charge pattern; (iv) in that pattern the occupied pairs form Fibonacci bilayers, giving a "foliated Fibonacci" phase. The 3D energetics question is timely and the multilayer CFT construction (Eqs. 16-22, App. C2) is a useful technical contribution. The central 3D results, (iii) and (iv), currently rest on a truncated short-range model whose intralayer part gives no penalty for redistributing charge between layers, and on very small clusters: two particles per occupied layer for N_l = 9.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First systematic ED study of quantum Hall stacks with N_l > 4 layers, including NNN interlayer interactions and layer-translation symmetry breaking.
- An explicit model wavefunction for the multilayer SU(N_l)_k / SU(3)_{N_l} states (Eq. 21, Eqs. S60-S73), together with an identification of the spin-TQFT, F_{6N_l} ⊠ conj(SU(3)_{N_l}) (Eq. S79). The central charges and degeneracies are internally consistent; for N_l = 2, F_12 ≡ F_-4 and c = 14/5, matching Eq. S27.
- Generalized SU(3)_{N_l} exclusion rules (App. K2), checked layer multiplet by multiplet against SU(N_l)-resolved ED spectra (Table S5, Fig. S10).
- For the bilayer, a consistent picture across sphere and torus. Torus ground-state degeneracy is 9 to 6 (Fig. 3, Fig. S1). Quasihole counting D_1 ⊕ D_3 ⊕ D_5 (Table III, Fig. 4a) argues against the interlayer Pfaffian, whose counting is in Table S3. Overlaps are given with Monte Carlo error bars (Fig. S2).
- A thin-torus mapping of the interlayer pseudopotential problem onto a transverse-field Ising model (Eq. 13, App. J). It shows that the pair hopping generated by V_0^(1) and V_1^(1) plays the role that single-particle tunneling plays in Ref. 84.
- A frank discussion of limitations: the unidentified D = 3 torus region (App. H), and the statement that the foliated Laughlin state within [1/3 1/3 0] cannot be settled (end of Sec. V.B.3).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The trimerized [1/3 1/3 0] order may come from the truncated interaction rather than from the physics of layered quantum Hall systems. The intralayer Hamiltonian has a single pseudopotential, V_1^(0) = 1 (Sec. II.A). With it, any layer at filling nu ≤ 1/3 has exactly zero intralayer energy, so moving electrons from a 2/9 layer into a 1/3 layer costs nothing within the layer. The only penalty comes from interlayer V_0^(1), V_1^(1) and V_0^(2). The classical model of Eq. 24 assumes an intralayer cost V^(0) nu_l^2 that the ED Hamiltonian does not contain at nu ≤ 1/3. A real layered system also has a Hartree (capacitive) cost for a period-3 modulation of sheet densities. Alternating charge sheets create fields between layers, with an energy per electron of order (e^2/eps l_B)(d/l_B) times the density modulation. At d ≲ l_B (Sec. VI.B) this is comparable to the quasihole gaps in Fig. 7. Neither the pseudopotential model nor the thin-torus treatment in Sec. V.B.2 includes this q_parallel → 0 electrostatic term. The authors acknowledge that long-range interactions are left for future work (Sec. VI.B), but the trimerization claim is precisely a long-wavelength charge-redistribution claim. The authors should:
   - repeat the sector competition of Fig. 7(a,b) and Fig. S5 with at least V_3^(0) (and ideally the full projected Coulomb interaction of Eqs. 30-31), and with the Hartree energy of the layer-density modulation included or estimated analytically;
   - show that the [1/3 1/3 0] region survives these additions.
   Without this, claim (iii), and hence (iv), applies only to the truncated model.

2. The sector competition on the sphere is fixed largely by commensurability, and the main uniform competitor is absent from the N_l = 9 data. The N_l = 9 system (Ne = 12, N_phi = 3, Fig. 7b, Fig. 8, Fig. S5) cannot host the uniform [2/9 2/9 2/9] configuration at all, as Sec. V.B.3 notes. With N_phi = 3, any layer holding ≤ 2 particles has zero intralayer energy (2 particles at N_phi = 3 is exactly the Laughlin shift), while 3 particles cost V_1^(0). Placing 12 particles in 9 layers then forces six layers with two particles each, and [220]^3 is essentially the arrangement with the fewest interlayer contacts. The trimerized region is therefore partly built into the choice of (Ne, N_phi, N_l). For N_l = 6 (Ne = 12, N_phi = 6), the [330] sector sits exactly at the Laughlin shift, while the uniform 2-per-layer sector is not at the shift of any incompressible state. Comparing energies across particle-per-layer sectors at fixed N_phi therefore favours the chosen shift. The authors should:
   - provide a size series in which the uniform competitor is present, for example N_l = 9 with Ne = 18, or N_l = 6 with other (Ne, N_phi);
   - compare energies per particle using the standard background-charge and shift corrections when sectors differ in per-layer density;
   - ideally, repeat the competition on the torus, where all sectors share the same N_phi without a shift bias.
   The phrases "spontaneously layer-trimerized" (abstract) and "appears to be robust" (end of Sec. V.B.3) should be qualified until this is done. Without interlayer tunneling the three-fold degeneracy in Fig. 8(c,d) is a sector (translation) degeneracy that holds by construction, so it does not test spontaneous symmetry breaking dynamically.

3. The identification of the foliated Fibonacci phase in the 9-layer system rests on one weak diagnostic. In Fig. 8, each occupied pair holds 2 + 2 particles at N_phi = 3 + 1. The only difference between the expected Fibonacci counting (D_0 ⊕ D_2 per bilayer) and decoupled-Laughlin counting (D_1 ⊗ D_1 = D_0 ⊕ D_1 ⊕ D_2) is whether one L = 1 multiplet per pair lies below the cut. Several problems follow:
   - Other bilayer candidates in Table S1 with shift 3, notably the interlayer Pfaffian, could give the same counting at this size, and the authors do not show they don't.
   - The quantity coloured in Fig. 7(c,d) ("quasi-hole gap difference", Delta_QH) is not defined precisely enough to reproduce.
   - The authors already have the model wavefunction Ψ_Fib (Eq. 12). For the N_l = 9 ground state in the [220]^3 sector, they should compute overlaps with ⊗Ψ_Fib and ⊗Ψ_330 (and with ⊗Ψ_PSS) across Fig. 7(d).
   - They should show that the counting at N_phi = 4 distinguishes Fibonacci from the interlayer Pfaffian for 2 + 2 particles, or else use a larger cluster, for example N_l = 6 with 4 + 4 per pair.
   - If possible, they should give the torus ground-state degeneracy in the [1/3 1/3 0] sector. The prediction is 3 × 6^{N_l/3} for the foliated Fibonacci phase versus 3 × 9^{N_l/3} for foliated Laughlin, up to the CDW factor.
   As things stand, the 9-layer evidence amounts to one L = 1 multiplet moving across a cut.

B. Should address

4. The parameter regime of the Fibonacci phase seems to lie outside what Coulomb interactions give, which undercuts the realizability claim in the abstract and Sec. VI. Sec. II.A states that physical interactions satisfy V_0^(a) > V_1^(a) and V_L^(1) < V_L^(0), and that "all phases we find appear in a reasonable portion of parameter space" obeying these inequalities. Yet Sec. VI.B requires V_1^(1) ~ V_1^(0) and V_1^(1) ≳ V_0^(1), and the blue regions of Fig. 7(c,d) lie mostly at V_1^(1) > V_0^(1). For the LLL Coulomb interaction, V_0/V_1 ≈ 2 at d = 0. Taking that limit, the point (V_1^(1), V_0^(1)) ≈ (1, 2) falls in the S = 1 / singlet region of Fig. 2, and for d > 0 the ratio V_0^(1)/V_1^(1) stays ≳ 2. That places the Coulomb trajectory along or above the Laughlin / (112) boundary (V_0^(1) ~ 2 V_1^(1)), not in the Fibonacci region. Eq. 31 is already in the paper, so the authors should:
   - plot the Coulomb trajectory as a function of d/l_B, with the NNN ratio V^(2)/V^(1), on Figs. 2, 3 and 7;
   - quantify how far Landau-level mixing, the Rytova-Keldysh form or dielectric engineering would have to move it.
   If the trajectory misses the Fibonacci region, the abstract's statement about van der Waals crystals should be softened.

5. The extrapolation of the foliated Laughlin gap is not convincing as presented. Three problems:
   - Fig. 5(a) uses N_l = 10 with only 2 particles per layer at N_phi = 3. At this size the overlap with Ψ_L^{⊗10} cannot tell decoupled Laughlin layers apart from many weakly correlated states.
   - The thermodynamic gap Delta_inf ~ 0.37 comes from fitting Delta = Delta_inf + a/N + b/N_l (Eq. S124) to Table S6. That table shows the gap converging essentially exponentially in N_l: at N = 2 it is identical to 8 digits for N_l ≥ 7. So the b/N_l term is the wrong functional form and mostly fits the N_l = 2, 3 rows. The per-N_l fits quoted in App. L drift from 0.596 (N_l = 2) to 0.461 (N_l = 3); a naive 1/N_l extrapolation of these two would give about 0.19, half the quoted value.
   - The 1/N fits include N = 2, and the data are visibly not linear in 1/N.
   The authors should:
   - fit at fixed N_l ≥ 4, where N_l convergence is complete, using only N ≥ 3;
   - quote the single-layer Laughlin gap of the same V_1^(0) Hamiltonian for comparison;
   - show how the Laughlin / (112) boundary moves with N and N_l, rather than reporting one parameter point (0.6, 0.3).
   The statement "stable up to strengths comparable to the intralayer interactions" (Sec. I.C) needs a boundary with a finite-size trend behind it.

6. The evidence for SU(3)_3 and SU(3)_4 order in three and four layers is thin, and the model wavefunctions are never tested numerically. The support consists of quasihole counting at one point (V_1^(1) = V_1^(0) = 1, V_0^(1) = 0) with 3 particles per layer (Fig. 4b,c) and the SU(N_l)-resolved comparison in Table S5. That table shows mismatches at 2L = 0, 4 and 6 for the trilayer with Ne = 6, and no comparison below 2L = 13 for Ne = 9. The authors should:
   - compute overlaps of the N_l = 3, 4 ED ground states with the wavefunction of Eq. 21. They argue in Sec. I.C that they "construct explicit model wave functions", yet Fig. S4(f) uses the ED state at a reference point as a proxy;
   - give torus ground-state degeneracies, predicted as 10 for N_l = 3 and 15 for N_l = 4 (Table II);
   - note that with NN-only interactions and periodic boundaries the N_l = 4 Hamiltonian is not SU(4) symmetric, since layers 1-3 and 2-4 do not interact, and state how this affects the claim of an "intrinsically four-layer order".

7. The text and the appendices disagree about the bilayer three-fold region. Sec. III.C and Fig. 3(b) label the torus D = 3 region "Ψ_singlet" and state that the Halperin (112) overlap is the larger one there. App. H, however, rules out both PH(1/3) and (112) for this region: the gap closes along an interpolation to the (112)-realizing Coulomb Hamiltonian. It concludes that the region's "topological character ... remains an open question". On the sphere, the S = 1 region is identified only through overlap with a Coulomb-singlet proxy (Fig. 2b). The authors should make the main text, Fig. 3(b) and Table I consistent with App. H. They should also say whether the S = 1 sphere region and the D = 3 torus region are the same phase; this matters because Sec. V.A builds the multilayer phase-separation argument on the (112) identification.

8. The thin-torus analysis is presented as explaining the ED phase diagram, but its predictions depend on the torus aspect ratio. From Eq. 13 and Eq. S106, the decoupled-Laughlin (ferromagnetic) phase requires |J_1| < V_2^∥ / 2. That is |V_0^(1) − κ^2 V_1^(1)| < 8 ε^3 κ^2 V_1^(0), a narrow wedge around V_0^(1) = κ^2 V_1^(1) that closes as ε → 0. The ED finds an extended region bounded by V_0^(1) ~ 2 V_1^(1). Matching the two would require κ^2 ≈ 2 (ε ≈ 0.37), which is far from the thin-torus limit. Similarly, Eq. 29 and Fig. 6 compare the classical advantage εκ^2 V_1^(1)/2 with a quantum gain whose size depends on κ, and Fig. 6 is only a schematic. The authors should:
   - state the κ-dependence explicitly;
   - plot Eqs. 27-29 for representative κ in place of the schematic Fig. 6;
   - tone down "the tendencies ... are consistent" where the thin-torus result predicts generic instability of the decoupled phase.

9. Interlayer tunneling is excluded, yet trimerization requires charge transfer between layers. Sec. V.B says a small tunneling is needed for the layers to redistribute charge and that the phases survive tunneling much smaller than the gap. But tunneling is itself the drive of the Laughlin-to-Fibonacci transition in Ref. 84, and it mixes the particle-per-layer sectors, adding kinetic energy for the uniform configuration. In a crystal with d ≲ l_B ≈ 8 nm, it is also unlikely to be small. The authors should estimate the tunneling scale at which the [1/3 1/3 0] state is destabilized, for example with second-order perturbation theory between sectors. A related point: in a 3D crystal the average filling is tuned only by B (Sec. VI.A). The paper should say over what range of average filling around 2/9 the trimerized state is expected to remain the ground state, which bears on whether it gives a plateau.

C. Minor

10. Shift selection on the sphere (Fig. 2a, Fig. S4) compares gaps at different N_phi. Small-system gap magnitudes depend strongly on N_phi, and the hashed and S = 0 regions stay unexplained (finite L, small S_z). The torus data partly address this. A sentence on how robust the yellow boundaries are to this criterion, for example comparing with ground-state energy per particle after background correction, would help.

11. The bilayer overlaps in Fig. S2 show the interlayer Pfaffian beating Ψ_Fib at every size, and the two model states overlapping each other (0.75 at 7 + 7) more than either overlaps ED. The main-text sentence "a high overlap (~0.67)" (Sec. III.B) should mention this, and should point to the S = 0 projection discussed in App. D as the natural next step.

12. Eq. 12 has interlayer exponent 4/3, whereas Eq. 21 / S60 give (1 − 1/k) = 2/3 per unordered adjacent pair. The two agree only if, for N_l = 2 with periodic boundaries, the pair (1,2) is counted twice. Please state this, since Fig. S4 explicitly avoids double counting the bilayer interaction in the Hamiltonian.

13. Fig. 4(b) (N_l = 3, Ne = 9, N_phi = 7) predicts half-integer multiplets, D_{3/2} ⊕ D_{5/2} ⊕ D_{9/2}, but the horizontal axis is labelled with integer L. Please relabel the axis, for example as 2L, as in Table S5.

14. Fig. 5(b) caption: "filling 2/3 and shift 3" is ambiguous for N_l-layer systems; presumably 1/3 per layer is meant. The abstract and Sec. V say "up to 10 layers", but Table S6 and Fig. 5(b) go to N_l = 13.

15. The Fig. S5 caption speaks of "different spin sector" and "full polarization", but the panel shows particle-per-layer configurations. The Fig. 7 caption has the typo "redregion".

16. Framing. The phases actually stabilized are stacks of decoupled 2D orders: Laughlin layers, or Fibonacci bilayers separated by empty layers. The genuinely interlayer-entangled foliated Fibonacci state of Fig. 1(c) and App. M is not found in the numerics, as the authors say. The abstract and Sec. I.A should make clear that the "fracton" character here is that of the simplest stacked foliated phases.

17. The model treats spinless, single-component electrons. For the candidate van der Waals and misfit platforms (Sec. VI.A), a sentence on spin and valley polarization at the relevant fields would help readers judge the material proposal.

18. The reference list contains duplicates: [84] = [88] (Vaezi and Barkeshli), [103] = [162], [109] = [200], [141] = [211], [191] = [215], [4] = [197].

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one realistic-interaction calculation: projected Coulomb with layer separation d, or at least V_1^(0) plus V_3^(0) plus a Hartree term for the layer-density modulation. Run it for the bilayer phase diagram and for the N_l = 6 and N_l = 9 sector competitions, and overlay the Coulomb d/l_B trajectory on Figs. 2, 3 and 7.
2. Use the model wavefunctions already in hand as diagnostics in the multilayer numerics. This means overlaps of N_l = 3, 4 ground states with Eq. 21, and overlaps of the N_l = 9 [220]^3 ground state with products of Ψ_Fib, Ψ_330 and Ψ_PSS.
3. Move the sector competition to the torus, or add sphere sizes that include the uniform 2/9 competitor, so that the trimerization does not rest on a single commensurate (Ne, N_phi, N_l) choice.
4. Replace the schematic Fig. 6 with the thin-torus energies of Eqs. 27-29 evaluated at specific κ. Also report the decoupled-Laughlin phase boundary as a function of N and N_l, rather than the gap at a single point.
