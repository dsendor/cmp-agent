Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper studies stacks of ν = 1/3 lowest-Landau-level layers coupled only by short-range interlayer Haldane pseudopotentials. It makes three claims. First, decoupled Laughlin layers (a foliated Laughlin phase) survive interlayer coupling up to V0^(1) ~ 2V1^(1). Second, NN-coupled stacks of 2, 3 and 4 layers host SU(3)_Nℓ non-Abelian orders, for which the authors give a CFT model wavefunction and spin-TQFT identification. Third, with NNN interactions included, the stack spontaneously trimerizes into [1/3 1/3 0] units, giving a foliated Fibonacci phase in exact diagonalization (ED) of up to 9–10 layers. Energetics of FQH multilayers beyond Nℓ = 4 have not been studied numerically before, so this is a real step. The multilayer SU(N)_k wavefunction construction and the exclusion-rule generalization are useful in their own right. The headline 3D result, however, depends on a charge-ordering mechanism and on very small per-layer systems, and both need more support.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First ED study of FQH multilayers with Nℓ well above 4. It combines sphere and torus geometries, quasihole counting, thin-torus analysis and Monte Carlo overlaps.
- Explicit, general CFT construction of SU(Nℓ)_k multilayer wavefunctions (Eq. 21, App. C2), with the spin-TQFT identified as F_{2kNℓ} ⊠ conj(SU(k)_Nℓ) (Eq. S79). The SU(3)_Nℓ generalized exclusion rules (App. K2) are checked against ED layer-multiplet decompositions (Table S5).
- A transparent thin-torus picture (Eq. 13, App. J): interlayer pseudopotentials generate correlated pair hopping, which maps onto the transverse-field Ising model of Ref. 84 without single-particle tunneling.
- The paper is candid about open issues: the hashed region in Fig. 2, the unidentified D = 3 torus region (App. H), and whether the foliated Laughlin state survives inside the trimerized sector.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The layer charge-density wave behind the foliated Fibonacci phase may be an artifact of the truncated short-range interaction.
   - The classical argument in Sec. V B1 (Eqs. 24–25) says the q_z = 2π/3 modulation wins when V^(1) + V^(2) > 2V^(0). That requires the q_z-dependent layer kernel λ(q) to be negative at some q_z.
   - For a real Coulomb interaction with a neutralizing background, the kernel at in-plane q → 0 is positive definite. Any layer-density modulation then costs a capacitive charging energy of order (e²/ε) d (Δn)² per area, which near d ~ lB is comparable to FQH gaps.
   - The truncated model drops this term. It keeps V1^(0), V0^(1), V1^(1), V0^(2) and sets the long-range tails of the intralayer and interlayer pseudopotentials to zero, and those tails are exactly what encodes the q → 0 difference between intralayer and interlayer interactions.
   - The trimerized region in Fig. 7(a,b) (roughly V1^(1) ≳ 0.3, V0^(1) ≳ 0.4) corresponds to interlayer Hartree couplings of the same order as the intralayer one, a regime Coulomb does not reach. The authors' own remark that the (112) state "describes a phase separated state" (Sec. III A, App. A) is a symptom of the same missing term.
   - Because the foliated Fibonacci phase (Fig. 1f, Figs. 7–8, abstract) exists only within the [1/3 1/3 0] sector, the main 3D conclusion currently holds for the pseudopotential model only.
   - What to do:
     - State where the neutralizing charge sits (dopants in each layer, or a 3D background).
     - Add the capacitive term, or keep a longer tail of Coulomb-derived V_m^(n) from Eq. (31), to the Hamiltonian behind Figs. 7, S5 and Eq. (24).
     - Show whether the trimerized sector survives.
     - If it does not, reframe the claim as a property of the short-range model and say which physical ingredient (for example dielectric engineering of the spacer, Sec. VI B) could make λ(2π/3) < 0.

2. The ED evidence for the foliated Fibonacci phase uses very small per-layer systems, and part of the sector competition is excluded by construction.
   - In the Nℓ = 9 calculation (Figs. 7b,d, 8), Ne = 12 and Nφ = 3. Each occupied layer holds only 2 electrons; quasiholes are probed with 2 electrons at Nφ = 4.
   - Because Ne = 12 is not divisible by 9, the uniform-filling competitor is absent (Sec. V B3). The larger trimerized region for Nℓ = 9 than for Nℓ = 6 is therefore partly built into the system choice.
   - The identification rests only on quasihole counting. For two particles per layer the multiplets are tiny (D1 per Laughlin layer, D0 ⊕ D2 per Fibonacci bilayer). The paper does not show that competing bilayer states (interlayer Pfaffian/PSS, (112), PH(1/3)) give different counting at these sizes. For the interlayer Pfaffian this was shown only at 5+5 (Table S3).
   - For Nℓ = 6 the text says the evidence is "stronger" (Sec. V B3). However, the overlap of the blue region of Fig. 7(c) with the gapped region of Fig. 7(a) is never plotted.
   - There is also no gap scaling or torus degeneracy for the foliated Fibonacci phase, unlike the foliated Laughlin phase (Fig. 5b).
   - What to do:
     - Give the predicted quasihole counting of the competing bilayer states at 2+2, Nφ = 4 and at 3+3, Nφ = 7.
     - Plot the intersection of the trimerized and Fibonacci-counting regions for Nℓ = 6.
     - Compute overlaps of the Nℓ = 6 and 9 ground states with products of the bilayer Fibonacci wavefunction (Eq. 12) and of Laughlin states.
     - Where feasible, add a larger per-layer size (for example Nℓ = 6 with 4 electrons per occupied layer) or the torus ground-state degeneracy of the trimerized state (3 × 6^{Nℓ/3} versus 3 × 9^{Nℓ/3}).

B. Should address

3. The bilayer Fibonacci identification and the location of the Laughlin–Fibonacci transition rest on weak diagnostics.
   - Overlaps:
     - Sec. III B quotes a Fibonacci overlap of ~0.67 at 7+7. App. D (Fig. S2) shows that the interlayer Pfaffian overlap is higher at every size (0.97, 0.91, 0.85, 0.78, 0.715 versus 0.94, 0.87, 0.80, 0.73, 0.67), and both fall steadily with Ne.
     - Fig. 2(b) compares only the Fibonacci and (330) overlaps.
     - On the torus (Fig. 3b), the "Fibonacci" reference is the ED ground state at V0^(1) = 0, V1^(1) = 1. This shows adiabatic continuity within the phase, not that the phase is Fibonacci.
   - Phase boundary: the Laughlin–Fibonacci boundary on the sphere is drawn where one overlap exceeds the other. App. G states that no gap collapse is seen, and Fig. S7 shows an ambiguous ×3/×6 structure near V1^(1) = 0.2.
   - The 6-fold torus degeneracy (Fig. S1) and the counting in Fig. 4(a) are the real evidence. They come from one or two sizes.
   - What to do:
     - Report the interlayer Pfaffian overlaps in the main text.
     - Project Ψ_Fib onto S = 0, as the authors suggest in App. D.
     - Add orbital entanglement-spectrum counting, which separates SU(3)_2 from Ising × U(1) edges.
     - Show the torus degeneracy along the cut of Fig. S7 for at least two sizes (Ne = 8 and 10), so that the 9 → 6 change is shown to be size-independent.

4. The stability and gap of the foliated Laughlin phase in many layers are not established at the claimed level.
   - Fig. 5(a) (Nℓ = 10) has 2 electrons per layer at Nφ = 3, where the Laughlin state is nearly trivial. The stability region drawn from it is therefore not strong evidence for large stacks.
   - The gap extrapolation (Fig. 5b, App. L):
     - It is done at a single point (V0^(1), V1^(1)) = (0.6, 0.3).
     - It uses Δ = Δ∞ + a/N + b/Nℓ. Table S6 shows the Nℓ dependence saturates (to eight digits for N = 2 beyond Nℓ ≈ 7), so a 1/Nℓ form is not justified.
     - The N = 2 points dominate the slope.
     - The two procedures give Δ∞ = 0.366 and 0.596/0.461 (for Nℓ = 2/3). That spread should appear as an uncertainty.
   - The gap is computed at fixed particle number per layer. The lowest excitation may instead be an interlayer charge transfer, which lies in another sector. Such transfers are included for Fig. 7 but not here.
   - What to do:
     - Include neighboring per-layer sectors in Δ.
     - Extrapolate along a cut toward the boundary, for example at several V0^(1) with V1^(1) fixed, so the phrase "stable up to interlayer interactions comparable to the intralayer interactions" is quantified.
     - Quote fit uncertainties, and check the extrapolation with N = 2 excluded.

5. The SU(3)_Nℓ claim for Nℓ = 3 and 4 rests on one system size each, and agreement with the prediction is partial.
   - Fig. 4(b,c) uses 3 electrons per layer.
   - Table S5:
     - For the trilayer with Ne = 9, ED either did not converge for 2L ≤ 11 or disagrees with the prediction at 2L = 13 and 15.
     - The bilayer with Ne = 8 disagrees at 2L = 0, 4, 6 and 12.
     - This does not match "excellent agreement" (Sec. IV B).
   - The model wavefunction of Eq. (21) is never compared with ED for Nℓ > 2; Fig. S4(f) uses an ED reference state instead.
   - No torus degeneracy (predicted 10 for Nℓ = 3 and 15 for Nℓ = 4, Table II) is shown.
   - Periodic layer boundary conditions make the Nℓ = 3 case all-to-all coupled, which differs from an open triple-layer well (Refs. 157–159).
   - What to do:
     - Add at least one more size or a torus degeneracy for Nℓ = 3.
     - Give Monte Carlo overlaps with Eq. (21).
     - Comment on open versus periodic layer boundary conditions.
     - Soften the agreement statement.

6. The paper never places the model's parameter regime against realistic interactions.
   - Sec. II A states that Coulomb gives V0^(a) > V1^(a) and argues that all phases lie in a physically reasonable region.
   - Sec. VI B says the Fibonacci phase needs V1^(1) ~ V1^(0) (so d ≲ lB) and V1^(1) ≳ V0^(1).
   - For Coulomb at d → 0, V0^(1)/V1^(1) ≈ 2. That sits on the decoupled-Laughlin boundary of Figs. 2–3, and the truncation drops V3^(0), V2^(1) and the NNN V1^(2).
   - What to do:
     - Evaluate Eq. (31), with finite well width or a Rytova–Keldysh form, for a range of d/lB and mark the resulting (V0^(1)/V1^(0), V1^(1)/V1^(0)) points on Figs. 2, 3 and 7.
     - Run at least one bilayer ED with the full Coulomb interaction versus d/lB to test whether the Fibonacci region is reached.
   - Without this, the statement in the abstract about realization in van der Waals crystals is unsupported.

7. The main text and App. H contradict each other on the 3-fold degenerate torus region.
   - Sec. III C says the Halperin (112) overlap "is larger throughout the 3-fold degenerate region", which implies a (112) identification. Fig. 2(b) also shows a high Ψ_singlet overlap.
   - App. H concludes that interpolation to a (112)-realizing Coulomb Hamiltonian closes the gap, "ruling out (112)", and that the region's character "remains an open question".
   - What to do:
     - Bring the main text into line with App. H.
     - Show the interpolation spectrum mentioned in App. H.
     - Explain how the high sphere singlet overlap fits with the torus result.

8. The experimental-realization discussion leaves out interlayer tunneling, density control and disorder.
   - The model has no single-particle tunneling. Sec. V B notes that tunneling is needed for the charge redistribution behind trimerization, and that the phases survive if tunneling is much smaller than the gap.
   - For misfit or van der Waals crystals with d of order 1 nm (Sec. VI A), interlayer tunneling is not obviously small compared with sub-meV FQH gaps.
   - Filling 1/3 at 10 T requires about 8 × 10^10 cm^-2 per layer, set by intrinsic doping, with matching disorder.
   - What to do: estimate tunneling scales and the needed doping homogeneity for the proposed platforms, and state the tunneling threshold relative to the computed gaps (in units of V1^(0) converted to meV).

C. Minor

9. Table II and App. A (Eq. S8) list a ground-state degeneracy of 1 for Nℓ ≡ 0 (mod 3), where det K = 0. A singular K matrix signals gapless neutral modes, as App. A itself says, not a unique gapped ground state. Mark that entry "gapless/undefined".

10. Text and figure mislabels:
    - Sec. III C says "In Fig. 3(b) we have plotted the overlap of the ground state on the sphere", but Fig. 3 is the torus.
    - Sec. V A says "energy gap at filling 2/3" for general Nℓ; this should be 1/3 per layer.
    - Fig. 7 caption reads "(a) Nℓ = 6 and (b) Nφ = 3".
    - Fig. 4(b) has a half-integer total L (predicted D_{3/2} ⊕ D_{5/2} ⊕ D_{9/2}) but integer axis ticks.
    - The colorbar labels in Figs. 2–3 overlap ("0 10 10 1").
    - The Fig. 8 caption contains "the corresponding" starting a sentence, and "redregion" appears in Fig. 7.
    - The Fig. S5 caption ("different spin sector") does not describe what is plotted.

11. State every fixed pseudopotential for each figure. In particular, give V1^(2) in Figs. 7–8 and say whether NNN couplings enter Fig. 5. Also state that "Ψ_Fib" in Fig. 3(b) and "SU(3)_Nℓ" in Fig. S4(f) are ED reference states, not model wavefunctions.

12. Several references are duplicated: [84]/[88], [109]/[200], [141]/[211] and [191]/[215].

13. Eq. (29) is an order-of-magnitude estimate. A direct comparison of its predicted window (~2V1^(0) − V0^(1)/κ², Fig. 6) with the ED boundaries of Fig. 7 would show how far the thin-torus picture can be trusted.

14. App. M's "foliated Fibonacci state" has no charge modulation and differs from the main-text phase. Use a distinct name to avoid confusion with Fig. 1(c) and (f).

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one calculation with Coulomb-derived interactions, including the long-range (capacitive) part, for both the bilayer phase diagram and the Nℓ = 6/9 sector competition. This would tell readers directly whether the trimerized Fibonacci phase is a model artifact or a physical possibility.
2. Build a uniform set of topological diagnostics for every phase claimed: torus degeneracy, quasihole counting with the predictions for competing states listed, and entanglement-spectrum counting, each at two or more sizes. Present them in one table.
3. Use the multilayer model wavefunctions the paper constructs: overlaps with Eq. (21) for Nℓ = 3, 4, and with products of bilayer Fibonacci states for the foliated phase.
4. Move the key caveats into the main text and abstract: the interlayer Pfaffian's higher overlaps, the 2-electron-per-layer sizes, and the exclusion of the uniform sector for Nℓ = 9. The strength of the evidence would then be clear without reading the appendices.
