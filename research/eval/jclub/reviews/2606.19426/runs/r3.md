Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper studies stacks of ν = 1/3 lowest-Landau-level layers with Haldane-pseudopotential interactions. It uses exact diagonalization (ED) for up to 10 layers, thin-torus analysis and conformal field theory (CFT) wavefunction constructions. It makes two claims about the three-dimensional limit. First, the decoupled-Laughlin foliated phase survives sizeable interlayer coupling. Second, next-nearest-neighbour (NNN) interlayer interactions can drive a spontaneous layer trimerization, [1/3 1/3 0]^{Nℓ/3}, whose occupied bilayers host the non-Abelian Fibonacci order. This would be a foliated (fracton-like) non-Abelian phase. Other parts are mostly technical additions to known bilayer results (Refs. 84–86): SU(3)_{Nℓ} generalized exclusion rules, the CFT wavefunctions for Nℓ layers and the spin-TQFT identification. The trimerization mechanism, and the idea that an empty buffer layer stabilizes a foliated Fibonacci stack, are the new and potentially interesting physics. They are also the parts with the thinnest numerical support.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The paper asks a clear new question: which foliated FQH phases are energetically favoured in a microscopic multilayer model, rather than only which are topologically allowed. It combines three complementary tools (sphere and torus ED, thin-torus perturbation theory, CFT/TQFT).
- The bilayer analysis is careful and self-consistent. Sphere shift sectors, torus degeneracies (9/6/3), Monte Carlo overlaps (Fig. S2), quasihole counting (Fig. 4a, Table III) and the rejection of the interlayer Pfaffian by its torus GSD (Fig. S1) and counting (Table S3) agree with one another and with Ref. 86.
- Two results are useful in their own right and other groups could reuse them: the generalized SU(3)_{Nℓ} exclusion principles with layer-multiplet resolution (App. K2, Table S5), and the explicit SU(N_ℓ)_k wavefunction (Eqs. 21, S73) with its spin-TQFT identification F_{2kNℓ} ⊠ SU(k)_{Nℓ}‾ (Eq. S79).
- The quasihole multiplet counts for the foliated Laughlin and foliated Fibonacci sectors in Fig. 8(a,b) match the predicted products of D1 and (D0⊕D2) multiplets. Apart from the L = 0 sector in (a), the counts agree number for number.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The foliated Fibonacci identification rests on two electrons per layer, where the counting barely distinguishes candidate states. The Nℓ = 9 data that carry the headline claim use Ne = 12, Nφ = 3 for the ground state and Nφ = 4 for quasiholes (Figs. 7b,d, 8, S5). Each occupied layer therefore holds 2 electrons in 4 orbitals, and each "Fibonacci bilayer" is a 2+2 system. At this size the Fibonacci quasihole manifold of one bilayer (D0⊕D2) differs from the decoupled-Laughlin one (D1⊗D1 = D0⊕D1⊕D2) only by removing a single D1 multiplet. That is a statement about how two pairs of particles combine, not yet evidence for a non-Abelian topological order. The paper does not show what other bilayer candidates would give at Ne = 2+2, Nφ = 4: the interlayer Pfaffian/PSS of Table S1, or any state that simply suppresses the layer-symmetric (or antisymmetric) pair channel. Nor does it show that the identification survives at three electrons per layer. That case is available: Nℓ = 6, Ne = 12 has 3 per occupied layer (Fig. 7a,c). For Nℓ = 6, though, the paper does not show that the blue quasihole region of Fig. 7(c) overlaps the gapped trimerized ground-state region of Fig. 7(a). The authors should:
   - quantify that overlap for Nℓ = 6, point by point;
   - give the predicted quasihole counting of the competing bilayer states at the sizes used;
   - add a second diagnostic of the foliated Fibonacci state, such as the overlap of the Nℓ = 6 ground state with the product of bilayer Fibonacci wavefunctions (Eq. 12) or of ED bilayer ground states;
   - ideally, give the torus ground-state degeneracy of a trimerized stack.
   Without this, the abstract's statement that "the foliated Fibonacci phase exists in the 9-layer system" is not established.

2. The case for spontaneous trimerization is built in a way that biases it against homogeneous competitors, and its main signature is trivially exact. The paper says so itself for Nℓ = 9 (Sec. V.B.3): "there is no competing state with homogeneous filling at that total particle number (since Ne = 12 is not divisible by Nℓ = 9)." The larger trimerized region in Fig. 7(b) and the dominance of 220220220 in Fig. S5 therefore partly follow from the choice of Ne. For Nℓ = 6, the trimerized and homogeneous [2/9]^6 sectors are compared at a single Nφ = 6 on the sphere. The candidate homogeneous state then has an undetermined shift, which is exactly the aliasing problem the authors flag for the bilayer in Sec. III.C. In addition, the layer particle numbers are conserved (no tunneling). The three-fold degeneracy at N_ℓ k_z/2π = 0, 3, 6 in Fig. 8(c,d) therefore follows from layer translation acting on superselection sectors, not from a dynamical ordering. To support "spontaneous layer-trimerization" the authors should:
   - compare trimerized and homogeneous sectors at several (Ne, Nφ) for which both have a well-defined candidate FQH state at the appropriate shift, or on the torus, where the shift problem is absent;
   - show the trend of the energy difference per particle with system size;
   - add a small interlayer tunneling t to the ED for Nℓ = 6, and show that the layer-density modulation, measured by a layer-resolved density structure factor at k_z = 2π/3, survives and that the quasi-degeneracy splits only weakly.

3. The claim that the foliated Laughlin phase survives in thick stacks rests on two particles per layer, a regime that Table S6 shows is insensitive to Nℓ. Fig. 5(a) (Nℓ = 10) uses Ne = 20, Nφ = 3, i.e. 2 electrons per layer. For two particles in 4 orbitals the intralayer L = 0 state is unique, so a near-unit overlap with Ψ_L^{⊗10} does not test the stability of Laughlin correlations. Table S6 confirms the problem. At N = 2 the gap is 1.94186054 for every Nℓ from 7 to 13, and constant to six digits from Nℓ = 4. So these data carry essentially no information about how the gap depends on Nℓ. The extrapolation Δ∞ ≈ 0.37 (Fig. 5b, Eq. S124) is a three-parameter fit to points concentrated at N = 2, 3, at one interaction point (V0^(1), V1^(1)) = (0.6, 0.3). The stability of foliated Laughlin order is mainly limited by N, not by Nℓ, and only Nℓ = 2 has more than four N values. The authors should:
   - give the uncoupled reference gap (V^(1) = 0) at the same sizes, so the reduction caused by interlayer coupling can be read off;
   - show finite-size scaling at points approaching the phase boundary (for example V0^(1) = 1.0 and 1.2 at V1^(1) = 0.3), not only deep inside;
   - for Nℓ ≥ 3, use the largest N per layer available, for example Nℓ = 3–4 with N = 4–5;
   - state that the Nℓ = 10 overlap map tests only the two-particle limit, unless larger N per layer can be reached.

B. Should address

4. The main text and App. H disagree about the three-fold-degenerate torus region. Sec. III.C and Fig. 3(b) label it Ψ_singlet and state that "the Halperin (112) overlap is larger throughout the 3-fold degenerate region." App. H (p. 44) reports that interpolating to the SU(2) Coulomb Hamiltonian closes the gap, "ruling out (112) as well." It concludes that "the topological character of this robustly gapped D = 3 region remains an open question." The bilayer phase diagram is the foundation for the multilayer discussion, including the phase-separation argument of Sec. V.A that relies on the (112)/S = 1 identification. The main text, Fig. 3 labels and Table I should therefore state the App. H conclusion consistently and give the actual (112) overlap values in this region.

5. The SU(3)_{Nℓ} identification for Nℓ = 3, 4 is weaker than "confirmed numerically" (Sec. I.C) or "excellent agreement" (Sec. IV.A). The evidence is one quasihole spectrum per Nℓ: Fig. 4(b), Ne = 9, Nφ = 7; Fig. 4(c), Ne = 12, Nφ = 7. Table S5 shows clear mismatches. The bilayer Ne = 8 counts disagree at 2L = 0, 4, 12. The trilayer Ne = 6 counts disagree at 2L = 0, 4, 6. The trilayer Ne = 9 counts disagree at 2L = 13, 15 and are not computed below that. Also, the trilayer has Ne = 9 and Nφ = 7, so all L are half-integer (predicted D3/2⊕D5/2⊕D9/2), but Fig. 4(b) plots states on an integer L axis. The authors should:
   - correct or explain that axis;
   - give torus ground-state degeneracies for Nℓ = 3 (predicted 10, Table II) and Nℓ = 4 (15), which are feasible at Ne = 6–9;
   - compute overlaps of the ED ground state with the explicit SU(3)_3 wavefunction of Eq. (21), which they construct but never evaluate. Fig. S4(f) instead uses the ED ground state at one parameter point as the reference.

6. Some overlap diagnostics are circular or favour the competitor. Fig. 3(b) (torus) and Fig. S4 (Nℓ = 3, 4) measure the overlap with the ED ground state at a reference point inside the putative phase. This shows adiabatic continuity with that point, not its identity. On the sphere, the Monte Carlo overlaps (Fig. S2) show the interlayer Pfaffian consistently above the Fibonacci wavefunction: 0.715 against 0.670 at Ne = 7+7, with both falling at similar rates. The text calls 0.67 a "high overlap" (p. 8) without that comparison. The rejection of the interlayer Pfaffian then relies on the torus GSD and on counting, which is reasonable. The authors should project Ψ_Fib onto S = 0, as they themselves suggest (App. D), and report the projected overlaps. They should also move the PSS comparison into the main text next to the 0.67 figure.

7. The physical relevance of the trimerized Fibonacci regime is not established, and the paper contradicts itself on it.
   - Sec. II.A says all phases found "can be stabilized with interaction coefficients that satisfy" V0^(a) > V1^(a) and V_L^(1) > V_L^(2). Yet the foliated Fibonacci region in Fig. 7(d) lies at V1^(1) > V0^(1), and Sec. VI.B states that the Fibonacci state requires V1^(1) ≳ V0^(1) and V1^(1) ~ V1^(0). Neither holds for Coulomb interactions with d > 0 (Eqs. 30–31).
   - The truncated pseudopotential model omits the long-wavelength electrostatic (capacitive) energy of layer-charge modulation relative to the dopant or background charge. A period-3 modulation of sheet densities costs energy of order e² n² d/ε per area. With d ≲ l_B and n ~ 1/(6π l_B²), this is a few percent of e²/(ε l_B) per particle, comparable to the gaps in Figs. 5 and 7. The classical model of Eq. (24) treats V^(0), V^(1), V^(2) as free constants, so it cannot capture this cost.
   - The authors should fix the Sec. II.A statement. They should also estimate the charging energy of the [1/3 1/3 0] configuration for realistic d/l_B and dielectric constants, and compare it with the trimerization energy gain and the quasihole gaps. Sec. VI should then say plainly which of the required conditions is unmet by any known platform.

8. The thin-torus mechanism for trimerization is qualitative, and its link to the ED is not demonstrated.
   - Fig. 6 is a schematic.
   - Eqs. (27)–(29) compare a classical cost −εκ²V1^(1)/2 per layer with a quantum gain of order ε|V0^(1) − κ²V1^(1)|. The outcome therefore depends on κ, which → ∞ in the thin-torus limit, while the ED is on the isotropic sphere.
   - The authors should plot the actual thin-torus energies of configurations A, B and C as functions of V1^(1) at a few κ.
   - Better still, they should follow the A/B energy difference from the thin-torus limit to the square torus in ED for Nℓ = 6. This would show whether the window in Fig. 6 persists away from κ → ∞.
   - The same holds for the bilayer Ising mapping (Eq. S106). Fig. S7 shows no gap closing between the 9-fold and 6-fold manifolds. The text's expectation that the gap closes in the thermodynamic limit should be supported by spectra at more than one Ne, or presented as conjecture.

9. The NNN interaction is incompletely specified, and its ratio is fixed by hand. Sec. II.A and Fig. 7 specify V0^(2) = V0^(1)/2 only. Eq. (26) and the quantum-gain estimate of Eq. (29) involve J^(2), which depends on V1^(2), and the text never states whether V1^(2) = 0 in the ED. The authors should state V1^(2) explicitly. The NNN sector is what creates the trimerization in the first place, so they should also show how the trimerized and Fibonacci regions of Fig. 7 move when V0^(2)/V0^(1) is varied, for example 0.3 and 0.7.

C. Minor

10. The Fig. 5(b) caption and the text (p. 14) state "filling 2/3" for all Nℓ. This should read ν = 1/3 per layer.
11. Several references are duplicated: [84]/[88] (Vaezi and Barkeshli), [103]/[162], [109]/[200], [141]/[211] and [191]/[215]. Ref. [196] (Skyrmions) is cited for the (112) singlet construction on p. 26 and looks misplaced.
12. In Fig. S9, the caption says Ne = 10 particles, while App. I refers to the corners of the Fig. 3 phase diagram (Ne = 8). The text says Ne = 8 and Ne = 10 give similar maxima. Please state which size is plotted.
13. In Fig. 8(a), the L = 0 count (18) exceeds the predicted 15. The main text attributes this to finite-size effects; the caption should say so too. Separately, the Fig. 7 caption reads "red regioncorresponds", and the Fig. 8 caption has a sentence beginning in lower case ("the corresponding k_z-resolved...").
14. The hashed region of Fig. 2(a) and the S = 0 region (Fig. S3) remain unidentified. One sentence in the main text listing what was checked, and what candidates remain, would help.
15. Eq. (5) and Eq. (S94) define κ the same way, but the thin-torus limit is described as L_y ≪ l_B. Please state the numerical κ (or L_y/l_B) at which the thin-torus energetics of Sec. V.B.2 are taken to apply.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Move the multilayer identification onto the torus, for example Nℓ = 3 (GSD 10 against 27) and a trimerized Nℓ = 6 stack. Torus ED removes the shift and aliasing ambiguity that limits every sphere comparison between sectors in Sec. V.B.
2. Use the paper's own explicit wavefunctions. Evaluate Eq. (21) for Nℓ = 3. For Nℓ = 6, evaluate products of bilayer Ψ_Fib with an empty layer interleaved, and report overlaps against size. Even modest sizes would complement the counting.
3. Add one realistic-interaction cut. Use layer-separated Coulomb pseudopotentials from Eq. (31) at a few d/l_B, including the uniform background, with a small tunneling term. Show where, if anywhere, the trimerized sector wins. This would turn Sec. VI from a wish list into a prediction.
4. Reorganize the abstract and Sec. I.C so that the strength of the evidence for each claim is stated. Robustness of the bilayer and few-layer results should be separated from the more tentative Nℓ = 9 foliated Fibonacci result, and the two-particle-per-layer limitation should be stated up front.
