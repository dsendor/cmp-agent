A. Must address

1. The layer charge-density wave behind the foliated Fibonacci phase may be an artifact of the truncated short-range interaction. The trimerized [1/3 1/3 0] order is a long-wavelength charge-redistribution claim, but the model omits the energy that controls such redistribution. The intralayer Hamiltonian has a single pseudopotential, V1^(0) = 1 (Sec. II A). With it, any layer at filling ν ≤ 1/3 has exactly zero intralayer energy, so moving electrons from a 2/9 layer into a 1/3 layer costs nothing within the layer. The only penalty comes from the interlayer V0^(1), V1^(1) and V0^(2). The classical argument of Sec. V B1 (Eqs. 24–25) assumes an intralayer cost V^(0) ν_l^2 that the ED Hamiltonian does not contain at ν ≤ 1/3, treats V^(0), V^(1), V^(2) as free constants, and says the q_z = 2π/3 modulation wins when V^(1) + V^(2) > 2V^(0); this requires the q_z-dependent layer kernel λ(q) to be negative at some q_z. For a real Coulomb interaction with a neutralizing background, the kernel at in-plane q → 0 is positive definite: a period-3 modulation of sheet densities costs a capacitive (Hartree) energy of order (e²/ε) d (Δn)² per area, or (e²/εl_B)(d/l_B) times the density modulation per electron. With d ≲ l_B (Sec. VI B) and n ~ 1/(6π l_B²) this is a few percent of e²/(εl_B) per particle, comparable to the FQH and quasihole gaps of Figs. 5 and 7. The truncation (V1^(0), V0^(1), V1^(1), V0^(2) only) sets the long-range tails of the intralayer and interlayer pseudopotentials to zero, and those tails are exactly what encodes the q → 0 difference between intralayer and interlayer interactions; neither the pseudopotential ED nor the thin-torus treatment of Sec. V B2 contains this term. The trimerized region of Fig. 7(a,b) (roughly V1^(1) ≳ 0.3, V0^(1) ≳ 0.4) corresponds to interlayer Hartree couplings of the same order as the intralayer one, a regime Coulomb does not reach, and the authors' remark that the (112) state "describes a phase separated state" (Sec. III A, App. A) is a symptom of the same missing term. Since the foliated Fibonacci phase (Fig. 1f, Figs. 7–8, abstract) exists only within the [1/3 1/3 0] sector, the main 3D conclusion currently holds for the pseudopotential model only. The authors should state where the neutralizing charge sits (dopants in each layer or a 3D background); repeat the sector competition of Figs. 7(a,b) and S5 and the analysis of Eq. (24) with at least V3^(0), ideally a longer tail of Coulomb-derived V_m^(n) from Eqs. (30–31) or the full projected Coulomb interaction, and with the Hartree energy of the layer-density modulation included or estimated analytically for realistic d/l_B and dielectric constants, compared against the trimerization energy gain and the quasihole gaps; and show whether the [1/3 1/3 0] region survives. If it does not, the claim should be reframed as a property of the short-range model, stating which physical ingredient (for example dielectric engineering of the spacer, Sec. VI B) could make λ(2π/3) < 0.

2. The case for spontaneous trimerization is fixed largely by commensurability and by construction, and the main uniform competitor is absent from the Nℓ = 9 data. As Sec. V B3 notes, the Nℓ = 9 system (Ne = 12, Nφ = 3; Figs. 7b, 8, S5) cannot host the uniform [2/9 2/9 2/9] configuration because 12 is not divisible by 9. With Nφ = 3, any layer holding ≤ 2 particles has zero intralayer energy (2 particles at Nφ = 3 is exactly the Laughlin shift) while 3 particles cost V1^(0), so 12 particles in 9 layers are forced into six layers with two particles each, and [220]^3 is essentially the arrangement with the fewest interlayer contacts. The larger trimerized region for Nℓ = 9 than for Nℓ = 6, and the dominance of 220220220 in Fig. S5, are therefore partly built into the choice of (Ne, Nφ, Nℓ). For Nℓ = 6 (Ne = 12, Nφ = 6) the trimerized and homogeneous [2/9]^6 sectors are compared at a single Nφ: the [330] sector sits exactly at the Laughlin shift while the uniform 2-per-layer sector is not at the shift of any incompressible state, so comparing energies across particle-per-layer sectors at fixed Nφ favours the chosen shift — the same aliasing problem the authors flag for the bilayer in Sec. III C. In addition, layer particle numbers are conserved (no tunneling), so the three-fold degeneracy at Nℓ k_z/2π = 0, 3, 6 in Fig. 8(c,d) is a translation degeneracy of superselection sectors that holds by construction and does not test dynamical symmetry breaking. The authors should provide a size series in which the uniform competitor is present (for example Nℓ = 9 with Ne = 18, or Nℓ = 6 at other (Ne, Nφ)) and in which both sectors have a well-defined candidate FQH state at the appropriate shift; compare energies per particle with the standard background-charge and shift corrections and show the trend of the difference with system size; ideally repeat the competition on the torus, where all sectors share the same Nφ without shift bias; and, to test spontaneity directly, add a small interlayer tunneling t to the Nℓ = 6 ED and show that the layer-density modulation (a layer-resolved density structure factor at k_z = 2π/3) survives and the quasi-degeneracy splits only weakly. Until then, "spontaneously layer-trimerized" (abstract) and "appears to be robust" (end of Sec. V B3) should be qualified.

3. The identification of the foliated Fibonacci phase rests on two electrons per occupied layer and a single quasihole-counting diagnostic that barely distinguishes candidate states. In the Nℓ = 9 calculation (Figs. 7b,d, 8, S5), Ne = 12 at Nφ = 3 for the ground state and Nφ = 4 for quasiholes, so each "Fibonacci bilayer" is a 2+2 system in 4 orbitals. The Fibonacci quasihole manifold per bilayer (D0 ⊕ D2) then differs from decoupled-Laughlin counting (D1 ⊗ D1 = D0 ⊕ D1 ⊕ D2) only by whether one L = 1 multiplet lies below the cut — a statement about how two pairs of particles combine rather than evidence of non-Abelian order. The paper does not show that competing bilayer candidates with shift 3 (interlayer Pfaffian/PSS of Table S1, (112), PH(1/3), or any state that simply suppresses the layer-symmetric or antisymmetric pair channel) give different counting at these sizes; for the interlayer Pfaffian this was shown only at 5+5 (Table S3). The quantity coloured in Fig. 7(c,d) (the "quasi-hole gap difference" Δ_QH) is not defined precisely enough to reproduce. For Nℓ = 6, which has 3 electrons per occupied layer and where the text calls the evidence "stronger" (Sec. V B3), the overlap of the blue quasihole region of Fig. 7(c) with the gapped trimerized region of Fig. 7(a) is never plotted. There is also no gap scaling or torus degeneracy for the foliated Fibonacci phase, unlike the foliated Laughlin phase (Fig. 5b). The authors should define Δ_QH; give the predicted quasihole counting of the competing bilayer states at 2+2, Nφ = 4 and 3+3, Nφ = 7; plot point by point the intersection of the trimerized and Fibonacci-counting regions for Nℓ = 6; compute overlaps of the Nℓ = 6 and 9 ground states (for Nℓ = 9 in the [220]^3 sector, across Fig. 7(d)) with products of bilayer Fibonacci wavefunctions (Eq. 12) with an empty layer interleaved, and with products of Ψ_330, Ψ_PSS, Laughlin states or ED bilayer ground states; where feasible add a larger per-layer size (for example Nℓ = 6 with 4+4 per pair); and give the torus ground-state degeneracy of a trimerized stack (3 × 6^{Nℓ/3} for foliated Fibonacci versus 3 × 9^{Nℓ/3} for foliated Laughlin, up to the CDW factor). Without this, the abstract's statement that "the foliated Fibonacci phase exists in the 9-layer system" is not established.

9. Interlayer tunneling, density control and disorder are left out, although trimerization requires charge transfer between layers. The model has no single-particle tunneling; Sec. V B says a small tunneling is needed for charge redistribution and that the phases survive tunneling much smaller than the gap. But tunneling itself drives the Laughlin-to-Fibonacci transition in Ref. 84 and mixes particle-per-layer sectors, adding kinetic energy that favours the uniform configuration; and for misfit or van der Waals crystals with d of order 1 nm (Sec. VI A), or any d ≲ l_B ≈ 8 nm, it is not obviously small compared with sub-meV FQH gaps. Filling 1/3 at 10 T requires about 8 × 10^10 cm^-2 per layer, set by intrinsic doping with matching disorder, and in a 3D crystal the average filling is tuned only by B. The authors should estimate the tunneling scale at which the [1/3 1/3 0] state is destabilized (for example by second-order perturbation theory between sectors) and state it relative to the computed gaps converted to meV; estimate tunneling scales and the required doping homogeneity for the proposed platforms; and say over what range of average filling around 2/9 the trimerized state remains the ground state, which bears on whether it gives a plateau.
