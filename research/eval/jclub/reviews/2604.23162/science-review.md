A. Must address

1. Nothing shows that the IP1 gap is a superconducting (pairing) gap, so "robust electron pairing" (title, abstract, Discussion) and the "AFM+SC" region of Fig. 4h are not established. All gap data are taken at a single temperature, 15 K (Figs. 2–3), and the gap is extracted only from symmetrized EDCs (Fig. 3f–j). Symmetrization assumes particle–hole symmetry about EF and produces an apparent gap wherever the band fails to reach EF, for example a pseudogap, an AFM- or hybridization-induced gap, or a band top lying just below EF near the major-axis vertices. It also removes the information needed to tell these apart from a pairing gap. The authors should: (i) measure the IP1 gap as a function of temperature in at least one region (for example the UD95K 6-layer region of Fig. 3d) through the host Tc and as close as possible to the Tc of the outer planes, showing it close or fill at a well-defined temperature and the coherence peak lose weight; (ii) show particle–hole symmetric Bogoliubov behaviour directly, through back-bending of the IP1 dispersion at kF along cuts near the major-axis vertices, or thermally populated states above EF in unsymmetrized, Fermi–Dirac-divided EDCs at elevated temperature; (iii) show the IP1 dispersion along the θ = 90° cut so readers can see that kF is crossed rather than approached, and that the gap minimum lies at the Fermi crossing rather than offset from it. Without these, "pairing" should become "energy gap". It is also unclear which Tc applies to an n = 5–8 region tens of µm across inside a host crystal with bulk Tc = 30, 95 or 111 K (see concern 10).

2. Interlayer proximity and interband hybridization are not ruled out as the origin of the IP1 gap, and either would explain the IP0-gapless / IP1-gapped contrast that the Discussion (p. 11) says it cannot explain. The paper's own data show that the IP1 gap is not governed by doping: IP1 at p = 0.020 is gapped while IP0 at p = 0.037 is gapless (Fig. 3n), and Δ0 extrapolates non-monotonically to 46, 30 and 60 meV for p = 0.020, 0.051 and 0.088 (p. 7). In 5- and 6-layer units IP1 sits directly next to the more heavily doped, superconducting OP (or, in 7/8-layer units, IP2), while IP0 is one layer further away; a gap induced on IP1 by coupling to OP accounts for both the IP1/IP0 contrast and the scatter of gap against doping without intrinsic pairing at p = 0.02. The paper argues interlayer hopping is weak (p. 8) but cites this rather than measuring it, and the model of Fig. 4 treats each layer independently (citing Ref. 22), so it cannot address the question. Separately, the text states that the IP1 and IP0 bands overlap in the EDCs (Fig. 3c–e), and the gap is largest near the major-axis vertices, where the IP1 pocket comes closest to the IP0 and outer-plane sheets, which is what a hybridization or avoided-crossing gap would do. The authors should: compare the IP1 gap in 7- and 8-layer regions (IP1 next to IP2) with 5- and 6-layer regions (IP1 next to OP) at similar IP1 doping; correlate the IP1 gap with the OP gap measured in the same region; show how the gap depends on momentum distance from the IP0 and outer-plane sheets; and calculate a multilayer tight-binding model with interlayer hopping t_perp and a pairing amplitude only on OP, constraining t_perp from the measured IP1–IP0 band separations, to show what proximity alone can produce. Without such tests the conclusions about pairing at p = 0.02 and pairing "facilitated by" AF order are not supported.

3. The assignment of each region to n = 5, 6, 7 or 8, and of each sheet to IP0, IP1 or IP2, rests entirely on Fermi-surface counting and pocket-size ratios, with no independent structural identification and no check of phase purity (pp. 4–5, Methods). Three sheets are taken to mean n = 5 or 6 and four sheets n = 7 or 8; odd and even n are then distinguished by whether the IP0 pocket is "nearly half" the size (Fig. 1c vs. 1e), and the same argument assigns Fig. 1f and 1g, each a single map, to n = 7 and 8. Because doping varies from region to region and crystal to crystal, a small IP0 pocket can mean either an even n or simply lower doping, and since the per-layer doping is itself what the paper measures, the argument is partly circular. With a ~10 µm spot on ~50 µm regions in crystals containing n = 1–9 intergrowths, and STEM (Ref. 27) showing intergrowths varying on scales well below the spot, the spot may average over several phases or stacking faults; superposed sheets from, say, 5- and 6-layer units could be mistaken for one phase, or a pocket mis-assigned to IP0 or IP1. The central IP0-gapless / IP1-gapped contrast depends on this assignment. The authors should: show the evidence for every region (Table S1), including the IP0/IP1 area ratio with uncertainty; show the real-space maps of Fig. S1 with the spot footprint, how each sheet's area varies across the region, and how spectra change near region boundaries; state how an n = 5 region is excluded from being read as an n = 6 region with a missing sheet; apply a doping sum rule (per-layer dopings adding to a plausible total for the unit, with OP doping consistent with the host; see concern 7); and, where possible, cross-check with c-axis information on the measured area (micro-XRD, post-ARPES cross-sectional STEM/TEM on a lamella, or kz/photon-energy dependence).

4. "Strong antiferromagnetic order" is assumed, not measured, and the pocket picture is partly circular. The coexistence claim (abstract; pp. 8, 10–11; Fig. 4h, "AFM metal" and "AFM+SC") rests on NMR in Hg- and Ba-based multilayers (Ref. 29), not on Bi-based intergrowths at these dopings, and on the pockets themselves, which are fitted with an AFM mean-field model. No folded (shadow) band, magnetic probe or bound on the AF transition temperature is presented. Pockets near (π/2, π/2) with suppressed outer-side weight also arise without long-range order (short-range AF correlations, YRZ-type pockets, or matrix-element effects), and the a + b cos θ intensity variation (Fig. 2g), the R/L ratio (Fig. 2h) and the comparison in Fig. S5 are only described qualitatively. Discriminating tests are possible within the existing data: whether each pocket is centred exactly on the AFM zone boundary at (π/2, π/2), as Néel folding requires, or displaced toward Γ; whether shadow intensity mirrors the pocket across the (π,0)–(0,π) line or the back side follows the AFM zone boundary; whether the backside weight and R/L follow mean-field coherence factors quantitatively at the fitted ΔMF = 0.68 eV; and whether a non-magnetic model fits equally well. The authors should present these tests, or else state clearly that AF order is inferred by analogy, tone down the abstract, and label (or remove) the AFM and AFM+SC regions of Fig. 4h as hypothetical rather than measured.

5. The claims of an intrinsic "metallic state" and an "abrupt transition" from the Mott insulator at "infinitesimal" doping go beyond what ARPES at p ≥ 0.007 and 15 K shows (abstract, pp. 9–11). No data are shown at p = 0 or between 0 and 0.007, so "abrupt" and "infinitesimal" are extrapolations; the p = 0.007 point comes from a single region (UD30K-R2) measured only with the hemispherical analyser, which the authors themselves call less reliable for pocket weight (pp. 6–7; see concern 14). A coherent peak and Fermi crossing at 15 K does not by itself establish a metallic ground state; weak localization, an AFM-band insulator with finite density of states, or a small gap below the error bars of Fig. 3k are not excluded (see concern 6), and there is no in-plane transport or temperature dependence of the spectral weight at EF. In addition, IP0 sits within a few ångströms of metallic, superconducting outer planes in a crystal with Tc up to 111 K, so screening, proximity or interlayer tunnelling may stabilize the coherent IP0 quasiparticle; it is not an isolated lightly doped CuO2 sheet. The authors should quantify quasiparticle weight and EDC linewidth against p for IP0 (Fig. 2f-type data for every pocket) to show whether Z extrapolates to a finite value as p → 0, add temperature-dependent data showing IP0 weight at EF persisting on cooling if possible, discuss the role of the metallic outer planes, and rephrase "abrupt transition upon infinitesimal doping" as an extrapolation, stating clearly which parts are inference.

B. Should address

6. The "gapless" IP0 claim needs a quantitative upper bound, and the symmetrized-EDC analysis can fail for very shallow bands. In Fig. 2a–d the IP0 band top is only about 11 meV above EF for a pocket with p ≈ 0.01, and at p = 0.007 (Fig. 3a, f) it must lie even closer. When the band top lies within a few kBT and a few meV of EF, a symmetrized EDC can show a peak at EF whether or not a small gap is present. The IP0 pockets in Fig. 3a, b also have weak peaks on the outer side (θ = 90°–180°). The comparison of a gapless IP0 with a ~12 meV IP1 gap at the same doping of 0.02 (Fig. 3k, n) is central to the interpretation and to Fig. 4h. The authors should state the smallest gap the analysis could resolve given ~1 meV resolution, 15 K and the measured linewidth; show Fermi–Dirac-divided spectra and the leading-edge midpoint along IP0 relative to gold at the major-axis vertices; and show one IP0 and one IP1 pocket at the same doping in the same region side by side with identical analysis. They should also discuss why a plane in a bulk superconductor with Tc = 111 K shows no proximity-induced gap, which is itself significant.

7. The conversion from pocket area to hole doping is not specified, and the pocket back side is weakly observed. The paper says only that p "is determined from its enclosed area" (p. 5). For pockets in an AF-reconstructed zone the result changes by a factor of two depending on the number of pockets per Brillouin zone, spin degeneracy, and whether the full or reduced zone is used; this sets the absolute scale, including the headline p = 0.007, and the comparison with Refs. 21, 22 overlaid in Figs. 3n and 4h. The R/L ratio approaches zero at p ≈ 0.08 (Fig. 2h), so at higher dopings the right half of the pocket, and hence its area, comes largely from a fit or ellipse assumption, which also bears on whether these are closed pockets or arcs. The authors should give the explicit Luttinger formula and confirm it matches the convention of Refs. 21, 22; explain how the back side is located when its weight is near zero, showing measured back-side kF points with uncertainties propagated into p; confirm that no other (e.g. antinodal) states contribute to the hole count; add horizontal error bars to p in Figs. 1h–j, 2h, 3n and 4f; and, for at least one region, sum the pocket-derived dopings of all planes (with OP from its large Fermi surface) and compare with the oxygen content implied by the host crystal.

8. The IP1 gap magnitudes and their doping trend are inconsistent between Fig. 3m and Fig. 3n and are weakly constrained. The text (pp. 7, 11) and Fig. 3n say the gap "decreases as the doping level approaches 0.02", with the maximum measured gap rising from ~12 meV at p = 0.02 to ~33 meV at p ≈ 0.08. Yet the d-wave fits in Fig. 3m give Δ0 = 46, 30 and 60 meV for p = 0.020, 0.051 and 0.088, which is non-monotonic and gives the lowest-doped pocket the second-largest pairing scale. On a small pocket centred at (π/2, π/2) the major-axis vertex reaches only |cos kx a − cos ky a|/2 ≈ 0.25–0.5, so the "maximum gap" of Fig. 3n mostly measures how far the pocket extends toward the antinode, and every Δ0 is a two- to four-fold linear extrapolation that assumes the simple d-wave form survives AFM reconstruction; the p = 0.020 and 0.051 points do not obviously lie on lines through the origin, and a linear dependence this close to the node is consistent with many gap forms. The authors should state which quantity measures pairing strength, compare pockets using Δ0 or the gap at a fixed |cos − cos|/2 with fit uncertainties for all IP1 pockets, show the fits, test higher harmonics and a non-d-wave form (e.g. a gap tied to the AF zone boundary), and revise the doping-trend statements and Fig. 4h. The headline "up to ~33 meV" (abstract) and "over 30 meV" (p. 3) should be reconciled with the Δ0 of 46–60 meV (p. 7) and presented with the pocket size they come from.

9. The IP1 gap extraction is hampered by overlapping IP0 and IP1 features (Fig. 3c–e, p. 7). The text notes that EDCs on the IP1 pocket contain both a weak, broad IP1 peak and a higher-binding-energy IP0 component. After symmetrization the IP0 tail could mimic or distort an IP1 coherence peak, especially near θ = ±90° where IP1 is weak. The authors should fit the two components explicitly, or use the momentum separation of the two pockets to choose k-points free of IP0 weight, and show the unsymmetrized EDCs with the fit decomposition at the largest-gap angles.

12. The mean-field t–U model is fitted qualitatively, gives limited independent support, and cannot address the central result (pp. 8–9, Fig. 4). With t = 0.32 eV, t′ = −0.063 eV, t″ = 0.031 eV and ΔMF = 0.68 eV fixed and only μ varied, the model is constrained by about three cuts per pocket, and since the dopings are derived from pocket areas in the same rigid-band picture, the linear μ(p) of Fig. 4f largely restates the assumption rather than testing it.
