Review outline:

Note on the material reviewed: the PDF contains the main text, Methods and Figs. 1-4. The Supplementary Materials it cites (Figs. S1-S5, Table S1 and the mean-field t-U model) are not in it. Where a concern touches material that may be in the supplement, I say so.

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use spatially resolved laser ARPES with a time-of-flight analyser to find n = 5-8 intergrowth regions on the surface of Bi2223 crystals. In the inner CuO2 planes of these regions they report small hole pockets centred at (pi/2, pi/2), with pocket-derived dopings from 0.007 to 0.088. The innermost planes (IP0) appear gapless, while the second innermost planes (IP1) show an anisotropic gap of up to about 33 meV. They read this as an intrinsic metallic state at vanishing doping and as pairing coexisting with strong antiferromagnetism. Small pockets had previously been seen only in apical-fluorine Ba-based five- and six-layer compounds (refs. 21, 22). Extending this to a Bi-based family, to n = 7 and 8, and to IP1 dopings near 0.09 would be a valuable addition. The conceptual claims, however (metallicity, an "abrupt" transition, superconducting pairing, coexistence with antiferromagnetic order, and disorder as the explanation for insulating single-layer cuprates), go beyond what the ARPES data shown here can establish.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A technically impressive result: phase-selective Fermi-surface mapping of micron-scale intergrowths, using two-dimensional momentum acquisition to identify the phases in real time (Methods; Fig. S1).
- Fermi pockets appear in several crystals (UD30K, UD95K, OP111K) and in eight regions, across four layer numbers (Fig. 1b-g). The systematics in Fig. 1h-j (nodal kF, axis lengths, u/v) are internally consistent.
- The data are of high quality: EDC quasiparticle peaks around the whole IP0 pocket (Fig. 2f), and spectral weight on the back side of the pocket is quantified (Fig. 2g,h).
- The doping range of IP1 (up to 0.088) reaches beyond what is available in the Ba-based multilayers. This allows the pocket evolution and gap to be followed further toward the regime where conventional phase diagrams place superconductivity.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Whether the IP1 gap is superconducting, and whether it is intrinsic to IP1, is not established. All gap data are taken at a single temperature, 15 K (Fig. 3). Two kinds of evidence are missing:
   - Superconductivity: there is no temperature dependence of the IP1 gap, no closing at a Tc, no Bogoliubov back-bending, and no particle-hole symmetric coherence peak above EF.
   - Origin of the gap: two alternatives are not addressed.
     - Proximity or interlayer pairing. The IP1 plane sits next to the outer or IP2 planes, which are superconducting with large gaps. The IP0 plane is one layer further away. This alone could explain why IP1 is gapped and IP0 is not, which the Discussion (p. 11) says it cannot explain. The paper argues that interlayer hopping is weak (p. 8), but cites this rather than measuring it.
     - Hybridization or avoided crossing. The text states that the IP1 and IP0 bands overlap in the EDCs (Fig. 3c-e). The gap is largest near the major-axis vertices, where the IP1 pocket comes closest to the IP0 and outer-plane sheets.

   Without this evidence, the abstract's phrase "robust electron pairing" and the AFM+SC region in Fig. 4h do not follow. The authors should:
   - measure the IP1 gap as a function of temperature in at least one region, for example the UD95K 6-layer region of Fig. 3d, through the host Tc and as close as possible to the Tc of the outer planes;
   - divide by the Fermi-Dirac function to check for particle-hole symmetry and Bogoliubov back-bending;
   - show how the gap depends on momentum distance from the IP0 and outer-plane sheets.

2. The gap magnitudes and their doping trend are inconsistent between Fig. 3m and Fig. 3n and depend on pocket geometry.
   - Fig. 3m gives extrapolated d-wave maxima Delta0 = 46, 30 and 60 meV for p = 0.020, 0.051 and 0.088. On this measure the lowest-doped pocket has a larger pairing scale than the p = 0.051 pocket.
   - Fig. 3n plots the largest gap measured on each pocket. It rises from about 12 meV at p = 0.02 to about 33 meV at p = 0.08, and the Discussion (p. 11) says the gap "decreases as the doping level approaches 0.02".
   - On a small pocket centred at (pi/2, pi/2), the major-axis vertex lies at small |cos kx a - cos ky a|/2. So the "maximum gap" of Fig. 3n mostly measures how far the pocket extends toward the antinode, not the pairing strength.
   - The Delta0 values are linear extrapolations by a factor of about 2-4 beyond the measured range, and the p = 0.020 and p = 0.051 points do not obviously lie on lines through the origin.

   The authors should compare pockets using Delta0, or the gap at a fixed |cos-cos|/2, with uncertainties from the fit. They should show whether a single d-wave form fits within error and revise the statements about the doping trend. The "33 meV" headline number should be presented together with the pocket size it comes from.

3. The gapless IP0 pockets and the gap analysis rest on symmetrized EDCs, which can fail for very shallow bands.
   - In Fig. 2a-d the IP0 band top is only about 11 meV above EF for a pocket with p of about 0.01. At p = 0.007 (Fig. 3a,f) the band top must lie even closer to EF.
   - Symmetrization assumes particle-hole symmetry and a band crossing well away from its extremum. When the band top lies within a few kBT and a few meV of EF, a symmetrized EDC can show a peak at EF whether or not a small gap is present.
   - The IP0 conclusion (no gap anywhere, Fig. 3k,n) is compared with a nonzero IP1 gap of about 12 meV at the same doping of 0.02. That comparison is central to the paper's interpretation and to the phase diagram in Fig. 4h.

   The authors should:
   - show Fermi-Dirac-divided spectra and the leading-edge midpoint along IP0, relative to gold, at the major-axis vertices;
   - state the smallest gap the analysis could resolve, given about 1 meV resolution and T = 15 K;
   - show at least one IP0 and one IP1 pocket at the same doping in the same region side by side with identical analysis.

4. The doping values depend on an unstated counting convention and on closure of the pocket's back side, which is weakly observed.
   - "The hole doping level ... is determined from its enclosed area" (p. 5), but the formula is not given. The answer differs by a factor of two depending on whether the four (pi/2, pi/2)-type pockets are counted in the full or the reduced Brillouin zone, and whether spin degeneracy is included.
   - The R/L spectral-weight ratio falls toward zero at p of about 0.08 (Fig. 2h). So at the higher dopings the right half of the pocket, and hence its area, comes largely from a fit or an ellipse assumption rather than from observation. This also bears on whether these features are closed pockets or arcs.

   The authors should:
   - give the explicit Luttinger formula and state whether it matches the convention of refs. 21, 22, which are overlaid in Figs. 3n and 4h;
   - show the measured kF points on the back side of each pocket, with uncertainties, and propagate them into the error in p;
   - check the sum: for one region, add the pocket-derived dopings of all planes (with OP from its large Fermi surface) and compare the total with the oxygen content implied by the host crystal.

5. The claims of "metallic state", "abrupt transition" and "infinitesimal doping" are not supported by the measurements made.
   - The paper shows quasiparticle peaks and a Fermi crossing at 15 K, in a sample whose outer planes are superconducting and metallic. It does not show in-plane transport, or a temperature dependence of the spectral weight at EF, that would separate a metal from a weakly localized or AFM-band insulator with a finite density of states.
   - The lowest doping measured is 0.007. Nothing between 0 and 0.007 is measured, so "infinitesimal" and "abrupt" are extrapolations.
   - The statement that insulating behaviour in single-layer and bilayer cuprates "must be re-evaluated in light of strong disorder effects" (p. 10) is not tested here. No disorder-tuned comparison or estimate of disorder in the inner planes is given.

   The authors should either moderate the abstract and Discussion, or add temperature-dependent data showing the IP0 quasiparticle weight at EF persisting with decreasing temperature. They should also state clearly which parts are inference.

6. The assignment of each spectrum to a specific n and plane relies entirely on Fermi-surface counting and pocket-size ratios. There is no independent structural identification of the region under the beam.
   - Three sheets are taken to mean n = 5 or 6, and four sheets n = 7 or 8. Odd and even n are then distinguished by whether the IP0 pocket is "nearly half" the size (Fig. 1c vs. 1e). The same argument assigns Fig. 1f and 1g to n = 7 and 8.
   - With a 10 µm spot on regions of about 50 µm in crystals containing n = 1-9 intergrowths, the spot may average over several phases or include stacking faults. The plane labels (IP0 vs. IP1), and hence the central IP0-gapless / IP1-gapped contrast, depend on this assignment.

   The authors should:
   - show the evidence for the assignments for every region in Table S1, including the IP0/IP1 area ratio with uncertainty;
   - show the real-space maps from Fig. S1 with the spot footprint, and how the spectra change near region boundaries;
   - where possible, cross-check with c-axis information on the same area (micro-XRD, or STEM/TEM on a lamella cut from a measured region), or with kz/photon-energy dependence;
   - state how an n = 5 region with OP, IP1 and IP0 is excluded from being read as, for example, an n = 6 region with a missing sheet.

B. Should address

7. The "strong antiferromagnetic order" is assumed, not measured. The coexistence claim in the abstract, the Discussion (pp. 10-11) and Fig. 4h relies on NMR in Hg- and Ba-based multilayers (ref. 29), not on these Bi-based intergrowths. The pockets themselves are fitted with an AFM mean-field model, which makes the argument circular. The authors should present direct ARPES evidence of (pi, pi) zone folding, for example a shadow band or the back side of the pocket following the AFM zone boundary. Otherwise they should describe the AFM order as inferred by analogy and remove the AFM+SC region from Fig. 4h.

8. The mean-field t-U fit gives limited independent support (pp. 8-9, Fig. 4).
   - The dopings are themselves derived from pocket areas computed in the same rigid-band picture. So the linear dependence of mu on p in Fig. 4f largely restates the rigid-band assumption rather than testing it.
   - The parameters {t, t', t'', Delta_MF} are fitted. The paper does not show how well they are constrained, whether t' and t'' are compatible with those used for the outer planes, or how the fit quality changes across the 0.007-0.088 range.
   - Delta_MF = 0.68 eV is held fixed across 0.007-0.088, which assumes that the AFM gap does not change with doping.
   - Labelling the band a Zhang-Rice singlet band (Fig. 4g) is a schematic, not an output of the model.

   The authors should report the residuals and confidence ranges of the parameters. They should test whether letting Delta_MF vary with p improves the fit. They should also compare the measured IP0 band-top energy (about 11 meV, Fig. 2) and Fermi velocity with the model at each doping.

9. The discrepancy with refs. 21, 22 needs more than a remark.
   - Here a gap of about 12 meV is found on an IP1 pocket at p = 0.020. Kurokawa et al. found no IP1 gap below p = 0.04, and gaps of about 5 meV above it.
   - The paper concludes that "many additional factors" matter (p. 8). Before invoking new physics, the authors should exclude a difference in doping convention (concern 4) and a difference in analysis (symmetrization vs. leading edge).
   - They should also discuss concrete structural differences: apical F vs. apical O, BiO vs. Ba-F reservoir, and the n-dependent spacing between IP1 and the superconducting outer planes.

10. The sample and region statistics are not shown in the main text.
    - Eight regions in three crystals are listed (Methods), but Table S1 is not in the submission. Figs. 3 and 4 do not show how many distinct regions contribute at each doping.
    - Repeated cleaves, or the same region measured on more than one occasion, are not shown.
    - The lowest-doping result (p = 0.007, UD30K-R2) comes from a single region measured only with the hemispherical analyser. The paper itself argues that this analyser underestimates the back-side weight (Fig. 2h).

    The authors should give the number of regions and cleaves per data point. They should also show the p = 0.007 pocket, or a comparably small one, reproduced with the ARToF system.

11. The Tc and doping of each intergrowth region are not known. Sample names (UD30K, UD95K, OP111K) refer to the host Bi2223 crystal, determined by bulk susceptibility. The Tc of the n = 5-8 regions, and whether their outer planes are superconducting at 15 K, are not stated. This matters for interpreting a gap at 15 K as superconducting and for placing points on Fig. 4h. The authors should state the outer-plane doping and gap for each region, and discuss what limits the Tc of the intergrowths.

12. The argument comparing the two analysers is not demonstrated (pp. 6-7, Fig. 2h). The paper claims the ARToF data are "more intrinsic" because of higher signal-to-noise and no detector nonlinearity. Matrix-element effects at 6.994 eV are not discussed, however. These include polarization, sample orientation relative to the analyser and photon incidence, and differences in geometry between the two setups, and any of them could produce the left/right asymmetry. The authors should state the polarization and geometry of both setups. They should test whether the R/L ratio changes when the sample is rotated by 90 degrees, or measured with the opposite polarization, on the same region.

C. Minor

13. Fig. 4a,b: the panel annotation reads "mu = -0.330 eV", while the caption and the dashed lines give mu1-mu3 = -0.556, -0.609 and -0.643 eV. Please reconcile these. Also state the energy reference, since E - EF and mu are both used.

14. Fig. 2g: the text gives a = 0.79 and b = 0.39 but does not say how the EDC weights were normalized. The right-hand axis normalization and the background subtraction (bottom curve of Fig. 2f) should be specified. The MDC and EDC curves use different axes, so their agreement cannot be judged.

15. Fig. 3k,m: there are no error bars on theta, and the legend in Fig. 3k merges IP0 pockets of p = 0.007 and 0.037 into one series. Please separate these, and state how the gap error bars ("uncertainty in determining the gap size") were obtained.

16. Fig. 3n uses asterisk symbols for refs. 21, 22, while the legend and Fig. 4h use triangles. Please make the symbols consistent, and state that the literature dopings were converted to the same convention.

17. Abstract and p. 3: "up to ~33 meV" (abstract) and "can reach over 30 meV" (introduction) sit beside extrapolated Delta0 values of 46-60 meV (p. 7). Please clarify which quantity the headline refers to.

18. Typo on p. 3: "wihch". The Fermi-level reference statement in the Methods uses nodal states that "remain gapless in the superconducting state". This is circular for the IP1 nodal-gap test, so please state that gold was the primary reference for all gap analyses.

19. Fig. 1 caption and Methods: the UD30K-R2 data (Fig. 1b, Fig. 3a,c) came from the hemispherical-analyser system. Please mark this in the figure captions, because it affects how the back-side weight should be compared.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one complete temperature series on a single 6-layer region, covering IP0, IP1 and OP from 15 K to above the Tc of the outer planes, with Fermi-Dirac-divided spectra. On its own this would settle whether the IP1 gap is superconducting, whether IP0 is truly gapless, and whether IP0 remains coherent at EF.
2. Provide a per-region table in the main text with: assigned n, the evidence for that assignment, the pocket areas and the Luttinger formula, the OP doping, the total doping, the analyser used, and the IP0/IP1 gaps (Delta0 and the gap at fixed |cos-cos|/2).
3. Calculate the proximity and hybridization scenario explicitly, using a multilayer tight-binding model with interlayer hopping t_perp and a gap only in the outer planes. Show whether it can reproduce a gapped IP1 and a gapless IP0 for reasonable t_perp, and use the measured IP1-IP0 band separations to constrain t_perp.
4. Tone down the conceptual claims ("disorder-free", "infinitesimal doping", "revised framework", "strong AFM order") to what the ARPES data support. Present the conclusion about disorder versus intrinsic insulating behaviour as a hypothesis motivated by the comparison, not as a demonstrated result.
