Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use spatially resolved laser ARPES with a time-of-flight analyser to find regions of n = 5–8 intergrowth phases on cleaved Bi2223 crystals. In the inner CuO2 planes they report hole pockets centred at (π/2, π/2) with dopings p = 0.007–0.088. The innermost-plane (IP0) pockets are gapless, while the second-innermost-plane (IP1) pockets have a d-wave-like gap of up to about 33 meV. Fermi pockets have so far been reported only in Ba2Can-1CunO2n(F,O)2 (n = 5, 6). Extending them to Bi-based multilayers, to n = 7 and 8, and to p up to about 0.09 is new and of broad interest. The strongest conclusions are that the lightly doped CuO2 plane is intrinsically metallic, that the transition from the Mott insulator is "abrupt", and that "robust electron pairing coexists with strong antiferromagnetic order". These go well beyond what the 15 K ARPES data establish on their own.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Clean, high-resolution Fermi-surface maps (Fig. 1b–g) show two or three pocket-like sheets plus the outer-plane sheet in several regions, from three crystals at three dopings.
- The pocket geometry (Fig. 1h–j), the angular distribution of spectral weight (Fig. 2g) and the right/left intensity ratio against doping (Fig. 2h) are treated systematically. Together they give a useful quantitative dataset for theory.
- The data extend the doping range of inner-plane pockets well beyond the earlier reports (p < 0.05) and report an IP1 gap much larger than the ~5 meV seen before (Fig. 3n). If the gap is shown to be superconducting, this is a significant result.
- A single parameter set of the mean-field t–U model, with only μ varied, reproduces all the pocket shapes (Fig. 4a–f). This is an economical description.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Nothing shows that the IP1 gap is a superconducting gap, so "robust electron pairing" is not established (Fig. 3, Discussion, abstract). All gap data come from symmetrized EDCs at a single temperature, 15 K. Symmetrization assumes particle–hole symmetry and removes the information needed to tell a pairing gap from other gaps (pseudogap, AF- or hybridization-induced gaps). Before the IP1 gap can be called pairing, the authors should show:
   - its temperature dependence through the relevant Tc, where the gap closes and the coherence peak loses weight;
   - particle–hole symmetric Bogoliubov behaviour, such as back-bending of the dispersion at k_F along cuts near the major-axis vertex, or thermally populated states above E_F at elevated T (unsymmetrized, Fermi-function-divided EDCs);
   - the gap minimum lying at the Fermi crossing rather than offset from it.

   The Tc of each intergrowth region is not known either. Susceptibility gives the Tc of the host Bi2223, which is not necessarily that of a 5–8-layer region tens of µm across. The authors should state what Tc they assign to each region and why.

2. Interlayer proximity or hybridization is not ruled out as the source of the IP1 gap. The paper itself notes that the IP1 gap is not governed by doping. IP1 at p = 0.020 is gapped, while IP0 at p = 0.037 is gapless (Fig. 3n). Δ0 extrapolates to 46, 30 and 60 meV for p = 0.020, 0.051 and 0.088, which is non-monotonic (p. 7). In 5- and 6-layer units, IP1 sits directly next to the more heavily doped, superconducting OP. IP0 is isolated from OP by IP1. A gap induced on IP1 by interlayer coupling to OP explains both the IP1/IP0 contrast and the scatter in gap against doping without any intrinsic pairing at p = 0.02. The model in Fig. 4 treats each layer independently, citing Ref. 22, so it cannot address this. Two things would test it:
   - compare the IP1 gap in 7- and 8-layer regions, where IP1 neighbours IP2 rather than OP, with 5- and 6-layer regions at similar IP1 doping;
   - correlate the IP1 gap with the OP gap measured in the same region.

   A layered model with interlayer tunnelling and a pairing amplitude only on OP would show what proximity alone can produce. Without such tests, the conclusions about pairing at p = 0.02 and pairing "facilitated by" AF order are not supported.

3. Antiferromagnetic order in these Bi-based intergrowth phases is assumed, not measured. The headline claim of pairing "coexisting with strong antiferromagnetic order" rests on Ref. 29. That is NMR on Hg- and Ba-based multilayers, not on Bi22(n−1)n intergrowths at the dopings studied here. The paper presents no evidence of AF order in these regions: no measured folded (shadow) band, no magnetic probe, no bound on the AF transition temperature. The a + b cos θ intensity variation (Fig. 2g) and the R/L ratio (Fig. 2h) fit AF folding coherence factors. They also fit short-range AF correlations or other matrix-element origins, and the comparison in Fig. S5 is only described qualitatively. The authors should:
   - state clearly that AF order is inferred;
   - if possible, quantify the backside weight against the mean-field AF coherence factors with ΔMF fixed, and test whether a non-magnetic model (for example a Yang–Rice–Zhang-type pocket) fits equally well;
   - tone down the abstract and Fig. 4h, which draws "AFM metal" and "AFM+SC" regions without measured phase boundaries.

4. Neither the assignment of a spectrum to a given n, and to IP0, IP1 or IP2, nor the purity of each region is independently verified (pp. 4–5, Methods). The 5/6/7/8-layer assignment rests on two things: counting the Fermi-surface sheets, and the assumption that the IP0 pocket roughly halves from odd to even n. The doping of each layer is itself what the paper measures, so the argument is partly circular. In particular, 7- and 8-layer regions (Fig. 1f, g) are each told apart by a single map. The STEM work cited (Ref. 27) shows intergrowths that can vary on scales well below the 10 µm laser spot. A spot covering a mixture of, for example, 5- and 6-layer units would give superposed sheets that could be mistaken for one phase, or a pocket mis-assigned to IP0 or IP1. Needed:
   - spatial maps of how each sheet's area varies across the ~50 µm region;
   - a consistency check, such as whether the per-layer dopings in a region add up to a plausible total for that unit and whether the OP doping agrees with the host;
   - ideally, a structural probe (micro-XRD or post-ARPES cross-sectional STEM) of a measured region.

   Without this, the layer-resolved conclusions (IP0 gapless, IP1 gapped) are not secure.

B. Should address

5. The claim of an "abrupt" metallic state at "infinitesimal" doping goes beyond the data (abstract, pp. 9–11). The lowest-doping point, p = 0.007, comes from the only region measured with the hemispherical analyser (UD30K-R2). The authors themselves call that instrument less reliable for pocket weight (p. 6–7). No data are shown at p = 0 or between 0 and 0.007. A coherent peak at E_F at 15 K does not by itself establish a metallic ground state. Weak localization or a small gap below the error bars of Fig. 3k would not be excluded. The authors should:
   - state the upper bound on any gap on the smallest pockets;
   - show the p = 0.007 pocket in more than one region or sample, ideally with the ARToF system;
   - rephrase "abrupt transition upon infinitesimal doping" as an extrapolation.

6. The conversion from pocket area to hole doping is not given (p. 5, Fig. 1). For pockets in an AF-reconstructed zone, p depends on the counting convention: number of pockets per Brillouin zone, spin degeneracy, and whether the reduced or full zone is used. The paper only says that p is "determined from its enclosed area". The back side of the pockets is very weak, with R/L approaching zero at p ~ 0.08 (Fig. 2h). The ellipse there is therefore partly extrapolated, and the doping error bars, which are not shown in Fig. 1h–j or Fig. 3n, could be sizeable. Please:
   - give the formula used;
   - explain how the back side is located when its weight is near zero;
   - add horizontal error bars to all doping axes.

7. The extrapolated Δ0 values and the d-wave claim are weakly constrained (Fig. 3m, p. 7). For the small IP1 pockets, |cos kx a − cos ky a|/2 reaches only about 0.25–0.5. Δ0 = 46, 30 and 60 meV is therefore a two- to four-fold extrapolation. A linear dependence on a pocket this close to the node is also consistent with many other gap forms. The headline "up to ~33 meV" (abstract) and "Δ0 of 60 meV" (text) should be reconciled, and the non-monotonic Δ0 against p discussed explicitly. Please show the fits with their uncertainties, and state whether a non-d-wave form (for example a gap tied to the AF zone boundary) fits equally well.

8. Gap extraction is hampered by overlapping IP0 and IP1 features (Fig. 3c–e). The text notes that the EDCs on the IP1 pocket contain both an IP1 peak and a higher-binding-energy IP0 component. After symmetrization, the IP0 tail could mimic or distort an IP1 coherence peak, especially near θ = ±90° where IP1 is weak. Please:
   - fit the two components explicitly, or use the momentum separation of the two pockets to choose k-points free of IP0 weight;
   - show the unsymmetrized EDCs with the fit decomposition for the largest-gap angles.

9. "Disorder-free" and "shielded from disorder" are asserted rather than demonstrated (Introduction, Discussion). The argument that insulating behaviour in single- and bilayer cuprates comes from disorder would be much stronger with a quantitative indicator from the same data. Candidates are the nodal MDC width (scattering rate) of IP0 and IP1 compared with OP, and with Bi2212 at comparable doping measured in the same system. As written, "Observations of insulating behaviour in single-layer and bilayer cuprates must be re-evaluated" rests on an untested assumption.

10. The model parameters and their meaning need justification (p. 8–9, Fig. 4). With t = 0.32 eV, t′ = −0.063 eV, t″ = 0.031 eV and ΔMF = 0.68 eV, the model fits only three cuts per pocket. Please state:
    - how many fitted quantities constrain these four parameters, and with what uncertainties;
    - whether ΔMF = 0.68 eV agrees with the measured charge-transfer gap of these phases (Ref. 27 correlates exactly this quantity with n);
    - why the same ΔMF is appropriate for IP0 and IP1 when the AF order parameter should depend on doping and layer position.

    The 2 eV upper band in Fig. 4a should also be reconciled with the CTB/ZRS/UHB schematic in Fig. 4g, which places the ZRS as a band separate from the CTB. That is a different picture from a mean-field SDW gap.

11. Sample statistics are thin. There are three crystals and eight regions. The 7- and 8-layer phases appear to be one region each. It is not clear how many distinct regions give the IP1 gap points in Fig. 3n, or whether the result survives re-cleaving. Please state for each data point in Figs. 1h–j, 2h, 3n and 4f which sample, region, n and spectrometer it comes from, in the main text or Fig. 3n's legend. At present this appears only in Table S1, which is not in the submitted PDF.

C. Minor

12. Fig. 4a carries the label "μ = −0.330 eV". The text and Fig. 4c–e use μ1 = −0.556, μ2 = −0.609 and μ3 = −0.643 eV, and Fig. 4f spans −0.55 to −0.65 eV. Please correct or explain.

13. Figs. S1–S5 and Table S1 are referenced throughout but are not included in the submitted file, so the region assignments, the hemispherical against ARToF comparison and the model fits cannot be checked.

14. The difference in R/L ratio between the hemispherical and ARToF analysers (Fig. 2h) is attributed to analyser nonlinearity. The comparison is between different samples and regions. A same-region comparison, or a detector-linearity calibration, would support the claim that the ARToF result is "more intrinsic".

15. With 6.994 eV photons, the inner planes of an 8-layer unit lie several nm below the surface. Please comment on how the escape depth affects the relative intensities of IP0, IP1 and OP, and whether it biases the spectral-weight comparisons.

16. Fig. 1h–j and Fig. 3n mix pockets from different n. Distinguish n by symbol so readers can see whether the trends hold within a fixed layer number.

17. Typos: "wihch" (p. 3); "substraction" (p. 6).

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Measure the temperature dependence of the IP1 gap and of the IP0 quasiparticle peak in at least one 5- or 6-layer region up to above the relevant Tc. Analyse the unsymmetrized, Fermi-function-divided spectra for Bogoliubov signatures. This single dataset would decide whether the IP1 gap reflects pairing.
2. Compare the IP1 gap between 5/6-layer regions (IP1 adjacent to OP) and 7/8-layer regions (IP1 adjacent to IP2) at matched IP1 doping. Add a minimal layered model with interlayer tunnelling, to separate intrinsic from proximity-induced gaps.
3. Give a per-region table in the main text with n, the per-layer dopings including back-side uncertainties, the instrument, and the sum rule against the host doping. Add a spatial map showing that each region is phase-pure on the scale of the laser spot.
4. Recast the abstract and Fig. 4h so that the AF order and the superconducting boundaries are presented as inferences, unless direct magnetic or temperature-dependent evidence is added.
