Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use spatially resolved laser ARPES with a time-of-flight analyzer to find micrometre-scale n = 5–8 intergrowth regions on the surface of Bi2223 crystals. They report Fermi pockets around (π/2, π/2) from the inner CuO2 planes, with Luttinger dopings from 0.007 to 0.088. This is a wider range than the earlier Ba2Ca(n-1)CunO2n(F,O)2 work (refs. 21, 22), and it is in a different cuprate family. Their main claims are threefold. The innermost plane (IP0) is metallic and gapless down to p = 0.007. The second-innermost plane (IP1) carries a d-wave-like gap of up to about 33 meV down to p = 0.02. Together these are said to define an "intrinsic", disorder-free phase diagram in which pairing coexists with strong antiferromagnetic (AFM) order. If the observations hold, they matter for the doped-Mott-insulator problem. However, the step from pockets and gaps at 15 K to "robust electron pairing" with "strong antiferromagnetic order" is not yet supported by the data shown.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The spatially resolved ARToF approach is a technical advance. It gives access to n = 5–8 Bi-based phases that are not available as single crystals, and to IP0/IP1 dopings well beyond the earlier p < 0.05 range.
- The data quality is high. Fig. 2f shows well-defined quasiparticle peaks along the entire IP0 pocket, and Figs. 1b–g show clearly resolved concentric pockets.
- The comparison is systematic across n, across host-crystal dopings (UD30K, UD95K, OP111K) and across planes. The pocket geometry evolves smoothly (Fig. 1h–j), and so does the R/L spectral-weight ratio (Fig. 2h).
- The contrast between gapless IP0 and gapped IP1 at overlapping dopings (Fig. 3n, Fig. 4h) is a striking observation and may constrain theory.
- A single minimal parameter set {t, t′, t″, ΔMF}, with only μ varying, describes all pockets (Fig. 4f). This is an economical description.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The IP1 gap is not shown to be a superconducting (pairing) gap.** The title, abstract and Discussion claim "robust electron pairing" with gaps up to 33 meV down to p = 0.02. All data are at a single temperature (15 K, Figs. 2–3), and the gap is extracted only from symmetrized EDCs (Fig. 3f–j). Symmetrization assumes particle–hole symmetry about EF. It produces an apparent gap wherever the band fails to reach EF, for example a pseudogap, an AFM- or hybridization-induced gap, or a band top lying just below EF near the major-axis vertices. The weak, broad IP1 EDCs overlap with the IP0 band (Fig. 3c–e, p. 7), and this makes the extraction harder still. To support a pairing interpretation the authors should:
   - measure the temperature dependence of the IP1 gap through the Tc of the host region, and show it closing or filling at a well-defined temperature;
   - show particle–hole symmetry directly: Bogoliubov back-bending of the IP1 dispersion near the major-axis vertices, or thermally populated states above EF after division by the Fermi function at elevated temperature;
   - show the IP1 dispersion along the θ = 90° cut, so readers can see that kF is crossed rather than approached.

   Without these, "pairing" should become "energy gap". It is also unclear what Tc applies to an intergrowth region inside a crystal with bulk Tc = 30, 95 or 111 K.

2. **"Strong antiferromagnetic order" is asserted, not measured, and the pocket picture is partly circular.** The coexistence claim (abstract; pp. 8, 10–11; Fig. 4h, "AFM metal" and "AFM+SC") rests on NMR results for other multilayer families (ref. 29) and on the pockets themselves. No magnetic probe is applied to these Bi-based intergrowths. Pockets near (π/2, π/2) with suppressed outer-side weight also arise without long-range order, for example in YRZ-type or short-range-correlation scenarios. Discriminating tests are possible within the existing data:
   - Is each pocket centred exactly on the AFM zone boundary at (π/2, π/2), as Néel-order folding requires, or displaced toward Γ?
   - Is there a shadow-band intensity mirroring the pocket across the (π,0)–(0,π) line?
   - Does the R/L weight ratio (Fig. 2h) follow mean-field coherence factors quantitatively at the fitted ΔMF = 0.68 eV?

   The authors should present these tests. Otherwise they should rephrase "coexisting with strong AFM order" as an inference, and label the AFM boundaries in Fig. 4h as hypothetical rather than measured.

3. **The doping-dependence narrative for the IP1 gap conflicts with the authors' own d-wave fits.** The text (pp. 7, 11) and Fig. 3n say that the IP1 gap "decreases as the doping level approaches 0.02". Yet the d-wave fits in Fig. 3m give extrapolated Δ0 = 46, 30 and 60 meV for p = 0.020, 0.051 and 0.088. This is non-monotonic, and the lowest-doped pocket has the second-largest Δ0. The small maximum gap at p = 0.020 in Fig. 3n most likely reflects the small pocket: its major-axis vertex reaches a small value of |cos kx a − cos ky a|/2. It need not reflect weaker pairing. In addition, every Δ0 is an extrapolation from |cos − cos|/2 ≲ 0.4–0.5 up to 1, and it assumes the simple d-wave form survives AFM reconstruction. The authors should:
   - state which quantity measures pairing strength, the gap at the pocket vertex or Δ0;
   - give Δ0 with uncertainties for all IP1 pockets;
   - check the gap function against higher harmonics;
   - revise the statements about doping dependence and Fig. 4h accordingly.

4. **"Abrupt transition from Mott insulator to metal" and "metallic state" go beyond what ARPES at p ≥ 0.007 shows.** The abstract and Discussion (pp. 9–10) claim that "infinitesimal" doping produces a metal. The data contain no p = 0 or near-zero reference from the same platform. Nor is there any measurement of how quasiparticle coherence evolves as p → 0. Further, IP0 sits within a few ångströms of metallic, superconducting outer planes in a crystal with Tc up to 111 K. Screening, proximity effects or interlayer tunnelling may therefore stabilize the coherent IP0 quasiparticle, so the plane is not an isolated lightly doped CuO2 sheet. The authors should:
   - quantify quasiparticle weight and EDC linewidth against p for IP0 (Fig. 2f-type data for every pocket), to show whether Z extrapolates to a finite value as p → 0;
   - discuss the role of the metallic outer planes;
   - soften "abrupt" and "infinitesimal" unless a jump can be demonstrated.

   The reinterpretation of the established single- and bilayer phase diagram as a disorder artefact (p. 10, "must be re-evaluated") also needs a more careful argument. It should include alternatives such as interlayer screening and the different apical environments.

B. Should address

5. **The phase (n) assignment rests on indirect, partly circular criteria.** Each region is assigned n = 5, 6, 7 or 8 from the number of Fermi-surface sheets and from the expectation that IP0 halves in size between odd and even n (p. 4). Doping varies from region to region and from crystal to crystal, so a small IP0 pocket can mean either an even n or simply lower doping. Similarly, three sheets may come from n = 5 or n = 6. The assignment matters for the layer-dependent conclusions and for the comparison with refs. 21 and 22. The authors should:
   - add independent evidence, such as STEM or core-level/c-axis information on the same measured regions, or a doping sum rule (total hole count across planes compared with expectations for each n);
   - show that the ~10 µm spot does not straddle domains of different n.

6. **The conversion from pocket area to doping is not specified.** Every doping value on every x-axis comes from "enclosed area" (p. 5). The paper does not state the Luttinger counting used: the number of pockets per Brillouin zone, the spin degeneracy, and whether the AFM reduced zone is used. This factor sets the absolute scale, including the headline p = 0.007, and the comparison with refs. 21 and 22. Hidden-assumption checks are also needed:
   - that no other states (for example antinodal ones) contribute to the hole count;
   - how the weak outer half of each pocket was traced when computing the area.

   Please give the formula, and add error bars to p in Figs. 1h–j, 2h, 3n and 4f.

7. **The "gapless" IP0 claim needs a quantitative upper bound.** The text says no gap is detected on any IP0 pocket (Fig. 3f, g, k). The data are at 15 K, and the IP0 pockets in Fig. 3a, b have weak peaks on the outer side (θ = 90°–180°). The authors should state the smallest gap the symmetrized-EDC analysis could resolve at that temperature and linewidth. They should also discuss why a plane in a bulk superconductor with Tc = 111 K shows no proximity-induced gap. That absence is itself significant and deserves comment.

8. **The disagreement with ref. 22 is not resolved.** Ref. 22 reports a gapless IP1 for p < 0.04 and gaps of about 5 meV. This paper finds a 12 meV gap at p = 0.020 and gaps up to 33 meV (p. 8). The text attributes the difference to "many additional factors". The authors should compare measurement temperatures, the definition of the gap (vertex value or Δ0), resolution, and how doping was obtained. They should also say whether the different apical environments (F/O versus BiO/SrO) could explain the difference. In addition, the IP1 point at p = 0.020 comes from the only region measured with the hemispherical analyzer (UD30K-R2; Methods, p. 13), so a check of that point with the ARToF system would help.

9. **Reproducibility and statistics are thin.** Eight regions on three crystals are reported (Methods; Table S1). Key data points, such as the lowest IP0 doping (0.007) and the lowest-doped gapped IP1 (0.020), each appear to come from a single region. The authors should show whether pockets of similar doping in different regions or crystals give the same gap and the same R/L ratio. They should also report whether results survive re-cleaving.

10. **The asymmetry in spectral weight is interpreted as intrinsic without matrix-element controls.** The R/L ratio (Fig. 2h) and the a + b cos θ form (Fig. 2g) are used as physical fingerprints, and the model is said to reproduce them (Fig. S5). Photoemission matrix elements at 6.994 eV depend strongly on polarization and geometry. The authors should show data at a second polarization or sample orientation, for example a pocket rotated by 90°. The claim that ARToF ratios are "more intrinsic" because of the "absence of nonlinear effects" (p. 6) also needs a detector-linearity calibration. Without one, the systematic offset between the filled and open symbols in Fig. 2h is unexplained.

11. **The comparison with the mean-field model is qualitative, and the model cannot address the central result.** All pockets are fitted with fixed {t, t′, t″, ΔMF}, varying only μ (Fig. 4f). The authors should:
   - state the fitting procedure and the cost function;
   - say whether ΔMF and t were fitted or fixed;
   - give parameter uncertainties;
   - show overlays in the main text, not only in Fig. S4.

    The model has no pairing term and treats IP0 and IP1 identically. It therefore cannot account for the IP0/IP1 dichotomy that the paper highlights. This limitation should be stated plainly. Also, ΔMF = 0.68 eV implies a gap of about 1.36 eV between the upper and lower bands. Please relate this to the ~1 eV charge-transfer gap cited on p. 10.

C. Minor

12. **Fig. 4 labelling is inconsistent.** Panel a lists "μ = −0.330 eV", while the text and panels c–e use μ1–μ3 = −0.556 to −0.643 eV. The vertical axis is labelled E − EF, but the μ lines sit near −0.2 to −0.3 eV rather than at zero. Please define the energy reference.

13. **Material cited in the text is missing from the PDF.** The text refers throughout to Figs. S1–S5, Table S1 and Supplementary Materials (model details), but none appear in this PDF. These items carry important content: the spatial maps, the per-region list, Fig. S2 and the model fits. Please include them.

14. **Error bars are missing or undefined.** Figs. 1h–j have no error bars. For Figs. 2h and 3k–n, "the uncertainty in determining the spectral weight/gap size" should be defined: fit errors, spread across cuts, or something else.

15. **The θ convention could confuse readers.** Fig. 2g runs from 0° to 360°, starting at L0. Figs. 3k–m use −120° to 120°, with ±90° at the major-axis vertices. Please state both conventions together in one place.

16. **Fig. 3n needs clearer marking.** The asterisk symbols for refs. 21 and 22 cannot be told apart from the new data at the printed size. Please mark the region and instrument for each point, especially the hemispherical-analyzer point.

17. **Typos:** "wihch" (p. 3), "substraction" (p. 6), "Fermi arc length" (p. 2; plural agreement), "Cun" formula formatting in the abstract.

18. **The host-crystal Tc does not stand for the region.** Methods states that intergrowth dopings "are expected to change accordingly" with host Tc. Please give the measured outer-plane (OP) doping for each region so that readers do not take the host Tc as a proxy for the region.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one temperature series, from 15 K to above the host Tc, on an IP1 pocket together with an IP0 pocket in the same region. This single measurement would turn "gap" into "pairing gap" and would also test the gapless IP0 against the gapped IP1 under identical conditions.
2. Add a plot of IP0 quasiparticle weight and width against p across all regions, so the "abrupt metal" claim can be quantified.
3. Test the AFM folding scenario directly. Check the pocket centre relative to (π/2, π/2), look for shadow intensity across the AFM zone boundary, and compare the R/L ratio quantitatively with the coherence factors at the fitted ΔMF.
4. Reframe Fig. 4h to separate measured quantities (pocket dopings, gaps) from inferred phase boundaries, and discuss why p alone does not organise the IP0 and IP1 data. The latter point weakens the idea of a single "intrinsic" phase diagram.
