Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript maps the transport phase diagram of dual-gated twisted bilayer WSe2 in devices at 5.0°, 4.8°, 4.3°, 4.2°, 4.1° and 3.8°. These angles bridge the two superconducting regimes reported earlier at 5° (near the Van Hove singularity, metallic AFM, no half-filling gap) and 3.65° (near a half-filling insulator). The authors report that the superconducting pocket moves continuously to lower filling and displacement field with the AFM/Fermi-surface-reconstruction region. They report that Tc, T_BKT and Jc decrease with twist angle, that a gap at ν = −1 appears only below about 4.3°, and they compare these results with FRG and Hartree-Fock calculations. A systematic twist-angle study connecting the two published phase diagrams would be useful to the moiré-superconductivity community. The strongest conclusions, however (spin-fluctuation pairing at all angles, the BCS limit, a single smoothly connected ground state), go well beyond what the transport data shown here establish.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First multi-device study of tWSe2 superconductivity across the 3.8–5° range, with the pocket tracked in n–D space in each device (Fig. 1d,e).
- Consistent phenomenology in every device: the superconducting pocket sits on the low-D edge of the resistive (presumably AFM) region, whether or not that region is gapped at ν = −1.
- Combines several superconducting measures (mean-field Tc, T_BKT, Jc) and thermally activated gaps (Fig. 3) with angle-resolved FRG and HF theory using a single microscopic model (Fig. 4).
- Gives a concrete answer to the question of whether the 3.65° and 5° superconductors are related, which the community will want.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The conclusion that pairing is spin-fluctuation mediated at all angles rests on adjacency alone. In the Results ("these findings indicate that superconductivity is spin-fluctuation mediated for all twist angles") and in the Discussion ("strongly suggests…"), the mechanism is inferred only from the superconducting pocket bordering a resistive region. That region's magnetic character is itself inferred from longitudinal and Hall magnetotransport in Figs. SI 4–5, and the abstract says "presumably antiferromagnetic". Adjacency to a correlated phase does not identify the pairing glue. A phonon-mediated or other conventional superconductor confined to the high-DOS edge of a symmetry-broken phase would show the same geometry, and so would one competing with that phase. To support the mechanism claim, the authors should do one of the following:
   - Give transport evidence that singles out magnetic fluctuations. Examples: how Tc scales with distance from the AFM boundary along n and along D; whether Tc correlates with the AFM onset temperature across angles; in-plane-field dependence of Tc compared with the Pauli limit in this Ising/spin-valley-locked system; how the pocket responds to a perpendicular field that suppresses the AFM state.
   - Otherwise, reduce the claim to "consistent with" and remove "indicate" from the Results and Discussion.

   The AFM identification should also be presented in the main text with its evidence, since every mechanistic statement depends on it.

2. The BCS-limit conclusion conflicts with the paper's own superconducting data. The Discussion concludes "the superconductivity is in the BCS limit for all twist angles", using band-theory superfluid stiffness ρs = 150–350 K, about 10³ × Tc. The paper's data point the other way.
   - Fig. 2b: at 3.8° T_BKT ≈ 0.10 K while the mean-field Tc ≈ 0.31 K, and the T_BKT/Tc ratio falls steadily with decreasing angle (≈0.39/0.445 at 5°, ≈0.21/0.28 at 4.2°). If ρs were ~10³ Tc, T_BKT would sit essentially at the mean-field Tc. A large gap between them implies a stiffness of order Tc, i.e. strong phase fluctuations. The authors attribute this to "twist angle disorder" without evidence.
   - Fig. 2c: Jc ≈ 1–16 nA/µm. A rough depairing estimate, Jc ~ (2e/ħ)ρs/ξ with ρs ≈ 150 K and ξ of tens of nm, gives about 10² µA/µm. That is four orders of magnitude above the measured values.

   Either the measured Jc and T_BKT are dominated by inhomogeneity (which then undermines their use as "smooth" angle-dependent intrinsic quantities), or the real stiffness is far below the band value (which undermines the BCS-limit statement). The authors should:
   - say which, and estimate ρs from the data (from T_BKT via the Nelson–Kosterlitz relation, and from Jc given an Hc2-derived ξ);
   - compare these estimates with the band value;
   - remove or justify the assertion that renormalisation by more than 10× "does not occur without extreme fine tuning". Measured stiffness in flat-band moiré superconductors argues against that assertion.

   The Uemura-plot placement in Fig. 2d inset uses a non-interacting band T_F rather than a measured superfluid density, so it cannot settle this question either.

3. The twist angles are not explained or validated, and there is one device per angle. The paper's central claim is a "smooth" and "continuous" evolution with θ, quoted to 0.1° (4.1°, 4.2°, 4.3° are distinct points in Figs. 2b,c and 3g). The Methods do not say how θ was determined, give no uncertainties, and say nothing about twist-angle inhomogeneity between contact pairs. The authors should:
   - give, for each device, the method (for example, the density of ν = −1 or full filling from gate capacitance, or Landau-fan/Brown–Zak oscillations);
   - give the uncertainty and the spread between contact pairs;
   - add horizontal error bars to Figs. 2b–d and 3g.

   Each angle is represented by a single device, so device-to-device variation at fixed angle cannot be separated from the θ dependence. This matters because the trends are not actually monotonic:
   - In Fig. 2b the mean-field Tc at 3.8° (≈0.31 K) is higher than at 4.2° (≈0.28 K) and 4.3° (≈0.30 K).
   - In Fig. 3g the gap at 3.8° (≈3.4 meV) is about half that at 4.1° (≈6.3 meV).

   The abstract's statement of "repeatability between different devices" is not demonstrated at any fixed angle. At least one repeat at a fixed angle, or multiple contact pairs within each device, would show the spread. The ±D superconducting pockets visible in each device (Fig. 1d) could also serve as an internal reproducibility check. Without these, "remarkably smooth evolution" should be softened.

B. Should address

4. The Tc criterion and the mixing of data from other groups. Tc is defined at 80% of R_n (Fig. 2b), which is an unusual choice. For the broad, noisy 3.8° transition in Fig. 2a this criterion inflates Tc relative to a 50% or zero-resistance criterion, and it produces the non-monotonic point noted above. In addition:
   - The 4.2° curve in Fig. 2a saturates at R/R_n ≈ 0.08 rather than zero, so this device does not show zero resistance in the plotted data. Yet the Discussion speaks of "a resistance that goes to zero at base temperature" at large angles.
   - Fig. 2b mixes the authors' data with open symbols from Refs. [1] and [28], which may use different Tc criteria. The "factor 2 between 5° and 3.5°" relies on these external points, and it is not stated which reference supplies the point near 3.5°.

   The authors should replot Fig. 2b with 50% and 10% criteria, state the criterion used for the literature points, and comment on the finite residual resistance at 4.2° (for example, contact or non-superconducting parallel path, given that this device alone uses TaSe2 contacts).

5. Excitation current, heating and Jc normalisation. The Methods state that superconducting characterisation used 1–5 nA bias. In Fig. 2c, Jc at 3.8° is ≈1 nA/µm, and the channel width is not given, so the critical current may be comparable to or below the excitation. In that case the 3.8° R(T), the broad transition, and the gap between Tc and T_BKT could be partly current-induced. The authors should:
   - give the device widths used to compute Jc and the excitation used for each curve in Fig. 2a;
   - show R(T) at reduced excitation for 3.8°.

   Jc is taken from dV/dI coherence-peak positions at a "fridge thermometer" temperature of 20 mK. Electron temperature and filtering are not described, and they should be, because the 3.8° Tc and Jc are close to the noise floor.

6. The experimental gap trend does not support the statement of "agreement" with Hartree-Fock. The text states that the calculated gaps "consistently increase with decreasing twist angle, in agreement with experiment". The measured gaps in Fig. 3g are non-monotonic: about 3.4, 6.3, 4.7 and 3.9 meV at 3.8°, 4.1°, 4.2° and 4.3°, with the largest gap at 4.1°. On the theory side:
   - The HF results depend strongly on ε. The Methods fix ε_HF = 2.2 ε_FRG = 30.8, which corresponds to the orange curve in Fig. 3g. That curve gives Δ ≈ 0 at 4.2° and only ≈2 meV at 3.8°, below all measured values, and predicts no gap at 4.2–4.3°, where gaps are measured.
   - The HF calculation also removes the symmetric Hartree renormalisation.

   The authors should state which ε is the model prediction and why the other curves are shown, and describe the agreement as qualitative (the gap opening below ~4.4°) rather than as matching the trend.

   The Arrhenius fits in Fig. 3f span only about 3–10 K (1/T = 0.1–0.3 K⁻¹), and some curves visibly saturate. The authors should state the fit window and how the error bars in Fig. 3g were obtained, and show the 3.8° fit, whose gap is anomalously small.

7. Contacts versus intrinsic insulating behaviour. The Methods say both two- and four-terminal measurements were used, but the figures do not say which is which. In TMD devices, contact resistance grows at low temperature and depends on the gates. The ν = −1 insulating state at 3.8° reaches 10⁶ Ω (Fig. 3a), and the "metal–insulator transition" at |D| ≈ 0.1–0.3 V/nm (Fig. 3b) could include a contact contribution. The 4.2° device uses different contacts (TaSe2) from the others (α-RuCl3 doped). The authors should state the configuration for every figure. For the insulating state, they should show that the four-terminal resistance and the two-terminal minus contact resistance agree, or show the contact resistance versus D and T. This matters because the "half-band insulator" result and the comparison with HF gaps are central.

8. The claim that superconductivity is "fully disconnected from the VHs" at low angles needs an experimental VHs position. In Figs. 4a–d, the VHs line R^VHS at 4.2° and 3.8° is drawn far from the superconducting pocket. It is not stated whether this line is measured (for example, a Hall-density sign change, which is how it was located at 5°) or taken from band theory. The supporting Fig. SI 6 is not included in the submission. Interactions and the AFM reconstruction can shift the experimental VHs substantially, so the Hall data locating the VHs at 3.8° and 4.2° should be shown in the main text.

   There is also a tension with the theory section. There, the shrinking superconducting pockets and falling Tc are attributed to "the decrease of the DOS away from the VH line", i.e. in FRG the superconductivity is still controlled by VHs proximity. The authors should reconcile the experimental statement that superconductivity is "not tied to the VHs" with an FRG explanation that relies on the VHs DOS.

9. The quantitative mapping between FRG and experiment is unclear.
   - Figs. 4e–h use the interlayer potential E_z (meV), while the experiment uses D (V/nm). The conversion, including the layer-polarisability/screening factor, is not given, so the claimed agreement in "approximately the same, experimentally-observed, locations" cannot be checked.
   - FRG uses ε = 14 and U = 3 eV, while HF uses ε ≈ 24–31. No independent justification is given for ε = 14 with hBN gate dielectrics.
   - Λ_c (meV scale in Fig. 4h) is a proxy, not a Tc. Whether its angle trend matches Fig. 2b should be shown on a common plot.
   - Static, one-loop truncated-unity FRG with a sharp cutoff is weak-coupling. It does not produce the ν = −1 insulator (HF is added separately). Its reliability in the "intermediate coupling" regime the authors place the system in should be discussed, together with a sensitivity check of the phase diagram to ε and U.

   The authors should provide the E_z–D conversion and overlay the experimental pocket outlines from Figs. 4a–d on the FRG maps.

10. Key evidence is in supplementary material that is not part of the submission. The identification of AFM order and the M1/M2 distinction (Figs. SI 4–5), the VHs placement (SI 6), the 4.1° and 4.3° devices (SI 2–3), HF ε-sensitivity (SI 9), and the interaction values, T_F and T_D definitions are all deferred to SI. None of this appears in the PDF provided. The criteria used to draw the phase boundaries in Figs. 4a–d (resistance thresholds, Hall features) are also not stated. Because the phase-diagram claims depend on these boundaries, the criteria and representative data should be in the paper.

11. The coupling-strength measures depend on non-interacting quantities. In Fig. 2d, T_F is defined as the band-theory chemical potential relative to the valence-band maximum, and T_D as the band Drude weight. With the AFM reconstruction adjacent to (and possibly coexisting with) the superconductor, the real Fermi energy may differ substantially. The 1% threshold for "strong coupling" is also presented as a convention without justification. The authors should state the n and D at which T_F and T_D are evaluated, test how sensitive they are to the model parameters, and present the Tc/T_F trend as model-dependent. A T_F from quantum-oscillation frequency or Hall density near the pocket would help.

C. Minor

12. Sign and label inconsistencies between Fig. 1e and the Fig. 2a caption:
   - For 4.8°, Fig. 1e shows the pocket at D = −0.25 to −0.45 V/nm, while the caption gives D = +0.37 V/nm.
   - For 4.2°, Fig. 1e shows D = +0.08 to +0.20 V/nm with an n axis labelled 5.0–6.0 (no minus sign), while the caption gives n = −5.53 × 10¹² cm⁻² and D = −0.12 V/nm.

   Please make consistent, and state whether the ±D pockets have the same Tc.

13. The notation switches between "ν = 1 hole per moiré cell" and "ν = −1" (text versus Fig. 3 and Fig. 4). Please use one convention.

14. "Fully insulating state (dρ/dT < 0 down to lowest temperature)": Fig. 3 data stop at 1.5–2.5 K. Please show the ν = −1 resistance at dilution-fridge temperatures, or qualify the statement.

15. The Discussion's suggestion of "a first-order SC–insulating AF phase competition" (Figs. SI 4–5) is not tested, for example with hysteresis in n, D or B. Either show such a test or label the statement as speculation.

16. The BKT criterion V ∝ I³ is shown only for 4.8° (Fig. 2a inset). Please show the I–V exponent versus T for the 3.8° device, where T_BKT is most decisive.

17. Methods: give filtering, electron-temperature calibration, lock-in excitation per measurement, and channel dimensions. "Data available upon reasonable request" is weak for a study whose value is a cross-device comparison. Please consider depositing the data behind Figs. 1–3.

18. Typos: "the the interplay" (p. 6); "systemically" should be "systematically" (p. 2); "tWSe2" is not subscripted in the acknowledgments.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a figure on superfluid stiffness and coupling that compares ρs estimated from T_BKT, from Jc and from Hc2-derived ξ with the band value. Reframe the BCS-limit conclusion according to what this shows.
- Document twist-angle determination and inhomogeneity per device. Where possible, add a second device or contact pair at one angle, and the ±D pockets, to quantify spread at fixed θ.
- Bring the AFM and VHs evidence (Hall density, magnetoresistance, phase-boundary criteria) into the main text for 3.8° and 4.2°.
- Put theory and experiment on common axes, with the E_z–D conversion, experimental pocket outlines overlaid on the FRG maps, and Λ_c versus θ beside Tc versus θ. Use a single, justified dielectric constant (or a stated range) for both FRG and HF.
