Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript measures transport in twisted bilayer WSe2 devices with twist angles from 5.0 to 3.8 degrees (four main devices, plus 4.3 and 4.1 degree devices in the SI). The aim is to connect the two superconducting phase diagrams reported at 5.0 and 3.65 degrees. The authors report that the superconducting pocket and an adjacent region of Fermi-surface reconstruction, presumed antiferromagnetic, move together to lower displacement field and to fillings closer to nu = -1 as the angle decreases. Over the same range Tc, T_BKT and Jc fall, and a gap opens at half filling below about 4.3 degrees. They compare these trends with FRG and Hartree-Fock calculations on a three-orbital Wannier model. An angle series of this kind is timely and would be useful, because it addresses whether the two published tWSe2 superconductors are the same phase. The strongest interpretive claims are that pairing is mediated by spin fluctuations at all angles and that the system is in the BCS limit. These rest on indirect evidence and on non-interacting band quantities, and they need more support or more careful wording.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- It is the first systematic multi-device study of how the tWSe2 superconducting and correlated phase diagram depends on twist angle, filling the gap between the two existing reports (Fig. 1d,e; Fig. 2b).
- Fig. 1d shows dual-gated n-D maps across devices. They show directly that the superconducting pocket stays on the low-D side of the reconstructed (M1/M2) region while the whole structure moves toward nu = -1.
- Several quantities are tracked against angle (mean-field Tc, T_BKT, Jc, half-filling gap). They give a multi-parameter picture rather than a single number.
- The theory comparison is a real one: FRG phase diagrams at the measured angles and HF gaps at nu = -1. It identifies IVC-AFM order and a crossover from incommensurate to commensurate order as the AFM region crosses nu = -1.
- The finding that the half-filling insulator at 3.8 degrees appears only in a narrow D window and only below about 8 K (Fig. 3a,b) constrains the "Mott insulator" interpretation of the 3.65 degree device in the literature.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. How the twist angle is determined, and its uncertainty and homogeneity, are not described. Every conclusion is a trend against theta (Figs. 1-4), but neither the Methods nor the main text explains how theta = 5.0, 4.8, 4.2, 3.8 degrees (and 4.3, 4.1) were obtained: from the nu = -1 or full-filling density, from Landau fans or quantum oscillations, or from the target angle at stacking. No error bars on theta are shown in Fig. 2b,c,d or Fig. 3g. The text itself suspects "twist angle disorder in the 3.8 degree device" to explain the broad transition and the large Tc-T_BKT gap (p. 4). This matters because the abstract's claim of "smooth evolution" rests on adjacent points that are only 0.1-0.4 degrees apart (4.1, 4.2, 4.3). The authors should:
   - state the calibration procedure;
   - give theta and its uncertainty for each device, ideally from several contact pairs so that spatial angle variation is quantified;
   - add horizontal error bars to Figs. 2b-d and 3g;
   - test the twist-disorder conjecture for 3.8 degrees by comparing the nu = -1 density, or the superconducting pocket position, across contact pairs.

2. The claim that superconductivity is spin-fluctuation mediated at all angles rests only on proximity. The abstract, p. 4 and p. 7 conclude that "the superconductivity is spin-fluctuation mediated for all twist angles," but the evidence offered is that the pocket sits next to a region whose AFM character is itself inferred indirectly ("presumably antiferromagnetic", abstract). Three gaps follow.
   - (a) The AFM identification at 4.2 and 3.8 degrees comes from magnetotransport in Figs. SI 4-5, which the main text only cites. Nothing in the main paper shows that the M1/M2 regions are magnetic rather than, for example, a valley-polarized or charge-ordered reconstruction.
   - (b) At 3.8 and 4.2 degrees the pocket borders both the AFM region and the fully gapped nu = -1 insulator (Fig. 1e, Fig. 4c,d). So the data cannot separate "adjacent to AFM" from "adjacent to a doped correlated insulator", which is the interpretation the 3.65 degree literature proposed. The statement that superconductivity is "neither necessarily tied ... to the half band insulator" (abstract) therefore needs a device or regime where the two boundaries are separated.
   - (c) Adjacency is also expected in competing-order scenarios that do not involve fluctuation-mediated pairing.
   The authors should either weaken the claim to "consistent with" or add discriminating evidence. Options: in-plane field dependence compared with the Pauli limit; how Tc and the pocket follow the AFM boundary as D is varied; an analysis of the FRG vertex showing that the SC channel is driven by the DW fluctuations (the FRG pairing symmetry and its origin are not reported); and the main-text magnetotransport that establishes the M1/M2 phases.

3. The superconducting state at the smallest angles is weakly characterized, and the "smooth evolution" of Tc does not quite match Fig. 2.
   - Fig. 2a: the 4.2 degree curve saturates at R/Rn of about 0.07 rather than zero. The 3.8 degree curve is noisy around zero, with negative excursions.
   - Fig. 2c: Jc at 3.8 degrees is about 1 nA/um, roughly 15 times smaller than at 5 degrees.
   - p. 7: the text describes low-angle superconductivity as "fragile ... often manifests as a marginal or failed SC state."
   - Fig. 2b: the mean-field Tc is not monotonic (3.8 degrees, about 0.31 K, is above 4.2 degrees, about 0.28 K, and 4.3 degrees, about 0.30 K). The statement that Tc is "decreasing continuously with decreasing twist angle, and dropping by approximately a factor 2 between 5 and 3.5 degrees" depends on the literature points, which may use different Tc definitions.
   The conclusion that the large- and small-angle pockets "are smoothly connected and therefore probably represent the same ground state order" (p. 4) needs a firmer base. The authors should:
   - show zero resistance within the noise floor for every device, using four-terminal data, and explain the residual at 4.2 degrees (contact or series path, partial percolation, or a non-superconducting parallel channel);
   - give R(T) and I-V data for the 4.3 and 4.1 degree devices;
   - use one Tc definition throughout (for example 50% of Rn, or zero resistance), applied to their own data and, where possible, to the literature points, or mark the literature points as differently defined;
   - state which angles the two triangles labelled [1] in Fig. 2b correspond to (Ref. 1 reports 3.65 degrees, yet two triangles appear near 3.45 and 3.65 degrees).
   Since T_BKT at 3.8 degrees (about 0.1 K) is only a third of the mean-field Tc, the 3.8 degree point should be treated as qualitatively different until the twist disorder is quantified.

B. Should address

4. The "BCS limit" and weak-coupling conclusions rest on non-interacting band quantities. Tc/T_F, Tc/T_D (Fig. 2d), the Uemura plot (Fig. 2d inset) and the superfluid stiffness values of 350 K to 150 K (p. 7) all come from the bare three-orbital Wannier band structure, not from measurement. The argument that renormalizing rho_s by "more than 10" would need "extreme fine tuning" (p. 7) is not obviously true for flat moiré bands, where measured stiffness can be far from band estimates. The authors' own data hint at strong suppression: Jc falls about 15-fold while the band rho_s falls only about 2.3-fold, and T_BKT/Tc_MF drops to about 0.3 at 3.8 degrees. There is also an internal tension. p. 4 reads the upward trend in Tc/T_F as evolution toward stronger correlation, p. 5 places 3.8 degrees in an "intermediate regime of moderate interactions," and p. 7 concludes "BCS limit for all twist angles." The authors should:
   - explain how rho_s is computed, including whether the quantum-geometric contribution is included, and why a value is quoted at 3.65 degrees, an angle not measured here;
   - estimate an experimental stiffness or coherence length (for example from T_BKT via the universal jump, from Jc, or from the perpendicular Hc2) and place those values on the Uemura plot;
   - reconcile the wording across pp. 4, 5 and 7.

5. The half-filling gap does not follow the angle trend the text claims, and the gap conventions are unclear. Fig. 3g gives an experimental gap of about 3.3 meV at 3.8 degrees, about 6.3 meV at 4.1, about 4.7 at 4.2 and about 3.9 at 4.3. The gap peaks at 4.1 degrees and is smallest at 3.8 degrees. Yet the text says the calculated gaps "consistently increase with decreasing twist angle, in agreement with experiment" (p. 5). For eps = 24, HF gives about 4.9 meV at 3.8 degrees, larger than experiment, while it underestimates experiment at 4.1-4.3 degrees. The authors should:
   - discuss this non-monotonic behaviour, which may reflect twist disorder at 3.8 degrees (see concern 1) or the choice of D;
   - give the 3.8 degree point an error bar;
   - state the fit range and the D used for each device;
   - make the activation convention (sigma ~ exp(-Delta/2k_BT)) match the HF quantity, i.e. the single-particle gap compared with the charge gap.
   The Methods say eps_HF = 2.2 eps_FRG (which gives about 30.8 for eps_FRG = 14), but Fig. 3g scans eps = 24-30.6. They should say which value is the principled one and how far the agreement depends on that choice.

6. Contacts and measurement configuration. The Methods mention both four-terminal and two-terminal measurements but do not say which panels use which. TMD contacts often become non-ohmic at low temperature. Two concerns follow:
   - At 3.8 degrees the half-filling resistance reaches about 10^6 ohm (Fig. 3a). Contact freeze-out could add to the apparent activation, and to the "MIT at about 8 K" (Fig. 3b).
   - Series contact resistance could account for the finite floor in Fig. 2a.
   The devices also use two contact schemes: alpha-RuCl3-doped contacts for 5, 4.8, 4.3, 4.1 and 3.8 degrees, and TaSe2 contacts for 4.2 degrees. The authors should label the configuration for each figure, show that the I-V curves are linear in the insulating regime at the lowest T, and confirm that the gap values do not depend on the choice of contact pair.

7. The theory-experiment comparison in Fig. 4 is qualitative, and the mapping between them is not given. The FRG diagrams (Fig. 4e-h) are plotted against E_z (meV), the experiment against D (V/nm), and the conversion (layer separation, hBN permittivity, screening) is not given. Without it the statement that FRG finds the orders in "approximately the same, experimentally-observed, locations" cannot be checked. Λc is of order 1-10 meV (Fig. 4h colour bar), while the measured Tc is about 0.03 meV. The authors should:
   - say whether the trend of Tc_max with angle in FRG matches Fig. 2b in relative terms;
   - state that U = 3 eV and eps = 14 are held fixed at all angles, and whether they were tuned against any of these data;
   - give the FRG momentum and frequency resolution and the convergence checks;
   - show where FRG puts the SC pocket at 3.8 degrees relative to the HF insulator (Fig. 4h puts both near nu = -1).
   The claim that "a detailed correspondence to theory places the 5-3.8 degree twist angle range in the crossover" (p. 2) is also somewhat circular, because the FRG used is a weak-to-moderate-coupling method.

8. The criteria for the phase boundaries in Fig. 4a-d are not stated. The M1, M2, insulator and SC regions are drawn "based on our low temperature transport measurements," but the resistance threshold, temperature and Hall criterion used for each boundary are not given, and the M1/M2 distinction is deferred to Fig. SI 5. Fig. 4a-d is the main summary of the experiment, so the authors should state the criteria quantitatively. They should also overlay the boundaries on the measured maps, at least in the SI, so readers can judge how well defined they are, especially for the diffuse M2 region.

9. There is no magnetic-field characterization of the superconductor. Neither the perpendicular nor the in-plane critical field is reported at any angle. Hc2(perp) against angle would give the coherence length. That is an independent, measured check on the BCS/crossover placement (compare xi with the interparticle spacing) and on the twist-disorder hypothesis. The in-plane Hc2 compared with the Pauli limit bears directly on the spin structure of the pairing claimed in concern 2. The authors should add at least the perpendicular field dependence for each device.

10. The temperature scale is not well defined at the lowest temperatures. Jc is taken at "T = 20 mK (as measured by the fridge thermometer)" (p. 4), and the dilution-fridge base and electron temperatures are not given. With T_BKT about 0.1 K at 3.8 degrees, Jc there is measured at T/Tc of about 0.2, but at about 0.05 for 5 degrees. So part of the steep fall in Jc may reflect mismatched reduced temperature or electron heating under d.c. bias, not intrinsic behaviour. The authors should give the electron temperature, for example from a Johnson-noise or Coulomb-blockade calibration or a stated estimate. They should also compare Jc at matched T/Tc, or extrapolate to T = 0, and confirm that the coherence-peak positions do not depend on the a.c. excitation amplitude.

11. There is one device per angle, yet the abstract claims "repeatability between different devices." Each angle is a single device, so variation between devices at a fixed angle is unknown. The scatter in Fig. 2b between 4.2 and 4.3 degrees, and between this work's 3.8 degree point and the literature 3.65 degree point, may be device-to-device variation rather than a trend with angle. The authors should either temper the repeatability claim or add a second device at one angle, and say whether any results were checked after thermal cycling.

12. The argument against a Mott insulator is brief. On p. 5 a narrow-in-D insulator that turns on sharply below about 8 K is taken to mean moderate interactions, because a Mott state is expected to persist to D = 0 (Ref. 47). An AFM-driven (Slater-like) gap that opens only where the reconstruction overlaps nu = -1 would behave this way too, but so might a strongly correlated insulator whose charge gap depends on layer polarization. The authors should give the D dependence of the gap at 3.8 degrees (not just at the maximum) and compare it with the HF prediction of the D window, to make the distinction quantitative.

C. Minor

13. The filling sign convention is inconsistent. The text uses "nu = 1" (pp. 3-4, "centered at nu = 1") and "nu = -1" (Figs. 1, 4 and elsewhere). Please use one convention.

14. Signs in Fig. 1e versus Fig. 2a. The 4.2 degree panel of Fig. 1e has a positive density axis (6.0 to 5.0) and the pocket at D of 0.08-0.20 V/nm. The caption of Fig. 2a gives the 4.2 degree max-Tc point at n = -5.53e12 cm^-2, D = -0.12 V/nm. Likewise the 4.8 degree pocket in Fig. 1e is at negative D, while Fig. 2a quotes +0.37 V/nm. Please make the signs consistent, or state that the pockets are symmetric in D.

15. Fig. 2b and the abstract describe the pocket as moving "to lower band filling," but the max-Tc points convert to about nu = -1.09 (5 degrees), -1.01 (4.8), -0.96 (4.2) and -1.01 (3.8). Please give nu for each max-Tc point and qualify the trend.

16. Fig. 2d: please define T_D (Drude weight in temperature units) more explicitly and identify the dashed lines in the Uemura inset. The "1%" threshold for strong coupling (Ref. 45) should be justified for a 2D system.

17. Figs. 3e-f include the 4.1 and 4.3 degree devices, which appear elsewhere only in the SI. Please list all six devices in a table with angle, contacts, dielectric thicknesses, measurement configuration and the figures each appears in.

18. Methods: give the dilution-fridge base temperature and the excitation used for the insulating measurements. "Identification of max Tc" would benefit from a figure, or a description of the line-cut step and resolution.

19. The data are "available upon reasonable request." Please consider depositing the source data for the figures in a public repository.

20. Typographical: "systemically" (p. 2) should be "systematically"; "the the interplay" (p. 6); "tWSe2" in the acknowledgments; "superconductivity ... with a resistance that goes to zero" (p. 7) should be qualified given Fig. 2a.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a compact table and figure that put angle calibration (with uncertainty and contact-pair spread), Tc (one definition), T_BKT, Jc at matched T/Tc, Hc2(perp) and the half-filling gap side by side for all six devices.
2. Move the key magnetotransport evidence for the AFM or IVC character of M1/M2 into the main text. Add an in-plane-field measurement on at least one large-angle and one small-angle device to test the spin-fluctuation pairing picture directly.
3. Make the FRG/HF comparison quantitative: give the E_z-to-D conversion, overlay the measured phase boundaries on the computed diagrams, and compare relative Tc_max (Λc) trends with Fig. 2b.
4. Replace or supplement the band-theory Uemura analysis with experimentally derived stiffness or coherence-length estimates. Reconcile the "BCS limit" and "intermediate coupling" wording across the paper.
