Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript maps the transport phase diagram (n, D, T) of twisted bilayer WSe2 in four main devices (5.0°, 4.8°, 4.2°, 3.8°, plus 4.3° and 4.1° in the SI). It aims to connect the two apparently different superconducting phase diagrams reported at 5° (Ref. 2) and 3.65° (Ref. 1). The central message is that the superconducting pocket always sits on the low-D edge of a Fermi-surface-reconstructed (presumably IVC-AFM) phase. As the angle decreases, the pocket moves with that phase to lower D and toward ν = -1, its Tc falls smoothly, and it detaches from the Van Hove line. FRG and Hartree-Fock calculations reproduce the trends qualitatively. A twist-angle series bridging the two published results is timely and useful to the moiré-superconductivity community. The mechanistic conclusions (spin-fluctuation pairing, one common ground state, BCS limit at all angles), however, go beyond what the transport data presently establish.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- It is the first systematic angle series of tWSe2 superconductivity from one group with a common fabrication and measurement approach. It directly addresses an open question raised by two recent Nature papers.
- The phase diagrams in Fig. 1d,e show clearly that the superconducting pocket tracks the resistive (AFM) region as it moves in (n, D) with angle.
- At 4.2° and below, a fully gapped half-filling state emerges only within a restricted D window and onsets sharply below about 8 K (Fig. 3a,b). This is an informative observation about the nature of the ν = -1 insulator.
- Several superconducting quantities are reported together across angles (mean-field Tc, TBKT, Jc, Tc/TF), with a theory comparison (FRG phase diagrams, Fig. 4e-h; HF gaps, Fig. 3g) that uses one microscopic model.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The spin-fluctuation mechanism and the "same ground state" conclusion rest on adjacency alone, and the AFM identification is indirect. The abstract, the end of "Superconductivity versus twist angle" ("these findings indicate that superconductivity is spin-fluctuation mediated for all twist angles") and the Discussion ("strongly suggests ... mediated by spin fluctuations") draw a mechanism from the observation that the SC pocket borders a resistive region. That region is identified as AFM from longitudinal and Hall magnetotransport (Figs. SI 4, SI 5, which are not part of the submitted PDF) and from the FRG assignment of IVC-AFM order. Proximity to a correlated phase does not identify the pairing glue: phonon- or valley-fluctuation-mediated pairing that is cut off by a competing order would produce the same topology. Similarly, "the large and small twist angle superconducting pockets are smoothly connected and therefore probably represent the same ground state order" is inferred from Tc and pocket location only. No property of the superconductor itself (Hc2 anisotropy, in-plane-field response, gap structure) is compared across angles. The authors should:
   - (a) show the magnetotransport evidence for the AFM phase at each angle in the main text, including how the M1/M2 boundaries in Fig. 4a-d are drawn and what signature (Hall density change, hysteresis, field dependence of the resistance peak) distinguishes them;
   - (b) give a quantitative link between the magnetic phase and the superconductor, for example the onset temperature of the resistive anomaly along the pocket boundary versus Tc, or how both evolve with D through the pocket;
   - (c) compare superconducting properties across angles that bear on "same order": perpendicular Hc2 and the Ginzburg-Landau coherence length, and the in-plane critical field relative to the Pauli limit given Ising spin-orbit coupling;
   - (d) otherwise reword the mechanism and same-order statements as hypotheses consistent with the data.

2. The "BCS limit at all twist angles" conclusion conflicts with the paper's own small-angle data. The Discussion concludes "the superconductivity is in the BCS limit for all twist angles". The evidence is a band-theory superfluid stiffness of 150-350 K and the argument that "renormalizations by factors of more than 10 do not occur without extreme fine tuning". Neither the stiffness nor its renormalization is measured, and no reference supports the second statement. In a 2D superconductor whose stiffness is about 100 times Tc, TBKT should be indistinguishable from the mean-field Tc. Yet Fig. 2b shows TBKT ≈ 0.10 K against Tc ≈ 0.31 K at 3.8°, and Jc collapses to about 1 nA/µm (Fig. 2c) while TBKT is still about 100 mK. These are the signatures expected of a strongly reduced phase stiffness or strong inhomogeneity. The authors attribute them to "twist angle disorder" by conjecture, with no supporting measurement. Fig. 2d likewise uses TF and TD from non-interacting bands. The authors should:
   - extract a lower bound on ρs from TBKT (the Nelson-Kosterlitz relation) at each angle and compare it with the band-theory value;
   - give a coherence length from Hc2 and compare it with the interparticle spacing;
   - either show independent evidence of angle disorder in the 3.8° device (see concern 3), or temper the BCS-limit statement and the abstract's characterization of the crossover.

3. The twist-angle calibration, its uncertainty and device statistics are not given, and the Tc trend is less monotonic than stated. The trend claims in Fig. 2b-d and Fig. 3g are plotted against θ without error bars. The main text and Methods do not say how θ was determined (e.g. from the density of ν = -1 and full filling, together with the gate capacitance and hBN thickness). Each angle is represented by a single device, so the abstract's claim of "repeatability between different devices" is not demonstrated: no angle is measured twice. Moreover, Fig. 2b shows the 3.8° mean-field Tc (≈0.31 K) above both the 4.2° (≈0.28 K) and 4.3° (≈0.30 K) points. This contradicts "decreasing continuously with decreasing twist angle", and the "factor 2 between 5° and 3.5°" relies on the external points from Ref. 1, which uses a different device and perhaps a different Tc criterion. The authors should:
   - state the θ extraction method and give θ uncertainties, and the spread of θ between contact pairs in each device (θ inhomogeneity);
   - report Tc at more than one contact pair per device;
   - describe the Tc trend accurately (including the 3.8° outlier), or explain the outlier with data.

B. Should address

4. The half-filling gap versus angle is described inaccurately, and the Arrhenius extraction is fragile. The text states that the calculated gaps "consistently increase with decreasing twist angle, in agreement with experiment". The experimental points in Fig. 3g are non-monotonic: about 6.3 meV at 4.1°, about 4.7 meV at 4.2°, about 3.9 meV at 4.3°, and about 3.3 meV at 3.8°. The smallest-angle device thus has the smallest finite gap. The fits in Fig. 3f use a narrow window (1/T ≈ 0.1-0.3 K⁻¹, i.e. about 3-10 K) directly below an insulating onset at about 8 K, where an order-parameter gap is still temperature dependent. The extracted Δ of 3-6 meV (35-70 K) is also large relative to that 8 K onset temperature for a Slater-type AFM gap. In addition:
   - Fig. 3g is shown only for D > 0, and the 4.8°/5.0° values are set to zero by hand;
   - the HF curves use ε = 24-30.6 with ε_HF = 2.2 ε_FRG, so the comparison involves a tuned parameter.

   The authors should:
   - correct the text;
   - show the fit ranges and the sensitivity of Δ to them;
   - report D < 0 gaps as a check;
   - discuss the ratio of Δ to the onset temperature in the context of their weak-coupling (Stoner/Slater) interpretation.

5. Zero resistance and the measurement configuration of the superconducting data are not clear. In Fig. 2a the 4.2° curve saturates at R/Rn ≈ 0.07 rather than zero, and the 3.8° curve is noisy and fluctuates around zero. Methods state that "four and two terminal" measurements were used, without saying which applies to Figs. 1e, 2 and 3. The 4.2° device also uses TaSe2 contacts, whereas the others use α-RuCl3-doped graphite contacts. A finite residual could come from contact resistance in a two-terminal geometry, from a non-percolating superconductor or from a probe that crosses a non-superconducting region. Any of these would affect the Tc, TBKT and Jc comparisons. The authors should:
   - state the configuration for every superconducting dataset;
   - explain the 4.2° residual;
   - discuss whether the different contact schemes (including possible doping or stray-field effects of α-RuCl3) could affect the superconducting or magnetic regions.

6. The claim that superconductivity is "not tied to the Van Hove singularity" needs the VHs located experimentally. The abstract and Discussion state that at small angles the SC pocket is "fully disconnected from the VHs" (supported by Fig. SI 6, not in the submission). In Fig. 4a-d the R_VHS line is drawn without saying whether it comes from measured Hall sign changes or from the band model, and at 4.2° it still passes close to the SC pocket. The authors should:
   - show the measured Hall-sign-change (or other) locus of the VHs in the main figure at each angle;
   - quantify the separation between it and the SC pocket in n or ν;
   - account for the possibility that the AFM reconstruction shifts the VHs.

7. The FRG and HF comparison is qualitative and its parameter choices need justification. Fig. 4e-h is plotted against Ez in meV, whereas the data are in D (V/nm), and the conversion (layer separation, hBN or WSe2 dielectric constants) is not given. The ν axes of Fig. 4a-d and Fig. 4e-h also span different ranges, which makes visual comparison hard. The FRG uses ε = 14 and U = 3 eV without justification of their angle independence. Λc is a proxy for the instability scale and is not converted to a Tc, so the "decreasing transition temperature" in the calculation cannot be compared with Fig. 2b. A one-loop static TUFRG is a weak-coupling method, so its reliability at 3.8°, where the authors themselves argue correlations are stronger, should be discussed. The authors should:
   - plot theory and experiment on common axes;
   - give the D-to-Ez conversion;
   - show how robust the SC/IVC-AFM boundaries are to ε and U;
   - compare predicted Λc ratios between angles with the measured Tc ratios.

8. Tc/TF, Tc/TD and the Uemura plot are built from non-interacting quantities. In Fig. 2d, TF is defined as the chemical-potential distance from the band maximum in the non-interacting Wannier model. At the relevant densities, however, the Fermi surface is reconstructed by the AFM order that the paper emphasizes. A conventional Uemura plot uses measured ρs or carrier density over effective mass, not a computed band filling, so the inset's placement relative to "known unconventional SC" is not like-for-like. The authors should:
   - state this clearly;
   - give the Hall-derived carrier density at the Tc maximum;
   - where possible, compute TF from it.

9. Central evidence lies in supplementary figures that are not included. The AFM identification at 4.2° and 3.8° (SI 5), the magnetotransport at 5.0° and 4.8° (SI 4), the VHs separation (SI 6), the 4.3° and 4.1° devices (SI 2, 3), device images (SI 1), the "failed SC state" (SI 4, 5) and the dielectric sensitivity of HF (SI 9) are all referenced but absent from the PDF. As submitted, several key claims cannot be assessed. The supplementary material should be provided, and the most load-bearing parts (items a-c of concern 1) moved into the main text.

C. Minor

10. Signs and values differ between Fig. 1e and the Fig. 2a caption:
    - the Fig. 2a caption gives the 4.2° optimum at n = -5.53×10¹² cm⁻², D = -0.12 V/nm, whereas the 4.2° panel of Fig. 1e spans D = 0.08-0.20 V/nm with positive density labels (6.0-5.0);
    - the 4.8° optimum is quoted at D = +0.37 V/nm, whereas the Fig. 1e map is at D ≈ -0.25 to -0.45 V/nm.

    Please make these consistent, or state which pocket (D > 0 or D < 0) is used in each figure.

11. Fig. 2c:
    - Jc is a current density, so the channel width of each device should be stated;
    - "coherence peaks" of dV/dI is non-standard wording for the critical-current peaks;
    - only the 4.3° trace is labelled in the inset;
    - the measurements are at "20 mK (as measured by the fridge thermometer)", so an estimate of electron temperature is needed, since Jc at 3.8° is compared with a TBKT of about 100 mK.

12. Fig. 2b shows two open triangles attributed to Ref. 1 (near 3.45° and 3.65°). The introduction mentions only 3.65°, and the text quotes "3.5°". Please identify both points and confirm that the same 80%-of-Rn criterion was applied to the literature data.

13. TBKT is defined by V ∝ I³, but only the 4.8° I-V curve is shown (Fig. 2a inset). For the broad 3.8° transition, please show the I-V curves and the exponent versus T. Heating and inhomogeneity can also produce power laws.

14. The filling sign is used inconsistently: "ν = 1" appears for half filling in the Introduction and "Half-band insulator" sections, and "ν = -1" elsewhere. Please use one convention.

15. The Methods on "Identification of max Tc" should state the density and displacement-field step sizes of the "fine line sweeps", since the Tc trend depends on locating the maximum.

16. Typos and wording: "systemically" (should be "systematically"); "the the"; "see Method" (should be "see Methods"); "the Fermi surface becomes fully gapped" when only a resistance increase with an activated fit is shown (could be "activated transport").

17. A data availability statement of "upon reasonable request" is weak for a dataset intended as a reference phase diagram. Depositing the maps and R(T) curves would help.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a main-text figure that shows, for each angle, the measured Hall density (or Hall sign change), the AFM boundaries and the SC pocket on common (ν, D) axes. Overlay the FRG results converted to the same axes. This would make the "SC at the AFM edge, detached from the VHs" statement directly visible.
2. Measure perpendicular and in-plane critical fields at the Tc maximum of every device. Coherence length versus angle, and Pauli-limit violation versus angle, would test both the BCS-limit statement and the "same order" statement far more directly than Tc alone.
3. Use TBKT to bound the superfluid stiffness and compare it with the band-theory ρs. Measuring a second device near 3.8° would show whether the widening gap between Tc and TBKT is intrinsic or comes from disorder.
4. Present the half-filling gap versus angle honestly as non-monotonic. Add fits for D < 0 and an analysis of Δ against the onset temperature, which would sharpen the argument that the ν = -1 insulator is an AFM (Slater-like) gap rather than a Mott gap.
