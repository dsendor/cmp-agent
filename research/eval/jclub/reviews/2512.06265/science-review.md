## A. Must address

1. **The spin-fluctuation mechanism and the "same ground state" conclusion rest on adjacency alone, and the AFM identification itself is indirect.** The abstract, the end of "Superconductivity versus twist angle" (p. 4: "these findings indicate that superconductivity is spin-fluctuation mediated for all twist angles") and the Discussion (p. 7: "strongly suggests ... mediated by spin fluctuations") infer the pairing mechanism only from the superconducting pocket bordering a resistive region. That region's magnetic character is itself inferred ("presumably antiferromagnetic", abstract) from longitudinal and Hall magnetotransport in Figs. SI 4-5, which the main text only cites and which are not in the submitted PDF, and from the FRG assignment of IVC-AFM order. Nothing in the main paper shows that the M1/M2 regions are magnetic rather than, for example, a valley-polarized or charge-ordered reconstruction. Adjacency does not identify the pairing glue: phonon-, valley-fluctuation-mediated or other conventional pairing confined to the high-DOS edge of a symmetry-broken phase, or competing with it, would give the same topology. At 3.8 and 4.2 degrees the pocket also borders both the AFM region and the fully gapped nu = -1 insulator (Fig. 1e, Fig. 4c,d), so the data cannot separate "adjacent to AFM" from "adjacent to a doped correlated insulator", the interpretation proposed for the 3.65 degree device; the abstract's statement that superconductivity is "neither necessarily tied ... to the half band insulator" needs a device or regime where the two boundaries are separated. Likewise, "the large and small twist angle superconducting pockets are smoothly connected and therefore probably represent the same ground state order" (p. 4) is inferred from Tc and pocket location only; no property of the superconductor itself (Hc2 anisotropy, in-plane-field response, gap structure) is compared across angles. The authors should: (a) move the magnetotransport evidence for the AFM phase at each angle into the main text, stating what signature (Hall density change, hysteresis, field dependence of the resistance peak) distinguishes M1 from M2; (b) give a quantitative link between magnetism and superconductivity, for example how Tc scales with distance from the AFM boundary along n and along D, whether Tc correlates with the AFM onset temperature across angles, the onset temperature of the resistive anomaly along the pocket boundary versus Tc, or how the pocket responds to a perpendicular field that suppresses the AFM state; (c) compare superconducting properties bearing on "same order" across angles, in particular the in-plane critical field relative to the Pauli limit in this Ising/spin-valley-locked system (see concern 8); (d) report the FRG pairing symmetry and an analysis of the FRG vertex showing whether the SC channel is driven by the density-wave fluctuations. Otherwise, the mechanism and same-order statements should be reworded as hypotheses "consistent with" the data, removing "indicate" from the Results and Discussion.

2. **The "BCS limit for all twist angles" conclusion rests on band-theory quantities and conflicts with the paper's own small-angle data.** The Discussion (p. 7) concludes "the superconductivity is in the BCS limit for all twist angles", using a band-theory superfluid stiffness of 350 K to 150 K (about 10^3 x Tc) and the unreferenced argument that renormalizations "by factors of more than 10 do not occur without extreme fine tuning". Neither the stiffness nor its renormalization is measured, and measured stiffness in flat-band moiré superconductors can be far below band estimates. The data point the other way. In Fig. 2b, T_BKT/Tc_MF falls steadily with decreasing angle (about 0.39/0.445 at 5 degrees, about 0.21/0.28 at 4.2 degrees, about 0.10/0.31 at 3.8 degrees); if rho_s were about 10^3 Tc, T_BKT would sit essentially at the mean-field Tc, so a large gap implies a stiffness of order Tc, i.e. strong phase fluctuations. In Fig. 2c, Jc falls about 15-fold (to about 1 nA/um at 3.8 degrees) while the band rho_s falls only about 2.3-fold, and a rough depairing estimate Jc ~ (2e/hbar) rho_s/xi with rho_s about 150 K and xi of tens of nm gives about 10^2 uA/um, four orders of magnitude above the measured 1-16 nA/um. Either the measured Jc and T_BKT are dominated by inhomogeneity, which undermines their use as smooth intrinsic angle-dependent quantities, or the real stiffness is far below the band value, which undermines the BCS-limit statement. The authors attribute the 3.8 degree behaviour to "twist angle disorder" by conjecture only. There is also internal tension: p. 4 reads the upward trend of Tc/T_F as evolution toward stronger correlation, p. 5 places 3.8 degrees in an "intermediate regime of moderate interactions", and p. 7 concludes "BCS limit for all twist angles"; the abstract's characterization of the crossover is affected too. The authors should: explain how rho_s is computed (including whether the quantum-geometric contribution is included) and why a value is quoted at 3.65 degrees, an angle not measured here; extract a lower bound on rho_s from T_BKT via the Nelson-Kosterlitz relation at each angle, and from Jc with an Hc2-derived xi, and compare these with the band value; give a coherence length from Hc2 and compare it with the interparticle spacing (see concern 8); place experimentally derived values on the Uemura plot of the Fig. 2d inset, whose current placement uses a non-interacting band T_F and cannot settle the question; either show independent evidence of twist disorder in the 3.8 degree device (see concern 3) or temper the BCS-limit statement; remove or justify the fine-tuning assertion; and reconcile the wording across pp. 4, 5 and 7.

3. **The twist-angle determination, its uncertainty and its homogeneity are not described.** Every conclusion is a trend against theta (Figs. 1-4), and the claimed "smooth" and "continuous" evolution rests on adjacent points only 0.1-0.4 degrees apart (4.1, 4.2, 4.3 degrees are distinct points in Figs. 2b,c and 3g). Yet neither the Methods nor the main text says how theta = 5.0, 4.8, 4.3, 4.2, 4.1, 3.8 degrees were obtained: from the nu = -1 or full-filling density with the gate capacitance and hBN thickness, from Landau-fan or Brown-Zak oscillations, or from the target angle at stacking. No uncertainties are given, there are no horizontal error bars in Figs. 2b-d and 3g, and nothing is said about angle inhomogeneity between contact pairs, even though the text itself invokes "twist angle disorder in the 3.8 degree device" (p. 4) to explain the broad transition and the large Tc-T_BKT gap. The authors should state the calibration procedure for each device; give theta and its uncertainty, ideally from several contact pairs so the spatial spread is quantified; add horizontal error bars to Figs. 2b-d and 3g; and test the twist-disorder conjecture at 3.8 degrees by comparing the nu = -1 density, or the superconducting pocket position, across contact pairs.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

4. **The Tc-versus-angle trend is less monotonic than stated, depends on an unusual Tc criterion, and mixes in literature data defined differently.** The text says Tc is "decreasing continuously with decreasing twist angle, and dropping by approximately a factor 2 between 5 and 3.5 degrees", but in Fig. 2b the 3.8 degree mean-field Tc (about 0.31 K) is above both the 4.2 degree (about 0.28 K) and 4.3 degree (about 0.30 K) points. Tc is defined at 80% of R_n, which for the broad, noisy 3.8 degree transition in Fig. 2a inflates Tc relative to a 50% or zero-resistance criterion and produces this outlier. The "factor 2" relies on the open symbols from Refs. [1] and [28], which may use different Tc criteria and different devices. Fig. 2b shows two open triangles attributed to Ref. 1 (near 3.45 and 3.65 degrees), whereas Ref. 1 reports 3.65 degrees, the introduction mentions only 3.65 degrees and the text quotes "3.5 degrees". The authors should use one Tc definition throughout (and replot Fig. 2b with, for example, 50% and 10% criteria), apply it to the literature points where possible or mark them as differently defined, identify which angles the two Ref. 1 triangles correspond to, and describe the trend accurately, including the 3.8 degree outlier, or explain the outlier with data. Since T_BKT at 3.8 degrees is only a third of the mean-field Tc, the 3.8 degree point should be treated as qualitatively different until the twist disorder is quantified (concern 3).

## B. Should address

5. **Zero resistance, the measurement configuration of the superconducting data, and the small-angle superconducting characterization.** In Fig. 2a the 4.2 degree curve saturates at R/R_n of about 0.07-0.08 rather than zero, and the 3.8 degree curve is noisy around zero with negative excursions; yet the Discussion (p. 7) speaks of superconductivity "with a resistance that goes to zero at base temperature", and the text describes low-angle superconductivity as "fragile ... often manifests as a marginal or failed SC state". The Methods mention both four- and two-terminal measurements without saying which applies to Figs. 1e, 2 and 3. The 4.2 degree device alone uses TaSe2 contacts; the others use alpha-RuCl3-doped graphite contacts. A finite residual could come from series contact resistance in a two-terminal geometry, a non-percolating superconductor, or a probe path crossing a non-superconducting region, and any of these would affect the Tc, T_BKT and Jc comparisons. R(T) and I-V data for the 4.3 and 4.1 degree devices are not shown. The authors should state the configuration for every superconducting dataset; show zero resistance within the noise floor for every device using four-terminal data; explain the 4.2 degree residual; give R(T) and I-V data for the 4.3 and 4.1 degree devices; discuss whether the different contact schemes (including possible doping or stray-field effects of alpha-RuCl3) could affect the superconducting or magnetic regions; and qualify the "goes to zero" statement.

6. **One device per angle, so the claimed "repeatability between different devices" is not demonstrated.** Each angle is represented by a single device, so device-to-device variation at fixed angle cannot be separated from the theta dependence. The scatter in Fig. 2b between 4.2 and 4.3 degrees, and between this work's 3.8 degree point and the literature 3.65 degree point, as well as the non-monotonic gaps in Fig. 3g, may reflect device variation rather than a trend with angle. The authors should either temper the repeatability claim and the phrase "remarkably smooth evolution", or add a second device at one angle (ideally near 3.8 degrees, to test whether the widening Tc-T_BKT gap is intrinsic); report Tc at more than one contact pair per device; use the +D and -D superconducting pockets visible in each device (Fig. 1d) as an internal reproducibility check, stating whether they have the same Tc; and say whether any results were checked after thermal cycling.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

7. **The half-filling gap versus angle is described inaccurately, the HF comparison depends on a tuned dielectric constant, and the Arrhenius extraction is fragile.** The text (p. 5) states that the calculated gaps "consistently increase with decreasing twist angle, in agreement with experiment". The measured gaps in Fig. 3g are non-monotonic: about 3.3-3.4 meV at 3.8 degrees, about 6.3 at 4.1, about 4.7 at 4.2 and about 3.9 at 4.3, so the gap peaks at 4.1 degrees and the smallest-angle device has the smallest finite gap. On the theory side, the Methods fix eps_HF = 2.2 eps_FRG = 30.8 (for eps_FRG = 14), which corresponds to the orange curve of Fig. 3g; that curve gives Delta of about 0 at 4.2 degrees and only about 2 meV at 3.8 degrees, below all measured values, and predicts no gap at 4.2-4.3 degrees, where gaps are measured. Fig. 3g scans eps = 24-30.6; for eps = 24 HF gives about 4.9 meV at 3.8 degrees, above experiment, while it underestimates experiment at 4.1-4.3 degrees. The HF calculation also removes the symmetric Hartree renormalization. On the experimental side, the fits in Fig. 3f use a narrow window (1/T of about 0.1-0.3 K^-1, i.e. about 3-10 K) directly below an insulating onset near 8 K, where an order-parameter gap is still temperature dependent, and some curves visibly saturate; the extracted Delta of 3-6 meV (35-70 K) is large relative to the 8 K onset for a Slater-type AFM gap. Fig. 3g is shown only for D > 0, and the 4.8 and 5.0 degree values are set to zero by hand. The activation convention (sigma ~ exp(-Delta/2k_BT)) must match the HF quantity compared (single-particle gap versus charge gap). The authors should correct the text and describe the agreement as qualitative (gap opening below about 4.4 degrees); discuss the non-monotonic behaviour, which may reflect twist disorder at 3.8 degrees (concern 3) or the choice of D; state which eps is the principled model prediction, why the other curves are shown, and how far the agreement depends on that choice; state the fit window, the D used for each device and how the error bars were obtained, and show the sensitivity of Delta to the fit range; show the 3.8 degree fit and give it an error bar; report D < 0 gaps as a check; and discuss the ratio of Delta to the onset temperature in the context of the weak-coupling (Stoner/Slater) interpretation.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **There is no magnetic-field characterization of the superconductor.** Neither the perpendicular nor the in-plane critical field is reported at any angle. Perpendicular Hc2 versus angle would give the Ginzburg-Landau coherence length, an independent, measured check on the BCS/crossover placement (xi compared with the interparticle spacing; concern 2), on the stiffness estimate from Jc, and on the twist-disorder hypothesis. The in-plane critical field compared with the Pauli limit, given Ising spin-orbit coupling, bears directly on the spin structure of the pairing and on the spin-fluctuation and "same order" claims (concern 1). The authors should add at least the perpendicular field dependence at the Tc maximum of each device, and the in-plane field dependence for at least one large-angle and one small-angle device.

9. **The criteria for the phase boundaries in Fig. 4a-d are not stated.** The M1, M2, insulator and superconducting regions are drawn "based on our low temperature transport measurements", but the resistance threshold, temperature and Hall criterion used for each boundary are not given, and the M1/M2 distinction is deferred to Fig. SI 5. Fig. 4a-d is the main summary of the experiment and every phase-diagram claim depends on these boundaries. The authors should state the criteria quantitatively, show representative data in the paper, and overlay the boundaries on the measured maps (at least in the SI) so readers can judge how well defined they are, especially for the diffuse M2 region.

10. **The claim that superconductivity is "fully disconnected from the VHs" at small angles needs an experimentally located Van Hove singularity.** The abstract and Discussion make this claim, supported by Fig. SI 6, which is not in the submission. In Fig. 4a-d the R_VHS line at 4.2 and 3.8 degrees is drawn without saying whether it comes from a measured Hall-density sign change (as at 5 degrees) or from the band model, and at 4.2 degrees it still passes close to the pocket. Interactions and the AFM reconstruction can shift the experimental VHs substantially. There is also a tension with the theory section, which attributes the shrinking pockets and falling Tc to "the decrease of the DOS away from the VH line", i.e. in FRG superconductivity is still controlled by VHs proximity. The authors should show the measured Hall-sign-change (or other) locus of the VHs at each angle in the main figure, quantify its separation from the SC pocket in n or nu, account for a possible AFM-induced shift, and reconcile the experimental "not tied to the VHs" statement with the FRG explanation.
Raised by: r2, r3

11. **The FRG and HF comparison with experiment is qualitative, and the mapping and parameter choices are not given.** Fig. 4e-h is plotted against E_z (meV) while the experiment uses D (V/nm); the conversion (layer separation, hBN/WSe2 permittivity, layer-polarisability/screening) is not given, so the claim that FRG finds the orders in "approximately the same, experimentally-observed, locations" cannot be checked. The nu axes of Fig. 4a-d and 4e-h also span different ranges. FRG uses eps = 14 and U = 3 eV, with no independent justification of eps = 14 for hBN gate dielectrics or of angle independence, while HF uses eps of about 24-31. Lambda_c (of order 1-10 meV, Fig. 4h colour bar) is a proxy for the instability scale, not a Tc (the measured Tc is about 0.03 meV), so the "decreasing transition temperature" in the calculation cannot be compared with Fig. 2b. Static, one-loop truncated-unity FRG with a sharp cutoff is a weak-to-moderate-coupling method that does not produce the nu = -1 insulator, so the statement that "a detailed correspondence to theory places the 5-3.8 degree twist angle range in the crossover" (p. 2) is somewhat circular, and the method's reliability at 3.8 degrees, where the authors argue correlations are stronger, should be discussed. The authors should give the E_z-to-D conversion and plot theory and experiment on common axes, overlaying the experimental pocket outlines and phase boundaries on the FRG maps; state that U and eps are held fixed at all angles and whether they were tuned against these data; show the robustness of the SC/IVC-AFM boundaries to eps and U; compare Lambda_c ratios (Tc_max trend) between angles with the measured Tc ratios on a common plot; give the FRG momentum and frequency resolution and convergence checks; show where FRG places the SC pocket at 3.8 degrees relative to the HF insulator (Fig. 4h puts both near nu = -1); and use a single, justified dielectric constant (or a stated range) for both FRG and HF.

12. **Contact resistance could contribute to the insulating behaviour.** The Methods mention two- and four-terminal measurements but the figures do not say which is which, and TMD contacts often become non-ohmic, gate-dependent and more resistive at low temperature. At 3.8 degrees the nu = -1 resistance reaches about 10^6 ohm (Fig. 3a), and contact freeze-out could add to the apparent activation and to the "metal-insulator transition" near 8 K at |D| of about 0.1-0.3 V/nm (Fig. 3b). The half-band insulator result and the HF gap comparison are central, so the authors should label the configuration for each figure; show that the I-V curves are linear in the insulating regime at the lowest T; show that the four-terminal resistance agrees with the two-terminal resistance minus the contact resistance, or show the contact resistance versus D and T; and confirm that the gap values do not depend on the choice of contact pair.

13. **The low-temperature scale, excitation and Jc normalization are not well defined.** Jc is taken from dV/dI peak positions at "T = 20 mK (as measured by the fridge thermometer)" (p. 4); the base and electron temperatures, filtering and calibration are not given. With T_BKT about 0.1 K at 3.8 degrees, Jc there is measured at T/Tc of about 0.2, versus about 0.05 at 5 degrees, so part of the steep fall in Jc may reflect mismatched reduced temperature or electron heating under d.c. bias. The Methods state 1-5 nA bias for superconducting characterization, and Jc at 3.8 degrees is about 1 nA/um with the channel width not given, so the critical current may be comparable to or below the excitation; the broad 3.8 degree transition and the Tc-T_BKT gap could then be partly current-induced. The authors should give the electron temperature (for example from Johnson-noise or Coulomb-blockade calibration, or a stated estimate) and the dilution-fridge base temperature; give the device widths used to compute Jc and the excitation used for each curve in Fig. 2a and for the insulating measurements; show R(T) at reduced excitation for 3.8 degrees; compare Jc at matched T/Tc or extrapolated to T = 0; and confirm that the peak positions do not depend on the a.c. excitation amplitude.

14. **Tc/T_F, Tc/T_D and the Uemura plot are built from non-interacting band quantities.** In Fig. 2d, T_F is the chemical-potential distance from the valence-band maximum in the non-interacting three-orbital Wannier model and T_D is the band Drude weight, but at the relevant densities the Fermi surface is reconstructed by the AFM order the paper emphasizes, so the real Fermi energy may differ substantially. A conventional Uemura plot uses a measured rho_s or carrier density over effective mass, so the inset's placement relative to "known unconventional SC" is not like-for-like. The "1%" threshold for strong coupling (Ref. 45) is presented as a convention without justification for a 2D system. The authors should state this limitation clearly and present the Tc/T_F trend as model-dependent; state the n and D at which T_F and T_D are evaluated and test their sensitivity to model parameters; give the Hall-derived carrier density at the Tc maximum and, where possible, compute T_F from it or from quantum-oscillation frequencies; define T_D (Drude weight in temperature units) explicitly; identify the dashed lines in the Uemura inset; and justify the 1% threshold.

16. **The argument against a Mott insulator is brief.** On p. 5, a narrow-in-D insulator that turns on sharply below about 8 K is taken to indicate moderate interactions, because a Mott state is expected to persist to D = 0 (Ref. 47). An AFM-driven (Slater-like) gap that opens only where the reconstruction overlaps nu = -1 would behave this way, but so might a strongly correlated insulator whose charge gap depends on layer polarization. The authors should give the D dependence of the gap at 3.8 degrees (not only at its maximum) and compare it with the HF-predicted D window, to make the distinction quantitative.
