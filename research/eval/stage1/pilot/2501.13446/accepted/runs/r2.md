Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript combines SQUID magnetometry in a diamond anvil cell, single-crystal and high-resolution X-ray diffraction, Raman spectroscopy, DFT and atomistic spin simulations to study CrBr3 under pressure. It reports that ferromagnetism is suppressed at about 5.8 GPa, far below the 8.4 GPa extrapolated from earlier work, and that a trigonal P-3m1 average structure (one Cr site at 2/3 occupancy) coexists with R-3 at ambient pressure and takes over under pressure. The authors attribute the loss of ferromagnetism to a growing fraction of "AA"-stacked layers with antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling. Direct magnetization data on CrBr3 up to and beyond the critical pressure are new and useful. The structural and mechanistic claims, however, depend on several identifications and modelling choices that the data do not yet establish.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The first DC magnetization measurement of CrBr3 through complete suppression of the ferromagnetic signal (Fig. 1, Fig. S1). It includes a second run in a redesigned cell and a decompression test showing that Tc and Msat recover (Fig. S1b).
- Single-crystal diffraction to 21.5 GPa with refined phase fractions and lattice parameters (Table S4). The data show that the "1/3" reflections characteristic of R-3 fade with pressure and stay absent after decompression (Fig. 3).
- A useful methodological warning: the P-3m1 and R-3 powder patterns are practically indistinguishable (Fig. S3), and cutting or mounting crystals changes the stacking (Methods, Fig. 4c–h). This bears on much earlier powder work on CrX3.
- Extensive documentation of how the pressure medium affects sample integrity (Figs. S9–S11).
- A combined DFT plus Monte Carlo framework (Fig. 6, Fig. S8) that could become quantitative if the concerns below are addressed.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The "AA stacking" that drives the magnetic model is not shown by the diffraction data, and the refined structure points to a different kind of disorder. The P-3m1 phase is refined in a 3.64 Å cell (a_R-3/√3) with a single Cr site (1a) at 2/3 occupancy (Table S3). This is the average structure expected when honeycomb layers sit at random among the three possible lateral positions, so the √3 honeycomb ordering in the plane is lost on averaging. True AA stacking, with honeycomb directly above honeycomb, would give an ordered one-layer cell (for example P-31m, Cr fully occupying 2c, 1a empty). That cell keeps sharp h−k≠3n reflections at integer L. The authors concede on p. 6 that they "are unable to distinguish" randomized occupancies from randomized A/B/C stacking. Yet p. 10–12 and the Conclusions treat the high-pressure phase as AA-stacked, and the DFT J_L(AA) in Fig. 6a is computed for an ideal AA bilayer. The magnetic mechanism depends on the local layer registry, so this gap is central. The authors should:
   - refine an ordered AA model (P-31m or equivalent) against the high-pressure data and compare it with the disordered P-3m1 model;
   - analyse the intensity distribution along the 10L and 11L rods (diffuse streaks versus sharp peaks) at 0, 6.4, 8.4 GPa and after decompression;
   - otherwise, compute J_L for the stacking registries actually compatible with the data (random A/B/C shifts, AB, and so on) and drop the AA label.

2. The Monte Carlo collapse of Tc may be an artefact of how the stacking disorder is placed and of what quantity is called Tc.
   - Mattis argument. Stacking changes occur between whole layers. A random sequence of FM and AFM interlayer bonds along c, with fully FM layers, is unfrustrated: a gauge transformation (flipping every spin in a layer) maps it onto the all-FM chain. The ordering temperature should therefore be essentially unchanged; only the net moment is compensated.
   - What the model actually does. The text says that "atoms from these phases are randomly distributed" (p. 11). Random signs at the level of individual atoms create in-plane frustration that whole-layer stacking cannot produce.
   - Which order parameter. If Tc in Fig. 6b and Fig. S8 is taken from the uniform magnetization or uniform susceptibility, a compensated layered order will look like "Tc = 0" even though the system is ordered.
   - Simulation cell versus block length. The cell is 10 layers (20×20×1 unit cells), while the paracrystal analysis gives blocks of 10 ± 2 nm, about 16 layers. That is longer than the whole simulation cell.
   - Bulk versus monolayer. The authors note (p. 12) that even a monolayer orders at about 70% of bulk Tc. It is hard to see how a change in interlayer sign alone can drive the ordering temperature to zero when J1 ≈ 1.4–2 meV.

   The authors should:
   - simulate layer-wise disorder, with block lengths taken from the paracrystal analysis, in cells thicker than one block;
   - report the staggered (layer-resolved) order parameter and the specific heat alongside the uniform magnetization;
   - state explicitly whether the predicted state above pc is ordered (a compensated AFM) or disordered. If it is ordered, the claim becomes "loss of net moment" rather than "collapse of magnetic order", which is a different statement.

3. The magnetization data above pc conflict with the authors' picture of intact Cr moments coupled by weak interlayer AFM. Msat is defined as M at 1 T and 2 K (Fig. 1), and it falls to zero at 6.24 GPa. The calculated |J_L(AA)| is at most about 0.25 meV per Cr even at 8 GPa (Fig. 6a). In a mixture where only some interfaces are AFM, a field of 1 T along the easy axis should flip much of the compensated structure. Even a fully A-type arrangement would show a large linear susceptibility and a metamagnetic step at a few tesla. Either way, M(1 T) should stay a sizeable fraction of 3 μB/Cr, not zero. Already at 1.92 GPa, Msat is about 2 μB/f.u. while Tc is nearly unchanged (Fig. 1 inset a). A fully saturated ferromagnet with intact moments should not show this; it suggests either a calibration or background issue or a non-ferromagnetic fraction that the stacking model does not explain. The authors should:
   - show M(H) up to the highest available field (7 T) at 2 K above pc;
   - show M(T) at 0.1–1 T above pc to look for an AFM cusp and a Curie–Weiss regime that would confirm local moments survive;
   - show the raw sample signal against the empty-cell background at each pressure, and state how the sample mass and the μB/f.u. normalization were fixed inside the DAC.

4. The quantitative link between structure and magnetism is not established, and the agreement in Fig. 6b may be built in.
   - Temperature mismatch. Phase fractions come from room-temperature diffraction on one crystal. The magnetism is measured at 2–35 K on different crystals whose initial P-3m1 fraction is unknown. The paper itself says this fraction varies with handling.
   - Not enough AA. Table S4 gives the AA fraction at 5.7–6.4 GPa as 1 − (0.50–0.42) = 0.50–0.58. The authors describe this as an upper bound, because the R-3 fraction is a lower bound (p. 8). Fig. S8, however, needs about 73% AA for Tc to vanish.
   - Undefined fitting curve. The "stacking variation model" uses an AA fraction that rises linearly "up to a critical pressure" (p. 12). Its slope and end point are not given; if they were chosen to reproduce pc, the agreement is by construction. It is also unclear whether Fig. 6b uses pressure-dependent J values or the fixed 5 GPa values of Fig. S8.

   The authors should:
   - feed the measured fractions from Table S4, with their uncertainties, into the simulation and state every free parameter;
   - ideally, measure the phase fraction at low temperature under pressure, or at least measure magnetization and diffraction on crystals from the same batch prepared the same way.

B. Should address

5. The decompression test does not discriminate between scenarios, and the stated explanation contradicts the diffraction. Fig. S1b shows full recovery of Tc and Msat after 10 GPa. The Conclusions explain this as "P-3m1 was not yet stabilized at 10 GPa while some volume fraction of R-3 persisted". However, the paper reports R-3 as undetectable above 8.4 GPa (p. 5, Table S4). In addition, Fig. 6a gives J_L(AA) slightly positive (FM) at ambient pressure. A recovered P-3m1/AA crystal would then be ferromagnetic anyway, so recovery says nothing about which phase is present. The authors should:
   - measure ambient-pressure M(T) and M(H) on the crystal recovered from 21.5 GPa (single-phase P-3m1 per Table S4);
   - measure diffraction on a crystal recovered from about 10 GPa.

   These two tests would show directly whether the magnetic suppression follows the stacking.

6. The phase fractions do not support the statement that R-3 is "undetectable above 8.4 GPa". Table S4 lists R-3 fractions of −0.1(7) at 8.4 GPa, 0.08(3) at 11.8 GPa and −0.03(2) at 21.5 GPa. A value of −0.1 ± 0.7 does not constrain the fraction, and 0.08(3) is non-zero at about 2.7σ. The Methods say the best sample started with about 80% R-3, but Table S4 gives 0.60(5) at 0 GPa. In the P-3m1 block the value is printed as 0.6(5). The authors should:
   - constrain the fractions to physical values;
   - explain the large uncertainty at 8.4 GPa;
   - reconcile the 80% and 60% figures;
   - say how many high-pressure diffraction runs were made and whether the vanishing pressure of R-3 is reproducible.

7. The lattice parameters in Table S4 and Fig. 2e contain anomalies that call the pressure calibration and the phase decomposition into question.
   - For R-3, c increases from 18.342(2) Å at ambient to 19.0(2) Å at 0.7 GPa, which is a 3.6% expansion under compression.
   - a drops by 1.9% between 0 and 0.7 GPa, then does not change between 0.7 and 3 GPa (6.189 versus 6.1897 Å).
   - At 0.7 GPa, the R-3 interlayer spacing (c/3 = 6.33 Å) and the P-3m1 spacing (c = 6.10 Å) in the same crystal differ by 4%. This contradicts the Conclusions' statement that "the layers are ideally spaced along the c-axis, irrespective of structure", which rests on the ambient reciprocal-space maps.

   The authors should explain these points, fit an equation of state, and compare the compressibilities with the DFT values (Fig. 2e) and with published data [12, 40].

8. Hydrostaticity and shear may drive the structural change. The methanol–ethanol–water medium is hydrostatic only to about 10.5 GPa (SI p. 9), yet the claim that P-3m1 becomes stable rests on points at 11.8–21.5 GPa and on decompression from 21.5 GPa. The paper also shows that cutting and bending create P-3m1 (Methods; Fig. 4c–h). Non-hydrostatic stress is therefore a plausible driver of both the growth of P-3m1 and its retention on decompression. The change of Raman slopes "around 10 GPa" (Fig. 5) coincides with the solidification of the medium, and Fig. 6 of [40] reports 15 GPa. The authors should discuss this. A diffraction run with helium or neon as the medium, or measurements of ruby line widths and pressure gradients at each point, would separate pressure from shear.

9. The Raman assignment is not backed by a symmetry analysis.
   - Mode count. For the average P-3m1 structure with Br at 2d and Cr at 1a (Table S3), factor-group analysis gives only A1g + Eg Raman-active modes. Yet three "hard" modes (Ph6–Ph8) are assigned to P-3m1.
   - Intensity loss. The fading of intensity is attributed to "progressive metallization" (p. 9), but the authors' own DFT gives a gap of 1.39 eV at 10 GPa (p. 10, Fig. S6).
   - Pressure mismatch. The soft "R-3" modes are followed to about 10 GPa, whereas diffraction places the disappearance of R-3 between 6.4 and 8.4 GPa.

   The authors should give the irreducible representations for both phases, plus the local AA or random-stacking structure if relevant. They should also show fitted peak positions with error bars instead of guide-to-the-eye lines, and either reconcile the two pressure scales or explain the difference.

10. The argument that "a single phase is insufficient" (Fig. 6b, red) rests on one set of DFT choices without tests of sensitivity. The rise in Tc for pure ABC comes from J_L(ABC) increasing from 0.4 to 1.05 meV, which outweighs a roughly 30% drop in J1 (Fig. 6a). An ambient J_L of 0.4 meV per Cr, against J1 = 2.1 meV, implies strong three-dimensional coupling. This should be compared with the interlayer exchange obtained from neutron scattering in [47].
    - The structure is relaxed with PBE+U (U = 2.8 eV) plus D3, while the exchange uses r2SCAN+rVV10; whether U is applied in the r2SCAN step is not stated.
    - J2 and J3 are fixed at their ambient experimental values.
    - The supercells and magnetic configurations used for the fit are not given.

    The authors should show how J1 and J_L(P) change with U (or with/without U) and with the functional, give the fitted configurations, and propagate that spread into Tc(P). Only then can the decrease in Tc be ruled out for a single phase.

11. The pressure at low temperature and the dependence on sample are not controlled.
    - Pressure is loaded and read with ruby at room temperature (Methods), but Tc is measured at 2–35 K.
    - The horizontal error bars represent overpressure during loading. In the first run they reach 3 GPa (Fig. S1a).
    - The two runs give pc values about 1 GPa apart, and run 1 has a finite Tc near 6.3 GPa.
    - Fig. 1 does not say which run it shows. The AC-susceptibility points (blue circles) come from a different technique and cell, with no details given.

    The authors should state the run and the sample for each data set and estimate the pressure at low temperature, by ruby measured at low temperature or by a manometer such as the Tc of Pb. pc should be quoted as a range covering both runs, not as 5.8 GPa.

12. The claims of "intrinsic trimorphism" and of a "so-far unreported" phase are overstated.
    - The monoclinic C2/m phase is not observed in this work.
    - The P-3m1 fraction depends on cutting, mounting and deformation (Methods; Fig. 4c–h; p. 6). That points to stacking faults induced by deformation rather than an intrinsic polymorph.
    - Average structures of this kind, caused by stacking disorder, are well documented for CrCl3, CrI3 and the related VI3 and α-RuCl3. The paper itself notes similar residual density in VI3 and VBr3 [38, 39].

    The abstract and conclusions should describe the phase as a stacking-disordered (or AA-type, if concern 1 is resolved) modification and cite the relevant stacking-fault literature.

C. Minor

13. Fig. 1: the pressures of the orange Msat squares do not match the pressures in the inset legend; the first point sits near 2.3 GPa, while the inset says 1.92 GPa. In inset a, the 6.24 GPa loop (green) still shows a small step and an offset, but the main panel plots Msat = 0. Please make these consistent and state how Msat = 0 was decided.

14. Eq. (1) (p. 3). The Ehrenfest relation is an exact thermodynamic identity, not a model that "treats magnetovolume effects in materials with almost isotropic elastic properties". It cannot fail to apply to van der Waals magnets; it only connects quantities. Its standard form for a second-order transition is ∂Tc/∂P = Δκ/Δβ with volume quantities, or uses a factor of 3 when linear expansion is meant. Please correct the form and rephrase the argument.

15. Paracrystal analysis (p. 8, Fig. S4). The 10 ± 2 nm block length is extracted from a deformed crystal at ambient pressure, with Poisson-distributed blocks assumed. Please show the fit to Fig. 4h, give its free parameters, and state whether pristine crystals give a different block length. The text links this length to the high-pressure state, and that link is not justified.

17. Data and code availability. "Data are available upon request" and "no custom code" are weak for a paper whose central argument depends on simulations. Please deposit the diffraction frames and CIFs, the magnetization raw data with backgrounds, the VASP inputs, the exchange-fit data and the UppASD input files.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Settle what the high-pressure stacking actually is: compare an ordered AA model with a random-stacking model, using rod scans or diffuse-scattering analysis. Then compute the interlayer exchange for the registry that is actually found.
2. Rebuild the spin simulation with layer-wise, block-structured stacking disorder in thick cells. Report the staggered order parameter and the specific heat so that compensated order can be told apart from loss of order. Feed in the measured phase fractions with no free fraction parameter.
3. Add high-field M(H) and M(T) at 0.1–1 T above pc, and measure the magnetization of the crystal recovered from 21.5 GPa. Together these directly test whether interlayer AFM between intact moments explains the vanishing net moment.
4. Run at least one diffraction (or Raman) experiment in a helium or neon medium, at low temperature if possible, to separate hydrostatic pressure from shear. This would also tie the structural fractions to the temperature range where the magnetism is measured.
