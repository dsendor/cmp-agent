Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports SQUID magnetization of a CrBr3 single crystal in a diamond anvil cell. The data show T_C and the 1 T moment falling faster and faster with pressure, with no ferromagnetic signal at 6.24 GPa (extrapolated p_c = 5.8 GPa). This is the first direct measurement of ferromagnetism being suppressed in CrBr3 above about 3 GPa. Room-temperature single-crystal XRD and reciprocal-space maps show that the as-grown and pressurized crystals contain, besides the rhombohedral R-3 phase, a component without the "1/3" reflections. The authors index it as P-3m1 with 2/3 Cr occupancy and call it AA stacking. Its share grows with pressure, and it is retained after decompression from 21.5 GPa. The proposed mechanism is that AA-stacked layer pairs with antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling grow in number, supported by DFT exchange constants and atomistic Monte Carlo. The high-pressure magnetization result is valuable. The link between that result and stacking, and the stacking-based mechanism itself, rest on weaker ground than the abstract and conclusions suggest.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The DAC SQUID magnetization reaches about 6 GPa in a vdW ferromagnet, a demanding measurement. It gives T_C(p) and M(H) at several pressures (Fig. 1) and shows recovery after decompression (Fig. S1b).
- The work combines several techniques on one material: single-crystal XRD under pressure up to 21.5 GPa, high-resolution reciprocal-space maps with a paracrystal analysis (Fig. 4), Raman spectroscopy to 16 GPa (Fig. 5), DFT, and Monte Carlo.
- The observation that cutting or deformation alone produces the stacking-disordered component (Methods, Fig. 4c-h) is practically useful for everyone working on CrX3 crystals. It also offers a plausible explanation for the scatter in earlier high-pressure reports.
- The authors present the mechanism as a scenario and call for a microscopic probe above p_c.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The Monte Carlo model that produces T_C -> 0 appears to rest on an unphysical randomization and conflicts with the paper's own argument about monolayers. Section "Model of magnetic behavior under pressure" (p. 11) says "atoms from these phases are randomly distributed", and the stacking-variation curve in Fig. 6b drops to T_C = 0 near 5.6 GPa. Stacking faults, however, act layer pair by layer pair. If each pair of adjacent layers is coupled uniformly by J_L(ABC) > 0 or J_L(AA) < 0, the stack is a one-dimensional chain of layers with no frustration. A gauge transformation (flipping the spins of whole layers) maps every such sequence onto a ferromagnet with couplings |J_L|. The ordering temperature should then stay finite, set by J_1, the anisotropy and |J_L|; only the net moment shrinks or cancels. The authors themselves note (p. 12) that a CrBr3 monolayer, with no interlayer coupling at all, orders at about 70% of the bulk T_C. Interlayer sign disorder alone therefore cannot plausibly drive T_C to zero while J_1 is still about 1.5-1.7 meV (Fig. 6a).

   A collapse to T_C = 0 can arise only from frustration inside layers, which per-atom random assignment of J_L creates artificially, or from finite-size or criterion artifacts: a 10-layer 20x20x1 cell, with T_C taken from a susceptibility peak. The authors should:
   - state exactly how "AA" and "ABC" are assigned in the simulation (per atom, per bond or per layer pair);
   - repeat the simulation with layer-resolved stacking disorder;
   - report the staggered (layer-gauged) order parameter as well as the net magnetization;
   - show a finite-size check.

   If the layer-resolved model keeps a finite ordering temperature, the claim should change from "collapse of magnetic order" to "loss of net moment through interlayer compensation". That reading has testable consequences; see concern 3.

2. The structural and magnetic data are taken under different conditions, so the correlation they rely on is not established. All XRD and Raman data are at room temperature (Methods, Fig. 2e, Fig. 3, Fig. 5); the magnetization is at 2-40 K. The R-3 phase is the low-temperature phase reached through a first-order transition at about 420 K, and stacking energetics depend on temperature. Room-temperature phase fractions therefore need not match the low-temperature ones. The pressures also do not line up: the 1/3 reflections are still visible at 6.4 GPa and gone by 8.4 GPa, while ferromagnetism is already gone at 6.24 GPa, measured in a different cell and medium and on a different crystal. The pressure was set at room temperature with the ruby read only there (p. 14), so the actual low-temperature pressure in the Be-Cu turnbuckle cell is unknown; the horizontal error bars in Fig. 1 are one-sided.

   The authors should:
   - provide low-temperature diffraction, or at least intensities of the 1/3 reflections near T_C, at one or two pressures around p_c;
   - plot the phase fractions of Table S4 on the same pressure axis as T_C and M_sat in a main-text figure, with uncertainties;
   - give the low-temperature pressure calibration for the magnetization cell.

   Without these, "the T_C decline correlates with the AA fraction" (p. 11) is an assumption, not a measurement.

3. The magnetization does not show that what is lost above p_c is order rather than net moment, and the M_sat analysis has a temperature confound. Field sweeps stop at 1 T (Fig. 1a), and "M_sat" is defined as the 1 T value at 2 K.
   - At 5.24 and 5.60 GPa, T_C is about 13 K and 4 K. At 2 K the moment is therefore reduced simply because the measurement temperature is close to T_C. The parallel fall of T_C and M_sat ("identical trend", p. 3) does not show a reduced Cr moment or a growing antiparallel layer fraction.
   - If the proposed AA-AFM layer sequence existed, J_L(AA) of about -0.2 meV (Fig. 6a) implies a spin-flip field of the order of a tesla. Sweeps to the 5-7 T available on a SQUID should then show a metamagnetic step and recovery of about 3 mu_B/Cr. That would directly support the AFM-interlayer scenario. A paramagnetic, unsaturated response would point instead to suppressed intralayer order.
   - The 6.24 GPa M(T) (Fig. 1b, green) should be examined for an antiferromagnetic cusp or kink. Field-cooled and zero-field-cooled data would also help.

   The authors should add high-field M(H) at 2 K and M(T) at fields above the expected spin-flip field, at pressures above and just below p_c. They should also compare moments at a fixed reduced temperature T/T_C.

4. The decompression results are internally inconsistent unless phase content is tracked on the magnetization sample. XRD shows the 1/3 reflections extinct at 8.4 GPa (Fig. 3c), and P-3m1 retained after release from 21.5 GPa (Fig. 3d). Yet ferromagnetism with unchanged T_C and M_sat returns after decompression from 10 GPa (Fig. S1b). The Conclusions resolve this by saying "the P-3m1 phase was not yet stabilized at 10 GPa while some volume fraction of R-3 persisted". This contradicts the XRD statement that R-3 is undetectable above 8.4 GPa, and no diffraction after release from about 10 GPa is shown. Two controls would test the central hypothesis directly:
   - XRD on a crystal decompressed from about 10 GPa;
   - magnetization at ambient pressure of a crystal recovered from 21.5 GPa, which the authors say remains metastable P-3m1.

   If the AA-stacked phase really carries antiparallel interlayer coupling, the recovered crystal should show strongly reduced net moment and anomalous M(H) at ambient pressure. If it is ferromagnetic, the scenario fails. This is the most decisive experiment within reach of the authors' existing setup.

B. Should address

5. "AA stacking" is not established by the data, yet the magnetic model needs it specifically. The authors state (p. 6) that the P-3m1 average with 2/3 Cr occupancy at 1a is equally explained by a random sequence of A, B and C layer positions, and that they "are unable to distinguish between these two scenarios". A random A/B/C sequence produces mostly AB-type (rhombohedral-like) and monoclinic-like neighbor pairs, not AA pairs. According to the bilayer calculations cited in refs. [25, 49], these have different signs and magnitudes of J_L. The magnetic model nevertheless uses the AA J_L throughout (Fig. 6a), and the Discussion and Conclusions present AA stacking as found.

   The authors should either:
   - give evidence for genuine AA registry, for example through diffuse scattering along the rods, specific (h0l) intensities, or the Br positions in the P-3m1 refinement; or
   - recompute the model for a random-stacking distribution of neighbor-pair types.

   The abstract and conclusions should then be worded to match what is shown.

6. A non-hydrostatic, shear-induced origin for the stacking disorder is not excluded. The authors find that cutting, bending and mounting alone produce the P-3m1 component (pp. 5-6, 14). The pressure medium is described only as "liquid" in all three DAC experiments, and its identity and hydrostatic limit are not given. Many common liquids become non-hydrostatic between about 3 and 10 GPa, which is the pressure range of interest. The growth of the disordered component with pressure, the 10 nm block size, and the broadened spots after release from 21.5 GPa (Fig. 3d) could all reflect shear rather than intrinsic thermodynamics.

   The authors should:
   - name the media;
   - report ruby linewidths or pressure gradients;
   - if possible, compare one run in a more hydrostatic medium (He or Ne) or show that the onset does not depend on the medium.

   "Pressure-induced phase transformation" in the title and abstract should be qualified accordingly.

7. Sample characterization differs between experiments, and the sample dependence of p_c is not quantified. The XRD crystal began at about 80% R-3, selected "somewhat a matter of chance" from 30+ crystals. The initial phase content of the magnetization crystals and of the Raman crystal is not reported. Fig. S1a shows two magnetization runs with different p_c, but the main text gives only the value 5.8 GPa. If p_c depends on the initial P-3m1 fraction, that would itself support the mechanism; if not, it argues against it.

   The authors should:
   - report p_c for every run;
   - state the initial phase fraction (from XRD on the same or a sibling crystal) for the magnetization and Raman samples;
   - discuss whether p_c tracks it.

   The model's statement that FM is lost at "45% of P-3m1" (p. 12) should also be reconciled with the about 20% P-3m1 already present at ambient pressure in samples that are fully ferromagnetic.

8. The input to the T_C(p) simulation is chosen, not independently determined, so the agreement in Fig. 6b is not predictive. The AA fraction is taken as "a linearly increasing function up to a critical pressure" (p. 12), and the concentration dependence uses exchange parameters fixed at 5 GPa (p. 11). The slope and endpoint of the AA fraction are effectively fitted. The authors should:
   - state how the slope and critical pressure were chosen;
   - use the measured (lower-bound) fractions of Table S4, mapped to the magnetization pressures, as the input;
   - use pressure-dependent J_1, J_L and MAE in the mixed-phase simulation, rather than the 5 GPa values for all concentrations.

   There is also an inconsistency to fix: the text says the pure ABC phase gives T_C = 39 K (Fig. S8, p. 11), whereas Fig. 6b starts both models at about 34 K.

9. The robustness of the DFT exchange constants is not tested. Structures were relaxed with PBE+U (U_eff = 2.8 eV) plus D3, while exchange constants were computed with r2SCAN+rVV10 (p. 15). It is not stated whether +U was used in the r2SCAN step, which supercells were used, or which stacking geometry (interlayer distance, Br registry) was used for the AA structure at each pressure. The sign change of J_L(AA) near 2 GPa involves energies of only about 0.1-0.3 meV per Cr (Fig. 6a), well within typical functional and U sensitivity. The authors should:
   - show J_L(AA) and J_L(ABC) for at least two values of U, or with and without U, and for one other vdW functional;
   - state the energy convergence;
   - give the supercell sizes.

   J_2 and J_3 are fixed at ambient experimental values for all pressures. This is defensible but should be checked at one pressure.

10. The Raman phase assignment is not supported by the paper's own lattice data. The hard modes are assigned to P-3m1 and the soft modes to R-3 on the grounds that P-3m1 is "less compressible" and has "3D character" (p. 9). However, Fig. 2e shows the a and c parameters of R-3 and P-3m1 nearly superposed over the common pressure range. The two structures also share identical layers, so local intralayer modes should differ little between them.

    The intensity loss is attributed to "progressive metallization", but the authors' DFT gives a gap of 1.39 eV at 10 GPa (p. 10), which is not metallic. The kink near 10 GPa (Fig. 5) also does not coincide with the XRD disappearance of R-3 (6.4-8.4 GPa) or with p_c. The authors should:
    - support the mode assignment with phonon calculations for both stackings, or with polarization selection rules;
    - otherwise present it as tentative and remove the metallization explanation.

11. "Intrinsic trimorphism" and "a so-far unreported phase" overstate the structural result. The monoclinic phase is not measured in this work. The P-3m1 component is, on the authors' own evidence, created by mechanical manipulation and is best described by a paracrystal of R-3 and P-3m1 blocks about 10 nm long (Fig. 4h). This fits a stacking-faulted average better than a distinct thermodynamic phase. Related signs of disorder, such as residual density on the Cr2 site, were reported previously for CrX3 and VX3 (refs. [38, 39]). The abstract and conclusions should describe this as stacking disorder with a P-3m1 average and make clear what is new relative to earlier observations.

12. Some details of the paracrystal analysis and occupancy refinement are missing. The 10 ± 2 nm block length comes from the deformed sample (Fig. 4h), not the as-grown one. The way the uncertainty was obtained and the reason for a Poisson distribution of block lengths should be stated. In the R-3 refinement, Cr1 + Cr2 occupancies were constrained to sum to 1. The authors should show that a model with an unconstrained sum, which would test for Cr vacancies or excess, is not preferred, given that EDX establishes 1:3 only to about a percent. The phase fractions from 1/3 intensities are stated to be lower bounds (p. 8). If so, the abstract's quantitative statement that R-3 "becomes undetectable above 8.4 GPa" should carry the detection limit.

13. The Ehrenfest argument is dismissed only qualitatively. On p. 3 the authors note that the negative thermal expansion below T_C [34] implies dT_C/dp < 0 through Eq. (1), and then say the approach "cannot correctly explain" the suppression. They should evaluate Eq. (1) with literature values of Δκ and Δα and compare it with the measured low-pressure slope. This would show whether the standard magnetoelastic picture accounts for the region below 3 GPa, so that only the accelerated drop needs the stacking mechanism. The introduction's statement that DFT predicts J_1 increasing with pressure also conflicts with the authors' own calculated J_1 decrease (Fig. 6a); the paper should comment on this.

C. Minor

14. Fig. 1: The ambient and low-pressure T_C points come from "AC susceptibility measurement", but the Methods give no cell, frequency or amplitude for these measurements. The definition of T_C from dχ/dT minima (inset b) should be stated. Error bars on M_sat, including from the empty-cell background subtraction, should be shown, since the sample signal in a DAC is small compared with the cell.

15. Fig. 1a: The loops at intermediate pressures show vertical offsets and finite high-field slopes. The text should state whether a diamagnetic or paramagnetic background was removed and how.

16. Fig. 3d: The post-decompression reflections are visibly broadened and smeared. The authors should comment on mosaicity or partial amorphization and how these affect the claim that P-3m1 is retained.

17. Several smaller corrections are needed:
    - "p_c ≈ 5.8" lacks units (Conclusions).
    - "Table 4S" should read "Table S4" (p. 8).
    - P-3m1 is called "hexagonal" on p. 6 but "trigonal" elsewhere.
    - "Fig.1e, f in [25]" should be made explicit.
    - Refs. [15] and [29] are duplicates, as are [17] and [37].
    - Ref. [34] is cited for the 3a Wyckoff site but is a thermal-expansion paper.
    - Several references have placeholder DOIs ("https://doi.org/").
    - The methods equation for H is numbered (1), clashing with Eq. (1) on p. 3.

18. Methods: The meaning of "E_AFL − E_FM = 2N J_L" depends on whether J_L is per bond or per ion and on the double-counting convention in H. The authors should state the convention explicitly so readers can compare the J_L values with those in ref. [25].

19. The Raman section lists four peaks at 0.14 GPa but labels Ph1 to Ph8 in Fig. 5. A table linking labels, symmetries and slopes would help.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Measure the magnetization, up to 5-7 T and at ambient pressure, of a crystal recovered from 21.5 GPa (metastable P-3m1). Do the same, together with an XRD check, for a crystal recovered from about 10 GPa. This tests the AA-AFM mechanism directly.
2. Extend the high-pressure M(H) to several tesla just below and above p_c to look for a spin-flip step, and read moments at a fixed T/T_C.
3. Redo the Monte Carlo with stacking disorder assigned layer pair by layer pair and with the measured phase fractions as input. Report both the net and the layer-staggered order parameters.
4. Give low-temperature structural information near p_c (low-temperature single-crystal XRD in the DAC, or neutron or XMCD data if available). State the pressure media and their hydrostaticity, and plot structure and magnetism on one pressure axis.
