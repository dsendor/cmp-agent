Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript combines SQUID magnetometry in a diamond anvil cell (DAC), single-crystal and high-resolution X-ray diffraction, Raman spectroscopy, DFT and atomistic Monte Carlo to study CrBr3 under pressure. It reports that ferromagnetism disappears near 5.8 GPa, well below the earlier extrapolated 8.4 GPa. It assigns the loss to a growing fraction of an "AA-stacked" trigonal P-3m1 phase with antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling. A direct magnetization measurement of the collapse in a vdW Cr trihalide would be a useful result. The stacking-driven mechanism is the more interesting claim, but at present it rests on a structural assignment and a model link that the data do not yet pin down.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First direct DC magnetization data showing both T_C and the 1 T moment of bulk CrBr3 falling to zero near 6 GPa (Fig. 1). A second run (Fig. S1a) and a decompression check (Fig. S1b) are included.
- A careful single-crystal account of the extra scattering density on the nominally vacant 3a Cr site (Tables S1-S3). Similar density in VI3 and VBr3 has usually been passed over as "stacking faults".
- High-resolution reciprocal-space maps with Hendricks-Teller paracrystal simulations (Fig. 4, Fig. S4) that give a block size of about 10 nm.
- A consistent high-pressure structural data set up to 21.5 GPa (Table S4), with the observation that the high-pressure form is retained after release from 21.5 GPa.
- The hydrostaticity of the pressure medium is treated with care (Figs. S9-S11).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The diffraction does not establish AA stacking, yet the magnetic mechanism depends on it. The P-3m1 model (Table S3: one Cr site at 1a with occupancy 2/3, a = 3.64 Å) is the averaged CdI2-type subcell. The authors state on p. 6 that they cannot tell randomized stacking from AA stacking. That distinction is the whole mechanism. Fig. 6a and the Monte Carlo model use DFT interlayer couplings for an ideal AA bilayer, which changes sign above about 2 GPa. A random or turbostratic stacking sequence would give a broad distribution of interlayer couplings, not the AA value.
   - The two cases predict different diffraction. An ordered AA honeycomb stacking (a √3 × √3 supercell, c ≈ 6.1 Å) keeps sharp honeycomb superstructure reflections at integer L. Random stacking turns them into diffuse rods along c*.
   - The paper's own observation is that the "1/3" reflections fade and vanish at 8.4 GPa (Fig. 3c, histograms). On the face of it, that fits loss of interlayer registry better than an ordered AA phase.
   - The authors should analyze intensity along L through the honeycomb superstructure positions (h - k ≠ 3n) at each pressure: sharp peaks at integer L for AA, diffuse rods for random stacking. They should also report whether any diffuse intensity remains at 8.4-21.5 GPa.
   - If ordered AA cannot be shown, the magnetic model should use stacking-averaged interlayer couplings, and the abstract and conclusions ("AA-stacked trigonal phase") should be reworded.

2. Reversibility contradicts the proposed structure-magnetism link. Fig. S1b shows full recovery of T_C and M_sat after decompression from 10 GPa. Table S4, however, gives an R-3 fraction of -0.1(7) at 8.4 GPa, and the text calls P-3m1 metastable on release. If R-3 is already gone by 8.4 GPa and the transformed phase is metastable, a crystal released from 10 GPa should not regain the full ferromagnetic response.
   - The conclusions resolve this by saying P-3m1 "was not yet stabilized at 10 GPa while some volume fraction of R-3 persisted". No structural data at or after 10 GPa support this, and it conflicts with the abstract ("undetectable above 8.4 GPa").
   - The authors should show XRD on a crystal decompressed from about 10 GPa, or magnetization on a crystal decompressed from above 15-20 GPa, where the structure is shown to stay P-3m1.
   - Without one of these, the magnetic collapse may be reversible and unrelated to the irreversible stacking change. Hydrostatic compression of the intralayer exchange and anisotropy alone would then remain a live explanation.

3. The magnetic state above p_c is not characterized, and the proposed state would have a measurable signature. All DAC data stop at 1 T (Fig. 1a) and T_C comes from 0.01 T (Fig. 1b). So "collapse of ferromagnetism" here means only that the 1 T moment and the low-field susceptibility anomaly vanish.
   - In the proposed scenario, layers keep their moments and couple antiparallel across AA interfaces with J_L(AA) ≈ -0.15 to -0.25 meV (Fig. 6a). Those couplings should be overcome at a spin-flip field of order 1-3 T. M(H) up to 5-7 T at 2 K above p_c should then recover most of 3 μB/Cr, while a genuine loss of moment or a glassy state would not.
   - The authors should measure M(H) to the maximum available field at 5.6 and 6.2 GPa or higher. They should also measure M(T) at several fields above p_c to look for a Néel-type cusp.
   - The "M_sat" read at 1 T near p_c should not be called a saturation moment unless saturation is shown.
   - Related point: the authors cite a monolayer CrBr3 T_C of about 70% of bulk (22-27 K). Layers decoupled or coupled antiparallel by a weak J_L should still order within each layer near that temperature. The measured T_C of about 4 K at 5.6 GPa is far below this. Interlayer stacking alone cannot explain it unless intralayer exchange or anisotropy also collapses, and the DFT (J1 falls by about 30% and the MAE by a factor of two by 8 GPa) does not show that. This gap should be discussed explicitly.

4. The quantitative link between model and data is weak, and partly circular.
   - (a) The "stacking variation model" in Fig. 6b assumes an AA fraction rising linearly up to a chosen critical pressure. The match to the experimental T_C(p) is therefore largely built in. The authors should state the free parameters, and should instead drive the model with the measured fractions in Table S4.
   - (b) Using those fractions undermines the picture. Fig. S8 needs about 73% AA to suppress T_C. Table S4 gives about 50-58% P-3m1 at 5.7-6.4 GPa, and the text says the R-3 fraction is a lower bound, so the AA fraction is an upper bound. Page 12 mentions "45% of P-3m1". At the measured fractions the model predicts T_C ≈ 25-30 K, not zero.
   - (c) The crystals start with 20-40% P-3m1 at ambient pressure (Table S4; Methods), but the model starts at 0% AA.
   - (d) A stack with mixed-sign but unfrustrated nearest-layer couplings has a collinear, Mattis-type ordered ground state. Its ordering temperature is set by the magnitudes of the couplings, not their signs. If the Monte Carlo T_C comes from the uniform magnetization or its susceptibility, it measures loss of net moment, not loss of order. The authors should give the order parameter used, and compute a layer-resolved or gauge-transformed staggered order parameter or the specific heat.
   - (e) With ten layers and periodic interlayer boundary conditions, an odd number of AFM bonds around the periodic stack frustrates the chain artificially. With only three disorder realizations, this can lower the computed T_C. The authors should use open boundaries or many more layers, and average over more realizations.

B. Should address

5. Structure and magnetism are measured on different crystals, at different temperatures. All structural and Raman data are at room temperature, while the magnetism is at 2-35 K.
   - Cr trihalides have stacking transitions on cooling (CrBr3 near 420 K at ambient, CrI3 near 220 K), and the boundary may move with pressure. The stacking at low temperature under pressure is unknown.
   - Methods say the P-3m1 fraction varies from crystal to crystal and with handling ("a matter of chance"). The phase fraction of the crystals used for magnetization is not given.
   - The authors should report XRD on the magnetization crystals, or crystals cut from the same piece, before loading. Ideally they should add low-temperature XRD at one pressure near p_c, or justify why the room-temperature stacking carries over to low temperature.

6. Pressure at the measurement point is uncertain, and overshoot matters for a metastable transformation.
   - The pressure is set and read with ruby at room temperature only. Turnbuckle Be-Cu cells can change pressure on cooling.
   - The first run shows overpressure during loading of up to several GPa (Fig. S1a; up to about 14 GPa at the last point).
   - If the stacking change is irreversible, the magnetization at each point reflects the maximum pressure reached, not the nominal pressure.
   - The two runs give p_c values about 1 GPa apart. The authors should state which run Fig. 1 shows, quote p_c with an uncertainty that covers both runs, and measure the low-temperature pressure (for example by the Pb or Sn superconducting transition in the cell) or estimate it.

7. Phase fractions and the "undetectable above 8.4 GPa" claim are not well constrained.
   - Table S4 gives R-3 fractions of -0.1(7), 0.08(3), 0.01(1) and -0.03(2) above 8.4 GPa. A value of -0.1 ± 0.7 does not show absence.
   - The starting fraction of 0.60(5) out of the DAC conflicts with the roughly 80% R-3 stated in Methods for "the best sample".
   - The authors should explain how the fractions and their errors were obtained (which reflections, the absorption correction in the DAC, and the occupancy model). They should reconcile the two starting values and soften the 8.4 GPa statement in the abstract.

8. The "new phase" and "intrinsic trimorphism" claims are not established.
   - Methods report that as-grown crystals were R-3 and that P-3m1 appeared after cutting. Page 6 says the P-3m1 fraction is "likely unintentionally modified" by mounting, and the RSM "deformed" state was produced by an unspecified external force. This points to deformation-induced stacking faults, not an intrinsic third polymorph.
   - The monoclinic phase is not observed in this work, so "trimorphism" relies on the literature.
   - The authors should show data from an undeformed as-grown crystal (for example the Fig. 4 RSM before any cutting). They should describe the deformation used for Figs. 4d and 4g quantitatively, and frame P-3m1 as an average description of stacking disorder unless a distinct ordered phase can be shown (see concern 1).

9. The Raman interpretation has alternative explanations it does not address.
   - (a) The slope change and the "hardening" of all modes near 10 GPa (Fig. 5) coincide with the 10.5 GPa hydrostatic limit of the methanol-ethanol-water medium the authors use. Non-hydrostatic stress commonly changes dω/dp.
   - (b) XRD places the disappearance of R-3 between 6.4 and 8.4 GPa, but the modes assigned to R-3 persist to about 10 GPa.
   - (c) The intensity loss is attributed to "progressive metallization", but the authors' own DFT gap is still 1.39 eV at 10 GPa. A shift of the resonance relative to the 514.5 nm line is the more likely cause.
   - (d) Assigning modes to phases by slope alone is not justified when the two phases share the same layer structure. A disordered average P-3m1 structure has no well-defined zone-center selection rules.
   - The authors should address (a)-(d), show polarization-resolved spectra if available, and present the phase assignment as tentative.

10. The sign of the AA interlayer coupling is a small DFT energy difference that has not been tested for robustness.
    - J_L(AA) in Fig. 6a lies between +0.05 and -0.25 meV and changes sign near 2 GPa. This is at the level of functional-dependent errors.
    - Structures were relaxed with PBE+U+D3 (U = 2.8 eV), but exchange was computed with r2SCAN+rVV10. Whether U was applied there, and on which structure, is not stated.
    - The lateral registry used for "AA" relative to ref. [25], and whether interlayer spacing was relaxed for the AA cell at each pressure, are not given.
    - The authors should show J_L(AA) and J_L(ABC) for at least one other functional or U value, and state the AA geometry.

11. Several features of the magnetization data need explanation (Fig. 1).
    - The loops in inset a are not antisymmetric about M = 0, which points to an offset or imperfect background subtraction.
    - The 6.24 GPa loop appears to retain a step of a few tenths of μB/f.u. around zero field, while the main panel plots M_sat ≈ 0 at that pressure.
    - The conversion of a microgram DAC sample to μB/f.u. (how the mass was determined, and whether the sample fragmented) is not described.
    - The AC susceptibility points (blue circles) come from a setup and pressure cell not described in Methods.
    - The authors should show raw and background-subtracted data, the normalization procedure, and the AC susceptibility method.

12. Some lattice parameters in Table S4 and Fig. 2e are anomalous.
    - c(R-3) at 0.7 GPa is 19.0(2) Å, larger than the ambient 18.342(2) Å. That gives c/3 = 6.33 Å, while the P-3m1 refinement of the same data gives 6.10(4) Å.
    - The claimed saturation of c above 11.8 GPa (p. 5) rests on non-monotonic values (5.33(7), 5.36(9), 5.21(8) Å) measured beyond the hydrostatic limit of the medium.
    - The authors should check the 0.7 GPa refinement and either drop the saturation claim or qualify it.

C. Minor

13. Raman labels: R-3 (point group C3i) has only Ag and Eg Raman-active modes, so the "A2g" label for the 181.8 cm^-1 mode (p. 8) should be corrected. The Fig. 5 caption's "green ones (blue)" is unclear.

    - The supplementary text cites "Fig. S7" for the concentration dependence that is in Fig. S8.
    - The Fig. S10 caption refers to Fig. S8 for the non-hydrostatic sample, which is Fig. S9.
    - Page 9 of the supplement cites "Figs S8-S10" for Figs. S9-S11.
    - Two equations are numbered (1).
    - The conclusions give "p_c ≈ 5.8" without units.
    - Table S4 lists the ambient P-3m1 fraction as "0.6(5)".
    - The text and Table S4 refer to "Table 4S" and "Table S4" inconsistently.

15. References:
    - [15] and [29] are the same paper, as are [17] and [37].
    - [34] (negative thermal expansion) is cited for the 3a Wyckoff site.
    - "Another study predicted a different magnetic order at 17 GPa" (p. 2) has no citation.
    - [32], [41], [42] and [53] carry placeholder DOIs.

16. The introduction says T_C "is expected to grow" because J1 rises with pressure. The authors' own DFT (Fig. 6a) shows J1 falling above about 1 GPa, so this framing should be reconciled with their results.

17. The abstract and conclusions present the ambient trimorphism as an experimental finding. The monoclinic phase is only cited, so the wording should say this.

18. Data availability "upon request" is weak for a structural paper. The CIFs for both refinements and the high-pressure data sets should be deposited (for example with the CSD/ICSD).

19. Fig. 2 caption: panels b and d are described as different views ("2/3 occupied Cr sites... in AA" vs "ABC sequence"), but the labels in the figure are hard to match to the text. The caption should state the viewing direction of each panel.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Run one decisive magnetization experiment: a single, structurally characterized crystal loaded beyond 10 GPa (or to about 20 GPa), with M(H) to 7 T at 2 K and M(T) at several fields measured during compression and after release. This would test the AFM-interlayer scenario (spin-flip recovery of the moment) and the link to the irreversible structural change at the same time.
2. Analyze the L-dependence of the honeycomb superstructure reflections and of the diffuse scattering at each pressure, to decide between ordered AA and random stacking. Then rebuild the magnetic model on whichever stacking statistics the data support.
3. Drive the Monte Carlo model with the measured phase fractions from Table S4, including the ambient P-3m1 fraction. Use open boundary conditions and a layer-resolved order parameter, and report the resulting T_C(p) with no adjustable critical pressure.
4. If possible, add a low-temperature diffraction point near p_c, or a microscopic magnetic probe (neutron diffraction, μSR, or XMCD in a DAC) above p_c. That would connect the room-temperature structure to the low-temperature magnetic state directly.
