A. Must address

1. Trapped flux and field history are not ruled out as the source of the broken time-reversal symmetry, and no training experiment tests the TRSB itself (Sec. IV, Fig. 2b,c, Fig. S6). The identity Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| holds only if the whole device is time-reversed, including any frozen-in magnetic configuration. Several ordinary effects break it without spontaneous TRSB of the order parameter. Abrikosov or Pearl vortices can be pinned in the wide, roughly µm-scale Sr2RuO4 banks, lead arms or Au/Ti leads near one arm. Flux can be trapped during cooldown in the magnet's remanent field. The superconducting magnet can show hysteresis or sweep-direction-dependent remanence, so H and -H are reached with different histories. Each acts as an internal magnetization that does not reverse with H. It gives an inhomogeneous phase bias that the single uniform shift H -> H + H0 of Eq. 1 cannot remove, and it can produce a finite Ic+ - |Ic-| at nominally zero field. The c-axis Hc1 of Sr2RuO4 is only a few mT and the sweeps in Fig. 2 reach ±10 mT, so vortex entry into the banks is likely; a vortex near a weak link is known to give exactly this kind of asymmetric, offset Ic(H) pattern. The paper does not say whether data were taken after zero-field cooling with the magnet degaussed or the remanent field nulled, or how the field was swept. Conversely, the domain-wall picture makes a testable prediction: if the TRSB comes from a spontaneously chosen domain configuration, its time-reversed partner should also occur. The sign of ΔIc and of the zero-field diode should then be random across zero-field thermal cycles (or set by pinning), and should follow the sign of a small cooling field, as in Kerr training. A sign that never changes across cooldowns would point to an extrinsic origin; a ΔIc that depends on field excursion would point to trapped flux. The authors should report ΔIc(H) (a) after repeated zero-field cooldowns through Tc with the remanent field nulled; (b) after field cooling in small +Hz and -Hz; (c) for both field-sweep directions and for different maximum field excursions (minor loops); and (d) after warming to just above Tc and re-cooling in zero field, to see whether the symmetric pattern is recovered. Sec. V says field-cooled and zero-field-cooled results are identical for the bistable Ic states; that statement should be extended to, and shown for, the TRSB data. Without these, TRSB of the order parameter cannot be separated from TRSB of the device's flux configuration.

2. The ΔIc error bars omit the dominant uncertainties, and instrumental systematics that would produce exactly the observed signal are not excluded (Sec. IV, Sec. S2, Fig. 2b,c, Fig. S9). The error bars come from a parametric bootstrap of voltage noise only; S2 itself says this "only accounts for the uncertainty in measured voltage". They exclude the stochastic spread of switching currents, which is the main uncertainty in Ic and is especially relevant given the telegraph noise near Ic documented in Sr2RuO4 junctions (Refs. 31-33). They also exclude slow drift and sweep-to-sweep reproducibility, and the paper does not say how many IV sweeps enter each point. The authors' own control shows a drift-induced ΔIc of about 1 µA, larger than its error bars (Fig. S9, last half-periods). In Ring 2 (Fig. 2c), ΔIc is a nearly field-independent negative offset of about 5-8 µA on Ic of 60-140 µA at all three temperatures, with jumps at the lobe minima. This is the form that several extrinsic sources would take: a dc offset in the current source or a ground loop (a shift δI gives ΔIc = 2δI at every field); a polarity-dependent voltage background such as a thermoelectric voltage not removed by subtracting the mean voltage near I = 0; the order in which polarities are swept; heating after switching at Ic of about 100 µA; and a polarity-dependent Ic criterion. The criterion is "0.1 or 0.2 I0RN depending on the degree of rounding", with RN taken from "a representative portion of the dataset", and the paper does not say which threshold was used for each dataset. This same offset is what the paper calls a zero-field diode effect "over the entire interference pattern". The authors should show raw IV curves at zero and a few other fields for both polarities, with the sweep order reversed; take Ic+(H) and Ic-(-H) with interleaved polarity at each field point; repeat the measurement with the current leads swapped and with the voltage leads swapped (an extrinsic offset follows the wiring, an intrinsic asymmetry follows the device); report the measured zero-current voltage and current offsets; show that ΔIc survives different criteria (for example 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 I0RN, and a dV/dI peak); show switching-current histograms at a few field points and recompute the significance of ΔIc from the measured switching distribution width; estimate error bars from repeated independent field sweeps; and give a systematic error budget (Ic criterion, current and voltage offsets, field remanence, temperature drift) next to the statistical one. Without this, the "statistically significant" TRSB in Figs. 2 and S6 is not established at the level the headline claim needs.

4. The evidence that the junctions are intrinsic domain walls, rather than FIB-, damage- or geometry-defined weak links, is indirect (Sec. III, Figs. 1, 2a, Figs. S1-S4). Both the TRSB attribution and the order-parameter conclusion depend on this, because extrinsic weak links containing magnetic damage or trapped flux could also break time-reversal symmetry. Shapiro steps (Fig. 1c,d, Fig. S3) show that a Josephson-like element exists somewhere between the voltage probes, but not where it is or that it is a chiral domain wall. The statement that "Shapiro steps can only originate from Josephson coupling" is too strong: phase-slip centres and phase-slip lines in narrow superconducting strips also lock to microwaves and give steps at hf/2e. The single-arm stage of Ring 2 (Figs. S3, S4) shows Shapiro steps and a Gaussian Ic(B) envelope, but any FIB-defined constriction or Ga-implanted or amorphized region could do the same, so it does not "confirm the presence of a chiral domain wall". Plausible alternatives include FIB damage at the arm necks (TEM limits damage to about 10 nm, but the arms are only 0.25-0.7 µm thick and Ga implantation is not quantified), local inhomogeneity, and weak links in the contact or bank regions. The argument that retrapping equals critical current excludes constriction junctions is not decisive, since constrictions shunted by a large normal channel or operated at IcRn far below Δ/e are overdamped. From Fig. S1 (Ic about 45-80 µA, Rn about 19-112 mΩ), IcRn is only about 1-8 µV (about 1.5 µV for Ring 1), orders of magnitude below Δ/e of about 0.2-0.3 mV, and Rn is the whole-device resistance so the junction value is unknown. The argument from high pattern symmetry is qualitative and circular, since symmetric FIB-milled necks would naturally give symmetric constriction junctions, and the absence of oscillations in 3K-phase rings is cited from earlier work (Refs. 38, 41) rather than shown. The authors should discuss the junction IcRn and its temperature dependence (Ambegaokar-Baratoff or KO-type against phase-slip behaviour); compare rings with different neck widths or asymmetrically milled arms, or re-mill the necks, to show where the phase drop sits; compare quantitatively with the TDGL predictions of Ref. 38; state how many nominally identical rings did and did not show junctions; show the 3K-phase data in the supplement; state what each of the four Ring 2 milling stages (Fig. S2) tells us about junction location; and temper the statement that the effects "cannot be explained by accidental weak links".

5. The narrow central lobe and its domain-wall-motion interpretation rest on a heuristic model with many free parameters, and conventional explanations are not excluded (Sec. IV, Fig. 2a, Sec. S3, Fig. S10). The central lobe is 0.73 Φ0 in Ring 1 and 0.74 Φ0 in Ring 3, against about 1.00 Φ0 for neighbouring lobes. The toy model introduces an ad hoc linear coupling α(Ψ+ - Ψ-)B (Eq. 3) that is not derived and is not a gauge-invariant free-energy term as written (a coupling of B to |Ψ+|² - |Ψ-|², an orbital moment, would be); it is not defined what Ψ+B means as a free-energy term, or why Φint scales as (φD + π/2)/(π/2) (Eq. 5). The sigmoid in Eq. 4 is ad hoc. The fits have Φint^max, φm, a, αI, βL and a Gaussian width free for about five lobes, yet overshoot the outer lobes, mismatch lobe widths (fit 0.91 versus data about 0.97), miss lobe amplitudes (Fig. S10b,c) and do not reproduce the skew of the central lobe. The fitted Φint^max = 0.24 Φ0 (Ring 1) implies sizeable spontaneous flux from a single-chirality ring, which should be compared with scanning SQUID and Hall bounds on chiral edge currents in Sr2RuO4 that lie orders of magnitude below chiral p-wave estimates. There is also a conceptual tension: if domain walls move reversibly with field, as the lack of sweep-direction dependence implies, then H -> -H should mirror the domain configuration and tend to restore Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| rather than break it. Conventional alternatives for a distinct central lobe include non-linearity or remanent-flux reversal of the magnet near zero field, field-dependent kinetic-inductance screening, flux focusing from the banks, and vortex entry into the banks at |H| of about 2-3 mT. The control SQUID (Figs. S8, S9) spans only ±2 mT with a period of about 1.6 mT, so it does not test the field range where the rings show the effect. Ring 3 shows the effect only below 1200 mK (Fig. S6), which needs an explanation within the model. The authors should calibrate the field near zero with a Hall sensor or measure the control SQUID over ±10 mT in the same magnet; take Ic(H) after zero-field cooling for first and return sweeps over a wider range, and show whether the central-lobe width depends on sweep range or history (distinguishing reversible domain-wall motion from vortex entry); report the number of free parameters, fitted values with uncertainties or posteriors, and their temperature dependence; give an order-of-magnitude estimate of domain flux against edge-current bounds; explain how reversible field-driven domain-wall positions coexist with the observed TRSB; define the quantities in Eqs. 3 and 5 or label them as phenomenological; and present the model as illustrative rather than as support for the domain-wall picture, ideally replacing it with the TDGL simulations of Ref. 38 including bias current and in-plane field so that the lobe width, zero-field diode and bistability can be compared quantitatively.

6. The remanent-field correction in Eq. 1 is ambiguous and may absorb or distort the reported asymmetry (Sec. S2, Eq. 1, Fig. 2). As written, Eq. 1 minimizes ∫[Ic+(H+H0) - Ic-(H+H0)]², the diode asymmetry at the same field, not the time-reversal comparison of Ic+(H) with |Ic-(-H)|; taken literally it does not implement the symmetrization described. It is unclear whether Ic- means |Ic-|, which quantity was minimized, and how this interacts with a genuine zero-field diode. Choosing H0 to minimize the very asymmetry later reported biases ΔIc towards zero; moreover, a SQUID built from two φ0-junctions with different anomalous phases gives a field-shifted pattern, so a shift-based correction partly absorbs exactly the intrinsic signature while leaving extrinsic distortions in place. The authors should correct Eq. 1 and state its sign convention; report the fitted H0 for each dataset and for each sweep direction, compared with the magnet's known remanence and an independent calibration (Hall sensor or reference superconductor in the same mount); and show ΔIc with and without the H0 correction.

9. The "unstrained" and "strain-free" claims are asserted rather than measured, yet they are used to reject the leading alternative scenario (Abstract, Sec. III, Sec. VII). The argument rests on Tc ≤ 1.5 K and RRR > 100, but these are bulk-averaged and the device Tc values (1.3-1.5 K, Table S1) lie at or below bulk. The flakes are 0.25-0.7 µm thick on SrTiO3 and capped with Ti/Au or SiOx/Au; differential thermal contraction can impose inhomogeneous strain of order 0.1% that splits Tc and TRSB without raising Tc towards 3 K, because Tc rises only gently at small strain. Local strain, Ga implantation or amorphization at the milled sidewalls and necks, exactly where the junctions are proposed to sit, are also not excluded, and the cited TEM (Ref. 43) is from other devices. The Discussion nonetheless rejects the accidental-degeneracy and strain- or dislocation-texture scenarios (Refs. 52-56) on the grounds that the crystals are "ultra-pure and strain-free". The authors should estimate the thermal-mismatch strain for this stack (or from the Tc width), give Raman, cross-sectional TEM or local strain mapping on a measured device if possible, and otherwise soften the argument, stating that discrimination against Refs. 55 and 56 rests mainly on domain-wall mobility.

11. The in-plane-field bistability has conventional explanations that are not excluded, and the field alignment is not given (Sec. V, Fig. 3, Figs. S13, S16). The SQUID period is about 4-4.7 mT; a 1° misalignment of 20 mT gives about 0.35 mT out of plane (about 0.08 Φ0 through the ring), and 1-3° of a 10-20 mT field gives 0.2-1 mT, a significant fraction of a flux quantum. No alignment procedure or out-of-plane calibration is given. If βL is not small the ring can host several fluxoid states that a large bias current can switch between. The observed symmetry, in which reversing either the current polarity or the in-plane field swaps the roles of the training currents, is the symmetry of a J x B force on vortices lying parallel to the ab plane, Josephson-like vortices between RuO2 layers, or vortices trapped at the necks, which a training current could push in or out. A 20 mT in-plane field through an arm cross-section of about 0.5 x 0.5 µm² gives several Φ0, so each junction is strongly field-modulated (Fig. S15); the argument that the Josephson penetration depth exceeds the junction width (Sec. VI) dismisses Abrikosov vortex flow but not current-driven switching between Josephson-vortex (fluxon) configurations in a wide junction, a known source of bistable Ic, nor vortex entry into the banks at training currents of about 110-120 µA. The optimal angle differs between rings (20° in Ring 1, 55° in Ring 2; Fig. S16) with no link to crystal axes, the map in Fig. S16b is irregular, and states are not reproduced after a field reset (Fig. S14); these point to sample-specific pinning. The authors should state the alignment accuracy and how it was set; show whether the bistability survives when the out-of-plane component is compensated or deliberately varied; give βL (from kinetic and geometric inductance) for each ring; show the Ic(Hz) pattern of each Ic state, since a relative shift by a fluxoid would identify a fluxoid-state change; test whether bistability persists at Hx below the in-plane vortex entry field; show the in-plane field and temperature dependence of the switching thresholds and of the Ic difference between states; report the crystal-axis orientation of each ring; and discuss what pins the domain configuration.
