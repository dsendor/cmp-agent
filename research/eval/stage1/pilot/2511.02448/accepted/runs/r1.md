Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper compares Ni L3 and Cu L3 RIXS on a nominally undoped, reportedly superconducting PrNiO2 film and an insulating CaCuO2 film. It covers magnons (with an out-of-plane coupling J⊥ included in a linear spin-wave fit), polarization-resolved dd excitations and the dispersion of the dxy orbital excitation. The most novel result is that the PNO dxy excitation disperses with the opposite sign to the cuprate's (maximum at Γ), and the authors explain this with nearest-neighbour orbital superexchange made effective by Hund's coupling. A side-by-side comparison of the two infinite-layer compounds with the same instrument and analysis is useful to the nickelate community. Several of the headline interpretations, however, rest on fixed or chosen parameters and on cross-material comparisons that are not yet controlled.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Both infinite-layer families are measured on the same beamline (ID32), at the same temperature (20 K) and with comparable resolution (40 and 42 meV). The two-dimensional momentum maps (Fig. 1d,e; Fig. 5) cover both high-symmetry directions.
- Polarization-resolved spectra at ±H, with single-ion cross-section calculations for matched geometries (Fig. 4), make the dd assignment more reliable for the nickelate than energy alone.
- The orbital dispersion in an infinite-layer nickelate appears to be new, and the opposite sign to CCO is a striking observation (Fig. 5).
- A microscopic spin-orbital model is given, including an explicit mapping to a polaronic problem (SM I). Its predicted NN orbiton hopping (13.7 meV) is compared with the fitted value (12.5 ± 3.8 meV).
- The data are deposited on Zenodo, and the supplement documents the fitting choices (Figs. 7–13).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The damping comparison does not support the claim that self-doping has "a much milder impact on spin order than chemical doping" (Fig. 3b; p. 4; abstract and conclusions).
   - Fig. 3b compares absolute damping: about 80–100 meV for PNO against about 200–500 meV for Bi2201.
   - The PNO magnon bandwidth is roughly half that of the cuprates (J1 = 46 against 82 meV). Relative to the mode energy, PNO damping (about 90/150–190 meV, γ/ω ≈ 0.5–0.6) is close to that of underdoped Bi2201 at comparable momenta. On that measure, PNO looks like an underdoped cuprate rather than a nearly undamped system.
   - Several other contributions to the PNO width are not separated from intrinsic damping:
     - inhomogeneity in a 5 nm, topotactically reduced, capped film (partial reduction, interfacial layers, the impurity phases invoked for the 0.7 eV peak);
     - the unresolved phonon and multi-magnon content, which the authors state they cannot disentangle in PNO;
     - the single-peak DHO fit near X, which the authors admit overestimates damping in CCO.
   - The "coexistence" of sharp spin excitations with superconductivity has two further problems:
     - The data were taken at 20 K, above Tc (zero resistance below 4 K).
     - RIXS averages over the whole film, so a superconducting fraction and a magnetically ordered-like fraction could be spatially separate.
   - The self-doping level of the measured film is not determined (for example from the Ni L3 XAS lineshape or the O K pre-edge), so "self-doping" and "chemical doping" cannot be put on a common axis.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Plot damping normalized by magnon energy or by J1 for all samples.
     - Fit PNO with a model that includes the phonon and a multi-magnon component, and show how much of the width remains.
     - Estimate the doped-hole concentration of the RIXS sample.
     - Restate the coexistence claim as normal-state coexistence in a film whose bulk superconducting fraction is unknown, unless a measurement below Tc or a Meissner fraction can be provided.

2. The dxy orbital dispersion in PNO (62–90 meV; Fig. 5a) needs to be shown to be intrinsic and not a fitting or cross-section artifact.
   - The peak is broad (0.4 eV FWHM in the single-ion model, SM G) and sits between the 0.7 eV "RE" peak and the broad dxz/yz feature.
   - The 0.7 eV peak itself grows toward the zone edge (p. 3). The scattering geometry changes continuously with Q at fixed 2θ, so the incident and scattered polarization projections change too.
   - Fig. 4 and p. 6 already show that the polarization character of the 1.29 eV peak differs between ±H and from theory. A Q-dependent reweighting of overlapping components could therefore move the fitted centroid by tens of meV without any real propagation.
   - The fitted shift is about 15–20% of the resolution-convolved linewidth. The quoted error bars are fit confidence intervals that ignore the correlations among the overlapping peak parameters, which the supplement shows to be strong for the dd fits (Fig. 8).
   - What the authors should do:
     - (i) Show the dispersion at the same |Q| in grazing incidence and grazing emission, and at ±H. An intrinsic dispersion must be even in Q, while cross-section artifacts are generally not.
     - (ii) Show that the result survives changes to the fit model: two versus three dd peaks (as in Fig. 7), the RE peak free versus fixed, and the dxz/yz width free versus fixed.
     - (iii) Report a model-independent measure such as the first moment of the spectrum over a fixed window around 1.3 eV.
     - (iv) Simulate, with the single-ion cross sections of Fig. 4, the apparent centroid shift expected from geometry alone along the measured path.
   - The same tests on CCO would show whether the sign reversal between the two materials is robust.

3. The quantitative agreement between orbiton theory and experiment (Table III) depends on chosen parameters, and the theory section has internal inconsistencies.
   - Sensitivity to J_H/U:
     - t1^orb = (jHS − jLS)/2 · J̄1^orb with jHS = 1/(1 − 3J_H/U). This is very sensitive to J_H/U: with J_H = 1.2 eV and U = 6.5 eV, 3J_H/U = 0.55.
     - Taking J_H = 1.0 eV (the CCO value) instead of 1.2 eV lowers (jHS − jLS)/2 from about 0.50 to about 0.34. That is a roughly 30% change in t1^orb, before any change in J1^orb.
     - The 80% hopping reduction and the J_H value are argued qualitatively (nephelauxetic effect) and are not independently determined.
   - Inconsistencies:
     - Formula against number: SM I states J̄1^orb = U·J1^orb/(U − 2J_H) ≈ 27 meV. With J1^orb = 21.6 meV, U = 6.5 eV and J_H = 1.2 eV, that formula gives about 34 meV. 27 meV corresponds to U/(U − J_H). Either the formula or the number is wrong, and t1^orb = 13.7 meV uses 27 meV.
     - Source of U: SM H says "our estimate of the Mott energy U value is exclusively based on the orbital dispersion". The Methods say U was taken from La4Ni3O8 (ref. 29). If U was tuned to the dispersion, the agreement in Table III is a fit, not a prediction.
   - Model approximations:
     - The model drops the orbiton–magnon coupling terms (Eqs. 16–17) and keeps a free orbiton. Yet the main text argues that NN orbiton motion is strongly frustrated by the string effect. The argument therefore relies on the very terms that are neglected.
     - The model assumes a long-range antiferromagnetic, ferro-orbital ground state, while PNO is a self-doped metal without reported long-range order.
     - The fitted t3^orb = 5.4 meV, about half of t1, is outside the model.
   - CCO cross-check: the theory gives a finite NN hopping for CCO (6.7 meV), but the CCO data were fitted with t2 only (Eq. 2). The CCO dispersion should be refitted with t1 and t2 free to test whether the data are compatible with 6.7 meV.
   - What the authors should do: give a sensitivity analysis of t1^orb and t2^orb over plausible ranges of U, J_H, Δ and the hopping scaling; fix the formula and the description of U; and present the agreement as consistency rather than validation.

B. Should address

4. The out-of-plane coupling J⊥, and the claim that PNO has three-dimensional antiferromagnetic correlations "similar to" CCO, are not constrained by the data (p. 5, SM F, Fig. 13, conclusions).
   - J⊥ = 5.9 meV is fixed, not fitted. The supporting gap at Γ comes from points within 0.05–0.125 r.l.u. of the specular condition, where the magnon merges with a strong elastic line and the error bars span about ±50 meV (Fig. 13). The phonon was also removed by hand in this range (SM D).
   - The L value of each spectrum is not given, although a J⊥-induced gap depends on L.
   - An XY-type anisotropy gap, which the authors mention (ref. 65), or a gap from the finite correlation length of a system without long-range order, would raise the energy near Γ in the same way. Neither is excluded.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Give L for each Q.
     - Measure or show the near-Γ magnon energy at two different L for the same in-plane Q (different incidence angles). This is the direct test of J⊥ against anisotropy.
     - Quote J⊥ as an upper bound (0–10 meV) wherever it is used.
   - The statement that the results "give a proof of the fully occurred topotactic reduction of the PNO samples and the absence of interstitial oxygen atoms" (p. 5) does not follow from a fixed J⊥ and should be removed or supported by structural data.

5. The four-neighbour linear spin-wave fit for PNO (Fig. 3a) is under-constrained, and its comparison with the literature is not quantitative.
   - Four in-plane couplings plus J⊥ are fitted to a dispersion that has about 45 meV of zone-boundary dispersion, large error bars near Γ, and a single-peak fit near X that the authors call approximate.
   - J2 to J4 are likely strongly correlated with each other and with J1.
   - Fig. 13 and Fig. 3a show the fit missing the data near (0.25,0.25), where the data lie about 20 meV below the curve. The authors also note that the maximum along [H,H] is at (0.2,0.2), which no J1–J4 Heisenberg model with this sign structure reproduces.
   - It is not stated whether the S = 1/2 quantum renormalization factor (Zc ≈ 1.18) is included. This affects J1 directly.
   - "In agreement with the literature [12, 24, 51]" needs numbers. For example, ref. 12 reported J1 ≈ 64 meV for NdNiO2, well above 46 meV, and ref. 47 reports values for PrNiO2 films.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Give the parameter covariance matrix.
     - Show fits with fewer couplings (J1–J2, or J1–J2–J3), with model-selection statistics.
     - State Zc.
     - Tabulate literature J values next to theirs.
   - The comparison of J1 between the two materials and the "J⊥/J1 = 13%" figure depend on these choices.

6. The sample basis is thin for the main claims.
   - Only one PNO film appears to have been measured. The CCO data are a reanalysis of a dataset partly published in refs. 45 and 55.
   - The superconductivity of nominally undoped PrNiO2 (onset about 11 K, zero resistance below 4 K) is itself unusual. It is supported only by reference to ref. 42; no ρ(T), XRD or XAS from the RIXS sample is shown.
   - The 0.7 eV peak is said to be stronger in samples with impurity-phase diffraction, yet no diffraction of this sample is shown.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Show ρ(T), XRD (c-axis parameter, absence of secondary phases) and Ni L3 XAS for the measured film, and state whether the same film was used for transport and RIXS.
     - Reproduce at least the magnon dispersion and the dxy dispersion on a second PNO film. Ideally, add a reference nickelate film (for example a non-superconducting or deliberately doped one) measured under the same conditions.

7. The interpretation of the mixed polarization character of the 1.29 eV peak as Pr 5d (or interstitial s) hybridization is one of several possibilities (Fig. 4, Fig. 12, p. 6).
   - Alternatives that are not addressed:
     - the overlap of the broad dxz/yz tail and the 0.7 eV peak, both partly non-crossed;
     - the unpolarized continuum the authors themselves attribute to self-doping;
     - the polarimeter efficiency correction and its uncertainty.
   - The text compares the data with Fig. 4(i) only by eye. Fig. 12 shows that the separated crossed and non-crossed components are noisy enough to need 15-point smoothing.
   - What the authors should do: fit the crossed and non-crossed spectra simultaneously with the same component set, and report the crossed fraction of the dxy component with its uncertainty, after propagating the polarimeter calibration. Then state what the expected fraction would be under each scenario.

8. The text's description of the nickelate correlation regime does not match the authors' own model parameters.
   - Table II uses Δ = 4.4 eV and U = 6.5 eV for PNO, so Δ < U. In the Zaanen–Sawatzky–Allen scheme invoked on p. 2, that is a charge-transfer, or at most a mixed, regime.
   - The conclusions nevertheless invoke "the Mott-Hubbard nature of the correlation in IL nickelates".
   - The abstract attributes the smaller J1 to larger Δ and smaller hopping, but no calculation links the Table II parameters to the measured J1. The three-band model used for J^orb could give the spin superexchange J1 for both compounds, which would be a direct consistency check of the parameter choice (46 against 82 meV).
   - What the authors should do: harmonize the wording and add that calculation.

9. Several elastic and quasi-elastic features are noted and then left unexplained, and the charge-order test is incomplete.
   - Unexplained features:
     - the elastic intensity rising toward X (Fig. 6; Fig. 10a);
     - the dispersion maximum at (0.2,0.2);
     - the elastic increase beyond (0.25,0.25), which the authors suggest is an incommensurate resonant reflection.
   - Each of these could affect the magnon fits at those momenta (Fig. 13, (0.25,0.25) region).
   - The "absence of charge order" (Fig. 6) is tested along [H,0] at one unspecified L, at about 0.025 r.l.u. steps, and with the elastic tail rising at both ends.
   - What the authors should do: give L, state the expected peak width and the detection limit relative to published 3a0 intensities, and check whether the rise toward 0.475 tracks the off-resonance or σ/π response expected for a structural or substrate reflection.

10. The dz² energy and its comparison with CCO are not determined (Table I: 2.96 ± 0.58 eV; p. 6; SM C).
   - Fig. 8 shows strong cross-correlation between the dxz/yz and dz² intensities, so the third peak's position and weight trade off against the dxz/yz tail.
   - The statement that dz² "is found at slightly larger energy than in the cuprate" is not supported at this uncertainty.
   - What the authors should do: remove the statement, or support it with a constrained fit (for example a polarization-selective channel where dz² is enhanced, using Fig. 4).

11. The strain argument (pp. 5–6) needs to be made quantitative.
   - The PNO strain is described as "compressive strain of about +0.9%"; under the usual convention compressive is negative.
   - Whether a 5 nm film under an STO cap is fully strained is not shown (no reciprocal-space map).
   - The direction "compressive strain generally reduces J" is asserted without a value from the cited cuprate studies.
   - What the authors should do: give the measured in-plane lattice parameter of the reduced film and a literature dJ1/dε, so the reader can see that strain cannot explain a 45% difference in J1.

C. Minor

12. Fig. 6 caption gives the integration window as "[−0.1; 0−1] eV", while the panel says (−0.1; 0.1) eV. Correct the caption.

13. In Fig. 3 and Fig. 13, the x-axis is labelled (0.25,0.25)–(0,0)–(0.5,0)–(0.25,0.25) for PNO. The PNO data were measured along (0.34,0.34)–(0,0)–(0.475,0)–(0.25,0.25) (Fig. 1e, Fig. 10), and points appear to the left of (0.25,0.25) in Fig. 13. Use the actual path labels.

14. Fig. 2a sums spectra over H = 0.4–0.475, across which the magnon disperses. If this panel is meant to show lineshape or width, use a single-Q spectrum, or state that the summed width is not used quantitatively.

15. Eq. 5 is described as normalized by "the product of the variances" but divides by σXσY. The formula also does not subtract means, so ρXY values above 1 (Figs. 8, 11, up to about 24) are not correlation coefficients. Define the quantity properly or show the fit covariance matrix instead.

16. The RE peak is given as 0.7 eV in the main text and 0.6 eV in SM B. In Fig. 4, PNO is shown at Q = ±0.4 and CCO at Q = −0.45 and +0.5, which complicates the side-by-side comparison. State why the momenta differ.

17. "Error bars represent 95% confidence intervals from the fittings" (Figs. 3, 5): state whether these include the systematic uncertainty from the fixed phonon removal near Γ and from the choice of background, which the supplement shows to matter.

18. The concluding statements on smaller electron–phonon interaction, the absence of charge density waves, and nickelates mimicking "the mechanisms for superconducting pairing" are not tested by the data. Mark them clearly as speculation or shorten them.

19. Typos and small points:
   - "Superconductive IL phase" should be "superconducting".
   - "a priori rather generic and unbiased" (p. 8) is redundant.
   - "Supplementary Information V" is a section reference inside the same document.
   - In SM I the sentence "N is the number of lattice sites" refers to a symbol that does not appear in Eq. 9.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one summary figure that puts PNO, CCO and doped cuprates on dimensionless axes (damping/ω, J⊥/J1, orbiton bandwidth/J1) with full uncertainties. This makes the "similar to cuprates but with reduced energies" message quantitative.
2. Give a short robustness appendix for the dxy dispersion: ±H and in/out geometry, fit-model variants and a first-moment analysis. This is the paper's most novel observation and deserves the strongest support.
3. Present the orbiton theory with a sensitivity band (t1^orb as a function of J_H/U and the hopping scaling), with Table III shown against that band. Apply the same model with t1 free to the CCO data.
4. Include the transport, diffraction and XAS characterization of the measured PNO film, and an estimate of its self-doping level. This anchors the "undoped superconducting" label on which the cuprate comparison depends.
