Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript compares Ni L3 and Cu L3 RIXS on two thin films with the same structure: a nominally undoped, superconducting infinite-layer nickelate, PrNiO2, and the insulating infinite-layer cuprate CaCuO2. It reports four things. (i) A linear-spin-wave (LSW) fit with in-plane J1 to J4 and an interlayer J⊥, giving J1 ≈ 46 meV against 82 meV and comparable J⊥ of about 6 to 7 meV. (ii) A momentum-independent magnon damping of about 100 meV in PrNiO2, smaller than in doped Bi2201. (iii) Polarization-resolved dd spectra compared with single-ion calculations. (iv) Most strikingly, a d_xy orbital dispersion of opposite sign in the two materials, assigned to nearest-neighbour (NN) orbital superexchange in the nickelate and next-nearest-neighbour (NNN) superexchange in the cuprate. A side-by-side comparison under matched experimental conditions is useful to the field. Of the results, the orbiton-sign result is the new one. The magnetic results largely confirm earlier RIXS work on infinite-layer nickelates, with J⊥ added.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Matched experiment: same beamline, same temperature (20 K), same scattering angle, and the same in-plane momentum paths for both compounds. This makes the direct comparison more credible than one assembled from the literature.
- Polarization analysis of the scattered photons on an infinite-layer nickelate is uncommon. It gives information on the symmetry of the dd excitations and on the magnon (Fig. 4).
- The opposite sign of the d_xy dispersion (Fig. 5) is visible in the raw maps. If it holds up, it is a new and clear contrast between the two families.
- Extending the LSW model to include J⊥ for a nickelate is a reasonable step. The out-of-plane coupling is rarely addressed.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The dispersion law used to extract J^orb is the NNN form, yet the conclusion is NN propagation in PrNiO2.**
   - What the paper does: Eq. (1), ε_k = 2J^orb cos(k_x)cos(k_y), is taken from Ref. 43, where it describes diagonal (NNN) orbiton hopping in CaCuO2. The same function is fitted to PrNiO2 to obtain J^orb = +14 ± 4 meV. That value is then identified with the calculated NN coupling, J^orb_NN = 14.5 meV (Table III).
   - Why the form matters: NN hopping on a square lattice gives a dispersion of the form ∝ (cos k_x + cos k_y), not cos k_x cos k_y. The two forms predict different ratios between the [H,0] and [H,H] bandwidths.
     - Product (NNN) form with J = 14 meV: about 4J ≈ 56 meV from Γ to (0.475,0), and only about 1.4J ≈ 20 meV from Γ to (0.34,0.34).
     - Sum (NN) form: the [H,H] change is larger than the [H,0] change.
   - What the data show: Fig. 5(a) quotes 90 meV on the [H,0] branch and 62 meV on the [H,H] branch. Neither is obviously consistent with Eq. (1) and J = 14 meV.
   - Why the sign alone is not enough: the sign of the fitted coefficient cannot discriminate NN from NNN propagation, because the two have different k-dependences.
   - Requests:
     - Fit the PrNiO2 (and CaCuO2) dispersions with the NN form, the NNN form and a combination of both. Report goodness of fit and the [H,0]/[H,H] bandwidth ratios.
     - Explain how a 90 meV branch is compatible with 4J^orb = 56 meV.
     - The Fig. 5 caption says the continuous line is a "charge transfer model fitting", while the text says Eq. (1). Clarify which it is.

2. **The robustness of the d_xy dispersion in PrNiO2 is not demonstrated.**
   - The size of the effect: the dispersion is 60 to 90 meV, extracted from a peak whose width is several hundred meV (Fig. 2(c)).
   - What the fitted position depends on: the peak overlaps the 0.7 eV "RE peak", the d_xz/yz component, the d_z² component and a charge continuum. Its fitted position is sensitive to how these components are constrained.
   - Why the decomposition can shift with momentum:
     - Along the path the scattering geometry changes, with grazing incidence on one side and grazing emission on the other at fixed 2θ.
     - The polarization character of the 1.29 eV peak itself changes with H (Fig. 4(a–c); text on p. 6).
     - So the relative weights of the overlapping components change with momentum. A momentum-dependent change in the decomposition can shift a fitted centroid without any intrinsic dispersion.
   - The CaCuO2 d_xy peak is narrow and well separated (Fig. 2(d)), so the same issue is much less severe there.
   - Requests:
     - State which fit parameters (widths, positions, amplitudes of neighbouring peaks, continuum shape) are free and which are fixed across Q.
     - Show that the dispersion survives alternative constraints. Useful tests: widths fixed versus free, the RE peak included versus excluded, a different continuum model, and a model-independent first moment over a fixed window.
     - Compare the ±H branches, which are equivalent by symmetry but measured in different geometries, and the σ versus π incidence data.
     - Discuss geometry-dependent self-absorption through the STO cap and film, which can distort line shapes at the Ni L3 edge.
   - Fig. 5(b) currently shows the stack only with fixed vertical guide lines. Overlaid spectra at Γ and at the zone boundary, normalized, would make the shift visible directly.

3. **J⊥ and the claim of "three-dimensional antiferromagnetic order" rest on a gap near Γ measured at an uncontrolled, varying L.**
   - What the paper says: the abstract and p. 4 conclude that both materials "support a three-dimensional antiferromagnetic order", from the 60 to 80 meV magnon energy near the in-plane zone centre at non-integer L.
   - Measurement problems:
     - The Methods state that L is not fixed during H scans and that "this is not an issue, since the in-plane Js are still dominating." This contradicts using the same data to extract J⊥.
     - With 2θ = 149.5° at 852.4 eV and c = 3.28 Å, L varies from roughly 0.44 r.l.u. at Γ to roughly 0.18 r.l.u. at H = 0.475 (similarly for CaCuO2). J⊥ is therefore constrained essentially by a handful of points near Γ.
     - Near Γ the magnon is at 60 to 80 meV. That is comparable to the 80 meV phonon, to the resolution (40 meV), and to the tail of a strong elastic line.
   - Alternative explanations the LSW fit does not exclude:
     - A single-ion or exchange anisotropy gap (mentioned with reference to Ref. 59, then set aside).
     - An artefact of the elastic/phonon subtraction.
   - Model assumptions not justified:
     - The LSW model presupposes a specific 3D Néel order (stacking vector), which is not stated.
     - The introduction itself notes that infinite-layer nickelates show no long-range order and show spin-glass behaviour.
   - Requests:
     - Report the L value of every spectrum and include it in the SpinW fit.
     - Show the Γ-point spectra and their decomposition explicitly.
     - Compare fits with J⊥ = 0 plus an anisotropy gap against fits with J⊥ and no anisotropy.
     - Ideally, measure an L-scan at fixed in-plane momentum (by changing 2θ or the incident energy within the resonance) to see the J⊥ dispersion directly.
     - Short of that, soften the "3D antiferromagnetic order" wording to "interlayer spin correlations".
   - Related overclaim: the statement that this also gives "a proof of fully occurred topotactic reduction ... and absence of interstitial oxygen atoms" (p. 5) does not follow from a magnon energy. Remove it, or support it with structural or XAS evidence.

4. **The theoretical support for NN versus NNN dominance is partly tuned, and the key asymmetry (the magnetic string effect) is asserted, not computed.**
   - Tuned inputs in Table II for PrNiO2:
     - Hoppings set to 80% of the CaCuO2 values.
     - Δ = 8.00 eV, chosen "sufficiently larger than U also providing reasonable results". This is higher than common electronic-structure estimates of a few eV.
   - The agreement between Theory J^orb_NN = 14.5 meV and the experimental 14 ± 4 meV (Table III) is presented as validation. With Δ chosen to give "reasonable results", it is not an independent test.
   - Inconsistency in the CaCuO2 case:
     - The model gives |J_NN| = 22 meV > |J_NNN| = 15 meV.
     - Yet the experiment is attributed to NNN, by invoking suppression of NN hopping by magnon dressing that is outside the model.
     - For PrNiO2 the same suppression is argued to be absent because there is no long-range AFM order. But the paper itself reports sharp, weakly damped magnons with J1 ≈ 46 meV and argues for 3D AFM correlations (concern 3). The string effect needs local AFM correlations over a few lattice spacings, not long-range order.
   - Requests:
     - Show J^orb_NN and J^orb_NNN as a function of Δ (for example 3 to 10 eV) and of the hopping scaling.
     - Check that the on-site parameters (ε_xy = 0.70 eV, ε_πσ = −1.00 eV) are consistent with the measured dd energies in Table I.
     - Give a quantitative argument, even an estimate based on the measured J1 and the spin correlation length, for why string-effect suppression of NN hopping is effective in CaCuO2 but not in PrNiO2.

B. Should address

5. **The magnon damping comparison does not isolate self-doping.**
   - The claim (p. 4): the ~100 meV damping in PrNiO2, smaller than the ≳ 200 meV in UD15K Bi2201 (Fig. 3(b)), "demonstrates that self-doping has a much milder impact on spin order".
   - Why it does not follow:
     - The effective hole count from self-doping in PrNiO2 is not estimated, so the two materials are not compared at equal doping.
     - The Bi2201 values come from a different experiment (Ref. 49) with a different resolution and possibly a different line-shape model.
     - Damping in a 5 nm, topotactically reduced film can also come from disorder, residual or excess oxygen, stacking faults or strain inhomogeneity. These are absent in single crystals.
     - Near Γ the magnon overlaps the phonon. Near (0.5,0), the paper notes that a single-peak fit overestimates damping because of the multimagnon continuum. The flat ~100 meV may therefore partly reflect the fit model rather than intrinsic physics.
   - Requests:
     - Refit the Bi2201 raw data (if available) with the same procedure and resolution convolution.
     - Compare with a non-superconducting or differently reduced nickelate, or with literature NdNiO2/LaNiO2 damping.
     - Give an estimate of the self-doped hole concentration.
     - Otherwise, rephrase the conclusion as a hypothesis.

6. **The LSW fit uncertainties and the model adequacy are not credible as stated.**
   - The table in Fig. 3(a) quotes J1 = 45.8 ± 0.3 meV and similarly tight values for J2 to J⊥, for five parameters fitted to a single dispersion along one path.
   - Why these are not credible:
     - These parameters are strongly correlated.
     - Systematic errors are not included: L variation, elastic/phonon subtraction, and the quantum renormalization factor Z_c, which the paper does not state whether it applied.
   - Model adequacy:
     - The PrNiO2 dispersion maximum lies at (0.2,0.2) rather than (0.25,0.25), and the elastic intensity rises beyond the zone boundary (p. 5, Fig. 1(e)). The authors suggest this may indicate stripe-like or incommensurate order.
     - If so, a Néel-based J1 to J4 model may absorb that physics into J2 to J4, which would bias them.
     - Sign conventions for J2 (negative in both materials) are not stated in the text.
   - Requests:
     - Report the fit covariance or correlation matrix, uncertainties that include systematic contributions, and whether Z_c was applied.
     - Test whether a reduced model (J1, J2, J3 with J3 = J2/2 as in a t–t′–U mapping, plus J⊥) fits the data equally well.
     - Show how J1 to J4 change when the anomalous [H,H] points beyond (0.2,0.2) are excluded.

7. **The single-ion comparison and the dd assignments are under-specified.**
   - Calculation inputs:
     - Parameters for the single-ion calculations in Fig. 4(g–l) are not given: crystal-field values, spin–orbit, exchange field, broadening.
     - If they are taken from the fitted energies in Table I, "well reproduced" refers mainly to the relative polarization weights. That should be stated and quantified, for example by crossed/total ratios per peak in data versus calculation.
   - Weak or untested assignments:
     - The 0.7 eV "RE peak" is assigned to Pr 5d hybridized states, then left out ("disregarding the peak at 0.7 eV").
     - The d_z² energy, 2.96 ± 0.58 eV, has an uncertainty too large to support "a possibly larger energy in the nickelate".
     - The mismatch of the d_xy intensity at H = 0.4 (σ) is attributed to Pr 5d or interstitial s states without a test. A photon-energy (resonance) dependence of the 0.7 eV and 1.29 eV features, or a comparison with a nickelate of a different rare earth, would help.
   - Consistency: the CaCuO2 d_xz/yz centre of mass is quoted as 2.05 eV. The simple average of 1.95 and 2.29 eV is 2.12 eV. Specify the weighting.

8. **Samples and structural comparability.**
   - Sample count: it is not stated how many PrNiO2 and CaCuO2 films were measured, or whether the key results (the J values and the d_xy sign) were reproduced on a second film.
   - Different substrates and strain:
     - The two films sit on different substrates (STO versus NGO), with different in-plane lattice constants (3.94 versus 3.86 Å).
     - The d_xy energy difference is partly attributed to this, but strain could also affect J1 and the orbiton hopping.
   - Requests:
     - State the strain state of each film.
     - Comment on how a substrate-induced change in hopping would affect the J1 ratio and J^orb.
     - Provide the reduction-quality evidence (XRD, XAS lineshape) in the paper rather than only by reference to Ref. 40.

9. **The concluding statements go well beyond the data.**
   - The summary (pp. 7–8) asserts several things that none of the measurements address:
     - The lower Tc in nickelates "is mainly due to the overall smaller energy of spin fluctuations".
     - The Mott–Hubbard character "might imply ... smaller electron-phonon interaction and the lack of charge density waves".
     - Infinite-layer nickelates "are indeed unconventional superconductors very similar to cuprates".
   - Measurements were taken only at 20 K, above Tc ≈ 4 to 11 K, and no superconducting-state or doping-dependent data are shown.
   - These should be clearly labelled as speculation or removed. The abstract should be checked for the same issue.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add an L-dependence measurement at fixed in-plane momentum, at least at Γ and at (0.5,0), for both compounds. This would turn the J⊥ claim into a direct measurement and separate it from an anisotropy gap.
2. For the orbiton, present fits with the NN and NNN dispersion forms side by side, with fit-model robustness tests. Make the opposite-sign result visible in overlaid, normalized spectra at Γ and at the zone boundary.
3. Turn the charge-transfer-model comparison into a sensitivity study over Δ and the hopping scaling, including at least a semi-quantitative treatment of the string-effect suppression of NN hopping as a function of the AFM correlation length.
4. Put the magnon damping comparison on a common footing: the same fitting procedure, and ideally an independent estimate of the self-doped hole density or a reference nickelate film.
