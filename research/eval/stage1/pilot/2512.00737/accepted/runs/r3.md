Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors measure Fe L3-edge RIXS with circularly polarized X-rays on the compensated polar magnet Fe2Mo3O8 (magnetic point group 6'mm', TN = 60 K). They report circular dichroism (CD) that appears below TN for a scattering plane lying in an m' plane and is absent for a scattering plane lying in an m (glide) plane. The CD sits on excitations assigned to the tetrahedral (Td, feature B, about 0.45 eV) and octahedral (Oh, feature A, about 70 meV) Fe sites. An ionic multiplet model with a staggered Weiss field reproduces the CD qualitatively. On this basis the authors propose "bi-altermagnetism", meaning both magnetic sublattices are independently altermagnetic, and they present RIXS-CD on dd excitations as a site-selective probe of altermagnetic order. The main experimental advance is being able to follow RIXS-CD through the ordering transition and to resolve it by site. This was not possible for the high-TN altermagnets studied before (MnTe, CrSb, alpha-Fe2O3). The concept is less new than the experiment: as argued in concern 4, the "bi-" property may follow from symmetry for this structure.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The paper shows CD appearing across a magnetic transition in a zero-magnetization system. Temperature series at eight temperatures are given in Figs. 3, S3 and S4, and the paramagnetic phase serves as an internal null.
- The scattering geometry is chosen so that the m' mirror forbids CD in the paramagnetic phase, so the CD is not confused with the CD that RIXS can show without broken time-reversal symmetry. A second geometry (m plane) where CD is forbidden even in the ordered phase is also measured (Fig. 2a inset, Fig. S2).
- The ferrimagnetic alternative is addressed directly. The model predicts that the ferrimagnetic state shows CD in both geometries (Fig. S9), so a null in the m geometry discriminates between the two states.
- The multiplet parameters (crystal field, J_H, xi_3d) are fixed from peak positions alone, without using the CD (Supplement section 4, criteria C1 to C3). Sensitivity checks on the trigonal distortion (Fig. S8) and on the Weiss field (Fig. S7) are included.
- The data are deposited on Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The Néel-domain state is not characterized, and no test shows that the CD is odd in the order parameter. Fe2Mo3O8 has two Néel domains (L parallel and antiparallel to c), related to each other by the 6_3 operation. If the CD is linear in L, as the Weiss-field model implies, equal domain populations inside the roughly 386 x 30 µm beam footprint would cancel it. A finite CD in Figs. 2a and 3 therefore requires a domain imbalance. The paper does not say how the sample was cooled (zero field? magnetoelectric cooling?), whether the CD sign and size are the same after repeated cooling through TN, or what sets the imbalance. This matters for two reasons. First, the sign and size of the CD cannot be compared with the model without knowing the domain state. Second, and more important, there is no measurement showing that the dichroism reverses when the magnetic order is reversed, which is the cleanest test that it is magnetic and not instrumental. Because the two domains are related by a 60° rotation about c, the model should predict how the CD at a given m' plane compares with the CD at the m' plane 60° away. If the model predicts a sign reversal, that comparison is a direct test. The authors should:
   - state the cooling protocol and the domain state;
   - repeat the 32 K m' measurement after several thermal cycles, and on different spots of the sample;
   - ideally either select domains (for example by magnetoelectric cooling in parallel E and H below the metamagnetic field) or measure the CD at symmetry-related azimuths, and show the predicted reversal or invariance of the CD for both features A and B;
   - check that the relative sign of the CD on A and on B, which the model ties to the antiparallel Oh and Td moments within a layer, matches the data.

2. The size of the CD and of the "null" results is never quantified. The paper says the CD "vanished within the experimental uncertainty" above TN (p. 4) and is absent "within the present experimental accuracy" in the m geometry. No difference spectra (RCP minus LCP), counting-statistics error bars or repeat scans are shown, so no uncertainty is defined. The panels themselves raise doubts:
   - In Fig. S2 (lower panel, m plane, 32 K) the elastic peak differs by about 8% between LCP (about 95) and RCP (about 88). There are also visible RCP/LCP differences near 0.45 to 0.55 eV and at 1.6 to 2.0 eV.
   - The elastic-line difference is comparable to the CD on feature A in the m' geometry (Fig. 2a, Fig. 3a), so the "allowed" and "forbidden" geometries are separated by a margin that has not been demonstrated.
   - The Fig. 2a inset, which is described as the corresponding m-plane low-energy spectrum, appears to have overlapping elastic peaks. The authors should explain whether it was processed differently from Fig. S2.
   - The m-geometry null is shown only at 32 K and at one unspecified Q, and the paper does not give that Q.

   The authors should:
   - plot RCP minus LCP difference spectra with Poisson error bars for both geometries at the same Q and temperature;
   - give the integrated CD (or the asymmetry (I_R minus I_L)/(I_R plus I_L)) of features A and B against temperature, with error bars, in the style of an order-parameter plot;
   - show that the normalization window (5 to 7 eV energy loss, Supplement section 3.1) itself carries no CD;
   - explain the elastic-line asymmetry in the m geometry.

3. The evidence for symmetry breaking on the Oh sublattice rests entirely on feature A, which is the least clean feature. Feature A sits at about 70 meV, on the tail of an elastic line measured with 45 meV resolution. That region also contains the elastic line, the electromagnons (4 to 10 meV), magnons (11 to 14 meV) and phonons (Supplement section 3.2), as well as the Td spin-orbital levels. Fig. S7a puts those Td levels at about 5 to 40 meV for Weiss fields of 3 to 5 meV, and the fitted peak_1 in Fig. S5 lies at 30 to 38 meV. Meanwhile the one well-separated Oh excitation, feature C at about 1 eV, shows no measurable CD (p. 4). Yet the model predicts clear CD there: in Fig. 2b and Figs. S7f to S7h the RCP and LCP intensities of the 1 eV peak differ visibly. The explanation offered, "large lifetime broadening and the character of the corresponding wave functions", is not shown. The paper therefore has one ambiguous Oh feature where the model agrees with the data and one clean Oh feature where it does not, and this undercuts the claim that both sublattices are seen to break the symmetry. The authors should:
   - fit the RCP and LCP spectra of feature A with the same constrained decomposition as in Fig. S5, with the elastic line fixed from the carbon-tape reference, and show which component carries the CD;
   - show the model's Td contribution to the CD in the 0 to 100 meV window, so that a Td origin of the A-region CD can be excluded;
   - recompute the Oh CD at feature C with a final-state broadening matched to the measured width of C, and show whether the predicted CD drops below the noise;
   - measure the CD of A and B as a function of incident photon energy across the L3 edge (an RCP/LCP version of Fig. 1e and Fig. 4c). The paper states that Td and Oh excitations resonate at different incident energies (p. 3), so a resonance profile of the CD gives site selectivity that does not depend on the energy-loss assignment.

B. Should address

4. The "bi-altermagnetism" claim needs an operational definition and a statement of what the RIXS data add. The m/m' selection rules are properties of the global magnetic point group 6'mm'. They would hold for any compensated order with this group, whether or not each sublattice is "independently" altermagnetic. In a non-centrosymmetric crystal, a compensated pair of moments related by a rotation or screw (here 6_3) cannot be related by inversion. Each Wyckoff orbit that is compensated is therefore altermagnetic by construction. "Bi-altermagnetism" then seems to follow from the known neutron magnetic structure and the space group, not from the RIXS measurement. In addition, the authors' own model makes the Td moment a Van Vleck moment, induced entirely by the exchange (Weiss) field (p. 5, Supplement p. 6), so in what sense the Td sublattice is altermagnetic "independently" is unclear. The authors should:
   - define bi-altermagnetism in spin-group terms;
   - state which alternative magnetic state the data exclude. For example, what would RIXS-CD show if only one sublattice were compensated by a rotation, or if one sublattice were disordered?
   - rephrase "the realization of bi-altermagnetism" accordingly. The defensible claim is site-resolved detection of the time-reversal symmetry breaking at both Fe sites.

5. The comparison with the Weiss-field model is described as quantitative but is shown only qualitatively. Page 5 says "the measured CD is quantitatively reproduced by an atomic (impurity) model", but there are several gaps:
   - The Weiss-field strength used in Figs. 2b and 2c is not stated, and 3 to 5 meV is described only as "a reasonable value".
   - The Weiss field acts on spin only, although the authors note that it could be more general (Supplement p. 5).
   - The CD amplitude depends on the Weiss field (Fig. S7).
   - No calculated and measured CD difference spectra are overlaid.

   The ferrimagnetic-versus-altermagnetic discrimination (Fig. S9) uses the same 3 meV field, and the statement that ferrimagnetic CD is "significantly stronger" depends on that choice. The authors should:
   - fix the Weiss field independently, for example from mean-field exchange estimates consistent with TN = 60 K or from published spin-wave exchange constants;
   - overlay the calculated and measured asymmetries for A and B;
   - show that the m-geometry CD expected for a ferrimagnetic state lies above the experimental upper bound for every Weiss field in the plausible range;
   - otherwise replace "quantitatively" with "qualitatively".

6. The spin-orbital assignment of feature A does not match the numbers quoted.
   - The main text (p. 5) states that a two-component fit gives "an energy spacing [that] follows a 2:3 ratio (see Fig. S5)". Fig. S5d gives peak_1 at about 31 to 38 meV and peak_2 at about 68 to 73 meV. The spacings (ground state to peak_1, then peak_1 to peak_2) are therefore about 34 and 36 meV, close to 1:1, not 2:3.
   - The Fig. 4a caption uses zeta = 52 meV and gives quintet levels at 23.7 to 25.6 meV and septet levels at 58 to 65 meV. Neither set matches 35 and 70 meV.
   - The simulations use xi_3d = 65 meV (Supplement C2).

   The fit uses two Lorentzians plus a Voigt profile and a background, applied to a shoulder only about 1.5 resolution widths from the elastic line, so the peak positions are not well constrained. The Supplement itself calls the fit a "constrained minimal decomposition". The authors should:
   - reconcile the ratio claim with the fitted energies, including the effect of the Weiss field and the trigonal distortion on the Jeff levels (Fig. S8a);
   - state which zeta applies where;
   - report the correlations between fit parameters, or compare models with one, two and three components.

7. Polarization-switching artifacts are not bounded. The CD is obtained by switching the EPU between helicities. The paper quotes a phase error of about 3.5°, a polarization degree above 98% and an LCP/RCP flux ratio of 1.1, which is normalized away. Two artifacts could produce a "CD" that depends on temperature:
   - Any residual linear component that differs between the two modes, multiplied by magnetic linear dichroism. Magnetic linear dichroism switches on at TN and could be strong in this hexagonal polar crystal.
   - A change in beam position or footprint between the modes.

   The paramagnetic null does not exclude the first, because linear dichroism is also absent above TN. The authors should:
   - measure LH and LV RIXS at 32 K and above TN in the m' geometry and estimate the leakage into RCP minus LCP;
   - record fluorescence-yield XAS with both helicities in the same geometry at 32 K. XMCD is forbidden by the 6'mm' symmetry (p. 4), so a vanishing XMCD would both validate the helicity switching and exclude a net moment from ferrimagnetic surface regions or impurities.

8. Reproducibility and sample characterization are thin.
   - All data appear to come from one crystal (Fig. S1).
   - The m and m' geometries require rotating the sample in azimuth, so the two geometries probe different spots and possibly different domain populations.
   - TN = 60 K is taken from the literature. The magnetization or specific heat of this crystal is not shown, although Fe2Mo3O8 is sensitive to stoichiometry.
   - The sample, an insulator, is mounted with carbon tape on a Cu plate. Beam heating is not discussed, yet the CD is already weak at 55 K (Fig. S3d).
   - Intensities shift between temperatures. Feature B drops from about 21 at 70 K to about 15 at 80 K (Fig. S4g to S4h), and the elastic peak is about 80 at 50 K but 90 to 97 at the other temperatures (Fig. S3).

   These shifts suggest that normalization or alignment varies by more than the CD itself. The authors should:
   - show M(T) for the measured crystal;
   - estimate the sample temperature under the beam;
   - explain the intensity changes;
   - show the key m'/m contrast on a second crystal or a second mounting.

9. The claim of "active orbital magnetism" (end of the Results section) is drawn from the model alone. The statement that Fe2Mo3O8 is "a rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism" rests on (S_c, L_c) values from the ionic model (Supplement p. 6). No measurement in the paper bears on the orbital moment. The authors should either support it, for example with XMCD sum rules in the field-induced ferrimagnetic phase or with literature g-factor or moment data, or present it as a model prediction.

C. Minor

10. The derivation in Supplement section 5 is heuristic and partly inconsistent:
    - The configuration is written as xy-down, but the down electron is then labelled m_l = -1.
    - Only the t2g electrons are kept.
    - The sign change of l when projected onto the t2g manifold (T–P isomorphism, l_eff = -l) is not included.

    As a result, the stated magnetic moment of the Jeff = 1 state (5/2 µB, attributed to an "anti-parallel" orbital moment) is doubtful: for Fe2+ the real orbital moment is expected to be parallel to the spin. Please check these statements against the full multiplet calculation, or cite a standard treatment (for example Abragam and Bleaney).

11. In the m geometry the relevant symmetry is a glide with a c/2 translation (Fig. 1b, p. 4). The statement that it forbids CD at finite Q_c = 0.65 relies on the RIXS signal being an incoherent sum of local site contributions. Please state this assumption explicitly, give the Q used for the m-plane data in Fig. S2 and the Fig. 2a inset, and note whether the argument holds for any coherent (dispersive) contribution within feature A.

12. Several model details are missing. Please give the Weiss-field value and the final-state broadening used in Figs. 1f, 2b and 2c, and the incident energy used in the simulations. Please also explain why the core-hole broadening Gamma = 0.3 eV is appropriate.

13. The argument that collective modes are "unlikely" to account for the CD (p. 5) rests on fits of spectra measured only along (0,0,L), with RCP only, and with error bars of about ±10 meV (Fig. S5d). The CD itself was measured at in-plane momentum components of only 0.03 to 0.05 r.l.u. Please soften this statement or support it with CD measured at larger in-plane Q.

14. Wording:
    - The abstract defines altermagnets as breaking "symmetry under the combined operations of parity and time reversal". This is imprecise; please use the spin-group definition.
    - The term "standard model of altermagnetism" is used without a definition.

15. Code availability is "upon reasonable request". Since the conclusions depend on the multiplet simulations, please deposit the input parameters and Hamiltonian files together with the Zenodo data.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one figure that summarizes the experimental CD: difference spectra with error bars for A and B, in both geometries, and the integrated asymmetry against temperature, overlaid on an order-parameter curve for the measured crystal.
2. Measure RCP/LCP incident-energy maps across the L3 edge (CD versus incident energy and energy loss). The different resonance energies of the Td and Oh sites would make the site attribution independent of the energy-loss assignment, and would show whether the CD in the A region follows the Oh or the Td resonance.
3. Measure the azimuthal dependence of the CD between neighbouring m' and m planes, and compare it with the model. A full azimuthal pattern, including any sign reversal between symmetry-related m' planes, is a much stronger symmetry fingerprint than two azimuths, and it also constrains the domain population.
4. Control the domains, for example by magnetoelectric cooling, and show that the CD reverses with the domain. This would turn the present signature into a direct proof that the dichroism is odd in the Néel vector.
