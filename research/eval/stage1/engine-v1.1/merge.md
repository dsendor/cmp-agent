Below are {n} independent reviews of the same condensed matter physics paper, written by separate runs of the same reviewer. Merge them into one review.

- Keep every distinct concern raised by any review. Two concerns are the same when they ask the authors for the same change; merge those into one, keeping the most specific wording and every concrete detail (figures, parameters, measurements) from each.
- Drop praise, summaries and repeated text.
- Put the concerns in priority order under three headings:
  A. Must address: a referee at a top-tier journal would require this before accepting the paper, because without it a main claim is unsupported or likely wrong. Many good papers have no item here, and a paper ready for publication has none. Do not fill this heading to match the others.
  B. Should address: a claim is weaker, narrower or less certain without this; the main conclusions stand without it.
  C. Minor.
  Number the concerns 1, 2, 3 and so on across all three headings, most important first. A concern raised by several reviews is not automatically more important; judge each by what it means for the paper's claims. Put a concern under A only if at least one review put it there and you agree a referee would require it; otherwise use B.
- Write each concern as a self-contained paragraph: what is missing or wrong, where in the paper, why it matters, and what the authors should do.
- End each concern with a line "Raised by:" naming every review that raised it, by its label (for example "Raised by: r1, r3").

- After the concerns, one line: "Verdict: ready", "Verdict: minor revision" or "Verdict: major revision", for this version as a referee at a top-tier journal would recommend it. Major revision means at least one A item; ready means the B and C items are optional.

Output only the merged review.
