Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use 6 eV TR-ARPES with sample bias, which covers the whole Brillouin zone in one acquisition, to follow the off-nodal and near-antinodal tomographic density of states (TDOS) of optimally doped Bi2212 after 300 meV mid-infrared excitation. At low fluence (~74 µJ/cm²) the TDOS stays particle-hole symmetric and Dynes-like. At high fluence (~138 µJ/cm²) the ω > 0 coherent weight is transiently suppressed for ~1–2 ps while the gap minimum stays at E_F, and symmetry returns by ~3.5 ps. The authors call this a "light-induced asymmetric pseudogap below T_c". They argue from it, and from a phenomenological 4×4 self-energy model, that superconductivity and the pseudogap compete once long-range coherence sets in. Access to the unoccupied near-antinodal states on ultrafast timescales is valuable and novel. However, the step from "transient particle-hole asymmetry at high fluence" to "competition between SC and pseudogap, challenging the phase-coherence paradigm" currently depends on two fluences, one sample and a flexible model. Several plausible alternatives have not been excluded.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A technically strong experiment. All three momentum cuts (plus a near-nodal one) are recorded in one interleaved pump-probe acquisition with the same geometry. This removes a common source of error when comparing momenta in TR-ARPES.
- The low-fluence data are an effective internal control. The same cuts, analysis chain and Dynes form give symmetric spectra at all delays, so the asymmetry is not an obvious artifact of the cut geometry or the Fermi-Dirac division alone.
- The unoccupied side of the near-antinodal spectrum is accessed directly, and the near-nodal cut shows no asymmetry (Fig. S3a,d). This is the momentum dependence expected if the effect is tied to the pseudogap.
- The comparison at matched nodal electronic temperature (Fig. 4, Fig. S6) is a sensible first attempt to separate pair-breaking from heating.
- The phenomenological self-energy (Eq. S14) is explicit. It reduces to the standard SC and PG forms in limits, and the parameters are tabulated (Table II).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The high-fluence state transiently exceeds T_c, so the "below T_c" headline and the reading of the asymmetric state are not established.** The text (p. 6) says that in HF, T_e "marginally exceeds T_c (~5%) for the first ~0.5 ps", and Fig. 2c shows HF T_e peaking near 97–98 K. The asymmetric spectra are at 0.6 ps and 1 ps (Fig. 3c,f). At 0.6 ps the HF T_e in Fig. 2c is still roughly 90 K, i.e. at T_c and not "well below" it as the abstract states. The simplest reading is therefore conventional: the HF pulse briefly drives the electronic system through T_c, long-range order collapses, and the order parameter recovers on its own (slow) timescale. Meanwhile the electrons cool below T_c, so the spectrum at 0.6–2 ps reflects a not-yet-recovered condensate in a ~80–90 K electronic bath, i.e. the equilibrium pseudogap near T_c. On this reading nothing specific to "light-induced" or "below T_c" is needed. The LF/HF comparison cannot separate this from the authors' picture, because only HF ever crosses T_c.
   *What to do:* measure a fluence series between LF and HF, including at least one fluence where the peak T_e (with error bar) stays clearly below T_c. Show whether the asymmetry (e.g. the Fig. 4e slope ratio at ~1 ps) appears there, and how it scales with fluence and with peak Γ_p. Without this, the abstract and title should be reworded to say that the asymmetry follows excitation that transiently brings T_e to or above T_c.

2. **A superconducting-only explanation with strong pair-breaking on a particle-hole-asymmetric band has not been excluded.** The asymmetry is judged against a Dynes function (Eq. 2), which assumes a flat, symmetric normal-state DOS. Near the antinode of Bi2212 the normal-state band DOS is strongly asymmetric because of the nearby van Hove singularity. The bilayer-split antibonding band also lies close to E_F, yet the model explicitly drops t⊥ = 108 meV (Sec. VIII A). Cut 1 is also not perpendicular to the Fermi surface (Fig. 2a), even though Eq. 2 requires that for a Dynes description. As Γ_p grows and the coherence peaks fill, the TDOS should revert toward the asymmetric band DOS even for a purely superconducting, phase-coherent state. Fig. S4 shows that HF Γ_p at cuts 1 and 2 is 2–3 times the LF value at the delays where asymmetry appears. The "SC-only" theory curve in Fig. 5b and Figs. S9–S10, however, appears to use the equilibrium Γ_p = 10 meV (Table II).
   *What to do:* compute the SC-only TDOS (H_0 = 0) with Γ_p and Δ taken from the HF Norman fits at 0.6–1 ps (Fig. S4), using the real tight-binding dispersion along cuts 1 and 2. If possible include bilayer splitting and the antibonding band with appropriate matrix elements. Show that this cannot reproduce the HF 1 ps TDOS. Also report a Dynes fit with an energy-dependent normal-state DOS taken from the 130 K data, to see whether the "red shadows" in Fig. 3c,f survive.

3. **A spatially or depth-inhomogeneous mixture of superconducting and normal/pseudogap regions has not been tested.** The authors reject a "simple weighted sum" (p. 8; Figs. S9a, S10a). However, what they test is a sum of *self-energies*. Inhomogeneity would instead give a weighted sum of *spectra*. Such inhomogeneity could come from a lateral fluence profile in the probed spot, from the pump absorption depth versus the probe escape depth, or from local T_c variations in Bi2212 at a fluence near threshold. The Discussion's statement that "the ~1 ps response time rules out potential inhomogeneous optical excitation effects" (p. 9) is not explained, and it is unclear why a response time would exclude spatial inhomogeneity.
   *What to do:* fit the HF 1 ps TDOS (cuts 1 and 2) with a linear combination of measured spectra: the HF 3.5 ps TDOS (or the τ < 0 TDOS) and the 130 K TDOS, rescaled where needed for the thermal/Fermi-function window. Report the residuals next to those of the interplay model. Give the pump and probe spot sizes, the pump absorption depth at 4 µm, and an estimate of the fluence spread within the probed area.

4. **The robustness of the ω > 0 signal, which carries the whole result, against the analysis chain is not shown.** The asymmetry lives at ω ≈ +5 to +25 meV. There the data are obtained by Lucy-Richardson deconvolution (15 iterations, chosen by eye), subtraction of a constant background taken at 100 ± 30 meV, and division by a Fermi function at the nodally determined T_e, extending to 4.5 k_B T_e. Each of these steps amplifies noise and systematic error exponentially on the unoccupied side. An error of a few kelvin in T_e, or a delay-dependent background (e.g. pump-induced or space-charge electrons, which can differ between LF and HF), would change the ω > 0 intensity selectively at early delays. No error bars appear in Figs. 3, 4a or 5b, and none on T_e in Fig. 2c.
   *What to do:* (i) propagate counting statistics through deconvolution and Fermi-Dirac division and show error bands on the TDOS curves; (ii) show that the HF/LF difference at 0.6–1 ps survives when T_e is varied by its uncertainty, when the deconvolution is skipped or the iteration number varied, and when the background is evaluated differently; (iii) show a division-free comparison, e.g. symmetrized EDCs or the raw (undivided) HF − LF difference at matched T_e (Fig. 4a/c), to demonstrate that the HF excess suppression is present before division.

B. Should address

5. **"Competition", "suppression of the pseudogap by long-range SC" and "challenge the paradigm" go beyond the evidence.** The data show (a) symmetric spectra when SC is intact, which agrees with earlier equilibrium ARPES, and (b) asymmetric spectra after strong pair breaking. The authors themselves note (p. 9) that restoring particle-hole symmetry "does not necessarily imply any changes in the pole responsible for the pseudogap". This undercuts the statement that "the pseudogap is suppressed" in the abstract and Discussion. Long-range phase coherence is never measured directly. It is inferred from the spectral shape, which makes the argument partly circular. Phase-fluctuation or preformed-pair pictures, in which the transient gap stays pinned at E_F (as observed), are dismissed without a quantitative comparison.
   *What to do:* temper the abstract and Discussion, or add a quantitative test showing that a phase-disordered pairing model with the measured Γ_p cannot produce the observed asymmetry while keeping the minimum at E_F.

6. **The phenomenological model is flexible, and its agreement with the data is shown only qualitatively.** The model has nine parameters (Table II). The pseudogap part (fixed pole ω_0 = 20 meV, d-wave H_k, frequency cutoff with ω_H = 50 meV, β_H = 4) is adjusted by hand to three equilibrium cuts. The transient curve then needs two further ad hoc scalings (80% Δ_0, 90% H_0). The auxiliary-fermion pairing is simply set to Δ̄_k = Δ_k, a choice that changes the result whenever Δ and H are both nonzero. The calculation also omits temperature, the Fermi function, energy resolution and bilayer splitting. Experiment and theory are plotted in separate panels with arbitrary y-axes (Figs. 5, S7), so the "remarkable agreement" cannot be judged.
   *What to do:* overlay theory and data on a common scale and report residuals. Show the χ² landscape over (Δ_0 scale, H_0 scale) to establish whether "only the combination" is unique. Test sensitivity to Δ̄ (e.g. Δ̄ = 0). Explain why the PG pole sits at +20 meV, the same energy as the measured 130 K PG minimum, and whether that is an input or an output.

7. **The slope-ratio metric (Fig. 4e, Fig. S6c) lacks error bars, and its fit windows change between figures.** The main text uses [−17, −7] and [7, 17] meV, while the Fig. S6 caption uses [−20, −4] and [4, 20] meV. The LF ratio scatters between roughly 0.6 and 1.6, comparable to the HF deviation. Points at τ < 0.5 ps are off scale and unexplained.
   *What to do:* give uncertainties for each point, use the same windows throughout or show that the result does not depend on the window, and quantify how significant the HF/LF difference is (e.g. averaged over 0.5–1.5 ps).

8. **Matched nodal T_e does not mean a matched state, so the "non-thermal" conclusion needs qualification.** Fig. 4 compares LF at 0.5 ps with HF at 1.1 ps. These differ in delay, in thermal history (HF has passed through T_c), in Γ_p (Fig. S4: roughly 5 vs 12 meV at cut 1), and probably in lattice/bosonic temperature. Fig. 2c also shows different pre-pump temperatures (~33 K LF vs ~38 K HF). That the asymmetry is "not driven by the electronic temperature" (p. 9) follows only in the narrow sense that nodal T_e is not the controlling variable. The antinodal quasiparticle distribution may also not be thermalized at the nodal T_e, given the delayed Γ_p response.
   *What to do:* state the Fig. 4 conclusion as "not determined by the nodal T_e alone". Check that the Fermi function at the antinode is consistent with the nodal T_e, e.g. from the occupied-side tail at cut 2.

9. **Number of samples and reproducibility.** The paper does not say how many samples or cleaves were measured, or whether the HF asymmetry was reproduced on more than one. The sample is cleaved and measured at 10 K under continuous pumping, which raises the question of surface aging over the "single iterative pump-probe acquisition".
   *What to do:* state the number of samples and cleaves, show the key HF/LF comparison (Fig. 4a or 4e) on a second sample, and show that τ < 0 spectra at the start and end of the acquisition agree.

10. **The equilibrium pseudogap reference is inconsistent and not matched in temperature.** The main text and Fig. 5 use 130 K, whereas Sec. VIII C and Fig. S8 state that the PG model was tuned to data at "~150 K". Comparing a 130 K spectrum with a transient state at T_e ≈ 85 K mixes in different thermal broadening and a different accessible ω > 0 window. This matters for the claim that the two curves "share similar spectral weight at ω ~ 20 meV" while having minima at different energies (pp. 7–8).
   *What to do:* clarify which temperature was used, and if possible add an equilibrium spectrum just above T_c (~95–100 K) as a closer reference.

11. **Uncertainty on T_e and consistency of the quoted values.** Fig. 4a states T_e ≈ 78 K for HF at 1.1 ps. Reading Fig. 2c, the HF curve at ~1.1 ps appears to be nearer 80–84 K. The "~5% above T_c" is comparable to the plausible uncertainty of a Fermi-edge fit with 18 meV resolution after deconvolution.
   *What to do:* add T_e error bars to Fig. 2c and state how the matched-T_e delays in Fig. 4 were chosen and how well they match.

C. Minor

12. Fig. 5: the caption describes panels a (top/bottom) and c, but the figure shows a and b with experiment on the left and theory on the right. The x-axis reads "E − E_F (eV)" where meV is meant.
13. Fig. 2 caption: "red arrow in panel c" should be panel b. The SI (p. 19) refers to "Fig. 1c of the main text" for T_e, which should be Fig. 2c.
14. Fig. 3 caption says EDCs are plotted up to 4.5 k_B T_e, while the Fig. S3 caption says k_B T_e. Please reconcile.
15. Notation: Eq. S8 uses Γ_sf where elsewhere Γ_pg is used. Eq. 4 uses Δ_k for the auxiliary-fermion pairing, while Eq. S12 uses Δ̄_k. Eq. S11 uses σ̄ for opposite spin, which clashes with the bar used for auxiliary fermions.
16. The 18 meV energy resolution and 300 fs time resolution are coarse relative to the 25 meV gap at cut 1 and the 0.6 ps delay. "High-resolution" in the abstract should be qualified, and the effect of the 300 fs resolution on the 0.6 ps spectra should be noted.
17. Give the absorbed (not only incident) fluence, the pump/probe spot sizes and polarization geometry, and state whether the 48 V bias affects momentum calibration and the k-integration windows of cuts 1 and 2.
18. Typos: "devonvoluted" (p. 10), "dahsed" (Fig. S3), "obtain" → "obtained" (p. 11), stray comma in the acknowledgements, "whe Fig. S8b" (p. 31). Refs. 36/59/60 duplicate [45]/[57].

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. A fluence scan (four or five fluences spanning LF to beyond HF), plotting the time-averaged slope-ratio deviation against peak T_e and peak Γ_p. This would show directly whether the asymmetry has a threshold tied to crossing T_c or to pair-breaking strength, which is the paper's central distinction.
2. A unified quantitative comparison: one figure overlaying the measured TDOS (with error bands) with three candidate descriptions on the same scale. These are (i) SC-only with the measured Γ_p on the real dispersion, (ii) a linear mixture of measured SC and PG spectra, and (iii) the interplay model. Residuals for each should be given.
3. Equilibrium temperature-dependent TDOS on the same sample at 60, 80, 90, 95 and 100 K. This would show whether any equilibrium state below T_c ever shows the asymmetry, and would give the natural reference for the transient spectra.
4. Report the theory with the bilayer band structure and the experimental resolution and Fermi window applied, so that the curves can be compared directly with the deconvolved data.
