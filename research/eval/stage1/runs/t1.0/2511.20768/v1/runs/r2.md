Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors use 6 eV time-resolved ARPES with sample biasing to reach the near-antinodal region of optimally doped Bi2212 (Tc ≈ 91 K). They report that a high pump fluence (HF, ~138 µJ/cm²) produces a transient particle-hole-asymmetric "pseudogap-like" tomographic DOS (TDOS) at 0.6–1 ps, while a low fluence (LF, ~74 µJ/cm²) leaves the TDOS symmetric. They argue that this happens at nodal electronic temperatures below Tc, that the asymmetry is non-thermal, and that a phenomenological self-energy fits it only if superconductivity and the pseudogap interact. If the asymmetry is shown to be robust, this would be one of the few momentum-resolved views of the unoccupied antinodal states while superconductivity is suppressed below Tc, and it bears directly on the competition between superconductivity and the pseudogap. The experimental strategy (full-zone mapping at 6 eV in a single pump-probe acquisition) is also of technical interest.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- Technical advance: all three momentum cuts (node, cut 1, cut 2) are taken in one acquisition without moving the sample (p. 4). This removes a common source of systematic error when comparing cuts.
- The experiment is well designed in that LF and HF are compared at matched nodal electronic temperature (Fig. 4a–d, Fig. S6). This is the right kind of control against a purely thermal explanation.
- The effect is reported along two cuts (Fig. 3c,f; Figs. S2, S6). It is seen both in the TDOS and in the EDCs at kF (Fig. S3), and it is absent on the near-nodal cut (Fig. S3a,d), as one would expect for a pseudogap-related effect.
- The phenomenological Nambu/auxiliary-fermion self-energy (Eqs. 4–7, S12–S14) is a transparent framework that reduces to the standard SC and PG forms (Eq. S15). It could be useful to others.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The ω > 0 asymmetry depends on Fermi–Dirac division using a temperature measured at the node, not at the cuts where the effect is seen.** The whole claim rests on weight above E_F (Fig. 3c,f red shading; Fig. 4a; Fig. 4e slope window [7,17] meV). That weight is recovered only by dividing by f(ω, T_e) with T_e taken from the momentum-integrated nodal EDC (Methods, p. 11; Fig. 2c). The asymmetry is very sensitive to this choice. At ω = 15 meV, f(80 K) ≈ 0.10 while f(70 K) ≈ 0.08. A ~10 K overestimate of the local temperature therefore suppresses the divided ω > 0 intensity by ~25%, which is comparable to the suppression shaded in Fig. 3c. The effect is strongest at 0.6–1 ps in HF, which is exactly when the distribution is furthest from equilibrium and the gapped antinodal states are most likely to have a different effective temperature or a non-thermal occupation (quasiparticle recombination bottleneck) than the gapless node. The matched-T_e comparison in Fig. 4 does not remove this worry, because the error would be specific to HF at early delays.
   - The authors should extract an effective temperature or occupation directly on cuts 1 and 2. For example, they could fit the Fermi-edge region away from the gap, or use the ratio of spectra at ±ω with a symmetry-independent method.
   - They should show how the slope ratio in Fig. 4e and the shaded area in Fig. 3c change for T_e varied by ±5 and ±10 K.
   - They should state what local T_e would be needed to remove the asymmetry entirely.
   - Without this, a division artifact cannot be excluded.

2. **Deconvolution and background subtraction act on the same 0–20 meV window that carries the effect.** The spectra are Lucy–Richardson deconvolved with 15 iterations and an 18 meV resolution (Methods, p. 10). A constant background, evaluated at 100 ± 30 meV above E_F, is then subtracted (p. 19; Fig. S1c), and only after that are the spectra divided by f. The resolution is comparable to the gap (25 meV on cut 1) and to the full slope-fit windows ([-17,-7] and [7,17] meV). Iterative deconvolution of a spectrum cut off by a steep Fermi edge can produce ringing and redistribute weight across E_F. A pump-induced background (pump-assisted photoemission, scattered or multiphoton signal) would scale with fluence and differ between LF and HF. Because f is ~0.05–0.1 in the window, subtracting a background that is wrong by a few percent of the signal changes the divided ω > 0 intensity by a large fraction.
   - The authors should show that the LF/HF asymmetry survives with no deconvolution. A cleaner approach is forward modelling: convolve [Dynes or the model TDOS] × f(T_e) with R(ω), fit the raw spectra, and test whether a symmetric DOS is rejected for HF at 0.6–1 ps but not for LF.
   - They should report the background level for LF and HF at each delay, and the sensitivity of Fig. 4e to the background (±1σ).
   - They should show the raw, undivided HF and LF spectra at matched T_e (the data of Fig. 4a before processing). Those spectra should already differ above E_F if the effect is real.

3. **The headline interpretation (competition switched on by long-range phase coherence; ground state set by competition) goes beyond what is measured.**
   - The abstract and Discussion (pp. 2, 8–10) say the data "challenge the paradigm" that superconductivity arises by establishing phase coherence in the pseudogap, and that the competition "is controlled by the presence or absence of long ranged phase coherence."
   - No observable in the paper measures phase coherence or superfluid density. Loss of long-range order at 0.6–1 ps and its restoration by 3.5 ps are inferred from the very asymmetry being interpreted. The Bogoliubov peak at about −40 meV remains "evident at all pump-probe delays and shows no significant energy shift" (p. 6), so pairing amplitude clearly persists.
   - What the data show is narrower: under HF excitation, a transient particle-hole asymmetry appears and later disappears while the gap magnitude stays nearly unchanged. The alternative that the pseudogap merely becomes visible when the coherent quasiparticle weight is reduced (no competition required) is not excluded. Fig. S9c–e shows that changing either coupling strength by hand changes the asymmetry.
   - The authors should either add an independent probe of coherence under the same conditions (e.g., cite or perform time-resolved THz/optical conductivity at matched absorbed fluence) or reword the claims. "Competition" and "phase coherence" would then become an interpretation consistent with the data, not a demonstration.

**B. Should address**

4. **The phenomenological model has many hand-set parameters, and its success does not single out "interplay."**
   - Table II has nine parameters. Several of them are adjusted by eye:
     - the pole position ω0 = 20 meV;
     - H0 = 150 meV;
     - a d-wave H_k chosen because the literature form "does not reproduce the experimental measurements" (p. 28);
     - the cutoff β_H, ω_H;
     - the auxiliary-fermion pairing Δ̄_k, simply set equal to Δ_k (p. 32).
   - The transient curve uses 80% Δ0 and 90% H0, chosen to match (p. 33). No goodness-of-fit, residuals or parameter uncertainties are given. The comparison in Fig. 5b (right) and Fig. S7b is visual only.
   - The "Sum" alternative (Fig. S9a/S10a) adds the self-energies at full strength. A fair null model would use a sum with reduced, freely fitted amplitudes and broadening (including a large Γp), fitted to the same data with the same number of parameters.
   - The model's pseudogap curve for the node (Fig. 5a right; Fig. S8b top) shows a sharp peak above E_F that is absent in the data.
   - The authors should fit the HF 1 ps TDOS quantitatively (χ² or residuals) with (i) the interplay model and (ii) a reduced-amplitude sum and/or a Dynes form with energy-dependent broadening. They should report the parameter uncertainties and explain the physical meaning of Δ̄_k.

5. **Only two fluences, an unstated number of samples, and a claim about "intermediate fluence" that is not supported by data.**
   - The abstract states that the pseudogap coexists with SC features "at intermediate times or at intermediate fluence," but only LF and HF are presented.
   - A threshold-like onset of the asymmetry as a function of fluence (at fixed T_e or fixed Γp) would greatly strengthen the claim that the effect is tied to loss of long-range order rather than growing smoothly with excitation. It would also connect to known fluence thresholds for SC suppression in Bi2212.
   - The number of samples and cleaves, and whether the HF asymmetry was reproduced on more than one sample, are not stated.
   - The authors should add a fluence series (at least 3–4 fluences, including fluences between 74 and 138 µJ/cm²) and state the sample statistics, or remove the "intermediate fluence" statement.

6. **The slope-ratio metric has no uncertainties, and its windows differ between cuts.** Fig. 4e and Fig. S6c show no error bars. The LF trace itself scatters between roughly 0.6 and 1.3 (Fig. 4e), which is comparable to the HF deviation at some delays. The fit windows are [7,17] meV for cut 1 (Fig. 4e) but [4,20] meV for cut 2 (Fig. S6c), with no reason given. The authors should give error bars from fit uncertainties or bootstrapping over the counting statistics, use the same windows on both cuts or show that the result does not depend on the window, and give a statistical significance for the HF–LF difference at 0.5–1.5 ps.

7. **"Electronic temperatures well below Tc" is overstated for the delays that show the effect.**
   - Fig. 2c shows HF T_e peaking at ~97 K and still ≈ 88–92 K at 0.6 ps. The text acknowledges that T_e "marginally exceeds Tc (~5%) for the first ~0.5 ps" (p. 6).
   - The asymmetry is reported at 0.6 and 1 ps (Fig. 3c,f), when T_e ≈ 85–90 K, close to Tc and inside the fluctuation regime of OP Bi2212.
   - The matched-T_e comparison (Fig. 4a, 78 K) is the relevant evidence, and it is a single pair of delays.
   - The authors should temper the title and abstract wording, or show the matched-T_e comparison at several temperatures (e.g., 70, 75, 80 K) with the asymmetry quantified.

8. **The TDOS along a finite cut need not be particle-hole symmetric in the normal state, and bilayer splitting is ignored.** The TDOS integrates along a momentum cut (red arrows in Fig. 3a,d) near the antinode. There the band is curved and close to its saddle point, and the bonding and antibonding bands of Bi2212 are split (t⊥ = 108 meV, which the authors explicitly drop; p. 27). An antibonding-band contribution near E_F on cut 2, or a band-structure asymmetry of the cut-integrated DOS, could change how weight above E_F responds when the gap fills and the broadening grows. The authors should:
   - state which band(s) are seen at 6 eV on cuts 1 and 2;
   - show the normal-state (above-Tc but below-T*) and model TDOS of the bare dispersion along each cut, to establish the symmetric baseline;
   - check that including t⊥ does not change the conclusions of the simulations.

9. **The comparison with the 130 K pseudogap relies on a separate equilibrium measurement whose conditions are not documented.**
   - Fig. 5a,b compare the transient spectra with a TDOS measured at 130 K. The SI then describes the pseudogap data as taken "~150 K" (p. 31). Which is correct?
   - It is not stated whether the 130 K data come from the same sample, cleave and alignment, or how the chemical potential and k_F were referenced at 130 K.
   - The statement that the light-induced and equilibrium PG "share similar spectral weight at ω ~ 20 meV" (p. 7) is not quantified, and it depends on normalisation choices that are not given.
   - The equilibrium low-temperature spectrum is modelled with H0 = 0 (pure SC). Many ARPES studies cited by the authors ([7, 17, 21]) report that the pseudogap persists below Tc near the antinode. The full-strength SC+PG model (black curves, Fig. S9c–e) should be compared directly with the τ < 0 data, to show whether a non-zero H0 is excluded at equilibrium.

10. **Pair-breaking rates quoted in the main text come from fits that the SI calls unstable.** On p. 6 the text states that in HF "the pair-breaking scattering rate reaches a value of ~15–20 meV for approximately 1–2 ps." The SI says the HF Norman fits are unstable because Γs broadens and Δ fills (p. 21), and Γs is fixed from the nodal cut. The comparison with Kondo et al. (saturation above 1.1 Tc) is then used as an argument. The authors should give uncertainties on Γp(τ) for HF, show how strongly Γp and Γs are correlated in the fit, and soften the quantitative statement accordingly.

**C. Minor**

11. **Excitation conditions and inhomogeneity.** Pump and probe spot sizes, the pump penetration depth at 4 µm compared with the 6 eV probing depth, and how the fluence was calibrated are not given. The statement that "the ~1 ps response time rules out potential inhomogeneous optical excitation effects" (p. 9) does not follow: lateral or depth inhomogeneity is set by geometry, not by timescales. Please give the spot-size ratio and the absorbed energy density, and estimate the spread in excitation across the probed volume. Note also that the pre-time-zero T_e differs between HF and LF (~40 K vs ~34 K in Fig. 2c), so the average heating differs.

12. **Sample bias.** Biasing the sample to 48 V (Methods) changes the momentum mapping and can couple to pump-induced surface photovoltage. Please describe how the momentum and energy were calibrated under bias, and whether the <2 meV transient shifts corrected in Fig. S1 were checked for fluence dependence.

13. **Figure, caption and text inconsistencies.**
   - Fig. 2 caption: "red arrow in panel c" should be panel b.
   - Fig. 4 caption: "~78 K in c and ~63 K in d" refers to panels a and b.
   - Fig. 5 caption: it describes "top/bottom" panels and a panel c, but the figure has left/right halves and no panel c. The x-axis of Fig. 5b reads "E − E_F (eV)" where meV is meant.
   - SI p. 19 refers to "Fig. 1c of the main text" for T_e; this should be Fig. 2c.
   - Fig. 3 caption says the EDCs are plotted up to 4.5 k_B T_e, while Fig. S3 says k_B T_e.
   - Eq. S8 uses Γ_sf, but the text and Table II use Γ_pg.
   - Typos: "devonvoluted" (p. 10), "dahsed" (Fig. S3), "constraint" for "constrain" (p. 26), "whe" (p. 31).

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a robustness figure for the central result. Show the HF/LF matched-T_e comparison (Fig. 4a) and the slope ratio (Fig. 4e) for (i) raw undeconvolved data analysed by forward modelling, (ii) T_e varied by ±5–10 K, and (iii) background varied within its uncertainty, all with error bars.
2. Add a fluence series between LF and HF, plotting the asymmetry metric against fluence and against Γp. This would test whether the effect has a threshold tied to loss of superconducting order.
3. Fit the transient TDOS quantitatively with the interplay model and with fairly parametrised alternatives (a reduced-amplitude sum, or Dynes with energy-dependent broadening), and report fit statistics and parameter uncertainties.
4. Narrow the interpretive language in the abstract and Discussion to what the spectra show (a transient, fluence-dependent particle-hole asymmetry consistent with an interacting SC+PG self-energy), unless an independent measure of phase coherence is added.
