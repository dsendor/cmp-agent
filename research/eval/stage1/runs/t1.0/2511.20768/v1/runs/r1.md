Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use 6 eV TR-ARPES with sample bias and deflector optics to reach the off-nodal (cut 1) and near-antinodal (cut 2) regions of optimally doped Bi2212 while measuring all cuts in one acquisition. They report that a 300 meV mid-infrared pump at high fluence (~138 uJ/cm2) makes the Fermi-function-divided TDOS particle-hole asymmetric for about 1-2 ps, with weight suppressed for omega > 0. Low fluence (~74 uJ/cm2) leaves it symmetric, and symmetry returns by ~3.5 ps. They read this as a light-induced pseudogap coexisting with superconducting correlations below Tc, and as evidence that the pseudogap and superconductivity compete once long-range order is present. A phenomenological self-energy that couples the two supports this reading. If the observation is robust, a pump-controlled switch of particle-hole symmetry in the unoccupied antinodal states would be a new and useful handle on the pseudogap-superconductivity problem. At present, however, the key signal sits in the energy window where the analysis chain is most fragile, and the strongest conclusions go beyond what the data establish.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Technically notable access: Fermi-surface mapping across all four quadrants with a 6 eV probe, and several momentum cuts recorded at the same time under identical pump-probe geometry. This removes a common source of scan-to-scan systematics in TR-ARPES.
- A clear, directly visible difference between LF and HF spectra (Fig. 3c,f and Fig. S2) that is reproduced on two cuts and in two analyses (TDOS and EDC at kF, Fig. S3). The near-nodal cut shows no asymmetry, a useful internal reference.
- An explicit attempt to separate the effect from electronic temperature by comparing LF and HF at matched nodal Te (Fig. 4, Fig. S6).
- A phenomenological model whose limits reduce to standard SC (Norman) and pseudogap self-energies (Eq. S15). Its parameters are largely anchored to equilibrium ARPES.
- The question addressed, particle-hole asymmetry of the antinodal spectrum when superconductivity is suppressed below Tc, is not accessible in equilibrium and matters to the field.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The asymmetry lies where Fermi-function division amplifies every systematic error, and its robustness is not shown. The entire effect is in the range 0 < omega < ~25 meV (Fig. 3c,f; slope window [7,17] meV in Fig. 4e). There the raw intensity is a small thermal tail, and the TDOS is obtained by a chain of operations (Methods; SI Sec. I):
   - Lucy-Richardson deconvolution of an 18 meV resolution with 15 iterations, chosen by eye;
   - subtraction of a constant background taken at 100 +/- 30 meV above EF;
   - a rigid chemical-potential correction of up to ~2 meV (Fig. S1b,d);
   - division by a Fermi-Dirac function at the nodal Te(tau).

   Each step produces an error that grows like 1/f(omega) above EF:
   - A small error in the background constant c adds c/f(omega), which rises roughly exponentially with omega. This alone can suppress or enhance the omega > 0 TDOS. The background at 100 meV also contains pump-induced hot-electron signal that differs between LF and HF.
   - A 1-2 meV error in EF, or a few-K error in Te, changes the omega > 0 slope by tens of percent at Te ~ 60-80 K.
   - Iterative deconvolution next to a steep edge is known to produce ringing and edge artifacts that depend on iteration number.

   Because the LF/HF contrast is exactly a change of the omega > 0 slope, the authors must show it is not produced by these steps. Concretely:
   - (i) forward-model the raw, non-deconvolved, non-divided EDCs/TDOS with [symmetric DOS x f(omega, Te)] convolved with R(omega), letting Te, EF and background float within their uncertainties, and show that a symmetric DOS is statistically excluded for HF at 0.6-1 ps but not for LF;
   - (ii) show the HF/LF difference as a function of deconvolution iterations (including none), background level and Te +/- its uncertainty;
   - (iii) give the uncertainties of Te(tau) and of the EF correction and propagate them into Fig. 4e.

2. The paper assumes the antinodal electrons share the nodal electronic distribution, which needs testing. The TDOS at cuts 1 and 2 is divided by a Fermi function with the Te fitted at the node (Methods, Eq. 1). Quasiparticle populations and recombination in Bi2212 are known to depend on momentum, and the paper itself argues for a non-thermal boson population at HF (p. 5). If the antinodal occupation at HF lags the nodal one, or is depleted relative to it (for example through a gap-dependent phonon bottleneck), dividing by the nodal f(omega) would produce an apparent suppression of omega > 0 weight with no change in the spectral function. This is the leading alternative explanation for the HF-only asymmetry and is not discussed. The authors should:
   - test the assumption directly, for instance by extracting an effective local temperature from the off-nodal and antinodal spectra in a way that does not presuppose particle-hole symmetry (such as the high-energy tail of the occupied side, or the population above the gap edge);
   - or show that the asymmetry survives plausible differences between nodal and antinodal occupation;
   - and show the analogous analysis on the near-nodal cut at the same HF delays (Fig. S3d shows near-nodal EDCs, but not TDOS slope ratios with uncertainties).

3. The finite momentum window and the band structure could make the TDOS asymmetric with no pseudogap at all. Eq. 1 integrates over a finite k-window (red arrows, Fig. 3a,d). This window looks asymmetric about kF, and cut 2 lies close to the saddle point and band bottom at roughly -40 meV. In the SC state, Bogoliubov back-bending keeps the omega > 0 weight within the window. When the gap is strongly filled at HF (Gamma_p ~ 15-20 meV, Fig. S4), the unoccupied branch disperses out of the window while the occupied weight remains, and the TDOS of the window becomes particle-hole asymmetric even for a purely superconducting spectral function with large pair-breaking. The Dynes function (Eq. 2) assumes a flat normal-state DOS and cannot capture this, so "inconsistent with the Dynes fit" (Fig. 3 caption) is not by itself evidence of a pseudogap. The authors should:
   - (i) show that the HF asymmetry is unchanged when the k-integration window is varied and made symmetric about kF;
   - (ii) compute, with their own Eq. 3, the tight-binding dispersion of Table I and the cut geometry, the TDOS for the pure SC self-energy (H0 = 0) using the HF values of Gamma_p and Gamma_s from Fig. S4, and show it remains symmetric.

   Only then does the asymmetry require the pseudogap term.

4. No equilibrium reference exists at the relevant temperatures, so "light-induced" and "non-thermal" are not established relative to equilibrium. The asymmetry is compared only with the low-temperature state and with a 130 K (or ~150 K, Sec. VIII C) pseudogap spectrum. The transient Te at the relevant delays is 78-90 K, i.e. 0.85-0.99 Tc (Fig. 2c). It is well established that near-antinodal spectra of optimally doped Bi2212 retain pseudogap-related particle-hole asymmetry below Tc in equilibrium (for example, kF/kG misalignment persisting into the SC state). Without equilibrium TDOS at about 40, 60, 75, 85 and 95 K, analysed identically, the reader cannot tell whether the HF transient reproduces the equilibrium state at Te or something new.

   Fig. 4 shows that LF and HF differ at the same nodal Te. However, the two cases also differ in deposited energy, lattice and boson temperature, and pair-breaking rate, so the equilibrium comparison is still needed to support "non-thermal". The authors should add the equilibrium temperature series, pass it through the same deconvolution, background and division pipeline, and overlay its slope ratio on Fig. 4e.

5. Coexistence versus a superposition of separate SC and PG responses is not tested against the data. The text (p. 8) states that a "simple weighted sum" of the 130 K PG curve and the 3.5 ps SC curve gives a minimum shifted from zero and a stronger omega > 0 peak, and cites Figs. S9 and S10. Those figures, however, show a sum of self-energies ("Sum", panel a) and variations of coupling strengths. They do not show a weighted sum of TDOS curves, which is the relevant model for spatial or phase separation (some regions or layers with SC suppressed, others not). The authors should:
   - fit the HF 1 ps TDOS (Fig. 5b, Fig. S7a) with a*TDOS(3.5 ps) + (1-a)*TDOS(130 K) and with the coupled model, and report goodness of fit for both;
   - account for the different Fermi-function division ranges of the 130 K and transient spectra.

   This comparison underpins the central claim that the two orders coexist rather than occupy different regions.

B. Should address

6. The slope ratio carries no uncertainties, and the LF scatter is large. In Fig. 4e the LF slope ratio ranges from about 0.6 to 1.6 over 0-4 ps. The HF deviation (minimum ~0.35-0.5) rests on roughly 4-6 points between 0.5 and 2 ps. Fig. S6c uses different fitting windows ([-20,-4] and [4,20] meV) from the main text ([-17,-7] and [7,17] meV), although it is described as "as defined in the main text". Please:
   - give error bars (from counting statistics and the propagated Te/EF uncertainties of concern 1);
   - show that the result does not depend on the fitting windows;
   - quantify the significance of the HF departure from 1 relative to the LF scatter;
   - use the same windows in both figures or justify the difference.

7. A single sample, a single doping and only two fluences limit the generality of the claim. The paper does not say how many samples or cleaves were measured or whether the HF asymmetry was reproduced. The abstract says the pseudogap coexists with SC features "at intermediate fluence", but only two fluences were measured, and HF is the highest. Please:
   - report reproducibility on a second sample or cleave;
   - add a fluence series that establishes whether the asymmetry has a threshold or grows continuously, and how it scales with Gamma_p;
   - ideally measure an overdoped sample beyond p*, where the pseudogap is absent, as the natural null control. There the same HF analysis should give a symmetric TDOS. A positive result there would point to the artifacts of concerns 1-3.

8. Long-range SC order is inferred, not measured. The discussion (pp. 8-9) attributes the LF/HF difference to the presence or absence of long-range phase coherence and states that competition "sets in only once SC becomes long-range". TR-ARPES at these fluences does not measure phase coherence. Please either:
   - provide or cite a complementary measurement at matched excitation (time-resolved THz or optical superfluid response with the same 4 um pump);
   - or rephrase these statements as hypotheses. The abstract's conclusions that the findings "challenge the paradigm" and "demonstrate that the two states compete" should be tempered accordingly.

9. The phenomenological model has more freedom than the comparison constrains. The model has nine parameters (Table II). The pseudogap pole is held at a fixed omega0 = 20 meV with a d-wave H_k chosen because the literature form "does not reproduce" the data (Sec. VIII B). The auxiliary-fermion gap is set equal to Delta_k by assumption. The transient curve requires an ad hoc 80% SC and 90% PG rescaling (Fig. S9b). The comparison with data (Fig. 5b, Fig. S7) is qualitative overlays in arbitrary units with no fit metric. The theoretical node curve in Fig. 5a shows an omega > 0 peak that is absent in the data. Please:
   - state which parameters were fitted to which data set;
   - show the sensitivity of the transient TDOS to Delta-bar_0 and omega0;
   - report a quantitative comparison;
   - explain why Delta0 = 50 meV is consistent with the measured gaps of ~25 meV and ~40 meV on cuts 1 and 2 (p. 5).

   The statement that the data are reproduced "only when the interplay is considered" (p. 4) holds only within this model family. Models such as YRZ-type ansatzes already include the interplay and should be acknowledged.

10. The electronic temperature is described as "well below Tc", but it is close to Tc at the key delays. The abstract and Fig. 1 caption state the asymmetry appears at electronic temperatures well below Tc. In Fig. 2c, however, HF Te exceeds Tc up to ~0.5 ps and is about 85-90 K at 0.6 ps, one of the two delays highlighted in Fig. 3c,f. The cleanest matched comparison is at 78 K, about 0.86 Tc. Please quote Te with uncertainties at every delay discussed and revise the wording. Superconducting fluctuations are significant in Bi2212 within ~10-20 K of Tc, which bears directly on the interpretation.

11. An independent spectroscopic signature of the pseudogap is missing. The identification of the asymmetric state as a "pseudogap" rests on suppressed omega > 0 weight alone. A pseudogap-specific signature at the same delays would strengthen the assignment and help exclude concerns 2-3:
   - a misalignment between kF and the back-bending momentum kG on cut 1 and cut 2;
   - or a shift of the gap minimum above EF, as at 130 K.

   The paper notes that the transient minimum stays at EF. Please analyse the momentum position of the back-bending in the HF transient.

12. Normalization in direct LF/HF comparisons is not specified. Fig. 4a,b and Fig. 5b overlay TDOS curves in arbitrary units, and the claim of suppressed omega > 0 coherent weight in HF depends on how they were normalized (at -80 meV? total area?). Please state the normalization and show that the conclusion does not depend on it.

13. The 130 K reference spectrum needs more detail. Fig. 5 cites 130 K, while Sec. VIII C and Fig. S8 cite ~150 K. Please specify:
   - the temperature(s) used;
   - whether these were measured on the same sample and cleave;
   - how far above EF the division is trusted at that temperature.

   The comparison of minima (+20 meV versus 0 meV) depends on this.

14. Excitation conditions and the inhomogeneity argument are incomplete. The following are not given:
   - pump and probe spot sizes;
   - how the fluence was calibrated and its uncertainty;
   - pump penetration depth at 4 um compared with the probe escape depth;
   - the pump-induced average heating values.

   The argument that the "~1 ps response time rules out potential inhomogeneous optical excitation effects" (p. 9) is not explained, and 1 ps is only about three times the 300 fs time resolution. Please supply these parameters and either justify the argument or remove it. Please also state whether the 48 V bias combined with the pump changes the momentum or energy calibration differently at LF and HF.

15. The argument against charge order is weak. The conclusion that charge-order correlations are unlikely to cause the pseudogap (p. 9) follows from an assumed absence of CDW-SC competition in Bi2212 and is not tested here. Please present it as speculation or remove it.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a single robustness figure for the HF/LF contrast covering deconvolution iterations, background level, Te/EF uncertainty, k-window choice, and a forward-model fit of the raw spectra. This would address concerns 1-3 together.
2. Measure an equilibrium temperature series (about 40-100 K) on the same cuts and pass it through the identical pipeline. Overlay its slope ratio, and its TDOS at matched Te, on Fig. 4.
3. Extend the experiment to more fluences and to an overdoped (pseudogap-free) sample as a null control. Report reproducibility across samples.
4. Turn the theory comparison into a quantitative fit, including the phase-separated weighted-sum alternative and a pure-SC model with the measured transient Gamma_p. Scale the paradigm-level conclusions to what that comparison supports.
