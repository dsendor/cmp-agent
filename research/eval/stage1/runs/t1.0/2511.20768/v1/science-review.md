# Merged review

## A. Must address

1. **The ω > 0 asymmetry sits where the analysis chain amplifies every systematic error, and its robustness is not shown.** The whole effect lies at 0 < ω ≲ 25 meV (Fig. 3c,f red shading; Fig. 4a; slope window [7,17] meV in Fig. 4e). In that range the raw intensity is a small thermal tail, and the TDOS comes from a chain of operations (Methods, p. 10–11; SI Sec. I): Lucy–Richardson deconvolution of an 18 meV resolution with 15 iterations chosen by eye; subtraction of a constant background taken at 100 ± 30 meV above E_F (Fig. S1c); a rigid chemical-potential correction of up to ~2 meV (Fig. S1b,d); and division by a Fermi–Dirac function at the nodal T_e(τ), extending to 4.5 k_BT_e. Each step introduces an error that grows like 1/f(ω) above E_F. A background error c adds c/f(ω), which rises roughly exponentially with ω; because f ≈ 0.05–0.1 in the window, a background wrong by a few percent of the signal changes the divided ω > 0 intensity by a large fraction. The background at 100 meV also contains pump-induced signal (hot electrons, pump-assisted or multiphoton photoemission, space charge) that differs between LF and HF. A 1–2 meV error in E_F or a few-K error in T_e changes the ω > 0 slope by tens of percent at T_e ~ 60–90 K: at ω = 15 meV, f(80 K) ≈ 0.10 while f(70 K) ≈ 0.08, so a 10 K error changes the divided intensity by ~25%, comparable to the suppression shaded in Fig. 3c. The resolution is comparable to the gap (25 meV on cut 1) and to the slope-fit windows, and iterative deconvolution next to a steep Fermi edge is known to cause ringing and to redistribute weight across E_F in an iteration-dependent way. No error bars are shown in Figs. 3, 4a or 5b, and none on T_e in Fig. 2c. Because the LF/HF contrast is precisely a change of the ω > 0 slope, the authors must show it is not produced by these steps: (i) forward-model the raw, non-deconvolved, undivided spectra as [symmetric DOS (Dynes or model TDOS) × f(ω, T_e)] ⊗ R(ω), letting T_e, E_F and background float within their uncertainties, and show that a symmetric DOS is statistically rejected for HF at 0.6–1 ps but not for LF; (ii) show the HF/LF difference as a function of deconvolution iteration number (including none), background level (±1σ, reported for LF and HF at each delay), and T_e varied by its uncertainty and by ±5 and ±10 K, and state what T_e would be needed to remove the asymmetry; (iii) give the uncertainties of T_e(τ) and of the E_F correction, propagate counting statistics and these uncertainties through to error bands on the TDOS curves and onto Fig. 4e; (iv) show a division-free comparison, e.g. the raw HF and LF spectra (or their difference, or symmetrized EDCs) at matched T_e (the data of Fig. 4a/c before processing), which should already differ above E_F if the effect is real.

2. **The antinodal electrons are assumed to share the nodal electronic distribution, which needs testing.** The TDOS on cuts 1 and 2 is divided by a Fermi function with T_e fitted to the momentum-integrated nodal EDC (Methods, Eq. 1; Fig. 2c). Quasiparticle populations and recombination in Bi2212 depend on momentum, and the paper itself argues for a non-thermal boson population at HF (p. 5) and shows a delayed Γ_p response. If the gapped antinodal occupation at HF lags or is depleted relative to the gapless node (for example through a gap-dependent phonon or recombination bottleneck), dividing by the nodal f(ω) would produce an apparent suppression of ω > 0 weight with no change in the spectral function. This error would be specific to HF at early delays, exactly when the distribution is furthest from equilibrium, so the matched-T_e comparison of Fig. 4 does not remove it. This is the leading alternative explanation for the HF-only asymmetry and is not discussed. The authors should extract an effective temperature or occupation directly on cuts 1 and 2 by a method that does not presuppose particle-hole symmetry (for example the high-energy tail of the occupied side at cut 2, the Fermi-edge region away from the gap, or the population above the gap edge); or show that the asymmetry survives plausible differences between nodal and antinodal occupation. They should also show the TDOS slope-ratio analysis, with uncertainties, on the near-nodal cut at the same HF delays (Fig. S3d shows only EDCs).

3. **A superconducting-only spectrum with strong pair breaking, on the real band structure and finite momentum window, could be particle-hole asymmetric without any pseudogap.** The asymmetry is judged against a Dynes function (Eq. 2), which assumes a flat, symmetric normal-state DOS and a cut perpendicular to the Fermi surface; cut 1 is not perpendicular (Fig. 2a). Near the antinode the band is curved and close to the saddle point and van Hove singularity (band bottom near −40 meV on cut 2), so the normal-state band DOS along the cut is itself asymmetric. The bilayer-split antibonding band also lies close to E_F, but the model drops t⊥ = 108 meV (Sec. VIII A, p. 27). Eq. 1 integrates over a finite k-window (red arrows, Fig. 3a,d) that looks asymmetric about k_F. In the SC state Bogoliubov back-bending keeps the ω > 0 weight inside the window; when the gap fills strongly at HF (Γ_p ≈ 15–20 meV, 2–3 times the LF value, Fig. S4), the unoccupied branch can disperse out of the window while occupied weight remains, and the TDOS reverts toward the asymmetric band DOS even for a purely superconducting, phase-coherent state. "Inconsistent with the Dynes fit" (Fig. 3 caption) is therefore not by itself evidence of a pseudogap. The "SC-only" theory curves in Fig. 5b and Figs. S9–S10 appear to use the equilibrium Γ_p = 10 meV (Table II), not the HF values. The authors should: (i) show that the HF asymmetry is unchanged when the k-integration window is varied and made symmetric about k_F; (ii) state which band(s) are seen at 6 eV on cuts 1 and 2; (iii) compute, with Eq. 3, the tight-binding dispersion of Table I (ideally including bilayer splitting and the antibonding band with appropriate matrix elements) and the actual cut geometry, the TDOS for the pure SC self-energy (H_0 = 0) using the HF Γ_p, Γ_s and Δ from the Norman fits at 0.6–1 ps (Fig. S4), and show it remains symmetric and cannot reproduce the HF 1 ps TDOS; (iv) show the normal-state (above T_c, below T*) and model TDOS of the bare dispersion along each cut to establish the symmetric baseline; (v) report a Dynes fit with an energy-dependent normal-state DOS taken from the 130 K data and see whether the red shadows in Fig. 3c,f survive. Only then does the asymmetry require the pseudogap term.

4. **The electronic temperature reaches or exceeds T_c in HF, so the "well below T_c" headline is not established and a conventional explanation is not excluded.** The abstract and Fig. 1 caption state that the asymmetry appears at electronic temperatures well below T_c. Fig. 2c shows HF T_e peaking near 97–98 K; the text (p. 6) concedes it "marginally exceeds T_c (~5%) for the first ~0.5 ps", and at 0.6 ps, one of the two delays highlighted in Fig. 3c,f, T_e is still about 85–92 K, at T_c and inside the superconducting-fluctuation regime of optimally doped Bi2212 (~10–20 K around T_c). The simplest reading is then conventional: the HF pulse briefly drives the electrons through T_c, long-range order collapses and recovers on its own slower timescale while the electrons cool, so the 0.6–2 ps spectra reflect a not-yet-recovered condensate in an ~80–90 K bath, i.e. the equilibrium pseudogap near T_c. Because only HF ever crosses T_c, the LF/HF comparison cannot separate this from the authors' picture. The cleanest matched comparison (Fig. 4a, 78 K, ~0.86 T_c) is a single pair of delays, and the quoted 78 K for HF at 1.1 ps appears inconsistent with Fig. 2c, which reads ~80–84 K near 1.1 ps. The "~5% above T_c" is comparable to the plausible uncertainty of a Fermi-edge fit at 18 meV resolution after deconvolution. The authors should add T_e error bars to Fig. 2c, quote T_e with uncertainty at every delay discussed, state how the matched-T_e delays in Fig. 4 were chosen and how well they match, and check the Fig. 4 caption values against Fig. 2c. They should measure at least one fluence where peak T_e (with error bar) stays clearly below T_c and show whether the asymmetry (e.g. the Fig. 4e slope ratio near 1 ps) appears there; and show the matched-T_e comparison at several temperatures (e.g. 70, 75, 80 K) with the asymmetry quantified. Otherwise the title and abstract should say that the asymmetry follows excitation that transiently brings T_e to or above T_c.

5. **There is no equilibrium reference at the relevant temperatures, so "light-induced" and "non-thermal" are not established.** The asymmetry is compared only with the low-temperature state and with a 130 K (or ~150 K) pseudogap spectrum, while the transient T_e at the relevant delays is 78–90 K (0.85–0.99 T_c). Near-antinodal spectra of optimally doped Bi2212 are known to retain pseudogap-related particle-hole asymmetry below T_c in equilibrium (e.g. k_F/k_G misalignment persisting into the SC state). Without equilibrium TDOS at, say, 40, 60, 75, 80, 85, 90, 95 and 100 K analysed identically, one cannot tell whether the HF transient reproduces the equilibrium state at T_e or is something new. The matched-nodal-T_e comparison of Fig. 4 does not settle this: LF at 0.5 ps and HF at 1.1 ps differ in delay, in thermal history (HF has passed through T_c), in deposited energy, in Γ_p (Fig. S4: roughly 5 vs 12 meV at cut 1), probably in lattice and bosonic temperature, and even in pre-time-zero temperature (~34 K LF vs ~38–40 K HF in Fig. 2c). The statement that the asymmetry is "not driven by the electronic temperature" (p. 9) therefore holds only in the narrow sense that nodal T_e alone is not the controlling variable. The authors should measure an equilibrium temperature series on the same sample and cuts, pass it through the identical deconvolution, background and division pipeline, overlay its slope ratio and its TDOS at matched T_e on Fig. 4, and restate the Fig. 4 conclusion as "not determined by the nodal T_e alone".

6. **Coexistence has not been tested against a spatially or depth-inhomogeneous mixture of SC and pseudogap regions.** The text (p. 8) rejects a "simple weighted sum" of the 130 K PG curve and the 3.5 ps SC curve, citing Figs. S9a and S10a. Those figures show a sum of self-energies ("Sum") and variations of coupling strengths, not a weighted sum of TDOS spectra, which is the relevant model for phase or spatial separation. Such inhomogeneity could arise from a lateral fluence profile within the probed spot, from the 4 µm pump absorption depth versus the 6 eV probe escape depth, or from local T_c variations in Bi2212 at a fluence near threshold. The Discussion's argument that "the ~1 ps response time rules out potential inhomogeneous optical excitation effects" (p. 9) does not follow, since lateral or depth inhomogeneity is set by geometry, not timescale (see concern 15). The authors should fit the HF 1 ps TDOS on cuts 1 and 2 (Fig. 5b, Fig. S7a) with a·TDOS(3.5 ps or τ < 0) + (1−a)·TDOS(130 K), accounting for the different Fermi-function division ranges of the 130 K and transient spectra, and report goodness of fit next to that of the coupled model. This comparison underpins the central claim that the two orders coexist rather than occupy different regions.

7. **The headline interpretation — competition switched on by long-range phase coherence, challenging the phase-coherence paradigm — goes beyond what is measured.** The abstract and Discussion (pp. 2, 8–10) state that the data "challenge the paradigm" that SC arises by establishing phase coherence in the pseudogap, "demonstrate that the two states compete", that competition "sets in only once SC becomes long-range" and "is controlled by the presence or absence of long ranged phase coherence", and that "the pseudogap is suppressed". No observable in the paper measures phase coherence or superfluid density; TR-ARPES at these fluences does not. Loss of long-range order at 0.6–1 ps and its recovery by 3.5 ps are inferred from the very asymmetry being interpreted, which makes the argument partly circular. The Bogoliubov peak near −40 meV remains "evident at all pump-probe delays and shows no significant energy shift" (p. 6), so pairing amplitude persists, and the authors themselves note (p. 9) that restoring particle-hole symmetry "does not necessarily imply any changes in the pole responsible for the pseudogap". The data show something narrower: under HF excitation a transient particle-hole asymmetry appears and disappears while the gap magnitude stays nearly unchanged. Alternatives are not excluded: the pseudogap may simply become visible when coherent quasiparticle weight is reduced (Fig. S9c–e shows that changing either coupling by hand changes the asymmetry), and phase-fluctuation or preformed-pair pictures, in which the gap stays pinned at E_F as observed, are dismissed without quantitative comparison. The authors should either add an independent probe of coherence under matched excitation (time-resolved THz or optical superfluid response with the same 4 µm pump, measured or cited), or a quantitative test showing that a phase-disordered pairing model with the measured Γ_p cannot produce the asymmetry while keeping the minimum at E_F; or reword the abstract and Discussion so that "competition" and "phase coherence" are presented as an interpretation consistent with the data rather than a demonstration.

## B. Should address

8. **The slope-ratio metric has no uncertainties, its windows change between figures, and the LF scatter is large.** Fig. 4e and Fig. S6c show no error bars. The LF ratio scatters between roughly 0.6 and 1.3–1.6 over 0–4 ps, comparable to the HF deviation at some delays, and the HF minimum (~0.35–0.5) rests on roughly 4–6 points between 0.5 and 2 ps. Points at τ < 0.5 ps are off scale and unexplained. The main text uses [−17,−7] and [7,17] meV (cut 1, Fig. 4e), while Fig. S6c (cut 2) uses [−20,−4] and [4,20] meV although described as "as defined in the main text". The authors should give error bars for each point (from fit uncertainties or bootstrapping over counting statistics, plus the propagated T_e/E_F uncertainties of concern 1), use the same windows throughout or show the result does not depend on the window, explain the off-scale early points, and quantify the significance of the HF departure from 1 relative to the LF scatter (e.g. averaged over 0.5–1.5 ps).

9. **The phenomenological model has more freedom than the comparison constrains, and agreement is shown only qualitatively.** The model has nine parameters (Table II). The pseudogap part is adjusted by hand: the pole at fixed ω_0 = 20 meV (the same energy as the measured 130 K PG minimum — input or output?), H_0 = 150 meV, a d-wave H_k chosen because the literature form "does not reproduce the experimental measurements" (Sec. VIII B, p. 28), and the cutoff ω_H = 50 meV, β_H = 4. The auxiliary-fermion pairing is simply set to Δ̄_k = Δ_k (p. 32), a choice that matters whenever Δ and H are both nonzero. The transient curve needs ad hoc 80% Δ_0 and 90% H_0 scalings (Fig. S9b, p. 33). The calculation omits temperature, the Fermi function, energy resolution and bilayer splitting. Theory and data are shown in separate panels with arbitrary y-axes (Figs. 5b, S7) with no fit metric, residuals or parameter uncertainties, so the "remarkable agreement" cannot be judged. The theoretical node curve (Fig. 5a right; Fig. S8b top) shows a sharp ω > 0 peak absent in the data, and Δ_0 = 50 meV must be reconciled with the measured gaps of ~25 meV and ~40 meV on cuts 1 and 2 (p. 5). The "Sum" alternative (Figs. S9a/S10a) adds self-energies at full strength; a fair null model would use reduced, freely fitted amplitudes and broadening (including a large Γ_p) with the same number of parameters. The equilibrium low-temperature spectrum is modelled with H_0 = 0, although many cited ARPES studies ([7, 17, 21]) report the pseudogap persisting near the antinode below T_c; the full SC+PG model (black curves, Fig. S9c–e) should be compared with the τ < 0 data to see whether nonzero H_0 is excluded at equilibrium. The authors should state which parameters were fitted to which data set; overlay theory (with resolution and Fermi window applied) and data on a common scale with residuals or χ²; fit the HF 1 ps TDOS with the interplay model and with a reduced-amplitude sum and/or Dynes with energy-dependent broadening; show the χ² landscape over (Δ_0 scale, H_0 scale) to establish whether "only the combination" is unique; show sensitivity to Δ̄ (including Δ̄ = 0) and ω_0, and explain the physical meaning of Δ̄_k. The claim that the data are reproduced "only when the interplay is considered" (p. 4) holds only within this model family; YRZ-type ansätze already include the interplay and should be acknowledged.

10. **Only two fluences are measured, and the "intermediate fluence" claim is unsupported.** The abstract says the pseudogap coexists with SC features "at intermediate times or at intermediate fluence", but only LF (~74 µJ/cm²) and HF (~138 µJ/cm², the highest) are presented. Whether the asymmetry has a threshold or grows continuously with excitation is central to the paper's distinction between loss of long-range order and smooth pair breaking, and would connect to known fluence thresholds for SC suppression in Bi2212. The authors should add a fluence series (at least 3–5 fluences, including several between 74 and 138 µJ/cm² and ideally beyond HF), plotting the asymmetry metric against fluence, peak T_e and peak Γ_p, or remove the "intermediate fluence" statement. Ideally they should also measure an overdoped sample beyond p*, where the pseudogap is absent, as a null control: the same HF analysis should give a symmetric TDOS there, and a positive result would point to the artifacts of concerns 1–3.

11. **The number of samples and cleaves, and reproducibility, are not stated.** The paper does not say how many samples or cleaves were measured or whether the HF asymmetry was reproduced on more than one. The sample is cleaved and measured at 10 K under continuous pumping, which raises the question of surface aging over the "single iterative pump-probe acquisition". The authors should state the sample statistics, show the key HF/LF comparison (Fig. 4a or 4e) on a second sample or cleave, and show that τ < 0 spectra at the start and end of the acquisition agree.

12. **The equilibrium pseudogap reference is inconsistently specified and poorly documented.** Fig. 5 and the main text use 130 K, while Sec. VIII C and Fig. S8 (p. 31) state the PG data and model tuning are at "~150 K". It is not stated whether these data come from the same sample, cleave and alignment, how the chemical potential and k_F were referenced at that temperature, or how far above E_F the Fermi-function division is trusted there. Comparing a 130 K spectrum with a transient at T_e ≈ 85 K also mixes different thermal broadening and a different accessible ω > 0 window. The claims that the light-induced and equilibrium PG "share similar spectral weight at ω ~ 20 meV" (p. 7) and that their minima differ (+20 meV vs 0 meV) depend on this and on unstated normalization. The authors should state the temperature(s) used and the measurement conditions, quantify the spectral-weight comparison with its normalization, and if possible add an equilibrium spectrum just above T_c (~95–100 K) as a closer reference.

14. **An independent spectroscopic signature of the pseudogap is missing.** The identification of the asymmetric state as a "pseudogap" rests on suppressed ω > 0 weight alone, while the transient minimum stays at E_F. A pseudogap-specific signature at the same delays, such as a misalignment between k_F and the back-bending momentum k_G on cuts 1 and 2, or a shift of the gap minimum above E_F as at 130 K, would strengthen the assignment and help exclude concerns 2–3. The authors should analyse the momentum position of the back-bending in the HF transient.

15. **Excitation conditions, the inhomogeneity argument and the effect of sample bias are incomplete.** Not given: pump and probe spot sizes and their ratio; polarization geometry; how the fluence was calibrated and its uncertainty; the absorbed (not only incident) fluence and energy density; the pump penetration depth at 4 µm versus the 6 eV probe escape depth; the average heating (the pre-time-zero T_e differs, ~34 K LF vs ~38–40 K HF in Fig. 2c). The argument that the "~1 ps response time rules out potential inhomogeneous optical excitation effects" (p. 9) is unexplained, does not follow since inhomogeneity is set by geometry, and 1 ps is only about three times the 300 fs resolution. The 48 V sample bias (Methods) changes the momentum mapping and can couple to pump-induced surface photovoltage. The authors should supply these parameters, estimate the spread of excitation across the probed volume, justify or remove the 1 ps argument, describe how momentum and energy were calibrated under bias (including the k-integration windows of cuts 1 and 2), and state whether the bias combined with the pump, and the <2 meV transient shifts corrected in Fig. S1, depend on fluence.
