# Merged review

## A. Must address

1. **The Hall response at ν = 2/3 is a ρxy peak with a large ρxx, not a quantized plateau, and the Hall conductance is not 7/3.** (Fig. 4d–f; ED Fig. 4c,i,o,u.) The headline claim rests on ρxy reaching 3h/7e² ≈ 11.06 kΩ "with an accuracy of 99%" (98% for B > 200 mT). Meanwhile ρxx has only a weak "local minimum … down to 3 kΩ" (p. 7) against a background of about 4 kΩ. ED Fig. 4i and the hysteresis loop in Fig. 4f show ρxx ≈ 0.3–0.4 × 3h/7e² ≈ 4–4.7 kΩ. When ρxx/ρxy ≈ 0.27–0.4, ρxy does not measure the Hall conductance. Inverting the tensor with ρxy = 11.06 kΩ and ρxx = 3 kΩ gives σxy = ρxy/(ρxx² + ρxy²) ≈ 2.17 e²/h and σxx ≈ 0.59 e²/h. That lies between 2 and 7/3 and is not 7/3 or any obvious simple fraction. ρxy also shows a sharp peak in ν (Fig. 4d) and a smooth dome in D (Fig. 4e) whose maximum only touches 3h/7e². A trace that sweeps continuously through a value will reach it somewhere. Many non-topological mixtures would give this signature, for example a C = 2 or C = 3 Chern domain in parallel with a resistive region. At B = 0, ρxy is only about 0.7–0.8 × 3h/7e² (Fig. 4f, ED Fig. 4u), and it falls to about 0.45 by 0.5 K. Percent-level admixtures from contact misalignment also shift ρxy. The authors should:
   - compute σxy and σxx from the measured tensor against ν, D, B and T around ν = 2/3, and report σxy with propagated uncertainty;
   - show ρxy and ρxx as functions of ν at several fixed D and B, and state whether a σxy plateau of finite width exists in ν or D, with its value and uncertainty, and with σxx → 0 as T is lowered;
   - give the lowest ρxx reached at base temperature, in ohms;
   - say explicitly whether the state is a zero-field anomalous state or a field-stabilized FCI, since quantization is claimed only for B⊥ > 200 mT (p. 7).

   If the claim does not survive this analysis, it should be reworded, for example as "a correlated anomalous Hall state at ν ≈ 2/3 with ρxy approaching 3h/7e²", and the title and abstract adjusted to match.

2. **The Streda slope that fixes C = 7/3 is drawn by eye, not fitted.** (Fig. 4g–i; ED Fig. 6i–j.) Fig. 4g–i and ED Fig. 6i–j overlay a single dashed line of slope 7/3 on broad features. The alternatives are close: C = 2 (an anomalous-Hall-crystal or CDW state with s = 2/3, which the authors' own mechanism A contains as a component), C = 5/2, C = 8/3 (νC for C = 4) and C = 3. Over 0–3 T, Streda lines from ν = 2/3 with slopes 2, 7/3 and 8/3 differ by only Δν ≈ 0.02. With n0 ≈ 1.11 × 10¹² cm⁻², Δν ≈ 0.022 C per tesla. Telling them apart needs a fitted slope with an error bar. The authors should fit the trajectory of the ρxx minimum (or of the σxy feature) in the (n, B) plane over the full 0–3 T range, at several D values and in both devices. They should report t = dν/d(φ/φ0) and the intercept s with uncertainties, show that the intercept stays at ν = 2/3 independently of D, and show that t excludes 2, 8/3 and 3.
   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

3. **Inhomogeneity, domain mixing or a partially polarized metal has not been ruled out as the source of the fractional (and high-C) Hall values.** (Fig. 4; ED Figs. 1d, 4, 6, 10, 11.) The 7/3 feature sits in a hysteretic part of the ν–D map, close to boundaries between integer states with different C (C = 3/4 at ν = 1, C = 2/3 sectors elsewhere). Chern or magnetic domains with different integer σxy between the Hall probes, together with dissipative transport along domain walls, would give exactly a fractional, non-plateau ρxy with several kΩ of ρxx. The energy scales also do not fit together. "Tc ≈ 3.3 K" comes from hysteresis (ED Fig. 4c). The activation gap is 0.026 meV (about 0.3 K) in D1 and 0.016 meV in D2 (ED Figs. 4o, 10c). ρxy at B = 0 halves by about 0.5 K (ED Fig. 4u). These numbers point more to a magnetic metal whose Hall response passes near 3h/7e² than to an incompressible state. Twist-angle variation across the Hall bar could also mix C = 4 (ν = 1) regions with neighbouring states and produce intermediate ρxy. The authors should:
   - report ρxy and ρxx from several independent Hall and longitudinal contact pairs in D1 (ED Fig. 1d shows more contacts than are used), and check whether the same value appears in each;
   - estimate twist-angle variation across the device, for example from the ν = 4 positions on different contact pairs;
   - add nonlocal or two-terminal against four-terminal measurements, which diagnose chiral edge transport and, for 7/3, would separate fractional edge counting from a C = 2 or C = 3 state with a dissipative bulk.

   A thermodynamic probe of incompressibility at ν = 2/3 (capacitance, SET or chemical potential), or local magnetometry or imaging, would greatly strengthen the claim. Without such evidence, the result should be phrased as a fractional anomalous Hall response, not an established FCI.
   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

4. **The second device does not reproduce the 7/3 quantization, and the explanation given is inconsistent with the integer data.** (ED Fig. 11a,b; p. 7.) In D2, which has the sharper ρxx minimum, ρxy at ν = 2/3 reaches only about 0.86 × 3h/7e² (ED Fig. 11a). The paper attributes this to "poor ρxy electrical contact quality". Yet the same probes give the integer states at 0.96–0.99 of quantization (ED Fig. 11a,b). A contact problem that removes 14% from the fractional state but only 1–4% from the integer states is not a consistent explanation. The more natural reading is that the ν = 2/3 value depends on the local mixture and that the D1 value is coincidental. The authors should:
   - report σxy for D2 and present the D1 and D2 data on the same footing;
   - test the contact explanation with alternative contact pairs or a reversed (Onsager-reciprocal) configuration;
   - check whether the ρxy deficit across all D2 states scales with ρxx, as contact mixing would predict.

   As it stands, the C = 7/3 quantization is seen in one device only.
   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

5. **The C = 5, 6 and 7 states near ν ≈ 3.2–3.3 are not established as distinct quantized Chern insulators.** (Fig. 3d,h–l; ED Figs. 7, 8, 9, 11b.) The abstract says all states from C = 2 to C = 7 "exhibit the quantum anomalous Hall effect", and the text describes "precise quantization". Several observations argue against this.
   - **Close values, narrow windows.** The target values h/5e², h/6e² and h/7e² (5.16, 4.30 and 3.69 kΩ) differ by only 15–20%. In Fig. 3d each assigned "plateau" spans a sliver of ν of order 0.02. A ρxy that falls continuously with ν while ρxx stays finite would produce exactly this sequence.
   - **No plateaus around the loop points.** The hysteresis loops (Fig. 3h–j) are taken at points about Δν ≈ 0.06 and ΔD ≈ 0.03 V/nm apart, and no ρxx(ν) or ρxy(ν) plateau is shown around them.
   - **ρxx is not small.** It is about 1 kΩ in the Fig. 3d windows and about 0.1 h/Ce² (several hundred ohms) in the loops (Fig. 3h–j; ED Fig. 9d–f). It is about 0.1–0.2 h/Ce² (roughly 1–1.7 kΩ) for the C = −2, −3 and 4 loops (Fig. 3e–g). None of this is "vanishing".
   - **ρxy rises on warming.** ρxy rises 15–25% above h/Ce² between about 0.5 and 2 K before collapsing near 2.3 K (Fig. 3l; ED Fig. 9g–i). The text calls this "completely anomalous" (p. 5) but does not explain it. In a single gapped Chern insulator, thermal dissipation reduces |σxy| and should not push ρxy 20% above h/Ce². The rise is what one expects if conduction is shared with a neighbouring lower-|C| domain (such as the adjacent C = 4 region), or if the base-temperature value is not a protected plateau.
   - **Domain-like behaviour.** The states sit at incommensurate fillings, are continuously distributed in ν (p. 5), are hysteretic in ν (ED Fig. 7d–e) and have coercive fields of only about 10–20 mT. Together this suggests domains.
   - **No fitted slopes or gaps.** Fig. 3k only places numerals on the derivative map, and at 1 T slopes 5, 6 and 7 differ by only about 0.02 in ν. No activation gaps are given for C = 5–7.

   The authors should:
   - give σxy and σxx (from the full tensor) for each state at base temperature, with ρxx in ohms at each hysteresis point;
   - show plateau widths in ν and D against an explicit plateau criterion;
   - give fitted Streda slopes and intercepts with uncertainties for C = 5, 6, 7 (and for −2 to −5) at more than one D;
   - measure with several contact pairs;
   - explain how ρxy can exceed h/Ce² on warming if each state is a gapped Chern insulator with that C.

   Otherwise, the region should be described as "anomalous Hall states with ρxy near h/Ce²", or the assignments called tentative.
   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

## B. Should address

6. **The density and twist-angle calibration that defines ν is partly circular and internally inconsistent.** (ED Fig. 2; Methods "Twist angle determination"; Fig. 4d.) The gate capacitances, and so the ν axis, are calibrated from Landau-fan lines that are themselves assigned C = 4, 5 and 7 (ED Fig. 2a,b). Chern numbers are then read off from slopes on that same axis. The ν = 4 density gives θ = 1.38°/1.37° (4n0 = 4.44 × 10¹² cm⁻² for D1), while the Brown–Zak periods give λ ≈ 10.4/10.5 nm and θ = 1.36°/1.35° (ED Fig. 2e). That is a 3–4% mismatch in n0. It matters at the level of the claims: the FCI peak is already at ν ≈ 0.68 in Fig. 4d, and a 4% rescaling moves it to about 0.70 and changes the Streda slopes. The commensurability argument ν = 2 + n/4 for the ν ≈ 3 cascade also depends on calibration at the few-percent level. The authors should:
   - calibrate the capacitance independently of assumed Chern numbers, for example from Shubnikov–de Haas oscillations away from the flat band or from the ν = ±4 band insulators alone;
   - reconcile the fan-based and Brown–Zak twist angles;
   - state which calibration defines ν and its uncertainty;
   - show how the position of the 2/3 feature shifts with D, and that it is pinned at 2/3 rather than at 0.68–0.70.

7. **Field antisymmetrization at ±0.1 T may mix hysteretic branches and distort the ν–D maps.** (Methods "Symmetrization"; Figs. 1f,g, 2a,b, 3a,b, 3d, 4a–e; ED Fig. 7.) The maps are (anti)symmetrized from data at B = ±0.1 T (Figs. 1, 3a,b, 4a–c) or ±0.2 T (Fig. 2a,b). Several states have coercive fields comparable to or above this:
   - about 0.15 T for C = −2 and C = −3 (Fig. 3e,f);
   - about 0.2 T for C = 4 near ν = 3 (Fig. 3g);
   - about 0.1 T for C = 3 at ν = 1 and for the FCI (Fig. 4f).

   They also depend on the ν sweep direction (ED Fig. 7). If the magnetization does not reverse between +B and −B, the two field signs are not time-reversed partners, and antisymmetrization cancels or distorts the anomalous signal. If the two maps were taken with different ν histories, combining them can create spurious intermediate ρxy values. This bears directly on the fractional and C = 5–7 assignments, and possibly on the asymmetry between the C > 0 and C < 0 regions (Fig. 3c against ED Fig. 7c). The authors should:
   - state the field-training protocol (sweep order, fast axis, training at large |B| before each map);
   - show raw, unsymmetrized ρxy(+B) and ρxy(−B) maps for Fig. 3a,b and Fig. 4c, and for the key linecuts (Figs. 3d, 4d–e), for both training directions;
   - confirm that the conclusions survive with single-field, field-trained data.

8. **The novelty claims ("beyond all known fractional Chern insulators", hysteretic Chern-sign switching) are overstated against prior work.** (Abstract; main text pp. 1–2, 7–9; Conclusion.) States with fractional Streda slope t and fractional intercept s that do not fit Landau-level-like sequences, including translation-symmetry-broken FCIs and "FCI + CDW" states, were reported in graphene/hBN moiré (Spanton et al., Science 2018) and in magic-angle graphene (Xie et al., Nature 2021). They also arise generally in Harper–Hofstadter and moiré bands. Anomalous Hall crystals and extended QAH states (Ref. 24) already give σxy ≠ νC. Both proposed mechanisms (ED Fig. 12) are built from known ingredients: a √3 × √3 CDW plus a Laughlin 1/3 state, or a 4 × 1 / 2 × 2 CDW plus a Halperin K-matrix state. That undercuts the claim that the state lies beyond existing frameworks. The doping-direction Chern-sign switching near ν = 3 has a close precedent in twisted monolayer–bilayer graphene (Polshyn et al., Nature 2020). The relation to Refs. 38 (high-C FCIs) and 43 (moiré-dependent Chern insulators in twisted crystalline flatbands) needs to be spelled out. The authors should make these comparisons explicitly and moderate the novelty statements in the title, abstract and conclusion.
   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

9. **"Entanglement" in the title and "entangled Chern insulators" in the text are neither defined nor evidenced.** (Title; Fig. 3 section heading, p. 5; p. 9; Methods pp. 17–18.) The title promises "Fractionalization and Entanglement of High Chern Insulators", but no measurement or calculation in the paper concerns quantum entanglement. On p. 5, "entangled" appears to mean coexisting, competing or spatially intertwined. "Valley-entangled" (p. 9, Methods) is only a property of a proposed trial wavefunction, the Halperin K = −(7,1;1,7) state, which is posited rather than derived. Readers will take the word as a physical claim. The authors should define the term operationally and support it, for example with an energetics calculation showing that the inter-valley (7,7,1) state is favoured, or remove it from the title and text.

10. **The proposed mechanisms for σxy = 7/3 are qualitative and untested, and one is not obviously self-consistent.** (Main text pp. 8–9; Methods "FCI based on C = 4 parent band"; ED Fig. 12.) The concerns differ for the two mechanisms.
    - **Mechanism A.** The C = 3 band is folded by a √3 × √3 order into C = (0, 1, 2) sub-bands, each holding 1/3 electron per original cell. At ν = 2/3, filling the C = 2 sub-band uses 1/3, and the remaining 1/3 completely fills the C = 1 sub-band in the folded zone. That would give σxy = 3, not a Laughlin 1/3 state. The "unfold" step in ED Fig. 12a assumes that the remaining electrons do not feel the charge order that created the sub-bands. This needs justification.
    - **Mechanism B.** It requires spontaneous intervalley particle–hole pairs at density 1/8 bound into a (7,7,1) Halperin state. The exponents are chosen to produce the needed −1/3 (p. 17), not derived. No energetic argument explains why this would beat the fully polarized states that HF finds.

    Neither charge order nor its energetics is computed, no many-body calculation (exact diagonalization or DMRG of the projected TBRTG model at ν = 2/3) is given, and no experimental signature of either order is shown. The authors should do one or more of the following:
    - provide such a calculation, for example HF or ED energetics in an enlarged cell;
    - derive and test distinguishing consequences, such as the associated anomalous Hall crystal with C = 2 at ν = 1/3 under mechanism A, other Diophantine lines in the Fig. 4g–i fans, commensurability features in the Brown–Zak or fan data, or the sign or valley character inferred from the hysteresis;
    - say which mechanism the data favour, or present both as speculative.

    Raised by: r1, r2, r3

11. **The activation gaps come from fits that do not support the quoted values, and they conflict with the Curie temperatures.** (Fig. 3m; ED Figs. 4m–r, 10c.) The fits for each state fail in different ways.
    - **C = 4 at ν = 1.** Δxx = 10.3 meV (about 120 K) comes from points at 1/T ≈ 0.10–0.15 K⁻¹, that is T ≈ 7–10 K. This range straddles Tc ≈ 8.5 K (ED Fig. 4a,m,s), where transport is governed by the loss of magnetic order, not by activation across a charge gap.
    - **C = 4 near ν = 3 and C = 3.** The gaps of 1.6 meV (about 19 K) for C = 4 near ν = 3, against Tc ≈ 4.5 K, and 0.6–1.4 meV for C = 3, against Tc ≈ 6 K, show the same mismatch.
    - **The 7/3 state.** Δ ≈ 0.026 meV (about 0.3 K) in D1 and 0.016 meV in D2, against a quoted Tc ≈ 3.3 K. ρxx changes by less than a factor of two over the fitted range (ED Figs. 4o, 10c), so an Arrhenius description is not meaningful. A 0.026 meV gap is also barely resolved at a 30 mK base temperature.
    - **The fits themselves.** They use few points over a narrow 1/T window, and the low-T data saturate.

    The gap is thus an order of magnitude off Tc, in opposite directions for different states. The authors should:
    - state the fit windows and the number of points;
    - define Δ consistently (Δ/2kBT or Δ/kBT);
    - show the fits on log axes over at least a decade in ρxx;
    - justify activated behaviour and separate the magnetic ordering temperature from the charge gap;
    - otherwise, drop the numerical gaps where the fit is not justified.

    Raised by: r1, r2, r3

14. **The continuum-model and Hartree–Fock agreement with the data is weaker than stated.** (Methods "Continuum Model" and "Hartree–Fock", pp. 15–16; Fig. 1c,d; ED Fig. 5.) The weaknesses are as follows.
    - **The C = 4 → 3 transition.** The agreement (predicted D/ε0 ≈ 0.78 V/nm, observed ≈ 0.74–0.75 V/nm) rests on a single linear proportionality between ΔU and D, anchored at the CNP gap closing (D/ε0 ≈ 0.18 V/nm). It is a one-parameter extrapolation, not an independent prediction. It also ignores interlayer screening, which makes ΔU sublinear in D at large D, and HF band renormalization at ν = 1.
    - **Parameters.** The continuum parameters come from Ref. 59 (w1 = 110 meV, u0 = 0.8, γ values), which describes a different structure. The interaction parameters ε = 6 and ξ = 10 nm are not justified against hBN dielectrics 10–20 nm thick on each side, which make the gate-to-gate separation several times larger. This sets U_ξ ≈ 24 meV, which controls polarization and the competition between states.
    - **The C = 3 state in HF.** In ED Fig. 5b (C = 3, ΔU = 16 meV), the HF gap at E_F looks very small, or the bands touch near Γ. The robustness of the C = 3 insulator in HF should be stated.
    - **The ν ≈ 3 cascade.** The HF is restricted to 3 bands per flavour without translation breaking. At ν = 3 it gives only C = 4, so it cannot address the ν ≈ 2.5–3.5 cascade, C = 5–7 or ν = 2/3. The explanation of the cascade as a 4× unit-cell enlargement (ν = 2 + n/4) is not compared with the measured Streda intercepts of the states at ν = 2.61, 2.73, 3.09, 3.23 and 3.29. A fitted (t, s) table for each state is needed to test whether s ∈ ℤ/4.

    The authors should show how sensitive the transition ΔU and the Chern numbers are to w1, u0, ε, ξ and the band projection. They should state explicitly that the cascade and the FCI are not captured by the calculations, and reword "remarkably close" accordingly.
    Raised by: r1, r2, r3

Both devices come from the same stack, differ in twist angle by only 0.01° and appear to have been measured in one cooldown. The 7/3 state is quantized only in D1. Given the strong hysteresis and history dependence, the authors should state:
    - how many devices were fabricated and measured;
    - whether data in different figures come from the same cooldown;
    - whether the ν = 3 cascade, the |C| = 5–7 features and the 7/3 feature reappear at the same (ν, D) after thermal cycling above Tc and to room temperature;
    - how sensitive the ν = 3 phase diagram is to sweep rate and waiting time.

    The claim that rhombohedral (ABCA) stacking survives fabrication rests on a single pair of Raman 2D spectra (ED Fig. 1c). It should be compared with a Bernal tetralayer reference or mapped across the Hall bar.
