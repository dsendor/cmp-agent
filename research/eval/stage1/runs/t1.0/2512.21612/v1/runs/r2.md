Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors introduce a new moiré platform, Bernal bilayer graphene twisted on rhombohedral tetralayer graphene (θ ≈ 1.37–1.38°), and report displacement-field-tuned quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) states with C = 4 and C = 3 at ν = 1, a cascade of hysteretic Chern insulators with |C| from 2 to 7 around ν = 3, and a fractional state at ν = 2/3 that they assign a many-body Chern number C = 7/3. If the fractional assignment held, it would be the first fractional Chern insulator whose Hall conductance is not νC of a parent band and lies outside the Jain and higher-C sequences, which would be a significant result. The integer high-C states at zero field are also of clear interest. As it stands, however, the fractional claim rests on a Hall resistance peak with a large longitudinal resistance alongside it, and the theory offered is speculative.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- New, well-defined heterostructure, with two devices on the same stack, Raman confirmation of rhombohedral stacking, and a twist angle cross-checked by Landau fans and Brown–Zak oscillations (Extended Data Fig. 2).
- The ν = 1 C = 4 QAH state is well quantized at zero field, with a large hysteresis loop and Tc ≈ 8.5 K (Fig. 2d, Extended Data Fig. 4a,s). The D-tuned C = 4 → 3 transition is reproduced in D2 (Extended Data Fig. 6) and matches the continuum-model band-Chern-number change semi-quantitatively.
- Around ν = 3 the data are rich: several hysteresis loops quantized at h/Ce² with |C| up to 7 (Fig. 3e–j), reproduced in D2 (Extended Data Fig. 8).
- The transport data set is extensive: hysteresis loops, Landau fans, temperature dependences and sweep-direction maps.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The C = 7/3 FCI assignment does not follow from the data. ρxx is large, ρxy peaks rather than forming a plateau, and the Hall conductance is not 7/3.**
   - **What the data show.** At ν = 2/3, ρxx reaches only a "local minimum … down to 3 kΩ" (p. 7; Fig. 4d), and Extended Data Fig. 4i shows ρxx ≈ 0.3–0.4 × 3h/7e² ≈ 4 kΩ. ρxy touches 3h/7e² ≈ 11.06 kΩ only at the apex of a narrow peak, both in ν (Fig. 4d) and in D (Fig. 4e). There is no plateau in either variable.
   - **Why ρxy alone cannot decide it.** With ρxx comparable to a third of ρxy, the relevant quantity is σxy = ρxy/(ρxx² + ρxy²). Using ρxx = 3 kΩ and ρxy = 11.06 kΩ gives σxy ≈ 2.17 e²/h, between 2 and 7/3 and not equal to 7/3. A Hall resistance that happens to pass through 3h/7e² at the top of a peak, beside a large dissipative channel, is expected for many non-topological mixtures, such as a C = 2 (or C = 3) Chern domain in parallel with a resistive region.
   - **What to do.**
     - Convert to σxx and σxy over the full (ν, D, B) range and show whether σxy forms a plateau at 7/3 with σxx → 0 as T is lowered.
     - Show the zero-field ρxy, which Fig. 4f indicates is only about 0.7 of 3h/7e².
     - Temper the claim if it does not survive this analysis.

2. **The Streda slope 7/3 is not quantitatively established against nearby alternatives.**
   - Figs. 4g–i and Extended Data Fig. 6i–j overlay a dashed line of slope 7/3 by eye. The distinguishing alternatives are close: C = 2 (an anomalous-Hall-crystal or CDW state with s = 2/3, which the authors' own mechanism 1 includes as a component), C = 8/3 (νC for C = 4), and C = 5/2.
   - The authors should fit the trajectory of the ρxx minimum (or of the σxy feature) in the (n, B) plane over the full 0–3 T range and report both the slope and the intercept with uncertainties. They should also show that the intercept is at ν = 2/3 independent of D.
   - The quantized ρxy is claimed only for B⊥ > 200 mT (p. 7). It should therefore be stated explicitly whether the state is a zero-field anomalous state or a field-stabilized one.

3. **The second device does not reproduce the quantization, and the explanation offered is inconsistent with the integer data.**
   - In D2, ρxy at ν = 2/3 reaches only ≈ 0.86 × 3h/7e² (Extended Data Fig. 11a). The authors attribute this to "poor ρxy electrical contact quality" (p. 7).
   - Yet the same contacts give the integer states at 0.96–0.99 of quantization (Extended Data Fig. 11a,b). A contact problem that removes 14% from the fractional state but only 1–4% from integer states measured through the same probes is not a consistent explanation.
   - A second interpretation fits better: the ν = 2/3 feature is not a quantized state and its ρxy value depends on the local mixture.
   - The authors should report σxy for D2 and use alternative contact pairs or a reversed configuration (Onsager reciprocity) to test the contact explanation. As it stands, the C = 7/3 quantization is seen in one device only.

4. **The headline claims of "entanglement" and of being "beyond all known fractional Chern insulators" are not supported.**
   - The title and abstract claim entanglement. No measurement in the paper bears on entanglement. "Entangled Chern insulators" (p. 5) appears to mean coexisting or competing phases, and the "valley-entangled topological phase" is only a property of a proposed trial wavefunction (Methods, pp. 17–18).
   - Both proposed mechanisms (Extended Data Fig. 12) are built from known ingredients: a √3×√3 CDW plus a Laughlin 1/3 state, or a 4×1/2×2 CDW plus a Halperin K-matrix state. This undercuts the statement that the state is beyond existing frameworks.
   - Neither the CDW orders nor their energetics are computed. The Halperin (7,7,1) state is chosen by fitting the needed −1/3 (p. 17), not derived.
   - The authors should either provide evidence (e.g. HF or ED energetics in an enlarged cell, or signatures of translation-symmetry breaking such as commensurability in the Brown–Zak or fan data) or rephrase the title, abstract and conclusion.

**B. Should address**

5. **Activation gaps and Tc are mutually inconsistent and the fits are poorly constrained.**
   - For C = 4 at ν = 1, Extended Data Fig. 4m gives Δxx ≈ 10.3 meV (~120 K) while Tc ≈ 8.5 K. For C = 7/3, Δ ≈ 0.026 meV (~0.3 K) while Tc is labelled ~3.3 K (Extended Data Fig. 4c,o). For D2 the fractional gap is 0.016 meV (Extended Data Fig. 10c).
   - The Arrhenius fits use only a few points over a narrow 1/T window, and the low-T data saturate.
   - The authors should state the fit range and define Δ consistently (e.g. Δ/2kBT or Δ/kBT). They should also discuss why the activation gap differs from Tc by an order of magnitude in opposite directions for different states.
   - A 0.026 meV gap at a 30 mK base temperature (Methods, p. 12; figures quote 10 mK) is barely resolved. The base and electron temperatures should be reconciled.

6. **The C = 5, 6, 7 states show ρxy rising above quantization with temperature, which questions the "precise quantization" and the QAH assignment.**
   - Fig. 3l and Extended Data Fig. 9g–i show ρxy increasing by ~20% above h/Ce² between 0.5 and 2 K before collapsing. The authors call this "completely anomalous" (p. 5) but do not explain it.
   - For a single Chern insulator, thermally activated dissipation reduces |σxy| but should not drive ρxy above h/Ce² by 20%, unless conduction is shared with a neighbouring lower-|C| domain or the ρxx/ρxy geometric mixing is not removed.
   - Fig. 3d shows ρxx clearly non-zero in the C = 5–7 windows.
   - These states sit at incommensurate fillings, are continuously distributed in ν (p. 5), are hysteretic in ν (Extended Data Fig. 7d–e), and have coercive fields of only ~10–20 mT.
   - Together this suggests a domain picture. The authors should present σxy and σxx, measure at several contact pairs, and show Streda slopes for each |C| = 5, 6, 7 state (currently the fan in Fig. 3k is only labelled).

7. **Labelling inconsistencies in the ν ≈ 3 gap analysis.**
   - Fig. 3e–h identify C = −2 at (2.61, 0.502), C = −3 at (2.73, 0.494), C = −4 at (3.09, 0.471) and C = 5 at (3.23, 0.503).
   - Fig. 3m instead plots points labelled C = 2 at (2.61, 0.502), C = 3 at (3.09, 0.471) and C = 4 at (3.23, 0.503), with the 1.63 meV gap assigned to "C = 4".
   - In Extended Data Fig. 4f,r, the 1.632 meV gap belongs to ν = 3.09, the C = −4 state.
   - The text (p. 5) repeats "C = 4". Signs and positions must be corrected. As plotted, the trend of gap with |C| cannot be checked.

8. **Antisymmetrization at ±0.1 T may mix hysteretic branches in the phase maps.**
   - The ν–D maps (Figs. 1f–g, 3a–b, 4a–c) are (anti)symmetrized from ±0.1 T. Several states have coercive fields of order or above 0.1 T: C = −2 and C = −3 switch near ±0.15 T (Fig. 3e,f), and C = 3 at ν = 1 needs ~100 mT.
   - At ±0.1 T, the two field signs may therefore not be time-reversed partners of the same magnetic state, especially given the strong ν-sweep history dependence the authors report.
   - The phase boundaries, and the asymmetry between C > 0 and C < 0 regions (Fig. 3c vs. Extended Data Fig. 7c), may partly reflect this procedure.
   - The authors should state the field-training protocol and show raw, unsymmetrized maps for both field-training directions.

9. **Hartree–Fock and continuum-model parameters and their comparison with experiment.**
   - The gate-screening length is set to ξ = 10 nm (p. 15). Yet the hBN dielectrics are 10–20 nm thick on each side (Methods, p. 12), so the gate-to-gate separation is several times larger. This affects the interaction strength, U_ξ ≈ 24 meV, which controls polarization and the competition of states.
   - The Δ_U ↔ D mapping is assumed linear and anchored to one point (the CNP gap closure). The C = 4 → 3 agreement at D/ε0 ≈ 0.74–0.78 V/nm is therefore a one-parameter extrapolation, not an independent prediction.
   - The HF is restricted to 3 bands per flavor and to no translation breaking, so it cannot address the ν ≈ 2.5–3.5 cascade or ν = 2/3.
   - The authors should justify ξ and ε, show sensitivity of the ν = 1 and ν = 3 results to them, and say explicitly that the ν = 3 cascade and the FCI are not captured by the calculations.

10. **The twist-angle and filling calibration is not fully consistent.**
    - The ν = 4 density gives θ = 1.38°/1.37° (Extended Data Fig. 2 caption), while the Brown–Zak periods give λ ≈ 10.4/10.5 nm and θ = 1.36°/1.35°. This is a ~3% mismatch in n0.
    - The fractional assignment (ν = 2/3) and the commensurability arguments (ν ≈ 2 + n/4) depend on the filling calibration at the few-percent level.
    - The authors should reconcile the two calibrations, quote the uncertainty in ν, and state how the position of the 2/3 feature shifts with D.

11. **Sample statistics and reproducibility.**
    - Both devices come from one stack and appear to have been measured in one cooldown.
    - The authors should state whether the C = 7/3 and the |C| = 5–7 features survive thermal cycling. They should also report how sensitive the ν = 3 phase diagram is to sweep rate and to waiting time, given the history dependence described on p. 5.

**C. Minor**

12. The Extended Data Fig. 6 title says device D2, but the caption for panels a–b says "for device D1". The main text says D2 has a "sharper ρxx minimal", but Extended Data Fig. 6g shows ρxy ≈ 9.5 kΩ, which should be stated numerically and compared with D1.
13. The base temperature is ~30 mK in the Methods but T = 10 mK in all captions. Clarify whether these are fridge or electron temperatures.
14. The 99% (Fig. 4d) and 98% (B > 200 mT) "accuracy" figures should be defined: at which field, with what uncertainty, and relative to which geometry factor.
15. Fig. 1c–d mark C = 4 at Δ_U = 10 meV and C = 3 at 16.5 meV. The text gives the transition at 15.35 meV in one place and 15.4 meV in another (pp. 15–16). Unify these.
16. Extended Data Fig. 4: the Curie temperature for C = 3 is "~6K", but panel t shows ρxy saturating at only ~0.55 h/3e² at zero field. The meaning of Tc for a state that is not quantized at B = 0 should be clarified.
17. Duplicated references: 9/12, 34/37, 36/57, 40/45, 42/49. Ref. 29 has a LaTeX artifact ("Moir\'e"). Several typos ("minimums", "filed", "Plank").

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Present the key fractional and high-C data in conductance (σxx, σxy) and in (n, B) Streda fits with error bars on slope and intercept. This is the single most important step to substantiate or revise the C = 7/3 claim.
2. Add a measurement that can distinguish a translation-symmetry-broken state, such as fan-diagram features at commensurate flux or a nonlocal or local probe, to test the CDW-based mechanisms the authors propose.
3. Reframe the title and abstract around what is demonstrated (high-C QAH states and a fractional Hall feature at ν = 2/3), and move "entanglement" and the "beyond all known FCIs" language to the discussion as hypotheses.
4. Report a second cooldown and multiple contact configurations for D1 and D2 to separate contact and domain effects from intrinsic quantization.
