Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors introduce a new moiré system, twisted Bernal bilayer on rhombohedral tetralayer graphene (θ ≈ 1.37–1.38°), and report three things: displacement-field-switchable quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) states with C = 4 and C = 3 at ν = 1, the C = 4 state with Tc ≈ 8.5 K; a dense set of zero-field Chern insulators with |C| = 2 to 7 between ν = 2.5 and 3.5; and a fractional state at ν = 2/3 with ρxy ≈ 3h/7e², which they read as an FCI with C = 7/3 ≠ νC. The integer high-Chern QAH states, especially the robust C = 4 state at ν = 1, are a clear and useful advance for rhombohedral and moiré graphene. The fractional C = 7/3 claim would be a major result if it holds, but the evidence is presently much weaker than the headline wording implies.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new, well-designed platform with dual graphite gates and a wide accessible (ν, D) phase space. The phase diagram is mapped carefully (Fig. 1f–g, Extended Data Fig. 3).
- The C = 4 QAH state at ν = 1 is convincing: ρxx ≈ 0, ρxy = h/4e² at B = 0 with clean hysteresis (Fig. 2c–d), a Streda slope consistent with C = 4 (Fig. 2h–i), and a Curie temperature of about 8.5 K (Extended Data Fig. 4a, s).
- The D-driven C = 4 → C = 3 switch at ν = 1 agrees semi-quantitatively with the continuum and Hartree–Fock picture (Methods; Extended Data Fig. 5). The predicted transition at D/ε0 ≈ 0.78 V/nm lies close to the observed ≈ 0.74–0.75 V/nm.
- The main ν = 1 and ν = 3 phenomenology is reproduced in a second device, D2 (Extended Data Figs. 6, 8, 11).
- Hysteresis in the ν sweep direction for the ν ≈ 3 states is documented thoroughly (Extended Data Fig. 7).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The transport evidence for a C = 7/3 FCI at ν = 2/3 is not sufficient.** The paper's most striking claim rests on the following, and none of it is conclusive:
   - In Fig. 4d, ρxy at ν ≈ 2/3 is a sharp peak in ν, not a plateau. In Fig. 4e it is a smooth dome in D whose maximum happens to touch 3h/7e². A trace that sweeps continuously through a value will reach it at some point; quantization requires a plateau of finite width in ν (and ideally in D and B) over which ρxy stays fixed while ρxx is suppressed.
   - ρxx does not vanish. The minimum is about 3 kΩ (text, p. 7), and in the hysteresis loop (Fig. 4f) ρxx ≈ 0.4 × 3h/7e² ≈ 4.7 kΩ, against ρxy ≈ 11 kΩ. With ρxx/ρxy ≈ 0.3–0.4, a "98–99 % quantization" of ρxy carries little meaning: σxy = ρxy/(ρxx² + ρxy²) then differs from 7/3 e²/h by roughly 10–15 %. Small admixtures from contact misalignment or inhomogeneity also shift ρxy by percent-level amounts.
   - In device D2, which has the sharper ρxx minimum, ρxy reaches only about 0.86 × 3h/7e² (Extended Data Fig. 11a). The paper attributes this to "poor ρxy electrical contact quality". However, the other D2 states sit at 0.96–0.99 of their quantized values, so the 7/3 state in D2 is anomalously far off. The more natural reading is that the D1 value is coincidental.
   - The Streda analysis (Fig. 4g–i) only overlays a dashed line. Over 0–3 T, slopes of 2, 7/3 and 8/3 differ by only Δν ≈ 0.02. The authors need to fit the slope and the intercept with uncertainties, and show that 7/3 is preferred over 2, 8/3 and 3 (for example with fitted ρxx-minimum trajectories in both devices).

   The authors should do the following:
   - Show ρxy and ρxx at several fixed D values and several B values as functions of ν, demonstrating a plateau.
   - Convert to σxy and σxx and report σxy with propagated uncertainty.
   - Measure ρxy with several Hall contact pairs in both devices, which also tests the contact explanation for D2.
   - Report the fitted Streda slope.

   Without these, the claim should be reworded as "a correlated anomalous Hall state at ν ≈ 2/3 with ρxy approaching 3h/7e²", and the title and abstract should be adjusted.

2. **The C = 5, 6, 7 assignments near ν ≈ 3.2–3.3 are not established as distinct quantized states.**
   - In Fig. 3d the three "plateaus" (h/5e², h/6e², h/7e²) each occupy a very narrow sliver of ν, of order 0.02. Here ρxx is about 1 kΩ, not zero, compared with ρxy ≈ 3.7–5.2 kΩ. The three quantized values differ by only 15–20 %. A ρxy that decreases continuously with ν while ρxx stays finite would produce exactly this sequence of apparent "plateaus".
   - The hysteresis loops (Fig. 3h–j) are taken at points separated by Δν ≈ 0.06 and ΔD ≈ 0.03 V/nm. No ρxx(ν) or ρxy(ν) plateau is shown around those points.
   - On heating, ρxy rises above the quantized value by 15–25 % (Fig. 3l; Extended Data Fig. 9g–i). The text calls this "completely anomalous", but it is also what one expects if the base-temperature value is not a protected plateau.
   - No activation gaps are given for C = 5, 6, 7.
   - The states sit at incommensurate fillings, so a fitted Streda slope for each one is essential (Fig. 3k only shows numerals placed on the derivative map).

   The authors should do the following:
   - Give quantitative Streda fits (slope and intercept) for each labelled state.
   - Show σxy(ν) and σxx(ν) at base temperature with an explicit plateau-width criterion.
   - Report ρxx at each hysteresis point.
   - Otherwise, describe the C = 5–7 region as "anomalous Hall states with ρxy near h/Ce²" rather than as distinct quantized Chern insulators.

3. **The "entanglement" in the title and the "entangled Chern insulators" in the text are not defined or tested.** The title promises "Fractionalization and Entanglement". The ν ≈ 3 states are called "entangled high-Chern insulators" (Section heading, p. 5), and the C = 7/3 mechanism is called "valley-entangled" (p. 9). No measurement or calculation addresses entanglement. On p. 5 the word seems to mean "competing" or "spatially intertwined". The Halperin construction in Methods (K = −(7,1;1,7)) is posited, not derived. Either define the term operationally and support it, for example with an energetics calculation showing that the inter-valley (7,7,1) state is favoured, or remove it from the title and text.

B. Should address

4. **Inconsistent state labels and positions in Fig. 3m and the gap discussion.**
   - Fig. 3m plots C = 2, 3, 4 (positive) at positions (2.61, 0.502), (3.09, 0.471) and (3.23, 0.503).
   - However, the Fig. 3 caption and Extended Data Fig. 4 assign ν = 2.61 to C = −2, ν = 2.73 to C = −3, ν = 3.09 to C = −4 and ν = 3.23 to C = 5.
   - The gap values plotted at "C = 3" (≈0.3/0.49 meV) match the C = −3 state at ν = 2.73 (Extended Data Fig. 4q), not the state at ν = 3.09.
   - The 1.63 meV value matches the C = −4 state at ν = 3.09 (Extended Data Fig. 4r), not ν = 3.23.
   - The text (p. 5) calls it "the C = 4 state".

   The signs, positions and assignments need to be reconciled, because the narrative about which states are "traditional" relies on this figure.

5. **Activation gaps are inconsistent with the reported Curie temperatures, and the fitting ranges are not justified.**
   - For C = 4 at ν = 1, Δxx ≈ 10.3 meV (≈120 K; Extended Data Fig. 4m) is fitted only over T ≈ 7–10 K, right at the magnetic transition (Tc ≈ 8.5 K). This is not a single-particle transport gap.
   - For the FCI, Δ ≈ 0.026 meV (≈0.3 K; Extended Data Fig. 4o) and 0.016 meV in D2 (Extended Data Fig. 10c), yet Tc ≈ 3.3 K is quoted (Extended Data Fig. 4c). Meanwhile ρxy falls to roughly half its value by 1 K (Extended Data Fig. 4u).
   - In Extended Data Fig. 4o, ρxx changes by less than a factor of 2 over the whole fitted range, which does not support an Arrhenius description.

   The authors should state the fit windows, show the fits on log axes over at least a decade in ρxx, and distinguish the magnetic ordering temperature from the charge gap.

6. **Filling-factor calibration and twist-angle inhomogeneity matter for identifying ν = 2/3.**
   - In Extended Data Fig. 2, the twist angle from 4n0 is 1.38°/1.37°, but Brown–Zak oscillations give 1.36°/1.35°. That is a 2–3 % discrepancy in moiré density, comparable to the distance between ν = 2/3 and the observed ρxy peak at ν ≈ 0.68 (Fig. 4d).
   - The authors should state which calibration defines ν, give its uncertainty, and show that the state is pinned at 2/3 rather than at, for example, 0.68–0.70.
   - They should also estimate twist-angle variation across the Hall bar, for example by comparing contact pairs or ν = 4 positions, since inhomogeneity can mix C = 4 (ν = 1) regions with neighbouring states and produce intermediate ρxy values.

7. **The zero-field QAH claim for C = 3 at ν = 1 contradicts the data shown.** Fig. 2e shows that ρxy at B = 0 is well below h/3e² (about 0.6–0.7 of it) and becomes quantized only above about 100 mT, as p. 4 itself states. The same paragraph then says "both states … can be sustained even at zero magnetic field". The authors should state precisely what is quantized at zero field and what is not, and give ρxy(B = 0) and ρxx(B = 0) for the C = 3 state.

8. **Field symmetrization alongside hysteresis in field and in ν.**
   - The (ν, D) maps are symmetrized from B = ±0.1 T. For several states, including the FCI (Fig. 4f) and the C = 3 state at ν = 1, the coercive field is about 0.1 T. The ±B maps may therefore correspond to different magnetic or domain states.
   - The ν ≈ 3 phase boundary also depends on the sweep direction (Extended Data Fig. 7). Combining +B and −B data taken in separate sweeps can then mix metastable states and create artificial intermediate ρxy values.
   - The authors should show the raw, unsymmetrized ρxy at ±B for the key linecuts (Figs. 3d, 4d–e), and state the sweep history used for each map.

9. **The theoretical mechanisms for C = 7/3 are speculative and partly in tension with the data and the literature.**
   - Mechanism 1 needs a √3 × √3 charge order, and mechanism 2 needs a 4 × 1 or 2 × 2 order plus a (7,7,1) inter-valley exciton liquid at density 1/8. No energetics, no many-body calculation and no experimental sign of either charge order is presented. The Halperin exponents are chosen to fit the slope.
   - The HF calculation at ν = 3 gives only C = 4 and does not address C = 5–7.
   - The statement that the state is "beyond all known fractional Chern insulators" should be weighed against existing frameworks: fractional (t, s) states with σxy ≠ νC arise generally when FCIs coexist with translation-symmetry breaking (for example FCIs in Harper–Hofstadter and moiré bands, and "FCI + CDW" states in twisted graphene at finite field).

   The authors should moderate the novelty claim and indicate which mechanism the data favour, for example through the sign or valley character inferred from the hysteresis.

10. **Sample statistics and controls.**
    - Both devices come from the same stack and differ by only 0.01°, and the FCI is quantized in only one of them. The authors should state how many devices were fabricated and measured, whether any results survived thermal cycling, and whether the ρxy deficit seen in all D2 states (Extended Data Fig. 11) scales with ρxx, as contact mixing would predict.
    - The claim that the rhombohedral (ABCA) stacking is preserved after fabrication rests on a single Raman 2D spectrum pair (Extended Data Fig. 1c). It should be compared with a Bernal tetralayer reference, or mapped across the Hall bar.

11. **The "unprecedentedly high" Tc of the C = 4 state needs context.** The claim should be compared quantitatively with existing graphene QAH Curie temperatures, for example rhombohedral tetralayer and pentalayer on SOC or moiré substrates (refs. 39–43). The criterion used to define Tc (onset of hysteresis versus loss of quantization) should be stated.

C. Minor

12. The base temperature is given as about 30 mK in Methods but T = 10 mK in every figure caption. State the electron temperature.
13. The Extended Data Fig. 6 title says device D2, but the caption says "for device D1".
14. The Fig. 4c colour-bar label reads "ρxx1" but the panel shows ρxy. In Fig. 4b the label should likewise be checked against the caption.
15. Extended Data Fig. 1d caption says "orange dashed squares", but the image shows white or red outlines.
16. Extended Data Fig. 2a–b labels C = 5 and C = 7 fan lines emanating from ν = 1 at D/ε0 = 0.711 V/nm. These are not discussed in the main text.
17. Several references are duplicated: 9/12, 34/37, 36/57, 40/45, 42/49.
18. Fig. 3m uses a positive C axis for states the rest of the paper labels negative (see concern 4). Fig. 3l combines C = −2, −3, −4 with positive labels in the legend.
19. In Methods, the linear mapping Δ_U ∝ D is anchored at a single point (D/ε0 = 0.18 V/nm). Note that interlayer screening makes this mapping nonlinear, so the agreement at 0.74–0.78 V/nm should be presented as approximate.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add fixed-B, fixed-D linecuts of σxy(ν) and σxx(ν) around ν = 2/3 and ν = 3.2–3.3 using several Hall contact pairs. Include quantitative Streda fits with uncertainties. Together these would turn the FCI and C = 5–7 claims from suggestive to convincing.
2. Measure nonlocal transport, or compare two-terminal and four-terminal resistances, in the C = 4 and C = 7/3 states to probe chiral edge channels. For C = 7/3, fractional edge-channel counting would distinguish it from a C = 2 or C = 3 state with dissipative bulk.
3. Measure local or thermodynamic probes, such as local compressibility (SET or chemical potential), to confirm incompressibility at ν = 2/3 and at the incommensurate ν ≈ 3.2 fillings. Look for evidence of the proposed charge orders.
4. Reframe the title, abstract and conclusions around the well-supported integer high-Chern QAH results. Present the C = 7/3 state as a candidate, unless the additional data above establish quantization.
