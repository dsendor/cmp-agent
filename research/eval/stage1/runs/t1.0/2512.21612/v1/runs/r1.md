Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors introduce a new moiré platform: Bernal bilayer graphene twisted on rhombohedral tetralayer graphene (TBRTG, θ ≈ 1.37–1.38°). They report three results. First, at ν = 1 a quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) state switches from C = 4 to C = 3 as D increases, and the C = 4 state has Tc ≈ 8.5 K. Second, near ν = 3 there is a hysteretic cascade of Chern insulators with |C| = 2 to 7. Third, at ν = 2/3 there is a fractional Chern insulator with σxy = 7/3 e²/h, which the authors argue lies outside both the Jain sequence and the conventional high-Chern-band picture (Cmb ≠ νC). If the 7/3 state and the C = 5–7 states hold up, the work would matter for the fractionalization of high-Chern bands. As submitted, the transport evidence for these two most novel claims falls short of the standard the claims require.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new and clearly tunable moiré system. Two devices on the same stack give consistent ν–D phase diagrams (Fig. 1, ED Figs. 6 and 8).
- The C = 4 and C = 3 QAH states at ν = 1 are well documented. They show ρxx ≈ 0 and ρxy ≈ h/4e² at zero field, hysteresis loops, Streda-consistent Landau fans (Fig. 2) and full temperature series (ED Fig. 4a,b,s,t). The D-tuned change in Chern number is qualitatively consistent with the continuum and Hartree–Fock calculations (ED Fig. 5).
- The ν-sweep-direction dependence of the Chern-number sign near ν = 3 (ED Figs. 7 and 8) is an interesting phenomenon, and it is reproduced in D2.
- The Methods give a concrete continuum-model Hamiltonian and HF details, plus explicit K-matrix bookkeeping for one candidate mechanism.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The Hall response at ν = 2/3 is a ρxy maximum with large ρxx, not a quantized plateau. The conversion to σxy does not give 7/3. (Fig. 4d–f, ED Fig. 4c,i,o,u, ED Fig. 11a)

   The headline claim rests on ρxy reaching 3h/7e² ≈ 11.06 kΩ "with an accuracy of 99%" while ρxx stays near 3 kΩ (Fig. 4d). The minimum is only weak against a background of about 4 kΩ.

   - When ρxx/ρxy ≈ 0.27, ρxy does not measure the Hall conductance. Inverting the tensor with ρxy = 11.06 kΩ and ρxx = 3 kΩ gives σxy ≈ 2.17 e²/h and σxx ≈ 0.59 e²/h. That is neither 7/3 nor any obvious simple fraction.
   - Agreement of ρxy with 3h/7e² is therefore not evidence of a 7/3 topological state. A dissipative state with a non-quantized σxy can pass through that value.
   - In both ν (Fig. 4d) and D (Fig. 4e), ρxy shows a sharp peak rather than a plateau. A ρxy maximum that only touches the target value is the signature one would expect from a coincidence.
   - At B = 0, ρxy ≈ 0.8 × 3h/7e² (ED Fig. 4u), and it falls to about 0.45 by 0.5 K.
   - In D2, which has the sharper ρxx minimum, ρxy reaches only about 0.86 of 3h/7e² (ED Fig. 11a).

   Attributing the D2 deviation to "poor contact quality" is not convincing. ED Fig. 11 shows the integer states in D2 deviating by only 2–4%, so a contact problem large enough to cause a 14% shortfall at 7/3 should also show up in the integer states.

   The authors should:
   - report σxy and σxx, computed from the measured tensor, against ν, D, B and T around ν = 2/3;
   - show whether a σxy plateau of finite width exists in ν or D, and its value with uncertainty;
   - give the lowest ρxx reached at the lowest T, in ohms;
   - present the D1 and D2 data on the same footing, with σxy for both devices;
   - if the claim remains, state clearly that zero-field quantization is not observed and that the state is a field-stabilized FCI, not a fractional QAH state.

2. The Streda analysis that fixes C = 7/3 is not quantified, and the density calibration it relies on is partly circular. (Fig. 4g–i, ED Fig. 2, Methods "Twist angle determination")

   - **Slope precision needed.** Over 0–3 T, Streda lines from ν = 2/3 with slopes 2, 7/3 and 8/3 differ in ν by only about 0.02 per unit of C/3. (With n0 ≈ 1.11 × 10¹² cm⁻², Δν ≈ 0.022 C per tesla.) Distinguishing them therefore needs a fitted slope with an error bar. Instead, Fig. 4g–i overlays a single drawn red line on broad features.
   - **Circular calibration.** The gate capacitances, and so the ν axis, are calibrated from Landau-fan lines that are themselves assigned C = 4, 5 and 7 (ED Fig. 2a,b). Chern numbers are then read off from slopes on that same axis.
   - **Calibrations disagree.** The Brown–Zak analysis gives λ ≈ 10.4 nm and θ = 1.36° for D1 (ED Fig. 2e). This implies n0 about 4% smaller than the 4n0 = 4.44 × 10¹² cm⁻² used for the ν axis (θ = 1.38°). A 4% rescaling moves the FCI peak, already at ν ≈ 0.68 in Fig. 4d, to about 0.70, and also moves the Streda slopes.

   The authors should:
   - calibrate capacitance independently of assumed Chern numbers, for example from Shubnikov–de Haas oscillations away from the flat band or from the ν = ±4 band insulators alone;
   - reconcile the fan-based and Brown–Zak twist angles;
   - fit ρxx-minimum or ρxy-plateau positions against B at several D values for the 7/3 state, reporting t = dν/d(φ/φ0) and the intercept s with uncertainties. Fixing C = 7/3 needs t determined well enough to exclude 2 and 8/3.

3. Inhomogeneity, domain mixing or a partially polarized metallic state has not been ruled out as the source of the fractional Hall value, nor has it been shown that the state is bulk and uniform. (Fig. 4, ED Figs. 4, 6, 10 and 11)

   The 7/3 feature appears in a region of the ν–D map that is hysteretic and close to boundaries between integer states with different C (C = 3/4 at ν = 1; C = 2/3 sectors elsewhere). A mixture of magnetic or Chern domains with different integer σxy between the Hall probes, together with dissipative transport through domain walls, would produce exactly a fractional, non-plateau ρxy with ρxx of several kΩ.

   The energy scales are also inconsistent:
   - "Tc ≈ 3.3 K" for the 7/3 state comes from hysteresis (ED Fig. 4c);
   - the activation gap is 0.026 meV (about 0.3 K) in D1 and 0.016 meV in D2 (ED Figs. 4o and 10c);
   - ρxy at B = 0 already drops by half by 0.5 K (ED Fig. 4u).

   These numbers point to a magnetic metal whose Hall response happens to pass near 3h/7e², rather than an incompressible state.

   The authors should:
   - report ρxy and ρxx from several independent Hall and longitudinal contact pairs in D1. ED Fig. 1d shows more contacts than are used;
   - check whether the same value appears in each pair;
   - add a nonlocal or two-terminal measurement, which is diagnostic of chiral edge transport.

   A thermodynamic probe of incompressibility at ν = 2/3 (capacitance or chemical potential), or local magnetometry or imaging, would greatly strengthen the claim. If none is available, the conclusion should be phrased as an observation of a fractional anomalous Hall response, not an established FCI.

4. The C = 5, 6 and 7 states are not established as distinct quantized Chern insulators. (Fig. 3d,h–l, ED Figs. 7, 8, 9 and 11b)

   The abstract states that all states from C = 2 to C = 7 "exhibit the quantum anomalous Hall effect", and the text describes "precise quantization". Several observations argue against this:

   - **Close plateau values.** The target values h/5e², h/6e² and h/7e² (5.16, 4.30 and 3.69 kΩ) differ by only about 15%. In Fig. 3d the shaded regions assigned to them are each only a few hundredths of ν wide.
   - **Non-zero ρxx.** The hysteresis loops show ρxx around 0.1 of h/Ce² (Fig. 3h–j, ED Fig. 9d–f), that is several hundred ohms. The C = −2, −3 and 4 loops (Fig. 3e–g) show ρxx of about 0.1–0.2 h/Ce², or roughly 1–1.7 kΩ. None of these is "vanishing".
   - **Anomalous temperature dependence.** On warming, ρxy for C = 5, 6 and 7 rises about 20–25% above the putative quantized value before collapsing at about 2.3 K (Fig. 3l, ED Fig. 9g–i). This is unlike any gapped Chern insulator. It is consistent with a ρxy that varies continuously and passes through these values at base temperature, for example because of competition with the neighbouring C = 4 region.
   - **Unfitted fan slopes.** In Fig. 3k the slopes are labelled by hand, not fitted. At 1 T, slopes 5, 6 and 7 differ by only about 0.02 in ν.

   The authors should:
   - give σxy (from the full tensor) for each state, with ρxx in ohms;
   - show fitted Streda slopes and intercepts with uncertainties for C = 5, 6 and 7 (and for −2 to −5), at more than one D;
   - show plateau widths in ν and D;
   - explain how ρxy can exceed h/Ce² on warming if the state is a gapped Chern insulator with that C.

   Without these, the paper should describe C = 5–7 as tentative.

B. Should address

5. The symmetrization protocol is unclear for hysteretic states, and the ν–D maps may be distorted by it. (Methods "Symmetrization", Figs. 1f,g, 2a,b, 3a,b and 4a–c)

   The maps are antisymmetrized from data at B = ±0.1 T (Fig. 1, Fig. 3a,b, Fig. 4a–c) or ±0.2 T (Fig. 2a,b). Yet several states have coercive fields comparable to or larger than this: about 0.15 T for C = −2 (Fig. 3e), about 0.2 T for C = 4 near ν = 3 (Fig. 3g), and up to about 0.15 T for C = −3 (Fig. 3f). They also depend on the ν sweep direction (ED Fig. 7).

   - If the magnetization does not reverse between +B and −B, antisymmetrization cancels or distorts the anomalous signal.
   - If the two field maps were taken in sequences with different ν histories, combining them can create spurious intermediate values, which is particularly relevant to the fractional and C = 5–7 assignments.

   The authors should:
   - state the field-training protocol (sweep order, fast axis, whether the sample was trained at large |B| before each map);
   - show raw, unsymmetrized ρxy(+B) and ρxy(−B) maps for Fig. 3a,b and Fig. 4c;
   - confirm that the conclusions survive using single-field, field-trained data.

6. The activation gaps are extracted from fits that do not support the quoted values. (Fig. 3m, ED Figs. 4m–r and 10c)

   - The C = 4 gap at ν = 1, Δxx = 10.3 meV (about 120 K), comes from points at 1/T ≈ 0.10–0.15 K⁻¹, that is T ≈ 7–10 K. This range straddles the Curie temperature of 8.5 K (ED Fig. 4a,m,s), where transport is governed by loss of magnetic order, not activation across a charge gap.
   - Gaps of 1.6 meV (about 19 K) for C = 4 near ν = 3 against Tc ≈ 4.5 K, and 0.6–1.4 meV for C = 3 against Tc ≈ 6 K, show the same mismatch.
   - For the 7/3 state, ρxx changes by less than a factor of two over the fitted range (ED Fig. 4o, ED Fig. 10c), so an Arrhenius gap is not meaningful.

   The authors should state the fit windows and the number of points, justify why activated behaviour applies, and discuss the discrepancy between gap and Tc. Alternatively, they should drop the numerical gaps where the fit is not justified.

7. The novelty claims are overstated relative to prior observations of fractional Hall slopes, charge-ordered Chern states and doping-switched Chern signs. (Abstract, main text pp. 1–2 and 7–9, Conclusion)

   - **"Beyond all known FCIs."** States with fractional Streda slope t and fractional intercept s that do not fit LL-like sequences, including translation-symmetry-broken FCIs, were reported in graphene/hBN moiré (Spanton et al., Science 2018) and in magic-angle graphene (Xie et al., Nature 2021). Anomalous Hall crystals and the extended QAH states (Ref. 24) already give σxy ≠ νC. Mechanism A in this paper is itself a combination of known ingredients, a charge-ordered Chern state plus a Laughlin state. The comparison with these precedents must be made explicitly.
   - **Hysteretic Chern-sign switching.** The switching with doping direction near ν = 3 has a close precedent in twisted monolayer–bilayer graphene (Polshyn et al., Nature 2020).
   - **Closely related recent preprints.** The relation to Refs. 38 (high-C FCIs) and 43 (moiré-dependent Chern insulators in twisted crystalline flatbands) needs to be spelled out.
   - **Unprecedented Tc.** The claim of an "unprecedentedly high Tc ≈ 8.5 K in graphene" needs a definition of Tc (onset of hysteresis or loss of quantization) and a comparison with the literature values it supersedes.

8. The proposed mechanisms for σxy = 7/3 are qualitative, untested and in one case not obviously self-consistent. (Main text pp. 8–9, Methods "FCI based on C = 4 parent band", ED Fig. 12)

   - **Mechanism A.** The C = 3 band is folded by a √3 × √3 order into C = (0, 1, 2) sub-bands, each holding 1/3 electron per original cell. At ν = 2/3, filling the C = 2 sub-band uses 1/3, and the remaining 1/3 completely fills the C = 1 sub-band in the folded zone. That would give σxy = 3, not a Laughlin 1/3 state. The "unfold" step in ED Fig. 12a assumes the remaining electrons do not feel the charge order that created the sub-bands. This needs justification.
   - **Mechanism B.** Mechanism B requires spontaneous intervalley particle–hole pairs at density 1/8, bound into a (7,7,1) Halperin state. No energetic argument is given for why this would be favoured over the fully polarized states the HF calculation finds.

   Neither mechanism is supported by any many-body calculation (exact diagonalization or DMRG of the projected TBRTG model at ν = 2/3).

   The authors should do one or more of the following:
   - provide such a calculation;
   - derive experimentally testable consequences, for example an associated anomalous Hall crystal with C = 2 at ν = 1/3 under Mechanism A, or other Diophantine lines in the Fig. 4g–i fans, and check them against the data;
   - otherwise present the mechanisms as speculative.

9. The quantitative agreement between the continuum model, HF and the data is weaker than stated. (Methods "Continuum Model" and "Hartree–Fock", Fig. 1c,d, ED Fig. 5)

   - The agreement at the C = 4 → 3 transition (predicted D/ε0 ≈ 0.78 V/nm, observed ≈ 0.74 V/nm) uses a single proportionality between ΔU and D, fixed from the CNP gap closing at D/ε0 ≈ 0.18 V/nm. It ignores layer screening, which makes ΔU sublinear in D at large D, and HF band renormalization at ν = 1.
   - The parameters are taken from Ref. 59 (w1 = 110 meV, u0 = 0.8, γ values), which describes a different structure. The interaction parameters ε = 6 and ξ = 10 nm are not justified against the 10–20 nm hBN thicknesses used.
   - In ED Fig. 5b (C = 3, ΔU = 16 meV) the HF gap at E_F looks very small, or the bands touch near Γ. The robustness of the C = 3 insulator in HF should be stated.
   - The explanation of the ν ≈ 3 cascade as a 4× unit-cell enlargement (ν = 2 + n/4) is not compared with the measured Streda intercepts. The states sit at ν = 2.61, 2.73, 3.09, 3.23 and 3.29. A fitted (t, s) table for each state is needed to test whether s ∈ ℤ/4.

   The authors should show how sensitive the transition ΔU and the Chern numbers are to w1, u0, ε and the band projection, and should reword "remarkably close" accordingly.

10. The term "entangled" is not defined or evidenced. (Title, Fig. 3 section heading and text: "entangled Chern insulators")

    The title promises "Entanglement of High Chern Insulators", but the paper presents no quantity or measurement related to quantum entanglement. "Entangled" appears to mean intertwined or competing in the phase diagram, or (in the Methods) valley-entangled for Mechanism B. The authors should define the term or replace it, since readers will otherwise take it as a physical claim.

11. Reproducibility across samples and cooldowns is limited. (Fig. 1e, ED Figs. 6, 8 and 11)

    Both devices come from the same stack with nearly identical twist angle. The 7/3 state is quantized only in D1, and there is no statement on thermal cycling. Given the strong hysteresis and history dependence, the authors should state:
    - whether the ν = 3 cascade and the 7/3 feature reappear at the same (ν, D) after a thermal cycle to above Tc and to room temperature;
    - whether data in different figures come from the same cooldown.

C. Minor

12. The Methods give a base temperature of about 30 mK, but every figure states T = 10 mK. Please reconcile, and state the estimated electron temperature.

13. Caption and label inconsistencies:
    - ED Fig. 6 is titled "for device D2", but its panels a–b say "for device D1".
    - The Fig. 4c colour bar is labelled ρxx1 but shows ρxy.
    - ED Fig. 4 gives D/ε0 = 0.5586 V/nm for the C = 4 state, while the Fig. 2d caption gives 0.586 V/nm.
    - Fig. 3m's x-axis is labelled C = 2, 3, 4, while the text refers to C = −2 and −3. Please use signed C or |C| consistently.
    - The Fig. 3l legend is hard to read.

14. Overlaying ρxx from D2 with ρxx and ρxy from D1 in the same panels (Fig. 4d,e) invites misreading. Please separate the devices or show ρxy for both.

15. Give ρxx in ohms, together with the Hall-bar aspect ratio used for the geometric factor, wherever ρxx is plotted in units of h/Ce².

16. The Methods state that H0 at ΔU = 0 has a "direct gap of about 1.8 meV" at CNP, whereas the data are metallic at small D. Clarify whether the model predicts an indirect overlap, that is, a semimetal.

17. The reference list contains duplicates: 9 and 12, 34 and 37, 36 and 57, 40 and 45, 42 and 49.

18. The phrase "unprecedented richness" and similar superlatives are used several times. Please temper them, given concern 7.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Rebuild the central evidence around the conductivity tensor. Plot σxy(ν, D, B, T) at ν = 2/3 and for the C = 5–7 states, show fitted Streda slopes and intercepts with uncertainties in a (t, s) table, and use a ν calibration that does not assume Chern numbers. This single change would resolve or sharpen concerns 1, 2, 4 and 9.
2. Add measurements that discriminate a bulk topological state from domains or dissipative Hall response:
   - several independent Hall pairs in D1;
   - nonlocal or two-terminal edge transport;
   - ideally a thermodynamic measurement (capacitance or chemical potential) of incompressibility at ν = 2/3 and in the ν ≈ 3.2 region.
3. Provide raw, single-field, field-trained data next to the antisymmetrized maps, together with an explicit description of the sweep and training protocol. Show that the hysteretic phase boundaries do not generate the fractional or high-C values.
4. Either support one mechanism for σxy = 7/3 with an exact-diagonalization or DMRG calculation of the projected TBRTG model, or derive and test a distinguishing prediction, such as the charge order (√3 × √3 against 4 × 1 or 2 × 2) and the associated integer Chern features each mechanism implies. If neither can be done, recast the 7/3 claim and the title more cautiously.
