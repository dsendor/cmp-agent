Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The paper reports time-resolved imaging of indirect-exciton (IX) photoluminescence (PL) cloud expansion in a MoSe2/WSe2 heterostructure (HS). In a window of low temperature (T ≲ 10 K) and intermediate excitation power (around P_ex ≈ 0.5 mW), the 1/e extent d_1/e(t) grows nearly linearly in time, up to about 20 µm within 5 ns. From this the authors extract very high IX diffusivities, velocities and mobilities, and they argue that the behaviour, together with its disappearance at higher T and at both lower and higher density, is qualitatively consistent with IX superfluidity in a Bose–Hubbard (BH) picture. Direct kinetic measurements of IX transport in TMD heterostructures are valuable, and a strongly non-diffusive regime would be significant. However, the paper's interpretive step, from "fast PL cloud expansion" to "anomalously high mobility" to "consistent with superfluidity", rests on a single observable and on qualitative arguments.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- Direct time-resolved measurement of d_1/e(t) on the ns scale. This separates linear from quadratic growth of d_1/e², which cw imaging cannot do. The distinction the authors draw on this point is correct and useful.
- A systematic map of the effect over T (1.7–50 K) and P_ex (5 µW–4 mW) (Figs. 1b–d and 3). The map shows a bounded region of fast expansion rather than a single anecdotal condition.
- Three simple transport models (diffusion, ballistic, drift-diffusion with interaction and landscape) are applied in parallel. They give a consistent ranking of conditions, and the authors say candidly that these are characterisations rather than accurate descriptions.
- The authors state that the phenomena are reproducible after thermal cycling to room temperature.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The d_1/e values exceed the region in which they are fitted, so d_1/e may track the flatness of the PL profile rather than the extent of IX transport.**
   - *Where:* The Fig. 1 caption states that d_1/e is obtained from exponential fits "in the region from the excitation spot to the HS edge, x = 0–9 µm". Yet Figs. 1b–d and 2 report d_1/e up to about 20–22 µm. In Fig. 1a the profiles at t = 2.5 and 11 ns are nearly flat across the whole 0–10 µm window.
   - *Why it matters:* When the decay length exceeds the fitted interval, the fitted d_1/e becomes extremely sensitive to small slopes, background, normalisation and noise. Growth of d_1/e from 10 to 20 µm then means a change in a tiny slope, not that the cloud front moved 10 µm. Several processes would also flatten a profile bounded by the HS edge without long-range IX transport:
     - IXs accumulating against the HS edge or a confining landscape;
     - IXs generated across the HS by scattered or reflected laser light or waveguided DX/PL emission;
     - saturation or quenching (exciton–exciton annihilation, heating) near the spot that lowers the centre intensity over time.
   - *What to do:*
     - Show the raw I(x,t) on linear scales for the 500 µW and 25 µW series, with uncertainties on d_1/e from the fit.
     - Use a transport measure that does not saturate in a bounded region, for example the mean-square displacement ⟨x²⟩(t) or the position of the cloud front at a fixed intensity threshold, and show that it also grows ballistically.
     - Show I(x,t) beyond the HS edge and on the side x < 0 to quantify the background from scattered light. Measure an excitation-spot position far from the edge, or along a different direction, to exclude edge-accumulation effects.
     - Show the integrated intensity versus t, so that redistribution can be distinguished from loss at the centre.

2. **The energy landscape may produce the fast expansion through drift, and the landscape is not measured in the conditions of interest.**
   - *Where:* In the third model, U(x) is taken to drop by about 10 meV over 10 µm (about 1 meV/µm, "outlined in SI"), and the transport is treated as 1D along an elongated HS.
   - *Why it matters:*
     - A gradient of 1 meV/µm acting on an IX produces directed drift whose velocity scales with µ. A fast, nearly linear d_1/e(t) is exactly what downhill drift looks like.
     - The suppression at higher T and at high density could then reflect changes in screening of U, IX energy renormalisation, or a change in the effective landscape, rather than a change in an intrinsic transport state.
     - The landscape is not measured as a function of P_ex and T. A gradient that sets the transport direction undercuts "anomalously high mobility": the extracted µ is µ∇U-limited and depends on the assumed U.
   - *What to do:*
     - Provide spatially and spectrally resolved PL, E(x,t), at the relevant P_ex and T, so that the IX energy profile and its time evolution are measured rather than assumed.
     - Compare expansion toward and away from the gradient, i.e. with the spot placed so that transport runs uphill.
     - Report µ and D both with and without the U term, and show how sensitive the conclusions are to the assumed gradient.

3. **The link to superfluidity rests only on qualitative trends that other mechanisms also produce.**
   - *Where:* The abstract, the "Comparison of IX transport with BH theory" section and the summary state that the high mobility, its disappearance with T and its non-monotonic density dependence are "qualitatively consistent with theoretical predictions of IX superfluidity".
   - *Why it matters:* Each trend has a conventional explanation:
     - The decrease at high density is expected from a Mott transition to an e–h plasma, from exciton–exciton annihilation shortening τ, or from heating of the IX gas by the laser.
     - The increase from low density is the standard screening/delocalisation behaviour, which the authors themselves describe.
     - The loss at T ≳ 10 K can come from phonon scattering or from changes in lifetime and radiative efficiency.

     The comparison with BH theory also requires a filling N per lattice site. This needs the moiré period (twist angle) and a reliable density. The twist angle is not given in the main text. The density comes from the mean-field "plate capacitor" formula, which the authors acknowledge underestimates n, by a factor that can be several. With both inputs this uncertain, "N ≈ 1/2" cannot be distinguished from N ≈ 0 or 1.
   - *What to do:*
     - Either provide a measurement specific to a coherent state, for example first-order coherence or spatial-coherence interferometry, a sharp threshold in T or n, or a critical-velocity or flow-suppression test, or substantially tone down the superfluidity framing in the title, abstract and conclusions.
     - At minimum, give the twist angle and moiré period with uncertainties, propagate the uncertainty in n into N, and discuss explicitly the Mott-transition, annihilation and heating alternatives for the high-density suppression, using IX linewidths and lifetimes versus P_ex.

**B. Should address**

4. **None of the three models describes the key data, so the extracted D, v and µ (Fig. 3) are not reliable quantities.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 2, at 500 µW.
   - *What is wrong:*
     - The measured d_1/e(t) is convex: it accelerates, reaching about 22 µm at 5 ns. All three simulated curves are concave and saturate near 13–15 µm.
     - The single-parameter least-squares fits therefore average over a systematic misfit.
     - Fig. 3 shows no error bars.
     - The 2D maps (a3, b3, c3) are interpolated over a sparse grid, with blank regions.
   - *Why it matters:* The headline "anomalously high mobility" is quoted from these fits. An accelerating expansion is not expected from ballistic motion or drift either. It hints at a time-dependent process, such as a growing background, heating, or a front-propagation or edge effect (see concern 1).
   - *What to do:* Show fit residuals, give confidence intervals on D, v and µ, mark the measured grid points on the maps, and discuss what causes the acceleration. Fit restricted time windows, or use a model with a time-dependent parameter, to test whether a single µ is meaningful.

5. **The claim of "long-range ballistic transport" is not consistent with the paper's own numbers.**
   - *Where:* The "d_1/e(t) growth kinetics" section relates τ_m ≈ µm, l_m ∝ τ_m and long-range ballistic transport.
   - *The estimate:*
     - For µ ≈ 5×10⁴ cm²/(eV·s) (Fig. 3c), m ≈ m₀ gives τ_m ≈ 30 ps.
     - The thermal velocity at 1.7 K is about 5×10³ m/s, so l_m ≈ 0.1–0.2 µm. This is two orders of magnitude below the ~20 µm expansion.
     - Even taking v ≈ 10⁶ cm/s from Fig. 3b, l_m is below 1 µm.
   - *What to do:* Quote τ_m and l_m explicitly in the main text, with values. Reconcile them with the expansion length scale, or remove the claim that the transport is long-range ballistic.

6. **The effective IX temperature and heating are not established.**
   - *Where:* The effect peaks at P_ex ≈ 0.5 mW and vanishes above about 1–4 mW (Fig. 1d) and above about 10 K (Fig. 1b). The transport is driven by a focused 84 ns pulse resonant with the WSe2 DX, and the authors compare k_BT ≲ 1 meV with the disorder and interaction energies.
   - *Why it matters:* The argument that the transport is inconsistent with thermally activated diffusion requires the IX temperature, not the bath temperature. Laser heating of the lattice and hot IX injection would modify both the T- and P_ex-dependence. The suppression at high P_ex could simply be heating, mirroring the suppression at high T.
   - *What to do:* Estimate the IX temperature, for example from the high-energy tail of the PL spectrum or from linewidth versus T and P_ex. Estimate local lattice heating at 0.5–4 mW, and show whether the high-P_ex suppression maps onto the high-T suppression once heating is included.

7. **Lifetime and radiative-efficiency variations with T, P_ex and position are not shown in the main text.**
   - *Where:* τ enters all three models and is measured "as outlined in SI". It is not given as a function of T and P_ex in the main text.
   - *Why it matters:* If τ shortens at high density (annihilation) or high T, the window for expansion shrinks. That would produce the apparent "disappearance" without any change in mobility. Likewise, spatial variation of the IX radiative efficiency (bright/dark states, local quenching) maps directly onto d_1/e of the PL, which need not equal d_1/e of the density.
   - *What to do:* Show τ(T, P_ex) next to Fig. 3. Discuss whether PL intensity is proportional to IX density across the cloud and over time.

8. **The disorder amplitude is inferred only from the 10 meV linewidth.**
   - *Where:* The argument that the transport is inconsistent with diffusion relies on the disorder amplitude being about 10 meV, inferred from the PL linewidth.
   - *Why it matters:* The linewidth also contains homogeneous, phonon and exciton–exciton broadening, as well as unresolved moiré or trion lines. Its relation to the static disorder that IXs experience along a 20 µm path is not established.
   - *What to do:* Show the linewidth versus T and P_ex, and preferably spatial maps of IX energy, to support the claim that the disorder is substantial and static where the fast transport occurs.

9. **Only one heterostructure, and apparently one excitation location, is shown.**
   - *Where:* The Methods and SI describe one MoSe2/WSe2 HS.
   - *Why it matters:* The phenomenon is attributed to an intrinsic transport state. Because the landscape, edges and twist angle are sample-specific, reproducibility across samples and excitation spots is essential to separate intrinsic behaviour from the geometry of this device. Thermal cycling of the same device does not address this.
   - *What to do:* Show the effect in a second HS, or at least at several excitation positions and directions in this one, with the spread in d_1/e(t) and µ.

10. **The comparison with earlier work and with the authors' own prior measurements is relegated to the SI.**
    - *Where:* Fig. S12 is the only comparison with previous TMD IX transport studies. The authors' related works (refs. 62, 66, 67) report long-range IX transport and localisation in similar MoSe2/WSe2 devices.
    - *What to do:* State in the main text what differs in this HS (twist angle, hBN encapsulation, gates, landscape), so that readers can see why efficient transport appears here and not before.

**C. Minor**

11. Figs. 1b–d: the data points carry no error bars, and the number of averaged acquisitions is not stated. The APD is scanned across the image plane, so profiles are built from sequential acquisitions. State how laser-power and position drift during a scan were controlled.
12. Fig. 3 a1/b1/c1: D, v and µ rise again at the highest temperatures (about 30–50 K) after a minimum near 20–30 K. This seems at odds with the statement that the efficient transport "vanishes with increasing temperature". Please comment.
13. Fig. 1c gives P_ex in µW, Fig. 1d in mW, and the maps in µW. Please use one unit throughout.
14. Approximate values are given as "ca." in some places and with "~" (rendered as "-") in others: "T - 10 K", "δE - 3 meV", "n - 2×10¹¹", "U(x) drops -10 meV on -10 µm". This is ambiguous and could be read as negative signs.
15. The Methods give a 0.1 ns rise time, but the Results section says 0.3 ns edge sharpness. Please reconcile.
16. Data are shown only up to about 5–11 ns, yet the pulse lasts 84 ns. Showing whether d_1/e saturates, and at what value, during the remainder of the pulse would help connect the time-resolved results to cw results in the literature.
17. Give the twist angle, the encapsulation and any gating in the main text, not only in the SI. These parameters are needed to interpret the moiré-potential and BH arguments.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Replace d_1/e from exponential fits within a 9 µm window with a robust measure of cloud spread, such as ⟨x²⟩(t) or a threshold-front position, computed from background-corrected I(x,t). Show that the ballistic-like regime survives this change.
2. Measure E(x,t) of the IX line to determine the landscape, the interaction shift and the IX temperature directly. Then refit with U measured rather than assumed, and include uncertainties on all extracted parameters.
3. Add one measurement specific to coherence (for example a spatial-coherence interferometer on the expanded cloud), or reframe the title, abstract and conclusion around "efficient non-diffusive IX transport" without implying superfluidity.
4. Demonstrate the effect on a second device or several excitation positions, including transport uphill against the landscape gradient.
