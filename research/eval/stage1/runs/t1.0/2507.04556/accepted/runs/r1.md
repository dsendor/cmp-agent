Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use time-resolved PL imaging to study interlayer (indirect) exciton transport in a MoSe2/WSe2 heterostructure. In a window of low temperature (below about 10 K) and intermediate excitation power (about 0.25 to 0.5 mW), they find that the 1/e extent of the IX cloud, d_1/e, grows roughly linearly in time, reaching about 20 µm within 5 ns. Diffusion, ballistic and drift-diffusion fits to this growth give very large transport coefficients (D up to about 10^3 cm^2/s, v about 10^6 cm/s, µ about 10^5 cm^2/eV s). The authors argue that these values, together with the loss of the effect at higher temperature and at both lower and higher density, are qualitatively consistent with IX superfluidity. If the result is robust, it is a notable result for the vdW exciton community: direct kinetics have been missing from most cw studies. However, both the headline "anomalously high mobility" and the link to superfluidity currently rest on a single observable, d_1/e(t), extracted from a cloud confined to a heterostructure about 9 µm long, with no independent coherence or temperature evidence.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Measuring transport kinetics directly with time-resolved imaging, rather than steady-state cloud sizes, is the right approach for separating diffusive from ballistic or drift-dominated transport. The rectangular 84 ns pulse with 0.1 to 0.3 ns edges makes the onset well defined.
- Fig. 3 maps temperature and power systematically, and the non-monotonic power dependence together with the loss of the effect near 10 K is a clear phenomenological fingerprint that other groups can test.
- The three analysis frameworks (diffusion, ballistic, and drift-diffusion with mean-field interaction and a built-in gradient) agree on where the fast-transport regime lies, so its location in the (T, P_ex) plane does not depend on the model.
- The authors state that the phenomena reproduce after several cooldowns to 2 K and warmups to room temperature.
- Source data are deposited.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Finite heterostructure size and how d_1/e is extracted. The Fig. 1 caption says that d_1/e comes from exponential fits to I(x,t) "in the region from the excitation spot to the HS edge, x = 0–9 µm". Yet the reported d_1/e reaches 15 to 22 µm (Figs. 1b, 1c, 2), which is two to three times the fit window. In Fig. 1a, the 11 ns profile is almost flat between 0 and 9 µm. When the cloud fills a bounded region and piles up at, or is reflected from, the HS edge, the profile flattens and the fitted decay length diverges whatever the intrinsic transport. That alone could produce the late-time upturn in d_1/e at 500 µW (Fig. 2, last three points, which none of the three models reproduce) and could make the growth look superlinear in d^2. This matters because the "anomalously fast d_1/e growth" and every derived D, v and µ in Fig. 3 come from these fits.
   The authors should:
   - simulate Eqs. 1 to 3 on a domain of the real HS length, with explicit boundary conditions (reflecting or absorbing edge, or edge-enhanced emission), and fit the full I(x,t) profiles rather than d_1/e;
   - report a measure that stays bounded in a finite domain, such as the mean-square displacement over the measured window or the arrival time of the front at fixed x;
   - show the profiles with fit uncertainties for the high-power, low-temperature points, and state the error on d_1/e when d_1/e exceeds the fit window;
   - if possible, repeat the measurement with the excitation spot farther from the edge, or along a longer direction of the HS.

2. Other explanations for the near-linear d_1/e(t) and the non-monotonic power dependence. The paper assigns linear growth to ballistic transport or high mobility, but several conventional mechanisms predict the same signatures and are not excluded.
   - (i) Drift down the built-in potential gradient. The SI describes U(x) dropping about 10 meV over 10 µm (1 meV/µm) along the measurement direction. Drift in a uniform gradient gives a constant velocity, so d_1/e grows linearly by construction. The extracted µ then depends on how well that gradient is known and on whether transport is measured only downhill. Measurements in the opposite (uphill) direction or along y, or a map of U(x) from the local IX energy with uncertainties, are needed.
   - (ii) Hot-exciton or phonon-wind expansion. Excitation at 1.689 eV is resonant with the WSe2 direct exciton (DX), so IXs form with excess energy. Hot-exciton and "halo" transport in TMDs (refs. 39, 42) produce fast early expansion that weakens as the lattice warms. The effect vanishing above 10 K does not, on its own, single out superfluidity.
   - (iii) The high-power suppression in Fig. 1d (1 to 4 mW) could come from laser heating of the exciton gas and lattice, exciton-exciton annihilation, or an exciton Mott crossover to an electron-hole plasma. Any of these would lower the extracted mobility without a superfluid-to-normal transition.
   The authors should give the spectrally resolved IX energy and linewidth versus x and t, the IX lifetime versus P_ex, and an estimate of exciton temperature versus P_ex. They should also explain why each alternative fails to reproduce the measured (T, P_ex) dependence.

3. Evidence for the superfluid interpretation. The link to superfluidity rests on a large mobility despite about 10 meV disorder, its loss above about 10 K, and its non-monotonic density dependence. None of these is unique to a superfluid, and the comparison with the Bose-Hubbard (BH) model is explicitly qualitative: superfluid for N between about 0 and about 1 per site, with a density estimate N ~ 1/2 whose uncertainty is not given. The abstract's phrase "qualitatively consistent with theoretical predictions of IX superfluidity" is careful, but the text then frames the result as "inconsistent with the diffusive transport". Direct signatures are available in this setup:
   - first-order spatial coherence of the IX emission from shift-interferometry, checked to appear in the same (T, P_ex) window as the fast transport;
   - a threshold-like change in transport at a critical density or temperature, with error bars;
   - the dependence on the size of the disorder or moiré potential.
   Without at least a coherence measurement or a quantitative BH estimate (hopping J, U and the predicted transition temperature for this moiré period), the superfluidity discussion should be cut back to a hypothesis and the title/abstract framing adjusted.

B. Should address

4. Effective exciton temperature. All temperatures quoted are cryostat temperatures, and the fast-transport regime is defined using k_B T ≲ 1 meV. The relevant powers reach 0.5 mW in a focused spot, with 84 ns pulses at a 300 ns period. The IX temperature, and likely the local lattice temperature, may sit well above 1.7 K. That bears on the "thermal energy too low to delocalize" argument, on the comparison with the BH transition temperature of about 10 K, and on the Einstein relation. Please estimate the IX temperature, for example from the high-energy tail of the IX spectrum or from the temperature calibration of linewidth or energy, as a function of P_ex and time after the pulse onset.

5. Using the Einstein relation in a regime claimed to be degenerate. Eq. 3 sets D' = µ k_B T. That holds for a classical, non-degenerate gas in local equilibrium, which is the opposite of the quantum-degenerate, superfluid regime invoked to interpret the result. It also ties D' to the cryostat T (see concern 4). Please state how µ changes if D' is left free or uses the degenerate-gas form, and discuss whether a local-equilibrium drift-diffusion description holds when the inferred mean free path is long.

6. Density and moiré-filling calibration. The density n of about 2×10^11 cm^-2 comes from the mean-field ("plate capacitor") formula with d_z of about 0.6 nm and ε of about 7.4, applied to a 3 meV shift. The text acknowledges that correlations raise n for a given shift, by factors that are commonly 2 to 5 in the cited literature. Converting n to N ~ 1/2 per site also needs the moiré period, but the twist angle, its uncertainty, the stacking (R or H) and any evidence of a moiré potential are not in the main text. Because the BH comparison depends on N, please give the twist angle with its uncertainty and how it was determined, the moiré cell area, and a density range that includes the correlation correction. Then show whether N ~ 1/2 survives.

7. Disorder amplitude. The roughly 10 meV IX PL linewidth is taken as the in-plane disorder amplitude, and this is central to the argument that high mobility "despite substantial disorder" is anomalous. The linewidth may also contain homogeneous broadening, unresolved singlet/triplet or moiré sub-bands, interaction broadening and spatial averaging over the gradient U(x). Please show the spectra (and the low-power linewidth) and justify the identification, or present 10 meV as an upper bound.

8. Quality and uncertainty of the fits in Figs. 2 and 3. In Fig. 2, all three models underestimate the 500 µW data at late times and are too steep at early times, and the text admits the approaches "do not aim to provide an accurate description". Fig. 3 nevertheless reports D, v and µ over two orders of magnitude without error bars. The colour maps (a3, b3, c3) are interpolated from a sparse, irregular grid, and the white unmeasured region may hide structure. Please:
   - give confidence intervals for every fitted parameter, together with the residuals;
   - mark the measured (T, P_ex) points on the maps;
   - comment on the non-monotonic features in Figs. 3a1, b1 and c1 (a dip near 25 to 30 K followed by a rise at the highest T), which the text does not discuss.

9. Mean free path and the "long-range ballistic" claim. The text claims l_m ∝ τ_m indicates "long-range ballistic transport", but the numbers are only in the SI. With µ ~ 10^5 cm^2/eV s and m ≈ m_0, τ_m is of order tens of picoseconds. With a thermal velocity at 1.7 K, l_m is then sub-micrometre, far shorter than the 20 µm expansion. Please give τ_m, the velocity assumed and l_m in the main text, and reconcile them with the d_1/e growth over 5 ns. Otherwise, rephrase "ballistic" as "drift-like" or "near-linear".

10. PL intensity as a measure of density, and the generation profile. Converting I(x,t) to n(x,t) assumes uniform IX radiative efficiency along x. Near the HS edge, with gradients in local stacking, strain or dielectric environment, and with conversion between bright and dark (or spin-forbidden) IX states, that assumption can fail. The generation term Λ(x) is taken from the MoSe2 DX profile, which ignores diffusion of WSe2 DXs and interlayer charge transfer before IX formation. Please show a spatial map of cw IX brightness or energy across the HS, and test how the fitted parameters depend on the assumed Λ(x).

11. Number of samples. The main text describes one MoSe2/WSe2 HS and does not say whether the effect was seen in other heterostructures or at other spots on this one. Reproducibility across cooldowns is stated but not shown. Given the extraordinary claim, please report how many devices or locations were measured and show the key Fig. 1b–d behaviour for at least a second device or position, ideally one with a different twist angle or with an hBN spacer, where the BH picture predicts a different outcome.

12. Comparison with earlier work. Fig. S12 compares the d_1/e(t) curves with earlier studies, but the main text does not explain why the effect was absent in earlier kinetic studies of similar MoSe2/WSe2 HS (refs. 52–54, 56, 57, 61), including the authors' own (refs. 66, 67). Nor does it relate the result to the recent superfluidity report in ref. 65. Please identify the sample or measurement parameters (twist angle, encapsulation, gating, temperature, density) that set this sample apart.

C. Minor

13. Mobility is given in cm^2/eV s. Please state the convention (with energy in eV) and, for comparison with the literature, also give values in cm^2/V s.

14. Fig. 1a: the DX profile and the times are hard to tell apart in the legend. Please state the IX spectral window used for I(x,t), and explain the data beyond x = 9 µm (out to 12 µm) that lie outside the HS edge given in the caption.

15. Eq. 3 and the text use both D and D' to avoid confusion. It would be clearer to write D_eff = µ k_B T explicitly, and to state the boundary conditions and numerical domain of the simulations in the main text.

16. In "U(x) drops ~10 meV on ~10 µm", please state the sign convention, that is, whether the IX energy decreases away from the excitation spot, and how the profile was measured.

17. The phrase "IX mass in the HS, close to the free electron mass" should cite a value with its source, and the statement "τ_m ∝ µm" (mean free time proportional to mobility times mass) should be written with its constants.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Replace the d_1/e analysis with a global fit of the full I(x,t) on a finite domain that matches the HS geometry and edge boundary conditions, and report fitted parameters with uncertainties. This one change would settle whether the "anomalously fast" growth is intrinsic.
2. Add a spatial-coherence measurement (shift-interferometry of the IX emission) across the same (T, P_ex) grid as Fig. 3. Fast transport appearing together with extended coherence would greatly strengthen the superfluid link.
3. Measure transport in the direction opposite to, and perpendicular to, the built-in gradient, and report the IX temperature versus power. Together these separate gradient drift and heating from intrinsic high mobility.
4. Make the BH comparison quantitative: estimate the moiré period from the twist angle, J and U from the moiré potential, and the predicted transition temperature and filling window, each with uncertainties. Compare these with the measured T of about 10 K and N of about 1/2 including the correlation correction to n.
