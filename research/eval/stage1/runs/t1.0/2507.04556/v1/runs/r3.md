Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors use time-resolved PL imaging to follow the expansion of an indirect-exciton (IX) cloud in a single hBN-encapsulated MoSe2/WSe2 heterostructure. At low temperature (≲10 K) and intermediate excitation power (~0.5 mW), they report an apparent diffusivity of D ~ 10^3 cm²/s, two to four orders of magnitude above earlier time-resolved values in TMD heterostructures. They argue that the expansion matches a "ballistic" model better than a diffusive one, and that it is consistent with a Bose–Hubbard (BH) superfluid in the moiré lattice. If the expansion is intrinsic, scattering-free IX transport would be an important result: it would tie together the authors' earlier cw observations of long-range IX transport and spin transport in the same sample. As the paper stands, however, the evidence only shows that the IX cloud expands fast in one sample. That this is ballistic or superfluid transport is not established. Ordinary interaction-driven (dipolar-pressure) expansion, extraction artifacts in a bounded sample, and non-equilibrium drive effects have not been excluded.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A direct, time-resolved measurement of IX cloud expansion in a vdW heterostructure, with a large parameter map in temperature and excitation power (Figs. 3, 4, S3, S6).
- A striking contrast within one sample between fast expansion (1.7 K, 0.5 mW; Fig. 1a) and slow, conventional expansion (50 K; Fig. S2). This gives a useful internal reference.
- The lifetime is measured as a function of power and temperature (Fig. S3) and fed into the simulations. Some robustness to the fit window is checked (Figs. S5, S6). Results are reported to survive repeated thermal cycling.
- The topic (long-range IX transport and moiré bosonic phases) is timely and of broad interest.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **Interaction-driven (dipolar repulsion) expansion is not excluded. The authors' own SI estimate implies it is large.**
   - *What the paper says.* The SI section "Estimates of IX mean free time and mean free path" writes the effective diffusivity as D = D0(1 + n·u0/kBT). The main text gives the interaction shift in the efficient-transport regime as δE = n·u0 ≈ 3 meV. At T = 1.7 K, kBT ≈ 0.15 meV.
   - *Why it matters.* The enhancement factor (1 + n·u0/kBT) is therefore ≈ 20, and it grows as T falls. This alone predicts an apparent D that is large at low T and falls with increasing T: exactly the trend the paper (p. 3–4) cites as evidence against diffusive transport. The non-monotonic power dependence (Fig. 3b) also needs no superfluid: at the highest powers the lifetime shortens (Fig. S3), the density saturates, and new species appear (Fig. S7).
   - *The drift picture gives the same speed.* A dipolar gas with n·u0 ≈ 3 meV and m ≈ m0 has a pressure-driven sound/expansion speed c ≈ (n·u0/m)^1/2 ≈ 2×10^6 cm/s. That is the same order as the fitted "ballistic" velocity v ≈ 7×10^5–10^6 cm/s (Fig. 4, Fig. S4). Hydrodynamic expansion of a strongly repulsive cloud is front-like and nearly linear in time, so it would also be fit better by Eq. 2 than by constant-D diffusion.
   - *Requested.* Simulate the nonlinear drift–diffusion equation with density-dependent D(n) = D0(1 + n·u0/kBT), using the measured δE(n) and τ(Pex, T), and fit D0 (not D) across the (Pex, T) map. Show whether a modest, T-independent D0 reproduces Figs. 1–3. Until this is done, "anomalously high diffusivity" and "ballistic" are not demonstrated. The quantity extracted with constant D in Eq. 1 is only an effective spreading rate.

2. **Eq. 2 is a unidirectional drift (advection) equation, not a model of ballistic transport, and the χ² comparison cannot discriminate the models.**
   - *Wrong model for the claim.* ∂n/∂t = −v∇n in 1D translates the whole profile in +x at speed v. Isotropic ballistic motion from a source would instead require counter-propagating ±v populations, or an angular distribution of velocities. A constant drift away from the spot is what one expects from a force: dipolar pressure (Concern 1), a strain or potential gradient towards the HS edge, or phonon wind. It does not require the absence of scattering. Fig. S4b shows that the "ballistic" simulation shifts the profile bodily to +x.
   - *Requested on the model.* Specify how Eq. 2 is applied on the x < 0 side of the spot. Compare the data with a genuinely ballistic kinetic model, for example a Boltzmann/free-flight model with an isotropic velocity distribution, the measured τ, and a finite mean free path as a free parameter.
   - *The χ² test is not a goodness-of-fit test.*
     - It is defined as Σ(m−s)²/(νs) (Fig. 5), with no measurement uncertainties, and ν counts time points rather than degrees of freedom.
     - Both models have one free parameter. Fig. 5a,b shows χ²_D and χ²_v comparable (~0.1) at most settings.
     - The distinction rests on a few points. In Fig. 5b at 1.7 and 6 K, χ²_v = 0.35–0.5 is not small.
     - The measured d1/e(t) in Fig. 2 bends upward after ~3 ns, which neither model reproduces.
   - *Requested on the statistics.* Give error bars on d1/e(t), proper reduced χ² values or likelihood ratios with uncertainties, and residual plots. Also say what physical process would produce the superlinear d1/e(t).

3. **The d1/e observable is extracted in a small, bounded sample, and boundary effects and edge emission may dominate it.**
   - *What is fitted.* d1/e is an exponential fit over x = 0–9 µm, and the HS edge lies at about x = 9–10 µm (Fig. 1a caption; Fig. S1b). Yet d1/e reaches ~22 µm (Figs. 1b–d, 2): more than twice the fit window and larger than the sample. At t = 11 ns the profile in Fig. 1a is essentially flat, with an upturn at x ≈ 9–11 µm. For a nearly flat profile, d1/e diverges and becomes very sensitive to small intensity changes at the edge.
   - *Alternative explanations.*
     - Accumulation or reflection of IXs at the HS boundary.
     - IX PL waveguided in the ~70 nm hBN stack and scattered out at layer edges.
     - Emission from regions outside the MoSe2/WSe2 overlap (the profiles extend to x ≈ 12 µm).
     - Any of these could produce a growing apparent d1/e without fast transport.
   - *Requested.*
     - State the simulation domain, the boundary conditions at the HS edges, and whether the simulated d1/e is extracted with the same 0–9 µm window and the same fit procedure as the data.
     - Present a model-independent observable: the arrival time (rise delay or half-rise time) of I(t) at fixed x versus x. A ballistic or drift front gives a linear delay with slope 1/v; diffusion gives a quadratic delay; waveguided emission gives none.
     - Add a measurement with the excitation spot moved to different positions, including the centre of the long axis and near the opposite edge.
     - Justify the 1D approximation. The HS is ~10 µm wide in y (Fig. S1b), so the expansion is 2D for the first ~1–2 ns. A 1D model then overestimates D by up to a factor of ~2 at early times.

4. **The superfluid / Bose–Hubbard interpretation is not supported quantitatively and is in tension with the paper's own numbers.**
   - *Disorder far exceeds the hopping.* The paper estimates disorder ~10 meV from the PL linewidth (14–20 meV, SI "IX PL spectra"), a moiré amplitude ~8 meV, and an inferred hopping J ~ 1.6 K ≈ 0.14 meV. With disorder about two orders of magnitude larger than J, the disordered BH model predicts a Bose glass, not a superfluid. The SI's percolation argument (superfluid puddles that grow as disorder falls) is qualitative and contradicts the linewidth estimate.
   - *The reasoning is circular.* J is inferred from the observed Tc ≈ 10 K via Tc ≈ 4πNJ. The moiré amplitude is then inferred from J and compared with literature. Neither step tests the BH picture.
   - *Group-velocity bound.* For a moiré band with J ≈ 0.14 meV and b = 17 nm, the maximum group velocity is ~2Jb/ħ ≈ 7×10^5 cm/s. The fitted v reaches 10^6 cm/s, which deserves comment.
   - *No direct evidence.* There is no direct evidence of superfluidity: no first-order coherence measurement (interferometry), no critical velocity, no sharp onset at Tc. In Fig. 3a, D varies smoothly from 1.7 to ~10 K and then shows an unexplained dip at 30 K followed by a rise at 40–50 K. The SI also notes that atomic reconstruction can modify the moiré potential. At a near-60° (H-stacked) 1.1° twist, reconstruction into domains is expected, which may invalidate the rigid moiré BH description.
   - *Requested.* Remove the superfluid statements from the abstract and conclusion, or support them with a quantitative comparison: a disordered BH phase diagram using the measured disorder, the expected v for the inferred J, and a coherence measurement if feasible.

**B. Should address**

5. **The SI's mean-free-path estimate contradicts the "long-range ballistic" statement in the main text.**
   - The main text (p. 3) argues that the high D implies anomalously high τm and lm and therefore ballistic transport. The SI, however, obtains τm ~ 0.2 ns and lm ~ 2 µm, and states that these formulae describe a "classical diffusive transport regime".
   - An lm of ~2 µm is about ten times smaller than the 20 µm cloud extension. Moreover, the SI reaches this value only after dividing D by the interaction factor (1 + n·u0/kBT) ≈ 20. This supports interaction-enhanced diffusion (Concern 1), not ballistic motion.
   - Please reconcile the two statements and give the numbers explicitly in the main text.

6. **Non-equilibrium drive effects (laser heating, hot IXs, phonon wind) are not addressed.**
   - Excitation is resonant with the WSe2 DX at 1.689 eV, ~300 meV above the IX (~1.39 eV), at up to several mW focused to a few-µm spot. Phonon wind and hot-carrier halos are known to produce fast, directional exciton expansion in TMDs (e.g. Ref. 38).
   - The efficient regime appears only at the lowest bath temperatures and intermediate powers, and transport is fastest during the first few ns of an 80 ns pulse. Both features are compatible with transient hot-IX or phonon effects.
   - *Requested.*
     - Give the spot size and peak power density.
     - Estimate the local lattice heating.
     - Report the IX spectral line position or width versus x and t as an effective-temperature probe.
     - Ideally, vary the excitation energy, for example excitation resonant with the MoSe2 DX or near-resonant with the IX, at matched IX density.

7. **The whole conclusion rests on one sample and, apparently, one excitation position.**
   - The SI states that efficient transport was realized in only one sample, and attributes its absence elsewhere to disorder.
   - There is no second device, no null or reference geometry, and no map over excitation position within the sample. A strain or thickness gradient, or a bubble, could create a built-in drift towards one edge. That is exactly what Eq. 2 describes.
   - *Requested.*
     - Show data for several excitation positions, and for expansion towards −x versus +x.
     - If possible, show a second device. At minimum, present a same-sample reference region (e.g. a different moiré domain) under matched conditions.
     - State the uncertainty on the claim that this sample is "less disordered" than others, given that its PL linewidth (14–20 meV) is not narrow.

8. **The density calibration and the "N ~ 1/2" filling are uncertain by large factors.**
   - n is obtained from the mean-field capacitor formula. Correlations are known to make this formula underestimate n by several-fold for dipolar excitons, and in a moiré lattice with strong on-site repulsion it is not appropriate.
   - The value δE ≈ 3 meV is not tied to a specific time, position, or spectral feature in Fig. S7. The density varies strongly in space and time across the measured cloud, so a single N cannot be assigned.
   - b = 17 nm follows from δθ = 1.1° estimated from flake edges. The accuracy of this alignment (typically ±0.5–1°) would change b², and thus N, by a factor of ~2–4.
   - Please give an uncertainty on δθ and N, specify where and when δE was measured, and acknowledge the range of N compatible with the data.

9. **Spectrally integrated detection mixes IX species.**
   - The transport is measured with an 850 nm long-pass filter that integrates all IX emission. Fig. S7 shows narrow localized lines at low power and a higher-energy "double-occupancy" line at high power.
   - Changes in d1/e could reflect a change in the emitting species, or in radiative efficiency versus x, rather than in exciton transport. The near-flat profile at long times (Fig. 1a) is especially sensitive to such changes.
   - Spectrally resolved (or at least band-filtered) profiles for the main and high-energy IX lines would clarify which population is expanding.

10. **The lifetime model and fit-window dependence affect the extracted D and v at the factor-of-several level.**
    - τ is measured from the decay after the pulse ends (Fig. S3) but is used during the pulse, when density, and thus τ, is position- and time-dependent.
    - Changing the fit window from 0–5 ns to 0–3 ns lowers the peak D from ~10^3 to ~5×10^2 cm²/s (compare Fig. 3a,b with Fig. S6a,b). The SI calls these "similar".
    - Please quantify the uncertainty of D and v, from the fit, the window choice and the τ model, and add error bars to Figs. 3 and 4.
    - The 2D colour maps (Figs. 3c, 4c, 5c,d, S3) are interpolated from a sparse grid. Please mark the measured points.

11. **The comparison with literature diffusivities is not like-for-like.**
    - The claim of "orders of magnitude higher" diffusivity compares D ~ 10^3 cm²/s at 1.7 K and an optimized power with D ~ 0.1–2 cm²/s from Refs. 52, 53 and 55. Those were obtained in different samples, temperatures and densities, and with different analyses (MSD of a Gaussian versus d1/e of an exponential tail).
    - Within this sample, the regular-regime value (e.g. ~10–30 cm²/s at 50 K or low power, Fig. 3) is already ~10 times above those references. This suggests a method-dependent offset.
    - Please analyse the present data with the same metric as the literature, for example the second moment of I(x,t), at least for one reference condition.

**C. Minor**

12. The Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 maps have essentially identical structure, because each converts the same d1/e(t) growth into one parameter. They do not independently support either model. Consider merging them or stating this explicitly.

13. Twist angle: for H stacking the relevant misalignment is from 60°. Please write it as 60° ± 1.1° (with uncertainty) to avoid confusion.

14. The Fig. S7 caption lists Pex = 5, 50 and 4000 µW, but the panel is labelled 5, 500 and 4000 µW.

15. In Eq. 1, Δ should be identified as the Laplacian, and the dimensionality used for Λ(x) and n should be stated (per unit length in 1D?).

16. The Fig. 1 caption says the fits are made for x = 0–9 µm "to the HS edge". In Fig. S1b the MoSe2 boundary at y = 0 appears to lie nearer x ≈ 12–15 µm. Please mark the HS edge on Fig. 1a.

17. The Fig. 2 caption and the main text mix "estimates" and "fits". The number of time points in each fit, and whether t = 0 points are included, should be stated.

18. "Superfluid" and "superfluidity" are used interchangeably with "ballistic". The text should keep these as distinct claims.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Fit all (Pex, T) data with a nonlinear drift–diffusion model, D(n) = D0(1 + n·u0/kBT) with the measured δE and τ. Report D0 and say whether anything remains unexplained.
2. Add model-independent transport observables: the front arrival time versus x, and second-moment spreading with error bars. Add spatial controls: several excitation positions, both expansion directions, and the location of the HS edge.
3. Bound heating and hot-carrier effects with spectrally resolved profiles and a change of excitation energy at matched density.
4. Narrow the conclusions to "fast, non-diffusive-looking IX expansion" unless coherence or a second sample supports the superfluid interpretation. Move the BH discussion to an outlook, with a quantitative disordered-BH comparison.
