Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors image the expansion of an interlayer-exciton (IX) photoluminescence cloud in one MoSe2/WSe2 heterostructure with time resolution, using 80 ns rectangular pulses and a scanned APD. In a window of low temperature (T below about 10 K) and intermediate power (around 0.5 mW), the 1/e decay length d_1/e grows from about 3 to about 22 µm within 5 ns. Fitting this growth with a constant-D diffusion model gives D ~ 10^3 cm^2/s, two to four orders of magnitude above the values usually reported for TMD interlayer excitons. A one-parameter "ballistic" advection model fits better by a chi^2 measure. The authors read the result as long-range ballistic transport consistent with Bose-Hubbard superfluidity in the moiré lattice. Time-resolved evidence for a non-diffusive IX transport regime in a vdW heterostructure would be significant, and it would give a microscopic basis for the same group's earlier cw long-range transport and spin transport results. As presented, however, the extraction of D and v and the choice between transport models do not yet rule out geometric, nonlinear and optical-readout explanations of the fast growth of d_1/e. The link to superfluidity is qualitative.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Direct time-resolved imaging of IX cloud expansion on a nanosecond scale, with a full map over temperature and power (Figs. 1, 3, 4, 5, S3). This is more informative than steady-state cw extent measurements.
- The non-monotonic dependence of the extracted D on power, and its suppression above about 10 K, is a distinctive pattern. It does not follow the trends expected for simple disorder-limited diffusion, where D rises with temperature and with density through screening.
- The lifetime is measured independently as a function of T and P_ex (Fig. S3) and fed into the simulations rather than fitted. The analysis is also repeated over a shorter fit window (Figs. S5, S6).
- The topic is timely, and the data could connect to the cw long-distance IX transport and spin transport reported in Refs. 64 and 65.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The size of the sample bounds the extracted d_1/e, D and v, and the model does not include the sample boundaries.** In Fig. 1a, d_1/e comes from an exponential fit over x = 0 to 9 µm, "from the excitation spot to the HS edge". Yet the values reported in Figs. 1b-d and 2 reach 20 to 22 µm, more than twice the fitted window. When the length of the fitted window exceeds the fitted decay length, d_1/e mainly measures how flat the profile is inside a finite box. A cloud that fills a closed region and piles up at a reflecting edge, or that loses particles at an absorbing edge, gives a flat profile whatever the microscopic transport. The latest profile in Fig. 1a is nearly flat out to x ~ 10 µm and then falls at the edge. The SI says only the t = 0 to 3 ns window stays "within the HS area". The main-text values of D and v (Figs. 3, 4) nevertheless come from 0 to 5 ns fits that include points beyond that window. The 0 to 3 ns fits (Fig. S6) give D about a factor of 2 lower at 1.7 K (about 350 against about 700 cm^2/s). Three further gaps compound the problem:
   - The simulations are one-dimensional, and their spatial domain and boundary conditions are not stated.
   - The HS is roughly 10 µm wide along y (Fig. S1b), comparable to the cloud size, so the 1D reduction and the lateral edges both matter.
   - The position of the excitation spot relative to every edge of the HS is not specified.

   The authors should:
   - include the real HS geometry, the spot position and explicit edge boundary conditions in the simulations (2D, or at least 1D with the measured edge position);
   - fit the measured profiles I(x,t) directly, rather than the single scalar d_1/e;
   - report D and v only from the window in which the cloud is unaffected by the edges;
   - show how sensitive the results are to the boundary condition chosen.

2. **Nonlinear density effects, which are known to mimic fast expansion, are not modelled.** Eq. 1 uses a constant D and a linear loss n/tau. At the densities where the effect is strongest, the paper itself invokes interaction energies of about 3 meV against k_BT ≈ 0.15 meV (n u0/k_BT ~ 20). Two processes then matter:
   - *Dipolar repulsion.* Expansion driven by IX repulsion gives a density-dependent D(n) = D0(1 + n u0/k_BT). The SI uses exactly this expression to estimate tau_m. This mechanism produces fast, front-like expansion at early times and accounts for large apparent diffusivities in TMD heterostructures (Ref. 55).
   - *Exciton-exciton annihilation and density-dependent recombination.* These flatten the centre of the profile and increase the apparent 1/e width with no transport at all, an effect well documented in TMD halo and expansion studies (for example Ref. 38).

   The power dependence in Fig. 3b (D rising roughly linearly with P_ex up to about 0.5 mW) is what both mechanisms would give. The observation that D falls again at higher power could come from the lifetime shortening seen in Fig. S3, or from heating. The authors acknowledge the constant-D simplification ("subject of future works"), but the anomalous D is the paper's central result, and it cannot be separated from these effects without modelling them. The authors should:
   - fit the data with a drift-diffusion model that includes D(n) from the measured energy shift and a bimolecular loss term γn^2, with γ fixed from the power dependence of the spatially integrated decay;
   - show whether a small D0, such as the 10 to 30 cm^2/s seen at 30 to 50 K, reproduces Fig. 1a once these terms are included;
   - check that the spatially integrated PL scales linearly with power in the regime of efficient transport.

3. **The comparison between diffusive and ballistic transport does not support "agrees with long-range ballistic transport".**
   - *What Eq. 2 describes.* As written, the term -v∇n is a one-directional advection equation. A source at x = 0 would then be translated, not spread symmetrically. The paper does not say how a symmetric outward ballistic expansion was implemented, for example sgn(x)·v, a distribution of velocities, or a kinetic equation. Fig. S4b shows a profile extending to both sides, which suggests something other than Eq. 2 was solved.
   - *The test statistic.* The chi^2 in Fig. 5 is Σ(m - s)^2/(ν s). This is a Pearson-type normalisation by the model value, not by the measurement uncertainty. ν is the number of points, not the degrees of freedom. No error bars on d_1/e are shown anywhere. The "χ^2 test" therefore has no statistical meaning, and the values (0.3 against 1.3 at best) do not establish that one model is excluded.
   - *Neither model fits.* At 500 µW, d_1/e(t) in Fig. 2 is convex (accelerating). Neither the √t-like diffusive curve nor the linear ballistic curve follows it, and the ballistic fit also undershoots the last points by about 5 µm. An accelerating d_1/e is what one would expect from the edge effects of Concern 1 or from a profile flattened by annihilation (Concern 2), not from free ballistic motion.
   - *Internal inconsistency.* The SI gives a mean free path l_m ~ 2 µm, while the IXs travel 10 to 20 µm. Transport over many times l_m is diffusive by definition. The main-text statement that IXs "move with no scattering over the range ~l_m" therefore does not support ballistic transport over the measured distances.

   The authors should:
   - state exactly which ballistic model was solved;
   - give uncertainties on d_1/e from the fits and repeated scans, and use them in a proper reduced chi^2 or a likelihood comparison;
   - compare models with the number of free parameters matched (each also allowing a free offset and time zero);
   - analyse the mean-squared displacement σ^2(t) of the profiles, where ballistic transport gives t^2 and diffusive transport gives t;
   - reconcile the l_m estimate with the ballistic interpretation, or withdraw that interpretation.

4. **The PL profile is used as the density profile without justification, and heating or hot-exciton effects are not excluded.** d_1/e is extracted from spectrally integrated PL (long-pass filter at 850 nm), so I(x,t) ∝ n(x,t) is assumed. Several features of this experiment make that assumption doubtful:
   - *Excess energy and local heating.* Excitation is resonant with the WSe2 DX at 1.689 eV, about 300 meV above the IX. At 0.5 mW focused to a spot a few µm across (the spot size and power density are not given), the local lattice and exciton temperatures near the spot can be well above the 1.7 K bath.
   - *Spatially varying emission efficiency.* Hot IXs leave the light cone and emit less. The emission therefore depends on position and time, and the profile can look flattened, or even dipped at the centre, as in the IX "inner ring" physics of Ref. 9 cited here.
   - *Mixed species.* Fig. S7 shows that the spectrum changes with power (narrow localized lines, then a second higher-energy line). Different species dominate at different positions and times, so integrated intensity is not a uniform measure of density.
   - *Phonon wind.* Non-equilibrium phonons generated at the spot can push excitons outward fast, which gives an alternative explanation of anomalous, non-monotonic expansion.

   The authors should:
   - measure spectrally resolved profiles, so that the local IX energy shift (a density proxy) and the linewidth (a temperature proxy) can be followed along x and t;
   - estimate the local temperature rise at 0.5 mW;
   - test whether the fast expansion persists at fixed absorbed density but with lower excess energy (excitation nearer the IX or the MoSe2 DX), or with a shorter pulse or lower duty cycle.

B. Should address

5. **The Bose-Hubbard superfluid interpretation is not independently tested, and the inputs to it are uncertain.** The abstract and conclusion state consistency with BH superfluidity. The supporting argument has several weak points:
   - *Filling.* The filling N ~ 1/2 rests on a mean-field (plate-capacitor) density estimate from a 3 meV shift, which can be off by a factor of several in correlated dipolar liquids. It also rests on a twist angle of 1.1° taken from flake edges. No uncertainty is given, the SI says SHG was deliberately not done, and with a(δθ)^-2 scaling a ±0.5° error changes N by a factor of 2 to 4. Near-60° (H-stacked) MoSe2/WSe2 at about 1° is also prone to atomic reconstruction (which the SI acknowledges), so b and the moiré amplitude may be ill-defined.
   - *Density varies within the measurement.* The density changes strongly in space and time across a single measurement, so assigning one N to "the efficient regime" is not meaningful.
   - *The T_c argument is circular.* J is inferred from the observed 10 K scale through T_c ~ 4πNJ, a moiré amplitude is then inferred from J, and this amplitude is declared consistent with the literature. That shows the numbers are self-consistent, not that the prediction is tested.
   - *Ballistic is not the same as superfluid.* Ballistic, scattering-free transport is not specific to a superfluid. No signature unique to superfluidity is presented: no spatial or temporal coherence (for example g^(1) from shift-interferometry, as this group has done in GaAs), no critical velocity, no vortices, no threshold.

   The authors should:
   - plot D against the estimated filling, with error bands that propagate the uncertainty in twist angle and density;
   - give an independent estimate of the moiré amplitude and J;
   - measure coherence in the efficient-transport regime, or else reword the abstract and conclusions to "phenomenological agreement with trends expected from" the BH model.

6. **The paper reports one sample, one spot and one direction of expansion.** The SI says the efficient transport "was realized in one sample". Only expansion from x = 0 towards the +x edge is analysed. The authors should:
   - show the expansion towards -x and along y, and from two or three other spot positions in the same HS, including positions farther from the edges;
   - report the spread of D and v between these positions.

   This would also help separate intrinsic transport from geometry (Concern 1) and from local inhomogeneity (the graphene electrode remnant near x = 2 µm, the flake boundaries). If other heterostructures were measured and showed ordinary diffusion, listing them with their parameters (twist angle, linewidth, stacking) would make the sample-specific claim much more informative.

7. **The extracted D and v have no uncertainties, and the maps hide how sparse the data are.**
   - Figs. 3 and 4 show single points with no error bars.
   - D changes by about a factor of 2 between the 0 to 5 ns and 0 to 3 ns windows (Fig. 3 against Fig. S6). This sensitivity is a systematic uncertainty larger than any statistical one and should be stated.
   - The colour maps in Figs. 3c, 4c, 5c,d and S3 are interpolated over a grid whose measured points are not marked.
   - The dip at about 30 K and the rise again at 40 to 50 K (Figs. 3a, 4a) are not discussed.

   The authors should mark the measured (T, P_ex) points on the maps, add error bars from the fit covariance and from repeated measurements, and comment on the features at 30 to 50 K.

8. **The source term and lifetime inputs need justification.**
   - Λ(x) is taken as the WSe2 DX PL profile, which already includes DX diffusion before charge transfer. The laser spot size is not given.
   - IX formation is assumed to be local and instantaneous. IXs may instead form after DX or charge transport in the WSe2 monolayer, which extends beyond the MoSe2 (edges at about 30 against 20 µm).
   - tau is defined as the "initial decay time" after the pulse ends (Fig. S3). This is a single number, but during a high-density pulse the decay is generally non-exponential, and tau depends on local density.

   The authors should state the laser spot profile, show that the results do not depend on the choice of Λ (laser profile against DX profile), and use a position- and density-dependent decay or the nonlinear loss term of Concern 2.

9. **The comparison with literature diffusivities is between different quantities.** The "orders of magnitude" comparison uses D from Refs. 52, 53 and 55, which mostly come from the evolution of the second moment or variance under pulsed excitation. Here D comes from a 1/e length of an exponential tail fit within a 9 µm window, under 80 ns quasi-cw pumping. The authors should apply the same variance-based analysis to their own data, at least at early times, before claiming a factor of 10^3 to 10^4. They should also compare at matched densities, since D in this sample is already 10 to 30 cm^2/s at 30 to 50 K.

C. Minor

10. In Fig. 1a the time labels read 11, 2.5, 1.5, 1 and 0.5 ns, but Figs. 1b-d stop at 5 ns and the analogous Fig. S2 uses 5 ns. Please correct the label, or explain why an 11 ns profile is shown but not analysed.
11. The Fig. S7 caption gives P_ex = 5, 50 and 4000 µW, but the panel is labelled 5, 500 and 4000 µW.
12. The main text says the pulse has "0.3 ns edge sharpness". The SI gives a rise time of 0.1 ns and a fall time of 0.3 ns. Please make these consistent.
13. The disorder amplitude is "~10 meV, estimated from the PL linewidth" in the main text, but the SI quotes linewidths of 14 and about 20 meV. Similarly, the maximum D is "~10^3" in the main text and "1.3·10^3" in the SI.
14. Eq. 1 uses DΔn, but the simulations are 1D. State the operator and domain actually used, and whether a 2D-to-1D correction was applied to D.
15. Give the laser spot size, the absorbed power density and the absorption in the WSe2 layer, so that readers can estimate carrier densities and heating.
16. State that "twist angle δθ = 1.1°" refers to the deviation from 60° (H stacking), and give its uncertainty.
17. The SI estimates of tau_m and l_m use the classical diffusive relations, as the SI itself notes. The main text should not present l_m as evidence for ballistic transport without this caveat.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Replace the d_1/e analysis with direct fits of the full I(x,t) maps to a drift-diffusion model that includes dipolar repulsion, a bimolecular loss and the real HS boundaries. Report which features, if any, cannot be reproduced with a modest D0.
2. Add spectrally resolved, time-resolved imaging (energy shift and linewidth against x and t) so that density and effective temperature can be tracked independently of PL intensity.
3. Measure expansion from several spot positions and directions, ideally far from the edges, and in a second heterostructure or a control region with a different twist angle or more disorder, where the effect should be absent.
4. If the superfluid connection is to stay in the abstract, add a coherence measurement in the efficient-transport window. Otherwise limit the claim to anomalously fast, non-diffusive transport.
