Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript proposes a MOS-like, tensile-strained Ge quantum well on relaxed Ge1−xSnx whose ground state is light-hole (LH)-like. Using 6-band k·p theory and Schrieffer–Wolff effective Hamiltonians for planar, channel and dot geometries, it predicts a linear-in-k Rashba term β1 that a static gate field Fz can tune through zero. The out-of-plane g-factor can also be tuned through zero, while the in-plane g-factor stays large (|g∥| ≈ 8). An electrically switchable linear Rashba term in a planar Ge platform would interest the spin-qubit and hybrid-device communities. However, the authors' own earlier work [56, 57] already established the large linear Rashba term of LH Ge/GeSn wells. The main new content is therefore the gate-tunable zero ("on/off switch") and its effect on the EDSR Rabi frequency. Whether that zero is robust to the approximations and to the device physics left out of the model decides how much the paper adds.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The idea is clear and attractive: in an asymmetric LH well, the competition between the GeSn/Ge interface and the gate field makes β1 change sign at a finite field, Fz* ≈ 4.4 V/µm for lz = 15 nm and x = 16%. This gives a "β-line" that, within the model, is independent of lx, ly and the field angle θ (Fig. 3b,c).
- Eq. (3) is a transparent analytic result linking the channel g⊥ to the planar g⊥ and β1, including a sign change of g⊥. It is consistent with the numerics in Fig. 2c.
- The paper connects material parameters to a qubit figure of merit (the Rabi frequency, Fig. 3), going beyond band-structure statements alone.
- The proposal can be tested experimentally with current GeSn growth.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The 6-band k·p model leaves out the conduction band, which matters most in exactly this regime.** The LH ground state requires large biaxial tensile strain in Ge. For x = 16%, ε∥ = a(GeSn)/a(Ge) − 1 is roughly 2%, enough to push tensile-strained Ge towards a direct gap. The Γ conduction band then moves close to the LH band. LH states mix strongly with the Γ6 conduction band through the Kane parameter P. This coupling contributes directly to the LH g-tensor, especially g∥ through the 2κ-like and Kane terms, and to the linear Rashba coefficient. The main text (p. 2, "computed from 6-band k·p theory [60]") gives no estimate of these corrections. The paper's central claims are the location of the β1 = 0 line, the zero of g⊥ and the magnitude g∥ ≈ −8, and all of them may shift or change quantitatively under 8-band treatment.
   - Repeat at least the Fig. 2b (β1) and Fig. 2a/2e (g⊥, g∥) calculations in an 8-band model with the strain-dependent Γ gap of Ge at this strain.
   - Alternatively, give a quantitative bound on the conduction-band corrections at x = 16% and lz = 15 nm, and show how much Fz* moves.

2. **The model parameters and band alignment are not stated, so the headline numbers cannot be checked.** All material inputs (Luttinger parameters beyond γ2 and γ3, κ, q, deformation potentials, elastic constants, the GeSn lattice constant and its bowing, and the Ge/GeSn valence-band offsets for HH and LH under strain) are deferred to Supplemental Material [60], which is not part of the submission. The same applies to the surface-barrier height and the boundary conditions. The position of the β-line depends on the triangular barrier at the Ge/GeSn interface (p. 2), so it depends directly on the valence-band offset. That offset is poorly known for Sn contents near 16%.
   - In the main text, list the parameters that set Fz*, g⊥ and g∥.
   - Show how Fz* and the zero of g⊥ move when the valence-band offset and the strain (for example partial relaxation of the GeSn barrier, ±0.2% in ε∥) vary within their realistic uncertainty.

3. **The "on/off switch" is shown only for spin–orbit-mediated EDSR with a purely in-plane drive. Other driving channels that would remain active on the β-line are not assessed.**
   - The Rabi frequency in Fig. 3 is computed from Ω = (eFac/ħ)|⟨0|y|1⟩| with a uniform drive along y. In a real gate-defined dot, the plunger or drive gate also modulates Fz.
   - Fig. 2a/2c and Fig. 3 show that g⊥ (and hence the g-tensor) depends very strongly on Fz, with g⊥ crossing zero. Driving through g-tensor modulation (g-TMR, ∝ ∂g/∂Fz) would therefore not vanish at F*z. For tilted fields it could be large.
   - The claim that the qubit is "put in idle mode" in the off state (Conclusion) therefore does not yet follow.
   - Compute the g-TMR contribution, with a realistic out-of-plane component of the drive field, along the β-line for the field orientations of Fig. 3b. Compare it with the SOI-mediated Ω at nearby Fz.
   - Also state whether the cubic terms β2 and β3 (and β3^c in Eq. 2) vanish at exactly the same Fz as β1. The text only says the contour lines "coincide" and that this is "suggesting" full tunability (p. 3). Plot all three coefficients against Fz at lz = 15 nm so the coincidence can be judged quantitatively. Any residual cubic term sets a floor on the "off" Rabi frequency.

**B. Should address**

4. **"Large" and "efficient" are not quantified in absolute units.** The abstract and conclusion call the Rashba spin–orbit interaction "inherently large" and EDSR "efficient". But Fig. 3 gives Ω only in units of ωB·Fac/Fz, with a scale factor of ×10^4. Normalising by Fz, the quantity being varied, makes the Fz dependence in Fig. 3b,c hard to read.
   - Give Ω in MHz for a realistic Fac (for example 1–10 kV/m) and B0 = 0.05 T.
   - Quote the spin–orbit length lso = ħ²/(m β1), or ħ/(m vso), against the dot size.
   - Compare directly with reported Ge/SiGe heavy-hole (HH) qubits and with Ge/Si nanowires at the same field and drive amplitude.

5. **The claim of reduced anisotropy for field-orientation requirements conflicts with the paper's own results.** The abstract says the LH g-tensor is "less anisotropic than that of state-of-the-art HH qubits, alleviating precise magnetic field orientation requirements". Yet the paper's main feature is that g⊥ is tuned through zero (Figs. 2c, 3a). Near the g-lines the g-tensor is maximally anisotropic, and Ω and the qubit frequency become very sensitive to θ (Fig. 3b). Specify the operating points where the claim holds, for example g⊥ ≈ −3 against g∥ ≈ −8 at lz = 15 nm, and give the angular sensitivity ∂(g*)/∂θ at those points against typical HH values.

6. **Charge-noise dephasing is not evaluated, although it is the natural cost of strong gate tunability.** Because g⊥ and β1 vary strongly with Fz, the qubit frequency will be sensitive to electric-field noise.
   - Give ∂f_Larmor/∂Fz along Fig. 3's parameter space and identify any sweet spots.
   - In particular, check whether the β-line coincides with a point of small ∂g/∂Fz.
   - Without this, the comparison with the cited "spinless" qubit proposal [63] and the claimed robustness are not supported.

7. **The electrostatics of the proposed device are not shown to be realistic.** Fields up to 15 V/µm in Ge are assumed, and the operating point is ≈ 4.4 V/µm. Ge0.84Sn0.16 has a small band gap (a few hundred meV), and Fig. 1b shows band bending of order 0.1–0.2 eV across the structure.
   - Show that the gate field does not induce electron accumulation or inversion in the GeSn barrier or parallel hole conduction, either of which would screen Fz.
   - Show that the field profile assumed (uniform Fz across Ge and GeSn) holds for a realistic gate stack.
   - Give the gate-voltage range corresponding to the Fz window of interest.

8. **The thin surface barrier is idealised.** The model takes the surface barrier to be a "very thin, large band offset" layer (p. 2), and the ground state is pushed towards it (Fig. 1b). Real Si caps or oxides add interface traps, roughness and strain relaxation, as well as interface-inversion-asymmetry terms. The authors' own introduction cites such interface terms (refs. 50, 51) as able to dominate Rashba physics in planar heterostructures. They could move or wash out the β1 = 0 line. At least estimate how an interface term of plausible size, or a few-monolayer change in lz (Fig. 2b), shifts Fz*.

9. **The results are shown for essentially one material point, x = 16% and lz = 15 nm, while Fig. 1b uses x = 10%.** The caption of Fig. 1b specifies x = 10%, whereas Fig. 1c and Fig. 2 use x = 16%. It is unclear whether x = 10% gives an LH ground state at all for the plotted parameters.
   - State the range of Sn content for which the β-line exists.
   - Show Fz* as a function of x and lz, since growth targets need this.
   - Discuss whether a 15 nm, about 2% tensile Ge layer on Ge0.84Sn0.16 is below the critical thickness for relaxation.

10. **The validity of the perturbative effective Hamiltonians near small gaps is not established.** The authors note that Eq. (1) fails for lz ≲ 10 nm, where ΔE → 0. However, the HH subband spacing also collapses near the dashed green line in Fig. 1c, where E2^H − E1^H ≈ 2 meV, and the Fig. 2 region may cross it.
    - Mark this boundary in Figs. 2 and 3.
    - Confirm β1, g⊥ and Ω by direct diagonalisation of the full k·p Hamiltonian in the dot geometry at a few representative points, especially on the β- and g-lines.

**C. Minor**

11. Fig. 3c: the legend labels the curves "lz = 25, 35, 45, 55 nm", but the caption and text say these are selected lx. Fig. 3b's caption specifies "φ = π/2", but φ is never defined. Please correct both.

12. The definition of lx and ly "relative to a hole sharing the same mass as a bare electron of mass m0" (p. 3) is confusing. Give the actual confinement energies ħωx and ħωy and the corresponding orbital splittings for the LH in-plane mass. This would let readers judge whether lx = 35 nm and ly = 60 nm are realistic for gate-defined dots.

13. The y-axis of Fig. 3 is normalised by Fz, so curves at different Fz are not on a common scale (see concern 4). Plotting Ω/(ωB·Fac) would be clearer.

14. The introduction cites the "inherently weak coupling of holes with surrounding nuclear spins" as an advantage of Ge holes. For LH states the hyperfine interaction is not Ising-like and differs from the HH case. Comment on whether this advantage carries over.

15. The claim that the large in-plane g-factor "facilitates the integration of superconductors" (abstract, conclusion) needs a short justification. For example, give the in-plane field needed for a topological or Zeeman-dominated regime compared with typical critical fields of Al, along with the relevant induced-gap considerations.

16. Typos: "valance" (p. 2), "wether" (p. 3).

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a short table of all model parameters and a robustness figure showing Fz*, the zero of g⊥ and g∥ as functions of Sn content, valence-band offset and residual strain. Include an 8-band check if possible.
2. Add a panel comparing the SOI-mediated Rabi frequency with g-TMR and charge-noise sensitivity along the β-line, so that the "on/off switch" claim is quantitative and device-relevant.
3. Express the main figures of merit (lso, Ω in MHz, angular sensitivity of g*) in absolute units, side by side with representative Ge/SiGe HH and Ge/Si nanowire values.
4. State the incremental advance over refs. [56, 57] explicitly in the introduction, and move the essential Supplemental Material content (parameters, validity of Eq. 3, cubic Rashba coefficients) into the main text or an appendix.
