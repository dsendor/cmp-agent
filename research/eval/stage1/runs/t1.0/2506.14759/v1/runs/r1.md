Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript proposes a "MOS-like" heterostructure: a tensile-strained Ge layer grown on relaxed Ge1-xSn_x and capped by a thin, large-offset surface barrier. Using 6-band k·p theory and Schrieffer-Wolff effective Hamiltonians, it argues that the light-hole (LH) ground state carries a large linear-in-k Rashba term β1. Because of the built-in asymmetry, β1 changes sign at a finite gate field F_z* ≈ 4.4 V/µm. This would give an electrically controlled on/off switch for EDSR, together with an out-of-plane g-factor that can be tuned through zero. Gate-tunable linear Rashba SOI in a planar (rather than quasi-1D) Ge platform would be of real interest to the hole-spin-qubit and hybrid-device communities. However, the work is entirely theoretical. Its novelty over the authors' earlier LH papers (Refs. 56, 57) lies mainly in the asymmetric single-heterointerface geometry and the channel and dot analysis. The practical claims depend on assumptions about growth, the surface barrier and achievable fields that the paper does not test.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear and physically transparent mechanism: the zero of β1 at finite F_z comes from the asymmetric confinement profile and survives in-plane confinement (the "β-line" is independent of l_x, l_y and θ in Fig. 3). This is a useful design principle.
- The analysis runs consistently through three dimensionalities (QW → channel → dot) with effective parameters derived from the same k·p model. The analytic relation Eq. (3) for the channel g-factor renormalisation links to known results in quasi-1D systems.
- It identifies distinct "β-lines" and "g-lines" with different responses to field tilt (Fig. 3b). This gives an experimentally testable signature for separating the two mechanisms behind Ω = 0.
- It is timely, given recent progress in Ge/GeSn growth (Refs. 16, 55) and the active search for alternatives to the weak cubic Rashba SOI of strained Ge/SiGe heavy-hole (HH) qubits.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **Structural feasibility of the modelled stack is not established.** The model (Figs. 1-3) assumes a fully strained, defect-free 15 nm (and up to 25 nm, Fig. 2a-b) Ge layer coherent on relaxed Ge0.84Sn0.16. The lattice mismatch here is roughly 2-2.5% in tension. The critical thickness for such a layer is likely comparable to or smaller than the thicknesses used, and partial relaxation directly reduces ε_∥, which is the quantity that produces the LH ground state. The region boundaries in Fig. 1c, the LH-HH splitting and the location of the β1 = 0 line all depend on the assumed strain.
   - The authors should state the assumed critical thickness and justify it, with experimental references where possible (Ref. 55 is the natural one).
   - They should show how the ΔE = 0 contour and F_z* move when ε_∥ is reduced by, for example, 10-30% through partial relaxation, and how they move with the residual compressive or tensile strain of the Ge1-xSn_x buffer.
   - Without this, the central claim that the material system "is shown to exhibit" a large, tunable linear RSOI applies only to an idealised structure.

2. **The surface barrier is idealised, yet it creates the asymmetry behind the on/off switch.** The paper treats the top barrier as "very thin, large band offset", possibly "a few ångströms" of Si, an oxide or a combination (p. 2), and models it with an effectively infinite or hard wall at z = 0. The β1 = 0 point exists because of the asymmetry between this wall and the Ge/GeSn triangular barrier, so F_z* depends on:
   - the barrier height and thickness;
   - wavefunction penetration into the barrier;
   - interface-induced HH-LH mixing, which reduces the symmetry to C2v at the Ge/oxide and Ge/GeSn interfaces (the paper itself cites this as relevant in Refs. 50-51);
   - fixed interface charge.

   A k·p envelope model with a hard wall cannot capture interface-inversion-asymmetry terms. These add a field-independent linear-in-k contribution that would generally prevent β1 from vanishing exactly. The abstract claim that the RSOI "completely vanish[es]" is therefore not supported at the level stated. The authors should:
   - replace the hard wall with a finite barrier (for example SiO2, GeO2 or a Si cap with realistic offsets and masses) and report how F_z* shifts;
   - estimate the size of interface-induced linear terms (for example with the boundary-condition or C2v-invariant approaches of Refs. 50-51) compared with β1 away from the switch point;
   - quantify the residual Rabi frequency at the "off" point compared with the "on" maximum.

3. **The proposed operating fields are unrealistic, and the paper does not say how F_z would be tuned independently in a device.** The interesting features appear at F_z ≈ 2-10 V/µm (β-line at 4.4 V/µm, g-lines at ≈2 and 7.5 V/µm, Rabi maximum near 7-10 V/µm in Fig. 3b).
   - A uniform field of 4-8 V/µm in Ge corresponds, by Gauss's law, to a displacement field matching a sheet density of order 10^12-10^13 cm^-2. In the oxide it implies several MV/cm, approaching breakdown for thin gate dielectrics.
   - In a single-hole gate-defined dot, the vertical field is not an independent knob with only top gates. A back gate, which would have to act through the GeSn buffer, or a specific dual-gate scheme is needed.
   - The authors should translate F_z into gate voltages and oxide fields for a concrete stack, discuss whether such fields are compatible with few-hole occupancy and with gate leakage and charge trapping at the Ge/oxide interface, and propose a gating geometry that tunes F_z across F_z* at fixed dot occupancy.

   Without this, the "fully gate-tunable" claim in the title is not shown to be reachable in practice.

4. **Absolute Rabi rates and qubit-relevant figures of merit are missing, so the advantage over HH Ge/SiGe is not demonstrated.** Fig. 3 gives Ω only in units of ω_B eF_ac/F_z, on a ×10^4 scale.
   - The paper never states the Rabi frequency in MHz for a realistic drive amplitude (for example F_ac of order a few kV/m at the dot) and B0 = 0.05 T.
   - It does not compare this with the same calculation for a conventional strained Ge/SiGe HH dot of the same size.

   The claims of "efficient EDSR" and of "alleviating limitations" of planar HH systems (pp. 1, 4) rest on that comparison. The authors should add absolute Ω for representative parameters, alongside the HH benchmark computed within the same framework. They should also quantify the trade-off between driving and dephasing:
   - the large gate tunability of g⊥ (Fig. 2c, Fig. 3) implies a large ∂g/∂F_z and hence charge-noise dephasing;
   - the paper should give ∂ω/∂F_z, or a quality factor estimate, along the β-line and g-lines and say whether any sweet spot coincides with the "on" state.

B. Should address

5. **Sensitivity to material parameters of Ge1-xSn_x is not assessed.**
   - The valence band offset between Ge and Ge1-xSn_x, the deformation potentials and the Luttinger parameters of the alloy are poorly constrained experimentally. The VBO in particular sets the depth of the triangular barrier in Fig. 1b and the hybridisation that controls the region boundaries in Fig. 1c.
   - The parametrisation sits in the Supplemental Material (Ref. 60). The main text should give the values used and show how F_z* and the β1 magnitude vary within the literature spread of the VBO and the strain-related parameters.
   - Small gaps at high Sn content (x = 16%) could also make 6-band k·p inadequate in the barrier, since the conduction band is not included. The authors should justify the 6-band choice, or check it against an 8-band calculation for at least one point.

6. **Eq. (3) is used outside its stated validity.** Eq. (3) is stated for |β1^p| l_x/α0 ≪ 1. The g⊥^c sign change, however, is predicted for l_x^2 ≥ α0^2 |g⊥^p| sqrt(γ∥^p)/(β1^p)^2, that is, (β1^p l_x/α0)^2 = |g⊥^p| sqrt(γ∥^p). With |g⊥^p| ≈ 3 this requires β1^p l_x/α0 of order unity or larger, outside the regime where Eq. (3) holds. The sign change is visible in the full numerical results (Fig. 2c), but the text attributes it to Eq. (3). The authors should state that the g-line positions come from the full calculation and give the actual validity window of Eq. (3).

7. **The perturbative effective models may break down close to the features of interest.**
   - Hamiltonian (1) is said to fail for l_z ≲ 10 nm. Near the ΔE = 0 contour and the sharp region-2/region-3 boundary (E_1^H − E_2^H ≪ ΔE, Fig. 1c), HH-LH quasi-degeneracies again shrink the energy denominators.
   - The QD Rabi frequency in Fig. 3 is computed perturbatively from the channel model, with in-plane confinement treated harmonically.
   - The authors should mark, on Figs. 2-3, the regions where the lowest excited level lies within a few meV of the ground state.
   - At least one point (for example the β-line in the QD) should be validated with a non-perturbative calculation, such as a direct diagonalisation of the full 6-band dot Hamiltonian.

8. **The β1 = 0 line is shown, but the cubic terms in the QC/QD are not.**
   - In the planar case, the paper notes that the zeros of β1, β2 and β3 "coincide" in (l_z, F_z)-space. This should be shown, not just stated, since it underpins the claim of a complete off-state.
   - In the channel (Eq. 2), β3^c k_y^2 adds to β1^c. For a dot with l_y = 60 nm, ⟨k_y^2⟩ ~ 1/l_y^2 is not negligible.
   - The authors should plot β3^p, β2^p and β3^c next to β1 (for example in Fig. 2), and confirm that Ω(F_z*) = 0 in Fig. 3 includes the cubic contribution.

9. **Disorder and inhomogeneity in the GeSn buffer are not considered.** Relaxed Ge1-xSn_x buffers typically carry Sn compositional fluctuations, Sn segregation, threading and misfit dislocations, and cross-hatch strain variations. These modulate ε_∥ locally and therefore the LH-HH splitting and F_z* from dot to dot. Since the on/off switch requires tuning to a specific field, dot-to-dot spread in F_z* sets whether a shared gate field can idle many qubits. The authors should estimate how much F_z* shifts per 0.1% of strain variation and per 1% of Sn variation, and comment on realistic inhomogeneity.

10. **The claims about hybrid superconducting devices are not substantiated.** The abstract and conclusion state that the large in-plane g-factor (g∥^c ≈ −8, Fig. 2e) "facilitates the integration of superconductors". Several points are missing:
    - the induced gap, interface transparency and proximity effect for LH states in this stack;
    - the requirement for a topological or Andreev-spin application, which is a large g together with SOI along a particular direction compared with the field;
    - compatibility of the "MOS-like" surface barrier with a superconductor contact.

    The authors should either support the claim with a short estimate (for example the field needed to close the induced gap, set against the critical field of Al) or tone it down.

11. **Hyperfine and phonon-relaxation implications of a LH ground state are not discussed.** LH states have a different, less anisotropic hyperfine coupling than HH states, and strong linear SOI enhances phonon-mediated relaxation. Since the paper motivates the platform with qubit performance, a brief estimate of T1 from SOI-mediated phonon coupling at the "on" point, and of the hyperfine coupling compared with HH, would put the advantages in context.

C. Minor

12. **Fig. 1 caption inconsistencies.**
    - Panel (b) uses x = 10% while (c) and the rest of the paper use x = 16%. Please explain why a different composition is shown in (b), or make them consistent.
    - The sentence "H1 and H2 are 2 meV apart on the dashed green line" belongs to panel (c), not (b).
    - The colour scale of panel (c) is labelled ε but its range (10^-2 to 10^2) and meaning should be stated in the caption.

13. **Fig. 3 notation.**
    - The caption of Fig. 3(a) introduces φ = π/2, but φ is not defined in the text. Only the polar angle θ appears.
    - Please define φ and the in-plane orientation of B with respect to the crystal axes ([100]/[010]), and explain why the Rabi frequency would be independent of it.
    - The units "Ω [ω_B eF_ac/F_z]" combined with a ×10^4 multiplier are hard to interpret. Please give the conversion explicitly.

14. **The terminology "MOS-like" and "surface barrier" should be pinned down.** Specify the actual material, thickness and band offsets assumed in the calculation, rather than listing options (Si, oxide or combination).

15. **The abstract and introduction overstate the work.** The abstract says the structure is "introduced and shown to exhibit" large, tunable RSOI. Make clear that this is a theoretical proposal based on k·p modelling.

16. **Notation for anisotropy.** "The LH g-tensor is less anisotropic than that of state-of-the-art HH qubits" should be quantified (for example g∥/g⊥ for this structure against typical Ge/SiGe HH values) in the main text.

17. **Key information sits only in the Supplemental Material.** The k·p model, perturbative framework, cubic Rashba parameters and material parametrisation are all deferred to Ref. 60. At least the main input parameters (strain, VBO, Luttinger parameters, barrier model) should appear in the main text or a table.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a "device-realism" figure. It would combine: (i) a finite surface barrier; (ii) a partially relaxed Ge layer; (iii) interface C2v mixing terms; (iv) the mapping of F_z to gate voltages and oxide fields for a concrete gate stack. On top of these, it would show how F_z* and the residual Ω at the off point shift.
2. Report absolute Rabi frequencies in MHz, coherence estimates (charge-noise dephasing from ∂g/∂F_z, phonon T1) and a side-by-side comparison with a Ge/SiGe HH dot computed in the same framework. This would turn qualitative advantages into quantitative ones.
3. Give a short experimental roadmap with signatures that distinguish β-lines from g-lines: the field-tilt dependence in Fig. 3b, the l_x independence of the β-line, and weak antilocalisation crossovers in Hall bars as a function of F_z. Experimentalists could then test the prediction in existing Ge/GeSn samples.
4. Give a parameter-sensitivity table, covering the Sn content, VBO, strain and l_z windows in which a LH ground state with β1 sign reversal survives, to guide growers on the required tolerances.
