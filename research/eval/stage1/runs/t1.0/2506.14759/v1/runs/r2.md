Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript proposes a MOS-like, tensile-strained Ge quantum well on relaxed Ge1−xSn_x (x ≈ 16%) whose ground state is light-hole (LH) like. Using 6-band k·p theory and Schrieffer–Wolff effective Hamiltonians, the authors predict a linear-in-k Rashba SOI (β1) that can be tuned through zero by the out-of-plane gate field, an out-of-plane g-factor that can also be tuned through zero, and a large in-plane g-factor (≈ −8). They propose these as an electrically switchable SOI "on/off" knob for EDSR spin qubits and as a platform for hybrid devices. The idea of building planar spin qubits around a LH ground state, where linear Rashba SOI comes from the planar geometry rather than from strain gradients or interface effects, is timely and could matter for the Ge hole-spin community. As it stands, though, the study is a single-particle calculation in an idealized structure. Its central device-level claims (a "complete" vanishing of the SOI, an idle mode, "large" SOI, and suitability for qubits and hybrids) are not yet tested against the physics most likely to change them.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The concept is clear and well motivated: a LH ground state turns the weak cubic HH Rashba term into a linear term that is compatible with EDSR in planar heterostructures.
- The calculation is laid out in a consistent hierarchy: a ground-state phase diagram (Fig. 1c), planar parameters (Fig. 2a–b), channel parameters (Fig. 2c–e) and quantum-dot Rabi frequencies (Fig. 3). The β-line and g-lines are traced through each geometry.
- The result that the β1 = 0 line barely depends on l_x, l_y and θ (Fig. 2d, Fig. 3a–c) has a clean physical explanation: it is inherited from the planar asymmetry. This is a useful design principle.
- The analytic expression for g⊥^c (Eq. 3), including the sign change at large l_x, gives physical insight that links this work to g-factor renormalization in 1D hole systems.
- The work connects naturally to recent experimental progress on Ge/GeSn growth (Refs. 55–57).

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **A 6-band k·p model may not hold for strongly tensile Ge, where the conduction band is close.** Section "The proposed heterostructure" and Ref. [60] say a 6-band model was used. Ge pseudomorphic on relaxed Ge0.84Sn0.16 carries roughly 2% biaxial tensile strain. At that strain the Γ conduction band falls well below its unstrained energy and Ge approaches a direct, narrow gap. The LH band couples to the conduction band through the Kane parameter far more strongly than the HH band does. That coupling sets the LH out-of-plane mass (γ∥^p, l_F), the LH g-factors (the in-plane g∥ ≈ −8 is a direct consequence of LH–CB/SO mixing), and the Rashba coefficients. The headline numbers (the position F* ≈ 4.4 V/µm of the β-line, g⊥^p ≈ −3, g∥^c ≈ −8, and the g-lines at 2 and 7.5 V/µm) could therefore shift substantially, or change qualitatively, once conduction-band coupling is included. Please do one of two things: (i) repeat the key calculations (Fig. 1c, Fig. 2b, Fig. 2c–e) with an 8-band (or larger) k·p model that includes the deformation-potential shift of the Γ conduction band at this strain, or (ii) show quantitatively, by stating the strained band gap and the LH–CB energy separation, that the 6-band truncation is safe. Also state the Luttinger and deformation parameters used, and how they were adjusted for the strained Ge.

2. **The claims of "complete" SOI vanishing and an idle mode are made only within the ideal model.** The abstract and conclusion state that the RSOI "completely vanish[es] at specific gate fields, effectively acting as an on/off SOI switch" and that in the off state "the qubit is put in idle mode". Figs. 2b, 2d and 3 show that β1 crosses zero in an ideal structure with a hard-wall surface barrier, an abrupt Ge/GeSn interface, homogeneous strain, uniform F_z and no alloy disorder. Several things will add residual linear SOI at F*:
   - interface inversion asymmetry and atomistic interface terms at the Ge/GeSn and Ge/cap interfaces (known to produce linear SOI for holes; see Refs. 50–53, cited in the introduction);
   - inhomogeneous strain from the gate electrodes (cited in the introduction as the dominant HH mechanism);
   - fluctuations in Sn content, well width and interface roughness, which move F* locally;
   - an in-plane field component from the confinement gates in a real QD.

   The paper says the cubic terms β2, β3 also vanish on the same contour ("the β^p = 0 contour line coincides in (l_z, F_z)-space for all three Rashba parameters"), but it shows no data or explanation for this, and any β3 left over still drives EDSR. To support an "off" switch, please (a) show β2 and β3 next to β1 (in the SM at least) and explain why they vanish together; (b) estimate the residual |β| at F* from at least one realistic perturbation, such as an interface-inversion-asymmetry term of plausible size, a ±1 ML change in l_z, or ±1% in x; and (c) state the off/on ratio of Ω that can realistically be reached. Otherwise, rephrase the claim as a SOI minimum ("sweet line") rather than a complete switch-off.

3. **Rabi frequencies come from a single driving mechanism, and the g-factors' strong gate dependence is not considered for driving or dephasing.** Fig. 3 computes Ω only for a uniform in-plane ac field F_ac e_y in a parabolic dot, so the only mechanism is linear SOI. The same paper shows that g⊥ varies by several units across F_z (Fig. 2a, 2c: g⊥^c changes sign twice between 0 and 10 V/µm). Real plunger-gate drives carry a substantial out-of-plane ac component δF_z, which produces g-tensor-modulation resonance (g-TMR) proportional to ∂g/∂F_z. For any B direction other than along a principal axis, this term will give a nonzero Ω on the β-line, so the claimed "idle mode" does not follow. By the same reasoning, a large |∂g⊥/∂F_z| makes the qubit frequency sensitive to charge noise, which bears directly on the claimed qubit advantages. Please (i) include the δF_z drive component (or a realistic gate-geometry drive) in the Rabi calculation of Fig. 3; (ii) plot ∂(g_eff)/∂F_z, or the resulting charge-noise-limited T2* for a typical noise amplitude, together with Ω, and identify any sweet spots; and (iii) compare the quality factor Ω/(dephasing rate) with planar HH Ge qubits.

**B. Should address**

4. **"Large" and "strong" SOI are not benchmarked quantitatively.** The title and abstract call the RSOI "inherently large" and "strong", but the paper gives only β1 in meV nm (up to a few meV nm in Fig. 2b, 2d) and Ω in normalized units [ω_B F_ac/F_z] (Fig. 3). Please give: the spin-orbit length l_so (using the in-plane LH mass) relative to typical dot sizes; absolute Rabi frequencies for a stated F_ac (for example 1–10 mV over the dot size) at B0 = 0.05 T; and a direct comparison, in the same units, with planar HH Ge (β3-driven EDSR) and with Ge/Si nanowire direct Rashba SOI. The normalization by F_z in Fig. 3 also hides the true F_z dependence at fixed F_ac and diverges as F_z → 0. Please plot Ω in absolute units, or explain the normalization.

5. **The physical surface barrier is not specified, yet the asymmetry that sets β1 = 0 depends on it.** The text says the surface barrier "is assumed to be a thin, large band offset material", for example "a very thin Si capping (a few ångströms), an oxide, or a combination thereof". β1 vanishes "due to the asymmetric confinement profile even at F_z = 0", so F* depends directly on the boundary condition at z = 0: barrier height, penetration, effective masses inside the barrier, and interface strain from a Si cap. Please (i) state how the barrier is modeled (infinite wall or finite offset, and its value); (ii) show how F* and g⊥ shift for realistic Si-cap and GeO_x/oxide barriers; and (iii) discuss interface trap and fixed-charge densities typical of Ge MOS interfaces, which will both screen F_z and add disorder.

6. **Device feasibility at the required fields and strains is not assessed.** The relevant physics spans F_z ≈ 2–15 V/µm inside Ge. With ε_Ge ≈ 16, continuity of displacement implies fields of order 60 V/µm (≈ 6 MV/cm) in an SiO2-like dielectric at the top of this range, close to dielectric breakdown and well into the regime of strong gate leakage and charge trapping. In addition, a 15 nm Ge layer at about 2% tensile strain on Ge0.84Sn0.16 may exceed the critical thickness for relaxation, and high-Sn relaxed GeSn buffers have well-known issues with Sn segregation and precipitation. Please state the strain value, compare l_z = 15 nm with the critical thickness, give the dielectric fields needed for the β-line and g-lines (which fall at moderate F_z and are the most relevant points), and comment on accumulation (carrier density, screening) at these fields. The single-particle, unscreened treatment should also be justified for the densities at which the channel is operated.

7. **The perturbative effective models are used where their control parameters are not small.** The text says Eq. (1) fails for l_z ≲ 10 nm, yet Fig. 2a–b extends down to l_z = 10 nm without marking where the perturbation theory holds. Eq. (3) assumes |β1^p| l_x/α0 ≪ 1. With α0 = ħ²/2m0 ≈ 38 meV nm² and β1 of a few meV nm, this ratio exceeds 1 already for l_x of a few tens of nm, and it is ≫ 1 at l_x = 100 nm (the top of Fig. 2c). Similarly, the QC effective Hamiltonian (Eq. 2) treats the N ≥ 1 orbital levels perturbatively, while the orbital spacing at l_x = 100 nm is small compared with the SOI energy. Please mark the region of validity on Figs. 2c–d, and benchmark Eqs. (2)–(3) and the Fig. 3 Rabi frequencies against exact diagonalization of the full k·p Hamiltonian with in-plane confinement for at least one representative (l_x, l_y, F_z). Ref. [60] says this is in the SM, but the SM is not available with the submission, so the claims cannot be checked as submitted.

8. **The g-tensor claims are only partly supported and partly contradict each other.** The abstract states that "the LH g-tensor is less anisotropic than that of state-of-the-art HH qubits, alleviating precise magnetic field orientation requirements". The paper shows g⊥ ≈ −3 to 0 against g∥ ≈ −8 (Fig. 2a, 2e). The anisotropy is therefore inverted rather than absent, and at the g-lines, which the conclusion promotes for leakage-free qubits, the g-tensor is maximally anisotropic. Only B in the y–z plane (polar angle θ) is considered. The in-plane anisotropy g_x vs g_y in the channel and dot, and the off-diagonal g-tensor components produced by in-plane confinement, are not shown. Please give the full g-tensor (principal values and axes) for the QD of Fig. 3, quantify the sensitivity of the qubit frequency to field misalignment and compare it with HH planar Ge, and reconcile the "less anisotropic" claim with the g-line proposal.

9. **The claims about hybrid superconducting devices are unsupported.** The abstract and conclusion state that the large in-plane g-factor "facilitates the integration of superconductors" and that the platform suits hybrid devices. No calculation addresses this. A superconductor on top would have to sit on, or replace, the surface barrier that the LH ground state needs. It would also modify the electrostatics (screening of F_z), the boundary condition, and possibly the strain. Please either support the claim (for example by estimating the in-plane critical-field requirement for a topological or Andreev regime, and how a metal top contact changes F_z, F* and the LH/HH ordering), or reduce it to a brief outlook statement.

10. **The finite simulation domain and the HH states in the GeSn buffer need checking.** Fig. 1b shows HH states (H3) localized in the Ge1−xSn_x layer in a field-induced triangular well, and region 3 of Fig. 1c is defined by such physics. The simulation z-domain appears to extend only to about −30 nm, whereas the relaxed buffer in a real device is thick, and the field there is set by electrostatics (depletion and screening), not by a uniform F_z. Please show that the ground-state ordering, ΔE and the Rashba parameters have converged with respect to domain size, and discuss how a realistic, non-uniform field profile in the buffer affects the sharp region-2/region-3 boundary (dashed line in Fig. 1c).

**C. Minor**

11. Fig. 1b uses x = 10%, while Fig. 1c and all later results use x = 16%. Please use x = 16% throughout, or explain why Fig. 1b differs.
12. The Fig. 1 caption says "H1 and H2 are 2 meV apart on the dashed green line", but Fig. 1b labels only H1, H3 and L1. The text writes E_1^H − E_2^H ≪ ΔE in one place and E_2^H − E_1^H = 2 meV in another. Please make the sign convention and labels consistent.
13. The Fig. 3 caption uses φ = π/2 in panel (b), but φ is never defined. The text writes "g∥ > g⊥" when both are negative; this should refer to magnitudes.
14. The Rabi formula Ω = (eF_ac/ħ)|⟨0|y|1⟩| is used along the g-lines, where the Zeeman splitting vanishes for θ = 0 and the qubit is not defined. Please say how Ω = 0 on the g-lines should be interpreted (as the absence of a qubit rather than the absence of SOI driving).
15. The quoted Luttinger parameters γ2 = 4.24, γ3 = 5.69 are bulk Ge values. Please state whether γ1 and the κ, q parameters (which set the g-factors) are bulk Ge values, and whether Sn interdiffusion into the well is neglected.
16. Ref. [60]: the Supplementary Material containing the k·p details, the GeSn parametrization and the consistency checks was not provided. It is needed for review.
17. Typos and wording: "wether" (p. 3), "valance band" (p. 2), "This large RSOI is a consequence of the LH-like ground state in Ge" (be precise: in the tensile-strained Ge well). Ref. [13] lacks journal volume and page.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a short robustness section, a table or figure showing how F*, the residual |β| at F*, and the g-line positions shift under ±1 ML in l_z, ±1% in x, a finite surface-barrier height, and a small interface-inversion-asymmetry term. This would turn the "on/off switch" from a model property into a design rule.
2. Report all qubit-relevant figures of merit in absolute units (Ω in MHz at a stated gate voltage amplitude, l_so, ∂f_Larmor/∂V_gate) side by side with a planar HH Ge reference computed with the same code.
3. Include g-TMR and charge-noise dephasing in the QD analysis (concern 3). If a regime exists where Ω is large while ∂f/∂F_z is small, highlight it, since it would considerably strengthen the case for this platform.
4. Validate the 6-band results with an 8-band calculation at the chosen strain (concern 1). Given the narrow strained Ge gap, this is the single check most likely to change the quantitative conclusions.
