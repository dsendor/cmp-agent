## A. Must address

1. **The 6-band k·p model leaves out the conduction band, which matters most in this strain regime.** The paper says the results are "computed from 6-band k·p theory [60]" (p. 2). Ge grown pseudomorphically on relaxed Ge0.84Sn0.16 carries roughly 2% biaxial tensile strain (ε∥ = a(GeSn)/a(Ge) − 1). At that strain the Γ conduction band falls well below its unstrained energy and Ge approaches a direct, narrow gap. The small gap of the high-Sn barrier adds to the problem. LH states couple to the Γ6 conduction band through the Kane parameter P far more strongly than HH states do. That coupling sets the LH out-of-plane mass (γ∥^p, l_F), the LH g-factors (g∥ ≈ −8 follows directly from LH–CB/SO mixing through the 2κ-like and Kane terms) and the Rashba coefficients. Every headline number could therefore shift, or change qualitatively, once conduction-band coupling is included: the β-line at F_z* ≈ 4.4 V/µm, g⊥^p ≈ −3, the zero of g⊥, g∥^c ≈ −8 and the g-lines at ≈2 and 7.5 V/µm. The main text gives no estimate of these corrections. The authors should either (i) repeat the key calculations (Fig. 1c, Fig. 2a–b and Fig. 2c–e) in an 8-band (or larger) model that includes the strain-dependent deformation-potential shift of the Γ conduction band, or at least check one point, or (ii) give a quantitative bound by stating the strained band gap and the LH–CB separation at x = 16%, l_z = 15 nm, and show how much F_z* moves.

2. **The surface barrier and interfaces are idealised, so the "complete" vanishing of the SOI and the idle mode hold only in the ideal model.** The abstract and conclusion state that the RSOI "completely vanish[es] at specific gate fields, effectively acting as an on/off SOI switch", and that in the off state "the qubit is put in idle mode". The zero of β1 exists because of the asymmetry between the top surface barrier and the triangular Ge/GeSn barrier ("due to the asymmetric confinement profile even at F_z = 0"), so F_z* depends directly on the boundary condition at z = 0. Yet the barrier is described only as "a thin, large band offset material", possibly "a very thin Si capping (a few ångströms), an oxide, or a combination thereof" (p. 2). It appears to be modelled as a hard wall, and the ground state is pushed towards it (Fig. 1b). The following effects are left out:
   - barrier height and thickness, wavefunction penetration, effective masses inside the barrier, and interface strain from a Si cap;
   - interface inversion asymmetry and interface-induced HH–LH mixing (C2v symmetry) at the Ge/cap and Ge/GeSn interfaces. The paper's own introduction cites these (Refs. 50–53) as able to dominate Rashba physics in planar heterostructures. A hard-wall envelope model cannot capture them, and they add a field-independent linear-in-k term that would generally stop β1 from vanishing exactly;
   - interface traps, roughness and fixed charge typical of Ge MOS interfaces, which screen F_z and add disorder;
   - inhomogeneous strain from the gate electrodes (cited in the introduction as the dominant HH mechanism) and in-plane field components from the confinement gates in a real dot.

   The authors should state the actual barrier material, thickness and band offset used, rather than a list of options, and say whether it is an infinite wall or a finite offset. They should replace the hard wall with a finite barrier for realistic Si-cap and SiO2/GeO_x stacks and report how F_z* and g⊥ shift. They should estimate the size of interface-induced linear terms (for example with the boundary-condition or C2v-invariant approaches of Refs. 50–51) relative to β1 away from F_z*, and give the residual |β| at F_z* and the realistic off/on ratio of Ω. Otherwise the claim should be rephrased as a SOI minimum ("sweet line") rather than a complete switch-off.

3. **The "on/off switch" is shown only for SOI-mediated EDSR with a purely in-plane drive; g-tensor-modulation driving would remain active on the β-line.** Fig. 3 computes Ω = (eF_ac/ħ)|⟨0|y|1⟩| for a uniform in-plane ac field F_ac e_y in a parabolic dot, so linear SOI is the only driving mechanism. In a real gate-defined dot the plunger or drive gate also modulates F_z. The paper itself shows that g⊥ depends very strongly on F_z (Fig. 2a, 2c, Fig. 3: g⊥^c changes sign twice between 0 and 10 V/µm). Driving by g-tensor modulation (g-TMR, ∝ ∂g/∂F_z) would therefore not vanish at F_z*, and for tilted fields (any B not along a principal axis) it could be large. The conclusion that the qubit is "put in idle mode" in the off state does not yet follow. The authors should include a realistic out-of-plane drive component δF_z (or a realistic gate-geometry drive) in the Rabi calculation, compute the g-TMR contribution along the β-line for the field orientations of Fig. 3b, and compare it with the SOI-mediated Ω at nearby F_z.

4. **The cubic Rashba terms are not shown to vanish with β1, although any residue sets a floor on the "off" Rabi frequency.** In the planar case the paper only states that "the β^p = 0 contour line coincides in (l_z, F_z)-space for all three Rashba parameters", which it says is "suggesting" full tunability (p. 3). It gives no data or explanation. In the channel (Eq. 2), β3^c k_y² adds to β1^c, and for a dot with l_y = 60 nm, ⟨k_y²⟩ ~ 1/l_y² is not negligible. The authors should plot β2^p, β3^p and β3^c next to β1 against F_z (for example at l_z = 15 nm, in Fig. 2 or at least the SM), explain why the zeros coincide, and confirm that Ω(F_z*) = 0 in Fig. 3 includes the cubic contribution.

5. **The modelled strain may not be achievable: the Ge layer may exceed its critical thickness.** The model (Figs. 1–3) assumes a fully strained, defect-free Ge layer, 15 nm thick and up to 25 nm in Fig. 2a–b, coherent on relaxed Ge0.84Sn0.16, a mismatch of about 2–2.5% in tension. The critical thickness for such a layer is likely comparable to or smaller than these thicknesses, and high-Sn relaxed GeSn buffers suffer from Sn segregation and precipitation. Partial relaxation directly reduces ε∥, which is what produces the LH ground state. The region boundaries in Fig. 1c, the LH–HH splitting and the location of the β1 = 0 line all depend on it. The authors should state the strain value and the assumed critical thickness and justify it, with experimental references where possible (Ref. 55 is the natural one). They should show how the ΔE = 0 contour, F_z* and the zero of g⊥ move when ε∥ is reduced through partial relaxation (for example by ±0.2% absolute, or 10–30% relative) and with residual strain in the GeSn buffer. Without this, the claim that the system "is shown to exhibit" large, tunable linear RSOI applies only to an idealised structure.

## B. Should address

6. **"Large", "strong" and "efficient" are not quantified in absolute units or benchmarked against HH Ge.** The title and abstract call the RSOI "inherently large" and "strong" and the EDSR "efficient", and the paper claims to alleviate limitations of planar HH systems (pp. 1, 4). But β1 is given only in meV nm (up to a few meV nm, Fig. 2b, 2d), and Ω only in units of ω_B eF_ac/F_z with a ×10^4 multiplier (Fig. 3). Normalising by F_z, the quantity being varied, hides the true F_z dependence at fixed F_ac, diverges as F_z → 0 and puts curves at different F_z on different scales. The authors should give Ω in MHz for a stated, realistic drive (for example F_ac of 1–10 kV/m, or 1–10 mV over the dot size) at B0 = 0.05 T, and plot Ω in absolute units or as Ω/(ω_B eF_ac), with the conversion stated explicitly. They should quote the spin–orbit length l_so = ħ²/(m β1) with the in-plane LH mass, against the dot size. They should also compare directly, in the same units, with a conventional strained Ge/SiGe HH dot of the same size computed in the same framework (β3-driven EDSR) and with Ge/Si nanowire direct Rashba SOI.

7. **Charge-noise dephasing, the natural cost of strong gate tunability, is not evaluated.** Because g⊥ (Fig. 2a, 2c, Fig. 3) and β1 vary strongly with F_z, the qubit frequency will be sensitive to electric-field noise. Without this, the claimed qubit advantages, the robustness and the comparison with the cited "spinless" qubit proposal [63] are not supported. The authors should give ∂ω/∂F_z (or ∂f_Larmor/∂V_gate) across the parameter space of Fig. 3, along the β-line and g-lines, together with Ω. They should identify any sweet spots, check whether the β-line or the "on" maximum coincides with a point of small ∂g/∂F_z, and estimate the charge-noise-limited T2* for a typical noise amplitude and the quality factor Ω/(dephasing rate) compared with planar HH Ge qubits.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **The operating fields and electrostatics are not shown to be reachable in a device.** The relevant features lie at F_z ≈ 2–15 V/µm inside Ge (β-line at 4.4 V/µm, g-lines at ≈2 and 7.5 V/µm, Rabi maximum near 7–10 V/µm in Fig. 3b). With ε_Ge ≈ 16, continuity of displacement implies sheet densities of order 10^12–10^13 cm^-2 and dielectric fields of several MV/cm (≈6 MV/cm in an SiO2-like oxide at the top of the range), close to breakdown and in the regime of strong leakage and charge trapping at the Ge/oxide interface. Ge0.84Sn0.16 has a gap of only a few hundred meV, and Fig. 1b shows band bending of order 0.1–0.2 eV, so the gate field may induce electron accumulation or inversion in the GeSn barrier, or parallel hole conduction, either of which would screen F_z. In a single-hole gate-defined dot, F_z is not an independent knob with top gates alone; a back gate acting through the GeSn buffer, or a dual-gate scheme, is needed. The authors should translate F_z into gate voltages and dielectric fields for a concrete stack, particularly at the β-line and g-lines; show that a uniform F_z across Ge and GeSn holds for a realistic gate stack and that no parasitic channel forms; justify the single-particle, unscreened treatment at the operating carrier density; and propose a gating geometry that tunes F_z across F_z* at fixed few-hole occupancy. Without this, the "fully gate-tunable" claim in the title is not shown to be reachable in practice.

9. **Model parameters are not stated in the main text, and sensitivity to poorly known GeSn parameters is not assessed.** All material inputs are deferred to Supplemental Material [60], which was not provided with the submission. These include the Luttinger parameters beyond γ2 = 4.24 and γ3 = 5.69 (both bulk Ge values), κ and q (which set the g-factors), deformation potentials, elastic constants, the GeSn lattice constant and bowing, the Ge/GeSn valence-band offsets for HH and LH under strain, and the surface-barrier height and boundary conditions. It is also unclear whether Sn interdiffusion into the well is neglected. The valence-band offset sets the depth of the triangular barrier in Fig. 1b and the hybridisation that controls the region boundaries in Fig. 1c, so it sets F_z* directly, and it is poorly known near 16% Sn. Results are shown essentially for one material point (x = 16%, l_z = 15 nm). The authors should list the parameters that set F_z*, g⊥ and g∥ in a main-text table, show how F_z*, the β1 magnitude and the zero of g⊥ vary within the literature spread of the VBO and the strain-related parameters, state the range of Sn content for which a LH ground state with a β-line exists, and show F_z* as a function of x and l_z to guide growth. The essential SM content (parameters, k·p framework, validity checks, cubic Rashba coefficients) should be made available for review.

10. **The perturbative effective Hamiltonians are used where their control parameters are not small.** Eq. (1) is said to fail for l_z ≲ 10 nm, yet Fig. 2a–b extends down to l_z = 10 nm without marking where it holds. Near the ΔE = 0 contour and the sharp region-2/region-3 boundary (dashed green line in Fig. 1c, where E_2^H − E_1^H ≈ 2 meV), HH–LH and HH–HH quasi-degeneracies shrink the energy denominators, and the Fig. 2 region may cross them. The channel Hamiltonian (Eq. 2) treats the N ≥ 1 orbital levels perturbatively, while at l_x = 100 nm the orbital spacing is small compared with the SOI energy. The QD Rabi frequency of Fig. 3 is computed perturbatively from the channel model with harmonic in-plane confinement. The authors should mark the regions of validity on Figs. 2–3 (for example where the lowest excited level lies within a few meV of the ground state), and benchmark β1, g⊥, Eqs. (2)–(3) and Ω against direct diagonalisation of the full 6-band Hamiltonian with in-plane confinement at a few representative (l_x, l_y, F_z) points, especially on the β-line and g-lines.

11. **Eq. (3) is used outside its stated validity to explain the g⊥ sign change.** Eq. (3) is stated for |β1^p| l_x/α0 ≪ 1. The g⊥^c sign change is predicted for l_x² ≥ α0² |g⊥^p| √(γ∥^p)/(β1^p)², that is (β1^p l_x/α0)² = |g⊥^p| √(γ∥^p), which with |g⊥^p| ≈ 3 requires β1^p l_x/α0 of order unity or larger. With α0 = ħ²/2m0 ≈ 38 meV nm² and β1 of a few meV nm, the ratio already exceeds 1 for l_x of a few tens of nm and is ≫ 1 at l_x = 100 nm (the top of Fig. 2c). The sign change is visible in the full numerics (Fig. 2c), but the text attributes it to Eq. (3). The authors should state that the g-line positions come from the full calculation and give the actual validity window of Eq. (3).

12. **Disorder and inhomogeneity are not considered, although they set whether a shared gate field can idle many qubits.** Relaxed Ge1−xSn_x buffers typically carry Sn compositional fluctuations, Sn segregation, threading and misfit dislocations, and cross-hatch strain variations. These, together with well-width fluctuations and interface roughness, modulate ε∥ and the confinement locally, and so shift the LH–HH splitting and F_z* from dot to dot. Since the switch requires tuning to a specific field, this spread matters. The authors should estimate how much F_z* shifts per 0.1% of strain variation, per 1% of Sn content and per ±1 ML in l_z, and comment on realistic inhomogeneity.

13. **The claim of a less anisotropic g-tensor conflicts with the paper's own results and is not quantified.** The abstract states that "the LH g-tensor is less anisotropic than that of state-of-the-art HH qubits, alleviating precise magnetic field orientation requirements". The paper shows g⊥ ≈ −3 to 0 against g∥ ≈ −8 (Fig. 2a, 2e), so the anisotropy is inverted rather than absent. At the g-lines, which the conclusion promotes for leakage-free qubits, the g-tensor is maximally anisotropic, and Ω and the qubit frequency become very sensitive to θ (Fig. 3b). Only B in the y–z plane is considered; the in-plane anisotropy g_x vs g_y and the off-diagonal components produced by in-plane confinement are not shown. The authors should give the full g-tensor (principal values and axes) for the dot of Fig. 3, quantify g∥/g⊥ and the angular sensitivity ∂g*/∂θ at the proposed operating points against typical Ge/SiGe HH values, state where the claim holds, and reconcile it with the g-line proposal.

14. **The claims about hybrid superconducting devices are unsupported.** The abstract and conclusion state that the large in-plane g-factor (g∥^c ≈ −8, Fig. 2e) "facilitates the integration of superconductors" and that the platform suits hybrid devices. No calculation addresses the induced gap, interface transparency or proximity effect for LH states in this stack, or the requirement for a topological or Andreev-spin application (large g together with SOI along a suitable direction relative to the field). A superconductor would have to sit on, or replace, the surface barrier the LH ground state needs, and would change the electrostatics (screening of F_z), the boundary condition and possibly the strain. The authors should support the claim with a short estimate (for example the in-plane field needed to close the induced gap or reach a topological regime against the critical field of Al, and how a metal top contact changes F_z, F_z* and the LH/HH ordering), or reduce it to a brief outlook.

15. **Convergence with respect to the simulation domain and the field profile in the buffer is not shown.** Fig. 1b shows HH states (H3) localised in the Ge1−xSn_x layer in a field-induced triangular well, and region 3 of Fig. 1c is defined by such states.
