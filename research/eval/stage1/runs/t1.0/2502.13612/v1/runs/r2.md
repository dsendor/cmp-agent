Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports in-plane resistivity and high-field (35 T) magnetoresistance on two combinatorial La2-xCexCuO4 films that span 0.116 ≤ x ≤ 0.188 in fine doping steps. It models the data with a Shockley-Chambers Boltzmann treatment in which a single doping-dependent factor g(x) scales an anisotropic T-linear scattering term peaked at the AFM hotspots. It argues that the drop of g(x) alongside Tc, together with a current-vertex-correction (CVC) account of the Hall sign change, identifies (π,π) spin fluctuations as the pairing glue in electron-doped cuprates. The dataset is valuable: dense doping coverage under matched growth conditions, high fields, and a careful contrast with hole-doped quadrature scaling. A transport-derived handle on the pairing interaction would be of broad interest. As presented, though, the headline conclusion ("superconductivity … is mediated by AFM spin fluctuations") rests on a correlation that is largely built into the analysis, and on a Hall-effect argument that is only semi-quantitative.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A unique, systematic MR dataset across the overdoped n-type dome (13 + 7 channels, 30–190 K, up to 35 T), with the striking non-Kohler behaviour: the MR peaks at 50–75 K and then falls even though ρ keeps decreasing (Fig. 2).
- A clear demonstration that the MR is orbital: the longitudinal MR is negligible at 75 K (Fig. 8). The absence of H/T quadrature scaling (Fig. 2h–l) contrasts sharply with the p-doped cuprates.
- A Boltzmann parameterisation that reproduces the zero-field resistivity, the H²-to-H crossover and the anomalous rise of the MR with T at x = 0.159 (Fig. 4). It is compared against several alternative scattering forms (Fig. 9).
- A thought-provoking argument about the electron/hole dichotomy that would interest a wide readership.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The g(x)–Tc correlation is largely built into the analysis and does not by itself establish a pairing coupling.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 5, Tables 3–4, pp. 6–8, Methods p. 10 and p. 16.
   - *What is wrong:* g(x) is the only parameter allowed to vary with x, and it is assigned by construction to the T-linear term (p. 10: "This is the main reason why the coupling strength g(x) was assigned uniquely to the T-linear term"). For S1 it is fixed mainly by ρ(0, 40 K). For S2 it is simply the measured A1 normalised to S1 at x = 0.159 (Table 4). So g(x) is essentially a re-expression of A1(x), and the A1–Tc correlation was already reported in Ref. [5] (Fig. 1 inset). The MR fixes the *form* of the anisotropy at one doping, but it does not independently determine the *strength* trend.
   - *Why it matters:* An inelastic scattering rate that tracks Tc is necessary for, but not evidence of, that channel being the pairing interaction. Fig. 5c also shows g(x) ≈ 0.55, not zero, where Tc → 0. The authors invoke Allen's λ_tr ≈ λ_ph (Fig. 7) as the conventional analogue, but they never extract a dimensionless λ_sf (the abstract names one; the paper never gives a value). Nor do they test whether its magnitude is compatible with the observed Tc through any gap-equation or McMillan-like estimate.
   - *A pairing-specific check:* In the authors' own picture the hotspots lie much closer to the nodes than to the antinodes (p. 21, "the node is three times closer to the AFM boundary"). For a d_{x²−y²} gap, (π,π) scattering between near-nodal points couples regions where the gap is small. Whether this channel can drive the observed Tc is therefore a non-trivial question the paper does not address.
   - *Request:*
     - Convert the fitted Γ_T into a dimensionless, Fermi-surface-averaged transport coupling, λ_tr(x) = (ħ/2πk_B)⟨dΓ/dT⟩.
     - Estimate the d-wave-projected pairing coupling from the same Γ(φ) and compare the implied Tc(x) with experiment, including the residual g at Tc = 0.
     - Without this, soften the title, abstract and conclusions from "identifies … the primary pairing glue" to a correlation between the anisotropic T-linear scattering and Tc.

2. **The identification of the anisotropic T-linear scattering with (π,π) spin fluctuations rests on a Hall analysis that is not shown at the level claimed.**
   - *Where:* p. 8 and Figs. 11–13. The main text says "we are able to capture the key features of R_H(T,x) beyond x > x_FSR, including the tendency of R_H to go negative with increasing T."
   - *What is wrong:* Fig. 13 gives only four single numbers of R_H, for four forms of Γ(φ), at one doping. No R_H(T) curve and no x-dependence is computed, and no sign change is produced. The CVC model also has several uncontrolled ingredients:
     - ξ_AFM and c are unknown, and ε(φ) is chosen as a fixed Gaussian with σ = 0.07.
     - The 1/(1−ε²) prefactor is dropped.
     - A simplified analytic k_F, v_F is used instead of the tight-binding FS of the MR fits.
     - The Jones-Zener (low-field) expansion is used, although R_H was measured at 14 T.
   - *Why it matters:* The argument against charge-fluctuation (antinodal) scattering is that Γ_AN raises R_H. That is drawn from one comparison (1.03×10⁻⁹ vs 4.3×10⁻¹⁰ m³/C), whose outcome depends on how ε(φ) and the anisotropic weight are chosen. The MR itself does not discriminate the microscopic origin: as the authors note on p. 23, Boltzmann transport "provides no direct information" on spin versus other bosons. The spin assignment therefore stands or falls with this Hall calculation.
   - *Request:*
     - Compute R_H(T) for 20–100 K at several x, using the same Γ(φ,T,x) and g(x) that fit the MR. Overlay the result on Fig. 11b, and show whether R_H decreases and changes sign with T.
     - Show how sensitive the conclusion is to ε amplitude, σ, ξ_AFM and the dropped prefactor.
     - Show that no reasonable antinodal (charge or phonon) scenario with CVCs reproduces the trend.
     - Use the same Fermi surface for the MR and the Hall calculations.

3. **The reproducibility of the MR magnitude between films is poor, and this undermines the use of MR magnitude as the quantitative constraint.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 10c,d and pp. 6–7.
   - *What is wrong:* At the one doping common to both films (x = 0.159), the MR of S2 is a factor ~3.5 smaller than that of S1. It also carries a low-field negative component, which the authors attribute to an unspecified origin, in a doping range beyond x_FSR where long-range AFM is absent. Each doping range is covered by a single film; the overlap point is the only reproducibility test, and it fails by a factor of 3.5.
   - *Why it matters:* The central modelling (Fig. 4, Fig. 5b, Table 2) fixes the anisotropy parameters using the MR magnitude at x = 0.159 in S1. If the same nominal composition gives a 3.5× different MR in a second film, then either a sample-dependent extrinsic contribution affects the MR, or S1 or S2 is not representative. Candidates for an extrinsic contribution include compositional inhomogeneity within a bridge, oxygen content, the negative MR term, and geometry. Either way, the conclusion that the MR magnitude is set by intrinsic inelastic anisotropy is weakened, and the S2 half of g(x) (Fig. 5c, open symbols) cannot be checked against MR at all.
   - *Request:*
     - Identify the origin of the S2 negative MR. Show its T- and field-orientation dependence, for example from the longitudinal MR at low T.
     - Show MR from at least one additional film, or non-combinatorial film, at a common doping (for example the x ≈ 0.15–0.16 films of Refs. [29, 31]) to establish which magnitude is intrinsic.
     - Report the channel-to-channel spread within S1.

**B. Should address**

4. **The g(x) extraction for S1 conflates residual-resistivity variation with the T-linear coupling.**
   - *Where:* Table 3 caption, p. 16, Table 2.
   - *What is wrong:* τ_imp⁻¹ = 27 ps⁻¹ and the hotspot height are held fixed for all x, and g(x) is tuned so that ρ(0, 40 K) and Δρ/ρ(0) at 20 T are reproduced. Any doping dependence of the residual resistivity (or of the geometric factor along the film) is therefore absorbed into g(x). The doping dependence of ρ0 is not shown for S1, since no continuous ρ(T) exists for S1 (p. 10), and Fig. 6d indicates that ρ0 varies across S2 channels. The authors themselves note (p. 16) that changing hotspot width or height changes ρ by ~1 μΩcm and "may explain the larger increase of g(x) below x = 0.14."
   - *Absolute resistivity is also uncertain:* S1 values are ~1/3 of Ref. [5], S2 is rescaled by 1.5, and Hall magnitudes differ similarly between film sets (Fig. 11). So the absolute scattering rates in Table 2, including the inferred α1 and the Planckian comparison (concern 6), carry a factor ~1.5–3 uncertainty.
   - *Fit quality:* Fig. 5b shows the simulation systematically underestimating Δρ/ρ(0), so the MR is in practice not fitted with the same fidelity as ρ.
   - *Request:*
     - Extract ρ0(x) and A1(x) for each S1 channel from the zero-field points available in Fig. 6b, and fit with ρ0 free per channel.
     - Show g(x) with uncertainty bands that propagate the geometric-factor uncertainty.
     - Report the ratio of fitted MR to data for each channel.

5. **The model is tested only over a narrow window, and "wholesale applicability" is overstated.**
   - *What was tested:* The T- and H-dependence is simulated only at x = 0.159 and 20–50 K (Fig. 4). Other dopings are simulated only at T = 40 K (Fig. 10).
   - *What is left out:* The most distinctive features of the data are not simulated:
     - the MR maximum at 50–75 K followed by its fall at lower T (Fig. 2a–g);
     - the breakdown of H/T scaling below 75 K (Fig. 2h–l);
     - the doping dependence of γ1(T) (Fig. 15);
     - the regime x < x_FSR.
     The discontinuity in dρ/dT at ~60 K (Fig. 6e) and the T² resistivity that extends far above 70 K, which the authors call "not yet understood", lie outside the model.
   - *Why it matters:* The discussion (p. 9) uses the "wholesale applicability [of Boltzmann theory] to LCCO" as a strong counter-argument for the hole-doped side. That generalisation needs the model to reproduce at least the full MR(T) at several dopings, using the fixed parameters plus g(x).
   - *Request:* Show simulated MR(H) and γ1(T) curves for 3–4 dopings over 20–100 K alongside the data, or restrict the claim.

6. **The Planckian comparison uses the wrong quantity.**
   - *Where:* p. 8 says the T-linear rate "varies by almost one order of magnitude across the doping series. Hence, the low-T T-linear resistivity … cannot be tied to the Planckian limit."
   - *Why it matters:*
     - The Planckian bound concerns the FS-averaged transport rate, ħ/τ = αk_BT with α ~ 1, and a variation of A1 alone does not refute it. In Refs. [14, 15], A1 also varies, through m* and n.
     - With ν = 12, the mean of sin¹²(2φ) is ≈ 0.23. With α1 = 0.75 ps⁻¹K⁻¹ (Table 2), the FS-averaged slope at x = 0.159 is ≈ 0.17 ps⁻¹K⁻¹ (≈ 1.3 k_B/ħ). At x = 0.116 it is ≈ 6.5 times that.
   - *Request:* Quote the FS-averaged α = ħ⟨Γ_T⟩/(k_BT) for each x, including its uncertainty from the thickness calibration (concern 4). Then state precisely what is, and is not, inconsistent with Planckian scattering.

7. **The model has internal inconsistencies, and its uniqueness is not established.**
   - *The hotspot term:* Γ_HS, the component attributed to AFM hotspots and responsible for the H-linear MR, is T- and x-independent (Table 2, "kept fixed"). Yet the spin-fluctuation strength g(x) falls tenfold across the series, and the H-linear slope also falls with x (Fig. 2h–l, Fig. 15). If both arise from (π,π) spin fluctuations, why does one stay fixed while the other collapses?
   - *Parameters borrowed or fixed:* ν = 12 is borrowed from hole-doped Tl2201 (p. 6). The HS angle (27.5°) is fixed. The T² term is made anisotropic "for simplicity". It is not stated whether the HS position follows the doping-dependent Fermi surfaces of Table 1 in the MR fits; it does in the Hall fits.
   - *Uniqueness:* Fig. 9 compares a handful of alternatives by eye, with no goodness-of-fit measure.
   - *Request:*
     - Give a χ² or residual for each Fig. 9 scenario.
     - Show how the conclusions (anisotropy maxima at hotspots, g(x) trend) change when ν (e.g. 4–16), the HS position, the anisotropy of the T² term and the x-dependence of Γ_HS are varied.
     - Show that the hotspot-peaked solution is a well-defined optimum rather than one of a family of adequate fits.

8. **The transverse MR in the fitting window (20–50 K) may contain non-orbital contributions.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 8 (longitudinal MR at 75 K only) and its accompanying text.
   - *What is missing:* The longitudinal MR is shown only at 75 K. The text notes a non-zero longitudinal MR at lower T, "attributed to the emergence of superconducting fluctuations". The fits are done at 20–50 K, where the following are all possible:
     - superconducting fluctuation MR, with Tc up to 25 K in S1;
     - the isotropic negative spin MR that is visible below x_FSR;
     - the compositional-gradient H-linear MR that the authors themselves raise on p. 8.
   - *Request:* Show the longitudinal (H ∥ I ∥ ab) MR at 20–50 K for several x, or subtract it, and estimate the fluctuation-conductivity contribution at 20 K for the lowest-x channels.

9. **The continuity of γ1 across x_FSR (Fig. 15) bridges two films with different MR magnitudes.**
   - *What is wrong:* Fig. 15 and pp. 8 and 23 argue for continuity across x_FSR, but the doping series in that figure changes from S1 to S2 at x = 0.159, where the MR differs by 3.5× (concern 3). It is not stated whether the γ1 values for S2 are rescaled.
   - *Why it matters:* The claimed smooth evolution "with no discontinuity near x_FSR" uses S1 channels only up to 0.159, where x_FSR = 0.14.
   - *Request:* State which data are scaled and by how much. Show γ1 at x = 0.159 for both films.

10. **The doping and Tc calibration of the individual channels is not documented.**
    - *What is missing:* Fig. 5c's top axis maps x to Tc, and the central correlation uses both, but neither x nor Tc per channel is tabulated. It is not stated how x is determined: by position along the gradient, by EDX or WDS, or by c-axis lattice parameter. Nor is it stated what composition spread exists across each microbridge (the paper suggests Δx ≲ 0.003). Tc for the S2 channels, which lie near the dome edge, is not shown.
    - *Request:* Tabulate x, its uncertainty, Tc (resistive onset and zero) and ρ0 for every channel. Show the superconducting transitions.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Recast the central claim as a quantitative test. Compute a dimensionless spin-fluctuation coupling from the fitted Γ(φ,T,x), project it onto the d-wave channel, and compare the predicted Tc(x) with the measured dome. This would turn a correlation into evidence.
2. Make the Hall analysis a genuine joint fit. Use one Fermi surface and one Γ(φ,T,x), compute R_H(T,x) and MR(H,T,x) together, and compare directly with Fig. 11b over 2–100 K. Include CVC parameter sensitivity.
3. Establish sample-independence. Measure MR on an additional (ideally non-combinatorial) film at one or two common dopings, explain the S2 negative MR, and fit ρ0 independently per channel.
4. Extend the simulations to the full measured window (20–100 K, several x, including x < x_FSR with γ1 < 0), and give parameter-sensitivity maps for ν, HS position and Γ_HS(x). This would show which features of the data actually require hotspot-peaked inelastic anisotropy.
