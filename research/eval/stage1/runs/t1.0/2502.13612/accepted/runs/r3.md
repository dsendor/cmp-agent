Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors measure in-plane resistivity and high-field (to 35 T) magnetoresistance on 19 closely spaced dopings (0.116 ≤ x ≤ 0.188) of combinatorial La2−xCexCuO4 films. They model the data with a Shockley–Chambers Boltzmann treatment that includes an anisotropic, T-dependent scattering rate peaked at the (π,π) hotspots. The amplitude g(x) of the T-linear part of that rate falls by about an order of magnitude as Tc goes to zero, and the authors read this as evidence that low-energy commensurate spin fluctuations are the pairing glue in electron-doped cuprates. The data set is unusually systematic, and the attempt to link a transport-derived, momentum-resolved coupling strength to Tc is new. However, the identification of the pairing glue rests on a correlation between two quantities that both vary monotonically with x, and on a semiquantitative Hall analysis. The strength of the headline claim therefore depends on the points raised below.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A dense, homogeneous doping series (Δx ≲ 0.003, 13 channels on one film) grown under identical conditions. This removes much of the sample-to-sample scatter that limits doping studies in cuprates.
- High-field MR to 35 T over 30–190 K, with derivative plots (Fig. 2h–l) that clearly show the absence of H/T quadrature scaling, in contrast to p-type cuprates.
- A longitudinal-field control (Extended Data Fig. 2) showing that the MR is orbital at 75 K.
- A transparent single-band SCTIF model that reproduces ρ(H,T) at x = 0.159 (Fig. 4), with "inferior" alternatives shown explicitly (Extended Data Fig. 3).
- A concrete, physically motivated proposal (CVCs plus nodal-leaning anisotropy) for the sign change of R_H(T) in overdoped LCCO.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The step from g(x)–Tc correlation to "pairing glue" is not established (main text p. 5, Fig. 5c, abstract).** Both g(x) and Tc decrease monotonically with x across the series. So does the proximity to AFM order, and so (in Fig. 1) do A1 and probably several other x-dependent quantities. A monotonic co-variation does not single out spin-fluctuation scattering as the cause of pairing.
   - The data also do not show the simplest expected form of the link. In Extended Data Table 3, g stays finite (0.66 at x = 0.174, 0.55 at x = 0.184–0.188) where Tc is already zero (Fig. 5c top axis). Between x = 0.141 and 0.188, g falls by only a factor of about 3.4.
   - Several things are needed to make the connection more than qualitative:
     - plot Tc directly against g(x), or against the angle-averaged transport coupling λ_tr derived from α1·g(x)·⟨β(φ)⟩;
     - convert α1 = 0.75 ps⁻¹K⁻¹ into an absolute λ_tr (my estimate is λ of order 1 at the hotspot at x = 0.159, smaller on average) so the reader can judge whether the magnitudes are plausible for Tc ≈ 8–25 K;
     - discuss why Tc vanishes while g stays finite (a threshold set by μ*, pair breaking by disorder in the films, or a falling spin-fluctuation energy scale);
     - show that the correlation is specific to the anisotropic channel, for example by showing that the isotropic T² coefficient α2, held fixed here, would not correlate equally well if it were freed.

   A weak-coupling or Eliashberg-type estimate of Tc from the extracted anisotropic coupling, even a rough one, would turn the correlation into a test. Otherwise the title and abstract should be scoped to "correlation consistent with".

2. **The spin-versus-charge discrimination rests on a semiquantitative Hall analysis using other samples' data (main text p. 4–5; Methods "Incorporation of CVCs" and "Anisotropic scattering term"; Extended Data Figs. 4–6).** The authors state that the MR alone cannot distinguish (π,π) spin-fluctuation hotspots from antinodal charge-fluctuation scattering (Fig. 4b vs 4c). The identification therefore hinges entirely on R_H. Three problems:
   - (i) No Hall data are reported for S1 or S2. Extended Data Fig. 4 shows R_H(T) from non-combinatorial films (ref. 27) and other combinatorial films (ref. 10).
   - (ii) Extended Data Fig. 6 gives a single R_H value for each static form of Γ(φ). The main text says the calculation is "able to capture the key features of R_H(T,x) beyond x > x_FSR, most notably the reduction in R_H with increasing T", but no computed R_H(T) or R_H(x) curve is shown and compared with data.
   - (iii) The CVC treatment adds AFM-induced vertex corrections by hand, with a Gaussian ε(φ) of chosen width σ = 0.07 and fixed height (eq. 12), for the spin scenario. It is not clear that the charge scenario received a comparably favourable treatment. There, CVCs tied to the (δ,0) charge-ordering wavevector, together with an anisotropy peaked where the curvature changes, should be explored.

   It is also unclear whether the Γ_N form used in the Hall calculation (broad lobes along the diagonals, Extended Data Fig. 6d) is the same as the β(φ) = sin¹²[2(φ ± (π/4 − HS))] used for the MR fits, which peaks at the hotspots. The Methods describe Γ_N as an additional component.

   The authors should:
   - calculate R_H(T) at several x using exactly the parameters of the MR fits (including g(x));
   - compare it quantitatively with Hall data, ideally measured on the same channels;
   - show how sensitive the result is to σ, the CVC amplitude and the hotspot width;
   - give the charge scenario an equivalent best effort.

3. **Most of the order-of-magnitude drop in g(x) comes from x < x_FSR, where the model's assumptions are least secure (Fig. 5c, Extended Data Table 3, Methods "Zero-field resistivity" and "Simulations").** g falls from 6.5 to 2.1 between x = 0.116 and 0.138, which is the AFM / reconstructed-FS side of x_FSR ≈ 0.14. Two features of the analysis are questionable there:
   - The simulations use an unreconstructed large FS (Fig. 3; Extended Data Table 1) and fit only one temperature and field (T = 40 K, 20 T). The text asserts that 40 K is "above T_FSR". No T_FSR(x) values are given for x = 0.116–0.138, and Fig. 1 suggests that T* near x ≈ 0.12 may exceed 40 K. The negative MR component and the MR maximum at 50–75 K (Fig. 2) also signal AFM-related physics in this range.
   - τ_imp⁻¹, α2, the hotspot amplitude and width, and the β(φ) shape are all held at their x = 0.159 values (Extended Data Table 2). Any change in residual resistivity across the film (oxygen content, disorder) is therefore absorbed into g(x).

   The authors should:
   - state T_FSR (or T*) for each channel and show that 40 K is above it;
   - show full ρ(H,T) fits (as in Fig. 4) for at least one low-x (≈0.116–0.125) and one high-x (≈0.18) channel, to demonstrate that the single-parameter model really reproduces the T and H dependences there;
   - fit g(x) with τ_imp⁻¹ (or ρ0) free per channel;
   - give uncertainties on g(x) in Fig. 5c and Extended Data Table 3.

   If the claim holds only for x > x_FSR, the "order of magnitude" statement in the abstract and main text should be revised.

**B. Should address**

4. **g(x) is by construction close to A1(x), so the MR does not independently "isolate" the anisotropic channel (eq. 1; Methods "Simulations"; Extended Data Table 3).**
   - In eq. (1), the entire T-linear term multiplies β(φ), so there is no isotropic T-linear component. The choice is motivated by A1(x) decreasing with x, but it builds in the outcome that the T-linear coefficient is entirely anisotropic and tracks A1.
   - For S2, g(x) is not obtained from the MR at all but taken directly from A1 in Extended Data Fig. 1c. The g(x) curve in Fig. 5c is therefore stitched from two different methods at x = 0.159, and the high-x flattening comes from resistivity alone.

   The authors should:
   - allow an isotropic T-linear term in addition to the anisotropic one and show what the MR data constrain about their ratio as a function of x;
   - for S2, also extract g from MR simulations to confirm agreement with the A1 route.

   This is what would make the MR-based claim go beyond the existing A1–Tc correlation (refs. 8, 10).

5. **Absolute calibration of combinatorial-film resistivity (Methods "Zero-field resistivity"; Extended Data Fig. 1a; Extended Data Fig. 4).**
   - The S2 resistivity had to be divided by 1.5 to match S1 below 70 K.
   - The combinatorial-film R_H values in Extended Data Fig. 4b (~13–15 × 10⁻¹⁰ m³/C) are roughly 1.6 times those of non-combinatorial films (Extended Data Fig. 4a, ~8–9 × 10⁻¹⁰). The Methods state that the computed isotropic R_H = 8.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ m³/C agrees "excellently" only with the latter.

   Both observations point to uncertainty in thickness or geometric factor. Because ρ(0, 40 K) in absolute units is one of the two targets used to set g(x) (Fig. 5a), a thickness gradient across the combinatorial substrate would map directly into g(x). The authors should give the film thickness profile and its uncertainty across S1 and S2 and state how geometric factors were determined. They should also discuss how a channel-to-channel geometric error of, say, 10–20% would propagate into g(x).

6. **Arbitrariness and uniqueness of the anisotropic form (main text p. 4; Fig. 4; Extended Data Fig. 3).**
   - The authors acknowledge that τ_HS⁻¹(φ) and β(φ) are "somewhat arbitrary". The choices include the exponent 12, HS = 0.48, a hotspot amplitude of 50 ps⁻¹ and a width of 0.05 rad.
   - Fig. 4 shows the fit only for x = 0.159 over 20–50 K.

   A sensitivity analysis is needed: how g(x) and the fit quality change when the exponent, hotspot width or HS position are varied within ranges that still fit x = 0.159. Without it, the reader cannot tell how robust the x dependence of g is to model choices. The Methods also note that the anisotropy "most likely" broadens toward the nodes. If so, the β(φ) used for the MR (peaked exactly at the hotspots) and the Γ_N used for the Hall effect should be reconciled into one form.

7. **Doping and Tc calibration per channel (main text p. 2; Fig. 5c top axis; Methods).**
   - The nominal x of each channel comes from its position on a composition-spread film. The paper does not show how x is verified (for example, c-axis lattice parameter or EDX across the film) or what the absolute uncertainty is.
   - The Tc scale on Fig. 5c is not tied to measured transitions of the same channels, and zero-field ρ(T) through Tc is shown only for S2 (Extended Data Fig. 1b).
   - A related issue: S1's zero-field ρ(T) is known only at discrete temperatures. A1 for the S1 channels (where most of the g variation lies) is therefore not measured directly, and the comparison with A1 in Fig. 1 relies on ref. 10.

   The authors should show ρ(T) through Tc for the S1 channels, tabulate measured Tc alongside g(x), and give the x calibration and its uncertainty. Since ref. 21 found similar MR in conventional films, a confirmation of g at one or two dopings in a non-combinatorial film would also strengthen the case.

8. **Composition gradient within a channel and the H-linear MR (Methods p. 12).** The authors note that compositional variation along the channel could produce H-linear MR through Hall-voltage inhomogeneity. They respond that H-linear MR also appears in non-combinatorial films. The hotspot term τ_HS⁻¹, introduced specifically to keep the MR H-linear, has no intrinsic T dependence and no microscopic justification. If part of the H-linear MR is extrinsic, the fixed hotspot parameters (and hence g) are biased. The authors should estimate Δx along a single channel and the expected size of the inhomogeneity-induced linear MR, and compare it with the measured slope.

9. **The conclusion about hole-doped cuprates goes beyond the data (abstract; main text p. 6).** The statement that the higher Tc in p-type cuprates "cannot be attributed solely to an increase in the coupling strength to the AFM fluctuations" rests on the failure of Boltzmann theory for p-type MR, not on any analysis carried out here. Tc in spin-fluctuation theories depends on the characteristic spin-fluctuation energy as well as the coupling, so even within n-type LCCO, g(x) alone does not fix Tc. This conclusion should be presented as a hypothesis, and the role of the fluctuation energy scale (from refs. 46, 61, 62) discussed.

**C. Minor**

10. Main text p. 5: "the threefold drop in ρ(0, 40 K) and the twofold drop in Δρ/ρ(0) (at 20 T)". Fig. 5a shows ρ(0, 40 K) falling from about 44 to 24 µΩ cm, which is about 1.8-fold. Please state which quantity is meant (for example, the inelastic part after subtracting ρ0).
11. Methods eq. (10): ε_k ≈ (1 − cξ²_AFM) < 1 decreases with increasing correlation length and would turn negative. Presumably ξ⁻² is intended (Kontani's form). Please correct it.
12. The Extended Data Table 1 title cites "Tang et al.⁵⁷", but ref. 57 is Chambers. The Tang tight-binding reference is ref. 26.
13. Fig. 2 text and caption differ on where the MR maximum at 50–75 K occurs. The text says "for x ≥ 0.12" and the caption says "for x > x_FSR". Please harmonize them.
14. The Extended Data Table 3 caption says g(x) "drops by roughly a factor of 2" (referring to S2 only), while the main text says it drops by an order of magnitude. Clarify that the factor of 2 refers to S2.
15. Extended Data Fig. 6 labels Γ_HS^s and Γ_HS^c, but the caption text mixes "Γ_HS'" and "Γ_HS". Use consistent notation, and state the temperature and g value at which each R_H value is computed.
16. Fig. 5c would be clearer with Tc on a linear secondary axis or as a separate panel (g vs Tc), since the current top axis is nonlinear.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a panel plotting Tc against an absolute, angle-averaged anisotropic coupling λ_tr(x) derived from α1·g(x), with error bars. If possible, add a simple weak-coupling or Eliashberg Tc estimate using the extracted kernel and literature spin-fluctuation energies.
2. Measure the Hall effect on the same S1/S2 channels and show computed R_H(T) at several x using the identical scattering parameters used for the MR, for both the spin and the charge scenarios.
3. Present full ρ(H,T) fits at the extremes of the doping range (x ≈ 0.12 and 0.18), and a parameter-sensitivity study of g(x) against the β(φ) exponent, hotspot width and position, and free τ_imp.
4. Scope the title and abstract to what the data show: a strong correlation between Tc and an anisotropic, hotspot-centred T-linear scattering rate. Present the hole-doped implications as a hypothesis.
