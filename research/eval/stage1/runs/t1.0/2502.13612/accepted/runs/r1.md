Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors measure in-plane magnetoresistance (MR) up to 35 T on two combinatorial La2-xCexCuO4 (LCCO) films covering 0.116 ≤ x ≤ 0.188 in steps of about 0.003. They find an orbital MR whose magnitude grows with temperature up to 50-75 K. They model it with a Shockley-Chambers Boltzmann calculation in which an anisotropic T-linear scattering term peaks near the (π,π) hotspots, and they report that its doping-dependent prefactor g(x) falls by about an order of magnitude in step with Tc. From this they conclude that commensurate low-energy spin fluctuations are the pairing glue in electron-doped cuprates. If the link held up, it would be an important result: the first transport-based connection in a cuprate between the strength of a momentum-resolved inelastic scatterer and Tc. The data set is unusually dense in doping. However, the step from data to "coupling constant" passes through a heavily constrained phenomenological model, and that step is where most of my concerns lie.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A very dense and internally consistent doping series. All 13 channels of S1 sit on one substrate, grown and measured under identical conditions, so the systematic sample-to-sample variation that usually limits doping studies of cuprates is reduced.
- A qualitatively striking observation that does not depend on the model. Below about 60 K, Δρ/ρ(0) decreases as ρ decreases (Fig. 2a-g). Kohler-type reasoning therefore requires the anisotropy to sit in the inelastic channel.
- The orbital character of the MR is checked with H || I || ab (Extended Data Fig. 2).
- The authors give a fair account of alternative scattering forms that fail: antinodal anisotropy, hotspots only, and mixed forms (Extended Data Fig. 3).
- The clear contrast with the H/T quadrature scaling of hole-doped cuprates (Fig. 2h-l) is itself a useful empirical result.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **g(x) mostly restates the resistivity, and the extra constraint from the MR is not shown.**
   - *What is done.* For S1, g(x) is the only free parameter. It is fixed per channel by matching two numbers: ρ(0, 40 K) and Δρ/ρ(0) at 20 T and 40 K (Fig. 5a,b; Extended Data Table 3). Γ0, α2, the hotspot height and width, the β(φ) exponent and the hotspot position are all held at their x = 0.159 values.
   - *Why this is a problem.* ρ(0, 40 K) falls from about 44 to 24 μΩ cm across S1. With Γ0 fixed, most of that drop must be absorbed by g(x). For S2, g(x) is not obtained from the MR at all; it is simply the normalized coefficient A1 of the T-linear resistivity (Extended Data Table 3 caption).
   - *Consequence.* The headline quantity is therefore essentially A1(x). Its correlation with Tc is already established (ref. 10, Fig. 1 inset). The paper's claimed advance is that the MR isolates the anisotropic inelastic part, but that is not demonstrated in Fig. 5.
   - *What is needed.*
     - Fit the full Δρ/ρ(H, T) data for each channel, at least at the end points x = 0.116, 0.131, 0.146 and 0.159, with Γ0 (and preferably the hotspot strength) free alongside g(x).
     - Show that the MR by itself, without the absolute ρ(0, 40 K), fixes g(x) and gives the same trend. Absolute resistivity in microbridges on a combinatorial film is the least reliable input; see concern 7.
     - Model the S2 MR curves (Fig. 2k,l) in the same way rather than substituting A1.

2. **Most of the order-of-magnitude drop in g(x) comes from channels where the model's Fermi surface does not apply.**
   - *Where the drop comes from.* In Extended Data Table 3, g(x) falls from 6.5 to 1.46 between x = 0.116 and 0.146. Above x_FSR ≈ 0.14 it falls only from about 1.46 to 0.55, roughly a factor 2.7. The S2 caption itself says "a factor of 2".
   - *Why the low-x channels are doubtful.* For x < x_FSR the authors' own data show several departures from the model's assumptions:
     - a negative MR component attributed to spin scattering (ref. 21);
     - a Hall coefficient that changes sign at low T (Extended Data Fig. 4b, x = 0.117);
     - a negative high-field slope below 20 K (Extended Data Fig. 7);
     - the Fermi-surface reconstruction shown in Fig. 1.
     Yet g(x) at these dopings is obtained with the unreconstructed large Fermi surface of Fig. 3 and with no isotropic negative MR term. The statement that at 40 K "signatures of AFM order are absent or weak" is asserted, not shown. In Fig. 1, T* at x ≈ 0.12 appears to lie well above 40 K.
   - *What is needed.*
     - Show that the 40 K data at x < 0.14 are free of the negative MR component, for example from the low-field curvature or the longitudinal MR at 40 K.
     - Justify using the unreconstructed Fermi surface there.
     - Alternatively, restrict the quantitative g–Tc claim to x > x_FSR and state the much smaller dynamic range plainly in the abstract and in Fig. 5.

3. **The link from transport coupling to pairing is only qualitative, and the data partly contradict a simple proportionality.**
   - *Mismatch with Tc.* In Fig. 5c, g(x) levels off at about 0.55 for x = 0.184-0.188, where Tc = 0 on the top axis. Tc appears to vanish near x ≈ 0.175 while g is still about 0.6.
   - *Missing estimate.* The abstract and introduction frame the result through the McMillan/λ_tr analogy, but no λ is ever extracted. Using 1/τ = 2πλk_BT/ħ with α1 = 0.75 ps⁻¹ K⁻¹ (Extended Data Table 2) gives λ ≈ 0.9 at the hotspot maximum for x = 0.159, about 6 for x = 0.116, and about 0.5 where Tc = 0. The angle average is smaller.
   - *What should be shown.* Whether these magnitudes are compatible with the measured Tc values under any reasonable spin-fluctuation Eliashberg or McMillan-type estimate. A residual λ of about 0.5 with no superconductivity needs explaining.
   - *s-wave versus d-wave coupling.* For d-wave pairing the relevant coupling is the d-channel projection of the interaction. The transport λ_tr weights (π,π) scattering in a different way. The near-equality λ_ph ≈ λ_tr in Supplementary Fig. 2 is an s-wave phonon result and does not carry over automatically.
   - *Other quantities that also fall with x.* Rule out that g(x) and Tc merely co-vary with something else that decreases with x, such as the AFM correlation length, ρ0 or proximity to x_FSR, rather than g setting Tc.
   - *What is needed.* A quantitative estimate, at least an order-of-magnitude Tc from the extracted coupling. A discussion of why g stays finite past the end of the dome. A softer title and abstract if neither can be supplied.

4. **The case for spin over charge scattering rests on a semiquantitative Hall calculation that is not tied to these samples.**
   - *What the paper says.* The MR alone cannot separate the spin-hotspot and charge (antinodal) scenarios (Fig. 4b,c). The discrimination comes entirely from the Jones-Zener plus current-vertex-correction (CVC) calculation of R_H (Methods; Extended Data Figs. 5 and 6).
   - *Weaknesses of that calculation.*
     - R_H is given as single numbers (8.9, 5.6, 10.3, 4.3 and 11.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ m³ C⁻¹) for fixed Γ(φ) shapes, not as R_H(T, x).
     - The CVC strength ε(φ) is an ad hoc Gaussian with σ = 0.07. Its prefactor 1/(1-ε²) is dropped, and its amplitude and temperature dependence are not tied to the spin-fluctuation parameters used for the MR.
     - The Hall data compared against come from other films (Extended Data Fig. 4).
     - The two Hall data sets disagree in magnitude near x ≈ 0.15-0.16: about 8.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ in the non-combinatorial films versus about 15 × 10⁻¹⁰ m³ C⁻¹ in the combinatorial ones. That discrepancy is as large as the differences between the scenarios being distinguished.
   - *What is needed.*
     - Compute R_H(T) for at least two dopings above x_FSR with exactly the Γ(φ, T, x) used for the MR, including g(x) and the T dependence, and compare with measured R_H(T). Ideally use Hall data taken on S1/S2 channels or on films from the same growth.
     - Show how sensitive the sign of the R_H trend is to σ, to the CVC amplitude and to the omitted prefactor.
     - Explain the factor ~1.7 discrepancy between the two Hall data sets.

B. Should address

5. **The model assumes, rather than tests, that the T-linear term itself is anisotropic.**
   - *The assumption.* Eq. (1) multiplies both g(x)α1T and α2T² by the same β(φ) "for simplicity". The claim "anisotropic T-linear scattering" therefore follows from that choice.
   - *Tests requested.*
     - Isotropic T-linear plus anisotropic T² (and the reverse) at x = 0.159 and at one other doping, to show whether the MR data separate the two.
     - Hold the T-independent hotspot term τ_HS⁻¹ fixed in height and width across all x (Extended Data Table 2), and also let it vary with x. The spin correlation length, and hence hotspot strength, should change with doping, which would feed into g(x).
     - Report how the β(φ) exponent (12) and the hotspot position parameter "HS = 0.48" were chosen, and how sensitive g(x) is to them.

6. **Non-orbital and superconducting-fluctuation contributions at the fitting temperature are not quantified.**
   - *What is shown.* The longitudinal-MR control (Extended Data Fig. 2) is at 75 K only, for three overdoped channels, and it is already slightly negative.
   - *What the Methods say.* "At lower temperatures a non-zero MR appears that we attribute to the emergence of SC fluctuations."
   - *Why it matters.* g(x) is fixed at 40 K. For x = 0.116-0.13, Tc is 20-25 K, and an isotropic negative spin MR is present (Fig. 2 text).
   - *What is needed.*
     - Show H || I MR at 20-40 K across x.
     - Estimate the paraconductivity contribution and its field suppression at 40 K and at 30 K (the lowest temperature in Fig. 4).
     - Either subtract these contributions or show that they are negligible compared with the orbital signal used to fix g(x).

7. **Calibration of composition, geometry and Tc in the combinatorial films needs documenting.**
   - *Geometry.* The S2 resistivity has to be divided by 1.5 to match S1 at the shared composition x = 0.159 (Extended Data Fig. 1a). This points to a geometric-factor or thickness uncertainty of that size. Because g(x) for S1 is tied to absolute ρ(0, 40 K) (concern 1), channel-to-channel thickness variation along the gradient enters g(x) directly.
   - *What is needed.*
     - How x was determined for each channel, and its uncertainty. Is the quoted Δx ≤ 0.003 a step size or an accuracy?
     - Whether thickness was measured along the gradient.
     - Whether oxygen/annealing uniformity was checked across the film.
     - The Tc of each channel as measured, with its criterion. The Tc axis in Fig. 5c is not obviously derived from these channels.
     - A comparison of MR(H) at one or two compositions with an ordinary single-composition film. This would also address the compositional-inhomogeneity route to H-linear MR raised in the Methods.

8. **The key quantitative result rests on one film per doping range, and the two films are analysed differently.**
   - S1 provides all the MR-based g(x) values. S2 provides only A1-based values.
   - The single shared composition (x = 0.159) shows a scale mismatch in resistivity, and no MR comparison between the films at that composition is shown.
   - Please show the S1 and S2 MR at x = 0.159 overlaid, at the same T.
   - If possible, show the anomalous T dependence and the g(x) trend in a second film covering 0.12-0.16.

9. **The conclusion about hole-doped cuprates in the abstract goes beyond the evidence.**
   - *What the paper claims.* The statement that the higher Tc of p-type cuprates "cannot be attributed solely to an increase in the coupling strength" rests on the fact that no Boltzmann parameterization of the hole-doped MR has yet been found.
   - *Why that is not enough.* That is an absence of a model, not a measurement. The final paragraphs also invoke larger U/t, a different Fermi-surface geometry and a dual coherent/incoherent response, any of which could break the analogy without bearing on the coupling.
   - *What is needed.* Present this as a hypothesis in the abstract and discussion, or support it with a direct test, for example by applying the same model to Tl2201 or Bi2201 MR data and showing quantitatively where it fails.

C. Minor

10. **Fold changes in the text do not match Fig. 5.** The text states "the threefold drop in ρ(0, 40 K) and twofold drop in Δρ/ρ(0)" across S1. In Fig. 5a, ρ(0, 40 K) falls from about 44 to 24 μΩ cm, less than a factor of 2. In Fig. 5b, Δρ/ρ(0) falls from about 0.046 to 0.026. Please correct the factors.

11. **Possible wrong citations.**
    - The Extended Data Table 1 title cites "Tang et al.⁵⁷", but ref. 57 is Chambers (1952). It should presumably be ref. 26 or 58.
    - The main text cites ref. 23 (Horio et al., protect annealing) for the ≤10% variation of v_F, whereas the Methods cite ref. 26.
    Please check both.

12. **Hotspot parameters are not defined.** In the Fig. 4b caption and Eq. (15), the units and meaning of "HS = 0.48" in β(φ) = sin¹²[2(φ ± (π/4 − HS))] are not given. Is it radians? Measured from where? The "HS width 0.05" in Extended Data Table 2 likewise has no units.

13. **Two scales are called g(x) without saying so.** The Extended Data Table 3 caption says g(x) drops "by roughly a factor of 2", while the main text says "an order of magnitude". Please make clear that the factor of 2 refers to S2 only. Also note that the S1 and S2 values are obtained by different procedures (see concern 1).

14. **Possible inconsistency in the residual resistivity at x = 0.159.**
    - The Methods give ρ0 ≈ 15 μΩ cm from τ_imp⁻¹, rising to 17.2 μΩ cm with the hotspot term, and compare this with "actual" ρ0 ≈ 17.5 μΩ cm from Extended Data Fig. 1.
    - Extended Data Fig. 1a shows S1 at x = 0.159 just above Tc at roughly 15-20 μΩ cm.
    - Fig. 4a(ii), however, shows ρ(0, 20.6 K) ≈ 19.5 μΩ cm, and Fig. 5a gives about 24 μΩ cm at 40 K.
    Please state which data set and temperature define "actual ρ0".

15. **Clarify the faint lines in Fig. 2h-l.** State the criterion (field or temperature) used to label curves as belonging to the "mixed state" and to exclude them.

16. **Explain the R_H normalization.** In Extended Data Fig. 6, explain why the Drude value is computed for 1 − 0.159 = 0.841 holes per unit cell, given that the simulated R_H is positive for an electron-doped compound. A brief note on the hole-like curvature of the LCCO Fermi surface would help readers.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a figure showing g(x) together with the other parameters (Γ0, hotspot strength) obtained from unconstrained per-channel fits of the full Δρ/ρ(H, T) data, with error bars from the fit covariance. This would show whether the MR, not the zero-field resistivity, determines the trend.
2. Convert g(x)α1 into a dimensionless coupling λ_tr(x), both at the hotspot and averaged over the Fermi surface. Plot it against the measured Tc of each channel, including the Tc = 0 channels, and discuss the residual coupling at the end of the dome.
3. Take Hall-effect data on the same combinatorial films. Simulate R_H(T, x) with the same Γ(φ, T, x) and CVC parameters that are used for the MR, so that the spin-versus-charge discrimination becomes a single global fit rather than separate demonstrations.
4. Move the hole-doped comparison into an explicitly speculative outlook paragraph. Reserve the abstract for what the LCCO data establish directly.
