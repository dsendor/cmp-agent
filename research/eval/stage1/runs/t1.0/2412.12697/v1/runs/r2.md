Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports spectroscopic ellipsometry on mm-sized delafossite CuAlO2 single crystals, measured separately on the (001) top face and a side face. It extracts the in-plane and out-of-plane dielectric functions. The authors report a resonant birefringence |Δn_max| = 3.67 and linear dichroism |Δk_max| = 5.21 near a sharp in-plane exciton at 3.78 eV, and |Δn| > 0.5 across the visible. They attribute the anisotropy to a quasi-two-dimensional Cu d_z2 → Cu p_x/y exciton, using a Penn-gap estimate of Eg, a 2D-versus-3D hydrogenic comparison and GGA band-structure projections. Direct measurement of both tensor components of a wide-gap oxide with strong anisotropy in the near UV would be useful to the photonics community. The novelty rests on three things: whether the extracted tensor components are quantitatively correct, whether the visible-range "transparency" is real, and whether the excitonic interpretation holds. Each needs considerably more support than the paper now gives.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First single-crystal measurement of both the ab-plane and c-axis optical response of CuAlO2 over 0.74–6.46 eV. This is possible because of a crystal-growth route that yields mm-sized crystals with facets.
- A very strong, sharp in-plane resonance at 3.78 eV with almost no c-axis spectral weight. This is a striking polarization selectivity, clearly visible already in the raw Ψ and Δ (Fig. S4).
- Temperature-dependent data from 7 to 300 K (Fig. S8) showing the resonance sharpening to about 120 meV.
- A physically appealing link between the linear O–Cu–O coordination and the polarization selection rule, which could guide the search for other dumbbell-coordinated materials.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The extraction of ε_ab and ε_c relies on an isotropic inversion that is not valid for this geometry, so the headline numbers are not established. Methods and Supplementary Note 2 convert Ψ and Δ from each face with the isotropic two-phase formula ε = sin²θ[1 + tan²θ((1−ρ)/(1+ρ))²]. They state that the result is "dominantly governed by the dielectric function along the parallel direction to the s-polarization". For a uniaxial crystal this is not generally true.
   - On the side face, with c along s, r_s depends on ε_c, but r_p depends on ε_ab, both in-plane along the plane of incidence and along the surface normal. Ψ = |r_p/r_s| and Δ therefore mix both components.
   - The classic result (Aspnes) is that the pseudo-dielectric function is weighted mainly toward the in-plane component lying in the plane of incidence, not the s direction.
   - On the top face, r_p also contains ε_c through the normal component.
   - The paper describes the procedure as "without any model fitting", but the isotropic, semi-infinite, roughness-free inversion is itself a model, and here an inappropriate one.

   Because |Δn_max| = 3.67, |Δk_max| = 5.21, n_ab,max = 5.66 and the |Δn| > 0.5 visible claim all come directly from these pseudo-dielectric functions, the systematic error could be large. Near the resonance, where ε_ab is huge and changes sign (ε_ab1 ≈ −20 at about 3.9 eV), leakage of ε_ab into the "c-axis" spectrum is most likely. The small feature near 4 eV in the c-axis Ψ (Fig. S4) may be such leakage.

   The authors should:
   - analyse both faces with a full uniaxial (Berreman/4×4) model fitted simultaneously to data from both faces, at several angles of incidence;
   - for the side face, measure at two in-plane azimuths (c along s and c in the plane of incidence) and include those data in the joint fit;
   - report generalized or Mueller-matrix ellipsometry (off-diagonal Jones elements) to confirm alignment;
   - include a surface-roughness/overlayer layer;
   - give the c-axis misorientation of the measured side face, for example from Laue on that face. A few degrees of miscut would mix ε_ab into ε_c.

   The corrected Δn(ω) and Δk(ω) should replace Fig. 2.

2. The claim of "transparency across the entire visible spectrum" conflicts with the authors' own absorption data and with the black colour of the crystals. Fig. S6 gives ln α ≈ 10.5–11.8 between 2 and 3.3 eV, which is α ≈ 4×10⁴ to 1.3×10⁵ cm⁻¹ and an absorption length of roughly 0.1–0.25 µm. Fig. 2d shows k_ab ≈ 0.1–0.2 across the visible. A material with these constants is opaque at any thickness relevant to a waveplate or polarizer. The crystals are black (Fig. 1b, Fig. S1).

   The paper handles this by defining the "transparent region" as the Urbach tail (main text p. 4; Fig. 3 caption). That is not a transparency criterion: the Urbach tail is absorption. The linear ln α over 2–3.35 eV has a slope of order 1 eV⁻¹, far shallower than a typical Urbach edge, which points to an extrinsic, defect-band absorption.

   The attribution to 1.4% Cu²⁺ (citing ref. 18) is asserted, not shown, and the same defects may also change n in the visible.

   To support the abstract and Fig. 3, the authors need:
   - transmission measurements on thin polished slabs of known thickness, or on colourless crystals;
   - a demonstration that the visible-range n_ab and n_c are intrinsic, for example by comparing crystals with different Cu²⁺ content or after annealing, and by comparing with the thin-film/powder index of ref. 16;
   - otherwise, a reworded claim: these crystals are absorbing in the visible, and intrinsic transparency is inferred.

   Ellipsometric k values of order 0.1 or below also carry substantial systematic uncertainty (see concern 6).

3. The 1.34 eV binding energy, and the "2D signature" Eb/Eg ≈ 1/4, rest on equating a single-oscillator Penn-gap parameter with the quasiparticle gap.
   - In Supplementary Note 4, ω_p = 5.12 eV comes from fitting ε_ab1 between 1 and 2.25 eV with a one-oscillator formula. The Penn gap is a spectrally averaged oscillator energy dominated by the strongest interband weight. It is not the onset of the continuum, and it lies above the gap in essentially all semiconductors.
   - The fit window is narrow and noisy (Fig. S7), and ε∞ and ω_p are strongly correlated. No uncertainty is given.
   - The window also sits where the defect absorption of concern 2 is present.
   - Eb = Eg − E_1s and the placement of CuAlO2 on the 1/4 line in Fig. 4a then follow directly from this choice. The agreement with sc-GW (5.1 eV) is suggestive, but GW gaps for delafossites vary by more than 1 eV between references, and agreement with one calculation is not a measurement.
   - The 1/4 scaling is an empirical trend for free-standing 2D sheets. Satisfying it is not evidence of 2D confinement in a bulk crystal: many bulk materials with strong excitons would fall near the line once Eg is defined this way.

   The authors should:
   - determine the continuum onset directly, from low-temperature (7 K) ε2 line-shape analysis (Elliott fit including the continuum), second-derivative spectra, or photoemission plus inverse photoemission;
   - give error bars on Eg and Eb;
   - remove or qualify the inference from Fig. 4a.

4. The assignment of the broad 4.47 eV feature to 2s and higher Rydberg states, and the conclusion that the exciton is "best described by the 2D model", are not demonstrated.

   (a) Internal contradiction. Section 5 describes the transition as "intratom-like" Cu d_z2 → Cu p_x/y. An exciton with Eb = 1.34 eV built from on-site orbitals has a Bohr radius comparable to the Cu–Cu spacing (2.85 Å), which is Frenkel-like. Hydrogenic Wannier models (2D or 3D) are then not applicable, and the Rydberg series would not be expected to follow Eq. (3)–(5).

   (b) Tuned 2D model. The 2D screened model has adjustable inputs:
   - Cu-layer thickness, taken as half the dumbbell length;
   - the choice between the two EMA roots for ε_Cu and ε_Al, justified by analogy to Al2O3, although the reported 2.34 does not match the 2.7 quoted for Al2O3;
   - n-dependent effective dielectric constants ε_n taken from ref. 44.
   
   The 3D comparison model is isotropic, with no screening and no anisotropic mass. The 2D model "winning" a match to a single broad hump is therefore not a fair discrimination. An anisotropic 3D or fractional-dimension model (ref. 41), with the same number of free parameters, is the appropriate competitor. A sensitivity analysis of the 2s energy to d1/d2 and the EMA root should be shown.

   (c) Alternative assignments not excluded. The 4.47 eV hump could be the onset of band-to-band continuum, an interband transition at another k-point (the GGA bands in Fig. 5a show several flat valence bands), or a vibronic sideband. In Fig. S8 the 4.47 eV feature does not sharpen with cooling while the 1s narrows strongly, which argues against a 2s Rydberg state.

   The authors should:
   - show low-temperature second-derivative spectra resolving any Rydberg structure;
   - compute ε2 from BSE, or at least RPA from DFT/GW, for both polarizations and compare quantitatively;
   - temper "conclusively demonstrate" (p. 6).

B. Should address

5. The "highest reported to date" claims compare unlike quantities, and Fig. 3 partly contradicts the text.
   - |Δn_max| = 3.67 and |Δk_max| = 5.21 occur at the exciton resonance (333 and 322 nm), where k_ab ≈ 5. Resonant values are not comparable to the transparent-range Δn of LiNbO3, calcite or rutile. Strongly excitonic anisotropic materials (for example ReS2, CrSBr, NbOCl2, h-BN in the deep UV) also have large resonant Δn and Δk, and they are not compared on the same resonant basis. The comparison should be split into transparent-range and resonant values, and the resonant claims should be benchmarked against resonant data from the literature.
   - In Fig. 3, MoS2 and C3H6N6F6·3H2O lie above CuAlO2 between 700 and 800 nm. The caption's "highest birefringence across the visible and ultraviolet regions" is therefore not what the figure shows. The text's criterion ("transparency below 1.5 eV") does not explain why these are plotted yet excluded.
   - h-BN (ref. 24), the obvious UV-transparent competitor, is not plotted.
   - The CuAlO2 stars in the "transparent region" extend to 370 nm, where α ≈ 10⁵ cm⁻¹ (Fig. S6).

6. Uncertainties are absent, although the visible-range claims sit near the reported thresholds.
   - In the visible, Ψ for the ab face is only about 3–6° and visibly noisy (Fig. S4), and the extracted ε_ab1 scatters by ±0.5 near 1.2 eV (Fig. S7).
   - The claim that |Δn| > 0.5 throughout the visible depends on values of about 0.55–0.6 at 750–800 nm (Fig. 3).
   - Error bands on n_ab, n_c, Δn and k should be given, including systematic contributions from angle-of-incidence error, roughness and the anisotropic-mixing correction of concern 1.
   - Depolarization should be reported, as an indicator of surface quality and of beam overfilling the facet.

7. Sample statistics and facet geometry are not given.
   - The crystals are 1–2 mm, and the side facet in Fig. 1b is visibly narrower than that. The beam spot size at 70° incidence (which elongates the footprint by about 3×), any use of focusing probes, and how overfill and edge scattering were excluded are not stated.
   - It is not stated how many crystals were measured, whether the top and side spectra come from the same crystal, and whether the 3.78 eV resonance and Δn reproduce from crystal to crystal.
   - "Smooth and shiny" surfaces should be quantified (AFM roughness), since Fig. S1 shows terraced and textured facets. Roughness directly reduces the apparent ε at high energy.

8. The theory does not connect quantitatively to the measured anisotropy, and the orbital-character argument is incomplete.
   - No ε_ab(ω) or ε_c(ω) is computed. Only band characters and a symmetry argument are given.
   - Fig. 5c shows only faint Cu p_x/y weight in the lowest conduction band along L–F. Fig. 5d shows sizable O p_x/y weight there. Cu 4s, which is commonly reported to contribute to the delafossite conduction-band minimum, is not projected at all.
   - The statement that c-polarized transitions are "forbidden" holds strictly only for a pure on-site d_z2 → p_x/y picture. With O and Cu-s admixture, the z-polarized matrix element is generically non-zero.
   - GGA without +U may misplace Cu-3d states. The cited GW evidence concerns other works' band ordering, not this calculation's matrix elements.

   The authors should compute polarization-resolved momentum matrix elements |⟨v|p_x,y,z|c⟩|² along L–F and the RPA/BSE ε2 tensor. They should check the robustness of the CB character with GGA+U or a hybrid functional.

9. The link between "two-dimensional confinement" and the anisotropy is asserted, not argued. A dipole selection rule (d_z2 → p_x/y allowed for E ∥ ab; p_z pushed up by the dumbbell) produces polarization selectivity whatever the exciton's dimensionality. Conversely, a 2D-confined exciton need not be polarization-selective. The title-level message ("enabled by ... two-dimensionally confined excitons") should make clear which ingredient carries the anisotropy. Ideally the authors would show, for example from a 3D anisotropic model with the same selection rule, that 2D confinement is needed to reach the measured Δn.

10. The Fano analysis and the line-width argument are under-specified.
   - Supplementary Note 5 places the "Fano-like behaviour" at 4.15 eV in the Fig. S8 caption, but assigns the Fano term to the 1s at 3.78 eV in the main text.
   - The fitted q parameter, the widths, the temperature dependence of the parameters, and the evidence that the asymmetry is not produced by the anisotropic-mixing artifact of concern 1 are not reported.
   - The statement that a 200 meV room-temperature width "suggests ... low-dimensional systems" (p. 3) is not a diagnostic: line width reflects phonon coupling and disorder, not dimensionality. A Toyozawa/Segall-type fit of width against T (Fig. S8) would at least extract the exciton–phonon coupling.

11. The application claims include physically excluded functions. CuAlO2 is centrosymmetric (R-3m, Table S1), so it has no linear electro-optic (Pockels) effect and no bulk χ(2). The suggestion of "electro-optic modulators and nonlinear optics" (p. 7) should be corrected or restricted to χ(3)/Kerr processes. More broadly, applications based on the resonant Δn are limited by k ≈ 5 at the same energy, and the application discussion should say so.

C. Minor

12. The text and figure disagree on the Δn ranges. Page 3–4 states that |Δn| "exceeds 0.5 across nearly the entire range, except ... 3.86–3.9 eV, and even surpasses 1 between 2.86 eV and 6.2 eV". Fig. 2e shows |Δn| dropping toward zero near 3.9 eV and to about 0.5 near 5.2 eV, so the "> 1" statement cannot hold over 2.86–6.2 eV.

13. The Fig. 2e caption says "black solid circles and red open squares", but the plot shows black open circles and red filled circles. The Fig. 5 caption cites refs. 12 and 40 for the band structure, while the text cites 12 and 50.

14. The Fig. 1c caption ("light polarized parallel to the c-axis is transmitted ... whereas light polarized parallel to the ab-plane is absorbed") holds only near the 3.8 eV resonance. It should state the energy range.

15. Several gap terms are used inconsistently: the "direct bandgap" of 3.71 eV (Tauc), the "direct optical bandgap" (abstract), the 1s exciton at 3.78 eV, and the "electronic bandgap" of 5.12 eV. A Tauc fit through an excitonic peak gives the exciton absorption edge, not a band gap. The terminology should be unified and the Tauc analysis qualified.

16. Supplementary Note 1 calls the single-crystal XRD analysis a "Rietveld refinement". It should report the structure-refinement details: atomic positions, R factors, site occupancies and any Cu deficiency. These bear on the Cu²⁺ attribution and on the dumbbell length used in the EMA.

17. Typos and wording: "semicondcutor" (p. 2); "This works" (Fig. 3); "which is the highest value for other UV range transparent anisotropic materials" (p. 4); "ε2," (Methods); "allocated until this threshold" (Supplementary Note 3).

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Re-analyse all ellipsometry with a full uniaxial model fitted jointly to both faces, several angles and two side-face azimuths, including roughness. Present Δn(ω) and Δk(ω) with error bands. This single step would make the headline numbers credible.
2. Measure transmission through thin polished slabs, ideally on crystals with reduced Cu²⁺ (for example after annealing), to establish the intrinsic visible-range absorption and index. Restate the transparency claims accordingly.
3. Use the existing 7 K data to locate the continuum onset and any Rydberg structure directly (derivative spectra, Elliott fit). Compare with a BSE-computed ε2 tensor for both polarizations rather than hydrogenic models.
4. Recast the comparison in Fig. 3 and the "highest reported" statements into separate transparent-range and resonant comparisons. Include h-BN and other excitonic anisotropic crystals on equal footing.
