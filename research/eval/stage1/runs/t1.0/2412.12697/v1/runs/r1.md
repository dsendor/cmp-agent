Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors grew millimetre-sized CuAlO2 single crystals and used spectroscopic ellipsometry on the (001) top face and a side face to report separate ab-plane and c-axis dielectric functions from 0.74 to 6.46 eV. They report a very strong, sharp ab-polarized excitonic peak at 3.78 eV that is absent for c-polarized light. This gives a resonant birefringence |Δn|max = 3.67 and |Δk|max = 5.21, and |Δn| > 0.5 throughout the visible. They attribute the effect to a quasi-two-dimensional Cu dz2 → Cu px/py exciton, using an Eb/Eg ≈ 1/4 argument, a comparison of 2D and 3D hydrogenic series, and GGA orbital projections. The first direct, polarization-resolved optical constants of bulk CuAlO2 would be a useful contribution. However, the two headline claims are not yet secured by the analysis shown: record-level anisotropy combined with visible transparency, and a 2D-confined exciton as its origin.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Large single crystals give, for the first time, access to both principal dielectric components of a delafossite. This is a real experimental advance over film and powder studies.
- The qualitative result is striking and plausible: a strong in-plane-only exciton at 3.78 eV, a large in-plane ε2 (≈47 at 300 K, ≈72 at 7 K) and a near-featureless c-axis response below 5 eV. It is consistent with the linear O-Cu-O coordination.
- Temperature-dependent data down to 7 K (Fig. S8, S9) and the raw Ψ, Δ spectra (Fig. S4) are provided.
- The proposed link between dumbbell coordination and polarization selection rules is simple and testable in other delafossites. The data also imply a natural hyperbolic window (ε_ab1 < 0 < ε_c1 from about 3.85 to 6 eV) that the authors do not exploit.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The extraction of ε_ab and ε_c from a uniaxial crystal is not justified, and the headline numbers depend on it. Methods and Supplementary Note 2 convert each (Ψ, Δ) pair with the isotropic two-phase inversion ε = sin²θ[1 + tan²θ((1−ρ)/(1+ρ))²]. They argue that the result is "dominantly governed by the dielectric function along the s-polarization".
   - This is not generally correct. In the side-face geometry of Fig. S3 (c along s), r_s depends on ε_c, while r_p depends only on ε_ab, because the in-plane p direction and the surface normal both lie in the ab-plane. Ψ and Δ are a ratio of the two, so the pseudo-dielectric function is a mixture of both components. Aspnes's well-known approximation (JOSA 70, 1275 (1980)) says ⟨ε⟩ is closest to the component along the intersection of the plane of incidence with the surface, not along s.
   - For the top face the mixing with ε_c is weaker but not negligible near 3.8 eV, where |ε_ab| is very large.
   - The c-axis Ψ and Δ in Fig. S4 show clear features near 4.0–4.2 eV, exactly where the ab exciton lies. This suggests leakage of ε_ab into the "c-axis" spectrum.
   - Because |Δn|max = 3.67, |Δk|max = 5.21 and n_ab,max = 5.66 all sit on this resonance, the record claims rest on this approximation.

   What the authors should do:
   - Treat both faces with the exact uniaxial Fresnel equations, inverted point by point. This needs no oscillator model, so the "no model fitting" spirit is preserved.
   - Measure the side face at two azimuths (c ⟂ and c ∥ to the plane of incidence) and at several angles of incidence, and invert all data jointly.
   - Show that the recovered ε_c no longer contains the 4 eV feature.
   - Quote uncertainties on n, k, Δn and Δk.

2. Spot footprint, surface condition and depolarization are not characterized. The crystals are 1–2 mm hexagonal pillars, and the side faces in Fig. S1 and Fig. 1b look visibly stepped and textured rather than optically flat. At 70° incidence the beam footprint is elongated about threefold. The paper does not say whether focusing optics were used, what the footprint was relative to the faces, or whether light reached the silver epoxy or copper cone.
   - Surface roughness, a Cu2+-rich or oxidized overlayer, and spill-over all reduce and distort the apparent ε2 peak, and so change n_ab,max and Δn_max.
   - The ab-plane Ψ below 2.5 eV is only about 5° and very noisy (Fig. S4). The low-energy ε_ab1 scatters by ±0.5–1 (Fig. S7), yet the visible-range Δn ≈ 0.5–0.7 claim comes from this region.

   What the authors should do:
   - Report the footprint, AFM roughness of both faces, and the measured depolarization spectra, which the M-2000 provides.
   - Include and justify a roughness or overlayer layer, or show that one changes the results negligibly.
   - Show reproducibility on a second crystal and a second side face.

3. The "transparency across the entire visible spectrum" and "without optical loss" claims contradict the paper's own data.
   - Fig. S6 gives ln α ≈ 10.5–11.5 between 2 and 3 eV, i.e. α ≈ 4×10⁴–10⁵ cm⁻¹. Fig. 2d gives k_ab ≈ 0.1–0.2 across the visible. That is an absorption length of a fraction of a micron, comparable to a strongly absorbing semiconductor, and the crystals are black (Fig. 1b).
   - The authors label this an "Urbach tail" from about 1.4% Cu2+ and define the transparent region as wherever ln α is linear. A linear ln α from 1 to 3.35 eV implies a characteristic energy of order 1 eV, which is not an Urbach tail in the usual sense. The definition also differs from how transparency is defined for the comparison materials in Fig. 3: rutile, calcite and LiNbO3 have α of order 10⁻² to 10⁰ cm⁻¹ there.
   - Ellipsometry on thick crystals has poor sensitivity to k ≲ 0.05, so the true sub-gap loss is in any case not determined.

   What the authors should do:
   - Measure polarized transmission (and ideally retardance) on a polished, oriented thin plate to obtain α_ab(λ), α_c(λ) and Δn directly in the visible.
   - Either show that intrinsic loss is low, for example on lighter-coloured or annealed crystals with lower Cu2+, or restate the claim. The present wording of the abstract and the Fig. 3 caption does not follow from the data.

4. The binding energy and the Eb/Eg ≈ 1/4 argument rest on a mis-identified band gap.
   - Eg = 5.12 eV is the ω_p parameter of a single-oscillator (Penn/Sellmeier) fit to noisy ε_ab1 between 1.0 and 2.25 eV (Supplementary Note 4, Eq. 1). This is an average oscillator energy weighted over all transitions, including the 3.78 eV exciton itself, not the quasiparticle gap. Its uncertainty from a two-parameter fit over such a narrow window is likely large, and none is quoted.
   - Eb = Eg − E1s = 1.34 eV inherits this. Its agreement with scGW (5.1 eV) and with one BSE value (1.2 eV, Ref. 13) is therefore coincidental support, not a measurement. The other BSE study (Ref. 12) gives 0.47 eV.
   - The paper uses three incompatible "gaps": the 3.71 eV "direct bandgap" from a Tauc plot (Fig. S5), which here is simply the low-energy flank of the exciton peak; 5.12 eV plotted as "direct bandgap" in Fig. 4a; and the literature 3.5 eV.
   - The 1/4 relation is an empirical correlation among 2D materials. Fig. 4a itself places bulk MoTe2 and bulk CrSBr on the line, so the ratio cannot discriminate 2D from 3D.

   What the authors should do:
   - Determine Eg independently, for example from the onset of the continuum in low-temperature spectra, from a Elliott-type fit of ε2 that includes the continuum, or from photoemission/inverse photoemission or published GW+BSE.
   - Give error bars, and drop or heavily qualify the 1/4-scaling argument.

5. The 2D-versus-3D hydrogenic comparison (Fig. 4c,d; Supplementary Notes 6–7) cannot establish 2D confinement.
   - With Eb = 1.34 eV and ε ≈ 5, the Wannier Bohr radius is about 1 Å, smaller than the Cu–Cu spacing (2.85 Å). The authors themselves describe the transition as "intratom-like" Cu d→p, i.e. Frenkel/charge-transfer. Neither hydrogenic series is applicable.
   - The pure 2D Rydberg series of Eq. 3 would put the 2s state at Eg − Eb/9 ≈ 4.97 eV, closer to the gap than the 3D value (≈4.8 eV). The 4.40 eV obtained in Fig. 4c therefore comes entirely from the n-dependent effective screening ε_n from the EMA construction, not from dimensionality.
   - That construction has ad hoc inputs:
     - a Cu-layer thickness of 2.038 Å called "half the dumbbell", whereas Cu–O is about 1.86 Å;
     - an Al2O3 reference ε of 2.7, whereas ε∞ of sapphire is about 3.1;
     - layer dielectric constants fitted at 0.74 eV, which already contain the exciton's own oscillator strength.
   - The broad 4.47 eV Lorentzian could equally be interband transitions at other k-points, the continuum onset, phonon sidebands or Cu2+-related transitions. Its integrated weight relative to 1s is not compared with the 2D prediction (f2s/f1s = 1/27 for ideal 2D).

   What the authors should do:
   - Show how the 2s position depends on the layer thickness and the ε inputs.
   - Compare oscillator strengths quantitatively.
   - Above all, see concern 6.

B. Should address

6. The theory does not test the claims quantitatively. The paper's central mechanistic claim is "an excitonic Cu d-p transition confined to the atomic-thick layer". However:
   - Fig. 5 shows only GGA band projections and single-particle Bloch functions at one k-point (L). The "forbidden for c-polarization" statement is argued from atomic-orbital parity, not from the little group at L or from computed momentum matrix elements.
   - Anisotropy from a dipole selection rule and 2D confinement of the exciton envelope are distinct statements. The first does not imply the second.
   - The primitive cell has 4 atoms, so GW+BSE is not "beyond the scope". Refs. 12, 13 and 48 have already done it.

   What the authors should do:
   - Report the computed ε_ab(ω) and ε_c(ω): RPA/IPA at minimum, BSE preferably.
   - Plot the exciton wavefunction or its extent along c, and give the k_z dispersion of the relevant bands.
   - Compare peak positions and intensity ratios with Fig. 2 and with the published BSE spectra.
   - State whether a Hubbard U or hybrid functional changes the dz2/4s/O pz composition of the top valence band at L.

7. The "highest reported to date" comparison is not like-for-like. |Δn|max = 3.67 and |Δk|max = 5.21 are resonant values at a strong exciton, where k_ab ≈ 5. These should be compared with resonant values in other strongly anisotropic excitonic materials, not with off-resonant birefringence of transparent crystals. Examples are the exciton region of h-BN, bulk TMDCs, CrSBr (Ref. 19), NbOCl2 and quasi-1D BaTiS3 (Ref. 10).
   - Fig. 3 itself shows MoS2 and C3H6N6F6·3H2O, and h-BN at about 0.7, with |Δn| larger than CuAlO2's about 0.6 at 600–800 nm. This contradicts the caption's "highest birefringence across the visible and ultraviolet regions".
   - "Linear dichroism" is used for Δk, whereas linear dichroism is usually Δα or the absorbance difference.

   What the authors should do: add a table of resonant Δn and Δk with sources, and narrow the claim to what the comparison supports.

8. Consistency with literature values is not checked.
   - The paper does not compare n_ab, n_c and ε∞ with the earlier refractive-index study (Ref. 16) and the FTIR ε∞ (Ref. 17), which it cites, or with thin-film data.
   - It attributes "direct bandgap of about 3.71 eV" to Ref. 15, which reports about 3.5 eV.
   - The Tauc extraction (Fig. S5) is fitted over about 0.05 eV on an excitonic line shape. The indirect value (3.45 eV) is obtained where the paper itself says defect absorption dominates (below 3.35 eV).

   What the authors should do: tabulate their values against prior work and discuss the discrepancies.

9. Sample characterization and reproducibility are thin.
   - The paper does not say how many crystals were measured, or whether the top-face and side-face data come from the same crystal.
   - The Cu2+ concentration (1.4%) is taken from Ref. 18 rather than measured on these crystals, although it is invoked to explain both the black colour and the sub-gap absorption.
   - The side-face index ((100) versus (110)) and its misorientation are not verified, for example by Laue.
   - Supplementary Note 1 calls a single-crystal structure solution a "Rietveld refinement" and gives no R-factors, occupancies or displacement parameters. Cu occupancy and Cu/Al antisite disorder matter for the optics.

   What the authors should do: add these, plus a second-crystal data set for Fig. 2.

10. The temperature-dependent exciton analysis is underused and partly misinterpreted.
    - The main text cites a 200 meV room-temperature linewidth as typical of low-dimensional systems. Linewidth is not a dimensionality diagnostic.
    - The Fano assignment (Supplementary Note 5) attributes the asymmetry to "coupling between 1s exciton and higher-order exciton". Fano interference requires a discrete state coupled to a continuum, which is not identified.
    - Asymmetry can also arise from the component mixing in concern 1.
    - Fig. S8 places the Fano feature at 4.15 eV, while the text discusses 4.47 eV.

    What the authors should do:
    - Fit E1s(T), Γ(T) and the oscillator strength versus T (exciton–phonon coupling).
    - Report the Fano q and its T-dependence.
    - Show the 7 K versions of Fig. 4b–d, where a Rydberg series would be best resolved.

11. No uncertainties are given anywhere. Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4a/b and the fitted Eg, Eb, ε∞, ε_Cu and ε_Al carry no error bars, even though the low-energy ab data are visibly noisy (Fig. S4, S7). The reader cannot judge whether |Δn| > 0.5 below 1.5 eV, or the 4.40 versus 4.47 eV agreement, is significant.

    What the authors should do: propagate the Ψ/Δ noise and angle uncertainty, and report fit covariances.

C. Minor

12. The Fig. 2 caption says "black solid circles and red open squares", but the panel shows black open circles (|Δn|) and red filled circles (|Δk|).

13. The text states |Δn| "surpasses 1 between 2.86 eV and 6.2 eV" (outside 3.86–3.9 eV), but Fig. 3 shows |Δn| dropping to about 0.7 near 210–220 nm (≈5.7–5.9 eV). Please check.

14. In Fig. 4a the x-axis label is "Direct bandgap", but the CuAlO2 point uses the 5.12 eV Penn gap, while the paper elsewhere calls 3.71 eV the direct bandgap.

15. The Fig. 5a caption cites Refs. 12 and 40 for consistency of the band structure, whereas the text cites Refs. 12 and 50.

16. "Without any model fitting" (Introduction, Supplementary Note 2) overstates the method. The isotropic semi-infinite substrate with no overlayer is itself a model.

17. The sentence "CuAlO2 achieves a maximum |Δn| value of 1.66 at 370 nm … which is the highest value for other UV range transparent anisotropic materials (Ref. 27)" is unclear. Please state what is being compared.

18. Typos and wording: "semicondcutor"; "transparency in the up to UV range"; "This works" (Fig. 3); "Supplementary note 7"; Ref. 22 page numbers "1309_1301-1310". In Methods, write (1−ρ)/(1+ρ) explicitly. The claim that excitons "prolong excited-state lifetimes" as a reason for a large ε2 peak needs a reference or removal.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Prepare thin, polished a-cut and c-cut plates and measure polarized transmission and retardance in the visible. This gives a direct, model-light determination of Δn and of the true intrinsic loss, and would settle concerns 1–3 together.
2. Add a GW+BSE (or at least RPA) calculation of the full dielectric tensor with exciton wavefunction plots. Use it, rather than hydrogenic models, to support or revise the "2D-confined exciton" interpretation.
3. Test the dumbbell mechanism comparatively by measuring, or at least calculating, CuGaO2 or CuInO2. A clear trend in anisotropy with the B-site would greatly strengthen the generality claimed in the Discussion.
4. Discuss the natural hyperbolic window (ε_ab1 < 0 < ε_c1, about 3.85–6 eV, Fig. 2a), its losses, and its relevance to UV exciton-polariton photonics. This may be a more distinctive application than visible birefringence, given the sub-gap absorption.
