Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors grew mm-sized CuAlO2 single crystals and measured the ab-plane and c-axis dielectric functions separately by spectroscopic ellipsometry on the top (001) and side (100) faces. They report a very sharp, purely in-plane exciton at 3.78 eV. They derive from it record birefringence and dichroism (|Δn_max| = 3.67, |Δk_max| = 5.21), and |Δn| > 0.5 across the visible range. They attribute the anisotropy to a quasi-two-dimensional Cu dz2 → Cu px/y exciton confined to the Cu+ layer. Separate optical constants for the two axes of a wide-gap delafossite would be a useful dataset, and the orbital picture is appealing. The headline claims, however, depend on an ellipsometric inversion, a "transparency" definition and an exciton-dimensionality analysis, and none of the three is yet secure.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- This is the first direct measurement of both ab and c optical constants of CuAlO2 on bulk single crystals. The raw Ψ and Δ are shown (Fig. S4), and the in-plane exciton is unambiguously strong and sharp (Fig. 2b).
- The polarization selectivity of the 3.78 eV transition is striking: ε_c2 is nearly zero below 5 eV. This anisotropy is qualitatively robust, whatever the details of the extraction.
- Temperature-dependent ε_ab2 from 7 to 300 K (Fig. S8) and the DFT orbital analysis (Fig. 5) give a coherent qualitative picture of an on-site Cu d → p excitation.
- A clear structural design principle (the linear O-Cu-O dumbbell) is offered that could guide searches in other delafossites.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The dielectric tensor components are extracted with an isotropic inversion that does not hold for this geometry. Every headline number depends on it.**
   - *What was done.* Methods (p. 9), Supplementary Note 2 and the Fig. 2 caption state that ε_ab and ε_c were obtained "without model fitting" from the two-phase isotropic formula ε = sin²θ[1 + tan²θ((1−ρ)/(1+ρ))²], measured at a single angle of incidence (70°). The justification is that the response "is dominated by the direction parallel to s-polarization."
   - *Why that does not hold.* For a uniaxial crystal this is not generally true. r_s probes the s direction, but r_p probes both the in-plane direction within the plane of incidence and the surface-normal component. The well-known result for pseudo-dielectric functions is that ⟨ε⟩ is dominated by the component along the intersection of the plane of incidence with the surface, not by the s direction. In the "c-axis" geometry (Fig. S3, right) c is along s, so the plane of incidence contains only ab directions, and r_p is governed entirely by ε_ab. The extracted "ε_c" is therefore a mixture involving ε_ab. In the "ab-plane" geometry, r_p also involves ε_c through the normal component.
   - *Where the error is largest.* The mixing matters most exactly where the claims are made. Near 3.78 eV, ε_ab1 swings from +25 to −20 and ε_ab2 reaches 47, so even a small admixture would distort n_c, k_c and hence Δn and Δk.
   - *Practical issues not described.*
     - The M-2000 spot size and whether focusing probes were used. At 70° the spot is elongated about 3×, and the side faces of 1–2 mm crystals (Fig. 1b, Fig. S1) may be overfilled, collecting light from the edges, the silver epoxy and the copper cone.
     - The azimuthal alignment accuracy of c relative to s.
     - Surface roughness. The as-grown side facets in Fig. S1 look visibly textured, and no overlayer or roughness layer is included, although a roughness layer strongly reduces the extracted peak ε2 and n.
   - *Requested.*
     - Re-analyse the data with a full uniaxial semi-infinite model (Berreman or 4×4), fitting both faces jointly with a common ε_ab and ε_c (point-by-point inversion is fine).
     - Add multiple angles of incidence (e.g., 60°, 65°, 70°, 75°) and at least two azimuths per face. Generalized or Mueller-matrix ellipsometry would also show whether off-diagonal conversion is really absent.
     - Include a roughness layer constrained by AFM.
     - State the spot size and confirm that it lies within each facet.
     - Report how |Δn_max|, |Δk_max| and n_ab,max = 5.66 change, with uncertainties.

2. **The "transparent across the visible" claim is contradicted by the paper's own absorption data.**
   - *The data.* Fig. S6 gives ln α ≈ 10.5–11.8 between 2 and 3.3 eV, i.e. α ≈ 4×10⁴ to 1.3×10⁵ cm⁻¹. The absorption length is therefore about 0.1–0.3 µm across the visible. This matches k_ab ≈ 0.1–0.3 in Fig. 2d and the black colour of the crystals (Fig. 1 caption). A material with these values is not transparent in any practical photonic sense.
   - *The definition used.* The paper defines the "transparent region" as the Urbach tail below the gap (p. 4; Fig. 3 caption) and attributes the absorption to about 1.4% Cu2+ taken from Ref. 18. Three problems follow:
     - The Cu2+ content of these crystals is not measured.
     - The slope in Fig. S6 corresponds to an "Urbach energy" of roughly 1 eV, far from a genuine band-tail value (tens of meV). The absorption is broad extrinsic (or intrinsic indirect/defect) absorption, not an Urbach edge.
     - The comparison materials in Fig. 3 (calcite, LiNbO3, rutile) are genuinely low-loss.
   - *Consequence.* Neither the abstract's "without optical loss across a broad spectral range" nor the comparison in Fig. 3 is currently supported.
   - *Requested.*
     - Measure transmission on a thinned, polished plate (ideally from a lighter-coloured, low-Cu2+ crystal) with both polarizations.
     - Quantify the intrinsic absorption floor, and determine the Cu2+ concentration of the measured crystals (EPR or XPS).
     - Either reframe the claim in terms of a loss figure of merit such as Δn/k (or Δn per absorption length), or restrict it to "below the direct gap."
     - Note that ellipsometry alone cannot establish k at the 10⁻³–10⁻⁴ level relevant to transparency.

3. **The evidence for a two-dimensional exciton is circular or under-constrained.**
   - *The binding energy.* The 2D claim rests on E_b = 1.34 eV and E_b/E_g ≈ 1/4 (Fig. 4a), with E_g = 5.12 eV taken from a single-oscillator "Penn gap" fit to ε_ab1 between 1 and 2.25 eV (Fig. S7). A Penn/Wemple single-oscillator energy is an average oscillator energy weighted by all transitions. Here it is dominated by the 3.78 eV exciton itself, and it is not the quasiparticle band edge. Identifying ω_p with E_g is unjustified, and the agreement with sc-GW (5.1 eV) may be coincidental. E_b is then just ω_p − 3.78 eV.
   - *The 1/4 ratio.* The ratio is a scaling observed empirically across 2D materials. It is not a test of dimensionality for a single compound, and several of the points in Fig. 4a are bulk crystals (bulk MoTe2, CrSBr).
   - *The 2D-vs-3D hydrogen comparison (Figs. 4c,d; Notes 6–7).*
     - It uses an E_b fixed by the Penn fit, an unspecified exciton reduced mass μ, and layer dielectric constants from an effective-medium construction with two arbitrary solutions.
     - It assumes equal linewidths.
     - It compares with a 2s feature at 4.47 eV that is itself a single broad Lorentzian of uncertain origin. Other interband transitions at different k points, Cu2+ or defect bands, or phonon sidebands are not excluded.
   - *Validity of hydrogenic models.* With E_b ≈ 1.3 eV and ε ~ 5, the exciton radius is of order a few Å, comparable to the Cu-Cu distance (2.85 Å). Neither hydrogenic model is then valid without central-cell corrections, and a "3D isotropic" model is a straw man. The relevant alternative is an anisotropic 3D (or Frenkel-like) exciton.
   - *The 7 K data.* These would be the natural test, but Fig. S8 shows no resolved 2s peak.
   - *Requested.*
     - Determine E_g independently: absorption-continuum onset at low temperature, photoemission plus inverse photoemission or STS, or a two-photon or Rydberg-series analysis.
     - Provide a GW-BSE calculation of ε_ab(ω) and ε_c(ω), giving the exciton wavefunction extent along c and in-plane, with the exciton position compared directly with experiment.
     - State μ and all fixed and free parameters of the hydrogen models.
     - Otherwise, soften "conclusively demonstrate" (p. 6) to "is consistent with."

B. Should address

4. **The "highest reported" claims are not supported, and in part are contradicted by Fig. 3.**
   - *Fig. 3 itself.* Between 800 and ~700 nm, the points labelled C3H3N3O3·3H2O and MoS2 lie well above the CuAlO2 curve (Δn ≈ 2–2.5 against ≈ 0.6). Yet the caption claims CuAlO2 has "the highest birefringence across the visible and ultraviolet regions."
   - *Peak values.* |Δn_max| = 3.67 and |Δk_max| = 5.21 occur at an absorbing resonance, where ε_ab1 changes sign. Other resonant or hyperbolic materials (e.g., excitonic vdW crystals such as CrSBr, Ref. 19; quasi-1D chalcogenides, Ref. 10) should be compared at resonance on the same footing. Otherwise the "highest to date" claim in the abstract compares resonant with off-resonant values.
   - *The 370 nm value.* "Δn = 1.66 at 370 nm within its transparent region" sits where α exceeds 10⁵ cm⁻¹ (Fig. S6).
   - *Requested.* Restate the claims with like-for-like comparisons. Remove or explain the inclusion of absorbing materials (MoS2) in a figure framed around transparency.

5. **The microscopic mechanism is argued qualitatively. The calculations do not show the anisotropy or the 2D confinement.**
   - *Missing quantities.* Section "Discussion" (pp. 6–7) and Fig. 5 infer selection rules from orbital projections. No momentum matrix elements and no calculated ε_ab(ω) and ε_c(ω) (even at the RPA level) are shown.
   - *The selection-rule argument.* An on-site d → p dipole transition is parity-allowed for both polarizations; dz2 → pz is allowed for E‖c. The c-axis suppression therefore depends on the energy ordering of pz versus px/y, which GGA may misplace.
   - *Dispersion along c.* The conduction band in Fig. 5a disperses appreciably along Z–Γ, which is not obviously compatible with a 2D-confined state.
   - *Requested.*
     - Compute the polarization-resolved dielectric tensor (GGA+scissor or GW, ideally with BSE).
     - Report the band masses along c and in-plane, which also gives μ for concern 3.
     - Compare quantitatively with Fig. 2.
     - Check sensitivity to U on the Cu d states or to a hybrid functional, given the known pseudopotential sensitivity noted in Note 7 (Ref. 13).
   - *Minor point.* The Methods list Cu 3p⁶3d¹⁰4s¹ valence configuration, but no spin-orbit or U settings are stated.

6. **The band-gap values are mutually inconsistent and the Tauc analysis is inappropriate.**
   - *The inconsistency.* The "direct optical bandgap" of 3.71 eV (Fig. S5; abstract) lies below the 1s exciton peak at 3.78 eV and far below the claimed electronic gap of 5.12 eV.
   - *The Tauc fit.* A (αE)² Tauc construction applied to the low-energy flank of a Lorentzian excitonic peak has no band-edge meaning.
   - *The indirect gap.* The value of 3.45 eV disagrees with the 3.0 eV in the literature (Ref. 15), and this is not discussed. The "transparency threshold" of 3.35 eV is a fourth number.
   - *Requested.* Use one consistent nomenclature: exciton onset, optical absorption edge and quasiparticle gap. Drop the Tauc-derived "direct bandgap," or justify it.

7. **The sample characterization covers a single crystal, and defect content is not measured.**
   - *Missing information.*
     - How many crystals were measured with ellipsometry.
     - Whether the ab and c spectra come from the same crystal.
     - Whether the values reproduce across crystals and after the 7 K cooldown.
   - *Defects.* The black colour is attributed to 1.4% Cu2+ citing Ref. 18, with no measurement on these samples. Table S1 lists only lattice parameters: no atomic positions, occupancies, displacement parameters or R-factors, although Cu or O off-stoichiometry is exactly what controls Cu2+. The table is also described as a "Rietveld refinement," which is not the method for single-crystal data.
   - *Why it matters.* Defect-related absorption bands near 3.5–4.5 eV could contribute to the 4.47 eV feature and to Δk.
   - *Requested.* Report the full refinement, a composition or valence measurement, and spectra from at least a second crystal for each orientation.

8. **The Fano assignment and the linewidth-based dimensionality argument are weak.**
   - *The Fano feature.* Fig. S8 claims a pronounced "Fano-like behavior at 4.15 eV" at low temperature, but no distinct antiresonance is visible in the plotted ε_ab2.
   - *The proposed mechanism.* Fano coupling of the 1s state to "higher-order excitons" lying above it is not a standard mechanism; a Fano lineshape requires a continuum degenerate with the discrete state.
   - *Fit parameters.* None are given for Fig. 4b or Fig. S9: energies, widths, the q parameter, uncertainties, or which parameters are fixed.
   - *The linewidth argument (p. 3).* A 200 meV room-temperature linewidth is called "typical of low-dimensional systems," but linewidth is set by phonon coupling and inhomogeneity, not dimensionality.
   - *Requested.* Tabulate the fit parameters at all four temperatures. Justify or drop the Fano term, for example by showing that a Lorentz or Toyozawa asymmetric lineshape fails. Remove the linewidth-dimensionality inference.

C. Minor

9. The text (p. 3–4) says |Δn| "surpasses 1 between 2.86 eV and 6.2 eV." Figs. 2e and 3 show |Δn| falling to ≈0.07 near 3.87 eV and below 1 around 5.2–5.8 eV. The p. 3 statement that |Δn| exceeds 0.5 "except 3.86–3.9 eV" also seems inconsistent with the dips near 5.5–6 eV in Fig. 2e. Please quote the ranges precisely.

10. The Fig. 2e caption says "black solid circles and red open squares." The plot shows black open circles for |Δn| and red filled circles for |Δk|. In Fig. 3, "This works" should read "This work." The Fig. 5 caption cites Refs. 12,40, while the text cites Refs. 12,50 for the same comparison.

11. No error bars or uncertainties are given for n, k, Δn, Δk, E_b, E_g or the hydrogen-model peak positions. Δn and Δk are reported only as absolute values; please state the sign (n_c < n_ab, negative uniaxial below the exciton).

12. Supplementary Note 6 takes ε(Al2O3) ≈ 2.7 at 0.74 eV, whereas sapphire has ε ≈ 3.1 (n ≈ 1.75). It also chooses between two EMA solutions by analogy. Please give the resulting ε_n values used in Eq. (5) and test how sensitive the 2s position is to them.

13. The Penn-gap fit (Fig. S7) includes visibly noisy points near 1.1–1.25 eV, probably the detector-switching region of the M-2000. Please exclude or explain them, and give the fit uncertainty on ω_p.

14. Phrasing on p. 4: "selected materials that exhibit transparency below 1.5 eV (~800 nm)" presumably means "transparent at photon energies above 1.5 eV." The Methods' transformation formula should be typeset with proper brackets, and "semicondcutor" (p. 2) and "allocated" (Note 3) need correcting.

15. The Methods state that low-temperature window effects were calibrated with a SiO2/Si wafer. Please state whether the windows affect the anisotropic analysis, given that the 7 K data are used for the Fano fit.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Re-extract ε_ab and ε_c with a full uniaxial model from multi-angle, multi-azimuth (ideally Mueller-matrix) data on both faces, including a roughness layer. Give uncertainties, and confirm that the beam spot is smaller than each facet.
2. Add polarized transmission on a thin polished plate, together with a measured Cu2+ content, to establish the actual optical loss. Reframe the photonic comparison around a loss-aware figure of merit.
3. Replace the Penn-gap and hydrogen-model argument with an independent determination of the quasiparticle gap and a GW-BSE calculation of the polarization-resolved spectrum and exciton spatial extent.
4. Temper the superlatives ("highest reported," "conclusively demonstrate," "without optical loss") so that they match the like-for-like evidence. Make the text, figures and captions agree on the quoted ranges and values.
