Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports the principal optical constants of millimetre-sized delafossite CuAlO2 single crystals, measured by spectroscopic ellipsometry on a (001) face and a side facet. It finds a sharp in-plane-only transition near 3.78 eV that gives |Δn|max = 3.67, |Δn| > 0.5 across the visible range, and a Type-II hyperbolic window between ENZ points near 3.8 and 6.2 eV. GGA calculations attribute this to a dipole-allowed Cu dz²→px/y transition enabled by the linear O–Cu–O dumbbell. A three-dimensionally bonded oxide with large UV–visible birefringence and UV hyperbolicity would be of real interest to the anisotropic-photonics community. However, every headline number rests on one simplified way of extracting the uniaxial dielectric tensor, and the "low-loss" framing is in tension with the paper's own sub-gap extinction data.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First direct, orientation-resolved measurement of both ε_ab and ε_c on bulk CuAlO2 single crystals, made possible by mm-sized faceted crystals. Bulk crystals avoid the substrate and texture problems of thin-film studies.
- The polarization selectivity of the 3.78 eV transition is very pronounced (ε_ab2 ≈ 47 against a featureless ε_c2 in the same range; Fig. 3b). It agrees qualitatively with earlier BSE predictions of a strong in-plane exciton.
- Normal-incidence reflectance (Fig. 3c) gives an independent partial check on ε_ab below about 4.1 eV.
- A clear, symmetry-based microscopic picture (Fig. 1c, Fig. 4) that is plausible and pedagogically useful.
- Source data are deposited.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The tensor extraction (Eq. 1) mixes ε_ab and ε_c by construction, so the reported principal components, and hence |Δn|, are pseudo-quantities of unknown accuracy.** In Methods and the main text (p. 2, p. 7), each geometry is inverted with the isotropic two-phase formula at a single angle (θ = 70°), on the assumption that the response is "dominated by the dielectric-function component parallel to the s-polarized electric field".
   - **Side facet.** With c along s, r_s depends on ε_c. But r_p depends on ε_ab, because both the in-plane p component and the surface-normal component lie in the ab plane. Ψ and Δ are set by r_p/r_s, so the pseudo-ε returned by Eq. 1 is a genuine mixture of ε_ab and ε_c, not ε_c.
   - **(001) face.** r_p involves ε_c through the surface-normal field component.
   - **Why it matters.** The contamination is largest where ε_ab is large and rapidly varying, that is, at 3.6–4.0 eV, exactly where |Δn|max = 3.67, |Δk|max = 5.22, the lower ENZ point and the start of the hyperbolic window are claimed.
   - **What to do.** Fit both orientations simultaneously with a uniaxial anisotropic model (e.g. WVASE/CompleteEASE "biaxial/uniaxial" layer) at several angles of incidence (e.g. 55°, 65°, 75°). Include a surface roughness/overlayer layer. Report fit quality (MSE), parameter correlations and uncertainty bands on ε_ab and ε_c. Show how much |Δn|max, the ENZ energies and the FOM change relative to the Eq. 1 values. Without this, the headline numbers cannot be accepted at the quoted precision.

2. **Azimuthal and tilt alignment of the side facet has not been verified, and the cross-polarization check is done on the wrong surface.** The text says Mueller-matrix measurements on the (001) surface validate the absence of cross-polarization mixing (p. 7; SI). On a (001) face the optic axis is along the surface normal, so the off-diagonal Jones elements vanish by symmetry at every azimuth. That measurement therefore says nothing about the side-facet geometry, where the c-axis must lie accurately along s.
   - Any in-plane rotation of c away from s, or a tilt of the facet away from (100)/(110), mixes ε_ab into the "c-axis" spectrum.
   - The orientation of the side facet is only asserted in Fig. 1b; no Laue or XRD rocking data are shown for it.
   - **What to do.** Show Mueller-matrix data (off-diagonal blocks), with azimuthal rotation, on the side facet itself. Quantify the facet misorientation by X-ray, and propagate the alignment uncertainty into ε_c and |Δn|.

3. **The "low-loss" and "wide-bandgap transparent" framing conflicts with the measured extinction.**
   - The authors report k_ab ≈ k_c ≈ 0.07 at 1 eV (p. 3). At 1.24 µm this corresponds to α = 4πk/λ ≈ 7 × 10³ cm⁻¹, an absorption length of roughly 1.4 µm.
   - Fig. 2b suggests k_ab is still larger (of order 0.2–0.5) across the visible.
   - The crystals are visibly black (Fig. 1b).
   - The maximum |Δn| = 3.67 occurs at 3.72 eV, which is at or above the stated direct gap of 3.71 eV and on the flank of the absorption peak, where k_ab is several units. It is therefore not a property of a transparent window.
   - The highlighted |Δn| = 1.66 at 370 nm (3.35 eV) lies below the direct gap but very close to the 3.45 eV indirect gap, and its k_ab is not given.

   **Why it matters.** The abstract and conclusions present CuAlO2 as bridging "broadband low-loss birefringence" with UV hyperbolicity, and Fig. 2e ranks it against transparent crystals using Δn alone.

   **What to do.**
   - Report k_ab and k_c (or α) alongside Δn at every quoted wavelength (370 nm, 400–780 nm, 333 nm).
   - Plot a loss-aware figure of merit, e.g. Δn/max(k_ab, k_c), or the retardance achievable per absorption length, against the comparison materials.
   - Restrict the "low-loss" language to the range where this figure is actually favourable.
   - Determine whether the sub-gap k is intrinsic (Cu²⁺ defects) or an artifact of surface roughness or depolarization in the pseudo-ε inversion (see 1, 4). Not distinguishing these leaves the loss claim undetermined either way.

4. **There is no independent, model-free check of Δn in the transparent region.** All the birefringence numbers come from reflection ellipsometry on as-grown, mechanically separated facets with no surface model.
   - The only cross-check, normal-incidence reflectance (Fig. 3c), probes ε_ab alone, covers only ≤ 4.13 eV, and is unpolarized.
   - Nothing independently checks n_c, which is the less certain component and sets Δn. The fairly low below-gap n_c ≈ 1.8–2.0 (Fig. 2a) is what one would expect if roughness or overlayers depress the pseudo-index.
   - **What to do.** Provide at least one independent measurement of Δn on the side facet, for example:
     - polarized reflectance on the side facet with E ∥ c and E ⊥ c, Kramers–Kronig- or model-checked against the ellipsometric ε_c and ε_ab;
     - transmission retardance through a thinned, polished side-cut plate at, e.g., 532 or 633 nm;
     - prism-coupling or interference-fringe data.

   Agreement at the 10% level in Δn at one or two visible wavelengths would substantially strengthen the central claim.

B. Should address

5. **The sample statistics and reproducibility are not stated.** The paper does not say how many crystals or facets were measured, whether the (001) and side-facet data come from the same crystal, or what the crystal-to-crystal spread in ε_ab, ε_c and the 3.78 eV peak is.
   - The authors attribute sub-gap absorption to a "reported ~1.4% Cu²⁺" concentration that was measured on other crystals (ref. 15), not on these.
   - **What to do.** State the number of crystals and facets. Show the spread of |Δn| at fixed wavelengths across at least two or three crystals. Characterize Cu²⁺/stoichiometry (e.g. EPR, XPS or chemical analysis) and the polytype (3R vs 2H, from Raman or XRD) on the measured crystals.

6. **The hyperbolic and ENZ claims need loss and accuracy qualifications.**
   - The lower "ENZ" crossing of ε_ab1 near 3.8 eV coincides with ε_ab2 ≈ 40–47 (Fig. 3a,b). |ε_ab| there is far from near-zero, so calling it an ENZ point is nominal.
   - Across the window, ε_ab1 is modest and negative while ε_ab2 is comparable (FOM ≈ 1.9 at best). This lossy, excitonic Reststrahlen-like response supports evanescent rather than well-propagating high-k modes.
   - The upper ENZ point (≈ 6.2 eV) sits close to the 6.46 eV limit of the measured range, where data quality and the Eq. 1 approximation are weakest.
   - ε_c1 in Fig. 3a appears to approach or dip near zero around 5.4–5.7 eV. If ε_c1 < 0 anywhere there, part of the claimed Type-II window would be doubly negative (metallic), not hyperbolic.
   - Fig. 3d (loss function) is computed from the same ε_ab, so it is not independent support.
   - The reflectance in Fig. 3c covers only the first ~0.3 eV of the 2.4 eV hyperbolic window.
   - **What to do.**
     - Show |ε| and ε2 at both ENZ points.
     - Show ε_c1 on an expanded scale with error bands over 5–6.5 eV.
     - Extend reflectance (polarized, both facets) into the deep UV if possible.
     - Describe the hyperbolic regime as lossy, in quantitative terms, in the abstract.

7. **The band-gap analysis is not reliable as presented.**
   - The "direct gap" of 3.71 eV (Fig. 2d inset) comes from a linear extrapolation of (αħω)² on the low-energy flank of a sharp peak at 3.78 eV that the authors themselves identify as excitonic. A Tauc analysis does not apply to an excitonic absorption line, so the extrapolated value has no clear meaning as a band gap. The continuum onset would lie above the exciton.
   - The text also cites an indirect gap of 3.45 eV (SI) and earlier experimental values that are lower.
   - **What to do.** Either fit the edge with an Elliott-type model (exciton plus continuum) to obtain the gap and the exciton binding energy, or present 3.71 eV as an absorption-onset energy rather than a band gap. Make the "wide bandgap" statements in the abstract consistent with the 3.45 eV indirect value and the sub-gap absorption.

8. **The microscopic attribution is qualitative and omits the excitonic physics that sets the magnitude.** The GGA/Wannier calculation identifies orbital characters and a selection rule, which is reasonable. But the size of the effect (ε_ab2 ≈ 47 and a sharp line) is excitonic, and GGA without electron–hole interaction cannot capture that.
   - The calculated ε_ab and ε_c are relegated to the SI and not compared quantitatively with experiment.
   - Spin–orbit coupling and any Hubbard U on Cu 3d are not stated.
   - The claim that the transition is "intra-atomic-like" Cu d→p is supported only by Bloch-function plots at a single k-point (L).
   - **What to do.**
     - Overlay the computed ε_ab and ε_c (scissor-shifted) on the measured spectra in the main text.
     - Report the computed ratio of in-plane to out-of-plane oscillator strength for the first transition.
     - Quantify the d_z²/p_x/y weights along the whole L–F segment that contributes.
     - Ideally, show a BSE calculation (or cite existing BSE ε_ab, ε_c with a direct comparison) for the excitonic enhancement.
     - Temper "providing a microscopic basis" accordingly.

9. **The beam footprint against facet size, and depolarization, are not reported.** The crystals are 1–2 mm long hexagonal pillars, and the side facets appear a few hundred µm wide (Fig. 1b, 100 µm scale bar). At θ = 70° the footprint is stretched by about 3×.
   - The paper does not say whether focusing probes were used, what the spot size was, or whether the beam overfilled the facet or hit edges and adjacent facets.
   - The depolarization spectra are not shown. Silver-epoxy mounting suppresses backside reflections from the mount but not scattering from facet edges.
   - **What to do.** Report spot size and focusing optics, and show depolarization against photon energy for both geometries. Confirm that measurements at a second angle give the same ε, a standard consistency test.

10. **The comparison in Fig. 2e is selective and somewhat inconsistent.**
    - The CuAlO2 curve and stars extend into the 340–370 nm region, where its own absorption is significant, while comparators are limited to their low-loss ranges.
    - The 1.55 eV gap cutoff excludes several known large-Δn crystals but includes MoS2, whose gap is close to the cutoff.
    - The "largest reported for natural anisotropic materials in the UV–visible" claims for |Δn|max = 3.67 and |Δk|max = 5.22 (p. 3) compare values taken at resonance with off-resonance values of other materials.
    - **What to do.** Use the same loss criterion for all materials, e.g. |Δn| only where max(k) < 0.1, or plot a loss-normalized FOM. State the selection rule explicitly and qualify the "largest" statements as at-resonance values.

C. Minor

11. **Reflectance normalization (p. 7, Fig. 3c).** The sample signal is divided by the Al-mirror signal, but the text does not say the ratio was multiplied by the known Al reflectance R_Al(λ). The "Al mirror artifact" near 1.5 eV is the Al interband feature, which suggests it was not. Correct for R_Al(λ), then state whether any scaling factor was applied in comparing measured and calculated R.

12. **Δε1 in Fig. 3e.** The largest Δε1 = |ε_ab1 − ε_c1| for CuAlO2 occurs around 3.7 eV, below the hyperbolic window and in the absorbing region. Plot Δε1 only within each material's hyperbolic window, or state clearly that the "largest Δε1" comparison (p. 5) includes non-hyperbolic energies.

13. **Fig. 3f readability.** The CuAlO2 FOM curve is hard to distinguish from the comparison curves, and the quoted maximum of 1.91 at 4.01 eV is not easy to locate. Use a log scale or annotate the maximum.

14. **Surface preparation.** State whether the facets were cleaned or polished, and how long after growth they were measured. Cu⁺ surfaces can oxidize to Cu²⁺/CuO, which would add sub-gap absorption.

15. **Fig. 1c schematic.** Labelling the E ∥ c transition "Forbidden" with a cross implies strictly zero absorption. Fig. 2b and Fig. 3b show finite k_c and ε_c2 across the range. Rephrase as "dipole-suppressed".

16. **Wording.**
    - "colossal" and "giant" are used interchangeably for Δn, and "natural oxide" versus "natural material" changes the scope of the claims. Use them consistently.
    - "n_ab,max = 5.66" should give the photon energy at which it occurs.
    - Ref. 49 is formatted as "science". Ref. 9 and ref. 29 carry 2026 dates that should be checked.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Replace the Eq. 1 pseudo-dielectric inversion with a multi-angle, two-orientation uniaxial model fit that includes surface roughness, and show uncertainty bands on ε_ab, ε_c, Δn, the ENZ energies and the FOM. This single change would address most of the concerns about reliability.
2. Add one independent measurement of Δn and k in the visible, such as polarized reflectance or transmission retardance on a side-cut plate, from at least two crystals.
3. Reframe the performance claims with an explicit loss metric: Δn together with k, and a hyperbolic FOM with ε2 at the ENZ points. State plainly which spectral ranges are genuinely low-loss.
4. Move the calculated anisotropic dielectric function into the main text with a quantitative comparison to experiment, and discuss the excitonic enhancement (via BSE or the literature) as the source of the magnitude of the anisotropy.
