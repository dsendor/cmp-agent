Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript combines cantilever torque magnetometry (up to 9 T, 2-90 K, three crystals) with broadband bolometric ESR (1-60 GHz, up to 1.5 T) on the CAMR ferrimagnet Mn3Si2Te6. It argues that the ground state is a coherently canted ferrimagnet stabilised by competing K1 < 0 and K2 > 0 anisotropy terms, and that a sixth-order term K3 is needed to explain why torque "switching markers" persist up to 9 T. It then uses the resulting K1-K2-K3 model to map field angle onto magnetization angle and replots published CAMR data against cos θ_M. A quantitative anisotropy energy for this material would be useful, and connecting it to CAMR is a sensible goal. However, the inferences from the operational torque metric θ_c, and the single-macrospin model behind them, are not yet established well enough to support the title claims.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Torque and ESR are used together on the same material: ESR constrains the curvature of the free energy near equilibrium, and torque tracks reorientation over a wide range of field and temperature.
- The torque data set is extensive: polar maps of d(τ/H)/dθ_H from 0.5 to 9 T and 2 to 85 K (Fig. 2a), with the key features reported in three crystals (Samples #1, #2 and #4).
- The ESR H//c dispersion (Fig. 3c) is concave-down and qualitatively unlike the near-linear hard-axis Kittel branch. This is an interesting observation in its own right.
- Replotting CAMR against the magnetization angle instead of the field angle (Fig. 4) is a physically well-motivated idea and gives a testable prediction (a field-independent near-plane slope of ln ρ versus cos θ_M).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The torque signature of canting is not shown to be unique to a canted state. On p. 3 the paper states that "in a strictly collinear ferrimagnet with a single uniaxial anisotropy, these extrema would remain pinned to the crystallographic axes." This is not generally true at finite field. For a single-domain uniaxial (Stoner-Wohlfarth-type) magnet below the anisotropy field, the magnetization lags the field, τ(θ_H) becomes sawtooth-like, and the extrema of dτ/dθ_H sit at field-dependent angles away from the principal axes. Fig. 1b itself shows this sawtooth shape at 0.8 T. The double-extremum structure around θ_H = 90° (Fig. 2b-d) and its separation 2θ_c are the central experimental evidence for canting, so the paper must show that they cannot arise from a K1-only (easy-plane) model at the same H/H_K, or from shape anisotropy, multi-domain reorientation or a small misalignment of the rotation plane. Action: overlay on Figs. 1c and 2b-d forward simulations of d(τ/H)/dθ_H computed with (i) K1 only, (ii) K1-K2 and (iii) K1-K2-K3, at the measured fields and with the measured M(T). Show where the extrema fall in each case.

2. The θ_c comparison that motivates K3 mixes quantities and conditions. In Fig. 3d the ESR-derived K1-K2 "θ_c" (orange) is a zero-field equilibrium angle, but it is compared with torque θ_c at 2, 5 and 9 T. Torque θ_c is defined operationally as half the separation of two derivative extrema (p. 4), and the paper never shows that it equals the equilibrium canting or cone angle. The text claims that the K1-K2 model predicts θ_c "collapses too rapidly with increasing temperature, particularly when the Zeeman energy approaches the quartic scale", but no K1-K2 prediction at 2, 5 or 9 T is shown. The K1-K2-K3 simulation (blue curves) is plotted on a separate right-hand axis labelled "Simulation of θ_c (a.u.)", so an angle is given in arbitrary units and the comparison with data is not quantitative. The dashed curve is also computed at 15 T, a field at which there are no data. The claimed agreement is with a non-monotonic temperature trend, but the simulated curve peaks near 30 K at roughly 20°, while the data peak at 16-19°, and the simulation is not on the same scale. Action: apply the same extrema-finding procedure to simulated torque derivatives. Plot the simulated θ_c(H, T) for the K1-K2 and K1-K2-K3 models on the same axis as the data at 2, 5 and 9 T, and give residuals. This would show whether K3 is required, and how much it improves the fit, at the stated fields.

3. The K1-K2-K3 parameters imply a large zero-field tilt that has to be reconciled with magnetization and neutron data. Minimising F = K1 sin²θ + K2 sin⁴θ + K3 sin⁶θ with the Fig. 3e values gives the following:
   - At 5 K (K1/M ≈ -2.4 T, K2/M ≈ 1.4 T, K3/M ≈ 0.9 T), sin²θ_M ≈ 0.56, so the moment sits about 42° out of the ab plane.
   - At 40 K (K1/M ≈ -1.4, K2/M ≈ 0.7, K3/M ≈ 1.1 T), the tilt is about 46°.
   - At 20-40 K, K1 + K2 + K3 ≥ 0, so the in-plane direction is no lower in energy than H//c.

   The K1-K2-only solution gives a tilt of about 22° from the plane, which is the ESR "θ_c" at 5 K. Adding K3 therefore roughly doubles the implied tilt, yet K1 and K2 are held fixed at their K3 = 0 ESR values (p. 5). Such a state should show (a) a large c-axis component in M(H) and a low-field c-axis susceptibility comparable to the in-plane one, and (b) a cone angle that the neutron study cited as [21] would have detected. Mn3Si2Te6 is usually described as an easy-plane magnet whose c axis is hard, and Fig. 1d shows a much larger in-plane response. The paper does not show M(H) along c or ab, although the Methods say SQUID data were taken. Action: show M(H) at 5, 20 and 40 K along both axes and compare them with the model's prediction. State the zero-field θ_M implied at each temperature and compare it quantitatively with the canting angle reported in [21]. Refit the ESR data with K3 included at the torque-derived value, so that the parameter set is self-consistent rather than built in stages.

4. The ESR evidence for canting rests on an H//c fit that returns an unphysical g-factor. The concave-down H//c dispersion is presented as the key ESR evidence for canting (pp. 4-5), but the same model gives g_c = 0.68 for this orientation. For Mn2+-based moments (g ≈ 2, quenched orbital moment) this means the model does not describe the H//c mode; it is not a nuisance parameter. The ESR window (≤ 1.5 T) is also well below the c-axis saturation field implied by the paper's own K1 (curvature at θ_M = 0 requires H > -2K1/M ≈ 4.8 T). For H//c the sample is therefore in a reoriented, probably multi-domain state, where domain modes, demagnetization and the two-sublattice ferrimagnetic (exchange) branch matter. A single-macrospin Smit-Beljers treatment may fail here, and the concave-down shape need not imply canting. Action: model the H//c data with a two-sublattice ferrimagnetic resonance treatment that includes demagnetization factors for the measured sample shape, or show that a collinear easy-plane model in the multi-domain regime cannot produce the concave-down branch at g ≈ 2. If g_c cannot be brought near 2, state this limitation clearly and do not use the H//c branch as support for canting.

B. Should address

5. "Canted ferrimagnetism" and "coherent" are not distinguished from an easy-cone macrospin state. The free energy (p. 4) describes one macrospin with an easy-cone anisotropy. The neutron result in [21] concerns non-collinear canting between Mn sublattices, which is a different object. In a trigonal crystal an easy cone is also degenerate (±θ_M and in azimuth), so domains are expected at low field, while "coherent" implies a single-domain, uniformly canted state. Nothing in the paper tests coherence. Examples of such tests would be the absence of hysteresis or Barkhausen-like features below 0.5 T, which Fig. S1 reportedly shows, or the scaling of torque amplitude with the expected single-domain value. Action: either adopt a two-sublattice model that produces sublattice canting and relate its parameters to K1-K3, or describe the state as an effective easy-cone / tilted-magnetization state. Explain what "coherent" means operationally and show the data that support it.

6. K1 and K2 at 20 and 40 K are weakly constrained, which undercuts the temperature trend. ESR at 20 and 40 K was measured only for H//ab (symbols in Fig. 3a). With a single orientation, K1 and K2 are strongly covariant. Accordingly, K2/M at 40 K is consistent with zero (Fig. 3e), and the ESR θ_c at 40 K is 4.7° with an error bar of roughly ±6° (Fig. 3d). The claim that K1-K2 "collapses too rapidly with temperature", and the crossing of K2 and K3, depend on these points. Action: measure ESR at θ_H = 80° and 0° at 20 and 40 K, or propagate the full covariance into the K3 determination and the θ_c prediction. Report how error bars on K1-K3 were obtained and the correlation coefficients.

7. Each parameter rests on few data. K3 is set by minimising the residual on θ_c(H) at one temperature, a few numbers per temperature, with K1 and K2 fixed from a different sample (Sample #3, ESR) than the torque sample (Sample #2). The full angular torque curves contain far more information. Action: fit the full τ(θ_H) or d(τ/H)/dθ_H curves at each H and T jointly with the ESR dispersions. Report goodness of fit with and without K3. If possible, calibrate the cantilever to give absolute torque, which would fix K/M independently of ESR and test consistency across samples.

8. The CAMR reparameterization uses a different sample at an unstated temperature, and parts of it are interpreted the wrong way round.
   - Fig. 4a is transport data from Seo et al. [9]: a different crystal, from a different growth, at a temperature not stated here. Fig. 4b does not say which temperature's K1-K3 set produced θ_M(θ_H).
   - Fig. 4b shows that near θ_H = 90° the slope dθ_M/dθ_H is well below 1; the 1 T curve is nearly flat. The whole 0-90° range in θ_H maps onto cos θ_M from about 0 to 0.23 (Fig. 4c), so the near-plane feature is compressed in θ_M, not "substantially broader" as stated on p. 6.
   - The collapse is shown using vertical offsets chosen freely for 2, 3 and 5 T. These offsets are needed because ρ0 = ρ(θ_H = 0) corresponds to a different θ_M at each field, so ln(ρ/ρ0) is not referenced to Δ(θ_M = 0) as the formula on p. 7 assumes.
   - The "weak hysteretic effect" correction to θ_M(θ_H) is not specified.

   Action: state the temperature of the transport data and use K1-K3 at that temperature. Fit the near-plane slope independently at each field and report uncertainties, rather than imposing the 1 T slope. Show the same replot using the K1-K2-only mapping, to test whether K3 is actually needed for the collapse. Describe the hysteresis correction. Translate the slope of -47.9 into the gap-modulation amplitude A (A ≈ 47.9 k_B T) and compare it with the SOC-induced gap changes calculated in [9]. Ideally, measure CAMR on a crystal from the same batch.

9. Torque-specific artifacts are not quantified. The analysis relies on the angular positions of derivative extrema accurate to a degree or so. The paper does not report:
   - the zero-angle calibration and the misalignment of the rotation plane relative to c, for the mechanical rotators (Samples #2 and #4) and the vector magnet (Sample #1);
   - the in-plane azimuth of the rotation axis ([100] versus [110]; Fig. 1a labels the axes [100], [001] and [110] in a way that does not match "θ_H measured from the c-axis");
   - the cantilever deflection, and hence the angle error, at 9 T for these strongly anisotropic crystals;
   - the empty-cantilever background and piezoresistor magnetoresistance.

   Out-of-plane misalignment, in particular, changes the torque near the hard axis. Action: give these numbers and show that θ_c is insensitive to them, for example by comparing θ_c across Samples #2 and #4 at matched T and H in the main text.

10. In-plane anisotropy and demagnetization are omitted from the model. The point group of Mn3Si2Te6 allows a six-fold in-plane term, which affects the H//ab zero-field ESR gap of about 10 GHz (Fig. 3a) and the torque, depending on the in-plane azimuth. Shape anisotropy of a plate-like crystal adds to K1. Action: state whether K1 includes demagnetization and give the sample dimensions for the ESR and torque crystals. Estimate the in-plane anisotropy, or justify neglecting it, given the observed zero-field gap.

11. Sample characterization is missing. Crystals are grown from a Mn-poor flux (1:2:6 molar ratio). In this compound, stoichiometry and Te vacancies are known to affect T_c and transport. No XRD, composition (EDS/WDS) or M_s data are given, yet parameters from four different crystals are combined. Action: provide structural and compositional characterization and M_s for the measured crystals, and show that T_c and torque features agree across Samples #1-#4.

C. Minor

12. The transition temperature is inconsistent. Fig. 1d gives T_c = 78 K, but the Fig. 2 caption says "Near T_c (70 K)", and the text treats 70 K as "near the transition" and 85 K as above it. Please use one value throughout.

13. The Fig. 1c caption says the low-temperature anomaly "emerges below 10 K", but Fig. 2 and Fig. 3d report θ_c up to 50-70 K. Please reconcile.

14. The notation for θ_c is inconsistent. The text (p. 4) uses θ_c1, θ_c2 and θ_c = (θ_c2 - θ_c1)/2; the Fig. 2 caption and panels use θ_1, θ_2 and 2θ_c.

15. Fig. 2e is a 3D colour map with unreadable axes and no numerical values. A 2D colour map, or line cuts with error bars, would be clearer. The torque θ_c points in Fig. 3d have no error bars; state how the extrema positions and their uncertainties were determined (smoothing, peak fitting).

16. Fig. 4c labels ln(ρ/ρ0) as "(a.u.)", although it is dimensionless. State the units of the slope and the temperature of the data in the caption.

17. On p. 3 the 85 K data are described with "the tendency of the magnitude ... is even reversed". Explain what is reversed and why, for example the paramagnetic torque sign.

18. The Fig. 1c polar plot shows a signed quantity on a radial axis, with negative values seemingly mapped to positive radius. Please clarify how negative values are displayed.

19. The ESR detection uses a resistive bolometer beneath the sample. Comment on microwave heating of the sample, and on whether the effective sample temperature differs from the bath temperature at the highest powers or frequencies.

20. The paper should cite and compare with Tanaka et al. [25] beyond its reported g_c, since that work also measures magnetic resonance in Mn3Si2Te6.

21. Language: "that governing high-field behavior" (Conclusion), "which already evident" (p. 7), "quantified" should be "quantifies" (p. 4), "Sample#2 and #4" should be "Samples #2 and #4", and "Mn3Si2Te6 as an exceptionally angle-sensitive" is missing a noun.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Build one forward model, starting from single-domain K1-only and K1-K2-K3 macrospin models and ideally including a two-sublattice version. Generate from it τ(θ_H), d(τ/H)/dθ_H, the ESR dispersions for all orientations, and M(H) along c and ab. Compare each output with data on common axes, so that the reader can see what each anisotropy term is required to explain.
2. Add M(H) along c and ab at 5, 20 and 40 K, and a quantitative comparison with the neutron canting angle. These are the most direct independent tests of the implied zero-field tilt.
3. Extend the ESR to θ_H = 80° and 0° at 20 and 40 K, and to higher fields if possible (above the c-axis reorientation), where K3 would affect the resonance curvature directly.
4. Measure CAMR on a crystal from the same growth batch and at a temperature where K1-K3 are known. Test the field-independent slope as a prediction with no free parameters, and include a K1-K2-only control mapping.
