Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript uses angle-resolved torque magnetometry (up to 9 T, 2–90 K) and broadband low-field ESR (1–60 GHz, up to 1.5 T) to build a phenomenological anisotropy free energy F = K1 sin²θM + K2 sin⁴θM + K3 sin⁶θM − MH cos(θH − θM) for the CAMR ferrimagnet Mn3Si2Te6. It then uses the resulting θM(θH) map to replot published CAMR data against cos θM. Quantifying how the magnetization rotates under tilted fields is a useful and timely goal for this material, because the transport literature has argued in terms of magnetization direction without a measured anisotropy energy. The three headline claims are (i) a "coherent canted ferrimagnetic" ground state, (ii) a sixth-order term K3 that is required to explain high-field torque, and (iii) a replot that collapses CAMR onto a field-independent linear trend in ln(ρ/ρ0) versus cos θM. All three currently rest on a single-macrospin model whose identification of observables with canting, and whose ESR implementation, are not adequately justified.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- It combines a static probe (torque, sensitive to dF/dθ) with a dynamic one (ESR, sensitive to curvature) on the same compound. That is the right strategy for constraining a higher-order anisotropy expansion.
- The torque-derivative data set is extensive (Fig. 2: 2–85 K, 0.5–9 T). It shows reproducible off-axis structure near the in-plane orientation, which is a real constraint on any model of this material.
- The broadband ESR maps (Fig. 3a–c) at three orientations, including a finite zero-field gap and a concave-down H∥c dispersion, are new experimental information for Mn3Si2Te6.
- Reframing CAMR in terms of θM rather than θH is a sensible and testable idea. Done quantitatively, it would help separate magnetic from electronic contributions to the angular transport.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The torque switching angle θc is not shown to measure magnetization canting, and "canted" is used for two different things.** (pp. 3–4, 6; Figs. 1c, 2, 3d; title and abstract)
   - *What is wrong.* θc is defined operationally as half the separation of two extrema of d(τ/H)/dθH near θH = 90° (p. 4; Fig. 2b–d caption). It is then read throughout as a canting angle of the equilibrium magnetization (Fig. 3d plots it in degrees against an ESR-"predicted" θc).
   - *Why the reading fails at high field.* For H much larger than the anisotropy fields (|K1/M| ≈ 2.4 T, while the data reach 9 T), M nearly follows H, and dτ/dθH → −d²F_anis/dθ² evaluated at θ ≈ θH. The positions of its extrema are then set only by the shape of the anisotropy function (the ratios K1:K2:K3), not by any tilt of the magnetization. So "switching markers persist up to 9 T" is the expected behaviour of any anisotropy whose second derivative has extrema off θ = 90°. It is not evidence that a canted state "survives" at 9 T, and calling it "sustains canting at high fields" in the abstract is not supported.
   - *Two meanings of canting.* In the macrospin model, a minimum of K1 sin² + K2 sin⁴ at intermediate θ (for K1/M = −2.40 T and K2/M = 1.40 T, sin²θ = −K1/2K2 ≈ 0.86, i.e. about 22° out of the plane) is an easy-cone state of the net moment. That is not the sublattice non-collinearity reported by neutron scattering (Ref. 21), yet the paper presents the two as the same thing ("consistent with a non-collinear canted state reported by neutron scattering", p. 3). A macrospin free energy cannot describe sublattice canting. A ferrimagnet with canted sublattices and a collinear ferrimagnet with an easy-cone anisotropy would give the same macrospin F.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Compute d(τ/H)/dθH directly from the fitted F at each measured field and temperature, overlay it on the Fig. 2b–d traces, and show that the extracted extrema reproduce θc(H, T).
     - State explicitly which θM tilt (if any) θc corresponds to at each field.
     - Either distinguish easy-cone macrospin tilt from sublattice canting throughout, or provide evidence that bears on the sublattice structure, such as a comparison with the canting angle and its temperature dependence in Ref. 21.

2. **The ESR analysis, which supplies K1/M and K2/M, is internally inconsistent as described.** (pp. 4–5; Fig. 3a–c, 3e)
   - *Zero-field gap.* The free energy contains no azimuthal (φ) dependence. In the Smit–Beljers formula (Ref. 24), ω = (γ/M sinθ)·[FθθFφφ − Fθφ²]^½. For a uniaxial F at zero field with the moment off the c axis, Fφφ = 0, so the uniform mode should be gapless (a Goldstone-like mode of the easy-cone or easy-plane state). The fitted dashed lines in Fig. 3a–c nevertheless reach finite frequencies at H = 0 (about 10 GHz for H∥ab and about 5 GHz for H∥c). That is impossible with the stated F unless in-plane anisotropy, a demagnetizing term, or an exchange (two-sublattice) mode is included.
   - *Orientation dependence at H = 0.* The zero-field frequency must be independent of the field orientation, because the state at H = 0 is the same, yet the fitted curves differ between panels.
   - *The H∥c g-factor.* g_c = 0.68 for H∥c (p. 5) is unphysical for Mn²⁺/Mn-based moments, where g ≈ 2 is expected. It signals that the single-mode macrospin model does not describe the H∥c data. Because H∥c is the only orientation far from the plane, treating g_c as "an effective parameter" (p. 5) means the remaining constraints come only from the near-plane geometries (90° and 80°). That undermines the claim that ESR "pins down K1/M and K2/M reliably" (p. 5).
   - *Missing ferrimagnetic modes.* A three-sublattice ferrimagnet (Mn1/Mn2) also has exchange modes. Whether the observed branch is the quasi-ferromagnetic mode needs to be stated.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Write the resonance condition actually used, including every term in F (in-plane, demagnetization) and the γ used, in the main text.
     - Refit with g fixed near 2 for all orientations, and report how K1/M and K2/M change and the fit quality.
     - Show the residuals and the K1–K2 covariance.
     - If a two-sublattice model is needed to reproduce H∥c, adopt it.

3. **The necessity of K3 is argued with a mismatched comparison and a free per-temperature fit.** (pp. 5–6; Fig. 3d, 3e)
   - *Mismatched comparison.* In Fig. 3d, the K1–K2 "prediction" (orange diamonds) is described as a zero-field limit, while the torque θc are measured at 2, 5 and 9 T. Showing that the two disagree does not demonstrate that K1–K2 fails at high field.
   - *Arbitrary units on the simulation.* The blue simulation curves are plotted on a separate axis in arbitrary units ("Simulation of θc (a.u.)"), so the K1–K2–K3 model is not compared quantitatively with the data either.
   - *Undefined simulation parameters.* The dashed curve is labelled "with B-field (15 T)", beyond the 9 T measured, and invokes an undefined "additional field-induced contribution". That contradicts the description of K3 as "field-independent" (p. 6).
   - *Free parameter per temperature.* K3/M is fitted separately at 5, 20 and 40 K to θc(H), with K1 and K2 held fixed. At 20 and 40 K, K1 and K2 come from ESR data apparently taken only for H∥ab (the symbols in Fig. 3a), which cannot constrain both parameters.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - At each temperature, plot measured θc(H) against forward-model θc(H) (computed as in item 1) for K1–K2 and for K1–K2–K3 on the same axis in degrees.
     - Give χ² or residuals and the number of data points per fitted parameter.
     - Test whether the K3 improvement survives when K1 and K2 are also allowed to vary within their ESR uncertainties.
     - Remove or justify the 15 T curve.

4. **The CAMR reparametrization is not an independent test and contains adjustable steps.** (pp. 6–7; Fig. 4)
   - *Provenance of the data.* The transport data are taken from Ref. 9, which used a different sample and an unstated temperature. Fig. 4b gives no temperature for the K values used either, even though Fig. 3e shows strong temperature dependence.
   - *Adjustable steps.* θM(θH) is "corrected" for hysteresis so that the maximum of ln(ρ/ρ0) falls at cos θM = 0 (pp. 6–7), which builds part of the desired result in. The collapse then uses a slope fitted only to 1 T plus a free vertical offset for each field.
   - *Narrow fitting window.* The near-plane linear window covers cos θM ≈ 0–0.2 at 1 T, where nearly any smooth function is linear. Mapping θH to θM near the plane amounts to a field-dependent stretch of the abscissa, so the improvement over the cos θH inset may be largely generic.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - State the transport temperature and use K values at that temperature.
     - Show the result without the hysteresis "correction".
     - Fit the slope independently at each field and report the values with uncertainties.
     - Compare against a null mapping, for example a K1-only model or a simple one-parameter rescaling of cos θH per field, to show that the measured K1–K2–K3 set gives a better collapse than a generic stretch.
     - Ideally, measure ρ(θH) on the same crystals or batch used for torque.
     - Check that the slope (−47.9, with units and the implied A/kBT) is physically reasonable.

**B. Should address**

5. **Parameters from different samples are combined, and the reproducibility evidence is not available.**
   - ESR is on Sample #3, the θc(H, T) map on Sample #2, and Fig. 1 on Sample #1 (0.8 T only). Reproducibility across #1, #2 and #4 is deferred to Supplementary Section 2, which was not provided with the submission, and Sample #4 appears nowhere in the main text.
   - Mn3Si2Te6 is known to be sensitive to stoichiometry and growth conditions, and all crystals come from one batch.
   - The authors should show θc(H) from at least two samples on the same axes, report the composition (EDS/WDS) and Tc of each sample, and comment on how sample-to-sample variation in K1/M, K2/M and Tc affects the combined fit.

6. **Torque artifacts and calibration are not discussed.** (pp. 3, 8; Figs. 1b, 2)
   - *Cantilever behaviour.* Piezoresistive cantilevers at 9 T with a strongly anisotropic sample can deflect enough to shift the true angle, and they can be nonlinear near the hard axis, where Fig. 1b shows abrupt torque jumps. Both directly affect the positions of derivative extrema.
   - *Angle calibration and rotation plane.* The angle offset and its calibration (how θH = 90° is located), the mechanical rotator's angular resolution, and the in-plane azimuth of the rotation plane are not given. The axis labels in Fig. 1a ([100] vertical, [001] and [110] in-plane) appear inconsistent with "c-axis".
   - *Cantilever background.* A background measurement of the bare cantilever is not mentioned.
   - *Extracting θc.* The method for locating extrema in a numerically differentiated signal, and the resulting uncertainty, are not described, and Fig. 3d has no error bars on θc.
   - *Absolute calibration.* Because τ is in arbitrary units, torque cannot independently check the magnitude of K; an absolutely calibrated torque would provide that cross-check.
   - The authors should describe angle calibration, background subtraction and deflection corrections, put error bars on θc, and show the hysteresis data (Fig. S1) in the main paper or SI.

7. **The choice of anisotropy terms ignores trigonal symmetry.** (pp. 4–5)
   - Mn3Si2Te6 is trigonal (P-31c). Besides uniaxial sin^2n θ terms, symmetry allows terms such as sin³θ cosθ cos3φ and sin⁶θ cos6φ. The first is antisymmetric about the plane for a given φ, so it can shift torque extrema away from θH = 90° and 270°, which is the very signature used for "canting" (Fig. 1c).
   - Calling K3 sin⁶θ "the simplest symmetry-allowed extension" is therefore incomplete.
   - The authors should specify the in-plane direction of the rotation plane, check whether θc depends on azimuth (one additional rotation plane would suffice), and justify neglecting the trigonal terms.

8. **The field and temperature evolution of the anomaly is inconsistent between figures.**
   - At 0.8 T, the anomaly "emerges below 10 K" and the polar plot is "predominantly two-fold" above 20 K (Fig. 1c, p. 3). In Fig. 2 and Fig. 3d, however, θc ≈ 12–19° persists at 20–50 K at 2–9 T, and is non-monotonic in T with a maximum near 30 K.
   - The authors should explain why a low-field signature that disappears above 10 K becomes robust at higher field and higher temperature, show θc at 0.5–1 T across temperature, and discuss whether the 30 K maximum reflects a physical change (for example a change in the sign of the effective anisotropy) or an extraction artifact.

9. **Magnetization data that would independently test the model are not shown.** (p. 8; Fig. 1d)
   - SQUID measurements were performed but only dχ/dT is shown. M(H) for H∥c and H∥ab at 5, 20 and 40 K would provide the hard-axis saturation field and the shape of the magnetization curve.
   - From the fitted values, the H∥c instability field is about −2K1/M ≈ 4.8 T at 5 K. This prediction is easily tested and also bears on Fig. 4b and 4c, where the 5 T data reach only cos θM ≈ 0.6 at θH = 0.
   - M(T) is also needed to convert the anisotropy fields K/M into energies and to judge their temperature dependence.
   - Demagnetization (shape anisotropy) of the platelets, which is not negligible relative to anisotropy fields of a few tesla, should be estimated.

10. **"Coherent" is asserted without evidence of single-domain behaviour.** (title, abstract, p. 6)
    - An easy-cone state is degenerate in φ and in ±θ, so domains are expected at low field. The hysteresis below 0.5 T noted on p. 4 and the abrupt torque changes near the hard axis (Fig. 1b) suggest domain or switching processes.
    - The authors should define "coherent" and support it, for example by showing that the torque traces are reversible above some field or that the ESR line is a single uniform mode, or else remove the term.

12. The switching-angle notation differs between the text (θc1, θc2, θc = (θc2 − θc1)/2) and the Fig. 2 caption (θ1, θ2, 2θc ≡ θ2 − θ1). Please unify.
13. Fig. 2e (3D colour map) is unreadable: the axis labels and tick values cannot be resolved. A 2D colour map with labelled axes would be clearer.
14. In Fig. 3d, put the simulation on the same θc axis in degrees, explain the 15 T label, and add error bars to the torque points.
15. Fig. 3e: the marker colour and label assignments for K2/M and K3/M (red and blue) should match the text and caption. State how the error bars at 40 K (which span zero for K2/M) were obtained.
16. Give the literature g_c value from Ref. 25 explicitly.
17. Fig. 4a reproduces data from Ref. 9. State the temperature, the current direction relative to the rotation plane, and whether permission or digitization was involved. Give units for the slope −47.9.
18. The ESR methods omit the sample dimensions, microwave power, and any check for bolometric heating. The temperature of the ESR fits (5 K) differs from the torque base temperature (2 K).
19. Supplementary Information is cited repeatedly (Sections 2, 4, 5, 6; Fig. S1) but was not included.
20. Language: for example "the near in-plane stiffness that governing high-field behavior" (p. 7), "which already evident" (p. 7), "exceptionally angle-sensitive with" (p. 2), and "This replot collapses the near the in-plane limit" (p. 7).

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Make the model-to-data comparison fully forward. Simulate d(τ/H)/dθH(θH; H, T) from a single parameter set and overlay it on the raw torque traces. This would turn θc from an operational metric into a quantitative test and clarify what it means physically.
2. Upgrade the ESR analysis to a two-sublattice (or at least φ-dependent) model that reproduces the zero-field gap and the H∥c branch with a physical g-factor. Extend measurements to higher fields or frequencies, where K3 would become visible in the dispersion.
3. Add M(H) curves, at least one additional rotation plane (a different azimuth), and a second torque sample. Together these would test the anisotropy hierarchy and the symmetry assumptions independently.
4. Measure angular transport on crystals from the same batch, at a temperature where K1, K2 and K3 are known. Test the θM collapse against a null mapping so that it becomes a real prediction rather than a reparametrization.
