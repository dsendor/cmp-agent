Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript combines cantilever torque magnetometry (up to 9 T, 2–90 K) and broadband low-field ESR (1–60 GHz, up to 1.5 T) on Mn3Si2Te6. It argues that the low-temperature state is a "coherent canted ferrimagnet" described by a macrospin free energy F = K1 sin²θM + K2 sin⁴θM + K3 sin⁶θM − MH cos(θH − θM). In this model, K1 < 0 and K2 > 0 come from ESR, and a sixth-order K3 > 0 is required by torque "switching angles" θc that persist to 9 T. The resulting θM(θH) map is then used to replot the CAMR data of Seo et al. (Ref. 9) as ln(ρ/ρ0) versus cos θM, where the near-plane slope is roughly field-independent. Quantifying how the magnetization rotates under tilted fields would be a useful step towards understanding CAMR in this compound, and the ESR+torque combination is a sensible route. In its present form, though, the key steps (from torque extrema to canting, from ESR to K1/K2, from the θc mismatch to K3, and from the mapping to transport) rest on a single-macrospin model whose assumptions are not tested against the most direct available data, such as M(H) curves and full torque-curve fits. The transport comparison also uses data from different samples at an unstated temperature.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The experimental question matters for CAMR, and the data set spans a wide range: torque from 0.5–9 T and 2–90 K on several crystals, plus multi-orientation ESR field–frequency maps.
- The low-temperature distortion of the torque derivative away from the high-symmetry directions (Figs. 1c, 2a–d) is a clear, reproducible-looking observation, and it supports a non-trivial anisotropy landscape.
- The H//c ESR dispersion (Fig. 3c) does not show the near-linear hard-axis Kittel behaviour. This is interesting in itself.
- Re-expressing angular transport in terms of the magnetization angle rather than the field angle is a physically well-motivated idea that could be valuable if done quantitatively.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **"Canted ferrimagnet" (sublattice non-collinearity) is not distinguished from a tilted collinear macrospin (easy-cone state).** The model on p. 4 is a single-macrospin free energy. With the reported K1/M = −2.40 T and K2/M = 1.40 T, its zero-field minimum lies at sin²θM = −K1/(2K2) ≈ 0.86, i.e. θM ≈ 68°. That is an easy-cone state with the net moment about 22° out of the plane, which matches the 22° ESR-derived θc at 5 K in Fig. 3d. That is a statement about the orientation of the net moment. It says nothing about non-collinearity between the Mn1 and Mn2 sublattices, which is what "canted ferrimagnetism" usually means and what neutron scattering (Ref. 21) addresses. The title, abstract and conclusions slide between the two meanings and cite Ref. 21 as support. Two things are needed:
   - (a) state explicitly which canting is meant;
   - (b) either show that the easy-cone angle agrees quantitatively with the neutron-determined moment direction, or use a two-sublattice model (with intersublattice exchange and sublattice-specific anisotropy) to show that the data require sublattice non-collinearity.
   
   The ESR is also treated with a single (quasi-ferromagnetic) mode. A two-sublattice ferrimagnet has an exchange mode as well, and its absence or presence in the 1–60 GHz window should be discussed.

2. **"Coherent" (single-domain) behaviour is assumed, not demonstrated.** An easy-cone state is degenerate under cos θM → −cos θM and around the cone, so multi-domain configurations are expected at low field. This matters most for Sample #1 at 0.8 T (Fig. 1b,c), where the "purely torque-based indication" of canting is extracted. Domain averaging, domain-wall pinning and hysteresis can all shift extrema in d(τ/H)/dθH. The hysteresis is acknowledged (Fig. S1, and the "weak hysteretic effect" in θM(θH) on p. 6–7), and Fig. 4b shows a jump at θH = 180°. The authors should:
   - show forward- and reverse-rotation data in the main text at low and high field;
   - show that the extrema positions are independent of sweep direction and field history;
   - justify the word "coherent", or drop it.

3. **The model is never tested against the most direct observables: M(H) curves and full torque curves.** SQUID magnetometry was done along both in-plane and out-of-plane directions (Sec. B), but only dχ/dT is shown (Fig. 1d). The K1–K2–K3 set predicts M(H) for H//ab and H//c, including the hard-axis saturation field and its temperature dependence. This is the most direct and model-independent check of the anisotropy constants, and it should be shown together with the model curves.

   Likewise, the torque analysis compresses each τ(θH) curve into one number, θc, taken from derivative extrema. The authors should fit or simulate whole τ(θH) and d(τ/H)/dθH curves at several fields and temperatures with the final parameter set and overlay them on the data of Fig. 1b and Fig. 2. Without this, it is not shown that the model reproduces the torque data beyond a single operational metric. Converting torque to absolute units (cantilever calibration plus sample mass) would give an independent amplitude constraint on K1 and M.

4. **The comparison establishing the need for K3 (Fig. 3d) is not quantitative as presented.** Four problems:
   - The simulated θc curves are plotted on a separate right-hand axis labelled "Simulation of θc (a.u.)". A forward model that sets K3 should predict θc in degrees on the same axis as the data.
   - The dashed curve is labelled "with B-field (15 T)", but the measurements go only to 9 T. The "additional field-induced contribution" is not defined anywhere in the main text.
   - It is not stated how the K1–K2 "ESR" diamonds were computed: zero-field cone angle or simulated derivative extrema at 2/5/9 T. The text says "zero-field limit". If so, a zero-field cone angle is being compared with field-angle separations of derivative extrema at 2–9 T, which are different quantities.
   - The operational θc = (θc2 − θc1)/2 is measured in θH space. It equals neither the equilibrium canting angle nor θM, and no model mapping between them is given.
   
   The authors should compute, with the K1–K2 model and with the K1–K2–K3 model, the predicted extrema of d(τ/H)/dθH at 2, 5 and 9 T at each temperature, and plot them against the measured θc on a common axis with residuals. Only then is the claim that "torque requires a substantial sixth-order term" demonstrated. As drawn, the solid and dashed simulations do not follow the measured non-monotonic T-dependence (for example the 9 T data dip at 10 K and peak at 30 K) closely enough to support the statement on p. 6 that the model "supports the non-monotonic temperature dependence".

5. **The ESR fit gives an unphysical g-factor for H//c, and the model is then accepted anyway.** The global fit on p. 5 gives g_a = 2.01 but g_c = 0.68. For Mn²⁺/Mn-based moments with small orbital contribution, g ≈ 2 is expected, and Ref. 25 reports a value close to that. A factor-of-three error in g along c means the model fails to describe the very data set that is cited as the key evidence for canting (the H//c dispersion in Fig. 3c). The manuscript states that this "does not affect the main conclusion", but K1/M and K2/M are taken from this same global fit, so their reliability is in question. Needed:
   - a fit with g_c fixed near 2, showing what K1, K2 (and K3) become and the resulting residuals;
   - an explicit treatment of demagnetization (thin-plate shape anisotropy adds an effective uniaxial term of order μ0Ms that enters K1 directly) and of misalignment;
   - a discussion of whether a second (exchange) mode or mode mixing is responsible.

6. **The transport analysis (Fig. 4) mixes samples and conditions, and the "collapse" is imposed rather than tested.** Several issues need fixing:
   - (i) The resistivity is taken from Seo et al. (Ref. 9), on different crystals, presumably from a different growth. The temperature of those data is not stated anywhere in the paper or the Fig. 4 caption, nor is the temperature of the anisotropy parameters used for Fig. 4b. Activated transport makes the slope ∝ A/kBT, so the temperature is essential.
   - (ii) The slope (−47.9) is fitted to the 1 T curve only, and the 2, 3 and 5 T curves are "vertically shifted" by hand. The offsets are large (ln(ρ/ρ0) maxima from ~9 to ~19) and are not explained. Field-independence should be tested by fitting each field separately and reporting the slopes with uncertainties. In the figure, the 5 T data bend away from the line over much of the range.
   - (iii) ρ0 ≡ ρ(θH = 0°) is identified with Δ(θM = 0°), but Fig. 4b shows that at 1–2 T the magnetization is far from θM = 0 when H//c. The cos θM range in Fig. 4c reaches only ~0.2 at 1 T. The normalization is therefore field-dependent in a way that the activation-gap expression on p. 7 does not account for.
   - (iv) The "hysteretic correction" that forces the maximum of ln(ρ/ρ0) to sit at cos θM = 0 is an ad hoc constraint that partly builds in the result. It should be described quantitatively, and the uncorrected mapping shown.
   - (v) Several 1 T and 2 T points sit at cos θM < 0. This needs explanation.
   
   Ideally, transport should be measured on a crystal from the same batch as the torque/ESR samples, at the same temperature. At minimum, the manuscript should present the comparison as qualitative and state the assumed temperature and sample differences.

**B. Should address**

7. **K3(T) is determined from very few constraints.** K3/M is extracted at only three temperatures (5, 20, 40 K) by fitting θc(H) with K1 and K2 fixed. At 20 K and 40 K, the ESR constraint on K1 and K2 comes only from H//ab points (Fig. 3a overlay), with no tilted or H//c data. In Fig. 3e, K1/M at 40 K has error bars of roughly ±1.2 T, and K2/M has error bars that cross zero. In addition, the 20 K and 40 K ESR points in Fig. 3a nearly overlap the 5 K dispersion, while K1/M is claimed to change from −2.4 to −1.4 T. Please:
   - give the covariance matrix of (K1, K2, K3) at each temperature and the number of data points used;
   - explain how K1 can change by ~40% while the H//ab resonance hardly moves;
   - say whether K3 > 0 survives within the uncertainties when K1 and K2 are allowed to vary within their errors.

8. **The symmetry analysis of the higher-order terms is incomplete.** Mn3Si2Te6 is trigonal (P-31c). At sixth order, the allowed terms include both sin⁶θ and the in-plane hexagonal term sin⁶θ cos 6φ. The rotation plane is described only as "an in-plane direction". In Fig. 1a the axes are labelled [100], [001] and [110], with [100] apparently drawn along c, which is confusing. The authors should:
   - specify the in-plane azimuth φ of the rotation plane for each sample;
   - discuss whether the cos 6φ term (or an in-plane anisotropy measured by in-plane torque rotation) could mimic or contribute to the "K3" stiffness;
   - justify omitting K2/K3 terms that depend on φ.

9. **Alternative explanations for derivative extrema persisting near the plane at 9 T are not excluded.** These include:
   - small misalignment of the rotation plane relative to c;
   - torque-induced cantilever deflection, which changes the true sample angle when torque is large, especially for an anisotropic ferrimagnet at 9 T;
   - piezoresistor nonlinearity;
   - a paramagnetic or field-induced magnetization component (the ferrimagnet is not saturated, and M itself depends on H and θH, while the model holds M fixed);
   - the temperature- and field-dependent M(H) entering K/M.
   
   The text alludes to a "field-induced contribution" (Fig. 3d) but does not develop it. A minimal check is to show that the extrema positions do not depend on cantilever orientation or sample size (Samples #2 versus #4), and to include M(H, θH) from SQUID in the model.

10. **Reproducibility across samples is asserted but not shown in the main text.** Each technique uses a different crystal: #1 for 0.8 T vector-magnet torque, #2 for CFMS torque, #3 for ESR, and #4 for PPMS torque. The ESR-derived parameters (Sample #3) are applied to torque on Sample #2 and to transport from a different study. At least one overlay of θc(T, H) for Samples #2 and #4, and ideally torque on Sample #3, should appear in the main text with the device-to-device spread quantified, since the K3 extraction depends on combining samples.

11. **Some temperature claims are inconsistent with the figures.** Fig. 1d gives Tc = 78 K. The Fig. 2 caption says "Near Tc (70 K)", and the text on p. 3 calls 70 K "near the transition" and 85 K "above the transition". The Fig. 1c caption says the anomaly "emerges below 10 K", while Fig. 2a and Fig. 3d show the θc features to 50–70 K. Clarify the onset temperature of the canting anomaly and how it relates to Tc. Also explain the "reversed tendency" of the derivative magnitude at 85 K, which may reflect a background or paramagnetic contribution that also affects lower-temperature data.

12. **The ESR data quality and fit window are limited.** In Fig. 3c (H//c), the absorption is visible mainly for |H| < ~0.2 T and near the highest frequencies. The fitted dispersion between these regions is not constrained by visible signal. Field-swept spectra at fixed frequencies, with resonance positions extracted and plotted as points with uncertainties, would make clear which data points drive the "concave-down" conclusion and the K1/K2 values. The zero-field gap (~10 GHz for H//ab in Fig. 3a) is itself a strong constraint on the cone state and should be compared explicitly with the model's prediction.

**C. Minor**

13. The switching-angle definition is labelled θc1, θc2 in the text but θ1, θ2 in the Fig. 2 caption. Use one notation.
14. Anisotropy "fields" are quoted as K/M in tesla. The conventional anisotropy field is 2K/M (or μ0-scaled), so state the convention, and give K in energy density using the measured Ms.
15. Fig. 2e is a 3D surface with unreadable axis labels. A 2D colour map or line plots with error bars would be clearer, and should state the uncertainty in θc from the extremum picking.
16. Fig. 4c's y-axis is labelled "(a.u.)" for a dimensionless log ratio. If vertical offsets are applied, say so on the axis.
17. Fig. 1b and Fig. 2 torque amplitudes are in arbitrary units. State whether curves at different temperatures and fields share a common scale.
18. The Experimental setup does not mention the field and angle resolution or the method of angle calibration for each setup (vector magnet versus rotator), which matters for extrema positions at the degree level.
19. Supplementary Information sections are cited throughout (S1, Sections 2, 4, 5, 6) but were not included with this submission, so the global fit procedure and simulation details cannot be checked.
20. There are typos and wording problems: "that quantified", "near in-plane stiffness that governing", "which already evident", "C.W.C..", and "maintext".

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Show M(H) along c and ab at several temperatures, with predictions from the final K1–K2–K3 parameters, and fit full torque curves rather than only θc. Together these would turn the anisotropy hierarchy from plausible to demonstrated.
2. Plot model-predicted derivative extrema in degrees on the same axis as the measured θc(T, H) for both the K1–K2 and K1–K2–K3 models, with uncertainty bands propagated from the fit covariance.
3. Redo the ESR fit with physically constrained g-factors, include demagnetization, and consider a two-sublattice model so that "canted ferrimagnet" can be separated from a tilted-macrospin (easy-cone) description.
4. Measure CAMR on a crystal from the same batch at the temperature where K1–K3 are known, fit the slope of ln ρ versus cos θM independently at each field, and report whether the slopes agree within error.
