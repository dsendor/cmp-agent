## A. Must address

1. **Laser-assisted photoemission (LAPE) from the Y-point valence band may produce the early Y-valley "conduction band" signal under AC pumping.** The conduction-band window in Fig. 1(a) is E − E_VBM = 1.15 ± 0.18 eV. This is almost exactly where the first pump sideband of the valence-band maximum appears (VBM + 1.2 eV), and the VBM sits at Y (Fig. 1b). An s-polarized pump still has an in-plane field that dresses photoelectrons with in-plane momentum, and the sideband amplitude scales roughly as E·k∥. It is therefore largest at Y when the field lies along AC and vanishes at Y when the field lies along ZZ. A LAPE replica of the Y-point VBM would thus appear only under AC pumping and only near temporal overlap. That is exactly the behaviour assigned to optical injection into the Y valleys (Fig. 2b, 2f): the Y intensity peaks at about 0 fs and decays within about 200–300 fs, only about twice the 135 fs pump duration convolved with the probe. Rotating the crystal (p. 6) removes the s/p asymmetry but not this E·k selectivity, and the paper raises LAPE only to justify not using p-polarization. The X-valley signals near t = 0 should also be shown to be free of sidebands. Because the "initial positive valley polarization followed by reversal" (Fig. 2h) rests on this early Y signal, the authors should: (i) show energy–momentum cuts through Y at t ≈ 0 and compare the dispersion with a valence-band replica shifted by 1.2 eV (a sideband reproduces the hole-like valence dispersion; real conduction electrons are electron-like); (ii) check the symmetric sideband at VBM − 1.2 eV, which cannot contain conduction-band population, and compare its time trace with the Y-valley trace; (iii) compare the Y-valley rise and decay with the measured pump–probe cross-correlation (for example a LAPE replica of a non-resonant feature or a metal reference); (iv) show the fluence dependence of the Y signal at t = 0, since the sideband scales linearly with pump intensity and the population differently; (v) analyze negative delays, where a pump-dressed signal should be symmetric about t₀. A pump polarized at 45° to the axes, or a lower pump photon energy, would give a further control. Without this, the measured Y-valley lifetime and the reversal are not established.

2. **The valley polarization P is not defined precisely, and as plotted it may reflect normalization rather than population.** Figs. 2(e,f) show "Norm. Int." for the X and Y valleys, and in Fig. 2(f) both experimental traces peak at ≈1, which suggests each was normalized to its own maximum. P is described only as "the normalized difference between intensity within CB X and Y valleys", and the sign convention must be inferred from Fig. 2(g) (≈ −1 when X is populated, so presumably P = (I_Y − I_X)/(I_Y + I_X)). If P was computed from separately normalized traces, its value at every delay, including the zero crossing and the reversal to ≈ −0.8 in Fig. 2(h), is an artefact of the normalization and not a population ratio. The same question applies to the TDBE curves: is P computed from absolute populations or from curves rescaled to the experiment? The headline numbers ("nearly unity", "reversal", "excellent agreement") all rest on P. The authors should state the formula and sign convention explicitly, state whether raw, background-subtracted counts in matched k-space and energy volumes were used, show the unnormalized per-valley X and Y intensities for both pump geometries on a common scale, and compute P for experiment and theory in the same way.

3. **Photoemission intensity is used as a proxy for valley population without correcting for matrix elements or probe geometry, which also differs between the two pump configurations.** The X and Y conduction states have different orbital character (Sn p_x-like vs p_y-like, p. 14). Switching from ZZ to AC pumping rotates the crystal by 90° under a fixed 65°-incidence XUV beam, so the XUV polarization and plane of incidence relative to those orbitals change, and the X and Y cross-sections differ from each other and between configurations. Fig. 2 itself shows that intensity is not simply proportional to population: in Fig. 2(b,d) the X valley at k_ZZ ≈ +1.1 Å⁻¹ is much brighter than the one at −1.1 Å⁻¹, and in Fig. 2(a,c) the valleys at ±1.1–1.15 Å⁻¹ differ strongly from those at ±0.4–0.45 Å⁻¹. Which replicas were integrated is not stated, and the momentum scales do not obviously match: Fig. 1(c) places X and Y at ≈0.35–0.37 Å⁻¹. In addition, bulk SnSe is probed at a single photon energy (21.6 eV), so X and Y may be sampled at different, unspecified k_z, while the TDBE is a k_z-averaged bulk calculation. A valley-dependent cross-section rescales I_X/I_Y and moves the apparent zero crossing and asymptote of P; a geometry-dependent one makes the ZZ-vs-AC comparison inexact. A measured value near ±1 therefore cannot yet be read as "nearly unity" population polarization. The authors should calibrate the relative X/Y photoemission efficiency in each geometry (for example from late-delay data once both configurations reach the same quasi-equilibrium distribution, from ground-state valence bands at both azimuths, from equivalent valleys in several zones, from calculated cross-sections, or by measuring both pump polarizations at fixed crystal orientation), or apply the same matrix-element-weighted integration to the TDBE output. They should report P separately for each symmetry-equivalent valley, explain the zone replicas and mark zone boundaries in Fig. 2, give the k_z probed, and show how the P curves change under reasonable variations of the relative X/Y scale.

4. **The TDBE is run at a different excitation condition and on a band structure whose key parameter is unvalidated, so the claim that it "fully captures" the dynamics is not established.** The simulation pumps at 0.8 eV, resonant with the PBE gap, whereas the experiment uses 1.2 eV (pp. 5, 15, 17). The excess energies in the X and Y valleys therefore differ between theory and experiment and between the two valleys, and the experimental excess energy is never stated. The authors say detuning is "not critical" (p. 5), but Fig. S2(d) shows that only 50–100 meV of detuning shifts the zero crossing of the AC polarization by ≈100 fs, comparable to the timescale compared in Fig. 2(h); the real excess energy may be several hundred meV, beyond the simulated range. The statement that detuning "up to ∼300 meV … still leads to near-unity initial valley polarization" (p. 23) does not appear to be supported by Fig. S1(c), where the ZZ curve rises well above −1 by 1.1 eV, and the p. 5 wording citing S1–S2 as showing insensitivity should match the figures. The direction and rate of Y→X scattering (and thermal back-scattering at 300 K) depend on the X–Y conduction-band offset relative to k_BT and the ≈20 meV phonon energy. This offset comes from PBE, apparently without spin–orbit coupling or van der Waals correction and with lattice constants (4.226, 4.547, 11.830 Å) about 2–3 % larger than experiment, especially a₃; it is not reported, although the trARPES data could measure it. It is also unclear how "the same intensity" maps onto carrier density when absorption at 0.8 eV (DFT) and 1.2 eV (experiment) differs. The agreement with the Δ = 0 run may therefore be partly fortuitous. The authors should report the measured and calculated X–Y offset, direct gaps and experimental excess energy; run the TDBE with a scissor-corrected band structure or a detuning matched to the experimental excess energy in each valley (~0.3–0.4 eV), or show insensitivity across that full range; correct the S1/S2 statements; and revise "fully captured" and "excellent agreement" accordingly.

## B. Should address

5. **The integration regions and background subtraction for the X- and Y-valley traces are not described.** The paper does not state the energy and momentum windows integrated for each valley, whether they enclose the same phase-space volume, whether hot electrons above the valley minimum are included, or how background (pre-time-zero signal, pump-only multiphoton emission, space charge, pump-induced secondary electrons) was subtracted. The CB contour in Fig. 1(a) uses a ±0.18 eV window, but the Fig. 2 traces are not described, and it is not stated whether the per-panel colour normalization of Figs. 2(a–d) carried into the traces. The early AC map (Fig. 2b) has a high, structured background; integration windows that are too wide would mix it in and bias P toward zero. These choices directly set P and should be stated and varied as a robustness check.

6. **The late-time X-valley disagreement after AC pumping is attributed to an implausible cause, and it undermines the claimed agreement in P.** Between 200 and 600 fs the simulated X population keeps rising while the measured X signal peaks at ≈200 fs and then decays (Fig. 2f). The authors attribute this to electron–hole recombination missing from the model (p. 10). Sub-picosecond recombination in an indirect-gap bulk semiconductor at room temperature needs justification, and the flat X signal under ZZ pumping (Fig. 2e) argues against it. More likely causes are hot electrons cooling below the lower edge of the 1.15 ± 0.18 eV window, carriers diffusing out of the nm-scale probing depth into the bulk (the TDBE is homogeneous), surface or defect trapping, scattering to other valleys or bands, k_z selection at 21.6 eV, or the normalization issue in concern 2. Because P is a ratio of populations that disagree by about a factor of two at 600 fs, the "excellent agreement" in Fig. 2(h) may come from compensating errors or from how each curve is normalized. The authors should show energy-resolved (E vs t) traces for each valley, the X signal under both pumps on a common scale and over longer delays, and the signal integrated over the whole conduction band; discuss these alternatives; and replace "excellent agreement" with a quantitative comparison.

7. **The attribution to the ≈20 meV second-highest optical branch rests on theory alone, uses an indirect proxy, and is stated too strongly.** The mode identification (Fig. 3) comes from nonequilibrium phonon "temperatures" 600 fs after the pump. This proxy depends on each mode's heat capacity, phase-space weight and other heating channels, and ΔT = T^AC − T^ZZ also mixes in intravalley cooling of hot carriers, which differs between configurations because the excess energies differ. It also mixes electron and hole contributions: the text says that under ZZ pumping "only optical modes around Γ are populated, since only intravalley scattering is possible" (p. 13), but Fig. S3(c,e) shows strong X→Y hole intervalley scattering under ZZ pumping, so Fig. 3(c) compares electron intervalley scattering (AC) against hole intervalley scattering (ZZ). Further points: from Fig. 1(c), q = k_X − k_Y ≈ (0.37, −0.30) Å⁻¹, not exactly along Γ–M, yet the analysis is restricted to Γ–M and the emitted phonons are spread along it rather than localized at that q; Fig. 3(c) shows the largest ΔT at or near Γ, which looks intravalley; several other optical modes also show positive ΔT; and the glide-symmetry argument (pp. 13–14, S4) only excludes modes odd under M̄_xy and does not single out the second-highest branch. "Unambiguously identify" (p. 13) should be softened and the result stated as a theoretical attribution. The authors should report branch-resolved Y→X scattering rates (|g|² weighted by phase space) at the actual momentum transfer, with a 2D q-resolved map, separate electron and hole contributions, and test whether switching off the identified branch removes the reversal. An experimental test would substantially strengthen the claim, such as the temperature dependence of the AC decay time (phonon occupation n_q vs n_q + 1, e.g. 80 K vs 300 K) or a coherent-phonon or diffraction signature of the predicted mode.

8. **Time resolution, time zero and quantitative timescales are missing.** The XUV pulse duration, the pump–probe cross-correlation, the delay step and the method used to set t₀ are not given, nor how experimental and simulated time zero were aligned. In Fig. 2(e) the measured X rise is visibly delayed relative to the TDBE, which may reflect a t₀ offset, and the paper suggests that X population appears "during the pump pulse". The "sub-picosecond" decay and reversal are not quantified by any fit. P is plotted at negative delays where both intensities are at background and P is undefined (Fig. 2h: ≈ +1 near −200 fs for experiment, and the TDBE curve starts at +1 before the pump arrives). The authors should report the instrument response and t₀ determination, fit both data and simulations with a rate model convolved with the cross-correlation, give τ(Y→X), the X rise time and the AC zero-crossing time with uncertainties, mask P where the signal is below background, and add error bars (counting statistics or repeated scans) to Fig. 2(e–h).

9. **Excitation density, fluence and heating are not characterized.** The pump fluence is 0.9 mJ/cm² at 1.2 eV, which in SnSe likely corresponds to ~10²⁰ cm⁻³ or more excited carriers, but neither the absorbed fluence nor the density is given and only one fluence is shown. At such densities electron–electron and electron–hole scattering, screening of the electron–phonon coupling, band-gap renormalization (which could change the X–Y offset) and hot-phonon effects can all change intervalley rates; none is in the TDBE, which has only electron–phonon collisions plus a phenomenological dephasing time, so the claim that phonon scattering is "the main driver" requires excluding them. Two-photon absorption (2 × 1.2 eV), cumulative lattice heating at 166 kHz and space charge are also not addressed. Because SnSe is optically anisotropic, s-polarized light at 65° incidence along AC and along ZZ is reflected and absorbed differently, so the two configurations may not have matched carrier densities or depths, whereas the TDBE assumes "the same pump pulse ... intensity as in the experiments"; it is also unclear whether the simulation field E₀ is the vacuum field or the field inside the material. The authors should give the absorbed densities for both configurations, show two or three fluences for at least the AC case (a phonon-limited mechanism predicts a density-independent τ(Y→X) and zero crossing), and quantify space-charge shifts.

10. **The "nonvolatile"/"time-independent" framing and the novelty claims go beyond the data.** The ZZ channel is called "nearly nonvolatile (time-independent)" (abstract, pp. 4, 11, 14), but delays stop at +600 fs. Physically, ZZ pumping injects electrons directly into the global CBM (X), so a constant −1 polarization is what any relaxation predicts, and it is also the end state under AC pumping (Fig. 2h, S3); the authors say as much in S3 ("as expected for semiconductors"). Both channels end in the same X-valley state, so neither stores which valley was addressed. The paper's own Fig. S3(c,e) shows the holes depolarizing and reversing under ZZ pumping on the same timescale, so the electron–hole pair, and any optical readout of it, is not nonvolatile in either channel. Relaxation between nondegenerate valleys (Γ→L/X in GaAs and Ge, the Si/diamond work in Ref. 32, and Ref. 38 on SnS) has been studied extensively, so "fundamentally distinct from all existing valleytronic platforms" (p. 14) and "first observation of ultrafast valleytronics in group-IV monochalcogenides" should be moderated. The comparison with WS₂ monolayers "under similar conditions" (p. 11) contrasts excitonic, circularly pumped, degenerate K/K′ valleys in a monolayer with free-carrier, linearly pumped, nondegenerate valleys in a bulk crystal, and should be presented only as a qualitative contrast. The authors should extend the delay range to several picoseconds (at least for ZZ) or restrict the wording, define "nonvolatile" quantitatively as a lifetime bound with uncertainty, discuss thermal repopulation of Y at room temperature given the X–Y offset, discuss the prior multivalley hot-carrier literature, and reframe the result, for example as asymmetric, unidirectional intervalley relaxation set by the X–Y offset, discussing which timescales would set any valleytronic functionality.

11. **Sample statistics, reproducibility and crystal alignment are not reported.** The paper does not say how many crystals or cleaves were measured, whether the AC/ZZ contrast was reproduced, whether the AC and ZZ data come from the same cleave and spot, or whether surface degradation during the sequential acquisition affected the comparison. Rotating the crystal by 90° can move the pump footprint (70 × 140 µm) and probe spot (45 × 35 µm) onto a different terrace. SnSe often contains 90° twin domains because the in-plane lattice constants are close, which would mix ZZ and AC excitation; the clean ZZ result argues against this, but it should be checked. Azimuthal misalignment also mixes X and Y selection. The authors should state how the crystal axes were identified (e.g. from the VBM position in Fig. 1a or the symmetry of constant-energy maps) and the alignment accuracy, give the number of independent datasets showing each result, and show the result on at least one other cleave or sample.

12. **The hole dynamics are presented as findings but are theory-only, although the experiment could test them.** The text (pp. 10–11) states that holes "undergo an ultrafast valley polarization reversal under ZZ pumping", and that the hole polarization is stable under AC pumping, based only on the TDBE (Fig. S3). The momentum microscope also records the valence band (Fig. 1a): pump-induced depletion at the VBM (Y) and at the secondary valence maximum along ZZ (X) would test this prediction directly and give an independent check of the model. The authors should show the measured valence-band dynamics for both pump geometries or clearly label the hole results as predictions.

13. **Computational details and parameters that affect the electron–phonon coupling are missing or untested.** Please state whether spin–orbit coupling and van der Waals corrections were included; whether the long-range Fröhlich (polar) term was included in the Wannier interpolation, given that SnSe is polar and Ref. 50 stresses Fröhlich coupling (this matters for intravalley rates and so for the competition between channels); how many bands are in the TDBE; and which detuning reference is used in S1–S2. Show convergence of the e-ph matrix elements and dynamics with respect to the 3 × 3 × 2 DFPT q-grid, the 45 × 45 × 10 interpolation grid and the 3 meV smearing for the collision integrals. Justify the 20 fs dephasing time and show its effect on the initial valley polarization and the early-time dynamics (Fig. 2f,h near t₀). The TDBE uses harmonic phonons and has no phonon–phonon scattering, although SnSe is strongly anharmonic and studied at 300 K; discuss the likely effect on the phonon temperatures reported at 600 fs.
