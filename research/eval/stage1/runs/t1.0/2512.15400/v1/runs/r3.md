Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors combine time-resolved XUV momentum microscopy with time-dependent Boltzmann equation (TDBE) simulations to follow conduction-band electrons in bulk SnSe after linearly polarized excitation along the zigzag (ZZ) or armchair (AC) axis. They report that ZZ pumping fills the X valleys (the global conduction band minimum, CBM) and the population stays there. AC pumping fills the Y valleys, and the electrons scatter to X within a few hundred femtoseconds, which reverses the valley polarization. TDBE assigns this scattering to one optical phonon branch near 20 meV. This would be the first momentum-resolved view of carrier dynamics after valley-selective excitation in a group-IV monochalcogenide, and the theory–experiment pairing is valuable. However, the quantitative valley-polarization claims depend on how photoemission intensities are turned into valley populations, and that step is not yet shown to be reliable.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The experiment is direct and attractive. Momentum microscopy images all X and Y valleys at once (Fig. 2a–d), and the contrast between ZZ and AC pumping is large and easy to see.
- Rotating the crystal rather than the pump polarization (pp. 5–6) is a thoughtful way to avoid laser-assisted photoemission from p-polarized pumping.
- The TDBE framework is first-principles: electron–phonon matrix elements come from EPW, and pump duration and intensity are taken from the experiment. It gives mode-resolved phonon populations (Fig. 3) that a purely phenomenological model could not.
- The predicted electron–hole mirror behaviour (Fig. S3) can be tested and is conceptually useful.
- The topic is timely given the recent interest in anisotropic, non-degenerate valley systems.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The valley-polarization definition and the normalization of the valley-resolved curves are not specified, and as plotted they may not measure population.**
   - Where: Figs. 2(e)–(h).
   - What is unclear: Figs. 2(e,f) show "Norm. Int." for the X and Y valleys. In Fig. 2(f) both the experimental X and Y traces peak at ≈1, which suggests each trace was normalized to its own maximum. Valley polarization is then described as "the normalized difference between intensity within CB X and Y valleys". The sign convention is not given; it can only be inferred from Fig. 2(g) (≈ −1 when X is populated, so presumably P = (I_Y − I_X)/(I_Y + I_X)). If P was computed from separately normalized traces, its value at every delay, including the zero crossing and the reversal to ≈ −0.8 in Fig. 2(h), is an artefact of the normalization. The same question applies to the TDBE curves: is P computed from absolute populations, or from curves rescaled to the experiment?
   - Why it matters: the headline numbers ("nearly unity", "reversal", "excellent agreement") all rest on P.
   - Requested: state the formula for P explicitly. State whether raw, background-subtracted counts in matched k-space and energy volumes were used. Show the unnormalized X and Y intensities for both pump geometries on a common scale. Give P for experiment and theory computed the same way.

2. **Photoemission intensity is used as a proxy for valley population without correcting for matrix elements or geometry, and the two pump configurations use different probe geometries.**
   - The problem: to change from ZZ to AC pumping, the crystal is rotated by 90° about its normal while the 65°-incidence XUV beam is fixed. The X valleys are therefore probed at different positions relative to the XUV plane of incidence in the two configurations, and so are the Y valleys. Photoemission matrix elements for the X (Sn p_x-like) and Y (p_y-like) conduction states (p. 14) depend on the XUV polarization and the direction of incidence relative to the orbital orientation.
   - Evidence that this matters: Fig. 2 itself shows strong intensity asymmetries between symmetry-equivalent valleys. In Fig. 2(b,d) the X valley at k_ZZ ≈ +1.1 Å⁻¹ is much brighter than the one at −1.1 Å⁻¹, and in Fig. 2(a,c) the valleys at ±1.15 Å⁻¹ differ from those at ±0.45 Å⁻¹. So intensity is not simply proportional to population.
   - Momentum positions also need explaining. The valleys in Fig. 2 sit at |k| ≈ 0.45 and ≈ 1.1–1.2 Å⁻¹, while the calculated contours in Fig. 1(c) place X and Y at ≈ 0.35 Å⁻¹. Which Brillouin-zone replicas were integrated, and why?
   - Why it matters: a valley-dependent photoemission cross-section rescales I_X/I_Y and moves the apparent zero crossing and asymptote of P in Fig. 2(h). A geometry-dependent cross-section makes the ZZ-versus-AC comparison inexact.
   - Requested:
     - Calibrate the relative X/Y photoemission efficiency in each geometry, for example from the late-delay or thermalized distribution, from equivalent valleys in several zones, or by measuring both pump polarizations at fixed crystal orientation and checking the laser-assisted photoemission contribution separately.
     - Report P for each symmetry-equivalent valley separately to show it is robust.
     - Reconcile the momentum scales of Fig. 1(c) and Fig. 2.

3. **The simulations are run at a different excitation condition from the experiment, so the claim that they "fully capture" the data is not yet established.**
   - Where: experiment pp. 5 and 15; simulations p. 17; Supplementary Figs. S1–S2.
   - The mismatch:
     - The experiment uses 1.2 eV photons. The simulations use 0.8 eV, resonant with the PBE gap.
     - The excess energy above the band edge is what matters, and the paper never states it for the experiment. The measured CB feature in Fig. 1(a) sits at 1.15 ± 0.18 eV above the VBM, which implies only a small excess energy, but this is not discussed.
     - The detuning study (Fig. S2) goes only up to Δ = 100 meV, and it shows that the AC decay and reversal times shift appreciably with detuning (the zero crossing moves by ≈100 fs between Δ = 0 and 100 meV).
     - The text says that detuning "up to ∼300 meV … still leads to near-unity initial valley polarization" (p. 23). Fig. S1(c) does not appear to support this for ZZ: the red curve rises well above −1 by 1.1 eV.
   - Electronic structure is also uncertain. The PBE lattice constants (a = 4.226, 4.547, 11.830 Å) are noticeably larger than the experimental values, and van der Waals and spin–orbit treatment are not stated. The direction and speed of Y→X scattering depend on the energy offset between the Y and X conduction-band minima relative to k_BT and to the ≈20 meV phonon energy. This offset is not given, either from DFT or from the measured spectra.
   - Requested:
     - Quote the experimental excess energy and the X–Y conduction-band offset measured in Fig. 1(a) and Fig. 2.
     - Compare these with the DFT values.
     - Run the TDBE at a detuning that matches the experimental excess energy, or show that the conclusions are insensitive across that full range.
     - Correct the S1 statement.

**B. Should address**

4. **The X-valley population after AC pumping disagrees with the simulation, and the proposed explanation (recombination) is implausible on this timescale.**
   - Where: Fig. 2(f); p. 10.
   - What is wrong: between 200 and 600 fs the simulated X-valley population keeps rising, while the measured X signal peaks at ≈200 fs and then decays. The authors attribute this to electron–hole recombination missing from the model. Sub-picosecond recombination in an indirect-gap semiconductor at room temperature would need justification.
   - Why it matters: if the X signal decays because carriers leave the probed region, the agreement in valley polarization (Fig. 2h) may come from compensating errors rather than correct Y→X rates.
   - Plausible alternatives include:
     - diffusion of carriers into the bulk, beyond the ~nm probing depth;
     - cooling of carriers out of the integrated energy window;
     - surface or defect trapping;
     - the normalization issue in concern 1.
   - Note also that under ZZ pumping the measured X signal stays flat (Fig. 2e), which argues against fast recombination being the explanation.
   - Requested: show the X-valley signal under both pumps on a common scale and over longer delays, specify the energy window, and discuss these alternatives. Change "excellent agreement" to a quantitative comparison, for example fitted decay times and zero-crossing delays with uncertainties.

5. **The excitation density is high, but carrier–carrier scattering and density effects are not considered.**
   - The issue: an absorbed fluence of 0.9 mJ/cm² at 1.2 eV in SnSe implies a large photoexcited density. That opens electron–electron and electron–hole scattering, screening of the electron–phonon coupling, band-gap renormalization, and hot-phonon effects. The TDBE includes only electron–phonon collisions plus a phenomenological 20 fs dephasing time.
   - Why it matters: the claim that phonon scattering is "the main driver" of Y→X transfer requires excluding these channels.
   - Requested:
     - Give the estimated excitation density.
     - Show a fluence dependence of the AC decay time and zero crossing; a phonon-limited mechanism predicts weak fluence dependence.
     - Ideally, show a temperature dependence. Phonon emission and absorption rates scale with n_q, so a low-temperature measurement would test the mechanism directly.
     - Show how sensitive the simulated dynamics are to τ_dph.

6. **The phonon-mode analysis in Fig. 3 overstates its selectivity and appears inconsistent with the hole dynamics in Fig. S3.**
   - Inconsistency with the holes: the text says that "upon ZZ pumping, only optical modes around Γ are populated, since only intravalley scattering is possible" (p. 13). But Fig. S3(c,e) shows that under ZZ pumping the holes undergo strong X→Y intervalley scattering, which should emit phonons near the same intervalley wavevector. So ΔT = T^AC − T^ZZ in Fig. 3(c) compares electron intervalley scattering (AC) against hole intervalley scattering (ZZ), not intervalley against intravalley. Phonons from hole scattering under AC pumping are also not separated out.
   - Symmetry argument: the glide-symmetry argument (pp. 13–14, S4) only rules out modes that are odd under M̄_xy. It does not single out the second-highest branch, and Fig. S4 shows comparable x and y components for Se. "Unambiguously identify" is too strong.
   - Wavevector: the wavevector connecting the X and Y valleys should be specified. The emitted phonons are spread along Γ–M rather than localized at that q; please explain why.
   - Requested: decompose the phonon emission into electron and hole contributions, give mode-resolved intervalley scattering rates (for example |g|² weighted by phase space, per branch), and temper the uniqueness claim.

7. **The timescales are not quantified, and the temporal calibration is missing.**
   - What is missing: the paper claims decay and reversal "on sub-picosecond timescales" but extracts no time constants. The cross-correlation width, the time-zero determination, the delay step and the error bars are not reported. Valley polarization is plotted at negative delays in Fig. 2(h) (≈ +1 near −200 fs), where both intensities are at background and P is undefined. The paper also suggests that X population appears "during the pump pulse".
   - Why it matters: the early-delay claims need a known time resolution to be meaningful.
   - Requested: report the instrument response, mask P where the signal is below background, and fit the Y-decay and X-rise times with uncertainties. Then compare these with the TDBE intervalley scattering time.

8. **The analysis procedure for the valley-resolved traces is not described.**
   - Missing details:
     - the k-space and energy integration regions for "X" and "Y";
     - background subtraction (pre-time-zero signal, space-charge, residual pump-induced emission);
     - whether the per-panel colour normalization in Figs. 2(a–d) was carried into the traces.
   - The early AC map (Fig. 2b) has a high and structured background. Integration windows that are too wide would mix in that background and bias P toward zero.
   - Also, an s-polarized in-plane pump field can still produce laser-assisted sidebands for electrons with in-plane momentum. The authors should show that the X- and Y-valley signals near t = 0 are free of such sidebands.

9. **The "nonvolatile" and "time-independent" claims extend beyond the data, and the framing as a new operating principle needs tempering.**
   - Delay range: the delays stop at +600 fs, so "time-independent" and "nearly nonvolatile" (abstract, p. 14) are supported only over about 0.6 ps.
   - Physics: the ZZ channel stays polarized because X is the global CBM. Relaxation of hot electrons into the lowest valley of an indirect, multivalley semiconductor is the expected behaviour (the authors say as much in S3: "as expected for semiconductors"). Calling this "fundamentally distinct from all existing valleytronic platforms" overstates the novelty.
   - Requested:
     - Extend the delay range or restrict the wording.
     - Discuss thermal repopulation of Y at room temperature given the X–Y offset.
     - Position the work against ultrafast studies of other multivalley semiconductors (for example Ge, GaAs Γ–L, and the Si/diamond work in Ref. 32) and against Ref. 38 on SnS.
   - The comparison with WS₂ monolayers (p. 11) mixes excitonic, degenerate-valley physics with bulk free carriers. It should be presented as a qualitative contrast only.

10. **Sample reproducibility and crystal orientation are not documented.**
    - The paper does not say how many samples or cleaves were measured, whether the AC/ZZ contrast was reproduced, or how degradation was checked over the acquisition time.
    - Domains: SnSe crystals often contain 90° twin domains, because the two in-plane lattice constants are close. A mixture of domains within the 45 × 35 µm probe spot would mix the ZZ and AC responses. The clean ZZ result argues against this, but the authors should state how the AC and ZZ axes were identified (for example from the valence band maximum position in Fig. 1a) and how accurately they were aligned.
    - Requested: show the result on at least one additional cleave or sample.

11. **The hole predictions are stated as results but not compared with the measured valence band.**
    - Where: p. 11; Fig. S3.
    - The issue: the momentum microscope records the valence band too (Fig. 1a). Yet the predicted reversal of hole valley polarization under ZZ pumping, and its stability under AC pumping, are presented as findings without experimental comparison.
    - Requested: show the measured valence-band hole dynamics at X and Y for both pump geometries, which would be a strong independent test of the model. Otherwise, clearly label these as predictions.

**C. Minor**

12. **Text and reference errors.**
    - P. 10: "slower decay of the intensity in the X valleys (blue curve)" should read "red curve".
    - P. 10: "as shown in Fig. 2(f), the TDBE-predicted valley depolarization" should refer to Fig. 2(h).
    - P. 26: "a nearly unity and time-dependent hole valley polarization" presumably means "time-independent".
    - Fig. 3 caption: "(a)–(c) … following photoexcitation along the ZZ and AC directions, respectively" should be "(a)–(b)".
    - The Data Availability statement is incomplete ("Zenodo .").
    - Ref. 48 is a placeholder ("[URL]").
    - Refs. 30 and 51 are duplicates.

13. **Computational details are missing.**
    - Please state whether spin–orbit coupling and van der Waals corrections were included.
    - Give the convergence of the 45 × 45 × 10 interpolation grid and of the 3 meV smearing for the collision integrals.
    - Explain the choice of a 20 fs dephasing time.
    - State how many bands are included in the TDBE.
    - The S1 and S2 text should state which detuning reference (DFT gap) is used.

14. **Figures need clearer labelling and normalization.**
    - Fig. 1(a) quotes the CB energy as 1.15 ± 0.18 eV above the VBM. Please state what this window represents and how it relates to the 1.2 eV pump.
    - In Figs. S3(c), the Y-valley hole population exceeds 1.5 in "normalized" units; please state the normalization.
    - Each panel of Fig. 2(a–d) appears to be individually normalized. State this, or use a common scale so that early and late maps can be compared.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Strengthen the experimental core:
   - Measure the AC/ZZ dynamics as a function of fluence and of sample temperature, for example 80 K versus 300 K. This would test the phonon-mediated mechanism directly.
   - Extend the delays to several picoseconds to test the "nonvolatile" channel.
2. Add the measured valence-band (hole) dynamics, to test the electron–hole mirror prediction of Fig. S3.
3. Give a quantitative theory–experiment comparison: fitted time constants with uncertainties, and TDBE at the experimental excess energy.
4. Report the intervalley scattering rates for each phonon branch from the TDBE, separating electron and hole contributions. This would make the mode identification in Fig. 3 quantitative rather than inferred from phonon temperatures.
