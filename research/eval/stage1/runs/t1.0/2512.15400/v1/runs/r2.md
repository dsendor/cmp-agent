Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports time- and angle-resolved XUV photoemission (momentum microscope, 21.6 eV probe, 1.2 eV linearly polarized pump) on bulk SnSe. It combines the data with first-principles time-dependent Boltzmann equation (TDBE) simulations. The authors argue that pumping along ZZ creates a nearly unity, time-independent X-valley polarization, while pumping along AC creates a Y-valley polarization that decays and reverses within a few hundred femtoseconds. The simulations attribute the reversal to Y→X intervalley scattering by the second-highest optical phonon branch (~20 meV). A momentum-resolved view of valley-selective excitation in group-IV monochalcogenides is new and of interest; to my knowledge, earlier experiments on these materials used static optics only. Whether the work supports the strong conceptual framing ("coexisting nonvolatile and volatile valley channels", "operating principle fundamentally distinct from all existing valleytronic platforms") is less clear, and the experimental valley-polarization signal needs more careful validation.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A direct, momentum-resolved observation of polarization-selective population of the X and Y conduction valleys in SnSe (Fig. 2a–d). The contrast between the two pump geometries is visually clear.
- The authors avoid laser-assisted photoemission and Fresnel asymmetries by rotating the crystal rather than the pump polarization, which is a careful choice.
- The ab initio TDBE framework (density matrix for photoexcitation, plus full electron–phonon collision integrals) is state of the art and gives a mode-resolved picture of the nonequilibrium phonon population (Fig. 3).
- Detuning (Figs. S1, S2) and hole dynamics (Fig. S3) are examined in the supplement.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **Laser-assisted photoemission (LAPE) of the Y-point valence band could mimic the early Y-valley "conduction band" signal under AC pumping.** The VBM sits at Y (Fig. 1b). The pump photon energy is 1.2 eV, and the CB contour shown in Fig. 1(a) is at E − E_VBM = 1.15 ± 0.18 eV. A first-order LAPE sideband of the Y-point VBM therefore falls at nearly the same energy and momentum as the CB Y valley. The LAPE amplitude for an in-plane (s-polarized) field scales roughly as (E·k∥). It is maximal at Y when the field is along AC and vanishes at Y when the field is along ZZ. This is exactly the observed pattern: Y intensity appears only under AC pumping (Fig. 2b, f), peaks at zero delay, and decays within ~200–300 fs. That is only about twice the 135 fs pump duration convolved with the probe. Rotating the crystal (p. 6) removes the s/p asymmetry but not this E·k selectivity. The initial positive valley polarization, and so the "reversal", rests on this early Y signal. The authors should:
   - show energy–momentum cuts through Y at t ≈ 0 and compare the CB Y-valley dispersion with a VB replica shifted by 1.2 eV;
   - compare the Y-signal temporal profile with the measured pump–probe cross-correlation (for example, a LAPE replica of a non-resonant feature, or a gold/metal reference);
   - show the fluence dependence of the Y signal at t = 0;
   - analyze negative delays (XUV first), where any LAPE-like pump-dressed signal should be symmetric about t₀.
   Without this, the measured Y-valley lifetime and the "reversal" are not established.

2. **The experimental valley polarization is defined from photoemission intensities without accounting for matrix elements, normalization or the probe geometry.** Fig. 2(e,f) plot "Norm. Int." for X and Y, and Fig. 2(g,h) take the normalized difference of these intensities. Several points are unclear:
   - whether each curve is normalized separately (both peak at ~1 in Fig. 2f, which suggests it is). If so, the polarization is not a population ratio.
   - XUV photoemission cross-sections at X and Y differ because of orbital character (p_x vs p_y Sn states, p. 14), the 65° incidence and the XUV polarization. They also differ between the two crystal azimuths, since rotating the crystal changes the XUV polarization relative to the crystal axes.
   - Bulk SnSe is probed at a single photon energy, so X and Y may be sampled at different, unspecified k_z.

   The claims of "nearly unity" polarization (ZZ) and of a sign reversal (AC) depend on the relative X/Y intensity scale. The authors should state the normalization and estimate the relative X/Y photoemission efficiencies, for example from the ground-state valence bands at both azimuths, from late-delay data once carriers have thermalized, or from calculated cross-sections. They should give the k_z probed, and show how the polarization curves change under reasonable variations of the relative scale. Valley polarization should not be plotted where both intensities are at background level (t < −100 fs in Fig. 2h, where the black curve sits near +1 while the signals are close to zero).

3. **The "nonvolatile channel" interpretation is not supported as stated.** Under ZZ pumping, electrons are injected directly into the global CBM (X). The time-independent −1 polarization (Fig. 2g) is therefore what any carrier relaxation predicts: it is also the end state under AC pumping (Fig. 2h, S3). Both channels end in the same X-valley state, so neither preserves the information about which valley was addressed. The "nonvolatile" channel does not store valley information; it simply prepares carriers in the equilibrium valley. The headline claim of "the coexistence of one nearly nonvolatile (time-independent) valley channel and one highly volatile channel" (abstract, pp. 4, 11, 14) needs to be reframed. The authors could, for example, describe the result as an asymmetric, unidirectional intervalley relaxation set by the X–Y energy offset. They should also discuss what quantity would serve as valley memory. The time window is also limited: the ZZ polarization is shown only to 600 fs, while the abstract and conclusions describe it as "time-independent".

4. **The quantitative agreement between TDBE and experiment is not a robust test, given the mismatched excitation conditions.** The simulations use a 0.8 eV pump, resonant with the PBE gap, whereas the experiment uses 1.2 eV (p. 5, Methods). The authors state that detuning is "not critical" (p. 5). However, Fig. S2(d) shows that only 50–100 meV of detuning shifts the zero crossing of the AC polarization by ~100 fs and changes the intermediate dynamics substantially. The real excess energy above the Y and X direct gaps in experiment is several hundred meV, beyond the range simulated. Three further points:
   - The X–Y conduction-band offset, which governs the direction and rate of Y→X scattering and back-scattering at 300 K, is taken from PBE without validation against experiment or a corrected band structure. The ARPES data could give this offset directly.
   - It is not clear how "the same intensity" maps onto excited carrier density when the absorption at 0.8 eV (DFT) and 1.2 eV (experiment) differs.
   - In Fig. 2(f) the simulated X population keeps rising up to 600 fs while the measured one does not. The suggested cause, electron–hole recombination (p. 10), is implausible on a sub-ps timescale in an indirect-gap bulk semiconductor. Out-of-plane transport out of the probe depth, scattering to other valleys or bands, or the normalization issue in concern 2 are more likely.

   Since the populations disagree, the agreement in polarization (a ratio) may be partly fortuitous. The authors should run the TDBE at an excess energy matched to the experiment (scissor-corrected bands, or detuning ~0.3–0.4 eV), report the extracted Y-valley decay time and zero-crossing time with uncertainties for both experiment and theory, and revise "fully captured" and "excellent agreement" accordingly.

**B. Should address**

5. **The phonon mechanism is identified only theoretically and is stated too strongly.** The identification of the ~20 meV second-highest optical branch (Fig. 3c) and the claim that the method "unambiguously identif[ies]" the coupled modes (p. 13) come entirely from the simulation. There are several issues:
   - The phonon temperatures at 600 fs also include intravalley cooling of the electrons that have already scattered into X with large excess energy. ΔT = T^AC − T^ZZ therefore mixes intervalley emission with differences in carrier energy distributions.
   - The analysis is restricted to Γ–M. That is appropriate for the X−Y wavevector, but the full q-resolved emission should be shown to confirm that intervalley scattering dominates.
   - Several other optical modes in Fig. 3(c) also show positive ΔT.
   - SnSe is polar and strongly coupled via Fröhlich interaction (the authors' Ref. 50). The Methods do not say whether the long-range polar contribution was included in the Wannier interpolation of g (this matters for intravalley rates and thus for the competition between channels), nor how the strong lattice anharmonicity of SnSe at 300 K (harmonic DFPT phonons) affects the result.

   The authors should report the computed Y→X intervalley scattering rate and its mode decomposition directly, test whether switching off the identified branch removes the reversal, and state that this is a theoretical attribution. An experimental test would substantially strengthen it, such as temperature dependence of the AC decay time (occupation of the ~20 meV mode) or the coherent phonon content of the data.

6. **Fluence, heating and carrier density are not characterized.** At 0.9 mJ/cm² and 1.2 eV, the excited carrier density, the possibility of two-photon absorption (2 × 1.2 eV well above the gap), lattice heating at 166 kHz, space charge, and band-gap renormalization (which could change the X–Y offset that controls the dynamics) are not addressed. Only one fluence is shown. The authors should estimate the excitation density, show the fluence dependence of the X and Y dynamics and the polarization, and quantify space-charge shifts.

7. **Time resolution, time zero and quantitative timescales are missing.** The XUV pulse duration, the overall cross-correlation and the method used to set t₀ are not given. In Fig. 2(e) the measured X rise is visibly delayed relative to TDBE, which may reflect a t₀ offset. The claimed "sub-picosecond" decay and reversal are not quantified by any fit. The authors should give the instrument response, fit the X and Y traces with rise and decay times convolved with it, report the uncertainties and the zero-crossing time of the AC polarization, and add error bars (counting statistics) to Fig. 2(e–h).

8. **The region-of-interest definitions for "X valley" and "Y valley" intensities are not specified.** The paper does not say which energy and momentum windows were integrated for each valley, whether the windows contain the same phase-space volume, whether hot electrons above the valley minimum are included, or how background (for example, pump-induced secondary electrons) was subtracted. The CB contour in Fig. 1(a) has a ±0.18 eV window, but the Fig. 2 traces are not described. These choices directly set the polarization values and should be stated and varied as a robustness check.

9. **Sample statistics and reproducibility are not reported.** There is no information on how many cleaves or samples were measured, whether the AC/ZZ contrast was reproduced, how accurately the azimuth was aligned (misalignment mixes the X and Y selection), or whether surface degradation over the acquisition time affected the comparison between the two azimuths, which were measured sequentially. The authors should report these and show the result on at least one other cleave.

10. **The hole dynamics are presented as findings but are theory-only.** On p. 11 the text says that holes "undergo an ultrafast valley polarization reversal under ZZ pumping" (Fig. S3) without stating that no measurement supports this. The momentum microscope should be able to see VB depletion (hole) dynamics at X and Y. Either show these data or label the hole results clearly as predictions.

11. **The comparison with TMDCs is not like-for-like.** The statement that "under similar conditions" WS₂ monolayers show symmetric depolarization (p. 11) compares excitonic, circularly pumped, degenerate K/K′ valleys in a monolayer with free-carrier, linearly pumped, nondegenerate valleys in a bulk crystal. Unidirectional relaxation toward the lower valley is generic to any nondegenerate multivalley semiconductor (for example, Γ→L/X transfer in GaAs or Ge, and the Si/diamond work in Ref. 32). Claims such as "fundamentally distinct from all existing valleytronic platforms" and "first observation of ultafast valleytronics in group-IV monochalcogenides" should be moderated and placed in this context.

12. **Several TDBE parameters are uncontrolled.** The 20 fs dephasing time is phenomenological, and its effect on the initial valley polarization and the early-time dynamics (Fig. 2f, h near t₀) is not tested. Convergence of the 45 × 45 × 10 grid and the 3 meV smearing for intervalley rates is not shown. The PBE lattice constants (4.226, 4.547, 11.830 Å) are noticeably larger than experiment, especially a₃, which may shift the X/Y offset. The authors should provide sensitivity tests for τ_dph and the grid, and state which axes correspond to ZZ and AC.

**C. Minor**

13. On p. 10 the X-valley intensity is called the "(blue curve)". It is the red curve.
14. The Fig. 3 caption says (a)–(c) are "following photoexcitation along the ZZ and AC directions, respectively", but there are three panels and (c) is the difference. Please clarify.
15. The Fig. 1(a) CB contour is "for the AC pump configuration", but no delay is given.
16. Ref. 48 is a placeholder ("[URL]"), the Data Availability statement lacks the Zenodo DOI, and Refs. 30 and 51 are duplicates.
17. Fig. 3(a,b): the colour scale saturates at 350 K. Please give the actual maximum mode temperatures and the total absorbed energy, to show that the phonon heating is consistent with the fluence.
18. Fig. S4 uses x/y/z without connecting them to ZZ/AC. The statement that "in-plane displacements dominate" holds for Sn but less clearly for Se (y ≈ x).
19. In Fig. 2(h) the TDBE curve starts at +1 before the pump arrives. Please explain how polarization is defined when populations vanish.
20. On p. 5, "Supplementary Materials" Figs. S1 and S2 are cited as demonstrating insensitivity to detuning. Fig. S2 actually shows sizable sensitivity for AC pumping, so the wording should match the figure.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a dedicated control section that rules out LAPE and pump-dressed artifacts at the Y point: energy–momentum cuts at t₀, cross-correlation, fluence dependence, and ideally a lower pump photon energy or a pump polarized out of the AC/ZZ axes (for example 45°, where both valleys should be populated in a predictable ratio).
2. Calibrate the X/Y photoemission intensity ratio and report valley polarization as a population ratio with error bars. A 45° pump measurement would also provide a useful internal calibration of the selection rules.
3. Measure the AC decay time as a function of sample temperature and pump photon energy, and compare with TDBE run at matched excess energy and scissor-corrected bands. This would turn the phonon-mode attribution into a quantitative, falsifiable prediction.
4. Reframe the conceptual claim around asymmetric, unidirectional intervalley relaxation in nondegenerate valleys, and discuss quantitatively which timescales (Y-valley lifetime vs. X-valley carrier lifetime) set any possible valleytronic functionality.
