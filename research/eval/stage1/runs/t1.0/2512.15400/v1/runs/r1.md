Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports time- and angle-resolved XUV photoemission (momentum microscope, 21.6 eV probe, 1.2 eV s-polarized pump at room temperature) on bulk SnSe. The pump polarization is aligned with either the zigzag (ZZ) or the armchair (AC) axis by rotating the crystal. The data are compared with time-dependent Boltzmann equation (TDBE) simulations built on DFT/DFPT/EPW electron-phonon couplings. The main results are: ZZ pumping populates only the conduction-band X valleys and gives a valley polarization that stays near -1; AC pumping first populates the Y valleys, and the valley polarization then decays and reverses within a few hundred femtoseconds. The simulations attribute the Y→X transfer to a roughly 20 meV optical phonon branch. This would be the first momentum-resolved look at nonequilibrium valley dynamics in a group-IV monochalcogenide, and the combined experiment and first-principles approach is attractive. However, the quantitative valley polarization, the attribution to a single phonon branch and the framing as a "fundamentally distinct operating principle" all need stronger support.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Direct momentum-space imaging of the conduction-band X and Y valleys after polarization-selective excitation. Optical absorption and photoluminescence studies (refs. 35, 36, 40) could not provide this.
- A clear qualitative contrast between the two pump geometries (Fig. 2a–d). It is consistent with the known linear-dichroic selection rules and with intervalley relaxation toward the global conduction-band minimum.
- Parameter-free (apart from pump polarization and a phenomenological dephasing time) ab initio TDBE simulations whose early-time Y- and X-valley transients resemble the data (Fig. 2f,h).
- A mode-resolved analysis of the nonequilibrium phonon population (Fig. 3) and a symmetry analysis of the intervalley electron-phonon matrix elements (SI S4). These give a microscopic picture that can be tested.
- Sensible choice to rotate the crystal rather than switch s/p pump polarization, which avoids p-polarized laser-assisted photoemission.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Laser-assisted photoemission (LAPE) may contribute to the early-time Y-valley signal under AC pumping. The conduction-band window in Fig. 1(a) is E − E_VBM = 1.15 ± 0.18 eV. This is almost exactly where the first pump sideband of the valence-band maximum appears (VBM + 1.2 eV), and the VBM sits at the Y valleys. With s-polarized pumping the in-plane field still dresses photoelectrons, and the dressing scales with A·k. When the pump field lies along AC, the effect is largest for electrons emitted with momentum along AC, i.e. at Y. When the pump lies along ZZ, the effect is smallest at Y. A LAPE replica of the Y-valley VBM would therefore appear only under AC pumping and only near temporal overlap. That is exactly the behaviour assigned to optical injection into the Y valleys (Fig. 2b, 2f: blue curve peaking at about 0 fs and decaying over about 200–300 fs). The paper raises LAPE only to justify not using p-polarization (p. 6). It does not show that the effect is negligible in the geometry actually used. Since the "initial positive valley polarization followed by reversal" (Fig. 2h) is the central result, the authors should do the following:
   - Show energy-momentum cuts through the Y valley at t ≈ 0. A sideband reproduces the hole-like valence dispersion; real conduction-band electrons are electron-like.
   - Check the symmetric sideband at VBM − 1.2 eV, which cannot contain conduction-band population, and compare its time trace with the Y-valley trace.
   - Compare the Y-valley rise and decay with the measured pump-probe cross-correlation.
   - Ideally, repeat at lower fluence, since the sideband scales linearly with pump intensity and the population differently.
   - Also state how time zero was determined.

2. It is unclear how the valley polarization is extracted, and whether photoemission matrix elements bias it. The Fig. 2 caption defines the valley polarization as the "normalized difference between intensity within CB X and Y valleys". Several points are unspecified, and each changes the plotted value:
   - Panels 2(e,f) are separately normalized ("Norm. Int."). Is Fig. 2(g,h) computed from raw counts or from these normalized curves? If the latter, it is not a population ratio.
   - Which valley replicas are integrated? Fig. 2(a,c) show strong spots at k_ZZ ≈ ±1.1 Å⁻¹ and weak ones at ±0.4 Å⁻¹. In Fig. 2(b,d) the X spot at +1.1 Å⁻¹ is much brighter than at −1.1 Å⁻¹.
   - What energy window, momentum radius and background (pre-time-zero, pump-only multiphoton) subtraction were used?
   - Most importantly: the X and Y valleys have different orbital character (Sn p_x vs p_y, p. 14). Rotating the crystal by 90° changes the XUV incidence plane and polarization relative to those orbitals. The photoemission cross-sections at X and Y therefore differ, and differ between the two configurations.

   Without a calibration, a measured value near ±1 cannot be read as "nearly unity" population polarization, and the ZZ and AC traces cannot be compared quantitatively. Possible calibrations are: comparing the X/Y intensity ratio at long delays, when both configurations should reach the same quasi-equilibrium distribution; or applying the same matrix-element-weighted integration to the TDBE output. The authors should give the sign convention, the integration regions and the per-valley raw counts, and estimate how matrix elements change the valley polarization.

3. Whether the theory-experiment agreement supports the mechanism depends on choices that are not tested. The paper says the TDBE "fully captures" the dynamics, with polarization as the only adjustable parameter (p. 5). But:
   - The simulation pumps resonantly at the PBE gap (0.8 eV), while the experiment uses 1.2 eV. The excess energies in the X and Y valleys are therefore different in theory and experiment, and they differ between the two valleys.
   - Fig. S2(d) shows that the zero crossing of the AC valley polarization moves by roughly 100 fs between Δ = 0 and 100 meV. That is comparable to the timescale being compared in Fig. 2(h). The agreement with the Δ = 0 run may therefore be partly fortuitous.
   - The Y→X scattering rate and its one-way character depend on the conduction-band offset between Y and X. In PBE without spin-orbit coupling or van der Waals correction, with lattice constants about 2–3 % larger than experiment (a3 = 11.83 Å), this offset is uncertain. The trARPES data could measure it directly.
   - The 20 fs dephasing time is a free parameter, and the alignment of experimental and simulated time zero is not described.
   - No time constants are extracted from either data set.

   The authors should do four things:
   - Report the measured and calculated X-Y conduction-band offset and direct gaps.
   - Run the TDBE with a scissor-corrected band structure, or with a detuning matched to the experimental excess energy in each valley.
   - Fit both the data and the simulations with a rate model convolved with the cross-correlation, and give τ(Y→X) with uncertainties.
   - Show how sensitive the result is to τ_dph and to the time-zero alignment.

B. Should address

4. The attribution to the roughly 20 meV optical branch rests on theory only, and on an indirect proxy. The mode identification (Fig. 3) comes from the nonequilibrium phonon "temperature" 600 fs after the pump. This proxy depends on each mode's heat capacity, its phase-space weight and which other processes heat it. It also does not separate Y→X emission from intravalley cooling of hot carriers, which differs between the two configurations because the excess energies differ. A more direct quantity would be the branch-resolved Y→X scattering rate, or the intervalley spectral function, evaluated at the actual momentum transfer q = k_X − k_Y. Further points:
   - From Fig. 1(c), q = k_X − k_Y ≈ (0.37, −0.30) Å⁻¹, which is not exactly along Γ–M. The text says "due to momentum conservation, the intervalley scattering of carriers leads to the emission of phonons along the Γ–M direction" (p. 13), so a 2D q-resolved map would be more convincing.
   - Fig. 3(c) shows the largest ΔT at or near Γ, which looks like intravalley rather than intervalley emission. Please explain.
   - No measurement tests the phonon mechanism. Temperature dependence (phonon occupation n_q vs n_q+1) or a coherent-phonon or diffraction signature at the predicted mode would do so. "Unambiguously identify" (p. 13) should be softened unless such evidence is added.

5. Excitation density and pump fluence. The pump fluence is 0.9 mJ/cm² at 1.2 eV, which in SnSe likely corresponds to roughly 10²⁰ cm⁻³ or more excited carriers. The paper does not give the absorbed fluence or the carrier density, and no fluence dependence is shown. At such densities, electron-electron scattering, screening of the electron-phonon coupling and band renormalization can all change intervalley rates, and none is in the TDBE. Two further issues follow:
   - Because SnSe is optically anisotropic, s-polarized light at 65° incidence along AC and along ZZ is reflected and absorbed differently. The two configurations may therefore not have matched carrier densities or depths, yet the TDBE assumes "the same pump pulse ... intensity as in the experiments".
   - It is unclear whether the simulation field E0 is the vacuum field or the field inside the material.

   The authors should give the absorbed densities for both configurations and show at least two or three fluences for the AC case. This would establish that τ(Y→X) does not depend on density, as a purely electron-phonon mechanism predicts.

6. The explanation for the late-time discrepancy is not plausible. Between 200 and 600 fs the TDBE X-valley population keeps rising, but the measured X signal decays (Fig. 2f). The authors attribute this to neglected electron-hole recombination (p. 10). Recombination within a few hundred femtoseconds in an indirect-gap semiconductor at these densities is unlikely and needs justification. More likely causes are:
   - hot electrons cooling below the lower edge of the 1.15 ± 0.18 eV window;
   - carriers diffusing out of the few-ångström photoemission probing depth into the bulk (the TDBE is homogeneous bulk);
   - surface recombination or trapping;
   - kz selection at 21.6 eV.

   The authors should show energy-resolved (E vs t) traces for each valley, integrate over the whole conduction band, and discuss which of these mechanisms applies. The same issue affects the "excellent agreement" claimed for Fig. 2(h). That valley polarization comes from populations that disagree by about a factor of two at 600 fs, so the agreement may be partly an artefact of how each curve is normalized.

7. "Nonvolatile", "time-independent" and the novelty claims are stronger than the data allow. The ZZ channel is called "nearly nonvolatile (time-independent)" (abstract and pp. 4, 11, 14), but the data stop at 600 fs. In addition, that electrons stay in the global conduction-band minimum is the expected outcome of relaxation, not a protected valley degree of freedom. The paper's own Fig. S3(c,e) shows that under ZZ pumping the holes depolarize and reverse on the same timescale. So the electron-hole pair, and any optical readout of it, is not nonvolatile in either channel. Relaxation between nondegenerate valleys (for example Γ→L/X in GaAs and Ge, and in Si, ref. 32) has been studied extensively with ultrafast optics and photoemission. The statement that the operating principle is "fundamentally distinct from all existing valleytronic platforms" (p. 14) should be put in that context. The authors should:
   - extend the delay range to several picoseconds, at least for the ZZ case;
   - define "nonvolatile" quantitatively, as a lifetime bound with uncertainty;
   - discuss the prior multivalley hot-carrier literature;
   - temper the framing.

8. The hole-dynamics claims are untested, though the experiment could test them. The main text (pp. 10–11) presents the hole valley reversal under ZZ pumping as a result. It comes only from TDBE (Fig. S3). The momentum microscope also records the valence band. Pump-induced depletion at the VBM (Y) and at the secondary valence maximum along ZZ would test this prediction directly and give an independent check of the model. The authors should either show these data or clearly label the hole dynamics as a prediction.

9. Reproducibility, sample quality and uncertainties. The paper does not say how many cleaves or crystals were measured, whether the AC and ZZ data come from the same cleave and spot, or whether the measurements were repeated. Rotating the crystal by 90° can move the pump and probe footprint (70 × 140 µm pump) onto a different terrace. SnSe crystals often contain twin domains, which would mix ZZ and AC excitation. No error bars appear in Fig. 2(e–h), and the azimuthal alignment accuracy is not given. The authors should add:
   - statistical error bars, from counting statistics or repeated scans;
   - the number of independent datasets showing each result;
   - how the crystal axes were identified, the misalignment uncertainty, and any evidence of twinning (for example the symmetry of the constant-energy maps).

10. Computational details that affect the electron-phonon coupling. Please state:
   - whether spin-orbit coupling was included, since Sn and Se are heavy;
   - whether the long-range Fröhlich (polar) term was included in the Wannier interpolation, given that SnSe is polar and ref. 50 stresses Fröhlich coupling;
   - whether the e-ph matrix elements and the TDBE dynamics are converged with respect to the 3 × 3 × 2 DFPT q-grid and the 45 × 45 × 10 interpolation grid.

   The TDBE also has no phonon-phonon scattering, although SnSe is strongly anharmonic and is studied at room temperature. Its likely effect on the phonon temperatures reported at 600 fs should be discussed. Finally, explain how the bulk 3D calculation, which averages over kz, compares with the fixed-kz cut at 21.6 eV photon energy.

C. Minor

11. Text-figure mismatches:
   - p. 10: "delayed rise and slower decay of the intensity in the X valleys (blue curve)" should say red curve.
   - p. 10: "as shown in Fig. 2(f), the TDBE-predicted valley depolarization dynamics" should refer to Fig. 2(h).
   - Define the sign convention of the valley polarization explicitly; from the figures it appears to be (I_Y − I_X)/(I_Y + I_X).

12. Fig. 1(a): state whether "± 0.02 eV" and "± 0.18 eV" are integration half-widths or uncertainties. Fig. 1(c) places X at about 0.37 Å⁻¹, while Fig. 2 shows the strongest X spots at about ±1.1 Å⁻¹. Explain that these are replicas in the next Brillouin zone and indicate the zone boundaries on Fig. 2. Fig. 1(b) is labelled a schematic; say whether it is the calculated PBE dispersion.

13. Experimental parameters are missing: XUV polarization, energy and momentum resolution, and the measured cross-correlation width (time resolution).

14. SI S4 and Table S1: define the x, y, z axes relative to ZZ and AC and the lattice vectors a1, a2, a3. The valleys lie on the Γ–X and Γ–Y lines rather than at the zone-boundary points, so the little-group argument should be phrased for the actual valley wavevectors. Fig. S4 shows out-of-plane (z) Sn displacements contributing about as much as y; the statement that "in-plane motions contribute largely" should be qualified.

15. References and housekeeping:
   - refs. 30 and 51 are duplicates;
   - ref. 48 is a placeholder ("[URL]");
   - the Zenodo link in Data Availability is missing;
   - "GST mbH" should be "GmbH";
   - "exchange-correlational" should be "exchange-correlation".

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add an energy-resolved analysis of the Y-valley signal near t = 0, with the symmetric VBM − ħω sideband and the cross-correlation, to rule out LAPE. Also add a short fluence series for the AC configuration. Together these would put the central "initial polarization followed by reversal" result on firm ground.
2. Calibrate the valley-polarization readout for photoemission matrix elements and give the raw per-valley counts with error bars. Fit a rate model to obtain τ(Y→X) for direct comparison with a TDBE run that uses corrected gaps and matched excess energies.
3. Use the valence-band data to test the predicted opposite hole valley dynamics, and extend the delays to several picoseconds. This would turn the theory-only claims about holes and "nonvolatility" into measured ones.
4. Replace or supplement the phonon-temperature proxy with branch-resolved Y→X scattering rates at the actual momentum transfer. If possible, add a room-temperature vs low-temperature comparison to test the phonon-occupation dependence the mechanism predicts.
