Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use STM/STS at 4.4 K to show pairs of in-gap states localized around a class of Ta-site defects (alpha, alpha', beta, beta', gamma) in the proposed excitonic insulator Ta2Pd3Te5. The states sit just inside the roughly 100 meV gap and extend over about 5-6 nm. The authors argue that these states are an excitonic analogue of Yu-Shiba-Rusinov (YSR) states, produced by a local charge dipole of a Pd atom on a Ta site. Their evidence is that the states follow the gap when it is reduced by local strain or by tip-induced carrier injection, and that a tight-binding model with a dipole potential reproduces them. Impurity-bound states in an exciton condensate have been predicted since Zittartz (1967) and not observed, so a convincing observation would be significant. The central question is whether the data single out an excitonic, pair-breaking mechanism rather than ordinary defect levels in a small-gap insulator.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- High-quality atomically resolved topography and dI/dV maps. A clear classification of defects separates those with in-gap states (about 17%) from the majority Pd-vacancy-type defects without them (Extended Data Fig. 1). This gives a useful internal null control.
- The tip-height gap-tuning experiment (Fig. 3e-g, Extended Data Figs. 7 and 8) follows the defect states and the host gap edges together, on five defect types, which is a good experimental design.
- The strained-stripe region, where the gap collapses into a pseudogap (Fig. 3a-d), provides a second, independent way of tuning the gap.
- DFT-simulated STM images that compare candidate defect structures (Fig. 4a-d, Extended Data Fig. 4) give a reasonable structural assignment.
- The topic is timely and connects impurity physics in superconductors to the active debate on excitonic order in Ta2Pd3Te5 and Ta2NiSe5.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The data and the model do not tell an excitonic, pair-breaking bound state apart from an ordinary defect level in a gapped semiconductor.**
   - The model (Methods, Eq. 1) puts the excitonic order in as a uniform, fixed interband coupling Delta between c and v orbitals. That term is mathematically the same as a single-particle hybridization gap.
   - A charge dipole in any such two-band insulator will bind states split off the conduction and valence band edges, with or without exciton condensation. Nothing in the calculation of Fig. 4e-g uses the many-body nature of Delta.
   - In the YSR problem, the defining physics is that the impurity locally breaks pairs, that is, suppresses the order parameter. This is absent because Delta is not computed self-consistently around the impurity.
   - The experimental "tuning" tests (Fig. 3) have the same limitation:
     - Any defect level whose binding energy scales with the gap would shift towards midgap as the gap shrinks.
     - Any localized level would broaden or merge into the continuum once the host becomes a metallic pseudogap.
   - So the statements that the result "solidly confirms the excitonic origin" (p. 1554) and that the states arise "through a charge-exciton version of the YSR mechanism" (abstract) do not yet follow.
   - The authors should:
     - (a) solve the model self-consistently for Delta(r) around the dipole, and show the local suppression of the order parameter and its signature in the data (for example a spatial map of the local gap edge near the defect, compared with Fig. 2k-o);
     - (b) repeat the Fig. 4e-g calculation with the same gap produced by a non-interacting hybridization or structural term, and state which observable differs;
     - (c) identify at least one experimental feature that the excitonic scenario predicts and a conventional donor/acceptor or dipole-bound level does not. Candidates are the relation between bound-state energy, gap and coherence length, the decay and oscillation of the wavefunction with the Fermi wavevector of the parent semimetal bands rather than the lattice, or the response to dipole strength.
   - This issue is sharper because the excitonic (rather than lattice-driven) nature of the gap in Ta2Pd3Te5 is still debated. The paper takes it as established from refs. 33-36.

2. **Other explanations for the tip-height "gap tuning" are not excluded, and the gap collapse coincides with a visible discontinuity in the data.**
   - The gap reduction from about 140 to 60 meV and the abrupt collapse (Fig. 3e, Extended Data Figs. 7 and 8) are attributed to carrier injection by the tip field. When the tip approaches to Delta-Z = -500 pm, "virtually touching" the surface, several other effects become strong:
     - tip-induced band bending;
     - charging or ionization of the defect level itself (which shifts defect resonances relative to the band edges);
     - tip-sample mechanical force, which induces local strain (the authors show separately that strain closes the gap);
     - a large current density and local heating.
   - In every panel of Extended Data Fig. 8a,b there is a sharp horizontal seam at Delta-Z of about -0.25 to -0.3 nm, where the colour scale and spectra change abruptly. The "abrupt collapse into a metallic pseudogap" happens exactly there.
   - The authors should:
     - show whether this seam reflects a change in acquisition (stitched datasets, setpoint or gain change, tip change) or a physical transition;
     - show the raw I(V) and the corresponding current-distance curves;
     - demonstrate reversibility (retract after approach and recover the original spectra);
     - estimate band bending for the PtIr tip on this ~100 meV-gap material;
     - estimate tip-induced force and strain at the closest approaches.
   - Without this, Fig. 3g cannot be read as a controlled variation of the excitonic order parameter at fixed defect potential. The added assumption in Fig. 4f, that the dipole strength drops "concomitantly" to 0.6 V0, is already an admission that the defect potential changes with tip height.

B. Should address

3. **The structural and charge assignment of the defect is not unique and is internally inconsistent with the formation energies.**
   - The caption of Extended Data Fig. 4 states that the Pd substitution, a Ta vacancy and a Ta-Pd interchange all reproduce the topography. The dipole argument, however, is shown only for Pd_Ta versus the Pd vacancy (Fig. 4b,d).
   - The authors should give the charge distribution and dipole for the Ta vacancy and the interchange defect, and explain why those would not also produce in-gap states.
   - Extended Data Table 1 gives Pd_Ta a formation energy of -0.948 eV under Pd-rich/Te-rich conditions. These match the nominal flux composition (molar ratio 2.0:4.5:7.5, Methods), under which the Pd vacancy has +0.756 eV. Pd_Ta should then dominate, yet the Pd vacancy is the majority defect (77.8%, Extended Data Fig. 1).
   - Only neutral defects appear to be considered. Since the mechanism rests on the defect charge, the charge state of Pd_Ta and the transition levels relative to the gap should be calculated.
   - Methods describe only the bulk DFT setup. The supercell or slab size, surface termination, k-mesh and relaxation used for the defect and STM simulations are needed.

4. **The quoted dipole moment and the link between DFT and model parameters are not quantitative.**
   - The dipole moment is given as 0.14 x 10^-12 C m per Pd atom (p. 1554). That is about 10^16 times larger than atomic dipoles (1 Debye = 3.3 x 10^-30 C m), so this is presumably a unit error.
   - The model parameters (t = 0.5 eV, mu = -1.5 eV, Delta = 50 meV, V0 = 0.24 eV, Gaussian widths 2a, lattice constant 0.25 nm, and t_c = 1.0 eV, t_v = 0.5 eV in Fig. 4g) are neither derived from the DFT dipole nor fitted to the data.
   - The model uses an isotropic 2D square lattice, but Ta2Pd3Te5 is quasi-one-dimensional. The bound states are visibly elongated relative to the chains (Fig. 2k-o, Extended Data Fig. 3).
   - The authors should:
     - map V0 and the charge geometry to the DFT charge distribution;
     - use anisotropic hoppings consistent with the ARPES and DFT bands (Fig. 1d);
     - compare bound-state energies relative to the gap edges and spatial extents along and across the chains quantitatively with Fig. 3g and Extended Data Fig. 3.
   - The coherence-length estimate of 6.5 nm should state the Fermi velocity and gap used. It should also be checked against the anisotropy.

5. **The particle-hole asymmetry is added by hand.**
   - The authors note that the measured E- peak is sharper and more robust than E+ (Fig. 3e,g). They reproduce this only by choosing a five-Gaussian charge distribution and unequal hoppings (Fig. 4g).
   - They also attribute the band-edge asymmetry to the spectator band VB2, which is absent from the two-band model.
   - The authors should include VB2, or at least show that the conclusions are robust to it. Otherwise the agreement in Fig. 4g carries little weight.

6. **There are no statistics of bound-state energies within each defect type, and the sample and tip reproducibility are not given.**
   - Fig. 2f-j and Fig. 3g show one representative per type, and the tip-height sweeps were done on "a few" non-trivial defects.
   - The authors should give histograms of E+ and E- (relative to the local gap edges) over many defects of each type. They should also state how many crystals, cleaves and tips were used, and show that the energies are reproducible.
   - In a YSR picture, defects with different dipole strengths should show systematically different binding energies, possibly approaching a level crossing. A plot of binding energy against a proxy for defect strength across alpha/beta/gamma would directly test the model.

7. **The strained-stripe control is limited.**
   - The statement that non-trivial defects in the stripe lack in-gap states (Fig. 3b,d) rests on three defects.
   - How the 2.4% strain variation is extracted from a 0.3 nm corrugation is deferred to the supplement.
   - Strain may also change local doping, or the defect's own structure and charge, so the absence of in-gap states could have causes other than loss of excitonic order.
   - The authors should give the number of defects examined and show how the defect topography compares with unstrained alpha/beta/beta' defects. Spectra of the same defect type at intermediate strain, showing a continuous shift of E+/E- with the local gap, would be far stronger than a present/absent comparison.

8. **No temperature dependence is reported.**
   - All STM data are at 4.4 K. If the in-gap states are tied to the excitonic order (T_c of about 365 K), their energy relative to the gap edges should follow Delta(T). Ordinary defect levels would instead follow the single-particle band edges.
   - Even spectra at one or two higher temperatures (for example 77 K and above 200 K) would give a test that does not involve the tip, free of the tip-field concerns in item 2.

9. **The dI/dV normalization and peak extraction are not described.**
   - Spectra are shown as "normalized dI/dV" (Figs. 2f-j, 3d-f, Extended Data Fig. 8). The normalization is not specified.
   - Normalization by I/V inside a gap can create or distort peaks near the gap edges.
   - The authors should state the normalization, show raw dI/dV for the key spectra, and explain how the energies in Fig. 3g were extracted (peak fit or maximum), with error bars.

C. Minor

10. Fig. 3g: the vertical axis is labelled "Bias (mV)" but the values (±0.05, 0.10) are in volts.
11. The text refers to "Fig. 4g (bottom)", but Fig. 4g has no top/bottom sub-panels.
12. Methods: "electric field of the tip (on the order of 10 MV)" has inconsistent units; give V/nm. The claim that the excitonic Stark effect is an order of magnitude smaller than doping should be backed by an estimate.
13. Gap values: the ARPES and Fig. 1e give about 100 meV, while the tip-height data start at about 140 meV. State which tip height corresponds to the "normal" gap and reconcile the numbers.
14. Bound-state extent: the text quotes "up to 6 nm" (p. 1552) and "approximately 5 nm" (Fig. 2 caption), and compares the theoretical 6.5 nm with "Fig. 1g". Use one quantitative definition (for example a decay length from a fit to the Extended Data Fig. 3a envelopes).
15. Extended Data Fig. 9: the 200 pA maps saturate (white regions), and the colour scales seem to differ between currents. Use a common normalization and quantify the broadening against current or tip height.
16. Extended Data Fig. 8 caption: an in-gap state of the alpha' defect inside the pseudogap "whose origin is not understood yet". Comment on whether this is consistent with the YSR picture, since the main text says the in-gap states disappear in the pseudogap regime.
17. Extended Data Table 1 caption: "rich PD" should read "rich Pd". Also clarify how "Pd-rich/Ta-rich" chemical potentials are chosen simultaneously under the stability limits.
18. Methods, ARPES: the 370 K spectrum in Fig. 1d needs its measurement conditions, since the cleave is stated at 80 K.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a self-consistent mean-field calculation of the excitonic order parameter around the dipole impurity. Include a side-by-side comparison with a non-excitonic hybridization gap, and state explicitly which observable distinguishes the two. Then test that observable experimentally, for example by mapping the local gap edges (order-parameter suppression) around the defects.
2. Make the gap tuning more convincing. Show raw I(V) and I(z), reversibility and a band-bending estimate, explain the seam in Extended Data Fig. 8, and add temperature-dependent STS as a tuning method that does not involve the tip.
3. Make the model quantitative and material-specific: use an anisotropic quasi-1D lattice, include VB2, and take the dipole potential from DFT. Then compare binding energies and decay lengths along and across the chains with statistics over many defects.
4. Correct the dipole-moment value and the unit and panel-reference errors. Also reconcile the formation-energy table with the observed defect populations under the actual growth conditions.
