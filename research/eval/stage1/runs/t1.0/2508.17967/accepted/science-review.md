**A. Must address**

1. **The absolute interaction potential and its "agreement with simulation" are partly circular, and the one independent quantity, a, disagrees.** Eq. (1) and the text on p. 1620 state that the recoil trajectories fix only the ratio F(r)/D. To convert this into V(r) in meV (Fig. 3b), the experimental dissipation constant D is set "by comparing F(a,b)(r) over the relevant range of r in the experiment and in the homogeneous case", which gives D "approximately four times larger" than in the defect-free simulation, attributed to roughness. The overall scale of the experimental curve in Fig. 3b is therefore fixed by matching it to the simulated curve, so the overlap of the orange and blue bands cannot be cited as independent validation (abstract: "in agreement with simulation results"; p. 1621: "overlapping uncertainty bands ... validate the robustness of the inverse parameter estimation method"; Conclusion: "excellent agreement with micromagnetic simulations, matching both the magnitude and range"; "the dominant contributions in both cases were identified"). The meV axis is only as good as this D. The paper itself states that a "does not depend on the value of D", so a is the clean test, and it fails: a* = 44 ± 4 nm (experiment) against 31 ± 3 nm (simulation), about 2.6–3σ and roughly 40% apart; b* = 247 ± 4 nm against 288 ± 13 nm; and the bands visibly separate for r ≳ 220 nm, where the Fig. 3b histogram shows much of the data lies. The authors should (i) state exactly how D_exp was determined and which part of Fig. 3b is an independent comparison; (ii) either determine D independently, for example from the measured v_x(J_x) slope in the coherent regime (Fig. 5, v_x = F_ext/(2αD)) with a stated spin Hall angle, efficiency factor and damping, from the measured skyrmion profile, or from FMR/ST-FMR damping on the same stack, and propagate its uncertainty into the Fig. 3b band; or report only F/D and a and make the comparison with micromagnetics on those quantities; (iii) acknowledge the discrepancy in a and discuss its likely origin (disorder, effective D, the assumed functional form, skyrmion deformation, or simulation geometry and parameters such as K_eff, DMI and J_IEC); and (iv) revise the abstract and conclusion accordingly.

2. **Pinning forces on the recoiling L/R skyrmions are omitted from the inversion, and an interaction-free recoil mechanism is not excluded.** The Methods and pp. 1619–1620 assume the post-pulse motion of L and R skyrmions is "governed solely by skyrmion–skyrmion interactions and dissipation", with pinned I skyrmions entering only as boundary positions. The paper's own evidence argues against this: pinning arises from an inhomogeneous landscape (grain structure, local K_eff, DMI and exchange variations; Methods); the spread in τ_relax between +J_x and −J_x is attributed to "the local energy landscape sampled during post-pulse recoil, arising from the combined effects of pinning and skyrmion–skyrmion interactions", the recoil "travers[ing] spatially distinct regions of this heterogeneous landscape"; recoil amplitudes and time constants "vary substantially" between classes; the L and R traces in Fig. 2d settle at a finite offset (about 10 nm) rather than returning to their starting positions; and the "immobile" class I skyrmions show the strongest recoil (Fig. 2 caption). A skyrmion displaced or elastically deformed inside its own pinning well would also relax back after the drive with an exponential, polarity-independent 3–20 ns return, even without neighbours. A restoring force comparable to the repulsion would be absorbed into a, b and the effective D, and could itself explain the factor-of-four "enhanced dissipation". The authors should: (i) bring the isolated-skyrmion, weakly interacting regime of Supplementary Section A3 into the main text or Extended Data as the null control, showing displacement versus time under matched J_x, pulse width and field, with recoil amplitude and τ_relax compared with those of L/R skyrmions in the lattice; if isolated skyrmions recoil, the extraction must include a pinning term; (ii) include a phenomenological pinning term (for example a harmonic or Gaussian well at each skyrmion's equilibrium position with fitted stiffness, or a measured landscape) in the Thiele model, show how a* and b* change and that the fit is not dominated by this term; (iii) fit +J_x and −J_x trajectories, and L and R classes, separately and show that consistent a and b result.

3. **Thermal relaxation after the pulse is a competing explanation for the 3–20 ns recoil.** Pulses of 2.8 × 10^11 A m^-2 (5 ns) to 1.2 × 10^12 A m^-2 in a Pt/Ir multilayer on a 100-nm SiN membrane, whose limited thermal conductivity the authors note (Microfabrication), will heat the strip, which then cools on a nanosecond-to-tens-of-nanoseconds timescale overlapping τ_relax. A transient temperature change alters M_s, K_eff, DMI and the degree of compensation, hence skyrmion size and equilibrium spacing; the resulting lattice relaxation would look like interaction-driven recoil but with a time constant set by heat flow, not by F/D. Heating also contributes thermal or creep components to v(J). Heating is discussed only in Supplementary Section A4. The authors should show in the main text (i) the estimated or measured peak temperature rise and cooling time for each current density and pulse width, for example from the time-resolved device resistance during and after the pulse or a thermal simulation; (ii) the skyrmion radius, ellipticity and contrast versus delay through and after the pulse, to test whether size relaxes on the same 3–20 ns scale; (iii) the dependence of τ_relax and recoil amplitude on pulse width at fixed current density (2.5, 5, 7.5 and 10 ns pulses are said to have been measured but are not shown), which would separate a thermal contribution (scaling with deposited energy) from interaction-driven relaxation (scaling with compression).

**B. Should address**

4. **Fit quality and the amount of data behind the inversion are not shown.** Fig. 3a, the only trajectory comparison shown (R1), has visible systematic offsets: in the top panel the predicted j_x > 0 line runs roughly 10–20 nm above the measured trace over the full 100 ns, and in the bottom panel both predicted lines sit about 10–15 nm above the measured y positions. The text nevertheless describes the comparison as capturing the relaxation, and the loss L2(a,b) is evaluated over an unspecified "early-time relaxation window" (Methods). The authors should state the window, show residuals for all fitted L and R trajectories, explain the offsets (starting-position error, drift of the pinned boundary, an unmodelled force), and report how many trajectories enter L2(a,b) and the resulting data-to-parameter ratio.

5. **The quoted uncertainties of a* and b* (±4 nm each) are unexplained and look too small.** Fig. 3a shows frame-to-frame position noise of about 5–10 nm at 20-nm resolution over displacements of only about 30 nm. The Methods mention a "loss landscape" without defining the uncertainty. Because b enters Eq. (1) only through the prefactor exp(b/a), a and b are strongly correlated, and independent ±4 nm errors may understate the uncertainty. The authors should show the L2(a,b) landscape in the main text or Extended Data, state the confidence-level criterion, report the a–b covariance, and state whether the 68% bands in Fig. 3b include the uncertainty in D, in the pinned-skyrmion boundary positions and in the tracked positions, or only fit statistics.

6. **Localization precision relative to the displacements analysed is not established.** Recoil amplitudes are 10–30 nm (Fig. 2c,d) against a quoted spatial resolution of about 20 nm. The authors should describe the contour or centroid algorithm, give the per-frame localization precision (for example from the scatter of static features or of I skyrmions between pulses), and explain how this noise propagates into τ_relax, a and b. In Fig. 4d velocities are position differences over 1 ns; a 5-nm localization error gives about 7 m/s velocity noise per skyrmion, so the authors should state whether the error bars reflect skyrmion-to-skyrmion spread or measurement noise.

7. **The exponential force law is imposed, not tested, and the fit's leverage over r is not shown.** Eq. (1) is assumed; neither the modified-Bessel K_1(r/λ) form common in skyrmion-interaction theory, nor a power law, nor a free-form or binned force is compared, and in a SyAFM residual dipolar and interlayer terms may contribute. Each recoil changes the inter-skyrmion separation by only tens of nanometres, yet the potential is drawn from about 150 to 400 nm, rising steeply to 300 meV at the short end where there are few data; the Fig. 3b histogram mainly shows the static distribution of separations (about 140–300 nm), not the range actually swept during recoil. The authors should plot the r range swept dynamically, restrict the plotted band or mark the extrapolated region, and compare at least one alternative functional form via L2 or an information criterion. Otherwise the "exponential decay" and "quantitatively map the interaction potential, continuously from a compressed state into a relaxed state" should be presented as a parametrization rather than a finding.

8. **The rigid-particle Thiele description conflicts with the observed skyrmion deformation.** The text reports "a breakdown of the rigid-particle approximation" near pinned skyrmions, with elongation and compression (Fig. 2b, R1), yet the inversion uses a rigid-particle Thiele equation with a centre-to-centre radial force. After the pulse, shape relaxation may release much of the energy and would also shift the fitted centre, so the extracted "interaction potential" may mix both. The authors should quantify skyrmion area and ellipticity versus time for L/R skyrmions after the pulse and show the deformation is small within the fitted window. Ideally they should also run the full inverse procedure on simulated relaxation trajectories that include a random pinning landscape and deformation, and show it recovers the known input potential (for example the static energy of fixed skyrmion pairs).

9. **Pump–probe averaging cannot by itself establish determinism, the absence of creep, or the absence of deformation.** The paper states the dynamics are "highly reproducible" and "deterministic", including "deterministic control ... even in the incoherent flow regime" (abstract), that there is "no discernible deformation", and that "a thermally activated creep regime ... is not resolved". Because contrast is averaged over billions of cycles, non-reproducible events are suppressed rather than shown absent: a skyrmion that fails to move or hops between pinning sites in some cycles would appear blurred or reduced in contrast, and its fitted centre would be biased. The authors should show the integrated XMCD contrast and apparent size of each tracked skyrmion versus delay compared with static images; static images before and after the pump–probe run to show the configuration did not evolve or train; any available single-shot images; and, for the coherent regime (Fig. 4), a quantitative deformation measure (size, ellipticity, or Delaunay bond-length distribution and lattice strain versus time) rather than contour overlays. The determinism and creep statements should be qualified accordingly.

10. **The "no inertia", "gigahertz" and "ultrafast" claims exceed what 1-ns sampling resolves.** Fig. 4d shows v_x tracking the voltage pulse within the 1-ns frame spacing with about three points per 2.5-ns pulse, and the text concludes "negligible inertia effects", "free from detectable Hall and inertial effects" and "no delay or phase shift between the sublattices" (p. 1621). For Thiele dynamics with ordinary damping the response time is far below 1 ns even for ferromagnetic skyrmions, and the inertial lags and transient sublattice separations predicted for SyAFMs (ref. 50) are likely sub-ns, so tracking the pulse does not distinguish AFM from FM behaviour. The authors should give, from their micromagnetics with J_IEC ≈ 0.38 mJ m^-2, the expected magnitude and duration of the transient sublattice separation and inertial lag, and show these would be resolved at 20 nm and 1 ns (and the 70–100 ps probe width); fit v_x(t) to the measured voltage pulse convolved with a first-order response and quote a lag time or upper bound with uncertainty; and otherwise narrow the claims to "no inertia above 1 ns". "Gigahertz-speed operation" and "ultrafast" (abstract, conclusion) should be removed or justified, since measured speeds are ≤ 50–60 m s^-1, far below the ~1 km s^-1 cited in the introduction, with nanosecond drive pulses.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

11. **Sublattice synchronization rests on separate acquisitions and partially mixed contrast.** Fe- and Co-edge movies are recorded in separate runs ("only one X-ray energy can be used at a time"), so "identical trajectories" with no delay (p. 1618, p. 1621; Supplementary Fig. 3, apparently the only evidence) requires spatial and temporal co-registration. Moreover, both sublattices contain both elements: sublattice A has Co0.6Fe0.2B0.2 and Fe0.6Co0.2B0.2 and sublattice B Co0.8B0.2, so the Co L3 image contains an opposite-sign contribution from sublattice A (roughly 20% of the Co by thickness), and Co L3 is only said to "predominantly" probe sublattice B. This reduces contrast and could bias the comparison. The authors should describe how drift and time zero were aligned between runs (for example timing to the pulse edge and registration on static features), quantify the fractional contribution of each sublattice to each edge contrast, give the per-skyrmion difference of A and B trajectories versus time with uncertainty, and state the smallest relative sublattice displacement that could be detected.

12. **The role of the class I ("immobile") skyrmions is inconsistent with their use as fixed boundary conditions.** Class I skyrmions serve as "reference scattering centres" and "dynamic boundary conditions", yet the Fig. 2 caption says they show "the strongest recoil", and Fig. 2c shows the class I average moving by about +20–25 nm during the first pulse, opposite in sign to class M (about −30 nm), then relaxing; this conflicts with Fig. 2a, which labels them as having minimal net displacement. Separately, the Methods say class M shows "minimal post-pulse relaxation", whereas Fig. 2c shows a clear ~10 nm decay for M. The authors should clarify what each class does during and after the pulse, explain why "immobile" skyrmions move against the drive, and state how the measured (rather than fixed) I positions enter the inversion and whether their motion is itself caused by the L/R skyrmions, which would make the problem many-body rather than reference-based.

13. **The velocity–current analysis and regime boundaries ignore the depinning threshold.** In Fig. 5 the linear fit of v_x (and the slopes used for θ_SkHA) appears constrained through the origin, although at 2.8 × 10^11 A m^-2 a large part of the lattice is pinned; a finite threshold or intercept would change the slope and its interpretation, including any D derived from it. Fig. 5 includes points near ±3.7–8 × 10^11 A m^-2 whose regime is not stated, and each regime is presented in detail at a single current density. The authors should state whether the intercept is free and report the fitted threshold; add the low-current mean velocity or say why it is excluded; and map the incoherent-to-coherent transition with current density (and pulse width) using a quantitative order parameter, such as the fraction of mobile skyrmions, the spread of displacements, or a Delaunay strain measure.

16. **The stringency of the skyrmion Hall angle bound and the compensation condition are not established.** θ_SkHA = 1.4° ± 1.5° comes from slope ratios over about eight or nine current values at |v_x| ≲ 50 m s^-1, and the incoherent-regime Hall angle (where pinning could deflect mobile skyrmions) is not reported.
