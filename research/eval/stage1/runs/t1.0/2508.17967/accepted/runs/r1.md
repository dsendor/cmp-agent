Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use element-selective pump–probe STXM (Fe and Co L3 edges, about 20 nm spatial and 1 ns temporal sampling) to image the nanosecond current-driven dynamics of a dense antiferromagnetic skyrmion lattice in a compensated Pt/Ir-based synthetic antiferromagnet. They report two regimes. At low current density (2.8 × 10^11 A m^-2) the flow is incoherent: mobile skyrmions are pushed against pinned ones and recoil after the pulse with time constants of 3–20 ns. At higher current density (up to 1.2 × 10^12 A m^-2) the lattice translates rigidly, with a skyrmion Hall angle of 1.4° ± 1.5°. From Thiele-equation inversion of the recoil trajectories they extract an exponentially decaying skyrmion–skyrmion repulsion, a* = 44 ± 4 nm and b* = 247 ± 4 nm. Real-space, time-resolved imaging of collective AFM skyrmion dynamics is new and of clear interest. The weakest part of the paper is the claim that the measurement gives a "quantitative" interaction potential that agrees with simulation.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- This is the first real-time, sublattice-resolved view of a dense AFM skyrmion lattice under SOT. Sublattice locking is shown directly by the inverted Fe/Co contrast, and both sublattices follow the same trajectories (Fig. 1b,c; Supplementary Fig. 3).
- The recoil dynamics in the incoherent regime are a qualitatively new observation. They show interaction-mediated relaxation that static before/after imaging cannot access.
- In the coherent regime, the velocity versus J_x data (Fig. 5) and the vanishing v_y across both current polarities show suppression of the skyrmion Hall effect in the time domain. The time-resolved velocity tracks the measured voltage pulse shape (Fig. 4d).
- The inverse-Thiele approach, which uses measured pinned-skyrmion positions as dynamic boundary conditions, is a reasonable and potentially general way to get interaction parameters out of non-equilibrium trajectories.
- The data are shared openly (Zenodo), and the analysis is described in enough detail to be reproduced.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The absolute interaction potential and its "agreement with simulation" are partly circular. Main text p. 1620 says only the ratio F(r)/D can be determined from the trajectories. The dissipation constant D used to convert this into V(r) in meV (Fig. 3b) is then set by "comparing F(a,b)(r) over the relevant range of r in the experiment and in the homogeneous case". This gives D four times larger than in the defect-free simulation, which is attributed to roughness. As written, the overall scale of the experimental curve in Fig. 3b is fixed by matching it to the simulated curve. The overlap of the orange and blue bands therefore cannot be cited as independent validation (abstract: "in agreement with simulation results"; p. 1621: "overlapping uncertainty bands ... validate the robustness"). Only the decay length a can be compared independently: 44 ± 4 nm against 31 ± 3 nm, which disagree by about 3σ. The authors should take one of two routes:
   (i) Determine D independently, for example from the measured velocity–current slope in the coherent regime (Fig. 5, v_x = F_ext/(2αD)) with the known spin Hall angle and efficiency factor, or from the measured skyrmion profile and damping.
   (ii) Report only F/D and a, and make the comparison with micromagnetics on those quantities.
   The abstract and conclusion should be revised to match, and the a mismatch should be discussed.

2. Pinning forces are omitted from the inversion, yet the data show they act on the recoiling skyrmions. Methods and p. 1619–1620 say the post-pulse motion of L and R skyrmions is "governed solely by skyrmion–skyrmion interactions and dissipation". However:
   - the L and R traces in Fig. 2d settle at a finite offset (about 10 nm) instead of returning to their starting positions;
   - the "immobile" class I skyrmions show the strongest recoil (Fig. 2 caption);
   - the paper attributes the spread in τ_relax between +J_x and −J_x to "the local energy landscape sampled during post-pulse recoil, arising from the combined effects of pinning and skyrmion–skyrmion interactions" (Methods).
   Each of these implies a local pinning or restoring force on the recoiling skyrmions that is comparable to the repulsion. If that force is left out, it will be absorbed into a, b and the effective D. This could also explain the factor-of-four "enhanced dissipation". The authors should:
   - include a phenomenological pinning term (for example a harmonic well at each skyrmion's equilibrium position, or a measured landscape) in the Thiele model and show how a* and b* change;
   - show that the fit is not dominated by this term;
   - fit +J_x and −J_x trajectories separately and show that the same a and b result.

3. The functional form and the fit's leverage over r are not demonstrated. The exponential force law of equation (1) is imposed, not tested. Each recoil trajectory changes the inter-skyrmion separation by only tens of nanometres (Fig. 3a shows position changes of about 0.03 µm). Yet the potential is drawn from about 150 to 400 nm, and the Fig. 3b histogram mostly shows the static distribution of separations, not the range of r actually swept during recoil. Fig. 3a also shows a visible systematic offset between predicted and measured trajectories, of about 10–20 nm in x and in y for the j_x > 0 case. The authors should:
   - report how many L and R trajectories enter the fit;
   - plot the r range swept dynamically;
   - compare the exponential with at least one physically motivated alternative, such as the modified Bessel K1 form common in skyrmion-interaction literature, or a power law, using the loss L2;
   - show the a–b correlation in the loss landscape in the main text or Extended Data;
   - explain the residual offset in Fig. 3a.
   Without this, "quantitatively map the interaction potential, continuously from a compressed state into a relaxed state" claims more than the data support.

B. Should address

4. Thermal and non-magnetic relaxation channels should be excluded for the 3–20 ns recoil. Joule heating during a 5-ns pulse at about 3 × 10^11 A m^-2 in a 100-nm SiN membrane will be followed by cooling on nanosecond-to-tens-of-nanosecond timescales, which overlaps τ_relax. A transient temperature change alters Ms, K_eff and DMI. That changes skyrmion size and the equilibrium spacing, which could produce an apparent relaxation of positions without any interaction-mediated recoil. Shape relaxation of the deformed skyrmions (the elongation and compression described for R1) would also shift the fitted centre. Joule heating is mentioned only as Supplementary Section A4. The authors should show the skyrmion diameter and ellipticity versus delay after the pulse, compare τ_relax with a modelled or measured thermal decay time of the membrane, and check whether τ_relax depends on pulse width (2.5–10 ns pulses are available).

5. Isolated-skyrmion control. The paper's interpretation depends on the recoil coming from interactions. The weakly interacting, dilute regime in Supplementary Section A3 is the natural null control. It should be shown quantitatively in the main text or Extended Data: displacement versus time for isolated skyrmions under the same J_x, pulse width and field, showing no recoil, or recoil of a stated, smaller magnitude.

6. The claim of "deterministic" dynamics in the incoherent regime needs support. Pump–probe averages over billions of cycles and suppresses non-reproducible events, so the method cannot by itself establish determinism. Suppose a mobile skyrmion fails to move, or hops between pinning sites, in some fraction of cycles. The averaged image would then show a blurred or reduced-contrast skyrmion, and its fitted centre would be biased. Determinism should be shown directly, for example with:
   - the integrated XMCD contrast and apparent size of each tracked skyrmion versus delay;
   - static images taken before and after the pump–probe run, to show the configuration did not evolve or train during the run;
   - any available single-shot images.
   The abstract's "deterministic control ... even in the incoherent flow regime" should be qualified accordingly.

7. The upper bounds on inertia and deformation should be quantified. The coherent-regime claims are "free from detectable Hall and inertial effects", "no delay or phase shift between the sublattices" and "no discernible deformation". They rest on 1-ns delay steps and 2.5-ns pulses, which give about three points per pulse in Fig. 4d. Predicted inertial lags in SyAFMs (ref. 50) may be well below 1 ns. The authors should:
   - fit v_x(t) to the measured voltage pulse convolved with a first-order response, and quote an upper bound on the inertial time;
   - quote an upper bound on the sublattice separation from the Fe- and Co-edge trajectories, including the uncertainty in aligning time zero between the two separate energy runs;
   - quantify skyrmion size and shape (and lattice strain, as in the Delaunay analysis of Fig. 2b) during the pulse.
   The "gigahertz-class" and "ultrafast" wording should be reconciled with the measured speeds of about 50 m/s, which are far below the about 1 km/s cited in the introduction.

8. The velocity–current analysis ignores the depinning threshold. In Fig. 5, the linear fit of v_x appears to be constrained through the origin. Yet at 2.8 × 10^11 A m^-2 a large part of the lattice is pinned. A finite threshold or intercept would change the slope and the interpretation. The authors should:
   - state whether the intercept is free;
   - add the low-current mean velocity, or say why it is excluded;
   - state which pulse widths were used at each J_x (Methods lists 2.5, 5, 7.5 and 10 ns), since pulse width and heating differ between points.

9. Current-density calibration. J_x is used quantitatively in Fig. 5, in the regime boundaries and for the SOT force, but how it is obtained is not described. The stack contains Ta(5), 25 × (Pt/Ir) repeats and Au pads, and the current split between the heavy-metal and ferromagnetic layers is not uniform. The authors should state:
   - the measured device resistance;
   - how the voltage in Fig. 4d is converted to J_x;
   - whether J_x refers to the whole stack or to the Pt layers;
   - the uncertainty in J_x.
   The same holds for the bipolar pulse amplitude in the low-current regime.

10. Single device, and how general the regimes are. The text implies one 2-µm-wide strip and one field of view, with 25 skyrmions in the coherent regime and roughly 16% of skyrmions (L and R) in the inversion. The authors should state how many devices and fields of view were measured, whether the incoherent-to-coherent crossover and the extracted a* reproduce in a second device or region, and whether results survive reinitialization of the lattice.

11. Through-thickness averaging and columnar assumptions. The XMCD signal averages over 25 repeats. The potential is given "per bilayer", V/N, which assumes rigid, uniform columnar tubes. The authors' own ref. 40 reports hybrid chiral skyrmion tubes in similar SyAFM multilayers, and the current density may differ across the stack (Ta seed, top versus bottom Pt). The authors should justify the uniform-tube assumption, for example by showing the simulated through-thickness profile under current and post-pulse. They should also discuss how depth-dependent deformation would appear in the averaged trajectories.

12. Sublattice contrast cross-talk. Sublattice A contains Co (Co0.6Fe0.2B0.2 and Fe0.6Co0.2B0.2), so the Co L3 image contains a contribution from sublattice A of opposite sign, roughly 20% of the Co by thickness. This reduces contrast and could bias the comparison of sublattice-B trajectories. It should be acknowledged and quantified when the authors claim the sublattices are synchronized.

C. Minor

13. Classification criteria. The thresholds used to assign classes I, M, L and R (displacement cut-offs and the spatial adjacency rule) should be stated quantitatively. The authors should also show how sensitive the 66/18/16% split is to these choices.

14. Class I displacement in Fig. 2c. During the first pulse the class I average appears to move by about +20 nm while class M moves by about −30 nm, which is opposite in sign. Please explain why "immobile" skyrmions move against the drive, and how this fits with Fig. 2a, which labels them as having minimal net displacement.

15. Time axes. Fig. 1e places the pulses at t = 0 and t = 1,000 ns. Fig. 4 places them at about 51 and 1,051 ns, consistent with a 1-µs spacing. Fig. 2c,d shows them at about 440 and about 1,480 ns, and the Fig. 2b colour bar runs from 440 to 455 ns. Please define the time origin for each figure consistently.

16. Prefactor in equation (1). Fixing the prefactor to 1 m s^-1 makes b a parameter that mixes amplitude and range. A short sentence noting that b sets the amplitude through exp(b/a), and is not an independent "interaction distance", would avoid confusion with the lattice periodicity line in Fig. 3b.

17. Error bars. Please state whether the 68% bands in Fig. 3b include the uncertainty in D and in the pinned-skyrmion boundary positions, or only the fit statistics. Please also state whether the error bars in Figs. 4 and 5 are standard deviations or standard errors (Fig. 5 caption says standard deviation, while the Hall angle uses the standard error of the slopes).

18. Angular-momentum compensation. The statement that γ is "nearly identical" for the CoFeB and CoB sublattices, so that magnetization and angular-momentum compensation coincide, should be supported by values or references. The residual 1.4° is attributed to γ asymmetry without an estimate.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Determine the dissipation constant D independently of micromagnetics, for example from the coherent-regime mobility in Fig. 5 or from the measured skyrmion profile. Present V(r) both in units of D and in absolute units, with that uncertainty propagated.
2. Add an Extended Data figure that tests the inversion: (i) exponential versus Bessel-type force law; (ii) fits with and without a local pinning term; (iii) separate fits for +J_x and −J_x; (iv) the r-range actually swept during recoil.
3. Show skyrmion size, ellipticity and integrated contrast versus delay in both regimes. This would rule out heating-driven size relaxation as the source of the recoil and support the reproducibility and "no deformation" claims.
4. Include the isolated-skyrmion control and a second device or field of view in the main narrative, so that the recoil can be attributed to interactions and shown to be general.
