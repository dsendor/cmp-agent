Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors use element-resolved pump–probe STXM (about 20 nm, 1 ns steps) on a compensated CoB/CoFeB–Ir synthetic antiferromagnet to image the nanosecond current-driven dynamics of a dense antiferromagnetic skyrmion lattice. They report two regimes. At low drive the flow is incoherent: mobile skyrmions are pushed against pinned ones and recoil after the pulse. At high drive the flow is coherent and viscous, with no measurable skyrmion Hall angle (1.4° ± 1.5°). From the post-pulse recoil trajectories they extract an exponentially decaying skyrmion–skyrmion repulsion by inverting the Thiele equation. Real-space, time-resolved imaging of AFM skyrmion ensembles has not been shown before. An interaction potential extracted from non-equilibrium trajectories, rather than from equilibrium statistics, would be a new and useful tool. The experiment is technically impressive. The quantitative claims about the potential, and the "no inertia / gigahertz operation" claims, are less secure than the imaging itself.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- This is the first direct nanosecond, sublattice-resolved real-space movie of current-driven AFM skyrmion lattice dynamics. Both sublattices are imaged at their own absorption edges (Fe and Co L3), which directly confirms that the two sublattices are locked during motion.
- The classification of skyrmions into immobile, mobile and boundary classes (I/M/L/R), and the observation of polarity-independent recoil, show plainly that interaction-mediated relaxation occurs in a pinned, dense lattice.
- The inverse-Thiele approach for extracting an interaction law from reproducible relaxation trajectories is new. It can in principle be applied to other particle-like textures.
- Over the range of current densities studied, the transverse velocity is suppressed and the longitudinal velocity is linear in current density (Fig. 5). This adds dynamic evidence for compensated SkHE suppression, beyond the before/after imaging used in earlier work.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The absolute interaction potential and its "agreement with simulation" depend on a dissipation constant that is not measured independently.**
   - *What is missing.* The text after Eq. (1) states that the trajectories fix only the ratio F(r)/D. The absolute D for the experiment is then inferred: "the effective dissipation constant D in the experiment is approximately four times larger than in a homogeneous system", obtained "by comparing F(r) over the relevant range of r in the experiment and in the homogeneous case".
   - *Why it matters.* If the factor of ~4 is chosen so that the experimental force matches the simulated one, then the overlap of the orange and blue bands in Fig. 3b is partly built in. It cannot also serve as validation ("The overlapping uncertainty bands between experiment and simulation validate the robustness of the inverse parameter estimation method"). The meV scale on the vertical axis of Fig. 3b is only as good as this D.
   - *What to do.*
     - State exactly how D_exp was determined.
     - Give D_exp with an uncertainty from an independent route, and propagate that uncertainty into the potential band of Fig. 3b. Possible routes: the measured v_x(J_x) slope in Fig. 5, using v = F_ext/(αD) with a stated θ_SH and α; the observed skyrmion profile; or ferromagnetic resonance or spin-torque FMR damping on the same stack.
     - Present the experiment–simulation comparison only for the quantities that are independent of D: a, and the shape of F/D.
     - Separately, the prefactor "1 m s⁻¹" in Eq. (1) is a convention, and b is defined relative to it. Say explicitly that b carries no independent physical meaning beyond setting the amplitude of F/D. As written, comparing b* between experiment and simulation reads like comparing a length scale.

2. **The fitted decay lengths do not agree, and the claim of quantitative agreement is overstated.**
   - *What is wrong.* The experiment gives a* = 44 ± 4 nm and the simulation gives a* = 31 ± 3 nm, a difference of about 2.6σ (about 40%). The amplitude parameters are b* = 247 ± 4 nm and 288 ± 13 nm. In Fig. 3b the two bands visibly separate for r ≳ 220 nm. The paper's own histogram (Fig. 3b, bottom) shows that much of the data lies in this range.
   - *Where the claims appear.* The text says the potential is in "agreement with simulation results" (abstract), finds "excellent agreement with micromagnetic simulations, matching both the magnitude and range" (Conclusion), and says the dominant contributions "were identified".
   - *What to do.*
     - Report the difference honestly and discuss its likely origin. Candidates include disorder, the effective D, deviations from the assumed functional form, skyrmion deformation, or the simulation geometry and parameters (K_eff, DMI, J_IEC).
     - The paper states that a "does not depend on the value of D", so a is the cleanest test of agreement. It currently disagrees.

3. **The inverse model neglects pinning forces on the L/R skyrmions whose trajectories it fits, and an interaction-free recoil mechanism is not excluded.**
   - *The modelling assumption.* The analysis assumes that the post-pulse motion "is governed solely by skyrmion–skyrmion interactions and dissipation". Pinned I skyrmions enter only as fixed boundary positions.
   - *The paper's own evidence against it.*
     - The paper states that pinning comes from a spatially inhomogeneous landscape (grain structure, local K_eff, DMI and exchange variations; Methods).
     - The relaxation times differ for +J_x and −J_x because the recoil "traverses spatially distinct regions of this heterogeneous landscape" (Methods).
     - The recoil amplitudes and time constants "vary substantially" between classes "suggesting differences in the local energy landscape".
     - Together these mean that pinning forces act on the L/R skyrmions during relaxation. Fitting their trajectories with a pure pairwise repulsion will absorb that landscape into a and b.
   - *An alternative mechanism.* A skyrmion that is elastically deformed or displaced inside its own pinning well relaxes back after the drive is removed even without neighbours. This would give an exponential post-pulse decay with a 3–20 ns time constant.
   - *What to do.*
     - Supplementary Section A3 (isolated skyrmions in the weakly interacting regime) is the right control. Bring it into the main text quantitatively: compare post-pulse recoil amplitude and τ_relax for isolated skyrmions under matched current density and pulse width with those of L/R skyrmions in the lattice.
     - Include a local pinning term in the Thiele inversion, for example a harmonic well at each skyrmion's equilibrium position with a fitted stiffness. Show whether a and b survive this.
     - Report the fit quality per trajectory, not only for R1 (Fig. 3a). Report how many L/R trajectories enter the loss L₂(a,b), and whether a and b fitted separately for +J_x and −J_x, and for L and R classes, are consistent.

**B. Should address**

4. **Rigid-particle Thiele description versus the observed skyrmion deformation.**
   - *The tension.* The text says that near pinned skyrmions "a breakdown of the rigid-particle approximation" occurs, with elongation and compression (Fig. 2b). Yet the inversion uses the rigid-particle Thiele equation with a centre-to-centre radial force. During relaxation, the dominant energy release may be shape relaxation of the skyrmion, not centre-of-mass repulsion. If so, the extracted "interaction potential" mixes both.
   - *What to do.* Quantify the skyrmion area and ellipticity versus time for L/R skyrmions after the pulse, and show the deformation is small during the fitted window. Alternatively, fit the micromagnetic relaxation trajectories with the same rigid-particle inversion and show that it recovers the potential obtained statically from the simulation (for example, from the energy of fixed skyrmion pairs).

5. **The tracking precision relative to the displacements analysed is not established.**
   - *The problem.* The recoil amplitudes are 10–30 nm (Fig. 2c,d), and in Fig. 3a the whole relaxation for R1 spans about 30 nm. The quoted spatial resolution is about 20 nm, and single-frame position noise in Fig. 3a looks to be about 5 nm.
   - *What to do.*
     - Describe the contour or centroid algorithm. Give the localization precision per frame, for example from the scatter of positions of static features or of I skyrmions between pulses.
     - Explain how this noise propagates into τ_relax and into a and b.
     - In Fig. 4d, velocities come from position differences over 1 ns. A 5 nm localization error gives about 7 m/s velocity noise per skyrmion, so state whether the error bars reflect skyrmion-to-skyrmion spread or measurement noise.

6. **The claims of "no inertial effects" and "gigahertz-speed" operation exceed what the data resolve.**
   - *What the data show.* The velocity follows the voltage pulse "without measurable delay" at 1 ns sampling (Fig. 4d), and no sublattice phase shift is detected "within the 1-ns time resolution".
   - *Why this is not enough.* The inertial or sublattice-separation timescales expected for these parameters (ref. 50) are likely in the sub-ns range. The relevant comparison is therefore between the predicted lag and the 1 ns (and 70–100 ps probe-width) resolution. The speeds reached (≈50 m s⁻¹ at 1.2 × 10¹² A m⁻²) are also modest compared with the 1 km s⁻¹ cited in the introduction.
   - *What to do.*
     - Give the predicted inertial timescale and the expected transient sublattice separation for J_IEC ≈ 0.38 mJ m⁻², and show it is above the detection limit. Otherwise rephrase as "no inertia above 1 ns".
     - Fit a lag time between v(t) and V(t) with an uncertainty.
     - Remove or justify "gigahertz" and "ultrafast operation" (abstract, conclusion), which the data do not test.

7. **The sublattice synchronization is inferred from separate acquisitions.**
   - *The issue.* Only one X-ray energy can be used at a time, so sublattice A and sublattice B movies are recorded in separate runs. The claim that both sublattices follow "identical trajectories" with no delay requires co-registration of the two movies in space and time.
   - *What to do.* Describe how drift and timing were aligned between Fe-edge and Co-edge runs, for example by a timing reference to the pulse edge and spatial registration on static features. Provide a quantitative comparison: the difference of A and B trajectories per skyrmion versus time, with an uncertainty. At present Supplementary Fig. 3 seems to be the only evidence.

8. **Joule heating and its post-pulse relaxation are not quantified, but they could produce or modify the recoil.**
   - *Why heating matters.* Current densities of 2.8 × 10¹¹ to 1.2 × 10¹² A m⁻² in a Pt/Ir multilayer on a 100 nm SiN membrane can heat the strip substantially. Heating is discussed only in Supplementary Section A4. As the film cools over nanoseconds after the pulse, M_s, K_eff and DMI recover, and skyrmion size and lattice spacing change. This can produce displacements on the 3–20 ns timescale that look like interaction-driven recoil. Heating also contributes thermal or creep components to v(J).
   - *What to do.*
     - Give the estimated peak temperature rise and its decay time for each current density and pulse width. Use the strip resistance during the pulse or a thermal simulation.
     - Show that the recoil τ_relax and amplitude do not scale with deposited energy in the way heating would predict, for example by comparing the different pulse widths (2.5, 5, 7.5, 10 ns) mentioned in the Methods at fixed current density.

9. **The regime boundaries and the number of devices are not shown.**
   - *What is shown.* The incoherent regime is presented at a single current density (2.8 × 10¹¹ A m⁻²) and the coherent regime in detail at a single value (1.2 × 10¹² A m⁻²).
   - *What is missing.*
     - Fig. 5 includes points near ±3.7–8 × 10¹¹ A m⁻², but which regime these belong to is not stated. A depinning threshold, which the Fig. 2 data imply, is not visible in the linear fit that passes through the origin.
     - The paper does not say how many devices were measured, or whether the incoherent-to-coherent transition and the extracted a and b were reproduced in a second strip or after re-nucleating the lattice.
   - *What to do.*
     - Add the fraction of mobile skyrmions (or the order parameter used to distinguish the regimes) as a function of J_x, with a threshold current.
     - State the number of devices and nucleation events.
     - Show at least one repeat of the potential extraction.

10. **The interpretation of the class I skyrmions is inconsistent with their role as fixed boundary conditions.**
    - *The inconsistency.* Class I is defined as "immobile", and the I skyrmions are used as "reference scattering centres" and "dynamic boundary conditions". However, the Fig. 2 caption states that "I and M skyrmion classes, with the I skyrmions showing the strongest recoil". Fig. 2c shows class I displacing by about +25 nm at the pulse and relaxing afterwards. Separately, the Methods state that class M shows "minimal post-pulse relaxation", whereas Fig. 2c shows a clear ~10 nm decay for M.
    - *What to do.* Clarify what moves during and after the pulse for each class. If I skyrmions themselves recoil by tens of nm, explain how their measured (rather than fixed) positions enter the inversion, and whether their motion is itself caused by the L/R skyrmions. The latter would make the problem many-body rather than reference-based.

11. **The choice of functional form and the range of validity of the potential are not tested.**
    - *The problem.* The exponential form of Eq. (1) is assumed. Neither alternative forms (for example, the modified-Bessel K₁-type forms often used for skyrmion–skyrmion repulsion) nor a free-form or binned force are compared.
    - *The data range.* The histogram in Fig. 3b shows that the data mainly cover r ≈ 140–300 nm. The potential is plotted down to about 150 nm, where it rises steeply to 300 meV, a region with few data.
    - *What to do.* Compare at least one alternative functional form via the loss L₂. Restrict the plotted band, or mark the extrapolated region.

12. **The Hall angle analysis relies on few points and does not separate the regimes.**
    - *The issue.* θ_SkHA = 1.4° ± 1.5° comes from fitting about eight current values. The skyrmion Hall angle in the incoherent regime (where pinning could deflect mobile skyrmions) is not reported.
    - *What to do.*
      - State whether the fit is constrained through the origin.
      - Report θ_SkHA for the low-current regime.
      - Give the residual net moment or angular momentum from the SQUID data, so readers can check whether the measured bound on θ_SkHA is consistent with the expected residual G.

**C. Minor**

13. **Velocity definition in Fig. 5.** "Mean velocity obtained from 25 individually tracked skyrmion trajectories" should be defined: peak velocity, or the average over the pulse duration. The applied pulse in Fig. 4d is roughly sinusoidal rather than flat-topped, so specify whether J_x is the peak or the mean current density.

14. **Per-bilayer normalization.** The potential is normalized "per bilayer" (V/N with N = 25). Justify that all 25 repeats contribute equally, given that STXM contrast is an average over layers. Also say whether layer-dependent skyrmion size or position (tubes, tilting) could matter.

15. **Thermal creep statement.** The statement that "a thermally activated creep regime ... is not resolved" needs justification. Pump–probe averaging over billions of cycles removes stochastic events by construction, so the absence of creep in the averaged images is not evidence that creep is absent. Rephrase accordingly.

16. **Class fractions and counts.** Give the number of skyrmions in each class behind the percentages (66% M, 18% I, 16% L/R). Supplementary Table 1 values for τ_relax would be more useful in a main-text figure or table, with uncertainties.

17. **Notation.** The notation 𝐅_(a,b) and V_(a*,b*) is cumbersome. "1 m s⁻¹" in Eq. (1) should be explained as a unit-fixing convention. The units of D (kg s⁻¹, or a dissipation tensor) should be stated.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Derive the absolute D independently, for example from the Fig. 5 slope with measured spin–orbit torque efficiency and damping. Present the potential with D's uncertainty propagated, so that the experiment–simulation comparison becomes a genuine test.
2. Run the full inverse procedure on simulated relaxation trajectories that include a random pinning landscape and skyrmion deformation. Show that the method recovers the known input potential. This would validate the rigid-particle, pinning-free assumptions directly.
3. Map the incoherent-to-coherent transition with current density (and pulse width). Use a quantitative order parameter, for example the spread of displacements or a lattice-strain measure from the Delaunay triangulation.
4. Temper the device-level claims ("gigahertz", "ultrafast", "deterministic control") to what the data show: reproducible, sub-100 m s⁻¹ coherent motion with no inertia detectable above 1 ns.
