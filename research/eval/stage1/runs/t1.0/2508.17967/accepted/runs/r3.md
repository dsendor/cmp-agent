Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors use pump–probe STXM (20 nm, 1 ns, element-selective at the Fe and Co L3 edges) to image, with nanosecond resolution, current-driven dynamics of a dense antiferromagnetic skyrmion lattice in a Pt/CoFeB/Ir synthetic antiferromagnet. They report two regimes: an incoherent regime at low current density with pinned, mobile and boundary skyrmions, where post-pulse recoil is used to extract an exponentially decaying skyrmion–skyrmion interaction potential through an inverse Thiele-equation analysis; and a coherent viscous-flow regime at higher current density with rigid-lattice translation, no measurable skyrmion Hall angle (1.4° ± 1.5°) and no resolvable inertia. Real-space, time-resolved imaging of AFM skyrmion ensembles is new, and an experimental, quantitative interaction law would interest both the skyrmion-device and the driven-lattice communities. The central quantitative result, the interaction potential, is the part most in need of support. It rests on a model in which only F/D is identified from the data, the absolute scale is fixed with help from simulation, and pinning and thermal relaxation are assumed absent after the pulse.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- First time-resolved, sublattice-selective real-space imaging of a current-driven AFM skyrmion lattice. The sublattice-resolved contrast inversion in Fig. 1b,c is a clean demonstration of the method.
- A clear phenomenological picture of two regimes (incoherent pinned/mobile coexistence against coherent flow), supported by displacement maps and class-averaged traces (Figs. 2 and 4).
- An attempt to go beyond qualitative statements and extract an interaction law from dynamics rather than from equilibrium statistics, with a comparison to micromagnetics (Fig. 3).
- Time-resolved velocity traces (Fig. 4d) that follow the drive waveform, together with a current-density sweep of v_x and v_y (Fig. 5) showing linear scaling and a skyrmion Hall angle consistent with zero.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The absolute interaction potential is not determined by the data alone; its agreement with simulation is partly built in.** Eq. (1) and the text on p. 1620 state that the scattering trajectories fix only F(r)/D. To convert to V(r) in meV (Fig. 3b), the authors infer that the experimental D is "approximately four times larger" than in a homogeneous system, and they infer this by comparing F(r) between experiment and the defect-free simulation. If D is chosen so that the experimental F matches the simulated F over the relevant range, then the overlap of the orange and blue bands in Fig. 3b, and the statement that "the dominant contributions in both cases were identified", follow in large part from that choice rather than being an independent test. Only a (the decay length) is independent of D, and a differs between experiment (44 ± 4 nm) and simulation (31 ± 3 nm) by about 2.6 combined standard deviations. The authors should (i) state explicitly how D was fixed and which part of Fig. 3b is an independent comparison; (ii) obtain D independently, for example from the measured velocity–current slope in Fig. 5 with v_x = F_ext/(2αD) and an independently estimated θ_SH and α, or from FMR damping on the same stack; (iii) present the comparison with simulation in terms of the D-independent quantity a, and the D-scaled quantity separately, with the discrepancy in a acknowledged and discussed rather than described as "excellent agreement".

2. **The post-pulse recoil is attributed entirely to skyrmion–skyrmion repulsion; pinning forces acting on the L and R skyrmions themselves are not ruled out.** The Thiele model (Methods; p. 1619–1620) includes only pairwise interaction and dissipation after the pulse, with pinned I skyrmions as boundary conditions. However, the paper itself argues that the landscape is set by grain structure and K_eff/DMI variations (Methods, Material deposition), that I skyrmions show the "strongest recoil" (Fig. 2c caption), and that relaxation times depend on polarity because "the subsequent recoil traverses spatially distinct regions of this heterogeneous landscape" (Methods). A displaced skyrmion relaxing back into its own pinning well would also give an exponential return opposite to the drive, independent of polarity. The fitted F(r) could therefore absorb a pinning restoring force. Needed: (i) the post-pulse behaviour of isolated skyrmions in the weakly interacting regime (Supplementary Section A3) shown in the main text. If isolated skyrmions do not recoil, that is the key null control; if they do, the interaction extraction must include a pinning term. (ii) A fit that includes a single-particle harmonic or Gaussian pinning term alongside Eq. (1), showing whether a, b remain stable. (iii) An explanation of why I skyrmions, which are supposed to be pinned reference centres, recoil most strongly, and how their motion is treated (they are "dynamic boundary conditions" in Methods, which means their motion is itself unexplained by the model).

3. **Thermal relaxation after the pulse is a competing explanation for the 3–20 ns recoil.** The incoherent-regime pulses (2.8 × 10^11 A m^-2, 5 ns) and the coherent-regime pulses (1.2 × 10^12 A m^-2) are applied to a stack on a 100-nm SiN membrane, whose limited thermal conductivity the authors themselves note (Microfabrication). Pulse heating changes M_s, DMI, anisotropy and the degree of compensation, hence skyrmion size and equilibrium spacing. Cooling on a nanosecond-to-tens-of-nanoseconds timescale would then drive a lattice relaxation that looks like an interaction-driven recoil but has a time constant set by heat flow, not by F/D. Heating is relegated to Supplementary Section A4. The authors should show in the main text (i) an estimate or measurement of the peak temperature rise and the cooling time of the strip (for example from the time-resolved device resistance during and after the pulse); (ii) the skyrmion radius and contrast as a function of delay through and after the pulse, to show whether size relaxes on the same 3–20 ns scale; (iii) the dependence of τ_relax on pulse width (2.5, 5, 7.5 and 10 ns are said to have been measured but are not shown), which would separate a thermal contribution (scaling with dissipated energy) from interaction-driven relaxation (scaling with compression).

**B. Should address**

4. **Predicted and measured trajectories in Fig. 3a show visible systematic offsets.** In the top panel of Fig. 3a, the solid black (predicted, j_x > 0) line runs roughly 10–20 nm above the measured blue–green trace over the full 100 ns; in the bottom panel, both predicted lines sit about 10–15 nm above the measured y positions. The text describes the comparison as capturing the relaxation dynamics. The loss L2(a,b) is evaluated over an "early-time relaxation window" only (Methods). Please (i) state the window, (ii) show residuals for all fitted L and R trajectories (not just R1), (iii) explain the offsets (starting-position error, drift of the pinned boundary, an unmodelled force), and (iv) report how many trajectories enter the fit and the resulting data-to-parameter ratio.

5. **The uncertainties of a* and b* (±4 nm each) are not explained, and appear small relative to the position noise and spatial resolution.** Fig. 3a shows frame-to-frame position noise of about 5–10 nm at a 20-nm resolution, over displacements of only about 30 nm. The Methods refer to a "loss landscape" without giving the uncertainty definition. Please show the L2(a,b) landscape in the main text or Extended Data, state the confidence-level criterion, describe whether position errors and the uncertainty in the pinned-skyrmion positions were propagated, and report the covariance between a and b. Note that in Eq. (1) b enters only as a prefactor exp(b/a), so a and b are strongly correlated; quoting independent ±4 nm errors may understate the uncertainty.

6. **The exponential form of the force is assumed, not tested.** Theory for skyrmion–skyrmion repulsion in chiral magnets commonly gives a modified-Bessel-type K_1(r/λ) dependence, which differs from a pure exponential at the separations probed (about 150–400 nm, Fig. 3b histogram), and in a SyAFM residual dipolar and interlayer terms may add other contributions. The claim of revealing "an exponential decay" should be supported by fitting at least one alternative functional form and comparing loss values (or an information criterion), or else be presented as a parametrization rather than a finding.

7. **Pump–probe averaging cannot establish the absence of stochasticity, deformation or inertia.** The paper states that the dynamics are "highly reproducible" and "deterministic" and that there is "no discernible deformation" (Conclusion; p. 1617; abstract). Because contrast is averaged over billions of cycles, non-reproducible components are suppressed rather than shown to be absent; a stochastic event in a minority of cycles would appear as blurred or weakened contrast, not as a distinct trajectory. Please quantify (i) skyrmion contrast amplitude and apparent width during and after the pulse compared with the static images, which would reveal averaging over variable trajectories, and (ii) if possible, compare with static images taken before and after a single pulse train. Similarly, "absence of dynamical deformation" in the coherent regime (Fig. 4) needs a quantitative measure (size, ellipticity or Delaunay bond-length distribution versus time), not only an overlay of contours.

8. **The time resolution limits the inertia and sublattice-synchronization claims.** Fig. 4d shows v_x tracking the applied voltage within the 1-ns frame spacing, and the text concludes "negligible inertia effects" and "no delay or phase shift between the sublattices" (p. 1621). For Thiele dynamics with ordinary damping the response time is far below 1 ns even for ferromagnetic skyrmions, so tracking the 2.5-ns pulse does not distinguish AFM from FM behaviour. The authors should give, from their micromagnetics with the measured J_IEC = 0.38 mJ m^-2, the expected magnitude and duration of the transient sublattice separation (ref. 50), and show that a separation of that size would have been resolved at 20 nm and 1 ns. Otherwise the claims should be narrowed to "below our resolution of X nm and 1 ns".

9. **Sublattice-resolved comparisons rely on separate measurements and partially mixed contrast.** Fe and Co edges are measured in separate runs ("only one X-ray energy can be used at a time"). Both FM layers contain both Fe and Co (Co0.6Fe0.2B0.2 and Fe0.6Co0.2B0.2 in sublattice A, Co0.8B0.2 in sublattice B), so each edge contrast is a weighted mixture, and Co L3 is only said to "predominantly" probe sublattice B. Statements that the two sublattices move in synchrony (p. 1618; Supplementary Fig. 3) and that no phase shift exists between them (p. 1621) therefore depend on (i) the timing calibration between runs and (ii) the degree of contrast mixing. Please quantify the fractional contribution of each sublattice to each edge contrast, state the timing alignment between runs, and estimate the smallest relative sublattice displacement that could be detected.

10. **Device statistics and the class definitions are not reported.** The paper does not state how many devices were measured, whether the incoherent and coherent regimes and the extracted (a*, b*) were reproduced on more than one strip, or whether results survive thermal cycling or re-nucleation of the lattice. The skyrmion classes (66% M, 18% I, 16% L/R; Methods) are defined by net displacement, but the threshold used is not given. Please give the number of devices and skyrmions per class, the classification criterion, and (a*, b*) for each L/R skyrmion or device separately to show their spread.

11. **The skyrmion Hall angle result is constrained by few points at modest velocity.** Fig. 5 has nine data points at |v_x| ≲ 50 m s^-1, and θ_SkHA = 1.4° ± 1.5° from slope ratios. A bound on the Hall angle is useful, but please compare it explicitly with the angle expected for an uncompensated ferromagnetic analogue at these velocities (or for the residual moment given M_s^eff = 0.85 × 10^6 A m^-1, which is not small), so that readers can judge how stringent the bound is. Also clarify whether the "full magnetization compensation" refers to M_s or to angular momentum: the effective magnetization quoted in Methods is (0.85 ± 0.15) × 10^6 A m^-1, which seems inconsistent with full compensation unless it is the average per-layer value rather than the net moment; this needs to be stated unambiguously.

12. **Field and pulse conditions during the dynamics are not stated clearly.** The static images in Fig. 1b,c are at B_z = −250 mT, while the text says the lattice is stable from −250 to +250 mT. The applied field during the time-resolved measurements, and the pulse width used for each figure (5 ns for Fig. 2, 2.5 ns for Figs. 4 and 5), should be stated in each caption, since field affects compensation, skyrmion size and therefore the interaction parameters.

**C. Minor**

13. "Ultrafast" and "gigahertz-speed operation" (abstract; Conclusion) are not shown: measured velocities are ≤ 60 m s^-1 and the drive pulses are nanoseconds long. Please rephrase or justify with an explicit estimate.

14. The linear fit of v_x against J_x in Fig. 5 is constrained (or appears constrained) through the origin, although a depinning threshold must exist given the incoherent regime at 2.8 × 10^11 A m^-2. State whether the intercept was free and report the fitted threshold.

15. Eq. (1): with the prefactor fixed at 1 m s^-1, b is the separation at which F/D = 1 m s^-1, not a "strength" in the usual sense. A sentence clarifying this would help, as would giving V(r) per bilayer and for the full 25-repeat stack consistently (Fig. 3b axis uses V/N).

16. The micromagnetic parameters (A, D_DMI, K_u, M_s, α, disorder model) used for the blue band in Fig. 3b and for Fig. 1d should be listed in the main text or Methods, with their sources (measured or assumed).

17. Fig. 2a colour scale is labelled "Δx (nm)" from 0 to 80, but the classes are defined by net displacement over the pulse; please clarify whether it is the absolute displacement and over which time interval.

18. The Delaunay triangulation analysis (Fig. 2b) is described as revealing local strain but no quantitative strain measure is extracted; either quantify (bond-length and angle distributions versus time) or present it as a visual aid.

19. The relaxation constants per class are given only in Supplementary Table 1; a compact summary of τ_relax and amplitude per class and polarity in the main text would support the claim that they "vary substantially".

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Fix D independently (from the v–J slope with independent θ_SH and α, or FMR) and recompute V(r), so that the comparison with micromagnetics in Fig. 3b becomes a genuine test.
2. Add the isolated-skyrmion recoil control and a fit with an explicit pinning term; together these would establish whether the recoil reflects interactions.
3. Report the time-dependent skyrmion size and device resistance (temperature proxy) through and after the pulse, and the pulse-width dependence of τ_relax, to separate thermal relaxation from interaction-driven recoil.
4. Present fit residuals, the L2(a,b) landscape with the a–b correlation, alternative functional forms, and (a*, b*) per skyrmion and per device, so the precision and generality of the interaction law are clear.
