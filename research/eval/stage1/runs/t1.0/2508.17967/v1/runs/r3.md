Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use element-specific time-resolved STXM (Fe and Co L3 edges) to image a current-driven skyrmion lattice in a compensated Pt/CoFeB/Ir synthetic antiferromagnet (SyAFM) with nanosecond resolution. They report two regimes. At J = 2.8 × 10^11 A/m², flow is incoherent: mobile skyrmions push against pinned ones and recoil after the pulse with 3–20 ns time constants. At J = 1.2 × 10^12 A/m², the lattice translates rigidly, with no measurable skyrmion Hall angle (1.4 ± 1.5°) and no detectable inertia. From the post-pulse trajectories, an inverse Thiele-equation analysis extracts an exponentially decaying skyrmion–skyrmion repulsion and compares it with micromagnetics. Time-resolved, sublattice-resolved imaging of a dense AFM skyrmion ensemble is new and technically impressive. The most original claim is the quantitative extraction of an interaction potential from deterministic scattering trajectories. That claim also rests on the most assumptions, and at present the evidence does not fully support it.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First real-space, nanosecond-resolved movies of a current-driven AFM (SyAFM) skyrmion lattice. Both sublattices are imaged separately by XMCD at their own absorption edges (Fig. 1b,c; Fig. 2; Supplementary Fig. S3).
- Clear qualitative contrast between a spatially fragmented low-drive response (Fig. 2a,c,d) and a coherent high-drive translation (Fig. 4a–c). This is useful phenomenology for multi-skyrmion devices.
- Direct time-domain velocity traces that follow the applied voltage (Fig. 4d), and a current-density series showing a skyrmion Hall angle consistent with zero (Fig. 5).
- An inverse-estimation framework that fits interaction parameters to measured trajectories, using measured neighbour positions as boundary conditions. This idea could be applied to other textures.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **Pump–probe averaging of stochastic events could mimic the exponential "recoil".** The whole scattering analysis assumes each frame shows one deterministic trajectory, repeated identically over billions of cycles. Relevant passages: p. 4 ("any non-reproducible skyrmion motion will average out"), p. 6 ("The dynamics are highly reproducible across all cycles, confirming their non-stochastic origin"), and p. 10 ("free from stochastic thermal activation"). Stochastic behaviour does not simply vanish in a stroboscopic average, though. Suppose a mobile skyrmion depins, or relaxes back from a metastable position, at a random time after the pulse in each cycle. The averaged contrast then shifts its centroid smoothly, approximately exponentially, while the texture blurs. That is exactly what Fig. 2c,d and Table 1 show, and it is what the Thiele inversion is fitted to. This matters especially at room temperature in a SyAFM, where the authors' own earlier work (ref. 20) reports enhanced thermal diffusion. Repeatability of the average does not prove that the individual events are deterministic.
   - Action: quantify the skyrmion contrast amplitude and apparent diameter (or edge width) frame by frame through the pulse and the relaxation window for the M, L, R and I classes. Compare them with the static images and with skyrmions that are far from any motion.
   - Action: if available, compare runs with different accumulation times or different beamtimes to test reproducibility of the relaxation itself.
   - Action: show that blurring stays within what the 20 nm resolution and tracking noise allow. Without this, interpreting τ_relax and V(r) as single-particle deterministic quantities is not established.

2. **Pinning forces are left out of the inverse Thiele analysis, yet the data show they act during relaxation.** The Methods (p. 18) and p. 10 say post-pulse motion of L and R skyrmions is "governed solely by skyrmion–skyrmion interactions and dissipation". Yet p. 9 attributes the restoring forces to "skyrmion–skyrmion repulsion and the surrounding pinning landscape". Fig. 2c also shows that the "immobile" class I moves about 20–25 nm during the pulse and then shows the "strongest recoil" (caption; Table 1 gives amplitudes of 17.5 and 20.1 nm). So class I skyrmions sit in restoring potential wells of their own. The L and R skyrmions, at the boundary of the pinned region, are presumably subject to similar local pinning potentials. Fitting their relaxation with a pairwise repulsion alone folds any pinning restoring force into a* and b*.
   - Action: include a local pinning term in the Thiele model, for example a harmonic well about each skyrmion's pre-pulse position with a fitted or independently constrained stiffness.
   - Action: show how a* and b* change when it is included.
   - Action: use the class I relaxation to constrain the pinning stiffness independently.
   - Action: report whether the I-class relaxation is consistent with the same V(r), given the I skyrmions' own neighbours.

3. **The experiment does not agree with simulation as closely as claimed, and the reported numbers are inconsistent.** The abstract states a range of "30 nm, in full agreement with micromagnetic simulations". The Methods (p. 18) give an experimental a* = (38 ± 5) nm. The value close to 30 nm, a* = (28 ± 4) nm, is the micromagnetic one (p. 10). The two ranges differ by about 1.6 combined standard deviations, so "full agreement" and "excellent agreement" (p. 15) overstate the comparison. The headline length scale quoted in the abstract appears to be the simulated rather than the measured value.
   - Action: report the experimental range in the abstract.
   - Action: state whether the 68% confidence intervals actually overlap.
   - Action: list every micromagnetic parameter (A, D_DMI, K_u, interlayer exchange, Ms per sublattice) and say which were measured independently. If D_DMI or the interlayer exchange were tuned to reproduce the observed lattice spacing or skyrmion size, agreement in b* (about 236–238 nm, close to the lattice spacing) is partly built in and is not an independent test.

4. **The rigid-particle Thiele model is used on skyrmions the authors say are deforming.** On p. 9 the authors say that skyrmions such as R1 show "a breakdown of the rigid-particle approximation, as asymmetric constraints induce spin structure deformation". Yet R1 is the example chosen to show the agreement of the rigid-particle Thiele fit (Fig. 3a). Shape relaxation, such as breathing or elongation relaxing back, shifts the measured contrast centroid independently of any centre-of-mass interaction. The relaxation timescale of internal modes in a SyAFM could fall in the observed 3–20 ns window.
   - Action: quantify skyrmion shape (aspect ratio, area) versus time during relaxation for the L and R skyrmions used in the inversion.
   - Action: show that centroid motion is not dominated by shape recovery, or restrict the fit to skyrmions with negligible deformation.

5. **The functional form and the probed range limit the "range of 30 nm" claim.** Eq. (1) imposes a single exponential, F/D = (1 m/s) exp(−(r − b)/a). In this form b only sets the prefactor, as exp(b/a), so a and b are strongly correlated. The separations actually probed (Fig. 3b, lower panel) run from about 130 to 400 nm, with most weight near 200 nm and 350 nm. The decay length a ≈ 30–40 nm is therefore extracted over distances of 4–10 a, and the strongly repulsive part shown in Fig. 3b below about 150 nm is an extrapolation.
   - Action: show the full two-dimensional loss landscape L(a, b) with the a–b correlation.
   - Action: test alternative forms, such as the modified-Bessel-function K1(r/λ) form expected for exchange-mediated texture overlap, or an exponential plus a constant. Report whether the data distinguish them.
   - Action: restrict the plotted potential and its confidence band to the r interval actually sampled.
   - Action: state how many skyrmions, trajectories and time points enter the fit, and the noise model behind the "68% confidence intervals".

6. **Heating at the higher current density is not estimated, and the coherent/incoherent distinction may partly reflect temperature.** The high-drive regime uses 1.2 × 10^12 A/m² in a roughly 30 nm thick multilayer on a 100 nm SiN membrane (Methods, pp. 16–17). Mitigation is described (1 µs delay, 10 mbar He), but no temperature rise during or after the pulse is given. Joule heating lowers pinning and changes the skyrmion size and anisotropy. It could therefore cause the depinning attributed to SOT (p. 13). Post-pulse cooling on the ns–100 ns scale could also contribute to the low-drive "recoil", since the equilibrium spacing and size depend on temperature.
   - Action: estimate the transient temperature rise, for example from resistance measured during the pulse or from thermal simulation of the membrane, for both current densities.
   - Action: show that the skyrmion size, measured over the relaxation window, is the same before and after the pulse.

B. Should address

7. **Number of devices and lattice regions.** The paper appears to rest on one 5 µm × 2 µm strip, one roughly 1 µm field of view (Fig. 2a), and about 25 skyrmions (Fig. 4). It does not say whether the incoherent-to-coherent crossover, the recoil time constants, or a* and b* were reproduced in another device, in another region of the same strip, or after re-nucleating the lattice with the bipolar pulse protocol.
   - Action: state the sample statistics.
   - Action: show at least one independent repeat of the inversion, so the extracted potential is not specific to one local pinning configuration.

8. **Table 1 contradicts the Fig. 2 caption, and the time constants lack uncertainties.** The Fig. 2 caption says "the I-skyrmions showing the strongest recoil and M types the weakest". In Table 1, however, R (21.7 and 22.7 nm) and L (19.2 and 18.9 nm) have larger amplitudes than I (17.5 and 20.1 nm). The τ_relax values carry no error bars, and some differ by large factors between polarities: R is 7.4 ns versus 18.9 ns, and L is 9.6 ns versus 3.9 ns. A symmetric bipolar drive acting on a pairwise potential does not obviously produce this asymmetry.
   - Action: give fit uncertainties, and show residuals or single- versus double-exponential comparisons.
   - Action: explain the polarity asymmetry, for example through an asymmetric pinning landscape, which would bear on concern 2.
   - Action: correct the caption.

9. **The basis for "no inertia" and "no sublattice delay" is not quantified.** On p. 13 the authors state that "no delay or phase shift between the sublattices is detected within the 1 ns time resolution, defying theoretical predictions" (ref. 43). The predicted magnitude and timescale of the transient interlayer separation and inertial lag for this stack's interlayer exchange are not given. If they are sub-nanosecond or only a few nanometres, the null result does not challenge theory.
   - Action: state the predicted delay and separation for the measured coupling, using the 650 mT spin-flop and 1.8 T saturation fields given on p. 16.
   - Action: explain how the Fe-edge and Co-edge datasets, recorded sequentially (p. 6), were registered in time and space, and with what accuracy.
   - Action: give an upper bound on inertial lag from fitting v(t) in Fig. 4d to the drive, for example by convolving with a first-order response of time constant τ_m.
   - Action: temper the claims of "robust GHz operation" (abstract, p. 15). Velocities of about 50 m/s with 2.5 ns pulses do not directly demonstrate GHz operation.

10. **The linear v(J) fit has no depinning threshold, which conflicts with the pinned regime.** Fig. 5 fits v_x linearly through the origin over ±1.2 × 10^12 A/m². The text calls this the viscous regime "where pinning is negligible" (p. 14), but points near ±4–5 × 10^11 A/m² are close to the current density at which the lattice is still partly pinned (2.8 × 10^11 A/m²).
   - Action: show the velocity in the incoherent regime on the same plot, and separate mobile from pinned skyrmions there.
   - Action: discuss the depinning threshold.
   - Action: state which sublattice Fig. 5 uses and whether θ_SkHA is the same for both.
   - Action: resolve the inconsistency between the caption ("slope of the linear fit to v_y versus v_x") and the text (the v versus J fits).

11. **The degree of compensation is not consistent with the stated parameters.** The sublattices have different thicknesses: FCB(0.4)/CFB(0.2) = 0.6 nm for A and CB(1.1) for B (Fig. 1a). Yet the Methods give the same Ms = 0.85 × 10^6 A/m "for each ferromagnetic sublattice" (p. 16). Taken literally, the moments of A and B differ by almost a factor of two. The absence of a Hall effect, and the neglect of the gyrotropic term in the inversion, rest on both magnetization and angular-momentum compensation.
   - Action: report the per-sublattice moment (Ms·t) and the net moment at remanence.
   - Action: estimate the residual gyrotropic term G_eff/D. Include it in the inversion, or show it is negligible for the y-motion seen in Fig. 3a.

12. **The conversion of V/D to energy (right axis of Fig. 3b, in meV) is not specified.** The text says V and D scale with the number of layers, but the value of D, how it was obtained (from micromagnetic profiles? from the v(J) slope with assumed θ_SH and B?), and its uncertainty are not given in the main text. The right-axis energy is also per bilayer, which assumes all 25 repeats are rigidly locked.
   - Action: give D and its source and uncertainty, and propagate the uncertainty into V.
   - Action: comment on the energy scale relative to k_BT. Near the equilibrium spacing (r ≈ 200 nm), V per bilayer is of order 10 meV.

13. **Which skyrmions serve as fixed scattering centres is unclear.** On p. 11 the text refers to "pinned M skyrmions that act as fixed neighbours", and the Methods (p. 18) refer to "surrounding pinned M skyrmions". By the definition on p. 6, M skyrmions are mobile and I skyrmions are pinned.
   - Action: clarify which class provides the boundary conditions, and whether those positions are time-dependent (the Methods say "dynamic boundary conditions").
   - Action: show which skyrmions are boundary skyrmions in Fig. 2a or 3.

14. **"No dynamical deformation" in the coherent regime is asserted, not shown.** The abstract and conclusion (p. 15) claim no discernible deformation. Fig. 4a,b overlays contours, but skyrmion size, ellipticity and nearest-neighbour distances are not plotted versus time.
   - Action: provide these quantities, for example a Delaunay bond-length distribution versus time, as in Fig. 2b for the low-drive case.

15. **The claimed novelty of the AFM skyrmion lattice and the comparison with prior work need care.** The introduction (p. 2) states that "an AFM skyrmion lattice has, to our knowledge, not yet been experimentally realized". Dense SyAFM skyrmion ensembles have been imaged before (refs. 19–22, 38).
   - Action: define precisely what is new: a lattice with hexagonal order, or time-resolved dynamics.
   - Action: reconcile the observed velocities of about 50 m/s at 1.2 × 10^12 A/m² with the much higher SyAFM velocities in ref. 21, for example by mobility per unit current density.

C. Minor

16. Fig. 1: the caption labels the device and pulse panel "(f)", but the panel is "(e)". The static images are taken at B_z = −250 mT, while the text (p. 4) describes the zero-field state. State the field applied during the dynamic measurements.

17. The pulse durations are inconsistent across figures. Fig. 1e shows 5 ns pulses at t = 0 and 1000 ns. The low-current pulse length used in Fig. 2 is not stated explicitly, and the pulse time origins differ between Fig. 2 (about 440 ns) and Fig. 4 (about 51 ns). State the pulse width, rise time and timing for each dataset.

18. Fig. 3a: the caption's colour description (purple to yellow, and green to blue) does not match the legend's colormaps. State the tracking precision of the skyrmion centre, as distinct from the 20 nm resolution, since the recoil amplitudes are 10–20 nm.

19. In p. 14, the expression F_ext = −(ħ/4pie) jθ_SH B is garbled and mixes j and J_x. Define B and the sign convention properly. Also define v_x = F_ext/2αD with consistent tensor notation.

20. Typos and wording: "scattring" (p. 3), "dynamicsand" (p. 3), "such as, such as" (p. 6), "a incoherent" (p. 6), "four cluster" (p. 6), "an radially" (p. 10), "analyis" (abstract), "SkHE effect" (p. 15). The Methods refer to both L(a, b) and L₂(a, b).

21. The STXM description claims "sub-nanosecond temporal resolution" (p. 17), but the main text gives 1 ns (p. 3). Use one consistent value, and give the frame spacing of the 2001-channel scheme.

22. Data and code are available only on request. Given the central role of the inverse method, depositing the trajectories and the fitting code would greatly strengthen reproducibility.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a blur and contrast analysis of the time-resolved frames, and a pinning-inclusive Thiele model. Together these would test directly whether the recoil reflects deterministic pairwise scattering (concerns 1, 2 and 4).
2. Repeat the inversion on a second region or device, and on the class I skyrmions. Show that a single V(r) describes all of them, with a common pinning stiffness.
3. Make the micromagnetic comparison independent: fix all parameters from measurements (SQUID, Brillouin light scattering or domain-spacing DMI, spin-flop field) before simulating, and report the experimental a* in the abstract.
4. Estimate the device temperature during and after the pulses at both current densities, and include the predicted inertial timescale for this stack, so that the "no inertia" result can be judged against theory.
