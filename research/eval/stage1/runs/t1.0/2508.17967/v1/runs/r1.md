Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports time-resolved pump-probe STXM of a current-driven skyrmion lattice in a compensated Pt/Ir-based synthetic antiferromagnet (SyAFM). Imaging is done at the Fe and Co L3 edges. At 2.8 x 10^11 A/m^2 the authors see an "incoherent" regime in which mobile skyrmions push against pinned ones and recoil after the pulse on 3–20 ns timescales. They fit these recoil trajectories with a Thiele-equation inverse method to obtain an exponential skyrmion–skyrmion repulsion with a range of about 30–40 nm. At 1.2 x 10^12 A/m^2 they see rigid lattice translation with no measurable skyrmion Hall angle (1.4 +/- 1.5 deg) and no resolvable inertia. Real-time imaging of a dense AFM skyrmion lattice under current is new and of broad interest, and so is extracting an interaction law from deterministic post-pulse trajectories. The weight of the paper rests on the claim that the recoil is governed by skyrmion–skyrmion repulsion and that the extracted potential is quantitative. At present that claim is not separated from pinning and thermal alternatives.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First time-resolved, nanosecond real-space imaging of a current-driven AFM (SyAFM) skyrmion lattice. Static before/after imaging cannot show the transient regimes reported here.
- A clear qualitative picture of two dynamical regimes (pinning-dominated incoherent flow, coherent flow). The class-resolved displacement traces (Fig. 2c,d) show reproducible recoil opposite to the prior drive for both current polarities.
- A conceptually attractive inverse method: the measured positions of the surrounding skyrmions serve as time-dependent boundary conditions in the Thiele equation, which gives an interaction law without assuming thermal equilibrium (unlike Boltzmann-inversion approaches).
- Time-resolved confirmation of a suppressed skyrmion Hall angle across both current polarities and a range of current densities (Figs. 4, 5). This is consistent with the static reports in the literature.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The inverse model has no pinning restoring force, yet the data show that pinning sets the relaxation. Eq. (1) and the Methods treat post-pulse motion as a balance between dissipation and skyrmion–skyrmion repulsion only. The paper's own data contradict this:
   (i) The "immobile" class I skyrmions are displaced by about 20–25 nm during the negative pulse and recoil back with the largest amplitude of all classes (Fig. 2c; Table 1: 17.5 and 20.1 nm, versus 12.8 and 9.1 nm for class M). This is the signature of skyrmions bound elastically to local pinning sites.
   (ii) In Fig. 3a, R1 relaxes to different final x positions after +Jx and after -Jx (about 1.085 and 1.11 um), so its rest position depends on pulse history.
   (iii) The predicted Thiele trajectories settle about 15–20 nm away from the measured ones in both x and y (Fig. 3a, solid line near 1.125 um against data near 1.11 um; y near 0.21 um against data near 0.19 um). That is a persistent force imbalance the model does not capture.
   If a local pinning well contributes a restoring force on R1 (and on the L/R skyrmions generally), the fit attributes that force to the skyrmion–skyrmion term, which biases a* and b*. This is central to the headline claim that the scattering potential was measured. The authors should:
   - add a pinning term to the Thiele model (for example a harmonic or Gaussian well centred on each skyrmion's pre-pulse position, with its stiffness as a free parameter) and report how a*, b* and the loss change;
   - use the class I recoil, where the skyrmion–skyrmion force should be small, to estimate the pinning stiffness independently;
   - show the residuals of the final positions with and without this term.

2. Thermal relaxation after the pulse is not excluded as the source of the 3–20 ns recoil. The device is a 25-repeat Pt-based stack on a 100 nm SiN membrane, driven with 5 ns pulses at 2.8 x 10^11 A/m^2 (and 2.5 ns at 1.2 x 10^12 A/m^2). Joule heating during the pulse changes Ms, anisotropy, DMI and interlayer coupling, and therefore skyrmion size and equilibrium spacing. The membrane cools on nanosecond to tens-of-nanosecond timescales, which overlap the reported relaxation times. A recoil driven by changing equilibrium sizes and positions during cooling would also be deterministic, opposite to the drive and exponential, and would not be a probe of the room-temperature interaction potential. The only mitigation described is the 1 us delay and the He gas (Methods), which addresses cumulative heating, not transient heating. The authors should:
   - estimate the peak temperature rise during and after each pulse, from the device resistance measured during the pulse (transmitted-pulse amplitude) or from a thermal model of the stack on the membrane;
   - report the time evolution of skyrmion diameter and contrast through the relaxation window;
   - show that the relaxation times do not scale with pulse energy in the way a thermal process would, for example by varying pulse length at fixed J.

3. The quantitative agreement between experiment and simulation is overstated, and the extracted parameters are not shown to be well constrained.
   - The Methods give a* = (38 +/- 5) nm and b* = (236 +/- 9) nm from experiment. The simulation gives a* = (28 +/- 4) nm and b* = (238 +/- 9) nm (p. 10). The ranges differ by about 1.6 combined standard deviations.
   - The abstract quotes "a range of 30 nm, in full agreement with micromagnetic simulations". The 30 nm is the simulated value, not the measured one.
   - Fig. 3b shows the bands overlapping only near r of about 200–250 nm and diverging below about 180 nm.
   The authors should:
   - quote the experimental a* in the abstract and describe the agreement as what it is;
   - show the full loss landscape L(a,b), including the a–b correlation, which is likely strong for an exponential with a free offset;
   - state how many skyrmions and trajectories enter the fit, and the number of data points against the number of free parameters, and define the "early-time relaxation window";
   - show fits for skyrmions other than R1, and a leave-one-out or bootstrap estimate of the parameter spread;
   - test whether the data distinguish the assumed exponential from the theoretically expected modified-Bessel (K1-like) form, or a power law, by comparing losses. Without this test, "reveals an exponential decay" is an assumption, not a result.

B. Should address

4. The Co L3 edge does not isolate sublattice B. Sublattice A is FCB(0.4)/CFB(0.2) (Fig. 1a), which contains Co, so the Co-edge XMCD signal is the Co moment of B minus the Co moment of A. The claims of "sublattice-resolved" imaging, of synchronized motion of both sublattices (Fig. S3), and of "no delay or phase shift between sublattices" (p. 13) depend on how selective the Co signal is. A relative displacement of the two sublattices would partly cancel in the Co channel rather than appear as a shift. The authors should give the Co content and Co moment fraction of each sublattice, estimate the resulting cross-contamination, and discuss what sublattice separation would be detectable given that and the 20 nm resolution.

5. The inertia and sublattice-delay conclusions are not compared with what is resolvable.
   - The Fe and Co movies are recorded sequentially, in separate runs. The paper does not say how a common time zero is established between them to well below 1 ns.
   - The 2.5 ns pulse is sampled by only a few frames (Fig. 4c,d). An inertial lag or transient interlayer separation smaller than about 1 ns or about 20 nm would not be seen.
   - The statement "defying theoretical predictions" (p. 13) needs the delay and separation that Ref. 43 predicts for this stack's interlayer coupling. If the prediction is below resolution, there is no contradiction.
   The authors should fit the displacement against the drive with a damped-mass model, quote an upper bound on the effective mass or delay time, and state the predicted values for comparison.

6. Table 1 has no uncertainties, and the polarity asymmetries are unexplained. Relaxation times for the same class differ by up to a factor of five between polarities (R: 7.4 against 18.9 ns; L: 9.6 against 3.9 ns; M: 9.9 against 5.1 ns). Without fit uncertainties it is unclear whether these differences are significant. The text uses them to argue for "differences in the local energy landscape" (p. 9). The authors should:
   - give the fit uncertainties and the number of skyrmions per class;
   - state whether the exponential includes a free offset;
   - discuss why polarity matters, for example asymmetric pinning or a history-dependent starting configuration, which ties back to concern 1.
   The classification itself (Fig. 2a) uses thresholds on net displacement that are not stated, and only the L and R classes enter the inverse fit. The class criteria and their sensitivity should be given.

7. Several quantities are needed to read the potential in energy units and to judge independence from the simulation.
   - The right axis of Fig. 3b converts V/D to meV per bilayer using a dissipation constant D. Neither the value of D nor the damping alpha and skyrmion profile used to compute it are given in the main text. Alpha does not appear to be measured, so the meV scale carries an unquantified systematic uncertainty.
   - For the micromagnetic comparison, the paper says only that the system "closely match[es]" the experiment. It does not say which parameters (A, D_DMI, K_u, interlayer coupling, alpha) were measured and which were tuned. If the simulation was tuned to the observed lattice spacing, the agreement in b* (set by the spacing) is largely built in and not independent.
   The authors should list these parameters with their sources in the main text or Methods.

8. Magnetization compensation is stated, not demonstrated quantitatively. The Methods give Ms = 0.85 x 10^6 A/m "for each ferromagnetic sublattice". The sublattices have different nominal thicknesses (0.6 nm for A against 1.1 nm for B), so equal Ms would give Ms*t differing by nearly a factor of two, which is not compensated. The suppressed skyrmion Hall angle and the argument that angular momentum is compensated (p. 15) rely on compensation. The authors should give Ms*t (or the remanent moment) of each sublattice, or the net moment of the stack from Fig. S1, and correct the statement.

9. The viscous-flow analysis ignores the depinning threshold, and the current density is not defined.
   - Fig. 5 fits vx against J with a line through the origin and calls this viscous flow where pinning is negligible. However, the incoherent, pinning-dominated regime exists at 2.8 x 10^11 A/m^2, and points near ±4 x 10^11 A/m^2 are included.
   - "Dynamic skyrmion velocity" is not defined: peak, or average over the pulse.
   - The paper does not say how J is computed (total stack cross-section or Pt layers only, and from the applied or the transmitted voltage).
   - The velocity of about 50 m/s at 1.2 x 10^12 A/m^2 is far below velocities reported for SyAFMs (Ref. 21). This deserves comment, since it bears on the spin-torque efficiency and on the device claims.
   The authors should state which data points belong to which regime, fit with a free intercept or threshold, define J and v, and discuss the mobility.

10. The absence of dynamical deformation is asserted, not measured. The abstract and conclusion claim no "dynamical deformation" in the flow regime. Fig. 4a,b shows only overlaid contours, and the trailing contours appear broadened. The authors should plot mean skyrmion diameter and ellipticity against time over the bipolar cycle, with uncertainties.

11. Reproducibility over cycles and devices is not quantified. Pump-probe averaging hides non-repeating events, as the authors note (p. 4), so "deterministic control over billions of cycles" needs evidence of how much of the signal is reproducible:
   - compare the XMCD contrast of moving skyrmions with the static contrast, frame by frame;
   - give the number of devices measured and whether the incoherent-regime recoil and the extracted potential were reproduced on a second device or after re-nucleation of the lattice;
   - show the current dependence between the two regimes, which would locate the crossover.

12. The device claims go beyond the data. "Robust GHz operation" (abstract, p. 15) is inferred from 2.5–5 ns pulses separated by 1 us, a duty cycle of about 10^-3, at velocities of about 50 m/s. The authors should either show repetition at higher rates or present GHz capability as a projection based on the absence of resolvable inertia.

C. Minor

13. The pinned and mobile labels are swapped in two places. On p. 11 the paper refers to "pinned M skyrmions that act as fixed neighbours", and the Methods (p. 18) to "the surrounding pinned M skyrmions". Elsewhere M denotes mobile and I immobile. Please correct, and state clearly which skyrmions serve as boundary conditions in the inverse fit.

14. The Fig. 1 caption refers to panel (f), but the device and pulse schematic is labelled (e).

15. The magnetic field applied during the time-resolved measurements is not stated. The static images in Fig. 1 are at Bz = -250 mT. Please give the field for Figs. 2–5.

16. The temporal resolution is described inconsistently: "1 ns" (p. 3), "sub-nanosecond" (Methods), and 70–100 ps X-ray pulses. Please state the frame spacing and the effective time resolution used in the analysis.

17. Error bars in Fig. 4c,d and Fig. 5 are not defined (standard deviation across skyrmions, or standard error).

18. The expression for F_ext on p. 14 mixes notation ("j" against J_x; "4pie"). "B" is introduced as an undefined "factor". In Fig. 5 the angle is shown with a superscript "0" instead of a degree sign.

19. Typos: "dynamicsand" (p. 3), "scattring" (p. 3), "ensembles" misspelt (p. 2), "a incoherent" (p. 6), "four cluster" (p. 6), "an radially" (p. 10), "such as, such as" (p. 6).

20. The code and data are available only on request. Given that the central result is an inverse-fitting procedure, deposit the trajectory data and the fitting code.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Fit the recoil of all L and R skyrmions jointly with a model that includes local pinning wells and skyrmion–skyrmion repulsion. Present the loss landscape and the parameter correlations, and compare exponential and Bessel-type interaction forms. This would turn the potential from an assumption-dependent fit into a robust measurement.
2. Characterize transient heating: record the transmitted pulse and estimate the temperature rise, and track skyrmion size through the relaxation window. Vary pulse length at fixed current density to separate thermal from mechanical relaxation.
3. Measure several current densities between 2.8 x 10^11 and 1.2 x 10^12 A/m^2 to map the crossover from incoherent to coherent flow. Report the fraction of mobile skyrmions and the relaxation amplitude against J.
4. Quantify how sublattice-selective the Co-edge contrast is, and put the sublattice-locking and inertia statements on a quantitative footing with explicit upper bounds.
