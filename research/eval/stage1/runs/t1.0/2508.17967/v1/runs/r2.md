Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript uses element-specific, time-resolved STXM to image the current-driven nanosecond dynamics of a skyrmion lattice in a compensated [Pt/FeCoB/Ir/Pt/CoB/Ir]×25 synthetic antiferromagnet (SyAFM). It reports two regimes. At 2.8×10¹¹ A/m² the flow is incoherent: some skyrmions are pinned, some move, and all of them recoil after the pulse with 3–20 ns time constants. At up to 1.2×10¹² A/m² the whole lattice translates coherently, with no detectable skyrmion Hall angle (1.4 ± 1.5°). The authors also fit a Thiele-equation model to the post-pulse trajectories and obtain an exponentially decaying skyrmion–skyrmion repulsion. Real-space nanosecond imaging of collective AFM skyrmion dynamics would be new and interesting, and a direct, non-equilibrium estimate of the interaction potential would be valuable. However, the central claim that the recoil is skyrmion–skyrmion scattering, and that it yields "the" interaction potential, rests on modelling assumptions that the presented data do not yet test.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- This is a technically demanding pump–probe STXM experiment on an AFM skyrmion lattice, with contrast from both sublattices (Fe and Co L₃ edges, Fig. 1b,c) and reproducible trajectories over billions of cycles.
- The data show a clear, spatially heterogeneous response to a uniform drive. The classification into classes I/M/L/R (Fig. 2a) and the Delaunay strain analysis (Fig. 2b) are a useful way to describe incoherent lattice flow.
- Fig. 4 and Fig. 5 show transverse velocity near zero over a range of current densities and both polarities in a time-resolved measurement, which adds to the static before/after studies (refs. 19, 21).
- The idea of extracting an interaction law from deterministic relaxation trajectories, rather than from thermal statistics (Boltzmann inversion, ref. 28), is attractive and could be widely applicable if validated.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The assignment of the post-pulse recoil to skyrmion–skyrmion scattering is not established, and the data contain features that point to other mechanisms.**
   - *Where.* Pages 9–11, Fig. 2c,d and Table 1.
   - *What the data show.* The text states that the recoil reflects "mobile skyrmions scatter[ing] off their pinned neighbours". Yet every class recoils, including class I, which is defined as immobile. In Table 1, class I has a larger recoil amplitude (17.5 and 20.1 nm) than class M (12.8 and 9.1 nm). The Fig. 2 caption itself says "I-skyrmions showing the strongest recoil and M types the weakest". If I skyrmions are the fixed scattering centres, they should not show the largest restoring motion. If they do move, they cannot serve as the fixed boundary conditions used in the inverse method.
   - *Other explanations the paper must rule out.*
     - Elastic restoring forces from the pinning sites themselves: a skyrmion displaced inside its pinning well relaxes back regardless of its neighbours.
     - Joule heating during the 5 ns pulse followed by cooling of the membrane. This changes Ms, K and DMI, and with them skyrmion size and equilibrium spacing; cooling on a SiN membrane can occur on ns–tens-of-ns scales.
     - Relaxation of the skyrmion shape (elongation/compression), which the authors describe for R1, moving the contrast centroid without any centre-of-mass scattering.
     - Oersted-field or field-like-torque transients at the pulse edges.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Measure the recoil in a low-density region or device, where skyrmions have no near neighbours. A scattering origin predicts much weaker recoil there; pinning or thermal origins do not.
     - Vary pulse amplitude and duration at fixed lattice configuration and show how the recoil amplitude and τ_relax scale.
     - Track skyrmion diameter versus time after the pulse, to separate breathing or thermal size changes from centre-of-mass motion.
     - Give an estimate or measurement of the transient device temperature (concern 6).

2. **The inverse Thiele extraction rests on untested model assumptions, and the reported agreement with simulation is overstated.**
   - *Where.* Pages 9–10, Eq. 1, Fig. 3 and Methods.
   - *What the model assumes.* The model contains only the pairwise repulsion and an isotropic dissipation D. It has no pinning potential, although p. 9 itself attributes the restoring force partly to "the surrounding pinning landscape". It also treats deformable skyrmions as rigid particles: the authors write that R1 shows "a breakdown of the rigid-particle approximation", yet R1 is the example fitted in Fig. 3a.
   - *Missing information about the fit.*
     - Which trajectories enter the loss, and how many.
     - How long the "early-time relaxation window" is, and whether a* and b* are stable when it changes.
     - The goodness of fit.
     - The a–b correlation. With F/D = 1 m s⁻¹·exp(−(r−b)/a), a and b are strongly covariant.
   - *Fig. 3a.* The predicted curves are offset from the data by roughly 10–20 nm in x and y (e.g. the −J_x branch: predicted about 1.125 µm, data about 1.11 µm). This suggests a missing force term or a mis-set initial condition.
   - *Numbers.* The experimental a* = 38 ± 5 nm (Methods) and the simulated a* = 28 ± 4 nm differ by about 1.6σ combined. The abstract quotes "a range of 30 nm, in full agreement with micromagnetic simulations", which matches neither value as the experimental result.
   - *Independence of the simulation.* The paper does not state whether the micromagnetic parameters (A, D_DMI, K, interlayer coupling) were measured independently or tuned to reproduce the experiment, and whether the simulated potential was extracted with the same inverse procedure. If the parameters were tuned, the agreement is partly built in.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Add a pinning term (e.g. a harmonic well per skyrmion, fitted to class I) and show whether a and b remain stable.
     - Report the full covariance and the dependence on the fit window.
     - Validate the procedure on simulated trajectories with a known potential, including pinning and deformation.
     - Quote the experimental a* consistently and temper "full agreement".

3. **The sublattice-resolved imaging, and the sublattice compensation it relies on, are not established as stated.**
   - *Co edge mixes both sublattices.* Sublattice A is FeCoB/CoFeB, which contains Co, so the Co L₃ contrast includes sublattice A as well as sublattice B (CoB). Only the Fe edge isolates one sublattice. This weakens three claims:
     - that both sublattices "move in synchrony" and show "robust sublattice locking" (pp. 6–7, Fig. S3);
     - that "no delay or phase shift between the sublattices is detected" (p. 13);
     - the "sublattice-resolved" framing (p. 4).
   - *What to do about it.* The authors should quantify the Co contribution from each sublattice, using thickness × Co concentration and XAS, or use a difference analysis. They should also state how the separately acquired Fe-edge and Co-edge movies were synchronised in time (same t₀ reference, jitter).
   - *Stated compensation is inconsistent.* Methods give Ms = 0.85×10⁶ A/m for each sublattice, while sublattice A is 0.6 nm thick (FCB 0.4 + CFB 0.2) and sublattice B is 1.1 nm (CB). Equal Ms with unequal thickness implies a roughly 2:1 moment imbalance. That contradicts "full magnetization compensation", on which the zero-gyrotropic-term assumption of the inverse method (p. 10) and the SkHE discussion both depend. Angular-momentum compensation is asserted, not measured.
   - *What the authors should do.* Clarify the per-sublattice Ms·t from the SQUID data (Fig. S1).

**B. Should address**

4. **The "absence of inertia" and "GHz operation" claims exceed the temporal resolution.**
   - *Where.* Abstract, pp. 13 and 15, Fig. 4d.
   - The velocities in Fig. 4d are finite differences at about 1 ns spacing during a 2.5 ns pulse. Inertial lags predicted for SyAFM skyrmions (ref. 43) are typically sub-ns, so a lag below about 0.5 ns would not be resolved.
   - "Tracking the voltage" compares to the applied voltage, not the current in the strip; reflections or RC filtering can shift the two.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Fit a displacement model with a finite mass or relaxation time to the Fig. 4c data and state the upper bound on the inertial time that the data allow.
     - Measure the transmitted current waveform.
     - Rephrase the claim as an upper bound on inertia. "Enabling robust GHz-class operation" is not demonstrated by 2.5 ns pulses repeated every 1 µs.

5. **The evidence for skyrmion Hall suppression and "viscous flow" needs a reference and a clearer analysis.**
   - *Where.* Figs. 4 and 5, pp. 13–15.
   - *Reference measurement.* No measurement shows that the method would detect a SkHE if one were present. An uncompensated or ferromagnetic reference stack, imaged and analysed the same way, would calibrate the sensitivity. The bound of ±1.5° should be compared with the angle expected for the measured moment imbalance (see concern 3).
   - *How θ_SkHA is extracted.* The Fig. 5 caption says θ_SkHA comes from the slope of v_y versus v_x, while the plot shows separate fits versus J_x. The method should be stated unambiguously.
   - *Is this viscous flow?* The linear fit of v_x passes essentially through zero, which implies no depinning threshold. Yet at 2.8×10¹¹ A/m² the lattice is strongly pinned, and points near 4×10¹¹ A/m² already appear in Fig. 5. Velocities of about 50 m/s at 1.2×10¹² A/m² are far below those reported for SyAFMs in ref. 21. It is therefore unclear whether the coherent regime is truly viscous flow or creep-like.
   - *What the authors should do.* State which regime each Fig. 5 point belongs to and include data around the threshold.

6. **Heating and current calibration are not characterised.**
   - The stack contains 25 repeats of Pt/Ir, sits on a 100 nm SiN membrane and is driven at up to 1.2×10¹² A/m².
   - The paper gives no temperature estimate, device resistance change, or current-distribution model. It also does not say how J is computed: which thickness, and whether shunting through Pt/Ir/Ta is included.
   - Heating is relevant to the recoil (concern 1), to the velocity–current linearity, and to the claim that results are "free from stochastic thermal activation" (p. 10). The same group reports enhanced thermal diffusion in similar SyAFMs (ref. 20).
   - *What the authors should do.* Report the resistance-based temperature rise during and after the pulse and the expected cooling time of the membrane.

7. **Determinism is assumed, not checked.**
   - Pump–probe averaging means partially stochastic events appear as blurred or reduced contrast, not as missing events. A trajectory fitted by a deterministic Thiele model could be an average over several distinct pathways.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Show the skyrmion contrast and apparent size versus time in the time-resolved frames, compared with static images.
     - Compare static images taken before and after the pump–probe run.
     - Report any skyrmions whose contrast fades during the cycle.
   - This is needed to support "dynamics that are reproducible over billions of cycles" and "deterministic control ... even in the incoherent flow regime".

8. **The number of devices and regions is not stated.**
   - The paper does not say how many strips, regions or lattice configurations were measured. It does not say whether the incoherent regime, the recoil timescales, and a*, b* recur in a second device or after re-nucleating the lattice.
   - The potential is extracted from L and R skyrmions in one field of view.
   - *What the authors should do.* Report at least one independent repetition, plus the spread in a* and b* across skyrmions or regions.

9. **Table 1 and the class-averaged recoil need uncertainties and an explanation of the asymmetry.**
   - Table 1 gives τ_relax and amplitudes without error bars.
   - Class R changes from 7.4 ns to 18.9 ns, and class L from 9.6 ns to 3.9 ns, between polarities. This is not discussed, although it bears directly on the potential being "reproducible" and on the claim of recoil "irrespective of the applied polarity".
   - It is not shown whether single exponentials describe the data, or whether several timescales are present.
   - The class thresholds used in Fig. 2a are not given, so the class averages depend on an unstated choice.
   - *What the authors should do.* Provide fit uncertainties and residuals, the class-assignment criteria, and a discussion of the L/R and ±J_x asymmetries.

10. **The conversion of V/D to energy (right axis of Fig. 3b) is not documented.**
    - The value of D, how it was obtained (computed from the static profile, from micromagnetics, or from α), and its uncertainty are not given.
    - Without these, the meV scale cannot be checked against k_BT or against literature.
    - Equation 1 also implies that only r ≳ 150 nm is constrained by data (histogram in Fig. 3b). The steep part of the curve below that should be marked as extrapolation.

11. **Through-thickness uniformity and the "no deformation" claim are not quantified.**
    - STXM gives a projection through 25 repeats. Hybrid or non-uniform skyrmion tubes have been reported in related SyAFMs (ref. 39).
    - The claims of "no discernible deformation" (p. 15) and "uniform translation without internal deformation" are not backed by a measured quantity such as diameter or ellipticity versus time with error bars.
    - *What the authors should do.* Add this quantity and comment on whether depth-dependent dynamics could be hidden by the projection.

12. **Field conditions are unclear.**
    - Fig. 1b,c are taken at B_z = −250 mT. The text describes zero-field nucleation and stability between ±250 mT.
    - The field at which the dynamic measurements of Figs. 2–5 were made is not stated. This matters for skyrmion size, spacing and the extracted potential.

**C. Minor**

13. P. 11 refers to "pinned M skyrmions that act as fixed neighbours", and Methods to "surrounding pinned M skyrmions". By the paper's own definitions, the pinned class is I. Please correct this, since it confuses which skyrmions provide the boundary conditions.
14. The Fig. 1 caption refers to panel "(f)", but the figure has (e). The Fig. 2 caption has "biopolar".
15. The driving-force expression on p. 14, F_ext = −(ħ/4pie) j θ_SH B, has garbled notation, and "B" is undefined. Please also define D (dissipative tensor) and α in v_x = F_ext/2αD.
16. The grey vertical lines in Fig. 3b (about 150 and 195 nm) are not explained. The "25% compression to 25% expansion" should state the equilibrium spacing it refers to.
17. The time resolution is quoted as "1 ns" (p. 3), "sub-nanosecond" (Methods) and 70–100 ps probe pulses. Please state the effective resolution of the analysed movies and the frame spacing.
18. The claim that "an AFM skyrmion lattice has, to our knowledge, not yet been experimentally realized" (p. 2) should be reconciled with refs. 20, 22 and 38, which report dense AFM/SyAFM skyrmion ensembles.
19. Fig. 4 says "all 25 observed skyrmions" while the text says "approximately 25". Please give the exact number and the field of view.
20. The Fig. 1e schematic shows a 1000 ns spacing, whereas Methods say a "1 µs delay". Please give the pulse rise and fall times and the repetition rate explicitly.
21. Data and analysis code are available only "upon reasonable request". Given that the central result is a fitting procedure, depositing the trajectories and the inverse-estimation code would strengthen reproducibility.
22. Typos: "ensembles" ("enembles"), "dynamics and" ("dynamicsand"), "scattering" ("scattring"), "analysis" ("analyis", in the abstract), "a incoherent", "four cluster".

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a control in which the scattering partner is absent: an isolated skyrmion, or a sparse region of the same device under identical pulses. Report its post-pulse response, to separate interaction-driven recoil from pinning-well, thermal or deformation relaxation.
2. Extend the inverse model to include per-skyrmion pinning wells. Benchmark the full pipeline on micromagnetic trajectories with a known potential, then report a*, b* with covariance, fit-window dependence, and spread across many L/R skyrmions and at least two devices.
3. Quantify the measurement's sensitivity limits: the transient temperature from device resistance, the upper bound on inertial lag from a mass-model fit, the SkHE sensitivity from a non-compensated reference stack, and the sublattice purity of the Co-edge signal.
4. Reconcile all reported numbers (a* in the abstract, main text and Methods; Ms·t of each sublattice; the class definitions), and calibrate the device-oriented claims ("GHz operation", "deterministic control") to what 2.5–5 ns pulses at 1 µs spacing actually demonstrate.
