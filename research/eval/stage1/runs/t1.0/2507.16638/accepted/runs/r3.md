Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors propose "ultrafast X-ray sonography": a laser-launched bipolar strain pulse crossing a film whose two coexisting structural phases give separate Bragg peaks. The phase-specific transient strain then shows where each phase sits in depth and in the plane. They apply the method to the laser-driven AFM–FM transition in a 44 nm FeRh(001) film measured at the EuXFEL MID instrument. By comparing measured sonograms with modelled ones for five discrete nucleation scenarios, they conclude that FM domains nucleate near the surface with an out-of-plane height of 19 ± 2 nm at 7.7 mJ cm⁻², and that the domains become laterally incomplete at the lowest fluence. Using the propagating strain pulse as a depth marker for phase coexistence is a neat, general idea, and the qualitative result (the AFM phase is compressed first, so the FM phase sits near the surface) is convincing. The quantitative claims, however, rest heavily on a constrained model, and the paper does not yet show how unique they are or how large their uncertainty really is.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The concept is clear and general (Fig. 1). It needs no dedicated transducer and in principle applies to any phase transition with two separable diffraction signatures.
- The raw data are high quality. Two Bragg peaks are well separated (Fig. 2b), the phase-specific strain transients are qualitatively different (Fig. 2c), and unpumped reference images within each pulse train track slow drifts.
- The qualitative conclusion follows almost directly from the data without modelling: the AFM peak is compressed first, so the AFM phase sits at the back and the FM phase at the front. This rules out pure in-plane coexistence (scenario II).
- The thermophysical model parameters were calibrated in an earlier experiment on the very same sample, and both data and code are publicly available.
- A fluence series (Figs. S3/S4) gives a physically sensible trend from in-plane-heterogeneous (scenario IV) to near-surface-layer (scenario III) nucleation.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The 19 ± 2 nm domain height is largely fixed by the scenario constraint and the integrated-intensity volume fraction, not measured independently by the strain pulse. In scenario III the Methods impose D_FM = V*_FM with V*_FM = 0.46, taken from integrated intensities. With an effective FeRh thickness of 44 − 5.5 ≈ 38.5 nm, that already gives ≈ 18–20 nm. The χ² minimisation in Fig. 4c is then over V*_FM, which is the same quantity. So the "out-of-plane domain size of 19 nm" is to a large extent a restatement of the measured FM fraction once a fully continuous near-surface layer is assumed. Fig. S2 varies the domain height directly, but the main text should state clearly which numbers the strain pulse determines independently and which follow from Eq. (2)–(3) plus the scenario choice. Requested: free fits in which D_FM and A_FM vary independently, so that V_FM = D_FM·A_FM is not fixed. Fit the strain dynamics alone, ignoring integrated intensity, and show the resulting two-dimensional χ²(D_FM, A_FM) map. Only such a fit shows that sonography itself pins down the depth.

2. The paper does not show that scenario III is statistically better than scenario IV, or than intermediate cases. In Fig. 4c the χ² curves for III and IV lie close together; their minima differ by roughly 10%. In Fig. 3, every scenario except II and V deviates from the data by amounts of similar size (grey areas in Fig. 3a, c, d). χ² is defined without measurement uncertainties (χ² = Σ|ΔI|²), so it is not a reduced chi-square, and its absolute differences cannot be read as significance. Scenario IV also uses an arbitrary choice, D_FM = 1.3 V*_FM. A continuum of D_FM/V*_FM values between 1 (scenario III) and 1/V*_FM (scenario I) should be scanned instead. Requested: weight χ² by the shot-noise / train-to-train uncertainty in I(t, q_z) obtained from the unpumped references or repeated delays. Report a reduced χ² with confidence intervals and scan D_FM continuously. State whether III and IV (and I) differ significantly at 7.7 mJ cm⁻² and at each fluence of Fig. S4.

3. The ±2 nm uncertainty leaves out model systematics. The quoted uncertainty comes only from the curvature of χ² against V*_FM (Fig. 4c). Yet the model includes several adjusted or assumed inputs that act directly on depth:
   - the optical penetration depth, "optimized" for the new incidence angle;
   - an ad hoc 5.5 nm non-diffracting bottom layer introduced "for overall better agreement";
   - a fixed nucleation stress of 1.32 GPa;
   - the sound velocity of 5.0 nm ps⁻¹ taken from literature;
   - an assumed vanishing in-plane expansion of the FM unit cell (used to set q_z0^FM = 4.196 Å⁻¹).

   Requested: a sensitivity analysis showing how the best-fit domain height and the III/IV discrimination shift when each of these is varied within plausible bounds. The optical penetration depth matters most, because the paper's own interpretation is that the depth of the FM domain equals the depth at which the excitation exceeds threshold. If the penetration depth is free, the fitted domain height and the excitation profile are degenerate.

B. Should address

4. The lateral fluence gradient within the X-ray footprint can itself produce in-plane heterogeneity. The X-ray beam is 50 µm, but at 21.9° incidence its footprint along the beam is ≈ 50/sin 21.9° ≈ 135 µm. That is comparable to the pump FWHM (136 × 142 µm), and the pump also arrives at ≈ 25°. Over the probed area the local fluence, and hence the local V_FM and transition depth, therefore varies substantially. An incoherent sum over a Gaussian fluence distribution would look like lateral coexistence (A_FM < 1) and a spread of domain heights. This bears directly on the low-fluence assignment to scenario IV and on the interpretation of the "lateral heterogeneous transition temperature". Requested: state the footprint and pump-spot geometry on the sample, and include the fluence distribution over the footprint in the model, or show that it is negligible.

5. The model assumes a single nucleation time while the Discussion invokes depth-dependent ones. Eq. (3) and the model fix τ = 8 ps everywhere in the film. In the Discussion, however, the depth-dependent rise times seen in earlier UXRD work are explained by an 8 ps and a 50 ps pathway acting at different depths. If slower nucleation occurs deeper, the time-dependent D_FM(t) is no longer the simple one assumed in scenarios I–V, and the fitted depth over 0–35 ps could be biased. Requested: test a model with a depth-dependent nucleation rate, or at least two time constants, and show whether the conclusion survives. Otherwise, temper the claim that the data "relate" to these pathways.

6. Model physics that could mimic the scenario signatures is left out. The authors neglect changes of sound velocity, density and acoustic impedance at the AFM/FM boundary, and any electronic (non-thermal) stress contribution that differs between phases. They argue these effects are small in FeRh, but they would produce partial reflections at the phase boundary, which is exactly the kind of feature that separates the scenarios. Requested: a quantitative estimate, using the reported phase-dependent elastic constants (ref. 44), of the reflection coefficient at the AFM/FM interface and its effect on the modelled peak positions. Requested in addition: one model run that includes it.

7. Strain in finite columnar domains and the FM reference lattice constant. The FM strain is referenced to q_z0^FM assuming no in-plane expansion, so the full 0.6% volume change goes into c. For ~20 nm-high columns embedded in an AFM matrix with lateral size ~25 nm (the mosaic block size), in-plane clamping will be partial and the out-of-plane expansion will lie between the uniaxial and isotropic limits. That changes the FM peak position, the strain transient, and so the inferred geometry. The claim that sonography "is not necessarily sensitive to the shape of the domains" should be backed by a calculation for at least one non-columnar or partially relaxed geometry. The 1D model cannot capture lateral strain coupling between domains, which is significant at 25 nm lateral size.

8. Sample integrity and reproducibility. The Methods say the bottom 5.5 nm of FeRh apparently no longer contributes to the Bragg peak, "probably the result of interface diffusion ... due to too strong laser excitations at a high-repetition rate". So the sample changed irreversibly under the beam. Requested:
   - whether the sample changed during the measurement series (for example, AFM peak intensity and width at negative delays over time, or the order in which fluences were measured);
   - whether the 7.7 mJ cm⁻² sonogram was reproduced on a fresh spot;
   - whether the degradation also affects the top interface with Pt.

   Only one sample is measured, so these checks are the only evidence that the result is not tied to a damaged region.

9. The in-plane domain size claim is not backed by the data shown. The Discussion states a "matching in-plane size of the nucleating FM domains (supplementary material S4)" with the ~25 nm mosaic blocks, and concludes that static granularity is crucial. Yet the Conclusion says lateral dimensions are accessible "indirectly, if at all", and the method as presented determines only A_FM, not a lateral length. Requested: explain how a lateral size was extracted, for example from Bragg peak widths or diffuse scattering along q_x, with uncertainties. Otherwise, remove or soften the claim.

10. Effective temporal resolution compared with the nucleation dynamics. The strain pulse takes ~8–9 ps to cross the film at 5 nm ps⁻¹, comparable to the τ = 8 ps nucleation time. The phase distribution therefore changes while the marker crosses it, which is the opposite of the paper's own stated requirement that the heterogeneity change "slower than the strain pulse propagation". Requested: discuss how far this limits the picosecond time resolution claimed in the abstract and how the time-resolved quantities D_FM(t) and A_FM(t) are constrained. The model sets them analytically rather than extracting them.

C. Minor

11. The Introduction says the results "directly visualiz[e]" near-surface nucleation. The localisation is model-based (concerns 1–3), so "infer" would be more accurate. The same applies to the title's "reveals".

12. Fig. 2c shows FM strain only when the FM peak's integral exceeds 10% of the total. Please state how the AFM/FM pseudo-Voigt fits behave at early delays, where the peaks overlap and the FM peak is weak. Please also show fit uncertainties (error bars) on the strain transients, which currently have none.

13. Fig. 4a: the axis label "q_z (Å)" should be Å⁻¹. Fig. 4c uses V*_FM in the axis label while the text writes V*_FM inconsistently in italic and upright. Define the "standard error" of V*_FM = 0.46 and say how it was obtained.

14. Methods: please give the pump spot size on the sample (projected) as opposed to the beam diameter, and the X-ray footprint, so that fluences can be reproduced. Clarify how the 3.9–11.7 mJ cm⁻² range was calculated from 1–3 µJ, given that the angle of incidence is 25°.

15. Base temperature is 300 K with T_c ≈ 370 K, and pulse trains arrive at 56.25 kHz. Please give the estimated quasi-static temperature rise within a train, not just a statement that the references show "negligible fluctuations" (Fig. S7). The threshold depth depends directly on the starting temperature.

16. Scenario IV's choice D_FM = 1.3 V*_FM is described as "an example". If it is kept, justify it; otherwise replace it by the continuous scan suggested in concern 2.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Present a two-parameter (D_FM, A_FM) fit with noise-weighted χ² and confidence regions, plus a systematic error budget from the main model inputs (penetration depth, dead layer, sound velocity, nucleation stress). This would turn the 19 ± 2 nm figure into a defensible measurement.
- Validate the method on a case with known answer: for example, a static thermal-equilibrium coexistence state, or a bilayer with known thicknesses of two distinguishable lattices. The demonstration would no longer rely solely on a model of the very transition under study.
- Use the double-pulse scheme proposed in the Discussion, or a dedicated transducer, to decouple the strain-pulse launch from the transition drive. Probing at several delays after nucleation would test the depth assignment with a cleaner, more nearly unipolar marker.
- Show the fluence dependence (Figs. S3/S4) in the main text, together with the depth predicted from the threshold fluence and the optical absorption profile. This independent comparison would strengthen the proposed link between the optical excitation profile and domain height.
