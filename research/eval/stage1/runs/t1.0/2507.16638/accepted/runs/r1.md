Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper introduces "ultrafast X-ray sonography". Femtosecond hard-X-ray diffraction at EuXFEL follows the separate Bragg peaks of the AFM and FM phases of a 44 nm FeRh(001) film, while a laser-launched bipolar strain pulse passes through the layer. Because each phase reports the strain averaged over the region it occupies, the phase-specific strain responses carry information on where the phase sits. By comparing the sonogram with 1D elastic and dynamical-diffraction simulations of five nucleation scenarios, the authors conclude that at 7.7 mJ cm^-2 FM domains nucleate as columns in the near-surface region with an out-of-plane height of about 19 ± 2 nm, and that at the lowest fluence the FM phase stays laterally incomplete. Using the strain pulse as a depth marker for a transient structural phase is an attractive idea, and the FeRh question it addresses (surface-confined versus depth-propagating nucleation) is relevant. Whether the specific numbers are robust depends on how well the model and the scenario comparison constrain them.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear, general concept (Fig. 1) that turns an existing technique (UXRD with phase-resolved Bragg peaks) into a probe of where a phase sits in depth, with no special sample preparation.
- High-quality XFEL data. Two well-separated Bragg peaks are followed with ps resolution (Fig. 2b). Unpumped reference shots within each pulse train are used to exclude heat accumulation (Fig. S7).
- The thermoelastic parameters were calibrated earlier on the same sample below the transition threshold (refs. 18, 29), which limits the number of new free parameters.
- Pure in-plane (II) and backside (V) nucleation are excluded qualitatively from the AFM/FM strain responses (Fig. 2c, Fig. 3b,e). These exclusions appear robust.
- A fluence series (Figs. S3, S4) gives a physically sensible picture: in-plane heterogeneity at low fluence, a near-surface continuous FM layer at higher fluence. It is also plausibly linked to the optical absorption profile.
- Data and code are publicly available.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The headline "≈20 nm domain height" seems to be set mainly by the fixed final FM volume fraction and the scenario assumption, not measured independently by the sonography. In scenario III the Methods impose D_FM(t) = V*_FM, and V*_FM = 0.46 is fixed from experiment. With d_layer = 44 nm this gives D_FM·d_layer ≈ 20 nm (or ≈ 18 nm if the 5.5 nm "inactive" bottom layer is excluded). The quoted uncertainty of 2 nm is also taken from the χ²(V*_FM) dependence (Fig. 4c). So the sonography appears to choose among discrete parameterisations (I–V), and the number then follows from the volume fraction. Scenario IV is represented by a single arbitrary choice, D_FM = 1.3 V*_FM ("as an example", Fig. 3 caption and Methods), not by a scan. Fig. S2 varies the domain height at t = 9 ps only.
   - Why it matters: the abstract claims that modelling the strain response "identifies the nucleation of domains of approximately 20 nm height". To support that wording, the height must be a fitted quantity constrained by the strain signatures.
   - What to do: fit D_FM and A_FM (with V_FM = D_FM·A_FM) as independent parameters over the full sonogram, ideally together with the depth position of the FM region. Show the two-dimensional χ² map (D_FM, A_FM) and state the confidence region. Then say explicitly how much of the 19 nm figure comes from the strain dynamics and how much from V*_FM.

2. The statistical basis for preferring scenario III over I and IV is not established. In Fig. 3a,c,d the deviations (grey areas) of scenarios I, III and IV look similar in size. In Fig. 4c the minima of the global residuals for III, IV and I differ by roughly 10–20% (≈0.085 versus ≈0.09–0.10). The residual χ² = Σ|ΔI|² is unweighted, with no noise model and no count of degrees of freedom. The text calls the comparison "quantitative" and says III "optimally describes" the data.
   - Why it matters: if the χ² difference between III and IV (or I) is within what noise, the 10% intensity threshold, or uncertainty in the fixed model parameters (concern 3) can produce, then neither the out-of-plane-versus-mixed conclusion nor the domain height is determined.
   - What to do: weight χ² by the measured shot-to-shot or delay-to-delay uncertainty and report reduced χ² values. Give a model-selection criterion (for example a likelihood ratio or information criterion, accounting for the different number of effective parameters). Show how the ranking of I/III/IV changes when the fixed inputs are varied within their uncertainties. Plotting residual maps ΔI(t, q_z) for each scenario, alongside Fig. S1, would show which delays and features actually discriminate between them.

3. The scenario discrimination depends on fixed or ad hoc model inputs whose influence is not tested. The model fixes:
   - the nucleation time constant τ = 8 ps, taken from earlier work;
   - the transformation stress, 1.32 GPa, applied instantaneously at nucleation;
   - an unchanged sound velocity and acoustic impedance across the AFM/FM boundary;
   - unchanged thermoelastic coupling in the FM phase.

   It also adjusts the optical penetration depth ("optimized") and assumes the bottom 5.5 nm of FeRh does not contribute to the Bragg peak, "for an overall better agreement". The last two choices act directly on the depth question. The absorption profile determines where the thermal stress and the near-surface strain come from, and a dead bottom layer changes both the effective thickness and the acoustic path that distinguishes surface from backside nucleation (III versus V, I versus III). The Methods say the conclusions "do not depend decisively" on these details but show no evidence.
   - What to do: provide a sensitivity analysis. Recompute χ² for scenarios I, III and IV while varying the penetration depth, the dead-layer thickness (0 to about 8 nm), τ (including the slower ~50 ps component the authors mention in the Discussion), the transformation stress, and a plausible change of v_s or density in the FM phase. List which parameters were free and which fixed in each fit (Table S1 should mark this). State how the "optimized" penetration depth was obtained, and whether it was optimised under the same scenario that is then selected.

B. Should address

4. The FM strain reference and in-plane clamping. The FM strain is defined relative to q_z = 4.196 Å^-1, "the expected lattice constant of the FM phase in case of a vanishing in-plane expansion". The nucleation stress is modelled as a 1D expansive stress equivalent to a 0.6% isotropic expansion. For columns about 25 nm wide (the mosaic block size), with height about 20 nm, the lateral constraint is neither fully clamped nor free. Partial in-plane relaxation changes the static FM out-of-plane lattice parameter and the size of the stress launched into the film. Both shift the FM peak position, which the paper treats as a sensitive measure of D_FM (Fig. 4a discussion).
   - What to do: discuss how much in-plane relaxation of nanoscale columns is expected or observed (static data from ref. 30). Show how the selected scenario and D_FM change if the FM reference q_z and the transformation stress correspond to partially relaxed columns.

5. Lateral fluence inhomogeneity within the X-ray footprint. At 21.9° incidence, the 50 µm X-ray focus covers about 130 µm along the beam direction. This is comparable to the 136 × 142 µm FWHM laser spot, which is itself elongated on the sample at 25° incidence. The probed region therefore sees a range of fluences.
   - Why it matters: given the strong fluence dependence of V*_FM and of the scenario (Fig. S3/S4), this naturally produces a distribution of FM depths across the footprint. That could mimic a mixed in-plane/out-of-plane heterogeneity, or bias the single D_FM extracted. It also affects the low-fluence conclusion that in-plane heterogeneity reflects local threshold variation.
   - What to do: report the footprint and spot geometry with orientations, and estimate the fluence spread across the probed area. Include a fluence-averaged version of scenario III (D_FM varying with local fluence) in the comparison.

6. The technique's own validity condition is only marginally met. The Discussion says sonography requires the heterogeneity "to change slower than the strain pulse propagation". Here the transit time through the film is 44 nm / 5 nm ps^-1 ≈ 9 ps, while the FM phase forms with τ = 8 ps. The strain pulse is also launched by the same excitation that drives the transition, and the analysis focuses on t ≈ 9 ps (Fig. 4a), when the FM fraction is still growing.
   - Why it matters: the simple decoding of Eq. (1) (d_phase = Δt_phase/v_s) cannot be used directly, and the result rests entirely on the forward model and its kinetic assumptions (concern 3).
   - What to do: state this limitation clearly in the main text, and temper statements such as "directly quantifies the transient out-of-plane dimension". Show that the conclusion holds for kinetic models other than a single exponential with randomly distributed nucleation delays.

7. No independent validation on a sample with known heterogeneity. The method is demonstrated on one film at one thickness. No reference shows that the forward model recovers a known depth profile under matched conditions. The below-threshold calibration (refs. 18, 29) tests the thermoelastic model but not the phase-localisation step.
   - What to do: if possible, add a measurement on a structure with a known two-phase or two-lattice-constant depth profile, or a different FeRh thickness, where the predicted D_FM changes in a known way. At minimum, run a synthetic recovery test: generate sonograms from a known D_FM/A_FM, add realistic noise, and show that the fitting procedure recovers the input and separates III from IV.

8. The fluence-dependent conclusions are central but appear only in the supplement, and are compared only qualitatively. The text says D_FM "can be approximated by the depth until which the optical excitation overcomes the equilibrium transition threshold" for fluences ≥ 5.2 mJ cm^-2, and that A*_FM = 0.66 at 3.9 mJ cm^-2.
   - What to do: bring a summary panel into the main text showing fitted D_FM and A_FM against fluence, with uncertainties. Overlay the threshold depth calculated from the absorption profile and the deposited energy density, so the reader can see whether the agreement is quantitative.

9. Sample stability during the experiment. The Methods attribute the inactive bottom 5.5 nm to FeRh/W interdiffusion "due to too strong laser excitations at a high repetition rate". This suggests the sample changed during the beamtime.
   - What to do: say whether the sonograms used here were recorded before or after this damage. Show reproducibility, for example the same fluence at the start and end, or repeated delay scans. Check whether the static Bragg peaks (position, width, AFM/FM ratio before time zero) drifted. Interdiffusion could also make the FM fraction or the transition temperature depth-dependent near the substrate, which bears on the I/III/V discrimination.

10. The in-plane domain size claim needs its derivation. The Discussion states that the in-plane size of the nucleating domains matches the mosaic blocks (supplementary S4), while the Conclusion says lateral dimensions are accessible "only indirectly, if at all".
   - What to do: in the main text, explain how the lateral size is obtained (for example from Bragg peak width or q_x profiles), how finite-size broadening is separated from inhomogeneous strain broadening, and what its uncertainty is. Otherwise, remove the statement.

11. The magnetic interpretation goes beyond the data. The Discussion links the structural results to the delayed rise of macroscopic magnetisation and to alignment of nucleated domains by domain-wall motion. No magnetic probe was used, and "the local magnetic order to emerge hand in hand with the structural changes" is an assumption.
   - What to do: present this clearly as a hypothesis, or remove it, and keep the conclusions to the structural phase.

12. Data selection and the 10% display threshold. Shots are kept only if the HIREX intensity is above 60% of the self-seeding mean and the normaliser is above 2/3 of its mean. The FM strain is shown only once the FM peak exceeds 10% of the integrated intensity (Fig. 2c).
   - What to do: say what fraction of shots was kept. Confirm that the selection does not correlate with delay or energy jitter: a small photon-energy shift moves both Bragg peaks in q_z, which is exactly the observable. State whether the early-time FM data (below 10%) were included in the χ² of Fig. 4c, since the earliest delays are where nucleation-depth information should be strongest.

C. Minor

13. Fig. 4a: the q_z axis label reads "(Å)" and should read Å^-1. In Fig. 4c the axis label V*_FM uses a different notation from the text, which uses a calligraphic V with an asterisk.
14. Eq. (1) and Fig. 1 assume a single sound velocity for both phases and neglect reflections. Say explicitly in the main text, near Eq. (1), that FeRh does not meet these assumptions: there is direct excitation of the layer and reflections at the Pt/FeRh/W interfaces.
15. Fig. 3 caption, scenario IV: explain why the factor 1.3 was chosen, or replace it with the best-fit value (see concern 1).
16. Fig. 2c: give error bars or the shot-noise level of the extracted strains, and state which fit model (single or double pseudo-Voigt) produced them, so that strain features can be compared with noise.
17. The incoherent averaging over nucleation delays weighted by Eq. (3) assumes uncorrelated nucleation sites. Add a sentence on how a finite lateral correlation, such as nucleation at defects or dislocations (ref. 48), would change the averaged sonogram.
18. The statement that only "pure symmetry changing transitions" are not limited by the speed of sound is too general. Volume changes in nanoscale domains can occur faster than the sound transit across the film. Please qualify it.
19. The Methods give the X-ray energy and bandwidth but not the effective time resolution, including pump–probe jitter. Please state it, since the early-delay features below 5 ps matter for the comparison.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Replace the discrete scenario comparison with a continuous fit over D_FM, A_FM and the FM region's depth position. Report confidence regions, a noise-weighted χ², and a synthetic-data recovery test.
- Add a parameter-sensitivity figure or table for the fixed and adjusted inputs (penetration depth, dead layer, τ, transformation stress, FM elastic constants, in-plane relaxation). This would show which conclusions are robust.
- Move the fluence dependence into the main text as D_FM(F) and A_FM(F) with the calculated threshold depth overlaid. This is the strongest physical test of the surface-nucleation picture.
- Demonstrate the proposed two-pulse scheme, or a measurement on a second thickness, even briefly. That would separate the strain marker from the phase-transition drive and support the claim of generality.
