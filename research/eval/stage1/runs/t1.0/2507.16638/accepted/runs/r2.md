Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper introduces "ultrafast X-ray sonography". A propagating picosecond strain pulse acts as a marker, and the phase-specific shifts of two coexisting Bragg peaks are used to work out where in a thin film each structural phase sits. The authors apply it to the laser-driven AFM-to-FM transition in a 44 nm FeRh(001) film measured at EuXFEL/MID. By comparing measured and modeled sonograms for five nucleation scenarios, they conclude that FM domains nucleate as near-surface columns of about 19 ± 2 nm height (scenario III) at 7.7 mJ cm⁻². The idea of reading phase heterogeneity from the phase-resolved strain response is appealing and broadly applicable. It goes beyond earlier UXRD work that tracked only volume fractions. The strength of the quantitative conclusion, however, depends entirely on a forward model with several fixed or tuned ingredients.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A conceptually clean and general idea (Fig. 1, Eqs. 1–2) that turns intrinsic or laser-generated strain pulses into a depth marker for coexisting phases. It needs no special sample structure.
- High-quality XFEL data in which both AFM and FM (002) Bragg peaks are resolved with sub-ps resolution over ~35 ps (Fig. 2b,c).
- The phase-specific strain transients by themselves rule out pure in-plane heterogeneity and backside nucleation (Fig. 2c, Fig. 3b,e). That is a robust qualitative result.
- The full sonogram modeling (UDKM1DSIM plus dynamical diffraction, incoherent averaging over nucleation delays) is a careful attempt to use peak shape and amplitude, not only position.
- Data and code are publicly available.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The extracted domain height may be degenerate with the optical penetration depth, which was tuned in the model. The Methods state that the thermophysical parameters were taken from earlier calibrations, but "only the optical penetration depth is optimized to account for the different incident angle". In scenario III, the FM thickness D_FM is set by the depth to which the excitation exceeds the transition threshold. The text says so itself: "D_FM can be approximated by the depth until which the optical excitation overcomes the equilibrium transition threshold". The thermal stress that launches the strain pulse also follows the same absorption profile. So the fitted penetration depth and the fitted 19 nm FM depth are not independent quantities: both shape the same strain profile near the surface.
   - The authors should state the optimized penetration depth and compare it with the value expected from FeRh optical constants at 800 nm and ~25° incidence.
   - They should show a two-dimensional χ² map over (penetration depth, D_FM), or at least the χ² minimum in D_FM for several fixed penetration depths.
   - Without this, the claim that the sonogram measures a "domain height", as distinct from the absorption depth that the model already encodes, is not established.

2. The quoted ±2 nm uncertainty reflects only the χ² curvature in the final FM volume fraction V*_FM (Fig. 4c), not the model systematics.
   - The domain height depends on several fixed inputs:
     - the sound velocity (5.0 nm ps⁻¹, taken from ref. 44 and assumed the same in both phases);
     - the 1.32 GPa transition stress;
     - Grüneisen parameters and the two-temperature model;
     - the fixed nucleation time τ = 8 ps;
     - the ad hoc assumption that the bottom 5.5 nm of the film does not contribute to the Bragg peak.
   - Each of these shifts strain-pulse transit times, and hence D_FM (Eq. 1). A 5–10% change in v_s alone corresponds to ~1–2 nm.
   - The authors should vary each parameter within its literature or calibration uncertainty, refit, and report a systematic error budget alongside the statistical one. The abstract's "approximately 20 nm" should then carry that combined uncertainty.

3. Scenarios III and IV are only weakly discriminated, and scenario IV is set up arbitrarily.
   - In Fig. 4c, the χ² minima for III and IV differ only modestly, and the absolute χ² is not normalized by noise. The reader therefore cannot judge whether the difference is significant.
   - Scenario IV fixes D_FM = 1.3 V*_FM with no justification ("As an example, we chose…").
   - A fairer test treats D_FM and A_FM as continuous free parameters, constrained only by V_FM = D_FM·A_FM. It would then report the χ² landscape over the whole mixed family (III being the limit A_FM → 1), using all delays rather than only t = 9 ps as in Fig. S2.
   - χ² should be weighted by the measurement uncertainty (shot-to-shot, i0-normalized). A reduced χ² or likelihood ratio should be given so that "scenario III optimally describes" becomes a statistical statement.
   - The central claim — near-surface columns that merge into a continuous ~19 nm layer — rests on this discrimination.

4. The bottom 5.5 nm of the FeRh layer is assumed not to contribute, which suggests the sample was damaged or changed during the experiment.
   - The Methods attribute this to "interface diffusion of FeRh and W due to too strong laser excitations at a high-repetition rate". This means the sample was altered by the measurement itself. The paper does not show static diffraction or reflectivity before and after the beamtime, nor any check that the response was stable over the acquisition (e.g., early versus late repeats at the same fluence).
   - The assumption also directly changes the film geometry that separates scenario III from V and sets the acoustic reflection times.
   - The authors should give evidence for the 5.5 nm figure (post-mortem XRR or TEM, or a fit with this thickness as a free parameter). They should show that the conclusions hold if it is set to zero or varied, and show that the sonogram is reproducible across the measurement time.

B. Should address

5. The X-ray footprint may sample a range of laser fluences, which could mimic lateral heterogeneity.
   - The X-rays are focused to 50 µm at 21.9° grazing incidence, giving a footprint of ~134 µm along the beam. This is comparable to the laser FWHM of 136 × 142 µm.
   - The probed region therefore spans a sizable range of local fluence, so V*_FM and the threshold depth vary across the footprint. This lateral fluence gradient produces an in-plane distribution of D_FM that resembles the mixed scenarios and broadens the Bragg peaks.
   - The paper should state whether the 7.7 mJ cm⁻² is peak or footprint-averaged fluence. The model should integrate over the actual footprint-weighted fluence distribution.
   - This matters especially for the low-fluence assignment of scenario IV (A*_FM = 0.66 at 3.9 mJ cm⁻²). That incomplete in-plane coverage could simply reflect footprint edges below threshold.

6. Heat accumulation within the pulse train and the effective initial temperature are not documented.
   - Six pump pulses at 56.25 kHz (17.8 µs spacing) and 10 Hz trains are averaged. Any residual heating from earlier pulses in the burst raises the initial temperature toward the ~370 K transition. That would change V*_FM and the threshold depth.
   - The authors mention unpumped reference images and Fig. S7, but they should show the sonogram, or at least the static peak position and V*_FM, separately for each pulse index in the burst. They should also estimate the inter-pulse temperature rise.
   - Fig. 2c shows a slightly negative AFM strain before and near t = 0. This should be explained: it could be a reference-position issue or residual heating.

7. The extracted FM strain at early delays has no error bars and has thresholding issues.
   - The FM strain is plotted only once its integral exceeds 10% of the total intensity. The scenario comparison in Fig. 3, however, relies strongly on the 4–10 ps window, where the FM peak is weak and two pseudo-Voigt fits may be biased by overlap with the AFM peak.
   - The authors should give fit uncertainties for q_z of both peaks (Fig. 2c, Fig. 3). They should also check that the fitting procedure applied to the simulated sonograms reproduces the model's true average strain, which would test fitting bias.

8. Several model simplifications that directly affect the strain marker are neglected without quantitative justification.
   - The FM/AFM boundary is treated as acoustically transparent, and phase-dependent sound velocity, electron–phonon coupling and thermal expansion are ignored. The authors argue these are small in FeRh (refs. 40, 44), but they give no numbers.
   - A phase-dependent v_s or elastic constant would change both the transit times (Eq. 1) and add reflections at the phase boundary, exactly where D_FM is read out.
   - The authors should quote the literature differences in elastic constants between phases and show a simulation that includes them.
   - Likewise, the stress is assumed to appear "instantaneously" upon nucleation, and nucleation follows a single exponential with τ = 8 ps. Yet the text invokes 8 ps and 50 ps pathways (ref. 37). τ should be refit, or a two-component rise tested.

9. The scenario set leaves out time-dependent domain depth.
   - In all scenarios except I, D_FM is constant in time once nucleated. In scenario I a continuous layer grows into the depth.
   - A natural alternative is columns whose height grows with time, e.g., propagation of the FM phase at a finite front velocity from a near-surface nucleus. The conclusion that the depth is set by the excitation profile "rather than a propagation of the FM phase into the depth" requires testing such a model explicitly.
   - The 35 ps window is short compared with the 50 ps component mentioned, which limits sensitivity to slow depth growth. That limitation should be stated.

10. The interpretation of the in-plane domain size goes beyond what this experiment measures.
   - The paper states that the in-plane size of the nucleating domains matches the mosaic crystal blocks (~25 nm) and points to "the crucial role of static structural heterogeneity". The method, however, is by the authors' own account largely insensitive to lateral dimensions ("can only be accessed indirectly, if at all").
   - The supporting analysis is only in Supplementary S4. Either the in-plane sizing should be presented in the main text with its uncertainty, or the conclusion should be phrased as a consistency statement.
   - Similarly, the phrase "quasi-three-dimensional sensitivity" in the Conclusion overstates what a one-dimensional model with an in-plane coverage factor provides.

11. Single sample, single geometry, and no matched below-threshold control in the same beamtime.
   - All results come from one 44 nm film. The thermophysical parameters were calibrated in earlier experiments (refs. 18, 29), at different incidence angles and possibly different sample conditions, given the damage noted in concern 4.
   - A sub-threshold fluence sonogram measured during the same run would validate the strain model without the phase transition, under identical conditions. Fig. S3 data at 3.9 mJ cm⁻² is still above threshold.
   - A second film thickness (e.g., thicker than the penetration depth by a larger margin) would directly test whether D_FM tracks the excitation depth rather than film-specific structure.

12. The fluence dependence (Figs. S3, S4) supports a key physical conclusion but is only in the supplement.
   - The statement that "D_FM can be approximated by the depth until which the optical excitation overcomes the equilibrium transition threshold" is a quantitative prediction. D_FM(F) should follow ln(F/F_th) times the penetration depth.
   - A main-text plot of the fitted D_FM and A_FM against fluence, with this prediction overlaid, would greatly strengthen the physical interpretation.

C. Minor

13. Fig. 4a: the q_z axis is labeled "(Å)". It should be Å⁻¹.

14. The abstract says "approximately 20 nm" while the text gives 19 ± 2 nm. Use one consistent value with its uncertainty.

15. The strain definition η(t) ≃ (q⁰⁰²_z,0 − q⁰⁰²_z(t))/q⁰⁰²_z(t) should state which reference q_z,0 is used for the AFM phase (pre-excitation average?). The FM reference is the "expected" 4.196 Å⁻¹ assuming vanishing in-plane expansion; the sensitivity of Fig. 2c to that assumption should be noted.

16. Clarify whether the 2 nm Pt capping layer and 8 nm W layer are included in the acoustic and optical model, with which parameters. Table S1 is referenced but the main text should list the key values (v_s, Grüneisen ratios, penetration depth).

17. The Methods describe the incoherent averaging over nucleation delays "weighted by the probability according to Eq. (3)". Specify whether the domains in different lateral positions are assumed to be acoustically independent (no lateral strain coupling between neighboring ~25 nm columns). That assumption is non-trivial for columns narrower than the film thickness.

18. In Fig. 3, describe the gray shading quantitatively (e.g., integrated |Δq_z|) so the reader can compare scenarios without relying on the eye.

19. The data selection (HIREX > 60% of mean SASE intensity, Jungfrau > 2/3 of mean) should be accompanied by the fraction of shots retained, and a check that the selection does not bias the pump–probe signal.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Replace the discrete scenario comparison with a continuous fit over (D_FM, A_FM, τ, penetration depth). Use a noise-weighted χ² and report correlations and the full systematic error budget for the domain height.
2. Validate the strain model with a below-threshold sonogram and before-and-after static characterization measured in the same beamtime. Show the sonogram's reproducibility over time and across the pulse train.
3. Model the actual fluence distribution across the X-ray footprint, and present the fluence dependence of D_FM and A_FM in the main text against the threshold-depth prediction.
4. Put the method's sensitivity limits in one place (minimum resolvable thickness set by v_s × temporal resolution, the maximum delay window, the role of interface reflections, insensitivity to lateral size). This would help readers apply the method to other systems.
