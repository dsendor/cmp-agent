# Merged review

## A. Must address

1. **The pump fluence varies laterally across the probed area, which can mimic the lateral and depth heterogeneity attributed to nanoscale nucleation (Methods, pp. 9–10).** The pump is 136 × 142 µm FWHM at ≈25° incidence. The X-ray beam is collimated/focused to 50 µm but hits the sample at 20.9°, so its footprint along the beam is ≈50 µm / sin(20.9°) ≈ 140 µm, essentially equal to the pump FWHM. The probed region therefore samples local fluences from the peak down to roughly half of it or less; at 7.7 mJ cm⁻² parts of the footprint are well above threshold and parts are near or below it. The paper assumes the opposite: "the large footprint of the pump pulse compared to the footprint of the probe pulse ensures a laterally homogeneous excitation" (p. 10), and "higher fluences … transform the entire near-surface region" (p. 7). Because the transition has a sharp fluence threshold, a Gaussian fluence profile alone produces a final in-plane coverage A*_FM < 1 and a depth D_FM that varies across the footprint — a macroscopic version of scenarios III/IV unrelated to nanoscale nucleation. This bears directly on the scenario ranking (Fig. 4c,d; Ext. Data Fig. E3), on V*_FM and the fluence assignment, on the low-fluence interpretation (A*_FM = 0.66 attributed to regions with the lowest transition temperature), on the identification of D_FM with the depth at which excitation exceeds threshold, and on the extra FM peak broadening used to size the domains. The authors should report the measured X-ray and pump footprints on the sample in both directions (including projection) and any relative pointing jitter, and state whether quoted fluences are peak or footprint-averaged; compute the fluence distribution sampled by the X-rays; include it in the sonogram model as a weighted incoherent sum over local fluences, each with its own D_FM and V_FM; and show whether scenario III still beats a laterally graded scenario I and whether the A*_FM = 0.66 result survives — or show quantitatively why the gradient is negligible. Ideally, repeat a key delay scan with a smaller X-ray footprint or larger pump spot.

2. **The scenario space is discrete, hand-built and over-constrained, so "scenario III" is a choice among five options rather than a measurement of D_FM(t) and A_FM(t) (Figs. 3, 4; Methods "Modelling of sonograms").** Each scenario fixes the relation between D_FM and A_FM by hand: D_FM = 1 (II), D_FM = V*_FM from t = 0 (III, V), or D_FM = 1.3 V*_FM chosen as "an example" (IV). Each assumes a time-independent D_FM, a sharp phase boundary and columns of uniform height; no scenario lets columns grow into the depth over time, although the text promises extraction of D_FM(t) and A_FM(t) "through the non-equilibrium pathway" (p. 3). The most natural alternative — a graded depth profile set by the optical absorption profile (red shading in Fig. 2a), in which the local FM fraction V_FM(z) falls continuously with depth and column height varies from site to site — is not tested. Surface nucleation is largely what exponential absorption in a 44 nm metal film predicts anyway; the non-trivial claim is the columnar, laterally heterogeneous early stage with a sharp depth cutoff, and that is distinguished from alternatives mainly by the residual at t = 9 ps (Fig. 4b,c), where D_FM is scanned only at that fixed delay. The headline "FM phase nucleates at the surface as narrow columnar domains" holds only if no physically reasonable unlisted heterogeneity fits equally well. The authors should fit a continuous, time-dependent parametrisation — e.g. D_FM(t) and A_FM(t) as free functions on a coarse time grid, D_FM(t) = D* f(t) with D* free and A_FM = V_FM/D_FM, or a depth-graded V_FM(z, t) tied to the local deposited energy density — and report the best fit with its confidence region (e.g. in (D*, growth-law) space). At minimum, add a graded, absorption-driven scenario and a scenario III variant with a distribution of column heights, and show the residual against D_FM and the time-resolved χ²_t for all delays, to check whether the preference for III comes from a narrow window around the strain-pulse exit (≈9 ps).

3. **The statistical significance of the residual ranking is not established (Fig. 4c,d; Ext. Data Fig. E3; p. 6).** χ² is an unweighted sum of squared intensity differences, with no noise estimate, reduced χ², number of degrees of freedom or uncertainty, and it does not account for the known systematic misfit of the FM peak width (E1c), which adds a common term to every scenario. The separations between scenarios I, III and IV are small: at 7.7 mJ cm⁻² the minima are ≈0.060 (III), ≈0.067 (IV) and ≈0.082 (I) in E3c (roughly 0.09 vs 0.10–0.12 in Fig. 4d); at 5.2 mJ cm⁻² (E3b) III ≈ 0.030 vs IV ≈ 0.032; at 3.9 mJ cm⁻² (E3a) IV ≈ 0.009 vs III ≈ 0.012, with I, III and IV within ≈0.005 near the measured V*_FM; and in Fig. 4a curves I, III and IV are hard to tell apart. The headline conclusion is a model-selection result, so without an error model it is unclear whether III vs IV, or III vs I at low fluence, is significant. The authors should weight χ² by the measured shot-to-shot and delay-to-delay intensity noise; give degrees of freedom, uncertainties on the minima and a likelihood-ratio or information-criterion comparison (e.g. bootstrap over pulse trains, shots or delays, or comparison of independent delay scans); and state explicitly which parameters were free for each scenario.

4. **The model omits phase-specific thermoelastic effects that act directly on the observable used to locate the phase boundary (Methods, p. 10; p. 5).** The amplitude and timing of the average FM strain are what discriminate D_FM ("the average strain … is a sensitive measure of the relative thickness", p. 5). Yet the model neglects the latent heat consumed by the transition, the change in electronic heat capacity and electron–phonon coupling in the FM phase, the different (reduced) thermal expansion of FM FeRh, and the electronic band-structure modification, and fixes the 0.6 % volumetric FM expansion and the Grüneisen parameters. Each changes the stress in exactly the near-surface FM region; latent heat in particular is a sizeable fraction of the deposited energy at these fluences, and at V_FM ≈ 0.46 these effects are not obviously minor for the strain amplitude. The statement that the identification "does not depend decisively on the details of the shape of the strain pulse" is asserted rather than demonstrated. The authors should repeat the scenario comparison with plausible bounds on these parameters (e.g. latent heat from calorimetry, FM thermal expansion from Ref. 32, FM expansion and Grüneisen parameters within their uncertainties) and show that the ranking of III over I and IV survives.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

5. **The 30 ± 10 nm lateral domain size rests on one unexplained broadening interpreted with an under-determined toy model (abstract; p. 8; Methods pp. 10–11 "Estimating the in-plane domain dimension"; Ext. Data Figs. E1c, E4).** The size is obtained by matching an "additional broadening of the FM Bragg peak by 0.06 Å⁻¹" along q_z, which the 1D model does not reproduce, to a five-site model of Poisson-coupled in-plane expansion. (i) The 0.06 Å⁻¹ value is hard to reconcile with E1c, where the FM widths are ≈0.012–0.03 Å⁻¹ (offset for clarity) and the data–model difference is below ≈0.01 Å⁻¹; the authors should state how and at which delays it was obtained. (ii) Other broadening sources are not excluded: depth-varying strain or c-axis gradient within the FM volume; a distribution of column heights and hence of finite-size widths, including a rough FM layer of thickness D_FM·44 nm ≈ 20 nm; the lateral fluence gradient (concern 1); inhomogeneous strain from clamping at FM/AFM interfaces and grain boundaries; inhomogeneous heating and transition temperatures (Ref. 42); the excluded bottom 5.5 nm (concern 6); the neglected FM thermal expansion and latent heat (concern 4); and the mosaic grain-size distribution. (iii) The inferred size nearly equals the ≈25 nm mosaic crystallite size (Ref. 32): FM "domains" may simply be grains transforming as units, in which case the coherent elastic coupling between neighbouring sites assumed in E4 (in-plane expansion propagating and being "erased" at the speed of sound) would not apply across grain boundaries, and nucleation would not be stochastic. (iv) The Poisson ratio and elastic constants used are not stated, and the ±10 nm uncertainty is estimated, not derived. (v) "Columnar" needs a defined column height: with D_FM ≈ 0.46 × 44 nm ≈ 20 nm, the domains are about as wide as they are tall. The authors should show the excess broadening against delay with error bars together with the predicted broadening for several l_FM (e.g. 10, 20, 30, 50/60, 100 nm) on the same axes; discuss and bound the alternative mechanisms, including the grain-boundary picture; if possible use the in-plane (q_x, q_y) peak shape, diffuse scattering or post-mortem imaging (e.g. XMCD-PEEM or MFM) as an independent check; and either derive the uncertainty or present 30 nm in the abstract as a model-dependent, order-of-magnitude estimate.

## B. Should address

6. **Two tuning choices that affect the depth result are not justified independently (Methods pp. 9–10).** The bottom 5.5 nm of FeRh is assumed not to contribute to the Bragg peak "for an overall better agreement", attributed to FeRh/W interdiffusion "due to too strong laser excitations at high-repetition rate"; and the optical penetration depth is "optimised" freely. Both directly affect the excitation depth profile and the strain-pulse transit times, and the dead layer is an ad hoc adjustment of exactly the depth region that separates scenario V (backside nucleation) and scenario I from III. The authors should give independent evidence for the dead layer (static XRD/XRR or TEM before and after the beamtime), show the fits and scenario ranking with the exclusion removed or varied, and state the fitted penetration depth with its uncertainty, compared with ellipsometry values for FeRh at 800 nm and 25° incidence.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

7. **Laser-induced sample modification and heat accumulation under burst-mode excitation are not ruled out (Methods pp. 9–10).** The sample is pumped at 56.25 kHz within 10 Hz trains at up to 11.7 mJ cm⁻², and the authors themselves invoke interface diffusion from "too strong laser excitations" — i.e. irreversible change during data collection. Given FeRh's thermal hysteresis, the base temperature may rise along the train (a baseline closer to 370 K would change V*_FM, the threshold and the depth profile), and some FM phase may persist between pulses 17.8 µs apart. The thermoelastic calibration from Ref. 26 predates the damage yet is reused, and the pumped data are scaled to an unpumped reference that could itself drift. The authors should show the unpumped (002) profile and pre-time-zero sonogram at the start and end of the series and along the pulse train (no FM peak, unchanged AFM position), the dependence of the signal on pulse index within the train, any post-beamtime static XRR/XRD or M(T) characterisation, when the damage occurred relative to the data shown, whether the fluence series (E2, E3) came from fresh or exposed spots, and the order in which fluences were measured, so the fluence series is not confounded with progressive damage.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **The nucleation kinetics and the volume fraction are fixed inputs, yet the data deviate from them in the window that decides the scenario (Eq. 3; p. 5; Ext. Data Fig. E1a).** τ = 8 ps is taken from the literature and applied in every scenario at all fluences. In E1a the FM intensity overshoots to ≈0.4 near 5 ps and dips before rising again, and the AFM intensity falls faster than the single exponential — in the < 10 ps window that drives the selection. V_FM(t) itself is inferred from integrated AFM/FM intensity ratios, which assumes equal structure factors and Debye–Waller factors at all delays, ignores dynamical/extinction differences between a thin surface layer and a full film, and assumes transient strain does not move intensity out of the integration window. Since V*_FM sets D_FM in scenario III, these assumptions enter directly. The authors should justify them, let τ (and possibly a delayed onset) vary per scenario and fluence within its uncertainty, propagate the V*_FM uncertainty into the comparison, explain the intensity overshoot (e.g. as extinction or dynamical diffraction), and show the early-delay deviations do not drive the ranking.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

9. **The in-plane expansion used to extract the domain size is left out of the model used to choose the scenario (pp. 8, 10).** The authors argue that laterally heterogeneous nucleation unlocks in-plane expansion, which via the Poisson effect changes the out-of-plane lattice constant of FM domains, yet scenarios II–V are simulated with a purely 1D model, and the discrimination relies mainly on the FM peak position and AFM peak width at ≈9 ps. E4b shows in-plane strains up to 0.4 %, comparable to the 0.6 % transition strain, so the Poisson shifts are not negligible for laterally heterogeneous scenarios and could change the ranking. Include this effect at least approximately in the scenario simulations, or show it does not affect the ranking, so that scenario selection and domain sizing use the same physics.
Raised by: r1

10. **The AFM peak-width discriminant may come from physics the model lacks (p. 7).** The authors state that at t = 9 ps the discrimination "mostly originates from the narrowing of the AFM Bragg peak" with increasing D_FM, attributed to FM domains straining adjacent AFM regions. In the model, however, laterally separated FM and AFM columns are averaged incoherently and are not elastically coupled, so the invoked lateral stress on the AFM matrix is absent. Clarify which model ingredient produces the AFM width change; if the key discriminant arises from physics outside the model, qualify the conclusion.
Raised by: r1

11. **The scenario IV parametrisation is inconsistent with the quoted low-fluence result (Methods p. 10; p. 7).** Scenario IV is defined by D_FM = 1.3 V*_FM, i.e. A*_FM = 1/1.3 ≈ 0.77, yet the text states that "a final in-plane coverage of A*_FM = 0.66 (scenario IV) matches the experiment best" at 3.9 mJ cm⁻². Explain how 0.66 was obtained and which D_FM was used at each fluence. If A*_FM (equivalently D_FM/V*_FM) was varied at low fluence, the same freedom should be allowed at higher fluences, where III and IV are close (concern 3).
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

12. **There is no independent validation of the method's depth sensitivity: one sample, no reference geometry, no matched-condition calibration.** All results come from a single 44 nm film, and the thermoelastic parameters come from earlier work (Refs. 26, 34) under different geometry and excitation. No measurement with a known answer is shown — e.g. a thinner film (uniform-depth transformation, scenarios collapse), a thicker or different-thickness film (different D_FM; Δt_phase scaling via Eq. 1), a W/MgO-only reference, or a sub-threshold sonogram in the same beamtime with only the AFM peak, which would test the strain model under the same footprint, timing and penetration depth (and on the damaged sample). Because the technique is presented as a main contribution, the authors should include such data if they exist, or discuss the limitation; at minimum, apply the full analysis to synthetic sonograms with realistic noise generated from known scenarios (I, III, IV, graded and mixed cases) and show the method recovers the input without confusing scenarios. A second FeRh thickness would also directly test whether D_FM tracks the optical excitation depth rather than the film thickness.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

13. **The temporal resolution limits the claim to resolve "nucleation" and transient heterogeneity.** The strain pulse crosses 44 nm in ≈8–9 ps (≈4 ps through a 20 nm FM layer), comparable to τ = 8 ps. The key discriminating snapshot (Fig. 4a–c) is at 9 ps, when V_FM has already reached ≈67 % of its final value, so the method characterises the heterogeneity after most of the conversion, not the nucleation events. The authors should state which delay window actually constrains D_FM and the resulting limits on resolving heterogeneity before ≈5 ps, and either extract a time-resolved D_FM(t) (e.g. by repeating Fig. 4c at several delays) or narrow wording such as "nucleates at the surface" and "directly visualising".
Raised by: r1, r2

14. **The fluence dependence of D_FM is claimed but not shown (p. 7).** The text states that D_FM "can be approximated by the depth until which the optical excitation overcomes the equilibrium transition threshold", revealing "the thermal character of the phase transition even during its non-equilibrium pathway", but no plot of fitted D_FM against fluence, compared with the threshold depth predicted from the absorption profile, is given. Add one with uncertainties, and reconcile the "thermal character" statement with the description of the pathway as non-thermal (electron-driven, τ = 8 ps vs 50 ps near equilibrium, p. 3).
Raised by: r3

15. **Equation 1 and the concept of Fig. 1 are never applied quantitatively to FeRh (pp. 2–3).** The concept assumes equal sound velocities, no interface reflections and a dedicated transducer; none holds here, and Δt_phase is never extracted from the data and converted to a thickness, so the claim that Δt "directly decodes the thickness of each phase" is not demonstrated. State this explicitly; if possible, show the Δt-based estimate of D_FM at high fluence next to the model-based one, and give the effective depth resolution achieved (set by v_s and the strain-pulse duration) with an error bar. A dedicated transducer or the proposed double-pulse scheme would make Eq. 1 directly applicable.
Raised by: r1, r3

16. **The claim to reconcile the FeRh literature is not substantiated (abstract; Introduction p. 2; p. 7).** The abstract says the results reconcile "the diverse experimental results in the literature on FeRh", but the only link is a qualitative remark that the slower magnetisation rise (Refs. 17, 25) is consistent with domain-wall-driven alignment of nucleated domains. No magnetic quantity was measured, and the experiment cannot distinguish structural FM domains with differently oriented magnetisation. Either make the comparison quantitative (e.g. domain-wall velocity × 30 nm, or predicted MOKE/XMCD rise times from A_FM(t) and the domain size, vs measured rise times in Ref. 25) or present it as a hypothesis and soften the abstract.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

17. **Generality and framing language overstates a model-based inference from one sample (abstract; p. 8).** "Universal", "non-invasive", "extremely sample-agnostic" and "directly visualising" are demonstrated on one 44 nm epitaxial film with an unusually favourable 0.6 % volume change and well-separated peaks; moreover the pump itself drives the transition and generates the strain pulse. State the requirements quantitatively — minimum peak separation relative to peak width, minimum volume fraction, achievable depth resolution — or narrow the language.
