Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors introduce "ultrafast X-ray sonography". The method uses the phase-specific transient strain from a propagating acoustic pulse, read out by time-resolved hard-X-ray diffraction at EuXFEL, to locate coexisting structural phases in depth and in plane. Applied to the laser-driven AFM–FM transition in a 44 nm FeRh(001) film, it concludes that the FM phase nucleates at the surface as columnar domains about 30 nm in diameter that do not extend through the film (scenario III). The idea of using a coherent strain pulse as a depth marker for phase coexistence is attractive and would add to the UXRD toolbox. However, the specific conclusions about FeRh rest on comparing data with a constrained 1D model, and the experimental geometry and statistics are not yet strong enough to make those conclusions secure.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The concept is simple and general. It needs no dedicated transducer, and the sonogram in Fig. 2b/3a clearly shows that the AFM and FM peaks respond to strain differently. This is a qualitatively new observable compared with the volume-fraction-only UXRD of earlier work.
- Using the full I(t, q_z) map (Fig. 4a,b) rather than fitted peak parameters alone is a sensible way to extract information about inhomogeneous strain, including the AFM peak narrowing.
- Several fluences are analysed (Ext. Data E2, E3), and the authors report a crossover from in-plane-only heterogeneity at low fluence to surface-layer nucleation at higher fluence. This is physically plausible and connects to the optical absorption profile.
- The sample is well characterised, and the thermoelastic parameters were calibrated in earlier work on the same film [18, 26, 32].

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The X-ray probe footprint is comparable to the pump spot, so the probed volume is not uniformly excited. This can mimic "lateral heterogeneity".**
   - *Where:* Methods p. 9–10. The pump FWHM is 136 × 142 µm at ≈25° incidence. The X-ray beam is focused to 50 µm and hits the sample at 20.9° incidence. The Methods (p. 10) state that "the large footprint of the pump pulse compared to the footprint of the probe pulse ensures a laterally homogeneous excitation of the probed volume".
   - *What is wrong:* At 20.9° the 50 µm beam projects to ≈50/sin(20.9°) ≈ 140 µm along the beam direction, essentially the same as the pump FWHM. Across the probed region the local fluence therefore varies by roughly a factor of two. At 7.7 mJ cm⁻², parts of the footprint are well above threshold and parts are near or below it.
   - *Why it matters:* This macroscopic lateral fluence gradient produces exactly the in-plane coexistence (A_FM < 1) and the depth variation (D_FM varying across the footprint) that the analysis attributes to intrinsic nucleation. It also affects:
     - V*_FM and the fluence assignment in Ext. Data E3;
     - the low-fluence interpretation (A*_FM = 0.66 attributed to a heterogeneous transition temperature);
     - the extra FM peak broadening used to size the domains.
   - *What to do:* Report the actual pump and probe footprints on the sample, including the projection, and any relative pointing jitter. Then either convolve the 1D model with the measured fluence distribution across the probed area and repeat the scenario discrimination (Figs. 4c,d, E3), or show quantitatively why the gradient is negligible.

2. **The scenario discrimination is not statistically quantified, and the model has unexamined choices that bear directly on depth sensitivity.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 4c,d and Ext. Data E3 present χ² values without uncertainties. The differences between scenarios I, III and IV are often small:
     - E3b (5.2 mJ cm⁻²): III ≈ 0.030 vs IV ≈ 0.032;
     - E3a: IV ≈ 0.009 vs III ≈ 0.012;
     - Fig. 4a: curves I, III and IV are hard to tell apart.
   - *Model choices that are never tested:*
     - **Bottom 5.5 nm removed.** Methods p. 10 says the bottom 5.5 nm of FeRh was assumed not to contribute to the Bragg peak "for an overall better agreement". This is an ad hoc adjustment of exactly the depth region that separates scenario V (backside nucleation) and scenario I from scenario III.
     - **τ fixed.** τ = 8 ps is taken from earlier work rather than fitted.
     - **D_FM tied to V*_FM.** In scenario III, D_FM is fixed to V*_FM; in scenario IV, D_FM = 1.3 V*_FM is chosen arbitrarily.
     - **Neglected physics.** Latent heat, the change in electronic heat capacity and electron–phonon coupling, and the different thermal expansion of the FM phase are all neglected (p. 10). At V_FM ≈ 0.46 these are not obviously minor for the strain amplitude. The claim that the result "does not depend decisively on the details of the shape of the strain pulse" is asserted but not demonstrated.
   - *Why it matters:* The headline conclusion that FM nucleates at the surface as non-percolating-in-depth columns is a model-selection result. Without an error model and a sensitivity analysis it is not clear the selection is robust.
   - *What to do:*
     - Normalise χ² by the measured intensity noise (shot-to-shot or delay-to-delay scatter), so that differences can be judged against noise.
     - Give the number of degrees of freedom and an information-criterion comparison or bootstrap over delays.
     - Show that scenario III still wins when (i) the 5.5 nm dead layer is removed or varied, (ii) τ is varied within its literature uncertainty, (iii) latent heat or a different FM expansion coefficient is included, and (iv) D_FM is treated as a continuous free parameter, or as a free function of time, rather than in discrete families.

3. **The 30 nm lateral domain size is not separated from the 25 nm crystallite size and rests on a toy model with an estimated, not computed, uncertainty.**
   - *Where:* Abstract, p. 8 and Methods p. 10–11, Ext. Data E4. The size is inferred from an "additional broadening of the FM Bragg peak by 0.06 Å⁻¹" along q_z, interpreted through a five-site in-plane expansion model with the Poisson effect.
   - *What is wrong:*
     - **No uniqueness test.** The domain size coincides with the known mosaic grain size (≈25 nm [32]). The analysis does not show that the observable could distinguish domains of 30 nm from domains bounded by grains, or from FM regions spanning several grains.
     - **Other sources of broadening are not excluded:** inhomogeneous strain from grain boundaries, the finite and possibly rough FM layer thickness (D_FM·44 nm ≈ 20 nm), the lateral fluence gradient (concern 1), and inhomogeneous heating.
     - **Inputs and uncertainty are not given.** The Poisson ratio and elastic constants used are not stated, and the ±10 nm uncertainty is "estimated" rather than derived.
     - **The quoted broadening does not match E1c.** The 0.06 Å⁻¹ value is hard to reconcile with Ext. Data E1c, where the full FM widths are ≈0.015–0.03 Å⁻¹ and the data–model difference appears to be well below 0.01 Å⁻¹.
   - *What to do:*
     - Clarify what 0.06 Å⁻¹ refers to.
     - Show the broadening predicted for a range of l_FM (e.g. 10, 20, 30, 60, 100 nm) against the data in E1c, so the reader can see the constraint.
     - Include the competing broadening mechanisms.
     - Soften the abstract's "approximately 30 nm diameter" claim, or tie it explicitly to the model, unless these tests make it robust.

**B. Should address**

4. **Temporal resolution and the claim to resolve "nucleation".**
   - *What is wrong:* The strain pulse crosses 44 nm in ~8–9 ps, which is comparable to τ = 8 ps. The key discriminating snapshot (Fig. 4a–c) is at t = 9 ps, when V_FM has already reached ~67% of its final value. The method therefore characterises the heterogeneity after most of the conversion has happened, not the nucleation events themselves.
   - *Related gap:* The text promises extraction of D_FM(t) and A_FM(t) "through the non-equilibrium pathway" (p. 3). In practice only time-independent D_FM families are compared.
   - *What to do:* State which delay window actually constrains D_FM. Either extract a time-resolved D_FM(t), for example by repeating Fig. 4c for several delays, or narrow the wording ("nucleates at the surface", "directly visualising").

5. **Sample damage and heat accumulation at MHz-burst excitation.**
   - *What is wrong:* Methods p. 10 attributes the non-contributing bottom 5.5 nm to "interface diffusion of FeRh and W … due to too strong laser excitations at a high-repetition rate". This means the sample changed during the experiment.
   - *Questions the paper does not answer:*
     - When did the damage occur relative to the data shown?
     - Was the sample re-characterised (XRR, static XRD, M(T)) after the beamtime?
     - Do the fluence series (E2, E3) come from fresh or exposed spots?
     - At 56 kHz within a train, does the baseline temperature rise along the train? A baseline closer to 370 K would change V*_FM, the threshold and the depth profile.
     - The calibration from [26] predates this damage, yet it is reused.
   - *What to do:* Show the static Bragg peak and the unpumped reference before and after exposure, and pulse-position-resolved results within the train. Also show a sub-threshold fluence measured in the same session to validate the thermoelastic model on the damaged sample.

6. **Numerical inconsistencies between the main figures and the Extended Data.**
   - Fig. 4d gives global χ² values of ≈0.09–0.17 and V*_FM = 0.46 for 7.7 mJ cm⁻². Ext. Data E3c, for the same fluence, shows χ² ≈ 0.06–0.16 with the experimental V*_FM line at ≈0.435.
   - For the lowest fluence the text gives A*_FM = 0.66 for scenario IV (p. 7). The definition D_FM = 1.3 V*_FM implies A*_FM = 1/1.3 ≈ 0.77.
   - *What to do:* Reconcile these numbers, and state which D_FM was used at each fluence.

7. **Determination of V*_FM and of the FM strain reference.**
   - *What is wrong:* V_FM is taken from the ratio of integrated AFM and FM Bragg intensities. This assumes equal structure factors and Debye–Waller factors, and ignores dynamical and extinction differences between a thin surface layer and a full film. The FM strain is computed "with respect to the expected equilibrium value" (p. 4), but that value is not given.
   - *Why it matters:* Both quantities enter the model directly: V*_FM sets D_FM in scenario III, and the FM strain sets the strain amplitude.
   - *What to do:* Specify both, and propagate their uncertainties into the scenario comparison.

8. **Overstated framing.**
   - The abstract claims the work is "reconciling the diverse experimental results in the literature on FeRh". The paper offers only one sentence linking surface columns to the slower magnetisation rise seen in [17, 25].
   - "Universal", "non-invasive", "sample-agnostic" and "directly visualising" overstate a model-based inference from a single sample. The pump itself drives the transition, and the strain pulse is generated by the same excitation.
   - *What to do:* Either support the literature reconciliation concretely (e.g. predict MOKE or XMCD rise times from A_FM(t) and the domain size) or narrow the language.

9. **Single sample, no reference geometry.**
   - *What is wrong:* All results come from one film. There is no sample where the outcome is known: for example, a thinner film where scenarios collapse, or a film of different thickness where the predicted Δt_phase changes as 1/v_s (Eq. 1).
   - *What to do:* Benchmarking the method's depth sensitivity on such a sample would substantially strengthen the methodological claim. At minimum, show a simulation-recovery test: generate synthetic sonograms for scenarios I, III and IV with realistic noise and show that the fitting recovers the right one.

**C. Minor**

10. Fig. 2c and Ext. Data E1 show no error bars. Give the number of shots and trains per delay and the uncertainty of the fitted peak parameters.
11. Fig. 1 assumes identical sound velocities and no reflections. State the longitudinal sound velocities and acoustic impedances used for FM and AFM FeRh, Pt and W, and whether FM and AFM elastic constants differ in the model.
12. Fig. 2a omits the 2 nm Pt cap. Its absorption and strain contribution should be stated in the Methods.
13. Ext. Data E3 caption: scenario V is "even larger … and therefore omitted". Please show it, since it is the scenario most affected by the 5.5 nm dead-layer assumption.
14. The data-selection thresholds (HIREX > 60%, normalisation > 2/3 of mean) could bias the selection toward particular pulse energies. Give the fraction of data retained per delay.
15. Typos and wording:
    - "continuos" (p. 10);
    - "constrains" → "constraints" (p. 10);
    - "Within increasing fluence" (E2 caption);
    - "ferromagentic" (E3 caption);
    - "Thus, the total FM Bragg peaks becomes" (p. 8).
16. The data and code are only "available upon request". Depositing the sonograms and the udkm1Dsim input files would allow the model-selection claims to be checked.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a noise-normalised, parameter-marginalised model comparison, together with a synthetic-data recovery test, as the central quantitative figure of the paper.
2. Include the measured lateral fluence distribution over the X-ray footprint in the simulations. Ideally, also measure with a tighter X-ray focus or a larger pump to show the scenario assignment does not change.
3. Validate the depth sensitivity on a second FeRh thickness, or with a dedicated transducer and the two-colour double-pulse scheme the authors propose.
4. Connect the extracted A_FM(t), D_FM and domain size to a measured or literature magnetisation rise time, to substantiate the claimed reconciliation of earlier results.
