Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript introduces "ultrafast X-ray sonography": time-resolved hard-X-ray diffraction combined with the propagating bipolar strain pulse that the pump launches, which serves as a depth marker for coexisting structural phases with distinct Bragg peaks. The authors apply it to the laser-driven AFM–FM transition in a 44 nm FeRh(001)/W/MgO film measured at EuXFEL MID. They compare the measured (002) sonogram with 1D elastic and dynamical-diffraction simulations of five nucleation scenarios, and conclude that the FM phase nucleates in the near-surface region (relative thickness ≈ V*_FM) as laterally separated columnar domains about 30 ± 10 nm across. Using phase-specific strain-pulse responses to resolve phase heterogeneity in depth and in plane is a useful idea. If the scenario discrimination and the domain-size estimate hold up, the method and the FeRh result would interest the ultrafast phase-transition community.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Conceptually clear idea (Fig. 1) that turns the usually unwanted strain pulse into a structural marker. It needs no transducer layer and in principle applies to any volume-changing transition with separable Bragg peaks.
- High-quality XFEL data in which the AFM and FM (002) peaks are resolved simultaneously, with clearly different transient strain responses for the two phases (Fig. 2b,c).
- Forward modelling of the full I(t, q_z) map rather than only fitted peak parameters, with a global residual that is evaluated against the independently measured V*_FM (Fig. 4d).
- A fluence series (Extended Data Figs. E2, E3) showing a crossover from scenario IV at 3.9 mJ cm⁻² to scenario III at ≥ 5.2 mJ cm⁻².
- The thermophysical parameters come from earlier work on the same sample, so the model has few free parameters.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The pump fluence varies laterally across the probed area, which could produce the in-plane heterogeneity attributed to stochastic nucleation.** The pump is 136 × 142 µm FWHM at ≈ 25° incidence. The X-ray beam is focused to 50 µm but hits the sample at 20.9°, so its footprint along the beam is ≈ 50/sin 20.9° ≈ 140 µm, about the same as the pump FWHM (Methods, p. 9). Inside the probed area the incident fluence therefore varies by a large factor between the centre and the edges. The paper assumes the opposite: "the large footprint of the pump pulse compared to the footprint of the probe pulse ensures a laterally homogeneous excitation" (p. 10), and "higher fluences … transform the entire near-surface region" (p. 7).

Because the transition has a sharp fluence threshold, a Gaussian fluence profile alone gives (i) a final in-plane coverage A*_FM < 1 and (ii) a depth D_FM that varies across the footprint. That is a macroscopic version of scenarios III/IV that has nothing to do with nanoscale nucleation. It bears directly on:
- the scenario ranking in Fig. 4 and Extended Data Fig. E3;
- the conclusion that at 3.9 mJ cm⁻² "the weak excitation overcomes the transition threshold only for regions exhibiting the lowest transition temperature";
- the identification of D_FM with the depth at which the excitation exceeds threshold.

The authors should:
- give the actual X-ray footprint on the sample, both directions, and the pump footprint after projection at 25°;
- compute the fluence distribution sampled by the X-rays;
- add that distribution to the sonogram model, as a weighted incoherent sum over local fluences, each with its own D_FM and V_FM, and show whether scenario III still beats a laterally graded scenario I.

2. **The FM domain size of 30 ± 10 nm rests on one unexplained broadening of the FM peak, attributed to one mechanism.** The size is obtained by matching an "additional broadening" of the FM peak along q_z (Extended Data Fig. E1c), which the 1D model does not reproduce, to a toy five-site model of Poisson-coupled in-plane expansion (Extended Data Fig. E4). Several other mechanisms could also broaden the FM peak and are not ruled out:
- a gradient of strain or c-axis lattice parameter within the nucleated FM volume;
- a distribution of D_FM across the footprint (concern 1);
- inhomogeneous strain at FM/AFM interfaces from clamping by the AFM matrix and substrate;
- the excluded bottom 5.5 nm (concern 5);
- the neglected FM-specific thermal expansion and latent heat (concern 4).

The ± 10 nm uncertainty is stated as an estimate rather than derived. The match to the ≈ 25 nm mosaic crystallite size [32] also suggests a different picture: domains simply follow grains, rather than nucleation sites being stochastic.

Two further points need checking:
- The text quotes an additional broadening of "0.06 Å⁻¹" (p. 11). In Extended Data Fig. E1c the FM widths are only ≈ 0.012–0.03 Å⁻¹ and the data–model difference is less than 0.01 Å⁻¹. Please check this number.
- The quoted "30 nm" and the headline "columnar domains" also need a definition of the column height. With D_FM ≈ 0.46 × 44 nm ≈ 20 nm, the domains are about as wide as they are tall.

The authors should:
- show how the predicted extra width depends on l_FM (e.g. 10, 20, 30, 50, 100 nm) on the same axes as the data, so the sensitivity and the ± 10 nm are quantitative;
- discuss and bound the alternative broadening sources;
- if possible, use the in-plane (q_x, q_y) peak shape or its time dependence as an independent check.

Otherwise the 30 nm figure in the abstract should be presented as model-dependent.

3. **Scenario III is preferred by small χ² differences, with no statistical significance and with known systematic misfits.**
- At 7.7 mJ cm⁻² the global residual minima in Fig. 4d and Extended Data Fig. E3c are ≈ 0.060 (III), ≈ 0.067 (IV) and ≈ 0.082 (I).
- At 3.9 mJ cm⁻² (E3a) the III, IV and I curves are within ≈ 0.005 of one another near the measured V*_FM.
- χ² is an unweighted sum of squared intensity differences (p. 6). It does not include counting or shot-to-shot noise and does not account for the known misfit of the FM peak width (E1c), which adds a systematic term to every scenario.

Without an estimate of the noise floor, e.g. by bootstrapping over pulse trains or comparing independent delay scans, the reader cannot tell whether III vs IV, or III vs I at low fluence, is significant.

The scenarios are also a small, discrete set of D_FM(t) and A_FM(t) histories:
- in III and V, D_FM is fixed at V*_FM from t = 0;
- IV uses one arbitrary choice, D_FM = 1.3 V*_FM;
- no scenario lets columns grow into the depth over time.

The authors should:
- give χ² with uncertainties, or a likelihood ratio, for each scenario;
- use a continuous parametrisation, e.g. D_FM(t) = D* f(t) with D* free and A_FM = V_FM/D_FM, and show the confidence region in (D*, growth law) space;
- show the time-resolved χ²_t (as in Fig. 4c) for all delays, to check whether the preference for III comes from a narrow delay window around the strain-pulse exit (≈ 9 ps).

4. **The model leaves out phase-specific thermoelastic effects that act directly on the observable used to locate the phase boundary.** The amplitude and timing of the average FM strain are what discriminate D_FM (p. 5: "the average strain … is a sensitive measure of the relative thickness"). The model neglects four effects (p. 10):
- the change in electronic heat capacity and electron–phonon coupling in the FM phase;
- the different thermal expansion of FM and AFM FeRh;
- the latent heat consumed by the transition;
- the electronic band-structure modification.

Each of these changes the stress in exactly the near-surface FM region. Latent heat in particular is a sizeable fraction of the deposited energy at these fluences. The statement that the identification "does not depend decisively on the details of the shape of the strain pulse" is asserted but not shown. The authors should repeat the scenario comparison with plausible bounds on these parameters, e.g. latent heat from calorimetry and FM thermal expansion from [32], and show that the ranking of III over I and IV survives.

B. Should address

5. **Two tuning choices affect the depth result and are not justified independently.**
- The bottom 5.5 nm of the FeRh layer is excluded from the Bragg peak "for an overall better agreement" and attributed to FeRh/W interdiffusion "due to too strong laser excitations at high-repetition rate" (p. 10).
- The optical penetration depth is optimised freely (p. 9).

Both directly affect the depth profile of the excitation and the strain-pulse transit times that Eq. (1) turns into thickness. The bottom-layer exclusion also affects how scenario V (near-substrate nucleation) is judged. The authors should:
- give independent evidence for the dead layer, e.g. static XRD/XRR or TEM before and after the beamtime, and show the fits with and without the exclusion;
- state the fitted penetration depth with its uncertainty and compare it with ellipsometry values for FeRh at 800 nm and 25° incidence.

6. **Laser-induced sample modification and heat accumulation are not ruled out.** The paper itself suggests that interface diffusion occurred because the excitation was "too strong … at high-repetition rate" (p. 10). This implies the sample changed irreversibly during data collection. The burst mode (56.25 kHz pump within a train, 10 Hz trains) also raises a second issue: FeRh is hysteretic, and the base temperature could rise along the train. Either would change V*_FM, the transition threshold and the unpumped reference, which the pumped data are scaled to.

The authors should:
- show that the unpumped (002) profile and the pre-time-zero sonogram stay the same across the measurement series and along the pulse train;
- report any dependence on pulse index within the train;
- state the order in which the fluences were measured, so that the fluence series (E2, E3) is not confounded with progressive damage.

7. **No reference measurement under matched conditions checks that the strain model is calibrated in this experiment.** The thermoelastic parameters come from earlier work [26, 34], made under different geometry and excitation. A sonogram measured in the same beamtime below the transition threshold, with only the AFM peak and no FM nucleation, would test the strain model under the same footprint, timing and penetration depth. Something similar holds for a W/MgO-only reference, or a sample with a different FeRh thickness. That test should be passed before the model is used to discriminate between nucleation scenarios. If such data exist, please include them; if not, discuss the limitation. A second FeRh thickness would also directly test the claim that D_FM tracks the optical excitation depth rather than the film thickness.

8. **The volume fraction and Eq. (3) are inputs, but the data deviate from them at early delays.**
- V_FM(t) is taken from intensity ratios with a fixed τ = 8 ps (p. 5).
- In Extended Data Fig. E1a the FM intensity overshoots to ≈ 0.4 at ≈ 5 ps, then dips before rising again, and the AFM intensity falls faster than the black single-exponential curve.

Inferring volume fractions from integrated intensities assumes equal structure factors and Debye–Waller factors for the two phases at all delays, and assumes that transient strain does not move intensity out of the integration window.

The authors should:
- justify these assumptions;
- test τ as a free parameter in the global fit;
- show that the early-delay deviations do not drive the scenario selection, since that selection relies most heavily on the < 10 ps window.

9. **The scenario IV parametrisation is inconsistent with the quoted low-fluence result.** Methods (p. 10) defines scenario IV as D_FM = 1.3 V*_FM, i.e. A*_FM = 1/1.3 ≈ 0.77. Yet p. 7 states that "a final in-plane coverage of A*_FM = 0.66 (scenario IV) matches the experiment best" at 3.9 mJ cm⁻². Please explain how A*_FM = 0.66 was obtained. If A*_FM (equivalently D_FM/V*_FM) was varied at low fluence, the same freedom should be allowed at the higher fluences, where III and IV are close (concern 3).

10. **The fluence dependence of D_FM is claimed but not shown.** The text states that D_FM "can be approximated by the depth until which the optical excitation overcomes the equilibrium transition threshold" and that this "reveals the thermal character of the phase transition even during its non-equilibrium pathway" (p. 7). No plot of fitted D_FM against fluence, compared with the threshold depth predicted from the absorption profile, is given. Please add one, with uncertainties. Also reconcile it with the description of the pathway as non-thermal (electron-driven, τ = 8 ps vs 50 ps near equilibrium, p. 3).

11. **Claims about magnetisation and literature reconciliation go beyond what was measured.** The abstract and introduction say the results reconcile "the diverse experimental results in the literature", and p. 7 links the slower rise of macroscopic magnetisation [17, 25] to domain-wall-driven alignment of the nucleated domains. No magnetic quantity was measured, and the experiment cannot distinguish structural FM domains with differently oriented magnetisation. Please either support this with a quantitative estimate (domain-wall velocity × 30 nm vs the magnetisation rise time in [25]) or present it as a hypothesis.

12. **Generality claims rest on a single system.** "Universal", "non-invasive" and "extremely sample-agnostic" (abstract, p. 8) are demonstrated on one 44 nm epitaxial film. That system has an unusually favourable 0.6 % volume change and well-separated peaks. Please state the requirements quantitatively: minimum peak separation relative to peak width, minimum volume fraction, and the depth resolution set by v_s and the strain-pulse duration. Also give the effective depth resolution achieved here, with an error bar, since Eq. (1) is introduced but never applied numerically to the data.

C. Minor

13. **The strain plots in Fig. 2c lack error bars and a stated reference.** The FM strain is "calculated with respect to the expected equilibrium value" (p. 4). Please state that value and its source, and give the intensity threshold below which FM points are suppressed. Please also add uncertainties from the pseudo-Voigt fits, which are largest when the FM peak is weak.

14. **The shot-selection criteria may bias the data.** Shots were kept only if the HIREX self-seeding intensity was > 60 % of the mean and i₀ > 2/3 of its mean (p. 9). Please report the fraction rejected, and confirm that rejection does not correlate with pump–probe delay or with position in the train.

15. **Time resolution is not specified.** Please state the time resolution (pump–probe jitter at MID, X-ray and pump durations), the delay step, and the time-zero determination.

16. **Fig. 4b, c need clearer labelling.** The horizontal lines for scenarios I–IV and the colour scale of ΔI are hard to relate to Fig. 3. Please label which D_FM(9 ps) each scenario corresponds to. For scenario I this should be ≈ 0.46(1 − e^{−9/8}) ≈ 0.31; for III it is 0.46. Also explain why χ²_t in Fig. 4c is plotted on a ≈ 0.002–0.004 scale while the global χ² is ≈ 0.09–0.17.

17. **Extended Data captions have errors.** The E2 caption says the panels are "above the threshold", yet at 3.9 mJ cm⁻² the FM signal is barely visible. The E3 caption says V is "even larger as scenario II" (should be "than"); please show scenario V rather than omitting it. In E1, the "offset for clarity" of the widths makes the quoted 0.06 Å⁻¹ hard to read off (see concern 2).

18. **Data and code are available only on request.** Given that the conclusions rest on forward modelling, please deposit the processed sonograms and the udkm1Dsim input files in a public repository.

19. **Typos:**
- "continous" (p. 10);
- "Within increasing fluence" (E2 caption);
- "Thus, the total FM Bragg peaks becomes" (p. 8);
- "If the domains would be much smaller" (p. 11).

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Model the actual pump fluence distribution over the X-ray footprint. Alternatively, repeat a key delay scan with a smaller X-ray footprint or a larger pump spot, to separate macroscopic lateral gradients from nanoscale nucleation.
2. Replace the discrete scenario set with a continuous (D_FM(t), A_FM(t)) fit that has noise-weighted residuals and confidence intervals, and include a sensitivity study of the neglected thermoelastic terms and the 5.5 nm dead layer.
3. Strengthen the domain-size claim with an independent probe of lateral structure: time-resolved in-plane (q_x) peak shape or diffuse scattering, or static post-mortem imaging (e.g. XMCD-PEEM or MFM of field- or temperature-induced FM domains in the same film) for comparison with the 25 nm grain size.
4. Implement the proposed double-pulse scheme, or a second film thickness, as a direct experimental validation that the sonogram encodes D_FM as claimed.
