Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript introduces "ultrafast X-ray sonography". The idea is to use the strain pulse launched by the pump as a depth marker. Each of two coexisting structural phases then shows its own transient strain response in time-resolved hard X-ray diffraction, and that response encodes where the phase sits in the film, both in depth and laterally. Applied to the laser-driven AFM-to-FM transition in a 44 nm FeRh(001) film measured at EuXFEL/MID, the authors conclude that the FM phase nucleates near the surface as columnar domains about 30 nm wide, which then merge into a continuous near-surface layer (scenario III). A way to extract three-dimensional phase heterogeneity from spatially averaging UXRD would be of broad interest. As presented, however, the central FeRh conclusions rest on choosing among five hand-built scenarios, on an unweighted residual and on a heavily constrained model. The robustness of that selection, and of the 30 nm domain size, is not yet established.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The concept (Fig. 1) is clear and general. In principle it applies to any first-order transition with a lattice-constant change and resolvable Bragg peaks, so it could be widely adopted.
- The data quality is high. The phase-resolved sonograms (Fig. 2b, Fig. 3a, Ext. Data Fig. E2) clearly resolve the emerging FM Bragg peak and its strain oscillations, which differ markedly from those of the AFM peak, at four fluences.
- The authors compare full diffraction profiles I(t, q_z) with dynamical diffraction simulations instead of relying only on fitted peak parameters. This is a sound approach, and they note correctly that peak fits are unreliable at early delays.
- The thermophysical parameters come from earlier work on the same sample (Refs. 18, 26). This limits the number of new free parameters.
- Two results are physically appealing and testable: pure in-plane heterogeneity (scenario II) is clearly excluded, and the heterogeneity changes with fluence (lateral at 3.9 mJ cm^-2, near-surface layer at higher fluence).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The scenario space is discrete, hand-built and over-constrained, so "scenario III" is a choice among five options rather than a measurement of D_FM(t) and A_FM(t). (Fig. 3, Fig. 4, Methods "Modelling of sonograms".)
   - Each scenario fixes the relation between D_FM and A_FM by hand: D_FM = 1 (II), D_FM = V*_FM (III, V), or D_FM = 1.3 V*_FM, described as "an example" (IV). Each also assumes a time-independent D_FM, a sharp phase boundary and columns of uniform height.
   - Physically, the most natural alternative is a graded depth profile set by the optical absorption profile (red shading in Fig. 2a). In that picture the local FM fraction V_FM(z) falls continuously with depth, and the FM column height varies from site to site. This is not tested.
   - Scenario III, "nucleation at the surface", is largely what exponential absorption in a 44 nm metal film predicts anyway. The non-trivial claim is the columnar, laterally heterogeneous early stage with a sharp depth cutoff. That claim is distinguished from the alternatives only by the residual at t = 9 ps (Fig. 4b, c), where D_FM is scanned only at a fixed delay.
   - Why it matters: "FM phase nucleates at the surface as narrow columnar domains" is the headline result. It holds only if no physically reasonable unlisted heterogeneity fits equally well.
   - What to do:
     - Fit a continuous, time-dependent parametrisation. For example, take D_FM(t) and A_FM(t) as free functions on a coarse time grid, or use a depth-graded FM fraction V_FM(z, t) tied to the local deposited energy density, and report the best fit and its confidence region.
     - At minimum, add a "graded, absorption-profile-driven" scenario and a scenario III variant with a distribution of column heights.
     - Show the residual against D_FM at several delays, not only at t = 9 ps.

2. The statistical and systematic significance of the residual ranking is not established. (Fig. 4c, d; Ext. Data Fig. E3.)
   - χ² is defined as an unweighted sum of squared intensity differences. No noise estimate, reduced χ², number of degrees of freedom or uncertainty on χ² is given.
   - The separations between scenarios I, III and IV are modest: roughly 0.09 against 0.10 to 0.12 in Fig. 4d. At 3.9 mJ cm^-2 (E3a), III and IV are almost indistinguishable, and I is not far behind.
   - The model also carries several fixed or tuned ingredients that act on exactly the observables that discriminate between scenarios:
     - the optical penetration depth, which was "optimised";
     - the exclusion of the bottom 5.5 nm "for an overall better agreement";
     - the 0.6 % volumetric FM expansion;
     - the neglected latent heat, the FM-specific electron-phonon coupling, the heat capacity, and the reduced thermal expansion of the FM phase (Methods, p. 10).
   - Why it matters: if plausible variations of these inputs reorder scenarios I, III and IV, the conclusion does not follow.
   - What to do:
     - Weight χ² by the measured shot-to-shot and delay-to-delay intensity noise.
     - Give uncertainties on the minima, for example by bootstrap over shots or delays.
     - Present a sensitivity analysis showing that III remains preferred when each fixed parameter (penetration depth, excluded interface thickness, FM expansion, Grüneisen parameters, latent heat) is varied within its uncertainty.
     - State explicitly which parameters were free for each scenario. The claim that neglecting these effects "demonstrates" insensitivity to the strain-pulse shape (p. 10) needs to be backed by such tests rather than asserted.

3. The pump fluence varies across the X-ray probe footprint, which can masquerade as lateral and depth heterogeneity. (Methods, p. 9.)
   - The X-rays are collimated to 50 µm and hit the sample at 20.9°. That gives a footprint along the beam of about 50 µm / sin(20.9°) ≈ 140 µm, comparable to the 136 × 142 µm FWHM of the pump.
   - The probed area therefore samples fluences from the peak down to roughly half of it. Regions near the footprint edge may stay below threshold, or transform only to a smaller depth. That alone produces A_FM < 1 and a spread of D_FM, which is exactly the signature attributed to intrinsic heterogeneity (scenario IV at 3.9 mJ cm^-2, and the lateral component of III).
   - The text states the opposite assumption: "the large footprint of the pump pulse compared to the footprint of the probe pulse ensures a laterally homogeneous excitation" (p. 10).
   - What to do:
     - Report the measured X-ray footprint and pump profile at the sample, and say whether the quoted fluences are peak or footprint-averaged.
     - Include the fluence distribution over the footprint in the simulations, as an incoherent average over local fluences, and show whether the scenario ranking and the A*_FM = 0.66 result at low fluence survive.

4. The 30 nm lateral domain size rests on an under-determined model and an unexplained broadening. (p. 8; Methods, "Estimating the in-plane domain dimension"; Ext. Data Figs. E1c and E4.)
   - The size comes from matching an "additional broadening of the FM Bragg peak by 0.06 Å^-1" with a toy model of five nucleation sites.
   - This number is hard to reconcile with Ext. Data Fig. E1c. There the FM widths are about 0.015 to 0.025 Å^-1 (offset) and differ from the model curves by much less than 0.06 Å^-1. Please state clearly how the 0.06 Å^-1 was obtained and at which delays.
   - Other sources of extra q_z broadening are not excluded:
     - depth-varying strain within the FM layer;
     - a distribution of column heights, which gives a distribution of finite-size widths;
     - the fluence gradient discussed in concern 3;
     - inhomogeneous transition temperatures (Ref. 42);
     - the mosaic grain-size distribution.
   - The inferred domain size (30 nm) is nearly equal to the mosaic crystallite size (25 nm, Ref. 32). The FM "domains" may simply be grains that transform as units. In that case the coherent elastic coupling between neighbouring sites assumed in E4 (in-plane expansion propagating and being "erased" at the speed of sound) would not apply across grain boundaries.
   - The ±10 nm uncertainty is estimated, not derived.
   - What to do:
     - Show the excess broadening against delay with error bars, and show the predicted broadening for several domain sizes (for example 10, 20, 30, 60 and 100 nm) on the same plot.
     - Discuss the grain-boundary alternative.
     - Either derive the uncertainty or present 30 nm as an order-of-magnitude estimate. The abstract currently states it as a result.

B. Should address

5. The in-plane expansion that is used to extract the domain size is left out of the model used to choose the scenario. (pp. 8 and 10.)
   - The authors argue that laterally heterogeneous nucleation unlocks in-plane expansion, which through the Poisson effect changes the out-of-plane lattice constant of the FM domains.
   - Yet scenarios II to V are simulated with a purely one-dimensional model, and the scenario discrimination relies mainly on the FM peak position and the AFM peak width at about 9 ps.
   - Extended Data Fig. E4b shows in-plane strains of up to 0.4 %, which is comparable to the 0.6 % transition strain. The Poisson-induced shifts are therefore not negligible for laterally heterogeneous scenarios, and including them could change the ranking.
   - Please include this effect, at least approximately, in the scenario simulations, or show that it does not affect the ranking.

6. Interpreting the AFM peak width is beyond what a one-dimensional model with incoherent averaging can capture. (p. 7.)
   - The authors state that at t = 9 ps the discrimination "mostly originates from the narrowing of the AFM Bragg peak" with increasing D_FM. They attribute it to FM domains straining the adjacent AFM regions.
   - In the model, however, laterally separated FM and AFM columns are averaged incoherently and are not elastically coupled. Lateral stress from FM columns acting on the AFM matrix, which is the mechanism invoked, is therefore absent.
   - Please clarify which model ingredient produces the AFM width change. If the key discriminant comes from physics the model lacks, the conclusion needs to be qualified.

7. The nucleation kinetics are fixed rather than fitted. (Eq. 3; Ext. Data Fig. E1a.)
   - The time constant τ = 8 ps is taken from the literature and applied in every scenario at all fluences.
   - Ext. Data Fig. E1a shows clear deviations of the measured FM and AFM intensities from the single exponential during the first 10 ps. The FM intensity overshoots near 5 ps and the AFM intensity drops faster. This is the same window that decides the scenario choice.
   - Please let τ, and possibly a delayed onset, vary for each scenario and fluence, and report whether the ranking changes. If the intensity overshoot is an extinction or dynamical-diffraction effect, explain it.

8. Sample condition and cumulative heating under burst-mode excitation. (Methods, pp. 9 and 10.)
   - The sample is pumped at 56.25 kHz within 10 Hz trains, at fluences up to 11.7 mJ cm^-2. The authors themselves invoke "interface diffusion of FeRh and W ... due to too strong laser excitations at high repetition rate" to justify removing the bottom 5.5 nm.
   - This points to irreversible modification during the experiment. It also raises two questions:
     - whether the starting temperature rises along the pulse train;
     - whether some FM phase persists between pulses 17.8 µs apart, given the thermal hysteresis of FeRh.
   - Please show:
     - unpumped reference profiles at the start and end of the measurement and along the pulse train, with no FM peak and an unchanged AFM peak position;
     - the dependence of the signal on position within the train;
     - any static X-ray reflectivity or diffraction taken after the beamtime.
   - The order in which the fluences were measured also matters, given the damage.

9. There is only one sample and no independent validation of the depth-sensitivity claim.
   - All conclusions come from a single 44 nm film. No sample or geometry was measured in which the answer is known in advance. Examples would be a film thinner than the penetration depth, where uniform-depth transformation is expected, or a thicker film, which should give a different D_FM.
   - Such a benchmark would demonstrate that sonography can reliably tell scenarios I, III and IV apart. This matters because the paper presents the technique itself as a main contribution.
   - If new data cannot be obtained, apply the full analysis to synthetic sonograms with realistic noise generated from a known scenario, including graded and mixed cases. Show that the method recovers the input and that the scenarios are not confused with each other.

10. The claim to reconcile the literature is not substantiated. (Abstract; Introduction, p. 2; p. 7.)
    - The abstract says the results reconcile "the diverse experimental results in the literature on FeRh". The only connection shown is a qualitative remark that a slower rise of the magnetisation (Refs. 17, 25) is consistent with domain-wall-driven alignment of the nucleated FM domains.
    - Either make the comparison concrete, for example by comparing predicted and measured magnetisation, XMCD or MOKE rise times for the inferred domain size and geometry, or soften the claim.

11. Equation 1 and the concept in Fig. 1 are not used quantitatively for FeRh. (pp. 2 and 3.)
    - The concept relies on equal sound velocities, no interface reflections and a dedicated transducer. None of these holds in the experiment, and the delay Δt_phase is never extracted from the FeRh data and converted to a thickness.
    - The claim that Δt "directly decodes the thickness of each phase" is therefore not demonstrated in this work.
    - State this explicitly. If possible, show the Δt-based estimate of D_FM at high fluence next to the model-based one.

C. Minor

12. The values of V*_FM are inconsistent between figures.
    - The main text and Fig. 4d give V*_FM = 0.46 at 7.7 mJ cm^-2.
    - In Ext. Data Fig. E3c the dashed line (the experimental V*_FM) sits at about 0.43, and the residual levels differ (III minimum about 0.06 there, about 0.09 in Fig. 4d).
    - Please reconcile these, or explain whether different delay ranges or normalisations were used.

13. The quoted in-plane coverage at low fluence does not match the scenario definition.
    - Scenario IV is defined with D_FM = 1.3 V*_FM, which gives A*_FM = 1/1.3 ≈ 0.77.
    - The text quotes A*_FM = 0.66 at 3.9 mJ cm^-2 as the best match (p. 7).
    - Please explain how 0.66 was obtained, and whether the factor 1.3 was varied.

14. Error bars are missing from the data. Fig. 2c and Ext. Data Fig. E1 show data with no error bars. Please give the number of shots per delay after filtering, and the uncertainties on the fitted intensity, position and width.

15. The FM strain reference needs justification.
    - The FM strain in Fig. 2c is computed relative to "the expected equilibrium value". Please state that value and its source.
    - Out-of-plane expansion in clamped films depends on the substrate (Ref. 32), and the clamping of narrow columns differs from that of a continuous layer.

16. The data selection needs documenting.
    - Shots were kept only if the HIREX self-seeding intensity exceeded 60 % of its mean and i0 exceeded 2/3 of its mean, and the pumped data were rescaled by the unpumped reference.
    - Please report the fraction of shots retained, and confirm that the filtering does not correlate with delay.

17. The temporal-resolution argument should be made quantitative.
    - The effective temporal resolution is set by the transit time of the strain pulse through the FM layer: about 4 ps for 20 nm, which is comparable to τ.
    - Please state the resulting limits on resolving "transient" heterogeneity before about 5 ps.

18. Fig. 3 caption: the solid lines in panels b to f are positions fitted to simulated sonograms. Say so explicitly. Also define the colour scale (√I, normalised to what).

19. Data and code are available only "upon request". Depositing the sonograms and the simulation inputs would let readers reproduce the scenario ranking.

20. Typographical: "constrains" should be "constraints" and "continous" should be "continuous" (p. 10); "Within increasing fluence" should be "With increasing fluence" (E2 caption).

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Replace the five fixed scenarios with a continuous fit of D_FM(t), A_FM(t) and an optional depth-graded FM fraction. Include the fluence distribution over the probe footprint and noise-weighted residuals, and report confidence regions.
2. Validate the method on synthetic data and, ideally, on a second film thickness where the expected heterogeneity differs. This would establish sonography as a quantitative tool and not only a model-selection procedure.
3. Add in-plane (Poisson) coupling to the scenario simulations so that scenario selection and the domain-size estimate use the same physics. Test explicitly whether the FM domains coincide with the 25 nm mosaic grains.
4. Use a dedicated transducer or the proposed double-pulse scheme to separate the driver of the transition from the strain marker. This would make the Δt-based depth reading in Eq. 1 directly applicable.
