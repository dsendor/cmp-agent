Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report 1H NMR on a single crystal of the quasi-1D S = 1/2 Heisenberg chain copper benzoate down to 0.03 K and fields down to 0.2 T. They identify three regimes. The first is a spinon regime with a temperature-independent fast 1/T1 and 1/T2G ∝ T^-1/2. The second is a low-temperature "random-singlet-like" regime, seen as a second, slow relaxation channel with 1/T1s ∝ T, a spectral weight w2 ∝ -ln(T/T0), and a Knight-shift upturn fitted to T^-1 ln^-2(Ω/T). The third is a high-field soliton regime with a gap Δ ∝ H^~0.8, close to the sine-Gordon H^2/3. Spinons and the field-induced soliton gap in copper benzoate are already established by neutron scattering, specific heat and earlier NMR, so the novelty rests mainly on the ultra-low-temperature, low-field claim: that weak, unintentional quenched disorder drives a random-singlet-like crossover that can be resolved locally by NMR. That claim would interest a broad audience if it held up. In its present form, though, it depends on attributing the slow relaxation channel and the Knight-shift upturn to random-singlet physics, without excluding several more ordinary explanations.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The data reach an unusual regime: 1H NMR at 0.03 K and 0.2 T on a clean quasi-1D chain, with 1/T1, 1/T2G, spectra and Knight shift all measured on the same crystal (Figs. 2-4).
- The separation of the recovery into fast and slow channels using ILTA (Fig. 3c) is a useful way to show a distribution of relaxation rates directly, and the logarithmic growth of w2 over a decade in temperature (Fig. 3d) is a clear empirical observation.
- The heating checks in Methods are careful: randomized recovery delays, a ±6 dB pulse-power scan and a recycle-delay check.
- The high-field gap from Kn and 1/T1 agrees quantitatively with specific heat and INS gaps (Fig. 4e), and the LTRG calculation of the uniform magnetization including the DM-induced canting (Fig. 4c) reproduces the plateau-like upturn of Kn at 5 T. Together these give a coherent picture of the soliton regime.
- QMC for a weakly random-bond chain (Fig. 3d inset) shows that even ΔJ/J ≈ 0.03 produces a logarithmic crossover in the correlation length within the measured window. This is a useful theoretical point in its own right.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The staggered-field (soliton) physics is not excluded at 0.2 T, even though it sets in at precisely the temperatures where the "RS-like" behaviour appears. The authors' own gap data (Fig. 4e: Δ ≈ 0.3 meV ≈ 3.5 K at 10 T, with Δ ∝ H^0.8 or H^2/3) extrapolate to Δ ≈ 0.15-0.25 K at 0.2 T. That is the same scale as the 0.4 K crossover, the w2 growth and the downturn of the fast 1/T1f below 0.2-0.3 K, which the text itself attributes to a "field-induced origin ... soliton behaviours" (p. 2). At 2 and 5 T the DM-induced canting produces a low-temperature upturn of Kn (Fig. 4b,c), qualitatively like the 0.2 T upturn in Fig. 3e that is fitted to the RS form. The Discussion's argument that there is "no Zeeman gap" at 0.2 T therefore needs to be demonstrated, not asserted. The authors should:
   - run the same LTRG calculation (staggered field plus DM canting) at 0.2 T, and at 0.5 T and 1 T, and overlay it on Fig. 3e and on 1/T1f in Fig. 3b;
   - show quantitatively that the Kn upturn and the onset of the slow channel at 0.2 T cannot come from the field-induced staggered magnetization of the clean chain;
   - ideally, measure 1/T1 and Kn at 0.2 T with the field along a direction where the staggered-field coefficient is minimal, since in copper benzoate this coefficient depends strongly on field direction. An RS-like signal that survives where the staggered field is suppressed would be a strong control.

2. The slow relaxation channel is assigned to an RS-like state without excluding simpler origins, and without a calculation showing that RS physics gives 1/T1s ∝ T. This channel (Fig. 3a-d) is the central evidence for the RS-like claim. Several alternatives need to be ruled out:
   - **Site overlap.** The P5 line is itself ambiguous: it is attributed "either to the H(3) site ... or to protons in the benzoate groups" (p. 2). Two protons with different hyperfine couplings can give two T1 components, and a temperature-dependent shift (Kn changes strongly below 0.8 K) can move spectral weight into or out of the excitation bandwidth, so w2 could change with temperature. Supplementary Note 5 is said to exclude spectral overlap. That conclusion should be supported in the main text by recoveries measured on different parts of the P5 line, and by the same ILTA analysis on the well-resolved H(1) line (P1/P2), which is presumably a single site.
   - **Relaxation near dilute paramagnetic defects.** Chain ends, broken bonds and free Cu spins can relax nearby protons, with nuclear spin diffusion spreading the effect. This also produces a fraction of nuclei with distinct relaxation whose weight grows on cooling as the defect moments polarize.
   - **Freezing of molecular motion.** Water or benzoate group motion freezing out, which the authors themselves raise as a source of disorder.

   The authors also need to tie the specific observables to RS theory:
   - The text cites RS predictions of a quadratic-in-T thermal conductivity, but not of a linear-in-T 1/T1. Earlier NMR on random Heisenberg chains (e.g. ref. 50, ref. 58) found a broad, continuous distribution of relaxation rates (stretched exponentials), not a two-component bimodal one. The authors should either compute the 1/T1 distribution from their QMC disorder model, for example from the local dynamic structure factor or from a strong-disorder RG estimate, or explain why a weak-disorder chain gives a separate slow channel with 1/T1s ∝ T and a spectral weight that grows logarithmically.
   - They should state explicitly what distinguishes their data from an impurity-relaxation scenario. Field dependence of 1/T1s, its dependence on the hyperfine coupling across P1-P5, and the absolute value of 1/T1s are the obvious discriminants.

3. The Knight-shift evidence at 0.2 T (Fig. 3e) needs an absolute field reference and a comparison against a Curie term.
   - **Absolute value at 0.2 T.** In the linear-response regime Kn should not depend on field. Yet the 0.2 T "spinon" Kn at 1-4 K is ≈ -0.7 to -0.8% (Fig. 3e), while at 2 T and 5 T the same H(1) site gives ≈ -0.3% at 2-4 K (Figs. 2c and 4b). At 0.2 T (γH ≈ 8.5 MHz) a remanent or trapped field of a few mT in a superconducting magnet, or an error in the reference frequency, shifts Kn by about 1%. The authors should state how the field was calibrated at 0.2 T, for example with a reference sample or by measuring at both field polarities, and reconcile the 0.2 T and 2 T values.
   - **The fitted form.** The fit Kn = aT^-1 ln^-2(Ω/T) is made over less than a decade (roughly 0.2-0.8 K), has no constant offset, and the data below 0.2 K, which turn down, are excluded as an artifact. Within this window the form is hard to distinguish from a Curie-like C/T + K0, which is what dilute free spins at chain breaks would produce. The authors should fit both forms with an offset, report the fit statistics and how stable Ω is, and show the excluded points.
   - **Checking the claimed artifact.** The explanation of the downturn below 0.2 K (signal loss on the low-frequency tail) can be tested by integrating the full line, or by measuring at a second frequency or field.

B. Should address

4. The disorder used in QMC does not match the disorder invoked in the Discussion, and the link from ξ to the NMR observables is only qualitative.
   - QMC uses a bimodal random-bond model (10% of bonds with ΔJ/J = 0.03, randomly placed). The Discussion argues instead for "domain-type" disorder: dirtier regions isolated by vast cleaner ones. The interpretation of w2 as a spatial volume fraction of RS-like regions next to "pure HAFC" regions (p. 4) only makes sense for the latter, because a dilute random-bond model has no spatially separate clean and dirty regions.
   - The 10% fraction and the 0.03 strength appear to be chosen, not derived. The authors should state how they were fixed, show how sensitive ξ(T) is to them, and show the pure chain (ΔJ = 0) analysed the same way in the Fig. 3d inset. Pure-chain ξ ∝ 1/T, possibly with log corrections, can look logarithmic over a limited window when plotted in this way.
   - ξ(T) is not w2(T). If possible, the authors should compute an NMR-relevant quantity from QMC, such as the local susceptibility distribution (which bears on the Kn upturn and the line broadening) or the fraction of sites with suppressed local correlations, and compare it with w2 and Kn quantitatively.

5. The robustness of the ILTA results (Fig. 3c,d) needs to be shown.
   - α = 0.001 was chosen by inspection. Extracted weights and peak positions from Tikhonov inversion depend on α, on the time window, and on how M(∞) and the inversion factor a are fixed.
   - At 0.03 K the slow rate is ~0.1-1 s^-1 (Fig. 3c), so the recovery has to extend to several T1s, i.e. tens of seconds. Fig. 3a ends near 10-30 s.
   - The authors should show w2(T) for several α values, state the maximum recovery delay at each temperature, and show that w2 obtained from ILTA agrees with w2 from the double-exponential fits. The double-exponential fit also fixes a common inversion factor a. They should report whether a_f and a_s were constrained, and whether they share a common M(∞).
   - A stretched-exponential fit, the standard description for random chains, should be compared with the double-exponential fit.

6. The spinon evidence at 0.2 T is narrower than the abstract states.
   - The abstract and p. 2 describe 1/T1f as nearly constant from 1.4 K to 0.03 K. Fig. 3b shows a downturn below ~0.3 K at 0.2 T, which the next paragraph acknowledges.
   - The predicted logarithmic correction (refs. 35, 41) would raise 1/T1 by roughly ln^1/2(J/T): about 1.5 times between 1.4 K and 0.03 K. It is not seen, and the paper does not explain why. A missing log correction weakens the claim that the data are a textbook realization of the HAFC.
   - The 1/T2G ∝ T^-1/2 fit spans only 0.4-2 K, a factor of 5 (Fig. 3b inset).
   - The authors should either account quantitatively for the missing log correction (for example, being cut off by the field or by the staggered field, see concern 1) or soften the claim, and state the temperature window of each fit.

7. Only one crystal was measured by NMR, and the disorder is not characterized independently.
   - The disorder is described as unintentional and of unknown origin (strain, frozen benzoate rotation), so its level should vary between crystals and growth batches. Showing the slow channel and w2(T) in a second crystal, ideally from a different batch, would separate intrinsic from sample-specific behaviour. So would measuring before and after thermal cycling.
   - The disorder strength ΔJ/J ≈ 0.02-0.05 is inferred from the same NMR energy scales it is meant to explain. An independent estimate would help: the low-temperature Curie tail in susceptibility (bulk susceptibility here stops at 1.8 K; ac susceptibility in the dilution refrigerator would reach the relevant range), a Schottky or low-T anomaly in the specific heat (Supplementary Note 3), or ESR linewidths.

8. The field misalignment is not quantified, although the staggered field depends on it. The resolved peak count is taken to show that the field is "slightly away from the crystallographic b-axis" (p. 2), but the angle is never given. In copper benzoate the staggered-field coefficient, and hence the soliton gap and the DM-induced canting, vary strongly with field direction. Both the 0.2 T analysis (concern 1) and the comparison of the gap with specific-heat and INS data (Fig. 4e) depend on this. The authors should estimate the misalignment angle, for example from the site splittings and the calculated hyperfine tensors in Supplementary Note 2, and propagate it into the staggered-field coefficient used in LTRG.

9. The extraction of the high-field gap needs more detail.
   - **The Kn fit.** Kn = a e^{-Δ/kBT} + b has three free parameters, and b is attributed to zero-temperature canting. The authors should state the fit range and show that b agrees with the LTRG canting value, rather than leaving b free.
   - **The 1/T1 fits.** The fits in Fig. 4d use a single activated form at the P5 site, whose assignment is uncertain. Sine-Gordon theory predicts different activation energies (Δ or 2Δ, and possibly breather contributions) depending on the process and the form factor. The authors should explain why the activation energy should equal the soliton gap Δ.
   - **The exponent.** α = 0.8(1) differs from 2/3. The authors should say whether the logarithmic correction to Δ ∝ H^2/3 ln^1/6(J/H) expected for the Heisenberg chain, or the field range used, accounts for this.
   - **Novelty.** Because the soliton gap in copper benzoate is already known from specific heat and INS, the authors should state clearly what the NMR determination adds.

C. Minor

10. In the Discussion, w2 "growing towards 100% in the zero-temperature limit" is an extrapolation of a logarithmic fit, which reaches 100% only near T ≈ 7 mK. It should be described as such.

11. Fig. 1 is drawn as a phase diagram with sharp-looking regions. The authors should say which data points define the boundaries, or label the figure as schematic. They should also note that the RS-like region overlaps the field range where the soliton gap is non-zero (see concern 1).

12. The symbol T0 is used both for the fitted w2 scale (0.40 K) and in the QMC inset fit ξ/a ∝ -ln(T/T0). The authors should give the QMC T0 value and use distinct symbols.

13. Fig. 3d inset: the QMC curve covers T/J ≈ 0.003-0.1. The authors should state the temperature range in kelvin used for the comparison, and report the error bars from the disorder average over the N_s = 32 realizations.

14. Fig. 4c: LTRG uses J = 18.2 K, while the text estimates J ≈ 18 K from the Bonner-Fisher peak. The authors should give the staggered-field coefficient and the DM parameters used, and the hyperfine constant used to convert M to Kn, in the main text or caption.

15. Methods, heating checks: the authors should state where the thermometer sits relative to the sample, and whether the proton line or the 1/T1 of a reference was used to confirm the sample temperature at 0.03 K. Sample thermalization in an insulator at this temperature is not automatic.

16. The statement that the "threefold increase of the FWHM upon cooling from 0.8 to 0.1 K" reveals a disorder-induced state refers to Supplementary Fig. 5b. A main-text panel, or a quantitative comparison with the broadening expected from the uniform Kn upturn alone, would make this argument checkable.

17. Methods: "Kn = (f/γH - 1) × 100%" requires H to be known to better than 10^-3 for the low-field data (see concern 3). The field calibration procedure should be stated.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one figure that places the 0.2 T data (Kn upturn, 1/T1f downturn and w2) alongside LTRG or analytic predictions for the clean chain with the actual staggered field at 0.2 T. This would show directly what remains unexplained and must be attributed to disorder.
2. Measure the slow-channel 1/T1s and w2 on the H(1) line and at several fields between 0.1 and 0.5 T. An RS-like contribution should depend on field in a characteristic way (polarization of weakly coupled singlets when gμBH ~ J_eff), whereas site overlap or impurity relaxation would not.
3. Compute an NMR observable directly from the disorder model: the distribution of local susceptibilities or local relaxation rates. This would make the comparison with w2, the line broadening and Kn quantitative, not just analogical.
4. Repeat the key low-temperature measurements on a second crystal, ideally one with deliberately increased disorder (for example, partial substitution or a faster growth). A systematic change of T0 and c with disorder level would be the most convincing evidence that the slow channel comes from quenched disorder.
