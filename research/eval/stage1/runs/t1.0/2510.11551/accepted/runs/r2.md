Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports ¹H NMR on single-crystal copper benzoate, a near-ideal S = 1/2 Heisenberg chain, down to 0.03 K and over fields from 0.2 to 10 T. It assigns three regimes. At 0.2 T, a temperature-independent fast 1/T₁ and a growing 1/T₂G are taken as signatures of spinons. Below 0.4 K, a slow, linear-in-T relaxation component whose weight grows as −ln(T/T₀), together with an upturn in the Knight shift, is taken as random-singlet-like behaviour. Above 1 T, an activated gap scaling roughly as H^{2/3} is taken as evidence for sine-Gordon solitons. The soliton and spinon results largely confirm established physics with a local probe. The new and potentially significant claim is the random-singlet-like crossover driven by weak quenched disorder in a nominally clean chain. That claim also rests on the most indirect evidence.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- Very low temperatures (30 mK) and fields (0.2 T) for NMR on a quasi-1D magnet, with careful site assignment and good spectral resolution (Fig. 2).
- A clear temperature-independent 1/T₁ at 0.2 T over a wide window, a textbook spinon signature that is rarely seen this cleanly.
- The field-induced gap is consistent across two NMR observables (K_n and 1/T₁) and agrees with earlier specific-heat and INS data (Fig. 4e). The LTRG calculation in Fig. 4c gives a quantitative account of the high-field K_n upturn.
- Reasonable heating checks are documented (Methods).
- Connecting the claims to the anomalous low-temperature thermal conductivity of the same batch (ref. 29) is a useful cross-link.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The rise of the slow-component weight w₂ may be an artifact of T₂ weighting rather than a growing "RS-like volume fraction".** The headline RS-like evidence is that w₂ grows logarithmically below T₀ ≈ 0.4 K (Fig. 3d) and is described as reaching ~65% at 0.03 K and "growing towards 100%". Yet the paper states that 1/T₂G diverges on cooling and "becomes too short to be reliably measured" below 0.4 K (Fig. 3b inset, p. 2). That is exactly where the slow component appears. In a spin-echo measurement, nuclei that couple strongly to the critical spinon fluctuations (fast T₁, short T₂) lose echo amplitude at fixed τ. Nuclei with slow T₁, which plausibly also have long T₂, keep theirs. The apparent fraction of the slow component would then grow on cooling even if the true population were fixed.
   - The w₂ from spectral analysis (Supplementary Fig. 5c) would be affected in the same way, so it is not an independent check.
   - Requested: give τ and the T₂ of each component at each temperature. Measure M(t) at several echo delays and extrapolate the component amplitudes to τ → 0. Show that total signal intensity × T is conserved through 0.4 K, i.e. that no "missing" fast-relaxing intensity appears.
   - Without this, the logarithmic growth of w₂, the T₀ and c parameters, and the "effective volume fraction" picture are not established.

2. **The low-field "RS-like" signatures need to be separated from the staggered-field (DM / g-tensor) effects that the authors themselves invoke at higher field.** At 0.2 T the field-induced gap should be of order 0.1 K: extrapolating Δ ∝ H^{2/3} or H^{0.8} from Fig. 4e. That is comparable to the 0.03–0.4 K window where the RS-like features are claimed. The paper already attributes the downturn of 1/T₁f below 0.3 K at 0.2 T to solitons (p. 2). The K_n upturn below 0.8 K at 0.2 T (Fig. 3e) looks qualitatively like the high-field upturns in Fig. 4b, which LTRG explains through DM-induced spin canting (Fig. 4c), moved to lower temperature.
   - Requested: run the same LTRG calculation (staggered field + DM) at 0.2 T and 0.5 T and overlay it on Fig. 3e. If the clean staggered-field model already gives an upturn of the observed size, the K_n evidence for an RS-like state disappears.
   - Similarly, show that the slow component and its weight are field-independent below ~0.5 T, using the 0.5 and 1.0 T ILTA maps (Supplementary Fig. 10) presented quantitatively. Its behaviour should differ from what a soliton gap ∝ H^{2/3} would produce.
   - Ideally add data at a lower field (e.g. 0.1 T) or with H along a direction that minimises the staggered field. In copper benzoate the staggered-field strength depends strongly on field orientation, which gives a natural null control.

3. **The link between the observations and a random-singlet state is qualitative, and some features do not match what RS theory predicts.**
   - In RS scenarios, the local susceptibility and local relaxation rates have a broad, continuous distribution. The NMR literature on random Heisenberg chains (ref. 50) finds broad, stretched-exponential relaxation, not two discrete components two orders of magnitude apart. Fig. 3c instead shows a bimodal P(1/T₁) with a gap between the modes.
   - The specific prediction 1/T₁s ∝ T is attributed to "quasi-localised spin excitations" via a thermal-conductivity reference (ref. 49). No NMR-relevant calculation is given.
   - The QMC (Fig. 3d inset) computes only a correlation length, which is then linked to w₂ by a heuristic ("increasing effective correlation length").
   - Requested: compute, with the same disordered model, a quantity NMR actually measures. Examples are the distribution of local susceptibilities (for K_n and the line broadening) or the distribution of local 1/T₁ (via QMC + analytic continuation, or real-time TN methods as already used in ref. 52). Then show that it produces a bimodal distribution, a linear-in-T slow mode, and a −ln T weight.
   - Also explain physically why a nucleus in an "RS-like region" relaxes 100× slower and linearly in T, while one in a "clean region" keeps the spinon rate. With ΔJ/J ≈ 0.03 on 10% of bonds, every chain segment of ~10 sites contains a modified bond, so it is unclear what the two spatial regions are.
   - Absent this, "random-singlet-like" should be presented as a hypothesis, and the title and abstract claims softened.

**B. Should address**

4. **Origin of the slow component: site and mechanism alternatives are not excluded.** P₅ is assigned "either to the H(3) site in water molecules or to protons in the benzoate groups" (p. 2). Benzoate protons have weak, mostly dipolar coupling. A slow component could come from such weakly coupled protons, from protons near paramagnetic defects or chain ends, or from a spin-diffusion bottleneck. The slow component could also come from freezing of water-molecule or proton motion below ~0.4 K, which would give a T-dependent spectral weight that has nothing to do with Cu spins. Supplementary Note 5 reportedly rules out spectral overlap using frequency dependence, but that argument should be summarised in the main text.
   - Requested: measure 1/T₁ and the two-component structure at another resolved site (e.g. H(1), P₁/P₂). Disorder-induced RS physics in the Cu chain should affect all sites coupled to Cu, scaled by their hyperfine couplings. Contamination by a second proton species would not.
   - Also report the ratio 1/T₁s / 1/T₁f across sites.

5. **Robustness of the ILTA and the w₂ extraction.** The Tikhonov parameter α = 0.001 is chosen by inspection (Methods), and the text notes that α "directly influences the distribution's width". w₂ is extracted from double-Lorentzian fits to the ILTA output, and an independent double-exponential fit also exists. Requested:
   - show w₂(T) for α spanning at least two decades;
   - compare w₂ with the amplitude a_s from the direct double-exponential fit;
   - give the number of recovery points and the S/N at each temperature;
   - state how the error bars in Fig. 3d were propagated.
   The −ln(T/T₀) form is fitted to roughly six points over one decade. Show that a power law w₂ ∝ T^{−x}, or a smooth crossover form, fits measurably worse (χ² or information criterion) before giving the logarithm physical meaning.

6. **The spinon signature, and the absence of the log correction, need more care.**
   - The text says 1/T₁f at 0.2 T is "nearly constant between 1.4 and 0.03 K". It also says 1/T₁f at 0.2 T "shows a weak downturn below 0.3 K" of field-induced origin (p. 2), and that it "remains broadly temperature-independent before showing field-induced suppression below 0.2 K" (p. 3). These need to be reconciled, and the range used to support the spinon claim stated precisely.
   - For the HAFC, theory predicts 1/T₁ ∝ ln^{1/2}(J/T), which over T/J ≈ 0.08 → 0.002 should be a sizeable increase. Its absence is noted but not explained.
   - The P₅ site's hyperfine form factor may filter the staggered (q = π) fluctuations that carry the log correction. Give the form factor at q = 0 and q = π for P₅, using the Supplementary Note 2 couplings, and discuss whether the observed constant is dominated by q = π.
   - Alternatively, compare with 1/T₁ at H(1).

7. **1/T₂G analysis.** The Gaussian T₂G in a hydrated organic compound has a large proton–proton direct dipolar contribution. The indirect (electron-mediated) contribution that diverges as T^{−1/2} must be separated from it. Requested:
   - state the calculated dipolar second moment;
   - show that it is subtracted, or that it is negligible;
   - note that the T^{−1/2} fit covers only ~0.4–2 K (half a decade), and add error bars on the exponent.
   The abstract's claim of a "divergence-like" 1/T₂G "upon cooling" is otherwise stronger than the data support.

8. **Single NMR crystal; disorder not characterised independently.** The NMR data come from one crystal (Methods). The disorder strength ΔJ/J ≈ 0.02–0.05 and the 10% bimodal-bond model are inferred, not measured, and the disorder "likely of the domain type" (Discussion) is speculation. Because the central claim is disorder-driven, requested:
   - reproduce the slow component and the w₂(T) behaviour on a second crystal, ideally from a different growth;
   - a check after thermal cycling;
   - any structural characterisation (X-ray rocking curves, mosaicity, water content / stoichiometry) that bounds the disorder.
   A deliberately disordered or deuterated/aged sample with a larger slow-component weight would be a strong positive control.

9. **The QMC needs a clean-chain reference and finite-size checks.**
   - The clean HAFC correlation length also grows on cooling (ξ ∝ J/T). The inset of Fig. 3d should therefore include the ΔJ = 0 result on the same axes, to show that the −ln T form is caused by disorder.
   - With L = 256 and open boundaries, the lowest T/J ≈ 0.003 gives ξ of order hundreds of sites in the clean limit. Finite-size saturation could mimic a slower-than-power-law growth. Show results for at least two L.
   - Justify the choice of 10% bonds and ΔJ = 0.03, and show sensitivity to these choices.

10. **K_n upturn fit and the "artifact" below 0.2 K.**
   - The empirical K_n ∝ T^{−1} ln^{−2}(Ω/T) is fitted over a narrow window (Fig. 3e). Compare it with a simple Curie (or Curie–Weiss) term from a small concentration of weakly coupled free spins, and quote the implied impurity concentration.
   - The downturn below 0.2 K is dismissed as an S/N artifact from signal beyond the spectrometer window. That should be demonstrated, for example by spectra over a wider frequency range, or by showing that the integrated intensity × T drops at the same temperature.
   - Alternatively, the downturn could reflect the soliton gap at 0.2 T (see concern 2) or imperfect thermalisation (concern 12).

11. **Field-induced gap extraction and exponent.**
   - The fitted exponent α = 0.8(1) is described as in "good consistency" with 2/3. Consider whether the log correction to Δ (∝ H^{2/3} ln^{1/6}) or a two-parameter fit would close the gap.
   - The activated forms are heuristic. For K_n = a e^{−Δ/T} + b, the LTRG calculation (Fig. 4c) could be fitted with the same form to show that the procedure returns the input gap.
   - For 1/T₁ = a e^{−Δ/T}, state whether the relevant process is expected to give Δ or 2Δ in the exponent. Intra-branch soliton scattering versus two-soliton processes give different activation energies, which affects the comparison in Fig. 4e.
   - The field is said to be "slightly away" from b (p. 2). The misalignment angle should be estimated (e.g. from the number of resolved lines), because the staggered field and the gap depend strongly on field direction in copper benzoate. This also matters for comparing with C_p and INS data taken in specified orientations.

12. **Sample temperature at the lowest temperatures.** The heating checks (pulse power, repetition time, random delays) are good. However, the thermometer is on the holder, and the sample is an insulator whose thermal conductivity the authors say is strongly suppressed below 0.3 K (ref. 29). Requested:
   - show that the nuclear/spin temperature tracks the bath below 0.1 K, e.g. from the equilibrium NMR intensity × T (Curie law of the nuclear magnetisation) down to 0.03 K;
   - give the thermal anchoring geometry and the waiting times after temperature changes.
   This directly affects the lowest-T points that drive the −ln T fits.

**C. Minor**

13. **Inconsistent onset temperature for the two-component fits.** The Fig. 3a caption says double-exponential fits are used "at 0.1 K and below", while the text says "below 0.2 K". Please make these consistent and state which temperatures were fitted in each way.

14. **The introduction overstates the T₂G range.** It says "a nearly temperature-independent 1/T₁ and a diverging 1/T₂G ∝ T^{−1/2}" below 1.4 K. T₂G is measurable only down to 0.4 K. Please revise.

15. **Fig. 1 boundaries are schematic.** The phase-diagram boundaries are not data points. Mark which features come from measurements (e.g. T₀ at 0.2, 0.5 and 1 T; gap onset fields) and state in the caption that the boundaries are schematic.

16. **The two T₀ values are given only in the Supplementary.** The text says the two w₂ analyses give "different values of T₀". Quote both values in the main text.

17. **w₂ units.** w₂ is plotted in % and c = 25(1), so c is in % per e-fold. State the units explicitly.

18. **Mismatched temperature ranges.** Fig. 3e shows K_n down to ~0.03 K on a log axis, while the RS fit window is not marked. Shade the fitted range. Similarly, mark the fitted range on Fig. 3d and in the inset of Fig. 3b.

19. **Fig. 4e axis and labels.** The theory curve is labelled in terms of the staggered field h, while the axis is μ₀H. State the conversion h = c_s H, giving c_s and its source, used to draw the dashed line.

20. **Phase-separation claim lacks support in the main text.** The "coexistence of gapped and gapless excitations through phase separation" above 0.5 T (Discussion) rests only on the Supplement. Either support it in the main text or phrase it as a possibility.

21. **Hyperfine coupling sign convention.** Clarify the sign convention for A_hf (−0.101 vs −0.118 T/μ_B) and how the experimental value was obtained, i.e. a K–χ plot over which temperature range.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add an echo-delay series and a τ → 0 extrapolation of both relaxation-component amplitudes, together with an intensity × T conservation check across 0.4 K. This single experiment would decide whether w₂ is a real volume fraction.
2. Calculate LTRG K_n(T) and the field-induced gap at 0.2–0.5 T with the same staggered-field + DM model used at 5 T, and overlay them on Fig. 3e. Use field orientation as a null control for the staggered-field contribution.
3. Compute an NMR-relevant observable from the disordered model (distribution of local susceptibility or local 1/T₁), and include clean-chain and finite-size references for the QMC correlation length.
4. Reproduce the slow component on a second crystal and on a second proton site (H(1)). Report ILTA robustness against α and against direct double-exponential fits.
