Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports ¹H NMR on a single crystal of copper benzoate, a well-established S = 1/2 Heisenberg chain, down to 0.03 K and fields as low as 0.2 T. It claims three distinct low-energy regimes in one material: spinons (temperature-independent 1/T₁ at 0.2 T), a disorder-induced random-singlet (RS) state (a slow T₁ component with 1/T₁s ∝ T whose weight grows as −ln(T/T₀) below 0.4 K), and sine-Gordon solitons at high field (activated Kₙ and 1/T₁ with Δ ∝ H^{2/3}). The ultra-low-field, ultra-low-temperature data are new and potentially valuable. Soliton excitations and the H^{2/3} gap in copper benzoate are already established (Dender et al. 1997; Essler 1999), so the novel element is the RS claim. As presented, that claim rests on an interpretation of a two-component T₁ recovery that the paper does not yet establish.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- Technically demanding NMR on a single crystal at 30 mK and 0.2 T, a regime not reached in earlier NMR work on this compound (the earlier work in Ref. 28 used polycrystals at 0.5 T and above 1.5 K).
- Clean resolution of a fast and a slow relaxation channel below ~0.4 K, displayed through ILTA (Fig. 3c), which is an informative way to show the data.
- Gaps extracted from Kₙ and 1/T₁ (Fig. 4d) agree well with specific-heat and INS gaps, which supports the high-field soliton picture.
- tanTRG calculations of the dipolar hyperfine field (Fig. 4e) give a microscopic reason for the low-temperature upturn of Kₙ.
- The question of how weak disorder shows up in a nearly ideal Heisenberg chain is of broad interest.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The slow T₁ component is not shown to come from a random-singlet state rather than from trivial origins.** The RS identification (Fig. 3c,d; pp. 3–4) rests entirely on a second relaxation channel with a smaller rate and growing weight below 0.4 K. Several more mundane explanations are not excluded:
   (i) *Site mixing at P₅.* P₅ is assigned to "either the H(3) site in water molecules or to protons in the benzoate groups" (p. 2), and the field is admittedly off the b-axis. A line made of inequivalent protons with different hyperfine form factors gives different relaxation. In a Heisenberg chain, a site whose form factor filters out the q = π fluctuations sees only the q ≈ 0 contribution, which itself gives 1/T₁ ∝ T and a much smaller rate. That is exactly the phenomenology attributed here to the RS state.
   (ii) *T₂ contrast and signal wipeout.* A temperature-dependent weight w₂ can arise if the fast-relaxing nuclei lose signal through a shortening T₂ on cooling, or if pulse-excitation conditions change.
   (iii) *Paramagnetic impurities or chain ends* acting as local relaxation centres.

   The authors should show:
   - the integrated spectral intensity × T versus T at 0.2 T, to test for wipeout;
   - T₂ of both components;
   - the T₁ recovery measured on other lines (P₁–P₄, H(1)/H(2)) to see whether the slow component appears on every site with the same weight;
   - the w₂(T) behaviour at a second field (0.5 T, now only in Supp. Fig. S3) and in a second orientation.

   Without these, the "two spatially distinct contributions" picture (p. 4) is an assumption.

2. **The mapping from w₂ to a correlation length, and the −ln T form itself, are not justified and conflict with RS theory.**
   - The text states that "the increase of w₂ directly reflects the growth of the thermal correlation length ξ" (p. 4). No argument is given for why the fraction of nuclei in the slow channel should be proportional to ξ.
   - For the infinite-randomness fixed point (Fisher, Ref. 16), the length scale at energy T grows as ξ ∝ ln²(Ω₀/T), not ln(1/T). The statement that ξ ∼ T^{−1/z} with z → ∞ "signifies" a ln T law does not fix the power of the logarithm.
   - The experimental fit covers about one decade (0.03–0.3 K, roughly eight points, Fig. 3d) with no error bars. It cannot distinguish ln T from ln² T, a weak power law, or a crossover function.
   - The extrapolation that w₂ → 1 at about 8 mK is then an artefact of the chosen form.

   The QMC result (Fig. 3e) does not rescue this. It covers T/J ≈ 0.005–0.1 with five points. The definition of ξ, the system sizes, the number of disorder realizations, the error bars, the fitted T₀, and a comparison with the clean chain (where ξ ∝ J/T) are not given in the main text. The authors should:
   - give a derivation, or at least a model calculation, connecting w₂ to a physical length or to the density of unpaired spins;
   - compare ln and ln² fits with uncertainties;
   - show QMC ξ(T) for both the clean and the disordered chain over a wider range;
   - ideally compute the NMR observables (the 1/T₁ distribution and its weights) directly for the disordered chain, for example with the tanTRG method already used for Fig. 4e.

3. **At 0.2 T the field is not negligible, by the paper's own gap formula, in the temperature range where spinons and the RS state are claimed.**
   - Scaling the measured Δ(5 T) ≈ 0.2 meV (Fig. 4d) with H^{2/3} gives Δ(0.2 T) ≈ 0.2 meV × (0.04)^{2/3} ≈ 0.023 meV ≈ 0.27 K. The Zeeman energy is also about 0.27 K. Both are comparable to T₀ = 0.4 K and lie well above the 0.03 K base temperature.
   - The paper itself notes a downturn of 1/T₁f at 0.2 T below 0.3 K (p. 3). This contradicts the abstract's and Fig. 3b's claim of a temperature-independent 1/T₁ "from 1.4 K down to 0.03 K", which establishes spinons "over more than a decade".
   - The same staggered-field gap must also affect the putative RS regions. The argument on p. 5 that field-polarized singlets with Δ ≈ gμ_BH give "effective gapless excitations" is qualitative and does not address the staggered-field gap at all.

   The authors should:
   - restrict the claimed spinon window to the range where 1/T₁f is actually flat;
   - quantify Δ(0.2 T) from their own fits;
   - measure (or at least show from existing data) how the slow component and w₂ evolve between 0.2, 0.5 and 1 T;
   - demonstrate that the RS signatures extrapolate to zero field rather than being shaped by the field-induced gap.

**B. Should address**

4. **The disorder is unidentified and its strength is assumed.** The QMC uses ΔJ/J ≈ 0.2 (p. 4). The paper does not say how this value was chosen, nor what in a "high-quality" crystal produces 20% bond randomness (water disorder, dehydration, Cu vacancies, impurities). For weak bond disorder in the Heisenberg chain, the crossover to the RS fixed point is expected at energies that are exponentially small in 1/(ΔJ/J)². An onset at T₀ ≈ 0.02J therefore needs justification. Describing the disorder as "weak" while it controls 60% of the relaxation weight at 0.04 K needs discussion. The authors should:
   - provide independent characterization: the low-T Curie tail of χ, impurity concentration, NMR linewidth versus T, and sample stoichiometry and water content;
   - relate ΔJ/J to these;
   - show how T₀ and a in the QMC depend on ΔJ/J.

5. **Only one crystal was measured.** All low-field and RS data come from one crystal (Methods). The comparison with Ref. 28 is attributed to sample form and conditions. Repeating the low-temperature T₁ recovery on a second crystal would test the robustness of the RS signature. This is especially true of a crystal from another growth batch, or one with deliberately varied disorder (e.g., partial dehydration or dilute nonmagnetic substitution). A systematic change of T₀ and a with disorder level would be the most direct evidence for a disorder origin.

6. **The ILTA and two-component fits need robustness checks and uncertainties.**
   - P(1/T₁) and w₂ come from Tikhonov-regularized ILTA, whose peak positions, widths and weights depend on noise and on the regularization parameter. The regularization choice, its effect on w₂, and error bars for w₂ in Fig. 3d should be given.
   - w₂ from ILTA should be compared with a_s/(a_f + a_s) from the biexponential fits.
   - The text says single-exponential fits are used above 0.4 K and biexponential fits "below 0.1 K" (p. 2; Fig. 3a caption: "0.1 K and below"). Yet w₂ is nonzero from about 0.3 K. The fitting procedure between 0.1 and 0.4 K should be stated.
   - The breadth of the slow-component distribution is cited as evidence for RS physics (p. 4). That breadth is partly set by the regularization, so its intrinsic width must be shown to be resolution-limited or not.

7. **The spinon assignment is only qualitative.**
   - A constant 1/T₁ is consistent with the q = π contribution of the HAFC, but the paper does not compare the magnitude of 1/T₁ with theory (Sachdev, Ref. 33).
   - It also does not consider the expected logarithmic correction, 1/T₁ ∝ ln^{1/2}(J/T), or the q ≈ 0 term ∝ T.
   - With J ≈ 18 K and the hyperfine coupling of P₅ obtainable from a Kₙ–χ plot, a quantitative estimate is possible and would make the spinon claim much stronger.
   - The hyperfine form factor of P₅ for q = π should also be given, since it controls whether the staggered fluctuations are seen at all; this links to concern 1.

8. **The Kₙ "activation" fits and their use to extract a soliton gap are not well motivated.**
   - On p. 5 the low-temperature upturn of |Kₙ| is assigned to the field-induced staggered magnetization, a ground-state order parameter that grows on cooling.
   - Yet Δ is extracted by fitting Kₙ = a e^{−Δ/k_BT} + b, which is the functional form of a thermally activated uniform susceptibility. That form would make the shift decrease toward zero, not grow and saturate.
   - The authors should explain why the temperature evolution of the staggered moment follows this form with the soliton gap as its scale, or fit the tanTRG Hₕf(T) curve, which has a known gap, with the same form to show that it recovers Δ.
   - The comparison in Fig. 4e is qualitative: arbitrary units, with a low-T/peak ratio of about 3 in the calculation against about 1.8 in the 5 T data of Fig. 4b. The purely dipolar hyperfine assumption for H(1), a water proton bonded to Cu-coordinated oxygen, should be tested by comparing calculated and measured hyperfine constants.
   - Averaging paired peaks to obtain Kₙ (p. 2) may average away a staggered contribution that should split the lines; this needs clarification.

9. **The 2 T relaxation contradicts a simple gapped picture.** In Fig. 4c the 2 T 1/T₁ passes through a minimum near 1–2 K and then rises on cooling to about 20 s⁻¹ at the lowest temperatures. A gap of roughly 0.15 meV (≈1.7 K) at 2 T is inferred from Kₙ in Fig. 4d. The low-T rise is not discussed, and no 1/T₁ gap is reported at 2 T. The authors should explain the rise (e.g., a slow component, disorder, or in-gap states), and say whether a two-component analysis like that at 0.2 T was attempted at high field. That analysis bears directly on whether the RS channel persists into the soliton regime, as Fig. 1 suggests.

10. **The gap formula and the field alignment need specification.**
    - The fitted relation Δ = 1.85J(h/J)^{2/3} (p. 5; Fig. 4d) does not define h. With h = gμ_BH and J = 18 K it would give Δ(5 T) ≈ 17 K, an order of magnitude too large. The staggered-field ratio c (h_s = c·gμ_BH) and the g-factor used must be stated, and the omitted logarithmic correction [ln(J/h_s)]^{1/6} of Oshikawa–Affleck discussed.
    - The staggered field depends strongly on field orientation, and the five-peak spectra show the field is off the b-axis. The misalignment angle should be estimated from the line positions, with its effect on c and Δ.

11. **Sample temperature at 30 mK needs to be established.** The base-temperature points (the 0.03 K recovery in Fig. 3a, w₂ at 0.03–0.06 K, and the 60 mK spectra in Fig. 4a) underpin both the RS logarithm and the spinon plateau. The authors should show that rf heating from the π/2 pulses and the eddy-current or thermal-link limitations do not raise the sample temperature: vary the pulse power and repetition time, and check the thermometry against, e.g., the nuclear Curie law of the signal intensity.

12. **The phase diagram (Fig. 1) and the thermal-conductivity link go beyond the data.**
    - Fig. 1 draws an RS region extending to about 3 T at T → 0 and a spinon region down to zero field, but the RS signature is shown only at 0.2 T (and 0.5 T in the SI). The boundaries should be data points, or the figure should be labelled explicitly as a sketch with the measured (H, T) points overlaid.
    - The statement that the RS state "readily accounts for" the thermal-conductivity downturn below 0.3 K (pp. 4–5) is speculative. It is unclear whether the ordering of temperature scales and the magnitude of suppression are quantitatively consistent.

13. **Novelty claims should be calibrated.** The abstract says the work identifies "all three excitation types" and the Discussion says "unambiguously identified", "first direct NMR evidence". Solitons and the H^{2/3} gap in copper benzoate are established by specific heat, INS and ESR, and constant 1/T₁ is the textbook spinon signature. Given concerns 1–3, the RS identification is better phrased as "consistent with" until the controls above are added.

**C. Minor**

14. Fig. 3d: the fitted amplitude a = 25 should carry units (% per e-fold), and the fit range should be stated. The constant zeros above 0.4 K appear to be drawn on the fit line rather than being independent results.
15. Fig. 3e: give T₀ for the QMC fit, the lattice size and the number of disorder realizations, and add error bars.
16. Fig. 4e caption and text: "H/J = 0.383" (caption) and "H = 0.383J (corresponding to 5 T)" (text) implicitly fix gμ_B. State the g-factor used and keep the notation for h consistent with the gap formula.
17. Fig. 2c and 4b: the inverted Kₙ axis (negative up) should be noted in the captions. Also state the sign convention for the hyperfine coupling, so that "upturn" is unambiguous.
18. Fig. 3b: the 0.5 T and 1 T data are plotted without discussion of their slow components. State whether only the fast component is shown.
19. p. 2: "Peak P₅ is attributed to either the H(3) site … or to protons in the benzoate groups". Clarify whether it is a superposition. Also explain how three peaks at 0.2 T and five at 2 T are consistent with a single fixed misalignment.
20. p. 5: "two inequivalent tetragonal axes" should say "local tetragonal (Jahn–Teller) axes of the two inequivalent Cu octahedra", or similar.
21. Ref. 45 is an arXiv preprint; update it if published. Supplementary Eq. S3 (Hamiltonian) should be summarized in the main text, given the tanTRG parameters "fitted to copper benzoate".
22. Methods: give the T₁ recovery saturation/inversion conditions and the spectral width excited by the 2 μs pulse relative to the P₅ linewidth, since partial excitation of overlapping lines can itself produce multi-exponential recovery.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Run a site-resolved, multi-field T₁ study at base temperature: two-component analysis on every resolved line at 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1 and 2 T, together with signal intensity × T and T₂. This would settle whether the slow channel is a bulk, disorder-driven property or a site- or field-specific effect.
2. Compute the NMR observables directly from theory. Use the disordered-chain QMC or tanTRG with the actual hyperfine form factors to predict P(1/T₁) and its weights, and compare these quantitatively with Fig. 3c,d, instead of relating w₂ to ξ by analogy.
3. Vary the disorder, using a second crystal with different growth conditions or controlled dilution, and show that T₀ and a track the disorder strength.
4. Give quantitative spinon and soliton checks: compare the magnitude of 1/T₁ at 0.2 T with Sachdev's prediction using measured hyperfine couplings, and fit the gap with a stated staggered-field ratio and alignment angle.
