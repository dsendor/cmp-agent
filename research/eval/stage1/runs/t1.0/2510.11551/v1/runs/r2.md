Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports ¹H NMR on a single crystal of the S = 1/2 Heisenberg chain copper benzoate, at fields down to 0.2 T and temperatures down to 0.03 K. It claims that three kinds of low-energy excitation can be told apart in one material: spinons (a temperature-independent 1/T₁ at 0.2 T), a disorder-induced random-singlet (RS) state (a second, slow relaxation channel below 0.4 K whose spectral weight grows as −ln(T/T₀)), and field-induced sine-Gordon solitons (activated K_n and 1/T₁ with Δ ∝ H^{2/3}). Seeing all three in one compound would be of broad interest. The RS claim is the new part. The spinon and soliton results largely confirm earlier specific-heat, INS, ESR and NMR work on this compound and on related staggered-field chains. As it stands, the RS identification rests on a chain of interpretive steps that are not demonstrated. This is my main reservation.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The experiments are technically demanding: single-crystal ¹H NMR down to 30 mK and 0.2 T, together with full frequency-swept spectra from 100 K to 60 mK (Figs. 2, 4a).
- The 0.2 T data show a nearly temperature-independent 1/T₁f over about a decade. Together with the field dependence in Fig. 3b, this gives a useful NMR view of the crossover from the critical chain to the gapped staggered-field regime.
- The NMR gaps from K_n and 1/T₁ (Fig. 4d) agree reasonably with the specific-heat gaps and follow H^{2/3}. This is a self-consistent check of the Oshikawa–Affleck/Essler picture by a local probe.
- The tanTRG calculation in Fig. 4e, with and without the staggered component, is a good way to trace the low-temperature K_n upturn to the staggered magnetization.
- Using ILTA to separate a fast intrinsic channel from a slow channel is a sensible idea, and the distributions are shown directly (Fig. 3c).

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The step from the slow-component weight w₂ to a correlation length ξ, and from there to an infinite-randomness RS state, is not justified (Fig. 3d,e; p. 3–4).** w₂ is the fraction of nuclear magnetization that relaxes slowly. The text says that "within the 1D limit, the increase of w₂ directly reflects the growth of the thermal correlation length ξ", but no derivation or model links a volume fraction of slowly relaxing protons to ξ. The headline claim "ξ ∝ −ln T" rests entirely on this identification.
   - There is also a problem with the theory being invoked. At the Dasgupta–Ma/Fisher infinite-randomness fixed point, length and energy scales are related by ln(Ω₀/Ω) ∝ L^{1/2}, so ξ ∝ ln²(T₀/T), not ln(T₀/T). The statement that ξ ∝ −ln T "signifies" an infinite-randomness fixed point with z → ∞ therefore does not follow as written.
   - The authors should either provide a physical model in which w₂ is a known function of ξ (for example, the fraction of sites within some distance of a decimated strong bond), or drop the "correlation length" language and present w₂(T) as an empirical result.
   - They should also discuss how a single logarithm fitted over about 1.1 decades with about eight points (Fig. 3d) can be told apart from a power law or from a saturating crossover. The extrapolation that "the RS state is expected to percolate throughout the entire system… near 8 mK" should be removed or labelled as speculative.

2. **At 0.2 T the system is not in the zero-field gapless regime, and the onset of the slow channel coincides with field-generated energy scales (Figs. 3b–d, 4d).** Using the H^{2/3} fit in Fig. 4d (about 0.18 meV at 4 T), the staggered-field gap expected at 0.2 T is about 0.024 meV ≈ 0.3 K. The Zeeman energy gμ_BH/k_B at 0.2 T is about 0.27 K. Both are essentially equal to the T₀ ≈ 0.4 K at which the slow component appears. They also match the "weak downturn below 0.3 K" of 1/T₁f that the authors themselves attribute to solitons.
   - This opens simpler explanations for the second channel, for example:
     - Zeeman polarization of weakly coupled or orphan spins (chain ends, defects), which suppresses their fluctuations and leaves nearby protons relaxing slowly;
     - partial opening of the soliton gap.
   - The Discussion argues that the RS picture survives a finite field, but this argument is qualitative.
   - The decisive test is the field dependence of the slow channel. The authors should give T₀, a, the onset temperature and 1/T₁s versus T at 0.5 T (Supplementary Fig. S3) and at 1 T, alongside the 0.2 T results. If T₀ scales with H, or the slow channel disappears as the gap grows, the RS interpretation becomes much weaker. If T₀ is field-independent, the RS case is strengthened.
   - The authors should also reconcile the claim of a "temperature-independent 1/T₁ … from 1.4 K to 0.03 K" with the expected ≈0.3 K gap at 0.2 T.

3. **The two-component decomposition and its assignment to spatially separated, disorder-affected regions need validation (Fig. 3a,c,d; Methods).** Three issues stand out.
   - **Which nuclei.** Peak P₅ is assigned to "either the H(3) site in water molecules or to protons in the benzoate groups". It is therefore probably a superposition of several inequivalent sites with different hyperfine couplings, and these would relax at different rates once the electronic fluctuations become anisotropic or gapped at low T. A slow component could then reflect site heterogeneity rather than disorder.
   - **Robustness of the analysis.** No evidence is given that w₂ and 1/T₁s are robust against the Tikhonov regularization parameter, the time window, or signal-to-noise. Fig. 3d has no error bars.
   - **The disorder itself.** It is never identified or quantified. There is no low-T Curie tail, impurity concentration, water-of-crystallization stoichiometry, or EPR estimate of free spins, even though the text simultaneously stresses "high-quality" crystals.

   The authors should:
   - show that the same two-component structure appears on a peak with a single, well-assigned site, such as P₁–P₄ of H(1)/H(2), or explain why that is impossible;
   - show the ILTA stability tests and the error bars on w₂;
   - compare the ILTA with a direct biexponential fit and with a stretched-exponential fit;
   - give an independent estimate of the defect concentration and check that it is consistent with a ≈ 25%.

**B. Should address**

4. **The QMC comparison is not independent, not quantitative, and lacks a clean reference (Fig. 3e; Supplementary Sec. S4).** The bond-disorder strength ΔJ/J ≈ 0.2 is not justified by any measurement. Fig. 3e shows that ξ is logarithmic over T/J ≈ 0.005–0.1, but:
   - the same calculation for ΔJ = 0, where ξ ∝ 1/T, is not shown;
   - T₀ in the QMC fit is not given;
   - the QMC ξ is not converted into a predicted w₂ or 1/T₁ to compare with the data.

   Plotting the clean and disordered ξ(T) together, ideally for two or three values of ΔJ/J, would show whether the logarithm comes from disorder or reflects a crossover window. The authors should also explain how disorder this weak produces RS behaviour already at T/J ≈ 0.02. For weak disorder the RS crossover scale is expected to be exponentially small in (J/ΔJ)². As far as I can see, the QMC curve does not resolve this question but sits in the crossover regime.

5. **The linear-in-T slow rate, 1/T₁s ∝ T, is not connected to any RS prediction (Fig. 3c).** Korringa-like 1/T₁ ∝ T can arise from many mechanisms, including:
   - relaxation through paramagnetic centres combined with nuclear spin diffusion;
   - protons far from Cu²⁺;
   - field-polarized free spins.

   The authors should state what the random-singlet theory predicts for the distribution of 1/T₁ and its temperature dependence (for example, the spectral distribution of local relaxation rates at the RS fixed point) and compare it with Fig. 3c quantitatively. Otherwise the "gapless excitation not captured by the pure HAFC" is a separate observation, not specific evidence for an RS state.

6. **The low-temperature upturn of 1/T₁ at 2 T (Fig. 4c), and the non-monotonic behaviour at 1 T (Fig. 3b), are not discussed.** The 2 T data in Fig. 4c reach a minimum near 1 K and then increase by about an order of magnitude on further cooling, although the gap at 2 T (Fig. 4d, ≈ 0.12 meV ≈ 1.4 K) predicts activated suppression. This in-gap relaxation is not explained. It may be the high-field counterpart of the disorder channel, which would be informative for the phase diagram, or it may be an experimental artefact. The authors should:
   - state whether this is the fast or the slow component;
   - apply the same ILTA analysis at 1 and 2 T;
   - explain why only the 5 T and 10 T 1/T₁ data are used for gap fits.

7. **The assignment of the activation energy to the soliton mass is not justified against breathers or the form of the relaxation process (Fig. 4b–d).** In the sine-Gordon description of copper benzoate (Essler 1999), the spectrum contains solitons, antisolitons and breathers, and the lightest breather B₁ can lie at or below the soliton mass. K_n probes thermal populations, so its activation energy should be set by the lightest excitation. At the NMR frequency, 1/T₁ relaxation requires two-particle (Raman-type) processes, and their activation energy need not equal the single-particle mass.
   - The fit forms a·e^{−Δ/k_BT} + b for K_n and a·e^{−Δ/k_BT} for 1/T₁ are used without justification.
   - The "excellent agreement" with INS is overstated. In Fig. 4d the INS points at about 7–8 T lie well below the NMR points.

   The authors should state which excitation each probe measures, justify the fit forms (or use the sine-Gordon expressions for the susceptibility and for 1/T₁), and give the uncertainties on Δ.

8. **Field orientation and the definition of the parameter h (Fig. 4d; p. 2, 4–5).** The authors say that the field is "slightly off" b, as shown by the five rather than four peaks, but they do not quantify the misalignment. In copper benzoate the staggered-field coefficient, and hence the gap prefactor, depends strongly on field direction. The specific-heat and INS data in Fig. 4d come from other studies with possibly different orientations.
   - The formula Δ = 1.85J(h/J)^{2/3} is quoted without defining h. If h = gμ_BH, it would give Δ ≈ 0.5 meV at 1 T, far above the data, so h must be the staggered field c·H with a specific c.
   - The authors should give the misalignment angle and how it was determined, the value of c used, and the field directions of the literature data. They should also comment on whether a common curve is legitimate.

9. **The effective sample temperature at 30–100 mK is not established.** All the RS evidence lies below 0.4 K, and the slow channel is defined by its temperature dependence. The paper does not report:
   - rf pulse power, repetition time or duty cycle;
   - checks for rf heating, for example 1/T₁ or K_n independent of pulse power and repetition rate at 30 mK;
   - how the crystal is thermally anchored.

   A brief demonstration that the lowest points are thermalized is needed, since saturation of 1/T₁ or of w₂ (the flat w₂ ≈ 0 points above T₀ aside) could otherwise be thermal.

10. **The tanTRG comparison for K_n is qualitative (Fig. 4e).** H_hf is shown in arbitrary units, the Hamiltonian parameters (staggered g-tensor and Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya terms) are only said to be "fitted to copper benzoate", and the dipolar-only coupling to H(1) is assumed. To support the claim that the computed upturn "closely reproduces the experimental data", the authors should:
    - convert H_hf to K_n using the known H(1) position and a computed dipolar tensor;
    - overlay the calculation on the measured K_n(T) at 5 T;
    - list the parameter values and their sources.

    They should also say whether a staggered moment would split the H(1) line, given that two inequivalent Cu environments are present, rather than only shift it.

11. **The phase diagram in Fig. 1 extends beyond the data.** The RS region is drawn up to about 3 T at T → 0, and the spinon–soliton boundary is drawn as a smooth crossover across the whole H–T plane. However, RS behaviour is analysed only at 0.2 T (plus 0.5 T in the supplement), and 1/T₁ data exist at only a handful of fields. The boundaries should be drawn from measured points (T₀ and Δ at each field), or the figure should state plainly which features are conjecture.

12. **The spinon identification needs a little more care (Fig. 3b).** For the S = 1/2 HAFC, 1/T₁ has a q ≈ π contribution with multiplicative logarithmic corrections, plus a q = 0 diffusive contribution. Whether the weakly coupled P₅ site filters the staggered fluctuations (the hyperfine form factor) affects which term is seen.
    - "Constant 1/T₁ over a decade" is a necessary signature but not a unique one. The authors should state the form-factor expectation for the P₅ site and whether the data can resolve the expected logarithmic variation.
    - The assignment of P₅ itself should be pinned down, since it affects both this point and Concern 3.

13. **Sample reproducibility.** Only one crystal is reported. Because the RS claim depends on quenched disorder, which differs from crystal to crystal, measuring at least one more crystal (ideally one with a different or deliberately increased defect density) would turn a single observation into a controlled test. A change in a and T₀ that tracks the defect density would be strong evidence for the RS interpretation.

**C. Minor**

14. The explanation of the earlier 1/T₁ ∼ T^{−0.25} result (Ajiro 1977) by "different experimental conditions" is hand-waving. The authors' own 0.5 T data could test directly whether a powder average at 0.5 T and above 1.5 K reproduces that exponent.
15. Fig. 3d and Fig. 4b–d lack error bars, or the caption does not say how they were obtained. The fitted parameters (T₀ = 0.4 K, a = 25, Δ values) should be given with uncertainties.
16. Fig. 3e: define T₀ for the QMC fit, state the system size and disorder distribution in the caption, and say how ξ was extracted (second moment, exponential fit, average over disorder realizations?).
17. Fig. 3c: the colour scale "P(T₁⁻¹)" is not normalized, and the dotted and solid lines are guides rather than fits. Please say so, and state the regularization parameter in the caption.
18. In Fig. 2c, K_n for H(1) at 2 T is shown on an inverted axis with negative values, and the text says K_n "increases" on cooling. Please be consistent about sign versus magnitude.
19. Wording: "unambiguously identified", "first direct NMR evidence" and "long-sought RS state" are stronger than the evidence supports. Please soften them, or support them once Concerns 1–3 are addressed.
20. The Methods should give the measurement frequencies, the pulse sequences used at 30 mK, the recovery-curve time windows and the field-calibration method. The data-availability statement ("upon request") should ideally be replaced by a public deposit.
21. "J ≈ 18 K, consistent with previous reports": please give the literature values (for example, Dender et al.) for direct comparison.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a field series of the ILTA analysis (0.2, 0.5, 1, 2 T) at low temperature, with T₀, a and 1/T₁s versus H. This single figure would separate the RS interpretation from Zeeman-polarized free spins or gap opening, and would place the Fig. 1 boundaries on measured points.
2. Repeat the slow-channel analysis on a well-assigned single-site peak (H(1) or H(2)), and on a second crystal with a different defect density. Pair this with an independent defect estimate (low-T susceptibility Curie tail or EPR).
3. Replace "w₂ reflects ξ" with an explicit model. For example, simulate the strong-disorder RG or QMC for ΔJ/J ≈ 0.2, compute the fraction of protons near decimated singlets and the distribution of local 1/T₁, and compare w₂(T) and P(1/T₁) directly. Show the clean-chain ξ(T) alongside.
4. Make the high-field analysis quantitative: specify the field orientation and staggered-field coefficient, use sine-Gordon expressions for the soliton/breather contributions to K_n and 1/T₁ in place of generic activation laws, and explain the low-temperature 1/T₁ upturn at 1–2 T.
