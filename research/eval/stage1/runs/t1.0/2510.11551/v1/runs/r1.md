Review outline:

Note: no supplementary information was provided with this submission, so this review is based on the main text and figures only. Where a concern may already be answered in the Supplementary Sections cited (S1, S3, S4), the request is to bring the key evidence into the main text or to point to it explicitly.

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports 1H NMR on a single crystal of copper benzoate, a well-studied S = 1/2 Heisenberg chain, down to 0.03 K and 0.2 T. It claims three regimes: spinons (temperature-independent 1/T1 at 0.2 T), a disorder-induced random-singlet (RS) state (a slow, linear-in-T relaxation channel whose ILTA spectral weight grows as -ln(T/T0)), and field-induced sine-Gordon solitons (activated Knight shift and 1/T1 with Delta proportional to H^(2/3)). The soliton part largely confirms earlier INS, specific-heat and NMR work on this compound and related chains. The new and potentially important claim is the NMR identification of an RS state produced by weak disorder at about 0.02J. As presented, that claim rests on one inversion-derived quantity from one crystal at essentially one field, with an interpretation (w2 proportional to a correlation length proportional to -ln T) that is not derived.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The measurements reach an unusually low combination of field and temperature (0.2 T, 0.03 K) for NMR on a quasi-1D antiferromagnet. The data set covers 0.2 to 10 T and 0.03 to 100 K.
- The bimodal relaxation below 0.4 K at 0.2 T (Fig. 3a, 3c) is a clear observation that needs an explanation. A site-resolved probe that separates a pure-chain channel from a disorder-affected channel is attractive.
- The field-induced gap from the Knight shift and from 1/T1 (Fig. 4d) agrees with the earlier INS and specific-heat gaps, which is a useful independent cross-check.
- The tanTRG calculation (Fig. 4e) points to the staggered magnetization as the source of the low-temperature Kn upturn. This is a physically sensible and testable mechanism.
- The work could connect to the anomalous thermal-conductivity downturn below 0.3 K reported in this compound.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The slow relaxation component has not been shown to be intrinsic to the chain or caused by disorder, and simpler explanations have not been ruled out (Fig. 3a, 3c, 3d; "Slow T1 component" section). The RS claim depends entirely on the slow component 1/T1s and its weight w2, measured on peak P5. The paper says P5 may come from "either the H(3) site in water molecules or protons in the benzoate groups" (and Fig. 2a shows the lines are not fully resolved at 0.2 T). Overlapping proton sites with very different hyperfine couplings would give exactly a two-component recovery with rates two orders of magnitude apart, because 1/T1 scales with the square of the hyperfine coupling. Other candidates that fit the data equally well are:
   - nuclei relaxing through a channel that becomes visible only once the fast channel saturates;
   - a field-induced effect. At 0.2 T, the authors' own Delta proportional to H^(2/3) law (scaled from about 0.2 meV at 5 T in Fig. 4d) gives a gap of roughly 0.2 to 0.3 K. That is close to T0 = 0.4 K, where the slow component appears.

   The authors should:
   - (i) measure the recovery at low temperature on a well-resolved, single-site line (P1 to P4, assigned to H(1) and H(2)) and show whether the same slow component with the same w2(T) appears there;
   - (ii) show the field dependence of T0 and a. The 0.5 T data in Supplementary Fig. S3 should be analysed like Fig. 3d, with 1 T added if possible. A T0 that tracks the H^(2/3) gap would point to a field-induced origin; a field-independent T0 would support disorder;
   - (iii) quote the slow fraction implied by site multiplicities, if P5 contains several sites.

   Without these checks, the slow channel cannot be attributed to an RS state.

2. The link between the ILTA weight w2 and a correlation length, and the claimed log law, are not derived and do not obviously agree with RS theory (Fig. 3d, 3e; p. 4, "Within the 1D limit, the increase of w2 directly reflects the growth of the thermal correlation length"). No argument is given for why the fraction of nuclei in the slow channel should be proportional to a correlation length. In Fisher's infinite-randomness RS fixed point, the length scale at energy T grows as ln^2(Omega0/T) (psi = 1/2), not as ln(1/T). The fraction of spins still undecimated falls as 1/ln^2(Omega0/T). Neither gives w2 linear in ln T. The manuscript also invokes "an infinite-randomness fixed point with z -> infinity" while reporting xi proportional to -ln T, which is not the infinite-randomness scaling.

   The fit in Fig. 3d spans about one decade (0.03 to 0.3 K) with roughly six points. The w2 = 0 points above 0.4 K are excluded by construction, and the extrapolation to w2 = 1 at 8 mK is a long reach. On the QMC (Fig. 3e), the paper does not state:
   - how xi is defined (from which correlation function);
   - why Delta J/J = 0.2 was chosen;
   - whether the -ln T range (T/J from about 0.005 to 0.15) is in the asymptotic RS regime or a weak-disorder crossover.

   The QMC is not compared quantitatively with the measured T0 = 0.02J or with the amplitude a.

   The authors should:
   - give a model, analytical or numerical (for example nuclear relaxation computed from the QMC or tanTRG disorder ensemble), that predicts w2(T) and 1/T1s proportional to T;
   - test ln against ln^2 and a weak power law on the same data, quoting fit quality and uncertainties;
   - fix the QMC disorder strength from an independent estimate rather than by eye.

3. The quenched disorder is not identified or characterized, and the result comes from one crystal (Materials and methods; p. 4, "weak quenched disorder unavoidable in real materials"). The paper attributes the state to weak disorder of strength about 0.2J but does not say what the disorder is. Possibilities include water loss from the trihydrate (which dehydrates readily), chemical impurities, stacking or bond defects, and surface degradation of the crystal during measurement. The paper gives no independent measure of its concentration, for example a low-temperature Curie tail in the susceptibility, ESR, or the magnitude of the Kn and linewidth tails. Only one crystal (2.5 x 0.4 x 3.0 mm) was measured.

   If the slow channel comes from disorder, its parameters (T0, a) should depend on disorder level. The authors should:
   - repeat the 0.2 T low-temperature relaxation on a second crystal, ideally one with deliberately different disorder (for example aged or partially dehydrated, or lightly substituted);
   - show that T0 and a change as expected while the fast spinon channel does not;
   - give an independent estimate of the defect concentration and compare it with Delta J/J = 0.2.

   This control is what would turn "consistent with RS" into an identification.

4. The "temperature-independent 1/T1 from 1.4 K to 0.03 K" spinon signature conflicts with the authors' own field-induced gap and with known logarithmic corrections (Fig. 3b; abstract; p. 3). Extrapolating Delta = 1.85J(h/J)^(2/3) from Fig. 4d to 0.2 T gives a gap of order 0.2 to 0.3 K. That gap should suppress 1/T1f strongly at 0.03 to 0.1 K, yet the 0.2 T data in Fig. 3b fall only weakly. The text itself notes a downturn below 0.3 K, so "constant over more than a decade" also conflicts with the paper's own description.

   For the isotropic S = 1/2 Heisenberg chain, 1/T1 from the staggered channel is not strictly constant. It carries a multiplicative ln^(1/2)(T0/T) correction (Sachdev; Sandvik; Starykh, Singh and Sandvik), which over T/J from 0.002 to 0.08 changes 1/T1 by tens of percent. At 0.03 K the fast channel also carries only about 35% of the weight (w2 is about 65%), so its rate is determined less precisely.

   The authors should:
   - plot the expected 0.2 T gap on Fig. 3b and explain why 1/T1f is not activated below it, or restrict the spinon claim to T above the gap;
   - compare 1/T1f quantitatively with the field-theory prediction including the log correction, using J = 18 K;
   - state the hyperfine form factor of P5 at q = pi versus q = 0. If the staggered contribution is filtered out at that site, 1/T1 is not a direct measure of the eta = 1 spinon correlations.

B. Should address

5. The 1 T and 2 T relaxation data contradict a simple gapped soliton picture, and the paper does not discuss them (Fig. 3b, 4c; Fig. 1).
   - At 2 T (Fig. 4c), 1/T1 rises on cooling below about 2 K and then levels off near 20 s^-1 down to about 0.1 K. That is not activated behaviour, even though Kn at 2 T is used to extract a gap of order 0.1 to 0.15 meV (about 1.5 K) in Fig. 4d.
   - At 1 T (Fig. 3b), 1/T1 is non-monotonic, with a minimum near 1 K and a maximum near 0.2 to 0.3 K.

   Only the 5 T and 10 T data show clean activation, so at 1 to 2 T the Kn-derived gap and the 1/T1 behaviour disagree. This matters for the phase diagram in Fig. 1, which places the soliton regime above about 0.5 T at low temperature. The authors should explain the 1 to 2 T 1/T1 behaviour (in-gap states, a slow component, breathers, or the same disorder channel), state whether those recoveries are also two-component, and mark on Fig. 1 the (H, T) points actually measured.

6. The robustness of the ILTA decomposition and the error bars on w2 are not shown (Fig. 3c, 3d). The w2 values come from a Tikhonov-regularized inversion. The weight split between two peaks, and especially the weight of a broad slow peak, depends on the regularization parameter, the time window (the slow channel at 30 mK has T1 of order 3 to 10 s, close to the end of the recovery window in Fig. 3a), and imperfect inversion (M(0) is not equal to -M(infinity)). The authors should:
   - give error bars on w2 and 1/T1s;
   - show that w2(T) does not change for a range of regularization parameters;
   - cross-check against direct two-exponential (a_s/(a_f + a_s)) and stretched-exponential fits of M(t);
   - make sure the longest recovery delay exceeds about 5 T1s at every temperature.

7. RS signatures other than the relaxation-rate distribution are not tested (Fig. 2; Discussion). An RS state has a Curie-like local susceptibility with a broad distribution, roughly chi proportional to 1/(T ln^2 T). This should appear as low-temperature broadening of the NMR lines and a change in the mean shift at 0.2 T. The paper states only that "no spectral line splitting is observed" (at 2 T). The authors should show the linewidth and Kn of a resolved line at 0.2 T (and 0.5 T) against temperature below 0.4 K, and check whether line broadening sets in near T0. Also, 1/T1s proportional to T is presented as the RS signature, but it is not shown to be what an RS model predicts for nuclei coupled to a distribution of singlet gaps. A calculation or reference is needed.

8. The argument that the RS state survives at 0.2 T is qualitative (Discussion, p. 5). At 0.2 T, g muB H/kB is about 0.27 K, comparable to T0 = 0.4 K and larger than most of the temperature range in Fig. 3d. In this regime singlets with J below the Zeeman energy are polarized, and the field also opens the staggered-field gap. The low-energy scaling of a random chain in a field differs from the zero-field RS fixed point. The authors should either give a calculation (QMC or tanTRG with random bonds, a uniform field and the staggered field) showing that w2 proportional to -ln T survives at h = 0.2 T, or show w2(T) at two or more fields, which ties in with concern 1(ii).

9. The gap extraction and the Delta proportional to H^(2/3) comparison need clearer definitions and parameters (Fig. 4b, 4d, caption and p. 5).
   - The form Kn = a exp(-Delta/kBT) + b is applied to an upturn that the authors attribute (Fig. 4e) to growth of the induced staggered magnetization. That is an order-parameter-like quantity, not a Boltzmann population, so the paper should justify why its temperature scale equals the soliton gap.
   - The caption calls the solid line in Fig. 4d "a fit", while the text calls it "the theoretical formula" Delta = 1.85J(h/J)^(2/3). With h = g muB H (as used in Fig. 4e, where H/J = 0.383 corresponds to 5 T), this formula gives about 1.5 meV at 5 T, several times larger than the plotted values of about 0.2 meV.
   - The relevant h must be the staggered field c_s g muB H, and in Oshikawa-Affleck theory there is also a logarithmic correction.

   The authors should state whether c_s is fitted or taken from the literature, define h, and quote fit uncertainties on Delta at each field. They should also give the field misalignment from b. The paper notes from the number of peaks that the field is slightly off b, and c_s and the gap are strongly anisotropic in copper benzoate. The INS and Cp gaps taken from [29] were measured at a specific orientation, so the misalignment needs to be known to justify the comparison.

10. The tanTRG comparison is qualitative and rests on an unchecked hyperfine assumption (Fig. 4e, p. 5). H(1) and H(2) belong to water molecules coordinated to Cu, so a transferred (contact) hyperfine term in addition to the dipolar one is plausible. The paper plots Hhf/H in arbitrary units and compares only the onset temperature (about 0.15J). The authors should:
   - give the dipolar tensors computed from the structure (Supplementary Sec. S1);
   - compare calculated and measured Kn(T) in absolute units at 2, 5 and 10 T, including the high-temperature slope (K versus chi) that constrains any contact term;
   - list the tanTRG Hamiltonian parameters (staggered g, DM vector) and their sources.

11. The sample temperature and RF heating at 30 to 100 mK are not documented (Methods; Fig. 3a, 3b). The paper uses pi/2 pulses of about 2 microseconds on an insulating hydrate in a dilution refrigerator, with recovery times up to about 10 s. Thermalization of the crystal and pulse heating directly affect a claim built on temperature dependence below 0.1 K, both 1/T1s proportional to T and w2 proportional to -ln T. The authors should describe how the sample was thermally anchored, show that 1/T1 and w2 do not depend on pulse power and repetition time at the lowest temperature, and give the thermometer location.

12. The high-temperature and field dependence of 1/T1 is not discussed (Fig. 3b, 4c). Between about 2 K and 100 K, 1/T1 rises by one to two orders of magnitude and depends strongly on field (0.2, 0.5, 1 T). For a Heisenberg chain with T up to several J, the staggered contribution alone should not produce such a large increase. This points to a q = 0 spin-diffusion contribution (field dependent, roughly H^(-1/2)) or lattice or water dynamics. Both would also contribute at 1 to 2 K, where the "constant" plateau begins. The authors should identify this contribution and show it is negligible in the window used for the spinon claim.

C. Minor

13. Fig. 1 is labelled schematic, but its boundaries (spinon below about 0.5 T, RS below 0.4 K, soliton above 0.5 T) are presented as results. Please overlay the measured points and the experimental crossover criteria (T0, the Delta(H) curve).

14. The peak assignment of P5 (H(3) or benzoate) should be resolved with the calculated dipolar shifts. This affects concerns 1 and 4. Please also explain the "x4" scaling and the line positions at 0.2 T in Fig. 2a.

15. The link to the thermal-conductivity downturn below 0.3 K [27, 41] is stated in the Discussion as an explanation ("readily accounts for"). It should be phrased as a hypothesis unless a quantitative estimate (for example kappa from the measured T0 and a) is given.

16. The dismissal of the earlier 1/T1 proportional to T^(-0.25) result [28] as due to "higher temperatures, higher field and polycrystalline sample" should be backed by showing your own 0.5 T data over the same temperature range.

17. "The first direct NMR evidence for the coexistence of these two types of excitations" should be toned down, or qualified with respect to earlier NMR on disordered and doped spin chains.

18. Fig. 3e gives xi/a against T/J without error bars, disorder distribution or system size. Please add these, and state the number of disorder realizations.

19. "Data available on request" is weak for a claim based on an inversion procedure. Please deposit the raw recovery curves M(t) and the ILTA code and parameters.

20. Typographical points: "Experimental evidence for a RS state are" should read "is"; "Fig. 3a, which segregates the effects" refers to Fig. 3c (the ILTA map), not 3a.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Use site and field as controls. Repeat the 0.2 T two-component analysis on a resolved H(1) or H(2) line and at 0.5 and 1 T, and plot T0(H) and a(H) together with the soliton gap Delta(H). This single figure would separate a disorder-induced channel from a field-induced one.
2. Add a second crystal with characterized, preferably varied, disorder (for example by controlled dehydration or substitution), with an independent defect-concentration estimate. Show that the slow channel scales with disorder while the fast spinon channel does not.
3. Replace the empirical w2 proportional to -ln T and xi proportional to -ln T identification with a computed observable. Simulate the distribution of local 1/T1 for a weakly random chain at the measured field and temperatures, using the same hyperfine couplings, and compare the simulated P(1/T1), w2(T) and 1/T1s(T) directly with Fig. 3c and 3d.
4. Present the spinon and soliton regimes quantitatively. Compare 1/T1f(T) with the log-corrected field-theory prediction using J = 18 K, and Kn(T) with tanTRG in absolute units. Discuss the non-activated 1/T1 at 1 to 2 T explicitly, since it bears on where the soliton regime begins.
