Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors report an in-plane anomalous Hall effect (IP-AHE) in (110)-oriented CaRuO3/La2/3Ca1/3MnO3/CaRuO3 (CRO/LCMO/CRO) trilayers on NdGaO3(110). The Hall signal follows the square in-plane hysteresis of LCMO. They attribute it to a monoclinic-like lowering of LCMO's symmetry, set by octahedral coupling at the CRO interfaces. They tune it through the CRO thickness and switch it on and off by ionic-liquid gating, which protonates the CRO. Getting IP-AHE in a conventional oxide ferromagnet, and tuning it through octahedral engineering, would be new and useful for planar Hall readout. As presented, though, the case that the signal is a genuine IP-AHE set by mirror-symmetry breaking in LCMO rests on very small structural distortions (β_LC − 90° ≈ 0.03–0.2°) and on Hall signals of a few nΩ·cm. Several alternative explanations are not yet excluded.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- Clean epitaxial trilayers, with structure characterized consistently by RSM, HAADF/ABF-STEM and GPA.
- A large, square, zero-field Hall response that tracks the in-plane M–H loop along the easy axis. It vanishes for the out-of-plane field geometry and depends on field angle (cos θ, Supp. Figs. 17–18).
- A systematic, non-monotonic dependence on t_CRO. It is mirrored by the extracted β_LC and backed by reference samples on NGO(001) and by a t_LC series.
- Gating gives reversible ON/OFF switching. In situ XRD indicates that the protonation is selective to CRO, while H_C and ρxx–T stay nearly unchanged.
- First-principles calculations (DFT+U+SOC, Wannier) show finite IP-AHE in tilted LCMO and none in cubic LCMO.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **An out-of-plane magnetization component is not excluded.** The monoclinic C2h symmetry proposed in Fig. 2i allows M along [1-10]_O and M along the normal [110]_O to mix. Any small, intrinsic out-of-plane canting of the remanent magnetization would therefore reverse together with the in-plane component. It would give a conventional AHE with exactly the square hysteresis of Fig. 1f.
   - The measured signal is tiny, ρ_AHE ≈ 5 nΩ·cm (Fig. 1e,f). The conventional AHE of LCMO with M out of plane (Fig. 1d, which the paper does not quantify in absolute terms at saturation) is typically orders of magnitude larger. A canting of well under a degree could account for the whole signal.
   - Fig. 1d shows only that a field along the normal produces little M. It does not bound the out-of-plane component of the remanent state reached with an in-plane field.
   - The θ-rotation and (001)-plane rotation tests (Supp. Figs. 17–20) cannot separate the two scenarios, because canting from a tilted easy axis would follow the same angular dependence.
   - The authors should do three things:
     - Measure the out-of-plane magnetization of the remanent in-plane state directly, with polar MOKE, XMCD at grazing and normal incidence, or a vector VSM/SQUID with a calibrated perpendicular pickup, and set an upper bound on the canting angle.
     - Compare that bound with the conventional AHE coefficient measured on the same sample in the out-of-plane geometry at saturation.
     - Discuss whether "IP-AHE" and "AHE from symmetry-allowed canting" can be told apart at all in this point group. If they cannot, the claim of a "direct Hall readout of in-plane magnetization" needs to be framed accordingly.

2. **The paper's own DFT conflicts with the mirror-symmetry-breaking argument.**
   - The text (pp. 4–5) argues that orthorhombic LCMO keeps mirror planes that forbid the in-plane-M-induced Ω_z. On this view the monoclinic distortion (β_LC ≠ 90°) is what activates IP-AHE.
   - Yet Fig. 3h reports finite σ_AHE-DFT ≈ 2 S·cm⁻¹ for the orthorhombic supercell with β_LC = 90.00°, and zero only for cubic LCMO with no octahedral rotation. This implies that a/a/c+ rotations alone, with M along [1-10] (a low-symmetry direction of Pbnm), already permit IP-AHE.
   - The calculation therefore supports "octahedral rotation activates IP-AHE", not "monoclinic mirror breaking activates IP-AHE". It also leaves the near-zero signal of the LCMO/NGO(110) single layer (β_LC − 90° ≈ 0.03°, Fig. 3f at t_CRO = 0) unexplained.
   - The authors should give an explicit magnetic point-group analysis of Pbnm and P21/m with M along [1-10]_O. It should state which components of the Hall pseudovector are allowed in each, and reconcile it with Fig. 3h. If OOR amplitude rather than β_LC is the real control parameter, the narrative and the title's "symmetry-engineered" framing should be adjusted.

3. **The t_CRO dependence may reflect parallel conduction and normalization, not β_LC.**
   - σ_AHE = ρ_AHE/ρxx² (p. 5) is a single-channel formula. Here the conducting stack contains two metallic CRO layers and an LCMO layer whose ρxx differ by large factors. The film thickness used to convert R into ρ (total stack or LCMO only) is not stated.
   - As t_CRO grows, CRO shunting raises the denominator and dilutes the Hall voltage. This alone produces a decrease at large t_CRO (Fig. 3f, 16 → 32 u.c.). The rise from 0 to 16 u.c. could come from improved LCMO conductivity and higher T_C/M_s with CRO (p. 3), or from a CRO interfacial contribution (concern 4).
   - The sign reversal at ~100 K seen only for t_CRO ≥ 32 u.c. or < 8 u.c. (Supp. Fig. 24) also points to more than one contributing channel.
   - The authors should do three things:
     - State the thickness used.
     - Analyse the data with a parallel-conductor model using ρxx measured on CRO-only and LCMO-only films grown under identical conditions.
     - Show the raw Hall resistance R_AHE and the sheet conductances for each t_CRO, so readers can judge whether the β_LC correlation survives the shunting correction.

4. **A CRO-borne or interfacial anomalous Hall contribution is not excluded.**
   - CaRuO3 is a strongly spin-orbit-coupled paramagnet near a ferromagnetic instability. Charge transfer at the interface (p. 3) can induce a proximity moment on Ru.
   - An AHE from a magnetized CRO interface region, possibly with its own Berry-curvature sign, would also scale non-monotonically with t_CRO. It would also vanish when CRO is protonated (Fig. 4), and it could reverse sign with temperature as in SrRuO3 (Supp. Fig. 24).
   - The gating experiment cannot separate "CRO changes LCMO symmetry" from "the CRO contribution is switched off". By the paper's own XRD, the LCMO lattice is unchanged below V_g ≈ 2.2 V, and σ_AHE vanishes at 1.4 V.
   - The authors should do three things:
     - Measure in-plane Hall on a CRO single layer and on CRO/LCMO bilayers (one interface).
     - Probe for an induced Ru moment, for example with Ru-edge XMCD or polarized neutron reflectometry.
     - Show that β_LC actually decreases after gating, by RSM or STEM on a gated sample. At present the "reduced monoclinic distortion of LCMO" in Fig. 4d (inset) and Supp. Fig. 37 rests only on DFT.

**B. Should address**

5. **The β_LC − 90° values may be below resolution.**
   - β_LC − 90° spans only 0.03–0.2° (Fig. 3g). The error bars are said to come from "broadening of the RSM diffraction spot", but the procedure is not described. The authors should state the instrument angular resolution, the peak-fitting model, and how a 0.01° tilt is separated from mosaicity, substrate miscut and sample-mounting tilt.
   - The text also says the opposite monoclinic tilts are "directly visible" in HAADF-STEM (Fig. 2f) and confirmed by GPA (Fig. 2h, colour scale ±10°). Tilts of ~0.1° cannot be seen by eye in Fig. 2f, nor resolved on a ±10° GPA scale. The authors should quantify the STEM/GPA angles with uncertainties, or temper these statements.
   - Supp. Table 1 values from more than one RSM reflection, and from more than one sample, would help.

6. **One sample per thickness, with no device spread.**
   - Each t_CRO point in Fig. 3f appears to be a single sample and Hall bar, with no error bar. The headline correlation (Fig. 3f vs 3g) depends on a single peak point at 16 u.c. and a sharp drop by 32 u.c., with no data between them.
   - The authors should report how many samples and devices were measured at each thickness and show their spread. Adding intermediate thicknesses (e.g. 20–28 u.c.) would show whether the drop is a smooth structural relaxation or a threshold.

7. **The antisymmetrization procedure for hysteretic data is incorrect as written.**
   - The Methods give R_xy^odd(H) = |R_xy(H) − R_xy(−H)|/2. The absolute value would remove the sign of the signal, yet the sign is central (inverted loop in Fig. 1f; sign reversal in Supp. Fig. 24).
   - For hysteretic loops, the odd part must be built from opposite sweep branches, R↑(H) − R↓(−H). Otherwise the field-even planar-Hall/AMR background is not removed in the coercive region.
   - The authors should state the exact procedure and show raw R_xy loops for both sweep directions next to the extracted ρ_AHE, including the planar-Hall background amplitude. With E ∥ M, a small angular misalignment produces a planar Hall term that switches at H_C.

8. **Experiment and theory disagree in magnitude.**
   - Experiment gives σ_AHE ≈ 0.1 S·cm⁻¹ (106 mS·cm⁻¹). DFT gives 2–8 S·cm⁻¹ (Fig. 3h; the axis units differ between Fig. 3f and 3h). This is a factor of 20–80.
   - The DFT structures are also LCMO(2–4 u.c.)/CRO superlattices with large U = 8 eV, J = 2 eV and VCA for Ca, far from the experimental 32-u.c. LCMO.
   - The comparison is therefore qualitative only. The authors should say so, discuss the discrepancy (U dependence, VCA, thickness, normalization), and ideally show σ_AHE-DFT vs U and vs Fermi-level position. Plotting experiment and theory with the same units would avoid misreading Fig. 3.

9. **The "electrical" ON/OFF switching is done at room temperature.**
   - V_g is applied at room temperature and the sample is then cooled with the gate held (Methods). The modulation therefore cannot be performed in situ at the measurement temperature. The abstract's "dynamic reconfiguration" and "fully reversible ON/OFF switching" should be qualified accordingly.
   - The number of ON/OFF cycles, any degradation (Supp. Fig. 32), and the proton concentration actually introduced (e.g. by SIMS, NRA or ToF-ERDA) should be reported.
   - It should also be shown that LCMO does not take up protons at 1.1–1.4 V. Unchanged lattice parameters are not proof of that.

10. **The (001)-oriented reference is not a clean null control.**
    - The NGO(001) control samples (Supp. Figs. 4, 23) differ in film orientation, strain state, magnetic anisotropy axis and current direction, not only in mirror-symmetry breaking.
    - A control that keeps the (110) geometry but removes CRO's octahedral coupling would isolate the claimed mechanism more cleanly. Examples are a non-rotating metallic spacer (e.g. SrRuO3 on a cubic template, or LaNiO3) in place of CRO, or an insulating cap with matched OOR.
    - If such a control is unavailable, the comparison should be presented as supportive rather than decisive.

11. **The temperature dependence and the scaling analysis need fuller presentation.**
    - The claim of a "predominantly intrinsic Berry-curvature origin" (Supp. Fig. 25) rests on a weak dependence of σ_AHE on σxx, while σ_AHE changes sign and varies nonlinearly with M.
    - The range of σxx spanned is not stated in the main text. For a moderately conducting oxide, a weak σ_AHE(σxx) dependence over a narrow σxx window cannot exclude side-jump or interfacial contributions.
    - The authors should state the σxx range and the fitting form (e.g. TYJ scaling) with its parameters.

**C. Minor**

12. **Crystallographic directions are inconsistent.**
    - The text repeatedly calls [110]_O the in-plane easy axis and the current direction ("E//[110]_O", "H//[110]_O (the in-plane easy axis)", Fig. 1 caption, Fig. 3 caption "E//H//LCMO[110]"). But [110]_O is the film normal for (110) substrates. The figures (Fig. 1f, Fig. 2i, Fig. 3h) use [-110]_O.
    - Please correct the text throughout. As written, the core geometry is ambiguous.

13. **Fig. 1e and the hard-axis data.**
    - With H ∥ [001]_O (the hard axis), reversing the [1-10] component at low field requires some field projection on the easy axis, i.e. misalignment. The sign and coercive field of the ρ_AHE loop in Fig. 1e therefore depend on that misalignment.
    - Please state the alignment accuracy of the rotator and comment on this.

14. **Out-of-plane loop magnitude.** Fig. 1d should give the saturated ρ_AHE in the out-of-plane geometry at high field. This is needed to judge concern 1.

15. **Units and normalization in Fig. 3.**
    - Fig. 3f/3g lack error bars on σ_AHE.
    - Fig. 3h uses S·cm⁻¹ while panels a–f use mS·cm⁻¹. This should be flagged in the caption.

16. **Wording of the zero-field value.** The text gives 103.7 mS·cm⁻¹ at zero field "close to the saturated value (106.3)". This ~3% difference is within typical device uncertainty, and "rarely been seen" (p. 5) should be backed with a quantitative comparison to refs. 14–18.

17. **Gating thresholds.** The text quotes V_g ≈ 1.1 V for the onset of σ_AHE suppression and ~+1.1 V for the CRO lattice change, but V_g ≥ 2.2 V for the LCMO lattice change. Please give these in one table with the gating temperature and duration, so the claimed CRO selectivity can be judged.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a direct, quantitative upper bound on the out-of-plane magnetization in the remanent in-plane state (polar MOKE or XMCD). Express the measured IP-AHE as a fraction of the out-of-plane AHE coefficient. This is the single most important control for the headline claim.
2. Add a short symmetry table listing the allowed Hall components for M ∥ [1-10]_O in Pbnm, P21/m and the cubic phase. Reconcile it with the finite DFT result for β_LC = 90.00°.
3. Run single-layer CRO and CRO/LCMO bilayer Hall controls, together with a parallel-conduction analysis of the t_CRO series. This would separate the LCMO-intrinsic mechanism from CRO shunting and CRO interfacial magnetism.
4. Measure β_LC directly on gated samples (RSM or STEM). This would turn the gating result from a correlation into direct evidence for the claimed symmetry control.
