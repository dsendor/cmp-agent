Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The paper reports an in-plane anomalous Hall effect (IP-AHE) in CaRuO3/La2/3Ca1/3MnO3/CaRuO3 (CRO/LCMO/CRO) trilayers on NdGaO3(110). The IP-AHE follows the square in-plane magnetic hysteresis of the LCMO layer, changes in a non-monotonic way with CRO buffer thickness, and is switched off reversibly by ionic-liquid gating. Taken together this would be the first IP-AHE in a ferromagnetic oxide heterostructure and the first IP-AHE that can be tuned electrically, which would interest both the Berry-curvature and the oxide-spintronics communities. As written, though, the central mechanism (a monoclinic, interface-induced mirror-symmetry breaking in LCMO "activates" the IP-AHE) rests on a symmetry argument and a thickness correlation that I do not think yet support it. The transport analysis also does not exclude the simpler explanations of parallel conduction and canted magnetization.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The material platform is new for IP-AHE, and the data are clean: a square IP-AHE loop with a remanent zero-field signal that tracks the M–H loop (Fig. 1f), and the expected projection behaviour for H along the hard axis (Fig. 1e).
- The paper combines several kinds of evidence: RSM, HAADF/ABF-STEM with GPA, angle-dependent transport (Supp. Figs. 17–21), controls on NGO(001), and DFT/Wannier calculations.
- The gating result (Fig. 4) switches the IP-AHE on and off while H_C and ρxx–T barely change. If its mechanism is pinned down, it is an attractive device-level demonstration.
- The Methods are reasonably complete (growth conditions, antisymmetrization, DFT settings), and the data are deposited.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The symmetry argument does not show that the monoclinic distortion is needed; orthorhombic Pbnm LCMO in (110) orientation already allows the IP-AHE.** On p. 4–5 the paper argues that bulk Pbnm LCMO has the mirrors M(010) and M(100), which forbid the IP-AHE, and that the interfaces remove them, leaving 2/m with M(001) and 2[001], which "allows" Ω along the film normal. With M ∥ [1̄10]_O, however, M(010), M(100), 2[100] and 2[010] are already broken by the magnetization itself.
   - The magnetic point group of bulk Pbnm with M ∥ [1̄10]_O is the same as the one the authors find for the monoclinic cell (1, 1̄, 2'[001], m'(001)).
   - That group forbids only Ω_z along [001]_O. It allows any Ω in the (001)_O plane, including along [110]_O, the film normal.
   - Physically, Ω_x ∝ M_x and Ω_y ∝ M_y with different coefficients because a_O ≠ b_O. That gives Ω·[110] ∝ (χ_aa − χ_bb)M ≠ 0 with no monoclinic tilt at all.

   The authors' own DFT confirms this: the orthorhombic supercell with β_LC = 90.00° gives a finite σ_AHE-DFT of about 2 S/cm (Fig. 3h). The only cell that gives zero is the cubic cell without octahedral rotations. The (001)-oriented control (Supp. Fig. 23) also does not discriminate, because Ω_z ∥ c is forbidden for in-plane M in orthorhombic symmetry as well.

   The claim that "mirror-symmetry breaking by the CRO buffer activates IP-AHE" and that β_LC is "the" control parameter therefore does not follow. What the data and DFT support is that the IP-AHE scales with the amplitude of the orthorhombic/octahedral-rotation distortion (a_O ≠ b_O, d_OR). The authors should:
   - redo the symmetry analysis including the magnetization direction, giving the magnetic point groups explicitly for the bulk-orthorhombic and monoclinic cases;
   - separate, in DFT, the effect of β_LC at fixed d_OR from the effect of d_OR at β_LC = 90°;
   - reframe the "symmetry-engineering" claim accordingly, in the title, abstract and Fig. 1b/2i.

2. **σ_AHE versus t_CRO can be explained by current shunting through the metallic CRO layers.** CaRuO3 is a good metal, and the trilayers are conducting stacks in parallel. The paper converts ρAHE to σ_AHE = ρAHE/ρxx² using the total ρxx (p. 5), which assumes a single homogeneous conductor. It does not state which thickness is used for ρ (total, or LCMO only).
   - As t_CRO grows from 16 to 64 u.c., an increasing share of the current flows in the non-magnetic CRO, so the Hall signal from LCMO is diluted roughly as (t_LC σ_LC / Σ t_i σ_i)². This alone reproduces the fall of σ_AHE at large t_CRO in Fig. 3f.
   - The β_LC data do not give a one-to-one relation with σ_AHE. From Fig. 3f,g: t_CRO = 4 u.c. has β_LC − 90° ≈ 0.13° and σ_AHE ≈ 18 mS/cm, while t_CRO = 32 u.c. has β_LC − 90° ≈ 0.085° and σ_AHE ≈ 5 mS/cm. Also, t_CRO = 2 u.c. has a smaller β_LC (≈0.06°) than 32 u.c. but a larger σ_AHE. So the correlation is not single-valued, as a shunting contribution would predict.
   - The authors should report ρxx (or sheet resistance) for each t_CRO and for single CRO and LCMO reference films at 10 K, and analyse the Hall data with a parallel-conductor model. They should then plot the intrinsic LCMO σ_AHE against β_LC (and against d_OR) directly, rather than overlaying two t_CRO dependences.
   - The t_LC series (Supp. Fig. 22) needs the same treatment.

3. **A canted out-of-plane magnetization component is not excluded as the source of the signal.** Any symmetry that allows Ω_z for M ∥ [1̄10]_O also allows a static canting of M out of the film plane, because both are axial, time-odd vectors. In a monoclinic LCMO cell whose c_p axis is tilted, the magnetic easy axis can be slightly canted out of plane, and the ordinary AHE of that small M_z would follow the in-plane hysteresis exactly as in Fig. 1f.
   - The measured IP-AHE is very small: ρAHE ≈ 5 nΩ cm, σ_AHE ≈ 0.1 S/cm. A canting of a fraction of a degree, combined with the out-of-plane AHE of LCMO or of interfacial (proximity-magnetized) CRO, could produce a signal of this size.
   - Rotating the field in the (001) plane (Supp. Figs. 19, 20) tests the external field direction, not the internal canting of M at H = 0.
   - The authors should (i) estimate quantitatively the canting angle needed to account for ρAHE, using the measured out-of-plane AHE of the same trilayer at saturation (Fig. 1d at high field) or of reference films, and (ii) bound the actual canting independently. Possible measurements are polar MOKE or XMCD at the remanent state, or the remanent out-of-plane moment from VSM after in-plane saturation.

   Without this, the "intrinsic in-plane" origin is not separated from a conventional AHE of a tilted moment. The two scenarios have the same symmetry but different physics and different device implications.

4. **The mechanism for gating is inferred, not measured on the gated trilayer.** The paper attributes the switching to protonation of CRO, which reduces the octahedral rotations and hence the monoclinic distortion of LCMO (p. 7, Fig. 4b,d insets). The evidence is:
   - in situ XRD on separate CRO and LCMO reference films;
   - unchanged ρxx–T and H_C;
   - DFT.

   No RSM, β_LC or d_OR measurement on the gated trilayer is shown, so the central link (gating → reduced β_LC → σ_AHE = 0) rests on the DFT insets. Several competing explanations are not addressed:
   - Protonated H_xCaRuO3 is reported to become ferromagnetic (ref. 48), which would add its own AHE or change the interfacial magnetism.
   - Protonation changes CRO's conductivity and so the current distribution between layers (see concern 2). Yet ρxx–T is reported nearly unchanged, which is itself puzzling if the CRO layers were transformed.
   - Protons or oxygen vacancies may enter LCMO near the interface, at concentrations below what bulk XRD of a reference film detects.

   The authors should provide:
   - RSMs (β_LC, d_OR) of a trilayer at V_g = 0, 1.2 V and 1.4 V, and after reversal;
   - ρxx and the ordinary Hall coefficient versus V_g on the same device;
   - M–H (including M_s) after gating;
   - gate leakage current;
   - SIMS or another depth-resolved H probe, if available.

**B. Should address**

5. **DFT and experiment differ in magnitude by about 50–100×, so the comparison is only qualitative.** σ_AHE-DFT is 2–8 S/cm (Fig. 3h), while the measured σ_AHE is 0.004–0.1 S/cm (Fig. 3a–f; the units are mS/cm). The paper describes the DFT as "consistent with the experimental trend".
   - The β_LC values in the DFT cells (90.0°, 90.08°, 90.16°) were chosen to bracket the experiment, but the OOR and Jahn–Teller amplitudes that produce them, and how they compare with the real film, are not stated.
   - The DFT also uses bulk-like LCMO with VCA for Ca and a large U = 8 eV, J = 2 eV, with no CRO and no interfaces in the AHE calculation.

   The authors should discuss the discrepancy (shunting, disorder, VCA, U dependence), show σ_AHE-DFT versus U, and state the OOR amplitudes in each supercell. Ideally they should compute σ_AHE for the relaxed CRO/LCMO superlattices of Supp. Fig. 31.

6. **The precision of β_LC from RSM is questionable.** The values of β_LC − 90° span only 0.03–0.2° (Fig. 3g), with error bars of about 0.005–0.01° said to come from "broadening of the RSM diffraction spot". For a 32-u.c. LCMO layer whose peaks lie close to the substrate and CRO peaks (Fig. 2a–d), it is not obvious that a 0.01° angular resolution in the derived β is achievable. The authors should:
   - describe the fitting procedure and instrument resolution, and show how β_LC is computed from the four asymmetric reflections;
   - propagate the uncertainties properly;
   - ideally cross-check against the STEM/GPA tilt angles for at least two t_CRO values.

   Because Fig. 3g is the main quantitative support for the mechanism, its uncertainty matters.

7. **Each condition appears to come from a single sample.** Figure 3 shows one sample per t_CRO, and the gating data come from one device. Given the non-monotonic dependence and the small signals, the authors should state how many samples or devices were measured per thickness, show the spread of σ_AHE and β_LC, and show at least one repeat of the 16-u.c. peak sample. For the "fully reversible ON/OFF switching" claim (Supp. Fig. 32), they should give the number of cycles and the cycle-to-cycle variation.

8. **The temperature dependence and scaling point to more than one contribution.** For some samples σ_AHE changes sign near 100 K (p. 5, Supp. Fig. 24), and the σ_AHE–M dependence is "strongly nonlinear and sign-changing". The paper compares this with SrRuO3 band topology, but a sign change is also what one expects from two contributions of opposite sign, for example LCMO plus interfacial CRO, or intrinsic plus a canting- or skew-type term. This weakens the "predominantly intrinsic Berry-curvature" assignment. The authors should:
   - show the scaling plots in the main text or describe them more fully;
   - test whether the sign change correlates with t_CRO in a way consistent with a CRO-interface contribution;
   - qualify the intrinsic assignment.

9. **The role of CRO interfacial magnetism is not examined.** The higher T_C and M_s with t_CRO are attributed to interfacial Ru⁴⁺→Mn⁴⁺ charge transfer (p. 3), which implies an induced moment on interfacial Ru. Ru has much stronger spin–orbit coupling than Mn, so the IP-AHE may come largely from the interfacial CRO, not from monoclinic LCMO. That would change the interpretation of both the t_CRO dependence and the gating (which modifies CRO). Element-specific data (XMCD at the Ru and Mn edges) or a discussion of this alternative would strengthen the assignment to LCMO.

10. **The antisymmetrization procedure for hysteretic data needs specifying.** The Methods give R_xy^odd(H) = |R_xy(H) − R_xy(−H)|/2. For hysteretic signals this must pair opposite sweep branches. The absolute-value bars as printed would also remove the sign of the signal. The authors should state the branch pairing and correct the formula. They should also show raw, unsymmetrized R_xy loops for one sample, so readers can judge the size of the even-in-H (planar Hall/AMR) background relative to the ~nΩ cm odd signal.

**C. Minor**

11. **Inconsistent current direction.** The Fig. 1 caption states "E is fixed along the [110]_O axis for all the AHE and IP-AHE measurements", and Figs. 3–4 state E ∥ H ∥ LCMO[110]_O. [110]_O is the film normal. The text says E ∥ [1̄10]_O. Please correct the overbars throughout.

12. **Inconsistent coercive field.** The text gives H_C ≈ 200 Oe for the 16-u.c. sample (p. 3, Fig. 1f), whereas Fig. 4c,d show H_C ≈ 280–300 Oe for nominally the same structure at V_g = 0. Please reconcile, for example sample-to-sample variation or the effect of the ionic liquid or Pt contacts.

13. **Units differ between experiment and DFT.** σ_AHE is given in mS cm⁻¹ for experiment and S cm⁻¹ for DFT in adjacent panels (Fig. 3f vs h), which hides the order-of-magnitude difference. Use common units, or state the ratio explicitly.

14. **Overstated literature comparison.** "Such a sizable zero-field IP-AHE has rarely been observed" (p. 5): 0.1 S/cm is small compared with IP-AHE values reported in several of the cited systems. Please give a quantitative comparison table or soften the statement.

15. **The thickness used for ρ is not stated.** The Methods should give the thickness used to convert resistances to ρxx and ρxy (total stack or LCMO only), and the Hall-bar dimensions.

16. **Missing DFT details.** The Methods describe 2×2×2 LCMO cells but give the cell expansion as "√2×√2×1 of the tetragonal cell". Please clarify the actual cell, the magnetic configuration, and whether the Ca disorder in VCA respects the assumed space group.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Recast the symmetry discussion around the magnetic point group with M included. Then present the octahedral-rotation/orthorhombic distortion amplitude, rather than the monoclinic tilt alone, as the tuning parameter, supported by DFT that varies d_OR and β_LC independently.
2. Add a parallel-conduction analysis using measured sheet resistances of each layer, and replot the intrinsic LCMO σ_AHE against the structural parameter, with sample-to-sample error bars.
3. Bound any out-of-plane canting of the remanent magnetization directly (polar MOKE/XMCD/VSM), and compare it quantitatively with the size of the IP-AHE.
4. Measure the structure of the gated trilayer itself (RSM at several V_g and after reversal), together with ρxx, n_H and M_s versus V_g, so that the gating mechanism rests on data from the device and not on reference films and DFT.
