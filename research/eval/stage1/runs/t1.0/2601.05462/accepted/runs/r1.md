Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report an in-plane anomalous Hall effect (IP-AHE) in CaRuO3/La2/3Ca1/3MnO3/CaRuO3 (CRO/LCMO/CRO) trilayers on NdGaO3(110). With M along the in-plane easy axis [1-10]o, the transverse resistivity shows a square hysteresis loop. They argue that the CRO buffers set a monoclinic-like distortion of LCMO (beta_LC) that breaks the last mirror symmetry and turns the IP-AHE on. They tune the effect non-monotonically with CRO thickness and switch it ON/OFF reversibly with ionic-liquid gating. An oxide platform where the IP-AHE follows a square in-plane hysteresis loop and can be switched by gating would be a useful addition to a field so far limited to a few layered and kagome magnets. Whether the symmetry mechanism and the intrinsic origin follow from the data is less clear, as set out below.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clean, square IP-AHE loop that tracks the in-plane M-H loop (Fig. 1f), with the expected suppression for M along [001]o (Fig. 1e) and a cosine angular dependence (Supp. Figs. 17, 18).
- A thorough structural characterisation: asymmetric RSMs on four reflections, HAADF/ABF-STEM and GPA, and a thickness series in both t_CRO and t_LC.
- Reversible gate control of the Hall signal at nearly constant rho_xx, supported by in situ XRD on reference films.
- First-principles Berry-curvature calculations that address the role of octahedral rotations directly.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The symmetry argument for why the monoclinic distortion is needed does not hold for M along [1-10]o, and the authors' own DFT contradicts it.
   - The paper argues (p. 4–5, Fig. 2i) that bulk Pbnm LCMO keeps mirrors that forbid an out-of-plane Berry-curvature component Omega_z (along [110]o), and that the interface-induced lowering to P2_1/m (C2h) is what "activates" the IP-AHE.
   - For M along [1-10]o, however, the magnetisation itself already breaks every element of mmm except inversion, 2_[001]·T and m_(001)·T. The magnetic point group of orthorhombic Pbnm LCMO is then 2'/m' with the primed elements along c.
   - Under m'_(001), any axial-vector component lying in the ab plane is invariant, and [110]o lies in the ab plane. Omega along [110]o, the film normal, is therefore already allowed in the orthorhombic structure. The extra elements that the heterointerfaces remove (M_(100), M_(010), 2_[100], 2_[010]) are already broken by M along [1-10].
   - For M along [001]o the unprimed m_(001) survives and forbids Omega along [110]. This agrees with Fig. 1e and with the NGO(001) control, but it does not show that the monoclinic distortion is required.
   - Fig. 3h and the text say the same: sigma_AHE-DFT "remains finite in all the orthorhombic supercells, even for the one with beta_LC = 90.0°", at about 2 S/cm. The ingredient that allows the effect is the a-a-c+ tilt pattern together with the (110) orientation, not the monoclinic lowering.
   - This changes the central framing of the title, abstract and Fig. 2i ("symmetry-engineered", "activates IP-AHE", "only remaining mirror plane"). It also affects how the t_CRO and gating results are read: beta_LC would then change the magnitude of an already-allowed response, through the OOR and Jahn-Teller amplitudes it stands for, rather than switch on a forbidden one.
   - What to do: redo the magnetic-point-group analysis for M along [1-10] in both the Pbnm and the P2_1/m cases and state explicitly which Omega components each allows. Reframe the mechanism to match. Explain why the tiny change in beta (about 0.2°) gives a roughly 30-fold change in sigma_AHE (Fig. 3f) if symmetry is not the controlling factor.

2. A thermal (anomalous Nernst) artifact has not been ruled out, and it has exactly the geometry and symmetry of the claimed signal.
   - With current along [1-10]o, Joule heating sets up a temperature gradient normal to the film, along [110]o, toward the NGO substrate.
   - The anomalous Nernst field E ∝ M × ∇T then points along [001]o. That is the in-plane Hall direction measured here.
   - This voltage is odd in M, follows the in-plane M-H hysteresis loop exactly, varies as cos(theta) when M is rotated in-plane, vanishes for M along [001], and does not depend on the in-plane direction of E. Each of these features is offered in the paper (Fig. 1e, f; Supp. Figs. 17, 18, 21) as evidence for Berry-curvature IP-AHE.
   - The measured rho_AHE is only about 5 nΩ·cm (Fig. 1e, f), so a small thermoelectric contribution would matter. A spin-Seebeck or spin-Hall-type voltage from the metallic CRO layers next to the magnet has the same symmetry.
   - The Methods do not say whether the measurement was DC or AC, give the current magnitude or density, or describe any current reversal.
   - What to do:
     - Show that V_xy is linear in I over at least a decade of current density.
     - For DC measurements, show that the hysteretic signal reverses with current polarity. For lock-in measurements, report the first and second harmonics separately.
     - Ideally, compare the result with a sample on a substrate of different thermal conductivity, or with an estimated anomalous Nernst coefficient for LCMO.

3. Parallel conduction in the trilayer is not handled, and it can produce the non-monotonic t_CRO dependence on its own.
   - sigma_AHE = rho_AHE/rho_xx^2 (p. 5) treats the CRO/LCMO/CRO stack as one homogeneous conductor. The paper does not say which thickness is used to compute rho (LCMO only, or the whole stack).
   - CaRuO3 is a good paramagnetic metal. As t_CRO increases from 0 to 64 u.c., a growing share of the current flows in the non-magnetic CRO, which dilutes the measured transverse voltage. At the same time, the squared rho_xx amplifies any change in the current distribution.
   - At small t_CRO, Tc and Ms both rise with t_CRO (p. 3), which points to better LCMO conductivity and magnetism. Those two trends alone could give a maximum at intermediate t_CRO, as in Fig. 3f, without any change in Berry curvature.
   - Because t_CRO changes beta_LC, Tc, Ms, rho_xx, interfacial charge transfer and the shunting fraction all at once, the correlation between Fig. 3f and Fig. 3g does not isolate beta_LC as the control parameter.
   - What to do:
     - Report the raw rho_xx (or sheet resistance), R_xy, Tc and Ms at 10 K for every t_CRO.
     - Analyse the Hall response with a parallel-conductor model that uses separately measured CRO single-film sheet conductances, and report the Hall conductance of the LCMO layer itself.
     - Plot sigma_AHE directly against beta_LC and against Ms, so the reader can judge which correlation is stronger.
     - The t_LC series (Supp. Fig. 22) would be more convincing if it were analysed the same way.

B. Should address

4. The structural parameter is tiny compared with its precision, and it is measured at a different temperature from transport.
   - beta_LC − 90° ranges from 0.03° to 0.2° (Fig. 3g). The error bars are "uncertainties originating from the broadening of the RSM diffraction spot".
   - The paper does not explain how a layer-averaged tilt of a few hundredths of a degree is extracted reliably from four asymmetric reflections of a 32 u.c. layer next to a near-lattice-matched substrate, given instrument resolution, peak overlap with CRO and NGO (Fig. 2a–d) and possible twin domains (mentioned for t_CRO ≥ 32 u.c.).
   - The RSMs appear to have been taken at room temperature, while sigma_AHE is measured at 10 K. Both LCMO and NGO change structurally on cooling, and LCMO goes through its ferromagnetic transition, so beta_LC at 10 K may differ.
   - Only one sample per thickness seems to have been measured, and the sigma_AHE points in Fig. 3f have no error bars.
   - The data also show a steep nonlinearity: sigma_AHE is about 18, 38 and 103 mS/cm at beta − 90° ≈ 0.13°, 0.15° and 0.20°. This contrasts with the nearly linear DFT trend.
   - What to do:
     - Describe the beta extraction and how its errors are propagated.
     - Give the RSM resolution.
     - Show at least one low-temperature RSM, or argue that beta does not change on cooling.
     - Add repeat samples at key thicknesses (for example 8 and 16 u.c.) to show sample-to-sample spread in both quantities.

5. The STEM evidence is overstated.
   - The text says Fig. 2f "clearly resolve[s] the beta angles" and that the opposite tilts are "directly visible".
   - A deviation of 0.1–0.2° cannot be seen by eye in a HAADF image. The drawn angles in Fig. 2f are schematic.
   - The GPA map in Fig. 2h uses a ±10° colour scale, and its contrast sits mainly at the interfaces.
   - What to do: quantify the tilt from the GPA or atom-position fits, with uncertainties per layer. State whether the STEM values agree with the RSM-derived 0.1–0.2°. Soften the language if they are below the STEM precision.

6. The DFT comparison is qualitative and does not isolate the monoclinic distortion.
   - The calculated sigma_AHE (2–8 S/cm, Fig. 3h) is about 20–80 times larger than the measured values (at most about 0.1 S/cm). This discrepancy is not discussed.
   - In the supercells, beta_LC is changed by changing the OOR and Jahn-Teller amplitudes together (p. 6, Supp. Fig. 26). The DFT trend therefore cannot separate a monoclinic-tilt effect from an OOR-amplitude effect.
   - The calculations also rest on several choices that the results may be sensitive to: Ca is treated with VCA in bulk LCMO; U = 8 eV and J = 2 eV are taken from LaMnO3, not a doped manganite; and a 0.02 eV broadening is applied. The paper does not test how sensitive the result is to U, J and the Fermi-level position.
   - The relaxed heterostructures of Supp. Fig. 31 are used only for beta, not for sigma.
   - What to do:
     - Vary beta at fixed OOR amplitude, and the OOR amplitude at beta = 90°.
     - Show the dependence on U and on the chemical potential.
     - Address the quantitative gap: for example, the effective magnetic volume, the shunting in concern 3, or the disorder broadening.

7. The gating mechanism is inferred from reference films, not shown in the trilayer.
   - The claim that ILG "reconfigures the symmetry breaking" rests on in situ XRD of separate CRO and LCMO films (Supp. Fig. 33) and on DFT. beta_LC is not measured in the gated trilayer; the insets of Fig. 4d are schematic.
   - Ref. 48 reports that HxCaRuO3 becomes ferromagnetic. Protonated CRO would then add a second magnetic, metallic layer, and its own (conventional or in-plane) Hall response or exchange coupling could cancel or change the signal. That is an alternative explanation for the OFF state.
   - It is also unclear how protons reach the bottom CRO beneath 32 u.c. of LCMO, given that the authors argue LCMO protonates only above 2.2 V.
   - Protonation is said to change CRO strongly, yet rho_xx is "nearly unchanged" (Supp. Fig. 34). These two statements need reconciling, which links back to concern 3.
   - H_C rises from about 250 to about 290 Oe at V_g = 1.1–1.3 V (Fig. 4d). That is a change of about 15%, not "nearly unchanged".
   - What to do:
     - Show RSMs (beta_LC) of the trilayer before and after gating and after reversal.
     - Show M-H at 10 K in the gated state, and rho_xx at 10 K.
     - Report the gate leakage current and the number of ON/OFF cycles.
     - Discuss whether the top and bottom CRO layers protonate differently.

8. The layer in which the IP-AHE originates is not established.
   - CRO has strong spin-orbit coupling and Ru–Mn interfacial charge transfer (refs. 30–32). An induced Ru moment at the interfaces, with its own Berry curvature, could carry part or all of the IP-AHE. That would also explain the sign changes in temperature (Supp. Fig. 24), which resemble SrRuO3.
   - The t_CRO = 0 film shows a small but finite sigma_AHE (Fig. 3a).
   - What to do:
     - Compare asymmetric structures: a bottom-CRO-only bilayer, a top-CRO-only bilayer, and a CRO/LCMO stack with a thin non-magnetic insulating spacer that preserves the OOR coupling but cuts the electronic one.
     - If possible, measure element-specific magnetism at the Ru edge (XMCD) or the depth profile of the magnetisation (polarised neutron reflectometry).

9. Out-of-plane contamination needs a quantitative bound.
   - Fig. 1d is plotted only up to 2.5 kOe. The text says rho_AHE "saturates at H ~ 4000 Oe" for H ⊥ (110), but this range is not shown, and the out-of-plane M has not saturated in the plotted range.
   - The linear background is taken from "H > H_S". For the hard out-of-plane axis, it is not clear that this regime lies within the field used.
   - What to do:
     - Show the full out-of-plane AHE up to saturation with the 7 T magnet, and give the conventional AHE magnitude per unit out-of-plane M.
     - From that, compute the tilt of M (or of the sample) that would be needed to give 5 nΩ·cm, and compare it with the alignment uncertainty in the rotator.
     - This would make Supp. Figs. 19 and 20 quantitative.

C. Minor

10. The antisymmetrisation formula in the Methods, R_xy^odd(H) = |R_xy(H) − R_xy(−H)|/2, contains an absolute value. This would remove the sign that the hysteresis loops depend on. For hysteretic data, state how branches are paired (ascending at +H with descending at −H).

11. State the thickness used to convert resistance into rho_xx and rho_AHE, and the units and normalisation behind the mS/cm (experiment) and S/cm (DFT) comparison.

12. The claim that a zero-field sigma_AHE of 103.7 mS/cm "has rarely been seen" in in-plane magnetised systems (refs. 14–18) should be backed by a table of reported values. For context, this value is about two to three orders of magnitude below typical conventional AHE in manganites and ruthenates.

13. The sign reversal near 100 K for t_CRO ≥ 32 u.c. or t_CRO < 8 u.c. (Supp. Fig. 24) is not discussed in relation to beta_LC. If beta controls the IP-AHE, the reason the sign depends on temperature differently in different samples should be addressed.

14. The arrows in Fig. 1d–f, and the definition of the sign of V_Hall in the Fig. 1d inset, could be explained in the caption: rho_AHE is negative at positive H in Fig. 1f ("inverted" loop).

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Replace the "symmetry activation" framing with a magnetic-point-group analysis for each M direction, in both the orthorhombic and monoclinic structures. Present beta_LC (or the OOR amplitude) as a magnitude-tuning parameter, supported by DFT that varies tilt and rotation separately.
2. Add a current-dependence and current-reversal (or harmonic) dataset to rule out thermoelectric and spin-caloritronic contributions. Add a parallel-conduction analysis giving the Hall conductance of the LCMO layer for every t_CRO.
3. Measure beta_LC in the gated trilayer (before, ON-to-OFF, and after reversal), together with 10 K magnetometry of the gated state.
4. Report multiple samples or devices at the key thicknesses, with error bars on sigma_AHE, and plot sigma_AHE directly against beta_LC.
