**A. Must address**

1. **The symmetry argument does not show that the monoclinic distortion is needed, and the paper's own DFT contradicts it.** On pp. 4–5 and in Fig. 2i the paper argues that bulk Pbnm LCMO keeps the mirrors M(100) and M(010), which forbid an out-of-plane Berry-curvature component Ω along the film normal [110]_O. On this view the interface-induced lowering to P2_1/m (C2h, "only remaining mirror plane") is what "activates" the IP-AHE. For M ∥ [1̄10]_O, however, the magnetisation already breaks M(100), M(010), 2[100] and 2[010]. The magnetic point group of orthorhombic Pbnm LCMO with M ∥ [1̄10]_O is then the same as the one the authors find for the monoclinic cell, {1, 1̄, 2'[001], m'(001)}. Under m'(001) any axial vector in the (001)_O plane is invariant, so the group forbids only Ω along [001]_O and allows Ω along [110]_O. Physically, Ω_x ∝ M_x and Ω_y ∝ M_y with different coefficients because a_O ≠ b_O, so Ω·[110] ∝ (χ_aa − χ_bb)M ≠ 0 without any monoclinic tilt. Fig. 3h says the same: σ_AHE-DFT "remains finite in all the orthorhombic supercells, even for the one with β_LC = 90.0°", at about 2 S/cm, and is zero only for cubic LCMO without octahedral rotations. What allows the effect is the a⁻a⁻c⁺ rotation pattern with the (110) orientation, not the monoclinic lowering. For M ∥ [001]_O the unprimed m(001) survives and forbids Ω along [110], which agrees with Fig. 1e; the NGO(001) control (Supp. Fig. 23) also does not discriminate, because Ω ∥ c is forbidden for in-plane M in orthorhombic symmetry as well. Neither shows that the monoclinic distortion is required. The near-zero signal of the LCMO/NGO(110) single layer (β_LC − 90° ≈ 0.03°, t_CRO = 0) is then also left unexplained. This changes the central framing in the title, abstract, Fig. 1b and Fig. 2i ("symmetry-engineered", "activates IP-AHE"), and how the t_CRO and gating results are read: β_LC would change the magnitude of an already-allowed response, through the octahedral-rotation (OOR, d_OR) and Jahn–Teller amplitudes it stands for, rather than switch on a forbidden one. The authors should give an explicit magnetic-point-group analysis, for example as a short table, for M ∥ [1̄10]_O and M ∥ [001]_O in the cubic, Pbnm and P2_1/m cases, stating which Hall-pseudovector components each allows, and reconcile it with Fig. 3h. They should separate in DFT the effect of β_LC at fixed d_OR from that of d_OR at β_LC = 90°, reframe the mechanism around the rotation/orthorhombic-distortion amplitude if that is the real control parameter, and explain why a change in β of about 0.2° gives a roughly 30-fold change in σ_AHE (Fig. 3f) if symmetry is not the controlling factor.

2. **An out-of-plane component of the magnetisation is not excluded as the source of the signal.** Any symmetry that allows Ω along the normal for M ∥ [1̄10]_O also allows a static canting of M out of the film plane, since both are axial, time-odd vectors; the C2h symmetry proposed in Fig. 2i mixes M along [1̄10]_O and along [110]_O. A small canting of the remanent magnetisation, from a slightly tilted easy axis or from sample misalignment, would reverse with the in-plane component and give a conventional AHE with exactly the square hysteresis of Fig. 1f. The measured signal is tiny (ρ_AHE ≈ 5 nΩ·cm, σ_AHE ≈ 0.1 S/cm, Fig. 1e,f), while the conventional AHE of LCMO, or of proximity-magnetised CRO, with M out of plane is typically orders of magnitude larger, so a canting well under a degree could account for all of it. Fig. 1d is plotted only up to 2.5 kOe; the text says ρ_AHE "saturates at H ~ 4000 Oe" for H ⊥ (110), but this range is not shown, the out-of-plane M has not saturated in the plotted range, and the saturated out-of-plane ρ_AHE is not given. For this hard axis it is also not clear that the linear-background regime "H > H_S" lies within the field used. Fig. 1d shows only that a normal field produces little M; it does not bound the out-of-plane component of the remanent state reached with an in-plane field. The θ-rotation and (001)-plane rotation tests (Supp. Figs. 17–20) test the external field direction, not the internal canting of M at H = 0, and a tilted easy axis would follow the same angular dependence. The authors should show the full out-of-plane AHE up to saturation with the 7 T magnet and give the conventional AHE per unit out-of-plane M; compute the canting (or sample tilt) needed to give 5 nΩ·cm and compare it with the rotator alignment uncertainty; and bound the actual canting of the remanent state independently, by polar MOKE, XMCD at grazing and normal incidence, or a vector VSM/SQUID with a calibrated perpendicular pickup. They should then express the IP-AHE as a fraction of the out-of-plane AHE coefficient and discuss whether "IP-AHE" and "AHE from symmetry-allowed canting" can be told apart in this point group at all; if not, the claim of a "direct Hall readout of in-plane magnetization" must be framed accordingly.

3. **A thermal (anomalous Nernst) artifact has not been ruled out, and it has exactly the geometry and symmetry of the claimed signal.** With current along [1̄10]_O, Joule heating sets up a temperature gradient normal to the film, along [110]_O toward the NGO substrate. The anomalous Nernst field E ∝ M × ∇T then points along [001]_O, the in-plane Hall direction measured here. This voltage is odd in M, follows the in-plane M–H loop exactly, varies as cos θ when M is rotated in-plane, vanishes for M ∥ [001]_O, and does not depend on the in-plane direction of E; each of these features is offered (Fig. 1e,f; Supp. Figs. 17, 18, 21) as evidence for a Berry-curvature IP-AHE. Because ρ_AHE is only about 5 nΩ·cm, a small thermoelectric contribution would matter; a spin-Seebeck or spin-Hall-type voltage from the metallic CRO layers next to the magnet has the same symmetry. The Methods do not say whether the measurement was DC or AC, give the current magnitude or density, or describe any current reversal. The authors should show that V_xy is linear in I over at least a decade of current density; for DC measurements show that the hysteretic signal reverses with current polarity, and for lock-in measurements report the first and second harmonics separately; and ideally compare with a sample on a substrate of different thermal conductivity or with an estimated anomalous Nernst coefficient for LCMO.

4. **Parallel conduction through the metallic CRO layers is not handled, and it can produce the non-monotonic t_CRO dependence on its own.** σ_AHE = ρ_AHE/ρxx² (p. 5) treats the CRO/LCMO/CRO stack as one homogeneous conductor, and the paper does not say which thickness is used to compute ρ (LCMO only or whole stack). CaRuO3 is a good paramagnetic metal; as t_CRO grows, an increasing share of the current flows in the non-magnetic CRO, diluting the LCMO Hall signal roughly as (t_LC σ_LC / Σ t_i σ_i)², while the squared ρxx amplifies any change in the current distribution. This alone reproduces the fall of σ_AHE at large t_CRO (Fig. 3f, 16 → 32 u.c.). At small t_CRO, T_C and M_s rise with t_CRO (p. 3), pointing to better LCMO conductivity and magnetism; these trends alone could give a maximum at intermediate t_CRO with no change in Berry curvature, as could a CRO interfacial contribution (concern 5). The β_LC data also do not give a single-valued relation with σ_AHE: from Fig. 3f,g, t_CRO = 4 u.c. has β_LC − 90° ≈ 0.13° and σ_AHE ≈ 18 mS/cm, t_CRO = 32 u.c. has ≈ 0.085° and ≈ 5 mS/cm, and t_CRO = 2 u.c. has a smaller β_LC (≈ 0.06°) than 32 u.c. but a larger σ_AHE, as a shunting contribution would predict. Because t_CRO changes β_LC, T_C, M_s, ρxx, interfacial charge transfer and the shunting fraction together, the overlay of Fig. 3f and 3g does not isolate β_LC as the control parameter. The authors should state the thickness used; report the raw ρxx (or sheet resistance), R_xy/R_AHE, T_C and M_s at 10 K for every t_CRO; analyse the Hall data with a parallel-conductor model using sheet conductances of CRO-only and LCMO-only films grown under identical conditions, and report the Hall conductance of the LCMO layer itself; and plot this intrinsic σ_AHE directly against β_LC, d_OR and M_s. The t_LC series (Supp. Fig. 22) needs the same treatment.

5. **A CRO-borne or interfacial anomalous Hall contribution is not excluded, so the layer in which the IP-AHE originates is not established.** The higher T_C and M_s with t_CRO are attributed to interfacial Ru⁴⁺→Mn⁴⁺ charge transfer (p. 3; refs. 30–32), which implies an induced moment on interfacial Ru. CaRuO3 is strongly spin-orbit-coupled and near a ferromagnetic instability, so a magnetised CRO interface region with its own Berry curvature could carry part or all of the IP-AHE. Such a contribution would also scale non-monotonically with t_CRO, vanish when CRO is protonated (Fig. 4), and could reverse sign with temperature as in SrRuO3 (Supp. Fig. 24). The gating experiment cannot separate "CRO changes LCMO symmetry" from "the CRO contribution is switched off": by the paper's own XRD the LCMO lattice is unchanged below V_g ≈ 2.2 V, yet σ_AHE vanishes at 1.4 V. The t_CRO = 0 film also shows a small but finite σ_AHE (Fig. 3a). The authors should measure in-plane Hall on a CRO single layer and on asymmetric structures (bottom-CRO-only and top-CRO-only bilayers, and a CRO/LCMO stack with a thin non-magnetic insulating spacer that keeps the OOR coupling but cuts the electronic one), and probe for an induced Ru moment by Ru- and Mn-edge XMCD or by polarised neutron reflectometry of the magnetisation depth profile. At minimum this alternative should be discussed.

6. **The gating mechanism is inferred from reference films and DFT, not measured on the gated trilayer.** The claim that ionic-liquid gating "reconfigures the symmetry breaking" by protonating CRO, reducing its octahedral rotations and hence the monoclinic distortion of LCMO (p. 7; Fig. 4b,d insets; Supp. Fig. 37), rests on in situ XRD of separate CRO and LCMO films (Supp. Fig. 33), on unchanged ρxx–T and H_C, and on DFT. β_LC is not measured in the gated trilayer, and the insets of Fig. 4d are schematic, so the central link gating → reduced β_LC → σ_AHE = 0 is not shown. Several competing explanations are not addressed. Protonated H_xCaRuO3 is reported to become ferromagnetic (ref. 48), which would add a second magnetic metallic layer whose own conventional or in-plane Hall response, or exchange coupling, could cancel or change the signal. Protonation should change CRO's conductivity and so the current distribution (concern 4), yet ρxx is "nearly unchanged" (Supp. Fig. 34); these statements need reconciling. Protons or oxygen vacancies may enter LCMO near the interface below what bulk XRD of a reference film detects, and it is unclear how protons reach the bottom CRO beneath 32 u.c. of LCMO if LCMO protonates only above 2.2 V. H_C also rises from about 250 to about 290 Oe at V_g = 1.1–1.3 V (Fig. 4d), a change of about 15%, not "nearly unchanged". The authors should show RSMs (β_LC, d_OR) or STEM of a trilayer at V_g = 0, 1.2 V and 1.4 V and after reversal; ρxx at 10 K and the ordinary Hall coefficient versus V_g on the same device; M–H (including M_s) at 10 K in the gated state; and SIMS or another depth-resolved H probe if available. They should discuss whether the top and bottom CRO layers protonate differently.

**B. Should address**

7. **β_LC is tiny compared with its likely precision, and it is measured at a different temperature from transport.** β_LC − 90° spans only 0.03–0.2° (Fig. 3g), with error bars of about 0.005–0.01° attributed to "broadening of the RSM diffraction spot". The paper does not explain how a layer-averaged tilt of a few hundredths of a degree is extracted reliably from four asymmetric reflections of a 32 u.c. layer on a near-lattice-matched substrate, given instrument resolution, peak overlap with CRO and NGO (Fig. 2a–d), mosaicity, substrate miscut, sample-mounting tilt and possible twin domains (mentioned for t_CRO ≥ 32 u.c.). The RSMs appear to be taken at room temperature while σ_AHE is measured at 10 K; LCMO and NGO change structurally on cooling and LCMO goes through its ferromagnetic transition, so β_LC at 10 K may differ. The data also show a steep nonlinearity (σ_AHE ≈ 18, 38 and 103 mS/cm at β − 90° ≈ 0.13°, 0.15° and 0.20°), in contrast with the nearly linear DFT trend. Because Fig. 3g is the main quantitative support for the mechanism, the authors should describe the fitting model and how β_LC is computed from the four reflections, give the instrument angular resolution, propagate the uncertainties, give Supp. Table 1 values from more than one reflection and sample, show at least one low-temperature RSM or argue that β does not change on cooling, and cross-check against STEM/GPA tilt angles for at least two t_CRO values.

8. **Each condition appears to come from a single sample or device.** Each t_CRO point in Fig. 3f appears to be one sample and Hall bar, with no error bar on σ_AHE, and the gating data come from one device. The headline correlation of Fig. 3f with 3g depends on a single peak point at 16 u.c. and a sharp drop by 32 u.c., with no data in between. The authors should state how many samples and devices were measured per thickness and show the spread of both σ_AHE and β_LC, add repeat samples at key thicknesses (for example 8 and 16 u.c.), and add intermediate thicknesses (for example 20–28 u.c.) to show whether the drop is a smooth structural relaxation or a threshold. For the "fully reversible ON/OFF switching" claim (Supp. Fig. 32) they should give the number of cycles and the cycle-to-cycle variation.

9. **The antisymmetrisation procedure for hysteretic data is incorrect as written.** The Methods give R_xy^odd(H) = |R_xy(H) − R_xy(−H)|/2. The absolute value would remove the sign of the signal, yet the sign is central (the inverted loop in Fig. 1f, the sign reversals in Supp. Fig. 24). For hysteretic loops the odd part must be built from opposite sweep branches, R↑(H) − R↓(−H); otherwise the field-even planar-Hall/AMR background is not removed in the coercive region, and with E ∥ M a small angular misalignment produces a planar Hall term that switches at H_C. The authors should correct the formula, state the branch pairing, and show raw R_xy loops for both sweep directions next to the extracted ρ_AHE for at least one sample, including the planar-Hall background amplitude relative to the ~nΩ·cm odd signal.

10. **The DFT comparison is only qualitative: it differs from experiment in magnitude by 20–100× and does not isolate the monoclinic distortion.** σ_AHE-DFT is 2–8 S/cm (Fig. 3h) while the measured σ_AHE is 0.004–0.1 S/cm (Fig. 3a–f, in mS/cm), yet the paper calls the DFT "consistent with the experimental trend" without discussing the gap. In the supercells β_LC (90.0°, 90.08°, 90.16°) is changed by changing the OOR and Jahn–Teller amplitudes together (p. 6, Supp. Fig. 26), and those amplitudes and their relation to the real film are not stated, so the DFT trend cannot separate a monoclinic-tilt effect from an OOR-amplitude effect. The calculations also rest on choices that may matter: VCA for Ca, U = 8 eV and J = 2 eV taken from LaMnO3 rather than a doped manganite, 0.02 eV broadening, and structures far from the experimental 32 u.c. LCMO without the real interfaces; the relaxed CRO/LCMO heterostructures of Supp. Fig. 31 are used only for β, not for σ. The authors should say the comparison is qualitative and discuss the discrepancy (effective magnetic volume, shunting from concern 4, disorder, VCA, U, thickness, normalisation); state the OOR amplitudes in each supercell; vary β at fixed OOR amplitude and the OOR amplitude at β = 90°; show σ_AHE-DFT versus U and versus chemical potential; and ideally compute σ_AHE for the relaxed superlattices of Supp. Fig. 31.

11. **The temperature dependence and scaling point to more than one contribution, weakening the "predominantly intrinsic" assignment.** For some samples (t_CRO ≥ 32 u.c. or < 8 u.c.) σ_AHE changes sign near 100 K (p. 5, Supp. Fig. 24), and the σ_AHE–M dependence is "strongly nonlinear and sign-changing". The paper compares this with SrRuO3 band topology, but a sign change is also what two contributions of opposite sign would give (LCMO plus interfacial CRO, or intrinsic plus a canting-, skew- or side-jump-type term). If β_LC controls the IP-AHE, the paper should also explain why the sign depends on temperature differently in different samples. The "intrinsic Berry-curvature origin" (Supp. Fig. 25) rests on a weak dependence of σ_AHE on σxx, but the σxx range spanned is not stated, and over a narrow window in a moderately conducting oxide that cannot exclude side-jump or interfacial contributions. The authors should show the scaling plots in the main text, state the σxx range and the fitting form (for example TYJ scaling) with its parameters, test whether the sign change correlates with t_CRO in a way consistent with a CRO-interface contribution, and qualify the intrinsic assignment.

12. **The STEM evidence for the tilts is overstated.** The text says Fig. 2f "clearly resolve[s] the β angles" and that the opposite monoclinic tilts are "directly visible" in HAADF-STEM, confirmed by GPA. A deviation of 0.1–0.2° cannot be seen by eye in a HAADF image, the drawn angles in Fig. 2f are schematic, and the GPA map of Fig. 2h uses a ±10° colour scale with contrast mainly at the interfaces. The authors should quantify the tilt from GPA or atom-position fits with per-layer uncertainties, state whether the STEM values agree with the RSM-derived 0.1–0.2°, and soften the language if these are below the STEM precision.

13. **The NGO(001) reference is not a clean null control.** The (001)-oriented samples (Supp. Figs. 4, 23) differ from the (110) trilayers in film orientation, strain state, magnetic anisotropy axis and current direction, not only in mirror-symmetry breaking. A control that keeps the (110) geometry but removes CRO's octahedral coupling would isolate the claimed mechanism more cleanly, for example a non-rotating metallic spacer (SrRuO3 on a cubic template, or LaNiO3) in place of CRO, or an insulating cap with matched OOR. If no such control is available, the (001) comparison should be presented as supportive rather than decisive.

14. **The "electrical" ON/OFF switching is set at room temperature, and its reversibility is not characterised.** V_g is applied at room temperature and the sample is then cooled with the gate held (Methods), so the modulation cannot be performed in situ at the measurement temperature; the abstract's "dynamic reconfiguration" and "fully reversible ON/OFF switching" should be qualified accordingly. The authors should report the number of ON/OFF cycles and any degradation (Supp. Fig. 32), the gate leakage current, and the proton concentration actually introduced (for example by SIMS, NRA or ToF-ERDA), and show that LCMO does not take up protons at 1.1–1.4 V; unchanged lattice parameters are not proof of that.
