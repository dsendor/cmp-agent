**A. Must address**

1. **The signal is not shown to be distinct from the conventional AHE of a small out-of-plane magnetization component, and the out-of-plane control is invalid as performed.** The observed signal is tiny: ρAHE ≈ 4.5–4.7 nΩ·cm (Fig. 1g), σAHE ≈ 0.10 S/cm with ρxx ≈ 200 μΩ·cm, a Hall angle of about 10⁻⁵. Fig. 1f is offered as showing that the conventional AHE is "negligible" for H⊥(110)O, but it covers only about ±2–2.5 kOe. Fig. 1c shows M reaches only ~0.7 μB/Mn at 2 kOe against ~3.5 μB/Mn in plane, so the out-of-plane magnetization is far from saturated (demagnetizing field 4πMs ≈ 6–8 kOe). In this regime M_z is linear in H, so the AHE ∝ M_z is also linear, and "subtracting the linear component" removes it together with the ordinary Hall term. Fig. 1f therefore says nothing about the conventional anomalous Hall coefficient of this stack, and the reader cannot judge how much canting would produce the signal. LCMO-based films typically show conventional σAHE of order 1–10 S/cm or more, so an out-of-plane tilt of M of order 0.1–1° (sub-percent canting) would reproduce it. Such a tilt could come from substrate miscut, or from an intrinsic tilt of the easy axis: the monoclinic distortion the authors invoke (c_p tilting toward a_p, β_LC − 90° ≈ 0.03–0.2°, Fig. 4g, with the only mirror perpendicular to [001]O) allows M along [-110]O to cant toward [110]O, the film normal. That canting has the same symmetry as the proposed Ω_z. It would also give a square loop, the cos θ dependence of Fig. 3c, the φ independence of Fig. 3e and a correlation with β_LC (Fig. 4f,g), so none of these tests separates IP-AHE from conventional AHE driven by M_z ∝ M_[-110]. The out-of-plane field rotations (Supplementary Figs. 15, 16) address misalignment of the applied field, not an intrinsic tilt of the easy axis, and are relegated to the Supplement where they cannot be judged. The authors should: measure ρxy for H⊥(110)O up to ~7 T, well beyond out-of-plane saturation, and extract the anomalous Hall coefficient R_s (or σAHE^z at full M_z) for the same trilayers; state the canting angle that would be needed to give the observed signal; bound the actual out-of-plane component when M lies along [-110]O independently (vector or rotating-sample magnetometry, out-of-plane SQUID remanence after in-plane saturation, polar MOKE, XMCD in normal incidence or PNR); report the substrate miscut angle and direction, and ideally compare samples with miscut along versus perpendicular to [-110]O; and bring the analysis of Supplementary Figs. 15–16 into the main text with explicit fits separating the two contributions, showing quantitatively that m_z times the conventional AHE coefficient is much smaller than the observed ρAHE. Without this, "in-plane AHE" and the Berry-curvature interpretation of Eq. (1) are not established.

2. **The symmetry argument that the CRO-induced monoclinic distortion is needed to activate the IP-AHE conflicts with bulk Pbnm symmetry and with the paper's own DFT.** The paper argues (pp. 6–8; Supplementary Fig. 7, Tables 1–2) that the CRO/LCMO interfaces remove the mirrors M(100), M(010) and the axes 2[100], 2[010], lowering LCMO to P2₁/m (C2h), and that only then does in-plane M ∥ [-110]O break the remaining mirror ⊥[001]O and activate Ω_z. But in bulk mmm, a magnetization along [-110]O already breaks m⊥a, m⊥b, 2a and 2b, because each maps [-110] onto [110] or its reverse. What survives is m⊥c and 2c combined with time reversal, plus inversion: the magnetic point group 2'/m' with c as the unique axis, the same group the authors obtain for the monoclinic cell. It permits a Hall pseudovector anywhere in the plane ⊥c, including along the film normal [110]O. The DFT agrees: the orthorhombic supercell with β_LC = 90.00° already gives a finite σDFT of about 4 S/cm (Fig. 4i,j), and only the cubic cell without octahedral rotations gives zero. The tCRO = 0 single LCMO/NGO film also shows a finite signal (Fig. 4a,f). So neither β_LC nor the CRO-induced removal of mirrors "activates" IP-AHE; at most the buffer changes its magnitude, and β_LC may simply correlate with the rotation amplitude. The authors should give a full magnetic-point-group analysis for bulk Pbnm LCMO in the (110)O orientation and for the P2₁/m trilayer, with M along [-110]O, [001]O and [110]O; state which σ_ij components each case allows and what, if anything, the reduced symmetry adds; show the single LCMO/NGO(110) film in the main text as the "no buffer" reference; and recast the claim as tuning the magnitude of an already-allowed IP-AHE. The statements "unambiguously couples to the CaRuO3-buffer-induced mirror-symmetry breaking" and "one-to-one correspondence" (abstract; p. 8), "monoclinic tilting angle βLC can be seen as a reliable parameter for evaluating the degree of mirror-symmetry breaking" (p. 7) and the Conclusion should be revised.

3. **Which layer carries the anomalous Hall current is not established; parallel conduction through the metallic CRO and a ruthenate or interfacial Hall contribution are not treated.** Each CRO buffer is 16 u.c. (about 6 nm) thick in the main samples, comparable to the 32 u.c. LCMO layer, and CRO is a good metal; the total thickness varies from 32 to 160 u.c. across the series. σAHE = ρAHE/ρxx² assumes a single homogeneous conductor, and the paper does not state which thickness defines ρ (all layers or LCMO only). In a parallel stack, a growing CRO thickness by itself redistributes current and changes the apparent σAHE; the decrease at tCRO = 32 and 64 u.c. (Fig. 4f) is what shunting by thick CRO would produce, and shunting alone could give a non-monotonic σAHE(tCRO). In addition, the interfacial Ru⁴⁺→Mn⁴⁺ charge transfer invoked to explain the higher T_C (p. 5) implies induced Ru moments (Refs. 30–32), and Ru has strong SOC; Ru-derived bands are known to give large, sign-changing Berry-curvature AHE, as in SrRuO3. The sign reversal near 100 K (Fig. 4h inset), which the authors themselves compare to SrRuO3 (p. 9), points to at least two competing contributions, possibly a Ru-derived or interfacial one, while the DFT models LCMO only. The authors should: state how ρxx and σAHE are computed and show raw R_xy and R_xx for each sample; give ρxx(T) for every tCRO and for CRO-only films on NGO; model the trilayer as parallel channels to extract the LCMO-layer Hall conductivity; measure an in-plane Hall control on a CRO-only film; measure CRO/LCMO bilayers with CRO only on top or only on the bottom (one interface), and an LCMO film with a non-magnetic, weak-SOC or insulating buffer that imposes similar octahedral tilts (or another buffer that changes β_LC without a conducting, high-SOC layer); measure element-specific interfacial magnetism (Ru/Mn XMCD); measure a t_LCMO series at fixed tCRO to test whether σAHE scales as a bulk or an interface quantity; decompose σAHE(T) into contributions of the two signs; and discuss whether the signal lives in LCMO, CRO or the interfaces.

4. **The gating experiment does not establish that IP-AHE is switched through a structural change in LCMO, and its own structural data point against it.** The XRD under gating shows the CRO(440) peak shifting while "the LCMO(440)o peak position remains nearly unchanged" (p. 11, Supplementary Fig. 24), yet σAHE drops to zero between 1.1 and 1.4 V (Fig. 5c,d). No measurement of β_LC or of the LCMO octahedral tilts after gating is shown; the inset of Fig. 5d is a schematic, and the proposed suppression of LCMO rotations rests only on DFT for bulk HxCaRuO3 (Supplementary Fig. 27). Simpler readings are not ruled out: the signal may be carried by the CRO layers or interfaces, which are the layers being protonated (concern 3); protonated CaRuO3 is reported to become ferromagnetic (Ref. 48), which could add a layer with its own, possibly opposite-sign, AHE; and protonation changes the CRO conductivity, interfacial charge transfer and carrier density, so the current split alone changes ρAHE/ρxx² without any change in Berry curvature. An unchanged H_C alone does not demonstrate "nearly invariant ferromagnetism". The ">2.5 V" protonation threshold for manganites (p. 10) is taken from the literature, not measured on these samples, and it is unclear how protons reach the bottom CRO layer through LCMO, or whether the top CRO alone is responsible. The authors should: measure RSMs (or at least the LCMO orthorhombicity) at Vg = 0 and 1.4 V and extract β_LC; report ρxx(Vg) and M–H loops (M_s and remanence along [-110]O, by SQUID) versus Vg; state the gating temperature and protocol relative to the 10 K measurement; quantify the reversibility of the ON/OFF switching in the main text (number of cycles with full loops, recovery fraction of σAHE and ρxx, gate leakage current); confirm whether the LCMO is protonated at 1.4 V and where the protons sit (e.g. SIMS depth profile, or neutron reflectometry); and gate a single-layer LCMO/NGO film under the same conditions as a control.

5. **The correlation between β_LC and σAHE across tCRO (Fig. 4f,g) is used as causal evidence, but other quantities co-vary with tCRO and the trend is weakly supported.** The text itself says T_C and M_s increase with tCRO through interfacial charge transfer (p. 5, Supplementary Fig. 4), ρxx is tCRO-dependent (p. 8), and remanence ratio and loop squareness differ visibly between Figs. 4a–e; carrier density, twin-domain fraction and strain relaxation also change. Each of these would change σAHE without invoking β_LC. The structural change is tiny (β_LC − 90° spans about 0.03–0.2°), and the correspondence is loose: β_LC − 90° differs by only ~0.05° between 8 and 16 u.c. yet σAHE changes 3×, while 4 u.c. (≈ 0.13°) and 32 u.c. (≈ 0.08°) differ by ~4× in σ. The non-monotonic trend rests on one sample per thickness with a single peak point at 16 u.c. (103.7 mS/cm, 3× the 8 u.c. and 20× the 32 u.c. value), no error bars on σAHE, and only two points beyond it. The explanation for the decrease above 16 u.c. (strain relaxation and a/b twin domains, p. 9) is asserted, not shown; twinning would also cut the CRO-imposed axis-to-axis relation and the uniaxial anisotropy. The authors should tabulate M_s, M_r/M_s, T_C, ρxx (10 K), twin fraction and β_LC for every tCRO; normalize σAHE by M_s or the remanent [-110]O magnetization (or show the anomalous Hall angle); plot σAHE against β_LC directly with both error bars and show that β_LC predicts σAHE better than the alternatives; show RSM/STEM evidence of twinning and relaxation for the 32 and 64 u.c. samples; and add samples at 24 u.c. and repeat samples at the key thicknesses (0, 8, 16, 32 u.c.).

8. **The DFT comparison is not quantitative, its parameters are not justified, and its trend does not isolate β_LC.** σDFT ≈ 4–9 S/cm (Fig. 4i–k), against at most ~0.1 S/cm measured (103.7 mS/cm, Fig. 4f): a gap of roughly 50–100×, which is not discussed. The calculated σ is already 4 S/cm at β_LC = 90.00° and rises only by a factor of two up to 90.16°, so octahedral rotation, not the tilt, dominates (concern 2). U = 8 eV, J = 2 eV on Mn 3d is unusually large and untested; VCA for La/Ca, a 2×2×2 cell and a Mn-3d-only Wannier projection (which may miss O 2p contributions) are approximations whose effect on a Berry-curvature quantity can be large. It is unclear how the β_LC = 90.08° and 90.16° structures were built if the structure was "fully relaxed" — which OOR and Jahn–Teller amplitudes were imposed — and varying them together does not isolate β_LC. The VASP relaxation and WIEN2k bands may correspond to different structures, the quoted 7×7×7 mesh is for the band structure rather than the Berry-curvature integral, and the calculation is for bulk LCMO without the CRO layers or interfaces. The authors should show the sensitivity of σDFT to U (for example 3–8 eV), Fermi-level position/doping, the Wannier window (including O 2p) and k-mesh convergence; vary β_LC at fixed OOR and JT amplitudes; describe the construction of the distorted cells; and discuss the magnitude mismatch, or explicitly present the DFT as a trend study.
