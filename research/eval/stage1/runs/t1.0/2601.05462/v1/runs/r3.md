Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors report an anomalous Hall signal driven by in-plane magnetization (IP-AHE) in CaRuO3/La2/3Ca1/3MnO3/CaRuO3 trilayers on NdGaO3(110). The signal is square and hysteretic at zero field, its size depends non-monotonically on CaRuO3 buffer thickness, and ionic-liquid gating suppresses it reversibly. The paper attributes all of this to a monoclinic-like distortion of the LCMO layer imposed by the CaRuO3 buffer. Finding IP-AHE in a conventional oxide ferromagnet with strong uniaxial anisotropy, and switching it electrically, would be a useful addition to a field so far limited to layered and Weyl magnets. However, the symmetry argument at the centre of the "symmetry-engineering" framing does not hold as stated, and the mechanism behind both tuning knobs is not isolated from simpler alternatives.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The IP-AHE signal is clean, remanent and square (Figs. 1g, 4d). Its angular dependence was checked in two independent ways:
  - rotating H in plane gives a cosine θ-dependence (Fig. 3b,c);
  - Hall bars cut at different in-plane orientations give a φ-independent signal (Fig. 3d,e), which rules out a planar-Hall origin. This is a well-designed control.
- The hard-axis loop (Fig. 1h) is explained naturally by the remanent [1̄10]O component, which ties the Hall signal to that magnetization component.
- The structural characterization is extensive: RSMs around four reflections, HAADF/ABF-STEM and GPA.
- A buffer-thickness series and an in-situ gating series are both included, so the paper offers more than a single-sample observation.
- The gating result (ON/OFF switching at roughly constant coercive field) is attractive from a device standpoint.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The symmetry argument does not single out the monoclinic distortion. IP-AHE is already allowed in orthorhombic (Pbnm) LCMO with M ∥ [1̄10]O.**
   - *The paper's argument (pp. 6–7).* The CRO/LCMO interfaces remove the mirrors M(100), M(010) and the axes 2[100], 2[010]. This lowers LCMO to P21/m (C2h). That leaves only the mirror ⊥[001]O, which in-plane M ∥ [1̄10]O then breaks, "activating" IP-AHE.
   - *Why this does not follow.* In bulk mmm, a magnetization along [1̄10]O already breaks m⊥a, m⊥b, 2a and 2b, because each maps [1̄10] onto [110] or its reverse. What survives is m⊥c combined with time reversal, 2c combined with time reversal, and inversion. This magnetic point group (2'/m') is the same one the authors obtain for the monoclinic cell. It permits a Hall pseudovector anywhere in the plane ⊥c, including along the film normal [110]O.
   - *The paper's own data agree.* The DFT finds a finite σDFT for orthorhombic LCMO with βLC = 90.00° (Fig. 4i), and the tCRO = 0 sample shows a finite signal (Fig. 4a).
   - *Consequence.* Neither the monoclinic tilt βLC nor the CRO-induced removal of mirrors is what "activates" IP-AHE. At most, the buffer changes its magnitude.
   - *Statements that should be revised:* "unambiguously couples to the CaRuO3-buffer-induced mirror-symmetry breaking" (abstract); "monoclinic tilting angle βLC can be seen as a reliable parameter for evaluating the degree of mirror-symmetry breaking" (p. 7); the conclusion.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Give the full magnetic-point-group analysis for both the Pbnm and P21/m cases with M ∥ [1̄10]O and M ∥ [001]O.
     - State explicitly what the reduced symmetry adds. Possibly it allows new tensor components, or it acts only through magnitude.
     - Reframe the claim accordingly. The single LCMO/NGO(110) film should be shown in the main text as the "no buffer" reference.

2. **The correlation between σAHE and βLC across tCRO (Fig. 4f,g) is not shown to be causal. Several other quantities change with tCRO.**
   - *Variables that co-vary with tCRO.* The text itself says Tc and Ms increase with tCRO through interfacial Ru⁴⁺→Mn⁴⁺ charge transfer (p. 5). ρxx is also tCRO-dependent (p. 8). The remanence ratio and loop squareness differ visibly between Figs. 4a–e. Each of these would change σAHE without invoking βLC.
   - *The size of the structural change is tiny.* The βLC − 90° values span only about 0.03–0.2°, one sample per thickness, with a single peak at 16 u.c. The proposed mechanism for the decrease beyond 16 u.c. (strain relaxation and a/b twin domains, p. 9) is not demonstrated; RSMs or STEM of the 32 and 64 u.c. samples would be needed.
   - *What the authors should show:*
     - Ms, Mr/Ms, Tc and ρxx (10 K) for every tCRO.
     - σAHE normalized by the remanent [1̄10]O magnetization.
     - At least two samples at the key thicknesses (0, 8, 16, 32 u.c.), so that the non-monotonic trend can be distinguished from sample-to-sample scatter.
     - A plot of σAHE against βLC directly, with both error bars.

3. **Which layer carries the anomalous Hall current is not established. Contributions from CaRuO3 or the interfaces are not excluded.**
   - *CaRuO3 conducts in parallel.* CRO is a metal, and the bilayer is measured as one conductor. σAHE = ρAHE/ρxx² treats the stack as homogeneous; the thickness used to compute ρ is not stated (all layers, or LCMO only).
   - *A ruthenate-like contribution is plausible.* Interfacial charge transfer and proximity to the ferromagnet can polarize CRO near the interface, and Ru has strong spin–orbit coupling. The authors themselves note that the sign reversal near 100 K in the weak-IP-AHE samples resembles the intrinsic AHE of SrRuO3 (p. 9, Fig. 4h inset). This points to at least two competing contributions with different temperature dependences.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - State how ρxx and σAHE are computed: thickness, and whether parallel conduction is corrected for.
     - Show ρxx(T) of single CRO films on NGO.
     - Measure a control trilayer in which CRO is replaced by a weak-SOC or insulating buffer that imposes a similar octahedral-rotation pattern, or report CRO/LCMO bilayers with CRO only on top or only on the bottom.
     - Decompose σAHE(T) into contributions with the two signs.

4. **The gating mechanism (protonation of CRO → reduced LCMO tilt → loss of IP-AHE) rests on indirect evidence, and simpler explanations are not ruled out.**
   - *Only CRO is shown to change structurally.* The XRD shows a CRO(440) shift but an unchanged LCMO(440) peak (Supplementary Fig. 24). The claimed decrease of the LCMO tilt is supported only by DFT (Supplementary Fig. 27), not by an RSM or STEM measurement of βLC after gating.
   - *Protonated CRO is itself magnetic.* The paper's ref. 48 reports emergent ferromagnetism in HxCaRuO3. Its Hall response could add to, or cancel, the LCMO signal.
   - *Current redistribution.* Protonation changes CRO's conductivity, and so the current split between the layers. This alone changes ρAHE/ρxx² without any change in Berry curvature.
   - *What the authors should show:*
     - ρxx versus Vg.
     - M–H loops (Ms and remanence along [1̄10]O) before and after gating. Hc alone is not "nearly invariant ferromagnetism".
     - An RSM-derived βLC at Vg = 0 and Vg = 1.4 V.
     - The gating temperature and protocol (the Methods do not say whether gating is done at room temperature before cooling to 10 K).
     - The number of reversible ON/OFF cycles in Supplementary Fig. 23, and whether σAHE recovers to its initial value.
     - A gated single-layer LCMO/NGO control, to test whether the LCMO itself responds below 1.5 V.

**B. Should address**

5. **The DFT comparison is qualitative, and its parameters are not justified.**
   - *Magnitude.* σDFT ≈ 4–9 S cm⁻¹ (Fig. 4i–k), against about 0.1 S cm⁻¹ measured (Fig. 4f): a gap of nearly two orders of magnitude, which is not discussed.
   - *The trend is not from the tilt.* The calculated σ is already 4 S cm⁻¹ at βLC = 90.00° and rises by only a factor of two up to 90.16°. Octahedral rotation, not the tilt, dominates (see concern 1).
   - *Parameters and construction.*
     - U = 8 eV, J = 2 eV is large for Mn 3d and is not tested.
     - It is unclear how structures with prescribed βLC = 90.08° and 90.16° were made if the structure was "fully relaxed".
     - VCA for La/Ca and a Mn-3d-only Wannier projection may miss O-p contributions to the Berry curvature.
     - The relaxation in VASP and the bands in WIEN2k may correspond to different structures.
   - *What the authors should do:* give the U-dependence, the construction of the distorted cells, Fermi-level/doping sensitivity, and convergence of σ with k-mesh.

6. **Fig. 4k appears to contradict the experiment.** The caption defines θ as the angle between M and [1̄10]O, yet σDFT is zero at θ = 0° and maximal at 90°. Experimentally, IP-AHE is maximal for M ∥ [1̄10]O and vanishes for M ∥ [001]O (Fig. 3c). The inset suggests θ may be measured from [001]O instead. The definition must be corrected, or the discrepancy explained, because the text cites Fig. 4k as confirming "the critical role of the uniaxial M//[1̄10]O".

7. **The STEM evidence for the tilt is overstated relative to the angles involved.**
   - The βLC − 90° values are ≤ 0.2°, yet the text says the tilting is "even visible directly in an atomic-scale STEM-HAADF image (Fig. 2i)".
   - The GPA map in Fig. 2g uses a ±10° colour scale, which cannot resolve 0.1° differences.
   - The authors should quantify the tilt from GPA with an uncertainty, compare it with the RSM values, and drop claims of direct visibility.
   - They should also explain how the "layer-averaged" RSM β separates the thin CRO layers (16 u.c.) from the substrate peak (Fig. 2a–d), and how the error bars in Fig. 4g were obtained.

8. **The temperature range and the sign change are not fully characterized.**
   - Tc ≥ 250 K, yet σAHE(T) is shown only up to 200 K, and the abstract and conclusion say IP-AHE "persists … up to 200 K". Data up to Tc are needed to say whether the signal vanishes before the magnetization does, which would itself indicate a non-LCMO origin.
   - The sign change near 100 K in some samples (Fig. 4h inset) should be discussed together with concern 3. Showing ρAHE versus M (or versus ρxx) scaling would help separate intrinsic from extrinsic contributions.

9. **Sample statistics and reproducibility are not given.**
   - The number of samples and devices per thickness, and the device-to-device spread, are not stated.
   - The φ-series (Fig. 3d,e) uses separate Hall bars; their resistivities and any normalization should be given.
   - Repeated cooldowns, or the same device measured after thermal cycling, would support the stability of the non-monotonic trend.

10. **The extraction of ρAHE needs more detail.**
    - The linear background is fitted at H > Hs ≈ 2 kOe, but the panels in Fig. 1g and Fig. 4 span only ±0.4–0.9 kOe.
    - For in-plane fields, the source of the linear slope (Fig. 1f) should be stated (misalignment, or ordinary Hall from the out-of-plane field component).
    - Planar Hall and AMR pickup from contact misalignment can produce even-in-H contributions. Was ρxy antisymmetrized, and how?

**C. Minor**

11. **Units disagree between Fig. 4h and Fig. 4a–f.** Fig. 4h plots σAHE in units of 10⁻² S cm⁻¹, with values reaching −100, i.e. −1 S cm⁻¹. Figs. 4a–f and the text give about 0.1 S cm⁻¹ (103.7 mS cm⁻¹). One of them is off by a factor of ten.

12. **Sign conventions are inconsistent.**
    - Fig. 4f plots σAHE on an inverted (negative) axis, while the text quotes +103.7 mS cm⁻¹.
    - The loops in Figs. 1g and 4d are described as "inverted".
    - A single sign convention, with the branch used for the zero-field value, should be stated.

13. **Fig. 3 caption errors.**
    - Panel (d) refers to "various ω values"; this should be φ.
    - Panel (c) quotes the saturated ρAHE at H = 1000 Oe, while the text (p. 8) says 2000 Oe.

14. **The Data availability statement is incomplete.** It ends with "available from".

15. **Wording is too strong in several places.** "Unambiguously" (abstract), "faithfully", and "rarely observed" (p. 9) should be toned down or supported with a quantitative comparison table of zero-field IP-AHE in other systems.

16. **The Conclusion begins with a lowercase "we".**

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Put the symmetry analysis on a firm magnetic-point-group footing for both orthorhombic and monoclinic LCMO, and reframe the work as tuning the magnitude of an already-allowed IP-AHE through octahedral-rotation engineering. The data may support this well; the "activation" framing they do not.
2. Add a CRO-free or weak-SOC-buffer control, together with Ms, Mr, ρxx and Tc versus tCRO, so that σAHE ∝ βLC can be separated from changes in magnetization and conduction.
3. Strengthen the gating section with ρxx(Vg), M–H loops and post-gating RSM of LCMO, and quantify the endurance of the ON/OFF switching.
4. Make the DFT comparison quantitative, or explicitly limit it to trends: report the U-dependence and resolve the Fig. 4k angle definition.
