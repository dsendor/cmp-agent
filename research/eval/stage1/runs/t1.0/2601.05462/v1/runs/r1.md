Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports a Hall signal that follows the in-plane magnetization of CaRuO3/La2/3Ca1/3MnO3/CaRuO3 trilayers on NdGaO3(110). The authors call it an in-plane anomalous Hall effect (IP-AHE). They attribute it to a monoclinic distortion of the LCMO layer imposed by the CaRuO3 (CRO) buffers, show that it varies non-monotonically with CRO thickness, and switch it off and on by ionic-liquid protonation. If the signal is a Berry-curvature Hall response to in-plane M, and if it is controlled by the LCMO octahedral distortion, an oxide platform with square zero-field loops and electrical switching would interest the Hall-effect and oxide-spintronics communities. As written, however, the evidence does not separate the proposed mechanism from more mundane explanations, and several of the paper's own results (the DFT at beta_LC = 90.00 deg, the unchanged LCMO lattice under gating) point away from it.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The transport data are clean. The rho_AHE loops are square, follow the easy-axis M-H loop (Fig. 1d,g), and survive at zero field. The hard-axis response (Fig. 1h) fits the uniaxial anisotropy picture.
- The angular checks are useful. Field rotation in-plane (Fig. 3b,c) and Hall bars cut along several directions (Fig. 3d,e) separate the signal from the ordinary planar Hall effect.
- The structural characterization is extensive: RSMs near four reflections, GPA on HAADF-STEM, and ABF imaging of octahedral coupling.
- There is a thickness series of seven tCRO values with matching structural (beta_LC) and transport data, which is the right kind of design for a tuning claim.
- Reversible gate switching of the Hall signal with almost unchanged coercive field (Fig. 5c,d) is an attractive functional result.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The signal is not shown to be distinct from the conventional AHE of a small out-of-plane magnetization component. The measured rho_AHE is only about 4.5 nOhm cm (Fig. 1g). Fig. 1f shows the out-of-plane Hall response only up to 2.5 kOe, where M_perp is far from saturated (Fig. 1c). So the paper never gives the conventional AHE coefficient of the same film, and the reader cannot judge how much out-of-plane canting would produce 4.5 nOhm cm. A sub-percent canting may be enough. The out-of-plane field rotations (Supplementary Figs. 15, 16) address misalignment of the applied field. They do not address an intrinsic tilt of the easy axis out of the film plane. The monoclinic distortion the authors invoke (c_p tilting toward a_p, beta != 90 deg, with the only mirror perpendicular to [001]o) allows exactly such a tilt: M along [-110]o can cant toward [110]o, the film normal. That canting has the same symmetry as the proposed Omega_z. It would also give the cos(theta) dependence of Fig. 3c, the phi independence of Fig. 3e, and a correlation with beta_LC (Fig. 4f,g). The authors should:
   - measure the saturated out-of-plane AHE of the same samples (fields above the demagnetizing field, about 1 T or more);
   - measure the out-of-plane magnetization component when M lies along [-110]o, using vector or rotating-sample magnetometry, polar MOKE, or XMCD in normal incidence;
   - show quantitatively that m_z times the conventional AHE coefficient is much smaller than the observed rho_AHE.
   Without this, "in-plane AHE" and the Berry-curvature interpretation of Eq. (1) are not established.

2. The symmetry argument that the CRO-induced monoclinic distortion is needed to activate the IP-AHE conflicts with the paper's own DFT and with bulk Pbnm symmetry. For Pbnm (point group mmm) with M along the in-plane diagonal [-110]o, the magnetic point group is 2'/m' with c (= [001]o) as the unique axis. That group allows a Hall pseudovector anywhere in the ab plane, including along [110]o, the film normal. The DFT agrees: the orthorhombic supercell with beta_LC = 90.00 deg already gives a finite sigma_DFT of about 4 S/cm (Fig. 4i). The tCRO = 0 single LCMO film also shows a nonzero signal (Fig. 4a). So mirror-symmetry breaking by the heterointerfaces (page 6; Supplementary Fig. 7, Tables 1-2) is not the activating condition. At most, beta_LC changes the magnitude. The authors should:
   - redo the symmetry analysis using magnetic point groups for bulk Pbnm LCMO in the (110)o orientation and for the P2_1/m trilayer;
   - state plainly which Hall components each group allows;
   - revise the "unambiguously couples to CaRuO3-buffer-induced mirror-symmetry breaking" claims in the abstract and Conclusion.

3. Parallel conduction through the metallic CRO layers, and a possible Hall contribution from those layers, are not treated. Each CRO buffer is 16 u.c. (about 6 nm) thick, comparable to the 32 u.c. LCMO layer, and CRO is metallic. The conductivity sigma_AHE = rho_AHE / rho_xx^2 assumes a single homogeneous conductor. In a parallel stack, a growing CRO thickness by itself redistributes current and changes the apparent sigma_AHE. The decrease at tCRO = 32 and 64 u.c. (Fig. 4f) is what shunting by thick CRO would produce. In addition, CRO/LCMO interfaces are known to carry induced Ru moments (Refs. 30-32), and CRO has stronger SOC. An interfacial ruthenate Hall contribution is a strong candidate source of the signal. The sign reversal near 100 K (Fig. 4h inset), which the authors themselves compare to SrRuO3, points the same way. The authors should:
   - give rho_xx(T) for every tCRO, and for CRO-only reference films;
   - model the trilayer as parallel channels to extract the LCMO-layer Hall conductivity;
   - measure an in-plane Hall control in a CRO-only film and in an LCMO film with a non-ruthenate buffer of similar octahedral tilt (or a different buffer that changes beta_LC without a conducting, high-SOC layer);
   - discuss whether the signal lives in LCMO, CRO, or the interfaces.

4. The gating experiment contradicts the proposed mechanism rather than supporting it. The XRD under gating shows the CRO(440) peak shifting while "the LCMO(440)o peak position remains nearly unchanged" (page 11, Supplementary Fig. 24). Yet sigma_AHE drops to zero between 1.1 and 1.4 V (Fig. 5c,d). If the IP-AHE is set by the LCMO distortion beta_LC, the paper must show that beta_LC itself changes under gating. The only evidence offered is DFT for bulk HxCaRuO3 and a schematic inset (Fig. 5d), with no measured beta_LC versus Vg. The simpler reading is that the signal is carried by the CRO layers or interfaces, which are the layers being protonated (see concern 3). Protonated CaRuO3 is also reported to become ferromagnetic (Ref. 48), which changes the magnetic and transport landscape. The authors should:
   - measure RSMs (or at least the LCMO orthorhombicity) at Vg = 0 and 1.4 V and extract beta_LC;
   - report rho_xx and M_s versus Vg;
   - gate an LCMO film without CRO under the same conditions as a control;
   - confirm where the protons sit (e.g., SIMS depth profile, or neutron reflectometry if available).

B. Should address

5. The correlation between beta_LC and sigma_AHE across tCRO (Fig. 4f,g) is used as causal evidence, but other quantities vary with tCRO as well. The authors themselves note that T_C and M_s increase with tCRO (page 5, Supplementary Fig. 4), and that charge transfer occurs at the interfaces. Carrier density, rho_xx, twin-domain fraction and strain relaxation also change. The authors should:
   - tabulate M_s, T_C, rho_xx, the twin fraction and beta_LC for every tCRO;
   - normalize the Hall signal by M_s (or show the anomalous Hall angle);
   - show that beta_LC predicts sigma_AHE better than these alternatives.
   The non-monotonic trend rests on a single peak point at 16 u.c. with only two points beyond it, so a second sample at 16 u.c., and ideally at 24 u.c., is needed.

6. The paper does not show that the response is intrinsic. Equation (1) and the DFT comparison assume an intrinsic Berry-curvature mechanism, but no scaling analysis is presented. The authors should plot sigma_AHE against sigma_xx using the temperature data (Fig. 4h, Supplementary Fig. 18) and across samples, and comment on the skew-scattering and side-jump regimes. The sign changes near 100 K for some tCRO need an explanation in this framework.

7. The DFT comparison is not quantitative and its parameters are arbitrary.
   - sigma_DFT is about 4-9 S/cm (Fig. 4i), while the measured value is at most about 0.1 S/cm (103.7 mS/cm), roughly a factor of 50-100 smaller. This gap is not discussed.
   - U = 8 eV, J = 2 eV on Mn 3d is unusually large. VCA for Ca, a 2x2x2 cell, and Wannierization of Mn 3d states only are approximations whose effect on a Berry-curvature quantity can be large.
   - How the beta_LC = 90.08 and 90.16 deg structures were built is unclear: which OOR and JT amplitudes were imposed, and whether they were relaxed. Varying OOR and JT amplitudes together does not isolate beta_LC.
   - The calculation is for bulk LCMO without the CRO layers or interfaces.
   The authors should show the sensitivity of sigma_DFT to U (for example 3-8 eV), the Fermi-level position, and the Wannier window. They should vary beta_LC at fixed OOR and JT amplitudes, and discuss the magnitude mismatch.

8. The extraction of beta_LC and its uncertainty need detail. beta_LC - 90 deg ranges from 0.03 to 0.2 deg (Fig. 4g), and the error bars are about 0.01 deg. beta_LC is called "layer-averaged" and computed from d_OR extracted from RSMs of a 12 nm layer, which sits next to CRO peaks and close to the NGO peak (Fig. 2a-d). The authors should:
   - state the fitting procedure, the peak-overlap handling and how the error bars were obtained;
   - reconcile these numbers with the GPA maps. Fig. 2g uses a +/-10 deg colour scale, so it is unclear whether a 0.1-0.2 deg difference can be resolved there, or "directly visible" in Fig. 2i as claimed.

9. Hall data processing and the number of samples and devices are not stated.
   - Was rho_xy antisymmetrized in field (or M) to remove AMR and planar-Hall admixtures from contact misalignment, or only linearly background-subtracted? For in-plane fields, the origin of a linear Hall background also needs explaining, since an ideal in-plane field gives no ordinary Hall voltage.
   - How many samples and Hall bars were measured at each tCRO? The phi-dependence (Fig. 3d,e) relies on separate Hall bars, so device-to-device spread must be shown to support "independent of phi".
   - Is the IP-AHE reproduced in a second 16 u.c. sample?

10. The reversibility and stability of the ON/OFF switching (Supplementary Fig. 23) are not quantified in the main text. Report:
   - the number of cycles;
   - the recovery fraction of sigma_AHE and rho_xx;
   - the temperature at which gating was done relative to the 10 K measurement;
   - whether the LCMO is protonated at 1.4 V.
   The literature threshold of above 2.5 V for manganites is used as an argument, but it is not measured on these samples.

C. Minor

11. Fig. 4h y-axis units are inconsistent with Fig. 4f. Fig. 4h is labelled in 10^-2 S cm^-1 and reaches about -100, which is about 1 S/cm, whereas Fig. 4f and the text give at most 103.7 mS/cm, about 0.1 S/cm. One of the two is off by a factor of 10.

12. The definition of theta differs between Fig. 3 and Fig. 4k. In Fig. 3, theta = 0 means M along [-110]o (maximum signal). In Fig. 4k, theta = 0 gives sigma_DFT = 0 and theta = 90 deg gives the maximum, although the caption defines theta as the angle from [-110]o. Please make them consistent.

13. Field value in Fig. 3c: the caption says rho_AHE at H = 1000 Oe, while the text (page 8) says H = 2000 Oe. Because H_s is about 2000 Oe along the hard axis, this changes the interpretation. Below H_s, M is not along H, so cos(theta) is not simply the projection of a saturated M. The phrase "residual M component pinned to the [-110]o easy axis" also needs clarifying.

14. The Fig. 3d caption says "various omega values"; it should be phi. In Fig. 4g, the caption says "90 deg - beta_LC" while the axis label and text say "beta_LC - 90 deg". The Fig. 4f axis is inverted (negative values upward) without comment.

15. The Fig. 2e caption defines beta as the angle between the (110)o and (1-10)o planes, while the text describes it as the tilt of c_p toward a_p. Please give one geometric definition and relate it to d_OR explicitly (the formula behind Refs. 35, 40, 41).

16. The statement that negligible magnetocrystalline anisotropy in LCMO implies a demagnetization- and magnetoelastic-dominated anisotropy (page 5) is qualitative. Demagnetization does not distinguish [001]o from [-110]o in-plane, so the in-plane uniaxial anisotropy must come from the strain term. Please rephrase.

17. The Data availability statement is incomplete ("available from" with no repository). The Conclusion begins with a lowercase "we".

18. The Methods use the cubic VCA cell "reoriented" from a "tetragonal unit-cell". Please clarify the parent structure, the lattice constants fixed to NGO, and whether the in-plane lattice was constrained to the substrate during relaxation.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a quantitative exclusion of out-of-plane-canting AHE: the conventional AHE at saturation, plus a measured m_z when M lies along [-110]o. This is the single most important addition.
2. Add reference samples that isolate where the signal comes from: CRO-only films, LCMO with no buffer and with a non-ruthenate buffer of comparable octahedral tilt, and LCMO on NGO(001) or other orientations where the symmetry forbids the response. Pair these with a parallel-channel analysis of the trilayer conductivity.
3. Measure beta_LC directly under gating, and show rho_xx, M_s and beta_LC versus Vg alongside sigma_AHE. This would make the electrical-control story support the structural mechanism rather than undercut it.
4. Recast the symmetry discussion in terms of magnetic point groups for each candidate structure. Present the DFT as a trend study with a stated U dependence and an explicit discussion of the roughly 50-100 times magnitude mismatch.
