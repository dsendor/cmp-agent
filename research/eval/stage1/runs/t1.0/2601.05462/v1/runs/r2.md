Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports an in-plane anomalous Hall effect (IP-AHE) in CaRuO3/La2/3Ca1/3MnO3/CaRuO3 (CRO/LCMO/CRO) trilayers on NdGaO3(110)O. The Hall signal follows a square in-plane hysteresis loop with M along [-110]O. The authors attribute it to mirror-symmetry breaking in LCMO induced by the CRO buffers, tune it with CRO thickness, and switch it off with ionic-liquid gating. An oxide platform with a robust, zero-field, hysteretic IP-AHE that can be tuned by the structure and by a gate would interest both the Berry-curvature community and the spintronics community. The central attribution, however, rests on a very small Hall angle (~10^-5). Ordinary out-of-plane AHE from a slightly tilted magnetization has not yet been excluded. The symmetry argument that the CRO-induced monoclinic distortion is *required* also conflicts with the authors' own DFT.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A clean, square, zero-field Hall hysteresis that tracks the in-plane M-H loop along [-110]O (Fig. 1d,g). It is odd in M and so cannot come from the planar Hall effect or AMR for this E//M configuration.
- A systematic dataset: field-angle (Fig. 3b,c) and current-angle (Fig. 3d,e) dependences, a CRO-thickness series (Fig. 4a–g) and temperature dependences (Fig. 4h).
- Detailed structural work (RSM, HAADF/ABF-STEM, GPA) on the octahedral coupling at the CRO/LCMO interfaces.
- Electrical ON/OFF control of the Hall signal by gating while H_C stays unchanged (Fig. 5c,d), which would be a useful functionality if its mechanism holds.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **A conventional AHE from a small out-of-plane M component is not excluded, and the out-of-plane control is invalid as performed.**
   - *Where.* Fig. 1f is offered as showing that the conventional AHE is "negligible" for H⊥(110)O. But it covers only ±2 kOe. Fig. 1c shows M reaches only ~0.7 μB/Mn at 2 kOe, against ~3.5 μB/Mn in plane. The out-of-plane magnetization is far from saturated (demagnetizing field 4πMs ≈ 6–8 kOe for LCMO).
   - *The problem.* In this regime M_z is linear in H. The AHE ∝ M_z is therefore also linear, and the procedure of "subtract[ing] the linear component" removes it together with the ordinary Hall term. Fig. 1f thus says nothing about the size of the conventional anomalous Hall coefficient of this stack.
   - *Why it matters.* The observed signal is tiny: ρAHE ≈ 4.7 nΩ·cm, and σAHE ≈ 0.10 S/cm with ρxx ≈ 200 μΩ·cm inferred from the paper's own numbers. LCMO-based films typically show conventional σAHE of order 1–10 S/cm or more. An out-of-plane tilt of M of order 0.1–1° would then reproduce the signal. A tilt of this size could come from substrate miscut, or from the easy axis following the monoclinically tilted c_p axis (β_LC − 90° ≈ 0.03–0.2°, Fig. 4g).
   - *Why the other tests do not settle it.* A tilted easy axis would also give a square loop, a cos θ dependence (Fig. 3c), independence of φ (Fig. 3e), and a correlation with β_LC (Fig. 4f,g). None of these tests separates IP-AHE from conventional AHE driven by M_z ∝ M_[-110]. The rotation in the (001)O plane is relegated to Supplementary Figs. 15 and 16 and cannot be judged from the main text.
   - *Requested.*
     - Measure ρxy for H⊥(110)O up to 7 T, well beyond out-of-plane saturation. Extract the anomalous Hall coefficient R_s (or σAHE^z at full M_z) for the same trilayers.
     - From this, state the canting angle of M out of plane that would be needed to give the observed signal.
     - Bound the actual canting independently: for example, polar MOKE or out-of-plane SQUID remanence after in-plane saturation, or XMCD/PNR. Report the substrate miscut angle and direction.
     - Ideally, compare samples with miscut along versus perpendicular to [-110]O, or do the analysis of Supp. Figs. 15–16 in the main text with explicit fits that separate the two contributions.

2. **The symmetry argument that CRO-induced monoclinic distortion is needed is contradicted by the paper's own DFT, so the claim of "symmetry engineering" is not supported.**
   - *Where.* Pages 7–8 argue that the interfaces lower LCMO to P2₁/m (C2h). Only then does M//[-110]O break the remaining mirror and activate Ω_z.
   - *The contradiction.* Figs. 4i,j show σDFT finite for the orthorhombic supercell with β_LC = 90.00°, and zero only for the cubic cell without OOR. Bulk Pbnm with M along the non-principal direction [1-10]O already breaks the relevant mirrors (M_(100), M_(010)). The magnetic point group with M//[1-10] retains 2_z·T, which permits a Hall pseudovector along [110]O. So IP-AHE is symmetry-allowed without any monoclinic distortion. The tCRO = 0 sample (LCMO/NGO) also shows a finite σAHE (Fig. 4a,f).
   - *Consequence.* The "one-to-one correspondence" between the CRO-induced mirror-symmetry breaking and IP-AHE (Abstract; p. 8: "unambiguously couples") does not follow. β_LC may well correlate with the OOR amplitude, but the monoclinic angle is not the symmetry switch.
   - *Requested.* Give a complete magnetic-point-group analysis for Pbnm and for P2₁/m with M along [-110]O, [001]O and [110]O. State which σ_ij components are allowed in each case, and recast the claim as tuning of magnitude rather than symmetry activation.

3. **The tCRO dependence (Fig. 4f,g) is the key evidence for tunability, but its normalization, statistics and alternatives are not established.**
   - *Normalization and shunting.* σAHE = ρAHE/ρxx² treats the whole trilayer as one conductor. The total thickness changes from 32 to 160 u.c. across the series, and CRO is itself a good metal. The paper should state which thickness defines ρ, and show raw R_xy and R_xx for each sample. Shunting by the CRO layers, and the varying fraction of current in LCMO, could alone produce a non-monotonic σAHE(tCRO).
   - *Statistics.* Each tCRO appears to be a single sample, with no error bars on σAHE. The peak value (103.7 mS/cm at 16 u.c.) is 3× the 8 u.c. value and 20× the 32 u.c. value. Please show device-to-device and sample-to-sample spread for at least the 8, 16 and 32 u.c. samples.
   - *Weak correlation.* β_LC − 90° differs by only ~0.05° between 8 and 16 u.c., yet σAHE changes 3×. Meanwhile 4 u.c. (β−90° ≈ 0.13°) and 32 u.c. (≈ 0.08°) differ by a factor of ~4 in σ. Plot σAHE against β_LC directly, with uncertainties.
   - *Evidence for the explanation.* The explanation for the decrease above 16 u.c. (strain relaxation and a/b twin domains) is asserted, not shown. RSM/STEM evidence of twinning and relaxation for the 32 and 64 u.c. samples is needed. Note that twinning would also cut the CRO-imposed axis-to-axis relation and the uniaxial anisotropy.

4. **Contributions from the CRO layers and the interfaces are not separated from LCMO.**
   - *The alternative.* Interfacial charge transfer Ru⁴⁺→Mn⁴⁺ is invoked to explain the higher T_C (p. 5). This implies interfacial Ru moments with strong SOC. Ru-derived bands are known to give large, sign-changing Berry-curvature AHE, as in SrRuO3.
   - *The authors' own data point this way.* The sign reversal near 100 K (Fig. 4h inset) is compared to SRO. This points to a Ru-derived or interfacial contribution rather than an intrinsic LCMO response, and the DFT models LCMO only.
   - *Requested controls.*
     - A CRO/LCMO bilayer (only one interface), and LCMO capped with a non-magnetic, weak-SOC buffer that imposes similar octahedral tilts.
     - Element-specific magnetism at the interface (Ru/Mn XMCD).
     - A thickness series in t_LCMO at fixed tCRO, to test whether σAHE scales as a bulk LCMO quantity or an interface quantity.

5. **The gating results do not establish that IP-AHE is switched through a structural symmetry change in LCMO.**
   - *Missing structural evidence.* The only structural evidence is a CRO(440) peak shift with an unchanged LCMO(440) position (Supp. Fig. 24). No measurement of β_LC or of the LCMO octahedral tilts after gating is shown. The inset of Fig. 5d is a schematic. The proposed suppression of OOR in LCMO comes from DFT alone (Supp. Fig. 27).
   - *Competing explanations.* Ref. 48 itself reports emergent ferromagnetism in HxCaRuO3, so gating may add a ferromagnetic layer with its own (possibly opposite-sign) AHE. It may also change the interfacial charge transfer, the carrier density, and the current distribution between layers.
   - *Requested.* Show ρxx(V_g) and M(V_g) (SQUID, not just H_C), together with RSM or β_LC after gating. Show the switching for multiple cycles with full loops, and report the gate leakage current. Also explain how protons reach the bottom CRO layer through LCMO when manganite protonation needs >2.5 V (p. 10), and whether the top CRO alone is responsible.

**B. Should address**

6. **The DFT comparison is not quantitative.** σDFT ≈ 4–9 S/cm (Fig. 4i) is about two orders of magnitude larger than the measured ~0.1 S/cm.
   - The β_LC values modeled (90.00–90.16°) are chosen to match experiment, but the OOR and Jahn-Teller amplitudes that produce them are not given.
   - U = 8 eV, J = 2 eV on Mn 3d is unusually large. VCA for La/Ca is used, and the Wannier model projects only Mn 3d states.
   - Please state how sensitive σDFT is to U, to including O 2p in the Wannier basis, and to k-mesh convergence of the Berry-curvature integral; the 7×7×7 mesh quoted is for the band structure. Also say whether the trend with β_LC survives at fixed OOR amplitude.
   - The DFT also says nothing about the CRO layers (see concern 4).

7. **Fig. 4k contradicts the text and caption.** The caption defines θ as the angle between M and [-110]O. σDFT vanishes at θ = 0 and is largest at θ = 90°. This is opposite to the experiment and to the text, where the IP-AHE is largest for M//[-110]O. The drawn axes suggest θ is measured from [001]O. Please correct the definition. The text calls the 90.16° supercell the one with largest σ; Fig. 4k is labeled β_LC = 90.16° and its θ = 90° value matches that bar, so the convention needs stating unambiguously.

8. **Units in Fig. 4h are inconsistent with Fig. 4a–f.** Fig. 4h is labeled σAHE in 10⁻² S·cm⁻¹ and reaches −100 for 16 u.c. at 10 K, which is −1 S/cm. Figs. 4d,f and the text give ~103.7 mS/cm, which is 0.1 S/cm. Either the axis unit or the data are off by a factor of 10.

9. **The angular dependence in Fig. 3 is taken below saturation and does not test the "dipolar Ω" claim.**
   - Fig. 3c uses ρAHE at H = 1000 Oe, but the text quotes "saturated ρAHE at H = 2000 Oe". At 1000 Oe M is not along H for most θ (Hs ≈ 2000 Oe along [001]O). The cosine therefore reflects the projection of the pinned M onto [-110]O, which any M_[-110]-odd mechanism would give (see concern 1).
   - The text also says ρAHE "decreases gradually to zero" at θ = 90° and then that it is "finite for 0° < θ ≤ 90°".
   - Please measure at fields large enough to saturate M along H, or plot ρAHE against the measured M_[-110](θ), and fix the inconsistent statements.

10. **The Hall analysis procedure is under-specified.**
    - Was ρxy antisymmetrized in H (or in M) to remove the contact-misalignment ρxx and planar-Hall contributions? This matters especially for Fig. 1h, where M rotates away from E and the planar Hall term ∝ sin 2θ_M becomes nonzero during the loop.
    - The linear background in Figs. 1g and 3d is fitted to "H > Hs" over a window of only ±0.4–1 kOe. State the fit range and the slope obtained, and compare with the ordinary Hall coefficient.

11. **Temperature dependence versus T_C.** T_C ≥ 250 K, yet σAHE is reported only up to 200 K, and its sign changes near 100 K in several samples.
    - Please extend Fig. 4h to T_C and show M_[-110](T) alongside.
    - A sign change with M unchanged in sign argues for at least two competing contributions. Discuss which one the symmetry/β_LC picture accounts for.
    - Clarify whether the sign changes relative to M, given that the loop in Fig. 1g is already "inverted" relative to Fig. 1d.

12. **The structural resolution for β_LC needs justification.** β_LC − 90° spans 0.03–0.2° with error bars of ~0.01° (Fig. 4g), extracted from layer-averaged RSM of 32 u.c. LCMO sandwiched between layers with opposite tilts.
    - Describe the fitting procedure and how peak overlap among NGO, CRO and LCMO was handled.
    - Explain how the GPA (Fig. 2h, color scale ±0.5°, featureless) resolves a 0.2° tilt.
    - Fig. 2g shows ω_(100)p on a ±10° scale with features only at the interfaces, which seems at odds with the claimed uniform opposite tilting across each layer.

**C. Minor**

13. Fig. 3 caption: "(d) … for various ω values" should read φ.
14. H_C is quoted as ~200 Oe (p. 5), but the 16 u.c. loops in Figs. 4d and 5c show ~250–300 Oe. Please clarify whether these are different samples or devices.
15. Fig. 4f plots negative σAHE on an inverted axis while Figs. 4a–e show loops of both signs. State the sign convention (relative to M and to the Hall-voltage definition in Fig. 1b) once and apply it consistently.
16. The data availability statement is incomplete ("available from").
17. The Methods state that the CRO layers have thickness t_CRO each, but do not say whether the top and bottom CRO were nominally identical in every sample, or give the actual measured thicknesses.
18. The conclusion begins with lowercase "we". "Unambiguously" (Abstract) and "faithfully" overstate the evidence given concerns 1–2.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a full-field out-of-plane Hall measurement and an independent bound on the out-of-plane magnetization tilt. Report the canting angle that would be needed to explain the signal as conventional AHE. This is the single most important addition.
2. Recast the symmetry discussion with a full magnetic-point-group analysis of Pbnm versus P2₁/m. Present β_LC as a knob for magnitude rather than an on/off symmetry switch, consistent with Fig. 4i.
3. Add controls that separate interfacial or Ru contributions from intrinsic LCMO: a bilayer, a different buffer, a t_LCMO series, and element-resolved XMCD.
4. For the gating, add structural (RSM) and magnetic (SQUID) characterization after gating, ρxx(V_g), and cycling data to tie the ON/OFF switching to a change in LCMO structure.
