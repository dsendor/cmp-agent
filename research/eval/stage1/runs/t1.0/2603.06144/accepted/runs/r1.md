Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use circularly polarized RIXS at the O K edge (531.25 eV) on an 80 nm free-standing BaTiO3 membrane fitted with top and bottom electrodes. They report a circular-dichroic (CD) contrast in the phonon region (0 to 0.1 eV) that reverses sign when the ferroelectric polarization is reversed (±3.5 V write, ±0.2 V hold), and they interpret this as electric-field switching of phonon angular momentum. They also report an azimuthal (q) dependence across four momentum transfers and compare it with the DFT helicity J·e_q of three modes (11, 38 and 100 meV). If it holds, this would be the first deterministic, electrically controlled reversal of phonon chirality at general q, which makes it of broad interest. The present data, however, support the central claims less firmly than the abstract ("quantitative agreement", "non-volatile", "g-wave") states.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The concept is clean and well motivated. The m(001) mirror relates the two polarization states, so reversing P should reverse the PAM at fixed q, and in-situ switching of a polar order parameter is a natural, previously untested way to check that chiral-phonon CD has a structural origin.
- The experimental design has several good internal checks. The C+ and C- field-switching traces in Fig. 3 (middle) are approximately mirror images, the F+ and F- CD traces in Fig. 3 (bottom) are approximately mirror images, and the '+ − − +' sequence is meant to cancel linear drift.
- The q points q2 and q4 form a mirror pair across the b* axis. The sample is rotated about the surface normal while the beam stays along c, so the lab geometry stays the same while the crystal orientation changes. q3 (φ = π/2) lies on a mirror plane and should give zero contrast. This is a sensible symmetry test (Fig. 4f–h, Table 1).
- Device fabrication on a free-standing membrane, with a top electrode thin enough to transmit X-rays, is a non-trivial technical achievement.
- The data and DFT outputs are deposited.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Field-induced effects other than phonon chirality are not excluded by any null measurement. The headline claim is that the CD that switches with the field "must arise from the phononic subsystem" (p. 3). That conclusion rests only on symmetry arguments, not on controls. Reversing the field also changes several things that are not chiral: piezoelectric strain and therefore membrane height and tilt under the 50 µm spot; the hold field itself (±0.2 V over 80 nm, about 25 kV/cm, which acts on BTO's very large electro-optic coefficients); interfacial charge and oxygen-vacancy distribution under the Cr contact; and the elastic-line intensity. The authors themselves note that birefringence produces strong CD that masks the phonon signal (Supplementary Fig. 8). At exact normal incidence along c it vanishes, but a free-standing membrane on a patterned electrode is unlikely to be perfectly flat, so local tilts give a finite in-plane projection. That projection rotates with φ and could produce a φ-dependent CD. The paper should show, under matched conditions:
   (a) the field-switching difference measured with linear (π or σ) polarization, which should be zero within error;
   (b) the field-switching and CD contrast integrated over the elastic line (−20 to +10 meV), as a quantitative null;
   (c) the field-independent part of the CD, [CD(F+) + CD(F−)]/2, which should vanish if all the CD comes from P;
   (d) ideally, the same protocol either above the Curie temperature (about 120 °C, where the CD should vanish and stop switching) or on a non-polar reference membrane, such as SrTiO3, with the same electrodes.
   Without at least (a) to (c), the attribution to chiral phonons is not secure.

2. The polarization state of the illuminated volume during RIXS is never measured.
   - Ferroelectricity was confirmed by PFM before device fabrication, with an Au tip on an Au bottom electrode (Fig. 1c, Methods). The finished device, with a Cr/Au top pad under X-ray illumination, is never characterized during the experiment.
   - The PFM loop is imprinted (coercive voltages of roughly −2 V and +2.6 V). The write voltage of 3.5 V sits exactly at the stated onset of degradation (Supplementary Fig. 5). The hold voltage of 0.2 V may not stabilize the state against X-ray-induced photocarriers.
   - The pad is 60 µm in diameter and the beam footprint is 50 µm along its long axis, so part of the signal may come from unswitched regions near the pad edge.
   - The abstract calls the effect "non-volatile", but every RIXS measurement was taken with a non-zero hold bias of the same sign as the write pulse, so remanence is not demonstrated.

   The authors should provide in-situ evidence of switching at the beamline. Options include switching-current or P–E loops recorded before and after each RIXS block, a structural or spectroscopic probe of polarization sign (for example, the Friedel-pair intensity asymmetry of a (00L) reflection, or SHG on the device), or a demonstration that the contrast persists at zero hold bias. They should also state how many devices and membranes were measured and whether the result was reproduced on more than one.

3. The comparison with DFT is not quantitative, contrary to the abstract.
   - In Fig. 4f–h each panel has its own free vertical scale relating "CD contrast (a.u.)" to J·e_q, and each region rests on only four points.
   - Given the mirror symmetry, any contrast that is odd under x → −x will cross zero at φ = π/2 and have opposite signs at q2 and q4. The agreement in sign and zero crossing therefore tests symmetry, not the DFT eigenvectors.
   - Where the data do test the calculation, the agreement is weak. In Fig. 4f the q1 point (φ ≈ 2.1) lies at about 0.4 ± 0.4, against a calculated value near 1.9. This contradicts the statement on p. 5 that the "CD contrast is largest near q1".
   - Magnitudes are not consistent across modes. The calculated J·e_q for the 100 meV mode (Fig. 4e, h: about 0.002) is roughly 50 times smaller than for the 11 meV mode (about 0.1), yet the measured contrasts in regions I and III differ by only a factor of about 4.

   The authors should do three things:
   - fit all regions with a single global scale factor, or with scale factors fixed by the calculated mode effective charges (Supplementary Section 4), and report χ²;
   - compare this fit with a symmetry-only model, for example a sin-type φ dependence with free amplitude;
   - compute a CD-RIXS spectrum convolved with the instrumental resolution, from the DFT helicities and electron–phonon weights, and overlay it on Fig. 3.

   Otherwise, "quantitative agreement" should be reworded as "consistent in sign and symmetry".

4. The mode assignment is finer than the energy resolution.
   - The stated resolution is about 30 meV FWHM (Methods). Yet the CD and field-switching traces in Fig. 3 show alternating positive and negative lobes about 15 to 20 meV wide, and these are attributed to individual modes at 11 and 38 meV.
   - Region I (0 to about 40 meV, Fig. 4a) overlaps the tail of the elastic line, which dominates the intensity.
   - The traces in Figs. 3 and 4a are Savitzky–Golay smoothed. Smoothing a noisy difference signal can create oscillations on the scale of the smoothing window.
   - The text states that BTO phonons end at about 100 meV and that features above this are multiphonon. The 100 meV feature (region III) sits on that boundary, next to a strong peak in the FY spectrum near 0.1 eV, so it may be multiphonon or partly elastic-tail and background in origin.
   - The 15 meV high-resolution data are used only to identify phonon energies, not to test the switching.

   The authors should:
   - show unsmoothed field-switching and CD data next to the smoothed traces;
   - fix the integration windows before analysis, based on the DFT dispersion convolved with the resolution, and state how robust the integrated contrasts are to the window edges;
   - if possible, show the field switching at 15 meV resolution, at least for one q;
   - justify assigning region III to a one-phonon mode.

5. The g-wave symmetry in the title is not established by the measured q range. All four q points lie between φ ≈ 1.40 and 2.11 rad (Table 1, Fig. 4f–h), a span of about 40° that contains a single nodal plane (φ = π/2). Many angular forms are odd under the mirror through b* and vanish at π/2, including p-wave and d-wave forms as well as g-wave. A g-wave (C4v, eight-sector) pattern requires further sign changes at the diagonal mirrors, φ = π/4 and 3π/4 (2.36 rad), which lie just beyond q1. The authors should add points across a diagonal mirror, for example φ ≈ 2.5 to 2.7, or in another quadrant, and show that the sign changes there. Otherwise they should restate the claim as consistency with, rather than demonstration of, g-wave symmetry, and adjust the title and abstract.

B. Should address

6. Error bars and statistical significance are unclear.
   - The caption of Fig. 3 says the error bars are s.d. "reflecting Poisson counting statistics". Methods says they are "the standard deviation of individual scans". These are different quantities, and for n = 16 the standard error of the mean differs from the s.d. by a factor of 4.
   - For Fig. 4f–h, the method of integrating and propagating errors within each window is not stated.

   The authors should:
   - state exactly what each error bar is;
   - give, for each region and q, the integrated switching contrast with its uncertainty and significance against zero;
   - show the integrated contrast as a time series over successive field reversals. A sign that alternates with every reversal and has no monotonic drift would directly support reversible switching and rule out beam damage or slow electromigration.

7. The domain structure and morphology of the membrane are not shown.
   - Free-standing 80 nm BTO membranes often contain 90° (a-) domains and wrinkles. Either would break the assumed geometry (c along the beam) and mix in in-plane polarization components that the 4mm picture of Fig. 1a does not include.
   - The text says "film orientation was confirmed by non-specular X-ray diffraction", but the tetragonality, the a-domain fraction, the rocking-curve width (that is, the tilt distribution under the spot) and lateral PFM are not reported in the main text.
   - The text also raises the 278 K orthorhombic transition and shear strain as a risk, without showing that the membrane is single-phase tetragonal at 295 K.

   These data should be given. The magnitude of the CD should be discussed in light of any a-domain fraction.

8. Oxygen sources other than the BTO lattice are not addressed.
   - The O K-edge signal passes through a 4 nm Cr / 8 nm Au top electrode. Cr in contact with BTO, or exposed at pad edges, is likely to be partly oxidized, and the device also contains photoresist residues, carbon tape and possibly adsorbates.
   - Any of these contributes to the O K-edge RIXS and XAS. A field-dependent redox reaction at the Cr/BTO interface could also produce a field-dependent, polarization-dependent spectral change.
   - The XAS used for calibration (Supplementary Fig. 6) should be compared with reference BTO.

   The authors should estimate the fraction of signal coming from non-BTO oxygen and show that the XAS does not change between F+ and F−.

9. The DFT model's assumptions need justification.
   - In harmonic DFT (PBE), tetragonal BaTiO3 is dynamically unstable toward the orthorhombic and rhombohedral phases. Imaginary or strongly anharmonic soft modes are expected, particularly in the low-energy branch relevant to region I (11 meV).
   - The paper does not say whether the calculated dispersion (Supplementary Fig. 11) has unstable branches or how they were treated.
   - The relaxed c = 4.11 Å overestimates the experimental 4.03 Å by 2%. This overestimates the Ti off-centring and therefore the size of the PAM.
   - The "internal pressure of 70 kPa" is implausibly small for changing lattice constants and is probably a units error.
   - It should be stated whether J in Eq. 2 and in Fig. 4 is the oxygen-only or the total-cell angular momentum, and why one rather than the other is appropriate for the O K-edge contrast.

10. Some interpretive statements go beyond the data.
   - The statement that the contrast "inverts upon measuring along an oppositely oriented q" (p. 5) is not what was measured. Only mirror-related in-plane components (q2 and q4) were compared, and q_z was the same for all points.
   - The Discussion's mention of controlling the "relative populations of right- and left-handed phonons" conflates reversal of eigenmode handedness with changes in thermal population. The latter is not measured and would not be expected at equilibrium.
   - The gyroelectric analogy (Eq. 4) is not made quantitative: no γ coefficient or bound is extracted.

   These statements should be tightened.

C. Minor

11. Fig. 3: the legend says "Gaussian fit" for the elastic line, but the text and Methods describe a half-Lorentzian fit to the energy-gain side. Please reconcile these. Please also state the vertical offset applied to the F+ and F− spectra in the top panel.

12. Methods: "hexagonal lattice constants" should read tetragonal. "−30 (15) meV" should read 30 (15) meV. The ESRF dataset (ref. 56) is dated 2028. On p. 3, "comparable to the that seen" contains a typo.

13. Fig. 4a: the legend has pink shading for q4, a dotted line for q2 and green for another q, while the caption says the shaded regions are the phonon windows I to III. Please make the mapping of lines and shading to q points and to regions unambiguous. Please also state how the "a.u." in Fig. 4f–h relates to the "a.u." in Fig. 3.

14. Fig. 4c–e: please mark the φ values of q1 to q4 on the colour maps and show the q_z-integration arc for all panels, not only e. The yellow arc in e is described in the caption but not referred to in panels c and d.

15. Fig. 1c: please state on which sample and electrode configuration the PFM loop was taken, and comment on the imprint, given that the write and hold voltages are symmetric.

16. Several conclusions depend on Supplementary Figs. 8, 10, 11, 15 and 17 (birefringence, switching protocol, dispersion, polarization vectors, convolution). Key elements of these, especially the birefringence demonstration and the weighted convolution in Fig. S17, should be summarized in the main text or in Extended Data.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a compact control figure. It should show, at one q: field switching with linear polarization; field switching integrated over the elastic line; the field-independent CD component; and the integrated contrast versus field-cycle number. If feasible, add a measurement above T_C or on a non-polar SrTiO3 membrane device. This would turn the symmetry argument into an experimental demonstration.
2. Extend the azimuthal scan across a diagonal mirror plane (φ ≈ 2.4 to 2.8 rad), and fit all three regions with one global scale factor tied to the calculated mode effective charges. This would directly test both the g-wave claim and the DFT eigenvectors.
3. Show a simulated CD-RIXS spectrum, convolved with the resolution, from the DFT helicities and electron–phonon weights, overlaid on the measured Fig. 3 traces. Integration windows should be defined from this simulation rather than from the data.
4. Demonstrate non-volatility by measuring the contrast at zero hold bias after the write pulse, and by checking the polarization state in situ, for example with switching currents recorded on the beamline.
