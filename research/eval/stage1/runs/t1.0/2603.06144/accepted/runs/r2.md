Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports circular-dichroic RIXS (CD-RIXS) at the O K edge on an 80 nm free-standing BaTiO3 membrane placed in a capacitor, and finds a momentum-dependent dichroic signal in the 0–100 meV phonon range that changes sign when the ferroelectric polarization is reversed. The authors read this as electric-field switching of phonon angular momentum (PAM) with g-wave (C4v) symmetry in reciprocal space, and compare it with DFT chiral projections J·e_q. Electrically switching phonon chirality in a technologically relevant ferroelectric would be a significant and timely result. It would extend earlier CD-RIXS work on chiral quartz and polar LiNbO3 from static to switchable chirality. The novelty is clear. The question is whether the evidence is strong enough for "deterministic", "non-volatile", "robust" and "in quantitative agreement with first-principles calculations".

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A new and well-chosen experimental idea: a single-domain, out-of-plane-polarized membrane in a capacitor lets the inversion-breaking order parameter be reversed in situ while the CD-RIXS geometry stays fixed. This is in principle a cleaner test of PAM than comparing different crystals or enantiomers.
- The measurement design is sensible. Both helicities are measured for both field states in an alternating "+ − − +" sequence to suppress drifts (Methods). Normal incidence along c is used to reduce birefringence. The O K-edge energy was refined to maximize the phonon response (Supplementary Fig. 7).
- The sign change between the near-mirror pair q2 = (−0.05, 0.23, 0.42) and q4 = (0.04, 0.23, 0.42) Å⁻¹ (Fig. 4a) is the kind of symmetry test the g-wave picture predicts.
- DFT phonon eigenvectors give a concrete, falsifiable prediction for the φ dependence of the chiral projection (Fig. 4c–h).
- Data and computational results are deposited.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **Polarization reversal is not separated from other field-odd effects of the bias protocol.** In each field configuration the sample is written at ±3.5 V and then held at ±0.2 V during measurement (Methods, "Ferroelectric switching in RIXS"; Fig. 3 caption, F± = ±0.2 V). So the sign of the applied DC bias flips together with the polarization. A field-odd change in the RIXS spectra could therefore come from the bias itself, not from reversal of P. Candidate sources: electrostatic or photo-induced charging of the surface under the electrode, piezoelectric strain or displacement of the membrane (which shifts the elastic line and the scattering geometry), field-driven oxygen-vacancy migration that changes the O K-edge absorption, or electroabsorption at the absorption edge. The incident energy (531.25 eV) sits on the leading edge of the O K-edge XAS, where a small edge shift produces a large intensity change. The paper never shows that the switched signal follows P rather than the bias. The authors should (i) measure the CD at zero hold bias after writing (this also tests the "non-volatile" claim, see concern 10); (ii) measure with the same sign of hold bias in both polarization states, or with a write pulse below the coercive voltage (for example ±1.5 V, where Fig. 1c shows no switching), where a P-driven signal must not switch; (iii) ideally show a hysteresis loop of the integrated CD (region I or II) against write voltage that matches the PFM loop in Fig. 1c; (iv) show the O K-edge XAS (TFY) for F+ and F− at fixed helicity, so that a field-induced edge shift at 531.25 eV can be ruled out.

2. **The statistical weight of the switching signal is not established, and smoothing is applied to the key data.** The field-switching and CD differences in Fig. 3 (middle and bottom) are about ±0.05–0.1 on spectra that peak near 1.2. The plotted error bars are often comparable to the features, especially above 50 meV. A Savitzky–Golay filter is applied "to guide the eye" in Fig. 3 and to the curves compared across q in Fig. 4a. Smoothing can create the oscillating, derivative-like shapes that the region assignment I/II/III relies on. The error-bar definition is also inconsistent: the Fig. 3 caption says the s.d. "reflects Poisson counting statistics", the Methods say "the error bars in an RIXS spectrum are the standard deviation of individual scans", and the Fig. 4 caption uses Gaussian propagation. The authors should (i) show the unsmoothed differences with the smoothed curve overlaid; (ii) state one clear error definition and whether bars are s.d. or s.e.m. over the n = 16 repetitions; (iii) give the significance (for example, integrated signal over its uncertainty, and a χ² against zero) for the field-switched CD in each region and at each q. Most important is the interaction term I(C+,F+) − I(C+,F−) − I(C−,F+) + I(C−,F−). It is the only combination that isolates a helicity-odd and field-odd response; the middle and bottom panels of Fig. 3 are two projections of the same four spectra, not "two observations" as stated in the text.

3. **"Quantitative agreement with first-principles calculations" is not supported by Fig. 4.** The abstract makes this claim, but the comparison in Fig. 4f–h has several limits:
   - The measured "CD contrast (a.u.)" and the calculated J·e_q are on unrelated scales, with a free scale factor apparently chosen separately for each region.
   - Only four φ points per region are compared, each with large error bars.
   - The authors state that RIXS cross-sections (electron–phonon coupling per mode) were not calculated.
   - In Fig. 4f the point near φ ≈ 2.1 rad (q1) is roughly 0.4 against a predicted ≈1.8.
   - The predicted J·e_q differs by about a factor of 50 between region I (colour scale ±0.1) and region III (±0.002), yet the measured contrasts in Fig. 4f and 4h are of similar magnitude. If CD ∝ J·e_q, as the text implies, this is a quantitative discrepancy. Otherwise a large, unknown, mode-dependent prefactor is absorbed.

   At 30 meV FWHM resolution, each integration window also contains several modes, presumably with different signs of J·e_q. What the data support is sign or φ-trend agreement. The authors should (i) fit each region with a single scale factor and report χ² and the fitted factor, and compare against simple alternative models (constant, sin φ, a geometry-only model); (ii) compute the predicted CD from all modes in each window, weighted by the mode effective charges they already compute (Supplementary Section 4) and convolved with the resolution; (iii) otherwise replace "quantitative agreement" with "qualitative (sign and trend) agreement".

4. **The attribution of the CD to phonon angular momentum, rather than to any P-odd dichroism of the scattering geometry, lacks a null control.** With P, k_in and k_out non-coplanar, the scattering geometry itself is chiral, and a polar crystal can show a P-odd circular dichroism in elastic, quasi-elastic or electronic scattering without any phonon PAM. The argument against other CD sources is only qualitative ("CD is absent for isotropic charge scattering and BTO is non-magnetic"). The authors should show that a contribution not of phonon origin is absent:
   - Field-switched CD of the elastic line itself, which can be separated using the carbon-tape reference and the energy-gain side.
   - Field-switched CD at a high-symmetry q where the model predicts J·e_q = 0. q3 = (0, 0.24, 0.42) Å⁻¹ lies on a mirror plane and should give zero; the authors should say so explicitly and quantify it, since Fig. 4f–h suggest roughly zero there.
   - Field-switched CD above the single-phonon cutoff (0.12–0.25 eV), where Fig. 3 still shows some structure.
   - Ideally a paraelectric or non-polar reference measured under the same bias, for example the device heated above T_C, or an SrTiO3 membrane in the same electrode geometry.
   - A consistency check on the anti-Stokes side: at 295 K the 11 and 38 meV modes have appreciable thermal population, so the energy-gain side should show a CD whose sign and magnitude are set by detailed balance.

**B. Should address**

5. **One device, one spot.** Fig. 3 states that each configuration was measured "as n = 16 independent experiments on the same sample position". These are repeated scans, not independent samples. Nothing shows that the effect appears in a second device or a second electrode pad (Fig. 2a shows several), or that it survives many switching cycles. The authors describe the effect as "robust". To support that, they should show the result on at least one more device or pad, and plot the integrated switched CD against the number of switching cycles.

6. **Domain state and switching completeness during the RIXS measurement are not verified.** Ferroelectricity was confirmed by PFM before device fabrication (Fig. 1c) with an Au tip electrode, not in the final capacitor under the X-ray beam. Fig. 1c shows a noticeably asymmetric amplitude loop, suggesting possible imprint, and signal degradation above 3.5 V is reported (Supplementary Fig. 5). Several factors could change the switching that is actually achieved under the beam: back-switching during the low hold bias, X-ray-induced charging, partial switching, or 90° a-domains (relevant because measurements are at 295 K, close to the 278 K orthorhombic transition, which the authors note can cause shear strain). An a-domain fraction would also change the birefringent background (concern 7). The authors should provide in situ evidence of switching in the measured device, for example P–E or switching-current loops recorded at the beamline, or XAS linear dichroism before and after switching. They should also state the beam-induced temperature rise.

7. **Residual birefringence contribution is not quantified.** Normal incidence along c is said to suppress birefringence. But the outgoing beam is not along c for any of the q in Table 1, and Supplementary Fig. 8 is described as showing "strong dichroism" from birefringence away from this geometry. A 180° switch leaves the optic axis unchanged. However, piezoelectric strain under ±0.2 V hold bias, or any a-domain population, would change Δn between the two states. The authors should estimate the residual birefringent CD in the actual geometry using Eq. (5) and the measured optical constants. They should also show that it is much smaller than, or does not switch like, the signal in Fig. 3.

8. **Mode assignment is loose compared with the energy resolution.** The text says the strongest contrast appears "around 20, 40 and 100 meV", but assigns it to modes at 11, 38 and 100 meV, and the dashed lines in Fig. 3 are drawn at 11, 38 and 100 meV. A feature at 11 meV (or 20 meV) with 30 meV FWHM resolution lies on the tail of the elastic line, where a field-induced elastic-line shift (concern 1) or a resolution-function asymmetry gives a derivative-shaped difference. The 100 meV feature sits at or above the top of the single-phonon spectrum (the text gives "~100 meV"; the highest LO branch of tetragonal BTO is lower), so multiphonon contributions cannot be excluded. The 15 meV high-resolution scan is used only to locate phonon peaks and was not used for switching. The authors should (i) fit the elastic line separately for F+ and F− and show its position and width do not change with field; (ii) model the region I signal after elastic subtraction; (iii) discuss whether region III is single-phonon.

9. **DFT structure and its effect on J·e_q.** The relaxed structure has c/a ≈ 1.030 (a = 3.99 Å, c = 4.11 Å) against an experimental c/a ≈ 1.0075 (4.00/4.03 Å). This is a well-known PBE overestimate of tetragonality, and it directly controls the Ti off-centring, the soft-mode energies and the inversion-breaking that produces the PAM. Membrane strain is not modelled. Because the comparison in Fig. 4 rests on the sign and φ dependence of J·e_q for specific modes, the authors should show that these are robust to the functional or to the c/a ratio, for example by repeating the calculation with PBEsol or at the experimental lattice parameters. They should also explain how the LO–TO non-analytic correction is treated away from Γ along the measured q.

10. **The claims of "non-volatile" and "deterministic" switching are not demonstrated.** All RIXS data were taken under a ±0.2 V holding potential, applied explicitly so that the domains remain stable. Non-volatility means the PAM reversal persists at zero bias, and this was not shown. Either measure at zero bias, with a retention time, or remove "non-volatile" from the abstract and discussion.

11. **Normalization between helicities and between field states is not described.** A 5% CD is sensitive to the helicity-dependent flux and degree of circular polarization of the source, and to the normalization of F± spectra (incident flux, elastic intensity, or total spectral weight). The authors should state the normalization, the degree of circular polarization for C+ and C− at 531 eV, and how the "+ − − +" sequence cancels a helicity-dependent flux.

**C. Minor**

12. Methods, DFT: "hexagonal lattice constants of a = 3.99 Å and c = 4.11 Å" should be "tetragonal". Also explain why an internal pressure of 70 kPa was imposed and how it was chosen.

13. The geometry description is unclear. The text says "the outgoing angle is chosen to minimize the q-component along c", yet q_c = 0.42 Å⁻¹ is the largest component for every q in Table 1. Please give the incidence and scattering angles, the definition of the azimuth φ (relative to which in-plane axis), and how each Table 1 q maps onto the φ values in Fig. 4f–h.

14. Fig. 4a shows field switching only for C− light. Show the C+ data, and the q1 and q3 curves, in the same panel or in the SI, so the reader can see that the q2/q4 reversal is helicity-consistent.

15. Eq. (3): ε_kli is described as "the non-local dielectric response", but in the gyration-tensor definition it is the Levi-Civita symbol, and the dielectric response is in λ. Please correct and define λ, γ and ν consistently with Eq. (4).

16. The beam footprint (50 µm long axis) is close to the electrode pad diameter (60 µm, Fig. 2a inset). State the alignment tolerance, and whether any of the signal comes from unpoled regions or from the Cr/Au electrode edge.

17. The statement that this contrast is "inaccessible to optical measurements" should be qualified. Optical gyrotropy linear in P exists in polar crystals (as Eq. (4) itself shows). What is new here is the access to finite q.

18. Several typos ("the that seen in LiNbO3", "spectra... was", "multiferrons" referencing), and dataset ref. 56 is dated 2028.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a single figure that shows the full control set: integrated switched CD for regions I–III against write voltage (hysteresis loop), at zero hold bias, at the mirror-plane q3 (null), for the elastic line, and above the single-phonon cutoff. This one figure would address most of the A-concerns.
2. Replace the free-scale comparison in Fig. 4f–h with a forward model: J·e_q weighted by mode effective charge, summed over modes in each window and resolution-convolved, with a single global scale factor and a reported χ².
3. Repeat the switching measurement on a second device or pad, and with the 15 meV high-resolution setting at least at q2 and q4, to separate the 11 meV mode from the elastic line.
4. Temper the abstract and conclusions ("quantitative", "non-volatile", "robust", "deterministic") to match what the data show, or add the measurements that justify those words.
