Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The paper reports circularly polarized RIXS at the O K edge on free-standing 80-nm BaTiO3 membranes with top and bottom electrodes. The authors claim that the circular dichroism (CD) of the low-energy (phonon) RIXS response reverses when the ferroelectric polarization is switched electrically, and that its dependence on azimuth follows the g-wave (C4v) pattern of phonon angular momentum (PAM) predicted by DFT. If the result holds, it is the first demonstration of deterministic, electrically reversible control of phonon chirality at general momenta, and it extends CD-RIXS for chiral phonons (quartz, LiNbO3) to an in-operando device. Interest across the chiral-phonon, ferroelectric and X-ray communities would be high. The central difficulty is that the effect is a few-percent difference signal at an energy resolution comparable to the phonon energies, so the case rests on excluding artifacts and on the strength of the statistics.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A conceptually clean test: reversing the polarization flips the sign of the inversion-breaking that produces non-degenerate chiral phonons, so the sign of the CD should flip with it. The experiment directly targets this symmetry prediction.
- A device approach (membrane, jigsaw-puzzle top electrode transparent enough at the O K edge) that allows in-situ switching during RIXS. The ferroelectric loop is characterized by PFM in the device geometry (Fig. 1c).
- Two ways of looking at the antisymmetric signal (field switching at fixed helicity, CD at fixed field) and a four-point azimuthal series compared with DFT phonon helicities (Fig. 4).
- Birefringence-induced dichroism, a known pitfall of CD-RIXS, is recognized and addressed by measuring at normal incidence.
- Data and computational results are deposited.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **Energy-shift and drift artifacts in difference spectra are not excluded.** The switching and CD signals (Fig. 3, middle and bottom; Fig. 4a) are differences of spectra with ~30 meV (high flux) or 15 meV (high resolution) FWHM resolution. The differences show derivative-like, alternating-sign lobes around the elastic line and the 40–100 meV features. This is the lineshape a sub-meV relative energy shift, a small change in elastic intensity, or a small change in scattering geometry between configurations would produce. Under bias the membrane is piezoelectric, so polarization reversal changes thickness and possibly the local tilt. That can shift the specular/elastic contribution and the detector position of the elastic line. The Methods say the zero of energy is set by fitting the gain side of the elastic line with a half-Lorentzian, but they do not show (i) the fitted elastic-line positions, widths and intensities for the four configurations (F±, C±), or (ii) that the difference spectra are unchanged when the spectra are aligned independently (for example to the carbon-tape reference, or by cross-correlating the phonon region). The authors should report the residual energy offsets and elastic intensities per configuration and per repetition. They should then simulate the difference spectrum produced by those offsets and show that it is much smaller than, or differently shaped from, the measured signal. Without this, the main observation could be an alignment artifact that correlates with field direction.

2. **No null control in which the field is applied but the effect should be absent.** Every test of the switching shares the same ingredients: bias on the electrodes, piezoelectric strain, polarization-dependent birefringence, and possibly charge injection. None separates "PAM reversal" from "anything that is odd in the polarization direction". Several controls are possible:
   - measure at a high-symmetry q, for example along the (110) mirror direction the authors themselves mention, or at q∥c, where J·e_q vanishes by symmetry and no switching should appear;
   - toggle a sub-coercive voltage (for example ±0.2 V without the ±3.5 V write pulses), so the field is present but the polarization does not reverse;
   - measure above T_C (~400 K), or in a paraelectric reference membrane with the same electrode stack, where non-degenerate chiral phonons should not exist.

   At least one such control, measured under matched conditions, is needed before the switching can be attributed to the phonon subsystem. As written, the sentence "Because we have shown that other sources of circular contrast in the RIXS signal are negligible, the CD that switches … must arise from the phononic subsystem" (p. 3) is not supported by anything shown in the main text.

3. **The statistical significance of the switching signal and of the "quantitative agreement" is not established.** In Fig. 3 (middle and bottom), many individual points have error bars comparable to the shaded lobes. The shaded areas are drawn from Savitzky–Golay-smoothed curves, which can visually turn noise into lobes. Please report, for each of the three energy windows, the integrated switching and CD with uncertainties and a significance against zero, using the 16 repetitions. Ideally also show a split-half test: the same difference computed between two halves of the repeats taken at the same field direction should be consistent with zero.

   In Fig. 4f–h, the "quantitative agreement with first-principles calculations" (abstract) rests on four points per region with large error bars (in region III, several are consistent with zero). Because the experimental CD is in arbitrary units, a scale factor must have been fitted. The authors should state how the scale was set (one factor per region or one overall), give χ² or an equivalent measure for the DFT curve against a constant/zero model and against simple alternatives, and moderate "quantitative" if only the sign pattern is tested.

4. **The relevant oppositely oriented q, the true g-wave test, was apparently not measured.** The conclusions state that "the CD–RIXS contrast inverts upon measuring along an oppositely oriented q". However, Table 1 contains only four q points, on an arc with q_y ≈ 0.2–0.24 Å⁻¹ and fixed q_z = 0.42 Å⁻¹, spanning φ ≈ 1.0–2.2 rad. The sign change within this arc (q2 vs q4) is consistent with a g-wave pattern, but also with any function odd about the (010)/(100) mirror, including one from a geometric or birefringence effect that depends on azimuth. To support g-wave symmetry specifically, please measure q points related by the C4 rotation (expected to give the same sign) and by the other mirror (opposite sign), or a fuller azimuthal range. Otherwise narrow the claim to "sign reversal across a mirror plane, consistent with the predicted g-wave pattern".

**B. Should address**

5. **Assigning modes at 11, 38 and 100 meV is not supported at this resolution.** The energy resolution is 30 meV FWHM (15 meV high-resolution), so an 11 meV feature lies within the elastic-line tail. The text also states that BTO phonon energies are limited to ~100 meV and that "features at higher energies are presumed to be multiphonon". Assigning region III (centred at 100 meV) to a single chiral phonon mode, and comparing its φ-dependence to the J·e_q of one DFT mode, therefore needs justification. Please show the high-resolution spectrum (the 15-meV scan is described but not shown in the main text) with a multi-peak decomposition, and discuss multiphonon and bimagnon-free continuum contributions in region III. Also explain why one mode per window is compared with the data, rather than the effective-charge-weighted sum over all modes within the resolution, which the text mentions only briefly (Supplementary Fig. 17).

6. **The link between the measured CD and J·e_q is assumed, not derived.** Eq. (1) gives the dichroic amplitude in terms of the oxygen 2p orbital rotation, and Fig. 4 compares it with the DFT phonon helicity J·e_q. Fig. 4b states that this is the oxygen-projected angular momentum. Please state precisely which quantity is plotted in Fig. 4c–h: total J or oxygen-only; how modes within each window are weighted (Franck–Condon effective charge?); and whether the predicted ratio of CD magnitudes between regions I, II and III matches experiment. The colour scales differ by more than an order of magnitude (±0.1 in Fig. 4c, ±0.05 in 4e, but ±0.002 in 4h), so a calculation predicting a 50× smaller helicity for region III while the measured contrast is comparable needs discussion.

7. **The domain state and the fraction switched during RIXS are not characterized.** PFM (Fig. 1c) was done before device completion, on a different electrode configuration. During RIXS the write pulse is ±3.5 V, followed by a 0.2 V hold. There is no in-situ evidence that the 50-µm-footprint region is single-domain and fully reversed each time, that no in-plane a-domains are present (tetragonal membranes often form a/c domain patterns), or that reversal is unaffected by X-ray illumination (photo-induced screening). Switching or polarization–voltage (P–V) loops measured on the actual RIXS device at the beamline, before and after the measurement series, and some estimate of the switched fraction would make the polarization-to-CD link quantitative. The fatigue reported above 3.5 V (Supplementary Fig. 5) makes this especially relevant, since the write voltage sits at that limit.

8. **Non-volatility is claimed but not tested.** The abstract describes a "robust and non-volatile gyroelectric effect", yet all data are taken under a ±0.2 V holding bias. Please show CD at zero bias after writing, or retention as a function of time, or remove "non-volatile".

9. **Sample statistics.** The 16 repetitions are on "the same sample position", and as far as I can tell there is one device. Please state how many membranes and devices were measured and whether the switching was reproduced on a second device or position. Even one confirmation would considerably strengthen the claim.

10. **Birefringence control at the actual geometry.** Normal incidence along c suppresses birefringence for the incident beam, but at a general q the scattered beam leaves off-axis through a birefringent, electro-optically active film whose optic axis reverses with P. The RIXS detection is polarization-insensitive, so this may be harmless, but the argument should be made explicitly. Please also show that the residual birefringence-induced CD (Supplementary Fig. 8) is odd or even under polarization reversal at the measurement geometry.

11. **Structural model for DFT.** The relaxed structure has c = 4.11 Å against an experimental 4.03 Å, so the PBE tetragonality is substantially overestimated. This inflates the Ti off-centring and probably the PAM splitting. The membrane is also not bulk. Please comment on how sensitive the signs and φ-dependence of J·e_q are to c/a (for example, a calculation at the experimental lattice parameters), because the comparison in Fig. 4f–h relies on them.

**C. Minor**

12. The middle and bottom panels of Fig. 3 are algebraic rearrangements of the same four spectra: [I(C+,F+) − I(C−,F+)] − [I(C+,F−) − I(C−,F−)] equals [I(C+,F+) − I(C+,F−)] − [I(C−,F+) − I(C−,F−)]. They are therefore not two independent observations, as the sentence "These two observations provide direct evidence" suggests. Rephrase, and show all four raw spectra so readers can see the size of each difference.

13. The error-bar definitions conflict. The Fig. 3 caption says the s.d. reflects Poisson counting statistics, while the Methods say the error bars are the standard deviation of individual scans. Please specify which applies to each figure.

14. Fig. 4a shows field switching only for C− light. Please also show C+, which should have the opposite sign.

15. The text after eq. (3) calls ε_kli "the non-local dielectric response" and λ "the momentum-dependent natural optical activity". In the standard form, ε is the Levi-Civita tensor. Please correct.

16. The Methods refer to "hexagonal lattice constants" for tetragonal BTO.

17. Labels such as "F^{+/-} = ±0.2 V" give a voltage, not a field. Give the field (~25 kV cm⁻¹ for 80 nm) or relabel.

18. The top electrode is 4 nm Cr / 8 nm Au. A Cr native oxide, and any residual surface oxygen, contributes O K-edge signal. Please estimate its share of the RIXS intensity and confirm it carries no field-odd CD.

19. Fig. 1b is described as "Modelled on ref. 27". State that it is schematic, or give its parameters.

20. The temperature sentence on p. 3 implies that 295 K was chosen to stay away from the 278 K transition. State the sample temperature stability and possible beam heating, since a modest local cooling or heating would not matter but a field-induced phase coexistence near 278 K would.

21. Typos: "comparable to the that seen", and "neuromorphic" spacing in a few places.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add one decisive null measurement: q on a mirror plane, a sub-coercive voltage toggle, or T > T_C. Combine it with an explicit energy-alignment analysis showing that drift cannot produce the difference spectra.
2. Quantify significance: report integrated region-by-region switching with uncertainties, a split-half (same-field) null difference, and a fit statistic for the DFT comparison with the scale factor stated.
3. Extend the azimuthal coverage, including C4-related and opposite-q points, so that g-wave symmetry is tested rather than inferred from a single arc.
4. Present the high-resolution spectra and a resolution-convolved, effective-charge-weighted simulation of the CD spectrum from DFT. Compare it with the data across the full 0–120 meV range, rather than one mode per window.
