**A. Must address**

1. **No null control separates phonon angular momentum from other polarization-odd or field-odd sources of circular contrast.** The headline claim is that the CD which switches with the field "must arise from the phononic subsystem" (p. 3), on the grounds that "we have shown that other sources of circular contrast in the RIXS signal are negligible". Nothing in the main text shows this; the argument is only qualitative ("CD is absent for isotropic charge scattering and BTO is non-magnetic"). With P, k_in and k_out non-coplanar, the scattering geometry itself is chiral, and a polar crystal can show P-odd circular dichroism in elastic, quasi-elastic or electronic scattering without any phonon PAM. Reversing P also reverses piezoelectric strain (membrane height and tilt under the 50 µm spot), the polarization-dependent birefringence, interfacial charge and the oxygen-vacancy distribution under the Cr contact, and the elastic-line intensity. The authors note that birefringence produces strong CD that masks the phonon signal (Supplementary Fig. 8); a free-standing membrane on a patterned electrode is unlikely to be perfectly flat, and local tilts give a finite in-plane projection that rotates with φ and could produce a φ-dependent CD. The authors should show, under matched conditions, at least: (a) the field-switching difference with linear (π or σ) polarization, which should be zero within error; (b) the field-switched CD integrated over the elastic line (about −20 to +10 meV, separable using the carbon-tape reference and the energy-gain side) as a quantitative null; (c) the field-independent part of the CD, [CD(F+) + CD(F−)]/2, which should vanish if all the CD comes from P; (d) the field-switched CD at a q where J·e_q vanishes by symmetry, such as q3 = (0, 0.24, 0.42) Å⁻¹ on the mirror plane (stated explicitly and quantified, since Fig. 4f–h suggest roughly zero there), along the (110) mirror direction the authors mention, or at q ∥ c; (e) the field-switched CD above the single-phonon cutoff (0.12–0.25 eV), where Fig. 3 still shows some structure; (f) an anti-Stokes consistency check, since at 295 K the 11 and 38 meV modes are appreciably populated and the energy-gain side should show a CD whose sign and magnitude are fixed by detailed balance; and ideally (g) the same protocol above T_C, where the CD should vanish and stop switching, or on a paraelectric or non-polar reference membrane (for example SrTiO3) with the same electrode stack. Without such controls the attribution to chiral phonons is not secure.

2. **Polarization reversal is not separated from field-odd effects of the bias protocol itself.** In each field configuration the sample is written at ±3.5 V and then held at ±0.2 V during measurement (Methods, "Ferroelectric switching in RIXS"; Fig. 3 caption, F± = ±0.2 V), so the sign of the applied DC bias flips together with P. The hold field (about 25 kV/cm over 80 nm) acts on BTO's very large electro-optic coefficients, and could also cause electrostatic or photo-induced surface charging, piezoelectric displacement (shifting the elastic line and the scattering geometry), field-driven oxygen-vacancy migration or electroabsorption. The incident energy (531.25 eV) sits on the leading edge of the O K-edge XAS, where a small edge shift produces a large intensity change. The paper never shows that the switched signal follows P rather than the bias. The authors should (i) measure the CD at zero hold bias after writing (which also tests non-volatility, concern 9); (ii) measure with the same sign of hold bias in both polarization states, or toggle a sub-coercive voltage (for example ±0.2 V without write pulses, or ±1.5 V writes, where Fig. 1c shows no switching), where a P-driven signal must not switch; (iii) ideally show a hysteresis loop of the integrated CD (region I or II) against write voltage that matches the PFM loop in Fig. 1c; and (iv) show the O K-edge XAS (TFY) for F+ and F− at fixed helicity, to rule out a field-induced edge shift at 531.25 eV.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

3. **Energy-shift and drift artifacts in the difference spectra are not excluded.** The switching and CD signals (Fig. 3 middle and bottom; Fig. 4a) are differences of spectra taken at about 30 meV FWHM resolution, and they show derivative-like, alternating-sign lobes around the elastic line and the 40–100 meV features. This is exactly the lineshape that a sub-meV relative energy shift, a small change in elastic intensity, or a small change in scattering geometry between configurations would produce. Under bias the piezoelectric membrane changes thickness and possibly local tilt, which can shift the specular/elastic contribution and the detector position of the elastic line. The Methods set the energy zero by fitting the gain side of the elastic line with a half-Lorentzian, but do not report the fitted elastic-line positions, widths and intensities for the four configurations (F±, C±), nor show that the differences survive independent alignment (for example to the carbon-tape reference, or by cross-correlating the phonon region). The authors should report the residual energy offsets and elastic intensities per configuration and per repetition, fit the elastic line separately for F+ and F− to show its position and width do not change with field, and simulate the difference spectrum those offsets would produce, showing that it is much smaller than, or differently shaped from, the measured signal. Otherwise the main observation could be an alignment artifact that correlates with field direction.

4. **The statistical significance of the switching signal is not established, and smoothing is applied to the key data.** The field-switching and CD differences in Fig. 3 are about ±0.05–0.1 on spectra that peak near 1.2, and many error bars are comparable to the shaded lobes, especially above 50 meV. A Savitzky–Golay filter is applied to Fig. 3 and to the curves compared across q in Fig. 4a, and the shaded lobes are drawn from the smoothed curves; smoothing a noisy difference signal can create oscillations on the scale of the smoothing window, and the region I/II/III assignment relies on these shapes. The error definition is inconsistent: the Fig. 3 caption says the s.d. "reflects Poisson counting statistics", the Methods say the error bars are "the standard deviation of individual scans", and the Fig. 4 caption uses Gaussian propagation; for n = 16, s.d. and s.e.m. differ by a factor of 4. The integration and error propagation within each window for Fig. 4f–h are not stated. The authors should (i) show the unsmoothed differences with the smoothed curves overlaid; (ii) state one clear error definition for each figure, and whether bars are s.d. or s.e.m. over the 16 repetitions; (iii) give, for each region and q, the integrated switching and CD contrast with uncertainty, significance against zero and a χ² against zero, with particular attention to the interaction term I(C+,F+) − I(C+,F−) − I(C−,F+) + I(C−,F−), the only combination isolating a helicity-odd and field-odd response; (iv) give a split-half null test, in which the same difference computed between two halves of the repeats at the same field direction is consistent with zero; and (v) show the integrated contrast as a time series over successive field reversals, where a sign alternating with every reversal without monotonic drift would directly support reversible switching and rule out beam damage or slow electromigration.

5. **The comparison with DFT is not quantitative, contrary to the abstract.** In Fig. 4f–h each panel relates "CD contrast (a.u.)" to J·e_q through its own free scale, apparently chosen separately for each region, and each region rests on four points with large error bars (in region III several are consistent with zero). Given the mirror symmetry, any contrast odd under x → −x crosses zero at φ = π/2 and has opposite signs at q2 and q4, so agreement in sign and zero crossing tests symmetry, not the DFT eigenvectors. Where the data do test the calculation, agreement is weak: in Fig. 4f the q1 point (φ ≈ 2.1 rad) is about 0.4 ± 0.4 against a predicted 1.8–1.9, contradicting the statement on p. 5 that the "CD contrast is largest near q1". Magnitudes are inconsistent across modes: the calculated J·e_q for the 100 meV mode (colour scale ±0.002, Fig. 4e, h) is about 50 times smaller than for the 11 meV mode (±0.1), yet the measured contrasts in regions I and III differ by only a factor of about 4; if CD ∝ J·e_q this is a quantitative discrepancy, and otherwise a large unknown mode-dependent prefactor is being absorbed. The RIXS cross-sections (electron–phonon coupling per mode) were not calculated, and at 30 meV resolution each window contains several modes, presumably with different signs of J·e_q, yet one mode per window is compared. It is also not stated whether J in Eq. (2) and Fig. 4c–h is the oxygen-projected or total-cell angular momentum (Fig. 4b suggests oxygen-only), nor why that choice suits the O K-edge contrast. The authors should (i) fit all regions with a single global scale factor, or with factors fixed by the calculated mode effective charges (Supplementary Section 4), and report χ² and the fitted factor; (ii) compare against simple alternatives (constant, zero, a sin-type φ dependence with free amplitude, a geometry-only model); (iii) compute the predicted CD-RIXS spectrum from all modes in each window, weighted by the effective charges and convolved with the resolution, and overlay it on Fig. 3 across 0–120 meV; and (iv) state whether the predicted ratio of CD magnitudes between regions I, II and III matches experiment. Otherwise "quantitative agreement" should become "qualitative (sign and symmetry) agreement".

6. **The g-wave symmetry in the title is not established by the measured q range.** All four q points (Table 1) lie on one arc with q_y ≈ 0.2–0.24 Å⁻¹ and fixed q_z = 0.42 Å⁻¹, spanning roughly φ ≈ 1.0–2.2 rad (φ ≈ 1.40–2.11 rad in Fig. 4f–h, about 40°) and containing a single nodal plane at φ = π/2. Many angular forms odd under the mirror through b* vanish there, including p-wave and d-wave forms, as well as any geometric or birefringence effect that depends on azimuth. A g-wave (C4v, eight-sector) pattern requires further sign changes at the diagonal mirrors (φ = π/4 and 3π/4 ≈ 2.36 rad, just beyond q1) and equal signs at C4-related points. Relatedly, the conclusion that the contrast "inverts upon measuring along an oppositely oriented q" (p. 5) is not what was measured: only mirror-related in-plane components (q2 and q4) were compared, at the same q_z. The authors should add points across a diagonal mirror (for example φ ≈ 2.4–2.8 rad), C4-related points and genuinely opposite q, and show the predicted sign pattern, or else restate the claim as "sign reversal across a mirror plane, consistent with the predicted g-wave pattern" and adjust the title, abstract and conclusions.

**B. Should address**

7. **The polarization state of the illuminated volume during RIXS is never measured.** Ferroelectricity was confirmed by PFM before device fabrication with an Au tip on an Au bottom electrode (Fig. 1c, Methods), not in the finished Cr/Au-capped capacitor under the X-ray beam; the paper should state on which sample and electrode configuration the Fig. 1c loop was taken. The loop is asymmetric and imprinted (coercive voltages of roughly −2 V and +2.6 V), yet symmetric write and hold voltages are used; the 3.5 V write voltage sits at the stated onset of degradation and fatigue (Supplementary Fig. 5); and the 0.2 V hold may not stabilize the state against back-switching, X-ray-induced photocarriers or charging. The 60 µm pad is barely larger than the 50 µm beam footprint, so part of the signal may come from unswitched regions or the Cr/Au electrode edge. The authors should provide in-situ evidence of switching in the measured device, for example switching-current or P–E/P–V loops recorded at the beamline before and after each RIXS block, a structural or spectroscopic probe of polarization sign (Friedel-pair asymmetry of a (00L) reflection, SHG, or XAS linear dichroism before and after switching), an estimate of the switched fraction, and the alignment tolerance of beam on pad.

8. **The mode assignment is finer than the energy resolution.** With about 30 meV FWHM resolution (Methods), the CD and switching traces in Fig. 3 show alternating lobes about 15–20 meV wide, attributed to individual modes at 11, 38 and 100 meV; the text says the strongest contrast appears "around 20, 40 and 100 meV", while the dashed lines are drawn at 11, 38 and 100 meV. Region I (0 to about 40 meV, Fig. 4a) lies on the tail of the dominant elastic line, where a field-induced elastic shift or resolution-function asymmetry gives a derivative-shaped difference. The text states that BTO phonons end at about 100 meV and that higher features are multiphonon; region III, centred at 100 meV next to a strong FY peak near 0.1 eV, sits at or above the top of the one-phonon spectrum, so multiphonon, continuum or elastic-tail/background contributions cannot be excluded. The 15 meV high-resolution data are used only to locate phonon energies, are not shown in the main text, and are not used to test switching. The authors should fix integration windows before analysis from the resolution-convolved DFT dispersion and state how robust the integrated contrasts are to the window edges; model the region I signal after elastic subtraction; show the high-resolution spectrum with a multi-peak decomposition; if possible measure the field switching at 15 meV resolution for at least one q (ideally q2 and q4); and justify assigning region III to a single one-phonon mode.

9. **"Non-volatile" and "deterministic" switching are not demonstrated.** The abstract describes a "robust and non-volatile gyroelectric effect", but every RIXS measurement was taken under a ±0.2 V holding bias of the same sign as the write pulse, applied explicitly so that the domains remain stable. Remanence was therefore not tested. The authors should measure the CD at zero bias after writing, with a retention time, or remove "non-volatile" (and temper "deterministic") in the abstract and discussion.

10. **The result rests on one device and one spot.** The n = 16 "independent experiments on the same sample position" (Fig. 3) are repeated scans, not independent samples, and nothing shows that the effect appears on a second device or electrode pad (Fig. 2a shows several) or survives many switching cycles. The authors should state how many membranes and devices were measured, show the result on at least one more device or pad, and plot the integrated switched CD against switching-cycle number to support "robust".

11. **The residual birefringence contribution at the actual geometry is not quantified.** Normal incidence along c suppresses birefringence for the incident beam, but the outgoing beam is off-axis for every q in Table 1 and passes through a birefringent, electro-optically active film, and Supplementary Fig. 8 shows "strong dichroism" from birefringence away from normal incidence. A 180° switch leaves the optic axis unchanged, but piezoelectric strain under the hold bias, local tilts or any a-domain population would change Δn between the two states. The RIXS detection is polarization-insensitive, which may make the outgoing path harmless, but this should be argued explicitly. The authors should estimate the residual birefringent CD in the actual geometry using Eq. (5) and the measured optical constants, and show that it is much smaller than, or does not switch like, the signal in Fig. 3 (that is, whether it is odd or even under polarization reversal).

12. **The domain structure, phase and morphology of the membrane are not shown.** Free-standing 80 nm BTO membranes often contain 90° a-domains and wrinkles, which would break the assumed geometry (c along the beam), mix in in-plane polarization components outside the 4mm picture of Fig. 1a, and change the birefringent background. The text says "film orientation was confirmed by non-specular X-ray diffraction", but the tetragonality, a-domain fraction, rocking-curve width (the tilt distribution under the spot) and lateral PFM are not reported. The text raises the 278 K orthorhombic transition and shear strain as a risk without showing that the membrane is single-phase tetragonal at 295 K. These data should be given, and the CD magnitude discussed in light of any a-domain fraction.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

13. **The DFT model's assumptions need justification.** The relaxed structure (a = 3.99 Å, c = 4.11 Å, c/a ≈ 1.030) strongly overestimates the experimental tetragonality (4.00/4.03 Å, c/a ≈ 1.0075), a known PBE problem that inflates the Ti off-centring, the soft-mode energies and the inversion-breaking that produces the PAM; membrane strain is not modelled. In harmonic PBE, tetragonal BaTiO3 is dynamically unstable toward the orthorhombic and rhombohedral phases, so imaginary or strongly anharmonic soft modes are expected, particularly in the low-energy branch relevant to region I; the paper does not say whether the dispersion (Supplementary Fig. 11) has unstable branches or how they were treated. The treatment of the LO–TO non-analytic correction away from Γ along the measured q is not explained. The "internal pressure of 70 kPa" is implausibly small to change lattice constants and is probably a units error; its purpose and choice should be explained. Because Fig. 4 rests on the signs and φ dependence of J·e_q, the authors should show these are robust to the functional or c/a, for example with PBEsol or at the experimental lattice parameters.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

14. **Oxygen sources other than the BTO lattice are not addressed.** The O K-edge signal passes through a 4 nm Cr / 8 nm Au top electrode. Cr in contact with BTO or exposed at pad edges is likely partly oxidized, and the device also contains photoresist residues, carbon tape and possibly surface adsorbates, all of which contribute to the O K-edge RIXS and XAS; a field-dependent redox reaction at the Cr/BTO interface could produce a field- and polarization-dependent spectral change. The authors should compare the calibration XAS (Supplementary Fig. 6) with reference BTO, estimate the fraction of signal from non-BTO oxygen, and show that it carries no field-odd CD and that the XAS does not change between F+ and F−.

The authors should state the normalization, the degree of circular polarization for C+ and C− at 531 eV, and how the "+ − − +" sequence cancels a helicity-dependent flux.
