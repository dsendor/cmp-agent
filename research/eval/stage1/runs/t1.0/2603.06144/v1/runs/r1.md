Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports circular-dichroic RIXS (CD-RIXS) at the O K edge on a single freestanding BaTiO3 membrane capacitor. The authors claim that reversing the ferroelectric polarisation with an out-of-plane field reverses the sign of the phonon-region dichroism, which they read as electric-field switching of phonon angular momentum (PAM). They support this with a four-point azimuthal (q) dependence compared to DFT-computed J·q. If established, this would be the first demonstration of deterministic, field-controlled reversal of phonon chirality at finite momentum. That would be a clear step beyond the static CD-RIXS observations in quartz and LiNbO3, and it fits the theoretical proposals for field-controlled PAM in perovskites. As presented, the central signal is small, sits on the elastic-line tail at an energy resolution comparable to the phonon energies, and lacks the controls needed to exclude artefacts that the field reversal itself could produce.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The question is timely and important. The authors combine switchable polar order with a momentum-resolved probe of phonon chirality, and the symmetry logic is sound: reversing P is equivalent to a mirror normal to c, which flips the in-plane PAM at fixed q.
- The experiment is technically ambitious. In situ switching of a freestanding BTO membrane behind a thin Au/Cr top electrode at a soft-X-ray RIXS beamline, with a '+ - - +' helicity sequence and repeated field cycles over about 15 h, is non-trivial.
- The design has two internal sign checks: helicity reversal and field reversal. It also tests the q dependence by azimuthal rotation, including a pair of q points (q2, q4) that are mirror images across a symmetry plane and for which DFT predicts opposite J·q.
- DFT phonon eigenvectors are computed and released, and the raw data have a DOI.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The dichroism has a derivative-like line shape centred on the elastic line, so an energy-shift or intensity artefact has not been excluded. In Fig. 3b(i,ii) and Fig. 4a, the largest CD and field-switching features alternate in sign across 0–0.1 eV: positive near 0.01–0.02 eV, negative near 0.04–0.05 eV, then positive again. Two artefacts would produce exactly this shape. One is a small relative energy shift between spectra, from elastic-line alignment error, beam or sample drift, or field-induced sample displacement. The other is a small difference in elastic or quasi-elastic intensity combined with the 30 meV resolution. The elastic peak is about 10 times stronger than the phonon features, so a shift of a few meV gives a difference signal of about 0.1 in the units of Fig. 3, which matches the reported contrast. The authors should:
   - show the elastic-peak position and integrated intensity for every individual scan, by helicity and by field state, through the 15 h sequence;
   - show the difference spectrum that a measured shift of that size would produce (shift a C+ spectrum by the observed scatter and subtract it);
   - report the CD integrated across the elastic line and in a window with no phonon weight;
   - state whether the zero of energy is set per scan or per summed spectrum.

   Without this, the sign reversal under field could reflect a reproducible field-dependent shift, for example from piezoelectric or electrostrictive height change of the membrane under ±0.2 V hold, which moves the scattering point on the spectrometer, rather than reversal of the PAM.

2. Elastic-line fitting from the energy-gain side is biased at room temperature. The text (p. 6) says the energy-gain side contains only thermally excited states and fits the elastic line to it with a half-Lorentzian. At room temperature, kT is about 25 meV. The 11 meV and 38 meV modes the authors invoke therefore have substantial anti-Stokes weight on exactly that side (Bose factors of roughly 0.6 and 0.2 relative to Stokes). This biases both the energy zero and the elastic subtraction, and it would differ between helicities if the anti-Stokes phonons themselves carry CD. The authors should fit Stokes and anti-Stokes phonons together with the elastic line under detailed balance, or calibrate zero independently (for example with a carbon-tape reference before and after each block). They should also show that the CD conclusions do not depend on the choice.

3. The two "independent" observations are the same data. With four spectra I(helicity, field), the CD at +E minus the CD at −E is identical to the field switching for C+ minus the field switching for C−. Fig. 3b(i) and 3b(ii) are therefore one double difference displayed twice, not two independent confirmations as stated at the top of p. 8. The genuinely independent tests are:
   - that CD(+E) and CD(−E) are each individually non-zero with opposite sign, and of equal magnitude within error;
   - that FS(C+) and FS(C−) are each non-zero with opposite sign.

   The four individual spectra should be shown with per-scan statistics, and each test should be quantified with an integrated value, an uncertainty and a significance against zero, per energy window. The error-bar definition also needs reconciling. The Fig. 3 caption says "Poisson statistics on re-binned data"; Methods says "standard deviation of individual scans".

4. There is no evidence that the X-ray-illuminated volume actually switched, and switching may be incomplete on one side. The PFM loop in Fig. 2b is strongly imprinted: the down-to-up branch (blue) switches only near +4 V, while the up-to-down branch (red) switches near −1 V. A write pulse of +3.5 V (Fig. 2c, Methods) is therefore below the apparent coercive voltage for that branch. The PFM was also measured before the experiment, presumably on a different area or device. In addition:
   - the BTO thickness is never given ("various thicknesses" in Methods), so the field in kV/cm and its relation to the coercive field cannot be assessed;
   - the beam (50 µm long axis) is almost as large as the top electrode (60 µm diameter in Fig. 2a), so part of the footprint may lie on unswitched or unelectroded membrane, especially with drift over 15 h.

   The authors should give the membrane thickness and show switching current or P–E loops measured in situ on the actual device at the beamline before, during and after the RIXS sequence. They should explain why 3.5 V is sufficient given Fig. 2b and show the beam footprint relative to the electrode. Ideally they would add a field-state-sensitive X-ray observable recorded at the same time, for example a polarisation-sensitive XAS or diffraction signature. A sub-coercive control is also needed: apply ±1 V write pulses, where no reversal should occur, and show that the CD does not reverse. This would separate polarisation reversal from any direct effect of the applied voltage.

5. There is no null control where the effect should be absent. Every measurement is on one device at q points where the PAM is predicted to be non-zero, so nothing shows that the analysis gives zero when it should. The paper's own symmetry argument offers natural nulls:
   - a q direction on a mirror plane, where J·q = 0 by symmetry (the white regions of Fig. 4c–e; q3 = (0, 0.24, 0.42) appears close to one, yet q3 is missing from Fig. 4a);
   - the same measurement above the Curie temperature (about 120 °C), where C4v polarity and the switching should vanish;
   - a region of the membrane outside the electrode, or an unpoled or multidomain state.

   At minimum, the q3 spectra and their CD should be shown, and the authors should say whether they vanish as predicted.

6. The assignment of the switching CD to specific chiral modes is not supported at the stated resolution. With 30 meV FWHM resolution, the authors attribute CD features at about 20, 40 and 100 meV to calculated modes at 11, 38 and 100 meV (p. 7). An 11 meV mode cannot be separated from the elastic line at that resolution. The 20 meV versus 11 meV mismatch is not explained. The 100 meV feature lies at the top of the BTO phonon spectrum, where p. 5 says features are presumed to be multi-phonon. The high-resolution (15 meV) scan is described as confirmation but is not shown in the main text. The spectra also show a clear feature near 0.2 eV that is not discussed. The authors should:
   - show the high-resolution spectra and their CD;
   - fit all spectra with resolution-convolved DFT-based mode intensities (using the Franck–Condon or effective-charge estimate they mention), with energies fixed and amplitudes free;
   - show which modes contribute to each integration window (regions I–III in Fig. 4a), and state how those windows were chosen.

B. Should address

7. The q-dependence test (Fig. 4f–h) is weak as a quantitative confirmation. There are four φ points per mode over about 1.2 rad, a free scale factor per mode ("when scaled accordingly"), and an acknowledged outlier at q1 in region I. With one free amplitude and a predicted curve that changes sign once in this range, agreement in sign at three or four points is a modest test. The authors should:
   - state the fitted scale factors and their uncertainties, and say whether their signs and relative magnitudes across modes agree with an independent expectation;
   - give a goodness of fit, and compare it with a null model such as a constant or a random sign;
   - say whether the Fig. 4 field-switching data were taken in both field states at all four q, and with both helicities.

   Fig. 4a shows only q1, q2 and q4, in C− light.

8. The link between CD contrast and J·q is asserted, not derived. Eq. 1 introduces A_m with an undefined angle φ, and the text then moves between "CD measures the circular motion of the oxygen atoms", "sensitive only to out-of-plane ion displacements" (p. 9) and the projection J·q. In addition, J is estimated as "the maximal area of the ellipse the oxygen atom traverses" rather than from the standard PAM expression l = ħ ε†Mε summed over sublattices. It is not stated which oxygen sublattice (apical O1 or equatorial O2) is used, although these differ in symmetry and in hybridisation with the Ti 3d states probed at 531.25 eV. The authors should:
   - give the derivation, or the reference relation, connecting the E1–E1 O K-edge CD-RIXS cross-section to the projection of the oxygen-sublattice PAM along q (or along the beam);
   - compute J with the standard expression, both per sublattice and in total;
   - show that the J·q maps in Fig. 4c–e are robust to this choice.

9. The DFT structure departs substantially from experiment in the quantity that controls the effect. The relaxed lattice (p. 13) has c/a = 4.11/3.99 = 1.030, against the experimental 4.03/4.00 = 1.0075. PBE is known to overestimate tetragonality in BaTiO3, and this inflates the polar distortion, which by the paper's own argument sets the size of the PAM. The text also calls the lattice "hexagonal", and the choice to relax "to an internal pressure of 70 kPa" with a D3 dispersion correction is not explained. The authors should repeat the key mode energies, eigenvectors and J·q with the experimental lattice parameters (or with PBEsol), report how mode energies and J change, and state how LO–TO splitting and Born effective charges were treated near the q values used. The freestanding membrane's actual lattice parameters from the XRD in Fig. S.1–2 should be compared as well.

10. The "non-volatile" claim conflicts with the protocol. The abstract and conclusions claim non-volatile control and stability for at least 15 h. However, a ±0.2 V hold voltage was applied during every measurement (Fig. 2c, p. 6), explicitly "to ensure the ferroelectric domains remain stable". The data therefore show reproducible switching under bias across several cycles, not retention at zero field. The data also do not show that the CD is stable within each state over time, because spectra are summed. The authors should either measure CD at zero hold voltage after writing, or remove the non-volatility claim. They should also show the CD per cycle and per block through the 15 h sequence (Fig. 2c shows about eight state changes) so that reproducibility and drift can be judged.

11. The exclusion of birefringence and domain effects depends on unstated assumptions. Birefringent CD is said to be removed by normal incidence along c (p. 5). This assumes a pure c-domain membrane with no a/c (90°) domains, no in-plane anisotropy from the transfer, clamping or wrinkling of the freestanding film, and exact alignment. It is unclear how normal incidence is compatible with the stated q, which has a large in-plane component, and with φ rotation; presumably the outgoing beam is off-normal. Fig. S.4 shows that birefringent CD can be "strong". The authors should:
   - give the actual incidence and exit angles for each q, and the misalignment tolerance;
   - estimate the residual birefringent CD at those angles from the Fig. S.4 model;
   - characterise the domain state of the measured membrane (for example by PFM on the device or by XRD for a-domain fractions);
   - explain why any residual birefringent CD would not also reverse under field. It could, since 180° switching may change the domain-wall configuration.

12. Only one device is shown. All results come from one membrane and electrode. The reported effect is a small difference signal that is vulnerable to device-specific artefacts such as electrode edges, leakage under bias, and charging. The authors should report whether other devices or membrane thicknesses were measured and whether the sign and magnitude of the switching reproduce. If only one device was measured, they should state this clearly.

13. Several conditions that could produce field-correlated changes are not reported. These include:
   - leakage current under hold bias, and the X-ray photocurrent under the electrode;
   - possible beam-induced oxygen-vacancy formation over 15 h at the O K edge (the XAS before and after should be compared and shown);
   - the sample temperature, and whether it stays within the tetragonal phase given beam and Joule heating;
   - the contribution of the 4 nm Cr / 8 nm Au top electrode, and of any oxidised Cr, to the O K-edge signal and its polarisation.

   A brief quantitative statement on each would strengthen the case that the CD is intrinsic to the BTO lattice.

14. The analogy to gyroelectricity is loose. Eqs. 3–4 are drawn from the electrogyration and gyroelectric response of chiral ferroelectrics such as Pb5Ge3O11. Those materials are optically active at q ≈ 0, whereas BTO (4mm) is achiral and the effect here relies on finite q. Calling the observation a "gyroelectric effect" (abstract, p. 8) needs either a derivation that maps the CD-RIXS observable onto a gyration tensor of the stated form for C4v at finite q, or softer wording.

C. Minor

15. The units of q are inconsistent. The main text quotes q = (−0.12, 0.20, 0.42) "r.l.u." (p. 5), while Table 1 gives the same numbers in Å⁻¹. At 531 eV the maximum momentum transfer is about 0.54 Å⁻¹, so only Å⁻¹ is kinematically possible. Please correct this, and also give the values in r.l.u. of the measured lattice.

16. The Fig. 3a axis is labelled "FY (a.u.)" for RIXS spectra. The spectra are vertically offset, but the offset is not stated. The normalisation (incident flux, acquisition time, or elastic or dd intensity) used before forming the CD should be specified, since the CD magnitude depends on it.

17. The Fig. 3 caption says Savitzky–Golay smoothing is applied "to guide the eye", but Fig. 4f–h integrate "SG smoothed" contrast (Fig. 4a caption). Please state whether the integrated values in Fig. 4f–h use raw or smoothed data, and give the SG window relative to the 30 meV resolution.

18. In Fig. 2c, the colour legend and the '+ - - +' sequence should be made consistent with the text. The time axis break near 4 h hides part of the protocol, so please show the full sequence.

19. The Fig. 2b caption says the loop "yields a measure of coercivity of approx 3 V", but the loop is markedly asymmetric (about −1 V and +4 V). Please give both coercive voltages and the imprint.

20. Eq. 1 uses φ without defining it, and the transfer matrix "Eq. 5 in Methods" is the Stokes polarisation matrix, not the RIXS scattering tensor. Please define the symbols and give the full scattering amplitude used.

21. Wording such as "perfect qualitative agreement" (p. 6), "excellent agreement" (abstract) and "within experimental error" (p. 9) should be replaced by quantitative statements.

22. Typos and other small points:
   - "an orbital magnetic moments" (p. 2);
   - "temporally applied" should be "temporarily" (p. 6);
   - "hexagonal lattice constants" (p. 13);
   - Ref. [52] is dated 2028;
   - the Supplementary file was not available to this reviewer, so statements relying on Figs. S.3–S.11 could not be checked.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a null-symmetry measurement and a temperature control. Measure the switching CD at a q on a mirror plane (J·q = 0), for example q3, and above Tc. Both should show no switching. Together with a sub-coercive-voltage control, this would turn a sign-reversal observation into a convincing attribution.
2. Present a per-scan, per-cycle analysis. Give elastic-peak position and intensity tracking and the CD for each field cycle with uncertainties. Show explicitly that a measured energy shift cannot reproduce the derivative-like contrast.
3. Replace the window-integrated comparison with a global fit. Fit all spectra (both helicities, both field states, all four q, plus the high-resolution data) with resolution-convolved DFT mode intensities and a single CD-to-J·q coupling per sublattice, and report fit quality against alternative models.
4. Strengthen the device characterisation and reproducibility. Give the membrane thickness, in situ P–E or switching-current data from the measured device, and the domain state. Add results from a second device, or state clearly that only one was measured.
