Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports circularly dichroic RIXS (CD-RIXS) at the O K edge on a freestanding BaTiO3 membrane placed in a capacitor, and argues that the sign of the phonon-related CD reverses when the ferroelectric polarization is reversed by an out-of-plane voltage. If it holds, this would be the first demonstration of deterministic, electric-field reversal of phonon angular momentum (PAM) at finite momentum. That is a timely and potentially high-impact result for chiral phononics. It extends earlier CD-RIXS work on quartz and LiNbO3 to a switchable polar system. As it stands, though, the evidence is a small dichroic signal close to the elastic line, measured on a single device, and the link between the measured CD and the computed PAM is only partly specified. The central claim is therefore not yet secured.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The experimental concept is strong and new. It combines in-situ ferroelectric switching with CD-RIXS at a general q, and it builds in two sign reversals (photon helicity and field) plus an azimuthal (q) dependence.
- The measurement protocol (Fig. 2c) is designed with care. It uses a "+ − − +" helicity sequence within each field state to cancel linear drift, and repeated switching cycles over about 16 h.
- Fig. 3b(i) shows CD spectra under the two field states that look roughly like mirror images. Fig. 4a/f–h shows a q-dependence of the field-switching contrast that is qualitatively consistent with the C4v sign pattern of the computed J·q.
- The topic suits a broad readership, and the platform (freestanding BTO on electrodes) is technologically relevant.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The geometry coupling the photon helicity to an in-plane PAM is not explained, and as described it appears self-contradictory.** The text states three things. First, the field-switching measurements "were performed at normal incidence with beam parallel to the optical axis, c" (p. 5; Methods: "largest face perpendicular to c"). Second, the PAM of the relevant modes "is expected to be perpendicular to this axis" and is "projected in-plane (perpendicular to the polarisation axis)" (p. 5). Third, "the outgoing angle is chosen to minimise the component along c". In a CD-RIXS experiment the angular momentum transferred by the incident and outgoing photons is set by their wavevectors. With the incident beam along c, the incident helicity has no projection on an in-plane J. The paper then claims (p. 9) that the CD "will only follow" the in-plane component of q and is "sensitive only to out-of-plane ion displacements". This is a third, different statement, and it is hard to reconcile with Eq. (2) and with J lying in-plane. The quantity compared with the data (J·q, Fig. 4c–h) is also not derived from the RIXS cross-section. Eq. (1) gives A_m with phase factors e^{±2iφ}, but it does not say which axis φ refers to, nor how A_m depends on the incident and scattered k-vectors, q, and J. Without a derivation, the paper does not establish that the measured sign tracks the PAM rather than another P-odd quantity. The authors should:
   - give the full scattering geometry (incident and outgoing k-vectors relative to the crystal axes, the scattering angle 2θ, and the analysed outgoing polarization, if any);
   - derive, at least within a minimal model, how the circular dichroism depends on J for their geometry, and why J·q rather than J·k_in (or J·(k_in − k_out)) is the relevant projection;
   - reconcile the "normal incidence" statement with q_z = 0.42 Å⁻¹ and the stated in-plane components.

2. **The dichroic signal is small, sits largely inside the elastic line, and the artifact checks needed to attribute it to phonons are missing.** In Fig. 3b the largest CD and field-switching features lie between 0 and about 0.05 eV. The resolution is about 30 meV FWHM (Methods), so region I of Fig. 4a (roughly 0–0.03 eV) is essentially inside the elastic peak. The CD features are also derivative-like, with adjacent positive and negative lobes in regions I and II. That shape is what a small relative energy shift or width change of a strong peak produces between the subtracted spectra. Phonon energies themselves are expected to be P-even: time reversal plus the C2 rotation of C4v map ω(q, −P) onto ω(q, +P). So an intrinsic field-induced energy shift is not expected, and a shift between helicities, from beam motion or energy calibration, is the natural alternative explanation. Several further points weaken the case:
   - The error bars are comparable to the signal at many points (Fig. 3b).
   - The data are Savitzky–Golay smoothed for display.
   - The normalization between C+ and C− spectra is not described: incident flux, beam position on the sample for the two undulator helicities, and alignment of the elastic line per scan.
   - Fig. 3b(i) and 3b(ii) are presented as "two observations" (p. 8), but they are algebraically the same combination of the four spectra: CD(+E) − CD(−E) ≡ FS(C+) − FS(C−). They are not independent evidence.

   The authors should:
   - show the four raw spectra I(C±, ±E) overlaid, with fitted elastic-line positions and widths for each, and state the residual shifts in meV;
   - subtract the fitted elastic line before forming differences, and report the CD of the elastic (zero-loss) intensity itself;
   - show the two "even" combinations, the helicity-independent field effect FS(C+) + FS(C−) and the field-independent CD, CD(+E) + CD(−E), to demonstrate that the P-odd, helicity-odd part dominates;
   - quantify significance in each energy window (integrated contrast ± uncertainty, or χ² against zero), using unsmoothed data;
   - show the high-resolution (15 meV) data at the same q, which are mentioned (p. 6) but not presented, ideally with CD and field switching, because they are the best way to separate phonon contrast from the elastic tail.

3. **The ferroelectric state during the RIXS measurement is not verified, and the write voltage appears marginal.** The PFM phase loop in Fig. 2b is strongly asymmetric (imprinted). Switching from negative to positive occurs at about +3.5 to +4.5 V, and the caption quotes a coercivity of "approx 3 V". The write pulse used during RIXS was ±3.5 V (Fig. 2c, Methods), so the positive-state write may be incomplete. The PFM was also done with a tip on the film, not through the 60 µm top electrode used in the beamline. Other points:
   - No switching current, P–E loop, or capacitance measurement acquired in situ at ID32 is shown.
   - There is no post-beamtime PFM showing that the probed area is a single domain in each state.
   - The film thickness of the measured membrane is not given ("various thicknesses", Methods), so the applied fields are unknown.
   - The 50 µm beam footprint is barely smaller than the 60 µm electrode (Fig. 2a, Fig. 3 caption), so part of the probed volume may be unswitched or unelectroded, especially as the beam drifts over 15 h.
   - Possible 90° (a/c) domains in a freestanding membrane are not discussed. They would both dilute the P-odd signal and introduce birefringence.

   The authors should provide:
   - an in-situ switching-current or polarization trace for each write pulse during the experiment;
   - the membrane thickness and the corresponding fields;
   - PFM or XRD evidence of domain population after the beamtime;
   - an estimate of the fraction of the beam footprint inside the electrode.

4. **The quantitative agreement with DFT rests on free scale factors, including a sign, applied to four points per mode.** The abstract claims "excellent agreement" and the text "perfect qualitative agreement" (p. 6). In Fig. 4f–h the measured contrasts are compared with J·q "when scaled accordingly" (p. 9). From the colour coding, the scale factor is positive in panel f: red, negative-J·q points have negative CD contrast. It appears negative in panel g: red points near φ ≈ 1.1 rad have positive contrast of about +1. The sign of the contrast relative to the polarization direction is the key physical prediction, so a per-mode free sign means the comparison tests only the shape of a sinusoid-like curve sampled at four φ values. The q1 point in Fig. 4f is called an outlier and attributed to mode smearing. In panel h the computed J·q (±0.002) is about 50 times smaller than for the 11 and 38 meV modes (±0.1, ±0.06), yet the measured contrast is of comparable magnitude (about 0.75 versus about 2 a.u.). This is not explained if the contrast is proportional to J·q. The mode assignment also has problems:
   - Measured features at about 20 and 40 meV are assigned to computed modes at 11 and 38 meV.
   - An 11 meV mode cannot be resolved from the elastic line at 30 meV resolution.
   - The 100 meV region is at the top of the BTO one-phonon spectrum, where the authors themselves expect multi-phonon contributions (p. 5).

   The authors should:
   - state which parameters are fitted in Fig. 4f–h (scale, sign, offset) and justify any sign difference between modes from the scattering matrix element;
   - provide a goodness-of-fit against a null model, for example a φ-independent or zero contrast;
   - show the computed dispersion and the intensity estimate based on mode effective charge in the main text, with the predicted RIXS line shape convolved with the resolution;
   - temper "excellent agreement" to what the comparison supports.

**B. Should address**

5. **The analogy to gyroelectricity is not justified for C4v.** The discussion (Eqs. 3–4) frames the result as a "gyroelectric effect" in analogy with Pb5Ge3O11, whose point group (3) is gyrotropic. Tetragonal BTO (4mm) is not an optically active class, so the q→0 gyration tensor g_ij in Eq. (4) vanishes by symmetry for a uniform polar state. The authors rely on finite-q (spatially dispersive) effects, which is a different thing. Eq. (3) also defines g_ij using the antisymmetric part of the non-local response, but the notation λ_klj is not defined. They should state precisely which tensor component they mean and its symmetry-allowed form for 4mm at finite q, or drop the "gyroelectric" label from the abstract.

6. **The non-volatility and 15-hour stability claims are not directly demonstrated.** All RIXS data were taken under a ±0.2 V holding bias (Fig. 2c), which the authors say is needed "to ensure the ferroelectric domains remain stable". "Non-volatile" (abstract and p. 3) therefore is not tested: no data are shown at zero bias or after removing the bias. "Stable for at least 15 hours" is inferred from summed data over a sequence of about five switching cycles. The authors should show the CD or field-switching contrast for each cycle separately versus time, ideally including at least one state measured at 0 V. Otherwise they should rephrase these claims.

7. **There is no null or reference measurement in which the effect should be absent.** All data come from one device and one membrane. Useful controls include:
   - q3 = (0, 0.24) lies on a σ_v mirror plane of C4v. There, J must be perpendicular to the mirror and J·q = 0, so q3 is a built-in symmetry null. It is plotted (Fig. 4a, dotted) but not analysed or emphasised. The integrated field-switching contrast at q3, with error bars, should be reported explicitly as a null control.
   - A measurement at a high-symmetry direction, or of a paraelectric or centrosymmetric reference such as an SrTiO3 membrane in the same electrode stack, would rule out electrode-, charging- or birefringence-related helicity effects.
   - The key result should be reproduced on a second device or membrane, or after a thermal cycle.

8. **The argument for mitigating birefringence is qualitative.** The authors acknowledge that birefringence produces strong CD in RIXS (Methods, Fig. S.4) and argue that normal incidence along c removes it. The outgoing beam is not along c, however, and a 4 nm Cr / 8 nm Au top electrode is in the beam path. A birefringence-induced CD should be P-even, since the optic axis is unchanged under P → −P. The P-even CD component (CD(+E) + CD(−E)) is therefore a direct measure of this background and should be shown and quantified (see concern 2). A linear electro-optic contribution under the 0.2 V hold bias would be P-odd and should be estimated.

9. **DFT details relevant to the chirality calculation are missing.** The relaxed structure has c/a = 4.11/3.99 ≈ 1.030, against 4.03/4.00 ≈ 1.0075 experimentally (Methods). PBE overestimates tetragonality, which directly sets the polar distortion and hence the magnitude of the predicted PAM. The paper does not say:
   - how the low-energy (soft-mode-derived) branches were treated, including whether any imaginary frequencies appear;
   - whether the non-analytic (LO–TO, Born effective charge) correction was included at |q| ≈ 0.48 Å⁻¹ (in-plane components of about 0.23 Å⁻¹);
   - whether the PAM was computed at fixed lattice constants equal to the experimental ones.

   Bulk-tetragonal calculations are compared with a free-standing membrane of unspecified thickness. The authors should show the sensitivity of J·q to c/a (for example at the experimental lattice parameters) and state the non-analytic treatment.

10. **Error-bar definitions are inconsistent.** Methods says RIXS error bars are "the standard deviation of individual scans", while the Fig. 3 caption says "estimated from Poisson statistics on re-binned data (by a factor 2)". The number of scans per spectrum, the total counting time per helicity and field state, and how errors propagate into the integrated contrasts in Fig. 4f–h are not given. These matter for judging significance (concern 2).

11. **Measurement temperature and beam-induced changes are not reported.** The sample temperature during RIXS is not stated, though room temperature is implied by "room temperature tetragonal phase". Prolonged soft X-ray exposure at the O K edge under bias can create or migrate oxygen vacancies and alter the leakage or imprint. XAS before and after (Methods mentions XAS "before/during") and the leakage current versus time should be shown, to exclude a slowly evolving electrostatic or defect contribution to the field-switching signal.

12. **The interpretation of the O K-edge matrix element is asserted rather than shown.** The statements that the CD "directly probes the rotation of the 2p orbitals" and "measures the circular motion of the oxygen atoms" (pp. 5–6), and that Ti displacements are not probed, need support. Hybridised Ti 3d–O 2p states are excited at 531.25 eV, and Ti motion modulates them, so the CD may weight Ti and O displacements differently from the mass-weighted J of Eq. (2). The comparison should use an O-projected angular momentum, or the authors should justify using the total J.

**C. Minor**

13. Momentum units are inconsistent. The text (p. 5) gives q = (−0.12, 0.20, 0.42) "r.l.u.", while Table 1 gives the same numbers in Å⁻¹. At 531 eV, |Q|_max ≈ 0.54 Å⁻¹, so 0.42 r.l.u. along c (about 0.65 Å⁻¹) would be inaccessible. Please correct the units and give q also in r.l.u.

14. Methods refers to "hexagonal lattice constants a = 3.99 Å and c = 4.11 Å". This should be tetragonal.

15. Ref. [52] is dated 2028.

16. The Fig. 3 caption mentions "positive electric field (0.2 V)". A voltage is not a field; please give the field (requires the thickness, see concern 3).

17. Fig. 4b labels the angular momentum as ω, which conflicts with ω used for frequency in Eq. (3). Also, "maximal area of the ellipse" as a measure of angular momentum should be defined quantitatively.

18. Fig. 1b is labelled "Energy (meV)" with ±10 meV wells for Ti displacements of ±0.05 Å, "taken from Cohen". Please state the original units and parameters.

19. Language: "temporally applied" should be "temporarily applied" (p. 6); "As an aide" should be "As an aside" (p. 8); "an orbital magnetic moments" (p. 2). "Phonons modes" also appears (p. 6).

20. The abstract claim that CD-RIXS "directly probe[s] the phonon angular momentum" should be qualified, given concern 1.

21. The Fig. 4 caption says contrast "matches the calculated values well for the 3 phonon modes". This should be qualified in light of the q1 outlier and the scaling (concern 4).

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a main-text figure with the raw four-spectrum dataset, elastic-line fits per spectrum, the P-even and helicity-even combinations, and integrated contrasts with significance. This one figure would address most of concerns 2, 8 and 10.
2. Provide a short derivation, in the main text or Methods, of the CD-RIXS dichroism for this geometry, linking it explicitly to the relevant projection of J. Use q3 as a symmetry-required null to support it.
3. Document the ferroelectric state in situ: switching currents per pulse, thickness and fields, and domain imaging after the beamtime. Report at least one state at zero bias to substantiate "non-volatile".
4. Replace the free per-mode scaling in Fig. 4f–h with a forward model: DFT eigenvectors and effective charges, convolved with the instrument resolution, giving predicted CD versus energy for each q. Compare it with the high-resolution data, and report the robustness of the DFT PAM to the c/a ratio.
