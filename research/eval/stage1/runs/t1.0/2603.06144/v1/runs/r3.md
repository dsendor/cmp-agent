Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports circularly dichroic RIXS (CD-RIXS) at the O K edge of a freestanding BaTiO3 membrane in a capacitor geometry. It claims that the low-energy (<0.12 eV) dichroic contrast, attributed to chiral phonons at a generic momentum point, reverses sign when the ferroelectric polarisation is reversed by an out-of-plane field. It also claims that the contrast follows the DFT-computed helicity J·q as the sample azimuth φ is rotated. Deterministic electrical control of phonon angular momentum in a technologically relevant ferroelectric would be a significant and timely step beyond the static observations in quartz and LiNbO3. The experiment, in situ ferroelectric switching combined with CD-RIXS, is novel and hard. However, the signal is small, sits largely on the tail of the elastic line, and is supported by a limited set of controls, so the evidence does not yet match the strength of the claims.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A new and demanding experimental combination: an electrically switchable freestanding ferroelectric membrane measured by CD-RIXS at ID32, with a measurement protocol (Fig. 2c, "+ − − +" helicity sequence, repeated switching) designed to suppress drifts.
- A symmetry-based expectation that can be tested: the CD should reverse with polarisation, with helicity and with the sign of J·q under φ rotation. The paper tests all three (Fig. 3b(i), 3b(ii), Fig. 4).
- First-principles phonon calculations give a mode-resolved helicity map (Fig. 4c–e) with which the φ dependence can be compared.
- The topic, electric-field control of PAM, is of broad current interest, and the data are deposited (ESRF DOI, Zenodo).

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **Possible elastic-line and normalisation artefacts in the difference spectra.** The largest CD and field-switching features (Fig. 3b(i,ii), Fig. 4a, region I) lie within 0–50 meV of the elastic line, where the resolution is ≈30 meV FWHM (Methods). In this range, intensity is dominated by the elastic peak (Fig. 3a, peak ≈1.0–1.5 a.u. against phonon features of ≈0.1–0.4). The difference spectra are bipolar, roughly antisymmetric about ≈20–40 meV. This is the shape a small relative energy shift, width change or intensity-normalisation mismatch of the elastic or quasi-elastic peak would produce. Such shifts are plausible between helicities (different undulator settings, beam position) and between field states (piezoelectric strain of the membrane changing the scattering geometry or footprint; field-induced changes in specular or diffuse elastic scattering). The paper says the elastic position is fixed by fitting the energy-gain side with a half-Lorentzian. It does not show: the fitted elastic positions, widths and amplitudes for each helicity and field state; how spectra were normalised (incident flux, total counts or XAS); or the difference spectra after subtracting the fitted elastic line. The authors should (i) tabulate elastic-line parameters per scan, helicity and field; (ii) show the CD and field-switching spectra after elastic-line subtraction and after deliberate ±few-meV relative shifts, to quantify how much of regions I and II survives; and (iii) state the normalisation explicitly. Without this, the central assignment of the contrast to phonon angular momentum is not secured.

2. **The helicity-even, field-odd component is not shown to vanish.** If the contrast is purely a PAM effect, reversing P must be equivalent to reversing helicity: I(C+, +E) = I(C−, −E), and the helicity-averaged spectrum must be independent of field. Fig. 3b(ii) instead shows that each helicity's spectrum changes on field reversal ("Field switching" for C+ and C− separately). The four combinations shown in 3b(i) and 3b(ii) are algebraically the same two differences rearranged, so 3b(ii) is not independent confirmation of 3b(i). The decisive analysis is missing: the authors should decompose the four spectra into helicity-even/field-even, helicity-odd/field-even, helicity-even/field-odd and helicity-odd/field-odd parts. They should then show that the field-odd helicity-even part (non-chiral field-induced changes, e.g. from strain, electroabsorption or domain-dependent diffuse scattering) is consistent with zero, and that the reported effect is the helicity-odd, field-odd part. They should also show that the helicity-odd, field-even part is small (a residual from birefringence or the instrument).

3. **Ferroelectric switching under the beam, and the domain state, are not verified.** The PFM loop in Fig. 2b is strongly imprinted: one branch switches near −1.5 to −2 V, the other near +3.5 to +4 V. The quoted coercivity is "approx 3 V", and the write pulses are only ±3.5 V. This makes it questionable whether the positive write fully switches the region under the 50 µm spot. In addition:
   - the thickness of the measured membrane is never given ("various thicknesses"), so the field and the ratio of write voltage to coercive field are unknown;
   - PFM and the I–V check were done before the beamtime, not during it, and not on the region probed;
   - the claim in Methods that the film has "a single ferroelectric domain state" is not supported by any measurement;
   - X-ray-induced photocurrents or charging in the beam could depolarise or pin domains during hours-long acquisitions.

   The authors should provide the membrane thickness, switching currents or P–E loops recorded in situ at the beamline (before, during and after the RIXS sequence), an estimate of the switched volume fraction, and the leakage current under the 0.2 V hold. A field-sensitive structural probe measured in the same geometry would also strongly support switching. Possibilities are the XAS linear dichroism or the elastic intensity of a Bragg-sensitive condition.

4. **Statistical significance and reproducibility are not quantified.** The CD amplitudes in Fig. 3b (≈±0.1 a.u.) are comparable to the error bars on many points, and the curves shown are Savitzky–Golay smoothed. Two descriptions of the error bars conflict: the Fig. 3 caption says "Poisson statistics on re-binned data", while Methods says "standard deviation of individual scans". Only one device appears to have been measured. The authors should:
   - report, for each energy region, the integrated contrast with its uncertainty and significance (e.g. in σ);
   - show the contrast for each individual switching cycle in Fig. 2c, to demonstrate that the sign follows the field every time rather than only in the average;
   - state which error estimate is used;
   - present the unsmoothed data in the main figures;
   - ideally, reproduce the switching on a second membrane or a second electrode pad.

**B. Should address**

5. **Mode assignment and the J·q comparison have many free parameters.** The strongest contrasts are at ≈20, 40 and 100 meV and are assigned to computed modes at 11, 38 and 100 meV (p. 7). The 20 vs 11 meV mismatch is not discussed, and with ≈30 meV resolution several modes contribute to each region (the paper itself invokes this for the q1 "outlier"). In Fig. 4f–h, the measured integrated contrast at four φ values is compared with J·q "when scaled accordingly". The scale factor (and its sign) appears to be free for each mode, and the integration windows (shaded regions I–III) are chosen after the fact. With four points, one free scale and a curve that changes sign once, agreement "within experimental error" is a weak test, especially as the q1 point in 4f deviates. The authors should state the free parameters, give a goodness-of-fit, and compare against null alternatives (e.g. a φ-independent contrast, or sin/cos forms not tied to the DFT). They should also fold the computed modes with the resolution and the effective-charge weights ("Supplementary Figure S.11") into a single predicted CD spectrum per q and compare it with the full measured spectrum, not just three windows.

6. **Built-in null and reference controls are under-used.** q3 = (0, 0.24, 0.42) lies on a mirror plane of C4v, where J·q should vanish (white line in Fig. 4c–e). Its field-switching spectrum is omitted from Fig. 4a, and it appears only as a single integrated point in 4f–h. Showing the full q3 spectrum alongside q1, q2 and q4 would be a valuable null test. Further controls would separate the PAM interpretation from other field-odd effects (concern 2):
   - an unpoled or depolarised state;
   - measurement above Tc, or on a non-polar reference membrane (e.g. SrTiO3) in the same electrode stack;
   - a q along a high-symmetry direction at both field polarities.

7. **The DFT model of tetragonal BTO.** At 0 K, PBE/DFT tetragonal BaTiO3 is not the ground state and typically has unstable (imaginary) modes. The Methods report c/a = 4.11/3.99 ≈ 1.03, against the experimental ≈1.0075, so tetragonality and polar displacement are overestimated. Both affect the low-energy mode frequencies and their chirality, precisely the 11 and 38 meV modes on which the assignment rests. The authors should show the full dispersion with any imaginary modes, explain how they were treated, and test the sensitivity of J·q (sign pattern and magnitude) to using the experimental lattice parameters and displacement. They should also clarify whether the membrane, which may be strained or have a different c/a, is in the tetragonal phase at the measurement temperature.

8. **The link from CD-RIXS to phonon angular momentum is assumed rather than derived.** Eq. (1) relates CD to the rotation of O 2p orbitals, and the text then states that "the CD contrast measures the circular motion of the oxygen atoms" and that it should scale with J·q. No RIXS cross-section calculation connects the phonon eigenvectors (O and Ti contributions, effective charges) to the dichroic intensity. Without one, the sign, the relative magnitudes between modes and the J·q scaling are unproven. A model calculation would strengthen the interpretation, even a simple one (e.g. Franck–Condon with circularly displaced O sites using the DFT eigenvectors). At minimum, the assumptions should be stated explicitly. The text also says the oxygen signal is "sensitive only to out-of-plane ion displacements", which seems at odds with the in-plane J being probed; this should be reconciled.

9. **Non-volatility and 15-hour stability are claimed but not shown.** All RIXS data were taken under a 0.2 V hold bias (Fig. 2c), so "non-volatile" control (Abstract, p. 3) is not demonstrated. Either measure at zero bias after poling or drop the claim. The statement that the contrast "remains stable for at least 15 hours" (Abstract, Fig. 3 caption, p. 10) needs a figure: the CD or field-switching amplitude per cycle against time across the 16 h sequence of Fig. 2c.

10. **Other sources of dichroism from the device stack.** The top electrode is 4 nm Cr / 8 nm Au, and Cr adhesion layers readily oxidise. Any Cr oxide, interfacial or adsorbed oxygen contributes to the O K-edge signal at 531.25 eV, the leading edge where such contributions are strongest. The birefringence discussion (p. 5, Methods Eq. 5, Fig. S.4) argues that normal incidence along c removes the effect, but the outgoing beam is far from c and the electrode layers are not addressed. The authors should quantify the O K-edge XAS from a bare electrode region, explain why the electrode cannot produce field-odd dichroism, and give the incidence angle and its tolerance for each q.

**C. Minor**

11. **Momentum units are inconsistent.** On p. 5, q = (−0.12, 0.20, 0.42) is given in "r.l.u.", while Table 1 gives Å⁻¹. At 531 eV the maximum momentum transfer is ≈0.54 Å⁻¹, so qz = 0.42 r.l.u. (≈0.66 Å⁻¹) would be kinematically inaccessible. Please correct and give the scattering angles 2θ and incidence angles used.

12. **Lattice wording in the Methods.** "Hexagonal lattice constants" should read tetragonal. Also comment on the c/a discrepancy noted in concern 7.

13. **The gyroelectric analogy is overstated.** Eq. (4) posits g ∝ Ps. With only two states (±P), linearity in P is not tested. Either rephrase as a sign reversal consistent with a P-odd response, or measure at intermediate (partially switched) states.

14. **Inconsistent colour scales in Fig. 4.** The colour bar of Fig. 4h spans ±0.002, while that of Fig. 4e spans ±0.05 for the same 100 meV mode. The legend of Fig. 4a (q4, q1, q2 with overbars?) should be clarified, and the units of "Δ_AM" in Fig. 4b defined.

15. **Text and wording errors.**
   - p. 3: "applied across the plane of the sample" conflicts with the out-of-plane field geometry.
   - "Temporally" should read "temporarily".
   - "perfect qualitative agreement" (p. 6) and "excellent agreement" (Abstract) are too strong given concern 5.
   - Ref. [52] is dated 2028.
   - Ref. [42] has garbled formatting.

16. **The supplementary material is not provided.** Several key elements are cited only to it: the XAS, the energy optimisation, the birefringence data, the dispersion and effective charges, and Fig. S.11. The main text should carry enough of them to support the central claims, particularly the birefringence check and the energy optimisation that fixed the 531.25 eV operating point.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a four-component (helicity × field) decomposition figure, with elastic-subtracted spectra and integrated contrasts with σ-significance per region. This would turn Fig. 3 from suggestive to convincing.
2. Document switching in situ (P–E loops or switching currents at ID32, membrane thickness, leakage under hold). Add a time series of contrast per switching cycle over the 15 h, plus a zero-bias measurement to support non-volatility.
3. Compute a resolution-convolved, effective-charge-weighted CD spectrum from the DFT eigenvectors for each measured q, including the mirror-plane q3 as a null. Compare it with the full spectra, using a stated and minimal set of scale parameters.
4. If beamtime allows, add a reference measurement: a non-polar membrane, the paraelectric phase, or an unpoled state. This would rule out field-odd contrast unrelated to phonon chirality.
