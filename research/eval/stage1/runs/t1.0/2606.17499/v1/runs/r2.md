Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript uses low-temperature STM/STS and spin-polarized STS (Cr/Fe-coated tips, ±1 T) to look at near-zero-energy in-gap states at four kinds of site on cleaved FeTe0.55Se0.45: a step edge, a Type I wrinkle, a Type II line defect with a bend, and a defect-free region with a donut-shaped zero-bias peak (ZBP), which the authors attribute to subsurface impurities. It concludes that all of them are trivial Yu-Shiba-Rusinov (YSR) states or coupled-YSR ensembles and not Majorana modes. A spin-resolved check of zero-energy states in Fe(Te,Se) is timely, because reported Majorana signatures in this material are disputed and spin-resolved studies are still scarce. The novelty depends on whether the spin-polarized data are free of field-induced and set-point artifacts, and on whether the conclusions for each defect type are actually supported by spin-resolved data. As submitted, they mostly are not.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A useful catalogue of the ways zero-bias features near structural and subsurface defects in Fe(Te,Se) can imitate Majorana signatures. This is a cautionary point that matters to the community.
- One study combines several diagnostics: spatial dispersion of the peak energy, phase-shift analysis of the lattice across the Type II defect (Fig. 4c,d) and spin-polarized tunneling.
- The opposite sign of the spin contrast at positive and negative bias at the line-defect bend (Fig. 4f) would, if robust, be a direct fingerprint of YSR physics. It is the kind of evidence the field needs.
- Model calculations of coupled YSR impurities (Fig. 4g–i, Fig. 2f) give the interpretation a concrete framework.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The spin-polarized contrast in Fig. 4f and Fig. 5k is not separated from field-induced and set-point artifacts. The SP-STS is done by comparing spectra at +1 T and −1 T (Fig. 4f) or ±1.2 T (Fig. 5k). These fields are perpendicular to the surface of a type-II superconductor with a small vortex spacing (about 45 nm at 1 T). Vortex positions are not guaranteed to be the same after the field is reversed, and a vortex a few tens of nm away changes the in-gap DOS through supercurrent (Doppler) shifts and quasiparticle leakage. Three further problems apply:
   - Spectra are normalized by a set point at −10 mV / −300 pA (Fig. 4f). Any spin polarization of the integrated normal-state LDOS between 0 and −10 mV therefore rescales the whole spectrum between field directions. This set-point effect can create an apparent bias-dependent "polarization", including a sign change.
   - The repeat +1 T curve at position 1 differs from the first +1 T curve by an amount comparable to the +1 T/−1 T difference, especially between −1 and 0 mV. Position 3 shows only −1 T and +1 T, not the three-step sequence the text describes.
   - In Fig. 5k the two +1.2 T values at P1 (≈1.98 and ≈1.72) differ by about as much as +1.2 T differs from −1.2 T at its lower value. No error bars or full spectra are given.

   Why it matters: the claim that the bend and the subsurface P1 states are spin-polarized YSR states, which is the central new result, rests entirely on these differences.

   What to do:
   - Show the same ±1 T sequence with a non-magnetic (W or PtIr) tip at the same positions, or with the same spin-polarized tip on a nearby bulk-gap region and on a known Fe adatom YSR state as positive and negative references.
   - Map the vortex lattice (zero-bias map at low magnification) at +1 T and −1 T for every SP-STS data set, so the distance to the nearest vortex is known.
   - Analyze set-point dependence: repeat at several set-point voltages, or normalize by the conductance outside the gap.
   - Report the asymmetry A = (dI/dV↑ − dI/dV↓)/(sum) with statistical error from repeated sweeps.
   - The Methods say the Cr tip is "dipped" into Fe on W(110) to make it switchable. Show that the tip magnetization actually reverses between ±1 T and does not switch at lower fields during the sequence. Fig. S7/S8 is cited but not available in this submission.

2. The spin-polarized evidence does not cover the step edge and the Type I wrinkle, and for P2 it gives a null result. The text nevertheless draws firm conclusions for all of them. The abstract says "by using spin-polarized scanning tunneling spectroscopy, we demonstrate that the near-zero-energy localized states on step edges and line defects originate from topologically trivial YSR states". However:
   - The step-edge analysis (Fig. 2) is spin-averaged STS plus a toy model.
   - The wrinkle analysis (Fig. 3a–d) rests on one cut showing two symmetric peaks, with no field or spin dependence at all.
   - For P2 the authors themselves say the spin sensitivity may be reduced and "other possible explanations … may exist" (p. 10). The Discussion and abstract still conclude that "none of them can be explained by MBS".

   An absent spin contrast at P2 (Fig. 5l, changes of a few percent with no error bars and a field sequence different from P1's) cannot discriminate MBS from a trivial state when the sensitivity of the measurement is unknown.

   What to do: either add SP-STS (with the controls in concern 1) at the step-edge ZBP (path B point 0) and at the wrinkle hotspot, or restrict the spin-based conclusions to the bend and P1. State explicitly that the step-edge, wrinkle and P2 assignments are inferred from spatial structure and models. Quantify the expected MBS spin polarization at P2 for the measured tip polarization, so a null result can be judged against a definite detection threshold.

3. The step-edge "splitting" is at or below the energy resolution, so the two-YSR decomposition is not established. Fig. 2e,f uses peaks at ±0.14 mV to model the ZBP and its apparent splitting. At T = 1.1 K the thermal broadening of a normal-metal tip is about 3.5 kBT ≈ 0.33 meV FWHM, so features 0.28 meV apart are not resolvable as separate peaks. The fit uses two different coupling strengths for spectra 1–2 and 3–6, and peak heights proportional to the data, so its agreement with the data has little discriminating power. The argument against an MBS, a "spectral weight transfer from negative to positive energy", is also not unique to YSR. Asymmetric weight can come from tip DOS, set-point normalization, or a nearby but distinct state.

   Why it matters: this is the basis for "the ZBP at the step edge results from a summation of two YSR peaks".

   What to do:
   - State the effective energy resolution (including Vosc and electron temperature) and fit the measured lineshapes with resolution-convolved models, comparing a single zero-energy peak against two split peaks with a goodness-of-fit criterion.
   - Give the number of free parameters.
   - Add magnetic-field and temperature dependence of the step-edge ZBP. A YSR doublet and an MBS respond differently to field: Zeeman splitting/shift versus pinning.
   - Ideally, check another step edge.

B. Should address

4. The logic linking the phase shift to non-topology needs qualifying, and its uncertainty is not given. The text concludes from a ≈0.5π shift for Bragg peak 1 and ≈0 for peak 2 (Fig. 4c,d; Fig. S6) that a π-shift Fu-Kane-type dispersing Majorana mode is excluded. Please:
   - give the phase-shift value with an uncertainty from the histogram in Fig. S6b;
   - describe how the lock-in phase method's reference region and averaging length were chosen;
   - explain why a single π/2 shift along one lattice direction excludes all 1D Majorana mechanisms, rather than only the specific π-junction scenario of Ref. [57].

   The Discussion should be narrowed to "inconsistent with the π-phase-shift scenario".

5. Gap filling at the bend (Fig. 3g,i) has other explanations that are not tested. The flat, filled gap within about 4 nm of the bend could come from a local suppression of superconductivity: strain, a grain boundary, or a Te/Se compositional change. It need not come from an ensemble of coupled subsurface Fe impurities (Fig. 4h,k; Fig. S10). The impurity model (Fig. 4h: number, positions, couplings) appears to be constructed to reproduce the data, and there is no independent evidence of subsurface Fe at the bend. What to do:
   - show gap-size/coherence-peak maps across the bend;
   - give the temperature dependence of the gap at the bend compared with the bulk;
   - compare the density of impurities assumed in the model with the excess-Fe content (the composition is given as FeTe0.55Se0.45 without the excess-Fe fraction y);
   - state which model parameters were fitted.

6. The "tip gating" explanation for the peak dispersion along line C (Fig. 5i,j) is asserted, not tested. Tip-induced band bending predicts a specific dependence on set-point current and voltage, that is, on tip height. Please show spectra at P1 for several set points. If the peak shifts with tip height, gating is supported. Otherwise another mechanism, such as spatial variation of the exchange coupling, should be considered. The inference about P2's depth from its "lower zero-energy DOS" is also qualitative. A depth estimate from the spatial decay or the donut size would make it concrete.

7. The argument excluding Andreev bound states is physically incorrect as stated. On p. 6 the text says Fe(Te,Se) "is an s±-wave superconductor with no quasiparticles inside the gap causing Andreev reflection" and that ABS "require two reflection walls". Andreev reflection does not require in-gap quasiparticles. More importantly, in a sign-changing s± superconductor, scattering at a surface or step that mixes the electron and hole pockets can create in-gap Andreev surface bound states without a second wall. This mechanism is a real competitor at step edges and should be addressed properly, for example through the dependence on step orientation or through model calculations.

8. The statistics, number of samples and reproducibility are not reported. Each defect type appears to be shown at one location: one step edge, one wrinkle hotspot, one bend, one donut region. Please report:
   - how many cleaves, samples and tips were used;
   - how many step edges, wrinkles, bends and subsurface ZBP sites were examined, and in what fraction each type of spectrum appears;
   - whether the SP-STS results at the bend and at P1 were reproduced with a second tip or at a second site.

   Without this, the general conclusion that ZBPs at these defect classes "cannot be attributed to Majorana" rests on single examples.

9. The comparison with vortex ZBPs and the absence of a field-dependence test at P1/P2 should be addressed. The ZBP at P1/P2 has a HWHM of about 0.5 mV, which the text calls "comparable to the ZBP observed in vortex cores at lower temperature". The dependence of the ZBP energy and width on field magnitude (0–3 T, available in this setup) and temperature is not shown. Zeeman splitting or shift of the P1/P2 peak would be a strong, tip-independent discriminator between YSR and MBS, and would complement the spin-polarized data.

10. The models are not documented in the submission. Note 3 of the SI and Figs. S2, S3, S10 and S11 underlie Figs. 2f, 4g,i and the P2 "model fit", but the SI is not available here. The main text gives no Hamiltonian, exchange or potential scattering values, tip polarization, or broadening for the model in Fig. 4i. The comparison between Fig. 4i and Fig. 4f is called "qualitative", and the two model positions A and B (Fig. 4h) are not mapped onto positions 1–3 of Fig. 4e. Please give the model parameters, state which were fitted, and show model and data on the same axes.

C. Minor

11. Fig. 2b: the y-axis reads "Model DOS (arb. u.)", but the caption describes measured dI/dV spectra at point 0 of A and B. Please correct the label, or clarify if this is a model.

12. Fig. 2: the caption gives 61 spectra along A and 60 along B over 12 nm, while the arrows in Fig. 2a are labeled 0 to 60. Fig. 2e plots 0–8 nm. Please make the spectrum counts, lengths and indices consistent, and mark the spectrum numbers 1–6 used in Fig. 2f on Fig. 2d.

13. p. 9: "Full-Width at Half-Maximum (HWHM)" is inconsistent. State which one is meant and how it was extracted, including the fit function and resolution.

14. Fig. 5 caption: "STM topography of the surface impurity region" contradicts the text, which describes a region "with no surface defects". The line lengths in the caption (12, 14 and 12 nm) do not match the about 10 nm axis range in Fig. 5j.

15. Fig. 4g uses meV on the energy axis while the other panels use mV. In Fig. 4i, the ±Δ/e markers should state the value of Δ used.

16. Fig. 4f: the dash-dot lines at about ±0.8 mV are not explained in the caption. The text says the contrast is confined to ±0.8 mV. Please indicate this explicitly.

17. Methods: the excess-Fe content y of Fe1+yTe0.55Se0.45, how Tc was determined, and the electron temperature are not given. Please also give the tip polarization obtained from the calibration.

18. The phrase "The ZBP in line B as if splits" (p. 5) and "splitted" should be reworded. The Introduction's claims about quantum "supremacy" in factoring and search could be trimmed.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add non-magnetic-tip and same-tip reference measurements under the identical ±B sequence, together with vortex maps, so the spin contrast in Figs. 4f and 5k can be separated from field and set-point effects. This is the single change that most strengthens the paper.
2. Use field-magnitude and temperature dependence of the ZBPs at the step edge, at P1 and at P2 as a tip-independent discriminator alongside the SP-STS.
3. Narrow the abstract and Discussion to what the spin-resolved data show (the bend and P1). Present the step-edge, wrinkle and P2 assignments as model-based interpretations.
4. Report statistics across several defects of each type and several tips, and include the full model parameters in the main text or Methods.
