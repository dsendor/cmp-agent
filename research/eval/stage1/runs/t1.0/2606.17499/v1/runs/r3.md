Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports STM/STS of near-zero-energy in-gap states at three kinds of defect on cleaved FeTe0.55Se0.45: step edges, Type I wrinkles and Type II line-defect bends, plus defect-free regions with subsurface impurities. It uses spin-polarized STS with a switchable Fe-coated Cr tip to argue that these states are trivial YSR-type states rather than Majorana bound states or dispersing Majorana modes. A systematic, spin-resolved test of zero-bias features in Fe(Te,Se) beyond vortex cores would be timely, and it bears on several high-profile Majorana claims in this material (for example the line-defect dispersing mode of Ref. 57). As presented, however, the paper is mostly a set of single case studies. The spin-resolved evidence covers only part of what the abstract claims, and the key field-reversal measurements lack the controls needed to show that the contrast is spin contrast.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The paper tackles an important and controversial question (are zero-bias features in Fe(Te,Se) topological?) with a technique, SP-STS on a superconductor, that few groups can apply.
- It surveys several defect types in one material and one setup, with high-quality topography and dense line spectroscopy (Figs. 2, 3, 5).
- It analyses the lattice phase shift at the Type II bend with the method of Ref. 41, finding about 0.5π for one Bragg peak and about 0 for the other (Fig. 4c,d). This is directly relevant to the π-shift interpretation of Ref. 57.
- The observation in Fig. 4f of a spin contrast that changes sign between positive and negative bias is, if it is confirmed to be spin contrast, a clear qualitative fingerprint that separates YSR-like states from a zero-energy MBS.
- The authors are open about the limits of the point-2 analysis (p. 10). This honesty should carry over into the abstract and conclusions.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The field-reversal SP-STS does not separate tip-spin contrast from field effects on the sample (Figs. 4f, 5k,l).**
   - *What the paper does.* The tip magnetization is reversed by reversing the external out-of-plane field (+1 T, −1 T, +1 T in Fig. 4f; ±1.2 T in Fig. 5k), and each spectrum is recorded in that same field.
   - *Why this is not enough.* At ±1 T, Fe(Te,Se) is in the mixed state, with a vortex spacing of roughly 45–50 nm. Vortices may sit at different positions after each field reversal and can pin at the very defects studied, which would change the local in-gap spectrum. Field-history effects (trapped flux, hysteresis) and the tip's own response could do the same, all without any spin contrast.
   - *What the data show.* The reproducibility check is not convincing. In Fig. 4f, position 1, the two +1 T curves (red and pink) differ visibly, by an amount similar to the ±1 T difference over parts of the ±0.8 mV window. Position 3 has only one +1 T curve (−1 T, then +1 T). In Fig. 5k the two +1.2 T values (≈1.98 and ≈1.72) and the two −1.2 T values (≈1.57 and ≈1.40) scatter by 0.17–0.26, against a ±field difference of about 0.3–0.4. No error bars are given.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Repeat the measurement with a non-spin-polarized (W or PtIr) tip, or with the Cr tip not coated with Fe, through the same field sequence at the same atomic positions.
     - Image vortex positions, for example with zero-bias maps over a larger area, at each field step.
     - Repeat each field polarity several times and give the statistical spread.
     - Show the same asymmetry analysis on a defect-free reference area, where no spin contrast is expected.
   - *Why it matters.* Without these controls, the central SP-STS claim of the abstract ("we demonstrate…") rests on an effect that sample-side physics could also produce.

2. **The abstract and Discussion claim more for step edges and wrinkles than the measurements support.**
   - *What is claimed.* The abstract states that "by using spin-polarized scanning tunneling spectroscopy, we demonstrate that the near-zero-energy localized states on step edges and line defects originate from topologically trivial YSR states."
   - *What was measured.* Spin-resolved data are shown only for the Type II bend (Fig. 4f) and the subsurface impurities (Fig. 5k,l). The step-edge assignment (Fig. 2) and the wrinkle assignment (Fig. 3a–d) rest only on non-spin-polarized spectra and a phenomenological toy model.
   - *The untested alternative.* The YSR (magnetic) assignment is not unique. Fe(Te,Se) is argued to be s±, and in an s± superconductor nonmagnetic scatterers, including edges and structural defects, can also bind in-gap states. In this situation the defining test of magnetism is exactly the spin- or field-resolved measurement that is missing for these two defect types. The step-edge "unpaired electrons" model of Fig. 1j is likewise not supported by any measurement.
   - *What the authors should do.* Either:
     - provide SP-STS (with the controls of concern 1) and field dependence at the step edge (Fig. 2, path B) and at the wrinkle hotspot (Fig. 3b,d); or
     - restate these conclusions as "consistent with" and change the abstract accordingly.

3. **The claim that the defect-free-region ZBPs "cannot be attributed to the presence of Majorana bound states" (abstract) and that "none of them can be explained by MBS" (Discussion) is not supported, especially for point 2.**
   - *Point 1.* The argument is the bias-dependent shift along line C, and this shift appears only in the last ~2 nm of the line (yellow region in Fig. 5i,j).
   - *Point 2.* The peak does not disperse, so the case rests entirely on the absence of a field response in Fig. 5l. The authors themselves note (p. 10) that a deeper defect reduces spin sensitivity, so a null spin result at point 2 is not discriminating.
   - *Mismatched protocols.* The field protocols for P1 and P2 differ. P1 used ±1.2 T reversals; P2 used a ramp of 0, −0.2, −0.6, −1.2, +0.2, +0.6, +1.2 T. The small-field steps may lie below the tip's switching field, which is not stated in the main text.
   - *A missing scenario.* The paper does not address the published proposal that interstitial Fe in Fe(Te,Se) can host zero-energy modes without a vortex ("quantum anomalous vortex" scenarios; cf. Ref. 47). For that scenario, a magnetic subsurface impurity is the expected host and not evidence against MBS.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Measure P2 with the same ±1.2 T reversal protocol as P1, repeated several times.
     - Show full spectra, not only the zero-bias value, at each field.
     - Discuss the anomalous-vortex scenario explicitly.
     - Bring the abstract and Discussion in line with the cautious statement already on p. 10.

**B. Should address**

4. **The step-edge "ZBP splitting" is at or below the energy resolution, and the toy-model "fit" is partly circular (Fig. 2d–f).**
   - *Resolution.* The reported split peaks sit at ±0.14 mV, a separation of 0.28 mV. At T = 1.1 K the thermal broadening alone is about 3.5 k_BT ≈ 0.33 mV, before lock-in broadening and any electron-temperature excess.
   - *Circularity.* The model in Fig. 2f takes two Lorentzians at fixed ±0.14 mV with heights set "proportional to the first 6 points shown in e". It therefore reproduces the measured intensities by construction.
   - *Free parameters.* The text says two different coupling strengths were used for spectra 1–2 and 3–6. That is free parameters added to fit six spectra.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - State the effective energy resolution, for example from the gap edge of a known reference.
     - Show that the zero-energy peak's width or shape changes significantly along path B, compared with a single peak broadened by the same instrument function.
     - Report all model parameters and how many were fitted.
     - Clarify the claim of "spectral weight transfer from negative to positive energy" (p. 6). In Fig. 2e the ±0.14 mV traces decay together.

5. **The arguments against Andreev bound states and topological edge states are not sound (p. 6).**
   - *The quasiparticle argument.* The statement that an s±-wave superconductor has "no quasiparticles inside the gap causing Andreev reflection" does not address the relevant physics. For a sign-changing gap, interfaces and nonmagnetic defects can produce in-gap Andreev-type bound states.
   - *The second-wall argument.* The requirement of "two reflection walls" applies to a particular confined geometry, not to edge states in general.
   - *The topological-edge-state argument.* Excluding topological edge states because peaks are seen "instead of an enhanced DOS within the whole superconducting gap" overlooks finite-size effects. It also overlooks the step-edge geometry here: a 1.23 nm step of two triple layers, not a true sample boundary.
   - *What the authors should do.* Revise these arguments or support them with data, for example the dependence on step height across several edges, or on edge orientation.

6. **The Fig. 3b map and the Fig. 3d spectra appear to disagree, and "clearly rules out" is too strong (p. 7).**
   - *The apparent inconsistency.* Fig. 3b shows a "very pronounced" zero-energy enhancement in the white box. Yet the line spectra across that region (Fig. 3d) show "two peaks with symmetrical energy relative to zero". The authors should explain how a pair of finite-energy peaks gives a pronounced 0 mV map signal. One possibility is that the line in Fig. 3c misses the hotspot; another is energy resolution. A spectrum taken exactly at the hotspot should be shown.
   - *The inference from inhomogeneity.* The conclusion that inhomogeneity "clearly rules out the existence of a topological dispersing mode" is too strong. Disorder or a varying local phase shift along a wrinkle could make any 1D mode inhomogeneous.
   - *What the authors should do.* Add the lattice phase-shift analysis (as in Fig. 4c,d) for the Type I wrinkle, or soften the statement.

7. **The "tip gating" explanation for the point-1 dispersion is asserted, not tested (Fig. 5i,j; p. 10).** Tip-induced band bending predicts a dependence of the peak energy on stabilization setpoint (V_stab, I_stab) and tip height. All Fig. 5 spectra are taken at one setpoint (−40 mV, −200 pA). The authors should repeat line C at two or three setpoints. The inference that point 2 is deeper (from its lower zero-bias DOS) is also indirect: the two points share the same color scale but have different spectral shapes (Fig. 5c). Supporting evidence, such as the spatial extent and symmetry of each feature, should be added.

8. **The SP-STS normalization and setpoint effect are not addressed.**
   - *The problem.* Spectra are stabilized at V_stab = −10 mV with a spin-polarized tip. Any spin polarization of the integrated current from 0 to −10 mV changes the tip height between field polarities, rescaling the whole spectrum.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Show the full-range spectra for both polarities.
     - Show that the background and the coherence peaks overlap after normalization.
     - Quantify the asymmetry (dI/dV↑ − dI/dV↓)/(dI/dV↑ + dI/dV↓) with uncertainties as a function of energy.
   - *Why it matters.* The claimed sign reversal of polarization within ±0.8 mV (Fig. 4f) and the non-zero crossing energy at positions 1 and 2 (p. 9) are sensitive to this effect. The crossing energies should be quoted with error bars.

9. **The models are under-specified and only qualitative (Figs. 2f, 4g–i; S2, S3, S10, S11).**
   - *Assumptions not justified.* The models assume a single-band s-wave superconductor (Fig. 4g), whereas the YSR physics of multiband s± Fe(Te,Se) differs. They also assume ferromagnetic coupling between impurities, and impurity configurations (Fig. 4h) that are not tied to any observed structure.
   - *Parameters and agreement not reported.* The main text does not give the exchange couplings, the inter-impurity distances, the number of impurities, or how these were chosen. The agreement is described as "qualitative", and the curves in Fig. 4i do not obviously resemble Fig. 4f (for example, the large peaks near ±Δ in Fig. 4i, panel A).
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Tabulate the parameters.
     - State which were fitted.
     - Show that a nonmagnetic or antiferromagnetic configuration fails to reproduce the data. Otherwise the model does not discriminate between scenarios.

10. **Statistics and reproducibility are not given.** Each defect class is represented by essentially one example: one step edge in Fig. 2, one wrinkle hotspot, one bend, and one P1/P2 pair. The authors should state:
    - how many step edges, wrinkles, bends and subsurface ZBP sites were examined;
    - in how many samples and with how many tips;
    - what fraction showed each behaviour (ZBP versus split peaks, presence of spin contrast).

    Without these numbers, the general claim that such features "could be misidentified as Majorana zero modes" and are trivial is a statement about selected instances.

11. **The phase-shift result needs uncertainties and a clearer link to the topological criterion (Fig. 4c,d; Fig. S6).**
    - *Uncertainty.* "A phase shift near 0.5π" should be given with an uncertainty and a distribution width.
    - *Robustness.* The authors should discuss how sensitive the result is to the choice of Bragg-peak window and reference lattice.
    - *The criterion.* They should state explicitly which displacement, and of which sublattice, the π criterion of Ref. 57 concerns, and how a shift of 0.5π along only one reciprocal-lattice direction maps onto it.
    - *What it shows.* A non-π shift argues against one specific mechanism. On its own it does not establish triviality, and the text should say so.

12. **Field and temperature dependences that would help discriminate are missing.** None of the ZBPs (step edge path B, point 2) is shown as a function of field magnitude or temperature. A YSR pair would shift or split with Zeeman energy, whereas an MBS stays pinned. The rate at which the peaks broaden with temperature also tests whether the step-edge "splitting" is genuine. The authors should add spectra at several fields (0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3 T) and temperatures at P2 and at the step-edge position.

**C. Minor**

13. Fig. 2b: the y-axis reads "Model DOS (arb. u.)" and the x-axis "Energy (mV)", but the caption describes measured dI/dV spectra at point 0. Correct the labels.
14. p. 9: "Full-Width at Half-Maximum (HWHM) near 0.5 mV". State whether this is the FWHM or the HWHM. When comparing with the vortex ZBP of Ref. 58, note that the temperatures differ.
15. Fig. 4f: the caption says the spectra were taken "at 1T, −1T and 1T again", but position 3 lacks the first +1 T curve. Explain why.
16. Fig. 4g uses "meV", while the rest of the paper uses mV for bias. Make the units consistent.
17. Fig. 2e: define the normalization ("Nor. dI/dV").
18. Fig. 2f: state how the "temperature broadening" was implemented, given that the peaks are described as Lorentzians.
19. The tip switching field, the degree of spin polarization, and the calibration (Figs. S7, S8) are central to the paper and should be summarized in the main text or Methods. The Supplementary Information was not available for this review, so the models in Note 3 and Figs. S2, S3, S6 and S9–S11 could not be assessed.
20. Methods: specify the lock-in modulation amplitude (V_osc = 0.03 mV appears only in the captions) and the setpoint conductance for each dataset. State whether multiple samples or cleaves were used.
21. Language: "splitted" (p. 5); "the ZBP in line B as if splits" (p. 5); "explained latterly" (p. 5); "more far away" (p. 10); "Enhanced in-gap density of state" (section heading, p. 6). Also, the introduction's statement that Majorana states are "stored in a particle number space" should be rephrased as fermion parity.
22. Fig. 1 caption: "The symmetry of the bound states do not need to be three-fold" — the verb should be "does". The caption should also explain why three-fold symmetry is drawn in Fig. 1b.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Build the paper around a consistent, controlled SP-STS protocol, applied to every defect class with the same field sequence and repeated reversals. Include a non-magnetic-tip control and a defect-free reference, and quantify the asymmetry with error bars.
2. Add the full field and temperature dependence of the zero-bias features at P2 and at the step edge. Track Zeeman shifts or splitting and vortex positions, to provide a discriminator that does not rely on tip spin.
3. Make the models quantitative: give all parameters and indicate which are fitted. Use a multiband or s± description where it matters, and show which alternative configurations (nonmagnetic, antiferromagnetic) fail.
4. Rescope the abstract and Discussion to what is demonstrated. The bend and P1 show evidence against an MBS origin. The step edge, the wrinkle and P2 are consistent with trivial states but not proven to be. Add statistics on how many sites of each type were examined.
