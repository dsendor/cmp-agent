Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use STM/STS and spin-polarized STS (SP-STS) at 1.1 K on FeTe0.55Se0.45 to look at near-zero-energy in-gap states at four kinds of site: step edges, strain wrinkles (Type I line defects), a bent Type II line defect, and defect-free regions above presumed subsurface impurities. They argue that none of these states are Majorana modes and attribute them to single or coupled Yu-Shiba-Rusinov (YSR) states. The central idea is to use spin resolution, especially the particle-hole antisymmetric spin polarization expected for YSR states, to tell trivial zero-bias peaks (ZBPs) from Majorana ones. This is timely given the open debate over ZBPs and line-defect modes in Fe(Te,Se). However, spin-resolved data exist for only two of the four defect classes, and the logic of the field-reversal measurement is not yet tight enough for the title's claim of "distinguishing" the two.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Surveying several defect classes in one material with one apparatus shows how common near-zero-energy states are in Fe(Te,Se). This is a useful caution for the field.
- The Type II bend is examined in several complementary ways: zero-energy maps, line spectra across and along the defect (Fig. 3f-i), a phase-shift analysis following Ref. [41] (Fig. 4c,d), and SP-STS at atomically defined positions (Fig. 4e,f). The sign reversal of the tip-polarization contrast between positive and negative bias at the bend (Fig. 4f) is the strongest evidence in the paper for a YSR-like origin.
- The defect-free ZBP (Fig. 5) is mapped spatially in three directions with peak-energy tracking (Fig. 5j), and the authors openly say the origin of the point-2 ZBP remains unresolved.
- The approach could become a practical protocol, if its logic is made rigorous.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The headline claim about step edges and wrinkles is not supported by spin-resolved data. The abstract states that "by using spin-polarized scanning tunneling spectroscopy, we demonstrate that the near-zero-energy localized states on step edges and line defects originate from topologically trivial Yu-Shiba-Rusinov states." The main text shows SP-STS only at the Type II bend (Fig. 4f) and at the two subsurface sites (Fig. 5k,l).
   - Step edges (Fig. 2): the YSR assignment rests on non-spin-resolved line spectra and a toy model.
   - Type I wrinkle (Fig. 3a-d): it rests only on "two peaks with symmetrical energy", which is a generic signature.
   - Resolution of the step-edge splitting: the model uses Lorentzians at ±0.14 mV (Fig. 2f). At T = 1.1 K the thermal resolution is about 3.5 k_BT ≈ 0.33 mV, so a ±0.14 mV splitting is below what the measurement can resolve.
   - Consequence: the claim that the step-edge "ZBP splits" into two YSR peaks (Fig. 2d, first 6 spectra) is an interpretation of peak shape and weight, not a resolved splitting.

   The authors should either:
   - add SP-STS and field-dependent (non-spin-polarized, 0 to 3 T) spectra at the step edge (path B, Fig. 2a) and at the wrinkle hot spot (white box, Fig. 3a,b); or
   - limit the abstract and Discussion to what the data show for these two defect classes.

2. The field-reversal protocol mixes up tip reversal, sample-spin reversal and other field effects, and this undermines both the positive result (bend, P1) and the null result (P2). The tip is switched by applying ±1 T (Fig. 4f) or ±1.2 T (Fig. 5k,l) perpendicular to the sample.
   - If the moments of the impurities responsible for the in-gap states are paramagnetic and follow the field, then tip and sample reverse together, and no SP contrast is expected even for a strongly spin-polarized state.
   - Ref. [59], which the authors cite for MBS spin polarization, gives a vortex MBS spin set by the field direction. A field-induced MBS would therefore also reverse together with the tip.
   - So the absence of contrast at P2 (Fig. 5l) does not show that the P2 ZBP is not a spin-polarized MBS. Conversely, the contrast at the bend and at P1 requires the impurity moments to be pinned (anisotropy or coupling), which the paper never shows.

   In addition, at ±1 T the sample is in the mixed state, with a vortex spacing of roughly 45-50 nm. Vortices can relocate between field sweeps and change the local DOS through supercurrents and nearby vortex-core states. The setpoint normalization (V_stab = -10 mV or -40 mV) also carries its own spin-polarized background.

   The authors should:
   - repeat the identical field sequence at the same sites with a non-magnetic or unpolarized tip, to isolate field-induced changes that are not spin contrast;
   - image vortex positions at each field step, or confirm that no vortex is within a few coherence lengths;
   - state explicitly whether the impurity moments are expected to be pinned or field-following, and what each scenario (YSR or MBS) predicts for the contrast;
   - rewrite the P2 conclusion accordingly.

3. The SP contrast is not quantified against noise or reproducibility.
   - Fig. 4f: the spectra are noisy, and the claimed contrast within ±0.8 mV is comparable to the trace-to-trace scatter. At position 3 the two traces differ by a nearly rigid offset over the whole ±2 mV window, which points to setpoint or normalization effects rather than in-gap spin polarization.
   - Position 3 lacks the first +1 T trace, although the caption says spectra were taken "at 1T, -1T and 1T again".
   - Fig. 5k: the P1 zero-energy DOS values are about 1.57, 1.98, 1.40 and 1.72 for -1.2, +1.2, -1.2 and +1.2 T. Both levels drift downward by roughly 10-15% between the first and second cycles, and there are only two cycles and no error bars.
   - Fig. 5l uses a different field sequence (0, ±0.2, ±0.6, ±1.2 T), so P1 and P2 cannot be compared directly.

   The authors should:
   - show asymmetry spectra A(V) = (g+ - g-)/(g+ + g-) with uncertainties obtained from repeated spectra;
   - perform several reversal cycles at each site, using the same field sequence at P1 and P2;
   - report the tip's spin contrast on a reference (for example an Fe adatom YSR state, as in Ref. [36]) immediately before and after each data set, to rule out tip changes.

4. No positive control, so no demonstrated discriminating criterion. The title promises to distinguish Majorana zero modes from trivial states. The paper shows only cases it classifies as trivial, and the criterion used changes from site to site:
   - peak splitting (step edge);
   - spatial localization along the defect (wrinkle);
   - phase shift ≠ π plus SP sign reversal (bend);
   - dispersion and SP response (Fig. 5).

   Without a measurement of what a candidate MBS looks like in SP-STS with the same tip and protocol, the reader cannot judge whether the method would ever return a "Majorana" verdict. The obvious example is a vortex-core ZBP in the same sample, which the authors compare with at the level of linewidth (Ref. [58]).

   The authors should add SP-STS on a vortex-core ZBP, or at minimum state the criteria explicitly as predictions for YSR versus MBS and apply them uniformly to each defect class. If neither is possible, the title should be revised.

B. Should address

5. The step-edge analysis rests on an ad hoc model whose parameters and fit quality are not shown.
   - Fig. 2f is called a fit ("to get the best fit, we used two different coupling strengths for spectra 1-2 and 3-6"), yet the caption says the peaks are fixed at ±0.14 mV with heights set by Fig. 2e. These two descriptions are inconsistent.
   - The measured spectra are never overlaid on the model.
   - The y-axis of Fig. 2b reads "Model DOS", although the caption says these are measured dI/dV spectra.

   The arguments against alternatives are also not secure:
   - The statement that Fe(Te,Se), as an s± superconductor, has "no quasiparticles inside the gap causing Andreev reflection" is not a valid exclusion. In a sign-changing s± state, non-magnetic scatterers and edges can themselves bind in-gap states, so in-gap peaks at an edge do not imply a magnetic scatterer.
   - The statement that Andreev bound states "require two reflection walls" is not general either: single-surface bound states exist for sign-changing order parameters.

   The authors should:
   - give the model parameters (J, impurity positions, broadening) and overlay data and model;
   - remove or correct the Andreev arguments;
   - provide a test that discriminates magnetic from non-magnetic in-gap states at the edge, such as field-induced Zeeman shift or splitting, or SP-STS.

6. The magnetic origin of the defects is assumed, not shown.
   - The interpretation throughout relies on excess interstitial Fe below the surface, and on "unpaired electrons" at step edges (Fig. 1j).
   - Figs. 3c and 5a explicitly show no adatoms near the relevant sites, and the excess-Fe content (Fe1+y) of the crystal is not quantified in Methods.
   - The step-edge model in Fig. 1j is a cartoon with no supporting evidence.

   The authors should:
   - quantify y, for example by EDX or ICP;
   - compare the areal density of zero-field in-gap hot spots (Fig. 1c,d, where several are visible) with the density expected from y and the detection depth L_d (Fig. 1b, which is never given a value);
   - ideally, compare with a crystal annealed to reduce excess Fe.

7. Phase-shift analysis (Fig. 4c,d, Fig. S6).
   - The phase shift of about 0.5π for Bragg peak 1 and about 0 for peak 2 comes from a single linecut across a single bend, with no uncertainty given in the main text.
   - The paper does not explain what lattice displacement this corresponds to, nor why a non-π shift in one Bragg direction excludes a dispersing Majorana mode, given that the original argument (Ref. [57]) concerns the Fu-Kane π-junction.
   - The authors should report the phase shift with an error bar, check it along several linecuts and at other Type II defects, and summarize which theoretical requirement the measured value violates.

8. The multi-impurity models in Figs. 4g-i and S10 are qualitative and largely unconstrained.
   - The irregular chain in Fig. 4h, with couplings chosen by hand, is not tied to any measured impurity position.
   - Fig. 4i matches Fig. 4f only loosely.
   - The crossing point of the ±1 T spectra away from zero bias is presented as "additional evidence". However, an off-zero crossing can also come from a spin-polarized normal-state background at the setpoint, or from Zeeman shifts at 1 T.

   The authors should:
   - give the parameter set;
   - show how robust the crossing-point shift is across parameter choices;
   - state what a hybridized or field-split MBS pair would predict for the same quantity, so that this is a discriminating test rather than a consistency check.

9. The tip-gating explanation for the dispersing P1 peak (Fig. 5i,j, up to about +0.5 mV near the end of line C) is asserted but not tested.
   - Tip-induced gating (Ref. [38]) has a characteristic dependence on setpoint bias and tip height. The authors should vary V_stab and I_stab at P1 and show the resulting change in the peak position or the ring radius.
   - The claim that the P2 impurity lies deeper because of a "lower zero-energy DOS" (Fig. 5c) rests on a small difference between two spectra with different coherence-peak heights, and needs to be quantified.

10. Statistics and reproducibility are not reported.
   - Each defect class is represented by essentially one example: one step edge, one wrinkle hot spot, one bend and one pair of subsurface sites.
   - The number of samples, cleaves and tips is not stated, and there is no tally of how many sites of each class showed ZBPs, split peaks or gap filling.
   - Fig. 1d shows several zero-energy hot spots in a defect-free area, but only one is analyzed.

   The authors should provide these counts and show at least a second example of each class, ideally with SP-STS.

11. All data are taken at a single temperature, 1.1 K, and non-spin-polarized field dependence is barely used, although fields up to 3 T are available.
   - A spin-S YSR state and a Majorana mode respond differently to in-gap Zeeman shifts. The ZBP at P2 and the step-edge ZBP are exactly the cases where the trends of peak energy and width with field (non-spin-polarized tip) and with temperature would help discriminate.
   - The authors should add these trends for at least the step edge and P2.

C. Minor

12. Page 9 says "Full-Width at Half-Maximum (HWHM) near 0.5 mV". Please state which quantity is meant and how it compares with the thermal and lock-in resolution.

13. Fig. 2 caption:
   - Panel c has 61 spectra and panel d has 60, both over 12 nm.
   - The text says the peaks fade "within 10 nm" for path A and "within 8 nm" for path B.
   Please make these consistent.

14. Fig. 5 caption calls the area a "surface impurity region", whereas the text describes it as having no surface defects and attributes the states to subsurface impurities.

15. Page 7 says the localization along the wrinkle "clearly rules out the existence of a topological dispersing mode". Since no dispersing mode is predicted for a strain wrinkle in the first place, this wording is stronger than the evidence warrants.

16. Methods:
   - The lock-in modulation is called "V_bias", whereas the captions use V_osc = 0.03 mV. Please state whether this is rms or peak.
   - Please give the tip's spin-polarization magnitude in the main text, not only in the SI.
   - Please state whether the Fe-coated Cr tip's switching field is well below 1 T.

17. Fig. 1j has no legend explaining what the red and blue triangles imply for magnetism. L_d in Fig. 1b is defined but never estimated.

18. The Introduction's statements about quantum computing ("supremacy in dealing with problems like factoring") are imprecise and not needed for this paper; consider shortening them.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Use the same calibrated SP tip to measure a vortex-core ZBP and a known Fe-adatom YSR state as positive references. Then apply one explicit, uniform set of criteria (spatial dispersion, field-dependent energy, SP sign structure across zero bias) to every defect class, and tabulate the outcome.
2. Pair every field-reversal SP measurement with the same sequence taken with a non-magnetic tip, and with images of vortex positions. Report asymmetry spectra with error bars from repeated cycles.
3. Extend SP-STS and field-dependent spectroscopy to the step edge and the wrinkle, so that the abstract's claim is backed by spin-resolved data. Otherwise narrow the abstract to the bend and the subsurface sites.
4. Add statistics across defects, tips and cleaves, and quantify the excess-Fe content, to support the claim that subsurface Fe is the common origin of these states.
