Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports electric-field manipulation of polar-vortex arrays in PbTiO3/SrTiO3 trilayers and (PTO16/STO12)10 superlattices on DyScO3. A biased AFM tip dragged along lithographic "channels" shifts a "vortex boundary" laterally by about 1 µm. Reversing the bias moves it back, and the moved state is reported to persist for up to 120 days. In-situ biased STEM, phase-field simulations and pulsed-tip experiments are added; the pulsed-tip experiments are said to create and annihilate "vortex rings". Deterministic, reversible and non-volatile control of vortex arrays would interest the polar-topology community. The central question is whether the experiments show vortices or vortex-domain boundaries being translated, or ordinary tip writing of a different polar state. The paper does not yet settle this.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- Two heterostructure types are studied with the same protocols, and the results look qualitatively similar in both.
- Three probes are combined: PFM at the micron scale, in-situ biased STEM at the nanometre scale, and phase-field simulation.
- Both bias polarities are tested, and the PFM retention data are long-term (2–120 days, Fig. 4a–f).
- The SS-PFM maps (Fig. 1e–f) could give useful spatially resolved switching information, if the analysis is made quantitative.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **What the "vortex boundary" is, and whether vortices survive in the written region, is not established.**
   - **What the data show.** In Figs. 2b–e, 3b–d and 4b, the region swept by the biased tip turns into a nearly uniform LPFM amplitude/phase region. The text calls its edge a "vortex boundary" that has moved. A uniform LPFM response over hundreds of nanometres is what one would expect if the tip had switched the vortex array into a different state there: an a1/a2 or in-plane-polarized ferroelectric phase, an out-of-plane c-domain, or a vortex array with a net in-plane polarization of the opposite sign.
   - **What is missing.** No data show that the swept region still contains vortices with the original ~10–14 nm period. Such data could be high-resolution PFM, cross-sectional STEM of a PFM-written area, or nano-focused or micro-diffraction satellite peaks. The paper also never says which polar degree of freedom the LPFM contrast reports: the net in-plane polarization of the vortex array (as in refs 1, 23), or individual vortices. Individual vortices cannot be resolved with a 25 nm tip radius (Methods).
   - **Why it matters.** Without this, "vortex motion" cannot be told apart from conventional tip-induced phase conversion or domain writing, followed by erasure with the opposite bias.
   - **What to do.**
     - Image the written regions with vertical PFM and with two orthogonal LPFM orientations (sample rotated by 90°).
     - Prepare a FIB cross-section from inside a PFM-written channel region and from a pristine region, and compare their STEM images.
     - State explicitly which polarization component the contrast tracks.

2. **The boundary position may simply follow the last channel written, which would make "motion" a re-definition of the written area.**
   - **The issue.** In Figs. 2b–e and 3b–d the new boundary appears close to the most recently written channel (CHP1, CHP2, CHP4, …). The text and Methods say multiple channels were used "one at a time" but give no channel coordinates.
   - **What to do.**
     - For each step, report the channel position and the boundary position afterwards. If the boundary always sits at, or a fixed tip-radius offset from, the last channel, the result is lithographic writing rather than field-driven boundary propagation.
     - Add a control in which the bias is applied along a channel far from (several hundred nm) or parallel to an existing boundary. Does the boundary move without the tip passing near it?
     - Report the displacement per step against channel spacing.
   - **Related point.** The claim of "long-range modifications across hundreds of nanometres, reflecting the inherently collective and nonlocal nature" (Conclusion) needs exactly this test.

3. **Reversibility and "return to original positions" are overstated and internally inconsistent.**
   - **Trilayer.** The forward displacement is ~1170 nm and the reverse ~890 nm (Fig. 2e vs 2i).
   - **Superlattice.** The forward displacement is again ~1170 nm and the reverse ~643 nm (Fig. 3 caption), and the boundary "became disrupted" after CHN3 (Fig. 3f).
   - **Contradiction.** These numbers do not support "returning the boundaries to their initial positions" (Conclusion) or "effectively move the boundary to its original position" (p. 7).
   - **What to do.**
     - Overlay pristine and final boundary positions.
     - Quantify the residual offset and its uncertainty.
     - Compare the vortex texture in the restored region with the pristine one, for example via the 2D FFT period and orientation of the PFM amplitude.
     - Show at least one region taken through several full forward/back cycles, so that reversibility can be judged against degradation.
   - **Coincidence to explain.** Both samples give exactly ~1170 nm forward. State whether this is a coincidence of channel layout, since it bears on concern 2.

4. **Single-region evidence, with no statistics or reproducibility across samples and regions.**
   - **What is shown.** Each main protocol appears to be shown once per sample type: one trilayer and one superlattice boundary-motion sequence, one retention region (Fig. 4), and one pulsed-field site (Fig. 5).
   - **What is missing.**
     - How many regions, samples and tips were tested.
     - How often the boundary moved in the bias direction versus not moving, or moving irregularly.
     - The spread of displacement per step.
   - **Why it matters.** The paper's selling points are "deterministic control" and "reproducibility and reliability" (p. 7), and these require such statistics.
   - **What to do.** Provide a table over all attempted experiments, including failures.

**B. Should address**

5. **Charge injection and electrostatic artefacts in PFM.**
   - **The concern.** ±5 V writing on a ~24 nm thick film, followed by imaging with drive amplitudes up to 1.25 V (Methods), can leave trapped surface or interface charge. Trapped charge produces LPFM/VPFM contrast and can bias subsequent imaging. The drive amplitude itself is large enough to perturb a low-coercivity vortex state.
   - **What to do.**
     - Provide KPFM or surface-potential maps of written regions over time.
     - Show that the imaging drive amplitude does not itself move boundaries (repeated scans at 0.6 V versus 1.25 V on the same area).
     - Show whether the written state survives surface cleaning or a mild anneal.
   - **What the retention data do and do not show.** The 120-day retention argues against purely charge-based contrast, but it does not show the contrast is vortex-related.

6. **The in-situ STEM experiments are not quantitatively linked to the PFM experiments.**
   - **Geometry and fields.** The STEM experiments use +15 to +20 V on a tungsten tip, a thinned lamella and an unknown contact geometry. The authors note that the a1/a2 superdomains vanish after thinning (p. 5). The boundary conditions, and therefore the energy landscape, differ from the film.
   - **What the images show.**
     - In Fig. 2j the change is a re-arrangement of cores from a zig-zag to a linear configuration within ~10–50 nm.
     - In Figs. 3g/4g it is a change of vortex orientation ([100] vs [010]) over tens of nm.
     - Neither is obviously the same process as the micron-scale LPFM boundary.
   - **What to do.**
     - Estimate the actual field in the lamella.
     - Discuss electron-beam effects (dose rates, charging).
     - Show beam-only controls, with no bias and the same dose, demonstrating that the boundary does not move.
     - Provide time-resolved image series, or videos, rather than selected frames.
   - **20-day result.** Specify how the lamella was stored and whether exactly the same area was re-imaged in Fig. 4g(IV).

7. **"Real-time observation" is not demonstrated by the PFM data.**
   - **The claim.** The abstract claims "real time observation of vortex motion".
   - **What the data are.** All PFM data are before/after snapshots taken after the field is removed. The STEM data are presented as discrete frames.
   - **What is missing.** No velocity, time constant or dependence on pulse duration or tip speed is reported. The trailing velocity is fixed at ~60 nm/s.
   - **What to do.** Either provide genuinely time-resolved data (for example STEM movies with timestamps, or displacement versus write time and tip velocity), or rephrase the claim.

8. **The phase-field mechanism is qualitative, and key parameters are missing.**
   - **Missing parameters.**
     - The applied field or potential magnitude.
     - The Lorentzian tip width.
     - The channel geometry in the simulation.
     - The domain-wall mobility L.
     - How "Step 2 … Step 6" map onto the experimental steps.
     - Whether the SRO electrode and the actual stack (STO/PTO/STO vs PTO/STO/PTO; see concern 13) are modelled.
   - **Scale mismatch.** The simulated boxes are ~100 nm (Fig. 2k–p, 3h), while the experimental displacements are ~1 µm.
   - **Mechanism claim.** The abstract's mechanism, "motion of the vortex boundary is controlled through the switching of the zigzag patterns of the vortex core", rests on these simulations and on one STEM sequence. It is not tested against the PFM data.
   - **Energy profile.** The energy profile (Supplementary Fig. 8) is described as showing switching "between two energy equivalent states". The paper should show that the initial and final states are degenerate in the simulation, and say what sets the barrier.
   - **Caption inconsistency.** The Fig. 2p caption says the boundary "returns to a configuration similar to the initial state" upon field removal. The panel is labelled "Step 6", and the text describes p as continued negative-field motion.
   - **What to do.** Provide a quantitative comparison, for example threshold bias versus film thickness, or displacement per pass.

9. **The SS-PFM analysis behind "reduced coercive field at vortex boundaries" is selective.**
   - **The issue.** The loops in Fig. 1g are averages over "10 representative points", with no selection criterion. The Vc maps (Fig. 1e–f) look noisy at the pixel level.
   - **What to do.**
     - Classify all 2500 grid points objectively (for example with a mask from the simultaneously acquired amplitude) and show Vc histograms for boundary and interior pixels, with means and standard errors.
     - Address imprint and electrostatic contributions to the loop shift.
     - Clarify whether the loops are vertical or lateral, and how the "vortex boundary" at the 40 nm grid pitch relates to the µm-scale boundary that is later moved.

10. **Identification of "vortex rings", "pairs of rings" and "hidden topological phases" lacks structural evidence.**
    - **What the data are.** In Fig. 5 the rings are closed contours in LPFM amplitude, which tip-written bubble domains also produce. In Fig. 5o–q the STEM "rings" are white boxes around bright point features, with no polarization mapping.
    - **What to do.** Show the polar texture, for example atom-resolved displacement maps or 4D-STEM/DPC, demonstrating a closed vortex-core loop rather than a circular domain or an extended defect. Without this, "hidden topological phases" (Conclusion) should be rephrased.

11. **The "stochastic" behaviour is inferred from a single voltage sweep.**
    - **The issue.** Fig. 5m–n tracks two vortices through one monotonic voltage ramp (0–4 V) at one site and fits them with smooth curves. A smooth fit implies a deterministic trend, which contradicts the stochastic interpretation.
    - **What to do.**
      - Repeat pulses at fixed voltage, at several sites, and report the distribution of outcomes. This is required to separate stochasticity from deterministic but history-dependent response; each pulse here acts on the state left by the previous one.
      - Remove the guide-to-the-eye curves, or state that they are not fits.
      - Give the displacement uncertainty (pixel size, drift correction).

12. **The interpretation of asymmetric "melting" under ±5 V (Supplementary Fig. 13) is unsupported.**
    - **The claim.** The statement that "positive fields induce repulsion … negative fields create attraction" assigns a sign of electrostatic interaction to vortex boundaries.
    - **What is missing.** No model or measurement supports that sign.
    - **What to do.** Either support it with simulations or polarization-direction mapping, or present it as an observation only.

**C. Minor**

13. **The sample stack is described inconsistently.**
    - Main text p. 4 and the Fig. 1 and Fig. 2 titles: "STO/PTO/STO".
    - Methods and the Fig. 4 caption: "PTO/STO/PTO trilayer".
    - The simulation (p. 16): STO/PTO/STO on DSO with no SRO.
    - Fig. 2j shows STO/PTO/STO.
    - Please correct, and state whether the SRO electrode is present in the trilayer and in the simulations.

14. **Channel labels in the text do not match the captions and figures.**
    - The text (p. 6) lists CHP1, CHP3, CHP5 and CHP6 and (p. 7) CHN1, CHN3, CHN5 and CHN6.
    - The Fig. 2 caption lists CHP1–CHP6 and CHN1–CHN5, and the figure panels show CHP1/2/4/6 and CHN1/2/3.
    - For the superlattice, the text cites CHP5 and CHP8, the caption CHP2–CHP7, and the panels CHP1/2/4 and CHN1/2.
    - The displacement "after CHN5" vs "after CHN6" is also ambiguous.

15. **Fig. 4 retention time.** The caption says panel f is "after 60 days", while the panel label and text say 120 days.

16. **Fig. 1 labelling.**
    - The caption uses "g" for both the loops and the simulations ("g-i").
    - The text cites "Fig. 1g" for the trilayer simulation (should be 1h).
    - Panel a is described as [100]/[001] in the caption but [100]/[010] in the text.
    - The Fig. 3g caption refers to alignment "along the (001)pc direction" in a plane-view image.

17. **Supplementary figure references are inconsistent.** Fig. 5 is described with "Supplementary Fig. 10j-k, 10l" on p. 10 alongside Supplementary Fig. 11; Fig. 2 with "Supplementary Fig. 3/4" in the caption but Figs. 4–5 in the text.

18. **Colour scales.** The colour scales of the PFM amplitude and phase change from panel to panel (Figs. 2–4), and several colour bars are unlabelled. Use common scales within a sequence so that contrast changes can be compared.

19. **Report fields as well as voltages.**
    - Give the estimated field, for example ±5 V across a ~24 nm film versus a ~100 nm superlattice.
    - Explain why the coercive voltages in SS-PFM (±5 V and ±8 V maxima) compare with the writing voltages as they do.
    - Give the tip–sample geometry for the STEM biasing.

20. **Novelty claims need qualification.** "First time demonstrates" (Conclusion) and "has yet to be realized" (p. 3) overlook earlier electric-field and tip control of vortex and toroidal order in the same system, including refs 5, 18 and 23. Discuss what is new relative to these works.

21. **The device claims are speculative relative to the data.** The motion is driven by a scanned tip at fixed speed, not by planar electrodes. The racetrack-memory, LLM and IoT framing and the 60 Tb/inch² figure should be toned down or tied to a concrete device geometry.

22. **Language.** The repeated use of "harnessing" as a synonym for "moving", and phrases such as "This underline physical mechanism" and "playing the local energy", reduce clarity.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add one decisive structural experiment: cross-sectional STEM (with polarization mapping) taken from inside and outside a PFM-written region. It would settle whether vortices were translated, preserved or converted.
2. Re-organize the boundary-motion data quantitatively, for each sample and region:
   - boundary position versus channel position;
   - displacement per pass versus bias, tip velocity and channel offset;
   - full cycle counts, including failed attempts.
3. Make the phase-field comparison quantitative: actual stack and electrode, stated field magnitudes, and a prediction (for example a threshold bias or its thickness dependence) that the experiment then tests.
4. Recast the pulsed-field section around repeated, statistically sampled trials at fixed voltage, and back the ring assignment with polarization-resolved imaging.
