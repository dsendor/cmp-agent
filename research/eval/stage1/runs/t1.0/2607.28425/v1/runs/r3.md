Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports a systematic transport study of eight-layer rhombohedral graphene on WSe2. Gate-defined pockets with zero resistance (SC) sit next to pockets that show a sharp superconducting-like drop at a similar onset temperature but saturate at a finite resistance (AM). The pockets have distinct perpendicular- and in-plane-field scales and an unusual non-monotonic dc-current response, and they merge under a small in-plane field into a region with a sharp SC/AM boundary. If the finite-resistance state is shown to be intrinsic, it would give a clean, gate-tunable setting for the long-standing anomalous-metal problem, in a regime (R_sheet ≪ R_Q) where current theory says such a state should not exist. That would be of broad interest. As the paper stands, the case for an intrinsic, homogeneous anomalous metal rests mainly on plausibility arguments rather than on decisive controls.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- High-quality data over a large parameter space (n, D, T, B⊥, B∥, I_dc) in a material system of strong current interest. SC and AM regions sit side by side in one device, so the same wiring and environment apply to both.
- The finite-resistance saturation is robust and flat over a wide temperature window (e.g., Fig. 1b red curve; Fig. 2i). It sets in well above the nominal base temperature, which argues against simple electron-temperature saturation.
- Reproduction across three voltage-probe pairs (Extended Data Fig. 1) and in a second device (Extended Data Figs. 9–11), and invariance of the phase boundaries under the contact (Si) gate (Extended Data Fig. 1b).
- The non-monotonic current response (Fig. 3; Extended Data Figs. 4, 10), where dc current restores zero differential resistance, is a striking and new observation.
- The authors openly discuss extrinsic explanations and their own limits (BKT validity, the unexplained instability and hysteresis).

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **Extrinsic non-equilibrium excitation (stray radiation) is not experimentally excluded.**
   - *What the paper says.* The authors cite Tamir et al. [45] as the key precedent: improved high-frequency filtering removed the anomalous metal in thin films. The Methods, however, describe only phosphor-bronze DC wiring and an on-board RC low-pass filter (10 kΩ, 1 nF, cutoff ~16 kHz). No microwave filtering (copper-powder, Eccosorb, or thermalized metal-powder filters), radiation-tight shielding of the FSE sample space, or attenuation at GHz frequencies is mentioned. An RC filter of this type is ineffective at the frequencies (≳ 2Δ/h, i.e., tens of GHz for T_c ~ 100 mK) that break pairs or excite phase slips.
   - *Why the paper's counter-argument falls short.* The paper argues that adjacent SC and AM pockets with similar T_c should be equally sensitive to residual excitation. But sensitivity to non-equilibrium excitation is set by the phase stiffness, the Josephson or condensation energy and the critical current, not by T_c. On the paper's own evidence these differ strongly between the two pockets: B⊥ critical fields differ by 10–100× (Fig. 4a–c) and the critical currents also differ (Fig. 3a). So the "comparable robustness" premise is not established. The ac-amplitude independence down to 500 pA is asserted but not shown, and it does not test radiation from the environment.
   - *Requested.*
     - (i) Describe the RF filtering and shielding in full.
     - (ii) Ideally, remeasure the AM saturation resistance after adding cold microwave filtering and/or shielding.
     - (iii) Alternatively or additionally, apply controlled broadband or microwave radiation and show that the SC and AM pockets respond in a way inconsistent with the AM simply being a more fragile superconductor.
     - (iv) Show the ac-amplitude dependence data and the AM resistance against excitation and dc bias near I_dc = 0.

   Without such controls, the central claim that the AM is intrinsic is not established.

2. **Intrinsic electronic phase coexistence (isospin domains or domain walls) is not addressed and is not excluded by the inhomogeneity arguments given.**
   - *Why it is a natural alternative.* The paper's case against inhomogeneity (p. 5–6; Methods, "Considerations of extrinsic origins") targets structural disorder: no known structural length scale near 30 nm, and identical gate-dependent footprints across probes and devices. But the AM pocket sits at the boundary of an isospin transition. Fig. 2a and Extended Data Fig. 8 show the SC adjacent to the spin-polarized half-metal and the AM bordering the degeneracy-four unpolarized metal. The SC pocket also shows hysteresis and an instability attributed to orbital magnetization or valley imbalance (Extended Data Fig. 3; Methods). Near first-order isospin transitions, coexisting domains or domain walls are generic. Their footprint in (n, D, B∥) is set by the band structure, so it would be reproducible across probes and devices, and their size is not tied to any structural length. A superconductor threaded by non-superconducting isospin domains or domain walls could produce a saturating finite resistance, an enhanced apparent critical field (the ~30 nm "ξ" of the AM), and multi-step I–V curves (Fig. 3d).
   - *Why the SNS argument does not cover this.* The SNS argument in the Methods (ξ_N diverging as T → 0) assumes a normal metal that proximitizes freely. It fails if the normal region is a different isospin phase. Example: a spin- or valley-polarized metal next to a spin-triplet or valley-polarized condensate whose pairing channel cannot leak into it.
   - *Requested.*
     - (i) Gate-sweep hysteresis (forward/backward in V_b or D) and B⊥/B∥ sweep hysteresis inside the AM pocket.
     - (ii) Quantum oscillations taken inside or across the AM pocket (Extended Data Fig. 8 cuts through the SC pocket only), to test whether its normal state is a single phase or shows mixed degeneracy.
     - (iii) The AM resistance per square for the three probe pairs of Extended Data Fig. 1, compared with their L/W. A homogeneous AM gives a probe-independent ρ; a few weak links or domain walls give resistances not proportional to segment length. Show this as a table or plot, not as color maps on a shared scale.
     - (iv) A discussion of how the finite B = 0 ρ_xy in the AM (see concern 4) fits a homogeneous state.

**B. Should address**

3. **The evidence that the SC and AM host different paired states is weaker than stated.**
   - *Mismatched T_c.* The Discussion says the pockets share "similar onset temperatures" with a "factor of four in the critical in-plane field." The Methods, however, give T_c ≈ 55 mK and B∥,c ≈ 1.39 T for the Fig. 1 SC pocket, and T_c ≈ 130 mK and B∥,c ≈ 3.93 T for the Fig. 1 AM pocket. The field ratio is ≈2.8, and the T_c differ by ≈2.4×. Normalized by T_c, both exceed the Pauli limit by nearly the same factor (≈14 and ≈16). On these numbers the in-plane response gives no evidence for distinct pairing.
   - *Mismatched conditions for the perpendicular fields.* The headline values (≈4 mT against ≈300 mT; Fig. 4a,b) are taken at B∥ = 350 mT, which itself suppresses the SC. Fig. 1d at B∥ = 0 suggests an SC perpendicular critical field of tens of mT, giving a much smaller ratio.
   - *The ξ ≈ 30 nm for the AM.* Applying the Ginzburg–Landau relation Φ0/2πξ² to the outer diamond of a finite-resistance, possibly inhomogeneous state assumes the very homogeneity in question.
   - *Requested.* Show B⊥,c and B∥,c for SC and AM against gate voltage along a trajectory that crosses the boundary, at matched B∥ (including B∥ = 0), each normalized by the local T_c. State the definition of "critical field" for the AM explicitly.

4. **The finite Hall signal in the AM is set aside too quickly.**
   - *What is shown.* ρ_xy is said to be finite but field-independent in the AM and is attributed to geometric mixing with ρ_xx (p. 2; Extended Data Figs. 2j–l, 11c–f). If it were mixing, ρ_xy/ρ_xx should be the same in the AM and in the adjacent normal state. A larger ratio in the AM would instead point to non-uniform current flow, which bears directly on concern 2.
   - *Requested.* Quantify the ratio ρ_xy/ρ_xx in the AM and in the adjacent normal state.

5. **The non-monotonic current response is described but not analyzed.**
   - *Why it matters.* Fig. 3f–g and Extended Data Fig. 4f–g show dc current driving dV/dI to (near) zero just before the transition to the normal state. This is presented as a constraint on theory (Discussion), but simpler explanations are not tested:
     - a series combination of regions with different critical currents and resistances, where the resistive region switches to a superconducting one at finite bias;
     - current-induced switching of an isospin domain;
     - self-heating or sweep-direction hysteresis.
   - *Requested.*
     - Show up- and down-sweeps of I_dc.
     - State the resolution of the "zero" differential resistance in this region.
     - Report V_xy recorded at the same time, which tests current redistribution.
     - Indicate whether a two-element series model reproduces the integrated I–V curves (Fig. 3d,g).

6. **Density calibration, and whether both pockets lie in the same band.**
   - *The inconsistency.* The Fig. 1 caption places the AM pocket at n = −1.25 × 10¹² cm⁻² and the SC at n = +1.34 × 10¹² cm⁻². In Fig. 1a the AM dashed line also lies at negative n. Yet the text treats both pockets as conduction-band states that merge under B∥ (Fig. 2b) and share the half-metal/unpolarized-metal boundary.
   - *Requested.* Clarify the sign and any offset in the n, D conversion (including the effect of the +15 V Si gate). If the AM is in fact a hole-doped state, the statement that SC and AM emerge "from nearly identical external parameters" needs revision. Relatedly, the claim that the second device reproduces the phase boundaries at "nearly identical regions of the (n, D) phase diagram" (Methods) is supported only by (V_b, V_t) maps with different gate voltages (Extended Data Fig. 9). Provide (n, D) maps for both devices on common axes.

7. **Effective electron temperature in this sample is not established.**
   - *What is given.* The Methods cite electron temperatures below 20 mK measured previously in other samples (quantum dots, nanowires) with the same wiring.
   - *Requested.* A bound for this device and cooldown would strengthen the case. For example, show that the SC pockets with the lowest T_c (≈40–50 mK, e.g., Extended Data Fig. 6d region or Fig. 2h edge) still reach zero resistance, and that the AM saturation onset temperature tracks T_c rather than a fixed temperature.

8. **Sample statistics and the second device.**
   - *Primary device.* Only one device shows clean behavior, and thermal-cycling reproducibility is not reported.
   - *Second device.* Here some AM contact pairs read a resistance exceeding the normal state (Extended Data Fig. 11b,c). That is hard to reconcile with a homogeneous AM and points to non-uniform current flow within the AM itself. The authors attribute it to sample inhomogeneity, but this observation cuts against the paper's own inhomogeneity argument.
   - *Requested.* State the number of cooldowns, and discuss what Extended Data Fig. 11 implies for the AM.

**C. Minor**

9. State the measurement noise floor that defines "zero resistance" (e.g., an upper bound in Ω for the SC pocket in Fig. 1b inset). The AM saturates at only ≈8–10 Ω (Figs. 1b, 2c), so the separation between "zero" and "finite" should be quantified.

10. Methods, ξ/ℓ_mf and k_Fξ for the AM: these use the SC pocket's density, degeneracy (f_ν = 1/2) and quantum-oscillation onset, although the AM borders the degeneracy-four metal at different n. Recompute them with AM-pocket parameters. Note also that ℓ_mf ≈ 5.9 μm exceeds the channel width, which makes the ρ = R·W/L conversion and the diffusive picture questionable.

11. The claim that the conductivity enhancement is "far larger than standard Aslamazov–Larkin corrections" (p. 6) should be quantified with the AL expression and the measured T_c and normal-state conductance.

12. BKT analysis (Extended Data Fig. 6c): T_BKT = 61 mK from α = 3. Compare this with the resistive T_c at the same gate point, and consider a Halperin–Nelson fit of R(T). The text calls the SC transition "standard BKT" while also noting that BKT is of questionable validity at micron scales; reconcile the two statements.

13. The trapped-flux offset of up to ~10 mT (Methods) is comparable to the SC perpendicular critical field (~4–10 mT). Discuss how this affects the B⊥ asymmetry and hysteresis attributed to intrinsic orbital magnetization (Fig. 1e; Extended Data Fig. 3).

14. The Discussion says the AM persists to "in-plane fields roughly four times larger than the SC," while the Methods give 3.93 T / 1.39 T ≈ 2.8. Make these consistent, and specify which gate points are compared.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a decisive environmental control: cold microwave filtering or shielding, or controlled irradiation, showing that the AM saturation resistance is unchanged while the SC reacts as expected. This is the single most persuasive addition.
2. Add a scaling test of the AM resistance with probe spacing, and gate- and field-sweep hysteresis inside the AM pocket. Together these address isospin-domain or domain-wall coexistence.
3. Present matched-condition, T_c-normalized comparisons of the SC and AM critical fields and currents along one gate trajectory crossing the boundary, so the "distinct paired states" conclusion rests on like-for-like data.
4. Summarize the extrinsic-origin tests in a table: mechanism, predicted signature, measured result, figure. Distinguish clearly between scenarios that are excluded experimentally and those that are argued against on plausibility.
