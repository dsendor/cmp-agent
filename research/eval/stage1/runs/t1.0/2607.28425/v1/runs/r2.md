Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports transport on eight-layer rhombohedral graphene on WSe2 in which gate-defined pockets with zero resistance (SC) sit next to pockets where the resistance drops sharply below a similar onset temperature but saturates at a finite value (AM). The AM pockets also show critical currents and critical fields, an unusual non-monotonic dc-current response, and perpendicular critical fields about an order of magnitude larger than the adjacent SC. Anomalous-metal behaviour has been studied for decades in disordered films, where extrinsic explanations are hard to exclude. A clean, gate-tunable system in which an AM and a true superconductor can be compared at nearly identical external parameters would be a valuable platform, and the phenomenology is well documented. The central interpretive claims, however, are that the AM is an intrinsic, paired, phase-incoherent state distinct from the adjacent SC, and that extrinsic and inhomogeneity explanations are strongly constrained. At present these rest largely on qualitative arguments and on a field-scale comparison whose criteria are not matched.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- High-quality, systematic data: maps in (n, D), T, B⊥, B∥ and I_dc, all measured in one device and largely reproduced across three voltage-probe pairs (Extended Data Fig. 1) and in a second device (Extended Data Figs. 9–11).
- Sub-ohm resolution distinguishes zero resistance (Fig. 1b inset) from a saturated value of about 8 Ω (Fig. 2c), so the two classes of pocket are clearly different in transport.
- Useful controls are already included: independence from the silicon contact gate (Extended Data Fig. 1b), consistency across contact pairs, a BKT analysis contrasting SC and AM (Extended Data Fig. 6), and in-plane-field evolution (Extended Data Fig. 7).
- The AM sits in an ultraclean (ℓ_mf about 6 μm), low-resistance (R□ ≪ R_Q) regime where standard dissipative theories predict it should not occur. If it is intrinsic, this is a real challenge to theory.
- The non-monotonic current response, where moderate I_dc restores zero differential resistance (Fig. 3f–g), is new and reproducible (Extended Data Figs. 4, 10).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The SC/AM perpendicular-field comparison uses mismatched criteria, so the "distinct paired states" conclusion is not yet supported. The main text (Fig. 4a–b) contrasts an SC field of about 4 mT with an AM field of about 300 mT and converts both to ξ ≈ 300 nm versus 30 nm. For the SC, the 4 mT is the field at which the inner, zero-resistance diamond (the vanishing of the critical current) closes. That is the field at which vortices or phase slips first produce dissipation, not the field at which pairing is destroyed. The data show that the SC's resistive transition in B⊥ extends much further. In Fig. 1d (B∥ = 0) the SC pocket stays below the normal resistance up to roughly 50–75 mT. In Fig. 4c (B∥ = 350 mT) the zero-resistance region ends near 10 mT, yet a reduced-resistance region continues to roughly 100 mT on the SC side. For the AM, by contrast, the "critical field" can only be the field at which the finite-resistance plateau returns to the normal state. The Methods add to the confusion: they state that critical fields were defined at 90% of the normal-state resistance, which does not match the inner-diamond reading used in the main text. This matters because the claim that the AM "is unlikely to be simply a phase-disordered version of the adjacent superconductor" (p. 5) rests mainly on this order-of-magnitude difference. If the SC's 90%-normal B_c⊥ is comparable to the AM's, the difference reduces to "the SC has a finite zero-resistance (pinned or phase-coherent) window and the AM does not", which is exactly what a phase-disordered SC would show. The authors should extract B_c⊥ for SC and AM with the same criterion (for example 50% and 90% of R_N), and separately the field at which the dissipationless window closes. They should plot both against V_b across the SC/AM boundary (Fig. 4c, Fig. 1d, and the B∥ = 0 data), and also give the in-plane critical fields with the same criterion at matched gate points. The conclusion should then be revised to match.

2. The double critical current may come from a single superconducting state with two scales, which the paper does not consider. In Fig. 3a–d the SC pocket shows an inner critical current (onset of dissipation) and an outer one (return to ohmic), with a flat, nearly dissipative-but-reduced region in between. The paper reads this as an "inner dome associated with the zero-resistance SC and an outer dome associated with the dissipative AM". In a 2D superconductor, however, a depinning or phase-slip current followed by a larger pair-breaking current is generic. The intermediate region is then flux flow or phase-slip dissipation of the same condensate. On this reading the AM pocket is where the depinning current has gone to zero while the pair-breaking scale survives. The smooth evolution from two steps to one step as V_b moves toward the AM (Fig. 3a) fits a continuous loss of phase stiffness at least as naturally as a crossing into a different paired state. The authors should either rule this out or present it as a live alternative. Possible tests:
   - the scaling of the inner and outer critical currents and critical fields with T and B⊥ (vortex-motion versus depairing forms);
   - whether the intermediate-region resistance scales with B⊥ like flux-flow resistance;
   - whether the intermediate-state resistance at fixed gate equals the AM plateau resistance at the neighbouring gate.

3. There is no direct evidence that the AM involves pairing, rather than being a reconstructed normal state with threshold behaviour. Every signature attributed to the AM is a drop in resistance below an onset temperature, together with its suppression by B⊥, B∥ and I_dc. A partial resistance drop (factor of about 4, Fig. 1b, Fig. 2c) can also come from an ordering transition in the normal state, such as an isospin or nematic reorder, or a density-wave state. The authors' own ref. [16] reports charge-density-wave states in rhombohedral graphene, and sliding density waves have threshold currents. The AM pocket also borders the half-metal/unpolarized-metal boundary (Fig. 2a), where Fermi-surface reconstructions are expected. The B∥ critical field of about 3.9 T "exceeding B_P by 16" is computed with a BCS Pauli formula, which presupposes pairing and does not establish it. The paper should give positive evidence for pairing in the AM pocket. Options include:
   - quantum oscillations measured inside the AM pocket (B⊥ above the AM critical field, at the same B∥) compared with those just outside, to show the normal-state Fermi surface is the same on both sides of the AM boundary, so the drop is not a Fermi-surface reconstruction;
   - magnetoresistance oscillations or interference in I_c(B⊥) at the relevant small fields;
   - the functional form of the I_dc and B⊥ suppression compared with superconducting fluctuation conductivity.
   Without such evidence the title and abstract should present the AM as a finite-resistance state with superconducting-like phenomenology.

4. The inhomogeneity discussion leaves out intrinsic electronic phase separation, which would be reproducible from device to device. The Methods argue against granular disorder because rhombohedral graphene "has no obvious length scale larger than the crystal lattice" and because percolation "should depend sensitively on geometric details". Neither argument applies to electronic phase coexistence near a first-order isospin transition. Such coexistence is set by band structure and electrostatics, so it would appear at the same (n, D) in every contact pair and in a second device, and its domain size (tens of nanometres to sub-micron, set by Coulomb frustration) is not structural. The SC pocket abuts the half-metal and the AM abuts the unpolarized metal (p. 3). The paper reports an instability and hysteresis around the SC pocket at B∥ = 0 (Fig. 1b–e, Extended Data Fig. 3), the SC/AM boundary is "abrupt" (Fig. 2c), and ρ_xy is finite in the AM but not in the SC (Extended Data Figs. 2, 11). Together these are suggestive of exactly this scenario: superconducting half-metal domains embedded in an unpolarized metal would give a finite, T-independent plateau, a large B⊥ scale tied to domain size (about 30 nm), and possibly current-driven domain rearrangement producing the non-monotonic dV/dI. The authors should test for coexistence directly:
   - gate- and field-sweep hysteresis inside the AM pocket and across the SC/AM boundary;
   - low-frequency or telegraph resistance noise in the AM against the SC and normal regions;
   - whether quantum oscillations in the AM pocket contain both the degeneracy-two and degeneracy-four frequencies (extending Extended Data Fig. 8c, which runs along V_t = 3.20 V and does not cross the V_t ≈ 1.1 V AM pocket of Fig. 1);
   - whether Hall density in the AM interpolates between the two phases.
   The statement that the data provide "strong evidence that the SC/AM distinction is not a consequence of inhomogeneity" (p. 9) should be limited to structural inhomogeneity unless these tests come out negative.

B. Should address

5. The radiation and nonequilibrium control is weaker than stated. The only filtering described is a single RC stage (10 kΩ, 1 nF) on a flex PCB. There is no mention of copper-powder, Eccosorb or other microwave filters, or of radiation-tight shielding at the sample stage. Ref. [45] showed that exactly this kind of unfiltered high-frequency radiation can turn a superconductor into an apparent anomalous metal. The counter-argument that "any source of external non-equilibrium excitation should affect both regions comparably" assumes the SC and AM have equal sensitivity to radiation. That has not been established: their gaps, superfluid stiffness and phase-slip barriers may differ widely, and concern 1 indicates the AM may have a much smaller phase stiffness. The excitation-amplitude independence "down to 500 pA" is stated but not shown, and it says nothing about broadband radiation. The authors should state the full filtering and shielding chain, show the excitation-amplitude dependence of the AM plateau, and ideally show either that the AM plateau is unchanged with added microwave filtering or that it responds to deliberately applied broadband noise or microwaves in a different way from the SC.

6. Heating and sweep-direction checks are missing for the non-monotonic current response. The region where increasing I_dc restores dV/dI ≈ 0 (Fig. 3f–g, Extended Data Figs. 4c, 10c) is a central novel observation. At 11 mK, electron–phonon cooling in graphene is extremely weak, so even picowatt dissipation can raise the electron temperature well above the bath, and the dissipated power differs across the I–V curve. The authors should (a) estimate the electron temperature against I_dc in the AM pocket, for example from the normal-state ρ(T) as a thermometer or from the BKT-pocket I_c(T) calibration; (b) show up- and down-sweeps in I_dc to test for hysteresis or switching; and (c) show that the integrated I–V curves (Fig. 3d, g) agree with directly measured dc voltages, since integrating lock-in dV/dI with an ac excitation (0.5–1 nA) comparable to the features (a few nA) can distort narrow structure.

7. The (n, D) assignment of the AM pocket and its isospin neighbours rests on limited data. The claim that the SC borders the half-metal and the AM borders the unpolarized metal comes from Fig. 2a and a single Landau fan at V_t = 3.20 V (Extended Data Fig. 8), which crosses the SC pocket at B∥ = 0. The AM pocket of Fig. 1 (V_t ≈ 1.1 V) and the merged region at B∥ = 350 mT, where most of the analysis is done, are not covered by any fan. A fan through the AM pocket, and across the SC/AM boundary at B∥ = 350 mT, is needed to support the phase assignment and the "two distinct states" reading of the sharp boundary.

8. The quantum-fluctuation interpretation is asserted rather than tested. The Discussion states that the T-independent saturation "is consistent with a scenario in which the relevant fluctuations are quantum mechanical in origin" and that the effect is "far larger than standard Aslamazov–Larkin corrections would predict", but no AL estimate is given. The authors should give the expected AL (and Maki–Thompson, given the clean limit) excess conductance in units of e²/ħ and compare it with the measured 1/ρ_AM − 1/ρ_N. They should also either state which specific observable would distinguish quantum fluctuations from a residual extrinsic dephasing rate, or soften the statement.

9. The comparison of critical temperatures is internally inconsistent. The main text says SC and AM share "similar maximum critical temperatures (≈ 100 mK) when adjacent". The Methods instead give T_c ≈ 55 mK for the SC and ≈ 130 mK for the AM analysed in Fig. 1, with in-plane critical fields of 1.39 T and 3.93 T. It is also unclear at which gate voltages the in-plane critical fields were measured: Fig. 1 is at B∥ = 0, and Fig. 4d is at V_t = 2.30 V, not at the Fig. 1 gate points. The "similar T_c" premise underpins the argument that a nonequilibrium source would affect both equally (concern 5), so the authors should tabulate T_c (same criterion), B_c⊥, B_c∥, inner and outer I_c, and R_AM/R_N at matched pairs of adjacent SC and AM gate points, at B∥ = 0 and at 350 mT.

10. Finite ρ_xy in the AM is not explained. Extended Data Figs. 2j–l and 11c–f show finite transverse signals in the AM region. The text attributes them to geometric mixing "possibly", but no field-antisymmetrization is shown. A finite, field-independent ρ_xy that turns on with the AM transition is itself a sign of non-uniform current flow below T_c, which bears on concern 4. The authors should show ρ_xy at ±B⊥, symmetrized and antisymmetrized, across the AM transition, and compare its onset temperature with that of ρ_xx.

11. The sample statistics and controls are limited. Two devices are reported, both eight-layer and both on WSe2. The second device is described as reproducing the pockets in "nearly identical regions of the (n, D) phase diagram", but Extended Data Figs. 9–11 are plotted in (V_b, V_t). The claim needs maps converted to (n, D) using each device's capacitances, with the SC and AM boundaries overlaid. The role of WSe2 in the AM is not addressed: the paper argues the SC lives on the surface away from WSe2, but no device without WSe2 (or of different thickness) is shown. Thermal cycling of the primary device is not reported either. At minimum, the authors should state whether the AM plateau value and the pocket positions survived a thermal cycle, and whether AM pockets appear in comparable devices without WSe2.

12. Residual flux and B⊥ offsets could matter for the AM. The SC's dissipationless window closes at B⊥ of only a few mT (Fig. 4b), and the Methods note offsets of up to about 10 mT from trapped flux in the magnet coils. B⊥ is zeroed by symmetrizing ρ_xx or maximizing I_c, but residual flux inhomogeneity or pinned vortices from prior field sweeps could produce finite resistance in a weakly pinned or low-stiffness condensate. The authors should state the field history before the zero-field AM data (Fig. 1b, f–h) and show that the AM plateau is unchanged after a magnet quench, demagnetization, or warming above the magnet's trapped-flux scale.

C. Minor

13. The temperature resolution of the saturation is unclear. The base temperature is quoted as 11–14 mK, while the confirmed electron temperatures are "below 20 mK" (quantum dots) and "down to 14 mK" (nanowires), measured in other samples and on different wiring. The flat portion of ρ(T) below about 20 mK therefore carries no temperature information. The claim of saturation "over a factor of five in temperature" should use the range in which the electron temperature is known.

14. The zero-resistance criterion for the SC should be quantified: the noise floor in Ω, after the W/L factor, and the definition of "below measurement resolution". Its comparison with the smallest AM plateau values (the ratio R_AM/R_N ≈ 0.1–0.4) should also be stated, and R_N should be defined.

15. The Methods compute k_F with f_ν = 1/2 (half metal) for both the SC and the AM, but the AM is stated to border the unpolarized metal (f_ν = 1/4). The AM values of k_Fξ and ξ/ℓ_mf should use the appropriate degeneracy. Relatedly, ℓ_mf ≈ 5.9 μm exceeds the channel width (scale bar in Extended Data Fig. 1a), so it is device-limited and should be presented as a lower bound.

16. The BKT statements are inconsistent. The main text says the SC "exhibits a standard BKT transition", while the Methods say a BKT analysis is "of questionable validity for the micron-scale samples". With ξ ≈ 300 nm, the sample is only a few coherence lengths wide. The main text should carry the same caveat.

17. The overshoot in ρ just below T_c in some AM traces (Fig. 2e, p. 4) and the intermediate plateau within the SC pocket should be quantified (amplitude, gate range), even if they are unexplained. They may bear on concerns 2 and 4.

18. In Fig. 1, the transition mentioned in the text at "V_b < −3.20 V" (p. 4) and the V_b values quoted for Figs. 3a, 4a and Extended Data Fig. 4 use different V_t. A small schematic marking every gate trajectory used, in one (n, D) map at B∥ = 350 mT, would make it much easier to check that the comparisons are made at the intended points.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Build one summary figure that compares adjacent SC and AM points with matched criteria: T_c, B_c⊥ (both the dissipationless and 90%-R_N definitions), B_c∥, inner and outer I_c, and R_AM/R_N, all against V_b across the boundary. This would turn the "distinct paired states" argument into a quantitative one.
2. Add a direct test for electronic phase coexistence in the AM pocket: gate and field hysteresis, resistance noise spectra, and quantum oscillations inside the AM. This is the single most informative addition for separating an intrinsic AM from a band-structure-pinned mixture.
3. Document the radiation filtering and add a deliberate-perturbation test (applied microwaves or broadband noise) comparing the SC and AM responses. Turning the nonequilibrium argument from an inference into a measurement would greatly strengthen the extrinsic-origin section.
4. Recast the Discussion's list of constraints on theory to separate what is measured (finite saturated R, critical scales, non-monotonic I–V) from what is interpreted (quantum fluctuations, distinct paired states). Temper the title and abstract language accordingly unless the pairing evidence in concern 3 is added.
