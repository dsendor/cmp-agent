Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report gate-tuned pockets in eight-layer rhombohedral graphene on WSe2. Some pockets reach zero resistance (SC). Others fall sharply below a critical temperature, field and current but then level off at a finite resistance of about 0.1 to 0.4 of the normal-state value (AM). The two kinds of pocket sit side by side in (n, D) space with a sharp boundary between them. Finding an anomalous-metal-like state next to true superconductivity, in a clean, continuously tunable system with normal-state conductance near 1000 e^2/h, would be a valuable new setting for a long-standing problem. The central interpretive claims depend on the following: (i) the AM saturation is intrinsic; (ii) the SC and AM differ in their paired state, not only in phase coherence. As presented, neither claim is yet secure.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The data are high quality and systematic. The paper gives full maps in (Vb, Vt), T, B_perp, B_par and I_dc, and the main features are reproduced across three voltage-probe pairs (Extended Data Fig. 1) and in a second device (Extended Data Figs. 9-11).
- The SC/AM boundary is sharp in gate space (Fig. 2c), and the two states can be compared at matched temperature on the same sample. This is a cleaner comparison than most thin-film anomalous-metal studies allow.
- The current response of the AM is non-monotonic: a moderate dc current drives dV/dI back to zero before the normal transition (Figs. 3e-g, Extended Data Figs. 4, 10). This observation is new and constraining.
- Several extrinsic explanations are tested directly in the data rather than only argued against: the silicon contact gate (Extended Data Fig. 1b), consistency across probe pairs, and ac-amplitude dependence.
- The closing list of constraints for theory is useful to the community.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The comparison of field scales uses different criteria for SC and AM, so it does not show that the paired states differ. The paper's claim that "the paired states in the two regimes are not identical" and that "the anomalous metal is then unlikely to be simply a phase-disordered version of the adjacent superconductor" (Fig. 4 and its section) rests on B_c,perp of about 4 mT (SC) versus about 300 mT (AM), and hence xi of about 300 nm versus about 30 nm.
   - The two field scales come from different features. The 4 mT value is the edge of the inner zero-resistance diamond (Fig. 4b). Figs. 3b and 4a show, at a single gate voltage, an inner zero-resistance dome nested inside an outer dissipative dome. The inner feature is therefore the scale on which global phase coherence (zero resistance) is lost, while the outer feature is the scale on which the pairing-induced resistance drop disappears.
   - On the authors' own data, the SC pocket also has a much larger onset field. In Fig. 4c the finite-resistance region above the SC pocket extends to tens of mT. At B_par = 0, Fig. 1d shows suppressed resistance up to roughly 50-75 mT, and Fig. 1e shows zero resistance up to about 10 mT.
   - Methods says critical fields were defined by the 90% normal-state criterion. However, the 4 mT quoted for the SC looks like a zero-resistance boundary.
   - What to do: extract B_c,perp for both pockets with the same criterion (90% of R_N), at the same B_par (including B_par = 0) and at matched reduced temperature. Recompute xi_SC, k_F xi and xi/l_mf from that value. If the SC onset field is comparable to the AM field, the "distinct paired states" conclusion and the related discussion and constraint list need to be revised. The in-plane comparison (Fig. 4d) has the same issue and should state which criterion defines B_par,c for each pocket.

2. There is no direct experimental test of external non-equilibrium excitation. The paper notes that stray high-frequency radiation can produce exactly this phenomenology (ref. [45]), but the Methods argument against it is indirect. Two points weaken it:
   - The argument says that "taking T_c as a proxy for the robustness of the paired state, any source of external non-equilibrium excitation should affect both regions comparably". The sensitivity of zero resistance to radiation is set by phase stiffness and vortex pinning, not by T_c. Concern 1 suggests these differ strongly between the two pockets.
   - The filtering described is a 10 kOhm/1 nF RC stage on the flex-PCB, with phosphor-bronze wiring and a fast-sample-exchange module. No microwave filtering (copper-powder, Eccosorb or similar) and no radiation-tight sample enclosure is described.

   What to do: show that R_sat is unchanged after adding microwave filtering and shielding at the mixing-chamber stage, or measure in a second, well-filtered cryostat. Alternatively, deliberately inject broadband or RF noise and compare how the SC zero-resistance state and the AM R_sat respond. The statement that R_sat does not depend on ac excitation down to 500 pA should be supported with data (R_sat versus excitation amplitude), not only stated in the text.

3. The AM sits in a different, two-surface normal state, and the paper does not test a conventional alternative this allows.
   - Fig. 2b marks "surface polarized" and "dual surface" regions. The SC cut (d) lies in the surface-polarized region, and the AM cuts (f, h) lie in the dual-surface region. The main text never discusses this.
   - Fig. 1a places the SC at n = +1.34 x 10^12 cm^-2 and the AM at n = -1.25 x 10^12 cm^-2. The two pockets therefore lie on opposite sides of net neutrality, consistent with compensating carriers on the two surfaces in the AM regime (ref. [6]).
   - This allows an alternative that the paper does not consider. Pairing may occur on one surface while carriers on the opposite surface stay normal. A finite four-terminal resistance could then arise from current conversion between the normal and paired layers near the edge-contacted probes, or from partial coupling between the layers. Such a state would show a sharp onset, finite T-independent saturation at a fraction of R_N, and well-defined critical currents and fields without being an intrinsic "Bose metal".
   - What to do: state explicitly whether the SC/AM boundary coincides with the surface-polarized/dual-surface boundary. Show how R_sat/R_N depends on D at fixed n across the AM pocket. Compare R_sat per unit length across probe pairs with different spacing: a resistance set by current conversion would not scale with length. Discuss this scenario in the extrinsic/inhomogeneity section.

B. Should address

4. Residual perpendicular field and flux flow are not excluded. Methods states that B_par may be offset by up to about 10 mT from trapped flux. The in-plane field is aligned by maximizing the critical current. A 1 degree misalignment at 350 mT gives about 6 mT perpendicular, which is comparable to the SC zero-resistance field scale. In an ultraclean, weakly pinned 2D superconductor, a small residual B_perp can give a finite flux-flow resistance (ref. [52], which the authors cite). The AM's much larger field scale could leave it in exactly such a regime.

   What to do: show R_sat of the AM at I_dc = 0 against B_perp in fine steps through zero, at B_par = 0 and at 350 mT, for both sweep directions, and show whether R_sat at its minimum is finite. Report data taken after the magnet has been demagnetized or quenched, or field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled.

5. Inhomogeneity in density or displacement field is not bounded quantitatively.
   - The pockets are narrow in gate voltage: the SC spans about 0.07 V in Vb (Fig. 1c, 3a).
   - The two-step temperature dependence in Figs. 2d-e (an intermediate plateau, then zero) and the inner/outer domes in Fig. 3b are what a channel with SC and AM segments in series would show.
   - What to do: give an estimate of delta n, for example from the width of the SC/AM boundary in Fig. 2c or the Landau-level broadening in Extended Data Fig. 8. Then test the series picture directly: is the intermediate plateau resistance in Fig. 2e equal to a fraction of the AM R_sat measured at neighbouring Vb, and does it vary between probe pairs? The argument from the 30 nm length scale also depends on the field-scale comparison questioned in concern 1.

6. The reproducibility evidence is thinner than the abstract's "tunability and reproducibility" suggests.
   - Only two devices are reported. Both are eight-layer and both sit on WSe2.
   - In the second device the AM is not clean. For V_xx2 the "AM" footprint shows resistance above the surrounding normal state (Extended Data Fig. 11b), and rho_xy does not reliably vanish in the SC.
   - Thermal cycling is not reported.
   - What to do: give a table of T_c, B_c,perp, B_par,c and R_sat/R_N for each pocket, device and probe pair, using the same criteria throughout, and state how many cooldowns were measured. Either narrow the title and abstract to WSe2-supported eight-layer devices or show the AM without WSe2.

7. The zero/finite distinction needs a stated resolution. The AM R_sat values are only a few Ohm: about 5-8 Ohm in Figs. 1b, 2c and 2i. The noise in the SC trace in the Fig. 1b inset is about 1 Ohm.

   What to do: state the resistance noise floor and an upper bound on the SC resistance. Show R_sat versus T on a log or semi-log scale down to base temperature for several AM gate points, so that "T-independent over a factor of five" can be judged. The electron temperature reaches below 20 mK only in other samples and wiring checks. Give some calibration for this sample, for example the lowest T at which the SC transition or the AM R(T) still changes.

8. The finite Hall signal in the AM is attributed to geometric mixing without a test. The text says rho_xy "remains finite in the AM pocket ... possibly due to geometric mixing with rho_xx" (Extended Data Figs. 2, 11). If it is mixing, rho_xy/rho_xx should equal the ratio in the adjacent normal state at the same gate voltages. A different ratio would point to non-uniform current flow (inhomogeneity) or to two-carrier transport (concern 3). Please report this comparison.

9. The quoted temperature and field ratios do not agree with one another or with the figures, and the noise argument uses them.
   - Critical temperatures: the main text says the SC and AM "share similar maximum critical temperatures (approximately 100 mK)". Methods gives T_c of about 55 mK (SC) and about 130 mK (AM) for the Fig. 1 pockets, and Fig. 1b shows onsets near 45 mK and about 110 mK.
   - In-plane critical fields: the main text says the AM survives in-plane fields "roughly four times larger" than the SC. Methods gives 3.93 T / 1.39 T, about 2.8. Fig. 4d (Vt = 2.30 V) suggests about 4.
   - What to do: specify which gate points and which criterion each number refers to. Revise the argument in concern 2 accordingly: the AM has the higher T_c yet fails to reach zero resistance.

10. The parameter estimates for the AM use SC-pocket inputs. Methods derives k_F and l_mf from the Landau fan at n = 1.3 x 10^12 cm^-2, the SC position, assuming a single degeneracy-two band. It then uses them to give k_F xi of about 9 and xi/l_mf of about 0.005 for the AM. The AM sits at n of about -1.25 x 10^12 cm^-2, next to an unpolarized (degeneracy-four), possibly two-surface metal. Please use quantum-oscillation data taken at the AM gate points, or present these numbers only as order-of-magnitude estimates.

11. The claim that the fluctuations are quantum is qualitative. The Discussion says the excess conductivity is "far larger than standard Aslamazov-Larkin corrections would predict". It also says that standard dissipative models predict quantum phase fluctuations are suppressed when R_sq << R_Q. Neither statement is compared quantitatively with the data. Please give the AL excess conductance (about e^2/16 hbar times T/(T - T_c)) next to the measured excess conductance on the same plot. Give the dimensionless dissipation parameter (R_Q/R_N or the equivalent Caldeira-Leggett parameter) for each pocket, so the reader can see how far outside existing frameworks the data lie.

C. Minor

12. The SNS argument in Methods says xi_N diverges as T goes to 0, so normal strips would become Josephson-coupled. This sits uneasily with the Josephson-junction-array literature the authors cite (refs. [33-38]), where anomalous metals appear in arrays with long normal coherence lengths. Please make this argument more precise or soften it.

13. The I-V curves in Figs. 3d, 3g and Extended Data Fig. 6 are integrated from lock-in dV/dI. In the AM, small offsets accumulate. A direct dc voltage measurement for at least one AM trace would confirm the flat-V region preceding the transition (Fig. 3g) and the power-law exponents in Extended Data Fig. 6f.

14. Field sweeps at B_par = 0 show an instability and hysteresis (Fig. 1e, Extended Data Fig. 3). Methods states that "all of our analyses ... are performed at B_par far above the field needed to suppress this instability", but Fig. 1, the headline zero-field figure, is at B_par = 0. Please reconcile, and say whether R_sat at B_par = 0 depends on sweep history.

15. The resistance overshoot just below T_c in the AM (Fig. 2, "overshoots its eventual base-temperature value") is mentioned but not shown clearly. Please indicate it in a specific trace and say whether it depends on cooling rate or excitation amplitude.

16. Fig. 2b introduces the "surface polarized/dual surface" labels and Extended Data Fig. 8 a half-metal/unpolarized assignment. Define both in the main text and give the criteria used, with reference to ref. [6]. Also say whether the half-metal assignment (no anomalous Hall effect, hence spin-polarized) was checked with in-plane field.

17. The BKT analysis (Extended Data Fig. 6) is described as of "questionable validity" for micron-scale samples, yet it is used to label the SC as "a standard BKT transition". Either state how T_BKT compares with T_c and with the finite-size crossover, or present it as illustrative only.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Re-extract all critical fields and temperatures for both pockets with one criterion and tabulate them by gate point, B_par, probe pair and device. Then rewrite the field-scale argument (Fig. 4) on that basis.
2. Add one direct test of external excitation (extra filtering or controlled noise injection), and show the R_sat versus excitation-amplitude data already in hand.
3. Map R_sat/R_N and the onset of the AM against the surface-polarized/dual-surface boundary and against D at fixed n. Discuss the scenario of one surface paired and the other normal, which could unify the observations without an intrinsic Bose metal.
4. Include a fine B_perp scan through zero, with history control, for the AM saturation resistance to rule out residual-flux flow.
