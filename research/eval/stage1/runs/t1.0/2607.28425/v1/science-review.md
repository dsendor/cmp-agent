A. Must address

1. The SC/AM field-scale comparison uses mismatched criteria and conditions, so it does not show that the paired states differ. The claim that "the paired states in the two regimes are not identical" and that the AM "is unlikely to be simply a phase-disordered version of the adjacent superconductor" (Fig. 4 and its section, p. 5) rests on B_c⊥ ≈ 4 mT (SC) versus ≈ 300 mT (AM), converted to ξ ≈ 300 nm versus ≈ 30 nm. The two numbers come from different features. For the SC, 4 mT is where the inner zero-resistance diamond closes (Fig. 4b), that is, where the critical current vanishes and vortices or phase slips first produce dissipation, not where pairing is destroyed. Figs. 3b and 4a show at a single gate voltage an inner zero-resistance dome nested inside an outer dissipative dome. For the AM, the "critical field" can only be where the finite-resistance plateau returns to the normal state. On the authors' own data the SC's resistive transition extends much further: in Fig. 1d (B∥ = 0) the SC pocket stays below R_N up to roughly 50–75 mT while Fig. 1e shows zero resistance only up to about 10 mT; in Fig. 4c (B∥ = 350 mT) the zero-resistance region ends near 10 mT yet a reduced-resistance region continues to tens of mT, up to about 100 mT, on the SC side. The headline values are also taken at B∥ = 350 mT, which itself suppresses the SC. Methods says critical fields are defined at 90% of R_N, which does not match the inner-diamond reading used in the main text. The in-plane comparison has the same problem: Methods give T_c ≈ 55 mK and B∥,c ≈ 1.39 T for the Fig. 1 SC and T_c ≈ 130 mK and B∥,c ≈ 3.93 T for the Fig. 1 AM, so normalized by T_c both exceed the Pauli limit by nearly the same factor (≈ 14 and ≈ 16), and the in-plane response gives no evidence for distinct pairing. Applying the GL relation Φ0/2πξ² to the outer diamond of a finite-resistance, possibly inhomogeneous state also assumes the homogeneity in question. If the SC's 90%-R_N field is comparable to the AM's, the difference reduces to "the SC has a finite zero-resistance window and the AM does not", which is exactly what a phase-disordered SC would show. The authors should extract B_c⊥ and B∥,c for both pockets with the same criterion (e.g. 50% and 90% of R_N), and separately the field at which the dissipationless window closes, at matched B∥ (including B∥ = 0) and matched reduced temperature, each normalized by the local T_c, plotted against V_b along a trajectory crossing the SC/AM boundary (Fig. 4c, Fig. 1d and B∥ = 0 data). State the definition of the AM critical field explicitly, recompute ξ_SC, k_Fξ and ξ/ℓ_mf, and revise the "distinct paired states" conclusion, the Discussion and the constraint list accordingly.

2. External non-equilibrium excitation (stray radiation) is not experimentally excluded. The paper cites Tamir et al. [45], where improved high-frequency filtering removed the anomalous metal in thin films, yet the Methods describe only phosphor-bronze wiring, a fast-sample-exchange module and a single on-board RC stage (10 kΩ, 1 nF, cutoff ~16 kHz) on the flex PCB. No copper-powder, Eccosorb or other microwave filtering, GHz attenuation or radiation-tight sample enclosure is mentioned; an RC filter of this kind is ineffective at the frequencies (≳ 2Δ/h, tens of GHz for T_c ~ 100 mK) that break pairs or excite phase slips. The counter-argument that, "taking T_c as a proxy for the robustness of the paired state, any source of external non-equilibrium excitation should affect both regions comparably" fails because sensitivity of zero resistance to radiation is set by phase stiffness, condensation or Josephson energy, critical current and vortex pinning, not T_c; on the paper's own evidence the B⊥ scales differ by 10–100× (Fig. 4a–c) and the critical currents differ (Fig. 3a). Moreover the premise of similar T_c is itself inconsistent with Methods (see concern 10): the AM has the higher T_c yet fails to reach zero resistance. The ac-amplitude independence "down to 500 pA" is stated but not shown, and does not test environmental radiation. The authors should describe the full filtering and shielding chain; show R_sat versus excitation amplitude and versus dc bias near I_dc = 0; and ideally show R_sat unchanged after adding cold microwave filtering and shielding at the mixing-chamber stage (or in a second, well-filtered cryostat), and/or deliberately apply broadband noise or microwaves and show that the SC and AM respond in a way inconsistent with the AM being a more fragile superconductor. Without this, the central claim that the AM is intrinsic is not established.

3. Intrinsic electronic phase coexistence (isospin domains or domain walls) is not addressed, and the inhomogeneity arguments given do not exclude it. The Methods ("Considerations of extrinsic origins", p. 5–6) argue that rhombohedral graphene "has no obvious length scale larger than the crystal lattice" and that percolation "should depend sensitively on geometric details". Both target structural disorder. Near a first-order isospin transition, coexisting domains are generic, their footprint in (n, D, B∥) is set by band structure and electrostatics, so it would be reproducible across contact pairs and devices, and their size (tens of nm to sub-micron, set by Coulomb frustration) is not structural. The SC abuts the half-metal and the AM the degeneracy-four unpolarized metal (Fig. 2a, Extended Data Fig. 8, p. 3); the SC pocket shows an instability and hysteresis attributed to orbital magnetization or valley imbalance at B∥ = 0 (Fig. 1b–e, Extended Data Fig. 3); the SC/AM boundary is "abrupt" (Fig. 2c); and ρ_xy is finite in the AM but not the SC (Extended Data Figs. 2, 11). Superconducting domains threaded by non-superconducting isospin domains or domain walls would give a finite T-independent plateau, a large B⊥ scale tied to domain size (the ~30 nm "ξ"), multi-step I–V curves (Fig. 3d), and possibly current-driven domain rearrangement producing the non-monotonic dV/dI. The SNS argument in Methods (ξ_N diverging as T → 0) assumes a normal metal that proximitizes freely; it fails if the normal region is a different isospin phase, e.g. a spin- or valley-polarized metal next to a triplet or valley-polarized condensate whose pairing channel cannot leak into it. The authors should test directly: forward/backward gate-sweep (V_b or D) and B⊥/B∥ sweep hysteresis inside the AM pocket and across the SC/AM boundary; low-frequency or telegraph resistance noise in the AM against the SC and normal regions; quantum oscillations inside or across the AM pocket to test for mixed degeneracy-two and degeneracy-four frequencies (Extended Data Fig. 8c runs along V_t = 3.20 V and does not cross the V_t ≈ 1.1 V AM pocket); whether the Hall density in the AM interpolates between the two phases; and the AM resistance per square for the three probe pairs of Extended Data Fig. 1 against their L/W, as a table or plot rather than colour maps on a shared scale (weak links or domain walls give resistances not proportional to segment length). Discuss how the finite ρ_xy in the AM fits a homogeneous state. Until these come out negative, the claim of "strong evidence that the SC/AM distinction is not a consequence of inhomogeneity" (p. 9) should be limited to structural inhomogeneity.

4. There is no positive evidence that the AM involves pairing rather than a reconstructed normal state with threshold behaviour. Every AM signature is a partial resistance drop (factor of about 4, Figs. 1b, 2c) below an onset temperature and its suppression by B⊥, B∥ and I_dc. An ordering transition in the normal state, such as an isospin or nematic reorder or a density wave, can do the same; the authors' ref. [16] reports charge-density-wave states in rhombohedral graphene, sliding density waves have threshold currents, and the AM borders the half-metal/unpolarized boundary (Fig. 2a) where Fermi-surface reconstructions are expected. The B∥,c ≈ 3.9 T "exceeding B_P by 16" uses a BCS Pauli formula that presupposes pairing. The authors should give positive evidence for pairing: quantum oscillations inside the AM pocket (B⊥ above the AM critical field, same B∥) compared with those just outside, to show the normal-state Fermi surface is the same on both sides of the boundary; magnetoresistance oscillations or interference in I_c(B⊥) at small fields; or a comparison of the functional form of the I_dc and B⊥ suppression with superconducting fluctuation conductivity. Otherwise the title and abstract should present the AM as a finite-resistance state with superconducting-like phenomenology.

5. The AM sits in a different, two-surface normal state, and a conventional alternative this allows is not tested. Fig. 2b marks "surface polarized" and "dual surface" regions: the SC cut (d) lies in the surface-polarized region and the AM cuts (f, h) in the dual-surface region, which the main text never discusses. Fig. 1a places the SC at n = +1.34 × 10¹² cm⁻² and the AM at n = −1.25 × 10¹² cm⁻², on opposite sides of net neutrality, consistent with compensating carriers on the two surfaces in the AM (ref. [6]). Pairing on one surface while the opposite surface stays normal could give a finite four-terminal resistance through current conversion between normal and paired layers near the edge-contacted probes, or through partial interlayer coupling, with a sharp onset, T-independent saturation at a fraction of R_N, and well-defined critical currents and fields, without an intrinsic "Bose metal". The authors should state whether the SC/AM boundary coincides with the surface-polarized/dual-surface boundary; show R_sat/R_N against D at fixed n across the AM pocket; compare R_sat per unit length across probe pairs of different spacing (a conversion resistance would not scale with length); and discuss this scenario in the extrinsic/inhomogeneity section.

6. The double critical current in the SC may come from a single condensate with two scales, which the paper does not consider. In Fig. 3a–d the SC pocket shows an inner critical current (onset of dissipation) and an outer one (return to ohmic), with a reduced-resistance region between, read as an "inner dome associated with the zero-resistance SC and an outer dome associated with the dissipative AM". In a 2D superconductor a depinning or phase-slip current followed by a larger pair-breaking current is generic, the intermediate region being flux-flow or phase-slip dissipation of the same condensate; the AM pocket would then be where the depinning current has gone to zero while the pair-breaking scale survives. The smooth evolution from two steps to one as V_b moves toward the AM (Fig. 3a) fits a continuous loss of phase stiffness at least as naturally as a crossing into a different paired state. The authors should rule this out or present it as a live alternative, e.g. by the T and B⊥ scaling of the inner and outer critical currents and fields (vortex-motion versus depairing forms), by testing whether the intermediate-region resistance scales with B⊥ like flux flow, and by testing whether the intermediate-state resistance at fixed gate equals the AM plateau resistance at the neighbouring gate.

B. Should address

7. Residual perpendicular field, trapped flux and flux flow are not excluded. Methods state that offsets of up to about 10 mT arise from trapped flux; B⊥ is zeroed by symmetrizing ρ_xx or maximizing I_c, and B∥ is aligned by maximizing the critical current. A 1° misalignment at 350 mT gives about 6 mT perpendicular, comparable to the SC zero-resistance field (≈ 4–10 mT, Fig. 4b). In an ultraclean, weakly pinned or low-stiffness 2D condensate, a small residual B⊥, flux inhomogeneity or vortices pinned from earlier sweeps can give a finite flux-flow resistance (ref. [52]), and the AM's larger field scale could leave it in exactly such a regime. The authors should show the AM R_sat at I_dc = 0 against B⊥ in fine steps through zero, at B∥ = 0 and 350 mT, for both sweep directions, and whether its minimum is finite; state the field history before the zero-field AM data (Fig. 1b, f–h); show that the plateau is unchanged after a magnet quench or demagnetization, warming above the trapped-flux scale, or field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled; and discuss how the trapped-flux offset affects the B⊥ asymmetry and hysteresis attributed to intrinsic orbital magnetization (Fig. 1e, Extended Data Fig. 3).

8. The non-monotonic current response is described but not analysed or checked for artefacts. The region where increasing I_dc drives dV/dI to (near) zero before the normal transition (Fig. 3e–g; Extended Data Figs. 4c, f–g, 10c) is presented as a central constraint on theory, but simpler explanations are untested: a series combination of regions with different critical currents and resistances, where a resistive region switches to superconducting at finite bias; current-induced switching of an isospin domain; and self-heating (at 11 mK electron–phonon cooling in graphene is extremely weak, so picowatt dissipation can raise the electron temperature well above the bath, and the dissipated power varies along the I–V curve). The I–V curves (Figs. 3d, g; Extended Data Fig. 6) are integrated from lock-in dV/dI with an ac excitation (0.5–1 nA) comparable to the features (a few nA), so offsets accumulate and narrow structure can be distorted. The authors should estimate the electron temperature against I_dc in the AM (e.g. from normal-state ρ(T) or the I_c(T) calibration); show up- and down-sweeps of I_dc; state the resolution of the "zero" differential resistance there; report V_xy recorded simultaneously to test current redistribution; confirm at least one AM trace, including the flat-V region (Fig. 3g) and the power-law exponents of Extended Data Fig. 6f, with directly measured dc voltage; and say whether a two-element series model reproduces the integrated I–V curves.
