Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript proposes a new kind of spin-polarized STM probe: a FIB-fabricated tip made from the higher-order topological insulator α-Bi4Br4. The claim is that its helical edge modes act as a nonmagnetic, bias-switchable spin filter. The authors use this tip on the layered altermagnet candidate KV2Se2O to claim real-space imaging of checkerboard antiferromagnetic order with a √2×√2 density-wave modulation, and a k-space visualization of d-wave spin splitting between the X and Y valleys. Both parts would be significant if established: a field-insensitive SP-STM probe that works without magnetic training, and the first atomic-scale, dual-space spin-resolved evidence for altermagnetism. The physical basis of the spin selectivity, however, and its separation from spin-independent anisotropies, are not yet demonstrated to the standard the headline claims ("unambiguously demonstrate", "topology-guaranteed spin filter") require.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The tip concept is original and potentially broadly useful. The fabrication route (FIB-milled Bi4Br4 strip on a PtIr tip) is described in detail and appears reproducible: more than twenty tips were made, and about half were usable.
- The topographic data on KV2Se2O are high quality. Arrow-shaped V-site defects (Fig. 3d–f) give an atomically registered handle on the two sublattices and a √2×√2 supercell. This is a useful structural result for a material whose phase transition near 100 K and magnetic structure are debated.
- The quasi-1D QPI along q_x and q_y (Fig. 4a–d), and its comparison with Wannier-based calculations, provide a band-resolved picture of the C-paired X/Y valleys.
- The bias-reversed q_x/q_y anisotropy with the Bi4Br4 tip (Fig. 5f–k) is absent with PtIr tips (Fig. 5b, c). It is reported for three tips, and in rotated domains within one sample (Extended Data Fig. 10). This is a striking observation that deserves careful follow-up.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **No quantitative mechanism for spin selectivity from a time-reversal-symmetric tip, and the sign of P across tips is unexplained (Fig. 1c–e; p. 3; Discussion; Fig. 5l).**
   - **The problem.** The whole k-space claim rests on the premise that tunneling into or out of the helical edge modes of a nonmagnetic tip selects opposite spins at opposite bias. For a T-invariant tip electrode, the spin-resolved tip DOS is spin-degenerate at every energy. In a standard Bardeen/Tersoff–Hamann picture, such a tip cannot produce spin contrast in dI/dV. Any spin selectivity must come from a nonequilibrium, direction-of-flow effect plus a geometric asymmetry that favors one propagation direction.
   - **The authors' own acknowledgement.** They concede this in the Discussion: the two branches "would cancel each other", and the polarization "varies among different tips, possibly due to the specific geometry of the tip apex". The selectivity is therefore not "topology-guaranteed": its magnitude and sign are set by an uncontrolled apex geometry.
   - **The unexplained consistency.** If the sign were set by which hinge or branch dominates, it should be random from tip to tip. Yet all three tips in Fig. 5l give the same sign: positive at negative energy and negative at positive energy. That consistency is itself unexplained, and it is more naturally expected from a spin-independent, bias-asymmetric anisotropy than from an apex-dependent spin filter.
   - **Requested.**
     - (a) A minimal tunneling model (e.g. a Landauer/Keldysh treatment of a point contact coupled to a helical channel with asymmetric drains) that predicts the sign and size of P (~±12%) and its bias odd-ness.
     - (b) A statement of how the spin quantization axis of the tip (S_z of Bi4Br4, set by the strip orientation on the PtIr post) is oriented relative to the KV2Se2O moments.
     - (c) The polarization for all five tips that showed spin resolution, with signs.
     - (d) An explanation of why the sign is consistent across tips.

2. **Spin-independent explanations of the bias-reversing q_x/q_y anisotropy are not excluded (Fig. 5f–k; p. 6–7).**
   - **The problem.** The rotation of the dominant QPI wavevector from q_x to q_y on reversing bias is taken as the "smoking gun". A bias-odd q_x/q_y anisotropy can also arise from spin-independent effects:
     - (i) A density-wave state that breaks the local C4 symmetry (the authors state that the √2×√2 pattern "breaks the original local rotational symmetry" and retains only M_1-10), combined with particle–hole asymmetric scattering from the arrow-shaped defects.
     - (ii) Orbital-selective tunneling matrix elements of a strongly anisotropic, quasi-1D Bi4Br4 tip apex. The [Bi-Br]n chains have a definite in-plane direction, so the tip's orbital character is itself C2. Orbital selectivity that differs for filled and empty tip states (the Bi4Br4 DOS is strongly asymmetric; Fig. 2b, f) can produce a bias-odd x/y contrast with no spin involved.
     - (iii) Setpoint effects. Spatially varying conductance normalisation at V_s = 0.4 V, combined with x/y-anisotropic LDOS, can imprint anisotropy differently at ±V.
   - **The PtIr control does not exclude (ii).** It rules out (i) only if done in the same FOV at the same energies, and it cannot address (ii), which is specific to the Bi4Br4 tip. The statement that artifacts from an anisotropic apex are "decisively" ruled out rests on the rotated-domain test (Extended Data Fig. 10) and is not developed in the main text.
   - **Requested.**
     - (a) Bi4Br4-tip QPI on a nonmagnetic but anisotropic reference surface, ideally a C2 quasi-1D system or the 2×1 K surface of KV2Se2O, showing that no bias-odd anisotropy appears.
     - (b) Bi4Br4-tip QPI on KV2Se2O above the density-wave/magnetic transition, or at least above ~100 K, where the spin contrast should vanish.
     - (c) The "bulk-state" Bi4Br4 tip, which has no helical tunneling channel, on the same FOV, as a near-ideal null that shares the apex geometry and orbital character.
     - (d) The domain-rotation data of Extended Data Fig. 10 brought into the main text, with the same tip shown, unchanged, on both domains.

3. **Internal contradiction on the moment direction, and interpretation of the Zeeman shifts (p. 5–6 vs Methods p. 18; Fig. 4g, h; Extended Data Fig. 7).**
   - **The contradiction.** The main text states that the opposite Zeeman shifts "reveal an in-plane AFM order" and that the dispersion matches calculations "with in-plane Néel AFM order". The Methods state that "the spin orientation for the edge modes is 'horizontal' (Fig. 1c), whereas that for the AFM order in KV2Se2O is out-of-plane". These statements are incompatible.
   - **Why the Zeeman data do not fit an in-plane order.** With an out-of-plane field, in-plane collinear moments on V_x and V_y would cant equally toward the field. Opposite energy shifts for V_x- and V_y-centred defect states point instead to an out-of-plane component, or to a sublattice-dependent g-tensor.
   - **The moment is not the V moment.** The 1.13 μ_B is the Zeeman response of a defect-bound subgap state. It is not the ordered V moment, so its agreement with neutron diffraction (ref. 67) is not evidence. The splitting of 0.9 meV between ±7 T is also comparable to the thermal broadening at 4.8 K (3.5 k_BT ≈ 1.5 meV).
   - **Requested.**
     - (a) State which data were taken in the sub-kelvin system.
     - (b) Give peak positions with fit uncertainties.
     - (c) Report statistics over many V_x and V_y defects: shift sign, magnitude, and the field dependence at intermediate fields rather than only ±7 T.
     - (d) Reconcile the moment direction throughout the manuscript.
     - (e) Explain how an S_z-quantized tip filter couples to the claimed moment orientation, given that P is reported to be finite.

4. **The 2D d-wave altermagnet claim depends on the spin-group symmetry of the reconstructed surface, which is not analysed (Fig. 3g; Discussion p. 7).**
   - **The missing analysis.** The √2×√2 density wave splits each of V_x and V_y into inequivalent sites (V_xa/V_xb, V_ya/V_yb). The surface additionally carries a √2×√2 (or 2×1) K reconstruction. Whether the resulting magnetic structure is still a d-wave altermagnet depends on its spin space group: opposite-spin sublattices must be connected by a rotation or mirror and not by translation or inversion. The paper asserts that the retained M_1-10 mirror "reveal[s] a modulation for a 2D d-wave altermagnetic order", but gives no spin-group analysis of the actual supercell including the moments.
   - **The bulk stacking.** The Discussion concedes that the bulk stacking may be G-type (refs. 63, 67) and that unit-cell steps could not be found. It therefore remains unclear whether the observed state is a property of the surface layer (with its K reconstruction and charge transfer) or of the bulk.
   - **Requested.** A spin-group analysis of the measured supercell, and DFT for the surface slab with the K overlayer, showing that d-wave X/Y spin splitting survives. "Unambiguously demonstrate" should be tempered to what a surface probe establishes.

**B. Should address**

5. **Polarization values lack uncertainties, and the selection of tips may be circular (Fig. 5l; Methods p. 17–18).**
   - P values are shown without error bars. Tip 3 shows only ~2–4%, which may be within the noise of window-integrated FT intensities.
   - The paper should state how "clear spin resolution" was defined for the five of ten tips. If it was defined by the presence of the q_x/q_y anisotropy, the selection is circular.
   - The authors should report P (with bootstrap or FOV-to-FOV errors) for every tip and FOV, including the null ones, and give the number of samples, cleaves and FOVs behind Fig. 5.

6. **The analysis pipeline may imprint or exaggerate anisotropy (Methods p. 19; Fig. 5j, k).**
   - The FTs were twofold symmetrized along q_x or q_y, drift-corrected with Lawler–Fujita, rotated and cropped, and then compared with their π/2-rotated copies. Each step can create or enhance an x/y difference, for example through imperfect correction of a slightly non-square lattice or a choice of symmetrization axis. The integration windows were also "adjusted accordingly at different energies".
   - The authors should show unsymmetrized raw FTs at ±V, give the window positions and sizes for each energy, and show M(q, eV) computed from PtIr and Bi4Br4 data with identical processing in the same FOV.
   - The Bragg-peak normalization I(Q_Bragg, eV) assumes isotropic Bragg intensities. Its x/y ratio should be reported.

7. **The Fe1.02Te benchmark is not decisive (Fig. 2g, h; p. 4).**
   - **Why the reversal is weak evidence.** A bias-reversed stripe contrast in a bicollinear antiferromagnet is presented as "the hallmark" of helical tunneling. Contrast inversion between ±V is, however, common for charge or orbital modulations. Fe1+yTe has a monoclinic distortion coupled to the bicollinear order, which can give spin-independent stripe contrast at some energies.
   - **What would establish it.**
     - The bias dependence of the stripe contrast over a range of energies, compared with PtIr data from the same FOV.
     - A known magnetic-tip (Cr or Fe-coated) result on the same sample.
     - Ideally, the twin-domain dependence, as a check that the contrast follows the magnetic domain.
   - The PtIr comparison is relegated to Supplementary Note 3.

8. **Identification of "helical-edge-mode" tips via a TLL fit is weak (Methods p. 17; Fig. 2f; Extended Data Fig. 3).**
   - **Alternative explanations.** A zero-bias suppression fitted with ρ ∝ T^α cosh(ε/2k_BT) is also produced by dynamical Coulomb blockade or environmental impedance in a resistive tip-to-post junction. Ga implantation and an amorphized surface from FIB milling are also known to cause such anomalies. The switching between "bulk" and "edge" states by voltage pulses could likewise reflect apex rearrangement rather than access to a topological hinge.
   - **Requested.** The fitted α values and their temperature dependence (if available), a comparison with α from edge modes in bulk Bi4Br4 crystals, and a discussion of what fixes the edge geometry at the apex. A demonstration that the apex hosts a gapless hinge rather than gapped edges would strengthen the claim.

9. **Assignment of the √2×√2 modulation to a bulk density wave (Fig. 3e–g; Extended Data Fig. 5; p. 5).**
   - The supercell period coincides with the √2×√2 K reconstruction of the surface studied. The argument against a surface origin rests on its appearance on 2×1 surfaces and across domain walls, shown only in Extended Data.
   - The statement that the "energy gap is strongly suppressed above 100 K" links the modulation to the bulk transition, but the persistence of the defect-correlation supercell itself has not been tracked with temperature.
   - **Requested.** Temperature-dependent topography and QPI of the supercell (or Q* intensity) through 100 K, and diffraction evidence for the bulk √2×√2 order if available.

10. **The ~20 meV gap and insulating behaviour disagree with the literature (Fig. 3b, c; Methods p. 18; Extended Data Fig. 4).**
    - KV2Se2O is reported elsewhere as a room-temperature metallic altermagnet (refs. 40, 41). Here, a ~20 meV hard gap and weakly insulating resistivity are found, which the authors note contradicts ref. 64.
    - The stoichiometry is K:V:Se = 0.975:1.995:2.023, with 0.5% V vacancies, and the gap is strongly inhomogeneous (Fig. 3c). Together these suggest that the samples may differ in doping or phase from those in which altermagnetic ARPES splitting was reported.
    - The authors should discuss whether the altermagnetic conclusion transfers, and should compare the measured band positions with ARPES.

11. **The theory comparison is partly fitted and qualitative (Fig. 4d; Fig. 5e; Methods p. 19–20).**
    - U = 1 eV was chosen as "the best fit for the experimental data". The match of the dispersion in Fig. 4d to in-plane Néel order is then not independent evidence for that order.
    - The authors should show the nonmagnetic and alternative magnetic (C-type, G-type, out-of-plane) calculations against the same data.
    - Fig. 5e (JDOS) is compared with Fig. 5j, k only by sign pattern. A simulated M(q, eV) with a model tip polarization P would allow a quantitative comparison.

12. **Field and temperature robustness of the tip are asserted but not shown (Discussion p. 7).**
    - The claim that the tips "are insensitive to the external magnetic field" and work at "elevated temperatures" is a central selling point, but no SP-QPI or Fe1.02Te contrast is shown at finite B or at higher T.
    - Showing P unchanged at, for example, 0 and 7 T would support both the nonmagnetic-tip premise and the practical claim.

**C. Minor**

13. **Typos and inconsistencies in labels and text.**
    - "Extended Dada Fig." appears several times.
    - "4. 8K"; "three accepts" (should be "aspects"); "statuses".
    - Fig. 4 caption: "V_xα"/"V_yα" vs "V_xa"/"V_ya".
    - Reference 68 and later appear only in the Methods list.

14. **Fig. 2h setup conditions.** The setup is listed as V_s = 50 mV, while the maps are at ±50 mV. State the setup for each map, and whether the setpoint was the same for both.

15. **Fig. 5 panel order and definitions.**
    - Panel l is placed before e.
    - The definition of P uses I(q_x¹) − I(q_y¹), but Fig. 5l is labelled "Spin contrast". Clarify which spin is positive.

16. **Subgap Friedel period.** The subgap states are said to oscillate with λ ≅ π/k_F. Specify k_F (from Fig. 4d) and show the comparison quantitatively.

17. **Defect-to-sublattice assignment.** The assignment of the twofold defects to V vacancies "two-layer underneath" relies on registry alone. Simulated STM images of a V vacancy, including the arrow-shaped asymmetry, would support it.

18. **Missing setup conditions and resolution.** Give the lock-in modulation and temperature for the Fig. 5 maps, and the q-resolution (FOV ~50 nm), relative to the window sizes used for P.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a dedicated null-control section for the Bi4Br4 tip:
   - the bulk-state Bi4Br4 tip and a PtIr tip on the same FOV;
   - the helical tip on a nonmagnetic anisotropic surface;
   - the helical tip above the KV2Se2O transition temperature.
2. Provide a quantitative tunneling model for spin selectivity with a T-symmetric helical tip, predicting the sign, magnitude and bias dependence of P. Use it to explain the tip-to-tip sign consistency in Fig. 5l.
3. Resolve the moment direction (in-plane vs out-of-plane) consistently across the main text, Methods and calculations, and back it with defect-resolved Zeeman statistics measured at sub-kelvin temperature.
4. Temper "unambiguously" and "topology-guaranteed" unless the controls above are added, and state clearly which conclusions concern the reconstructed surface layer and which concern the bulk.
