Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript proposes using FIB-fabricated α-Bi4Br4 tips, in which tunneling is said to be dominated by helical edge (hinge) modes, as nonmagnetic spin-selective STM probes whose spin sensitivity reverses with bias polarity. It applies these tips to KV2Se2O and claims (i) a √2×√2 density-wave modulation of the V lattice that retains one diagonal mirror, (ii) opposite local moments on the Vx and Vy sublattices from field-dependent subgap states, and (iii) a bias-reversing qx/qy anisotropy in QPI, read as a direct momentum-space image of d-wave altermagnetic spin splitting. If both the tip mechanism and the sample assignment hold, this would be a new SP-STM modality and the first atomic-scale, dual-space look at an altermagnet, which would interest a broad readership. As presented, however, both rest on indirect evidence. The physics that would let a time-reversal-symmetric tip produce spin contrast is not given, and the bias-reversing anisotropy has non-spin explanations that the paper has not excluded.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The concept is original. Using a topological material's edge modes as an intrinsic spin filter, with no tip magnetization to train or keep stable, would be a useful addition to the SP-STM toolbox.
- The paper characterizes the Bi4Br4 tips seriously: SEM images, spectra on Au(111) compared with bulk-crystal spectra measured with PtIr tips, TLL-like zero-bias suppression, and in-situ switching between bulk-state and edge-mode tips.
- The KV2Se2O surface work is careful in places. Cryo-cleaving gives ordered K surfaces, the arrow-shaped V-site defects are atomically registered, the defect correlations reveal a √2×√2 supercell, and the paper includes a gap map and QPI dispersion compared with DFT.
- Several kinds of evidence are combined: real-space defect symmetry, Zeeman shifts of subgap states, and spin-resolved QPI with a PtIr reference tip. The paper also reports how many tips were made and how many showed spin resolution.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The mechanism by which a time-reversal-symmetric tip yields bias-reversing spin contrast is not established.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 1c–e, p. 3 ("electrons injected into or launched from the tip will carry opposite spins (tied to the opposite group velocities)"), Discussion p. 7.
   - *What is wrong:* At any energy a helical edge carries both +k/↑ and −k/↓ states. Tunneling into or out of an edge mode at the apex has no intrinsic propagation direction. Selecting one branch requires a broken symmetry in the junction (which end of the edge the apex sits on, an asymmetric coupling to the two branches). The paper concedes this implicitly ("tunneling involving two branches … would cancel"; P varies between tips). A tip that keeps time-reversal symmetry, in linear-response elastic tunneling, gives zero spin-polarized conductance for a single channel. The bias-sign rule in Fig. 1e is therefore not a generic consequence of helicality. It needs a specific model of the apex geometry, the branch coupling, and how the sign of V sets the spin.
   - *Why it matters:* Every spin assignment in Figs. 2g–h and 5 depends on this mechanism.
   - *What to do:* Give a tunneling model, at least a tight-binding junction calculation, showing that the bias sign reverses the selected spin. State which assumptions about apex geometry this needs, and how the tip's spin quantization axis relates to the sample axes. Then derive from that model the expected size and sign of P.

2. **The Fe1.02Te benchmark does not separate spin contrast from energy-dependent charge or orbital contrast.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 2g–h, p. 4.
   - *What is wrong:* A stripe contrast that reverses between +50 and −50 mV can come from a spin-averaged LDOS with particle-hole asymmetry. Bicollinear Fe1+yTe also has a monoclinic distortion and an accompanying charge/orbital modulation at the same wavevector. Contrast reversal with bias is common in nonmagnetic STM of density waves. The PtIr comparison (Supplementary Note 3, not available here) only shows the stripes are absent in topography, not in bias-dependent dI/dV maps.
   - *Why it matters:* This is the only test of spin sensitivity on a system with a known spin structure.
   - *What to do:* Show PtIr (and bulk-state Bi4Br4) dI/dV maps of the same Fe1.02Te area at ±50 mV and at several other energies, demonstrating that the stripe contrast and its reversal are absent. Better still, add a test that only spin can explain: a field-switched Fe1+yTe domain or magnetic adatoms of known moment, or a direct comparison with a Fe-coated tip on the same surface. Show the energy dependence of the contrast, not two biases only.

3. **The bias-reversing qx/qy QPI anisotropy has non-spin explanations that the paper does not exclude.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 5f–l, pp. 6–7 ("cannot be attributed to artifacts … this decisively rules out…").
   - *What is wrong:*
     - By the authors' own account (Fig. 3g), the sample is not C4 symmetric: the √2×√2 density wave keeps only M_1-10, and there are 2×1 K domains. A spin-independent qx/qy intensity difference is therefore allowed.
     - Its sign can change with energy because the Vx- and Vy-derived bands, and the density-wave gap edges (Fig. 4d), carry different spectral weights above and below E_F.
     - An anisotropic or multi-apex tip also produces direction-dependent QPI weights that need not be symmetric in energy.
     - The PtIr reference (Fig. 5b–c) appears to come from different fields of view, and possibly different samples and surface domains. Matched conditions are not stated.
   - *What to do:*
     - Take PtIr and Bi4Br4 data, or bulk-state and edge-mode states of the same Bi4Br4 tip (switched in situ, as in Ext. Data Fig. 3e–f), on the same field of view and domain, at the same energies and setpoints.
     - Show a full energy dependence of P(E) for the nonmagnetic tip with error bars.
     - Show that on a rotated domain (Ext. Data Fig. 10) the anisotropy follows the sample axes while the tip's spin axis is fixed.
     - Ideally, measure the same region with two Bi4Br4 tips of different crystal orientation. The predicted change of sign or amplitude would be a strong discriminating test.

4. **The in-plane versus out-of-plane magnetic moment direction is internally inconsistent, and this undermines both the Zeeman analysis and the spin-filter argument.**
   - *Where:* p. 6 ("This finding reveals an in-plane AFM order"); Ext. Data Fig. 7 (DFT with in-plane Néel order); Methods p. 18 ("the spin orientation for the edge modes is 'horizontal' … whereas that for the AFM order in KV2Se2O is out-of-plane").
   - *What is wrong:* The paper states both an in-plane and an out-of-plane moment direction. With a field perpendicular to the sample (±7 T, Fig. 4g–h), in-plane collinear moments on the two sublattices would cant identically, and to first order would not give opposite Zeeman shifts. Opposite shifts point instead to an out-of-plane component that is opposite on the two sublattices.
   - *Why it matters:* The spin filter is said to be sensitive to "horizontal" spin. If the sample moments are out of plane, it is unclear what spin projection the tip measures and why there is any contrast. If they are in plane, the Zeeman result needs a different explanation.
   - *What to do:* Resolve this. State the moment direction, justify it with the field data (ideally adding in-plane field data), and state the tip's spin axis.

5. **Whether the proposed structure is an altermagnet has not been established by a symmetry analysis.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 3g, p. 5; Discussion p. 7.
   - *What is wrong:*
     - The paper does not determine the spins of the Vxa/Vxb (and Vya/Vyb) inequivalent sites within the √2×√2 supercell. Without them the spin space group of the reconstructed surface layer is undetermined.
     - Depending on these spins, the supercell could include a translation (or translation plus spin flip) that relates opposite spins. Or it could make the two spin sublattices inequivalent, giving a ferrimagnet with net moment rather than an altermagnet.
     - The paper also acknowledges that neutron data favour G-type stacking. That would make the bulk spin-degenerate, which is not a bulk d-wave altermagnet. The surface layer also sits under a √2×√2 K reconstruction that by itself lowers the symmetry.
   - *Why it matters:* The headline "d-wave altermagnet" (abstract: "a layered d-wave altermagnet") needs the full magnetic structure.
   - *What to do:*
     - Give the full spin-group analysis for the proposed surface magnetic structure, including the density wave and the K reconstruction.
     - State which spin configurations of the a/b sites are consistent with the data, and show that the d-wave spin splitting survives in the folded Brillouin zone.
     - Narrow the claim to "the top V2Se2O layer" and drop "unambiguously" unless interlayer order can be addressed.

B. Should address

6. **Tip selection risks circularity.**
   - *Where:* Methods pp. 17–18.
   - *What is wrong:* Of more than twenty tips, ten were used on KV2Se2O and "only five of them show clear spin resolution". The other five are attributed after the fact to a spin-axis mismatch, an explanation that itself conflicts with concern 4. If "spin resolution" on KV2Se2O means "shows the qx/qy anisotropy", the selection uses the outcome being tested.
   - *What to do:* Report, for every tip, its Au(111) classification, its Fe1.02Te result (only two tips were tested there), and its KV2Se2O P(E), including tips with no contrast. State the selection criterion before showing the data.

7. **The analysis pipeline and the statistics of P are not robust.**
   - *Where:* Methods p. 19; Fig. 5g, i, l.
   - *What is wrong:*
     - The Fourier transforms are "twofold symmetrized along qx or qy". The integration windows are shifted at each energy, and the normalization uses Bragg peaks that come from the K reconstruction, whose intensities may be bias dependent and anisotropic.
     - Fig. 5l has no error bars. Tip 3 gives only about 3–5%, Tip 1 has data at negative energies only, and the sign change near 10 meV lies inside the about 20 meV gap.
   - *What to do:*
     - Show raw, unsymmetrized Fourier transforms.
     - Fix the windows from an independent criterion, such as the DFT dispersion, and show that the results are insensitive to window size and position.
     - Normalize in alternative ways, for example by total power or by the setpoint-effect-corrected L(r) map.
     - Give statistical uncertainties on P, for example from bootstrap over sub-fields of view.
     - Test for the setpoint effect: Vs = 0.4 V for all maps, while the normalization at each energy depends on the integrated LDOS.

8. **The local moment from the subgap-state Zeeman shifts is over-interpreted.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 4g–h, p. 5.
   - *What is wrong:* The defects are assigned as V vacancies, so the subgap state lives on neighbouring sites. Converting a 0.9 meV splitting over 14 T into a "local moment of 1.13 μB" assumes a free-spin Zeeman form with a known g factor. In a strongly exchange-coupled AFM, a bound state's field shift does not measure a site moment.
   - *What to do:*
     - Show the full field dependence (more than ±7 T, linearity, hysteresis) for several defects of each type, with statistics on the sign.
     - State which tip was used, whether field-induced tip changes were excluded, and how the peaks were fitted.
     - Temper the comparison with the neutron moment.

9. **DFT parameters were chosen by fit, and the comparison does not discriminate between magnetic orders.**
   - *Where:* Methods p. 20; Fig. 4c–d; Fig. 5e.
   - *What is wrong:* U = 1 eV was chosen because it "provided the best fit". The QPI dispersion along qx is then said to "well match" in-plane Néel AFM. A quasi-1D band dispersion near E_F is unlikely to distinguish Néel, C-type, G-type or non-magnetic states.
   - *What to do:* Show calculated CECs, JDOS and spin-resolved QPI for the competing orders, and with and without the √2×√2 density wave. Use these to show which features specifically require altermagnetic spin splitting. Also say whether the JDOS in Fig. 5e includes the density-wave folding and the tip's matrix elements.

10. **Evidence that the tip tunnels through edge modes is indirect.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 2f, Ext. Data Fig. 3; Methods p. 17.
   - *What is wrong:* Identifying an "edge-mode tip" rests on a zero-bias suppression fitted with a TLL form. A zero-bias suppression can also come from dynamical Coulomb blockade at the PtIr/Bi4Br4/Pt-pad junction, or from Ga-implanted, amorphized surface layers left by FIB milling. The TLL expression given, ρ ∝ T^α cosh(ε/2k_BT), is not the standard finite-temperature form (which includes the |Γ(...)|² factor) and should be corrected.
   - *What to do:* Give the fitted α values with uncertainties and temperature dependence. Show TEM or EDS of the apex if available. Discuss why environmental Coulomb blockade is ruled out, and how the hinge-mode location relates to the milled apex geometry.

11. **The claim that the tips are "insensitive to the external magnetic field" is not demonstrated.**
   - *Where:* Discussion p. 7.
   - *What to do:* If Bi4Br4 tips were used in Fig. 4g–h, show that P or the Fe1.02Te contrast is unchanged with field. Otherwise remove or qualify the claim.

12. **The intrinsic √2×√2 density wave and its relation to the 100 K transition rest on data not shown in the main text.**
   - *Where:* p. 5, Ext. Data Fig. 5.
   - *What to do:*
     - Quantify the defect spatial correlation (correlation function versus distance, number of defects, domain sizes).
     - Show the persistence on 2×1 surfaces.
     - Give the temperature dependence of the gap with explicit temperatures.
     - Support the "V vacancy two layers underneath" assignment with simulated defect images.
     - The gap map threshold (current below 0.3 pA at a fixed setpoint) depends on the setpoint. Discuss its robustness.

C. Minor

13. **Overclaiming wording.** "Unambiguously demonstrate", "decisively rules out", "topology-guaranteed spin filter" and "smoking gun" overstate indirect evidence. Remove or qualify them.

14. **Typos and labels.**
   - "Extended Dada Fig." appears in several places.
   - "4. 8K" (p. 17) should read 4.8 K.
   - In Fig. 5, panel l is placed before e.
   - In Fig. 4, "V_m" is used where "V_mod" is meant.
   - "he National Natural Science Foundation" is missing the "t".

15. **Undefined quantity.** The spin Hall conductivity "3.62×e/4π per layer" is quoted without context. Clarify the units and the meaning.

16. **Missing basic information.**
   - The temperature of the QPI and field measurements (4.8 K or sub-kelvin).
   - Which tip type was used for Fig. 4.
   - The number of samples and cleaves behind Fig. 5.

17. **Transport and the energy gap.** Weakly insulating ρ(T) is said to be consistent with the STM gap. Show whether the transport shows an anomaly near 100 K, and give the corresponding activation energy.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Run an in-situ control on the same area: switch one Bi4Br4 tip between its bulk-state and edge-mode configurations, image the same KV2Se2O field of view in each, and show that P(E) appears only in the edge-mode state. This would remove most worries about field of view, domain and sample.
2. Give a microscopic tunneling model of the helical tip that predicts the sign and size of P, together with the tip's spin quantization axis. Test it with tips of known, different crystallographic orientation.
3. Benchmark the tip on a magnetic system whose spin contrast can be switched (magnetic adatoms or field-switchable domains), so that spin sensitivity is shown independently of any bias-reversal argument.
4. Narrow the altermagnetism claim to what the data support (the surface V2Se2O layer, with an explicit spin-group analysis that includes the density wave), and present bulk stacking as open.
