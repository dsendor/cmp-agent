Review outline:

Note on scope: the file reviewed contains the main text, Figures 1-5 and Methods (21 pages). The Extended Data figures and Supplementary Notes cited throughout (for example ED Figs. 1-10, Supplementary Notes 2-8) are not included, so this assessment rests on the main text and main figures. Where a concern touches material said to be in the Extended Data, I ask for it to be shown quantitatively or moved into the main text rather than assuming it is absent.

1. Significance and novelty

The paper proposes a new kind of spin-sensitive STM probe: a FIB-fabricated tip made from the higher-order topological insulator / 3D quantum spin Hall material alpha-Bi4Br4. The tip's helical edge modes are meant to select tunneling spin through the sign of the bias. The authors use it on the layered altermagnet candidate KV2Se2O to claim three things: (i) a √2×√2 density-wave supercell that keeps one diagonal mirror, (ii) opposite spin polarization of defect-bound states on the Vx and Vy sublattices, and (iii) a qx/qy QPI intensity anisotropy that rotates by π/2 when the bias is reversed, read as direct k-space evidence of d-wave spin splitting. A nonmagnetic, field-insensitive spin probe would be a significant technical advance, and atomic-scale real- and momentum-space evidence for altermagnetism is timely. However, the central inferences rest on a spin-selection mechanism that is not modelled and on a bias-reversing anisotropy that has several non-spin explanations, none of which is excluded by a matched control.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A creative and potentially general idea: using the spin-momentum locking of a topological material as an intrinsically nonmagnetic spin filter for STM, which would avoid the training, field dependence and instability of magnetic-cluster tips.
- Substantial technical work: more than twenty FIB-fabricated Bi4Br4 tips, calibration on Au(111), in-situ switching between "bulk-state" and "helical-edge-mode" tip states (Fig. 2f), and atomic resolution on difficult cryo-cleaved KV2Se2O surfaces.
- A detailed atomic-scale characterization of KV2Se2O surfaces: ordered √2×√2 K reconstruction, a ~20 meV gap with nanoscale variation (Fig. 3b,c), classification of arrow-shaped V-site defects into four types, and unidirectional QPI with dispersion compared against DFT (Fig. 4a-d).
- Fig. 5 shows a bias-reversing qx/qy QPI anisotropy with three BiBr tips, a π/2-rotated domain (ED Fig. 10), and a PtIr comparison. If the remaining alternatives can be excluded, this is an attractive signature.
- The opposite-sign magnetic-field shifts of subgap states on Vx and Vy defects (Fig. 4g,h) are a promising sublattice-resolved probe.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The physical mechanism by which bias polarity selects spin is asserted, not shown, and it must respect reciprocity. (Figs. 1c-e and 5d; pages 3 and 7.) The argument is that electrons "injected into or launched from" the tip travel along the helical edge in opposite directions and therefore carry opposite spins. At any energy, however, a helical edge contains both counter-propagating, oppositely spin-polarized branches, and the time-reversal-symmetric tip has no net spin polarization. Spin selectivity can only arise from a non-equilibrium, geometry-dependent current path along a single continuous edge from the apex to the reservoir, as proposed for SmB6 nanowires (ref. 62). The paper itself notes (Fig. 1c, page 7) that two branches or hinges cancel. Moreover, two-terminal linear-response conductance obeys G(M) = G(-M) (Onsager). A signal that depends on the sign of the sample spin and reverses with bias must therefore be a nonlinear effect, whose size should scale with bias and current in a definite way. Without a model, the reader cannot judge whether ±12% polarization at 10-40 mV and ~1 nA through a GΩ junction is plausible. The authors should:
   - give a quantitative model of the apex geometry, the number of edge channels contributing, how the current divides along hinges, and the resulting P(V);
   - test its predictions: P versus setpoint current and tip height at fixed bias, and P(V) measured continuously through V = 0 rather than only at |V| ≥ 5-10 meV;
   - state the spin quantization axis of the tip relative to the sample for each tip.
   This mechanism underlies every spin-resolved claim in the paper.

2. The bias-reversing qx/qy anisotropy (Fig. 5f-k) is not shown to be spin-borne rather than charge-, orbital- or tip-borne. The authors themselves report a √2×√2 density wave whose pattern "potentially breaks the original local rotational symmetry" and keeps only the M1-10 mirror (Fig. 3g, page 5). A C2-symmetric, particle-hole-asymmetric electronic structure can by itself give qx ≠ qy contrast that reverses between filled and empty states, especially across the ~20 meV gap in which the ±10/±15 meV maps sit. Three other non-spin sources are possible:
   - The tip is cut from a quasi-1D crystal. Its apex orbital character is anisotropic and differs above and below EF, so it can weight qx and qy differently at the two bias polarities.
   - Vx defects scatter along x and Vy defects along y (Fig. 4a). Unequal numbers of the two defect types in a FOV therefore bias I(qx)/I(qy) directly.
   - The quantity M(q,eV) = [I(q) - R_{π/2}I(q)]/I(Q_Bragg) is a B1g nematic order parameter. Calling it a "magnetic contrast map" assumes the conclusion.

   The π/2-rotated domain in ED Fig. 10 shows that the anisotropy follows the sample rather than the tip. It does not separate spin from a C2 charge or orbital domain, because a √2×√2 density-wave domain would rotate in the same way. The existing PtIr control (Fig. 5b,c) has three weaknesses: it is at ±15 meV while the BiBr data are at ±10/−15 meV; it does not appear to be the same FOV; and it does not report the Vx/Vy defect counts. The decisive control is already available: the authors can switch a BiBr tip in situ between its bulk-state and helical-edge-mode states (page 4; Methods). They should show, on the same FOV and at identical energies, setpoints and analysis windows:
   - M(q,eV) and P(E) with the helical-edge-mode tip, the bulk-state BiBr tip and a PtIr tip;
   - the number of Vx and Vy defects in each FOV;
   - the full energy dependence of P for the non-spin tips, not a single energy pair.

   Only if the bulk-state and PtIr tips give P ≈ 0 at all energies on the same area, while the helical tip gives the reversal, does Fig. 5 support "smoking gun" evidence of d-wave spin splitting.

3. The Zeeman analysis of the subgap states (Fig. 4g,h; page 5) does not establish a 1.13 μB V moment or opposite sublattice spins at the level claimed. An energy difference of 0.9 meV between ±7 T gives an effective moment of about 1.1 μB. That is simply what a g = 2, S = 1/2 quasiparticle level would give. It reflects the spin of the bound quasiparticle state, not the size of the ordered V moment, so the comparison with the neutron moment (ref. 67) is not meaningful as stated. The data have further gaps:
   - Only one defect of each type is shown, with spectra at +7 T and −7 T only. A full field sweep (0, ±1, ..., ±7 T) is needed to establish linearity, the shift of each peak separately, and the absence of tip changes. Statistics over many Vx and Vy defects (Supplementary Note 5) should be summarized in the main text.
   - The energy scale is comparable to thermal broadening at 4.8 K (3.5 k_BT ≈ 1.4 meV). The authors should state whether these data were taken in the sub-kelvin Unisoku system, and with which tip and what effective electron temperature.
   - In a collinear antiferromagnet, the sign of a bound state's Zeeman shift is determined by its spin relative to the field. If the moments are in-plane, as stated on page 5, a perpendicular field couples to them only through canting, and opposite shifts on the two sublattices are not obviously expected. The authors should model the shift for the proposed spin structure.

4. The spin orientation of the AFM order is stated inconsistently, and this affects whether the helical tip can sense it at all.
   - The main text concludes "an in-plane AFM order" (page 6), and Fig. 4d/ED Fig. 7 compare with "in-plane Néel AFM order".
   - Methods (page 18) explains why only five of ten tips showed spin resolution by saying the edge-mode spin is "horizontal" whereas the KV2Se2O "AFM order ... is out-of-plane".
   - The perpendicular-field shifts in Fig. 4g,h are more naturally read as out-of-plane moments.

   The tip polarization measures the projection of the sample spin on the tip's quantization axis. The authors must therefore state a single moment direction with its evidence (neutron, DFT magnetic anisotropy, field data). They should then show that the measured P is consistent with the geometry of each tip's Bi4Br4 crystal axes relative to the sample Néel vector. As it stands, the paper's own explanation of the tips that fail implies that the tips that succeed should not see the order either.

B. Should address

5. The abstract's real-space claim is not directly supported. The abstract says that "atomic-registered mapping demonstrates the checkerboard antiferromagnetic order". The real-space evidence actually presented has two parts. One is a √2×√2 supercell inferred from where the arrow-shaped defects sit (Fig. 3d-g), which is a structural or charge statement. The other is the field shifts of two defects (Fig. 4g,h). No spin-polarized topograph or dI/dV map is shown that resolves Vx against Vy sublattice contrast and reverses with bias, analogous to the FeTe test in Fig. 2g,h. Either such maps should be added (with a PtIr or bulk-state reference on the same FOV), or the abstract and the discussion ("unambiguously demonstrate", page 7) should be narrowed.

6. The density wave is not separated from the surface K reconstruction. The √2×√2 K reconstruction (Fig. 3a) has the same periodicity as the claimed V-lattice supercell. The nesting vector Q* "is the same as the surface Bragg vector" (Fig. 4c caption), so the band folding and gap in Fig. 4d could be imposed by the surface K potential rather than by a bulk density wave. The argument that the correlation persists on 2×1 surfaces and that the gap is suppressed above 100 K rests on ED Fig. 5. The authors should:
   - show quantitatively, in the main text, the defect-sublattice correlation on 2×1 domains;
   - give the temperature dependence of the supercell itself, not only of the gap;
   - provide a bulk probe of the superlattice (for example, XRD or electron diffraction superlattice reflections on these crystals).
   The weakly insulating transport (Methods) also differs from the metallic behaviour reported in ref. 64. Sample dependence (K content, V vacancies) should be discussed, since it bears on whether this is a generic property of KV2Se2O.

7. The symmetry analysis of the combined density-wave and magnetic structure is incomplete.
   - The altermagnet claim requires that no translation or inversion maps opposite-spin sublattices in the folded √2×√2 cell with the proposed spin structure. The text only notes one surviving M1-10 mirror. The authors should give the spin space group of the combined structure, include the supercell in the DFT/JDOS used for Fig. 5e, and state which bands the qx^1 and qy^1 vectors connect after folding.
   - The Discussion acknowledges that neutron data favour G-type stacking, for which the bulk would be Kramers-degenerate. The STM signal then reflects the top layer or the surface termination with K reconstruction. The title and abstract claims ("2D d-wave altermagnet" in a layered material) should be stated as a surface-layer result, and the authors should discuss how far the probed layer is representative.

8. The FeTe benchmark is not conclusive (Fig. 2g,h).
   - Enayat et al. (ref. 51) made spin-polarized tips precisely by picking up excess Fe from the Fe1+yTe surface. A BiBr tip scanning Fe1.02Te could therefore become a conventional magnetic-cluster tip.
   - Contrast inversion between filled- and empty-state images is common for charge modulations, so bias reversal alone is not "the hallmark for the tunneling of helical states".
   The authors should show three things on the same FeTe area: that the stripe contrast is absent with the bulk-state BiBr tip; that it is unchanged under reversal of an applied field (a cluster tip with remanent moment would respond); and the full P(V), not only ±50 mV. They should also state whether any tip used on KV2Se2O had previously been used on FeTe.

9. The evidence that tunneling occurs through helical edge modes at the apex is indirect.
   - The "helical-edge-mode" tip state is identified by a zero-bias suppression fitted to a Tomonaga-Luttinger form (Methods, ED Fig. 3). Power-law zero-bias anomalies also arise from dynamical Coulomb blockade in a resistive environment, which is plausible for a semiconducting strip bonded with FIB-deposited Pt. FIB milling also amorphizes and Ga-implants surface layers to tens of nm, comparable to the 44 nm apex (Fig. 2e).
   - The fitted α values with uncertainties, the temperature dependence of the anomaly (which distinguishes TLL from environmental blockade), and any TEM/EDS of an apex should be given.
   - The claim that the tips are "insensitive to the external magnetic field" (page 7) is untested in the paper. Helical edge modes acquire a Zeeman gap in field, and Fig. 5 data appear to be at zero field. SP-QPI at finite field would test this claim and would also help separate spin from charge in concern 2.

10. Reproducibility and statistics of the polarization are thin (Fig. 5l; Methods page 18).
   - Five of ten tips showed spin resolution, yet only three appear in Fig. 5l. Tip 1 has data only at negative bias, and Tip 3 reaches only ~3-5%.
   - If the spin axis is set by the orientation of each tip's crystal, which is not controlled relative to the sample axes, the sign of P should vary between tips. Instead all three tips show the same sign pattern. This should be explained, as it is more naturally expected from a sample-borne C2 effect.
   - The q-space integration windows are "adjusted accordingly at different energies" (Methods). Their positions, and the robustness of P to window size and placement, should be shown.
   - P should carry error bars, for example from FOV subsampling or from background variation.
   - The number of samples, cleaves and FOVs behind Fig. 5 should be stated.

11. The theory is not compared quantitatively with the data. U = 1 eV was chosen because it "provided the best fit for the experimental data" (Methods), so the agreement in Fig. 4d and ED Fig. 7 is partly by construction. Sensitivity to U and to C- versus G-type order should be shown. The spin-resolved JDOS in Fig. 5e is compared only qualitatively. A calculation that includes a partially spin-polarized tip (with the polarization from concern 1) and predicts the magnitude and energy dependence of P would make the k-space claim testable.

C. Minor

12. Sign inconsistency in the polarization. With P = (I(qx) - I(qy))/(I(qx) + I(qy)), the statement that qx is more intense at positive bias (page 6; Fig. 5f,g) implies P > 0 at positive bias. Fig. 5l shows P < 0 at positive bias and P > 0 at negative bias. Please reconcile the definition, the text and the figure, including the "↑<↓" labels in Fig. 5.

13. Mismatched energies between the PtIr and BiBr comparison (Fig. 5b,c at ±15 meV against Fig. 5f,j at +10 meV). Please use identical energies and colour scales. State whether Fig. 5b,c, f and h come from the same sample and cleave, and give the defect counts in each FOV.

14. Methods states that the FTs were "twofold symmetrized ... along qx or qy" and rotated. Please confirm that no operation mixes qx and qy before M and P are computed, and show one unsymmetrized FT.

15. Fig. 3b: indicate where each spectrum was taken. Fig. 3c: the gap is defined by a 0.3 pA current threshold from a 1.5 nA setpoint, so state how sensitive the map is to this threshold. Fig. 4d: explain the statement that back-folding not at k_F is "typical for density-wave order".

16. Stoichiometry (Methods page 18). The K:V:Se ratio 0.975:1.995:2.023 combines a surface vacancy count with EDS. Give the EDS uncertainty, which typically exceeds the quoted precision.

17. Wording. "Topology-guaranteed", "decisively rules out" (page 7) and "unambiguously demonstrate" overstate the case. The edge modes in the 3D QSHI are described as "slightly gapped" on some hinges and Sz is only approximately conserved (Methods), so the spin filter is neither guaranteed nor complete, which the 12% polarization reflects.

18. Typos and editorial: "three accepts" (page 3), "Extended Dada Fig." (pages 16-17), "4. 8K", "statuses", "Phenomenally" (presumably "Phenomenologically"), "colinear", "he National Natural Science Foundation". Fig. 2h axis labels are hard to read.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Use the in-situ switchability of the BiBr tip as the central control. Acquire the same QPI FOV with the helical-edge-mode state, the bulk-state tip and a PtIr tip at identical energies, setpoints and windows. Report M(q,E) and P(E) with uncertainties for all three, together with the Vx/Vy defect counts.
2. Add a minimal transport/tunneling model of the Bi4Br4 apex that respects reciprocity and predicts P(V, I_set). Test it with P versus setpoint current and with P measured continuously through zero bias, and reconcile the tip spin axis with a single, well-supported moment direction for KV2Se2O.
3. Strengthen the real-space sublattice evidence. Add full field sweeps and defect statistics for the Zeeman shifts at sub-kelvin temperature, and spin-polarized maps resolving Vx/Vy contrast with bias reversal, each with a non-spin reference on the same area.
4. Separate the √2×√2 density wave from the surface K reconstruction. Use a bulk diffraction probe and the 2×1 surfaces, and give the spin-group analysis and DFT/JDOS of the combined density-wave and magnetic structure. Then frame the title and abstract as a surface-layer d-wave altermagnet if bulk G-type stacking cannot be excluded.
