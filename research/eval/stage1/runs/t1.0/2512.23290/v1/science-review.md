A. Must address

1. **The mechanism by which a time-reversal-symmetric tip gives bias-reversing spin contrast is asserted, not shown (Fig. 1c–e; p. 3; Discussion p. 7; Fig. 5l).** The argument is that electrons "injected into or launched from the tip will carry opposite spins (tied to the opposite group velocities)". At any energy, though, a helical edge carries both counter-propagating, oppositely spin-polarized branches (+k/↑ and −k/↓). A time-reversal-invariant tip has a spin-degenerate DOS at every energy, so in a Bardeen/Tersoff–Hamann picture, or in linear-response elastic tunneling through a single channel, it gives no spin contrast. Two-terminal linear-response conductance also obeys Onsager reciprocity, G(M) = G(−M). Spin selectivity can therefore only come from a non-equilibrium, direction-of-flow effect combined with a geometric asymmetry of the junction that favours one branch: for example, a single continuous edge from the apex to the reservoir, as proposed for SmB6 nanowires (ref. 62), or asymmetric coupling to the two branches. A signal that depends on the sign of the sample spin and reverses with bias must then be nonlinear, and its size should scale with bias and current in a definite way. The paper concedes the point: tunneling through two branches or hinges "would cancel each other", and P "varies among different tips, possibly due to the specific geometry of the tip apex". The selectivity is therefore not "topology-guaranteed"; its magnitude and sign are set by an uncontrolled apex geometry. This creates a further puzzle. If the sign of P were set by which hinge or branch dominates, or by the uncontrolled orientation of each tip's crystal relative to the sample, it should vary from tip to tip. Yet all three tips in Fig. 5l show the same pattern, positive at negative energy and negative at positive energy. That consistency is more naturally expected from a sample-borne, spin-independent, bias-asymmetric anisotropy. Without a model, the reader cannot judge whether ~±12% polarization at 10–40 mV and ~1 nA through a GΩ junction is plausible. Every spin assignment in Figs. 2g–h and 5 depends on this mechanism. The authors should:
   - give a quantitative tunneling model (a Landauer/Keldysh treatment of a point contact coupled to a helical channel with asymmetric drains, or at least a tight-binding junction calculation) that respects reciprocity. It should specify the apex geometry, the number of edge channels contributing and how the current divides along hinges, and predict the sign, size (~±12%) and bias-oddness of P(V);
   - test the model's predictions: P versus setpoint current and tip height at fixed bias, and P(V) measured continuously through V = 0 rather than only at |V| ≥ 5–10 meV;
   - state how each tip's spin quantization axis (S_z of Bi4Br4, set by the strip orientation on the PtIr post) is oriented relative to the sample axes and the KV2Se2O moments;
   - report P, with signs, for all five tips that showed spin resolution, and explain why the sign is the same across tips.

2. **Spin-independent explanations of the bias-reversing q_x/q_y QPI anisotropy are not excluded, and the PtIr control is not matched (Fig. 5b–c, f–l; pp. 6–7).** The rotation of the dominant QPI wavevector from q_x to q_y on reversing bias is presented as a "smoking gun" that "cannot be attributed to artifacts" and "decisively rules out" alternatives. By the authors' own account (Fig. 3g, p. 5), though, the sample is not C4 symmetric: the √2×√2 density wave "potentially breaks the original local rotational symmetry" and keeps only the M_1-10 mirror, and there are also 2×1 K domains. Several non-spin sources can therefore give a q_x/q_y contrast that reverses with bias:
   - A C2-symmetric, particle-hole-asymmetric electronic structure. The V_x- and V_y-derived bands and the density-wave gap edges (Fig. 4d) carry different spectral weight above and below E_F. The ±10/±15 meV maps sit inside the ~20 meV gap, and the sign change of P near 10 meV lies within it. Scattering from the arrow-shaped defects may also be particle-hole asymmetric.
   - Orbital-selective tunneling matrix elements of the tip. The tip is cut from a quasi-1D crystal whose [Bi-Br]n chains have a definite in-plane direction, so its apex orbital character is C2. Its DOS is strongly asymmetric between filled and empty states (Fig. 2b, f), so it can weight q_x and q_y differently at the two bias polarities. A multi-apex tip has a similar effect.
   - Defect populations. V_x defects scatter along x and V_y defects along y (Fig. 4a), so unequal numbers of the two types in a field of view (FOV) bias I(q_x)/I(q_y) directly.
   - The setpoint effect. All maps use V_s = 0.4 V, and spatially varying conductance normalization combined with an x/y-anisotropic LDOS can imprint anisotropy differently at ±V.

   The quantity M(q,eV) = [I(q) − R_{π/2}I(q)]/I(Q_Bragg) is a B1g nematic order parameter; calling it a "magnetic contrast map" assumes the conclusion. The π/2-rotated domain in Extended Data Fig. 10 shows that the anisotropy follows the sample rather than the tip. It does not separate spin from a C2 charge or orbital domain, however, because a √2×√2 density-wave domain would rotate in the same way. It is also not developed in the main text. The PtIr control (Fig. 5b, c) has several weaknesses. It is at ±15 meV while the BiBr maps are at +10/−15 meV (Fig. 5f, j). It does not appear to come from the same FOV, and possibly not from the same sample, cleave or surface domain. It does not report V_x/V_y defect counts. And it cannot address tip-orbital effects specific to Bi4Br4. The decisive control is already available, because a BiBr tip can be switched in situ between its bulk-state and helical-edge-mode states (p. 4; Methods; Extended Data Fig. 3e–f). The authors should:
   - show M(q,eV) and P(E) for the helical-edge-mode tip, the bulk-state BiBr tip and a PtIr tip on the same FOV and domain, at identical energies, setpoints, colour scales and analysis windows, with the number of V_x and V_y defects in each FOV and whether all data come from the same sample and cleave;
   - give the full energy dependence of P, with error bars, for the non-spin tips, not a single energy pair;
   - measure Bi4Br4-tip QPI on a nonmagnetic but anisotropic reference surface, ideally a C2 quasi-1D system or the 2×1 K surface of KV2Se2O, and on KV2Se2O above the ~100 K transition, where spin contrast should vanish;
   - move the rotated-domain data into the main text, with the same tip shown unchanged on both domains and the tip's spin axis fixed;
   - ideally, measure one region with two Bi4Br4 tips of different crystal orientation (where the model of concern 1 predicts a change of sign or amplitude) and, at finite field, where a spin-borne signal should respond.

   Only if the bulk-state and PtIr tips give P ≈ 0 at all energies on the same area, while the helical tip gives the reversal, does Fig. 5 support "smoking gun" evidence of d-wave spin splitting.

3. **The direction of the magnetic moment is stated inconsistently, and this affects both the Zeeman analysis and whether the tip can sense the order at all (p. 6; Fig. 4d; Extended Data Fig. 7; Methods p. 18).** The main text concludes that the opposite Zeeman shifts "reveal an in-plane AFM order", and Fig. 4d/Extended Data Fig. 7 compare with calculations "with in-plane Néel AFM order". Methods (p. 18) explains why only five of ten tips showed spin resolution by saying the edge-mode spin is "horizontal" whereas the KV2Se2O "AFM order … is out-of-plane". These statements are incompatible. The field data also sit uneasily with in-plane moments. Under an out-of-plane field (±7 T, Fig. 4g, h), in-plane collinear moments on V_x and V_y would cant equally toward the field and, to first order, would not give opposite Zeeman shifts. Opposite shifts point instead to an opposite out-of-plane component on the two sublattices, or to a sublattice-dependent g-tensor. Because the tip polarization measures the projection of the sample spin on the tip's quantization axis, the inconsistency matters: as written, the paper's explanation of the tips that fail implies that the tips that succeed should not see the order either. The authors should state a single moment direction with its evidence (neutron, DFT magnetic anisotropy, field data, ideally adding in-plane field data). They should then show that the measured P is consistent with the geometry of each tip's Bi4Br4 crystal axes relative to the sample Néel vector, and explain how an S_z-quantized filter couples to that orientation given that P is finite.

4. **Whether the proposed structure is a d-wave altermagnet has not been established by a symmetry analysis, and the claim concerns the surface layer, not the bulk (Fig. 3g, p. 5; Fig. 5e; Discussion p. 7).** The √2×√2 density wave splits each sublattice into inequivalent sites (V_xa/V_xb, V_ya/V_yb), and the surface also carries a √2×√2 (or 2×1) K reconstruction. Whether the resulting magnetic structure is still a d-wave altermagnet depends on its spin space group: opposite-spin sublattices must be connected by a rotation or mirror, and not by a translation or inversion. The paper does not determine the spins of the a/b sites within the supercell. Depending on them, the supercell could contain a translation (or translation plus spin flip) relating opposite spins, or it could make the two spin sublattices inequivalent and give a ferrimagnet with a net moment. The paper asserts only that the retained M_1-10 mirror "reveal[s] a modulation for a 2D d-wave altermagnetic order". The Discussion also concedes that neutron data favour G-type stacking (refs. 63, 67), for which the bulk is Kramers-degenerate and not a bulk d-wave altermagnet, and that unit-cell steps could not be found. The STM signal therefore reflects the top layer with its K reconstruction and charge transfer. The authors should:
   - give the spin space group of the combined density-wave, K-reconstruction and magnetic structure, and state which a/b spin configurations are consistent with the data;
   - show, by DFT of the surface slab with the K overlayer and with the supercell included in the DFT/JDOS used for Fig. 5e, that the d-wave X/Y spin splitting survives in the folded Brillouin zone, and state which bands q_x¹ and q_y¹ connect after folding;
   - state the title and abstract claims ("a layered d-wave altermagnet", "2D d-wave altermagnet") as a surface-layer result, discuss how far the probed layer is representative of the bulk, present bulk stacking as open, and drop "unambiguously demonstrate" unless interlayer order can be addressed.

5. **The Zeeman analysis of the subgap states does not establish a 1.13 μ_B V moment or opposite sublattice spins at the level claimed (Fig. 4g, h; p. 5).** An energy difference of 0.9 meV between ±7 T gives an effective moment of about 1.1 μ_B, which is simply what a g = 2, S = 1/2 quasiparticle level gives. Converting it into a "local moment" assumes a free-spin Zeeman form with a known g factor. The defects are assigned as V vacancies, so the bound state lives on neighbouring sites, and in a strongly exchange-coupled antiferromagnet a bound state's field shift reflects the spin of that quasiparticle state, not the ordered site moment. The comparison with the neutron moment (ref. 67) is therefore not evidence as stated. In a collinear antiferromagnet the sign of the shift is set by the bound state's spin relative to the field, so the shifts expected for the proposed spin structure should be modelled (see concern 3). The data are also thin. Only one defect of each type is shown, at +7 T and −7 T only. The 0.9 meV splitting is comparable to thermal broadening at 4.8 K (3.5 k_BT ≈ 1.4–1.5 meV). The authors should:
   - show a full field sweep (0, ±1, …, ±7 T) establishing linearity, the shift of each peak separately, any hysteresis, and the absence of tip changes;
   - give peak positions with fit uncertainties and the fitting procedure;
   - summarize statistics over many V_x and V_y defects in the main text (Supplementary Note 5), including the sign and magnitude of the shift;
   - state whether these data were taken in the sub-kelvin Unisoku system, with which tip, and at what effective electron temperature;
   - temper the comparison with the neutron moment.

8. **The Fe1.02Te benchmark does not separate spin contrast from charge or orbital contrast, or from a magnetic-cluster tip (Fig. 2g, h; p. 4; Supplementary Note 3).** This is the only test of spin sensitivity on a system with a known spin structure. A stripe contrast that reverses between +50 and −50 mV is presented as "the hallmark for the tunneling of helical states". Contrast inversion between filled- and empty-state images is, however, common for charge modulations, and a spin-averaged particle-hole-asymmetric LDOS can produce it. Bicollinear Fe1+yTe also has a monoclinic distortion and an accompanying charge/orbital modulation at the same wavevector, which can give spin-independent stripe contrast. Moreover, Enayat et al. (ref. 51) made spin-polarized tips precisely by picking up excess Fe from the Fe1+yTe surface, so a BiBr tip scanning Fe1.02Te could become a conventional magnetic-cluster tip. The PtIr comparison is relegated to Supplementary Note 3 and appears to show only that the stripes are absent in topography, not in bias-dependent dI/dV maps. The authors should, on the same Fe1.02Te area:
   - show PtIr and bulk-state Bi4Br4 dI/dV maps at ±50 mV and at several other energies, demonstrating that the stripe contrast and its reversal are absent;
   - give the full energy dependence P(V) of the stripe contrast, not only ±50 mV;
   - show that the contrast is unchanged under reversal of an applied field (a cluster tip with a remanent moment would respond);
   - ideally add a test only spin can explain: twin- or field-switched-domain dependence showing the contrast follows the magnetic domain, magnetic adatoms of known moment, or a direct comparison with a Cr- or Fe-coated tip on the same surface.

   They should also state whether any tip used on KV2Se2O had previously been used on Fe1.02Te.

10. **The FT analysis pipeline may create or exaggerate the x/y anisotropy (Methods p. 19; Fig. 5g, i–l).** The FTs were "twofold symmetrized … along q_x or q_y", drift-corrected with Lawler–Fujita, rotated and cropped, and then compared with their π/2-rotated copies. Each step can create or enhance an x/y difference, for example through imperfect correction of a slightly non-square lattice or the choice of symmetrization axis. The integration windows were "adjusted accordingly at different energies". The normalization uses Bragg peaks that come from the K reconstruction, whose intensities may be bias dependent and anisotropic. The authors should:
    - confirm that no operation mixes q_x and q_y before M and P are computed, and show raw, unsymmetrized FTs at ±V;
    - give the window positions and sizes at each energy, fix them from an independent criterion such as the DFT dispersion, and show that P is insensitive to window size and placement and to the q-resolution set by the ~50 nm FOV;
    - report the x/y ratio of the Bragg intensities and normalize in alternative ways, for example by total power or by a setpoint-corrected L(r) map;
    - compute M(q,eV) from PtIr and Bi4Br4 data with identical processing in the same FOV.

11. **The evidence that tunneling occurs through gapless helical edge modes at the apex is indirect (Fig. 2e, f; Extended Data Fig. 3; Methods p. 17).** The "helical-edge-mode" tip state is identified by a zero-bias suppression fitted to a Tomonaga–Luttinger (TLL) form. Power-law zero-bias anomalies also arise from dynamical Coulomb blockade in a resistive environment, plausible for a semiconducting strip in a PtIr/Bi4Br4/FIB-deposited-Pt junction. FIB milling also amorphizes and Ga-implants surface layers to tens of nm, comparable to the 44 nm apex (Fig. 2e). The switching between "bulk" and "edge" states by voltage pulses could reflect apex rearrangement rather than access to a topological hinge. The TLL expression given, ρ ∝ T^α cosh(ε/2k_BT), is not the standard finite-temperature form, which includes a |Γ(…)|² factor, and should be corrected. The authors should give the fitted α values with uncertainties and their temperature dependence (which distinguishes TLL from environmental blockade), compare them with α for edge modes in bulk Bi4Br4 crystals, show TEM/EDS of an apex if available, and discuss why environmental Coulomb blockade is ruled out and what fixes the hinge location relative to the milled apex. A demonstration that the apex hosts a gapless hinge rather than gapped edges would strengthen the claim.
