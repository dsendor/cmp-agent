Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports field-free, bidirectional, current-driven switching of the resistance of nanoscale PtMn3/MgO/PtMn3 all-antiferromagnetic tunnel junctions (AFMTJs). It reports TMR up to 363% at room temperature and switching current densities of about 10 MA/cm². The authors attribute the switching to a new torque, "octupole-driven spin-transfer torque" (OTT): an imbalance between intra- and inter-sublattice spin currents, inferred from DFT conduction-channel maps and put into a three-sublattice LLG model. If the switching is shown to be a reversal of the octupole order driven by spin transfer, this would be the first electrical write and read of an all-AFM tunnel junction through the junction itself, which would matter for both fundamental and applied spintronics. As submitted, the link between the resistance switching and the magnetic order rests on indirect evidence, and the theory is qualitative.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The topic is timely and important. Current-induced switching through an AFMTJ is a long-sought, Onsager-reciprocal partner to the TMR already reported in noncollinear AFMTJs.
- Devices are made on 150 mm thermally oxidized Si wafers with industrial sputter tools. Switching is reported in pillars from 50 to 200 nm across 36 devices (Fig. 2b, Extended Data Fig. 1).
- The temperature dependence (Fig. 2c) is consistent with a magnetic origin: switching and TMR disappear above ~360 K, near the reported noncollinear-to-collinear transition of Mn3Pt.
- The sublattice-resolved conduction-channel picture (Fig. 3) gives an intuitive route from a spin-neutral total current to a nonzero staggered torque, and it is connected to the cluster-octupole description (Extended Data Fig. 6).

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **No evidence that the resistance states are magnetic (octupole) states rather than resistive switching of the barrier.** The only evidence for switching is two-terminal R versus pulse-current loops (Fig. 2a,c; Extended Data Fig. 1). Bipolar resistive switching in thin MgO (oxygen-vacancy or filament formation, interface redox at the Mn-rich electrodes) gives exactly this kind of signal: square or gradual bipolar hysteresis, high/low ratios of several hundred percent, and a broad device-to-device distribution (Fig. 2b: most devices <100%, one at 363%). The bias is also in the range where that happens. In Fig. 2a (100 nm pillar) the high state is ~2.4 kΩ at a switching current of ~1 mA, i.e. ~2.4 V across a nominal 2 nm MgO, which is ~1.2 V/nm and near the MgO breakdown and soft-breakdown regime. In several Extended Data Fig. 1 devices (e.g. 100 nm pillars driven to ±4–6 mA) the dissipated power is several mW. The manuscript has no measurement that ties the two resistance levels to the PtMn3 magnetic configuration. The authors should provide:
   - (a) magnetic-field-swept R(H) loops on the same devices, showing that the same high and low levels can be set by field, as was done for the field-switched PtMn3 and Mn3Sn AFMTJs (refs. 11, 12);
   - (b) the dependence of the switching current on an applied magnetic field;
   - (c) a reference junction in which the effect should be absent, measured under matched pulse conditions. Options: Pt/PtMn3/MgO/Pt with a nonmagnetic counter-electrode, a stack with a collinear AFM or nonmagnetic metal in place of one PtMn3, or the same stack above 360 K with the current amplitude rescaled;
   - (d) I–V curves and bias-dependent TMR, so that the read resistances can be compared with known MgO tunnelling behaviour.
   
   Without at least (a) or (c), "OTT switching of the CMO" is not established.

2. **The switching polarity is not consistent across nominally identical devices.** In spin-transfer switching the current direction that favours P or AP is fixed by the stack: which layer is free and the sign of the torque. In Fig. 2a, positive current switches low→high and negative current switches high→low. In Fig. 2c, positive current switches high→low. Extended Data Fig. 1 contains both senses: in panel a, positive current takes the device low→high; in the first panel of c, positive current takes it high→low. Fig. 2d labels positive J as AP→P, the opposite of Fig. 2a. The OTT model (Eqs. 4–8, Extended Data Fig. 5) predicts one polarity for a given stack. If the polarity really varies from device to device, that argues against a spin-transfer mechanism and is typical of filamentary or thermally driven switching. The authors should:
   - state the current-sign convention;
   - tabulate the polarity for all 36 devices;
   - explain any polarity variation within the OTT picture, for example by showing which electrode switches in each case.

3. **The microscopic mechanism assumes coherent, k∥-conserving, sublattice-preserving tunnelling, which the measured structure does not support.** The theory (page 7; Fig. 3c–e) assumes ballistic transport with conserved k∥ and electrons that "preferentially retain their sublattice identity" between the two electrodes. The STEM data (Fig. 1c,d and page 5) show:
   - MgO that "did not indicate well-defined crystallization";
   - visible roughness at the bottom interface;
   - polycrystalline PtMn3 with only a predominant (111) texture.
   
   Across an amorphous barrier, k∥ is not conserved. For in-plane rotated grains on the two sides there is no fixed correspondence between sublattice i in the fixed layer and sublattice i in the free layer, so the β1 (intra) versus β2 (inter) distinction that drives the torque (Eqs. 5–8) is not defined grain by grain. The same issue applies to explaining a 363% TMR with such a barrier. The authors should either show the mechanism survives with these features or narrow the claim. Possible routes:
   - a calculation with k∥ not conserved (diffusive or amorphous-barrier limit);
   - averaging over relative in-plane grain rotations;
   - an explicit argument that the net staggered torque survives incoherent tunnelling;
   - an independent structural measurement showing in-plane grain registry across the barrier.

4. **The magnetic state of the electrodes is not characterised in the device films.** The noncollinear T1g order requires chemically ordered L1₂ Mn3Pt at the right stoichiometry. The device stack is deposited at room temperature with no post-annealing (Methods). The FFTs in Fig. 1d index only fundamental (111) reflections, which do not show L1₂ chemical order; superlattice reflections such as (100) or (110) would. The composition of the device PtMn3 is not reported. The NV sample is a different film: co-sputtered, on MgO(100) annealed at 850 °C, and (100) oriented. The 360 K disappearance in Fig. 2c is consistent with the Mn3Pt transition but is the only magnetic evidence for the device films. The authors should provide, for the device stack itself:
   - XRD or STEM evidence of L1₂ ordering;
   - composition (e.g. RBS, XRF or EDS);
   - ideally a magnetic or magnetotransport signature of noncollinear order, such as the anomalous Hall effect in an unpatterned Hall bar of the same PtMn3/Pt.

**B. Should address**

5. **Joule heating is not quantified, although the device sits close to its transition temperature.** The pulses are 1 ms long (and up to 50 ms in Fig. 2d) at ~10–80 MA/cm² through a resistive barrier. By the authors' own data, switching vanishes at 360 K, only ~65 K above the measurement temperature. Millisecond pulses reach thermal steady state. If the free layer is heated above the noncollinear ordering temperature during the pulse, the "switching" may be thermally assisted reordering set by a current-induced field, an Oersted or thermal gradient, or exchange from the other layer, not a spin torque. The authors should:
   - estimate the pulse temperature, for example from the temperature dependence of the low-bias resistance (Fig. 2c) used as a thermometer against the resistance during the pulse;
   - show the switching current versus pulse width down to the ns regime, where heating is much smaller.

6. **The energy-barrier analysis is internally inconsistent and its agreement with theory is circular.**
   - In Fig. 2d the two branches have clearly different slopes and intercepts. From the plotted lines, AP→P gives Δ ≈ 25–30 kT and P→AP gives Δ ≈ 50 kT. The single value of 39 kT is not explained; it may be an average.
   - The paper states (page 7) that Δ = 39 kT is "consistent with our theoretical estimation". However, Methods (page 23) says Keff = 1 kJ/m³ was extracted by *matching* the numerics to 39 kT, so there is no independent check.
   - The thermal-activation model (ref. 50) assumes a single-domain macrospin. The text says some devices switch by domains (page 6). t0 = 1 ps is assumed, not justified for this system.
   - Fig. 2d is from an unspecified device. Its J values (~50 and −80 MA/cm² at ln(tp/t0) ≈ 20.7, i.e. 1 ms) are 5–8 times the "~11 MA/cm²" quoted for 1 ms pulses in the text and abstract.
   
   The authors should report Δ for each branch with fit uncertainties, identify the device, and either derive Keff independently or remove the "consistent with theory" statement.

7. **The quoted switching current density does not match the data shown.** The text states that pillars from 50 to 200 nm switch "at similar J values" (page 6). From Extended Data Fig. 1:
   - 50 nm, ~0.4–0.6 mA: ~20–30 MA/cm²;
   - 100 nm devices switching at ~1–5 mA: ~13–65 MA/cm²;
   - 200 nm, ~2.5–4 mA: ~8–13 MA/cm².
   
   Fig. 2d gives 50–90 MA/cm². The authors should give a table of switching currents, diameters, J, RA and TMR for all measured devices, and state whether J_c depends on diameter.

8. **Which layer switches, and why, is not established.** Both electrodes are the same material with no pinning layer. They differ only in thickness (7 nm bottom, 10 nm top) and interface quality. STT acts on both electrodes with opposite sign. The paper does not say which layer is "free". The modelling is also inconsistent on this point: Methods uses a 7 nm layer for the energy barrier (Extended Data Fig. 3b) but d = 10 nm for the free layer in the LLG simulation (Extended Data Table 1). The authors should state which layer they believe switches and give evidence, for example thickness-asymmetry variation. They should also explain why the other layer does not also switch at similar current density, given that its anisotropy is the same in the model.

9. **The theory does not quantify the torque, and β1 − β2 is not computed.** The DFT section presents k∥-resolved channel maps (Fig. 3a,b) and a schematic argument at a single k-point k1 along Γ–K (Fig. 3c,d). The key quantity, βeff = β1 − β2 integrated over the Brillouin zone, is never calculated. In the LLG simulation it is set to 1, the maximum possible (Extended Data Table 1). The value η = 0.53 is not traced to any calculation. The statements that the mechanism is "confirmed by our DFT calculations" (page 4) and that the simulation shows switching "consistent with the experimental observations" are therefore not quantitative. The authors should:
   - compute the BZ-integrated intra- and inter-sublattice spin currents, or better, the non-equilibrium staggered torque for an actual PtMn3/MgO/PtMn3 junction including interfaces (e.g. NEGF);
   - use the resulting βeff and η in the LLG model, and compare the predicted critical current with experiment.

10. **The micromagnetic model is not connected to the experimental regime, and its parameters disagree with the rest of the paper.**
    - Extended Data Table 1 uses Ms = 153 kA/m, Ku = 10 kJ/m³, A_AB = −10 pJ/m and a0 = 0.5 nm. The energy-barrier model uses Ms = 1110 emu/cm³, J = 280 MJ/m³ and Keff = 1 kJ/m³. These differ by an order of magnitude in Ms and K.
    - The simulation is a zero-temperature macrospin switching in ~3 ps at J = 10 MA/cm². The critical current is not determined, and the simulation is not related to the ms-pulse, thermally activated experiment.
    
    The authors should use one consistent parameter set, find the zero-temperature critical current density as a function of βeff and K, and include thermal fluctuations if the comparison is to the Fig. 2d data.

11. **The NV domain-size measurement does not represent the devices and lacks controls.** The NV film (Extended Data Fig. 2) is (100)-oriented, epitaxial and grown after an 850 °C substrate anneal by co-sputtering. The devices are (111)-textured, polycrystalline and unannealed. The 100–170 nm "domain size" is not described: how it was extracted from the Fourier spectrum is not stated. The stray field could come from grain-scale surface moments, roughness or uncompensated Mn, not octupole domains. A nonmagnetic or above-transition control scan is missing. The authors should either measure domain or grain sizes in the device stack (e.g. grain-size statistics from plan-view TEM) or soften the argument that domain size explains which devices switch abruptly (page 6).

12. **The temperature dependence comes from one device, with no check of reversibility.** Fig. 2c shows one device whose switching vanishes at 360 K. Irreversible changes, such as barrier degradation or interdiffusion after cycling to 400 K, could also remove the hysteresis. The authors should show that switching and TMR come back on cooling to 300 K. They should also give the temperature dependence of J_c and TMR for several devices. This would also test whether the J_c(T) trend follows the ordering temperature.

13. **Statistics, yield and endurance are not given.** Fig. 2a shows a single loop. The authors should report:
    - how many devices were fabricated and how many switch;
    - cycle-to-cycle distributions of R_high, R_low and I_c over many loops;
    - endurance;
    - retention of both states.
    
    These numbers are needed to support "reversible, repeatable" switching and the memory claims. The headline 363% comes from one device out of 36 (Fig. 2b). The abstract and title should present it as a maximum, not as typical behaviour.

**C. Minor**

14. The colourbar of Fig. 3b runs from 0 to 15, but the plotted quantity is a spin polarization N(i↓) − N(i↑), and the text says the minority channel dominates along the perpendicular Γ–M axis, which should give negative values. Clarify whether the plotted quantity is signed and fix the colourbar. Fig. 3c–e are schematic and should be labelled as such.
15. The notation for majority and minority is confusing: majority is written N(i↓) and minority N(i↑) (page 8, Extended Data Fig. 4), and "octupole minority" is T₊ (page 9, Methods). Define the conventions once.
16. Methods says PBE was used, but ref. 64 is the PBEsol paper. The choice of U = 2 eV for metallic Mn in PtMn3 needs justification or a sensitivity test. State whether the lattice was relaxed, and with what magnetic configuration.
17. Methods (page 26) refers to the torques "as indicated in Extended Data Fig. 1a with the green arrows"; this should be Extended Data Fig. 5a. Eq. (11) is written as a scalar (2Ku/Ms m_i·e_i) but should be the vector 2Ku/Ms (m_i·e_i) e_i.
18. The diameter and RA of the device in Fig. 2c are not given, and the read-current magnitude is not stated (Methods says only that the read current is "1 ms"). State whether the TMR includes series lead and electrode resistance.
19. The error bars in Fig. 2d are not defined: number of repetitions, standard deviation or range.
20. Reference list: number 34 is missing, ref. 56 is cited in the main text before 51–55, and refs. 60, 61 (cited as Keff literature values) do not obviously report PtMn3 anisotropy. Typo "theorectical" in the author contributions.
21. Some outlook statements go beyond the data: "fully eliminating dipole interaction" (a thin-film PtMn3 may carry a small net moment), and the "room-temperature terahertz technologies" in the abstract. No dynamics were measured. Tone these down.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add field-swept R(H) loops and the field dependence of the switching current on the same devices, plus a nonmagnetic-electrode reference junction. Together these would turn the central claim from plausible to convincing.
2. Measure switching with ns pulses and quantify the pulse temperature. This separates spin-torque switching from thermally assisted reordering near the 360 K transition, and gives a far more reliable energy-barrier and intrinsic-J_c analysis than 0.1–50 ms pulses.
3. Turn the theory into numbers: compute βeff (and η) by integrating the conduction-channel analysis over the BZ, or better, compute the torque for the real junction. Then use one consistent parameter set in the energy-barrier and LLG models, and compare the predicted J_c with experiment.
4. Characterise the device PtMn3 directly: L1₂ order, composition, grain size and texture, and the anomalous Hall effect in a companion film. Then discuss how polycrystallinity and the amorphous MgO affect both the 363% TMR and the k∥-conserving OTT mechanism.
