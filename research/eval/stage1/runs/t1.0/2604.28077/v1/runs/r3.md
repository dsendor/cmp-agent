Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors use a 10 mK qPlus non-contact AFM to take Kelvin-probe (CPD) and dissipation spectroscopy on LaAlO3/SrTiO3 interfaces patterned by ULV-EBL and c-AFM. They claim that the dissipation signals superconductivity locally: quadratic (ohmic) bias dependence is suppressed, and a ΔV⁴ term appears. From one CPD/dissipation line cut taken near the critical field, they conclude that superconductivity is concentrated in ~200 nm, optimally doped strips at the edges of the patterned regions, which would support the one-dimensional edge-channel picture of LAO/STO nanostructures. Applying mK nc-AFM dissipation to a buried oxide superconductor is new and technically hard, and spatially resolving where superconductivity lives in patterned LAO/STO is an important open question. However, the identification of the dissipation contrast with superconductivity rests almost entirely on a magnetic-field sweep at one point, and there is no temperature dependence and no normal-state reference.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- This is the first scanning-probe measurement on patterned LAO/STO at temperatures below the superconducting Tc. The same sample also has in situ magnetotransport, which is a rare and valuable combination.
- CPD contrast between written and unwritten regions is clear. The bare-STO control (Supp. Fig. 3, Section 2) shows that the contrast requires the LAO layer.
- The field-bias dissipation map (Fig. 4c) shows a strong, nearly symmetric field dependence on a ~100 mT scale. That scale is plausibly related to superconductivity and deserves to be reported.
- The question is timely, the device platform is well established, and the method could be applied to many other buried superconducting interfaces.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **There is no temperature dependence and no normal-state reference at zero field, so the dissipation contrast cannot yet be attributed to superconductivity.**
   - Where: Figs. 4b and 5; text pp. 8–9 and 13.
   - The problem: the central claim is that the reduced ΔV² dissipation and the ΔV⁴ term at point A (Fig. 4b) are "a robust local marker of superconductivity." Every dissipation dataset was taken at T = 10 mK.
   - Why the comparison does not isolate superconductivity: point A lies in a conducting 2DES and point B on insulating, unwritten interface. Their dissipation would differ even in the normal state. In the standard Joule/electronic-friction picture (Volokitin–Persson, ref. 32; Kisiel, refs. 17, 31), the loss depends non-monotonically on sheet resistance and screening length. A well-screening normal metal can therefore show much less loss than a poorly conducting dielectric with mobile trapped charge.
   - The reference experiment the claim needs: Kisiel et al. (ref. 31), which the authors rely on for the ΔV⁴ interpretation, compared spectra above and below Tc at the same location.
   - Requested: Γ(V) at point A (and in the 200 nm strip of Fig. 5) at T well above Tc (e.g. 400–600 mK), and at zero field versus B ≫ Bc (e.g. 0.5–1 T), with the same tip height and amplitude. Report how α and β change. If the ΔV⁴ term and the low baseline survive above Tc, they are not superconducting signatures.

2. **The ΔV⁴ "marker" is also present at the non-superconducting reference point, and the fit is not discriminating as presented.**
   - Where: Fig. 4b caption and text p. 9.
   - The text says point B shows "a clear quadratic dependence." Yet the caption gives β/α = 1.84 ± 0.01 V⁻² for B, against 3.85 ± 0.03 V⁻² for A: a factor of two, not presence versus absence.
   - Over the bias range used (|ΔV| up to ~0.7 V for A, ~0.5 V for B), the quartic term contributes a comparable fraction of the curvature at both points.
   - The two curves also span different bias windows, so the fitted β/α depends on the fit range. It is not stated whether a constant offset was a free parameter.
   - Quoted ±0.01 uncertainties from least squares do not capture systematics such as fit window, offset treatment, CPD drift and height drift.
   - Requested:
     - Fit both points over the same |ΔV| window, with the offset stated.
     - Show residuals for the pure-quadratic and quadratic-plus-quartic models, with an F-test or information criterion.
     - Give β/α across many points inside and outside the pattern (a histogram or map), rather than one point each.
   - Without this, "ΔV⁴ emerges in superconducting regions" is not supported.

3. **The field scale of the dissipation does not match the transport critical field quoted as its "direct connection."**
   - Where: text pp. 10–11, 13; Figs. 4c–e, 1e.
   - What the text claims: dissipation at the CPD "reach[es] a maximum as it approaches the critical magnetic field" (~200 mT), and the curvature reversal occurs as the field approaches 200 mT. This coincidence is offered as "a direct connection between the dissipation signals and the superconducting state."
   - What the figures show: in Fig. 4e the dissipation maxima are at ≈ −70 mT and ≈ +90 mT, and Γ has already fallen substantially by ±200 mT. In Fig. 4d the 42 mT (orange) curve already departs from the convex shape, and the 102 mT (cyan) curve is concave.
   - So the dissipation crossover happens at roughly half the transport Bc of Device A. It is closer to Device B's Bc of ≈ 55 mT (Supp. Fig. 4), a value the main text never states.
   - The transport Bc is also measured between contacts 4 and 5 (Supp. Fig. 5a). That path runs through the upper "Y"/1D-channel region, not the bottom of the 2D channel where Figs. 4–5 were taken (Fig. 2c orange box).
   - Requested:
     - Reconcile the field scales quantitatively.
     - Measure transport across the 2D channel actually probed (e.g. contacts 3–7 or 7–8), or explain why the 4–5 Bc applies there.
     - Correct the text to describe Fig. 4e accurately.

4. **The "~200 nm superconducting edge channel" rests on a single line cut, taken above the transport critical field, with no zero-field or field-dependent spatial maps.**
   - Where: Fig. 5 (B = −235 mT); abstract; Outlook.
   - The headline spatial result comes from one Y-line at X = 125 nm (Fig. 5h). It was recorded at −235 mT, which is above the ≈200 mT transport Bc that the paper otherwise uses as the threshold for losing superconductivity.
   - The paper argues the strip is a more robust, optimally doped superconductor. Two alternatives are not excluded:
     - Normal-state electronic friction depends non-monotonically on carrier density/sheet conductance (ref. 32), so a minimum in Γ at intermediate Δn across a density gradient is expected without superconductivity.
     - The strip coincides with the steepest CPD gradient (Fig. 5a,b), where lateral electrostatic forces and tip-height feedback change most rapidly.
   - Requested:
     - The same Y–V_B map at B = 0, at several fields between 0 and 300 mT, and above Tc. The strip's dissipation contrast should disappear above Tc and at a field higher than the interior's if the interpretation holds.
     - Maps along several X positions and at the other boundary of the channel (the opposite edge, ~1 µm away), to show the strip lies at both edges as an "edge channel" picture requires.
     - The width with an uncertainty, and the definition used for "~200 nm."

5. **The tip–sample distance and feedback conditions during the spectroscopy are not specified, and height differences can produce the A/B and strip contrast.**
   - Where: Figs. 4a, 5; SI pp. 27–28.
   - Topography contrast is explicitly electrostatic: up to 8 nm apparent height in Fig. 4a, produced by Δf feedback on CPD differences (p. 6). Points A and B, and the pixels across the Fig. 5 boundary, are therefore at different tip heights. Dissipation (and the strength of tip-induced gating) depends steeply on distance.
   - It is not stated whether the Γ(V) sweeps were taken in constant-height mode, with feedback opened at a fixed V_Bias setpoint, or with the setpoint bias at the local CPD.
   - Requested:
     - State the protocol.
     - Give the absolute tip height at A and B.
     - Show Γ(V) at matched tip–sample distance, or Γ(z) at fixed ΔV at both locations, demonstrating that the A/B contrast and the Fig. 5h minimum are not distance effects.

**B. Should address**

6. **The density calibration (Eq. 1) and its comparison with the superconducting dome are not justified at the level needed for "optimally doped" and "overdoped" assignments.**
   - Where: pp. 7–8, 12; Figs. 3, 5g–h.
   - The model and its inputs:
     - Eq. 1 is a parallel-plate relation through ~1.5 nm of LAO with εr = 25.
     - It ignores the tip-induced field, the very large and field-dependent permittivity of STO at mK, quantum capacitance (which the authors invoke elsewhere, ref. 34) and surface adsorbates/protonation. The SI says surface protonation creates the 2DEG in c-AFM devices.
     - It yields Δn relative to an arbitrarily chosen "non-patterned" pixel: CPD = 1.024 V in Fig. 3, but 0.07 V in Fig. 5.
   - Why this matters: the CPD in Fig. 2e varies smoothly across the whole 1.5 µm frame, including well outside the patterned boundaries, so the reference is not flat. Absolute densities are then compared with the dome range n ≈ (1.5–6.5) × 10¹³ cm⁻², which refers to absolute Hall densities.
   - The assignments disagree between figures:
     - Fig. 3 places the channel at Δn ≈ 8 × 10¹³ cm⁻² (text says ≈ 1 × 10¹⁴), i.e. overdoped.
     - Fig. 5h puts the whole patterned side at ≲ 5 × 10¹³ cm⁻², and the "optimal" strip at ~2–3.5 × 10¹³.
   - The text says Eq. 1 is an upper bound because it neglects surface dipoles. The sign of the dipole correction is not established, so "upper bound" is unjustified.
   - Requested:
     - An independent calibration, e.g. Hall density in the same device, CPD versus side-gate or back-gate voltage, or electrostatic simulation including the STO permittivity.
     - An uncertainty on Δn.
     - Softening of the dome assignments to what the calibration supports.

7. **The lateral resolution of the CPD and dissipation maps is not established, yet the paper draws ~200 nm spatial conclusions.**
   - Where: Figs. 2c, 2e, 3b, 5.
   - The CPD and apparent-height features are far broader than the written patterns. The 10–20 nm 1D wire appears as a several-hundred-nm-wide stripe in Fig. 2c. The density rise in Fig. 3b extends ~0.6 µm beyond the dashed boundary.
   - It is unclear how much of this is genuine lateral spreading of charge (e.g. in high-ε STO) and how much is electrostatic convolution from the tip apex, cone and cantilever at 3–5 nm amplitude and Δf setpoints of −0.8 to −3 Hz.
   - Requested:
     - Estimate the point-spread function, e.g. by modelling the tip–sample electrostatics or imaging a known sharp feature such as the 1D wire.
     - Discuss whether a ~200 nm dissipation feature is resolution-limited.
     - Show CPD and dissipation over the narrow 1D wire, which would directly test the one-dimensional claims.

8. **The electrical configuration of the device during AFM is not described.**
   - Where: Methods; pp. 5–9.
   - It is not stated which electrodes were grounded or biased while scanning. The written channels are galvanically connected to the electrodes, while unwritten regions are insulating and electrically floating.
   - Consequence: V_Bias then acts differently on the two regions, and the CPD "contrast" and the ΔV dependence of dissipation may partly reflect connectivity rather than work-function or pairing differences.
   - Requested: state the configuration. Ideally repeat a CPD/dissipation measurement with the channel electrodes floated versus grounded.

9. **Tip-induced gating is acknowledged but not separated from the claimed superconducting signature.**
   - Where: pp. 9–11, 13.
   - V_Bias swings of ~1.4 V across ~1.5 nm of LAO strongly gate the 2DES under the tip, as the authors themselves invoke to explain the concave curves and "dome-shaped" dissipation versus bias.
   - This local gating means a ΔV⁴ term can arise from density-dependent screening or quantum capacitance (ref. 34) in the normal state.
   - The interpretation of the concave curves also relies on an untested chain: bias → doping → lower local Bc.
   - Requested: compare with a normal-state (above-Tc) bias dependence (see concern 1). Quantify the expected tip-induced Δn per volt from the measured CPD/capacitance, and show it is either negligible or consistent with the proposed mechanism.

10. **The dissipation results come from a single device, effectively a single location, with no reproducibility evidence.**
    - All dissipation data are from Device A, from a region at the bottom of the 2D channel.
    - Device B is used only for CPD and transport. Its transport differs substantially:
      - Bc ≈ 55 mT versus ~200 mT;
      - the resistance minimum is ≈ 1.6 kΩ rather than zero (Supp. Fig. 4b), which should itself be explained.
    - Requested:
      - Dissipation spectroscopy on Device B, and at other boundaries of Device A (the other side of the 2D channel, the waveguide, the 1D wire).
      - Whether the results survive thermal cycling or a tip change.
      - Whether the field-dependent dissipation map is reproducible with a different tip.

11. **The proximity-effect discussion is not quantitatively consistent with the numbers given.**
    - Where: pp. 13–14.
    - With ξ ≈ 60–100 nm and a ~1 µm wide channel, proximity from the two edges reaches only ~10–20% of the interior. That contradicts the statement that it "would influence a substantial portion of the channel interior" and explains superconducting signatures "throughout the patterned region at zero field."
    - Overdoped LAO/STO on the dome is still intrinsically superconducting, so the premise that the interior "lacks its own robust superconducting gap" needs evidence.
    - The claim that the interior loses superconductivity first with field was not measured spatially: the field sweep is at one point, and the spatial map at one field.
    - Requested: remove the claim that this picture is "quantitatively supported," or support it with spatially resolved field series.

12. **Heating and effective electron temperature are unknown.**
    - The superconducting scales are Tc ≲ 300 mK and Bc ~ 100–200 mT, yet no estimate is given of tip-induced heating or electron temperature under the oscillating, biased tip and the qPlus excitation.
    - The paper should give the power dissipated (Γ × ω²A² is directly available from the data). It should also report one check of the dissipation spectra or transport (Ic, Bc) with the tip engaged versus retracted.

13. **The claim that the field dependence is symmetric and the low-field features are intrinsic needs support.**
    - Where: Fig. 4c,e.
    - The low-dissipation band near zero field is offset toward negative field (≈ −10 to −30 mT in Fig. 4c), and a "glitch" is attributed to AFM feedback.
    - With a superconducting magnet, a remanent field offset or trapped flux is likely. The sweep direction and any hysteresis should be reported.
    - Requested: give the field sweep direction, check for hysteresis by sweeping both ways, and state whether the field offset is calibrated. The width of the zero-field minimum (~±20 mT) is itself a characteristic field that deserves discussion.

**C. Minor**

14. **The critical current quoted for Device A does not match Fig. 1.** The text says both devices have Ic = 30 nA. Fig. 1e–f for Device A show resistance peaks at ≈ ±12 nA at 47 mT, and the zero-field diamond tip near ~20 nA. Give the zero-field Ic per device.
15. **Device B's transport labels are inconsistent.** Supp. Fig. 4a–b are labelled R₄₇ versus I₄₅, but Supp. Fig. 5b describes current applied 1→5 with voltage on 4/7. Make the labels and schematic consistent. Also clarify whether Fig. 1e is a two-terminal measurement, since Supp. Fig. 5a shows the voltage measured across the same contacts that carry the current.
16. **The explanation of the Fig. 1e asymmetry is unsupported.** The caption asserts that the asymmetric current dependence "does not affect the superconducting properties probed here," without support. Either support it or soften it.
17. **Figure references are wrong in several places.**
    - p. 13: "green curve in Fig. 4b." Fig. 4b has only curves A (teal) and B (purple).
    - "Fig. 4d … (blue and purple curves)": check that the colours match the arrowheads.
    - Fig. 5 caption: "statistical uncertainty for (f) and (g)." Panel g is a schematic; this should be (h).
    - Fig. 4 caption refers to an orange box in Fig. 2c, which is hard to see.
18. **Sign conventions and CPD ranges need clarifying.** Fig. 2e spans CPD 0.1–1.5 V, while Fig. 5a spans −0.5 to 0.15 V on the same device. Explain the shift (a different tip? a work-function change?). State the sign convention for CPD (SI: CPD = φ_tip − φ_sample) consistently with Eq. 1.
19. **The SI mislabels Supp. Fig. 2a.** It is described as a "Schematic diagram of ULV-EBL patterning," but it shows c-AFM.
20. **Some wording overstates the results.**
    - "directly visualize where superconductivity resides" (p. 4) and "provides a direct local diagnostic" (p. 12) overstate what is an indirect, model-dependent signature.
    - "μc LAO/STO" (Supp. Fig. 4 caption) should read "uc."
21. **The previous-work survey is not accurate.** The statement that no previous scanning probe was performed below Tc should be checked against the literature (e.g. scanning SQUID susceptometry of LAO/STO superconductivity) and reworded. Ref. 10 as cited concerns ferromagnetism; cite the superconducting scanning-SQUID work where relevant.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Make temperature the main control. Repeat Fig. 4b and Fig. 5h at T > Tc and at B = 0, and show the full Γ(V, T) evolution at one point. This single dataset would decide whether the dissipation contrast is superconducting.
2. Turn the single line cut into a field- and position-resolved dataset: Y–V_B maps at several fields (0 to 300 mT), at several X positions, and on both edges of the 2D channel and across the 1D wire. That would test the claimed hierarchy of edge versus interior critical fields directly.
3. Calibrate density independently (gate-dependent CPD in the same device, or Hall), add electrostatic modelling of the tip–2DES coupling including STO's permittivity, and give error bars on Δn and on the strip width.
4. Present β/α (or the residual quadratic weight) as a spatial map with a defined fit window. Correlate it with CPD across both devices, so the "ΔV⁴ marker" is shown to be systematic rather than drawn from two hand-picked points.
