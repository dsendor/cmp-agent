Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports ultra-low-temperature (10 mK) non-contact AFM, KPFM and dissipation spectroscopy on LAO/STO interfaces patterned by ULV-EBL and c-AFM. The authors claim that the bias dependence of tip–sample dissipation (a ΔV⁴ term on top of a suppressed ΔV² term) is a local marker of superconductivity, and that superconductivity sits preferentially in ~200 nm edge strips at "optimal" doping, with the interior proximitized. A local probe of superconductivity in patterned LAO/STO at dilution-refrigerator temperatures would matter, because it bears directly on the one-dimensional-superconductivity question raised by transport (width-independent Ic, pairing without superconductivity). The technical achievement of combining mK nc-AFM/KPFM with in situ transport on written oxide nanostructures is real. However, the link between the dissipation signal and superconductivity is so far a set of correlations measured at essentially one location in one device, without the controls that would separate it from conductivity, electrostatic and instrumental effects.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The experiment is hard and new: nc-AFM/KPFM at 10 mK with simultaneous magnetotransport on patterned LAO/STO devices, and the patterned structures are visible in CPD and topography.
- Two patterning methods (ULV-EBL and c-AFM) are compared. Etched STO reference regions show no CPD contrast (Supp. Fig. 3), which is a useful control for the KPFM contrast.
- The dissipation maps show a clear, non-trivial field and bias dependence (Fig. 4c–e) and a spatially narrow low-dissipation strip at a patterned boundary (Fig. 5). These are interesting observations worth reporting whatever their final interpretation.
- In the Outlook the authors are appropriately cautious ("suggestive of the one-dimensional hypothesis", "more work is needed").

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The ΔV⁴ dissipation term is not shown to be specific to superconductivity: there is no normal-state reference at the same location.** The central diagnostic (p. 9; Fig. 4b) compares point A (inside the patterned, conducting region) with point B (outside, on insulating LAO/STO). That comparison mixes "conducting vs insulating" with "superconducting vs normal". Several facts in the paper weaken the assignment:
   - Point B also needs a quartic term: the Fig. 4b caption gives β/α = 1.84 V⁻² for B versus 3.85 V⁻² for A. The ΔV⁴ component is therefore present outside the superconducting region and differs only by a factor ~2.
   - The two curves are fitted over different bias windows relative to their CPDs (A: about −0.7 V to +0.7 V around a CPD of ≈−0.5 V; B: about −0.4 V to +0.4 V around ≈0.1 V). The fitted β/α of a truncated polynomial depends strongly on the window.
   - Above the transport critical field, the patterned region does not recover the convex "ohmic" ΔV² parabola the model predicts for the normal state. Instead the curves become concave (Fig. 4d, 162 and 222 mT). So the paper's own normal-state model does not describe the normal state at point A.
   - The non-contact friction literature the authors cite (Volokitin/Persson, ref. 32) predicts that Joule dissipation depends non-monotonically on sheet conductivity. Lower dissipation in a well-conducting 2DES than over a poorly screened insulating region is therefore expected without any superconductivity.

   What is needed: bias spectra at point A (i) at 10 mK and B = 0, (ii) at 10 mK and B well above Hc (e.g. 0.5–1 T), and (iii) at B = 0 and T above Tc (e.g. 400–500 mT-equivalent temperature, i.e. T ≈ 0.4–1 K), all with the same tip, height and fit window. Ideally add a temperature series through Tc. The claim needs the ΔV⁴/ΔV² ratio (or the absolute α) to change at Tc and Hc, and not otherwise. Without this, the abstract's "local diagnostic of superconductivity" is not established.

2. **The field dependence does not match the transport critical field as claimed, and it was measured on a different part of the device.**
   - p. 10–11 states that dissipation increases with field, "reaching a maximum as it approaches the critical magnetic field" of ~200 mT. Fig. 4e instead shows maxima at about ±70–100 mT, with dissipation already falling by 200 mT. The text itself later says "enhanced dissipation around ≈±100 mT".
   - The curvature reversal in Fig. 4d is already visible at 42 mT (orange, double-humped).
   - The transport critical field (Fig. 1e) was measured between contacts 4 and 5. According to Fig. 1d and Supp. Fig. 5a, that current path runs through the upper V/1D/fan structures, not the bottom of the 2D channel where Fig. 4 was recorded. The local and global critical fields therefore come from different channels.

   Alternative explanations for a non-monotonic Γ(B) also need to be excluded:
   - Vortex entry and tip-driven vortex motion in a 1 µm wide film in perpendicular field.
   - Normal-state magnetoresistance of the 2DES changing its Joule dissipation.
   - Most specifically: the probe tips are Ir (Methods, p. 26–27), and Ir is itself a superconductor with Tc ≈ 0.1 K and a critical field of only a few mT. The sharp dissip­ation minimum within roughly ±20 mT in Fig. 4e (and the "glitch" mentioned in the caption) could come from the tip rather than the sample.

   Please show the same B-sweep at point B and over the etched bare-STO reference region (a null control with matched tip and height). Discuss tip superconductivity explicitly. Measure transport across the 2D channel itself (e.g. contacts 7–3 or 7–4), so that the local and global critical fields refer to the same structure.

3. **The "≈200 nm superconducting edge channel" rests on one line cut, at one edge, at a field above the transport Hc, under constant-Δf feedback.**
   - Fig. 5 was recorded at B = −235 mT. That is above the ~200 mT critical field of Fig. 1e, where the paper's own logic says the sample is not globally superconducting. The Discussion's resolution, that the edge "likely has a higher critical field" (p. 13–14), is an assumption and is not measured.
   - Only one boundary of the 1 µm 2D channel is mapped, so "edge channels" on both sides, and their independence of channel width (one of the paper's motivating claims), are not tested.
   - Topography is taken at constant Δf. The paper notes that CPD contrast changes the tip height (p. 6; Methods p. 27), and the CPD changes by 0.65 V across the Fig. 5 area. The tip–sample distance, and with it the capacitive coupling and dissipation, therefore change across exactly the boundary where the dissipation minimum appears. The minimum is present at all three biases (−900, −300, +300 mV; Fig. 5c–e), which fits an electrostatic/geometric origin at least as well as a superconducting one.

   What is needed:
   - Constant-height dissipation maps, or at least the tip-height trace along Fig. 5h.
   - The same map at zero field and at high field (e.g. 1 T), where the strip should vanish if it is superconducting.
   - Both edges of the 2D channel, and ideally channels of different widths and the second device.
   - A statement of the lateral resolution of the dissipation and CPD signals: tip cone and cantilever contributions, and the 3.2–5 nm amplitude.

4. **Converting CPD to carrier density, and placing regions on the superconducting dome, is not justified. The paper's own numbers contradict the "overdoped interior" conclusion.**
   - Eq. (1) treats the CPD change as due only to interfacial charge across the LAO capacitor. Yet the Supplement states that "the presence of surface charge following patterning … is essential for generating measurable contrast" and invokes surface protonation (p. 30). Both ULV-EBL and c-AFM writing are thought to work by modifying surface adsorbates and charge. A surface-charge/dipole contribution then enters at leading order, not as a correction that makes Eq. (1) "an upper bound".
   - The sign conventions are inconsistent. Eq. (1) defines Δφ = φ_non-patterned − φ_patterned > 0 for added carriers, i.e. a lower work function in the patterned region. Supp. p. 29–30 states that a lower CPD in the patterned region means a *higher* work function and more carriers.
   - The reference CPD differs between figures (1.024 V in Fig. 3; 0.07 V in Fig. 5h), and absolute CPDs shift by ~1 V between the Fig. 2e and Fig. 5a areas, which suggests tip changes.
   - Δn is a *change* relative to an unpatterned region whose own density is unknown, so it cannot be placed on an absolute-n dome.
   - Quantitatively:
     - Fig. 5h gives Δn ≈ 4.5–5 × 10¹³ cm⁻² in the patterned interior. That lies inside the "optimal range" n ≈ 1.5–6.5 × 10¹³ cm⁻² quoted on p. 7, not in the overdoped regime.
     - The low-dissipation strip sits at Δn ≈ 2.5–3.5 × 10¹³ cm⁻², also inside that range.
     - Fig. 3b peaks at ~8 × 10¹³ cm⁻², while the text says ~1 × 10¹⁴ cm⁻².
     - Device B's 0.3 V CPD contrast at 6 uc implies Δn ≈ 2 × 10¹³ cm⁻², roughly five times lower than Device A, yet both show Ic ≈ 30 nA.

   Since the under/optimal/overdoped labelling (Fig. 5g,h) carries the whole edge-channel and proximity narrative, please:
   - Calibrate CPD against density independently, for example with a side-gate sweep of CPD alongside transport, or Hall density from a written Hall bar on the same chip.
   - Resolve the sign convention.
   - Use one fixed reference.
   - Present the density assignment as qualitative unless calibrated.

**B. Should address**

5. **FM-AFM dissipation artifacts are not ruled out.** The excitation signal in FM-AFM is known to contain "apparent dissipation" from frequency-dependent transfer functions of the piezo/excitation path and from PLL or amplitude-controller crosstalk whenever Δf changes. Here Δf varies parabolically with bias by construction (Supp. Fig. 1b), so a bias-dependent apparent dissipation would naturally produce even-order (ΔV², ΔV⁴) terms. Please:
   - Report k, f₀, Q, V_exc,0 and the controller bandwidths.
   - Show that Γ(V) does not simply track Δf(V). For example, compare spectra taken at different heights with the same Δf range, or verify that the phase is at resonance throughout.
   - Show the dissipation recorded at the CPD as a function of height.

   In Fig. 4b the A and B spectra were also presumably taken at different tip heights because of constant-Δf feedback. State the heights, or repeat at matched height.

6. **Reproducibility: effectively one device and one location for the dissipation claims.**
   - All dissipation data come from Device A, one point (Fig. 4b,c), and one rectangular area (Fig. 5).
   - Device B is used only for CPD and transport, so the claimed diagnostic is not tested on the second patterning method.
   - There is no statement on repeat spectra, tip-to-tip variation (the CPD offsets suggest tip changes), or thermal cycling.

   Please add dissipation spectra and field sweeps at several points inside and outside the pattern, on both devices, with spread shown. The quoted β/α uncertainties (±0.03, ±0.01 V⁻²) are fit-only and say nothing about reproducibility.

7. **The effective sample temperature during spectroscopy is not established.** Tc in LAO/STO is ≲ 300 mK and the transport critical currents are ~30 nA, so the 2DES is fragile. The tip is oscillated at 18 kHz with 3–5 nm amplitude, and biases up to −1.2 V are applied a few nm from a 1.6 nm LAO barrier. Leakage or displacement currents and the dissipated power (Γ ~ 10⁻⁷ kg/s × v²) could locally heat the electron system. Please:
   - Estimate the power dissipated into the sample per cycle.
   - Check whether transport through the channel (Ic, R) changes with the tip engaged and biased versus retracted. This is directly possible with the in situ transport setup.

8. **The proximity-effect explanation is presented as "quantitatively supported" (p. 14) but is not quantitative.**
   - With ξ ≈ 60–100 nm, proximity from both edges covers at most ~20% of a 1 µm channel. That does not "naturally explain" superconducting signatures throughout the interior at zero field.
   - No Γ profile across the full channel width at zero field is shown to test this.
   - At −235 mT the entire interior is above the transport Hc anyway, so the "interior fails first" hierarchy is not independently demonstrated.

   Either give a spatial dissipation profile versus field across the whole width and compare it with a proximity length, or recast this section as a hypothesis.

9. **Transport characterization needs clarification.**
   - Supp. Fig. 5a shows the voltmeter across the same contacts (4, 5) as the current source, i.e. a two-terminal measurement through the 1 MΩ pseudo-current source. Yet Fig. 1f reaches ~0 Ω, which would require vanishing contact resistance. Please confirm the configuration and whether a line resistance was subtracted.
   - Device B's R₄₇ minimum is ~1.6 kΩ (Supp. Fig. 4b), not zero, so "the transport measurements confirm the superconducting nature … in both devices" (p. 5) is overstated for B.
   - The text gives Ic = 30 nA for both devices, while the switching peaks in Fig. 1f (47 mT) are at ±12 nA. Give the zero-field Ic for Device A explicitly.
   - p. 5 cites Supp. Fig. 4c (the waveguide transconductance map) in the Device A paragraph, but it belongs to Device B.
   - Device A's Hc (~200 mT) and Device B's (~55 mT) differ by a factor ~4. Discuss this, given that the paper links local dissipation to the global Hc.

10. **The fit procedure and the physical meaning of the ΔV⁴ term are under-specified.**
    - Give the fitted α and β with units and the bias window used, and show how β/α varies with the window.
    - Test whether a ΔV⁶ term or an asymmetric model is needed, since Fig. 4b curve A is visibly asymmetric about its minimum.
    - The interpretation "suppression of low-energy excitations due to the quasiparticle gap opening" (p. 9) involves a gap of order 40–60 µeV. Biases are ~1 V and the tip oscillates at 18 kHz (≈ 75 neV). Explain how the gap enters the bias dependence of Γ at these scales, or soften the statement.
    - The Nb analogy (refs. 31, 32) concerns a bulk metal with a well-defined normal-state ohmic V² law. As noted in concern 1, that law is not observed above Hc here.

**C. Minor**

11. Figure/curve references are inconsistent:
    - p. 12 and p. 13 refer to "the green curve in Fig. 4b", but Fig. 4b has only teal (A) and purple (B) curves.
    - p. 13 describes "Fig. 4d above the critical field (blue and purple curves)", but blue is 162 mT, below the stated ~200 mT Hc.
    - p. 10 calls the 222 mT curve "green", whereas green/teal is 102 mT.
    - The Fig. 5 caption gives statistical uncertainties for "(f) and (g)", but (g) is a schematic; this presumably means (h).

12. Supp. p. 30 states that "without patterning, the LAO layer is below the critical thickness required to induce a 2DEG". This is standard for 3 uc but not for 6 uc (Device B), which normally exceeds the ~4 uc critical thickness. Please explain why unpatterned 6 uc (and 4 uc) regions are insulating here, e.g. surface treatment as in ref. 4.

13. The Fig. 1 caption explains an "asymmetric dependence on current" by contact transparency or built-in asymmetry and asserts that neither "affects the superconducting properties probed here". This is unsupported. Either show it, or drop the claim.

14. The Introduction (p. 3) states that no previous scanning probe was operated below Tc of LAO/STO. Scanning SQUID susceptometry has imaged the diamagnetic (superfluid) response of LAO/STO below Tc (e.g. Bert et al.). Ref. 10 as cited (Kalisky et al., ferromagnetism critical thickness) does not address superconductivity. Please revise the claim and the citation.

15. Fig. 2e and Fig. 3a show a smooth CPD/density gradient over ~1 µm, with the density maximum lying largely outside the dashed 10–20 nm 1D line boundaries. Comment on how the written-line width compares with the measured ~600 nm density profile, and on whether long-range electrostatic averaging by the tip cone and cantilever sets this width.

16. The Fig. 4e caption notes a feedback glitch at small negative field. Since the zero-field minimum is a key feature, show a repeat sweep without the glitch.

17. Data availability: given the analysis choices (CPD reference, fit windows), the raw Δf(V) and excitation(V) data cubes should be deposited.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Build the core of the paper around a temperature- and field-controlled comparison at fixed tip position and height: Γ(V) at 10 mK/0 T, 10 mK/≫Hc, and T > Tc/0 T, inside the channel and over the insulating and etched-STO reference regions. This would turn the ΔV⁴ observation from a correlation into a diagnostic.
2. Use the in situ transport capability to measure the same structure that is imaged (the 2D channel), and to check for tip-induced heating or perturbation of Ic during spectroscopy.
3. Calibrate the CPD-to-density conversion with a side-gate sweep (CPD and transport versus V_SG). Express doping assignments relative to the measured transport dome of the same device rather than literature ranges.
4. Map both edges of channels of different widths, at zero and high field, in both devices, with constant-height dissipation. This is the direct test of the width-independent, edge-localized superconductivity the paper is motivated by. Until then, align the abstract's wording ("features confined to edge channels of order ≈200 nm") with the Outlook's more cautious framing.
