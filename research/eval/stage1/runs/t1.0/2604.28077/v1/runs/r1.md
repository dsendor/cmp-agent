Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript uses millikelvin (10 mK) frequency-modulation non-contact AFM, Kelvin probe force microscopy and tip-sample dissipation spectroscopy on LAO/STO interfaces patterned by ULV-EBL and c-AFM. It argues that (i) CPD maps give the local carrier density of the written channels, (ii) a ΔV^4 term in the bias-dependent dissipation is a local marker of superconductivity, (iii) the field dependence of the dissipation follows the transport critical field, and (iv) a dissipation minimum about 200 nm wide at the edge of a 2D channel, at "intermediate" density, points to edge-localized superconductivity, which would support the one-dimensional picture of LAO/STO nanostructures. A local probe that works at the temperatures where interface superconductivity exists, on patterned devices, would be valuable and the instrumentation is impressive. As the paper stands, however, the link between the dissipation signal and superconductivity rests on correlations that lack the controls needed to rule out electrostatic, dielectric and feedback-related explanations.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- It is technically demanding and, to my knowledge, the first nc-AFM, KPFM and dissipation study of written LAO/STO nanostructures at 10 mK, done in the same cooldown as in-situ transport on the same device (Fig. 1e,f; Supplementary Fig. 4).
- The KPFM contrast between written and unwritten regions, together with its absence on bare STO (Supplementary Fig. 3c, Section 2), is a clean and useful demonstration that the written 2DES can be seen through the LAO.
- The 2D maps of dissipation against field and bias (Fig. 4c) and the 3D grid of dissipation against position and bias (Fig. 5) are rich data sets that could, with proper controls, establish dissipation spectroscopy as a local probe of oxide-interface superconductivity.
- The question it addresses (where superconductivity resides in patterned LAO/STO, and whether this explains width-independent critical currents) is important and of broad interest.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. There is no measurement across Tc, and no null control for the field dependence. The case that the dissipation tracks superconductivity rests entirely on the magnetic-field dependence at one location near point A (Fig. 4c–e). No dissipation data are shown as a function of temperature, nor at the same location above Tc. No field map is shown at the unpatterned point B, on the bare-STO reference area that exists on Device B (Supplementary Fig. 3), or with the tip retracted. Several non-superconducting mechanisms could produce field-dependent dissipation of this size:
   - spin- and charge-related transitions in oxygen-deficient STO surfaces, which the authors' own ref. 17 detected by nc-AFM dissipation;
   - field dependence of the qPlus sensor's intrinsic damping Γ0, or of the excitation electronics;
   - magnetoresistance of the normal 2DES altering the ohmic loss.

   The authors should do four things:
   - measure Γ(V_bias) at points A and B from base temperature to well above Tc (e.g. 50, 150, 250, 400, 600 mK) at zero field;
   - repeat the field map of Fig. 4c at point B and on bare STO under identical setpoints;
   - record Γ0(B) with the tip retracted;
   - show that the A/B contrast and the ΔV^4 term vanish at T > Tc while persisting in the controls.

   Without this, the title's claim of "local probing of superconductivity" is not established.

2. The ΔV^4 "marker" is not shown to be specific to superconductivity, and may be a feedback or geometry artifact.
   - The fit in Fig. 4b gives a nonzero quartic term at the unpatterned point B as well (β/α = 1.84 V^-2, against 3.85 V^-2 at A). The text nonetheless describes B as quadratic and A as having an emergent quartic component. A factor of two in β/α is not a qualitative distinction.
   - The two curves cover different bias windows around their CPDs: |ΔV| up to about 0.7 V for A, but only about 0.4–0.45 V for B. For B the quartic term equals the quadratic one only at |ΔV| ≈ 0.74 V, outside the measured range, so β for B is poorly constrained and the ratios are not comparable.
   - The written fit form αΔV^2 + βΔV^4 has no constant, yet both curves have a baseline of 18–23 × 10^-8 kg/s. The full fit function and the free parameters need to be stated.
   - Most importantly, the spectra appear to be taken under constant-Δf feedback (the Fig. 4e caption mentions an "AFM feedback" glitch). If so, the tip height changes with bias as the electrostatic force varies roughly as ΔV^2, and the distance dependence of Γ will generate higher-order bias terms with no superconductivity involved. This effect would also differ between regions of different CPD and dC/dz.

   The authors should do the following:
   - acquire constant-height Γ(V) spectra, or record and show z(V) for every spectrum, and correct for the distance dependence using measured Γ(z) curves;
   - fit both regions over the same |ΔV| window, with a constant term, and report α, β and the offset separately;
   - give the distribution of β/α over many pixels inside and outside the pattern, at zero field and above B_c and T_c.

   The ΔV^4 term can only be called a marker of superconductivity if it is suppressed in the normal state at the same location.

3. The key zero-field and near-critical-field cuts are compromised and do not match the stated critical field.
   - The red "−18 mT" curve in Fig. 4d is the main evidence for convex, superconducting behaviour in the field map. It lies at the small negative field where the caption of Fig. 4e reports an AFM feedback glitch, and where the map in Fig. 4c shows a narrow anomalous dark band next to a high-dissipation sliver at about 0 mT.
   - The dissipation minimum in Fig. 4e spans only about ±20 mT, and its lowest points come from this glitch region.
   - The dissipation maxima sit at about ±80–100 mT, not at the 200 mT critical field. Yet the text says dissipation reaches a maximum "as it approaches the critical magnetic field" and that the crossover "occurs as the field approaches 200 mT".
   - The "green curve in Fig. 4d", described as near 200 mT, is the 102 mT cut according to the arrowheads.
   - The map is asymmetric, with the negative-field band extending to about −130 mT and the positive one to about +100 mT. This suggests a field offset from remanence in the superconducting magnet, or sweep-direction hysteresis.
   - The zero-field point spectrum at A (Fig. 4b) has a minimum of about 18 × 10^-8 kg/s and strong convex curvature. The −18 mT cut is about 9 × 10^-8 kg/s and nearly flat. The text calls the two "consistent", but different setpoints (Δf = −1.0 Hz vs the unstated setpoint in 4b) make them incomparable.

   The authors should:
   - re-measure the low-field region without the glitch, sweeping in both directions;
   - calibrate and correct the magnet's remanent field (for example from the transport diamond in Fig. 1e taken in the same sweep);
   - state which field scale (about 30 mT, about 100 mT or 200 mT) they identify with which physical process, and revise the text accordingly.

4. The headline edge-channel result rests on one line cut, at one field above B_c, on one edge, and the evidence partly contradicts its interpretation.
   - The roughly 200 nm dissipation minimum (Fig. 5h) is shown only at B = −235 mT, which is above the transport critical field. It is shown at only one boundary of the 2D channel, and only at X = 125 nm of a 250 nm-wide grid.
   - No zero-field or low-field map of the same area is shown. The opposite edge and the channel interior are not mapped, so neither edge localization nor the edge/interior contrast is demonstrated. The 1D channel and the waveguide, which motivate the paper, are not studied by dissipation at all.
   - In Fig. 5b–e the dissipation-minimum strip at Y ≈ 400–600 nm is essentially independent of bias from −900 mV to +300 mV. Elsewhere the authors argue that tip bias locally dopes the interface and shifts the system along the dome (p. 11, p. 13). If the strip marked the optimally doped part of a density gradient, tip-gating should move it in Y with bias. Its bias independence argues against the doping interpretation and is more consistent with a geometric or electrostatic feature tied to the boundary.
   - The strip coincides with the region of steepest CPD gradient (Fig. 5a,h). There, lateral electrostatic forces, the changing dC/dz and the constant-Δf height adjustments all vary rapidly. Any of these could produce a local extremum in Γ at a conductor–insulator boundary whether or not the system is superconducting.

   The authors should:
   - map Γ(x, y, V) across both edges and the interior of the 2D channel at zero field, at several fields up to and above B_c, and above T_c;
   - show that the edge strip exists only in the superconducting state;
   - show that a comparable conductor–insulator boundary in the normal state (above T_c) gives no such dip;
   - show the z(x, y) images recorded simultaneously with Fig. 5.

B. Should address

5. The CPD-to-density conversion is not reliable enough to place regions on the superconducting dome, and the numbers in the paper do not agree with each other.
   - Eq. (1) treats the LAO as an ideal parallel-plate capacitor with ε_r = 25. It ignores the surface charge and dipole that c-AFM and ULV-EBL writing are believed to create, which is the writing mechanism itself (protonation and adsorbates, see the Supplementary discussion and ref. 4). It also ignores the extent of the 2DES into STO and quantum capacitance. The authors themselves call it an upper bound.
   - Despite this, Fig. 5g,h labels regions "underdoped", "optimal" and "overdoped". The whole Δn range in Fig. 5h (about 0.5–5 × 10^13 cm^-2) lies inside the "optimal range" of 1.5–6.5 × 10^13 cm^-2 quoted on p. 7, so the "overdoped" blue region is by the paper's own numbers within the optimal window.
   - The density scales disagree within Device A. Fig. 3 (peak about 8 × 10^13 cm^-2, reference CPD 1.024 V, CPD range 0.1–1.5 V) differs from Fig. 5 (plateau about 5 × 10^13 cm^-2, reference CPD 0.07 V, CPD range −0.5 to +0.15 V). The roughly 1 V shift in absolute CPD between two areas of the same device suggests the tip work function changed, which also undermines the assumption of constant φ_tip.
   - Device B (6 uc) shows a CPD contrast of only about 0.3 V (Supplementary Fig. 3c). Through Eq. (1) with the thicker LAO this implies Δn ≈ 2 × 10^13 cm^-2, five times lower than Device A. This is not discussed.
   - In Fig. 3b the density outside the "estimated pattern boundary" is already about 6–8 × 10^13 cm^-2. Either the profile is strongly broadened by the long-range electrostatic averaging of KPFM (tip apex and shank), or the boundaries are misregistered.

   The authors should do three things:
   - explain how the pattern boundaries were registered to the AFM images;
   - estimate the KPFM lateral resolution at the 3–5 nm amplitude used, for example by modelling the CPD step that a sharp boundary would produce;
   - calibrate Eq. (1) against an independent density, such as Hall data on a co-written sheet or the side-gate dependence of the CPD. Without that calibration, the dome assignment should be called qualitative.

6. The transport benchmark is taken on a different part of the device from the dissipation data, and with weak superconducting signatures.
   - The 200 mT critical field of Device A comes from contacts 4–5 (Fig. 1e; Supplementary Fig. 5a). That path runs through the V-shaped upper channels and the Y-junction, not the 2D channel where Figs. 4 and 5 were taken.
   - The measurement is two-terminal: the voltmeter sits across the same contacts that carry the current. The main text, however, describes a "four-terminal transconductance map".
   - No R(T) or Tc is shown for either device.
   - Device B has a critical field of about 55 mT, and its resistance drops only from about 2 kΩ to about 1.6 kΩ (Supplementary Fig. 4a,b), so its superconductivity is marginal.
   - The comparison of the local "critical field" with transport is therefore between different channels.

   The authors should:
   - provide four-terminal R(T) and R(B) through the 2D channel imaged (for example using contacts 3 and 7);
   - give Tc for both devices;
   - state the Device B critical field in the main text rather than implying equivalence with Device A.

7. The dissipation results are not shown to be reproducible.
   - All dissipation data come from one device (A), at two point locations and in one boundary grid, apparently with one tip.
   - Device B is used only for KPFM.
   - Different figures use different setpoints (Δf = −3.0, −1.0 and −0.8 Hz; amplitudes of 3.2–5.0 nm), so absolute Γ values cannot be compared across figures, and the tip-sample distance is never stated.

   The authors should:
   - show the key observations (A/B contrast, ΔV^4 term, field dependence, edge strip) on at least a second location and preferably on Device B;
   - state the tip-sample distance, or show Γ(z), for each data set.

8. The mechanism by which superconductivity suppresses the measured dissipation is not established for this geometry.
   - The ohmic and phonon arguments come from Nb films (refs. 31 and 32), where the tip couples directly to a metallic surface. Here the 2DES is buried under about 1.5 nm of LAO and sits on a quantum paraelectric with a huge, lossy dielectric response at 10 mK.
   - The unpatterned point B is insulating, yet it is described as showing "capacitive energy losses typically observed on metallic surfaces due to ohmic dissipation". The authors should explain what carries ohmic current there.
   - A simpler explanation of lower Γ at A is that the 2DES screens the tip field from the lossy STO below, which would hold in the normal state as well.

   The authors should provide an order-of-magnitude estimate, for example using the Volokitin–Persson framework (ref. 32), of:
   - the expected Γ for a 2DES with the measured sheet resistance (about 20 kΩ normal state from Fig. 1f), compared with STO dielectric loss;
   - the expected change in Γ on entering the superconducting state.

9. The local perturbation caused by the tip is not quantified.
   - A bias of up to about 1.2 V applied across a nanometre-scale gap plus 1.5 nm of LAO can induce a local density change comparable to the Δn attributed to patterning itself. Eq. (1) gives about 1 V per 10^14 cm^-2.
   - The authors invoke this tip-gating to explain the dome-shaped Γ(V) at finite field, but never estimate it.

   The authors should estimate the tip-induced Δn and its lateral extent as a function of bias. They should then discuss whether the measurement probes the intrinsic state or a tip-gated one, and reconcile this with the bias independence of the edge strip (concern 4).

10. The proximity-effect and 1D interpretations are presented more strongly than the data allow.
   - The Discussion calls the edge-plus-proximity picture "quantitatively supported", but no calculation is given.
   - With ξ ≈ 60–100 nm, proximity from the edges would affect only about 20% of a 1 µm channel. That does not "naturally explain" superconducting signatures throughout the patterned region.
   - A 200 nm strip is wider than ξ, so it is not one-dimensional in the superconducting sense. Its relation to the width-independent critical current of ref. 7 is therefore not demonstrated.

   The authors should either model the proposed density profile and proximity effect (for example the spatial pair amplitude against field) or present this section explicitly as a hypothesis. They should also align the abstract ("features confined to edge channels") with the Outlook's more cautious wording ("suggestive").

11. The novelty statement about previous local probes is inaccurate. The Introduction states that no earlier scanning probe "was performed at the ultra-low temperatures necessary to probe the superconducting state". Scanning SQUID susceptometry has imaged the superfluid response of LAO/STO below Tc, for example Bert et al., Nature Phys. 7, 767 (2011) and Phys. Rev. B 86, 060503 (2012), including its spatial inhomogeneity and gate dependence. The authors should cite this work and restate what is new: nanometre-scale resolution on written nanostructures and combined CPD and dissipation imaging.

12. The effective electron temperature during scanning is unknown. Transport and SPM are both reported at 10 mK, but the transport was presumably taken with the tip retracted. Tip oscillation, applied bias and the excitation drive could raise the local or device electron temperature, which would matter for the claim that the edge strip survives beyond B_c. The authors should repeat the transport measurement with the tip engaged and oscillating at the scanning setpoints, for example by comparing the critical current or the R(B) diamond with the tip retracted and engaged.

C. Minor

13. There are several mismatches between the text and the figures:
   - "resembling the green curve in Fig. 4b" (p. 12) and "as shown in Fig. 4b green curve" (p. 13): Fig. 4b has teal and purple curves, not green.
   - The "green curve in Fig. 4d", described as near 200 mT, is the 102 mT cut.
   - The Fig. 5 caption says "statistical uncertainty for (f) and (g)", but (g) is a schematic; it should be (h).
   - "Significantly less energy dissipation" inside the patterned region is said of minima of about 18 vs about 23 × 10^-8 kg/s, which is a modest difference taken at different setpoints and biases.

14. In the Supplementary Information, the 6 uc LAO of Device B is described as "below the critical thickness required to induce a 2DEG". Six unit cells is above the usual 4 uc threshold, so this needs explanation or correction. The Fig. 1e caption also asserts that the current asymmetry "does not affect the superconducting properties probed here" without support.

15. The fit uncertainties in Fig. 4b (β/α = 3.85 ± 0.03 and 1.84 ± 0.01 V^-2) are purely statistical. They ignore the correlation between α and β and the systematic effect of the fit window. The authors should report the full fit function, the free and fixed parameters, and the sensitivity to the bias range.

16. Fig. 2c is a stitched mosaic acquired with amplitudes varying from 3.2 to 5.0 nm. Because the topographic contrast is electrostatic (p. 6), the authors should state the setpoint for each tile, or note that the contrast is not quantitatively comparable across tiles.

17. The sign convention between Eq. (1) (Δφ_CPD = φ_non-patterned − φ_patterned) and the Supplementary definition (CPD = φ_tip − φ_sample, where a decrease in CPD means an increase in work function) should be made explicit, together with the expected sign of the change for added interface electrons.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a temperature series (Γ(V) and Γ maps at about 4–6 temperatures through Tc) at matched setpoints on patterned, unpatterned and bare-STO regions. This one data set would turn the correlational argument into a direct test.
2. Take dissipation spectroscopy at constant height, or with simultaneous z(V) and Γ(z), so that the electrostatic feedback contribution can be separated from intrinsic loss before the ΔV^2 and ΔV^4 terms are interpreted.
3. Map both edges and the interior of the 2D channel, and ideally the 1D wire and the waveguide, at zero field and in a field series, so that edge localization and its field hierarchy are shown directly rather than inferred from one cut above B_c.
4. Calibrate the CPD-to-density conversion independently (Hall data on co-written structures, or side-gate dependence of the CPD), keep the tip state fixed between Figs. 3 and 5, and model the KPFM lateral resolution, so that the "optimal doping strip" assignment rests on quantitative footing.
