Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports reflectance-contrast spectroscopy of hBN-encapsulated, gated monolayer WSe2 at 5 K in the low-density electron-doped regime. It makes four claims: (i) a repulsive-polaron umklapp resonance shows a zero-field Wigner crystal (WC) that melts near n_c ≈ 7×10^11 cm^-2 (r_s ≈ 12); (ii) new resonances above the triplet and singlet attractive polarons are "Wigner polarons" (WPs), meaning attractive polarons dressed by WC phonons, whose splitting from the AP (ΔE_WP) measures the WC phonon frequency; (iii) circularly polarized pumping spin/valley-polarizes the WC and selects WP_T or WP_S; (iv) pulsed sub-gap excitation melts the WC optically, removing the umklapp while the WPs survive. An optical probe of WC lattice dynamics in a TMD monolayer would be new and of broad interest, and the extension from MoSe2 to WSe2 (with its triplet/singlet trion pair) is useful. Whether the paper delivers this depends mainly on whether the WP assignment is unique and whether the WP features are specific to the crystal. The current data do not yet settle either point.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear, reproducible umklapp signature of the RP in WSe2 (Fig. 1c, Fig. 2b). ΔE_U is linear in n, the extracted exciton mass is sensible, and a similar n_c is reported across devices and spots (Fig. S1). This extends the MoSe2 result to a second material.
- New spectral features (WP_T, WP_S) above the two attractive-polaron branches. They blueshift with density and follow the valley selectivity of their parent trions under circular pumping (Fig. 3b–d). This is suggestive of a common origin with T⁻/S⁻.
- A contrasting response of the umklapp and the WP features to optical excitation (Fig. 4a,b). If it is established carefully, this could separate static (long-range) from dynamic (local) signatures of the crystal.
- A clearly written experimental section with an explicit procedure for extracting the umklapp energy (dispersive Lorentzian subtraction, three cross-checked methods).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The WP assignment is not tested against the obvious alternative of AP umklapp, and the quantitative comparison with the phonon model is not shown. (p. 4–5, Fig. 2d, Supplementary Discussion 1.) If the WC folds the exciton band for the RP, it should also fold the dispersion of the attractive polarons/trions. That would produce higher-energy satellites above T⁻ and S⁻ that blueshift with density, are identical for the two branches, and inherit their valley selectivity (Fig. 3). These are exactly the properties used to identify the WPs. The two pictures differ in their density scaling: umklapp gives ΔE ∝ n/m*, with m* the trion/AP mass (≈ m_X + m_e), whereas the phonon picture gives n^{3/4}. The authors themselves state that "the power law cannot be unambiguously extracted" and that a linear fit is also possible (Fig. S5). They also differ in magnitude. With a = 4.16 meV/(10^11 cm^-2)^{3/4}, ΔE_WP ≈ 16 meV at 6×10^11 cm^-2, which exceeds the RP umklapp splitting at the same density (~12 meV, Fig. 2b), whereas a heavier-mass AP umklapp should lie below it. The authors should:
   - overlay on Fig. 2d the parameter-free AP-umklapp prediction, using the trion mass from the fitted m_X and m_e, as well as the minimal phonon-model prediction (the 1.5× discrepancy should be visible, not only quoted);
   - fit both models with the same density window and report χ² or residuals;
   - give the independently computed WC phonon energies (transverse/longitudinal, Debye scale) at a few densities, with the ε and m used, to show that ~15 meV is a plausible phonon-sideband energy. A naive classical estimate of the WC plasma/Debye scale at 6×10^11 cm^-2 with m = 0.4 m0 and ε = 4.5 gives only a few meV.

   They should also explain why a phonon dressing of the AP produces a resolved peak rather than a broad sideband, and why no analogous sideband appears on the RP. As it stands, the headline identification "WP–AP splitting is a direct measure of the characteristic phonon frequency" rests on a model (Ref. 34, Supplementary Discussion 1) that the reader cannot inspect, and on a scaling the authors concede is not determined by the data.

2. The WP features are not shown to be specific to the crystal; the data suggest they may persist where the crystal is absent. (Fig. 2d, Fig. 4, p. 6–7.) The dashed WP traces in Fig. 2d extend to top gate ≈ 1.1 V. On the figure's top axis this corresponds to roughly 8–9×10^11 cm^-2, i.e. above the n_c ≈ 7×10^11 cm^-2 at which the umklapp disappears. In Fig. 4b the WPs survive at a pump power where the umklapp is gone. The authors rationalize this as WPs probing "local short-range correlations" in a "partially melted regime". However, that argument concedes that the WPs do not require long-range crystalline order. That undercuts the title claim that WPs probe the dynamics *of a Wigner crystal* and the interpretation of a as the crystal stiffness. To establish specificity the authors should:
   - show the temperature dependence of the WP and umklapp features together across the thermal melting transition (the thermal-melting setup is described in Methods and Figs. S8–S9 exist, but no WP-versus-T data are discussed in the main text);
   - map the WP amplitude and energy continuously through n_c, normalized to the parent AP oscillator strength, and state explicitly at which density each feature disappears;
   - compare with a density/temperature regime well into the liquid, to show what happens to the "WP" there, since a phonon sideband should evolve into a different (e.g. plasmon/Fermi-sea) dressing.

   If the WPs survive in the liquid, the claims should be reframed as probing short-range correlations rather than WC phonons.

3. The optical-melting attribution rests on an arbitrary threshold and on insufficient thermometry. (p. 6–7, Fig. 4, Methods p. 15, Figs. S7–S11.) The "Umklapp critical density" in Fig. 4c is defined by a fixed dR_C/dV cutoff (−0.005 to −0.0065). Any mechanism that broadens or weakens the umklapp feature would lower this "critical density" without melting the crystal: inhomogeneous broadening, an increased electron temperature, or reduced RP oscillator strength. The heating argument relies on the 1s exciton energy staying "essentially unchanged", but several gaps remain:
   - the band-gap shift of WSe2 is very flat at low T (Varshni behaviour), so a 1s shift below resolution does not exclude lattice heating to the 10–20 K range;
   - the melting temperature of the WC and the corresponding ~2 meV prediction must be given explicitly, with the spectral resolution of the 1s fit;
   - the electron temperature, which the authors acknowledge may exceed the lattice temperature, is the relevant quantity for melting and is not constrained;
   - the 650–800 nm pump window starts at ~1.91 eV, essentially at the quoted free-carrier gap (~1.9 eV), and the graphite gate and contacts also absorb, so photodoping and photo-heating are not excluded.

   The authors should normalize the umklapp spectral weight to that of the RP and track its linewidth versus power. They should compare the power dependence of the umklapp with its temperature dependence (equivalent "effective temperature" mapping). They should repeat the measurement with the pump tuned off the exciton (e.g. well below the trion energies) at equal absorbed power. Without these controls, "optical melting" should be stated as a hypothesis, as the discussion section partly does, rather than as a demonstrated result (abstract: "we show optical melting of the Wigner crystal").

B. Should address

4. The spin-control experiment does not show that what is polarized is the WC, nor quantify the polarization. (p. 5–6, Fig. 3, Methods p. 13.) The pump is a 4 μW CW 635 nm (1.95 eV) beam. That is above the stated free-carrier gap, so it photodopes and heats, and it is three orders of magnitude above the 40 nW probe. Points to address:
   - In Fig. 4, a 4000 nW *pulsed* sub-gap pump already destroys the umklapp, yet in Fig. 3b,c the umklapp survives a 4 μW above-gap CW pump. This needs reconciling (different absorption, spot size, or duty-cycle effects) and argues that pump-induced heating/photodoping should be characterized in Fig. 3 as well.
   - Fig. 3 is shown at a single density in Fig. 3d and over gate ranges below ~1 V. The valley selectivity of WP_T/WP_S should be shown as a function of density across n_c. If the same selectivity occurs in the electron liquid (as known for trions, Ref. 49), the statement "all-optical control of spins in the Wigner crystal" is not WC-specific.
   - The degree of electron valley polarization should be estimated quantitatively (e.g. from the T⁻/S⁻ weight ratio), with σ⁻ pump / σ⁺ probe and linear-pump controls showing that the effect reverses with helicity.
   - The statement in Fig. 3d that the umklapp is "largely unaffected" should be quantified (U_RP amplitude and energy under co, cross and unpumped conditions with error bars).

5. The melting density, r_s ≈ 12 and the "enhanced stability" claim need error analysis and a discussion of what the umklapp actually detects. (p. 4–5.) r_s scales as m_e/(ε√n), and the inputs are not internally consistent or propagated:
   - ε_hBN = 3.5 (or 3.1) is used for the density, while ε = 4.5 is used for r_s;
   - the density carries a stated ~10% systematic uncertainty;
   - m_e = 0.4 m0 is taken without justification for the relevant spin-split conduction band.

   An r_s value with an uncertainty range should be given. More importantly, n_c is defined as the density where the umklapp becomes "indiscernible". A correlated liquid with a pronounced structure-factor peak can also give a (broadened) umklapp-like feature, and conversely a weakening feature can drop below the noise before the crystal melts. The authors should discuss which way each bias goes, and show the umklapp amplitude/linewidth versus n near n_c, before attributing a factor of ~3 discrepancy with the clean-limit r_s ≈ 31–38 to disorder or Berry curvature. A temperature-dependent n_c, or a comparison of devices with different disorder (Fig. S1), would strengthen this.

6. Exciton-density estimates in Fig. 4c are underspecified and probably uncertain by more than the plotted error bars. (Methods p. 15, p. 7.) The conversion n_X = PαΓ/(Aħω) uses an absorbance "10% for example", and the spot size A and the bright/dark lifetimes Γ are not given. For 200 ps pulses the peak-power approach assumes a quasi-steady state, which holds for ~ps bright excitons but not obviously for long-lived dark excitons, whose population may accumulate across pulses at 40 MHz (25 ns period). The conclusion that bright excitons "alone are unlikely to melt the WC" while dark excitons (~10^11 cm^-2, a sizeable fraction of n_c) cannot be ruled out depends entirely on these numbers. The authors should list all parameters with sources, propagate their uncertainties into the top/bottom axes of Fig. 4c, and treat inter-pulse accumulation of dark excitons explicitly.

7. The exciton-mass fit and its stated uncertainty are inconsistent, and the fit range includes data above n_c. (p. 4, Fig. 2b.) The text gives m_X ~ (0.85 ± 0.8) m0, a ~95% uncertainty. That cannot follow from the stated "~10% uncertainty in the carrier density" (presumably ± 0.08 is meant), and the value is used as evidence of agreement with m_X ~ 0.81 m0. In addition, Fig. 2b shows ΔE_U points up to ~8×10^11 cm^-2, beyond the n_c ≈ 7×10^11 cm^-2 at which the umklapp is said to be indiscernible, and the high-density points scatter strongly. The authors should state the fit range, whether the intercept was fixed at zero, the statistical and systematic uncertainties separately, and why points above n_c are shown.

8. Device statistics for the WP, spin and optical-melting results are not given. (p. 4, Methods.) Reproducibility across devices and spots is reported only for the umklapp n_c (Fig. S1). The paper does not state how many devices show WP_T/WP_S, the a parameter, the circular-pumping selectivity, or the optical-melting trend, nor whether these were measured on the same Device 1. The authors should give a, n_c and the power threshold of Fig. 4c for each device measured, and state whether the results survive thermal cycling.

9. The theory underpinning the WP interpretation is not available for assessment. (Ref. 34, Supplementary Discussion 1.) Ref. 34 is an arXiv preprint without an identifier, by overlapping authors. The comparison "a is around 1.5 times the value predicted by a minimal phonon model" requires the model's assumptions to be stated in the paper: classical versus quantum WC phonons, exciton–electron interaction strength, trion binding, dielectric screening, and treatment of disorder. In particular, the paper should state which parameters enter the predicted a and whether any are adjusted. The discussion should also address why the RP is "relatively insensitive" to phonon dressing whereas the AP is strongly dressed, as this asymmetry is central to distinguishing WPs from simple AP umklapp (concern 1).

10. Several framing statements go beyond what the experiment measures. (Abstract, Conclusion.) The experiment is steady-state reflectance with ~200 ps pulses and no time resolution. "Achieving ultrafast optical control" and "probing the dynamics" in the time-domain sense are not demonstrated; the "dynamics" accessed are spectral (phonon-dressed) features. The abstract states as established "we show optical melting" and "all-optical control of spins in the Wigner crystal", although the main text treats the mechanism as open. Rephrase to match the evidence: e.g. "suppression of the umklapp signature under pulsed sub-gap excitation", and "valley-selective WP scattering under circular pumping".

C. Minor

The main text cites Fig. S5 for a linear fit of the WP splitting, while Methods cites Fig. S5 for the doping dependence of ΔE_U from three extraction methods. The main text cites Figs. S2–S4 for density calibration, while Methods cites Fig. S12 for the doping onset. The Fig. 2d colour-bar label reads "−0.050" with the zero tick merged; the top density axis of Fig. 2d (to 12×10^11 cm^-2) should be reconciled with Fig. 1c/2b. Fig. 2c is labelled "Wigner crystal polarons" in the panel but "Wigner polarons" in the text.

12. Fig. 3d labels WP_S and WP_T as adjacent minima whose differences between co/cross/no-pump are small. Add fitted amplitudes with uncertainties, and state the density (gate voltage) of the line cut. Also state whether "no pumping" was measured with the 635 nm beam blocked or with linear polarization.

13. Methods: the derivative R′_C uses points n and n+2 (a two-step finite difference); state the gate step and its effect on resolution. The "modified colormap" with power-law exponent p = 0.6 is applied to all main figures. Provide linear-scale versions of Fig. 2d and Fig. 4a,b in the SI so that the visibility of the weak WP features can be judged without the nonlinear mapping.

14. Language and typos: "donate" → "denote" (Fig. 1 caption); "experience remain robust but experience" (p. 6); "To access the magnitude" → "To assess" (p. 7); "Therefor" (p. 14); "Umklapp/umklapp" capitalization is inconsistent. Ref. 34 needs an arXiv number. Ref. 7's arXiv identifier is malformed ("arXiv:250910624").

15. The "single free parameter" in ΔE_WP = a n^{3/4} assumes ΔE_WP → 0 at n → 0. State the density onset used for each gate voltage (doping onset from 2s/RP, Fig. S12) and how its uncertainty propagates into a = 4.16 ± 0.11. The quoted ±0.11 appears to be purely statistical.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a figure that plots, versus density on common axes, the RP-umklapp splitting, the WP_T/WP_S splittings, the parameter-free AP-umklapp prediction, and the computed WC phonon energies. This one comparison would do most of the work of establishing (or reframing) the WP assignment.
2. Present a joint temperature/density map of the umklapp and WP spectral weights (normalized to their parent resonances) through thermal melting. Use it as a calibrated reference for interpreting the optical-pumping data as melting versus heating.
3. Present the WC-specific spin claims as a density series across n_c, with helicity-reversed and linear-pump controls and a quantitative valley-polarization estimate.
4. Include the Supplementary Discussion 1 model in enough detail (Hamiltonian, parameters, approximations) that the 1.5× discrepancy in a can be reproduced and its sources (disorder, quantum fluctuations, screening) discussed quantitatively.
