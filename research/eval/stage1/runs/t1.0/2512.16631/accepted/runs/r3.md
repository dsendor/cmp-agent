Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports reflectance spectroscopy of a gated, hBN-encapsulated WSe2 monolayer at 5 K at low electron density. It makes four claims. (i) An exciton umklapp resonance above the repulsive polaron signals a zero-field Wigner crystal (WC) that melts near n_c ≈ 7×10^11 cm^-2 (r_s ≈ 12). (ii) Two new resonances above the triplet and singlet attractive polarons are "Wigner polarons" (WPs), meaning attractive polarons dressed by WC phonons, whose splitting from the AP scales as n^3/4. (iii) Circularly polarized pumping valley-polarizes the electrons and selects WP_T or WP_S in a one-to-one correspondence with the AP branches. (iv) Increasing the power of the pulsed white-light excitation "optically melts" the WC: the umklapp feature disappears while the WPs survive. An optical probe of WC lattice dynamics, rather than of static order alone, would be a significant advance, and extending umklapp spectroscopy from MoSe2 to WSe2 is useful. However, the central new identification (WPs as phonon-dressed APs specific to the crystal) and the optical-melting interpretation are not yet established against simpler alternatives.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The data are clean. The umklapp splitting is linear in density and gives an exciton mass (~0.85 m0) consistent with independent measurements (Fig. 2b), which supports the density calibration and the umklapp assignment.
- The new satellite resonances above T⁻ and S⁻ (Figs. 1c, 2d) are reproducible. They blueshift with density while the APs stay fixed, and they are seen on the hole side too (Fig. S6).
- The polarization-resolved data (Fig. 3) cleanly link WP_T to T⁻ and WP_S to S⁻, while the RP umklapp is insensitive to valley polarization. This is a useful and fairly model-independent result.
- The work is backed by a dedicated theory (Ref. 34), and the paper is candid that the n^3/4 law is not uniquely determined.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The WP features are not shown to require a crystal; a Fermi-liquid origin is not excluded.**
   - *Where:* pp. 4–5; Figs. 1c, 2d; Fig. S5.
   - *What the paper shows:* WP_T and WP_S are attributed to WC phonon dressing of the APs. The only quantitative support is the fit ΔE_WP = a n^3/4. The authors themselves state that "the power law cannot be unambiguously extracted" and that a linear fit is also possible (Fig. S5).
   - *Why the scaling cannot decide it:* A satellite whose splitting from the trion grows linearly with n is what one expects from Fermi-sea processes. Examples are shake-up satellites, where the trion recoils and leaves an electron–hole excitation, and trion–plasmon or exciton–electron scattering continua. These scale with E_F ∝ n. So the density dependence cannot separate the WC-phonon picture from an electron-liquid picture.
   - *What the paper lacks:*
     - The test that would decide it is whether WP_T and WP_S disappear, or change qualitatively, when the WC melts. The paper does not show this.
     - In Fig. 2d and Fig. 1c the WP traces seem to fade around top gate ≈ 1.0–1.2 V. It is not stated whether this coincides with n_c ≈ 7×10^11 cm^-2 (top axis of Fig. 2d).
     - In the optical-melting data the WPs are said to "remain robust" after the umklapp has vanished (p. 6). Read at face value, this suggests the WPs do not need long-range crystalline order. That weakens, rather than supports, the claim that they "probe the dynamics of a Wigner crystal".
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Show WP energies and spectral weights against density through and beyond n_c, with the umklapp disappearance marked on the same axis.
     - Show the temperature dependence (the Methods mention an ATC100 thermal-melting setup) of the umklapp and WP features together, to establish whether the WPs vanish at the thermal melting temperature.
     - Compare quantitatively with the Fermi-liquid prediction for a shake-up or recoil satellite at the same densities. Explain why such a satellite would be absent, or would differ, in the liquid phase above n_c.
     - If the WPs persist in the liquid, reframe them as probes of short-range correlation, consistent with the authors' own remark on p. 6–7, and soften the title and abstract accordingly.

2. **The "optical melting" claim rests on a threshold criterion that confounds weaker umklapp signal with melting, and heating and photodoping are not fully excluded.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 4; pp. 6–7; Methods p. 15.
   - *The threshold criterion:* The critical density in Fig. 4c is defined as the density where dR_C/dV crosses a fixed value (−0.005 to −0.0065). This quantity depends on the umklapp oscillator strength, its linewidth and the gate-derivative processing. Any of the following would move the extracted "critical density" without any change in where the crystal melts:
     - power-induced broadening;
     - saturation or bleaching of the RP;
     - a reduced reflectance contrast.

     The error bars reflect only the choice of threshold.
   - *Heating:* Lattice heating is excluded through the 1s exciton energy (Fig. S10). That argument needs the WC melting temperature, which is not given in the main text, and the 1s shift expected at that temperature (Figs. S8–S9). The authors also concede that the electronic temperature may exceed the lattice temperature. Electronic heating is exactly the mechanism that would melt the crystal without shifting the 1s exciton.
   - *Pump and probe are the same beam:* From the Methods (40 nW standard, 40–8000 nW for melting), the "pump" appears to be the same pulsed supercontinuum that probes the sample. This should be stated explicitly. Nonlinear (power-dependent) changes of the reflectance itself must then be disentangled from changes in the crystal.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Plot the umklapp spectral weight, normalized to the RP weight, and its linewidth against power at fixed density.
     - Give the thermal-melting density and temperature dependence, n_c(T), and use it to bound the electronic temperature under illumination.
     - Show that the doping onset voltage, the RP blueshift–density relation and the 2s energy are unchanged with power. This would exclude photodoping and photogating.
     - Ideally, separate pump and probe, using a weak probe and a pump at a different energy.

**B. Should address**

3. **The exciton-density estimates behind the exciton-screening mechanism are internally inconsistent and very uncertain.**
   - *Where:* p. 7; Fig. 4c; Methods p. 15.
   - *Inconsistent numbers:* The text says the dark-exciton density is "approximately 10^11 cm^-2", but the top axis of Fig. 4c runs to ~6×10^11 cm^-2. That is comparable to n_c and to the electron density itself.
   - *Implausible implications:* If dark excitons really reached such densities, they should shift or broaden the RP and AP measurably. The authors report no such shifts. Conversely, the bright-exciton densities are ~10^9 cm^-2. It is hard to see how these alone reduce n_c from 6.7 to 0.8×10^11 cm^-2.
   - *Unclear formula:* The Methods formula n_X = PαΓ/Aħω uses the peak power for a 200 ps pulse. Dark-exciton lifetimes (Refs. 52–53) exceed the 25 ns repetition period, so accumulation across pulses (the average power) should matter instead.
   - *What the authors should do:* State the lifetimes, spot size and absorbance actually used; explain the pulsed versus steady-state treatment; and give an uncertainty for the exciton density. Then discuss whether the mechanism is still plausible.

4. **The WC-phonon model and the "quantitative agreement" with a factor-1.5 discrepancy need to be explicit.**
   - *Where:* p. 5; Fig. 2d; Supplementary Discussion 1.
   - *What is reported:* The fitted a = 4.16 meV/(10^11 cm^-2)^3/4 gives a WP–AP splitting of ~13–16 meV at 5–6×10^11 cm^-2.
   - *Why the size of the splitting is questionable:* A naive transverse or longitudinal Debye scale of a 2D WC at this density, ħ(e²/4πε0 ε m* a³)^1/2 with m* = 0.4 m0 and ε = 4.5, is only a few meV.
   - *What the main text lacks:*
     - the model's expression for the splitting;
     - which mass enters (electron or trion);
     - which phonon branch and wavevector dominate;
     - whether one-phonon or multi-phonon processes are involved;
     - why a sideband appears as a distinct peak rather than a continuum.
   - *Fitting choices to clarify:*
     - The dashed lines in Fig. 2d are overlaid on a colour map, not fitted to extracted peak positions with uncertainties.
     - It is not stated which density range was fitted, or how the weak low-density WP signal was handled.
     - Calling a factor of 1.5 "good quantitative agreement" while attributing it to a "stiffer crystal" is not convincing without these details.
   - *What the authors should do:* Supply the formula and parameters, and show extracted peak positions with error bars together with both the n^3/4 and linear fits and their residuals.

5. **The valley-polarization ("spin control") claim is not quantified, not specific to the crystal, and was measured under a CW pump that may itself perturb the crystal.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 3; Methods p. 13.
   - *Pump conditions:* The pump is 635 nm (1.95 eV), above the ~1.9 eV free-carrier gap quoted on p. 6, at 4 μW CW. That is a higher average power than the 4000 nW pulsed excitation that "melts" the crystal in Fig. 4, yet the umklapp feature persists in Figs. 3b–c. The authors should reconcile this. A possible reading is that the melting depends on resonant exciton generation rather than absorbed power, which would itself be informative.
   - *No degree of polarization:* The paper does not give a degree of electron valley polarization, for example from the T⁻/S⁻ weight ratio against the unpumped case.
   - *No liquid-phase comparison:* It does not show whether the same pumping polarizes the electron liquid above n_c. Since Ref. 49 already reports optical pumping of polarization in electron-doped WSe2, "all-optical control of spins in the Wigner crystal" should be shown to differ from that known effect, or the wording narrowed.
   - *Unsupported abstract wording:* The abstract's statement that this "directly prob[es] valley-dependent Wigner polaron scattering" is supported only by the line cuts at a single density in Fig. 3d. Density-resolved WP_T/WP_S weights under co- and cross-polarization would make it convincing.

6. **The r_s estimate and the "enhanced stability" claim depend on assumed parameters that differ from those used elsewhere in the paper.**
   - *Where:* pp. 4–5; Methods p. 14.
   - *Inconsistent dielectric constants:* r_s ≈ 12 uses ε = 4.5 and m* = 0.4 m0. The density calibration uses ε_hBN = 3.5, with 3.1 also mentioned. The choice of ε should be consistent, or justified separately for in-plane screening and gate capacitance.
   - *Unaccounted sensitivities:*
     - The literature values of the WSe2 conduction-band mass span roughly 0.3–0.5 m0.
     - The graphite gates are 20–31 nm away while the interparticle spacing is ~13 nm at n_c, so gate screening may modify the effective interaction.

     Neither is accounted for. Both could shift r_s substantially.
   - *What the authors should do:* Give an uncertainty range for r_s, and discuss how gate screening affects the comparison with the clean-limit r_s ≈ 31–38 and with MoSe2. Without this, "even stronger than MoSe2" is not supported.

7. **Sample statistics and reproducibility are given only for the umklapp feature.**
   - *Where:* Fig. S1; Methods ("Device 1, for example").
   - *What is missing:* Fig. S1 reports similar n_c across devices and spots, but it is not stated how many devices show the WP resonances, the polarization selectivity and the optical-melting behaviour.
   - *What the authors should do:* Report the number of devices and positions for each result, and the spread in the fitted a and in n_c, so readers can judge whether the WPs are generic or tied to one disorder landscape.

**C. Minor**

8. **Exciton-mass uncertainty.** The mass is quoted as m_X ~ (0.85 ± 0.8) m0 (p. 4). This is presumably ± 0.08. With a ~10% density uncertainty, the uncertainty should be about 0.09 m0. Please correct it.

9. **Data beyond n_c in Fig. 2b.** Fig. 2b shows umklapp splittings extracted up to ~8×10^11 cm^-2, beyond the stated n_c ≈ 7×10^11 cm^-2 where the feature is "indiscernible". Please clarify which points are reliable, and mark n_c on the plot.

10. **Fig. S5 described inconsistently.** The main text (p. 5) says Fig. S5 shows a linear fit to the WP–AP splitting. The Methods (p. 14) say Fig. S5 shows ΔE_U from the three extraction methods. One of these references is wrong.

11. **Gating protocol unclear.** The Methods say "A single-gate scan is used", but the density formula includes both gates. Please state which gate was swept and what the other gate was held at for each figure.

12. **WP visibility in Fig. 4b.** In Fig. 4b (4000 nW) the WPs are not labelled and are barely visible. The claim that they "remain robust" relies entirely on Fig. S11. Consider adding WP line cuts at low and high power to the main figure.

13. **Fig. 3d legend and caption disagree.** The legend says "no pumping"; the caption says "unpolarized WCs". Please also state the density or gate voltage of the line cuts.

14. **Non-linear colour scale.** The power-law colour scale (p = 0.6) visually amplifies weak features. Please show at least one map, or line cuts, on a linear scale for the WP region, so readers can judge the signal-to-noise ratio.

15. **Typos and wording:**
    - "To access the magnitude" → "assess" (p. 7);
    - "the Wigner polarons experience remain robust" (p. 6);
    - "Therefor" (p. 14);
    - "AP spectroscopy" in the Conclusion; probably "optical" was meant;
    - "T⁻ and S⁻ donate" → "denote" (Fig. 1 caption).

    Ref. 34 lacks an arXiv identifier.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add one figure that tracks the umklapp weight, the WP_T/WP_S energies and weights, and the AP weights across n_c, at base temperature and at several temperatures. This is the single most important test of whether WPs are crystal-specific.
2. Make the pump–probe protocol for Fig. 4 explicit. If possible, repeat it with a weak probe and a separate pump, either resonant with the exciton or below it. This would separate exciton-induced effects from heating and nonlinear reflectance.
3. Replace the fixed-threshold "critical density" with a fit-based umklapp spectral weight normalized to the RP, and propagate realistic uncertainties in exciton density onto the Fig. 4c axes.
4. Move the explicit phonon-sideband formula and its parameters into the main text or Methods, and show peak-position fits with residuals for the competing scaling laws.
