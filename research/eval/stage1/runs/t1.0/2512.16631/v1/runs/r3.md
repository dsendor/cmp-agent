Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports reflectance spectroscopy of a gated, hBN-encapsulated monolayer WSe2 at low electron density. It makes four claims: (i) an umklapp replica of the repulsive polaron (RP) shows a zero-field Wigner crystal (WC) in WSe2, the first in this material, stable up to n_c ≈ 7×10^11 cm^-2 (r_s ≈ 12) and up to ~25 K; (ii) two new resonances above the triplet and singlet attractive polarons (APs) are "Wigner polarons" (WPs), i.e. APs dressed by WC phonons, so that the WP–AP splitting measures the WC phonon energy; (iii) circularly polarized pumping valley-polarizes the electrons and switches between WP_T and WP_S; (iv) pulsed below-gap excitation melts the WC optically, removing the umklapp peak while the WPs survive. If the WP assignment holds, it would be a new optical probe of WC lattice dynamics, and the WSe2 platform would be useful. As presented, however, the identification of the WPs, the WC interpretation of the umklapp feature at such high r_s and temperature, and the optical-melting claim all rest on one-parameter fits and qualitative trends, with key alternatives not ruled out.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The data look clean. The RP umklapp splitting is linear in n (Fig. 2b) and gives an exciton mass of ~0.85 m0, close to independent values. Three independent extraction methods agree (Fig. S3).
- The two satellite resonances above T− and S− are new and striking. Their polarization selectivity (Fig. 3) matches the triplet/singlet parentage in a natural way.
- Thermal (Figs. S7, S8) and power-dependent (Figs. 4, S6) datasets are both given, and there is a thermometry check against the 1s exciton shift (Fig. S9).
- The phenomenon of the umklapp and satellite features reacting differently to optical excitation is interesting and could stimulate theory.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The WP assignment does not exclude umklapp replicas of the attractive polarons. An umklapp replica of a trion-like AP would sit above T− and S−, blueshift with n, and keep the triplet/singlet polarization selectivity of its parent. That matches every property of WP_T and WP_S used in the paper (pp. 4–5, Figs. 1c, 2d, 3). The expected splitting is ΔE = h²n/(√3 m_T). The paper's own RP umklapp slope is ≈2 meV per 10^11 cm^-2 for m_X ≈ 0.85 m0, so with m_T ≈ m_X + m_e ≈ 1.25 m0 this predicts ≈1.35 meV per 10^11 cm^-2. The linear fit in Fig. S4 gives A ≈ 1.50 meV/(10^11 cm^-2), which is very close. The paper also concedes (p. 5; SI p. 2) that n^3/4 and linear dependences cannot be told apart. The polarization data of Fig. 3 cannot discriminate either, because an AP umklapp inherits the parent's valley selectivity. The authors should:
   - fit the WP data with the umklapp form, using a fixed m_T and zero intercept, and compare goodness-of-fit with the n^3/4 phonon model over the same density window;
   - give the theory's prediction (Ref. 41) for the relative oscillator strengths of AP-umklapp and phonon-sideband features;
   - use observables that separate the two pictures: the WP/AP oscillator-strength ratio against n (a Franck–Condon factor against a structure-factor weight), the ratio of WP linewidth to AP linewidth, and whether ΔE_WP/ΔE_U follows the m_X/m_T scaling expected for umklapp or the n^-1/4 scaling expected for phonons.

   Without this, the central claim that the satellites "reveal Wigner crystal dynamics" (title, abstract) is not established.

2. Under optical pumping the WP resonance survives while the umklapp disappears. This contradicts the claim that WPs are a WC signature. On p. 6 and in Figs. 4b and S10, the umklapp "nearly vanishes" at ~4000 nW, "suggesting optical melting", while the WPs "remain robust" up to 8000 nW. If WPs are APs dressed by WC phonons, they should disappear (or turn into something qualitatively different) when the crystal melts. One of two things must then be true: either the umklapp loss is not melting, or the WP is not specific to the crystalline phase (for example, it comes from short-range correlations or local vibrations that also exist in a correlated liquid). The thermal data point the other way: in Fig. S7 the WP labels and features are gone by 10 K, while U_RP persists to 15 K. The authors need to resolve this inconsistency explicitly. They should:
   - quantify the WP energy, linewidth and oscillator strength against both T and pump power, and against n across n_c;
   - state whether the WPs vanish at the density-driven melting point n_c, which Fig. 2d does not show clearly;
   - temper the title and abstract if the WP cannot be shown to require long-range crystalline order.

3. The evidence for a WC (rather than a strongly correlated liquid) at r_s ≈ 12 and T up to ~25 K is thin. The only diagnostic is the presence of the RP umklapp feature, and its "disappearance" is defined by an arbitrary fixed threshold in dR_C/dV (−0.005 to −0.0065, Methods p. 14; ±0.005 in Fig. S8).
   - The inferred r_s ≈ 12 (p. 4) is a factor ~3 below the clean-limit value of ~31–38.
   - The melting temperature ~25 K (SI p. 3) is roughly an order of magnitude above the classical estimate. At n ≈ 10^11 cm^-2 with ε ≈ 4.5, the Coulomb energy is ~18 meV, and Γ ≈ 130 gives T_m ≈ 1–2 K.
   - Attributing both discrepancies to disorder is speculative.
   - A correlated liquid with a sharply peaked static structure factor can also produce an umklapp-like replica whose weight falls off gradually with n and T.

   The authors should:
   - fit the umklapp oscillator strength (normalized to the RP oscillator strength, which itself changes strongly with n and pump power, Fig. S11c) as a continuous function of n, T and power, and show whether it falls sharply, as a transition would, or gradually, as a crossover would;
   - show the linewidth of the umklapp peak, which tracks correlation length;
   - show how n_c depends on the chosen threshold;
   - discuss explicitly how the observation distinguishes a WC from short-range Wigner-like correlations.

   The phrases "first observation of WC in WSe2" and "zero-field Wigner crystals" should be scoped accordingly.

B. Should address

4. The optical-melting claim does not establish non-thermal melting or the role of excitons.
   - The pump and the probe are the same filtered supercontinuum beam (Methods p. 12; p. 6), so increasing "pump" power also changes the probe. A separate weak probe at fixed power is needed to show that the umklapp loss is not a probe-intensity artifact.
   - The filter range, 650–800 nm, extends to 1.91 eV, at or above the quoted free-carrier gap of ~1.9 eV (p. 6). The pump also spans the 2s/3s Rydberg states, so photo-carrier generation and photodoping through hBN or graphite defects are not excluded. The "no appreciable shift in RP and AP energies" argument is qualitative; RP energy against gate voltage at several powers should be overlaid quantitatively, with the implied density shift bounded.
   - The thermometry (Fig. S9) is done on the 1s exciton, presumably at charge neutrality, not in the doped regime where the WC lives. Its resolution is ~0.2 meV, which by the orange curve corresponds to ~5–8 K, comparable to the temperatures at which n* starts to fall (Fig. S8). The dark-cyan points also drop by ~0.2 meV at the lowest powers.
   - Please map the power dependence of n* onto an equivalent temperature using Fig. S8 and compare it with the bound from the 1s exciton. Repeat the thermometry using the RP or T− energy at a doping inside the WC regime.

5. The exciton-density estimates behind Fig. 4c are not reproducible and look internally inconsistent.
   - Methods (p. 13) gives n_X = PαΓ/(Aħω) with peak power P = P_avg/(f_R t_p), multiplied by the lifetime. For lifetimes longer than the 200 ps pulse, as claimed for dark excitons, the density created per pulse is bounded by the pulse energy times α/(Aħω), not peak power times lifetime. The dark-exciton density is then overestimated.
   - The values used for α ("10% for example"), the spot size A, the lifetimes, and the absorbed fraction of a broadband 650–800 nm pulse are not given.
   - The text says the dark-exciton density is ~10^11 cm^-2 near threshold, while the top axis of Fig. 4c reaches ~6×10^11 cm^-2. The text also says this is "still lower than n_c", yet at the highest power n_c itself has dropped to ~1×10^11 cm^-2.
   - The power corresponding to each point in Fig. 4c is not stated.

   Please give all parameters, correct the formula, and state which powers the Fig. 4c points correspond to.

6. The phonon-model comparison is only semi-quantitative.
   - The SI model is an Einstein oscillator with ΔE_WP = 2ω_T. The numerical inputs (m_e, m_T, κ, the value of ζ, and the resulting predicted a) should be given explicitly.
   - A 50% discrepancy (a_exp ≈ 1.5 a_theory) is described as "good quantitative agreement" (p. 5). It is also unexplained that the crystal appears stiffer than predicted near melting, where it should soften.
   - The linear alternative needs a ~7 meV zero-density intercept that the authors say no mechanism explains (SI p. 2). That cuts against the phonon picture just as much as it supports n^3/4.
   - The claim that both WPs have identical splittings (p. 4) should be tested by fitting a separately for WP_T and WP_S and quoting uncertainties.
   - The fitted curves in Fig. 2d start near V_t ≈ −0.3 V, while the doping onset in Figs. 1b and S11 appears near 0 V. Please clarify the density origin used in the fit.

7. Valley polarization is asserted, not quantified (Fig. 3; p. 5).
   - The degree of electron valley polarization should be extracted, for example from the T−/S− oscillator-strength ratio against the unpumped case, with pump-power and gate dependence.
   - The 635 nm (1.95 eV) CW pump at 4 µW lies above the quoted free-carrier gap. It will create free carriers and may heat the sample, yet the umklapp state under this pump is not characterized against the unpumped case.
   - The probe polarization and the "no pumping" detection configuration should be specified.
   - The interpretation that pumping K polarizes electrons into −K relies on Refs. 53–54. Showing that the effect reverses with the pump helicity, and is absent under linear pumping, would make it convincing.
   - "All-optical control of spins in the Wigner crystal" should be qualified, since a polarized electron population need not be crystalline.

8. Only one device (and apparently one spot) is shown. The number of samples and spots measured is not stated. The umklapp and WP features, n_c, and the WP coefficient a should be reproduced in at least one more device or spatial location, with their spread. This is especially important because disorder is invoked to explain the anomalously large r_s and T_m.

9. Density calibration needs more detail.
   - The hBN thicknesses and the onset voltage are not given.
   - Quantum capacitance and trapped or localized carriers near onset are neglected.
   - The ±10% uncertainty comes only from ε_hBN.
   - Since n_c, r_s, ΔE_U and the m_X extraction all scale with n, please list t_hBN values, show the Fig. S11 onset determination quantitatively, and propagate the full uncertainty to r_s and n_c.
   - Using the fitted m_X as a consistency check is partly circular with the density calibration; say so.

C. Minor

10. On p. 4, "m_X ~ (0.85 ± 0.8) m0" is presumably ±0.08.
11. The y-axis of Fig. 4c reads "×10^1 cm^-2"; this should presumably be ×10^11. Also, n_c at zero pump (~6.6×10^11) differs slightly from the 7×10^11 quoted in the text; reconcile these.
12. Fig. S3 contains outliers (~17–19 meV near 3.8×10^11 cm^-2) and increasing scatter above 6×10^11 cm^-2. Please explain them and state whether they were excluded from the Fig. 2b fit.
13. Figs. 1 and 3: "donate" should be "denote". On p. 6, "the Wigner polarons experience remain robust" needs rewording.
14. Methods: the derivative is a finite difference over two gate steps. Give the gate step, and confirm that the derivative does not create apparent satellite features near the strong RP/AP lines. Showing raw R_C line cuts with the WP peaks would help.
15. "Ultrafast optical control" (abstract, conclusion) is not demonstrated: no time-resolved measurement is reported and the pulses are ~200 ps. Rephrase.
16. Ref. 41 (the theory on which the WP interpretation rests) lacks an arXiv number. The SI model should be self-contained enough to reproduce the predicted a.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a quantitative comparison between the phonon-sideband and AP-umklapp interpretations of the WPs: fits, oscillator-strength and linewidth trends, and the ratio ΔE_WP/ΔE_U against n.
2. Present the umklapp and WP oscillator strengths (normalized) as continuous functions of n, T and pump power on common axes, so the reader can see where each signature disappears and whether the two disappear together.
3. Perform a two-beam experiment, with a weak fixed probe and a separately tunable (strictly below-gap, narrowband) pump. Resonant T−/1s pumping versus off-resonant pumping would separate exciton-mediated melting from heating and photodoping.
4. Show data from a second device and from a magnetic-field-calibrated density (for example, Landau-level or SdH-like features in reflectance) to anchor n_c and r_s.
