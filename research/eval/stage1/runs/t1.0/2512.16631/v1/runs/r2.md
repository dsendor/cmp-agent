Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report gate-dependent reflectance of an hBN-encapsulated, dual-gated WSe2 monolayer at low electron density. They identify an umklapp satellite of the repulsive polaron (RP), which they take as a signature of a zero-field Wigner crystal (WC), the first in WSe2. They also report two new resonances above the triplet and singlet attractive polarons (AP), which they assign to "Wigner polarons" (WP): AP states dressed by phonon pairs of the WC. They then present circular-pump control of the T−/WP_T versus S−/WP_S weights, and a pump-power-driven suppression of the umklapp feature that they call "optical melting". An optical probe of the internal (phonon) dynamics of an electron crystal would be significant. The WP resonances are new, and the umklapp result in a second TMD broadens the platform. At present, however, the assignment of the WP features to WC phonons, and the interpretation of the melting data, rest on fits that cannot discriminate between models and on internal inconsistencies that need resolving.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The umklapp satellite is reproduced in WSe2. ΔE_U is linear in n, with a slope that gives a reasonable exciton mass (Fig. 2b; the extraction is cross-checked by three methods in Figs. S1–S3).
- New resonances WP_T and WP_S appear above both AP branches and blueshift with density (Figs. 1c, 2d). Their polarization selectivity (Fig. 3) follows that of T− and S−, so they are clearly AP-derived.
- The dichotomy is interesting and potentially important: the umklapp feature is quenched by optical excitation while the WP features persist (Figs. 4a,b, S6, S10).
- The data are supported by a microscopic theory (Ref. 41, SI Section 1) that predicts a WP–AP splitting of 2ω_T ∝ n^{3/4}, of the right order of magnitude.
- The temperature dependence of the umklapp feature (Figs. S7, S8) and a lattice-heating check from the 1s energy (Fig. S9) are included.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The WP assignment is not shown to be unique, and the most obvious alternative, umklapp scattering of the attractive polarons themselves, is not excluded.** In a WC, a trion (AP) of mass m_T ≈ m_X + m_e ≈ 1.2–1.3 m0 should acquire its own umklapp satellite at ΔE = h²n/(√3 m_T). The paper's own numbers predict this.
   - Fig. 2b gives ΔE_U ≈ 2 meV per 10¹¹ cm⁻² for the RP with m_X ≈ 0.85 m0. An AP umklapp line would then have a slope of ≈ 2 × 0.85/1.25 ≈ 1.4 meV per 10¹¹ cm⁻².
   - That is essentially the slope of the linear fit the authors report for ΔE_WP in Fig. S4 (A ≈ 1.50 meV per 10¹¹ cm⁻²).
   - The text (p. 4) argues that "there exists only one repulsive polaron and one umklapp peak because these resonances originate from exciton-electron scattering states". This explains why there is no separate RP umklapp per spin channel. It does not explain why bound trions, which are not scattering states, would show no umklapp satellite.
   - The 7 meV intercept of the linear fit is the only feature that distinguishes the two pictures. The SI admits that the n^{3/4} and linear forms cannot be told apart, because the WP lines are not resolved at low density.

   Other candidates (hybridization with the RP, inhomogeneous regions, Fano or interference artefacts of the dispersive line shape in a derivative map) are not discussed either.

   **What the authors should do:**
   - State the expected AP-umklapp energy, and its oscillator strength from the theory of Ref. 41, and show quantitatively why the observed lines are not it.
   - Extract WP peak positions with error bars, versus calibrated density rather than gate voltage (Fig. 2d is plotted against V_TG while Fig. 2b uses n). Fit both models with the AP energies and the density onset treated consistently.
   - Ideally, show a test that discriminates the models: the ratio of WP_T and WP_S splittings, the n-dependence of the WP linewidth or oscillator strength, or the trion-mass dependence (for example in another TMD, or with T− against S− if their masses differ).

2. **The WP and umklapp features respond oppositely to temperature and to optical excitation, which undercuts the claim that both report the same WC.**
   - Under pumping, the umklapp line vanishes at about 4000 nW while the WPs "remain robust" up to 8000 nW (Figs. 4a,b, S6, S10).
   - With temperature, the WP labels appear only in the 5 K map of Fig. S7. At 10 K and 15 K the umklapp line is still present but no WP is marked or evident.
   - Along the density axis, the WPs are traced in Fig. 2d only to about V_TG ≈ 1.0–1.3 V. It is not shown whether they disappear at n_c ≈ 7×10¹¹ cm⁻², where the umklapp line disappears.

   If the WP is a WC phonon sideband, it should vanish wherever the crystal melts. If it survives "optical melting", either the umklapp disappearance is not melting, or the WP is not a crystal phonon feature: it could be a short-range-correlation feature of a liquid, or something else entirely. The headline claim, "Wigner polarons reveal Wigner crystal dynamics", depends on resolving this.

   **What the authors should do:**
   - Show the WP energies, linewidths and amplitudes as functions of n, T and pump power on the same axes as the umklapp amplitude, including at and above n_c and T_m.
   - Explain within the model of Ref. 41 why a phonon-dressed polaron would survive melting of the lattice that supplies the phonons.

3. **The evidence that the umklapp feature signals a long-range-ordered WC sits uneasily with the inferred r_s and melting temperature, and the melting criteria are arbitrary.**
   - The authors estimate melting at r_s ≈ 12, versus ≈ 31–38 predicted in the clean limit, and attribute the gap to disorder without testing this.
   - The umklapp line survives to about 25 K (Figs. S7, S8). A classical-melting estimate with the paper's own parameters (ε ≈ 4.5, Γ ≈ 130) gives T_m of only a few kelvin at n ≈ 5×10¹¹ cm⁻². The Coulomb energy there is about 20 meV, so k_BT_m ≈ e²√(πn)/(4πε₀εΓ) ≈ 0.3 meV.
   - The critical densities in Fig. 4c and Fig. S8 come from a fixed threshold on dR_C/dV (−0.005 to −0.0065, or ±0.005). Such a threshold also tracks changes in the overall RP oscillator strength and linewidth (Fig. S11), not only crystalline order.

   An umklapp-like satellite needs only a density modulation at wavevector k_W on the exciton's length scale. A correlated liquid with strong short-range order, or a disorder-pinned glass, could produce it. Neither would support statements about "melting" or "WC phonons".

   **What the authors should do:**
   - Normalize the umklapp amplitude to the RP oscillator strength, and show that n* and T_m are insensitive to the choice of threshold.
   - Compare T_m(n) with classical and quantum melting estimates, and discuss explicitly why the crystal survives to 25 K.
   - Temper the WC language, or bring further evidence of crystalline order: the umklapp linewidth or its coherence versus n, or comparison with Ref. 5's criteria.

B. Should address

4. **Sample count and reproducibility are not stated.** All the data appear to come from one device and, probably, one spot. The paper does not say how many devices or locations show the umklapp and WP features, whether WP_T and WP_S appear in every device, or how n_c, T_m and the WP prefactor a vary between them. Given the disorder-based explanation of the enhanced stability (p. 5), device-to-device variation of n_c is directly relevant. The authors should report the number of devices, show the key maps (Fig. 1c, Fig. 2d) for at least one more device or spot, and quote the spread in n_c and a.

5. **The n^{3/4} fit and the "good quantitative agreement" claim need more support** (Fig. 2d; p. 5; SI Section 1).
   - The fit has one free parameter, but it also fixes the AP reference energies and the doping onset. The trion mass, dielectric constant and electron mass used to compute the theoretical prefactor are not given.
   - The measured a is 1.5 times the model value, and this is called "good quantitative agreement".
   - The harmonic model treats electrons in fixed parabolic wells. Near r_s ≈ 12, close to melting, zero-point motion is large and anharmonicity should be strong: ħω_e ≈ 9 meV is a sizeable fraction of the ≈ 20 meV Coulomb energy per electron at 5×10¹¹ cm⁻².

   The authors should list all parameters, give the error on a, show residuals for the n^{3/4} and linear fits with fit statistics, and discuss the validity of the harmonic approximation (for example the Lindemann ratio at the measured densities). They should also explain why the 7 meV intercept of the linear fit is unphysical, rather than an offset of the kind any AP-derived satellite would have.

6. **The "optical control of WC spin" (Fig. 3) does not establish anything specific to the WC, and the degree of polarization is not quantified.**
   - Circular-pump valley polarization of resident electrons in WSe2 is known (Refs. 53, 54). That WP_T and WP_S follow the T− and S− selection rules shows they are AP-derived, but any AP-derived satellite would do the same, including an AP umklapp (Concern 1). So Fig. 3 does not discriminate between models of the WP.
   - The pump is 635 nm (≈ 1.95 eV) at 4 µW CW. By the paper's own number (free-carrier gap ≈ 1.9 eV, p. 6) this lies above the gap, so it can photodope and heat the sample.
   - No pump-power dependence, degree of polarization, or check of the RP and umklapp energies under the pump is shown.

   The authors should quantify the electron valley polarization: from the T−/S− weight ratio, calibrated against a magnetic-field measurement where the polarization saturates. They should give the pump-power dependence, and confirm that the umklapp line, and therefore the crystal, persists unchanged under the pump (Fig. S5 suggests it does, but at only one density). Claims that this "directly probes valley-dependent WP scattering well above the magnetic ordering temperature" should be phrased as control of the valley polarization of the electrons.

7. **The optical-melting interpretation has unresolved heating and density-estimation issues** (p. 6, Fig. 4c, Methods).
   - The pump appears to be the same pulsed supercontinuum used as the reflectance probe (Methods: "a pulsed white laser is utilized as the pumping source"). If so, "pump power" is the probe power, and there is no independent pump-off reference. This should be stated clearly.
   - The 1s-energy thermometer (Fig. S9) is coarse at low T: the 1s energy shifts by under 1 meV between 5 and 10 K. The pump series shows a small initial redshift, consistent with a few kelvin of lattice heating. Electron heating, which the authors acknowledge, is not bounded at all, and even modest electron heating matters given the steep n*(T) in Fig. S8. An electronic thermometer would help, for example the T−/S− or umklapp linewidth calibrated against Fig. S7.
   - The injected-density formula uses α = 10% "for example", with no stated spot size A or lifetimes Γ. It converts average power to peak power by assuming a 200 ps pulse, although the band is filtered to 650–800 nm.
   - The text says the dark-exciton density at threshold is "approximately 10¹¹ cm⁻²" and "still lower than n_c". The top axis of Fig. 4c reaches about 6×10¹¹ cm⁻² at the highest bright-exciton density, comparable to n_c.

   The authors should reconcile these numbers, give all the parameters with uncertainties, and report the pump wavelength range and probe conditions explicitly.

8. **The density calibration and the derived quantities need clarifying** (Methods, p. 4).
   - The quoted exciton mass m_X ≈ (0.85 ± 0.8) m0 carries an almost 100% uncertainty. That is presumably a typo (± 0.08?), but as written it voids the comparison with 0.81 m0.
   - The Methods say a "single-gate scan" is used, while the density formula involves both gates. Which gate was swept in each figure, and the hBN thicknesses, should be stated.
   - The density uses ε_hBN = 3.5, while r_s uses ε = 4.5, and the electron mass of 0.4 m0 is not justified for the relevant conduction band. These choices directly set r_s ≈ 12, so their uncertainty should be propagated.
   - In Fig. 2b, the fit range is not stated, nor whether the fit is constrained through the origin. The points above 6×10¹¹ cm⁻² scatter strongly. Fig. S3 shows outliers near 3.8×10¹¹ cm⁻², and the Lorentzian method stops there.
   - The RP is a dressed exciton, so the umklapp splitting probes the RP mass, not the bare exciton mass. This should be discussed.

C. Minor

9. Fig. S10 is cited on p. 6 for the WP behaviour "at this pumping power" (≈ 4000 nW), but it shows 8000 nW. Please show the 4000 nW WP data, or correct the reference.

10. Figs. 1c, 2d, 3b,c, 4a,b and S4–S10 use top-gate voltage as the x-axis. Please add a calibrated density axis, so that n_c, the WP extent and Fig. 2b can be compared directly.

11. Fig. 4c: the zero-pump critical density (≈ 6.6×10¹¹ cm⁻²) differs from n_c ≈ 7×10¹¹ cm⁻² (p. 4) and from n* at 5 K in Fig. S8 (≈ 6.9×10¹¹ cm⁻²). Please state the threshold used in each case. Also explain how the error bars in Fig. 4c were obtained.

12. The Fig. 4c inset depicts "exciton screening of electron repulsion" as the mechanism, while the text explicitly leaves the mechanism open. Please align the caption with the text.

13. The SI states that "None of these effects can, however, explain the large zero-density AP-WP splitting implied by the intercept". This argument should appear in the main text alongside Fig. S4, together with the theory's predicted WP oscillator strength relative to the AP.

14. The Methods define the derivative R'_C over a two-step voltage interval (V_{g,n+2} − V_{g,n}), but give no voltage step size. Please state it, together with the spectral resolution, since the WP–AP splittings near onset approach a few meV.

15. Typos and references: "donate" should be "denote" (Fig. 1 and Fig. S6 captions); "WP polarons experience remain robust" (p. 6); Ref. 7's arXiv number is malformed, and Ref. 41 lacks an arXiv identifier. That theory carries the key WP model, so it should be identifiable.

16. "This constitutes the first observation of WC in WSe2" (p. 4): please qualify this with the caveats of Concern 3, and cite any prior WSe2-based reports of electron solids (including in moiré or bilayer geometries) to clarify the scope of the priority claim.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Put the umklapp amplitude (normalized to the RP oscillator strength) and the WP energy, linewidth and amplitude on common n, T and pump-power axes in one main-text figure. This would expose directly whether the two signatures track the same phase.
2. Include an explicit model comparison, WP as a phonon-pair sideband versus WP as AP umklapp, with predicted energies, slopes and oscillator strengths, using the Ref. 41 framework and the measured m_X.
3. Add magnetic-field data, even modest out-of-plane fields, to calibrate the valley polarization in Fig. 3. These would also test whether the WP_T/WP_S splitting and intensity depend on the spin order of the crystal, which would give more weight to the "spin-ordered WC" language.
4. Measure a second device, preferably one with a different expected disorder level, to test the disorder explanation of the low r_s and high T_m, and to establish that the WP features are reproducible.
