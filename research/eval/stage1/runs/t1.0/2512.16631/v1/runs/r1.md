Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports reflectance spectroscopy of an hBN-encapsulated, graphite-gated WSe2 monolayer at low electron density. It makes four claims: (i) an exciton umklapp resonance above the repulsive polaron (RP) shows that a zero-field Wigner crystal (WC) forms below n_c ~ 7x10^11 cm^-2 (r_s ~ 12), presented as the first WC in WSe2; (ii) two new resonances above the triplet and singlet attractive polarons (WP_T, WP_S) are "Wigner polarons", meaning attractive polarons dressed by WC phonons, with a splitting that scales as n^(3/4); (iii) circularly polarized pumping controls the WC spin/valley polarization, and WP_T and WP_S follow it selectively; (iv) a pulsed broadband pump melts the WC "optically", quenching the umklapp while the WP survives. If (ii) holds, it would be a new spectroscopic handle on the internal phonon dynamics of an electron solid, beyond the static order seen through umklapp and the collective pinning modes seen in THz. This would interest a broad audience. At present the identification of the WP features, and their specific link to the WC, rests mainly on fitting a power law over a narrow window and on a minimal harmonic model. The direct tests that would tie these features to crystalline order are missing or point the other way.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The data quality is high. Fig. 1b shows a narrow 1s line (gamma ~ 4.5 meV, Fig. S11b) and resolved 2s/3s Rydberg states, and the umklapp feature is clearly visible in dR_C/dV (Fig. 1c, Fig. 2b).
- The RP-umklapp splitting is linear in n and gives an exciton mass (~0.85 m0) consistent with independent measurements. Three independent extraction methods agree (Figs. S2, S3). This is a solid, quantitative confirmation that the umklapp physics seen in MoSe2 (Ref. 5) carries over to WSe2.
- The WP_T/WP_S features are new. The helicity-resolved data (Fig. 3b,c), in which WP_T appears with T- under co-polarization and WP_S with S- under cross-polarization, are a clean and attractive check of the proposed one-to-one link between each WP and its parent attractive polaron.
- The power-dependent data (Figs. 4, S6, S10) show that the umklapp and WP features respond to excitation in markedly different ways. This is interesting whatever the final interpretation.
- The authors are candid about the limits of the n^(3/4) fit (Fig. S4) and about the open mechanism of optical melting.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The WP resonances are not distinguished from other density-dependent satellites, in particular umklapp replicas of the attractive polarons. The central new claim (Abstract; p. 4-5; Fig. 2c,d) is that WP_T and WP_S are attractive polarons (APs) dressed by WC phonons, with Delta E_WP = 2 omega_T proportional to n^(3/4). The paper argues (p. 4) that "only one repulsive polaron and one umklapp peak" exist because the AP is a bound state. However, an AP (trion) moving in a periodic WC also has a folded band. Its umklapp replica would sit above each AP at Delta E ~ h^2 n/(sqrt(3) m_T), linear in n, with a smaller slope than the RP umklapp because m_T > m_X. The authors' own linear fit (Fig. S4) gives a slope of ~1.5 meV per 10^11 cm^-2. The RP umklapp slope is ~2.0 meV per 10^11 cm^-2 (Fig. 2b). The ratio, ~0.75, is close to m_X/(m_X + m_e) ~ 0.85/1.25 ~ 0.7 with the masses quoted on p. 4. The 7 meV intercept in Fig. S4 is roughly the T-S splitting itself: the WP_S line in Fig. S4 extrapolates to the T- energy. A feature tied to the other trion, or another fine-structure satellite, is therefore also a candidate. Because the n^(3/4) and linear-plus-offset forms cannot be told apart over the density window shown (p. 5, Fig. S4), the power-law fit does not discriminate between these pictures.
   - The authors should test predictions that differ between the scenarios:
     - Show whether the WP splitting extrapolates to zero at the onset of doping. This needs data, or at least an upper bound on the feature, at the lowest densities, where n^(3/4) and linear-plus-offset differ most.
     - Compare the WP slope and curvature with the RP-umklapp data from the same spectra, plotted on a common density axis with error bars. At present, extracted WP energies against n with uncertainties are not shown at all; Fig. 2d only overlays theory curves on a colour map.
     - Report the WP linewidths and oscillator strengths against n and compare them with what the phonon-sideband model predicts (Huang-Rhys factor, expected sideband weight).
     - Discuss explicitly why an AP umklapp replica is absent or negligible according to the theory of Ref. 41.

2. The link between the WP features and crystalline order is not shown, and the data in hand appear to point the other way. If WPs are AP-plus-WC-phonon states, they should disappear where the WC melts. At present they do not track the umklapp signal in the way the interpretation requires:
   - Thermal melting (Fig. S7): the WP features are already gone at 10 K, while the umklapp signal persists to 15 K and n* remains ~6x10^11 cm^-2 at 10 K (Fig. S8).
   - Optical melting (Fig. 4b, Fig. S10, p. 6): the umklapp nearly vanishes at ~4000 nW, yet the WP is said to "remain robust", surviving with broadening and a redshift up to 8000 nW.
   - Density: the paper does not state whether the WPs disappear at n_c. In Fig. 1c, WP_S fades near ~1.2 V, while the umklapp persists to ~1.5 V.

   A feature that survives the loss of long-range order but vanishes under modest heating is hard to reconcile with "a direct measure of the characteristic phonon frequency of the Wigner crystal" (p. 4). It may instead reflect short-range correlations in a liquid, or something unrelated to the WC. The authors should:
   - plot WP amplitude, energy and width against temperature (5-15 K in fine steps), against density up to and beyond n_c, and against pump power, on the same axes as the umklapp amplitude;
   - explain within their model why WPs require less order than umklapp scattering but are more fragile thermally.

   Without this, the title claim ("Wigner polarons reveal Wigner crystal dynamics") does not follow.

3. The claim of "optical melting" rests on a fixed-amplitude threshold and on a heating check that cannot reach the electron temperature. The critical density in Fig. 4c (and Fig. S8) is defined as the density at which dR_C/dV crosses a fixed value (-0.005 to -0.0065 in Methods; "-0.005/+0.005" in the Fig. S8 caption). The umklapp amplitude in dR_C/dV scales with the RP oscillator strength and linewidth, with the exciton-electron coupling, and with the Debye-Waller factor of the lattice, not only with the presence of order. Any pump-induced broadening or bleaching of the RP, or saturation at the peak power of ~0.5 mW reached at 4 uW average, lowers the umklapp contrast below a fixed threshold without melting. In addition:
   - The pulsed "pump" is the same supercontinuum source as the reflectance probe (Methods), so the measurement is time-integrated over the pulse itself. Its 650-800 nm window starts at 1.91 eV, at or above the ~1.9 eV free-carrier gap quoted on p. 6.
   - Fig. S9 shows that the 1s energy barely moves below ~8 K, and a ~0.2 meV drop is already visible at the lowest pump powers. The 1s shift therefore bounds the lattice temperature only to roughly 8-10 K, and says nothing about the electron temperature, which the authors acknowledge may be higher (p. 6).

   The authors should:
   - normalize the umklapp amplitude to the fitted RP oscillator strength and width at each power, and show the umklapp-to-RP weight ratio against n and power;
   - show that n* is robust to the choice of threshold;
   - compare the umklapp line shape at a given pump power with a thermal-melting data set matched in RP linewidth;
   - if possible, separate the pump and probe (spectrally or in time, for example with a weak delayed probe) so that a nonequilibrium electron temperature can be distinguished from a change in the ground state.

   Unless this is done, "optical melting" should be stated as "pump-induced suppression of the umklapp resonance".

B. Should address

4. Number of devices and reproducibility are not reported. The Methods describe the fabrication of "a device", and every figure appears to come from one sample at one spot. The WP features are weak: in Fig. 3d they are a few percent of the T- dip. Their observation in at least one more device, and at several spots on this one, with the spread in n_c, a and the WP-AP splitting, is needed to establish them as intrinsic. Spatial mapping of the umklapp and WP amplitudes would also address the possibility that the "satellites" come from regions with different local doping or dielectric environment.

5. Density calibration, gate geometry and screening are under-specified. The hBN thicknesses and the gate-to-sample distances are not given. These enter the density (Methods, Eq. for n). They also determine whether gate screening matters: the WC lattice constant at n_c is ~13 nm, and if either gate lies within a few tens of nm, screening softens the long-wavelength phonons and changes both the melting density and the WP stiffness. The SI model (Eq. S1) uses an unscreened Coulomb constant (zeta = 11.034). Three further issues:
   - The Methods state a "single-gate scan", while Fig. 1a shows two gates and the density formula includes both. Please state exactly which gate was swept and how the other was held.
   - The paper uses eps_hBN = 3.5 for the capacitance but kappa = 4.5 for r_s and the phonon model. The choice of each should be justified (out-of-plane against geometric mean).
   - The 10% density uncertainty derived from 3.5 against 3.1 does not include the uncertainty in hBN thickness or in the onset voltage.

   Please give the thicknesses, include gate screening in the phonon estimate, and propagate the uncertainty into r_s, m_X and a. An independent density calibration from magneto-optics (Landau-level or Shubnikov-de Haas features, as in Refs. 59, 62) would remove the main systematic.

6. The quantitative WC parameters sit far from expectations, and the paper treats this only briefly. With kappa = 4.5 and m_e = 0.4 m0, the reported melting at r_s ~ 12 is about three times below the clean-limit value (~31-38). A thermal-melting estimate is also far off. The classical plasma parameter Gamma = E_C/k_BT ~ 130 implies T_m of a few kelvin at n ~ 5x10^11 cm^-2, yet the umklapp survives to 15-25 K (Figs. S7, S8). Both discrepancies are attributed to disorder (p. 4-5; SI section 2). They are large enough to raise the question of whether the umklapp here reflects a disorder-pinned glass or short-range correlations rather than a crystal. The authors should discuss these numbers quantitatively. They should address what umklapp intensity a correlated liquid with a structure-factor peak would produce, and what the umklapp linewidth against n says about the correlation length. They should temper "first observation of WC in WSe2" accordingly, or support it with evidence for long-range order (for example the dependence of umklapp linewidth on n and T).

7. The theory for WPs is a single-oscillator (Einstein) model, while the WC has a gapless phonon continuum. In SI section 1, the trion sits in a harmonic well with the electron's frequency, phonons are created in pairs, and 2 omega_T is compared with the data. A sideband coupled to an acoustic phonon continuum would generally be broad and asymmetric, not a sharp resonance. The factor ~1.5 discrepancy is attributed to disorder and stiffness without a calculation. The comparison also depends on m_e, m_T, kappa and gate screening (concern 5), each uncertain at the tens-of-percent level, so "good quantitative agreement" (p. 5) is not yet earned. Please:
   - state which features of the WP (sharpness, weight, equal splitting for T and S) follow from the full model of Ref. 41 rather than the Einstein approximation;
   - give the predicted WP/AP weight ratio and linewidth and compare them with the data;
   - show how a changes over the plausible ranges of m_e, kappa and gate distance.

   Ref. 41 is an unpublished arXiv preprint by co-authors, listed without an identifier. The key results needed to judge the interpretation should be summarized in the SI.

8. The spin-control experiment does not isolate anything specific to the WC. Valley pumping of resident electrons in WSe2 by circularly polarized light is established (Refs. 53, 54). The helicity dependence of T- and S- in Fig. 3 reproduces that. The new element is that WP_T and WP_S follow their parent APs, which is a useful check of the WP assignment (concern 1) but not "all-optical control of spins in the Wigner crystal" as such. Specific gaps:
   - The electron spin/valley polarization is not quantified. Please extract it from the T-/S- weight ratio and compare with a magneto-optical calibration.
   - No comparison is made with the same measurement above n_c or above the melting temperature, where the electrons are a liquid. Such a comparison would show whether anything about the polarization or the WP response is WC-specific.
   - The 635 nm pump (1.95 eV) is above the ~1.9 eV free-carrier gap quoted on p. 6 and is CW at 4 uW. Photodoping or heating could shift density between the "pumped" and "no pumping" traces in Fig. 3d. Please show that the RP energy (and hence n) is matched in the compared traces.
   - The statement that the umklapp is "largely unaffected" (Fig. S5) should be quantified, with an uncertainty, and compared with the model prediction.

9. The injected exciton densities in Fig. 4c are order-of-magnitude estimates whose assumptions are not justified. The formula n_X = P alpha Gamma/(A hbar omega), applied to peak power, assumes that the lifetime is short compared with the 200 ps pulse. This holds for bright excitons but not for dark excitons with lifetimes of hundreds of ps to ns. For those, the per-pulse photon number and inter-pulse accumulation (25 ns period) should be used. The absorbance ("10%, for example"), spot size and the spectral overlap of the 650-800 nm broadband pump with the absorption spectrum are not given. The power corresponding to each point in Fig. 4c is not shown either. Because the conclusion that "bright excitons alone are unlikely to melt the WC" (p. 6) rests on these numbers, please state every input, include the pulse-train accumulation for the dark population, add a pump-power axis to Fig. 4c, and give error bars on the densities.

10. The extraction of the umklapp energy at small splitting needs care. For n < ~2.5x10^11 cm^-2, Delta E_U (< 5 meV) is smaller than the RP linewidth (~4.5-6 meV, Fig. S11b). The umklapp position then depends on subtracting the dispersive-Lorentzian RP fit, whose residuals dominate near E_X (Fig. S2). In Fig. S3, the Lorentzian-fit method gives a ~1.5 meV offset at n -> 0 that the other methods do not, and there are outliers at ~3.7x10^11 cm^-2 (17-19 meV). Please state whether the linear fit in Fig. 2b is constrained through the origin, how the outliers are treated, and how the uncertainty in m_X is obtained (it is quoted as "0.85 +/- 0.8 m0" on p. 4, presumably a typo for 0.08).

C. Minor

11. Fig. 4b does not label the WP features and they are hard to see, although the text says they "remain robust" at this power. Please mark them, or show line cuts at fixed density for several powers, comparing WP and umklapp amplitudes directly.

12. The threshold used to define the critical density differs between Methods (-0.005 to -0.0065) and the Fig. S8 caption (-0.005/+0.005). Please use one criterion so that Figs. 4c and S8 can be compared, and give the source of the error bars in Fig. 4c.

13. The inset of Fig. 4c and its caption name a mechanism ("exciton screening of electron repulsion") that the main text explicitly leaves open. Please align the two.

14. The Methods say R is normalized "by dividing the reflected light intensity R0 from the nearby bare hBN region". The background region (bare hBN on graphite? SiO2?) should be specified, since it affects the interference phase alpha_0 used in the fits. The strong gate dependence of tan alpha_0 in Fig. S11d (0 to 0.6) also deserves comment, because it is not obviously an interference effect.

15. Fig. S9: error bars "increased by a factor of 2 for clarity" are unusual. Please show the true uncertainties and add the 8000 nW point used in Fig. S10.

16. Text and labels:
   - "donate" should be "denote" (captions of Fig. 1 and Fig. S6).
   - The y-axis units of Fig. 4c and Fig. S8 appear as "x10^1 cm^-2".
   - "Wigner polarons experience remain robust" (p. 6).
   - "Spin-" (p. 6) should be "spin-forbidden".
   - "Attodory" (Methods).

17. Please give the gate-voltage step used in the finite-difference derivative R'_C(V_g,n), the spot size, and the integration time. Please also say whether the gate sweeps were hysteresis-free.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one summary figure: amplitudes and energies of the umklapp, WP_T and WP_S on a common density axis (with n_c marked), for several temperatures and pump powers. This is the most direct way to show, or refute, that the WPs are tied to crystalline order (concerns 1-3).
2. Measure in a magnetic field. Landau-level or Shubnikov-de Haas features would calibrate the density independently (concern 5). A Zeeman-polarized reference would calibrate the optically pumped valley polarization (concern 8). The field dependence of WP_T against WP_S would give an independent test of their spin character.
3. Use a separate, spectrally narrow, time-delayed pump with a weak probe for the melting experiments. The recovery time of the umklapp signal would distinguish heating of the electron system, accumulation of long-lived dark excitons, and a genuine exciton-induced change in the ground state.
4. Repeat the key results in a second device, ideally with a different gate distance. The predicted change in phonon stiffness and melting density with gate screening would be a strong quantitative test of the WP-phonon picture.
