Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports magnetotransport in (001)-oriented MBE Cd3As2 films (210–560 nm) with electron densities from 2.9×10^16 to 2.3×10^17 cm^-3. It interprets a kink or shoulder in ρyx(H), and a matching lump in ρxx(H), as signatures of the 3D quasi-quantum Hall effect (QQHE). It models ρxx(H) as a QHE-scaling term (αH∂Hρyx) plus strong-Coulomb-disorder magnetoresistance terms, and argues that lowering the density, which is needed to reach the quantum limit at modest fields, also strengthens the screened Coulomb disorder (eV0/EF) and so obscures the QQHE. The question of how disorder limits 3D QQHE in gapless systems matters, and a tunable-density Dirac semimetal platform would be useful. The paper also presents a k·p Landau-band Kubo calculation of QQHE in Cd3As2. As presented, however, the evidence is mostly qualitative. Both central attributions, that the ρyx features are QQHE and that the background is strong-Coulomb-disorder MR, rest on assumptions the paper does not test.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A systematic series of six films spanning nearly an order of magnitude in carrier density. All films are measured in the same way, and the change from linear, saturating-tanθH behaviour (Sample F) to kinked ρyx and super-quadratic MR (Samples A–E) is clear (Figs. 3, 4).
- A high-field measurement on Sample D to 31.5 T (Fig. 4k) that shows a flat ρyx segment. Its value (ρyx^FP ≈ −155 mΩ cm) is said to agree with the theoretical final-plateau value.
- A microscopic k·p/Kubo calculation of the QQHE for Cd3As2, including the Berry-curvature contribution to σxy (Fig. 1e,f; Methods).
- A physically motivated framework that links the observed MR to a known disorder parameter (eV0/EF). It gives a falsifiable direction for future work: reduce the charged-defect density and see whether the QQHE sharpens.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The low-field ρyx features are not shown quantitatively to be QQHE plateaus.** In the main text, only Sample D (Fig. 4k) is compared with a predicted plateau value, and that comparison is relegated to Supplementary Note 7 without the number being stated in the main text. For Samples A–E below 14 T, the step in ρyx is asserted to be "a signature of a broadened plateau at higher filling factor" (p. 5), but no filling factor is assigned and no expected plateau value or field position is given. To support the headline claim ("we have observed evidence of the 3D QQHE"), the authors should, for each sample:
   - (i) estimate kF,z from the measured n, or better from SdH frequencies, using the known Fermi-velocity anisotropy of Cd3As2;
   - (ii) predict the fields at which the N = 1→0 (and N = 2→1) Landau bands depopulate and the corresponding ρyx ≈ (h/e²)(π/kF,z)/ν values;
   - (iii) overlay these on Figs. 3e and 4f–j.

   The paper also says that features "evolve from oscillations" (Supplementary Note 4). If so, a Landau-index fan with the kink field, the ρxx lump at ~5.7 T in Sample E, and the oscillation minima on one plot would directly test the Landau-band origin. Without this, the kink could be any field-dependent change in the Hall slope.

2. **Multicarrier and parallel-conduction explanations are dismissed only qualitatively.** On p. 3 the authors argue that two-band models cannot give a rapidly changing, nonmonotonic FMR or an abrupt Hall-slope change, and they do not pursue it further. At n ≈ 3×10^16 cm^-3 in a 210–560 nm film, the bulk sheet density is only ~10^12 cm^-2. That is comparable to typical interface-accumulation, surface-state or buffer-layer sheet densities. Several channels could produce a field-dependent Hall slope and large MR:
   - a parallel channel in the Zn_xCd_{1−x}Te buffer or at the GaAs interface;
   - topological surface (Fermi-arc) states;
   - a depleted or accumulated surface layer, which matters especially because these films are not capped (p. 6 notes that depositing dielectrics has been challenging).

   A two-carrier channel that freezes out or enters its own quantum limit at a few tesla can produce a shoulder in ρyx. The authors should:
   - show an explicit two-carrier (or mobility-spectrum) fit of σxx(H) and σxy(H) for Samples A–C, and where it fails;
   - give the expected conductance of the buffer and of a bare-buffer reference sample;
   - use the thickness variation they already have (Sample B is 210 nm, the others 350–560 nm) to test whether the features scale as bulk (3D) or sheet (2D) quantities.

   Tilted-field measurements would be the most direct test. For a bulk 3D QQHE, the plateau field and value are set by kF,z along the field direction and by the full 3D Fermi surface. For a 2D or surface channel, the features scale with the perpendicular field component only.

3. **The empirical model (Eq. 1) does not uniquely establish the Coulomb-disorder interpretation.**
   - Eq. 1 has α, β, γ, δ and ρ0 free per sample, plus a crossover Fermi function with Hc = 9.6 T and w = 1.5 T fixed globally. α is "determined from a fit, often matching the size of the lump-like feature" (p. 4), so the QQHE term is partly tuned by eye.
   - The fits are shown only as gray dashed curves on half of each panel (Fig. 4a–d). No residuals, parameter uncertainties or parameter correlations are given. Only four samples are fitted, and Sample E is excluded by construction.
   - The Fermi-function crossover between the H² and H log(δ√H) regimes is ad hoc. Its centre Hc ≈ 9.6 T is not linked to any physical scale, such as the quantum-limit field of each sample or the field at which the magnetic length equals the disorder correlation length. Yet in the Rodionov et al. theory that crossover should depend on n and eV0.
   - To claim that the background is strong-Coulomb-disorder MR, the authors should show that:
     - (i) the fitted β, γ and δ are consistent in magnitude with the theory of Ref. 25, using each sample's independently estimated eV0 and mobility;
     - (ii) alternative backgrounds (classical two-band MR, guiding-centre linear MR of Ref. 24, or a simple polynomial) fit worse with the same number of parameters;
     - (iii) Hc and w, if left free, either stay constant or follow the expected scaling.

   The trend of β and γ decreasing with n (Fig. 4l) could also reflect the change in mobility and Fermi surface size alone. It is not independent evidence of a strong-to-weak disorder crossover.

4. **The disorder-strength estimates rest on an assumption that conflicts with how the density is varied.** Figs. 2b and 4m place each sample relative to eV0 = EF by "assuming an average concentration of charged point defects" (~10^18 cm^-3, Fig. 2 caption), the same for every sample. In Cd3As2, however, the electron density is set by native donors (Cd interstitials and As vacancies, with Cd vacancies acting as acceptors; Refs. 21, 62). Samples with n ranging from 2.9×10^16 to 2.3×10^17 cm^-3 must therefore differ in their defect populations or compensation. Highly compensated films with low n and a high total charged-defect density would sit much further into the strong-disorder regime than uncompensated ones. The paper's central quantitative picture (Fig. 4m: "Cd3As2 crosses into a regime of strong Coulomb disorder") is only as good as this assumption. The authors should:
   - state how the sample densities were varied (growth conditions or post-growth treatment);
   - give an independent estimate of N_imp for each sample, for example from the transport lifetime or mobility through Coulomb-scattering theory, from the Dingle temperature or quantum lifetime, or from defect spectroscopy;
   - show how the sample positions in Fig. 4m move over a plausible range of N_imp.

   The non-monotonic mobility in Fig. 2c should also be explained within the picture: A has 1.10, B 0.67 and C 1.52 ×10^4 cm²/Vs. This trend is not the monotonic increase in disorder with decreasing n that the model implies, so it suggests sample-to-sample defect variation that is not captured by the shared defect concentration.

B. Should address

5. **Cross-sample comparisons are made at different temperatures.** Fig. 3 data are at 2 K, Fig. 4 data at 5 K, except Sample D at 0.4 K (Fig. 4 caption). Fig. 4l compares β and γ across samples, with Sample D measured at an order of magnitude lower temperature. The paper itself shows that the features fade with temperature (Supplementary Note 4). The authors should refit all samples at a common temperature (or show that the parameters are temperature-independent between 0.4 and 5 K), and say which temperature the densities in Fig. 2c refer to (n_2K).

6. **Theory and experiment are compared only qualitatively.** The calculation in Fig. 1e,f is done at a single EF ≈ 20 meV with a phenomenological broadening η ("weak" and "moderate" disorder). The caption admits that it does not include LB broadening in the DOS, and it is not a model of screened Coulomb disorder. To connect theory and data, the authors should:
   - compute ρxx(H) and ρyx(H) at each sample's measured EF (Fig. 2c);
   - state the g-factor, k·p parameters and η used;
   - overlay the predicted step fields on the measured curves.

   The text states that "our theoretical calculations when including moderate disorder reflect this" (p. 4), but which features of Fig. 1f are matched in the data, and how well, is not shown. If the calculation is to support the Sample D plateau value, the main text should give the predicted and measured numbers and their uncertainty, including the uncertainty from film thickness and Hall-bar geometry in converting resistance to resistivity.

7. **The 3D character of the Fermi surface is not shown for the samples that carry the claim.** The text says the authors "are able to verify a 3D Fermi surface in films of a slightly higher carrier density (Supplementary Note 6)" (p. 4). Excluding a 2D or confined-film origin (Discussion, p. 6) requires the 3D character in Samples A–E themselves. The authors should show angle-dependent oscillation frequencies or kink fields for at least one of Samples A–D. Otherwise the claim should be narrowed.

8. **One device per density, with no reproducibility data.** Each density is represented by one film and apparently one Hall bar. Thickness also varies non-monotonically with density (440, 210, 560, 350, 380, 350 nm for A–F), so density and thickness are confounded. The authors should report whether the kink field and the lump are reproduced in a second Hall bar from the same film and after thermal cycling. They should also state the ρxx/ρyx symmetrisation procedure. Contact misalignment in a 2 mm × 0.56 mm bar with huge MR (FMR ≈ 900%) can mix a large ρxx into ρyx and produce kinks. The Fig. 4 left/right split display (raw data for H < 0, fits for H > 0) hides whether the data are field-symmetric.

9. **The carrier-density and Fermi-energy extraction is not robust for non-linear Hall data.** n is taken from the ρyx slope below 1 T (Methods), and EF from n using the DOS (Fig. 2c). For Samples A–C the Hall response is visibly non-linear, and the low-field slope depends on the field window if a second carrier is present (see concern 2). The authors should:
   - give uncertainties on n and EF;
   - cross-check n against SdH frequencies where oscillations exist;
   - propagate these uncertainties into eV0/EF.

   The classification into weak- and strong-disorder regimes in Figs. 2b and 4m depends on these numbers.

10. **The temperature dependence is not used quantitatively.** The fading of the ρxx lump and the ρyx kink with temperature is described only qualitatively (p. 3; Supplementary Note 4). For a Landau-band origin, the amplitude should follow a Lifshitz–Kosevich-like thermal damping with a cyclotron mass consistent with the measured EF and Dirac velocity. Showing this would separate a Landau-quantization feature from a multiband or freeze-out effect.

11. **The comparison with ZrTe5/HfTe5 is speculative.** The Discussion attributes cleaner QQHE in the pentatellurides to a larger dielectric constant and lower charged-defect density, placing them at eV0 of "a few meV" in Fig. 4m. The inputs used for these points (defect density, ε, EF) should be tabulated with their sources, along with an uncertainty range. Otherwise this part of Fig. 4m should be presented as illustrative.

C. Minor

12. On p. 6 the text says "as shown in Fig. 4j" for the pentatelluride eV0 estimate. The relevant panel is Fig. 4m.
13. The Fig. 2b caption says the circles are estimates for Samples A–E, but Fig. 2c lists Samples A–F. State whether F is plotted and where.
14. Eq. 1 writes the second crossover function as f(H, Hc, −w). Define this explicitly. Also state the units of δ, the dimensions of α, and whether ρ0 is fixed to the measured zero-field value.
15. Fig. 4l: the arrows that assign β and γ to the left and right axes are easy to miss. Add error bars (from the fits) to the four points.
16. Fig. 4f inset: define ρxy^FP and state whether the expression shown is evaluated with the measured σxx.
17. Fig. 4k: give the measured plateau value with its uncertainty in the main text, and the predicted value from Supplementary Note 7.
18. Fig. 3: state the temperatures for each trace in the caption (the legend lists 2, 20, 40, 80 K). The arrows in Fig. 3d,e should be given a field value in the text.
19. Methods: specify whether the Fig. 4 data come from the PPMS AC/DC setup or the 30 T resistive magnet, and the excitation currents used (Joule heating at 0.4 K).
20. Data availability "upon reasonable request" is weak for a study whose central conclusions rest on fits. Consider depositing the raw ρxx(H) and ρyx(H) data and the fit code.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a Landau-index analysis for each sample, with predicted depopulation fields and plateau values from the measured EF, overlaid on ρyx(H) and dρxx/dH. This turns "consistent with QQHE" into a quantitative test.
2. Measure tilted-field dependence (and, if possible, use the thickness series) to separate bulk 3D QQHE from surface, interface or buffer channels. Show an explicit two-carrier fit to rule out multiband transport.
3. Constrain the charged-defect density independently for each sample (quantum versus transport lifetime, compensation analysis), and recompute eV0/EF with uncertainties, so that Fig. 4m is data-driven rather than assumption-driven.
4. Benchmark Eq. 1 against alternative background models with matched parameter counts. Report fit uncertainties and residuals, and relate Hc and the fitted β, γ and δ to the theory of Ref. 25 using independently known parameters.
