Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors measure magnetotransport in MBE-grown Cd3As2 films with electron densities of 2.9×10^16 to 2.3×10^17 cm^-3. They interpret a step-like feature in ρyx(H), and a coincident lump in ρxx(H), as signatures of the 3D quasi-quantum Hall effect (QQHE). They report a Hall plateau near the expected quantum-limit value for one sample (Sample D, 19.5–24 T, 0.4 K). They then model ρxx(H) as a QQHE term (α H ∂Hρyx) plus a phenomenological strong-Coulomb-disorder background, and argue that charged-defect disorder increasingly obscures the QQHE as the density is lowered. If the argument holds, the picture that low density both enables the QQHE and spoils it through weaker screening would be a useful framework for 3D QQHE searches in Dirac semimetals. Its novelty rests on two links that are currently weakly supported: that the observed features are specifically QQHE, and that the background is specifically Coulomb disorder.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A family of films spanning almost an order of magnitude in carrier density, all from the same growth platform, giving a systematic density series in a material where low densities are hard to reach.
- High-field data (Sample D to 31.5 T) showing saturation of ρyx in the quantum limit at a value close to a calculated ρyx^FP.
- A k·p Landau-band calculation of σxx and σxy in Cd3As2 (Fig. 1e,f; Methods) that shows how disorder broadening rounds QQHE features.
- A physically motivated link to charged-defect screening (Fig. 2) that could explain why QQHE in Cd3As2 is less clean than in ZrTe5/HfTe5.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **A ρyx plateau with ρxx ≫ ρyx does not establish quasi-quantized σxy, and other quantum-limit mechanisms are not excluded (Fig. 4k, Results p. 5, Supplementary Note 7).**
   - In Fig. 4k, at 20–30 T, ρxx rises to roughly 600–800 mΩ cm, while ρyx sits near the claimed ρyx^FP ≈ 155 mΩ cm. So ρxx/ρyx ≈ 4–5, i.e. tan θH ≪ 1.
   - The QQHE is a statement about σxy ∝ (e²/h) k_F,z,0/π. The identification ρyx = πh/(e² k_F,z,0) holds only when ρxy ≈ 1/σxy, i.e. σxx ≪ σxy. The Methods state this condition explicitly ("In the weak-disorder case, we always find σxy ≫ σxx"), and the Introduction says Coulomb scattering "spoil[s] the approximation ρxy ≈ σxy^-1". By the authors' own reasoning, a ρyx plateau in Sample D at these fields is therefore not evidence of σxy quasi-quantization.
   - A rapidly rising ρxx together with a flattening ρyx in the quantum limit is also the textbook behaviour of magnetic freeze-out or field-induced localization, which the authors themselves cite for ZrTe5 (ref. 38). It can also come from the onset of two-carrier conduction (electron–hole puddles, the buffer layer) at high field.
   - The "excellent agreement" with ρyx^FP also depends on k_F,z,0. This is taken from the k·p model at the Hall density (Supplementary Note 7) and comes with no uncertainty.
   - What to do:
     - Invert the tensor and show σxy(H) and σxx(H) for Sample D up to 31.5 T, and test whether σxy, not ρyx, plateaus at (e²/h) k_F,z,0/π.
     - Give the uncertainty on k_F,z,0 (from the n uncertainty and the Fermi-surface anisotropy) and hence on the 155 mΩ cm value.
     - Show the temperature dependence of the high-field ρxx to separate a freeze-out or insulating upturn from a QQHE plateau.
     - Report whether the plateau appears in more than one device or cooldown.

2. **The sub-14 T kink in ρyx and lump in ρxx are not shown to be specifically QQHE rather than a generic last-Landau-band crossing, two-carrier transport or tensor-component mixing (Figs. 3d–f, 4a–j; Results pp. 3–5).**
   - The central experimental signature for Samples A–E is "the step-like slope change in ρyx(H) visible in all Samples A–E below 14 T", read as "a broadened plateau at higher filling factor". Several points are missing:
     - No filling index is assigned to these features.
     - Their field positions are not compared with the Landau-band crossing fields calculated for each sample's n.
     - Their positions are not shown to scale with n as the 3D model predicts.
   - The authors themselves say the QQHE "evolve[s] from standard Shubnikov–de Haas oscillations". Without a quantitative criterion, the feature cannot be told apart from the final SdH extremum.
   - The multiband explanation is dismissed on qualitative grounds: non-monotonic FMR and a sharp Hall slope change "are not typical". This sits awkwardly with the paper's own picture. When eV0 ≳ EF (Sample A has eV0/EF ≈ 1 in Fig. 2b), charge puddles imply coexisting electron and hole regions, i.e. inherently multi-carrier transport.
   - The ZnxCd1−xTe buffer and the film surfaces are possible parallel channels that are not discussed.
   - The coincidence between the ρyx kink and the ρxx lump is also exactly what an imperfectly antisymmetrized ρyx would show, since ρxx ≫ ρyx here. The Methods do not say whether ρyx was antisymmetrized and ρxx symmetrized in ±H. The left-half "raw data" presentation in Fig. 4a–e suggests ρxx was not symmetrized.
   - What to do:
     - State the (anti)symmetrization procedure.
     - Index each feature against the calculated Landau-band crossings for the measured n of each sample (a table of predicted against observed fields).
     - Perform a two-carrier fit of ρxx and ρyx for at least Samples A–C, showing it fails.
     - Ideally, measure the feature's dependence on field angle (see concern 4).

3. **The Coulomb-disorder strength is never measured, and the sample regimes inferred from it contradict the model applied (Figs. 2b, 4l, 4m; Supplementary Note 5).**
   - eV0 is calculated from an assumed charged-defect concentration of about 10^18 cm^-3, taken to be the same in all films ("assuming an average concentration of charged point defects"). The paper's conclusion is that larger eV0 at lower n obscures the QQHE, but no measured quantity tracks eV0 or the Landau-band broadening independently. Examples would be a Dingle analysis of the quantum lifetime, the thermal smearing of the features, or the transport-to-quantum lifetime ratio.
   - Fig. 2b and Fig. 4m place only Sample A near eV0 ≈ EF; Samples B–D lie below the eV0 = EF line (eV0/EF ≈ 0.6–0.9). Yet Eq. (1) is described as valid "in the limit of strong Coulomb disorder" and is applied to A–D. Meanwhile E, with a similar eV0/EF ≈ 0.5, is excluded as "weak Coulomb disorder".
   - The trend of β and γ with n in Fig. 4l is also confounded:
     - The low-field H² term is generic classical MR, scaling as ρ0(μH)². The mobilities in Fig. 2c are non-monotonic in n (1.10, 0.67, 1.52, 1.01 ×10^4 cm²/Vs for A–D), and ρ0 differs between samples.
     - The film thicknesses differ (440, 210, 560, 350 nm for A–D).
     - Sample D's ρxx in Fig. 4d is at 0.4 K, while the others are at 5 K.
   - What to do:
     - Extract a quantum or Dingle scattering time, or the temperature scale over which the features fade (Supplementary Note 4), for each sample, and show that it tracks the calculated eV0/EF.
     - Show that β is not simply explained by ρ0μ².
     - Reconcile the regime assignment of Fig. 4m with the use of the strong-disorder expression. Either revise the defect concentration with independent evidence (e.g. from ref. 21 or 62) or restate Eq. (1) as purely empirical.

4. **The 3D (bulk) nature of the QQHE is assumed from thickness > 100 nm, but at these densities the films may host only a few quantized subbands (Discussion p. 6; Methods "Film growth").**
   - The paper distinguishes its result from the 2D QHE and Weyl-orbit physics seen in thinner Cd3As2 by stating that all films are "well within the bulk-like thickness regime (>100 nm)". At n ≈ 3–6×10^16 cm^-3, however, EF ≈ 20–28 meV (Fig. 2c).
   - The confinement subband spacing πħv/d with v ~ 10^6 m/s is about 10 meV for Sample B (210 nm) and about 6 meV for 350 nm (Samples D, F). These are a significant fraction of EF, so only a few kz subbands are occupied and the system is closer to quasi-2D than the k·p bulk model assumes.
   - The bulk-like Fermi-surface evidence (Supplementary Note 6) comes from a film "of a slightly higher carrier density", not from the samples showing the features.
   - Surface states, which the paper notes are predicted to accompany 3D QQHE, and Weyl-orbit contributions are excluded only by argument.
   - What to do:
     - Measure the field-angle dependence of the ρyx kink and ρxx lump in at least one low-density sample (A–C). A 3D ellipsoidal Fermi surface and 2D subbands or surface states predict distinct angular scaling.
     - Estimate the subband spacing against EF and Landau-band broadening for each film.
     - Explain why sample thickness (210–560 nm) has no visible effect in the series.

**B. Should address**

5. **The theoretical QQHE plateaus require a fixed chemical potential; for fixed density they disappear (Methods p. 7, Fig. 1e,f).**
   - The Methods state that "for fixed charge density calculations, the 3D QQHE plateaus disappear and one recovers the (quasi)classical … dependence". Fig. 1e,f, which motivates the whole interpretation, uses EF fixed at 20 meV.
   - Whether the films are closer to fixed μ or fixed n therefore decides whether QQHE plateaus are expected at all. Pinning by a reservoir such as defect states, the buffer or the surfaces is the 3D analogue of the localized-state reservoir in the 2D QHE, and the paper does not justify it.
   - The authors should give the physical reservoir that pins μ in their films and its expected capacity. They should also show the fixed-n calculation alongside Fig. 1e,f in the main text, so readers can judge which matches the data.

6. **Eq. (1) has many parameters, some fixed by hand, and its fit quality is not quantified (p. 5, Fig. 4a–d, Supplementary Notes 2–3).**
   - The model has seven parameters: ρ0, α, β, γ, δ, Hc and w. Hc = 9.6 T and w = 1.5 T are fixed globally, although the crossover field from H² to H log(δ√H) should physically depend on eV0 and EF, which vary between samples.
   - α "is determined from a fit, often matching the size of the lump-like feature", so the QQHE share is partly set by eye.
   - The fits are shown only on the positive-field half, without residuals or parameter uncertainties. Fig. 4l has no error bars.
   - The authors should:
     - fit with Hc and w free, or justify their sample-independence;
     - report the parameter uncertainties and correlations, especially between α and β/γ;
     - show residuals;
     - show that the conclusion "the QQHE component comprises a larger portion … with increasing carrier density" survives these changes.

7. **The density is varied across different films rather than within one sample, with one device per density and mismatched temperatures (Figs. 2c, 3, 4; Methods).**
   - Each density is a different film with a different thickness. Defect concentration, strain and buffer quality may also vary from film to film, yet the analysis assumes a common defect concentration.
   - No second device from the same film, and no thermal cycle, is shown.
   - Fig. 3 data are at 2 K, Fig. 4a–j at 5 K, and Fig. 4d and 4k (Sample D) at 0.4 K. The Fig. 4 series and the Fig. 4l trend therefore mix temperatures.
   - The authors should present all samples at a common temperature in Fig. 4 and include at least one repeat device. They should also discuss growth-to-growth variation in defect density, e.g. by using Hall data or growth conditions to argue the defect concentration is constant.

8. **The Hall carrier density, and hence EF and eV0/EF, assumes single-band transport (Methods; Fig. 2c).**
   - n is "extracted from the low field (μ0H < 1 T) slopes of ρyx". If parallel conduction or electron–hole puddles contribute, which is expected near eV0 ≈ EF, the Hall slope misestimates the electron density. That error propagates into EF, k_F,z,0, ρyx^FP and the regime map of Fig. 4m.
   - Where available, the authors should cross-check n against quantum-oscillation frequencies. They should also quantify how a plausible parallel channel would shift the Fig. 2c values and the 155 mΩ cm prediction.

9. **The comparison with the pentatellurides in Fig. 4m is an estimate, not a measurement (Discussion p. 6).**
   - The statement that ZrTe5/HfTe5 sit at eV0 of "a few meV" rests on an assumed larger dielectric constant and lower defect density, not on calculations or data shown.
   - Because this comparison carries the paper's broader claim that Coulomb disorder is why clean QQHE appears in pentatellurides but not in Cd3As2, the authors should give the inputs (ε, defect concentration, EF) used for those points and their ranges, or present the comparison as qualitative.

10. **The data supporting the strong-disorder interpretation are mostly in the Supplementary Notes, which are not available for this review.**
    - Key evidence is referred out: the temperature dependence (Note 4), the eV0 calculation (Note 5), the Fermi-surface dimensionality (Note 6) and the ρyx^FP calculation (Note 7).
    - At minimum, the authors should bring two pieces into the main text: the temperature dependence of the Sample C feature, and the comparison of predicted and observed fields and plateau values for each sample.

**C. Minor**

11. Fig. 4k: the dashed line labelled "ρyx^FP = 155 mΩ cm" is drawn on the panel without making clear which axis it refers to. The ρyx axis is inverted (0 to −300). Please mark the plateau on the ρyx axis and state the sign convention.
12. Fig. 1e is labelled "Ec ~ 20 meV", while the caption and text say the chemical potential/EF is 20 meV. ρyx^FP is written as πh/(e²k_F,z,0) in the text and as (h/e²)(π/k_F,z,0) in Fig. 1e. Please unify the notation.
13. The text (p. 5) calls the Eq. (1) fits "gray dashed lines", while the Fig. 4 caption says "gray, dotted". Please make these consistent.
14. Fig. 4l: the units of β (mΩ cm T^-2) and γ (mΩ cm T^-1) should be checked against Eq. (1), where γ multiplies H log(δ√H). Please give δ's per-sample values in the main text, not only "δ ~ 0.32 T^-1/2".
15. Methods: please state the excitation current and verify the absence of self-heating at 0.4 K in the resistive magnet. Also say whether the Sample D 0.4 K data come from the same device and cooldown as Fig. 4d.
16. The "first time" and "first calculations of this nature" claims (p. 2–3) should be qualified or supported with a brief comparison to prior k·p Landau-level calculations for Cd3As2 (e.g. refs. 46–48).
17. Fig. 3d: the FMR curves appear slightly asymmetric in ±H. If they are unsymmetrized, please say so; if symmetrized, the residual asymmetry should be explained.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Present σxy(H) and σxx(H), not just ρyx and ρxx, for all samples. Test quasi-quantization against (e²/h) k_F,z,0/π, with an error band from the uncertainty in n.
2. Provide an independent measure of disorder broadening (Dingle or quantum lifetime, or the activation and thermal-smearing scale of the features) for each sample, and correlate it with the calculated eV0/EF. This would turn the Coulomb-disorder hypothesis into a test.
3. Field-angle-dependent measurements on one low-density film would settle the 3D-versus-quasi-2D (subband or surface) origin of the features.
4. Electrostatic gating of a single film, even over a modest range, would remove the film-to-film confounds (thickness, defect density, mobility) from the density dependence that is central to the paper's argument.
