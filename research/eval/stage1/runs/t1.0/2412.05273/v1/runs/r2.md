Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors grow (001) Cd3As2 thin films by MBE with low-temperature carrier densities of about 2.9–23×10^16 cm^-3. They report a kink in ρxy (at H_S ≈ 3.5 T in Sample C) together with a shoulder or lump in ρxx, and read this as a quasi-quantum Hall effect (QQHE) sitting on a magnetoresistance background. They attribute the background to strong Coulomb disorder from charged point defects, and they model ρxx empirically (Eq. 1) as a QQHE term αH∂Hρxy plus Coulomb-disorder terms βH² and γH log(δ√H). Linking the visibility of 3D quantum-limit transport to the ratio eV0/E_F is a useful idea for the TSM community, and a Fermi level tunable by growth is a real asset. In its current form, though, the identification of the QQHE and the attribution of the background to Coulomb disorder are both inferred rather than shown.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- One film platform covers a range of low carrier densities (Fig. 1e), so trends can be compared under nominally similar growth conditions.
- The changes with density are systematic and striking: linear MR in Sample E, and quadratic-to-super-quadratic MR with a Hall kink at lower n (Figs. 2 and 3).
- The paper frames a physically sensible tension. Lowering E_F brings the quantum limit within reach, but it also weakens screening of charged defects. This gives the field a concrete design guideline.
- A k·p Landau-band calculation (Fig. 1b, Methods) gives a microscopic reference for what disorder-free QQHE features should look like in Cd3As2.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The QQHE identification lacks a quantitative test.** The QQHE claim (Abstract; Summary: "we have observed evidence of the 3D QQHE") rests on a change of slope in ρxy at H_S (Fig. 2e, Fig. 3e–h) that coincides with features in ρxx. Nothing in the paper connects these features quantitatively to Landau-level physics in the specific samples. Three things are needed:
   - (i) The plateau or kink value of ρxy (or σxy) compared with the QQHE expectation σxy ≈ (e²/h)(k_F,z/π), or with the Fermi wavevector obtained from n. This normalization is the standard test used for ZrTe5 and HfTe5 (Refs. 7, 10, 15).
   - (ii) The observed kink fields for Samples A–D compared with the computed field of the last Landau-band crossing (or the quantum-limit field) for each n and E_F, using the same k·p model as Fig. 1b. A rough check suggests the kink fields might scale plausibly: about 3.5 T for C and about 5.7 T for D, a ratio of 1.6 against (n_D/n_C)^{2/3} ≈ 1.4. The authors should show this scaling explicitly across all samples, with uncertainties.
   - (iii) A direct overlay of the simulated ρxy and ρxx for each sample's E_F against the data. Fig. 1b is computed only for E_F = 20 meV and is never compared with any measured trace.

   Without these, the kink could be any field-dependent change in transport, and the central claim stays qualitative.

2. **The Coulomb-disorder attribution has no independent measurement, and the paper's own regime assignment is inconsistent.** The background is attributed to strong Coulomb disorder (eV0 > E_F). However, no quantity tied to the disorder is measured: not the charged-defect density N_i, not the compensation ratio, and not a ratio of quantum to transport lifetime.
   - eV0 in Figs. 1d and 3j is computed from Ref. 9 with assumed parameters that the main text never states (N_i, dielectric constant, velocity).
   - Reading Fig. 3j and Fig. 1d, only Sample A sits clearly at eV0/E_F > 1. Sample B is about at 1. Sample C (E_F = 27.4 meV, eV0 ≈ 21 meV) sits at about 0.8, on the weak-disorder side of the dashed line.
   - Even so, the strong-disorder terms of Eq. 1 are applied to Samples A–C, and the text describes a crossover into strong disorder across these samples. Samples D and E (ratios about 0.6 and 0.35) are called weak.
   - The abstract says the authors "tune the defect concentrations", but no defect characterization is presented.
   - Mobility does not track the proposed disorder ordering (Fig. 1e): B has μ = 0.67 compared with 1.10 for A and 1.52 for C.

   The authors should state N_i and the other inputs for each sample and show how sensitive eV0 is to them. They should reconcile the regime assignment with their own Figs. 1d and 3j, and ideally give an independent disorder measure. Options include the Dingle temperature or quantum mobility from whatever oscillations exist in Sample E or D (the quantum-to-transport lifetime ratio is a recognized fingerprint of long-range Coulomb disorder), or a defect-density estimate from growth or structural characterization.

3. **The main alternative explanations for the Hall kink in thin films are not excluded.** The paper argues against multiband transport, Berry curvature and localization (p. 3), but it does not address the possibilities most specific to 200–560 nm Cd3As2 films:
   - (a) Topological surface states or Weyl-orbit transport, which give quantized Hall features in Cd3As2 films (Refs. 30, 31).
   - (b) Finite-thickness subband quantization. The Fermi wavelength 2π/k_F is a few tens of nm at these densities, so a 200 nm film is only a few wavelengths thick.
   - (c) Parallel conduction through the ZnxTe1−x buffer or interface layers, which would make the "single band" argument fail.
   - (d) Admixture of ρxx into ρxy from contact misalignment. Because ρxx has a sharp shoulder at H_S, any admixture produces a correlated kink in ρxy. This is exactly the coincidence used as evidence (Fig. 2d,e).

   The Methods do not say whether ρxy was antisymmetrized or ρxx symmetrized in field. The authors should state and show this, report each sample's thickness, and test whether H_S and the kink amplitude scale with thickness (or with the 3D density) as bulk QQHE requires. They should also rule out parallel conduction in the buffer, for example with a buffer-only Hall measurement.

B. Should address

4. **The Eq. 1 fit has many free parameters and does not discriminate between models.**
   - Per sample, α, β, γ and δ are free (four parameters), on top of a global Fermi-function crossover with H_c = 9.6 T and w = 1.5 T fixed by hand. These are fitted to smooth monotonic curves (Fig. 3a–c, gray), and "good agreement" with that freedom is weak evidence.
   - The H² low-field term is generic: classical orbital MR, two-carrier MR and Parish–Littlewood mobility-fluctuation MR all give it.
   - The crossover field should depend on disorder strength and E_F (Ref. 29), so fixing H_c globally across samples with different eV0/E_F works against the claimed physics.

   The authors should do four things. First, report parameter uncertainties and correlations; Fig. 3i has no error bars. Second, show that the fitted β, γ and δ agree, at least in magnitude, with the values Ref. 29 predicts from the computed eV0 and the material parameters. Third, show fits with H_c and w free for each sample. Fourth, compare against at least one alternative background model, such as classical mobility-fluctuation MR or the guiding-center diffusion mechanism of Ref. 17, using a goodness-of-fit criterion.

5. **The relation ρxx ∝ H∂Hρxy is not specific to the QHE.** The QQHE term αH∂Hρxy (Eq. 1, Fig. 3a–d light traces) uses the measured ρxy, which itself contains the non-QQHE background. Empirically, the relation holds generally in inhomogeneous conductors and follows from density fluctuations (Simon–Halperin-type arguments). That it works well for Sample D is therefore not independent evidence of the QQHE. The authors should say what in this decomposition is unique to Landau-level physics. They should also explain why α (0.17–0.35) takes the values it does, for example relating it to density inhomogeneity, and check whether α correlates with the computed disorder.

6. **The E_F estimate and the validity of the disorder model near the Lifshitz point are not justified.**
   - E_F is obtained from n using a DOS (Fig. 1e; Supplementary Note 1 is not available in this submission). The tabulated values rise faster than the n^{1/3} expected for a Dirac cone. Relative to A, the predicted values would be about 30 meV for D (33.7 tabulated) and about 41 meV for E (50.5 tabulated).
   - For Cd3As2, energies of 20–50 meV are near the scale of the Lifshitz transition between the two Dirac cones, and the velocity is strongly anisotropic. The isotropic single-Dirac-cone screening model of Ref. 9 may therefore not apply quantitatively.

   Because eV0/E_F is the paper's organizing variable, the authors should give the dispersion and degeneracy used, an uncertainty on E_F, and a discussion of how anisotropy and the two-node structure change eV0.

7. **There is one film per density, sample metadata are missing, and reproducibility is not shown.** Each carrier density is one film, and the thicknesses (200–560 nm) are not assigned to samples. Hall-bar dimensions and contact geometry are also not given. The trend in Fig. 3i rests on three points (A–C). The authors should give a per-sample table (thickness, n, μ, ρ0, growth parameters), show a second device or film near one density to establish spread, and state whether the features survive thermal cycling.

8. **The temperature dependence is not analysed, and the measurement temperatures are inconsistent.**
   - The text says remnants of the H_S feature persist up to 80 K (Fig. 2d–f). Whether that is consistent with the Landau-band spacing at H_S, compared with thermal broadening, is never discussed. Persistence to 80 K could also point to a classical origin.
   - Fig. 3 is at 5 K, while Fig. 2, Fig. 1e and the densities n_2K are at 2 K. The fits are shown at only one temperature.

   The authors should fit Eq. 1 at several temperatures and show that α (QQHE) and β/γ (disorder) behave as their physical assignments require. For example, the QQHE term should be thermally suppressed on the Landau-level energy scale, while the disorder terms should follow the temperature dependence of screening.

9. **The pentatelluride comparison is asserted rather than computed.** Fig. 3j places ZrTe5 and HfTe5 at an eV0 of "a few meV", and the Discussion attributes cleaner QQHE in flux-grown ZrTe5 to weaker Coulomb disorder. No input values (N_i, dielectric constant, velocity, E_F) or calculation are given. The authors should give the parameters and Ref. 9 estimates for each cited pentatelluride sample, or present the comparison as speculation.

10. **Supporting data described in the text are not shown.** On p. 4 the authors state that "magnetotransport at different fixed magnetic field angles are consistent with a 3D Fermi surface". These data are needed to support a 3D (rather than surface or quasi-2D) origin of the features, and they should be shown, at minimum as the angle dependence of H_S. Likewise, the quantum oscillations in Sample E (Fig. 2a–c) could give k_F and the quantum lifetime directly, but they are not analysed.

C. Minor

11. **Fig. 2a label.** The in-panel density label for Sample E should be checked against the caption and Fig. 1e (2.3×10^17 cm^-3). The units in the header of Fig. 1e's carrier-density column should likewise be checked against the text (2.9×10^16 cm^-3 for Sample A).
12. **Fig. 3i.** The figure lacks error bars, and the solid and dashed lines are not explained. The paper also reports only δ ≈ 0.32 T^-1/2 without an uncertainty or any comparison with the δ expected from Ref. 29.
13. **Fig. 1b.** The ρxx bump trace is magnified ×250 relative to the disorder trace. The authors should state explicitly that the disorder-free QQHE ρxx predicted by the model is orders of magnitude smaller than the measured lumps, and explain how the experimental lump can then be "large compared to the non-QQHE background" in Sample D. The method used to add disorder to the simulation should be summarized in the main text.
14. **Overclaiming.** "Our results represent the first study of its kind" (Discussion) and "Our observations serve to confirm the existence of QQHE" (Introduction) go beyond what is shown, given Refs. 30–32 and the qualitative nature of the identification. Similarly, the abstract's statement that "a zero resistivity state is obscured" implies that a zero-resistivity state would otherwise be expected. That expectation is not established for 3D QQHE (see Ref. 8).
15. **Typos and wording.**
    - Abstract: "tunability in the Fermi has" should read "tunability of the Fermi level has".
    - p. 3: "mangetotransport".
    - p. 6: "rules the roost" is informal.
    - Methods: "115 C" should read "115 °C".
    - Fig. 1e: the mobility units "(10^4 cm²/Vs)" should be checked.
    - Methods: "8×8" Hamiltonian, but the text then says "(8×(n_LB^max+1))^2", which reads as a size squared. Clarify.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a figure that normalizes σxy by e²/h·(k_F/π) for all samples, with the calculated Landau-band crossing fields marked on the ρxy and ρxx data for Samples A–D. This would make the QQHE case quantitative.
2. Measure the dependence on film thickness and on field angle for one low-density composition. Together with field-antisymmetrized Hall data, this would separate bulk QQHE from surface, Weyl-orbit, subband and contact-admixture mechanisms.
3. Use the gating the authors propose in the Discussion, on a single film, to sweep E_F at fixed defect density. Disorder strength and Fermi level would then be varied independently of growth, which removes the confound of sample-to-sample differences.
4. Give an independent measure of Coulomb disorder, such as the quantum-to-transport lifetime ratio from oscillations, the compensation estimated from growth stoichiometry, or local probes. Use it to test the predicted eV0 values in Figs. 1d and 3j, rather than relying on calculated eV0 alone.
