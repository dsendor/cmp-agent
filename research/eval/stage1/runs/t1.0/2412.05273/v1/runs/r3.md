Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports low-field magnetotransport in MBE-grown (001) Cd3As2 films whose electron densities run from about 2.9×10^16 to 2.3×10^17 cm^-3. Defect chemistry sets the density. The authors read a kink or step in ρxy (at H_S ≈ 3.5 T in Sample C, and a lump near 5.7 T in Sample D) as a quasi-quantum Hall (QQHE) signature. They argue that a growing, super-quadratic ρxx background at low density comes from strong Coulomb disorder, and they model it with an empirical form taken from Rodionov et al. Linking the visibility of 3D QQHE features to charged-defect disorder, with the Fermi level set by growth, is a useful and timely idea for the TSM community. As presented, however, both the QQHE identification and the Coulomb-disorder attribution rest mainly on qualitative resemblance and on a phenomenological fit, not on discriminating tests.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A rare thin-film Cd3As2 series that reaches the quantum limit below ~10 T, with densities spread over nearly an order of magnitude. This is a real materials achievement and a useful platform.
- A clear, systematic evolution with density: linear MR with a saturating tan θH at high n (Sample E), giving way to a quadratic, then super-quadratic MR with a kink in ρxy at low n (Figs. 2, 3).
- A physically motivated picture: reducing E_F weakens screening, so lowering density both helps and hurts QQHE observation. If it holds, this is a useful design guideline beyond Cd3As2.
- A k·p Landau-level calculation of σxx and σxy (Fig. 1b, Methods) that gives a theoretical reference for where plateau-like features should appear.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The QQHE identification is not distinguished from ordinary quantum-limit Landau-level physics or from conventional Hall non-linearity.**
   - *What the paper shows:* the QQHE assignment rests on a single kink in ρxy (Fig. 2e, H_S ≈ 3.5 T, Sample C) and a lump in ρxx near 5.7 T (Sample D) that tracks αH∂Hρxy (Fig. 3d).
   - *Why this is not specific:* the paper itself calls QQHE an extension of quantum oscillations (Discussion). The empirical relation ρxx ∝ H∂Hρxy also holds for ordinary SdH oscillations in 2D and 3D, so its success in Fig. 3d does not single out QQHE.
   - *My estimate for Sample C:* assuming fourfold degeneracy and an isotropic Fermi surface, n = 5.8×10^16 cm^-3 gives k_F ≈ 1×10^8 m^-1 and an SdH frequency of ~3 T. The kink at 3.5 T is therefore just where the last Landau-level crossing, i.e. the onset of the quantum limit, would be expected.
   - *What a convincing case needs:*
     - H_S, or the lump field, for every sample (A–D), plotted against n and compared with the Landau-level crossing fields from the k·p model at each sample's own E_F. Fig. 1b is computed only for E_F = 20 meV and is compared with no sample quantitatively.
     - The ρxy plateau values compared with the QQHE expectation that plateau height scales with Fermi wavelength along the field, (h/e²)(λ_F,z/2). The 3D QQHE literature (refs. 7, 10) uses this check.
     - The quoted SdH frequency or frequencies from Sample E, and any oscillations in D, to fix k_F independently of the Hall slope.
     - The tilted-field data the text mentions ("magnetotransport at different fixed magnetic field angles are consistent with a 3D Fermi surface"), which are not shown.
   - *Consequence:* without these, the claims "Our observations serve to confirm the existence of QQHE" and "we have observed evidence of the 3D QQHE" are not supported beyond "a quantum-limit feature consistent with QQHE".

2. **Whether the samples are three-dimensional is not established given the film thicknesses.**
   - *The issue:* the films are 200–560 nm thick (Methods), and which sample has which thickness is not stated.
   - *My estimate:* with v_F ~ 0.5–1×10^6 m/s, the confinement subband spacing ħv_Fπ/d for d = 200 nm is ~5–10 meV. That is comparable to the quoted E_F of 20–34 meV, so only a few subbands would be occupied in the lowest-density films.
   - *Why it matters:* the "3D QQHE" interpretation and the use of a 3D DOS to get E_F (Fig. 1e) both assume bulk 3D states. Quasi-2D subband Landau levels would produce Hall steps by a different mechanism. Surface and Weyl-orbit transport in Cd3As2 films (refs. 30, 31) is also known to give quantum Hall features in this thickness range.
   - *Requested:*
     - Thickness for each sample.
     - An estimate of the subband spacing.
     - Angle-dependent ρxy and ρxx, to show the features scale with total field rather than with its perpendicular component, as expected for 3D versus 2D.
     - Ideally, a comparison of two films with similar n but different thickness.

3. **The Coulomb-disorder origin of the ρxx background is asserted, not tested against alternatives.**
   - *The issue:* the super-quadratic ρxx background (Fig. 3a–c) is attributed to strong Coulomb disorder because it can be fit with Eq. (1). Several other mechanisms predict a density-dependent, super-quadratic MR in the quantum limit and are not ruled out:
     - **Magnetic freeze-out / field-induced metal–insulator behaviour in the ultra-quantum limit.** It is known in low-density ZrTe5 (refs. 7, 24) and would grow sharply as n falls. It predicts an activated temperature dependence of the high-field ρxx.
     - **Parallel conduction.** Candidates are the ZnxTe1−x buffer, the GaAs substrate interface, surface states, or a minority hole or second carrier. The two-band model is dismissed only qualitatively ("nonmonotonicity in the Hall effect of multiband models is typically smooth").
     - **Classical inhomogeneity-driven MR** (Parish–Littlewood type) from density puddles. This is the same physical ingredient but would predict different field and temperature scaling than Rodionov's theory.
   - *Requested:*
     - A simultaneous two-band fit to ρxx(H) and ρxy(H) for Samples A–C, showing that it fails.
     - The temperature dependence of the background (data to 80 K exist for Sample C in Fig. 2d).
     - A comparison of the measured β, γ and δ with Rodionov's predicted magnitudes, using each sample's estimated disorder strength and v_F, rather than only their trend with n.

   One feature of Fig. 2d needs discussion here: the FMR of Sample C appears to grow with increasing temperature (80 K trace highest). This is unusual for a disorder-limited quantum-limit MR and may point to a thermally activated parallel channel.

4. **The weak/strong-disorder classification contradicts the paper's own figures, and eV0 is not measured.**
   - *The contradiction:* the text states that "Samples A–C" are in the strong Coulomb disorder limit (eV0 > E_F) and fits only them with the strong-disorder MR form. Yet in Fig. 3j, Sample C (E_F = 27.4 meV, eV0 ≈ 21 meV) lies clearly below the eV0 = E_F line, and Sample B lies on it. Fig. 1d likewise places C at eV0/E_F ≈ 0.7–0.8.
   - *The deeper problem:* eV0 is not measured. It is computed from Skinner's theory (ref. 9) with parameters given only in the Supplementary Information, including the charged-impurity density N_imp. For compensated defect-doped films, N_imp may be much larger than n (Fig. 1c mentions ~10^18 cm^-3 "in some films"), and eV0 depends strongly on it.
   - *Consequence:* the crossover the paper claims to have tuned through is therefore model-dependent.
   - *Requested:*
     - The N_imp, dielectric constant and v_F used for each sample.
     - An explanation of how N_imp was estimated for each film.
     - Either a consistent treatment of Sample C (weak limit, like D) or a justification for fitting it with the strong-limit form.
     - The uncertainty bands on eV0 in Figs. 1d and 3j.

5. **The fit in Eq. (1) has too many adjustable elements to support the conclusions drawn from it.**
   - *The free elements:* for each of three samples, α, β, γ and δ are free (with ρ0 subtracted). The crossover field H_c = 9.6 T and width w = 1.5 T are fixed globally by hand. α is "chosen to match the size of the lump-like feature" by eye. Only one field polarity is fit.
   - *Mismatch with the theory:* in Rodionov's theory the H² → H log(δ√H) crossover field depends on disorder strength and E_F. A global H_c is therefore inconsistent with a density series in which disorder strength is said to vary.
   - *Requested:*
     - Fits with H_c (and w) free or tied to theory.
     - Parameter uncertainties and correlations, especially between α and β, which both contribute below H_c.
     - Residuals.
     - A test of whether a simpler form (e.g. power law plus αH∂Hρxy) fits equally well.
   - *Consequence:* the decrease of β and γ with n in Fig. 3i (three points, no error bars) is the main quantitative support for the Coulomb-disorder picture, so its robustness needs to be shown.

B. Should address

6. **Carrier densities are quoted inconsistently.**
   - Sample E is 2.3×10^17 cm^-3 in the text and Fig. 2 caption, but the label in Fig. 2a reads 2.3×10^16 cm^-3.
   - The table in Fig. 1e gives n_2K in units of 10^15 cm^-3 (A = 2.9, E = 23.2). This contradicts the abstract and Fig. 3a (A = 2.9×10^16) and the x-axis of Fig. 1d.
   - These are the paper's central control variable, so the authors should correct the units and check every figure.

7. **The Fermi energies in Fig. 1e need justification.**
   - *The discrepancy:* for a fourfold-degenerate Dirac band, E_F ∝ n^(1/3). But E_F(E)/E_F(A) = 2.48, whereas (n_E/n_A)^(1/3) = 2.0.
   - *Absolute values:* with v_F ~ 1×10^6 m/s, n = 2.9×10^16 cm^-3 gives E_F ≈ 50 meV rather than 20 meV.
   - *Requested:* state the DOS and velocity (and anisotropy) used. E_F enters eV0/E_F directly, so this choice moves samples across the strong/weak boundary.

8. **The mobility trend does not obviously fit the screening picture.** The Fig. 1e mobilities are non-monotonic in n: B 0.67, A 1.10, D 1.22, C 1.52, E 2.59 ×10^4 cm²/Vs. If scattering from screened charged defects dominates and strengthens as n drops, a systematic decrease of mobility with n would be expected. The authors should discuss this, together with any differences in thickness, growth conditions or defect density between samples. Sample count is also an issue:
   - Each density point is a single film.
   - Repeatability on a second Hall bar or second film at similar n is not shown.
   - Thermal-cycling reproducibility is not shown.

9. **Temperatures and conditions are mismatched across the analysis.** Densities and E_F are quoted at 2 K (Fig. 1e, Fig. 2), but the fits in Fig. 3 use 5 K data. The authors should either use matched temperatures or show that n, ρ0 and the features do not change between 2 and 5 K. The temperature evolution in Fig. 2d–f (features "visible in data up to 80 K") should also be discussed quantitatively: QQHE features should wash out on the Landau-level-spacing scale, whereas disorder MR should not.

10. **Symmetrization and contact mixing are not described.**
    - *The gap:* Methods do not say whether ρxx was symmetrized and ρxy antisymmetrized in field. Fig. 3a–d shows measured data only for H < 0, with fits for H > 0.
    - *Why it matters:* in Hall bars, mixing of ρxy into ρxx can produce asymmetric lumps of exactly the kind attributed to QQHE.
    - *Requested:* show both field polarities of raw data and state the symmetrization procedure.

11. **The claim that a zero-resistivity state is "obscured" by the background overreaches.**
    - *The issue:* the abstract states that "a zero resistivity state is obscured by a carrier density dependent background".
    - *Why it overreaches:* ρ0 itself is 8–30 mΩ cm. The QQHE term αH∂Hρxy is modelled as an additive contribution to a finite ρxx. Nothing in the analysis shows that removing the background would leave vanishing ρxx. Even the pentatelluride references, with small disorder, show finite ρxx minima.
    - *Requested:* rephrase, or show the background-subtracted ρxx and how close its minima come to zero.

12. **The theory–experiment comparison is qualitative.**
    - *The issue:* Fig. 1b shows simulated ρxy and ρxx for E_F = 20 meV, plus a "disorder" trace, but neither is compared directly with data.
    - *Specific mismatches:*
      - The simulated disorder-free ρxx is multiplied by 250, so its absolute scale is far from the data.
      - How disorder was included in the light-blue trace is deferred to the SI.
      - The simulated step fields for 20 meV are not compared with Sample A (E_F = 20.4 meV), which is the obvious test.
    - *Requested:* overlay the simulation for each sample's E_F on its data, or at least tabulate the predicted and observed feature fields.

C. Minor

13. Much of the essential modelling is deferred to Supplementary Notes 1–3: the disorder model, the parameters in Fig. 1d, the parameter table for Eq. (1), and the regimes of validity. These were not available for this review. The main text should at least state the Skinner-model inputs and the fit parameters with uncertainties.
14. The estimate that pentatellurides have "a disorder potential of a few meV" (Fig. 3j shaded ellipse, Discussion) needs its inputs stated (dielectric constant, N_imp, E_F), since it supports the comparative argument.
15. Refs. 20 and 38 are the same paper (Jeon et al., Nat. Mater. 13, 851).
16. Fig. 3i needs error bars. Its legend for which marker or line corresponds to β and which to γ is conveyed only by arrows.
17. Fig. 1 caption refers to "SI Section 1" while the text uses "Supplementary Note 1"; make the references consistent.
18. Typos and language: "mangetotransport" (p. 3), "rules the roost" (Discussion), ""rounded"" quotation marks (Methods), "115 C". Also, "our results represent the first study of its kind" should be tempered given refs. 30–32.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one figure that tests the QQHE assignment quantitatively. It should plot, for all samples, the observed feature fields and ρxy plateau values against those predicted from the k·p Landau levels at each sample's E_F, with SdH-derived k_F where available.
2. Use the temperature dependence and a simultaneous two-carrier fit to rule out parallel conduction and magnetic freeze-out as the origin of the background. Add angle-dependent data to establish 3D behaviour in films of known thickness.
3. Make the disorder classification self-consistent. Report N_imp, ε and v_F for each sample with uncertainties on eV0/E_F, and fit Eq. (1) only where the strong-disorder condition actually holds, or with a crossover field tied to theory.
4. Strengthen the density-tuning claim with independent defect characterization (e.g. stoichiometry or STM) and a gated or second-sample measurement at fixed defect density. The Discussion already identifies this route as the ideal way to separate E_F from disorder.
