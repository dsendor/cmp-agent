Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors grow (001) Cd3As2 films by MBE with low-field Hall densities of about 2.9-23 x 10^16 cm^-3. At the lowest densities the magnetotransport shows a kink in rho_xy, a shoulder in rho_xx and a super-quadratic high-field magnetoresistance. They read these as a quasi-quantum Hall effect (QQHE) sitting on top of magnetoresistance from strong Coulomb disorder, and they fit them with an empirical expression (Eq. 1) that combines the 2D resistivity scaling rule, alpha*H*dH(rho_xy), with the strong-disorder magnetoresistance forms of Rodionov et al. The topic matters: the 3D QQHE is under active study, and a material whose Fermi level can be tuned is useful. The specific angle here is that lowering E_F increases eV0/E_F, so the disorder background grows. The evidence, however, is phenomenological. The QQHE is identified from a slope change in rho_xy without a quantitative quantization test. The size of the disorder is calculated rather than measured. The decomposition in Eq. 1 is largely set by fitting. Similar Cd3As2 film transport has been reported before (Refs. 30-32).

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A systematic set of films grown on one platform, with the Fermi level tuned by defect chemistry over nearly an order of magnitude in carrier density. This is a genuine experimental capability that bulk crystals do not offer.
- The idea that lowering E_F to reach the quantum limit also weakens screening, and so strengthens Coulomb disorder, is useful and testable. It may help reconcile the very different QQHE quality seen in pentatellurides and in Dirac semimetals.
- The zero-field resistivities agree with the tabulated n and mu (for example, Sample C: 1/(n e mu) is about 7 mOhm cm, matching Fig. 3c). The basic transport characterization is internally consistent.
- The paper is clearly motivated, and the discussion of how strategies for growing 3D QHE materials should handle charged defects is constructive.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The single-carrier analysis that sets n, E_F and eV0/E_F is contradicted by the authors' own Hall data. Methods state that n comes from the rho_xy slope below 1 T, and Fig. 1e converts this n to E_F using a single Dirac pocket. The high-field Hall slopes in Fig. 3e-h are much smaller than that n implies:
   - Sample A (n = 2.9 x 10^16 cm^-3): H/(ne) predicts about -300 mOhm cm at 14 T, but Fig. 3e shows about -100 mOhm cm.
   - Sample B: about -240 predicted, about -95 measured.
   - Sample C: about -150 predicted, about -95 measured (Fig. 2e and Fig. 3g).
   - Sample D: about -94 predicted, about -75 measured.

   The mismatch grows as n falls, reaching roughly a factor of three for Sample A. It points to field-dependent or multiple conduction channels (surface or Weyl-orbit states, buffer or interface conduction, or density inhomogeneity), or to a field-induced shift in density. It cannot be dismissed in a few qualitative sentences, as on p. 3. It also bears directly on the claims. E_F (Fig. 1e), eV0/E_F (Figs. 1d and 3j), the quantum-limit field scales and the "strong disorder" classification all assume one well-defined carrier density. Moreover, mu B is about 1 at 1 T, so the "low-field" slope is not in the weak-field limit.

   The authors should:
   - fit rho_xx(H) and rho_xy(H) jointly with two-carrier (or multicarrier) models and report the result;
   - compare low- and high-field Hall densities for every sample;
   - compare n with the quantum-oscillation frequency where oscillations are visible (Sample E, Fig. 2a);
   - explain why the high-field Hall response departs from H/(ne) if the system has a single pocket in the quantum limit.

2. The QQHE identification is not tested quantitatively. The evidence is a change in rho_xy slope near H_S (Fig. 2e and Fig. 3e-h) and its correlation with a lump in rho_xx. In ZrTe5 and HfTe5 the QQHE was established by comparing plateau values with (h/e^2)(lambda_F,z/2), using the Fermi wavevector from quantum oscillations, and by checking that the plateau fields track the last Landau-level crossing. None of these tests appears here. The authors should:
   - compare the rho_xy values at the step with the expected quasi-quantized value for each sample's density and Fermi-surface anisotropy;
   - plot H_S against n for Samples A-D and compare with the quantum-limit field from the k.p model in Methods. For an isotropic, fourfold-degenerate Dirac pocket, H_S should scale roughly as n^(2/3); a back-of-envelope estimate gives a last-crossing field of about 3 T for Sample C, near the quoted 3.5 T, so this check is easy and informative;
   - show the angle-dependent data that the text says are "consistent with a 3D Fermi surface" (p. 4). The data are not shown, yet they are what separates a bulk 3D QQHE from surface-state or Weyl-orbit quantum Hall transport, which Refs. 30-31 reported in Cd3As2 films;
   - give each sample's thickness (the Methods give a range of 200-560 nm) and test whether the step depends on thickness. Without these checks a two-carrier or surface-channel origin of the Hall kink (concern 1) remains open, and so does the claim (p. 3) that "our observations serve to confirm the existence of QQHE in a 3D gapless Dirac semimetal".

3. The Coulomb-disorder interpretation rests on a calculated disorder strength and an empirical fit. It is not independently tested.
   - eV0 in Figs. 1d and 3j comes from Skinner's theory (Ref. 9). That calculation needs a charged-impurity density and a dielectric constant, which are not given in the main text. The films are tuned by changing defect concentrations, so the impurity density is unlikely to be the same in every sample, and the very low n implies strong compensation (N_i much larger than n). The phrase "As we have hypothesized and shown" (p. 6) is therefore not supported by a measurement.
   - Eq. 1 has free parameters alpha, beta, gamma and delta for each sample. It also uses a crossover field H_c = 9.6 T and width w = 1.5 T, fixed globally with no stated justification. Yet in Ref. 29 the crossover field depends on disorder strength and density, so a sample-independent H_c is inconsistent with the theory being invoked.
   - The trend in beta and gamma (Fig. 3i) rests on three samples. It is not separated from the trivial dependence of classical magnetoresistance on mobility; note that mobility is non-monotonic, with Sample B at 0.67 compared with 1.10 for Sample A.
   - Alternative backgrounds have not been tried: classical inhomogeneity magnetoresistance (Parish-Littlewood) and two-band magnetoresistance both give a quadratic-to-linear or super-quadratic crossover. They should be fitted with the same number of parameters.

   The authors should:
   - provide an independent disorder measure, such as the Dingle temperature or the ratio of quantum to transport mobility from Sample E and Sample D oscillations, or temperature-dependent mobility analysis;
   - state and justify the impurity density and dielectric constant used for eV0, with the spread from plausible ranges shown as error bands in Fig. 3j;
   - compare the fitted beta, gamma and delta quantitatively with the magnitudes Rodionov et al. predict for these n, v and dielectric constant;
   - show that a competing background model fits worse.

B. Should address

4. The QQHE component is not uniquely separated. In Fig. 3a-d, alpha "is chosen to match the size of the lump-like feature in rho_xx" (p. 5), so agreement in amplitude holds by construction. For Samples A-C, alpha is fitted together with the background, so how the signal is split between QQHE and disorder is not unique. The authors should report uncertainties and parameter correlations for alpha, beta, gamma and delta, either here or in the SI table. They should show residuals, and show that the peak positions of alpha*H*dH(rho_xy) match the rho_xx features independently of alpha. They should also state whether rho_xx and rho_xy were symmetrized and antisymmetrized: Fig. 3 shows data for H < 0 and fits for H > 0, and contact misalignment can mix rho_xy into rho_xx near the kink.

5. Temperature conditions and dependence. The carrier densities and Fig. 2 are at 2 K, but the decomposition in Fig. 3 is at 5 K. The text states that remnants of the structure survive to 80 K (p. 3), but the temperature dependence is not analyzed. A Landau-quantization feature should be thermally damped on the scale of the Landau-level spacing and broadening. The disorder background should change in a different way through screening and mobility. Fitting Eq. 1 at several temperatures, with alpha(T) compared against the expected thermal damping, would help separate the two contributions and test the claimed superposition.

6. Number of samples and reproducibility. Each density is represented by a single film and, apparently, a single Hall bar. Film thickness varies from 200 to 560 nm and is not tabulated per sample, and mobility does not vary monotonically with n. The authors should:
   - list thickness, Hall-bar dimensions and growth conditions for each sample;
   - show the key behaviour (the kink at H_S and the super-quadratic magnetoresistance) in a second device or film at similar n;
   - rule out thickness, or buffer conduction through the Zn(Cd)Te layer, as a hidden variable that correlates with n.

7. The theory is not compared quantitatively with the data. Fig. 1b shows a k.p simulation at E_F = 20 meV, but it is never overlaid on, or quantitatively compared with, the measured rho_xy and rho_xx of the sample closest in E_F (Sample A). The Fermi velocity, g-factor and disorder treatment are deferred to the supplement. The table in Fig. 1e also needs explaining. E_F does not scale as n^(1/3), as a linear Dirac band would require: from Sample A, Sample E would be expected at about 41 meV, against the 50.5 meV listed, and Sample D at about 30 meV, against 33.7 meV. The authors should state the dispersion and parameters used. They should then use the measured n of each sample to predict the Landau-level crossing fields and plateau heights, and compare these with the data.

8. Some claims go beyond the evidence and the novelty is not placed against prior work. Claims such as "confirm the existence of QQHE" (p. 3), "a zero resistivity state is obscured" by Coulomb disorder (abstract), and "it is Coulomb disorder that rules the roost" (p. 6) are stronger than the data allow. In particular, rho_xx in a 3D QQHE need not vanish even without disorder, because the dispersion along the field remains. The estimate of a "few meV" disorder potential for pentatellurides (p. 7 and Fig. 3j) is drawn as a region but is not calculated. The authors should state what is new relative to Refs. 30-32 and earlier quantum-limit work on bulk Cd3As2. They should either compute eV0 for ZrTe5 and HfTe5 with stated parameters or label that region as schematic.

C. Minor

9. Fig. 2a labels Sample E as "2.3 x 10^16 cm^-3", whereas the caption, the text and Fig. 1e give 2.3 x 10^17 cm^-3. The Hall slope in Fig. 2b supports 10^17.
10. The abstract and p. 3 give the density range as "about 2-20 x 10^16 cm^-3", but Sample E is 23.2 x 10^16 cm^-3. The abstract is also missing a word: "tunability in the Fermi [level]".
11. Methods give the buffer as "ZnxTe1-x". Presumably the buffer is Zn(x)Cd(1-x)Te; please correct this and give x. Also confirm the growth temperature ("115 C").
12. Fig. 3i: define the marker and line conventions (circles and solid line for beta, squares and dashed line for gamma) in the caption, not by arrows alone. Fig. 3j: define how the "Visible QQHE" shading was determined. Fig. 2: state which panels show which temperatures.
13. Fig. 1b: give the disorder parameters used for the light-blue trace and explain the x250 scaling, so readers can judge how far the clean model is from the data.
14. References 20 and 38 are the same paper.
15. Typos: "mangetotransport" (p. 3), "requisite" used for "consequently" (p. 4 and p. 5), "corequisitely" (p. 7).
16. Please consider depositing the transport data and fit code rather than making them available "upon reasonable request". This matters because the main result depends on the fits.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a summary figure across Samples A-E: H_S, the rho_xy value at the step, and the high-field and low-field Hall densities plotted against n, each compared with k.p predictions. This one figure would address concerns 1, 2 and 7.
2. Use quantum oscillations, in Sample E and wherever they are visible in D, to obtain the Fermi wavevector, the Fermi-surface anisotropy and the Dingle temperature. This gives an independent anchor for E_F and the disorder broadening.
3. Since the conclusion itself proposes gating, a gated device, even on a single film, would separate the effects of E_F from the effects of changing defect density. That is the central confound when samples are compared with one another.
4. Present field-angle and thickness dependence, so that the 3D bulk QQHE is distinguished from surface-state and Weyl-orbit quantum Hall transport in Cd3As2 films.
