A. Must address

1. **The QQHE identification is not tested quantitatively and is not distinguished from ordinary quantum-limit physics.** The QQHE claim (Abstract; Introduction, p. 3: "our observations serve to confirm the existence of QQHE in a 3D gapless Dirac semimetal"; Summary: "we have observed evidence of the 3D QQHE") rests on a change of slope in ρxy at H_S (H_S ≈ 3.5 T in Sample C, Fig. 2e; Fig. 3e–h) and its coincidence with a lump or shoulder in ρxx (near 5.7 T in Sample D, tracked by αH∂Hρxy in Fig. 3d). Nothing connects these features quantitatively to Landau-level physics in the specific samples. A back-of-envelope estimate for Sample C (n = 5.8×10^16 cm^-3, fourfold degeneracy, isotropic Fermi surface) gives k_F ≈ 1×10^8 m^-1, an SdH frequency of ~3 T and a last-crossing field of ~3 T, so the kink sits just where the onset of the quantum limit would be expected; the paper itself calls the QQHE an extension of quantum oscillations. The kink fields may scale plausibly (C ≈ 3.5 T, D ≈ 5.7 T, ratio 1.6 against (n_D/n_C)^{2/3} ≈ 1.4), but this has to be shown. In ZrTe5 and HfTe5 (Refs. 7, 10, 15) the QQHE was established by comparing plateau values with (h/e²)(λ_F,z/2), i.e. σxy ≈ (e²/h)(k_F,z/π), with k_F from quantum oscillations, and by checking that plateau fields track the last Landau-level crossing. The authors should: (i) compare the ρxy (or σxy) value at the step with the quasi-quantized expectation for each sample's density and Fermi-surface anisotropy; (ii) plot H_S (or the lump field) against n for Samples A–D, with uncertainties, and compare with the last Landau-band crossing or quantum-limit field computed from the k·p model in Methods at each sample's own E_F; (iii) quote the SdH frequency or frequencies from Sample E (Fig. 2a–c), and from any oscillations in D, to fix k_F independently of the Hall slope. Without these the kink could be any field-dependent change in transport, and the claim should be narrowed to "a quantum-limit feature consistent with QQHE".

2. **The single-carrier analysis that sets n, E_F and eV0/E_F is contradicted by the authors' own Hall data, and parallel or multiple conduction channels are not excluded.** Methods state that n comes from the ρxy slope below 1 T, and Fig. 1e converts it to E_F assuming a single Dirac pocket. The high-field Hall slopes are much smaller than this n implies: Sample A (n = 2.9×10^16 cm^-3) should give H/(ne) ≈ −300 mΩ cm at 14 T but Fig. 3e shows ≈ −100; Sample B ≈ −240 predicted vs ≈ −95 measured; Sample C ≈ −150 vs ≈ −95 (Fig. 2e, Fig. 3g); Sample D ≈ −94 vs ≈ −75. The mismatch grows as n falls, to roughly a factor of three for A. Moreover μB ≈ 1 at 1 T, so the "low-field" slope is not in the weak-field limit. This points to field-dependent or multiple conduction channels: surface or Weyl-orbit states, parallel conduction through the ZnxTe1−x buffer, the GaAs interface, a minority hole or second carrier, density inhomogeneity, or a field-induced density shift. The two-band explanation is dismissed only qualitatively on p. 3 ("nonmonotonicity in the Hall effect of multiband models is typically smooth"). Parallel conduction would make the single-band argument fail and could also produce the super-quadratic ρxx background. Since E_F (Fig. 1e), eV0/E_F (Figs. 1d, 3j), the quantum-limit field scales and the strong-disorder classification all assume one well-defined density, the authors should: fit ρxx(H) and ρxy(H) jointly with two-carrier (or multicarrier) models for every sample, at least A–C, and show whether such fits fail; compare low- and high-field Hall densities for every sample; compare n with the quantum-oscillation frequency where oscillations are visible (Sample E, Fig. 2a); explain why the high-field Hall response departs from H/(ne) if the system is a single pocket in the quantum limit; and rule out buffer conduction, e.g. with a buffer-only Hall measurement.

3. **The three-dimensional, bulk character of the features is not established for films of this thickness.** The films are 200–560 nm thick (Methods), but thickness is not assigned to samples. With v_F ~ 0.5–1×10^6 m/s the confinement subband spacing ħv_Fπ/d for d = 200 nm is ~5–10 meV, comparable to E_F = 20–34 meV, and 2π/k_F is a few tens of nm, so only a few subbands may be occupied in the lowest-density films. Quasi-2D subband Landau levels would produce Hall steps by a different mechanism, and surface-state and Weyl-orbit transport in Cd3As2 films (Refs. 30, 31) is known to give quantized Hall features in this thickness range. Both the "3D QQHE" interpretation and the use of a 3D DOS to obtain E_F assume bulk 3D states. The text states (p. 4) that "magnetotransport at different fixed magnetic field angles are consistent with a 3D Fermi surface", but these data are not shown. The authors should report each sample's thickness and an estimate of the subband spacing; show the angle-dependent ρxy and ρxx (at minimum the angle dependence of H_S) to demonstrate that the features scale with total rather than perpendicular field; and test whether H_S and the kink amplitude depend on thickness, ideally by comparing two films of similar n and different thickness.

4. **The Coulomb-disorder strength is calculated, not measured, and its inputs are not stated.** eV0 in Figs. 1d and 3j comes from Skinner's theory (Ref. 9), which requires a charged-impurity density N_imp, a dielectric constant and a velocity; these appear only in the Supplementary Information, not the main text. No disorder-related quantity is measured: not N_imp, not the compensation ratio, not a quantum-to-transport lifetime ratio. The abstract says the authors "tune the defect concentrations", but no defect characterization is presented. Because the films are tuned by defect chemistry, N_imp is unlikely to be the same in every sample, and the very low n implies strong compensation (N_imp ≫ n; Fig. 1c mentions ~10^18 cm^-3 "in some films"), to which eV0 is very sensitive. The statement "As we have hypothesized and shown" (p. 6) is therefore not supported by a measurement, and the crossover the paper claims to tune through is model-dependent. The authors should state N_imp, dielectric constant and v_F for each sample and how N_imp was estimated, show the spread of eV0 over plausible inputs as error bands in Figs. 1d and 3j, and provide an independent disorder measure: the Dingle temperature or quantum-to-transport mobility ratio from oscillations in Samples E and D (a recognized fingerprint of long-range Coulomb disorder), a temperature-dependent mobility analysis, or defect-density estimates from growth stoichiometry, structural characterization or local probes. A gated measurement on one film, as the Discussion itself proposes, would vary E_F at fixed defect density and remove the central confound of sample-to-sample differences.

5. **The weak/strong-disorder classification contradicts the paper's own figures.** The text places Samples A–C in the strong Coulomb-disorder limit (eV0 > E_F), describes a crossover into strong disorder across them, and fits only them with the strong-disorder terms of Eq. 1, calling D and E (ratios ≈ 0.6 and 0.35) weak. Yet in Fig. 3j only Sample A lies clearly above eV0 = E_F; Sample B sits on the line, and Sample C (E_F = 27.4 meV, eV0 ≈ 21 meV) lies below it, at eV0/E_F ≈ 0.7–0.8 in Fig. 1d, on the weak side. The authors should either treat Sample C consistently with D (weak limit) or justify fitting it with the strong-limit form, and fit Eq. 1 only where the strong-disorder condition actually holds.

6. **The Coulomb-disorder origin of the ρxx background is not tested against alternatives.** The super-quadratic background (Fig. 3a–c) is attributed to strong Coulomb disorder because Eq. 1 fits it, but the low-field H² term is generic and several other mechanisms give a density-dependent quadratic-to-linear or super-quadratic crossover: classical inhomogeneity (Parish–Littlewood mobility-fluctuation) MR from density puddles, which would scale differently in field and temperature; two-band MR from parallel conduction (concern 2); the guiding-center diffusion mechanism of Ref. 17; and magnetic freeze-out or field-induced metal–insulator behaviour in the ultra-quantum limit, known in low-density ZrTe5 (Refs. 7, 24), which would grow sharply as n falls and predicts an activated temperature dependence. Fig. 2d also needs discussion: the field-dependent MR of Sample C appears to grow with increasing temperature (80 K trace highest), which is unusual for disorder-limited quantum-limit MR and may indicate a thermally activated parallel channel. The authors should fit at least one competing background model with the same number of parameters and compare by a goodness-of-fit criterion, and use the temperature dependence of the background to discriminate between them.

7. **The Eq. 1 fit has too many adjustable elements to support the conclusions drawn from it.** For each of Samples A–C, α, β, γ and δ are free (with ρ0 subtracted), on top of a crossover field H_c = 9.6 T and width w = 1.5 T fixed globally without justification, and the fits are to smooth monotonic curves, so "good agreement" is weak evidence. In Rodionov et al. (Ref. 29) the H² → H log(δ√H) crossover field depends on disorder strength and E_F, so a sample-independent H_c contradicts the theory being invoked across a series in which disorder is said to vary. The decrease of β and γ with n in Fig. 3i is the main quantitative support for the Coulomb-disorder picture, yet it rests on three points without error bars, δ ≈ 0.32 T^-1/2 is given without uncertainty, and the trend is not separated from the trivial dependence of classical MR on mobility (mobility is non-monotonic: B 0.67 vs A 1.10). The authors should: show fits with H_c and w free per sample or tied to theory; report uncertainties, correlations (especially between α and β, which both contribute below H_c) and residuals; test whether a simpler form (e.g. power law plus αH∂Hρxy) fits equally well; and compare the fitted β, γ and δ quantitatively with the magnitudes Rodionov et al. predict for each sample's n, v_F, dielectric constant and estimated disorder, not just their trend with n.

B. Should address

8. **Field symmetrization and contact mixing are not described.** Methods do not say whether ρxx was symmetrized and ρxy antisymmetrized in field, and Fig. 3a–d shows measured data only for H < 0 with fits for H > 0. Contact misalignment mixes ρxy into ρxx, producing asymmetric lumps of the kind attributed to the QQHE, and mixes ρxx into ρxy: because ρxx has a sharp shoulder at H_S, any admixture produces a correlated kink in ρxy, which is exactly the coincidence used as evidence (Fig. 2d,e). The authors should state the symmetrization procedure and show raw data for both field polarities.

9. **The QQHE component of Eq. 1 is neither unique nor specific to the QHE.** The term αH∂Hρxy uses the measured ρxy, which itself contains the non-QQHE background. The relation ρxx ∝ H∂Hρxy holds generally in inhomogeneous conductors (Simon–Halperin-type arguments from density fluctuations) and for ordinary SdH oscillations in 2D and 3D, so its success in Fig. 3d for Sample D is not independent evidence of the QQHE. In Fig. 3a–d α "is chosen to match the size of the lump-like feature in ρxx" (p. 5), so amplitude agreement holds by construction, and for A–C α is fitted together with the background, so the split between QQHE and disorder is not unique. The authors should state what in the decomposition is unique to Landau-level physics; show that the peak positions of αH∂Hρxy match the ρxx features independently of α; explain the values of α (0.17–0.35), e.g. in terms of density inhomogeneity; and check whether α correlates with the computed disorder.

10. **The Fermi energies in Fig. 1e are not justified.** E_F is obtained from n via a DOS described only in Supplementary Note 1. For a fourfold-degenerate Dirac band E_F ∝ n^{1/3}, but the tabulated values rise faster: scaling from Sample A predicts ≈ 30 meV for D (33.7 listed) and ≈ 41 meV for E (50.5 listed), i.e. E_F(E)/E_F(A) = 2.48 against (n_E/n_A)^{1/3} = 2.0. The absolute scale is also questionable: with v_F ~ 1×10^6 m/s, n = 2.9×10^16 cm^-3 gives E_F ≈ 50 meV rather than 20 meV. In Cd3As2, 20–50 meV is near the Lifshitz transition between the two Dirac cones and the velocity is strongly anisotropic, so the isotropic single-cone screening model of Ref. 9 may not apply quantitatively. Because eV0/E_F is the organizing variable and this choice moves samples across the strong/weak boundary, the authors should state the dispersion, velocity, anisotropy and degeneracy used, give an uncertainty on E_F, and discuss how anisotropy and the two-node structure change eV0.

11. **Temperature dependence is not analysed and measurement temperatures are mismatched.** Densities, E_F (Fig. 1e) and Fig. 2 are at 2 K, but the Fig. 3 decomposition uses 5 K data, and fits are shown at only one temperature. The text says remnants of the H_S structure survive to 80 K (p. 3, Fig. 2d–f), but whether that is consistent with the Landau-band spacing at H_S relative to thermal broadening is never discussed; persistence to 80 K could point to a classical origin. A Landau-quantization feature should be thermally damped on the scale of the level spacing and broadening, while the disorder background should change through screening and mobility. The authors should use matched temperatures or show that n, ρ0 and the features do not change between 2 and 5 K, and fit Eq. 1 at several temperatures to show that α(T) follows the expected thermal damping and β, γ follow the temperature dependence of screening.

14. **The mobility trend does not fit the screening picture.** Fig. 1e mobilities are non-monotonic in n: B 0.67, A 1.10, D 1.22, C 1.52, E 2.59 ×10^4 cm²/Vs. If scattering from poorly screened charged defects dominates and strengthens as n falls, mobility should decrease systematically with n, and the proposed disorder ordering should be reflected in μ. The authors should discuss this, together with any differences in thickness, growth conditions or defect density between samples.

15. **Several claims go beyond the evidence and the novelty is not placed against prior work.** "Our observations serve to confirm the existence of QQHE" (p. 3), "our results represent the first study of its kind" (Discussion) and "it is Coulomb disorder that rules the roost" (p. 6, also informal) are stronger than the data allow, given Refs. 30–32 and the qualitative identification. The abstract's "a zero resistivity state is obscured by a carrier density dependent background" implies that vanishing ρxx would otherwise be expected; this is not established for the 3D QQHE, where dispersion along the field keeps ρxx finite even without disorder (Ref. 8), ρ0 itself is 8–30 mΩ cm, the QQHE term is modelled as additive to a finite ρxx, and even low-disorder pentatellurides show finite ρxx minima. The authors should temper these statements, rephrase or show background-subtracted ρxx and how close its minima come to zero, and state what is new relative to Refs. 30–32 and earlier quantum-limit work on bulk Cd3As2.
