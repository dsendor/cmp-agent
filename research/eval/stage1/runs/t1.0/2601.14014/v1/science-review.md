## A. Must address

1. **The C = N family rests on one device per stacking, and its N = 5 member is a previously published device with almost no supporting data in this manuscript.** The headline claim (abstract; Fig. 1) is that C = N for N = 3, 4, 5, "experimentally confirmed by quantized Hall resistance and the Streda formula". For the (1+5) L device D3, however, the only data shown is the single hysteresis loop at one (ν, D) point in Fig. 1c (right). D3 has no Landau fan or Streda analysis; no ν–D map (Extended Data Fig. 2 covers only D1, D2 and D4, although the text says maps are given "for all devices"); no temperature dependence (Extended Data Fig. 4 omits it); no Raman confirmation of stacking (Extended Data Fig. 1 omits it); and no Brown–Zak fit supporting its Extended Data Tab. 1 twist angle (Extended Data Fig. 3 omits it). D3 also has a different gate geometry (metallic top gate), and only the Methods say it "has been systematically studied previously [39]"; the main text presents it alongside the new devices without saying so. A plateau at h/5e² = 5.16 kΩ taken alone could come from a misassigned geometry or an incompletely quantized state; the Streda slope is what fixes C. The largest member of the headline family therefore depends on previously published data. The authors should include the D3 Streda fan, ν–D map, temperature dependence and stacking characterization in this manuscript (or cite precisely which figures of [39] contain them), state in the main text that D3 comes from [39], and otherwise limit the claim that the Streda formula confirms C = N to N = 3 and 4.

2. **The C = 3 → C = 4 transition in (2+4) L (D4) is not shown to be a topological, gap-closing transition rather than a first-order switch between competing ordered states or a domain mixture.** The text (p. 5–6, Fig. 4) calls this a "displacement-field-driven topological phase transition" that "agrees well with band structure calculations"; it is the third headline claim. Between the plateaus in Fig. 4b,f (−0.641 < D < −0.608 V nm⁻¹), ρxy interpolates between h/3e² and h/4e² and the local ρxx maximum near D ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹ is small (about 0.05 h/e²). That pattern fits either (i) closing and reopening of the Chern gap at fixed isospin order, as Fig. 4a implies, or (ii) a first-order transition between states with different spin, valley or layer polarization, with spatial coexistence of C = 3 and C = 4 domains over a range of D; the scan-history effects the authors see in D1 (Fig. 3i, Extended Data Fig. 6) make (ii) plausible. No D-hysteresis test is shown here, although one is shown for ν = 3 (Fig. 3i). The paper also does not say what the Hartree–Fock calculation shows about the nature of the transition (a continuous change in the Chern number of one band, or a change of polarized flavor), nor whether the HF ground state is gapless at intermediate Δ. Finally, Fig. 4a uses two hand-picked values, Δ = 11.5 and 10.0 meV, while the experimental windows are −0.683 to −0.641 and −0.608 to −0.571 V nm⁻¹; with no D-to-Δ conversion, "agrees well" amounts to agreement on ordering only, and the experimental windows (about 0.04 V nm⁻¹ apart) cannot be compared with the theoretical windows in Extended Data Fig. 10d (about 1–2 meV wide). The authors should show forward and backward D sweeps at fixed ν = 1.02 and state whether the boundary is hysteretic; show zero-field hysteresis loops (ρxx and ρxy at B = 0) at several D inside the transition window; report whether ρxy at intermediate D is a weighted mix of h/3e² and h/4e²; extract activation gaps of both states versus D from Extended Data Fig. 5 and show whether the gap closes at the boundary; give the D-to-Δ conversion including screening, with the predicted width of the intermediate region; and explain what the HF calculation says along the path between the two states. The "topological transistor" proposal in the Discussion also depends on the outcome.

3. **Quantization precision is never stated, and ρxx does not clearly vanish for some headline states.** The central claims rest on ρxy plateaus "at h/Ce²", but quantization appears only by eye on plots scaled from 0 to 0.4 h/e², and "precisely quantized", "vanishing ρxx" and "unambiguous" (p. 2, p. 6) are asserted from line plots. Some states are visibly imperfect. For the D4 C = 4 state, ρxy is about 0.97–0.99 h/4e² at base temperature and 0.93–0.99 h/4e² between roughly 1 and 4 K (Extended Data Fig. 4l), ρxx is clearly finite at 0.3 K (Extended Data Fig. 4k,l; roughly 0.02–0.05 h/e² in the C = 4 window of Extended Data Fig. 5b, against near zero in the C = 3 window), and the h/4e² plateau in Fig. 4f is less flat than the h/3e² one. The ρxx trace in Fig. 3g sits visibly above zero, and Fig. 2c shows ρxy reaching h/3e² only over a narrow range of ν. Calling a state a C = 4 QAH insulator requires ρxx ≪ ρxy, and distinguishing C = 5 requires resolving 5.16 kΩ from 6.45 kΩ. For every state claimed (D1 C = 3 at ν = 1 and C = ±3 at ν ≈ 3; D2 C = 4; D3 C = 5; D4 C = 3 and C = 4; Figs. 1c, 2c,d,g,h, 3g, 4c,d), the authors should tabulate ρxy/(h/Ce²) and ρxx in ohms at B = 0 and base temperature, with uncertainties and the excitation current used, and replace the adjectives with these numbers.

4. **The proposed mechanism for the D-driven sign reversal at ν ≈ 3 is not supported, and the data are equally consistent with magnetization reversal of the same |C| = 3 state.** On p. 5 the authors explain the ν-driven switch by orbital-magnetization sign reversal across the gap (the same Chern state with flipped valley polarization, in the picture of [48, 49]), but attribute the D-driven switch to "a displacement-field-induced topological phase transition, in which D modulates the distribution of Berry curvature and changes the Chern number of the corresponding valley Chern band". Nothing in the paper separates the two. The D sweeps in Fig. 3i are hysteretic in the same way as the ν sweep in Fig. 3h: forward, +h/3e² for −0.330 < D < −0.293 V nm⁻¹ then −h/3e²; reverse, −h/3e² for −0.307 < D < −0.270 then +h/3e². Only the sign changes, never |C|, and a switch whose position depends on sweep direction is what a D-dependent orbital magnetization changing sign under the fixed B = 0.1 T would produce. Going from +3 to −3 is naturally a change in which valley is doubly occupied, whereas a Berry-curvature-driven change would generally pass through a different |C|. The abrupt jumps near D ≈ −0.33 to −0.335 V nm⁻¹ in Fig. 3i look like domain switching, but without ρxx along the same sweeps one cannot tell whether they are well-defined Chern states or multi-domain mixtures. The claimed distinction from [32] ("opposite Chern numbers can coexist within the same scan direction of D") holds for any hysteretic switch. All switching data (Fig. 3a–d,h,i; Extended Data Figs. 6, 7) are taken at B = 0.1 T, which itself biases the magnetization, and no calculation addresses ν = 3 (Extended Data Fig. 10 covers only the first conduction band at ν = 1). The authors should add ρxx to Fig. 3h,i; take zero-field hysteresis loops on a dense grid of D at fixed ν across the switching region and show whether the B = 0 remanent sign is set by D or only by history; demonstrate electrical switching read out at B = 0; measure the coercive field versus ν and D and show whether it diverges or changes sign at the boundary (the hallmark of magnetization reversal), and whether the coercive-field sign relative to the ρxy sign differs across the boundary; and give the flavor configuration yielding |C| = 3 at ν = 3 together with an HF calculation for (1+3) L at ν = 3 showing the valley Chern number changing sign with Δ, alongside the orbital magnetization. Otherwise the D switching should be described as electric-field-controlled magnetization (chirality) reversal, with the mechanism stated as a hypothesis; the statement that chirality is switched by "three independent parameters: B, ν and D" should also note that switching by B is generic to any ferromagnet.

## B. Should address

5. **The Streda-formula Chern numbers need fitted slopes with uncertainties.** C = 3, 4 and 5 differ by only about 25–33% in dn/dB, and converting slope to C uses n_s, gate capacitances calibrated from quantum oscillations, and offsets n₀ and D₀, each with its own uncertainty. The white dashed lines in Figs. 2b,f, 3e,f and 4g,h are drawn guides, not fits. The authors should fit dν/dB along the ρxx minimum or ρxy plateau, report C with an error bar that propagates the twist-angle and capacitance uncertainties, and show that C ± 1 is excluded.
Raised by: r1, r3

6. **Reproducibility and twist-angle homogeneity are not shown.** Each claim (C = 3, C = 4, C = 5, the ν = 3 switching, the C = 3 → 4 transition) rests on one device (D1, D2, D3, D4; Extended Data Tab. 1), with no data from other contact pairs, other devices or thermal cycles. Each device has one twist angle, apparently from a single contact pair, and the three (1+N) devices sit at different θ (1.29°, 1.16°, 1.39°), so angle dependence and device-to-device spread cannot be judged. Local twist-angle variation can produce plateau-like features and sign changes; the QAH windows in D are narrow (about 0.03–0.04 V nm⁻¹ in D4) and the fractional-filling features rely on uniform ν. The authors should report n_s and θ from several contact pairs on each Hall bar and show whether the plateaus appear in more than one pair, add a second device for at least one stacking (especially (1+4) C = 4 and the (2+4) transition), and otherwise state that each result comes from a single device.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

7. **Rhombohedral stacking in the measured channel is not established.** The Raman 2D spectra used to separate rhombohedral from Bernal regions in D1 and D2 are nearly indistinguishable (Extended Data Fig. 1e,k), and the FWHM maps (Extended Data Fig. 1d,j,p) are of the stack and are not registered to the final Hall-bar footprint (Extended Data Fig. 1f,l,r). Rhombohedral-to-Bernal relaxation during transfer and etching is a known failure mode, and a domain wall or mixed-stacking channel would affect quantization, hysteresis and possibly C. The authors should overlay the Hall-bar outline on the Raman maps (or map the etched channel), quantify the 2D-peak width or asymmetry difference between the stackings, and provide equivalent characterization for D3.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **The Hartree–Fock comparison is qualitative and its parameters are tuned, so "confirm" overstates what it shows.** Δ is chosen per device to give the desired C (9.0, 12.5 and 11.0 meV in Fig. 1b; 11.5 and 10.0 meV in Fig. 4a) with no mapping to the measured D. ε_r = 20 is large, and the screening length differs between (1+5) (53 nm) and the other configurations (28 nm), without justification. Only "the first five conduction bands" are kept; the treatment of remote and valence bands, the double-counting subtraction and lattice relaxation are not stated, although HF results in rhombohedral moiré systems depend on these. The HF assumes full spin and valley polarization (it is unstated whether this is imposed or emerges self-consistently), and identifying total C with the valley Chern number of the first conduction band assumes no competing intervalley-coherent or layer-polarized order; no energetic comparison with competing states is shown. Extended Data Fig. 10 shows C = N over a wide region for (1+3) and (1+4) but only small, fragmented regions for (1+5) and (2+4), with gapless points and lower Chern numbers nearby. The Methods say the single-particle Hamiltonian alone gives the topological band and HF is included "for completeness", while the Fig. 1b caption calls the bands self-consistent HF. The Supplementary Information cited for "mathematical details and physical parameters" (SWMcC parameters, interlayer tunnelings) was not included with the submission. The authors should provide the SI; resolve the single-particle/HF inconsistency; show that C = N and the C = 3/4 transition survive reasonable variation of ε_r, d_s, the band cutoff and the subtraction scheme; give a Δ(D) conversion including layer screening; mark each device's measured (θ, D) window on Extended Data Fig. 10; and soften "confirm" to "consistent with" unless the comparison becomes parameter-free.

9. **Field symmetrization may misbehave in hysteretic regimes, and the unsymmetrized Fig. 3 maps mix ρxy and ρxx.** Figs. 2a and 4b are symmetrized between ±0.1 T or ±90 mT, but near ν = 3 the coercive field reaches about 60–80 mT (Fig. 3g, left), comparable to the field used; if one sign of B does not fully polarize the sample, antisymmetrization mixes magnetic states. Likewise the D1 loops in Fig. 2c,d (coercive fields up to about 30 mT) are taken at ±50 mT without confirming saturation. Figs. 3a–d, 3h, 3i and Extended Data Figs. 6–7 are taken at a single B = 0.1 T, and their captions do not say whether they are raw or symmetrized; ρxx reaches about 20 kΩ around the switching region, so raw ρxy contains a geometric admixture of ρxx, which makes the boundaries quoted to 0.001–0.003 V nm⁻¹ (p. 5) and the "nearly rectangular" loop uncertain. The authors should state the procedure for every map, confirm |B| exceeds the coercive field everywhere symmetrized data are shown, estimate the ρxx admixture in the Fig. 3 maps (or apply the +B forward / −B backward antisymmetrization used for the loops), and show the −0.1 T maps.

10. **The temperature dependence is not analyzed, and the Curie-temperature and "up to 3 K" statements overreach.** No energy gaps are extracted from Extended Data Figs. 4 and 5. For the D4 C = 4 state, ρxx rises roughly linearly from the lowest temperature (Extended Data Fig. 4l), which does not look activated. "Decent quantization persisting up to 3 K and Curie temperature reaching approximately 8 K" applies only to D1 (Extended Data Fig. 4a), and even there ρxy at 3 K is visibly below h/3e² (Extended Data Fig. 4c); D2 and both D4 states lose hysteresis near 5.5–6 K, and the D4 C = 4 ρxy has fallen to about 0.93 h/4e² by 3 K. The authors should extract Arrhenius gaps for each state where activated behavior holds and compare them with the HF gaps in Figs. 1b and 4a, define how the Curie temperature was determined and quote it per device, and state the quantization range per device with the deviation at 3 K.

11. **The C = 2 state at ν = 3/2 and the "incipient fractional Chern insulator" at ν = 7/3 are not established.** A C = 2 Chern insulator should give ρxy = h/2e², but in Extended Data Fig. 8c the zero-field ρxy at ν = 3/2 saturates near 0.3–0.32 h/e² at 0.5 K, close to h/3e² of the adjacent C = 3 state, and ρxx is not shown; the assignment rests on a Streda line drawn in Extended Data Fig. 8b with no slope uncertainty. Regions with nearby fillings, from twist-angle or density inhomogeneity, could produce such a signal. At ν ≈ 7/3, Extended Data Fig. 9c shows ρxy near 0.25 h/e², but the paper never states the expected Hall conductance or Streda slope for the proposed fractional state, no slope is extracted from Extended Data Fig. 9b, and a fan dispersing from 7/3 is not evident there. The authors should state the expected σxy for each proposed state, give fitted Streda slopes with uncertainties and ρxx at these fillings, and explain ρxy ≈ h/3e² at ν = 3/2, addressing the inhomogeneity explanation. Otherwise they should describe these as anomalous Hall features of unknown topology and remove "identified", "C = 2 Chern insulator", "doubling of the moiré unit cell" and "incipient fractional Chern insulator" from the main text and the Discussion (p. 4, p. 6).

12. **Novelty relative to Refs. [39–41, 54, 55] is not stated.** Ref. [39] is from the same group, covers twisted rhombohedral graphene with high Chern numbers and is the source of D3; Ref. [54] reports twisted (2+4) graphene, the same system as D4; Ref. [55] the twisted (1+N) family. Yet the Introduction says "experimental access … remains elusive" and the Discussion that the D-driven C = 3 to 4 transition "has remained elusive" (p. 6). The authors should add a short comparison stating, for each member of the family, which stackings, angles and Chern numbers each reference reports, which observations here are new (for example the (1+3)/(1+4) members and the D-driven |C| change), and how they agree or disagree with those works.

13. **The sign convention for D appears inconsistent with the stated physical picture.** Methods and Fig. 1a define positive D as pointing from the rhombohedral multilayer toward the monolayer, with the rhombohedral stack on the bottom. Positive D then corresponds to a higher bottom-gate potential, drawing electrons toward the outer rhombohedral layer, away from the moiré interface; yet the text (p. 3) says QAH states appear at large negative D "when electrons are polarized away from moiré interface". This determines which layer hosts the carriers and whether the experimental states correspond to the calculated sign of Δ. The authors should clarify the convention, mark Δ and its sign relative to D on Fig. 1a, and state which side the electrons are polarized toward in the QAH regime.
Raised by: r1, r3

14. **Spin and valley polarization is assumed rather than tested experimentally.** The p. 3 statement that interactions produce "a spin- and valley-polarized orbital Chern band" is not tested, for example through the in-plane field dependence of the ν = 1 and ν = 3 states, and the ν = 3 sign-switching argument relies on which flavors are occupied. The authors should add such a test or present the polarization as an assumption.
