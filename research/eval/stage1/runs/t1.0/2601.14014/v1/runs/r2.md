Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) states at moiré filling ν = 1 with C = 3, 4 and 5 in twisted monolayer–rhombohedral N-layer graphene, (1+N) L, for N = 3, 4, 5. It also reports |C| = 3 states near ν = 3 in (1+3) L whose Hall sign reverses with ν or D, and a displacement-field-driven transition between quantized C = 3 and C = 4 plateaus at ν = 1 in twisted Bernal bilayer–rhombohedral tetralayer, (2+4) L. A zero-field transition between two quantized high-C states at fixed filling, together with a single-material family in which C follows the layer number, would interest a broad readership. The novelty needs to be stated more carefully, though. The C = 5 device (D3) was already "systematically studied previously [39]". Refs. [40, 41, 54, 55] report closely related high-C QAH states in twisted rhombohedral graphene, and Ref. [54] covers the same (2+4) L system.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- Zero-field hysteresis loops with ρxy at h/3e², h/4e² and h/5e² and small ρxx (Fig. 1c), with Streda-formula fan slopes C = 3 and C = 4 for D1 and D2 (Fig. 2b,f).
- In D4 (Fig. 4), two distinct quantized plateaus (h/3e² and h/4e²) appear in a single D sweep at ν = 1.02. Each is supported by its own hysteresis loop (Fig. 4c,d) and Streda slope (Fig. 4g,h), and the temperature dependence is in Extended Data Fig. 5.
- Twist angles are cross-checked by full-filling resistance peaks and Brown–Zak oscillations, which agree to about 0.01° (Extended Data Fig. 3, Extended Data Tab. 1).
- The Hartree–Fock phase diagrams in Extended Data Fig. 10 give a testable map of C against θ and Δ for each stacking.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The C = N family rests on one device per stacking, and the N = 5 member is a previously published device with no supporting data here.** The headline claim (abstract; Fig. 1) is that C = N for N = 3, 4, 5, "experimentally confirmed by quantized Hall resistance and the Streda formula". For N = 5, however, the only data in this manuscript is the single hysteresis loop in Fig. 1c. D3 has no Landau fan or Streda analysis, no ν–D map (Extended Data Fig. 2 covers only D1, D2 and D4), no temperature dependence (Extended Data Fig. 4 omits D3), and no Raman characterization (Extended Data Fig. 1 omits D3). D3 also has a different gate geometry (metallic top gate), and the Methods say it "has been systematically studied previously [39]". The main text presents D3 alongside the new devices without saying this. More generally, each of (1+3), (1+4) and (2+4) is represented by a single device, so the reader cannot judge reproducibility, angle dependence (the three (1+N) devices sit at different θ: 1.29°, 1.16°, 1.39°), or whether C = N is robust against device-to-device variation.
   - In the main text, state that the D3 data come from Ref. [39], and say which results are new relative to [39–41, 54, 55].
   - For D3, show the Streda/fan analysis, at least in Extended Data. Otherwise, limit the claim that the Streda formula confirms C = N to N = 3 and 4.
   - Add a second device, or at least a second contact pair or Hall-bar segment, for at least one stacking, especially (2+4) L. If none exist, state that each result comes from a single device.

2. **The claim that the D-driven sign reversal at ν ≈ 3 is a "displacement-field-induced topological phase transition" is not supported. The data are equally consistent with a magnetization reversal of the same |C| = 3 state.** On p. 5 the authors explain the ν-driven switch through orbital-magnetization sign reversal across the gap: the same Chern state with the valley polarization flipped, in the Polshyn/Zhu picture [48, 49]. They then attribute the D-driven switch to a different mechanism, D changing "the Chern number of the corresponding valley Chern band". Nothing in the paper separates the two. The D-sweep data in Fig. 3i are hysteretic in the same way as the ν sweep in Fig. 3h. Forward sweep: +h/3e² for −0.330 < D < −0.293 V nm⁻¹, then −h/3e². Reverse sweep: −h/3e² for −0.307 < D < −0.270, then +h/3e². In both sweeps only the sign changes, never |C|. A switch whose position depends on sweep direction and which leaves |C| unchanged is exactly what a D-dependent orbital magnetization changing sign under the fixed B = 0.1 T would produce. The paper presents "opposite Chern numbers can coexist within the same scan direction of D" as distinct from [32], but both scan directions show both signs, as does any hysteretic switch. No calculation in the paper addresses ν = 3 at all: Extended Data Fig. 10 covers only the first conduction band at ν = 1. To support the topological-transition interpretation, the authors should:
   - (i) take zero-field hysteresis loops (as in Fig. 3g) on a dense grid of D at fixed ν across the switching region, and show whether the B = 0 remanent sign of ρxy at a given D is set by D or only by history;
   - (ii) show whether the coercive field goes to zero, or diverges, at the switching boundary. Diverging coercivity at the boundary is the hallmark of the magnetization-reversal mechanism.
   - (iii) give a Hartree–Fock calculation at ν = 3 for (1+3) L showing that the valley Chern number of the relevant band changes sign with Δ, together with the orbital magnetization against ν and Δ.

   Otherwise, describe the D-switching as electric-field-controlled magnetization (chirality) reversal, not as a topological phase transition. The statement that chirality is switched by "three independent parameters: B, ν and D" should also note that switching by B is generic to any ferromagnet.

**B. Should address**

3. **The nature of the C = 3 → C = 4 transition in D4 is not characterized.** Between the two plateaus in Fig. 4f (−0.641 < D < −0.608 V nm⁻¹), ρxy takes intermediate values and the local ρxx maximum near D ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹ is small, only about 0.05 h/e². That pattern fits either a bulk gap closing or spatial coexistence of C = 3 and C = 4 domains, the latter as for the ν = 3 states in the same family of devices.
   - Show D sweeps in both directions at ν = 1.02 and test for hysteresis.
   - Show how the transition region evolves with temperature and how the gaps behave on each side, for example activation gaps against D. Extended Data Fig. 5 contains the raw ingredients but no analysis.
   - Show zero-field hysteresis loops at several D values inside the transition window.
   - Report whether ρxy at intermediate D can be described as a weighted mix of h/3e² and h/4e².

   This matters for whether the result is a continuous gap-closing topological transition, as Fig. 4a implies, or a first-order switch between competing orders. The "topological transistor" proposal in the Discussion also depends on it.

4. **The theory–experiment comparison is qualitative, and its parameters are not justified.**
   - Δ is chosen per device to give the desired C. Fig. 1b uses Δ = 9.0, 12.5 and 11.0 meV, and Fig. 4a uses Δ = 11.5 meV for C = 3 and 10.0 meV for C = 4. No mapping from the experimental D to Δ is given, so "agrees well with band structure calculations" cannot be checked. The experimental C = 3 and C = 4 windows lie about 0.04 V nm⁻¹ apart, while the theoretical windows in Extended Data Fig. 10d are about 1–2 meV wide.
   - ε_r = 20 and screening lengths of 53 nm or 28 nm are used without justification.
   - Only "the first five conduction bands" are kept. It is not stated how the remote and valence bands are treated, which interaction scheme or double-counting subtraction is used, or whether lattice relaxation is included. Hartree–Fock results in rhombohedral moiré systems are known to depend sensitively on these choices.
   - The Methods refer to a Supplementary Information for "mathematical details and physical parameters" (SWMcC parameters, interlayer tunnelings), but no such document accompanies the submission.

   The authors should provide the SI, give a Δ(D) conversion that includes layer screening, and show C against Δ at the measured θ for each device. They should also show that C = N, and the C = 3/4 transition, survive reasonable variation of ε_r, the band cutoff and the subtraction scheme. Then they should state where the measured D windows fall on that map.

5. **Quantization accuracy and ρxx values are not reported, and the temperature statements overreach.** "Precisely quantized" and "vanishing ρxx" are asserted from line plots only.
   - Give ρxy/(h/Ce²) and ρxx in ohms at base temperature and B = 0 for each state (D1 C = 3, D2 C = 4, D3 C = 5, D4 C = 3 and C = 4, D1 C = ±3 at ν ≈ 3). Distinguishing C = 5 from neighbours relies on the 5.16 kΩ plateau being resolved from 6.45 kΩ, and some states are visibly imperfect. For example, ρxx in Extended Data Fig. 4k,l for the D4 C = 4 state is a sizeable fraction of h/4e² even at 0.3 K.
   - The text states "decent quantization persisting up to 3 K and Curie temperature reaching approximately 8 K". In Extended Data Fig. 4 this applies to D1 only: D2 and D4 lose hysteresis near 5.5–6 K, and ρxy in D1 at 3 K is already visibly below 1 (in units of h/3e²) in Extended Data Fig. 4c.
   - Define how the "Curie temperature" was obtained, and quote it per device.

6. **The claims of a C = 2 state at ν = 3/2 and an "incipient fractional Chern insulator" at ν = 7/3 are not supported by the data shown.**
   - A C = 2 Chern insulator should give ρxy = h/2e² ≈ 0.5 h/e². In Extended Data Fig. 8c the zero-field ρxy at ν = 3/2 is only about 0.3 h/e² at 0.5 K, and ρxx is not shown. The only evidence is a fan feature with a Streda slope labelled C = 2 (Extended Data Fig. 8b).
   - For ν = 7/3, the paper does not state which fractional Hall conductance or Streda slope is expected. No slope is extracted from Extended Data Fig. 9b, and ρxy (about 0.25 h/e²) is not compared with any candidate value.
   - Either quantify these states (fitted Streda slopes with uncertainties, ρxx, expected σxy for the proposed state) or describe them only as anomalous Hall features at these fillings, and remove the "C = 2 Chern insulator", "doubling of the moiré unit cell" and "incipient fractional Chern insulator" language from the main text and the Discussion.

7. **The Fig. 3 maps are not symmetrized, so ρxy and ρxx mix.** Figs. 3a–d, 3h, 3i and Extended Data Figs. 6–7 are taken at a single B = 0.1 T. This is unavoidable for hysteretic sweeps, but ρxx is large (up to 20 kΩ) around the switching region, so raw ρxy contains a geometric admixture of ρxx. The boundaries quoted to 0.001–0.003 V nm⁻¹ (p. 5) and the "nearly rectangular" loop are therefore uncertain.
   - Report the mixing level, for example by checking ρxy at ρxx maxima, or apply the forward/backward antisymmetrization already used for the hysteresis loops: +B forward combined with −B backward.
   - Show the corresponding −0.1 T maps.

8. **Stacking and twist-angle homogeneity in the measured region are not established.** For D1 and D2, the Raman 2D spectra used to separate rhombohedral from Bernal regions (Extended Data Fig. 1e,k) are nearly indistinguishable. The FWHM maps (Extended Data Fig. 1d,j,p) are not registered to the final Hall-bar footprint in Extended Data Fig. 1f,l,r. Partial relaxation of ABC to ABA during processing is a known failure mode, and a mixed-stacking channel could alter or mask C.
   - Overlay the Raman maps on the final device outline.
   - Report Hall data from more than one pair of contacts, so that the plateaus are shown not to depend on the probes used. Use the comparison to bound twist-angle inhomogeneity along the bar.

9. **The interpretation as spin- and valley-polarized states is assumed rather than shown.** The p. 3 statement that interactions produce "a spin- and valley-polarized orbital Chern band" is not tested; for example, the in-plane field dependence of the ν = 1 and ν = 3 states is not measured. The ν = 3 sign-switching argument in particular relies on which flavours are occupied. Either add such a test or present the polarization as an assumption.

**C. Minor**

10. The Extended Data Fig. 4 caption assigns ρxx to panels a, d, g, j and ρxy to b, e, h, k, but the panels show the opposite. The Extended Data Fig. 5 caption likewise swaps (a) and (b).
11. The p. 5 text says Fig. 4f is taken "along red vertical line in Fig. 4b", while the caption says "yellow vertical line". The pink star is at D = −0.603 V nm⁻¹ in the caption but the line cut is at −0.602 V nm⁻¹. Please harmonize.
12. The Extended Data Fig. 2 caption says all maps were measured at T = 0.3 K, but D2 was measured in the dilution refrigerator (Methods). Please check.
13. Extended Data Fig. 3d–f: the x-axis is labelled Φ₀/Φ but represents the integer q. The text also refers to "minima in Rxx" without stating the D value or showing error bars on the fitted λ.
14. Fig. 2 uses B = ±0.1 T for panels a and b but ±50 mT for panels c and d in D1, and ±90 mT throughout for D2. State why the fields differ, and confirm that the hysteresis loops (coercive fields up to about 30 mT) are saturated at ±50 mT.
15. Typos and wording: "Cuire temperature" (p. 4); "Twisted angle determination" (Methods; should be "Twist angle"); "Streda" (Středa); "the external displacement-field-induced potential on the each layer" (Methods).
16. "Data available upon reasonable request." Given the claims of precise quantization, depositing the raw data for Figs. 1–4 in a public repository would strengthen the paper.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Present a single, quantitative "layer engineering" figure for all devices. Plot the measured C, the D window of each QAH state, and the measured Δ(D) mapped onto the Extended Data Fig. 10 phase diagrams at each device's θ. This would turn the "design principle" from an assertion into a tested prediction.
2. For the C = 3 → 4 transition, add the transport gap against D on both sides of the transition, from Arrhenius fits to the Extended Data Fig. 5 data and more. A gap closing to zero at D ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹ would be the cleanest evidence for a genuine topological phase transition.
3. Map the sign of the remanent B = 0 Hall resistance against (ν, D) near ν = 3 after preparing the state with a fixed field. Show it alongside a calculated orbital-magnetization sign map. This separates magnetization switching from Chern-number change.
4. Add a short paragraph comparing directly with Refs. [39–41, 54, 55], covering which stackings, angles and Chern numbers each reports. Readers can then see what this work adds, for example the (1+3)/(1+4) members and the D-driven |C| change.
