Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports zero-field quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) states in twisted monolayer–rhombohedral N-layer graphene, with Chern number C = N for N = 3, 4, 5 at moiré filling ν = 1. It also reports a sign-switchable |C| = 3 state near ν = 3 in the (1+3) L device, and a displacement-field-driven change from C = 3 to C = 4 at ν = 1 in twisted Bernal bilayer–rhombohedral tetralayer (2+4) L graphene. A family of intrinsic high-C QAH insulators whose Chern number is set by layer number, and a gate-driven change of |C| between two quantized plateaus, would interest the moiré and topological-matter communities. The novelty relative to the same group's earlier work on device D3 ([39]) and to the concurrent reports [54,55] (and [40,41]) needs to be stated more sharply.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- Hysteresis loops at zero field with plateaus near h/3e², h/4e² and h/5e² and small ρxx (Fig. 1c, Fig. 2c,d,g,h), checked against Streda slopes for C = 3 and 4 (Fig. 2b,f).
- Two quantized plateaus (h/3e² and h/4e²) within a single D sweep at fixed ν in device D4 (Fig. 4f), each with its own hysteresis loop (Fig. 4c,d) and Landau fan (Fig. 4g,h). If it holds up, this is a striking result.
- Twist angles were determined two independent ways (full filling and Brown–Zak oscillations) and agree (Extended Data Fig. 3, Extended Data Tab. 1).
- Temperature dependence was measured for three of the four states (Extended Data Fig. 4).
- The calculated phase diagrams (Extended Data Fig. 10) give a usable design map.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **Quantization precision is never stated, and ρxx is not clearly vanishing for some of the headline states.**
   - *What is missing.* The central claims (C = 3, 4, 5 at ν = 1 and C = 4 in D4) rest on ρxy plateaus "at h/Ce²". Nowhere does the paper give the measured ρxy/(h/Ce²), the residual ρxx, or the Hall angle at base temperature and B = 0.
   - *Where the data fall short.* For the D4 C = 4 state:
     - Extended Data Fig. 4l shows ρxy at about 0.93–0.99 h/4e² between roughly 1 and 4 K, and ρxx that is clearly finite and rising almost linearly from the lowest temperature.
     - Extended Data Fig. 5b shows ρxx of roughly 0.02–0.05 h/e² in the C = 4 window at 0.3 K, against near zero in the C = 3 window.
     - The h/4e² plateau in Fig. 4f is visibly less flat than the h/3e² plateau.
   - *Why it matters.* Distinguishing h/4e² (6.45 kΩ) from h/3e² (8.6 kΩ) is easy, but calling the state a QAH insulator with C = 4 requires ρxx ≪ ρxy. Describing the loops in Fig. 4c,d as "precisely quantized" requires a number.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - For every state claimed (D1 C = ±3 at ν = 1 and ν = 3; D2 C = 4; D3 C = 5; D4 C = 3 and C = 4), tabulate ρxy/(h/Ce²) and ρxx at B = 0 and base temperature, with uncertainties.
     - Report the excitation current used for each.
     - Give the Streda slopes in Figs. 2b,f, 3e,f and 4g,h as fitted values with error bars rather than drawn lines. Each slope depends on the n_s calibration, so the uncertainty from the gate capacitance should be propagated.

2. **The C = 5 member of the family is not supported by data in this manuscript, and its overlap with prior work is not explained.**
   - *What is missing.* The (1+5) L device D3 appears only in one hysteresis loop (Fig. 1c), and the text says it "has been systematically studied previously [39]". This manuscript shows no ν–D map, Landau fan or Streda analysis, temperature dependence, or Brown–Zak fit for D3:
     - Extended Data Figs. 2, 3 and 4 cover D1, D2 and D4 only.
     - The Brown–Zak twist angle for D3 in Extended Data Tab. 1 has no supporting figure.
   - *Why it matters.* The abstract's "C = N where N = 3, 4, 5" is presented as a central new result. If the C = 5 evidence is already published, the new contribution here is C = 3, C = 4 and the D4 transition, and the paper should say so.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Include the D3 ν–D map, Landau fan with Streda slope, and T-dependence (or cite precisely which figure of [39] contains them).
     - State explicitly what is new relative to [39] and to [54,55] for each member of the family.

3. **The "displacement-field-driven topological phase transition" from C = 3 to C = 4 is not shown to be a topological (gap-closing) transition rather than a first-order switch between competing ordered states.**
   - *What is missing.* In Fig. 4b,f the two plateaus are separated by a ρxx maximum near D ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹. Between −0.641 and −0.608 V nm⁻¹, ρxy interpolates smoothly between the two values. That intermediate region could have either of two origins:
     - (i) closing and reopening of the Chern gap at a fixed isospin order, as the band-structure picture of Fig. 4a implies; or
     - (ii) a first-order transition between two different symmetry-broken states (different spin/valley/layer polarization), with domain coexistence over a range of D. The scan-history effects the authors themselves see in D1 (Fig. 3i, Extended Data Fig. 6) make (ii) plausible.
   - *Why it matters.* "Topological phase transition between two distinct QAH states" is the third headline claim. Only (i) supports the stated interpretation and the statement that it "agrees well with band structure calculations".
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Show D sweeps in both directions at fixed ν = 1.02 in D4, and say whether the C = 3/C = 4 boundary is hysteretic.
     - Show B dependence of the intermediate region, including ρxx and ρxy at B = 0 from hysteresis loops at several D across the boundary.
     - Extract activation gaps of both states versus D from the temperature data (Extended Data Fig. 5) and show whether the gap goes to zero at the boundary.
     - Give the mapping between experimental D and the calculated Δ. The calculation of Fig. 4a uses two hand-picked values (Δ = 11.5 and 10.0 meV), so the claimed agreement is currently qualitative: the sign of the trend only.

**B. Should address**

4. **The mechanism of the D-induced chirality reversal at ν ≈ 3 is asserted, not tested, and the switching is shown only at finite field.**
   - *The assertion.* The text attributes ν-induced switching to the edge-state contribution to orbital magnetization reversing sign. It attributes D-induced switching to "a displacement-field-induced topological phase transition, in which D modulates the distribution of Berry curvature and changes the Chern number" (p. 5).
   - *Why the data do not separate the two.* Both readings produce a sign change of ρxy within a single sweep. The data in Fig. 3i, with several abrupt jumps near D ≈ −0.33 V nm⁻¹ and coexistence of both signs along one scan direction, look like domain switching. All switching data (Fig. 3a–d,h,i; Extended Data Figs. 6, 7) are taken at B = 0.1 T, which itself biases the magnetization.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Show electrical switching at B = 0, preferably by sweeping ν or D and then reading ρxy at B = 0. That is the standard test of nonvolatile electrical control.
     - Test whether the D-driven reversal reverses the sign of the orbital magnetization (switching polarity versus B) or only the sign of C at fixed valley polarization. The two mechanisms predict different relations between the coercive-field sign and the ρxy sign on either side of the boundary.
     - State whether the fixed-B maps in Fig. 3 are raw or symmetrized. If raw, estimate the ρxx admixture.

5. **There is a single device per layer configuration, and nothing on reproducibility or spatial homogeneity.**
   - *The gap.* Each claim (C = 3, C = 4, C = 5, the ν = 3 switching, the C = 3→4 transition) rests on one device: D1, D2, D3 and D4 respectively. Results from other contact pairs on the same Hall bar, other devices, or thermal cycles are not shown.
   - *Why it matters.* In twisted samples, local twist-angle variation and stacking-domain boundaries (rhombohedral-to-Bernal relaxation is common) can produce plateau-like features and sign changes. This is especially relevant for the ν = 3 domains and the D4 transition window.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Show data from a second contact pair, and ideally a second device, for at least the (1+4) C = 4 state and the (2+4) transition.
     - Report the twist-angle spread across contact pairs.
     - Confirm by Raman mapping of the finished Hall bar region that the measured channel is entirely rhombohedral. Extended Data Fig. 1 shows mapping of the stack, not the etched channel.

6. **The calculations are presented as confirming C = N, but their parameters are tuned and their key assumptions are not checked.**
   - *What is not explained.*
     - Why εr = 20 was used, and how sensitive the results are to it.
     - How the per-device Δ values (9.0, 12.5, 11.0 meV in Fig. 1b; 11.5/10.0 meV in Fig. 4a) were chosen.
     - How they relate to the measured D windows.
   - *Hidden assumptions.* The Hartree–Fock treatment assumes a fully spin- and valley-polarized state. The identification of total C with the valley Chern number of the first conduction band assumes such a state with no competing intervalley-coherent or layer-polarized order. Energetic comparison with competing states is not shown.
   - *Uneven support across the family.* Extended Data Fig. 10 shows C = N over a wide range for (1+3) and (1+4), but only a small, fragmented region for (1+5) and (2+4), with gapless points nearby.
   - *Internal inconsistency.* The Methods say the single-particle Hamiltonian alone gives the topological band and HF is included "for completeness". Fig. 1b's caption, however, calls the bands self-consistent HF.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Provide the Supplementary Information that the Methods cite. It is not included with this submission.
     - Show the dependence on εr and d_s.
     - Map Δ to D, for example via a self-consistent screening estimate.
     - Soften "confirm" to "consistent with" unless the comparison is made parameter-free.

7. **The sign convention for D appears inconsistent with the stated physical picture.**
   - *The apparent conflict.* Methods and Fig. 1a define positive D as pointing from the rhombohedral multilayer toward the monolayer, and Fig. 1a places the rhombohedral stack on the bottom. Positive D then corresponds to a higher bottom-gate potential, which draws electrons toward the outer rhombohedral layer, away from the moiré interface. The text (p. 3), however, says QAH states appear at large negative D "when electrons are polarized away from moiré interface".
   - *Why it matters.* This determines which layer hosts the carriers, and therefore whether the experimental states correspond to the calculated sign of Δ.
   - *What the authors should do.* Clarify the convention and state explicitly which side the electrons are polarized toward in the QAH regime.

8. **The claims at ν = 3/2 (C = 2) and ν = 7/3 ("incipient fractional Chern insulator") go beyond the data.**
   - *ν = 3/2.* In Extended Data Fig. 8c the zero-field ρxy is about 0.3 h/e², close to h/3e² rather than the h/2e² expected for C = 2. The C = 2 assignment rests only on a Streda line drawn in Extended Data Fig. 8b, with no slope uncertainty.
   - *ν = 7/3.* No expected quantized value is stated, and ρxy at 0.5 K is about 0.25 h/e². The reading of Extended Data Fig. 9b as a Landau-fan dispersion from 7/3 is not evident in the figure.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - State the fitted Streda slopes with uncertainties and the expected ρxy for each proposed state.
     - Explain the ρxy ≈ h/3e² at ν = 3/2.
     - Otherwise describe these as anomalous Hall features of unknown topology and remove "identified" and "incipient fractional Chern insulator" from the main text and the Discussion.

**C. Minor**

9. **Swapped panel descriptions in two Extended Data captions.**
   - The caption of Extended Data Fig. 4 assigns panels a, d, g, j to ρxx and b, e, h, k to ρxy. The figure shows the opposite.
   - The caption of Extended Data Fig. 5 says (a) is ρxx and (b) is ρxy. The axes show the reverse.

10. **Field values in the text do not match the figures.** The text (p. 3) gives "B = ±0.1 T (±90 mT)", which is not the same field. The caption of Fig. 2c,d gives ±50 mT. Please give the actual values.

11. **Curie-temperature and quantization-range claims are not stated per device.** "Cuire temperature reaching approximately 8 K" (typo) holds only for D1 (Extended Data Fig. 4a). D2 and both D4 states lose hysteresis at about 5.5–6 K. "Decent quantization persisting up to 3 K" should likewise be stated per device, with the deviation at 3 K given.

12. **Extended Data Tab. 1, twist angles.**
    - For D1, the full-filling and Brown–Zak values agree to all quoted digits (10.92 nm). Please give the uncertainties of both methods.
    - Clarify how the "≈" twist angles relate to the fit errors in Extended Data Fig. 3d–f.

13. **Small numerical mismatches between figures and text.** The pink star in Fig. 4d is at D = −0.603 V nm⁻¹, while the text and Fig. 4e use −0.602 V nm⁻¹. In Fig. 3, the C = +3 state is quoted at D = −0.310 V nm⁻¹, but the fans (Fig. 3e,f) are at −0.317 V nm⁻¹. Please state at which D each state's Streda analysis was done.

14. **Measurement details needed to judge quantization.** Give the Hall-bar W/L used for ρxx and the contact configuration. Also give the excitation current for each dataset (1–5 nA is quoted), together with a check that heating at 50 mK does not affect the reported quantization.

15. **Data availability.** "Available upon reasonable request" is weak for claims of this kind. Please deposit the source data for the main and Extended Data figures.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a summary table: for each device and state, give C from ρxy, C from the Streda slope (with error), ρxx at B = 0, the coercive field and T_C. Readers could then judge the whole family at a glance.
2. For D4, add a dual-direction D sweep and a gap-versus-D plot across the C = 3/C = 4 boundary. This is the cleanest way to establish that the transition is topological.
3. Demonstrate zero-field, nonvolatile electrical switching of the ν = 3 state. This would make the "topological transistor" outlook in the Discussion concrete.
4. Present the theory as a predictive map: give Δ(D) and the εr sensitivity, and mark the experimental (θ, D) points of all four devices on Extended Data Fig. 10.
