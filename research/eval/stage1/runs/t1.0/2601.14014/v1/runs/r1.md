Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) states with high Chern number in twisted monolayer-rhombohedral N-layer graphene. It finds C = 3, 4 and 5 at ν = 1 for N = 3, 4 and 5. In the (1+3) device it finds |C| = 3 at ν = 3, with a sign that can be switched by doping or by displacement field. In a twisted Bernal bilayer-rhombohedral tetralayer it finds a displacement-field-driven transition between C = 3 and C = 4 at ν = 1. Layer-tunable intrinsic Chern numbers above 1, and a D-driven change in |C| between two quantized states, would be significant for moiré topological matter. However, the paper cites several closely related recent works on the same family of systems (Refs. 39–41, 54, 55), including the same group's earlier study of the (1+5) device used here. The novelty beyond that work needs to be stated precisely.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Hall resistance quantized at h/3e², h/4e² and h/5e², with hysteresis down to B = 0, in three layer configurations (Fig. 1c). Streda-formula fans support C = 3 and C = 4 for D1 and D2 (Fig. 2b,f).
- Two distinct quantized plateaus, h/3e² and h/4e², at ν ≈ 1 in a single device (D4), separated in D (Fig. 4b–h). Each has its own hysteresis loop, Landau fan and temperature dependence (Extended Data Figs. 4g–l and 5).
- Twist angles cross-checked by full-filling density and by Brown–Zak oscillations, which agree to within 0.01° (Extended Data Fig. 3 and Tab. 1).
- Hartree–Fock continuum calculations (Fig. 1b, Fig. 4a, Extended Data Fig. 10) give a design map of valley Chern number against θ and Δ. This is useful to the community if its parameter sensitivity is documented.
- The ν = 3 sign switching is documented in detail, with scan-direction maps and difference maps (Fig. 3, Extended Data Figs. 6–7).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The layer dependence C = N rests on one device per configuration, and the N = 5 case is not documented in this paper.** Each configuration (1+3), (1+4), (1+5) and (2+4) is represented by a single device (Extended Data Tab. 1), so no reproducibility or device-to-device spread is shown. The paper states that D3, the (1+5) device, "has been systematically studied previously [39]". For D3 the paper shows only one hysteresis loop (Fig. 1c, right). The rest is missing:
   - no Streda fan;
   - no ν–D map (Extended Data Fig. 2 omits D3, although the text says maps are given "for all devices");
   - no temperature dependence (Extended Data Fig. 4);
   - no Raman confirmation of stacking (Extended Data Fig. 1).

   The headline claim, "C = N for N = 3, 4, 5", therefore depends on previously published data for its largest member, and on a single loop at a single (ν, D) point. A plateau at h/5e² = 5.16 kΩ, taken alone, could come from a misassigned geometry or an incompletely quantized state; the Streda slope is what fixes C. What to do:
   - Include the D3 data that establish C = 5 in this manuscript: Streda fan, ν–D map, temperature dependence and stacking characterization.
   - State clearly which results are new.
   - Ideally, show at least one more device for one of the (1+N) configurations, to establish reproducibility of the C = N assignment.

2. **The C = 3 to C = 4 transition in (2+4) L is not shown to be a band-topology transition, as opposed to a switch between competing ordered states or a domain mixture.** The text (p. 5–6, Fig. 4) calls this a "displacement-field-driven topological phase transition" that "agrees well with band structure calculations". Several points need attention:
   - **Competing states.** Within Hartree–Fock, the two states could differ in which valley or spin flavor is polarized, or in layer polarization, rather than in a continuous change of the Chern number of one band. The paper does not say which mechanism the calculation shows. It also does not say whether the HF ground state at the intermediate Δ is gapless.
   - **The C = 4 state is weaker than claimed.** At 0.3 K in D4, ρxx in this state is clearly nonzero (Extended Data Fig. 4k,l; Extended Data Fig. 5b), and ρxy is about 0.97–0.99 h/4e². This conflicts with "precisely quantized" and "vanishing ρxx" (p. 6).
   - **No D-hysteresis test.** The paper does not show whether the transition is hysteretic in D. A forward and backward D sweep at fixed ν = 1.02 would help distinguish a first-order switch between competing states from a gap-closing transition. Such a sweep is shown for the ν = 3 case (Fig. 3i) but not here.
   - **No quantitative link between D and Δ.** Fig. 4a uses Δ = 11.5 and 10.0 meV. The experimental windows are −0.683 to −0.641 and −0.608 to −0.571 V nm⁻¹. With no conversion between them, "agrees well" amounts to agreement on ordering only.

   What to do:
   - Show forward and backward D sweeps across the transition.
   - Report the residual ρxx and the deviation of ρxy from quantization for both states.
   - State the D-to-Δ conversion, including screening, and the predicted width of the intermediate region, and compare these with experiment.
   - Explain what the HF calculation says about the nature of the transition, including the gap along the path between the two states.

3. **The mechanism proposed for the D-induced sign reversal at ν = 3 is not supported.** On p. 5 the authors attribute the D-driven switching between C = +3 and −3 to "a displacement-field-induced topological phase transition, in which D modulates the distribution of Berry curvature and changes the Chern number of the corresponding valley Chern band". The data do not support this:
   - The switching is strongly hysteretic and depends on scan history (Fig. 3i; Extended Data Fig. 6). That behavior is characteristic of reversal of valley polarization or magnetization, as the authors themselves argue for the ν-driven switch, not of a change in band topology.
   - Going from +3 to −3 is naturally a change in which valley is doubly occupied at ν = 3. A Berry-curvature-driven change would generally pass through a different |C|.
   - The paper gives no calculation for ν = 3.
   - Fig. 3h,i show only ρxy, at B = 0.1 T. Without ρxx along the same sweeps, one cannot judge whether the intermediate excursions, such as the spikes near D ≈ −0.335 V nm⁻¹ in Fig. 3i, are well-defined Chern states or multi-domain mixtures.

   What to do:
   - Add ρxx to Fig. 3h,i.
   - Measure the coercive field as a function of ν and D across the switching region. The orbital-magnetization picture (Ref. 49) predicts a divergence or sign change of the coercive field at the point where the magnetization reverses.
   - Give the flavor configuration that yields |C| = 3 at ν = 3, and either support the D-driven mechanism with a calculation or restate it as a hypothesis.

B. Should address

4. **The precision of quantization is not quantified anywhere.** Quantization appears only by eye, on plots scaled from 0 to 0.4 h/e². What to do:
   - For each state, give ρxy/(h/Ce²) and ρxx at zero field and base temperature, with uncertainties. This applies to Figs. 1c, 2c,d,g,h, 3g and 4c,d.
   - Relevant cases: Extended Data Fig. 4l shows ρxy falling to about 0.93 h/4e² by 3 K; the ρxx trace in Fig. 3g sits visibly above zero; and Fig. 2c shows ρxy reaching h/3e² only over a narrow range of ν.
   - Replace the adjectives "precisely" and "unambiguous" (p. 2, p. 6) with numbers.

5. **The Streda-formula Chern numbers need fitted slopes with uncertainties.** C = 3, 4 and 5 differ by only about 25–33% in dn/dB. Converting slope to C uses n_s, gate capacitances calibrated from quantum oscillations, and offsets n₀ and D₀. Each of these carries uncertainty. The white dashed lines in Figs. 2b,f, 3e,f and 4g,h are guides drawn by eye. What to do:
   - Fit dν/dB along the ρxx minimum or ρxy plateau, and report C with an error bar that includes the uncertainty in twist angle and capacitance.
   - Show that C ± 1 is excluded.

6. **The evidence for rhombohedral stacking in the measured region is weak.** The 2D-peak Raman spectra for "rhombohedral" and "Bernal" regions are almost identical for D1 and D2 (Extended Data Fig. 1e,k). The FWHM maps are taken on the final stack, but the Hall-bar outline is not marked on them. Rhombohedral domains often relax to Bernal during transfer and etching, and a domain wall inside the Hall bar would affect both the quantization and the hysteresis. What to do:
   - Overlay the Hall-bar region on the Raman maps (Extended Data Fig. 1d,j,p).
   - Quantify the 2D-peak width or asymmetry difference between the two stackings.
   - Provide equivalent characterization for D3.

7. **The Hartree–Fock parameters are chosen per device and could be tuned, so "confirm" overstates what the calculation shows.** Methods use ε_r = 20, which is large, and a screening length d_s that differs between (1+5) (53 nm) and the other configurations (28 nm). Δ is chosen separately for each device (9.0, 12.5 and 11.0 meV in Fig. 1b) and is not tied to the measured D. Extended Data Fig. 10 shows that C = N holds only in a restricted part of the (θ, Δ) plane for (1+4) and (1+5), and that lower Chern numbers appear elsewhere. What to do:
   - Show how the Chern number of the first conduction band depends on ε_r.
   - Mark each device's (θ, Δ) range, converted from its experimental D window, on Extended Data Fig. 10.
   - State whether spin and valley polarization at ν = 1 is imposed or emerges self-consistently.
   - Note that the Supplementary Information containing the model parameters was not part of the material reviewed here.

8. **The C = 2 state at ν = 3/2 and the "incipient fractional Chern insulator" at ν = 7/3 are not established, and their values may point to contamination from neighboring states.**
   - At ν = 3/2, Extended Data Fig. 8c shows ρxy saturating near 0.32 h/e² at 0.5 K. That is close to h/3e², the value of the adjacent C = 3 state, and far from h/2e². Regions with nearby fillings, from twist-angle or density inhomogeneity, could produce such a signal.
   - At ν ≈ 7/3, Extended Data Fig. 9c shows ρxy near 0.25 h/e², but the paper never states the value expected for the proposed fractional state.
   - What to do: state the expected σxy for each proposed state, show the Streda slopes with uncertainties and ρxx at these fillings, and address the inhomogeneity explanation. Otherwise move these results out of the abstract-level narrative and the Discussion (p. 4, p. 6).

9. **Novelty relative to Refs. 39–41, 54 and 55 is not stated.** Ref. 39 is from the same group and covers twisted rhombohedral graphene with high Chern numbers, and D3 comes from that study. Ref. 54 reports twisted (2+4) graphene and Ref. 55 the twisted (1+N) family. The Introduction says "experimental access … remains elusive" and that the D-driven C = 3 to 4 transition "has remained elusive" (p. 6). What to do: state concretely which observations are reported here for the first time, and how they agree or disagree with Refs. 39–41, 54 and 55.

10. **Symmetrization may misbehave in hysteretic regimes.**
    - Fig. 2a and Fig. 4b are symmetrized between ±0.1 T or ±90 mT. Near ν = 3 the coercive field reaches about 60–80 mT (Fig. 3g, left), which is comparable to the field used. If one sign of B does not fully polarize the sample, antisymmetrization can mix magnetic states.
    - The captions of Fig. 3a–d do not say whether those maps are raw or symmetrized.
    - What to do: state the procedure for every map, and confirm that |B| exceeds the coercive field everywhere in the region shown. Otherwise show both field polarities separately.

11. **Twist-angle homogeneity is not shown.** Each device has one twist angle (Extended Data Tab. 1), apparently from a single contact pair. The QAH windows in D are narrow (about 0.03–0.04 V nm⁻¹ in D4), and the fractional-filling features rely on the uniformity of ν. What to do: report n_s and θ from several contact pairs on each Hall bar, and show whether the plateaus appear in more than one pair.

12. **No energy gaps are extracted.** The temperature dependences (Extended Data Figs. 4 and 5) are not analyzed. For the (2+4) C = 4 state, ρxx rises roughly linearly from the lowest temperature (Extended Data Fig. 4l), which does not look activated. Gaps for each state, extracted by Arrhenius fits where activated behavior holds, would allow:
    - comparison with the HF gaps in Fig. 1b and Fig. 4a;
    - a test of whether the transition in D4 shows gap closing (concern 2);
    - a check on the statement "decent quantization persisting up to 3 K".

    The authors should also define how the "Curie temperature reaching approximately 8 K" was determined.

C. Minor

14. Panel labels in two captions are swapped. The Extended Data Fig. 4 caption assigns ρxx to panels a, d, g, j and ρxy to b, e, h, k; the plots show the reverse. The Extended Data Fig. 5 caption likewise gives "ρxx (a)" and "ρxy (b)", but panel a is ρxy.
15. The field values for symmetrization are inconsistent:
    - p. 3 says "B = ±0.1 T (±90 mT)";
    - the Fig. 2a caption gives ±0.1 T for D1, while Fig. 2c,d use ±50 mT and Fig. 2e uses ±90 mT.

    Please give one value for each panel.
16. Extended Data Fig. 2 omits D3 despite the text, and its caption gives T = 0.3 K for D2. Methods says D2 was measured in a dilution refrigerator at about 50 mK.
17. The Fig. 4a k-path is labeled "Γ K M K Γ", whereas Fig. 1b uses K'. Please make the labels consistent. Also mark Δ and the sign convention relative to D on Fig. 1a.
18. Typos and wording: "Cuire temperature" (p. 4); "Twisted angle determination" (Methods); "Resistivity were obtained" (Methods); "QAH insulator is uniquely characterized" (Abstract).
19. Extended Data Fig. 10: define the "×" symbol more precisely (gapless at which level of theory?). Also give the meaning of the light-colored squares outside the C = N region.
20. Data availability "upon reasonable request" falls short of current journal standards. Please deposit the source data for the main and Extended Data figures.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Consolidate the (1+N) series with full data for every device, including D3 (Streda fit with error bar, ν–D map, temperature dependence, stacking), and add a second device for at least one configuration.
2. Map coercive field and hysteresis against D across both the ν = 3 sign switch in D1 and the C = 3 to C = 4 transition in D4. Compare the results with the orbital-magnetization and band-topology pictures to separate valley or magnetization reversal from a change of Chern number.
3. Make the comparison with theory quantitative. Convert D to Δ, place each device's experimental window on Extended Data Fig. 10, compare measured activation gaps with HF gaps, and show how sensitive the results are to ε_r.
4. Present the ν = 3/2 and ν = 7/3 observations cautiously, with the expected Hall values stated and the possibility of contamination from the adjacent C = 3 state addressed. Otherwise remove them from the main claims.
