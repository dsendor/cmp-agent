Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) states at moiré filling ν = 1 in twisted monolayer–rhombohedral N-layer graphene, with Chern numbers C = 3, 4, 5 for N = 3, 4, 5. It also reports sign reversal of C near ν = 3 in a (1+3) L device, driven by either ν or D, and a displacement-field-driven transition between C = 3 and C = 4 QAH states in a twisted Bernal-bilayer/rhombohedral-tetralayer (2+4) L device. A single platform where the Chern magnitude is set by the layer stack and then tuned by D is a timely and attractive result, and the zero-field quantized plateaus at h/3e², h/4e² and h/5e² are striking. Novelty needs to be stated more carefully, however. Device D3 (the only C = 5 device) was "systematically studied previously" in the authors' own ref. [39]. The note added cites independent published works on the same (2+4) L system [54] and on the (1+N) L family [55]. The incremental advance over refs. [39–41, 54, 55] is not spelled out.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Zero-field hysteresis loops with plateaus close to h/Ce² for C = 3, 4, 5 (Fig. 1c), with Chern numbers cross-checked by Středa fan slopes for C = 3 and C = 4 (Figs. 2b, 2f, 4g, 4h).
- The C = 3 state in (1+3) L is reproduced in three devices (D1, D6, D7) with different twist angles (1.29°, 1.24°, 1.22°), and the C = ±3 state near ν = 3 appears in all three (Extended Data Figs. 8, 9).
- A D-driven C = 3 → C = 4 transition with both plateaus quantized at zero field in device D4 (Fig. 4c, d, f), plus a second (2+4) L device, D5 (Extended Data Fig. 7).
- Temperature dependence and activation gaps for each QAH state (Extended Data Figs. 3, 4).
- Twist angles are determined two ways that agree (Extended Data Table 1).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The headline "N sets C" result for N = 4 and N = 5 rests on one device each, and the N = 5 device was already published. The abstract and main text present C = N for N = 3, 4, 5 as a systematic layer-engineering law ("the layer number N = 3, 4, 5 directly sets the Chern number"). Extended Data Table 1 shows that (1+4) L is a single device (D2) and (1+5) L is a single device (D3). Methods states that D3 "has been systematically studied previously [39]" and has a different gate geometry (metallic top gate, d_s = 53 nm in the calculation). For D3, the new manuscript shows only a hysteresis loop (Fig. 1c): no ν–D map, no Středa fan and no temperature dependence. So the C = 5 point is neither new nor independently corroborated here, and the C = 4 point is a single device. The authors should (i) state clearly which D3 data are reproduced from ref. [39]; (ii) add a Středa fan and a ν–D map for D3, or cite where they appear; (iii) ideally add a second (1+4) L device, or at least show the twist-angle window over which C = 4 is expected (Extended Data Fig. 10b) and where D2 sits in it. The novelty claims relative to refs. [39], [54] and [55] should also be stated explicitly.

2. The theory–experiment link that underpins "design principle" and "agrees well with band structure calculations" is qualitative, with parameters chosen rather than mapped from experiment. Fig. 1b uses "optimized" interlayer potentials Δ = 9.0, 12.5 and 11.0 meV. Fig. 4a uses Δ = 11.5 and 10.0 meV. The calculation uses ε_r = 20, a large value given without justification. Methods states that the single-particle Hamiltonian alone already gives the isolated Chern band in some regimes, so the role of Hartree–Fock is unclear. No conversion from the experimental D (e.g. −0.669 and −0.602 V nm⁻¹ for D4) to Δ is given, including layer screening, and the sign convention of Δ relative to the experimental negative D is not stated. For the (2+4) L transition, the paper needs to show that the calculated transition occurs in the right direction (C = 3 at larger |D|, C = 4 at smaller |D|) and at roughly the right D for θ = 1.21°. It should also say how sensitive the predicted C is to ε_r and to the remote-band projection (five bands). The authors should mark the measured devices (θ, estimated Δ) on Extended Data Fig. 10. They should also give the D→Δ mapping with its uncertainty, and show the predicted Chern number versus ε_r for at least the (2+4) L case. Without this, "agrees well" is not demonstrated, and the "design principle" is a posteriori.

3. The claim of "well-developed quantization and suppressed longitudinal resistance … across seven devices" is not supported for all of them, and quantization accuracy is never stated numerically. Specific cases:
   - Extended Data Fig. 7c (device D5, C = 3 at the green star): ρ_xy does not reach h/3e² near zero field. The loop shows intermediate steps, and the corresponding ν sweep in Extended Data Fig. 7f (top) is taken at B = 1.5 T.
   - Extended Data Figs. 8c and 9c (devices D6, D7, ν ≈ 3): ρ_xy is plotted at ±h/3e², but ρ_xx stays at roughly 0.15–0.2 h/e² across the loop. That is not a QAH state with vanishing ρ_xx, and with ρ_xx comparable to ρ_xy the plateau value of ρ_xy alone is not diagnostic.
   - Fig. 2g (D2) shows a finite ρ_xx minimum, and Fig. 4f shows ρ_xx of order 0.05 h/e² over the C = 4 plateau.
   The authors should give, for each device and state, ρ_xy/(h/Ce²) and ρ_xx at B = 0 and base temperature, with uncertainties. They should also revise the "seven devices" statement to distinguish fully quantized states from anomalous Hall states with partial quantization. This matters because the robustness claim ("improved robustness compared to other systems") rests on it.

B. Should address

4. The nature of the C = 3 → C = 4 transition in D4 is not characterized. Fig. 4f shows a single D sweep, and the boundary near D ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹ shows only a modest ρ_xx maximum. Two scenarios are not distinguished: a gap-closing topological transition, or a first-order switch between two competing magnetic/valley orders with domain coexistence. The (1+3) L data in Fig. 3j show strong D-sweep hysteresis and coexisting domains, so the same could apply here. The authors should show forward and backward D sweeps through the transition at B = 0 (after training) and at small B. They should also show ρ_xx at the boundary as a function of temperature (Extended Data Fig. 4a, b at the boundary itself), and the magnitude of the gap near the boundary. Hysteresis and a non-closing gap would point to a first-order transition rather than a band-inversion transition, and would change how the result should be described ("topological phase transition" versus "switching between competing Chern insulators").

5. The mechanism proposed for the ν- and D-driven chirality reversal near ν = 3 is not tested. The explanation invokes the edge-state contribution to orbital magnetization reversing sign across the gap [49], and a D-dependent stabilization of K versus K′ (Fig. 3k). Fig. 3k is a schematic with no calculation behind it. No calculated orbital magnetization versus ν or D for the (1+3) L bands is shown, and no measured coercive-field trend is given, e.g. a divergence of the coercive field where M changes sign, as in refs. [48, 49]. The switching data (Fig. 3i, j) are taken at B = 0.1 T, not at zero field, so the statement that chirality can be "controllably switched" by ν or D alone, as written in the abstract, is not yet shown at B = 0. The authors should (i) show switching by ν and D at B = 0 after field training, with repeated cycles to show reproducibility; (ii) plot the coercive field versus ν and D across the sign-change line; and (iii) either compute M(ν, D) from their HF bands or soften the mechanistic claims.

6. Coexisting opposite Chern numbers within one D scan (regions ① and ④ in Fig. 3j) could reflect multi-domain configurations rather than uniform states. The quantization at ±h/3e² in a single Hall pair does not exclude a domain wall elsewhere in the bar. Measurements from a second Hall-voltage pair and two-terminal or nonlocal resistances in each region would establish that the states are uniform. This bears on the claim that this behavior was "not observed in previous reports."

7. The C = 2 state at ν = 3/2 is presented as an identified Chern insulator from cell doubling, but the evidence is a Středa slope only. In Extended Data Fig. 5c, ρ_xy at ν = 3/2 and B → 0 is close to 0.3 h/e², not h/2e² = 0.5 h/e². This is close to the C = 3 value, suggesting the anomalous Hall signal may be a tail of the neighboring C = 3 state. The fan in Extended Data Fig. 5b shows the C = 2 line only at high field. The text should describe this as a field-stabilized C = 2 state (Chern insulator at finite B), not as a QAH/CDW at zero field. The claim of simultaneous breaking of time-reversal and translation symmetry should also be softened unless additional evidence is provided.

8. Středa fits are shown as dashed guide lines without fitted slopes or uncertainties. C = 3 and C = 4 differ by 25% in slope, so the ν→n conversion (twist angle, gate capacitances) and the fit range matter. For Figs. 2b, 2f, 3e, 3f, 4g and 4h, please give the fitted dn/dB (in units of e/h) with errors, and note the field range used. This applies especially to D4, where both C values are claimed in the same device.

9. The evidence for rhombohedral stacking in the final devices is weak in the data shown. The Raman 2D spectra for "rhombohedral" and "Bernal" points in Extended Data Fig. 1e and 1k are nearly indistinguishable. The FWHM maps (Extended Data Fig. 1d, j, p) show contrast, but the area of the Hall bar relative to the rhombohedral domain is not indicated. The stacking of the Bernal bilayer in (2+4) L is also not verified. Partial relaxation to Bernal stacking during fabrication would change the band topology. The authors should overlay the final Hall-bar outline on the Raman maps and give the spectral criterion (FWHM threshold, asymmetry) used to assign stacking.

10. Twist-angle inhomogeneity is not discussed. Extended Data Table 1 lists twist angles from full filling and from Brown–Zak oscillations that agree to 0.01° in six of seven devices. This is reassuring, but there is no estimate of the spatial variation of θ between contact pairs, and no comparison of ν = 4 peak positions from different contact pairs. The C value in (1+4) and (2+4) L depends sensitively on θ (Extended Data Fig. 10b, d), so an estimate of θ variation would strengthen the assignment.

11. Activation-gap analysis (Extended Data Fig. 4c, d). The Arrhenius fits cover only a narrow high-temperature window. Below roughly 1/T ≈ 0.5 K⁻¹ the data saturate, likely from variable-range hopping or residual conduction, and the fitted window is not specified. The fits are done at B = 30 mT while the other data are at ±90 mT. The y-axis of δρ_xy is logarithmic but labeled with "0". The authors should specify the fit range, state why B = 30 mT was used, and compare the extracted gaps (2.1 and 1.0 meV) with the HF gaps of Fig. 4a. The C = 4 gap is half the C = 3 gap, yet the Curie temperatures look similar (~6 K, Extended Data Fig. 3i, l); a comment on this would be useful.

C. Minor

12. Fig. 4: the text refers to "a linecut scan along red vertical line in Fig. 4b," while the caption says "yellow vertical line." The pink-star D value is −0.603 V nm⁻¹ in the caption but the white line is −0.602 V nm⁻¹ in the text; please reconcile.

13. Field values are inconsistent. The text says Fig. 2a, e were measured "under a small magnetic field of B = ±0.1 T (±90 mT)," whereas the caption gives ±0.1 T for Fig. 2a and ±90 mT for Fig. 2e. Please give the actual field for each panel.

14. The text says quantization persists "up to 3 K and Curie temperature reaching approximately 8 K." From Extended Data Fig. 3, this holds for D1, but D2 and D4 lose the hysteresis near 5–6 K. Please give these values per device. Also correct "Cuire" to "Curie."

15. Extended Data Fig. 4 caption: "(Δ_xx and Δ_xx)" should read "(Δ_xx and Δ_xy)."

16. Several items are referenced but not included in the submission: Supplementary Information (Fig. S1 Brown–Zak, Fig. S2 D-scan hysteresis, Fig. S5, Tab. S1, continuum-model parameters). The D-scan-direction dependence (Fig. S2) in particular supports a main-text claim and should be available to referees.

17. Methods: ν = 4n/n_s with n_s = 4/A reduces to ν = nA; stating this directly would be clearer. The sign convention of D (positive from rhombohedral toward monolayer) should also be connected explicitly to "electrons polarized away from the moiré interface" at negative D.

18. The discussion's "topological transistor" and "chiral Majorana edge modes" statements are speculative and could be shortened. The statement that ρ_xy at ν = 7/3 fails to quantize "due to residual disorder or imperfect contact" is not substantiated.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a single summary table listing, for every device and state, θ, D, T, B, ρ_xy/(h/Ce²), ρ_xx at B = 0, the Středa slope with error, the activation gap and T_C. Mark explicitly which data were previously published in ref. [39].
2. Overlay the measured (θ, Δ(D)) points of all seven devices on the calculated Chern-number phase diagrams (Extended Data Fig. 10), with the D→Δ conversion explained. This would turn the "design principle" from an illustration into a quantitative test.
3. For the (2+4) L transition, add D-sweep hysteresis at B = 0 and the temperature dependence at the boundary to establish whether the transition is gap-closing or first-order.
4. For the ν = 3 chirality reversal, show zero-field switching with repeated cycles, coercive field versus ν and D, and a calculated orbital magnetization, to support the proposed edge-magnetization mechanism.
