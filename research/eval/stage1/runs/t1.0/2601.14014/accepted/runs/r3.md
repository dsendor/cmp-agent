Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports zero-field quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) states in twisted monolayer–rhombohedral N-layer graphene with C = N for N = 3, 4, 5 at ν = 1. It also reports a sign reversal of C = ±3 near ν = 3 in a (1+3) L device, tuned by filling and displacement field, and a displacement-field-driven transition between quantized C = 3 and C = 4 states at ν = 1 in twisted Bernal bilayer–rhombohedral tetralayer graphene. High-Chern-number QAH states with tunable C are of clear interest. A D-tuned transition between two different nonzero quantized Chern numbers in one device is the most distinctive result. How much is new is harder to judge. The N = 5 device was studied before (Ref. 39, same group), D-switchable chirality was reported earlier (Ref. 33, also the same group), and closely related independent work exists on (2+4) L and (1+N) L (Refs. 54, 55). The "layer-engineered design" framing rests on a few devices and on a calculation with adjustable parameters.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- Well-quantized zero-field ρxy = h/3e², h/4e² and h/5e² with clear hysteresis (Fig. 1c), and for N = 3, 4 a Středa slope that agrees with the quantized value (Fig. 2b, f).
- Two quantized plateaus, h/3e² and h/4e², in one D sweep at fixed ν ≈ 1 (Fig. 4f). Both have zero-field hysteresis (Fig. 4c, d) and Středa fans (Fig. 4g, h), and the result is reproduced at a second twist angle in device D5 (Extended Data Fig. 7).
- The (1+3) L results are reproduced in three devices (D1, D6, D7; Extended Data Figs. 8, 9), including the C = −3 state near ν = 3.
- The twist angle is determined two ways (full filling and Brown–Zak oscillations; Extended Data Tab. 1), and the transport data are clearly presented with temperature dependence (Extended Data Figs. 3, 4).

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The layer series C = N rests on one device each for N = 4 and N = 5, and the N = 5 evidence is thin and not new.** The headline claim is that "the layer number N = 3, 4, 5 directly sets the Chern number" (Abstract; p. 3). For N = 3 there are three devices with fans. N = 4 has only device D2. N = 5 has only device D3, which, according to Methods, "has been systematically studied previously [39]" and has a metallic top gate, unlike the other devices. For D3 the paper shows a single hysteresis loop (Fig. 1c). It shows no ν–D map (Extended Data Fig. 2 covers D1, D2 and D4 only), no Středa fan, no ρxx or ρxy line cuts, and no temperature dependence. So the C = 5 point, which turns three data points into a "systematic layer-dependent evolution", is neither independently cross-checked (h/5e² against h/4e² is a 20% difference, and it could be confused with a partially quantized C = 4 state with parallel conduction) nor new. The authors should:
   - show the full Středa analysis, ν–D map, line cuts and T dependence for D3;
   - state clearly what was already reported in Ref. 39;
   - ideally add a second (1+4) and a second (1+5) device.

   If they cannot, the "systematic"/"programmable" language should be narrowed to what one device per N supports.

2. **The calculations that "confirm" C = N and the C = 3 → 4 transition contain adjustable parameters, and the paper never links them to the experiment.** Fig. 1b uses an "optimized interlayer potential Δ" (9.0, 12.5 and 11.0 meV for the three devices), ε_r = 20, and a screening length d_s of 53 nm or 28 nm. Fig. 4a uses Δ = 11.5 meV and 10.0 meV to produce C = 3 and C = 4. Extended Data Fig. 10 shows that the Chern number of the first conduction band depends strongly on θ and Δ. For (1+4) and (1+5) in particular, the C = N region appears to sit near its boundary at the measured angles (θ = 1.16° and 1.39°), and large parts of the (θ, Δ) plane give other values. The paper gives no conversion from the experimental D (for example −0.508 V nm⁻¹ for D2, or the transition at D ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹ in D4) to Δ, which would need to include layer screening. It does not say whether Δ was chosen to reproduce the observed C. It also does not justify ε_r = 20. The continuum-model parameters are deferred to a Supplementary Information that is not available here. The statements that the data "directly confirm the layer-dependent Chern number predicted by our band calculations" (p. 3) and that the transition "agrees well with band structure calculations" (p. 6), and the "design" claim in the Discussion, therefore lack an independent prediction. The authors should:
   - compute Δ(D) self-consistently, or with a stated screening model, for each device;
   - mark the experimental (θ, Δ) of each device on Extended Data Fig. 10;
   - show that the predicted C, and for D4 and D5 the predicted D of the C = 3/4 boundary (D4 ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹, and the D5 window), match without adjustment;
   - test sensitivity to ε_r and to the remote-hopping parameters;
   - put the full parameter set in the paper.

**B. Should address**

3. **Chirality switching is shown only at a fixed 0.1 T bias field, in one device, and the mechanism is a cartoon.** All of the ν- and D-driven sign reversals (Fig. 3a–d, i, j) were measured at B = 0.1 T. A sign change of ρxy at fixed B as ν or D varies is the expected signature of a sign change of the orbital magnetization M relative to B. It does not by itself show that a remanent C = +3 state can be written into a C = −3 state electrically at B = 0. To support "in-situ electrical control" of chirality in the sense of the Abstract, the authors should:
   - prepare the state at B = 0, sweep ν or D through the switching window, return, and read out at B = 0;
   - or show the same Fig. 3i/j loops at B = 0 and at B = −0.1 T, where the switching direction should reverse if M·B drives it.

   Fig. 3k is described as showing the "free energy stability of valley-polarized states", but it is a schematic, not a calculation. The edge-versus-bulk magnetization argument (p. 5) would be testable by computing the orbital magnetization of the HF ground state as a function of ν and Δ and showing that it changes sign near ν = 2.905–2.947 and D ≈ −0.29 V nm⁻¹. The switching is shown only in D1. D6 and D7 show the C = −3 state (Extended Data Figs. 8c, 9c), but not the ν or D switching. The novelty claim that coexistence of opposite C within one D scan was "not observed in previous reports [32]" should also be compared explicitly with Ref. 33 (D-switchable chirality in rhombohedral graphene from this group).

4. **The nature of the C = 3 → C = 4 transition is not established.** The paper calls it a "topological phase transition" and attributes it to a change in band topology (Fig. 4a, Δ = 11.5 against 10.0 meV). Another possibility is a first-order switch between competing interaction-driven ground states, for example with different layer or flavor polarization of the occupied band. Only one D sweep direction is shown (Fig. 4f, Extended Data Fig. 4a, b). The authors should:
   - show forward and backward D sweeps at ν = 1.02, to test for hysteresis and coexistence (they show such hysteresis for D in Fig. 3j);
   - report whether a gap closes, for example the activation gap versus D through the ρxx peak at D ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹;
   - compare HF total energies of the competing C = 3 and C = 4 states across D, rather than the topology of a single band at two Δ values.

   For the reproduction in D5 (Extended Data Fig. 7), the C = 3 plateau is quantized only at B = 1.5 T (Fig. 7f top), and the zero-field loop at the green star (Fig. 7c) is neither cleanly quantized nor single-step. So D5 supports the zero-field C = 3 ↔ C = 4 transition only partly, and the text should say so.

5. **The evidence that the active channel is rhombohedral and has the stated layer count is indirect.** Layer number is set by optical contrast, and stacking by IR imaging and Raman (Methods). In Extended Data Fig. 1e and 1k (D1 and D2) the "rhombohedral" and "Bernal" 2D spectra almost overlap. The Raman maps (Fig. 1d, j, p) are not overlaid on the final Hall-bar outlines (Fig. 1f, l, r), so the reader cannot check that the measured channel and contacts lie inside a single rhombohedral domain. In D4 (Fig. 1p, q) Bernal regions are clearly present in the stack. Stacking relaxation during transfer and AFM cutting is a known risk. Because the layer-programming argument depends on the channel really being (1+N) L with rhombohedral N, the authors should:
   - overlay the Raman FWHM maps on the Hall bars;
   - quote a quantitative 2D-FWHM or asymmetry criterion, with its spread inside the channel;
   - give an independent layer-number check, such as the Landau-level sequence at ν = 0 or ±4, or the AFM step height.

6. **The quality of quantization is not quantified, and "well-developed quantization across seven devices" is overstated.** No values are given for the deviation of ρxy from h/Ce² or for the residual ρxx at base temperature. In several panels ρxx is visibly finite at the plateau:
   - Fig. 2c, d: D1, roughly 0.03–0.05 h/e²;
   - Extended Data Figs. 8c and 9c: D6 and D7 at ν ≈ 3, roughly 0.15–0.2 h/e²;
   - Extended Data Fig. 7c: D5 C = 3.

   For each device and state, the authors should tabulate ρxy/(h/Ce²) and ρxx in ohms at base temperature and zero field, with uncertainties. They should also show raw (unsymmetrized) data for at least the key loops, and say whether quantization holds on more than one contact pair. The Discussion statement on p. 6 should then be scoped to what the table supports.

7. **The C = 2 Chern insulator at ν = 3/2 is not supported by its zero-field Hall response.** The text says a C = 2 Chern insulator "is identified" at ν = 3/2, indicating doubling of the moiré cell and "topological charge order" (p. 4). In Extended Data Fig. 5c the zero-field ρxy at ν = 3/2 saturates near ≈ 0.33 h/e², which is close to h/3e² and not h/2e² = 0.5 h/e². The C = 2 assignment rests only on the slope of one feature in the fan (Fig. 5b). The authors should:
   - show ρxy and ρxx along the C = 2 trajectory at finite B, demonstrating ρxy = h/2e² and ρxx → 0;
   - explain the h/3e²-like zero-field value;
   - or describe this as a Středa feature consistent with C = 2, not an identified Chern insulator.

   Similar caution applies to the ν = 7/3 features (Extended Data Fig. 6) that the Discussion cites in support of the fractional Chern insulator outlook.

8. **The activation gaps in Extended Data Fig. 4c, d are extracted from a narrow window near the magnetic transition.** The Arrhenius fits seem to use only the few highest-temperature points (roughly 4–6 K), where ρxy is already collapsing (the Curie temperature in Extended Data Fig. 3i, l is about 6 K). Below that window, ρxx flattens at a finite value. The resulting Δ ≈ 2.1 and 1.0 meV (about 24 K and 12 K) are larger than the ordering temperature, which suggests the fits capture the loss of magnetic order rather than a charge gap. The authors should state the fit range and the number of points, show the fit residuals, and discuss whether these numbers are charge gaps. The fits use B = 30 mT, while the maps use 90 mT; this should be explained.

9. **The sign of D appears inconsistent with the physical picture.** The authors define D = (C_bV_b − C_tV_t)/2ε₀, with D > 0 pointing from the rhombohedral layers toward the monolayer (Methods, Fig. 1a). Negative D then points from the monolayer toward the rhombohedral stack, which pushes electrons toward the monolayer, that is, toward the moiré interface. The text says the reverse: QAH states appear "when electrons are polarized away from moiré interface by applying a sufficiently large negative D" (p. 3), and it relies on this to match Refs. 15 and 39. Either the sign convention or the statement is wrong. This matters for the sign of Δ in the calculations (concern 2) and for comparison with the literature, so it should be checked and corrected.

10. **The relation to closely related work needs to be stated precisely.** Ref. 39 (same group) reports high-C QAH in twisted rhombohedral graphene and includes device D3. Ref. 33 reports D-switchable chirality. Ref. 41 reports a "programmable QAH insulator in twisted crystalline flatbands". Refs. 54 and 55 independently study (2+4) L and (1+N) L. Given the claim that the D-driven transition "has remained elusive" (p. 6), the authors should state in the main text which results are new here, in particular the (1+4) C = 4 state, the (2+4) C = 3 ↔ 4 transition, and the ν- and D-switching at ν = 3.

**C. Minor**

11. Extended Data Fig. 3 caption: ρxx and ρxy are swapped. Panels a, d, g, j show ρxy and panels b, e, h, k show ρxx.
12. Extended Data Fig. 4 caption: (a) and (b) are swapped, since panel a shows ρxy and panel b shows ρxx. The text reads "Δxx and Δxx", which should be "Δxx and Δxy". The log-scale δρxy axis shows a "0" tick.
13. Extended Data Tab. 1: the two methods give identical twist angles and moiré wavelengths to 0.01° and 0.01 nm for D1 and D4–D7. Please give uncertainties for both methods and say whether θ was checked on more than one contact pair, to address twist-angle inhomogeneity.
14. "Quantization persisting up to 3 K and Curie temperature reaching approximately 8 K" (p. 4) applies only to D1. For D2 and D4 the Curie temperature is about 5.5–6 K (Extended Data Fig. 3). Please state these per device. Also, "Cuire" should be "Curie".
15. Several statements depend on Supplementary material (Figs. S1, S2, S5, Tab. S1, and the continuum-model parameters) that is not included. In particular, the claim that the absent ν = 7/3 quantization comes from "residual disorder or imperfect contact (see Tab. S1)" cannot be checked.
16. Extended Data Fig. 10: the white (uncolored) squares are not explained; only "×" (gapless) is defined. In Fig. 1b the "C = 4" label overlaps the band.
17. Methods: please explain how n₀ and D₀ were determined. D values are compared across devices with different dielectric and gate geometries, including the metallic top gate of D3, and the conversion may differ between them.
18. p. 3 says "B = ±0.1 T (±90 mT)", while Fig. 2c, d use ±50 mT. Please state the field used for each panel consistently. For base-temperature data at 50 mK, the electron temperature should be stated or estimated.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a figure that plots, for every device, the measured C against N (or the layer configuration), with the experimental (θ, Δ(D)) of each device placed on the calculated Chern-number phase diagrams of Extended Data Fig. 10. This would turn the "design principle" from an assertion into a tested prediction.
2. Add a table giving the quantization accuracy and residual ρxx for each device and state, and include the full D3 data set (fans, maps, T dependence).
3. Show the zero-field electrical writing and reading of chirality in D1, and in D6 or D7 if possible, together with HF orbital-magnetization curves M(ν, D).
4. For the (2+4) transition, add D-sweep hysteresis, gap against D, and HF energy comparisons, so that a change in band topology can be told apart from a first-order switch between competing polarized states.
