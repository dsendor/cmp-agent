A. Must address

1. The "C = N" layer-programming law rests on one device each for N = 4 and N = 5, and the N = 5 device was published before. The central claim is that "the layer number N = 3, 4, 5 directly sets the Chern number" (Abstract; p. 3; Discussion: "validates a design principle"). N = 3 is reproduced in three devices with fans (D1, D6, D7), but Extended Data Table 1 shows that N = 4 rests only on D2 and N = 5 only on D3. Methods states that D3 "has been systematically studied previously [39]" and has a metallic top gate rather than a graphite one (d_s = 53 nm in the calculation), so it is neither a new nor a matched device. For D3 the paper shows a single hysteresis loop (Fig. 1c, right): no Středa fan, no ν–D map (Extended Data Fig. 2 covers D1, D2 and D4 only), no ρxx or ρxy line cuts, no temperature dependence and no Raman characterization (Extended Data Fig. 1 covers D1, D2 and D4 only). Since h/5e² and h/4e² differ by only 20%, the C = 5 point could not be told apart here from a partially quantized C = 4 state with parallel conduction, and it is the point that turns three data points into a "systematic layer-dependent evolution". Device-to-device spread, and whether C = 4 or 5 appears in every device of a given N, cannot be judged. The authors should (i) state clearly which D3 data are new and which are reproduced from ref. 39; (ii) show the Středa analysis, ν–D map, line cuts and temperature dependence for D3 in this manuscript, or cite where they appear; (iii) add a second (1+4) device and ideally a new graphite-gated (1+5) device, or at least show where D2 sits in the twist-angle window over which C = 4 is expected (Extended Data Fig. 10b). If more devices cannot be added, the "systematic"/"programmable" language should be narrowed to what one device per N supports.

2. The theory that "confirms" C = N and the C = 3 → C = 4 transition uses parameters chosen per device and is never linked quantitatively to the experiment. The text says the Hartree–Fock bands "directly confirm the layer-dependent Chern number predicted by our band calculations" (p. 3; Fig. 1b) and that the transition "agrees well with band structure calculations" (p. 6; Fig. 4a). However, Fig. 1b uses an "optimized interlayer potential Δ" of 9.0, 12.5 and 11.0 meV for the three devices, and Fig. 4a uses Δ = 11.5 versus 10.0 meV to produce C = 3 and C = 4; it is not said whether Δ was chosen to reproduce the observed C. The screening parameters also differ between configurations (d_s = 53 nm for (1+5), 28 nm for the others), and ε_r = 20 is large and unjustified. Extended Data Fig. 10 shows that the calculated Chern number depends strongly on θ and Δ; for (1+4) and (1+5) the C = N region sits near its boundary at the measured angles (θ = 1.16° and 1.39°), with C = 5 occupying only a narrow corner of the (θ, Δ) plane, and much of the map giving other values. No conversion from experimental D to Δ (including layer screening) is given, so the calculation cannot predict where the QAH states appear (e.g. −0.540 < D < −0.420 V nm⁻¹ in D1; −0.508 V nm⁻¹ in D2; transition near D ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹ in D4, with C = 3 at −0.669 and C = 4 at −0.602 V nm⁻¹). Fig. 4a assigns C = 3 to the larger Δ, and experimentally C = 3 appears at larger |D|; this ordering must be shown to follow from a D→Δ mapping, not asserted. The sign of Δ relative to the experimental negative D is not stated. Methods also says the single-particle Hamiltonian alone already gives an isolated Chern band in some regimes, so the role of Hartree–Fock is unclear, and it is not stated which spin/valley-polarized ground state is assumed or whether it is an output or an input of the calculation. The continuum-model parameters are deferred to unavailable Supplementary Information. The authors should: supply a D→Δ conversion (self-consistent or electrostatic, including interlayer screening) with uncertainty for each device; mark the measured (θ, Δ(D)) of every device on Extended Data Fig. 10; plot predicted against measured C(D) for D1, D2 and D4/D5, showing that the C = 3/4 boundary in D4 (≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹) and the D5 window are reproduced without adjustment; show the sensitivity to ε_r, d_s, the remote-hopping parameters and the five-band projection; state the assumed ground state; and give the full parameter set in the paper. Without this, "agrees well" is not demonstrated and the "design principle" (Discussion; Extended Data Fig. 10 as a "prediction") is a posteriori.

3. The C = 3 → C = 4 transition in the (2+4) system is not shown to be a bulk topological transition, and its zero-field reproduction in D5 is incomplete. The paper calls it a "topological phase transition" from a change in band topology (Fig. 4a). In D4 (Fig. 4f) only a single D sweep direction is shown (also in Extended Data Fig. 4a, b); between the plateaus (−0.641 < D < −0.608 V nm⁻¹) ρxy changes smoothly between h/3e² and h/4e², and ρxx shows only a modest bump of roughly 0.1 h/e² near D ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹. Three scenarios give the same data and are not distinguished: a gap-closing band-inversion transition; a first-order switch between competing interaction-driven ground states with different layer or flavor polarization; and spatial coexistence of C = 3 and C = 4 domains across the Hall bar from twist-angle or D inhomogeneity. The (1+3) data in Fig. 3j show strong D-sweep hysteresis and coexisting domains, so the same could apply here. The authors should show forward and backward D sweeps at ν = 1.02 through the transition at B = 0 (after training) and at small B; compare several contact pairs or Hall crosses along the bar; report the activation gap versus D through the ρxx peak and the temperature dependence of ρxx at the boundary itself; and compare HF total energies of the competing C = 3 and C = 4 states across D rather than the topology of one band at two Δ values. Hysteresis and a non-closing gap would mean the result should be described as "switching between competing Chern insulators" rather than a topological transition. In D5 the C = 3 state is shown at B = 1.5 T (Extended Data Fig. 7f, top), and its zero-field loop at the green star (Extended Data Fig. 7c) is broad, has intermediate steps, does not reach h/3e², and has ρxx ≈ 0.1 h/e². The zero-field D-driven transition therefore rests on D4 alone; the text should say so, or zero-field quantization of C = 3 in D5 should be provided.

4. Quantization quality is never stated numerically, and "well-developed quantization and suppressed longitudinal resistance … across seven devices" with "improved robustness compared to other systems" overstates the data. The text repeatedly says ρxx vanishes and calls the quantization "precise", but ρxx is visibly finite in several panels: Fig. 2c, d (D1, roughly 0.03–0.05 h/e²); Fig. 2g (D2, finite minimum); Fig. 4f (a few hundredths of h/e², ~1 kΩ, on the h/4e² plateau); Extended Data Fig. 4b (~0.05 h/e² at 0.3 K); Extended Data Fig. 7c (D5, C = 3); and Extended Data Figs. 8c and 9c (D6, D7 at ν ≈ 3), where ρxx stays at roughly 0.15–0.2 h/e² across the loop so that the ρxy plateau alone is not diagnostic. 5. The electrical switching of chirality near ν = 3 is shown only at B = 0.1 T, only in D1, and its mechanism is a schematic. The Abstract claims in-situ electrical switching of the sign of C, but every ν- and D-driven sweep (Figs. 3a–d, 3i, 3j) is taken at B = 0.1 T. A sign change of ρxy at fixed B as ν or D varies is the expected signature of a sign change of orbital magnetization M relative to B, and a finite field biases valley selection; it does not show that a remanent C = +3 state can be written into C = −3 electrically at B = 0. ρxx is not shown in Figs. 3i and 3j, so vanishing ρxx throughout the switching ranges cannot be judged. D6 and D7 show the C = −3 state (Extended Data Figs. 8c, 9c) but not the switching. Fig. 3k, described as the "free energy stability of valley-polarized states", is a cartoon, and the bulk-plus-edge orbital-magnetization argument (refs. 48, 49; p. 5) is not tested. The authors should (i) prepare the state at B = 0, sweep ν or D through the switching window, return and read out at B = 0, with repeated cycles; (ii) show the Fig. 3i/j loops at B = −0.1 T, where the switching direction should reverse if M·B drives it; (iii) show ρxx for Figs. 3i, j; (iv) plot the coercive field versus ν and D across the sign-change line (a divergence is expected where M changes sign); (v) compute M(ν, D) of both valleys from the HF bands and show it changes sign near ν = 2.905–2.947 and D ≈ −0.29 V nm⁻¹, or soften the mechanistic claims; and (vi) show the switching in D6 or D7 if possible. The novelty claim should be compared explicitly with ref. 32 and with ref. 33 (D-switchable chirality in rhombohedral graphene from the same group).

7. Rhombohedral stacking and layer count in the measured channel are not well established. Layer number is set by optical contrast and stacking by IR imaging and Raman (Methods). The Raman 2D spectra for "rhombohedral" and "Bernal" points are almost indistinguishable for D2 (Extended Data Fig. 1k) and only slightly different for D1 (Extended Data Fig. 1e). The FWHM maps (Extended Data Fig. 1d, j, p) show contrast but are not overlaid on the final Hall bars (Extended Data Fig. 1f, l, r), and in D4 (Extended Data Fig. 1p, q) Bernal regions are clearly present in the stack. The Bernal stacking of the bilayer in (2+4) is not verified, and D3 has no stacking characterization at all. Rhombohedral domains can relax to Bernal during transfer, AFM cutting/cleaning and etching, and mixed stacking would change the band topology and expected C, which undercuts the layer-programming argument. The authors should overlay Raman maps of the finished devices on the Hall-bar outlines; quote a quantitative 2D-FWHM or asymmetry criterion with its spread inside the channel; and give an independent transport or structural fingerprint of rhombohedral stacking and layer number for each device (D-dependent gap at charge neutrality, Landau-level sequence at D = 0 or ν = 0, ±4, or AFM step height).

8. The sign of D appears inconsistent with the physical picture used. Methods defines D = (C_bV_b − C_tV_t)/2ε₀ with D > 0 pointing from the rhombohedral layers toward the monolayer (Fig. 1a). Negative D then pushes electrons toward the monolayer, i.e. toward the moiré interface, yet the text says QAH states appear "when electrons are polarized away from moiré interface by applying a sufficiently large negative D" (p. 3) and uses this to match refs. 15 and 39. Either the convention or the statement is wrong. Because this sets the sign of Δ in the calculations (concern 2) and the comparison with the literature, the authors should check it, correct it and connect the convention explicitly to the layer polarization.

9. The "C = 2 Chern insulator at ν = 3/2" is not supported by the Hall resistance. The text says a C = 2 Chern insulator "is identified" at ν = 3/2, indicating doubling of the moiré cell and "topological charge order" with simultaneous breaking of time-reversal and translation symmetry (p. 4; Extended Data Fig. 5). The only evidence for C = 2 is one Středa line in Extended Data Fig. 5b, visible at high field and coexisting with a C = 3 line. Similar caution applies to the ν = 7/3 features (Extended Data Fig. 6) that the Discussion cites for the fractional Chern insulator outlook; the statement that their non-quantization is "due to residual disorder or imperfect contact (see Tab. 10. The activation-gap analysis (Extended Data Fig. 4c, d) has an inconsistent functional form and a narrow fit window near the magnetic transition. ρxx is fitted to exp(−Δ/2k_BT) but δρxy to exp(−Δ/k_BT), yet both give the same Δ (2.09 vs 2.13 meV for C = 3; 1.04 vs 1.03 meV for C = 4), which would imply δρxy activates twice as steeply as ρxx; this needs explanation, or one formula is misstated. The fits appear to use only the few highest-temperature points (1/T < ~0.5 K⁻¹, roughly 2–6 K), where ρxy is already collapsing toward the ~6 K Curie temperature (Extended Data Fig. 3i, l); below this window ρxx saturates at a finite value. The resulting gaps (~24 K and 12 K) exceed the ordering temperature, suggesting the fits capture the loss of magnetic order rather than a charge gap. The fits are at B = 30 mT, while the maps use 90 mT. The authors should state the fit range and number of points, show residuals, use a consistent definition, explain the choice of field, discuss whether these are charge gaps, compare them with the HF gaps of Fig. 4a, and comment on why the C = 4 gap is half the C = 3 gap while the Curie temperatures are similar.
