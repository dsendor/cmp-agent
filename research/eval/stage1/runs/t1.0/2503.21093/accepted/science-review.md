**A. Must address**

1. **Metallicity persists far above the Curie temperature, which contradicts the central causal claim.** The paper states that metallicity is "a direct consequence of this DMI-associated magnetic reconstruction" (p. 2, Fig. 1 caption) and that "metallic spectral weight appears at the Fermi level only when ferromagnetism is induced" (abstract, Introduction, p. 2). Yet in Fig. 2A all three heterostructures are metallic (dρ/dT > 0, ρ ≈ 0.2–0.4 mΩ cm) from 10 K up to 300 K, while the ferromagnetic transition is placed at 102–123 K (kinks in ρ(T); M(T) in SI Fig. S3A). Between ~120 K and 300 K the bilayers are paramagnetic yet still metallic, so the conduction cannot be a consequence of long-range FM order. The authors should either reframe the claims (abstract, Fig. 1 caption, Discussion) as metallicity of interfacial, hybridization or thickness origin coexisting with ferromagnetism, or argue explicitly for a short-range or fluctuating magnetic mechanism and explain why the bilayers remain metallic at 300 K. To keep the causal claim they need to show that the electronic structure changes at T_C: ARPES (EDCs at X, f_coh, spectral weight at E_F, m*) above (e.g. 150–200 K) and below T_C, and ρ(T) with its derivative across T_C. If the coherent weight at E_F survives above T_C, the magnetism-first mechanism is not supported.

2. **A total-thickness control is missing, so the metallicity cannot be attributed to the interface.** The authors state (p. 2) that SIO and SRO films are insulating "below a critical thickness of 3 u.c.", yet the bilayers are 3 u.c. (2SIO/1SRO, 1SIO/2SRO) or 4 u.c. (2SIO/2SRO) thick in total, and the authors themselves attribute enhanced metallicity to "increased total thickness" (p. 3). The obvious alternative is that a 3–4 u.c. slab of two bulk-metallic perovskites simply exceeds the dimensional-confinement threshold for metallicity and ferromagnetism in SRO (refs. 20, 33, 34), or that SIO acts as a buffer or cap that relieves the SRO interface dead layer. Fig. 2A also shows that 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/2SRO have similar, lowest resistivities, with conductivity rising mainly with SRO thickness, which points as much to a thickness-driven SRO effect as to an interfacial one. The authors should show ρ(T), M(H) and ARPES for single SRO and SIO films of 3 and 4 u.c., grown, capped and contacted identically, and a control bilayer in which SIO is replaced by a non-SOC, non-magnetic perovskite of similar thickness and octahedral connectivity (e.g. 2 u.c. SrTiO3 or SrSnO3 on 1–2 u.c. SRO, or 2SRO/STO/2SIO). Only if these stay insulating while the SIO/SRO bilayers are metallic does the result point to an interface-specific mechanism.

3. **Intermixing, charge transfer and Ir–Ru hybridization are not excluded experimentally.** The Discussion (p. 4) dismisses the charge-transfer and hybridization picture of Nelson et al. (ref. 26) by citing ref. 31, not with data on these samples, and concedes that "we cannot completely rule out the possibility of elemental interdiffusion". RHEED oscillations (SI Figs. S1, S2) monitor growth rate, not chemical abruptness. In 1–2 u.c. layers even partial Ir/Ru intermixing produces SrIr1−xRuxO3, which is metallic and ferromagnetic over a wide composition range and could alone account for the metallicity, the FM hysteresis and a T_C near 100 K; the 1-u.c. SRO sample is the most vulnerable. The authors should provide cross-sectional HAADF-STEM with EELS/EDS chemical profiles across the interface on at least one bilayer (or at least XRD/XRR) to establish layer thickness, sharpness and intermixing, and a valence probe comparing bilayers with single films (core-level XPS of Ir 4f and Ru 3d, available in the same ARPES system, or XAS at the Ir L and Ru M/L edges) to bound Ir→Ru charge transfer. Without these, the step from "metal" to "DMI-driven magnetic reconstruction" is not supported.

4. **The proposed magnetic configuration, and the DMI itself, are not observed experimentally.** The state proposed (FM Ru, c-AFM Ir, ~90° Ir–Ru canting set by a staggered DMI) and the nc-AFM to c-AFM reorientation in SIO rest entirely on calculations; the paper itself refers to the "insulating antiferromagnetic nature of the individual ultrathin films inferred from the first-principles calculations" (p. 3). SQUID magnetometry gives only the net moment, normalized per Ru in Fig. 2B on the assumption that Ir contributes nothing, and cannot separate Ru and Ir contributions, detect a c-AFM SIO layer or a chiral component. The single-film magnetometry ("does not show clear ferromagnetic hysteresis") is described but not plotted. The authors should add XMCD at the Ir L2,3 and Ru edges to show whether Ru is ferromagnetic and whether Ir carries a net, canted or antiparallel moment; XMLD, resonant x-ray scattering or a magneto-optical probe sensitive to the AFM or canted components; any signature of DMI (chirality-sensitive transport, a topological-Hall-like contribution, field-angle dependence); and raw M(T) and M(H) for the 2-u.c. single films over the same field and temperature range in Fig. 2B or a cited SI figure. Absent such data, the title, abstract and Fig. 1 should present the DMI scenario as a model-based hypothesis consistent with, not demonstrated by, the experiment.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

5. **The calculations do not test whether metallicity requires the reconstructed magnetic state, and the DMI is not quantified.** Fig. 4 compares the isolated films in their own ground states (nc-AFM SIO, c-AFM SRO) with the bilayers in the reconstructed state (c-AFM SIO / FM SRO), so structure, hybridization, interface termination and magnetic order all change at once. The decisive counterfactual is missing: the DOS of the same bilayer geometry constrained to the single-film magnetic orders (nc-AFM SIO on c-AFM SRO), with no magnetic order, and with SOC (hence DMI) switched off. If the bilayer is metallic in those configurations too, metallicity comes from interface hybridization or charge transfer, not from the magnetic reconstruction. These DOS and the total-energy differences between configurations (with and without SOC) should be in the main text. The authors should also state how the "staggered DMI" enters the LSDA+U+SOC result (only implicitly) versus the model Hamiltonian, and give the DMI vector magnitudes, Heisenberg exchange and anisotropy constants and their origin (fitted to DFT total energies or chosen). DMI is usually a small, SOC-order correction to exchange and normally favors canting, so the claim that it drives each layer toward more collinear order (nc-AFM to c-AFM in SIO, AFM to FM in SRO) is counterintuitive and needs a quantitative comparison of D with the Ru–Ru and Ir–Ir exchange.

**B. Should address**

6. **The DFT parameters may decide the insulating versus metallic outcome, and their robustness is not shown.** Methods use U = 5 eV and J_H = 0.15U for Ru, Ir and Ti alike, which is large for 5d Ir (typically ~1.5–2.5 eV) and 4d Ru (~1–3 eV); a large U can by itself make 2-u.c. slabs insulating, so the "insulating constituents" baseline may be an artifact. The text says the ground states were explored "for a range of electron correlation strengths (U_Ir and U_Ru)" (p. 2), but no such results appear in the main paper. The authors should show the gaps of 2SIO and 2SRO and the bilayer DOS at E_F as functions of U_Ir and U_Ru (a phase diagram), demonstrating a physically reasonable window where films are insulating, bilayers metallic and the magnetic ground state stable. The gaps should be quoted numerically and compared with ARPES leading-edge positions: in Fig. 4A (2SIO) there appears to be spectral weight close to E_F, E_F should be marked more clearly, and panels A/B versus C/D are labeled inconsistently. They should also discuss that structures were relaxed without SOC although the octahedral tilts and the DMI are SOC-sensitive, and that the slab is uncapped and SrO-terminated while transport samples carry a 15-u.c. STO cap.

7. **The layer and orbital origin of the ARPES bands is not identified.** The stacking order is not stated explicitly, though growth and the DFT slabs (STO/SRO/SIO) suggest SIO is on top. At hν = 82 eV the probing depth is about one unit cell, so in 2SIO/1SRO the buried SRO is strongly attenuated and the hole pocket at X could be Ir J_eff = 1/2, Ru t2g or an interface-hybridized state; this determines which layer "became metallic" and is needed to interpret the evolution of f_coh and m*. The paper compares no measured dispersion or Fermi surface with calculated bands (Fig. 4 shows only DOS) and does not use the circular-polarization data it says it collected. The authors should state the stacking order, show the DFT band structure along X–Γ–X for each bilayer in the proposed magnetic configuration next to the ARPES dispersions, and use photon-energy, resonant or polarization dependence to assign the band in Fig. 3.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **The effective-mass trend contradicts the text, and the fits are not quantified.** The text says m* "decreases from 0.57 m_e for the 2SIO/2SRO heterostructure to 0.5 m_e and 0.41 m_e for the 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/1SRO heterostructures... further confirming the enhanced metallicity with increased total thickness" (p. 3). But the lightest mass belongs to the thinnest, least coherent and most resistive sample (2SIO/1SRO, f_coh = 26%), and the heaviest to the most coherent, most metallic one (f_coh = 55%), so the trend runs opposite to the claim. The masses are also far below the several m_e typical of SRO. In Fig. 3D the band maximum lies ~0.1 eV above E_F, so the parabolic fits extrapolate into unoccupied states over a narrow range and m* depends strongly on the fitting window. The authors should give the fitted k-range, uncertainties on m* and their sensitivity to the band top, and either correct the interpretation or explain why a lighter mass is expected in the less metallic sample.

9. **The f_coh extraction is underspecified and does not track transport.** The coherent/incoherent decomposition (Fig. 3 A.iii–C.iii) depends on line shapes, background, Fermi–Dirac cutoff, energy resolution and k-integration window, none of which are given, nor which parameters were free or the uncertainty on f_coh. The step from 26% (2SIO/1SRO) to 28% (1SIO/2SRO) is probably within systematic error, so the claimed monotonic "enhancement of metallic character" rests on the 55% point alone. Moreover f_coh does not track Fig. 2A, where 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/2SRO have similar, lowest ρ. The authors should specify the fit model, give error bars, show robustness to reasonable variations, discuss the mismatch with resistivity, and add EDCs at other k-points and f_coh (or equivalent) for the single films as reference.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

10. **The magnetization analysis is thin.** The text gives saturated moments of 0.6 μB/Ru (2SIO/1SRO) and 1 μB/Ru (1SIO/2SRO, 2SIO/2SRO), but in Fig. 2B all three loops appear to saturate near ±1 μB/Ru; this should be reconciled, and any real trend explained within the DMI model. For 1–2 u.c. of SRO on a 5×5 mm STO substrate the moment is ~10⁻⁶–10⁻⁵ emu, comparable to substrate diamagnetic and impurity backgrounds, so the authors should describe the background subtraction, show raw data and a bare-substrate (and STO-capped substrate) reference measured identically, give the error on the moment, and state how the Ru count was obtained (RHEED oscillations vs. XRR/STEM) and why normalizing per Ru is justified when Ir may carry moment. The perpendicular anisotropy is asserted from out-of-plane loop squareness and MR alone; in-plane M(H) on the same samples is needed. All loops are at 10 K; M(H) at several temperatures would tie the moment to T_C.

11. **Transport/SQUID and ARPES samples are in different states.** Transport and SQUID samples carry a 15-u.c. STO cap and a post-capping anneal and are measured ex situ; ARPES samples are uncapped and measured in situ. Capping can change termination, oxygen stoichiometry, band bending and the dead-layer behavior of a 3–4 u.c. stack. The authors should state the capping of every sample, including whether the 2-u.c. single-film references in Fig. 2A, B were capped and processed identically, and discuss or test the effect of the cap (e.g. transport on uncapped samples, XPS before and after capping, or a capped single-film control under ARPES-comparable conditions).

12. **Reproducibility is not reported.** The number of samples grown and measured per configuration is not stated, and there is no sample-to-sample spread for ρ(10 K), T_C, saturated moment or f_coh. The authors should report the number of samples per configuration and show the spread, at least for transport and magnetometry, and measure a second sample of 2SIO/1SRO, the most surprising case.

13. **Resistivity normalization and transport details are unclear.** It is not stated which thickness (SRO only or total) converts sheet resistance to ρ for the bilayers versus the single films, although the more than 10⁴ difference in ρ in Fig. 2A depends on it. For 3–4 u.c. films the sheet resistance compared with h/e² is the more meaningful quantity; it should be reported at 10 K, along with whether any upturn appears below 10 K. The authors should also explain how van der Pauw contacts were made to a 3–4 u.c. layer under 15 u.c. of STO, and, since growth at 0.15 mbar can reduce the STO substrate, show that a bare STO substrate processed identically (including the anneal and cap) is insulating.

The authors should show dρ/dT or an expanded view for each bilayer next to the corresponding M(T) curve (SI Fig. S3A), mark T_C, and tabulate the transport and magnetization T_C sample by sample.

15. **The structure underlying the interfacial DMI is not characterized.** The staggered DMI is attributed to octahedral rotations and tilts at the interface, but the only structural evidence is a 2×2 LEED periodicity, which is used as input to the calculations (Methods) yet not shown, and Methods admit that "precise structural and magnetic configurations cannot be fully resolved". The authors should show the LEED patterns, state explicitly how the 2×2 periodicity relates to the √2×√2 tilt pattern assumed for the staggered DMI, and where possible measure half-order Bragg peaks to establish the octahedral rotation pattern.
