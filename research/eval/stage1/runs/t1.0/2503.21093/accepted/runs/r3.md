Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports that bilayers built from 1–2 u.c. SrIrO3 (SIO) and 1–2 u.c. SrRuO3 (SRO), each insulating on its own at this thickness, are metallic and ferromagnetic when stacked on SrTiO3. The evidence is transport, SQUID magnetometry and in-situ ARPES, and the authors attribute the effect to a staggered interfacial Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction (DMI) identified with LSDA+U and a model Hamiltonian. If the DMI-driven magnetic reconstruction were established, it would be a new way to tune the metal–insulator transition in spin–orbit-coupled oxides. Metallicity at SIO/SRO interfaces has already been reported, though (Nelson et al., ref. 26, attributed to Ir–Ru hybridization and charge transfer). The new part is therefore the mechanism, and that mechanism currently rests almost entirely on calculations, with no experimental test that tells it apart from the established alternatives.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clean set of samples from layer-by-layer growth with RHEED control, in-situ ARPES on uncapped films, and transport and magnetometry on a systematic series (2SIO/1SRO, 1SIO/2SRO, 2SIO/2SRO) plus single-layer references.
- A striking experimental contrast: insulating 2-u.c. SIO and SRO films become metals with resistivity of about 0.2–0.4 mΩ cm (Fig. 2A), with a Fermi surface and quasiparticle peak in ARPES (Fig. 3).
- Ferromagnetic hysteresis with perpendicular anisotropy in bilayers containing only one or two SRO unit cells (Fig. 2B).
- A concrete microscopic proposal (staggered DMI arising from octahedral rotations at the broken-inversion interface) that can be tested.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The causal claim, that metallicity follows from ferromagnetic reconstruction, is contradicted by the paper's own transport data. The abstract and text say "metallic spectral weight appears at the Fermi level only when ferromagnetism is induced" (p. 2) and that metallicity is "a direct consequence of this DMI-associated magnetic reconstruction" (p. 2, Fig. 1 caption). Yet Fig. 2A shows all three heterostructures metallic (dρ/dT > 0, ρ ≈ 0.2–0.4 mΩ cm) up to 300 K, far above the Curie temperatures of about 102–123 K deduced from the ρ(T) kinks and M(T). Metallicity in the paramagnetic phase cannot be a consequence of ferromagnetic order. If the authors mean that the bilayer ground state is metallic for another reason (hybridization, charge transfer, a thickness effect) and ferromagnetism coexists with it, the claims should be reframed that way. To keep the causal claim they need to show that the electronic structure changes at T_C: ARPES (f_coh, spectral weight at E_F, m*) above and below T_C, and ρ(T) and its derivative across T_C. They should also explain why the bilayers remain metallic at 300 K.

2. Nothing in the experiments distinguishes the proposed mechanism from interfacial charge transfer, Ir–Ru hybridization or Ru/Ir intermixing. The Discussion (p. 4) dismisses charge transfer by citing ref. 31 rather than this paper's own data, and states that interdiffusion "cannot be completely ruled out". Ru-substituted SrIrO3 and Ir-substituted SrRuO3 are themselves metallic over wide composition ranges, so even modest intermixing in a 3–4 u.c. stack could produce metallicity, and the 1-u.c. SRO sample is the most vulnerable. The authors should provide:
   - cross-sectional HAADF-STEM with EELS/EDS chemical profiles across the interface;
   - core-level XPS (Ir 4f, Ru 3d), or XAS at the Ir L and Ru M/L edges, comparing bilayers with single films to quantify valence changes and charge transfer;
   - photon-energy or resonant ARPES, or at least a band-by-band comparison with DFT, to say whether the hole pocket at X is Ir- or Ru-derived (see concern 5).

   Without these, the step from "metal" to "DMI-driven magnetic reconstruction" is not supported.

3. The magnetic reconstruction itself (nc-AFM to c-AFM in SIO, AFM to FM in SRO) is not observed: it is inferred from calculations. The paper's own words are "insulating antiferromagnetic nature of the individual ultrathin films inferred from the first-principles calculations" (p. 3). No measurement shows antiferromagnetism in the 2-u.c. single films, collinear AFM in SIO within the bilayer, or which ion carries the ferromagnetic moment. SQUID gives only the total moment, normalized per Ru on the assumption that Ir contributes nothing. Element-specific probes are needed: XMCD at the Ir L2,3 and Ru edges for the bilayers, XMLD or resonant x-ray scattering for the AFM components, or at minimum M(T) and M(H) for the single films over the same field and temperature range (which the paper says show no clear hysteresis but does not plot). If the Ir moment couples to the Ru moment (canted, or antiparallel as is often seen at SRO/SIO interfaces), that is direct evidence for or against the proposed configuration.

4. A controlled thickness series is missing, so the metallicity cannot be attributed to the interface rather than total thickness. By the authors' own statement, SIO and SRO are insulating only "below a critical thickness of 3 u.c." (p. 2), but the bilayers are 3 or 4 u.c. thick in total. The relevant controls are single SRO films of 3 and 4 u.c. and single SIO films of 3 and 4 u.c., grown, capped and contacted identically, to show that a homogeneous film of the same total thickness stays insulating. Transport (Fig. 2A) also shows the 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/2SRO resistivities are close, with conductivity rising mainly with SRO thickness. That points as much to a thickness-driven SRO effect as to an interfacial one, and the authors should address it.

B. Should address

5. The ARPES interpretation lacks layer and orbital assignment, and its trends contradict the text.
   - Growth order puts SIO on top (STO/SRO/SIO in the DFT slabs). At hν = 82 eV the probing depth is about one unit cell, so in 2SIO/1SRO the buried SRO layer is strongly attenuated. The pocket at X could therefore be an Ir state, a Ru state, or an interface-hybridized state, and this determines which layer "became metallic".
   - The authors should state the stacking order explicitly and compare the measured dispersions directly with the DFT bands for the proposed magnetic configuration (Fig. 4 shows only DOS).
   - The m* trend runs against the text. The text says m* falls "from 0.57 m_e for the 2SIO/2SRO heterostructure to 0.5 m_e and 0.41 m_e for the 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/1SRO heterostructures... further confirming the enhanced metallicity with increased total thickness" (p. 3). But the lightest mass belongs to the thinnest-SRO, lowest-f_coh sample, and the heaviest to the most metallic one. Read at face value, the trend contradicts the stated conclusion.
   - The parabolic fits extend to a band maximum about 0.1 eV above E_F (Fig. 3D), outside the measured range, and the masses carry no uncertainties. The authors should give error bars, the k-range fitted, and the sensitivity to the fixed band top.

6. The coherent/incoherent decomposition (Fig. 3 A.iii–C.iii) is underspecified. The authors should give:
   - the line shapes used, the background, and the treatment of the Fermi–Dirac cutoff and energy resolution;
   - which parameters were free, and the uncertainty on f_coh.

   f_coh is 26% versus 28% for 2SIO/1SRO and 1SIO/2SRO, a difference that is probably within fit uncertainty, so the claimed monotonic "enhancement of metallic character" rests mainly on the 55% point. EDCs at other k-points, and f_coh for the single films for reference, would make the comparison meaningful.

7. Magnetometry numbers and normalization:
   - Consistency: the text quotes 0.6 μB/Ru for 2SIO/1SRO and 1 μB/Ru for the others, but in Fig. 2B all three loops appear to saturate near ±1 μB/Ru. Please reconcile.
   - Normalization: for one unit cell of SRO on a 5×5 mm STO substrate the moment is of order 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁵ emu. The authors should describe the substrate diamagnetic and paramagnetic background subtraction, show raw data and a bare-substrate (and STO-capped substrate) control, and say how the Ru count was obtained (RHEED oscillations vs. XRR/STEM).
   - Anisotropy: PMA is asserted from the squareness of out-of-plane loops alone. In-plane M(H) should be shown alongside.
   - Temperature: all loops are at 10 K only. M(H) at several temperatures would tie the moment to the T_C values.

8. The DFT setup may decide the conclusions, and its robustness is not shown.
   - The text says the ground states were explored "for a range of electron correlation strengths (U_Ir and U_Ru)" (p. 2), yet Methods fix U = 5 eV, J_H = 0.15U for Ru, Ir and Ti. U = 5 eV is unusually large for 5d Ir and 4d Ru, and a large U can by itself make 2-u.c. slabs insulating. The authors should show the gap of the single films and the metallicity of the bilayers as functions of U.
   - Structures were relaxed without SOC, although the DMI and the octahedral tilts it relies on are SOC-sensitive.
   - The slab is uncapped with SrO termination, while transport samples carry a 15-u.c. STO cap. This affects which measurement the calculation corresponds to.
   - DOS at E_F is used as a proxy for conductivity ("calculations indicate higher conductivity in the 1SIO/2SRO heterostructure compared to 2SIO/1SRO", p. 4), which it is not.

9. The DMI mechanism needs quantitative support.
   - The DMI vector magnitudes, the Heisenberg and anisotropy exchange constants, and how they were obtained (fitted to DFT total energies, or chosen) are not given in the main text.
   - The claim that DMI, which normally favors canting, makes each layer more collinear (nc-AFM to c-AFM in SIO, AFM to FM in SRO) is counterintuitive. It needs explicit energy comparisons: total energies of the candidate configurations with and without SOC, and the energy scale of the DMI relative to the Ru–Ru and Ir–Ir exchange.
   - The key counterfactual is missing. The authors should compute the bilayer constrained to the single-film magnetic orders (nc-AFM SIO + c-AFM SRO). If that state is also metallic, metallicity comes from interfacial hybridization or charge transfer, not from the magnetic reconstruction.

10. Transport measurement details and reproducibility:
   - How were van der Pauw contacts made to a 3–4 u.c. layer buried under 15 u.c. of STO?
   - Were the single-film references capped and processed identically?
   - What thickness is used to convert sheet resistance to ρ for the bilayers versus the single films? The more than 10⁴ difference in ρ in Fig. 2A depends on it.
   - Growth at 0.15 mbar can reduce the STO substrate. The 1 mbar anneal is mentioned, but a bare STO substrate processed identically (including the cap) should be shown to be insulating.
   - How many samples of each configuration were measured, and how reproducible are ρ(T), T_C and the saturated moment? A second sample, at least for 2SIO/1SRO, would strengthen the most surprising case.

11. The kinks in ρ(T) at 102–123 K are said to mark T_C, but they are not visible on the scale of Fig. 2A. The authors should show dρ/dT, or an expanded view, for each bilayer next to the corresponding M(T) curve from the SI.

C. Minor

12. The Fig. 3 caption labels panels "(A) 2SIO/1SRO, (B) 2SIO/1SRO, and (C) 2SIO/2SRO"; panel B is 1SIO/2SRO.
13. Fig. 1 is a schematic of the proposed mechanism but is presented in the Results as an established finding ("is therefore a direct consequence"). The wording should match the level of evidence.
14. The single-film magnetometry ("does not show clear ferromagnetic hysteresis") should be plotted in Fig. 2B, or referenced to a specific SI figure.
15. In the Fig. 4 DOS for 2SIO and 2SRO (A, B), it is hard to identify the gap the text describes. The authors should state the gap size and mark E_F more clearly; panels A/B versus C/D also use inconsistent labeling.
16. Fig. 2A axis label "Temprature" is misspelled. The broken ρ axis hides the absolute scale of the bilayer curves, so a log-scale plot would be clearer.
17. The LEED 2×2 periodicity is cited as structural input to the calculations, but LEED patterns are not shown in the main text. Its relation to the √2×√2 tilt pattern assumed for the staggered DMI should be stated explicitly.
18. The introduction's mention of altermagnetism is not connected to the results and could be removed or tied in.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Reframe the central claim as coexisting interfacial metallicity and ferromagnetism, unless temperature-dependent ARPES and transport across T_C show that metallicity tracks the magnetic order.
2. Add element-specific magnetism (Ir and Ru XMCD) and interface chemistry (STEM-EELS, XPS/XAS valence). Together these would separate DMI-driven reconstruction from charge transfer and intermixing, and set the work clearly apart from ref. 26.
3. Add single-film controls of 3 and 4 u.c. matching the total bilayer thickness, plus multiple samples per configuration.
4. Strengthen the theory: a U-dependence study, energies with and without the DMI, a constrained-magnetic-order calculation for the bilayer, and a direct band comparison with the ARPES dispersions.
