Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The paper reports that bilayers of ultrathin SrIrO3 (SIO) and SrRuO3 (SRO), each insulating at 1–2 unit cells (u.c.), become metallic and show ferromagnetic hysteresis. In-situ ARPES shows a Fermi surface with a hole pocket at X. DFT+U and a spin model attribute the change to a staggered interfacial Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction (DMI), which would turn SRO ferromagnetic and SIO collinear antiferromagnetic. Interfacial magnetic interactions as a route to an insulator–metal transition in spin–orbit oxides is a timely idea. However, metallicity in SIO/SRO has been reported before (Nelson et al., ref. 26). The new element is the DMI-driven magnetic mechanism, and that rests almost entirely on calculations. The experiments do not yet separate it from simpler explanations: total-thickness effects, intermixing, or charge transfer.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- Three heterostructure configurations are compared, with transport, magnetometry and in-situ ARPES on the same growth series. Two single-layer references (2-u.c. SIO, 2-u.c. SRO) are included.
- The in-situ ARPES (Fig. 3) is of good quality for films this thin. It shows a Fermi surface and a quasiparticle peak at X in all three heterostructures, with a clear contrast to the gapped single films (Fig. 2C, D).
- The proposed staggered-DMI mechanism is physically motivated and could be tested. If confirmed, it would be a useful design principle.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **Metallicity persists far above the magnetic ordering temperature, which contradicts the central causal claim.**
   - *What the paper says:* metallicity is "a direct consequence of DMI-driven magnetic reconstruction" (Fig. 1 caption) and appears "only when ferromagnetism is induced" (Introduction).
   - *What the data show:* in Fig. 2A, all three heterostructures have positive dρ/dT and low resistivity (~0.2–0.4 mΩ cm) from 10 K up to 300 K. The ferromagnetic transition is placed at 102–123 K (kinks in ρ(T); M(T) in SI Fig. S3A). Above T_C the system is paramagnetic, so no FM order is present, yet the bilayers stay metallic. The conductivity therefore cannot be a consequence of long-range FM order.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Reconcile this. Either the metallicity comes from the interface/hybridization (or thickness) itself, with FM order as a by-product, or a short-range or fluctuating magnetic mechanism has to be argued explicitly.
     - Ideally, add ARPES above T_C (e.g. 150–200 K) to show whether the X-point quasiparticle peak and f_coh change across T_C.
     - Rephrase the causal claims in the abstract, Fig. 1 caption and Discussion to match.

2. **Total-thickness control is missing.**
   - *The problem:* the authors state (Results, p. 2) that SIO and SRO films are insulating "below a critical thickness of 3 u.c.". Yet the heterostructures are 3 u.c. (2SIO/1SRO, 1SIO/2SRO) and 4 u.c. (2SIO/2SRO) thick in total. The authors themselves attribute enhanced metallicity to "increased total thickness" (p. 3). The obvious alternative is that a 3–4 u.c. slab of two bulk-metallic perovskites simply exceeds the dimensional-confinement threshold, with no special interfacial magnetism needed.
   - *What the authors should do:* show ρ(T), M(H) and ARPES for single-layer 3-u.c. and 4-u.c. SRO and SIO (same substrate, cap and growth conditions). Only if these stay insulating while the bilayers are metallic does the result point to an interface-specific mechanism.
   - Similarly, a 2SRO/STO/2SIO or 2SRO/2STO reference would test whether any capping/neighbor layer stabilizes metallicity.

3. **Intermixing and charge transfer are not excluded experimentally.**
   - *Intermixing:* the Discussion concedes "we cannot completely rule out the possibility of elemental interdiffusion". RHEED oscillations (SI Figs. S1, S2) monitor growth rate, not chemical abruptness. In 1–2 u.c. layers, even partial Ir/Ru intermixing produces SrIr1−xRuxO3, a known metal or ferromagnet across a wide composition range. That alone could account for metallicity, FM hysteresis and a T_C near 100 K.
   - *Charge transfer:* this is dismissed by citing a previous study (ref. 31), not with data on these samples.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Provide atomically resolved STEM-HAADF/EELS (or equivalent) on at least one heterostructure.
     - Provide a valence probe (Ir L-edge and Ru L/M-edge XAS, or core-level XPS available in the same ARPES system) to bound Ir/Ru valence changes.

4. **No experimental evidence for the proposed magnetic configuration or for DMI.**
   - *The problem:* the specific state (FM SRO + collinear AFM SIO, chiral canting set by staggered DMI) comes only from calculations. Bulk SQUID magnetometry measures the net moment and cannot distinguish Ru from Ir contributions, nor detect a c-AFM SIO layer or a chiral component.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Add element-specific XMCD at the Ru and Ir edges, or resonant x-ray scattering, to show whether Ir carries a net or staggered moment and whether Ru is ferromagnetic.
     - Add any signature of DMI (e.g. chirality-sensitive transport, a topological-Hall-like contribution, or field-angle-dependent behavior).
     - Absent such data, present the DMI scenario clearly as a hypothesis consistent with, not demonstrated by, the experiment. The Discussion's final paragraph partly does this, but the abstract and Fig. 1 do not.

**B. Should address**

5. **The calculations do not isolate DMI as the cause of the metallic DOS.**
   - *The problem:* Fig. 4 compares isolated films and heterostructures, each in its own ground-state magnetic order. Structure, hybridization, interface termination and magnetic order all change at once.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Compute, for the same heterostructure geometry, the DOS with the magnetic order constrained to the isolated-film orders (nc-AFM SIO, c-AFM SRO) and to the reconstructed order. This would show whether the magnetic order, rather than the interface itself, closes the gap.
     - Give the effective-model Hamiltonian parameters (exchange J, DMI magnitude D, anisotropy) and their origin (fitted to DFT or chosen) in the main text, with D compared to J. DMI is usually a small, SOC-order correction to exchange, so the claim that it drives an AFM-to-FM transition needs a quantitative justification.

6. **The choice of U and its sensitivity.**
   - *The problem:* Methods use U = 5 eV, J_H = 0.15U for Ru, Ir and Ti alike. That is large for 4d/5d ions (typical values are ~1–3 eV for Ru and ~1–2 eV for Ir) and directly controls whether the thin films come out insulating. The text says a range of U_Ir, U_Ru was explored, but the main text does not show it.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Show a phase diagram (gap vs U_Ir, U_Ru) for the single films and the bilayers. This would demonstrate a U window in which films are insulating and bilayers metallic, and would show the magnetic ground state is stable across it.
     - Clarify how geometry relaxed without SOC affects octahedral tilts and hence the DMI.

7. **ARPES layer sensitivity and band assignment.**
   - *The problem:* at hν = 82 eV the probing depth is roughly one unit cell. The paper does not state which layer is on top in each heterostructure (the calculations use STO-SRO-SIO stacking), nor which orbital or element (Ir J_eff = 1/2 or Ru t2g) the X-point hole pocket belongs to.
   - *Why it matters:* the evolution of f_coh and m* across the series cannot be interpreted without this. If SIO is on top in all samples, the spectra mainly reflect SIO plus proximity-induced weight.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - State the stacking order.
     - Compare the measured dispersions with the calculated band structure of the heterostructures.
     - Use photon-energy or polarization dependence to assign orbital character.

8. **Effective-mass trend contradicts the text, and the values are implausibly light.**
   - *Text against figure:* the text says m* "decreases from 0.57 m_e for 2SIO/2SRO to 0.5 m_e and 0.41 m_e for 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/1SRO, further confirming the enhanced metallicity with increased total thickness". But the lightest mass belongs to the thinnest sample (2SIO/1SRO, which has the lowest f_coh and the highest ρ), and the heaviest to the thickest and most metallic. The trend runs opposite to the claim.
   - *Fit concerns:*
     - The masses are far below the several m_e typical of SRO.
     - The fitted band maximum in Fig. 3D lies ~0.1 eV above E_F, so it is extrapolated from a parabolic fit over a narrow range.
   - *What the authors should do:* give fit ranges and uncertainties, and either correct the interpretation or explain why a lighter mass is expected in the less metallic sample.

9. **f_coh extraction has no stated uncertainty or model dependence.**
   - *The problem:* the coherent/incoherent decomposition (Fig. 3 A.iii–C.iii) depends on the line shapes, Fermi-function treatment, background and k-integration window. The step from 26% to 28% is well within plausible systematic error. Only the jump to 55% may be significant.
   - *What the authors should do:* specify the fit functions and free parameters, give error bars, and show robustness to reasonable variations of the model.

10. **Transport and ARPES samples are in different states.**
    - *The problem:* ARPES is done in situ on uncapped films, but transport and SQUID samples carry a 15-u.c. STO cap and a post-capping anneal. Capping can change the surface/interface termination, oxygen stoichiometry and band bending of a 3–4 u.c. stack.
    - *What the authors should do:*
      - State whether the single-layer references in Fig. 2A, B were capped identically.
      - Discuss or test (e.g. ARPES of a capped-then-measured sample is not possible, but transport on uncapped samples or XPS before and after capping is) whether the cap affects conductivity.

11. **Magnetometry details and normalization.**
    - *The problem:* moments of 0.6–1 μB/Ru in 1–2 u.c. layers correspond to total moments of order 10⁻⁶ emu on a mm-scale substrate. This is comparable to typical STO-substrate impurity and diamagnetic backgrounds.
    - *What the authors should do:*
      - Describe the background subtraction.
      - Show a bare-substrate-plus-cap reference measured identically.
      - Show the raw M(H) for the isolated 2-u.c. films in the main text (currently only described).
      - Justify normalizing per Ru when the proposed scenario allows Ir to carry moment.
      - Support the PMA claim by showing the in-plane loop alongside the out-of-plane loop.
    - *Saturation trend:* 2SIO/1SRO saturates at 0.6 μB/Ru, while 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/2SRO both reach 1 μB/Ru. Explain this trend within the DMI model.

12. **Reproducibility.**
    - *The problem:* the number of samples grown and measured per configuration is not stated, and there is no sample-to-sample spread for ρ, T_C, saturated moment or f_coh.
    - *What the authors should do:* report how many samples were measured per configuration and show the spread, at least for the transport and magnetometry.

**C. Minor**

13. **Fig. 3 labels.** The caption lists "(A) 1SIO/2SRO, (B) 2SIO/1SRO", but the panels are labelled A = 2SIO/1SRO and B = 1SIO/2SRO. Make them consistent.

14. **Fig. 2A axis and kinks.**
    - The broken, non-linear resistivity axis (3400, 1700, then 0.42, 0.21) makes it hard to compare the curves. Use a log scale or separate panels.
    - The "kinks" at 102–123 K are not visible at this scale. Show dρ/dT and mark T_C.
    - The 2SRO curve starts only around 60–70 K. State the measurable range.
    - Typo: "Temprature".

15. **Gap sizes.** Quantify the gaps of the single films, from ARPES leading-edge positions and from the DFT DOS in Fig. 4A, B, and compare them.

16. **Missing LEED data.** The 2×2 LEED periodicity is used to justify the octahedral rotations in the calculations (Methods), but the LEED patterns are not shown. Include them.

17. **DOS is not conductivity.** "Calculations indicate higher conductivity in 1SIO/2SRO than 2SIO/1SRO" is inferred from the DOS alone. Either compute transport quantities (e.g. a Drude weight or Boltzmann conductivity) or soften the statement.

18. **Comparison with ref. 26.** Place the results more precisely relative to Nelson et al. (ref. 26): sample stacking, thicknesses and their observed magnetism. Make clear what is new beyond the theoretical mechanism.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add temperature-dependent ARPES and transport analysis across T_C, to test directly whether metallicity is tied to magnetic order (concern 1).
2. Add 3- and 4-u.c. single-layer controls and STEM/EELS of the interface. Together these would separate the interfacial mechanism from thickness and intermixing effects (concerns 2–3).
3. Add element-resolved magnetism (XMCD at the Ru and Ir edges) to test the predicted FM-SRO/c-AFM-SIO configuration (concern 4).
4. Add constrained-magnetic-order DFT calculations and a U-sensitivity map, with the model parameters stated. This would show that magnetic reconstruction, not structure or hybridization alone, closes the gap (concerns 5–6).
