Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports that bilayers of ultrathin SrIrO3 (SIO) and SrRuO3 (SRO), each insulating and non-ferromagnetic on its own at 1–2 u.c., become metallic and ferromagnetic when stacked. It supports this with transport, SQUID magnetometry and in-situ ARPES. Using LSDA+U+SOC and a spin model, it argues that a staggered interfacial Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction (DMI) reconstructs the magnetism (nc-AFM to c-AFM in SIO, AFM to FM in SRO), and that this reconstruction produces the metallicity. Metallicity at the SIO/SRO interface was already reported by Nelson et al. (Ref. 26), and there it was attributed to Ir–Ru hybridization and charge transfer. What is new here is the claim that interfacial magnetic reconstruction driven by DMI is the mechanism. That claim is the part least supported by the data shown.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The heterostructures are grown and measured in situ with a combined PLD–ARPES setup. The ARPES (Fig. 3) shows a Fermi surface and a quasiparticle peak at X in all three bilayers, which is direct spectroscopic evidence that metallic states form in a system built from insulating ultrathin layers.
- The data set combines transport, magnetization and photoemission across three thickness combinations (2SIO/1SRO, 1SIO/2SRO, 2SIO/2SRO) and compares each with the single 2 u.c. films.
- Square out-of-plane hysteresis loops (Fig. 2B), together with AHE and MR in the SI, give clear evidence of ferromagnetism with perpendicular anisotropy in the bilayers.
- Interfacial DMI as a handle for coupling magnetic and electronic reconstruction in 5d/4d oxide heterostructures is an interesting idea and worth testing.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **Metallicity persists far above the Curie temperature, which contradicts the proposed causal chain.** In Fig. 2A, ρ(T) of all three heterostructures is metallic (dρ/dT > 0) from 10 K up to 300 K. The text puts the ferromagnetic transition (the kinks in ρ(T)) at 102–123 K. The central claim (abstract; Fig. 1F; "metallic spectral weight appears at the Fermi level only when ferromagnetism is induced") is that metallicity is a consequence of the DMI-driven magnetic reconstruction to FM SRO. Between ~120 K and 300 K the bilayers are paramagnetic yet still metallic. The authors need to explain how a magnetic-order-driven insulator-to-metal transition gives metallic conduction in the paramagnetic phase. Otherwise the paper should present the magnetic reconstruction as concurrent with the metallicity, not as its cause. Temperature-dependent ARPES (or at least EDCs at X) above and below T_C would test the claim directly: if the coherent weight at E_F survives above T_C, the magnetism-first mechanism is not supported.

2. **The calculations do not test whether metallicity requires the reconstructed magnetic state.** Fig. 4 compares DOS of the isolated films in their own ground states (nc-AFM SIO, c-AFM SRO) with the bilayers in the reconstructed state (c-AFM SIO / FM SRO). The decisive control is missing: the bilayer DOS computed with the magnetic configurations of the isolated layers (nc-AFM SIO on c-AFM SRO), with no magnetic order, and with SOC (and hence DMI) switched off. If the bilayer is metallic in those configurations too, the metallicity comes from interface hybridization or charge transfer and not from the magnetic reconstruction. The authors should show these DOS together with the total-energy differences between configurations in the main text. They should also make clear how the "staggered DMI" enters the LSDA+U+SOC result, which contains it only implicitly, versus the model Hamiltonian, and give the DMI magnitude next to the Heisenberg and anisotropy energies.

3. **Charge transfer and simple thickness effects are not ruled out experimentally.** The Discussion rejects the charge-transfer and hybridization picture of Ref. 26 by citing a related study (Ref. 31), not with data on these samples. Two simpler explanations remain open:
   - (i) Ir→Ru charge transfer or interface hybridization creates states at E_F.
   - (ii) The total thickness of the bilayer (3–4 u.c.) reaches the critical thickness for metallicity and ferromagnetism in SRO (Refs. 20, 33, 34), or SIO acts as a metallic-like buffer or cap that relieves the SRO surface/interface dead layer.

   The paper needs to address both:
   - Element-specific spectroscopy (Ir and Ru core levels in XPS, or Ir L-edge and Ru M/L-edge XAS) to bound valence changes.
   - A control bilayer in which SIO is replaced by a non-SOC, non-magnetic perovskite of similar thickness and octahedral connectivity (e.g., 2 u.c. SrTiO3 or SrSnO3 on 1–2 u.c. SRO), measured under the same conditions.
   - Single SRO films of 3 and 4 u.c. grown the same way.

   Without these, the observed metallicity cannot be assigned to the SIO/SRO magnetic interface specifically.

4. **Ferromagnetism is attributed to Ru, and c-AFM order to SIO, without element-resolved evidence.** The magnetization in Fig. 2B is given in μB/Ru, which assumes Ir contributes no net moment. The proposed state has specific orders on both sublattices (FM Ru, c-AFM Ir, ~90° Ir–Ru canting), but none of these is measured. The SIO c-AFM order and the nc-to-c reorientation in particular rest entirely on theory. XMCD at the Ru and Ir edges would test the central magnetic claims, as would resonant x-ray scattering or a magneto-optical probe sensitive to the canted component. Without such data, the abstract and title should say that the DMI picture is a model-based interpretation.

B. Should address

5. **The ARPES trends contradict the text's interpretation.** The text says the effective mass "decreases from 0.57 m_e for 2SIO/2SRO … to 0.41 m_e for 2SIO/1SRO, further confirming the enhanced metallicity with increased total thickness" (p. 3). But the lightest mass belongs to the thinnest, least coherent sample (f_coh = 26%), and the heaviest to the most coherent one (f_coh = 55%). Either the sentence or the reasoning needs correcting. In addition:
   - f_coh (26% → 28% → 55%) does not track resistivity in Fig. 2A, where 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/2SRO have similar, lowest ρ while 2SIO/1SRO is highest. This mismatch should be discussed.
   - In Fig. 3D the band maximum lies ~0.1 eV above E_F. The parabolic fits therefore extrapolate into unoccupied states, and m* depends strongly on the fitting window. The fit range, the uncertainties on m*, and the fit uncertainties on f_coh should be given.
   - The coherent/incoherent decomposition should specify its line shapes, background, Fermi–Dirac cutoff and energy resolution.

6. **The orbital and layer origin of the ARPES bands is not identified.** From the Methods, SIO appears to be grown on top of SRO. At hν = 82 eV the photoemission signal is dominated by the top unit cell(s), so the hole pocket at X may be mainly Ir-derived. The paper does not compare the measured dispersions or Fermi surface with the calculated band structure of the bilayers, and does not use the circular-polarization (dichroic) information it says it collected. The authors should:
   - state the stacking order explicitly;
   - show the DFT band structure along X–Γ–X for each bilayer;
   - assign the band seen in Fig. 3 (J_eff = 1/2 vs Ru t2g).

   This would turn the qualitative "consistency" with theory into a quantitative comparison.

7. **The DFT parameters strongly bias the insulating vs. metallic outcome.** The authors use U = 5 eV and J_H = 0.15U for Ru, Ir and Ti alike (Methods). This value is large for 5d Ir (typically 1.5–2.5 eV) and for Ru 4d. The gaps of the isolated 2 u.c. films, and so the "insulating constituents" baseline of the theory, may be artifacts of this U. The main text should show how the gaps of 2SIO and 2SRO and the bilayer DOS at E_F change with U_Ir and U_Ru, and in particular whether the bilayer stays metallic and the isolated films stay insulating over a physically reasonable range. The abstract mentions a range of U, but no such results appear in the main paper. Fig. 4 also needs fixing: in panel A (2SIO) there seems to be spectral weight close to E_F, so the gap values should be quoted numerically.

8. **The structure underlying the interfacial-DMI mechanism is not characterized.** The staggered DMI is attributed to octahedral rotations and tilts at the interface. The only structural evidence is a 2×2 LEED periodicity, and the Methods admit that "precise structural and magnetic configurations cannot be fully resolved." Interdiffusion is also acknowledged as a possibility, and with 1 u.c. layers it could dominate. The authors should give:
   - cross-sectional STEM-HAADF/EELS (or at least XRD/XRR) on at least one bilayer, to establish layer thickness, sharpness and intermixing;
   - where possible, half-order Bragg peaks to show the octahedral rotation pattern assumed in the calculations.

9. **The samples used for each probe differ, and reproducibility is not reported.** Transport and SQUID samples carry a 15 u.c. STO cap and are measured ex situ. ARPES samples are uncapped and measured in situ. The paper does not say whether the 2 u.c. reference films for transport were capped the same way. A capping layer can itself change the electronic state of 1–2 u.c. SRO, since SRO dead-layer behavior is sensitive to its interfaces. Please:
   - state the capping of every sample;
   - give how many samples of each structure were grown and measured;
   - show sample-to-sample spread in ρ(10 K), T_C and M_s;
   - show at least one capped single-film control with ARPES-comparable conditions.

10. **The quantitative magnetization analysis is thin.**
    - The text gives saturated moments of 0.6 μB/Ru (2SIO/1SRO) and 1 μB/Ru (1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/2SRO). In Fig. 2B, however, all three loops appear to saturate near ~1 μB/Ru, which contradicts the 0.6 μB/Ru value.
    - For 1 u.c. of SRO on a mm-sized STO substrate the signal is tiny. The substrate diamagnetic and paramagnetic subtraction and the error on the moment per Ru should be described.
    - The perpendicular magnetic anisotropy claim rests on out-of-plane loop squareness and MR. An in-plane M–H loop on the same sample is needed to establish the anisotropy directly.

11. **Resistivity normalization and the meaning of "metallic."** It is unclear which thickness was used to convert to ρ (SRO only, or total). For 3–4 u.c. films, sheet resistance compared with h/e² is the more meaningful quantity. Sheet resistance at 10 K should be reported, along with whether any low-temperature upturn appears below 10 K. The kinks at 102–123 K should be matched sample by sample to T_C from M(T) (SI Fig. S3A) in a table.

C. Minor

12. In the caption of Fig. 3, panel A is labeled 1SIO/2SRO and panel B 2SIO/1SRO, but the figure labels row A as 2SIO/1SRO and row B as 1SIO/2SRO. Please make these consistent. Also check that the f_coh values (26/28/55%) are assigned to the right samples.
13. The caption of Fig. 2 calls the 2 u.c. films "monolayers"; they are bilayers of unit cells. In Fig. 2A the axis label "Temprature" is misspelled, and the broken y-axis should be marked more clearly. The single-film curves end at intermediate temperatures without explanation (presumably the measurement limit); please state this.
14. Fig. 2C/2D: give the normalization of the valence-band spectra and a reference Fermi edge (e.g., polycrystalline gold) so that "suppressed spectral weight" and "finite DOS at E_F" can be judged quantitatively.
15. "Insulator-to-metal transition (IMT)" is used for what is a comparison between different samples, not a transition driven by a control parameter. Consider rewording.
16. Figure 1 is a schematic, but it reads like a result (the DOS in panels B, D, F). Please label it as schematic. Also clarify in the Results text that Fig. 1E's spin arrangement is the computed ground state, not one that was measured.
17. The Discussion says the DMI "is tunable through external electric and magnetic fields" (Ref. 45). This is speculative for this system and could be shortened or framed as an outlook.
18. The Data availability statement ("all data are available in the main text") does not cover raw ARPES and transport data. Consider depositing them.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one decisive mechanism test. Options: temperature-dependent ARPES across T_C (Concern 1), and/or DFT DOS of the bilayer in the unreconstructed magnetic configuration and with SOC off (Concern 2). Either would directly show whether magnetic reconstruction is needed for metallicity.
2. Add the non-SOC spacer control bilayer and thicker single-SRO references (Concern 3), to separate the SIO-specific interfacial effect from thickness and capping effects.
3. Add element-resolved magnetism (Ru and Ir XMCD) and cross-sectional STEM/EELS on at least one bilayer (Concerns 4 and 8). These would place the magnetic and structural assumptions on measured ground.
4. Present the U-dependence of the gaps and the DFT band structure next to the ARPES dispersions in the main text (Concerns 6 and 7), so the theory–experiment comparison becomes quantitative.
