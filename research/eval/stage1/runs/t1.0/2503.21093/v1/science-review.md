A. Must address

1. **Interfacial charge transfer is not ruled out, and the manuscript does not engage with Ref. 29.** Ref. 29 (Nelson et al., Sci. Adv. 2022) studied ultrathin SrIrO3/SrRuO3 heterostructures, essentially this system, and attributed their persistent metallicity to interfacial charge transfer between Ir and Ru. The present manuscript cites Ref. 29 only in passing, as background for thickness-driven transitions (p. 4), and never discusses it as an alternative. Charge transfer of a fraction of an electron dopes both layers away from half filling of the Jeff = 1/2 (Ir) and t2g^4 (Ru) manifolds. That alone can close the Mott/Slater gaps and produce metallicity without any change of magnetic order, and SRO could then order as an itinerant ferromagnet as a consequence of the metallicity rather than its cause. On this reading DMI plays no role, and the causal direction claimed in the title and abstract (DMI as "the key driving force"; the transition "driven by interface-induced magnetic reconstruction") does not follow. The authors should: (i) measure the Ir and Ru valence on the same in-situ samples, e.g. Ir L-edge and Ru L/M-edge XAS, or Ir 4f and Ru 3d core levels in the photoemission setup they already use; (ii) report the layer-resolved charges (Bader or orbital occupations), d occupancies and moments from their DFT for the bilayers and the isolated films, with and without the magnetic reconstruction; (iii) compute the heterostructure DOS with charge transfer present but the magnetic order held fixed in the single-film configurations (nc-AFM SIO + c-AFM SRO); and (iv) explain explicitly why the present result is new relative to Ref. 29 and why DMI, rather than doping, is the driver.

2. **The bilayers are metallic far above the magnetic ordering temperature, which is hard to reconcile with ferromagnetic reconstruction causing the IMT.** Fig. 2A shows all three heterostructures with resistivities of about 0.2–0.45 mΩ cm and positive dρ/dT all the way to 300 K, while the text places the ferromagnetic transition at kinks at 102–123 K. Between about 120 K and 300 K the bilayers are therefore metallic while paramagnetic, with resistivity roughly four orders of magnitude below that of the 2-u.c. single films. This conflicts with the central claim (Abstract; Sec. A: "The emergence of metallicity … is therefore a direct outcome of the DMI-driven magnetic reconstruction"; Discussion: "DMI dominates … thereby driving magnetic reconstruction and the emergence of metallicity") and is equally consistent with the reverse causality: metallicity arises for another reason and SRO then orders as an itinerant ferromagnet, as thicker SRO does. The authors should either argue, with evidence such as M(T) or magnetoresistance above Tc, that short-range or fluctuating order above Tc suffices; or show by DFT in a disordered/paramagnetic or nonmagnetic configuration of the heterostructure that the bilayer would be insulating without order; or provide a field- or temperature-tuned measurement showing that the conductivity changes when the magnetic state changes. Otherwise the title claim must be rephrased.

3. **The DFT counterfactual needed to support "metallic only when ferromagnetic" is missing.** The Introduction (p. 4) states that "electronic states at the Fermi level emerge exclusively when ferromagnetism is induced by interface-driven magnetic reconstruction", but Sec. D compares only the single films (c-AFM SRO, nc-AFM SIO) with the heterostructures in the reconstructed state (c-AFM SIO + FM SRO); Fig. 4C,D show only that reconstructed case. No gapped AFM heterostructure DOS is shown. The decisive calculation is the same SIO/SRO slab, at the same structure, constrained to the unreconstructed order (nc-AFM SIO / c-AFM SRO, c-AFM SIO / c-AFM SRO and other AFM SRO arrangements), and to non-magnetic and fully FM (FM/FM) configurations, together with the total energies of all these configurations showing that the proposed c-AFM-SIO / FM-SRO state is the ground state. Without this, the metallic DOS in Fig. 4C,D shows only that the reconstructed slab is metallic, which could simply reflect band alignment and charge transfer in a thicker slab, not that the reconstruction makes it metallic.

4. **DMI is asserted, not computed or isolated, as the driving interaction.** The "effective model Hamiltonian" is invoked several times (pp. 4, 6, 7) but described only in words. The manuscript never gives the Hamiltonian's form, the exchange constants, the DMI magnitude or its direction on the Ru–Ir bonds, the anisotropies, or how these were obtained (fitted to DFT energies or assumed). DMI is a relativistic correction usually a few percent of the symmetric exchange; converting the AFM order of the isolated films into FM SRO by DMI alone requires D comparable to J, and this has to be shown. The Discussion also invokes competing Ru–Ir anisotropic coupling, magnetocrystalline anisotropy and DMI, none of them quantified. The authors should write out the model, tabulate J, D and K extracted from DFT energies of competing configurations, give the magnetic phase diagram of the model as a function of D/J, and perform a control in which DMI is removed: DFT with SOC switched off (or with the DMI-active octahedral rotations suppressed) at fixed structure, and the model solved with D = 0, showing that the FM metallic state disappears.

5. **Total thickness and the SRO critical thickness are confounded with the interface effect.** The insulating references are 2 u.c., while the bilayers contain 3 u.c. (2SIO/1SRO, 1SIO/2SRO) or 4 u.c. (2SIO/2SRO) of conducting perovskite. The ferromagnetic-metal critical thickness of SrRuO3 on SrTiO3 is reported at about 2–4 u.c. (literature values up to 3–5 u.c.), depending on growth, termination and capping (Refs. 25, 26, 28); the manuscript itself places the critical thickness at about 3 u.c., and its Fig. 3 discussion attributes the trends to "increased total thickness". The trend of conductivity with SRO content (Fig. 2A, Sec. C) also fits a pure thickness reading. The authors should grow, cap and measure identically (transport, SQUID, ARPES) 3- and 4-u.c. single SRO and SIO films, and ideally a structure in which the Ir–Ru interface is absent but thickness is matched: a bilayer with a non-magnetic spacer (e.g. 2SIO/1 u.c. STO/2SRO), or 2SRO capped by a non-magnetic weak-SOC perovskite of similar lattice parameter (SrTiO3, SrHfO3, SrZrO3) in place of SIO, or SIO on SrSnO3. If such a structure is metallic, the role of SIO-specific DMI is called into question. Without these controls the IMT cannot be attributed to the interface, still less to its magnetism.

6. **There is no structural or chemical characterisation of the interfaces.** No XRD/XRR, reciprocal space map, STEM/EELS, RHEED oscillations or LEED patterns are shown, although RHEED and LEED are mentioned in Methods. Ru/Ir intermixing at a 1–2 u.c. interface would give a SrRu1−xIrxO3 alloy, which is metallic over a wide composition range and can be ferromagnetic, explaining the results without any magnetic reconstruction. Ru volatility (RuO4 loss) also affects stoichiometry in ultrathin SRO. The DMI mechanism further depends on specific octahedral tilts and rotations across the interface, and the DFT was relaxed with a fixed STO in-plane constant and SrO termination that are not established for the real samples. The authors should show RHEED oscillations for each layer, the LEED patterns including half-order superstructure spots indicating the rotation pattern, XRD showing thickness and coherency, and atomically resolved cross-sectional HAADF-STEM with EELS line profiles/maps of Ir and Ru, establishing actual layer counts, interface sharpness and the termination of each interface.

7. **The magnetic state of each layer, and of the reference films, is not measured.** The mechanism specifies c-AFM SIO and FM SRO in the bilayer versus nc-AFM SIO and c-AFM SRO in the single films (Fig. 1), but the experiment provides only a net M–H loop of the whole bilayer (Fig. 2B). No magnetisation data are shown for the 2SIO and 2SRO reference films; their antiferromagnetism is taken from literature and DFT, and for 2-u.c. SRO it is debated (Ref. 25 reports loss of ferromagnetism; AFM order there is largely a theory prediction, Ref. 24 being itself a DFT study). The headline "antiferromagnetic insulator → ferromagnetic metal" therefore rests on an assumption for both reference films. The authors should measure M–H and M(T) of the 2SIO and 2SRO films under identical conditions, including the STO cap, and provide element-specific evidence for the layer-resolved spin arrangement: XMCD at the Ir L2,3 and Ru L/M edges on bilayers and single films (does Ir carry a net moment or is it c-AFM; is the Ru moment out of plane?), and ideally linear dichroism or resonant scattering for the SIO AFM component and a probe of canting/chirality.

8. **The ferromagnetism, moment values and Curie temperature of the bilayers need stronger support.** Only 10-K loops in a single, unstated field orientation are shown (Fig. 2B). The quoted moments, 0.6 μB/Ru for 2SIO/1SRO and 1 μB/Ru for the others (p. 5), approach bulk SRO although SRO films this thin on STO are normally non-ferromagnetic, and the signal from a 1–2 u.c. layer on a ~0.5 mm STO substrate with a 15-u.c. STO cap sits near the SQUID floor on a large diamagnetic background. Normalising per Ru assumes Ir carries no net moment, contradicting the authors' own model in which Ir moments are reorganised and canted by DMI. The authors should: describe the diamagnetic and paramagnetic background subtraction and how sample area/volume was determined; show raw data and a bare, processed STO substrate with cap as a null control; address any Ir contribution and compare with the DFT moments; give the field orientation and add in-plane loops to support the claimed out-of-plane easy axis; and present M(T), or remanence versus T, to tie Tc to the ρ(T) kinks at 102–123 K. Anomalous Hall loops tracking Fig. 2B would show that the ferromagnetism resides in the conducting channel.

B. Should address

9. **The transport/SQUID samples and the ARPES samples are not equivalent.** Transport and SQUID samples carry a 15-u.c. STO cap deposited before the 1-mbar anneal and are measured ex situ; ARPES samples are uncapped and measured in situ (Methods, pp. 8–9). A polar or strain-imposing cap can itself change the ground state of a 1–2 u.c. layer and alters the octahedral rotations, termination and symmetry at the top surface on which the DMI mechanism depends. It is not stated whether the 2SIO and 2SRO reference films in Fig. 2A were capped and annealed identically (which also matters for excluding substrate conduction), or whether ARPES samples were annealed identically. The authors should give the full history of every sample and ideally show that capped and uncapped bilayers give consistent results, or that capped reference films remain insulating.

10. **The layer and orbital origin of the ARPES states is not identified.** At hν = 82 eV the probing depth is only a few Å (≲1 nm), so in these SIO-on-top bilayers (SRO grown first) the spectra in Figs. 2C,D and 3 are dominated by the top SIO layer and one interface; ARPES shows the SIO surface layer is metallic but not the state of the SRO layer. The claim of a "metallized ground state in the SIO top layer" (p. 4) and the assignment of the X-point hole pocket need support. The authors should state the stacking order explicitly; separate Ir from Ru character through photon-energy dependence (e.g. near Cooper minima), resonant photoemission or polarisation dependence; compare the measured Fermi surface and dispersions with DFT band structures unfolded onto the same Brillouin zone (with the octahedral-rotation supercell) for the proposed and competing magnetic configurations, since at present the theory gives only DOS; and ideally measure a bilayer with reversed stacking (SRO on top).

11. **The effective-mass analysis runs opposite to the conclusion drawn from it (p. 6, Fig. 3D).** The text says m* "decreases from 0.57 me for 2SIO/2SRO to 0.5 me and 0.41 me for 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/1SRO, further confirming the enhanced metallicity with increased total thickness". But the thickest and most conducting sample (2SIO/2SRO) has the heaviest mass, and 2SIO/1SRO, the most resistive in Fig. 2A, the lightest; a larger m* usually indicates stronger correlation, not enhanced metallicity. The band is hole-like with its maximum ~0.1 eV above EF, yet m* is called an "electron effective mass", and the parabolic fits extrapolate that maximum from MDC peaks spanning roughly −0.05 to −0.22 eV, so the masses depend strongly on fit constraints; no uncertainties are given, and the differences between 0.41, 0.50 and 0.57 me may be within error. The authors should state fit ranges, free parameters and uncertainties, compare with the DFT band mass, reconcile the m* trend with the transport and coherent-weight trends (e.g. in terms of mass renormalisation relative to DFT or coherent weight Z), and otherwise remove the claim that m* tracks metallicity.

12. **The DFT parameter choices are not justified and their robustness is not tested (Methods D).** U = 5 eV with JH = 0.15U is applied to Ru 4d and Ir 5d; typical values for these extended orbitals are about 1–3 eV, and so large a U is the choice most likely to open gaps in the films and favour particular magnetic states. The Results (p. 4) say ground states were studied "for a range of electron correlation strengths (U_Ir and U_Ru)", but no such scan is shown. Relaxations were done without SOC, although the DMI argument relies on octahedral distortions that can couple to SOC in iridates. The slabs are SrO-terminated with vacuum and model neither the 15-u.c. STO cap of the transport samples nor the exposed SIO surface in ARPES; the SrO termination should be reconciled with that expected after growth on TiO2-terminated STO (Ref. 28). Methods say "SRO-SIO layers were varied with 2 to 4 u.c.", but Fig. 4 shows only 3-u.c. bilayers and omits 2SIO/2SRO, the sample with the clearest ARPES. The authors should show the magnetic ground state and gap of films and bilayers versus U_Ir and U_Ru, demonstrate that "insulating film, metallic bilayer" survives at conventional U, check that relaxation with SOC does not change the tilts, and add 2SIO/2SRO.

13. **The DOS-based claims in Fig. 4 do not match the text, and DOS is equated with conductivity.** Fig. 4B (2SRO) shows a large dxy peak just below EF with weight running up to about 0, so the "clear gap" (p. 7) is not evident on the plotted scale. Fig. 4D shows little SIO weight near EF in the 1SIO layer. The text infers "higher conductivity in the 1SIO/2SRO heterostructure compared to 2SIO/1SRO" (p. 7) from DOS alone, but finite DOS at EF does not determine conductivity. The authors should give gap values, total and layer-resolved DOS and N(EF) on a scale where the gap or its absence is visible, and either compute a transport-relevant quantity (band velocities, Drude weight or a Boltzmann estimate) or limit the statement to DOS at EF.

15. **The resistivity kinks are not visible, and the low-temperature upturn is not discussed.** The kinks at 102–123 K assigned to the PM–FM transition of SRO are not visible on the plotted scale of Fig. 2A; the authors should show dρ/dT, list the kink temperature for each sample and correlate it with M(T). 16. **Transport analysis details are missing.** The bilayer resistivities (~0.2–0.4 mΩ cm) are comparable to thick SRO films, which is remarkable for 3–4 u.c. The authors should state which thickness (full bilayer or one layer) converts sheet resistance to ρ, and show sheet resistance (Ω/□) compared with h/e², the 2D IMT scale.
