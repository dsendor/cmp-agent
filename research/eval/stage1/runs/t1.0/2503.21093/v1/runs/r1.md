Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports that bilayers of ultrathin SrIrO3 (1–2 u.c.) and SrRuO3 (1–2 u.c.) on SrTiO3 are metallic and ferromagnetic, while 2-u.c. films of either material alone are insulating. Evidence comes from van der Pauw transport, SQUID hysteresis loops and in-situ ARPES. Using LSDA+U+SOC slab calculations and a model Hamiltonian, the authors attribute the effect to a staggered interfacial Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI) that turns the antiferromagnetic insulators into a ferromagnetic metal. A magnetism-driven insulator-to-metal transition at an oxide interface would interest a broad audience. However, the experiments establish metallicity and ferromagnetism in the bilayers, not the proposed mechanism. The basic observation of persistent metallicity in ultrathin SIO/SRO heterostructures was already reported (Ref. 29, Nelson et al., Sci. Adv. 2022), and that work attributed it to interfacial charge transfer.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Matched sets of single films and bilayers grown under the same nominal PLD conditions, with transport, magnetometry and in-situ ARPES on the same set of nominal structures.
- The in-situ ARPES (Fig. 3) shows a clear Fermi surface, a hole-like pocket at X and a coherent quasiparticle peak in the bilayers. This is direct spectroscopic evidence of metallicity in structures only 3–4 u.c. thick.
- The SQUID loops (Fig. 2B) show hysteresis with sizeable remanence in all three bilayers. This sets them apart from the reference films, which are reported as non-ferromagnetic.
- The idea that a symmetry-allowed interfacial DMI could reorganise magnetic order in correlated SOC oxides is worth exploring, and the DFT framework could test it.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Interfacial charge transfer is not ruled out, and the mechanism claim does not engage with Ref. 29. Ref. 29 (Nelson et al.) reported Ir→Ru charge transfer and persistent metallicity in ultrathin SIO/SRO heterostructures. It is cited only in passing (p. 4) and never discussed. Charge transfer dopes both layers away from half filling of the Jeff = 1/2 (Ir) and t2g^4 (Ru) manifolds, which can by itself close Mott/Slater gaps and stabilise itinerant ferromagnetism in SRO. That explanation would not require DMI at all. The authors should:
   - report the layer-resolved charges, d occupancies and moments from their DFT, for the bilayer and for the isolated films;
   - measure the valence states experimentally, for example with Ir L- and Ru L/M-edge XAS or core-level (Ir 4f, Ru 3d) photoemission on the same in-situ samples;
   - explain why the present result is new relative to Ref. 29.
   Without this, the abstract's statement that DMI is "the key driving force" is not supported.

2. No experiment tests the DMI mechanism, and the calculations do not isolate it. The DMI-driven reconstruction (c-AFM SIO plus FM SRO, with the Ir and Ru spins at 90°) rests only on the theory.
   - Experiment: no element-specific magnetic probe was used. Ir and Ru L-edge XMCD would test whether Ir carries a net moment or is c-AFM, and whether the Ru moment is out of plane. No probe of chirality or canting was used either.
   - Theory: the model Hamiltonian is described only in words (pp. 4, 6). No Hamiltonian, exchange constants, D vector magnitude, or method of extracting these from DFT is given. The authors should show that DMI, a relativistic correction usually much smaller than the Heisenberg exchange, actually decides the ground state. Concretely, they should:
     - give total energies of competing magnetic configurations in the bilayer slab, including nc-AFM SIO with c-AFM SRO, c-AFM SIO with c-AFM SRO, FM/FM, and the proposed c-AFM/FM;
     - extract J and D from those energies;
     - show that the gap closes only when the FM configuration is imposed. The Introduction claims (p. 4) that states at EF emerge "exclusively when ferromagnetism is induced", but no gapped AFM heterostructure DOS is shown to support it.
   A further control would be a calculation with SOC switched off, or with the DMI-active octahedral rotations suppressed, showing that the FM metallic state disappears.

3. Total thickness and SRO critical thickness are confounded with the interface effect. The bilayers have 3–4 u.c. of conducting perovskite in total, whereas the insulating references are 2 u.c. The ferromagnetic-metal critical thickness of SrRuO3 on SrTiO3 is reported at about 2–4 u.c. depending on growth and termination (Refs. 25, 26, 28). In addition, the authors' own Fig. 3 discussion attributes the trends to "increased total thickness". The controls needed are:
   - 3-u.c. and 4-u.c. single SRO and SIO films, grown and capped identically and measured by transport, SQUID and ARPES;
   - ideally, a bilayer in which one layer is replaced by a non-magnetic, weak-SOC perovskite of similar lattice parameter (for example SrTiO3 spacer/SRO, or SrSnO3/SIO), to separate the "added unit cell" effect from the specifically SIO/SRO interface.
   Without these, the IMT cannot be attributed to the interface, still less to its magnetism.

4. The magnetic state of the reference films and the magnetic data for the bilayers are not adequately established.
   - The premise that 2-u.c. SIO is nc-AFM and 2-u.c. SRO is c-AFM rests on DFT and literature (Ref. 24 is itself a DFT study). No magnetisation data are shown for the 2SIO and 2SRO reference films. These are needed to back the "AFM insulator to FM metal" narrative.
   - For the bilayers, only 10-K loops in a single field orientation are shown (Fig. 2B). Yet the text asserts:
     - an out-of-plane easy axis, which requires in-plane loops as well;
     - a paramagnetic-to-ferromagnetic transition at 102–123 K, which requires M(T) or remanence versus T to tie the ρ(T) kinks to Tc.
   - The substrate background subtraction for a 1–2 u.c. film (a diamagnetic STO substrate plus a 15-u.c. STO cap) should be described, since the signal is tiny.
   - Normalising "per Ru" assigns the whole moment to Ru. Any Ir contribution, which the model says is compensated, should be addressed.
   - The 1 μB/Ru for a 2-u.c. SRO layer is close to thick-film values. This needs comment, together with comparison to the DFT moments.

B. Should address

5. There is no structural or chemical characterisation of the interface. No XRD, reciprocal space map, STEM/EELS, RHEED oscillation, or LEED pattern is shown; LEED is mentioned in Methods but not presented. Ru/Ir intermixing at a 1–2 u.c. interface would produce SrRu1−xIrxO3, which is metallic over a wide composition range and would explain metallicity without any magnetic reconstruction. Ru volatility (RuO4 loss) also affects stoichiometry in ultrathin SRO. The authors should show atomically resolved STEM with EELS line profiles, and the RHEED oscillations for each layer, to establish the actual layer counts and interface sharpness. The octahedral tilt pattern that underpins the staggered DMI argument should also be characterised, for example by half-order Bragg peaks or LEED superstructure spots.

6. The transport and ARPES samples are not equivalent. Transport and SQUID samples carry a 15-u.c. STO cap deposited before the 1-mbar anneal. ARPES samples are uncapped and measured in situ (Methods, pp. 8–9). Also:
   - It is not stated whether the 2SIO and 2SRO reference films used in transport were capped and annealed identically.
   - Capping changes octahedral rotations, the termination and the symmetry at the top surface. The proposed DMI mechanism depends on exactly these.
   The authors should state the history of every sample and, ideally, show that a capped bilayer and an uncapped bilayer give consistent transport, or that capped reference films remain insulating.

7. Fig. 3 has a labelling inconsistency, and the effective-mass argument runs backwards.
   - The Fig. 3 panels are labelled A = 2SIO/1SRO and B = 1SIO/2SRO, while the caption assigns (A) to 1SIO/2SRO and (B) to 2SIO/1SRO. This makes it unclear which EDC belongs to which sample, and hence whether the claimed rise in coherent weight with SRO thickness holds.
   - The text (p. 6) says m* "decreases from 0.57 me for 2SIO/2SRO to 0.5 and 0.41 me" and that this confirms "enhanced metallicity with increased total thickness". But the thickest sample has the heaviest mass. A larger m* usually indicates stronger correlation, not enhanced metallicity.
   - The parabolic fits extrapolate a band maximum about 0.1 eV above EF from data below about −0.05 eV. The band is hole-like, yet it is called an "electron" mass.
   The authors should:
   - fix the labels;
   - give fit ranges and uncertainties for m* (the differences between 0.41, 0.50 and 0.57 me may be within error);
   - reconcile the m* trend with the transport trend and with the coherent-weight trend.

8. The orbital and layer origin of the ARPES states is not identified. At hν = 82 eV and with UV-range surface sensitivity, the spectra are dominated by the top SIO layer and the interface. The claim of a "metallized ground state in the SIO top layer" (p. 4) needs support:
   - Photon-energy dependence, for example near the Ir 5d or Ru 4d Cooper minima, or resonant photoemission, should separate Ir from Ru character.
   - The measured Fermi surface and the X-point hole pocket should be compared with the DFT band structure (unfolded onto the same Brillouin zone) for the proposed c-AFM/FM configuration and for the competing ones. At present the theory gives only DOS, so no quantitative comparison with ARPES is made.

9. The DFT parameter choices are not justified and their robustness is not tested.
   - Methods use U = 5 eV and JH = 0.15U on Ru, Ir and Ti. This is large for Ir 5d and Ru 4d, where typical values are about 1.5–3 eV, and it is the choice most likely to open gaps in the films and to favour particular magnetic states.
   - The Results (p. 4) say the films were studied "for a range of electron correlation strengths (U_Ir and U_Ru)", but no such scan is shown.
   - Relaxations were done without SOC. However, the DMI argument depends on the octahedral distortions, which can couple to SOC in iridates.
   The authors should show the phase diagram versus U_Ir and U_Ru, with the magnetic ground state and gap for the films and the bilayers. They should also show that the bilayer conclusion survives at more conventional U.

10. The DOS-based claims in Fig. 4 do not match the text.
    - Fig. 4B (2SRO) shows a large dxy peak just below EF with weight running up to about 0. A "clear gap" (p. 7) is not evident on the plotted scale.
    - Fig. 4D shows almost no SIO weight below EF in the 1SIO layer.
    - The text infers "higher conductivity in 1SIO/2SRO compared to 2SIO/1SRO" (p. 7) from the DOS alone. DOS at EF is not conductivity.
    The authors should give gap values, total and layer-resolved DOS on a scale where the gap or its absence is visible, and band velocities or a Boltzmann/Drude estimate if conductivity is to be compared.

11. The resistivity values and the kinks need clarification.
    - The bilayer resistivities (about 0.2–0.4 mΩ cm, Fig. 2A) are comparable to thick SRO films, which is remarkable for 3–4 u.c. The thickness used to convert sheet resistance to ρ should be stated, and sheet resistance compared with h/e².
    - The "kinks at 102 to 123 K" are not visible on the plotted scale. dρ/dT should be shown.
    - The 2SIO/1SRO curve has a resistivity minimum and an upturn at low T, which should be discussed (weak localisation or Kondo-like behaviour versus incipient insulating behaviour).
    - The 2SRO curve stops near 60 K and the 2SIO curve near 70 K. The temperature at which they exceed the measurement range should be stated.

12. Reproducibility is not shown. Each configuration appears to come from a single sample. Given how sensitive ultrathin SRO and SIO are to stoichiometry and termination, the authors should report how many samples of each structure were grown and measured, and show the spread in ρ(10 K), moment and coercive field.

13. The magnetic configuration is described inconsistently across the paper.
    - Section A says the DMI makes SIO collinear and SRO FM.
    - Section D says the staggered DMI "favors a noncollinear magnetic phase between SIO and SRO".
    - The Discussion says Ir magnetocrystalline anisotropy favours out-of-plane spins. Yet Fig. 1E shows the Ir spins in plane, and the Fig. 4 insets differ again.
    - The SQUID out-of-plane easy axis is attributed to the SRO layer without discussing the Ir sublattice.
    A single, explicit statement of the computed ground-state spin configuration (directions and moments of each ion, layer by layer) is needed, and it should be checked against the measured anisotropy.

C. Minor

14. "Insulator-to-metal transition" describes a comparison between different samples, not a transition driven by a control parameter in one system. "Emergent metallicity" or "interface-induced metallic state" would be more accurate.

15. Fig. 1 is a schematic, yet the text (p. 5) cites Fig. 1F as indicating "a finite density of states at the Fermi level". It should be marked clearly as schematic, or replaced by computed DOS.

16. Smaller text and figure points:
    - Fig. 2A axis label "Temprature" should read "Temperature".
    - The broken y-axis in Fig. 2A hides the scale of the reference films; a log scale would be clearer.
    - Fig. 2C/D spectra need a stated normalisation.
    - The Introduction says the ultrathin SRO is "collinear antiferromagnetic insulating" (Refs. 20–27). Several of these references concern Ca3Ru2O7 or Ca2RuO4, not SrRuO3 films, and should be checked.

17. Methods omit:
    - growth rates and the RHEED evidence for each layer;
    - van der Pauw contact geometry and current;
    - SQUID field orientation;
    - ARPES energy and momentum resolution;
    - the Brillouin zone convention used in Fig. 3 (M, Γ, X in a rotated zone).
    The DFT slab terminations (SrO) should be reconciled with the actual surface termination expected after growth on TiO2-terminated STO (Ref. 28).

18. Ref. 48 has garbled author names ("K. G and F. J"). The "Data availability" statement ("All data are available in the main text") is insufficient for the raw ARPES and DFT data.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add element-specific spectroscopy on the same in-situ samples: Ir and Ru L-edge XAS/XMCD, plus core-level photoemission. This would determine valence (charge transfer) and the moment on each ion together, and directly test the c-AFM-SIO/FM-SRO picture against the charge-transfer scenario of Ref. 29.
2. Grow the thickness-matched and spacer controls: 3- and 4-u.c. single SRO and SIO films, and SRO/STO or SIO/non-magnetic spacer bilayers. Measure them alongside the existing samples, with several samples per structure.
3. Make the theory quantitative and falsifiable:
   - write out the model Hamiltonian;
   - tabulate J and D extracted from DFT energies of competing configurations;
   - show the U dependence;
   - compare unfolded DFT bands with the measured Fermi surface and dispersions.
4. Strengthen the magnetism-transport link:
   - M(T) and in-plane versus out-of-plane loops for every sample;
   - dρ/dT showing Tc;
   - anomalous Hall effect, which would show that the ferromagnetism resides in the conducting layer.
