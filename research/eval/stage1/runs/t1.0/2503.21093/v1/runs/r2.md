Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports that bilayers of ultrathin SrIrO3 (SIO) and SrRuO3 (SRO) on SrTiO3, each layer only 1–2 u.c. thick, are metallic and ferromagnetic, while 2-u.c. single films of either material are insulating. The authors attribute this to an interfacial, staggered Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction (DMI). In their picture the DMI reconstructs the magnetic order (SIO from nc-AFM to c-AFM, SRO from c-AFM to FM), and the metallicity follows from that. An insulator-to-metal transition driven purely by magnetic reconstruction at an interface would be an interesting and broadly relevant result. However, the experiments shown (resistivity, a SQUID loop at 10 K, ARPES) establish only that the bilayers are metallic and carry a remanent moment. The proposed mechanism and its causal direction rest on schematic DFT results, and these do not test the mechanism against the obvious alternatives: interfacial charge transfer, total-thickness effects and intermixing.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The material system is topical. A clean demonstration of emergent metallicity and ferromagnetism in 3–4 u.c. SIO/SRO bilayers would interest the oxide-interface and spin–orbit-correlated communities.
- The experimental approach is complementary: transport and SQUID on capped samples, plus in-situ ARPES on samples grown in the same system without air exposure. This is a strength.
- The ARPES data in Fig. 3 show a Fermi surface and a quasiparticle peak at X in all three bilayers. This is direct spectroscopic evidence of a metallic state in at least the top layer.
- Three bilayer stacking ratios were studied, which gives some systematic variation of the result.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The bilayers are metallic far above the magnetic ordering temperature. This contradicts the claim that ferromagnetic reconstruction causes the IMT.**
   - Fig. 2A shows all three heterostructures with resistivities of about 0.2–0.45 mΩ·cm and positive dρ/dT all the way to 300 K.
   - The text places the ferromagnetic transition at the kinks, at 102–123 K.
   - So between about 120 K and 300 K the bilayers are metallic while paramagnetic. Their resistivity is roughly four orders of magnitude below that of the 2-u.c. single films.
   - This is hard to reconcile with the central claim (Abstract; Sec. A: "The emergence of metallicity … is therefore a direct outcome of the DMI-driven magnetic reconstruction"; Discussion: "DMI dominates … thereby driving magnetic reconstruction and the emergence of metallicity").
   - It is equally consistent with the reverse causality: metallicity arises for some other reason, and the SRO layer then orders ferromagnetically as an itinerant ferromagnet, as thicker SRO does.
   - The authors should address this directly:
     - by arguing that short-range or fluctuating order above Tc is enough, with evidence such as M(T), or magnetoresistance above Tc;
     - or by showing that the bilayer would be insulating in the paramagnetic state, through DFT in a disordered or nonmagnetic configuration of the heterostructure.
   - Otherwise the title claim must be rephrased.

2. **Interfacial charge transfer is not considered, although the authors cite it.**
   - Ref. 29 (Nelson et al., Sci. Adv. 2022) reports interfacial charge transfer from Ir to Ru, and persistent metallicity, in ultrathin SIO/SRO heterostructures — essentially this system.
   - Charge transfer dopes both Mott/Slater-insulating layers. That alone could produce both metallicity and SRO ferromagnetism, without any role for DMI.
   - The manuscript cites Ref. 29 only for thickness-driven transitions and does not discuss this mechanism.
   - The authors should:
     - quantify the charge transfer: Ir and Ru valence from XAS/XPS core levels (Ir 4f, Ru 3d), or Ir/Ru d occupancies from the DFT slabs;
     - compute the heterostructure DOS with charge transfer present but the magnetic order held fixed in the single-film configurations (nc-AFM SIO + c-AFM SRO);
     - explain why DMI, rather than doping, is the driver.

3. **The DFT counterfactual that would support "metallic only when FM" is missing.**
   - The Introduction states that "electronic states at the Fermi level emerge exclusively when ferromagnetism is induced by interface-driven magnetic reconstruction".
   - But Sec. D says the comparison covers only the single films (c-AFM SRO, nc-AFM SIO) and the heterostructures in the reconstructed state (c-AFM SIO + FM SRO). Fig. 4C–D show only that reconstructed case.
   - The decisive calculation is absent: the same SIO/SRO slab, with the same structure, constrained to the unreconstructed order (nc-AFM SIO / c-AFM SRO, and other AFM SRO arrangements).
   - Also missing are the total energies of the competing magnetic configurations, which would show that the FM-SRO / c-AFM-SIO state is the ground state.
   - Without these, the DOS in Fig. 4 shows only that the reconstructed slab is metallic, not that the reconstruction is what makes it metallic.

4. **DMI is asserted, not computed or isolated, as the driving interaction.**
   - The manuscript never gives:
     - the DMI magnitude, its direction for the Ru–Ir bonds, or a comparison with the Ru–Ir symmetric exchange and the single-ion anisotropy;
     - the model Hamiltonian (form, parameters, how the parameters were extracted);
     - any calculation in which DMI is switched off (for example, SOC removed on the interface bonds, or the model solved with D = 0).
   - DMI is usually a small fraction of the symmetric exchange. Converting AFM SRO to FM SRO by DMI alone would require D to be comparable to J, and this needs to be shown.
   - The Discussion invokes competing Ru–Ir anisotropic coupling, magnetocrystalline anisotropy and DMI, but none of these is quantified.
   - Needed:
     - the extracted J, D and K values;
     - the magnetic phase diagram of the model as a function of D/J;
     - DFT energies with and without SOC for the competing configurations.

5. **Total-thickness and dimensionality controls are missing.**
   - The comparison is between 2-u.c. single films and bilayers of 3 u.c. (2SIO/1SRO, 1SIO/2SRO) and 4 u.c. (2SIO/2SRO).
   - The manuscript itself places the critical thickness for the IMT at about 3 u.c. ("below a critical thickness of 3 u.c."). Literature values for SRO on STO lie at 3–5 u.c. and depend on capping.
   - The metallicity could therefore simply reflect total conducting thickness above threshold. The trend that conductivity increases with SRO content (Fig. 2A, Sec. C) also fits this reading.
   - Needed:
     - single 3-u.c. and 4-u.c. SRO and SIO films, grown and STO-capped identically, with transport and SQUID;
     - ideally also an SRO/STO/SIO or SRO/SrTiO3 reference, in which the Ir–Ru interface (and hence the Ir–Ru DMI) is absent but thickness is matched.

6. **No structural or chemical characterization of the interfaces.**
   - The DMI mechanism depends on specific octahedral tilts and rotations across the interface. Intermixing of Ir and Ru over 1–2 u.c. would produce a Sr(Ir,Ru)O3 alloy, which is metallic and can be ferromagnetic.
   - The manuscript mentions RHEED oscillations and LEED patterns but shows neither. No XRD/XRR, STEM/EELS or octahedral-rotation data are given.
   - The DFT was relaxed with a fixed STO in-plane constant and an SrO termination. Whether the real samples have that termination and those rotation patterns is not established.
   - Needed:
     - RHEED oscillations and the LEED patterns, including half-order spots that indicate the rotation pattern;
     - cross-sectional STEM-EELS mapping of Ir/Ru across the interface;
     - XRD showing thickness and coherency.

B. Should address

7. **The magnetization data cannot establish ferromagnetism intrinsic to 1–2 u.c. of SRO.**
   - Fig. 2B quotes 0.6 μB/Ru for a single unit cell of SRO and 1 μB/Ru for 2 u.c. These values approach bulk SRO, even though films this thin on STO are normally non-ferromagnetic.
   - For a ~1 u.c. layer on a mm-scale substrate, the signal is near the SQUID floor. It sits on a large diamagnetic STO background, plus a 15-u.c. STO cap.
   - The manuscript does not state:
     - how the background was subtracted;
     - how the sample area or volume was determined;
     - why the whole moment is normalized per Ru, ignoring any Ir contribution, which by the authors' own model is a canted AFM.
   - Missing measurements:
     - loops for the 2-u.c. SIO and SRO single films measured identically. These are needed to support "insulating antiferromagnetic nature of the individual ultrathin films", which is currently taken from literature and theory;
     - M(T) curves locating Tc and comparing it with the ρ(T) kinks;
     - in-plane loops supporting the stated out-of-plane easy axis.
   - Element-specific XMCD at the Ru and Ir edges would also directly test the proposed spin arrangement: FM Ru, c-AFM Ir. That arrangement is the core of the paper.

8. **The resistivity kinks at 102–123 K are not visible in Fig. 2A.**
   - The kinks are assigned to the PM-FM transition of SRO. On the plotted scale the bilayer curves look smooth.
   - Please show dρ/dT, list the kink temperature for each sample, and correlate it with M(T).
   - The 2SIO/1SRO curve also shows a resistivity minimum and upturn below about 30 K. This should be discussed, as it is relevant to whether the thinnest-SRO bilayer is truly metallic down to low T.

9. **The transport and ARPES samples differ, and ARPES mostly probes the top SIO layer.**
   - Transport and SQUID samples carry a 15-u.c. STO cap and were measured ex situ. ARPES samples are uncapped and measured in situ.
   - At hν = 82 eV the probing depth is only a few Å. The ARPES signal therefore comes mainly from the top layer, which is SIO for all bilayers, given that SRO was grown first.
   - So ARPES shows that the SIO surface layer is metallic. It does not show the SRO layer's state.
   - The authors should:
     - state the stacking order explicitly;
     - say whether ARPES samples were annealed identically;
     - assign the orbital and layer character of the X-point hole pocket, for example by comparison with DFT band structures or Fermi surfaces of the slab, which are not shown;
     - ideally compare with a bilayer in reversed stacking (SRO on top).

10. **The ARPES spectral comparison in Fig. 2C–D lacks normalization and a Fermi-edge reference.**
    - The claim of suppressed spectral weight and an energy gap in the single films rests on these spectra. Yet the normalization procedure is not stated (area, a high-binding-energy feature, photon flux), and no gold/metal Fermi-edge reference is given.
    - Insulating 2-u.c. films measured at 10 K can charge, which shifts and suppresses spectra.
    - Please:
      - give the normalization;
      - show the Fermi-edge fits, with EF referenced to a metal in contact;
      - quantify the gap or leading-edge shift for each film.

11. **The effective-mass analysis is internally inconsistent and physically ambiguous.**
    - Sec. C says m* "decreases from 0.57 me for the 2SIO/2SRO heterostructure to 0.5 me and 0.41 me for the 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/1SRO heterostructures … further confirming the enhanced metallicity with increased total thickness".
    - But the most conducting sample (2SIO/2SRO) has the heaviest mass, and the least conducting (2SIO/1SRO) the lightest. A lighter mass does not by itself indicate weaker metallicity, so the inference as written does not follow.
    - The band is hole-like with its maximum about 0.1 eV above EF (Fig. 3D). Yet it is called an "electron effective mass", and the parabolic fit is extrapolated above EF from data below it.
    - Please:
      - state the fit range, the free parameters and the uncertainties on m*;
      - compare with the DFT band mass;
      - reconsider the interpretation (for example, mass renormalization relative to DFT, or in terms of the coherent weight Z).

12. **The coherent/incoherent decomposition in Fig. 3(iii) is not specified.**
    - The functional forms, the background, the constraints and the resulting coherent spectral weights are not given.
    - The text claims that coherent weight increases from 2SIO/1SRO to 1SIO/2SRO to 2SIO/2SRO. Please give the quantitative ratio with uncertainties and show that the conclusion is robust to the choice of line shape.

13. **The choice of U, and the claimed study across a "range of electron correlation strengths", are not documented.**
    - Methods use U = 5 eV, JH = 0.15U, for "Ru, Ir, and Ti 3d orbitals". Ru is 4d and Ir is 5d. For 4d/5d oxides, 5 eV is far above the values usually used (about 1–3 eV) and could artificially open or close gaps.
    - Sec. A states that ground states were investigated for a range of U_Ir and U_Ru, but no such results are shown.
    - Please show the gap and the magnetic ground state of the single films and the bilayers as functions of U_Ir and U_Ru. This would show that "insulating single film, metallic bilayer" is not a U-tuned artifact.
    - Please also report the slabs used: "SRO-SIO layers were varied with 2 to 4 u.c.", but Fig. 4 shows only 3-u.c. bilayers, and 2SIO/2SRO, the sample with the clearest ARPES, is not calculated.

14. **DOS is equated with conductivity.**
    - Sec. D states that "the calculations indicate higher conductivity in the 1SIO/2SRO heterostructure compared to 2SIO/1SRO", based on DOS. A finite DOS at EF does not determine conductivity.
    - Either compute a transport-relevant quantity (for example, Drude weight or Fermi velocity), or limit the statement to DOS at EF.
    - Fig. 4D actually seems to show reduced weight near EF on the SIO layer. Please give the layer-resolved N(EF) for each slab.

15. **Sample statistics and reproducibility are not reported.**
    - The manuscript does not say how many samples of each stack were grown and measured, whether the metallicity and moment are reproducible, or what the sample-to-sample spread is.
    - Please state this. Ideally, show at least a second sample of one bilayer in transport and SQUID.

16. **Transport analysis details are missing.**
    - Which thickness was used to convert sheet resistance to ρ (the full bilayer or one layer)?
    - Did the van der Pauw contacts reach both layers through the STO cap?
    - Was conduction through the STO substrate or cap excluded? The capped single films being insulating partly addresses this; please say so explicitly.
    - Sheet resistance in units of h/e² would make the comparison with the 2D IMT criterion clearer.
    - Hall data giving carrier type and density would also directly test charge transfer (concern 2).

C. Minor

17. **Fig. 3 panel labels do not match the caption and text.**
    - The figure labels row A as 2SIO/1SRO and row B as 1SIO/2SRO. The caption says "(A) 1SIO/2SRO, (B) 2SIO/1SRO".
    - The Fig. 3D legend should also be checked against the m* values quoted in the text.

18. **Fig. 4's caption does not match the text on the Ir–Ru configuration.**
    - The caption says DMI "stabilizes non-collinear spin orientation between Ir and Ru". The text (Sec. A, Discussion) describes c-AFM Ir and FM Ru, with the interlayer angle rotated by 90°.
    - Please make the figure insets, caption and text consistent, and state the spin directions explicitly.

19. **Fig. 1B, D, F are schematic DOS sketches, not calculations.**
    - Since Fig. 1 is presented as "Schematic mechanism", please mark it clearly as a cartoon.
    - Please also make sure the orbital assignment of the bilayer metallic states (Jeff = 1/2 and xy) agrees with Fig. 4. Fig. 4C–D attribute the weight at EF largely to dxz/dyz.

20. **Space-group symmetry is assumed without justification.**
    - The text invokes "space group 62" (Pbnm) to motivate the staggered DMI.
    - Under STO strain and at 1–2 u.c. the symmetry may differ. Please state the symmetry of the relaxed slab and the tilt pattern obtained.

21. **The Methods say magnetic measurements were made "at 10 K" only.** Specify the field orientation, the substrate background subtraction, and the sample dimensions.

22. **Typos and accuracy.**
    - "Temprature" in Fig. 2A.
    - "spi-orbit" in Ref. 9.
    - Ref. 48 has garbled author names.
    - The Methods list "Ti 3d" together with Ru and Ir.
    - The DMI statement "vector D lying along a symmetry axis within the interface plane" should give the actual D direction found in the calculations.

23. **"All data are available in the main text" is not accurate.** The LEED, RHEED and model-Hamiltonian details are not shown. Please provide the data, or a supplement with these details.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Perform element-specific XMCD/XAS at the Ru L/M and Ir L edges on the bilayers and the single films. This would establish:
   - the FM Ru / c-AFM Ir arrangement;
   - the Ir and Ru valence, which tests charge transfer.
   Together these would settle the competing interpretations far more directly than macroscopic SQUID.
2. Add matched-thickness controls (3- and 4-u.c. single SRO and SIO films, and SRO/STO/SIO with a spacer). Show transport and M(T) for all samples on a common plot together with the bilayers.
3. Extend the DFT and model work:
   - constrained calculations of the unreconstructed magnetic order in the bilayer;
   - nonmagnetic and paramagnetic proxies;
   - total-energy comparisons of the competing configurations;
   - extracted J, D and K values;
   - calculations without SOC or DMI;
   - U-dependence.
   This would make the DMI-driven causal chain quantitative.
4. Reframe the claims to match the evidence if these tests are not possible. For example: "emergent metallicity and ferromagnetism in ultrathin SIO/SRO bilayers, consistent with an interfacial magnetic reconstruction", rather than a demonstrated DMI-driven IMT. Also discuss explicitly why the metallic state persists in the paramagnetic regime above Tc.
