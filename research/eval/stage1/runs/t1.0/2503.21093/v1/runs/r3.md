Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports that bilayers of 1–2 u.c. SrIrO3 and 1–2 u.c. SrRuO3 on SrTiO3 are metallic and ferromagnetic, even though 2-u.c. films of either material are insulating. The authors attribute this to an interfacial magnetic reconstruction driven by a staggered Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction (DMI). Turning two ultrathin correlated insulators into a ferromagnetic metal by interface coupling alone would be of broad interest. As submitted, though, the metallicity and ferromagnetism are documented on one set of samples, and the link to a DMI-driven magnetic mechanism rests on qualitative DFT and schematic arguments. The mechanism, which is the paper's central novelty claim, is not yet supported.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The experimental contrast is striking. The 2SIO and 2SRO single films are strongly insulating (Fig. 2A), and all three bilayers have metallic ρ(T) with resistivities of ~0.2–0.4 mΩ cm.
- Three independent probes are combined on the same nominal structures: transport, SQUID magnetometry and in-situ ARPES. ARPES resolves a Fermi surface and a quasiparticle peak at X (Fig. 3), which goes beyond a transport-only claim.
- The idea that interfacial spin–orbit-driven exchange (DMI) could switch the electronic ground state is interesting and testable. The system is well chosen, given the known dimensional insulator–metal transitions in SIO and SRO.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **Interfacial charge transfer, a competing explanation, is not excluded, even though prior work on this very system proposes it.** Ref. 29 (Nelson et al., Sci. Adv. 2022) studied ultrathin SrIrO3/SrRuO3 heterostructures and attributed their persistent metallicity to Ru→Ir interfacial charge transfer. The present manuscript cites ref. 29 only as background for thickness-dependent transitions (p. 4) and never discusses it as an alternative. Charge transfer of a fraction of an electron would dope both Mott/Slater-like layers and can produce metallicity without any change of magnetic order. On this reading, the ferromagnetism would be a consequence of the metallicity (itinerant SRO), not its cause. The causal direction claimed in the title and abstract ("transition is driven by interface-induced magnetic reconstruction") therefore does not follow from the data.
   - The authors should quantify the Ir and Ru valence and any charge transfer, for example with Ir L-edge/Ru L- or M-edge XAS, or Ir 4f/Ru 3d core levels from the ARPES/XPS setup they already use.
   - They should report the Bader or orbital occupations from their DFT, with and without the magnetic reconstruction.
   - They should explicitly compare these with Nelson et al.

2. **The DFT does not isolate magnetism, or DMI, as the cause of metallicity.** The key theoretical statement (p. 4: "electronic states at the Fermi level emerge exclusively when ferromagnetism is induced"; p. 6: "DMI dominates over other interactions") is not supported by any shown calculation. Fig. 4C,D shows only the heterostructures in their claimed ground state. To support "exclusively", the authors need to show:
   - the DOS of the same heterostructure slabs constrained to the unreconstructed configurations (nc-AFM SIO plus c-AFM SRO, as in the isolated films), and to non-magnetic and fully FM configurations;
   - the total energies of all of these;
   - whether the gap survives when SOC (hence DMI) is switched off at fixed structure.

   Without this comparison, the metallic DOS in Fig. 4C,D could simply reflect band alignment and charge transfer in a thicker slab. The "effective model Hamiltonian" is invoked several times (pp. 4, 6, 7) but never written down. Its exchange constants, DMI vector magnitude, anisotropies, and the way they were extracted (fitted to DFT energies? assumed?) must be given, with an estimate of |D|/J showing that DMI can in fact overcome the Heisenberg exchange that stabilises AFM order in the isolated films. A DMI that is typically a few percent of J rarely converts an AFM into an FM on its own. A quantitative argument is essential.

3. **The magnetic state claimed for each layer is not measured.** The mechanism specifies c-AFM SIO and FM SRO in the bilayer, versus nc-AFM SIO and c-AFM SRO in the single films (Fig. 1). The experiment provides only a net M–H loop of the whole bilayer (Fig. 2B).
   - No magnetisation data are shown for the 2SIO and 2SRO single films. Their antiferromagnetism is taken from literature and DFT, and for 2-u.c. SRO this is debated (ref. 25 reports loss of ferromagnetism, and AFM order there is largely a theory prediction, ref. 24). The headline contrast "antiferromagnetic insulator → ferromagnetic metal" therefore rests on an assumption for both reference films.
   - Element-specific evidence is needed for the layer-resolved claim, for example XMCD at the Ir L2,3 and Ru L/M edges, and ideally linear dichroism or resonant scattering for the SIO AFM component.
   - At minimum, M–H and M(T) of the 2SIO and 2SRO single films should be measured under identical conditions, including the STO cap.

4. **Ferromagnetism, the moment values and the Curie temperature need stronger support.** The moments are quoted per Ru (0.6 µB/Ru for 2SIO/1SRO, 1 µB/Ru for the others, p. 5). This assumes Ir carries no net moment, which contradicts the paper's own model in which Ir moments are reorganised and canted by DMI. A value of ~1 µB/Ru, close to bulk SRO, in a 1–2 u.c. layer is surprisingly large. Signals from a 1-u.c. SRO layer on a ~0.5 mm STO substrate are tiny.
   - The authors should describe the diamagnetic and paramagnetic background subtraction.
   - They should show raw data and a bare, processed STO substrate (with cap) as a null control.
   - They should give the field orientation of each loop. The "easy axis along the surface normal" claim requires an in-plane loop for comparison, which is not shown.
   - The ρ(T) kinks at 102–123 K are assigned to the FM transition (p. 5), but no M(T) curve is presented. M(T), or anomalous Hall data showing that ferromagnetism and the kink coincide and that the anomalous Hall loop tracks Fig. 2B, would directly connect magnetism to the conducting channel.

B. Should address

5. **The effective-mass analysis is internally inconsistent with the conclusion drawn from it (p. 6, Fig. 3D).**
   - The text states that m* "decreases from 0.57 me for 2SIO/2SRO to 0.5 me and 0.41 me for 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/1SRO, further confirming the enhanced metallicity with increased total thickness." Yet the thickest sample (2SIO/2SRO, 4 u.c.) has the heaviest mass, and 2SIO/1SRO, the most resistive in Fig. 2A, has the lightest. The trend runs opposite to the claim.
   - The band is hole-like with its maximum ~0.1 eV above EF, yet the text calls m* an "electron effective mass".
   - The parabolic fits extrapolate a band maximum that lies entirely above EF, from MDC peaks spanning roughly −0.05 to −0.22 eV. The quoted masses therefore depend strongly on the fit constraints, and no uncertainties are given.

   The authors should give error bars, state which fit parameters were free, and either revise the interpretation or remove the claim that m* tracks metallicity.

6. **The ARPES and transport samples differ, and it is unclear which layer ARPES probes.** ARPES is performed in situ on uncapped bilayers. Transport and SQUID are measured on samples with a 15-u.c. STO cap deposited before the 1 mbar anneal (Methods A).
   - A polar or strain-imposing cap can itself change the ground state of a 1–2 u.c. layer. The authors should state whether the single-film references in Fig. 2A were capped identically.
   - At hν = 82 eV the probing depth is ≲1 nm. In SIO-on-top bilayers, the spectra in Figs. 2C,D and 3 are therefore dominated by the top SIO and a single interface, so the assignment of the Fermi-surface pocket at X to "metallized SIO" (p. 4) needs support. The authors should compare with DFT band structures (unfolded, with the octahedral-rotation supercell) for each bilayer, and discuss Ir versus Ru orbital character, for example through photon-energy or polarisation dependence.
   - The LEED patterns mentioned in Methods C are not shown. They would establish surface order and rotation-induced superstructure.

7. **The structure and interface quality of the bilayers are not characterised.** An obvious alternative source of metallicity is Ir/Ru intermixing at the interface: SrIr1−xRuxO3 alloys can be metallic and ferromagnetic. A thicker effective conducting region is another possibility. The manuscript shows no RHEED oscillations, XRD/XRR, STEM/EELS or termination analysis.
   - The authors should provide at least the RHEED oscillations for each layer and an HAADF-STEM/EELS cross-section of one bilayer, establishing layer counts and intermixing.
   - They should state the termination of each interface, since the DFT uses SrO-terminated slabs (Methods D).

8. **Missing thickness controls separate "interface coupling" from "total thickness".** Ultrathin SRO is known to become metallic at about 3–4 u.c. (refs. 25, 26, 28), and the bilayers are 3–4 u.c. in total.
   - The obvious controls are single SRO films of 3 and 4 u.c. (and SIO of 3–4 u.c.) grown and capped identically.
   - A bilayer with a non-magnetic insulating spacer, for example 2SIO/1 u.c. STO/2SRO, would test whether direct Ir–Ru coupling is required.
   - So would a 2SRO film capped with a non-magnetic, weak-SOC perovskite such as SrHfO3 or SrZrO3 in place of SIO. If such a structure is metallic, the role of SIO-specific DMI is called into question.

9. **Sample statistics and the conducting path.** The transport and magnetometry appear to come from one sample per stacking, with no indication of reproducibility, device-to-device spread or thermal cycling.
   - Growth at 650 °C in 0.15 mbar O2 on STO can produce substrate oxygen vacancies. The insulating single films argue against substrate conduction, but only if they underwent the identical cap-and-anneal process. The authors should state this, or show a bare processed substrate.
   - Given the ~1–2 nm conducting thickness, the van der Pauw data should also be shown as sheet resistance (Ω/□). The values should be compared with the ~h/e² scale and with the resistivity assumed per layer.
   - The lowest temperature measured is 10 K. Whether the 2SIO/1SRO upturn below ~40 K (Fig. 2A) signals weak localisation or a re-entrant insulating state is not discussed.

10. **DFT methodological choices need justification and sensitivity tests (Methods D).**
    - U = 5 eV with JH = 0.15U is applied to Ru 4d and Ir 5d. That value is unusually large for extended 4d and 5d orbitals; typical values are 1–3 eV. With U this large, the gaps and magnetic ground states in Fig. 4A,B may be artefacts of U.
    - The text (p. 4) says a range of U_Ir and U_Ru was explored, but no U dependence is shown. The authors should show that the single-film gaps and the bilayer metallicity persist over a physically reasonable U range.
    - Structural relaxation was done without SOC, yet the DMI claim relies on octahedral distortions in a strong-SOC system. The authors should check that relaxation with SOC does not change the tilts.
    - The DFT slabs are SrO-terminated with a vacuum layer. They model neither the 15-u.c. STO cap of the transport samples nor the exposed SIO surface in ARPES.
    - 2SIO/2SRO, measured experimentally in all panels, is absent from Fig. 4.
    - The statement that "calculations indicate higher conductivity in the 1SIO/2SRO heterostructure compared to 2SIO/1SRO" (p. 7) is based on DOS alone. No conductivity, Drude weight or Fermi-velocity calculation is shown. The authors should compute it or rephrase.

11. **A schematic is used as evidence.** On p. 5, the "finite density of states (DOS) at the Fermi level (Fig. 1F)" is cited as indicating metallicity and as showing that metallicity "is therefore a direct outcome of the DMI-driven magnetic reconstruction". Fig. 1F is a cartoon. The authors should cite the computed DOS (Fig. 4) and soften the causal language accordingly.

12. **The description of the magnetic configuration is inconsistent.** Fig. 1 caption: "staggered DMI on apical oxygens … leading to a c-AFM state in the SIO layer and a FM state in the SRO layer." Fig. 4 caption: DMI "stabilizes non-collinear spin orientation between Ir and Ru." Text p. 6: the simplest model "predicts one layer with collinear FM order and another with c-AFM order", with Ru and Ir spins at 90°. Discussion, p. 7: "Anisotropic Ru-Ir coupling, exhibiting AFM coupling along the out-of-plane direction." These statements should be reconciled into one explicit spin configuration, with moment directions and canting angles from DFT. That configuration should then be checked against the measured out-of-plane easy axis and the net moment.

C. Minor

13. **Fig. 3 panel labels do not match the caption.** The figure labels row A as 2SIO/1SRO and row B as 1SIO/2SRO. The caption assigns (A) to 1SIO/2SRO and (B) to 2SIO/1SRO.
14. **The coherent/incoherent decomposition is unspecified.** The fitting model behind Fig. 3(iii) is not described (line shapes, Fermi-function convolution, resolution, background). "Relative weight of the coherent peak" (p. 6) should be quantified.
15. **Fig. 2C,D lacks a normalisation statement.** The normalisation of the valence-band spectra is not given (integrated area? O 2p peak?). The comparison of near-EF weight between films depends on it.
16. **The DFT Methods contain wording errors.** "U and Hund's exchange JH were included for Ru, Ir, and Ti 3d orbitals": Ru is 4d and Ir is 5d. "Space group 62" should be named (Pbnm/Pnma) with the rotation pattern given.
17. **Fig. 2A is hard to read and has a typo.** The broken y-axis makes the single-film curves unreadable below ~100 K. The axis label reads "Temprature". A log scale would be clearer.
18. **The laser and beamline used are unclear.** Methods A mentions both a KrF excimer and an Nd:YAG laser without saying which was used for which target. Acknowledgments mention Dreamline at SSRF, but no data from it appear.
19. **Several references are incomplete or wrong.** Ref. 48 lists authors as "K. G and F. J" (Kresse and Furthmüller). Ref. 45 gives the year as 2007 (it should be 2017). Ref. 9 has the typo "spi-orbit".
20. **The data availability statement is insufficient.** "All data are available in the main text" does not cover raw ARPES, magnetometry and DFT inputs. A repository deposit is recommended.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one decisive experiment linking magnetism to metallicity in the bilayer:
   - element-resolved XMCD (Ir and Ru) together with anomalous Hall/MR loops, and M(T) through the ~110 K kink;
   - or a field- or temperature-tuned measurement showing that the conductivity changes when the magnetic state changes.
2. Add the DFT "counterfactual" calculations: the bilayer in unreconstructed magnetic configurations, without SOC, and across U. Write out the model Hamiltonian with numerical J, D and anisotropy values, so the DMI-driven mechanism is quantitative rather than asserted.
3. Discuss Nelson et al. (ref. 29) directly, and measure or calculate charge transfer, so readers can judge whether magnetic reconstruction or electronic doping is primary.
4. If these tests cannot be done, reframe the title and abstract. The supported result is "emergent ferromagnetic metallicity in ultrathin SIO/SRO bilayers, consistent with a DMI-assisted magnetic reconstruction in DFT", rather than a demonstration that DMI drives the insulator-to-metal transition.
