Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors drive ~2 ps current pulses through 10 µm × 30 µm strips of epitaxial NbN and YBCO embedded in an on-chip coplanar waveguide with photoconductive switches. The DC critical current is limited by vortex motion and heating; here, transmission instead collapses abruptly in NbN at about 2.2 × J_c (≈220 GA/m² at 7 K). The authors identify this threshold as the intrinsic depairing current J_c*, compare its temperature dependence with a simple depairing formula and with dirty-limit Usadel simulations, and contrast it with a gradual suppression in YBCO that they attribute to d-wave symmetry. Using on-chip picosecond transport to bypass vortex dynamics and access depairing in a transport geometry is an attractive and timely idea. The NbN threshold data are clean. However, the identification of the threshold with intrinsic BCS depairing, and especially the d-wave interpretation of the YBCO data, are not yet established by the evidence shown.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A new experimental way to reach very high, non-dissipative current densities in superconducting films on picosecond timescales, with a quantitative readout of both the reflected and the transmitted waveforms.
- The NbN threshold is sharp, appears for both pulse polarities (Fig. 4a, S14a), shifts systematically with temperature (Fig. S14), and decreases with temperature much more slowly than the DC J_c (Fig. 5a).
- The DC-bias offset experiment (Fig. S12) is a well-designed internal check. The threshold shifts by 19.45 mA for a 19.25 mA bias, which supports both the absolute current calibration and the idea that a total-current threshold is being crossed.
- DC and picosecond measurements were made on the same device. The DC J_c was checked against delay time for heating (Fig. S6), and λ and ξ were extracted independently on the same film (S8).
- The topic matters for superconducting electronics and for fundamental limits on supercurrent.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The sharp NbN drop is not shown to be intrinsic depairing rather than an electrothermal or edge-nucleated instability within the pulse.** The Discussion rules out only pre-existing vortices (field cooling) and full vortex crossing (displacement of tens of nm in 2 ps). It does not address two other routes:
   - **Thermal runaway inside the pulse.** Once any dissipation begins, it grows quickly. By my estimate, a 47 mA pulse (the threshold current in Fig. S12) through R_n ≈ 140 Ω dissipates I²R·τ ≈ 0.6 pJ in 2 ps. The condensation energy of the 10 × 30 × 0.02 µm³ strip is B_c²V/2μ0 ≈ 0.04 pJ, with B_c ≈ Φ0/(2√2πλξ) ≈ 0.13 T from the authors' λ and ξ. A small seed of dissipation (a weak spot, an edge phase slip, or a kinematic vortex, which moves much faster than the Abrikosov vortex speeds cited in refs. 7, 18–22) could therefore self-amplify into a sharp collapse within the pulse. In that case the "threshold" would reflect the weakest point plus feedback, not the uniform depairing of the bulk.
   - **The gradual approach to the normal state above threshold.** This tail (Fig. 4a, 2.5–6 J_c) is not reproduced by the adiabatic BCS model (Fig. 5b, S20). It is suggestive of heating.

   The experiments that would separate these routes are:
   - (i) Threshold versus pulse duration or shape (for example by changing the switch gap or using a dispersive line). An intrinsic depairing threshold should not depend on duration; a thermal or vortex-nucleation threshold should.
   - (ii) A two-pulse (strong pump, weak probe) experiment showing how quickly superconductivity recovers after a supra-threshold pulse. Recovery on the quasiparticle-recombination time and recovery on a thermal time look different.
   - (iii) Threshold versus strip width. This should include strips with w ≪ Λ_Pearl, where edge barriers and current crowding differ (see concern 8).
   - (iv) Threshold versus a small perpendicular magnetic field. Depairing should depend weakly on field, edge vortex entry strongly.
   - (v) Time-resolved transmitted and reflected waveforms at intermediate amplitudes (between 0.2 and 6.5 J_c, Fig. 3c), showing when within the pulse the response changes.

   The vortex argument should also be made quantitative. It should be shown that the voltage across the strip just above threshold (of order volts) would require an impossibly large vortex flux rate for the displacements allowed.

2. **The YBCO data do not support the conclusion that a d-wave superconductor has no well-defined depairing current, or that gap symmetry is being probed.**
   - **The statement is incorrect as theory.** "In superconductors with an anisotropic gap … J_c* is not a well-defined quantity" (Discussion) does not hold. In a d-wave superconductor, nodal quasiparticles make the superfluid density fall linearly with superfluid velocity (the nonlinear Meissner effect), but the supercurrent j(v_s) still has a maximum, which is the depairing current.
   - **The YBCO depairing current is probably never reached.** For YBCO this maximum is usually estimated at several thousand GA/m² at low temperature. The largest transmitted current in Fig. 4c,d is ≈300 GA/m², so the absence of a maximum most plausibly means the depairing current was not reached.
   - **The gradual suppression has extrinsic explanations.** It starts at the lowest currents, well below the DC J_c. Its size (a ~50% drop in E_T at ~300 GA/m²) looks much larger than the intrinsic nonlinear Meissner effect would give at J/J_d ~ 0.1. Twin boundaries, low-angle grain boundaries or weak links (Josephson-like nonlinear inductance), vortex entry in a film at T/T_c ≈ 0.6, or heating are more natural candidates. The authors themselves note that grain-boundary phase slips dominate in polycrystalline films (ref. 26), and XRD (Fig. S2) does not exclude twinning or low-angle boundaries.
   - **The normal-state reference is at the wrong temperature.** The 110 K transmittance underestimates the normal-state transmittance at 50 K, because YBCO's resistance is roughly linear in T (Fig. S10). Extrapolating, R_n(50 K) ≈ 140 Ω versus ≈230 Ω at 110 K. In a lumped model, T ≈ 2Z0/(2Z0+R) ≈ 0.42 versus 0.30. The 43% transmittance at 6 J_c (S6) and ~0.37 at 8 J_c (Fig. 4c) are therefore close to what a fully normal (or heated) strip at 50 K would give. This removes the basis for "incomplete suppression of superconductivity".

   To support any symmetry-related claim, the authors need:
   - a d-wave calculation of transmittance versus current (nonlinear Meissner effect plus depairing) compared quantitatively with Fig. 4c,d;
   - YBCO data at several temperatures, including low T/T_c, and at matched T/T_c with NbN (7 K is 0.48 T_c; 50 K is 0.59 T_c);
   - a comparison of current along the nodal [110] and antinodal [100] directions, which predicts a specific anisotropy (the bridge orientation relative to the YBCO axes is not stated);
   - ideally a second s-wave material, or a cleaner, untwinned d-wave film.

   Without these, the claims in the abstract and the summary ("reflecting its d-wave symmetry", "new approach to probing the gap symmetry") should be removed or heavily qualified.

3. **The quantitative agreement with "microscopic BCS" depairing (Fig. 5a; abstract: "well described by microscopic dynamics based on BCS theory") is not a discriminating test as calculated.**
   - **The formula mixes clean and dirty limits.** Eq. 9 uses the Landau criterion ħk_F v_s = Δ with v_s = ħ/(πmξ). That expression identifies ξ with the clean BCS coherence length ξ0 = ħv_F/(πΔ). The ξ(0) = 5.24 nm inserted, however, is the dirty-limit Ginzburg–Landau length from B_c2 (WHH), which for disordered NbN is much shorter than ξ0. The Landau criterion is also not the depairing condition in the dirty limit, which is the limit the authors' own Usadel simulations assume.
   - **The prefactor is uncertain by tens of percent.** The standard Ginzburg–Landau expression j_d = Φ0/(3√3πμ0λ²ξ) gives ≈0.6 times the authors' formula for the same λ and ξ (≈160 versus ≈260 GA/m² at T = 0). Dirty-limit (Kupriyanov–Lukichev / Usadel) results differ again. Agreement to ~10% with one particular prefactor therefore does not test BCS.
   - **The temperature dependences are ad hoc.** λ(T) in Eq. 10 is the two-fluid 1 − t⁴ form, not the dirty-BCS one, and ξ(T) in Eq. 11 is not standard and differs from Eq. 27.
   - **Eq. 29 is not used for Fig. 5a.** The authors have a full Usadel calculation (Eq. 29), but Fig. 5a uses the simple formula. The Usadel J_c*(T), with all inputs fixed independently, should be what is compared with the data, along with the GL and dirty-limit benchmarks.
   - **The model may not reproduce the measured kinetic inductance.** S10.3 states that in the model L_s = ħR_n/[πΔ tanh(Δ/2k_BT)]. With R_n = 140 Ω and a typical NbN gap (Δ ≈ 2.2 meV at 7 K), this gives L_s ≈ 14 pH. The value fitted from the reflected pulse (Fig. S16) is 23 pH (λ = 350 nm). The authors should state which Δ and L_s enter the simulation, and show that the weak-pulse transmittance and reflectance are reproduced with the same parameters that set J_c*. The SI claims this ("The successful application of our theory at weak pulses proves the validity…") but does not show it.
   - **Tuned parameters should be listed.** The Dynes parameter η (8% of T_c^BCS) is said in the main text to be chosen partly "to reproduce the experimental results", and T_c^BCS is rescaled. Every parameter adjusted to the data should be listed.

**B. Should address**

4. **The BCS-versus-tDGL comparison (Fig. 5b, S20) does not establish what the text claims.**
   - The tDGL model takes "the microscopically predicted depairing current values … as input" (S10.2). It therefore tests only the sharpness of the drop, not the threshold.
   - The sharpness in tDGL depends on τ_GL and on how the normal channel is treated, and neither τ_GL nor σ_n is given. Any model whose j(q) has a maximum produces a jump under current drive when relaxation is fast. Why the tDGL curve is smooth therefore needs explanation: is it set by τ_GL relative to the 2 ps pulse?
   - The adiabatic assumption is justified circularly in the SI ("Since the two values are consistent, we can justify the use of instantaneous equilibrium theory"). It should instead be supported by an estimate of the gap relaxation and quasiparticle recombination times in NbN at 7 K relative to the pulse.
   - Neither model reproduces the gradual post-threshold tail (see concern 1).

5. **Only one device per material, and no uncertainties.** Each material rests on a single strip (S3, S4). Fig. 5a has no error bars on J_c* or J_c. The authors should show that the threshold and the ratio J_c*/J_c are reproduced in at least one more device per material, ideally of different width, and survive thermal cycling. They should also quote uncertainties combining the calibration (Eq. 1 factors), the reading of the "local maximum" (Fig. 4b inset, S14d) and device geometry.

6. **The effective sample temperature and optical exposure under laser illumination are not characterized.** The 515 nm pump and probe beams illuminate switches a short distance from the strip. The repetition rate, average power, spot positions and how the pulse amplitude is varied (switch bias or laser power) are not given. Average heating would shift the T axis of Fig. 5a, and scattered light could break pairs directly. The authors should:
   - report these parameters;
   - show that the weak-pulse transmittance or kinetic inductance does not depend on laser power at fixed switch bias;
   - use the weak-pulse kinetic inductance L_kin(T) as an in-situ thermometer.

7. **The definition and extraction of J_c* from the transmitted peak.** J_c* is read as the local maximum of J_T,peak versus J_IN,peak (Fig. 4b inset, S14d). If depairing occurs on the rising edge, the transmitted waveform is reshaped, and its peak is not the current at which the transition began. The "leftward tilt" in Fig. 4a is a symptom of this.
   - Full time traces across the transition should be shown, and the instantaneous current at which the response departs from the inductive one should be extracted.
   - The calibration factors in Eq. 1 (1.414 for a "correlation effect" and 0.85 for switch reflections) are partly ad hoc. The S12 check, which is good, should state its temperature, be repeated at several bias values, and be done for YBCO as well.

8. **The uniformity of the current distribution is overstated.** S8.3 and S10.1 argue that the current is uniform because Λ_Pearl ≈ 12–13 µm is "larger than the sample's width" (10 µm). For w ≈ Λ the sheet current is still enhanced at the edges. Current crowding at the inner corners where the 10 µm strip meets the CPW signal line can also locally exceed the mean current by a large factor. The quoted J_c* is a cross-section average, so the local current at depairing may be higher, and edge vortex nucleation would be favored. A width series, or at least an electromagnetic simulation of the sheet-current distribution including the junction geometry, is needed to relate the measured average to the intrinsic J_c*.

9. **The claim J_c* ≫ J_c and the novelty relative to prior depairing measurements need tempering.** At 7 K the ratio is 2.2. A DC J_c of 100 GA/m² (10 MA/cm²) in NbN is already a substantial fraction of the depairing value, and DC measurements on narrow NbN and Nb strips, analyzed with Kupriyanov–Lukichev / Clem–Kogan theory (refs. 11, 12), have approached depairing. THz pair-breaking studies (refs. 9, 10) are also related. The introduction should state precisely what the picosecond method adds relative to these: larger widths, the time domain, and same-device comparison. The abstract's "J_c* ≫ J_c" should reflect the actual ratio and its temperature dependence.

10. **YBCO device details.** S1.2 says 50 nm of Au was deposited in situ on YBCO, and S2 says the YBCO/Au stack was patterned with KI/I₂ etching. It is not stated that the Au was removed from the bridge itself. Any residual Au, or etch damage, would shunt or degrade the bridge and change both the normal-state reference and the gradual suppression. The authors should state this explicitly, together with the bridge orientation relative to the YBCO a/b axes and twinning.

**C. Minor**

11. Inconsistent YBCO normal-state transmittance: S6 states "T = 110 K (peak ≈ 23%)", but Fig. 4c shows ≈0.30 at 110 K and Fig. S13b shows ~0.2. Please reconcile.
12. S3 refers to "Fig. S4b" for the delay-time dependence; this should be Fig. S6b. S4 refers to "Fig. 10b"; this should be Fig. S10b. In Fig. S9a the y-axis label R_sample should read device resistance.
13. The abstract's "vortices are inertially immobile" is inaccurate. The argument is about limited vortex velocity (and nucleation time), not inertia.
14. The introduction says H_c is "experimentally not accessible". It is routinely obtained from magnetization or specific-heat integration; please rephrase.
15. In S10.2, Φ is defined but does not appear in Eqs. 17–20, and the sign and normalization of Q_s differ between Eq. 18 and the definition in S10.1. The "(w)" in Eq. 14 is undefined.
16. Typos: "normal sate", "FHMW", "Lorenz force", "Meaurement", "electron change", "m0 is the vacuum permeability". Ref. 31 (main) and ref. 11 (SI) are incomplete.
17. Fig. 5a: please mark which points come from Fig. S14d and S7, and state T_c used for T/T_c. Fig. 2a/S7: state the voltage criterion used to define J_c.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a pulse-duration and/or pump–probe recovery measurement on NbN. This is the most direct way to separate intrinsic depairing from electrothermal or vortex-nucleation instabilities within the pulse.
2. Replace the simple Landau-criterion estimate in Fig. 5a with the full dirty-limit Usadel J_c*(T), computed with independently fixed parameters and checked against the measured weak-pulse kinetic inductance. Show GL and Kupriyanov–Lukichev curves for reference.
3. For YBCO, either reach much higher current densities (narrower or thinner bridges) and compare with a d-wave nonlinear-Meissner-plus-depairing calculation at several temperatures and two current orientations, or reframe the YBCO section as an observation of gradual nonlinear suppression without a symmetry claim.
4. Measure a width series (including w ≪ Λ_Pearl) and a second device per material, with error bars on J_c*, to establish that the threshold is an intrinsic material property.
