Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use on-chip photoconductive-switch ("ultrafast transport") circuits to drive ~2 ps current pulses through 10 µm wide NbN and YBCO strips. They report that NbN carries supercurrent up to a sharp threshold Jc* ≈ 2.2 Jc (≈220 GA/m² at 7 K), where Jc is the ms-pulse transport critical current, and that Jc*(T) agrees with a microscopic estimate built from the measured λ and ξ. YBCO instead shows a gradual suppression, which the authors attribute to d-wave nodes. A transport probe of the intrinsic depairing current, free of vortex motion and self-heating, would be useful and of broad interest. However, the quantitative agreement with theory, the interpretation of the YBCO data, and the claim that vortices are bypassed are not yet established at the level the abstract states.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The measurement idea is attractive and timely. Ps current pulses, with the incident, reflected and transmitted waveforms resolved in the time domain on the same device used for the DC Jc, are a clean way to separate fast intrinsic dynamics from slow vortex and thermal processes.
- The NbN data are of good quality. The plateau and sharp drop in the transmittance (Fig. 4a, Fig. S14a,b) are symmetric for positive and negative pulses. The non-monotonic J_T,peak versus J_in,peak curve (Fig. 4b, Fig. S14d) is a distinctive signature. The threshold moves systematically with temperature (Fig. S14).
- The DC-bias test in Fig. S12 is a useful internal check: the threshold shifts by 19.45 mA under a 19.25 mA DC bias. It supports both the absolute pulse-current calibration and the additivity of the pulse current and the DC current.
- The supporting characterization is broad: Bc2(T) gives ξ(0), the reflected-pulse fit gives Lkin and λ, the delay-time test of the DC Jc is shown, and a dirty-limit Usadel calculation is compared with tDGL.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The calculated Jc*(T) rests on a clean-limit Landau criterion with a prefactor that does not match standard dirty-limit theory, so the "excellent agreement" in Fig. 5a may be partly coincidental.
   - What the paper does: the main text (p. 10) and SI Eq. 9 set ħk_F v_s = Δ and write Jc* = 2ħe n_s/(π m ξ). With n_s = m/(2e²μ0λ²), this becomes Jc* = Φ0/(π² μ0 λ² ξ). The inputs are ξ from Bc2 (a GL/dirty-limit coherence length) and λ(T) from a Gorter–Casimir two-fluid form (Eq. 10).
   - Why this is a problem: the standard GL depairing current is Φ0/(3√3 π μ0 λ² ξ), smaller by a factor 3√3/π ≈ 1.65. The Kupriyanov–Lukichev dirty-limit result at low T is also below the Landau-type estimate. In the dirty limit (ξ ≈ 5 nm, short mean free path) the Landau criterion ħk_F v_s = Δ does not set depairing. Pair breaking is gradual and the gap closes at a finite superfluid momentum. The identity Δ/(ħk_F) = ħ/(π m ξ) uses the clean BCS (Pippard) coherence length, not the ξ extracted from Bc2.
   - Numbers: with λ = 350 nm and ξ = 5.24 nm, the paper's formula gives ≈260 GA/m² at T→0, matching the dashed line. The GL expression would give ≈160 GA/m², below the measured 220 GA/m² at 7 K.
   - Why it matters: the claim that the threshold is the intrinsic depairing current rests largely on this agreement.
   - Requested:
     - Derive Jc*(T) in the dirty limit consistently (Kupriyanov–Lukichev, or the authors' own Usadel route, SI Eq. 29) with the measured ξ(0), λ(0) or ρ_n, and Tc, and plot it in Fig. 5a with propagated uncertainties.
     - State explicitly which parameters are free.
     - Reconcile the two theoretical routes. The main text says the measured Jc* lies slightly below the Fig. 5a estimate, while the Usadel simulation is said to reproduce the threshold.

2. In YBCO, the 110 K "normal-state" reference is not the normal-state response at 50 K, so the "incomplete suppression" and "gradual approach to the normal state" conclusions may follow from the choice of baseline.
   - What the paper does: Fig. 4c,d and SI S6 compare the 50 K data with the response at 110 K. They state that at 6×Jc the transmittance (≈43%) exceeds the 110 K value (≈23%), "indicating incomplete suppression of superconductivity."
   - Why this is a problem: unlike NbN, whose R(T) is flat above Tc (Fig. S5a), YBCO has a strongly T-dependent normal-state resistance (Fig. S10a, roughly linear in T).
   - Estimate: in the lumped series-element picture, T ≈ 2Z0/(2Z0 + R). R ≈ 230 Ω at 110 K gives T ≈ 0.30. Extrapolating R(T) linearly to 50 K gives roughly 100–120 Ω and T ≈ 0.45–0.5. This is close to the 43% reported at 6×Jc. The data may therefore be consistent with a fully resistive state at 50 K, or one heated above it, rather than partial depairing.
   - Requested:
     - Estimate the normal-state transmittance expected at 50 K, for example from the extrapolated ρ(T) passed through the SI circuit model (Eqs. 5–8 / 21–24), and redraw Fig. 4c,d against that reference.
     - Discuss how electron heating during the resistive part of the pulse shifts the reference.

3. The d-wave interpretation of the YBCO data has no supporting calculation, and other explanations are not excluded.
   - What the paper claims (p. 7, p. 10): in a d-wave superconductor "there is no well-defined intrinsic current density threshold" and Jc* "is not a well-defined quantity."
   - Why this is a problem: theory for d-wave superconductors (nonlinear Meissner effect; Doppler-shift depairing calculations) still gives a supercurrent-versus-superfluid-momentum curve with a maximum, so a depairing current exists. Nodes mainly change the low-current nonlinearity and the T dependence.
   - Other explanations for a smeared threshold:
     - The film's broad transition (82–90 K, Fig. S10b).
     - Reduced Tc (85 K, which suggests oxygen deficiency).
     - Twin domains, which an r-sapphire/CeO2 film normally has despite the "monocrystalline" description.
     - An implied λ(50 K) ≈ 0.7 µm, obtained from the quoted Pearl length of 21 µm with d = 50 nm. This is several times typical values for good YBCO films and points to strong disorder or inhomogeneity.
     - Heating in a sample whose normal resistance depends on temperature (see concern 2).
   - Only one temperature (50 K, ≈0.6 Tc) and one device are shown.
   - Requested:
     - A d-wave calculation (for example quasiclassical, in the same framework as the NbN Usadel model) of the transmittance versus J_T,peak, compared quantitatively with Fig. 4c.
     - YBCO data at several temperatures, including well below 50 K, where the d-wave and inhomogeneity pictures predict different trends.
     - Evidence on film homogeneity.
   - Without these, the abstract's statement that the gradual suppression "reflects its d-wave symmetry," and the closing claim of a "new approach to probing gap symmetry," are not supported.

B. Should address

4. The claim that ps pulses bypass vortex motion rests on an order-of-magnitude argument (p. 9), not on a measurement.
   - The comparison is between two limits only: ms pulses (Fig. S6) and ~2 ps pulses.
   - Requested:
     - (i) A field-cooled control. Measure the ps transmittance curve with a known vortex density (perpendicular field, field cooled) and show that Jc* is essentially unchanged while the DC Jc drops.
     - (ii) A pulse-duration dependence, for example by broadening the pulse with a slower switch, a filter or a pulse train, to show the crossover from Jc* toward Jc.
   - Also discuss edge-barrier-controlled vortex entry. In a 10 µm wide, 20 nm thick film with Pearl length ≈12–13 µm, the DC Ic may be set by the edge barrier rather than bulk depinning, which changes what the ratio Jc*/Jc means.

5. Absolute current-density calibration and its uncertainty.
   - Every J axis rests on SI Eq. 1. This contains an assumed correlation prefactor of 1.414 and an assumed average transmission factor of 0.85 (from a ≈30% switch reflection taken from Ref. 2), and on a film thickness quoted as "≈20 nm."
   - Each enters Jc* linearly. None has a stated uncertainty, and Fig. 5a has no error bars.
   - The Fig. S12 bias test is valuable but uses a single bias value.
   - Requested:
     - The threshold shift at several DC biases, including negative bias, showing a slope of one.
     - A measured film thickness (for example XRR or AFM) with its uncertainty.
     - Error bars on Jc* and Jc in Fig. 5a that propagate these uncertainties. This is needed to judge the comparison in concern 1.

6. One device per material.
   - All NbN results come from one strip, and all YBCO results from one strip.
   - The sharp NbN threshold and its ratio to Jc should be reproduced on at least one more NbN device, preferably of a different width, to test for current crowding or edge effects. A second YBCO device is also needed.
   - State whether the threshold is reproducible after thermal cycling and after repeated driving above Jc*.

7. Heating and effective sample temperature are not addressed for the ps measurements.
   - Missing details: the laser repetition rate, the pump fluence on the generation switch, how the pulse amplitude is varied (switch bias voltage or optical power), and whether scattered 515 nm light reaches the strip.
   - Repetition-rate-averaged heating, or quasiparticles injected optically, would lower the effective temperature at which Jc* is measured and would affect the Jc*(T) comparison.
   - Estimate of heating within a pulse: once resistive (ρ ≈ 9×10⁻⁷ Ω m from R_n = 140 Ω), a 2 ps pulse at ~2×10¹¹ A/m² deposits ~10⁵ J/m³. That is enough to raise the NbN electron temperature by tens of kelvin, which will shape the curve above Jc*.
   - Requested:
     - Show that the low-drive transmittance and Jc* do not depend on repetition rate or on scattered light (for example by moving or blocking the excitation of the reference switch).
     - Discuss electron heating above threshold.

8. The Usadel simulation (Fig. 5b, Fig. S20, SI S10) needs a clearer account of its fitted parameters and assumptions.
   - The main text says the Dynes broadening is set "to reproduce the experimental results." The SI says it is fixed at 8% of Tc^BCS for numerical stability, and later refers to a "Dynes β of zero." It is therefore unclear whether η is fixed or fitted, and how sensitive the sharpness and position of the drop are to it. Show results for several η.
   - The adiabatic assumption needs justification against the quasiparticle inelastic and recombination times in NbN (≳10 ps), which exceed the pulse duration. This matters most once the gap collapses within the pulse.
   - The tDGL comparison may be a weak foil. In a uniform strip, tDGL also has a maximum in J(Q_s). The smoothness of the grey curve probably reflects the chosen τ_GL and the two-fluid parameters, which are not given. Report them.
   - A sharper test is the low-current nonlinearity. The Usadel model predicts that n_s, and hence Lkin, falls noticeably before Jc* (Fig. S19a). The reflected waveforms, fitted with the SI circuit model as a function of drive, should give Lkin(J) for comparison with theory, independently of the transmittance threshold.

9. Time-resolved waveforms near threshold are not shown.
   - Fig. 3c and Fig. S13c show waveforms only far below and far above threshold.
   - Jc* is identified from peak amplitudes alone (Fig. 4b inset), yet in the transition region the sample becomes resistive during the pulse.
   - Requested: incident, reflected and transmitted waveforms at drives just below, at and just above Jc* (for example 1.8, 2.2 and 2.6 Jc). These would show whether the transmitted current is clipped at Jc* within the pulse and when in the pulse the switch to the resistive state occurs. They would also check whether the "local maximum" in J_T,peak corresponds to the instantaneous current at which depairing occurs.

10. Uniformity of the current distribution.
   - The argument that the current is uniform (SI S8.3, S10.1) uses Pearl length ≈ 12–13 µm against a width of 10 µm, which is comparable, not much larger. The edge enhancement is therefore modest but not negligible.
   - Current crowding at the transitions between the CPW signal line and the strip (Fig. S3 and S8 insets) could also set a lower local threshold.
   - Requested: estimate the edge and corner current enhancement for this geometry (for example a 2D London/Pearl solution, or the tDGL geometry already set up), and state how it affects the extracted Jc*.

11. Prior measurements of the depairing current.
   - The introduction says direct transport measurements of Jc* "remain challenging" and cites mainly THz and theory work.
   - Pulsed-current transport measurements of the depairing current already exist. Examples are narrow Al strips compared with Kupriyanov–Lukichev theory (Romijn et al.), and short-pulse measurements on cuprate and MgB2 films (Kunchur and co-workers). Measurements on NbN nanowires reach a large fraction of the depairing current at DC.
   - Requested: place the present results relative to this work, both to clarify what is new (ps time resolution, s-wave versus d-wave comparison) and to compare the extracted Jc* for NbN with literature values.

C. Minor

12. "Jc* >> Jc" in the abstract and the "Jc* = 2.2 × Jc ... intrinsic depairing limit" framing overstate the result. At 7 K the ratio is 2.2. Also, as the main text notes, the measured values lie below the calculated ones. Temper the wording, and state that Jc is a sample-dependent quantity, so the ratio is not universal.

13. Inconsistent YBCO normalization. SI S6 quotes transmitted peaks of ≈43% at 6×Jc and ≈23% at 110 K, and Fig. S13b shows ≈0.2 at 110 K. Fig. 4c, however, shows the 110 K transmittance at ≈0.3 and the 50 K data reaching ≈0.4 only at ~8×Jc. Fig. S13b is at 40 K while Fig. S13c and Fig. 4c are at 50 K. Make the normalizations and temperatures consistent.

14. SI S8.2 calls the fitted value Lkin(0) = 23 pH and quotes λ(0) = 350 nm, but the fit is to the 7 K reflected pulse (Fig. S16). Label it Lkin(7 K) and correct to T = 0 consistently. The quoted Pearl lengths, 12.2 µm at 0 K and 13 µm at 7 K, already imply this correction.

15. Theory notation and functional forms:
   - Eq. 11 for ξ(T) uses an unusual tanh form, which differs from Eq. 27, ξ(T) = ξ(0)[Δ(0)/Δ(T)]^1/2. Use one form and give a reference.
   - Eq. 10 uses the two-fluid λ(T), not the dirty-BCS form.
   - In Eqs. 13 and 25, λ denotes both penetration depth and BCS coupling, and β denotes both 1/T and the Dynes parameter.
   - Eq. 17 defines Φ but does not use it.

16. Figure and text references:
   - SI p. 16 says "As shown in Fig. S4b" for the delay-time data, which are in Fig. S6b.
   - "In Fig. 10b" should read "Fig. S10b."
   - The y-axis of Fig. S9a reads R_sample but shows the device resistance.
   - Fig. 5a has no units or legend for the solid lines through the data.
   - Typo: "normal sate" (p. 3).
   - Ref. 31 (Gor'kov) is incomplete.

17. Describe the YBCO film as epitaxial rather than "monocrystalline" unless twinning has been excluded. The text argues that domain boundaries would hide intra-domain depairing (p. 10), so the twin state matters for concern 3.

18. Methods details: state the base temperature stability, the number of averages per point, and the noise level of the transmittance (error bars in Fig. 4a,c and Fig. S14). Also state how the sign-reversed (negative) pulses were produced.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a field-cooled and a pulse-duration series on NbN (concern 4). Together with the existing DC-bias shift, this would turn the "vortices are inertially frozen" argument into a measurement and would be the strongest support for the headline claim.
2. Extract Lkin(J), equivalently n_s(J), below threshold from the reflected waveforms and compare it with the Usadel prediction in Fig. S19. This tests the microscopic model independently of the threshold position and of the Jc* formula.
3. Replace the Landau-criterion estimate with a dirty-limit calculation and show the measured Jc*(T) with propagated calibration uncertainties against it (concerns 1 and 5).
4. For YBCO, add measurements at several temperatures, a normal-state reference matched to 50 K, and a d-wave calculation. Alternatively, present the YBCO result more cautiously, as a qualitative contrast rather than evidence of gap symmetry.
