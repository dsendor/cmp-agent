Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors send ~2 ps current pulses through a coplanar-waveguide-embedded NbN strip and a YBCO strip. They report that in NbN the supercurrent survives up to a sharp threshold Jc* ≈ 2.2 × Jc(DC) at 7 K, which they identify with the intrinsic (Cooper-pair) depairing current, and that YBCO instead shows a gradual suppression, which they attribute to its d-wave gap. Measuring the depairing current by transport, without vortex entry and Joule heating getting in the way, is a long-standing goal. A clean picosecond method would matter both as a spectroscopic tool and for superconducting electronics. The technique (on-chip photoconductive-switch transport) is established, and applying it to the depairing limit is new. However, the two headline interpretations (intrinsic depairing that is not reached in DC because vortices are inertially frozen, and a d-wave origin of the YBCO behaviour) currently rest mainly on plausibility arguments, not on discriminating controls.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A clear, reproducible-looking threshold in NbN (Fig. 4a,b). It is symmetric for positive and negative pulses and is followed at five temperatures (Fig. S14).
- A useful internal check of the absolute current calibration: a DC bias of 19.25 mA shifts the picosecond threshold by 19.45 mA (Fig. S12).
- The DC Jc and the picosecond Jc* are measured on the same device, so the ratio Jc*/Jc does not depend on the cross-section.
- A microscopic (Usadel, dirty-limit) simulation that reproduces the shape of the NbN transmittance collapse much better than tDGL (Fig. 5b, Fig. S20), including at 12 K where both models converge.
- Experimentally, the result is simple to state and potentially general.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **No experiment tests the proposed mechanism: that the picosecond and DC limits differ because vortices are inertially frozen.** The central claim is that DC transport is limited by vortex entry and motion plus heating, while a 2 ps pulse is too short for vortices to move, so the intrinsic Jc* is reached (abstract; Discussion p. 9). The evidence is a kinematic estimate (vortex velocity of tens of km/s × 2 ps ≈ tens of nm), zero-field cooling, and the NbN/YBCO contrast. None of these checks the mechanism directly.
   - In a zero-field-cooled strip of width 10 µm, which is smaller than the Pearl length (13 µm at 7 K, SI S8.3), there are no pinned vortices to depin. The DC Jc here is set by vortex entry at the edges and the resulting thermal runaway, not by the "Lorentz-force depinning" of Fig. 1b and p. 3. The narrative should be corrected.
   - The decisive experiments are:
     - **Pulse-duration dependence.** Vary the pulse length (2 ps to tens or hundreds of ps, e.g. with a different switch material, filters, or a dispersive line) and show a crossover from Jc* towards Jc on the timescale predicted by vortex entry and transit times.
     - **Magnetic-field dependence.** Field-cool to put a known vortex density in the strip. DC Jc should fall strongly, while Jc* should remain close to the depairing value if the vortex-inertia picture is correct.
     - Optionally, **devices with different pinning or edge quality** (e.g. different widths, or an ion-irradiated strip, cf. ref. 21). Jc should change while Jc* stays fixed.
   - Without at least one of these, "Jc* >> Jc because vortices are inertially immobile" remains a hypothesis. The observation could equally be a pulse-length-dependent instability threshold.

2. **The quantitative agreement with "microscopic BCS dynamics" (abstract, Fig. 5a) rests on an internally inconsistent formula and partly tuned parameters.**
   - **Fig. 5a formula.** The analytic Jc*(T) in Fig. 5a and Eq. (9) applies the clean-limit Landau criterion ħ k_F v_s = Δ together with the clean BCS relation ξ0 = ħv_F/(πΔ). It then inserts the GL/dirty-limit coherence length ξ(0) = 5.24 nm extracted from Bc2 (Eqs. 2–3), plus a two-fluid λ(T) ∝ (1 − t⁴)^(−1/2) (Eq. 10). The authors themselves stress that NbN is in the strongly disordered, dirty limit (p. 11, SI S10). In that limit depairing is not governed by the Landau criterion: the film becomes gapless before the current maximum. The appropriate result is Kupriyanov–Lukichev / Clem–Kogan (ref. 11), with j_d(0) ≈ 1.491 Δ0^(3/2)/(e ρ_n (ħD)^(1/2)).
   - **Our rough estimate from the paper's own numbers.** Inputs:
     - R_n ≈ 140 Ω over ~3 squares gives ρ_n ≈ 90 µΩ cm;
     - the Bc2 slope ≈ 1.2 T/K gives D ≈ 0.9 cm²/s;
     - Δ0 ≈ 1.76 k_B Tc.
     These give j_d(0) of several hundred GA/m², noticeably above the ≈ 250 GA/m² of the dashed curve in Fig. 5a. Either our estimate or the paper's formula is off by a factor of order 2. The agreement called "excellent" therefore needs to be demonstrated with the correct dirty-limit expression.
   - **Usadel simulation (Fig. 5b, S20).** The Dynes parameter η is "set to … reproduce the experimental results" (p. 11; 8% of Tc^BCS in S10.3). The normal-state resistivity is "set to match the transmittance at T > Tc" rather than taken from the DC R_n. Tc^BCS is also rescaled.
   - **Requested.** Report the parameter-free dirty-limit prediction of Jc*(T) from the measured ρ_n, D (from Bc2) and Tc. List which parameters in the Usadel simulation are fitted and which are fixed, and show how the predicted threshold depends on η (e.g. η = 2%, 5%, 8%). Overlay the Usadel threshold at all five temperatures, not only at 7 and 12 K. If agreement holds only for a tuned η, the abstract should say "consistent with", not "well described by".

3. **The d-wave interpretation of the YBCO data is not supported by a calculation, a temperature dependence, or a control, and simpler explanations are not excluded.** The second headline claim (abstract; Fig. 4c,d; p. 7) is based on one YBCO device at one temperature (50 K), with no model.
   - **The film itself.** The resistive transition is broad (~83–90 K, Fig. S10b). The Pearl length quoted for YBCO, P_L(50 K) = 21 µm with d = 50 nm (SI S8.3), implies λ(50 K) ≈ 0.7 µm. That is several times larger than typical optimally doped YBCO, pointing to reduced or inhomogeneous superfluid density. YBCO on r-cut sapphire/CeO2 is also normally twinned and may contain grain or twin boundaries. The "monocrystalline" statement (p. 10) rests only on Fig. S2.
   - **Alternative explanations.** A distribution of local Jc* values (Tc and oxygen inhomogeneity), phase slips at twin or grain boundaries (which the authors themselves invoke for polycrystalline films, p. 10, ref. 26), or heating would all smear the threshold without any reference to gap symmetry.
   - **Gap symmetry does not by itself predict this.** A d-wave superconductor still has a well-defined maximum supercurrent (Eilenberger/Usadel calculations give a current maximum, softened but present). Gap anisotropy alone does not obviously turn a sharp collapse into a nearly linear decrease that starts at J ≈ Jc(DC) and has not saturated at 8 × Jc.
   - **Requested.**
     - (i) A d-wave calculation (with the same lumped-circuit model as for NbN) predicting the transmittance versus J_T and its temperature dependence.
     - (ii) YBCO data at several temperatures, including close to Tc, where all models should converge.
     - (iii) The crystallographic direction of the current relative to the Cu–O bonds or nodes.
     - (iv) Ideally, a second YBCO device or a disordered s-wave control with a comparably broad transition, to show that inhomogeneity alone does not produce the gradual curve.
   - Otherwise the conclusion should be narrowed to "YBCO shows no sharp threshold".

**B. Should address**

4. **One device per material.** All results come from a single NbN device and a single YBCO device (SI S3, S4). Show Jc* (and Jc) for at least one more NbN device, ideally with a different width or thickness, with the device-to-device spread. Also state whether the threshold is unchanged after thermal cycling. A device-independent Jc* despite a device-dependent Jc would itself be strong evidence for claim A1.

5. **Heating, stray illumination, and repetition-rate effects on the picosecond threshold are not addressed.** The measurement is a stroboscopic average over many laser shots.
   - **Repetition rate and stray light.** The laser repetition rate, the average optical power on the switches, and the distance from the switches to the strip are not given. Stray 515 nm light or substrate heating could lower the effective gap.
   - **Self-heating after onset.** Once the film turns resistive, the energy dumped per pulse is large: I² R t ≈ (45 mA)² × 140 Ω × 2 ps ≈ 0.5 pJ into ~6 µm³ of film. This could be enough to raise the electron temperature well above Tc. A small precursor dissipation could therefore trigger a thermal runaway (hotspot) that mimics a sharp threshold.
   - **Requested.**
     - Show that the threshold does not depend on the excitation-switch fluence at fixed current (varying bias voltage versus laser power to obtain the same current) or on the repetition rate.
     - Estimate the electron temperature rise within the pulse.
     - Show the full time-resolved transmitted waveforms across the threshold (not only peak values). This would indicate whether the suppression starts at the current maximum or develops later in the pulse, as a thermal instability would.

6. **The adiabatic and equilibrium assumption in the Usadel model is justified a posteriori.** SI S10.1 assumes the order parameter follows the current instantaneously with an equilibrium (tanh) distribution function. The final paragraph of SI S10.3 then argues that agreement with the data justifies that assumption, which is circular. In NbN, τ_GL at 7 K is sub-ps, but energy relaxation (electron–phonon) is ~10 ps. Under 2 ps drive the quasiparticle distribution is likely not thermal, which affects both n_s and the threshold. Give the relevant timescales (τ_GL, τ_E, the gap relaxation time) at each temperature. Discuss why a dynamic threshold should coincide with the static depairing current rather than exceed it, as is often found for short pulses.

7. **The DC Jc determination and the ratio Jc* = 2.2 Jc.**
   - **Pulse length.** The heating test (SI p. 16, Fig. S6) is described as using "µs-long current pulses", but Fig. S6 shows delays of 0.5–2.5 ms and a 0.4 ms window. On these timescales the film and substrate are in thermal steady state, so invariance with delay does not exclude self-heating at the onset.
   - **Definition of Jc.** It is not stated whether Jc is taken at the first resistance onset or at the midpoint.
   - **Requested.** Because the headline ratio and the "order of magnitude" statement at 0.8 Tc (p. 10) depend on it, define Jc explicitly. Ideally, give Jc from shorter (µs or sub-µs) pulses as well.

8. **The material parameters in the different analyses are inconsistent.**
   - **Kinetic inductance.** L_kin = 23 pH from the fit of the reflected pulse at 7 K is used to define λ(0) = 350 nm (SI S8.2). The fit is at T/Tc ≈ 0.48 and is labelled as the zero-temperature value without correction. Meanwhile, Eq. (26) of the BCS simulation fixes L_s from R_n and Δ. With R_n ≈ 140 Ω and Δ ≈ 2.2 meV this gives roughly 13–15 pH, not 23 pH.
   - **Two-fluid fit.** The two-fluid circuit fit assumes L_n0 = L_s0 without justification.
   - **Requested.** Reconcile these numbers, since λ (through n_s) enters Jc* in Eq. (9) quadratically.

9. **Absolute current density and current uniformity.**
   - **Calibration.** The calibration uses two assumed factors in Eq. (1): 1.414 for the switch–pulse correlation and 0.85 for the reflection at the switch. The bias-shift test (Fig. S12) validates the transmitted-current scale at one temperature. State whether the same check holds for the incoming-pulse switch, which sets the x-axis of Fig. 4b and S14c,d.
   - **Uniformity.** The uniformity argument (P_L > w) is static. At ~0.3 THz, and at the corners where the 10 µm strip joins the wider signal line (Fig. S3, S8), current crowding could raise the local J above the average and trigger an early transition.
   - **Requested.** Quantify the thickness uncertainty (which scales every J), and give an electromagnetic simulation or estimate of current crowding at the strip ends.

**C. Minor**

10. **Fig. 4c versus SI.** The 110 K normalized transmittance in Fig. 4c is ≈ 0.30, whereas SI p. 21 quotes "peak ≈ 23%" at 110 K. The text also says the YBCO transmittance decreases "both before and after J_T,peak reaches the DC critical current density" (p. 7), but Fig. 4c is nearly flat up to J ≈ Jc. Please reconcile both.

11. **"Nearly an order of magnitude" (p. 10).** At 0.8 Tc, Fig. 5a shows Jc ≈ 15–20 and Jc* ≈ 120 GA/m², a ratio of ~6–8. Please state the ratio.

12. **Cross-references.** In SI p. 16, "As shown in Fig. S4b" should be S6b. In SI p. 17, "Fig. 10b" should be S10b. The text says "µs-long" pulses but the data are ms (see concern 7).

13. **Equations.**
    - Eq. (11) is typeset with nested square roots that do not correspond to the standard dirty-limit ξ(T) = ξ(0)[Δ(0)/Δ(T)]^(1/2) of Eq. (27). Please make them consistent.
    - Eq. (10) uses an empirical two-fluid λ(T) inside a "dirty-limit" treatment.
    - Eq. (17) is labelled as containing a "voltage potential Φ" that does not appear.
    - The Dynes parameter is called η in one place and β in another (the "Dynes β of zero" near Eq. 25 clashes with β used as 1/T).

14. **Fig. 1 and the introduction.**
    - Saying H_c is "experimentally not accessible" (p. 3) is too strong: it is routinely obtained from magnetization or calorimetry.
    - Fig. 1b shows J_c1 < J_c < J_c* as universal, but in narrow strips with edge barriers J_c can exceed the vortex-entry estimate.

15. **Fig. 4a.** The "slight leftward tilt" is a multivalued region when plotted against J_T. Plotting E_T,peak against J_IN,peak alongside would make the threshold definition unambiguous. Also state how Jc* is extracted from the local maximum (the dashed lines in Fig. S14d) and its uncertainty.

16. **Typos.** "normal sate" (p. 3), "FHMW" (p. 5, SI), "Meaurement" (Fig. S6), "electron change" (SI S8.2), "show" → "shown" (Fig. 5 caption).

17. **Pearl length.** In SI S8.3 and S10.1 the Pearl length is written both as 2λ²/d and as 2λ_L²/t. Give the YBCO λ value explicitly; it is relevant to concern 3.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a pulse-width series and a field-cooled (finite vortex density) series on NbN. Together they would turn the vortex-inertia argument into a demonstrated mechanism.
2. Replace the analytic Landau-criterion estimate with the dirty-limit (Kupriyanov–Lukichev / Usadel) depairing current computed from independently measured ρ_n, D and Tc, and show the Usadel threshold against data at all measured temperatures with η fixed.
3. For YBCO, add a temperature series, the current direction relative to the crystal axes, a d-wave calculation, and an assessment of film inhomogeneity (e.g. the λ implied by the measured kinetic inductance, the Tc width). Or else narrow the d-wave claim.
4. Show the full time-domain transmitted and reflected waveforms around the threshold, and a second NbN device. This would show when, within the pulse, superconductivity collapses, and that Jc* is device-independent.
