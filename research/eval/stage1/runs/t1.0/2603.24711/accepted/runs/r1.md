Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors send picosecond (~2 ps FWHM) current pulses, launched and sampled on-chip by photoconductive switches, through narrow NbN (s-wave) and YBa2Cu3O7 (d-wave) thin-film strips. The idea is to outrun vortex entry and self-heating, which limit the d.c. critical current. In NbN they see a sharp collapse of the transmitted pulse at about 2.2 x J_c (d.c.). They identify this threshold with the intrinsic depairing current J_c*, and its temperature dependence follows a Landau-criterion estimate and a dirty-limit Usadel simulation. In YBCO the suppression is gradual, and they attribute this to the d-wave gap. A transport-based, time-domain route to the depairing current would be useful to the superconductivity and superconducting-electronics communities. But it is only convincing if the absolute current scale and the mechanism of the threshold are pinned down, and the d-wave interpretation currently rests on thin evidence.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The on-chip picosecond launch/sample architecture is a real experimental advance. It measures incoming, reflected and transmitted waveforms in a single device, so impedance changes can be read directly in the time domain.
- The NbN threshold is sharp and appears for both pulse polarities (filled and open symbols in Fig. 4a). The 20 K normal-state data are flat over the same amplitude range (Fig. 4a, red), which is a useful built-in check that the launch and sampling switches respond linearly.
- The transmitted level above threshold converges to the 20 K normal-state value (Fig. 4a), and the high-drive J_T versus J_IN slope matches the normal-state slope (Fig. 4b). Together these show that the film is driven into a resistive state within the pulse.
- The temperature dependence of the threshold (Fig. 5a) differs qualitatively from that of d.c. J_c, which supports the claim that it is a different and more intrinsic quantity than d.c. J_c.
- The microscopic (Usadel) modelling of the time-dependent response, set against tDGL, is a reasonable step towards a quantitative description.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The absolute current-density scale, and therefore the "2.2 x J_c" headline and the agreement in Fig. 5a, depend on a calibration chain that the main text does not validate.
   - How J is obtained: from the sampled photocurrent through E(t) w = S J(t) Z0 (p. 1812), with details deferred to SI section 5.
   - The sampling switch has a finite response time. The measured peak of a ~2 ps pulse is a convolution of the true waveform with the switch response, so the true peak current in the strip is probably higher than J_T,peak. The bandwidth limit of the sampling scheme should be stated, and its effect on the peak values deconvolved or bounded.
   - Converting photocurrent to absolute field needs an independent calibration. The authors should show at least one cross-check of the absolute scale, for example:
     - the 20 K normal-state transmission compared with the d.c. normal-state resistance of the same strip (Fig. 2a gives about 140 Ω, which predicts T = 2Z0/(2Z0+R) ≈ 0.42; the authors should state whether this matches the 0.4 level in Figs. 3b and 4a quantitatively);
     - pulses long enough (ns) that the switching current reverts to the d.c. J_c.
   - The film thickness (~20 nm), the strip width (≈10 µm) and Z0 (≈50 Ω) are all approximate and enter J directly. An uncertainty budget for J_c* is needed.
   - Fig. 5a has no error bars on either J_c or J_c*. Without them, the statement that the data are "slightly smaller" than the calculation, and the claim of "excellent agreement", cannot be judged.

2. The paper does not test directly that the threshold is current-driven depairing rather than another ultrafast pair-breaking or heating channel.
   - What the identification rests on now: a single pulse width (~2 ps), an argument that vortices cannot move far enough, and agreement with a model.
   - Pulse spectrum against the gap: a 2 ps pulse has spectral weight up to roughly 0.5–1 THz. For NbN with T_c ≈ 15 K, 2Δ(0) ≈ 4.5 meV ≈ 1.1 THz, and 2Δ is smaller at 7 K. Pair breaking by direct absorption of the pulse's high-frequency components, and fast electron heating once any quasiparticles form, are known for intense THz fields in NbN (ref. 10).
   - Pulse-duration dependence: the key discriminating measurement is the threshold as a function of pulse width (or rise time). An intrinsic depairing threshold should be insensitive to duration over a picosecond window and should cross over to d.c. J_c only at the vortex/thermal timescale. A threshold that tracks fluence or the spectral content near 2Δ would point elsewhere.
   - Waveforms across the transition: Fig. 3c shows waveforms only at 0.2 x J_c and 6.5 x J_c. A series of transmitted and reflected waveforms through the transition (1.5–3 x J_c) would show when the depairing happens within the pulse, and whether the change starts as an inductive one (nonlinear kinetic inductance, which is expected below J_c*) and then becomes resistive. This is the time-domain signature that would tie the threshold to J_c*.
   - Requested: the pulse spectrum, a pulse-width series (or at least two widths), and the intermediate-current waveforms.

3. The d-wave interpretation of YBCO is not supported by the present data, yet it is half of the abstract's conclusion.
   - Temperature mismatch: YBCO is shown at a single temperature, 50 K (T/T_c ≈ 0.59), while the sharp NbN response is shown at 7 K (T/T_c ≈ 0.47). At higher T/T_c, thermal smearing broadens the transition even in an s-wave film. The NbN data at T/T_c ≈ 0.67 and 0.83 (SI section 7) should be shown next to the YBCO curve. YBCO should also be measured at low temperature (for example 10–20 K), where the nodal contribution would dominate if the d-wave argument holds.
   - Size of the currents: the currents reached in Fig. 4c,d (J_T up to ~3 x J_c ≈ 150 GA m^-2) look well below standard estimates of the YBCO depairing current at 50 K. A Ginzburg–Landau estimate with λ ≈ 150 nm and ξ ≈ 1.5 nm gives a few thousand GA m^-2 at low temperature, still several hundred at T/T_c ≈ 0.6. Doppler-shifting nodal quasiparticles (the nonlinear Meissner effect) reduces n_s only by order J/J_dep. Before attributing a drop of E_T from 0.8 to near the normal-state level to intrinsic d-wave depairing, the authors need to show quantitatively that the effect can be that large at these currents.
   - Extrinsic routes the paper does not exclude: twin boundaries and low-angle grain boundaries in commercial c-axis films on CeO2-buffered sapphire, T_c inhomogeneity, and weak links. Each could give a gradual suppression. The statement that the films are "monocrystalline" (p. 1814) needs structural evidence: XRD rocking curves, φ-scans and twin-domain characterization. The d.c. R(J) curve in Fig. 2b is already gradual, which hints at inhomogeneity.
   - Mismatched normal-state reference: the reference is taken at 110 K. YBCO's normal-state resistivity is roughly linear in T, so a "normal" strip at 50 K would have lower resistance and higher transmission than at 110 K. Saying the 50 K curve "converges to the value characteristic of the normal state" therefore compares unlike states. The authors should estimate R_n(50 K) and show the expected fully depaired level.
   - Overstatement about d-wave depairing: the claim that "J_c* is not a well-defined quantity" in d-wave superconductors goes beyond the theory. In d-wave theory a depairing (superflow-instability) current still exists, preceded by a gradual nodal suppression of n_s. The claim should be softened, or supported with a d-wave calculation (SI section 11.4 is only qualitative tDGL).

B. Should address

4. The analytic J_c* estimate (p. 1814) does not fit the material it is applied to.
   - The Landau-type criterion ħk_F·v_s = Δ, with ξ = ħv_F/(πΔ) and the electron mass m, is a clean-limit argument. NbN films 20 nm thick are strongly in the dirty limit, which is why the simulation uses the Usadel equation. In the dirty limit, depairing is governed by the pair-breaking parameter (Kupriyanov–Lukichev), not by a Doppler shift that reaches the gap.
   - Please state:
     - which n_s(T) and ξ(T) were used (SI section 9);
     - whether they were measured independently or adjusted;
     - what values of m and n_s enter;
     - how many free parameters there are against the four J_c* points in Fig. 5a.
   - A parameter-free comparison with the dirty-limit Kupriyanov–Lukichev depairing current, computed from the measured ρ_n, T_c and diffusion constant, would be a much stronger test.

5. The Usadel–tDGL comparison does not discriminate between models as much as the text suggests.
   - The Dynes broadening is described as "set to guarantee numerical convergence and reproduce the experimental results" (p. 1814), so it is effectively a fitting parameter for the shape in Fig. 5b.
   - tDGL is not valid at T/T_c ≈ 0.47 to begin with. Its failure therefore does not confirm the BCS picture, and the BCS curve misses the tail.
   - Please give the Dynes Γ used and show how sensitive the transition width and position are to Γ. Say which features of Fig. 5b are predicted and which are fitted.

6. Vortex exclusion is argued, not measured.
   - The argument (p. 1813–1814) uses vortex velocities of "a few tens of km/s", but the self-field at the strip edges is substantial at the peak current. A rough estimate of the peak current is I ≈ J_c* w d ≈ 2.2×10^11 A m^-2 × 10 µm × 20 nm ≈ 44 mA. The edge field is enhanced in a thin strip, likely well above H_c1. So vortex nucleation at the edges is energetically allowed during the pulse.
   - Requested:
     - a quantitative upper bound on the voltage that edge vortex motion over ~2 ps could produce, compared with the ~140 Ω normal-state response;
     - preferably, a control measured under a known applied perpendicular field or after field cooling, where pre-existing vortices are present. If the picosecond threshold is intrinsic, it should be nearly insensitive to the field, while d.c. J_c is strongly suppressed. This would be a direct experimental test of the central premise.

7. Current distribution and the definition of the current-density axis.
   - J is defined as total current divided by the cross-section S. For a 20 nm NbN film the Pearl length 2λ²/d is likely comparable to the ~10 µm width. Whether the picosecond current is uniform across the strip, or crowds at the edges (as for Meissner screening currents), needs to be stated. Edge crowding would lower the apparent J_c* and would localize depairing at the edges.
   - Fig. 4a uses J_T,peak as the abscissa. Because J_T itself drops at the transition, the curve becomes multivalued (the "leftward tilt"). Plotting against J_IN,peak alongside, or against an estimated current in the strip that accounts for reflection, would make the threshold easier to read and remove the circularity.

8. Samples and reproducibility.
   - The paper does not say how many NbN and YBCO devices were measured, whether J_c* was reproduced in more than one device, or what the device-to-device spread is.
   - It is also unclear whether the d.c. J_c in Fig. 2 comes from the same strips used for the picosecond measurements. The 2.2 ratio depends on both quantities coming from the same strip.
   - Please give the number of devices, J_c and J_c* for each, and whether the results survive thermal cycling.

9. Heating and illumination.
   - The 515 nm beams illuminate switches close to the strip (Fig. 3a). Scattered light or substrate heating could produce quasiparticles in the film or raise its effective temperature.
   - The effective sample temperature under illumination at a 50 kHz repetition rate should be established, for example from the kinetic-inductance response or from d.c. R(T) with the beams on and off.
   - Heating after depairing within the pulse probably shapes the post-threshold tail that the BCS simulation misses. The authors should discuss this and consider including electron–phonon heating in the model.

10. The claims of generality are stronger than the data.
   - The abstract and discussion propose the method as a probe of gap symmetry "in systems that cannot be directly accessed by spectroscopic techniques" (p. 1814). With one s-wave and one d-wave material, at one temperature each for the comparison, the method has not been shown to tell symmetries apart independently of extrinsic factors (see concern 3).
   - The application claim about generating "ultrashort, strong magnetic field pulses" is not developed.
   - These statements should be tempered.

C. Minor

11. Fig. 2b and the text disagree on the YBCO d.c. J_c, and the criterion is undefined.
   - The text says J_c is "slightly below 50 GA m^-2" for YBCO at 50 K. In Fig. 2b the 50 K curve appears to rise over roughly 40–60 GA m^-2 and keeps increasing without reaching a clear normal-state plateau.
   - Please define the J_c criterion (resistance threshold or voltage criterion), especially for the gradual YBCO curves.

12. Fig. 5a and the text disagree on the J_c*/J_c ratio near 0.8 T_c.
   - The text says J_c is "nearly an order of magnitude" smaller than J_c* at 0.8 T_c.
   - Read from Fig. 5a, the ratio near T/T_c ≈ 0.83 looks closer to ~6 (J_c ≈ 20, J_c* ≈ 120–130 GA m^-2).
   - Please quote the actual values.

13. Fig. 5a shows five d.c. J_c points but four J_c* points, while Fig. 2a shows only 7, 8.5 and 10 K. Please list the temperatures and say where the d.c. data for the other temperatures appear.

14. The J_c* expression (p. 1814) needs its quantities defined.
   - n_s should be defined explicitly as the pair density or the electron density. The factor 2e in J_c* = n_s v_s (2e) depends on this choice.
   - m should be specified as free or effective, and the value used should be given.

15. In Fig. 4a the filled and open symbols mark pulse polarity, but this is not distinguished in Fig. 4b. The inset label "J_c*" should be tied to a stated numerical value with an uncertainty.

16. Fig. 3b shows about 90% transmission at 7 K. Please say whether the missing ~10% is accounted for by the kinetic-inductance reflection alone or also by radiative or contact losses (contact resistance ~3 Ω).

17. The Methods give approximate thicknesses (~20 nm, ~50 nm). Please give measured values with uncertainties, and state the exact strip dimensions for each device.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Fit the inductive reflected and transmitted waveforms below threshold to extract L_kin(J), and hence n_s(J). Comparing the measured current-induced suppression of superfluid density directly with the Usadel prediction, before the collapse, would be a strong and largely parameter-free test of the depairing picture. For YBCO the same analysis would separate nodal (∝ J) suppression from extrinsic weak-link behaviour.
2. Add a pulse-duration series and an applied-field (or field-cooled) series for NbN. The threshold should stay fixed while d.c. J_c changes, which would demonstrate directly that vortices and heating are bypassed.
3. Measure YBCO at several temperatures down to well below 50 K, and NbN at the same reduced temperature as YBCO. Show both in a single figure on a J/J_dep(T) axis.
4. Include the dirty-limit Kupriyanov–Lukichev J_dep(T) curve, computed from independently measured ρ_n, T_c and D, in Fig. 5a as a parameter-free benchmark.
