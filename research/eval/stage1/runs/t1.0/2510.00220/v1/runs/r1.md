Review outline:

Note on scope: the file I reviewed has the 12-page main text, Methods and references. The Extended Data figures (1-11) and the Supplementary Information it cites were not in it. Where a concern touches material the text says is in those sections, I say so and ask that the key evidence be made visible or brought into the main text.

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports that, in marginally twisted double-bilayer graphene, gate voltages reversibly drive a hysteretic switch between a "Bernal-dominant" (B-dominant) and a "rhombohedral-Bernal mixed" (RB-mixed) tetralayer domain configuration. The two states are bistable over a large region of the (n, D) plane. At fixed gate voltages, the RB-mixed state shows a hysteretic anomalous Hall effect (AHE) and the B-dominant state does not. The authors also find that the AHE sign reverses between +D and -D, and they tentatively attribute this to Bernal/rhombohedral domain boundaries. Gate-driven switching of correlated magnetism through a stacking transition would be a notable addition to slidetronics and to rhombohedral-graphene physics. However, the stacking identity of the domains in the measured device and the structural origin of the bistability are inferred rather than shown directly.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The basic phenomenon is clear and striking. At identical gate voltages, the Hall response is hysteretic (about ±150 Ω at B = 0) in one history-prepared state and linear in the other (Fig. 4c,d and g,h). This is a clean history-dependent on/off comparison.
- The switching is repeatable under gate pulses (Fig. 1d), and the bistable region is mapped carefully in both sweep directions with both gates (Fig. 2). The resulting phase diagram (Fig. 3a) is mirror-symmetric about D = 0.
- A simple tight-binding energy model (Fig. 3b) qualitatively reproduces two features of the boundary: the hole-doping trend, and the enlargement of the rhombohedral-accessible region at large |D| near charge neutrality.
- The D-odd AHE is unexpected for centrosymmetric stackings, and according to the text it is reproduced in a second device (Extended Data Figs. 10, 11). It could point to new physics at stacking domain walls.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The stacking identity of the domains in device A is not established, and with a near-180° twist it is not obvious that the domains are ABAB/ABCA.
   - Where: Methods (Device Fabrication) states that device A, which supplies all main-text data, was assembled with "nearly-180°" rotation, and that "one of the layers underwent an additional global shift". Main text, p. 2, and Fig. 1b.
   - What is wrong: For an AB bilayer placed on an AB bilayer at about 0°, the low-energy interface registries give ABAB and ABCA domains. Rotating the top Bernal bilayer by 180° (Bernal bilayer symmetry D3d has no C2z) turns it into its "AC" partner. Simple registry counting then gives ABAC/ABCB-type stackings at the domain level. These are the non-centrosymmetric, polar tetralayer polytypes reported to carry spontaneous polarization (the authors' own Ref. 37).
   - Why it matters: If the domains in device A are polar, two interpretations change.
     - The 22 mV KPFM contrast, which the authors read as a work-function difference, could instead be a polarization contrast.
     - Polar domains would break inversion symmetry. That would give a much more mundane explanation of the D-odd AHE than domain-wall physics.
   - Further gap: The only real-space image (Fig. 1b) comes from a different heterostructure on SiO2, not from device A. The "global shift" invoked to recover ABAB/ABCA is asserted, not demonstrated.
   - What to do:
     - (i) Give the stacking-registry argument explicitly for the 0° and 180° assemblies.
     - (ii) Characterize device A itself, after fabrication or at least before contacting: Raman 2D-band mapping, which distinguishes ABAB, ABCA and ABCB, and KPFM or near-field imaging.
     - (iii) State which of device A and device B has which domain types.
     - (iv) Check whether the charge-neutrality D offset D0 differs between the two states. A polar stacking would shift it.

2. There is no direct evidence that the resistance bistability is a stacking transition and that the RB-mixed state contains rhombohedral tetralayer.
   - What the paper offers: The structural assignment rests on three things:
     - the hysteresis itself (Fig. 1c);
     - the absence of clear quantum oscillations in the RB-mixed state (Extended Data Fig. 3);
     - the analogy to imaging studies (Refs. 27, 28) carried out on other samples.
   - Other explanations not yet excluded:
     - gate hysteresis from charge trapping in hBN or at the graphite gate;
     - electronic (rather than structural) bistability;
     - contact- or edge-related switching. The switching occurs "only in part of the device" (Extended Data Fig. 4), and the jumps change sign and magnitude between contact pairs (Extended Data Fig. 7).
   - Why the stated evidence is weak: Missing quantum oscillations are a weak discriminator, because disorder or inhomogeneous current paths also suppress them.
   - What to do:
     - (i) Compare Landau fans or Hall density in both states at the same gate voltages, to show that n0, D0 and the gate capacitances are unchanged. This rules out trapped charge.
     - (ii) Show a rhombohedral-specific electronic signature in the RB-mixed state that is absent in the B-dominant state. Examples are the large-|D| gap at charge neutrality (compare the neutrality resistance and its temperature dependence in Fig. 2b vs 2c inside the boxed regions), or Landau-level or oscillation features matching ABCA tetralayer.
     - (iii) Report the switching thresholds versus sweep rate and temperature.
     - (iv) Show the same measurement on a region or device without pre-existing rhombohedral domains (pure Bernal) fabricated and measured identically. The text currently cites literature for the absence of switching in single-domain samples (Refs. 20, 23, 40) rather than an own control.

B. Should address

3. The D-odd AHE is attributed to domain boundaries, but simpler explanations of the sign reversal are not excluded.
   - Where: p. 7 and Fig. 4a,e,c,g.
   - Sweep-path issue: The RB-mixed state at +D and at -D is prepared along different gate paths (Fig. 2a vs 2e; Fig. 4 caption). Because domain-wall motion is the mechanism of the switching, the domain configuration at (n, +D) and at (n, -D) need not be the same.
   - Geometric-Hall issue: In a Hall bar only partly covered by magnetic patches, the sign and magnitude of the voltage picked up at a given Hall pair depend on where the magnetic region sits relative to the probes. This could produce an apparent D-odd sign without any intrinsic inversion breaking.
   - Inversion-breaking arguments not yet strong enough: The arguments against extrinsic inversion breaking (no moiré features in Rxx; a mirror-symmetric phase boundary) are suggestive but weaker than the AHE sign test, which is sensitive to small symmetry breaking. No hBN-graphene alignment angles are given.
   - What to do:
     - (i) Measure Ryx at both Hall pairs, and at ±D after preparing the RB-mixed state with matched protocols. For example, reach +D and -D from the same corner via paths related by top/bottom-gate exchange, and check that Rxx is the same at both.
     - (ii) Use the intermediate domain states (the multiple jumps, blue dashed lines in Fig. 3a) as a test. If the D-odd AHE comes from domain walls, its magnitude should track the wall configuration, while the D-even component (Fig. 4i) should track rhombohedral area.
     - (iii) Report the hBN alignment angles determined from crystal edges.
     - (iv) Make clear that a polar-stacking explanation (Concern 1) is ruled out before invoking domain-wall physics. The abstract currently presents the domain-boundary origin more prominently than the evidence supports.

4. The theory comparison (Fig. 3b) is presented as quantitative ("agree well"), but how the parameters were fixed and what is compared are unclear.
   - Free parameters: The model has two free parameters, ΔΦ (taken from KPFM, on a different sample, on SiO2 and at room temperature) and ΔU(0,0) (determined in the SI). If ΔU(0,0) was chosen to match the boundary, the agreement is partly fitted.
   - Ensemble: At fixed gate voltages, the relevant thermodynamic potential includes the electrostatic work by the gates. Because the two stackings have different quantum capacitance, they do not have the same n and D at the same voltages. It is not stated whether ΔU is computed at fixed (n, D) or at fixed (Vt, Vb).
   - Constant-ΔF assumption: Identifying each jump with a constant-ΔF contour assumes the domain-wall pinning barrier does not depend on n and D, which is not justified.
   - Only one boundary is modelled: Only the RB-to-B boundary is mapped. The B-to-RB transition appears at n ≈ -15.5×10^12 cm^-2, at or near the edge of the gate range (Fig. 1c), so the two spinodal-like boundaries cannot bracket ΔF = 0.
   - What to do:
     - (i) State how ΔU(0,0) was obtained and whether it was fitted to the transport data.
     - (ii) Show the sensitivity of the contours to ΔΦ and ΔU(0,0).
     - (iii) Clarify the ensemble used.
     - (iv) Give a quantitative measure of agreement, such as the ΔU values along each experimental contour, which should be constant if the picture holds.
     - (v) State whether -15.5×10^12 cm^-2 is a genuine threshold or the limit of the accessible gate range.

5. Reproducibility across devices and contacts is unclear from the main text.
   - Device count: All main-text data come from device A. Device B (0.03° twist) is said to reproduce the AHE sign reversal, but the text does not say whether device B also shows the gate-driven structural switching and the on/off AHE.
   - Contact dependence: Switching occurs only in part of device A (Extended Data Fig. 4), and the Rxx jumps differ in sign between contact pairs.
   - What to do: State how many devices were fabricated and how many showed switching. Show the key Fig. 4 comparison (same gate voltages, hysteretic vs. linear Ryx) in a second device, or at a second Hall pair in device A. Say whether the switching and the AHE survive a thermal cycle to room temperature.

6. "Nonvolatile" is not quantified.
   - What is shown: Retention is shown only over about 300 s (Fig. 1d), and cycling over "many cycles" (Extended Data Fig. 9, number not given in the text).
   - What holding the state requires: Fig. 4 and Fig. 3a indicate that at Vt = Vb = 0 the device is B-dominant. The RB-mixed state therefore persists only while non-zero gate voltages are held inside the bistable window. That is weaker than the usual meaning of non-volatility in memory, where the state is retained with no applied bias.
   - Temperature robustness: For "toggling quantum phases", it matters whether the RB-mixed state survives warming above the Curie temperature (about 8 K) and recooling at fixed gates. If it does, the magnetism can be reset independently of the stacking.
   - What to do: Report retention times at the operating point. Report the number of cycles and any drift. Show a warming/cooling test at fixed gates. State explicitly which gate voltages must be held.

7. The magnetic characterization of the "ferromagnet vs paramagnet" claim is thin.
   - What is shown: Field sweeps reach only ±0.15 T, and the coercive field is about 0.1 T (Fig. 4c,g), so it is unclear whether the loops are saturated. Hysteresis is shown at only one or two gate points per sign of D.
   - Antisymmetrization: Ryx was anti-symmetrized (Methods). For hysteretic data this has to combine the forward sweep with the reversed backward sweep. Mis-pairing would distort the loop shape and the remanence.
   - Contact mixing: The resistance network is strongly inhomogeneous and contact-dependent (p. 5). Mixing of Rxx into Ryx in a magnetic, hysteretic system may not be fully removed by antisymmetrization.
   - What to do:
     - Extend the field range until the loops saturate.
     - State the antisymmetrization procedure for loops.
     - Show raw, unsymmetrized Rxx and Ryx loops.
     - Bring the temperature dependence and Curie-temperature determination (Extended Data Fig. 8) into the main text or describe it there.
     - Show that the B-dominant state has no hysteresis over the same field range, at the identical gate point, and at a temperature well below 1.5 K if available.

8. The rhombohedral fraction and the meaning of the two states are only qualitative.
   - What is missing: "B-dominant" is said to possibly contain small rhombohedral patches and domain walls (p. 2), and the RB-mixed fraction is not quantified. Yet the claimed physics depends on how much rhombohedral area and how much wall length each state has: the AHE magnitude "significantly larger" than in pure tetralayer, and the proposed proximity effect.
   - What to do: Estimate the area fractions, for example from quantum-oscillation amplitudes, from the Hall density, or from the KPFM domain statistics of a comparable sample. Discuss how a modest rhombohedral area fraction can produce an AHE larger than that of a pure rhombohedral device. That fact by itself suggests the measured Ryx is not a simple area average.

C. Minor

9. The claim on p. 7 that this is the "first realization of a magnet-to-non-magnet nonvolatile phase transition controllable entirely by electrostatic means" should be tempered or made more precise. Gate-hysteretic switching of Chern/magnetic states has been reported in moiré graphene systems with anomalous gate hysteresis (moiré "ferroelectric" devices), and electrical switching of magnetic order has been reported in moiré Chern magnets. State what is new: switching driven by a stacking change.

10. "First-order quantum phase transition" (p. 2) is not an accurate description. The transition is a structural first-order transition between metastable configurations driven by gate voltage at 1.5 K, and no tuning to a T = 0 critical point is shown. "Gate-driven first-order structural transition" would be more accurate.

11. Fig. 1d:
   - Give the pulse durations and amplitudes.
   - Give the read-out density, and state that it lies inside the bistable window.
   - Give the sweep rate used in Fig. 1c.
   - The 30 Ω and 11 Ω levels in Fig. 1d should be reconciled with Fig. 1c at the corresponding n.

12. Fig. 3:
   - Fig. 3a overlays blue contours from a different contact configuration on data from the main configuration. Mark them clearly as such, or show them on their own map.
   - Fig. 3b should state the sign convention of ΔU (which sign favours rhombohedral) and the reference density/displacement used to define the contours.

13. Fig. 2 and Fig. 4 captions: spell out the exact gate path used to prepare each state at each (n, D) point. Readers need this to judge Concern 3, because the domain configuration depends on history.

14. The text says the field dependence of the switching boundary yields the magnetic-susceptibility difference between the two structures (p. 3, Extended Data Fig. 5). The main text should give the Clausius-Clapeyron-type relation used, the assumptions (constant barrier, field-independent pinning), and the resulting value with uncertainty, or else drop the claim from the main text.

15. Methods:
   - The density and displacement calibration (n0, D0, Ct, Cb from Landau levels in the B-dominant state) should be stated with uncertainties.
   - Confirm that the same calibration applies in the RB-mixed state.
   - Give the hBN thicknesses.
   - The measurement currents of 0.5 to 10 nA are fine, but state the electron-heating checks (for example, current independence of the AHE).

16. The KPFM work-function difference used in the theory is measured at room temperature, under ambient conditions, on a sample on SiO2 or on a stamp. Comment on how well this ΔΦ transfers to the encapsulated, gated device at 1.5 K.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Settle the stacking identity of device A directly, using Raman 2D mapping and/or KPFM on the finished device, and state explicitly which domain types a 180° versus 0° assembly produces. This one step would resolve the structural interpretation (Concern 1) and constrain the origin of the D-odd AHE (Concern 3).
2. Add one transport signature that is specific to rhombohedral tetralayer in the RB-mixed state, together with a Landau-fan check that n0 and D0 do not change between states. Together these would rule out charge-trapping and contact explanations of the bistability.
3. Test the domain-wall hypothesis quantitatively. Measure the D-odd and D-even AHE components across the intermediate domain states (the multiple jumps in Fig. 3a), at both Hall pairs, after preparing the state at +D and -D with matched protocols.
4. Present the theory comparison with fitted and fixed parameters clearly separated, show the parameter sensitivity, and give the computed ΔU along each experimental contour to show whether it is really constant.
