Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors report gate-voltage-driven, history-dependent bistability in marginally twisted double-bilayer (tetralayer) graphene. They interpret it as a first-order sliding transition between a "Bernal-dominant" state and a "rhombohedral-Bernal mixed" state, and show that the mixed state hosts an anomalous Hall effect (AHE) that is absent at the same gate voltages in the Bernal-dominant state. They also report that the AHE changes sign under D → −D and attribute this to the Bernal/rhombohedral domain boundaries. Electrostatic, nonvolatile switching of a correlated magnetic phase through a stacking change would be a significant result for slidetronics and rhombohedral-graphene physics. The gate-history bistability of the AHE (Fig. 4c vs 4d) is striking. However, the structural interpretation and the origin of the D-odd AHE currently rest on indirect transport arguments.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A clear, reproducible, gate-history-dependent switching of R_xx (Fig. 1c,d) and a systematic protocol for mapping the switching boundary over the full (n, D) plane (Fig. 2, Fig. 3a).
- At identical gate voltages, a hysteretic AHE with coercive field ~0.1 T appears in one history state and is absent in the other (Fig. 4c,d,g,h). This is a direct demonstration of nonvolatile, electrostatically controlled on/off switching of an AHE.
- The phase boundary is mirror-symmetric in D, and its shape agrees qualitatively with a tight-binding energy-difference calculation (Fig. 3b). This supports a doping- and field-driven thermodynamic origin over a purely random one.
- The D-odd AHE is reported in a second device (Extended Data Figs. 10, 11), and the AHE pocket at large |D| and small n coincides with where the quarter-metal is reported in rhombohedral tetralayer graphene.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **No direct structural evidence that the two transport states differ in stacking, and the main competing explanation is not excluded.** The identification of "RB-mixed" vs "B-dominant" rests on three things: R_xx hysteresis (Fig. 1c), the absence of clear quantum oscillations in the hole-doped state (Extended Data Fig. 3), and qualitative agreement with a calculation (Fig. 3b). The KPFM image (Fig. 1b) is from a different heterostructure on SiO₂ with no gates. It shows that the starting material has mixed domains, not that gating changes them in this device.

   The switch into the RB-mixed state happens only at the extreme of the gate range: n ≈ −15.5×10¹² cm⁻² is the left edge of Fig. 1c, and the Fig. 2 protocol resets the device by "doping to the maximum hole density". That pattern is also what charge injection into, or trapping in, the hBN dielectric at the largest gate voltages would produce. Trapped charge also gives history-dependent, nonvolatile resistance changes that relax at a threshold.

   The n and D calibration was done "from the Landau levels in the B-dominant state" only (Methods). The paper therefore does not show that the same (V_t, V_b) gives the same n and D in both states. That is the premise of "recorded at exactly the same gate voltage" (Fig. 4) and of every boundary in Fig. 3a.

   The authors should:
   - show Landau fans or Hall-slope carrier densities in both states at the same gate voltages, demonstrating that n₀ and D₀ do not shift between them;
   - show that the switching threshold scales with n and D (doping) rather than with the absolute voltage on either gate. The Fig. 2a vs 2e comparison partly addresses this, but it should be stated explicitly, for example by plotting the threshold against V_t and V_b separately;
   - provide direct structural evidence in the same device or an equivalent one. Options are Raman 2D-band mapping after gating into each state (ABAB and ABCA have distinct 2D lineshapes), in-situ KPFM or nano-IR on a gated device, or at minimum KPFM on the device before and after switching.

2. **The stacking assignment for device A, which provides nearly all the data, is not established, and a polar stacking would change the interpretation.** Device A was made from two bilayers at "nearly 180°" twist, and "one of the layers underwent an additional global shift" (Methods). A 180°-rotated AB bilayer placed on AB gives local ABAC/ABCB domains, not ABAB/ABCA. ABCB/ABAC is the non-centrosymmetric, spontaneously polarized tetralayer polytype (cf. ref. [37], which the authors cite for the work-function difference). The paper then invokes the same "global shift" to argue that device A nonetheless ended up with ABAB/ABCA domains. That shift is not characterized. The KPFM contrast of 22 mV (Extended Data Fig. 2) is also of the order expected for polar-domain contrast.

   This matters for two reasons. If any ABCB/ABAC regions are present, they would naturally (a) break inversion and give a D-odd AHE without invoking domain-wall physics, and (b) switch through sliding-ferroelectric motion. Both are alternatives to the paper's two central interpretations.

   The authors should determine the stacking of the domains in device A directly, for example by Raman 2D lineshape, SHG (which is non-zero only for the polar stacking), or KPFM under applied D on the device. They should also explain how the "global shift" was identified. They should state whether device B (0.03°, which naturally gives ABAB/ABCA) reproduces the full switching phase diagram, not only the AHE sign reversal.

3. **In a strongly inhomogeneous, multi-domain conductor, the Hall measurement cannot by itself establish either the "ferromagnet" label or the D-odd sign.** The paper itself states that R_xx jumps differ "in magnitude and sign" between contact pairs (Extended Data Fig. 7, p. 5), that switching occurs "only in part of the device" (Extended Data Fig. 4), and that current paths are "complex".

   Under these conditions the transverse voltage at a single Hall pair mixes longitudinal and Hall components in a way that symmetrization does not fully remove. Its sign and magnitude can be set by where the magnetic patch sits relative to the voltage probes. This may also explain why |R_yx| is "significantly larger" than in pure rhombohedral tetralayer.

   The maps in Fig. 4a,b,e,f were taken at a single field, B = 0.03 T. They cannot have been antisymmetrized, and with R_xx ~10–100 Ω and comparable R_yx a small contact misalignment matters. The magnetization history before each map line is also not stated.

   The authors should:
   - show R_yx(B) loops for both D signs on at least two independent Hall pairs, including the opposite side of the Hall bar, in the same device, and state which pair was used in Fig. 4;
   - show R_xx(B) alongside each loop;
   - describe how hysteretic loops were antisymmetrized: forward sweep combined with the reversed backward sweep;
   - show the B = 0 remanent R_yx after training with +B and −B fields at the diamond and star points;
   - report the Hall angle or σ_xy, not only R_yx.

   If the D-odd sign survives on several contact pairs and in device B with a different geometry, the conclusion is much stronger.

**B. Should address**

4. **The domain-boundary origin of the D-odd AHE is a hypothesis; the paper presents it as the leading interpretation without testing it, and the alternatives are dismissed too quickly.** The paper rules out device asymmetry by citing (i) the absence of moiré features in R_xx, (ii) the D-mirror symmetry of the R_xx phase boundary, and (iii) reproduction in a second device. Point (ii) constrains the structural energetics, not the Berry-curvature sign of a magnetic state that may be sensitive to small layer-asymmetric perturbations. Point (iii) does not exclude a systematic asymmetry, because both devices were built the same way: graphite/hBN bottom gate, PC pickup, top gate on top.

   The authors should:
   - report the hBN alignment angles relative to the graphene (optical edges or second-harmonic data), the top and bottom hBN thicknesses, and the ratio C_t/C_b;
   - test whether the AHE sign follows D or the layer that holds carriers, for example by checking whether the sign reversal occurs exactly at D = 0 or is offset;
   - outline a falsifiable prediction of the domain-boundary picture. For instance, how does the AHE magnitude scale with domain-wall density across the multiple ΔF = const steps in Fig. 3a? An AHE that grows with each additional domain-wall-motion step would be strong support.

   The Abstract and Discussion ("establishes domain boundaries ... as unique and pristine platforms") should be worded as conjecture until such a test exists.

5. **The theory–experiment comparison in Fig. 3b is qualitative, and how its parameters were chosen is unclear.** The calculation has two free parameters, ΔΦ and ΔU(0,0).
   - ΔΦ comes from KPFM on a different sample, at ambient and room temperature, while on a stamp or on SiO₂ (Extended Data Fig. 2).
   - ΔU(0,0) is "explained in the Supplementary Information". If it was fitted to the experimental boundary, then the "remarkable" agreement is partly by construction.
   - The calculation appears to be a non-interacting tight-binding band energy. It is unclear whether it includes self-consistent interlayer screening of D (which strongly reduces the rhombohedral gap), the electrostatic energy of the gates, or interaction-driven polarized states. These states lower the rhombohedral energy precisely in the regions near the black boxes in Fig. 3a.
   - It is also unclear which thermodynamic potential is compared. At fixed gate voltages the relevant quantity is not ΔU at fixed (n, D).

   The authors should:
   - state which parameters are fitted;
   - give the value of ΔU along each experimental contour, demonstrating that each blue dashed line maps to a constant ΔU and that successive lines are ordered consistently;
   - show the sensitivity of the boundary shape to ΔΦ within the KPFM uncertainty and to the tight-binding parameter set.

6. **The "switching at constant ΔF" assumption, used to extract thermodynamic quantities, is not justified.** The text (p. 3, p. 5) treats each R_xx jump as a ΔF = const contour and uses the field dependence of the boundary to extract a magnetic-susceptibility difference (Extended Data Fig. 5). This requires the domain-wall pinning barrier to be independent of n, D and B. It also requires the walls to move quasi-statically, without thermally activated creep that depends on sweep rate.

   No temperature, sweep-rate or waiting-time dependence of the switching boundary is shown. All data are at 1.5 K. The authors should show the switching threshold against sweep rate and at several temperatures. For a pinned first-order transition, the hysteresis width should shrink with temperature and the threshold may drift with rate. They should also state the assumptions and uncertainty of the susceptibility extraction in the main text, or else remove the claim from the main text.

7. **The magnetism claim needs fuller characterization.**
   - The Curie temperature of "around 8 K" (Extended Data Fig. 8) is given without the criterion used: onset of hysteresis, remanent R_yx(B = 0), or an Arrott-type analysis.
   - The B-dominant state is called a "paramagnet" on the basis of a linear R_yx at a few points at 1.5 K and 0.03 T. "Non-magnetic at 1.5 K in this (n, D) window" is the accurate statement.
   - The "negative D-even AHE" at the pentagon (Fig. 4i) shows only a nonlinear R_yx(B) with no hysteresis. A nonlinear Hall response also arises from multiband ordinary Hall transport, which is expected when rhombohedral and Bernal regions with different carrier types and mobilities conduct in parallel. Its temperature dependence alone does not distinguish the two.

   The authors should fit the pentagon data to a two-band ordinary Hall model as a null hypothesis, show remanence if any, and show the T-dependence of the hysteresis loops (Fig. 4c,g) in the main text or Extended Data.

8. **Reproducibility and device count.**
   - Main-text data come from device A only. Device B appears only for the AHE sign reversal.
   - It is not stated whether device B shows the Fig. 2/3 switching boundary, or how many cooldowns each device went through.
   - The "highly robust and reproducible" switching (Fig. 1d) shows about six cycles. Extended Data Fig. 9 is cited for "many cycles", but the cycle count, the drift in the R_xx levels, and the failure rate should be quantified.
   - It is not stated whether the switching boundary and the AHE survive thermal cycling to room temperature, which could relax the domain configuration.

   The authors should give a table listing devices, twist angles, cooldowns, which features each device shows, and cycle counts.

9. **The evidence that the hole-doped state is "mixed" is weak.** The absence of clear quantum oscillations in the hole-doped state is attributed to superposition of oscillations from the two domain types (Extended Data Fig. 3). Domain-wall scattering, disorder, or lower mobility would also suppress them. The authors should show the Fourier spectra of R_xx(1/B) in both states at comparable |n|. If the frequencies of the RB-mixed state can be identified with rhombohedral and Bernal Fermi-surface areas, the mixed-state assignment would be much firmer.

**C. Minor**

10. Priority claim (p. 7): "the first realization of a magnet-to-non-magnet nonvolatile phase transition controllable entirely by electrostatic means". This claim should be qualified. Gate-hysteretic switching of magnetic and correlated states has been reported in moiré graphene and in graphene/hBN devices with ferroelectric-like gate hysteresis, and ref. [45] is ionic rather than purely electrostatic. Please discuss these and state precisely what is new.
11. Terminology: the paper calls a structural (stacking) transition a "first-order quantum phase transition" (p. 2). The transition is between metastable structures driven by doping, and is hysteretic. "First-order structural transition" is more accurate.
12. Methods lack the Hall-bar dimensions, the hBN thicknesses, the values of C_t and C_b, and the gate leakage currents at the extreme gate voltages used to reach n = −15.5×10¹² cm⁻². These are needed to assess the charge-trapping concern (item 1).
13. Fig. 1 caption: "The Bernal regions is larger" should read "are larger". The caption assigns the −4.1 and −15.5×10¹² cm⁻² thresholds to "forward and backward scans, respectively", while the text refers to the −15.5 jump on the "blue curve". Please check the two are consistent with the legend arrows.
14. Fig. 3b: give the definition and sign convention of ΔU (in meV nm⁻², R minus B?), and mark which color favors which stacking. Fig. 3a: explain what the black-boxed regions near n ≈ 0 at large |D| represent in terms of the phase diagram (RB-mixed accessible there).
15. Fig. 4b,f: the B-dominant maps are noisy near n < 1×10¹² cm⁻². Please state whether this reflects contact resistance at low density, and give the current used (0.5 nA) in the caption.
16. KPFM (Fig. 1b, Extended Data Fig. 2): give the measurement conditions (ambient or not, tip calibration) and the uncertainty of the 22 mV work-function difference, since it enters the calculation directly.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add one direct structural probe in a gated device: Raman 2D mapping, SHG, or KPFM through the top gate after switching each way. It would settle concerns 1, 2 and 9 together and turn the paper's central inference into a demonstration.
2. Present the AHE from several Hall pairs and in both devices, with full loops and remanence after field training at ±B. Report σ_xy where possible, so that the D-odd sign reversal clearly does not depend on the measurement geometry.
3. Turn the domain-boundary hypothesis into a testable prediction: AHE magnitude against the number of domain-wall-motion steps in Fig. 3a, or against domain-wall density across devices with different twist angles. Test it, or present it explicitly as a conjecture.
4. Make the Fig. 3b comparison quantitative. State which parameters are fitted, give ΔU along each experimental contour, and include screening, or discuss its effect on the predicted large-D rhombohedral pocket.
