Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript maps the semimetallic regime of rhombohedral hexalayer graphene, where conduction and valence bands overlap at low displacement field, using dual-gated transport and quantum-oscillation fermiology. It reports a field-driven, flavor-selective band inversion; a superconducting-like state (SC1, onset ~170 mK) confined to a "leaf-shaped" region where two emergent Fermi surfaces coexist; a ferrovalley orbital magnet near E = 0; and a second, "ferroelectric" orbital magnet near n = 0, E ≈ −35 mV/nm, whose magnetic-hysteresis polarity reverses with E. The authors read this reversal as evidence for a ΔP·M multiferroic order parameter. The semimetallic window of thick rhombohedral graphene has seen little study, and a careful fermiology of it would be valuable. However, the two headline claims in the title, superconductivity and ferroelectric orbital magnetism, rest at present on indirect signatures.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The n–E transport map (Fig. 1e) is high quality and very rich, and the semimetallic regime of N = 6 rhombohedral graphene is new territory.
- The fermiology is systematic. FFT maps along many horizontal and vertical cuts (Figs. 2, 3, S3–S8) plus the normalized frequency f_v give a concrete way to link resistive phase boundaries to Lifshitz transitions. The assignment of layer polarization from the tilt of boundaries in the n–E plane is a useful idea.
- The E-controlled reversal of the magnetic-hysteresis polarity (Fig. 4e) is, if it survives the checks below, a striking observation that goes beyond the pentalayer multiferroicity of ref. 10.
- Supporting data are extensive: temperature dependences (Figs. S1, S2, S6, S11), in-plane field maps (Fig. S9) and raw oscillation data (Figs. S4, S7, S8).

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The superconductivity claim lacks direct evidence, and the observed drops are small.** SC1 never reaches zero resistance. In Fig. 1f, R_xx falls only to a finite value below ~170 mK. SC2 (Fig. S12b) drops from ~175 Ω to ~140 Ω, about 20%, with T_c ≈ 80 mK. The text attributes the residual resistance to "insufficient filtering" (p. 6), but the same set-up resolves 9 mK base temperature and fine quantum oscillations, and no electron-temperature calibration is given. A finite-resistance drop with a nonlinear dV/dI peak (Figs. 3e, 3f) can also come from a filamentary or percolating superconducting path through an inhomogeneous region, from Joule-heating-driven nonlinearity in a strongly T-dependent correlated metal, or from a current-driven transition between competing correlated states. The title and abstract nonetheless state "superconductivity". The authors should:
   - show R_xx(T) and dV/dI on several contact pairs, and in at least one of the other two devices;
   - give the residual resistance as a fraction of the normal-state value for each pair;
   - provide an electron-temperature calibration (for example, Shubnikov–de Haas amplitude against mixing-chamber temperature);
   - show I–V curves with a clear critical current and its B⊥ and T dependence, rather than only dV/dI color maps;
   - if possible, look for Fraunhofer-like or Little–Parks-type phase-coherent signatures.
   
   Otherwise the claim should be stated as "superconducting-like resistance drop" throughout, including the title.

2. **The Pauli-limit violation is inconsistent with the out-of-plane critical field unless field alignment is extraordinarily good.** The text says the low-resistance state "persists up to an in-plane magnetic field of approximately 6 T — more than an order of magnitude higher than the estimated Pauli limit of ~300 mT" (p. 6, Fig. 3g, Fig. S9). Yet Fig. 3e shows the SC1 features suppressed by an out-of-plane field of a few mT, and Fig. S12d gives B⊥,c ≈ 1 mT for SC2. A misalignment of only 0.05° at 6 T already produces ~5 mT perpendicular, so either the sample was aligned to better than ~0.05°, which is not reported, or the low-resistance state at several tesla is not the same state that a few mT of B⊥ destroys. In addition:
   - Fig. S9 shows that the in-plane field strongly reshapes the phase diagram, suppressing the triangular orbital-magnet region and moving boundaries. Following the "left blue strip" in a single n–E line cut (Fig. 3g) does not establish that the same phase is tracked.
   - In a ~2 nm thick hexalayer with layer-polarized carriers, an in-plane field also couples orbitally.
   - A Pauli-limit violation is expected for any spin-polarized or valley-polarized pairing, so it does not by itself single out an unconventional state.
   
   The authors should:
   - state how the in-plane field was aligned and give the residual-angle uncertainty;
   - show dV/dI against I at several B∥ values (for example 0, 2, 4, 6 T), including the B⊥ dependence at finite B∥;
   - define the critical field by a stated criterion (for example, 50% of the normal-state resistance);
   - map the SC1 boundary in n–E at each B∥, so that the same region is being followed.

3. **The ΔP·M interpretation of the hysteresis-polarity reversal is not established against simpler explanations.** The key observation is that the sign of the R_xy loop in B⊥ flips between E = −35.5 and −36.2 mV/nm at fixed n (Fig. 4e), which the authors take to mean that "M·B alone does not capture the system's energetics" (p. 7). Several alternatives give the same sign flip without an electric polarization:
   - **Change in the sign of the anomalous Hall response.** In orbital magnets the anomalous Hall conductivity, and even the orbital magnetization, can change sign as the Fermi level moves through Berry-curvature hot spots at fixed valley polarization. This is well known from moiré orbital Chern magnets. A small change in E near a band edge (this region lies next to the band-inversion and Lifshitz features of Fig. 2) could flip the sign of σ_xy for a given valley polarization, reversing the apparent loop direction with no change in the coupling to B.
   - **Branch mixing.** The reversal occurs "when the magnetic hysteresis is the largest", inside the narrow E window where the E-hysteresis itself is open (Fig. 4a–d). A B-sweep there may switch the system between the two E-branches rather than reversing M within one state.
   - **R_xx–R_xy mixing.** R_xx is itself hysteretic and of kΩ scale (Fig. S2c), and the Hall signal is only a few hundred Ω. Without antisymmetrization (forward sweep at +B against backward sweep at −B) an R_xx admixture could dominate the loop shape.
   
   The authors should:
   - antisymmetrize all loops;
   - show R_xx loops alongside R_xy at the same E values;
   - measure loops at fixed E prepared from both E-branches (approached from above and from below);
   - track the sign of the anomalous Hall step continuously across E and n to see whether it follows a Berry-curvature sign change;
   - present a minimal free-energy model showing that a ΔP·M term reproduces the observed sign change and the linear-in-B⊥ shrinkage of the E-loop (Fig. 4c,d).

4. **The "ferroelectric" character, a spontaneous switchable electric polarization, is inferred only from the Hall signal.** The E-hysteresis in Fig. 4a,b and Fig. 1i is seen through R_xy under a stabilizing 50 mT field. Two things are missing:
   - **An independent signature of layer polarization.** There is no hysteresis in a quantity that is not tied to M, such as R_xx at B = 0, penetration-field or layer-resolved capacitance, or the positions of Landau levels and band edges in forward versus backward sweeps.
   - **Exclusion of gate hysteresis.** Charge trapping in hBN, the SiO₂ or the Si back gate, or slow relaxation of the global Si gate held at −90 to −100 V (Methods) could offset E by the few mV/nm involved (the switching fields differ by only ~3 mV/nm: −34 against −37 mV/nm).
   
   The "unipolar" loop, with both switching fields on the same side, is equally consistent with a magnetoelectric switching of M driven by E-tunable Berry curvature, without spontaneous P. The authors should:
   - show that sharp, independent features (for example Landau-level crossings or the CI boundary) do not shift between forward and backward E sweeps over the same range;
   - show the dependence on sweep rate and sweep range;
   - show B = 0 data for R_xx and, ideally, capacitance.
   
   Without these, "ferroelectric" should be replaced by a description of the observed E-hysteresis.

**B. Should address**

5. **The flavor-resolved band picture and the band inversion are schematic, not calculated.** Figs. 2g–j are hand-drawn diagrams based on a hypothesized two-by-two emergent-gap flavor splitting (pp. 3–4). The tight-binding model in Fig. 1a–d is non-interacting and does not contain the correlated gap. The paper itself notes that the E = 0 Fermi surfaces are two-fold, not the four-fold the picture predicts, and that the ferrovalley triangle contradicts the assumed layer antiferromagnet (p. 5). The authors should carry out at least a self-consistent Hartree–Fock calculation on the 2N-band model with the assumed flavor order. They should then compare quantitatively the predicted Lifshitz-line positions and tilts in n–E, and the f_v values, with Fig. 2a–f. This is what turns "consistent with a band inversion" into evidence for one.

6. **The calibration from gate voltages to n, E and U is not given.** The Methods give no hBN thicknesses, gate capacitances, or formulas for n and E. They also do not say how the experimental E maps onto the tight-binding U (Fig. 1a spans U = ±2 meV/layer, while the experiment spans ±100 mV/nm), including screening. The f_v normalization (f_v = f_FFT·e/nh), and therefore every degeneracy assignment (peaks at 1/2 or 1/4), depends on an accurate n. The authors should:
   - give the calibration and say how n was cross-checked (Hall density, or Landau-fan slopes from the full-metal regions);
   - state the uncertainty in f_v;
   - give the E ↔ U conversion used to compare Fig. 1a with Fig. 1e, and the 100 mV/nm gap-opening threshold with theory.

7. **The identification of the "emergent Fermi surfaces" FS1 and FS2 needs more support.** The claim (Fig. 3b–d, p. 6) is that FS1 and FS2 are new pockets inside a reconstructed band, two-fold degenerate because "the higher f_v peak exceeds the sum of FS1 and FS2 by 0.5". Four things need to be addressed:
   - These low-f_v features lie near the bottom of the resolvable frequency range for a 0.4–1.6 T window. The FFT window, the background subtraction and the apodization are not stated, and the low-frequency end of an FFT is the most prone to artifacts from the non-oscillatory background.
   - Combination frequencies (f_i − f_j) from magnetic breakdown or chemical-potential oscillations could also produce low-frequency branches that track differences of larger orbits. The authors should check whether FS1 and FS2 coincide with differences of the major branches.
   - The rapid disappearance with temperature (Fig. S6) is cited as evidence of an "emergent nature", but it could simply reflect a large cyclotron mass. A Lifshitz–Kosevich fit giving m* for FS1, FS2 and the main branches would be informative.
   - The sum rule is violated over wide ranges (Fig. S5), and the violations are attributed to unresolved heavy pockets. If such pockets are present, the degeneracy inferred from the sum rule (two-fold FS1/FS2) becomes uncertain.
   
   The text of Fig. S6 also gives the range as "1.5 × 10¹² < |n| < 33 × 10¹² cm⁻²", which is presumably 3.3.

8. **The magnetic field used for fermiology may itself alter the phases being mapped.** The fermiology uses B⊥ up to 1.6–2 T. The orbital magnetic phases respond to tens of mT, and Fig. 2a at 1.5 T differs visibly from Fig. 1e at 0 T. Identifying zero-field resistive boundaries with Lifshitz transitions seen at 0.4–1.6 T assumes the band structure does not change with B in this range, which is questionable for flavor-polarized states with large orbital moments. The authors should show how the phase boundaries move with B⊥ (for example, n–E maps at 0, 0.5 and 1.5 T) and restrict the claimed correspondence to boundaries that do not move.

9. **Reproducibility across devices is asserted, not shown.** Methods state "Three devices were fabricated and measured with similar results", but every figure appears to come from one device and one contact configuration (Fig. S13f). The authors should show, in at least a second device, the central results: SC1 and its in-plane field dependence, the Fig. 4e polarity reversal, and the leaf-shaped fermiology. They should also report whether the E-hysteresis and the polarity reversal survive thermal cycling. Given how small the SC and ferroelectric regions are in n–E, their presence in only one device would considerably weaken the claims.

10. **The stacking integrity of the measured channel after fabrication is not verified.** Rhombohedral domains are known to relax to Bernal stacking during transfer, annealing and etching. Raman mapping (Fig. S13c) was done on the bare flake before stacking and etching. Bernal or mixed-stacking inclusions within the Hall bar would give exactly the kind of inhomogeneity that could produce a non-zero residual resistance in SC1 and SC2 (concern 1). The authors should report post-fabrication Raman of the encapsulated channel, or argue from transport (for example, the absence of Bernal-specific Landau-level sequences).

11. **The valley-polarized assignment of the triangular phase is weakly supported.** The text states that valley-polarized orbital magnetism "is incompatible with the widely assumed layer antiferromagnet". The supporting argument in Fig. S9 is that an in-plane field suppresses the triangle because Zeeman energy favours competing states. A valley-polarized state can also be spin-polarized, so this argument does not discriminate between flavor orders. The authors should state which flavor structure they propose, or present the assignment as tentative, perhaps supported by the size of ΔR_xy compared with the expected Chern number.

12. **The transition temperature of the finite-field multiferroic is inconsistently stated.** The main text gives "T_c = 1.5 K (Fig. S11)". Fig. S11 instead describes a vanishing coercive field at 1.3 K, soft switching at 2 K, a Hall signal surviving to 3 K, and a "two-step transition". The authors should define the quantity quoted as T_c and give both temperatures with criteria.

13. **The proposed link between superconductivity and the multiferroic phases is speculative.** The statement "Because both multiferroic order is accompanied by a superconducting state, we note a potential connection" rests on proximity in the n–E plane. SC1 lies in the leaf region next to the triangle, and SC2 lies near the comet (Fig. S12a appears to place SC2 at n ≈ −2 × 10¹² cm⁻², E ≈ 35–55 mV/nm, which is not obviously adjacent to the comet at n ≈ 0, E ≈ −35 mV/nm). The authors should either show the relative positions on a common map and give a physical argument, or soften the statement.

**C. Minor**

14. The abstract places the ferroelectric state "at large fields around charge neutrality", but it sits at E ≈ −35 mV/nm, well below the ~100 mV/nm band-insulator threshold. Please reword.
15. Fig. 4c,d give n = −0.46 × 10¹¹ cm⁻², while Fig. S10b,c give n = +0.46 × 10¹¹ cm⁻² for apparently the same maps. Please reconcile the signs.
16. The Fig. S10 caption refers to "White arrows in e to f", which should be b and c. The Fig. S12a caption says its dashed line marks the Fig. 3g linecut, while the Fig. 3g caption refers to Fig. S12a for two superconducting phases. Please make these cross-references consistent.
17. "~30 meV/nm" (p. 6) should be mV/nm. Also: "electron electron-doped" (Fig. S3 title), "hole hole-doped" (Fig. S5 text), "both multiferroic order is", and "situate the correlated insulator" (p. 3).
18. The labels for the orbital-magnet phases differ between places: Fig. 1 legend "Multiferroic orbital magnetism" and "Unipolar multiferroic orbital magnetism"; caption "Ferrovalley" and "Ferroelectric"; text "ferrovalley" and "ferroelectric". Please use one consistent nomenclature.
19. The layer-polarization formula in Methods is missing parentheses: it should read (|ψ_top|² − |ψ_bot|²)/(|ψ_top|² + |ψ_bot|²).
20. The gap extraction in Fig. S1b fits only a narrow high-temperature range. Please state the fit window, and note that the 29 meV CI gap is far larger than the U scale of Fig. 1a.
21. Give the excitation currents used for the lock-in measurements and for the zero-bias SC data, and state whether R_xy loops were antisymmetrized (see concern 3).

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Rescope the title and abstract to match the evidence: a "superconducting-like" state, and "electric-field-switchable orbital magnetism with E-reversible hysteresis polarity". Upgrade the wording only if zero resistance, a well-aligned in-plane critical field and an independent polarization signature are demonstrated.
2. Add a self-consistent Hartree–Fock calculation for the proposed 2×2 (and 3:1) flavor orders, and compare its Lifshitz lines and f_v values quantitatively with Figs. 2 and 3. This would turn the fermiology into the strongest part of the paper.
3. Include data from a second device for the three headline observations (SC1, the Fig. 4e polarity reversal, and the leaf-shaped emergent Fermi surfaces), together with post-fabrication Raman of the channel.
4. Add a short methods section on the FFT analysis (window, background, resolution in f_v), the n/E calibration, and the field-alignment procedure, so readers can judge the low-frequency and high-B∥ claims.
