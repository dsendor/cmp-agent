Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript maps the semimetallic regime of rhombohedral hexalayer graphene, where conduction and valence bands overlap at low displacement field. It uses quantum-oscillation fermiology (the normalized frequency f_v) to tie a dense network of resistance features to Lifshitz transitions of a two-flavor band structure, and from this infers an electric-field-driven band inversion in one flavor. On this map the authors report a superconducting-like state (SC1, onset about 170 mK) with a large in-plane critical field, a butterfly-hysteresis "ferrovalley" orbital magnet near E = 0, and a second orbital magnet near n = 0, E ≈ −35 mV/nm. They interpret the second magnet's unipolar electric-field hysteresis and E-reversible magnetic hysteresis as evidence for a ΔP·M multiferroic order parameter. Studying the overlapping-band regime of thick rhombohedral graphene is timely, and the fermiology dataset is extensive. However, the two headline claims in the title (superconductivity and ferroelectric orbital magnetism) currently rest on indirect transport signatures that have plausible alternative explanations.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A very detailed, high-quality n–E–B dataset on a hexalayer rhombohedral device, with fermiology shown along many cuts (Figs. 2, 3, S3–S8), including raw data alongside the FFTs.
- A coherent, physically appealing picture: many phase boundaries in Fig. 1e follow from layer-polarized band edges whose tilts in the n–E plane identify top- or bottom-layer character (Fig. 2a).
- The finite-E orbital magnet (Fig. 4) behaves unusually: its E-hysteresis is unipolar, and the polarity of its B-hysteresis reverses with E (Fig. 4e). This behavior differs from the pentalayer multiferroic of ref. 10 and could be interesting if established.
- A low-resistance state that is suppressed by millitesla out-of-plane fields but survives several tesla in plane (Figs. 3e, 3g, S9). If confirmed as superconductivity, this would add to the growing family of rhombohedral superconductors.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Superconductivity in SC1 (and SC2) is not established, yet it appears in the title and abstract.
   - What the data show: the resistance never reaches zero. In Fig. 1f, R_xx drops from roughly 200 Ω to roughly 80–100 Ω. For SC2 (Fig. S12b) the drop is only from about 175 Ω to about 140 Ω, about 20%. The text attributes the residual resistance to "insufficient filtering" but tests this in no way.
   - Why the current evidence is not specific: a dV/dI peak at finite bias (Figs. 1f, 3e, 3f, S12c,d) is not unique to superconductivity. Any state whose resistance depends strongly on temperature, such as a resistive drop at an isospin or first-order transition, will produce such peaks through Joule or electron heating. A partial resistance drop can also come from superconducting filaments or puddles in an inhomogeneous channel.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Remeasure with improved low-temperature filtering, or with lower excitation, until the resistance saturates.
     - Report R_xx(T) from several voltage-probe pairs of the Hall bar (contacts 1–6 in Fig. S13f), to test whether the residual resistance is spatially uniform.
     - Show R_xx(T) curves at several B⊥ and B∥ values, demonstrating a systematic suppression of T_c.
     - Show I–V curves with a well-defined critical current and its temperature dependence.
     - Ideally, show a phase-coherence signature, such as Fraunhofer-like patterns or flux-periodic magnetoresistance.
   - What to change if zero resistance cannot be reached: retitle and reword the claim as "superconducting-like", as the main text already does in places.

2. The order-parameter conclusion ("ΔP·M", "ferroelectric orbital magnetism") does not follow from the data as presented.
   - Assumption 1, that R_xy measures M: the argument in Fig. 4e is that the sign of the magnetic hysteresis tracks M, so its E-driven reversal implies coupling to P. But the anomalous Hall signal measures the Berry curvature of the occupied states, not the orbital magnetization. In graphene moiré and rhombohedral systems, the sign relation between σ_xy and M is known to reverse when the chemical potential, or the band filling within a flavor, moves through the Berry-curvature hot spot. This is the mechanism behind the electrically switchable magnetism reported in twisted mono-bilayer graphene. A valley-polarized state with fixed valley index and no "ferroelectric" degree of freedom could therefore show a reversal of hysteresis polarity as E is tuned.
   - Assumption 2, that P is bistable: P is never measured. No capacitance, layer-polarization or penetration-field measurement is presented, and the "3:1 polarized state" is speculative. A unipolar E-hysteresis loop, with both switching fields on the same side (about −34 and −37 mV/nm), is the generic signature of a first-order transition between two phases with different layer polarization and magnetization. It is not, by itself, evidence of a ferroelectric that holds two remanent polarizations at a common field.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Analyze the shift of the switching fields with B⊥. Fig. 4c,d show the loop shrinking linearly with B⊥, and the slope dE_c/dB⊥ ~ ΔM/ΔP gives a quantitative constraint. Compare it with estimates of ΔM, and of ΔP from the band structure.
     - Discuss and rule out the Berry-curvature sign-change mechanism explicitly, for example by computing the sign of σ_xy and of M for candidate flavor configurations within their tight-binding model.
     - Show whether a counterpart state exists at E ≈ +35 mV/nm. The ±E asymmetry is itself informative about whether the state is a polarization-switching phase.
     - Unless these are done, reword the claim to "an electrically switchable orbital magnet with E-reversible Hall hysteresis".

3. Extrinsic origins of the E-hysteresis and the Hall hysteresis are not excluded (Figs. 4a–d, 1h, 1i, S2, S10, S11).
   - Gate hysteresis: hysteresis in a dual-gate sweep can arise from charge trapping in the hBN or at the SiO₂/Si interface, especially since the Si back gate is held at −90 to −100 V for contacts (Methods). The authors should show:
     - that the same E sweep in a nearby non-magnetic region (for example, the right end of the "comet", Fig. S10) gives no hysteresis at the same sweep rate;
     - that the switching fields are independent of sweep rate and sweep range;
     - that n-sweeps at fixed E are consistent with the E-sweeps.
   - Longitudinal admixture in R_xy: R_xx itself is strongly hysteretic in the same regions (Fig. S2a,c,e), and R_xy appears to be raw, at a single B = 50 mT. Admixture of R_xx into R_xy could therefore generate or distort the hysteresis loops. The authors should:
     - antisymmetrize, using R_xy(+B, sweep up) against R_xy(−B, sweep down) and the corresponding E-sweep pairs;
     - show B = 0 Hall data, or data from both field polarities, for Fig. 4a–d.
   - Why it matters: the "unipolar" character, the sign reversal in Fig. 4e, and the step amplitudes (about 300–400 Ω) all depend on this.

B. Should address

4. The fermiology assignments are underdetermined and need independent support (Figs. 2b–f, 3b–d, S5).
   - Carrier type and degeneracy: the sign s_i (electron or hole) and degeneracy k_i of each pocket are assigned so that Σ s_i k_i f_i = 1. In several regions, however, the sum rule is violated (Fig. S5f,i), and different sign and degeneracy choices can satisfy it equally well. The electron-like character of the pockets that underpins the "band overlap" and "band inversion" claims (for example, "a new electron pocket emerges in Fig. 2c") should be established independently. Options include the sign of the Landau-level slope dn/dB in fan diagrams, the Hall sign of the minority carriers, or Lifshitz–Kosevich mass analysis comparing electron-like and hole-like branches.
   - Field effects on the phases: the oscillations are analyzed over B⊥ ≈ 0.4–1.6 T. In rhombohedral graphene, phase boundaries between isospin states shift appreciably with B⊥, and orbital magnetic states are favored by B⊥. Fig. 2a (1.5 T) and Fig. 1e (0 T) differ visibly. The authors should show that the Lifshitz lines identified at finite B extrapolate to the zero-field boundaries used to place SC1 and the magnets.
   - FFT procedure: please give the field window, background subtraction, windowing function and resulting f_v resolution. This matters especially for the low-f_v pockets FS1 and FS2 "reaching zero frequency" (Fig. 3b,c), where background subtraction dominates.

5. The "electric-field-driven band inversion" is inferred qualitatively and not tested against a quantitative model (Figs. 1a–d, 2g–j, 2f).
   - What the inference rests on: the opposite tilts of the dotted and dashed lines in the n–E plane, plus the cartoons in Fig. 2g–j. The flavor-split bands themselves are drawn by hand and are not computed.
   - Missing model details: the tight-binding parameters (γ0–γ4, δ, Δ) used for hexalayer are not listed, and the band overlap at U = 0 is very sensitive to γ2 and γ3. The mapping between U (meV/layer, ±2 in Fig. 1a) and the experimental E (±100 mV/nm) is not given, and screening is not included.
   - What the authors should do: include at least a self-consistent Hartree, or a phenomenological flavor-split, model whose Lifshitz lines are overlaid on Fig. 2a. They should show that the predicted slopes, crossing point (E ≈ 42 mV/nm at n = −1.26×10¹² cm⁻²) and threshold field for the band-insulator gap (about 100 mV/nm) match.
   - Without this, "band inversion" (a term with topological connotations, given refs. 25–27) should be described more cautiously, for example as a crossing of layer-polarized band edges within one flavor.

6. The Pauli-limit comparison and its interpretation need qualification (Figs. 3g, S9).
   - Ambiguous reference temperature: the claimed violation by more than an order of magnitude compares a "low-resistance state" persisting to about 6 T with a Pauli field of about 300 mT computed from the 170 mK onset. The S9 maps in which SC1 "persists" at 2 and 5 T show only R_xx at an unspecified temperature. The authors should show R_xx(T) or dV/dI(I) at several B∥, demonstrating that a superconducting transition, rather than a normal-state resistance minimum, survives.
   - Normal-state polarization: if the normal state is spin polarized (the fermiology finds two-fold degenerate pockets in this region), the Pauli limit does not apply. The large B∥ would then be expected and would not be anomalous, as in spin-polarized rhombohedral trilayer superconductivity (ref. 11).
   - Field alignment: the misalignment of the in-plane field, and the resulting B⊥ component at 6 T, should be quantified, given that SC1 is killed by a few mT of B⊥ (Fig. 3e).

7. The link between SC1 and "two emergent Fermi surfaces" (a "dual carrier origin") rests on visual coincidence of regions (Figs. 3a, 3d).
   - What is needed: a direct overlay of the zero-field SC region, defined by a criterion such as R_xx below 50% of normal-state resistance at base temperature, on the FS1/FS2 manifolds of Fig. 3d. A statement of whether SC appears anywhere outside the overlap region (for example, SC2 in Fig. S12a) is also needed.
   - Temperature dependence of FS1/FS2: Fig. S6 shows FS1/FS2 fading quickly with T, which the authors call evidence of "emergent nature". This could simply reflect a large cyclotron mass. A Lifshitz–Kosevich mass extraction would distinguish the two, and would also test whether these pockets are interaction-driven.

8. Reproducibility and sample characterization.
   - Device coverage: the Methods state that "three devices were fabricated and measured with similar results", but all data appear to come from one device. The authors should show at least the n–E map, SC1 and the finite-E magnet in a second device, or state clearly which features were reproduced.
   - hBN alignment: the alignment of the graphene to either hBN is not reported. Alignment would produce a moiré potential that strongly changes rhombohedral multilayers and could explain the ±E asymmetry in where SC1 (positive E) and the finite-E magnet (negative E) appear.
   - Stacking integrity: rhombohedral stacking is known to relax to Bernal during transfer. Raman or other confirmation after fabrication, and a check that no domain wall crosses the Hall bar, should be shown.
   - Thermal cycling: whether the hysteretic states are reproducible after thermal cycling should be stated.

9. The calibration of n and E is not described.
   - What is missing: the hBN thicknesses, gate capacitances, the definition of E (D/ε₀ or otherwise), the zero offsets, and the uncertainty in n.
   - Why it matters: f_v = f_FFT·e/(nh) is normalized by n, so a few-percent capacitance error shifts all f_v values and the sum-rule tests. The location of features such as "E ≈ −35 mV/nm" also depends on the calibration.
   - What to report: the calibration, for example from Landau-level fan slopes and the E-symmetry of the band-insulator gap.

10. The claims about the ferrovalley triangle need clearer reasoning (Figs. 1h, S2, S9).
   - Internal inconsistency: the main text says the butterfly hysteresis is called ferrovalley "due to the absence of electric dipole and Berry curvature at E = 0". Orbital magnetism requires Berry curvature, so this statement is internally inconsistent; the intended argument should be stated precisely.
   - Layer antiferromagnet argument: the state is "incompatible with the widely assumed layer antiferromagnet". But the triangle is at n ≈ −0.68×10¹² cm⁻², not at charge neutrality, so the comparison with the CI ground state needs justification.
   - In-plane field argument: the argument in Fig. S9 that suppression by in-plane field confirms valley polarization is not straightforward. The authors should explain which competing state gains Zeeman energy.
   - Temperature scale: the transition temperature is given only as "> 3.6 K" (Fig. S2h), and all S2 data are at 1 K rather than base temperature. This should be reconciled with the other temperature scales quoted.

11. The suggested connection between SC2 and the ferroelectric orbital magnet is not supported by the data shown.
   - The text says SC2 "appears in the nearby region of the ferroelectric orbital magnetism (Fig. S12)". But Fig. S12a places the two low-resistance pockets at n ≈ −1 to −2×10¹² cm⁻² and E ≈ +40 to +55 mV/nm. The finite-E magnet is at n ≈ 0 and E ≈ −35 mV/nm (Fig. 1e, green square). Likewise, SC1 (n ≈ −1.7×10¹² cm⁻², E ≈ +35 mV/nm) is not adjacent to the ferrovalley triangle in Fig. 1e.
   - What to do: clarify the positions, or remove the "potential connection between these phases".

12. Electron temperature and heating.
   - Base temperature is quoted as 9 mK, but the electron temperature is not calibrated. The SC2 onset (about 80 mK) and the critical currents (tens of nA through a 4.7 MΩ bias resistor) are small.
   - The excitation current used for the R_xx maps and for R(T) should be stated.
   - The authors should show that R_xx(T) in Figs. 1f and S12b is independent of excitation.

C. Minor

13. Inconsistencies in numbers and labels:
   - Fig. 4c,d give n = −0.46×10¹¹ cm⁻², while the corresponding maps in Fig. S10b,c give n = +0.46×10¹¹ cm⁻².
   - The Fig. S10 caption refers to "white arrows in e to f", but the panels are b and c.
   - "E increases above ~30 meV/nm" should be mV/nm.
   - The Fig. S6 caption gives "1.5×10¹² < |n| < 33×10¹²", presumably 3.3×10¹².
   - Fig. S11a labels R_xy in kΩ with values up to 3000.
   - "electron electron-doped" (Fig. S3) and "hole hole-doped" (Fig. S5 text).
   - The layer-polarization formula in the Methods lacks parentheses.
   - The text reference "f₃ Fig. S5i" is garbled.

14. The Arrhenius gaps in Fig. S1b (29 meV for the CI, 23 meV for the band insulator) are fitted over a narrow, high-temperature window, about 20–45 K. The caption itself says the gap is temperature-dependent in this range. Please state the fit range and uncertainty, or describe these values as rough activation energies.

15. Several transition temperatures are quoted for the finite-E magnet: T_c = 1.5 K (main text), coercivity vanishing at 1.3 K, and the Hall signal vanishing at 3 K (Fig. S11). Please define the criterion behind each number.

16. The Methods should give:
   - the lock-in excitation current;
   - the Hall-bar dimensions and which contact pairs were used for R_xx and R_xy;
   - the B⊥ ranges used for each FFT;
   - the sweep rates used for the hysteresis measurements.

17. Please compare the phase diagram, superconductivity and anomalous Hall regions explicitly with the concurrent hexalayer studies cited as refs. 16 and 19. The specific novelty of this work (the semimetal fermiology and the finite-E magnet) should be stated against them.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Strengthen the superconductivity evidence with improved filtering and multi-contact R(T), plus I–V and R(T) at finite B⊥ and B∥. If zero resistance remains unattainable, reframe SC1 and SC2 as "superconducting-like" throughout, including the title.
2. Make the ΔP·M claim quantitative. Use the linear B⊥-dependence of the switching fields to extract ΔM/ΔP. Compare it with a model of candidate flavor configurations that computes both M and σ_xy. Explicitly exclude the Berry-curvature sign-change explanation for the reversal of hysteresis polarity.
3. Add a self-consistent or phenomenological flavor-split band model with stated parameters and an explicit U↔E mapping. Overlay its predicted Lifshitz lines on Fig. 2a to make the band-inversion picture quantitative.
4. Include data from a second device and report the hBN alignment and the post-fabrication stacking checks. This would establish that the phase diagram, SC1 and the finite-E magnet are intrinsic to rhombohedral hexalayer graphene.
