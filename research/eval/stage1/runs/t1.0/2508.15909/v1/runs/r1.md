Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript maps the semimetallic regime of dual-gated rhombohedral hexalayer graphene at small displacement field, where conduction and valence bands overlap. That regime has received less attention than the gapped, single-band regime at large field. Using quantum-oscillation fermiology, the authors assign several resistance boundaries to Lifshitz transitions of a two-flavor band structure and argue for a field-driven band inversion in one flavor. They also report a "superconducting-like" state (SC1, onset about 170 mK) inside a small "leaf" region where two emergent Fermi surfaces overlap, and an electric-field-hysteretic anomalous Hall state near n = 0, E ≈ −35 mV/nm that they call ferroelectric orbital magnetism with a ΔP·M order parameter. The phase diagram and the fermiology are a useful contribution. The two headline claims, superconductivity (in the title) and ferroelectricity, rest on evidence that is currently indirect and open to simpler explanations.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The device is high quality and gives clean quantum oscillations over a wide n–E range. The normalized-frequency (f_v) analysis is carried out systematically along many cuts (Figs. 2, 3, S3–S8). It gives a concrete, testable link between resistance features in the n–E map and Lifshitz transitions.
- Electron pockets at negative n (Fig. 2c, 2f) are directly observed, which gives experimental access to the band overlap in a regime that is usually inferred only from theory.
- The reversal of the magnetic-hysteresis polarity with E at fixed n (Fig. 4e) is a striking observation. If it is properly established, it would distinguish this state from the pentalayer multiferroic of Ref. 10.
- The phase diagram is broad and clearly presented (Fig. 1e). It ties together the correlated insulator, the ferrovalley triangle, the superconducting-like pockets and the comet-shaped hysteretic region, and is a valuable reference for the field.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The superconductivity claim, including the title, is not established by the data shown.
   - Where: Fig. 1f, Fig. 3e–g, Fig. S12, and the final paragraph before Fig. 4.
   - What the data show: in SC1 the resistance falls from about 200 Ω to about 100 Ω (Fig. 1f inset) and never approaches zero. In SC2 it falls from about 175 Ω to about 140 Ω, roughly 20% (Fig. S12b). The authors attribute the residual resistance to "insufficient filtering", but they show no evidence that filtering is the limitation. The dilution refrigerator reaches 9 mK, and Tc ≈ 170 mK should be easy to resolve.
   - Why it matters: a partial resistance drop together with a nonlinear dV/dI that is suppressed by a few mT of B⊥ is also consistent with a percolating or filamentary superconducting path. It is also consistent with phase coexistence near a first-order boundary; the leaf region is very narrow in n–E, so density or displacement-field inhomogeneity across the Hall bar would sample adjacent phases. A third option is a correlated-state crossover whose resistance depends on current.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Report the electron temperature, the filtering stages and the excitation current.
     - Show R(T) and dV/dI from several contact pairs (pins 1–6 in Fig. S13f), so that a filament or partial-path scenario can be tested.
     - Report the fraction of the Hall bar that is in the leaf phase at a given gate setting.
     - Show the n and E dependence of the residual resistance. For example: does the minimum resistance improve at the centre of the leaf and grow toward its edges?
     - Look for Fraunhofer-like or Josephson-like modulation of the critical current in small B⊥, or any other phase-coherent signature.
   - Without these, the title and abstract should say "superconducting-like" consistently, as the main text does.

2. The claim that SC1 survives about 6 T in plane, "more than an order of magnitude" above the Pauli limit, does not account for field misalignment.
   - Where: Fig. 3g, Fig. S9, and the text near Fig. 3.
   - Why it matters: the out-of-plane critical field of the nonlinear dV/dI is only a few mT for SC1 (Fig. 3e, ±10 mT axis) and about 1 mT for SC2 (Fig. S12d). At 6 T, a misalignment of only 0.05° gives about 5 mT out of plane. Robustness to in-plane field is therefore surprising, and the paper does not say how the in-plane field was aligned or how alignment was checked.
   - There is also an internal inconsistency. The text quotes about 6 T, while Fig. S9 states that SC1 "shrinks" at 5 T and closes only at 10 T. And Fig. 3g tracks a resistance minimum, not a superconducting transition.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Describe the rotator or alignment procedure and the residual misalignment, for example from the Hall voltage measured in "in-plane" field.
     - Show R(T) curves at several fixed B∥ and extract Tc(B∥), so the Pauli-limit comparison is made with a measured transition rather than a colour-map feature.
     - State which Tc criterion (onset or midpoint) was used for the ~300 mT estimate.
     - If the violation survives, discuss the candidate mechanisms specifically, for example a spin-polarized or spin-triplet pairing state as reported in rhombohedral trilayer graphene.

3. The ferroelectric interpretation of the comet-region hysteresis has two simpler alternatives that are not ruled out.
   - Where: Fig. 4, Figs. S10 and S11, and the paragraph on ΔP·M.
   - First alternative, ordinary first-order hysteresis.
     - The E-hysteresis is "unipolar": both switching fields lie near −34 and −37 mV/nm (Fig. 1i, Fig. 4a,b). Ferroelectric switching implies two states of opposite polarization at the same applied field, switchable in both directions, with remanence.
     - A narrow hysteresis window between two nearby negative fields is what one expects from an ordinary first-order transition between two phases, for example between a layer-polarized and a partially polarized metal, with metastability on both sides.
     - The linear shrinking of the loop with B⊥ (Fig. 4c,d) fits a Zeeman-type M·B term shifting a first-order boundary. It does not show that a polarization is being switched.
   - Second alternative, a change of Hall sign rather than of magnetization.
     - The sign reversal of the B-hysteresis at E ≈ −36 mV/nm (Fig. 4e) is taken to show that M·B alone does not describe the energetics.
     - However, the anomalous Hall signal is set by the Berry curvature of the occupied states. Its sign can flip between two phases of the same valley polarization, for example across a layer-polarization change or a band inversion, which is the very mechanism the paper describes in Fig. 2.
     - In that case the B-hysteresis polarity of R_xy reverses without any sign change of the orbital magnetization, and no ΔP·M coupling is needed.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Provide a direct measure of layer polarization or electric dipole: a penetration-field or layer-resolved capacitance measurement, or at least a top-gate/bottom-gate asymmetric sweep showing remanent P at the same E.
     - Show that M, not just the sign of the anomalous Hall effect, reverses. A magnetization-sensitive probe (nanoSQUID or MOKE) would settle this, or a theoretical argument that the Chern number or Berry-curvature sign is the same on both sides of the transition.
     - Write down the Landau free energy that contains "ΔP·M" and show which of its predictions the data test.

4. The device's alignment to the hBN, and extrinsic sources of gate hysteresis, are not addressed. Both are leading alternative explanations for anomalous E-field hysteresis in graphene.
   - Where: the Methods and Fig. S13.
   - Why it matters: in graphene/hBN heterostructures, unconventional "ferroelectric" hysteresis in gate or displacement-field sweeps has been tied to near-alignment with hBN and to charge trapping or interfacial polarization in the dielectric.
   - The Methods give neither hBN thicknesses nor the twist angles to the top and bottom hBN. Nor do they report whether moiré features (satellite resistance peaks or Brown–Zak oscillations) are absent.
   - The silicon gate is held at −90 to −100 V for hole-side contact doping and at +95 V for the electron side. A change in this condition, and the charge trapping in SiO2 that a large back-gate voltage can cause, could affect hysteresis measured in the contact regions.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Report the hBN alignment angles, for example from straight edges or SHG.
     - Show the absence of moiré superlattice features over the full density range.
     - Show that the E-hysteresis does not depend on sweep rate or on the n–E path, and that it is absent in neighbouring regions measured with the same sweep protocol. Fig. 4a,b partly does this and should be extended explicitly.
     - Show that the comet-region behaviour is unchanged when the Si gate voltage is varied within the range that keeps the contacts transparent.

5. Every result shown comes from one device, although the Methods say three were measured "with similar results".
   - Where: the Methods.
   - Why it matters: both SC phases occupy tiny, narrow regions of n–E. The ferroelectric comet is also small and sits next to sharp phase boundaries. Features like these are the most sensitive to stacking domains, strain and disorder.
   - What the authors should do: show, at minimum, the n–E map, the SC1 R(T)/dV/dI and the Fig. 4e polarity reversal for a second device. If the other devices do not show SC1, SC2 or the comet state, say so and discuss why.

B. Should address

6. The "electric-field-driven band inversion" in the abstract rests on a qualitative two-flavor rigid-band picture and is not checked quantitatively against a calculation.
   - Where: Fig. 2g–j and Fig. 1a,b.
   - Why it matters:
     - The tight-binding model in Fig. 1a,b is single-particle and in units of U (meV/layer). The interaction-driven "emergent gaps" that define flavors A and B are put in by hand and never calculated.
     - The conversion between U and the applied E, including layer screening, is not given, so the inversion field (≈42 mV/nm in Fig. 2f) and the Lifshitz lines in Fig. 2a cannot be compared with any model.
     - The paper itself lists observations inconsistent with this picture: two-fold rather than four-fold annular surfaces at E = 0 (Fig. 2e), valley-polarized magnetism at charge neutrality, and the "leaf" Fermi surfaces in Fig. 3b that are "incompatible with an n-independent band structure".
   - What the authors should do:
     - Give the U–E conversion and the parameter set used.
     - Run at least a Hartree–Fock or mean-field calculation with flavor-dependent layer polarization, and overlay the predicted Lifshitz lines on Fig. 2a.
     - Explain how layer polarization is assigned from line tilts, and how robust that assignment is.
     - Otherwise, soften "reveal" to "is consistent with".

7. The quantum-oscillation extraction is underspecified, which matters most for the low-frequency peaks that carry the leaf-region argument.
   - Where: Figs. 2b–f, 3b–d, S4–S8.
   - The analysis details are missing: background subtraction, windowing, the 1/B range used (0.4–1.6 T or 0.5–2 T judging from the raw data), and whether the data were interpolated onto a uniform 1/B grid.
   - Peaks near f_v → 0 are a particular problem. FS1 and FS2 in Fig. 3b are said to emerge and vanish at zero frequency (dots and triangles), and the "half-dome" manifolds in Fig. 3d sit at f_v < 0.1. At n ≈ 2×10^12 cm⁻², f_v = 0.05 corresponds to about 4 T, which completes only a few periods over this 1/B window. That is close to where background-subtraction residues and window artefacts appear.
   - The fermiology is also measured at B⊥ up to 1.6–2 T. Yet the phases it is used to explain are defined at B = 0 (Fig. 1e), and several of them (the ferrovalley triangle, SC1, the comet) respond strongly to tens of mT.
   - What the authors should do:
     - State the procedure and the frequency resolution.
     - Show that the low-f_v peaks are stable under changes of window and background.
     - Discuss, or quantify from Fig. 1e versus Fig. 2a, how much the phase boundaries shift between 0 and 1.5 T.

8. Fermi-surface degeneracies are assigned through the f_v sum rule, but violations are explained after the fact, so the logic is partly circular.
   - Where: Fig. S5.
   - Why it matters: the claim that FS1 and FS2 are two-fold degenerate ("the higher f_v peak exceeds the sum of FS1 and FS2 by 0.5") depends on the sum rule holding inside the leaf. Elsewhere, violations are attributed to unresolved heavy pockets. The same unresolved pockets could be present in the leaf, and that would change the degeneracy assignment and hence the "dual-carrier" link to SC1.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Plot the sum (grey points, as in Fig. S5i) explicitly across the leaf region.
     - State the uncertainty on each f_v.
     - Discuss which alternative degeneracy assignments remain allowed.
     - Compare with Hall density, which gives an independent check of the total carrier count.

9. "Emergent nature" is inferred from FS1 and FS2 fading with temperature, without a Lifshitz–Kosevich comparison.
   - Where: Fig. S6 and the text after Fig. 3b.
   - Why it matters: oscillation amplitudes fall with temperature for all pockets, and fastest for heavy ones. Disappearance between 150 mK and 1 K therefore does not by itself show that the pockets are interaction-driven.
   - What the authors should do: extract effective masses with LK fits for FS1 and FS2 and for a reference pocket, and show whether the FS1/FS2 suppression is anomalous, for example because the pocket frequency itself shifts or vanishes rather than just losing amplitude.

10. Self-heating and current calibration in the dV/dI data are not given.
    - Where: Fig. 1f, Fig. 3e,f, Fig. S12c,d.
    - The dV/dI features occur at about 50 nA, but the AC excitation amplitude is not stated. The "sharp drop" Tc is taken from a single R(T) trace.
    - In Fig. S12b, SC2 shows a step of about 30 Ω on a 175 Ω background, about as large as the scatter well below Tc.
    - What the authors should do: state the AC excitation and the electron temperature at base, show that the R(T) transitions do not depend on excitation, and give the Tc criterion.
    - Given its size, SC2 should not be used to argue for "a potential connection" between multiferroicity and superconductivity without stronger evidence.

11. There is no check that the stacking stayed rhombohedral after transfer and fabrication.
    - Where: Methods and Fig. S13.
    - Why it matters: Raman mapping was done before transfer. Rhombohedral domains are known to relax toward Bernal stacking during transfer and annealing, and a partial domain wall inside the Hall bar would produce exactly the kind of inhomogeneity that gives partial resistance drops and phase coexistence (concern 1).
    - What the authors should do: show Raman of the finished stack in the channel region, or argue from transport (for example the CI gap at E = 0 and the band-insulator threshold) that the whole channel is rhombohedral.

12. The interpretation of the zero-field triangle as ferrovalley, and of its suppression by in-plane field, is asserted rather than tested.
    - Where: Fig. S9 and the text on the E = 0 anomalies.
    - What is claimed: Fig. S9 argues that suppression by in-plane field "is consistent with their valley-polarized nature" because Zeeman energy dominates. The main text says that valley polarization at charge neutrality is "incompatible with the widely assumed layer antiferromagnet".
    - Why it is not yet tested: suppression by in-plane field is equally consistent with a competing spin-polarized phase becoming favored. And no direct test distinguishes the candidate flavor orders at E = 0.
    - What the authors should do: give the critical in-plane field as a function of n and compare the slope with the expected Zeeman energy difference between candidate states, or soften the assignment.

13. Temperature scales for the multiferroic states are quoted inconsistently.
    - Where: main text versus Fig. S11 and Fig. S2.
    - The main text gives "T_c = 1.5 K" for the finite-field multiferroic. Fig. S11 says the coercive field vanishes at about 1.3 K, the switch becomes soft at 2 K, and the signal vanishes at 3 K. Fig. S2 gives a transition above 3.6 K for the triangle.
    - The statement that the loop "shrinks linearly" with B⊥ (Fig. 4c,d) is not quantified.
    - What the authors should do: define the criterion behind each T_c, plot the coercive E width and ΔR_xy against T and B⊥, and use one consistent value in the main text.

C. Minor

14. The sign of the density is inconsistent: Fig. 4c,d use n = −0.46×10^11 cm⁻², while Fig. S10b,c use n = +0.46×10^11 cm⁻² for what appears to be the same measurement. The Fig. S10 caption also refers to "white arrows in e to f", which should be b and c.

15. Some axis units appear wrong. Fig. S1a has R_xx in kΩ spanning 10^2 to 10^6, and Fig. S11a has R_xy in kΩ up to 3000. Both are presumably Ω.

16. Fig. S6 caption: "1.5 × 10^12 < |n| < 33 × 10^12" is presumably 3.3 × 10^12.

17. Methods: the layer-polarization formula needs brackets, (|ψ_top|² − |ψ_bot|²)/(|ψ_top|² + |ψ_bot|²). Also give the formulas and hBN thicknesses used to convert gate voltages to n and E, and any offsets.

18. Units mix "meV/nm" and "mV/nm" (for example "above ~30 meV/nm" in the Fig. 3 discussion). Fig. S3 title: "electron electron-doped". The Fig. 2 caption reference "f3 Fig. S5i" is unclear. "Additional information ... at XXX" needs completing.

19. The Fig. 3 caption says Fig. 3g cuts through "two superconducting phases (see Fig. S12a)", while Fig. S12a says the dashed line marks the cut of Fig. 3g. Please make the cross-references consistent and mark the cut in the main figure.

20. Fig. 1e mentions "BI and CI" labels that are hard to find in the panel, and the symbols for SC1, the triangle and the comet are small. Labelling the phases directly on Fig. 1e would help readers.

21. The statement that the f_v sum rule is "violated" in partially flavor-polarized regimes cites other rhombohedral systems. Please quote the magnitude of the violations in this device.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Narrow the headline claims to what the data establish: "superconducting-like" states and "electric-field-hysteretic anomalous Hall state". Alternatively, add the measurements in concerns 1–3 that would justify "superconductivity" and "ferroelectric".
2. Add a self-consistent mean-field calculation with explicit flavor and layer polarization and the U–E conversion. Overlay its Lifshitz lines on Fig. 2a so that the band-inversion assignment becomes quantitative rather than schematic.
3. Add a short reproducibility panel (Extended Data) from a second device covering the n–E map, SC1 and the Fig. 4e polarity reversal, together with hBN alignment information and a post-fabrication Raman map.
4. Consolidate the fermiology methods (FFT window, background, resolution, field range) and present Lifshitz–Kosevich masses for the key pockets. This would make the "emergent Fermi surface" argument and the link between the leaf region and SC1 much more convincing.
