Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use MBE growth to lower the carrier density of (001) Cd3As2 films to n ≈ 2.9–23 × 10^16 cm^-3. In the low-density samples they observe a kink in ρxy (near H_S ≈ 3.5 T in Sample C, ≈ 5.7 T in Sample D) with a matching feature in ρxx, and a large super-quadratic magnetoresistance background that grows as n decreases. They interpret this as a quasi-quantum Hall effect (QQHE) plus Coulomb-disorder scattering that becomes stronger at low n, and they describe the data with an empirical expression (Eq. 1). The question is timely: QQHE claims in ZrTe5 and HfTe5 have attracted wide interest, and asking why ρxx never vanishes in 3D systems is useful. The novelty depends on two things the manuscript does not yet show quantitatively: that the ρxy feature is QQHE rather than an ordinary quantum-limit or Landau-level-crossing anomaly, and that the background comes from Coulomb disorder specifically.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A series of five films spanning about an order of magnitude in carrier density. MBE control of the defect-limited Fermi level is a real experimental asset.
- A clear, systematic evolution with n: linear MR at high n (Sample E), and at low n a quadratic-to-super-quadratic MR with a ρxy kink (Samples A–D) (Figs. 2, 3).
- Temperature dependence up to 80 K is shown for two samples (Fig. 2).
- A physically motivated message: lowering E_F also weakens screening of charged defects, so the two must be engineered together. This is relevant beyond Cd3As2.
- A k.p Landau-band calculation (Fig. 1b, Methods) gives a theoretical reference for the clean QQHE line shape.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. No quantitative test identifies the ρxy feature as QQHE. Where: Results (Fig. 2e, Fig. 3e–h), Summary ("we have observed evidence of the 3D QQHE").
   - What is missing: the evidence for QQHE is qualitative. There is a change of slope in ρxy at H_S, and a lump in ρxx that the scaling αH∂Hρxy can match with a freely chosen α (0.17–0.35).
   - The standard tests used for ZrTe5/HfTe5 (Refs. 7, 10, 15) are absent:
     - (i) the plateau or kink value of ρxy (or σxy) compared with the expected (e²/h)(k_F,z/π) or n·e/B scale computed from the measured n;
     - (ii) the field of the feature compared with the field at which the last Landau band crosses E_F, computed for each sample from its n (or from the k.p model at the Table 1e E_F);
     - (iii) a scaling of the feature field with n across Samples A–D. A rough estimate with a spin-degenerate isotropic Fermi surface for two Dirac nodes gives B_QL ≈ 3 T for Sample C and ≈ 4 T for Sample D. This is in the right range but has not been shown, and the ratio of the observed fields (5.7/3.5 ≈ 1.6) differs from (n_D/n_C)^{2/3} ≈ 1.4.
   - Why it matters: a slope change in ρxy near the quantum limit is generic to any 3D metal entering the QL. It does not by itself distinguish QQHE from an ordinary Landau-level-crossing anomaly, a Zeeman-split level crossing (Cd3As2 has a large g-factor), or field-induced changes in carrier density.
   - What to do:
     - Plot σxy (with σxy = ρxy/(ρxx² + ρxy²)), normalized by e²/h·k_F,z/π, against B/B_QL for all samples.
     - Mark the computed Landau-band crossing fields on Figs. 2–3.
     - Compare the k.p calculation of Fig. 1b, evaluated at each sample's E_F, directly with the measured ρxy.

2. The Coulomb-disorder origin of the background rests on a flexible fit and on a disorder axis that is computed from n rather than measured. Where: Eq. 1, Fig. 3a–c, i, j, Fig. 1d.
   - The fit has four free parameters per sample (α, β, γ, δ), plus H_c = 9.6 T and w = 1.5 T, which were fixed globally without justification. It is applied to the positive-field half of three samples only.
   - "Good agreement" with this functional form does not discriminate between mechanisms. Other routes give super-quadratic or non-saturating MR together with nonlinear Hall at low n:
     - classical mobility-fluctuation (Parish–Littlewood) MR in inhomogeneous films;
     - parallel conduction (surface states, the ZnCdTe buffer interface, depleted layers);
     - field-induced freeze-out or magnetic-field-dependent carrier density in the quantum limit (cf. Ref. 24 on ZrTe5).
   - eV0/E_F in Figs. 1d and 3j comes from the Skinner formula (Ref. 9) using n. The x and y axes of Fig. 3j are therefore functions of the same measured quantity, and any background that grows as n decreases would line up with "stronger Coulomb disorder".
   - The impurity density N_imp, which enters eV0, is not stated per sample. The paper itself says that n was tuned by changing defect concentrations, so N_imp is presumably not constant across samples.
   - Fixing H_c globally is also in tension with the cited theory (Ref. 29), in which the H² to H log(δ√H) crossover field depends on the disorder strength and density.
   - What to do:
     - Give the N_imp, dielectric constant and Fermi velocity used for each sample in Fig. 1d/3j.
     - Provide an independent experimental handle on disorder, for example the Dingle temperature or quantum mobility from the oscillations in Samples D/E compared with the transport mobility, or two samples at similar n with different compensation. Gating one film, which the authors themselves propose, would be ideal.
     - Show that at least one competing model (a two-carrier fit to ρxx and ρxy together, or a mobility-fluctuation model) fails where Eq. 1 succeeds.
     - Report fit uncertainties and parameter correlations, and test whether letting H_c and w vary per sample changes the conclusions.
   - Without this, the central claim that "Coulomb disorder rules the roost" is a hypothesis consistent with the data rather than a demonstrated result.

B. Should address

3. Carrier densities are inconsistent across the manuscript, and this bears on which regime each sample is assigned to.
   - Sample E is given as 2.3 × 10^17 cm^-3 in the text and in the Fig. 2 caption, and is placed at ≈2.3 × 10^17 in Fig. 1d. But Fig. 2a is labeled 2.3 × 10^16 cm^-3, and Table 1e lists 23.2 in units of 10^15 cm^-3 (= 2.3 × 10^16).
   - The E_F values in Table 1e also rise faster than n^{1/3} (for example, E_F of B/A is 1.11 while (n_B/n_A)^{1/3} is 1.08; E_F of E/A is 2.48 while (n_E/n_A)^{1/3} is 2.0 if n_E = 2.3 × 10^16). The DOS model, Fermi velocity and anisotropy used to convert n into E_F should be stated.
   - The table reports 2 K densities, while Fig. 3 is at 5 K.
   - Please correct the values and give uncertainties on n and E_F. A factor-of-ten error in n_E changes where Sample E sits in Figs. 1d and 3j.

4. The densities come from a single-band low-field Hall slope, which contradicts the samples' own nonlinear ρxy. Where: Methods and Fig. 1e.
   - The authors argue that one band crosses E_F. Yet the low-n samples show strongly nonlinear ρxy, and low-density Cd3As2 films can have surface-state or interface carriers.
   - Please show that the low-field Hall density agrees with the density from quantum-oscillation frequencies (available in Samples D/E) and with the k.p estimate. Also show the full ρxx and ρxy simultaneously fitted with a two-carrier model, to bound any second channel.

5. Alternatives to a 3D bulk origin are dismissed without data. Where: p. 3–4.
   - The paper states that "magnetotransport at different fixed magnetic field angles [is] consistent with a 3D Fermi surface", but these data are not shown. Thin-film Cd3As2 has shown quantum Hall effects from Weyl orbits and confined subbands (Refs. 30, 31).
   - Film thicknesses (200–560 nm) are not given per sample, and the effects of thickness on MR and the kink are not separated from the effects of n.
   - Please give the thickness of each sample, show the angle dependence of H_S (with B tracked along k_z for a 3D Fermi surface), and comment on whether the feature scales with the perpendicular field component as a 2D effect would.

6. One film per density; reproducibility and device-to-device spread are not shown.
   - Each carrier density is represented by one film, and apparently by one Hall bar. Sample B has a lower mobility (0.67 × 10^4 cm²/Vs) than both A and C, so mobility does not track n monotonically and may be a confounding variable for the background size.
   - Please show a second Hall bar or film near one of the densities, and discuss mobility as an alternative organizing variable for β and γ.

7. Data processing is not described. Fig. 3 shows only negative-field data and positive-field fits, and Fig. 2d shows visible asymmetry.
   - Please state whether ρxx and ρxy were symmetrized and antisymmetrized, give the Hall bar dimensions and the geometric-factor uncertainty, and show both field polarities of the raw data in the SI.

8. The theoretical comparison is only illustrative. Where: Fig. 1b.
   - The k.p calculation is shown only for E_F = 20 meV. The clean ρxx is multiplied by 250, and the "disorder" trace is a smooth curve whose construction is deferred to an SI that does not accompany this manuscript.
   - The relation ρxx ≈ αH∂Hρxy is borrowed from 2D, where it has a specific origin in density inhomogeneity (Simon–Halperin). The meaning of α, and why it varies by a factor of two between samples, is not discussed.
   - Please compute ρxx and ρxy for each sample's E_F (and Zeeman g-factor) and overlay them on the data. Please also discuss the physical content of α, or whether it is related to the same inhomogeneity invoked as Coulomb disorder.

9. The temperature dependence is not used to test the disorder picture.
   - Fig. 2 shows data up to 80 K, but the temperature evolution of the background is not analyzed. Thermal screening and changes in mobility would modify eV0 and the H² and H log terms in a predictable way.
   - Fitting Eq. 1 at several temperatures for Samples A–C would test whether β and γ behave as the strong-Coulomb-disorder model predicts.

10. The comparison with the pentatellurides in Fig. 3j is not quantitative.
    - The "few meV" eV0 assigned to ZrTe5/HfTe5 has no stated inputs (N_imp, ε, v_F), so it cannot support the argument that weaker Coulomb disorder explains the cleaner QQHE there.
    - Please give the parameters and their sources, or present this comparison as qualitative.

C. Minor

11. The abstract says that "a zero resistivity state is obscured" by Coulomb disorder. Even the clean calculation in Fig. 1b and the 3D QQHE literature give finite ρxx, so a vanishing-resistance state is not expected regardless of disorder. Please rephrase.

12. The H_S marker in Fig. 2d/e sits closer to 4 T than to the 3.5 T quoted in the text. Please make them consistent.

13. Fig. 3 caption: Sample D's right half shows only the αH∂Hρxy trace, not a fit; please say so. Fig. 3i: give the uncertainties of β and γ, and note that there are only three points.

14. The δ ≈ 0.32 T^-1/2 is said to be "related to the disorder strength and the Fermi velocity". Please give the expression, and check whether the extracted value is consistent with the eV0 values in Fig. 3j.

15. The claim of being "the first study of its kind" should be tempered given Refs. 30–32. Please state precisely what is new relative to those works.

16. Language and typos: "lack of tunability in the Fermi thus far", "mangetotransport", "requisitely", "corequisitely", "rules the roost", and "will be undoubtedly useful". The Table 1e headers should state the temperature consistently.

17. Data availability "upon reasonable request". Depositing the raw ρxx(H) and ρxy(H) and the fit code would let others test alternative models of the background.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a figure showing σxy normalized by (e²/h)k_F,z/π against B/B_QL for Samples A–E, with the computed Landau-band crossing fields marked. This would make the QQHE identification quantitative.
2. Obtain an independent measure of disorder (Dingle analysis, quantum versus transport mobility, or a gated film at fixed defect density) so that the disorder axis of Fig. 3j does not rest only on n.
3. Fit a two-carrier or mobility-fluctuation model alongside Eq. 1 and report which ones fail, with parameter uncertainties and per-sample H_c.
4. Show the angle dependence and the per-sample thickness to establish the 3D bulk origin of the kink.

5. Verdict: major revision
