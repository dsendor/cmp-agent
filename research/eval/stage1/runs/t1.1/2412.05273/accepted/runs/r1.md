Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports magnetotransport in MBE-grown (001) Cd3As2 films with low electron densities (2.9 to 9.3 x 10^16 cm^-3, plus a 2.3 x 10^17 cm^-3 reference). It interprets the kinks in rho_yx(H) and the lumps in rho_xx(H) as a three-dimensional quasi-quantum Hall effect (QQHE) that is partly hidden by screened Coulomb disorder from charged Cd point defects. The main ideas are that lowering the Fermi level both makes the quantum limit easier to reach and makes disorder stronger, and that this trade-off can be expressed with a single parameter, eV0/E_F. Combining a k.p Landau-band conductivity calculation, a density series and an empirical decomposition of rho_xx is new for Cd3As2. The framing is useful for the wider search for 3D QQHE beyond the pentatellurides. How strong the QQHE identification is depends on points 1 to 4 below.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- It is a systematic density series (six films, n from 2.9 x 10^16 to 2.3 x 10^17 cm^-3) from a single growth platform, so E_F is tuned in a controlled way. This is rare for Cd3As2, where bulk crystals usually have much higher densities.
- The step-like change in rho_yx coincides with a feature in rho_xx (Fig. 3d-f; Fig. 4a-j). Sample D, measured to 31.5 T at 0.4 K, shows a flattening of rho_yx between about 19.5 and 24 T (Fig. 4k).
- An 8x8 k.p Landau-band Kubo/Berry-curvature calculation (Fig. 1e,f; Methods) gives a concrete prediction for Cd3As2 at E_F of about 20 meV. It shows that plateaus appear at fixed chemical potential and disappear at fixed density.
- The paper links its result to the Coulomb-disorder magnetoresistance theory (Skinner; Rodionov et al.), which offers a single explanation for linear MR at high density and super-linear MR at low density.
- The discussion of why the pentatellurides show cleaner QQHE (better screening, fewer charged defects; Fig. 4m) gives other groups an actionable, testable route.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The headline plateau comparison is made in resistivity, in a regime where rho_xx is much larger than rho_yx. The statement that the plateau agrees with the predicted value therefore does not test the quantity that is actually quasi-quantized.
   - Where: Fig. 4k and the related text ("The final QQHE plateau is visible between 19.5 and 24 T and in excellent agreement with the theoretical value"), together with the plateau marker rho_yx^FP = pi h/(e^2 k_F,z,0) in Fig. 4f and in Fig. 1e.
   - What is wrong: the quasi-quantized quantity in the theory (Fig. 1; Methods) is sigma_xy, approximately (e^2/h) k_F,z/pi. The step from that to rho_yx = pi h/(e^2 k_F,z) requires rho_xx << rho_yx. In Fig. 4k, rho_xx is roughly 500 to 800 mOhm cm between 19.5 and 24 T, while rho_yx is about 155 mOhm cm, so tan(theta_H) is about 0.2 to 0.3. The authors themselves note that disorder "spoil[s] the approximation rho_xy ~ sigma_xy^-1". Under these conditions sigma_xy = rho_yx/(rho_xx^2 + rho_yx^2) changes strongly with field across the window where rho_yx is flat. A flat rho_yx then does not imply a flat sigma_xy, and agreement of rho_yx with pi h/(e^2 k_F,z) may be coincidental.
   - A further problem: a value of pi h/(e^2 k_F,z) equals the classical Hall resistivity B/(ne) at the field where the lowest Landau band is reached. Matching that number alone does not separate quasi-quantization from an ordinary Hall slope that bends because of the large rho_xx or a second carrier.
   - What to do:
     - Invert the measured tensor and plot sigma_xy(H) and sigma_xx(H) for Sample D (and Sample A). Compare sigma_xy with (e^2/h) k_F,z/pi.
     - State the k_F,z used for rho_yx^FP and where it comes from (Hall n with an assumed anisotropy, quantum oscillations, or k.p). Give its uncertainty.
     - Compare the measured tensor with the calculated tensor for moderate disorder (Fig. 1e,f, blue), not with the clean-limit plateau value.
   - If Supplementary Note 7 already does this in conductivity, the main text and the figure should say so explicitly.

B. Should address

2. The measured films never show whether a fixed chemical potential or a fixed density applies, yet the plateau depends on it.
   - Where: Methods ("For fixed charge density calculations, the 3D QQHE plateaus disappear") and Fig. 1e.
   - Why it matters: the authors' own calculation produces plateaus only at fixed chemical potential. The paper does not say what pins mu in these films (defect states, the Zn_xCd_1-xTe buffer, surface states) or show evidence that it is pinned.
   - What to do:
     - Discuss which constraint is appropriate.
     - Show whether the Hall-derived carrier number departs from its low-field value at high field. This can be done by comparing the low-field Hall slope with rho_yx at 20 to 30 T in Fig. 4k.
     - Show the fixed-density calculation for Sample D's parameters alongside the data.

3. A two-carrier or puddle explanation is ruled out only qualitatively, although the authors' own disorder picture predicts it.
   - Where: page 3 ("A two-band model can generate non-linear Hall effect and quadratic FMR... Nonmonotonicity is typically not observed in multiband model FMR").
   - Why it matters: the paper places Samples A to D in the strong Coulomb disorder regime, with eV0 comparable to or larger than E_F (Figs. 2b, 4m), with E_F of about 20 to 28 meV and eV0 of about 17 to 20 meV. In that regime, local hole pockets and electron-hole puddles near the Dirac point are expected. Mixed electron and hole conduction gives a nonlinear Hall effect, large quadratic MR and slope changes in rho_yx. The kink in Fig. 3e and the large FMR in Fig. 3d could have this origin. The low-field Hall density is also not unique if two carriers are present.
   - What to do:
     - Fit sigma_xx(H) and sigma_xy(H) jointly with a two-carrier model for Samples A to E. Show where and why it fails, in particular whether it can reproduce the coincident rho_xx lump and rho_yx kink.
     - State whether the buffer or substrate can conduct in parallel.

4. Nothing quantitatively links the field of the feature in each sample to the calculated quantum-limit field.
   - Where: Figs. 3d-f and 4a-j, and Supplementary Note 4 (features "move to lower fields" with decreasing n).
   - Why it matters: quantum oscillations are weak or absent in the low-density films (page 4, mobilities 0.67 to 2.6 x 10^4 cm^2/Vs). So nothing in the main text shows independently that the kink field is the quantum-limit field, or the field of the last few Landau bands, for that density. A QQHE origin predicts specific crossing fields that scale with n (roughly as n^(2/3) for a fixed anisotropy). A disorder or two-carrier origin need not.
   - What to do: tabulate, for Samples A to E, the field of the rho_yx slope change and of the rho_xx lump next to the k.p-calculated fields at which the n = 1 and n = 0 Landau bands cross E_F, using each sample's n. Plot the two against each other.

5. The empirical decomposition in Eq. 1 has many free parameters, and some of its constraints look inconsistent with the physics it is meant to represent.
   - Where: Eq. 1, Fig. 4a-d and 4l, and Supplementary Note 3.
   - Which parameters are free: per sample, alpha, beta, gamma and delta are free, rho_0 is taken from data, and H_c = 9.6 T and w = 1.5 T are fixed for all samples. A smooth background built from H^2 and H log terms plus a derivative term can fit most smooth monotonic curves up to 14 T.
   - The log term: with delta of about 0.32 T^-1/2, log(delta sqrt(H)) crosses zero at about 9.8 T. That is essentially H_c, so the switched-on term gamma H log(delta sqrt(H)) is small over most of the 9.6 to 14 T window and is constrained by only a few tesla of data.
   - The crossover field: in Rodionov et al. it depends on n and the disorder strength, so fixing H_c globally builds in an assumption.
   - Sample E: it is excluded because it is "in the weak Coulomb disorder limit". Yet from Fig. 2b its eV0/E_F (about 0.5) differs only modestly from Sample D's (about 0.6), so the threshold looks arbitrary.
   - What to do:
     - Report parameter uncertainties and covariances.
     - Show fits with H_c free per sample.
     - Show that the decomposition is unique by fitting without the alpha term (and without the Coulomb terms) and reporting how much worse the fit is.
     - Test whether beta and gamma scale with n and eV0 as Rodionov et al. predict, rather than only decreasing monotonically (Fig. 4l).

6. The disorder strength is estimated, not measured, and the main conclusion depends on it.
   - Where: Fig. 2a ("concentrations of ~10^18 cm^-3"), Fig. 2b ("assuming an average concentration of charged point defects") and Fig. 4m.
   - Why it matters: whether a sample counts as weak or strong Coulomb disorder depends on the assumed density of charged defects and on the dielectric constant. Neither is measured for these films. The pentatelluride estimates in Fig. 4m rest on similar assumptions.
   - What to do:
     - Propagate a realistic range of defect density, and of compensation (net n of about 3 x 10^16 against roughly 10^18 charged defects means strong compensation), into the eV0/E_F points.
     - Use any independent constraint available, such as the quantum versus transport lifetime from Sample F's oscillations or the Dingle temperature, to check that eV0 increases as n decreases.

7. Mobility does not fall monotonically with n, and film thickness varies; this complicates the narrative that lower n means more disorder.
   - Where: Fig. 2c. Mobility is 1.10, 0.67, 1.52, 1.01 and 1.22 x 10^4 cm^2/Vs for Samples A to E. Methods gives thicknesses of 440, 210, 560, 350, 380 and 350 nm.
   - Why it matters: Sample B, the thinnest film, has the lowest mobility, and Sample C has a higher mobility than D. Film quality and thickness differ between samples as well as E_F. The paper attributes trends in beta, gamma and the visibility of the QQHE to n alone.
   - What to do:
     - Discuss sample-to-sample variation in disorder that does not come from the Fermi level.
     - Where possible, add repeat devices at similar n, or gate a single film. The paper already identifies gating as future work, but even a small gate-induced shift on one film would separate n from growth disorder.

8. The density comparison mixes temperatures.
   - Where: Fig. 4 caption. Samples A to E are shown at 5 K, except Sample D at 0.4 K. Fig. 3 is at 2 K, and Sample D in Fig. 4k is at 0.4 K.
   - Why it matters: Landau-band broadening and the visibility of the QQHE depend on temperature (Supplementary Note 4). The β and γ trends in Fig. 4l include one point taken at a different temperature.
   - What to do: show Sample D at 5 K in Fig. 4d (or all samples at a common temperature) and refit.

9. The paper cannot tell a 3D (bulk) origin from a surface or 2D one by thickness alone.
   - Where: Discussion, page 6.
   - Why it matters: the 3D assignment rests on the films being thicker than 100 nm and on oscillation analyses that are possible only for the higher-density film (Supplementary Note 6). Surface states and Weyl-orbit physics have been reported in Cd3As2 films of comparable thickness.
   - What to do: show the dependence on field angle of the rho_yx kink and rho_xx lump for one low-n sample. For a 3D origin these features should track the anisotropic bulk Fermi surface, not the perpendicular field.

C. Minor

10. Sign convention in Eq. 1. On page 4 the text writes rho_xx = -alpha H d(rho_yx)/dH, but Eq. 1 has +alpha H d(rho_yx)/dH. With rho_yx < 0 for H > 0 (Fig. 4f-j) and alpha reported as positive (0.16 to 0.35), the QQHE term would come out negative. Make the sign convention consistent.

11. Symmetrization. Methods should state whether rho_xx was symmetrized and rho_yx antisymmetrized in field. It should also explain why the fits in Fig. 4a-d use only H > 0.

12. Fig. 4l. Label the axes directly instead of using arrows, give error bars on beta and gamma, and list all fit parameters, including alpha and delta per sample, in a main-text table.

13. Fig. 1 caption. It says the calculations "do not account for the effects of LB broadening on the DOS". Briefly state how disorder enters (a constant eta?) and what values of eta correspond to "weak" and "moderate", so readers can relate them to the measured mobilities.

14. Fig. 2c. E_F is estimated from n using the DOS. State which DOS (k.p model, anisotropy, number of Dirac nodes) was used. The same choice sets k_F,z, which enters rho_yx^FP.

15. Fig. 4m. Explain how the "Visible QQHE" shaded region and the pentatelluride ellipse were constructed. As drawn they read as qualitative guides.

16. Data availability "upon reasonable request" is weak for a dataset this central. Consider depositing the rho_xx and rho_yx data in a repository.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Present the high-field data for Samples A and D as a conductivity tensor next to the k.p calculation at matched n and a disorder strength consistent with the measured mobility. This would turn "qualitative similarity" into a quantitative test of the QQHE.
2. Add a single figure comparing, sample by sample, the measured feature fields with the calculated Landau-band crossing fields.
3. Add a two-carrier or puddle-model fit as an explicit alternative, and report how it fails, if it does.
4. A gated or post-growth-annealed film that changes n or the defect density with the growth fixed would separate the Fermi-level and defect contributions to eV0. This is the experiment the paper's own framework calls for.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
