Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use pump-probe STXM (about 20 nm spatial and 1 ns temporal resolution, element-selective at the Fe and Co L3 edges) to image the current-driven nanosecond dynamics of a dense antiferromagnetic skyrmion lattice in a compensated CoFeB/Ir/Pt synthetic antiferromagnet. They identify an "incoherent" low-current regime, in which mobile skyrmions are pushed against pinned ones and recoil after the pulse, and a "coherent viscous-flow" regime at higher current. From the recoil trajectories they invert the Thiele equation to obtain an exponentially decaying skyrmion-skyrmion interaction potential, and they show that the skyrmion Hall angle is zero within error (1.4° ± 1.5°). Real-space, time-resolved imaging of interacting AFM skyrmions has not been reported before. Recovering an interaction law from deterministic scattering trajectories, rather than from equilibrium statistics, is a new idea that could be widely useful.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The paper is the first time-resolved, sublattice-selective, real-space imaging of a dense AFM skyrmion lattice under spin-orbit torque. The data quality is high: individual skyrmions can be tracked frame by frame at 1 ns steps (Figs. 2 and 4, Supplementary Video 3).
- The classification of skyrmions into I, M, L and R classes, and the observation of post-pulse recoil opposite to the drive with 3–20 ns relaxation times (Fig. 2c,d), are clear and physically interesting. They show that lattice elasticity and pinning act together.
- The inverse Thiele method uses pinned skyrmions as boundary conditions. It is a new way to access interaction forces in a dense ensemble without assuming thermal equilibrium, and the authors compare it with micromagnetics (Fig. 3b).
- The velocity is linear in current and the transverse velocity is zero within error over ±1.2 × 10^12 A m^-2 (Fig. 5). Together with the time-resolved velocity tracking the bipolar pulse shape (Fig. 4d), this directly demonstrates Hall-free, lag-free motion during the pulse, not only before and after it.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The absolute scale of the interaction potential, and its "agreement" with micromagnetics, is partly fixed by construction (main text p. 1620 around Eq. 1; Fig. 3b; Methods "Determination of the skyrmion–skyrmion interaction potential").
   - The trajectories determine only F/D = (1 m s^-1) exp(-(r-b)/a). In this form the dissipation constant D and the strength parameter b are degenerate: rescaling D by a factor k is the same as shifting b by a·ln k.
   - The authors state that the experimental D is "approximately four times larger" than in the defect-free simulation. They infer this "by comparing F(r) over the relevant range of r in the experiment and in the homogeneous case", which means using the simulation to calibrate the experiment.
   - The overlap of the orange and blue bands in Fig. 3b, in absolute meV, therefore cannot count as independent validation of the experimental potential. Only the decay length a is a genuinely independent comparison. Even a disagrees: a* = 44 ± 4 nm in experiment against 31 ± 3 nm in simulation, about 2.5σ apart.
   - The main text also never gives the numerical value of D, or how it is obtained (α, the dissipation tensor of the skyrmion profile, layer count). Without it the reader cannot reproduce the meV scale of V/N.

   Since "a quantitative framework for AFM skyrmion interactions" and agreement with simulation are headline claims, the authors should do one of the following:
   - (i) determine D independently of the interaction fit, for example from the measured v_x(J_x) slope in Fig. 5 combined with an independent estimate of the spin-torque efficiency B and θ_SH, or from a micromagnetic simulation that includes the measured roughness or grain disorder;
   - (ii) report the experimental result as F/D and a, compare only those to simulation, and revise the abstract and text accordingly.

   They should also state D explicitly. And they should discuss the a mismatch (44 against 31 nm) rather than calling it agreement.

B. Should address

2. Pinning and thermal relaxation are not separated from the interaction force in the recoil fits (Methods; Supplementary Section B; Supplementary Table 1).
   - The inverse model attributes the post-pulse recoil of L and R skyrmions entirely to pairwise repulsion from neighbours, with the I skyrmions as fixed boundaries. But the L and R skyrmions sit at the edge of a pinned region and are themselves in a disordered landscape. The authors invoke this landscape to explain why τ_relax differs for +J_x and −J_x (Methods, last paragraph of the classification section).
   - The 3–20 ns relaxation times are also comparable to the cooling time of a 100-nm SiN membrane after a high-current pulse. A decaying temperature would relax K_eff, the skyrmion size and therefore the equilibrium spacing on the same timescale.
   - Internal shape relaxation of skyrmions that were elongated or compressed during the pulse (Fig. 2b) is a third possible source of slow recoil.
   - Requested analyses:
     - Show that an interaction-only model describes several L and R skyrmions with one common (a, b), not only R1. Report how many trajectories enter the fit and the per-skyrmion scatter of a and b.
     - Test whether adding a local pinning potential term changes a and b.
     - Use the Joule-heating data in Supplementary Section A4 to estimate the temperature transient during the 0–100 ns fit window.

3. The predicted trajectories in Fig. 3a are offset from the measured ones.
   - For j_x > 0 the solid black x-trajectory lies roughly 10–20 nm above the measured trace throughout the 100 ns window, including at t = 0.
   - The y-trajectories show a similar offset, of the same order as the total recoil amplitude (about 30 nm).
   - The Methods state that starting positions are taken from the experimental data, so an offset already at t = 0 is unexpected.
   - The authors should explain the offset. If it comes from a fitted rigid offset or from defining position differently (centroid against core), they should say so. They should also give the loss L2(a, b) and residuals per trajectory, so that the quality of the fit, and the ±4 nm uncertainties on a* and b*, can be judged. Those uncertainties are small compared with the 20 nm spatial resolution.

4. The functional form of the interaction is assumed, not tested.
   - Eq. (1) imposes a pure exponential. Theory for skyrmion repulsion often gives a modified-Bessel form (K1(r/a)), and dipolar tails appear in incompletely compensated stacks.
   - The data in Fig. 3b sample distances of about 140–400 nm, a range over which several forms may fit equally well.
   - The authors should fit at least one alternative form and report the change in loss. This would support the phrase "revealing an exponential decay" in the abstract.

5. The sublattice selectivity of the Co edge, and the cross-sublattice timing claim, need clarification (p. 1616; Fig. 1b,c; Methods "Material deposition"; p. 1621).
   - Sublattice A contains Co0.6Fe0.2B0.2 (0.2 nm) and Fe0.6Co0.2B0.2 (0.4 nm), so the Co L3 signal is not purely sublattice B. It also contains an inverted contribution from A. The authors should quantify the relative Co content of A and B and the resulting contrast mixing.
   - The statement that "no delay or phase shift between the sublattices is detected within the 1-ns time resolution" compares two separate pump-probe acquisitions taken at different photon energies (only one energy can be used at a time). The authors should explain how time zero and the pulse-delay synchronization were aligned between the Fe and Co acquisitions.
   - Given the contrast mixing and the 20-nm spatial resolution, they should give an explicit upper bound on any transient interlayer separation. They should then compare it with the separation predicted by the cited inertia model (ref. 50) for the measured J_IEC ≈ 0.38 mJ m^-2.

6. The two regimes and the transition between them are only loosely characterized (pp. 1617–1621; Fig. 5).
   - The incoherent regime is shown at J = 2.8 × 10^11 A m^-2, and the coherent regime is quantified at 1.2 × 10^12 A m^-2.
   - Fig. 5 contains points at about 3.5–5 × 10^11 A m^-2, close to the incoherent current. The linear fit of v_x passes through the origin with no visible depinning offset, which is surprising given that a large fraction of skyrmions are pinned at 2.8 × 10^11 A m^-2.
   - The authors should state which regime each Fig. 5 point belongs to, and fit v_x with a free intercept or threshold.
   - Ideally, they should show a quantity that tracks coherence (for example the fraction of class I skyrmions, or the spread of displacements) against J, to locate the crossover.
   - They should also state which pulse widths (2.5–10 ns, Methods) were used for each point, and whether the "mean velocity" is a plateau value or a pulse-averaged one.

7. The number of devices and the reproducibility across samples are not stated (Methods).
   - The class fractions (66% M, 18% I, 16% L/R) and the interaction parameters appear to come from a single strip and field of view.
   - The authors should state how many devices and fields of view were measured, and whether the recoil phenomenology and a* reproduce in a second device or after re-nucleation of the lattice. The pinning landscape, and hence the class assignment, is sample specific.

8. The "absence of inertia" claim should be quantified (pp. 1621–1622; Fig. 4d).
   - Velocities are obtained by differencing positions at 1 ns spacing with about 20 nm resolution. The single-frame velocity uncertainty can therefore be of order 10 m s^-1 or more, as the Fig. 4d error bars suggest.
   - "Negligible inertia" should be turned into an upper bound: for example, a fit of the measured v_x(t) to the voltage pulse convolved with a first-order response gives a maximum lag time or effective mass.
   - The authors should also state that a sub-ns lag would not be resolvable with this setup.

C. Minor

9. The Methods define the effective saturation magnetization M_s^eff = (M_s,A t_A + M_s,B t_B)/(t_A + t_B), a thickness-weighted sum, and quote 0.85 × 10^6 A m^-1. Please clarify how compensation (M_s,A t_A ≈ M_s,B t_B) was verified quantitatively, for example the residual net moment from the SQUID loop. A residual uncompensated moment would affect both the gyrotropic term assumed to vanish in the inverse model and the small residual Hall angle discussed on p. 1622.

10. Fig. 2c: immediately after the negative pulse, the class I trace first shows a positive excursion of about +25 nm before dropping below zero and relaxing. Please comment on this transient, since class I is described as showing "only minimal displacement".

11. Fig. 3b plots V/N "per bilayer", which assumes that all 25 repeats host vertically aligned, identical skyrmion tubes. Please state this assumption and whether the micromagnetic simulation uses the full 25-repeat stack or a reduced one.

12. The abstract says the framework demonstrates "deterministic control ... even in the incoherent flow regime". Pump-probe averaging can only show reproducible motion. Stochastic events average out and would appear only as reduced contrast. Please quantify the reduction in dynamic contrast relative to static images as a bound on non-reproducible motion.

13. Fig. 5 gives the error bars as standard deviations across 25 skyrmions. For the fits, the standard error of the mean, or the weighting used, should be stated.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Determine D independently, from the v_x(J) slope with an independent torque-efficiency estimate or from disorder-inclusive micromagnetics. Then present the potential with an explicit D and its uncertainty.
2. Fit a common (a, b) to all L and R trajectories for both current polarities, with an optional pinning term. Report residuals, the stability of the parameters, and a comparison with a Bessel-function form.
3. Add a current-density sweep that tracks a coherence measure, such as the class I fraction or the displacement variance, to show where the incoherent-to-coherent crossover occurs.
4. Show the recoil phenomenology and the extracted a* in a second device or a second lattice configuration.

5. Verdict: major revision
