Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports that the angular momentum of phonons at general wavevectors in a polar crystal can be reversed by switching the ferroelectric polarization with an electric field. The samples are free-standing 80 nm BaTiO3 membranes with top and bottom electrodes, and the probe is circularly dichroic RIXS at the O K edge. Earlier chiral-phonon RIXS work (quartz, LiNbO3) showed static phonon handedness. Turning a symmetry-protected handedness on and off in situ with a voltage, in a technologically relevant ferroelectric, is a clear conceptual step and of broad interest. The measurement is technically demanding: soft-X-ray RIXS CD on an electrically biased oxide membrane through a thin metal electrode.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The two dichroic quantities behave as the symmetry argument requires. In Fig. 3 the field-switching difference reverses when the photon helicity is reversed, and the CD reverses when the field is reversed. A plain field-induced change in intensity, or a plain helicity-dependent change in beam or optics, could not produce both of these on its own.
- In Fig. 4a the switching contrast reverses between q2 and q4, which lie on either side of the (010)-containing mirror plane. It is near zero at q3, which lies on the mirror. This is the mirror-odd behaviour expected of the phonon helicity J·e_q in C4v.
- The experiment is designed with care. Free-standing membranes avoid substrate clamping. The ferroelectric character is checked by PFM in the device geometry (Fig. 1c). A write-and-hold voltage protocol is used. Normal incidence along c suppresses the birefringence-induced CD identified in recent work (ref. 38). Each configuration was measured 16 times.
- DFPT phonon eigenvectors give a concrete prediction of the azimuthal dependence of J·e_q for three groups of modes. The measured sign pattern across the four q points follows these predictions in all three energy regions.
- Data and calculations are deposited publicly.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The central claim is that the RIXS-CD signal attributed to phonons reverses with the ferroelectric polarization, and the field/helicity cross-check in Fig. 3 together with the mirror-odd q dependence in Fig. 4 support it adequately. The items below concern how far the stronger wording ("quantitative agreement", "g-wave", "non-volatile", "robust") is supported, and controls that would make the result harder to question.

B. Should address

1. **The "quantitative agreement" with first principles amounts to agreement in sign and angular shape only; the relative magnitudes appear inconsistent.**
   - Where: the abstract says the dichroism is "in quantitative agreement with first-principles calculations". In Fig. 4f–h the integrated CD (arbitrary units) is overlaid on J·e_q, and each panel has its own colour scale: about ±0.1 for region I, ±0.06 for II and ±0.002 for III.
   - What is wrong:
     - The calculated helicity of the ~100 meV mode group is about 50 times smaller than that of the 11 meV group, yet its measured integrated CD (about 0.45 at q1) is only about 4–5 times smaller than region I's (about 2).
     - Within region I, the text says the CD is "largest near q1". The data in Fig. 4f instead give a larger value at q2 (φ ≈ 1.78 rad, about 0.9) than at q1 (φ ≈ 2.11 rad, about 0.4). The calculated curve peaks near q1.
   - Why it matters: with one free scale factor per region and four points, Fig. 4f–h tests the sign and rough φ dependence, not the magnitudes.
   - What to do:
     - State explicitly which quantities are fitted (per-region scale factors?) and which are predicted.
     - Explain how the electron–phonon (Franck–Condon) weighting of Supplementary Section 4 and Supplementary Fig. 17 enters the comparison, and whether it accounts for the region III amplitude.
     - Or soften "quantitative" to "agreement in sign and azimuthal dependence".
     - Correct the "largest near q1" statement, or qualify it by region.

2. **The g-wave character is inferred from symmetry; the data test only one mirror plane.**
   - Where: Table 1 and Fig. 4.
   - What is missing: the four q points span φ ≈ 1.40–2.11 rad (from the q values in Table 1), and so cross only the mirror at φ = π/2. A helicity that is odd under that single mirror is also consistent with lower-order patterns. The sign changes at the diagonal mirrors (φ = π/4, 3π/4), which distinguish the g-wave (C4v) pattern, are not sampled; q1 lies about 0.25 rad short of 3π/4.
   - Why it matters: the title and the abstract present the g-wave nature as an observation.
   - What to do: if possible, measure at least one point beyond φ = 3π/4, or at a q related by C4 rotation, to show the second sign change. Otherwise, state clearly that the g-wave form is the symmetry-required pattern for P4mm and that the experiment confirms its mirror-odd component.

3. **The link between the measured CD and the helicity J·e_q needs to be made explicit for this geometry.**
   - Where: page 3 and Eq. (1). The incident beam is along c (normal incidence), the PAM is stated to be "projected in-plane", and the outgoing polarization is not analysed. The text also says the outgoing angle "is chosen to minimize the q-component along c", but in Table 1 q_z = 0.42 Å⁻¹ is the largest component of every q.
   - What is unclear: a reader would naively expect the CD to probe the projection of J on the incident photon axis, which here is perpendicular to the claimed J. The comparison in Fig. 4 instead uses J·e_q.
   - What to do:
     - Summarise in the main text, or in Methods, why the CD in this geometry is proportional to J·e_q (or to J weighted by both k_in and k_out). The derivation is referred to Supplementary Section 3.
     - Give the incidence and scattering angles for each q in Table 1.
     - Reconcile the "minimize q_z" sentence with the tabulated q_z.

4. **Elastic-line and near-elastic artifacts in region I.**
   - Where: region I (0–40 meV) lies within roughly one resolution width (about 30 meV FWHM) of the elastic line. The field-switching and CD traces in Fig. 3 (middle and bottom) also show structure of similar size on the energy-gain side (−0.05 to 0 eV).
   - The risk: a small shift or width change of the elastic or quasi-elastic peak between configurations produces a derivative-shaped difference concentrated exactly in region I. Possible causes are piezoelectric displacement of the membrane under the two hold voltages, or small beam-position or energy changes when the undulator helicity is switched.
   - What to do:
     - Report the fitted elastic-line position, width and intensity for each of the four field/polarization configurations.
     - Show that the polarization-summed spectra (C+ + C−) do not change with field.
     - Use the anti-Stokes side as a check. At 295 K the 11 meV modes are substantially populated, so detailed balance and the symmetry argument predict a specific sign and magnitude of CD at negative energy loss. Showing this would both test the phonon origin and rule out an elastic artifact.

5. **No null or reference measurement under the same voltage protocol.**
   - What is missing: all switching data come from the electroded BTO region. There is no measurement of the field-switching contrast, under the same write/hold sequence, on a region where it should vanish. Candidate references:
     - a non-electroded or short-circuited region of the same membrane;
     - the carbon tape used for energy calibration;
     - a q on the mirror plane (q3) with full statistics, shown as a dedicated null.
   - Why it matters: such a reference would directly exclude electrode-related, charging or beam-induced contributions that are odd in both field and helicity. The cross-symmetry argument makes these unlikely but does not exclude them.

6. **The polarization state under the X-ray spot is not verified in situ, and the device-level evidence is thin.**
   - Where: the ferroelectric switching shown is PFM measured before the experiment (Fig. 1c), using a tip and Au bottom electrode. The device's 1 kHz cycling is described only in the Methods and Supplementary Fig. 5.
   - What is missing:
     - No P–V loop or switching-current measurement on the actual device at the beamline, before and after X-ray exposure, is shown.
     - The text does not say how many devices or membranes were measured.
     - It does not say how many full switching cycles (+ → − → +) the RIXS data span.
   - Why it matters: the abstract calls the effect "robust" and "reversible". X-ray illumination of a thin ferroelectric can cause imprint, photo-induced charge or partial back-switching, and 90° a-domains in an 80 nm membrane would also reduce or distort the signal.
   - What to do:
     - Show switching current or P–V data for the measured device, ideally under illumination.
     - State the number of devices and switching cycles.
     - Show the field-switching contrast as a function of cycle number, or for a second device.

7. **"Non-volatile" is not directly tested.**
   - Where: the abstract claims a "non-volatile gyroelectric effect", but every RIXS measurement was taken under a ±0.2 V holding bias (Methods).
   - Why it matters: 0.2 V is well below the coercive voltage (about 2 V in Fig. 1c), so retention is plausible, but it is not demonstrated.
   - What to do: measure at least one configuration at zero bias after writing, or show polarization retention data for the device over the duration of a measurement. Otherwise, drop "non-volatile" from the abstract.

8. **Statistical significance of the per-region signals.**
   - Where: the spectra in Fig. 3 are Savitzky–Golay smoothed. Several error bars in Fig. 4f–h, especially region III and q3/q4 in region I, are comparable to the values.
   - What to do:
     - State whether the integrated values in Fig. 4f–h use raw or smoothed data.
     - Give for each region a significance of the switching signal against zero, for example the integrated value divided by its uncertainty, or χ² against a null.
     - Clarify how the error bars were obtained. The Fig. 3 caption says "s.d. reflects Poisson counting statistics", while the Methods say the error bars "are the standard deviation of individual scans". It is also unclear whether these are standard deviations or standard errors over the 16 repeats.

9. **Reliability of the harmonic DFT phonons for the low-energy modes at room temperature.**
   - Where: the 11 meV group is assigned using T = 0 harmonic DFPT phonons of tetragonal BTO. Tetragonal BTO at room temperature has strongly anharmonic, partly order–disorder Ti dynamics, and in PBE the tetragonal phase often has unstable branches away from Γ. The relaxed c = 4.11 Å is about 2% larger than experiment, so the tetragonality and Ti off-centring, and with them the computed PAM, are likely overestimated.
   - What to do:
     - Show the computed dispersion along the measured q path and state whether imaginary modes appear.
     - Comment on how sensitive the sign and φ dependence of J·e_q are to the c/a ratio, for example by repeating the calculation at the experimental lattice constants.
   - Separately, the ~100 meV region lies at the top of the one-phonon spectrum (ref. 39), where the paper says multiphonon features begin. Justify attributing region III to a single-phonon mode rather than to two-phonon combinations.

C. Minor

10. The text says the strongest contrast is "around 20, 40 and 100 meV" and attributes it to modes at "11, 38 and 100 meV". Explain the 20 vs 11 meV offset, for example by the resolution or by the overlap of several branches.
11. Methods: "hexagonal lattice constants" should read tetragonal. Also check the "internal pressure of 70 kPa": a pressure that small would not change the lattice constants appreciably, so the unit or the value may be wrong.
12. Fig. 1b: state how the double-well energy scale (about 12 meV barrier) and the ±0.05 Å displacement were obtained from ref. 27, and whether they apply to the strain-free membrane.
13. Fig. 3: label the y-axes consistently. "Field switching" and "CD contrast" should be defined as normalized or raw differences, and the normalization stated. Also state the counting time per spectrum.
14. Fig. 4c–e: mark the measured azimuths φ for q1–q4 numerically, and say whether J in Fig. 4b and in the colour maps is the total or the oxygen-projected PAM.
15. Methods, "Ferroelectric switching in RIXS": explain the "+ − − +" helicity sequence and how drift is removed, as this is central to the field-switching difference.
16. Data availability: the ESRF dataset citation gives the year 2028. Check this.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Show the energy-gain (anti-Stokes) CD and test it against detailed balance. This is the single most convincing internal check that the switching signal comes from thermally populated chiral phonons rather than from the elastic line.
2. Extend the azimuthal map beyond φ = 3π/4, or to a C4-related q, to show the second sign change of the g-wave pattern directly.
3. Add one figure panel with the device-level ferroelectric characterization performed at the beamline (switching current or P–V loop, retention at zero bias), together with the number of devices and switching cycles.
4. Replace "quantitative agreement" with a statement of exactly what is compared: sign and φ dependence with per-region scaling. If the electron–phonon weighting can account for the region amplitudes, show that as a genuine quantitative test.

5. Verdict: minor revision
