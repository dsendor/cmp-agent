Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports circular-dichroism RIXS (CD-RIXS) at the O K edge on a freestanding BaTiO3 membrane in a capacitor geometry. It claims that the CD in the phonon region (0–0.1 eV energy loss) changes sign when the ferroelectric polarisation is reversed, and also when the in-plane momentum transfer is mirrored (q2 to q4). This is presented as electric-field switching of phonon angular momentum (PAM). Electric-field control of PAM in BaTiO3 has been predicted (Ref. 28), and CD-RIXS detection of chiral phonons has been shown in quartz and LiNbO3 (Refs. 17, 20). An experimental demonstration of deterministic, field-reversible phonon chirality in a technologically mature ferroelectric would therefore be new and of broad interest. As presented, the signal is small, close to the elastic line, near the noise level, and from one device and one spot. The evidence that it is intrinsic, field-odd and mode-specific needs strengthening.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The question matters, and the material and device platform (a freestanding BTO membrane with top and bottom electrodes, compatible with RIXS) is well chosen. It is an experimental step beyond static chiral-phonon detection.
- The protocol is sensible. Polarisation is switched repeatedly over about 15 h, helicity is alternated in a '+ − − +' sequence to suppress drifts (Fig. 2c), and incidence is normal along c to suppress birefringence-induced CD (Ref. 39).
- There are two symmetry handles: reversing the field and mirroring q about the (010)/(100) mirror (q2 vs q4, Fig. 4a). q3 lies on the mirror line, where the helicity should vanish, so it serves as a built-in null. The measured integrated contrast there is close to zero (Fig. 4f).
- The data are matched to first-principles phonon eigenvectors (Fig. 4c–h), and the data and computations are deposited.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Statistical significance of the field-odd CD, and exclusion of derivative-type artifacts near the elastic line (Fig. 3b, Fig. 4a). The strongest features in Fig. 3b(i,ii) and Fig. 4a form a bipolar pair: positive around 10–25 meV, negative around 35–50 meV. They sit on the steep high-energy tail of the elastic line, at energies at or below the 30 meV resolution. That shape is what a small relative energy shift, width change or intensity change of the elastic line between helicities or between field states would produce in a difference spectrum. Plausible causes include:
   - a helicity-dependent energy offset of the undulator or monochromator;
   - sample displacement or tilt from piezoelectric or electrostrictive strain under ±3.5 V write and ±0.2 V hold;
   - drift of the elastic-line zero.

   The single-point error bars are comparable to the feature amplitudes. The shaded areas and curves are Savitzky–Golay smoothed, which can make noise look like structure. Panels (i) and (ii) of Fig. 3b are also not independent: they are linear combinations of the same four spectra. Indeed CD(+E) − CD(−E) = FS(C+) − FS(C−) identically. So the statement that "these two observations provide direct evidence" overstates the case.

   The authors should:
   - fit each of the four spectra (C±, ±E) with an elastic line plus phonon components, all sharing a common zero and width, and report the field-odd, helicity-odd change of the fitted phonon amplitudes with uncertainties;
   - show the elastic-line (or elastic-region) CD and field switching explicitly, with fitted elastic positions and widths for each condition;
   - show the helicity-even, field-odd combination [I(C+,+E) + I(C−,+E)] − [I(C+,−E) + I(C−,−E)], which should be small if the effect is purely chiral;
   - quantify significance, for example by χ² against zero or a split-half analysis (first vs second half of the 15 h, odd vs even switching cycles), so the reproducibility of the sign reversal across cycles can be judged;
   - show the unsmoothed data alongside the smoothed curves in the main figures.

2. Verification that the polarisation in the probed volume was actually reversed during the RIXS measurement, and support for the "non-volatile" claim (abstract, Fig. 2, Methods "Ferroelectric switching in RIXS").
   - Switching is established only before the beamtime, by IV/oscilloscope and by a PFM phase loop (Fig. 2b). It is not stated whether the PFM was on the same membrane and electrode.
   - No in-situ check is reported: no switching-current transient at each write pulse, no capacitance or polarisation readout, and no X-ray structural or spectroscopic signature of the polar state.
   - Under prolonged X-ray exposure at 531 eV, oxide membranes can develop photo-induced charge, oxygen-vacancy migration or leakage, which can pin domains or change the coercive voltage.
   - The film thickness is not given ("various thicknesses" in Methods), so the applied fields (3.5 V write, 0.2 V hold) and their relation to the coercive field cannot be assessed. The Fig. 2b loop also shows a sizeable imprint (switching near −1.5 V and +3.5 V).
   - Every measurement was taken with a ±0.2 V hold applied. Remanence, and hence non-volatility, is therefore not demonstrated. A sign-reversed DC bias during the measurement is also a possible extrinsic source of field-odd signal.
   - "Stable for at least 15 hours" appears to mean reproducible over about six switching cycles in 15 h (Fig. 2c), each state lasting about 1–1.5 h. It does not mean one state persisting for 15 h.

   The authors should:
   - give the membrane thickness;
   - provide in-situ switching-current or P–E data recorded during the beamtime;
   - measure CD-RIXS at zero bias after switching, to show remanence;
   - ideally include a control such as a sub-coercive write pulse (for example ±1.5 V, which should leave the CD unchanged) or a spot outside the electrode;
   - otherwise rephrase "non-volatile" and "stable for 15 hours" to match what was measured.

B. Should address

3. Mode assignment and quantitative comparison with DFT (p. 7, Fig. 4f–h). The experimental CD extrema at about 20, 40 and 100 meV are assigned to computed modes at 11, 38 and 100 meV. The 11 vs 20 meV mismatch is large compared with typical DFT errors for BTO. With 30 meV resolution, regions I and II each contain several modes (the text admits this for q1). The high-resolution (15 meV) scan, which is said to confirm the assignment, is not shown in the main text, and "perfect qualitative agreement" is not substantiated.

   In Fig. 4f–h a separate free scale factor is applied to each region, and only four q points are compared. One of these is called an outlier and another (q3) is a symmetry-required zero, so the test is weak. The magnitudes are also inconsistent with a simple proportionality. The computed J·q for the 100 meV mode (Fig. 4h colour bar, about ±0.002) is about 50 times smaller than for the 11 meV mode (about ±0.1), yet the measured contrasts differ only by about a factor of 3–4. This implies very different RIXS coupling constants, which should be justified.

   The authors should:
   - show the high-resolution spectra in the main text;
   - compute the resolution-convolved, coupling-weighted CD spectrum (the mode effective charge or Franck–Condon weights of Sec. S4 multiplied by the PAM projection onto the relevant X-ray direction) and compare it with the data, with one global scale factor rather than one per region;
   - state how many free parameters enter Fig. 4f–h.

4. Which projection of J the CD measures, and how PAM is defined (Eqs. 1–2, p. 9, Fig. 4b). The text variously says that:
   - CD measures the circular motion of the oxygen atoms (p. 6);
   - CD is sensitive "only to out-of-plane ion displacements" (p. 9);
   - CD follows J·q, the phonon helicity (p. 9).

   In Ref. 17 the CD-RIXS contrast is tied to the PAM projection on the photon wavevector(s), not to q. In the present geometry (normal incidence, outgoing beam about 30–35° above the surface for |q| ≈ 0.48 Å⁻¹), the incident and outgoing wavevectors differ greatly from q. The PAM is also "estimated as the maximal area of the ellipse the oxygen atom traverses". This is non-standard, and it is unclear whether the result is the mass-weighted, eigenvector-based l = ħ ε†Mε summed over all atoms or an oxygen-only quantity.

   The authors should:
   - derive explicitly which component of the (oxygen-projected) PAM enters A_m for the actual k_in and k_out;
   - recompute the Fig. 4c–h maps for that quantity using the standard PAM definition;
   - check that the predicted sign change between q2 and q4, and the zero at q3, still hold.

5. DFT structure versus the measured sample (Methods, DFT). The relaxed PBE-D3 cell has c/a = 4.11/3.99 ≈ 1.03, compared with about 1.01 experimentally (the paper's own reference values are 4.00/4.03). PBE is known to overestimate tetragonality in BTO, and this directly enlarges the polar distortion that produces the PT-breaking, and hence the PAM. The structure of the measured membrane is not given (c/a, thickness, strain after release). Room-temperature tetragonal BTO is also strongly anharmonic, with an order–disorder component, so harmonic phonons of the relaxed cell may misplace the soft polar modes in the 10–40 meV range. LO–TO splitting (Born effective charges, non-analytic correction) should be stated as included or not.

   The authors should:
   - repeat the calculations at the experimental lattice parameters (or with a functional such as PBEsol), or show the sensitivity of mode energies and J·q to c/a;
   - report the membrane's lattice parameters from the XRD in the supplementary information.

6. Single device, single spot, no reference sample. All results come from one membrane and one electrode, with no device-to-device or spot-to-spot repetition. The freestanding film in Fig. 2a is visibly cracked, and a/c-domain or wrinkle content within the 50 μm spot is not characterised. In-plane anisotropy from a-domains or wrinkles could produce a birefringence-type CD that is odd under the φ mirror. A reference measurement under the same protocol on a non-polar membrane (for example SrTiO3 at room temperature) or a centrosymmetric region, or at a q along c or on a mirror line under field switching, would confirm that the protocol yields no field-odd CD where none is expected. At minimum, report the CD and field switching at q3 explicitly, together with the domain characterisation.

7. Birefringence control. The paper argues that normal incidence along c removes birefringence-induced CD, but only for the incident beam and only if the probed volume is a single c-domain. Supplementary Fig. S.4 shows strong CD under other geometries. The authors should quantify the residual (for example from sample tilt or a-domain fraction) and explain why any residual term would be odd under polarisation reversal. A uniaxial dielectric tensor is even in P_s.

8. Gyroelectric analogy (pp. 8–9, Eqs. 3–4). Point group 4mm is not optically active, so the long-wavelength gyration tensor of BTO vanishes. The analogy with Pb5Ge3O11 (point group 3, optically active) therefore holds only at finite q, through higher-order spatial dispersion. The discussion should state this explicitly and give the form of the finite-q pseudoscalar or pseudotensor allowed by 4mm (the "g-type" pattern) rather than invoking Eq. 4 directly.

C. Minor

9. Units. Page 5 gives q = (−0.12, 0.20, 0.42) "r.l.u.", while Table 1 and Fig. 4 use Å⁻¹. In r.l.u., q_z = 0.42 would correspond to about 0.65 Å⁻¹, which exceeds 2k at 531 eV (about 0.54 Å⁻¹), so Å⁻¹ is presumably correct. Please also give the incident and outgoing angles for each q.
10. Error bars. Fig. 3's caption says error bars come from Poisson statistics on data rebinned by 2, while Methods says they are standard deviations of individual scans. State which is used where, and how the integrated-contrast error bars in Fig. 4f–h were propagated.
11. Normalisation. State how the C+ and C− spectra are normalised (incident flux, degree of circular polarisation, acquisition time), and whether Fig. 3a has a vertical offset.
12. Methods, DFT. "Hexagonal lattice constants" should read tetragonal.
13. Fig. 1b. The double-well axis shows Ti displacements of about ±0.05 Å with a barrier of about 20 meV. State the source and whether these are DFT values for the computed structure.
14. Fig. 2. Give the PFM measurement location relative to the measured electrode. Clarify the phrase "the area corresponds to roughly the x-ray spot size (50 microns long axis)" given a 60 μm pad and a cracked membrane.
15. Wording. "Excellent agreement" (abstract) and "perfect qualitative agreement" (p. 6) overstate what the data show. Ref. 52 is dated 2028.
16. Videos of the mode eigenvectors are cited but should also be described by their numerical PAM values in a supplementary table (mode energy, oxygen-projected and total PAM components at each q).

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Replace the smoothed difference curves with a joint model fit of all four (helicity × field) spectra. Report the field-odd, helicity-odd phonon amplitude with uncertainties, show its reproducibility across individual switching cycles, and show the helicity-even, field-odd residual as an artifact check.
2. Record in-situ switching currents during the beamtime and add a zero-bias (remanent) CD-RIXS measurement together with a sub-coercive-pulse control. This would directly support "non-volatile" and rule out bias-induced artifacts.
3. Show the 15 meV high-resolution spectra in the main text. Compare them with a resolution-convolved, coupling-weighted DFT CD spectrum, computed at experimental lattice parameters with a single global scale factor.
4. Add more q points, including points across both mirror lines and the reverse φ range, plus a second electrode or membrane, so the C4v pattern of the field-switching contrast is mapped rather than sampled at four points.

5. Verdict: major revision
