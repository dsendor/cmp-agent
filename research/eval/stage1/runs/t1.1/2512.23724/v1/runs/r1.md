Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports STM/STS of cleaved Ta2Pd3Te5, a candidate excitonic insulator. It finds pairs of in-gap states (E- below and E+ above E_F) bound to one class of point defects, which DFT image simulations assign to Ta vacancies. The authors interpret these as the long-predicted excitonic analogue of Yu-Shiba-Rusinov (YSR) states, induced by the charge dipole of the vacancy. An impurity-bound state that demonstrably arises from local breaking of electron-hole pairing would be a significant result, both as a fingerprint of excitonic order in a material where the order is still debated and as a new local probe. The present evidence, however, shows defect-bound states inside a gap that tracks the gap. It does not yet show that these states are specific to an excitonic condensate rather than ordinary shallow-defect states of a gapped semiconductor.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- High-quality atomically resolved topography and dI/dV maps. The defects are classified into five types (alpha, alpha', beta, beta', gamma), and the in-gap states show clear spatial extent (about 5 nm) for each type (Fig. 2, Supp. Fig. 8, Supp. Fig. 9).
- A useful internal contrast: defects with in-gap states (assigned to Ta vacancies) versus defects without them (assigned to Pd vacancies; Supp. Figs. 6, 7, 10).
- Two ways of changing the gap locally: a naturally strained stripe where the gap closes into a pseudogap (Fig. 3a-d), and tip-height-dependent gap reduction (Fig. 3e-g, Supp. Figs. 14-16). The in-gap states follow the gap edges and disappear when the gap closes.
- A combination of DFT (vacancy structure, charge redistribution) and a minimal model of a charged impurity in a gapped two-band system (Fig. 4f).
- The topic connects two communities (YSR physics and excitonic insulators), and the material has a high transition temperature.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The data and model do not separate excitonic pair-breaking (YSR) states from ordinary impurity states of a gapped semiconductor or hybridization insulator. This is the central claim of the title and abstract. Any charged or dipolar point defect in a 100 meV-gap semiconductor binds shallow states split off from the band edges. A dipole has both attractive and repulsive regions for electrons, so it naturally binds one state below the conduction-band edge and one above the valence-band edge. Such states are many nanometres in extent when the effective mass is small and screening is strong. They vanish when the gap closes, and they move when the band edges move. The three arguments offered against this have problems:
   - The extent of the states "immediately suggests" a many-body origin (p. 5). This does not follow: the hydrogenic Bohr radius of a shallow state is also nanometres.
   - The states disappear in the metallic stripe (p. 6), which "clearly indicates" an excitonic origin. This is expected for any band-edge bound state once the gap closes.
   - The states shift with the gap under tip-height tuning (p. 7, "solidly confirms"). This is also expected for any band-edge bound state.
   The model in Eq. (1) has the same problem. Delta is a fixed, uniform, site-local c-v hybridization. It is not determined self-consistently and is not suppressed around the impurity. Mathematically, Eq. (1) is a non-interacting hybridization-gap insulator, so the bound states in Fig. 4f are those of a band insulator with a dipole potential, and nothing in the calculation involves pair breaking. The same holds for the monopole/dipole contrast.
   What to do:
   - Compute Delta_i self-consistently with an interaction term and show the local suppression of the order parameter around the defect.
   - Compare with a trivial band insulator of the same gap and band masses, to show what is distinctive.
   - Identify an experimental observable that differs between the two scenarios. Examples: the scaling of E+/- with Delta at fixed defect potential (YSR energies scale with Delta, while hydrogenic binding energies are set by the effective mass and dielectric constant and do not); spatial anticorrelation between E+ and E- intensity, or oscillations at the excitonic coherence length rather than at an effective-mass Bohr radius; a local reduction of the gap edges near the defect.
   - Without such a discriminator, retitle and rephrase the claim (e.g., "defect-bound in-gap states in the excitonic insulator Ta2Pd3Te5") and present the YSR mechanism as one interpretation.

2. Tip-induced band bending and tip-induced charging of the defect are not ruled out as the origin of the sharp peaks and of their Delta Z dependence. Ta2Pd3Te5 at 4 K is a small-gap semiconductor, so the tip electric field can bend the bands and change the charge state of a defect level during the bias sweep. Several features in the data look like such effects:
   - The E- feature is a very sharp spike or step (Figs. 2f-j, 3f; Supp. Figs. 16c and 18).
   - Bright parabolic arcs on the filled-state side move with tip height in Fig. 3e (right) and Supp. Fig. 16a.
   - The in-gap intensity in Fig. 2k-o appears as discs a few nm across.
   - The authors themselves invoke strong tip fields ("Wannier-Stark ladder", Supp. Fig. 14) and a "quantum contact" regime where the current saturates (Supp. Fig. 15).
   If the peaks are charging transitions or band-bending-shifted defect levels, then their energies and their apparent tracking of the gap edges under Delta Z reflect tip-sample electrostatics, not excitonic physics. The method of gap tuning (carrier injection by the tunnelling current) is itself contested for Ta2NiSe5 (refs. 54, 55), and the paper does not discuss this. What to do:
   - Show set-point dependence of E+/- at fixed lateral position, varying current at constant bias and bias at constant current.
   - Show dI/dV maps at several energies around E+/- to check whether the bright discs expand with bias (the signature of charging rings) or keep a fixed shape.
   - Show line spectra across the defect, checking whether E+/- shift with lateral distance.
   - Give an estimate of tip-induced band bending for the work-function difference and tip heights used.
   - Repeat the measurements with at least one other tip.

B. Should address

3. The strain interpretation of the metallic stripe is not established. The text attributes the gap collapse to "strain variation of epsilon ≈ 2.4%" (p. 6). Supp. Fig. 13 obtains this from the excess FWHM of an FFT Bragg peak, Delta_FWHM/Q_B. That quantity reflects the spread of local lattice periodicity together with window size and processing. It is not a uniform strain, and the same caption also quotes "about 1%" shear. The stripe is a topographic protrusion of about 0.2-0.3 nm (Fig. 3c), which could equally come from a subsurface intercalant, stacking fault, domain wall or local doping. Any of these would close the gap without strain. Since this region serves as the "gap-off" control, the authors should:
   - map the local lattice constants directly (e.g., Lawler-Fujita drift-corrected displacement fields) to give strain components and their sign;
   - show the band-edge positions across the stripe, to separate a rigid shift (doping) from gap closure;
   - discuss alternative origins of the stripe;
   - state whether a similar stripe was found elsewhere or in another cleave.

4. Statistics and reproducibility are not reported. The paper does not state how many defects of each type were measured, how many cleaves or crystals were studied, or how many tips were used. The table in Supp. Fig. 9b gives single E+/- values with no spread or uncertainty. The tip-height sweeps (Fig. 3g, Supp. Fig. 16) appear to be one curve per defect type. The authors should report N per defect type, the distribution of E+/-, and the sweep repeated on several defects of the same type. Device-to-device spread and tip-to-tip spread are what establish that the five types are distinct and that the E+/- energies reflect intrinsic defect physics.

5. The DFT assignment and the "charged versus neutral" argument need strengthening.
   - The argument that DFT "do[es] not predict the pairs of in-gap LDOS" (p. 6) is not informative. PBE gives essentially no gap for this system (Supp. Fig. 5), so DFT could not produce gap-edge bound states in either scenario.
   - The vacancy calculations do not say what the charge state of the defect cell is. They also do not state the supercell size, slab or bulk geometry, or the image-dipole corrections. All of this matters for the claimed dipole and for the "non-charged" Pd vacancy used to argue against a strain mechanism (p. 8).
   - The evidence that the Ta vacancy is "locally charged" rests on a 0.3 eV rigid shift of one Te PDOS (Supp. Fig. 10f). Supp. Fig. 11 shows that this differs between the alpha/alpha' and beta/beta' sites.
   - Formation energies of the competing vacancies (Te, Pd, Ta; top and second layer) should be given, to support assigning the much more numerous trivial defects (Supp. Fig. 6) to Pd vacancies and the rarer ones to Ta vacancies.
   - The gamma defect is assigned only by exclusion ("thought to be").
   - The simulated Pd-vacancy images in Supp. Fig. 7a appear blank.

6. Model parameters are not connected to the DFT dipole or to the measured energies. The model has these parameters:
   - 2D square lattice;
   - particle-hole symmetric bands with t = 0.5 eV and mu = -1.5 eV;
   - Gaussian width sigma = 2 nm, larger than the charge separation d = 1 nm;
   - strengths V0 up to 0.24 eV (dipole) and 0.10 eV (monopole).
   The text does not explain how these relate to the DFT charge distribution (Fig. 4d,e) or to the quasi-1D anisotropic Dirac bands and the additional VB2 band of the real material. The comparison with data is qualitative. The asymmetry of E+ and E-, which is the most visible feature of the data, is attributed to VB2 without calculation. The authors should:
   - use an anisotropic two-band model with realistic velocities, gap and VB2, and derive V0 and the geometry from DFT;
   - show that the observed E+/- and decay lengths follow for the different vacancy sites;
   - add a dipole with sigma comparable to d, to show the result is not tied to the specific choice.

7. A quantitative energy-versus-gap relation is missing. The abstract states that the in-gap energies are "strongly correlated with the excitonic band gap". Fig. 3g plots dashed guide lines against Delta Z, not E+/- against the measured local gap. The authors should plot E+/- (and E+/- normalized to the local gap) against the gap measured at the same tip height for each defect type. This test would also help with concern 1: in a YSR picture E/Delta should be constant at fixed impurity coupling, which can be checked against the shallow-state picture.

8. The magnetic-field test cannot discriminate the alternatives it is meant to exclude. Supp. Fig. 18 reports no splitting at 2.5 T and 1.26 K. A Zeeman splitting at 2.5 T is of order 0.3 meV for g = 2, far below the 5-10 meV width of the in-gap peaks, so the absence of visible splitting does not exclude a spin-degenerate topological defect state. Either quantify the expected splitting against the linewidth and soften the conclusion on p. 8, or use a test sensitive to the topological alternative.

9. The temperature dependence is absent. All in-gap data are at 4.4 K, or at 1.26 K for Supp. Fig. 18. A YSR state should follow the order parameter as T approaches T_c, whereas shallow states of a band gap would not care about the ordering temperature in the same way. Even a limited variable-temperature measurement (e.g., 4-80 K, or higher if possible) of E+/- and of the gap would strengthen the link to the condensate. Alternatively, state that this is outside the present scope.

10. Comparison with the coherence length. The in-gap states extend about 5-6 nm (Fig. 2, p. 5). Compare this with the excitonic coherence length estimated from the ARPES band velocity and the gap (xi ~ hbar v / Delta), and with an effective-mass Bohr radius. This quantitative comparison would make the "many-body" argument concrete.

C. Minor

11. The dipole moment value p_z = -0.45 x 10^-12 C·m (p. 7, Supp. Fig. 17) cannot be right. One electron charge times one ångström is 1.6 x 10^-29 C·m, so the units or normalization (per area? per cell?) need correcting. The moment is also labelled p_z, while the text and Fig. 4e describe it as lying along y (b), perpendicular to the chains. The lower panel of Fig. 4e is labelled z (Å) while the caption says x-axis. Please make the axes consistent.

12. The Methods state that all STM/STS was done at 4.4 K, but Supp. Fig. 18 is at 1.26 K. Please describe the second set-up or condition, and give the lock-in modulation amplitude, set-point conditions and normalization procedure for the "normalized dI/dV" used throughout.

13. Gap values are inconsistent. The text quotes about 100 meV from ARPES and STS (p. 4), about 140 meV at normal tunnelling conditions (p. 7), and 365 K versus 370 K for the transition temperature (p. 4). Please reconcile these, and state how the STS gap edges are defined.

14. Fig. 1d is described as "ARPES intensity maps" but appears to show only guide lines. Please show the underlying ARPES data (or move it to the SI), together with the temperatures and cuts.

15. The caption of Supp. Fig. 13 refers to "Fig. 4a" for the strained area, which is Fig. 3a. Supplementary figures are numbered 5-20 in the SI but cited as S1-S16 in the text. Typos: "cureves" (Supp. Figs. 15, 16) and "exhibited" (p. 2).

16. References 29 and 31 are duplicates. Ref. 61, cited for the experimental lattice constants in the Methods, is a topological edge-state paper; please check it.

17. The data availability statement contradicts itself: "available on request" and "provided as Source Data file". Please clarify.

18. Supp. Fig. 16 notes an in-gap state of the alpha' defect inside the pseudogap in the quantum contact regime, "whose origin is not understood". This bears on the claim that the states require the excitonic gap. It deserves a sentence in the main text.

19. In Fig. 3d the defect spectra inside the stripe show a deeper zero-bias dip than the grey reference band. Please comment on whether this is a local Coulomb or pseudogap effect at the defect.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a self-consistent mean-field calculation (Delta_i allowed to vary in space) with an explicit interaction. Contrast it with a non-interacting band insulator of the same gap. Then identify and measure one observable that differs between the two, such as the E/Delta scaling under tip tuning or the spatial profile of the gap edges near the defect.
- Add a full set-point, energy-resolved map and line-cut analysis around one alpha defect to exclude tip-induced charging and band bending. Repeat the key Delta Z sweep with a second tip and on a second cleave.
- Replace the FFT-width strain estimate with a real-space displacement-field analysis of the stripe, and show the band-edge profile across it.
- Tone down "solidly confirms", "clearly indicates" and "establish" until the discriminating evidence is in place.

5. Verdict: major revision
