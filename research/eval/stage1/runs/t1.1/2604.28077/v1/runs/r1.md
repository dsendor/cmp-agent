Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use non-contact AFM at 10 mK, with Kelvin probe and dissipation spectroscopy, on LaAlO3/SrTiO3 devices patterned by ULV-EBL and c-AFM. They report three things: a ~1.5 V contact-potential contrast across written channels, converted into a carrier-density profile; a change in the bias dependence of dissipation, from ΔV² outside the channel to αΔV² + βΔV⁴ inside it, which they propose as a local marker of superconductivity; and, near the critical field, a dissipation minimum ~200 nm wide at the channel edge, which they read as an optimally doped superconducting edge channel. A local probe of superconductivity in patterned LAO/STO at millikelvin temperatures would bear directly on the "1D edge superconductivity" debate (Pai et al., PRL 2018), so the work could have high impact. As presented, though, the superconducting origin of the dissipation contrast and the edge-channel picture both rest on a narrow set of data with no controls.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The experiment is technically demanding: a millikelvin qPlus AFM/KPFM combined with in-situ magnetotransport, on devices written and transported in vacuum. The capability is new for this materials system.
- The KPFM contrast is clear: about 1.5 V on Device A (Fig. 2e) and about 0.3 V on Device B (Supp. Fig. 3c). Its absence on etched bare STO (Supp. Fig. 3c, Section 2) is a useful null control.
- The dissipation depends strongly on field and bias, with a sharp minimum near zero field and maxima at about ±100 mT (Fig. 4c,e). This points to a link with the superconducting state that is worth developing.
- The data set (3D Δf/excitation grids against bias, position and field) is rich. The paper is honest that its 1D conclusions are "suggestive" (Outlook).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The superconducting origin of the dissipation contrast is not separated from normal-state conductivity and electrostatic contrast.**
   - *What the paper uses.* The central diagnostic is the comparison in Fig. 4b between point A (inside the channel) and point B (outside). Point A shows a lower baseline, a weaker ΔV² term and a ΔV⁴ term.
   - *The alternative.* A and B also differ in sheet conductance (insulating against metallic 2DES), CPD (about −0.5 V against about +0.1 V), local carrier density and tip height (Fig. 4a, about several nm of Z contrast under constant-Δf feedback). Electronic-friction theory (Volokitin–Persson, ref. 32) predicts that the ohmic dissipation coefficient depends non-monotonically on sheet resistance. A conducting normal-state 2DES that screens well would therefore also show a much smaller ΔV² term than a poorly conducting region. A lower baseline inside the channel is therefore expected without superconductivity.
   - *Controls that are missing.* The tests that would isolate superconductivity are:
     - temperature dependence at fixed tip position through Tc (about 200–300 mK);
     - the same A/B comparison at fields well above Bc;
     - field dependence at the reference point B.

     None is shown. The only field dependence is at point A (Fig. 4c,e). Above the critical field, point A does not revert to the convex ΔV² form seen at B; it becomes concave (Fig. 4d, 162 and 222 mT). The simple picture (ohmic ΔV² in the normal state, ΔV⁴ when ohmic loss is gapped) therefore does not describe the data.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Show Γ(V_bias) at A and B at matched tip–sample distance, at B = 0 and at |B| ≫ Bc (for example 300 mT and above).
     - Show Γ(B) at point B.
     - Ideally, show Γ(V_bias) at point A as a function of temperature through Tc.

     Without these, the claim that the ΔV⁴ term is "a robust local marker of superconductivity" (p. 9) is not supported.

2. **The ~200 nm edge-channel claim rests on one line cut, at one boundary, at one field above the transport critical field, in one device.**
   - *Where the evidence comes from.* The edge-channel conclusion (abstract, Fig. 5, Discussion, Outlook) comes entirely from Fig. 5. That is a single rectangular area covering one edge of the 2D channel, taken at B = −235 mT. Device A's transport critical field is about 200 mT (Fig. 1e; the text calls −235 mT "near the critical field"), so the map was taken outside the field range where transport shows superconductivity.
   - *Specific problems.*
     - The dissipation minimum in Fig. 5h is at Y ≈ 450–500 nm. The estimated patterned boundary at X = 125 nm (green dashed line in Fig. 5a) lies at about Y ≈ 580–600 nm. The minimum therefore appears to sit on the unpatterned side of the boundary, which the text does not discuss.
     - No zero-field spatial map of Γ is shown. The opposite edge of the 1 µm channel was not mapped, so the edge-channel picture (superconductivity at both edges, proximitized interior) is not tested.
     - The 1D wire and the waveguide were not mapped either, although they are the geometries that motivate the study.
     - Device B has no dissipation data.
     - The minimum may be the depletion fringe at the boundary of a normal conductor, where density and conductivity pass through the value of maximal or minimal electronic friction. If so, it would show up at any field and temperature.
   - *What the authors should do.* Provide Γ maps across both edges of the 2D channel and across the 1D wire:
     - at zero field, at intermediate fields and well above Bc;
     - ideally, also above Tc.

     They should show that the narrow minimum appears only in the superconducting state, and should reproduce it on at least one more boundary or device.

3. **The acquisition mode of the bias spectroscopy is not stated, and tip-height or feedback artifacts could produce the ΔV⁴ term.**
   - *What is missing.* The SI does not say whether the Γ(V_bias) curves in Figs. 4b, 4c and 5b were taken with the distance feedback open (constant height) or closed (constant Δf). The captions give a Δf setpoint "used for feedback".
   - *Why it matters.* If feedback was active during the bias sweep, the tip retracts as |V_CPD − V_bias| grows, because Δf ∝ ΔV². Dissipation depends steeply on tip–sample distance, so the height modulation alone would make Γ deviate from a pure ΔV² law and generate apparent higher-order terms. Even with feedback open, two effects add to this:
     - Δf changes strongly with bias, and the amplitude/PLL controllers can turn that into "apparent dissipation" crosstalk.
     - A bias of up to 1.2 V across a few-nm gap gates the 2DES locally, which the authors acknowledge (p. 11). This changes the local density and conductivity during the sweep.
   - *Why this is an A item.* Any of these effects alone could give a convex curve with a ΔV⁴ component, and they would differ between A and B because Z and CPD differ there.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - State the acquisition mode and the absolute tip–sample distance for each data set.
     - Show the simultaneously recorded Δf(V) and Z(V).
     - Show the distance dependence of Γ at A and B.
     - Show that the β/α contrast survives at matched tip height.

B. Should address

4. **The carrier-density calibration is underdetermined, yet the doping-dome assignment depends on absolute densities.**
   - *Calibration choices.*
     - Eq. (1) gives only Δn relative to an unpatterned reference, which is assumed to have zero density.
     - The reference CPD is picked by hand: 1.024 V at the bottom-left corner of Fig. 2e, although the bottom-right corner of that image is about 1.5 V, and 0.07 V at the bottom-right corner of Fig. 5a.
     - The CPD range on the same device shifts from 0.1–1.5 V (Fig. 2e) to −0.5–0.15 V (Fig. 5a). This suggests a change in tip work function between data sets, which contradicts the assumption that φ_tip is constant.
     - ε_r = 25 is a bulk literature value applied to a 4 uc film at 10 mK.
     - Surface-dipole and protonation contributions, which the SI itself names as the mechanism behind writing, are neglected.
   - *Internal inconsistency.* The "overdoped" plateau in Fig. 5h has Δn ≈ 4.5–5 × 10¹³ cm⁻². That lies inside the "optimal" range of 1.5–6.5 × 10¹³ cm⁻² quoted on p. 7. The labels under-, optimal- and over-doped in Fig. 5g,h and in the Discussion are therefore not supported by the authors' own numbers. Separately, Fig. 3b (peak about 8 × 10¹³) and the text (Δn ≈ 1 × 10¹⁴) do not quite agree.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Give uncertainties for Δn that include the choice of reference and ε_r.
     - Explain the ~1 V CPD offset between Fig. 2e and Fig. 5a.
     - Either drop the absolute doping labels or anchor them independently, for example with Hall density measured on the same channel or by tuning the side gate and following how the minimum moves.

5. **The field dependence in Fig. 4e does not match the text, and transport and local data come from different parts of the device.**
   - *What the figure shows.* The text says the dissipation crossover happens "as the field approaches 200 mT", which it ties to the transport Bc (p. 11). In Fig. 4e, however, Γ peaks at about ±80–100 mT and is already falling by 200 mT. The sharp minimum is only about ±20 mT wide.
   - *Which segment each measurement covers.* The Bc of about 200 mT comes from R45 between contacts 4 and 5 (Fig. 1d,e; Supp. Fig. 5a). That path runs through the upper "Y" region, not through the 2D channel near contact 7 where Figs. 4 and 5 were taken.
   - *Data quality at zero field.* The zero-field minimum, the key field signature, sits next to a feedback "glitch" at small negative field (Fig. 4e caption).
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Report transport through the 2D channel itself.
     - Show Γ(B) for both sweep directions, to rule out hysteresis or trapped flux.
     - Repeat the zero-field region without the glitch.
     - Revise the text to match where the extrema actually are.

6. **The fits in Fig. 4b do not support the stated qualitative difference.**
   - *Mismatch with the text.* The text says point B shows "clear quadratic dependence", yet its fit gives β/α = 1.84 V⁻², about half the value at A (3.85 V⁻²).
   - *Unequal ranges.* The two curves span different ΔV ranges: roughly ±0.7 V at A and roughly ±0.4 V at B. The quartic term only matters for |ΔV| ≳ 0.5 V, so the comparison is not like for like.
   - *Missing fit details.* The fit form omits a constant offset that the data clearly need. The free parameters and the bias window are not given.
   - *Potential at point B.* B lies on an insulating, electrically floating region. The local potential there is not set by V_bias in the same way as on the grounded 2DES, so "CPD" and ΔV mean different things at A and B.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Fit both curves over the same ΔV range, with all parameters listed.
     - Report β/α as a spatial map, not at two points.
     - Discuss how the bias is referenced on the insulating region.

7. **The proximity-effect explanation is quantitatively inconsistent.**
   - *The numbers.* The Discussion says ξ ≈ 60–100 nm from each edge "naturally" explains superconducting signatures "throughout" a ~1 µm channel (p. 14). Proximity regions of about ξ from each edge would cover only about 120–200 nm of the 1 µm width, leaving most of the interior uncovered.
   - *Missing evidence.* No spatially resolved field dependence is shown that would demonstrate the interior losing its signature before the edges. The claimed "spatial hierarchy" is not in the data.
   - *What the authors should do.* Either show Γ(x, B) across the full channel width, or present the proximity picture as speculation.

8. **Device B does not show a clear superconducting transition, and the transport numbers in the text do not match the figures.**
   - *Device B.* Its resistance at zero current (Supp. Fig. 4b) is about 1.5–2 kΩ, compared with a normal value of about 2 kΩ. That is not zero resistance. Its Bc is about 55 mT, against about 200 mT for Device A.
   - *Device A.* The text says "both devices show Ic of 30 nA". The Fig. 1f line cut for Device A shows switching at about ±12 nA, and the zero-field Ic of Device A is not stated.
   - *Device B thickness.* The SI says that without patterning "the LAO layer is below the critical thickness". That is surprising for 6 uc LAO and needs justification.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Report Ic and Bc for each device at zero field.
     - Clarify whether the configuration is four-terminal or quasi-two-terminal (Supp. Fig. 5 shows the voltage taken at the same pads as the current).
     - Either show dissipation data on Device B or describe the result as a single-device observation.

9. **Prior local imaging of superconductivity in LAO/STO is misstated.**
   - *The claim.* The introduction (p. 3) says that none of the earlier scanning-probe techniques "was performed at the ultra-low temperatures necessary to probe the superconducting state" and that none "directly imaged superconductivity".
   - *Why it is wrong.* Scanning SQUID susceptometry has imaged the superconducting diamagnetic response and superfluid density of LAO/STO below Tc (for example Bert et al., Nature Physics 7, 767 (2011), and their later gate-tuned work).
   - *What the authors should do.* Correct the statement and explain what this AFM approach adds: spatial resolution, and compatibility with nanoscale written structures.

10. **The effective sample temperature during spectroscopy is unknown.**
    - *Possible heating sources.*
      - the tip oscillates at 3–5 nm amplitude;
      - biases reach 1.2 V;
      - the Ir tip sits close to a 2DES whose Tc is only about 200–300 mK.
    - *Why it matters.* Tip-induced or excitation-related heating would shift the local state toward Tc and could itself produce field-dependent dissipation.
    - *What the authors should do.* Show that the Γ features do not depend on oscillation amplitude, and state the electron temperature of the device, measured for example from transport with the tip engaged.

C. Minor

11. **Figure and text cross-references are inconsistent.**
    - The Discussion refers to the "green curve" in Fig. 4b, but Fig. 4b has teal (A) and purple (B) curves.
    - The Fig. 5 caption gives the uncertainty for "(f) and (g)", but (g) is a schematic; this should probably read (h).
    - The main text (p. 5) cites Supp. Fig. 4c in the Device A paragraph, but that panel shows Device B.
    - Supp. Fig. 4a is labelled R47 against I45, while Supp. Fig. 5b shows current injected at contact 1.
    - The caption of Supp. Fig. 2a says "ULV-EBL" for the c-AFM device.
    - The "sky blue areas in Supp. Fig. 2a" appear to be in Supp. Fig. 3a.

12. **The 42 mT curve in Fig. 4d is not discussed.** This orange curve has a double-hump shape with a central minimum, which is neither convex nor concave. Its meaning should be addressed.

13. **The Fig. 1e caption makes an unsupported claim.** It asserts that the asymmetric current dependence "does not affect the superconducting properties", with no evidence. Either support it or remove it.

14. **SPM parameters are missing.** Please give:
    - the qPlus spring constant;
    - the method used to calibrate the amplitude;
    - the PLL and amplitude-controller bandwidths;
    - the intrinsic Γ0;
    - the tip preparation and any tip changes between data sets.

    Γ0 = k/(2πf0Q) is comparable in size to the measured Γ, so the baseline subtraction matters.

15. **"Statistical uncertainty is less than the symbol size" is not an adequate error estimate.** For maps with a single acquisition per pixel, the authors should give the point-to-point noise and the repeatability between sweeps.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Take Γ(V_bias) maps at a fixed tip height across the full width of the 2D channel and across the 1D wire. Do this at zero field and at fields well above Bc, and ideally at T above Tc as well. That would show directly which features are tied to superconductivity.
2. Use the side gate to tune the channel density during the dissipation mapping. Following how the dissipation minimum moves with gate voltage would test the "optimal-doping edge" picture without relying on the Eq. (1) calibration.
3. Measure transport through the same 2D channel segment that is imaged. If possible, do it with the tip engaged, so that the local and global critical fields and temperatures can be compared directly.
4. Reframe the abstract and conclusions around what the data show: a field-sensitive dissipation contrast between patterned and unpatterned regions. Present the edge-channel and proximity interpretations as hypotheses until the controls above are in place.

5. Verdict: major revision
