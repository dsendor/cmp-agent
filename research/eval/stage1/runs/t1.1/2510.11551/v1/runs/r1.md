Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use 1H NMR on a single crystal of copper benzoate, at fields down to 0.2 T and temperatures down to 0.03 K, to identify three low-energy regimes of an S = 1/2 Heisenberg chain:
- spinons: a temperature-independent fast 1/T1 at 0.2 T;
- a disorder-induced random-singlet (RS) state: a slow relaxation channel with 1/T1s ∝ T, whose spectral weight w2 grows as -ln(T/T0) below 0.4 K;
- field-induced sine-Gordon solitons: gaps from the Knight shift and 1/T1 that scale as H^{2/3}.

Seeing all three in one material, and especially a microscopic NMR signature of an RS state produced by weak disorder, would interest a broad audience. The soliton part largely confirms earlier specific-heat, neutron, ESR and theory work on this compound. The new part, and the part that needs the most support, is the low-field claim about spinons coexisting with an RS state.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The measurement range is technically demanding (0.2 T, 30 mK, single crystal) and clearly goes beyond the earlier polycrystal NMR (Ref. 28).
- The two-component recovery at 0.2 T is visible in the raw M(t) curves (Fig. 3a), not only after the inverse Laplace analysis. Its weight changes smoothly and systematically with temperature (Fig. 3d).
- The gaps from the Knight shift agree reasonably with specific heat and INS (Fig. 4d). The tanTRG calculation (Fig. 4e) usefully ties the low-temperature Knight-shift upturn to the staggered magnetization.
- The paper combines experiment with QMC and tanTRG calculations, and the narrative across the H-T diagram is clear.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The spinon plateau at 0.2 T conflicts with the paper's own gap scaling, and 1/T1 depends strongly on field.**
   - *The conflict.* The fit in Fig. 4d (Δ ∝ H^{2/3}, about 0.05 meV at 1 T and about 0.1 meV at 2 T) extrapolates to Δ ≈ 0.02 meV ≈ 0.2–0.25 K at 0.2 T. A soliton gap that size should strongly suppress 1/T1 between 0.1 and 0.03 K. Yet the text calls 1/T1f "nearly constant between 1.4 K and 0.03 K" (Fig. 3b) and takes this as the hallmark of gapless spinons with η = 1. The same problem appears at 1 T: the Knight-shift gap is about 0.6 K, but 1/T1 at 1 T in Fig. 3b stays of order 10 s^-1 down to about 0.06 K rather than falling off exponentially.
   - *The low-temperature rise at 2 T.* At 2 T, 1/T1 at P5 (Fig. 4c) rises on cooling below about 1 K to about 20 s^-1 at the lowest temperatures. This is the opposite of gapped behaviour, even though the Knight shift at 2 T gives a finite gap (Fig. 4d). The text does not comment on it.
   - *The field dependence above the gap.* As read from Figs. 3b and 4c, 1/T1 at P5 near 1–2 K falls by roughly an order of magnitude between 0.2 T (about 60 s^-1), 1 T and 2 T. At these temperatures the system should be well above any soliton gap, where 1/T1 from the q = π spinon channel should depend only weakly on field.
   - *Why it matters.* Together these points suggest that the low-field, low-temperature 1/T1 is not set by the spinon (or soliton) continuum alone. Candidates include a q ≈ 0 diffusive contribution with a strong ω dependence, a different set of protons contributing to "P5" at different fields, or extrinsic relaxation.
   - *What to do.* Reconcile the 0.2 T and 1 T data with the H^{2/3} gap: show where that law should break down at low field, or show that the expected gap lies below the temperature window. Explain the low-temperature enhancement at 2 T. Explain the field dependence of 1/T1 at T well above Δ, for example with 1/T1 against field at a fixed temperature of about 1–2 K.

2. **The two-component recovery is not shown to come from spatially separate RS regions rather than overlapping proton sites, the uniform spin channel, or measurement artifacts.**
   - *Overlapping sites.* P5 is assigned to "either the H(3) site ... or protons in the benzoate groups" (p. 2). There are sixteen inequivalent protons, the field is slightly misaligned, and at 0.2 T the hyperfine and dipolar shifts are ten times smaller than at 2 T. So the line used for all the RS analysis at 0.2 T very likely contains several sites with different hyperfine form factors.
   - *The uniform channel.* Protons whose form factor cancels the staggered (q = π) channel see mainly the uniform (q = 0) contribution. In a clean Heisenberg chain that contribution gives 1/T1 ∝ T (Sachdev, Ref. 33), which is exactly the behaviour assigned here to the RS state. The apparent growth of w2 could then reflect temperature-dependent shifts moving lines into or out of the excitation window, or changes in nuclear spin diffusion between sites, rather than disorder.
   - *Missing data.* The spectra in Fig. 2b are at 2 T; no spectra at 0.2 T as a function of temperature are shown for the range where w2 changes.
   - *What to do.*
     - Show 0.2 T spectra against temperature together with the excitation bandwidth.
     - Repeat the recovery analysis on a well-resolved line with strong hyperfine coupling (P1–P4, for example at 0.5 T, where Supplementary Fig. S3 already has data). A spatial RS origin requires the same two-component structure, the same w2(T) and the same 1/T1s ∝ T there. A single-site origin would not give that.
     - Show w2(T) at 0.5 T alongside 0.2 T and compare the fitted T0 and a.
     - Confirm the w2(T) extraction with constrained two-exponential fits, not only the Tikhonov-regularized ILTA, which can broaden or split distributions. Give the uncertainties on w2 (Fig. 3d has no error bars).
     - Show that the nuclear and electronic temperature stays at the bath temperature at 30–100 mK under the pulse sequence, by varying rf power and repetition time.

3. **The link between w2 and a correlation length, and the claimed RS scaling, is not justified.**
   - *w2 is a nuclear fraction, not a length.* The central RS claim is that w2 "directly reflects the growth of the thermal correlation length ξ" and that ξ ∝ -ln T "signifies" the infinite-randomness fixed point (p. 4). But w2 is the fraction of nuclei in the slow channel. The paper gives no derivation of why it should be proportional to a correlation length.
   - *The scaling disagrees with the cited theory.* In the Fisher RS fixed point (Refs. 15–16), length and energy scale as L ~ ln²(Ω0/T), not ln(Ω0/T).
   - *The QMC support is not yet discriminating.* The QMC in Fig. 3e uses ΔJ/J = 0.2, and the main text does not say how this value was chosen. The definition of ξ is not given in the main text. There is no clean-chain reference: for a clean chain ξ also grows on cooling, as v/T, so over a decade in T/J a logarithmic fit alone does not single out RS physics.
   - *The mapping is only qualitative.* The data give T0 = 0.4 K, but the QMC is plotted against T/J with its own T0; the two are compared only in shape.
   - *What to do.*
     - Define the QMC ξ.
     - Show the clean (ΔJ = 0) QMC alongside, and show how ξ(T) depends on disorder strength.
     - Justify ΔJ/J = 0.2 independently, for example from the susceptibility Curie tail or a structural estimate of the disorder.
     - Either derive a quantitative relation between w2 and ξ or an RS-predicted distribution P(1/T1), or soften the claim to "consistent with" an RS state.
   - *Context.* The physical origin of the quenched disorder in copper benzoate (water content, vacancies, stacking) is not discussed. Measuring a second crystal with different disorder would be the most direct test that T0 and a track disorder.

B. Should address

4. **Gap extraction from the Knight shift uses an ad hoc form.**
   - *The functional form.* Kn = a e^{-Δ/kBT} + b (Fig. 4b) is fitted to a shift whose magnitude grows on cooling. The tanTRG attributes this growth to the staggered magnetization, not to activated uniform susceptibility. The paper does not explain why the staggered moment should approach saturation with a pure Arrhenius form, without the power-law prefactor expected from a dilute soliton gas.
   - *What is missing.* The fit ranges, the uncertainties on Δ and the sensitivity of Δ to the functional form are not given. Error bars are absent in Fig. 4d.
   - *The tanTRG comparison.* It is shown only at 5 T and in arbitrary units (Fig. 4e). The text calls it close reproduction of the data.
   - *What to do.* Overlay the calculated shift, with the hyperfine coupling stated, on the measured Kn(T) at several fields. Say which Hamiltonian parameters (staggered g tensor, DM vector) were fixed and from which source.

5. **The gap formula is ill-defined and the agreement is overstated.**
   - *The formula.* The text gives Δ = 1.85 J (h/J)^{2/3}. With h = gμBH and J ≈ 18 K this gives about 1.5 meV at 5 T, roughly eight times the plotted values. So h must be the staggered field h_s = cH, but neither c nor the logarithmic correction of Oshikawa–Affleck (Ref. 42) is stated.
   - *Is it a fit?* The caption calls the curve "a fit"; the text calls it "the theoretical form". Say which.
   - *"Excellent agreement".* The INS points (Ref. 29) lie systematically below the curve at 7–9 T, so "excellent agreement with all experimental data" overstates the match.

6. **Field misalignment is not quantified.**
   - The paper notes that the field is "slightly off" the b axis, because five lines are resolved where four are expected for H ∥ b.
   - In copper benzoate the staggered-field ratio c depends strongly on field direction (Refs. 22, 23, 30). Misalignment therefore changes the gap, the Knight-shift upturn and the low-field 1/T1.
   - Give the misalignment angle and its uncertainty, for example from the line positions and a dipolar calculation, and propagate it into the expected c.

7. **All data come from one crystal.**
   - The paper gives no evidence on reproducibility (a second crystal, or thermal cycling). This matters most for the RS claim, whose parameters T0 and a are by construction sample-specific.
   - At minimum, state how many cool-downs the 0.2 T data span. If possible, show w2(T) for a second crystal.

8. **The RS in a finite field and the thermal-conductivity link are only qualitative.**
   - The Discussion argues that an RS-like gapless response survives at 0.2 T because the Zeeman energy (about 0.27 K) closes gaps of weak singlets. But that energy is comparable to the 0.4 K onset and to the extrapolated soliton gap. The staggered field also acts on unpaired spins.
   - Likewise, the claim that the RS state "readily accounts" for the drop in thermal conductivity below 0.3 K (Ref. 27) is not quantified, for example by comparison with the T² prediction of Ref. 41.
   - Estimate the fraction of singlets polarized at 0.2 and 0.5 T for the inferred disorder strength, and compare with the w2 values at the two fields.

C. Minor

9. **Fig. 1 is drawn without data points.** The boundaries are schematic. The RS region is drawn up to about 3 T at low temperature, but the RS analysis is shown only at 0.2 T (and 0.5 T in the SI). Overlay the measured (H, T) points and the crossover criteria.
10. **The 0.2 T data are described inconsistently.** The text calls 1/T1f "nearly constant" from 1.4 K to 0.03 K, and also describes a downturn below 0.2–0.3 K at 0.2 T (p. 3). State the range of constancy consistently and give its uncertainty.
11. **Fig. 4c caption.** It says the fits are "based on a gapped excitation spectrum", while the text uses a simple Arrhenius form. Specify the form, and say why no fit is shown at 2 T.
12. **Small presentation points.**
    - Write the logarithm as ξ ∝ ln(T0/T) rather than "-ln T".
    - State that a = 25 refers to w2 in percent.
    - The colour-scale units of P(1/T1) in Fig. 3c are unclear.
    - Spectra are plotted as "Intensity*T" without explanation.
13. **Data availability.** Data are available only "upon request". A public deposition of the M(t) curves would let readers check the ILTA analysis independently.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a resolved-site control: the two-component recovery and w2(T) on a strongly coupled line (P1–P4) at 0.5 T, and 0.2 T spectra against temperature. This would settle whether the slow channel is a spatial (disorder) effect or a site and form-factor effect.
2. Present 1/T1 against field at fixed temperatures (for example 1.5 K and 0.1 K) from 0.2 to 10 T on the same line. This would connect the spinon, RS and soliton regimes quantitatively and test the H^{2/3} gap where it matters most, at low field.
3. Strengthen the theory side. Give the clean-chain QMC reference, define ξ, justify the disorder strength, and compare the measured P(1/T1) or average 1/T1 with RS-phase predictions for NMR relaxation rather than with the correlation length alone.
4. Characterize the disorder independently, for example with the low-temperature susceptibility Curie tail or impurity concentration, ideally on a second crystal.

5. Verdict: major revision
