Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports 1H NMR on a single crystal of copper benzoate, a nearly ideal S = 1/2 Heisenberg chain, at fields down to 0.2 T and temperatures down to 0.03 K. It claims three regimes. Spinons show up as a temperature-independent 1/T1 and 1/T2G ∝ T^-1/2. Field-induced soliton gaps follow Δ ∝ H^α with α ≈ 0.8(1), compared with 2/3, and agree with specific heat and INS. A random-singlet-like (RS-like) state below about 0.4 K is identified from a second, slow T1 component: 1/T1s ∝ T, with a spectral weight w2 that grows as −ln(T/T0), plus an upturn in the Knight shift. Separating intrinsic chain dynamics from a weak-disorder channel by local probe at these temperatures is new and interesting. The spinon and soliton parts largely confirm known physics, so the novelty rests mainly on the RS-like assignment.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The measurement range is technically demanding: 0.03 K and 0.2 T on a chain with J ≈ 18 K. It reaches T/J ≈ 0.002, which few chain materials allow.
- The spectra are carefully assigned to sites, and the calculated hyperfine coupling (−0.101 T/μB) agrees with experiment (−0.118 T/μB).
- The two-component relaxation is shown directly in the raw M(t) curves (Fig. 3a), and the ILTA map (Fig. 3c) makes the separation visible.
- The paper reports heating checks: randomised recovery times, ±6 dB pulse-power changes, recycle-delay changes and thermometer stability.
- The soliton gaps from NMR agree quantitatively with independent specific-heat and INS data (Fig. 4e). The LTRG calculations reproduce the field-induced uniform magnetisation from the DM-induced canting (Fig. 4c).
- The paper offers a plausible microscopic link to the reported drop in thermal conductivity below 0.3 K.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The paper hedges its central new claim as "RS-like". The evidence is internally consistent and a reasonable referee would accept it as qualified. The points below would sharpen or narrow the claim; none is required for it to stand.

B. Should address

1. Alternatives to the RS interpretation of the slow component are not excluded quantitatively (pp. 4–6, Fig. 3c–e).
   - The RS-like assignment rests on three observations: 1/T1s ∝ T, w2 ∝ −ln(T/T0) over about one decade (0.03–0.4 K), and the Kn upturn. Each could also come from simpler sources of weak disorder:
     - protons near chain breaks or chain ends, where staggered moments (orphan spins) form;
     - dilute paramagnetic impurities, which give a Curie-like shift and a relaxation channel that grows on cooling;
     - a continuous distribution of local couplings that is not an RS fixed point.
   - The Discussion argues against a "locally disordered state" in one sentence (p. 6). It needs a quantitative comparison.
   - Fit the same data with these alternatives: Curie or Curie–Weiss for Kn, a power law T^−α for w2, and an impurity-relaxation model for 1/T1s. Report goodness of fit.
   - State which feature, if any, is unique to the RS state. Candidates are the ln^−2 correction in Kn and the specific T-linear 1/T1s with its prefactor.

2. The Knight-shift fit window is narrow, and the lowest-temperature data are set aside (Fig. 3e, p. 5).
   - The form K ∝ T^−1 ln^−2(Ω/T), with Ω ≈ 10 K, is fitted roughly between 0.2 and 0.8 K. There ln(Ω/T) varies only from about 2.5 to 4, so the form cannot be told apart from a Curie law or T^−α with α < 1.
   - The authors attribute the downturn below 0.2 K to line broadening and spectrometer bandwidth. That is the regime where RS behaviour should be most evident.
   - The abstract nevertheless speaks of a "divergence" of Kn.
   - Show the full line shape at 0.03–0.2 K with the bandwidth limit marked. Better, measure with stepped-frequency sweeps covering the low-frequency tail, and compare Kn from the first moment with Kn from the peak.
   - Compare Ω with an independent estimate, or treat it explicitly as a free phenomenological parameter.

3. The link between w2(T) and the QMC correlation length is heuristic, and the disorder model does not match the proposed disorder picture (p. 4, inset of Fig. 3d; Methods; Discussion, p. 5).
   - The QMC uses a bimodal distribution in which 10% of bonds are modified by Δ/J = 0.03, spread uniformly along the chain.
   - The Discussion instead proposes domain-type disorder: "dirtier regions isolated by vast cleaner ones".
   - The paper maps a calculated correlation length ξ onto a measured volume fraction w2 only because both scale as −ln T. That is a qualitative analogy, not a test.
   - The quoted disorder strength Δ/J ≈ 0.02–0.05 is model-dependent.
   - Show QMC for at least two more disorder strengths and fractions, to establish whether the crossover temperature T0 scales with Δ/J as the model implies.
   - Calculate a quantity closer to what NMR measures, such as the distribution of local susceptibilities or local 1/T1. A clustered-disorder model matching the proposed picture would make the comparison stronger.
   - With open boundary conditions, the extraction of ξ in Eq. (3) uses S(π + δq), which assumes periodic momenta. Confirm that edge spins do not affect ξ at the lowest temperatures.

4. The extracted w2 and its error bars may depend on the inversion settings (Fig. 3c, d; Methods).
   - The Tikhonov inversion is ill-posed. w2 and the logarithmic fit (T0 = 0.40(2) K, c = 25(1)) are taken from double-Lorentzian fits to P(1/T1) at a single regularisation parameter, α = 0.001.
   - Show w2(T) for α varied over at least an order of magnitude either side of 0.001.
   - Overlay w2 from direct biexponential fits of M(t) (the a_f and a_s weights).
   - Report the error bars from both methods. The quoted precision on c and T0 should reflect this systematic spread.

5. The 0.2 T data may not be in the zero-field limit at the lowest temperatures (Fig. 3b, p. 2; Discussion, p. 6).
   - At 0.2 T the Zeeman energy gμBH is about 0.27 K. Extrapolating Δ ∝ H^{2/3} or H^0.8 from Fig. 4e also gives a soliton gap of order 0.1 K.
   - Both scales fall within the window where the spinon-only constant 1/T1 and the onset of RS-like behaviour are claimed.
   - The text acknowledges a "weak downturn below 0.3 K" in 1/T1f at 0.2 T, yet still states that 1/T1 is constant from 1.4 to 0.03 K.
   - The missing ln^{1/2} correction is attributed to solitons. That would also mean the fast channel at T < 0.3 K is not purely spinon-like.
   - Quantify the expected soliton gap at 0.2 T for the actual field orientation.
   - State over which interval 1/T1f is truly constant within error.
   - Discuss whether the RS-like slow channel and its T-linear form could be affected by the same field-induced gap.

6. Field misalignment from the b-axis is not quantified (p. 2, Fig. 2a; Fig. 4e).
   - Five proton peaks are resolved instead of four, which the authors read as a slight misalignment. In copper benzoate, however, the staggered-field ratio h/H depends strongly on field direction (Dender et al., Oshikawa–Affleck), so the gap amplitude does too.
   - Estimate the misalignment angle from the spectra, for example by simulating line positions with the known hyperfine tensors.
   - Propagate that angle into the effective staggered field and the theoretical curve in Fig. 4e.
   - This also bears on concern 7.

7. The gap exponent is presented as more consistent with theory than the data support (p. 5, Fig. 4e).
   - The fitted α = 0.8(1) is described as being in "good consistency" with 2/3.
   - The Oshikawa–Affleck result includes a logarithmic factor, Δ ∝ h^{2/3} |ln h|^{1/6}. The staggered field also enters through a direction-dependent coefficient.
   - Fit with the exponent fixed at 2/3, with and without the log correction, and compare residuals.
   - Make clear which Δ values come from Kn, which from 1/T1, and how the free constant b in the Kn fit affects Δ. With only a few points below the gap scale at each field, that parameter correlation may be large.

8. The key result comes from one NMR crystal (Methods).
   - Three crystals were used, one for each technique. The RS-like channel is attributed to unintentional, possibly growth-dependent disorder ("stress or unbalanced benzoate group rotation freezing").
   - It is therefore important to show that T0, c and the T-linear 1/T1s reproduce on a second crystal, ideally one from a different growth or with a different cooling history.
   - A sample with deliberately increased disorder, or one annealed or quenched differently, would be a strong control for the disorder origin. At minimum, state whether the slow component survives thermal cycling.

9. Spatial separation of the two T1 channels is not discussed against proton spin diffusion (p. 3).
   - The proton density in this hydrated organic crystal is high, so proton–proton spin diffusion tends to average T1 between nearby regions.
   - Two components whose rates differ by about 100 times, with w2 near 65%, imply the slow and fast regions are large compared with the spin-diffusion length over T1, or are decoupled by a shift gradient.
   - Estimate the spin-diffusion length for the P5 protons and give the minimum domain size it implies. This would also test the domain-type disorder picture.

C. Minor

10. Extrapolating w2 = −c ln(T/T0) with c = 25 gives w2 = 100% at T ≈ 7 mK, below which it would exceed unity. Do not describe w2 as "growing to 100% in the zero-temperature limit" (p. 6) as if it followed from the fit. State that the logarithmic form is valid only over the measured window.

11. Fig. 1 is a schematic phase diagram with no data points. Plot the measured crossover points, such as T0 and the temperatures where the gap opens at each field, so readers can see which boundaries are data-based.

12. The 1/T2G ∝ T^−1/2 behaviour is fitted only from about 0.4 to 2 K, less than a decade (inset of Fig. 3b). Give the fit range and compare with the expected logarithmic corrections.

13. Peak P5 is assigned to "either" H(3) or benzoate protons. Supplementary Note 5 argues that the two-component relaxation is not from spectral overlap. Summarise in the main text why overlapping sites with different hyperfine couplings cannot produce the slow component, given that it appears only below 0.4 K.

14. The suppression of thermal conductivity is given as a consequence of the RS-like state ("readily accounts for", p. 6). This is a plausible but untested link and should be worded as a hypothesis.

15. The Discussion's comparison of the Knight-shift "nearly doubling" and the "threefold increase of the FWHM" should point to the figure panels where these numbers can be checked.

16. The Fig. 4e caption writes Δ ~ J(h/J)^{0.8(1)}, while the text writes Δ ∝ H^α. Make the notation consistent and state the conversion from H to h.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a short subsection or table that tests the RS scenario against the impurity, orphan-spin and Curie alternatives using the same Kn, w2 and 1/T1s data.
2. Repeat the 0.2 T T1 measurement on a second crystal, ideally including one with deliberately changed disorder, to show how T0 and c depend on the sample.
3. Extend the QMC to several disorder strengths and to a clustered-disorder model, and calculate local susceptibility or local 1/T1 distributions that can be compared directly with P(1/T1).
4. Measure the field misalignment and include it in the staggered-field analysis of the soliton gap.

5. Verdict

Verdict: minor revision
