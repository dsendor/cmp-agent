Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports time- and angle-resolved XUV photoemission (momentum microscope) measurements on bulk SnSe in which the IR pump polarization is set along the zigzag (ZZ) or armchair (AC) axis by rotating the crystal. ZZ pumping populates only the X conduction valleys, which hold the global conduction-band minimum (CBM), and the resulting valley polarization stays nearly constant. AC pumping first populates the Y valleys, and the polarization then decays and reverses within a few hundred femtoseconds. Time-dependent Boltzmann equation (TDBE) simulations built on DFT/DFPT/EPW reproduce this and attribute Y→X transfer to a ~20 meV optical phonon branch. To my knowledge this is the first momentum-resolved measurement of valley-selective excitation and intervalley relaxation in a group-IV monochalcogenide, and it adds a direct look in momentum space to earlier static linear-dichroism and photoluminescence work. Whether the result is new as physics, beyond being a first observation, depends on how it is framed. That a hot population relaxes from a higher valley into the global minimum in an indirect-gap multivalley semiconductor is well known (for example Γ→L/X scattering in GaAs and Ge). The paper needs to show what is specific to SnSe.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clean, visually convincing contrast between ZZ and AC pumping in full momentum maps (Fig. 2a–d), obtained in the same apparatus at the same fluence. The design is sound: the crystal is rotated rather than the pump switched from s to p polarization, which keeps the pump geometry the same.
- A first-principles TDBE model with no parameters fitted to the photoemission data. It reproduces the early-time valley-resolved dynamics and the timescale of the polarization reversal (Fig. 2f,h).
- A mode-resolved analysis of the phonon population (Fig. 3) plus a symmetry and orbital analysis (SI S4). Together these give a concrete, falsifiable proposal for which phonon carries Y→X scattering.
- The SI checks how sensitive the initial selection rules and the subsequent dynamics are to pump detuning (Figs. S1, S2).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **Laser-assisted photoemission (LAPE) and the coherent pump–probe overlap could contaminate the early-time Y-valley "conduction-band" signal under AC pumping.** This signal is what establishes the claimed initial positive valley polarization and its reversal (Fig. 2b, f, h). Several features of the data make this a real possibility:
   - The valence-band maximum (VBM) is at the Y valleys. With a 1.2 eV pump, the first LAPE sideband of the VBM falls at E − E_VBM ≈ 1.2 eV, inside the conduction-band integration window (1.15 ± 0.18 eV, Fig. 1a), at the Y-valley momentum.
   - For an s-polarized (in-plane) pump field, the LAPE amplitude scales roughly with k∥·A. In the AC configuration the Y valleys lie along the pump polarization, so the sideband is largest exactly there. In the ZZ configuration Y is perpendicular to A, so a sideband would be suppressed. This would reproduce the observed ZZ/AC asymmetry of the Y signal without any real Y population.
   - The experimental Y trace in Fig. 2f peaks at ~0 fs and falls steeply over a time comparable to the pump–probe cross-correlation. The "early" momentum map (Fig. 2b) integrates from −125 to +125 fs, which is centred on the overlap.

   The authors state (p. 6) that the s-polarized geometry avoids "strong laser-assisted photoemission", but they show no evidence that sidebands are negligible at 0.9 mJ/cm². This bears directly on the headline claim of an optically prepared, nearly unit Y polarization that then reverses. The authors should:
   - show energy–momentum cuts (E vs k_AC through Y) at and away from zero delay, so that a VB replica shifted by 1.2 eV can be identified or ruled out;
   - give the measured cross-correlation, for example from a LAPE signal at a momentum or energy with no conduction-band states, and explain how time zero was set;
   - show that the Y signal's rise, peak and decay differ from the cross-correlation, or subtract the sideband;
   - ideally, show the fluence dependence of the early Y signal. Real population should be close to linear in fluence; LAPE scales with intensity but has a characteristic temporal shape.

2. **The conversion from valley intensities to a valley polarization is not specified or controlled, yet the quantitative claims rest on it.** These claims are "nearly unity", the reversal to ≈ −0.7, and "excellent agreement" with TDBE (Fig. 2g,h).
   - Photoemission intensity is population × matrix element. Matrix elements differ between the X and Y valleys (different orbital character, as the paper itself stresses on p. 14 and in Fig. 3d). They also change when the crystal is rotated by 90° relative to the fixed XUV polarization and incidence plane.
   - Fig. 2a,b already show strong intensity asymmetries between equivalent valleys, both ±k and inner (~±0.5 Å⁻¹) versus outer (~±1 Å⁻¹) spots.
   - The Methods do not say which momentum and energy regions define "X" and "Y", whether first-zone or higher-zone replicas are used, or how background is subtracted.
   - The caption of Fig. 2e,f says "Norm. Int." without saying whether each curve is normalized separately. If it is, the difference ratio in Fig. 2g,h has no meaning as a population ratio.
   - The polarization formula and its sign convention are not given. Fig. 2g sits at −1 for X-only population, so P appears to be (Y−X)/(Y+X), whereas the caption says "difference between X and Y".

   The authors should:
   - state the integration regions and overlay the Brillouin zone on Fig. 2a–d;
   - give the exact formula and normalization;
   - discuss or estimate the relative X/Y cross-sections, for example from late-delay or thermally populated data in both orientations, or from summing all equivalent valleys in several zones;
   - show how the extracted crossing time and asymptotic polarization depend on these choices.

   Without this, the claim that the TDBE agrees quantitatively is not supported. Only the qualitative trend is.

B. Should address

3. **The later-time X-valley dynamics under AC pumping contradict the model, and the explanation offered is implausible.**
   - In the experiment (Fig. 2f) the X population peaks near 150–200 fs and then decreases. Under ZZ pumping (Fig. 2e) the X population stays near its maximum out to 600 fs.
   - The TDBE instead predicts that X keeps rising to 600 fs. The text attributes the difference to neglected electron–hole recombination (p. 10). SnSe has an indirect gap, so recombination on a sub-picosecond timescale is hard to reconcile with this. Recombination would also affect the ZZ case, where no decay is seen.
   - More plausible causes are specific to the measurement or the excitation density: carriers diffusing out of the probed surface region, electrons cooling below a fixed energy window, transfer to other valleys or bands, or hot-carrier and e–e effects at high density.

   This matters because the agreement in Fig. 2h partly comes from both the numerator and the denominator of P being off. The authors should explain why AC and ZZ differ here, test the energy window (by showing energy-resolved X-valley traces), and moderate the statement that the behaviour is "fully captured".

4. **The phonon mechanism is a theoretical result with no direct experimental test, and its identification is stated more strongly than the analysis allows.**
   - Fig. 3 reads mode temperatures along Γ–M only, at a single time (600 fs). The Y→X momentum transfer q = k_X − k_Y lies along Γ–M only if the valley positions scale with the reciprocal lattice constants. With a₁ ≠ a₂ and valleys away from the zone boundary, this is not guaranteed.
   - Intervalley phonons at other q are not shown.
   - The symmetry argument (p. 14, SI S4) allows every glide-even branch. The SI says so, but the main text implies it singles out the second-highest branch.

   The authors should:
   - compute mode-resolved Y→X scattering rates, or a mode-decomposed transfer rate integrated over all q connecting the valleys;
   - repeat the TDBE with the coupling of the ~20 meV branch switched off or scaled down, to show that the depolarization time depends on it;
   - replace "unambiguously identify" with wording that matches this evidence.

   Fluence-dependent data, or temperature-dependent data that changes phonon occupation, would be the natural experimental check.

5. **The excitation conditions in theory and experiment do not match, and the excitation density is not reported.**
   - The experiment uses 1.2 eV photons at 0.9 mJ/cm². The TDBE uses 0.8 eV photons resonant with the PBE gap and the "same intensity" (p. 5, Methods). Neither the experimental nor the simulated absorbed carrier density is given.
   - At this fluence the density is probably high (likely ≳10²⁰ cm⁻³). At such densities, carrier–carrier scattering, screening of the e–ph coupling (including the polar Fröhlich coupling the authors link to SnSe, ref. 50), and band renormalization may all matter, and the TDBE leaves them out.
   - Fig. S2 shows that the intermediate AC dynamics are "significantly more sensitive" to detuning, yet the experimental detuning above the true direct gaps at X and Y is not stated. It could be several hundred meV, possibly beyond the ≤100 meV range simulated in Fig. S2. Fig. S1c suggests the initial ZZ polarization drops noticeably above ~1.1 eV in DFT energy.

   The authors should:
   - give the excess energy relative to the experimental direct gaps (from their own data or the literature);
   - extend Fig. S2 to the experimental detuning, or rescale the gap (scissor) so that the simulated excess energy matches;
   - report the estimated excitation density;
   - ideally, show at least one lower fluence. That would also address possible average or peak heating at 166 kHz.

6. **The framing of novelty and of "nonvolatile" valley polarization goes beyond what the data show.**
   - The ZZ polarization stays constant because the X valleys are the global CBM. Any electron population thermalizes there, so the stability is energetic relaxation, not a protected valley degree of freedom.
   - The AC "reversal" is the same relaxation seen from the higher valley.
   - Calling this a coexistence of "nonvolatile" and "volatile" channels and "an operating principle fundamentally distinct from all existing valleytronic platforms" (pp. 4, 11, 14) overstates the case. The comparison with symmetric K/K′ depolarization in WS₂ (p. 11) compares degenerate with non-degenerate valleys, where different questions are being asked.

   The authors should:
   - place the result alongside established intervalley scattering in multivalley semiconductors (GaAs Γ–L, Ge, Si);
   - say what is new (momentum-resolved, polarization-selected access in an anisotropic layered crystal, and the phonon assignment);
   - define what "nonvolatile" means on the ~ps scale studied. It presumably ends with recombination or valley-to-valley thermal equilibrium, and the paper does not measure that.

7. **Reproducibility and the measured range.**
   - The paper does not say how many samples or cleaves were measured.
   - It does not say whether the ZZ/AC contrast was reproduced after re-orienting the same cleave or on a second sample, or whether the probe spot moves when the hexapod rotates the sample.
   - The traces in Fig. 2e–h have no error bars or statistics.
   - Data are shown only at room temperature, at one fluence and out to only +600 fs.

   Because the effect is defined by comparing two separate orientations, the authors should show that it holds on at least two cleaves or regions, give uncertainty estimates for P(t), and, if available, extend the delay range far enough to see where the X-valley population and the polarization settle.

8. **DFT setup and momentum positions.**
   - Methods give PBE without stating whether spin–orbit coupling or van der Waals corrections are included. The X–Y conduction-band energy offset and the curvature of each valley control the Y→X rate and any thermal back-transfer.
   - The calculated valley positions in Fig. 1c (~±0.35–0.4 Å⁻¹) differ visibly from the experimental spot positions in Fig. 2a–d (~±0.45–0.5 and ~±1.0–1.1 Å⁻¹).

   The authors should:
   - compare the DFT X–Y conduction-band offset and valley positions with their own measured values;
   - state the spin–orbit setting and justify it;
   - check that the conclusions hold for a plausible error in the offset.

   The 20 fs dephasing time is phenomenological. So the statement that pump polarization is "the only adjustable parameter" (p. 5) should be qualified, and the sensitivity to this time should be shown.

C. Minor

9. Text and figure mismatches:
   - p. 10 says the X-valley intensity is the "blue curve"; it is red.
   - p. 10 says "as shown in Fig. 2(f), the TDBE-predicted valley depolarization dynamics…"; this should be Fig. 2(h).
   - SI p. 26 says "nearly unity and time-dependent hole valley polarization" under AC pumping; Fig. S3f shows it time-independent.
   - In Fig. 3, the caption assigns "(a)–(c)" to ZZ and AC "respectively" and then defines (c) separately; the caption should be reworded.

10. Fig. 1a reports conduction-band contours at "1.15 ± 0.18 eV" without saying whether this is the integration window used for all of Fig. 2. Please state the energy windows used in Fig. 2a–h.

11. The valley labels "X" and "Y" refer to valleys along Γ–X and Γ–Y, not to the high-symmetry points themselves (Fig. 1c places the label "X" near the zone edge). Please define the nomenclature clearly to avoid confusion with the X and Y points.

12. Hole dynamics (SI S3) are presented only from theory. The VBM data are already acquired (Fig. 1a). Even a qualitative experimental check of X-valley hole depletion under ZZ pumping would strengthen the S3 discussion; otherwise its theoretical status should be stated in the main text (p. 11).

13. The Data Availability statement ends with "Zenodo ." and gives no identifier, and ref. 48 is a placeholder ("[URL]").

14. Bulk SnSe from commercial sources is typically p-doped by Sn vacancies. Please state the doping or Fermi-level position, and whether the equilibrium hole density affects the hole dynamics discussed in the SI.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a figure (main text or SI) with energy–momentum cuts through the X and Y valleys for both orientations at several delays. This would show the conduction-band signals, any LAPE replicas and the energy windows directly, and would resolve concerns 1, 2 and 10 together.
- A continuous scan of the pump polarization angle (or crystal azimuth) between ZZ and AC, showing the initial polarization following the expected cos²/sin² dependence, would show the selection rule directly rather than from two points.
- A fluence series, and if possible a lower sample temperature, would test both the excitation-density assumptions of the TDBE and the phonon-occupation dependence expected from the proposed mechanism.
- A short TDBE "knock-out" calculation, with the ~20 meV branch's intervalley coupling switched off, would turn the phonon assignment from correlational to causal within the model.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
