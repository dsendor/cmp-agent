Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports reflectance spectroscopy of an hBN-encapsulated, graphite-gated WSe2 monolayer at 5 K and zero field. It shows three things. First, an exciton umklapp resonance above the repulsive polaron (RP) that disappears above n_c ≈ 7×10^11 cm^-2 (r_s ≈ 12). Second, two new resonances above the triplet and singlet attractive polarons, which the authors call Wigner polarons (WP_T, WP_S): attractive polarons dressed by Wigner-crystal phonons. Third, valley-selective enhancement of these features under circular pumping, and suppression of the umklapp feature (but not the WPs) under pulsed broadband excitation, read as optical melting. If the WP assignment holds, these would be the first optical resonances that report the internal phonon dynamics of a zero-field Wigner crystal rather than only its static order. That is a real conceptual advance over umklapp-based detection. Extending umklapp detection from MoSe2 to WSe2, with a much smaller melting r_s, is useful in its own right.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clean, high-quality data set. The umklapp splitting ΔE_U (Fig. 2b) is linear in n and gives an exciton mass of about 0.85 m0, consistent with independent magneto-optical values. I checked that ΔE_U = h²n/(√3 m_X) reproduces the plotted magnitudes (about 14 meV at 7×10^11 cm^-2). This supports the density calibration and the umklapp assignment.
- The WP features are new. Their one-to-one pairing with T⁻ and S⁻ is shown directly by circular-polarization selectivity (Fig. 3b–d): WP_T follows T⁻ under co-polarization and WP_S follows S⁻ under cross-polarization, while U_RP is unaffected. This is a non-trivial check of the scattering picture.
- The WP–AP splitting has a clear physical meaning (the Wigner-crystal phonon scale). It fits a theory-motivated n^{3/4} law, and the fitted coefficient is within a factor of 1.5 of a parameter-free estimate.
- The contrasting responses of the umklapp feature (static order) and the WPs (local dynamics) to optical excitation are intriguing and open a direction for optical control of correlated phases.
- The authors state openly the limits of the n^{3/4} fit (Fig. S5) and of the melting mechanism.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The WP resonances have not been shown to be specific to the Wigner crystal. The central claim (title, abstract, Fig. 2c,d) is that WP_T and WP_S are attractive polarons dressed by Wigner-crystal phonons, so they "probe the dynamics of a Wigner crystal". Three observations in the paper leave this open.
   (i) In Fig. 2d the dashed WP lines and the visible WP features reach top-gate voltages of about 1.0 V (WP_T) and about 1.3 V (WP_S). On the top density axis of the same panel these correspond to roughly 9–11×10^11 cm^-2, clearly above n_c ≈ 7×10^11 cm^-2, where the umklapp feature has disappeared. The same appears in Fig. 1c. The text explains a similar persistence under optical pumping by the WPs being "more sensitive to local short-range correlations", but it does not discuss the density case. If the WPs survive into the phase the umklapp criterion calls a liquid, then either the WPs are not WC-specific (they could be sidebands from a strongly correlated liquid's collective modes or short-range order), or the umklapp disappearance does not mark melting. Either way the paper's framing has to change.
   (ii) The main text shows no temperature dependence of the WP features. Methods describe a thermal-melting setup (ATC100), and Figs. S8–S9 are cited only for the 1s-exciton redshift. The obvious test is to track the WP–AP splitting, oscillator strength and linewidth together with the umklapp feature across the thermal melting temperature, at several densities below n_c. If the WPs vanish together with U_RP, that would strongly support the assignment. If they persist, the "phonon of the crystal" interpretation has to be qualified.
   (iii) The density dependence does not discriminate between models. The authors concede a linear fit also works (Fig. S5), and the n^{3/4} fit has one free parameter that comes out 1.5 times the model value.
   The authors should do three things. Show the WP features against temperature (and, if available, in a second device). Mark n_c explicitly in Fig. 2d and Fig. 1c, and state where the WPs disappear relative to it. Explain in the main text why the WPs are expected only in the crystal and not in a correlated liquid with the same r_s, using the theory of Ref. 34 and Supplementary Discussion 1. They should also rule out explicitly that the WPs are umklapp replicas of the attractive polarons (trion umklapp). The numbers already argue against this: a trion-mass umklapp should have a splitting about m_X/m_T ≈ 2/3 of ΔE_U, whereas the fitted ΔE_WP (about 16 meV at 6×10^11 cm^-2) exceeds ΔE_U (about 12 meV). This argument belongs in the paper.

B. Should address

2. The criterion used for optical melting may confuse broadening or signal loss with melting. Fig. 4c defines the "umklapp critical density" as the density where dR_C/dV crosses a fixed threshold (−0.005 to −0.0065), applied at every pump power. Excitation also broadens resonances and changes the overall reflectance contrast, and at the highest power the WPs broaden (Fig. S11). A fixed absolute threshold will therefore report a lower critical density even if the crystal is intact but its umklapp line is broadened or weakened. Pump and probe are the same filtered supercontinuum beam, so the "pump power" also changes the probe conditions. The authors should normalize the umklapp weight (for example to the RP oscillator strength at the same density and power), report umklapp linewidth and ΔE_U against power, and show that the collapse of n_c from about 6.7 to about 0.8×10^11 cm^-2 does not depend on the threshold choice beyond the error bars already shown.

3. Effective electronic temperature under pumping is unknown, and the lattice thermometer is weak. The argument against heating (Supplementary Discussion 2, Figs. S8–S10) is that a 1s-exciton shift of about 2 meV would be expected if the lattice reached the melting temperature. The 1s bandgap shift is very flat at low temperature, so this argument depends on what the thermal melting temperature is. That temperature is not given in the main text, and it should be. The authors themselves note that the electron temperature may exceed the lattice temperature, and this is exactly the quantity that matters for melting an electron crystal. A thermometer closer to the electrons would make the case much stronger: for example, compare the power dependence of the umklapp intensity with its temperature dependence at the same density, or use the RP/AP linewidths. Without it, "optical melting by exciton-mediated softening" and "hot-electron melting" cannot be told apart, and the abstract's framing should be tempered.

4. The exciton-density estimate behind Fig. 4c and the mechanism discussion is loosely specified. Methods use n_X = PαΓ/(Aħω), with absorbance "10% for example", an unspecified beam area A and lifetimes Γ that are not stated. Peak power is computed as P_avg/(f_R t_p) for a 200 ps pulse, yet steady-state rate equations are used. Whether a 200 ps pulse is treated as long or short compared with the lifetimes should be stated. Spectral filtering to 650–800 nm also reaches about 1.91 eV and covers the 2s/3s resonances, so the statement that free carriers are not generated needs justification. The text quotes a dark-exciton density of "approximately 10^11 cm^-2" near threshold, while the top axis of Fig. 4c reaches about 6×10^11 cm^-2, comparable to n_c. Please give the spot size, absorbance and lifetimes with their sources, propagate the uncertainties, and reconcile the text and the figure axis. These numbers carry the argument that bright excitons alone cannot melt the crystal.

5. The spin-control experiment needs a liquid-phase control and a quantified polarization. In Fig. 3 the co- and cross-polarized contrast of T⁻/S⁻ follows from pump-induced valley polarization, which is known to occur in doped WSe2 at any density (Ref. 49). The WC-specific point is that the WP_T/WP_S selectivity tracks T⁻/S⁻ while U_RP does not change. To support "all-optical control of spins in the Wigner crystal", the authors should do three things. Estimate the degree of electron valley polarization, for example from the T⁻/S⁻ weight ratio. Show the same measurement above n_c for comparison. Clarify whether the CW 635 nm pump at 4 µW, which is above the free-carrier gap, heats the sample or changes density. This matters because 4000 nW of pulsed excitation (Fig. 4b) is reported to almost erase the umklapp feature, while 4 µW CW in Fig. 3 leaves it intact. The difference presumably comes from peak power or wavelength, and it should be explained explicitly.

6. The low melting r_s ≈ 12 needs an uncertainty and a consistent dielectric constant. r_s is computed with ε = 4.5, while the density calibration uses ε_hBN = 3.5 (and mentions 3.1). Please explain why the two differ, propagate the uncertainties in density, effective mass (0.4 m0) and ε into an error bar on r_s, and say how n_c is defined (the density where the umklapp signal falls below a threshold, or an extrapolation of its intensity to zero). The umklapp feature in a correlated liquid could also persist from short-range order (a peak in the structure factor near k_W), which would bias the melting estimate in either direction. Since the paper reports an r_s about three times below the clean-limit theory and attributes the difference to disorder or Berry curvature, these numbers deserve a careful treatment.

7. Reproducibility across devices is shown only for the umklapp feature. Fig. S1 covers multiple devices and spots, but the paper does not say whether the WP features, the spin selectivity and the optical-melting trend were seen in more than one device ("Device 1" appears in Methods). Please state how many devices show WPs, and give n_c and the fitted coefficient a for each.

8. The theoretical basis is not available for evaluation. The WP interpretation, the claimed one-to-one AP–WP correspondence and the spin insensitivity of the RP/umklapp all rest on Ref. 34, which is cited as an arXiv preprint without an identifier. The model behind the "1.5 times" comparison of a with theory should be summarized in the main text or Methods: which phonon branch, which coupling, and which parameters are fixed. The fitted a should also be compared with the Wigner-crystal transverse/longitudinal Debye energies computed from the same m and ε used for r_s.

C. Minor

9. The exciton mass is given as m_X ~ (0.85 ± 0.8) m0. This is presumably ±0.08, which matches the stated 10% density uncertainty. Please correct it.

10. Fig. 2b plots ΔE_U up to about 8×10^11 cm^-2, beyond the stated n_c ≈ 7×10^11 cm^-2 above which the umklapp feature is "indiscernible", and the points above 7×10^11 scatter widely. Either restrict the fit range and say so, or reconcile this with the definition of n_c.

11. The word "ultrafast" (abstract and conclusion) is not supported. All measurements are time-integrated with 200 ps pulses at 40 MHz. Either remove it or present it explicitly as an outlook.

12. Methods: the power-law colormap (p = 0.6) is a reasonable choice for showing weak features, but the colorbars in Figs. 1c, 2d, 3b,c and 4a,b should state that the scale is nonlinear, so readers do not judge relative intensities by eye.

13. The derivative R'_C is taken over two gate steps, but the gate step size, and therefore the density resolution, is not given. Please state it, because the gate step limits how precisely n_c and the low-density WP onset can be located.

14. Typos and wording: "T- and S- donate" should be "denote" (Fig. 1 caption); "Therefor" (Methods); "To access the magnitude" should be "assess"; "the Wigner polarons experience remain robust but experience" (p. 6).

15. Fig. 4c: the meaning of the "bright/dark" dual axes is clear only from the caption. Labelling the two limiting assumptions directly on the axes would help. The inset schematic (exciton screening) presents a mechanism the text says is unresolved, so it should be marked as one possible scenario.

16. Hole-side data (Fig. S6) are mentioned only briefly. If the WP–AP splitting there gives an independent stiffness parameter a with the hole mass, quoting it would strengthen the phonon interpretation.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a main-text figure of the temperature dependence of U_RP, WP_T and WP_S at fixed densities below n_c. Overlay the WP termination density on Fig. 2d against n_c. This would turn the WP assignment from plausible into convincing.
- Extract and plot the WP linewidth and oscillator strength against density and pump power alongside the energy. The authors note that these quantities carry the exciton–phonon coupling, and they would test the phonon-softening scenario for optical melting directly.
- Use a self-consistent, normalized melting criterion (for example, umklapp-to-RP spectral-weight ratio) for density, temperature and pump power alike, so that thermal and optical melting can be compared on one scale.
- Put the explicit argument against attractive-polaron umklapp (ΔE_WP > ΔE_U despite the heavier trion mass) in the main text. It is a simple, strong discriminator the data already provide.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
