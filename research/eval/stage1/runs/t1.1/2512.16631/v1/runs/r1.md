Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports exciton umklapp signatures of a zero-field Wigner crystal (WC) in hBN-encapsulated monolayer WSe2. It also reports two new resonances above the triplet and singlet attractive polarons (WP_T, WP_S), which the authors identify as "Wigner polarons": trions dressed by WC phonons. If that identification holds, it would be the first optical probe of the internal vibrational dynamics of a WC, which would be of broad interest. Two further results are presented: circular-pump control of the valley/spin polarization of the electron lattice, and a pump-power-driven disappearance of the umklapp feature, interpreted as optical melting. The novelty rests mainly on the Wigner polaron identification and the optical melting claim. Both currently need firmer support.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- WSe2 is a new platform for zero-field WC physics, and its spin-valley-resolved trion structure (T⁻ and S⁻) is a genuine advantage over MoSe2. The paper uses it well in Fig. 3, where WP_T follows T⁻ and WP_S follows S⁻ under co- and cross-circular pumping.
- The umklapp analysis is careful. Three independent extraction methods agree (Figs. S1–S3). The RP–umklapp splitting is linear in n, and the fit gives an exciton mass (~0.85 m0) consistent with independent measurements.
- A microscopic theory (Ref. 41, SI Sec. 1) predicts the WP–AP splitting with no adjustable parameters. Its prefactor is within a factor of ~1.5 of the data, which I could reproduce from Eq. S1: about 3 meV per (10¹¹ cm⁻²)^{3/4}, against the fitted 4.16.
- The contrast between the umklapp and WP responses to optical excitation is intriguing and could open a route to probing WC dynamics optically.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The Wigner polaron resonances are not shown to depend on the Wigner crystal. The paper's own data suggest they can be decoupled from it (main text p. 4–6; Figs. 1c, 2d, 3, 4, S6, S7, S10). The headline claim is that WP_T and WP_S are trions dressed by WC phonons, so they should exist only where the WC exists.
   - The evidence offered does not discriminate this interpretation from alternatives:
     - The features blueshift with density. Any density-dependent satellite would do so.
     - The n^{3/4} fit has one free parameter, but the authors themselves show that a linear law with a ~7 meV intercept fits equally well (Fig. S4).
     - The polarization selectivity (Fig. 3) shows only that each feature is a satellite of its parent trion. Any trion-derived feature would inherit the same valley selection rules.
   - The data that should connect WP to the WC point the other way:
     - Under pulsed pumping, the umklapp, the paper's indicator of crystalline order, nearly vanishes by ~4000 nW. Yet the text states that the WPs "remain robust" (p. 6). A resonance that survives when the crystal it is supposed to probe has melted is hard to reconcile with a phonon sideband of that crystal.
     - In temperature, the WP labels are already absent at 10 K in Fig. S7b, while the umklapp persists to 15–25 K (Figs. S7, S8). So the two features also decouple, in the opposite order.
   - The paper calls these differences "intriguing" but does not discuss what they imply for the identification itself.
   - What I would ask for:
     - (i) Extracted WP amplitude and energy versus n and T on the same axes as the umklapp amplitude. This should show whether WP vanishes at n_c and at T_m, which is the central test.
     - (ii) Raw R_C or dR_C/dE spectra, not only dR_C/dV maps, showing WP as a distinct resonance. This would exclude a gate-derivative artifact from the broadening and gaining AP lineshape.
     - (iii) The hole-doped side as a control, if accessible. There is a single AP branch there, so a single WP is predicted.
     - (iv) An explicit discussion of other assignments (trion fine structure or excited trion states, AP–RP hybridization, shake-up continua) and why they are excluded.
     - (v) A reconciliation of the WP's survival under pumping with its proposed origin.

2. The "optical melting" claim is inferred from a fixed-threshold amplitude criterion. This does not separate melting from reduced visibility of a weak satellite (Fig. 4, Methods p. 14, Figs. S6, S9).
   - The critical density n* in Fig. 4c is the density at which dR_C/dV of the umklapp crosses a fixed value (−0.005 to −0.0065).
   - The pulsed white-light source is both pump and probe ("simultaneously measuring their reflectance spectrum"). At 4000 nW the peak power is ~0.5 W in 200 ps, so the spectrum is a nonlinear, time-averaged response. Phase-space filling, exciton–exciton broadening or saturation of the RP oscillator strength would all reduce the umklapp amplitude and move a fixed-threshold crossing to lower n, even with the electron lattice unchanged.
   - To support melting, please:
     - (i) Show the umklapp amplitude normalized to the RP amplitude and linewidth at each power.
     - (ii) Show ΔE_U versus n at each power. A surviving but weakened crystal should keep ΔE_U = h²n/(√3 m_X).
     - (iii) Show how n* depends on the threshold choice, and why the threshold differs from the ±0.005 used in Fig. S8.
     - (iv) Ideally, separate the pump from a weak probe (a CW or delayed probe), so that the WC is read out under linear-response conditions.
   - Without these, the data establish that the umklapp feature fades under strong pulsed illumination, not that the WC melts.

B. Should address

3. The WC assignment sits far outside expected stability bounds, and the paper attributes this to disorder without evidence (p. 4–5; SI Sec. 2; Fig. S8).
   - The extracted r_s ≈ 12 is a factor of ~2.5–3 below the clean-limit value of ~31–38.
   - The umklapp persists to ~25 K. At n ≈ 3×10¹¹ cm⁻² and 20 K (Fig. S8), I estimate a classical plasma parameter Γ ≈ 20, using κ = 4.5. That is well below Γ ≈ 137 for classical melting, and the quantum melting temperature is lower still.
   - An umklapp satellite at k_W requires density correlations at that wavevector, not necessarily long-range order. A strongly correlated liquid or a pinned glass with short-range triangular order could produce it.
   - Please discuss this quantitatively. Useful evidence would be the umklapp linewidth relative to the RP and its evolution through n_c and T_m, and an estimate of the correlation length the satellite requires. Alternatively, temper "WC" to a state with short-range crystalline correlations where appropriate.
   - The hBN thicknesses, and hence the graphite-gate distances, are not reported. Gate screening at distances comparable to the lattice constant (~10 nm at n_c) changes both r_s and WC stability, so these numbers are needed to interpret r_s.

4. Electronic heating is not excluded as the cause of the umklapp suppression (p. 6, Fig. S9).
   - The 1s exciton energy measures the lattice temperature, as the authors note. The relevant energy scale for melting is ~2 meV (T_m ≈ 25 K at low n, lower at intermediate n). Exciton–electron scattering and Auger-type processes during the 200 ps pulse can heat the electrons without heating the lattice.
   - Fig. S9 itself shows a ~0.2 meV redshift at the lowest powers. By the orange calibration curve this corresponds to roughly 7–8 K. The statement of "negligible lattice heating" should therefore be quantified with an upper bound on T.
   - One check is already in the data. Compare a high-power spectrum with a spectrum at elevated temperature and matched density (Fig. S7). Thermal melting appears to remove WP before the umklapp, whereas optical excitation does the reverse. If this holds, it is a useful argument against simple electron heating and should be made explicitly.

5. The exciton-density estimates in Fig. 4c are not reproducible, and the text contradicts the figure (Methods p. 13–14; p. 6).
   - The formula n_X = PαΓ/(Aħω) applied to peak power is valid only when the lifetime Γ is much shorter than the 200 ps pulse. For dark excitons with ns or longer lifetimes it is not. Per-pulse fluence, and pile-up between pulses 25 ns apart, would then set the density.
   - The values of α ("10% for example"), the spot size A and the lifetimes Γ are not given. Only part of the 650–800 nm band is absorbed resonantly.
   - The text says the dark-exciton density is "approximately 10¹¹ cm⁻²", "still lower than n_c". The top axis of Fig. 4c, however, reaches ~6×10¹¹ cm⁻², comparable to n_c, at the point where the umklapp nearly vanishes.
   - This matters for the proposed exciton-mediated softening mechanism. Please give all parameters, treat the pulsed case correctly, and reconcile the figure with the text.

6. Spin-control claims are qualitative and partly overstated (Fig. 3, p. 5; abstract).
   - The degree of valley/spin polarization of the resident electrons is not quantified. It could be estimated from the T⁻/S⁻ weight ratio or measured by Kerr rotation, and its dependence on pump power should be shown.
   - The schematic in Fig. 3a and the phrase "spin-ordered electron arrays" suggest ferromagnetic order. What is shown is an optically pumped, non-equilibrium valley polarization.
   - The abstract's "well above the magnetic ordering temperature" needs an estimate of that temperature, for example the exchange scale at r_s ≈ 12.
   - The 635 nm CW pump (1.95 eV) lies above the ~1.9 eV free-carrier gap quoted on p. 6, so photodoping should be checked.
   - The umklapp survives the 4 µW CW pump (Figs. 3b,c) but nearly vanishes under 4 µW average pulsed excitation (Fig. 4b). This comparison should be discussed, because it bears directly on the mechanism of concern 2.

7. The density dependence of ΔE_WP is shown only as dashed curves overlaid on colour maps (Fig. 2d, Fig. S4).
   - Please extract WP peak positions with uncertainties versus calibrated n. Plot ΔE_WP on log-log axes, and compare the n^{3/4} and linear-plus-offset fits with a goodness-of-fit measure.
   - State the predicted prefactor from Eq. S1 numerically, with the masses and κ used. The harmonic single-site model is questionable at r_s ≈ 12, where zero-point motion is large. Its limits should be stated alongside the claim of "good quantitative agreement".

8. All data appear to come from one device and one spot. The number of devices, and whether umklapp, WP and the pump effects reproduce across devices or locations, are not stated. Please report this. If a second device or spot was measured, show its umklapp and WP maps.

9. The density calibration needs more detail (Methods p. 13; Fig. S11).
   - The hBN thicknesses are not given.
   - ε_hBN = 3.5 is used for the capacitance, while κ = 4.5 is used for r_s. Please explain both choices.
   - "A single-gate scan" is used for a dual-gated device. Specify which gate and what the other gate was held at.
   - Comment on contact behaviour at low density and 5 K, which can make the parallel-plate model fail. The Landau-level or RP-blueshift cross-check (Ref. 62) is described as "qualitatively similar". A quantitative comparison would support the 10% uncertainty that propagates into m_X and r_s.

C. Minor

10. "m_X ~ (0.85 ± 0.8) m0" (p. 4) has an uncertainty almost equal to the value, which contradicts the stated ~10% uncertainty. It is presumably ±0.08; please correct it. Below ~3×10¹¹ cm⁻², ΔE_U is smaller than the RP linewidth (~4.5–6 meV, Fig. S11b), and Fig. S3 shows outliers near 3.8×10¹¹ cm⁻². Please state how the low-density points and outliers are treated in the Fig. 2b fit, and whether the intercept was free.
11. Critical densities are quoted inconsistently: n_c > 7×10¹¹ cm⁻² (p. 4), ~6.6×10¹¹ cm⁻² at the lowest power in Fig. 4c, and ~7×10¹¹ cm⁻² in Fig. S8. The thresholds also differ (−0.005 to −0.0065 against ±0.005). Please harmonize these and explain the error bars in Fig. 4c.
12. The text cites Fig. S10 for the WP redshift and broadening "at this pumping power" (4000 nW), but Fig. S10 is at 8000 nW. WP is not labelled in Fig. 4b or in Figs. S6b–f, so the claim that the WPs "remain robust" cannot be checked from the figures. Please add line cuts at 40, 4000 and 8000 nW.
13. The pulsed pump is said to lie below the free-carrier gap (~1.9 eV), but its 650 nm edge (1.907 eV) is marginally above it. The band also covers the 2s and 3s states. Please state the spot size and the gate-voltage step used for R'_C.
14. Typos and figure details:
    - "donate" should be "denote" in the Fig. 1 and Fig. S6 captions.
    - "the Wigner polarons experience remain robust" (p. 6) needs rewording.
    - Fig. 1a does not show a top graphite gate, although the text and the voltage labels imply one.
    - Ref. 41 (the theory the paper depends on) and SI Ref. 1 lack arXiv identifiers. Ref. 7's identifier is malformatted.
    - The axis assignments in the Fig. S9 caption ("bottom/left", "top/left") should be clarified.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Make the case for the Wigner polaron identification with one combined figure. It would show WP and umklapp amplitudes, and ΔE_WP, versus density and temperature on shared axes, together with raw spectra showing WP as a resonance. If WP and umklapp vanish together at n_c and T_m in equilibrium, the opposite optical response becomes a strength rather than a contradiction.
2. Use a two-beam scheme: a separate pump, and a weak, delayed or CW probe. This would make the melting measurement linear in the probe, and a pump–probe delay scan would distinguish electron heating (ps to ns recovery) from long-lived dark-exciton effects. It would also test the proposed exciton-mediated softening directly.
3. Exploit WSe2's spin-valley structure further. Measuring the WP_T/WP_S weights as a function of the calibrated degree of electron polarization would test the theory of Ref. 41 quantitatively, beyond the on/off selection rule.
4. Measure the hole-doped side or a second material (for example MoSe2, where the umklapp is established) to test whether the predicted number and mass scaling of WP branches holds.

5. Verdict: major revision
