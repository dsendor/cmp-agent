Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper introduces "ultrafast X-ray sonography": a laser-launched picosecond strain pulse acts as a depth marker, and the phase-specific strain response read from two coexisting Bragg peaks shows where each phase sits in the film. It applies the method to the laser-driven AFM-to-FM transition in a 44 nm FeRh(001) film measured at EuXFEL/MID. It reports that FM domains nucleate as columns in a near-surface region about 19 ± 2 nm high that later merges into a continuous layer (scenario III), and that at the lowest fluence the FM region stays laterally incomplete (scenario IV). Adding depth information to the volume fraction that standard UXRD already gives is a useful and fairly general idea. The FeRh result helps explain the probe-depth-dependent rise times reported before.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The idea is simple and broadly applicable: no dedicated transducer is needed, and the same excitation drives the transition and launches the marker strain pulse.
- The data are clean. The AFM and FM (002) peaks are clearly separated (Fig. 2b), and the phase-specific strain transients (Fig. 2c) differ qualitatively, so pure in-plane heterogeneity (scenario II) is excluded without any modelling.
- The analysis compares full modelled peak profiles with the data (Fig. 4a,b), not only peak positions, and the paper shows why positions alone are not enough.
- The thermoelastic parameters were calibrated independently on the same sample in earlier work, which limits the number of free parameters.
- Data and code are openly available (EuXFEL DOI, Zenodo), and the fluence series (Figs. S3, S4) adds some generality.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The main conclusions rest on a model-based comparison that is reasonably supported. The issues below make the quantitative domain height and the choice of scenario less certain, but they do not overturn the central claims.

B. Should address

1. The 19 ± 2 nm domain height and its error bar do not account for uncertainty in the model. The uncertainty comes only from the χ² dependence on V*_FM (Fig. 4c, p. 5). The extraction, however, depends on several inputs that are fixed or tuned:
   - the optical penetration depth, which the Methods (p. 6) say was "optimized" for this incidence angle;
   - the assumption that the bottom 5.5 nm of FeRh does not contribute to its Bragg peak, introduced "for an overall better agreement";
   - the fixed nucleation time constant τ = 8 ps;
   - the transformation stress of 1.32 GPa;
   - the sound velocity of 5.0 nm/ps taken from the literature.

   The penetration depth matters most. The paper itself says D_FM "can be approximated by the depth until which the optical excitation overcomes the equilibrium transition threshold" (p. 5), so the extracted domain height and the tuned penetration depth are likely strongly correlated. The authors should show how the best-fit D_FM, and the ranking of scenarios III and IV, change when each of these inputs is varied within a plausible range. They should also state the penetration depth they used next to literature values for FeRh at 800 nm and 25° incidence. Without this, the "20 nm" headline is the result of one parameter set, not a measured quantity with a defensible error bar.

2. The scenarios are not clearly distinguished by statistics, and the set of scenarios is limited.
   - In Fig. 4c the minima for scenarios III and IV (about 0.085 vs about 0.09) and for I (about 0.10) are close. χ² is an unweighted sum of squared residuals (p. 5), with no noise normalisation, no degrees of freedom and no significance criterion. The authors should weight the residuals by the measured shot-to-shot or delay-to-delay uncertainty and report a reduced χ², or use a resampling test, to show that III is preferred over I and IV by more than the noise.
   - Scenario IV uses an arbitrary D_FM = 1.3 V*_FM (Methods, p. 7). D_FM should be a free parameter in IV, otherwise IV is not being compared with III on equal terms.
   - Every scenario has a sharp FM/AFM boundary. Exponential optical absorption plus electron diffusion should instead give a graded FM fraction with depth, so the paper should test a graded-profile scenario, or say why the data rule one out. This also bears on whether "domain height" is the right description.

3. A one-dimensional elastic model is applied to three-dimensional columnar domains. The mixed scenarios (III, IV) place FM columns about 20 nm high and, by the paper's own estimate, about 25 nm wide (p. 5; mosaic block model) inside an AFM matrix. Columns of this aspect ratio are partly clamped in-plane by the surrounding AFM, so their out-of-plane expansion, and the reference lattice constant q_z0 = 4.196 Å⁻¹ chosen for "vanishing in-plane expansion" (p. 3), may differ from the laterally homogeneous 1D case. The incoherent averaging over lateral nucleation sites (p. 7) also leaves out elastic coupling between neighbouring FM and AFM columns. The authors should estimate how in-plane clamping and lateral strain coupling would change the FM peak position and the strain-pulse signature, for example with a simple 2D/3D finite-element estimate or an analytic Poisson-ratio argument. They should also discuss whether this could shift the inferred D_FM or favour one scenario over another.

4. The claims about lateral domain size are not supported by the main-text analysis and appear to contradict it. On p. 3 the paper states that sonography "is not necessarily sensitive to the shape of the domains", and the Conclusion says lateral dimensions "can only be accessed indirectly, if at all". Yet on p. 5 it argues that "the matching in-plane size of the nucleating FM domains (Supplementary Note S4) and the mosaic crystal building blocks" shows the role of static heterogeneity. The authors should state explicitly how the in-plane size is obtained (for example from peak widths along q_x or from A_FM), give its uncertainty, and reconcile it with the statement that the method is insensitive to lateral size. Otherwise this interpretive sentence should be toned down.

5. The FM volume fraction relies on an assumption about the fixed-angle measurement geometry. V*_FM = 0.46 fixes D_FM·A_FM for every scenario (Eq. 2, Methods), and it comes from the ratio of integrated intensities measured at a single sample angle of 21.90°, between the two Bragg angles (p. 6). For a mosaic film this captures only a cut through each peak. Rocking-width or tilt differences between AFM and FM regions, structure-factor or Debye-Waller differences, and strain-induced shifts along q_x would all bias the intensity ratio. The authors should state whether rocking curves or an equivalent check confirm that the ratio is a faithful volume fraction, or propagate a corresponding systematic error into V*_FM and hence into D_FM.

6. The phase heterogeneity changes on the same timescale as the probing strain pulse. The paper's own criterion (p. 6) is that the heterogeneity must change more slowly than the strain pulse propagates. Here the FM fraction rises with τ = 8 ps, while the pulse crosses the about 38 nm active FeRh layer in about 8 ps at 5 nm/ps, so the two timescales are the same. The model includes this through the incoherent average over nucleation delays. Still, describing the result as a domain "height" (a static quantity) needs qualification. The authors should say which delay window mainly constrains D_FM; Fig. 4a uses t = 9 ps, when the FM fraction is still only about 2/3 of its final value. They should also discuss how a different or non-exponential nucleation law would change the inference.

7. The evidence covers one sample and, in the main text, essentially one fluence. All results come from a single 44 nm FeRh/W/MgO film, already damaged at the interface by high-repetition excitation (the 5.5 nm non-contributing layer, p. 6–7). The authors should confirm that the sample response did not drift over the measurement, for example by comparing early and late repeats at the same fluence, and say whether the six pulses within each 56 kHz burst give the same response, since inter-pulse heating could pre-condition the film near 370 K. They should also state the depth-resolution limits for other film thicknesses, so that claims of generality ("universal", "versatile platform") are matched by what one sample can show.

C. Minor

8. Fig. 4a: the x-axis is labelled "q_z (Å)" but should read Å⁻¹. Fig. 4c uses "global quadratic deviation" in the caption and "global residual" on the axis and in the text; one term should be used throughout.

9. Fig. 2c has no error bars, and the FM strain is shown only once the FM peak holds more than 10% of the intensity. Uncertainties from the pseudo-Voigt fits should be shown, and the early-time FM points left out of the fit should be stated.

10. Eq. (1) and the statement that the delay "directly decodes the thickness of each phase" (p. 2) describe an idealised case. Here reflections and direct photoexcitation mean D_FM can be obtained only through full modelling (pp. 4, 5). This should be said more plainly when Eq. (1) is introduced.

11. The Methods state that density, sound-velocity and thermal-expansion changes in the FM phase are neglected because they are "rather small" (p. 7). The literature values (refs. 40, 44) and the size of the resulting error in the propagation delay should be given.

12. The discussion of magnetic ordering and domain-wall motion (p. 5) goes beyond what a purely structural probe can establish. It is appropriately hedged, but it could be shortened or clearly marked as speculation.

13. The sample was grown by "magnetron sputtering from an equiatomic FeRh target". The film stoichiometry and the width of the equilibrium transition (for example from M(T)) should be given, since compositional gradients with depth could themselves set where nucleation is preferred.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a sensitivity table or figure for D_FM and for the scenario ranking against optical penetration depth, the dead-layer thickness, τ, the transformation stress and the sound velocity, and quote a total uncertainty.
- Use a noise-weighted χ², let D_FM vary freely in scenario IV, and add a graded-depth-profile scenario, so the selection of scenario III is statistically defensible.
- Give a short estimate of in-plane clamping effects on columnar FM domains of this aspect ratio.
- Demonstrate the method on a second film thickness or a second sample, even briefly, to support the claim of generality.

5. Verdict

Verdict: minor revision
