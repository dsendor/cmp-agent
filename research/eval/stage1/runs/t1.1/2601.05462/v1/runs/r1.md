Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports an in-plane anomalous Hall effect (IP-AHE) in CaRuO3/La2/3Ca1/3MnO3/CaRuO3 trilayers on NdGaO3(110). The Hall signal tracks the square in-plane hysteresis loop along the easy axis [-110]O and is absent for M//[001]O. The authors tune it non-monotonically with CaRuO3 buffer thickness and switch it off with ionic-liquid gating. A perovskite-oxide platform with a sizable remanent IP-AHE and an electrical knob would be a useful addition to a field so far limited to layered and topological magnets. However, the central claim is that CaRuO3-induced mirror-symmetry breaking (monoclinic tilt beta_LC) activates and controls the effect, and in its current form neither the symmetry analysis nor the layer attribution supports that claim.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The basic phenomenology is clean and internally consistent. The Hall signal follows the easy-axis M-H loop (Fig. 1d,g), vanishes in the out-of-plane geometry (Fig. 1f), follows cos(theta) under in-plane field rotation (Fig. 3b,c) and does not depend on current direction across Hall bars patterned at different phi (Fig. 3d,e). The phi independence is a good discriminator against planar-Hall and many thermoelectric artifacts.
- The structural characterization is thorough: RSMs about four reflections, HAADF/ABF-STEM and GPA (Fig. 2). It gives a layer-resolved picture of opposite monoclinic-like tilts in CaRuO3 and LCMO.
- The zero-field remanent signal (about 104 mS cm^-1, nearly equal to the saturated value) and the electrical ON/OFF behaviour are attractive for planar spintronic readout.
- The thickness series (seven tCRO values, Fig. 4a-g) and the temperature dependence (Fig. 4h) give a reasonably broad data set.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The symmetry argument that the monoclinic distortion is needed for IP-AHE does not hold, and the paper's own DFT contradicts it. The text (pp. 6-8, Fig. 3a) argues that interfaces lower LCMO from Pbnm to P2_1/m, leaving only the mirror perpendicular to [001]O. This lowering is presented as what allows M//[-110]O to produce Omega_z, with beta_LC as "a reliable parameter for evaluating the degree of mirror-symmetry breaking". For a (110)O-oriented Pbnm crystal (point group mmm, axes a, b, c), the linear axial-vector response coupling M to Omega is diagonal in (a, b, c). Rotated to x = [-110]O and z = [110]O, the off-diagonal element is chi_zx = (chi_bb - chi_aa)/2. This element is generically non-zero because a is not equal to b. So Omega_[110] proportional to M_[-110] is already allowed in bulk orthorhombic LCMO without any monoclinic tilt. The same mmm analysis forbids Omega_z for M//[001]O, so the null in Fig. 1h does not distinguish the two pictures. The DFT says the same: sigma_DFT is finite for the orthorhombic cell with beta_LC = 90.0 degrees (Fig. 4i, p. 10) and vanishes only in the cubic cell, which has no a/b anisotropy.
   - Why it matters: the paper's main framing is "CaRuO3-buffer-induced mirror-symmetry breaking" (abstract), with beta_LC as the control parameter and gating as "reconfiguring the symmetry breaking". If the effect is symmetry-allowed in single-domain orthorhombic (110) LCMO, then tCRO and gating tune the magnitude of an already-allowed response, for example through octahedral rotation amplitude, orthorhombic anisotropy or band filling. They do not switch a symmetry constraint.
   - What to do: redo the symmetry analysis for the (110)-oriented Pbnm parent, and state explicitly which tensor components are allowed in mmm and in 2/m. Then reframe the role of the monoclinic tilt, or show quantitatively that it, rather than orthorhombicity or OOR amplitude, controls the magnitude.

2. The IP-AHE is not shown to come from LCMO rather than from CaRuO3 or the CaRuO3/LCMO interfaces, and the beta_LC-sigma_AHE correlation is not causal. Every tuning knob acts first on CaRuO3. In Fig. 4f,g, tCRO changes the CaRuO3 thickness and the parallel conduction. Supplementary Fig. 4, cited on p. 5, shows that Tc and Ms also change with tCRO. Gating protonates CaRuO3 (p. 10), while the LCMO(440) peak "remains nearly unchanged" (p. 11). CaRuO3 has strong spin-orbit coupling, the same orthorhombic/monoclinic structure, and is close to a ferromagnetic instability. Interfacial Ru near a ferromagnetic manganite can be proximity-polarized, and the authors themselves invoke Ru4+ to Mn4+ charge transfer. The sign reversal near 100 K is described as "similar to the intrinsic AHE of SRO thin films" (p. 9). It points to at least two competing contributions with different temperature dependences.
   - Confounders in the tCRO trend: the decrease for tCRO >= 32 u.c. is attributed to strain relaxation and a/b twin domains. In the orthorhombic picture of item 1, twin variants related by a 90-degree rotation about [110]O swap a and b, so their contributions cancel. Twinning alone could then produce the drop without any change in beta_LC. Normalizing sigma_AHE by the total trilayer thickness also changes with tCRO.
   - What to do:
     - Vary the LCMO thickness at fixed tCRO = 16 u.c. to separate a bulk-LCMO contribution from an interface one.
     - Measure bilayers with CaRuO3 only below or only above the LCMO.
     - Replace CaRuO3 with an insulating or low-SOC perovskite buffer whose octahedral rotations can be matched. This tests whether the structural transfer, rather than Ru spin-orbit coupling or proximity, matters.
     - Quantify twin-domain fractions versus tCRO, for example from RSM or STEM over large areas.
     - Report sheet conductances, not only sigma = rho_AHE/rho_xx^2 for the whole stack, so that the reader can see how much of the trend comes from parallel conduction in CaRuO3.
   - Without these controls the attribution to beta_LC of LCMO in Figs. 4f,g and in the conclusion is a hypothesis.

3. The gating result is not shown to come from a structural or symmetry reconfiguration with "minimal change in ferromagnetism". The evidence for unchanged magnetism is Hc from the Hall loops (Fig. 5d). Hc rises from about 250 to about 290 Oe at 1.1-1.3 V, and at 1.4 V, where the signal vanishes, no loop exists from which Hc can be extracted. A vanishing remanent Hall signal along [-110]O is equally consistent with other explanations:
   - the easy axis rotating toward [001]O after protonation (Fig. 1h shows that this alone suppresses the signal);
   - multidomain demagnetization;
   - loss of ferromagnetism in an interfacial layer;
   - cancellation by an opposite-sign contribution from HxCaRuO3, which ref. 48 reports to be ferromagnetic.

   There is also no experimental measurement of beta_LC or of the LCMO octahedral rotations after gating. Only a 2theta-omega CaRuO3 peak shift is reported (Supplementary Fig. 24), and the octahedral-coupling mechanism rests on DFT with x = 1 (Fig. 5b, Supplementary Fig. 27).
   - What to do:
     - Provide M-H loops along [-110]O and [001]O by SQUID after gating to 1.4 V (or the anisotropic magnetoresistance as a proxy if magnetometry of the gated device is impossible).
     - Provide RSM-derived beta_LC before and after gating.
     - Provide rho_xx and raw rho_AHE versus Vg, since sigma_AHE depends on rho_xx squared.
     - Give an estimate of the proton content.
   - The temperature protocol must also be stated. Gating presumably happens near room temperature (the XRD was recorded in situ), with Hall measurement at 10 K. Readers need to know whether the device was thermally cycled between Vg steps, and how many devices showed the switching.

B. Should address

4. The out-of-plane AHE contribution needs a quantitative bound. In a monoclinic film, the magnetic easy axis can tilt slightly out of plane. This gives M_z proportional to M_[-110], which by symmetry is indistinguishable from the claimed effect and would produce an ordinary AHE that tracks the in-plane loop. The Hall angle here is very small: rho_AHE of about 4.5 nOhm cm, and rho_xx of about 200 microOhm cm, inferred from sigma_AHE = 104 mS cm^-1. So a canting of order a degree could matter. Fig. 1f covers only ±2.5 kOe, far below the out-of-plane saturation field of a manganite film, so it does not give the conventional AHE coefficient. Please measure out-of-plane Hall up to full saturation (several tesla) at 10 K to obtain the conventional coefficient R_s. Then state the canting angle that would be needed to explain the observed signal, and compare it with the bound from Supplementary Figs. 15-16 or from magnetometry.

5. The Hall measurement protocol is not described. The Methods give no current magnitude, no DC or AC mode, no current-reversal or antisymmetrization procedure, and no field-alignment accuracy for the rotator. With in-plane M, a vertical temperature gradient from Joule heating produces an anomalous Nernst voltage along z x M, which is exactly the Hall direction for the phi = 0 bar. The phi independence in Fig. 3e argues against a dominant contribution of this kind but does not quantify it. Please state the procedure and show rho_AHE versus current density (it should be linear-response and independent of the sign of I). Also explain how the planar Hall component, which is even in M, was removed in Fig. 3b,d.

6. The DFT comparison is only qualitative, and some modelling choices are unusual.
   - The calculated sigma_DFT (about 4-9 S cm^-1) exceeds the experimental value (about 0.1 S cm^-1) by roughly two orders of magnitude. This is not discussed.
   - The beta_LC values modelled (90.0, 90.08 and 90.16 degrees) are close to experiment, but the experiment shows about a 30-fold change in sigma between beta_LC - 90 of about 0.03 and about 0.2 degrees (Fig. 4f,g). DFT gives a factor of about 2.
   - The calculation uses U = 8 eV and J = 2 eV on Mn with VCA for Ca, and a Wannier basis of Mn 3d only, which omits the O 2p states. These choices are not tested; please show the sensitivity of sigma_DFT to U and to the Wannier basis.
   - It is unclear how OOR and Jahn-Teller amplitudes were set to produce each beta_LC after "full relaxation" (p. 15).
   - No calculation includes CaRuO3 or an interface, although item 2 makes that the key question.

7. The extraction of beta_LC needs more detail. Fig. 4g plots beta_LC - 90 degrees with error bars of about 0.01 degrees for a 32 u.c. LCMO layer whose RSM peaks sit close to the substrate and CaRuO3 peaks (Fig. 2a-d). Please describe the fitting (peak positions, which reflections, how d_OR maps to beta), how the error bars were obtained, and whether beta_LC is a layer average or is weighted toward the interfaces. The GPA maps (Fig. 2h, color scale ±0.5 degrees) should also state their precision relative to a 0.1-degree effect.

8. Reproducibility is not documented. The paper does not state how many samples were grown per tCRO, whether the key 16 u.c. result was reproduced in more than one film, or whether the points in Fig. 4f carry sample-to-sample spread (Fig. 4f has no error bars). Please state this and add the spread.

9. The temperature dependence (Fig. 4h) stops at 200 K although Tc >= 250 K. The text says the effect "persists up to 200 K" and the Conclusion repeats this. Please extend to Tc, and discuss the sign change in light of item 2: two contributions, or one Berry-curvature contribution with a Fermi-level-dependent sign.

C. Minor

10. Fig. 4h axis units: the label "10^-2 S cm^-1" with values down to -100 implies -1 S cm^-1 = -1000 mS cm^-1 for tCRO = 16 u.c. That contradicts Fig. 4d and the text (103.7 mS cm^-1). The unit should presumably be 10^-3 S cm^-1.

11. On p. 9 the text says σAHE reverses sign near 100 K for tCRO ≤ 8 u.c. In Fig. 4h the tCRO = 8 u.c. curve stays negative up to 200 K, and only the 0, 2 and 4 u.c. curves reverse. Please correct the text.

12. The text (p. 8) says the saturated rho_AHE in Fig. 3c was taken at H = 2000 Oe; the caption says 1000 Oe.

13. Fig. 4k: the caption defines theta as the angle between M and [-110]O. However, sigma_DFT is zero at theta = 0 and largest at 90 degrees, the opposite of the experiment and of the text, and the inset appears to measure theta from [001]O. Please make the definition consistent with Fig. 3a.

14. The Fig. 3 caption refers in (d) to "various omega values"; this should be phi. The signed radial axis in the Fig. 3c polar plot (-5 at the centre-top to +5 at the bottom) is confusing. A Cartesian plot of rho_AHE versus theta with a cos(theta) fit would be clearer.

15. Fig. 1c (out-of-plane M-H) shows a linear slope reaching about 0.6 mu_B/Mn at 2.5 kOe. Please state how the paramagnetic NdGaO3 background was handled in all M-H data, since Nd3+ contributes strongly at 10 K.

16. The Data availability statement is incomplete ("available from").

17. The claim that such a zero-field IP-AHE was "rarely observed" (p. 9) should be put in context with absolute numbers, both rho_AHE and the anomalous Hall angle, from refs. 14-18. In absolute terms 0.1 S cm^-1 is small; the novelty is the remanence and the tunability.

18. In the Conclusion, "we established" should start with a capital letter. The Fig. 5 caption should state the protonation level modelled (x = 1) as distinct from the experimental, unknown x.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one decisive layer-attribution experiment: an LCMO-thickness series at fixed tCRO, or a structural-mimic buffer without Ru. This would turn the beta_LC-sigma_AHE correlation into a causal statement.
2. Rewrite the symmetry section to start from the (110)-oriented Pbnm point group. Then present the CaRuO3-induced distortion as tuning the magnitude, which is a still-interesting and more defensible claim.
3. For the gating experiment, add post-gating magnetometry along both in-plane axes and an RSM, so that "electrically switched symmetry" can be told apart from "electrically switched anisotropy or magnetization".
4. Report raw rho_xy, rho_xx and sheet conductances for all samples in a supplementary table, so that readers can check the conversion to sigma_AHE and the effect of parallel conduction in CaRuO3.

5. Verdict: major revision
