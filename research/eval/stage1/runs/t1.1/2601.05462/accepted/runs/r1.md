Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report an in-plane anomalous Hall effect (IP-AHE) in CaRuO3/La2/3Ca1/3MnO3/CaRuO3 (CRO/LCMO/CRO) trilayers on NdGaO3(110). The Hall signal follows the square in-plane magnetization loop along [1-10]_O and reaches about 104 mS/cm at zero field. They attribute it to a CRO-induced monoclinic-like distortion of LCMO that breaks the last mirror symmetry. They show the signal can be tuned by the CRO thickness and switched off reversibly by ionic-liquid protonation of CRO. IP-AHE in a strong ferromagnet with a square, zero-field-remanent loop, built in a widely used oxide platform, is a useful step past the weak-moment and semimetal systems studied so far. The physical attribution (monoclinic distortion of LCMO as the enabling and tuning parameter) is the part that needs more support.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear zero-field IP-AHE that tracks the in-plane M-H loop along the easy axis (Fig. 1f). Supporting checks include angle dependence of H (Supp. Figs. 17, 18), rotation of H in the (001) plane to exclude out-of-plane M (Supp. Figs. 19, 20), and independence from the current direction (Supp. Fig. 21).
- The structural characterization is thorough and comes from complementary methods: asymmetric RSMs along four directions, HAADF/ABF-STEM and GPA (Fig. 2, Supp. Figs. 7-15).
- Systematic tCRO and tLC series. Control samples on NGO(001) show negligible IP-AHE (Supp. Figs. 4, 23).
- Reversible gate control of the IP-AHE while Hc and rho_xx-T stay nearly unchanged (Fig. 4d, Supp. Figs. 32, 34). This points to a structural rather than magnetic origin of the switching.
- DFT and Wannier calculations give the same trend in magnitude with beta_LC (Fig. 3h, i).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The symmetry argument for why the monoclinic distortion is needed conflicts with the paper's own DFT.
   - Where: main text (pp. 4-5 and Fig. 2i) and DFT (p. 6, Fig. 3h).
   - The argument: the monoclinic P21/m distortion of LCMO leaves only the M(001) mirror and the 2[001] axis, and M along [1-10]_O removes them, which "activates" Omega_z.
   - The problem: for M along [1-10]_O, the orthorhombic Pbnm cell (mmm point group) already loses every mirror and every twofold axis that could forbid a Hall vector along the film normal [110]_O.
     - The mirrors perpendicular to a and b each flip one in-plane component of M.
     - The mirror perpendicular to c and the 2_c axis flip M.
     - With Omega_i = chi_ii M_i and a ≠ b, the component along the [110] normal scales as (chi_a - chi_b). This is generally nonzero.
   - The paper's DFT agrees with this: the orthorhombic supercell with beta_LC = 90.00° gives a finite sigma_AHE-DFT of about 2 S/cm (Fig. 3h), and only the cubic cell without octahedral rotations gives zero.
   - So the calculations show that the octahedral rotation (OOR) and orthorhombicity, not the monoclinic tilt, switch on the IP-AHE. The tilt only changes its size.
   - Why it matters:
     - It changes the central claim of "monoclinic distortion-enabled mirror-symmetry breaking".
     - It also leaves unexplained why single LCMO/NGO(110) films and tCRO = 0 trilayers, which are orthorhombic, show a much smaller signal.
   - What to do:
     - Give a complete magnetic-point-group analysis for both the orthorhombic and the monoclinic cell, with M along [1-10]_O and along [001]_O.
     - State plainly whether IP-AHE is symmetry-allowed in the (110)-oriented orthorhombic film.
     - Recast the narrative: either as a magnitude effect of distortion within an already-allowed channel, or by identifying a symmetry element that truly distinguishes the two cases.
     - Reconcile Fig. 2i with Fig. 3h.

2. The data do not establish which layer carries the IP-AHE, and the alternative of a magnetized interfacial CRO layer is not excluded.
   - Why the alternative is plausible: CRO is a metallic, strong-SOC layer at an interface where the paper itself invokes Ru-Mn charge transfer and an enhanced Tc (p. 3).
   - Several observations fit an interfacial-ruthenate origin at least as well as an LCMO origin:
     - The sign reversal of sigma_AHE near 100 K, which the authors themselves compare to the SrRuO3 AHE (p. 5, Supp. Fig. 24).
     - The enhancement when tLC is reduced (Supp. Fig. 22).
     - The gating result: protonation acts on CRO, kills the IP-AHE, and leaves LCMO magnetism (Hc) unchanged (Fig. 4d).
   - The supporting DFT is done only for an LCMO-only cell (Fig. 3h, i). Its predicted sigma (2-8 S/cm) is about 20-80 times larger than measured (about 0.1 S/cm). So it does not quantitatively support the LCMO assignment either.
   - What to do:
     - Compute the Berry-curvature AHE for the CRO/LCMO superlattices already relaxed (Supp. Fig. 31), including the induced Ru moment.
     - Or give experimental evidence that separates the layers. Options include:
       - a trilayer with a nonmagnetic, low-SOC buffer that produces a comparable monoclinic distortion;
       - XMCD at the Ru edge;
       - LCMO-thickness scaling of sigma_AHE expressed per LCMO volume versus per interface.
     - Discuss how protonating CRO could remove an interfacial ruthenate contribution directly, rather than only through reduced octahedral coupling.

B. Should address

3. The sigma_AHE = rho_AHE/rho_xx^2 conversion treats a three-layer parallel conductor as one homogeneous film (p. 5, Fig. 3a-f).
   - The current is shared by two metallic CRO layers and the LCMO layer in proportions that change with tCRO and temperature. The extracted sigma_AHE therefore mixes the intrinsic Hall conductivity of the active layer with shunting.
   - This bears directly on the non-monotonic tCRO dependence: the peak at 16 u.c. and the 20-fold drop at 32 u.c. could partly come from shunting through the thicker CRO.
   - What to do:
     - Show rho_xx for each tCRO.
     - Model the parallel conduction using separately measured CRO and LCMO single-layer resistivities.
     - Report the Hall conductance per sheet (sigma_xy times thickness) alongside the bulk-normalized values.

4. The tCRO trend rests on one sample per thickness, and the explanation for the drop at 32 u.c. is not demonstrated.
   - The peak at 16 u.c. is a single point (Fig. 3f).
   - The drop at 32 u.c. is assigned to "microscopic strain relaxation and formation of a/b twin domains", but no twin-domain or relaxation data are shown for that sample.
   - What to do:
     - State how many samples were grown at each tCRO, and show the sample-to-sample spread, especially at 16 and 32 u.c.
     - Give RSM or STEM evidence of twinning or relaxation at 32 and 64 u.c.

5. The structural parameter beta_LC - 90° (0.03-0.2°, Fig. 3g) is very small compared with the RSM peak widths and the STEM resolution.
   - The error bars are described as coming from "broadening of the RSM diffraction spot". Report how they were computed, the instrumental resolution, and how beta is extracted from the four asymmetric reflections.
   - The text says the opposite monoclinic tilts are "directly visible" in HAADF-STEM (Fig. 2f), and the GPA map has a ±10° color scale (Fig. 2h).
     - A 0.1-0.2° tilt cannot be seen by eye in Fig. 2f.
     - The GPA contrast at the interfaces is of order degrees, which is inconsistent with the RSM-averaged values.
   - What to do: reconcile the STEM/GPA magnitudes with the RSM beta values, or soften the "directly visible" statement.

6. The upper bound on any out-of-plane contribution is not quantified.
   - The H-rotation data (Supp. Figs. 19, 20) argue against out-of-plane M. However, the main-text out-of-plane loop (Fig. 1d) covers only ±2.5 kOe, while saturation is stated at about 4000 Oe. So the full conventional AHE magnitude at Mz saturation is not shown.
   - What to do:
     - Show the out-of-plane rho_AHE to full saturation.
     - Then estimate the easy-axis canting toward [110]_O that would be needed to produce the observed IP-AHE.
     - Compare that canting with the monoclinic tilt and with the measured M-H loops. This would close off the "tilted easy axis" explanation, which is the most natural alternative for a square loop that tracks M.

7. The antisymmetrization formula and the in-plane field misalignment need clarification (Methods; Supp. Fig. 16).
   - The formula R_xy^odd(H) = |R_xy(H) - R_xy(-H)|/2 contains an absolute value, which would remove the sign of the Hall signal.
   - For hysteretic loops, antisymmetrization must pair opposite sweep branches. The planar Hall effect and AMR are even in M, but their contribution through in-plane field misalignment and domain states during switching must be handled branch by branch.
   - What to do:
     - Give the exact procedure used.
     - Show raw rho_xy loops for H along [1-10]_O and along [001]_O.
     - State the in-plane alignment accuracy between H, E and [1-10]_O.

8. The gating claims (Fig. 4, Methods) are stronger than the protocol supports.
   - Each V_g is applied at room temperature, and the sample is then cooled to 10 K. The modulation is therefore a non-volatile, room-temperature ionic reconfiguration, not dynamic electrical control at the operating temperature. The abstract and Discussion wording ("dynamic reconfiguration", "programmable Hall functionalities") should reflect this.
   - Evidence that the mechanism is protonation of CRO is indirect:
     - In-situ XRD is done on separate CRO and LCMO reference films (Supp. Fig. 33), not on the trilayer.
     - There is no direct hydrogen detection (for example SIMS or nuclear reaction analysis).
     - Oxygen-vacancy or electrostatic contributions are not excluded.
   - What to do:
     - Report the number of ON/OFF cycles and the cycle-to-cycle spread (Supp. Fig. 32).
     - Report the gate leakage current.
     - If possible, give a structural signature in the gated trilayer itself.
     - Since H_xCaRuO3 is reported to be ferromagnetic (ref. 48), comment on whether protonated CRO introduces a magnetic contribution.

9. The DFT setup and its comparison with experiment are only qualitative (Fig. 3h, i, Methods).
   - The setup has several choices that need justification or a sensitivity test:
     - The Ca doping is treated in VCA.
     - U = 8 eV and J = 2 eV are taken from LaMnO3.
     - The beta_LC values are imposed by hand in a 2x2x2 cell.
   - The calculated sigma is one to two orders of magnitude larger than the measured value, and this is not discussed.
   - What to do:
     - Show how sigma_AHE-DFT depends on U and on the Fermi-level position.
     - Discuss the magnitude mismatch, including disorder and the shunting in item 3.
     - Clarify how the imposed beta_LC values were chosen and how they relate to the relaxed CRO/LCMO structures in Supp. Fig. 31.

10. The temperature dependence of the IP-AHE and its scaling (Supp. Figs. 24, 25) receive one paragraph.
    - The sign change near 100 K appears only for some tCRO. Because this bears on intrinsic versus extrinsic mechanisms and on which layer carries the signal (item 2), the main text should show sigma_AHE(T) for several tCRO together with M(T).
    - The text also says the IP-AHE vanishes above about 200 K while Tc exceeds 250 K. This should be explained.

C. Minor

11. Units: sigma_AHE is given in mS·cm⁻¹ for experiment and in S·cm⁻¹ for DFT. Use one unit or point out the factor of about 1000 between them.
12. The claim that a zero-field IP-AHE of 103.7 mS/cm "has rarely been seen" in layered magnets with in-plane anisotropy (p. 5) should be backed by a short comparison table with refs. 14-18.
13. Fig. 1e: label the H_S position. Fig. 1d: extend the field range or state the saturation value in the caption.
14. Fig. 3g: define the error bars in the caption, and say whether the 64 u.c. point in Fig. 3g has a matching transport point in Fig. 3f (the sigma panel stops at about 64 with a near-zero value, but this is not stated).
15. Fig. 4d: give the Hc error bars, and state whether the V_g points come from one device measured in sequence or from several devices.
16. Methods: give the Hall-bar dimensions, the excitation current, and the number of devices measured per sample.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a section on magnetic point groups that treats the orthorhombic and monoclinic (110) films side by side, and present the monoclinic distortion as a tuning knob within an already-allowed channel if that is what the analysis shows.
- Separate the layer contributions with a parallel-conduction model and a CRO-free or low-SOC-buffer control, and give the sheet Hall conductance.
- Present the tCRO series with replicate samples and direct structural evidence for the relaxation and twinning at large tCRO.
- Include DFT for the CRO/LCMO superlattice itself, including Ru states, with a U-sensitivity check.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
