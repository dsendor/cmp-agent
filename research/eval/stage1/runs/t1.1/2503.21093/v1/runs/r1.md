Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports that bilayers of ultrathin SrIrO3 (SIO) and SrRuO3 (SRO), 1 to 2 unit cells each, grown on SrTiO3 are metallic and ferromagnetic, although 2-u.c. films of either material alone are insulating. It attributes this to an interfacial magnetic reconstruction driven by a staggered Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI). The evidence is transport, SQUID magnetometry, in-situ ARPES and LSDA+U calculations. Turning two insulators into a metal by interfacial magnetic reconstruction alone would be of broad interest. However, the experiments show only that the bilayers are metallic and ferromagnetic. They do not isolate the proposed mechanism, and persistent metallicity in ultrathin SIO/SRO heterostructures has already been reported and explained by interfacial charge transfer (ref. 29).

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clean, reproducible-looking contrast: the 2-u.c. single films are strongly insulating (Fig. 2A) with suppressed spectral weight at E_F (Fig. 2C,D), while all three bilayers are metallic with a Fermi surface and a quasiparticle peak at X (Fig. 3).
- In-situ ARPES on uncapped films grown in the same chamber, with Fermi surface maps and EDC analysis for three stackings, is an experimentally demanding dataset for 3 to 4 u.c. oxide heterostructures.
- Clear hysteresis loops (Fig. 2B) show that ferromagnetism appears in structures with only 1 to 2 u.c. of SRO, below the reported critical thickness for itinerant ferromagnetism in SRO (ref. 25). If confirmed, this is notable in itself.
- The combination of growth, spectroscopy and first-principles theory on one system is well matched to the question.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The leading alternative explanations for the metallicity are not tested, so the claim that magnetic reconstruction drives the IMT is unsupported.
   - Where: Introduction, Sections B and C, and the Discussion. The Discussion contrasts the mechanism only with polar-discontinuity 2DEGs.
   - Charge transfer: ref. 29 (Nelson et al., Sci. Adv. 2022) studied exactly SIO/SRO heterostructures. It reported interfacial charge transfer from Ir to Ru and persistent metallicity of ultrathin bilayers. The manuscript cites this work only for thickness-dependent IMTs and does not discuss its central result. Charge transfer dopes both layers away from the half-filled Jeff = 1/2 and t2g^4 configurations and can produce metallicity whatever the magnetic order.
   - Total thickness: 1SIO/2SRO is 3 u.c. and 2SIO/2SRO is 4 u.c. of conducting perovskite. This is at or above the single-film critical thicknesses the authors themselves quote ("below a critical thickness of 3 u.c."). The Ir/Ru interface may therefore simply add conducting layers.
   - Intermixing: Ir/Ru interdiffusion over 1 to 2 u.c. would produce a SrIr1-xRuxO3 layer.
   - What is missing: none of these alternatives is excluded experimentally. There are no reference films of matched total thickness (3- and 4-u.c. SRO and SIO). There is no bilayer with an insulating spacer (for example, 1 u.c. STO between SIO and SRO) that would suppress charge transfer and DMI while keeping the layer thicknesses. There is no structural or chemical characterization: no STEM/EELS, XRD or X-ray reflectivity, and the RHEED oscillations and LEED patterns mentioned in Methods are not shown.
   - What to do: add matched-thickness single-film references and a spacer control, show RHEED and LEED data and ideally STEM/EELS of interface sharpness, and quantify Ir/Ru valence (XAS at the Ir L and Ru L/M edges, or the DFT charges/occupations). Then discuss explicitly why charge transfer (ref. 29) cannot account for the result.

2. The theory does not show that the DMI, or the magnetic reconstruction, causes the metallicity.
   - Where: Section D, Fig. 1, Fig. 4, Discussion.
   - Fig. 4C,D gives the DOS of the heterostructures only in the imposed reconstructed state (c-AFM SIO with FM SRO).
   - Missing controls: there is no heterostructure calculation with the magnetic order constrained to the single-film ground states (nc-AFM SIO and c-AFM SRO), or with a nonmagnetic or AFM SRO layer. Without these one cannot tell whether the gap closes because of the magnetic order or because of hybridization and charge transfer at the interface.
   - Missing energetics: no total energies are given to show that the FM-SRO/c-AFM-SIO configuration is the ground state of the bilayer. The DMI vector and its magnitude relative to the Ru-Ru, Ir-Ir and Ru-Ir exchange are not extracted. The "effective model Hamiltonian" is not written down and its parameters are not given.
   - Why the DMI argument needs these numbers: DMI is normally a small, relativistic correction to exchange that cants moments. The claim that it converts AFM SRO into FM SRO needs a quantitative D/J comparison. The model also has to show why it gives collinear FM rather than a canted or spiral state; Section D itself says the DMI "favors a noncollinear magnetic phase between SIO and SRO". Staggered DMI is also present in bulk Pbnm perovskites (ref. 30), so the specifically interfacial term should be identified.
   - What to do:
     - Give total energies for the competing magnetic configurations in each bilayer.
     - Give the DOS (layer- and element-resolved) for the unreconstructed configurations.
     - Give the extracted exchange and DMI parameters and the model Hamiltonian with its solution.
     - Ideally, show a calculation or model in which the DMI is removed and the reconstruction, and with it the metallicity, disappears.

3. The magnetic states, both before and after reconstruction, are asserted rather than measured.
   - Where: Section B, Fig. 2B, Fig. 1.
   - Reference films: the AFM order of the 2-u.c. single films is taken from DFT and the literature. No magnetization data are shown for 2SIO or 2SRO. Ultrathin SRO on STO is often reported as nonmagnetic or weakly ferromagnetic rather than collinear AFM, so a measured reference matters.
   - Bilayers: the evidence is M(H) at a single temperature, 10 K, along one field direction. There is no M(T) to establish Tc, and no link is shown between the ρ(T) kinks at 102 to 123 K and a magnetic transition.
   - Which layer is magnetic: nothing is element-specific. The proposed configuration (FM Ru, c-AFM Ir, roughly 90° between Ru and Ir moments, Fig. 1E) is not tested, and Ir may contribute to the 0.6 to 1 µB per Ru moment.
   - Normalization: it is not explained how the STO substrate and cap diamagnetic background was removed, or how the moment was normalized per Ru for 1 u.c. of SRO. A value of 1 µB/Ru in 2 u.c. of SRO is comparable to thick-film values and needs a stated uncertainty.
   - What to do:
     - Add M(T) for all bilayers and the reference films.
     - Add the in-plane versus out-of-plane loops that support the stated easy axis.
     - Add the anomalous Hall effect, which would directly tie the ferromagnetism to the conducting layer.
     - Ideally, add XMCD at the Ru and Ir edges to establish which sublattice carries the net moment and its orientation.

B. Should address

4. The ARPES labels and the effective-mass argument are internally inconsistent.
   - Labels: in Fig. 3 the row labels are A = 2SIO/1SRO and B = 1SIO/2SRO, but the caption says (A) 1SIO/2SRO and (B) 2SIO/1SRO.
   - Effective-mass logic: the text says m* "decreases from 0.57 m_e for 2SIO/2SRO to 0.5 and 0.41 m_e for 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/1SRO", "further confirming the enhanced metallicity with increased total thickness". But the thickest structure (2SIO/2SRO) has the largest m*, and transport and coherent weight both suggest it is the most metallic. The trend runs against the stated conclusion.
   - Coherent weight: the claimed increase from 2SIO/1SRO to 1SIO/2SRO is not evident in Fig. 3A.iii versus B.iii.
   - Effective-mass fits: the band is hole-like yet called an "electron effective mass". The parabolic fits in Fig. 3D extrapolate a band maximum about 0.1 eV above E_F from data at or below about -0.05 eV. The m* differences (0.41 to 0.57) are given without uncertainties or a fit range.
   - Band origin: the orbital and layer origin of the X-point pocket is not assigned, and it is not compared with the calculated bands or with the Fermi surfaces in ref. 29.
   - What to do: correct the labels, give error bars and fit windows, revise the interpretation, and compare with DFT band structures (unfolded to the measured zone).

5. The transport data need more detail and more samples.
   - Upturn: Fig. 2A shows that 2SIO/1SRO has a resistivity minimum and an upturn below roughly 40 to 50 K, yet the text says all heterostructures are metallic. This should be addressed, for example by field dependence to separate weak localization from Kondo-like behaviour.
   - Kinks: the "kinks" at 102 to 123 K are not visible on the plotted scale. Show dρ/dT and state which kink belongs to which sample.
   - Sheet resistance: the thickness used to convert to ρ is not stated. Sheet resistance compared with h/e^2 would be more meaningful for 3- to 4-u.c. structures.
   - Temperature range: the reference films are plotted only above about 50 to 70 K.
   - Samples: one sample per stacking appears to have been measured. Show reproducibility over at least two samples of each bilayer.

6. The transport/magnetometry samples and the ARPES samples differ, as does the theory geometry.
   - Transport and SQUID samples carry a 15-u.c. STO cap and were annealed after capping. ARPES samples are bare, and the DFT uses an SrO-terminated slab facing vacuum.
   - Ultrathin SRO and SIO are known to be sensitive to termination and capping (ref. 28). The insulating behaviour of the capped single films, and the metallic state of the uncapped bilayers, should therefore be shown under matched conditions. At minimum, give ARPES-equivalent reference and capped transport references, and state explicitly that the 2SIO and 2SRO transport films were capped identically.

7. The DFT settings and coverage need justification.
   - The Hubbard U and J_H: U = 5 eV with J_H = 0.15U on the Ir 5d and Ru 4d shells is far above the values usually used for these compounds (about 1.5 to 3 eV). Section A says a range of U_Ir and U_Ru was studied, but no results are shown. Report the U dependence of the gap and the magnetic ground state for the films and bilayers.
   - Methods text: Methods states "Ru, Ir, and Ti 3d orbitals", which is incorrect for Ru and Ir.
   - Coverage: 2SIO/2SRO, the main experimental sample, is not shown in Fig. 4.
   - Fig. 4B: the 2SRO DOS has a large d_xy peak within about 0.1 eV of E_F. The size of the gap should be stated, given the claim of a "clear gap".
   - Resolution: layer-resolved DOS (interfacial versus surface Ir/Ru) is needed to show where the metallic states reside.
   - Fig. 1B, D, F are schematic and should be clearly labelled as such, separately from computed results.

8. The literature on DMI and topological Hall effects at SRO/SIO interfaces is missing.
   - Interfacial DMI at SrRuO3/SrIrO3 interfaces has been studied extensively through topological-Hall-like signals (for example, the Matsuno et al. Sci. Adv. 2016 bilayers and follow-up work).
   - Together with ref. 29, this should be discussed so that what is new here is clear: the metallicity of the bilayer, its magnetic origin, or the DMI-driven FM conversion.

9. The growth order and Ru stability are not addressed.
   - Ordering: SRO was grown first and SIO on top. Whether the reversed stacking (SIO/SRO/STO versus SRO/SIO/STO) gives the same result would test the symmetry-breaking argument.
   - Ru loss: Ru loss as volatile RuO_x during SIO growth and the 30-min anneal at 650 °C could reduce the actual SRO thickness, particularly for the 1-u.c. layer.
   - What to do: show RHEED oscillations for each layer and an independent thickness check.

C. Minor

10. Fig. 4 caption vs text: the caption says the staggered DMI "stabilizes non-collinear spin orientation between Ir and Ru", while the Discussion says it favours collinear FM SRO and c-AFM SIO. Clarify the distinction between intralayer collinearity and interlayer noncollinearity consistently throughout.
11. Section A title: the title "Engineering Ferromagnetic Metallicity ... via Interfacial DMI" presents the conclusion before the evidence. Section A also contains no data, only a description of the mechanism.
12. Missing data and loose references in Methods:
   - LEED patterns are mentioned in Methods but not shown.
   - The Shanghai Dreamline beamtime is acknowledged but no data from it appear.
   - "All data are available in the main text" does not cover the DFT structures and inputs.
13. Typos and reference errors:
   - "Temprature" (Fig. 2A axis).
   - "Our analysis support"; "associated with to octahedral tilting".
   - Ref. 9: "spi-orbit".
   - Ref. 45: year should be 2017.
   - Ref. 48: authors given as "K. G and F. J".
   - Ref. 28: "ttermination".
14. Fig. 2C,D normalization: state how the valence-band spectra were normalized, and the energy resolution and E_F reference.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a spacer-layer control (SIO/1-u.c. STO/SRO) and matched-thickness single films, together with XAS/XMCD at the Ir and Ru edges. Together these would separate magnetic reconstruction from charge transfer and dimensionality.
2. Add M(T) and anomalous Hall measurements on the same capped samples, and show dρ/dT, to connect the 100 to 120 K features to a ferromagnetic transition in the conducting layer.
3. Provide a quantitative theory section:
   - total energies of the competing magnetic states;
   - extracted J and D parameters;
   - the DOS for the unreconstructed configurations;
   - U dependence;
   - a calculated band structure compared directly with the ARPES Fermi surface at X.
4. Reframe the comparison with ref. 29 and the existing SRO/SIO DMI literature, so that the specific new claim is stated precisely and defended.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
