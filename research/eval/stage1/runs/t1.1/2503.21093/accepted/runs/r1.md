Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports that SrIrO3/SrRuO3 (SIO/SRO) bilayers built from layers that are insulating on their own (1-2 u.c. each, on SrTiO3) are metallic and show ferromagnetic hysteresis. It supports this with transport, SQUID magnetometry, in-situ ARPES (a Fermi surface with a hole pocket at X, coherent quasiparticle weight) and LSDA+U calculations. It proposes that a staggered interfacial Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI) reorganises the magnetism (SRO from AFM to FM, SIO from noncollinear to collinear AFM) and that this drives the metallicity. Metallicity at this interface was already reported by Nelson et al. (Sci. Adv. 2022), with a metallic SRO layer. What is new here is that both constituent layers are insulating, the in-situ ARPES on the bilayers, and the DMI-based magnetic mechanism. Interest in the paper depends on how firmly the metallicity is tied to the interface and to the magnetic reconstruction, rather than to total thickness or to charge transfer.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear and reproducible-looking phenomenon: three bilayer stackings (2SIO/1SRO, 1SIO/2SRO, 2SIO/2SRO) are all metallic from 10 to 300 K. The 2-u.c. single-layer films are insulating (Fig. 2A).
- Several probes agree: transport, M-H loops with remanence (Fig. 2B), and in-situ ARPES valence-band and Fermi-surface data (Figs. 2C-D, 3) taken on the same growth system without exposing the sample to air.
- The ARPES is of good quality for 3-4 u.c. films: a closed Fermi contour, a dispersing band crossing E_F, and EDCs that are analysed quantitatively (f_coh).
- It links to current interest in interfacial DMI and chiral magnetism in oxide spin-orbitronics, and offers a concrete microscopic model (Fig. 1, Fig. 4).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. No control separates the interface effect from a total-thickness effect. The paper states (p. 2) that "below a critical thickness of 3 u.c., both SRO and SIO films are insulating", and the literature it cites (refs. 20, 32-34) puts the metal/FM threshold of SRO on STO at about 2-4 u.c. Yet the metallic samples are 3 u.c. (2SIO/1SRO, 1SIO/2SRO) and 4 u.c. (2SIO/2SRO) thick, and the only insulating references are 2 u.c. films. The trend the authors themselves stress, that metallicity improves with SRO thickness and total thickness (f_coh, ρ), is also what a dimensional-crossover picture predicts. Without references of matched total thickness, the central claim that metallicity "emerges from insulating constituent layers" through an interfacial mechanism is not established. The authors should show ρ(T), M-H and, ideally, ARPES under identical growth, capping and measurement conditions for:
   - 3 u.c. and 4 u.c. SRO alone;
   - 3 u.c. and 4 u.c. SIO alone;
   - a non-SOC spacer bilayer such as 2 u.c. SRO/1-2 u.c. SrTiO3 (or SrZrO3), which gives the same layer thickness and capping without an Ir-Ru interface.

   If the 3-4 u.c. single films are insulating and nonmagnetic while the bilayers are not, the claim stands. If not, it must be reformulated.

2. The data do not show that metallicity is caused by the magnetic reconstruction. The text says metallic weight appears at E_F "only when ferromagnetism is induced" (p. 2) and that the metallicity is "a direct consequence of this DMI-associated magnetic reconstruction" (p. 2, Fig. 1 caption). But Fig. 2A shows all three bilayers metallic (dρ/dT > 0) up to 300 K, far above the Curie temperatures of about 102-123 K identified from the ρ(T) kinks and M(T). The paramagnetic state of the bilayers is therefore already metallic, which argues against magnetic order as the origin of the metallicity, at least in the simple form stated. On the theory side, Fig. 4 compares DOS only for the assumed magnetic ground states. It never asks whether the bilayer is metallic in DFT for:
   - a nonmagnetic configuration;
   - an AFM-SRO/nc-AFM-SIO configuration;
   - the same configuration with the DMI or SOC switched off.

   Those comparisons are what would isolate the role of the DMI. The authors should:
   - discuss the metallic paramagnetic regime explicitly;
   - provide the DFT/model comparisons above, which show whether the gap closes because of the magnetic configuration or because of interface hybridisation and charge redistribution;
   - tone down "direct consequence" in the main text and Fig. 1 caption to match the more cautious abstract and Discussion ("consistent with", "plausible").

B. Should address

3. Charge transfer and intermixing are set aside without the authors' own measurements. The Discussion argues against a charge-transfer-only picture by citing a band-shift study on a different heterostructure (ref. 31), and against intermixing by citing RHEED oscillations (Figs. S1-S2). For 1-2 u.c. layers, a few percent of Ir/Ru exchange or a fractional electron transfer (as in Nelson et al.) could account for both metallicity and ferromagnetism, since Sr(Ru,Ir)O3 alloys are themselves metallic and ferromagnetic over wide ranges. The authors should add at least one of the following:
   - atomically resolved STEM-EELS/EDS across the interface;
   - Ir 4f / Ru 3d core-level XPS or Ir L / Ru L/M-edge XAS on bilayers versus single films, to quantify valence changes.

   They should also estimate the DMI energy scale against the charge-transfer energy scale.

4. There is no layer-resolved magnetic evidence for the proposed spin configuration. The magnetometry (Fig. 2B) is integral and is normalised per Ru (0.6 and 1 μB/Ru), which assumes the whole moment sits on Ru. The model's key predictions are FM SRO, collinear AFM SIO, and a chiral component perpendicular to D. Neither magnetometry nor ARPES tests these. Element-specific XMCD at the Ir L2,3 and Ru edges would show whether Ir carries a net or canted moment and would justify the per-Ru normalisation. If XMCD is not possible, the authors should state clearly that the SIO c-AFM state and the DMI chirality are model inferences only, and give the normalisation used if Ir moments are allowed. The 0.6 μB/Ru for a single Ru layer in 2SIO/1SRO is large compared with the reduced moments usually found in few-u.c. SRO, so it deserves comment.

5. The magnetometry and transport details are too thin for ultrathin films. A 1-2 u.c. film on a 0.5 mm STO substrate gives a tiny moment against a large diamagnetic and paramagnetic background. Please state:
   - how the substrate and cap background was subtracted;
   - the sample area and thickness used to convert to μB/Ru;
   - whether a bare STO + 15 u.c. STO cap reference was measured.

   For transport, ρ is quoted in mΩ cm, which requires a conducting thickness. Please state which thickness was used, give sheet resistance (Ω/sq), and include a capped STO-only reference annealed identically, to exclude a parallel channel from oxygen vacancies in the substrate or cap. The ρ(T) kinks at 102-123 K that are used to identify T_C are not visible on the scale of Fig. 2A. Please show dρ/dT or an expanded view, and the M(T) data, alongside them.

6. Only one sample per stacking seems to be shown, and the samples behind each probe differ. There is no statement of how many samples per stacking were grown and measured, or of sample-to-sample spread in ρ(10 K), T_C and M_s. Transport and SQUID were measured on STO-capped samples ex situ, ARPES on uncapped samples in situ. Please report reproducibility and comment on whether the cap changes the boundary conditions (it creates a second interface on the SRO or SIO side) in a way that affects the comparison with ARPES and with the slab calculations, which use vacuum.

7. The ARPES layer attribution and the comparison with theory are missing. The top layer is SIO, and at hν = 82 eV the probing depth is comparable to 1-2 u.c. The hole pocket at X and the coherent peak may therefore come mainly from the SIO layer or from the interface. The paper does not say which layer or orbital (Jeff = 1/2 versus Ru t2g) the observed band derives from. It also does not compare the measured dispersion and Fermi surface with the DFT band structure of the proposed magnetic state, so the "supported by first-principles calculations" link is qualitative only (DOS at E_F, Fig. 4). The authors should:
   - overlay or compare the calculated bands (with the 2×2 reconstruction) with Fig. 3;
   - use photon-energy or polarisation dependence to assign the orbital character;
   - discuss why circular polarisation does not introduce dichroic intensity asymmetries that bias the MDC/EDC fits.

8. The effective-mass trend is read in the wrong direction, and the parabolic fit is not well constrained. The text says m* "decreases from 0.57 m_e for 2SIO/2SRO to 0.5 m_e ... and 0.41 m_e for 2SIO/1SRO, further confirming the enhanced metallicity with increased total thickness" (p. 3). The thickest and most coherent sample (f_coh = 55%) has the heaviest mass, so the m* trend does not straightforwardly support increased metallicity and may point to stronger correlations. In addition, the hole band maximum in Fig. 3D lies about 0.1 eV above E_F, so the parabola apex is extrapolated from data below E_F over a narrow k window. Please give fit uncertainties on m* and on the band top, state the k/energy range fitted, and revise the interpretation.

9. The f_coh decomposition is model dependent. The split into coherent and incoherent parts (Fig. 3 A-C iii) depends on the line shapes chosen, the Fermi-function and resolution convolution, and the background. Please specify these, give uncertainties on f_coh, and explain whether the small difference between 26% and 28% is significant.

10. The DFT parameter choice may be driving the insulating and metallic outcomes. The Methods use U = 5 eV, J_H = 0.15U for Ru, Ir and Ti. That is large for 4d/5d oxides, where 1-3 eV is common, and the insulating 2-u.c. films and the metallic bilayers are both U-dependent results. The text mentions a "range of correlation strengths" but the main text shows no U-scan. SOC is excluded from the structural relaxation even though the DMI depends on the octahedral rotations. Please show:
   - the main-text conclusion (gap in single films, metallicity in bilayers, ground-state ordering) as a function of U_Ir and U_Ru;
   - energy differences between the competing magnetic configurations and their sensitivity to U;
   - how the DMI vector magnitude in the effective model was obtained (computed from DFT or fitted), and its size relative to the exchange.

C. Minor

11. The Fig. 3 caption lists "(A) 1SIO/2SRO, (B) 2SIO/1SRO, and (C) 2SIO/2SRO", but the panel labels read A = 2SIO/1SRO and B = 1SIO/2SRO. Please make them consistent.

12. The PMA claim rests on "pronounced squareness" of the out-of-plane loops, but the loops in Fig. 2B are noticeably sheared, with saturation near 2-3 T. Please show the in-plane M-H for comparison in the main text, or soften the wording. Fig. 2B has no legend identifying which curves belong to which stacking, and the single-film M-H data cited in the text are not shown.

13. "Temprature" is misspelled in the Fig. 2A axis label. The broken ρ axis in Fig. 2A makes the relative resistivities of the bilayers hard to read; a log scale would help.

14. In the Discussion, "metal-insulator transition (mit)" and "IMT" are used inconsistently, and "undelaying" should be "underlying".

15. Fig. 4: please mark the gap size for the 2SIO and 2SRO panels, and say which spin channel is plotted, since the legends show both arrows.

16. The data availability statement ("All data are available in the main text or the supplementary materials") does not cover the raw ARPES, transport and magnetometry data. A repository deposit would help.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a thickness-matched control series (3 and 4 u.c. SRO and SIO single films, plus an SRO/STO bilayer). This single addition would turn the "interfacial" attribution from plausible into demonstrated.
- Measure element-specific XMCD and XAS at the Ir and Ru edges on bilayers versus single films. This would test both the proposed FM-SRO / AFM-SIO configuration and the size of the charge transfer.
- In DFT, compare the bilayer DOS across magnetic configurations and with SOC/DMI switched off, and across U. This shows directly whether the magnetic reconstruction is what closes the gap.
- Address the metallicity above T_C explicitly, for example with ARPES or transport analysis above and below T_C, to clarify whether magnetic order changes the electronic structure (band splitting, coherence) or only coexists with an already metallic interface.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
