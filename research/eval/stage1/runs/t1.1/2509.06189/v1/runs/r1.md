Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports electric-field control of polar vortex regions in PbTiO3/SrTiO3 trilayers (STO20/PTO20/STO20) and [PTO16/STO12]10 superlattices on DyScO3. Using a PFM tip dragged along predefined paths ("trailing bias"), the authors move a "vortex boundary" by about 1 µm and move it back with the opposite polarity. The written states persist for up to 120 days. In-situ STEM biasing shows similar boundary motion, local voltage pulses produce stochastic rearrangements and closed "vortex ring" features, and phase-field simulations attribute the boundary motion to switching of the zigzag arrangement of vortex cores. Deterministic, reversible and non-volatile electrical control of polar vortex textures would interest the polar-topology community. However, what the PFM contrast shows before and after writing is not established. Electric-field manipulation of vortices in the same system has also been reported before (refs. 18, 5 and 23 of the manuscript). Together these leave the advance over ordinary tip-induced domain writing unclear in this version.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Well-grown samples in two geometries (trilayer and superlattice), characterized by XRD, RSM, cross-sectional and plane-view STEM, and PFM, consistent with established vortex phases.
- Reversible, polarity-dependent displacement of a mesoscale boundary over about 1 µm, with written states stable for 2 to 120 days (Fig. 4a-f), which is relevant to non-volatile devices.
- Several techniques point the same way: PFM (Figs. 2-4), in-situ biasing STEM in cross-section (Fig. 2j) and plane view (Figs. 3g, 4g), and phase-field simulations (Figs. 2k-p, 3h).
- At the atomic scale, Fig. 2j directly images a change in vortex-core registry, from zigzag to aligned, together with a moving boundary between the two arrangements. This is the most convincing microscopic evidence in the paper.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The PFM data do not show what state the "moved" region is in, so they cannot separate vortex-boundary motion from ordinary tip-induced switching into a ferroelectric (a1/a2 or c) state.**
   - **Resolution.** The vortex period is about 14 nm (trilayer) and about 10 nm (superlattice), as given on p. 4 from RSM. The PFM tip radius is 25 nm (Methods), so lateral PFM cannot resolve individual vortices. The "vortices" outlined by white dashed lines in Figs. 1c-d, 2a and 3a are features hundreds of nanometres wide.
   - **What changes after writing.** After CHP1-CHP6 (Fig. 2b-e) and in Fig. 4b, the region swept by the tip becomes a uniform, featureless strip in both LPFM amplitude and phase, about 1 µm wide. This is the signature of an area poled into a single in-plane polarization state. Such a state could be an a-domain, a net in-plane polarized "FE" phase (which the XRD in Supplementary Fig. 2a shows coexists with the vortex phase), or a vortex array with a net lateral polar component.
   - **What would settle it.** The central claim, that vortices move, rests on showing that the swept region still contains vortices, and of what kind. The authors should provide:
     - (i) cross-sectional STEM, or at least DF-TEM, of a lamella cut across a PFM-written strip and its boundary;
     - (ii) vertical PFM together with LPFM at two sample rotations (0° and 90°), to determine the polarization direction in the written region;
     - (iii) if possible, local diffraction (nano-beam or micro-XRD/RSM) on a large written area, showing whether the vortex satellite peaks survive.
   - **Definition.** The text should also define precisely what "vortex boundary" means in PFM. It could mean a boundary between vortex arrays of different orientation, of different chirality or net lateral polarization, or between vortex and FE phases.

2. **The claimed reversibility and the claimed restoration to the original position are not supported quantitatively, and the numbers in the figures, captions and text disagree.**
   - **Trilayer.** The rightward displacement is about 1170 nm after CHP6, but the leftward displacement is about 890 nm after CHN5 (p. 7). Yet the text says this "effectively move[s] the boundary to its original position" (Fig. 2i).
   - **Superlattice.** The Fig. 3 caption gives about 1170 nm rightward (the same number as the trilayer) and about 643 nm leftward. It also says the boundary "became disrupted" after CHN3, and the main text mentions "disruptions observed after several field cycles".
   - **Channel labels.** The channel labels disagree between text, captions and panels:
     - Fig. 2: the text lists CHP1, 3, 5, 6; the caption lists CHP2 and CHP3 for panel b; the panels show CHP1, CHP2 and CHP4.
     - Fig. 3: the text lists CHP5 and CHP8; the caption lists CHP2-CHP7; the panels show CHP4.
   - **Missing data.** No plot of boundary position against step number or against tip-path position is given. The pristine and restored images are not overlaid or difference-mapped, and only one forward-reverse cycle is shown per sample.
   - **What to provide.** Since "reversible" and "deterministic" motion are headline claims, the authors should:
     - plot boundary position against channel index for each forward and reverse sequence;
     - state how the boundary position was measured, with an uncertainty;
     - show at least several full cycles, in more than one region and sample;
     - reconcile all channel labels and displacement values.

3. **The identification of "vortex rings", "pairs of rings" and "hidden topological phases" (Fig. 5, Conclusion) is not supported.**
   - **PFM evidence.** In Fig. 5b-l, a "ring" is a closed contour in LPFM amplitude a few hundred nanometres across. That is far larger than a single vortex and is unresolved at the 25-nm tip radius. A closed loop of low amplitude is also what a tip-written bubble domain, a ring of domain walls around a poled spot, or a circular a1/a2 rearrangement would produce.
   - **STEM evidence.** The plane-view images in Fig. 5o-q show a boxed region with bright spots and some curvature of the stripe contrast. The polarization texture that would make these "rings" (closed vortex tubes, or toroidal loops with a defined topology) is not shown.
   - **Simulations.** No phase-field simulation of the pulse experiment is presented.
   - **What to provide.** The authors should either:
     - show the polarization texture of a ring, from 3D phase-field simulations of the point-pulse protocol together with polarization mapping (atomic-displacement or DPC) of a ring in STEM; or
     - describe these features in neutral terms (closed domain-wall loops) and remove "hidden topological phases" from the Conclusion.

B. Should address

4. **The "real-time observation" claim in the abstract and the "first time" claim in the Conclusion are overstated relative to the data and the literature.**
   - **Real time.** All PFM data are taken after the field is removed (Methods: "After the application of bias voltage, area has been scanned"). They are therefore before-and-after snapshots, not real-time observations. Only the in-situ STEM is close to real time, and it is shown as three or four frames with no time stamps or frame rate.
   - **Prior work.** Ref. 18 (Chen et al., Sci. China 2022) already reports electrically driven motion, destruction and chirality change of polar vortices in PTO/STO superlattices. Refs. 5 and 23 report field switching of the vortex phase.
   - **What to do.** The authors should state concretely what is new here: the trailing-field geometry, the reversibility, the retention, or the atomic-scale registry change. They should add in-situ STEM movies or time series with timestamps if they are available.

5. **The electric field and the possible artifacts in the in-situ STEM experiments are not characterized.**
   - **Field.** Voltages of +15, +17 and +20 V are applied with a tungsten tip to FIB lamellae (Figs. 2j, 3g, 4g). The field in the PTO layer, the tip-sample contact (touching or not), the leakage current, and possible Joule heating or electron-beam effects are not given.
   - **Geometry.** In cross-section (Fig. 2j), the tip position relative to the 20-uc PTO layer, and hence whether the field is in-plane or out-of-plane, is not stated.
   - **Missing control.** No beam-only control is shown, that is, the same imaging dose with no bias applied.
   - **What to provide.** The authors should give the I-V characteristics, an estimate of the field from the geometry, and a zero-bias imaging control under the same dose, so that the registry change can be attributed to the field rather than to beam or thermal effects.

6. **The switching-spectroscopy evidence for a lower coercive bias at vortex boundaries (Fig. 1e-g) is weak.**
   - **Data.** The loops in Fig. 1g come from 10 hand-picked points. The difference between "boundary" and "core" loops is small, especially in the trilayer, and no error bars or spread are shown. Fig. 1e-f are noisy maps with saturated colour scales.
   - **Definitions.** At PFM resolution, the "vortex core" and "vortex boundary" pixels refer to mesoscale contrast features, not to individual vortices.
   - **Artifacts.** Electrostatic and contact-potential contributions to off-field SS-PFM loops are not addressed.
   - **What to provide.** The authors should give histograms of Vc for pixels classified as boundary or interior, a description of how pixels were classified, and a statistical test. They should also temper "significantly reduced" accordingly.

7. **There is no voltage, speed or threshold dependence for the trailing-field motion.**
   - **Current data.** All trailing-field experiments use ±5 V at about 60 nm/s for one minute.
   - **Missing measurements.** Two things would test the proposed energy-barrier picture (Supplementary Fig. 8) and distinguish boundary depinning from local poling:
     - (a) the threshold voltage below which the boundary does not move;
     - (b) how the displacement per pass depends on voltage, tip speed and distance from the boundary.
   - **Mechanism.** Displacements of hundreds of nanometres per channel are much larger than the tip radius. The text calls this "long-range" and "nonlocal" but does not explain it. Surface charge spreading or the tip-path spacing could be a simple explanation, and should be checked (for example by scanning the same channel twice and by varying the channel spacing).

8. **The comparison between phase-field simulations and experiment is qualitative.**
   - **Scale and inputs.** The simulated cells are 400×200 and 200×200 unit cells, roughly 80-160 nm across. The experiments involve micrometre displacements. The applied field magnitude, the channel geometry, and the parameters of the Lorentzian tip potential are not given, and the kinetic coefficient is not specified.
   - **Internal inconsistency.** The Fig. 2 caption (panel p) says the boundary "returns to a configuration similar to the initial state" upon field removal. The main text says boundaries "maintain their displaced positions" after the field is removed.
   - **What to provide.** The authors should report the simulation fields in physical units, compare them with the experimental tip fields, and clarify what panel p shows.
   - **Energy profile.** The interpretation of Supplementary Fig. 8 (an energy maximum when the field switches sign) is not tied to any measured quantity.

9. **Reproducibility across samples and regions is not documented.**
   - **What is shown.** Apart from the second trilayer region in Fig. 4a-b, it is not stated how many samples, regions and tips were used. It is also not stated how often trailing-field writing failed or gave irregular motion.
   - **Tip condition.** Tip wear or contamination after many ±5-7 V passes could change the effective field. The amplitude colour scales differ widely between panels (for example 55 pm in Fig. 3f against 245 pm in Fig. 3b), which may indicate changes in tip condition.
   - **What to report.** The number of samples, regions and successful attempts, and whether the tip was changed or checked between sequences.

10. **The claim that the pulse experiments show "stochastic" vortex motion rests on a single voltage sequence (Fig. 5).**
    - **What the data are.** Figs. 5m-n plot two tracked features against voltage, but only up to 4 V, while the experiment goes to 7 V. There is one realization at one site.
    - **Problem.** Calling the behaviour stochastic requires repeated runs under identical pulses, showing different outcomes. A single history-dependent sequence cannot separate stochastic from deterministic but history-dependent behaviour.
    - **What to do.** Repeat the same pulse at the same and at equivalent sites, or soften the claim.

11. **Topography and the surface after writing are not shown.**
    - **Possible effects.** Biases of ±5-7 V with a Pt/Cr tip in air can cause tip-induced electrochemistry, surface charge deposition, or surface damage. Each of these can change the LPFM contrast and persist for long times.
    - **What to provide.** Topography images before and after writing, and Kelvin-probe or surface-potential maps if possible. These would rule out the possibility that the uniform written strip is partly a surface or charge artifact.

C. Minor

12. **Figure and caption inconsistencies.**
    - Fig. 4: the caption says "f, after 60 days", but the panel and text say 120 days. The caption also skips panel b.
    - Fig. 1a: the caption says [100]pc and [001]pc, but the text and the panel say [100]pc and [010]pc.
    - Fig. 1: the text cites "Fig. 1g" for the trilayer phase-field simulation, but the simulations are in panels h-i, and the caption says "g-i".
    - Fig. 3g: the caption refers to (100)pc, (010)pc and (001)pc directions where crystallographic directions with square brackets are meant.

13. **Supplementary figure cross-references disagree.**
    - The Fig. 2 caption points to Supplementary Figs. 3 and 4 for the step details, while the text points to Supplementary Figs. 3, 4-5.
    - The text on p. 10 cites "Supplementary Fig. 10j-k" and "10l" for the pulse sequence, while Supplementary Fig. 10 is said to be the stability data and Fig. 11 the pulse data.

14. **Methods omissions.**
    - The channel spacing and length are not given, and the "lithographically defined channel" is not explained. It appears to be a software-defined tip path, not physical lithography, and should be stated as such.
    - The DART drive amplitude varies (0.6-1.25 V) across scans. Since the claims rest on amplitude contrast, the authors should state whether the drive was kept the same within a sequence.
    - The ambient humidity and temperature during PFM writing should be given.

15. **Language.** The word "harness" is used throughout in place of "move", "control" or "drive" ("harnessing with leftward displacement"), which obscures the meaning. The manuscript also has many typos: "staies", "revel", "Brucker", "piezoresponce", "underline physical mechanism".

16. **Abstract and introduction.** Statements about memory density (60 Tb/inch²), relevance to large language models, and sub-THz processing are not tested here and should be removed or toned down.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Cut a FIB cross-section across one PFM-written strip and its edge, and image it with STEM and DF-TEM, ideally with polarization mapping. This single experiment would establish what the trailing field does to the vortex lattice and would connect the micrometre-scale PFM data to the atomic-scale registry change in Fig. 2j.
2. Present boundary position against channel index for repeated forward-reverse cycles in several regions, with uncertainties. Add a voltage and tip-speed series to find the threshold for motion.
3. Run phase-field simulations of the point-pulse protocol, and use them, together with STEM polarization maps, to decide whether the closed loops in Fig. 5 are topologically distinct objects.
4. Restructure the text so the novel result (reversible, non-volatile, polarity-controlled motion of a vortex-registry boundary) is stated precisely and set against refs. 5, 18 and 23. Reserve "real time" for the in-situ STEM data.
