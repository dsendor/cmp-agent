Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors grew mm-sized CuAlO2 single crystals and used them to measure the ab-plane and c-axis dielectric functions separately by spectroscopic ellipsometry on the top (001) and side (100) faces. They report a very large in-plane/out-of-plane contrast from 0.74 to 6.46 eV, with |Δn|max = 3.67 and |Δk|max = 5.21 at the 3.78 eV exciton. They attribute it to a quasi-two-dimensional Cu d_z2 → p_x/y exciton confined to the Cu+ layers. A direct, face-resolved measurement of the anisotropic optical constants of a delafossite is new and useful, because earlier work relied on films, powders or anisotropic model fits. The physical picture, a strongly bound exciton with in-plane polarization in the Cu layers, was already put forward by earlier GW-BSE work (refs. 12, 13, 48). The novelty therefore lies mainly in the experimental data. In their present form, the "transparent across the visible" and "two-dimensionally confined exciton" claims go beyond what the data show.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The crystals are large enough to measure both principal components on natural faces of the same material. Single-crystal XRD (Fig. S2, Table S1) confirms the R-3m delafossite structure.
- The qualitative result is robust and striking: an intense, sharp (about 200 meV at 300 K, about 120 meV at 7 K) exciton at 3.78 eV appears only for E ∥ ab (Fig. 2b; raw Ψ, Δ in Fig. S4), while ε_c2 is nearly featureless below 5 eV.
- The temperature-dependent ε_ab2 (Fig. S8) and the combined ε1/ε2 fits (Fig. S9) give a consistent description of the main resonance.
- The DFT orbital projections (Fig. 5) give a clear, intuitive selection-rule picture of why c-polarized light couples weakly to the lowest transition.
- If properly qualified, the off-resonance birefringence of about 0.5–0.7 in the red/near-IR, rising toward the UV, is a useful figure of merit for a wide-gap oxide.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The claim of "transparency across the visible spectrum" is contradicted by the paper's own absorption data.
   - Where: Abstract, Introduction, Results p. 4, Fig. 1c, Fig. 3, Supplementary Note 3.
   - What the data show: Fig. S6 gives ln α ≈ 10.5–11.8 between 2 and 3.3 eV, i.e. α ≈ 4×10^4 to 1.3×10^5 cm^-1, and Fig. 2f is consistent with this. That is an absorption length of about 0.1–0.3 µm across the visible. The crystals are black (Fig. 1b, Fig. S1), which the caption attributes to about 1.4% Cu2+.
   - The definition used: the manuscript calls the "transparent region" the Urbach tail "where defect-induced absorption occurs". This is not a definition of transparency that a photonics reader would recognize, and it is not the criterion applied to the comparison materials in Fig. 3, whose values come from transmission-grade crystals.
   - The reasoning is also circular. Calling the visible absorption extrinsic (from Cu2+) does not show that intrinsic CuAlO2 has the same Δn, because the same defects may change n_ab and n_c differently.
   - No transmission data are shown. The statement in Fig. 1c that c-polarized light "is transmitted through the ac-plane (Left panel of Fig. 1b)" is not supported by any measurement.
   - What to do: (i) Measure polarized transmission (and ideally prism-coupling or interferometric Δn) on a thinned, polished c-cut and a-cut plate, or on crystals with reduced Cu2+ (for example after reducing anneals), and report α(ω) for both polarizations in the visible. (ii) Otherwise, restate the claim as birefringence below the intrinsic absorption edge, give the measured extrinsic absorption explicitly, and remove "transparency" from the abstract, title-level claims and Fig. 3.

2. The electronic gap Eg = 5.12 eV, and hence Eb = 1.34 eV and the "1/4 scaling" signature of two-dimensionality, rest on a single-oscillator (Penn) fit that does not measure a band gap.
   - Where: Discussion p. 4–5, Fig. 4a, Supplementary Note 4, Fig. S7.
   - Why the fit cannot give a gap: Eq. (1) is fitted to ε_ab1 between 1 and 2.25 eV, where ε1 rises only from about 5.5 to 6.5 and the data are noisy below 1.3 eV. The resulting ω_p is a spectral-weight-averaged oscillator energy. Below 3.7 eV the dispersion of ε_ab1 is dominated by the 3.78 eV exciton itself, which carries most of the oscillator strength, so ω_p cannot be identified with the quasiparticle gap. The value is also poorly constrained, and no uncertainty is given.
   - Inconsistent gap values: the paper reports a "direct bandgap" of 3.71 eV (from a Tauc plot) and an electronic gap of 5.12 eV, and treats agreement with sc-GW (5.1 eV) as confirmation.
   - Limits of the scaling argument: the Eb ≈ Eg/4 relation (refs. 28–29) was derived for freestanding monolayers. Its use as a criterion for confinement in a bulk 3D crystal is not justified, and bulk excitonic insulators with large Eb would fall on the same line.
   - What to do: determine Eg independently. Options include identifying the onset of the continuum in ε_ab2 with an Elliott/Sommerfeld lineshape fit that includes the continuum, photoemission plus inverse photoemission, or a quantitative GW-BSE comparison. Then propagate the uncertainty in Eg into Eb and into Fig. 4. Without this, Fig. 4a should not be presented as evidence of two-dimensionality.

3. The 2D-versus-3D exciton comparison (Fig. 4c,d; Supplementary Notes 6–7) does not discriminate between the two models, so the "conclusively demonstrate" statement is not supported.
   - Unequal comparison: both models are evaluated at a fixed Eb = 1.34 eV, which comes from concern 2. In the 3D hydrogenic model the 2s level then sits at 3.78 + (3/4)(1.34) ≈ 4.79 eV (the paper quotes 4.84 eV). The 2D model gets its 2s at 4.40 eV only through n-dependent screening ε_n built from an effective-medium partition. That partition uses Cu-layer and AlO2-layer thicknesses (d1 = 0.36, d2 = 0.64) chosen by hand, and the root for ε_Cu is selected by an Al2O3 analogy.
   - A 3D model can fit the same feature: an isotropic 3D model with Eb ≈ 0.92 eV (Eg ≈ 4.70 eV) puts 2s at 4.47 eV, and nothing in the data excludes this, given concern 2. Note S7 tests only Eb = 0.47 eV.
   - Other origins of the 4.47 eV feature are not excluded. It is broad, and the models include no band-to-band continuum. It could equally come from the Sommerfeld-enhanced continuum, from transitions at other k-points (the band structure in Fig. 5a has several), or from vibronic sidebands.
   - The physically appropriate comparison for a uniaxial bulk crystal is an anisotropic (effective-mass and dielectric-anisotropy) or fractional-dimension exciton model (ref. 41), not an isotropic hydrogen atom.
   - What to do: (i) Fit both the 2D and the 3D/anisotropic models with Eb and Eg free, including a continuum term. (ii) Report the goodness of fit and parameter uncertainties. (iii) Show how sensitive the 2D result is to d1/d2 and to the choice of ε_Cu. (iv) Ideally, use the low-temperature data (Fig. S8, 7 K, narrower lines) to look for a resolved 2s feature.

B. Should address

4. It is not shown that the single-angle, isotropic pseudo-dielectric inversion returns the true principal components, and no error bars are given.
   - Where: Methods, Supplementary Note 2, Fig. S3–S4, Fig. 2. The headline numbers (|Δn| = 3.67, n_ab,max = 5.66, |Δk| = 5.21) are obtained by inverting Ψ, Δ measured at one angle (70°) and one azimuth with the isotropic two-phase formula, on the stated grounds that the s-polarized component dominates.
   - The approximation is not quantified. On the top face the p-wave also samples ε_c through the normal field component. On the side face rp depends on ε_ab while rs depends on ε_c, so the ratio ρ mixes both.
   - Error sources not addressed:
     - surface roughness or overlayers on the side faces, which look textured in Fig. S1;
     - beam-spot overfill on 1–2 mm faces at 70° incidence, which would pick up the epoxy and copper mount;
     - depolarization;
     - in the claimed transparent range, incoherent back-side reflections. Silver epoxy does not remove these in a mm-thick transparent crystal.
   - What to do: (i) Add multi-angle measurements (for example 55–75°) and, for the side face, a second azimuth (c ∥ plane of incidence), or Mueller-matrix generalized ellipsometry. (ii) Fit a uniaxial model jointly to both faces with a roughness layer and report the depolarization index. (iii) Check Kramers–Kronig consistency. (iv) Give confidence intervals for n_ab, n_c, Δn and Δk. (v) Compare with the previously reported refractive index of CuAlO2 (ref. 16), which is cited but never compared.

5. The "highest reported to date" claims compare unlike quantities.
   - Where: Abstract, Results, Fig. 2e, Fig. 3.
   - At-resonance maxima: |Δn|max and |Δk|max occur at the exciton resonance, where k_ab ≈ 5 and α ≈ 2×10^6 cm^-1. These are resonant values in a strongly absorbing region. Resonant extrema in other strongly excitonic or hyperbolic crystals should be compared on the same footing (same definition, same spectral region), not against below-gap values of LiNbO3 or calcite.
   - Fig. 3 itself contradicts "highest across the visible": in the figure, MoS2 and the C3H6N6F6·3H2O crystal lie well above CuAlO2 (about 2–2.4 versus about 0.6) between about 700 and 800 nm, and the h-BN points lie at or above it near 600–800 nm.
   - The Fig. 3 caption's claim of the "highest birefringence across the visible and ultraviolet regions" therefore needs to be restricted to a specified window and a specified transparency criterion. Concern 1 bears on whether the CuAlO2 points qualify as transparent.
   - What to do: add a table listing each material's Δn at fixed wavelengths, its absorption coefficient there and the measurement method, and restate the record claims accordingly.

6. The theoretical support for the selection rule and for 2D confinement is qualitative.
   - Where: Discussion, Fig. 5, Methods.
   - The argument rests on orbital projections and Bloch-function plots at L. No momentum matrix elements or anisotropic RPA/BSE ε_ab2 and ε_c2 are computed, although the relevant comparison with the measured spectra (Fig. 2b) would be direct.
   - Real-space localization of single-particle Bloch states in the Cu layer is not evidence of a 2D exciton. Two things would be needed: the dispersion and effective masses of the band edges along c (Γ–Z) versus in-plane, and the exciton envelope.
   - PBE without +U or a hybrid functional is used for Cu 3d states whose position is known to be functional-sensitive. Whether the d_z2/p_x/y character of the band edges survives PBE+U or HSE should be shown. The Methods also do not state whether the relaxed or the experimental lattice (Table S1) was used.
   - Prior work: Laskowski et al. (ref. 12) already computed anisotropic BSE spectra for CuAlO2, and the new data should be compared with them quantitatively. This would also clarify what is new in the mechanism.

7. The gap values are internally inconsistent.
   - Where: Abstract, Results p. 3–4, Supplementary Note 3.
   - The "direct bandgap of 3.71 eV" comes from an (αE)^2 Tauc extrapolation over 3.7–3.8 eV, which is the low-energy flank of the 3.78 eV exciton peak. A Tauc analysis assumes band-to-band absorption and is not meaningful on an excitonic line. The paper elsewhere places the electronic gap at 5.12 eV.
   - The indirect gap of 3.45 eV is extracted from a region already dominated by the Urbach/defect tail.
   - The 3.71 eV value is attributed to ref. 15 in the main text but presented as the authors' own measurement in Note 3.
   - What to do: call 3.71 eV the optical (excitonic) absorption onset, and reconcile it with Eg.

8. The Fano assignment of the 1s exciton is physically unclear.
   - Where: Discussion p. 5, Supplementary Note 5, Fig. S8–S9.
   - A Fano profile requires a discrete state degenerate with a continuum. In the authors' own picture the 1s state lies 1.34 eV below the band continuum and below the 2s state. "Coupling between 1s exciton and higher-order exciton" therefore cannot produce a Fano lineshape on the 1s line.
   - The Fano parameter q, its temperature dependence and the continuum it couples to are not given. Note S5 also places the Fano-like feature at 4.15 eV, while the Lorentz term is at 4.47 eV.
   - What to do: identify the continuum, for example phonon-assisted or indirect transitions given the indirect gap. Otherwise, use a simpler asymmetric lineshape (for example a Toyozawa or phonon-sideband model) and report the fitted parameters with uncertainties.

9. The number of samples and reproducibility are not stated, and the role of Cu2+ is not tested.
   - Where: Methods, Fig. 2.
   - The paper does not say how many crystals were measured, whether both faces came from the same crystal, or how much the spectra spread between crystals and between spots.
   - Because about 1.4% Cu2+ (a d9 ion with its own anisotropic d–d and charge-transfer absorption) is present, its effect on n_c and n_ab in the visible should be bounded. Options include comparing crystals with different Cu2+ content or annealing history, or comparing with literature films.
   - What to do: report data from at least two crystals for each orientation.

10. The 200 meV room-temperature linewidth is used to argue for low dimensionality (Results p. 3), but linewidth is set by exciton–phonon and disorder broadening, not by dimensionality. Ref. 19 concerns a vdW magnet. The argument should be removed or supported, for example by the temperature dependence of the linewidth extracted from Fig. S8.

C. Minor

11. The Fig. 2e caption says "black solid circles" for |Δn| and "red open squares" for |Δk|. The plot shows black open circles and red filled circles.

12. The text (p. 4) says |Δn| "surpasses 1 between 2.86 eV and 6.2 eV", except at 3.86–3.9 eV. Fig. 2e and Fig. 3, however, show further dips near 5.2–5.5 eV, and in Fig. 3 near 320 nm the dip falls to about 0.07. Please state the ranges precisely.

13. Fig. 1c caption: the statement that c-polarized light is transmitted through the crystal shown in Fig. 1b is not an observation shown in the paper (see concern 1).

14. Supplementary Note 1 refers to "Rietveld refinement" for single-crystal XRD. Please report the structure-refinement statistics (R1, wR2, GoF), atomic positions, and any Cu occupancy or oxygen excess relevant to the Cu2+ content.

15. Supplementary Note 6: the "half of the O-Cu-O dumbbell (2.038 Å)" does not obviously match the Cu–O bond length implied by the refined structure. Please give the source of the layer thicknesses and the full ε_n values used for each n.

16. Supplementary Note 7: Eq. (3) gives negative oscillator strengths, and the hydrogenic energies omit ε. Please define the units and signs.

17. Reference handling:
    - the Fig. 5a caption cites refs. 12, 40, while the main text cites 12, 50 for the same statement;
    - the abstract and text quote 3.71 eV with ref. 15.

18. Typographical errors: "semicondcutor" (p. 2), "This works" (Fig. 3), "is allocated until this threshold" (Note 3), "intratom-like", and the Fig. 1b caption phrase "due to the sufficient thickness".

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add polarized transmission spectra on thinned plates for both orientations. This single measurement would settle the transparency claim and give an independent check of the ellipsometric n_c and n_ab in the visible.
2. Note that ε_ab1 < 0 while ε_c1 > 0 roughly between 3.9 and 5.5 eV (Fig. 2a). This is a natural hyperbolic window in a bulk oxide, which the paper does not mention. It may be a more defensible "record" property than the resonant Δn, and it connects to ref. 19.
3. Compute anisotropic GW-BSE (or at least RPA with a scissor) ε_ab2 and ε_c2 and overlay them on Fig. 2b. This would make the selection-rule argument quantitative and would give Eg and Eb without relying on the Penn fit.
4. Present the low-temperature (7 K) dielectric tensor for both orientations. Narrower lines would sharpen both the exciton-series analysis and the anisotropy figures of merit.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
