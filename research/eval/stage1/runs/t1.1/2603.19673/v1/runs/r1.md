Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper combines cantilever torque magnetometry (up to 9 T, 2-90 K) with broadband bolometric ESR (1-60 GHz, up to 1.5 T) on Mn3Si2Te6. From these it builds a phenomenological anisotropy energy F = K1 sin^2 θM + K2 sin^4 θM + K3 sin^6 θM - MH cos(θH - θM). The authors argue that the low-temperature state is a coherently canted ferrimagnet stabilised by K1 < 0 and K2 > 0, and that a sixth-order term K3 is needed to keep the torque "switching angle" θc finite up to 9 T. They then map the published CAMR data of Seo et al. from θH onto the computed θM. Knowing how the magnetization actually rotates under a tilted field is the missing link in the CAMR story, so a reliable anisotropy model would be useful. As it stands, however, the evidence that the state is canted and that K3 is required rests on model comparisons that the paper does not show quantitatively.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The question is a real one. CAMR in Mn3Si2Te6 is usually plotted against θH, while the electronic structure follows θM. A measured θM(θH) map would help everyone working on this material.
- Two complementary probes are used together: torque, which tracks the global angular response over a wide field and temperature range, and ESR, which measures the local free-energy curvature. The torque data set in Fig. 2 (2-85 K, 0.5-9 T) is extensive.
- The low-temperature derivative extrema in d(τ/H)/dθH that are offset from θH = 90° (Figs. 1c, 2b-d) are a clear, reproducible-looking empirical feature, and the operational definition of θc is simple.
- In Fig. 4c, plotting ln(ρ/ρ0) against cosθM gives a near-plane slope that is common to 1-5 T, whereas the inset shows a field-dependent slope when the same data are plotted against cosθH. If this holds up, it is an attractive, testable result.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The ESR argument for canting compares the H//c data with the wrong collinear benchmark, and the fit that supplies K1 and K2 contains an unphysical g-factor (pp. 4-5, Fig. 3a-c).
   - What the paper says: the concave-down H//c dispersion is "inconsistent with a strictly collinear hard-axis response", because a collinear easy-plane magnet should give ω_hard ∝ (H - M_eff).
   - Why the benchmark is wrong: that linear Kittel form holds only above the hard-axis saturation field. In Mn3Si2Te6 that field is several tesla at low temperature. The ESR window stops at 1.5 T. Below saturation the magnetization of a plain easy-plane magnet stays mostly in the plane and tilts gradually, so the mode is not the hard-axis Kittel mode. Its dispersion depends on in-plane (trigonal) anisotropy, demagnetization and small misalignment, and can be square-root-like.
   - The g-factor: the same global fit returns g_c = 0.68, far from the g ≈ 2 expected for Mn2+ and reported in Ref. 25. The authors set this aside as an "effective parameter". But the H//c data are both the main ESR evidence for canting and part of the global fit that fixes K1/M = -2.40 T and K2/M = 1.40 T. These K1 and K2 values are then held fixed in the torque analysis.
   - A factor-of-three error in g at H//c means the single-mode macrospin model does not describe that orientation. The K1 and K2 values, and the "ESR zero-field θc" in Fig. 3d, then inherit an unknown bias.
   - What to do:
     - Simulate the collinear model (K2 = K3 = 0, with in-plane anisotropy and demagnetization of the actual ESR crystal, and a realistic misalignment) for the same three geometries, and show that it fails.
     - Refit with g fixed near 2 for all orientations and report how K1 and K2 move.
     - Or fit with a two-sublattice ferrimagnetic resonance model.
     - Without this, the ESR does not independently support canting.

2. The paper does not show quantitatively that K3 is required (pp. 5-6, Fig. 3d, 3e).
   - The central claim is that a K1-K2 model with ESR-fixed parameters cannot reproduce θc(T, H) up to 9 T, while adding K3 can. Fig. 3d does not show this.
     - The "simulations" are drawn on a separate right-hand axis in arbitrary units ("Simulation of θc (a.u.)"). A switching angle is in degrees, so this cannot be a quantitative comparison.
     - The simulations are drawn at 0 T and 15 T, not at the measured 2, 5 and 9 T.
     - The dashed line includes "an additional field-induced contribution" that is not defined anywhere in the main text.
     - The K1-K2 comparison is given only as a "zero-field limit" (orange diamonds). Comparing that with torque values taken at 2-9 T is not like with like.
   - Fit freedom: K3/M is fitted separately at each of 5, 20 and 40 K to θc(H) at a few fields. K1 and K2 are fixed to ESR values whose reliability is in question (concern 1). Two more checks are needed:
     - whether a K1-K2 model with K1 and K2 free can fit θc(H);
     - whether the need for K3 survives once K1 and K2 are allowed to move within their ESR uncertainties.
   - Physical plausibility: Zeeman energy at 9 T is much larger than K3/M ≈ 1 T. It is not obvious that a ~1 T sixth-order term keeps a ~15° marker separation at 9 T. This needs to be shown, not asserted.
   - Alternative explanation: in a two-sublattice ferrimagnet, field-induced sublattice canting acts as a field-dependent effective anisotropy. K3 may be absorbing exactly that, as the "field-induced contribution" in the dashed curve already hints.
   - What to do:
     - Overlay forward-model θc(H) curves in degrees on the measured θc at 2, 5 and 9 T for each temperature, for (i) the K1-K2 model with ESR-fixed and with free parameters, and (ii) the K1-K2-K3 model.
     - Give the residuals and the number of data points per fitted parameter.
     - State and justify any field-induced term.

3. The paper does not establish that the torque markers signal a "coherent canted ferrimagnet" rather than an ordinary easy-plane response or an easy-cone macrospin state (pp. 3-4, Figs. 1c, 2).
   - The argument is that a collinear ferrimagnet with a single uniaxial anisotropy would keep the extrema of d(τ/H)/dθH pinned at 90°/270°. No simulated derivative for that case, at the experimental fields and anisotropy ratio, is shown.
     - A derivative of a nonlinear torque curve can show a local minimum at 90° with two flanking maxima, depending on H/H_A.
     - A small tilt of the c axis out of the rotation plane, or a weak trigonal in-plane anisotropy, can also displace or split features.
   - Two different meanings of "canting" are being equated:
     - What the model describes: with K1/M = -2.4 T and K2/M = 1.4 T, F has its minimum at sin²θM = -K1/(2K2) ≈ 0.86, so θM ≈ 68°. This is an easy-cone state of the net macrospin, about 22° out of the plane.
     - What the neutrons see (Ref. 21): a relative non-collinearity of the sublattice moments.
     - These are not the same thing. The paper gives no quantitative comparison with the neutron canting angle or direction.
   - An easy cone predicts testable magnetization signatures, and the paper has SQUID data but shows only dχ/dT:
     - a finite c-axis remanent component;
     - an in-plane saturation magnetization reduced by about cos22°;
     - characteristic M(H) curvature and hysteresis.
   - "Coherent" implies a single-domain cone, but an easy cone is degenerate (±θ0 and azimuthal), and domain formation is not discussed.
   - What to do:
     - Show the simulated torque and d(τ/H)/dθH for a K1-only model (with demagnetization and a stated misalignment) at 0.8-9 T, next to the data.
     - Show M(H) for H//ab and H//c at 2-5 K and compare with the saturation fields and moments predicted by the fitted K1-K2-K3 set.
     - Compare the inferred tilt with the neutron structure quantitatively.
     - Either justify "coherent canted ferrimagnet" or rephrase it as a macrospin easy-cone description.

B. Should address

4. The CAMR reparameterization (Fig. 4) has several unstated or ad hoc steps that weaken the "field-independent slope" result.
   - Mismatched samples and temperature: the transport data come from a different crystal (Seo et al., Ref. 9), and the measurement temperature is not given. The θM(θH) map uses anisotropy parameters from Samples #2/#3; at which temperature is not stated.
   - Wrong reference point:
     - ρ0 is defined at θH = 0, but at 1-2 T the magnetization is not along c there. The 1 T data span only cosθM ≈ 0-0.22, so θM ≥ 77° for all θH.
     - The activated-transport expression uses Δ(θM = 0) as the reference, so the reference point is wrong.
     - A slope of -47.9 extended to cosθM = 1 would also give ln(ρab/ρc) ≈ 48, far beyond the data. This underlines that the linear regime covers only a narrow angular range.
   - Ad hoc steps: the hysteresis "correction" that forces the maximum to sit at cosθM = 0 is ad hoc. The slope is fitted on 1 T alone, and the other fields get free vertical offsets.
   - What to do:
     - State the transport temperature and use parameters at that temperature.
     - Describe the hysteresis correction explicitly.
     - Fit each field's slope independently, with uncertainties.
     - Show whether the collapse also occurs with a K1-only or K1-K2 mapping, i.e. whether it discriminates between the anisotropy models.
     - Convert the slope into the prefactor A (in meV) and compare it with the gap modulation in Ref. 9.

5. Reproducibility across samples is limited in the main text.
   - Each key data set comes from a different crystal:
     - 0.8 T torque: Sample #1;
     - θc(T, H) map: Sample #2;
     - ESR: Sample #3;
     - Sample #4 is mentioned only in the Methods.
   - The θc values that drive the K3 fit come from a single crystal.
   - What to do: show θc(H) at matching temperatures for at least two crystals, including the PPMS sample #4, in the main text or a clearly referenced SI figure. State the spread, and confirm that the features survive thermal cycling and remounting. (The SI was not available to this reviewer; Sections 2 and 4-6 carry essential content and should be summarised in the main text.)

6. The rotation geometry and angle calibration are not specified well enough for a quantitative angular analysis.
   - The in-plane direction contained in the rotation plane is not stated: [100] or [110], given the trigonal P-31c structure. Fig. 1a labels the vertical axis [100] while calling it the c axis.
   - No uncertainty is given for:
     - the θH zero;
     - the tilt of c out of the rotation plane;
     - the mechanical-rotator backlash in the CFMS versus PPMS setups.
   - θc is a difference of extremum positions, so a constant offset cancels, but a tilt of the c axis does not.
   - What to do: specify the geometry, give the misalignment estimate, and include it in the forward model.

7. The extraction of θc and its uncertainties are not described.
   - θc is defined from extrema of a numerical derivative of torque data. The paper does not give:
     - the smoothing used;
     - the angular step;
     - how extrema are located when they are broad (e.g. 40 K, Fig. 2d);
     - how sweep-direction hysteresis below 0.5 T is handled.
   - The torque θc values in Fig. 3d carry no error bars, yet they are the main constraint for K3.
   - What to do: provide these details and error bars, and show raw τ(θH) next to the derivative for at least one high-field trace.

8. The temperature dependence of the fitted constants and of θc is not interpreted physically.
   - In Fig. 3e, K3/M rises relative to K2/M on warming and exceeds it at 40 K. In Fig. 3d, θc is non-monotonic, peaking near 30 K.
   - Callen-Callen scaling would make higher-order terms fall faster with M(T).
   - The authors argue the coefficients are "effective", which is fair. But then the claimed microscopic picture ("symmetry-allowed sixth-order term provides near-plane stiffness") is correspondingly weaker.
   - The 20 K and 40 K ESR constraints seem to come only from a few H//ab points (Fig. 3a symbols), so K1 and K2 at those temperatures are poorly determined (large error bars in Fig. 3e).
   - What to do:
     - Plot the K's against the measured M(T) to test the scaling.
     - Discuss the origin of the 30 K maximum.
     - Show the ESR data used at 20 K and 40 K.

9. The ESR data should be compared quantitatively with Ref. 25 (Tanaka et al.), which reports magnetic resonance in the same compound. Do the mode frequencies, zero-field gap (about 10 GHz in Fig. 3a) and g-values agree, and does Ref. 25 need canting to explain its data?

C. Minor

10. Two transition temperatures appear: Tc = 78 K (Fig. 1d) and "near Tc (70 K)" (Fig. 2 caption, p. 3). Please make them consistent.
11. The Fig. 1c caption says the anomaly "emerges below 10 K" at 0.8 T, while Fig. 2 and Fig. 3d report finite θc up to 50-70 K at higher fields. Please reconcile these or say that the onset depends on field.
12. On p. 3, the high-temperature signal is called "predominantly two-fold symmetric" in contrast to low temperature. The low-temperature pattern is also two-fold; what changes is the position of the extrema, not the symmetry. Please reword.
13. The notation is inconsistent: the text uses θc1, θc2 and the caption uses θ1, θ2. The 3D surface in Fig. 2e has unreadable axes and hides the data points. A 2D colour map or θc(H) line plots with error bars would be clearer.
14. K values are given only as K/M in tesla. Please state the convention (factor of 2 in the anisotropy field), and give K in energy density using the measured M(T).
15. Fig. 4a reproduces published data. Please state the temperature, the current direction and the rotation plane, and confirm the permission and attribution. The Fig. 4b axis covers 0-360°, but the hysteresis branch used is not indicated.
16. The title and abstract stress "nodal-line"; nothing in this work tests the band topology. Consider toning this down.
17. Language: "that quantified" should be "that quantifies" (p. 4); "that governing" should be "that governs" (p. 7); "the near the in-plane limit" (p. 7); "maintext".

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one figure that overlays forward-model torque, d(τ/H)/dθH and θc(H) for three models (K1-only, K1-K2, K1-K2-K3), all in physical units, on the measured data at 2, 5 and 9 T and at 5, 20 and 40 K. This would directly support the two headline claims.
2. Add M(H) loops along c and ab at low temperature, and test them against the fitted anisotropy (saturation fields, remanent c-component, reduction of in-plane moment for an easy cone).
3. Use a two-sublattice ferrimagnetic model for the ESR, or at least fix g ≈ 2, so that the K1 and K2 used downstream do not depend on an unphysical g_c.
4. Extend ESR to higher fields or tilt angles near the plane (e.g. 85-89°), where the K3 term is predicted to matter. This would give an independent check of K3 rather than relying solely on the torque markers.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
