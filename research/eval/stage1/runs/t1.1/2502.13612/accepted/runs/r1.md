Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper measures the in-plane magnetoresistance (MR) up to 35 T in combinatorial La2-xCexCuO4 (LCCO) films from x = 0.116 to 0.188 in fine doping steps. It models ρ(H,T) with Shockley–Chambers Boltzmann transport. Matching the data requires an anisotropic T-linear scattering rate peaked at the (π,π) hotspots, and the prefactor g(x) of that anisotropic rate falls by about an order of magnitude as Tc goes to zero. The correlation between T-linear resistivity (A1) and Tc in LCCO was already known (refs 8, 10). What is new is separating out the anisotropic inelastic channel, assigning it to commensurate spin fluctuations through a current-vertex-correction (CVC) argument about the Hall coefficient, and contrasting this with the H/T-scaling MR of hole-doped cuprates. If the main results hold, they give a direct transport link between electron–boson coupling strength and Tc in a cuprate, which would interest a broad readership.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The MR data set is extensive and unusually systematic: 19 doping levels on two films grown under identical conditions, measured from 8 K to 190 K up to 35 T (Fig. 2). The non-monotonic T dependence of Δρ/ρ(0), peaking at 50–75 K for x > x_FSR, is a clear, model-independent observation that breaks Kohler scaling.
- The longitudinal MR is negligible at 75 K (Extended Data Fig. 2). This supports treating the transverse MR as orbital.
- The modelling is constrained. At x = 0.159, one parameter set fits ρ(0,T), Δρ/ρ(0) and dρ/dH at four temperatures (Fig. 4). Across S1, a single doping-dependent parameter g(x) fits two observables per channel, ρ(0,40 K) and Δρ/ρ(0) at 20 T (Fig. 5a,b).
- The alternatives that fail are shown explicitly (Extended Data Fig. 3): an anisotropic rate without hotspots, antinodal hotspots, and hotspots alone. This shows which ingredients the data demand.
- The contrast with the hole-doped side (no H/T quadrature scaling in LCCO) is a useful observation in its own right.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The central experimental observations are well documented. The paper's evidence for the anisotropic T-linear channel and its decline with x is the kind a referee would accept, though the points below would make it more robust.

B. Should address

1. The robustness and uniqueness of g(x) is not demonstrated (Fig. 5, Extended Data Tables 2 and 3). g(x) is extracted with τ_imp⁻¹ = 27 ps⁻¹, α2, the hotspot height and width, and β(φ) all held at their x = 0.159 values for every S1 channel. All of the variation in ρ(0,40 K) (about 44 to 24 μΩ cm) is therefore assigned to the anisotropic T-linear term.
   - ρ0(x) for S1 is not shown. Ext. Data Fig. 1a gives ρ(0,T) for x = 0.159 only. A higher residual resistivity in the lower-x channels, which is plausible closer to the AFM and upturn regime, would inflate g at low x.
   - It is not tested whether a doping-dependent hotspot strength, a doping-dependent isotropic T-linear term, or an x-dependent τ_imp could also match the two observables per channel.
   - Fig. 5c and Extended Data Table 3 give no uncertainty on g(x).

   Because g(x) is the quantity linked to Tc, the authors should:
   - show ρ0(x) (or ρ at 2 K in field) for every S1 channel;
   - refit with τ_imp free per channel, and separately with the hotspot amplitude free;
   - quote confidence intervals on g(x) from the fit residuals.

2. The lowest-doping channels are modelled with an unreconstructed Fermi surface without per-channel justification. The S1 points at x = 0.116–0.138 carry most of the order-of-magnitude drop in g(x) (6.5 to 2.1), and they sit below x_FSR = 0.14. The text says the 40 K analysis is "above T_FSR" for all channels, but several observations bear on this:
   - Extended Data Fig. 4b shows R_H at x = 0.117 rising to a maximum near 20 K and then changing sign.
   - Extended Data Fig. 7 shows γ1 for x < x_FSR turning down sharply below about 20 K.
   - Fig. 2 notes a negative MR component at these dopings.
   - Fig. 1 suggests T* at x ≈ 0.12 may be close to 40 K.

   The authors should:
   - give T* or T_FSR for each channel, with its source;
   - show that the x = 0.116–0.125 fits are insensitive to the choice of fit temperature, for example by repeating at 50 or 60 K;
   - state explicitly how much of the "order of magnitude" depends on these channels.

3. The g(x) curve combines two different extraction methods. For S2 (x = 0.159–0.188), g(x) is not fitted from MR. It is set from A1, the T-linear coefficient of ρ(0,T), normalized at x = 0.159 (Extended Data Table 3 caption). The S2 half of Fig. 5c therefore restates the known A1–Tc correlation; it does not show that the anisotropic channel weakens. MR isotherms for S2 exist (Fig. 2k,l, x = 0.169 and 0.188). The authors should:
   - apply the same ρ(0,40 K) plus Δρ/ρ(0)(20 T) fit to the S2 channels;
   - mark the two methods distinctly in Fig. 5c.

   The x = 0.188 end-member, where Tc is about 0, is the most informative point for the claim that the coupling vanishes with Tc.

4. The spin-versus-charge discrimination is semi-quantitative, and the main text overstates it. The main text says that with CVCs "we are able to capture the key features of R_H(T,x) beyond x > x_FSR, most notably the reduction in R_H with increasing T". Extended Data Fig. 6, however, gives only single R_H values for five fixed Γ(φ) shapes: 8.9, 5.6, 4.3 and 10.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ m³ C⁻¹, plus 1.18 × 10⁻⁹ m³ C⁻¹ for Γ_AN with charge hotspots. No calculated R_H(T) or R_H(x) is compared with data. Further gaps:
   - The CVC model has its own fixed choices: the Gaussian ε(φ) with σ = 0.07, the neglect of the 1/(1−ε²) prefactor, and the low-field Jones–Zener expansion applied to data taken at 14 T.
   - The R_H data used (Extended Data Fig. 4) come from other films (refs 27 and 10), not S1 or S2.
   - The charge scenario is not given a matching treatment of vertex corrections at its own wavevector. It is dismissed with a qualitative flat-segment argument.

   Since the "spin-fluctuation" assignment in the title rests on this step, the authors should:
   - compute R_H(T) for x = 0.159 using the same α1, α2 and g as the MR fit, and overlay it on measured R_H;
   - ideally measure R_H on S1 and S2 channels;
   - soften the "capture key features of R_H(T,x)" wording to match what is shown.

5. The absolute resistivity calibration of the combinatorial films is uncertain, and the fit uses absolute values. The two films disagree in ρ by a factor of 1.5 at the common doping x = 0.159 (Extended Data Fig. 1a), and S2 is simply scaled. The combinatorial-film R_H values in Extended Data Fig. 4b (about 15 × 10⁻¹⁰ m³ C⁻¹ at low T for x = 0.154) are about 1.7 times larger than both the non-combinatorial value (about 8.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ m³ C⁻¹ for x = 0.15–0.16, panel a) and the Drude estimate the authors cite. That suggests a geometric-factor or thickness error of similar size. τ_imp, α1 and α2 are set from absolute ρ(0,T) of S1 and then used with absolute k_F and v_F. The authors should:
   - state the thickness and geometric-factor uncertainty for each film;
   - show how a 1.5-fold change in absolute ρ would change the inferred anisotropy and the hotspot parameters.

6. Non-orbital contributions to the MR are excluded only at 75 K. The longitudinal-MR test (Extended Data Fig. 2) is shown only at T = 75 K and only for x ≥ 0.159. The key observation, that the MR decreases below 50–75 K, relies on low-T isotherms. At those temperatures the authors themselves note:
   - a finite longitudinal MR, attributed to superconducting fluctuations;
   - for x < x_FSR, a negative isotropic spin-scattering MR (ref. 21).

   Either effect can reduce Δρ/ρ(0) at low T and mimic a falling anisotropy. The authors should:
   - show H ∥ I ∥ ab MR at 20–40 K for several dopings, including x < x_FSR;
   - subtract it, or show it is negligible, before the MR curves enter the fits of Figs. 4 and 5.

7. The pairing conclusion goes beyond what the correlation shows. The abstract says the correlation "identifies low-energy spin fluctuations as the primary pairing glue". The evidence is a monotonic co-variation of g(x) and Tc. Disorder, carrier density, Fermi-surface shape and the T-linear coefficient all change with x as well. No estimate of a coupling constant λ_sf is made from g(x)α1 and compared quantitatively with Tc, for example with a McMillan-type formula, an Eliashberg estimate or a spin-fluctuation Tc formula. The authors should either:
   - temper the abstract and concluding wording to "consistent with" or "evidence for", as the title does; or
   - convert g(x)α1 (via the transport λ_tr = ħ/(2πk_B) · d(1/τ)/dT) into λ_tr(x) and show that the implied Tc(x) is quantitatively reasonable.

C. Minor

8. Text and Fig. 5 disagree on the size of the drops. The main text speaks of "the threefold drop in ρ(0,40 K) and the twofold drop in Δρ/ρ(0) (at 20 T) across S1". Fig. 5a shows ρ(0,40 K) falling from about 44 to about 24 μΩ cm, less than twofold. Clarify whether "threefold" refers to the inelastic part ρ − ρ0, and give the numbers.

9. Fig. 4c(iii)–(iv) does not support the text. The text says both the spin and charge scenarios "reproduce essentially all of the features of ρ(H,T), including the absolute magnitude". In Fig. 4c(iii) the charge-scenario Δρ/ρ(0) at 30 T and 50 K is about 0.035, against about 0.06 in the data (Fig. 4a(iii)). Fig. 4c(iv) likewise reaches about 0.04 against about 0.06. Correct the statement. If anything, this discrepancy helps the authors' case for the spin scenario.

10. There is a citation mismatch. The Extended Data Table 1 title cites "Tang et al.57", but ref. 57 is Chambers. The Methods text cites ref. 26 (Tang et al., PRB 104) for the same tight-binding conditions. Please check the reference numbering in the Methods and Extended Data.

11. In Extended Data Fig. 3b4, the 50 K simulated derivative lies below the 40 K and 30 K curves at low and intermediate fields, and its shape differs qualitatively from the other rows. Please check the legend and colour assignment, or comment on it in the caption.

12. Extended Data Table 3 says g(x) drops "by roughly a factor of 2", while Fig. 5 says it drops "by one order of magnitude". The first refers to S2 alone and the second to S1 and S2 combined; state this explicitly.

13. Give, per channel, the nominal Ce content, its uncertainty (the text states Δx ≲ 0.003) and the measured Tc. The Tc axis at the top of Fig. 5c is non-linear and only partially labelled, and the S2 points sit at Tc ≲ 8 K. A table of Tc(x) for S1 and S2 would make the g–Tc correlation checkable.

14. Several modelling choices are given without justification: the exponent 12 in β(φ) = sin¹²[2(φ ± (π/4 − HS))], HS = 0.48, and the hotspot width of 0.05 (units unspecified). The authors should briefly state how sensitive the x = 0.159 fit is to each. Supplementary Fig. 3 is referenced for β(φ), but the hotspot parameters are not discussed.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Fit the full ρ(H,T) isotherms, not only one temperature and field, at two or three further dopings spanning the range (for example x = 0.12, 0.14 and 0.188), using the single g(x). This would show that one doping-dependent parameter suffices across the phase diagram, not just at 40 K and 20 T.
- Convert g(x)α1 into a dimensionless transport coupling λ_tr(x), Fermi-surface-averaged and resolved at the hotspots, and plot it against Tc. This would make the analogy with λ_ph and λ_tr in conventional superconductors (Supplementary Fig. 2) quantitative.
- Measure the Hall effect on the same combinatorial channels. Fit R_H(T) and MR(H,T) with one parameter set; the authors themselves suggest this is possible.
- Compare g(x) with independent measures of the strength of low-energy (π,π) fluctuations in LCCO or PLCCO as a function of x, for example spin-fluctuation spectral weight or correlation length from neutron or RIXS data. This would tie the transport coupling to the fluctuation spectrum, not only to Tc.
