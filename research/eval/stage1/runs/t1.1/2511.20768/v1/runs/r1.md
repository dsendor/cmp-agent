Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use 6 eV time-resolved ARPES with sample bias and a deflector analyzer to reach the off-nodal and near-antinodal region of optimally doped Bi2212 (Tc about 91 K) in a single acquisition. They report that 300 meV pumping at high fluence (about 138 µJ/cm²) makes the tomographic DOS (TDOS) transiently particle-hole asymmetric for about 1 to 2 ps, with suppressed weight above E_F, while the same analysis at low fluence (about 74 µJ/cm²) and at matched nodal electronic temperature stays symmetric. Symmetry returns by about 3.5 ps. From this, and from a phenomenological SC plus pseudogap self-energy, they conclude that the pseudogap and superconductivity compete once phase coherence is established. Looking at the unoccupied side of the antinodal gap out of equilibrium below Tc is new and could matter to the pseudogap debate. The headline, however, rests on a few meV of spectral weight above E_F, which is recovered only by deconvolution and Fermi-Dirac division.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The technique is a real advance. Low-photon-energy TR-ARPES covers all four quadrants and the node, cut 1 and cut 2 in one pump-probe acquisition (Fig. 2a), so the geometry is identical across cuts, which removes a common source of systematic error.
- There is a built-in control. The low-fluence data are processed the same way and stay symmetric and Dynes-like at every delay (Fig. 3b,e; Fig. S2). The near-nodal cut shows no asymmetry at high fluence (Fig. S3a,d).
- The matched-temperature comparison is a sensible design. In Fig. 4a,b and Fig. S6a,b, nodal EDCs that share one Fermi-Dirac fit (Fig. 4c,d) separate the effect of electronic temperature from the effect of fluence.
- The result is seen in two independent analyses (TDOS, and EDCs at k_F with Norman fits, Fig. S3), on two cuts, and with a slope-ratio metric (Fig. 4e, Fig. S6c).
- The phenomenological 4x4 Nambu model with auxiliary fermions (Eq. 4, Eq. S14) is clearly written. It reduces to the standard SC and pseudogap self-energies in the right limits (Eq. S15).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The asymmetry above E_F is not shown to be robust against the data processing.**
   - *What the claim rests on:* the ω > 0 TDOS within about 5 to 25 meV of E_F at 0.6 to 1 ps (Fig. 3c,f; Fig. 4a).
   - *How that weight is obtained:* (i) Lucy-Richardson deconvolution with 15 iterations of an 18 meV resolution; (ii) subtraction of a constant background evaluated at +100 meV; (iii) division by a Fermi-Dirac function at the nodal T_e(τ) with a rigid chemical-potential correction (Methods; Fig. S1).
   - *Why this is fragile:* at T_e ≈ 80 to 88 K, k_BT is about 7 meV, so the points above E_F sit where f(ω) falls by one to two orders of magnitude. A small error in T_e, in μ, or in the background is amplified there.
   - *The untested assumption:* the analysis assumes the near-antinodal states share the nodal quasi-equilibrium distribution at 0.6 to 1 ps. This is exactly the window in which the authors themselves invoke a non-thermal bosonic population and a delayed rise in Γ_p (Fig. S4). Gapped antinodal states that equilibrate more slowly, or at a different effective temperature or chemical potential, than nodal states would change the occupation of the upper Bogoliubov branch. After division by the nodal f(ω), this would appear as suppressed or enhanced ω > 0 weight, which is the signature claimed. The low-fluence and near-nodal controls test the procedure, but not this assumption at high fluence and short delay.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Propagate the uncertainties in T_e, μ and background into error bands on the TDOS in Fig. 3c,f and Fig. 4a.
     - Show that the asymmetry survives a forward-modelling analysis without deconvolution and division. For example, fit the raw EDCs with [Dynes or Norman spectral function × f(ω; T_e, μ)] ⊗ R(ω), letting T_e and μ at the antinode vary freely, and report whether a symmetric spectral function with a free antinodal distribution can describe the high-fluence 0.6 to 1 ps data.
     - Give the antinodal effective temperature extracted this way, alongside the nodal one.

2. **No equilibrium temperature dependence just below Tc, and "well below Tc" is overstated.**
   - *The overstatement:* the abstract and Fig. 1 say the asymmetric state appears "for electronic temperatures well below the equilibrium superconducting critical temperature". But Fig. 2c shows T_e(HF) ≈ 95 K near 0.2 ps and about 85 to 90 K at 0.6 ps. The text itself says T_e exceeds Tc by about 5% for the first 0.5 ps. At 1.1 ps T_e ≈ 78 K ≈ 0.86 Tc.
   - *Why the missing data matter:* the only equilibrium references are the τ < 0 low-temperature spectra and a single pseudogap spectrum at 130 K (Fig. 5a). There are no equilibrium TDOS along cuts 1 and 2 at, say, 70, 80, 85, 90 and 100 K. In optimally doped Bi2212, earlier equilibrium ARPES reports gap filling and pseudogap-related changes developing below Tc near the antinode (refs. 17, 21, 45). Without the equilibrium series, the reader cannot tell whether the transient spectrum at 0.6 ps is essentially the near-Tc thermal spectrum or genuinely non-thermal. The low-fluence comparison at 78 K (Fig. 4a) helps. However, low fluence never reaches the 85 to 90 K of the high-fluence 0.6 ps curve, so that delay has no matched control.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Add equilibrium TDOS and slope-ratio values for cuts 1 and 2 at several temperatures between 70 and 100 K, processed identically, and overlay them on Fig. 4e.
     - Restrict the "well below Tc" wording to the delays where T_e is actually below Tc.

3. **Spatial inhomogeneity has not been excluded as the source of the "coexistence".**
   - *The alternative:* a mixture of regions, some still superconducting and some driven into the pseudogap state, would also give a spectrum with SC-like and pseudogap-like features at intermediate fluence or delay. Such regions could arise laterally, from the Gaussian pump profile against the probe spot, or in depth, from the pump penetration depth against the 6 eV probe depth. The Discussion dismisses this with "the ~1 ps response time rules out potential inhomogeneous optical excitation effects", but gives no argument for why the time scale excludes it.
   - *What is missing:* pump and probe spot sizes are not stated, nor the pump absorption depth at 4 µm, nor whether the quoted fluences are peak or average values.
   - *How the theory comparison falls short:* the text equates a "simple weighted sum" with "two separate self-energies" (Fig. S9a, S10a). A self-energy sum is not the inhomogeneous alternative. That alternative is a weighted average of spectral functions, A_mix = x A_SC + (1 − x) A_PG.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Report the spot sizes, absorption depth and fluence definition.
     - Estimate the fluence distribution actually sampled by the probe.
     - Fit the high-fluence 1 ps TDOS with a weighted average of the measured high-fluence 3.5 ps (or low-fluence) TDOS and the measured or appropriately modelled pseudogap TDOS, letting x vary freely. Show quantitatively whether this mixture fails where the interplay self-energy succeeds, especially in the position of the minimum and the slope above E_F.

B. Should address

4. **The fluence dependence is too sparse for the threshold and "intermediate fluence" claims.**
   - The abstract says the effect appears "for a sufficiently high excitation fluence" and that coexistence occurs "at intermediate fluence". Only two fluences, about a factor of 2 apart, are measured.
   - Whether the asymmetry turns on at a threshold, perhaps one tied to the fluence that saturates Γ_p or suppresses the condensate, or grows continuously bears directly on the competition picture.
   - The authors should add a fluence series (at least four or five fluences), plotting the minimum slope ratio and Γ_p(max) against fluence, or else soften the wording.

5. **Number of samples and reproducibility are not stated.**
   - The paper does not say how many samples or cleaves were measured. It also does not say whether the 130 K equilibrium pseudogap data (Fig. 5a; "~150 K" in Fig. S8) come from the same sample and cleave as the low-temperature pump-probe data, and in what order (thermal cycling, surface ageing).
   - The asymmetry is a small effect in a noisy part of the spectrum, so the authors should show it in at least one further sample or cleave and state the measurement sequence.

6. **The slope-ratio metric lacks uncertainties, and its windows are inconsistent.**
   - Fig. 4e and Fig. S6c have no error bars. The low-fluence ratio scatters between roughly 0.5 and 1.3 (cut 2 in Fig. S6c even more), so the significance of the high-fluence dip to about 0.35 is hard to judge.
   - The fitting windows differ between cuts: [−17,−7] and [7,17] meV for cut 1, and [−20,−4] and [4,20] meV for cut 2. Neither choice is justified.
   - The authors should give fit uncertainties, ideally including the T_e uncertainty from concern 1. They should also show that the result is stable when the window is varied, and use a single window or explain why the windows differ.

7. **The phenomenological theory has many free choices and is compared with the data only by eye.**
   - *Fitted, not predicted:* the transient-state curve uses Δ'_0 = 0.8 Δ_0 and H'_0 = 0.9 H_0, chosen to resemble the data (Fig. S9b). The auxiliary-fermion pairing is simply set to equal the electronic gap, with the same d-wave form. This ingredient is precisely what makes the "interplay" self-energy differ from the "sum", and it has no independent justification.
   - *An ad hoc pseudogap form:* the pole is fixed at ω_0 = 20 meV with a d-wave coupling H_k (Eq. S8), unlike the cluster-DMFT forms cited (refs. 61 to 64), and a frequency cut-off is added (Eq. S16).
   - *Parameters not tied to the measurement:*
     - Δ_0 = 50 meV is set using equilibrium EDCs from Fig. S3. It is not shown to reproduce the measured gaps of about 25 and 40 meV on cuts 1 and 2.
     - Γ_s = 7 meV is used both at low temperature and in the 130 K pseudogap state.
     - None of the transient parameters is linked to the extracted Γ_p(τ) or T_e(τ).
   - *Theory against data is visibly off:* in Fig. 5b the simulated SC curve has a much weaker coherence peak than the 3.5 ps data. The simulated pseudogap minimum (about +15 meV) also differs from the measured 130 K curve.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Present the comparison as a fit with stated free parameters and residuals.
     - Show how sensitive the conclusion is to the choice of auxiliary-fermion gap.
     - Test whether the transient spectra can be described by varying only Γ_p and Δ in the interplay model, consistent with Fig. S4.
   - Until then, "demonstrate that the two states compete" should read "are consistent with".

8. **"Pseudogap suppressed" and "challenge the paradigm" go beyond the data.**
   - *The overreach:* the abstract states that "the pseudogap is suppressed" when superconductivity is restored, and that the data challenge the picture of superconductivity emerging from phase coherence in the pseudogap.
   - *The authors' own caveat:* the Discussion concedes that restored particle-hole symmetry "does not necessarily imply any changes in the pole responsible for the pseudogap"; it could be a redistribution of weight into the Bogoliubov peaks. In other words, the data do not distinguish a weakened pseudogap from one whose signature is masked by coherent superconductivity.
   - *Earlier studies left out:* the paper should also discuss equilibrium reports of particle-hole asymmetry and k_F misalignment in the antinodal region below Tc (e.g., refs. 12, 13 and related work). It should explain how the low-temperature symmetric spectra here square with them.
   - *What the authors should do:* tone down the abstract and conclusions accordingly.

9. **The non-thermal argument relies on comparing different delays.**
   - In Fig. 4a, low fluence at 0.5 ps is compared with high fluence at 1.1 ps at the same nodal T_e. These states differ not only in fluence but in Γ_p (Fig. S4), in the presumed non-thermal boson population, and in the condensate fraction.
   - The comparison shows that T_e alone does not set the spectrum. It does not show that pair breaking alone produces the asymmetry, since Γ_p is also larger at high fluence. A low-fluence curve with Γ_p comparable to the high-fluence one may not exist, which is part of the point.
   - The authors should state explicitly which variable (Γ_p, condensate fraction or phase coherence) they believe controls the asymmetry, and plot the slope ratio against Γ_p(τ) for both fluences to test it.

10. **Apparatus effects of the 48 V bias and the high-fluence pump are not characterized.**
    - Biasing the sample enlarges the momentum window. However, it also introduces k- and energy-dependent distortions and can couple to pump-induced surface photovoltage or space charge. These effects could differ between low and high fluence and between cuts at large emission angle.
    - The rigid shift correction (< 2 meV, Fig. S1) is shown only at the node.
    - The authors should show that the k_F positions and the dispersion along cuts 1 and 2 are unchanged (apart from the rigid shift) at high fluence, and that the energy resolution at large angle matches the 18 meV assumed in the deconvolution.

11. **The high-fluence Norman fits are acknowledged to be unstable.**
    - Section IV says the high-fluence fits are "unstable" because Γ_s broadens and Δ fills, yet the main text quotes Γ_p of about 15 to 20 meV from them. No uncertainties are given (Fig. S4).
    - The same section says Γ_s is fixed from the nodal cut, while also saying Γ_s broadens significantly at high fluence. It is unclear which value is used.
    - The authors should give fit uncertainties and the fixed or free status of each parameter at high fluence.

C. Minor

12. **Fig. 5:**
    - The x-axis reads "E − E_F (eV)" but should be meV.
    - The caption describes panel a as "Experimental (top) and theoretical (bottom)", but the panels are left and right.
    - The caption refers to a panel c that does not exist; the simulations are the right half of panel b.

13. **Fig. 2c caption:** it says the integration region is the "red arrow in panel c", but the arrow is in panel b. The SI (Sec. I) refers to "Fig. 1c of the main text" for T_e, which should be Fig. 2c.

14. **Temperature of the pseudogap reference:** it is given as 130 K in Fig. 5a and the main text, but as "~150 K" in Sec. VIII C for the data in Fig. S8. Clarify whether these are the same data.

15. **Plotting cut-off:** it is "4.5 × k_B T_e" in the Fig. 3 caption but "k_B T_e" in the Fig. S3 caption. State the cut-off used in each figure.

16. **Notation:** Eq. S8 uses Γ_sf while the text and Table II use Γ_pg. Eq. S7 writes ε̄_k in the pole, but Eq. 4 and Eq. S12 use ω_0 or ε̄_k inconsistently with Eq. S10 ("ε̄_k").

17. **Axis labels:** Fig. 2a labels the vertical axis k_x and the horizontal k_y with cuts at fixed k_y, while the text describes Fig. 2b as along Γ–Y with k_x < 0. Clarify the axis convention and the direction of each cut.

18. **Typos:** "devonvoluted" (Methods), "dahsed" (Fig. S3), "obtain" for "obtained" (Methods), "whe" (Sec. VIII C), and the stray comma in the acknowledgements.

19. **Undefined "high resolution":** the 18 meV energy resolution and 300 fs time resolution are modest compared with the 7 to 20 meV features discussed. The phrase "high-resolution" in the abstract should be qualified, or the effective resolution after deconvolution stated.

20. **CDW argument:** the Discussion argues that charge order is unlikely to cause the pseudogap because the pseudogap is suppressed when superconductivity is restored. This inference depends on concern 8 and should be phrased more cautiously.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Replace deconvolution plus Fermi-Dirac division with a forward model (spectral function × f(ω; T_e^AN, μ^AN) ⊗ R), fitted to the raw EDCs, with free antinodal T_e and μ. Present the asymmetry with error bands derived from it.
2. Add an equilibrium temperature series (70 to 100 K) on the same sample and cuts, and a fluence series of at least four to five points. Place all of them on one plot of slope ratio against T_e and Γ_p.
3. Test an explicit spatial-mixture model (a weighted average of measured spectral functions) alongside the interplay self-energy. Report goodness of fit and the free parameters for each.
4. Plot the slope ratio against the simultaneously extracted Γ_p(τ) for both fluences. This would turn the qualitative link between pair breaking and asymmetry, noted in the text, into a quantitative test.

5. Verdict: major revision
