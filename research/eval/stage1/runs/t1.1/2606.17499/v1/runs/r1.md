Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript uses low-temperature STM/STS, and spin-polarized STS with a Cr tip, to look at near-zero-energy in-gap states at four kinds of site on cleaved FeTe0.55Se0.45: step edges, Type I wrinkles, a Type II line defect with a bend, and defect-free regions above subsurface impurities. It argues that none of these states is a Majorana bound state or dispersing Majorana mode, and that most are (coupled) Yu-Shiba-Rusinov states. The cautionary message matters for the Fe(Te,Se) Majorana debate, and adding spin resolution is a sensible step. Novelty is moderate, however. The trivial character of the bend-type line defect (phase shift near pi/2) and of several defect-bound states has already been argued in Refs. 38, 39 and 41. The new element is the spin-resolved data, and in the present version those data are sparse and poorly controlled.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Spin-polarized STS on in-gap states in Fe(Te,Se) is experimentally demanding and rarely done. The tip-switching protocol (+1 T / -1 T / +1 T, Fe-coated Cr tip) is a reasonable way to obtain spin contrast.
- One paper covers several defect classes, with high-quality spatially resolved line spectra (Figs. 2c,d; 3d,g,i; 5g-i) and atomically resolved topography.
- The lattice-phase analysis of the Type II bend (Fig. 4b-d) independently reproduces a phase shift well below pi, consistent with Ref. 41.
- The general message, that a zero-bias peak alone is not evidence for a Majorana mode, is useful to the community.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The abstract's spin-resolved claim for step edges and wrinkles is not backed by spin-resolved data.**
   - *Where:* The abstract says: "By using spin-polarized scanning tunneling spectroscopy, we demonstrate that the near-zero-energy localized states on step edges and line defects originate from topologically trivial Yu-Shiba-Rusinov states." In the main text, SP-STS is shown only for three points on one Type II bend (Fig. 4f) and two points in a defect-free region (Fig. 5k,l).
   - *Step edges (Fig. 2):* The YSR assignment rests on spatially resolved, spin-integrated spectra and a toy model of two Lorentzians at ±0.14 mV whose heights are taken from the data (Fig. 2e,f).
   - *Type I wrinkle (Fig. 3a-d):* The assignment rests only on "two peaks with symmetrical energy relative to zero", which the text itself calls merely "compatible with the presence of a YSR bound state".
   - *Why this matters:* Particle-hole symmetric pairs of in-gap peaks are not specific to magnetic scatterers. In an s± superconductor, nonmagnetic defects with interband scattering are pair-breaking and produce in-gap bound states. Strain at wrinkles and steps can also locally suppress the gap. Low-energy Caroli-de Gennes-like or Andreev-type states at line defects are a further alternative. Without spin or field information, the magnetic (YSR) origin at steps and wrinkles is an assumption.
   - *Requested:* Either (a) provide SP-STS and/or Zeeman-field-dependent spectra at the step-edge ZBP (path B, points 0-6) and at the wrinkle hot spot (white box, Fig. 3a,b), or (b) reword the abstract and Discussion so that the YSR assignment for these defects is presented as a plausible interpretation, not a demonstration, and add a discussion of the nonmagnetic s± alternative.

2. **The spin contrast in Figs. 4f and 5k lacks the controls needed to attribute it to spin-polarized tunneling.**
   - *What the claim rests on:* The bend assignment relies on a +1 T / -1 T difference in dI/dV within ±0.8 mV and on its sign reversal between positive and negative bias (Fig. 4f). The P1 result in Fig. 5k relies on a zero-bias conductance changing between 1.4 and 2.0 under ±1.2 T. Several field-reversal artifacts can produce such differences:
   - *(i) Vortices.* At 1-1.2 T perpendicular field the sample contains a vortex lattice (spacing about 45 nm). Vortex positions are generally not reproduced after reversing the field, and line defects are natural pinning sites. A vortex core within a few nm of the measurement point changes the in-gap DOS strongly, including at zero bias. The authors should state where the vortices were in each field state, for example with large-scale zero-bias maps taken at each field before the point spectra.
   - *(ii) Impurity moments that follow the field.* If the impurity moments responsible for the YSR states follow a 1 T field, the tip and the impurity flip together, and the expected spin contrast is zero. The authors need to state, and if possible show, that the impurity moments do not reverse while the tip's Fe-coated apex does. This bears directly on how the contrast is interpreted.
   - *(iii) Tip changes and drift.* Fig. 4f shows a repeat at +1 T only for positions 1 and 2. Position 3 has only one -1 T and one +1 T curve. The two +1 T curves at position 1 visibly differ, so the size of the irreproducible part is unclear.
   - *(iv) Setpoint normalization.* Spectra are stabilized at -10 mV, -300 pA. If the tip is spin-polarized at the setpoint bias, normalization rescales the curves and can produce apparent contrast whose sign depends on bias.
   - *(v) Uncertainty in Fig. 5k.* The bars carry no error bars. Of the four values, two at +1.2 T (1.98 and 1.72) and two at -1.2 T (1.57 and 1.40), the spread within each sign is comparable to the difference between signs.
   - *Requested:*
     - repeat the same field sequence with a non-spin-polarized tip (or the same Cr tip, characterized as non-polarized) at the same positions;
     - measure a reference position on the clean gap away from any in-gap state;
     - give the asymmetry (dI/dV(+B) - dI/dV(-B))/(sum) with an uncertainty from repeated sweeps, at several positions;
     - report the vortex configuration at each field;
     - show the in-gap peak energies as a function of field magnitude (Zeeman shift) as an independent check of magnetic character.
   The calibration in Figs. S7/S8 should be summarized in the main text with the measured tip polarization, because this sets the sensitivity.

3. **The conclusion that the ZBP in defect-free regions "cannot be attributed to the presence of Majorana bound states" is not supported for point P2.**
   - *What the paper shows:* The abstract and Discussion ("we found that none of them can be explained by MBS") state this firmly. For P2, however, the evidence against an MBS is the absence of a field-dependent zero-bias signal (Fig. 5l). The text itself says that "the spin sensitivity of the measurement can be reduced" when the defect is deeper and that "other possible explanations for the zero-energy bound state at point 2 may exist."
   - *Why the null result does not exclude an MBS:* A null spin-polarized result excludes an MBS only if the expected MBS spin polarization at that location, with this tip polarization and at 1.1 K, would have exceeded the noise floor in Fig. 5l. No such estimate is given. Ref. 59 predicts spin polarization for a vortex-bound MZM, and its applicability to a zero-field, non-vortex ZBP is not discussed.
   - *Missing physical scenario:* The paper gives no scenario in which an MBS could arise at a defect-free spot at 0 T, so the hypothesis being tested is not clearly defined.
   - *What the P2 data do show:* The ZBP does not disperse spatially (Fig. 5g,h,j), has the same HWHM as at P1, and lies at zero energy. These data are consistent with a deeper YSR state close to the quantum phase transition, but they do not exclude other origins.
   - *Requested:* Either supply a quantitative sensitivity bound (expected versus measured contrast) and further discriminating data such as the field dependence of the peak energy, temperature dependence, or tip-height and setpoint dependence of the peak energy, or narrow the abstract and conclusion to state that P2 remains unresolved.

B. Should address

4. **Number of samples, cleaves and defects is not stated.**
   - *What is missing:* Methods does not say how many samples, cleaves, tips or defects of each class were measured. Each class appears to be represented by one example: one step edge (Fig. 2), one wrinkle hot spot (Fig. 3a-d), one bend (Figs. 3e-i, 4) and one donut region (Fig. 5).
   - *Why it matters:* The title and abstract generalize to "multiple structural defects" and to the categories as a whole.
   - *Requested:* State how often each category shows near-zero-energy states, how many instances were measured with SP-STS, and whether the spin contrast was reproduced on a second bend or step edge and with a second tip.

5. **The argument against Andreev bound states at the step edge is physically incorrect.**
   - *Where:* p. 6.
   - *What is wrong:* The text says Andreev bound states are excluded because Fe(Te,Se) is s± "with no quasiparticles inside the gap causing Andreev reflection" and because Andreev states "require two reflection walls". Surface Andreev bound states form at a single surface or step when quasiparticles scatter between momentum regions with opposite gap sign, and s± pairing with interband scattering at a step is the standard case where such in-gap states are expected.
   - *Requested:* Rewrite this paragraph. The authors should use, for example, the spatial decay length, the energy of the path A peaks, or field dependence to discriminate edge-scattering states from YSR states.

6. **The step-edge splitting is close to the energy resolution and the model is not a fit.**
   - *Resolution:* At T = 1.1 K the thermal resolution (about 3.5 k_B T ≈ 0.33 meV) is comparable to the claimed splitting (±0.14 mV). The "ZBP splitting" in Fig. 2d and the "spectral weight transfer from negative to positive energy" therefore need quantitative support.
   - *Fig. 2e:* The ±0.14 mV traces largely overlap within the noise beyond about 1 nm, so the weight transfer is not evident.
   - *Fig. 2f:* The model takes the peak positions as fixed at ±0.14 mV and the peak heights from the data, with "two different coupling strengths" for spectra 1-2 and 3-6. It therefore has as many free parameters as features to reproduce.
   - *Missing values:* The energies of the path A peaks are not given.
   - *Requested:*
     - report fitted peak energies with uncertainties along both paths;
     - fit raw spectra by convolving with the Fermi function of the tip and the lock-in broadening, including the measured electron temperature;
     - state which parameters of the two-impurity model (note 3 of SI, Figs. S2-S3) are free and which are fixed.

7. **Tip gating and trapped flux at P1/P2 need checks.**
   - *Tip gating:* The donut-shaped zero-bias map (Fig. 5b) and the positive shift of the peak toward the edge along line C (Fig. 5i,j) are typical of tip-induced charging or band bending. The paper invokes this (Ref. 38) without testing it. Setpoint and tip-height dependence of the donut radius and of the peak energy would confirm it.
   - *Trapped flux:* The 0 T ZBP data (Fig. 5c) should be checked against a pinned vortex left over from previous field excursions. The authors should state the field history before these maps and confirm that no flux was trapped, for example from the coherence-peak height across a larger field of view.

8. **The SP-STS models are illustrative and only qualitatively compared with the data.**
   - *What is wrong:* The ensemble model (Fig. 4h,i; Fig. S10) has impurity positions, couplings and depths chosen by hand. The comparison with Fig. 4f uses two hand-picked points (A, B) and is qualitative. The bias axes in Fig. 4i are in units of Δ/e and are not matched to the measured ±2 mV window.
   - *Why it matters:* The claim that a crossing point away from zero energy is "additional evidence" for ferromagnetically coupled impurities (Fig. 4g) is weak. A single YSR state with particle-hole asymmetric weights, or the setpoint effect, can also shift the crossing point, and the crossing energy is not given with an uncertainty.
   - *Requested:* State the model parameters, give the crossing energies with error bars, and show that the model's predictions differ from those of a single-impurity alternative.

9. **The assumption that excess Fe is magnetic is not checked.**
   - *What is assumed:* The assignment of every in-gap state to subsurface excess Fe assumes that this Fe is magnetic and lies within the probing depth L_d (Fig. 1b). No independent estimate of excess-Fe concentration or depth distribution is given.
   - *Requested:* Give the expected density of such sites per area for this stoichiometry and compare it with the observed density of zero-energy spots (Fig. 1d). This would test whether the YSR picture is quantitatively plausible.

10. **Novelty relative to Refs. 38, 39 and 41 is not stated.**
    - *What is missing:* Ref. 41 already reported a pi/2 phase shift and trivial character for this type of line defect. Refs. 38 and 39 already reported dispersing and trivial ZBPs from subsurface impurities.
    - *Requested:* State explicitly what this work adds beyond these references, presumably the spin-resolved data, and compare the phase-shift values and their uncertainties (Fig. S6b) with Ref. 41.

C. Minor

11. **Label errors in Fig. 2.** In Fig. 2b the y-axis reads "Model DOS", but the caption says these are measured dI/dV spectra at point 0. In Fig. 2f the caption describes Lorentzians at ±0.14 mV, while the text describes YSR peaks plus temperature broadening. These should be made consistent.

12. **FWHM/HWHM mix-up.** On p. 9, "Full-Width at Half-Maximum (HWHM) near 0.5 mV" mixes FWHM and HWHM. Please state which is meant. The comparison to the vortex ZBP of Ref. 58 should be made at matched temperature and resolution.

13. **Inconsistent setpoint signs.** The signs of the tunneling parameters are inconsistent: Fig. 4a has V = 0.1 V, I = 2 nA, but Fig. 4e and Fig. 3e,f,h have -0.1 V, -2 nA for the same kind of image. The Methods call the lock-in modulation "V_bias", whereas the captions use V_osc.

14. **Fig. 5 caption contradicts the text.** The caption calls the area a "surface impurity region", while the text stresses that it has no surface defects.

15. **Language.** "The ZBP in line B as if splits", "splitted peaks" and "Spectra Positions" should be corrected. The Introduction spends a page on general quantum computing that could be cut in favor of a more specific discussion of earlier spin-resolved work on Fe(Te,Se) (Refs. 35-37).

16. **Field-dependence data are hard to read.** In Fig. 4f the dashed lines at about ±0.8 mV should be defined in the caption. Fig. 5l uses a different field sequence (including 0 T and ±0.2, ±0.6 T) from Fig. 5k, which makes the P1/P2 comparison less direct. The authors should use the same sequence or explain why it differs.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a non-magnetic-tip control under the identical field sequence. Also give a quantitative spin-asymmetry analysis with uncertainties for every SP-STS dataset.
2. Measure the Zeeman shift and the temperature dependence of the in-gap peaks at the step edge, the wrinkle and the bend. These directly test the magnetic (YSR) assignment and do not depend on the tip.
3. Add a statistical overview, as a table in the SI, of the defects of each class, how many host near-zero-energy states, and how many were checked with SP-STS.
4. Reframe the abstract and conclusion as a set of cautionary cases with graded confidence: bend (spin-resolved, strongest), step edge and wrinkle (spectroscopic, interpretive), and P2 (unresolved).

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
