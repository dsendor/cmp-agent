Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use time-resolved PL imaging of one MoSe2/WSe2 heterostructure to report an interlayer-exciton (IX) cloud whose 1/e extent grows from about 3 to about 22 μm within 5 ns. They fit this with constant-D diffusion and find D ~ 10^3 cm^2/s, two to four orders of magnitude above earlier time-resolved values in TMD heterostructures. They argue that a "ballistic" model fits better and that the T and density dependence is consistent with a Bose-Hubbard superfluid in the moiré lattice. If the result held, it would be striking: it would give a time-domain counterpart to the long-range cw transport the same group reported earlier (Refs. 64, 65) and connect to the search for exciton superfluidity in moiré systems. In its current form, though, the extraction of the transport parameter, the transport model and the exclusion of conventional mechanisms are not secure enough to support the headline interpretation.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A direct, time-resolved measurement of IX cloud expansion, rather than steady-state cw profiles. This addresses an acknowledged gap in the earlier long-range-transport reports from this sample.
- A systematic map over temperature (1.7 to ~50 K) and excitation power (5 μW to 4 mW) (Figs. 3, 4, S3), with a well-defined region where expansion is fast. Below about 10 K the behaviour is non-monotonic in power, which is not what simple disorder-screening diffusion predicts.
- The lifetime is measured at each (P_ex, T) and fed into the simulations (Fig. S3). The result is checked against the fit window (0-3 ns vs 0-5 ns, Figs. S5, S6). The authors state that the effect survives repeated thermal cycling.
- A comparison regime (T = 50 K, Fig. S2) shows conventional slow expansion in the same sample and setup. This partly serves as an internal reference.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The "ballistic" model, Eq. 2, describes one-directional drift, not ballistic expansion. In Eq. 2 the transport term is −v∇n with a single velocity v. In 1D this is advection of the whole cloud toward +x. The simulated profiles in Fig. S4b show exactly that: the peak moves from x ≈ 0 to x ≈ 4-5 μm by 2.5 ns, and the profile at negative x is suppressed. Ballistic expansion of particles leaving a source needs a distribution of velocity directions, for example terms ±v for the two half-lines in 1D, or a kinetic/Boltzmann description. A pure drift model fitting better than diffusion (Fig. 5) is therefore evidence for a directed flow, for example down a potential gradient, not for scattering-free transport. It also raises the question of why the cloud expands only toward +x.

   This bears directly on two headline claims: "agrees with long-range ballistic transport" and the link to superfluidity. The authors should:
   - state the model explicitly, including its boundary conditions;
   - redo the comparison with a genuinely ballistic (bidirectional or kinetic) model;
   - show I(x,t) on both sides of the excitation spot (negative x) and along y, to establish whether the expansion is symmetric;
   - if it is asymmetric, discuss drift sources. These include strain or dielectric gradients, the sample edge, and the residue of the detached multilayer-graphene electrode at x ≈ 2 μm, y = 0, which lies right in the transport path (SI, Heterostructure section).

2. d_1/e is poorly defined in the fast-transport regime, and the HS edge is not treated. The profiles are fit with a single exponential over x = 0-9 μm, from the spot to the HS edge (Fig. 1 caption). The highest quoted values, d_1/e ≈ 20-22 μm (Fig. 1b-d), are more than twice the fit window. A 1/e length that much larger than the window means the profile is almost flat across it (Fig. 1a, t = 11 ns). There, d_1/e diverges for small changes in slope and becomes very sensitive to background, to a constant offset from scattered or waveguided PL, and to any edge accumulation or enhanced edge emission.

   The upward-curving late-time d_1/e(t) at 500 μW (Fig. 2) is what one would expect from this divergence. Neither model reproduces it. The simulations treat the system as unbounded 1D (SI), yet the HS ends at about 9-12 μm, and particles reaching a reflecting or absorbing edge change the profile shape.

   The authors should:
   - give uncertainties on d_1/e(t) from the fits;
   - include the HS boundary in the simulations (reflecting and absorbing cases);
   - fit the simulated I(x,t) directly to the measured I(x,t), rather than reducing both to d_1/e. The SI already computes I(x,t) (Fig. S4), so this is a direct comparison;
   - alternatively, use a moment-based width (mean-squared displacement) restricted to the region where the signal is well above background.

3. Interaction-driven (dipolar-repulsion) expansion is not excluded, and it predicts the observed trends. The SI itself states that interactions give an effective D = D0(1 + n u0/kBT). With δE ≈ 3 meV and kBT ≈ 0.15 meV at 1.7 K, this enhancement factor is about 20. It grows with density and falls as 1/T, which is qualitatively the trend in Fig. 3a,b below the high-power turnover. Repulsion-driven fast expansion of IXs in MoSe2/WSe2 is documented (Ref. 55) and produces super-diffusive, density-dependent early-time expansion. A constant-D fit cannot capture this.

   The authors argue that 3 meV is too small to screen 10-20 meV disorder. That argument concerns screening, not the pressure-driven drift term, which acts on density gradients regardless of disorder screening. The decrease of D above 0.5 mW also has conventional candidates: laser heating, the shortened lifetime at high power (Fig. S3), and exciton-exciton annihilation.

   The authors should:
   - simulate the nonlinear drift-diffusion equation with the measured τ(P,T) and an independently constrained D0, and compare it quantitatively with d_1/e(t) and I(x,t);
   - show the time-resolved PL energy shift versus x and t, which tracks the local density and gradient directly;
   - show expansion after the pulse ends (t > 80 ns). There, interaction-driven expansion slows as the density drops, whereas a superfluid or ballistic picture makes a different prediction.

B. Should address

4. The χ² comparison (Fig. 5) has no measurement uncertainties and no clear significance. χ² = Σ(m_t − s_t)²/(ν s_t) uses neither experimental error bars nor a standard normalization. All values are at or below about 1.4. It is therefore unclear whether either model is rejected, or whether the difference between χ_D² and χ_v² is significant given point-to-point scatter, which is visible in Fig. 2 at 25 μW.

   Both models also fail at late times at 500 μW (Fig. 2b, last points ≈ 22 μm vs fit ≈ 17 μm). The authors should give d_1/e uncertainties, use a standard reduced χ², and report fit residuals. The claim "agrees with ballistic transport" should be limited to what the statistics support.

5. Internal inconsistency between "ballistic" and the mean free path estimate. The SI estimates l_m ≈ 2 μm for the largest D. That is about 1/10 of the cloud extent and smaller than the 9-μm fit window. On length scales much larger than l_m, transport should look diffusive, which contradicts the conclusion that the expansion is ballistic over about 20 μm. The SI also notes that the τ_m and l_m formulas assume diffusive transport.

   In addition:
   - the formula τ_m ~ m D0/kBT should use the non-interacting D0, but the estimate inserts the full D;
   - m ≈ m0 is assumed without justification.

   The main text should quote l_m and τ_m with these caveats, and reconcile l_m ≈ 2 μm with the ballistic interpretation.

6. Superfluid / Bose-Hubbard interpretation lacks direct evidence and rests partly on circular estimates. The specific problems:
   - No coherence measurement. This group has done first-order-coherence interferometry in GaAs systems (Refs. 4, 20).
   - No threshold or critical density, critical velocity, or abrupt change in the PL spectrum is shown.
   - J ≈ 1.6 K is inferred from the observed T_c ≈ 10 K, and the moiré amplitude of about 8 meV is then inferred from J. This agreement with the literature is therefore not an independent test.
   - The filling N ≈ 1/2 relies on the plate-capacitor formula, which is known to underestimate density in correlated dipolar liquids, often by a large factor. It also relies on a twist angle of 1.1° estimated from edges, with no uncertainty given. With a different density, N ≈ 1/2 could just as well be N ≈ 1 or above.
   - The authors themselves report disorder (10-20 meV linewidths) far larger than J. In the BH picture, that favours a Bose glass.
   - The double-occupancy line (Fig. S7) appears at 4 mW, while D already falls from about 0.5 mW. A clearer link between the transport suppression and the onset of N ≥ 1, for example via spectrally resolved transport, would make the comparison less qualitative.

   At minimum, the abstract and summary should present the BH connection as speculative, give uncertainties on n and δθ, and acknowledge the Bose-glass tension.

7. The effective temperature at high excitation power is not known. At 0.5-4 mW of quasi-cw 80-ns pulses, the local lattice and exciton temperatures may differ substantially from the bath temperature. The PL lineshape or the high-energy tail could provide an estimate. Because the main evidence is the T dependence (efficient transport vanishing above about 10 K) and the power dependence (suppression above 0.5 mW), the authors should estimate the exciton temperature at each power. They should also check whether the high-power decrease of D tracks heating, for example by comparing the T dependence at low power with the power dependence at 1.7 K.

8. Single sample, and a single excitation position. The SI states that the efficient transport has been seen in only one sample. Within that sample, position dependence matters, because the effect could be tied to a local feature such as the electrode residue, the edge or local strain. The authors should show the transport for several excitation spots and directions in this HS. They should also note that this sample was used in Refs. 64, 65, so those cw results are not independent confirmation.

9. The spectrally integrated signal mixes several IX species. The transport images integrate all IX PL above 1.459 eV. That includes localized narrow lines at low density and the higher-energy double-occupancy line at high density (Fig. S7), as well as possibly spin-singlet/triplet IXs, which have different g-factors and different radiative efficiencies. Changes in which species emits as the cloud spreads and dilutes can mimic profile broadening. Spectrally resolved d_1/e(t), even for two energy windows, would test this.

10. The 1D approximation is not quantified. The cloud expands in 2D within a finite-width strip (Fig. S1b), but the simulations are 1D. How much this changes the D and v estimates depends on the strip width relative to the cloud size and on what happens at the lateral edges. A 2D simulation with the actual MoSe2 outline would show whether the order-of-magnitude D survives.

11. Comparison with prior fast-transport and superfluidity reports. Ref. 53 reports distinct transport phases in MoSe2/WSe2 with higher D at high density. Ref. 55 reports repulsion-driven transport. Ref. 63 reports imaging of IX superfluidity. A quantitative comparison with these, covering D, density and temperature ranges, and twist angle, would place the "orders of magnitude higher" claim in context. The current comparison (D up to 0.1-2 cm²/s) leaves them aside.

C. Minor

12. Fig. S7 caption gives P_ex = 5, 50 and 4000 μW, but the panel labels read 5, 500 and 4000 μW.
13. The main text gives a laser edge sharpness of 0.3 ns. The SI gives a rise time of 0.1 ns and a fall time of 0.3 ns. Please make these consistent.
14. The main text quotes a disorder amplitude of about 10 meV "from the PL linewidth". The SI quotes linewidths of about 14 meV (low density) and about 20 meV (efficient-transport regime). Please state how 10 meV follows from these.
15. Figs. 3c, 4c and 5c,d are interpolated colour maps. Please mark the actual measured (P_ex, T) points and explain the blank regions.
16. Fig. 1a shows profiles up to t = 11 ns, but d_1/e(t) and the fits stop at 5 ns. Please explain the choice of fit window and show d_1/e beyond 5 ns.
17. The excitation spot size and optical spatial resolution should be stated explicitly, in addition to the 1.1 μm APD aperture. Please also explain why the DX profile is a faithful proxy for Λ(x).
18. The phrase "mean free time τ_m ∝ D" in the main text needs the T dependence (τ_m ∝ mD/kBT). Otherwise the comparison across temperatures is misleading.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Fit full I(x,t) maps, including negative x and the HS boundary, with three models: diffusion, nonlinear drift-diffusion with dipolar repulsion, and a bidirectional ballistic or kinetic model. Use error bars throughout and report likelihood or reduced-χ² comparisons.
- Add spatially and temporally resolved PL energy (density) maps, and expansion after the pulse ends. These separate pressure-driven expansion from scattering-free transport.
- Add a coherence measurement, for example shift-interferometry of the IX emission, in the efficient-transport region. This would turn the superfluid connection from "consistent with" into a test.
- Repeat the key measurement at several spots in this HS, and ideally in a second sample with a known twist angle, for example confirmed by SHG on a sacrificial region.

5. Verdict: major revision
