Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use time-resolved PL imaging to follow how an indirect-exciton (IX) cloud in a MoSe2/WSe2 heterostructure expands. In a window of low temperature (T below about 10 K) and intermediate excitation power (about 100 to 1000 µW), the 1/e extent d_{1/e} grows nearly linearly in time, to about 20 µm within 5 ns. Fitting with diffusion, ballistic and drift-diffusion models gives very large transport coefficients (µ of about 10^4 to 10^5 cm^2/eV s). The authors call this regime "efficient transport kinetics". They argue it is inconsistent with ordinary diffusive transport and qualitatively consistent with IX superfluidity, including the Bose-Hubbard picture. Direct kinetic measurements of IX transport in van der Waals heterostructures are valuable, and a regime with this temperature and density dependence would be of broad interest. As presented, though, the quantitative extraction and the exclusion of conventional mechanisms are thinner than the headline wording suggests.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Time-resolved imaging of d_{1/e}(t) separates linear-in-time from square-root-in-time growth. Continuous-wave measurements, which make up most of the vdW IX transport literature, cannot do this.
- A systematic two-dimensional map over temperature and excitation power (Fig. 3, panels a3, b3, c3) shows a bounded region of fast expansion. The result is therefore not a single-condition observation.
- The non-monotonic dependence on temperature and power is reproduced by three different analysis frameworks (Fig. 3). So the qualitative trend does not hinge on one model.
- The authors state that the phenomena reproduce after several cooldowns to 2 K and warm-ups to room temperature.
- The interpretive claims are carefully hedged ("qualitatively consistent"), and the paper says plainly that the models are rough characterisations, not an accurate description.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The key observable, d_{1/e}, is extracted beyond the spatial window where it is defined, and could be shaped by the sample edge and by depletion near the spot instead of by transport.
   - What the paper does. In Fig. 1a, d_{1/e} comes from exponential fits to I(x,t) over x = 0 to 9 µm, which the caption says runs from the excitation spot to the heterostructure edge. Yet the values plotted in Figs. 1b, 1c, 1d and 2 reach 20 to 23 µm. A 1/e length 2.5 times the fit window is a measure of how flat the profile is, not a distance the excitons travelled, and it is very sensitive to noise and to small distortions of the profile.
   - Two features of Fig. 1a suggest the flattening is not purely expansion. The late-time profiles (2.5 and 11 ns) are non-monotonic: the intensity at x = 0 falls below the intensity at x of about 4 to 6 µm. The edge at about 9 µm is also a reflecting boundary at which IXs can pile up.
   - Mechanisms that would flatten the profile and raise d_{1/e} without fast IX motion:
     - (i) density-dependent losses at the spot: exciton-exciton annihilation, Auger processes, or a reduced radiative fraction from screening or Mott dissociation (compare the "halo" effects of Ref. 39);
     - (ii) local heating at the spot;
     - (iii) accumulation at the heterostructure edge;
     - (iv) a drop of IX emission efficiency near the spot caused by the energy landscape U(x).
   - Partial argument already in the paper. The fast regime disappears at the highest powers (2 to 4 mW, Fig. 1d), which argues against the simplest saturation picture. It does not rule out (iii) or (iv), and it does not make a 23 µm length inside a 9 µm window meaningful.
   - Why it matters. The headline result is the "anomalously high mobility" and the near-quadratic growth of d_{1/e}^2. Both are computed directly from these d_{1/e} values (Figs. 2 and 3).
   - What the authors should do:
     - Show the full normalised profiles I(x,t), including x < 0 and beyond the edge, at several times for the 500 µW and 25 µW cases.
     - Use a metric that stays within the measured window: the mean-square displacement of the normalised profile, the position of a fixed fractional intensity, or the arrival time of the PL front at fixed x.
     - Show that the spatially integrated IX intensity and the IX decay time at x = 0 do not change anomalously in the fast regime, which would rule out density-dependent losses.
     - Report the IX energy profile E(x,t) alongside, so that changes in U(x) and in the interaction shift are visible.
     - Fit the simulations to the full I(x,t), not to d_{1/e}(t) alone.
   - Without this, the extracted D, v and µ are not established, even as order-of-magnitude estimates.

B. Should address

2. The fitted models do not reproduce the data they are used to quantify.
   - In Fig. 2, at 500 µW, all three simulations stay below the measured d_{1/e}(t) from about 3 ns on. The data reach about 22 µm at 5 ns, while the curves stay near 13 to 15 µm. The data also curve upward at late times, whereas the models curve downward.
   - The parameters in Fig. 3 are therefore least-squares compromises to a functional form that does not describe the data. How they compare to each other across conditions, and how large they are in absolute terms, is uncertain.
   - The authors should:
     - give uncertainties for D, v and µ (from fit covariance or from varying the fit window);
     - state which parameters were fixed (τ, u0, U(x), the generation profile) and how the result changes when U(x) or u0 is varied within its uncertainty;
     - comment on the late-time upturn.

3. The three parameter sets are not mutually consistent, and the conversion to a mean free path needs to be shown in the main text.
   - The Einstein relation used in Eq. 3 gives D' = µ k_B T. With µ of about 5 × 10^4 cm^2/eV s at 1.7 K (Fig. 3, panel c1), D' is of order 10 cm^2/s. Fig. 3, panel a1 instead reports D of about 10^3 cm^2/s.
   - Similarly, τ_m = µ m with m close to m0 gives τ_m of a few tens of ps. Combined with a thermal velocity at 2 K (about 5 × 10^5 cm/s), this gives a mean free path of order 0.1 to 1 µm. That is well below the 5 to 20 µm scales cited as "long-range ballistic transport".
   - The authors should put the numbers for τ_m and l_m and their derivation in the main text, and explain which effective temperature (lattice or exciton) enters the Einstein relation. They should also reconcile the claim of long-range ballistic transport with the extracted mean free path.

4. Drift down the built-in gradient is an alternative source of the linear expansion.
   - The model includes U(x) dropping by about 10 meV over 10 µm, that is a gradient of about 1 meV/µm. With µ of about 10^4 cm^2/eV s, this alone drives a drift velocity of about 1 µm/ns, comparable to the measured front speed of about 4 µm/ns. Drift at constant velocity in a constant gradient also gives d_{1/e} roughly linear in t.
   - The linear growth therefore does not by itself point to ballistic or superfluid motion. How the result depends on U(x), which is determined in the SI, is central.
   - The authors should:
     - show d_{1/e}(t) toward a region without the gradient (for example x < 0, or another direction from the spot), or from a second excitation spot where U(x) differs;
     - show the measured E(x) used to build U(x).
   - An expansion that is symmetric, or that does not depend on the sign of the gradient, would strengthen the claim considerably.

5. The argument that the transport is not diffusive is not complete.
   - The text (Comparison of IX transport with diffusive transport) argues that diffusive transport should increase with T and with n, so suppression at higher T and n rules it out.
   - However, in the clean limit, conventional non-superfluid transport also loses mobility with increasing T, through phonon scattering. At high density it can be suppressed by heating, by annihilation, or by an IX-to-plasma crossover. The low-density suppression is the usual localisation effect.
   - The observed trends are therefore expected from a combination of conventional mechanisms. The authors' own argument that k_B T and the interaction energy are both below the disorder amplitude covers only the thermally activated hopping scenario.
   - Fig. 3, panel c1 also shows µ rising again between about 25 and 40 K, which is not discussed.
   - The authors should:
     - discuss phonon-limited band-like IX transport and estimate its mobility scale;
     - discuss the high-T upturn;
     - temper the statement that the result "is inconsistent with the diffusive transport", or support it quantitatively.

6. Only one heterostructure is reported.
   - All data come from one MoSe2/WSe2 sample and apparently from one excitation position.
   - The claim of an anomalous, possibly collective, transport regime would be much stronger if the fast-expansion window and the threshold near 10 K appeared in at least one more sample, or at several spots on this sample with different local disorder.
   - Spot-to-spot or device-to-device spread of the crossover temperature and of the optimal power should be reported.

7. The exciton temperature and laser heating are not established.
   - The fast regime appears at 0.1 to 1 mW of focused excitation, resonant with the WSe2 DX, and it disappears at 2 to 4 mW and above about 10 K. Heating of the lattice and of the IX gas by the laser could cause both cutoffs.
   - The authors should estimate or measure the local IX temperature, for example from the high-energy tail of the IX PL or from the DX linewidth at the spot, as a function of P_ex. They should then show the T and P_ex maps against effective rather than bath temperature.

8. The comparison with Bose-Hubbard theory rests on loosely calibrated inputs.
   - Occupation N of about 1/2 per moiré site is obtained from a 3 meV energy shift using the mean-field u0. The text itself notes that correlations lower u0 and therefore raise n for a given shift, which could push N towards 1, where the BH model predicts a Mott insulator.
   - The moiré period and twist angle, and the stacking (H or R), are not given in the main text, yet N per site depends on them.
   - The PL linewidth of about 10 meV is taken as the disorder amplitude. That identification is not justified: the linewidth also contains homogeneous and multi-species contributions.
   - The authors should:
     - give the twist angle and its uncertainty and the moiré period;
     - give n with a range spanning mean-field and correlated estimates;
     - state what BH phase follows for the full plausible range of N and of the disorder strength;
     - compare the ~10 K threshold with a quantitative superfluid-transition estimate (for example a BKT temperature for the stated n and mass).
   - A coherence measurement, such as first-order coherence by shift-interferometry in the fast regime, would be the most direct test of the superfluidity interpretation. If one is not feasible, the authors should say so explicitly.

C. Minor

9. The IX lifetime τ, which enters all three models, is given only in the SI. Its value, and any dependence on T and P_ex, should be stated in the main text, together with the time resolution of the APD detection relative to the 0.3 ns edges and the early-time data in Fig. 1.

10. The profiles are measured by scanning the APD across the image plane. Each x point is therefore recorded separately. The authors should state how laser-power and alignment drift during a scan are controlled, and whether points were remeasured.

11. Fig. 3, panels a3, b3 and c3: the white (no-data) region at high T and low P_ex should be explained. The density of measured (T, P_ex) points underlying the interpolated colour maps should be indicated, for example by overlaying markers.

12. The units cm^2/(eV s) for µ are unusual. They should be defined explicitly (drift velocity per energy gradient), and the relation to cm^2/(V s) should be noted for readers comparing with charge mobilities.

13. "Efficient transport kinetics" is not defined quantitatively. A working criterion (for example a d_{1/e}^2(t) exponent above some threshold, or µ above some value) would make the boundary in Fig. 3 reproducible.

14. The comparison with earlier vdW studies (Fig. S12) is central to the word "anomalous". A short summary in the main text, with the d_{1/e} growth rates from Refs. 52 to 54, 56, 57 and 61 at comparable T and density, would help.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Show I(x,t) and E(x,t) maps directly, and adopt a displacement metric that stays within the measured window (mean-square displacement or front arrival time). This addresses concern 1 and makes the transport claims model-independent.
- Measure expansion in more than one direction from the spot, or at several spots, to separate gradient-driven drift from intrinsic fast transport, and to show reproducibility within the sample and, ideally, in a second sample.
- Add an independent probe of the proposed collective state in the fast regime, such as spatial coherence by interferometry or a linewidth or energy signature tracking the same (T, P_ex) boundary.
- Report the parameters with uncertainties, reconcile D, µ and τ_m, and give a quantitative superfluid-temperature estimate to set against the ~10 K threshold.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
