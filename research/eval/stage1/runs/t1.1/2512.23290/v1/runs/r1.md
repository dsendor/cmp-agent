Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript proposes a new kind of spin-polarized STM probe. It is a FIB-fabricated tip made from the higher-order topological insulator alpha-Bi4Br4, whose helical edge modes are meant to act as a non-magnetic spin filter, with the selected spin set by the sign of the bias. The authors apply it to the layered altermagnet candidate KV2Se2O. They report a sqrt2 x sqrt2 density-wave modulation of the V sublattices, Zeeman shifts of defect-bound states that move in opposite directions on the Vx and Vy sublattices, and a qx/qy QPI intensity anisotropy that rotates by pi/2 when the bias is reversed. They take the last result as momentum-space evidence for d-wave altermagnetic spin splitting. If it holds up, a field-insensitive spin-polarized tip that needs no training would be a significant technical advance, and local evidence for altermagnetic order in KV2Se2O, a debated material, would interest a broad audience. Both claims, however, rest on a spin-filtering mechanism that the paper asserts but does not model or independently calibrate with enough care.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The tip concept is original and, if validated, broadly useful: no magnetization, insensitive to field, and robust to mild tip-sample contact.
- Building the tip involves a substantial FIB nanofabrication effort. Its spectroscopic characterization on Au(111) reproduces the bulk-gap and edge-mode spectra of alpha-Bi4Br4 measured with PtIr tips (Fig. 2b,f), and the tip can be switched in situ between bulk-state and edge-mode behaviour.
- On Fe1.02Te, the helical-edge-mode tips resolve the bicollinear stripe modulation, which is reported to be absent with PtIr tips (Fig. 2g,h; Supplementary Note 3).
- The real-space analysis of KV2Se2O is careful and informative: four arrow-shaped V-site defect types, their long-range alternation giving a sqrt2 x sqrt2 supercell that also appears on 2x1-reconstructed surfaces, and a ~20 meV gap that closes near the ~100 K transition (Fig. 3; Extended Data Fig. 5).
- Defect-bound states show linear, sublattice-dependent Zeeman shifts of opposite sign (Fig. 4g,h). This is a spin-sensitive observable that does not depend on the new tip.
- The QPI anisotropy rotates with bias sign, is absent with PtIr tips (Fig. 5b,c), appears with several tips and samples, and swaps between rotated domains measured with the same tip (Extended Data Fig. 10). Together these give a coherent, multi-pronged dataset.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The spin-filtering mechanism of the helical-edge-mode tip is asserted, not modelled.
   - What the paper says: Fig. 1c-e, pages 3-4 and the Discussion argue that, because the edge states are T-paired, "electrons injected into or launched from the tip will carry opposite spins," with the spin selected by bias polarity.
   - Why this is not enough: in equilibrium the Kramers partners at a given energy have equal amplitude at any point on the edge, so the local spin density of a non-magnetic, time-reversal-invariant tip is zero. In a two-terminal junction with one non-magnetic electrode, Onsager reciprocity also requires the linear conductance to be even in the sample magnetization. A spin-dependent dI/dV can therefore arise only as a non-equilibrium, bias-odd effect, and it needs an asymmetry in how injected or extracted electrons leave the apex (edge termination, asymmetric branches, connection to the PtIr reservoir). The Discussion itself says that two branches cancel and that P depends on apex geometry. The "topology-guaranteed spin filter" and "simultaneous detection of opposite-spin states at positive and negative biases" therefore depend on a geometry-specific, non-equilibrium mechanism the paper does not describe.
   - Why it matters: everything in Fig. 5, and the interpretation of the Fe1.02Te contrast, depends on this mechanism.
   - What to do:
     - Provide a minimal tunneling or Landauer model with the tip apex as a terminal on a helical edge connected to a reservoir.
     - Show under what conditions the spin-resolved dI/dV is nonzero and odd in bias.
     - Show how large P can be, and how it depends on apex geometry and on the angle between the edge spin axis and the sample moments.
     - State explicitly how this is reconciled with the Onsager constraint.

2. The independent calibration of the tip's spin sensitivity is thin, and the spin assignment risks circularity.
   - What the paper shows: only two tips were tested on Fe1.02Te (Methods), and the evidence is a single pair of maps at ±50 mV (Fig. 2h).
   - Why that is not enough: bias-reversed contrast of a stripe modulation is not unique to spin-polarized tunneling. A charge or orbital modulation that accompanies the monoclinic bicollinear phase can invert between filled and empty states, as CDW contrast commonly does. Seeing stripes at all, and not seeing them with PtIr, argues for spin sensitivity, but it does not establish that the polarization reverses with bias.
   - The selection problem: on KV2Se2O, five of ten tips "show clear spin resolution." If that classification came from the KV2Se2O anisotropy itself, the selection is circular.
   - What to do:
     - Show the energy dependence of the Fe1.02Te stripe contrast over a range of positive and negative biases with the helical-edge-mode tip.
     - Compare it under matched conditions with a conventional Fe- or Cr-coated magnetic tip, or with field-trained tips, so the expected spin-contrast energy dependence is known.
     - Most decisive: use the in-situ switch between bulk-state and edge-mode tip states (Extended Data Fig. 3e,f). Re-measure the same FOV on Fe1.02Te and on KV2Se2O with the same tip in both states. The stripe contrast and the qx/qy anisotropy should vanish in the bulk-state configuration. This control removes differences in sample region, defect population and apex shape between the PtIr and BiBr datasets.
     - State the criterion for "spin resolution" used to select the five tips, and whether every tip calibrated on Au(111) and landed on ordered KV2Se2O was included in Fig. 5l.

3. The qx/qy anisotropy in Fig. 5 needs quantitative controls and error estimates before it can serve as the "smoking gun."
   - What is shown: P reaches about ±12% for one tip, is about 3% for Tip3, and Tip1 has points only at negative energies (Fig. 5l). No error bars are given.
   - Analysis choices that could create or alter the effect:
     - The FTs are twofold-symmetrized.
     - The integration windows are moved with energy.
     - The normalization uses the intensity of surface (K-reconstruction) Bragg peaks, which is itself bias-dependent.
   - Sample-side explanations that are not excluded:
     - An imbalance between Vx- and Vy-site defects in a given FOV would make qx^1 and qy^1 unequal even for spin-summed tunneling.
     - Particle-hole asymmetry of the density-wave gap edges (Fig. 4d) could make that imbalance appear differently at +E and -E.
     - The PtIr reference (Fig. 5b,c) is at ±15 meV in, presumably, a different FOV and sample. The BiBr data are at ±10 and ±15 meV.
   - What to do:
     - Report the number of Vx and Vy defects in each FOV used for Fig. 5 and Extended Data Figs. 8-10.
     - Give error bars on P, for example from window size and position, sub-FOV bootstrapping, and symmetrized against unsymmetrized FTs.
     - Show the raw unsymmetrized FTs.
     - Present PtIr data at the same energies, ideally on the same cleave.
     - Promote the domain-swap observation of Extended Data Fig. 10 to the main text, with quantified P in both domains. Two time-reversed domains measured with one tip at the same bias give opposite anisotropy. That is the strongest available argument against tip anisotropy or energy-dependent sample artifacts, and it deserves quantitative treatment.

4. The magnetic moment direction is stated inconsistently, and the Zeeman analysis does not support the moment value quoted.
   - The inconsistency: page 6 concludes "an in-plane AFM order," and the DFT uses in-plane Néel order (Extended Data Fig. 7). The Methods (page 18) explain the low tip yield by saying the KV2Se2O AFM order "is out-of-plane."
   - The field geometry: the Zeeman data (Fig. 4g,h) use fields perpendicular to the sample. For moments that lie strictly in-plane, the first-order shift of a spin-polarized bound state in a perpendicular field vanishes. Opposite, linear shifts for Vx and Vy therefore imply an out-of-plane spin component, or need another explanation such as field-induced canting.
   - The moment value: the 1.13 muB figure comes from 0.9 meV over 14 T. That number is an effective g-factor of the bound quasiparticle state. It is not the ordered V moment, so the agreement with neutron diffraction (ref. 67) is not meaningful as stated.
   - The resolution: at 4.8 K, thermal broadening (~1.4 meV) exceeds the 0.9 meV splitting. The temperature at which Fig. 4g,h were taken, and which tip was used, are not given.
   - What to do:
     - Resolve the moment direction.
     - Explain how a perpendicular field produces opposite linear shifts for the assumed spin orientation.
     - Report the measurement temperature and energy resolution.
     - Show the field dependence at intermediate fields to establish linearity.
     - Give the number of Vx and Vy defects measured and the spread of their shifts (Supplementary Note 5).
     - Either drop the comparison with the neutron moment or justify it.

B. Should address

5. Which tip recorded the gap, density-wave and QPI dispersion data is not stated, and tip DOS could imprint on them.
   - The problem: the edge-mode tip has a Tomonaga-Luttinger-like suppression of DOS at EF, and the bulk-state tip has a 0.25 eV gap (Fig. 2f). Either could distort or mimic the ~20 meV hard gap of KV2Se2O (Figs. 3b, 4e), the gap map (Fig. 3c), its closing above 100 K, and the gap-edge spectral weight in Fig. 4d.
   - What to do: state the tip for every panel, and show the KV2Se2O gap and its temperature dependence with PtIr tips.

6. Alternative origins of the edge-mode tip spectrum are not excluded.
   - The claim: the zero-bias suppression on Au(111) is attributed to a helical TLL, and the TLL fit is taken to "prove that the tunneling is indeed mediated by helical edge modes" (Methods, Extended Data Fig. 3b-e).
   - The alternatives: a zero-bias anomaly can also come from dynamical Coulomb blockade or disorder at a poorly conducting apex. Ga implantation and amorphization from FIB milling, or the Pt bonding pads, could produce such an apex. A power-law fit does not distinguish these cases.
   - What to do:
     - Give the fitted alpha and its tip-to-tip spread.
     - Show the temperature dependence if available.
     - Discuss Ga contamination, citing any EDS or TEM of the apex.
     - Report how the switching pulses change the apex.

7. The spin quantization axis of the tip relative to the sample is not characterized.
   - The paper says the edge modes are nearly Sz-quantized along a crystal axis of the tip, so the measured contrast should scale with the projection of that axis onto the Néel vector. The orientation of the Bi4Br4 crystal in each tip relative to the sample axes is not reported.
   - What to do: report that orientation for the tips in Fig. 5l, from the SEM geometry and fabrication. Discuss whether the sign and magnitude of P, which vary between tips, are consistent with it. This would also test the explanation for why only half the tips are spin-sensitive.

8. The density-wave assignment and its relation to the magnetic order need more support.
   - The sqrt2 x sqrt2 supercell is inferred from defect arrow shapes and their correlations (Fig. 3d-g). Its periodicity coincides with the sqrt2 x sqrt2 K reconstruction.
   - The 2x1 surface data help, but a few points remain open:
     - Whether Q* (identified with the surface Bragg vector in Fig. 4c) is visible as an intrinsic LDOS modulation away from defects.
     - Whether the gap closing near 100 K is a magnetic or a density-wave transition.
     - Whether the density-wave pattern, which keeps only M_1-10, preserves the spin-group symmetry relating Vx and Vy assumed in Fig. 5e.
   - What to do: show the Q* modulation in defect-free regions and its energy dependence (contrast inversion across the gap), and state the symmetry of the combined density-wave plus Néel state.

9. The theory comparison is only semi-quantitative.
   - U = 1 eV is chosen as the "best fit."
   - The DFT band in Fig. 4d does not include the Q* folding that dominates the data.
   - The spin-resolved JDOS in Fig. 5e is not computed with any model of the tip's spin selection.
   - What to do:
     - Compare the energies and band dispersions of the Néel, C-type, G-type and non-magnetic states against the measured q_x^1 dispersion.
     - Include the sqrt2 x sqrt2 folding in the Wannier model.
     - Compute the expected magnitude of M(q, eV) for a given tip polarization, so the 12% can be put in context.

10. The claims outrun the evidence in places.
    - The abstract says "atomic-registered mapping demonstrates the checkerboard antiferromagnetic order." Fig. 3 shows a structural or charge density-wave pattern from defect geometry. No spin-polarized real-space image distinguishes Vx and Vy contrast reversing with bias.
    - "Unambiguously demonstrate a 2D d-wave altermagnetic order" is likewise strong, given that the stacking (C- versus G-type) is open and the bulk is reported as G-type (ref. 67).
    - "Decisively rules out" tip artifacts (page 7) depends on items 2 and 3.
    - What to do: either present a sublattice-resolved, bias-reversed spin-polarized topograph or dI/dV map, or moderate the wording.

11. Statistics on samples and tips are incomplete.
    - The number of cleaves and samples behind Fig. 5 and Extended Data Figs. 8-10 is not given.
    - It is not stated whether each spin-sensitive tip was first validated on Fe1.02Te or only on Au(111).
    - Whether the effect survives tip pulses or changes of the apex is not reported.
    - What to do: tabulate tip, sample, FOV, energies and P for every dataset.

C. Minor

12. The sign convention of P conflicts with the text.
    - The text says q_x^1 dominates at positive bias, and P = (I(q_x^1) - I(q_y^1))/(I(q_x^1) + I(q_y^1)), which would make P positive at positive energy.
    - Fig. 5l shows P negative at positive energies.
    - Please reconcile the definition, the axis labels and the arrow labels ("up < down") in Fig. 5f-k.

13. The TLL formula in the Methods is incomplete. The standard form includes |Gamma((1+alpha)/2 + i epsilon/(2 pi k_B T))|^2. Please give the full expression used and the fitted parameters.

14. The panel order in Fig. 5 is confusing: panel l sits above panel e, and panel e is placed among b-k. "BiBr" and "Bi4Br4" tips are used interchangeably; pick one.

15. The setup conditions of several figures (Figs. 3b,c; 4a-h; 5) do not state the tip type or the measurement temperature.

16. Typos and other small issues:
    - "Extended Dada Fig." (several places), "4. 8K", "he National Natural Science Foundation".
    - Reference 68 is cited in the Methods without context. Check the numbering of refs. 68-74 against the main reference list.

17. Explain in the main text the gap-map definition used in Fig. 3c (current below 0.3 pA), and how setpoint normalization affects it.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Use the in-situ switch between bulk-state and edge-mode tip states as a same-tip, same-FOV control on both Fe1.02Te and KV2Se2O. This is the cleanest way to show that the contrast comes from the helical modes.
2. Add a minimal transport model of tunneling through a helical edge terminating at an apex. It should give the sign, bias-oddness and expected magnitude of the spin contrast, consistent with Onsager reciprocity.
3. Move the domain-swap data (Extended Data Fig. 10) into the main text with quantified P. Add defect counts per sublattice and error bars for every FOV.
4. Acquire a sublattice-resolved, bias-reversed spin-polarized image of the Vx and Vy sites to support the real-space AFM claim directly. Resolve the in-plane versus out-of-plane moment direction together with the Zeeman data.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
