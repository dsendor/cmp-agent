Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors drive ~2 ps current pulses through a 20 nm NbN strip and a 50 nm YBCO strip embedded in an on-chip coplanar waveguide with photoconductive switches. In NbN they observe a sharp collapse of the superconducting transmission at J_T,peak ≈ 220 GA/m² (≈ 2.2 × the DC critical current density at 7 K). They identify this threshold as the intrinsic depairing current J_c* and reproduce its temperature dependence and the shape of the transition with a dirty-limit Usadel calculation. In YBCO the suppression is gradual, which they attribute to d-wave nodes. Using picosecond on-chip transport to outrun vortex entry and self-heating, and so reach the pair-breaking limit in a wide strip, is attractive and timely. The NbN data are clean and internally consistent. The d-wave interpretation and the strength of the quantitative theory comparison are the weakest parts.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear, well-calibrated threshold in NbN that is the same for positive and negative pulses (Fig. 4a, S14a). It is measured at four temperatures, and its temperature dependence differs clearly from that of the DC J_c (Fig. 5a).
- A strong internal calibration check (Fig. S12). A DC bias of 19.25 mA shifts the picosecond threshold by 19.45 mA. This supports the absolute current calibration, and it shows that the threshold acts on the total current density, as depairing requires.
- J_c* and J_c are measured on the same device. The DC J_c is checked against delay time to exclude contact heating (Fig. S6), and the input parameters (ξ from B_c2(T), λ from the fitted kinetic inductance, R_n from transport) are taken from the same sample.
- The microscopic Usadel modelling (S10) reproduces the abruptness of the collapse at 7 K and the broadening at 12 K better than tDGL (Fig. S20). This gives a physically interesting point about where GL-type descriptions fail far below T_c.
- The method could be applied to other superconductors, including ones that are hard to study spectroscopically.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The d-wave interpretation of the YBCO data is not supported against simpler explanations (abstract; Fig. 4c,d; Discussion p. 10; S6).
   - What the paper claims: the gradual suppression in YBCO "reflects its d-wave symmetry", J_c* "is not a well-defined quantity" in a d-wave superconductor, and the method offers "a new approach to probing the gap symmetry".
   - No calculation backs this. There is no d-wave counterpart to the Usadel/BCS model used for NbN. The theory of the nonlinear Meissner effect and of supercurrent-driven depairing in nodal superconductors predicts a specific, modest reduction of n_s with v_s at low current. It also predicts that j(Q) still has a maximum, so a depairing current still exists in a d-wave superconductor; it is not undefined. The statement that J_c* is not well defined for a d-wave gap is therefore at best imprecise, and the absence of a local maximum in Fig. 4d needs a quantitative explanation.
   - Possible reason no maximum was seen: the authors' own numbers imply λ(50 K) ≈ 0.7 µm (from P_L = 21 µm and d = 50 nm, S8.3). With a typical YBCO ξ of ~2 nm this gives a GL-scale depairing current of order 10² GA/m². Whether the measured range (J_T,peak up to ~300 GA/m²) actually reaches the expected maximum is not discussed.
   - Competing explanation: the very large λ, the broad resistive transition (Fig. S10b, ~84 to 90 K) and the rounded DC I-V onset at 50 to 60 K (Fig. 2b) point to a granular or inhomogeneous film. The film is sputtered on r-cut sapphire with a CeO2 buffer, where twin and low-angle grain boundaries are common. A distribution of local critical currents, or phase slips at weak links, would give exactly the gradual, monotonic suppression seen. The authors themselves note (p. 10) that polycrystalline films show boundary-dominated nonlinear response.
   - The two materials are also compared at different reduced temperatures (T/T_c ≈ 0.48 for NbN, 0.59 for YBCO) and with different degrees of disorder.
   - The third argument against vortices (p. 9, "distinct responses ... suggest ... gap symmetry") assumes the conclusion it supports.
   - What the authors should do, as a minimum:
     - provide a d-wave model calculation (for example a nodal BCS model or a Doppler-shift model with the measured λ and ξ) of transmittance against J_T,peak, compared quantitatively with Fig. 4c,d;
     - estimate the expected YBCO depairing current from measured parameters and compare it with the range probed;
     - address film granularity (rocking curves, J_c(T) and R(T) width, ideally a cleaner YBCO film or a lower temperature such as 20 to 30 K, where nodal and inhomogeneity contributions should scale differently).
   - Alternatively, the authors could reduce the YBCO claim to an observation and remove the gap-symmetry conclusions from the abstract and summary.

B. Should address

2. Only one device of each material is reported, and the strip width is never varied (S3, S4).
   - The NbN conclusion rests on one 10 µm × 30 µm × 20 nm strip, apparently from one cooldown. Device-to-device spread of J_c* and agreement after thermal cycling are needed to show that 220 GA/m² is a material property.
   - A strip-width series would be especially informative. The DC J_c of a wide thin strip is set by vortex entry over the edge barrier and Pearl-length effects, so it should depend on w. The intrinsic J_c* should not, as long as w ≲ P_L.
   - Showing that J_c* is width-independent while J_c changes would be the most direct demonstration that the picosecond threshold is not vortex- or edge-related.

3. The quantitative agreement with "BCS theory" is less discriminating than presented (p. 10, Fig. 5a, S9, S10).
   - The closed-form estimate J_c* = ħ/(π e μ0 λ² ξ) (Eq. 9 with Eq. 10 for n_s) applies the clean-limit Landau criterion ħk_F v_s = Δ with ξ = ħ/(π m v_s). But it inserts the dirty-limit GL coherence length obtained from B_c2 via WHH, so the two definitions of ξ are mixed.
   - With the same λ = 350 nm and ξ = 5.24 nm, the standard GL depairing expression Φ0/(3√3 π μ0 λ² ξ) gives ≈ 160 GA/m² at T = 0. That is a factor 3√3/π ≈ 1.65 below the authors' ≈ 260 GA/m². The ~10% agreement in Fig. 5a therefore sits inside an O(1) prefactor ambiguity.
   - The temperature dependences used (Eq. 10, the Gorter–Casimir λ²(T) ∝ 1/(1 − t⁴); Eq. 11) are empirical rather than dirty-BCS forms.
   - The Usadel calculation (Eqs. 27 to 29) is the appropriate dirty-limit theory. Its J_c*(T) should be plotted directly in Fig. 5a alongside, or instead of, Eq. 9, with error bands propagated from the uncertainties in λ, ξ, film thickness and width.
   - Fitting status of the Dynes broadening: the main text says it is chosen "to reproduce the experimental results", while S10.3 says it is chosen for numerical convergence. The authors should state which, and show how the predicted threshold and transition width depend on η.
   - A consistency check also deserves comment. S10.3 states that L_s is fixed by R_n and Δ (Eq. 26). By our estimate, R_n = 140 Ω and Δ ≈ 1.76 k_B T_c give L_s ≈ ħR_n/(πΔ tanh(Δ/2k_BT)) ≈ 14 pH at 7 K, versus the 23 pH fitted in Fig. S16. If this mismatch is real, it bears on whether the weak-pulse response "proves the validity of the dirty limit approximation".

4. The time-domain evidence for depairing and the adiabatic assumption are not shown (Fig. 4, S10.1, S20 text).
   - Only peak values are presented. The direct signature of a current-limited superfluid would be the transmitted waveform J_T(t) just above threshold: clipping or flattening near J_c*, and the time within the pulse at which the reflection changes from inductive to resistive.
   - Fig. 3c shows only 0.2 J_c and 6.5 J_c. Waveforms at, say, 1.8, 2.2, 2.5 and 3 J_c should be added.
   - The Usadel model assumes the order parameter follows the current instantaneously. The justification given ("since the two values are consistent, we can justify the use of instantaneous equilibrium theory") is circular. Gap and quasiparticle relaxation in NbN after pair breaking take ~10 ps, comparable to or longer than the pulse, which matters most for the gradual post-threshold tail.
   - The contrast with tDGL (Fig. 5b) needs clarification. GL also has a maximum in j(Q), so whether the tDGL curve is broad because of the GL form of n_s(Q) or because of the chosen τ_GL relaxation time should be stated, along with the τ_GL value used.

5. The effective sample temperature during and after the pulses is not established.
   - Optical heating: the 515 nm pulses illuminate switches on the same chip. The distance to the strip, the laser fluence, the repetition rate, and whether pulse amplitude is varied by switch bias or laser power are not given. A control is needed, such as J_c* or the low-amplitude kinetic inductance measured against laser power at fixed bias, or DC J_c measured with the laser on.
   - Joule heating: once the strip is normal, a ~50 mA pulse into 140 Ω for ~2 ps deposits ~0.7 pJ in ~6×10⁻¹⁸ m³. Using approximate NbN heat capacities, our order-of-magnitude estimate is an electron-phonon temperature rise of tens of kelvin within one pulse, growing quickly at 5 to 7 J_c.
   - This does not affect the threshold itself. It does affect the interpretation of the gradual approach to the normal state above J_c* (Fig. 4a, S20) and the claim that self-heating is "bypassed". The authors should estimate it, state the repetition rate, and show that the sample recovers fully between pulses (for example that the result is independent of repetition rate).

6. The arguments excluding vortex effects could be made experimental (Discussion, p. 9).
   - Zero-field cooling and a velocity bound are reasonable, but a direct test is straightforward. Repeat the picosecond measurement after field cooling to a known vortex density, or in a small applied perpendicular field. Field cooling strongly reduces the DC J_c. If J_c* is essentially unchanged, apart from the expected Meissner-current correction, this would directly demonstrate that vortices are inertially irrelevant on ps timescales.
   - The uniform-current argument also needs care. P_L(7 K) ≈ 13 µm is comparable to, not much larger than, the 10 µm width (S8.3, S10.1), so some edge current enhancement and corner crowding where the strip meets the signal line are expected. The authors should quantify the edge-to-mean current ratio, since local depairing at edges would bias the extracted average J_c* low.

7. The work is not positioned against prior attempts to reach the depairing current.
   - The introduction states that direct transport measurements of J_c* "remain challenging" and cites [8–15]. It does not discuss earlier fast-pulsed transport measurements of pair-breaking currents, such as nanosecond-pulse work on Al, Nb and MgB2 strips. Nor does it discuss DC measurements in narrow NbN strips and nanowires (e.g. SNSPD-type devices), where currents approaching the theoretical depairing value are routinely reached.
   - THz-field-driven supercurrent and pair-breaking experiments in NbN (ref. 10) and YBCO (ref. 9) are cited but not compared quantitatively.
   - The authors should state what the picosecond method adds (wide strips, time resolution, a calibrated current). They should also compare their J_c*(0) with literature depairing values for NbN, for example Kupriyanov–Lukichev estimates and the Clem–Kogan analysis (ref. 11).

C. Minor

8. Error bars and an uncertainty budget are missing for J_c* (Fig. 4b inset, Fig. 5a). The uncertainty should include film thickness (≈ 20 nm) and width, the 0.85 and 1.414 factors in Eq. (1), and the switch calibration slope. It should also state how the local maximum was located (smoothing, criterion). Fig. S12 validates the calibration, and the residual uncertainty should be quoted.

9. The headline ratio "J_c* = 2.2 × J_c" depends on geometry and defects, because J_c is extrinsic (as the authors note). The abstract should also give the absolute value (~220 GA/m² at 7 K).
   - Relatedly, for an unpinned 10 µm thin strip the DC J_c is more likely limited by edge-barrier vortex entry than by "vortex depinning through the Lorentz force" (p. 3).
   - The statement that H_c is "experimentally not accessible" (Fig. 1 caption, p. 3) is incorrect. H_c is routinely obtained from the area under the magnetization curve or from the condensation energy.

10. In S8.2 the kinetic inductance fitted at 7 K (Fig. S16) is labelled L_kin(0) and λ(0) = 350 nm. S8.3 and S10.2 then use λ(7 K) ≈ 350 to 360 nm. The authors should state clearly whether a temperature correction was applied before inserting λ(0) into Eq. (10).

11. Wording: "at 0.8 × T_c, J_c is nearly an order of magnitude smaller than J_c*" (p. 10). Fig. 5a shows roughly 18 versus 120 GA/m² at 12 K, a factor of ~6.5.

12. YBCO normalization and ranges.
    - The value of J_c(50 K) used to normalize Fig. 4c should be stated explicitly.
    - The authors should check that the J_T,peak ranges in Fig. 4c (up to ~8 J_c) and Fig. 4d (J_T,peak up to ~290 GA/m²) are mutually consistent.
    - Fig. S13 uses 40 K for the linear-response comparison but 50 K for the high-current data. A 50 K low-amplitude trace would make the comparison cleaner.

13. Cross-references and typos.
    - S3 text cites "Fig. S4b" for the delay-time test; this should be Fig. S6b.
    - S4 cites "Fig. 10b"; this should be Fig. S10b.
    - Fig. S9a's y-axis is labelled R_Sample but shows the device resistance.
    - Spelling and term errors: "normal sate", "Lorenz force", "FHMW", "Meaurement" (Fig. S6a), and "m0" for μ0 in S8.2.
    - The incomplete reference 31 (Gor'kov) should be completed.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a strip-width series in NbN, ideally with a second film. This would show that J_c* is width-independent while the DC J_c is not, and would give the device-to-device spread.
2. Add field-cooled or in-field picosecond measurements to show directly that pinned or trapped vortices do not change J_c*.
3. Replace or supplement the closed-form Eq. 9 with the dirty-limit Usadel J_c*(T), with propagated uncertainties. For YBCO, add a d-wave calculation so that the s-wave versus d-wave contrast becomes quantitative.
4. Show full time-domain transmitted and reflected waveforms across the threshold, and measure the recovery with a double-pulse (two-pulse delay) experiment. This would test the adiabatic assumption and the role of quasiparticle relaxation and heating in the post-threshold regime.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
