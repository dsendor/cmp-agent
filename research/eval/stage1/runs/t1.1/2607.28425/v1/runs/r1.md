Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors study an eight-layer rhombohedral graphene device on WSe2. At millikelvin temperatures they find gate-defined pockets with zero resistance (SC) next to pockets whose resistance drops sharply below a similar Tc and then saturates at a finite value (anomalous metal, AM). The two pockets have about the same Tc. Their perpendicular critical fields differ by roughly two orders of magnitude, their in-plane critical fields differ by about a factor of four, and the AM shows an unusual current response in which dV/dI falls with increasing current. If the finite resistance is intrinsic, a clean, highly conducting, gate-tunable system with R_N << R_Q is a valuable new platform for the long-standing anomalous-metal problem. The phenomenology also challenges dissipative-bath explanations. The value of the paper therefore rests on how convincingly extrinsic and inhomogeneous origins are excluded.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The SC/AM contrast is clear and attractive. Two pockets in the same device, under the same wiring, filtering and base temperature, have similar Tc, but one reaches zero resistance and the other does not (Fig. 1b, Fig. 2c-i). This is a better internal control than most thin-film anomalous-metal studies have.
- The data set is extensive: n-D maps, T, B_perp, B_par and I_dc dependences, three voltage-probe pairs (Extended Data Fig. 1), a contact-gate control (Extended Data Fig. 1b), and a second device that reproduces the phase boundaries and the field-scale hierarchy (Extended Data Figs. 9-11).
- The non-monotonic current response (Fig. 3, Extended Data Figs. 4, 10) is new and reproduces in both devices. The constraints listed in the Discussion are a useful target for theory.
- The authors are careful about what they cannot exclude. The abstract and Discussion frame the AM as reproducing "key phenomenology" rather than claiming a proven Bose metal.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The microwave and stray-radiation explanation is argued indirectly and not tested.** Ref. [45] (Tamir et al.) showed that in thin films the anomalous-metal resistance disappears once high-frequency radiation is filtered. Here the only filtering described in Methods is an RC low-pass stage on the flex-PCB (10 kOhm, 1 nF, so a cutoff of about 16 kHz). Lumped RC filters of this kind usually attenuate poorly at GHz frequencies. No powder, copper-tape or thermocoax filtering is mentioned, and no shielding of the sample space is described. The authors rely on three arguments:
   - the saturation resistance does not depend on ac excitation down to 500 pA;
   - the AM pocket is sharply bounded in gate space;
   - the adjacent SC pocket has a similar Tc but reaches zero resistance.

   The third argument assumes that Tc measures how sensitive a state is to non-equilibrium excitation. In fact that sensitivity depends on the gap, the superfluid stiffness and the quasiparticle relaxation rates. The paper's own data say the two states differ in these respects: there is a factor of about 10 in ξ, and the AM pocket's normal-state resistance is about 10 times lower than the SC pocket's (Fig. 1b, about 40 Ohm against about 450 Ohm). A state with lower stiffness or a smaller gap could be driven dissipative by radiation that the SC pocket tolerates. The headline claim is that the finite resistance is intrinsic and that "the tunability and reproducibility ... place strong constraints on extrinsic explanations". That claim needs a direct test. The authors should do at least one of the following:
   - re-measure the AM saturation resistance at a fixed gate point after adding high-frequency filtering (powder or thermocoax filters close to the sample) and improved radiation shielding;
   - or deliberately apply calibrated microwave radiation and compare how the AM saturation resistance and the SC zero-resistance state respond.

   Either result would be informative. If R_sat is unchanged under better filtering, the extrinsic origin is strongly constrained. If it falls, the paper's conclusions change.

2. **Intrinsic isospin phase coexistence at the half-metal/unpolarized-metal boundary is not excluded, and the reproducibility argument does not exclude it.**
   - The text places the AM pocket next to the unpolarized metal and the SC pocket next to the half-metal (Fig. 2a, Extended Data Fig. 8).
   - It states that the two-step (SC-to-AM) behaviour sets in where the normal state shows the resistance peak "signifying the transition between half-metal and unpolarized-metal phases" (p. 4, discussion of Fig. 3a).
   - In Fig. 2a the AM strip appears to follow that isospin boundary along its whole length.

   Isospin transitions in rhombohedral graphene are often first order, so a mesoscale mixture of isospin domains is a natural, intrinsic, gate-controlled source of the inhomogeneous SC/normal texture that the authors consider and set aside. The case against inhomogeneity has two parts (Discussion; Methods, "granular disorder"):
   - there is "no known structural length scale" of about 30 nm;
   - the same features reproduce across probes and across two devices.

   Neither argument applies to domains set by the electronic phase diagram. Such domains would appear at the same (n, D) in every device and on every probe pair, with a size set by domain-wall energetics rather than by structure. They could also produce the larger B_perp scale and the non-monotonic I-V, through current-driven domain reconfiguration. The B_par-induced instability and hysteresis at zero field (Extended Data Fig. 3) also point to orbital or valley domains. The authors should:
   - map the normal-state isospin transition precisely across the AM pocket, using the quantum-oscillation frequency against n along several Vt cuts, and show whether the AM pocket lies on the transition line or inside a single-phase region well away from it;
   - look for hysteresis in Vb, Vt and B_perp sweeps, and for telegraph noise, within the AM pocket at base temperature;
   - show the AM R(T) and dV/dI at gate points clearly inside the unpolarized-metal phase, away from the boundary, if such points exist.

   Without this, the statement that the AM "is unlikely to be simply" an inhomogeneous or phase-disordered version of the SC is not supported.

B. Should address

3. **The comparison of field scales does not use matched definitions or matched Tc, and it underpins the claim that the SC and AM are distinct paired states.**
   - Perpendicular field. The SC scale of about 4 mT (ξ ≈ 300 nm) is read from the inner diamond in Fig. 4a,b, which marks the loss of zero resistance. The AM scale of about 300 mT is read from the outer diamond, where pairing-related suppression disappears. At a single gate point (Fig. 4a), the inner-to-outer structure is just what a single 2D superconductor shows when it is field-tuned into an anomalous metal: zero resistance is lost at small B through vortex motion, and the suppressed-resistance state survives up to Hc2. The SC pocket in Fig. 1d also shows resistance suppression extending to roughly 50-75 mT, well above 4 mT. Methods defines critical fields by a 90%-of-normal criterion, and that criterion does not appear to give 4 mT for the SC.
   - In-plane field. Methods quotes B_par,c ≈ 1.39 T at Tc ≈ 55 mK for the SC and 3.93 T at Tc ≈ 130 mK for the AM. These give Pauli-limit violation ratios of 14 and 16, which are essentially the same once normalised by Tc. The "factor of four" in in-plane field (Fig. 4d, Discussion) therefore largely reflects the different Tc of the two pockets compared.

   The authors should extract B_c,perp and B_c,par with the same criterion (the 90%-of-R_N one) for both pockets and normalise by the local Tc. They should then show these quantities along a gate cut through the SC/AM boundary at fixed B_par, and revise ξ_SC and the "distinct paired states" claim accordingly.

4. **The saturation needs to be shown quantitatively, and the electron temperature in this sample is not established.**
   - "Saturation" is shown on linear axes over roughly 11-100 mK (Fig. 1b, Fig. 2e,g,i).
   - The electron temperature (below 20 mK) was established on other samples (quantum dots, nanowires) with the same wiring, not on this device under these conditions.

   The authors should do two things. First, plot R(T) of the AM on logarithmic axes, with the noise floor marked, at several gate points and at B_par = 0 and 350 mT, and give an upper bound on dR/dT at low T. Second, use a thermometer inside the sample, for example the T-dependence of the SC critical current or of the BKT exponent at base temperature, to show that the electron temperature tracks the bath below about 30 mK. This also bears on the comparison between the SC and AM pockets.

5. **The non-monotonic current response needs controls before it is presented as an intrinsic property of the AM (Fig. 3f-g, Extended Data Figs. 4, 10).** The fall in dV/dI to about zero just below the outer critical current could arise from several mechanisms:
   - a current-induced change of the isospin or valley state, which is known to switch with current in rhombohedral graphene;
   - redistribution of current between parallel SC and normal paths;
   - self-heating that moves the sample across the SC/AM boundary in gate-temperature space.

   The authors should show up and down sweeps of I_dc (to test for hysteresis), the dependence on ac excitation at finite I_dc, and whether the effect appears in ρ_xy or on all probe pairs at once. They should also comment on the near-zero "dV/dI" plateau in Fig. 3g, whose integrated V is flat at finite current. Is this a re-entrant zero-resistance state, and does it reach the noise floor?

6. **The n and D values for the two pockets need to be reconciled with the description of them as adjacent, merging conduction-band states.**
   - The caption of Fig. 1 places the SC at n = +1.34 × 10^12 cm^-2 and the AM at n = -1.25 × 10^12 cm^-2, and in Fig. 1a they sit on opposite sides of n = 0.
   - The text says the superconducting pockets "arise from conduction-band carriers". It also says the two pockets merge into a contiguous region under B_par (Fig. 2a,b, Extended Data Fig. 7), and that the AM borders the unpolarized metal at the boundary with the half-metal.

   The authors should state the carrier type of the AM pocket and give the conversion parameters (Ct, Cb, offsets, and the role of the +15 V Si gate). They should also show the white and black dashed cuts of Fig. 1a on the Vb-Vt maps of Fig. 2. If the AM is hole-like, much of the discussion of adjacent states with nearly identical external parameters needs rewording.

7. **Only two devices were measured, the second is inhomogeneous, and thermal cycling is not reported.**
   - In the second device, ρ_xy does not vanish in the SC state, and probe pair Vxx2 shows a resistance in the AM pocket that exceeds the normal state (Extended Data Fig. 11).
   - So whether the saturation resistance, and not only the phase boundaries, is reproducible rests on one device.

   The authors should report R_sat/R_N for the cleaner regions of the second device. They should also state whether R_sat and the pocket boundaries in the primary device survived a thermal cycle.

8. **The behaviour at B_par = 0 is less characterised than at 350 mT.**
   - The abstract emphasises zero magnetic field, but most of the AM characterisation (Figs. 2-4) is at B_par = 350 mT.
   - At that field the pockets are enlarged and merged, and Zeeman coupling to a spin-polarized half-metal background is substantial.
   - At B_par = 0 there is an instability and hysteresis whose origin is unknown (Fig. 1e, Extended Data Fig. 3).

   The authors should show more explicitly that R_sat/R_N and the field-scale hierarchy at B_par = 0 (or 30 mT) match those at 350 mT. That would show that the in-plane field is not creating the AM regime.

9. **The anomalous Hall signal in the AM pocket.** ρ_xy is finite in the AM but independent of field, which the authors attribute to geometric mixing (Extended Data Fig. 2). Geometric mixing from ρ_xx would scale with ρ_xx across the pocket boundary. The authors should test this scaling quantitatively, or antisymmetrise in B_perp within the AM. A spontaneous Hall component would bear on whether the AM coexists with valley polarization or orbital magnetization.

C. Minor

10. **The mean-free-path estimate.** The estimate ℓ_mf ≈ 5.9 µm comes from the onset field of quantum oscillations. That onset is set by the quantum (not transport) lifetime, and 5.9 µm is comparable to or larger than the channel dimensions (Extended Data Fig. 1a, 5 µm scale bar). The clean-limit conclusion ξ/ℓ << 1 is probably right, but the authors should state these caveats.

11. **The "weak coupling" statement.** kFξ ≈ 9 for the AM is not obviously weak coupling, and the corresponding sentence in Methods should be softened.

12. **Methods defines Tc and Bc by a 90%-of-normal criterion, but the text and figures sometimes quote zero-resistance or diamond-edge values.** The authors should state which criterion each quoted number uses. The main text gives "similar maximum critical temperatures (≈ 100 mK)", while Methods gives 55 mK for the SC and 130 mK for the AM. The authors should reconcile the two.

13. **Fig. 2d.** The overshoot of the AM resistance just below Tc should be shown as line traces, and its reproducibility across probe pairs noted.

14. **BKT analysis (Extended Data Fig. 6).** The authors themselves question whether BKT applies at micron scale. They should give the sample size relative to ξ and λ_Pearl, or move the BKT temperature out of the main narrative.

15. **Captions.** The caption of Fig. 1h gives Vb, but the corresponding Vt should be stated explicitly. For the Fig. 2c line traces, the authors should state the averaging window in volts.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Map R_sat/R_N and R_sat(B_perp) across the AM pocket. In thin-film anomalous metals R_sat usually grows as a power law in B_perp, so this would allow a direct comparison with the thin-film phenomenology the paper invokes.
- Add a local probe, or a smaller Hall-bar segment (for example by AFM anodic oxidation, which the authors cite), to test whether the AM finite resistance persists in segments smaller than the hypothesised 30 nm-to-micron texture.
- Measure the noise (telegraph or 1/f) of the AM state at base temperature. This would help distinguish domain switching from a homogeneous dissipative state.
- State more directly that the SC/AM contrast under identical wiring is the paper's strongest argument, and test it as proposed in concern 1.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
