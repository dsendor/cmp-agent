Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper proposes a MOS-like tensile-strained Ge quantum well on relaxed Ge1-xSn_x (x around 16%) with a thin, high surface barrier. It argues from 6-band k·p theory and Schrieffer-Wolff effective models that the light-hole (LH) ground state has a large linear-in-k Rashba term β1. Because the confinement is asymmetric, this term passes through zero at a finite gate field F* ≈ 4.4 V/µm, which would act as an on/off switch for EDSR. The out-of-plane g-factor can be tuned through zero, and the in-plane g-factor is large (about -8). The idea of a gate-defined, planar, "inherited" linear Rashba sweet line is attractive for hole-spin qubits. The incremental step beyond the authors' earlier LH work (Refs. 56, 57) is the zero crossing and the extension to channels and dots. The significance rests on whether the predicted zero crossings are robust to how the structure and the band model are idealized.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear, physically motivated proposal for strong, linear-in-k Rashba SOI in a planar geometry. Today's planar Ge/SiGe HH qubits lack this.
- The QC and QD calculations show that the β1 = 0 line (the "β-line") is independent of in-plane confinement (l_x, l_y) and of field angle θ. Fig. 3c and Fig. 3b separate it cleanly from the g-lines, which do move with l_x and vanish with an in-plane field. This is a useful, testable distinction.
- Eq. (3) gives a compact analytic link between the planar parameters and the channel g-factor, including a sign change of g⊥^c. The approach is consistent with established g-tensor renormalization in quasi-1D systems.
- The paper is concise and well connected to current experimental activity in GeSn growth (Ref. 55).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The band model may not be valid at the strain level used, and the key numbers depend on it. With x = 16%, the epitaxial tensile strain in Ge is of order 2%. At that strain Ge is close to, or past, the indirect-to-direct crossover, and the direct gap shrinks to a few hundred meV. For light holes, coupling to the Γ6 conduction band through the Kane parameter P is the leading correction to the LH out-of-plane and in-plane masses, to κ (and hence g⊥, g∥), and to the linear Rashba coefficient. The main text says only that a 6-band model is used (Fig. 1c, Ref. 60). The headline quantities are F* (where β1 = 0), the g⊥ = 0 lines and g∥ ≈ -8 (Fig. 2e). All of them come from near-cancellations between competing contributions, so modest band-parameter changes could move or remove them. The authors should:
   - recompute β1^p, g⊥^p and g∥^c (Fig. 2a, 2b, 2e) with an 8-band model that includes strain-dependent gap narrowing, or justify quantitatively why the remote-band corrections are negligible here;
   - state the Luttinger parameters, κ, deformation potentials and Ge/GeSn band offsets used for strained Ge and for the alloy, with their uncertainties. Bowing for GeSn is poorly constrained at x = 16%.

2. The β1 = 0 line is not shown to survive a realistic surface barrier. The vanishing of β1 at finite F_z "due to the asymmetric confinement profile even at F_z = 0" depends on how the wavefunction meets the surface barrier. The text treats the barrier as "a very thin, large band offset material" and suggests a few-ångström Si cap, an oxide, or both, but gives no barrier height, thickness, effective-mass or boundary-condition parameters. At the relevant fields (Fig. 1b) the hole is pushed toward this surface, so β1, which comes from the asymmetry of the envelope, is sensitive to it. The authors should show how F* and the g⊥ = 0 lines move as they vary:
   - barrier height (for example 0.3 to 1 eV or more);
   - barrier thickness (hard wall versus finite);
   - the mass mismatch or interface boundary condition.

   They should also say whether the zero crossing persists for a concrete Si/SiO2 cap. Without this, the claim of a robust, fully gate-tunable RSOI on/off switch is not established for any buildable device.

B. Should address

3. The "completely vanish" claim ignores other spin-orbit mechanisms. The abstract and conclusion say the RSOI "completely vanish[es]" at specific fields, which places the qubit in an idle mode. Fig. 3 shows Ω = 0 at the β-line, but within a model that keeps only the Luttinger-Kohn Rashba terms of an ideal [001] heterostructure. In a real device the drive would not vanish exactly, because of:
   - interface inversion asymmetry and C2v terms at the Ge/GeSn and Ge/cap interfaces (Ref. 50);
   - inhomogeneous strain from gates and thermal mismatch (Refs. 51-53);
   - g-tensor modulation by the AC field along y;
   - the cubic terms β2, β3, whose zero crossing is said only to "coincide" with β1 = 0, with the evidence in the SM.

   The authors should estimate the residual Rabi frequency at the β-line from at least the interface and inhomogeneous-strain terms, show β2 and β3 explicitly in the main text, and soften "completely" or "fully" accordingly.

4. The Rabi frequencies are in normalized units with no realistic qubit metrics. Fig. 3 gives Ω only in units of ω_B F_ac/F_z, so the reader cannot judge whether the "on" state is fast compared with existing Ge HH qubits. The paper should:
   - give absolute Rabi frequencies, for example in MHz for a realistic F_ac at B0 = 0.05 T;
   - give the spin-orbit length l_so = α0/β1 at representative F_z;
   - compare directly with planar Ge/SiGe HH devices at equal dot size.

   It should also discuss charge-noise dephasing. Near the β-line and the g-lines, dg/dF_z and dΩ/dF_z set qubit coherence, and whether the β-line is also a dephasing sweet spot (dg/dF_z = 0) is central to the "idle mode" argument.

5. Growth feasibility (critical thickness, Sn content and interface) is not addressed. A 15 nm Ge layer under about 2% tensile strain is likely beyond the Matthews-Blakeslee or People-Bean critical thickness. Relaxation would reduce the LH-HH splitting and could lose the LH ground state (region boundaries in Fig. 1c). Sn segregation into the well and alloy disorder in the barrier would also shift F*, given the strong x dependence noted for the region boundaries. The authors should:
   - cite or estimate the achievable strain and thickness (Ref. 55 reports experimental structures; state their x and l_z);
   - show how F* and the LH-ground-state window shift with x, for example x = 10-18%. Fig. 1b uses x = 10% while all other results use x = 16%, and the reason is not explained.

6. The field range and the MOS gate stack are not discussed. F_z up to 15 V/µm in Ge requires substantially larger fields in the cap or oxide. The paper should:
   - state the corresponding gate voltage and oxide field for a plausible stack, checking against breakdown;
   - discuss whether F* ≈ 4.4 V/µm and the g-line fields (about 2 and 7.5 V/µm) can be reached while keeping the dot occupation at one hole;
   - discuss the effect of interface traps at a surface where the hole wavefunction resides.

7. The novelty relative to Refs. 56 and 57 is not stated. The large linear Rashba term for LH states in Ge/GeSn has already been reported by the same authors. The paper should state explicitly what is new here (the zero crossing of β1, the channel and dot g-tensor, Eq. (3)) and what was already known, so that the claim of introducing the system is properly scoped.

8. The g-tensor anisotropy claim needs qualification. The abstract says the LH g-tensor is "less anisotropic than that of state-of-the-art HH qubits," which alleviates field-alignment requirements. However, the paper's own result is that g⊥^c passes through zero (the g-lines). Near those lines the anisotropy is arbitrarily large, and the Zeeman splitting depends sensitively on θ (Fig. 3b). The authors should give g∥/g⊥ over the operating range and identify a regime where both the RSOI is on and alignment sensitivity is low.

9. The validity of the perturbative treatment needs a stated criterion. g⊥^p diverges for l_z ≲ 10 nm because ΔE → 0 (Fig. 2a), and the region boundaries in Fig. 1c show where H1 approaches L1. The paper should state the condition used to trust Eq. (1) and Eq. (2), for example a minimum ΔE relative to the relevant matrix elements. It should also mark the excluded region in Fig. 2. It should state how Ω in Fig. 3 was computed: from the effective Hamiltonian (2) plus y-confinement, or from a full numerical diagonalization. It should confirm agreement between the two near the β-line.

10. The superconducting-hybrid claims are not supported by any calculation. The abstract and title frame the system as suited to hybrid superconducting-semiconducting devices, but no calculation of induced pairing, proximity or topological regimes is given. Either add a short quantitative estimate (for example the Zeeman field needed relative to a realistic induced gap, using g∥ ≈ -8, alongside l_so) or reduce the claim to a remark.

C. Minor

11. Fig. 3 caption defines φ = π/2, but φ is not introduced anywhere in the text. Define it (presumably the azimuth of B) or remove it.

12. The Fig. 1 caption mixes parameters: panel (b) uses x = 10% and F_z = 6 V/µm, panel (c) uses x = 16%. State why panel (b) uses a different composition. Also make clear that the 2 meV H1-H2 contour (dashed green line) is the region 2/3 boundary discussed in the text.

13. Fig. 2a and 2b: the text says g⊥^p ≈ -3 for all F_z at l_z = 15 nm. Panel (a) shows contours between about -2 and -3 at that thickness. Quote the actual range.

14. Eq. (1): define 1/l_B² consistently. The text writes "1/l_B² = [K−, K+]/2 = eB/ħ", which mixes an operator commutator with a number. Also state the sign convention for g∥^c and g⊥^c relative to the bare electron g.

15. Typos: "valance band" should be "valence band", and "wether" should be "whether" (page 3). In the abstract, the term "MOS-like" should be defined on first use.

16. Panel labels: Fig. 2c, 2d and 3a-3c lack units or scale information for Ω in a form readers can reuse. Consider adding the F_z positions of the β-line and g-lines as vertical guides in Fig. 3b and 3c.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add an 8-band and surface-barrier sensitivity analysis, for example a supplementary figure of F*(barrier height, x, l_z), to show that the β1 = 0 and g⊥ = 0 lines are robust design features.
- Convert Fig. 3 to absolute units and add a comparison panel with a planar Ge/SiGe HH dot of equal size.
- Estimate the residual drive at the β-line from interface inversion asymmetry and inhomogeneous strain, and the dephasing sensitivity dg/dF_z along the β-line.
- Give a concrete growth and gate-stack recipe (x, l_z below the critical thickness, cap material and thickness, oxide) and show the predicted F*, g-factors and Ω for that structure.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
