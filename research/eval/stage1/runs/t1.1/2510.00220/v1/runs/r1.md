Review outline:

Note on scope: the PDF supplied contains the 12-page main text, Methods and references. The Extended Data figures (1-11) and the Supplementary Information it cites are not included, so I judged claims that rest on them from the main-text description alone. Where a concern may already be answered there, I say so.

1. Significance and novelty

The authors build a marginally twisted double-bilayer graphene device with mixed Bernal (ABAB) and rhombohedral (ABCA) tetralayer domains. They report that the dual gates switch it, hysteretically, between a "Bernal-dominant" state and a "rhombohedral-Bernal mixed" state. In the mixed state a hysteretic anomalous Hall effect (AHE) appears at large |D| and small electron density, and it is absent at the same gate voltages in the Bernal-dominant state. Using a gate to turn a correlated magnetic phase on and off through a stacking change is an attractive idea that has not been shown in transport before. It joins two very active areas: sliding ferroelectrics and magnetism in rhombohedral graphene. The observation that the AHE reverses sign with D is unexpected and could be interesting in its own right. The paper's interpretation of it, as a domain-boundary effect, is explicitly tentative.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Clear, reproducible bistability in R_xx versus n (Fig. 1c) and repeated switching with gate pulses (Fig. 1d). The forward and backward traces coincide outside the hysteretic window. This argues against a simple shift of the charge-neutrality point from charge trapping.
- A full (n, D) map of the switching boundary from a systematic, history-controlled protocol (Fig. 2). The boundary is mirror-symmetric about D = 0 (Fig. 3a).
- At identical gate voltages, a hysteretic AHE (|R_yx| of about 150 Ω, coercive field about 0.08 T) appears in one state and not the other (Fig. 4c,d and 4g,h). This is the paper's strongest piece of evidence. The AHE window in n and D matches where pure rhombohedral tetralayer graphene shows a quarter-metal phase, which independently supports the presence of rhombohedral regions.
- A tight-binding free-energy calculation (Fig. 3b) reproduces the main features of the phase boundary: hole doping favours rhombohedral stacking, and large |D| near charge neutrality also favours it.
- The D-odd AHE sign is reported to reproduce in a second device (Extended Data Figs. 10-11).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The central claim is that gate history selects, at fixed gate voltage, between a state with hysteretic AHE and one without it. The data in Figs. 1, 2 and 4 support that claim at the level such journals usually accept for transport papers. The items below would strengthen or narrow the interpretation.

B. Should address

1. What "nonvolatile" means here, and retention (title, abstract, Fig. 1d, Fig. 3a, Discussion). By Fig. 3a, the point n = D = 0 (V_t = V_b = 0) lies in the white central region to the right of the black dashed boundary, where only the B-dominant state exists. Methods and the Fig. 4 caption confirm this: the B-dominant state is prepared by "going to V_t = V_b = 0". So the RB-mixed state, and the magnetism with it, seems to be lost whenever the gates return to zero. It survives only while a finite gate bias is held inside the bistable window. In memory terminology, "nonvolatile" usually means the state is kept without applied bias. What the paper shows is bistability, or history dependence, at fixed gate voltage. In addition, Fig. 1d holds each state for only about 30 s, and no retention-time data are given. The authors should:
   - state explicitly whether the RB-mixed state survives with the gates at zero or floating;
   - show R_xx (and R_yx in the AHE window) over hours at a fixed gate voltage in both states;
   - say whether the domain configuration and the phase boundary survive thermal cycling, to above the Curie temperature and to room temperature;
   - qualify "nonvolatile" and the memory-device claims in the Discussion to match.

2. No direct structural evidence that the device's stacking changes (Fig. 1b, Extended Data Figs. 2-3, Methods). The KPFM image in Fig. 1b, and the 22 mV work-function difference, come from a different heterostructure on SiO2/Si, and Extended Data Fig. 2a from a sample still on the stamp. Neither is device A. In device A, the identification of the RB-mixed state rests on indirect evidence: the absence of quantum oscillations (Extended Data Fig. 3), the appearance of AHE, and agreement with the calculation. Without quantum oscillations, disorder or inhomogeneity could equally be responsible. The authors should do one of the following:
   - show any direct stacking probe on device A, or on an equivalent device, before and after switching (Raman 2D lineshape, nano-IR or KPFM through a thin top gate, or KPFM before top-gate deposition);
   - if that is impossible, extract more from transport. Low-field Hall density in both states at the same gate voltages would confirm that n is unchanged. Landau-fan features specific to ABCA, such as the layer-antiferromagnetic gap at n = D = 0 or quarter-metal signatures in R_xx, would positively identify rhombohedral regions.

   They should also report the domain fraction and layout of device A (or Extended Data Fig. 1, if it shows this), because the paper's explanation of the contact-dependent sign and magnitude (Extended Data Fig. 7) depends on it.

3. The D-odd AHE argument assumes the +D and −D RB-mixed states are inversion partners (Fig. 4e-h, pp. 6-7). The symmetry argument holds only if the structure measured at +D is related by inversion to the structure measured at −D. But the paper stresses that the domain configuration depends on history (pp. 2-3, Extended Data Fig. 4). Per the Fig. 4 caption, the +D and −D RB-mixed states are reached by different gate paths, along opposite edges of the gate window. The two states may therefore have different domain-wall configurations, and the sign difference could reflect that rather than an intrinsic D-odd response. The authors should:
   - prepare the RB-mixed state once, move between +D and −D without crossing the switching boundary (if Fig. 3a allows such a path), and check that the sign reversal persists;
   - repeat the ±D comparison over several independent resets and both contact pairs;
   - state whether the device-B data were taken with the same preparation protocol.

   Separately, the claim that the AHE "originates from the domain boundary" is a hypothesis. The abstract's "suggests" is appropriate, but the Discussion's statement that domain boundaries are "unique and pristine platforms" goes further than the data.

4. Weak support for the D-even "negative AHE" component (Fig. 4i,j, Extended Data Fig. 8). At the pentagon position, Fig. 4i shows a smooth, non-hysteretic, S-shaped R_yx(B) that is steeper than the B-dominant trace (Fig. 4j). In a mixed-domain sample, ordinary Hall conduction through two populations with different densities and mobilities (R and B domains, or two pockets) also gives a nonlinear R_yx(B) that depends on temperature. The authors should:
   - extend the B range well beyond 0.15 T and fit a two-carrier model;
   - show whether the nonlinearity has a characteristic temperature that matches the hysteretic AHE (about 8 K) or evolves smoothly;
   - check whether R_xx(B) shows the corresponding magnetoresistance.

   Until then, the "two-component" summary (p. 7) should be presented more cautiously.

5. The theory comparison is qualitative, and its inputs are not independent (Fig. 3b, Methods "Calculation"). The calculated ΔU has two free parameters. ΔΦ comes from KPFM on a different, partly unencapsulated sample, where substrate and encapsulation can change the work-function difference. ΔU(0,0) is determined by a procedure described only in the SI. The experimental lines are contours of constant ΔF at different, unknown pinning barriers, so overlaying them on the ΔU colour map tests shape, not magnitude. Also, the regions that matter most for the paper, large |D| near charge neutrality, are where rhombohedral tetralayer graphene orders by spontaneous symmetry breaking. A non-interacting tight-binding energy, apparently without self-consistent screening of D, may not be reliable there. The authors should:
   - state the ΔU value of each overlaid contour and how ΔU(0,0) was fixed;
   - show how sensitive the boundary shape is to ΔΦ and to Hartree screening;
   - tone down "agree well" or make it quantitative, for example by plotting ΔU along each experimental contour to show it is roughly constant.

6. Most data come from one device, and spread across contacts and devices is not shown in the main text (Methods; Extended Data Figs. 4, 7, 10, 11). All data except Extended Data Figs. 10-11 are from device A. Device B differs in construction (0.03° rather than about 180° rotation). The switching occurs only in part of device A, and both the magnitude and the sign of the R_xx jumps depend on the contact pair. The authors should state in the main text:
   - whether device B also shows the gate-driven switching and the AHE on/off contrast, not just the D-odd sign;
   - which contact pairs show AHE in device A, and whether its sign is the same on each.

7. Switching threshold versus sweep limit (Fig. 1c, Fig. 2a, Fig. 3a). The transition into the RB-mixed state occurs at n = −15.5 × 10^12 cm^-2, which seems to be the most negative density reached. Fig. 3a likewise excludes "the n < −15.5 × 10^12 cm^-2 region". It is therefore unclear whether this is a true threshold or whether switching needs dwelling at the gate limit, close to dielectric-breakdown fields. The authors should:
   - give the maximum gate voltages and fields used;
   - show whether the switching density depends on sweep rate or dwell time;
   - show whether switching ever occurs before the end of the sweep.

   This also bears on how practical the memory-device claims are.

C. Minor

8. "Paramagnet" (abstract, p. 6). The B-dominant state shows only an ordinary Hall effect. No magnetic susceptibility measurement shows that it is paramagnetic. "Non-magnetic" or "no detectable AHE" would be more accurate.

9. Priority claim (p. 7, "first realization of a magnet-to-non-magnet nonvolatile phase transition controllable entirely by electrostatic means"). Gate-history-dependent, hysteretic switching of orbital magnetism has been reported in moiré and rhombohedral graphene systems, although there the magnetization is reversed rather than switched off. The claim should be narrowed to "via a structural (stacking) transition" and set against that prior work.

10. Magnetic susceptibility extraction (p. 3, Extended Data Fig. 5). Extracting Δχ from the B-dependence of the switching boundary assumes that the pinning barrier, and hence the ΔF at which switching occurs, does not depend on B. The authors should state this assumption and report the size of the extracted Δχ in the main text.

11. Maps at a single field (Fig. 4a,b,e,f). The R_yx maps are taken at B = 0.03 T, and the paper does not say whether they are antisymmetrized or how the magnetization was trained beforehand. Please state this. Ideally, add B = 0 remanent maps after training, which would show the magnetic region without the ordinary Hall background.

12. Calibration of n and D (Methods). n0, D0 and the capacitances are calibrated from Landau levels in the B-dominant state. Please confirm that the same conversion applies in the RB-mixed state. Rhombohedral regions have a different work function and quantum capacitance, and the region boundaries in Fig. 4a versus 4e are compared with the pure-ABCA phase diagram of Ref. 23.

13. Fig. 2d,h colour scales. ΔR_xx/R̄_xx can range from −2 to 2, but the colour bar is clipped to ±1, while Fig. 3a uses ±2. Please make these consistent or explain the choice. The Fig. 2 caption says the black dashed line is the RB-mixed-to-B-dominant boundary, while Fig. 3a also overlays blue lines from another contact configuration. Please mark these clearly in Fig. 3b as well.

14. Device geometry. Please give the hBN and graphite gate thicknesses for both devices and an optical image of the Hall bar. The paper rules out top/bottom asymmetry partly by appeal to the gate structure, and readers need these details to judge that argument.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Image the domain configuration of the transport device, for example by KPFM before top-gate deposition or by nano-IR, and relate it to the contact pairs that show switching and AHE. This would turn the domain-boundary hypothesis into a testable statement.
- Use a preparation sequence in which the +D and −D RB-mixed states share the same domain configuration (concern 3). That is the cleanest test of whether the AHE is truly D-odd.
- Add retention data at fixed gate voltage, and a statement on stability at zero gate (concern 1). If the state is lost at zero gate, reframe the device language as "gate-history-controlled bistability".
- Make the theory comparison quantitative: state the contour ΔU values, test sensitivity to ΔΦ and screening, and use a ΔΦ measured under conditions matching the device.

5. Verdict

Verdict: minor revision
