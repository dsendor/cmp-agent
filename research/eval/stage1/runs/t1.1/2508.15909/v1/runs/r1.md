Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript maps the transport phase diagram of rhombohedral hexalayer graphene in the semimetallic regime at small displacement field, where conduction and valence bands overlap. Most earlier work on rhombohedral multilayers studied the gapped, large-field regime, so this regime is less explored. Using normalized quantum-oscillation frequencies (f_ν), the authors argue for a two-by-two flavor splitting at charge neutrality and an electric-field-driven band inversion in one flavor. They also report a "superconducting-like" state (SC1, about 170 mK) that sits where two emergent Fermi surfaces overlap and appears to exceed the Pauli limit, plus a second, finite-field multiferroic orbital magnet whose magnetic hysteresis reverses sign with E. The fermiology data set is extensive and new for this regime. The two headline claims in the title, "superconductivity" and "ferroelectric" orbital magnetism, rest on indirect evidence that in its present form does not exclude other explanations.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- High-quality, dense quantum-oscillation fermiology across a large n–E range (Figs. 2, 3, S3–S8), with sum-rule bookkeeping (Fig. S5). This is a useful resource for the semimetallic regime of thick rhombohedral graphene.
- A clear empirical link between resistive phase boundaries and Lifshitz transitions. The opposite tilts of the purple and orange lines in the n–E plane (Fig. 2a) give a simple, physically transparent way to assign layer polarization to band features.
- Interesting phenomenology near n = 0, E ≈ −35 mV/nm: a unipolar E-hysteresis loop (Fig. 1i, Fig. 4a,b), and a reversal of the B-hysteresis polarity with E (Fig. 4e). It is distinct from the butterfly loop of the E = 0 triangle (Fig. S2) and from the pentalayer result (ref. 10).
- A superconducting-like state in a semimetallic region with apparently large in-plane field resilience would be timely, given the many recent reports of superconductivity in rhombohedral graphene (refs. 15–18, 24).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The superconductivity claim in the title is not established by the data shown. The main text calls SC1 "superconducting-like" and concedes that "the resistance does not reach zero". The title and abstract still claim superconductivity. In Fig. 1f (inset) R_xx falls only from about 200 Ω to about 100 Ω at the 170 mK "T_c". For SC2 (Fig. S12b) it falls from about 175 Ω to about 140 Ω, a 20% drop. The remaining evidence is a nonlinear dV/dI that is suppressed by a few mT of B⊥ (Fig. 3e) and by temperature (Fig. 3f). Other mechanisms can produce that signature in a strongly temperature-dependent correlated metal next to first-order phase boundaries: self-heating, current-driven switching between competing phases, or percolating domains of a different flavor order. These are especially plausible here because the "leaf" region borders the multiferroic triangle. Possible stacking-domain or flavor-domain inhomogeneity could also give partial shunting by a filamentary superconductor. To support the claim, the authors should:
   - show R_xx(T) and dV/dI on several contact pairs, including one spanning the full Hall bar, to show whether the residual resistance is a series or inhomogeneity contribution;
   - state the excitation current and the electron temperature, and show what improved filtering does, since the paper blames insufficient filtering;
   - show whether the dV/dI peak current scales with the resistance drop, as a critical current would, and track the n–E extent of the nonlinearity against the leaf boundary;
   - extract B_c2⊥(T) and a Ginzburg–Landau coherence length and compare it with the mean free path;
   - test for any phase-coherent signature (for example, Fraunhofer-like or Little–Parks-like modulation if the geometry allows, or a BKT-type V ∝ I^α analysis).
   If zero resistance cannot be shown, the title and abstract should be changed to "superconducting-like" or "signatures of superconductivity", consistent with the main text.

2. The in-plane critical field claim (about 6 T, "more than an order of magnitude" above the about 300 mT Pauli limit) conflicts with the out-of-plane sensitivity unless the field alignment is controlled far better than the paper states. Fig. 3e shows the dV/dI features suppressed by B⊥ of only a few mT, and Fig. S12d gives a critical B⊥ of about 1 mT for SC2. A misalignment of 0.05° at 6 T gives about 5 mT of perpendicular field, which on this evidence should already kill the state. Either the sample was aligned to better than roughly 0.01–0.05°, with the alignment procedure and residual B⊥ compensation described, or the "low-resistance state" persisting to 6 T in Fig. 3g and Fig. S9 is not the same state that is suppressed by mT-scale B⊥. A non-superconducting resistance feature tied to the Fermi-surface reconstruction is a candidate. There is also an ambiguity in labelling. Fig. 3g is said to cut through "two superconducting phases" along the dashed line of Fig. S12a, and it is not stated which strip is SC1 and which is SC2. Fig. S9 says SC1 "shrinks" at 5 T and closes at 10 T, while the text says it persists to about 6 T. The authors should:
   - describe the in-plane alignment and the B⊥ compensation;
   - show dV/dI versus I at several B∥ (not only R_xx colour maps on a 0.1–100 kΩ log scale, on which a 100 Ω change is invisible), demonstrating that the same nonlinear signature survives;
   - define the critical field quantitatively (for example, 50% of the normal-state resistance).
   Without this, the Pauli-limit violation, which is a central selling point of SC1, is unsupported.

3. The "ferroelectric" assignment and the ΔP·M order parameter are inferred from R_xy alone. Two alternatives are not excluded.
   - First, the unipolar E-loop (switching at about −34 and about −37 mV/nm, both at negative E) is the phenomenology of a hysteretic first-order transition between two distinct phases. It is not by itself evidence of a switchable polar order: there is no ±P reversal under ±E, and no polarization is measured.
   - Second, a reversal of the B-hysteresis sign with a gate parameter is known in orbital magnets whose orbital magnetization changes sign while the anomalous Hall sign does not, or vice versa. Examples are electrical switching of orbital magnetism in moiré graphene when the chemical potential crosses a gap, where edge-state contributions dominate M. The statement "Since R_xy reflects the direction of magnetization" is therefore not generally true for a non-quantized anomalous Hall signal (Fig. 4e, ΔR_xy about 300–400 Ω). The sign of σ_xy relative to M depends on the Berry-curvature distribution of the occupied states, which here changes with E by construction. A change in the sign of M·B coupling relative to R_xy can then occur without any electric polarization.
   The decisive tests the authors should add:
   - (i) At fixed E inside the E-hysteresis window, check whether the polarity of the B-loop depends on the E-history: approached from above versus from below. This is what a P memory would require.
   - (ii) Give an independent probe of layer polarization or of a change in polarization. Penetration-field capacitance would do, or comparing top-gate and bottom-gate transfer through the transition, since a spontaneous ΔP should shift the effective E seen by the sample.
   - (iii) Give at least a minimal model, for example a free energy with P, M and their coupling, or a mean-field calculation for the proposed 3:1 state, showing that the 3:1 state carries both a net P and an M whose relative sign flips across the transition.
   Otherwise the title should say something like "electric-field-switchable orbital magnetism", and the ΔP·M language should be presented as a hypothesis.

B. Should address

4. Reproducibility across devices is asserted but not shown. The Methods state "three devices were fabricated and measured with similar results", yet every figure appears to come from one device, and the device behind each figure is not identified. For SC1, SC2 and the finite-E multiferroic state, which are small features in narrow n–E windows, the authors should show at least an n–E map and the key signature (R_xx(T) drop, the E-loop, the sign reversal of the B-hysteresis) from a second device, or state plainly which phenomena were seen in only one device. Report whether the features survive thermal cycling, since flavor-ordered and stacking states in rhombohedral graphene are known to be history- and strain-dependent.

5. The band inversion and two-by-two flavor picture (Fig. 2g–j) is a schematic and is not compared quantitatively with any calculation. The tight-binding model (Fig. 1a–d) is single-particle and cannot produce the emergent gaps. The flavored bands in Fig. 2g–j are drawn by hand. The authors themselves list anomalies the picture does not explain: two-fold rather than four-fold degeneracy to the right of the long-dashed line at E = 0, and valley-polarized magnetism incompatible with a layer antiferromagnet. A self-consistent Hartree–Fock calculation, or at least a phenomenological model with flavor-dependent emergent potentials fitted to a few Lifshitz points, should be added. It should be tested on whether it reproduces:
   - the positions and tilts of the purple and orange lines in Fig. 2a;
   - the crossing point at E ≈ 42 mV/nm (Fig. 2f);
   - the f_ν values of FS1 and FS2.
   The tight-binding parameters "taken from previous literature" (refs. 4, 36) should be listed, and the conversion between U (meV/layer) and E (mV/nm), including the dielectric screening assumed, should be stated. Without that conversion the comparison of the about 100 mV/nm transport gap threshold with Fig. 1a cannot be checked.

6. The link between SC1 and the "two emergent Fermi surfaces" is suggestive but not demonstrated. FS1 and FS2 are measured at B⊥ = 0.4–1.6 T, where the low-field superconducting-like state is absent. They also "disappear rapidly with increasing temperature" (Fig. S6, already faint at 314–500 mK), which by itself indicates heavy mass or small Fermi surfaces rather than an "emergent nature". The overlay in Fig. 3d does not mark where SC1 lies. The authors should:
   - overlay the zero-field SC1 region (for example, a contour of the R_xx drop at base temperature) on the f_ν manifolds of Fig. 3d;
   - extract effective masses for FS1 and FS2 from Lifshitz–Kosevich fits to the temperature series in Fig. S6;
   - soften "hints at a possible dual carrier origin" unless the coincidence is shown quantitatively.

7. Fermiology analysis details are needed to judge the low-frequency peaks that carry the Fig. 3 argument. f_ν peaks near zero frequency (Fig. 3b–d, Figs. S7, S8) are sensitive to background subtraction, windowing and the finite 1/B range (0.4–1.6 T). The authors should:
   - state how the smooth background was removed, the window function, the 1/B range and the resulting f_ν resolution at each n;
   - show that the low-f_ν arcs are stable when these choices change;
   - explain how the peak manifolds in Fig. 3d were assigned across cuts. The peaks are "sketched" by hand in Figs. S7 and S8, and an objective peak-finding criterion would help.
   The sum-rule violations in "partially flavor polarized" regimes (Fig. S5) are attributed to unresolved heavy pockets. This is plausible, but it means that in those regions the Fermi-surface inventory used to argue for band inversion is incomplete, and the text should say so.

8. The Hall data need standard antisymmetrization and Rxx-mixing controls. R_xy maps at a fixed B⊥ = 50 mT (Fig. 4a,b; Fig. S2b; Fig. S11b–d) and E-sweep loops can contain admixture from R_xx. R_xx itself switches at the same transitions (Fig. S2a,c), and in Hall bars with contacts at slightly different positions this produces apparent "Hall" hysteresis. The authors should state whether R_xy was antisymmetrized (for hysteretic data, by combining the forward sweep at +B with the backward sweep at −B), and show antisymmetrized loops for Fig. 4e and Fig. S10a.

9. The quoted transition temperatures for the finite-E multiferroic state are inconsistent. The main text says "T_c = 1.5 K (Fig. S11)". Fig. S11 reports that the coercive field vanishes at 1.3 K, that the switching becomes soft at 2 K, that the E-hysteresis vanishes at 1.5 K, and that the Hall signal disappears only at 3 K, which the caption calls a "two-step transition". Please define which order each temperature refers to: the ferroic E-memory, the magnetic coercivity, the anomalous Hall signal. Compare these with the E = 0 triangle, where hysteresis persists to 3.6 K and vanishes by 4.8 K (Fig. S2g,h).

10. The integrity of the rhombohedral stacking after fabrication is not verified. Raman mapping (Fig. S13) was done before AFM isolation and stacking. Rhombohedral domains are known to relax to Bernal during transfer and etching. Post-fabrication Raman of the measured Hall-bar region, or another argument such as reproducing the expected band-insulator threshold, would rule out mixed-stacking regions. Such regions could cause inhomogeneous features, notably the partial resistance drop in concern 1.

C. Minor

11. Carrier density and displacement field calibration: give the hBN thicknesses, the gate capacitances, the formulas used for n and E (including any offset), and how they were checked against Landau-level or quantum-oscillation densities.

12. The excitation current and frequency used for the R_xx maps and for the R(T) curves in Fig. 1f and Fig. S12b are not given. The Methods mention a Keithley 2450 through 4.7 MΩ for DC bias only. Please state the AC excitation.

13. Fig. 4c,d are labelled n = −0.46×10¹¹ cm⁻², whereas the apparently identical maps in Fig. S10b,c are labelled n = +0.46×10¹¹ cm⁻². Fig. S10 also refers to "white arrows in e to f", but the panels are b and c. Please correct.

14. Fig. S11a: the y-axis reads "R_xx (kΩ)" with values up to 3000. These are presumably offset R_xy curves in Ω. The caption also refers to "a, b" and then "b, c, d" inconsistently.

15. Fig. S6 caption: "1.5 × 10¹² < |n| < 33 × 10¹²" is presumably 3.3 × 10¹². Fig. S3 title: "electron electron-doped". Fig. S5 text: "hole hole-doped".

16. The Methods formula for layer polarization lacks parentheses: (|ψ_top|² − |ψ_bot|²)/(|ψ_top|² + |ψ_bot|²).

17. The sentence explaining the name "ferrovalley" ("due to the absence of electric dipole and Berry curvature at E = 0") is unclear. A valley-polarized orbital magnet requires nonzero valley-resolved Berry curvature. Presumably the intended meaning is the absence of net layer polarization. Please rephrase.

18. Fig. S1: give the temperature range used for the Arrhenius fits. Comment on whether the 23 and 29 meV gaps are consistent with the 100 mV/nm onset and with the tight-binding gap at that field.

19. Fig. 1e and Fig. 2a: the dashed phase-boundary lines are drawn by eye. Indicate which are taken from fermiology and which from R_xx features alone.

20. The out-of-plane critical field of SC1 should be quoted numerically: Fig. 3e suggests a few mT, and Fig. S12d gives about 1 mT for SC2. This sets the scale for concern 2.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Reframe the title and abstract around the strongest result, the fermiology of the correlated semimetal and the E-driven flavor band inversion. Describe SC1 and SC2 as superconducting-like unless zero resistance and phase-coherence evidence are added.
- Add the E-history test of the B-loop polarity (concern 3) and a capacitance or dual-gate check of spontaneous layer polarization. These would turn the ferroelectric claim from an interpretation into a demonstration.
- Add one quantitative mean-field band model with flavor-dependent emergent gaps, fitted to a few Lifshitz points and then tested on the rest of Fig. 2a. This would make the band-inversion claim falsifiable.
- Present key data from the second and third devices in a single supplementary figure, giving a phase-by-phase table of which device showed what.

5. Verdict: major revision
