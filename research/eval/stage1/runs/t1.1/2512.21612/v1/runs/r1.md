Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper introduces a new moiré system, Bernal bilayer graphene twisted on rhombohedral tetralayer graphene (θ ≈ 1.37–1.38°). It reports a displacement-field-tuned switch between C = 4 and C = 3 quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) states at ν = 1, a cascade of zero-field Chern insulators with |C| = 2 to 7 near ν = 3, and a fractional state at ν = 2/3 with ρxy ≈ 3h/7e², which the authors interpret as an FCI with C = 7/3 that lies outside Landau-level and known high-Chern FCI frameworks. A robust C = 4 QAH state and a family of high-C anomalous Hall states in a new graphene moiré platform would be a useful experimental addition. A genuine σxy = 7/3 fractional state at ν = 2/3 would be a headline result. However, the evidence for that fractional state is currently much weaker than the framing suggests.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new and well-made heterostructure. Two Hall-bar devices (D1, D2) come from the same stack, rhombohedral stacking is checked by Raman before and after assembly (Extended Data Fig. 1), and the density is calibrated by both the ν = 4 gap and Brown–Zak oscillations (Extended Data Fig. 2).
- The C = 4 and C = 3 QAH states at ν = 1 are convincingly shown. ρxx is near zero, ρxy is on the plateau over a wide D range (Fig. 2c), there are full hysteresis loops (Fig. 2d–e), Streda fans (Fig. 2h–k), and a reproduction in D2 (Extended Data Fig. 6). The D-driven C = 4 → 3 change matches the non-interacting band topology change in the continuum model, and the crossing field is predicted to within about 5%.
- The ν ≈ 3 region contains many anomalous Hall states with hysteresis loops (Fig. 3e–j). It also shows a gate-sweep-direction-dependent phase boundary (Extended Data Fig. 7), and both features are reproduced in D2 (Extended Data Fig. 8). This is a rich and potentially important phenomenology.
- The authors are candid that the parent-band Chern number and the mechanism of the ν = 2/3 state are unsettled, and they give two concrete microscopic scenarios.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The claim that the ν = 2/3 state has quantized σxy = 7/3 does not follow from the data shown (Fig. 4d–f, Extended Data Figs. 4c, 4i, 4u, 11a).
   - The quantization claim rests on ρxy reaching 3h/7e² (≈ 11.06 kΩ) to 98–99% while ρxx stays at about 3–4 kΩ. With ρxx that large, ρxy no longer measures σxy. Inverting the tensor with ρxx ≈ 3–4 kΩ and ρxy ≈ 11 kΩ gives σxy ≈ 2.1–2.2 e²/h, not 7/3. That value is closer to an integer C = 2 than to 7/3.
   - In Fig. 4d (versus ν) and Fig. 4e (versus D), ρxy does not form a plateau. It forms a narrow peak (width about 0.01 in ν) and a dome. "Quantization" is then read off at the peak or at the "optimal D". When a non-quantized ρxy passes through a maximum, the maximum can sit near any chosen rational value, so matching 3h/7e² at the peak is weak evidence.
   - The value is reached only at B⊥ ≳ 0.2–0.3 T. At B = 0, ρxy is about 0.7–0.8 × 3h/7e² (Fig. 4f; Extended Data Fig. 4c, 4u). The "anomalous" (zero-field) fractional claim therefore lacks support.
   - What to do:
     - Report σxx and σxy, obtained by inverting the measured tensor, as functions of ν, D and B for both devices.
     - Show whether σxy plateaus at 7/3 over a finite window, at any field, while σxx goes to zero.
     - If ρxx cannot be brought much below ρxy, reframe the result as a fractional-filling anomalous Hall feature whose Hall value is near 3h/7e², not as a quantized FCI. Revise the title, abstract and conclusion accordingly.

2. The evidence that a gapped, incompressible state exists at ν = 2/3 with a fractional Streda slope is incomplete. Alternatives have not been excluded.
   - **Streda slope.** In Fig. 4g–i and Extended Data Fig. 6i–j the slope is shown only as a hand-drawn dashed line labelled C = 7/3. Over 0–3 T in this device (λ ≈ 10.4 nm), slopes of 2, 7/3 and 8/3 differ by only about 0.02–0.03 in ν. Please extract the ρxx-minimum and ρxy-feature trajectories, fit slope and intercept with uncertainties, and show the fits against the C = 2, 7/3, 8/3 and 3 lines. The intercept should also be fitted: the ρxy peak in Fig. 4d sits at ν ≈ 0.68–0.69, not 0.667. In addition, the two twist-angle calibrations differ (1.38° from ν = 4 versus 1.36° from Brown–Zak, Extended Data Fig. 2), which is about a 3% density-scale difference. Together these mean that assigning the intercept to exactly 2/3 needs to be demonstrated.
   - **Energy scales.** The activation gap is Δ ≈ 0.026 meV (≈ 0.3 K) in D1 and 0.016 meV in D2 (Extended Data Figs. 4o, 10c). Yet the hysteretic Hall signal persists to Tc ≈ 3.3 K (Extended Data Fig. 4c). The fits use a few points on a ρxx that saturates near 3 kΩ. A Hall response that outlives the transport gap by an order of magnitude suggests that most of ρxy comes from a magnetic, valley-polarized metallic background, not from a fractional gap. The authors should explain this and give the fitting range and the number of points.
   - **Inhomogeneity.** The feature lies next to the C = 4/C = 3 region at ν = 1 (Fig. 4a–c). It could arise from coexisting domains or twist-angle variation (for example, part of the channel in a C = 2 or C = 3 state and part metallic). This should be ruled out by measuring ρxy and ρxx on several contact pairs within D1, and by showing the local filling from different voltage-probe pairs.
   - **Device D2.** D2 has the sharper ρxx minimum but reaches only about 0.86 × 3h/7e² (Extended Data Fig. 11a). The authors attribute this to contact quality. The same figure, however, shows D2's integer states within about 3% of quantization, so contact quality cannot explain a 14% deficit for the fractional state alone. The more natural reading is that the 7/3 value is not robust across devices. Please address this directly.

B. Should address

3. The |C| = 5, 6, 7 states near ν ≈ 3.2–3.3 (Fig. 3d, 3h–j, 3l; Extended Data Fig. 9) need stronger support. They are quantized only in narrow ν windows (≈ 0.02 wide, Fig. 3d). h/6e² and h/7e² differ by only about 15%. Most importantly, ρxy rises about 20–25% above h/Ce² on warming before collapsing (Fig. 3l, Extended Data Fig. 9g–i). A quantized Chern insulator should not show this, and the authors themselves describe it as "completely anomalous".
   - Please give σxx and σxy at these points.
   - Give fitted Streda slopes and intercepts for each state from Fig. 3k and Extended Data Fig. 7f, 7i–j.
   - Show whether the states are discrete phases with finite-width plateaus, or a smooth evolution of ρxy that passes through h/Ce² values.
   - The claim of "continuous variation of Chern numbers via doping" in non-commensurate regions needs this analysis before it can be stated.

4. The phase maps were taken at fields below the coercive fields of the states shown. Fig. 3a–b, Fig. 4a–c and Extended Data Fig. 7 were measured at B⊥ = ±0.1 T and then (anti)symmetrized. The C = −2 and C = −3 states switch at about ±0.12–0.15 T (Fig. 3e–f), and the 7/3 state at about 0.1 T (Fig. 4f).
   - At ±0.1 T the magnetization need not reverse between the two field polarities. When it does not reverse, antisymmetrization cancels the anomalous part rather than isolating it, and the result depends on sweep history.
   - Please state the field-history protocol for each map.
   - Show the raw ρxy at +B and −B for the key regions.
   - Ideally, repeat the key maps at |B| above the largest coercive field. Some of the sharp boundaries in Fig. 3a–c, and the sweep-direction dependence, may partly reflect this protocol.

5. The activation gaps and Tc values are inconsistent, and the "unprecedented Tc" claim is unsupported.
   - For the C = 4 state at ν = 1, Extended Data Fig. 4m gives Δxx ≈ 10.3 meV (≈ 120 K) from points between about 6.7 and 8.3 K. That range straddles the magnetic transition at Tc ≈ 8.5 K, so the fit measures how sharply the ordering collapses, not a single-particle gap.
   - Please report gaps only from the activated regime, with fitting ranges stated. Explain the difference between Δxx and Δxy (for example 0.60 versus 1.41 meV for C = 3).
   - Compare Tc ≈ 8.5 K quantitatively with the QAH onset temperatures in the rhombohedral and moiré graphene works cited (refs. 39–43, 24, 26) before calling it unprecedented.
   - Define Tc consistently: hysteresis onset versus loss of quantization.

6. The title and novelty framing go beyond what is measured.
   - "Entanglement" in the title and "entangled Chern insulators" in the ν = 3 section are not defined or measured. The only entanglement in the paper is a property of one proposed trial wavefunction (the valley-entangled Halperin state). Please define the term operationally or remove it.
   - The statement that the 7/3 state is "beyond all known FCIs" needs comparing with fractional states that have both fractional Streda slope and fractional intercept in Hofstadter and moiré systems (for example, fractional Chern insulators in bilayer graphene/hBN superlattices, Spanton et al., Science 2018), and with charge-density-wave-reconstructed topological states.
   - The authors' own first mechanism is a √3×√3 charge order plus a Laughlin state in a C = 1 band. That is a combination of known ingredients, which sits uneasily with the claim that the state lies outside current theory.

7. The theory supports the integer states only qualitatively and does not address the fractional or high-C states.
   - Δ_U is mapped to D by a proportionality fixed at one point (3.25 meV ↔ 0.18 V/nm). Please justify this mapping, or compute Δ_U from the gate electrostatics with layer screening.
   - ε ≈ 6 and ξ = 10 nm are stated without justification. ξ should reflect the actual gate-to-sample distances (hBN is 10–20 nm on each side), and the sensitivity of the C = 4 → 3 boundary to these choices should be shown.
   - At ν = 3 the HF result is said to give C = ±4 "consistent with experiment over the explored displacement-field range". The experiment instead shows C = −4 only at ν ≈ 3.09 together with many other states. Please state this more carefully.
   - Neither proposed mechanism for 7/3 is backed by an energetic calculation. The Halperin (7,7,1) choice is made to reproduce the target slope. At minimum, the authors should say which experimental observable (for example, a neutral-mode signature, B-dependence of the intercept, or Hall drag) could distinguish the two mechanisms.

8. Twist-angle homogeneity and the commensurability argument need stronger support.
   - The interpretation of the ν ≈ 2.5–3.5 states as 4× supercell states at ν = 2 + n/4 (Methods) rests on precise filling assignments.
   - The two twist-angle methods disagree at the 0.02° level in both devices.
   - Please give the uncertainty in ν, show twist-angle consistency across contact pairs, and quote the fitted Streda intercepts of the ν ≈ 2.5–3.5 states to test whether they cluster at multiples of 1/4.

C. Minor

9. Fig. 3m and the text mislabel the gaps.
   - The text quotes the 1.63 meV gap for "the C = 4 state". Extended Data Fig. 4r shows that this value belongs to the C = −4 state at (3.09, 0.471).
   - In Fig. 3m, the bracket (3.09, 0.471) is placed at |C| = 3 and (3.23, 0.503) at |C| = 4. Going by Fig. 3e–h, the |C| = 3 state is at (2.73, 0.494), and (3.23, 0.503) is a C = 5 state.
   - Please correct the labels and use signed C throughout.

10. Several temperatures, field values and captions are inconsistent.
   - The Methods give a base temperature of about 30 mK, but all figures state T = 10 mK. Please state which is meant, and give an estimate of the electron temperature, which matters for a 0.3 K gap.
   - Extended Data Fig. 4 caption gives D/ε0 = 0.5586 V/nm, but Fig. 2d gives 0.586 V/nm.
   - Extended Data Fig. 6 is titled "device D2" but its panels a–b say "device D1".
   - Extended Data Fig. 10 has "B⊥ = +-0.1T".
   - Extended Data Fig. 2 caption has "Plank".

11. The text says "ρxy quantized to 3h/7e² with an accuracy of 99%" (Fig. 4d, at 0.3 T), then "98% when B⊥ > 200 mT", while Extended Data Fig. 11a shows about 0.975 at 0.2 T. Please quote one value with its field and uncertainty.

12. Duplicate references: 12 = 9, 37 = 34, 45 = 40, 49 = 42, 57 = 36.

13. The Streda captions say the dashed lines "strictly follow" the Streda formula, but the lines are guides to the eye. Please either fit them or soften the wording.

14. In the hysteresis-loop panels (for example Fig. 3e–f, 4f), ρxx is plotted in units of h/Ce². Showing ρxx in kΩ, or as ρxx/ρxy, would make the residual dissipation easier to judge.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Present all key states (ν = 2/3, the C = 5–7 states, and C = 3 at ν = 1) in conductivity form (σxx, σxy), with fitted Streda slopes and intercepts and their error bars, in one summary table that also gives the gap, Tc and the coercive field.
2. Measure the ν = 2/3 feature on several contact pairs and after a thermal cycle, and repeat it at higher field (1–3 T). If the state is a genuine FCI, σxx should fall and σxy should flatten along the Streda trajectory, which would greatly strengthen the claim.
3. Add HF or exact-diagonalization energetics in a √3×√3 or 2×2 supercell at ν = 2/3, even on small clusters, to show whether either proposed mechanism is competitive.
4. Retitle and reframe around what is firmly established: the C = 4/C = 3 QAH switching and the high-C cascade near ν = 3. Present the ν = 2/3 state as a candidate fractional anomalous Hall state, unless the additional evidence above establishes quantization.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
