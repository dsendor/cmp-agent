Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports zero-field quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) states at moiré filling ν = 1 in twisted monolayer–rhombohedral N-layer graphene, with Chern number C = N for N = 3, 4, 5. It also shows that the sign of C at ν ≈ 3 in a (1+3) L device switches with doping and with displacement field, and that a (2+4) L device undergoes a displacement-field-driven transition between C = 3 and C = 4 QAH states. A single, systematic platform in which layer number sets |C|, and in which |C| itself (not only its sign) can be changed by a gate, is a useful step for high-Chern-number moiré physics. The novelty is reduced somewhat by the authors' own earlier work on the (1+5) device (ref. 39) and by concurrent studies of the same structures (refs. 40, 41, 54, 55).

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The Chern number is determined two independent ways, from quantized ρxy at B = 0 with hysteresis and from the Středa slope of Landau fans: for C = 3 and 4 at ν = 1 (Fig. 2b,f), C = ±3 at ν ≈ 3 (Fig. 3e,f) and C = 3 and 4 in the (2+4) device (Fig. 4g,h).
- The key results are reproduced. C = 3 at ν = 1 and C = ±3 at ν ≈ 3 appear in three (1+3) L devices (D1, D6, D7; Extended Data Figs. 8, 9). The C = 3 to C = 4 transition appears in two (2+4) L devices with different twist angles (D4, D5; Extended Data Fig. 7).
- The D-driven change of |C| between two quantized plateaus in one device (Fig. 4f) is shown cleanly. Its temperature evolution and activation gaps are given in Extended Data Fig. 4.
- The twist angle is cross-checked by full-filling density and by Brown–Zak oscillations (Extended Data Table 1). Raman and IR characterization of the rhombohedral domains is given (Extended Data Fig. 1).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The main claims (C = N at ν = 1, gate-switchable chirality near ν = 3, and a D-driven C = 3 to C = 4 transition) each rest on quantized zero-field ρxy, hysteresis and Středa analysis, in more than one device where it matters most. A referee at this level would accept that evidence. The items below would narrow or strengthen the claims but do not overturn them.

B. Should address

1. The claim that "the layer number N directly sets the Chern number" is stronger than the evidence and the authors' own calculations support. (Abstract; p. 3; Discussion "the Chern number can be programmed by selecting specific layer configurations"; Extended Data Fig. 10.)
   - Extended Data Fig. 10 shows that C of the first conduction band depends strongly on θ and on Δ. For (1+5) L, C = 5 occupies only a limited window. For (2+4) L the same structure gives C = 3 or 4 depending on Δ, which is the paper's own Fig. 4.
   - The interlayer potentials used in Fig. 1b (Δ = 9.0, 12.5, 11.0 meV) are described as "optimized", but the paper does not say how they were chosen or whether they correspond to the experimental D at which the QAH states are seen.
   - The evidence in hand is one (1+4) device (D2) and one (1+5) device (D3), and D3 was already studied in ref. 39. This paper shows no Středa fan and no temperature dependence for D3 (Fig. 1c only).
   - The authors should do three things. Rephrase the claim as C = N holding within a stated range of θ and Δ. Mark each measured device's (θ, estimated Δ) on the Extended Data Fig. 10 maps. State explicitly which (1+5) evidence is new here and which is from ref. 39, including where the Středa confirmation of C = 5 can be found.

2. The comparison between theory and experiment for the C = 3 to C = 4 transition is qualitative, but the text says it "agrees well with band structure calculations". (p. 6; Fig. 4a; Extended Data Fig. 10d.)
   - The two Δ values in Fig. 4a (11.5 and 10.0 meV) are not linked to the measured D values (−0.669 and −0.602 V nm⁻¹) by any stated D-to-Δ conversion. This conversion is nontrivial because of interlayer screening, which the authors themselves invoke on p. 4.
   - The calculation fixes εr = 20 and ds = 28 or 53 nm "to emulate the experiment". εr = 20 is large, and the paper does not show that the C = 3 / C = 4 boundary is robust to this choice.
   - A real test is available in the data. D4 (θ = 1.21°) has its transition near D ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹. D5 (θ = 1.45°) shows C = 3 near −0.71 and C = 4 near −0.56 V nm⁻¹. The authors should give a D-to-Δ mapping (for example a self-consistent screening estimate) and compare the predicted critical Δc(θ) with the measured transition fields in both devices. They should also show how the phase boundary moves when εr is varied over a reasonable range.

3. The physical picture for chirality switching near ν = 3 is not supported by a calculation, and the state itself is not explained. (Fig. 3k; pp. 4–5.)
   - Fig. 3k is a schematic free-energy landscape. No calculated orbital magnetization (bulk plus edge) as a function of ν and D is shown, so the claim that "the displacement field can directly modify the energetic stability of valley-polarized states" stays a hypothesis.
   - The paper also does not explain why the state near ν = 3, at three electrons per moiré cell, carries |C| = 3, or which band is involved. The Hartree–Fock calculations address only ν = 1.
   - The authors should compute the orbital magnetization of the K- and K′-polarized ν = 3 states versus D (and ν), and show whether its sign changes in the experimental window. If that is beyond scope, they should present Fig. 3k explicitly as a conjecture and weaken the wording.

4. Electrical switching of chirality is shown only in a small field, not at B = 0. (Fig. 3i,j.)
   - The ν- and D-sweep hysteresis loops in Fig. 3i,j are taken at B = 0.1 T, and the maps in Fig. 3a–d are also taken at 0.1 T. The abstract presents in-situ electrical control as a central claim. For that claim the relevant demonstration is switching at B = 0, or at least a showing that the 0.1 T bias field does not determine which valley is selected.
   - ρxx is not shown alongside Fig. 3i,j. Nonzero ρxx at and between switching events would indicate domain coexistence. That matters especially for the statement that opposite Chern numbers "coexist within a single scan direction" (regions ① and ④).
   - The authors should add D (and ν) sweep loops at B = 0 after field training, with ρxx shown, and ideally repeated cycles to show that the switching is deterministic.

5. Quantization quality is overstated for several devices and states. (Discussion p. 6: "well-developed quantization and suppressed longitudinal resistance … across seven devices.")
   - In D5 the C = 3 state needs B = 1.5 T to quantize (Extended Data Fig. 7f, top), and its hysteresis loop (Extended Data Fig. 7c) shows multi-step switching without a clean zero-field plateau.
   - In D6 and D7 the ν ≈ 3 states show ρxx of roughly 0.1–0.15 h/e² (Extended Data Figs. 8c, 9c).
   - In the D4 C = 4 plateau, ρxx stays at a few percent of h/e² (Fig. 4f; Extended Data Fig. 4b).
   - The authors should tabulate, for each device and state, the deviation of ρxy from h/Ce², the residual ρxx at base temperature, and the field at which these were measured. They should then soften the "seven devices" statement to match.

6. The fractional-filling claims go beyond the data. (p. 4; Extended Data Figs. 5, 6; Discussion.)
   - The "Chern insulator with C = 2 at ν = 3/2" rests on a Středa line in Extended Data Fig. 5b. At low field, ρxy in Extended Data Fig. 5c saturates near 0.3 h/e², not h/2e², and no ρxx minimum is shown at that point.
   - The interpretation as an interaction-induced doubling of the moiré cell or topological charge order (refs. 16, 47) is therefore not established by these data.
   - The authors should label these as signatures consistent with a C = 2 state, and show ρxx and ρxy versus B at fixed ν = 3/2 up to fields where the Středa state is quantized, if it is.

7. Verification of rhombohedral stacking in the measured channel is indirect. (Extended Data Fig. 1.)
   - The 2D-peak spectra used to tell rhombohedral from Bernal domains in the final stacks (panels e, k) differ only slightly.
   - The paper does not show that the Hall-bar region lies entirely within a rhombohedral domain after transfer and processing. Stacking-order relaxation is a known risk in rhombohedral multilayers.
   - The authors should overlay the final Hall-bar outline on the Raman FWHM maps for each device, and state whether any device showed signs of mixed stacking. For example, an asymmetric ν–D map inconsistent with the expected layer structure would be such a sign.

8. The uncertainty in twist angle and its spatial homogeneity are not given. (Extended Data Table 1.)
   - For D1 and D4–D7, the two "independent" methods give identical θ to 0.01° and identical λ to 0.01 nm. This is surprising unless the methods share a calibration: the gate capacitances are themselves calibrated from quantum oscillations (Methods).
   - The authors should give uncertainties for each method and state whether they are truly independent. They should also report θ from more than one contact pair, if available, because twist-angle inhomogeneity bears directly on the interpretation of the D-driven C = 3 / C = 4 transition and of the coexisting opposite-C regions in Fig. 3j.

C. Minor

9. Extended Data Fig. 3 caption: panels a, d, g, j are said to show ρxx and b, e, h, k ρxy, but the figure shows the reverse. Extended Data Fig. 4 caption has the same swap for panels a and b, and it writes "(Δxx and Δxx)" where Δxx and Δxy are meant. The δρxy axes in Extended Data Fig. 4c,d show a "0" tick on a logarithmic scale.

10. Field values are inconsistent. On p. 3, "B = ±0.1 T (±90 mT)" are not the same value. Fig. 2a uses ±0.1 T, Fig. 2e uses ±90 mT, and Fig. 2c,d use ±50 mT. Please state the field for each panel consistently.

11. Fig. 4f is described in the text as a cut along the "red vertical line" and in the caption as along the "yellow vertical line". The pink-star D is −0.603 V nm⁻¹ in the caption and −0.602 V nm⁻¹ elsewhere. Twist angles in the text (D2: 1.16°, D3: 1.39°) differ from the full-filling values in Extended Data Table 1 (1.15°, 1.40°). Please state which value is used.

12. The statement "QAH persisting up to 3 K and Curie temperature approximately 8 K" (p. 4) applies to D1 only. D2 and D4 lose hysteresis near 5–6 K (Extended Data Fig. 3). Please give these numbers per device. The Arrhenius fits in Extended Data Fig. 4c,d use a narrow high-temperature range; please state the fit window. Please also comment on why the activation gaps (about 2.1 and 1.0 meV) are much larger than the Curie temperatures.

13. Typos: "Cuire" (p. 4), "Twisted angle determination" (Methods heading), "Resistivity were obtained" (Methods).

14. Data are available only "upon reasonable request". Depositing the data behind the main figures in a public repository would help reproducibility.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one combined figure or table that places every measured device and state on the calculated C(θ, Δ) maps of Extended Data Fig. 10, using an explicit D-to-Δ conversion. This would turn the "design principle" from a slogan into a quantitative test.
2. Show B = 0 electrical switching of chirality (ν and D sweeps after field training, with ρxx), repeated over several cycles. This is the strongest form of the "topological transistor" claim made in the Discussion.
3. Provide a theoretical account of the |C| = 3 states at ν ≈ 3 and of their orbital magnetization as a function of D, so that the switching mechanism in Fig. 3k is calculated rather than schematic.
4. Clarify the relation to ref. 39, the authors' earlier work that includes D3, and to the concurrent works refs. 40, 41, 54 and 55, so readers can see what is new here: the (1+3) and (1+4) data, the chirality switching and the |C|-changing transition.

5. Verdict: minor revision
