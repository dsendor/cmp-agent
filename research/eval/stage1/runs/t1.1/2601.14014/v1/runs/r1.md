Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports zero-field quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) states with high Chern numbers in twisted rhombohedral graphene. It finds C = N at ν = 1 in twisted monolayer–rhombohedral N-layer graphene, (1+N) L, for N = 3, 4, 5. It also reports C = ±3 at ν = 3 in (1+3) L, with the sign switched by doping or by D, and a D-driven transition from C = 3 to C = 4 at ν = 1 in twisted Bernal bilayer–rhombohedral tetralayer, (2+4) L. A layer-programmable Chern number, and a transition between two nonzero quantized Chern states controlled by a gate, would interest the moiré and topological-matter communities. The novelty is narrowed by the authors' own earlier study of the same (1+5) device (Ref. 39) and by the concurrent reports on (2+4) L and on the (1+N) family (Refs. 40, 41, 54, 55). The manuscript does not say clearly what is new relative to these works.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Several independent checks agree on the Chern number. At zero field, ρxy sits on h/Ce² with ρxx near zero, and hysteresis loops are shown for C = 3, 4, 5 (Fig. 1c) and for C = 3 and 4 in D4 (Fig. 4c,d). The Streda slopes from Landau fans agree with the plateau values (Figs. 2b,f; 3e,f; 4g,h).
- The twist angles found from full filling and from Brown–Zak oscillations agree to within 0.01° (Extended Data Table 1, Extended Data Fig. 3). This argues against strong twist-angle disorder in D1, D2 and D4.
- In D4 a single gate-tunable parameter moves the system between two quantized states with different |C| (Fig. 4f). Temperature dependence for both states is given in Extended Data Figs. 4g–l and 5. The comparison with Hartree–Fock is qualitatively consistent.
- The sign switching at ν = 3 is documented with ν- and D-scan-direction maps, difference maps and hysteresis loops (Fig. 3, Extended Data Figs. 6, 7).
- Hartree–Fock phase diagrams for each stacking (Extended Data Fig. 10) give the experiments a predictive frame.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. Each headline claim is supported by zero-field quantization together with at least one independent check. The items below would make the claims stronger and better defined.

B. Should address

1. **The C = 5 result rests on one loop from a previously published device.** The claim that C = N holds for N = 3, 4, 5 is the first headline result. For N = 5, the manuscript shows only one hysteresis loop (Fig. 1c, right, D3, D = −0.620 V nm⁻¹).
   - What is missing for D3: no ν–D map, no Landau fan or Streda analysis, no temperature dependence and no Raman characterization. The text (p. 3) says "the full ν–D map of ρxx at B = 0 T for all devices are provided in Extended Data Fig. 2", but that figure contains only D1, D2 and D4. Extended Data Fig. 3 likewise omits D3, although Extended Data Table 1 lists a Brown–Zak twist angle for it.
   - Prior publication: Methods states that D3 "has been systematically studied previously [39]".
   - What to do: add the Streda fan, the ν–D map and the T dependence for D3, or state explicitly that these are published in Ref. 39. Then say which (1+5) results, if any, are new here.

2. **The novelty relative to Refs. 39–41, 54 and 55 needs to be stated.** Ref. 39 (same group, "Diverse high-Chern-number QAH insulators in twisted rhombohedral graphene") and Ref. 55 ("Family of high-Chern-number orbital magnets in twisted rhombohedral graphene") appear to cover the C = N family. Ref. 54 covers (2+4) L. The introduction cites these only as "very recent advances", and the note added gives no comparison.
   - What to do: add a short paragraph listing which observations are new. The candidates are the C = 3↔4 transition at fixed ν, the D-switching of opposite Chern numbers within one scan direction, and the C = 4 state in (1+4) L.
   - Where results overlap, compare the numbers: the Chern numbers, D windows, Curie temperatures and twist angles reported in those works.

3. **The C = 3 → C = 4 transition needs to be shown to be a bulk transition, not coexisting domains.**
   - What the data show: in Fig. 4f the two plateaus are separated by a window (−0.641 < D < −0.608 V nm⁻¹). There ρxy takes intermediate values and ρxx rises only modestly, to about 0.05–0.1 h/e².
   - Why it matters: an intermediate ρxy with low ρxx is also what a Hall bar would show if it held C = 3 and C = 4 regions side by side. Such regions could arise from small gradients in twist angle, D or strain, so a smooth crossover in ρxy does not by itself demonstrate a transition between uniform phases.
   - What to do:
     - Show ρxx and ρxy across the transition for more than one pair of voltage probes on the Hall bar (Fig. 1a shows several contacts).
     - Report whether the transition window depends on the D-sweep direction.
     - Extract thermally activated gaps of the C = 3 and C = 4 states as a function of D (from Extended Data Fig. 5b and similar data). A gap that shrinks toward D ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹ would support a gap-closing transition. It is the closest transport analogue of the band inversion in Fig. 4a.

4. **The quantization accuracy is not stated.** The text calls the plateaus "precisely quantized" (pp. 4, 6), but no number is given.
   - Several traces are visibly imperfect:
     - In Extended Data Fig. 4l, for the C = 4 state of D4, ρxx at 0.3 K is clearly nonzero and ρxy is slightly below 1 in units of h/4e².
     - In Fig. 4e (bottom) and Fig. 4f, ρxx in the C = 4 window is roughly 0.03–0.05 h/e².
     - In Fig. 2c, ρxx at the ν = 1 minimum is not obviously zero.
   - What to do: for each state (C = 3, 4, 5 at ν = 1; ±3 at ν = 3; 3 and 4 in D4), tabulate at base temperature and B = 0 the deviation of ρxy from h/Ce² in percent and the residual ρxx.
   - Use the measured W/L for each Hall bar, and say whether the ρxx-to-ρxy conversion is reliable for these geometries.

5. **The Streda analysis is shown as drawn lines, not fits.** Figs. 2b,f, 3e,f and 4g,h show white dashed lines labelled with integer C.
   - Why it matters: the slopes for C = 3 and C = 4 differ by only about 0.06 in ν per tesla over the 0–2 T range. This is most relevant in D4, where the two values are the main claim.
   - What to do: fit the ν positions of the ρxx minima (or ρxy plateau centres) against B and give the fitted C with its uncertainty.
   - State that the ν-to-density conversion uses the Brown–Zak calibration, so readers can see that the Streda C is independent of the plateau value.

6. **The mechanism proposed for the D-induced sign reversal at ν = 3 is not tested.** On p. 5 the authors state that "the underlying mechanism is a displacement-field-induced topological phase transition, in which D modulates the distribution of Berry curvature and changes the Chern number of the corresponding valley Chern band."
   - The alternative: the authors themselves give it on p. 5, where the sign of the bulk-plus-edge orbital magnetization changes and the valley polarization reverses (Refs. 48, 49). That mechanism could also act along D at fixed ν, because D shifts the chemical potential within the gap.
   - Why it matters: the two pictures make different predictions. In the first, the valley Chern number changes sign. In the second, the valley polarization flips and the band topology is unchanged.
   - What to do: provide a Hartree–Fock calculation at ν = 3 for θ = 1.29° showing whether the C = ±3 state comes from a sign change of the valley Chern number as Δ varies, or tone the statement down.
   - Further checks: the D-switching in Fig. 3i is shown only at B = 0.1 T. Show whether it persists at B = 0, as the ν-switching does, and how it depends on |B|.

7. **The theory is compared with the data only qualitatively, and its parameters are chosen by hand.**
   - The parameter choices: Fig. 1b uses an "optimized" Δ for each device (9.0, 12.5, 11.0 meV). The screening uses εr = 20 with d_s = 53 or 28 nm. Only the first five conduction bands are kept (Methods).
   - What is missing: the paper does not map experimental D onto Δ. Such a mapping would include layer screening and the D0 offset, and the paper does not say how D0 is determined. Without it, one cannot tell whether the D windows in which QAH states appear (Figs. 2, 4b) or the D4 transition at D ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹ match the calculated Δ ≈ 10–11.5 meV.
   - What to do:
     - Give the D-to-Δ conversion used, and compare the predicted and observed D windows for each device.
     - Explain the choice of εr = 20, and show that the Chern numbers in Extended Data Fig. 10 are robust to εr and to how many bands are kept (valence-band screening is omitted).
     - Show the band spin/valley polarization assumed in the Hartree–Fock ground state.

8. **The D sign convention appears inconsistent with the stated layer polarization.**
   - The stated convention: Fig. 1a and Methods define D > 0 as pointing from the rhombohedral stack (bottom) toward the monolayer (top), with D = (CbVb − CtVt)/2ε0.
   - The resulting physics: a positive bottom-gate voltage gives D > 0 and draws electrons toward the bottom of the rhombohedral stack, away from the twisted interface. QAH states appear at D < 0, which in this convention pushes electrons toward the monolayer and the moiré interface.
   - The conflict: the text (p. 3) says the QAH states appear "when electrons are polarized away from moiré interface by applying a sufficiently large negative D".
   - What to do: clarify the geometry (for example, whether some stacks are flipped) and how the sign of Δ in the calculations relates to D. The physical picture of which layer hosts the Chern band depends on this.

9. **Only one device per stacking was measured, and no reproducibility is shown.** Each stacking is represented by one device (D1–D4), and no data from second devices or from thermal cycles are shown.
   - The C = N pattern across stackings partly makes up for this.
   - Claims that depend on details are more vulnerable: the D-driven sign switching at ν = 3 and the C = 3/4 transition in (2+4) L. Both depend on fine features such as the narrow D windows and the shape of the domain boundaries in Fig. 3b,d.
   - What to do: state how many devices of each stacking were fabricated and measured, and whether any failed to show the states. If a second (1+3) or (2+4) device exists, include it, even at lower quality.

10. **The secondary claims at ν = 3/2 and ν = 7/3 go beyond the data.**
    - ν = 3/2: the text (p. 4) says "the signature of a Chern insulator with C = 2 is identified", which indicates "interaction-induced doubling of the moiré unit cell". In Extended Data Fig. 8c, however, the zero-field ρxy at 0.5 K is about 0.3 h/e², well below the h/2e² expected for C = 2.
    - ν = 7/3: Extended Data Fig. 9 shows ρxy of about 0.25 h/e² near ν = 7/3, with no Streda line drawn. The expected σxy for the proposed fractional state is not given.
    - What to do:
      - Describe both features as unquantized anomalous Hall states with Streda slopes. For ν = 3/2, give the fitted slope and its intercept, which tests the doubled-cell interpretation.
      - For ν = 7/3, state which Chern number and fractional Hall conductance are proposed, and show the fitted dispersion.
      - Soften "identified" and "fractional Chern insulator" in the Discussion accordingly.

C. Minor

11. Extended Data Fig. 4 caption: the panel assignments are swapped. Panels a, d, g, j show ρxy and panels b, e, h, k show ρxx, opposite to what the caption says.

12. Extended Data Fig. 5 caption: "symmetrized ρxx (a) and anti-symmetrized ρxy (b)" is reversed; panel a shows ρxy.

13. The main text (p. 5) refers to "linecut scan along red vertical line in Fig. 4b", while the Fig. 4 caption says "yellow vertical line". The pink-star position is D = −0.603 V nm⁻¹ in the caption but D = −0.602 V nm⁻¹ for the linecut in Fig. 4e. Make these consistent.

14. Extended Data Fig. 2 says all maps were taken at T = 0.3 K, but Methods says D2 was measured in the dilution refrigerator at about 50 mK. Clarify which applies.

15. The field values are inconsistent. Figs. 2a and 3 use B = ±0.1 T, Fig. 2c,d uses ±50 mT, and Figs. 2e–h and 4 use ±90 mT. The text conflates "±0.1 T (±90 mT)" (p. 3). State the actual values and the reason for each choice.

16. Extended Data Fig. 1e,k: the 2D Raman spectra for rhombohedral and Bernal regions in the final stacks are barely distinguishable. Give the FWHM or asymmetry values that were used to assign the stacking, and say whether the region under the Hall bar was mapped after fabrication.

17. Methods: "ν = 4n/ns, where ns = 4/A" reduces to ν = nA. Give the area A from the Brown–Zak λ directly. Also state how the gate capacitances and the offsets n0 and D0 were determined.

18. The higher |D| threshold in (1+4) L compared with (1+3) L is attributed to interlayer screening (p. 3–4). These are different devices with different D0 and hBN thicknesses, so this comparison needs caution or support from the calculation.

19. The text says "Cuire temperature"; this should be "Curie temperature". The Discussion statements about Majorana edge modes and a "topological transistor" are speculative. Shorten them or mark them as outlook.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a summary table listing, for every QAH state, the following: the device; ν; the D window; the Chern number from the plateau and from the Streda fit, with its uncertainty; the deviation of ρxy from h/Ce²; the residual ρxx; the Curie temperature; and the activation gap.
- In D4, map the activation gap or ρxx(T) across the full D range of the C = 3 → 4 transition, and repeat the D-linecut on a second contact pair. Together these would show whether the transition is a bulk gap closing.
- Overlay the calculated Chern-number phase boundaries (Extended Data Fig. 10), converted to D with an explicit D-to-Δ mapping, on the measured ν–D maps for each stacking. This would turn the qualitative agreement into a test of the theory.
- Bring the D3 data into the same format as the other devices (map, fan, temperature dependence) so the C = N family is documented uniformly.

5. Verdict: minor revision
