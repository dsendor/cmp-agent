# Concerns

## A. Must address

1. **Extrinsic non-equilibrium excitation (stray radiation) is not experimentally excluded.** The authors cite Tamir et al. [45] as the key precedent: in thin films, better high-frequency filtering removed exactly this anomalous-metal phenomenology. Yet the Methods describe only phosphor-bronze DC wiring, a fast-sample-exchange module and a single on-board RC low-pass stage on the flex-PCB (10 kΩ, 1 nF, cutoff about 16 kHz). There is no copper-powder, Eccosorb or other cold microwave filtering, and no radiation-tight shielding of the sample space or GHz attenuation. An RC filter of this type does nothing at the frequencies that break pairs or excite phase slips (≳ 2Δ/h, tens of GHz for T_c ~ 100 mK). The paper's counter-argument is that, "taking T_c as a proxy for the robustness of the paired state, any source of external non-equilibrium excitation should affect both regions comparably". This does not hold. The sensitivity of zero resistance to radiation is set by phase stiffness, condensation or Josephson energy, critical current and vortex pinning, not by T_c. On the paper's own data these differ strongly between the pockets: perpendicular critical fields differ by 10–100× (Fig. 4a–c) and the critical currents also differ (Fig. 3a). The "similar T_c" premise is also itself inconsistent (see concern 11): the AM has the higher T_c in the Fig. 1 pockets yet fails to reach zero resistance. The statement that R_sat does not depend on ac excitation down to 500 pA is asserted but not shown, and it says nothing about broadband radiation from the environment. Since the central claim is that the AM is intrinsic, this needs a direct test. The authors should (i) describe the full filtering and shielding chain; (ii) show R_sat after adding cold microwave filtering and/or shielding at the mixing-chamber stage, or in a second, well-filtered cryostat; or (iii) deliberately apply broadband noise or microwaves and show that the SC zero-resistance state and the AM R_sat respond in a way that is inconsistent with the AM simply being a more fragile superconductor; and (iv) show the data on R_sat against ac excitation amplitude and against dc bias near I_dc = 0.

2. **Intrinsic electronic phase coexistence (isospin domains or domain walls) is not addressed, and the inhomogeneity arguments given do not exclude it.** The case against inhomogeneity (pp. 5–6 and Methods, "Considerations of extrinsic origins") targets structural disorder. It says rhombohedral graphene "has no obvious length scale larger than the crystal lattice", that percolation "should depend sensitively on geometric details", and that the footprints are identical across probe pairs and devices. None of this applies to electronic phase coexistence near a first-order isospin transition. Such coexistence is set by band structure and electrostatics, so it would recur at the same (n, D, B∥) in every contact pair and in a second device. Its domain size (tens of nanometres to sub-micron, set by Coulomb frustration) is tied to no structural length. Several observations point to this scenario. The SC abuts the spin-polarized half-metal and the AM abuts the degeneracy-four unpolarized metal (Fig. 2a, Extended Data Fig. 8, p. 3). The SC pocket shows an instability and hysteresis at B∥ = 0 that are attributed to orbital magnetization or valley imbalance (Fig. 1b–e, Extended Data Fig. 3). The SC/AM boundary is "abrupt" (Fig. 2c). ρ_xy is finite in the AM but not in the SC (Extended Data Figs. 2, 11; concern 9). In the second device some AM contact pairs read above the normal state (Extended Data Fig. 11b,c). Superconducting domains threaded by non-superconducting isospin domains or domain walls would give a finite, T-independent plateau. They would give an enhanced apparent critical field tied to domain size (the ~30 nm "ξ" of the AM), multi-step I–V curves (Fig. 3d), and possibly current-driven domain rearrangement producing the non-monotonic dV/dI. The SNS argument in the Methods (ξ_N diverging as T → 0, so normal strips become Josephson-coupled) assumes a normal metal that proximitizes freely. It fails when the normal region is a different isospin phase, for example a spin- or valley-polarized metal next to a spin-triplet or valley-polarized condensate whose pairing channel cannot leak into it. The authors should test for coexistence directly:
   - forward/backward gate-sweep hysteresis (V_b or D) and B⊥/B∥ sweep hysteresis inside the AM pocket and across the SC/AM boundary;
   - low-frequency or telegraph resistance noise in the AM, compared with the SC and normal regions;
   - quantum oscillations inside or across the AM pocket, to test whether they contain both the degeneracy-two and degeneracy-four frequencies (Extended Data Fig. 8c runs along V_t = 3.20 V and does not cross the V_t ≈ 1.1 V AM pocket of Fig. 1);
   - whether the Hall density in the AM interpolates between the two phases;
   - the AM resistance per square for the three probe pairs of Extended Data Fig. 1, set against their L/W and shown as a table or plot rather than color maps on a shared scale. A homogeneous AM gives a probe-independent ρ; a few weak links or domain walls give resistances that do not scale with segment length.
   Unless these tests come out negative, the statement that the data provide "strong evidence that the SC/AM distinction is not a consequence of inhomogeneity" (p. 9) should be limited to structural inhomogeneity.

3. **The SC/AM critical-field comparison uses mismatched criteria, so it does not show that the paired states differ.** The claims that "the paired states in the two regimes are not identical" and that the AM "is unlikely to be simply a phase-disordered version of the adjacent superconductor" (Fig. 4 and its section, p. 5) rest mainly on B_c⊥ ≈ 4 mT for the SC against ≈ 300 mT for the AM, converted to ξ ≈ 300 nm against 30 nm. The two numbers come from different features. For the SC, 4 mT is the field at which the inner zero-resistance diamond closes (Fig. 4b), where the critical current vanishes. That is the scale at which vortices or phase slips first cause dissipation and global phase coherence is lost, not the scale at which pairing is destroyed. For the AM, the "critical field" can only be where the finite-resistance plateau returns to the normal state. The SC's own resistive transition extends much further. In Fig. 1d (B∥ = 0) the SC pocket stays below R_N up to roughly 50–75 mT, while Fig. 1e shows zero resistance only up to about 10 mT. In Fig. 4c (B∥ = 350 mT) the zero-resistance region ends near 10 mT, but reduced resistance continues to tens of mT, up to about 100 mT on the SC side. The headline values are also taken at B∥ = 350 mT, which itself suppresses the SC. The Methods state that critical fields were defined at 90% of the normal-state resistance, which does not match the inner-diamond reading used in the main text. In addition, applying the Ginzburg–Landau relation Φ0/2πξ² to the outer diamond of a finite-resistance, possibly inhomogeneous state assumes the homogeneity that is in question. If the SC's 90%-R_N field is comparable to the AM's, the difference reduces to "the SC has a finite zero-resistance window and the AM does not", which is exactly what a phase-disordered SC would show. The authors should extract B_c⊥ for both pockets with the same criteria (for example 50% and 90% of R_N), and separately the field at which the dissipationless window closes. These should be taken at matched B∥ (including B∥ = 0) and matched reduced temperature, normalized by the local T_c, and plotted against V_b along a trajectory crossing the SC/AM boundary (Fig. 4c, Fig. 1d and the B∥ = 0 data). The in-plane comparison (Fig. 4d) has the same problem: B∥,c should be given with the same criterion at matched gate points. The definition of "critical field" for the AM should be stated explicitly. ξ_SC, k_Fξ and ξ/ℓ_mf should be recomputed from the matched values, and the "distinct paired states" conclusion, the related Discussion and the list of constraints for theory revised to match.

## B. Should address

4. **There is no positive evidence that the AM involves pairing.** Every signature attributed to the AM is a resistance drop below an onset temperature, together with its suppression by B⊥, B∥ and I_dc. A partial drop (a factor of about 4, Fig. 1b, Fig. 2c) can also come from an ordering transition in the normal state, such as an isospin or nematic reorder or a density wave. The authors' ref. [16] reports charge-density-wave states in rhombohedral graphene, and sliding density waves have threshold currents. The AM pocket also borders the half-metal/unpolarized-metal boundary (Fig. 2a), where Fermi-surface reconstructions are expected. The in-plane critical field of about 3.9 T "exceeding B_P by 16" uses a BCS Pauli formula that presupposes pairing and does not establish it. The adjacency to the SC and the critical-current and critical-field phenomenology make pairing plausible, but positive evidence would strengthen the claim. Options: quantum oscillations inside the AM pocket (B⊥ above the AM critical field, at the same B∥) compared with those just outside, to show the normal-state Fermi surface is the same on both sides of the AM boundary; magnetoresistance oscillations or interference in I_c(B⊥) at small fields; and a comparison of the functional form of the I_dc and B⊥ suppression with superconducting fluctuation conductivity. Without such evidence, the title, abstract and the Discussion's list of constraints should present the AM as a finite-resistance state with superconducting-like phenomenology, and should separate what is measured (finite saturated R, critical scales, non-monotonic I–V) from what is interpreted (quantum fluctuations, distinct paired states).

5. **The double critical current may come from a single superconducting state with two scales.** In Fig. 3a–d the SC pocket shows an inner critical current (onset of dissipation) and an outer one (return to ohmic), with a reduced but dissipative region in between. The paper reads this as an "inner dome associated with the zero-resistance SC and an outer dome associated with the dissipative AM". In a 2D superconductor, a depinning or phase-slip current followed by a larger pair-breaking current is generic, and the intermediate region is then flux-flow or phase-slip dissipation of the same condensate. On this reading the AM pocket is where the depinning current has gone to zero while the pair-breaking scale survives. The smooth evolution from two steps to one as V_b moves toward the AM (Fig. 3a) fits a continuous loss of phase stiffness at least as well as a crossing into a different paired state. The authors should rule this out or present it as a live alternative. Tests: how the inner and outer critical currents and critical fields scale with T and B⊥ (vortex-motion versus depairing forms); whether the intermediate-region resistance scales with B⊥ like flux-flow resistance; and whether the intermediate-state resistance at fixed gate equals the AM plateau resistance at the neighbouring gate.
Raised by: r2

6. **The AM lies in a two-surface normal state, which allows an untested conventional alternative.** Fig. 2b marks "surface polarized" and "dual surface" regions. The SC cut (d) lies in the surface-polarized region and the AM cuts (f, h) in the dual-surface region, but the main text never discusses this. If the AM sits at net negative n while the SC is at positive n (Fig. 1a; see concern 12), that is consistent with compensating carriers on the two surfaces in the AM regime (ref. [6]). Pairing might then occur on one surface while carriers on the opposite surface stay normal. A finite four-terminal resistance could arise from current conversion between the normal and paired layers near the edge-contacted probes, or from partial interlayer coupling. Such a state would show a sharp onset, a T-independent saturation at a fraction of R_N, and well-defined critical currents and fields, without being an intrinsic "Bose metal". The authors should state whether the SC/AM boundary coincides with the surface-polarized/dual-surface boundary. They should show how R_sat/R_N depends on D at fixed n across the AM pocket, and compare R_sat per unit length across probe pairs with different spacing, since a resistance set by current conversion would not scale with length. They should also discuss this scenario in the extrinsic/inhomogeneity section.

7. **Density and displacement-field inhomogeneity is not bounded quantitatively.** The pockets are narrow in gate voltage: the SC spans about 0.07 V in V_b (Figs. 1c, 3a). The two-step temperature dependence in Figs. 2d–e (an intermediate plateau, then zero) and the inner and outer domes in Fig. 3b are what a channel with SC and AM segments in series would show. The authors should estimate δn, for example from the width of the SC/AM boundary in Fig. 2c or from the Landau-level broadening in Extended Data Fig. 8. They should then test the series picture directly: is the intermediate plateau resistance in Fig. 2e equal to a fraction of the AM R_sat at neighbouring V_b, and does it vary between probe pairs? The argument from the 30 nm length scale also depends on the field-scale comparison questioned in concern 3.
Raised by: r1

8. **Residual perpendicular field, trapped flux and flux flow are not excluded.** The Methods note offsets of up to about 10 mT from trapped flux in the magnet coils. B⊥ is zeroed by symmetrizing ρ_xx or maximizing I_c, and the in-plane field is aligned the same way. A 1° misalignment at 350 mT gives about 6 mT perpendicular, which is comparable to the SC zero-resistance field scale (a few mT, Fig. 4b). In an ultraclean, weakly pinned or low-stiffness 2D condensate, a small residual B⊥, inhomogeneous residual flux, or pinned vortices left by earlier sweeps can give a finite flux-flow resistance (ref. [52], which the authors cite). The AM's much larger field scale could leave it in exactly that regime. The authors should state the field history before the zero-field AM data (Fig. 1b, f–h). They should show R_sat of the AM at I_dc = 0 against B⊥ in fine steps through zero, at B∥ = 0 and at 350 mT, for both sweep directions, and state whether R_sat at its minimum is finite. They should also show that the plateau is unchanged after a magnet quench or demagnetization, after warming above the magnet's trapped-flux scale, or between field-cooled and zero-field-cooled runs.
Raised by: r1, r2

9. **The finite Hall signal in the AM is set aside without a test.** ρ_xy "remains finite in the AM pocket ... possibly due to geometric mixing with ρ_xx" and is described as field-independent (p. 2; Extended Data Figs. 2j–l, 11c–f), but no field antisymmetrization is shown. If it is mixing, ρ_xy/ρ_xx should equal the ratio in the adjacent normal state at the same gate voltages. A different ratio, or a field-independent ρ_xy that switches on with the AM transition, would point to non-uniform current flow below T_c (concern 2) or to two-carrier transport (concern 6). The authors should show ρ_xy at ±B⊥, symmetrized and antisymmetrized, across the AM transition. They should compare its onset temperature with that of ρ_xx, and report ρ_xy/ρ_xx in the AM and in the adjacent normal state.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

10. **The non-monotonic current response is not checked against heating, switching or series-element explanations.** The region where moderate I_dc drives dV/dI back to (near) zero before the normal transition (Fig. 3e–g; Extended Data Figs. 4c, 4f–g, 10c) is a central new observation and is presented as a constraint on theory. Simpler explanations are not tested. These include a series combination of regions with different critical currents and resistances, where a resistive region switches to superconducting at finite bias; current-induced switching of an isospin domain; and self-heating. At 11 mK electron–phonon cooling in graphene is extremely weak, so even picowatt dissipation can raise the electron temperature well above the bath, and the dissipated power varies along the I–V curve. Also, the I–V curves (Figs. 3d, 3g; Extended Data Fig. 6) are integrated from lock-in dV/dI with an ac excitation (0.5–1 nA) comparable to the features (a few nA). In the AM, small offsets accumulate in this integration and narrow structure can be distorted. The authors should (a) estimate the electron temperature against I_dc in the AM pocket, for example using the normal-state ρ(T) or the I_c(T) calibration as a thermometer; (b) show up- and down-sweeps of I_dc; (c) state the resolution of the "zero" differential resistance in this region; (d) report V_xy recorded at the same time, which tests for current redistribution; (e) confirm at least one AM trace, including the flat-V region (Fig. 3g) and the power-law exponents (Extended Data Fig. 6f), with a direct dc voltage measurement; and (f) say whether a two-element series model reproduces the integrated I–V curves.

11. **The quoted critical temperatures and field ratios disagree with one another and with the figures.** The main text says SC and AM "share similar maximum critical temperatures (approximately 100 mK)" when adjacent. The Methods give T_c ≈ 55 mK (SC) and ≈ 130 mK (AM) for the Fig. 1 pockets, and Fig. 1b shows onsets near 45 mK and about 110 mK. The Discussion says the AM survives in-plane fields "roughly four times larger" than the SC. The Methods give 3.93 T / 1.39 T ≈ 2.8, while Fig. 4d (V_t = 2.30 V, not the Fig. 1 gate points) suggests about 4. Normalized by T_c, both pockets exceed the Pauli limit by nearly the same factor (≈ 14 and ≈ 16), so on these numbers the in-plane response gives no evidence for distinct pairing. It is unclear at which gate voltages the in-plane critical fields were measured, since Fig. 1 is at B∥ = 0. The "similar T_c" premise also underpins the non-equilibrium argument (concern 1). The authors should specify the gate points and criterion behind each number. They should tabulate T_c (same criterion), B_c⊥, B_c∥, inner and outer I_c, and R_AM/R_N at matched pairs of adjacent SC and AM gate points, at B∥ = 0 and 350 mT, and revise the arguments that rely on these ratios.

12. **The density sign, and whether both pockets lie in the same band, need clarifying.** The Fig. 1 caption places the SC at n = +1.34 × 10¹² cm⁻² and the AM at n = −1.25 × 10¹² cm⁻², and in Fig. 1a the AM dashed line also lies at negative n. Yet the text treats both pockets as conduction-band states that merge under B∥ (Fig. 2b) and share the half-metal/unpolarized-metal boundary. The authors should clarify the sign and any offset in the n, D conversion, including the effect of the +15 V Si gate. If the AM is in fact on the opposite side of neutrality, the statement that SC and AM emerge "from nearly identical external parameters" needs revision, and the two-surface scenario of concern 6 becomes more pressing.

13. **The isospin-phase assignment around the AM pocket rests on limited data.** The claim that the SC borders the half-metal and the AM borders the unpolarized metal comes from Fig. 2a and a single Landau fan at V_t = 3.20 V (Extended Data Fig. 8), which crosses the SC pocket at B∥ = 0. No fan covers the AM pocket of Fig. 1 (V_t ≈ 1.1 V) or the merged region at B∥ = 350 mT, where most of the analysis is done. The authors should add a fan through the AM pocket and across the SC/AM boundary at B∥ = 350 mT, to support both the phase assignment and the "two distinct states" reading of the sharp boundary.
Raised by: r2, r3

14. **The electron temperature in this sample, and hence the range of the saturation, is not established.** The base temperature is quoted as 11–14 mK. Electron temperatures "below 20 mK" (quantum dots) and "down to 14 mK" (nanowires) were measured in other samples, on different wiring. The flat part of ρ(T) below about 20 mK therefore carries no confirmed temperature information, and "T-independent over a factor of five in temperature" should use only the range in which the electron temperature is known. The authors should give a bound for this device and cooldown. For example, they could show that SC pockets with the lowest T_c (≈ 40–50 mK, e.g. the Extended Data Fig. 6d region or the Fig. 2h edge) still reach zero resistance, give the lowest T at which the SC transition or the AM R(T) still changes, and show that the AM saturation onset tracks T_c rather than a fixed temperature. R_sat against T should be shown on a log or semi-log scale down to base temperature for several AM gate points.

15. **The reproducibility evidence is thinner than the abstract's "tunability and reproducibility" suggests.** Only two devices are reported, both eight-layer and both on WSe2, and thermal cycling is not reported. In the second device the AM is not clean. For V_xx2 the AM footprint reads above the surrounding normal state (Extended Data Fig. 11b,c), and ρ_xy does not reliably vanish in the SC. Non-uniform current flow within the AM itself cuts against the paper's own inhomogeneity argument, yet the authors attribute it to sample inhomogeneity without discussion. The claim that the second device reproduces the pockets in "nearly identical regions of the (n, D) phase diagram" is supported only by (V_b, V_t) maps with different gate voltages (Extended Data Figs. 9–11). The role of WSe2 is not addressed: the paper argues the SC lives on the surface away from WSe2, but no device without WSe2 or of different thickness is shown. The authors should convert both devices' maps to common (n, D) axes with the SC and AM boundaries overlaid. They should give a table of T_c, B_c⊥, B∥,c and R_sat/R_N for each pocket, device and probe pair with consistent criteria, and state the number of cooldowns and whether the plateau value and pocket positions survived a thermal cycle. They should also discuss what Extended Data Fig. 11 implies for the AM. Finally, they should either narrow the title and abstract to WSe2-supported eight-layer devices or show the AM in a device without WSe2.

16. **The claim that the fluctuations are quantum is qualitative.** The Discussion says the T-independent saturation "is consistent with a scenario in which the relevant fluctuations are quantum mechanical in origin" and that the excess conductivity is "far larger than standard Aslamazov–Larkin corrections would predict" (p. 6). It also says that standard dissipative models suppress quantum phase fluctuations when R_sq ≪ R_Q. Neither statement is compared quantitatively with the data. The authors should plot the expected AL excess conductance (about e²/16ħ × T/(T − T_c)), and Maki–Thompson given the clean limit, against the measured 1/ρ_AM − 1/ρ_N, using the measured T_c and normal-state conductance. They should give the dimensionless dissipation parameter (R_Q/R_N or the equivalent Caldeira–Leggett parameter) for each pocket. They should also either name an observable that would distinguish quantum fluctuations from a residual extrinsic dephasing rate, or soften the statement.

## C. Minor

17. **The zero/finite distinction needs a stated resolution.** The AM R_sat values are only a few ohms (about 5–10 Ω in Figs. 1b, 2c, 2i), while the noise in the SC trace in the Fig. 1b inset is about 1 Ω. The authors should state the resistance noise floor in Ω after the W/L factor, give an upper bound on the SC resistance, define "below measurement resolution" and R_N, and compare the floor with the smallest AM plateau values (R_AM/R_N ≈ 0.1–0.4).
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

18. **The AM parameter estimates use SC-pocket inputs.** The Methods derive k_F and ℓ_mf from the Landau fan at n = 1.3 × 10¹² cm⁻² (the SC position), assuming a single degeneracy-two band (f_ν = 1/2). They then use these to give k_Fξ ≈ 9 and ξ/ℓ_mf ≈ 0.005 for the AM. But the AM sits at n ≈ −1.25 × 10¹² cm⁻², next to a degeneracy-four (f_ν = 1/4), possibly two-surface metal. The authors should use quantum-oscillation data at the AM gate points and the appropriate degeneracy, or present these numbers only as order-of-magnitude estimates. Also, ℓ_mf ≈ 5.9 μm exceeds the channel width (scale bar in Extended Data Fig. 1a). It is therefore device-limited and should be given as a lower bound, and the ρ = R·W/L conversion and the diffusive picture should be qualified.

19. **The BKT statements are inconsistent.** The main text says the SC "exhibits a standard BKT transition", but the Methods call the BKT analysis (Extended Data Fig. 6) "of questionable validity" for micron-scale samples. With ξ ≈ 300 nm, the sample is only a few coherence lengths wide. The authors should compare T_BKT = 61 mK (from α = 3, Extended Data Fig. 6c) with the resistive T_c at the same gate point and with the finite-size crossover. They should consider a Halperin–Nelson fit of R(T), and either carry the caveat into the main text or present the analysis as illustrative only.

The authors should make the argument more precise or soften it (see also concern 2 for its failure when the normal region is a different isospin phase).
