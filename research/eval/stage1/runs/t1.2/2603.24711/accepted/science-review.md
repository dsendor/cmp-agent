# Concerns

## A. Must address

1. **The d-wave interpretation of the YBCO data is not established, yet it is half of the abstract's conclusion.** The Discussion states that in d-wave superconductors "J_c* is not a well-defined quantity and is therefore not observable", and the abstract says the gradual suppression in YBCO reflects its d-wave symmetry. Several problems undercut this. (i) *Theory:* microscopic treatments of supercurrent-carrying nodal superconductors (nonlinear Meissner effect, d-wave BCS depairing) still give a J(v_s) with a maximum, so a depairing (superflow-instability) current exists; nodal quasiparticles add a gradual low-v_s suppression of n_s on top of it, of order J/J_dep, and do not remove the threshold. The claim goes beyond the theory, and SI section 11.4 is only a qualitative tDGL treatment. (ii) *Currents reached:* with λ ≈ 150–250 nm and ξ ≈ 1.5–2 nm, a Ginzburg–Landau estimate gives a YBCO depairing current of a few thousand GA m⁻² at low temperature and several hundred at T/T_c ≈ 0.6, i.e. of order 10³ GA m⁻². Fig. 4d reaches only J_T ≈ 200–230 GA m⁻² (Fig. 4c roughly 3–8 J_c), and the high-current data in Fig. 4c have not reached the normal-state reference. The experiment may simply not reach J_c*, and the authors must show quantitatively that a nodal effect can drop E_T from 0.8 to near the normal level at these currents. (iii) *Temperature mismatch:* YBCO is shown at a single temperature, 50 K (T/T_c ≈ 0.59), while the sharp NbN response is at 7 K (T/T_c ≈ 0.47); thermal smearing broadens the transition at higher T/T_c even in an s-wave film. (iv) *Extrinsic routes not excluded:* twin boundaries, low-angle grain boundaries and weak links in commercial c-axis films on CeO2-buffered sapphire; T_c inhomogeneity (the d.c. R(J) curve in Fig. 2b is already gradual); vortex entry, since λ is larger and the edge barrier weaker in YBCO; and current crowding at the edges, since a 50 nm film has a Pearl length 2λ²/d of roughly 1–2.5 µm, much smaller than the 10 µm width, so the edges reach depairing well before the average current does. The word "monocrystalline" (p. 1814) has no structural support. (v) *Mismatched normal-state reference:* the reference is taken at 110 K, but YBCO's normal-state resistivity is roughly linear in T, so a normal strip at 50 K would have lower resistance and higher transmission; saying the 50 K curve "converges to the value characteristic of the normal state" compares unlike states. (vi) The YBCO device and calibration details are only in SI section 6, so the reader cannot check that the geometry matches the NbN device. The authors should: give an explicit J_c*(50 K) estimate for YBCO from independently measured λ and ξ and compare it with the maximum current reached; measure YBCO at lower temperature (e.g. 10–20 K), where the nodal contribution would dominate, and show NbN at matched reduced temperature (T/T_c ≈ 0.6–0.8; the SI section 7 data at 0.67 and 0.83 appear to exist) next to the YBCO curve, ideally on a common J/J_dep(T) axis; estimate R_n(50 K) and the expected fully depaired transmission level; characterize twinning and grain structure (XRD rocking curves, φ-scans, twin domains); model the current distribution across the strip; and either support the d-wave attribution with a d-wave microscopic calculation of the transmission or reduce the YBCO statement to an observation and soften the claim that J_c* is ill-defined in d-wave superconductors.

2. **The absolute current-density scale, on which both the "2.2 × J_c" headline and the agreement in Fig. 5a rest, is not validated and carries no uncertainty.** J is obtained from E(t)·w = S·J(t)·Z0 (p. 1812), with Z0 ≈ 50 Ω, S from a nominal "approximately 20 nm" thickness and ≈10 µm width, and the absolute field from a photoconductive-sampling calibration described only in SI section 5. The J_c*/J_c ratio combines this optoelectronic calibration with an entirely separate d.c. calibration, and the comparison with the calculated J_c*(T) is absolute, so every uncertainty enters directly. Specific issues: the sampling switch has a finite response time, so the measured peak of a ~2 ps pulse is a convolution and the true peak current in the strip is probably higher than J_T,peak; whether the absolute switch response is known to better than a factor of ~2 is not stated; Z0 was not measured or simulated for the loaded line; the impedance discontinuity at the film section and the ~3 Ω contacts may change the relation between transmitted field and current in the film; thickness and strip dimensions are approximate (Methods gives ~20 nm and ~50 nm). Fig. 5a has no error bars on either J_c or J_c*, so "slightly smaller" and "excellent agreement" cannot be judged. The authors should give the full calibration chain with an uncertainty budget; state the bandwidth of the sampling scheme and deconvolve or bound its effect on peak values; measure or simulate Z0 for the actual structure; measure the film thickness (XRR, TEM or AFM) and give exact strip dimensions for each device; add error bars to Figs. 4b and 5a; and cross-check the absolute scale independently — for example, the d.c. normal-state resistance in Fig. 2a (~140–150 Ω) predicts a transmission T = 2Z0/(2Z0+R) ≈ 0.42, which should be compared quantitatively with the ~0.4 level of the 20 K data in Figs. 3b and 4a, together with the reflection amplitude; or long (ns) pulses should recover the d.c. J_c.

3. **The vortex-exclusion argument ignores the pulse's self-field and edge current crowding, and the reported J_c* is an average over a non-uniform current profile.** The Discussion rules out vortices by zero-field cooling below 1 µT and by a maximum vortex displacement of "a few tens of nanometres" at speeds of a few tens of km s⁻¹ (pp. 1813–1814). At threshold the pulse carries J ≈ 220 GA m⁻² through 10 µm × 20 nm, i.e. I ≈ 44 mA. In a thin strip this produces a large self-field concentrated at the edges, likely well above H_c1, and the local edge current density exceeds the average J = I/S used throughout. Even for NbN the Pearl length (λ ≈ 400 nm gives ~16 µm) is comparable to the width, and at the frequencies in a ~2 ps pulse the current need not be uniform. Vortex entry over the edge barrier is set by the local edge current density, not the ambient field, so zero-field cooling does not exclude pulse-induced entry. Vortices need move only ~ξ to tens of nm to create a dissipative edge layer, and fast vortex chains, kinematic vortices and phase-slip lines (Larkin–Ovchinnikov-type instability, refs. 7, 17, 23) can move faster than assumed and are known to give fast resistive responses in NbN. Edge crowding would also lower the apparent J_c* and localize depairing at the edges. The authors should compute the transverse current distribution at the relevant frequencies (Pearl length at 7 K, edge-to-centre ratio) and state whether J_c* is an average or local value; state the edge self-field at J_c* and compare the edge current density with the Bean–Livingston/edge-barrier entry criterion; give a quantitative upper bound on the voltage edge-vortex motion over ~2 ps could produce, compared with the ~140 Ω normal-state response; and ideally (a) measure strips of several widths (e.g. 1, 3, 10 µm, including w below the Pearl length) — an intrinsic J_c* should become width-independent while an edge-entry threshold would scale differently — and (b) measure under a known perpendicular field or after field cooling, where the picosecond threshold should be nearly field-insensitive while d.c. J_c is strongly suppressed.

## B. Should address

4. **The threshold has not been separated from within-pulse heating or direct pair breaking by the pulse's high-frequency content.** The identification rests on one pulse width (~2 ps), the vortex argument and agreement with a model. An abrupt drop in transmitted amplitude can also come from runaway heating seeded at the leading edge (hotspot or thermal instability); NbN is the archetypal fast-hotspot material, with electron–phonon times ~10 ps and nonequilibrium quasiparticles forming in a few ps. A 2 ps pulse has spectral weight to ~0.5–1 THz, while 2Δ(0) ≈ 4.5 meV ≈ 1.1 THz for T_c ≈ 15 K and is smaller at 7 K, and pair breaking by intense THz fields in NbN is known (ref. 10). The threshold's sharpness and its temperature dependence relative to d.c. J_c argue against a trivial thermal origin, but no direct test is given. The authors should show the pulse spectrum relative to 2Δ(T); measure the threshold as a function of pulse width or rise time (at least two widths) — an intrinsic threshold should be duration-independent over a picosecond window and cross over to d.c. J_c only on vortex/thermal timescales, while heating scales with deposited energy; estimate the energy deposited per pulse and the electron temperature in the 20 nm film over the 2.2–6.5 J_c range; and ideally perform a strong-pulse/weak-probe experiment at variable delay, whose recovery timescale would distinguish a nonthermal depaired state (pair breaking plus recombination) from a hot normal state cooling into the substrate. Heating after depairing probably shapes the post-threshold tail that the BCS simulation misses; this should be discussed, and electron–phonon heating considered in the model.

5. **The analytic J_c* estimate uses a clean-limit criterion for a dirty-limit superconductor, and its inputs are unspecified.** The Fig. 5a calculation (p. 1814) uses the Landau criterion ħk_F·v_s = Δ with ξ = ħv_F/(πΔ), giving J_c* = 2ħe n_s/(πmξ). But NbN films 20 nm thick are strongly disordered, the measured ξ (~5 nm, from H_c2) is the dirty GL length, and the paper's own simulation uses the dirty-limit Usadel equation. In the dirty limit depairing is governed by the Kupriyanov–Lukichev pair-breaking parameter, with different prefactor and temperature dependence, so the Discussion's statement that the threshold "originates from the competition between quasiparticle energy shift and the superconducting gap" does not follow from the model that reproduces the data. The authors should state which n_s(T), ξ(T) and m were used (SI section 9), whether they were measured independently or adjusted, and how many free parameters there are against the four J_c* points; compare the data with the parameter-free Kupriyanov–Lukichev J_dep(T) from measured ρ_n, T_c, diffusion constant, λ(T) and H_c2(T); quantify the discrepancy near 0.8 T_c (roughly 20–30% in Fig. 5a) instead of calling it "slightly smaller"; and reconcile the physical picture in the Discussion with the dirty-limit model.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

6. **The Usadel simulation is partly tuned, and its comparison with tDGL is not a fair discriminant.** The Dynes broadening is "set to guarantee numerical convergence and reproduce the experimental results" (p. 1814), making it effectively a fit parameter for the shape in Fig. 5b, and the simulation misses the high-current tail. tDGL is not valid at T/T_c ≈ 0.47, so its failure does not by itself confirm the BCS picture. The authors should report the Dynes Γ and compare it with tunnelling or optical estimates for NbN; show how sensitive the threshold position and sharpness are to Γ; list the free parameters and state which features of Fig. 5b are predictions and which fits, including whether the absolute J_c* is predicted or only the shape on a J/J_c axis; and give tDGL the same freedom, or base the claim that the sharpness is an s-wave dirty-limit fingerprint on a prediction a plausible alternative fails to reproduce.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

7. **The full time-domain waveforms are not used to separate inductive from resistive response.** The analysis relies on peak amplitudes, and Fig. 3c shows waveforms only at 0.2 J_c and 6.5 J_c. As J approaches J_c*, the nonlinear kinetic inductance rises, which alone increases reflection and lowers the transmitted peak without dissipation; the 7 K reflected pulse already has an inductive shape (Fig. 3b). The incident, reflected and transmitted waveforms contain the time-dependent complex impedance. The authors should show waveforms through the transition (1.5–3 J_c) to reveal when in the pulse the change happens and whether it starts inductive and becomes resistive; extract L_kin(J), hence n_s(J), and R(J) below and above J_c* for both materials; and show that a resistive component appears at J_c*. Comparing the measured current-induced suppression of n_s below threshold with the Usadel prediction would be a largely parameter-free test of depairing, and in YBCO would separate nodal (∝ J) suppression from weak-link behaviour.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **How J_c* is extracted from the fold-back in Fig. 4 is unclear.** Fig. 4a uses J_T,peak as the abscissa; because J_T drops at the transition, the curve becomes multivalued (the "slight leftward tilt"), and the local maximum in J_T,peak (Fig. 4b inset) means the transmitted peak falls as drive increases. If depairing occurs when J reaches J_c* on the rising edge, the transmitted peak depends on when in the pulse the collapse occurs, so the quoted J_c* may depend on rise time and on the definition. The authors should state how J_c* is defined (maximum of J_T,peak, onset of the drop, or other) and how it is defined at higher temperatures where the maximum may be less pronounced; plot against J_IN,peak alongside, or against an estimated in-strip current that accounts for reflection; mark in Fig. 4b the J_c* point used in Fig. 5a with a numerical value and uncertainty; and optionally sketch the transmitted-versus-incoming relation with the multivalued region marked.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

9. **Reproducibility across devices is not documented.** The paper does not say how many NbN and YBCO devices were measured, whether the ≈2.2 J_c ratio and the YBCO gradual response reproduce across devices, cooldowns and thermal cycling, or what the spread is; J_c*(T) rests on four points without error bars. It is also unclear whether the d.c. J_c in Fig. 2 comes from the same strips used for the picosecond measurements, which the ratio requires. The authors should report J_c and J_c* (and their ratio) for two to three devices per material, including a second YBCO device, state whether d.c. and pulsed data come from the same strip, and add J_c* points closer to T_c, where clean- and dirty-limit predictions differ most.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

10. **The effective sample temperature and stray-light exposure under illumination are not established.** The 515 nm beams illuminate switches close to the strip (Fig. 3a); scattered light could create quasiparticles in the film, and substrate heating from the 50 kHz train (chopped at 1 kHz) could raise the effective temperature. Comparing J_c with J_c* at a common temperature assumes this is negligible. The authors should determine the sample temperature with beams on and off (e.g. from the kinetic-inductance response or d.c. R(T)), add a control with the launching beam on and switch bias off to rule out optical quasiparticle injection, and show that the threshold does not depend on repetition rate or average laser power.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

11. **Prior depairing measurements are not placed in context.** Depairing-limited d.c. critical currents have been reached in narrow strips and nanowires (w below the Pearl length, ξ-scale constrictions; e.g. ref. 14 and related work), and pulsed and microwave measurements near J_d exist. The statement that "direct transport measurements of J_c* remain challenging" should be set against this work, with a clear account of what the picosecond technique adds (wide films, no nanopatterning).
