A. Must address

1. **Interfacial charge transfer is not ruled out, and the paper does not engage with Ref. 29.** Ref. 29 (Nelson et al., Sci. Adv. 2022) studied ultrathin SrIrO3/SrRuO3 heterostructures, essentially this system, reported persistent metallicity and attributed it to interfacial charge transfer between Ir and Ru. The manuscript cites Ref. 29 only in passing, as background for thickness-dependent transitions (p. 4), and never discusses it as an alternative. A fraction of an electron transferred across the interface dopes both layers away from half filling of the Jeff = 1/2 (Ir) and t2g^4 (Ru) manifolds. That alone can close Mott/Slater gaps and stabilise itinerant ferromagnetism in SRO, with no role for DMI. On this reading the ferromagnetism follows from the metallicity rather than causing it, so the title and abstract ("driven by interface-induced magnetic reconstruction"; DMI as "the key driving force") do not follow from the data. The authors should: (i) report layer-resolved charges (Bader or d occupancies) and moments from their DFT, for the bilayer and the isolated films, with and without the magnetic reconstruction; (ii) compute the heterostructure DOS with charge transfer present but the magnetic order held at the single-film configurations (nc-AFM SIO + c-AFM SRO); (iii) measure the Ir and Ru valence, for example by Ir L-edge and Ru L/M-edge XAS or by Ir 4f and Ru 3d core-level photoemission in the in-situ setup they already use; Hall data giving carrier type and density would also help; (iv) state explicitly what is new relative to Ref. 29 and why DMI, rather than doping, is the driver.

2. **The bilayers are metallic far above the magnetic ordering temperature, which conflicts with the claimed causal direction.** Fig. 2A shows all three heterostructures with ρ ≈ 0.2–0.45 mΩ cm and positive dρ/dT up to 300 K, about four orders of magnitude below the 2-u.c. single films. The text places the ferromagnetic transition at the kinks at 102–123 K. Between roughly 120 K and 300 K the bilayers are therefore metallic while paramagnetic. This is hard to reconcile with the central claim (Sec. A: "The emergence of metallicity … is therefore a direct outcome of the DMI-driven magnetic reconstruction"; Discussion: "DMI dominates … thereby driving magnetic reconstruction and the emergence of metallicity"). It is equally consistent with the reverse causality: the bilayer is metallic for another reason, and SRO then orders as an itinerant ferromagnet, as thicker SRO does. The authors should address this directly, either by showing that short-range or fluctuating order above Tc suffices (with evidence such as M(T) or magnetoresistance above Tc), or by showing in DFT that the heterostructure would be insulating in a paramagnetic, disordered-moment or nonmagnetic configuration. Otherwise the title claim should be rephrased, for example as "emergent ferromagnetic metallicity in ultrathin SIO/SRO bilayers, consistent with an interfacial magnetic reconstruction".

3. **The DFT does not show that the bilayer is metallic only in the reconstructed magnetic state.** The Introduction (p. 4) states that "electronic states at the Fermi level emerge exclusively when ferromagnetism is induced by interface-driven magnetic reconstruction". But Sec. D compares only the single films (c-AFM SRO, nc-AFM SIO) with the heterostructures in the reconstructed state (c-AFM SIO + FM SRO), and Fig. 4C,D show only that case. No gapped AFM heterostructure DOS is shown. The decisive calculation is absent: the same SIO/SRO slab, at the same structure, constrained to the unreconstructed order (nc-AFM SIO / c-AFM SRO), to other AFM SRO arrangements (c-AFM SIO / c-AFM SRO), to FM/FM and to nonmagnetic configurations, with the DOS and total energy of each. These energies are also needed to show that the proposed c-AFM/FM state is the ground state. A further control is whether the gap survives when SOC is switched off at fixed structure, or when the DMI-active octahedral rotations are suppressed. Without this, the metallic DOS in Fig. 4C,D could simply reflect band alignment and charge transfer in a thicker slab: it shows that the reconstructed slab is metallic, not that the reconstruction makes it metallic.

4. **DMI is asserted, not computed or isolated, as the driving interaction.** The "effective model Hamiltonian" is invoked several times (pp. 4, 6, 7) but described only in words. The manuscript gives no Hamiltonian, no exchange constants, no DMI vector magnitude or direction for the Ru–Ir bonds (the text says only that D lies "along a symmetry axis within the interface plane"), no single-ion anisotropy, and no account of how the parameters were obtained (fitted to DFT energies, or assumed). DMI is a relativistic correction usually a few percent of the symmetric Heisenberg exchange; converting AFM SRO into FM SRO by DMI alone would need |D| comparable to J, and this must be shown. The Discussion also invokes anisotropic Ru–Ir coupling and magnetocrystalline anisotropy without quantifying either. The authors should write out the model, tabulate J, D and K extracted from DFT energies of the competing configurations (concern 3), give the model's magnetic phase diagram versus D/J, and show a calculation with DMI switched off (D = 0 in the model, or SOC removed on the interface bonds) in which the FM metallic state disappears.

5. **Total thickness and the SRO critical thickness are confounded with the interface effect.** The insulating references are 2 u.c., while the bilayers contain 3 u.c. (2SIO/1SRO, 1SIO/2SRO) or 4 u.c. (2SIO/2SRO) of conducting perovskite. The manuscript itself places the critical thickness at about 3 u.c., and the literature places the metal/ferromagnet threshold of SRO on STO at about 2–5 u.c., depending on growth, termination and capping (Refs. 25, 26, 28). The authors' own Fig. 3 discussion attributes the trends to "increased total thickness", and the rise of conductivity with SRO content (Fig. 2A, Sec. C) fits this reading too. The needed controls are 3- and 4-u.c. single SRO and SIO films, grown, capped and annealed identically and measured by transport, SQUID (including M(T)) and ARPES. Ideally the authors should also measure thickness-matched structures without the Ir–Ru interface: a bilayer with a non-magnetic spacer (for example 2SIO/1 u.c. STO/2SRO), or 2SRO capped with a non-magnetic, weak-SOC perovskite such as SrTiO3, SrHfO3 or SrZrO3 in place of SIO (and similarly SrSnO3/SIO). If such a structure is metallic, the role of SIO-specific DMI is called into question. Without these controls the IMT cannot be attributed to the interface, still less to its magnetism.

6. **There is no structural or chemical characterisation of the bilayers or their interface.** No XRD/XRR, reciprocal space map, STEM/EELS, termination analysis, RHEED oscillations or LEED patterns are shown, although RHEED and LEED are mentioned in Methods. Ir/Ru intermixing over 1–2 u.c. would produce a SrRu1−xIrxO3 alloy, which is metallic, and can be ferromagnetic, over a wide composition range; that would explain the results without any magnetic reconstruction. Ru volatility (RuO4 loss) also affects the stoichiometry of ultrathin SRO, and the actual layer counts are not established. The DMI argument further depends on a specific octahedral tilt and rotation pattern across the interface, and the DFT was relaxed with a fixed STO in-plane constant and SrO termination, neither verified for the real samples. The authors should show RHEED oscillations for each layer, the LEED patterns (including half-order spots revealing the rotation pattern), XRD establishing thickness and coherency, and an atomically resolved HAADF-STEM cross-section with EELS line profiles of Ir and Ru for at least one bilayer. They should also state the termination of each interface.

B. Should address

7. **The magnetic state of each layer, and of the reference films, is not measured.** The mechanism specifies nc-AFM SIO and c-AFM SRO in the single films and c-AFM SIO plus FM SRO (with Ir and Ru spins at 90°) in the bilayers (Fig. 1). Experimentally there is only a net M–H loop of each whole bilayer (Fig. 2B). No magnetisation data are shown for the 2SIO and 2SRO references; their antiferromagnetism is taken from DFT and literature, and for 2-u.c. SRO this is debated (Ref. 25 reports loss of ferromagnetism; AFM order rests largely on a DFT prediction, Ref. 24). The "antiferromagnetic insulator → ferromagnetic metal" contrast therefore rests on an assumption for both reference films. At minimum the authors should measure M–H and M(T) of the 2SIO and 2SRO films under identical conditions, including the STO cap. Element-specific XMCD at the Ir L2,3 and Ru L/M edges on the bilayers and the single films would test whether Ir carries a net moment or is compensated, and whether the Ru moment is out of plane. Linear dichroism or resonant scattering would probe the SIO AFM component and any canting or chirality.

8. **The ferromagnetism data for the bilayers need stronger support.** Only 10-K loops in a single, unstated field orientation are shown (Fig. 2B). The moments, 0.6 μB/Ru for 2SIO/1SRO and 1 μB/Ru for the others (p. 5), approach bulk SRO, although SRO this thin on STO is normally non-ferromagnetic. The signal from a 1–2 u.c. layer on a ~0.5 mm STO substrate is near the SQUID floor and sits on a large diamagnetic background plus the 15-u.c. STO cap. Normalising per Ru also assumes Ir carries no net moment, which contradicts the paper's own model of reorganised and canted Ir spins. The authors should: describe the diamagnetic and paramagnetic background subtraction and how sample area/volume was determined; show raw data and a bare, processed (capped and annealed) STO substrate as a null control; give the field orientation and add in-plane loops to support the claimed out-of-plane easy axis; show M(T) or remanence versus T to tie Tc to the ρ(T) kinks at 102–123 K; and compare the measured moments with the DFT moments. Anomalous Hall loops tracking Fig. 2B would show that the ferromagnetism resides in the conducting channel.

9. **The transport/SQUID samples and the ARPES samples are not equivalent.** Transport and SQUID samples carry a 15-u.c. STO cap deposited before the 1-mbar anneal and are measured ex situ; ARPES samples are uncapped and measured in situ (Methods, pp. 8–9). A polar or strain-imposing cap changes octahedral rotations, termination and the top-surface symmetry, on which the DMI mechanism depends, and can itself change the ground state of a 1–2 u.c. layer. It is also not stated whether the 2SIO and 2SRO references in Fig. 2A were capped and annealed identically, which matters both for the comparison and for excluding substrate conduction from oxygen vacancies formed at 650 °C in 0.15 mbar O2. The DFT slabs (SrO-terminated, with vacuum) model neither the cap nor the exposed SIO surface. The authors should give the full history (growth, capping, annealing) of every sample, state the stacking order, and ideally show that capped and uncapped bilayers give consistent results, or that capped reference films remain insulating.

10. **The layer and orbital origin of the ARPES states is not identified.** At hν = 82 eV the probing depth is ≲1 nm, so in the SIO-on-top bilayers the spectra in Figs. 2C,D and 3 are dominated by the top SIO layer and one interface. ARPES thus shows that the SIO surface is metallic, not the state of the SRO layer, and the assignment of the X-point hole pocket to "metallized SIO" (p. 4) needs support. The authors should compare the measured Fermi surface and dispersions with DFT band structures of each slab, unfolded onto the same Brillouin zone with the octahedral-rotation supercell, for the proposed c-AFM/FM configuration and the competing ones; at present the theory gives only DOS and no quantitative comparison is made. Photon-energy dependence (near the Ir 5d or Ru 4d Cooper minima), resonant photoemission or polarisation dependence would separate Ir from Ru character. A bilayer with reversed stacking (SRO on top) would also help.

11. **The DFT parameter choices are not justified and their robustness is not tested.** Methods use U = 5 eV and JH = 0.15U on Ru, Ir and Ti (written as "Ti 3d orbitals" for all three; Ru is 4d and Ir is 5d). This U is large for 4d and 5d oxides, where about 1–3 eV is typical, and it is the choice most likely to open gaps in the films and favour particular magnetic states, so the gaps and ground states in Fig. 4A,B may be artefacts of U. The text (p. 4) says a range of U_Ir and U_Ru was studied, but no results are shown. Relaxations were done without SOC, although the DMI argument relies on octahedral distortions in a strong-SOC system. Slabs are said to span 2–4 u.c., but Fig. 4 shows only 3-u.c. bilayers; 2SIO/2SRO, the sample with the clearest ARPES, is not calculated. The authors should show the magnetic ground state and gap of the films and bilayers versus U_Ir and U_Ru, demonstrate that "insulating film, metallic bilayer" survives at conventional U, check that relaxation with SOC does not change the tilts, and add the 2SIO/2SRO slab.

12. **The effective-mass analysis contradicts the conclusion drawn from it (p. 6, Fig. 3D).** The text says m* "decreases from 0.57 me for 2SIO/2SRO to 0.5 me and 0.41 me for 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/1SRO, further confirming the enhanced metallicity with increased total thickness". But the thickest and most conducting sample has the heaviest mass, and 2SIO/1SRO, the most resistive in Fig. 2A, the lightest; a larger m* usually signals stronger correlation, so the trend runs opposite to the claim. The band is hole-like, yet m* is called an "electron effective mass". The parabolic fits extrapolate a band maximum about 0.1 eV above EF from MDC peaks spanning roughly −0.05 to −0.22 eV, so the masses depend strongly on the fit constraints, and the differences between 0.41, 0.50 and 0.57 me may be within error. The authors should give fit ranges, free parameters and uncertainties, compare with the DFT band mass (mass renormalisation), reconcile the m* trend with the transport and coherent-weight trends, and otherwise revise or remove the claim.

13. **The magnetic configuration is described inconsistently.** The Fig. 1 caption and Sec. A describe DMI producing c-AFM (or collinear) SIO and FM SRO; Sec. D says the simplest model "predicts one layer with collinear FM order and another with c-AFM order", with Ru and Ir spins at 90°, while also saying the staggered DMI "favors a noncollinear magnetic phase between SIO and SRO"; the Fig. 4 caption says DMI "stabilizes non-collinear spin orientation between Ir and Ru"; the Discussion invokes "anisotropic Ru-Ir coupling, exhibiting AFM coupling along the out-of-plane direction" and says Ir magnetocrystalline anisotropy favours out-of-plane spins, yet Fig. 1E shows Ir spins in plane and the Fig. 4 insets differ again. The SQUID out-of-plane easy axis is attributed to SRO without discussing the Ir sublattice. The authors should give one explicit computed ground state (directions, moments and canting angles of each ion, layer by layer), make the figures, captions and text consistent with it, and check it against the measured anisotropy and net moment.

14. **The transport analysis needs clarification.** The bilayer resistivities (~0.2–0.4 mΩ cm) are comparable to thick SRO films, remarkable for 3–4 u.c. The authors should state which thickness was used to convert sheet resistance to ρ (full bilayer or one layer), show sheet resistance (Ω/□) compared with h/e², state whether the van der Pauw contacts reach both layers through the STO cap, and state explicitly how conduction through the substrate or cap is excluded. The kinks at 102–123 K are not visible on the plotted scale: dρ/dT should be shown, with the kink temperature for each sample correlated with M(T). The 2SIO/1SRO curve has a resistivity minimum and upturn below ~30–40 K that should be discussed (weak localisation or Kondo-like behaviour versus re-entrant insulating behaviour), since it bears on whether that bilayer is truly metallic. The temperatures at which the 2SRO (~60 K) and 2SIO (~70 K) curves leave the measurement range should be stated.

15. **The DOS-based claims in Fig. 4 do not match the text, and DOS is equated with conductivity.** Fig. 4B (2SRO) shows a large dxy peak just below EF with weight running up to about zero, so the "clear gap" (p. 7) is not evident on the plotted scale. Fig. 4D shows almost no SIO weight near EF in the 1SIO layer. The statement that "the calculations indicate higher conductivity in the 1SIO/2SRO heterostructure compared to 2SIO/1SRO" (p. 7) rests on DOS alone, which does not determine conductivity. The authors should give gap values, total and layer-resolved DOS (and N(EF)) on a scale where a gap or its absence is visible, and either compute a transport quantity (Fermi velocities, Drude weight, Boltzmann estimate) or limit the statement to DOS at EF.

16. **The ARPES spectra in Fig. 2C,D lack normalisation and a Fermi-edge reference.** The claim of suppressed spectral weight and a gap in the single films rests on these spectra, but the normalisation (integrated area, O 2p peak, photon flux) is not stated, and no gold or metal Fermi-edge reference is given. Insulating 2-u.c. films measured at 10 K can charge, which shifts and suppresses spectra. The authors should state the normalisation, show Fermi-edge fits with EF referenced to a metal in contact, and quantify the gap or leading-edge shift for each film.
