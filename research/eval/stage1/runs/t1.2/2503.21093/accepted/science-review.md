A. Must address

1. **Metallicity persists far above the Curie temperature, which contradicts the central causal claim.** The abstract, the Introduction and the Fig. 1 caption state that metallicity is "a direct consequence of this DMI-associated magnetic reconstruction" and that "metallic spectral weight appears at the Fermi level only when ferromagnetism is induced". Yet in Fig. 2A all three heterostructures are metallic (dρ/dT > 0, ρ ≈ 0.2–0.4 mΩ cm) from 10 K up to 300 K, while the ferromagnetic transition is placed at about 102–123 K (kinks in ρ(T); M(T) in SI Fig. S3A). Between ~120 K and 300 K the bilayers are paramagnetic but still metallic, so their conductivity cannot be a consequence of long-range ferromagnetic order. The authors must reconcile this. Either the metallicity comes from the interface itself (hybridization, charge transfer or a thickness effect) and ferromagnetism coexists with it, in which case the abstract, Fig. 1 caption and Discussion should be reframed as concurrent metallicity and magnetism rather than cause and effect; or a short-range or fluctuating magnetic mechanism must be argued explicitly. To keep the causal claim they should show that the electronic structure changes at T_C: temperature-dependent ARPES (EDCs at X, f_coh, spectral weight at E_F, m*) above and below T_C (e.g. at 150–200 K), together with ρ(T) and dρ/dT across T_C. If the coherent weight at E_F survives above T_C, the magnetism-first mechanism is not supported. They should also explain why the bilayers remain metallic at 300 K.

2. **No thickness or spacer controls, so the metallicity cannot be assigned to the SIO/SRO interface.** The paper states (Results, p. 2) that SIO and SRO films are insulating "below a critical thickness of 3 u.c.", yet the heterostructures are 3 u.c. (2SIO/1SRO, 1SIO/2SRO) and 4 u.c. (2SIO/2SRO) thick in total, and the authors themselves attribute enhanced metallicity to "increased total thickness" (p. 3). The obvious alternative is that a 3–4 u.c. slab of two bulk-metallic perovskites simply crosses the dimensional-confinement threshold for metallicity and ferromagnetism in SRO (Refs. 20, 33, 34), or that SIO acts as a buffer or cap that relieves the SRO surface/interface dead layer, with no special interfacial magnetism needed. Fig. 2A supports this reading: 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/2SRO have similar, lowest resistivities, and conductivity rises mainly with SRO thickness. The authors should show ρ(T), M(H) and ARPES for single SRO and SIO films of 3 and 4 u.c. grown, capped and contacted identically, and a control bilayer in which SIO is replaced by a non-SOC, non-magnetic perovskite of similar thickness and octahedral connectivity (e.g. 2 u.c. SrTiO3 or SrSnO3 on 1–2 u.c. SRO). Only if the single films stay insulating and the spacer control differs from the SIO/SRO bilayers does the result point to an interface-specific mechanism.

3. **Intermixing and charge transfer are not excluded experimentally.** The Discussion (p. 4) concedes that "we cannot completely rule out the possibility of elemental interdiffusion", and dismisses the charge-transfer and Ir–Ru hybridization picture of Nelson et al. (Ref. 26) by citing a related study (Ref. 31) rather than data on these samples. RHEED oscillations (SI Figs. S1, S2) monitor growth rate, not chemical abruptness. In 1–2 u.c. layers even partial Ir/Ru intermixing produces SrIr1−xRuxO3, which is metallic or ferromagnetic over a wide composition range and could by itself account for the metallicity, the ferromagnetic hysteresis and a T_C near 100 K; the 1-u.c. SRO sample is the most vulnerable. Ir→Ru charge transfer could likewise create states at E_F. The authors should provide atomically resolved cross-sectional HAADF-STEM with EELS/EDS chemical profiles across the interface of at least one heterostructure (or at minimum XRD/XRR for layer thickness and sharpness), and a valence probe comparing bilayers with single films: core-level XPS (Ir 4f, Ru 3d), possibly in the same ARPES system, or XAS at the Ir L and Ru M/L edges. Without these, the step from "metal" to "DMI-driven magnetic reconstruction" is not supported.

B. Should address

4. **The proposed magnetic configuration and the DMI are not observed; they come only from calculations.** The specific state (FM Ru, c-AFM Ir, ~90° Ir–Ru canting set by staggered DMI) and the nc-AFM-to-c-AFM reorientation in SIO rest entirely on theory; the paper itself refers to the "insulating antiferromagnetic nature of the individual ultrathin films inferred from the first-principles calculations" (p. 3). SQUID magnetometry measures only the net moment, and Fig. 2B normalizes it per Ru on the assumption that Ir contributes nothing, even though the proposed scenario allows an Ir moment. No measurement shows antiferromagnetism in the 2-u.c. single films, collinear AFM in SIO within the bilayer, or which ion carries the ferromagnetic moment. The authors should add element-specific XMCD at the Ir L2,3 and Ru edges, XMLD or resonant x-ray scattering for the AFM and canted components, or a magneto-optical probe; whether Ir couples to Ru (canted, or antiparallel as often seen at SRO/SIO interfaces) would directly test the configuration. Any signature of DMI (chirality-sensitive transport, a topological-Hall-like contribution, field-angle dependence) would help. Absent such data, the title, abstract and Fig. 1 should present the DMI scenario as a model-based hypothesis consistent with, not demonstrated by, the experiment; the final paragraph of the Discussion partly does this already.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

5. **The calculations do not test whether metallicity requires the reconstructed magnetic state, and the DMI is not quantified.** Fig. 4 compares the isolated films in their own ground states (nc-AFM SIO, c-AFM SRO) with the bilayers in the reconstructed state (c-AFM SIO / FM SRO), so structure, hybridization, interface termination and magnetic order all change at once. The decisive counterfactual is missing: the bilayer DOS, in the same geometry, with the magnetic order constrained to the single-film orders (nc-AFM SIO + c-AFM SRO), with no magnetic order, and with SOC (and hence DMI) switched off. If the bilayer is metallic in those configurations too, the metallicity comes from interfacial hybridization or charge transfer, not from magnetic reconstruction. The authors should show these DOS in the main text together with the total-energy differences between candidate configurations with and without SOC. They should also explain how the staggered DMI enters the LSDA+U+SOC result (where it is only implicit) versus the model Hamiltonian, and give the model parameters (Heisenberg exchange J, DMI magnitude D, anisotropy) and their origin (fitted to DFT total energies or chosen). Since DMI is normally a small SOC-order correction that favors canting, the claim that it makes each layer more collinear and drives an AFM-to-FM transition needs an explicit comparison of D with the Ru–Ru and Ir–Ir exchange.

6. **The DFT setup may decide the insulating-versus-metallic outcome, and its robustness is not shown.** Methods use U = 5 eV and J_H = 0.15U for Ru, Ir and Ti alike. This is large for 5d Ir (typically ~1–2.5 eV) and for 4d Ru (~1–3 eV), and a large U can by itself make 2-u.c. slabs insulating, so the "insulating constituents" baseline of the theory may be an artifact. The text says ground states were explored "for a range of electron correlation strengths (U_Ir and U_Ru)" (p. 2), but no such results appear in the main paper. The authors should show the gaps of 2SIO and 2SRO and the bilayer DOS at E_F as functions of U_Ir and U_Ru, demonstrating a physically reasonable window in which the films are insulating, the bilayers metallic and the magnetic ground state stable. They should also address that structures were relaxed without SOC although the octahedral tilts and the DMI are SOC-sensitive, and that the slab is uncapped and SrO-terminated while the transport samples carry a 15-u.c. STO cap, which affects which measurement the calculation corresponds to.

7. **The layer and orbital origin of the ARPES bands is not identified.** The calculations use STO/SRO/SIO stacking and the Methods suggest SIO is grown on top, but the paper does not state the stacking order of each sample. At hν = 82 eV the probing depth is about one unit cell, so in 2SIO/1SRO the buried SRO is strongly attenuated, and the hole pocket at X could be an Ir J_eff = 1/2 state, a Ru t2g state or an interface-hybridized state. This determines which layer "became metallic" and is needed to interpret the evolution of f_coh and m* across the series. The authors should state the stacking order explicitly, show the DFT band structure along X–Γ–X for each bilayer in the proposed magnetic configuration (Fig. 4 shows only DOS) and compare it directly with the measured dispersions and Fermi surface, and use photon-energy, resonant or the collected circular-polarization (dichroic) data to assign the orbital character of the band in Fig. 3.

8. **The effective-mass trend contradicts the text's interpretation, and the fits are not robust.** The text says m* "decreases from 0.57 m_e for the 2SIO/2SRO heterostructure to 0.5 m_e and 0.41 m_e for the 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/1SRO heterostructures... further confirming the enhanced metallicity with increased total thickness" (p. 3). But the lightest mass belongs to the thinnest, least coherent and most resistive sample (2SIO/1SRO, f_coh = 26%), and the heaviest to the thickest and most coherent one (f_coh = 55%), so the trend runs opposite to the claim. The masses are also far below the several m_e typical of SRO. In Fig. 3D the band maximum lies ~0.1 eV above E_F, so the parabolic fits extrapolate into unoccupied states over a narrow range and m* depends strongly on the fitting window. The authors should give the fitted k-range, uncertainties on m*, and sensitivity to the band top, and either correct the interpretation or explain why a lighter mass is expected in the less metallic sample.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

9. **The coherent-weight fraction f_coh is underspecified and does not track transport.** The coherent/incoherent decomposition (Fig. 3 A.iii–C.iii) depends on the line shapes, background, Fermi–Dirac cutoff, energy resolution and k-integration window, none of which are specified, and no uncertainty is given. The step from 26% to 28% (2SIO/1SRO to 1SIO/2SRO) is probably within systematic error, so the claimed monotonic "enhancement of metallic character" rests on the 55% point alone. Moreover, f_coh does not track resistivity in Fig. 2A, where 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/2SRO have similar, lowest ρ while 2SIO/1SRO is highest. The authors should specify the fit functions and free parameters, give error bars, show robustness to reasonable model variations, add EDCs at other k-points and f_coh for the single films as a reference, and discuss the mismatch with transport.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

10. **The quantitative magnetometry is thin.** The text gives saturated moments of 0.6 μB/Ru for 2SIO/1SRO and 1 μB/Ru for 1SIO/2SRO and 2SIO/2SRO, but in Fig. 2B all three loops appear to saturate near ±1 μB/Ru; this must be reconciled, and if the 0.6 vs 1 μB/Ru trend is real it should be explained within the DMI model. For one unit cell of SRO on a 5×5 mm STO substrate the total moment is of order 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁵ emu, comparable to typical substrate impurity and diamagnetic backgrounds. The authors should describe the background subtraction and the error on the moment per Ru, show raw data and a bare-substrate (and STO-capped substrate) reference measured identically, and state how the Ru count was obtained (RHEED oscillations vs XRR/STEM). The single-film M(H), described as showing "no clear ferromagnetic hysteresis", should be plotted in Fig. 2B or a cited SI figure. The perpendicular anisotropy claim rests on out-of-plane loop squareness and MR alone; an in-plane M(H) on the same sample is needed. All loops are at 10 K; M(H) at several temperatures would tie the moment to the T_C values.

11. **The samples used for each probe differ.** Transport and SQUID samples carry a 15-u.c. STO cap and a post-capping anneal and are measured ex situ, while ARPES samples are uncapped and measured in situ. A cap can change the termination, oxygen stoichiometry and band bending of a 3–4 u.c. stack, and SRO dead-layer behavior is sensitive to its interfaces. The authors should state the capping of every sample, including whether the 2-u.c. single-film references in Fig. 2A, B were capped and processed identically, and discuss or test whether the cap affects conductivity (e.g. transport on uncapped samples, or XPS before and after capping).

12. **The structure underlying the interfacial DMI is not characterized.** The staggered DMI is attributed to octahedral rotations and tilts at the broken-inversion interface, but the only structural evidence is a 2×2 LEED periodicity, and the Methods admit that "precise structural and magnetic configurations cannot be fully resolved." The authors should show, where possible, half-order Bragg peaks or equivalent diffraction evidence for the octahedral rotation pattern assumed in the calculations, and state explicitly how the 2×2 LEED periodicity relates to the √2×√2 tilt pattern used for the staggered DMI.
Raised by: r1, r3

13. **Transport measurement details.** It is unclear which thickness (SRO only, or total) is used to convert sheet resistance to ρ for the bilayers versus the single films; the >10⁴ difference in ρ in Fig. 2A depends on it. For 3–4 u.c. films the sheet resistance at 10 K compared with h/e² is the more meaningful quantity and should be reported, along with whether any upturn appears below 10 K. The authors should explain how van der Pauw contacts were made to a 3–4 u.c. layer buried under 15 u.c. of STO, and, since growth at 0.15 mbar can reduce the STO substrate, show that a bare STO substrate processed identically (including anneal and cap) is insulating.

The authors should report the number of samples and show the spread, at least for transport and magnetometry; a second sample of 2SIO/1SRO, the most surprising case, would be especially valuable.
