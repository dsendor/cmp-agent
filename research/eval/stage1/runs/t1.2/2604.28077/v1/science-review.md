**A. Must address**

1. **No normal-state reference: the dissipation contrast is not yet shown to come from superconductivity.** Every dissipation data set was taken at T = 10 mK, and the case that dissipation tracks superconductivity rests on the comparison of point A (inside the conducting written region) with point B (outside, on insulating LAO/STO) in Fig. 4b, and on one magnetic-field sweep near point A (Fig. 4c–e). The A/B comparison mixes "conducting vs insulating" with "superconducting vs normal": in the Joule/electronic-friction picture the authors cite (Volokitin–Persson, ref. 32; Kisiel, refs. 17, 31) the loss depends non-monotonically on sheet resistance and screening, so a well-screening normal 2DES can show much less loss than a poorly conducting dielectric even with no superconductivity. No dissipation data are shown as a function of temperature or above Tc at the same location, and no field map is shown at point B, over the etched bare-STO reference area (Supp. Fig. 3), or with the tip retracted. Other mechanisms could produce field-dependent dissipation of this size: spin- and charge-related transitions in oxygen-deficient STO surfaces (which the authors' own ref. 17 detected by nc-AFM dissipation), field dependence of the qPlus sensor's intrinsic damping Γ0 or of the excitation electronics, and normal-state magnetoresistance of the 2DES altering the ohmic loss. Kisiel et al. (ref. 31), on whom the ΔV⁴ interpretation relies, compared spectra above and below Tc at the same location. The authors should measure Γ(V_bias) at points A and B, and in the 200 nm strip of Fig. 5, at base temperature and well above Tc (e.g. a series at 50, 150, 250, 400, 600 mK) at zero field, and at B = 0 versus B ≫ Bc (e.g. 0.5–1 T), all with the same tip, height, amplitude and fit window; repeat the field map of Fig. 4c at point B and on bare STO under identical setpoints; record Γ0(B) with the tip retracted; and report how α and β change. The ΔV⁴ term and the A/B contrast must change at Tc and Bc and persist in neither the normal state nor the controls. Without this, the title's "local probing of superconductivity" and the abstract's "local diagnostic of superconductivity" are not established.

2. **The ΔV⁴ "marker" is also present at the non-superconducting reference point, and the fit as presented does not discriminate.** The text (p. 9) describes point B as showing "a clear quadratic dependence" and A as having an emergent quartic component, yet the Fig. 4b caption gives β/α = 1.84 ± 0.01 V⁻² at B against 3.85 ± 0.03 V⁻² at A: a factor of two, not presence versus absence. The two curves cover different bias windows around their CPDs (A: |ΔV| up to about 0.7 V around a CPD of ≈ −0.5 V; B: about 0.4–0.5 V around ≈ 0.1 V). For B the quartic term equals the quadratic one only at |ΔV| ≈ 0.74 V, outside the measured range, so β at B is poorly constrained, and the fitted β/α of a truncated polynomial depends strongly on the window, so the ratios are not comparable. The written fit form αΔV² + βΔV⁴ has no constant, yet both curves sit on a baseline of 18–23 × 10⁻⁸ kg/s; it is not stated whether an offset was free. Curve A is visibly asymmetric about its minimum. The quoted uncertainties are least-squares only and ignore the α–β correlation and systematics (fit window, offset treatment, CPD and height drift). The authors should state the full fit function and free/fixed parameters; fit both points over the same |ΔV| window with a constant term and report α, β and the offset separately, with units; show how β/α varies with the window; show residuals for pure-quadratic, quadratic-plus-quartic and, if warranted, ΔV⁶ or asymmetric models, with an F-test or information criterion; and present β/α as a distribution or map over many pixels inside and outside the pattern, correlated with CPD and across both devices, rather than at two hand-picked points. Without this, "ΔV⁴ emerges in superconducting regions" is not supported.

3. **The "≈200 nm superconducting edge channel" rests on one line cut, at one edge, at a field above the transport critical field, and parts of the data argue against its interpretation.** The dissipation minimum (Fig. 5h) is shown only at B = −235 mT, above the ≈200 mT transport Bc of Fig. 1e that the paper otherwise uses as the threshold for losing superconductivity; the Discussion's resolution that the edge "likely has a higher critical field" (pp. 13–14) is assumed, not measured. Only one Y-line at X = 125 nm of a 250 nm-wide grid, at one boundary of the 1 µm 2D channel, is shown. No zero-field or field series of the same area is shown, the opposite edge and the channel interior are not mapped, and the 1D wire and waveguide that motivate the paper are not studied by dissipation, so neither edge localization, nor edge/interior contrast, nor independence of channel width is tested. In Fig. 5b–e the minimum at Y ≈ 400–600 nm is essentially independent of bias from −900 mV to +300 mV; since the authors argue elsewhere (pp. 11, 13) that tip bias locally dopes the interface and moves it along the dome, a strip marking the optimally doped part of a density gradient should shift in Y with bias. The strip also coincides with the steepest CPD gradient (Fig. 5a,b,h; CPD changes by 0.65 V across the area), where lateral electrostatic forces, dC/dz and constant-Δf height adjustments change most rapidly, and normal-state electronic friction is itself non-monotonic in carrier density (ref. 32); either could produce a Γ minimum at a conductor–insulator boundary without superconductivity. The authors should map Γ(x, y, V) across both edges and the interior of the 2D channel at B = 0, at several fields from 0 to about 300 mT and well above Bc (e.g. 1 T), and above Tc, at several X positions, ideally also across the 1D wire, channels of different widths and on the second device; show that the strip exists only in the superconducting state and that a comparable boundary above Tc gives no dip; show the simultaneously recorded z(x, y) images or use constant-height maps; and give the strip width with an uncertainty and its definition.

4. **The field scale of the dissipation does not match the transport critical field offered as its "direct connection".** The text (pp. 10–11, 13) says dissipation at the CPD increases with field, "reaching a maximum as it approaches the critical magnetic field" (~200 mT), and that the curvature crossover "occurs as the field approaches 200 mT"; this coincidence is presented as "a direct connection between the dissipation signals and the superconducting state". In Fig. 4e the maxima are at about −70 to −100 mT and +80 to +100 mT, and Γ has already fallen substantially by ±200 mT (the text itself later says "enhanced dissipation around ≈±100 mT"). In Fig. 4d the 42 mT (orange) curve is already double-humped and the 102 mT curve is concave; the "green curve in Fig. 4d" described as near 200 mT is the 102 mT cut by the arrowheads. The dissipation minimum spans only about ±20 mT, itself a characteristic field. The crossover is thus at roughly half Device A's transport Bc, closer to Device B's ≈55 mT (Supp. Fig. 4), which the main text never states. The authors should state which field scale (≈20–30 mT, ≈100 mT, 200 mT) they identify with which physical process, reconcile the scales quantitatively, and correct the text to describe Figs. 4d,e accurately.

**B. Should address**

5. **Tip–sample distance and feedback conditions during spectroscopy are not specified, and height changes can generate both the A/B and the strip contrast.** Topographic contrast is explicitly electrostatic (up to 8 nm apparent height in Fig. 4a, from Δf feedback on CPD differences, p. 6; Methods p. 27), so points A and B and the pixels across the Fig. 5 boundary sit at different heights, and dissipation and tip gating depend steeply on distance. The spectra appear to be taken under constant-Δf feedback (the Fig. 4e caption mentions an "AFM feedback" glitch); if so, the tip height changes with bias as the electrostatic force varies roughly as ΔV², and the distance dependence of Γ generates higher-order bias terms with no superconductivity involved, differently in regions of different CPD and dC/dz. The authors should state the protocol (constant height, feedback opened at a fixed bias, or setpoint bias at the local CPD) and the absolute tip height at A, B and across Fig. 5; acquire constant-height Γ(V) spectra or record z(V) for every spectrum and correct using measured Γ(z) curves; and show Γ(z) at fixed ΔV at both locations, demonstrating that the A/B contrast and the Fig. 5h minimum are not distance effects. Setpoints also differ between figures (Δf = −3.0, −1.0, −0.8 Hz; amplitudes 3.2–5.0 nm), so absolute Γ values cannot be compared across figures.

6. **Instrumental "apparent dissipation" in FM-AFM is not ruled out.** The excitation signal contains apparent dissipation from frequency-dependent transfer functions of the piezo/excitation path and from PLL or amplitude-controller crosstalk whenever Δf changes. Here Δf varies parabolically with bias by construction (Supp. Fig. 1b), so a bias-dependent apparent dissipation would naturally produce even-order (ΔV², ΔV⁴) terms; the sensor's intrinsic damping Γ0 and the electronics could also depend on field. The authors should report k, f₀, Q, V_exc,0 and controller bandwidths; show that Γ(V) does not simply track Δf(V), e.g. by comparing spectra at different heights spanning the same Δf range or verifying the phase stays at resonance; and show the dissipation at the CPD versus height.
Raised by: r1, r2

7. **The mechanism by which superconductivity would suppress the measured dissipation is not established for this geometry.** The ohmic and phonon arguments come from Nb (refs. 31, 32), a bulk metal directly under the tip with a well-defined normal-state ohmic V² law. Here the 2DES is buried under ≈1.5 nm of LAO on a quantum paraelectric with a huge, lossy dielectric response at 10 mK. Above Bc the patterned region does not recover the convex ΔV² parabola the model predicts for the normal state; the curves become concave (Fig. 4d, 162 and 222 mT), so the paper's own normal-state model does not describe the normal state at A. Point B is insulating yet is said to show "capacitive energy losses typically observed on metallic surfaces due to ohmic dissipation"; what carries the ohmic current there needs explaining. A simpler explanation of the lower Γ at A is that the 2DES screens the tip field from the lossy STO below, which would hold in the normal state. The interpretation "suppression of low-energy excitations due to the quasiparticle gap opening" (p. 9) involves a gap of order 40–60 µeV, while the biases are ~1 V and the oscillation quantum at 18 kHz is ≈75 neV; how the gap enters the bias dependence at these scales is unexplained. The authors should give an order-of-magnitude estimate (e.g. in the Volokitin–Persson framework) of Γ for a 2DES of the measured sheet resistance (≈20 kΩ normal state, Fig. 1f) compared with STO dielectric loss, and of the expected change on entering the superconducting state, or soften the mechanistic statements.

The sharp dissipation minimum within about ±20 mT in Fig. 4e, and the glitch noted in its caption, could originate in the tip rather than the sample. The authors should discuss tip superconductivity explicitly and use the null controls of concern 1 (field sweep over point B and bare STO with the same tip) to exclude it.

9. **The low-field region of the field map is compromised and possibly offset.** The red "−18 mT" curve in Fig. 4d, the main evidence for convex, superconducting behaviour in the field map, lies at the small negative field where the Fig. 4e caption reports an AFM feedback glitch and where Fig. 4c shows a narrow anomalous dark band beside a high-dissipation sliver at about 0 mT; the lowest points of the Fig. 4e minimum come from this region. The low-dissipation band is offset toward negative field (≈ −10 to −30 mT), and the map is asymmetric (the negative-field band extends to about −130 mT, the positive one to about +100 mT), suggesting remanent field or trapped flux in the superconducting magnet or sweep hysteresis. 10. **The CPD-to-density conversion cannot place regions on the superconducting dome, and the paper's numbers contradict the "overdoped interior" assignment.** Eq. (1) is a parallel-plate relation through ≈1.5 nm LAO with ε_r = 25. It ignores the surface charge, adsorbates and protonation that the Supplement (p. 30) calls "essential for generating measurable contrast" and that are thought to be the writing mechanism of both ULV-EBL and c-AFM (ref. 4), so a surface dipole enters at leading order and, with its sign unknown, "upper bound" is unjustified; it also ignores the 2DES extent into STO, STO's large field-dependent permittivity at mK, the tip field and quantum capacitance (ref. 34). Δn is relative to an arbitrarily chosen "non-patterned" pixel (CPD = 1.024 V in Fig. 3, 0.07 V in Fig. 5h) whose own density is unknown, and the CPD in Fig. 2e varies smoothly even outside the pattern, so Δn cannot be compared with the dome range n ≈ (1.5–6.5) × 10¹³ cm⁻², which refers to absolute Hall densities. (1) independently (Hall density on a co-written Hall bar, CPD versus side- or back-gate voltage alongside transport, or electrostatic simulation including STO permittivity), express doping relative to the transport dome of the same device, use one fixed reference and a fixed tip state, give an uncertainty on Δn, and otherwise present the dome assignments as qualitative.

11. **The lateral resolution of the CPD and dissipation maps is not established, yet ~200 nm conclusions are drawn.** The CPD and apparent-height features are far broader than the written patterns: the 10–20 nm 1D wire appears as a several-hundred-nm stripe in Fig. 2c, the density maximum in Figs. 2e and 3a lies largely outside the dashed 1D-line boundaries over a ~600 nm profile, and in Fig. 3b the density outside the "estimated pattern boundary" is already ≈6–8 × 10¹³ cm⁻², extending ~0.6 µm beyond it. Either long-range electrostatic averaging by the tip apex, cone and cantilever (3–5 nm amplitude, Δf of −0.8 to −3 Hz) broadens the profiles, the charge genuinely spreads (e.g. in high-ε STO), or the boundaries are misregistered. 14. **The local gating by the biased tip is invoked but not quantified or separated from the superconducting signature.** Biases up to ≈1.2 V (swings of ~1.4 V) across a nanometre gap plus ≈1.5 nm LAO can induce a density change comparable to the Δn attributed to patterning (Eq.
