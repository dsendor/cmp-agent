A. Must address

1. The superconductivity claim, which appears in the title and abstract, is not established by the data shown (Fig. 1f, Figs. 3e–g, Fig. S12, and the paragraph before Fig. 4). The resistance never approaches zero. In SC1 R_xx falls from about 200 Ω to about 80–100 Ω below the ~170 mK onset (Fig. 1f inset). In SC2 it falls from about 175 Ω to about 140 Ω, roughly 20%, with T_c ≈ 80 mK (Fig. S12b). The text attributes the residual resistance to "insufficient filtering" but tests this in no way, although the same set-up reaches 9 mK and resolves fine quantum oscillations. A partial drop with a finite-bias dV/dI peak that a few mT of B⊥ suppresses is not specific to superconductivity. It also fits a filamentary or percolating superconducting path through an inhomogeneous channel. Because the leaf region is very narrow in n–E, density or displacement-field inhomogeneity across the Hall bar would sample neighbouring phases, giving phase coexistence near a first-order boundary. It also fits Joule- or electron-heating nonlinearity in a strongly temperature-dependent correlated metal, or a current-driven transition between competing correlated states. The authors should: remeasure with better filtering or lower excitation until the resistance saturates; show R_xx(T) and dV/dI on several contact pairs (contacts 1–6 in Fig. S13f) and give the residual resistance as a fraction of the normal-state value for each pair; estimate what fraction of the Hall bar is in the leaf phase at a given gate setting; show how the residual resistance depends on n and E (does it fall toward the centre of the leaf and rise toward its edges?); show I–V curves with a well-defined critical current and its T and B⊥ dependence, not only dV/dI colour maps; show R_xx(T) at several B⊥ and B∥ with a systematic suppression of T_c; and, ideally, look for a phase-coherent signature (Fraunhofer-like or Josephson-like modulation of the critical current, or Little–Parks-type flux-periodic magnetoresistance). If zero resistance cannot be reached, the title and abstract should say "superconducting-like resistance drop", as the main text already does in places.

2. The "ferroelectric" part of "ferroelectric orbital magnetism" (a spontaneous, switchable electric polarization and a ΔP·M order parameter) is inferred only from the Hall signal, and the E-hysteresis has a simpler reading (Figs. 1i, 4a–d, S10, S11, and the ΔP·M paragraph). P is never measured. There is no capacitance, penetration-field or layer-resolved measurement, and no hysteresis in any quantity not tied to M: neither R_xx at B = 0 nor band-edge or Landau-level positions in forward against backward sweeps. The "3:1 polarized state" is speculative. The E-loop is "unipolar": both switching fields lie on the same side, near −34 and −37 mV/nm, and the loop is seen in R_xy under a stabilizing 50 mT field. A ferroelectric would hold two remanent polarizations of opposite sign at a common field and switch in both directions. A narrow window between two nearby negative fields is instead the generic signature of an ordinary first-order transition between two phases of different layer polarization and magnetization, for example a layer-polarized and a partially polarized metal, with metastability on both sides. It is equally consistent with E-tunable Berry curvature switching M with no spontaneous P. The linear shrinking of the loop with B⊥ (Fig. 4c,d) fits a Zeeman-type M·B term shifting a first-order boundary and does not show that a polarization is switched. The authors should: give a direct measure of layer polarization or electric dipole (penetration-field or layer-resolved capacitance, or at least asymmetric top-gate/bottom-gate sweeps showing remanent P at the same E); show B = 0 R_xx data; quantify the linear B⊥ shift of the switching fields, since the slope dE_c/dB⊥ ~ ΔM/ΔP gives a quantitative constraint to compare with band-structure estimates of ΔM and ΔP; show whether a counterpart state exists near E ≈ +35 mV/nm, since the ±E asymmetry bears on whether a polarization is being switched; and write down the Landau free energy containing ΔP·M and show which of its predictions, including the linear shrinkage, the data test. Without such evidence, "ferroelectric" should be replaced by a description of what is observed, for example "an electrically switchable orbital magnet with E-reversible Hall hysteresis".

3. The reversal of the B-hysteresis polarity with E at fixed n (Fig. 4e, between E = −35.5 and −36.2 mV/nm) is taken to show that "M·B alone does not capture the system's energetics" and so to require a ΔP·M coupling. Simpler explanations are not ruled out. The anomalous Hall signal measures the Berry curvature of the occupied states, not the orbital magnetization. Its sign, and even the sign of the orbital magnetization, can flip at fixed valley polarization as the Fermi level or the filling within a flavor moves through a Berry-curvature hot spot, or across a layer-polarization change or band inversion. That is the mechanism the paper itself describes in Fig. 2, and this region lies next to the band-inversion and Lifshitz features. It is also the known mechanism behind electrically switchable magnetism in moiré orbital Chern magnets such as twisted mono-bilayer graphene. The R_xy loop polarity could then reverse with no sign change of M and no ferroelectric degree of freedom. A second possibility is branch mixing. The reversal occurs "when the magnetic hysteresis is the largest", inside the narrow E window where the E-loop is open, so a B sweep there may switch the system between the two E-branches rather than reverse M within one state. The authors should: discuss and exclude the Berry-curvature sign change explicitly, for example by computing the signs of σ_xy and M for candidate flavor configurations in their model, or by showing with a magnetization-sensitive probe (nanoSQUID, MOKE) that M itself reverses; track the sign of the anomalous Hall step continuously across E and n; and measure B-loops at fixed E prepared from both E-branches, approached from above and from below.

B. Should address

4. Extrinsic sources of E-hysteresis are not excluded (Figs. 1h, 1i, 4a–d, S2, S10, S11; Methods). Hysteresis in dual-gate sweeps can come from charge trapping in the hBN or at the SiO₂/Si interface, or from slow relaxation of the global Si gate. That gate is held at −90 to −100 V for hole-side contacts and +95 V for the electron side, and an offset of only ~3 mV/nm (the gap between the switching fields) would suffice. The authors should show that: the same E sweep in a nearby non-magnetic region (for example the right end of the comet, Fig. S10) shows no hysteresis at the same sweep rate; the switching fields do not depend on sweep rate, sweep range or n–E path; n-sweeps at fixed E agree with E-sweeps; sharp independent features (Landau-level crossings, the CI boundary) do not shift between forward and backward E sweeps; and the comet behaviour is unchanged when the Si gate voltage is varied within the range that keeps the contacts transparent. Fig. 4a,b partly addresses path dependence and should be extended explicitly.

5. The Hall loops do not appear to be antisymmetrized (Figs. 4a–e, 1h, 1i, S2, S10, S11). R_xx is itself strongly hysteretic and of kΩ scale in the same regions (Fig. S2a,c,e), while the Hall steps are only about 300–400 Ω, and R_xy appears to be raw at a single B = 50 mT. Admixture of R_xx could generate or distort the loops, and the unipolar character, the sign reversal of Fig. 4e and the step amplitudes all depend on this. The authors should antisymmetrize all loops (R_xy at +B on one sweep direction against −B on the opposite direction, and the corresponding E-sweep pairs), show data at both field polarities, show R_xx loops alongside R_xy at the same E, and state in the Methods whether antisymmetrization was done.

6. The claim that SC1 survives about 6 T in plane, "more than an order of magnitude" above the ~300 mT Pauli limit, does not address field alignment and is not tied to a measured transition (Fig. 3g, Fig. S9, text near Fig. 3). The out-of-plane critical field is only a few mT for SC1 (Fig. 3e, ±10 mT axis) and about 1 mT for SC2 (Fig. S12d). At 6 T a misalignment of 0.05° already gives about 5 mT out of plane, so either the alignment was better than ~0.05°, which is not reported, or the state at several tesla is not the one a few mT of B⊥ destroys. Fig. 3g tracks a resistance minimum (the "left blue strip") in one line cut, not a transition. Fig. S9 shows R_xx at an unspecified temperature and that B∥ strongly reshapes the phase diagram, so the same phase may not be followed. The text quotes ~6 T while Fig. S9 says SC1 "shrinks" at 5 T and closes only at 10 T. An in-plane field also couples orbitally in a ~2 nm thick layer-polarized hexalayer. Finally, if the normal state is spin polarized, which the two-fold pockets in this region suggest, the Pauli limit does not apply and a large B∥ is expected, as in spin-polarized rhombohedral trilayer superconductivity (ref. 11). The authors should describe the alignment procedure and the residual angle (for example from the Hall voltage in nominally in-plane field); show R(T) and dV/dI against I at several B∥ (for example 0, 2, 4, 6 T), including the B⊥ dependence at finite B∥, and extract T_c(B∥) with a stated criterion; map the SC1 boundary in n–E at each B∥; state the T_c criterion (onset or midpoint) behind the ~300 mT estimate; and discuss the candidate mechanisms, including spin-polarized or spin-triplet pairing.

8. There is no check that the stacking stayed rhombohedral after fabrication (Methods, Fig. S13c). Raman mapping was done on the bare flake before transfer, and rhombohedral domains are known to relax toward Bernal stacking during transfer, annealing and etching. A Bernal or mixed-stacking inclusion or domain wall in the Hall bar would produce the inhomogeneity that gives partial resistance drops and phase coexistence (concern 1). The authors should show Raman of the finished, encapsulated channel, or argue from transport (for example the CI gap at E = 0, the band-insulator threshold, or the absence of Bernal-specific Landau-level sequences) that the whole channel is rhombohedral.

9. The alignment of the graphene to the top and bottom hBN is not reported (Methods, Fig. S13). Near-alignment produces a moiré potential that strongly changes rhombohedral multilayers. In graphene/hBN structures it has also been tied to anomalous "ferroelectric" gate hysteresis, and here it could explain the ±E asymmetry between SC1 (positive E) and the finite-E magnet (negative E). The authors should report the twist angles (for example from straight edges or SHG) and the hBN thicknesses, and show that moiré features (satellite resistance peaks, Brown–Zak oscillations) are absent over the full density range.

10. The "electric-field-driven band inversion" in the abstract rests on a qualitative two-flavor rigid-band picture that is not checked against a calculation (Figs. 1a–d, 2f, 2g–j). The tight-binding model is single-particle; the interaction-driven emergent gaps that define flavors A and B are drawn by hand. The parameters (γ0–γ4, δ, Δ) are not listed, though the band overlap at U = 0 is very sensitive to γ2 and γ3. The mapping between U (±2 meV/layer in Fig. 1a) and the experimental E (±100 mV/nm), including screening, is not given, so the inversion field (≈42 mV/nm at n = −1.26×10¹² cm⁻²), the Lifshitz lines of Fig. 2a and the ~100 mV/nm band-insulator threshold cannot be compared with any model. The paper itself lists observations at odds with the picture: two-fold rather than four-fold annular surfaces at E = 0 (Fig. 2e), valley-polarized magnetism near neutrality, and leaf Fermi surfaces (Fig. 3b) "incompatible with an n-independent band structure". The authors should give the parameters and the U–E conversion; carry out at least a self-consistent Hartree or Hartree–Fock calculation (or a phenomenological flavor-split model) for the proposed 2×2 and 3:1 flavor orders with flavor-dependent layer polarization; overlay the predicted Lifshitz lines on Fig. 2a and compare slopes, the crossing point and f_v values with Figs. 2–3; and explain how layer polarization is assigned from line tilts and how robust that assignment is. Otherwise "reveal" should become "is consistent with", and "band inversion", which carries topological connotations given refs. 25–27, should be described as a crossing of layer-polarized band edges within one flavor.

11. The calibration of n and E is not described (Methods). The hBN thicknesses, gate capacitances, the definition of E (D/ε₀ or otherwise), zero offsets and the uncertainty in n are missing. Since f_v = f_FFT·e/(nh) is normalized by n, a few-percent capacitance error shifts every f_v and hence every degeneracy assignment (peaks at 1/2 or 1/4) and sum-rule test. The positions of features such as E ≈ −35 mV/nm also depend on it. The authors should give the calibration and how n was cross-checked (Hall density, Landau-fan slopes in the fully metallic regions, the E-symmetry of the band-insulator gap) and state the uncertainty in f_v.

12. The fermiology is measured at B⊥ ≈ 0.4–2 T, but the phases it explains are defined at B = 0 (Fig. 1e), and several of them (the ferrovalley triangle, SC1, the comet) respond to tens of mT. In rhombohedral graphene, isospin phase boundaries shift appreciably with B⊥, and orbital magnetic states with large moments are favoured by it; Fig. 2a (1.5 T) differs visibly from Fig. 1e (0 T). Identifying zero-field resistive boundaries with Lifshitz transitions seen at finite field assumes the band structure does not change with B. The authors should show how the boundaries move with B⊥ (for example n–E maps at 0, 0.5 and 1.5 T), show that the Lifshitz lines extrapolate to the zero-field boundaries used to place SC1 and the magnets, and restrict the claimed correspondence to boundaries that do not move.

14. The carrier type and degeneracy of each pocket are assigned through the f_v sum rule Σ s_i k_i f_i = 1, but violations elsewhere are explained after the fact by unresolved heavy pockets, so the logic is partly circular (Fig. S5, Figs. 2c, 3b). Where the rule is violated (Fig. S5f,i), different sign and degeneracy choices can satisfy it equally well, and the electron-like character of the pockets that underpins the band-overlap and band-inversion claims ("a new electron pocket emerges in Fig. 2c") is not independently established.
