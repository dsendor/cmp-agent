Concerns

A. Must address

None. Each main claim is supported by evidence that a reasonable referee at a top-tier journal would accept. The quantized ρxy = h/3e², h/4e² and h/5e² at zero field (Fig. 1c) agrees with Středa slopes for the new states (Figs. 2b, 2f, 4g, 4h). The C = 3 and C = 4 plateaus are both quantized at zero field in one D sweep in D4 (Fig. 4c, d, f). The C = ±3 states near ν = 3 are reproduced in D1, D6 and D7. The concerns below would narrow or strengthen these claims; none of them undermines them.

B. Should address

1. The layer series C = N rests on one device each for N = 4 and N = 5, and the N = 5 device was published before. The headline claim is that "the layer number N = 3, 4, 5 directly sets the Chern number" (Abstract; p. 3; Discussion: "validates a design principle"). N = 3 is reproduced in D1, D6 and D7, but N = 4 rests only on D2 and N = 5 only on D3 (Extended Data Table 1). According to the Methods, D3 "has been systematically studied previously [39]" and was built with a metallic top gate rather than a graphite one (d_s = 53 nm in the calculation, against 28 nm for the others), so it is not a new, matched device. For D3 the manuscript shows only one hysteresis loop (Fig. 1c, right). It gives no Středa fan, no ν–D map (Extended Data Fig. 2 covers only D1, D2 and D4), no ρxx or ρxy line cuts, no temperature dependence and no Raman characterization (Extended Data Fig. 1 covers only D1, D2 and D4). Without a Středa check, h/5e² is not independently distinguished from a partially quantized C = 4 state with parallel conduction, since the two differ by only 20%. Device-to-device spread, and whether C = 4 or 5 appears in every device of a given N, cannot be judged. The authors should (i) state clearly which D3 data are new and which come from ref. 39; (ii) show the Středa analysis, ν–D map, line cuts and temperature dependence for D3 in this manuscript, or cite exactly where they appear; (iii) ideally add a second (1+4) device and a new graphite-gated (1+5) device, or at least show where D2 sits within the twist-angle window in which C = 4 is expected (Extended Data Fig. 10b). If more devices cannot be added, the "systematic" and "programmable" language should be narrowed to what one device per N supports.

2. The calculations that "confirm" C = N and the C = 3 → C = 4 transition use parameters chosen per device, and the paper does not link them to the measured D. Fig. 1b uses an "optimized interlayer potential Δ" of 9.0, 12.5 and 11.0 meV for the three configurations. Fig. 4a uses Δ = 11.5 and 10.0 meV to produce C = 3 and C = 4. The calculations use ε_r = 20, a large value given without justification, and d_s = 53 nm for (1+5) against 28 nm for the others. Extended Data Fig. 10 shows that the Chern number of the first conduction band depends strongly on θ and Δ. For (1+4) and (1+5) in particular, the C = N region is narrow and the measured angles (θ = 1.16° and 1.39°) appear to sit near its boundary, while much of the (θ, Δ) plane gives smaller C. No conversion from the experimental D (e.g. −0.540 < D < −0.420 V nm⁻¹ for D1, −0.508 V nm⁻¹ for D2, −0.669 and −0.602 V nm⁻¹ for D4, with the transition near −0.628 V nm⁻¹) to Δ is given, including layer screening. The paper does not say whether Δ was chosen to reproduce the observed C. The sign of Δ relative to the experimental negative D is not stated. For the transition, Fig. 4a assigns C = 3 to the larger Δ, and experimentally C = 3 appears at the larger |D|; this ordering needs to follow from a D-to-Δ mapping, not be asserted. The Methods state that the single-particle Hamiltonian alone already gives an isolated Chern band in some regimes, so the role of Hartree–Fock and which spin/valley-polarized ground state is assumed (an input or an output of the calculation) are unclear. The continuum-model parameters are deferred to a Supplementary Information that is not available. As it stands, the statements that the data "directly confirm the layer-dependent Chern number predicted by our band calculations" (p. 3), that the transition "agrees well with band structure calculations" (p. 6), and that Extended Data Fig. 10 is a "prediction" supporting "design-driven" programming of C are a posteriori. The authors should compute Δ(D) self-consistently or with a stated electrostatic screening model for each device, with its uncertainty; mark the experimental (θ, Δ) of each device on Extended Data Fig. 10; show that the predicted C(D), and for D4 and D5 the predicted position of the C = 3/4 boundary, match the measurements without adjustment; show the sensitivity of the predicted C to ε_r, d_s, the remote-hopping parameters and the five-band projection, at least for the (2+4) case; and put the full parameter set in the paper. Otherwise the "agrees well" and "design principle" language should be softened.

3. The nature of the C = 3 → C = 4 transition in the (2+4) system is not established, and its reproduction in D5 is incomplete. The paper calls it a "topological phase transition" driven by a change in band topology (Fig. 4a). In D4 (Fig. 4f), between the plateaus (−0.641 < D < −0.608 V nm⁻¹) ρxy changes smoothly from h/3e² to h/4e², while ρxx shows only a modest bump of roughly 0.1 h/e² near D ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹. Three scenarios give the same intermediate ρxy and are not distinguished: a gap-closing band-inversion transition; a first-order switch between competing interaction-driven ground states with different layer, valley or flavor polarization; and spatial phase separation into C = 3 and C = 4 regions across the Hall bar from twist-angle or D inhomogeneity. Only one D-sweep direction is shown (Fig. 4f; Extended Data Fig. 4a, b), although the (1+3) data in Fig. 3j show strong D-sweep hysteresis. The authors should show forward and backward D sweeps through the transition at B = 0 after training and at small B; compare several contact pairs or Hall crosses along the bar to detect coexistence; show the temperature dependence of ρxx and the activation gap as a function of D through the boundary itself, to test whether a gap closes; and compare HF total energies of the competing C = 3 and C = 4 states across D, rather than the topology of a single band at two Δ values. Hysteresis and a non-closing gap would point to "switching between competing Chern insulators" rather than a "topological phase transition", and the wording should follow the result. In D5, the C = 3 state is shown quantized only at B = 1.5 T (Extended Data Fig. 7f, top), and its zero-field loop at the green star (Extended Data Fig. 7c) is broad, has intermediate steps, does not reach h/3e², and has ρxx ≈ 0.1 h/e². The zero-field transition between two QAH states therefore rests on D4 alone; the text should say so, or the authors should provide zero-field quantization of C = 3 in D5.

4. The quality of quantization is never quantified, and "well-developed quantization and suppressed longitudinal resistance … across seven devices" and "improved robustness compared to other systems" overstate the data. The text repeatedly says ρxx vanishes and calls the quantization "precise", but in several panels ρxx is visibly finite on the plateau: Fig. 2c, d (D1, roughly 0.03–0.05 h/e²); Fig. 2g (D2, finite ρxx minimum); Fig. 4f (D4, roughly 0.05 h/e², about 1 kΩ, on the C = 4 plateau); Extended Data Fig. 4b (about 0.05 h/e² across the range at 0.3 K); Extended Data Fig. 7c (D5, C = 3, not quantized at zero field); and Extended Data Figs. 8c and 9c (D6 and D7 at ν ≈ 3, where ρxy is plotted at ±h/3e² but ρxx stays at roughly 0.15–0.2 h/e², so the plateau value alone is not diagnostic of a QAH state). The hysteresis loops are also (anti)symmetrized by combining up- and down-sweeps (Methods), which forces them to be symmetric and could hide asymmetry or an exchange-bias-like offset. The authors should tabulate, for each device and state, ρxy/(h/Ce²) (deviation in %), ρxx in ohms at B = 0 and base temperature, with uncertainties, and the temperature at which ρxy deviates by more than 1%; show raw, unsymmetrized Rxy(B) for at least one key loop per device; state whether quantization holds on more than one contact pair; and revise the "seven devices" statement to separate fully quantized states from anomalous Hall states with partial quantization. "Improved robustness" should be supported by a quantitative comparison with refs. 15, 31, 32 and 39–41, or removed.

5. Electrical switching of chirality near ν = 3 is shown only at B = 0.1 T and in one device, and the proposed mechanism is not calculated. The Abstract claims in-situ electrical switching of the sign of C, but every ν- and D-driven reversal (Figs. 3a–d, 3i, 3j) is measured at B = 0.1 T. A sign change of ρxy at fixed B as ν or D varies is the expected signature of a sign change of the orbital magnetization M relative to B; it does not by itself show that a remanent C = +3 state can be written into C = −3 electrically at zero field. The authors should prepare the state at B = 0, sweep ν or D through the switching window, return, and read out at B = 0, with repeated cycles; and show the Fig. 3i/j loops at B = 0 and at B = −0.1 T, where the switching direction should reverse if M·B drives it. ρxx is not shown in Figs. 3i and 3j, so it cannot be judged whether ρxy is accompanied by vanishing ρxx throughout the switching ranges; it should be added. Fig. 3k, described as the "free energy stability of valley-polarized states", is a schematic. The bulk-plus-edge orbital-magnetization explanation (p. 5; refs. 48, 49) would be testable by computing M(ν, D or Δ) for both valleys from the HF ground state and showing that it changes sign near ν = 2.905–2.947 and D ≈ −0.29 V nm⁻¹, and by plotting the measured coercive field against ν and D across the sign-change line (a divergence where M changes sign, as in refs. 48, 49). Otherwise the mechanistic claims should be softened. The switching is shown only in D1; D6 and D7 show the C = −3 state (Extended Data Figs. 8c, 9c) but not the switching. The statement that opposite Chern numbers "coexist within a single scan direction of D (regions ① and ④)" reads as a sequential switch during one sweep and should be reworded. The novelty claim that this was "not observed in previous reports [32]" should be compared explicitly with ref. 33 (D-switchable chirality in rhombohedral graphene from the same group).

6. The sign convention of D appears inconsistent with the physical picture. The Methods define D = (C_bV_b − C_tV_t)/2ε₀, with D > 0 pointing from the rhombohedral layers toward the monolayer (Fig. 1a). Negative D then pushes electrons toward the monolayer, that is, toward the moiré interface. The text says the reverse: QAH states appear "when electrons are polarized away from moiré interface by applying a sufficiently large negative D" (p. 3), and it relies on this to match refs. 15 and 39. Either the convention or the statement is wrong. Because this sets the sign of Δ in the calculations (concern 2) and the comparison with the literature, the authors should check it, correct it, and connect the convention explicitly to the layer polarization in the text.

7. Rhombohedral stacking and layer count in the measured channel are not well established. Layer number is set by optical contrast and stacking by IR imaging and Raman (Methods). In Extended Data Fig. 1e and 1k (D1 and D2), the "rhombohedral" and "Bernal" 2D spectra almost overlap. The FWHM maps (Extended Data Fig. 1d, j, p) show contrast, but they are not overlaid on the final Hall-bar outlines (Fig. 1f, l, r), so the reader cannot check that the channel and contacts lie inside one rhombohedral domain. In D4 (Extended Data Fig. 1p, q), Bernal regions are clearly present in the stack, and the stacking of the Bernal bilayer in (2+4) is not verified. D3 has no stacking characterization in this manuscript. Rhombohedral domains can relax to Bernal during transfer, AFM cutting and etching, and mixed stacking would change the band topology and the expected C, on which the layer-programming argument depends. The authors should overlay the Raman maps of the finished devices on the Hall bars; quote the spectral criterion (2D FWHM threshold or asymmetry) and its spread inside the channel; and give an independent transport or structural check of stacking and layer number in each device, such as the D-dependent gap at charge neutrality, the Landau-level sequence at D = 0 or at ν = 0, ±4, or the AFM step height.

8. The "C = 2 Chern insulator at ν = 3/2" is not supported by its Hall response. The text says a C = 2 Chern insulator "is identified" at ν = 3/2 (Extended Data Fig. 5), indicating doubling of the moiré cell, "topological charge order" and simultaneous breaking of time-reversal and translation symmetry (p. 4). The zero-field ρxy at ν = 3/2 (Extended Data Fig. 5c) saturates near 0.3–0.33 h/e², close to h/3e² rather than h/2e² = 0.5 h/e², which suggests a tail of the neighboring C = 3 state; ρxx is not shown. The only evidence for C = 2 is a Středa line in Extended Data Fig. 5b, visible at high field and coexisting with a C = 3 line. The authors should show ρxy and ρxx along the C = 2 trajectory at finite B, demonstrating ρxy = h/2e² and ρxx → 0, give the field at which the plateau develops, and explain the h/3e²-like zero-field value. Otherwise the claim should be weakened to a field-stabilized C = 2 feature emanating from ν = 3/2, with the charge-order interpretation presented as a possibility. Similar caution applies to the ν = 7/3 features (Extended Data Fig. 6) that the Discussion cites in support of the fractional Chern insulator outlook.

9. The activation-gap analysis (Extended Data Fig. 4c, d) uses an inconsistent functional form and a narrow window near the magnetic transition. ρxx is fitted to exp(−Δ/2k_BT) but δρxy to exp(−Δ/k_BT), yet the two give the same Δ (2.09 against 2.13 meV for C = 3; 1.04 against 1.03 meV for C = 4), which would imply δρxy activates twice as steeply as ρxx; this needs explanation, or one formula is misstated. The fits appear to use only the few highest-temperature points (1/T < ~0.5 K⁻¹, roughly 2–6 K), close to the Curie temperature (about 6 K in Extended Data Fig. 3i, l), where ρxy is already collapsing and the gap and magnetization are both temperature-dependent. Below that window ρxx flattens at a finite value. The extracted gaps (about 24 K and 12 K) exceed the ordering temperature, which suggests the fits may capture the loss of magnetic order rather than a charge gap. The C = 4 gap is half the C = 3 gap, yet the Curie temperatures look similar. The fits are done at B = 30 mT while the maps use 90 mT. The authors should state the fit range and number of points, show residuals, use a consistent definition, explain the choice of field, discuss whether these numbers are charge gaps, and compare them with the HF gaps of Fig. 4a.

10. The novelty relative to closely related work, much of it from the same group, needs to be stated explicitly. Ref. 39 (same group, including device D3) reports high-C QAH in twisted rhombohedral graphene; ref. 33 (same group) reports D-switchable chirality; ref. 41 reports a "programmable QAH insulator in twisted crystalline flatbands"; refs. 40 and 41 report high-C QAH; and refs. 54 and 55 (Note added) independently study twisted Bernal bilayer–rhombohedral tetralayer graphene and the twisted monolayer–rhombohedral multilayer family. The introduction says how C evolves with N "remains unclear", and p. 6 says the D-driven transition "has remained elusive", without saying what these works already established. The authors should add a short, specific comparison in the main text: which Chern numbers and configurations were reported before, whether the C = 3 ↔ 4 transition or the D-driven chirality switching appears in refs. 54 and 55, and what is new here, in particular the (1+4) C = 4 state, the (2+4) transition and the ν- and D-switching at ν = 3.

11. Twist-angle homogeneity and filling calibration are not quantified. The full-filling and Brown–Zak twist angles agree to 0.01° (and moiré wavelengths to 0.01 nm) for most devices (Extended Data Table 1), but no uncertainty is given for either method, and only one contact configuration seems to have been used. Because the C value in the (1+4) and (2+4) systems depends sensitively on θ (Extended Data Fig. 10b, d), the authors should report the spread of θ and of the ν = 4 (full-filling) position between contact pairs, with uncertainties. The QAH states in Figs. 3 and 4 sit at ν = 2.905, 2.947 and 1.02 rather than at integer fillings; the authors should state whether this offset reflects calibration error in n₀ or the gate capacitances, or genuine physics such as an extended QAH region as in ref. 31.

13. Opposite Chern numbers within one D scan (regions ① and ④ in Fig. 3j) could reflect multi-domain configurations rather than uniform states.
