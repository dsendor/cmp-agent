## A. Must address

1. **The C = 5 member of the C = N family is not supported by data in this manuscript.** The abstract and Fig. 1 claim C = N for N = 3, 4, 5, "experimentally confirmed by quantized Hall resistance and the Streda formula". For N = 5, though, the only data shown is the device D3 hysteresis loop at a single (ν, D) point in Fig. 1c (right). D3 has no Landau fan or Streda analysis and no ν–D map: Extended Data Fig. 2 covers only D1, D2 and D4, although the text says maps are given "for all devices". It also has no temperature dependence (Extended Data Fig. 4 leaves it out), no Raman stacking characterization (Extended Data Fig. 1 leaves it out) and no Brown–Zak fit behind its twist angle in Extended Data Tab. 1. D3 also has a different gate geometry, with a metallic top gate. The Methods say it "has been systematically studied previously [39]", but the main text presents it alongside the new devices without saying so. A plateau near h/5e² = 5.16 kΩ could, taken alone, come from a misassigned geometry or an incompletely quantized state. The Streda slope is what fixes C, and for N = 5 the paper shows none, so the headline claim rests on previously published data for its largest member. The authors should include the D3 data that establish C = 5 (Streda fan with a fitted slope, ν–D map, temperature dependence, stacking characterization) or cite exactly which figures of Ref. [39] contain them. They should also say in the main text that D3 comes from Ref. [39]. Otherwise they should limit the Streda-confirmed claim to N = 3 and 4.

## B. Should address

2. **The C = 3 to C = 4 transition in (2+4) L device D4 is not shown to be a gap-closing band-topology transition rather than a first-order switch between competing ordered states or a domain mixture.** The text (p. 5–6, Fig. 4) calls it a "displacement-field-driven topological phase transition" that "agrees well with band structure calculations". Between the plateaus in Fig. 4b,f (−0.641 < D < −0.608 V nm⁻¹), ρxy moves smoothly between h/3e² and h/4e², and the ρxx maximum near D ≈ −0.628 V nm⁻¹ is small, about 0.05 h/e². That fits either (i) closing and reopening of the Chern gap at fixed isospin order, as Fig. 4a implies, or (ii) a first-order switch between states with different spin, valley or layer polarization, with C = 3 and C = 4 domains coexisting. The scan-history effects in D1 (Fig. 3i, Extended Data Fig. 6) make (ii) plausible. The paper does not say which mechanism the Hartree–Fock calculation shows, or whether its ground state is gapless at intermediate Δ. The comparison with theory is also only qualitative. Fig. 4a uses two hand-picked values, Δ = 11.5 and 10.0 meV, while the experimental windows run from −0.683 to −0.641 and from −0.608 to −0.571 V nm⁻¹, and no conversion links them. "Agrees well" therefore amounts to agreement on ordering only. The authors should:
   - show forward and backward D sweeps at fixed ν = 1.02 and test for hysteresis. Fig. 3i has such a sweep for the ν = 3 case, but there is none here;
   - show zero-field hysteresis loops (ρxx and ρxy) at several D values inside the transition window;
   - test whether ρxy at intermediate D is a weighted mix of h/3e² and h/4e²;
   - extract activation gaps of both states against D from the Extended Data Fig. 5 data and show whether the gap goes to zero at the boundary;
   - state the D-to-Δ conversion, including screening, and the predicted width of the intermediate region, and explain what the calculation says about the gap along the path between the two states.

   This determines whether the "topological phase transition" and "topological transistor" language in the Discussion is warranted.

3. **The mechanism proposed for the D-driven sign reversal of |C| = 3 at ν ≈ 3 in (1+3) L is not supported, and the data fit an ordinary magnetization (valley-polarization) reversal equally well.** On p. 5 the authors explain the ν-driven switch as a sign reversal of orbital magnetization across the gap (Refs. [48, 49]). The D-driven switch they attribute to "a displacement-field-induced topological phase transition, in which D modulates the distribution of Berry curvature and changes the Chern number of the corresponding valley Chern band". Nothing in the paper separates the two. The D sweeps in Fig. 3i are hysteretic in the same way as the ν sweep in Fig. 3h. In the forward sweep ρxy is +h/3e² for −0.330 < D < −0.293 V nm⁻¹ and then −h/3e²; in the reverse sweep it is −h/3e² for −0.307 < D < −0.270 and then +h/3e². Only the sign changes, never |C|. That is what a D-dependent orbital magnetization changing sign under the fixed bias field would produce: going from +3 to −3 is naturally a change in which valley is doubly occupied, whereas a Berry-curvature-driven change would generally pass through a different |C|. The several abrupt jumps near D ≈ −0.33 to −0.335 V nm⁻¹ look like domain switching. Saying "opposite Chern numbers can coexist within the same scan direction" does not distinguish this from Ref. [32], because any hysteretic switch shows both signs in both directions. No calculation in the paper addresses ν = 3: Extended Data Fig. 10 covers only the first conduction band at ν = 1. All switching data (Fig. 3a–d,h,i; Extended Data Figs. 6–7) are taken at B = 0.1 T, which itself biases the magnetization, and Fig. 3h,i show no ρxx. The authors should:
   - add ρxx to Fig. 3h,i;
   - take zero-field hysteresis loops on a dense grid of D at fixed ν across the switching region, and show whether the remanent B = 0 sign of ρxy is set by D or only by history. This also tests nonvolatile electrical switching at B = 0;
   - map the coercive field against ν and D, since the orbital-magnetization picture predicts a divergence or sign change at the reversal point, and compare the coercive-field polarity with the ρxy sign on each side;
   - give the flavor configuration that yields |C| = 3 at ν = 3, and an HF calculation at ν = 3 showing whether the valley Chern number changes sign with Δ, together with the orbital magnetization.

   Otherwise they should describe the effect as electric-field-controlled magnetization (chirality) reversal and present the Berry-curvature mechanism as a hypothesis. The claim of switching by "three independent parameters: B, ν and D" should also note that switching by B is generic to any ferromagnet.

4. **The precision of quantization is never stated, and ρxx is clearly finite for some headline states.** "Precisely quantized", "vanishing ρxx" and "unambiguous" (p. 2, p. 6) are asserted from line plots scaled from 0 to 0.4 h/e². For the D4 C = 4 state, ρxy is about 0.97–0.99 h/4e² at base temperature and falls to about 0.93 by 3 K (Extended Data Fig. 4l). Its ρxx is roughly 0.02–0.05 h/e² at 0.3 K (Extended Data Figs. 4k,l and 5b), against near zero in the C = 3 window, and the h/4e² plateau in Fig. 4f is less flat than the h/3e² one. Elsewhere, the ρxx trace in Fig. 3g sits visibly above zero, and Fig. 2c reaches h/3e² only over a narrow range of ν. Calling a state a C = 4 QAH insulator requires ρxx ≪ ρxy, and telling C = 5 from its neighbours requires resolving the plateau. The authors should tabulate ρxy/(h/Ce²) and ρxx (in ohms), with uncertainties, at B = 0 and base temperature, for every claimed state: D1 C = 3 at ν = 1 and C = ±3 at ν ≈ 3; D2 C = 4; D3 C = 5; D4 C = 3 and C = 4. The table should also give the excitation current. Figs. 1c, 2c,d,g,h, 3g and 4c,d are the relevant panels. The adjectives should be replaced with these numbers.

5. **Each result rests on a single device, and nothing is shown on reproducibility or twist-angle homogeneity.** (1+3), (1+4), (1+5) and (2+4) L are each represented by one device (Extended Data Tab. 1: D1, D2, D3, D4). The (1+N) devices sit at different twist angles (1.29°, 1.16°, 1.39°), so angle dependence and device-to-device spread cannot be judged. Each twist angle seems to come from one contact pair. In twisted samples, local twist-angle variation and stacking-domain boundaries can produce plateau-like features and sign changes. The QAH windows in D are narrow (about 0.03–0.04 V nm⁻¹ in D4), and the fractional-filling features depend on ν being uniform. The authors should report n_s and θ from several contact pairs on each Hall bar and show whether the plateaus appear in more than one pair. Ideally they should add a second device for at least one configuration, especially the (1+4) C = 4 state and the (2+4) transition. If no second device exists, they should state that each result comes from a single device.

6. **The Hartree–Fock calculations are described as confirming C = N, but their parameters are set per device and their key assumptions are untested.** The Methods use a large ε_r = 20. The screening length d_s is 53 nm for (1+5) and 28 nm for the other configurations, with no justification for either value. Δ is chosen separately for each device (9.0, 12.5 and 11.0 meV in Fig. 1b; 11.5 and 10.0 meV in Fig. 4a) and is not tied to the measured D. Several details are missing: only "the first five conduction bands" are kept, with no word on how remote and valence bands are treated, on the interaction or double-counting scheme, or on lattice relaxation. Rhombohedral moiré HF results are known to depend sensitively on these choices. The calculation assumes a fully spin- and valley-polarized state and does not compare its energy with intervalley-coherent or layer-polarized competitors. It is also not stated whether that polarization at ν = 1 is imposed or emerges self-consistently. Extended Data Fig. 10 shows C = N over a wide range for (1+3) and (1+4) but only in a small, fragmented region for (1+5) and (2+4), with lower Chern numbers and gapless points nearby. The Methods say the single-particle Hamiltonian alone gives the topological band and that HF is included "for completeness", while the Fig. 1b caption calls the bands self-consistent HF. The Supplementary Information the Methods cite for "mathematical details and physical parameters" did not accompany the submission. The authors should provide it and show how C depends on ε_r, d_s, the band cutoff and the subtraction scheme. They should give a Δ(D) conversion with layer screening and mark each device's measured (θ, D) window on Extended Data Fig. 10. Unless the comparison is made parameter-free, "confirm" should be softened to "consistent with".

7. **The Streda-formula Chern numbers need fitted slopes with uncertainties.** C = 3, 4 and 5 differ by only about 25–33% in dn/dB. Converting slope to C uses n_s, gate capacitances calibrated from quantum oscillations, and the offsets n₀ and D₀, and each carries uncertainty. The white dashed lines in Figs. 2b,f, 3e,f and 4g,h are drawn by eye. The authors should fit dν/dB along the ρxx minimum or ρxy plateau and report C with an error bar that propagates the twist-angle and capacitance uncertainties. They should also show that C ± 1 is excluded.
Raised by: r1, r3

8. **The evidence for rhombohedral stacking in the measured channel is weak.** For D1 and D2, the Raman 2D-peak spectra of the "rhombohedral" and "Bernal" regions are nearly indistinguishable (Extended Data Fig. 1e,k). The FWHM maps (Extended Data Fig. 1d,j,p) were taken on the stack and are not registered to the final Hall-bar footprint (Extended Data Fig. 1f,l,r). Rhombohedral domains often relax to Bernal during transfer and etching, and a domain wall or mixed stacking inside the Hall bar would affect both the quantization and the hysteresis, and could alter C. The authors should overlay the Hall-bar outline on the Raman maps, ideally map the etched channel itself, quantify the difference in 2D-peak width or asymmetry between the two stackings, and provide equivalent characterization for D3.

9. **The C = 2 state at ν = 3/2 and the "incipient fractional Chern insulator" at ν = 7/3 are not established.** A C = 2 state should give ρxy = h/2e² ≈ 0.5 h/e². At ν = 3/2, though, Extended Data Fig. 8c shows ρxy saturating near 0.3–0.32 h/e² at 0.5 K. That is close to h/3e², the value of the adjacent C = 3 state, which points to contamination from nearby fillings through twist-angle or density inhomogeneity. ρxx is not shown, and the C = 2 assignment rests only on a Streda line drawn in Extended Data Fig. 8b without an uncertainty. At ν ≈ 7/3, ρxy is about 0.25 h/e² (Extended Data Fig. 9c). The paper never states the Hall value or Streda slope expected for the proposed fractional state, extracts no slope from Extended Data Fig. 9b, and the claimed fan dispersion from 7/3 is not evident in that figure. The authors should state the expected σxy for each proposed state, give fitted Streda slopes with uncertainties and ρxx at these fillings, and address the inhomogeneity explanation, including why ρxy ≈ h/3e² at ν = 3/2. Otherwise they should describe these as anomalous Hall features of unknown topology, and remove "identified", "C = 2 Chern insulator", "doubling of the moiré unit cell" and "incipient fractional Chern insulator" from the main text and the Discussion (p. 4, p. 6).

12. **The field-symmetrization procedure may fail in hysteretic regimes, and unsymmetrized maps mix ρxx into ρxy.** Fig. 2a and Fig. 4b are symmetrized between ±0.1 T or ±90 mT. Near ν = 3 the coercive field reaches about 60–80 mT (Fig. 3g), comparable to the field used. If one sign of B does not fully polarize the sample, antisymmetrization mixes magnetic states. Figs. 3a–d, 3h, 3i and Extended Data Figs. 6–7 are taken at a single B = 0.1 T, and their captions do not say whether they are raw or symmetrized. Around the switching region ρxx is large (up to about 20 kΩ), so raw ρxy contains a geometric admixture of ρxx. The switching boundaries quoted to 0.001–0.003 V nm⁻¹ (p. 5) and the "nearly rectangular" loop are therefore uncertain. The authors should state the procedure for every map and confirm that |B| exceeds the coercive field throughout each symmetrized region, or else show both polarities separately. For the fixed-field maps, they should either estimate the ρxx admixture (for example from ρxy at ρxx maxima) or apply the forward/backward antisymmetrization already used for the hysteresis loops (+B forward with −B backward), and show the −0.1 T maps.

13. **The sign convention for D appears to conflict with the stated physical picture.** The Methods and Fig. 1a define positive D as pointing from the rhombohedral multilayer toward the monolayer, and Fig. 1a puts the rhombohedral stack at the bottom. Positive D then means a higher bottom-gate potential, which pulls electrons toward the outer rhombohedral layer, away from the moiré interface. The text (p. 3), however, says the QAH states appear at large negative D "when electrons are polarized away from moiré interface". This determines which layer hosts the carriers, and so whether the measured states correspond to the sign of Δ used in the calculations. The authors should clarify the convention, state which side the electrons are polarized toward in the QAH regime, and mark Δ and its sign relative to D on Fig. 1a.
