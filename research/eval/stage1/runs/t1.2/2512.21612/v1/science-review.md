## A. Must address

1. **The ν = 2/3 state is not shown to be quantized: ρxy peaks rather than plateaus, ρxx is large, the Hall conductance is not 7/3, and the second device does not reproduce the value.** (Fig. 4d–f, Extended Data Figs. 4c,i,o,u and 11a; p. 7.) The headline C = 7/3 FCI rests on ρxy reaching 3h/7e² ≈ 11.06 kΩ "with an accuracy of 99%" (98% for B > 200 mT), while ρxx has only a weak local minimum of about 3 kΩ against a ~4 kΩ background (Fig. 4d). In the hysteresis loop ρxx ≈ 0.3–0.4 × 3h/7e² ≈ 4–4.7 kΩ (Fig. 4f, Extended Data Fig. 4i). With ρxx/ρxy ≈ 0.27–0.4, ρxy does not measure the Hall conductance. Inverting the tensor with ρxy = 11.06 kΩ and ρxx = 3 kΩ gives σxy ≈ 2.17 e²/h and σxx ≈ 0.59 e²/h, between 2 and 7/3 and not equal to any simple fraction, so σxy differs from 7/3 by roughly 10–15%. In ν (Fig. 4d) ρxy is a sharp peak, and in D (Fig. 4e) a smooth dome whose maximum just touches 3h/7e²; there is no plateau of finite width in either variable. A trace that sweeps continuously through a value will reach it somewhere, and a fractional, non-plateau ρxy beside a large dissipative channel is what a C = 2 or C = 3 domain in parallel with a resistive region would give. At B = 0, ρxy is only about 0.7–0.8 of 3h/7e² (Fig. 4f, Extended Data Fig. 4u) and falls to about 0.45 by 0.5 K. In device D2, which has the sharper ρxx minimum, ρxy reaches only about 0.86 of 3h/7e² (Extended Data Fig. 11a). The authors attribute this to "poor ρxy electrical contact quality", but the same D2 contacts give the integer states at 0.96–0.99 of quantization (Extended Data Fig. 11a,b); a contact problem that removes 14% from the fractional state and only 1–4% from the integer ones is not a consistent explanation, and the more natural reading is that the D1 value is coincidental. As it stands the 7/3 quantization is seen in one device only. The authors should: compute σxy and σxx from the measured tensor against ν, D, B and T around ν = 2/3 for both D1 and D2 on the same footing, with propagated uncertainty; show ρxy and ρxx (and σxy, σxx) versus ν at several fixed D and B values and state whether a σxy plateau of finite width exists, with its value, and whether σxx → 0 as T is lowered; give the lowest ρxx at base temperature in ohms; test the D2 contact explanation with alternative contact pairs or reversed configurations (Onsager reciprocity), and check whether the ρxy deficit of all D2 states scales with ρxx, as contact mixing would predict; and state explicitly that zero-field quantization is not observed and whether the state is a field-stabilized FCI rather than a fractional QAH state. If the claim does not survive this analysis, it should be reworded as, for example, "a correlated anomalous Hall state at ν ≈ 2/3 with ρxy approaching 3h/7e²", with the title and abstract adjusted.

2. **The Streda slope that fixes C = 7/3 is drawn by eye and not distinguished from nearby alternatives.** (Fig. 4g–i, Extended Data Fig. 6i–j.) The Chern number of the fractional state is read from a single dashed line of slope 7/3 overlaid on broad features. The competing slopes are close: C = 2 (an anomalous-Hall-crystal or CDW state with s = 2/3, which the authors' own mechanism 1 contains as a component), C = 5/2, C = 8/3 (νC for the C = 4 band) and C = 3. With n0 ≈ 1.11 × 10¹² cm⁻², Δν ≈ 0.022 C per tesla, so over 0–3 T slopes of 2, 7/3 and 8/3 differ in ν by only about 0.02. Without a fitted slope and intercept with error bars, the data do not single out 7/3, and the claim that σxy ≠ νC depends on exactly this distinction. The authors should fit the trajectory of the ρxx minimum (or of the σxy feature) in the (n, B) plane over the full 0–3 T range, at several D values and in both devices, and report t = dν/d(φ/φ0) and the intercept s with uncertainties, showing that t excludes 2, 8/3 and 3 and that the intercept sits at ν = 2/3 independent of D.

3. **The C = 5, 6 and 7 states near ν ≈ 3.2–3.3 are not established as distinct quantized Chern insulators.** (Fig. 3d,h–l, Extended Data Figs. 7, 8, 9 and 11b; abstract; p. 5.) The abstract states that all states from C = 2 to C = 7 "exhibit the quantum anomalous Hall effect", and the text describes "precise quantization". The target values h/5e², h/6e² and h/7e² (5.16, 4.30 and 3.69 kΩ) differ by only 15–20%, and in Fig. 3d the shaded windows assigned to them each span only about 0.02 in ν, with ρxx of about 1 kΩ, not zero. A ρxy that decreases continuously with ν while ρxx stays finite would produce exactly this sequence of apparent plateaus. The hysteresis loops (Fig. 3h–j, Extended Data Fig. 9d–f) are taken at points separated by Δν ≈ 0.06 and ΔD ≈ 0.03 V/nm, with ρxx around 0.1 of h/Ce² (several hundred ohms); the C = −2, −3 and 4 loops (Fig. 3e–g) show ρxx of 0.1–0.2 h/Ce², roughly 1–1.7 kΩ, and no ρxx(ν) or ρxy(ν) plateau is shown around the loop points. On warming, ρxy for C = 5–7 rises 15–25% above h/Ce² between about 0.5 and 2 K before collapsing near 2.3 K (Fig. 3l, Extended Data Fig. 9g–i). The paper calls this "completely anomalous" but does not explain it; for a single gapped Chern insulator, thermally activated dissipation reduces |σxy| and should not drive ρxy above h/Ce², whereas it is natural if conduction is shared with a neighbouring lower-|C| region (e.g. the adjacent C = 4 region) or if the base-temperature value is not a protected plateau. The states also sit at incommensurate fillings, are continuously distributed in ν, are hysteretic in ν (Extended Data Fig. 7d–e), have coercive fields of only ~10–20 mT, and have no reported activation gaps. In Fig. 3k the fan slopes are numerals placed on the derivative map, not fits, and at 1 T slopes 5, 6 and 7 differ by only about 0.02 in ν. The authors should give σxy and σxx (from the full tensor) for each state with ρxx in ohms at each hysteresis point; show σxy(ν) and σxx(ν) at base temperature with an explicit plateau-width criterion in ν and D; show fitted Streda slopes and intercepts with uncertainties for C = 5, 6, 7 (and for −2 to −5) at more than one D; measure several contact pairs; and explain how ρxy can exceed h/Ce² on warming. Otherwise the C = 5–7 region should be described as tentative, for example "anomalous Hall states with ρxy near h/Ce²".

## B. Should address

4. **Inhomogeneity, domain mixing or a partially polarized metal has not been excluded as the source of the fractional and high-C Hall values, and bulk, uniform character has not been shown.** (Fig. 4, Extended Data Figs. 1d, 4, 6, 10 and 11.) The 7/3 feature lies in a hysteretic region of the ν–D map close to boundaries between integer states with different C (C = 3/4 at ν = 1; C = 2/3 sectors elsewhere). A mixture of magnetic or Chern domains with different integer σxy between the Hall probes, with dissipative transport through domain walls, would give a fractional, non-plateau ρxy with ρxx of several kΩ. The energy scales point the same way: "Tc ≈ 3.3 K" for the 7/3 state comes from hysteresis (Extended Data Fig. 4c), whereas the activation gap is 0.026 meV (~0.3 K) in D1 and 0.016 meV in D2 (Extended Data Figs. 4o, 10c), and ρxy at B = 0 already halves by 0.5–1 K (Extended Data Fig. 4u), which looks more like a magnetic metal whose Hall response passes near 3h/7e² than an incompressible state. Twist-angle variation across the Hall bar could also mix C = 4 (ν = 1) regions with neighbouring states and produce intermediate ρxy. The authors should report ρxy and ρxx from several independent Hall and longitudinal contact pairs in D1 (Extended Data Fig. 1d shows more contacts than are used) and check whether the same value appears in each; estimate twist-angle variation across the bar (e.g. ν = 4 positions per contact pair); and add nonlocal or two-terminal versus four-terminal measurements in the C = 4 and C = 7/3 states, which probe chiral edge transport and, for 7/3, could distinguish fractional edge counting from a C = 2 or 3 state with dissipative bulk. A thermodynamic probe of incompressibility (capacitance, SET or chemical potential) at ν = 2/3 and at the incommensurate ν ≈ 3.2 fillings, or local magnetometry/imaging, would greatly strengthen the claims. Failing this, the conclusion should be phrased as a fractional anomalous Hall response rather than an established FCI.

5. **The filling-factor calibration is partly circular and internally inconsistent at the few-percent level that matters for identifying ν = 2/3 and the ν ≈ 3 commensurabilities.** (Extended Data Fig. 2, Methods "Twist angle determination", Fig. 4d.) The gate capacitances, and hence the ν axis, are calibrated from Landau-fan lines that are themselves assigned C = 4, 5 and 7 (Extended Data Fig. 2a,b), and Chern numbers are then read from slopes on that same axis. The ν = 4 density gives θ = 1.38°/1.37° (4n0 = 4.44 × 10¹² cm⁻²), whereas the Brown–Zak periods give λ ≈ 10.4/10.5 nm and θ = 1.36°/1.35°, a 2–4% mismatch in n0. A 4% rescaling moves the FCI peak, already at ν ≈ 0.68 in Fig. 4d, to about 0.70, and shifts the Streda slopes; the arguments that the ν ≈ 3 states sit at ν = 2 + n/4 depend on the calibration at the same level. The authors should calibrate capacitance independently of assumed Chern numbers (e.g. Shubnikov–de Haas oscillations away from the flat band, or the ν = ±4 band insulators alone), reconcile the fan-based and Brown–Zak twist angles, state which calibration defines ν and its uncertainty, and show how the position of the 2/3 feature moves with D and that it is pinned at 2/3 rather than at 0.68–0.70.

6. **Antisymmetrization at small fields may distort the maps of hysteretic states.** (Methods "Symmetrization"; Figs. 1f,g, 2a,b, 3a,b, 3c, 3d, 4a–e; Extended Data Fig. 7c.) The ν–D maps are antisymmetrized from B = ±0.1 T (±0.2 T for Fig. 2a,b), yet several states have coercive fields comparable to or above this: about 0.15 T for C = −2 and up to ~0.15 T for C = −3 (Fig. 3e,f), about 0.2 T for C = 4 near ν = 3 (Fig. 3g), about 0.1 T for C = 3 at ν = 1 and for the FCI (Fig. 4f). They also depend on the ν-sweep direction (Extended Data Fig. 7). If the magnetization does not reverse between +B and −B, the two maps are not time-reversed partners and antisymmetrization cancels or distorts the anomalous signal; if the two maps were taken with different ν histories, combining them can create spurious intermediate ρxy values, which bears directly on the fractional and C = 5–7 assignments and on the asymmetry between C > 0 and C < 0 regions (Fig. 3c vs. Extended Data Fig. 7c). The authors should state the field-training protocol (sweep order, fast axis, training at large |B| before each map), show raw unsymmetrized ρxy(+B) and ρxy(−B) maps for Figs. 3a,b and 4c and the key linecuts (Figs. 3d, 4d–e) for both training directions, and confirm that the conclusions survive with single-field, field-trained data.

7. **The "entanglement" in the title and the "entangled Chern insulators" in the text are neither defined nor evidenced.** (Title; Fig. 3 section heading, p. 5; p. 9; Methods pp. 17–18.) The title promises "Fractionalization and Entanglement of High Chern Insulators", but no measurement or calculation bears on quantum entanglement. On p. 5 "entangled" appears to mean coexisting, competing or spatially intertwined phases, and "valley-entangled" refers only to a property of the proposed (7,7,1) trial wavefunction, whose K-matrix K = −(7,1;1,7) is posited to fit the needed −1/3 rather than derived. Readers will take the word as a physical claim. 8. **The novelty claims are overstated relative to prior work.** (Abstract; main text pp. 1–2, 7–9; Conclusion.) (i) "Beyond all known FCIs": states with fractional Streda slope t and fractional intercept s outside LL-like sequences, including translation-symmetry-broken FCIs, were reported in graphene/hBN moiré (Spanton et al., Science 2018), in magic-angle graphene (Xie et al., Nature 2021) and in Harper–Hofstadter/moiré settings generally ("FCI + CDW" states); anomalous Hall crystals and the extended QAH states (Ref. 24) already give σxy ≠ νC; and both proposed mechanisms are built from known ingredients (a √3×√3 CDW plus a Laughlin 1/3 state, or a 4×1/2×2 CDW plus a Halperin state). (ii) Hysteretic Chern-sign switching with doping direction near ν = 3 has a close precedent in twisted monolayer–bilayer graphene (Polshyn et al., Nature 2020). (iii) The relation to closely related recent work, Refs. 38 (high-C FCIs) and 43 (moiré-dependent Chern insulators in twisted crystalline flatbands), needs to be spelled out. (iv) The "unprecedentedly high Tc ≈ 8.5 K in graphene" needs a stated criterion (onset of hysteresis versus loss of quantization) and a quantitative comparison with existing graphene QAH Curie temperatures, e.g. rhombohedral tetralayer and pentalayer on SOC or moiré substrates (Refs. 39–43). The authors should make these comparisons explicitly and moderate the claims and superlatives such as "unprecedented richness".

9. **The proposed mechanisms for σxy = 7/3 are qualitative, untested and in one case not obviously self-consistent.** (Main text pp. 8–9; Methods "FCI based on C = 4 parent band", p. 17; Extended Data Fig. 12.) In mechanism A, the C = 3 band is folded by a √3×√3 order into C = (0, 1, 2) sub-bands, each holding 1/3 electron per original cell; at ν = 2/3, filling the C = 2 sub-band uses 1/3 and the remaining 1/3 would completely fill the C = 1 sub-band in the folded zone, giving σxy = 3, not a Laughlin 1/3 state. The "unfold" step in Extended Data Fig. 12a assumes the remaining electrons do not feel the charge order that created the sub-bands, which needs justification. Mechanism B requires spontaneous intervalley particle–hole pairs at density 1/8 bound into a (7,7,1) Halperin state, with the exponents chosen to fit the slope, and no energetic argument for why it beats the fully polarized states HF finds. No many-body calculation (ED or DMRG of the projected TBRTG model at ν = 2/3), no energetics of either charge order and no experimental sign of either order is presented. The authors should provide such a calculation, or derive and test distinguishing predictions (e.g. a C = 2 anomalous Hall crystal at ν = 1/3 under mechanism A, other Diophantine lines in the Fig. 4g–i fans, commensurability features in the Brown–Zak or fan data, the sign or valley character inferred from hysteresis, √3×√3 versus 4×1 or 2×2 order), indicate which mechanism the data favour, or otherwise present the mechanisms as speculative.

10. **The activation gaps come from fits that do not support the quoted values, and they are inconsistent with the Curie temperatures.** (Fig. 3m; Extended Data Figs. 4m–r, 10c.) For C = 4 at ν = 1, Δxx ≈ 10.3 meV (~120 K) is fitted from points at 1/T ≈ 0.10–0.15 K⁻¹, i.e. T ≈ 7–10 K, straddling Tc ≈ 8.5 K (Extended Data Fig. 4a,m,s), where transport is governed by the loss of magnetic order rather than activation across a charge gap. The same mismatch appears for C = 4 near ν = 3 (1.6 meV, ~19 K, against Tc ≈ 4.5 K) and for C = 3 (0.6–1.4 meV against Tc ≈ 6 K). For the 7/3 state the gap is 0.026 meV (~0.3 K) in D1 and 0.016 meV in D2 against a quoted Tc ≈ 3.3 K, so gap and Tc differ by an order of magnitude in opposite directions for different states; and ρxx changes by less than a factor of two over the fitted range (Extended Data Figs. 4o, 10c), so an Arrhenius gap is not meaningful. The fits use few points over narrow windows with low-T saturation, and a 0.026 meV gap is barely resolved at a 30 mK base temperature. The authors should state fit windows and point counts, define Δ consistently (Δ/2kBT or Δ/kBT), show fits on log axes over at least a decade in ρxx, distinguish the magnetic ordering temperature from the charge gap and discuss the discrepancy, or drop numerical gaps where the fit is not justified.

13. **The quantitative agreement between the continuum model, HF and the data is weaker than stated, and the calculations' scope should be made explicit.** (Methods "Continuum Model" and "Hartree–Fock", pp. 15–16; Fig. 1c,d; Extended Data Fig. 5.) The C = 4 → 3 agreement (predicted D/ε0 ≈ 0.78 V/nm, observed ≈ 0.74–0.75 V/nm) uses a single linear ΔU ∝ D proportionality anchored at the CNP gap closing at D/ε0 ≈ 0.18 V/nm, ignoring layer screening (which makes ΔU sublinear at large D) and HF renormalization at ν = 1, so it is a one-parameter extrapolation rather than an independent prediction. The band parameters come from Ref. 59 (w1 = 110 meV, u0 = 0.8, γ values), which describes a different structure, and the interaction parameters ε = 6 and gate-screening length ξ = 10 nm are not justified against the 10–20 nm hBN thicknesses on each side; this sets U_ξ ≈ 24 meV, which controls polarization and the competition of states. In Extended Data Fig. 5b (C = 3, ΔU = 16 meV) the HF gap at E_F looks very small or the bands touch near Γ. HF is restricted to 3 bands per flavour with no translation breaking, gives only C = 4 at ν = 3, and so cannot address the ν ≈ 2.5–3.5 cascade, C = 5–7 or ν = 2/3. The 4× unit-cell (ν = 2 + n/4) explanation of the cascade is not compared with the measured Streda intercepts at ν = 2.61, 2.73, 3.09, 3.23 and 3.29; a fitted (t, s) table is needed to test whether s ∈ ℤ/4. The authors should show the sensitivity of the transition ΔU and Chern numbers to w1, u0, ε, ξ and band projection, state the robustness of the C = 3 HF insulator, say explicitly what the calculations do not capture, and reword "remarkably close" as approximate.
