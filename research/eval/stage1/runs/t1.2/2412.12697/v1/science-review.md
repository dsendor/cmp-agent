A. Must address

1. The tensor components are extracted with an isotropic inversion that does not hold for a uniaxial crystal, and every headline number depends on it. Methods (p. 9), Supplementary Note 2 and the Fig. 2 caption convert each (Ψ, Δ) pair, measured at a single 70° angle of incidence, with the isotropic two-phase formula ε = sin²θ[1 + tan²θ((1−ρ)/(1+ρ))²]. The stated justification is that the result is "dominantly governed by the dielectric function along the s-polarization". This is not generally correct. In the side-face geometry (Fig. S3, c along s), r_s depends on ε_c, but r_p depends only on ε_ab, because the in-plane p direction and the surface normal both lie in the ab-plane. Ψ = |r_p/r_s| and Δ therefore mix both components. The standard result (Aspnes, JOSA 70, 1275 (1980)) is that the pseudo-dielectric function is closest to the component along the intersection of the plane of incidence with the surface, not along s. On the top face, r_p also contains ε_c through the normal component; the mixing is weaker but not negligible near 3.8 eV, where |ε_ab| is very large. The error is largest exactly where the claims are made: ε_ab1 swings from about +25 to −20 and ε_ab2 reaches about 47 near 3.78–3.9 eV, and the c-axis Ψ and Δ in Fig. S4 show features near 4.0–4.2 eV, which suggests leakage of ε_ab into the "c-axis" spectrum. A few degrees of miscut or azimuthal misalignment of c relative to s would add further mixing, and neither is stated. Since |Δn|max = 3.67, |Δk|max = 5.21, n_ab,max = 5.66 and the |Δn| > 0.5 visible claim all come directly from these pseudo-dielectric functions, they are not yet established. The authors should invert both faces with the exact uniaxial Fresnel (Berreman/4×4) equations, jointly and point by point (no oscillator model is needed); measure the side face at two azimuths (c along s and c in the plane of incidence) and at several angles of incidence (e.g. 60–75°); use generalized or Mueller-matrix ellipsometry to check that off-diagonal conversion is absent; give the c-axis misorientation of the measured side face (e.g. from Laue); show that the recovered ε_c no longer contains the 4 eV feature; and report how |Δn|max, |Δk|max and n_ab,max change, with uncertainties. The corrected Δn(ω) and Δk(ω) should replace Fig. 2.

2. The claims of "transparency across the entire visible spectrum" and "without optical loss" (abstract, Fig. 3 caption) are contradicted by the paper's own data. Fig. S6 gives ln α ≈ 10.5–11.8 between 2 and 3.3 eV, i.e. α ≈ 4×10⁴ to 1.3×10⁵ cm⁻¹ and an absorption length of about 0.1–0.3 µm. Fig. 2d gives k_ab ≈ 0.1–0.3 across the visible, and the crystals are black (Fig. 1b, Fig. S1). Such a material is opaque at any thickness relevant to a waveplate or polarizer. The paper handles this by defining the "transparent region" as wherever ln α is linear, labelled an Urbach tail (p. 4; Fig. 3 caption). An Urbach tail is absorption, not transparency, and the slope of ln α from about 1–2 to 3.35 eV implies a characteristic energy of order 1 eV, far from a genuine band-tail value (tens of meV); this is broad extrinsic or defect absorption. The attribution to about 1.4% Cu²⁺ is taken from Ref. 18, not measured on these crystals, and the same defects may also change n in the visible. The definition also differs from that of the comparison materials in Fig. 3 (rutile, calcite, LiNbO3), whose α is of order 10⁻² to 10⁰ cm⁻¹. In addition, ellipsometry on thick crystals cannot determine k below about 0.05, let alone at the 10⁻³–10⁻⁴ level relevant to transparency. The authors should measure polarized transmission (and ideally retardance) on thin, polished, oriented plates of known thickness, ideally from lighter-coloured, annealed or low-Cu²⁺ crystals, to obtain α_ab(λ), α_c(λ) and Δn directly in the visible; determine the Cu²⁺ content of the measured crystals (EPR or XPS); show that the visible-range n_ab and n_c are intrinsic, for example by comparing crystals with different Cu²⁺ content and with the index of Ref. 16; and otherwise restate the claim, for example as "below the direct gap" or with a loss-aware figure of merit such as Δn/k or Δn per absorption length.

3. The exciton binding energy and the Eb/Eg ≈ 1/4 "2D signature" (Fig. 4a) rest on equating a single-oscillator Penn-gap parameter with the quasiparticle gap. Eg = 5.12 eV is the ω_p parameter of a one-oscillator (Penn/Wemple/Sellmeier) fit to ε_ab1 between 1.0 and 2.25 eV (Supplementary Note 4, Eq. 1, Fig. S7). This is an average oscillator energy weighted over all transitions, here dominated by the 3.78 eV exciton itself, and it lies above the gap in essentially all semiconductors. The fit window is narrow and noisy (it includes visibly scattered points near 1.1–1.25 eV, probably the M-2000 detector-switching region), sits where the defect absorption of concern 2 is present, and ε∞ and ω_p are strongly correlated; no uncertainty is quoted. Eb = Eg − E1s = 1.34 eV simply inherits this choice. Agreement with sc-GW (5.1 eV) and one BSE value (1.2 eV, Ref. 13) is coincidental support rather than a measurement: GW gaps for delafossites vary by more than 1 eV between references, and the other BSE study (Ref. 12) gives 0.47 eV. The 1/4 relation is an empirical correlation among 2D materials, not a test of dimensionality for one compound, and Fig. 4a itself places bulk MoTe2 and bulk CrSBr on the line. The authors should determine Eg independently (continuum onset from an Elliott-type fit or second-derivative analysis of the 7 K ε2, photoemission plus inverse photoemission or STS, or published GW+BSE), give error bars on Eg and Eb, and drop or heavily qualify the inference from Fig. 4a.

4. The 2D-versus-3D hydrogenic comparison (Fig. 4c,d; Supplementary Notes 6–7) and the assignment of the broad 4.47 eV feature to 2s and higher Rydberg states do not establish 2D confinement, so "conclusively demonstrate" (p. 6) is not supported. First, the models are not applicable: with Eb ≈ 1.34 eV and ε ≈ 5 the Wannier Bohr radius is about 1 Å to a few Å, comparable to or smaller than the Cu–Cu spacing (2.85 Å), and the paper itself calls the transition "intratom-like" Cu d→p, i.e. Frenkel-like; neither hydrogenic series holds without central-cell corrections. Second, the discrimination comes from the inputs, not from dimensionality: a pure 2D series (Eq. 3) would put 2s at Eg − Eb/9 ≈ 4.97 eV, closer to the gap than the 3D value (≈4.8 eV), so the 4.40 eV obtained comes entirely from the n-dependent effective screening ε_n (from Ref. 44) of the EMA construction. That construction has ad hoc inputs: a Cu-layer thickness of 2.038 Å called "half the dumbbell" (Cu–O is about 1.86 Å); a choice between two EMA roots justified by analogy with Al2O3, with an Al2O3 reference ε of 2.7 although sapphire's ε∞ is about 3.1 and the reported 2.34 matches neither; layer dielectric constants fitted at 0.74 eV, which already contain the exciton's own oscillator strength; and an unspecified reduced mass μ and assumed equal linewidths. The 3D competitor is isotropic and unscreened, a straw man; an anisotropic 3D or fractional-dimension model (Ref. 41) with the same number of parameters is the fair comparison. Third, alternative assignments of the 4.47 eV hump are not excluded: continuum onset, interband transitions at other k-points (Fig. 5a shows several flat valence bands), phonon sidebands, or Cu²⁺/defect bands. In Fig. S8 the hump does not sharpen on cooling while the 1s narrows strongly, and no 2s peak is resolved at 7 K, which argues against a Rydberg state; its integrated weight relative to 1s is not compared with the ideal 2D ratio f2s/f1s = 1/27. The authors should state μ and all fixed and free parameters, give the resulting ε_n, show how the 2s position depends on layer thickness, the EMA root and the ε inputs, compare oscillator strengths quantitatively, show 7 K second-derivative spectra and 7 K versions of Fig. 4b–d, and preferably replace the hydrogenic argument with the calculation of concern 5. Otherwise "conclusively demonstrate" should become "is consistent with".

B. Should address

5. The theory does not test the mechanism quantitatively. Fig. 5 shows only GGA band projections and Bloch functions at one k-point (L); no ε_ab(ω), ε_c(ω) or momentum matrix elements are computed, even at the RPA level. The statement that c-polarized transitions are "forbidden" is argued from atomic-orbital parity, not from the little group at L or computed matrix elements; an on-site d→p transition is parity-allowed for both polarizations (dz2 → pz is allowed for E∥c), so the c-axis suppression depends on the energy ordering of pz versus px/y, which GGA without +U may misplace. Fig. 5c shows only faint Cu px/y weight in the lowest conduction band along L–F and Fig. 5d sizable O px/y weight, while Cu 4s, commonly reported at the delafossite conduction-band minimum, is not projected; with O and Cu-s admixture the z-polarized matrix element is generically non-zero. The conduction band in Fig. 5a also disperses appreciably along Z–Γ, which is not obviously compatible with a 2D-confined state. The primitive cell has 4 atoms, so GW+BSE is not "beyond the scope", and Refs. 12, 13 and 48 have done it. The authors should compute the polarization-resolved dielectric tensor (RPA/IPA at minimum, GW+BSE preferably) and |⟨v|p_x,y,z|c⟩|² along L–F; plot the exciton wavefunction and its extent along c and in-plane; report band masses along c and in-plane (which also gives μ for concern 4); compare peak positions and intensity ratios with Fig. 2 and with published BSE spectra; check whether GGA+U or a hybrid functional changes the dz2/4s/O pz composition of the bands at L, given the pseudopotential sensitivity noted in Note 7 (Ref. 13); and state the spin-orbit and U settings in Methods.

7. The link between two-dimensional confinement and the anisotropy is asserted, not argued. A dipole selection rule (dz2 → px/y allowed for E∥ab, with pz pushed up by the dumbbell) produces polarization selectivity whatever the exciton's dimensionality; conversely, a 2D-confined exciton need not be polarization-selective. The title ("enabled by ... two-dimensionally confined excitons") should make clear which ingredient carries the anisotropy. Ideally the authors would show, for example with a 3D anisotropic model having the same selection rule, that 2D confinement is needed to reach the measured Δn.

8. The beam footprint, surface condition and depolarization are not characterized. The crystals are 1–2 mm hexagonal pillars, and the side faces in Fig. 1b and Fig. S1 are visibly narrower, stepped and textured rather than optically flat. At 70° incidence the footprint is elongated about threefold, yet the paper does not state the spot size, whether focusing probes were used, or how overfill and collection of light from edges, the silver epoxy or the copper cone were excluded. Surface roughness, a Cu²⁺-rich or oxidized overlayer, and spill-over all reduce and distort the apparent ε2 peak and n, and hence n_ab,max and Δn_max; no roughness or overlayer layer is included, and "smooth and shiny" is not quantified. The authors should report the footprint relative to each facet, AFM roughness of both faces, and the depolarization spectra the M-2000 provides; include an AFM-constrained roughness/overlayer layer or show it changes the results negligibly.

12. The temperature-dependent exciton analysis, the Fano assignment and the linewidth argument are under-specified. The statement that a 200 meV room-temperature linewidth is "typical of low-dimensional systems" (p. 3) is not a diagnostic: linewidth reflects phonon coupling and inhomogeneity, not dimensionality. Supplementary Note 5 attributes asymmetry to "coupling between 1s exciton and higher-order exciton", but Fano interference requires a discrete state degenerate with a continuum, which is not identified; the asymmetry could also come from the component mixing of concern 1. Fig. S8 places the "Fano-like behavior" at 4.15 eV, where no distinct antiresonance is visible, while the main text assigns the Fano term to the 1s at 3.78 eV and discusses 4.47 eV. No fit parameters (energies, widths, q, uncertainties, which are fixed) are given for Fig. 4b or Fig. S9. The authors should tabulate the fit parameters at all four temperatures, fit E1s(T), Γ(T) and oscillator strength versus T (Toyozawa/Segall-type exciton–phonon coupling), report q and its T-dependence, justify the Fano term by showing that a Lorentzian or Toyozawa asymmetric lineshape fails, or drop it, and remove the linewidth-dimensionality inference.
