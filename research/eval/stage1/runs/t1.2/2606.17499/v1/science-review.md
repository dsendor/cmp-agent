A. Must address

1. The field-reversal SP-STS does not separate tip-spin contrast from field effects on the sample (Figs. 4f, 5k,l). The tip magnetization is reversed by reversing the out-of-plane field (+1 T, −1 T, +1 T in Fig. 4f; ±1.2 T in Fig. 5k), and each spectrum is recorded in that field. At ±1 T Fe(Te,Se) is in the mixed state with a vortex spacing of roughly 45–50 nm. Vortices can sit at different positions after each reversal, and can pin at the very defects studied. A vortex a few tens of nm away changes the in-gap DOS through supercurrent (Doppler) shifts, quasiparticle leakage and nearby vortex-core states. Field history (trapped flux, hysteresis) and the tip's own response could also change the spectra without any spin contrast. A further logical problem: if the impurity moments responsible for the in-gap states are paramagnetic and follow the field, tip and sample reverse together, so no SP contrast is expected even for a strongly spin-polarized state. Ref. [59], which the authors cite for MBS spin polarization, gives a vortex-MBS spin set by the field direction, so a field-induced MBS would also reverse with the tip. Hence the absence of contrast at P2 (Fig. 5l) does not show that P2 is not a spin-polarized MBS, and the contrast at the bend and at P1 requires the impurity moments to be pinned (by anisotropy or coupling), which the paper never shows. The Methods say the Cr tip is "dipped" into Fe on W(110) to make it switchable, but there is no demonstration in the main text that the tip reverses between ±1 T, or that it does not switch at lower fields during a sequence; Figs. S7/S8 were not available. Because the claim that the bend and P1 states are spin-polarized YSR states is the central new result, the authors should: repeat the identical field sequence at the same atomic positions with a non-magnetic (W or PtIr) tip, or a Cr tip without the Fe coating; repeat it with the same SP tip on a defect-free reference area, where no contrast is expected, and on a known Fe-adatom YSR state; image the vortex lattice (low-magnification zero-bias maps) at each field step so the distance to the nearest vortex is known; state the tip switching field and show the tip reverses as assumed; state whether the impurity moments are expected to be pinned or field-following and what each scenario (YSR or MBS) predicts for the contrast; and rewrite the P2 conclusion accordingly.

3. The abstract's claim for step edges and Type I wrinkles is not supported by spin-resolved data. The abstract states that "by using spin-polarized scanning tunneling spectroscopy, we demonstrate that the near-zero-energy localized states on step edges and line defects originate from topologically trivial Yu-Shiba-Rusinov states", but SP-STS is shown only at the Type II bend (Fig. 4f) and the subsurface sites (Fig. 5k,l). The step-edge assignment (Fig. 2) rests on spin-averaged line spectra and a toy model; the wrinkle assignment (Fig. 3a–d) rests on one cut showing "two peaks with symmetrical energy", a generic signature, with no field or spin dependence. The YSR (magnetic) assignment is not unique: in an s± superconductor nonmagnetic scatterers, including edges and structural defects, can also bind in-gap states, so the defining test of magnetism is exactly the spin- or field-resolved measurement that is missing here. The step-edge "unpaired electrons" picture of Fig. 1j is likewise not supported by any measurement. The authors should either add SP-STS (with the controls of concern 1) and field-dependent spectra (0–3 T, non-spin-polarized) at the step-edge ZBP (path B, point 0, Fig. 2a) and at the wrinkle hotspot (white box, Fig. 3a,b,d), or restate these assignments as "consistent with" model-based interpretations and narrow the abstract and Discussion to the bend and P1.

The conclusion that the defect-free-region ZBPs "cannot be attributed to the presence of Majorana bound states" (abstract) and that "none of them can be explained by MBS" (Discussion) is not supported, especially for point 2. For P1 the argument is the bias-dependent shift along line C, which appears only in the last ~2 nm of the line (yellow region, Fig. 5i,j). The P2 peak does not disperse, so the case rests entirely on the absent field response in Fig. 5l: changes of a few percent, no error bars, and a field protocol different from P1's. The authors themselves note (p. 10) that a deeper defect reduces spin sensitivity and that "other possible explanations … may exist", so a null result of unknown sensitivity cannot discriminate MBS from a trivial state (see also concern 1 on co-reversal of tip and sample spin). The paper also does not address proposals that interstitial Fe in Fe(Te,Se) can host zero-energy modes without an applied vortex ("quantum anomalous vortex" scenarios; cf. Ref. 47), for which a magnetic subsurface impurity is the expected host rather than evidence against MBS. The authors should measure P2 with the same repeated ±1.2 T reversal protocol as P1, show full spectra at each field, quantify the MBS spin polarization expected at P2 for the measured tip polarization so a null result can be judged against a detection threshold, discuss the anomalous-vortex scenario, and bring the abstract and Discussion in line with the cautious statement on p. 10.

B. Should address

5. The setpoint normalization of the SP spectra is not addressed. Spectra are stabilized at V_stab = −10 mV / −300 pA (Fig. 4f) or −40 mV (Fig. 5) with a spin-polarized tip. Any spin polarization of the integrated LDOS between 0 and V_stab changes the tip height between field polarities and rescales the whole spectrum, which can create an apparent bias-dependent "polarization", including a sign change; the rigid offset at position 3 of Fig. 4f is suggestive. The claimed sign reversal within ±0.8 mV and the non-zero crossing energies at positions 1 and 2 (p. 9) are sensitive to this. The authors should show full-range spectra for both polarities, show that the background and coherence peaks overlap after normalization (or normalize by the conductance outside the gap), repeat at several setpoint voltages, and quote the crossing energies with error bars.

6. There is no positive control, so no discriminating criterion is demonstrated. The title promises to distinguish Majorana zero modes from trivial states, but the paper shows only cases it classifies as trivial, and the criterion changes from site to site: peak splitting (step edge), spatial localization along the defect (wrinkle), phase shift ≠ π plus SP sign reversal (bend), dispersion and SP response (Fig. 5). Without seeing what a candidate MBS looks like in SP-STS with the same tip and protocol, the reader cannot judge whether the method would ever return a "Majorana" verdict. The obvious reference is a vortex-core ZBP in the same sample, which the paper compares with only at the level of linewidth (Ref. [58]). The authors should add SP-STS on a vortex-core ZBP, or at minimum state the YSR-versus-MBS predictions explicitly and apply them uniformly to each defect class in a table; otherwise the title should be revised.

7. Field-magnitude and temperature dependences, which would give a tip-independent discriminator, are missing. All data are at 1.1 K, and non-spin-polarized field dependence is not used although fields up to 3 T are available. A YSR state would shift or split with Zeeman energy whereas an MBS stays pinned, and the temperature dependence of the peak widths tests whether the step-edge "splitting" is genuine. The P1/P2 ZBP (HWHM about 0.5 mV, called "comparable to the ZBP observed in vortex cores") and the step-edge ZBP are exactly the cases where this would help. The authors should add spectra at several fields (for example 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3 T) and temperatures at the step edge (path B), P1 and P2.

8. The step-edge "ZBP splitting" is at or below the energy resolution, and the toy-model fit is partly circular (Fig. 2d–f). The model uses Lorentzians at ±0.14 mV, a separation of 0.28 mV, while at T = 1.1 K thermal broadening alone is about 3.5 k_BT ≈ 0.33 mV FWHM, before lock-in broadening and any electron-temperature excess. The claimed split in the first six spectra of Fig. 2d is therefore an interpretation of peak shape and weight, not a resolved splitting. Fig. 2f is called a fit ("to get the best fit, we used two different coupling strengths for spectra 1-2 and 3-6"), yet the caption says the peaks are fixed at ±0.14 mV with heights "proportional to the first 6 points shown in e", so the intensities are reproduced by construction, with extra free parameters for six spectra; data and model are never overlaid. The "spectral weight transfer from negative to positive energy" argument (p. 6) is not unique to YSR (tip DOS, set-point normalization or a nearby distinct state can do it), and in Fig. 2e the ±0.14 mV traces appear to decay together. The authors should state the effective energy resolution (including V_osc and electron temperature, for example from a reference gap edge), fit the measured lineshapes with resolution-convolved models comparing a single zero-energy peak against two split peaks with a goodness-of-fit criterion, give all model parameters (J, impurity positions, broadening) and how many were fitted, overlay data and model, clarify the weight-transfer claim, and ideally check another step edge.

10. The arguments excluding Andreev bound states and topological edge states are not sound (p. 6). The statement that Fe(Te,Se), as an s± superconductor, has "no quasiparticles inside the gap causing Andreev reflection" is not a valid exclusion: Andreev reflection does not require in-gap quasiparticles, and in a sign-changing s± state, scattering at a surface or step that mixes electron and hole pockets, or at nonmagnetic defects, can create in-gap Andreev-type bound states. That ABS "require two reflection walls" applies only to particular confined geometries; single-surface bound states exist for sign-changing order parameters. Excluding topological edge states because peaks are seen "instead of an enhanced DOS within the whole superconducting gap" overlooks finite-size effects and the geometry here, a 1.23 nm step of two triple layers rather than a true sample boundary. This mechanism is a real competitor at step edges. The authors should correct these arguments and support the exclusion with data, for example dependence on step height or edge orientation across several edges, model calculations, or a magnetic-versus-nonmagnetic test (Zeeman response or SP-STS).
