## A. Must address

1. **There is no direct evidence that the resistance bistability is a stacking transition, or that the RB-mixed state contains rhombohedral tetralayer.** The central claim is a "sliding-induced first-order structural phase transition between Bernal and rhombohedral tetralayer graphene". It rests on three things. The first is the R_xx hysteresis and jumps (Fig. 1c, Fig. 2). The second is the absence of clear quantum oscillations in the hole-doped state, attributed to superposed oscillations from the two domain types (Extended Data Fig. 3). The third is the analogy to imaging studies on other samples (Refs. 27, 28), together with qualitative agreement with a calculation (Fig. 3b). No structural probe of device A is shown in either state. The only real-space image (Fig. 1b, KPFM) comes from a separate heterostructure on SiO2 with no gates, and Extended Data Fig. 2a comes from a stack still on the stamp. These images show that the starting material has mixed domains, not that gating changes them in this device. Missing quantum oscillations do not discriminate well, since domain-wall scattering, disorder, lower mobility or inhomogeneous current paths also suppress them. As it stands, the data are also consistent with purely electronic bistability in a fixed domain landscape, for example hysteresis between competing correlated states, which is well documented in rhombohedral multilayers. Contact- or edge-related switching is another possibility: the switching occurs "only in part of the device" (Extended Data Fig. 4), and the jumps change sign and magnitude between contact pairs (Extended Data Fig. 7). Because the switching is said to be domain-wall motion within a fixed mixed-domain landscape, the authors need to show that the rhombohedral area in the device actually changes between the two states. Roughly in order of strength, they could:
   - (i) image a device after setting it into each state, with spatially resolved Raman (the 2D-band lineshape distinguishes ABAB, ABCA and ABCB), KPFM or nano-IR/near-field imaging. A sister device, a thin top hBN without top graphite, or room-temperature imaging after a low-temperature switching cycle would all be informative.
   - (ii) show a rhombohedral-specific electronic signature in the RB-mixed state that is absent in the B-dominant state. Examples are a Landau-level or quantum-oscillation sequence matching ABCA tetralayer, Fourier spectra of R_xx(1/B) in both states at comparable |n| whose frequencies can be identified with rhombohedral and Bernal Fermi-surface areas, or the large-|D| gap at charge neutrality. For the gap, compare the neutrality resistance and its temperature dependence in Fig. 2b and 2c inside the boxed regions.
   - (iii) estimate quantitatively how much rhombohedral area must change to produce the observed ΔR_xx.
   - (iv) show a control on a region or device with no pre-existing rhombohedral domains (pure Bernal), fabricated and measured in the same way. At present the text cites the literature for the absence of switching in single-domain samples (Refs. 20, 23, 40) instead of showing its own control.

2. **The stacking identity of the domains in device A, which supplies essentially all main-text data, is not established, and a polar stacking would change the interpretation.** The Methods (Device Fabrication) state that device A was assembled with a "nearly-180°" rotation and that "one of the layers underwent an additional global shift". An AB bilayer placed on an AB bilayer at about 0° gives ABAB and ABCA domains at the low-energy registries. Rotating the top Bernal bilayer by 180° turns it into its "AC" partner (the D3d symmetry of a Bernal bilayer has no C2z). Simple registry counting then gives ABAC/ABCB-type domains, the non-centrosymmetric, spontaneously polarized tetralayer polytypes discussed in the authors' own Ref. 37. The "global shift" invoked to recover ABAB/ABCA is asserted but not characterized or demonstrated, and Fig. 1b is not from device A. Polar domains in device A would matter in three ways. (a) The 22 mV KPFM contrast (Extended Data Fig. 2), which the authors read as a work-function difference, is of the order expected for polar-domain contrast. (b) Polar regions break inversion symmetry and would give a D-odd AHE without any domain-wall physics. (c) The switching could then be sliding-ferroelectric domain motion, not a Bernal-rhombohedral transition. Each is an alternative to one of the paper's central interpretations. The authors should give the stacking-registry argument explicitly for the 0° and 180° assemblies and explain how the global shift was identified. They should determine the domain stacking in device A directly, for example by Raman 2D lineshape, by SHG (non-zero only for the polar stacking) or by KPFM under applied D on the device, and state which of devices A and B has which domain types. They should check whether the charge-neutrality offset D0 differs between the two states, since a polar stacking would shift it. They should also state whether device B (0.03° twist, which naturally gives ABAB/ABCA) reproduces the full switching phase diagram, and not only the AHE sign reversal.

3. **The paper does not show that the two states have the same carrier density and displacement field at the same gate voltages, so gate-history artifacts are not excluded.** The switch into the RB-mixed state is triggered only at the extreme of the gate range. In Fig. 1c the backward-sweep jump at n ≈ −15.5×10^12 cm^-2 sits essentially at the left edge of the scan, and the Fig. 2 protocol resets the device by "doping to the maximum hole density". Large gate excursions are a classic cause of charge injection into, or trapping in, the hBN, the graphite/hBN interfaces or the contacts. Trapped charge shifts n0 and D0 and produces hysteretic, history-dependent, apparently nonvolatile transport and Hall signals that relax at a threshold. The Methods calibrate n0, D0, Ct and Cb only "from the Landau levels in the B-dominant state". The paper therefore does not show that the same (Vt, Vb) gives the same n and D in the RB-mixed state, yet that is the premise of "recorded at exactly the same gate voltage" (Fig. 4c vs 4d, 4g vs 4h) and of every boundary in Fig. 3a. Even without trapping, the two stackings differ in quantum capacitance and layer polarization, which would shift n and D at fixed voltages. The authors should:
   - extract Landau fans, or the Hall density and the charge-neutrality-point position, in both states at the same gate voltages, and show that n0, D0 and the capacitances agree;
   - show that the −15.5×10^12 cm^-2 threshold is an intrinsic switching density and not the limit of the accessible gate range, by sweeping further or by showing how the threshold depends on D, sweep rate and dwell time;
   - show that the threshold follows n and D, not the absolute voltage on either gate, for example by plotting it against Vt and Vb separately (the Fig. 2a vs 2e comparison partly addresses this);
   - report gate leakage currents at the extreme voltages;
   - state explicitly why trapped charge could not produce the mirror-symmetric, multi-contour structure of Fig. 3a. If the contours follow constant ΔF, that is a usable discriminator.

## B. Should address

4. **In a strongly inhomogeneous, multi-domain conductor, the Hall measurement as presented does not by itself establish the "ferromagnet" state or the sign of its AHE.** The paper says that the R_xx jumps differ "in magnitude and sign" between contact pairs (Extended Data Fig. 7, p. 5), that switching occurs "only in part of the device" (Extended Data Fig. 4), and that current paths are "complex". Under these conditions the transverse voltage at a single Hall pair mixes longitudinal and Hall components. A hysteretic longitudinal magnetoresistance of a switching domain can appear in R_yx through inhomogeneous current paths. In a Hall bar only partly covered by magnetic patches, the sign and magnitude of the signal at a given pair depend on where the magnetic region sits relative to the probes. That could produce an apparent D-odd sign without intrinsic inversion breaking, and could explain why |R_yx| is "significantly larger" than in pure rhombohedral tetralayer. For hysteretic data, antisymmetrization removes R_xx mixing only if the forward sweep at +B is paired with the reversed backward sweep. Mis-pairing would distort the loop shape and the remanence. The field sweeps reach only ±0.15 T, which barely exceeds the coercive field of about 0.1 T (Fig. 4c,g), so it is unclear whether the loops saturate and whether the high-field branches merge. The maps in Fig. 4a,b,e,f were taken at a single field, B = 0.03 T, below the coercive field. They cannot have been antisymmetrized, a small contact misalignment matters when R_xx (~10–100 Ω) is comparable to R_yx, and their sign depends on the field and magnetization history before each line, which is not stated. The authors should:
   - state which Hall pair was used in Fig. 4, and show R_yx(B) loops for both signs of D on at least two independent Hall pairs in the same device, including the opposite side of the Hall bar;
   - show raw, unsymmetrized R_yx and R_xx loops together, and describe exactly how hysteretic loops were antisymmetrized;
   - extend the field range until the loops saturate;
   - state the field-training protocol for each map, and show the B = 0 remanent R_yx after training at +B and at −B at the diamond and star points;
   - check Onsager reciprocity under current and voltage contact permutation, and report the Hall angle or σ_xy as well as R_yx;
   - show that the B-dominant state has no hysteresis over the same field range at the identical gate point, and at temperatures below 1.5 K if available.

   If the D-odd sign survives on several contact pairs and in device B, which has a different geometry, the conclusion is much stronger.

   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

5. **The domain-boundary origin of the D-odd AHE (abstract, p. 7) is an untested hypothesis, and simpler explanations of the sign reversal are not excluded.** The positive-D and negative-D loops are taken at non-mirrored points: the diamond at about (n, D/ε0) ≈ (1.3, +1.0) and the star at about (1.4, −1.13). The maps in Fig. 4e show both AHE signs within the negative-D region, so the AHE sign evidently varies with (n, D). The RB-mixed state at +D and at −D is also prepared along different gate paths (Fig. 2a vs 2e; Fig. 4 caption, "going along the edge of the gate boundary... for each line"). Since domain-wall motion is the proposed switching mechanism, the two signs may belong to two different domain configurations, not to one state under D → −D. The arguments against extrinsic inversion breaking are suggestive but weak against a test as sensitive as the AHE sign. Those arguments are the absence of moiré features in R_xx, the D-mirror symmetry of the phase boundary, and reproduction in device B. The mirror symmetry constrains the structural energetics, not the Berry-curvature sign of a magnetic state that can respond to small layer-asymmetric perturbations. Reproduction in device B does not exclude a systematic asymmetry, because both devices were built the same way (graphite/hBN bottom gate, PC pickup, top gate on top). No hBN-graphene alignment angles are given. The paper also gives no symmetry analysis showing that an ABCA/ABAB boundary (a partial dislocation at the 2–3 interface) breaks inversion in the relevant sense. Nor does it estimate how quasi-1D boundary regions could give a Hall signal larger than that of pure rhombohedral tetralayer (Ref. 23). The polar-stacking alternative (Concern 2) must be excluded before domain-wall physics is invoked. The authors should:
   - take loops at exactly mirrored (n, ±D) points, preferably several pairs, reached from a single prepared RB-mixed state, for example by sweeping D continuously through zero at fixed n within the bistable window, or by reaching +D and −D through paths related by exchanging the top and bottom gates, with R_xx checked to be the same at both;
   - test whether the sign reversal occurs exactly at D = 0 or is offset, that is, whether the sign follows D or the layer that holds the carriers;
   - report the hBN alignment angles (from crystal edges or SHG), the hBN thicknesses and C_t/C_b;
   - make the hypothesis falsifiable by using the intermediate domain states (the multiple jumps, blue dashed lines in Fig. 3a). If the D-odd AHE comes from domain walls, its magnitude should track the wall configuration, while the D-even component (Fig. 4i) should track the rhombohedral area;
   - supply the symmetry analysis and the magnitude estimate.

   The key device-B data, a mirrored-point loop pair, should appear in the main text. Until such tests exist, the Abstract and the Discussion ("establishes domain boundaries ... as unique and pristine platforms") should present the domain-boundary origin as a conjecture.

   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

6. **The theory comparison in Fig. 3b is described as quantitative ("agree well", "remarkable"), but the parameter choice, the ensemble and the comparison itself are unclear.** The model has two free parameters. ΔΦ is taken from KPFM (22 mV) on a different sample, on SiO2 or on the stamp, at room temperature and in ambient conditions. ΔU(0,0) is "explained in the Supplementary Information". If ΔU(0,0) was fitted to the experimental boundary, the agreement is partly by construction. Nothing shows that ΔU is in fact constant along each experimental contour. It is not stated whether ΔU is evaluated at fixed (n, D) or at fixed (Vt, Vb). At fixed gate voltages the relevant thermodynamic potential includes the electrostatic work done by the gates, and the two stackings have different quantum capacitance. The calculation appears to be a non-interacting tight-binding band energy. It is unclear whether it includes self-consistent interlayer screening of D, which strongly reduces the rhombohedral gap. The region of interest at large |D| and small n (the black boxes in Fig. 3a) hosts spin-valley-polarized correlated phases, whose interaction energies (of order meV per carrier) lower the rhombohedral energy and could shift the contours. Only the RB-to-B boundary is modelled. The B-to-RB transition lies at or near the gate-range limit, so the two spinodal-like boundaries cannot bracket ΔF = 0. The authors should state which parameters are fitted and how ΔU(0,0) was obtained. They should plot the calculated ΔU along each experimental contour with its spread, showing that each blue dashed line maps to a constant ΔU and that successive lines are ordered consistently. They should show the sensitivity of the contours to ΔΦ within the KPFM uncertainty, to ΔU(0,0) and to the tight-binding parameter set, clarify the ensemble, and include screening, or discuss how screening and interactions would change the large-|D| rhombohedral pocket.

   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

7. **The switching is characterized only at 1.5 K, and "nonvolatile" is not quantified.** A first-order transition governed by domain-wall pinning (pp. 3, 5) predicts a hysteresis width and thresholds that depend on temperature, and possibly on sweep rate and dwell time. No such data are shown. Retention is shown only over about 300 s, with roughly six or seven cycles (Fig. 1d). Extended Data Fig. 9 is cited for "many cycles" without a cycle count, a drift figure or a failure rate. Fig. 3a and Fig. 4 indicate that the device is B-dominant at Vt = Vb = 0. The RB-mixed state therefore persists only while non-zero gate voltages are held inside the bistable window, which is weaker than the usual meaning of non-volatility in memory, where the state is kept with no bias applied. For "toggling quantum phases", it also matters whether the RB-mixed state survives warming above the ~8 K Curie temperature and recooling at fixed gates. If it does, the magnetism can be reset independently of the stacking. The authors should show:
   - the switching thresholds and hysteresis width versus temperature, at least up to and above ~8 K and ideally to tens of kelvin, to establish that the structure and not the magnetism drives the bistability;
   - the dependence on sweep rate and dwell time;
   - retention of each state over hours at the AHE operating point;
   - the number of cycles and the drift of the R_xx levels;
   - a warming and recooling test at fixed gates.

   They should also state which gate voltages must be held.

   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **Reproducibility across devices and contacts is unclear.** All main-text data come from device A, and device B (0.03° twist) appears only for the AHE sign reversal (Extended Data Figs. 10–11). The text does not say whether device B shows the gate-driven structural switching, the Fig. 2/3 boundary and the on/off AHE. In device A the switching occurs only in part of the device (Extended Data Fig. 4), and the R_xx jumps differ in sign between contact pairs. Since fabrication and relaxation set the domain landscape, device-to-device variation of the switching densities is central to the "on-demand toggling" claim. The authors should state how many devices or regions were fabricated and measured, and how many showed switching, preferably as a table listing devices, twist angles, cooldowns, the features each shows and the cycle counts. They should show the key Fig. 4 comparison (same gate voltages, hysteretic vs linear R_yx) in a second device or at a second Hall pair, and say whether the switching boundary and the AHE survive thermal cycling to room temperature, which could relax the domain configuration.

9. **The assumption that switching occurs at constant ΔF, used to extract thermodynamic quantities, is not justified.** The text (pp. 3, 5) identifies each R_xx jump with a ΔF = const contour. It also uses the field dependence of the switching boundary to extract a magnetic-susceptibility difference between the two structures (Extended Data Fig. 5 and the SI). This requires a domain-wall pinning barrier independent of n, D and B, and quasi-static wall motion without rate-dependent thermally activated creep. Neither is demonstrated (see Concern 7). The susceptibility result appears in the main text only as a statement and cannot be assessed as submitted. The main text should give the Clausius-Clapeyron-type relation used, its assumptions (constant barrier, field-independent pinning), and the resulting value with uncertainty, sign and a comparison with the expected orbital susceptibilities of ABAB and ABCA tetralayers. Otherwise the claim should be removed from the main text.

   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

10. **The "weak but finite" D-even AHE at the pentagon (Fig. 4i) may be an ordinary multi-carrier Hall effect.** The pentagon data show a nonlinear R_yx(B) with no hysteresis. In a mixed-domain sample where Bernal and rhombohedral regions with different carrier types and mobilities conduct in parallel, multiband ordinary Hall transport naturally gives a nonlinear R_yx(B) over ±0.15 T. The temperature dependence alone (Extended Data Fig. 8) does not separate the two. The authors should fit the pentagon data to a two-band ordinary Hall model over a wider field range as a null hypothesis and show any remanence. They should bring the temperature dependence into the main text or a provided supplement if the "two-component AHE" summary (p. 7) is kept.

    Raised by: r2, r3

11. **The rhombohedral fraction and the content of the two states are only qualitative.** The paper says "B-dominant" may contain small rhombohedral patches and domain walls (p. 2), and it does not quantify the rhombohedral fraction of the RB-mixed state. The claimed physics depends on how much rhombohedral area and how much wall length each state has: the AHE magnitude "significantly larger" than in pure tetralayer, and the proposed proximity effect. If a modest rhombohedral area fraction gives an AHE larger than that of a pure rhombohedral device, the measured R_yx is not a simple area average, and this needs explaining. The authors should estimate the area fractions, for example from quantum-oscillation amplitudes, from the Hall density, or from KPFM domain statistics of a comparable sample, and discuss the AHE magnitude in that light.

13. **Priority claim (p. 7).** The claim of "the first realization of a magnet-to-non-magnet nonvolatile phase transition controllable entirely by electrostatic means" should be qualified or removed. Gate-hysteretic switching between magnetic or Chern and non-magnetic correlated states, including AHE states, has been reported in moiré graphene and in graphene/hBN devices with ferroelectric-like gate hysteresis. Electrical switching of magnetic order has been reported in moiré Chern magnets. Ref. 45 is ionic, not purely electrostatic. The authors should discuss these works and state precisely what is new: switching driven by a change in stacking.
