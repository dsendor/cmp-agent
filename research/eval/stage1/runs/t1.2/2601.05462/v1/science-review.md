**A. Must address**

1. **A conventional AHE from a small out-of-plane magnetization component is not excluded, and the out-of-plane control is invalid as performed.** The observed signal is tiny: ρAHE ≈ 4.5–4.7 nΩ·cm (Fig. 1g), σAHE ≈ 0.10 S/cm with ρxx ≈ 200 μΩ·cm, a Hall angle of ~10⁻⁵. Fig. 1f is offered as showing that the conventional AHE is "negligible" for H ⊥ (110)O, but it covers only ±2–2.5 kOe. There M_⊥ is far from saturated (Fig. 1c: ~0.7 μB/Mn at 2 kOe against ~3.5 μB/Mn in plane; the demagnetizing field 4πMs ≈ 6–8 kOe). In this regime M_z is linear in H, so the AHE ∝ M_z is also linear and is removed together with the ordinary Hall term when "the linear component" is subtracted. Fig. 1f therefore gives no conventional AHE coefficient for this stack, and the reader cannot judge how much canting would produce the signal. With conventional σAHE of order 1–10 S/cm typical of LCMO films, an out-of-plane tilt of ~0.1–1° (or less) would suffice. Such a tilt could come from substrate miscut, or from an intrinsic tilt of the easy axis: the monoclinic distortion the authors invoke (c_p tilting toward a_p, β_LC − 90° ≈ 0.03–0.2°, only the mirror ⊥ [001]O surviving) allows M along [-110]O to cant toward [110]O, the film normal. That canting has the same symmetry as the proposed Ω_z and would equally give a square loop, the cos θ dependence of Fig. 3c, the φ independence of Fig. 3e, and a correlation with β_LC (Fig. 4f,g). The out-of-plane field rotations (Supplementary Figs. 15, 16) address misalignment of the applied field, not an intrinsic tilt of M, and cannot be judged from the main text. The authors should: measure ρxy for H ⊥ (110)O well beyond out-of-plane saturation (≥ 1 T, ideally up to 7 T) on the same trilayers and extract R_s (σAHE at full M_z); state the canting angle needed to reproduce the observed signal; bound the actual out-of-plane component when M lies along [-110]O independently (vector or rotating-sample magnetometry, out-of-plane SQUID remanence after in-plane saturation, polar MOKE, normal-incidence XMCD or PNR); report the substrate miscut angle and direction, ideally comparing samples with miscut along versus perpendicular to [-110]O; and bring the analysis of Supp. Figs. 15–16 into the main text with explicit fits that separate the two contributions, showing quantitatively that m_z times the conventional AHE coefficient is much smaller than the observed ρAHE. Without this, "in-plane AHE" and the Berry-curvature interpretation of Eq. (1) are not established.

2. **The symmetry argument that the CRO-induced monoclinic distortion is needed to activate the IP-AHE conflicts with bulk Pbnm symmetry and with the paper's own DFT and data.** On pp. 6–8 (Supplementary Fig. 7, Tables 1–2) the paper argues that the CRO/LCMO interfaces remove the mirrors M(100), M(010) and the axes 2[100], 2[010], lowering LCMO to P2₁/m (C2h), so that only the mirror ⊥ [001]O remains and M ∥ [-110]O then breaks it, "activating" Ω_z. But in bulk mmm a magnetization along [-110]O already breaks m⊥a, m⊥b, 2a and 2b, because each maps [-110] onto [110] or its reverse. What survives is m⊥c·T, 2c·T and inversion: the magnetic point group 2'/m' with c unique, the same group the authors obtain for the monoclinic cell. It allows a Hall pseudovector anywhere in the ab plane, including along the film normal [110]O. The DFT agrees: the orthorhombic supercell with β_LC = 90.00° already gives σDFT ≈ 4 S/cm (Fig. 4i,j), which vanishes only for the cubic cell without octahedral rotations, and the tCRO = 0 LCMO/NGO film shows a finite signal (Fig. 4a,f). So neither β_LC nor the CRO-induced removal of mirrors is the activating condition; at most the buffer changes the magnitude, and the "one-to-one correspondence" and the statements "unambiguously couples to the CaRuO3-buffer-induced mirror-symmetry breaking" (abstract), "β_LC can be seen as a reliable parameter for evaluating the degree of mirror-symmetry breaking" (p. 7) and the conclusion do not follow. The authors should give a full magnetic-point-group analysis for bulk Pbnm LCMO in the (110)O orientation and for the P2₁/m trilayer, with M along [-110]O, [001]O and [110]O; state which σ_ij components each allows and what, if anything, the reduced symmetry adds; show the single LCMO/NGO(110) film in the main text as the no-buffer reference (and, ideally, LCMO in an orientation such as NGO(001) where symmetry forbids the response); and recast the work as tuning the magnitude of an already-allowed IP-AHE through octahedral-rotation engineering rather than symmetry activation.

3. **Which layer carries the anomalous Hall current is not established; parallel conduction through, and Hall contributions from, the metallic CaRuO3 layers and the interfaces are not treated.** Each CRO buffer (16 u.c., ~6 nm in the main samples) is comparable to the 32 u.c. LCMO layer, CRO is metallic, and the total thickness ranges from 32 to 160 u.c. across the series. σAHE = ρAHE/ρxx² assumes one homogeneous conductor, and the thickness used to define ρ is not stated. In a parallel stack, the CRO thickness alone redistributes current and changes the apparent σAHE; the decrease at tCRO = 32 and 64 u.c. (Fig. 4f) is what shunting by thick CRO would produce, and shunting could alone generate a non-monotonic σAHE(tCRO). Moreover, the interfacial Ru⁴⁺→Mn⁴⁺ charge transfer invoked for the higher T_C (p. 5) and proximity to the ferromagnet imply interfacial Ru moments (Refs. 30–32) with strong SOC, and Ru-derived bands are known to give large, sign-changing Berry-curvature AHE. The authors themselves compare the sign reversal near 100 K (Fig. 4h inset) to SrRuO3, which points to a Ru-derived or interfacial contribution and to at least two competing terms, while the DFT models LCMO alone. The authors should: state how ρxx and σAHE are computed and show raw R_xy and R_xx for every sample; give ρxx(T) for every tCRO and for CRO-only reference films on NGO; model the stack as parallel channels to extract the LCMO-layer Hall conductivity; measure an in-plane Hall control on a CRO-only film; measure LCMO with a non-ruthenate, weak-SOC (or insulating) buffer that imposes a similar octahedral tilt, and CRO/LCMO bilayers with CRO only on top or only on the bottom; measure a t_LCMO series at fixed tCRO to test whether σAHE scales as a bulk or interface quantity; add element-specific interface magnetism (Ru/Mn XMCD); decompose σAHE(T) into the two contributions of opposite sign; and discuss whether the signal lives in LCMO, CRO or the interfaces.

4. **The gating experiment does not establish that the IP-AHE is switched through a structural change in LCMO, and its own structural data point the other way.** The XRD under gating shows the CRO(440) peak shifting while "the LCMO(440)o peak position remains nearly unchanged" (p. 11, Supplementary Fig. 24), yet σAHE drops to zero between 1.1 and 1.4 V (Fig. 5c,d). The proposed reduction of the LCMO tilt rests only on DFT for HxCaRuO3 (Supplementary Fig. 27) and a schematic inset (Fig. 5d); β_LC is not measured versus Vg. Simpler readings are not ruled out: the protonated layers are the CRO layers, which may carry the signal (concern 3); HxCaRuO3 is reported to become ferromagnetic (Ref. 48), so gating may add a magnetic layer with its own, possibly opposite-sign, AHE; and protonation changes CRO's conductivity and so the current split between layers, which alone changes ρAHE/ρxx² without any change in Berry curvature, as could changes in interfacial charge transfer and carrier density. A nearly unchanged H_C is not evidence of "nearly invariant ferromagnetism". It is also unclear how protons reach the bottom CRO through LCMO when manganite protonation is said to need > 2.5 V (p. 10), or whether the top CRO alone is responsible. The authors should measure RSMs (or at least the LCMO orthorhombicity) at Vg = 0 and 1.4 V and extract β_LC; report ρxx and M–H loops (Ms and remanence along [-110]O, by SQUID) versus Vg; gate a single LCMO/NGO film without CRO under the same conditions as a control, to test whether LCMO itself responds below 1.5 V; and confirm where the protons sit (e.g. a SIMS depth profile, or neutron reflectometry).

**B. Should address**

5. **The correlation between β_LC and σAHE across tCRO (Fig. 4f,g) is presented as causal, but other quantities co-vary with tCRO and the proposed explanation of the decrease is not demonstrated.** The text says T_C and M_s increase with tCRO through interfacial charge transfer (p. 5, Supplementary Fig. 4), and ρxx is tCRO-dependent (p. 8); carrier density, twin-domain fraction, strain relaxation, remanence ratio and loop squareness (visibly different across Figs. 4a–e) also change, and each could change σAHE without invoking β_LC. The structural change is tiny (β_LC − 90° spans ~0.03–0.2°) and the correlation is loose: β_LC differs by only ~0.05° between 8 and 16 u.c. while σAHE changes 3×, whereas 4 u.c. (≈ 0.13°) and 32 u.c. (≈ 0.08°) differ by ~4× in σ. The explanation for the decrease above 16 u.c. (strain relaxation and a/b twin domains, p. 9) is asserted, not shown; twinning would also cut the CRO-imposed axis relation and the uniaxial anisotropy. The authors should tabulate M_s, M_r/M_s, T_C, ρxx (10 K), twin fraction and β_LC for every tCRO; normalize the Hall signal by M_s or the remanent [-110]O magnetization (or give the anomalous Hall angle); plot σAHE directly against β_LC with both error bars and show that β_LC predicts σAHE better than the alternatives; and give RSM/STEM evidence of twinning and relaxation in the 32 and 64 u.c. samples.

7. **The Hall analysis procedure is under-specified.** It is not stated whether ρxy was antisymmetrized in H (or M) to remove ρxx, AMR and planar-Hall admixtures from contact misalignment, or only linearly background-subtracted. This matters especially for Fig. 1h, where M rotates away from E and the planar Hall term ∝ sin 2θ_M becomes nonzero during the loop. The linear background in Figs. 1g, 3d and 4 is said to be fitted at H > Hs ≈ 2 kOe, but the panels span only ±0.4–1 kOe. For in-plane fields an ideal geometry gives no ordinary Hall voltage, so the origin of the linear slope (Fig. 1f; field misalignment or an out-of-plane field component) needs explaining. The authors should describe the symmetrization, give the fit range and fitted slope, and compare the slope with the ordinary Hall coefficient.

8. **The angular dependence in Fig. 3c is taken below saturation and does not test the "dipolar Ω" claim.** The caption gives ρAHE at H = 1000 Oe while the text (p. 8) says "saturated ρAHE at H = 2000 Oe". With Hs ≈ 2000 Oe along the hard axis [001]O, at 1000 Oe M is not along H for most θ, so the cosine reflects the projection of the pinned M onto [-110]O, which any M_[-110]-odd mechanism (including concern 1) would give. The text also says ρAHE "decreases gradually to zero" at θ = 90° and then that it is "finite for 0° < θ ≤ 90°", and the phrase "residual M component pinned to the [-110]O easy axis" is unclear. The authors should state the field used, measure at fields large enough to saturate M along H (or plot ρAHE against the measured M_[-110](θ)), and fix the inconsistent statements.

11. **The DFT comparison is qualitative and its parameters and construction are not justified.** σDFT ≈ 4–9 S/cm (Fig. 4i–k) is roughly 50–100 times the measured ≤ 0.1 S/cm (103.7 mS/cm), a gap that is not discussed.
