**A. Must address**

1. **The symmetry argument does not show that the monoclinic distortion is needed, and the authors' own DFT contradicts it.** The paper argues (pp. 4–5, Fig. 2i) that bulk Pbnm LCMO keeps mirrors (M(100), M(010)) that forbid a Berry-curvature component along the film normal [110]_O, and that the interface-induced lowering to P2_1/m (C2h) "activates" the IP-AHE. For M ∥ [1̄10]_O, however, the magnetization itself already breaks M(100), M(010), 2[100] and 2[010]. The magnetic point group of orthorhombic Pbnm LCMO with M ∥ [1̄10]_O is then the same as the one the authors find for the monoclinic cell (1, 1̄, 2'[001], m'(001)). That group forbids only Ω along [001]_O and allows any Ω in the (001)_O plane, including along [110]_O. Physically, Ω_x ∝ M_x and Ω_y ∝ M_y with different coefficients because a_O ≠ b_O, giving Ω·[110] ∝ (χ_aa − χ_bb)M ≠ 0 with no monoclinic tilt. For M ∥ [001]_O the unprimed m(001) survives and forbids Ω along [110]; this agrees with Fig. 1e and with the NGO(001) control (Supp. Fig. 23), but neither discriminates, because Ω ∥ c is forbidden for in-plane M in orthorhombic symmetry as well. Fig. 3h and the text confirm this: σ_AHE-DFT "remains finite in all the orthorhombic supercells, even for the one with β_LC = 90.0°", at about 2 S/cm, and is zero only for cubic LCMO without octahedral rotations. What the data and DFT support is that the a⁻a⁻c⁺ rotation pattern with (110) orientation allows the effect, and that β_LC (or the OOR and Jahn–Teller amplitudes it stands for) tunes the magnitude of an already-allowed response rather than switching on a forbidden one. This changes the central framing of the title, abstract and Figs. 1b and 2i ("symmetry-engineered", "activates IP-AHE", "only remaining mirror plane") and how the t_CRO and gating results are read. It also leaves the near-zero signal of the single LCMO/NGO(110) layer (β_LC − 90° ≈ 0.03°, t_CRO = 0) unexplained. The authors should give an explicit magnetic-point-group analysis (a short table) for M ∥ [1̄10]_O in Pbnm, P2_1/m and the cubic phase, stating which Hall-pseudovector components each allows; reconcile it with the finite DFT result at β_LC = 90.00°; separate in DFT the effect of β_LC at fixed OOR amplitude from that of the OOR amplitude at β_LC = 90°; reframe the mechanism accordingly; and explain why a change in β of about 0.2° gives a roughly 30-fold change in σ_AHE (Fig. 3f) if symmetry is not the controlling factor.

2. **An out-of-plane magnetization component, from intrinsic canting or misalignment, is not quantitatively excluded.** Any symmetry that allows Ω along the normal for M ∥ [1̄10]_O also allows a static canting of M out of the film plane, since both are axial, time-odd vectors; the C2h symmetry proposed in Fig. 2i explicitly mixes M along [1̄10]_O and along [110]_O. A small out-of-plane canting of the remanent magnetization (for example from an easy axis tilted with the monoclinic c_p axis) would reverse with the in-plane component and give a conventional AHE with exactly the square hysteresis of Fig. 1f. The measured signal is tiny (ρ_AHE ≈ 5 nΩ·cm, σ_AHE ≈ 0.1 S/cm), while the conventional AHE of LCMO, or of proximity-magnetized interfacial CRO, with M out of plane is typically orders of magnitude larger, so a canting or sample/field misalignment well under a degree could account for the whole signal. Fig. 1d is plotted only up to 2.5 kOe, where the out-of-plane M has not saturated, although the text says ρ_AHE "saturates at H ~ 4000 Oe"; the saturated out-of-plane AHE is never given in absolute terms, and it is unclear that the "H > H_S" regime used for the linear background lies within the field range for this hard axis. Fig. 1d shows only that a normal field produces little M; it does not bound the out-of-plane component of the remanent state reached with an in-plane field. The θ-rotation and (001)-plane rotation tests (Supp. Figs. 17–20) test the external field direction and cannot separate the two scenarios, since canting from a tilted easy axis follows the same angular dependence. The authors should (i) show the full out-of-plane AHE up to saturation (with the 7 T magnet) and give the conventional AHE coefficient per unit out-of-plane M for the same trilayer; (ii) compute the canting or tilt angle needed to give 5 nΩ·cm and compare it with the rotator's alignment uncertainty; (iii) bound the actual out-of-plane remanent moment directly, with polar MOKE, XMCD at normal and grazing incidence, or vector VSM/SQUID after in-plane saturation; and (iv) discuss whether IP-AHE and AHE from symmetry-allowed canting can be told apart at all in this point group, framing the "direct Hall readout of in-plane magnetization" claim accordingly.

3. **The non-monotonic t_CRO dependence can be produced by parallel conduction and normalization, not β_LC.** The paper converts ρ_AHE to σ_AHE = ρ_AHE/ρxx² (p. 5) as if the CRO/LCMO/CRO stack were one homogeneous conductor, and does not state which thickness (whole stack or LCMO only) is used to convert resistance to ρ, nor the Hall-bar dimensions. CaRuO3 is a good paramagnetic metal; as t_CRO grows (to 64 u.c.), an increasing share of the current flows in the non-magnetic CRO, diluting the LCMO Hall signal roughly as (t_LC σ_LC / Σ t_i σ_i)², while the squared ρxx amplifies any change in current distribution. This alone reproduces the drop at large t_CRO (16 → 32 u.c., Fig. 3f). The rise from 0 to 16 u.c. could come from improved LCMO conductivity and the higher T_C and M_s with CRO (p. 3), or from a CRO interfacial contribution (concern 6). Because t_CRO changes β_LC, T_C, M_s, ρxx, interfacial charge transfer and the shunting fraction together, the overlay of Fig. 3f and Fig. 3g does not isolate β_LC. Nor is the relation single-valued: t_CRO = 4 u.c. has β_LC − 90° ≈ 0.13° and σ_AHE ≈ 18 mS/cm, whereas 32 u.c. has β_LC − 90° ≈ 0.085° and ≈ 5 mS/cm, yet 2 u.c. has a smaller β_LC (≈ 0.06°) than 32 u.c. but a larger σ_AHE. The authors should state the thickness used; report raw ρxx (or sheet resistance), R_xy/R_AHE, T_C and M_s at 10 K for every t_CRO; measure ρxx of CRO-only and LCMO-only reference films grown under identical conditions and analyse the Hall data with a parallel-conductor model to give the Hall conductance of the LCMO layer itself; plot that intrinsic σ_AHE directly against β_LC, the OOR amplitude and M_s; and apply the same analysis to the t_LC series (Supp. Fig. 22).

4. **A thermoelectric (anomalous Nernst or spin-caloritronic) artifact has not been ruled out, and it has the geometry and symmetry of the claimed signal.** With current along [1̄10]_O, Joule heating sets up a temperature gradient along the film normal [110]_O toward the NGO substrate. The anomalous Nernst field E ∝ M × ∇T then points along [001]_O, the in-plane Hall direction measured here. This voltage is odd in M, follows the in-plane M–H loop, varies as cos θ with in-plane M rotation and vanishes for M ∥ [001]_O — each of which the paper offers (Fig. 1e,f; Supp. Figs. 17, 18, 21) as evidence for a Berry-curvature IP-AHE. A spin-Seebeck or spin-Hall-type voltage from the metallic CRO adjacent to the magnet has the same symmetry. Given ρ_AHE ≈ 5 nΩ·cm, a small thermoelectric contribution would matter. The Methods do not say whether the measurement was DC or AC, the current magnitude or density, or whether current was reversed. The authors should show that V_xy is linear in I over at least a decade of current density; for DC measurements, show that the hysteretic signal reverses with current polarity, or for lock-in measurements report first and second harmonics separately; and ideally compare with a substrate of different thermal conductivity or an estimated anomalous Nernst coefficient for LCMO.

**B. Should address**

5. **The gating mechanism is inferred from reference films and DFT, not measured on the gated trilayer.** The switching is attributed to protonation of CRO, which reduces the octahedral rotations and hence the monoclinic distortion of LCMO (p. 7, Fig. 4b,d insets, Supp. Fig. 37). The evidence is in situ XRD on separate CRO and LCMO films (Supp. Fig. 33), nearly unchanged ρxx–T and H_C, and DFT; β_LC and the OOR amplitude are never measured in the gated trilayer, and the Fig. 4d insets are schematic. Competing explanations are not addressed: protonated H_xCaRuO3 becomes ferromagnetic (ref. 48), which would add a second magnetic metallic layer with its own Hall response or exchange coupling; protonation would change CRO conductivity and the current distribution (concern 3), yet ρxx is "nearly unchanged" (Supp. Fig. 34), which needs reconciling; protons or oxygen vacancies may enter LCMO near the interfaces below the detection limit of bulk XRD on a reference film; and it is unclear how protons reach the bottom CRO beneath 32 u.c. of LCMO, given that LCMO is said to protonate only above 2.2 V. Since the gating also removes any CRO-borne contribution (concern 6), it cannot by itself separate "CRO changes LCMO symmetry" from "the CRO contribution is switched off". H_C also rises from about 250 to about 290 Oe at V_g = 1.1–1.3 V (Fig. 4d), about 15%, which is not "nearly unchanged". The authors should show RSMs (β_LC, OOR amplitude) or STEM of a trilayer at V_g = 0, 1.2 V and 1.4 V and after reversal; ρxx at 10 K and the ordinary Hall coefficient versus V_g on the same device; M–H at 10 K (including M_s) in the gated state; the gate leakage current; a depth-resolved H probe (SIMS) if available; and discuss whether top and bottom CRO protonate differently.

6. **The layer in which the IP-AHE originates is not established; a CRO-borne or interfacial contribution is not excluded.** The higher T_C and M_s with t_CRO are attributed to interfacial Ru⁴⁺→Mn⁴⁺ charge transfer (p. 3; refs. 30–32), which implies an induced moment on interfacial Ru. CaRuO3 is strongly spin-orbit coupled and close to a ferromagnetic instability, so a magnetized CRO interface region with its own Berry curvature could carry part or all of the IP-AHE. Such a contribution would also scale non-monotonically with t_CRO, vanish when CRO is protonated (Fig. 4), and could change sign with temperature as in SrRuO3 (Supp. Fig. 24). The t_CRO = 0 film still shows a small but finite σ_AHE (Fig. 3a). The authors should measure in-plane Hall on a CRO single layer and on asymmetric bilayers (bottom-CRO-only and top-CRO-only); ideally test a CRO/LCMO stack with a thin non-magnetic spacer that preserves the OOR coupling but cuts the electronic one; and probe for an induced Ru moment with Ru- and Mn-edge XMCD or polarized neutron reflectometry, or at least discuss this alternative.

7. **β_LC is tiny compared with its likely precision, and is measured at a different temperature from transport.** β_LC − 90° spans only 0.03–0.2° (Fig. 3g), with error bars of about 0.005–0.01° attributed to "broadening of the RSM diffraction spot", but the procedure is not described. It is not obvious how a layer-averaged tilt of a few hundredths of a degree is extracted reliably from four asymmetric reflections of a 32-u.c. layer whose peaks lie near those of CRO and the near-lattice-matched NGO (Fig. 2a–d), given instrument resolution, mosaicity, substrate miscut, mounting tilt and the twin domains mentioned for t_CRO ≥ 32 u.c. The RSMs also appear to be taken at room temperature, whereas σ_AHE is measured at 10 K; LCMO and NGO change structurally on cooling and LCMO passes through its ferromagnetic transition. The data imply a steep nonlinearity (σ_AHE ≈ 18, 38 and 103 mS/cm at β − 90° ≈ 0.13°, 0.15° and 0.20°), in contrast to the nearly linear DFT trend. Since Fig. 3g is the main quantitative support for the mechanism, the authors should describe the fitting model and how β_LC is computed from the four reflections, give the instrument angular resolution, propagate the errors properly, report Supp. Table 1 values from more than one reflection and sample, show at least one low-temperature RSM or argue that β does not change on cooling, and cross-check against quantified STEM/GPA angles for at least two t_CRO values.

9. **The DFT comparison is only qualitative: it is 20–100 times larger than experiment and rests on untested choices.** σ_AHE-DFT is 2–8 S/cm (Fig. 3h), against a measured 0.004–0.1 S/cm, yet the paper calls the DFT "consistent with the experimental trend" without discussing the gap. In the supercells β_LC (90.0°, 90.08°, 90.16°) is changed by varying OOR and Jahn–Teller amplitudes together (p. 6, Supp. Fig. 26), and these amplitudes are not stated or compared with the real films. The AHE calculation uses bulk-like LCMO (or thin LCMO in superlattices far from the 32-u.c. experiment) with VCA for Ca, U = 8 eV and J = 2 eV taken from LaMnO3 rather than a doped manganite, and 0.02 eV broadening, with no sensitivity tests; the relaxed heterostructures of Supp. Fig. 31 are used only for β, not for σ. The authors should state the OOR amplitudes in each supercell, show σ_AHE-DFT versus U and chemical potential, discuss the quantitative gap (shunting, effective magnetic volume, disorder broadening, VCA, thickness), ideally compute σ_AHE for the relaxed CRO/LCMO superlattices, and describe the comparison as qualitative.

10. **The antisymmetrization procedure for hysteretic data is incorrect as written and the even-in-H background is not shown.** The Methods give R_xy^odd(H) = |R_xy(H) − R_xy(−H)|/2. The absolute value would remove the sign of the signal, yet the sign is central (the inverted loop in Fig. 1f, the sign reversals in Supp. Fig. 24). For hysteretic loops the odd part must be built from opposite sweep branches, R↑(H) − R↓(−H); otherwise the field-even planar-Hall/AMR background is not removed in the coercive region, and with E ∥ M a small angular misalignment produces a planar-Hall term that switches at H_C. The authors should correct the formula, state the branch pairing, and show raw R_xy loops for both sweep directions for one sample next to the extracted ρ_AHE, with the planar-Hall background amplitude relative to the ~nΩ·cm odd signal.

11. **The temperature dependence and scaling point to more than one contribution, weakening the "predominantly intrinsic" assignment.** σ_AHE changes sign near 100 K for t_CRO ≥ 32 u.c. or < 8 u.c. (p. 5, Supp. Fig. 24), and σ_AHE versus M is "strongly nonlinear and sign-changing". The paper compares this with SrRuO3 band topology, but a sign change is also what two opposite-sign contributions would give (LCMO plus interfacial CRO, or intrinsic plus a canting- or skew-type term). If β_LC controls the IP-AHE, the sample-dependent temperature of the sign change should be explained in relation to β_LC. The intrinsic claim (Supp. Fig. 25) rests on a weak dependence of σ_AHE on σxx over a σxx range not stated in the main text; over a narrow window this cannot exclude side-jump or interfacial contributions. The authors should present the scaling analysis more fully (σxx range, fitting form such as TYJ, parameters), test whether the sign change correlates with t_CRO as a CRO-interface contribution would, and qualify the intrinsic assignment.

12. **The STEM evidence for the tilt is overstated.** The text says Fig. 2f "clearly resolve[s] the β angles" and that opposite tilts are "directly visible" in HAADF-STEM, confirmed by GPA (Fig. 2h). 14. **The (001)-oriented reference is not a clean null control.** The NGO(001) samples (Supp. Figs. 4, 23) differ from the (110) samples in film orientation, strain, magnetic anisotropy axis and current direction, not only in mirror-symmetry breaking, and, as noted in concern 1, Ω ∥ c is forbidden for in-plane M there even in orthorhombic symmetry.
