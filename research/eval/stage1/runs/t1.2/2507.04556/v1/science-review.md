**A. Must address**

1. **Interaction-driven (dipolar-repulsion) expansion and other density nonlinearities are not modelled, and the paper's own numbers imply they are large.** Eq. 1 uses a constant, density-independent D and a linear loss n/τ, and every comparison with diffusion (Fig. 2a, Fig. 5, the "Comparison with diffusive transport" paragraph, p. 3–4) rests on it. Yet the SI section "Estimates of IX mean free time and mean free path" writes D = D0(1 + n·u0/k_BT), and the main text gives n·u0 ≈ δE ≈ 3 meV in the efficient regime, against k_BT ≈ 0.15 meV at 1.7 K. The enhancement factor is therefore ≈ 20 at 1.7 K, falling to ≈ 2 at 30 K. Classical dipolar-pressure expansion thus predicts an apparent D that is large at low T, falls with rising T, grows with density, and can turn over at high P_ex where τ shortens sharply (Fig. S3), the density saturates and new species appear (Fig. S7) — the very T and P_ex trends (Figs. 3a, 3b) the paper cites as evidence against diffusion. Nonlinear diffusion with D ∝ n gives sharp-fronted profiles whose width grows faster than √t and nearly linearly, which a constant-velocity model would fit better; and the pressure-driven expansion speed c ≈ (n·u0/m)^1/2 ≈ 2×10^6 cm/s for m ≈ m0 is the same order as the fitted "ballistic" v ≈ 7×10^5–10^6 cm/s (Fig. 4, Fig. S4). Exciton–exciton annihilation and density-dependent recombination add a second nonlinear effect: they saturate emission at the centre and flatten the profile, raising the apparent 1/e width without any transport (as documented in TMD halo studies, e.g. Ref. 38; dipolar expansion also accounts for large apparent diffusivities in Ref. 55). The authors call the constant-D simplification a "subject of future works", but the anomalous D is the paper's central result and cannot be separated from these effects without modelling them; the χ² preference for Eq. 2 may only show that constant-D diffusion is the wrong diffusive model. The authors should simulate ∂n/∂t = ∇[D0(1 + u0 n/k_BT)∇n] + Λ − n/τ(n) − γn², using the measured τ(P_ex, T), a calibrated δE(n), and γ fixed from the power dependence of the spatially integrated decay, with D0 as the only free parameter (or fixed from low-density data). They should fit D0 across the full (T, P_ex) map, show whether a modest, T-independent D0 (e.g. the 10–30 cm²/s seen at 30–50 K) reproduces Figs. 1–3, and check that the spatially integrated PL scales linearly with power in the efficient regime. Until then, the constant-D quantity from Eq. 1 is only an effective spreading rate, and neither "anomalously high diffusivity" nor "inconsistent with diffusive transport" is demonstrated.

2. **The observable d_1/e is ill-defined in the efficient regime because the cloud fills a small, bounded sample, and the model contains no boundaries.** d_1/e comes from an exponential fit over x = 0–9 µm, "from the excitation spot to the HS edge" (Fig. 1 caption), yet the reported values reach 20–22 µm (Figs. 1b–d, 2): more than twice the fit window and comparable to or larger than the MoSe2 extent (~20 µm). The latest profiles in Fig. 1a (2.5–11 ns) are nearly flat over the window, with a fall or upturn at x ≈ 9–11 µm; for such a profile d_1/e diverges and is set by a small residual slope, so it is very sensitive to noise and background subtraction, to IX pile-up or reflection (or loss) at the HS edge, to PL waveguided in the ~70 nm hBN stack and scattered out at layer edges, to emission from regions outside the MoSe2/WSe2 overlap (profiles extend to x ≈ 12 µm), and to spatial variation of emission efficiency. The SI says only the 0–3 ns window stays "within the HS area", but the main-text D and v (Figs. 3, 4) come from 0–5 ns fits; the 0–3 ns fits (Fig. S6) give D about a factor of 2 lower at 1.7 K (~350–500 against ~700–1000 cm²/s). The simulations are 1D, with no stated domain or edge boundary condition; the HS is ~10 µm wide in y (Fig. S1b), comparable to the cloud, so the expansion is 2D during the first ~1–2 ns and a 1D model can overestimate D by up to ~2; and the spot position relative to every HS edge is not given. At 1.7 K and 500 µW, d_1/e(t) accelerates (convex, Figs. 1b, 2; Fig. 2b and Fig. S5b undershoot the last points by ~5 µm after ~2–3 ns), which neither model reproduces and which points to edge effects or a changing emission profile rather than constant-velocity motion. The authors should include the real HS geometry, spot position and explicit edge boundary conditions in the simulations (2D, or 1D with the measured edge), and test sensitivity to the boundary condition; state whether simulated d_1/e is extracted with the same window and procedure as the data; fit the full I(x,t) maps (with residuals) rather than a single scalar; report D and v only from times before the cloud reaches an edge; show 2D (x, y) images or y-profiles to justify the 1D reduction; and add a model-independent observable such as the second moment σ²(t) before the cloud reaches the edge, or the arrival (half-rise) time of I(t) at fixed x versus x (linear for a ballistic or drift front, quadratic for diffusion, none for waveguided emission).

3. **Eq. 2 is a one-directional advection equation, and the χ² comparison cannot discriminate diffusive from ballistic transport, so "agrees with long-range ballistic transport" is not supported.** As written, ∂n/∂t = −v∇n translates the whole profile in +x; isotropic ballistic expansion from a source would need counter-propagating ±v populations, v·sgn(x), an angular velocity distribution or a kinetic equation. Fig. S4b shows the "ballistic" profile shifted bodily to +x, yet profiles on both sides of the spot are shown, so it is unclear what was solved on the x < 0 side, or why a Crank–Nicolson diffusion solver (SI ref. 7) suits an advection equation. A constant drift away from the spot is what a force produces — dipolar pressure (Concern 1), a strain or potential gradient towards the edge, phonon wind — and does not require the absence of scattering. The test statistic χ² = Σ(m − s)²/(ν s) (p. 3, Fig. 5) is normalised by the model value rather than the measurement variance, has units of length, and counts time points rather than degrees of freedom; no error bars on d_1/e appear anywhere. Its absolute values therefore have no meaning: χ²_D and χ²_v are comparable (~0.1) at most settings, the distinction rests on a few points (best case 0.3 against 1.3), and at 1.7 and 6 K χ²_v = 0.35–0.5 is not small. Neither curve follows the convex d_1/e(t) of Fig. 2. The authors should state exactly which ballistic model was solved and compare with a genuinely ballistic kinetic model (e.g. free-flight/Boltzmann with isotropic velocities, the measured τ and a finite mean free path as a parameter); give uncertainties on d_1/e from the fit covariance and repeated scans and use them in a proper reduced χ² or likelihood-ratio comparison with confidence intervals on D and v; match the number of free parameters (each model also allowing a free offset and time zero); show residuals; analyse σ²(t) (t² for ballistic, t for diffusive); and say what process would produce the superlinear d_1/e(t).

4. **The Bose–Hubbard superfluid interpretation is not supported by evidence specific to a superfluid and is in tension with the paper's own numbers.** The abstract and conclusion state consistency with BH superfluidity ("Comparison of IX transport with BH theory", p. 4). Ballistic-looking transport is not specific to a superfluid; hot, interaction-driven or phonon-wind-driven classical transport would also produce it. The T_c argument is circular: J ~ 1.6 K is inferred from the observed ~10 K vanishing temperature via T_c ~ 4πNJ, a moiré amplitude is inferred from J, and its agreement with literature depths is therefore not an independent test. The paper estimates disorder ~10 meV (linewidths 14–20 meV in the SI), about two orders of magnitude above J ≈ 0.14 meV — a regime where the disordered BH model predicts a Bose glass, not a superfluid; the paper uses the strong disorder to argue against diffusion while ignoring its consequence for the BH phase diagram, and the SI's percolation picture (superfluid puddles) is qualitative and at odds with the linewidth estimate. For J ≈ 0.14 meV and b = 17 nm the maximum band group velocity ~2Jb/ħ ≈ 7×10^5 cm/s, below the fitted v of up to 10^6 cm/s. There is no coherence measurement (e.g. g^(1) by shift-interferometry, as this group has done in GaAs), no critical velocity, no vortices and no sharp onset at T_c: D in Fig. 3a varies smoothly from 1.7 to ~10 K. At a near-60° (H-stacked) ~1° twist, atomic reconstruction into domains is expected (the SI acknowledges reconstruction can modify the potential), which may invalidate a rigid moiré BH description. The authors should either provide superfluid-specific evidence — first-order coherence in and out of the efficient-transport region, a quantitative disordered-BH phase diagram using the measured disorder, the expected v for the inferred J, an independent estimate of the moiré amplitude and J — or remove "superfluid" from the abstract and conclusions and reword to, e.g., "non-diffusive, anomalously fast transport whose T and n dependence is qualitatively reminiscent of BH predictions", moving the BH discussion to an outlook.

**B. Should address**

5. **Laser heating, hot IXs and phonon wind are not excluded.** Excitation is resonant with the WSe2 DX at 1.689 eV, ~300 meV above the IX (~1.39 eV), at up to several mW focused to a spot a few µm across; neither the spot size, the absorbed power density nor the WSe2 absorption is given. The local lattice and exciton temperatures near the spot may then be well above the 1.7 K bath, hot IXs leave the light cone and emit less (flattening or dipping the central profile, as in the "inner ring" physics of Ref. 9), and hot-exciton transport can be fast and quasi-ballistic at early times. Non-equilibrium phonons generated at the spot can push excitons outward quickly; phonon wind and hot-carrier halos are known to produce fast, directional expansion in TMDs (e.g. Ref. 38). That the efficient regime appears at the lowest bath temperatures and intermediate powers, and is fastest in the first few ns of an 80 ns pulse, is compatible with such transient effects. The authors should give the spot size, peak absorbed power density and absorption so readers can estimate densities and heating; estimate the local temperature rise at 0.5 mW; report an IX effective temperature (linewidth or high-energy tail) versus P_ex, x and t; and test whether the fast expansion persists at matched IX density with lower excess energy (excitation resonant with the MoSe2 DX, below the WSe2 DX, or near the IX), or with a shorter pulse or lower duty cycle.

6. **The conclusion rests on one sample, one excitation position and one expansion direction.** The SI says efficient transport "was realized in one sample" and attributes its absence elsewhere to disorder; only expansion from x = 0 towards the +x edge is analysed, and the profiles at x < 0 in Fig. 1a fall quickly without explanation. A strain or thickness gradient or a bubble could create a built-in drift towards one edge — exactly what Eq. 2 describes — and local inhomogeneity (the graphene electrode remnant near x = 2 µm, the flake boundaries) or geometry-related light propagation could also be involved. The authors should show expansion towards −x and along y, and from two or three other spot positions including the centre of the long axis, the opposite edge and positions where the HS extends on both sides of the spot, and report the spread of D and v; show a second device or at least a same-sample reference region (e.g. a different moiré domain) under matched conditions; list any other heterostructures measured that showed ordinary diffusion, with twist angle, linewidth and stacking; and state the basis for calling this sample "less disordered", given its 14–20 meV linewidth.

7. **The mean-free-path estimate contradicts the "long-range ballistic" statement.** The main text (p. 3) argues that the high D implies anomalously large τ_m and l_m and that IXs "move with no scattering over the range ~l_m", indicating "long-range ballistic transport". The SI obtains τ_m ≈ 0.2 ns and l_m ≈ 2 µm from classical diffusive relations (which the SI itself says describe a "classical diffusive transport regime"), using an IX mass "~ free electron mass" (computed MoSe2/WSe2 IX masses are ~1.0–1.3 m0) and an unspecified velocity in l_m = vτ_m, and only after dividing D by the interaction factor ≈ 20. An l_m of ~2 µm, ten times smaller than the ~20 µm expansion, makes the transport diffusive over the measured range by the authors' own estimate, and the division by (1 + n·u0/k_BT) supports interaction-enhanced diffusion (Concern 1). Also, the fitted v ≈ 10^6 cm/s at 1.7 K is about twice the thermal velocity √(k_BT/m) ≈ 5×10^5 cm/s and decreases with T while the thermal velocity grows. The authors should give the numbers explicitly in the main text with the diffusive caveat, compare v(T) with the thermal velocity and with the dipolar drift velocity expected from the density gradient, and reconcile l_m with the transport distance or withdraw the ballistic interpretation.

8. **The generation profile Λ(x) and the IX formation dynamics are not characterised, and generation tails could mimic a large d_1/e.** Λ(x) is taken as the WSe2 DX PL profile, which already includes DX diffusion before charge transfer, and IX formation is assumed local and instantaneous. IXs may instead form after DX or electron/hole transport in the WSe2 or MoSe2 layers or monolayer regions (the WSe2 extends beyond the MoSe2, edges at ~30 against ~20 µm); at high P_ex, DX diffusion or annihilation broadens the effective source, consistent with the power-dependent early-time offset of d_1/e (~3–4 µm at t ≈ 0, higher at 4 mW in Fig. 1d). Laser light scattered or guided in the ~70 nm hBN/TMD stack, or guided or reabsorbed PL, would generate IXs far from the spot; the DX profile is shown only to ~10^-2 of its peak, and a 10^-2–10^-3 tail combined with centre saturation could mimic a large d_1/e. The authors should state the laser spot profile; show the reflected-laser and DX profiles on a log scale to the noise floor under the same conditions; measure the DX profile at each P_ex and use it as Λ; show that results do not depend on the choice of Λ (laser against DX profile); and report the fitted d_1/e(t = 0) against power.

9. **Spectrally integrated detection assumes PL intensity is proportional to IX density and mixes IX species.** d_1/e is extracted from all emission through an 850 nm long-pass filter. Fig. S7 shows the spectrum changing with power (narrow localized lines at low power, a higher-energy "double-occupancy" line at high power), so different species may dominate at different positions and times, and changes in d_1/e could reflect a change in emitting species or radiative efficiency along x rather than transport; the near-flat late-time profile (Fig. 1a) is especially sensitive to this. The authors should measure spectrally resolved (or at least band-filtered) profiles for the main and high-energy IX lines, check that the expanded signal has the IX spectrum and the same peak energy as at the spot, and follow the local energy shift (a density proxy) and linewidth (a temperature proxy) along x and t.

11. **The lifetime enters as a single constant measured after the pulse, although it varies with density during the pulse.** τ is the "initial decay time" after the pulse ends (Fig. S3) but is used during a high-density pulse, when decay is generally non-exponential and τ depends on local, time-varying density. In the efficient regime τ ≈ 3 ns, comparable to the 5 ns window, so the treatment of τ(n(t)) strongly affects the extracted D. The authors should use a position- and density-dependent decay (or the nonlinear loss of Concern 1) and test how sensitive D and v are to using τ(n) instead of a constant τ.

12. **The density, filling and twist-angle calibrations lack uncertainties, so N ~ 1/2 is not quantitative.** n comes from the mean-field plate-capacitor formula with an assumed ε ≈ 7.4; correlations make this formula underestimate n by several-fold for dipolar excitons, and in a moiré lattice with strong on-site repulsion it is not appropriate. δE ≈ 3 meV is quoted for "the efficient IX transport regime" but is not tied to a time, position or spectral feature (Fig. S7), and no δE(P_ex) or δE(t) is shown, though the density changes by an order of magnitude across Fig. 3b and during the pulse; a single N cannot be assigned to a cloud whose density varies strongly in space and time. The twist δθ = 1.1° is estimated from flake edges (typically ±0.5–1°; SHG deliberately not done), so b = 17 nm and, via b², N could change by a factor of ~2–4; the role of the 0.2% lattice mismatch in the moiré period and the fact that 1.1° is the deviation from 60° (H stacking) are not stated. The uncertainty in N plausibly spans the whole 0 < N < 1 range, undermining the "superfluid at N ~ 1/2, insulator at N ~ 1" assignment. The authors should show the IX energy shift against P_ex and t with derived n and N (e.g. on a secondary axis of Fig. 3b) with error bands propagating the twist-angle and capacitor-formula uncertainties; plot D against estimated filling; write the angle as 60° ± 1.1° with its uncertainty; consider SHG on a non-critical region or after the transport measurements, or an independent density calibration (reflectance or doping-dependent spectra).
