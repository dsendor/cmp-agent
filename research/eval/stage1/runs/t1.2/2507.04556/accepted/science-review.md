A. Must address

1. d_1/e is reported far beyond the window in which it is fitted, so the headline growth may reflect a flattening profile in a bounded heterostructure rather than IX transport. The Fig. 1 caption (page 2) says d_1/e comes from least-squares exponential fits of I(x,t) "in the region from the excitation spot to the HS edge, x = 0–9 µm". Yet the reported d_1/e reaches about 15–25 µm (Figs. 1b–d, Fig. 2), two to three times the fit window, and in Fig. 1a the 2.5 ns and 11 ns profiles are nearly flat across 0–10 µm. A decay length much longer than the fit interval is set only by a small fractional drop in intensity across the window, so it is very sensitive to noise, background subtraction, normalisation, slight curvature of the profile and any edge effect at x ≈ 9 µm, and it diverges as a profile flattens. Several processes flatten a profile bounded by the HS edge without long-range transport: IXs piling up at or reflecting from the HS edge or a confining landscape; IXs generated across the HS by scattered or reflected laser light or waveguided DX/PL emission; and saturation or quenching near the spot (exciton–exciton annihilation, heating) that lowers the centre intensity over time. Edge accumulation alone could produce the late-time upturn of d_1/e at 500 µW (Fig. 2, last three points, which none of the three models reproduce) and make the growth look superlinear in d². Because the "anomalously fast d_1/e growth" and every D, v and µ in Fig. 3 come from these fits, the authors should: show the raw I(x,t) profiles for the fast-regime (500 µW, 1.7 K) and 25 µW series on linear and log scales, with fit uncertainties on d_1/e and a stated error when d_1/e exceeds the window; use a measure that stays bounded in a finite domain and does not depend on the window, such as the mean-square displacement ⟨x²⟩(t), the cloud centroid, or the position or arrival time of a fixed-intensity front at fixed x, and show that the ballistic-like regime survives; simulate Eqs. 1–3 on a domain of the real HS length with explicit boundary conditions (reflecting or absorbing edge, edge-enhanced emission) and fit the full I(x,t) rather than d_1/e(t); show I(x,t) beyond the HS edge and at x < 0 to quantify scattered-light background; show the integrated intensity versus t to separate redistribution from loss at the centre; and, if possible, repeat the measurement with the spot farther from the edge or along a longer or different direction of the HS.

B. Should address

2. Drift down the built-in potential gradient is not separated from intrinsic mobility. The drift-diffusion model uses a landscape U(x) that "drops ~10 meV on ~10 µm", approximated as a 1 meV/µm gradient along the measurement direction ("outlined in SI"), with transport treated as 1D along an elongated strip toward the HS edge. Drift in a uniform gradient gives a constant velocity, so near-linear d_1/e(t) follows by construction; with µ ≈ 10^4 cm²/eVs this gradient alone gives µ∇U ≈ 10^5 cm/s ≈ 1 µm/ns, a sizeable fraction of the observed expansion rate. The extracted µ then depends on how well U is known, the landscape is not measured at the P_ex and T of interest, and the suppression at high T or density could reflect changes in screening of U or IX energy renormalisation rather than a change of transport state. The anomaly could thus be partly a property of this device's landscape. The authors should measure spatially and spectrally resolved PL, E(x,t), at the relevant P_ex and T so that U(x) and its time evolution are measured with uncertainties rather than assumed; compare expansion uphill and downhill (opposite direction from the spot, or several spot positions along the strip) and along y; and report µ and D with U varied within its uncertainty and set to zero.

3. The link to superfluidity rests on qualitative trends that conventional mechanisms also produce, and no coherence measurement is given. The abstract, the "Comparison of IX transport with BH theory" section and the conclusion say the high mobility despite about 10 meV disorder, its loss above about 10 K and its non-monotonic density dependence are "qualitatively consistent with theoretical predictions of IX superfluidity", while the text also frames the result as "inconsistent with the diffusive transport". Each trend has an ordinary explanation: high mobility from gradient drift (concern 2) or the metric (concern 1); the rise from low density from standard screening/delocalisation, as the authors themselves describe; the loss above about 10 K from phonon scattering, thermally activated trapping, or temperature-dependent lifetime and emission efficiency; and the high-density decrease from a Mott crossover to an e–h plasma, exciton–exciton annihilation shortening τ, or laser heating. The BH comparison is explicitly qualitative (superfluid for N between about 0 and about 1 per site, with N ~ 1/2 given without uncertainty). The authors should either add a measurement specific to a coherent state, such as first-order spatial coherence from shift-interferometry checked to appear in the same (T, P_ex) window as the fast transport, a sharp threshold in T or n with error bars and the expected scaling, a critical-velocity or flow-suppression test, or the dependence on disorder or moiré depth; or scope the title, abstract and conclusion to "fast, non-diffusive IX transport kinetics" with superfluidity as one hypothesis among several, stating which observation would distinguish it from landscape drift plus heating. In either case they should discuss the Mott, annihilation and heating alternatives explicitly, using IX linewidths and lifetimes versus P_ex.

4. None of the three models describes the fast-regime data, and the extracted D, v and µ carry no uncertainties. In Fig. 2 at 500 µW the measured d_1/e(t) is convex, accelerating to about 22–23 µm at 5 ns, while all three simulated curves are concave and saturate near 13–17 µm; the models are also too steep at early times, and the diffusion model misses the early-time shape. The single-parameter fits therefore average over a systematic misfit, and an accelerating expansion is not expected from ballistic motion or drift either, hinting at a time-dependent process (growing background, heating, edge or front effect; see concern 1). The paper admits the approaches are "rough estimates" that "do not aim to provide an accurate description" (pages 2 and 4), yet the abstract's "anomalously high IX mobility" is quoted from them, and Fig. 3 reports D, v and µ over two orders of magnitude with no error bars, with colour maps (a3, b3, c3) interpolated from a sparse, irregular grid and a white unmeasured region. The authors should give residuals or a goodness-of-fit measure and confidence intervals for every fitted parameter, state which inputs are fixed (τ, U, u0, Λ) and how sensitive the results are to each, mark the measured (T, P_ex) points on the maps, discuss the cause of the acceleration, and test whether a single µ is meaningful by fitting restricted time windows or allowing a time-dependent parameter.

5. The effective IX and lattice temperature is not established, and heating could explain the high-power suppression. All quoted temperatures are cryostat temperatures, and the argument against thermally activated diffusion uses k_BT ≲ 1 meV against the disorder and interaction energies. The transport is driven by 84 ns pulses at a 300 ns period, focused and resonant with the WSe2 DX, at 0.5–4 mW. The IX gas and the local lattice may sit well above 1.7 K, which bears on the k_BT argument, on the comparison with a BH transition temperature of about 10 K, and on the Einstein relation (concern 10). The decrease of D, v and µ above about 500 µW (Figs. 1d, 3 a2–c2) could simply be heating, mirroring the T-dependence in Figs. 3 a1–c1. The authors should estimate the IX temperature versus P_ex and time after pulse onset, for example from the high-energy tail of the IX spectrum or from calibrated linewidth or energy versus T, estimate local lattice heating at 0.5–4 mW, and show whether the high-P_ex suppression coincides with an effective temperature of about 10 K or more.

6. The claim of "long-range ballistic transport" conflicts with a simple mean-free-path estimate. Page 4 states τ_m ≈ µm (IX mass m) and that l_m ∝ τ_m "indicates long-range ballistic transport", but gives numbers only in the SI. With µ ≈ 3–5×10^4 cm²/eVs (Fig. 3c) up to about 10^5 cm²/eVs and m ≈ m0, τ_m is about 17–30 ps up to tens of picoseconds. With the thermal velocity at 1.7 K (about 5×10^5 cm/s) or the fitted v of a few 10^5 to 10^6 cm/s (Fig. 3b), l_m is about 0.1–0.2 µm and below 1 µm in any case: two orders of magnitude below the roughly 20 µm expansion and comparable to the optical resolution. If transport is diffusive above about 0.1 µm, linear growth must come from drift, not ballistic flight. The authors should quote τ_m, the assumed velocity and l_m in the main text and reconcile them with the d_1/e growth over 5 ns, or replace "ballistic" with "drift-like" or "near-linear".

7. The density and moiré-filling calibration is too uncertain to support N ≈ 1/2 in the BH comparison. The density n ≈ 2×10^11 cm⁻² comes from the mean-field "plate capacitor" formula (d_z ≈ 0.6 nm, ε ≈ 7.4) applied to δE ≈ 3 meV; the authors acknowledge that correlations raise n for a given shift, by factors commonly 2–5 in the cited literature. The reference energy for δE is not stated, nor whether δE is measured in the same 0–5 ns window as the transport. Converting n to N needs the moiré period, but the twist angle and its uncertainty, the stacking (R or H), the encapsulation, any gating, and evidence for a moiré potential are not in the main text. The moiré potential is described as deep (page 4), in which case the BH tunnelling J would be small and the expected superfluid temperature needs an estimate to compare with the observed 10 K. With these uncertainties N ≈ 1/2 cannot be distinguished from N ≈ 0 or 1. The authors should give the twist angle with its uncertainty and how it was determined, the moiré cell area, δE(P_ex) and n(P_ex) with the range from mean-field to correlated estimates, the resulting range of N, and estimates of J, U and the BH (or BKT) transition temperature and filling window with uncertainties; they should also replot Fig. 3 against n, since the abstract speaks of density dependence.

8. IX lifetime and radiative efficiency, and the assumption that PL intensity tracks density, are not shown in the main text. τ enters all three models and is measured "as outlined in SI", but τ(T, P_ex) is not shown. If τ shortens at high density (annihilation) or high T, the expansion window shrinks and the effect "disappears" without any change in mobility. Converting I(x,t) to n(x,t) also assumes a uniform IX radiative efficiency along x and over time, which can fail near the HS edge, with gradients in local stacking, strain or dielectric environment, and with conversion between bright and dark or spin-forbidden IX states; spatial variation of brightness maps directly onto the PL d_1/e. The authors should show τ(T, P_ex) next to Fig. 3, show a spatial map of cw IX brightness and energy across the HS, and discuss whether PL intensity is proportional to IX density across the cloud and over time.

The main text, Methods and SI describe a single MoSe2/WSe2 HS with a single spot near one end of an elongated strip; reproducibility after thermal cycling is stated but not shown, and it does not address sample- or geometry-specific causes, since the landscape, edges and twist angle are device-specific. 10. The drift-diffusion model uses the classical Einstein relation in a regime claimed to be quantum degenerate. Eq. 3 sets D' = µk_BT with a mean-field interaction energy; both hold for a classical, non-degenerate gas in local equilibrium, the opposite of the degenerate BH superfluid regime invoked to interpret the result, and the mean-field u0 is known to underestimate density, as the paper notes. The relation also ties D' to the cryostat temperature (concern 5), and a local-equilibrium description is questionable if the mean free path is long. The authors should state how µ changes if D' is left free or uses the degenerate-gas form, or state that the extracted µ has no strict meaning in the regime they propose.

11. The disorder amplitude is inferred only from the roughly 10 meV IX PL linewidth. This identification is central to the argument (page 4) that the transport is inconsistent with diffusion because k_BT and the 3 meV interaction energy are both below the disorder, and to "high mobility despite substantial disorder". The linewidth also contains homogeneous, phonon and exciton–exciton broadening, unresolved singlet/triplet, moiré or trion lines, and spatial averaging over the gradient U(x); its relation to the static disorder IXs experience along a 20 µm path is not established. The authors should show the spectra, the low-power linewidth and the linewidth versus T and P_ex, preferably with spatial maps of IX energy where the fast transport occurs, and justify the identification or present 10 meV as an upper bound.

12. Hot-exciton expansion, IX formation dynamics and the generation profile at early times are not addressed. Excitation at 1.689 eV is resonant with the WSe2 DX, so IXs form with excess energy; hot-exciton, phonon-wind and "halo" transport in TMDs (refs. 39, 42) produce fast early expansion that weakens as the lattice warms, so the loss of the effect above 10 K does not alone single out superfluidity. The generation term Λ(x) is taken from the MoSe2 DX PL profile, ignoring WSe2 DX diffusion and interlayer charge transfer before IX formation, and IX formation is assumed to follow the pulse instantly. The IX formation time, the 0.1–0.3 ns pulse rise, the APD time resolution and the spatial resolution (d_1/e ≈ 3 µm at t = 0) also affect the inferred early-time velocity. The authors should explain why hot-exciton expansion fails to reproduce the measured (T, P_ex) dependence, state the APD time resolution and the optical point-spread function, test how the fitted parameters depend on the assumed Λ(x), and show that the fits are insensitive to excluding the first nanosecond.
