**A. Must address**

1. **The absolute interaction potential and its "agreement with simulation" are partly built in, and the one independent quantity, a, disagrees.** The text after Eq. (1) (p. 1620) states that the scattering trajectories fix only the ratio F(r)/D. The dissipation constant D needed to convert this into V(r) in meV (Fig. 3b) is then set "by comparing F(r) over the relevant range of r in the experiment and in the homogeneous case", which gives a D "approximately four times larger" than in the defect-free simulation, attributed to roughness. As written, the overall scale of the experimental curve in Fig. 3b is fixed by matching it to the simulated curve, so the overlap of the orange and blue bands cannot be cited as independent validation (abstract: "in agreement with simulation results"; p. 1621: "The overlapping uncertainty bands between experiment and simulation validate the robustness of the inverse parameter estimation method"; Conclusion: "excellent agreement with micromagnetic simulations, matching both the magnitude and range"; "the dominant contributions in both cases were identified"). The meV scale is only as good as this D. The paper itself says a "does not depend on the value of D", so a is the cleanest test, and it currently fails it: a* = 44 ± 4 nm in the experiment against 31 ± 3 nm in simulation, a difference of about 40% and 2.6–3σ (b* = 247 ± 4 nm against 288 ± 13 nm). In Fig. 3b the two bands visibly separate for r ≳ 220 nm, where the Fig. 3b histogram shows much of the data lies. The authors should (i) state exactly how D_exp was fixed and which part of Fig. 3b is an independent comparison; (ii) either determine D independently, with an uncertainty propagated into the band of Fig. 3b, for example from the measured v_x(J_x) slope in the coherent regime (Fig. 5, v_x = F_ext/(2αD)) with stated spin Hall angle, efficiency factor and α, from the measured skyrmion profile and damping, or from FMR/ST-FMR damping on the same stack, or report only F/D and a and make the comparison with micromagnetics on those quantities; (iii) acknowledge and discuss the discrepancy in a, with candidate origins such as disorder, the effective D, the assumed functional form, skyrmion deformation, or the simulation geometry and parameters (K_eff, DMI, J_IEC); and (iv) revise the abstract and conclusion to match.

2. **Pinning forces on the recoiling L and R skyrmions are omitted from the inversion, and an interaction-free recoil mechanism has not been excluded.** Methods and p. 1619–1620 assume that the post-pulse motion of L and R skyrmions "is governed solely by skyrmion–skyrmion interactions and dissipation", with pinned I skyrmions entering only as boundary positions. The paper's own evidence argues against this: the pinning landscape comes from grain structure and local K_eff, DMI and exchange variations (Methods, Material deposition); the relaxation times differ between +J_x and −J_x because "the subsequent recoil traverses spatially distinct regions of this heterogeneous landscape", "arising from the combined effects of pinning and skyrmion–skyrmion interactions" (Methods); recoil amplitudes and time constants "vary substantially" between classes, "suggesting differences in the local energy landscape"; and the L and R traces in Fig. 2d settle at a finite offset of about 10 nm instead of returning to their starting positions. A skyrmion displaced or elastically deformed inside its own pinning well would relax back after the drive even without neighbours, giving an exponential return opposite to the drive, independent of polarity, on a 3–20 ns timescale. Any such restoring force would be absorbed into a, b and the effective D, and could also explain the factor-of-four "enhanced dissipation". The authors should (i) bring the weakly interacting, dilute-regime data of Supplementary Section A3 into the main text or Extended Data as the null control, with displacement versus time, recoil amplitude and τ_relax for isolated skyrmions under matched J_x, pulse width and field: if isolated skyrmions do not recoil this settles the point, and if they do the extraction must include a pinning term; (ii) add a phenomenological single-particle pinning term (for example a harmonic or Gaussian well at each skyrmion's equilibrium position with a fitted stiffness, or a measured landscape) to the Thiele model, show how a* and b* change, and show that the fit is not dominated by this term; (iii) fit +J_x and −J_x trajectories, and L and R classes, separately and show that consistent a and b result. Running the full inverse procedure on simulated relaxation trajectories that include a random pinning landscape, and showing it recovers the known input potential, would validate the pinning-free assumption directly.

**B. Should address**

3. **Thermal relaxation after the pulse is a competing explanation for the 3–20 ns recoil.** Pulses of 2.8 × 10¹¹ A m⁻² (5 ns) to 1.2 × 10¹² A m⁻² in a Pt/Ir multilayer on a 100-nm SiN membrane, whose limited thermal conductivity the authors note (Microfabrication), can heat the strip substantially, and cooling on nanosecond-to-tens-of-nanosecond timescales overlaps τ_relax. A transient temperature change alters M_s, K_eff, DMI and the degree of compensation, hence skyrmion size and equilibrium spacing, which can produce an apparent relaxation of positions with a time constant set by heat flow rather than by F/D. Heating may also add thermal or creep components to v(J). Heating is discussed only in Supplementary Section A4. The authors should give, in the main text, the estimated or measured peak temperature rise and cooling time for each current density and pulse width (for example from the time-resolved device resistance during and after the pulse, or a thermal simulation); show skyrmion radius, ellipticity and contrast versus delay through and after the pulse, to see whether size relaxes on the same 3–20 ns scale; and show the dependence of τ_relax and recoil amplitude on pulse width at fixed current density (2.5, 5, 7.5 and 10 ns are said in Methods to have been measured but are not shown), which would separate a thermal contribution scaling with dissipated energy from interaction-driven relaxation scaling with compression.

4. **The exponential force law is imposed, not tested, and the range of r it is drawn over exceeds what the recoil samples.** Eq. (1) assumes an exponential; neither alternative forms nor a free-form or binned force are compared. Theory for skyrmion–skyrmion repulsion commonly gives a modified-Bessel K₁(r/λ) form, which differs from a pure exponential at the separations probed, and in a SyAFM residual dipolar and interlayer terms may contribute. Each recoil trajectory changes the inter-skyrmion separation by only tens of nanometres (Fig. 3a shows position changes of about 0.03 µm), yet the potential is drawn from about 150 to 400 nm, rising steeply to about 300 meV near 150 nm where there are few data. The Fig. 3b histogram (data mainly at r ≈ 140–300 nm) shows the static distribution of separations, not the range actually swept during recoil. The authors should plot the r range swept dynamically; compare the exponential with at least one physically motivated alternative (K₁-type, power law) via the loss L₂ or an information criterion; and restrict the plotted band or mark the extrapolated region. Otherwise "an exponential decay" and "quantitatively map the interaction potential, continuously from a compressed state into a relaxed state" should be presented as a parametrization rather than a finding.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

5. **The predicted and measured trajectories in Fig. 3a show systematic offsets, and the fit's data base is not reported.** In the top panel of Fig. 3a the predicted j_x > 0 line runs roughly 10–20 nm above the measured trace over the full 100 ns; in the bottom panel both predicted lines sit about 10–15 nm above the measured y positions. Fit quality is shown only for R1, and L₂(a,b) is evaluated over an unspecified "early-time relaxation window" (Methods). The authors should state the window; report how many L and R trajectories (about 16% of skyrmions) enter the fit and the resulting data-to-parameter ratio; show residuals for all fitted trajectories; explain the offsets (starting-position error, drift of the pinned boundary, an unmodelled force); and give (a*, b*) for each L/R skyrmion separately to show their spread.

6. **Tracking precision, and the stated uncertainties of a* and b*, are not established.** Recoil amplitudes are 10–30 nm (Fig. 2c,d) and the whole R1 relaxation spans about 30 nm, against a spatial resolution of about 20 nm and apparent frame-to-frame position noise of about 5–10 nm in Fig. 3a. Yet a* and b* carry ±4 nm each, with only a reference to a "loss landscape". Because b enters Eq. (1) only through exp(b/a), a and b are strongly correlated, so independent ±4 nm errors may understate the uncertainty. The authors should describe the contour or centroid algorithm and give the per-frame localization precision (for example from the scatter of static features or I skyrmions between pulses); state how this noise propagates into τ_relax, a and b, and whether uncertainty in the pinned-skyrmion boundary positions is included; show the L₂(a,b) landscape with its confidence criterion and the a–b covariance in the main text or Extended Data. For Fig. 4d, where velocities come from 1-ns position differences (a 5 nm error gives about 7 m/s noise per skyrmion), state whether the error bars reflect skyrmion-to-skyrmion spread or measurement noise.

7. **The class I "immobile" skyrmions move, which conflicts with their role as fixed reference centres.** Class I is defined as immobile and used as "reference scattering centres" and "dynamic boundary conditions", and Fig. 2a labels them as having minimal net displacement. However, the Fig. 2 caption states that the "I skyrmions [show] the strongest recoil", and Fig. 2c shows class I displacing by about +20 to +25 nm during the first pulse, opposite in sign to class M (about −30 nm), then relaxing. Separately, Methods say class M shows "minimal post-pulse relaxation", whereas Fig. 2c shows a clear ~10 nm decay for M. The authors should clarify what moves during and after the pulse for each class; explain why immobile skyrmions move against the drive; state how the measured (rather than fixed) I positions enter the inversion; and say whether I motion is itself driven by the L/R skyrmions, which would make the problem many-body rather than reference-based and leave the I motion unexplained by the model.

8. **The rigid-particle Thiele description is in tension with the observed skyrmion deformation.** The text reports "a breakdown of the rigid-particle approximation" near pinned skyrmions, with elongation and compression (Fig. 2b, skyrmion R1), yet the inversion uses the rigid-particle Thiele equation with a centre-to-centre radial force. During relaxation, shape relaxation of the deformed skyrmions may release much of the energy and would also shift the fitted centre, so the extracted "interaction potential" may mix both. The authors should quantify skyrmion area and ellipticity versus time for the L/R skyrmions after the pulse and show the deformation is small in the fitted window, or apply the same rigid-particle inversion to micromagnetic relaxation trajectories and show it recovers the potential obtained statically from simulation (for example from the energy of fixed skyrmion pairs).
Raised by: r1, r2

9. **Pump–probe averaging cannot by itself establish determinism, absence of deformation, or absence of creep.** The paper calls the dynamics "highly reproducible" and "deterministic", claims "deterministic control ... even in the incoherent flow regime" (abstract), "no discernible deformation" (Conclusion; p. 1617), and states that "a thermally activated creep regime ... is not resolved". Contrast is averaged over billions of cycles, so non-reproducible components are suppressed rather than shown absent: a skyrmion that fails to move or hops between pinning sites in some fraction of cycles would appear blurred or with reduced contrast, and its fitted centre would be biased. The authors should show the integrated XMCD contrast and apparent size and width of each tracked skyrmion versus delay compared with static images; static images taken before and after the pump–probe run (or a single pulse train) to show the configuration did not evolve or train; any single-shot images available; and, for the coherent regime (Fig. 4), a quantitative deformation measure (size, ellipticity or Delaunay bond-length distribution versus time) rather than a contour overlay. The "deterministic" and creep statements should be qualified accordingly.

10. **The 1-ns sampling limits the "no inertia" and "no sublattice delay" claims.** Fig. 4d shows v_x following the voltage pulse "without measurable delay", with about three points per 2.5-ns pulse, and the text concludes "negligible inertia effects", "free from detectable Hall and inertial effects" and "no delay or phase shift between the sublattices" within the 1-ns resolution (p. 1621). Predicted inertial lags and transient sublattice separations in SyAFMs (ref. 50) are likely sub-ns, and for ordinary Thiele dynamics the response is far below 1 ns even for ferromagnetic skyrmions, so tracking the pulse does not distinguish AFM from FM behaviour. The authors should give, from micromagnetics with the measured J_IEC ≈ 0.38 mJ m⁻², the expected magnitude and duration of the inertial lag and sublattice separation and show it would have been resolved at 20 nm and 1 ns (and 70–100 ps probe width); fit v_x(t) to the measured voltage pulse convolved with a first-order response and quote a lag time or upper bound with uncertainty; and otherwise narrow the claims to "below our resolution of X nm and 1 ns".

11. **Sublattice synchronization is inferred from separate acquisitions with partially mixed element contrast.** Fe- and Co-edge movies are recorded in separate runs ("only one X-ray energy can be used at a time"), so the claim that both sublattices follow "identical trajectories" with no delay (p. 1618, p. 1621; Supplementary Fig. 3) requires co-registration in space and time. In addition, both layers contain Fe and Co (Co0.6Fe0.2B0.2 and Fe0.6Co0.2B0.2 in sublattice A, Co0.8B0.2 in sublattice B), so each edge contrast is a weighted mixture; the Co L3 image contains an opposite-sign contribution from sublattice A of roughly 20% of the Co by thickness, and is only said to "predominantly" probe sublattice B. This reduces contrast and could bias the comparison. The authors should describe how drift and time zero were aligned between runs (for example a timing reference to the pulse edge and spatial registration on static features); quantify the fractional contribution of each sublattice to each edge contrast; and give the difference of A and B trajectories per skyrmion versus time with an uncertainty, i.e. the smallest relative sublattice displacement that could be detected.

15. **The stringency of the skyrmion Hall angle bound, and the compensation it rests on, are unclear.** θ_SkHA = 1.4° ± 1.5° comes from slope ratios over about eight or nine current values at |v_x| ≲ 50 m s⁻¹, and the Hall angle in the incoherent regime, where pinning could deflect mobile skyrmions, is not reported. 16. **Through-thickness averaging and the uniform columnar-tube assumption are not justified.** The XMCD signal averages over 25 repeats, and the potential is given "per bilayer", V/N with N = 25, which assumes rigid, uniform columnar tubes to which all repeats contribute equally. The authors' own ref. 40 reports hybrid chiral skyrmion tubes in similar SyAFM multilayers, and the current density may differ across the stack (Ta seed, top versus bottom Pt). The authors should justify the assumption, for example with the simulated through-thickness profile under current and post-pulse, and discuss how depth-dependent size, position or tilting would appear in the averaged trajectories.
