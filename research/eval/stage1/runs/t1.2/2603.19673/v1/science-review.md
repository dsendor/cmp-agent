## A. Must address

1. **The torque switching angle θc is not shown to be a signature of, or a measure of, magnetization canting.** θc is defined operationally (p. 4; Fig. 2b–d caption) as half the separation of two extrema of d(τ/H)/dθH near θH = 90°, and it is then read throughout as an equilibrium canting angle (Fig. 3d plots it in degrees against an ESR-"predicted" θc). The double-extremum structure is the central experimental evidence for canting, but the paper does not show that it is unique to a canted state. The statement on p. 3 that "in a strictly collinear ferrimagnet with a single uniaxial anisotropy, these extrema would remain pinned to the crystallographic axes" is not generally true at finite field: for a single-domain uniaxial (Stoner–Wohlfarth-type) magnet below the anisotropy field, M lags H, τ(θH) becomes sawtooth-like (as Fig. 1b itself shows at 0.8 T), and the extrema of dτ/dθH sit at field-dependent angles away from the principal axes. At high field the reading fails in another way: for H well above the anisotropy fields (|K1/M| ≈ 2.4 T, data to 9 T), M nearly follows H and dτ/dθH → −d²F_anis/dθ² evaluated near θH, so the extrema positions are set only by the shape of the anisotropy function (the ratios K1:K2:K3), not by any tilt of M. "Switching markers persist up to 9 T" is therefore the expected behaviour of any anisotropy whose second derivative has extrema off 90°, and does not show that canting "survives" or is "sustained" at high field, as the abstract says. Shape anisotropy, multi-domain reorientation and a small misalignment of the rotation plane could also produce off-axis extrema. The authors should compute d(τ/H)/dθH directly from the free energy at the measured fields and temperatures, using the measured M(T), for (i) K1 only (easy plane), (ii) K1–K2 and (iii) K1–K2–K3, overlay the results on the traces of Figs. 1b, 1c and 2b–d, show where the extrema fall in each case, and state which θM tilt (if any) θc corresponds to at each field. Fitting whole τ(θH) and d(τ/H)/dθH curves, rather than compressing each into one number, would also show whether the model reproduces the torque data beyond a single operational metric.

2. **The comparison in Fig. 3d that motivates K3 mixes different quantities and is not quantitative.** The ESR-derived K1–K2 "θc" (orange diamonds) is described as a zero-field limit, but it is compared with torque θc measured at 2, 5 and 9 T, and θc is a separation of derivative extrema in θH space that equals neither the equilibrium canting angle nor θM; no mapping between them is given. The text claims that the K1–K2 model makes θc "collapse too rapidly with increasing temperature, particularly when the Zeeman energy approaches the quartic scale", but no K1–K2 prediction at 2, 5 or 9 T is shown. The K1–K2–K3 simulation (blue curves) sits on a separate right-hand axis labelled "Simulation of θc (a.u.)", so an angle is given in arbitrary units and the model is not compared with data quantitatively. The dashed curve is computed "with B-field (15 T)", beyond the 9 T measured, and invokes an undefined "additional field-induced contribution", which contradicts the description of K3 as field-independent (p. 6). The claimed agreement with the non-monotonic temperature trend is also weak: the simulated curve peaks near 30 K at roughly 20° while the data peak at 16–19°, and the 9 T data dip at 10 K and peak at 30 K in a way the curves do not follow closely; this does not support the statement on p. 6 that the model "supports the non-monotonic temperature dependence". The authors should apply the same extrema-finding procedure to simulated torque derivatives, plot simulated θc(H, T) for the K1–K2 and K1–K2–K3 models on the same axis in degrees as the data at 2, 5 and 9 T, give residuals or χ² and the number of data points per fitted parameter, and remove or justify the 15 T curve. Only then is the claim that torque "requires a substantial sixth-order term" demonstrated.

3. **The ESR analysis that supplies K1/M and K2/M is internally inconsistent with the stated model.** (pp. 4–5; Fig. 3a–c, 3e.) First, the global fit gives g_a = 2.01 but g_c = 0.68 for H∥c. For Mn²⁺/Mn-based moments with quenched orbital moment g ≈ 2 is expected (Ref. 25 reports a value close to that), so a factor-of-three error means the model does not describe the H∥c mode, which is precisely the concave-down dispersion (Fig. 3c) cited as the key ESR evidence for canting. Treating g_c as "an effective parameter" leaves only near-plane geometries (90° and 80°) as constraints, which undercuts the claim that ESR "pins down K1/M and K2/M reliably", and K1/M and K2/M come from this same fit. Second, the ESR window (≤ 1.5 T) is well below the c-axis instability field implied by the paper's own K1 (curvature at θM = 0 requires H > −2K1/M ≈ 4.8 T), so for H∥c the sample is in a reoriented, probably multi-domain state where domain modes, demagnetization and the ferrimagnetic exchange branch matter and a single-macrospin Smit–Beljers treatment may fail; the concave-down shape need not imply canting. Third, the free energy has no azimuthal dependence, so in the Smit–Beljers formula Fφφ = 0 at zero field for a moment off the c axis and the uniform mode should be gapless; yet the fitted curves reach finite zero-field frequencies (about 10 GHz for H∥ab, about 5 GHz for H∥c), and those zero-field frequencies differ between orientations even though the zero-field state is the same. This is impossible with the stated F unless in-plane anisotropy, demagnetization or an exchange mode is included. Fourth, a two- or three-sublattice (Mn1/Mn2) ferrimagnet also has exchange modes, and whether the observed branch is the quasi-ferromagnetic mode, and whether an exchange mode or mode mixing appears in 1–60 GHz, is not discussed. The authors should write the resonance condition actually used in the main text (every term in F and the γ used); refit with g fixed near 2 for all orientations and report how K1/M and K2/M change, with residuals and the K1–K2 covariance; include demagnetization for the measured sample shape (a thin-plate term of order μ0Ms enters K1 directly) and misalignment; compare the model's zero-field gap with the observed one; and, if needed to reproduce H∥c, adopt a two-sublattice ferrimagnetic resonance model or show that a collinear easy-plane model in the multi-domain regime cannot produce the concave-down branch at g ≈ 2. If g_c cannot be brought near 2, the H∥c branch should not be used as support for canting.

4. **The final K1–K2–K3 parameters imply a large zero-field tilt that is never checked against magnetization or neutron data.** Minimising F = K1 sin²θ + K2 sin⁴θ + K3 sin⁶θ with the Fig. 3e values gives sin²θM ≈ 0.56 at 5 K (K1/M ≈ −2.4 T, K2/M ≈ 1.4 T, K3/M ≈ 0.9 T), i.e. the moment about 42° out of the ab plane, and about 46° at 40 K (K1/M ≈ −1.4, K2/M ≈ 0.7, K3/M ≈ 1.1 T); at 20–40 K K1 + K2 + K3 ≥ 0, so the in-plane direction is no lower in energy than H∥c. The K1–K2-only solution gives about 22° (the ESR "θc" at 5 K), so adding K3 roughly doubles the implied tilt, yet K1 and K2 are held at their K3 = 0 ESR values (p. 5). Such a state should show a large c-axis component in M(H), a low-field c-axis susceptibility comparable to the in-plane one, and a cone angle that the neutron study (Ref. 21) would have detected, whereas Mn3Si2Te6 is usually described as easy-plane with a hard c axis and Fig. 1d shows a much larger in-plane response. SQUID data were taken along both directions (Methods) but only dχ/dT is shown. The model also predicts the H∥c instability field (≈ 4.8 T at 5 K), which bears on Fig. 4b–c, where the 5 T data reach only cos θM ≈ 0.6 at θH = 0. The authors should show M(H) along c and ab at 5, 20 and 40 K with the model's predictions, state the zero-field θM implied at each temperature and compare it quantitatively with Ref. 21, use M(T) to convert K/M into energies, and refit the ESR with K3 included at the torque-derived value so that the parameter set is self-consistent rather than built in stages.

5. **The CAMR reparameterization (Fig. 4) mixes samples and conditions, and the collapse is partly imposed rather than tested.** The resistivity in Fig. 4a is from Seo et al. (Ref. 9), on a different crystal from a different growth, at a temperature not stated anywhere; Fig. 4b does not say which temperature's K1–K3 set produced θM(θH), although Fig. 3e shows strong temperature dependence and activated transport makes the slope ∝ A/kBT. The slope (−47.9) is fitted to the 1 T curve only and the 2, 3 and 5 T curves are vertically shifted by hand with large, unexplained offsets (ln(ρ/ρ0) maxima from ~9 to ~19); the 5 T data bend away from the line over much of the range. The offsets are needed because ρ0 = ρ(θH = 0) corresponds to a different θM at each field (at 1–2 T the magnetization is far from θM = 0 when H∥c), so ln(ρ/ρ0) is not referenced to Δ(θM = 0) as the p. 7 formula assumes. The "weak hysteretic effect" correction that places the maximum of ln(ρ/ρ0) at cos θM = 0 is not specified and builds part of the result in, and several 1 T and 2 T points sit at cos θM < 0 without explanation. Fig. 4b shows dθM/dθH well below 1 near θH = 90° (the 1 T curve is nearly flat), so 0–90° in θH maps to cos θM ≈ 0–0.23 (Fig. 4c): the near-plane feature is compressed in θM, not "substantially broader" as stated on p. 6, and over so narrow a window nearly any smooth function is linear, so the improvement over the cos θH inset may be a generic field-dependent stretch. The authors should state the transport temperature and use K1–K3 at that temperature; describe the hysteresis correction and show the uncorrected mapping; fit the near-plane slope independently at each field with uncertainties instead of imposing the 1 T slope; compare against null mappings (K1-only, K1–K2-only, or a one-parameter per-field rescaling of cos θH) to show that K3 is needed for the collapse; give units for the slope, translate it into the gap-modulation amplitude (A ≈ 47.9 kBT) and compare with the SOC-induced gap changes calculated in Ref. 9; and ideally measure CAMR on a crystal from the same batch at a temperature where K1–K3 are known. At minimum the comparison should be presented as qualitative, with the sample and temperature differences stated.

6. **"Canted ferrimagnet" (sublattice non-collinearity) is not distinguished from a tilted collinear macrospin (easy-cone state).** The free energy on p. 4 describes one macrospin. With K1/M = −2.40 T and K2/M = 1.40 T its zero-field minimum is at sin²θM = −K1/(2K2) ≈ 0.86, an easy-cone state with the net moment about 22° out of the plane. That is a statement about the direction of the net moment and says nothing about non-collinearity between the Mn1 and Mn2 sublattices, which is what "canted ferrimagnetism" usually means and what the neutron study (Ref. 21) addresses; a ferrimagnet with canted sublattices and a collinear ferrimagnet with easy-cone anisotropy give the same macrospin F. The title, abstract and conclusions slide between the two meanings and cite Ref. 21 as support ("consistent with a non-collinear canted state reported by neutron scattering", p. 3). The authors should state which canting is meant and either describe the state as an effective easy-cone / tilted-magnetization state throughout, or show quantitative agreement of the cone angle and its temperature dependence with the neutron-determined moment direction, or adopt a two-sublattice model (intersublattice exchange plus sublattice-specific anisotropy) that produces sublattice canting and relate its parameters to K1–K3.

## B. Should address

7. **"Coherent" (single-domain) behaviour is assumed, not demonstrated.** An easy-cone state in a trigonal crystal is degenerate under ±θM and in azimuth, so domains are expected at low field, whereas "coherent" implies a uniformly canted single-domain state; nothing in the paper tests this. It matters most for Sample #1 at 0.8 T (Fig. 1b,c), where the "purely torque-based indication" of canting is extracted, because domain averaging, domain-wall pinning and hysteresis can shift extrema in d(τ/H)/dθH. Hysteresis below 0.5 T is acknowledged (p. 4, Fig. S1, the "weak hysteretic effect" on pp. 6–7), Fig. 1b shows abrupt torque changes near the hard axis, and Fig. 4b shows a jump at θH = 180°. The authors should define "coherent" operationally and support it, for example with forward and reverse rotations in the main text at low and high field showing that extrema positions are independent of sweep direction and field history, reversibility above some field, scaling of the torque amplitude with the expected single-domain value, or evidence that the ESR line is a single uniform mode; otherwise the word should be dropped.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **K1, K2 and K3 rest on few, weakly constrained data, which undercuts their temperature trend.** ESR at 20 and 40 K was measured only for H∥ab (symbols in Fig. 3a), so K1 and K2 are strongly covariant there: K1/M at 40 K has error bars of roughly ±1.2 T, K2/M at 40 K is consistent with zero, and the ESR θc at 40 K is 4.7° ± ~6° (Fig. 3d). The 20 and 40 K ESR points nearly overlap the 5 K dispersion even though K1/M is claimed to change from −2.4 to −1.4 T. K3/M is then fitted separately at only 5, 20 and 40 K to a few θc(H) values with K1 and K2 held fixed, and those come from a different sample (Sample #3, ESR) than the torque (Sample #2). The claim that K1–K2 "collapses too rapidly with temperature" and the crossing of K2 and K3 depend on these points. The authors should report how the error bars were obtained, the covariance matrix of (K1, K2, K3) at each temperature and the number of data points used; explain how K1 changes by ~40% while the H∥ab resonance hardly moves; test whether K3 > 0 survives when K1 and K2 vary within their errors; measure ESR at θH = 80° and 0° at 20 and 40 K; and preferably fit the full torque curves jointly with the ESR dispersions, reporting goodness of fit with and without K3. Calibrating the cantilever for absolute torque (with sample mass) would fix K/M independently of ESR and test consistency across samples.

9. **Torque artifacts and calibration are not quantified, though the analysis depends on extrema positions accurate to about a degree.** The paper does not give the zero-angle calibration (how θH = 90° is located), the angular resolution, or the misalignment of the rotation plane relative to c for the mechanical rotators (Samples #2 and #4) and the vector magnet (Sample #1); out-of-plane misalignment changes the torque near the hard axis. Nor does it give the cantilever deflection, and hence the angle error, at 9 T for these strongly anisotropic crystals; piezoresistor nonlinearity near the hard axis, where Fig. 1b shows abrupt jumps; the empty-cantilever background and piezoresistor magnetoresistance; or how extrema are located in the numerically differentiated signal (smoothing, peak fitting) and their uncertainty (Fig. 3d has no error bars on θc). The model also holds M fixed, whereas the ferrimagnet is not saturated and M depends on H and θH; the "field-induced contribution" mentioned for Fig. 3d is not developed. The authors should give these numbers, describe angle calibration, background subtraction and deflection corrections, put error bars on θc, include M(H, θH) from SQUID in the model, and show that θc is insensitive to these effects, for example by showing that extrema positions do not depend on cantilever orientation or sample size (Samples #2 versus #4 at matched T and H).

10. **The choice of anisotropy terms ignores trigonal symmetry and the in-plane azimuth.** Mn3Si2Te6 is trigonal (P-31c). Besides uniaxial sin^(2n)θ terms, symmetry allows terms such as sin³θ cosθ cos3φ and sin⁶θ cos6φ. The first is antisymmetric about the plane for a given φ and can shift torque extrema away from θH = 90° and 270°, the very signature used for canting (Fig. 1c); the six-fold in-plane term affects the H∥ab zero-field ESR gap and could contribute to the apparent "K3" stiffness. Calling K3 sin⁶θ "the simplest symmetry-allowed extension" is therefore incomplete. The rotation plane is described only as "an in-plane direction", and Fig. 1a labels the axes [100], [001] and [110] with [100] apparently along the vertical, which does not match "θH measured from the c-axis". The authors should specify the in-plane azimuth ([100] versus [110]) of the rotation plane for each sample, state whether K1 includes demagnetization and give the sample dimensions for the ESR and torque crystals, check whether θc depends on azimuth (one additional rotation plane, or an in-plane torque rotation, would suffice), and justify neglecting the φ-dependent terms.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

11. **Parameters from different crystals are combined without characterization or shown reproducibility.** Each technique uses a different crystal: #1 for 0.8 T vector-magnet torque, #2 for the θc(H, T) map, #3 for ESR, #4 for PPMS torque (which appears nowhere in the main text), plus transport from another study. Reproducibility is deferred to Supplementary Section 2, which was not provided. Crystals are grown from a Mn-poor flux (1:2:6 molar ratio), and stoichiometry and Te vacancies in this compound are known to affect Tc and transport, yet no XRD, composition (EDS/WDS) or Ms is given. The authors should provide structural and compositional characterization, Ms and Tc for each crystal; show θc(T, H) from at least two samples (e.g. #2 and #4, ideally torque on #3) on the same axes in the main text with the device-to-device spread quantified; and comment on how sample-to-sample variation in K1/M, K2/M and Tc affects the combined fit.

12. **The temperature and field evolution of the anomaly is inconsistent between figures.** At 0.8 T the anomaly "emerges below 10 K" and the polar plot is "predominantly two-fold" above 20 K (Fig. 1c, p. 3), but in Fig. 2 and Fig. 3d θc ≈ 12–19° persists at 20–50 K (features to 50–70 K) at 2–9 T and is non-monotonic in T with a maximum near 30 K.
