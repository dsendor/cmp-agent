## A. Must address

1. **Laser-assisted photoemission (LAPE) of the Y-point valence band may produce the early Y-valley "conduction band" signal under AC pumping.** The VBM sits at the Y valleys (Fig. 1b). The conduction-band window in Fig. 1(a) is E − E_VBM = 1.15 ± 0.18 eV, almost exactly where the first pump sideband of the VBM falls (VBM + 1.2 eV). The pump is s-polarized, but its in-plane field still dresses photoelectrons, with an amplitude that scales roughly as E·k∥. That amplitude is largest at Y when the field lies along AC and vanishes at Y when it lies along ZZ. A LAPE replica of the Y-point VBM would therefore appear only under AC pumping and only near temporal overlap. This is exactly the behaviour assigned to optical injection into Y (Fig. 2b, 2f): the Y intensity peaks at about 0 fs and decays within about 200–300 fs, only about twice the 135 fs pump duration convolved with the probe. Rotating the crystal (p. 6) removes the s/p asymmetry but not this E·k selectivity, and the paper raises LAPE only to justify not using p-polarization; it does not show the effect is negligible in the geometry actually used. The initial positive valley polarization, and hence the "reversal" in Fig. 2(h), rests on this early Y signal, so it is the central result. The same check should cover the X-valley signal near t = 0. The authors should: show energy–momentum cuts through the Y valley at t ≈ 0 and compare them with a valence-band replica shifted by 1.2 eV (a sideband reproduces the hole-like valence dispersion, while real conduction electrons are electron-like); compare the time trace of the symmetric sideband at VBM − 1.2 eV, which cannot contain conduction-band population, with the Y-valley trace; compare the Y-valley rise and decay with the measured pump–probe cross-correlation (for example from a non-resonant LAPE feature or a metal reference); analyze negative delays, where a pump-dressed signal should be symmetric about t₀; and show the fluence dependence of the Y signal at t = 0, since a sideband scales linearly with pump intensity and a population does not. A measurement with the pump polarized at 45°, or at a lower pump photon energy, would be a further control.

2. **How the experimental valley polarization is extracted is not specified, and as plotted it may not measure population.** The Fig. 2 caption defines valley polarization only as the "normalized difference between intensity within CB X and Y valleys". The sign convention is not given; from Fig. 2(g) (≈ −1 when X is populated) it appears to be P = (I_Y − I_X)/(I_Y + I_X). Figs. 2(e,f) plot "Norm. Int.", and in Fig. 2(f) both experimental X and Y traces peak at ≈ 1, which suggests each trace was normalized to its own maximum. If P in Fig. 2(g,h) was computed from these separately normalized curves, its value at every delay, including the zero crossing and the reversal to ≈ −0.8, is an artefact of the normalization rather than a population ratio. The same question applies to the TDBE curves: is P computed from absolute populations or from rescaled curves? The integration procedure is also not described: which energy window and momentum radius define "X" and "Y" in the Fig. 2 traces (the ±0.18 eV window of Fig. 1(a) is given only for the contour), whether the windows enclose the same phase-space volume, whether hot electrons above the valley minimum are included, and how background (pre-time-zero signal, pump-only multiphoton emission, space charge, residual pump-induced secondary electrons) was subtracted. The early AC map (Fig. 2b) has a high, structured background, and windows that are too wide would mix it in and bias P toward zero. Finally, P is plotted at negative delays (≈ +1 near −200 fs in Fig. 2h, and the TDBE curve starts at +1 before the pump), where both intensities are at background and P is undefined. Since "nearly unity", "reversal" and "excellent agreement" all rest on P, the authors should state the formula and sign convention explicitly, state whether raw background-subtracted counts in matched volumes were used, show the unnormalized per-valley X and Y intensities for both pump geometries on a common scale, compute P the same way for experiment and theory, mask P where the signal is below background, and show that P is robust to reasonable variations of the integration windows. They should also state whether the per-panel colour normalization of Figs. 2(a–d) was carried into the traces.

3. **The "nonvolatile" channel and the novelty framing are not supported as stated.** The ZZ channel is called "nearly nonvolatile (time-independent)" in the abstract and on pp. 4, 11 and 14, but the delays stop at +600 fs. More fundamentally, ZZ pumping injects electrons directly into X, the global conduction-band minimum, so a constant −1 polarization is what any carrier relaxation predicts; it is also the end state under AC pumping (Fig. 2h, Fig. S3), and the authors themselves write "as expected for semiconductors" in S3. Both channels end in the same X-valley state, so neither stores which valley was addressed. The paper's own Fig. S3(c,e) shows that under ZZ pumping the holes depolarize and reverse on the same timescale, so the electron–hole pair, and any optical readout of it, is not nonvolatile in either channel. Unidirectional relaxation toward the lower valley is generic to nondegenerate multivalley semiconductors (Γ→L/X transfer in GaAs and Ge, the Si/diamond work of Ref. 32, and Ref. 38 on SnS), which have been studied extensively with ultrafast optics and photoemission. The comparison on p. 11 with WS₂ monolayers "under similar conditions" sets excitonic, circularly pumped, degenerate K/K′ valleys in a monolayer against free-carrier, linearly pumped, nondegenerate valleys in a bulk crystal. The claims of "coexistence of one nearly nonvolatile … and one highly volatile channel", an operating principle "fundamentally distinct from all existing valleytronic platforms" (p. 14), and the "first observation of ultrafast valleytronics in group-IV monochalcogenides" therefore overstate what is shown. The authors should extend the delay range to several picoseconds, at least for ZZ, or restrict the wording to the measured window; define "nonvolatile" quantitatively as a lifetime bound with uncertainty; discuss thermal repopulation of Y at room temperature given the X–Y offset; reframe the result as asymmetric, unidirectional intervalley relaxation set by the X–Y energy offset, and say which quantity could serve as valley memory and which timescales (Y-valley lifetime vs X-valley carrier lifetime) would set any valleytronic function; place the work against the prior multivalley hot-carrier literature; and present the WS₂ comparison as a qualitative contrast only.

## B. Should address

4. **Photoemission intensity is used as a proxy for valley population without correcting for matrix elements, probe geometry or k_z.** To switch from ZZ to AC pumping the crystal is rotated by 90° while the 65°-incidence XUV beam stays fixed, so the X and Y valleys sit at different positions relative to the XUV plane of incidence and polarization in the two configurations. The X (Sn p_x-like) and Y (p_y-like) conduction states (p. 14) therefore have different photoemission cross-sections, and these differ between the two azimuths. Fig. 2 itself shows that intensity is not simply proportional to population: in Fig. 2(b,d) the X spot at k_ZZ ≈ +1.1 Å⁻¹ is much brighter than the one at −1.1 Å⁻¹, and in Fig. 2(a,c) the spots at ±1.1–1.15 Å⁻¹ differ strongly from those at ±0.4–0.45 Å⁻¹. The momentum positions also need explaining: Fig. 1(c) places X and Y at ≈ 0.35–0.37 Å⁻¹, while the strongest spots in Fig. 2 are at ≈ 1.1–1.2 Å⁻¹, presumably replicas in the next Brillouin zone; it is not said which replicas were integrated. In addition, bulk SnSe is probed at a single photon energy (21.6 eV), so X and Y may be sampled at different, unspecified k_z, whereas the TDBE averages over k_z. A valley-dependent cross-section rescales I_X/I_Y and moves the apparent zero crossing and asymptote of P, and a geometry-dependent one makes the ZZ-versus-AC comparison inexact; without calibration, a value near ±1 cannot be read as "nearly unity" population polarization. The authors should calibrate the relative X/Y photoemission efficiency in each geometry, for example from the X/Y ratio at long delays (when both configurations should reach the same quasi-equilibrium), from the ground-state valence bands at both azimuths, from calculated cross-sections, by applying matrix-element-weighted integration to the TDBE output, or with a 45° pump that populates both valleys in a predictable ratio. They should report P separately for each symmetry-equivalent valley, state which replicas were integrated and mark the zone boundaries on Fig. 2, give the k_z probed and discuss how it compares with the k_z-averaged calculation, and show how the polarization curves change under reasonable variations of the relative scale.

5. **The TDBE is run at a different excitation condition from the experiment, so "fully captures" and "excellent agreement" are not established.** The simulation pumps at 0.8 eV, resonant with the PBE gap, while the experiment uses 1.2 eV (pp. 5, 15, 17), so the excess energies in the X and Y valleys differ between theory and experiment and between the two valleys. The experimental excess energy is never stated (the CB feature at 1.15 ± 0.18 eV above the VBM implies a small one, which is not discussed). The paper says detuning is "not critical" (p. 5) and that detuning "up to ∼300 meV … still leads to near-unity initial valley polarization" (p. 23), but Fig. S2(d) shows the zero crossing of the AC polarization moving by about 100 fs between Δ = 0 and 100 meV, comparable to the timescale being compared in Fig. 2(h), and Fig. S1(c) shows the ZZ (red) curve rising well above −1 by 1.1 eV. The direction and rate of Y→X scattering, and back-scattering at 300 K, depend on the Y–X conduction-band offset relative to k_BT and to the ≈ 20 meV phonon energy; this offset comes from PBE without stated spin–orbit or van der Waals treatment and with lattice constants (4.226, 4.547, 11.830 Å) about 2–3 % larger than experiment, especially a₃, and it is not validated, although the trARPES data could measure it. It is also unclear how "the same pump pulse … intensity as in the experiments" maps onto carrier density when absorption at 0.8 eV (DFT) and 1.2 eV differs. The 20 fs dephasing time is a free parameter whose effect on the initial polarization and early dynamics is not tested, and the alignment of experimental and simulated time zero is not described. Because the X populations disagree (concern 7), the agreement in the polarization ratio may be partly fortuitous. The authors should report the measured and calculated X–Y conduction-band offset and direct gaps; run the TDBE with a scissor-corrected band structure or a detuning matched to the experimental excess energy in each valley (≈ 0.3–0.4 eV), or show insensitivity across that full range; show sensitivity to τ_dph and to the time-zero alignment; compare fitted Y-decay and zero-crossing times with uncertainties for theory and experiment; correct the S1/S2 statements on detuning; and revise "fully captured" and "excellent agreement" accordingly.

6. **The attribution of Y→X transfer to the ≈ 20 meV optical branch is theoretical only, rests on an indirect proxy, and is stated too strongly.** The mode identification (Fig. 3) comes from nonequilibrium phonon "temperatures" 600 fs after the pump, which depend on each mode's heat capacity, phase-space weight and other heating processes. ΔT = T^AC − T^ZZ also mixes intervalley emission with intravalley cooling of hot carriers, which differs between configurations because the excess energies differ. The text says that under ZZ pumping "only optical modes around Γ are populated, since only intravalley scattering is possible" (p. 13), but Fig. S3(c,e) shows strong X→Y hole intervalley scattering under ZZ pumping, which should emit phonons near the same wavevector; ΔT therefore compares electron intervalley scattering (AC) against hole intervalley scattering (ZZ), and hole contributions under AC are not separated. The X–Y momentum transfer from Fig. 1(c), q ≈ (0.37, −0.30) Å⁻¹, is not exactly along Γ–M, yet the analysis is restricted to Γ–M ("due to momentum conservation … phonons along the Γ–M direction", p. 13), the emitted phonons are spread along Γ–M rather than localized at that q, Fig. 3(c) shows the largest ΔT at or near Γ (which looks intravalley), and several other optical modes also show positive ΔT. The glide-symmetry argument (pp. 13–14, SI S4) only excludes modes odd under M̄_xy and does not single out the second-highest branch. SnSe is polar (the authors' Ref. 50 stresses Fröhlich coupling) and strongly anharmonic at 300 K, but the Methods do not say whether the long-range polar term was included in the Wannier interpolation of g, which affects intravalley rates and so the competition between channels, and the harmonic DFPT phonons without phonon–phonon scattering may misrepresent the 600 fs phonon temperatures. The authors should report branch-resolved Y→X scattering rates (or the intervalley spectral function) at the actual momentum transfer, with a 2D q-resolved map and electron and hole contributions separated; test whether switching off the identified branch removes the reversal; explain the Γ-centred ΔT; label the attribution as theoretical and soften "unambiguously identify" (p. 13). An experimental test, such as the temperature dependence of the AC decay time (n_q vs n_q + 1, e.g. 80 K vs 300 K) or a coherent-phonon or diffraction signature of the predicted mode, would substantially strengthen it.

7. **The late-time X-valley discrepancy is explained by an implausible mechanism.** After AC pumping, the simulated X population keeps rising from 200 to 600 fs while the measured X signal peaks near 200 fs and then decays (Fig. 2f). The authors attribute this to electron–hole recombination missing from the model (p. 10), but sub-picosecond recombination in an indirect-gap bulk semiconductor at room temperature is unlikely, and the flat X signal under ZZ pumping (Fig. 2e) argues against it. More likely causes are hot electrons cooling below the lower edge of the 1.15 ± 0.18 eV window, carrier diffusion out of the few-ångström to nm probing depth (the TDBE is homogeneous bulk), surface or defect trapping, scattering into other valleys or bands, k_z selection at 21.6 eV, or the normalization issue of concern 2. If carriers leave the probed region, the agreement in valley polarization (Fig. 2h), which comes from populations that differ by about a factor of two at 600 fs, may arise from compensating errors rather than correct Y→X rates. The authors should show energy-resolved (E vs t) traces for each valley, the X signal under both pumps on a common scale and over longer delays, and the signal integrated over the whole conduction band, and discuss which mechanism applies.

8. **Excitation density, fluence and heating are not characterized.** At 0.9 mJ/cm² and 1.2 eV the excited density in SnSe is likely ≳ 10²⁰ cm⁻³, but neither the absorbed fluence nor the carrier density is given and only one fluence is shown. At such densities electron–electron and electron–hole scattering, screening of the electron–phonon coupling, band-gap renormalization (which could change the X–Y offset that controls the dynamics) and hot-phonon effects can all alter intervalley rates, and none is in the TDBE, which has only electron–phonon collisions plus phenomenological dephasing; this is needed to support phonon scattering as "the main driver". Two-photon absorption (2 × 1.2 eV, well above the gap), cumulative lattice heating at 166 kHz and space charge are also not addressed. Because SnSe is optically anisotropic, s-polarized light at 65° incidence along AC and along ZZ is reflected and absorbed differently, so the two configurations may not have matched carrier densities or depths, and it is unclear whether the simulation field E₀ is the vacuum field or the internal one. The authors should give the absorbed densities for both configurations, show the X and Y dynamics and polarization for at least two or three fluences in the AC case (a phonon-limited mechanism predicts a density-independent τ(Y→X)), and quantify space-charge shifts.

9. **Time resolution and time zero are not given, and no timescales are extracted.** The XUV pulse duration, the pump–probe cross-correlation, the delay step and the method for determining t₀ are not reported, and the paper suggests X population appears "during the pump pulse" without a stated time resolution. In Fig. 2(e) the measured X rise is visibly delayed relative to the TDBE, which may reflect a t₀ offset. The "sub-picosecond" decay and reversal are not quantified by any fit, and Fig. 2(e–h) has no error bars. The authors should report the instrument response and t₀ determination, fit the X and Y traces from both experiment and TDBE with a rate model convolved with the cross-correlation, give τ(Y→X), rise times and the AC zero-crossing time with uncertainties, and add error bars from counting statistics or repeated scans.

10. **Sample statistics, reproducibility and crystal alignment are not documented.** The paper does not say how many crystals or cleaves were measured, whether the AC/ZZ contrast was reproduced, whether the two configurations, measured sequentially, come from the same cleave and spot (a 90° rotation can move the 70 × 140 µm pump and 45 × 35 µm probe footprints onto a different terrace), or whether surface degradation over the acquisition time affected the comparison. SnSe often contains 90° twin domains because the in-plane lattice constants are close, and a domain mixture or azimuthal misalignment would mix ZZ and AC excitation; the clean ZZ result argues against this, but the alignment accuracy is not given. The authors should state how the crystal axes were identified (for example from the VBM position in Fig. 1a or the symmetry of constant-energy maps) and the misalignment uncertainty, report the number of independent datasets showing each result, and show the result on at least one additional cleave or sample.

11. **The hole-dynamics claims are theory-only, though the experiment could test them.** On pp. 10–11 the text says holes "undergo an ultrafast valley polarization reversal under ZZ pumping" and stay polarized under AC pumping, based only on TDBE (Fig. S3), without saying that no measurement supports this. The momentum microscope also records the valence band (Fig. 1a), so pump-induced depletion at the VBM (Y) and at the secondary valence maximum along ZZ (X) for both geometries would directly test the predicted electron–hole mirror behaviour and give an independent check of the model. The authors should show these data or label the hole results clearly as predictions.
