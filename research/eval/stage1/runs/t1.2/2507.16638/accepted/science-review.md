A. Must address

1. The headline "≈20 nm" (19 ± 2 nm) domain height seems to come mainly from the fixed final FM volume fraction and the scenario assumption, not from an independent measurement by the strain pulse. In scenario III the Methods impose D_FM(t) = V*_FM, and V*_FM = 0.46 is fixed from integrated Bragg intensities. With d_layer = 44 nm this gives D_FM·d_layer ≈ 20 nm, or ≈ 18 nm if the 5.5 nm "inactive" bottom layer is excluded (44 − 5.5 ≈ 38.5 nm). The χ² minimisation in Fig. 4c is then carried out over V*_FM, the same quantity, and the ±2 nm comes from that χ²(V*_FM) curvature. Once a continuous near-surface layer is assumed, the "out-of-plane domain size of 19 nm" is therefore largely a restatement of the measured FM fraction. The sonography mainly chooses among discrete parameterisations (I–V). Fig. S2 varies the domain height directly, but only at t = 9 ps. Scenario IV is represented by one arbitrary choice, D_FM = 1.3 V*_FM ("as an example", Fig. 3 caption and Methods), and is not scanned. This matters because the abstract says that modelling the strain response "identifies the nucleation of domains of approximately 20 nm height". That wording requires the height to be a fitted quantity that the strain signatures constrain. The authors should fit D_FM and A_FM as independent parameters over the full sonogram, with V_FM = D_FM·A_FM not fixed in advance. This would scan the mixed family continuously from D_FM/V*_FM = 1 (scenario III) to 1/V*_FM (scenario I), ideally with the depth position of the FM region as a further parameter. They should also run a fit to the strain dynamics alone that ignores the integrated intensity. They should show the two-dimensional χ²(D_FM, A_FM) map with its confidence region, and say explicitly how much of the 19 nm figure comes from the strain dynamics and how much from V*_FM.

B. Should address

2. The statistical basis for preferring scenario III over IV, over I, and over intermediate cases is not established. In Fig. 3a,c,d the deviations (grey areas) for scenarios I, III and IV look similar in size. In Fig. 4c the minima of the global residuals for III, IV and I differ by only about 10–20% (≈0.085 against ≈0.09–0.10). The residual χ² = Σ|ΔI|² carries no measurement uncertainties, no noise model and no count of degrees of freedom, so it is not a reduced χ² and its differences cannot be read as significance. Yet the text calls the comparison "quantitative" and says III "optimally describes" the data. If the gap between III and IV (or I) is within what noise, the 10% intensity threshold, or the fixed model inputs (concern 4) can produce, then neither the conclusion of out-of-plane rather than mixed heterogeneity nor the domain height is determined. The authors should weight χ² by the measured shot-to-shot, train-to-train or delay-to-delay uncertainty in I(t, q_z), obtained from the i0-normalised unpumped references or repeated delays. They should report reduced χ² values with confidence intervals and a model-selection criterion (likelihood ratio or information criterion) that accounts for the different numbers of effective parameters. They should state whether III, IV and I differ significantly at 7.7 mJ cm⁻² and at each fluence in Fig. S4, and show how the ranking changes when the fixed inputs are varied. Residual maps ΔI(t, q_z) for each scenario, shown next to Fig. S1, would show which delays and features actually discriminate between them.

3. The extracted domain height may be degenerate with the optical penetration depth, which was tuned in the model. The Methods say the thermophysical parameters come from earlier calibrations, but "only the optical penetration depth is optimized to account for the different incident angle". The paper's own interpretation is that "D_FM can be approximated by the depth until which the optical excitation overcomes the equilibrium transition threshold". The thermal stress that launches the strain pulse follows the same absorption profile. The fitted penetration depth and the fitted ~19 nm FM depth are therefore not independent: both shape the same near-surface strain profile. Unless this is resolved, the sonogram has not been shown to measure a "domain height" as distinct from the absorption depth the model already encodes. The authors should state the optimised penetration depth and how it was obtained, including whether it was optimised under the same scenario that is then selected. They should compare it with the value expected from the FeRh optical constants at 800 nm and ~25° incidence. They should show a two-dimensional χ² map over (penetration depth, D_FM), or at least the χ² minimum in D_FM for several fixed penetration depths.

4. The ±2 nm uncertainty covers only the χ² curvature in V*_FM (Fig. 4c) and leaves out the model systematics. The scenario discrimination and the domain height depend on several fixed or ad hoc inputs:
   - the sound velocity of 5.0 nm ps⁻¹ from ref. 44, assumed equal in both phases;
   - the 1.32 GPa transformation stress, applied instantaneously at nucleation;
   - the Grüneisen parameters and the two-temperature model;
   - the fixed nucleation time τ = 8 ps;
   - the assumption, made "for an overall better agreement", that the bottom 5.5 nm of FeRh does not contribute to the Bragg peak.

   Each of these shifts strain-pulse transit times, and with them D_FM (Eq. 1). A 5–10% change in v_s alone corresponds to ~1–2 nm. The dead layer changes both the effective thickness and the acoustic path that distinguishes surface from backside nucleation (III against V, I against III). The Methods say the conclusions "do not depend decisively" on these details but show no evidence. The authors should vary each input within its literature or calibration uncertainty, with the dead layer varied from 0 to about 8 nm, and recompute χ² for scenarios I, III and IV. They should report a systematic error budget next to the statistical one and attach the combined uncertainty to the abstract's "approximately 20 nm". Table S1 should mark which parameters were free and which were fixed in each fit.

5. The sample appears to have changed during the experiment, and reproducibility is not shown. The Methods attribute the inactive bottom 5.5 nm to "interface diffusion of FeRh and W due to too strong laser excitations at a high-repetition rate", so the measurement itself altered the sample irreversibly. Only one sample is measured, so stability checks are the only evidence that the result is not tied to a damaged region. The assumption also directly changes the film geometry that separates scenario III from V and sets the acoustic reflection times. Interdiffusion could also make the FM fraction or the transition temperature depth-dependent near the substrate, which bears on the I/III/V discrimination. The authors should give evidence for the 5.5 nm figure, such as post-mortem XRR or TEM, or a fit with this thickness free. They should say whether the sonograms used were recorded before or after the damage, and in what order the fluences were measured. They should check whether the static Bragg peaks (position, width, AFM intensity, AFM/FM ratio at negative delays) drifted over time. They should show reproducibility, either the same fluence measured at the start and end of the series or the 7.7 mJ cm⁻² sonogram repeated on a fresh spot. They should also say whether the degradation affects the top Pt interface, and show that the conclusions hold when the dead layer is set to zero or varied.

6. The X-ray footprint samples a range of laser fluences, and this could mimic lateral heterogeneity. The 50 µm X-ray focus at 21.9° incidence gives a footprint of ≈ 50/sin 21.9° ≈ 130–135 µm along the beam. That is comparable to the 136 × 142 µm FWHM pump spot, which is itself elongated on the sample by the ≈ 25° pump incidence. Local fluence, and with it V_FM and the threshold depth, therefore varies substantially across the probed area. An incoherent sum over this fluence distribution would look like lateral coexistence (A_FM < 1), a spread of domain heights and broadened Bragg peaks. It could also bias the single extracted D_FM. This bears directly on the low-fluence assignment to scenario IV (A*_FM = 0.66 at 3.9 mJ cm⁻²), where incomplete in-plane coverage might simply reflect footprint edges below threshold, and on the interpretation as a laterally heterogeneous transition temperature. The authors should report the footprint and the projected pump-spot geometry on the sample, with orientations. They should state whether 7.7 mJ cm⁻² is the peak or the footprint-averaged fluence, and explain how the 3.9–11.7 mJ cm⁻² range was calculated from 1–3 µJ at 25° incidence. They should then include the footprint-weighted fluence distribution in the model, as a fluence-averaged scenario III with D_FM following the local fluence, or show that its effect is negligible.

7. The nucleation kinetics are modelled with one fixed time constant, while the Discussion invokes several. Eq. (3) and the model use a single exponential with τ = 8 ps everywhere in the film, randomly distributed nucleation delays, and a stress that appears instantaneously upon nucleation. The Discussion, however, explains the depth-dependent rise times seen in earlier UXRD work by an 8 ps and a 50 ps pathway acting at different depths (ref. 37). If slower nucleation occurs deeper, D_FM(t) is no longer the simple form assumed in scenarios I–V, and the depth fitted over 0–35 ps could be biased. Because the analysis relies entirely on the forward model (concern 10), the conclusion should be shown to hold under other kinetic models. The authors should refit τ, test a two-component rise or a depth-dependent nucleation rate, and show whether the selected scenario and D_FM survive. Otherwise they should temper the claim that the data "relate" to these pathways.

8. The model omits phase-boundary acoustics that could mimic the scenario signatures. The FM/AFM boundary is treated as acoustically transparent. Changes of sound velocity, density, elastic constants and acoustic impedance between the phases are ignored, as are phase-dependent electron–phonon coupling, thermal expansion and any non-thermal electronic stress. The authors argue these effects are small in FeRh (refs. 40, 44) but give no numbers. A phase-dependent v_s would change the transit times in Eq. (1) and create partial reflections at the phase boundary, exactly where D_FM is read out and exactly the kind of feature that separates the scenarios. The authors should quote the literature differences in elastic constants between the phases (ref. 44) and estimate the AFM/FM reflection coefficient and its effect on the modelled peak positions. They should include at least one model run with these differences included.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

9. The FM strain reference and the in-plane clamping of finite columns are not justified. The FM strain is referred to q_z = 4.196 Å⁻¹, "the expected lattice constant of the FM phase in case of a vanishing in-plane expansion", so the full 0.6% volume change goes into c. The nucleation stress is modelled as a 1D expansive stress equivalent to that expansion. For columns about 20 nm high and about 25 nm wide (the mosaic block size) embedded in an AFM matrix, the lateral constraint is neither fully clamped nor free, and the out-of-plane expansion will lie between the uniaxial and isotropic limits. Partial in-plane relaxation changes both the static FM out-of-plane lattice parameter and the stress launched into the film. Both shift the FM peak position, which the paper treats as a sensitive measure of D_FM (Fig. 4a discussion), and change the inferred geometry. The authors should discuss the in-plane relaxation expected or observed for nanoscale columns, for example from the static data of ref. 30. They should show how the selected scenario and D_FM change when the FM reference q_z and the transformation stress correspond to partially relaxed columns. The claim that sonography "is not necessarily sensitive to the shape of the domains" should be backed by a calculation for at least one non-columnar or partially relaxed geometry. They should also state which reference q_z,0 is used for the AFM strain (for example the pre-excitation average) and note how sensitive Fig. 2c is to the FM reference assumption.

10. The method's own validity condition is only marginally met. The Discussion says sonography requires the heterogeneity "to change slower than the strain pulse propagation". Here the strain pulse crosses the film in 44 nm / 5 nm ps⁻¹ ≈ 8–9 ps, while the FM phase forms with τ = 8 ps. The pulse is also launched by the same excitation that drives the transition, and the analysis concentrates on t ≈ 9 ps (Fig. 4a), when the FM fraction is still growing. The simple decoding of Eq. (1), d_phase = Δt_phase/v_s, therefore cannot be used directly. The result rests entirely on the forward model and its kinetic assumptions. D_FM(t) and A_FM(t) are set analytically by the model rather than extracted. The authors should state this limitation clearly in the main text and discuss how far it limits the picosecond time resolution claimed in the abstract. They should temper statements such as "directly quantifies the transient out-of-plane dimension", and explain how the time-resolved quantities are actually constrained.
Raised by: r1, r3

11. The scenario set leaves out a domain depth that grows with time. In every scenario except I, D_FM is constant once nucleated, and in scenario I a continuous layer grows into the depth. A natural alternative is not tested: columns whose height grows with time, for example an FM front propagating at finite velocity from a near-surface nucleus. The conclusion that the depth is set by the excitation profile "rather than a propagation of the FM phase into the depth" requires testing such a model explicitly. The 35 ps window is short compared with the 50 ps component the authors mention, which limits sensitivity to slow depth growth, and the paper should state this limitation.

12. The method is not validated on a case with a known answer, and there is no matched control. All results come from one 44 nm film. No reference shows that the forward model recovers a known depth profile under matched conditions. The below-threshold calibration (refs. 18, 29) tests the thermoelastic model but not the phase-localisation step, and it was done in earlier experiments at different incidence angles and possibly different sample conditions (concern 5). The 3.9 mJ cm⁻² data in Fig. S3 are still above threshold, so no sub-threshold sonogram from the same beamtime exists. The authors should, if possible, add a measurement on a structure with a known depth profile of two distinguishable lattices: a bilayer, a static thermal-equilibrium coexistence state, or a second FeRh thickness (for example one exceeding the penetration depth by a wider margin) where D_FM should change in a known way if it tracks the excitation depth. A sub-threshold sonogram from the same run would validate the strain model under identical conditions. At minimum, they should run a synthetic recovery test: generate sonograms from a known D_FM and A_FM, add realistic noise, and show that the fitting procedure recovers the input and separates III from IV.

13. The extracted strains at early delays have no error bars and may be biased by the fit. Fig. 2c shows the FM strain only once the FM peak's integral exceeds 10% of the total intensity. The scenario comparison in Fig. 3, however, relies heavily on the 4–10 ps window, where the FM peak is weak and overlaps the AFM peak, and where nucleation-depth information should be strongest. A two-peak pseudo-Voigt fit may be biased there. The authors should give fit uncertainties, or the shot-noise level, for q_z of both peaks in Fig. 2c and Fig. 3, and state which fit model (single or double pseudo-Voigt) produced them. They should state whether the early-time FM data below the 10% threshold enter the χ² of Fig. 4c. They should apply the same fitting procedure to the simulated sonograms to check that it reproduces the model's true average strain, which would expose any fitting bias.

14. Heat accumulation within the pulse train and the effective initial temperature are not documented. Six pump pulses at 56.25 kHz (17.8 µs spacing) in 10 Hz trains are averaged, starting from a base temperature of 300 K, with the transition at ≈ 370 K. Residual heating from earlier pulses in the burst would raise the starting temperature, which directly changes V*_FM and the threshold depth. The paper cites unpumped reference images and Fig. S7 showing "negligible fluctuations", but no quantitative estimate. The authors should estimate the quasi-static temperature rise within a train. They should show the sonogram, or at least the static peak position and V*_FM, separately for each pulse index in the burst. They should also explain the slightly negative AFM strain before and near t = 0 in Fig. 2c, which could reflect a problem with the reference position or residual heating.

15. The in-plane domain size claim is not supported by the analysis shown. The Discussion states that the in-plane size of the nucleating FM domains matches the ~25 nm mosaic blocks (supplementary S4) and concludes that static structural heterogeneity plays a "crucial role". The Conclusion, however, says lateral dimensions can be accessed "only indirectly, if at all", and the method as presented determines A_FM, not a lateral length.
