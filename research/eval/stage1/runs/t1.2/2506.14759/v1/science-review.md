Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript proposes a MOS-like heterostructure: a tensile-strained Ge layer grown on relaxed Ge1−xSn_x (x ≈ 16%) and capped by a thin, large-offset surface barrier, whose ground state is light-hole (LH) like. Using 6-band k·p theory and Schrieffer–Wolff effective Hamiltonians for planar, channel and dot geometries, the authors predict a linear-in-k Rashba term β1 that changes sign at a finite gate field (F_z* ≈ 4.4 V/µm for l_z = 15 nm), an out-of-plane g-factor that can be tuned through zero, and a large in-plane g-factor (g∥ ≈ −8). They propose this as an electrically switchable SOI "on/off" knob for EDSR and as a platform for hybrid devices. A gate-tunable linear Rashba term in planar Ge would interest the hole-spin-qubit and hybrid-device communities. The large linear Rashba term of LH Ge/GeSn wells was already established in the authors' earlier work (Refs. 56, 57), so the main new content is the asymmetric single-heterointerface geometry, the gate-tunable zero and its effect on the Rabi frequency. The work is a single-particle calculation in an idealised structure, and its device-level claims depend on physics the model leaves out.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The mechanism is clear: the asymmetry between the surface wall and the Ge/GeSn triangular barrier makes β1 change sign at finite F_z, and the resulting "β-line" is essentially independent of l_x, l_y and θ (Figs. 2d, 3), which is a useful design principle.
- The analysis runs consistently from quantum well to channel to dot (Figs. 1c, 2, 3), with effective parameters from the same k·p model.
- Eq. (3) gives a transparent analytic link between the channel g⊥ and the planar g⊥ and β1, connecting to g-factor renormalisation in 1D hole systems.
- Distinct β-lines and g-lines with different responses to field tilt (Fig. 3b) give an experimentally testable signature.
- The proposal is timely given recent Ge/GeSn growth (Refs. 16, 55) and can be tested experimentally.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The claim that the Rashba SOI "completely vanishes" is supported only within an idealised model that omits terms known to give residual linear SOI.** The abstract and conclusion state that the RSOI "completely vanish[es] at specific gate fields, effectively acting as an on/off SOI switch" and that in the off state "the qubit is put in idle mode". Figs. 2b, 2d and 3 show β1 crossing zero in a structure with a hard-wall surface barrier, abrupt interfaces, homogeneous strain, uniform F_z and no alloy disorder. A k·p envelope model with a hard wall cannot capture interface-inversion-asymmetry terms: HH–LH mixing at the Ge/cap and Ge/GeSn interfaces reduces the symmetry to C2v and adds a field-independent linear-in-k contribution that would generally prevent β1 from vanishing exactly. The paper's own introduction cites such terms (Refs. 50–53) as able to dominate Rashba physics in planar hole heterostructures. Inhomogeneous strain from the gate electrodes and in-plane field components from the confinement gates would add further residual SOI at F_z*. The authors should estimate the size of interface-induced linear terms (for example with the boundary-condition or C2v-invariant approaches of Refs. 50–51) relative to β1 away from the switch point, and state the residual Rabi frequency, or the realistic off/on ratio of Ω, at F_z*. Otherwise the claim should be rephrased as an SOI minimum ("sweet line") rather than a complete switch-off.

**B. Should address**

2. **The vanishing of the cubic Rashba terms at the same field is asserted, not shown.** In the planar case the text says the β^p = 0 contour "coincides in (l_z, F_z)-space for all three Rashba parameters", "suggesting" full tunability (p. 3), but no data or explanation are given. Any residual cubic term sets a floor on the off-state Rabi frequency. In the channel (Eq. 2), β3^c k_y^2 adds to β1^c, and for a dot with l_y = 60 nm, ⟨k_y^2⟩ ~ 1/l_y^2 is not negligible. The authors should plot β2^p, β3^p and β3^c next to β1 against F_z at l_z = 15 nm (for example in Fig. 2, or at least in the SM), explain why they vanish together, and confirm that Ω(F_z*) = 0 in Fig. 3 includes the cubic contribution.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

3. **Driving through g-tensor modulation is not included, so the "idle mode" at the β-line does not follow.** Fig. 3 computes Ω = (eF_ac/ħ)|⟨0|y|1⟩| for a uniform in-plane drive along y in a parabolic dot, so the only mechanism is linear SOI. In a real gate-defined dot the plunger or drive gate also carries an out-of-plane component δF_z, and Figs. 2a, 2c and 3 show that g⊥ varies by several units with F_z and crosses zero twice between 0 and 10 V/µm. g-TMR, proportional to ∂g/∂F_z, would therefore give nonzero Ω on the β-line for any B direction not along a principal axis, and could be large for tilted fields. The authors should include a realistic δF_z drive component (or a realistic gate-geometry drive) in the Fig. 3 calculation for the field orientations of Fig. 3b, and compare the g-TMR contribution along the β-line with the SOI-mediated Ω at nearby F_z.
Raised by: r2, r3

4. **The surface barrier is idealised and unspecified, yet it creates the asymmetry that sets F_z*.** The barrier is described only as "very thin, large band offset", possibly "a few ångströms" of Si, an oxide or a combination (p. 2), and is modelled as an effectively hard wall at z = 0; "MOS-like" is never pinned to a concrete material. Because β1 vanishes "due to the asymmetric confinement profile", F_z* depends on the barrier height and thickness, wavefunction penetration, effective masses inside the barrier, interface strain from a Si cap, and fixed interface charge. The authors should state the material, thickness and offset assumed; replace the hard wall with a finite barrier (Si cap, SiO2, GeO2 with realistic offsets and masses) and report how F_z* and g⊥ shift; and discuss the interface trap and fixed-charge densities typical of Ge MOS interfaces, which both screen F_z and add disorder.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

5. **The 6-band k·p model omits the conduction band, which may matter most in this strongly tensile regime.** Ge pseudomorphic on Ge0.84Sn0.16 carries roughly 2% biaxial tensile strain, which pushes the Γ conduction band down towards a direct, narrow gap. LH states couple to Γ6 through the Kane parameter far more strongly than HH states, and that coupling enters the LH out-of-plane mass, the LH g-factors (g∥ ≈ −8 in particular) and the Rashba coefficients. The small gap at x = 16% may also make the 6-band model inadequate in the barrier. The headline numbers (F_z* ≈ 4.4 V/µm, g⊥^p ≈ −3, g∥^c ≈ −8, g-lines at ≈2 and 7.5 V/µm) could shift substantially. The authors should repeat at least Figs. 1c, 2a–b and 2c–e in an 8-band model with the strain-dependent Γ gap, or give the strained band gap and LH–CB separation and a quantitative bound on the corrections, including how much F_z* moves.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

6. **Structural feasibility of the strained Ge layer is not established.** The model assumes a fully strained, defect-free 15 nm Ge layer (up to 25 nm in Fig. 2a–b) coherent on relaxed Ge0.84Sn0.16, a mismatch of roughly 2–2.5% in tension. The critical thickness is likely comparable to or smaller than these thicknesses, and partial relaxation directly reduces ε∥, which produces the LH ground state. The region boundaries of Fig. 1c, the LH–HH splitting and the β1 = 0 line all depend on the assumed strain; high-Sn relaxed buffers also suffer from Sn segregation and precipitation. The authors should state the strain value and the assumed critical thickness with experimental references (Ref. 55 is natural), and show how the ΔE = 0 contour and F_z* move when ε∥ is reduced by 10–30% (or by ±0.2%) through partial relaxation, and with residual strain in the GeSn buffer.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

7. **Material parameters are not stated in the main text and their uncertainty is not assessed; the Supplemental Material was not provided.** All inputs (Luttinger parameters beyond γ2 = 4.24 and γ3 = 5.69, κ, q, deformation potentials, elastic constants, the GeSn lattice constant and bowing, the strained Ge/GeSn HH and LH valence-band offsets, the barrier height and boundary conditions) are deferred to Ref. 60, which is not part of the submission. The VBO, poorly constrained near 16% Sn, sets the depth of the triangular barrier (Fig. 1b) and therefore the β-line and the region boundaries of Fig. 1c. It is also unclear whether bulk Ge values are used for γ1, κ and q (which set the g-factors) and whether Sn interdiffusion into the well is neglected. The authors should give a parameter table in the main text or an appendix, provide the SM, and show how F_z*, the β1 magnitude and the zero of g⊥ vary within the literature spread of the VBO and the strain-related parameters.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **The required fields and the device electrostatics are not shown to be realistic.** The relevant features lie at F_z ≈ 2–15 V/µm in Ge (β-line at 4.4 V/µm, g-lines at ≈2 and 7.5 V/µm, Rabi maximum near 7–10 V/µm). With ε_Ge ≈ 16, continuity of displacement implies several MV/cm in an SiO2-like dielectric (≈6 MV/cm at the top of the range), near breakdown and in the regime of strong leakage and charge trapping; the displacement corresponds to sheet densities of order 10^12–10^13 cm^-2. Ge0.84Sn0.16 has a gap of a few hundred meV and Fig. 1b shows 0.1–0.2 eV band bending, so electron accumulation or inversion in the GeSn, or parallel hole conduction, could screen F_z. With top gates alone, F_z is not an independent knob at fixed dot occupancy. The authors should translate F_z into gate voltages and oxide fields for a concrete stack, propose a gating geometry (for example dual gates) that sweeps F_z across F_z* at fixed occupancy, show that the assumed uniform field holds without parasitic accumulation, and justify the single-particle, unscreened treatment at the operating densities. Without this the "fully gate-tunable" claim in the title is not shown to be reachable.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

9. **"Large" SOI and "efficient" EDSR are not quantified in absolute units or benchmarked.** β1 is given in meV nm (a few meV nm in Figs. 2b, 2d) and Ω only in units of ω_B eF_ac/F_z with a ×10^4 multiplier (Fig. 3). Normalising by F_z, the varied quantity, hides the true F_z dependence at fixed F_ac and diverges as F_z → 0. The claims of "inherently large" RSOI and of "alleviating limitations" of planar HH systems (pp. 1, 4) rest on a comparison that is not made. The authors should give Ω in MHz for a stated drive (for example F_ac of 1–10 kV/m, or 1–10 mV over the dot) at B0 = 0.05 T, plot Ω/(ω_B eF_ac) or Ω in absolute units, quote the spin–orbit length l_so with the in-plane LH mass against the dot size, and compare in the same framework with a planar Ge/SiGe HH dot of the same size and with Ge/Si nanowire direct Rashba SOI.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

10. **Charge-noise dephasing, the natural cost of strong gate tunability, is not evaluated.** The large F_z dependence of g⊥ (Figs. 2a, 2c, 3) and of β1 implies a large ∂ω/∂F_z and hence sensitivity to electric-field noise. Without this, the claimed qubit advantages and the comparison with the "spinless" qubit proposal (Ref. 63) are not supported. The authors should give ∂f_Larmor/∂F_z (or a charge-noise-limited T2* for a typical noise amplitude) across the parameter space of Fig. 3, identify sweet spots, check whether any coincides with the β-line or the "on" maximum, and compare the quality factor Ω/(dephasing rate) with planar HH Ge qubits.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

11. **The perturbative effective models may fail close to the features of interest, and no non-perturbative check is shown.** Eq. (1) is said to fail for l_z ≲ 10 nm, yet Fig. 2a–b extends to l_z = 10 nm without marking validity. Near the ΔE = 0 contour and the region-2/region-3 boundary of Fig. 1c, where E_2^H − E_1^H ≈ 2 meV, energy denominators shrink. The QC Hamiltonian (Eq. 2) treats N ≥ 1 orbital levels perturbatively although the orbital spacing at l_x = 100 nm is small compared with the SOI energy, and the Fig. 3 Rabi frequency is computed perturbatively from the channel model with harmonic in-plane confinement. The authors should mark on Figs. 2–3 the regions where the lowest excited level lies within a few meV of the ground state, and validate β1, g⊥ and Ω at a few representative points, especially on the β- and g-lines, by direct diagonalisation of the full 6-band dot Hamiltonian. Ref. 60 says this is in the SM, which was not available.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

12. **Eq. (3) is applied outside its stated validity.** Eq. (3) assumes |β1^p| l_x/α0 ≪ 1, but the g⊥^c sign change is predicted for (β1^p l_x/α0)^2 ≥ |g⊥^p| sqrt(γ∥^p), which with |g⊥^p| ≈ 3 requires β1^p l_x/α0 of order unity or larger. With α0 ≈ 38 meV nm² and β1 of a few meV nm, the ratio exceeds 1 for l_x of a few tens of nm and is ≫ 1 at l_x = 100 nm (top of Fig. 2c). The sign change is visible in the full numerics, but the text attributes it to Eq. (3). The authors should state that the g-line positions come from the full calculation and give the actual validity window of Eq. (3).
Raised by: r1, r2

13. **Disorder and inhomogeneity will spread F_z* from dot to dot.** Relaxed GeSn buffers carry Sn compositional fluctuations, Sn segregation, threading and misfit dislocations and cross-hatch strain variations; well-width fluctuations and interface roughness add further variation. These modulate ε∥ and the confinement locally and so move F_z*. Since the off state requires tuning to a specific field, this spread decides whether a shared gate field can idle many qubits. The authors should estimate the shift of F_z* per 0.1% of strain, per 1% of Sn content and per monolayer of l_z, and comment on realistic inhomogeneity.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

14. **The claim of a less anisotropic g-tensor conflicts with the paper's own results.** The abstract says the LH g-tensor "is less anisotropic than that of state-of-the-art HH qubits, alleviating precise magnetic field orientation requirements". The paper shows g⊥ ≈ −3 to 0 against g∥ ≈ −8 (Figs. 2a, 2e), so the anisotropy is inverted rather than absent, and at the g-lines that the conclusion promotes the g-tensor is maximally anisotropic, with Ω and the qubit frequency very sensitive to θ (Fig. 3b). Only B in the y–z plane is considered; in-plane anisotropy g_x vs g_y and off-diagonal components from in-plane confinement are not shown. The authors should give the full g-tensor (principal values and axes) for the dot of Fig. 3, quantify g∥/g⊥ and the angular sensitivity of the qubit frequency at the stated operating points against typical Ge/SiGe HH values, and restrict or reconcile the claim with the g-line proposal.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

15. **The claims about hybrid superconducting devices are unsupported.** The abstract and conclusion state that the large in-plane g-factor (g∥^c ≈ −8, Fig. 2e) "facilitates the integration of superconductors", but no calculation addresses this. Missing are the induced gap, interface transparency and proximity effect for LH states; the requirement for topological or Andreev-spin applications (large g together with SOI along a suitable direction relative to the field); and compatibility with the surface barrier the LH ground state needs, since a superconductor on top would change the boundary condition, the electrostatics (screening of F_z) and possibly the strain. The authors should support the claim with a short estimate (for example the in-plane field needed to close the induced gap or reach a topological regime, against the critical field of Al, and how a metal top contact changes F_z*, the LH/HH ordering) or reduce it to an outlook statement.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

16. **Results are given for essentially one material point.** Nearly all results use x = 16% and l_z = 15 nm, and it is unclear whether x = 10% (used in Fig. 1b) gives an LH ground state at all for the plotted parameters. Growers need to know the window. The authors should state the range of Sn content for which the β-line exists and show F_z* as a function of x and l_z, ideally with a sensitivity table of the Sn content, VBO, strain and l_z windows in which an LH ground state with β1 sign reversal survives.
Raised by: r1, r3

17. **Convergence with respect to the simulation domain and the field profile in the buffer is not shown.** Fig. 1b shows HH states (H3) localised in a field-induced triangular well in the GeSn layer, and region 3 of Fig. 1c is defined by such states. The z-domain appears to extend only to about −30 nm, whereas a real relaxed buffer is thick and its field is set by depletion and screening rather than a uniform F_z. The authors should show that the ground-state ordering, ΔE and the Rashba parameters are converged in domain size, and discuss how a realistic non-uniform field in the buffer affects the sharp region-2/region-3 boundary (dashed line in Fig. 1c).

**C. Minor**

18. **Hyperfine and phonon-relaxation implications of an LH ground state are not discussed.** The introduction cites the "inherently weak coupling of holes with surrounding nuclear spins" as an advantage, but for LH states the hyperfine interaction is not Ising-like and differs from the HH case, and strong linear SOI enhances phonon-mediated relaxation. A brief estimate of the hyperfine coupling relative to HH and of the SOI-mediated phonon T1 at the "on" point would put the claimed advantages in context.

19. **The advance over the authors' earlier work should be stated explicitly.** Refs. 56 and 57 already established the large linear Rashba term of LH Ge/GeSn wells. The introduction should say clearly that the new content is the asymmetric single-heterointerface geometry, the gate-tunable zero and the channel and dot analysis.

Make clear that this is a theoretical proposal based on k·p modelling and that the LH ground state is a property of the tensile-strained Ge well.
