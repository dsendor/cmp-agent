Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors co-fabricate five symmetric T-shaped Al/InAs planar Josephson junctions on one chip. The junctions differ only in the longitudinal width Wsc of the superconducting contacts (0.3, 0.6, 0.9 and 1.2 µm, plus an unetched "Wsc → ∞" device). For each one they measure the critical current and the in-plane-field superconducting diode effect, taking the critical currents as the global extrema of a narrow out-of-plane field sweep. They report four findings:
- Ic saturates exponentially with Wsc, on a length (586 nm) close to a dirty-limit coherence length.
- The peak diode efficiency generally grows with Wsc.
- The field Bη of maximum efficiency scales linearly with 1/Wsc and saturates for Wsc ≳ 2ξ.
- The field B* of the critical-current extremum depends strongly on gate voltage.

Few experiments vary this geometric parameter systematically, and doing so is a useful way to separate the mechanisms proposed for the diode effect in planar junctions. The B⊥-extremisation protocol is also a sensible way to suppress out-of-plane artefacts. The mechanistic conclusions, however, rest on one device per width and on scalings that more than one mechanism predicts. The quantitative link to a coherence length depends on parameter choices that the paper itself states inconsistently.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- All devices come from the same heterostructure and chip, with nominally identical junction length (80 nm) and width (4 µm). That makes Wsc a reasonably clean control parameter.
- The B⊥-extremisation procedure (Eq. 1) is well motivated. Fig. 1d–f shows that it removes nonreciprocity from Fraunhofer-pattern shifts. Fig. 1h shows the θ = 0 control (field parallel to the current), where η stays near zero.
- The data are antisymmetric in B∥, η ≈ 0 at zero field, and Ic changes monotonically with Wsc (Fig. 3a). This internal consistency supports the data quality.
- The Wsc-dependence of Bη (Fig. 5c) is a large effect: a factor of about 3 between 0.3 µm and 1.2 µm. It is seen at three gate voltages.
- Raw data and analysis scripts are deposited on Zenodo.
- The discussion of mechanisms is careful about what the data do not show, such as the absence of Φ0-periodic oscillations of η and the mismatch between the single-mode estimates and the measured Bη.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. One device per contact width; the trends in the abstract are not shown to be reproducible.
   - **What the paper shows.** Each Wsc is represented by a single junction (Fig. 3a, 5a). No device-to-device spread is given. The paper says the chip was cooled down several times (Methods), but does not say whether the key quantities (Ic, max η, Bη, B*) were reproduced across cooldowns.
   - **Why it matters.** This is most serious for max η (Fig. 5b), which is non-monotonic: about 8.5% at Wsc = 1.2 µm against about 10% at 0.9 µm. The text concedes the trend "does not follow a clear exponential trend", and Fig. 5b has no error bars. Yet the abstract states that "the diode efficiency increase[s] with contact width". With one junction per width, variation between devices cannot be separated from the geometric effect: transparency, small differences in Wn, Al etch undercut, or disorder at the S/N interface could each produce it.
   - **What to do.** Measure at least one more junction for some widths, ideally the extremes, or show that the same device gives the same values after a thermal cycle. Put error bars on Fig. 5b. Qualify the abstract claim about η if the trend cannot be shown to exceed device-to-device scatter.

2. Kinetic inductance and how current is fed into the narrow Al strips are an untested alternative explanation for both Ic(Wsc) and Bη(Wsc).
   - **Size of the effect.** The Al contacts are 10 nm thick, 4 µm long strips of width Wsc, fed from a T-shaped stem (Fig. 1a). For such a strip the sheet kinetic inductance is of order 10 pH/□. For Wsc = 0.3 µm (about 13 squares along the junction) this gives a phase drop along the junction of order a radian at I ≈ 2 µA. For 1.2 µm it is about four times smaller.
   - **Consequences.** A phase gradient along the junction suppresses the measured Ic and shifts the Fraunhofer pattern. The authors themselves note B⊥+ ≠ B⊥− "due to the magnetic field induced by the slightly asymmetric critical current density". The gradient scales as 1/Wsc, so it could mimic both the "coherence-length" suppression of Ic (Eq. 2) and contribute to the 1/Wsc scaling of Bη. The text mentions kinetic inductance only in passing, in the discussion of the single-mode estimates.
   - **What to do.**
     - Report the normal-state sheet resistance of the Al and estimate the kinetic phase drop for each device.
     - State whether the two contacts are fed from the same end or opposite ends of the junction.
     - Use the Fraunhofer shapes already measured (lobe periods, lifting of minima, the B⊥+/B⊥− offset as a function of Wsc) to bound how non-uniform the current is.
     - Ideally, measure Ic(Wsc) at several temperatures. A coherence-length mechanism predicts ξ(T) to vary in a specific way that a kinetic-inductance mechanism does not.

3. The agreement between the fitted ξ = 586 nm and the dirty-limit coherence length depends on parameter values that the paper gives inconsistently.
   - **The inconsistency.** The main text uses n = 6 × 10^12 cm⁻² and ℓ = 130 nm "from magnetotransport data", which give vF ≈ 3 × 10^6 m/s, ξ_BCS ≈ 2.5 µm and √(ξ_BCS ℓ) ≈ 570 nm. The Methods instead report n = 8.14 × 10^11 cm⁻² and ℓe = 147 nm from van der Pauw measurements on the same wafer. Those give vF ≈ 1.1 × 10^6 m/s, ξ_BCS ≈ 0.9 µm and √(ξ_BCS ℓ) ≈ 370 nm, which no longer "agrees well" with 586 nm.
   - **Which medium.** It is not stated which coherence length is relevant: that of the proximitised 2DEG under the Al, or that of the 10 nm Al film itself, which would be much shorter. Nor is it stated why the bulk Al gap (Δ = 1.764 kBTc) rather than an induced gap should enter ξ_BCS.
   - **Why it matters.** The quantitative match is the main evidence that the Wsc-scaling of Ic reflects the Andreev-bound-state coherence length.
   - **What to do.** Reconcile the two sets of transport parameters (for example, the density under the Al versus a bare-2DEG Hall bar), say which one applies, and show the dirty-limit estimate with a realistic range. Also justify the choice of gap.

B. Should address

4. The 1/Wsc scaling of Bη does not single out the orbital mechanism, and none of the quantitative estimates match the data.
   - **The same scaling from other mechanisms.** The authors note that any finite-momentum mechanism has zeroes of η set by a resonance condition, 2q(2Wsc + Wn) = (2n + 1)π/2. Each of q_M, q_orb and q_RZ is linear in B∥, so all three predict Bη ∝ 1/(2Wsc + Wn). The Conclusion's statement that the Wsc-dependence "invites an orbital interpretation" is therefore not supported over the alternatives.
   - **The estimates.** The single-mode estimates (about 13 mT Meissner, about 4 mT orbital, about 690 mT Rashba–Zeeman) bracket the observed 90–270 mT without matching it. The orbital picture also predicts Φ0-periodic structure, which is not observed.
   - **What to do.** Fit the Wsc → finite data to the resonance form (offset Wn) as well as to Eq. 4, and compare the two. Better, compare quantitatively with a multimode BdG or tight-binding calculation that includes finite Wsc, Rashba coupling, Zeeman and orbital terms. Then state which features, if any, discriminate the mechanisms. Otherwise, soften the mechanistic language in the abstract and Conclusion.

5. The linear fit in Fig. 5c is not Eq. 4, and the "saturation" rests on one point.
   - **The intercept.** Eq. 4 (Bη Wsc d = Φ) is a line through the origin in Bη versus 1/Wsc. The plotted line has slope ≈ 74 mT·µm, matching the legend "Φ/Φ0 = 0.036 nm⁻¹ × d", but it also has an apparent intercept of about 20–30 mT at 1/Wsc = 0. Please state whether the intercept was free, and what a finite intercept means physically. It may point to the resonance form in concern 4.
   - **Φ and d are not independent.** Only the product Φ/d is fitted, so the quoted Φ/Φ0 ≈ 0.36–0.61 simply restates the assumed d = 10–17 nm. It is not an independent test of Φ < Φ0.
   - **Saturation.** The claim that Bη "saturates for Wsc ≳ 2ξ ≈ 1.15 µm" rests on the Wsc → ∞ device lying near the 1.2 µm device. That device has a different geometry and processing (see concern 9).
   - **What to do.** Show the fit parameters with uncertainties, a fit through the origin, and residuals.

6. The analysis of Ic(Wsc) in Fig. 3b needs more transparency.
   - **How Ic∞ was obtained.** The ordinate 1/ln[Ic∞/(Ic∞ − max Ic)] equals ξ/Wsc only if Ic∞ is known. Please state whether Ic∞ is the measured Ic of the Wsc → ∞ device or a free fit parameter.
   - **The point at the origin.** If Ic∞ is the measured value, the point at the origin is fixed by construction and should not be shown as data.
   - **Sensitivity.** For the wider devices Ic∞ − Ic is small, so the linearisation strongly amplifies errors in Ic∞. Rough reading of Fig. 3a gives single-device ξ values between about 0.45 and 0.6 µm.
   - **What to do.**
     - Show error bars in Fig. 3b, including the uncertainty in Ic∞.
     - Fit Eq. 2 directly to Ic(Wsc) with Ic∞ free.
     - Compare with at least one alternative functional form, for example the kinetic-phase suppression of concern 2.
   - **Uncertainty in Wsc.** Wsc was "confirmed by scanning electron micrographs of nominally identical proxy devices", not the measured devices. Wet-etch undercut of tens of nm is a large fraction of 0.3 µm and of 1/Wsc. Quote the Wsc uncertainty and carry it into Fig. 3b, 3c and 5c.

7. Max η is compared at very different fields, which confounds its Wsc-dependence.
   - **The confound.** η peaks at about 90 mT for wide contacts and about 270 mT for Wsc = 0.3 µm. At higher B∥, Zeeman and orbital pair breaking suppress the induced gap and Ic (Fig. 3a). The smaller max η of narrow devices may therefore reflect where the peak sits on the field axis, rather than an intrinsic geometric reduction of nonreciprocity.
   - **What to do.** Compare η at a fixed B∥, or as a function of the reduced variable B∥Wsc, or after normalising by Ic(B∥)/Ic(0). Discuss whether the Wsc-dependence of max η survives.

8. The gate dependence of B* is interpreted through Eq. 3 without independent inputs, and some features fall outside it.
   - **Several parameters move together.** A gate changes α, and also kF, ET and τ, and it strongly changes Ic (Fig. 1g). Assigning the trend in Fig. 4 to rising α and falling τ needs independent measures:
     - α(Vg), from weak antilocalisation on the same wafer with the same gate stack;
     - transparency or ET per device and gate voltage (Rn, IcRn, excess current or MAR).
   - **Negative B* values.** B* is negative (about −5 mT) for several devices at Vg < 0 (Fig. 3c, Fig. 4). Eq. 3 gives a positive magnitude, so this needs explaining. It may be a sign reversal like that of ref. 8.
   - **Mismatched gate voltages.** The "Vg < 0" series mixes different voltages for different devices (about −4 V to −3 V in Fig. 4), so Fig. 3c compares devices at unequal gate settings.
   - **Gated area.** Please state which regions the gate covers. In particular, does it act on the bare 2DEG beyond the distal edge of the Al strips? If so, it also tunes the Andreev reflection that sets the Wsc-dependence.

9. The Wsc → ∞ reference device differs in more than Wsc, yet anchors three of the conclusions.
   - **How it differs.** It has unetched leads, so the current spreads differently and the screening and vortex environment of the large Al leads is different. Its data also go through a separate smoothing procedure.
   - **What depends on it.** It sets Ic∞ in Eq. 2 and Fig. 3b, gives the largest max η (about 16%) in Fig. 5b, and is the evidence for the Bη saturation in Fig. 5c.
   - **What to do.** Describe the smoothing in the main text and show that the extracted quantities are insensitive to it. Discuss whether the geometry differences could account for its outlying η.

10. The error bars and the field zero need clarifying.
    - **What the error bars include.** They are the RMS residual of a local fit to the Fraunhofer extremum (Methods). This does not capture switching-current stochasticity, sweep-to-sweep reproducibility or drift. Please state how many sweeps underlie each point, and give a switching-current histogram width for comparison.
    - **Field zero.** B* is only a few mT for several devices, comparable to typical remanent fields in a vector magnet. Clarify whether B* is computed as half the difference of argmax Ic+ and argmin Ic−, which cancels a field offset, or relative to a nominal zero.
    - **Field misalignment.** At B∥ = 1 T, the stated 2° misalignment gives about 35 mT out of plane, many Fraunhofer periods. Briefly explain in the main text how the central lobe is tracked over that range.

C. Minor

11. Fig. 1: identify which device (which Wsc) is shown in panels c–h. In panel g, give the field and the device.
12. Fig. 3b and Fig. 5b lack error bars, whereas Fig. 3c and 5c have them. In Fig. 3c, the Vg = +10 V series has no Wsc = 0.3 µm point; say why.
13. ξ_ABS = ξ/√τ |sin(φ/2)| equals ξ only for τ → 1 and φ → π. The phase at the critical current is not π in general. Briefly state what this approximation does to the comparison with ξ.
14. Meissner estimate: justify taking λL → d_sc/2 for a 10 nm film, where λL is much larger than the thickness, and state the screening model used.
15. Typography: "Φ/Φ0 ≈ 0.36−−0.61" and "d = 10−−17 nm" should read 0.36–0.61 and 10–17 nm. In the Fig. 5c legend, write the slope with explicit units.
16. References:
    - Several have missing spaces, for example "ofAndreevbound", "planarJosephsonJunctions" and "inaHybridFour-Terminal".
    - Some published works are cited only as arXiv preprints, e.g. refs 7, 9 and 13. Ref. 12 is given as "Nature Nanotechnology arXiv:2212.13460"; give the full journal reference.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Measure Ic(Wsc) and Bη(Wsc) at two or three temperatures. ξ(T) and the coherence-length picture predict a specific temperature dependence of the fitted length, which would separate it from kinetic-inductance and geometric explanations.
2. Report Rn, IcRn and the Al sheet resistance for every device. That would let you separate changes in transparency and Thouless energy from geometric effects, and estimate the kinetic phase drop along each contact.
3. Add a multimode BdG or tight-binding simulation of the actual geometry (finite Wsc, Wn = 80 nm, 4 µm width) that includes Rashba, Zeeman and orbital terms. Compare it quantitatively with Fig. 3b and Fig. 5c, so that each mechanism is tested on its predicted Bη rather than only on the 1/Wsc scaling.
4. A useful control would be an asymmetric device with one narrow and one wide contact, or a series of junctions with different Wn at fixed Wsc. These would separate the resonance condition 2q(2Wsc + Wn) from the flux area Wsc·d.
