Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper looks at how the longitudinal width W_sc of the superconducting contacts affects the critical current and the superconducting diode effect in symmetric T-shaped Al/InAs planar Josephson junctions in an in-plane field. Five junctions (W_sc = 0.3, 0.6, 0.9 and 1.2 µm, plus an unetched "W_sc → ∞" device) were co-fabricated on one chip. The paper reports that I_c grows as 1 − exp(−W_sc/ξ) with ξ ≈ 586 nm. It also reports that the peak diode efficiency grows with W_sc, and that the field B_η at which η peaks scales linearly with 1/W_sc and saturates near 2ξ. The field B*, where I_c peaks, is small and depends strongly on gate voltage. Few studies vary this geometric parameter systematically, so the data set is useful for telling diode-effect mechanisms apart. However, the interpretation rests on one device per width and on parameter estimates that are not self-consistent, and no single mechanism accounts for the field scales measured.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A controlled geometric variable. All devices are on one chip and from one heterostructure, with the same 80 nm junction length and 4 µm width, and only W_sc changes.
- Careful handling of out-of-plane field artifacts. At every (B_∥, V_g) point the authors sweep B_⊥ narrowly and take I_c+ and I_c− at their own Fraunhofer extrema (Eq. 1, Fig. 1d–f). This removes nonreciprocity caused by a B_⊥ offset or an asymmetric J_c(x). They sweep switching currents outward from zero to avoid retrapping hysteresis.
- Useful null checks. η(B_∥ = 0) ≈ 0 within error, so no spurious zero-field diode effect is seen. With the field parallel to the current (θ = 0, Fig. 1h), η stays near zero, as the symmetry argument expects.
- Two field scales (B* and B_η) behave differently with W_sc and V_g. This separation helps distinguish mechanisms.
- Raw data and analysis scripts are deposited on Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. There is one device per contact width, and devices are not normalized against each other. This affects Figs. 3, 4 and 5 and every main claim. Each W_sc value is represented by a single junction. The I_c(W_sc) fit in Fig. 3b has four non-trivial points plus a reference point that defines I_c^∞. The conclusions that I_c follows Eq. (2) and that max η increases with W_sc (Fig. 5b) are therefore exposed to ordinary device-to-device variation. Such variation can come from the actual junction length W_n (an 80 nm gap set by wet etching, to which I_c is very sensitive), interface transparency, local density, or Al etch damage. The max η data are already non-monotonic: 1.2 µm gives about 8.5% and 0.9 µm about 10%. The reference device is also not like the others. Its leads are unetched, so its transverse geometry and screening differ, and it alone needed data smoothing (supplementary section "data smoothing for W_sc → ∞"). Yet it fixes I_c^∞ in Eq. (2) and it is the only evidence that B_η "saturates" in Fig. 5c. The 1.2 µm point lies on the linear fit, so only the ∞ point departs from it. The authors should:
   - report the normal-state resistance R_N, I_cR_N, and the excess current or another transparency measure for every device, so the W_sc trend can be separated from variation in transparency or W_n;
   - state the measured W_n of each device;
   - if at all possible, add a second device at one or more widths, or a second chip, to show the spread;
   - state whether the key data were taken in one cooldown or are reproducible across the "multiple" cooldowns mentioned in the Methods;
   - show that the saturation claim survives without the smoothed ∞ device, or soften it.

2. The agreement between the fitted ξ = 586 nm and the dirty-limit estimate of about 570 nm rests on inconsistent parameters (page 2–3, Methods). The text uses n = 6×10^12 cm^−2, ℓ = 130 nm and v_F = 3×10^6 m/s. The Methods give n = 8.14×10^11 cm^−2 and ℓ_e = 147 nm for the same wafer. With the Methods values, v_F ≈ 1.1×10^6 m/s and ξ_BCS = ħv_F/πΔ ≈ 0.9 µm, so √(ξ_BCS ℓ) ≈ 0.37 µm. That is well below the fitted 586 nm. A density of 6×10^12 cm^−2 and v_F = 3×10^6 m/s are also unusually high for an InAs 2DEG. In addition, the estimate uses the bulk Al gap of 250 µeV rather than the induced gap Δ* in the proximitized 2DEG, which is smaller. That substitution changes ξ substantially. Since "the critical current reflects the induced coherence length of the Andreev bound states" is a headline conclusion, the authors should:
   - state which density, mobility and gap apply under the Al, and where each comes from (for example, gated magnetotransport or tunnelling spectroscopy of Δ*);
   - correct or explain the order-of-magnitude difference in n;
   - give the resulting ξ with its uncertainty range.

   They should also address explanations for the I_c(W_sc) trend other than ABS leakage past the distal Al edge:
   - a W_sc-dependent induced gap from inverse proximity or finite-size effects in narrow Al strips;
   - edge damage from the Al etch;
   - the uncertainty in W_sc itself (concern 9).

   Eq. (2) is one model with one parameter, so a quantitative comparison with a microscopic calculation would strengthen the link to ξ. One option is a BdG or tight-binding model of the T-geometry with a bare 2DEG beyond the contacts.

B. Should address

3. The orbital interpretation of B_η(1/W_sc) is not internally consistent (Fig. 5c, Eqs. 4 and 6, pages 4–5).
   - The fitted line in Fig. 5c appears to have a clearly non-zero intercept, about 25 mT at 1/W_sc = 0. But Eq. (4), B_η = Φ/(W_sc d), passes through the origin. The authors should state whether an offset was a free parameter, give the fit parameters and their uncertainties, and explain the intercept physically.
   - The flux area is taken as W_sc·d. The geometry of Fig. 2 could equally suggest (2W_sc + W_n)·d or a ξ-limited width. The choice should be justified, since it sets the inferred Φ/Φ_0 ≈ 0.36–0.61.
   - The sign changes of η at 400–600 mT correspond to several Φ_0, not Φ_0, for every device. For example, W_sc = 0.9 µm with d = 17 nm gives about 3.7 Φ_0 at 0.5 T. No Φ_0-periodic oscillation is seen either.
   - The orbital finite-q estimate in Eq. (6) gives B_η ≈ 4 mT and has no W_sc dependence. Yet the W_sc dependence in Fig. 5c is attributed to orbital flux.
   - None of the three estimates (13, 4 and 690 mT) comes close to the observed 90–270 mT.

   The paper should state plainly that the mechanism setting B_η is not identified, or give a calculation of η(B_∥) for finite W_sc that reproduces both the scale and the 1/W_sc slope. The statement that the data are "consistent with an orbital mechanism" needs to be tempered accordingly.

4. The gate dependence of B* is attributed to Rashba α and transparency τ, but neither is measured (Figs. 3c and 4, Eq. 3). The right-hand axis of Fig. 4 asserts that α increases and τ decreases with V_g. The α trend is cited from ref. 46, which used a different sample, and the τ trend is not supported by any data. Eq. (3) also contains k_F and E_T, both of which change with V_g. B* ∝ k_so/k_F, and k_so/k_F = αm*/(ħ²k_F) falls as density rises unless α rises faster. Several devices also show negative B* at negative V_g: about −5 mT for 1.2 µm in Figs. 3c and 4, and about −2 mT for 0.9 µm. Eq. (3) gives only a positive B*, so these sign changes are not captured. The authors should:
   - provide gate-dependent density and τ for each device, for example from R_N, excess current or MAR versus V_g;
   - ideally provide α(V_g) from weak antilocalization on a Hall bar from the same wafer;
   - discuss the negative B* values;
   - explain the non-monotonic W_sc dependence of B* at V_g = 0 (a minimum near 0.6 µm, then a rise at 0.3 µm), given that Eq. (3) is stated to hold only for W_sc → ∞.

   The fit to "Eq. (1) of ref. 8" that yields B* should be written out in the supplement, with its free and fixed parameters.

5. The gate voltages are not matched across devices (Figs. 3c, 4, 5c). "V_g < 0" means a different voltage for each device: roughly −4, −3.5 and −3 V in Fig. 4. The 0.3 µm device has no V_g = 10 V point. Because the curve in Fig. 1g pinches off near −5 V, small offsets in V_g correspond to large changes in density near depletion. Comparing devices at mismatched V_g weakens the conclusion that B* depends on both W_sc and V_g. The authors should:
   - list the exact V_g for each point;
   - convert V_g to density, or to normalized I_c(V_g)/I_c(0), for each device;
   - explain why the 0.3 µm device lacks the 10 V data.

6. The statistics and uncertainty of the switching current are under-specified (Methods, Fig. 1h, Figs. 3 and 5). I_c± are single switching events from a dc ramp, fitted locally around the Fraunhofer extremum. The error bars are the RMS deviation from that local fit, propagated "assuming zero covariance". Premature switching and its stochastic spread are not characterized, and asymmetric switching histograms can produce or mask a few-percent η. The authors should:
   - report the number of sweeps per point and the switching-current distribution width at a representative field;
   - state the ramp rate and filtering;
   - check that η is unchanged when the median of repeated switching events is used.

   I_c+ and I_c− are extracted at different B_⊥ values and pass through the same fitting procedure, so their errors are likely correlated. Assuming zero covariance may overstate or understate the error on η.

7. Temperature dependence is missing. All data are taken at about 30 mK base temperature, and the electron temperature is not given. The mechanisms the paper weighs (Meissner screening, orbital finite-q, Rashba–Zeeman finite-q) predict different temperature dependences of η and B_η. ξ in Eq. (2) is also temperature dependent through the induced gap. Even one I_c(W_sc) and η(B_∥) data set at an elevated temperature (for example 0.5–1 K) would test whether the extracted length follows the expected ξ(T) and would help separate the mechanisms.

8. Angle dependence and the null control are limited. Only θ = 0 and θ = π/2 are shown, and θ = 0 apparently only for one device (Fig. 1h). A full in-plane angle dependence of η would test the sin θ form expected for both spin–orbit and orbital mechanisms, for example at B_η for the narrowest and widest devices. It would also bound the effect of the 2° misalignment noted in the Methods. The θ = 0 control should be shown for every W_sc, since a misalignment or geometry-specific artifact could itself depend on W_sc.

9. The calibration of W_sc is indirect. W_sc was confirmed by SEM of "nominally identical proxy devices" rather than the measured junctions (page 2). Wet-etch undercut of the 10 nm Al can be tens of nm, which is significant for W_sc = 0.3 µm and for W_n = 80 nm. The 0.3 µm device sits at the largest lever arm in both Fig. 3b and Fig. 5c. The authors should give uncertainties for W_sc and W_n, image the measured devices after measurement if possible, and propagate these uncertainties into ξ and the Fig. 5c slope.

C. Minor

10. Fig. 3b has no error bars, and the uncertainty on ξ = 586 nm is not stated. The y-variable 1/ln[I_c^∞/(I_c^∞ − max I_c)] is very sensitive to I_c^∞ as max I_c → I_c^∞. The authors should show the error propagation, including the uncertainty of I_c^∞.

11. The statement that all but the 0.3 µm device show "a weak sign change in the range 400–600 mT" (page 4) cannot be verified by eye in Fig. 5a. An inset or zoom with error bands would help.

12. The expression ξ_ABS = ξ/√τ |sin(φ/2)| and the statement that it "approaches ξ at the critical current in the high-transparency limit" need a clearer derivation or reference to the exact equation. As written, ξ_ABS diverges as sin(φ/2) → 0.

13. The difference between Eq. (6) and ref. 20 is noted in passing. A short derivation in the supplement would let readers check the 0.8Δ factor.

14. Eq. (4) uses d = 10–17 nm, "excluding or including half of the Al and InAs layer thicknesses". The effective separation used for the line in Fig. 5c should be stated explicitly, and the legend "Φ/Φ_0 = 0.036 nm^−1 × d" should be explained in the caption.

15. Fig. 4 shows the same data as Fig. 3c. One of the two could be moved to the supplement, or the redundancy noted more prominently.

16. Several references have missing spaces or arXiv-style formatting, for example refs. 1, 7, 9, 12, 13 and 53. The Al thickness inferred from the critical field (ref. 37) should be quoted with its uncertainty.

17. The Methods quote mobility as "9.86×10^3 cm²/Vs" while the main text uses different transport parameters (see concern 2). A single table of material parameters, each with its source, would avoid confusion.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a table of per-device characterization: W_sc and W_n from SEM of the measured devices, R_N, I_cR_N, the excess current or transparency, and I_c(V_g) pinch-off. Use it to normalize the W_sc trends.
2. Measure Δ* and α(V_g) independently, from a tunnelling or MAR measurement and from gated weak antilocalization on the same wafer. Use them to recompute ξ and to test Eq. (3) quantitatively rather than qualitatively.
3. Add a numerical model of the actual T-shaped geometry with finite-width contacts and an in-plane field, including orbital and Rashba–Zeeman terms. Use it to predict B* and B_η versus W_sc, and say which mechanism sets each field scale.
4. Extend the measurements to more devices per width, to a few temperatures, and to a full in-plane field angle dependence. Together these would turn the observed trends into a discriminating test of the proposed mechanisms.
