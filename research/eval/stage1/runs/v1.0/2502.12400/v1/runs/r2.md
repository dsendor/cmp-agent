Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript varies one geometric parameter, the longitudinal width W_sc of the Al contacts (0.3, 0.6, 0.9 and 1.2 µm plus a "W_sc → ∞" device), in co-fabricated, gated Al/InAs planar Josephson junctions in an in-plane field. It reports how the critical current, the diode efficiency and two characteristic fields (B_* and B_η) depend on that width. Using contact geometry as a control knob to separate superconducting diode mechanisms is a useful idea, and the data set is a reasonable first step. However, each conclusion rests on one device per width, a phenomenological model with ambiguities, and qualitative comparisons with theory. As written, the paper shows trends but does not yet establish the mechanisms it proposes.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- All devices were co-fabricated on one 1.6 mm × 1.6 mm chip with the same 80 nm separation, so some growth- and process-related variation is reduced.
- The critical-current measurement compensates for out-of-plane field by taking the extremum of the central Fraunhofer lobe at each in-plane field and gate voltage. This addresses the most common artifact in in-plane diode measurements.
- The systematic trends are clear and potentially useful for device design: I_c increases with W_sc (Fig. 2a), the peak |η| at W_sc → ∞ is larger than for the finite widths (Fig. 4b), B_η depends strongly on W_sc but weakly on gate (Fig. 4c), and B_* depends strongly on gate (Fig. 3).
- The paper works through order-of-magnitude estimates for three finite-momentum mechanisms in Sec. IV (q_orb, q_M, q_RZ). It also states openly that none of them quantitatively matches the measured field scales.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. One device per contact width, with no normalization for junction transparency. Each W_sc is represented by a single junction (Figs. 2–4). Both headline trends, I_c(W_sc) in Fig. 2 and max|η|(W_sc) and B_η(W_sc) in Fig. 4, therefore assume that the 4–20% device-to-device spread usual in nominally identical planar JJs is negligible. That spread typically comes from interface transparency, the exact contact separation after the wet etch of the Al, and disorder in the 2DEG.
   - The fitted ξ is sensitive to this. With I_c^∞ fixed by a single device, a ±5% change in I_c^∞ changes 1/ln(I_c^∞/(I_c^∞ − I_c)) for the 1.2 µm device by roughly ±15–20%.
   - Fig. 4b is non-monotonic (the 1.2 µm device has lower max|η| than the 0.9 µm device). This is what device-to-device scatter would look like.
   - The authors should report the normal-state resistance R_n, I_cR_n and, if available, the excess current for every device. They should also show I_c(W_sc) normalized by 1/R_n. Ideally they should add at least a second device for several widths so that the spread can be compared with the W_sc dependence. The separation of each junction should be measured directly, not only on proxy devices.

2. The model behind Eq. (1), and the agreement between ξ = 586 nm and ξ ≈ 570 nm, are not on firm footing (Sec. III, Fig. 2b).
   - (i) Factor of two. The text says the reflection probability is proportional to the amplitude ψ ∝ exp(−W_sc/ξ). A probability would normally go as |ψ|² ∝ exp(−2W_sc/ξ). That would double the extracted ξ to about 1.2 µm and remove the quoted agreement. The authors need to justify Eq. (1), for example from a BdG/tight-binding calculation of a junction with finite-width proximitized contacts, and show which length enters.
   - (ii) Which length is fitted. Eq. (1) fits ξ_ABS = ξ/(√τ |sin(φ/2)|). This reduces to ξ only if τ → 1 and φ_c → π. Yet Sec. III invokes changes in τ with gate voltage, and for τ < 1 the critical phase is below π. So the fitted length is not ξ unless τ is shown to be close to 1.
   - (iii) Which gap and Fermi velocity. The "dirty-limit" estimate uses the Al gap (1.764 k_B T_c), together with the density and mean free path of the bare 2DEG from magnetotransport. The ABS decays into the Al-covered, proximitized 2DEG. There the relevant gap is the induced gap, and the density, Fermi velocity and mean free path differ from those of the bare 2DEG.
   - (iv) Single-mode picture. A 4 µm-wide junction has many transverse modes with different longitudinal velocities, so there is a distribution of decay lengths.
   - (v) Alternative explanations for the reduced I_c in narrow contacts are not ruled out:
     - Phase drop along a narrow, 10 nm-thick Al strip from its kinetic inductance. A rough estimate of 8 pH per square gives about 100 pH over 4 µm for W_sc = 0.3 µm, which corresponds to a phase drop of order 1 rad at 5 µA. This scales as 1/W_sc. The authors themselves note kinetic inductance as non-negligible in Sec. IV.
     - Gap suppression in narrow Al strips.
     - Current crowding where the strips connect to the leads.

   The authors should discuss these quantitatively. Two simple checks would help: fitting ξ at the other gate voltages (the data in S.IV already exist), and at a second temperature. Under the coherence-length interpretation, ξ should be largely gate-independent, because the gate is screened over the Al-covered region. It should also grow with temperature as the induced gap shrinks.

3. The orbital interpretation of B_η and the claimed saturation for W_sc ≳ 2ξ are not supported by Fig. 4c as presented.
   - (i) The fit is not Eq. (3). Eq. (3) gives B_η = Φ/(W_sc d), a line through the origin in W_sc⁻¹. The fitted line in Fig. 4c has an intercept of about 25 mT at W_sc⁻¹ = 0. The intercept should be stated, justified physically, or removed.
   - (ii) The fit does not test the model. Φ and d are both free, so the fit determines only their product. "Φ/Φ0 ≈ 0.36–0.61 for d = 10–17 nm" is a range chosen to look reasonable, not a test. An orbital mechanism with a Φ0-periodic structure would predict a specific Φ, near Φ0/2 or tied to the first sign change at Φ0. Yet the paper reports that the sign changes do not occur at Φ0 and that no Φ0-periodic oscillation is seen (Sec. IV). Those are the key signatures of the proposed mechanism, and they are absent.
   - (iii) The two orbital estimates in the paper disagree. Eq. (5) with q_orb predicts B^max ≈ 4 mT. This is independent of W_sc and should apply to the W_sc → ∞ device, but that device has B_η ≈ 90–115 mT. The authors should reconcile the flux picture (Eq. 3) with the finite-momentum picture (Eq. 5).
   - (iv) The saturation claim rests on one point. Between W_sc = 1.2 µm and the "∞" point there are no data. Both the 1.2 and 0.9 µm points lie on the line, so the onset of saturation is not determined. It could lie anywhere between 1.2 µm and the actual width of the "∞" contact. The dashed line at (2ξ)⁻¹ is not tested by the data.
   - (v) Other mechanisms scale the same way. Meissner screening and vortex entry in the Al strip cross-section (d_sc × W_sc) also give field scales that scale as 1/W_sc for an in-plane field along the strip, so 1/W_sc scaling does not single out the orbital mechanism.

   To support the abstract's statements, the authors would need:
   - the real width of the "∞" contact, with the point plotted at that width;
   - devices with W_sc between about 1.2 and 5 µm;
   - a control that separates the orbital effect from Meissner and vortex mechanisms, such as a different Al thickness or 2DEG depth, or rotating the in-plane field toward the current direction.

B. Should address

4. The gate dependence of B_* (Figs. 2c, 3) is assigned to spin–orbit coupling without measuring the quantities Eq. (2) depends on.
   - The paper asserts that α increases with V_g and that τ decreases with V_g. The second is counterintuitive, since higher density usually raises transparency, and neither is measured.
   - The gate also changes g*, and possibly n and E_T. These do not all cancel in Eq. (2) once the contacts and junction region respond differently.
   - Eq. (2) is never evaluated numerically and compared in magnitude with the measured 0–40 mT values.
   - The authors should give gate-dependent α (from weak antilocalization on a Hall bar, Ref. 45), τ (from I_cR_n or multiple Andreev reflection), and n. They should then compare Eq. (2) quantitatively. Eq. (2) is derived for W_sc → ∞, yet B_* is plotted against W_sc (Fig. 2c) and called a "nontrivial dependence". The authors should state what the W_sc dependence of B_* is meant to show.

5. Extracting B_* is delicate at the few-mT scale. B_* lies between −5 and 40 mT and is obtained from "low-field fits to Eq. (1) of Ref. 8", but the fit function, the field step and the fit window are not given.
   - A remanent field in the in-plane magnet, or trapped flux, of a few mT shifts argmax I_c+ and argmin I_c− in opposite directions.
   - The authors should state whether B_* is the average of argmax I_c+ and −argmin I_c−, which would cancel such an offset, or comes from one branch. They should show the two separately, together with sweep-direction (hysteresis) data.
   - The negative B_* values (around −5 mT for 1.2 µm at V_g < 0) need comment, since they reverse the sign of the effect.

6. The W_sc dependence of the diode efficiency is weaker than the text suggests (Fig. 4b). Apart from the "∞" device (16.5%), max|η| ranges from about 5% to 10% and is non-monotonic. No error bars are given, so the "general trend" depends largely on one device. The authors should add uncertainties, from switching-current statistics and repeated sweeps, to Fig. 4b. They should either qualify the claim in the abstract ("diode efficiency increases with contact width") or support it with more devices (see concern 1).

7. Key measurement details are missing from the main text, and the supplement was not available to this reviewer. The diode efficiencies are a few percent, so the following directly affect η:
   - the mixing-chamber and electron temperature;
   - the I_c criterion (switching or voltage threshold) and current sweep rate;
   - the number of sweeps per point and the width of the switching distribution;
   - whether I_c+ and I_c− are measured in separate sweeps starting from zero bias;
   - whether the antisymmetry I_c+(B∥) = −I_c−(−B∥) stated for Fig. 2a is observed or imposed by symmetrization.

   Taking I_c+ and I_c− at different out-of-plane fields (the extrema of each branch) removes Fraunhofer shifts, but it also removes any genuine diode contribution from those shifts. The authors should state this and show one raw Fraunhofer map at large B∥.

8. The device geometry relevant to the "rear interface" is unclear (Fig. 1a, Sec. II). From the false-colored SEM it is not clear what lies beyond the outer edge of each Al strip: etched mesa, bare 2DEG, or wider Al. It is also unclear how current reaches the strips (along their full length or from one end), and what the "W_sc → ∞" device actually is. These details determine:
   - the boundary condition behind Eq. (1);
   - the kinetic-inductance phase gradient raised in concern 2;
   - the actual position of the "∞" point in Figs. 2b and 4c.

   In addition, the Ti/Au gate lead crosses one contact (Fig. 1a), so the devices are not strictly mirror-symmetric. A comment on whether this asymmetry can contribute to the nonreciprocity is needed, for example a comparison with reversed current-lead geometry.

C. Minor

9. Fig. 2b: the W_sc → ∞ point at the origin is zero by construction (I_c = I_c^∞), so it is not a data point. Please mark it as such, treat I_c^∞ as a free parameter (or propagate its uncertainty), and quote an uncertainty and residuals for ξ = 586 nm.

10. Figs. 2c and 3: "V_g < 0" refers to different voltages for different devices (roughly −2.5 to −5 V in Fig. 3), and some devices have two negative-gate points. Please give the exact V_g for each point, explain why there is no V_g = 10 V point for W_sc = 0.3 µm, and calibrate V_g to density for each device so that comparisons are made at matched density.

11. Fig. 4c has no error bars on B_η, and Fig. 2c has error bars on only some points. Please add uncertainties throughout.

12. The Meissner estimate takes λ_L → d_sc/2 with d_sc = 10 nm. That thickness is "inferred" from the 2.7 T critical field rather than measured (Sec. II and S.I). Please state the nominal growth thickness and justify this limit of the λ_L parameter.

13. Fig. 1a: the caption says the superconductor is blue and the semiconductor green. This makes it hard to tell which large regions are Al leads and which are bare semiconductor. Please clarify the color scheme and label the leads.

14. Sec. IV: the claim that "q_orb > q_M > q_RZ … suggesting orbital effects are strong" sits uneasily with the predicted B^max values (4 mT, 13 mT, 690 mT), none of which is close to the observed B_η of 90–270 mT. The concluding interpretation should be worded to match.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Rotate the in-plane field from perpendicular to parallel to the current for at least two widths. The flux through the W_sc·d area and the Rashba–Zeeman finite-momentum term have distinct angular dependences, so this would help separate orbital from spin–orbit contributions.
2. Measure the temperature dependence of I_c(W_sc) and of B_η. If ξ follows the induced gap and B_η stays tied to a fixed flux, this would strongly support the two central interpretations.
3. Add a BdG or tight-binding simulation of a multimode planar junction with finite-width proximitized contacts, an in-plane field and Rashba coupling. This would test Eq. (1), the 1/W_sc scaling of B_η, and whether 2ξ is the relevant saturation scale, and it would put Eqs. (2) and (5) on a quantitative footing for this geometry.
4. Fill the gap between W_sc = 1.2 µm and the "∞" device with several intermediate widths, and add devices from a heterostructure with a different Al thickness or 2DEG depth. This would test both the saturation claim and the d dependence implied by Eq. (3).
