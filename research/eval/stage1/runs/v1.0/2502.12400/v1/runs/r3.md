Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript measures how the longitudinal width W_sc of the superconducting contacts affects the critical current and the superconducting diode effect in Al/InAs planar Josephson junctions in an in-plane field perpendicular to the current. Five co-fabricated devices (W_sc = 0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.2 µm and a nominally "infinite" contact) give three trends. I_c follows a saturating exponential in W_sc with a length ξ ≈ 586 nm. The peak diode efficiency grows with W_sc. The field B_η at which η is largest goes roughly linearly in 1/W_sc and is only weakly gate-dependent, while the smaller field B_* at which I_c is largest depends strongly on the gate. Using contact geometry as a control parameter to separate diode mechanisms is a useful idea, and the dataset is relevant to the large planar-JJ and topological-superconductivity community. However, the mechanism assignments (orbital for B_η, spin-orbit for B_*) currently rest on one device per geometry and on qualitative consistency arguments that do not discriminate between mechanisms.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The approach is systematic. All devices share one chip and one heterostructure with a nominally identical 80 nm junction length, so contact width is in principle the only design variable.
- Fig. 2(a) shows a clear and visually convincing increase of I_c with W_sc. The extracted ξ = 586 nm is close to a simple dirty-limit estimate built from independently measured n, ℓ and T_c.
- The paper separates two characteristic fields, B_* (I_c extremum) and B_η (η extremum), and shows that they respond differently to W_sc and V_g (Figs. 2(c), 3, 4(c)). This could be a genuinely useful diagnostic.
- The authors compensate the out-of-plane field by tracking the Fraunhofer central lobe at every in-plane field and gate setting, which reduces one of the main extrinsic diode sources.
- Section IV gives explicit order-of-magnitude estimates of the finite-momentum scale for the Meissner, orbital and Rashba-Zeeman mechanisms (Eq. 5), so readers can check them.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. One device per contact width, with no device-to-device spread or device-level characterization (Figs. 2, 3, 4).
   - Every conclusion of the paper is a trend across five devices, each with a different W_sc. No geometry is repeated, so device-to-device variation cannot be separated from a genuine W_sc dependence. Such variation in planar Al/InAs junctions comes from interface transparency, local density, etch quality and the effective junction length.
   - The danger is visible in Fig. 4(b). max|η| is about 8.5% for W_sc = 1.2 µm but about 10% for 0.9 µm, so the "general trend" is non-monotonic. The W_sc → ∞ device (about 16.5%) carries much of the trend.
   - No normal-state resistance R_N, I_cR_N product, excess current or transparency estimate is reported for each device. These are exactly the quantities that would show whether the devices differ only in W_sc. They also enter the analysis directly: τ appears in Eq. (2) and in ξ_ABS.
   - The authors should:
     - tabulate R_N, I_cR_N, I_exc (or a transparency estimate) and the measured junction separation for every device;
     - ideally add at least a second device for some widths (or a second chip), so the spread in I_c, max|η|, B_* and B_η can be compared with the size of the claimed trends;
     - state whether all devices were measured in the same cooldown and whether the key quantities survive thermal cycling. T_c is quoted as "measured during multiple cooldowns", but the device data are not tied to specific cooldowns.

2. The orbital interpretation of B_η is not supported quantitatively, and it conflicts with the paper's own estimates (Fig. 4(c), Section IV).
   - The fit and its parameters:
     - The fit of B_η to Eq. (3) excludes the W_sc → ∞ point and includes a free, clearly nonzero intercept (about 25 mT at W_sc⁻¹ = 0 in Fig. 4(c)). A pure flux-quantization picture B_η W_sc d = const has no intercept.
     - The fit constrains only the product Φ/d. The quoted Φ/Φ_0 ≈ 0.36–0.61 follows entirely from an assumed d = 10–17 nm, not from the data.
     - Only four points remain, and the "saturation for W_sc ≳ 2ξ" rests on two of them (1.2 µm and ∞).
   - The orbital picture's own predictions are not observed:
     - The text concedes that the orbital mechanism predicts η sign changes at Φ = Φ_0 and Φ_0-periodic oscillations, and that neither is seen. The sign changes appear at 400–600 mT for all devices.
     - If the flux through W_sc·d controls η, the sign-change field should also scale as 1/W_sc. For W_sc = 0.9 µm and d = 15 nm, Φ_0 corresponds to about 150 mT, roughly a factor of 3 below the observed crossings. The paper does not report the crossing fields against W_sc.
     - Assigning the missing oscillations to Zeeman collapse of the induced gap is not tested.
   - The paper's own orbital estimate contradicts the data:
     - In Section IV, the orbital Cooper-pair momentum q_orb = (e/ħ)B∥d does not depend on W_sc at all.
     - It predicts B∥^max ≈ 4 mT, while the measured B_η is about 90–265 mT. The Meissner estimate (13 mT) and the Rashba-Zeeman estimate (690 mT) also do not reproduce the data or its W_sc scaling.
     - The sentence "the above estimates … are a particularly good match to the experimental values observed in Fig. 4(c)" is therefore either a typo (missing "not") or incorrect. Either way it must be fixed, because it is the only quantitative link between model and data in the section.
   - The authors should:
     - fit B_η with and without a free intercept and report the parameter uncertainties;
     - plot the η sign-change field against W_sc⁻¹;
     - either give a model in which the η extremum is set by flux through W_sc·d and reproduces the magnitude of B_η, or reduce the claim to "B_η decreases with W_sc".

3. The gate dependence of B_* is attributed to spin-orbit coupling without measuring the spin-orbit strength, and B_* itself is small enough to be sensitive to field offsets (Figs. 2(c), 3, Eq. 2).
   - What the gate changes:
     - A gate changes the density, k_F, v_F, the mean free path, the induced gap, τ and E_T, not just α.
     - Eq. (2) depends on k_so/k_F, (1−τ)^{1/4} and E_T. The Meissner and orbital estimates in Eq. (5) also depend on v_F and Δ, so they are gate-dependent too.
     - The paper measures none of α(V_g), n(V_g), ℓ(V_g) or τ(V_g) for these samples. The direction of α with V_g is taken from Ref. [45], and the right-hand axis of Fig. 3 is schematic.
     - "Gate tunability lends credence to a spin-orbit interpretation" therefore does not exclude the other mechanisms.
     - Conversely, the text uses the weak gate dependence of B_η as also "consistent with a spin–orbit mechanism". So both strong and weak gate dependence are presented as support for spin-orbit coupling.
   - Sensitivity of B_* to field offsets and fitting:
     - B_* is 5–40 mT and changes sign for some devices at V_g < 0 (Fig. 3, orange and green). This is comparable to typical remanent-field offsets and to the hysteresis of superconducting vector magnets.
     - The paper does not say how the zero of B∥ was calibrated. It also does not say whether I_c+(B∥) = −I_c−(−B∥) was checked independently, or imposed by averaging, at the few-mT level.
     - B_* is extracted from "low-field fits to Eq. (1) of Ref. [8]". That fit function and its free parameters are not given here.
   - The "V_g < 0" series in Fig. 2(c) is taken at a different negative gate voltage for each device (Fig. 3: about −3 to −4.5 V). The W_sc dependence of that series therefore mixes in gate dependence.
   - The authors should:
     - measure n(V_g) and α(V_g), for example with Hall and weak-antilocalization measurements on a co-fabricated Hall bar, and τ(V_g) from R_N or the excess current;
     - show that B_* scales with k_so/k_F as Eq. (2) predicts, rather than merely increasing;
     - document the field-offset calibration, the sweep direction and the fit function used for B_*;
     - compare devices at a common negative gate voltage.

B. Should address

4. The coherence-length interpretation of I_c(W_sc) is heuristic and alternatives are not excluded (Eq. 1, Fig. 2(b)).
   - The model is not derived and the comparison is loose:
     - Eq. (1) is motivated by a reflection "probability proportional to the amplitude" at the rear interface, not by a calculation. Existing finite-contact-width theory (e.g. Refs. [31–33]) is not compared quantitatively.
     - The fit has effectively one free parameter on four nontrivial points: I_c^∞ is fixed by the W_sc → ∞ device, which sits at the origin by construction. No uncertainty on ξ is given.
     - The comparison with the dirty-limit estimate (570 nm) depends on choices made outside the fit:
       - Δ is taken from the Al T_c rather than the induced gap in the 2DEG.
       - n and ℓ come from magnetotransport on bare 2DEG rather than the Al-covered region.
       - Order-unity prefactors in the dirty-limit coherence length are dropped.
     - The 3% agreement is therefore within the uncertainty of the estimate, not a sharp test.
   - Alternatives the paper does not rule out:
     - The 10 nm Al strips are 4 µm long and only 0.3–1.2 µm wide. Their kinetic inductance can be comparable to the Josephson inductance Φ_0/2πI_c ≈ 66 pH at 5 µA. That would produce phase gradients along the junction width and suppress I_c for narrow contacts; the conclusion itself acknowledges that kinetic inductance is neglected.
     - A weaker induced gap or inverse-proximity effect in narrow contacts would act similarly.
     - So would current paths through exposed 2DEG behind the contact (see Concern 5).
   - I_c is evaluated at the field maximum B_*, where the diode and finite-momentum effects already act, rather than at B∥ = 0.
   - The authors should:
     - give the uncertainty on ξ;
     - repeat the analysis with I_c(B∥ = 0);
     - test the interpretation using the W_sc dependence at several gate voltages and/or temperatures. If the interpretation holds, ξ should track √(v_F ℓ/Δ) as density and Δ(T) change.
     - estimate the kinetic inductance of the contacts from their sheet resistance.

5. The contact geometry and its uncertainty are under-specified (Section II, Fig. 1(a)).
   - Contact widths:
     - W_sc and the 80 nm separation were not measured on the measured devices. They were "confirmed by scanning electron micrographs of nominally identical proxy devices".
     - Al wet etching typically undercuts by tens of nm, which matters at W_sc = 0.3 µm and in the 80 nm gap.
     - Figs. 2(b), 2(c), 4(b) and 4(c) have no horizontal error bars.
   - The "W_sc → ∞" device:
     - Its actual dimensions and geometry are not given, nor whether it is T-shaped at all.
     - Placing it at W_sc⁻¹ = 0 is an assumption that drives both the I_c fit and the η trend.
   - From Fig. 1(a), it is unclear what lies behind the rear edge of each contact, whether exposed 2DEG or etched mesa, and how current reaches the narrow strips. This determines whether the rear interface is S/vacuum, as Eq. (1) assumes, or S/normal 2DEG. In the latter case a parallel normal path and lateral proximity would complicate both the I_c(W_sc) and η analyses.
   - The authors should:
     - give measured (or proxy-derived) W_sc with uncertainties;
     - describe the W_sc → ∞ geometry;
     - label the regions and current path in Fig. 1(a) unambiguously.

6. The out-of-plane compensation could itself create a W_sc-dependent diode signal (Section II, Section S.III).
   - Taking I_c+ as the maximum and I_c− as the minimum of the Fraunhofer pattern, each at its own out-of-plane field, is reasonable. However, a relative B_z shift between the I_c+ and I_c− lobes is itself a diode signature, from inhomogeneous current or self-field effects.
   - Flux focusing by the contacts, and hence the Fraunhofer period and any misalignment-induced B_z, depends on W_sc.
   - The main text should show, for each device:
     - representative Fraunhofer maps at several B∥;
     - the B_z offset of the I_c± maxima against B∥;
     - the Fraunhofer period against W_sc.
   - This would show that the W_sc trends in η are not driven by residual out-of-plane flux or flux focusing.

7. Measurement details that set the error bars are missing from the main text.
   - Not stated:
     - the base or electron temperature;
     - the I_c criterion (voltage threshold or dV/dI peak);
     - the current-sweep protocol (sweeps from zero toward each polarity, or through zero);
     - whether the junctions are hysteretic;
     - the switching-current statistics behind the error bars on B_* and the shaded bands on η.
   - With I_c ≈ 2–5 µA and η of a few percent in the narrow devices, switching-current fluctuations and heating can be comparable to I_c+ − |I_c−|.
   - The authors should state these items and show that η is unchanged under reversed sweep direction.

8. The explanation for the W_sc → ∞ deviation is unsupported (Fig. 4(c)).
   - Excluding the W_sc → ∞ point from the Eq. (3) fit is justified by "formation of Josephson vortices between the Al and InAs layers", with no supporting evidence or estimate.
   - The authors should either give an estimate, such as the Josephson penetration length for the Al/InAs interlayer junction compared with W_sc, or present the exclusion as an open discrepancy.
   - The saturation of B_η for W_sc ≳ 2ξ is attributed to the same physics. With only two points in that regime, it should be stated tentatively.

9. The gate dependence of η and B_η is relegated to the SI, though it is central to separating mechanisms (Fig. 4(c), Section S.IV).
   - Fig. 4(c) shows B_η at three gate voltages, but max|η| against V_g, and the η(B∥) traces at other gates, are not in the main text.
   - The paper's mechanism argument contrasts gate-sensitive B_* with gate-insensitive B_η, so both should appear side by side with uncertainties.
   - The Al thickness d_sc = 10 nm, which enters q_M, is inferred from the in-plane critical field rather than measured (e.g. by TEM or growth calibration). Its uncertainty should be propagated.

C. Minor

10. Section III: ξ_ABS = ξ/(√τ|sin(φ/2)|) approaches ξ only if φ → π at I_c. That holds only for τ → 1, whereas Eq. (2) and the discussion assume τ < 1. Please state the range of φ at I_c, and the τ, appropriate for these junctions, or drop the identification ξ_ABS ≈ ξ.

11. Section IV: the resonance condition 2q(2W_sc + W_n) = (2n+1)π/2 for η zeros is introduced but never compared with the data. Either test it against the observed crossing fields or remove it.

12. Fig. 2(c) and Fig. 3 show the same B_* data. Please check that the V_g < 0 values agree between the two plots. In particular, the W_sc = 1.2 µm point appears near −3 mT in Fig. 2(c) but near −5.5 mT in Fig. 3. Also give the specific negative gate voltage for each device in the caption.

13. Fig. 4(c): the legend "Φ/Φ_0 = 0.036 nm⁻¹ × d" should state the fitted slope and intercept, with uncertainties, in field·length units. The dashed (2ξ)⁻¹ line should carry the uncertainty on ξ.

14. Figs. 2(b) and 4(b) have no error bars. Please add them, propagated from the I_c and η uncertainties.

15. The low-field fit function of Ref. [8] used to extract B_* should be written out in the paper, with its free and fixed parameters.

16. Section III calls Δ = 1.764 k_B T_c the "induced gap", but it is the BCS gap of the Al film. Please distinguish the two, or give an independent measure of the induced gap.

17. The color coding in Fig. 1(a) is hard to read: large areas appear green, labeled "semiconductor", while the caption describes T-shaped superconducting contacts. A labeled schematic plan view next to the SEM would help.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Measure a second device for at least two of the widths (for example 0.3 µm and 1.2 µm) and report R_N, I_cR_N and the excess current for all devices. This would turn the W_sc trends from suggestive into established.
2. Measure n, α and the mean free path against gate voltage on a co-fabricated Hall bar. Then test Eq. (2) quantitatively (B_* against k_so/k_F) and check whether ξ from Eq. (1) tracks √(v_F ℓ/Δ) at several gate voltages.
3. Plot the η sign-change field against 1/W_sc alongside B_η, and compare both with a calculation that includes finite W_sc and the multimode effects the authors mention (Costa et al.). Mechanism assignments should rest on whether the measured fields and their scaling are reproduced, not on qualitative consistency.
4. Add temperature dependence of I_c and of max|η| for the narrowest and widest contacts. This would test the coherence-length picture through ξ(T), check for heating, and help separate gap-collapse explanations from geometric ones for the missing Φ_0 oscillations.
