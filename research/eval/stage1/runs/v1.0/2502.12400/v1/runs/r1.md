Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript studies how the longitudinal width W_sc of the superconducting contacts in T-shaped Al/InAs planar Josephson junctions affects the critical current and the superconducting diode effect in an in-plane field. Varying the device geometry on a single chip is a useful idea, because it could separate diode mechanisms that are otherwise hard to tell apart (orbital/flux, Meissner, Rashba–Zeeman). The main reported observations are: I_c saturates exponentially with W_sc on a length of about 590 nm; the peak diode efficiency rises with W_sc; the field of maximum efficiency B_η scales roughly linearly with 1/W_sc; and the low field B* at which I_c is extremal is gate-tunable. As presented, the work is a set of qualitative trends from one device per geometry. The mechanistic interpretation is not yet backed by quantitative comparison and in places conflicts with the paper's own estimates.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- All devices were co-fabricated within 1.6 mm × 1.6 mm on one chip, with a nominally fixed 80 nm junction length, so the contact width is the intended main variable.
- The I_c(W_sc) data are smooth and match a simple saturating form (Fig. 2a,b). The fitted length (586 nm) has the right order of magnitude for the induced coherence length estimated from independent transport parameters.
- B_η changes strongly with W_sc (Fig. 4c) while B* depends strongly on gate voltage (Fig. 3). Two characteristic fields that respond differently to geometry and to gating are a useful empirical result for the diode-effect literature.
- The comparison of momentum-shift estimates (q_orb > q_M > q_RZ, Eq. 5) is transparent and shows the authors know the main competing mechanisms.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The orbital interpretation of B_η conflicts with the paper's own orbital estimate, and the 1/W_sc scaling is not derived from any model. Section IV and the Conclusion say the W_sc dependence of B_η "invites an orbital interpretation", based on the flux relation B_∥ W_sc d = Φ (Eq. 3) and the fit in Fig. 4c. However, the orbital finite-momentum mechanism the paper then quantifies (Ref. 15, q_orb = (e/ħ) B_∥ d) does not depend on W_sc. Combined with Eq. 5 it predicts B^max ≈ 4 mT, which is 25–70 times smaller than the measured B_η ≈ 90–270 mT. The text admits that "none of the above estimates … are a particularly good match", yet the Conclusion keeps the orbital attribution. Further problems:
   - The fit in Fig. 4c has a sizeable intercept (about 25 mT at W_sc⁻¹ = 0), while Eq. 3 implies B_η ∝ 1/W_sc through the origin.
   - The reported Φ/Φ0 ≈ 0.36–0.61 is not a test, because it follows from whatever d is assumed. No model is given that predicts the flux at which η should peak.
   - The claimed saturation for W_sc ≳ 2ξ rests on one point (W_sc → ∞), and that point is excluded from the fit. The only other point in that regime is W_sc = 1.2 µm, which sits at 1/(2ξ).

   The authors should either:
   - (a) give a model, for example a flux-threaded SNS/interferometer picture or a BdG simulation with finite-width contacts, that predicts B_η(W_sc) including its prefactor and intercept, and compare it quantitatively with Fig. 4c; or
   - (b) present the 1/W_sc trend as empirical and soften the orbital attribution in the abstract and Conclusion.

   A direct check of Eq. 3 is already available in the data. The text says the sign changes of η at 400–600 mT occur at Φ > Φ0. The authors should plot the sign-change field against 1/W_sc for every device. If the flux picture holds, it should scale like B_η, with a fixed ratio.

2. With one device per contact width, unmeasured W_sc and no normalization, the cross-device trends are not separated from device-to-device variation. Each W_sc in Figs. 2 and 4 is one junction. The W_sc values are nominal: they were checked by SEM on "proxy devices", not on the measured junctions (Section II). Wet-etch undercut of a 10 nm Al film can easily shift W_sc by tens of nm, which is a large fraction of 1/W_sc for the 0.3 µm device. No normal-state resistance R_n, I_cR_n product or excess current is reported per device, so variations in junction length, interface transparency or 2DEG density cannot be normalized out. These problems matter for two claims:
   - The η claim is fragile. Fig. 4b is not monotonic (W_sc = 0.9 µm gives about 10%, W_sc = 1.2 µm about 8.5%). Peak η then roughly doubles from 1.2 µm to W_sc → ∞ (about 16.5%), even though I_c is already about 90% saturated at 1.2 µm. This jump is not explained, and the W_sc → ∞ device is never defined: is it unetched Al, does it have the same lead and gate layout, and is it a T-geometry at all?
   - Figs. 2b and 4b,c have no error bars on either axis.

   The authors should:
   - report measured W_sc (SEM after measurement) and R_n, I_cR_n for every device and gate voltage;
   - add error bars from switching-current statistics and from the W_sc uncertainty;
   - define the W_sc → ∞ device;
   - ideally, measure a second device at one or more widths to show the spread. A device between 1.2 µm and "∞" would also be needed to support the saturation claims in item 1.

3. Identifying the fitted 586 nm with the induced coherence length relies on assumptions the paper does not justify. Section III makes four:
   - (i) That ξ_ABS ≈ ξ because τ → 1. However, the measured I_c ≈ 5 µA for a 4 µm wide junction with n = 6 × 10¹² cm⁻² is only about 10% of the ballistic estimate N·eΔ/ħ ≈ (k_F W/π)(eΔ/ħ) ≈ 45 µA. This points to moderate transparency or a reduced induced gap. If τ < 1, ξ_ABS = ξ/(√τ |sin(φ/2)|) exceeds ξ, and the agreement with 570 nm becomes accidental.
   - (ii) That the 2D dirty-limit formula carries no prefactor. With D = v_F ℓ/2, ξ = √(ħD/Δ) gives about 1.25 times the quoted value. The 3% agreement is therefore well inside an O(1) convention uncertainty.
   - (iii) That Δ is the Al bulk gap (1.764 k_B T_c) rather than the induced gap, and that ℓ = 130 nm from bare-2DEG magnetotransport also holds under the Al.
   - (iv) That Eq. 1 holds. Its justification is heuristic: it says the reflection "probability" is proportional to the wavefunction "amplitude". It also assumes a reflecting rear boundary. Fig. 1a appears to show semiconductor (green) beyond the Al strips. If the 2DEG continues behind the contacts, the rear boundary is an S/N interface into a normal reservoir. The suppression of I_c would then reflect quasiparticle leakage or inverse proximity, not reflection, and the decay length need not be the induced coherence length.

   Finally, Fig. 2b is effectively a one-parameter fit with four points, and every point depends on the single measured I_c^∞. Fitting I_c^∞ as a free parameter, or testing how sensitive ξ is to it, is necessary.

   The authors should:
   - state the mesa layout behind the contacts;
   - estimate τ from I_cR_n or excess current;
   - give the uncertainty of ξ, including its correlation with I_c^∞;
   - present the 570 nm comparison as order-of-magnitude consistency;
   - ideally, measure I_c(W_sc) at several temperatures to check whether the extracted length scales as expected with Δ(T).

B. Should address

4. The SOC attribution of the gate dependence of B* is not tested against other gate effects. Section III and Fig. 3 attribute the rise of B* with V_g to a larger Rashba α and a smaller τ, through Eq. 2. However, the gate also changes the density, and with it k_F (which appears in Eq. 2), v_F, E_T and ξ. Decreasing transparency at more positive gate voltage, i.e. higher density, runs against the usual trend in these heterostructures and needs evidence. No independent α(V_g) is shown, for example weak antilocalization on a co-fabricated Hall bar; Ref. 45 is cited but its numbers are not used. Nor is R_n(V_g) or I_cR_n(V_g) shown for these devices.

   The authors should:
   - plot R_n, I_c and an estimate of τ against V_g for each device;
   - use measured or cited α(V_g) and n(V_g) to estimate how much B* should change through Eq. 2, and compare that estimate with the factor of about 2–6 change seen in Fig. 3.

   Eq. 2 was derived for W_sc → ∞, as the authors note. The clear W_sc dependence of B* in Fig. 2c, including the upturn for W_sc = 0.3 µm, is called "nontrivial" and then left unexplained, so the paper says nothing about what sets B* in finite-width contacts.

5. Out-of-plane field and field-offset artifacts that scale with W_sc are not controlled for. Several observations point to a field offset:
   - B* is negative for several V_g < 0 points (Figs. 2c and 3, down to about −5 mT). Eq. 2 cannot give this, so it suggests a residual offset or a misalignment-induced out-of-plane component of a few mT. That offset is comparable to the W_sc differences being interpreted.
   - Flux focusing by the superconducting contacts makes the effective area for B_z grow with W_sc, so any misalignment couples more strongly in wider-contact devices.
   - Taking the maximum of I_c+ and the minimum of I_c− separately over a narrow B_z window (Section S.III) can itself change the apparent asymmetry.

   The authors should:
   - state the misalignment angle and the B_z compensation procedure in the main text;
   - show that B* and B_η do not change when the B_z window or its centre is varied;
   - report field-sweep hysteresis and sweep direction, since vortices trapped in the wider Al strips are a W_sc-dependent possibility;
   - comment on whether the negative B* values give a lower bound on the B* uncertainty.

6. A null control with the in-plane field along the current is missing. For the Rashba–Zeeman, Meissner and flux (Eq. 3) mechanisms alike, the diode effect should vanish or be strongly suppressed when B_∥ is parallel to I. Extrinsic contributions should not vanish in the same way: B_z leakage, self-field effects and current crowding in the T-geometry. Measuring η and I_c±(B_∥) for at least two field angles, for at least the narrowest and widest devices, would show that the W_sc trends in Fig. 4 come from the intended mechanisms.

7. The current distribution along the 4 µm contact strip in the T-geometry is not considered. Current enters each contact strip from a central lead (Fig. 1a) and must spread along the 4 µm junction width through a 10 nm Al strip of width W_sc. For narrow strips, the strip's kinetic inductance and resistance can produce a phase gradient along the junction. That gradient grows as the gap is suppressed by B_∥. It could reduce I_c and add a geometric, current-direction-dependent asymmetry, which would be an alternative explanation for both the I_c(W_sc) and η(W_sc) trends. The authors mention kinetic inductance only in passing (Section IV). They should estimate the phase drop along the strip, for example from the sheet kinetic inductance of their Al at the relevant fields, or simulate the current distribution, and show it is negligible for W_sc = 0.3 µm.

8. The definition of I_c, its statistics and the effective temperature are not given in the main text. It is not stated whether I_c is a switching current, how many sweeps were averaged, how large the switching-current spread is, whether the junctions are hysteretic, or what the base and electron temperatures are. These matter because the η values in Fig. 4b (about 5–16%) are computed from differences of I_c+ and |I_c−|. The shaded bands in Fig. 4a are not explained, and Fig. 4b carries no uncertainty. The authors should give the measurement protocol and propagate switching statistics into η and into the extracted fields B* and B_η.

9. The extraction of B* is not described in the main text. Figs. 2c and 3 use "low-field fits to Eq. (1) of Ref. 8", but the reader is not told the fitted function, which parameters are free, the field range used, or how the error bars were obtained. Because B* is only 5–40 mT, while I_c changes over hundreds of mT, the result may depend on the fit window. The authors should state the procedure in the main text and show that B* is stable when the window is changed.

C. Minor

10. Figs. 2c and 3 are meant to show the same data, but some V_g < 0 points do not obviously agree. For example, the W_sc = 1.2 µm point is about −3 mT in Fig. 2c, while the orange points in Fig. 3 at negative V_g read about −5.5 mT and −1.5 mT. The negative gate voltage also differs between devices. The authors should state the exact V_g behind each "V_g < 0" marker in Figs. 2c and 4c, and note that these comparisons are made at mismatched gate voltages.

11. Fig. 4c: the legend "Φ/Φ0 = 0.036 nm⁻¹ × d" does not show the fitted functional form or the intercept. Please give the fit equation, both parameters with uncertainties, and an explanation of the intercept.

12. Fig. 1: the caption colours superconductor blue and semiconductor green. Please state explicitly which regions have been mesa-etched and which still contain the 2DEG (see item 3), and show where the gate lies relative to the contacts for different W_sc. If the gate overlaps different fractions of the contact or bare-2DEG regions in different devices, the gate lever arm differs between them.

13. The layer order "(In,Ga)As/InAs/(In,Ga)As (4 nm/4 nm/10 nm)" in Section II should be stated as top-to-bottom or bottom-to-top to match Fig. 1b. The range d = 10–17 nm should be tied explicitly to these layers.

14. Section III: the expression for ξ_ABS should give the phase at the critical current that is assumed. The sentence about reflection "probability" being proportional to the "amplitude" should be corrected or justified.

15. The antisymmetry I_c+(B_∥) = −I_c−(−B_∥) is stated qualitatively. Please quantify how large the deviations are, as a consistency check on field offsets (related to item 5).

16. The Al thickness of 10 nm is inferred from the parallel critical field via Ref. 37. If an MBE growth-calibrated thickness exists, please give it, since d_sc enters the q_M estimate directly.

17. The abstract says B_η shows a linear trend that "saturates" at large W_sc. Given item 1, this should be worded more cautiously.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add contact widths between 1.2 µm and about 3 µm, and several devices per width, with measured W_sc and R_n. This would turn the saturation claims for both I_c and B_η into tests rather than single-point observations.
2. Carry out a minimal BdG or tight-binding simulation of the actual geometry: finite W_sc, the 2DEG or etched region behind the contacts, Rashba SOC, Zeeman and the vector potential across the Al–2DEG separation. Use it to predict I_c(W_sc), η(W_sc), B_η(W_sc) and B*(V_g) on the same axes as the data. This is the most direct way to back the mechanistic statements in the Conclusion.
3. Use temperature dependence as an internal check. ξ(T) should follow Δ(T), and the various characteristic fields should respond differently with temperature depending on whether they come from orbital or from Zeeman/SOC effects.
4. Report field-angle dependence (B_∥ ⊥ I versus B_∥ ∥ I) and gate-dependent R_n and density for the same devices. This would give the null control and the parameters Eq. 2 needs, so the SOC interpretation of B*(V_g) can be quantitative.
