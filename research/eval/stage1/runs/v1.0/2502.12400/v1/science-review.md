A. Must address

1. The orbital interpretation of B_η conflicts with the paper's own estimates, and Fig. 4c does not test it. Section IV, the abstract and the Conclusion say the W_sc dependence of B_η "invites an orbital interpretation", based on the flux relation B_∥ W_sc d = Φ (Eq. 3) and the fit in Fig. 4c. The fit does not test this. Eq. 3 gives B_η = Φ/(W_sc d), a line through the origin in W_sc⁻¹, but the fitted line has a free, clearly nonzero intercept of about 25 mT at W_sc⁻¹ = 0. Φ and d are both free, so the fit fixes only their product, and the quoted Φ/Φ0 ≈ 0.36–0.61 follows entirely from the assumed d = 10–17 nm. No model is given that predicts the flux at which η should peak; an orbital mechanism with Φ0-periodic structure would predict a specific Φ, near Φ0/2 or tied to a first sign change at Φ0. The orbital picture's own signatures are absent. The text concedes that η sign changes at Φ = Φ0 and Φ0-periodic oscillations are not seen. The sign changes appear at 400–600 mT for all devices, whereas for W_sc = 0.9 µm and d = 15 nm Φ0 corresponds to about 150 mT, roughly a factor of 3 lower. The sign-change fields are not reported against W_sc, and attributing the missing oscillations to Zeeman collapse of the induced gap is untested. The paper's quantitative orbital estimate also contradicts the data. In Section IV, q_orb = (e/ħ)B_∥d does not depend on W_sc, and with Eq. 5 it predicts B^max ≈ 4 mT. That should apply to the W_sc → ∞ device too, yet the measured B_η is about 90–270 mT, 25–70 times larger. The Meissner (13 mT) and Rashba–Zeeman (690 mT) estimates also miss the data and its W_sc scaling. The sentence that "the above estimates … are a particularly good match" to Fig. 4c is either a typo (missing "not") or wrong, and must be fixed because it is the section's only quantitative link between model and data. The Section IV statement that "q_orb > q_M > q_RZ … suggesting orbital effects are strong" sits uneasily with these mismatches. The 1/W_sc scaling does not single out the orbital mechanism either: Meissner screening and vortex entry in the Al strip cross-section (d_sc × W_sc) also give field scales that go as 1/W_sc for an in-plane field along the strip. Finally, the claimed saturation for W_sc ≳ 2ξ rests on the single W_sc → ∞ point, which is excluded from the fit and placed at W_sc⁻¹ = 0 without its real width. There are no data between 1.2 µm (which sits at 1/(2ξ)) and "∞", so the onset of saturation is undetermined and the dashed (2ξ)⁻¹ line is untested. The authors should: fit B_η with and without a free intercept and report parameter uncertainties; plot the η sign-change field against 1/W_sc for every device, which under the flux picture should scale like B_η with a fixed ratio; plot the "∞" point at the real contact width; reconcile the flux picture (Eq. 3) with the finite-momentum picture (Eq. 5). They should then either give a model (for example a flux-threaded SNS/interferometer picture or a BdG simulation with finite-width contacts) that predicts B_η(W_sc), including prefactor and intercept, and compare it quantitatively with Fig. 4c, or present the trend as empirical ("B_η decreases with W_sc") and soften the orbital attribution and the word "saturates" in the abstract and Conclusion. To support the mechanism and saturation claims as stated, they would need devices with W_sc between about 1.2 and 5 µm and a control that separates orbital from Meissner and vortex mechanisms, such as a different Al thickness or 2DEG depth, or rotating the in-plane field toward the current (see concern 10).

2. With one device per contact width, unmeasured W_sc and no per-device normalization, the cross-device trends are not separated from device-to-device variation. Every conclusion in Figs. 2–4 is a trend across five junctions, one per W_sc, so it assumes that the 4–20% spread usual in nominally identical planar Al/InAs junctions is negligible. That spread comes from interface transparency, local 2DEG density and disorder, etch quality and the effective junction length. The W_sc values and the 80 nm separation are nominal: they were checked by SEM on "nominally identical proxy devices", not on the measured junctions (Section II). Al wet etching typically undercuts by tens of nm, which matters for the 80 nm gap and is a large fraction of 1/W_sc for the 0.3 µm device. No normal-state resistance R_n, I_cR_n product, excess current or transparency estimate is reported for any device, although τ enters Eq. 2 and ξ_ABS directly. The consequences are visible: Fig. 4b is non-monotonic (about 10% at 0.9 µm, about 8.5% at 1.2 µm), which is what scatter would look like, and the fitted ξ is sensitive because I_c^∞ is fixed by a single device (a ±5% change in I_c^∞ shifts 1/ln(I_c^∞/(I_c^∞ − I_c)) for the 1.2 µm device by roughly ±15–20%). The W_sc → ∞ device, which carries much of the I_c fit and the η trend, is never defined: its actual dimensions, whether it is unetched Al, whether it has the same lead and gate layout, and whether it is T-shaped at all. Figs. 2b, 2c, 4b and 4c have no horizontal error bars. It is also not stated whether all devices were measured in the same cooldown; T_c is quoted from "multiple cooldowns", but the device data are not tied to specific cooldowns. The authors should tabulate measured W_sc and junction separation (SEM after measurement), R_n, I_cR_n and I_exc (or a transparency estimate) for every device and gate voltage; show I_c(W_sc) normalized by 1/R_n; define the W_sc → ∞ device; add horizontal error bars from the W_sc uncertainty; state the cooldown history and whether key quantities survive thermal cycling; and ideally add a second device for several widths (for example 0.3 and 1.2 µm), or a second chip, so the spread in I_c, max|η|, B* and B_η can be compared with the size of the claimed trends.

3. Identifying the fitted 586 nm with the induced coherence length rests on an undeveloped model (Eq. 1) and assumptions the paper does not justify (Section III, Fig. 2b). Eq. 1 is motivated heuristically, by a reflection "probability" proportional to the wavefunction "amplitude" ψ ∝ exp(−W_sc/ξ), not by a calculation, and existing finite-contact-width theory (Refs. 31–33) is not compared quantitatively. A probability would normally go as |ψ|² ∝ exp(−2W_sc/ξ), which would double the extracted length to about 1.2 µm and remove the quoted agreement. The fit determines ξ_ABS = ξ/(√τ |sin(φ/2)|), which reduces to ξ only if τ → 1 and φ_c → π; yet Sec. III and Eq. 2 invoke τ < 1 and a gate-dependent τ, and for τ < 1 the critical phase is below π. The measured I_c ≈ 5 µA for a 4 µm wide junction at n = 6 × 10¹² cm⁻² is only about 10% of the ballistic estimate (k_F W/π)(eΔ/ħ) ≈ 45 µA, pointing to moderate transparency or a reduced induced gap; then ξ_ABS > ξ and the agreement with 570 nm would be accidental. The dirty-limit comparison also depends on choices outside the fit: Δ = 1.764 k_B T_c is the BCS gap of the Al film, though the text calls it the "induced gap"; n, v_F and ℓ = 130 nm come from bare-2DEG magnetotransport, not the Al-covered proximitized region; and order-unity prefactors are dropped (with D = v_F ℓ/2, ξ = √(ħD/Δ) gives about 1.25 times the quoted value). The 3% agreement is therefore well inside the uncertainty of the estimate. A 4 µm wide junction has many transverse modes with different longitudinal velocities, hence a distribution of decay lengths, not one. Fig. 2b is effectively a one-parameter fit on four points, with every point depending on the single measured I_c^∞; the W_sc → ∞ point at the origin is zero by construction and is not a data point, and no uncertainty or residuals are given for ξ. I_c is also evaluated at the field maximum B*, where finite-momentum and diode effects already act, rather than at B_∥ = 0. The authors should: justify Eq. 1, for example from a BdG/tight-binding calculation of a junction with finite-width proximitized contacts, showing which length enters, and correct or justify the probability/amplitude sentence; state the assumed phase at I_c and the τ appropriate for these junctions, estimating τ from I_cR_n or the excess current, or drop the identification ξ_ABS ≈ ξ; distinguish the Al gap from the induced gap or measure the latter; mark the origin point in Fig. 2b as constructed, treat I_c^∞ as a free parameter or propagate its uncertainty, and quote ξ with uncertainty, residuals and its correlation with I_c^∞; repeat the analysis with I_c(B_∥ = 0); present the 570 nm comparison as order-of-magnitude consistency; and test the interpretation by fitting ξ at the other gate voltages (data in S.IV already exist) and at other temperatures. Under the coherence-length picture ξ should be largely gate-independent if the gate is screened under the Al, or track √(v_F ℓ/Δ) as density changes, and should grow as Δ(T) shrinks.

B. Should address

4. The gate dependence of B* is attributed to spin–orbit coupling without measuring the quantities Eq. 2 depends on (Section III, Figs. 2c and 3). The paper asserts that the Rashba α increases and τ decreases with V_g. The second is counterintuitive, since higher density usually raises transparency in these heterostructures, and neither is measured: α(V_g) is taken from Ref. 45 without using its numbers, and the right-hand axis of Fig. 3 is schematic. The gate also changes n, k_F (which appears in Eq. 2 via k_so/k_F), v_F, ℓ, g*, the induced gap, τ and E_T, and these need not cancel when the contacts and junction region respond differently. The Meissner and orbital estimates in Eq. 5 depend on v_F and Δ and so are gate-dependent too, so "gate tunability lends credence to a spin-orbit interpretation" does not exclude other mechanisms. Conversely, the weak gate dependence of B_η is also called "consistent with a spin–orbit mechanism", so both strong and weak gate dependence are taken as support. Eq. 2 is never evaluated numerically and compared in magnitude with the measured 0–40 mT values or with the factor of about 2–6 change in Fig. 3. Eq. 2 was derived for W_sc → ∞, yet B* is plotted against W_sc in Fig. 2c, and the clear W_sc dependence there, including the upturn for W_sc = 0.3 µm, is called "nontrivial" and left unexplained. The authors should measure or cite with numbers n(V_g), α(V_g) and ℓ(V_g) (Hall and weak-antilocalization measurements on a co-fabricated Hall bar) and τ(V_g) from R_n, I_cR_n, the excess current or multiple Andreev reflection; plot R_n, I_c and τ against V_g for each device; evaluate Eq. 2 quantitatively and show that B* scales with k_so/k_F as predicted, rather than merely increasing; and state what the W_sc dependence of B* is meant to show and what sets B* in finite-width contacts.

5. The extraction of B* and the calibration of the field zero are not described, although B* is only a few mT (Figs. 2c and 3). B* lies between about −5 and 40 mT and is obtained from "low-field fits to Eq. (1) of Ref. 8", but the fitted function, the free and fixed parameters, the field step, the fit window and the origin of the error bars are not given. Because I_c changes over hundreds of mT, the result may depend on the window. A remanent field in the in-plane magnet, trapped flux, or magnet hysteresis of a few mT would shift argmax I_c+ and argmin I_c− in opposite directions, and is comparable to the W_sc differences being interpreted. The negative B* values for several V_g < 0 points (for example about −5 mT for 1.2 µm) cannot come from Eq. 2 and reverse the sign of the effect, which suggests such an offset or a misalignment-induced out-of-plane component. The paper does not say how the zero of B_∥ was calibrated, or whether the antisymmetry I_c+(B_∥) = −I_c−(−B_∥) stated for Fig. 2a was observed independently or imposed by symmetrization; it is stated only qualitatively. The authors should write out the fit function and procedure in the main text and show that B* is stable when the fit window is changed; state whether B* is the average of argmax I_c+ and −argmin I_c− (which would cancel an offset) or comes from one branch, and show the two branches separately; document the field-offset calibration, sweep direction and field-sweep hysteresis (vortices trapped in the wider Al strips are a W_sc-dependent possibility); quantify the deviations from antisymmetry; and comment on the negative values and whether they give a lower bound on the B* uncertainty.

6. The out-of-plane field compensation could itself create or remove a W_sc-dependent diode signal (Section II, Section S.III). I_c+ and I_c− are taken as the maximum and minimum of the central Fraunhofer lobe, each at its own out-of-plane field and within a narrow B_z window. Selecting extrema separately can change the apparent asymmetry; a relative B_z shift between the I_c+ and I_c− lobes is itself a diode signature, from inhomogeneous current or self-field effects, so the procedure may also remove a genuine contribution. Flux focusing by the superconducting contacts makes the effective area for B_z, the Fraunhofer period and the coupling to any misalignment grow with W_sc, so residual out-of-plane flux affects wider-contact devices more strongly. The authors should state the misalignment angle and compensation procedure in the main text; show for each device representative raw Fraunhofer maps at several B_∥ (at least one at large B_∥), the B_z offset of the I_c± maxima against B_∥, and the Fraunhofer period against W_sc; and show that η, B* and B_η do not change when the B_z window or its centre is varied.

7. Alternative explanations for the W_sc dependence of I_c and η, in particular the current distribution along the narrow contact strips, are not considered. In the T-geometry, current enters each contact strip from a lead (Fig. 1a) and must spread along the 4 µm junction width through a 10 nm Al strip only 0.3–1.2 µm wide. The strip's kinetic inductance and resistance can produce a phase gradient along the junction: a rough estimate of 8 pH per square gives about 100 pH over 4 µm for W_sc = 0.3 µm, comparable to the Josephson inductance Φ0/2πI_c ≈ 66 pH at 5 µA and corresponding to a phase drop of order 1 rad. This scales as 1/W_sc and grows as the gap is suppressed by B_∥, so it could both reduce I_c and add a geometric, current-direction-dependent asymmetry, explaining the I_c(W_sc) and η(W_sc) trends without invoking the coherence length. The authors acknowledge kinetic inductance only in passing (Section IV and the Conclusion). Gap suppression or inverse proximity in narrow Al strips and current crowding where the strips meet the leads would act similarly. The authors should estimate the phase drop along the strip from the sheet kinetic inductance (or sheet resistance) of their Al at the relevant fields, or simulate the current distribution, show it is negligible for W_sc = 0.3 µm, and discuss the other alternatives quantitatively.

8. The device geometry behind and around the contacts is unclear (Section II, Fig. 1a). From the false-coloured SEM it is not clear what lies beyond the rear edge of each Al strip: etched mesa, bare 2DEG or wider Al. Fig. 1a appears to show semiconductor beyond the strips. If the 2DEG continues behind the contacts, the rear boundary is an S/N interface into a normal reservoir rather than the reflecting S/vacuum boundary Eq. 1 assumes; the suppression of I_c would then reflect quasiparticle leakage, a parallel normal path or lateral and inverse proximity, the decay length need not be the induced coherence length, and the η analysis would be complicated as well. It is also unclear how current reaches the strips (along their full length or from one end), which sets the phase gradient in concern 7. The authors should state the mesa layout behind the contacts and the current path, and describe the geometry that determines the boundary condition behind Eq. 1.

10. A null control with the in-plane field along the current is missing. For the Rashba–Zeeman, Meissner and flux (Eq. 3) mechanisms alike, the diode effect should vanish or be strongly suppressed when B_∥ is parallel to I, while extrinsic contributions (B_z leakage, self-field effects, current crowding in the T-geometry) need not vanish in the same way. The flux through W_sc·d and the Rashba–Zeeman finite-momentum term also have distinct angular dependences, so rotation would help separate orbital from spin–orbit contributions. The authors should measure η and I_c±(B_∥) for at least two field angles, from perpendicular to parallel to the current, for at least the narrowest and widest devices, to show that the W_sc trends in Fig. 4 come from the intended mechanisms.
