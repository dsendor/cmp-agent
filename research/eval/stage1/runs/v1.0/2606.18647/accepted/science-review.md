The main text says the negative Rxy in the (X + X-) region "unambiguously demonstrates the presence of Coulomb drag and the trion Hall effect". But equation (7) gives Rxy ≈ -(B/e)[(1+f)/n_e + f^2(1 - σX0/σe0)/n_T]. With no mobile trions (σT0 → 0, so f → 0) this reduces to Rxy ≈ -B/(n_e e), still negative, and Rxy,drag ≈ -B/(n_e e) is unchanged. Likewise equation (8) with f = 0 still gives Rxy,drag ≈ B/(n_h e) in the (X + X+) region. Physically, perfectly dragged neutral excitons carry the hole-layer current, and with the Mo layer open the free electrons must counterflow; the transverse field from the free electrons is passed to the hole layer through the excitons. That alone produces an electron-like Hall voltage on the hole layer, with no bound trions. Trions enter only through f and the f^2/n_T term. The negative sign therefore shows perfect exciton or trion drag plus free electrons, not trions as such, and the trion assignment currently rests on the phase-boundary geometry (Rxx metal-insulator boundaries, p = 2n and n = 2p lines) and the RC valley argument, not on the Hall data. 3. The conductivity tensors in equations (2) and (3) need derivation, because as written they appear inconsistent with the net charge of the trion and with Onsager reciprocity. In Σ^- the trion enters as J_e = 2σT(E_e - E_h) and J_h = -σT(E_e - E_h), so the trion contribution depends only on E_e - E_h and vanishes for E_e = E_h, the response of a neutral exciton.
