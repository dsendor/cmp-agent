Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors study dual-gated MoSe2/WSe2 electron–hole double layers in a perpendicular magnetic field. They identify the regions of the Vb–Vg phase diagram where excitons and trions coexist, (X + X+) and (X + X−), using Rxx, Coulomb drag and valley-resolved reflection contrast. They then report a Hall response that they attribute to charged trions. The key observation is an electron-like (negative) Hall resistance on the hole layer, with the electron layer open, inside the negative-trion region. It disappears on heating above roughly 8 K and at high doping. Equilibrium trion fluids in these heterostructures were reported recently (refs 38, 39). Showing that trions act as mobile charge carriers that respond to the Lorentz force would be a natural and interesting next step, and a step towards trion quantum Hall physics. How convincing the claim is depends on how well the sign-reversed Hall signal is shown to be intrinsic and unique to trions, and how quantitatively the drag model is tested against the data.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The device platform is mature. It has separate contacts to each layer, exciton contact regions and Bi/Pt contacts. Single-layer transport, drag transport and valley-resolved optics on the same type of structure give three independent ways to locate the trion regions.
- The valley-resolved RC analysis (Fig. 3, Extended Data Figs. 5, 6 and 8) is a strong consistency check. Positive trions appear only once both K and K′ hole valleys are occupied (region III), as expected for a spin-singlet trion. The trion regions disappear at 10 K and at 9 T.
- The headline observation is qualitatively striking and clean in the colour maps (Fig. 4a–d). A negative Rxy on a hole-doped layer, confined to the (X + X−) region and disappearing with temperature, is hard to obtain from single-layer physics.
- The four-by-four conductivity-tensor model (equations (1)–(8)) is simple and transparent. It makes sign predictions that the data follow.
- The magnetic-field evolution of the phase diagram (Extended Data Figs. 4 and 8) adds a further dependence beyond temperature, consistent with Zeeman destabilization of singlet trions.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The paper does not show that the Hall signals were made antisymmetric in field, or checked for Rxx admixture and Onsager reciprocity. This is the central measurement. In the trion regions, Rxx and Rxx,drag are 10^4–10^6 Ω (Figs 2a, 3a; Extended Data Fig. 4). The Hall resistances discussed are a few to ~10 kΩ (Fig. 4a–e). Even a small misalignment of the Hall probes, or density inhomogeneity between the probes, would mix a longitudinal component into Rxy of the same size as the claimed signal. That longitudinal component also depends strongly on temperature and gate voltage, because Rxx is insulating-like in the trion regions (Fig. 2b,c). The sign reversal of Rxy below ~4 K (Fig. 4e) and the sign changes near the grey regions (Fig. 4a,b) could arise this way. The Methods (Electrical measurements) do not say whether Rxy and Rxy,drag were antisymmetrized under B → −B. They also do not say whether the out-of-phase lock-in component was negligible, which matters at 7.33 Hz with source impedances near the 100-MΩ preamplifier input.
   - Show raw ±B data and the antisymmetrized Rxy and Rxy,drag at representative points in both trion regions, at 1.5 K and 10 K.
   - Show the quadrature signal.
   - Ideally, check the Onsager reciprocity of the drag Hall signal: drive the W layer and measure the Hall voltage on the Mo layer, and compare with Rxy,drag at reversed field.
   - Without these checks, the negative Hall resistance, the quantity the paper's title rests on, is not established as a genuine Hall response.

2. The paper does not rule out explanations of the negative Hall resistance that need no mobile trions. The main text says that "the negative Hall resistance unambiguously demonstrates the presence of Coulomb drag and the trion Hall effect." In the model, however, a negative Rxy on the hole layer arises because current in the electron layer is balanced by counterflow of free electrons while the hole layer is dragged. Two alternatives need to be excluded:
   - (i) Frictional (non-bound) Coulomb drag between free electrons and the hole layer. At d ≈ 2 nm and low density this can be strong, and it is known to produce Hall drag. The model sets it to zero by assumption.
   - (ii) Excitons with perfect drag plus free electrons, without trions. Here σT = 0 but σX0 ≠ 0.

   Using equations (2), (4) and (7), the authors should show explicitly what Rxy and Rxy,drag each scenario predicts. In particular, does an exciton-plus-free-electron fluid with perfect exciton drag give a negative Rxy on an open-circuited configuration? If it does, the sign alone does not single out trions, and the evidence reduces to the coincidence of the sign-reversed region with the (X + X−) boundaries from Rxx and RC. The argument would then need to rest on that coincidence and be stated accordingly.

   The model's assumption of perfect drag for excitons and trions also needs to be reconciled with Extended Data Fig. 3b. There the measured drag ratio inside the trion regions is only ~0.2–0.3, against ~1 in the X region. The authors should state whether the model, with reasonable σ values, reproduces this drag ratio.

3. The key result is shown in only one device. All Hall data in Fig. 4 and Extended Data Fig. 4 come from device 3. Device 1 provides the Rxx phase diagram and oscillations (Fig. 2; Extended Data Fig. 7), and device 2 the drag and optics (Fig. 3; Extended Data Figs. 5, 6 and 8). The main text says the crossover temperature "is of the same order of magnitude as in other devices studied in this work (Methods provides discussions on Rxy,drag)". The Methods contain no such discussion, and no Rxy or Rxy,drag data from a second device are shown. For a headline claim resting on a sign-reversed Hall signal, the authors should show:
   - Rxy and Rxy,drag maps, or at least line cuts and T-dependences, in both trion regions for at least one more device;
   - the device-to-device spread of the crossover temperature.

B. Should address

4. The comparison between model and data is qualitative only. Equations (7) and (8) contain n_e, n_h, n_T, f and the conductivity ratios, but none is extracted from or fitted to the data. Fig. 4f shows the Hall density alongside the calibrated total densities, but no model curve. The authors should:
   - use the gate-calibrated densities and the constraint n_total = n_free + 2n_T + n_X (or equivalent) to fit Rxy(Vg) and Rxy,drag(Vg) at 1.5 K in both regions with a minimal parameter set;
   - report the extracted n_T and f, and state which parameters are free.

   The model also omits free holes in the (X + X−) region. Yet Rxy there is positive at high temperature and changes sign only below ~4 K (Fig. 4e). Including thermally activated free holes would let the authors explain why Rxy changes sign near 4 K while Rxy,drag already departs from zero near 8 K. It would also give a more meaningful binding-energy estimate than reading off a crossover temperature.

5. Interlayer leakage and AC coupling are not characterized. The devices operate at Vb ≈ 0.76–0.88 V across a 1.5–2 nm hBN barrier, where tunnelling is not negligible. Tunnelling current between the layers would appear directly as a spurious drag signal and could alter the Hall voltages on the open-circuited layer. The voltage-transformer scheme (Extended Data Fig. 2b) reduces AC coupling between the layers, but the residual coupling is not quantified.
   - Report the DC interlayer leakage current as a function of Vb and Vg over the trion regions.
   - Report the residual AC interlayer signal, for example from a drag measurement at a point where the layers should be decoupled (high T, or outside the bound-state regions).

6. The Hall density is extracted in the quantizing regime. Fig. 4f uses n_H = B/(eRxy) at 4 T. The paper states the small-field approximation Rxy ≈ B/(n_h e) for the hole layer. At 4 T and 1.5 K, however, the hole cyclotron energy (~1 meV) is comparable to or larger than kT, and quantum oscillations are strong in the same regions (Fig. 2a; Extended Data Figs. 3, 4 and 7). The authors should show Rxy versus B at fixed (Vg, Vb) in both trion regions, down to fields well below 1 T, to establish linearity and the sign at low field. Extended Data Fig. 4e is a start, but it begins at 0 T with the trion region opening only above ~2 T. The authors should discuss how Landau quantization affects the interpretation of n_H and of equations (7) and (8).

7. The trion binding-energy estimates are inconsistent and their uncertainties are not stated. The optical estimate (Methods) gives E_T,b ≈ 1.2 meV, from device 2 at 6 T. The transport crossover gives ~0.7 meV (8 K), from device 3 at 4 T. The paper itself says the binding energy depends on B and density, so the "good agreement" is not a like-for-like comparison. In addition:
   - The optical estimate depends on d (quoted as 1.5–2 nm). Through C_2L this changes E_T,b by about 30%.
   - The estimate uses m_h = 0.45 m0 and g_s g_v = 2. Near the injection threshold into the minority valley at 6 T, the relevant compressibility may be that of a single valley.
   - Setting kT_crossover ≈ E_b underestimates the binding energy in a 2D Saha-type dissociation, where ionization happens at kT well below E_b.

   The authors should give error bars, compare at matched B in the same device where possible, and soften the agreement claim.

8. The quantum-oscillation analysis behind "not trion Landau levels" is fragile (Extended Data Fig. 7; main text conclusion). The normalized amplitude is defined relative to Rxx at 3.3 K for the (X + X+) cut. That forces the amplitude to zero at 3.3 K by construction and steepens the apparent decay. The background Rxx in this region is also strongly T-dependent through trion dissociation (Fig. 2c), so the Lifshitz–Kosevich form does not obviously apply. The fit uses only ~1.5–3 K. All of these bias the extracted 0.65 m_e. The conclusion that the oscillations are not from trion Landau levels may well be right, but it should not rest on this mass alone. Specifically:
   - Extract the amplitude after subtracting a smooth background at each temperature.
   - Fit over a range not pinned to the reference temperature.
   - Give an error bar on the mass.
   - Support the alternative interpretation (recurring transitions between hole quantum Hall states and the trion phase) with the B dependence of the oscillation positions, for example their slope in the Vb–Vg plane against the expected Landau-level filling of the hole layer.

9. Different devices and conditions are compared across techniques. The RC identification of trions (device 2, Δ = 11 V, 6 T) is said to be "fully consistent" with transport, but transport comes from device 3 (Δ = 9 V, 4 T) and device 1 (Δ = 13 V). The overlay in Extended Data Figs. 5 and 6 uses region boundaries "identified from the Rxx measurements". It is unclear whether these boundaries come from device 2 itself. Optical illumination of up to 50 nW µm⁻² on a highly insulating excitonic state can also photodope it or heat it locally, which shifts the boundaries. The authors should:
   - state which device the boundaries in Extended Data Figs. 5 and 6 come from;
   - show a power dependence of the RC boundaries;
   - ideally, show transport and optics on the same device under matched B and Δ.

10. The claimed disappearance at high doping is not shown in one dimension. The abstract says the effect disappears "at high doping densities". In Fig. 4b, however, the negative Rxy occupies only part of the (X + X−) polygon, mainly near its lower boundary. Fig. 4d is a single Vb cut. The authors should:
    - show Rxy and Rxy,drag along a cut through the (X + X−) region towards its upper (dissociation) boundary;
    - explain why the negative Hall signal does not fill the region defined from Rxx.

C. Minor

11. The hole mass is inconsistent. The quantum capacitance in the Methods uses m_h ≈ 0.45 m0, but the authors' own Lifshitz–Kosevich fit in the pi region gives 0.32 m_e (Extended Data Fig. 7d). Reconcile the two, or state which applies.
12. The hole cyclotron energy at 4 T is quoted as "about 0.8 meV". With m_h = 0.45 m0, ħeB/m_h ≈ 1.0 meV; with 0.32 m_e it is ≈ 1.4 meV. Please check the number and the mass used.
13. The main text refers to "Methods provides discussions on Rxy,drag" for other devices, but there is no such discussion in the Methods (see also concern 3).
14. In equation (7), Rxy,drag ≈ −B/(n_e e) does not depend on n_T, yet the drag Hall signal is said to be negligible outside the trion regions. Explain the range of validity of this approximation, and why the drag Hall magnitude in the (X + X−) region (Fig. 4e, lower panel) is much smaller than in the (X + X+) region.
15. Justify the statement that bilayer MoSe2 "functions as monolayer MoSe2 under high Δ" with data or a reference. It affects the electron mass, the valley structure and the interpretation of the Mo-layer RC (Extended Data Fig. 6).
16. Fig. 4e: label the panels by quantity and region more explicitly. Mark the 4 K sign change of Rxy and the ~8 K onset of Rxy,drag. Extended Data Fig. 4 caption: "(X + X+/−)" is rendered inconsistently.
17. Fig. 3b: the region labels (i)–(iv) and their valley occupancies are hard to read. Make them consistent with the I–IV notation of Extended Data Fig. 6b.
18. Soften "unambiguously demonstrates" (main text, negative-trion paragraph) unless concern 2 is resolved.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one figure from a second device with antisymmetrized Rxy and Rxy,drag line cuts and temperature dependences through both trion regions. Include a reciprocity check of the drag Hall signal.
2. Fit the conductivity-tensor model, extended to include thermally activated free carriers, quantitatively to Fig. 4f and Fig. 4e. Show alongside it the predictions of the exciton-plus-free-electron and frictional-drag scenarios, so that readers can see why only trions reproduce the data.
3. Show the low-field Rxy(B) in the (X + X−) region to separate the classical trion Hall response from Landau-quantization effects.
4. Give uncertainties on the trion binding energy, from both the optical threshold and the transport crossover, measured on the same device and at the same field. Use this to sharpen the outlook on what binding energy is needed for trion quantum Hall states.
