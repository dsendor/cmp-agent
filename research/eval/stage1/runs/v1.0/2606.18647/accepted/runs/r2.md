Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports magnetotransport and Coulomb drag in dual-gated MoSe2/WSe2 electron-hole double layers. Under a perpendicular field it identifies (X + X+) and (X + X-) regions next to the excitonic insulator, and in these regions it finds large Hall drag resistances whose sign follows the trion charge. In the (X + X-) region the hole layer shows an electron-like (negative) Hall resistance, which the authors call the trion Hall effect. If trions can be shown to respond to the Lorentz force as mobile charged composites, the result extends the recent zero-field reports of equilibrium trion fluids (refs 38, 39) and points toward trion quantum Hall physics. As presented, the transport signatures do not yet separate trion physics from a simpler mixture of excitons and free carriers, and that weakens the headline claim.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- It is the first transport study of charged interlayer composites in a magnetic field. The sign reversal of R_xy,drag between the positive-trion and negative-trion regions (Fig. 4a), and the negative R_xy on the hole layer in the (X + X-) region (Fig. 4b), are striking and qualitatively new observations.
- Several methods give a consistent picture of the phase diagram: R_xx, R_xx,drag and its temperature dependence (Fig. 2, Fig. 3a), the drag ratio (Extended Data Fig. 3), and valley-resolved reflection contrast (RC) that ties the positive-trion region to occupation of both K and K' hole valleys (Fig. 3b, Extended Data Figs. 5, 6, 8). The spin-singlet picture is consistent with the regions shrinking at high field (Extended Data Figs. 4, 8).
- The four-by-four conductivity-tensor model in Methods (equations 1-8) is a useful framework that others can apply.
- The Methods estimate of the trion binding energy from the injection-threshold shift (about 1.2 meV) checks out numerically (C_Q ≈ 30 µF cm^-2, C_2L ≈ 1.55 µF cm^-2). It is of the same order as the crossover temperature scale.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The Hall signals do not by themselves distinguish trions from a mixture of excitons and free carriers. The authors' own model shows this. In equation (7), setting the trion conductivity σ_T0 = 0 (f = 0, no mobile trions) still gives R_xy ≈ -B/(n_e e) and R_xy,drag ≈ -B/(n_e e) in the (X + X-) region. In equation (8), f = 0 still gives R_xy,drag ≈ B/(n_h e) in the (X + X+) region. Physically, perfectly dragged excitons plus free electrons in an open-circuit electron layer already make the hole layer's Hall voltage electron-like: the transverse field from the free electrons' Hall response is passed to the hole layer through the excitons. So the sign of the hole-layer Hall response, which the main text says "unambiguously demonstrates the presence of Coulomb drag and the trion Hall effect", follows from exciton drag plus excess carriers. Trions enter only through f and the f²/n_T term.
   - Why it matters: the title claim is that trions, as charged bound composites, show a Hall effect. The evidence for bound trions currently comes from the insulating R_xx and from RC, not from the Hall data.
   - What to do: fit the gate and temperature dependence of n_H in Fig. 4f, or of R_xy and R_xy,drag along the V_b = 0.88 V cut, using equations (7) and (8) with calibrated n_e, n_h and n_T. Show where the trion term is needed and the f = 0 limit fails. Alternatively, identify an observable the two scenarios predict differently. Candidates are the magnitude of n_H relative to n − 2p, the behaviour as the n = 2p boundary is approached (where n_e → 0), and the relation between R_xy and R_xy,drag. Without this, the text should say that the data show a Hall response consistent with mobile trions, not a demonstration of one.

2. The Hall resistances are not shown to be antisymmetrized in field, while R_xx in the same regions is 10^5-10^7 Ω. Figure 4 and Extended Data Fig. 4 show R_xy and R_xy,drag only at positive B: 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 T, and 0-12 T in Extended Data Fig. 4e. Hall resistances of order 10 kΩ next to longitudinal resistances that are 10-1000 times larger can be dominated by contact misalignment or inhomogeneous current paths. A small R_xx admixture changes sign with the gradient of R_xx and could mimic a sign change near phase boundaries; the blue fringes along the lower boundary in Fig. 4a,b and Extended Data Fig. 4 look like this. Concern 1 makes this more pressing, because the claim rests on sign.
   - What to do: show that R_xy(B) = −R_xy(−B) and R_xy,drag(B) = −R_xy,drag(−B) for the Fig. 4c-e data, report the symmetric parts, and state whether the maps are antisymmetrized.
   - Also show R_xy,drag versus B in both regions (it is missing from Extended Data Fig. 4e). It should be linear at small B, where the paper's Drude-like analysis applies.

3. The assumption of perfect Coulomb drag for trions has no direct support and appears to conflict with the data. Methods (after equation 2) assumes perfect Coulomb drag for excitons and trions, negligible frictional drag of free carriers, and neglects free electrons in the (X + X+) region and free holes in the (X + X-) region. Extended Data Fig. 3b shows a drag ratio near 1 only in the X region; inside the trion regions it is about 0.2-0.4. Part of this deficit may come from free carriers carrying current. But the paper does not show that the measured drag ratio agrees with the conductivity fractions implied by its model, and it does not bound the frictional-drag contribution of free carriers near the trion dissociation boundary.
   - Why it matters: frictional Hall drag between free electrons and free holes also produces Hall drag signals, and these are known to have their own temperature dependence.
   - What to do: compare the measured drag ratio in both trion regions with the model prediction. Then assess how relaxing the perfect-drag and no-friction assumptions changes the sign and magnitude of R_xy,drag.

B. Should address

4. The Hall data come from one device, and the phase-diagram identification is spread across three devices with different Δ.
   - The R_xx identification is in device 1 (Δ = 13 V, V_b ≈ 0.76 V; Fig. 2), RC in device 2 (Δ = 11 V; Fig. 3, Extended Data Figs. 5, 6, 8), and the Hall effect in device 3 (Δ = 9 V; Fig. 4, Extended Data Figs. 3, 4).
   - The (X + X±) boundaries used to interpret RC are taken from transport maps, presumably of device 2, and the crossover temperature is said to be of the same order "in other devices studied in this work", but those data are not shown.
   - What to do: show R_xy and R_xy,drag maps and the Fig. 4e temperature dependence for at least one more device. State how many devices showed the negative hole-layer Hall resistance. Ideally show RC and Hall data on the same device.

5. Interlayer leakage and capacitive pickup are not quantified.
   - The barrier in the channel is 1.5-2 nm of hBN, with V_b ≈ 0.76-0.9 V across it.
   - Drag measurements are prone to spurious signals from interlayer tunnelling currents and from a.c. coupling between layers. The latter is why the potentiometer and transformer scheme of Extended Data Fig. 2b is used.
   - What to do: report the d.c. interlayer leakage current over the V_b range of Figs. 2-4, the out-of-phase lock-in components, and the dependence of R_xy,drag on excitation amplitude and frequency. This shows that the drag signal is linear, in phase, and not set by tunnelling. Linearity also bears on heating of the highly resistive trion regions.

6. The small-field, semiclassical model is applied where Landau quantization is clearly important.
   - Equations (6)-(8) linearize in ω_c τ ("under a small perpendicular magnetic field B"). Yet the analysis is at 4 T, where the paper says the free-hole cyclotron energy (about 0.8 meV) is comparable to the trion binding energy (about 0.7 meV), and where quantum oscillations are visible in the (X + X+) region (Extended Data Figs. 3b, 7).
   - What to do: give the hole and electron mobilities, or ω_c τ, at the measurement field. Discuss whether R_xy ≈ B/(n_h e) and the n_H analysis of Fig. 4f can be used quantitatively. Data at lower field (for example 2-3 T, where the trion regions already exist; Extended Data Fig. 4a,b) would strengthen the analysis.

7. The binding-energy estimates and the crossover temperatures need reconciling.
   - The main text takes 8 K (≈0.7 meV) from Fig. 4c as the trion binding energy. Methods gives about 1.2 meV from the RC injection threshold at 6 T in device 2.
   - In Fig. 4e, R_xy,drag in the (X + X-) region turns negative below about 6-7 K, while R_xy turns negative only below about 4 K. The paper notes this without explaining it.
   - The RC estimate depends on the interlayer separation, quoted as "d ≈ 2 nm" in Methods and "1.5-2 nm" in the main text; on m_h; and on comparing 1.5 K with 10 K data.
   - What to do: give uncertainties. Explain why a crossover temperature k_B T* should equal the binding energy rather than a fraction of it, given the entropy of dissociation. Explain the different onset temperatures of R_xy and R_xy,drag within the model.

8. The way phase boundaries are drawn is not stated. The (X + X±) triangles are traced by eye from a metal-insulator transition in R_xx (blue dashed lines in Figs. 2a, 4a,b, Extended Data Fig. 4). These boundaries are then used to label regions in the RC maps (Extended Data Figs. 5, 6) and to define the shaded regions in Fig. 4f.
   - What to do: state the criterion, for example the sign of dR_xx/dT between which temperatures, and show the boundaries with uncertainty.
   - The negative-trion region is notably less well defined in the RC data: the σ− X_Mo,a map in Extended Data Fig. 6c shows only a faint feature. Quantify the agreement between the RC-derived and transport-derived boundaries, rather than calling the identification "fully consistent".

9. The interpretation of the oscillations in the (X + X+) region is speculative and could be stated more cautiously.
   - The effective mass of 0.65 m_e (Extended Data Fig. 7d) is extracted from a Lifshitz-Kosevich fit over only about 1.5-3 K, with an insulating, temperature-dependent background removed by referencing to R_xx(3.3 K).
   - The explanation, recurring transitions between hole quantum Hall states and the trion phase, is plausible but not tested.
   - The "expected trion mass (≈1.4-1.6 m_e)" has no stated source or derivation.
   - What to do: give the fit uncertainty and the source of the expected mass. Show the oscillation period in density, which would test whether the period tracks the free-hole filling as the proposed scenario implies.

C. Minor

10. Fig. 4f: the dashed "total" density lines depend on the gate and interlayer capacitances. Give these values and their uncertainties in Methods, and state how n and p are separately obtained from V_g, V_b and Δ inside the trion regions.

11. The text and Fig. 3b label the RC regions differently: the figure uses (i)-(iv), the text says "region III" and "region II", and Extended Data Fig. 6 uses capital numerals. Make them consistent.

12. The Extended Data Fig. 7 caption normalizes by R_xx at T = 3.3 K for panel b, but the legend's highest temperature is 3.2 K. Panel c is referenced to 6.6 K while its legend ends at 6.5 K. Clarify.

13. Methods says bilayer MoSe2 "behaves effectively like a monolayer" because electrons sit in the layer closer to WSe2. Give evidence, for example from RC or capacitance, that the second MoSe2 layer is not populated in the density range studied, since that layer would add free electrons with weaker drag.

14. Define the sign convention for R_xy,drag explicitly (drive direction relative to the measured Hall voltage). Say whether "hole-like" and "electron-like" refer to the measured layer's carriers or to the driven layer's.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a quantitative model-versus-data figure. Plot n_H (Fig. 4f) and R_xy,drag along the V_b = 0.88 V cut against equations (7) and (8). Show both the trion-including case and the f = 0 (exciton plus free carrier) limit, so readers can see what the trion contribution adds.
2. Show field-antisymmetrized R_xy and R_xy,drag versus B (both signs, including low field) in both trion regions, plus a reproduction of Fig. 4c-e in a second device.
3. Report interlayer leakage, excitation-amplitude linearity and the out-of-phase components for the drag channel.
4. Bring the main-text wording into line with what the data establish. For example, replace "unambiguously demonstrates" with a statement that the Hall response is consistent with mobile trions plus Coulomb drag, unless the analysis in suggestion 1 excludes the alternatives.
