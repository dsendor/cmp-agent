Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors study dual-gated MoSe2/WSe2 electron-hole double layers with a thin (1.5-2 nm) hBN barrier at 1.5 K in a perpendicular magnetic field. They report that the narrow trion lines (p = 2n and n = 2p) seen at zero field widen into triangular (X + X+) and (X + X-) regions, which they identify from insulating Rxx and large Rxx,drag on the W layer and from valley-resolved reflection contrast. In the (X + X-) region, with the Mo layer open-circuited, the hole layer shows a negative (electron-like) Hall resistance, which the authors call a "trion Hall effect" mediated by Coulomb drag. It vanishes above about 8 K and outside the trion regions. A Hall response of equilibrium trions in a tunable Bose-Fermi mixture would be new and timely, following the recent equilibrium-trion and perfect-drag papers (refs. 32, 33, 38, 39). The headline interpretation, however, rests on a phenomenological four-component conductivity model whose central prediction (the sign of Rxy) does not, as written, single out trions from excitons plus free electrons.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The measurement set is broad: Rxx, Rxy, Rxx,drag and Rxy,drag maps over Vb-Vg, plus temperature (Fig. 4c-e) and magnetic-field (Extended Data Fig. 4) series, and independent valley-resolved optical identification of doping regions (Fig. 3, Extended Data Figs. 5, 6, 8).
- The negative Rxy in the hole layer is a striking, counterintuitive signal. It is confined to the region identified as (X + X-), weakens smoothly with temperature (Fig. 4d,e), and is absent at high field where the spin-singlet trions are expected to become unstable (Extended Data Fig. 4d,e). These serve as partial internal controls.
- Several pieces of evidence point the same way: the Zeeman-driven shrinking of the trion regions, the optical valley-occupancy argument that positive trions appear only when both K and K' hole valleys are occupied (Fig. 3b), and the binding-energy estimate from the RC injection threshold (Methods, about 1.2 meV).
- The experimental methods (exciton contacts, Bi/Pt contacts, transformer-based drag circuit) are described in useful detail.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The sign of Rxy in the hole layer does not, on the authors' own model, distinguish trions from excitons plus free electrons. The main text calls the negative Rxy something that "unambiguously demonstrates the presence of Coulomb drag and the trion Hall effect". But equation (7) gives Rxy ≈ -(B/e)[(1+f)/n_e + f^2(1 - σX0/σe0)/n_T]. In the limit of no trions (σT0 → 0, so f → 0) this reduces to Rxy ≈ -B/(n_e e), which is still negative. Physically, perfectly dragged neutral excitons carry the hole-layer current, and with the Mo layer open the free electrons must counterflow. That alone produces an electron-like Hall voltage on the hole layer, with no bound trions needed. The negative sign therefore shows perfect exciton or trion drag plus free electrons, not trions as such. The trion assignment currently rests on the phase-boundary geometry (Rxx metal-insulator boundaries, p = 2n and n = 2p lines) and the RC valley argument, not on the Hall effect itself. The authors should identify and test an observable that does separate the two scenarios. Examples:
   (a) a quantitative comparison of the measured Rxy and Rxy,drag with equations (7) and (8), using capacitance-derived n and p, to extract f (or n_T) as a function of Vg and show it is clearly non-zero;
   (b) in the (X + X+) region, plotting the reference curves p - n (holes bound only in excitons) and p - 2n (maximal trion formation) on Fig. 4f alongside n_H(1.5 K), since equation (8) makes n_H the free-hole density and the deficit below p - n directly counts holes bound in trions (as read from the figure, n_H(1.5 K) falls roughly linearly to zero at Vg = 0, which needs to be shown inconsistent with p - n);
   (c) the corresponding check that Rxy,drag ≈ -B/(n_e e) in the (X + X-) region matches the expected free-electron density.
   A reference measurement would also help: Rxy on the hole layer at matched densities in a device or regime where excitons form but trions do not, if one is available. Without this, the title claim is "Hall response of a drag-coupled Bose-Fermi mixture" rather than a trion Hall effect.

2. Hall signals are measured on a strongly insulating layer, and the paper does not show antisymmetrization in field. In the trion regions Rxx on the W layer is 10^4-10^6 Ω (Fig. 2a,b; Extended Data Fig. 4), while the Hall signals of interest are around 5-10 kΩ (Fig. 4a,b,e). A longitudinal admixture from a small Hall-probe misalignment, or a contact-dependent offset, would then be comparable to the signal and can take either sign. The headline result is a sign change of Rxy, so it is essential to show that Rxy and Rxy,drag are antisymmetric under B → -B. The Methods and figure captions do not say whether the data are antisymmetrized, and Extended Data Fig. 4e shows only positive B. The authors should:
   - present Rxy(B) and Rxy(-B) (and the drag equivalents) at a representative point in each trion region;
   - state the antisymmetrization procedure;
   - show the symmetric component, to show that it is small or has been removed;
   - report the lock-in phase (out-of-phase component) in the high-impedance regions, where the 100 MΩ preamplifier input and line capacitance at 7.33 Hz may matter.

3. The conductivity tensors in equations (2) and (3) need derivation, because as written they appear inconsistent with the net charge of the trion and with Onsager reciprocity. In Σ^- the trion enters as J_e = 2σT(E_e - E_h) and J_h = -σT(E_e - E_h). The trion contribution therefore depends only on E_e - E_h and vanishes for E_e = E_h, the response expected of a neutral exciton. A negative trion (two electrons, one hole) in uniform equal fields feels a net force of -eE and should move. Treating the trion as a rigid particle with mobility μ gives contributions proportional to [[4, -2], [-2, 1]], which is symmetric as Onsager reciprocity requires at B = 0. The written [[2, -2], [-1, 1]] is not symmetric. The same applies to Σ^+. These matrices are what produce equations (7) and (8), the n_T terms in Rxy and Rxy,drag, and the claim that excitons only add "corrections" without changing the picture. The authors should derive the tensor from a two-layer force-balance picture, or explain the convention for σT that makes the stated form correct. They should then check whether the sign and magnitude predictions in the (X + X-) region still hold, and in particular whether trions and excitons can be distinguished (see concern 1).

B. Should address

4. The trion Hall effect is shown in one device. Fig. 2 and Extended Data Fig. 7 use device 1, Fig. 3 and Extended Data Figs. 5, 6 and 8 use device 2 (optics), and all Hall and drag-Hall data (Fig. 4, Extended Data Figs. 3 and 4) come from device 3. The text refers to "other devices studied in this work" only for the binding-energy scale. Please show Rxy and Rxy,drag maps, including the negative-Rxy region, for at least one more device (device 1 has Hall contacts, Extended Data Fig. 1a), and comment on device-to-device spread in the crossover temperature and the size of the negative Rxy. If the effect was absent in any measured device, say so and explain why.

5. Interlayer leakage and coupling artifacts in the drag and "open-circuit" configurations are not quantified. The layers are biased by Vb ≈ 0.76-0.88 V across a 1.5-2 nm hBN barrier, close to the gap-closing bias, where tunnelling can be significant (the paper itself cites enhanced tunnelling, ref. 28). In the standard Hall configuration (Extended Data Fig. 2a) the Mo layer is tied to the Vb source at Mo-10. Any a.c. leakage from the W layer into the Mo layer then returns through that source, so the Mo layer is not strictly open-circuit, which equations (4) and (7) assume. In the drag configuration, the potentiometer balancing of a.c. bias is described, but residual capacitive or tunnelling pickup is not bounded. The authors should report the d.c. and a.c. interlayer leakage currents across the trion regions, the out-of-phase drag signals, and frequency independence of the drag. An Onsager reciprocity check (swapping drive and drag layers, with B reversed) would show that the drag Hall signals are intrinsic.

6. The densities and phase boundaries behind Fig. 4f and the region assignments need calibration details and uncertainties. The dashed n and p lines in Fig. 4f and the p = 2n and n = 2p lines in Figs. 2, 4 and the Extended Data come from "calibrated gate capacitances". It is not stated how the onset of each layer's doping (which depends on Vb, Δ and the contact-region doping), quantum capacitance, and the 1.5-2 nm uncertainty in barrier thickness are handled. The upper and lower boundaries of the trion regions are drawn from the Rxx metal-insulator transition, but no criterion is given (a threshold in dRxx/dT? a resistance value?). The quantitative tests in concern 1 depend on these numbers, so please give the calibration procedure, an uncertainty band on n and p in Fig. 4f, and the boundary criterion. Please also show that the boundaries from Rxx (device 3) and from RC (device 2) agree when expressed in density, not only in Vb-Vg.

7. The temperature scales and the binding-energy estimate are not consistent with each other. The main text equates the about 8 K crossover with the trion binding energy ("about 8 K (or 0.7 meV)"). The Methods estimate about 1.2 meV (about 14 K) for positive trions from the RC injection threshold. Fig. 4e shows Rxy in the (X + X-) region turning negative only below about 4 K, while Rxy,drag grows below about 7-8 K. For a dissociation equilibrium (a Saha-type balance), the temperature at which half the bound states dissociate is well below E_b/k_B by a logarithmic entropy factor, so equating the crossover temperature with E_b underestimates the binding energy. Please model the thermal dissociation, for example n_T(T) from a mass-action law fed into equations (7) and (8). Use it to reconcile the two transport crossovers and the optical estimate, and state the uncertainty of the 1.2 meV value from the 25 mV threshold reading and the barrier-thickness uncertainty in C_2L.

8. The linear-response, small-B model is applied at 4 T without justification. Equation (6) linearizes in ω_c τ, and the text uses R_xy ≈ B/(n_h e) "for small B fields", yet all Hall data are at 4 T. Please show that ω_c τ ≪ 1 for each species at 4 T, or show Rxy and Rxy,drag as a function of B (from Extended Data Fig. 4e) at fixed Vg inside the (X + X-) region, with both signs of B, to test linearity and where the sign reverses. Extended Data Fig. 4e shows negative Rxy only between about 2 and 6 T. Discuss whether its onset at low field reflects the trion region opening up rather than a Hall response linear in B.

9. The account of the resistance oscillations in the (X + X+) region is speculative and rests on a thin fit. The 0.65 m_e cyclotron mass comes from a Lifshitz-Kosevich fit over 1.5-3 K (Extended Data Fig. 7d). The amplitude is defined against a 3.3 K reference that forces it to about zero at the top of the fit range, which biases the extracted mass, and is taken in a strongly insulating region where Lifshitz-Kosevich theory need not apply. The alternative, "recurring transitions between quantum Hall states and the trion phase", is offered without a test. Please either support it, for example with the density periodicity of the stripes against the Landau-level degeneracy of free holes and their field dependence, or present it more tentatively. Please also check the stated free-hole cyclotron energy (see concern 12).

10. Optical and transport data come from different devices and conditions. The RC maps (device 2, Δ = 11 V, 6 T) are used to back the trion assignment of transport regions in device 3 (Δ = 9 V, 4 T). Broadband illumination at up to 50 nW µm^-2 could locally heat the sample or photodope it, which matters for bound states with meV binding energies at 1.5 K. Please state the illumination power used and show that the RC boundaries do not depend on it. Ideally, show Rxx,drag maps from device 2 under the same conditions as its RC data (Fig. 3a is partly this) alongside the optical boundaries.
