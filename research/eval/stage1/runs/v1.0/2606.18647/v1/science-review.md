A. Must address

1. The Hall signatures are not unique to trions; the authors' own model produces them from excitons plus excess free carriers. The Methods give, for the negative-trion region, Rxy ≈ −(B/e)[(1+f)/n_e + f²(1−σX0/σe0)/n_T] with f = σT0/(σT0+σX0). Setting σT = 0 (f = 0; only excitons and free electrons) leaves Rxy ≈ −B/(e n_e): an electron-like Hall response in the hole layer with no trions at all. This is the familiar perfect-drag mechanism (Refs. 30, 31, ED Fig. 8). With the Mo layer open, any W-layer current carried by excitons must be returned by free electrons in the Mo layer, so the hole-layer resistivity is ρ_h = ρ_X + ρ_e (the channels act in series) and its Hall part is the free-electron Hall resistivity, transmitted because excitons pin E_h = E_e. The same reasoning applies to Rxy(drag) (Fig. 3b, Fig. 4c,d): with current driven in the Mo layer and the W layer open, exciton coupling makes the W-layer transverse field follow the Hall field of the free excess carriers, so the drag Hall signal takes the sign of the majority excess carrier, positive for p > n and negative for n > p. That is exactly the sign change across Vg = 0 presented as the trion signature. On the positive-trion side the authors themselves find Rxy ≈ B/(n_h e), carried only by free holes (p. 5), so the drop of n_H in Fig. 3d only shows that holes leave the free pool, whether bound into excitons or trions (the text says as much: "loss of free holes to the formation of trions and excitons"). The statements in the abstract and on p. 6 that the negative Rxy "unambiguously demonstrates the presence of a trion Hall effect and trion drags" therefore do not follow. The authors should: (a) derive Rxx, Rxy, Rxx(drag) and Rxy(drag) explicitly from Eqs. (1)–(5) with and without the trion term, and fit all four together along the Vb ≈ 0.88 V cut (red line in Fig. 3a) at 1.5 K and 10 K, fixing n, p and n_T from gate electrostatics and the RC-determined valley occupations and using as few free scattering times as possible, showing that σT = 0 fails quantitatively and σT ≠ 0 succeeds; (b) overlay in Fig. 3d the net excess density |p − n| expected for excitons plus free carriers and identify where n_H departs from it in a way only trions explain (each positive trion removes two holes, so the two pictures predict different n_H(Vg); Fig. 3d shows |n_H| ranging from well below to well above the gate-determined electron density, which a fit could exploit), and show whether the magnitude of the negative Rxy or the Hall drag exceeds what free electrons plus exciton drag can give at the measured n_e; (c) consider other discriminating observables, such as the ratio Rxy(drag)/Rxy, the B dependence of Rxy(drag), or the drag-channel Hall angle versus trion density at fixed excess density; (d) use a null case where trions are absent, such as the X region inside the EI triangle or the electron-doped side outside the trion triangle at matched exciton density. The optical evidence for trions (Fig. 2c) is stronger than the transport evidence for a trion-specific Hall effect, and the paper should say which result carries which claim. Without a discriminating test, the title and central claim should be restated as a Hall response of a mixed exciton/trion/free-carrier fluid, "consistent with" rather than demonstrating a trion Hall effect.

2. The Hall data are taken where longitudinal resistance exceeds the Hall resistance by one to three orders of magnitude, and the paper does not show how the Hall components were isolated. In the trion regions Rxx is 10^4–10^7 Ω (Fig. 1d,e, ED Figs. 6, 7) and Rxx(drag) reaches 10^6 Ω (Fig. 2a, ED Fig. 3a), while the reported Rxy and Rxy(drag) are about 2–15 kΩ. Contact misalignment of a fraction of a percent, or an inhomogeneous current path, would mix Rxx into Rxy with arbitrary sign and could create both the sign change across Vg ≈ 0 and the negative values on the n side. The negative-Rxy region sits directly against the grey "completely insulating" masks in Fig. 3a, Fig. 4a and ED Fig. 6b,c; the text itself notes that "Rxy cannot be reliably measured near Vg = 0V due to the diverging Rxx" (p. 5) and that Rxx is much higher on the negative-trion side (p. 4). The appearance of the Hall signals below about 8 K (Fig. 4) closely tracks the rise of Rxx in ED Fig. 7b, which an admixture would also produce. Neither Methods nor captions state that Rxy and Rxy(drag) were antisymmetrized, and Fig. 3c covers only B > 0. In Fig. 4d both Rxy(drag) curves settle at about −0.3 kΩ at 10 K regardless of trion sign, suggesting a field-even offset, whereas the text calls Rxy(drag) negligible above 8 K. Other mechanisms can also produce kΩ-scale transverse voltages here: capacitive and out-of-phase pickup at 7.33 Hz; loading of the 100 MΩ preamplifier input by MΩ-to-10 MΩ-scale source impedances on highly resistive voltage probes; interlayer AC coupling in the drag configuration (minimized only by a potentiometer). In addition, the circuit in ED Fig. 2a appears to take the Hall voltage between W-3 and W-4 with W-3 also on the current path, in which case contact resistance would add directly to "Rxy". The authors should: show Rxy and Rxy(drag) at ±B and the antisymmetrized [R(+B) − R(−B)]/2 results for the maps and line cuts of Figs. 3a–c and 4, together with the symmetric (Rxx-like) part, to demonstrate that it does not drive the sign change; show Rxy(B) through B = 0 at fixed (Vg, Vb) in both trion regions; report the out-of-phase lock-in components, the contact resistances and the criterion used for the grey masks; give the Hall-probe geometry and the expected admixture fraction; check Onsager reciprocity of the Hall drag, Rxy(drag),Mo→W(B) = Rxy(drag),W→Mo(−B); and explain or quantify the 10 K Rxy(drag) offset.

3. Interlayer leakage is not characterized, and it is an alternative, artifactual origin of the negative Rxy. Vb ≈ 0.86–0.90 V is applied across a 1.5–2 nm hBN barrier, and the Mo layer is tied (AC-grounded) to the Vb DC source through one contact. Any tunnelling current into the Mo layer would provide a parallel path that contaminates both the "open-circuit" Rxy and the drag signals, and would add an electron contribution to the measured "hole-layer" Hall voltage. The authors should report the interlayer leakage current versus Vb and Vg over the full range of Fig. 3a and in both trion regions, and show that it is negligible compared with the signals.

4. The conductivity-tensor model in Methods appears internally inconsistent, is incompletely specified, and is never compared quantitatively with data. A negative trion (two electrons in the Mo layer, one hole in the W layer) moving with velocity v ∝ (−2eE_e + eE_h) contributes J_e ∝ (4E_e − 2E_h) and J_h ∝ (−2E_e + E_h); that block is symmetric, as Onsager reciprocity requires at B = 0. Eq. (1) instead has the asymmetric trion block [[2, −2], [−1, 1]]σT, and Eq. (2) its transpose for positive trions. It is not stated how the 2×2 field-dependent σT of Eq. (3) enters the 4×4 tensor Σ, or what sign ωc,T takes for each trion type; the closed-form Rxy and the parameter f depend on these choices. σe0 in the Rxy expression is not defined, and the term f²(1−σX0/σe0)/n_T can take either sign depending on unknown ratios, so the model does not even fix the sign of Rxy without further input. The expression for Rxy(drag), which underlies the headline Hall-drag claim (Fig. 3b, Fig. 4c,d), is never given, nor are Rxx and Rxx(drag). The limits used (ωcτ ≪ 1 or not, Landau quantization neglected at 4 T) are not stated, and the drag conductivity σD is neglected "at low temperatures" without saying whether that holds at 8–10 K, where the comparison is made. The authors should correct or justify Eqs. (1)–(2), define all symbols, state the approximations, give explicit Rxx, Rxy, Rxx(drag) and Rxy(drag) for both trion types, and compare them quantitatively with Fig. 3d and Fig. 4b,d, for example by constraining n_e, n_T and n_X from the gate densities plus Rxx and Rxx(drag) and then predicting Rxy and Rxy(drag).

B. Should address

5. A Hall effect, a response linear in B at fixed particle densities, is not demonstrated. Fig. 3c sweeps B at fixed Vb = 0.88 V, where the trion region appears near 2 T and disappears near 6 T, so B changes the phase as well as the Lorentz force. Moreover, the whole phase diagram shifts upward in Vb with B (ED Fig. 6a–d, where the EI apex moves from about 0.875 V to above 0.90 V), so the appearance of negative Rxy between 2 and 6 T and its disappearance above may partly reflect the fixed cut geometrically entering and leaving the triangles rather than trion stabilization and destabilization. The authors should show Rxy and Rxy(drag) (antisymmetrized) versus B at fixed (n, p) points that stay inside the trion regions over a finite field window, for example at intermediate Vb near the triangle apex, tracking the region in (Vg, Vb) if needed, or Rxy maps at several fields with boundaries marked. They should also measure the low-field Hall slope (for example |B| ≤ 1–3 T), where Landau quantization and Zeeman polarization are weaker; the narrow trion line already present at B = 0 along p = 2n (Fig. 1d) offers the cleanest test of a Lorentz force on trions.

7. The ~8 K scale attributed to trion ionization may be the exciton or excitonic-insulator scale, and a crossover temperature is not a binding energy. The onset of Rxy(drag) and Rxy (Fig. 4) is equated with a trion binding energy of 0.7 meV (p. 6), but ED Fig. 7b shows the insulating behaviour at Vg = 0 (the EI itself) also emerging below about 8 K at 4 T, and drag from excitons alone would switch on at the exciton-formation scale; the Rxy(drag) onset in Fig. 4c is not separated from the onset of exciton drag. The claim that the binding energy "decreases gradually from ≈ 8 K near charge neutrality to zero near |Vg| ≈ 0.06 V" has no supporting figure. In a Saha-type ionization of a dense or degenerate 2D mixture, the crossover temperature lies well below E_b/k_B because of entropy, so equating 8 K with 0.7 meV underestimates E_b. The authors should plot the extracted crossover temperature against Vg together with the onset temperature of Rxx(drag) or the drag ratio inside the EI region under the same conditions, show that the trion-region scale differs from the EI/exciton scale (or discuss why they coincide), and explain how the crossover temperature maps onto a binding energy.

9. The semiclassical Drude framework is applied deep in an insulating regime. In the trion regions Rxx is far above h/e² (Fig. 1e, ED Fig. 7c) and the paper describes the behaviour as insulating (dRxx/dT < 0). Eqs. (3) assume Drude tensors with well-defined ω_cτ for trions, excitons and free carriers, and both the reading of n_H = B/(eRxy) as a carrier density and the picture of mobile trions undergoing Lorentz deflection rest on that regime; in hopping or strongly localized transport the Hall coefficient is not simply related to carrier density or sign. The authors should state the estimated ω_cτ and mobilities of each species at 4 T, discuss whether the large Rxx in the trion regions reflects intrinsic insulating behaviour or the open-circuit drag constraint (Rxx diverging because the Mo layer cannot carry counterflow), and explain why a semiclassical tensor remains appropriate.

11. The conversion of the 25 mV threshold shift to E_T,b ≈ 1.2 meV (Methods) rests on unjustified assumptions, and the two binding-energy estimates (1.2 meV from optics at 6 T, 0.7 meV from transport at 4 T) differ by almost a factor of two yet are described as "in good agreement". The lever arm C_Q^-1/(C_Q^-1 + C_2L^-1) ≈ 0.05 uses a zero-field, constant 2D hole density of states with g_v = 2 (m_h = 0.45 m0), but at 6 T the hole cyclotron energy (~1.5 meV) is comparable to or exceeds E_T,b, so the density of states is Landau-quantized, and the injection is into a single, initially empty spin-valley species (g_s g_v = 1 would roughly halve C_Q). The electron-layer (bilayer MoSe2) quantum capacitance, the exciton compressibility and the negative compressibility expected near the EI are neglected. C_2L uses εr ≈ 3.5 and d = 2 nm without uncertainty, although Methods give 1.5–2 nm, and ignores that electrons in bilayer MoSe2 may reside partly in the layer far from the WSe2. The "expected injection threshold without interlayer Coulomb interactions" (red solid line in Fig. 2c, ED Figs. 3–5), the baseline for the shift, is not described (how computed, with which g-factors and band offsets). Also, 6 T is where the trion regions are said to vanish in device 2 (Fig. 3c, ED Fig. 6), so the choice of field needs comment. The authors should describe the red-line calculation, include both layers' quantum capacitances at finite field, propagate these uncertainties into an error bar on E_T,b, and compare the optical, transport and valley-Zeeman scales at matched field and temperature, with these caveats.

12. The paper does not explain why the trion regions appear and expand with B. At B = 0 the trion region is a narrow strip along p = 2n (Fig. 1d); at 2–6 T it becomes two broad triangles (Fig. 1e, Fig. 3c, ED Fig. 6); above 6 T it vanishes. The disappearance is attributed to the valley Zeeman energy exceeding the binding energy, but the onset near 2 T and the expansion are not explained. The authors' own estimate puts the cyclotron energy (≈ 0.8 meV at 4 T) at the binding-energy scale, and quantum oscillations are seen inside the trion regions (ED Fig. 8), with vertical stripes visible in Fig. 3c, so Landau quantization of free carriers, rather than trion formation alone, may shape the high-Rxx triangles and the Rxy structure. The authors should explain the field-induced stabilization (for example via valley Zeeman splitting relative to the Fermi energy of the minority species) and discuss the role of Landau quantization.

13. The electron layer is treated as a monolayer without justification. Figs. 1b and 2d and the valley analysis of the Mo-layer polarons (ED Fig. 3) treat bilayer MoSe2 as a single-valley-pair, spin-valley-locked monolayer. In 2H bilayer MoSe2 the conduction band carries an additional layer degree of freedom, and the spin-valley ordering differs between the two layers. This affects the spin-singlet negative-trion argument, the identification of σ± polaron features with K/K′ electron occupation, and the effective interlayer separation used in C_2L. The authors should state the layer polarization of the electrons in the channel under the applied Δ and justify the monolayer-like valley picture, for example using optical signatures from the two MoSe2 layers.

14. Linear response and heating are not demonstrated against a sub-meV energy scale. A 1 mV RMS AC voltage excitation is used (Methods); in the insulating regime a substantial fraction may drop across the channel rather than the contacts, which is comparable to or larger than the inferred trion binding energy (0.7–1.2 meV) and the cyclotron energy. The authors should state how much of the excitation drops across the channel and show that Rxy and Rxy(drag) in the trion regions are independent of excitation amplitude, with no heating. Combining the amplitude dependence with the temperature dependence could also independently confirm the sub-meV scale (if the signals are suppressed at an excitation corresponding to the binding energy) and rule out heating as a source of the T dependence.
