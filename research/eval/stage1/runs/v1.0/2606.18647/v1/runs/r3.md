Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports transport signatures of a Hall response of interlayer trions in dual-gated MoSe2(bilayer)/hBN/WSe2(monolayer) electron-hole double layers under a perpendicular field. It builds on recent reports of equilibrium trion fluids in the same platform (Refs. 36, 37). The new elements are four. The finite-B phase diagram contains two triangular trion regions (X+X+ and X+X-), identified with Rxx, Rxx(drag) and valley-resolved reflection contrast. There is a sign-changing Hall drag. The Hall density of the hole layer drops in the positive-trion region. The headline result is an electron-like (negative) Hall resistance in the hole-doped WSe2 layer in the negative-trion region. A Hall response of charged composite bosons/fermions in an equilibrium fluid would be a notable result and a step towards trion Landau quantization. Whether the data single out trions, rather than excitons plus free carriers, is the main open question.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The experimental platform is mature, and three complementary probes are applied to the same phase diagram: single-layer Rxx/Rxy, Coulomb drag (Rxx(drag), Rxy(drag), drag ratio) and circularly resolved reflection contrast.
- The valley-resolved RC (Fig. 2b–d, ED Fig. 3) gives a direct, microscopic picture of which valleys are occupied in each layer. The lowered K-valley injection threshold is an appealing thermodynamic signature of spin-singlet trion formation.
- The negative Rxy measured in a layer whose free carriers are holes (Fig. 3a, 3d, 4a–b) is a striking, easily stated observation.
- The temperature (Fig. 4, ED Figs. 5, 7) and field (Fig. 3c, ED Figs. 4, 6) evolution are mutually consistent at the qualitative level. Trion features appear below about 8 K and in a window of about 2–6 T, and the optical and transport scales agree roughly.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The authors' own model predicts an electron-like Hall signal in the hole layer without any trions, so the negative Rxy does not uniquely demonstrate a trion Hall effect. In the Methods, the negative-trion result is Rxy ≈ −(B/e)[(1+f)/n_e + f²(1−σX0/σe0)/n_T], with f = σT0/(σT0+σX0). Setting σT = 0 (no trions, f = 0) leaves Rxy ≈ −B/(e n_e). This is the Hall response of free electrons in the Mo-layer, transmitted to the W-layer by drag of neutral excitons. In other words, an electron-doped excitonic insulator with strong exciton drag (the "perfect drag" regime of Refs. 30, 31, ED Fig. 8) already gives an electron-Hall signal in the hole layer. The statement on p. 6 that the negative Hall resistance "unambiguously demonstrates the presence of a trion Hall effect" therefore does not follow from the model as written. The same applies to the positive-trion side: there the model gives Rxy ≈ B/(n_h e), so the drop of n_H in Fig. 3d only counts holes removed from the free-carrier pool. Holes bound into excitons or into trions give the same result, and the authors say as much ("loss of free holes to the formation of trions and excitons"). The authors should identify an observable that separates "trions carrying charge under the Lorentz force" from "excitons dragging, plus free carriers in the other layer". For example:
   (a) Compute, from Eqs. (1)–(5), all four measured quantities (Rxx, Rxy, Rxx(drag), Rxy(drag)) in both scenarios (σT = 0 versus σT ≠ 0). Compare them quantitatively with the Vg dependence along the red line in Fig. 3a, using n_e, n_h and n_T constrained by the gate electrostatics and the RC-determined valley occupations.
   (b) Show that the magnitude of the negative Rxy, or of the Hall drag, exceeds what free electrons plus exciton drag can give at the measured n_e. Fig. 3d shows |n_H| ranging from well below to well above the total gate-determined electron density, which a fit could use.
   (c) Use the X region inside the EI triangle, or the electron-doped side outside the trion triangle at matched exciton density, as the null case where trions are absent.
   Without such a comparison, the central claim should be reworded as consistent with, rather than demonstrating, a trion Hall effect.

2. The Hall signals are not shown to be antisymmetric in B, and they are measured where Rxx is 10^4–10^6 Ω. The trion regions are exactly where Rxx and Rxx(drag) are largest (Fig. 1e, Fig. 2a, ED Fig. 6: 10^5–10^6 Ω, adjacent to grey "completely insulating" areas). Typical Hall values there are 5–15 kΩ (Figs. 3, 4). Even a small geometric misalignment of the Hall probes, or an inhomogeneous current path, will mix Rxx or Rxx(drag) into Rxy or Rxy(drag) with an arbitrary sign. Such mixing could create the sign change across Vg ≈ 0 and the negative values on the n side. Neither the Methods nor the captions say that Rxy and Rxy(drag) were antisymmetrized under B → −B, and Fig. 3c covers only positive B. The appearance below about 8 K in Fig. 4 closely tracks the rise of Rxx in ED Fig. 7b, which is what an admixture would also produce. In addition, the circuit in ED Fig. 2a appears to take the Hall voltage between W-3 and W-4, and W-3 also appears to be on the current path. If so, contact resistance would add directly to "Rxy". The authors should:
   - show Rxy and Rxy(drag) at ±B and the antisymmetrized results for the maps and line cuts in Figs. 3 and 4;
   - show Rxy(B) through B = 0 at fixed (Vg, Vb) in both trion regions;
   - give the Hall-probe geometry and the expected admixture fraction;
   - for the Hall drag, check Onsager reciprocity by interchanging drive and drag layers (Rxy(drag)(B) for Mo→W versus W→Mo at −B).

B. Should address

3. The Drude conductivity-tensor framework is applied deep in an insulating regime. In the trion regions Rxx is far above h/e² (Fig. 1e, ED Fig. 7c), and the paper itself describes the behaviour as insulating (dRxx/dT < 0). Eqs. (3) use Drude tensors with well-defined ω_c τ for trions, excitons and free carriers. The interpretation n_H = B/(eRxy) as a carrier density, and the description of trions as mobile particles undergoing Lorentz deflection, both rest on that regime. In hopping or strongly localized transport the Hall coefficient is not simply related to carrier density or sign. The authors should state the estimated ω_c τ and mobilities of each species at 4 T. They should also discuss whether Rxx in the trion regions reflects intrinsic insulating behaviour or the open-circuit drag constraint (Rxx diverging because the Mo-layer cannot carry counterflow). Finally, they should explain why a semiclassical tensor is still appropriate.

4. The Hall effect is shown in a single device. Rxy, Rxy(drag), the B dependence (Fig. 3c) and the T dependence (Fig. 4) all come from device 2. The B = 0 and 4 T phase diagrams come from device 1, and the RC and Rxx(drag) at 6 T from device 3. The Vb scales differ noticeably between devices: the trion triangle is at Vb ≈ 0.75–0.78 V in device 1 (Fig. 1d,e) and ≈ 0.86–0.89 V in device 2 (Fig. 3a). The trion boundaries in Fig. 3 are therefore drawn by analogy with phase boundaries determined optically in another device and cryostat. The authors should show Rxy and Rxy(drag) in at least one other device (device 1 or 3), including the negative Rxy on the electron-trion side. They should also explain how the dashed boundaries in Figs. 3–4 and ED Fig. 6 were placed for device 2.

5. Excitation level, heating and interlayer leakage are not characterized against a sub-meV energy scale. The samples are driven with a 1 mV RMS AC voltage (Methods), which is comparable to or larger than the inferred trion binding energy (0.7–1.2 meV) and the cyclotron energy. How much of this drops across the channel rather than the contacts is not stated. The bias Vb ≈ 0.88 V is applied across a 1.5–2 nm hBN barrier, yet no interlayer tunnelling current is reported. The voltage probes see source impedances up to about 10 MΩ against a 100 MΩ preamplifier input at 7.33 Hz, so loading and capacitive phase shifts can be significant. The authors should:
   - show that Rxy and Rxy(drag) in the trion regions do not depend on excitation amplitude;
   - report the interlayer leakage current versus Vb over the range used;
   - report the out-of-phase lock-in components;
   - state the criterion for the grey "completely insulating" masks.

6. The trion binding-energy estimate rests on several unexamined choices. The conversion of the 25 mV threshold shift to E_T,b ≈ 1.2 meV (Methods) has four problems:
   - It uses a constant 2D hole density of states with g_s g_v = 2, at B = 6 T, where only one spin-valley species is being injected (g_s g_v = 1 would roughly halve C_Q and change the result).
   - The hole cyclotron energy (≈ 1.5 meV at 6 T) exceeds the claimed binding energy, so the density of states is Landau-quantized, not constant.
   - The electron-layer quantum capacitance and the exciton compressibility are neglected.
   - The "expected injection threshold without interlayer Coulomb interactions" (red line in Fig. 2c, ED Figs. 4–5) is not described: how it was calculated, and with which g-factors and band offsets.

   Further, the ionization crossover temperature in Fig. 4 (about 8 K, quoted as 0.7 meV) is not a binding energy; for a degenerate 2D mixture, ionization typically sets in at k_BT well below E_b because of entropy. The quoted agreement among 1.2 meV, 0.7 meV and the valley Zeeman scale should be presented with uncertainties and with these caveats.

7. Why the trion regions expand with B, and what this means for the "Lorentz force" interpretation, is unexplained. At B = 0 the trion region is a narrow strip along p = 2n (Fig. 1d). At 2–6 T it becomes two broad triangles (Fig. 1e, Fig. 3c, ED Fig. 6), and above 6 T it vanishes. The vanishing is explained by Zeeman polarization, but the onset at about 2 T and the expansion are not. Because trions exist only at finite B, the field dependence of Rxy in Fig. 3c mixes changes in the phase diagram with the Hall response. The paper therefore cannot show that the trion Hall resistance scales linearly with B, the most direct sign of a Lorentz-force origin. The authors should explain the field-induced stabilization (for example through valley Zeeman splitting relative to the Fermi energy of the minority species). They should also show Rxy/B, or the antisymmetrized Hall slope, at fixed (n, p) points that remain inside the trion region over a finite field range.

8. The density calibration behind Fig. 3d is not documented. The dashed electron and hole densities in Fig. 3d are said to come from "calibrated gate capacitances", but the hBN thicknesses, capacitances, how Vb enters n and p, the charge-neutrality reference and the uncertainty are not given. The text also assumes Δ is proportional to the electric field because the top and bottom hBN are "nearly the same" thickness. The comparison of n_H with these lines is the key quantitative evidence on the positive-trion side and at T = 10 K. The authors should give the electrostatic model and its parameters, explain how the calibration was checked (for example against quantum oscillations or n_H outside the bound-state regions), and add error bands. They should also explain the "weak anomaly" near Vg = 0 at 10 K, which reaches about 3 × 10^12 cm^-2, well above the expected density; "remnant electron-hole interactions" is not sufficient.

9. The optical and transport phase diagrams are compared across different conditions. RC is measured in a different cryostat (1.6 K base, 9 T) with white-light illumination of a type-II heterobilayer. Photodoping or photoinduced interlayer charge transfer can shift thresholds in such structures, and the stated incident intensity limit (also see item 14) is given without evidence that the thresholds are independent of intensity. The authors should show that the injection thresholds in Fig. 2c do not depend on light intensity. They should also overlay the RC-derived boundaries on Rxx(drag) and Rxy measured in the same device (device 3), so that the trion assignment of the Hall features does not depend on transferring boundaries between devices.

C. Minor

10. The cyclotron energy quoted in the Conclusion (≈ 0.8 meV for free holes at 4 T) does not match the hole mass used in the Methods. With m_h = 0.45 m0, ħω_c = ħeB/m_h ≈ 1.03 meV at 4 T. Please reconcile.

11. The Fig. 4 caption says a sign change "in both Rxy and Rxy(drag)" occurs for T ≲ 8 K. The text and Fig. 4b show that Rxy on the negative-trion side becomes negative only below about 4 K. Please correct the caption.

12. The y-axis of ED Fig. 7c is labelled "T (K)" but shows Rxx (Ω).

13. The two descriptions of the ED Fig. 1 outlines contradict each other. The ED Fig. 1 caption says the top gate is the black dashed line, the bottom gate the red line, and the exciton-contact hBN the pink line. The Methods say the black and red solid lines denote the WSe2 and MoSe2 flakes, and that the gates are dashed black and red lines. Please make them consistent and label all outlines.

14. Notation and typographical issues:
   - σe0 in the negative-trion Rxy formula is not defined. Please define it and note that the sign of the second term depends on σX0/σe0.
   - "nW/µm^-2" should be nW/µm².
   - "To further simply" should be "simplify".
   - "70^0C" should be 70 °C.
   - The Rxx(drag) map in Fig. 2a is at 6 T (device 3), but the text introduces it as correlated with the 4 T Rxx map in Fig. 1e.

15. Fig. 3d: the 1.5 K curve has a gap between Vg ≈ 0 and 0.02 V where Rxy is unmeasurable, so the "sign change" at Vg > 0 happens across missing data. Please state this explicitly. Please also mark the trion-region boundaries on the n_H plot.

16. Fig. 4 shows Rxy down to 1.5 K but Rxy(drag) only down to 2 K. Please explain the difference or harmonize.

17. The Methods should give the channel dimensions and Hall-bar aspect ratio for each device, so that readers can estimate geometric mixing and convert resistances to resistivities.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a quantitative model figure. Plot Rxy, Rxy(drag) and Rxx(drag) versus Vg along the red line in Fig. 3a, computed from Eqs. (1)–(5) with and without trion conductivity, overlaid on the data. This would turn the headline claim from qualitative to quantitative and address concern 1 directly.
2. Present B-antisymmetrized Hall and Hall-drag data, plus a layer-interchange (Onsager) drag measurement, for at least two devices. This is the cleanest way to rule out Rxx admixture (concerns 2 and 4).
3. Measure the Hall slope dRxy/dB at fixed (n, p) points chosen to stay inside the trion region over a field window, for example at intermediate Vb near the triangle apex. Lorentz-force linearity would then be tested directly (concern 7).
4. Combine the excitation-amplitude dependence with the temperature dependence. If the trion Hall signals are suppressed at an excitation voltage that corresponds to the binding energy, this would independently confirm the sub-meV scale and rule out heating as a source of the T dependence (concerns 5 and 6).
