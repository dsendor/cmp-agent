Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors study dual-gated MoSe2(bilayer)/hBN/WSe2(monolayer) electron-hole double layers in a perpendicular magnetic field. They report that the trion-fluid regions of the phase diagram, previously identified at B = 0 along the p = 2n and n = 2p lines (Refs. 36, 37), expand into two triangular regions at finite field. Valley-resolved reflection contrast supports this. In these regions the Hall drag resistance changes sign between positive and negative trions, and the single-layer Hall resistance of the hole (W) layer becomes negative on the negative-trion side. The paper interprets this as a "trion Hall effect" from the Lorentz force on charged composite particles. A Hall response of an equilibrium trion fluid would be a new and attractive result for this rapidly developing platform. Whether the data establish it depends on two things: separating the trion contribution from the Hall response that excitons plus excess free carriers already produce, and showing that the Hall signals are free of longitudinal mixing in a strongly insulating regime.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Timely topic. It extends the recent equilibrium-trion work in electron-hole bilayers to magnetotransport, and it opens the question of quantum oscillations and Landau quantization of composite particles.
- The data set is broad: Rxx, Rxy, Rxx(drag), Rxy(drag), drag ratio and helicity-resolved reflection contrast, with field (Fig. 3c, ED Figs. 4, 6) and temperature (Fig. 4, ED Figs. 5, 7) dependences.
- The helicity-resolved RC analysis (Fig. 2b–d, ED Fig. 3) gives a clear valley-occupation picture. The lowered K-valley injection threshold in the (X+X+) region (Fig. 2c) is direct, thermodynamic-like evidence for an extra bound state. Its disappearance at 9 T (ED Fig. 4d) and at 10 K (ED Fig. 5d) is consistent with the spin-singlet trion picture.
- The sign change of Rxy(drag) across charge neutrality and the negative Rxy in a hole layer are striking, reproducible-looking features (Figs. 3a,b, 4).
- A conductivity-tensor model (Methods, Eqs. 1–5) is provided as a framework for interpreting the data.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The Hall signatures shown are not unique to trions. Under the paper's own model they also arise from excitons plus excess free carriers. In the negative-trion region the Methods give Rxy ≈ −(B/e)[(1+f)/n_e + f²(1−σX0/σe0)/n_T], with f = σT0/(σT0+σX0). Setting f = 0 (no trions, only excitons and free electrons) leaves Rxy = −B/(e n_e): an electron-Hall response in the hole layer with no trion at all. The physics is the familiar perfect-drag mechanism. With the Mo layer open, any current in the W layer carried by excitons must be returned by free electrons in the Mo layer. Their Hall field is transmitted to the W layer because excitons pin E_h = E_e. The same reasoning applies to Rxy(drag) (Fig. 3b, Fig. 4c,d). With a current driven in the Mo layer and the W layer open, exciton coupling forces the W-layer transverse field to follow the Hall field of the free excess carriers. The drag Hall signal then takes the sign of the majority excess carrier: positive for p > n and negative for n > p. That is the sign change across Vg = 0 reported as the trion signature. In the positive-trion region the authors themselves find Rxy ≈ B/(n_h e), carried only by free holes, so Fig. 3a and the left side of Fig. 3d are not trion Hall signals either. As a result, the statements in the abstract and on p. 6 ("unambiguously demonstrates the presence of a trion Hall effect") are not supported by the evidence presented.
   What to do:
   (a) Derive Rxy(drag) and Rxx(drag) explicitly from Eq. (5), both with and without the trion term.
   (b) Fit Rxx, Rxy, Rxx(drag) and Rxy(drag) together along the Vb ≈ 0.88 V cut. Fix n and p from the gate capacitances and leave as few free scattering times as possible. Show that a model with σT = 0 fails, quantitatively, to reproduce the data and that one with σT ≠ 0 succeeds.
   (c) In Fig. 3d, overlay the net excess density |p − n| (the expectation for excitons plus free carriers) and identify where n_H departs from it in a way only trions can explain. Since each positive trion removes two holes, the excess-carrier and trion pictures make different predictions for n_H versus Vg, and those predictions can be compared.
   (d) Identify an observable unique to charged composites. One candidate is the dependence of the Hall angle in the drag channel on trion density at fixed excess density. Without such a discriminating test, the title claim needs to be restated as a Hall response of a mixed exciton/trion/free-carrier fluid.

2. The Hall data come from a regime where the longitudinal resistance exceeds the Hall resistance by one to three orders of magnitude, and the paper does not state how the Hall components were isolated. Rxx in the trion regions is 10^4–10^6 Ω (Fig. 1e, ED Fig. 6), and Rxx(drag) reaches 10^6 Ω (Fig. 2a, ED Fig. 3a). The reported Rxy and Rxy(drag) are about 2–15 kΩ. Contact misalignment of a fraction of a percent would therefore produce Rxy-sized signals. The negative Rxy region sits directly against the grey "completely insulating" area in Fig. 3a, Fig. 4a and ED Fig. 6b,c, where Rxx diverges. The authors also note that "Rxy cannot be reliably measured near Vg = 0V due to the diverging Rxx" (p. 5) and that Rxx is much higher on the negative-trion side (p. 4). Fig. 3c shows only B > 0. In Fig. 4d both Rxy(drag) curves settle at about −0.3 kΩ at 10 K, regardless of trion sign. This suggests a field-even offset, whereas the text describes Rxy(drag) as negligible above 8 K.
   What to do:
   (a) State whether all Hall quantities were antisymmetrized, [R(+B) − R(−B)]/2. If not, remeasure Figs. 3a–c and 4 at ±B and show both raw components.
   (b) Show the symmetric (Rxx-like) part, to demonstrate that it does not drive the sign change.
   (c) Report the out-of-phase lock-in component and the contact resistances in these regions. The 100 MΩ preamplifier input impedance and the 7.33 Hz excitation can produce loading and phase errors when voltage-probe contacts become highly resistive.
   (d) Check the drag Hall signal against Onsager reciprocity: Rxy(drag),Mo→W(B) = Rxy(drag),W→Mo(−B).

B. Should address

3. The temperature scale attributed to trion ionization may be the exciton or excitonic-insulator scale. The ~8 K onset of Rxy(drag) and Rxy (Fig. 4) is equated with trion binding (0.7 meV, p. 6). However, ED Fig. 7b shows the insulating behaviour at Vg = 0 (the EI itself) also emerging below ~8 K, and drag from excitons alone would switch on at the exciton-formation scale. The claim that the binding energy "decreases gradually from ≈ 8 K near charge neutrality to zero near |Vg| ≈ 0.06 V" has no supporting figure. The authors should:
   - plot the extracted crossover temperature against Vg, together with the onset temperature of Rxx(drag) or the drag ratio at Vg = 0 under the same conditions;
   - show that the trion-region scale differs from the EI/exciton scale;
   - explain how a crossover temperature maps onto a binding energy. In a Saha-type ionization the crossover temperature lies well below E_b/k_B, so equating 8 K with 0.7 meV underestimates E_b.

4. The two binding-energy estimates differ by almost a factor of two (1.2 meV from optics at 6 T, Methods; 0.7 meV from transport at 4 T), yet they are described as "in good agreement". The capacitance conversion (Methods) has several problems:
   - C_Q is computed with g_v = 2 and a constant zero-field density of states. At 6 T the hole cyclotron energy (~1.5 meV for m = 0.45 m0) is comparable to E_T,b, and the relevant injection is into a single, initially empty valley.
   - It neglects the negative compressibility expected near the EI.
   - It uses d = 2 nm although Methods give 1.5–2 nm.
   - It ignores that electrons in bilayer MoSe2 may reside partly in the layer far from the WSe2.
   Please propagate these uncertainties into an error bar on E_T,b, and compare field and temperature at matched conditions.

5. Hidden assumption about the bilayer MoSe2 electron layer. Figs. 1b and 2d and the valley analysis of the Mo-layer polarons (ED Fig. 3) treat the electron layer as a single-valley-pair, spin-valley-locked monolayer. In 2H bilayer MoSe2 the conduction band carries an additional layer degree of freedom, and the spin-valley ordering differs between the two layers. This affects:
   - the spin-singlet negative-trion argument;
   - the identification of σ± polaron features with K/K′ electron occupation;
   - the effective interlayer separation used in C_2L.
   The authors should state the layer polarization of the electrons in the channel under the applied Δ and justify the monolayer-like valley picture. Optical signatures from the two MoSe2 layers would help.

6. Evidence for negative trions is thinner and partly inconsistent. The negative-trion valley analysis is relegated to ED Fig. 3, and there are two problems:
   - The ED Fig. 3 caption says negative trions lie between the red and black dashed lines "when both valleys are occupied in the Mo-layer and only one valley is occupied in the W-layer". The panels, however, mark the thresholds in the same order as in Fig. 2 (red = both W valleys, at lower Vb). That ordering corresponds to the positive-trion condition.
   - The line cut (blue dashed line in ED Fig. 3a at Vg ≈ 0.11–0.12 V) appears to lie at or beyond the edge of the (X+X−) triangle.
   Please correct the caption and threshold labels, and show a line cut clearly inside the (X+X−) region. Also give the lowered injection threshold for the Mo K′ valley there (the analogue of the red solid line in Fig. 2c). The headline electron-Hall result rests on this region.

7. The Hall result is shown in one device, and the different measurements are spread across devices. Rxy and Rxy(drag) (Figs. 3, 4, ED Figs. 6, 8) come only from device 2. The Rxx phase diagrams (Fig. 1d,e, ED Fig. 7) come from device 1. The RC and Rxx(drag) maps (Fig. 2, ED Figs. 3–5) come from device 3, which is not shown in ED Fig. 1 or described. The trion regions sit at very different Vb in each device: about 0.74–0.78 V, 0.86–0.89 V and 0.84–0.90 V. Nevertheless, the dashed boundaries "corresponding to those in Fig. 1f" are overlaid on all of them. The authors should:
   - show Rxy and Rxy(drag), including the negative Rxy, in at least one more device;
   - state how the kite-shaped boundaries were determined in each device: from which data, by hand or by an electrostatic model;
   - add device 3 to ED Fig. 1.

8. Interlayer leakage and excitation level are not characterized.
   - Leakage: Vb ≈ 0.86–0.90 V is applied across a 1.5–2 nm hBN barrier, and the Mo layer is tied to a DC source through one contact. Tunnelling current would provide a parallel path that contaminates both the "open-circuit" Rxy and the drag signals. Please report the interlayer leakage current versus Vb and Vg in the trion regions.
   - Excitation: a 1 mV RMS voltage excitation is used. In the insulating regime a substantial fraction can drop across the channel, which is comparable to the ~1 meV trion binding energy. Please show that Rxy and Rxy(drag) are in linear response, with no excitation-amplitude dependence or heating.

9. Explain why the trion regions expand with B. At B = 0 the trion region is a narrow strip along p = 2n (Fig. 1d). At 3–6 T it becomes two broad triangles (Fig. 1e, ED Fig. 6). The text explains why the regions disappear at high field (valley Zeeman energy exceeding binding) but not why they appear and grow. The authors' own estimate puts the cyclotron energy (≈ 0.8 meV at 4 T) at the scale of the binding energy, and quantum oscillations are seen inside the trion regions (ED Fig. 8). It is therefore possible that Landau quantization of the free carriers, rather than trion formation alone, shapes the high-Rxx triangles and the Rxy structure; Fig. 3c indeed shows vertical stripes. Please discuss, and if possible show the Hall response at lower field (2–3 T) where Landau quantization is weaker.

10. The field sweep in Fig. 3c is taken at fixed Vb = 0.88 V, while the whole phase diagram shifts upward in Vb with B (ED Fig. 6a–d, where the EI apex moves from about 0.875 V to above 0.90 V). The appearance of the negative Rxy between 2 and 6 T and its disappearance above it may therefore partly reflect the fixed cut entering and leaving the triangles geometrically, rather than trion stabilization and destabilization. A B-sweep that follows a fixed position relative to the EI apex, or Rxy maps at several fields with the boundaries marked, would separate the two effects.

C. Minor

11. The Methods model needs more detail. σe0 in the Rxy expression is not defined. The limits used (ωcτ ≪ 1 or not, Landau quantization neglected at 4 T) should be stated. The full expressions for Rxx, Rxx(drag) and Rxy(drag) from Eqs. (4)–(5) should be given, not only Rxy. The drag conductivity σD is neglected "at low temperatures", but it is not stated whether this holds at 8–10 K, where the comparison is made.

12. The Fig. 4 caption states that a sign change in Rxy appears for T ≲ 8 K. The text and Fig. 4b show that Rxy on the negative-trion side becomes negative only below ≈ 4 K. Please reconcile.

13. Fig. 2a: the inset is labelled R_xx although the panel shows Rxx(drag). The blue dashed line-cut position appears at Vg ≈ −0.08 V, whereas the text quotes Vg ≈ −0.06 V.

14. ED Fig. 7c: the y-axis is labelled "T (K)" and should be Rxx (Ω).

15. Methods: "50 nW/μm^−2" should read nW/μm². "70^0C" should be "70 °C". "To further simply" should be "simplify". The optical intensity limit should be accompanied by an estimate or check (e.g., a power series) that optical heating or photodoping does not shift the ~1 meV threshold features in ED Figs. 4–5.

16. The statement that Rxx is higher in the (X+X−) region "because holes in negative trions are much more tightly bound" (p. 4) is unclear and needs justification or a reference.

17. The jump in n_H near Vg ≈ 0.06 V in Fig. 3d is a divergence where Rxy passes through zero. Plotting Rxy, or the Hall conductivity, alongside would make this region easier to read.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a single figure that sets the measured n_H(Vg) and Rxy(drag)(Vg) along the Vb ≈ 0.88 V cut against model curves for (i) excitons plus excess free carriers and (ii) excitons plus trions plus free carriers. Use gate-calibrated densities and the 1.5 K and 10 K data. This would turn the qualitative "trion Hall effect" interpretation into a quantitative test.
2. Present antisymmetrized ±B Hall data with the symmetric component and Onsager-reciprocal drag measurements, ideally in a second device. This would remove the main artifact concern.
3. Extract the crossover temperature as a function of Vg, and the RC threshold shift as a function of B and T, from the same device. Compare both to the EI onset temperature, so that the binding-energy scale is established independently of exciton formation.
4. Clarify the electronic structure of the bilayer MoSe2 electron layer (layer polarization, valley and spin ordering). Correct the negative-trion valley analysis in ED Fig. 3, since the headline electron-Hall result depends on it.
