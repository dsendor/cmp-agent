Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports Hall and Hall-drag measurements on dual-gated MoSe2(bilayer)/hBN/WSe2(monolayer) electron-hole double layers in the regime where an equilibrium trion fluid was recently established (Refs. 36, 37). Its central claims are three. Under a perpendicular field, the trion regions of the phase diagram widen into two triangles. Inside them a "trion Hall effect" appears: a large Hall drag that changes sign between positive and negative trions, and a negative (electron-like) Hall resistance in the hole-doped WSe2 layer on the negative-trion side. Both features vanish above about 8 K or at |Vg| ≳ 0.06 V. Transport of charged composite particles in a magnetic field is a natural and timely next step for this platform. The negative Hall resistance in a hole layer is a striking observation. Whether it specifically shows the Lorentz force on bound trions, rather than any Coulomb-coupled electron-hole fluid, is the main open question.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- High-quality transport on a hard platform: Rxx, Rxy, Rxx(drag) and Rxy(drag) maps over (Vg, Vb, B, T), with near-perfect drag in the excitonic-insulator (EI) region (Extended Data Fig. 8).
- Valley- and handedness-resolved reflection contrast (Fig. 2, Extended Data Figs. 3-5) provides spectroscopic evidence, independent of transport, that both hole valleys are occupied and that the injection threshold is lowered inside the X+X+ region. This supports a spin-singlet trion picture and its suppression by the valley Zeeman effect.
- The sign change of Rxy and Rxy(drag) across charge neutrality, and its disappearance with temperature (Fig. 4) and with doping (Fig. 3d), give a consistent phenomenology across several control parameters.
- A minimal multi-component conductivity-tensor model is offered (Methods), which makes the interpretation testable in principle.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The authors' own model predicts an electron-like Hall response in the hole layer without any trions, so the central inference is not established. Main text p. 6 states that the negative Rxy "unambiguously demonstrates the presence of a trion Hall effect and trion drags." In the negative-trion formula, Rxy ≈ −(B/e)[(1+f)/n_e + f²(1−σX0/σe0)/n_T] with f = σT0/(σT0+σX0). Setting f = 0 (no trions, only excitons plus free electrons) gives Rxy ≈ −B/(e n_e). This can be checked directly. For a Mo layer in open circuit containing excitons and free electrons, the hole-layer resistivity is ρ_h = ρ_X + ρ_e, because the exciton and free-electron channels act in series. Its Hall component is the free-electron Hall resistivity, −B/(e n_e). In other words, perfect exciton drag alone transfers the electron Hall voltage into the hole layer.

   The same holds on the positive side. There the text says that Rxy ≈ B/(e n_h) measures only free holes (p. 5), so the drop of n_H in Fig. 3d shows that holes become bound, not that trions experience a Lorentz force. Likewise, a sign change of Rxy(drag) that follows the sign of the excess carriers would be expected in any exciton plus excess-carrier mixture with strong exciton drag.

   The sign of the Hall response therefore does not discriminate trions from a Bose-Fermi mixture of excitons and unbound carriers. The authors should:
   (a) identify a transport observable that differs quantitatively between the two scenarios. Candidates include the magnitude of n_H on the negative side compared with the excess electron density n − p from the gate calibration, the ratio Rxy(drag)/Rxy, or the B dependence of Rxy(drag).
   (b) fit Fig. 3d and Fig. 4 with both scenarios.
   (c) moderate the wording ("unambiguously") if no such discriminator exists. The optical evidence for trions (Fig. 2c) is stronger than the transport evidence for a trion-specific Hall effect, and the paper should say which result carries which claim.

2. The conductivity-tensor model in Methods appears internally inconsistent and is not compared quantitatively with data. Take a negative trion (two electrons in the Mo layer, one hole in the W layer) moving with velocity v ∝ (−2eE_e + eE_h). It contributes J_e ∝ (4E_e − 2E_h) and J_h ∝ (−2E_e + E_h). That trion block is symmetric, as Onsager reciprocity requires at B = 0. Eq. (1) instead has the asymmetric trion block [[2, −2], [−1, 1]]σT, and Eq. (2) has the transpose for positive trions. It is also not stated how the 2×2 field-dependent σT of Eq. (3) enters the 4×4 tensor Σ, or what sign ωc,T takes for positive and negative trions. The closed-form Rxy expression and the parameter f depend on these choices.

   Second, the expression for Rxy(drag), which underlies the headline Hall-drag claim (Fig. 3b, Fig. 4c,d), is never given.

   Third, no parameters are extracted. Within the bracket, the term f²(1−σX0/σe0)/n_T can take either sign, depending on unknown ratios, so the model does not even fix the sign of Rxy without additional input.

   The authors should correct or justify Eqs. (1)-(2), give explicit Rxy and Rxy(drag) for both trion types, and compare them quantitatively with Fig. 3d and Fig. 4b,d. One way is to constrain n_e, n_T and n_X from the gate densities plus Rxx and Rxx(drag), then predict Rxy and Rxy(drag).

3. The integrity of the Hall signal next to diverging longitudinal resistance is not demonstrated. The largest Rxy and Rxy(drag) (Fig. 3a,b, Fig. 4a,c) sit directly beside grey-masked regions where Rxx reaches 10^5-10^7 Ω (Fig. 1d,e, Extended Data Fig. 7). In that regime, several effects can produce a kΩ-scale transverse voltage:
   - a small longitudinal admixture from contact misalignment;
   - capacitive and out-of-phase pickup at 7.33 Hz;
   - preamplifier loading (100 MΩ input impedance against MΩ-scale sample resistance);
   - interlayer AC coupling in the drag configuration (minimized only by a potentiometer).

   Methods do not say whether Rxy was antisymmetrized in field, and Fig. 3c shows only B > 0. A further possibility is interlayer leakage. At Vb ≈ 0.88 V across 1.5-2 nm of hBN, any tunneling current into the Mo layer, which is AC-grounded through the Vb source, would add an electron contribution to the measured "hole-layer" Hall voltage. That would be an alternative, artifactual origin of the negative Rxy.

   The authors should:
   - show Rxy and Rxy(drag) antisymmetrized from ±B data, together with the symmetric parts;
   - report the out-of-phase lock-in components and the criterion used for the grey mask;
   - show the interlayer leakage current against Vb and Vg over the Fig. 3a range;
   - demonstrate Onsager reciprocity of the Hall drag (swap drive and sense layers and reverse B) and linearity in drive amplitude.

B. Should address

4. Each key result rests on a single device, and phase boundaries are transferred between devices. The Hall and Hall-drag results, including the negative Rxy, are shown only for device 2 (Figs. 3, 4, Extended Data Figs. 6, 8). The optics and Rxx(drag) are from device 3 (Fig. 2, Extended Data Fig. 3), and the Rxx maps and the T dependence of Rxx are from device 1 (Fig. 1, Extended Data Fig. 7). The Vb windows differ substantially: the trion region sits near 0.74-0.78 V in device 1, 0.86-0.89 V in device 2 and 0.84-0.89 V in device 3. Yet the dashed boundaries in every device are said to "correspond to those in Fig. 1f." The authors should:
   - show the negative Rxy and the Hall-drag sign change in at least one more device;
   - explain how the triangle boundaries were determined in each device (fitted to that device's data, or copied and rescaled);
   - state whether results survived thermal cycling.

5. The 8 K scale is attributed to trion ionization, but it may reflect the exciton or EI scale. Extended Data Fig. 7b shows that the insulating behaviour around Vg = 0, which includes the EI region, also appears below about 8 K at 4 T, and the Rxy(drag) onset in Fig. 4c is not separated from the onset of exciton drag. The 8 K crossover is converted to a binding energy of 0.7 meV by kBT. Two problems follow:
   - a thermal ionization crossover in a degenerate or dense fluid generally occurs well below Eb/kB (a Saha-type entropy factor), so the conversion is not justified;
   - the result disagrees with the 1.2 meV obtained from the threshold shift.

   The authors should show the T dependence of Rxx(drag), or the drag ratio, inside the EI region alongside the trion regions. They should also discuss why the exciton and trion scales coincide, or differ, and reconcile the two binding-energy estimates.

6. The binding-energy conversion (Methods) rests on assumptions that are not justified. The lever arm C_Q^-1/(C_Q^-1 + C_2L^-1) ≈ 0.05 uses a zero-field, single-carrier 2D density of states for WSe2 holes (m_h = 0.45 m0, gs = 1, gv = 2). The estimate is made at 6 T, where the authors themselves note that the hole cyclotron energy (about 0.8 meV at 4 T, so larger at 6 T) is comparable to the binding energy and the density of states is Landau-quantized. The electron-layer quantum capacitance (bilayer MoSe2) is omitted, and C_2L uses εr ≈ 3.5 with d ≈ 2 nm without uncertainties. The "expected injection threshold without interactions" (red solid line, Fig. 2c, Extended Data Figs. 3-5) is the baseline for the 25 mV shift, yet how it was computed is not stated. The authors should describe how the red line is obtained, include both layers' quantum capacitances at finite field, and quote an uncertainty. Note also that 6 T is where the trion regions are said to vanish in device 2 (Fig. 3c, Extended Data Fig. 6), so the field chosen for the estimate needs comment.

7. A Hall effect (a response linear in B at fixed particle densities) is not demonstrated. Fig. 3c scans B at fixed Vb, where the trion region itself appears near 2 T and disappears near 6 T, so B changes the phase as well as the Lorentz force. A narrow trion region already exists at B = 0 along p = 2n (Fig. 1d). The authors should show Rxy and Rxy(drag) against B at fixed points inside the trion regions, at low fields (for example |B| ≤ 1-2 T, antisymmetrized), tracking the region in (Vg, Vb) if needed. This would establish the low-field Hall slope before Landau quantization and Zeeman polarization set in.

8. The gate calibration underlying Fig. 3d is not described. The dashed electron and hole densities in Fig. 3d come from "calibrated gate capacitances," but Methods give no calibration procedure (Hall density away from the EI? Landau-level fan?) or uncertainty. The 10 K curve shows a peak near Vg ≈ 0.01 V reaching about 2.9 × 10^12 cm^-2, roughly twice the expected hole density. The text calls this a "weak anomaly," which understates it. Because Concerns 1 and 2 call for a quantitative comparison with these densities, the calibration and its uncertainty need to be given, and the 10 K anomaly explained.

9. The interpretation of the electron-hole asymmetry is unsupported. The much larger Rxx in the X+X− region is attributed to holes being "much more tightly bound" in negative trions (p. 4). No evidence is given for this, and the threshold-shift analysis is presented only for positive trions. A corresponding binding-energy estimate for negative trions from Extended Data Fig. 3c would test the claim, and would also bear on the different temperature evolution of the two signs in Fig. 4b.

C. Minor

10. In Fig. 4d, the n-type Rxy(drag) remains at about −0.3 kΩ at 10 K, whereas the text says Rxy(drag) is negligible for T ≳ 8 K. Please comment, or quantify "negligible."
11. The colour scales of Fig. 3a and Fig. 4a saturate at ±10 kΩ, while Fig. 4b shows Rxy ≈ 17 kΩ. Consider extending the scale or noting the saturation.
12. On p. 3, the text cites Fig. 2a as the Rxx(drag) map correlated with the 4 T Rxx map of Fig. 1e, but Fig. 2a is at 6 T and from a different device. The inset of Fig. 2a is labelled Rxx rather than Rxx(drag).
13. The y-axis of Extended Data Fig. 7c is labelled "T (K)" and should be Rxx (Ω).
14. The Extended Data Fig. 1 caption (top gate black dashed, bottom gate red, exciton-contact hBN pink) disagrees with Methods (black and red solid lines are the WSe2 and MoSe2 flakes; the gates are dashed black and red).
15. In the optical Methods, "50nW/µm^-2" should be nW/µm². Also correct "70^0C" and "To further simply the expressions."
16. The conclusion's statement that the vertical quantum-oscillation stripes "demonstrate the survival of exciton binding" is not supported by an analysis in the paper (for example, the oscillation period against Vg compared with the free-hole density). Either add it or soften the wording.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a side-by-side model comparison, trions present against an exciton plus free-carrier mixture without trions, for Rxy, Rxy(drag) and n_H, using independently constrained densities. State which experimental feature only the trion scenario reproduces.
2. Provide a measurement-integrity Extended Data figure: ±B antisymmetrization, out-of-phase components, leakage current, the drag reciprocity check, and the masking criterion.
3. Exploit the B = 0 trion line (Fig. 1d) to measure the low-field Hall slope and Hall drag of the trion fluid. This avoids Landau quantization and Zeeman effects and gives the cleanest test of a Lorentz force on trions.
4. Reproduce Figs. 3d and 4b in a second device and add a single summary table of the devices, their Vb offsets and which figure comes from which device.
