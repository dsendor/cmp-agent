A. Must address

1. The chemical identity and composition of the intercalant are not established. The paper calls the guest α-RuCl3 throughout and describes the samples as "fully characterized" or given a "comprehensive characterization". The only evidence, however, is c-axis expansion and (00l)/(hk0) diffraction indexed by analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 graphite intercalation compounds (Synthesis and Structure analysis sections, pp. 2-3 and 6-7, assumptions 1-4, Table I, Fig. S1). No composition or phase-identity measurement is shown: no EDS/WDS, no XPS, no mass uptake, TGA or chemical analysis giving the C:Ru:Cl ratio, no Raman, and no check for Ag or AgCl incorporation. The growth takes place at 830-900 °C in a Cl2-rich ampoule that contains Ag/AgCl, where Ru chlorides can sublime and react in several ways. A chlorine-rich, reduced or Ag-contaminated ruthenium chloride, or co-intercalated Cl2, therefore cannot be excluded. The layer between the graphene sheets need not be stoichiometric α-RuCl3, and it need not keep the honeycomb Ru network that underlies the Kitaev motivation. The authors should report composition by EDS/WDS on cleaved surfaces and cross-sections, or by weight gain, giving C/RuCl3 for each stage. They should report the Ru oxidation state from XPS. They should show Raman spectra for both stages: the graphite G-band splitting into bounding and interior modes, and its shift, is the standard check of stage and charge transfer in acceptor GICs, and the RuCl3 phonon modes test the intercalant structure. They should also show that no Ag-containing phase is present.

2. The assignment of SdH frequencies to specific DFT pockets depends on an adjustable Fermi-level shift and does not discriminate between models (Electronic properties section, pp. 5-6; Figs. 2g,h and 5). Without the shift, the calculations predict 131 and 444 T for AB|AB (stage-2-like), against one observed peak at 170 T. They predict 70 and 296 T for ABAB|ABAB and 28, 311 and 330 T for ABCA|ABCA (stage-4-like), against observed peaks at 91 and 176 T. Agreement is then reached with a rigid shift of about 0.1 eV, applied only to the stage-4 models (giving 27/183 T and 126/214 T). The sign of the shift is not given, and its physical justification ("plausible variations in interlayer spacing") is not quantified. No shifted result is given for stage 2, so it remains unexplained why the 444 T pocket is absent and why 131 T does not match 170 T. With one free parameter and two or three predicted pockets per model, both stage-4 stackings can be brought close to the data, so the agreement is weak evidence. Stage 2 and stage 4 also show nearly the same main frequency (β ≈ 170 and 176 T), although DFT predicts different pocket sets for the two stages. The simplest reading is a common origin, and this is not discussed. Candidates are the RuCl3-adjacent interface graphene layers, which the authors' own Fig. 3b shows carry most of the depletion in both stages, a minority phase common to both samples, or stage-2-like domains in the stage-4 crystal. The absence of α (91 T) in stage 2 is attributed to "sample-dependent" behaviour, but only one sample per stage is shown. The experiment has more discriminating observables than are used. The authors should:
   - measure temperature-dependent amplitudes (about 1.8-20 K) with Lifshitz-Kosevich fits, and compare the cyclotron mass of each frequency with the DFT band mass of each pocket (m* = ħk_F/v_F for near-Dirac bands);
   - measure the field-angle dependence to test the F ∝ 1/cos θ behaviour expected of quasi-2D graphene-derived pockets, as opposed to 3D or RuCl3-derived orbits;
   - give Dingle temperatures, to judge whether a 444 T pocket could have escaped detection below 9 T;
   - show the Hall data, which the Methods say were measured (antisymmetrized ρxy) but which are not shown. The Hall coefficient and any field nonlinearity give carrier sign and density, which should be compared with the Luttinger count of the observed pockets, with the DFT charge transfer (about 0.09-0.10 e per RuCl3, Fig. S4b), and with published graphene/α-RuCl3 hole densities (refs. 10, 11, 34). For scale, a single fourfold-degenerate Dirac-like pocket at 170 T corresponds to about 1.6 × 10^13 cm^-2 holes per graphene layer and E_F ≈ 0.45-0.5 eV;
   - measure SdH on more than one crystal per stage;
   - state which DFT pocket each measured frequency is assigned to, at which E_F, how the shift was chosen, its sign, and what it implies for the transferred charge, and then state which stacking, if any, the data favour and the residual mismatch.

3. The electronic-structure method makes α-RuCl3 metallic, which undermines both the computed charge transfer and the premise that only graphene states produce the oscillations (Methods, p. 7; Figs. 3-5; Fig. S5b,c). All calculations are non-spin-polarized GGA without U or spin-orbit coupling. At this level the Ru t2g bands sit at the Fermi level: Fig. S5b,c shows RuCl3-derived bands crossing E_F, with dispersive features near K. Yet the main text says α-RuCl3 "remain[s] rather insulating" (p. 5) and that "only the graphene layers are expected to contribute" to the oscillations. In this setup the transferred charge, which sets the Dirac-point shift and therefore every pocket area, is fixed by aligning the graphene Dirac point with a spurious metallic Ru manifold. Its position is known to depend on U, SOC and magnetic order. Earlier work on graphene/α-RuCl3, including refs. 12 and 35 by some of the same authors, used GGA+U+SOC with zigzag order for this reason. Since the paper itself concludes that the frequencies are "highly sensitive" to charge transfer, this choice directly affects the comparison in concern 2. The authors should repeat at least the A|A, AB|AB and ABAB|ABAB models with GGA+U(+SOC) in a zigzag or ferromagnetic configuration, or justify why non-magnetic GGA reproduces the charge transfer. They should report how the Dirac-point shift and pocket frequencies change with U, and state whether any RuCl3-derived Fermi-surface sheets remain that could themselves account for the 91 T or 170 T orbits.

4. The stage and, especially, the graphene stacking assignments are not determined by the data shown (Table I, Fig. 1, Fig. S1, assumptions 1-4 in Methods). Only 00l reflections (powder) and hk0 reflections (single crystal) are observed; l ≠ 0 reflections are absent by geometry (Fig. S1 caption). The 00l indices listed are all even for stage 2 (004, 006, 008, 0012, 0022) and all multiples of 3 for stage 4 (006, 0012, 0015, 0018, 0033, 0036, 0051). The data therefore establish identity periods of about 12.7 Å and 19.4 Å, consistent with a roughly 9.35-9.4 Å intercalant sandwich plus one or three 3.35 Å graphite gaps, but only once the analogy is accepted. The doubled and tripled cells (25.4 Å and 58.2 Å) and the stacking sequences in Table I (A|AB|B; "AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB") follow from assumption 4, not from the measurements. For stage 4 the paper explicitly cannot tell Bernal from rhombohedral blocks. The in-plane description is also internally inconsistent. Assumption 1 says the RuCl3 layer is incommensurate with graphite, yet assumption 2 and the indexing use a commensurate supercell: the hk0 reflections of both stages are indexed with a common hexagonal cell, a = 29.62(3) Å and 29.5(4) Å, close to 12×12 graphene / 5×5 RuCl3 (29.62/2.46 ≈ 12.0; 29.62/5.97 ≈ 4.96). The values of N, M, n and m are never stated. Residual graphite is visible in Fig. S1, but the fraction of graphite and of neighbouring stages is not quantified. It is also not stated whether the transport crystals were the ones characterized by XRD. This matters because the paper's central theory result is that "the stacking sequence controls the number and size of Fermi hole pockets", and the title, abstract and conclusions link the samples to rhombohedral flat-band physics. If the stacking is not measured, the SdH comparison cannot test that statement. The authors should collect l ≠ 0 reflections (rotation, rocking, or tilted single-crystal or Laue geometry) to determine the stacking and its order, or present the stacking as a hypothesis and remove claims that depend on rhombohedral stacking. They should state the in-plane supercell explicitly and resolve the commensurate/incommensurate contradiction, quantify phase fractions by fitting the 00l series, and check the staging of the transport specimens.

5. The DFT geometries are not tied to the measured c-axis periods, and the stated parameters conflict with each other (Methods, p. 7; pp. 4-5; SI; Fig. S4a). The main text fixes d_Gr-RuCl3 = 3.60 Å "suggested by X-ray data" and d_Gr-Gr = 3.54 Å. The XRD gives only the identity periods, however, and 3.54 Å is larger than graphite's 3.35 Å. The SI instead uses d_Gr-RuCl3 = 3.765 Å and a graphene-graphene distance of 10.241 Å across the RuCl3 layer. It is not stated whether d_Gr-RuCl3 is measured from the Ru plane or the Cl plane. The XRD repeats imply an intercalant sandwich of about 9.35 Å (12.7 − 3.35 ≈ 19.4 − 3 × 3.35 ≈ 9.35 Å), but Fig. S4a places the "experimental" A|A reference near c ≈ 10.0 Å. Fig. S4a shows the charge transfer changing by about 10% over c = 9.3-10.5 Å, with hole doping increasing as c shrinks. Using the experimental spacing would therefore push the computed frequencies further above experiment, against the direction needed for agreement. The SI text says the charge transfer "is strongly affected" by this distance, while the main text calls it insensitive, and the paper itself states that the frequencies are "highly sensitive to the interlayer distances". The authors should tabulate, for each model, the c-axis repeat against the measured 25.4/2 Å and 58.2/3 Å. They should use one consistent set of distances, defined in a figure, and recompute charge transfer and pocket frequencies at the experimental sandwich thickness. They should report the sensitivity of the frequencies to d_Gr-RuCl3 (and to U, concern 3), which would turn the 0.1 eV shift of concern 2 into a calculated quantity or show that it cannot be one.

B. Should address

6. The "platform for exotic quantum phases" framing in the title, abstract and conclusions, including the Kitaev and spin-liquid motivation, is not supported by any measurement. No magnetic susceptibility, magnetization, specific heat, or transport anomaly near the bulk α-RuCl3 ordering temperature (about 7 K) is shown, although the SdH data at 1.8 K are taken below that temperature. The paper does not establish whether the intercalated RuCl3 layers keep local moments, order magnetically, or remain Kitaev-like after receiving about 0.1 e/f.u. (Fig. S4b). Nor does it probe flat bands or correlated behaviour of the graphene blocks, and the rhombohedral (ABCA) stacking is not experimentally identified (concern 4). The authors should add χ(T) (H ∥ ab and H ∥ c) and/or C(T) for both stages, compared with pristine α-RuCl3, together with low-field ρ(T) around 2-15 K. Otherwise they should narrow the title, abstract and conclusions to what is shown: a new staged intercalation compound with strongly hole-doped graphene layers.

7. The novelty claim conflicts with the paper's own reference list. The Conclusions (p. 6) state that the compound was synthesized "for the first time". However, ref. 27 (Postnikov et al., J. Struct. Chem. 18, 819 (1977)) is titled "Synthesis and structure of layer-type graphite compounds with ruthenium, palladium, osmium chlorides", and ref. 30 covers intercalation of metal chlorides more broadly. The authors should discuss what that earlier work reported (staging, c axis, composition, in-plane structure) and state precisely what is new. If earlier RuCl3-graphite compounds exist, the novelty should be reframed around single-crystal growth with staging control, the coincidence-lattice structure, identification of the α-phase honeycomb layer, or the quantum-oscillation characterization.

8. The controls do not rule out doping by chlorine or growth by-products, and they are not matched to the intercalated samples (p. 2, Synthesis; p. 3, Transport; Fig. 2b,c). A control is needed to show that the high-frequency pocket comes from RuCl3 intercalation and not from Cl2 exposure or chlorine/AgCl-derived species. The Cl2-annealed Kish graphite in Fig. 2b is used only for zero-field ρ(T); its c-axis XRD, magnetoresistance and SdH spectrum are not shown. The controls also differ from the samples. The intercalation host is natural graphite flakes from HQ Graphene, whereas the transport reference and the annealed control are Kish graphite, so differences in mosaicity, defect density and RRR enter the comparison. The control was also annealed at 900 °C "for several days", whereas the growth runs lasted 22-48 h. The authors should show XRD and SdH (at 1.8 K and 9 T) and Hall data for the actual host flakes, both pristine and Cl2-annealed under the growth time and temperature profile. Ideally they should also show a sample grown with AgCl but no RuCl3 under the same two-zone conditions.

9. Sample statistics and reproducibility are not reported (Fig. 2b-e). Fig. 2 shows one sample per stage, and the stage-4 and stage-2 samples come from different growth durations. The paper does not say how many growths and samples per stage were measured, whether the 91 T and 170-176 T frequencies recur across samples and batches, or how often the time-to-stage mapping (22 h → stage 4, 48 h → stage 2) reproduces. Stage 4 is missing from Fig. 2b and appears only in Fig. 2e,h. The residual resistivity at 1.8 K differs by about 15× between stage 2 (about 10 µΩ cm, Fig. 2d) and stage 4 (about 150 µΩ cm, Fig. 2e), and the magnetoresistance shape also differs (near-linear compared with quadratic). The text attributes this only to geometric "uncertainties" and acknowledges that cutting changes the RRR. Metal-chloride GICs can deintercalate, but air and thermal-cycling stability are not reported. The authors should report the number of samples and the spread of each frequency, add stage-4 ρ(T), discuss the resistivity difference, and comment on stability.

10. The FFT analysis is not described, and the harmonic labels do not match twice the fundamentals (Fig. 2f-h; p. 3). In Fig. 2g the peak labelled 2β is at 359 T, whereas 2 × 170 T = 340 T. In Fig. 2h, 2β = 347 T, whereas 2 × 176 T = 352 T. For Kish graphite, "2ν1 = 9.3 T" compares with 2 × 4.13 = 8.26 T and coincides with ν1 + ν2 = 9.29 T. Oscillations appear above 4 T, so the window is presumably about 4-9 T, giving a resolution of about 1/(1/4 − 1/9) ≈ 7 T. The 19 T stage-2 offset is therefore well beyond resolution, and the 359 T peak may be a separate pocket, such as the second DFT pocket, which matters for concern 2. Conversely, the resolution is comparable to the stage-2/stage-4 difference in β (170 vs 176 T) and to the 347/352 T mismatch. For Kish graphite, ν1 = 4.13 T and ν2 = 5.16 T are about 1 T apart, comparable to the roughly 1.8 T resolution of a 1.5-9 T window. α (91 T) has only about 12 periods in the window. The authors should show the background-subtracted Δρ against 1/B, and state the polynomial order, field windows, windowing, padding, resulting resolution and frequency uncertainties. They should show that α and β are robust to these choices and revisit the harmonic labels.

11. The charge-transfer and strain numbers are not mutually consistent. In Fig. S4a the graphene loses about 0.0143-0.0144 e per C for A|A in the strained 2×2 RuCl3 / 5×5 graphene cell. With 50 C per cell this is about 0.72 e, or about 0.18 e per RuCl3 formula unit, yet Fig. S4b gives about 0.089 e/f.u. on RuCl3 for the same geometry. Unless the normalizations differ, which should then be stated, charge neutrality is not satisfied. The Bader charge should also be compared with the hole density implied by the computed pocket areas (Luttinger count). The strain is quoted as about 4% tensile on RuCl3 with graphene fixed at 1.42 Å in the main text, but as about 1% with a stretched C-C distance of 1.434 Å in the SI. For the 2×2/5×5 cell the arithmetic gives about 3%. The authors should reconcile these numbers, state the normalization of each figure, and state which layer is strained in each model, and by how much.

12. The computed quantum-oscillation frequencies have an unquantified systematic error (Methods; Fig. 5). Fermi contours are drawn by joining the "highest intensity points" of smeared, broadened unfolded spectra, and the paper says only that this introduces a "small systematic error". The pockets in Fig. 5c-e overlap within the broadening. For the small pockets (28 T, 70 T) and the closely spaced 311/330 T pair, this error could change the pocket assignment. Only A|A is cross-checked with the dhva/tricubic method. The authors should give an uncertainty for each computed frequency, for example by varying broadening, k-mesh and contour choice. They should extend the dhva cross-check to the multilayer models, especially stage 2, where the comparison matters most, state at which k_z the extremal areas are taken, and report any k_z dispersion.
