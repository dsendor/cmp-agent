Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports vapor-transport growth of graphite intercalated with RuCl3 layers, using AgCl photolysis as a Cl2 source. It assigns stage-2 and stage-4 phases from X-ray diffraction, reports Shubnikov–de Haas (SdH) frequencies of about 170–176 T (both stages) and 91 T (stage 4 only), and compares these with unfolded non-spin-polarized GGA Fermi surfaces for several model stackings. A bulk, three-dimensional analogue of graphene/α-RuCl3 heterostructures is an attractive idea. It could combine the large charge transfer seen in those heterostructures with the stacking-dependent band physics of multilayer graphene. In its present form the work is a preliminary synthesis report. The identity and structure of the intercalant are not pinned down. The DFT–SdH agreement depends on an adjustable Fermi-level shift. Nothing is measured about the magnetic or correlated properties that the title and abstract promise.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A simple, reproducible-sounding route (two-zone CVT with photolytically generated Cl2) gives graphite with a strongly expanded c axis. Single-crystal (hk0) patterns show in-plane superstructure reflections consistent with a 5×5 RuCl3 / 12×12 graphene coincidence cell (29.62 Å / 2.46 Å ≈ 12.0; 29.62 Å / 5.97 Å ≈ 4.96).
- The SdH data show a clear change from pristine Kish graphite (4–5 T) to intercalated samples (about 170 T and 91 T). This is direct evidence of a much larger Fermi surface, consistent with substantial hole doping of the graphene layers.
- The DFT part is systematic. It covers several stackings, benchmarks the strained reduced cell against the unstrained 5×5/12×12 cell, unfolds onto graphene states and gives layer-resolved charge redistribution. It is a useful starting point for interpreting future experiments on this material class.
- The topic connects two active communities (intercalation compounds and α-RuCl3/graphene proximity physics).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The chemical identity of the intercalant is not established. The paper calls it α-RuCl3 throughout and calls the result "comprehensive characterization", but offers no composition or phase-identity measurement.
   - Where: Synthesis section, Structure analysis section, Table I, Fig. S1.
   - What is missing: the only structural evidence is (00l) and (hk0) diffraction indexed by analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 compounds. There is no EDS/WDS or XPS, no mass uptake or chemical analysis giving the C:Ru:Cl ratio, no Raman, and no check for Ag or AgCl incorporation. Ag is present in the ampoule.
   - Why it matters: at 830–900 °C in excess Cl2, Ru chlorides can sublime and react in several ways. The layered phase that ends up between graphene sheets need not be stoichiometric α-RuCl3, and it need not keep the honeycomb Ru network that underlies the "Kitaev" motivation.
   - What to do:
     - Report the composition: EDS/WDS on cleaved surfaces and cross-sections, or weight gain, giving C/RuCl3 per stage.
     - Report Ru oxidation state (XPS).
     - Show Raman spectra (graphite G-band splitting into bounding/interior modes is a standard stage check; RuCl3 phonon modes test the intercalant structure).
     - Show that no Ag-containing phase is present.

2. The stage and, especially, the graphene stacking assignments are not determined by the data shown.
   - Where: Table I, Fig. 1, Fig. S1, and assumptions 1–4 in Methods.
   - Stage: c = 25.4 Å and 58.2 Å are consistent with stages 2 and 4 if one assumes a sandwich thickness of about 9.4 Å and 3.35 Å graphite spacing. But the stage follows from the c-axis value only once that analogy is accepted. The powder patterns are described as "highly oriented" and the single-crystal patterns show residual graphite. The fraction of graphite and of other stages is not quantified.
   - Stacking: only l = 0 reflections are observed (Fig. S1 caption). The XRD therefore cannot separate AB from ABC stacking of the graphene blocks, or the A|A registry across the RuCl3 layer. For stage 4, Table I itself lists two alternatives ("AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB").
   - Internal contradiction: assumption 1 states the RuCl3 layer is incommensurate with graphite, while assumption 2 and the indexing use a commensurate n×m / N×M supercell.
   - Why it matters: the paper's central theory result is that "the stacking sequence controls the number and size of Fermi hole pockets". If the stacking is not measured, the SdH comparison cannot test that statement.
   - What to do:
     - Collect l ≠ 0 reflections (tilted or rotation single-crystal geometry) to determine the stacking and its order.
     - Quantify phase fractions (graphite, neighboring stages) by fitting the (00l) series.
     - Resolve the commensurate/incommensurate contradiction.
     - State explicitly whether the crystals used for transport were the ones characterized by XRD.

3. The agreement between DFT and the SdH frequencies is produced by an adjustable Fermi-level shift and does not discriminate between models.
   - Where: Electronic properties section; Figs. 2g–h and 5.
   - Unshifted predictions against the data:

     | Model | Predicted pockets (T) | Observed (T) |
     |---|---|---|
     | AB\|AB (S2-like) | 131, 444 | 170 |
     | ABAB\|ABAB (S4-like) | 70, 296 | 91, 176 |
     | ABCA\|ABCA (S4-like) | 28, 311, 330 | 91, 176 |

   - The text then applies a ~0.1 eV shift, and gives shifted values only for the stage-4 models (27/183 T and 126/214 T). The direction of the shift and the physical justification ("plausible variations in interlayer spacing") are not quantified. No shifted result is given for stage 2, where the predicted 444 T pocket is not observed. With one free parameter and two or three predicted pockets per model, both stage-4 stackings can be brought near the data.
   - The near-identical β frequency in S2 (170 T) and S4 (176 T) is not discussed. DFT predicts quite different pockets for the two stages, and the similarity could equally indicate a shared, stage-independent pocket or S2-like domains in the S4 crystal.
   - The absence of α in S2 is called "sample-dependent", but only one sample per stage appears to have been measured.
   - What to do:
     - Measure the SdH temperature dependence (1.8–20 K) to extract effective masses and compare them with DFT band masses for each model and pocket.
     - Measure the field-angle dependence to confirm the expected F ∝ 1/cos θ behaviour of quasi-2D graphene-derived pockets.
     - Give Dingle temperatures, to judge whether a 444 T pocket could have escaped detection below 9 T.
     - Report how the Fermi-level shift is chosen, its sign, and what it implies for the carrier count.
     - Then state which (if any) stacking the data favour, with the residual mismatch.

4. The electronic-structure method makes the RuCl3 layer metallic, which undermines both the charge-transfer magnitude and the premise that only graphene states produce the oscillations.
   - Where: Methods; Figs. 3–5; Fig. S5b,c.
   - The problem: all calculations are non-spin-polarized GGA without U and without spin-orbit coupling. In this approximation α-RuCl3 is metallic: Fig. S5b,c shows RuCl3-derived bands crossing E_F, with dispersive features near K. Yet the paper argues that α-RuCl3 remains "rather insulating" and that "only the graphene layers are expected to contribute" to the quantum oscillations.
   - Why it matters: the amount of transferred charge, which sets the Dirac-point shift and so every pocket area, is controlled by where the empty Ru t2g states lie relative to the graphene Dirac point. That position is known to depend on U, SOC and magnetic order. The authors' own earlier work (Ref. 12) used GGA+U+SOC with zigzag order for this reason.
   - What to do:
     - Repeat at least the A|A and the stage-2/stage-4 models with GGA+U(+SOC), in a magnetic (e.g. zigzag) or appropriately constrained configuration.
     - Show how the Dirac-point shift and pocket frequencies change with U.
     - Show that no RuCl3-derived Fermi-surface sheets remain that could account for the 91 T or 170 T orbits.

5. The novelty claim conflicts with the authors' own reference list.
   - Where: the Conclusions state that the compound was synthesized "for the first time".
   - The conflict: Ref. 27 (Postnikov et al., J. Struct. Chem. 18, 819 (1977)) is titled "Synthesis and structure of layer-type graphite compounds with ruthenium, palladium, osmium chlorides".
   - What to do: discuss explicitly what that and related earlier work reported (stage, c-axis, composition) and how the present compound differs. If earlier RuCl3–graphite compounds exist, reframe the novelty around single-crystal growth, the coincidence-lattice structure and the quantum-oscillation characterization.

B. Should address

6. The Hall data were measured but are not shown, and they are the most direct test of the charge-transfer picture.
   - Where: Methods state that transverse resistivities were antisymmetrized.
   - What they would test: the sign and density of carriers from the Hall coefficient (and its field nonlinearity, if several pockets are present), checked against the Luttinger count from the SdH frequencies and the DFT charge transfer (Fig. S4b, about 0.09–0.10 e per RuCl3).
   - A scale for the comparison: a single 170 T Dirac-like pocket with fourfold degeneracy corresponds to about 1.6 × 10^13 cm^-2 per layer and E_F ≈ 0.45–0.5 eV. Please include ρxy(B) for both stages and this comparison.

7. The title and abstract claim a "platform for exotic quantum phases" and invoke Kitaev and spin-liquid physics, but no magnetic, thermodynamic or correlated-electron property is measured.
   - Neither the RuCl3 layers' local moments nor magnetic order is checked. Bulk α-RuCl3 orders at about 7 K, and the SdH data at 1.8 K are below that temperature.
   - What to do: provide magnetic susceptibility (H ∥ ab and H ∥ c) and/or specific heat for both stages, with ρ(T) near 7 K, to show whether the intercalated layers order, and whether the charge transfer of about 0.1 e/f.u. leaves them magnetic. Otherwise, tone down the title and abstract to what is shown.

8. Sample statistics and controls in transport are thin.
   - Coverage: Fig. 2b shows ρ(T) for Kish, Cl2-annealed Kish and stage 2, but not stage 4. Stage 4 appears only in Fig. 2e,h. The paper does not say how many samples per stage were measured, or whether the 91 T and 170–176 T frequencies recur across samples and growth batches.
   - Missing control: the Cl2-annealed graphite control is used only for ρ(T). Its magnetoresistance and FFT are not shown, but they are the relevant null control for the claim that the SdH changes come from RuCl3 intercalation rather than Cl or AgCl exposure.
   - The synthesis time-to-stage mapping (22 h → stage 4, 48 h → stage 2) is given without stating how often it reproduces.
   - What to do: report the number of samples and the spread of F, show the annealed-graphite SdH, add stage-4 ρ(T), and comment on air stability or deintercalation, which is common in metal-chloride graphite intercalation compounds.

9. The structural input to DFT is not tied to the measured c axes, and the values quoted are inconsistent between the main text and the Supplement.
   - The main text fixes d_Gr–RuCl3 ≈ 3.60 Å and d_Gr–Gr = 3.54 Å "suggested by X-ray data". The XRD in fact gives only c, and 3.54 Å is larger than graphite's 3.35 Å.
   - The SI uses d_Gr–RuCl3 = 3.765 Å and d_Gr–Gr = 10.241 Å.
   - It is not stated whether d_Gr–RuCl3 is measured from the Ru plane or the Cl plane.
   - Fig. S4a shows the charge transfer changing by about 10% over c = 9.3–10.5 Å, and the SI text says the charge transfer "is strongly affected" by this distance. The main text describes it as insensitive.
   - What to do: give the c axis of each model cell and compare it with 25.4/2 Å and 58.2/3 Å. Use one consistent set of distances, define them in a figure, and give the sensitivity of the pocket frequencies to d_Gr–RuCl3 directly, since this is the stated reason for the Fermi-level shift.

10. The charge-transfer numbers may not be mutually consistent and should be checked.
    - Fig. S4a gives about −0.0143 e per C for A|A in the strained 2×2 RuCl3 / 5×5 graphene cell, which is about 0.72 e per 50 C, i.e. about 0.18 e per RuCl3 formula unit.
    - Fig. S4b gives 0.089 e/f.u. on RuCl3 for the same A|A geometry.
    - Unless the normalizations differ (which should then be stated), charge neutrality is not satisfied.
    - The strain is quoted as ≈4% in the main text and ≈1% in the SI. These appear to refer to different cells, but this is not stated.
    - Please reconcile and state the normalization of each figure.

11. The extraction of quantum-oscillation frequencies from smeared unfolded spectral functions needs error bars.
    - Where: Methods; Fig. 5.
    - Fermi contours are drawn by joining the "highest intensity points" of broadened unfolded spectra. The resulting "small systematic error" is not quantified. Only A|A is cross-checked with the dhva code.
    - The pockets in Fig. 5c–e overlap within the broadening, which is exactly where pocket assignment is ambiguous.
    - What to do: give an uncertainty for each computed frequency (from broadening, k-mesh and contour choice), extend the dhva cross-check to at least one multilayer model, and report k_z dispersion if any.

12. Details of the SdH analysis are missing.
    - Where: Fig. 2f–h and the text on page 3.
    - Not given: the field window used for the FFT (the text says oscillations appear above 4 T, so about 4–9 T), the background polynomial order, the windowing, and the resulting frequency resolution (roughly 7 T for a 4–9 T window).
    - Why it matters: the resolution is comparable to the S2/S4 difference in β (170 vs 176 T) and to the 2β vs 2×β mismatch (347 vs 352 T). The paper also does not say whether α (91 T, only about 12 periods in the window) survives changes of background and window.
    - What to do: show the background-subtracted oscillations against 1/B, and state the resolution so readers can judge whether β differs between stages.

C. Minor

13. Several labelling errors need correcting:
    - Table I caption: "α-CuI3 layer" should be RuCl3.
    - Structure section: "29.5(A) Å" should read 29.5(4) Å.
    - Fig. 2a: "Graphte" should be "Graphite".
    - Fig. S1 caption: it calls (a,b) the XRD images and (c,d) the integrated profiles, but the figure shows the reverse.
    - Fig. S3 caption: it refers to "supercells S1 and S2", but the SI text calls these orientations A and B. "S1/S2" clashes with the stage labels S2/S4.

14. Units should be checked across Fig. 2. Fig. 2c gives ρxx of Kish graphite in mΩ cm, Fig. 2b in μΩ cm, and Fig. 2d,e in μΩ cm. Please confirm the units, and, since absolute values are said to be unreliable, consider also plotting MR ratios.

15. RRR is defined as R(300 K)/R(2 K), but the lowest temperature is 1.8 K. RRR values are mentioned without being given. Please list RRR for every sample, since mechanical damage is said to change it.

16. Fig. 2b shows the Cl2-annealed Kish graphite but does not discuss it quantitatively. State what change, if any, annealing alone produced.

17. The notation for stackings should be harmonized and explained: "A|AB|B", "AB|BCAB|BC", "AB|BABA|AB", and the reduction to "AB|AB" and "ABAB|ABAB". The paper should also justify the claim that the cross-RuCl3 registry (AA in Table I, versus not AA in the DFT AB|AB model) is irrelevant for the pocket structure, not only for the charge transfer.

18. Methods: say whether the "Γ-point" relaxations were checked against a denser k-mesh for the 5×5/12×12 cell. Give the D3 setting used in the final band calculations, and state whether van der Waals corrections affect the fixed interlayer distances.

19. Consider depositing the raw transport and XRD data rather than offering them "upon reasonable request".

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a compact composition and phase-identity package: EDS/WDS or mass uptake, Raman (graphite G-band splitting for staging and RuCl3 modes), and XPS for the Ru valence. Add XRD with l ≠ 0 reflections to fix the stacking. Together these turn the synthesis claim into a solid structural result.
2. Turn the SdH data into a quantitative test: temperature-dependent amplitudes (masses), angle dependence (dimensionality), Hall data (carrier sign and density), and several samples per stage. Then compare masses, frequencies and carrier counts with DFT without a free Fermi-level shift, or with a shift fixed independently by the Hall density.
3. Redo the key DFT models with GGA+U+SOC and magnetic order, and report how sensitive the pocket frequencies are to U and to d_Gr–RuCl3. This would also test whether the RuCl3 layers stay insulating in the calculation.
4. Either add a magnetic or thermodynamic measurement (susceptibility, specific heat) showing what state the intercalated RuCl3 layers are in, or reframe the title and abstract around the demonstrated result: a new staged intercalation compound with strongly hole-doped graphene layers.
