Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports vapor-transport growth of graphite intercalated with RuCl3 layers (stage 2 and stage 4, "C-RuCl3"), using AgCl photolysis to supply Cl2. It characterizes the material with X-ray diffraction and Shubnikov-de Haas (SdH) oscillations, and interprets the results with non-spin-polarized GGA calculations of charge transfer and graphene-derived hole pockets. A bulk analogue of the graphene/alpha-RuCl3 heterostructure would interest the community: it would give bulk-sensitive probes access to charge-transfer-doped multilayer graphene next to a Kitaev magnet. As submitted, the work is a first synthesis and characterization report. The identity and structure of the intercalant, the assignment of the SdH frequencies, and the framing as a platform for "exotic quantum phases" are not yet well supported.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A simple and potentially reproducible synthesis route. The AgCl/UV chlorine source is a useful practical detail, and the time dependence (22 h gives stage 4, 48 h gives stage 2) is reported.
- The c-axis expansions are large and clearly indexed (25.4 Å and 58.2 Å; Fig. S1). The 00l reflections follow l = 2n for stage 2 and l = 3n for stage 4, which gives identity periods of about 12.7 Å and 19.4 Å. These fit a roughly 9.4 Å intercalant sandwich plus one or three graphene gaps, as expected for stage 2 and stage 4.
- SdH frequencies of 170 to 176 T are clearly resolved, far above graphite's 4 to 5 T. This is direct evidence of a large Fermi surface reconstruction consistent with heavy hole doping.
- DFT of several stacking geometries gives a clear qualitative picture: charge is concentrated on the interface graphene layers, and the number of hole pockets depends on stacking.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The intercalant's identity, composition and in-plane structure are not established. The paper's case that the guest is alpha-RuCl3 rests on c-axis expansion and an analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 GICs (pp. 6-7, assumptions 1-4). No chemical or compositional analysis is shown: no EDX/WDX, mass uptake, XPS or Raman. The growth also takes place in a Cl2-rich ampoule containing Ag/AgCl, so a chlorine-rich ruthenium chloride, co-intercalated Cl2, or Ag-containing species cannot be excluded. The in-plane description is also internally inconsistent. Assumption 1 says the RuCl3 layer is "incommensurate with the graphite host". Yet the hk0 reflections of both stages are indexed with a single common hexagonal cell, a = 29.62(3) Å and 29.5(4) Å (Table I, Fig. S1c,d), which implies a commensurate supercell close to 12×12 graphene / 5×5 RuCl3. The values of N, M, n and m are never stated. Without the l ≠ 0 reflections (absent by geometry, Fig. S1 caption), the stacking sequences in Table I come from the assumed analogy, not from data. For stage 4 the paper explicitly cannot tell Bernal from rhombohedral blocks ("AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB"). The title, abstract and conclusions call the samples "fully characterized" and link them to rhombohedral flat-band physics, which needs both points settled. What to do:
   - Add compositional data: EDX/WDX on cleaved crystals, and ideally XPS for the Ru valence and Cl content.
   - Add Raman spectra. The G-band splitting and shift are the standard check of stage and charge transfer in acceptor GICs.
   - State the in-plane supercell explicitly and reconcile it with the "incommensurate" assumption.
   - Either obtain l ≠ 0 data (rotation, or tilted single-crystal or Laue geometry) to fix the graphene stacking, or remove claims that depend on rhombohedral stacking.
   - Quantify the residual graphite and any mixed-stage fraction seen in Fig. S1.

2. The assignment of SdH frequencies to specific DFT pockets rests on one adjustable Fermi-level shift and leaves several mismatches. Unshifted DFT gives 131 T and 444 T for stage 2, against one measured 170 T peak. It gives 70/296 T (ABAB) or 28/311/330 T (ABCA) for stage 4, against 91 and 176 T (p. 6). Agreement is then obtained by shifting E_F by about 0.1 eV, and only for the stage-4 geometries. No shift is reported for stage 2, so it remains unexplained there why the 444 T pocket is absent and 131 T does not match 170 T. With one free parameter and one or two measured frequencies, the agreement does not discriminate between models. The text also attributes the missing alpha peak to "sample-dependent" behaviour, but only one sample per stage is shown. The experiment has more discriminating observables than are used:
   - Temperature-dependent oscillation amplitudes give the cyclotron mass of each frequency. This can be compared with the band velocity of each DFT pocket (m* = ħk_F/v_F for near-Dirac bands).
   - The angle dependence of each frequency would show whether it scales as 1/cos(theta), as expected for a graphene-derived quasi-2D pocket, rather than coming from a 3D pocket or a RuCl3-derived band.
   - The Hall data were measured (the Methods say transverse resistivities were antisymmetrized) but are not shown. They give the carrier sign and density, which can be checked against the Luttinger count of the observed pockets. For example, a single fourfold-degenerate pocket at 170 T corresponds to about 1.6×10^13 cm^-2 holes per graphene layer.

   Please add T-dependent amplitudes with Lifshitz-Kosevich fits, angle-dependent FFTs, and Hall data. Also state explicitly which DFT pocket each measured frequency is assigned to, at which E_F, and what the shift implies for the transferred charge.

3. The electronic-structure model does not represent alpha-RuCl3 adequately for the quantity that drives the result, which is charge transfer. All calculations are non-spin-polarized GGA without U or spin-orbit coupling (Methods, p. 7). At that level alpha-RuCl3 is metallic, and the supplementary bands show RuCl3-derived states crossing E_F (Fig. S5b,c). The main text nevertheless says the RuCl3 layer remains "rather insulating" (p. 5). In this setup the amount of charge transferred is set by aligning the graphene Dirac point with a spurious metallic Ru t2g manifold. Earlier work on graphene/alpha-RuCl3, including references 12 and 35 by some of the same authors, used GGA+U+SOC with magnetic order for this reason. Since the paper's own conclusion is that the frequencies are "highly sensitive" to charge transfer, this choice directly affects the comparison in Concern 2. The authors should:
   - repeat at least the stage-2 and one stage-4 geometry in GGA+SOC+U with zigzag (or ferromagnetic) order, or justify why non-magnetic GGA reproduces the charge transfer;
   - report how the pocket areas change;
   - state whether any RuCl3-derived Fermi surface sheets exist, since these could themselves produce oscillations.

4. The DFT geometries are not matched to the measured c-axis periods, and the stated parameters conflict with each other. The main text fixes d_Gr-RuCl3 = 3.60 Å "suggested by X-ray data" and d_Gr-Gr = 3.54 Å (p. 7). The XRD gives only the identity periods, about 12.7 Å (stage 2) and about 19.4 Å (stage 4), so the paper should show how 3.60 Å was derived from them. The SI uses d_Gr-RuCl3 = 3.765 Å and a graphene-graphene distance of 10.241 Å across the RuCl3 layer. Fig. S4a marks the reference at c ≈ 10.0 Å, while the measured sandwich thickness is about 9.35 Å (12.7 − 3.35). Fig. S4a also shows hole doping increasing as c shrinks. Using the experimental spacing would therefore push the computed frequencies further above experiment, against the direction needed for agreement. The authors should:
   - tabulate, for each model, the c-axis repeat compared with the measured one;
   - recompute charge transfer and frequencies at the experimental sandwich thickness;
   - present the 0.1 eV shift (Concern 2) together with this result.

B. Should address

5. The Cl2-annealed control is shown only in the zero-field resistivity. A control is needed to show that the high-frequency pocket comes from RuCl3 intercalation and not from chlorine doping or chlorine/AgCl-derived species introduced during growth. The Cl2-annealed Kish graphite in Fig. 2b is a good start but is used only for rho(T). Please show XRD (c-axis) and SdH for the annealed control at 1.8 K and 9 T. Ideally also show a sample grown with AgCl but no RuCl3, under the same two-zone conditions.

6. Reproducibility and sample count are not reported. Fig. 2 shows one sample per stage, and the stage-4 and stage-2 data come from different growth durations. The zero-field resistivity at 1.8 K differs by about 15× between stage 2 (about 10 µΩ cm, Fig. 2d) and stage 4 (about 150 µΩ cm, Fig. 2e). The magnetoresistance shape also differs (near-linear compared with quadratic). The text attributes only "uncertainties" to geometry. Stage 4 is also missing from Fig. 2b. Please state:
   - how many growths and how many samples per stage were measured;
   - whether the 170 to 176 T and 91 T frequencies recur from sample to sample;
   - the air and thermal-cycling stability of the intercalate;
   - rho(T) for stage 4.

7. The FFT peak assignments are not consistent with the stated harmonics. In Fig. 2g the peak labelled 2β is at 359 T, while 2 × 170 T = 340 T. In Fig. 2h 2β = 347 T, while 2 × 176 = 352 T. For a 4 to 9 T window the FFT resolution is about 1/(1/4 − 1/9) ≈ 7 T. The stage-2 offset (about 19 T) is therefore well beyond resolution and may be a separate pocket, such as the second DFT pocket. In graphite, the "2ν1 = 9.3 T" peak coincides with ν1 + ν2 = 9.29 T rather than 2ν1 = 8.26 T. Please:
   - show the background-subtracted oscillations against 1/B;
   - state the polynomial order, window and padding used;
   - give frequency uncertainties;
   - revisit the harmonic labels.

8. The charge-transfer numbers should be checked for internal consistency. In Fig. S4a the graphene layer loses about 0.0144 e per C in the A|A geometry. With 50 C per 2×2 RuCl3 cell (5×5 graphene) this is about 0.18 e per RuCl3 formula unit, yet Fig. S4b gives about 0.089 e per formula unit on RuCl3 for A|A. Please reconcile this, and compare the Bader charge with the hole density implied by the computed pocket areas (Luttinger count). The stated strain is also inconsistent:
   - The main text says about 4% tensile strain on RuCl3 with graphene fixed at 1.42 Å.
   - The SI reports about 1% and a stretched C-C distance of 1.434 Å.
   - For the 2×2/5×5 cell the arithmetic gives about 3%.

   Please state which layer is strained in each model, and by how much.

9. The novelty claim and framing go beyond the evidence. Ref. 27 (Postnikov et al., 1977) already reports layered graphite compounds with ruthenium chlorides, so "synthesized ... for the first time" needs to be reconciled with that work. The title and abstract present the material as a platform for exotic correlated, magnetic and topological phases. However, no measurement probes the RuCl3 layers' magnetism, such as susceptibility, magnetization, specific heat, or a transport anomaly near 7 K. Nor does any measurement probe flat bands or correlated behaviour of the graphene blocks, and the rhombohedral stacking is not established (Concern 1). Please either add basic magnetic characterization (for example chi(T) for both stages compared with pristine alpha-RuCl3, to show whether the doped layers order) or narrow the claims to synthesis and charge-transfer doping.

C. Minor

10. Table I caption: "the alpha-CuI3 layer" should read alpha-RuCl3. The stage-4 a-axis is written "29.5(A)" on p. 3 and "29.5(4)" in Table I.
11. Fig. S3 caption labels the two orientations "S1 and S2", which clashes with the S2/S4 stage notation. It also contains a stray sentence fragment ("Single layer graphene/single layer RuCl3"). The SI text says the Dirac cone is shifted "above the Fermi surface"; this should say Fermi level.
12. Fig. 2b: the room-temperature resistivity of pristine Kish graphite (about 100 µΩ cm) is well above typical in-plane values. Together with the RRR sensitivity to cutting noted in the text, this suggests geometric error. Please give sample dimensions and estimated uncertainties, or plot normalized resistivity.
13. The Fermi surface panels in Fig. 5 are smeared by unfolding, and the frequencies are extracted by hand from "highest intensity points" (Methods). Please give an uncertainty on each computed frequency. Please also show the dhva cross-check for more than the A|A structure, especially for stage 2, where the comparison matters most. The acknowledgements refer to the code as "hdva", which looks like a typo.
14. Fig. 1 includes a stage-1 A|A stacking that was not observed. The caption should say it is a reference model only.
15. Data are available only "upon reasonable request". Depositing the XRD patterns, raw magnetotransport data and DFT structures would help readers check the frequency analysis.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one compositional probe and Raman spectroscopy for both stages. Together these would confirm that the intercalant is RuCl3 and give an independent, standard measure of the graphene doping to compare with the DFT and SdH values.
2. Extend the quantum-oscillation study to temperature- and angle-dependent measurements, and show the Hall data. Use the resulting cyclotron masses, angle dependence and carrier densities, rather than frequency alone, to assign pockets to stacking models.
3. Redo the key calculations with SOC, U and magnetic order, at the experimentally determined interlayer spacings, so that the remaining discrepancy with experiment can be attributed with some confidence.
4. Measure magnetic susceptibility, or the low-temperature transport near the alpha-RuCl3 ordering temperature, to show whether the intercalated RuCl3 layers keep their magnetic character. This is the property that would justify the "exotic quantum phases" framing.
