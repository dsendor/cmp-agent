Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports the vapour-transport growth of graphite intercalated with α-RuCl3 (C-RuCl3), using AgCl photolysis as a Cl2 source. It assigns stage-2 and stage-4 phases from X-ray diffraction and observes Shubnikov-de Haas (SdH) frequencies near 170-176 T (and 91 T in stage 4), far above those of pristine graphite. It interprets these with non-spin-polarized GGA calculations of graphene-to-RuCl3 charge transfer for model stackings. A bulk, stage-controlled analogue of graphene/α-RuCl3 heterostructures would be a useful materials platform. As written, however, the work is mainly a synthesis report. Its structural, compositional and electronic assignments rest on limited data, and the "exotic quantum phases" framing has no supporting measurement. The novelty claim ("for the first time") also needs to be reconciled with the older literature on ruthenium-chloride graphite intercalation compounds, including ref. 27.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A reproducible two-zone vapour-transport route with an in-situ Cl2 source, giving at least two distinct staged phases depending on reaction time (22 h, stage 4; 48 h, stage 2).
- Synchrotron and laboratory XRD showing a large c-axis expansion. The 00l repeat distances (about 12.7 Å and 19.4 Å) agree with a single intercalant-layer spacing of about 9.35 Å plus (n-1) graphite gaps for n = 2 and n = 4, which is internally consistent. The in-plane supercell a ≈ 29.6 Å is close to 12 × a(graphene) and 5 × a(RuCl3).
- High-frequency SdH oscillations that clearly differ from the host graphite, which is direct evidence of a strongly modified, likely hole-doped graphene Fermi surface.
- A DFT framework (charge-density redistribution, unfolded bands and Fermi surfaces for several stackings) that gives a qualitative picture: interface graphene layers carry most of the doping and inner layers are screened.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The SdH frequencies are not shown to come from the assigned stages, and the agreement with DFT is not established (Fig. 2g,h; Fig. 5; pp. 5-6).
   - Stage 2 and stage 4 show essentially the same main frequency (β ≈ 170 T and 176 T). The calculations, however, predict different pocket sets for the two stages: AB|AB gives 131 and 444 T; ABAB|ABAB gives 70 and 296 T; ABCA|ABCA gives 28, 311 and 330 T. None of the predicted stage-2 frequencies is observed, and the 444 T pocket is absent.
   - Agreement for stage 4 is reached only after introducing a rigid Fermi-level shift of about 0.1 eV, an adjustable parameter. Stage 2 is never brought into agreement. With two to three predicted pockets per structure and one free shift, matching one or two observed frequencies is weak evidence.
   - Because β is nearly stage-independent, the simplest reading is a common origin. Candidates are the RuCl3-adjacent (interface) graphene layers, which the authors' own Fig. 3b shows carry most of the depletion in both stages, or a minority phase common to both samples. The paper should address this directly.
   - Requested data:
     - Angle-dependent SdH, F(θ) ∝ 1/cosθ, to show the pockets are 2D and tied to the layers.
     - Temperature-dependent amplitudes with Lifshitz-Kosevich fits to extract cyclotron masses, compared with the DFT band masses of each pocket.
     - The Hall data, which the Methods say were measured (antisymmetrized ρxy) but which are not shown. They give carrier sign and density.
     - SdH on more than one crystal per stage.
     - A Luttinger-count comparison. With spin and valley degeneracy, F = 170 T corresponds to about 1.6 × 10^13 cm^-2 holes per pocket. Compare this with the DFT doping per layer and with published graphene/α-RuCl3 hole densities (refs 10, 11, 34).

2. The DFT treatment of α-RuCl3 and the structural inputs undermine the quantitative frequency comparison (Methods; Fig. S5; Fig. S4a; pp. 4-5).
   - Correlation and magnetism: the calculations are non-spin-polarized GGA without U or spin-orbit coupling. In this approximation the Ru t2g bands sit at the Fermi level: Fig. S5c shows α-RuCl3 bands crossing E_F. This contradicts the statement that α-RuCl3 "remain[s] rather insulating" (p. 5). It also means the Fermi level, and hence the graphene hole doping, is pinned by an artificially metallic RuCl3 manifold. Earlier work on graphene/α-RuCl3 (ref. 12) found that the transferred charge depends on U, SOC and the magnetic configuration.
   - Interlayer spacings: the calculations use d(Gr-RuCl3) = 3.60 Å (Methods), or 3.765 Å with d(Gr-Gr) = 10.24 Å across RuCl3 (SI), plus a graphite gap of 3.54 Å. The XRD repeats instead imply an intercalant-layer spacing of about 9.35 Å with standard 3.35 Å graphite gaps, since 12.7 - 3.35 ≈ 19.4 - 3 × 3.35 ≈ 9.35 Å. Fig. S4a places the "experimental" A|A cell near c ≈ 10.0 Å, and over this range the computed charge transfer changes by roughly 10%. The paper itself states that the frequencies are "highly sensitive to the interlayer distances".
   - Strain: the main text says the strain is about 4%, while the SI says about 1%.
   - Requested work:
     - Repeat at least the AB|AB and ABAB|ABAB Fermi-surface calculations with GGA+U(+SOC) in a zigzag or ferromagnetic configuration, at XRD-consistent spacings.
     - Report how the pocket frequencies vary with U and with d. This turns the 0.1 eV shift from a fit parameter into a calculated quantity, or shows that it cannot be one.

3. The structure, stacking and composition of the intercalant are inferred mainly by analogy rather than measured (pp. 2-3, 6-7; Table I; Fig. S1).
   - Only 00l reflections (powder) and hk0 reflections (single crystal) are observed. The 00l indices listed in Fig. S1 are all even for stage 2 (004, 006, 008, 0012, 0022) and all multiples of 3 for stage 4 (006, 0012, 0015, 0018, 0033, 0036, 0051). The data therefore establish repeats of about 12.7 Å and 19.4 Å. The doubled and tripled cells (25.4 Å, 58.2 Å) and the stacking sequences in Table I (A|AB|B; AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB) follow from assumption 4, not from the measurements. These stackings are then used to choose between Bernal and rhombohedral models in the DFT.
   - Assumption 1 states that RuCl3 is incommensurate with the host, yet the hk0 pattern is indexed with a commensurate 12 × 12 / 5 × 5 supercell. The authors should state which holds.
   - No chemical analysis is given, such as EDS/WDS stoichiometry, mass uptake or TGA, XPS of the Ru valence, or Raman of the graphene G-band shift or of the RuCl3 modes. Without it, it is not shown that the intercalant is stoichiometric RuCl3 with honeycomb (α-like) order. Alternatives include a Cl-rich, reduced or Ag-contaminated layer, given that the synthesis uses AgCl and excess Cl2.
   - Residual graphite is seen in Fig. S1, but phase fractions and the possibility of mixed staging are not quantified.
   - It is not stated whether the transport pieces themselves were characterized by XRD.
   - The authors should:
     - provide the composition analysis above;
     - quantify phase fractions;
     - check the staging of the transport specimens;
     - either obtain l ≠ 0 reflections (for example by rocking or tilting the crystal) or present the stacking as a hypothesis.

B. Should address

4. The novelty claim needs revision (p. 6, "for the first time"; Introduction). Ref. 27 (Postnikov et al., 1977) reports the synthesis and structure of graphite intercalation compounds with ruthenium chlorides, and ref. 30 covers the intercalation of metal chlorides more broadly. The authors should discuss these earlier compounds, including their reported staging and in-plane structure, and state precisely what is new. Possibilities include single-crystal staging control, quantum oscillations, or identification of the α-phase honeycomb layer.

5. The "exotic quantum phases" framing in the title, abstract and conclusions is not supported by any measurement. No magnetic susceptibility, specific heat or magnetotransport across the bulk α-RuCl3 ordering temperature (about 7 K) is shown. The paper does not establish whether the intercalated RuCl3 layers order magnetically, whether they remain Kitaev-like after receiving about 0.1 e/f.u. (Fig. S4b), or whether any flat-band or rhombohedral physics is present. The rhombohedral (ABCA) stacking in particular is not experimentally identified (see concern 3). The authors should either add χ(T) or C(T) and low-field ρ(T) around 2-15 K, or tone down the title and conclusions to the results actually shown.

6. The reference controls are not matched to the intercalated samples (p. 2, Synthesis; p. 3, Transport; Fig. 2b,c).
   - Host: the intercalation host is natural graphite flakes from HQ Graphene, but the transport reference and the Cl2-annealed control are Kish graphite. Differences in host mosaicity, defect density and RRR therefore enter the comparison.
   - Annealing: the control was annealed at 900 °C "for several days", whereas the growth runs lasted 22-48 h.
   - Only ρ(T) is shown for the annealed control. Its SdH spectrum, which would rule out Cl or chloride-induced doping without RuCl3, is not shown.
   - The authors should measure the actual host flakes, both pristine and Cl2-annealed under the growth time and temperature profile, including SdH and Hall.

7. Details of the FFT analysis and inconsistencies in the harmonics (Fig. 2f-h; p. 3).
   - The labelled harmonics do not match twice the fundamentals: 2ν1 = 9.3 T versus 2 × 4.13 = 8.26 T; 2β = 359 T versus 340 T in stage 2; 2β = 347 T versus 352 T in stage 4.
   - For a 4-9 T window, the FFT resolution is about 1/(1/4 - 1/9) ≈ 7 T, so the 19 T mismatch in stage 2 is significant. The 359 T peak may be a separate pocket, which matters for concern 1.
   - For Kish graphite, ν1 = 4.13 T and ν2 = 5.16 T are about 1 T apart, comparable to the roughly 1.8 T resolution of a 1.5-9 T window.
   - The authors should show the background-subtracted Δρ versus 1/B traces, state the polynomial order and field windows, and show that α (91 T, stage 4 only) and β are robust to these choices.
   - It is also unclear why the 91 T pocket is absent from stage 2. The proposed explanation ("sample-dependent absence of the α frequency") needs multiple samples to support it.

8. The method for extracting Fermi-surface areas has an unquantified systematic error (Methods; Fig. 5). Areas are obtained by joining the maxima of smeared unfolded spectral weight, and the paper says only that this introduces a "small systematic error". For the small pockets (28 T, 70 T) and the closely spaced 311/330 T pair, this error could change the conclusions. The authors should quantify it, for example by varying the broadening and k-mesh, and should apply the dhva/tricubic method used for A|A to all structures. They should also state at which kz the extremal areas are taken.

9. The resistivity data and sample statistics are thin (Fig. 2b-e).
   - The stage-4 ρ(T) is absent from Fig. 2b.
   - Stage 4 has a residual resistivity about 15 times larger than stage 2 (Fig. 2d,e), which is not discussed.
   - The text acknowledges that cutting changes the RRR.
   - The number of crystals measured per stage, the reproducibility of ρ and of the SdH frequencies, and the air and thermal-cycling stability of the compounds are not reported. Metal-chloride GICs can deintercalate.
   - The authors should report these.

C. Minor

10. Table I caption: "α-CuI3" should read α-RuCl3. The listed stage-2 stacking "A|AB|B" should be explained in the text.
11. Fig. S1: the caption assigns (a,b) to the XRD images and (c,d) to the integrated profiles, but the figure and the main text use the opposite assignment.
12. In Fig. S3 the supercells are labelled "S1" and "S2", which clashes with the S2/S4 stage notation used elsewhere. The caption also ends with a fragment ("Single layer graphene/single layer RuCl3").
13. Fig. 4 and p. 5 report rigid shifts of 0.68 and 0.48 eV, while the SI quotes about 0.7 eV. The authors should state which cell, strained or unstrained, each value refers to.
14. Typos: "Graphte" (Fig. 2a), "heretostructures", "ampule", "of of Tennessee", "hdva code" in the Acknowledgements.
15. The data availability statement ("upon reasonable request") is weak for a materials-discovery paper. The authors should deposit the XRD images, the magnetotransport data and the DFT structures.
16. The Introduction cites references 4 and 39-42 on rhombohedral flat bands. It should state clearly that the flat-band formation in Fig. 4 is computed only for the idealized model stackings.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add angle- and temperature-dependent SdH, together with Hall data, on several crystals per stage. Use these to assign each frequency to interface or inner graphene layers via Luttinger counts and effective masses, rather than via a tuned Fermi-level shift.
2. Recompute the stage-2 and stage-4 Fermi surfaces with GGA+U+SOC in a magnetic state, at interlayer spacings consistent with the measured 9.35 Å intercalant repeat. Present the sensitivity of the pocket frequencies to U and d.
3. Establish composition and local structure with EDS/XPS and Raman, and try to obtain l ≠ 0 reflections. This would settle the stacking, which currently decides between the Bernal and rhombohedral scenarios.
4. Add a basic magnetic characterization (χ(T), C(T)) of the intercalated RuCl3 layers, so that the claims about a Kitaev or exotic-phase platform rest on data.
