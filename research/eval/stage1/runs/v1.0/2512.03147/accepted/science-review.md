A. Must address

1. **The graphene stacking sequence is assumed, not measured.** The Methods say the powder pattern contains only (00l) reflections ("hkl (h = k = 0)") and the single-crystal patterns only (hk0) reflections ("hkl (l = 0)"). The stackings in Table 1 (A|AB|B for stage 2; AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB for stage 4) are then "concluded" from the four assumptions in the Methods, by analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 GICs. Neither data set constrains the lateral registry of graphene layers across a RuCl3 gallery or within a graphene block, so AA, Bernal (AB) and rhombohedral (ABC) sequences cannot be told apart. The c-period multiplicity (two repeats for stage 2, three for stage 4) is consistent with several registries, and the two candidate stage-4 sequences in Table 1 already show that the data do not choose. Yet the Conclusions say "we determined the crystal structure", and the motivation (flat bands of ABC graphene), Fig. 1b–f and the DFT comparison of ABAB|ABAB versus ABCA|ABCA (Figs. 4c,d and 5d,e) all depend on this distinction. Table 1, Fig. 1c,d and the DFT models also use different stage-4 sequences. The authors should: show the indexed XRD patterns (powder 00l for both stages, and in-plane hk0 spots assigned to graphite or RuCl3, with any superlattice spots) in the main text rather than only in the Supplementary Material; collect off-axis (h0l)/(hhl)/(hkl, l ≠ 0) reflections (for example a rotation or precession dataset at SPring-8) or zone-axis cross-sectional HRTEM/STEM that resolves graphene registry, and/or refine the 00l intensities (structure-factor fit, not just positions) for the staged models; make Table 1, Fig. 1 and the DFT models refer to the same candidate stackings; and state clearly which stacking features are measured and which are assumed. If the registry cannot be measured, they should say plainly that it is undetermined, remove the implication that rhombohedral blocks are present, and recast the DFT stackings as a survey of models rather than an identification.

2. **The agreement between computed and measured SdH frequencies rests on a free Fermi-level shift and does not discriminate between stackings.** At the stated charge transfer the models give 511 T (A|A), 131/444 T (AB|AB, the stage-2 model) against a single measured β = 170 T, and 70/296 T (ABAB|ABAB) or 28/311/330 T (ABCA|ABCA) against measured 91 and 176 T in stage 4. Neither stage matches. "Good agreement" (p. 5) is then claimed after an ad hoc rigid 0.1 eV shift, giving 27/183 T and 126/214 T, which still differ from the data by 20–40 T, and only after predicted pockets are allowed to drop below E_F; further shifts are invoked to explain the absence of α in stage 2. No shifted result is given for AB|AB, the model assigned to the measured stage-2 sample, the size and sign of the shift are not tied to any independent quantity (Hall density, Bader charge, interlayer distance), and unobserved predicted frequencies (for example 444 T in stage 2) are not explained by amplitude arguments. With two to three pockets per model, a free shift and a free choice of stacking, the comparison has nearly as many adjustable choices as data points; the text itself says that "only a limited range of physically reasonable parameters" reproduces the data. The claim that the response is "governed by the stacking sequence" is therefore not shown. The authors should plot the frequencies of every model as a continuous function of E_F shift (or transferred charge per C) in one figure with the measured stage-2 and stage-4 frequencies overlaid; give the shifted AB|AB result; say whether a single shared shift fits both stages and whether ABAB and ABCA need different shifts (that is, whether the data can discriminate at all); state the kz dependence of the computed extremal areas (bands use a 6x6x2 mesh and stage-4 blocks are coupled along c); and tone the conclusion down to "consistent with strong hole doping of the graphene layers" unless the stacking is independently fixed.

3. **The nearly identical β frequency in stage 2 and stage 4, and the sample-dependent α, may point to a common or minority-phase origin.** β is 170 T in stage 2 and 176 T in stage 4 (Fig. 2g,h), equal within FFT resolution, while the DFT predicts very different pocket sets for the two stages (131/444 T against 70/296 T or 28/311/330 T). A natural alternative is that β comes from the graphene layer directly adjacent to RuCl3, whose doping is set by the interface and is largely independent of stage; Fig. 3b supports this, since in the four-layer models the hole depletion concentrates on the interface layers. β could also come from a minority phase common to both samples, or the stage-4 crystal could contain stage-2 regions. The 91 T α peak appears only in some samples ("sample-dependent absence of the α frequency"), which raises the same question. If any of these holds, the oscillation spectrum is not evidence for the assigned stage or stacking. The authors should discuss these scenarios explicitly, compare the interface-layer pocket area across all models, and test them with the effective mass of β in stage 2 versus stage 4 and with angle dependence (concern 5).
Raised by: r2, r3

4. **The DFT level of theory may misplace the RuCl3 bands that set the charge transfer.** All band structures and Bader charges are non-spin-polarized GGA without Hubbard U or spin-orbit coupling. In that treatment α-RuCl3 is metallic with its t2g states at E_F, whereas it is a spin-orbit-assisted Mott insulator. The size of the graphene-to-RuCl3 charge transfer, and any hybridization with the graphene π bands, depends on where the empty Ru t2g (upper Hubbard) states lie relative to the Dirac point, which depends on U, magnetic order and SOC; earlier graphene/α-RuCl3 work, including refs. 12 and 34 (partly by the same authors), used GGA+U(+SOC) with zigzag order for this reason. The text's statements that RuCl3 stays "rather insulating" and the Ru atoms keep "their magnetic nature" (p. 4) cannot be supported by a non-magnetic calculation. The 0.68/0.48 eV graphene shifts, and hence every computed frequency, rest on this choice, and the later 0.1 eV correction is of the same order as plausible U-induced shifts (the text shows frequencies change by tens to hundreds of tesla per 0.1 eV). The authors should repeat at least A|A or AB|AB and one four-layer stacking (ABCA|ABCA) with GGA+U over a range of U in a magnetic (zigzag or ferromagnetic) configuration, ideally with SOC; report how the Dirac-point shift, transferred charge and pocket areas change; and justify the 0.1 eV shift against this sensitivity rather than by unmodelled "stacking faults".

5. **The two-dimensional, graphene-layer origin of the pockets and the "three-dimensional" character are not tested experimentally.** The field is applied only along c and only one temperature is shown per sample (1.8 K and 2.5 K). There is no angle dependence of F, which would show whether α and β are quasi-2D sheets (F ∝ 1/cos θ) as the graphene-layer interpretation requires, rather than a 3D pocket of residual graphite or a disordered phase. There are no Lifshitz–Kosevich effective masses, which would discriminate sharply between Dirac-like (AB), bilayer-like and flat-band (ABC) pockets, and no Dingle analysis to argue whether the unobserved predicted pockets should be visible up to 9 T. The title calls the compound a three-dimensional platform, but only in-plane ρxx is measured; with about 9 Å RuCl3 galleries the graphene blocks may be electronically decoupled, which is what the 2D-pocket analysis implicitly assumes. The authors should add rotation-angle SdH and temperature-dependent SdH (m*, T_D) on crystals characterised by XRD and STEM, compare m* with the calculated cyclotron masses of each candidate pocket, and ideally measure c-axis resistivity (ρc/ρab).
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

6. **The title, abstract and conclusions claim a platform for correlated, magnetic or topological phases that no measurement probes.** The title promises a "three-dimensional platform for exotic quantum phases", the abstract claims "comprehensive characterization" and "access to novel correlated and topological states", and the introduction calls RuCl3 "magnetically active" with "possible spin-liquid behavior". Yet there are no magnetization, susceptibility, specific heat, or neutron/muon data, and the calculations are non-magnetic. Whether the intercalated single RuCl3 layers keep local moments, order (bulk α-RuCl3 orders near 7 K) or are quenched by the roughly 0.5 eV hole transfer, which should change the Ru valence substantially, is the key question for the proposed use; the rhombohedral flat-band link also depends on the unconfirmed ABCA assignment. The authors should either add at least M(T) and M(H) with the field in-plane and along c on the stage-2 and stage-4 crystals (with low-temperature specific heat if possible), or retitle and rewrite the abstract and conclusions as a synthesis, structural and fermiology study of a stage-2/stage-4 GIC with heavily hole-doped graphene, stating that magnetic and correlated properties remain untested. The Conclusions' closing caveat ("subject to further spectroscopic and magnetic validation") should be reflected in the abstract.

B. Should address

7. **SdH harmonic labels are inconsistent with the fundamentals, and the FFT procedure is not described.** In Fig. 2f ν1 = 4.13 T but 2ν1 is at 9.3 T rather than 8.26 T; in Fig. 2g β = 170 T but 2β is at 359 T rather than 340 T; in Fig. 2h β = 176 T and 2β = 347 T (expected 352 T). With a 4–9 T window the resolution is about 7 T, so the 19 T offset in stage 2 is not a resolution effect; conversely, for the Kish graphite FFT a 1.5–9 T window gives only about 1.8 T resolution, making the 4.13 and 5.16 T peaks hard to resolve separately. A peak near 360 T could be one of the predicted larger pockets (296–444 T), which would change the comparison in concern 2. The authors should state the field window, background-subtraction order and windowing function for each FFT, give the resulting resolution and an uncertainty for every quoted frequency, and either justify the harmonic labels (for example from temperature dependence, since harmonics decay with twice the mass) or reassign these peaks as independent orbits.

8. **Hall, SdH and DFT carrier counts are not shown or cross-checked.** The Hall density of 7.8 x 10^20 cm^-3 (p. 3) is quoted without the sample, temperature, carrier sign, fitting model or ρxy(B) data. A single-band slope is questionable in a multicarrier GIC with residual graphite. A Luttinger count shows a discrepancy: a 170 T pocket with 4-fold degeneracy corresponds to about 1.6 x 10^13 cm^-2 holes per graphene layer (E_F about 0.47 eV); with two layers per 12.7 Å in stage 2 this is about 2.6 x 10^20 cm^-3, roughly a factor of three below the Hall value. The authors should show ρxy(B) for both stages, state which sample and temperature the quoted value refers to, and tabulate per sample the density from Hall (with sign and model), from the sum of SdH pocket areas, and from the DFT/Bader charge converted to a volume density, discussing any discrepancy (multiband Hall, unresolved pockets, inner graphene layers, residual graphite). This is also an independent constraint on the Fermi-level shift in concern 2.

9. **Structural approximations in the DFT models are not bounded or derived from the data.** The 2x2 RuCl3/5x5 graphene cell imposes about 4% tensile strain on RuCl3, while the measured a = 29.6 Å implies RuCl3 slightly compressed (5 x 5.92 Å) on a 12x12 graphene cell; the benchmark against an unstrained 5x5/12x12 cell is shown only for A|A and only via the band shift. The graphene–RuCl3 distance is fixed at 3.60 Å as "suggested by X-ray data", but the XRD gives only the c repeat; how 3.60 Å follows from c = 25.4 Å (for example a 12.7 − 3.35 = 9.35 Å gallery split between RuCl3 thickness and two van der Waals gaps) is not shown. d_Gr-Gr = 3.54 Å is well above graphite's 3.35 Å and unjustified. Relaxations use the Γ point only, and the stage-2 structure is simplified from A|AB|B to AB|AB on the grounds that charge transfer is insensitive to registry, although bands near K (interlayer coupling across the gallery) may not be. Since the Discussion says the Fermi surfaces are "highly sensitive to the interlayer distances", the authors should derive the distances from the measured c values or relax them with D3, give the change in pocket areas (in tesla) between strained and unstrained cells for at least one multilayer stacking and for d_Gr-RuCl3 and d_Gr-Gr varied by about ±0.1 Å, and say in light of these numbers whether the 20–40 T residual mismatch is significant.

10. **The chemical composition of the intercalant is not characterised.** No stoichiometry (C_xRuCl3 from mass uptake, EDS/WDS or EELS) is given, and nothing excludes co-intercalated Cl2, Ag or AgCl from the photolysis source, reduced Ru species, or chlorine-only intercalated regions, which the Cl2-anneal control in Fig. 2b shows can change ρ(T). Without this it is not shown that the intercalant is stoichiometric RuCl3 rather than RuCl3-x or a mixed chloride. The authors should provide STEM-EDS/EELS maps across the layers with Ru:Cl ratios, confirm the absence of Ag, and give the Ru oxidation state (for example by XPS) if possible.

11. **The Cl2-annealed graphite control stops at resistivity.** The control is shown only as an R(T) curve (Fig. 2b). Chlorine, AgCl/Ag residues or acceptor-type surface doping could themselves create high-frequency hole pockets or minority phases. The authors should show the magnetoresistance and SdH/FFT of the Cl2-annealed graphite, and ideally of graphite treated with AgCl alone, measured and analysed identically (temperature, field range, window), to confirm that the 91 and 170 T orbits require RuCl3.

12. **Phase purity, stage mixing and the STEM evidence are not quantified.** The text mentions "a small portion of residual pure graphite" without phase fractions, gives no (00l) linewidth or Hendricks–Teller-type mixed-staging analysis, and notes that longer reactions give mixed staging while the SdH discussion invokes stacking faults. The STEM evidence is only in the SI, described as "qualitative", and the text concedes that "a fully resolved layer-by-layer intercalation pattern is not observed throughout the sample". Quantum oscillations are dominated by the highest-mobility regions, so the SdH frequency alone does not prove "uniform intercalation". The authors should give stage and graphite fractions from the XRD (00l) intensities and linewidths, and bring representative cross-sectional HAADF images into the main text with line profiles of the repeat distance compared with 12.7 Å and 19.4 Å, statistics of stage domains, and the unintercalated fraction.
