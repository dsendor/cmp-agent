Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors grow graphite intercalated with α-RuCl3 by two-zone chemical vapour transport, using AgCl photodecomposition to supply Cl2. They assign stage-2 and stage-4 compounds from X-ray c-axis spacings. They report Shubnikov-de Haas (SdH) frequencies of about 170-176 T (and 91 T in one stage-4 sample), far above those of pristine graphite, and they interpret these with unfolded GGA Fermi surfaces of model supercells in which graphene donates holes to RuCl3. A bulk, three-dimensional counterpart of the much-studied graphene/α-RuCl3 heterostructures would be a useful materials platform. The synthesis route and the large, coherent SdH frequencies are the main new results. The structural assignment (stacking), the link between the measured frequencies and particular stackings, and the "exotic quantum phases" framing are much less firmly established than the text suggests.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A workable synthesis route. The text reports that intercalation fails without excess Cl2, and the AgCl source is a practical solution to that.
- The c-axis spacings (25.4 Å and 58.2 Å) fit the usual staging relation for metal-chloride GICs (I_c ≈ 9.4 Å + 3.35 Å × (n − 1), so about 12.7 Å × 2 and 19.4 Å × 3). The stage assignment from the powder 00l lines is therefore plausible.
- The intercalated crystals show clean, high-frequency SdH oscillations with visible harmonics, which indicates coherent, heavily doped graphene sheets with reasonable mobility.
- The Cl2-annealed Kish graphite control in Fig. 2b separates the effect of the chlorine atmosphere from the effect of RuCl3 intercalation.
- The DFT shows how stacking and interface charge transfer change the hole pockets near K. This gives a framework that later spectroscopy (ARPES, quantum oscillations under tilt) can test.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The crystal structure and stacking are assumed, not determined.**
   - **What is claimed.** The Conclusions say "we determined the crystal structure", and Table 1 lists specific stackings: A|AB|B for stage 2, and AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB for stage 4. Fig. 1 and all the DFT models build on these.
   - **What the data support.** The Methods state that the single-crystal data contain only l = 0 reflections and the powder data only h = k = 0 reflections. Without mixed hkl reflections, the registry of graphene layers across a RuCl3 layer (AA versus AB versus ABC) and the stacking inside the graphene blocks cannot be determined. The stacking was taken from "the analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3, and AlCl3" plus assumption 4 in the Methods.
   - **An internal inconsistency.** Assumption 1 states that RuCl3 is incommensurate with the graphite host. Yet a single hexagonal cell with a = 29.6 Å is reported. This is close to 12 × a_graphene (29.5 Å) and to 5 × a_RuCl3 (about 29.9 Å), which suggests a commensurate or near-commensurate superlattice.
   - **Why it matters.** The Fermi-surface interpretation, and the claim of access to rhombohedral flat-band physics (ABCA in stage 4), depend entirely on the stacking.
   - **What to do.**
     - Present the in-plane diffraction explicitly in the main text: indexed hk0 spots, which spots belong to graphite and which to RuCl3, and whether superlattice spots exist.
     - State whether the RuCl3 layer is commensurate.
     - Either obtain off-axis (hkl, l ≠ 0) data or 00l intensity refinement (structure-factor fit of the 00l intensities to the proposed stage and layer positions), or rephrase stacking as a hypothesis.
     - Quantify stage purity, for example from 00l linewidths or a mixed-stage analysis. The SdH discussion invokes stacking faults, and the synthesis section notes that longer reactions give mixed staging.

2. **The match between quantum oscillations and DFT does not discriminate between stackings, and a key observation goes against the stacking-sensitivity argument.**
   - **The data.** The dominant frequency is nearly identical in stage 2 (β = 170 T) and stage 4 (β = 176 T), as shown in Fig. 2g,h. The calculations instead predict quite different pocket sets: 131/444 T for AB|AB, 70/296 T for ABAB|ABAB, and 28/311/330 T for ABCA|ABCA. Neither calculated set reproduces β in stage 2.
   - **The fitting freedom.** Agreement is reached only after a rigid 0.1 eV Fermi-level shift, and only for the stage-4 structures: 27/183 T and 126/214 T. Pockets that do not appear in the data are then allowed to disappear below E_F. With one free shift and the freedom to drop pockets, almost any stacking can be made to fit two numbers. The text itself says that "only a limited range of physically reasonable parameters" reproduces the data. That makes the conclusion that the response is "governed by the stacking sequence" unsupported.
   - **The 91 T peak.** The 91 T α frequency appears only in some samples ("sample-dependent absence of the α frequency"). This raises the question of whether it comes from a minority phase.
   - **What to do.**
     - Give the shifted calculation for AB|AB, the stage-2 case, and state whether any single, shared shift fits both stages.
     - Discuss why stage 2 and stage 4 share β. Possible explanations are that the outer graphene layer next to RuCl3 dominates in both stages, or that the stage-4 crystal contains stage-2 regions.
     - Test the assignment experimentally with tilt-angle dependence of F (a 1/cosθ law for quasi-2D pockets) and with Lifshitz-Kosevich effective masses compared with the calculated band masses (see concern 4). The SdH frequency alone cannot single out a stacking.

3. **The calculated charge transfer rests on choices that the paper does not justify, and the Fermi surfaces depend on it directly.**
   - **No magnetism, U or spin-orbit coupling.** All band structures and Bader charges use non-spin-polarized GGA without U or spin-orbit coupling. α-RuCl3 is a spin-orbit-assisted Mott insulator. The size of graphene-to-RuCl3 charge transfer depends on where the empty Ru t2g states lie relative to the Dirac point, which in turn depends on U, magnetic order and spin-orbit coupling. Earlier graphene/RuCl3 work, including ref. 12 by some of the same authors, used GGA+U(+SOC) with magnetic order. A nonmagnetic metallic RuCl3 can over- or under-estimate the doping, and the authors' own text shows that frequencies change by tens to hundreds of tesla per 0.1 eV.
   - **Strain.** The models put a 4% tensile strain on RuCl3. The measured a = 29.6 Å instead implies RuCl3 slightly compressed (5 × 5.92 Å) on a 12 × 12 graphene cell. The benchmark against an unstrained cell is shown only for A|A.
   - **Interlayer distances.** These are fixed at d_Gr-RuCl3 = 3.60 Å and d_Gr-Gr = 3.54 Å. The latter is well above graphite's 3.35 Å and is not justified. The repeat distances from Table 1 (about 12.7 Å per stage-2 repeat and about 19.4 Å per stage-4 repeat) can be compared with these choices, but the paper does not do so. The Discussion states that the Fermi surfaces are "highly sensitive to the interlayer distances", so these fixed values matter.
   - **What to do.**
     - Repeat at least the AB|AB and ABCA|ABCA charge transfer and Fermi level with GGA+U (with a magnetic configuration, and ideally with spin-orbit coupling).
     - Use interlayer distances that follow from the measured c values, or relax them with D3 included.
     - Report how the computed frequencies move with U and with these distances, so that the 0.1 eV shift can be judged against the method's real uncertainty.

B. Should address

4. **No temperature-dependent or angle-dependent SdH analysis.**
   - Only single-temperature FFTs (1.8 K and 2.5 K) at one field orientation are shown.
   - **What to do.**
     - Extract cyclotron masses from Lifshitz-Kosevich fits for α and β, and a Dingle temperature. Compare the masses with the band masses of the candidate pockets: a flat-band ABC pocket and a Dirac-like AB pocket should differ strongly.
     - Measure F(θ) to confirm that the oscillations come from quasi-2D sheets (doped graphene layers) and not from a three-dimensional pocket of residual graphite or a disordered phase.
   - This is the most direct experimental route to connect the oscillations to a structure.

5. **The Hall data and carrier count are not shown or cross-checked.**
   - **What is reported.** A carrier density of 7.8 × 10^20 cm^-3 is quoted for an unspecified sample, from the slope of ρ_xy, with no figure.
   - **Why it matters.** Graphite intercalation compounds are multicarrier systems, and residual graphite is present according to the XRD, so a single-band slope may be misleading.
   - **A rough check.** A 170 T hole pocket per graphene layer (with spin and valley degeneracy 4) corresponds to about 1.6 × 10^13 cm^-2. With two such layers per 12.7 Å, this gives about 2.6 × 10^20 cm^-3, a factor of about 3 below the Hall value.
   - **What to do.**
     - Show ρ_xy(B) for both stages.
     - State which sample and temperature the 7.8 × 10^20 cm^-3 value refers to.
     - Compare the Luttinger count from the SdH frequencies, the Hall density and the DFT/Bader charge per carbon. Reporting the Bader charges per graphene layer in the main text would make this possible.
   - This would be an independent test of the calculated charge transfer.

6. **Reproducibility and sample statistics are asserted but not shown.**
   - The text says that a second piece of the same stage-2 crystal and "multiple independently prepared samples reproduced the same FFT peak", and that the α frequency is absent in some samples. No data, sample count or spread is given.
   - **What to do.**
     - State how many crystals of each stage were measured.
     - Show their FFTs, with the frequency spread and which ones show α, in the SI.
     - Link each transport sample to an XRD characterisation of the same crystal or batch.
   - The SdH frequency alone does not prove "uniform intercalation". Quantum oscillations are dominated by the highest-mobility regions and are insensitive to minority phases with short scattering times.

7. **Chemical composition and possible side products are not characterised.**
   - The paper gives no stoichiometry (for example C_xRuCl3 from mass uptake, EDS or EELS).
   - It gives no check for co-intercalated Cl2, Ag or AgCl from the photolysis source, or for reduced Ru species.
   - The Cl2-anneal control shows that chlorine alone changes ρ(T), so chlorine-only intercalated regions could contribute to transport.
   - **What to do.** Provide elemental analysis (STEM-EDS/EELS maps across the layers, and XPS for the Ru oxidation state if possible). Confirm that the intercalant is RuCl3 and that Ag is absent.

8. **STEM evidence is qualitative and kept in the SI.**
   - The text concedes that "a fully resolved layer-by-layer intercalation pattern is not observed throughout the sample".
   - **What to do.** Show representative cross-sectional HAADF images in the main text, with:
     - line profiles giving the measured repeat distance, to compare with the 12.7 Å and 19.4 Å expected for stage 2 and stage 4;
     - statistics of stage domains;
     - the fraction of unintercalated graphite.
   - This would also address concern 1 directly.

9. **The title, abstract and conclusions overstate what is shown.**
   - The title promises a "three-dimensional platform for exotic quantum phases", and the abstract says "comprehensive characterization".
   - No measurement probes magnetism, correlation or topology: there is no magnetisation, specific heat or susceptibility. Whether the intercalated RuCl3 keeps local moments or Kitaev-like behaviour after charge transfer is unknown.
   - The rhombohedral flat-band link depends on the unconfirmed ABCA assignment.
   - **What to do.** Add at least magnetic susceptibility versus temperature, to show whether Ru moments survive and whether any ordering occurs. Otherwise, temper the title and abstract to what the data show: a new stage-2/stage-4 GIC with heavily hole-doped graphene layers.

10. **The "first time" claim needs reconciling with prior work.**
    - The Conclusions state that RuCl3 graphite intercalation is achieved "for the first time". Yet ref. 27 (Postnikov et al., 1977) concerns layer-type graphite compounds with ruthenium chlorides.
    - **What to do.** State clearly what is new relative to that and other earlier Ru-chloride GIC reports: for example the phase (α-RuCl3), staging control, crystal quality or quantum oscillations.

C. Minor

11. **FFT procedure.**
    - **Missing details.** The field window, polynomial background order and windowing function used for each FFT are not given.
    - **Resolution concerns.**
      - For the Kish graphite FFT (Fig. 2f), a 1.5-9 T window gives a resolution of only about 1.8 T. This makes ν1 = 4.13 T and ν2 = 5.16 T hard to resolve as separate peaks.
      - The labelled harmonics deviate from integer multiples: 2ν1 = 9.3 T versus 8.26 T, and 2β = 359 T versus 2 × 170 = 340 T in Fig. 2g. These deviations should be explained or the labels revised.
    - **What to do.** Give the window limits and the resulting frequency resolution and uncertainty for every quoted frequency.

12. **Resistivity data.**
    - Fig. 2b shows the stage-2 sample with lower resistivity than Kish graphite over the whole temperature range, but the text cautions that absolute values are unreliable.
    - **What to do.**
      - Give the sample dimensions, the uncertainties and the RRR values.
      - Explain why Kish graphite was measured at 2.5 K while the intercalated samples were measured at 1.8 K.
      - Report whether ρ(T) shows any anomaly near the bulk α-RuCl3 ordering temperature (about 7-14 K).

13. **Table 1 footnote.** It reads "α-CuI3"; it should read α-RuCl3. The stage-4 stacking notation in Table 1 (AB|BCAB|BC) also differs from Fig. 1c,d and the DFT models (ABAB|ABAB, ABCA|ABCA). Explain this mapping once, clearly.

14. **Smearing error in the Fermi surfaces.** The unfolded Fermi surfaces in Fig. 5 are smeared, and the text acknowledges "a small systematic error" from choosing the highest-intensity points. Quantify this error in tesla. The dhva cross-check is given only for A|A; extend it to at least one multilayer stacking.

15. **Missing quantitative values in the main text.**
    - State the charge transferred per formula unit and per graphene layer for each geometry, and compare with ARPES on graphene/α-RuCl3 (ref. 33).
    - The rigid shifts of 0.68 eV and 0.48 eV quoted for A|A and AB|AB would be easier to assess alongside the corresponding hole densities.

16. **XRD figures.** Move at least the indexed powder 00l pattern for both stages into the main text. The c values and stage assignment are central, and they are currently supported only by figures in the Supplementary Material.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add angle-resolved and temperature-dependent SdH (F(θ), m*, T_D) on the same crystals that were characterised by XRD and STEM. Use these, together with the Hall data, to test the specific pocket assignments.
2. Redo the key DFT cases with GGA+U (magnetic configuration, and ideally spin-orbit coupling) and with interlayer distances set by the measured c values. Present a frequency-versus-E_F map for each stacking, so that readers can see how uniquely the data select a structure.
3. Strengthen the structural determination with off-axis diffraction or 00l intensity refinement, quantitative STEM-EDS/EELS of the stage sequence, and an explicit statement on commensurability.
4. Add a magnetic measurement (susceptibility or magnetisation) to support the stated motivation of combining Kitaev magnetism with doped graphene. Otherwise, reframe the paper as a synthesis and Fermiology study.
