Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports what it calls the first synthesis of bulk graphite intercalated with α-RuCl3. The crystals are grown by chemical vapour transport, using AgCl as a chlorine source. The authors assign stage-2 and stage-4 structures from X-ray diffraction, measure Shubnikov-de Haas (SdH) oscillations at much higher frequencies than in pristine graphite (β ≈ 170–176 T, plus α ≈ 91 T in stage 4), and interpret them with unfolded DFT Fermi surfaces of hole-doped graphene layers. A bulk, three-dimensional version of the much-studied graphene/α-RuCl3 charge-transfer interface would be a useful new material, and the synthesis route is itself a contribution. As presented, though, the evidence fixes the c-axis periodicity but not the graphene stacking sequence. The quantum-oscillation comparison with theory is only semi-quantitative and depends on a tuned Fermi-level shift. The "exotic quantum phases" in the title and abstract are not tested experimentally at all.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new synthesis route (two-zone CVT with UV-decomposed AgCl supplying Cl2), with the practical finding that excess Cl2 is needed for intercalation. Others can reproduce and use it.
- The c-axis expansions (25.4 Å and 58.2 Å, against 6.7 Å for graphite) are clear evidence of intercalation. They are consistent with stage-2 and stage-4 repeat units: 25.4 ≈ 2 × (9.4 + 3.35) Å and 58.2 ≈ 3 × (9.4 + 3 × 3.35) Å.
- Clean, high-frequency SdH oscillations with harmonics show a large Fermi-surface change relative to pristine graphite. A second piece of the same S2 crystal gives the same frequency.
- The DFT workflow (strained supercells, Bader analysis, band unfolding, Onsager frequencies, cross-checked with a dHvA extraction code) is a sensible framework for linking stacking and charge transfer to the oscillation spectrum. It shows qualitatively that hole pockets at K multiply with graphene layer number.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The graphene stacking sequence is assumed, not measured.**
   - *Where:* Table 1; Fig. 1b–d; Methods, assumptions 1–4.
   - *What is missing:* The stacking sequences in Table 1 (A|AB|B; AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB) come from the four assumptions in Methods, by analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 intercalation compounds. The powder data give only (00l) reflections, and the single-crystal patterns give only (hk0) reflections. Neither fixes the in-plane registry of graphene layers across the RuCl3 layer or within a graphene block, so Bernal and rhombohedral sequences cannot be told apart. The two candidate stage-4 sequences in Table 1 already show that the data do not choose between them.
   - *Why it matters:* The motivation (flat bands of ABC graphene), Fig. 1f and the DFT comparison (ABAB vs ABCA, Figs. 4c,d and 5d,e) all depend on this distinction.
   - *What to do:* Provide off-axis (h0l)/(hkl) reflections, for example from a rotation or precession dataset at SPring-8, or cross-sectional HRTEM/STEM along a zone axis that resolves the graphene registry. If that is not possible, state plainly that the graphene stacking is undetermined. Remove the implication that rhombohedral blocks are present, and treat the DFT stackings as models.

2. **The quantum-oscillation agreement depends on a free Fermi-level shift and is not shown for the stage-2 sample.**
   - *Where:* Electronic properties section and Fig. 5.
   - *What is wrong:* At the stated charge transfer the computed frequencies are:

     | Model | Computed frequencies | Measured |
     |---|---|---|
     | A\|A | 511 T | — |
     | AB\|AB (S2-like) | 131 T, 444 T | single β = 170 T (S2) |
     | ABAB / ABCA (S4-like) | 70, 296, 28 T / 311, 330 T | 91 T and 176 T (S4) |

     Neither stage matches. "Good agreement" is then obtained by shifting E_F by about 0.1 eV, and this is shown only for ABCA (27 and 214 T, or 126 and 214 T). Three points are open:
     - No shifted result is given for AB|AB, the model the paper assigns to the measured S2 sample.
     - The direction and size of the shift are not tied to any independent quantity (Hall density, Bader charge, interlayer distance).
     - Further shifts that push a pocket below E_F are invoked to explain why α is absent in S2.

     With one or two adjustable parameters and two to three pockets per model, several stackings could probably be fitted. The claim that the oscillations are "governed by the stacking sequence" is therefore not shown.
   - *What to do:* Give the frequencies of every model as a continuous function of E_F shift (or of transferred charge per C) in one figure, with the measured S2 and S4 frequencies overlaid. Say whether one shift fits both stages. State whether the shift needed for ABAB differs from that for ABCA, that is, whether the data can discriminate between them at all.

3. **The near-identical β frequency in stage 2 and stage 4 needs an explanation, and could point to a common origin.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 2g,h.
   - *What is missing:* β is 170 T in S2 and 176 T in S4, within the FFT resolution. The DFT, however, predicts quite different pocket sets for the two stages (131/444 T against 70/296/28 or 311/330 T). A natural alternative is that β comes from the graphene layer directly adjacent to RuCl3, whose doping is set by the interface and is largely independent of stage. It could also come from a minority phase common to both samples.
   - *Why it matters:* Fig. 3b supports this alternative: in the four-layer models the depletion is concentrated on the interface layers. If so, the oscillation spectrum is not evidence for the assigned stage or stacking.
   - *What to do:* Discuss this scenario explicitly. Compare the interface-layer pocket area across all models. Test the scenario with temperature-dependent amplitudes (effective mass of β in S2 vs S4) and with angle dependence (concern 4).

4. **The dimensionality and origin of the pockets are not established experimentally.**
   - *Where:* Methods (field applied only along c); title and abstract ("three-dimensional platform").
   - *What is missing:* The field is applied only along c, and only one temperature is shown per sample (1.8 K, 2.5 K). There is no angle dependence of F, which would show whether the β and α pockets are two-dimensional (F ∝ 1/cos θ), as the graphene-layer interpretation requires. There is no temperature dependence of the amplitude (Lifshitz–Kosevich effective mass), which could be compared with the DFT band masses and would test the flat-band narrative. There is no Dingle analysis.
   - *Why it matters:* These are standard measurements that separate the proposed graphene-derived Fermi surfaces from other sources. They are also the only way to support the "three-dimensional" label: with about 9 Å RuCl3 spacers, the graphene blocks may be electronically two-dimensional.
   - *What to do:* Add rotation-angle SdH and T-dependent SdH (m*), and compare m* with the unfolded DFT bands.

5. **The DFT setup may misplace the RuCl3 bands, which control the charge transfer.**
   - *Where:* Computational details; Figs. 3–5.
   - *What is wrong:* The calculations are non-spin-polarized GGA with no Hubbard U and no spin-orbit coupling. In that treatment α-RuCl3 is metallic, and its t2g states sit at E_F. Charge transfer to graphene and possible hybridization with the graphene π bands are then known to depend sensitively on U, magnetic order and SOC. Earlier graphene/α-RuCl3 work (the authors' refs. 12 and 34) used GGA+SOC+U with zigzag order for this reason.
   - *Why it matters:* The text says RuCl3 is "rather insulating" with Ru "keeping their magnetic nature", which a non-magnetic GGA calculation cannot show. The 0.68/0.48 eV graphene shifts, and hence every computed frequency, rest on this choice. The 4% tensile strain applied to RuCl3 also shifts its bands. It is benchmarked only for A|A (SI), not for the multilayer stackings.
   - *What to do:* Repeat at least the AB|AB and one four-layer stacking with GGA+U(+SOC) in a magnetic configuration (or test the U dependence). Report the transferred charge per C and per Ru numerically in the main text, and show how the computed frequencies change.

B. Should address

6. **The SdH harmonic assignments are inconsistent with the fundamentals.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 2f–h.
   - *What is wrong:*
     - Fig. 2f: ν1 = 4.13 T, but the peak labelled 2ν1 is at 9.3 T, not 8.26 T.
     - Fig. 2g: β = 170 T, but 2β = 359 T rather than 340 T.
     - Fig. 2h: β = 176 T and 2β = 347 T (expected 352 T).

     With a field window of roughly 4–9 T, the FFT resolution is about 7 T, so the 19 T discrepancy in S2 and the 1 T discrepancy in Fig. 2f are not resolution effects.
   - *What to do:* State the field window, the background subtraction and the frequency uncertainties. Either justify the harmonic labels (for example by T dependence, since harmonics decay with twice the mass), or reassign these peaks as independent frequencies. A peak near 360 T could correspond to one of the predicted larger pockets (296–444 T), which would change the comparison in concern 2.

7. **Phase purity, staging fidelity and composition are not quantified.**
   - *Where:* Structure analysis; Fig. S1 (referenced, not shown here).
   - *What is missing:*
     - Residual pure graphite is reported, but no phase fractions are given.
     - There are no (00l) linewidths or Hendricks–Teller-type analysis of mixed staging.
     - There is no chemical analysis (EDS/WDS, mass uptake, or STEM-EELS) confirming the Ru:Cl ratio of the intercalant, excluding Ag or AgCl incorporation from the chlorine source, or excluding Cl2/Cl-only intercalation.
     - STEM is relegated to the SI, and the text admits a layer-by-layer pattern "is not observed throughout the sample".
   - *What to do:* Show a STEM image with a measured repeat distance compared with 12.7 Å and 19.4 Å. Add elemental mapping. Estimate the minority-stage and graphite fractions, since these matter for which phase gives which oscillation.

8. **Null controls stop at resistivity.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 2b; transport section.
   - *What is missing:* Graphite annealed in Cl2 serves as a control in Fig. 2b, but its magnetoresistance and SdH spectrum are not shown. Chlorine or acceptor-type surface doping could itself create high-frequency hole pockets.
   - *What to do:* Show the SdH/FFT of the Cl2-annealed graphite, ideally also of graphite treated with AgCl alone, under the same conditions (T, field range, analysis window).

9. **The Hall density is reported without data or comparison.**
   - *Where:* Transport section.
   - *What is missing:* The Hall density of 7.8 × 10^20 cm^-3 is quoted, but the sample, temperature and ρxy(B) data are not given.
   - *Why it matters:* Luttinger counting from β = 170 T (spin and valley degeneracy 4) gives about 1.6 × 10^13 cm^-2 per graphene layer. For stage 2 (two layers per 12.7 Å) that is roughly 2.6 × 10^20 cm^-3, a factor of about 3 below the Hall value. The paper should show the Hall data, and should compare the Hall and SdH densities with the DFT transferred charge. This is an independent constraint on the Fermi-level shift in concern 2.

10. **The number of samples and the reproducibility are stated but not shown.**
    - *Where:* Transport section.
    - *What is missing:* "Measurements on multiple independently prepared samples reproduced the same FFT peak" is given without the number of samples or the data. The absence of α in S2 is attributed to "sample-dependent" behaviour, but no S4 sample lacking α, or S2 sample showing it, is presented.
    - *What to do:* Add a table of all measured crystals (stage, growth batch, frequencies, RRR) and the corresponding FFTs in the SI.

11. **The title and abstract overstate what is shown.**
    - *Where:* Title, abstract and conclusions.
    - *What is wrong:* The title ("platform for exotic quantum phases") and abstract ("comprehensive characterization", "access to novel correlated and topological states") are not supported by any measurement of magnetism, correlation or topology. The paper has no magnetization or susceptibility data showing whether the intercalated RuCl3 orders (bulk α-RuCl3 orders near 7 K) or keeps local moments, no specific heat, and no evidence of flat-band physics.
    - *What to do:* Either add basic magnetic characterization, M(T) with the field in-plane and along c, or retitle the paper and restate these as prospects. The Conclusions' closing caveat ("subject to further spectroscopic and magnetic validation") should be reflected in the abstract.

12. **Structural parameters in the DFT models do not follow from the XRD.**
    - *Where:* Methods; structure optimization section.
    - *What is missing:* The graphene–RuCl3 distance is fixed at 3.60 Å as "suggested by X-ray data", but the XRD gives only the c repeat, not this separation. How 3.60 Å follows from c = 25.4 Å (for example, 12.7 − 3.35 = 9.35 Å for the gallery, and its split between RuCl3 thickness and two van der Waals gaps) should be shown. The S2 structure is also simplified from A|AB|B to AB|AB. The justification given is insensitivity of the charge transfer to registry, but the band structure near K (for example, interlayer coupling across the gallery) may not be insensitive.
    - *What to do:* Give the derivation. Show the sensitivity of the frequencies to d(Gr–RuCl3) and d(Gr–Gr), which the text identifies as the main source of discrepancy.

C. Minor

13. **Possible unit inconsistency in Fig. 2.** Fig. 2b gives Kish graphite ρxx up to about 100 μΩ cm at 300 K, while Fig. 2c shows it in mΩ cm (up to 15 mΩ cm at 9 T). If the units are correct, give the zero-field values at 2.5 K so the large magnetoresistance can be checked against Fig. 2b. State the geometric uncertainty of the absolute resistivities, since the text says they "should not be considered absolute".

14. **Error bars and uncertainties are missing.**
    - Measured frequencies are given without error bars.
    - The computed frequencies inherit an unquantified "systematic error" from smeared unfolded Fermi surfaces (Methods). Estimate it, for example from the spread between the ridge-tracing method and the dhva code for A|A.

15. **Small text corrections.**
    - The Table 1 footnote reads "α-CuI3 layer"; it should read α-RuCl3.
    - "Graphte" appears in Fig. 2a.
    - Table 1 gives the stage-4 a-axis uncertainty as 29.5(4) Å, while the text gives 29.5(A) Å.

16. **The XRD Methods text needs clarifying.** It is hard to follow which data (powder vs single crystal) give a and which give c, given that "all the observed peaks are for hkl (l = 0)". State the supercell relation explicitly, for example a = 29.6 Å ≈ 12 a_graphene ≈ 5 a_RuCl3, and its incommensurability.

17. **The Fermi-surface panels lack frequency labels.** In Fig. 5b–e, label each pocket with its computed frequency, and mark the 0.1 eV-shifted contours alongside the unshifted ones.

18. **More synthesis detail is needed.** Give the reaction-time dependence (stage 4 at 22 h, stage 2 at 48 h, mixed or disordered phases beyond that) with XRD at each time. Comment on air or moisture stability of the crystals, which matters for reproducibility.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one decisive structural measurement of the graphene registry (off-axis XRD or zone-axis cross-sectional STEM with elemental mapping), so that the stacking used in the DFT is measured rather than assumed.
2. Turn the oscillation comparison into a constrained test. Measure the angle and temperature dependence (2D character, m*). Then fit one Fermi-level shift per model, constrained by the Hall density and the Bader charge, across both stages, and report which stackings survive.
3. Recompute at least two stackings with GGA+U+SOC in a magnetic state, so that the RuCl3 bands, and hence the charge transfer, are treated at the level used in earlier graphene/α-RuCl3 work.
4. Add basic magnetic characterization (M(T), M(H)) of the intercalated crystals. Without it, the "exotic quantum phases" framing is unsupported. With it, the paper would make a much stronger case for the platform it proposes.
