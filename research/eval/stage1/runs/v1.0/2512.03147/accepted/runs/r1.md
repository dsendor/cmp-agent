Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports chemical-vapour-transport growth of graphite intercalated with RuCl3, using AgCl as an in-situ Cl2 source. It assigns stage-2 and stage-4 structures from X-ray diffraction and reports Shubnikov-de Haas (SdH) frequencies of about 170 T (and 91 T in stage 4), far above those of pristine graphite. It interprets these frequencies using non-spin-polarized GGA calculations of charge transfer and unfolded graphene Fermi surfaces. A bulk, staged compound that combines multilayer graphene blocks with a Kitaev-candidate layer would be a useful three-dimensional counterpart to graphene/alpha-RuCl3 heterostructures, and the synthesis route could interest the intercalation community. However, the title and abstract promise a "platform for exotic quantum phases". The evidence shown establishes intercalation, staging and strong hole doping, not any correlated, magnetic or topological property. The novelty claim ("for the first time") also needs to be reconciled with older work on graphite-ruthenium chloride compounds that the paper itself cites (ref. 27).

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A simple, reproducible-sounding CVT synthesis with a clear practical finding: excess Cl2 is required for RuCl3 to intercalate. The AgCl/UV route is described in enough detail to repeat.
- The c-axis expansions (25.4 Å for stage 2, 58.2 Å for stage 4) agree well with the expected repeat lengths. With an intercalant gallery of about 9.4 Å and graphite spacing of 3.35 Å, stage 2 gives about 2 x 12.7 Å and stage 4 gives about 3 x 19.4 Å, so the staging assignment is plausible.
- The SdH data are clean: sharp FFT peaks with harmonics. Large-frequency oscillations in a bulk graphite intercalation compound (GIC) give a direct handle on doping of the graphene layers.
- The DFT results are a reasonable starting framework: Bader charges, planar-averaged charge, and unfolded bands and Fermi surfaces for several stackings. The observation that charge depletion concentrates on the interface graphene layers in stage 4 is physically sensible.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The headline framing is not supported by any measurement of correlated, magnetic or topological behaviour. The title, abstract and conclusions present the material as a "platform for exotic quantum phases". The introduction calls RuCl3 "magnetically active" with "possible spin-liquid behavior". The DFT section says the Ru atoms keep "their magnetic nature" (p. 4). Yet no magnetization, susceptibility, specific heat or neutron/muon data are shown, and the calculations are explicitly non-magnetic. Whether the intercalated single RuCl3 layers keep local moments, order (bulk alpha-RuCl3 orders near 7 K) or are quenched by the about 0.5 eV hole transfer is the key question for the proposed use. The interfacial charge transfer is large enough to change the Ru valence substantially. The authors should either (a) add at least M(T) and M(H) for H parallel and perpendicular to c on the stage-2 and stage-4 crystals, with low-temperature specific heat if possible, or (b) retitle the paper and rewrite the abstract and conclusions as a synthesis, structural and fermiology study. They should also state that the magnetic and correlated properties remain untested.

2. The stacking assignments that the DFT and the SdH interpretation rest on are not determined by the data shown. The Methods say the powder pattern contains only (00l) reflections and the single-crystal patterns only (hk0)-type reflections ("hkl (l = 0)"). The stacking sequence (AB|AB, ABAB|ABAB, ABCA|ABCA, AB|BCAB|BC) is then "concluded" from four assumed rules by analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 GICs. Neither data set constrains the lateral registry of graphene layers across a RuCl3 gallery or within a four-layer block. The c-period multiplicity (two repeats for stage 2, three for stage 4) is consistent with several registries. Table 1 lists stage 4 as "AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB", while Fig. 1c,d and the DFT use ABAB|ABAB and ABCA|ABCA, which are different sequences. The motivating rhombohedral (ABC) stacking is therefore neither established nor excluded. The authors should:
   - show the indexed XRD patterns in the main text, with (00l) intensity refinement for the staged models (intensities, not just positions);
   - collect off-axis (h0l)/(hhl) reflections, or use cross-sectional STEM along a zone axis that resolves graphene registry;
   - make Table 1, Fig. 1 and the DFT models refer to the same candidate stackings;
   - state clearly which stacking features are measured and which are assumed.

3. The agreement between computed and measured quantum-oscillation frequencies is overstated, and the key fermiology tests are missing.
   - Stage 2: the AB|AB model predicts 131 T and 444 T against a measured single beta about 170 T.
   - Stage 4: the models predict 70/296 T (ABAB|ABAB) and 28/311/330 T (ABCA|ABCA) against measured 91 and 176 T.
   - An ad hoc 0.1 eV rigid Fermi-level shift gives 27/183 T and 126/214 T. These still differ from the data by 20-40 T, and the shift only reproduces the data if a predicted pocket is pushed below E_F. The text then calls this "good agreement" (p. 5).

   With two to three predicted pockets per structure, a free Fermi-level shift and a free choice of stacking, the comparison has nearly as many adjustable choices as data points. The missing predicted frequencies (for example 444 T in stage 2) are not explained by amplitude arguments.

   The authors should add the following:
   - temperature-dependent SdH amplitudes with Lifshitz-Kosevich fits for effective masses, compared with the calculated cyclotron masses of each pocket. This is a much sharper discriminator between Dirac-like, bilayer-like and flat-band-derived pockets than the frequency alone.
   - Dingle analysis, to argue whether the unobserved pockets should be visible up to 9 T.
   - the angular dependence of the frequencies (F proportional to 1/cos theta for 2D pockets), to confirm that the orbits come from the graphene layers.
   - a statement of the kz dependence of the computed extremal areas (the band calculations use a 6x6x2 mesh, and stage-4 blocks are coupled along c).
   - a toned-down conclusion: the frequencies are consistent with strong hole doping of the graphene layers, but do not yet fix the stacking.

B. Should address

4. The charge counts from Hall, SdH and DFT are not cross-checked. The Hall density of about 7.8 x 10^20 cm^-3 (p. 3) is given without naming the sample, the carrier sign, or the linearity of rho_xy(B). A single-band reading is questionable for a multi-pocket system with residual graphite. A simple Luttinger count helps here. For a Dirac pocket with 4-fold degeneracy, F = 170 T corresponds to about 1.6 x 10^13 cm^-2 holes per graphene layer (E_F about 0.47 eV). With two graphene layers per 12.7 Å repeat in stage 2, that is about 2.6 x 10^20 cm^-3, roughly a factor of three below the Hall value. The DFT Bader charge (Fig. S4) can likewise be converted to a volume density. The authors should tabulate, for each sample, the carrier density from Hall (with sign and fitting model), from the sum of SdH pocket areas, and from DFT. They should then discuss any discrepancy (multi-band Hall, unresolved pockets, inner graphene layers, residual graphite).

5. The level of theory used to compute the charge transfer is not justified. Charge transfer at graphene/alpha-RuCl3 interfaces depends on where the empty Ru t2g (upper Hubbard) states lie relative to the graphene Dirac point. Earlier work (refs. 12, 34) obtained this with GGA+U(+SOC) and a magnetic configuration. Here the calculations are non-spin-polarized GGA with no U and no SOC (Methods). In that approximation bulk RuCl3 is metallic, which is at odds with the text's statement that RuCl3 remains "rather insulating" and its Ru atoms magnetic. The 0.68/0.48 eV band shifts, and hence every computed SdH frequency, may therefore depend on the functional. The later 0.1 eV correction is of the same order as plausible U-induced shifts. The authors should:
   - repeat at least the A|A and AB|AB cases with GGA+U (a range of U) in a zigzag or ferromagnetic configuration;
   - report how the graphene Dirac-point shift and pocket areas change;
   - justify the 0.1 eV shift by this sensitivity, not by "stacking faults" that are not modelled.

6. The approximations in the structural model need quantitative bounds. The 2x2 RuCl3/5x5 graphene cell imposes about 4% tensile strain on RuCl3. This is benchmarked against a 5x5/12x12 cell only for A|A, and only through the band shift. The graphene-RuCl3 distance is fixed at 3.60 Å from the measured gallery, and relaxations use the Gamma point only. The unfolded Fermi-pocket boundaries are taken from the highest-intensity points, with a systematic error the authors acknowledge but do not quantify. The authors should:
   - give the change in pocket areas (in tesla) between strained and unstrained cells, at least for A|A, and for d_Gr-RuCl3 varied by about ±0.1 Å, since they state that the areas are "highly sensitive to the interlayer distances";
   - quantify the unfolding-boundary uncertainty;
   - in light of these numbers, say whether the 20-40 T residual mismatch is significant.

7. The controls against non-intercalant explanations are incomplete. The Cl2-annealed Kish graphite control is shown only as an R(T) curve (Fig. 2b). Chlorine or AgCl/Ag residues could themselves dope graphite or form minority phases. The authors should show magnetoresistance and SdH for the Cl2-annealed control, measured identically, to confirm that the 91/170 T orbits require RuCl3. They should also give the chemical composition of the intercalated crystals: STEM-EDS or EELS Ru:Cl ratios, absence of Ag, and the Ru oxidation state if available. Without this, it is not shown that the intercalant is stoichiometric RuCl3 rather than RuCl3-x or a mixed chloride.

8. Phase purity, stage mixing and sample-to-sample reproducibility are asserted but not shown. The text mentions "a small portion of residual pure graphite", a second crystal piece and "multiple independently prepared samples" with the same FFT peak, without numbers or data. The STEM evidence (only in the SI) is described as "qualitative", with no layer-by-layer staging "throughout the sample". The stage-4 sample shows an extra 91 T frequency that is "sample-dependent". The authors should:
   - state how many crystals of each stage were measured;
   - show overlaid FFTs from at least two independently grown crystals per stage;
   - give stage fractions from the XRD (00l) intensities;
   - bring a representative cross-sectional STEM image with the measured gallery spacings into the main text.

9. Neither the "three-dimensional" character nor the transport anisotropy is characterized. The paper calls the compound a three-dimensional platform, but only in-plane rho_xx is measured. The RuCl3 galleries are expected to decouple the graphene blocks electronically. The angle-dependent SdH requested in point 3, and ideally c-axis resistivity (rho_c/rho_ab), would show whether the system is a 3D electronic material or a stack of decoupled doped multilayers. The latter is what the 2D-pocket analysis implicitly assumes.

10. Some transport observations are not discussed.
    - The stage-4 zero-field resistivity at 1.8 K (about 150 microohm cm, Fig. 2e) is roughly ten times that of stage 2 (about 10-15 microohm cm, Fig. 2d), yet only stage 2 appears in the R(T) panel (Fig. 2b).
    - The residual resistivity ratios are not given, although the text says blade cutting alters the RRR.
    - Given the acknowledged geometric uncertainty, the authors should show R(T) for the stage-4 sample and report RRR for all samples. They should also comment on whether the higher resistivity of stage 4 reflects intrinsic physics, disorder or geometry.
    - The measurement used a conventional four-terminal method with field symmetrization. Contact configuration and current magnitude (to exclude heating at 1.8 K) should be stated.

C. Minor

11. Harmonic labels: in Fig. 2g the peak labelled 2beta is at 359 T, whereas 2 x 170 = 340 T. The field window of 4-9 T gives an FFT resolution of about 7 T, so the 19 T offset is larger than the resolution. Please check whether this peak is a harmonic or an independent orbit, and state the field window and windowing used for each FFT.

12. In Fig. 2h the frequency axis is labelled from 200 T, yet the alpha = 91 T peak is drawn at its left edge. Please correct the axis.

13. Fig. 1 caption: panels c and d are both labelled "(stage 4: S4)" with different stackings, and these differ from the stackings in Table 1. Please harmonize them (see point 2).

14. Table 1 reports in-plane a about 29.6 Å, which matches 12 x a_graphene (29.5 Å) and about 5 x a_RuCl3 (29.85 Å). Yet Methods assumption 1 states that RuCl3 is incommensurate with graphite. Please explain how a was obtained from the single-crystal data and reconcile it with the incommensurability assumption.

15. Methods, "Structure analysis": the powder data are described as "hkl (h = k = 0)" and the single-crystal data as "hkl (l = 0)". Please clarify which reflections were observed in each measurement and show them indexed.

16. Since the intercalant is a single RuCl3 layer, the "alpha" polytype label (which describes bulk stacking) has no meaning inside the GIC. Please use "RuCl3 layers" for the intercalated material or justify the label, and comment on whether beta-RuCl3 (cf. ref. 34) could form at 830-900 °C.

17. The novelty claim "synthesized graphite intercalated with RuCl3 for the first time" (Conclusions) should be reconciled with ref. 27 (Postnikov et al., 1977, graphite compounds with ruthenium chlorides) and other early RuCl3-GIC literature. The authors should state precisely what is new: the stage control, the CVT/AgCl route, the quantum oscillations.

18. The SdH frequencies of Kish graphite (4.13 and 5.16 T) are said to agree with the literature. Since graphite has electron and hole pockets with known values, please assign them.

19. Please state the electron dose and check for beam damage or deintercalation in the STEM measurements, since RuCl3 is beam-sensitive.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a minimal magnetic characterization (M(T), M(H) for both field orientations) of the stage-2 and stage-4 crystals. This alone would tell readers whether the "magnetically active" premise survives intercalation and would substantially strengthen the paper's significance.
2. Make the fermiology quantitative: temperature-dependent SdH with effective masses, angular dependence and a Luttinger count tied to the Hall density. Present computed cyclotron masses and frequencies, with uncertainty bands from U, interlayer distance and strain, side by side with the data in a single table.
3. Constrain the stacking directly (off-axis XRD or zone-axis STEM). If that is not possible, recast the DFT section as a survey of the candidate stackings rather than an identification.
4. Move the key structural evidence (indexed XRD, a representative STEM cross-section with EDS) from the Supplementary Material into the main text. Add the Cl2-annealed graphite SdH control alongside Fig. 2.
