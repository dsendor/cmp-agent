Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports Ni L3 and Cu L3 RIXS on a nominally undoped, reportedly superconducting PrNiO2 film and on an insulating CaCuO2 film, measured in the same apparatus and analysed in the same way. It makes three main claims. First, the nearest-neighbour exchange J1 in PNO is about half that of CCO (46 vs 82 meV), while the interlayer coupling J⊥ is similar (6 vs 7 meV), "indicating" 3D antiferromagnetic order in both. Second, self-doping damps magnons much less than chemical doping does in cuprates. Third, the d_xy orbital excitation in PNO disperses with the opposite sign to CCO, which the authors attribute to nearest-neighbour rather than next-nearest-neighbour orbital superexchange. A side-by-side comparison of the two infinite-layer families is useful. The orbiton dispersion and the inclusion of J⊥ in a nickelate spin-wave fit would be new. However, the two most novel claims rest on fitting choices that the manuscript does not yet test.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Both materials were measured on the same beamline with similar resolution (40 and 42 meV), at the same temperature (20 K) and along the same Brillouin-zone paths. This makes the comparison more direct than comparisons across the literature.
- The PNO magnon is well defined across most of the zone (Fig. 1e, Fig. 3a), with a broadening of about 100 meV (Fig. 3b). This is a relevant data point for the debate on magnetism in "parent" infinite-layer nickelates.
- Polarization-resolved spectra (crossed and non-crossed) are shown for both compounds (Fig. 4a–f) and compared with single-ion cross-sections (Fig. 4g–l). This gives a symmetry-based handle on the dd assignments.
- The proposed orbiton dispersion in a nickelate (Fig. 5a), if it holds up, adds a new observable linking orbital and magnetic degrees of freedom.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **3D antiferromagnetic order and the size of J⊥ are inferred, not measured, and the L dependence needed to extract J⊥ is not controlled.**
   - *Where:* the abstract states that "both materials support a three-dimensional antiferromagnetic order". J⊥ = 5.9 ± 1.1 meV for PNO (Fig. 3a) is justified only by the magnon energy staying "large" (60–80 meV) near Γ "at non-integer L" (p. 4).
   - *The measurement:* all data were taken at a fixed scattering angle 2θ = 149.5°. L therefore changes along every in-plane cut, and the Methods state that "this is not an issue, since the in-plane Js are still dominating". That is inconsistent with fitting J⊥ from exactly those points. The L value of each data point is not given, nor whether SpinW used the actual (H, K, L) of each point.
   - *Fit degeneracy near Γ:* the energies there (60–80 meV) are close to the phonon the authors place at about 80 meV and to the 40 meV resolution. Near specular the elastic line is very strong (Fig. 1e). The Γ-point error bars in Fig. 3a are the largest of the dataset.
   - *Other explanations:* a finite energy at Γ could also come from an exchange anisotropy gap (Ref. 59, which the authors cite), from finite film thickness (15–20 unit cells), from the paramagnon character of a system without long-range order, or from Pr 4f–Ni coupling. None of these is ruled out.
   - *Order itself:* no evidence of order is shown: no magnetic Bragg peak, no temperature dependence, and the intro states that bulk infinite-layer nickelates lack long-range order. Linear spin-wave theory of a Néel state is assumed rather than tested.
   - *Overstatement:* the statement on p. 5 that the J⊥ value is "a proof of the fully occurred topotactic reduction … and the absence of interstitial oxygen" does not follow.
   - *Requested:*
     - (a) Tabulate L for every point in Fig. 3a and confirm that the LSW fit used it.
     - (b) Measure the magnon at fixed in-plane Q near Γ while varying L (by changing 2θ, or with different incident geometries). Show that the energy follows the LSW J⊥ form rather than an L-independent gap.
     - (c) Compare fits with J⊥ = 0 plus an anisotropy gap against fits with J⊥ ≠ 0, with goodness of fit.
     - (d) Remove "order" from the abstract unless direct evidence is provided; "3D spin correlations" is supportable.

2. **The functional form used to fit the orbiton dispersion (Eq. 1) is the next-nearest-neighbour form, so it cannot support the nearest-neighbour conclusion, and the fitted J_orb does not reproduce the bandwidths labelled in Fig. 5a.**
   - *The functional form:* Eq. 1, ε_k = 2J^orb cos k_x cos k_y, is the dispersion produced by hopping along the square's diagonals (NNN). It gives equal energies at Γ and M. Nearest-neighbour propagation gives the form ∝ (cos k_x + cos k_y), with the extremum at M opposite to Γ.
   - *Why the sign alone is not diagnostic:* fitting the PNO data with the NNN form and then calling the positive sign NN propagation (p. 6–7) conflates sign and range. The two cases differ in the shape of the dispersion along [H,H] relative to [H,0], not in sign.
   - *Quantitative mismatch:* with J^orb = 14 meV, Eq. 1 predicts
     - about 56 meV change from Γ to (0.475, 0);
     - about 20 meV change from Γ to (0.34, 0.34).

     Fig. 5a reports 90 meV and 62 meV. The [H,H] branch is off by a factor of about 3. This suggests the NNN form does not describe the PNO data, and that the reported J^orb (and its agreement with theory in Table III) is not meaningful as it stands.
   - *Caption conflict:* the Fig. 5a caption calls the line a "charge transfer model fitting", while the text says it is Eq. 1.
   - *Requested:* fit the [H,0] and [H,H] branches jointly with NN, NNN and NN+NNN forms. Overlay all fits on the data and report χ² and parameters for each. State which form is used for which compound. The NN/NNN conclusion should rest on that comparison.

3. **The PNO d_xy dispersion (60–90 meV on a peak about 0.5 eV wide) has not been shown to be robust against fitting and geometry artifacts.**
   - *Fit components:* in Fig. 2c the d_xy peak overlaps with
     - the 0.7 eV "RE peak";
     - the d_xz/yz peak at 1.93 eV;
     - a broad tail.

     Fig. 4 shows that the relative cross-sections of d_xy and d_xz/yz change strongly with geometry and with the sign of H. Along an in-plane cut at fixed 2θ, the incidence angle changes continuously, so the weights of the overlapping components, the self-absorption and the polarization projection all vary with Q.
   - *Why this matters:* with free widths and intensities, a centroid shift of tens of meV can arise purely from changing component weights. The sign of the effect relative to CCO, where the peaks are narrow and well separated (Fig. 5d), could therefore differ for instrumental rather than physical reasons. Fig. 5b shows no visible shift by eye against the vertical guide lines.
   - *Requested:*
     - (a) State which fit parameters were free, fixed or shared across Q (widths, the RE-peak position and weight, the continuum).
     - (b) Show the fit robustness: refit with fixed widths, without the 0.7 eV component, and with a free continuum.
     - (c) Compare dispersions at +H and −H, where |Q| is the same but the geometry differs. An intrinsic dispersion must be symmetric.
     - (d) Show σ-polarized cuts as an independent check.
     - (e) Give the d_xy linewidth and its Q dependence.

B. Should address

4. **The damping comparison with Bi2201 does not by itself support "self-doping has a much milder impact on spin order than chemical doping".**
   - *Where:* Fig. 3b and p. 4.
   - *Unknown doping:* the effective hole count in PNO is never estimated. Without it, comparing to UD15K Bi2201 (and calling self-doping "non-disruptive") compares samples at unknown relative doping. Ni L3 XAS, Hall data or the O K pre-edge from these films could provide an estimate.
   - *Fit model:* the PNO damping (~100 meV) depends on the background model. No continuum is included for PNO (Fig. 2a; the phonon and magnon overtones are omitted). The authors themselves note that the single-peak fit overestimates the CCO damping near X.
   - *Other causes of broadening:* in a 5 nm film reduced from the perovskite, inhomogeneous broadening (partial reduction, c-axis defects, strain relaxation) could contribute, and this is not separated from intrinsic damping.
   - *Bi2201 comparison:* the Bi2201 values come from a different fitting by a different study (Ref. 49), at a different resolution and possibly with a different background.
   - *Requested:* refit the Bi2201 spectra (or a representative subset) with the present procedure, or demonstrate equivalence. Show how sensitive the PNO damping is to adding a continuum. Give an estimate of the self-doping level.

5. **The theoretical J_orb values in Tables II–III are not independent of the data, so the agreement 14.5 vs 14 ± 4 meV is weak support.**
   - *Parameter choices:*
     - The PNO hoppings are scaled to 80% of the CCO values.
     - Δ = 8 eV was chosen as "sufficiently larger than U also providing reasonable results", which is effectively a tuning.
     - U = 3.2 eV is taken from Refs. 5 and 25. Those are effective one-band U values, not the on-site multiplet Hubbard U that a multiband charge-transfer model requires. Mixing the two is questionable.
   - *CCO inconsistency:* for CCO the same model gives J_NN = 22 meV > |J_NNN| = 15 meV, and the authors invoke a magnetic string effect to suppress NN. That effect is not quantified.
   - *Internal tension:* for PNO the authors argue the string effect is "absent or much weaker" because there is no long-range order. Yet they assert 3D AFM order (Concern 1) and find J1 = 46 meV, a sizeable fraction of the CCO value.
   - *Requested:*
     - (a) Show J_NN and J_NNN as functions of Δ (e.g. 4–9 eV), U and the hoppings.
     - (b) Justify the PNO parameter set against literature estimates of Δ for infinite-layer nickelates. These are commonly substantially below 8 eV.
     - (c) Give a quantitative criterion, even an estimate of orbiton–magnon coupling relative to J1, for when the string effect suppresses NN propagation in one compound but not the other.

6. **The LSW parameters for PNO are weakly constrained and are not compared quantitatively with the literature.**
   - *Constraint:* five exchange constants are fitted to a single path that does not reach X (it stops at 0.475). The [H,H] branch has an anomalous maximum at (0.2,0.2) rather than at (0.25,0.25) (p. 5), and it is not clear whether the fit was forced through it. J2, J3 and J4 are known to be strongly correlated with J1 in such fits. The quoted uncertainties (e.g. J1 = 45.8 ± 0.3 meV) look implausibly small for a 5-parameter fit with error bars as large as in Fig. 3a.
   - *Literature:* "in agreement with the literature [12, 24, 46]" needs numbers. Previous RIXS reports on parent infinite-layer nickelates gave a larger J1 with a 2D model. The difference from 46 meV should be stated and explained (rare-earth or strain dependence, inclusion of J⊥, different longer-range terms).
   - *Requested:* give the parameter covariance matrix. Show fits with a reduced set (J1, J2, J⊥ only), and with and without the [H,H] points beyond (0.2,0.2). Tabulate the literature values.

7. **Sample characterization supporting "superconducting PrNiO2" is not shown, and each result rests on one film per compound.**
   - *What is missing:* the claim that the RIXS sample is superconducting (Tc = 4 K, onset about 11 K, p. 8) is supported only by citing Ref. 40. No R(T), XRD or XAS of the measured film is shown. It is not stated whether the RIXS film is the one whose transport was measured, or how long it was exposed between reduction and measurement.
   - *Temperature:* all RIXS was taken at 20 K, above the superconducting onset. The "coexistence of superconductivity and sharp spin excitations" (p. 3) therefore refers to the normal state of a superconducting compound; this should be stated.
   - *Requested:* include R(T), XRD and Ni L3 XAS of the measured film in the SI, together with the number of films measured and any film-to-film reproducibility of J1, the damping and the d_xy dispersion.

8. **Several dd assignments depend on unstated single-ion parameters.**
   - *Parameters:* the single-ion calculations (Fig. 4g–l) do not list their crystal-field parameters (10Dq/Ds/Dt or the orbital energies), spin–orbit coupling, core-hole lifetime or exchange field.
   - *CCO d_xz/yz splitting:* the calculation splits d_xz and d_yz in CCO, which requires an orthorhombic distortion. The Methods give a = b = 3.86 Å, but the film is on orthorhombic NdGaO3(110), which would naturally provide such a distortion. The splitting should be quantified and justified (in-plane XRD of a and b).
   - *Excluded peaks:* the PNO peak at 0.7 eV is "disregarded" and assigned to Pr 5d hybridized states without independent evidence. Its Q and polarization dependence should be shown, since it directly affects concern 3.
   - *d_z2:* the PNO d_z2 energy (2.96 ± 0.58 eV, Table I) is essentially unconstrained. The text statement that it is "possibly larger" than in CCO should be removed or qualified.
   - *Requested:* add the parameter table.

9. **The conclusions go well beyond the data.**
   - *Where:* p. 7–8.
   - *Unsupported statements:*
     - "IL nickelates are indeed mimicking the essential physics of cuprates, including the mechanisms for superconducting pairing";
     - the lower Tc "is mainly due to the overall smaller energy of spin fluctuations";
     - the Mott-Hubbard character "might imply … smaller electron-phonon interaction and the lack of charge density waves";
     - IL nickelates "are indeed unconventional superconductors very similar to cuprates".

     None of these is tested by the measurements, which concern undoped, normal-state, one-magnon and dd excitations.
   - *Requested:* label these as speculation, or remove them.

C. Minor

10. The text places the phonon at ~80 meV (p. 3), but in Fig. 2a the fitted phonon is centred near 55–60 meV. Please reconcile, and give the fitted phonon energies for both compounds.

11. The Fig. 5c caption says the paths are those of the Fig. 1(d) inset, but the CCO cut ends at (0.33, 0.17), which is not in that inset. Please describe the actual path.

12. Methods, CCO: a "sheet resistance of 2 mΩ" corresponding to "~6000 mΩ·cm" is dimensionally and numerically inconsistent for an insulating film. The unit is likely MΩ/sq; please correct.

13. On p. 4 the J⊥/J1 ratio is compared with the intra-unit-cell coupling of bilayer YBCO. Infinite-layer compounds have uniformly stacked planes, so this comparison is not physically equivalent. Clarify what is meant, or drop it.

14. The suggestion of stripe order from the (0.2,0.2) dispersion maximum and the elastic intensity (p. 5) is unsupported. Either show the elastic intensity versus Q (with specular contributions accounted for) or reduce it to a remark.

15. The Supplementary Information (Ref. 52) and the Zenodo link are placeholders. The fitting details on which concerns 2, 3 and 6 depend are said to be there; they must be available for review.

16. Fig. 3 states that the error bars are 95% confidence intervals from fits. Please state whether counting statistics were propagated and how the summed spectra (Fig. 2a, H = 0.4–0.475) were combined.

17. The PNO lattice parameter a = 3.94 Å is larger than that of the SrTiO3 substrate (3.905 Å). Please state whether the film is relaxed, and comment on the strain state of both films, since the d_xy energies are compared across different substrates.

18. Typos and wording:
   - "out-to-in-plane";

   - "Åand";
   - "infiite" (Ref. 19);
   - "Acknowledgments" duplicated as a heading.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add an L-dependent measurement of the magnon near Γ at fixed in-plane momentum for both compounds. This directly tests J⊥ against an anisotropy gap and would make the 3D claim robust.
2. Refit the orbiton dispersion with NN, NNN and mixed forms on the joint [H,0] and [H,H] data, including ±H and σ/π consistency checks. Present the comparison as a figure in the main text.
3. Provide a sensitivity map of the calculated J_NN and J_NNN versus Δ and U for PNO, using parameters consistent with a multiband charge-transfer model. Quantify the orbiton–magnon coupling that is invoked to explain the opposite sign in CCO.
4. Estimate the effective self-doping of the measured PNO film (XAS or transport) and place it on the Bi2201 damping-versus-doping plot, after harmonizing the fitting procedure.
