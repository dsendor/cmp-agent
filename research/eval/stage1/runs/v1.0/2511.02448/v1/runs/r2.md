Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports Ni and Cu L3-edge RIXS on a nominally undoped but reportedly superconducting PrNiO2 film and on an infinite-layer CaCuO2 film, both measured in the same setup. It compares magnon dispersions and damping, polarization-resolved dd excitations and the momentum dispersion of the dxy orbital excitation. Its most distinctive claim is that the dxy orbiton in PrNiO2 disperses with the opposite sign to CaCuO2. The authors attribute this to nearest-neighbour rather than next-nearest-neighbour orbital superexchange, and they also add an interlayer coupling J⊥ to the linear spin-wave description of an infinite-layer nickelate. Having both compounds in one study, measured on one instrument with one analysis, is useful. As it stands, though, the orbital-propagation interpretation and the claims about 3D order and "mild self-doping" are not yet supported strongly enough.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Side-by-side RIXS on an infinite-layer nickelate and an infinite-layer cuprate, at the same beamline, temperature and scattering angle, with a common fitting procedure (Figs. 1-3). This gives a cleaner comparison of J1 and the magnon damping than putting results from different papers together.
- Polarization-resolved spectra for both compounds at matched geometries (Fig. 4), with single-ion cross-section calculations alongside. These constrain the dd assignments and show an unpolarized continuum in PrNiO2 that is absent in CaCuO2.
- A first report of a momentum dispersion of the dd excitations in an infinite-layer nickelate (Fig. 5). If it holds up, it adds a new observable to the cuprate-nickelate comparison.
- A sharp, dispersive magnon (damping about 100 meV) in a film the authors' earlier work identifies as superconducting. This is relevant to the debate over the parent state of infinite-layer nickelates.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The dispersion formula does not match the nearest-neighbour interpretation. Equation (1), ε_k = 2J^orb cos(kx)cos(ky), is the tight-binding form for hopping along the square diagonals, i.e. a next-nearest-neighbour process (cos(kx+ky) + cos(kx−ky) = 2cos kx cos ky). An orbiton that moves by nearest-neighbour orbital superexchange should instead disperse as ~2J_NN(cos kx + cos ky). Flipping the sign of the coefficient of cos kx cos ky does not turn an NNN process into an NN process. Yet the paper fits both compounds with Eq. (1) and reads the positive J^orb = 14 ± 4 meV in PrNiO2 as nearest-neighbour superexchange. Table III then sets this fitted coefficient against a calculated J_NN (14.5 meV).

   The two forms make clearly different predictions along the measured paths:
   - NN form: equal bandwidths of 4J from Γ to (0.5,0) and from Γ to (0.25,0.25), with the energy still falling towards (0.34,0.34).
   - NNN form: 4J along [H,0] but only about 1.4J from Γ to (0.34,0.34).

   The numbers quoted in Fig. 5 also do not match the fit. Eq. (1) with J^orb = 14 meV gives about 56 meV from Γ to (0.475,0) and about 20 meV from Γ to (0.34,0.34). Fig. 5(a) marks 90 meV and 62 meV. For CaCuO2, J^orb = −11 meV gives 44 and 22 meV, against 56 and 34 meV marked in Fig. 5(c). Equation (1) also has no constant on-site term, although the data sit at about 1.3 and 1.65 eV.

   The authors should:
   - write the full dispersion, ε0 + 2J_NN(cos kx + cos ky) + 4J_NNN cos kx cos ky;
   - fit both compounds with J_NN and J_NNN free, and report the values, uncertainties and the correlation between them;
   - show the fitted curves on the data along both cuts;
   - reconcile the marked bandwidths with the fit.

   The central claim, NN propagation in the nickelate against NNN in the cuprate, depends on doing this.

2. The PrNiO2 dxy dispersion needs to be shown to be robust. In PrNiO2 the dxy feature (Fig. 2c) is a Gaussian roughly 0.5 eV wide. It sits on top of three other components: the 0.7 eV "RE peak", the dxz/yz peak about 0.6 eV higher, and an unpolarized continuum (Fig. 4a-c). The claimed dispersion (60-90 meV) is a small fraction of that linewidth.

   The geometry also changes as H is scanned. With 2θ fixed at 149.5°, the incidence angle changes along the cut, and so does L. Fig. 4 shows that the relative weights of dxy, dxz/yz and the continuum depend strongly on geometry and polarization. That alone could move the fitted dxy centroid without any intrinsic dispersion. It is also notable that the maximum of the dxy energy in Fig. 5(a) coincides with the specular point, where the elastic and low-energy intensity is largest (Fig. 1e).

   The authors should:
   - show the dispersion is symmetric for +H and −H (grazing incidence against grazing emission) at equal |H|;
   - show it is stable under different fit models: free against fixed widths, with or without the 0.7 eV peak and the continuum, and constrained against free dxz/yz positions;
   - overlay raw, normalized spectra at Γ and at the zone boundary, so the shift can be seen without a fit;
   - estimate how large an apparent shift the polarization and geometry factors from the single-ion calculation produce for overlapping peaks of these widths.

   The vertical guide lines in Fig. 5(b) do not make a 60-90 meV shift in a feature about 500 meV wide visible.

3. The orbital argument contradicts the magnetic argument. The spin section concludes that PrNiO2 has a sharp magnon, J1 of about 46 meV and comparable J⊥, "indicating that both materials support a three-dimensional antiferromagnetic order" (abstract and p. 4). The orbital section then argues that the magnetic string effect, which suppresses NN orbiton hopping in CaCuO2, is "absent or much weaker" in PrNiO2 because "the nickelate … does not present evidence of long-range AFM order" (p. 6). These two statements cannot both support the conclusions.

   The string effect depends on antiferromagnetic correlations on the timescale of orbiton hopping, not on static long-range order. The authors' own data show strong short-range correlations: a well-defined magnon with J1 of about 46 meV. The charge-transfer model in Table III also contains no magnon coupling. In CaCuO2 it predicts a positive J_NN of 22 meV that exceeds |J_NNN|, and the authors set this aside by invoking the string effect without calculating it.

   The authors should either:
   - estimate the suppression of NN hopping by spin-orbital coupling for each compound, using the measured J1, for example the ratio J^orb_NN/J1 that controls the string effect in the Wohlfeld et al. framework; or
   - present the NN/NNN interpretation as a hypothesis and make the abstract consistent with it.

B. Should address

4. The charge-transfer model calculation gives only weak support for the interpretation. For PrNiO2, the hoppings are set to 80% of the CaCuO2 values. Δ = 8 eV was "chosen sufficiently larger than U also providing reasonable results" (p. 7), which reads as tuning against the outcome. U = 3.2 eV for Ni is far below the 8 eV used for Cu, and also below the 5-7 eV usually assumed for nickelates. Agreement to within 0.5 meV between J_NN (14.5 meV) and the measurement (14 ± 4 meV) is therefore not independent evidence.

   The authors should:
   - list all parameters with their sources;
   - show how J_NN and J_NNN vary as Δ, U and t_pd are swept over literature ranges, for example Δ from 4 to 9 eV and U from 3 to 7 eV;
   - state whether the sign of the NN-NNN competition survives across that range.

   The model equations should also be given in the Supplement, rather than only by reference to Ref. 43, together with the definition of the orbital superexchange.

5. J⊥ and the claim of 3D antiferromagnetic order. The Methods state that L was not fixed but varies with H (fixed 2θ), and call this "not an issue since the in-plane Js are still dominating". However, J⊥ is extracted from exactly the region where L matters: the magnon energy near in-plane Γ (60-80 meV). The authors should:
   - give the L value of every data point in Fig. 3(a) and include it explicitly in the SpinW fit;
   - consider the alternative explanation for a finite energy near Γ, namely an exchange anisotropy (easy-plane or XY gap), which Ref. 59 raises and the text acknowledges is hard to separate. Measuring at two scattering angles, i.e. two L values at the same in-plane Γ point, would separate a J⊥ dispersion from an anisotropy gap;
   - account for the near-Γ points in PrNiO2 lying within about 1.5-2 resolution widths of a very strong specular elastic line and a phonon at about 60 meV (Fig. 2a, Fig. 1e), with large error bars. Show those fits and the sensitivity of J⊥ to them.

   In addition, a dispersion consistent with LSW does not establish long-range 3D order, especially in a material whose bulk shows no long-range order (refs. 13, 19, 20). "Support a 3D antiferromagnetic order" should become "show three-dimensional spin correlations" unless direct evidence of order is provided.

6. The damping comparison and "mild self-doping". The claim that self-doping has a "much milder impact on spin order" than chemical doping (p. 4, Fig. 3b) rests on three weak points:
   - Absolute damping is compared between a system with J1 of about 46 meV and Bi2201 with J of order 150 meV. In units of the magnon energy or of J1, PrNiO2's damping (about 100 meV against magnon energies of 100-200 meV) is not obviously smaller than underdoped Bi2201's (about 250 meV against roughly 300 meV).
   - The Bi2201 values come from a different paper with a different resolution and fit model.
   - The hole concentration from self-doping in this film is never estimated.

   The authors should normalize the damping, for example as γ/ω(q) or γ/J1, and estimate the self-doped hole density. Ni L3 or O K XAS, or Hall data on the measured film, would serve. Better still, compare with Sr-doped infinite-layer nickelates (Ref. 12 and the authors' own Ref. 46), where the doping dependence of damping in the same material family is known.

7. Characterization of the PrNiO2 film that was actually measured. Several conclusions depend on this specific film being fully reduced, single-phase and superconducting:
   - the superconductivity framing in the title and abstract;
   - "a proof of the fully occurred topotactic reduction" (p. 5), which is circular when inferred from the RIXS spectra themselves;
   - the 0.7 eV "RE peak";
   - the unpolarized continuum.

   Superconductivity in undoped PrNiO2 is not universally reproduced; the reference given is the authors' own Ref. 40. The authors should show, for the film used in RIXS:
   - the resistivity curve;
   - XRD before and after reduction;
   - Ni L-edge XAS, which would expose residual Ni2+/Ni3+ or partly reduced regions.

   They should also state that all RIXS data were taken at 20 K, above both Tc (4 K) and the onset (about 11 K), so nothing was measured in the superconducting state. Finally, they should discuss whether the STO cap modifies the spin and charge response (cf. Ref. 45).

8. Uniqueness and comparability of the exchange constants. Five parameters (J1-J4, J⊥) are fitted to a single dispersion per compound, and J4 and the sign conventions are defined only in a small inset of Fig. 3(a). J1 = 45.8 meV for PrNiO2 is said to agree with the literature, but Ref. 12 reported about 64 meV for NdNiO2 using a different parameter set.

   The authors should:
   - define every coupling in the text;
   - report the fit covariance (J1, J2 and J3 are expected to be strongly correlated when fitted only along M-Γ-X-M);
   - compare model-independent quantities across compounds and references, namely the zone-boundary energies at (0.5,0) and (0.25,0.25).

   The substrates differ (STO against NGO; a = 3.94 against 3.86 Å), so part of the factor of about two in J1 may reflect lattice parameter and strain, as Ref. 57 suggests. This should be discussed.

9. The single-ion analysis and the dd assignments. The crystal-field, spin-orbit, exchange-field and broadening parameters behind Fig. 4(g-l) are not given. The splitting of dxz and dyz in CaCuO2 requires lowering the symmetry below D4h; the authors should state the source, for example orthorhombic strain from NGO(110), and its size. Other explanations for the "fourth peak" should be considered, such as a dd-plus-magnon sideband or a collective-orbiton feature as in Ref. 43.

   The assignment of the 0.7 eV peak to Pr 5d hybridized states, and the suggestion that interstitial s states enhance the dxy intensity, are untested. An incident-energy dependence across the Ni L3 resonance would show whether the 0.7 eV feature and the continuum are Raman-like (fixed loss) or fluorescence-like. That information also matters for concern 2.

10. The concluding claims go beyond the data. Several statements in the conclusions are not tested here:
    - the lower Tc is "mainly due to" smaller spin-fluctuation energy;
    - the Mott-Hubbard character implies weaker electron-phonon coupling;
    - "the lack of charge density waves" in infinite-layer nickelates.

    The last contradicts reports of charge order in infinite-layer nickelates, including the authors' own Ref. 45. These should be removed or presented as open questions with appropriate citations.

C. Minor

11. The elastic maximum near (0.2,0.2) and the extra elastic intensity beyond (0.25,0.25) are read as possible stripe order (p. 5). Without an L scan, a temperature dependence or a peak width compared with the specular tail and with substrate or cap reflections, this should be stated more cautiously. The same section's statement that the magnons "give a proof" of full reduction should be softened.

12. The Supplementary Information (Ref. 52) and the data repository are placeholders. The fitting details (DHO convolution, which widths are fixed, how the phonon and RE components are constrained, and how the 95% intervals are computed) are needed to judge Figs. 3 and 5.

13. Internal inconsistencies:
    - The Fig. 5(a) caption calls the line a "charge transfer model fitting", while the text says it is the Eq. (1) fit.
    - The CaCuO2 path in Fig. 5(c) ends at (0.33,0.17), which is not in the Fig. 1(d) inset that the caption refers to.
    - Table I quotes energies "at BZ edge" without saying which Q, although the peaks disperse.
    - The dxz/yz "center of mass" of 2.05 eV for CaCuO2 does not follow from the Table I values (1.95 and 2.29 eV) without stated weights.
    - Fig. 2(a) is a sum over H = 0.4-0.475, which broadens a dispersing magnon. State whether the damping values in Fig. 3(b) come from individual spectra.

14. Editorial: "out-to-in-plane", "tridimensional", "Åand", a duplicated "Acknowledgments" heading, the meaning of the vertical lines in Fig. 5(b,d), and the undefined sign convention for J2 in the Fig. 3 inset.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Refit the dxy dispersions of both compounds with the general form ε0 + 2J_NN(cos kx + cos ky) + 4J_NNN cos kx cos ky. Present both cuts with ±H data and a fit-model robustness test, so that the claimed sign reversal and its NN/NNN origin rest on a well-defined fit.
2. Add a second scattering angle, or L-resolved points at in-plane Γ, to separate J⊥ from an anisotropy gap, and include the actual L of every point in the spin-wave fits.
3. Put the damping comparison in units of J1 or ω(q). Anchor it with a self-doping estimate from XAS on the same film, and with Sr-doped nickelate data from the same group.
4. Include a parameter-sensitivity map of J_NN and J_NNN from the charge-transfer model, together with an explicit estimate of the spin-orbital (string) suppression for each compound based on its measured J1.
