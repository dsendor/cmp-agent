Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript compares Ni L3 and Cu L3 RIXS data, resolved in momentum and in polarization, for two materials: a nominally undoped but reportedly superconducting infinite-layer PrNiO2 film (PNO) and the insulating infinite-layer cuprate CaCuO2 (CCO). It covers both the magnon and the dd/orbiton regions. The new results are: a linear-spin-wave fit with J1 to J4 and an interlayer J_perp for an infinite-layer nickelate; the observation that the PNO d_xy orbital excitation disperses with the opposite sign to that in CCO; and a spin-orbital (Kugel-Khomskii) calculation that assigns this to nearest-neighbour orbital superexchange enabled by Hund's coupling. A side-by-side comparison of a nickelate and a cuprate measured on the same instrument is useful to the field. However, several headline statements (three-dimensional correlations, opposite orbiton dispersion, coexistence with superconductivity) rest on narrow evidence.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Both materials were measured on the same spectrometer (ERIXS, ID32) with similar resolution (40 and 42 meV) and the same 2theta, which makes a direct comparison possible. The data are deposited on Zenodo.
- The PNO magnon dispersion is mapped across most of the M-Gamma-X-M path (Fig. 1e, Fig. 3a). The damping is extracted and set against chemically doped Bi2201 (Fig. 3b), which gives a quantitative view of self-doping against chemical doping.
- The polarization-resolved dd spectra are compared with single-ion cross-sections (Fig. 4), and the parts that disagree are discussed openly: the mixed character of the 1.29 eV d_xy peak and the fourth peak in CCO.
- The orbiton dispersion in PNO (Fig. 5a) is a new observation. The theoretical mechanism (Hund's exchange giving a free nearest-neighbour orbiton hopping, Eqs. 6 to 20) is worked out explicitly and gives a number (t1_orb = 13.7 meV) that can be compared with the measured 12.5 ± 3.8 meV.
- The supplement documents the fit choices: low-H fits (Fig. 9), the two-peak dd fit (Fig. 7), parameter trends (Fig. 10) and the J_perp scan (Fig. 13).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The claim of three-dimensional antiferromagnetic correlations, with J_perp comparable in PNO and CCO, is not established by the data. In Fig. 3a and Methods, J_perp for PNO is fixed at 5.9 meV rather than fitted. Supplementary Fig. 13 shows that it is constrained only by the few points closest to Gamma. Those points have error bars of roughly ±50 meV (around 75 meV near Q = 0.05), and Supplementary D and Fig. 9 say the magnon merges with the specular elastic line there. Even so, the abstract, the main text ("J_perp/J1 of 8.5% for CCO and 13% for PNO", "comparable value of the spin excitation energy at in-plane zone center") and the Conclusions ("clearly confers a certain degree of tridimensionality") treat J_perp as measured. Two further problems weaken it:
   - Other causes of an apparent gap at Gamma are not considered: an easy-plane or easy-axis anisotropy (which the authors mention via Ref. 65), a finite in-plane correlation length in a sample without long-range order (a spin-glass-like state is cited in the Introduction), and the bias created when a 100 meV-wide damped mode is fitted next to a strong elastic tail.
   - Because 2theta is fixed, L changes along every cut (Methods). The paper does not say whether the LSW model was evaluated at the actual L of each point, and no L-dependence was measured at fixed in-plane Q.

   The authors should do one of the following. They can measure the L dependence of the magnon energy near Gamma (for example, by rotating the sample at fixed H = K = 0.05 to 0.1 to change L), which directly tests interlayer dispersion. They can fit J_perp freely and report its uncertainty, including correlations with J1 to J4, and show that an anisotropy gap or a correlation-length cutoff fits worse. Or they can reduce the three-dimensionality claim in the abstract and Conclusions to an upper bound (0 to 10 meV, as in Supplementary F). The statement that the results "give a proof of the fully occurred topotactic reduction ... and the absence of interstitial oxygen atoms" (p. 5) depends on this same constraint and on Fig. 6, and should be removed or heavily qualified.

2. The robustness of the PNO d_xy orbiton dispersion, and so of the "opposite dispersion" headline, is not demonstrated. In Fig. 5a the d_xy energy changes by 62 to 90 meV. That is small next to the peak's own width: the single-ion calculation uses an FWHM of 0.4 eV, and Fig. 2c shows strong overlap with both the 0.7 eV feature and the broad d_xz/yz peak. Supplementary Fig. 8 shows that the fitted parameters of the overlapping components are strongly cross-correlated. Several things vary along the cut and could produce a shift of a few tens of meV in the fitted centroid without any real dispersion:
   - the 0.7 eV feature, which by the authors' own account (p. 3) grows toward the zone edge;
   - the relative weight of d_xz/yz, which changes with scattering geometry and polarization (Fig. 4);
   - self-absorption, which changes between grazing incidence and grazing emission;
   - L itself.

   The fitted hoppings are t1 = 12.5 ± 3.8, t2 = 0.2 ± 4.8 and t3 = 5.4 ± 1.7 meV. These are three free hoppings plus an offset fitted to a dispersion of about 60 to 90 meV, so the ±H symmetry and the model dependence matter. The authors should:
   - show that the dispersion survives changes to the fit model: with and without the RE peak, with widths fixed across Q, with the d_z2 component removed (as in Fig. 7), and using a model-free first moment over a fixed window;
   - compare equivalent momenta measured in grazing incidence and grazing emission (±H);
   - show the d_xy energy from sigma-polarized maps if they exist;
   - give the d_xy fitted width and amplitude against Q next to Fig. 5a.

   Fig. 5b, with vertical lines fixed at the minimum, is not enough to judge a shift of a few tens of meV on a 0.4 eV-wide peak. If only one sign of dispersion survives every fit model, the claim stands. Otherwise it should be presented as tentative.

3. The agreement between theory and experiment for t1_orb (13.7 against 12.5 meV, Table III) depends on parameters that are chosen rather than determined, and its sensitivity is not shown. From Eq. 20 and the expressions below Eq. 8, t1_orb is proportional to (j_HS − j_LS), with j_HS = 1/(1 − 3J_H/U). With J_H = 1.2 eV and U = 6.5 eV, 3J_H/U = 0.55, so the result sits near a divergence:
   - Lowering J_H to 1.0 eV at the same U reduces (j_HS − j_LS)/2 from about 0.51 to about 0.34, roughly a third, before J̄1 changes at all.
   - The CCO result (t1_orb = 6.7 meV) uses J_H = 1.0 and U = 8 eV. The contrast between the two materials therefore depends largely on these choices.

   The choices are loosely justified. U is taken from the trilayer La4Ni3O8 (Methods), while Supplementary H says the U estimate "is exclusively based on the orbital dispersion", which contradicts Methods. J_H is "based on the atomic value" through a nephelauxetic argument. The hoppings are set to 80% of CCO "to account for the larger d-p splitting". Δ comes from DFT.

   There is also an arithmetic problem. Supplementary I gives J̄1 = U J1_orb/(U − 2J_H) ≈ 27 meV. With J1_orb = 21.6 meV (Table III), U = 6.5 and J_H = 1.2 eV, this formula gives about 34 meV. The value 27 meV corresponds to U/(U − J_H). The authors should:
   - correct or clarify this;
   - state clearly whether U was fitted to the orbiton dispersion (if so, the "agreement" in Table III is partly circular);
   - show t1_orb and J1_orb, J2_orb as functions of U, J_H and Δ over physically reasonable ranges, for both PNO and CCO.

   In addition, the theory drops the orbiton-magnon coupling terms (Eqs. 16 and 17). Yet the main-text argument (p. 8) stresses that string-type scattering strongly suppresses nearest-neighbour hopping. The authors should estimate how much these terms would renormalize t1_orb, for example with the self-consistent Born approximation used in Ref. 45, or at least give a bound. They should also explain why neglecting t3, which is about half of t1 experimentally, does not undermine the comparison.

B. Should address

4. The "coexistence of superconductivity and sharp spin excitations" (pp. 3 to 4) was not measured. All RIXS data were taken at T = 20 K (Methods), above both the onset (just below 11 K) and the zero-resistance temperature (below 4 K). Also, the transport of the specific film measured by RIXS is not shown; Methods only says "the resulting samples show a complete superconducting behavior" and refers to Ref. 42. Superconductivity in nominally undoped PrNiO2 is itself unusual, and it is the basis for the paper's framing. The authors should show R(T) for the film or films actually measured, including after the RIXS beamtime. They should rephrase the coexistence statement as "in a film that becomes superconducting below ...", or else provide data below Tc. The description of the PNO magnon as "sharp" (p. 2) should also be reconciled with the roughly 100 meV damping in Fig. 3b, which is far from resolution-limited.

5. The comparison of damping with Bi2201 (Fig. 3b) does not separate self-doping from other sources of broadening. Possible other sources are disorder and inhomogeneous reduction in a 5 nm film, residual apical oxygen, impurity phases (which the authors themselves invoke for the 0.7 eV peak), and the fact that phonon overtones and multi-magnon continua were left out of the PNO fit (Supplementary B) but included for CCO. The conclusion that self-doping has "a much milder impact on spin order" and offers "a non-disruptive way to achieve superconductivity" needs:
   - a check that the PNO damping is not inflated by the missing multi-magnon and bimagnon terms;
   - confirmation that the Bi2201 values of Ref. 54 were obtained with the same damped-harmonic-oscillator definition and resolution treatment;
   - ideally, a comparison with a non-superconducting or differently reduced PNO film.

6. The strain discussion (pp. 5 to 6) contradicts itself, and the strain state is not measured. The text says that compressive (tensile) strain generally reduces (enhances) the exchange. It then says that in PNO, which is under compression, "strain would tend to increase J1". The signs are also unconventional ("+0.9%" for compressive, "−0.1%" for tensile). More importantly, the PNO in-plane lattice parameter given in Methods (a = 3.94 Å) is the bulk-like value, not 3.905 Å, so it is unclear whether the film is coherently strained at all. The authors should give reciprocal-space maps or the measured in-plane lattice parameters of both films, state the correct direction of the expected change in J1 with citations, and then reassess whether strain could account for part of the 46 against 82 meV difference in J1.

7. The LSW fit and the quoted exchange constants need a closer look:
   - The fitted uncertainties (for example J1 = 45.8 ± 0.3 meV, J4 = 8.0 ± 0.4 meV) look implausibly small for five parameters fitted to the scattered points in Fig. 3a. Correlations between J1, J3 and J4, and the effect of fixing J_perp, are not reported.
   - Along [H,H] the PNO dispersion peaks near (0.2,0.2), not at (0.25,0.25), and the data near (0.25,0.25) fall below the fit (Fig. 3a, Fig. 13). The authors themselves link this to a possible incommensurate reflection or an elastic artifact (p. 6).
   - The sign convention for J2 (negative) is not defined.
   - The statement that J1 agrees "with the literature [12, 24, 51]" is not quantified. Values in the literature come from different models (J1-J2 only, with or without quantum renormalization), so a J1 from a four-neighbour model is not directly comparable.

   The authors should report the parameter covariance, show how robust J1 is when the [H,H] points beyond about 0.2 are excluded or J3 and J4 are set to zero, and give a small table setting their J1 against published infinite-layer nickelate values with the model used for each.

8. Neither the 0.7 eV feature nor the extra d_xy weight in PNO is pinned down, and both feed into the dd interpretation. The text adopts the Ni 3d-Pr 5d hybridization picture while acknowledging a possible extrinsic origin linked to impurity phases (p. 3). It then uses that feature to explain the mixed polarization character of d_xy and the enhanced d_xy intensity under sigma polarization ("a hint of stronger hybridization of Pr 5d with Ni 3d_xy", p. 6). No XAS, incident-energy (resonance) dependence or structural characterization of impurity phases in this film is shown. The authors should provide:
   - the Ni L3 XAS of the measured film;
   - RIXS spectra at a few incident energies across the L3 edge, to tell a Ni-centred excitation from a feature with different resonance behaviour;
   - XRD evidence on secondary phases.

   Without these, the hybridization interpretation of the d_xy polarization anomaly should be stated as one of several possibilities.

9. The Conclusions go well beyond the data. Statements that infinite-layer nickelates "mimic the essential physics of cuprates, including the mechanisms for superconducting pairing", that the lower Tc is "mainly due to the overall smaller energy of spin fluctuations", and that Mott-Hubbard character implies "a smaller electron-phonon interaction and the lack of charge density waves" are not tested by any measurement here. There is also an internal inconsistency: the Conclusions emphasize the "Mott-Hubbard nature" of the nickelate, yet the parameters used (Table II: Δ = 4.4 eV < U = 6.5 eV) place PNO in the charge-transfer regime, consistent with the mixed picture in the Introduction. These sentences should be recast as hypotheses, and the Δ/U statement made consistent.

10. Sample statistics and provenance are not stated. It is not said how many PNO films were measured, whether the magnon and orbiton dispersions were reproduced on a second film, or whether all PNO data come from one beamtime. The CCO data are a re-analysis of data already published in Refs. 45 and 55. Where the re-analysis gives different numbers from those papers (for example J values or d_xy energies), the differences should be noted. The authors should state the number of samples and show the key PNO results (magnon zone-boundary energy and d_xy dispersion) on at least one more film, or state clearly that this is a single-sample study.

11. The test for charge order (Supplementary A, Fig. 6) is limited. It integrates the quasi-elastic signal along [H,0] at whatever L the fixed 2theta gives, with pi polarization only. The charge-order peaks reported in infinite-layer nickelates have a specific L dependence and resonant energy, and the elastic background here is dominated by specular and high-H increases. The authors should say what L is reached near H = 1/3, whether the incident energy matches where the reported charge-order signal resonates, and give a detection limit relative to the intensity reported in Refs. 49 and 57. They should also not use this null result as evidence of complete topotactic reduction.

C. Minor

12. The single-ion cross-section calculations (Supplementary G, Fig. 4g-l) use excitation widths chosen by hand (for example an FWHM of 1.2 eV for d_z2 in PNO, and separate nsf/sf widths for xz and yz in CCO) to "reproduce the remaining peaks". It should be stated which comparisons in Fig. 4 are predictions and which depend on these tuned widths.

13. The d_z2 energy in PNO (Table I: 2.96 ± 0.58 eV) is essentially unconstrained. Statements such as "slightly larger energy than in the cuprate" (p. 6) should be dropped or qualified.

14. The cross-correlation in Eq. 5 is said to be normalized by the product of variances, but it divides by σ_X σ_Y and does not subtract means. The values in Figs. 8 and 11 (up to about 24) are therefore not interpretable as correlation coefficients. Use the standard Pearson definition, or better, report the fit covariance matrix at each Q.

15. Editorial and consistency points:
   - The caption of Fig. 6 gives the integration window as "[−0.1; 0−1] eV", while the figure says (−0.1; 0.1) eV.
   - Supplementary figures are cited as "Figures 9 and 13" and "Supplementary Information V" without a consistent numbering scheme.
   - The caption of Fig. 2a says the data were "obtained as the sum of different spectra along [H,0] with H between 0.4 and 0.475", but Fig. 2b uses a single Q. The authors should explain why the summing was needed for PNO and whether it affects the fitted damping.
   - The caption of Fig. 3 states 95% confidence intervals, whereas Fig. 6 uses shot-noise error bars. Use one convention or state both clearly.
   - The polarimeter efficiency correction applied to the crossed and non-crossed components (Fig. 4, Fig. 12) should be stated.

16. The L value of each spectrum (or the mapping of L against H for the fixed 2theta = 149.5°) should be tabulated or plotted, since it bears on both the J_perp discussion and the dd intensities.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a short L-dependent measurement of the low-energy magnon near Gamma for PNO. This single addition would turn the three-dimensionality argument from inference into measurement.
- Present the PNO d_xy dispersion with a model-independent estimator alongside the multi-peak fit, and include the ±H and grazing-incidence/emission comparison, so that readers can judge the "opposite dispersion" result directly.
- Add a sensitivity map of the theoretical t1_orb (and t1_orb for CCO) over U, J_H and Δ, and correct the formula for J̄1, so the theory-experiment agreement in Table III can be weighed properly.
- Include the transport, XAS and structural characterization (R(T), reciprocal-space maps, phase purity) of the specific PNO film measured. This would support both the "superconducting" label and the strain and impurity-phase arguments.
