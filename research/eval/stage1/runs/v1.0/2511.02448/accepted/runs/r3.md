Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript compares Ni L3 and Cu L3 RIXS on a nominally undoped, superconducting infinite-layer PrNiO2 (PNO) film and an insulating infinite-layer CaCuO2 (CCO) film. It covers the magnon dispersion and damping, the polarization-resolved dd excitations, and the momentum dispersion of the dxy orbital excitation. The most novel element is the report that the PNO dxy orbiton disperses with the opposite shape to CCO (maximum at Gamma). The authors attribute this to nearest-neighbour orbital superexchange enabled by Hund's coupling, in contrast to the next-nearest-neighbour mechanism proposed earlier for CCO. Putting an infinite-layer nickelate and cuprate side by side, with polarimetry and an orbiton model, is a useful contribution. However, several headline statements (the orbiton mechanism, the comparable J_perp, full topotactic reduction, the implications for Tc) currently rest on fixed or chosen parameters, or on fits to broad, overlapping features, more than on direct evidence.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A like-for-like comparison of two infinite-layer compounds, measured on the same beamline (ID32/ERIXS) at similar resolution (40 and 42 meV), with full momentum maps along M-Gamma-X-M (Fig. 1d,e).
- The reduction of the in-plane magnetic energy scale in PNO relative to CCO is robust in the raw data: zone-boundary magnon energy of about 200 meV against about 400 meV (Figs. 1, 3a).
- Polarization-resolved spectra of both the magnon and the dd region in a nickelate, compared with single-ion cross-sections (Fig. 4). Such data are scarce for infinite-layer nickelates.
- A momentum-resolved study of the dd excitations in a nickelate, with a concrete microscopic model for orbiton propagation (Tables II-III, SM section I).
- Low-Q fits, J_perp sensitivity tests and fit-parameter trends are given in the supplementary sections, and the data are deposited on Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The PNO dxy "dispersion" may not be intrinsic. The claimed dispersion (Fig. 5a,b) is a centroid shift of about 60-90 meV in a peak whose fitted width is about 0.4 eV. That peak sits between the q-dependent 0.7 eV "RE" feature, which grows toward the zone edge (Fig. 1b; Sec. II), and a broad, unresolved dxz/yz plus d3z2 tail with strongly cross-correlated fit parameters (Figs. 7-8). Two extrinsic mechanisms could produce a shift of this size. First, the incidence angle changes along each cut at fixed 2theta = 149.5 deg, so the scattering cross-sections change along the cut. Second, the spin-flip and non-spin-flip components of a dd excitation differ in energy by an amount of order the exchange energy, which is tens of meV for J1 of about 46 meV; any q- or geometry-dependent change in their relative weight moves the centroid. Overlapping neighbours whose weight changes with q do the same.
   This matters because the opposite dispersion relative to CCO is the paper's main new result and the starting point for the orbital-superexchange analysis.
   The authors should:
   - compare the dxy peak position at +H and -H at the same |Q| (grazing incidence against grazing emission), using the spectra already taken at H = +/-0.4 (Fig. 4a,b). An intrinsic dispersion is symmetric; a cross-section artifact is not.
   - show that the centroid trend survives (i) fixing the 0.7 eV peak and the dxz/yz/d3z2 widths, (ii) the two-peak model of Fig. 7, and (iii) a model-free first-moment analysis over a fixed window.
   - use single-ion calculations along the actual Q path, including the sf/nsf energy offset, to estimate the apparent shift that geometry alone produces.
   - if possible, repeat one cut at a different scattering angle.

2. The theory-experiment agreement in Table III is not independent of parameter choices, and those choices are internally inconsistent.
   - The calculated NN hopping is t1^orb = (jHS - jLS)/2 * J1~^orb, with jHS = 1/(1 - 3JH/U). This is very sensitive to JH/U. With JH = 1.0 eV instead of 1.2 eV at U = 6.5 eV, the prefactor drops by about one third.
   - J1~^orb in turn depends on Delta (4.4 eV, taken from DFT), on hoppings set by hand to 80% of the CCO values, and on U taken from La4Ni3O8.
   - The Methods state that U was taken from the trilayer nickelate. SM section H states instead that "our estimate of the Mott energy U value is exclusively based on the orbital dispersion". If U was tuned to the orbital dispersion, the agreement between theory t1 (13.7 meV) and experiment t1 (12.5 +/- 3.8 meV) is partly circular.
   - The same three-band parameters should reproduce the measured spin exchange, and this is not shown. A simple estimate J proportional to t_pd^4/Delta^3 with Table II values (0.8^4 x (1.8/4.4)^3) gives J_PNO/J_CCO of about 0.03, far below the measured ratio of about 0.56 (46/82 meV). The parameter set may therefore not describe PNO's superexchange at all.
   The authors should:
   - state unambiguously how U was obtained;
   - compute the spin J1 for both compounds from the Table II parameters and compare it with Fig. 3a;
   - propagate reasonable ranges of JH, U, Delta and t_pd into an uncertainty band on the theoretical t1^orb, J1^orb and J2^orb, and show whether the sign and NN dominance survive across that range.

3. The claim of comparable out-of-plane exchange and similar three-dimensional antiferromagnetic correlations (abstract; Sec. II; Conclusions) rests on a parameter that was not fitted. For PNO, J_perp = 5.9 meV was held fixed (Fig. 3a table, "fix"). Fig. 13 shows that it is constrained only by the few points nearest Gamma, whose error bars are about 30-50 meV and where the magnon merges with the elastic line (Fig. 9). Other effects can lift the energy near Gamma:
   - an XY or easy-plane anisotropy (the authors' Ref. 65 is cited for this);
   - a finite correlation length in a film that, by the authors' own statement, has no established long-range order;
   - the broad damped lineshape (damping about 100 meV) fitted with a single DHO convolved with resolution.
   In addition, L is not fixed in the measurement (Methods), so the J_perp-induced gap depends on the L of each point.
   This matters because "3D AF correlations in both materials" and "J_perp/J1 = 13%" are stated as findings. The further claim that the J_perp result gives "a proof of the fully occurred topotactic reduction ... and the absence of interstitial oxygen atoms" does not follow at all.
   The authors should:
   - state whether the LSW fit uses the actual (H, K, L) of each spectrum;
   - fit J_perp as a free parameter, or with an anisotropy gap as an alternative, and report the confidence interval and the chi^2 comparison;
   - ideally, measure an L scan at the in-plane zone centre (for example at a different scattering angle or incident geometry) to test for an L dependence of the low-energy magnon.
   Otherwise the 3D statement should be reduced to "consistent with J_perp in the range 0-10 meV" and the topotactic-reduction claim removed.

B. Should address

4. The J1 values and their comparison with the literature are model-dependent. Five exchange parameters (J1-J4 and J_perp) are fitted to a single dispersion along M-Gamma-X-M that stops at H = 0.475 and does not reach X. Their covariances are not reported. The quoted uncertainties (for example J1 = 45.8 +/- 0.3 meV) look small for a magnon that is about 100 meV wide. Earlier nickelate RIXS studies (Refs. 12, 47) mostly used J1-J2 descriptions and reported larger J1, so the statement "in agreement with the literature" (46 meV) compares parameters from different Hamiltonians. The authors should:
   - report the correlation matrix and how the 95% intervals were obtained;
   - refit both compounds with the same reduced model (J1-J2, or t/U with ring exchange) for a like-for-like comparison with prior work;
   - express the PNO/CCO comparison in terms of model-independent quantities, such as E(0.5,0), E(0.25,0.25) and their difference.

5. The magnon damping comparison does not isolate self-doping (Fig. 3b; Sec. II). The PNO width of about 80-100 meV is compared with Bi2201 widths from a different sample family, a different experiment and a different fitting chain (Ref. 54). The hole content from self-doping in PNO is unknown. The broadening could also come from extrinsic sources: thin-film disorder, partial reduction, the capping interface, L-integration, or the unfitted multimagnon/bimagnon weight that the PNO fit omits. The statement that self-doping has "a much milder impact on spin order than chemical doping" therefore does not follow quantitatively. The authors should:
   - fit the PNO and Bi2201 spectra with the same model and resolution;
   - estimate the self-doped hole density (for example from the O K or Ni L3 XAS pre-peak, or from Hall data in Ref. 42);
   - discuss extrinsic sources of damping;
   - ideally, compare with a non-superconducting or differently reduced PNO film.

6. Sample characterization relevant to the RIXS conclusions is missing.
   - No Ni L3 or O K XAS is shown, so a residual Ni2+ (incompletely reduced) fraction is not excluded. Such a fraction would add dd weight near 1-2 eV and could produce the mixed polarization of the 1.29 eV peak and the unpolarized continuum (Fig. 4).
   - The text links the 0.7 eV peak to impurity phases (Ref. 49) and notes unexplained elastic features near X and an incommensurate maximum near (0.2,0.2) (Sec. II). These point to secondary phases that should be checked.
   - The absence of charge order (Fig. 6) is used to "confirm the fully occurred topotactic reduction". However, the authors' own Ref. 50 reports that STO-capped films do not show the (1/3,0) order. The intensity window of +/-0.1 eV and a single incident energy (852.48 eV, not necessarily the energy at which the order resonates) further weaken the test.
   - The number of PNO films measured, whether the RIXS film is the one whose rho(T) defines Tc, and whether key results were reproduced on a second film are not stated.
   The authors should:
   - add XAS;
   - show rho(T) and XRD for the measured sample;
   - state the sample count;
   - temper the reduction claim.

7. The polarimetric analysis needs its procedure and a sensitivity analysis (Fig. 4, Fig. 12; SM section G).
   - The paper does not describe how the crossed and non-crossed components are extracted: polarimeter efficiency, multilayer reflectivity ratio, normalization.
   - The PNO components are noisy enough to require 15-point smoothing (Fig. 12).
   - The single-ion calculations use the experimental peak energies of Table I as input, and per-peak widths chosen to match. For CCO the dxz and dyz features were also given separate sf/nsf widths. The phrase "well reproduced within a single-ion model" therefore tests only relative polarization weights, and only visually.
   - The spin direction (in-plane, 45 deg) is assumed for PNO, which has no established long-range order. This choice affects the sf/nsf weights.
   The authors should:
   - describe the polarimeter calibration;
   - give quantitative crossed/total ratios with uncertainties for each peak, measured against theory;
   - show how the PNO predictions change for other spin orientations, or for a spin-averaged paramagnetic case, before interpreting the dxy discrepancy as Pr 5d or interstitial-s hybridization.

8. The CCO and PNO orbiton dispersions are fitted with different models, and the fitted parameters do not match the quoted bandwidths.
   - CCO is fitted with a pure NNN term (Eq. 2, with no constant offset written). PNO is fitted with NN, NNN and third-neighbour terms (Eq. 1). The "opposite dispersion" and "NN vs NNN" contrast therefore partly reflects the choice of model. SM section I itself estimates a finite NN orbiton hopping of t1 = 6.7 meV for CCO.
   - Eq. 2 with t2 = -13.6 meV gives bandwidths of 54 meV along Gamma-X and 27 meV along Gamma-(0.25,0.25). Fig. 5c quotes 70 and 61 meV, and the white curve at (0.25,0.25) appears close to the maximum rather than midway.
   - For PNO, Eq. 1 with the quoted t1, t2 and t3 gives about 50 meV along Gamma-(0.475,0) and about 100 meV toward (0.34,0.34). Fig. 5a quotes the reverse (90 and 62 meV).
   The authors should:
   - fit both data sets with the same Eq. 1 form and report the parameters with covariances;
   - check that the plotted curves correspond to the quoted parameters;
   - reconcile the bandwidths with the arrows in Fig. 5.

9. The free-orbiton approximation drops terms larger than the one kept (SM Eqs. 15-19). The kept free hopping has prefactor (jHS - jLS)/2, about 0.5. The neglected orbiton-magnon terms have prefactors (jHS + jLS)/2, about 1.7, and jHS, about 2.2. The main text itself stresses that magnon dressing "strongly" suppresses NN orbiton motion. Dropping these terms because they produce mostly incoherent weight does not establish that the coherent dispersion is unrenormalized. The fitted t3, about half of t1, is also left unexplained. The authors should:
   - at least estimate the renormalization of the coherent orbiton band, for example with a self-consistent Born or linear-string calculation like the CCO treatment in Ref. 45;
   - state the expected quasiparticle weight;
   - relate the large PNO dxy width (about 0.4 eV FWHM) to the incoherent part.

10. The strain discussion is internally inconsistent (Sec. II, p. 5-6).
    - The text states that compressive strain "generally reduc[es]" the exchange, then that strain "would tend to increase J1 in PNO".
    - The strain is quoted as "+0.9%" while called compressive.
    - The film lattice parameter a = 3.94 A quoted in the Methods equals the bulk value used to compute the mismatch. Either the 5 nm film is relaxed, in which case there is no 0.9% strain, or a is not the measured in-plane parameter.
    The authors should report the measured in-plane lattice parameter (from RSM) and correct the sign logic.

11. Several conclusions go beyond the data (Conclusions; abstract):
    - that the lower Tc of nickelates "is mainly due to" smaller spin-fluctuation energy;
    - the "Mott-Hubbard nature" causing weaker electron-phonon coupling and no CDW;
    - that the rare earth "enables a doping mechanism";
    - that the larger charge-transfer energy Delta is a result. Delta is an input taken from DFT and is not measured here.
    The paper itself leaves the origin of the 0.7 eV peak open, possibly extrinsic. These statements should be presented as hypotheses, or removed.

C. Minor

12. Table I gives the PNO d3z2 energy as 2.96 +/- 0.58 eV. The text says it lies at "slightly larger energy than in the cuprate" (2.68 eV), which the uncertainty does not support. Fig. 8 shows strong intensity cross-correlations for this peak.

13. The ratio J_perp/J1 = 13% for PNO (Sec. II) uses the fixed J_perp. Quote it as a range (0-22% for J_perp = 0-10 meV).

14. The cross-correlation of Eq. 5 is neither mean-subtracted nor normalized by the number of terms, so the values of 24 in Fig. 8 and 2.2 in Fig. 11 cannot be read as correlation coefficients. Use the standard parameter covariance or correlation matrix from the fit instead.

15. Fig. 4(d),(j) are labelled Q = (-0.45,0) for CCO, while the text says H = +/-0.5.

16. Fig. 2(a) sums spectra over H = 0.4-0.475, across which the magnon disperses. State this when quoting widths from that panel.

17. Typographical and notation issues:
    - Eq. 1 has a malformed "cos(2πky)".
    - Fig. 6 caption reads "[-0.1; 0-1] eV".
    - "Superconductive IL phase" should read "superconducting".
    - The Fig. 1 caption's "triple path" should be defined.
    - The Methods say both the hopping integrals and Delta are "taken from" Ref. 6 while also being scaled by hand; clarify which is which.

18. All data are at 20 K, above Tc of about 4 K. The repeated "coexistence of superconductivity and sharp spin excitations" should specify that the spectra are in the normal state of a superconducting film.

19. The resistive transition is broad: onset just below 11 K, zero resistance below 4 K. Show rho(T) for the measured film and briefly discuss inhomogeneity.

20. The elastic intensity rise near X and the maximum near (0.2,0.2) are mentioned but not analyzed. If kinematics prevent a check, say what other measurement would distinguish an impurity-phase reflection from an intrinsic feature.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a direct test of the intrinsic orbiton dispersion: compare +H and -H at equal |Q|, use a model-free first moment, and simulate geometry-induced centroid shifts (single-ion with sf/nsf offsets). This would turn the paper's most novel claim into a robust one.
2. Make the microscopic modelling self-consistent. Use one documented parameter set (with uncertainty ranges) that reproduces the spin J1 ratio and the orbiton hoppings in both compounds, and fit the CCO and PNO orbiton dispersions with the same functional form.
3. Add sample-level evidence: Ni L3 and O K XAS, rho(T) and XRD of the measured film, the number of films measured, and an estimate of the self-doped hole density. Use it to replace inferential claims of "full topotactic reduction".
4. Recast the 3D-coupling and damping comparisons in model-independent terms: gap at Gamma with its confidence interval, possibly L-dependent; widths fitted with identical models for PNO, CCO and Bi2201. Move the speculative implications for Tc to a clearly labelled outlook.
