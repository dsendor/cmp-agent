A. Must address

1. No quantitative estimate shows that Harper broadening can produce the observed linewidth at 300-500 T (pp. 8-9, Fig. 4b). The only argument offered is that at 300 T, ell_B ≈ 14.8 Å ≈ 3.5 a0 and the flux per unit cell is alpha = phi/phi0 ≈ 0.013 (q ≈ 77 in alpha = p/q, i.e. roughly 77 unit cells per flux quantum; the value reproduces with a0 = 4.27 Å). In the Harper/Hofstadter problem at small alpha, the width that the periodic potential gives to the lowest Landau levels near a tight-binding band edge is controlled by the overlap of neighbouring cyclotron orbits and is exponentially small in 1/alpha, so at alpha ≈ 0.01 it is expected to be vanishingly small. The Cu 4s conduction band and the 3d valence bands have eV-scale widths, while hbar*omega_c at 300 T is only about 80 meV for mu* = 0.415 m_e; on the 15 Å scale the carriers should still see a well-averaged potential, and the leading lattice correction at ell_B/a0 = 3.5 should be a smooth band non-parabolicity of order (a0/ell_B)^2. Since ell_B falls smoothly as B^(-1/2), nothing in the proposed mechanism produces a "sudden" onset or a threshold at 300 T. The exciton is also neutral, so the authors must explain how electron and hole Harper broadening at alpha ≈ 0.013 would carry over to a bound p0 state whose relative motion involves both carriers. In addition, the phrase "the significant increase of the degeneracy of the energy levels" (p. 9) has the physics backwards: the lattice lifts the Landau-level degeneracy and splits each level into subbands. This matters because the title and abstract depend on Harper physics being able to account for the observed excess width at 450-500 T (estimated by the reviews at roughly 4-5 meV, 5-10 meV, or 10-20 meV, depending on how Fig. 4a is read). The paper calls the agreement "at least qualitative" (p. 9) but gives no magnitude. The authors should compute the Harper (Hofstadter-type) bandwidth versus B from 0 to 500 T, for example with a tight-binding model of the Cu2O conduction and valence bands using realistic hopping parameters or published band structures, the real cubic lattice and Peierls phases for B || [001], or with a lattice-corrected magneto-exciton calculation, treating both carriers. They should overlay the predicted extra FWHM on Fig. 4a and state the mechanism precisely. If the prediction is orders of magnitude below the observed width, the title, abstract and conclusions must change, and the Harper attribution should at most be presented as one speculative candidate among several.

2. Intrinsic, non-lattice mechanisms that would produce both the excess broadening and the shortfall in energy relative to the hydrogen model above 300 T are not considered (pp. 6-9, Fig. 2, Fig. 4a). Each of the following is expected in exactly this field range in Cu2O. (a) Valence-band non-parabolicity and mixing between the yellow (Gamma7+) and green (Gamma8+) bands: the spin-orbit splitting is about 130 meV, and cyclotron energies reach several tens of meV for the hole at 300 T and about 140 meV for mu = 0.415 m_e at 500 T. As the wavefunction is squeezed it samples larger k and the effective hole mass grows, the same physics behind the known quantum defect of the Cu2O P series. This naturally produces an "increase of the reduced effective mass" (p. 3), which is what the authors report, and is captured by multiband k·p theory. (b) Magneto-polaron resonances and screening: the internal energy spacings approach and exceed the Cu2O LO-phonon energies (about 19 and 82 meV). The hole cyclotron energy, 0.116 meV/T × B / m_h with m_h ≈ 0.58-0.69, crosses the ~82 meV Gamma4- LO mode between roughly 400 and 500 T, and resonant Fröhlich coupling is a textbook cause of both broadening and level bending at such crossings; the crossover from static to high-frequency dielectric screening would also shift and broaden the level. Each of these is a breakdown of the simple hydrogen model, but not Harper broadening, so without excluding them "evidence for Harper broadening" does not follow. The authors should estimate each mechanism quantitatively with literature band parameters and phonon energies, give the field at which it becomes important, and compare, at minimum, a multiband k·p estimate of the yellow-green mixing correction with the observed energy deviation of about 8-10 meV at 500 T. They should explain why the data favour a lattice-periodicity effect over these.

3. Level crossing, anticrossing or an unresolved neighbouring line could explain the high-field anomaly (Fig. 2, Fig. 4a, Fig. S3a). The authors describe 2p0(Sz=+1) as "well separated from other excitonic transitions", but their own Fig. S3(a) shows several calculated levels approaching or crossing it between 200 and 500 T: notably 2p-1(Sz=0) (grey dashed), 2p-1(Sz=+1), 2p0(Sz=0) and branches descending from 3p. 4. The 300 T upturn rests on four or five points (350, 400, 450, 500 T, arguably 300 T) from a single technique, EMFC, apparently from a single destructive shot on a single crystal, with no stated reproducibility or controls for experimental artifacts (Fig. 4a, Fig. 2, Methods D). Neither the NDPM nor the STC data reach above about 200 T to cross-check them. The manuscript does not state: how many EMFC shots were taken and whether the upturn appears in more than one shot, or in the up- and down-sweep of one shot; how the field at the sample was measured (pickup coil, Faraday rotation), how it was calibrated, and its uncertainty at 300-500 T; the field homogeneity across the 1.8 × 1.8 mm sample and the 800 µm probed spot as the liner implodes; the streak-camera time window and resolution, the dB/dt at each field (which grows steeply near peak field) and hence the field interval averaged in one spectrum, and how the B(t) trace was synchronised with the spectra; the spectral resolution of the STC/EMFC polychromator compared with the NDPM CCD spectrometer; whether the EMFC spectra were polarisation-resolved (unpolarised detection would superpose the rapidly rising sigma- lines); and whether the sample stays at 45 K during the shot, and why 45 K was chosen. Each of these can add width that grows with field: field inhomogeneity and field sweep within an exposure contribute (dE/dB)·ΔB, and dE/dB for 2p0(Sz=+1) rises above 200 T. Sample heating during the pulse (eddy currents, shock) would produce exactly the pair of signatures reported, a red shift from band-gap shrinkage plus phonon broadening. A few-percent error in B would shift the points by 10-25 T, comparable in effect to the few-meV energy deviation in Fig. 2. The authors should report the number of shots and show the upturn in at least two independent EMFC runs or in up- and down-sweep data; give the field uncertainty and the inhomogeneity in tesla at each field and add horizontal error bars to Fig. 2 and Fig. 4; convert inhomogeneity and field change during each exposure into linewidth contributions using dE/dB from Fig. 2 and subtract or bound them in Fig. 4a; give an estimate or proxy of the sample temperature during the shot (for example from the 3p or background absorption edge, or from a zero-field series of the 2p linewidth and energy at 45-150 K showing what heating would be needed to mimic the data); state the polarisation configuration and report linewidths only from polarisation-resolved spectra; and add the missing details to the Methods for STC and EMFC. Raw streak images and B(t) traces would help.

5. The high-field linewidths and the "sudden" upturn depend on fits to a weak feature that are not shown, and the statistical case for a threshold is not made (Fig. 1b, Fig. 3, Fig. 4). In Fig. 1b the 2p0(Sz=+1) feature at 350-500 T is a small dip or step barely above the noise on a steep background, and it sits on the low-energy tail of the rising absorption step that moves through 2.17-2.20 eV; its width therefore depends strongly on the background model and the line shape. Only the 120 T fit, where the feature is strong, is shown (Fig. 3); the Fig. 3a background (orange) is a hand-chosen smooth curve whose form above 300 T is not shown. The "asymmetrical Gaussian" is not defined (the Fig. 3 caption says "multiple Gaussian functions"), and it is not said how the FWHM of an asymmetric profile is computed. The origin of the FWHM error bars (fit standard error or shot-to-shot spread) is not explained, and the 500 T point has the smallest error bar, which is odd for the weakest feature. Relative to the blue guide line, fitted up to 300 T, the 350 and 400 T points sit about 0.7 above it with error bars of about ±0.5, so only the 450 and 500 T points clearly deviate; a quadratic or other smooth form over the full range might describe the data equally well, and plotting Fig. 4b against log ell_B with a vertical line at 300 T suggests a threshold that the theory does not motivate. The authors should show the fits at 300, 350, 400, 450 and 500 T with residuals; state which parameters were free or fixed, including the asymmetry and background form; report how the FWHM changes with alternative backgrounds (polynomial order, fit window) and with symmetric versus asymmetric line shapes; explain the error bars; compare linear, linear-plus-threshold, quadratic and power-law models with a goodness-of-fit or statistical criterion so that "sudden" is supported rather than read off the plot; and plot the integrated intensity of the line versus field, to distinguish genuine broadening at constant oscillator strength from a fading line whose width is poorly constrained.

6. The argument that rules out the 2p±1 assignment uses the hydrogen-atom form of the orbital Zeeman term, and the selection-rule reasoning is not derived (p. 6 point (ii); SI Sec. I-II, Fig. S2, Fig. S3). The paper states that "the exactly ħω_c splitting of the 2p±1 states is imposed by time reversal symmetry", with omega_c = eB/mu, and deduces mu* ≈ 2 m_e, "five times too large", if the strong lines were 2p±1. That holds only when one particle is infinitely heavy. For an exciton at zero centre-of-mass momentum with comparable masses, the linear orbital term in the relative-motion Hamiltonian is (e*hbar*B/2)(1/m_e - 1/m_h)·L_z, not (e*hbar*B/2mu)·L_z. With literature Cu2O masses (m_e ≈ 0.99-1.0, m_h ≈ 0.6-0.7 m_e), the expected 2p+1 to 2p-1 slope is about 0.05-0.08 meV/T, several times smaller than ħω_c(mu) and the same order as the observed 0.11-0.116 meV/T in Fig. S2. The reductio therefore fails, the two hypotheses predict nearly the same linear slope (Fig. S2 shows them overlapping) so the slope alone cannot discriminate, and the fits of the m ≠ 0 branches in Fig. S3 (Makado-McGill plus spin Zeeman only) omit this term. Further gaps: the text says the p excitons arise from an electric-quadrupole transition (p. 5; SI p. 3), whereas in Elliott's classification the yellow np excitons are dipole-allowed (Gamma4-, second class) and it is the 1s state that is quadrupole-allowed, so the selection-rule reasoning built on this must be redone; the claim that "sigma± couples strongly only with spin-flip transitions regardless of m" (SI p. 5) is asserted from fits, not derived, and the SI's conclusion that the shoulders are 2p+1(Sz=-1) and 2p-1(Sz=+1) contradicts the selection rules stated a paragraph earlier; and the Gamma7+ hole is a spin-orbit-coupled state, so the "Sz" labels, the pure S = 0/1 spin structure and the "hole g-factor close to zero" (p. 6) need justification and a literature value with uncertainty for B || [001]. This matters because the assignment is a headline claim of the abstract and both mu* and the "deviation from the hydrogen model" depend on it. The authors should redo the Fig. S2 comparison with the correct (1/m_e - 1/m_h) coefficient and literature masses; state which hydrogen-model Hamiltonian (with or without the mass-asymmetry term) was used in the fits; derive the Faraday-geometry selection rules for the Gamma5- p-exciton states with the Gamma7+ hole for B || k || [001] (O_h → C4h), including the hole g-factor, ideally as a table of which of the nine 2p sublevels appear in sigma+ and sigma- and with what strength; present the alternative assignment side by side with residuals; and discuss explicitly how and why this assignment differs from Artyukhin et al. 2018, Zielińska-Raczyńska et al. 2019 and Kobayashi et al. 1989 (refs. 18, 11, 27).

7. How mu* = 0.415 ± 0.01 m_e was extracted is unclear, and the quoted uncertainty is not supported (Table I, Fig. 2, p. 6, SI Sec. II). Ry* = 103 meV is "imposed by the measured zero field 2p-3p splitting which has to follow the 1/n² Rydberg series", but Cu2O p excitons have non-zero quantum defects from band non-parabolicity (Kazimierczuk et al., ref. 24), and Ry* is 5% above the accepted 98 meV, so this biases mu*. E_g = 2.1756 eV is given without uncertainty, so it is unclear whether it was fixed or fitted, and it differs from commonly cited gap values; it is also not stated how g = 2.0 ± 0.1 was obtained (Fig. S2 or the global fit). The diamagnetic curvature fixes mu* only together with Ry* (equivalently epsilon), and the two are strongly correlated. The 3p and 2p0(Sz=-1) branches are followed only to about 130 T, so above that only the single 2p0(Sz=+1) branch constrains the fit, and "fits very well to the four levels up to 300 T" overstates the constraint. The fit range ends exactly where the deviation is claimed, which makes the deviation partly circular. The authors should state which parameters were free, which points and field range entered the fit and how they were weighted; give the covariance between mu*, Ry* and g; show how mu* changes if the fit stops at 150, 200 or 250 T, if Ry* is set to 98 meV, or if a quantum defect is included; and discuss why mu* = 0.415 lies at or above the top of the quoted literature range (0.35-0.41 m_e), for example polaron corrections or the model's neglect of mass anisotropy and central-cell effects. Without this, ±0.01 m_e (2.4%) looks optimistic.

10. The picture of an exciton reaching lattice scale is not put on numbers, and the threshold ell_B/a0 = 3.5 is not motivated (pp. 8-9, Fig. 4b). The argument compares the free-carrier ell_B with a0, but the relevant object is the bound 2p0 exciton. At 300 T, ell_B ≈ 15 Å is similar to the 1s Bohr radius of the yellow exciton (around 7-10 Å for epsilon ≈ 7.4, mu* = 0.415), whereas the in-plane extent of 2p0 is set by the relative-motion magnetic length and its extent along B, set by the Coulomb interaction, stays larger. The authors should give the in-plane and longitudinal sizes of 2p0 at 300 and 500 T from the hydrogen model, explain why lattice effects should switch on at ell_B/a0 = 3.5 rather than at some other ratio, and justify using the cubic-cell flux alpha (p. 9) for a six-atom cell whose Cu sublattice has a different spacing.
