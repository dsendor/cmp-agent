Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors measure magneto-absorption of the yellow 2p and 3p excitons in one Cu2O crystal using three pulsed-field techniques (NDPM to 60 T, single-turn coil to about 150 T, EMFC to 500 T). They make three claims: (i) the two strong 2p lines are the spin-Zeeman-split 2p0 orthoexciton states (Sz = ±1), not 2p±1; (ii) a hydrogen-model fit gives mu* = 0.415 ± 0.01 me; (iii) above 300 T the 2p0(Sz=+1) linewidth rises sharply and its energy falls below the hydrogen model, which they offer as possible evidence for Harper broadening, meaning a breakdown of the effective-mass approximation once the magnetic length approaches the lattice constant. Excitonic spectra at 500 T in a bulk semiconductor are rare and hard to obtain, and a signature of lattice-scale (Hofstadter/Harper) physics in a natural crystal would be of wide interest. As the manuscript stands, however, the Harper interpretation rests on a qualitative argument and on about five linewidth points from EMFC, and the alternative explanations have not been tested.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The data are experimentally demanding: one Cu2O crystal followed from 0 to 500 T across three field-generation methods, which allows a direct cross-check between techniques.
- Below 31 T the NDPM data agree with the DC-field data of Artyukhin et al. (Fig. S1), which gives some confidence in the energy calibration of the pulsed-field spectra.
- The 2p0(Sz=+1) line is followed in energy to 500 T. This extends the magneto-exciton energy map of Cu2O far beyond earlier work, and the deviation from the hydrogen model above about 300 T (Fig. 2) is itself a useful observation, whatever its cause.
- The question asked is clear and worthwhile: where does the effective-mass description of a Wannier exciton fail as ell_B shrinks toward the lattice constant?

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. No quantitative support that the broadening is Harper broadening (pp. 8–9, Fig. 4b). The argument is only that at 300 T, ell_B ≈ 14.8 Å ≈ 3.5 a0 and alpha = phi/phi0 ≈ 0.013 (I reproduce this value with a0 = 4.27 Å). Harper/Hofstadter physics does not predict a threshold at ell_B ≈ 3.5 a0. In the low-flux limit, the Landau levels near a tight-binding band edge broaden into Harper subbands whose widths are exponentially small in 1/alpha. For alpha ≈ 0.013 (q ≈ 77 in alpha = p/q), and for Cu2O conduction (Cu 4s) and valence bands with bandwidths of order eV, against cyclotron energies of tens of meV, the expected lattice-induced width of the lowest Landau-level-derived states looks negligible next to the roughly 10–20 meV broadening reported. Also, ell_B falls smoothly as B^(-1/2), so nothing in the proposed mechanism explains a "sudden" onset at 300 T. The description of the mechanism as "the significant increase of the degeneracy of the energy levels" (p. 9) is not correct either: Harper physics splits and broadens Landau levels; it does not increase their degeneracy. Finally, the exciton is neutral, so the authors must explain how electron and hole Harper broadening at alpha ≈ 0.013 would carry over to a bound p0 state whose extent along B is set by the Coulomb interaction.
   - What to do: compute the expected broadening. For example, use a tight-binding model of the Cu2O conduction and valence bands with Peierls phases for B || [001] (or a lattice-corrected magneto-exciton calculation), give the width of the relevant states versus B from 0 to 500 T, and compare it with Fig. 4a. If the predicted width is orders of magnitude below 10–20 meV, the title and abstract need to change.

2. Alternative explanations for both the broadening and the energy deviation are not considered (pp. 6–9). Each of the following is expected in exactly this field range in Cu2O and would produce excess broadening and a slower-than-hydrogenic shift, without lattice-scale physics.
   - (a) Valence-band nonparabolicity and mixing between the yellow (Γ7+) and green (Γ8+) bands. As the wavefunction is squeezed in real space, it reaches larger k, and the effective hole mass grows. This is the same physics behind the known quantum defect of the Cu2O P series. It naturally gives an "increase of reduced effective mass" (p. 3), which is what the authors report.
   - (b) Magneto-polaron resonances with Cu2O's polar LO phonons. The ~82 meV Γ4− LO mode in particular: the hole cyclotron energy, 0.116 meV/T × B / m_h with m_h ≈ 0.58–0.69, crosses ~82 meV between roughly 400 and 500 T. Resonant Fröhlich coupling is a textbook cause of both broadening and level bending at such crossings.
   - (c) Crossing or anticrossing with other states. In Fig. S3(a), the 2p−1 (Sz=0) and 2p−1 (Sz=+1) branches and the 2p0 (Sz=0) branch pass close to the measured 2p0(Sz=+1) energies between 200 and 500 T. The measured points lie between the purple and gray dashed curves, so the line above 300 T could be an unresolved superposition, or a state mixed with field-brightened s/d or dark states.
   - What to do: estimate each mechanism quantitatively using literature band parameters and phonon energies, and show why each is ruled out. Without this, "evidence for Harper broadening" does not follow from the data.

3. The robustness of the high-field linewidths is not established (Figs. 1b, 3, 4a; Methods D). The claim rests on four or five EMFC points (350–500 T), and the 2p0(Sz=+1) feature at those fields is a weak step on a steep background (Fig. 1b). The manuscript does not report any of the following:
   - how many EMFC shots were taken, and whether the upturn appears in more than one shot;
   - the streak-camera time resolution, the dB/dt at each field, and therefore the field-averaging within one spectrum (dB/dt in EMFC grows steeply near peak field);
   - the field inhomogeneity across the 1.8 × 1.8 mm sample inside the imploding liner;
   - how B is measured, and its uncertainty at 300–500 T;
   - whether polarization was resolved in the EMFC runs, which matters because unpolarized detection would superpose the rapidly rising σ− lines;
   - the spectral resolution of the polychromator;
   - the sample temperature during the shot.

   The FWHM also depends on the background model and on the "asymmetrical Gaussian" line shape. Only the 120 T fit is shown (Fig. 3), where the feature is strong. The authors should:
   - show the fits at 300, 350, 400, 450 and 500 T, with residuals;
   - state which parameters were free and which were fixed, including the asymmetry and the background form;
   - show how the FWHM changes under reasonable alternative backgrounds;
   - explain how the error bars in Fig. 4 were obtained. The 500 T point has the smallest error bar, which is odd for the weakest feature.

   Without these, instrumental broadening, field averaging or fitting artefacts cannot be excluded.

4. The argument used to assign the 2p0(Sz=±1) states rests on an incorrect expression for the orbital Zeeman splitting (p. 6 point (ii); SI Sec. I, Fig. S2). The paper says that "the exactly ħωc splitting of the 2p±1 states is imposed by time reversal symmetry", and from this deduces mu* ≈ 2 me if the strong lines were 2p±1. That holds only when one particle is infinitely heavy. For an exciton with comparable electron and hole masses, the linear orbital term in the relative-motion Hamiltonian is proportional to (1/m_e − 1/m_h)·L_z·B, not to 1/mu. With m_e ≈ 0.99 and m_h ≈ 0.6–0.7, the m = ±1 splitting is several times smaller than ħωc(mu), and is of the same order as the 0.116 meV/T slope in Fig. S2. So the reductio that rules out 2p±1 fails, and the fits of the m ≠ 0 branches in Fig. S3 (Makado–McGill plus spin Zeeman only) leave out this term. In addition:
   - The paper says the yellow series "originate[s] from an electric quadrupole transition". In fact the np excitons are dipole-allowed (Γ4− symmetry); it is the 1s state that is quadrupole-allowed. The selection-rule reasoning built on this should be redone.
   - The Γ7+ hole is treated as having g ≈ 0 and a pure S = 0/1 spin structure. This needs justification for B || [001].
   - The SI's conclusion that the shoulders are 2p+1(Sz=−1) and 2p−1(Sz=+1), and that "σ± couples strongly only with spin-flip transitions regardless of m", contradicts the selection rules stated a paragraph earlier and is not derived.

   Because both mu* and the "deviation from the hydrogen model" depend on the assignment, the authors should redo the assignment with the correct exciton Zeeman Hamiltonian and a group-theoretical analysis of Faraday-geometry selection rules for B || k || [001] (Oh → C4h), including the Γ7+ hole g-factor.

B. Should address

5. How the fit was done and how mu* = 0.415 ± 0.01 me was extracted is unclear (Fig. 2, Table I, p. 6).
   - Which parameters were free? Eg = 2.1756 eV is quoted without uncertainty, and it differs from commonly cited gap values.
   - What field range was used? Was the 300–500 T range excluded by choice, and if so the "deviation" is partly defined by that choice.
   - How was the ±0.01 obtained, and what are the correlations between mu*, Ry* and g?
   - Ry* is "imposed by the measured zero field 2p–3p splitting which has to follow the 1/n² Rydberg series". The Cu2O P series is known not to follow 1/n² exactly (quantum defects from band nonparabolicity), so this fixes Ry* with a bias that propagates into mu*.
   - Above about 130 T only one level (2p0 Sz=+1) constrains the fit, so the statement "fits very well to the four levels up to 300 T" overstates the constraint.
   - Please give the fitting procedure and the parameter uncertainties with and without the >200 T points. Also quantify the claimed "increase of reduced effective mass" by fitting the 300–500 T data with mu* free.

6. Data taken at different temperatures are combined without justification (Figs. 2 and 4). NDPM data are at 1.3 K (1.5 K in Fig. S1), STC at 10 K and EMFC at 45 K. The Fig. 2 caption says "T = 45 K" for all points, including Artyukhin's 1.2 K data. Fig. 4 normalizes every dataset by a single zero-field FWHM of about 2.9 meV without saying at which temperature it was measured. Phonon broadening of the 2p line between 1.3 and 45 K is not negligible. The authors should:
   - normalize each technique by its own zero-field width at the matching temperature;
   - show the zero-field 2p linewidth versus temperature;
   - state whether the EMFC sample temperature can rise during the shot (eddy currents, shock), and whether such heating would mimic a field-dependent broadening.

7. The statistical case for a "sudden" upturn is weak (Fig. 4a). Relative to the blue line, the 350 and 400 T points sit about 0.7 above it, with error bars of about ±0.5, so only the 450 and 500 T points clearly deviate. The authors should compare a linear model with alternatives (linear plus threshold, quadratic, power law) using a goodness-of-fit criterion. They should also explain the linear-in-B baseline itself: for 2p0(Sz=+1), dE/dB is nearly zero up to about 150 T, so ordinary inhomogeneous broadening via the field dispersion does not account for it. Fig. 4b, plotted against log ell_B with a vertical line at 300 T, gives an impression of a threshold that the theory has not motivated (see concern 1).

8. Relation to earlier megagauss and high-field work on Cu2O. Kobayashi et al. (Ref. 27) measured the yellow excitons in megagauss fields, and Zielińska-Raczyńska et al. (Ref. 11) modelled magneto-excitons from weak to high fields. The manuscript should say whether either reported linewidths or energies in the overlapping field range, whether they are consistent with Figs. 2 and 4, and what is new here beyond the extension to 500 T. In particular, does the Ref. 11 model, which includes the valence-band structure, reproduce the >300 T energy deviation without lattice effects?

9. Only one sample, and possible strain from mounting (Methods A). All results come from one 48 μm crystal glued with Stycast 1266 to quartz. Differential thermal contraction at 1.3–45 K can strain the crystal and split or broaden the p lines, and strain could enhance field-induced mixing. The key high-field linewidth result should be reproduced on a second crystal, or at least on a second EMFC shot. If neither is possible, the authors should discuss strain, for example by comparing the zero-field 2p/3p linewidths and positions with strain-free literature samples.

10. The energy panel and the linewidth panel are not tied together quantitatively (pp. 8–9). The energy shortfall above 300 T and the linewidth upturn are presented as two signatures of one mechanism, but no model links their sizes. A minimal step would be to show whether a single mechanism (Harper, nonparabolicity, polaron coupling or level mixing) reproduces both the about 10 meV energy shortfall at 500 T in Fig. 2 and the about 14 meV excess width.

C. Minor

11. Several figure references are broken: "Fig. ?? (a-b)" on p. 3 of the main text, and "Fig. ??(a)" in the Fig. S2 caption.

12. The polarization colour coding is inconsistent.
    - The p. 3 text says the blue and green arrows in Fig. 1(a) correspond to σ+ and σ−, but the green arrow marks 2p0(Sz=+1).
    - The Fig. 2 caption says green = σ+.
    - The Fig. S3 caption says circles = σ− and squares = σ+, while its panels use colour, not shape, for the branches.

    Please make one convention and use it throughout.

13. The Methods state that σ± in the NDPM was selected by reversing the field direction with a fixed polarizer. This should be said in the Fig. 1(a) caption, together with a note on whether the sample position and field calibration are identical for the two field polarities.

14. The maximum field is quoted as 500 T in the abstract and figures and 520 T in the text and Methods. The STC field range is "up to 200 T" in the Methods but the data stop at about 150 T. Please reconcile these.

15. Fig. 4 has no field (horizontal) error bars. At 300–500 T in EMFC, the field uncertainty is probably several percent and should be shown.

16. Typos and notation: "Faraady" (Fig. 1 caption); "2P", "3P0" and "p−1" (Fig. 2 legend, SI p. 5); "the FWHM … exhibit"; "Makado and McGill calculations considers"; "Fig. 4 (a)" spacing. The SI title differs from the main title ("yellow series exciton" versus "yellow exciton series").

17. The lattice constant a0 = 4.27 Å (Ref. 33) is a computed value; the experimental value of about 4.27 Å should be cited. Please also state that the top axis of Fig. 4b uses ell_B/a0.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a calculation, ideally for the real Cu2O band structure, of the excitonic 2p0 energies and widths up to 500 T that includes valence-band nonparabolicity (yellow–green mixing), the correct exciton Zeeman terms with m_e ≠ m_h, and Fröhlich coupling to the LO phonons. Then add Peierls-phase lattice effects on top and show what extra they contribute. This would turn "possible evidence" into a quantitative statement in either direction.
2. Present the EMFC data in full: raw streak images, B(t) traces, the 300–500 T spectra with fits and residuals, and repeat shots. Report linewidths only for polarization-resolved spectra, so that overlap with the σ− branches is excluded.
3. Use the 3p0(Sz=+1) line, or any other state that can be followed above 200 T, as an internal reference. If Harper broadening scales with ell_B/a0, the more extended 3p states should show the effect differently from 2p0 (earlier or larger, depending on the mechanism). Polaron or anticrossing mechanisms would instead produce resonances at specific fields.
4. Recast the assignment discussion (main text and SI Sec. I) around a symmetry-based selection-rule table for B || k || [001], so that readers can check which of the nine 2p sublevels should appear in σ+ and σ−, and with what relative strength.
