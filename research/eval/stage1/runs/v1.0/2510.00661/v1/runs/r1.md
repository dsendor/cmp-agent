Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report magneto-absorption of the yellow exciton series of Cu2O from 0 to about 500 T on one crystal, combining non-destructive pulsed magnets (NDPM), single-turn coil (STC) and electromagnetic flux compression (EMFC). They reassign the two strong 2p (and 3p) features from 2p±1 to the spin-Zeeman-split 2p0(Sz=±1) states, extract mu* = 0.415 ± 0.01 m_e, and report that above about 300 T the 2p0(Sz=+1) linewidth rises faster than linearly while its energy falls below the hydrogen-model prediction. They read this as possible evidence for Harper broadening, meaning a breakdown of the effective-mass approximation when the magnetic length (about 15 Å) nears the lattice constant (4.27 Å). Following an exciton across this field range in one crystal is new and experimentally impressive. The link to Harper physics would be of broad interest, but as it stands it is a qualitative suggestion, not a demonstrated mechanism.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Unique data: optical spectra of a bulk Wannier exciton up to 500 T, with continuity checked across three field-generation methods on the same crystal (Fig. 2, Fig. 4a).
- Below 31 T the NDPM data agree well with the DC-field data of Artyukhin et al. (Fig. S1), which supports the energy calibration.
- A single hydrogen-model parameter set describes four branches (2p0 and 3p0, both spin states) up to 150 T and 2p0(Sz=+1) up to 300 T (Fig. 2, Fig. S3).
- The question of when the effective-mass description of excitons fails at lattice-scale magnetic lengths is well posed and relevant beyond Cu2O.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. No quantitative estimate shows that Harper broadening should be visible at 300 T (pp. 8-9, Fig. 4b). At 300 T the flux per unit cell is alpha = 0.013 (p. 9). The Harper/Hofstadter broadening of the lowest Landau levels near a band edge falls off roughly exponentially in 1/alpha, so for alpha of about 0.01 it is expected to be vanishingly small. In addition, hbar*omega_c at 300 T (about 80 meV for mu* = 0.415) is far below the eV-scale widths of the Cu 4s conduction band and 3d valence bands. The leading lattice correction at ell_B/a0 = 3.5 should be a smooth band non-parabolicity of order (a0/ell_B)^2, not a sudden threshold. The paper calls the agreement "at least qualitative" (p. 9) but gives no calculation of the expected magnitude or of how sharp the onset should be.
   - Why it matters: the observed extra width at 450-500 T is about 5-10 meV, and the title claim depends on Harper physics being able to produce it.
   - What to do: compute the Harper (or Hofstadter-type) bandwidth for the relevant bands at alpha = 0.01-0.02. Use a tight-binding model with realistic Cu2O hopping parameters (or published band structures) and the real cubic lattice, and treat both carriers, since the exciton relative motion involves electron and hole. Show the predicted extra FWHM against B on Fig. 4. If the prediction is orders of magnitude too small, the interpretation has to change.
   - Also: the phrase "significant increase of the degeneracy of the energy levels" (p. 9) has it backwards. Harper broadening lifts the Landau-level degeneracy into subbands. Please state the mechanism precisely.

2. Effective-mass explanations for the deviations above 300 T are not ruled out (Fig. 2, Fig. 4a, Fig. S3). Three such explanations need to be excluded.
   - (i) Level crossing and mixing. In the authors' own Fig. S3(a), several calculated levels approach or cross the 2p0(Sz=+1) branch in the 300-500 T window, notably 2p-1(Sz=0) (grey dashed) and branches descending from 3p. Above 300 T the measured 2p0(Sz=+1) energy also lies below the hydrogen curve. An unresolved neighbouring line, or an anticrossing with such a level, would produce both the apparent broadening and the downward energy deviation without any lattice-scale physics.
   - (ii) Coupling to the Gamma8+ (green) valence band. The spin-orbit splitting is about 130 meV, and at 300 T the hole cyclotron energy is several tens of meV. Yellow-green mixing and band non-parabolicity, both captured by multiband k·p theory, should therefore become significant in this field range. They would bend the energy and add width.
   - (iii) The rising absorption step. The step that moves through 2.17-2.20 eV in Fig. 1b grows, and the 2p feature sits on its low-energy tail. The extracted width therefore depends on how the background is modelled.
   - What to do: in Fig. 4a and Fig. S3, mark which calculated levels lie within one FWHM of 2p0(Sz=+1) at each field. Fit the 350-500 T spectra with and without an additional component. Show the 2p0(Sz=+1) residuals (data minus model) against B with the hydrogen curve from Fig. 2. At minimum, estimate the yellow-green mixing correction with a multiband k·p model and compare it with the observed energy deviation of about 8 meV at 500 T.

3. The 300 T upturn rests on a few data points from a single technique, with no stated reproducibility or artifact controls (Fig. 4a, Methods D). The anomaly is carried by the 350, 400, 450 and 500 T points, all from EMFC. Neither the NDPM nor the STC data reach above 200 T to cross-check them. Several things are not stated:
   - how many EMFC shots were taken;
   - whether the upturn appears in more than one shot (or in the up-sweep and down-sweep of one shot);
   - how the field at the sample was measured and calibrated, and with what uncertainty at 500 T;
   - the spatial homogeneity of the field across the 1.8 × 1.8 mm sample as the liner implodes;
   - the streak-camera exposure per spectrum and the dB/dt during it (field change within one exposure);
   - the spectral resolution of the STC/EMFC polychromator against that of the NDPM spectrometer;
   - whether the sample temperature stays at 45 K during the shot.

   Each of these can add width that grows with field. Field inhomogeneity contributes (dE/dB)·ΔB, and dE/dB for 2p0(Sz=+1) is rising above 200 T. In Fig. 1b the 2p feature at 350-500 T is barely above the noise, so the fit also becomes less constrained exactly where the upturn appears.
   - What to do: report the number of shots and show the upturn in at least two independent EMFC runs, or in up- and down-sweep data. Give the field uncertainty and an estimate of the field inhomogeneity across the sample, in tesla, at each field. Convert the field inhomogeneity and the field change during each exposure into linewidth contributions using dE/dB from Fig. 2, and subtract or bound them in Fig. 4a.

4. The argument that rules out the 2p±1 assignment assumes the wrong orbital Zeeman splitting (p. 6 and SI Sec. I, Fig. S2). The authors state that the 2p+1/2p-1 splitting is "exactly hbar*omega_c ... imposed by time reversal symmetry", with omega_c = eB/mu. That holds for hydrogen, where the nucleus is infinitely heavy. For an exciton with comparable electron and hole masses, the linear term in the relative-motion Hamiltonian is (e*hbar*B/2)(1/m_e - 1/m_h)·m, not (e*hbar*B/2mu)·m.
   - Worked example: with literature Cu2O masses (m_e of about 1.0, m_h of about 0.6-0.7 m_e), the expected 2p+1 to 2p-1 slope is about 0.05-0.08 meV/T. That is the same order as the observed 0.11 meV/T in Fig. S2. So the "mu = 2 m_e, five times too large" argument does not exclude the conventional assignment. The two hypotheses also predict nearly the same linear slope (Fig. S2 shows them overlapping), so the slope alone cannot discriminate between them.
   - Further gaps:
     - The claim that "sigma± couples strongly only with spin-flip transitions regardless of m" (SI p. 5) is asserted from fits, not derived.
     - The Gamma7+ hole is a spin-orbit-coupled state, so the "Sz" labels and the "hole g-factor close to zero" (p. 6) need justification.
     - The main text says the p excitons arise from an electric-quadrupole transition (p. 5; SI p. 3). In Elliott's classification, the yellow np excitons are dipole-allowed (second class); it is the 1s state that is quadrupole-allowed.
   - Why it matters: the assignment is a headline claim of the abstract, and mu* and the "deviation from the hydrogen model" both depend on it.
   - What to do: redo the comparison in Fig. S2 with the correct exciton orbital Zeeman term, including the m_e/m_h dependence. Derive the sigma± selection rules for the Gamma5- p-exciton states in O_h with the Gamma7+ hole. Discuss explicitly how this assignment differs from Artyukhin et al. 2018, Zielińska-Raczyńska et al. 2019 and Kobayashi et al. 1989 (refs. 18, 11, 27), and why.

B. Should address

5. The quoted mu* uncertainty is not supported by the fit description (Table I, p. 6, SI Sec. II).
   - Ry* = 103 meV is "imposed" by the 2p-3p splitting under a pure 1/n^2 law. Cu2O p excitons are known to have non-zero quantum defects (Kazimierczuk et al., ref. 24), and Ry* is 5% above the accepted 98 meV.
   - E_g = 2.1756 eV is given without an uncertainty, so it is unclear whether it was fixed or fitted.
   - The diamagnetic curvature fixes mu* only together with Ry* (equivalently epsilon), and the two are strongly correlated.
   - What to do: state which parameters were free. Give the covariance between mu*, Ry* and g, and the field range used in the fit (does it exclude the points above 300 T?). Show how mu* changes if Ry* is set to 98 meV or a quantum defect is included. Without this, ±0.01 m_e (2.4%) looks optimistic, and "close to the high end of the literature range" cannot be judged.

6. The "increase of the reduced effective mass" above 300 T (p. 3 and p. 7) is stated but never quantified. No fit with a field-dependent mu*, and no mu*(B) plot, is shown. Please extract an effective mu*(B), or the energy residual, from the 2p0(Sz=+1) data with its uncertainty. Also state whether a smooth non-parabolic correction (see concern 2) reproduces it.

7. The case for a "sudden" change rather than smooth superlinear growth is not made (Fig. 4a, Fig. 3).
   - The blue line is a guide fitted up to 300 T. A quadratic or other smooth form fitted over the full range might describe the data equally well.
   - The FWHM error bars are not explained (fit standard error? shot-to-shot spread?).
   - The "asymmetric Gaussian" is not defined, and it is not said which width is reported.
   - The background in Fig. 3a (orange) is a hand-chosen smooth curve, and its form above 300 T is not shown.
   - What to do: show the raw spectra and fits for 300, 400 and 500 T as in Fig. 3. Report the FWHM sensitivity to the choice of background. Compare a piecewise-linear and a smooth model with a statistical criterion.
   - The linear width-vs-B regime below 300 T also needs a physical explanation. The QW luminescence reference (ref. 31) concerns a different system. Field-induced mechanisms intrinsic to bulk excitons, such as the motional magneto-Stark effect for finite-K excitons, should be considered for both the linear part and any upturn.

8. Temperatures and normalisation are inconsistent across the data sets (Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 4, Methods).
   - The three data sets were taken at different temperatures: NDPM at 1.3 K (1.5 K in Fig. S1, 1.2 K for ref. 18), STC at 10 K and EMFC at 45 K. Yet the Fig. 2 caption and the Fig. S3 caption assign all data to 45 K.
   - The exciton linewidth depends on temperature through phonon scattering, so normalising each data set to "the zero-field FWHM (about 2.9 meV)" mixes conditions.
   - What to do: state the zero-field FWHM and spectral resolution for each technique and temperature, and how each set in Fig. 4 was normalised. Ideally, measure the 2p FWHM against temperature (1.3-45 K) at zero or low field, to show that 45 K neither sets nor changes the field dependence.

9. Why the 2p0(Sz=-1) line disappears above about 150 T is not explained (Fig. 1b, Fig. 2). In STC it is clear at 120 T and well separated (Fig. 3), but in EMFC only the Sz=+1 line is followed. Absorption strength should not depend on thermal population, so this disappearance is informative in itself.
   - What to do: state whether the EMFC spectra were polarisation-resolved, and in which configuration. Explain where the Sz=-1 and 3p lines go: do they merge into the rising step, broaden, or lose oscillator strength? If the 3p0 or 2p0(Sz=-1) widths can be extracted up to 150-200 T, compare their field dependence with that of 2p0(Sz=+1). A mechanism controlled by ell_B should affect all states at a similar field.

10. The physical picture of an exciton reaching lattice scale needs to be put on numbers. At 300 T, ell_B is about 15 Å, which is similar to the 1s Bohr radius of the yellow exciton (around 7-10 Å for epsilon of about 7.4, mu* = 0.415). The in-plane extent of a 2p0 state, however, is set by ell_B and its extent along B is larger. Please give the in-plane and longitudinal sizes of 2p0 at 300 and 500 T from the hydrogen model. Explain why lattice effects should switch on at ell_B/a0 = 3.5 rather than at some other ratio. Also justify using the cubic-cell flux alpha (p. 9) for a six-atom cell whose Cu sublattice has a different spacing.

C. Minor

12. The maximum field is given as 500 T in the abstract, figures and conclusions, but as 520 T on p. 3 and in Methods D. Please reconcile, and state the field reached in the shot shown.

13. Fig. 2 legend: capitalisation is inconsistent ("2P0", "3P0"). The 3p0 NDPM and STC symbols are hard to tell apart. The caption says the light symbols below 32 T are from ref. 18, but it should also say which symbols are NDPM (1.3 K) and STC (10 K).

15. Fig. 3b: the component Gaussians are drawn truncated (e.g., 2p0(Sz=+1) starts at about 0.3 at 2.141 eV, and 3p+1 is cut off). Please plot the full components. Also label the 3p+1 component consistently (no spin index, unlike the others).

16. Fig. 4b: the top axis is labelled "(a0)", which readers may take as the exciton Bohr radius. Please label it "ell_B / a (lattice constant)". The dashed line should sit at 3.5 on that axis.

17. Ref. 15 (Hofstadter spectrum in quantum Hall superlattices) is cited for exciton wave-function shrinkage in quantum wells and superlattices (p. 2). Please check this citation.

18. Typos: "Faraady" (Fig. 1 caption); "The Makado and McGill calculations considers" (p. 6); "exhibit" should be "exhibits" (p. 3); a missing space in "techniques.The" (p. 3).

19. Table I: give E_g with an uncertainty or mark it as fixed, and state how g = 2.0 ± 0.1 was obtained (from Fig. S2 or from the global fit).

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a theory panel to Fig. 4. It should overlay three calculated extra widths against B: (a) the Harper bandwidth for realistic Cu2O tight-binding parameters, (b) the broadening expected from nearby crossing levels in Fig. S3, and (c) the instrumental contributions from field inhomogeneity and dB/dt. This would turn "possible evidence" into a testable comparison.
2. Measure a second EMFC shot, ideally with polarisation resolution, to confirm the upturn and track 2p0(Sz=-1) and 3p0 above 150 T. A second material with a different lattice constant but similar exciton parameters would test whether the threshold scales with ell_B/a0 as the Harper interpretation predicts.
3. Rebuild the quantum-number assignment on the correct exciton orbital Zeeman term (1/m_e - 1/m_h) and a symmetry-based selection-rule analysis. Present the alternative assignment side by side with residuals.
4. Report fit details in full: free and fixed parameters, covariances, background model, field ranges, and the per-technique resolution and temperature. Readers can then judge the 2.4% uncertainty on mu* and the significance of the 300 T threshold.
