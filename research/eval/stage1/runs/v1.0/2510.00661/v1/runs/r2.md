Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports magneto-absorption of the yellow 2p and 3p excitons of a single Cu2O crystal over 0–500 T, measured with non-destructive pulsed magnets, a single-turn coil and electromagnetic flux compression (EMFC). It makes three claims. First, the two strong σ+/σ− lines are reassigned as the spin-Zeeman-split 2p0^{Sz=±1} states. Second, a hydrogen-model fit gives μ* = 0.415 ± 0.01 m_e. Third, above 300 T the 2p0^{Sz=+1} line broadens faster and shifts less than the hydrogen model predicts, which the authors present as possible evidence of Harper broadening, i.e. a breakdown of the effective-mass approximation when ℓ_B ≈ 3.5 a0. Following a hydrogenic level to 500 T in a bulk crystal is a real experimental achievement, and a lattice-scale breakdown of the effective-mass picture would be of broad interest. As written, though, the headline interpretation rests on five line widths from what appears to be a single destructive shot, with no quantitative link to Harper physics.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The data set is unique: one crystal measured with three field-generation techniques up to 500 T. The overlap regions agree with the DC data of Artyukhin et al. (Fig. 2, Fig. S1), which supports the field calibration and the line identification below about 130 T.
- The 2p0^{Sz=+1} line can be followed continuously to 500 T (Fig. 1b, Fig. 2). This is the highest-field optical tracking of a Cu2O exciton level I am aware of.
- The reassignment of the strong lines has practical consequences for the Cu2O literature. It is argued from the polarization dependence and the size of the splitting (Fig. S2).
- The hydrogen-model fit with a single parameter set describes four levels well up to about 300 T (Fig. 2, Fig. S3).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. No quantitative estimate shows that Harper broadening can be appreciable at 300 T in Cu2O (pp. 8–9).
   - At 300 T the flux per unit cell is α = φ/φ0 ≈ 0.013, as the authors themselves state, so roughly 77 unit cells share one flux quantum. In the Harper/Hofstadter problem, the width that the periodic potential gives to a low Landau level at small α is controlled by the overlap of neighbouring cyclotron orbits. That width is expected to be very small, typically exponentially small in 1/α for a smooth tight-binding band. It does not switch on abruptly at a threshold ℓ_B/a0.
   - The conduction band of Cu2O (Cu 4s) is broad. On the length scale of ℓ_B = 14.8 Å the carriers should still see a well-averaged potential.
   - The text says the broadening comes from "the significant increase of the degeneracy of the energy levels". Harper physics does the opposite: the lattice lifts the Landau-level degeneracy and splits each level into subbands.
   - The argument also compares the free-carrier ℓ_B with a0. The relevant object is the bound 2p0 exciton. In the strong-field limit its transverse size is set by the relative-motion magnetic length, and its extent along B stays larger.
   - What to do: give an order-of-magnitude estimate of the Harper bandwidth for the lowest Landau-level manifold of Cu2O at 300–500 T, for example a tight-binding model with the measured masses or a k·p model with lattice corrections, and show it is comparable to the observed excess width of about 4–5 meV at 450–500 T. Without this the title and abstract are not supported. If no such estimate can be produced, the Harper attribution should be presented as one speculative possibility among several.

2. Experimental causes of the >300 T anomaly are not excluded.
   - Both anomalies above 300 T, the extra width (Fig. 4a) and the shortfall in energy relative to the hydrogen model (Fig. 2), come only from the EMFC data. The same pair of signatures (red shift plus broadening) is what sample heating during the EMFC pulse would produce: the band gap shrinks and the phonon broadening grows with temperature. The measurement already starts at 45 K, and the effective sample temperature near the field peak is not known.
   - The 500 T spectra also integrate over field inhomogeneity across the 1.8 mm sample and the 800 µm probed spot. They further integrate over the field sweep within the streak-camera time bin. Both effects broaden a line by (dE/dB)·ΔB, and this grows at high field.
   - What to do:
     - (i) State the field homogeneity over the probed volume at 300–500 T and the effective time resolution, i.e. the field interval averaged per spectrum. Convert these into a broadening using the slope of the 2p0^{Sz=+1} line.
     - (ii) Give an estimate or proxy of the sample temperature during the shot, for example from the 3p or background absorption edge, or from a zero-field temperature series of the 2p linewidth and energy at 45–150 K. This would show what heating would be needed to mimic the data.
     - (iii) State how many EMFC shots were taken. Since the same crystal was used for all techniques, the EMFC result appears to be a single shot on a single sample. Show that the upturn reproduces in at least one more shot, ideally including the down-sweep.

3. Intrinsic alternatives to Harper physics are not considered.
   - Level crossings and mixing. The authors describe 2p0^{Sz=+1} as "well separated from other excitonic transitions", but their own Fig. S3a shows several predicted levels (for example 2p_{-1}^{Sz=0} and other 2p_{-1} branches) approaching the 2p0^{Sz=+1} curve in the 300–500 T range. An unresolved neighbouring line, or an anticrossing with one, would produce both an apparent shift and an apparent broadening.
   - Polaron and screening effects. As the exciton shrinks, its internal energy spacings approach and exceed the Cu2O LO-phonon energies (about 19 and 82 meV). By my rough estimate, the reduced-mass cyclotron energy reaches about 80 meV near 300 T. Magneto-polaron resonances and the crossover from static to high-frequency dielectric screening would then shift and broaden the level.
   - Band mixing. Above a few hundred tesla the cyclotron energies (about 140 meV for μ = 0.415 m_e at 500 T) approach the about 130 meV spin-orbit splitting between the Γ7+ and Γ8+ valence bands. That brings in mixing with the green-series bands and nonparabolicity.
   - Each of these is a breakdown of the simple hydrogen model, but not Harper broadening. What to do: discuss each mechanism, estimate the field at which it becomes important, and explain why the data favour a lattice-periodicity effect.

4. The "sudden increase above 300 T" depends on a few weak-feature fits that are not shown (Fig. 1b, Fig. 4a).
   - The claim rests on five points (350, 400, 450, 500 T, and arguably 300 T).
   - In Fig. 1b the 2p0^{Sz=+1} feature at 350–500 T is a small dip on a steep, noisy background. Its width then depends strongly on the background model and on the asymmetric line shape chosen.
   - Only one fit, at 120 T where the feature is strong (Fig. 3), is shown.
   - What to do:
     - Show the fits at 300, 400 and 500 T with residuals.
     - Explain how the FWHM error bars were obtained.
     - Test how sensitive the widths are to the background form (polynomial order, fit window) and to symmetric versus asymmetric line shapes.
     - Compare a smooth superlinear model with a "linear plus threshold" model statistically, so that "sudden" or "abrupt" is supported rather than read off the plot.
     - The integrated intensity of the line versus field would also help: a genuine broadening at constant oscillator strength is distinguishable from a fading line whose width is poorly constrained.

B. Should address

5. The argument against the 2p±1 assignment uses the hydrogen-atom form of the orbital Zeeman term (p. 6; SI Sec. I, Fig. S2).
   - The text says the 2p±1 splitting is "exactly ħω_c" with the reduced mass. That holds for hydrogen, where m_p ≫ m_e. For an exciton at zero centre-of-mass momentum, the linear-in-m term is proportional to (1/m_e − 1/m_h), not to 1/μ.
   - In Cu2O the electron and hole masses are comparable, so the expected orbital splitting is considerably smaller than ħeB/μ. The "μ ≈ 2 m_e, five times too large" argument in Fig. S2 therefore does not exclude the orbital assignment as stated.
   - The spin-Zeeman argument assumes g_h ≈ 0 for the Γ7+ hole; this needs a literature value with uncertainty.
   - The claim that σ± couple "only strongly with the spin-flip transitions" (SI Sec. II) needs a derivation from the quadrupole selection rules for k ∥ [001].
   - What to do: redo the comparison with the correct (1/m_e − 1/m_h) coefficient and literature values of m_e and m_h. State which hydrogen-model Hamiltonian (with or without the mass-asymmetry term) was used in the fits. Engage explicitly with the assignments of Artyukhin et al., Zielińska-Raczyńska et al. and Kobayashi et al.

6. How μ* = 0.415 ± 0.01 m_e is determined, and the claimed mass increase above 300 T, need clarification (Fig. 2, Table I).
   - Ry* is fixed by the zero-field 2p–3p spacing. The 3p and 2p0^{Sz=−1} branches are followed only to about 130 T. The mass is therefore effectively set by the single 2p0^{Sz=+1} branch between about 130 and 300 T.
   - The fit range ends exactly where the deviation is claimed. This is partly circular.
   - What to do:
     - State which points and field range entered the fit and how they were weighted.
     - Report the correlations among μ*, Ry* and g.
     - Show how μ* changes if the fit stops at 150, 200 or 250 T.
     - Quantify the "increase of the reduced effective mass" above 300 T claimed on p. 3. At present it is asserted, not extracted.
     - Note that μ* = 0.415 lies at or above the top of the quoted literature range (0.35–0.41 m_e). Possible reasons such as polaron corrections or the model's neglect of mass anisotropy and central-cell effects should be discussed.

7. Fig. 4 combines widths taken at different temperatures and with different instruments.
   - The NDPM data are at 1.3 K, the STC data at 10 K and the EMFC data at 45 K. All are divided by a single zero-field FWHM (≃ 2.9 meV) whose temperature and setup are not specified.
   - The streak-camera/polychromator resolution in STC and EMFC is likely different from the CCD spectrometer used in NDPM.
   - The linear trend up to 300 T, which defines the "baseline" for the upturn, may therefore partly reflect normalization and resolution differences.
   - What to do: give the zero-field FWHM and the instrumental resolution for each setup and temperature. Normalize each data set by its own zero-field width. Ideally, plot absolute FWHM with the instrumental contribution stated.

8. The absorption may saturate in the 48 µm sample (Methods A, Fig. 1b).
   - Near 2.17–2.2 eV the yellow-series continuum and the phonon-assisted background make the crystal strongly absorbing. The high-field EMFC spectra approach the noise floor in this region.
   - Saturated optical density, or stray light, flattens and distorts line shapes and can bias widths.
   - What to do: show raw transmitted intensities and the reference spectrum used to compute absorption. State the maximum optical density and the stray-light level, and confirm that the 2p0^{Sz=+1} line at high field lies in a range where the absorption is linear.

9. Field calibration and uncertainty for the STC and EMFC data are not given.
   - The energy deviation from the hydrogen model above 300 T (Fig. 2) is a few meV. A few-percent error in B would move the points horizontally by 10–25 T, which is a comparable shift given the curve's slope.
   - What to do: describe how the field was measured (pickup coil, Faraday rotation), give its uncertainty, and add horizontal error bars to Fig. 2 and Fig. 4.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a short theory section, or SI calculation, estimating the Harper/lattice correction to the 2p0 exciton in Cu2O at 300–500 T. Compare it with the size of polaron, band-mixing and level-crossing effects so readers can judge which mechanism dominates.
2. Add an SI panel with all high-field EMFC fits (300–500 T): residuals, background variations and integrated intensity versus field. Add a reproducibility shot if one exists.
3. Measure the zero-field 2p linewidth and energy from 1.5 K to about 150 K with the STC/EMFC optics. This calibrates both the instrumental resolution and the heating signature that the EMFC anomaly must be distinguished from.
4. Unless concerns 1–4 are resolved, rephrase the abstract and conclusions to describe an observed deviation from the hydrogenic effective-mass model above 300 T, with Harper broadening as one candidate explanation.
