Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript introduces an all-dry route to few-layer Ta2NiSe5: the substrate material (Au or ALD Al2O3) is deposited onto the cleaved crystal, then the stack is transferred by Au cold welding. Polarized Raman spectroscopy is then used to follow the orthorhombic-to-monoclinic transition in 4-18 layer flakes. The headline result is that 4L and 6L flakes on Au show a transition that is lowered (Tc^avg ≈ 220 ± 40 K and 250 ± 40 K) and broadened, while a 4L flake on Al2O3 transitions sharply at ≈ 280 K. The authors attribute this to an interfacial charge-transfer/screening gradient and conclude that excitonic interactions account for at least 100 K of the 326 K bulk Tc. The method could be useful, and substrate-controlled tuning of the transition bears on the long-running excitonic-versus-lattice debate. However, the quantitative excitonic attribution currently rests on a small data set and on interpretive steps that are not yet tested.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The exfoliation and transfer protocol (Fig. 1C-E) reliably gives 10-100 µm few-layer flakes of a material that otherwise fragments into ribbons (Fig. 1B). It is dry, compatible with inert environments and likely transferable to other rippled vdW compounds.
- The Raman protocol is careful about substrate contributions. Bare Au/Cr and Al2O3 are featureless in the ac channel (Fig. 2F, G), so the ac analysis is attributable to the flake. Selection-rule changes across the transition are demonstrated in a 4L flake (Fig. 2B-E).
- Both a structural marker (onset of Ag(m4)/Ag(m5), Fig. 3) and electronic markers (Fano asymmetry of B2g(o1) and the quasi-elastic response, Fig. 4) are tracked on the same flakes. The thick references (7L/Al2O3 and 18L/Au) reproduce bulk Tc, which anchors the method.
- The Al2O3 samples are effectively a spacer control: the same Au/cold-weld stack, but with 50-100 nm of dielectric between flake and metal. That is a well-chosen comparison.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Laser heating and the effective sample temperature are not documented, and heating alone could produce a lowered, broadened transition.
   - What the paper does: Methods (p. 17) state that about 1 mW of 632.8 nm light was used and that "the actual temperature" was checked with the Stokes/anti-Stokes ratio. No ratios, inferred temperatures or power dependences are shown for any flake.
   - Why this could explain the result: Heating above the stage temperature makes the transition appear at a lower nominal temperature. A temperature gradient across the focused spot and through the stack broadens it. Both are the signatures attributed here to the Au interface.
   - Why the stacks may differ: The Au flakes sit on 20-50 nm of evaporated Au over a ~2 nm Cr layer on SiO2/Si or sapphire. The Al2O3 flakes have an extra 50-100 nm oxide layer. Optical absorption in the flake, the local field and the thermal boundary resistance all differ between these stacks. The 18L/Au reference does not settle this, because absorption and heat spreading in an 18L flake differ from those in 4L.
   - What to do:
     - Show the Stokes/anti-Stokes temperature against stage temperature for each of the five flakes, especially 4L/Au and 4L/Al2O3.
     - Repeat the 4L/Au temperature series at reduced power (for example 0.25 and 0.5 mW) to show that Tc and the width do not change with power.

2. The interfacial-gradient model is stated but not tested quantitatively, and parts of the data appear to contradict it.
   - Onset versus the same-thickness reference: The model says layers far from Au keep a Tc "closer to room temperature" (p. 4, Fig. 5B inset). Yet the first kink for 4L/Au, which should mark the highest-Tc layers, is at 220 K (Fig. 3T). That is about 60 K below the 4L/Al2O3 Tc of 280 K. So even the top layers appear strongly suppressed, which does not fit an effect "confined to one or two layers" (p. 14).
   - Assignment of Tc^avg: Tc^avg is set equal to the first (onset) kink temperature and then given ±40 K (p. 10). In a distribution of local Tc, the onset kink marks the upper end, not the average.
   - The ±40 K arithmetic: the extra kink separation is 100 − 60 = 40 K. This more naturally corresponds to a total spread of about 40 K (±20 K), with the centre below the onset. As written, both the centre and the width of Tc^avg are ambiguous.
   - Depth weighting near the metal: The in-plane electric field is strongly suppressed close to a metallic surface. Layers adjacent to Au should therefore contribute least to the Raman signal, not become "detectable in the overall signal" (p. 13-14). The ~100-fold lower intensity of 4L/Au compared with 4L/Al2O3 (colour scales in Fig. 3G and 3J) is consistent with this.
   - What to do:
     - Compute the depth-dependent excitation and collection field (transfer-matrix calculation for flake/Au/Cr/substrate and flake/Al2O3/Au stacks at 632.8 nm).
     - Build a layer-resolved model: a weighted sum of bulk-like spectra, each with its own local Tc. Show that it reproduces the onset temperature, the kink separation and the Fano and quasi-elastic evolution of 6L and 4L on Au.
     - Define Tc^avg from that model rather than from the onset kink.

3. The size of the suppression attributed to excitonic interactions is overstated relative to the paper's own reference.
   - What the paper claims: The abstract and Discussion (p. 15) state a reduction "by over 100 K" and that excitonic mechanisms lower Tc "by at least 100 K". Both use bulk 326 K as the reference.
   - Why the reference is wrong: The 4L/Al2O3 flake already sits at 280 K (−15%), so part of the 4L/Au reduction is a thickness or encapsulation effect unrelated to the conducting substrate. Against the same-thickness control, 4L/Au is lowered by about 60 K.
   - Why "at least" does not hold: With the quoted ±40 K, the reduction is 60 ± 40 K, and even relative to bulk it is 106 ± 40 K. Neither supports a lower bound of 100 K.
   - Further point: "At least" also assumes that Au removes excitonic correlations completely in the measured layers, which is not shown.
   - What to do: Restate the magnitude against the 4L/Al2O3 control with propagated uncertainties, and revise the abstract and the excitonic-share estimate (p. 15-16) accordingly.

4. Quasi-elastic and Fano responses are treated asymmetrically, and this drives the "excitonic rather than electron-lattice" conclusion.
   - What the paper does: The 17% drop in the maximum −1/q is dismissed as "simple temperature broadening" (p. 15). The disappearance of the quasi-elastic peak in 4L/Au (Fig. 4T) is read as destruction of excitonic fluctuations.
   - Why both need the same test: A distribution of local Tc would also smear and lower the peak of χ''ac(10 cm⁻¹, T). The 6L/Au data (Fig. 4S) already show a broad plateau rather than a peak.
   - Signal-to-noise and stray light:
     - The 4L/Au spectra are scaled ×50 and the 6L/Au spectra ×25.
     - A Rayleigh leak at 15 cm⁻¹ had to be removed by interpolation (Methods, p. 18). The 10 cm⁻¹ point used for the quasi-elastic analysis is even closer to the laser line on a single-grating instrument.
     - Fig. 4T is flat at about 3.5 (×50) over the whole temperature range. This may mean the quasi-elastic response is below detection, or is dominated by stray light, rather than suppressed.
   - What to do:
     - Apply the same Tc-distribution model (concern 2) to both −1/q(T) and χ''ac(10 cm⁻¹, T), using the 18L or 4L/Al2O3 curves as the local response.
     - Test whether the absence of the 4L/Au quasi-elastic peak goes beyond what broadening and signal-to-noise predict.
     - Show raw low-frequency spectra (below 25 cm⁻¹) with the filter edge and the stray-light background for all flakes.
     - Consider integrating χ'' over a window (for example 8-20 cm⁻¹) instead of using a single point.

5. Each condition rests on a single flake.
   - What the paper shows: Five flakes in total, one per thickness/substrate combination. The central contrast (4L/Au versus 4L/Al2O3) and the 6L/Au result each come from one flake.
   - Why it matters: Flake-to-flake variation from local strain, interface quality, folds and laser position could produce Tc shifts of tens of kelvin. That is comparable to the effect being claimed.
   - What to do: Measure at least two or three additional 4L (and ideally 6L) flakes on each substrate, preferably from different exfoliation runs, and show the spread in Tc and width. A thickness-matched 6L/Al2O3 flake would also separate thickness from substrate effects better than the present 7L/Al2O3 versus 6L/Au pairing.

B. Should address

6. Non-electronic origins of the Au interface effect are not excluded.
   - The two interfaces are made very differently: Au is thermally evaporated directly onto the freshly cleaved Ta2NiSe5, whereas Al2O3 is grown by ALD at 100-150 °C with TMA/H2O.
   - Hot Au adatoms can react with surface Se, intercalate into the first vdW gap, or create defects and local strain. Any of these could suppress or smear the transition without charge transfer.
   - Ta2NiSe5 is known to be highly strain-sensitive (Ref. 17).
   - The 4L/Al2O3 control rules out generic oxidation (p. 15), but not damage specific to the Au interface.
   - The wafer under each sample (SiO2/Si or sapphire, Methods p. 16) is not specified. Differential thermal contraction on cooling would therefore differ between samples.
   - What to do:
     - Provide a cross-sectional STEM/EDX image of the Ta2NiSe5/Au interface, or XPS of the Au-Se chemistry.
     - Compare high-temperature (orthorhombic) phonon frequencies of each flake with bulk values to bound residual strain.
     - State the wafer for each sample.
     - A useful control is the 2L/4L ribbons already obtained on non-evaporated Au(111)/mica (Fig. 1B). A Raman temperature series on them would test whether a pristine Au contact without evaporation produces the same suppression.

7. Charge transfer and screening are inferred but not measured or estimated.
   - The paper gives no estimate of the work-function offset between Au and Ta2NiSe5, the sign of the transferred charge (Fig. 5B suggests electrons) or the resulting sheet density. Nor does it measure doping, for example by phonon frequency shifts, KPFM or XPS core-level shifts.
   - Without this, "charge transfer" versus "screening (mirror charges)" (p. 15) cannot be separated, and the excitonic interpretation is not anchored to a quantity.
   - What to do:
     - Estimate the transferred density.
     - Ideally, add a control that screens without transferring charge (Ta2NiSe5 on an hBN spacer over Au, or on graphite) or one that varies the substrate work function.
     - Relate the estimated density to the gate-induced densities in Ref. 60. The present one-sentence note (p. 16) acknowledges that Ref. 60 reaches a different conclusion but does not say what that conclusion is or how the two results could be reconciled.

8. The procedure for extracting Tc may itself broaden or shift the transition.
   - The 2D maps are smoothed with a Gaussian of 10-15 K in temperature (Methods, p. 18), comparable to the temperature spacing and to the claimed Tc uncertainties. The second-derivative maps (Fig. 3K-O) are built from these smoothed maps.
   - It is not clear whether the peak positions in Fig. 3P-T come from smoothed or unsmoothed spectra.
   - The kink criterion relies on a "small but abrupt" shift of the leaked Ag(o3)* mode, comparable to the 1.3 cm⁻¹ point spacing. The 4L/Au kink is described as "less defined".
   - What to do:
     - Extract peak positions and intensities from Lorentzian/Fano fits to the raw (unsmoothed) spectra at each measured temperature, with fit uncertainties.
     - Use the integrated intensity of Ag(m4)/Ag(m5) as a function of temperature (an order-parameter-like quantity) as an additional, less subjective Tc and width metric.
     - Justify the 15% criterion used for the electronic Tc (Fig. 4P-S).

9. Temperature control and calibration across 300 K are unclear.
   - Methods (p. 17) mention a cryostat "for measurements below 300 K". The data extend to 340-400 K (Fig. 4A-E, K-T), right where the thick-flake transitions are located.
   - Please state how the high-temperature data were acquired (same cryostat with a heater, or a different stage). If different, show that the two datasets agree in the overlap region.
   - This affects the quoted 320-330 K values and the agreement with bulk.

10. The large difference in Raman intensity between substrates is not discussed.
    - The 4L/Au signal is roughly 100 times weaker than 4L/Al2O3 (colour scales in Fig. 3G and 3J; scale factors in Fig. 3E and 4E).
    - Besides the field-node effect (concern 2), this could indicate reduced crystallinity, quenching or partial coverage.
    - What to do: Comment on it, compare with the expected interference enhancement for each stack, and show a room-temperature aa spectrum of 4L/Al2O3 next to Fig. 2B for direct comparison of linewidths.

11. The Fano fits need more detail.
    - The 20-80 cm⁻¹ fit window contains the leaked Ag(o1)* mode near 35 cm⁻¹ (Fig. 4F) and, on Au, the interpolated Rayleigh artefact at 15 cm⁻¹.
    - Please specify:
      - whether a background was included;
      - how Ag(o1)* was handled;
      - which parameters were free;
      - how robust −1/q is to the fit window.
    - Error bars "smaller than the circle symbols" (Fig. 4 caption) look optimistic for the ×50 4L/Au spectra. Please report fit uncertainties, ideally including systematic variation with the fit window.

C. Minor

12. Supplementary Notes 1-3 (azimuthal dependence, additional structural analysis, AFM thickness determination) are cited but not included in the submission. In particular, layer counts on amorphous Au, whose roughness is comparable to a single layer, should be supported by AFM profiles with step-height statistics.

13. The structural marker relies partly on Ag(o3)*, which leaks into the ac channel through polarizer/analyzer misalignment (Fig. 2C, p. 8). Please quantify the leakage ratio for each flake and confirm it is the same on both substrates, since the kink criterion depends on this mode.

14. The Fig. 4 caption says the dashed guides are in panels (H) and (J), whereas the text (p. 11) and the figure place them in (H) and (I).

15. The abstract describes 4L/Al2O3 as showing "nearly bulk-like properties", while the text reports a 15% reduction in Tc. Please align the wording.

16. The Fig. 5B "mixed" region and error bars should state which definition of Tc^avg (onset, centre or width) they represent, consistent with the resolution of concern 2.

17. On p. 3, "exctonic" should read "excitonic". On p. 17, state the laser spot size and the power density, not only the power.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Build a single forward model (optical field profile through the stack, plus layer-resolved local Tc, plus bulk-like local Raman response) and fit it jointly to the structural (Fig. 3), Fano (Fig. 4K-O) and quasi-elastic (Fig. 4P-T) data. This would turn the qualitative interface picture into a testable, quantitative statement about how deep the Au effect reaches.
2. Add the decisive controls in one sample series:
   - several 4L flakes each on Au and on Al2O3;
   - a 4L flake on Au with a thin hBN or Al2O3 spacer (only a few nm);
   - a Raman power series on 4L/Au.
3. Characterise the Au/Ta2NiSe5 interface directly (STEM/EDX or XPS), and estimate the transferred charge density for comparison with the gating study (Ref. 60).
4. Restate the headline numbers against the same-thickness Al2O3 reference with propagated uncertainties. Frame the excitonic share of Tc as an estimate whose bounds depend on the assumptions of the interface model.
