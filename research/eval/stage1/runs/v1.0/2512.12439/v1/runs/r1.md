Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript presents an all-dry route to few-layer Ta2NiSe5 flakes. Au or ALD Al2O3 is evaporated onto a cleaved crystal and the stack is transferred by Au cold welding. Polarized Raman spectroscopy then tracks the orthorhombic-to-monoclinic transition and its electronic signatures in 4L to 18L flakes. The headline result is that 4L and 6L flakes on Au show a transition that is lowered (to an "average" T_c of about 220 K for 4L) and broadened. In contrast, 4L on Al2O3 stays sharp at about 280 K. The authors read this as an interfacial charge-transfer and screening gradient that suppresses excitonic interactions, and they put the excitonic share of the 326 K T_c at 100 K or more. The fabrication method and the substrate-contrast idea are useful and timely, given the ongoing excitonic-versus-lattice debate. However, the quantitative claims and the mechanistic attribution go further than the data can presently support.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The fabrication protocol is new and practical. Evaporated Au or Al2O3 plus Au cold welding yields 10 to 100 µm few-layer flakes of a rippled, hard-to-exfoliate vdW compound, and the process can be run in inert gas or UHV (Fig. 1). This is likely to be useful well beyond Ta2NiSe5.
- The Raman methodology is careful in several respects:
  - substrate reference spectra in both channels (Fig. 2F,G) justify the use of the ac channel;
  - the B2g/Ag selection-rule change is shown in a 4L flake on Au (Fig. 2B to E);
  - the structural transition (Ag(m4)/Ag(m5) onset, Fig. 3) and the electronic transition (Fano asymmetry and quasi-elastic intensity, Fig. 4) are tracked in the same flakes.
- Structural and electronic T_c values agree for the sharp samples (7L/Al2O3: 320 vs 330 K; 4L/Al2O3: 280 vs 287 K; 18L/Au: 320 vs 306 K). This gives some confidence in the two criteria.
- At equal thickness (4L), the transition on Au and on Al2O3 is qualitatively different, which shows that the substrate matters.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Charge transfer and screening are inferred, not shown, and strain or interfacial chemistry has not been excluded (pp. 14 to 15, Fig. 5B).
   - **What the claim rests on.** The mechanism ("charge transfer and electrostatic screening ... are the likely culprit") rests only on the Au versus Al2O3 contrast. No measurement in the paper shows charge transfer: no carrier density, no core-level or work-function data, no band-edge shift, and not even the sign of the doping.
   - **Alternatives that the same contrast would produce.**
     - The two substrates differ in more than conductivity. Au is thermally evaporated directly onto the Ta2NiSe5 surface, where Au-Se reaction, Au intercalation or interdiffusion, and evaporation damage are all possible.
     - The Al2O3 samples instead see ALD at 100 to 150 °C with TMA/H2O.
     - The mechanical boundary conditions also differ: the press during cold welding, and different thermal-expansion mismatch on cooling from 340 K down to 10 K.
   - **Why strain matters here.** Ta2NiSe5 T_c is known to be very sensitive to pressure and uniaxial strain (refs. 7, 14 to 17, cited by the authors). A strain explanation would also have no special connection to excitons.
   - **What the authors should do.**
     - (i) Compare the absolute frequencies of the Ag modes in 4L/Au and 4L/Al2O3 at matched temperatures in the orthorhombic and monoclinic phases, and use known mode Grüneisen or strain coefficients to bound the strain.
     - (ii) Characterize the Ta2NiSe5/Au interface chemically, for example by XPS of the Au-Ta2NiSe5 interface or cross-sectional TEM, to exclude reaction or intercalation.
     - (iii) Ideally, add a control that separates electrostatics from chemistry and strain. One option is a monolayer hBN or a very thin (about 1 nm) Al2O3 spacer between Au and Ta2NiSe5. Another is Au deposited on top of a flake already on Al2O3.
   - Note that the existing "Al2O3" samples already sit on Au/Cr below 50 to 100 nm of Al2O3. This should be stated explicitly, because it is effectively a thick-spacer control.
   - Without such evidence, "manifesting the presence of excitonic interactions" (abstract) does not follow.

2. The magnitude of the headline suppression ("reduced by over 100 K") depends on an ad hoc definition and on the wrong reference (abstract; pp. 10, 13, 15).
   - **Inconsistent definitions.**
     - For the sharp flakes, T_c is defined as the onset kink (the solid triangle).
     - For 6L and 4L on Au, the same onset kink (250 K and 220 K) is relabelled T_c^avg. A ±40 K "broadening" is then attached, inferred from the gap between the first and second kinks (100 K vs 60 K).
     - If 220 K is the onset, the average transition temperature of a broadened transition would lie below it. If it is the average, then the onset criterion is being applied inconsistently.
     - The assumption that the second kink sits at a fixed 60 K below T_c, and that any extra gap measures symmetric broadening, is not justified physically.
   - **Wrong reference for the substrate effect.** The relevant comparison is 4L/Al2O3 (280 K) against 4L/Au (220 ± 40 K), a difference of about 60 ± 40 K, not the bulk value of 326 K. Part of the reduction from bulk is evidently thickness-related, since 4L/Al2O3 is already 15% below bulk.
   - **What the authors should do.**
     - Use one consistent criterion for all five samples, for example the midpoint and width from fits of the Ag(m5) intensity or the Ag(o3)*/Ag(m4) shift versus T.
     - Quote the substrate-induced shift at fixed thickness.
     - Revise "reduced by over 100 K" and "suppression of excitonic mechanisms lowers the 326 K T_c ... by at least 100 K" (p. 15) accordingly. The ±40 K uncertainty alone already makes "at least" untenable.

3. The data do not support the layer-resolved "T_c gradient" picture as stated (pp. 4, 13 to 14, Fig. 5B inset).
   - **Internal inconsistency.** The model has the interfacial one or two layers transitioning more than 100 K lower, while the surface layers stay near bulk ("closer to room temperature"). If that were so, the 4L/Au flake should show a monoclinic signal (Ag(m4)/Ag(m5) onset, Fig. 3T) starting near 280 to 320 K from the top layers. The observed onset is instead 220 K (6L: 250 K). Even the least-affected layers are therefore strongly shifted.
   - **Interlayer coupling.** In a 4L crystal with interlayer coupling, an independent layer-by-layer T_c is not obvious either.
   - **Depth sensitivity of Raman on Au.** At 632.8 nm on a reflective Au film, the standing-wave field is strongly reduced at the metal surface. Raman sensitivity is therefore weighted away from the interfacial layers, the very layers claimed to dominate the low-T_c signal. The roughly 50 to 100 times weaker signal of 4L/Au compared with 4L/Al2O3 (color scales in Figs. 3I,J and 4I,J, and the ×50 factors) is consistent with this.
   - **What the authors should do.**
     - Perform a transfer-matrix calculation of the layer-resolved Raman weight for both stacks (Ta2NiSe5/Au/Cr and Ta2NiSe5/Al2O3/Au/Cr).
     - Show quantitatively that a layer-dependent T_c profile reproduces the measured temperature dependence of Ag(m5) intensity and peak position.
     - Separately, rule out lateral inhomogeneity as the source of broadening, for example from amorphous-Au roughness, partial contact or local strain. Raman maps across each flake at a few temperatures would do this.
   - **The 1-to-2-layer confinement argument.** The claim that the interface effect is "confined to one or two layers" (p. 14) is a deduction from the persistence of broadening. It needs to come out of such modelling.

4. The attribution "Au weakens electron-hole, not electron-lattice, interactions" is not established (p. 15).
   - **The evidence offered.** The argument rests on two observations: the quasi-elastic peak (QEP) disappears for 4L/Au, while the Fano maximum -1/q drops only about 17%.
   - **Circular reasoning.** The QEP's microscopic origin is contested (direct excitonic fluctuations, ref. 54, versus exciton-coupled acoustic phonon, ref. 55). The paper uses the Au-induced suppression to "affirm" that the QEP is excitonic, and then uses the QEP to conclude that Au suppresses excitonic interactions.
   - **Detection limits for the QEP.**
     - The QEP is read at 10 cm⁻¹ in the weakest samples.
     - This is next to the laser line, and next to a spurious 15 cm⁻¹ Rayleigh leakage that was removed and interpolated only for the Au samples (Methods, p. 18).
     - A peak broadened over about 100 K at a signal 50 times weaker may simply fall below the noise rather than be destroyed.
   - **The Fano asymmetry is not a clean measure of electron-lattice coupling.** In an EI scenario, the continuum it couples to is the electron-hole continuum itself, and its temperature dependence tracks the gap.
   - **Ref. 60.** The authors themselves note that a gating study (ref. 60) reaches a different conclusion.
   - **What the authors should do.**
     - Give detection limits for the QEP in 4L/Au, for example by injecting the scaled 4L/Al2O3 QEP into the 4L/Au noise.
     - Show the raw low-frequency spectra without interpolation.
     - Discuss why a hybridization-gap or band-Jahn-Teller transition would not also be suppressed by doping and screening; chemical-potential shifts reduce T_c there too.
     - Soften "establishes an excitonic component" (p. 16) unless such discriminating evidence is added.

B. Should address

5. There is one flake per thickness and substrate, the thickness series is unmatched across substrates, and the headline sample is a single 4L/Au flake.
   - Five samples in total are shown. No second 4L/Au or 4L/Al2O3 flake is shown, and there is no 6L or 18L on Al2O3. A thickness effect on Al2O3 (4L is already 15% lowered) therefore cannot be separated from the substrate effect at 6L.
   - Given likely flake-to-flake variation in contact quality with amorphous Au, the authors should:
     - show at least one more 4L/Au and 4L/Al2O3 flake;
     - give the spread of T_c;
     - include a 6L/Al2O3 flake;
     - state whether the results survive thermal cycling.

6. Temperature smoothing may artificially broaden the transition (Methods, pp. 17 to 18; Fig. 3K to T).
   - The 2D maps are resampled at 5 K and smoothed with a 10 to 15 K Gaussian in temperature. The second derivatives (Fig. 3K to O) are computed from these smoothed maps.
   - It is unclear whether the peak positions in Fig. 3P to T (and hence the kinks and the "broadening") come from raw spectra or from the smoothed maps.
   - A 10 to 15 K smoothing is comparable to the claimed differences. The authors should state this explicitly and extract all kinks from unsmoothed individual spectra.

7. Laser heating and the sample temperature are not reported per sample (Methods, p. 17).
   - About 1 mW is used at 632.8 nm, and the text says that Stokes/anti-Stokes ratios were checked. However, neither the resulting temperatures nor the spot size are given.
   - Heating can lower apparent T_c and can differ strongly between flakes on Au (high thermal conductivity, different absorption) and on Al2O3. Its direction is not obvious, and it bears directly on the 60 K substrate difference.
   - The authors should tabulate the effective temperatures from the Stokes/anti-Stokes ratio for each sample and temperature region, and show a power-dependence check near T_c.

8. The statement that Al2O3 has no "notable interface effect" sits uneasily with a 15% T_c reduction at 4L (pp. 13, 15).
   - The paper treats 4L/Al2O3 both as moderately perturbed and as the clean control ("absence of any notable interface effect"; it "serves as a control to exclude a degradation effect").
   - The authors should explain the 40 K reduction: thickness, strain, or ALD processing.
   - Also, a sharp transition in the Al2O3-protected flake does not exclude degradation specific to the Au interface. The process histories differ (direct Au evaporation versus an ALD-capped surface).

9. Fano-fit details and uncertainties are insufficient (Eq. 1; Fig. 4K to O).
   - Eq. 1 has no separate background term. The 20 to 80 cm⁻¹ window also contains the Ag(o1)* leakage mode near 35 cm⁻¹ and Ag(m1) at low T.
   - The paper states that error bars are "smaller than the symbols" even for the ×50-scaled 4L/Au data. These are presumably statistical fit errors only.
   - The authors should:
     - state which parameters are free;
     - explain how the extra modes and the background were treated;
     - give realistic uncertainties, for example from varying the fit window and background.
   - The 17% reduction in maximum -1/q, on which concern 4 rests, should be compared with 4L/Al2O3 (about 0.44), not with 18L/Au.

10. Layer counts and the supplementary material are not available for assessment.
   - Supplementary Notes 1 to 3 (azimuthal selection rules, additional structural analysis, AFM thickness determination) are referenced but are not included in the submission.
   - Layer counts on rough, amorphous Au are particularly uncertain, and a ±1L error changes the interpretation of 4L versus 6L.
   - The authors should provide:
     - the AFM profiles with step heights;
     - the Au roughness;
     - the per-layer thickness used (about 0.63 nm per layer?);
     - the azimuthal data for the 4L flakes.

C. Minor

11. The Fig. 4 caption says the dashed guide curves are in "(H) and (J)", whereas the figure and the text (p. 11, "Fig. 4, H and I") place them in H and I.

12. The T_c error bars are set only by half the temperature spacing (p. 8). They do not include the ambiguity in identifying a kink in broadened data, which is significant for the open triangles in Fig. 3S,T ("roughly determine"). Please state this, or use a fit-based uncertainty.

13. The electronic T_c uses a "within 15% of maximum" criterion (Fig. 4P to S). This choice is arbitrary and sets the width directly. Please justify it or show the sensitivity to the threshold.

14. Fig. 1B shows ribbons on epitaxial Au(111)/mica, whereas all Raman samples are on amorphous Au/Cr. Please state the Au morphology and roughness of the measured substrates, and whether Cr could diffuse to the surface through 20 to 50 nm of Au.

15. Typo: "exctonic" (p. 3).

16. Please discuss the comparison with ref. 45 (5L on SiO2, T_c reduced by 9% or less) and with ref. 60 more concretely. In particular, the gated, hBN-encapsulated flakes in ref. 60 provide a cleaner electrostatic-doping comparison, and readers will want to know whether the two results are compatible.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a spacer series between Au and Ta2NiSe5, for example 1L hBN, a few nm of Al2O3, and the existing 50 to 100 nm. This would turn the Au/Al2O3 dichotomy into a quantitative screening-length or charge-transfer dependence, and would separate electrostatics from chemistry and strain.
2. Model the depth-resolved Raman sensitivity, and fit a layer-dependent T_c profile to the Ag(m5) intensity versus T for 4L and 6L on Au. Complement this with spatial Raman maps.
3. Measure the interfacial doping directly, for example by XPS or UPS on cold-welded flakes, Kelvin-probe work-function differences, or the optical gap or band edge by reflectance contrast. Then compare the inferred carrier density with the doping levels in K-dosing (refs. 26 to 29) and gating (ref. 60) studies.
4. Report reproducibility on additional 4L/Au and 4L/Al2O3 flakes, and quote the substrate effect as a shift at fixed thickness with a single, uniformly applied T_c criterion.
