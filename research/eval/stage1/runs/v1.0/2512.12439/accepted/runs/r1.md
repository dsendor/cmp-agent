Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports a dry exfoliation route for Ta2NiSe5. Au or Al2O3 is evaporated or ALD-deposited onto a cleaved crystal and then transferred by Au cold welding. Using polarized Raman spectroscopy on five flakes, the authors find that 4L Ta2NiSe5 on Al2O3 keeps a sharp transition about 40 K below the bulk Tc, while 6L and 4L flakes on Au show a lowered and strongly broadened transition. The authors read this as a vertical Tc gradient caused by screening and charge transfer from Au. They go on to argue that the Au substrate mainly suppresses the excitonic contribution, worth up to about 150 K of the 326 K Tc. The fabrication method and the substrate-dependent Raman phenomenology are of clear interest. The microscopic interpretation, and especially the quantitative split of Tc into excitonic and lattice parts, rests on single samples and on an inference chain with several untested assumptions.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A practical, solvent-free transfer protocol that gives few-layer Ta2NiSe5 flakes 10 to 100 um across on both a metal and an insulator. This could be useful well beyond this compound.
- Careful use of the ac channel to reject substrate signals. Bare Au/Cr and Al2O3 reference spectra are shown under matched conditions (Fig. 2f, g).
- Structural signatures (onset of Ag(m4) and Ag(m5), Fig. 3) and electronic signatures (Fano asymmetry and quasi-elastic weight, Fig. 4) are tracked together on the same flakes, and the two sets of Tc values broadly agree (Fig. 5).
- The 18L/Au and 7L/Al2O3 flakes reproduce the bulk Tc. This is an internal check that the transfer process itself does not destroy the transition.
- The work opens a substrate- and interface-based way to tune the transition. It complements chemical doping, K dosing and gating.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Only one flake per condition, so the central comparison is not shown to be reproducible. Each of the five conditions (7L/Al2O3, 4L/Al2O3, 18L/Au, 6L/Au, 4L/Au) appears to rest on a single flake, and the headline contrast is between one 4L/Al2O3 flake and one 4L/Au flake. Thin-flake Tc values are known to scatter: the authors themselves cite 309 K for 8L on SiO2 and modest gate shifts in thin flakes. A 40 K versus 150 K difference from two flakes cannot be separated from flake-to-flake variation in strain, defects, ALD or evaporation conditions, or thickness errors. The authors should:
   - measure at least two or three more flakes for each of 4L/Au and 4L/Al2O3, ideally from different exfoliation runs;
   - show the spread of the onset and width of the transition;
   - if possible, measure a single flake that spans both substrates. A partially Al2O3-covered region before Au deposition would give Au and Al2O3 contact side by side, removing flake-to-flake variation from the comparison.

2. The Au and Al2O3 samples differ in more than the substrate's conductivity. The paper attributes the whole difference to screening and charge transfer from a metal (pp. 14 to 15). But the two interfaces are made in very different ways:
   - Al2O3 is grown by ALD at 100 to 150 °C with TMA and H2O directly on Ta2NiSe5. This is a chemically reactive, heated process that could passivate, oxidize or otherwise modify the first layer.
   - Au is thermally evaporated onto the crystal at room temperature, with a different thermal history and different adhesion. It also conforms differently to the rippled surface, which the authors themselves invoke (p. 4).

   Ta2NiSe5's Tc is very sensitive to uniaxial strain (Ref. 16), so a strain difference imposed by the substrate is a direct alternative explanation for both the suppression and the broadening. Note also that a gradient of interfacial strain decaying away from the substrate would produce the same vertical Tc gradient that the authors attribute to screening. The authors should:
   - compare the frequencies and linewidths of strain-sensitive Ag phonons well above Tc (for example at 340 to 400 K) and well below Tc across all five flakes, to bound any relative strain;
   - discuss interfacial strain explicitly;
   - ideally add a control in which the substrate is a non-metal deposited the same way as Au (for example an evaporated dielectric), or Au separated from the flake by a thin evaporated insulating spacer. The spacer would test whether the effect needs direct electronic contact.

3. The optical depth sensitivity near a metal substrate goes against the claim that the interfacial layers are detected. The interpretation (pp. 13 to 14) needs the Raman signal from the 1 or 2 layers next to Au to be a substantial part of the total. But the tangential optical field at a good-metal interface is strongly suppressed at 632.8 nm. The layers next to Au should then contribute least to the Raman signal, while those on Al2O3 (itself on Au, 50 to 100 nm away) sit in a very different standing-wave pattern. Fig. 3e is scaled ×50 and Fig. 3c only ×3, which shows how much the depth weighting differs between the samples. If the interfacial layers are weighted weakly, the observed broadening needs either a larger perturbed region or a different cause. The authors should:
   - give a transfer-matrix calculation of the layer-resolved Raman weight (incident × scattered field intensity) for each stack;
   - build that weighting into the toy model of Supplementary Note 4, if it is not already there;
   - show that the measured lineshapes are consistent with the extent of the interface region they propose.

4. The data do not separate excitonic from electron-lattice mechanisms, and the quantitative decomposition goes too far. The conclusion that Au "weaken[s] electron-hole interactions" rather than electron-lattice coupling, and that excitonic mechanisms account for about 150 K of Tc with about 180 K left over (p. 16), rests on four assumptions:
   - (a) the disappearance of the quasi-elastic peak in 4L/Au means excitonic fluctuations are destroyed, not just buried in noise;
   - (b) the 16% drop of the maximum of -1/q measures unchanged electron-phonon coupling;
   - (c) the interfacial layers lose their excitonic contribution completely;
   - (d) the excitonic and lattice contributions to Tc add.

   None of these is shown. Electron doping and metallic screening also suppress a hybridization- or band-Jahn-Teller-type structural instability, because they move the Fermi level away from compensation and screen the interband matrix elements. A Tc reduction under charge transfer is therefore expected in both pictures. The Fano parameter also depends on the continuum's density of states, which charge transfer changes directly. So a "not strongly reduced" -1/q does not isolate the electron-phonon coupling constant. The authors should either:
   - give a model calculation (even a minimal two-band model with phonon coupling plus screened interaction) showing that the observed pattern discriminates between the scenarios; or
   - substantially soften the abstract, Discussion and Conclusions, which say the results "confirm the existence of excitonic effects" and assign a 150 K excitonic scale.

5. The electronic Tc for 4L/Au is not measured independently. For 4L/Au the quasi-elastic peak is undetectable, and Tc^avg ≈ 220±46 K is obtained by assuming the same width as 6L/Au and a ~30 K shift read from -1/q (p. 12). The ±46 K is copied from the 6L flake. This point is then plotted in Fig. 5b as if it were an independent electronic estimate, and it supports the agreement between the structural and electronic transitions. The authors should either:
   - extract a Tc and width for 4L/Au directly from the -1/q(T) curve with a stated criterion that they also apply to all other flakes; or
   - mark this point clearly as an estimate in Fig. 5b and leave it out of the argument that the structural and electronic transitions agree.

B. Should address

6. The transition width is defined in an ad hoc way. The ±40 K broadening for the 6L and 4L flakes on Au comes from the spacing between the first and second kinks in the Ag(m4) position. The spacing is 100 K on Au versus 60 K elsewhere, giving (100 − 60) = 40 K (pp. 9 to 10). This assumes the second kink is a fixed intrinsic offset below Tc whose broadening maps directly onto the distribution of Tc. Neither assumption is justified, and the kinks in Figs. 3s and 3t are described as "broadened and less defined", on peak positions quantized at 1.3 cm^-1. A more robust measure would be the temperature dependence of the integrated intensity of Ag(m4) and Ag(m5) in the ac channel, which scales with the square of the monoclinic order parameter. It could be fitted to an order-parameter form convolved with a distribution of Tc, with fitted widths and uncertainties reported for all five flakes.

7. The Tc gradient across the layers is inferred, not tested. Lateral inhomogeneity could also produce the broadening: thickness steps or wrinkles within the laser spot, strain from the roughness of the amorphous Au, or local Au–flake contact varying across the flake. The authors should:
   - give the spot size and show Raman maps, or at least spectra at several positions on the 4L/Au and 6L/Au flakes;
   - check whether the width depends on position.

   The layer-number scaling should also be checked quantitatively. If 1 or 2 interfacial layers are perturbed, the mixed fraction is about 2/4 for 4L but only about 2/6 for 6L. Yet both flakes show the same ±40 K width and similar kink spacing. The authors should explain how this fits the gradient picture.

8. Sample temperature and laser heating are not quantified. The Methods say Stokes/anti-Stokes ratios were checked at about 1 mW, but no numbers are given. The thermal anchoring of a 4L flake on Au and on 50 to 100 nm of ALD Al2O3 differs, so a laser-heating offset of different size in different flakes could shift the apparent Tc. The Methods also describe a cryostat only "for measurements below 300 K". It is not stated how the 300 to 400 K spectra were taken, or whether a change of setup or window calibration happens near 300 K. That is exactly where the 7L/Al2O3 and 18L/Au transitions (~320 K) lie. The authors should:
   - report the Stokes/anti-Stokes temperatures against nominal temperature for each flake, or at least for the two 4L flakes;
   - describe the high-temperature setup;
   - show that the data join continuously across 300 K.

9. Charge transfer is asserted, not measured. Neither the amount nor the sign of charge transfer from Au is estimated. The Au work function (polycrystalline, on Cr) versus that of Ta2NiSe5 (~5.4 eV, cited) is not discussed. No observable shift tied to doping is shown, such as a change in phonon frequency or line asymmetry at high temperature relative to Al2O3, or a change in the electronic background. Without at least an order-of-magnitude estimate of the transferred carrier density, set against the doping levels at which K dosing or substitution suppress Tc, charge transfer cannot be weighed against screening. Charge transfer is also part of the paper's argument for an excitonic mechanism. The authors should give such an estimate (for example from the work-function difference and an interfacial capacitance) and, if available, any Raman or Kelvin-probe evidence.

10. The 40 K suppression for 4L on Al2O3 is unexplained. The text calls 4L/Al2O3 "moderately perturbed" with "minimal influence from the substrate", yet Tc drops by about 40 K between 7L and 4L. Is this an intrinsic thickness effect, an effect of the ALD interface, or strain? This sets the baseline against which the Au effect is measured: the "150 K" suppression on Au partly overlaps the 40 K seen on Al2O3. The authors should discuss the thickness dependence on Al2O3 against Ref. 45 and Ref. 50 (gate-tuned thin flakes). Ideally they should also add a thickness-matched comparison, for example 6L or 7L on both substrates, which the present set (7L/Al2O3 versus 6L/Au) only roughly gives.

11. The quasi-elastic peak suppression in 4L/Au may be limited by signal-to-noise. Fig. 4t shows χ''(10 cm^-1) at ×50 scaling. In this flake a Rayleigh leakage peak at 15 cm^-1 was removed by interpolation (Methods). The statement that the quasi-elastic peak is "suppressed beyond detection" and that this "affirms" its excitonic origin (p. 15) should be backed by:
   - the noise level and an upper bound on the quasi-elastic amplitude, normalized to a phonon intensity in the same spectrum;
   - a demonstration that the Rayleigh removal does not affect the 10 cm^-1 point.

12. The Fano fits need more detail. Fig. 4k–o reports fit errors "smaller than the symbols". The paper should say:
   - whether the background is free or fixed;
   - how the overlap with Ag(m1)/Ag(o1)* is handled for the 6L and 4L Au flakes (the text says a Lorentzian is included only for 7L/Al2O3 and 18L/Au);
   - how correlated q, γ and A are.

   Fit-only errors understate the uncertainty when the lineshape changes across the transition. The Discussion compares maxima of -1/q (0.38 versus 0.45). That comparison needs realistic uncertainties, for example from varying the fit window or background model.

C. Minor

13. Page 12: "By comparing -1/q of the 6L and 4L flakes on Au on the same plot (see Fig. 3o)" should refer to Fig. 4o.

14. The Tc uncertainties in Fig. 3 (for example 320 +10/−14 K) are half the spacing between neighbouring temperatures, while the electronic Tc values use a 15%-of-maximum window. These two error definitions are not equivalent, yet they are plotted side by side in Fig. 5. State this in the Fig. 5 caption.

15. Layer count: the main text relies on AFM thickness for 4L and 6L on rough amorphous Au. Give the measured step heights with uncertainties in the main text or in a table cited there, since a one-layer error changes the interpretation of the interface fraction.

16. Give the laser spot size, the power density, the exposure of the samples to air between peeling and measurement, and the time between fabrication and measurement for each flake.

17. Only one excitation wavelength (632.8 nm) is used. Resonance conditions may change with thickness and substrate. Comment on whether intensity ratios between flakes (for example the ×50 factor) can be compared.

18. Fig. 4 caption: define the green "long tail" region and the dashed guides in Figs. 4h and 4i more precisely, since they are used qualitatively in the text.

19. The abstract calls the effect "atomic-scale". This should be stated as an inference from the 4L/6L comparison, with its assumptions given, rather than as a measured fact.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add matched controls that separate the candidate mechanisms: more 4L flakes on each substrate; Au separated from the flake by an evaporated insulating spacer; and, if possible, a single flake bridging the Au and Al2O3 regions. Together these address reproducibility, strain or chemistry, and screening versus charge transfer.
2. Model the layer-resolved Raman sensitivity of each stack with a transfer-matrix calculation, and include it in the toy model of a layer-dependent Tc. Fit the order-parameter intensity of Ag(m4) and Ag(m5) with a distribution of Tc to get the transition widths quantitatively.
3. Use high-temperature phonon frequencies and linewidths as built-in gauges of strain and doping across all flakes, and estimate the transferred charge density.
4. Recast the excitonic-versus-lattice discussion as one consistent interpretation among possible ones, unless a model calculation shows that the observed pattern of Fano and quasi-elastic behavior discriminates between them. Drop or qualify the numerical 150 K / 180 K split.
