A. Must address

1. The curvature signature is not shown to be specific to flat bands, and competing explanations for sub- or superlinear rho(T) are not excluded (abstract, title, Figs. 3, 4(a), 4(e), Eq. 1, conclusion). The model's only input is a steep energy dependence of D(E) within a few k_BT of E_F (Figs. 1(c), 2). Any transition-metal compound with E_F near a d-band edge has this feature: Pd, Pt, A15 compounds and Heusler alloys show sublinear or superlinear rho(T) for exactly this reason. That is Mott's s-d scattering, which the authors cite (Refs. 29, 45–47). Several other mechanisms also curve rho(T) above room temperature and are not discussed: resistivity saturation near the Mott-Ioffe-Regel limit (the parallel-resistor form of Fisk-Webb and Wiesmann et al.); thermal expansion and phonon softening, including the constant-pressure versus constant-volume difference up to 862 K; anharmonic and multiphonon scattering; spin-fluctuation or paramagnon scattering, relevant for Ni3In (a strange metal near magnetic order), CaRh2, and the Co-, Pd- and Pt-based compounds; a temperature-dependent chemical potential; and the electron-electron scattering the model explicitly omits (p. 2). Saturation is a particular problem: the two compounds that show clear sublinearity, Ni3In and CaRh2, are also the ones with the largest resistivity (about 100–160 microOhm cm in Fig. 4(a)), exactly where saturation is generic, so Fig. 4(a) cannot separate the FB mechanism from saturation. As it stands the agreement in Fig. 4(a) shows consistency, not inference. The authors should: (i) fit the same data with a parallel-resistor model (rho_sat as a parameter), with Eq. 1 plus a saturation term, with plain BG plus thermal expansion, with constant-DOS BG, and with BG plus a T^2 electron-electron term, using the same number of free parameters, and compare residuals, goodness of fit and the physical plausibility of the parameters; (ii) include a reference metal with a smooth D(E) near E_F, ideally isostructural to a FB compound (a Cu3Au-type or CoSn-type analog without a FB near E_F), measured with the same setup over the same range, and show that Eq. 1 reduces to BG and rho(T) is linear above Theta_D; (iii) include a metal with a steep but ordinary (non-flat) d-band edge such as Pd or Pt, and discuss whether such metals are "flat-band" positives of the diagnostic, showing what extra information distinguishes a flat band; (iv) ideally show a low-resistivity compound whose FB edge lies above E_F, or a sublinear compound with no FB near E_F, so that sublinearity is seen to track the FB rather than the magnitude of rho; and (v) if a flat band cannot be distinguished from an ordinary steep DOS feature, narrow the claim to "steep DOS features near E_F".

The claim that rho(T) can locate or track the FB is not validated independently (abstract, "one can infer the position of FBs"; p. 3, the SM Eq. 6 fit formula "to pinpoint the position of the FB"). In Fig. 4(a) the FB position is an input through D_DFT(E), and only the prefactor C is fitted; this tests whether the DFT DOS is compatible with the data, not whether rho(T) determines E_F − E_edge. The inverse procedure (SM Eq. 6 with E_edge and m_FB free) is never applied in the main text: no extracted Delta or m_FB values, uncertainties or parameter correlations (Delta against m_FB against C, and the choice of n and Theta_D) are reported. The only extraction of a position is the CoSn argument in Fig. 4(e), where a visually chosen departure from linearity near 400 K is equated with Delta_DFT/3, and Fig. 3(e) establishes the factor 1/3 for a single parameter set only (m_FB = 10 m0, Delta = 100 meV). 3. The assumptions behind Eq. 1 are not stated or justified, and they control the sign of the effect (Eq. 1, Figs. 2, 3). (a) Chemical potential: it is not said whether mu is held fixed or computed self-consistently. With a strongly asymmetric, high-DOS band within about 20–100 meV of E_F (Figs. 2(b), 4(b)) and k_BT up to about 75 meV, mu(T) shifts by roughly (pi^2/6)(k_BT)^2 D'/D, which can be tens of meV at 800 K, and this shift alone can produce strong sub- or superlinear rho(T). (b) Conducting carriers: Eq. 1 keeps only D_DFT^-1(E) inside the thermal average, so sigma(E) is set only by tau(E) ∝ 1/D_total(E). It drops the energy dependence of the carrier velocity v^2(E) and of the conducting-band DOS, which enter sigma(E) = e^2 D(E) v^2(E) tau(E); this holds only if carriers live entirely in a dispersive band, while the total DFT DOS, including the FB states, is used as the scattering phase space. FB states also carry current with small v, and v^2(E) varies strongly in the same window. Using the total DOS rather than, for example, the DOS of the flat orbital character needs justification. (c) Factoring the thermal average of D^-1 out of the phonon integral as a multiplicative prefactor to the BG integral is an ad hoc separation; in a proper Boltzmann treatment the energy-dependent rate sits inside the transport integral weighted by D_c(E)v_c^2(E). (d) The electron-phonon matrix element is assumed energy- and momentum-independent for intraband and interband channels alike. (e) The scattering phase space is evaluated quasi-elastically at D(E), although k_BTheta_D ≈ 20–40 meV is comparable to Delta ≈ 20–100 meV, so D(E ± hbar omega) is the relevant quantity. The authors should derive Eq. 1 from the two-band Boltzmann model in the main text or SM, state each assumption, and show that the conclusions (Fig. 3 and the Fig. 4(a) fits) survive with v(E) included, the rate inside the transport integral, a self-consistent mu(T), and inelastic D(E ± hbar omega) phase space.

4. The "single free parameter" statement (p. 3) is not accurate, and fit quality is judged only by eye (Eq. 1, Fig. 4(a)). Eq. 1 also contains Theta_D and the exponent n (3 or 5), and it has no residual resistivity term, although the data clearly have rho0 ≠ 0 (Ni3In and Ca(Rh,Ru)2 start well above zero). The text does not say which n was used for which compound, whether Theta_D was fixed (from specific heat, and to what value) or fitted, how rho0 was handled, or the fit range. For polycrystals the geometric-factor uncertainty and grain-boundary contributions further loosen the absolute scale. Six fits are overlaid on a crowded panel, and several (for example Pd3Pb above 500 K) visibly deviate. Since the evidence for the mechanism is "good agreement" in Fig. 4(a), the authors should give, per compound in a table, every input and whether it is fixed (with source) or free: DOS source and settings, n, Theta_D, rho0, fit range, fitted C and the implied lambda_ep or lambda_tr. They should show residuals (data minus fit) and a goodness-of-fit metric for each compound, and show the BG fit without the DOS factor and with the same freedom as a null comparison, so that the improvement attributable to D_DFT(E) can be judged. The fitted C could also be checked against independent lambda_ep estimates (for example from the low-T T^5 coefficient in CoSn), turning the free prefactor into a consistency test.

B. Should address

5. Electron correlations are neglected in exactly the materials where they matter (p. 2, "we do not include electron-electron scattering"; p. 4, Ni3In strange-metal behaviour, Ref. 23). Fig. 4(b) gives a DFT D(E_F) of about 2.8 states/eV for Ni3In, corresponding via gamma = (pi^2/3)k_B^2 D(E_F) to about 6.6 mJ mol^-1 K^-2, whereas Fig. 4(c) shows gamma ≈ 50 mJ mol^-1 K^-2: a mass enhancement of about 7, far beyond typical electron-phonon values; for CoSn it is about 3. FB positions and widths in d-electron kagome and pyrochlore metals are renormalized relative to GGA-PBE, and for Ni3In ARPES and DMFT place the FB differently from GGA, so the bare DFT DOS as scattering phase space is questionable. Ni3In nonetheless serves as the main sublinear example, although its rho_ab is T-linear down to the lowest temperatures, which BG with n = 3 or 5 cannot reproduce. The authors should quantify the enhancement for each compound (with per-formula-unit normalization), discuss how a correlation-renormalized FB (DFT+DMFT or the ARPES position) would change the Eq. 1 fits, explain why electron-electron scattering, which can also give superlinear rho(T), is negligible at 300–860 K, and show the Ni3In fit range and low-T mismatch explicitly, explaining why an e-ph-only fit over 1.5–300 K is meaningful when the low-T behaviour is non-phononic.

6. The model band structure does not match the flat bands in the real materials (Fig. 1(c), Fig. 2 insets, Fig. 4(b)). The model uses a heavy parabolic band with a single edge, giving a monotonic, step-like sigma(E). The schematic in Fig. 1(c) and the DFT DOS in Fig. 4(b) instead show narrow peaks with two sides; with E_F near a peak both sides are thermally sampled, and the clean sub/superlinear dichotomy tied to "E_F above/below the edge" is not obvious. Kagome FBs also touch a dispersive band at a quadratic band-touching point rather than being detached heavy parabolas. The authors should show model results for a peak-shaped DOS (for example a Lorentzian or a kagome tight-binding DOS) with E_F on either side of the peak, and define operationally what "FB edge" means for the DFT DOS in Fig. 4(b).

7. The semiclassical treatment is used outside its range of validity (Fig. 3(c), Fig. 4(a)). For m_FB = 100 m0, Fig. 3(c) reaches a resistivity maximum near 500 microOhm cm, well beyond the Ioffe-Regel limit, and Ni3In and Ca(Rh,Ru)2 lie at 100–160 microOhm cm, where the mean free path approaches the lattice spacing and Boltzmann transport with a well-defined tau(E) is doubtful. 11. Anisotropy and sample form are not accounted for (Fig. 4(d,e)). CoSn is measured along c only, Ni3In data are in-plane, and Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb are polycrystals that average over anisotropy and add grain-boundary scattering. The kagome FB is quasi-two-dimensional and arises from in-plane interference, so interband scattering into it affects c-axis and in-plane conductivity differently through v_z^2 weighting; a scalar, isotropic DOS model cannot capture this. The authors should add in-plane rho(T) for CoSn and test whether the same Delta/3 onset appears, or explain why c-axis curvature tests the in-plane FB, and comment on how grain boundaries and porosity affect the high-T curvature of the polycrystals.

15. The relation to prior work and what is new are unclear (p. 1, "inspired by Mott's early work"; Refs. 29, 33, 43, 45–48). The mechanism, a scattering rate proportional to the DOS of a heavy band producing rho(T) curvature set by the band-edge position, is Mott's s-d model, and DOS-driven rho(T) curvature is classic for A15 compounds and Pd and Pt. Energy-dependent scattering and a two-parabolic-band interband framework already appear in the authors' Refs. 45–48, and the Ni3In and Ni3Ge rho(T) were analysed with first-principles electron-phonon (Phoebe) calculations in Refs. 33 and 43. The manuscript should place the work in this literature, state precisely what is new (application to FB compounds, the T ≈ Delta/3 criterion, the inverse fit procedure, the new CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb data), and compare Eq. 1 quantitatively with the ab initio RTA results for the same compounds.
