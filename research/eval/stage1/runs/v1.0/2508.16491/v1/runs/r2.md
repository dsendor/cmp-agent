Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript proposes that sub- or superlinear curvature in the high-temperature resistivity ρ(T) of metals hosting flat bands (FBs) comes from phonon-mediated interband scattering into the FB. The scattering rate follows the energy-dependent DOS, so the sign of the curvature encodes whether E_F lies below or above the FB edge. The authors support this with a two-parabolic-band Boltzmann model (Figs. 2, 3) and with fits of a modified Bloch-Grüneisen formula (Eq. 1) that uses DFT densities of states. The fits cover six intermetallics, three of them measured here up to 862 K (Fig. 4). A cheap transport-based diagnostic for FB placement would be useful. However, the physical mechanism is essentially Mott's s-d scattering picture, and the paper does not yet show that the diagnostic is specific to flat bands or that it can locate a FB without already knowing where it is.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A simple, physically transparent framework connects a widely measured quantity, ρ(T) over a broad temperature range, to the energy landscape of FB states. It may be useful to the large community studying kagome, pyrochlore and Cu3Au-type FB compounds.
- New high-temperature (up to 862 K) resistivity data on CoSn single crystals and on polycrystalline Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb, plus specific heat on Pd3Pb.
- Fig. 3(e) scales the temperature at which deviations from Bloch-Grüneisen begin to T ≈ Δ/3. This gives a concrete, testable prediction, and the CoSn data in Fig. 4(e) are checked against it.
- The fits in Fig. 4(a) reproduce qualitatively different curvatures across compounds, using the DFT DOS as input.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The curvature signature is not shown to be unique to flat bands, and the standard alternative explanations are not excluded** (Figs. 3, 4(a), 4(e); conclusion paragraph). The central claim is that sublinear or superlinear ρ(T) at elevated temperature reveals the FB position. Several well-known mechanisms produce the same curvatures in intermetallics:
   - Resistivity saturation, the parallel-resistor or Ioffe-Regel behavior of Fisk-Webb and Wiesmann et al. Ni3In and Ca(Rh,Ru)2 reach 100–160 μΩ cm in Fig. 4(a), exactly the regime where saturation is generic.
   - Mott s-d scattering in ordinary transition metals with a steep d-band DOS near E_F, such as Pd and Pt. These show sublinear ρ(T) without any "flat band" in the sense of the introduction.
   - Thermal expansion and phonon softening at constant pressure up to 862 K.
   - Electron-electron T² contributions, which the model explicitly omits.
   - Temperature-dependent chemical-potential shifts, and anharmonic or multiphonon scattering.

   Without discriminating these, the agreement in Fig. 4(a) shows consistency, not inference. The authors should:
   - fit the sublinear data with a parallel-resistor form (ρ_sat as a parameter) and compare fit quality and parameter plausibility with Eq. 1;
   - include at least one reference metal with a smooth DOS near E_F, ideally isostructural to a FB compound (e.g., a Cu3Au-type or CoSn-type analog without the FB near E_F), measured with the same setup over the same range, and show that it gives linear ρ(T) above Θ_D;
   - discuss explicitly why Pd/Pt-type d-band metals are or are not "flat-band" positives of this diagnostic.

2. **The paper does not demonstrate that the FB position can actually be extracted from ρ(T)** (abstract, "one can infer the position of FBs"; page 3, "derived a fit formula (see Eq. 6 in the SM) to pinpoint the position of the FB"). In Fig. 4(a) the FB position is an input, through D_DFT(E); only the prefactor C is fitted. This tests whether the DFT DOS is compatible with the data, not whether ρ(T) determines E_F − E_edge. The SM fit formula (Eq. 6), with E_edge and m_FB as free parameters, is the actual "tracking" tool, but no extracted values, uncertainties or parameter correlations are shown in the main text.

   The authors should:
   - fit each compound with E_F − E_edge (and m_FB) free and report the values with confidence intervals;
   - compare these blind estimates with independent determinations (ARPES for CoSn and Ni3In, DFT, specific heat);
   - show how strongly the Eq. 1 fit quality depends on a rigid shift of D_DFT(E) by, say, ±20, ±50 and ±100 meV. If shifts of tens of meV do not appreciably change χ², the method cannot "pinpoint" the FB.

B. Should address

3. **Eq. 1 is not derived in the main text, and the claim of a single free parameter needs qualification** (Eq. 1 and the text after it).
   - Eq. 1 contains no residual resistivity, yet the data in Fig. 4(a) clearly have ρ0 ≠ 0 (e.g., Ni3In and Ca(Rh,Ru)2 start well above zero). Θ_D and n also enter, and the paper does not say how they were fixed for each compound (from specific heat? which value of n for which compound?).
   - Factoring the thermal average of D⁻¹ out of the phonon integral, as a multiplicative prefactor to the BG integral, is an ad hoc separation. In a proper Boltzmann treatment the energy-dependent rate enters inside the transport integral, weighted by D_c(E)v_c²(E) of the conducting band. Eq. 1 implicitly assumes D_c v_c² is energy-independent.
   - The chemical potential is held fixed. With a strongly asymmetric DOS near E_F, μ(T) shifts by roughly (π²/6)(k_BT)²D′/D, which can be tens of meV at 800 K. This enters the same physics the method relies on.

   The authors should state every parameter (fixed and free) and its source for each compound, and include ρ0 explicitly. They should also show that the result survives a calculation in which the energy-dependent rate sits inside the transport integral and μ(T) is computed self-consistently.

4. **The model band structure does not match the FBs in the real materials** (Fig. 1(c) vs. Fig. 2 insets vs. Fig. 4(b)).
   - The model uses a heavy parabolic band with a single edge, giving a monotonic, step-like σ(E).
   - The schematic in Fig. 1(c) and the DFT DOS in Fig. 4(b) instead show narrow peaks with two sides. With E_F near a peak, both sides are thermally sampled, and the clean sub/superlinear dichotomy tied to "E_F above/below the edge" is not obvious.
   - Kagome FBs touch a dispersive band at a quadratic band-touching point; they are not detached heavy parabolas.

   Please show model results for a peak-shaped DOS, e.g., a Lorentzian or a kagome tight-binding DOS with E_F on either side of the peak. Please also clarify what "FB edge" means operationally for the DFT DOS in Fig. 4(b).

5. **Correlation effects are neglected in exactly the materials where they matter** (page 1, "we do not include electron-electron scattering"; page 4, Ni3In strange-metal behavior). Fig. 4(b) gives a DFT DOS at E_F of about 2.8 states/eV for Ni3In. Via γ = (π²/3)k_B²D(E_F), that corresponds to about 6.6 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻², whereas Fig. 4(c) shows γ ≈ 50 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻². This is a mass enhancement of about 7, far beyond typical electron-phonon values. For CoSn the enhancement is about 3. Such renormalization narrows the FB and may shift it relative to E_F, so using the bare DFT DOS as the scattering phase space is questionable. Ni3In also shows T-linear ρ_ab down to the lowest temperatures, which the authors themselves attribute to non-Fermi-liquid physics.

   Please:
   - quantify the enhancement for each compound (with the per-formula-unit normalization stated);
   - discuss how a renormalized FB would change Eq. 1;
   - explain why e-e scattering, which can also give superlinear ρ(T), is negligible at 300–860 K.

6. **The semiclassical treatment is used outside its range of validity** (Fig. 3(c); Fig. 4(a)). For m_FB = 100 m0, Fig. 3(c) gives a resistivity maximum near 500 μΩ cm. Ni3In and Ca(Rh,Ru)2 lie at 100–160 μΩ cm. At these values the mean free path approaches the lattice spacing, and Boltzmann transport with a well-defined τ(E) is doubtful. The authors should estimate k_F·l (or l/a) for the fitted compounds at the highest temperatures and discuss the limits of the framework. The semiconductor-like maximum in Fig. 3(c), offered as a striking prediction, needs this caveat.

7. **Experimental details for the high-temperature transport are insufficient** (page 2; Fig. 4(a)). Please report:
   - how many samples of each compound were measured, and whether the curvature reproduces between samples;
   - the setups used for 1.5–300 K and 300–862 K, and how the two ranges were joined;
   - the atmosphere, and whether the data on heating and cooling coincide (oxidation, Sn/Pb loss, defect annealing or ordering changes in Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb near 800 K can all alter the slope);
   - the uncertainty in the geometric factor.

   Polycrystals average over anisotropy and add grain-boundary scattering. For CoSn only c-axis data are shown, although the kagome FB is a predominantly in-plane feature. ρ_ab of CoSn would test whether the curvature is anisotropic in the way the DFT DOS-only model (which is isotropic) assumes.

8. **The specific-heat comparison needs checking and completion** (page 4; Fig. 4(c); Fig. S2).
   - γ ≈ 0.03 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² for Pd3Pb is implausibly small for a metal with four atoms per formula unit, more than an order of magnitude below a free-electron estimate. In Fig. 4(c) the Pd3Pb bar also looks larger than 0.03. Please check the value, the units and the per-mole normalization (per formula unit or per atom), and show the C/T vs T² fit.
   - Pt3Sn and Ni3Ge are fitted in Fig. 4(a) but omitted from Fig. 4(c) and from the discussion of FB position. Please include them or explain why not.
   - A larger γ is consistent with any DOS difference at E_F. It supports the FB-edge assignment only when compared quantitatively with D_DFT(E_F), as in concern 5.

9. **Relation to prior work and novelty** (page 1, "inspired by Mott's early work"; refs. 45–48). The mechanism (scattering rate ∝ DOS of a heavy band, producing ρ(T) curvature set by the band-edge position) is Mott's s-d model. Energy-dependent scattering phase space producing anomalous ρ(T) has been discussed by some of the same authors (refs. 45–48), including a two-parabolic-band framework (ref. 48). The manuscript should state clearly what is new: the application to FB compounds, the T ≈ Δ/3 criterion, the inverse fit procedure, or the high-temperature data. The claim that the behavior holds "universally among different material classes" should be toned down to match six compounds.

C. Minor

10. **Compound labels:** the legend of Fig. 4(a) reads "Ca(Rh,Ru)2, Wakefield et al.", while the text and Figs. 4(b, c) say CaRh2. Please state the exact composition used and whether Ru substitution shifts E_F relative to the DFT DOS of stoichiometric CaRh2.

11. **CaRh2 DOS resolution:** the poorest fit (CaRh2) is attributed to the coarse Materials Project DOS. Since the authors performed DFT for other compounds, a properly converged DOS on a fine energy grid for CaRh2 would replace this conjecture with a test.

12. **Fig. 3(e) inset vs. Fig. 2(a):** the inset is described as σ(E) "from Fig. 2(a)" (m_FB = 10 m0), but it shows a very sharp step, closer to the m_FB = 100 m0 case of Fig. 2(b). Please check which σ(E) is plotted. Also check the Fig. 3 caption wording: "(a) and (c) above E_F" refers to the FB edge position and could be phrased more clearly.

13. **CoSn edge position:** the text says the FB edge in CoSn is "more than 0.1 eV below E_F", but the "FB edge" arrow in Fig. 4(b) sits at about −0.09 eV. Please give the Δ_DFT value used for the T ≈ 400 K ≈ Δ_DFT/3 estimate in Fig. 4(e), and say how the departure from linearity was defined (the fit range of the dashed line).

14. **Supplementary material not available:** the model parameters (band offsets, coupling constants, how absolute μΩ cm values in Fig. 3 are set), Eq. 6, and Figs. S1 and S2 are all deferred to the SM, which is not included (ref. [30] is a placeholder link). Key model parameters should be stated in the main text so that Figs. 2 and 3 can be reproduced.

15. **Fig. 4(d):** please give ρ0 and the RRR of the CoSn crystal. Note that n ≈ 5 is the generic expectation for any clean metal with no FB states at E_F, so it is weak positive evidence for the FB assignment. Quoting r² = 0.991 adds little; residuals would be more informative.

16. **Fit quality in Fig. 4(a):** fit residuals and the fitted C values (or the implied λ_tr) for each compound would let readers judge fit quality and physical plausibility. Several fits (e.g., Pd3Pb above 500 K) visibly deviate.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Measure the thermopower S(T) on the authors' own CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb samples over the same temperature range. The paper itself notes that the sign of S distinguishes the scenarios. This would be an independent, same-sample test of the inferred FB position and would strengthen the paper considerably.
2. Do a blind test: extract E_F − E_edge from ρ(T) alone using the SM fit formula, then compare with ARPES and DFT in a table. Include at least one non-FB control metal.
3. Track the FB position systematically within one family using a doping or pressure series (e.g., electron/hole doping of CoSn or Ni3In, or the Ca(Rh,Ru)2 series), and show the curvature changing sign as E_F crosses the FB edge. This would be the most convincing demonstration of "tracking".
4. Discuss the domain of validity explicitly: Boltzmann transport limits, the role of correlation-renormalized FBs, and when saturation or e-e scattering would mask the effect. This helps readers apply the method responsibly.
