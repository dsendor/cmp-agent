Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript proposes that the shape of the high-temperature resistivity rho(T) in metals can locate a flat band (FB) relative to E_F. It models the phonon-mediated interband scattering rate as proportional to the density of states (tau_ep^-1 ~ D(E)). The model gives a sublinear rho(T) when E_F lies below the FB edge and a superlinear rho(T) when it lies above. The authors fit data on five intermetallics (Ni3In, CaRh2, Ni3Ge, Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb, CoSn) with a modified Bloch-Grüneisen formula (Eq. 1) that uses the DFT density of states. A cheap transport probe of FB position would be useful. However, the physics is essentially the Mott s-d scattering picture (Ref. 29) and the DOS-structure explanations of rho(T) curvature in A15 and transition-metal compounds, so the novelty lies in applying it to flat-band materials. Whether the signature is specific to flat bands remains to be shown.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A simple, physically clear mechanism that links a widely available measurement (rho(T) up to ~860 K) to the energy landscape near E_F. It is presented with clean model calculations (Figs. 2, 3), including the scaling with T/Delta in Fig. 3(e).
- New high-temperature data (1.5–862 K) on CoSn single crystals and on polycrystalline Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb. The low-temperature T^5 law in CoSn (Fig. 4(d)) is a clean result.
- Fits with one free prefactor reproduce the qualitative curvature across several material classes (Fig. 4(a)). The FB placement agrees qualitatively with the Sommerfeld coefficients (Fig. 4(c)).
- The proposed link to thermopower sign gives a further testable prediction.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The signature is not shown to be specific to flat bands, and competing explanations for rho(T) curvature are not excluded. The model's only input is a steep energy dependence of D(E) within a few k_BT of E_F (Figs. 1(c), 2, Eq. 1). Any transition-metal compound with E_F near a d-band edge has this feature: Pd, Pt, A15 compounds and Heusler alloys all show sublinear or superlinear rho(T) for exactly this reason. That is Mott's s-d scattering, which the authors themselves cite (Refs. 29, 45–47). Other mechanisms also curve rho(T) above room temperature:
   - resistivity saturation near the Ioffe-Regel limit (parallel-resistor behaviour). Ni3In and CaRh2 reach 100–160 microOhm cm (Fig. 4(a)), where saturation is generic;
   - thermal expansion and phonon softening or anharmonicity;
   - spin-fluctuation scattering, which is relevant for Ni3In, CaRh2, Pd- and Pt-based compounds;
   - the electron-electron scattering that the model explicitly omits (p. 2).

   The title and abstract claim a method for "detecting flat bands". To support that claim, the authors should:
   - (i) fit a parallel-resistor (saturation) model and a plain BG-plus-thermal-expansion model to the same data and compare goodness of fit with Eq. 1;
   - (ii) include a reference metal with a smooth D(E) near E_F, where Eq. 1 should reduce to BG;
   - (iii) include a metal with a steep but ordinary (non-flat) d-band edge, to show what extra information distinguishes a flat band;
   - (iv) narrow the claim to "steep DOS features near E_F" if (iii) cannot be distinguished.

2. The claim that the method can locate the FB is not validated independently. The paper presents only forward fits that use DFT DOS as input (Fig. 4(a)), plus qualitative agreement in sign with gamma (Fig. 4(c)). The inverse procedure is what the abstract and conclusion advertise: the fit formula of SM Eq. 6, used to "pinpoint the position of the FB" without DOS information (p. 3). Yet the main text never applies it to any material. It reports no extracted Delta, m_FB or uncertainties, and does not discuss parameter correlations (Delta against m_FB against C, and the choice of n and Theta_D). The authors should:
   - apply SM Eq. 6 to each compound, giving extracted Delta values with confidence intervals;
   - compare these values with ARPES and DFT FB positions where they exist (CoSn, Ni3In);
   - ideally test a prediction on a series in which E_F is tuned by doping (for example, electron or hole doped CoSn, or a Ni3In/Ni3Sn alloy) and show that the extracted Delta moves as expected.

   Without this, "one can infer the position of FBs" is an assertion.

3. The model assumptions behind Eq. 1 need to be stated and justified, because they control the sign of the effect.
   - Eq. 1 keeps only D_DFT^-1(E) inside the thermal average. It drops the energy dependence of the carrier velocity v^2(E) and of the carrier DOS of the conducting band, which enter sigma(E) = e^2 D(E) v^2(E) tau(E).
   - It assumes an energy- and momentum-independent electron-phonon matrix element for intraband and interband channels alike.
   - It uses the total DFT DOS, which includes the FB states themselves, as both the scattering phase space and (implicitly) not a conducting channel.
   - It is unclear whether the chemical potential is held fixed or allowed to shift with temperature. For the strongly asymmetric DOS in Fig. 2(b) and Fig. 4(b), mu(T) shifts appreciably at 300–900 K, which would change the predicted curvature.
   - It mixes the BG T-dependence with exponent n = 3 or 5 and an energy-resolved integral. The text does not say which n was used for which compound, nor whether Theta_D is fixed (and to what value) or fitted. The statement that C is "the only free parameter" therefore needs qualification.

   The authors should derive Eq. 1 from the two-band Boltzmann model in the main text or SM, list all fixed parameters with their sources per compound, and show that the conclusions survive including v(E) and a temperature-dependent mu.

B. Should address

4. Electron correlations in Ni3In undermine the purely electron-phonon interpretation. The authors note that Ni3In is a strange metal with n = 1 rho(T) at low T (p. 4, Ref. 23). In such a system the FB position and width are renormalized relative to GGA-PBE, and electron-electron scattering contributes at all temperatures. Ni3In nonetheless serves as the main sublinear example. The authors should discuss how correlation-induced FB renormalization (for example DFT+DMFT or the ARPES-measured FB position) would change the fit. They should also discuss why an e-ph-only fit across 1.5–300 K is meaningful when the low-T behaviour is explicitly non-phononic.

5. The DOS input is poor for at least one compound and its effect on the fit is not quantified. For CaRh2 the DOS comes from the Materials Project with "very coarse resolution", and this is the worst fit (p. 3). For CoSn, the DFT details (functional, SOC, k-mesh, smearing or tetrahedron method) are not given in the main text. Smearing directly controls the FB edge sharpness that drives the effect. The authors should recompute the CaRh2 DOS on a fine grid, show how the fits change with broadening, and list DFT settings for all compounds.

6. Anisotropy and sample form are not accounted for. CoSn is measured along c only (Fig. 4(d,e)), Ni3In data are taken in the ab plane, and Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb are polycrystals. In kagome metals the flat band arises from in-plane interference, and transport along c and ab can couple to it very differently. A scalar DOS model cannot capture this. The authors should show CoSn rho(T) along both directions, or explain why the c-axis curvature tests the in-plane FB. They should also comment on how grain boundaries and porosity affect the high-T curvature in the polycrystals.

7. The integrity of the high-temperature measurements is not documented. Data extend to 862 K. The main text does not describe the apparatus, atmosphere, contact geometry or heating and cooling protocol. At these temperatures, oxidation, vacancy annealing, Pb or Sn loss and contact degradation can bend rho(T). The authors should show heating and cooling curves overlaid, and rho at room temperature before and after the high-T run, for CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb. They should also state how many samples of each were measured.

8. The Sommerfeld coefficient of Pd3Pb looks implausible. gamma ≈ 0.03 mJ mol^-1 K^-2 (p. 4, Fig. 4(c), Fig. S2) is two orders of magnitude below a free-electron estimate for a four-atom intermetallic, which should be about 1 mJ mol^-1 K^-2 or more. Even for a semimetal this would need explanation. The authors should check the fit (phonon term, nuclear or impurity contributions, fit range, per-formula-unit normalization) and compare with the DFT D(E_F) of Pd3Pb. Fig. 4(c) also omits Pt3Sn and Ni3Ge, which are fitted in Fig. 4(a). Their gamma values should be added so that the correlation is tested on all compounds.

9. The onset criterion in Fig. 4(e) depends on the choice of reference. The claim that rho(T) of CoSn deviates from linear at about 400 K ≈ Delta_DFT/3 depends on the window used to define the dashed linear reference. Superlinearity above room temperature is also common from thermal expansion. The authors should:
   - state the fit window;
   - show the derivative drho/dT against T, which makes the onset visible without a reference line;
   - compare it with the predicted d^2rho/dT^2 from Eq. 1.

10. Agreement in Fig. 4(a) is judged by eye only. The fits for six compounds are overlaid on a crowded panel. Residuals (data minus fit) for each compound, a goodness-of-fit metric, and the fitted C (with implied lambda_ep) would let the reader judge "good agreement". The fitted C values could also be checked against independent lambda_ep estimates (for example from the low-T T^5 coefficient in CoSn), turning a free prefactor into a consistency test.

C. Minor

11. The text states that rho(T) was collected from 1.5 to 862 K for Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb and CoSn (p. 2). The CoSn data in Fig. 4(a) and 4(e) appear to end near 720 K. Please reconcile.

12. Fig. 3(c) reaches about 500 microOhm cm, well beyond the Ioffe-Regel limit, where the Boltzmann treatment fails. Note this limitation, or include saturation, so that the "semiconductor-like" maximum is not read as a robust prediction.

13. The quoted uncertainty in n = 4.99(2) for CoSn (Fig. 4(d)) is likely only the statistical fit error. Please state how n changes with the upper fit limit (for example 30, 40 and 55 K), and give rho_0 and RRR.

14. The Fig. 3 caption, "FB edge (a) and (c) above E_F and (b) and (d) below E_F", could be phrased in terms of the sign of E_F − E_edge to match the panel legends and Fig. 4(b,c) labels.

15. Ref. 30 is a placeholder URL. There are typos: "we do not included" (p. 2), and "Ca(Rh,Ru)2" in the Fig. 4(a) legend against "CaRh2" elsewhere. Please specify which composition the data and DOS refer to.

16. The Fig. 3 model curves are given in microOhm cm. State the parameters (C, Theta_D, band widths) that set the absolute scale.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add an independent transport check. The paper itself proposes thermopower: measure S(T) to high temperature on CoSn and Pd3Pb and compare its sign and magnitude with the same two-band model and DFT DOS. This would test the energy asymmetry that the resistivity argument relies on.
2. Present a short "how to use" demonstration: apply SM Eq. 6 blind to one compound, extract Delta and m_FB with uncertainties, then reveal the ARPES or DFT value.
3. Put the work in context with the classic literature on DOS-driven rho(T) curvature (Mott s-d scattering, A15 compounds, Pd and Pt), and state clearly what is new for flat bands.
4. Provide a figure or table, per compound, of all inputs (DOS source and settings, n, Theta_D, rho_0, fitted C, fit range).
