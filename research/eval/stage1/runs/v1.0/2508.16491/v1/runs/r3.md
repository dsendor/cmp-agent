Review outline:

Note on scope: the PDF I was given has the six-page main text only. The Supplemental Material [30] it cites (model details, Eq. 6, Figs. S1 and S2) was not included, so I judge it only through what the main text says about it.

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript proposes that the shape of the high-temperature resistivity, sublinear or superlinear relative to Bloch-Grüneisen (BG), shows whether a flat-band (FB) edge lies above or below E_F and roughly how far away it is. The mechanism is phonon-mediated interband scattering into a large, rapidly varying density of states. To support this, the authors pair a Mott-type two-parabolic-band Boltzmann model with BG fits that use the DFT DOS for five intermetallics (Ni3In, CaRh2, Ni3Ge, Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb) and new data on CoSn up to 862 K. A cheap transport-based diagnostic for FB proximity would be useful. However, the physics is essentially the classic Mott s–d picture, and closely related energy-dependent-scattering analyses already exist from the same group (Refs. 45–48, 33, 43). The novelty therefore lies mostly in applying the idea to flat-band compounds and in the claim that it can locate the FB. That claim is the part least supported by the evidence shown.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A physically transparent, low-cost idea. Fermi's golden rule makes τ_ep^-1 follow D(E), and thermal broadening of −∂f/∂E then produces sub- or superlinear ρ(T). This links a routine measurement to a topical question.
- New high-temperature data (1.5–862 K) on CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb, plus specific-heat data on Pd3Pb, add to the literature data in a consistent comparison (Fig. 4a).
- The model calculations (Figs. 2, 3) are clearly presented. The scaling plot in Fig. 3(e), with ρ versus T/Δ, gives an intuitive energy scale.
- The qualitative correlation is suggestive. Compounds with large γ and an FB near or above E_F (Ni3In, CaRh2) show sublinear ρ(T); those with small γ (CoSn, Pd3Pb) show linear or superlinear ρ(T) (Figs. 4a–c).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Competing explanations for sublinear ρ(T) are not ruled out. The central inference is "sublinear ρ(T) at high T ⇒ FB edge above E_F" (abstract; Fig. 3a; Fig. 4a). Sublinear high-T resistivity is generic in transition-metal compounds, and other well-known causes are not discussed:
   - resistivity saturation as the mean free path approaches the Mott–Ioffe–Regel limit (the parallel-resistor form). Ni3In and CaRh2 reach about 120–160 µΩ cm, exactly where saturation is usually seen;
   - a temperature-dependent chemical potential;
   - thermal expansion and phonon softening, including the constant-pressure versus constant-volume difference;
   - spin-fluctuation scattering. Ni3In is a strange metal near magnetic order, and Ni- and Co-based compounds often carry paramagnon contributions;
   - electron–electron scattering, which the model explicitly omits (p. 2).
   
   The two compounds that show sublinearity are also the ones with the largest ρ, so the correlation in Fig. 4(a) cannot tell the FB mechanism apart from saturation. The authors should:
   - fit the same data with a parallel-resistor or saturation model, and with Eq. 1 plus a saturation term, and compare the fit quality and the physical plausibility of the parameters;
   - estimate k_F·l at the highest temperatures for each compound;
   - ideally, show a low-resistivity compound whose FB edge lies above E_F, or a sublinear compound with no FB near E_F, to show that sublinearity tracks the FB rather than the magnitude of ρ.

2. The claim to locate the FB is not tested quantitatively. The title and abstract say one can "infer the position of FBs near E_F" and "track" them. In practice, Fig. 4(a) uses the DFT DOS as input and fits only a prefactor C. That shows consistency with DFT, not an independent determination of E_F − E_edge. The only extraction of a position is the CoSn argument in Fig. 4(e), where a visually chosen departure from linearity near 400 K is equated with Δ_DFT/3. Fig. 3(e) shows that factor 1/3 for one parameter set only (m_FB = 10 m0, Δ = 100 meV). The authors should:
   - treat a rigid shift of E_F relative to the DFT DOS as a fit parameter, or apply the SM Eq. 6 fit formula to every compound;
   - report the extracted E_F − E_edge (and m_FB) with uncertainties, and show how χ² varies with the shift;
   - compare these values with independent ARPES results (Refs. 23, 32, 38, 39 for Ni3In, CaRh2 and CoSn) and with γ;
   - show how the "Δ/3" onset criterion depends on m_FB, on the FB bandwidth and DOS shape, and on n.
   
   Without this, the claimed method has not been shown to work beyond checking DFT.

3. The model's construction needs justification:
   - Chemical potential. With a narrow, high-DOS band within about 20–100 meV of E_F and k_BT up to about 75 meV, μ(T) shifts substantially when particle number is conserved. That shift alone produces strong sub- or superlinear ρ(T). Neither Figs. 2–3 nor Eq. 1 say whether μ is fixed or computed self-consistently. The authors should state this, and recompute Fig. 3 and the Fig. 4(a) fits with μ(T) determined from the DOS.
   - Conducting carriers. In Eq. 1, ρ^-1 ∝ ∫ D^-1 (−∂f/∂E) dE. This implicitly assumes that σ(E) is set only by τ(E) ∝ 1/D_total(E), with the carrier velocity and DOS constant. That holds only if the carriers live entirely in the dispersive band while D_DFT is the total DOS, the "s–d" assumption. For real compounds, the FB states also carry current (with small v), and v²(E) varies strongly through the same energy window. The authors should state this assumption and justify using the total DFT DOS rather than, for example, the DOS of the flat orbital character.
   - Quasi-elastic approximation. The scattering phase space is evaluated at D(E). For Δ comparable to the phonon energies (k_BΘ_D ≈ 20–40 meV against Δ of 20–100 meV), the relevant quantity is D(E ± ħω). This should be discussed or included.

4. The "single-parameter fit" statement (p. 3) is not accurate as written. Eq. 1 also contains Θ_D, the exponent n (3 or 5), and presumably the residual resistivity ρ0. The text does not say which n was used for each compound, where each Θ_D came from (fixed from specific heat or fitted), or how ρ0 was handled. For a polycrystal, the geometric-factor uncertainty and grain-boundary contributions further loosen the absolute scale. Since the evidence for the mechanism is "good agreement" in Fig. 4(a), the authors should:
   - list for each compound every parameter and whether it is fixed (with source) or free, plus the fit range;
   - show the BG fit without the DOS factor and with the same freedom, as a null comparison, with residuals. The improvement attributable to D_DFT(E) can then be judged.
   
   Ni3In needs particular care. The text concedes that ρ_ab(T) is linear in T down to the lowest temperatures (strange-metal behaviour, p. 4), which BG with n = 3 or 5 cannot reproduce at low T. The fit range and the low-T mismatch should be shown explicitly.

B. Should address

5. The DFT input is taken as fixed and reliable. FB positions in d-electron kagome and pyrochlore metals are known to be renormalized by correlations; for Ni3In, ARPES and DMFT place the FB differently from GGA. The DOS is also used unchanged up to 862 K, ignoring thermal expansion and band broadening. The authors themselves attribute the poor CaRh2 fit to the coarse Materials Project DOS grid (p. 3). These points make the agreement in Fig. 4(a) sensitive to inputs that are not controlled. The authors should:
   - recompute the CaRh2 DOS on a fine grid rather than speculating that it "would most likely" improve;
   - give the k-mesh and smearing for all DOS used, and the spin–orbit setting (important for Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb);
   - show how sensitive the fits are to DOS smearing and to small rigid shifts.

6. The data on the new samples are thinly documented. CoSn single crystals, and Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb polycrystals, were measured to 862 K, but the text gives no number of samples, geometry, contact method, atmosphere, or heating/cooling cycles. Sn and Pb are volatile and oxidizable, and high-temperature runs can change stoichiometry or contacts. Since the claimed signatures are subtle curvature changes above 400 K (Fig. 4e), the authors should:
   - show heating and cooling curves, and a repeat low-T measurement after the high-T run;
   - say whether the curvature appears in more than one sample;
   - state the stoichiometry and phase purity (XRD/EDX) of the polycrystals.

7. The CoSn transport is anisotropic, but the model has a scalar DOS. CoSn was measured along c, and the kagome FB is quasi-two-dimensional. Interband scattering into the FB affects c-axis and in-plane conductivity differently through v_z² weighting. The Ni3In data used are in-plane. The authors should discuss this, and ideally add an in-plane ρ(T) for CoSn to test whether the same Δ/3 onset appears.

8. The specific-heat comparison in Fig. 4(c) is qualitative and incomplete:
   - It contains four compounds; Ni3Ge and Pt3Sn are missing although both are in Fig. 4(a).
   - γ includes electron–phonon and correlation mass enhancement, and is set by D(E_F) rather than by the position of the edge.
   - The value γ ≈ 0.03 mJ mol^-1 K^-2 for Pd3Pb (p. 4) is about two orders of magnitude below typical metallic values per formula unit, and needs checking for units or normalization. It would imply an almost vanishing DOS, which should be compared with the Pd3Pb DFT DOS.
   
   The authors should add the missing compounds, compare the measured γ with the bare DFT γ to get λ, and confirm the Pd3Pb value.

9. "Universal" is overstated (abstract; p. 1 "holds truly universally"). The evidence is six intermetallics, two with clear sublinearity and two from literature fits, and no counterexample was sought. The claimed T/Δ ≈ 1/3 scale was derived for one m_FB and one Δ. The wording should be narrowed, or the parameter dependence demonstrated (see concern 2).

10. Relation to prior work needs to be clearer. The two-parabolic-band interband model and the energy-dependent-τ approach to ρ(T) appear in the authors' Refs. 45–48. The Ni3In and Ni3Ge ρ(T) analyses in Refs. 33 and 43 used first-principles electron–phonon (Phoebe) calculations. The manuscript should state precisely what is new relative to these: for example, the explicit FB-position diagnostic and the new CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb data. It should also explain how Eq. 1 compares quantitatively with the ab initio RTA results for the same compounds.

11. Pt3Sn and Ni3Ge receive no analysis beyond the fit. Fig. 4(a) includes them, but the text gives neither their DFT DOS, the sign of E_F − E_edge, nor whether their curvature matches the prediction. Each compound should be classified explicitly: the predicted curvature from DFT against the observed curvature. This is the actual test of the method.

12. The thermopower proposal is unsupported by data. The conclusion proposes the sign of S as a discriminator of whether the FB lies above or below E_F, but no S(T) is shown. The two-band model already makes a quantitative S(T) prediction. Measuring S(T) on CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb (already in hand) would give an independent check on the inferred edge positions, and would address concern 1, since saturation does not produce the same S signature.

C. Minor

13. Fig. 3 caption: "(a) and (c) above E_F and (b) and (d) below E_F" refers to the position of the FB edge relative to E_F. The panel legends give E_F − E_edge instead. Please state this unambiguously so readers do not take it as the position of E_F.

14. Figs. 2–3 give σ(E) in arbitrary units, yet Fig. 3 reports ρ in µΩ cm (up to about 500 µΩ cm in Fig. 3c). State how the absolute scale and C were fixed in the model calculations. Also state the model's other parameters: the dispersive-band bottom, the FB bandwidth, Θ_D, and n.

15. Fig. 4(d): the quoted n = 4.99(2) with r² = 0.991 suggests an uncertainty that the visible scatter does not support. State whether ρ0 was fixed or fitted, how sensitive n is to the fit window (1.5–55 K), and what Θ_D is implied. Agreement with n = 5 requires Θ_D well above 55 K, which is worth stating.

16. Fig. 4(e): give the fit range of the dashed linear line and an uncertainty for the 400 K departure point. The arrow currently relies on visual judgement.

17. Fig. 4(b): mark the FB edge positions numerically in the text, for example "≈ +0.03 eV for Ni3In, ≈ −0.10 eV for CoSn", consistent with the "more than 0.1 eV below E_F" wording on p. 4. Also state the DFT code and settings for the CoSn DOS calculated in this work.

18. Fig. 4(a): the legend gives "Ca(Rh,Ru)2", while the text says CaRh2. Clarify which composition the data and the DFT DOS refer to.

19. Several references are incomplete (for example, Ref. 9 has no volume number; Ref. 30 is a placeholder URL). The SM figures S1 and S2 are cited for key statements; please make sure they are included and self-contained.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a benchmark table. For each compound, list: DFT E_F − E_edge; E_F − E_edge (and m_FB) extracted from ρ(T) via SM Eq. 6 with uncertainties; ARPES value where available; measured γ against DFT γ; and observed curvature. This would turn the qualitative picture into a test of the method.
2. Fit all high-T data with competing models (saturation/parallel resistor, constant-DOS BG, BG plus T² electron–electron scattering) using the same number of free parameters, and report the residuals. Show that the DOS-weighted model is preferred specifically for the FB compounds.
3. Recompute the model curves with a self-consistent μ(T) and with inelastic D(E ± ħω) phase space. Map the onset T/Δ over a grid of m_FB and Δ to establish, or qualify, the "Δ/3" rule.
4. Add an experimental control in which E_F is moved relative to the FB within one material family, for example a doping or substitution series such as Co1−xFexSn or In-site substitution in Ni3In. The predicted change from superlinear to sublinear curvature, together with the change in γ and in the sign of S, would be the most convincing demonstration that ρ(T) tracks the flat band.
