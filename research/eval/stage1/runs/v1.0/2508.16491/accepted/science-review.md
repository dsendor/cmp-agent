A. Must address

1. The signature is not shown to be specific to flat bands, and no null control is presented. The abstract and conclusion present sublinear (or superlinear) high-T ρ(T) as "a clear and universal fingerprint of the proximity of a FB to E_F". By the paper's own model (τ ∝ 1/D(E), thermal smearing across a steep DOS feature; Methods, Eqs. 5-7; Fig. S1), the only requirement is a sharp DOS structure near E_F. A d-band edge (Pd and Pt, the classic Mott/Wilson s-d cases, ref. [33]; also Ni, V, Nb), a van Hove singularity or a pseudogap would do the same. Sublinear or saturating ρ(T) is textbook behaviour in A15 compounds such as Nb3Sn and V3Si, in Heusler compounds and in skutterudites (including refs. 53-57 by the same group). Every material in Figs. 4-5 was chosen because it is already known or predicted to host a flat band. No compound with a steep DOS feature but no flat band is shown, and there is no blind case where the diagnosis was made before the band position was known. The authors should apply the same analysis (curvature criterion, Eq. 1 fit, T ≈ Δ/3 onset) to at least one reference metal with a sharp non-flat-band DOS feature near E_F and to one with a featureless DOS, ideally isostructural and of similar resistivity and measured in the same setups (Ni3Sn partly serves; an explicit no-FB Cu3Au-type or CoSn-type reference would be better). They should show what the method returns for each and state what distinguishes a flat band from a generic DOS edge in their criterion. Otherwise "flat band" should be reworded to "steep DOS feature near E_F" wherever the evidence goes no further.

2. Other explanations for the high-T curvature are dismissed qualitatively (one paragraph, p. 6) but not excluded quantitatively. (i) Resistivity saturation: the paper says the compounds are "good metals (well below the MIR limit)" but gives no numbers. The bending in Ni3In, Ca(Rh,Ru)2 and Ni3Ge sets in at 40-100 µΩ cm (Fig. 5a). Ni3In reaches about 110-120 µΩ cm at 300 K in Fig. 5a, polycrystalline Ni3In in Fig. 4a keeps rising to about 75-80 µΩ cm above ρ0 at 860 K, and CaRh2 reaches about 160 µΩ cm at 860 K. These values are comparable to typical ρ_sat of 100-200 µΩ cm in transition-metal compounds, where the parallel-resistor form 1/ρ = 1/ρ_ideal + 1/ρ_sat (Wiesmann) produces exactly this sublinearity. (ii) Spin-fluctuation scattering, not mentioned at all: Ni3In is a near-ferromagnetic strange metal and Ni3Ge belongs to the nearly-ferromagnetic Ni3X family. Paramagnon scattering gives ρ(T) crossing over from T² through linear to saturating, which mimics the sublinear signature. (iii) Thermal expansion and anharmonic phonon softening at constant pressure up to 860 K change Θ_D, bandwidth and electron-phonon coupling. They give curvature of a few per cent, comparable to the CoSn deviation in Fig. 5e. (iv) Also undiscussed or treated only qualitatively: temperature-dependent shifts of the chemical potential when a large DOS feature lies near E_F, Debye-Waller effects, thermally activated carriers in semimetals, and Kondo coherence. The authors should estimate ρ_sat and the mean free path at the highest temperature for each compound, using DFT Fermi velocities and plasma frequencies and the interatomic spacing. They should fit the Fig. 5a data with competing forms, at least a standard BG law with free Θ_D plus a parallel-resistor saturation term and a spin-fluctuation form, with parameter counts matched to Eq. 1, and report χ² or residuals side by side. They should also address spin fluctuations with susceptibility data or an argument from the spin-fluctuation temperature, and comment on thermal-expansion corrections to Θ_D. Until these alternatives are shown to fail where Eq. 1 succeeds, the agreement in Fig. 5a does not single out interband scattering.

3. The paper never shows that ρ(T) alone can locate the flat band; the fits take the FB position from DFT. The title and abstract say the method "tracks" and "locates" FBs and gives |E_F − E_edge|. Methods (Eq. 7) says fitting ρ(T) yields E_F − E_edge and m₂^{3/2}/D₁ without DFT input, and p. 6 calls this "particularly useful when the DFT-calculated DOS is unavailable or unreliable". Yet every fit in Fig. 5a uses Eq. (1) with the DFT DOS fixed (presumably at the DFT E_F), with only the prefactor C free. The FB position is an input, not an output, so the fits test consistency with DFT rather than locating anything. This is circular for the paper's main selling point. Eq. 7 is never applied to any measured dataset, and no extracted E_F − E_edge with uncertainty is reported. The problem is sharper because DFT-PBE is known to misplace kagome flat bands by tens of meV, the same scale as the |E_F − E_edge| the method is meant to resolve, and Fig. 5b (DFT DOS) anchors the interpretation. The authors should (i) fit each compound (Ni3In1−xSnx, CoSn, Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb and the rest) with Eq. 7, or Eq. 1 with E_F as a free rigid shift of D_DFT, and tabulate E_F − E_edge with confidence intervals and parameter correlations, showing whether E_F − E_edge, the mass ratio and C can be identified separately from a smooth ρ(T) curve; (ii) show χ² against the rigid shift to demonstrate how sharply the data constrain the FB position; (iii) compare the results blind with independent determinations: ARPES for CoSn (refs. 29, 30, 45) and the composition at which the edge crosses E_F in Ni3In1−xSnx; and (iv) compare the DFT FB positions with ARPES or state their expected error. A blind prediction for a compound whose FB position is not yet known would show the screening value directly.

4. The most direct test of the high-T fingerprint, ρ(T) to 860 K for the intermediate Ni3In1−xSnx compositions, is not shown. The model's main predictions concern the high-T curvature, its onset at T ≈ Δ/3 (Fig. 3e), and how both evolve as |E_F − E_edge| changes. Yet Fig. 4b shows the substitution series only from 2 to 100 K, and Fig. 4a shows 2-860 K data only for the end members x = 0 and x = 1. Using the slope at 100 K as a proxy for σ(E) supports the energy dependence of τ but does not test the proposed high-T curvature criterion. The authors should show ρ(T) up to 860 K for all x (at least x = 0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6). They should show that the curvature changes sign where the edge crosses E_F (x ≈ 0.5), and plot the deviation from BG against T/Δ for all x, with Δ from the alloy-DFT mapping, to test the predicted collapse and the Δ/3 onset directly.

B. Should address

5. The Eq. (1) fits in Fig. 5a are underspecified, so the "single free parameter" description and the claimed "good agreement with all the FB materials" cannot be judged. Eq. (1) contains C, Θ_D, n and the DOS, and Fig. 5a plots total ρ with non-zero intercepts (e.g. ρ0 ≈ 10 µΩ cm for Ni3In), so ρ0 must also have been fixed or fitted. Not reported for each compound: Θ_D and its source (Methods says only "e.g. from specific heat"), the exponent n and the basis for choosing it, ρ0, the fit temperature window, how the DFT DOS was broadened, which DFT DOS was used, the fitted C, and residuals. The text says n = 5 for intraband and n = 3 for interband scattering, yet CoSn shows a low-T exponent of 4.99 (Fig. 5d). Which n is used for CoSn in Fig. 5a, and does the choice change the high-T curvature? The "single free parameter" count also depends on fixing n. Several curves (CoSn, Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb, Ni3Ge) are nearly linear, so a one-parameter fit will look good with almost any DOS. The authors should give a table of all inputs and fitted values with uncertainties, show residual plots, repeat the fits with n and Θ_D varied within their uncertainties, and show that a standard BG fit with the same free parameters (plus Θ_D) fits noticeably worse.

6. The experimental reconstruction of σ(E) in Fig. 4c rests on assumptions that are not justified or stated. (i) Taking (ρ − ρ0)⁻¹ as the intrinsic electron-phonon σ assumes Matthiessen's rule in a concentrated, disordered solid solution, where deviations are largest when ρ0 and the DOS vary strongly. (ii) Changing x also changes Θ_D, the phonon spectra and the electron-phonon coupling, not only E_F. At 100 K (k_BT ≈ 9 meV, inside the non-linear BG regime), (ρ − ρ0) is very sensitive to Θ_D(x). (iii) The experimental points are a thermal average over roughly ±4k_BT ≈ ±35 meV at 100 K but are compared with T = 0 σ(E) curves, and the step being resolved is about 50 meV wide. Disorder broadening in the alloy smears the edge further. (iv) The points are in "arb. u." and their scaling relative to the three model curves (and the plateau normalisation of the model curves) is not stated, so the visual agreement may include a free scale factor. (v) The x → E mapping uses "alloy-averaged DFT" (ref. 39) without saying whether it is VCA, CPA or supercells, and with no uncertainty. (vi) Geometric-factor errors of 10-20 % across polycrystalline samples (porosity, microcracks, contact spacing, grain boundaries, polycrystalline averaging of strongly anisotropic Ni3In) feed directly into (ρ − ρ0)⁻¹. The authors should state the normalisation, describe the mapping, and add horizontal and vertical error bars including geometric-factor uncertainty. They should show Θ_D(x), e.g. from specific heat, or repeat the reconstruction at other reference temperatures (200 K, 300 K, or the linear regime). They should also compare with model σ convolved with −∂f/∂E at the reference temperature. The claim that the data follow D⁻¹ rather than the D⁻² of electron-electron scattering (p. 4) needs a quantitative test, such as the ratio of the high and low σ plateaus against the DOS ratio from Fig. 5b, or log σ_exp against log D_DFT across x, with uncertainties.

8. The CoSn inference of a FB about 0.1 eV below E_F (Fig. 5e) rests on a small deviation from a hand-drawn linear reference and on a Δ/3 factor of unknown generality. The fit range of the dashed line (apparently about 200-400 K) and the criterion for "deviation" (e.g. a residual threshold) are not given, and the size of the superlinear excess depends on them. The factor Δ/3 comes from one model setting (m_FB = 10 m0, parabolic bands; Fig. 3e). It depends on m_FB, n_CB and, per the finite-bandwidth results in Fig. S1, on the bandwidth W, so it is not universal. How the "≈1/3" positions in Fig. 3e were determined (e.g. a fixed percentage deviation from BG) is not defined. Anharmonic or thermal-expansion contributions would give similar superlinear curvature (Concern 2). 9. The physical approximations in the 2PB model are not justified for the parameter range used. (i) Quasi-elastic scattering: τ(E) ∝ 1/D(E) at the carrier energy ignores the phonon energy. With Θ_D = 400 K (k_BΘ_D ≈ 34 meV) and Δ = 20-100 meV (Fig. 3), interband transitions sample D(E ± ħω) and smear the edge on a scale comparable to Δ. (ii) Chemical potential: with a large, asymmetric DOS at the edge (m_FB = 100 m0), μ can shift by tens of meV by 1000 K at fixed carrier number. Methods do not say whether μ or N is held fixed, and this bears directly on the Δ/3 onset and the non-metallic hump in Fig. 3c. (iii) Factorisation of the T dependence into a BG term times an energy integral (Eqs. 5-7) is exact only if the phonon-occupation factor is independent of electron energy across the window. (iv) The model uses n = 3 and Θ_D = 400 K for all of Fig. 3, although the Wilson T³ law holds only at low T. The authors should evaluate Eq. 3 with the full inelastic kernel and with N conserved for one parameter set from Fig. 3, show that the sub/superlinear distinction survives n = 5 and other Θ_D, and explain how the absolute scale of ρ in Fig. 3 was fixed.

10. The high-temperature measurements up to 860 K lack controls for sample changes and for joining the two setups. Low-T data (helium bath, spot-welded wires) and high-T data (ZEM3, He atmosphere) come from different setups. The paper does not say how the segments were matched (same bar, same contacts, any rescaling or offset near 300 K) or show the overlap region. Heating polycrystalline intermetallics and Sn-flux-grown CoSn to 860 K can cause annealing, ordering changes, Sn/Pb loss, oxidation or contact degradation. CoSn from Sn flux may carry Sn inclusions, which melt at 505 K, inside the high-T range. Because the key observable is the slope and curvature of ρ(T), these artefacts matter directly. The authors should show heating and cooling curves, a repeat low-T measurement (ρ0 before and after) following the high-T run, and a check for kinks near 505 K in CoSn. Low-T data were taken while the sample warmed spontaneously, so the thermometry lag should be stated.

14. The specific-heat support in Fig. 5c needs checking and does not discriminate as presented. The value γ ≈ 0.03 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² for Pd3Pb (p. 6) is about two orders of magnitude below typical metals, below Cu per atom and below a free-electron estimate for a Pd-based metal. It would correspond to a DOS of order 0.01 states eV⁻¹ f.u.⁻¹, and the Pd3Pb bar in Fig. 5c appears larger than 0.03 on the plotted scale. If correct, Pd3Pb is effectively a semimetal, which weakens its use as a "good metal" test of the electron-phonon picture and brings in thermally activated carriers as an alternative cause of curvature. γ values for compounds with 2, 3 and 4 atoms per formula unit (CoSn, CaRh2, Ni3In) are compared without stating whether they are per mole of formula units or of atoms. γ also includes electron-phonon, correlation and spin-fluctuation mass enhancement (e.g. Ni3In, γ ≈ 51 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻²), so it is not a bare DOS measure, and values from different sources are not compared with DFT. The authors should confirm the Pd3Pb value and units (fit range, Schottky or nuclear contributions), show the C/T versus T² data and fit (Fig. S4), state units per atom, and compare each γ with the DFT D(E_F) (bare γ_band) to separate mass renormalisation from DOS.

15. Transport anisotropy is not addressed. Ni3In and CoSn are quasi-2D kagome systems with strongly anisotropic velocities, but the isotropic 2PB model is compared with polycrystalline (Ni3In1−xSnx) and c-axis-only (CoSn) data, and the consequences of mixing directions are not discussed. The kagome flat bands are mainly in-plane. The authors should discuss this and measure CoSn along ab. That would also test the mechanism's prediction (phase space, not velocities) that the curvature and its onset do not depend on direction.
