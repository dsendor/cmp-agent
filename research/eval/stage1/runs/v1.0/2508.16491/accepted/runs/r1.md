Review outline:

Note on scope: the PDF reviewed (12 pages: main text, Methods, figure captions) refers repeatedly to Supplementary Figs. S1 to S4 (finite-bandwidth model, ab initio ρ(T) of Ni3In, CoSn high-temperature data, specific heat). These are not included in the file. Where a concern may already be answered there, I say so.

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript argues that the curvature of ρ(T) at elevated temperatures (sublinear when E_F lies inside a flat band, superlinear when it lies just outside) is a general transport signature of a nearby flat band (FB). The mechanism is phonon-mediated interband scattering, with τ⁻¹(E) ∝ D(E). The authors back this with a two-parabolic-band Boltzmann model, a Ni3In1−xSnx substitution series, an ab initio electron-phonon calculation for Ni3In, and a modified Bloch-Grüneisen (BG) formula fitted to six intermetallics. The physics itself is Mott's s-d interband scattering picture applied to modern kagome, pyrochlore and Cu3Au-type FB compounds. The new part is the framing as a quick screening tool and the experimental reconstruction of σ(E) across the Ni3In-Ni3Sn series. Whether the signature is specific enough to locate flat bands, as opposed to any steep DOS feature, is the central question the paper leaves open.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A transparent, physically sensible mechanism that ties high-temperature ρ(T) curvature to the energy dependence of the scattering phase space. It is a useful counterpoint to purely electron-electron explanations of anomalous transport in FB metals.
- The Ni3In1−xSnx series (Fig. 4b,c) is a good experimental design. Tuning E_F through the FB edge within one solid solution and reconstructing a step in σ(E) is the most direct evidence in the paper.
- The two-band model's predictions are robust across three levels of description (2PB model, τ ∝ 1/D_DFT and first-principles BTE-RTA, Fig. 4c). This supports the claim that the phase space, rather than the matrix elements, dominates.
- New high-temperature (to 860 K) data on CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb extend the empirical base.
- The phenomenology is simple enough to be widely used if its specificity is established.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The signature is not shown to be unique to flat bands, and no null control is presented. The headline claim (abstract: "a clear and universal fingerprint of the proximity of a FB to E_F") requires that sublinear high-T ρ(T) does not occur commonly without a FB. By the authors' own model, any sharp rise in D(E) near E_F produces it: the d-band edge in Pd, Pt, Ni, V and Nb, A15 compounds such as Nb3Sn and V3Si, and Heusler alloys. Sublinear or saturating ρ(T) is the textbook behaviour of these materials (Mott's s-d model, ref. [33], and the A15 literature). Several other mechanisms also produce it: resistivity saturation (parallel-resistor behaviour), thermal expansion and anharmonic phonon softening, thermally activated carriers in semimetals, and Kondo coherence. The paper discusses these only in one paragraph (p. 6), dismissing them qualitatively. The authors should (i) show the same analysis on at least one reference metal with a steep DOS but no flat band, and on one with a smooth DOS (e.g., a simple sp metal or a broad-band transition-metal compound) measured in the same setups, and state what distinguishes a "flat band" from a generic DOS edge in their criterion. (ii) Fit the Fig. 5a data with competing forms, at least a standard BG law with free Θ_D plus a parallel-resistor (Wiesmann) saturation term, and report goodness-of-fit and parameter counts side by side with Eq. (1). Without this the method locates "a steep DOS feature near E_F", which is a narrower claim than the title and abstract make.

2. The method's stated purpose, locating the FB from ρ(T), is never tested quantitatively against an independent measurement. Methods (Eq. 7) claims that fitting ρ(T) gives E_F − E_edge and m₂^{3/2}/D₁ without DFT input, and p. 6 calls this "particularly useful when the DFT-calculated DOS is unavailable or unreliable". Yet no material is fitted with Eq. 7. No extracted E_F − E_edge values with uncertainties are reported, and none are compared with ARPES or quantum-oscillation positions. CoSn is an ideal test case, since its FB has been located by ARPES (refs. [29,30]). Instead, Fig. 5a uses Eq. (1) with the DFT DOS fixed, so the FB position is an input rather than an output. The authors should (i) apply Eq. 7 (or Eq. 1 with E_F as a free rigid shift of D_DFT) to each compound and tabulate the fitted E_F − E_edge with confidence intervals. (ii) Compare these with ARPES for CoSn and with the substitution-series position for Ni3In. (iii) Show how χ² varies with a rigid shift of E_F relative to D_DFT, to show the data actually constrain the FB position. This matters because GGA-PBE is known to misplace kagome flat bands by tens of meV, the same scale as the |E_F − E_edge| the method is meant to resolve.

3. The "single free parameter" description of the Eq. (1) fits is not accurate as presented, and no fit details are given. Eq. (1) contains C, Θ_D, n and the DOS, and the plotted data in Fig. 5a are total ρ (non-zero intercepts, e.g. ρ₀ ≈ 10 µΩ cm for Ni3In), so ρ₀ must also have been set or fitted. The manuscript does not report, per compound: the Θ_D used and its source, whether n = 3 or 5 was chosen and on what basis, ρ₀, the fitted C, the fit temperature range, or residuals. The text says n is 5 for intraband and 3 for interband scattering, yet the CoSn low-T exponent is 4.99 (Fig. 5d). So which n is used for CoSn in Fig. 5a, and does the choice change the high-T curvature? A table of all inputs and fitted values, residual plots, and fits with n and Θ_D varied within their uncertainties are needed before "good agreement with all the FB materials" can be judged. Several curves (Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb, Ni3Ge) are nearly linear, so agreement with them discriminates little.

4. The experimental reconstruction of σ(E) in Fig. 4c rests on assumptions that are not justified. (i) Taking (ρ − ρ₀)⁻¹ as the intrinsic σ assumes Matthiessen's rule in a concentrated solid solution. Deviations from Matthiessen's rule are large exactly in alloys with strong disorder scattering and a steep DOS. (ii) Changing x also changes Θ_D, phonon spectra and electron-phonon coupling, not only E_F. At 100 K, which is well inside the non-linear BG regime, (ρ − ρ₀) is very sensitive to Θ_D. (iii) The experimental points are a thermal average over roughly ±4k_BT ≈ ±35 meV at 100 K, but they are compared with T = 0 σ(E) curves, and the step being resolved is about 50 meV wide. (iv) The y-axis is in arbitrary units, and the scaling of the experimental points relative to the three model curves is not stated. (v) The x → E mapping uses "alloy-averaged DFT" (VCA? CPA? supercells?) that is not described, and its uncertainty is not shown as horizontal error bars. The authors should state the normalisation, show Θ_D(x) (e.g. from specific heat) or repeat the reconstruction at a temperature where ρ is in the linear regime, add horizontal and vertical error bars (including geometric-factor uncertainty for polycrystals), and compare with model σ convolved with −∂f/∂E at 100 K. The statement that the data follow D⁻¹ rather than D⁻² (p. 4) needs a quantitative test: the ratio of the high and low plateaus in σ against the DOS ratio from Fig. 5b, with uncertainties.

B. Should address

5. Sample characterisation and reproducibility are not shown. Phase purity (XRD, metallography/EDX) is not presented for Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb, CoSn or the Ni3In1−xSnx series; the only check mentioned is mass loss below 0.05 %. Each composition appears to be one sample, with no sample-to-sample spread. For the alloy series, the actual (measured) compositions should be given. The x = 0.4 and 0.6 samples were measured by a different person (author contributions), possibly in a different setup, yet these straddle the claimed E_F = E_edge crossing at x ≈ 0.5.

6. The high-temperature measurements up to 860 K need controls for sample changes and for joining the two setups. Low-T (helium bath, spot-welded wires) and high-T (ZEM3, He atmosphere) data come from different setups. How were the two segments matched: same bar, same contact geometry, any rescaling at overlap? Is the overlap region shown? Heating polycrystalline intermetallics and Sn-flux-grown CoSn to 860 K can cause annealing, ordering changes, oxidation or evaporation. CoSn crystals from Sn flux may carry Sn inclusions, which melt at 505 K, inside the high-T range. Heating and cooling curves, a repeat low-T measurement after the high-T run, and a check for kinks near 505 K in CoSn should be shown. ρ₀ before and after cycling would be a simple indicator.

7. The saturation (MIR) argument is asserted, not quantified. The paper states that the compounds are "good metals (well below the MIR limit)" (p. 6), but Ni3In reaches about 110 to 120 µΩ cm at 300 K (Fig. 5a) and CaRh2 about 160 µΩ cm at 860 K. These are comparable to typical saturation values of 100 to 200 µΩ cm in transition-metal compounds. The authors should estimate ρ_sat for each compound (carrier density and Fermi velocity from DFT, interatomic spacing) and the mean free path at the highest temperature. Relatedly, the 2PB model in Fig. 3c gives ρ up to 500 µΩ cm, far beyond where Boltzmann theory applies. That panel should be flagged as outside the model's range of validity rather than presented as a prediction of semiconductor-like ρ(T).

8. The independence from microscopic details is argued from one calculation. The abstract says the characteristic emerges "independently of microscopic details, such as the electron-phonon coupling strength, as confirmed by comparison with ab initio electron-phonon transport calculations". The ab initio calculation is shown only for Ni3In (Fig. 4c; Fig. S2 and Ni3Ge from ref. [36]). In Fig. 4c the BTE-RTA σ(E) oscillates strongly below the edge, and its step is less sharp than the 2PB curve. A quantitative statement is needed, for example the extracted step height and width from each approach, and ideally a BTE-RTA calculation for CoSn, where the FB sits below E_F and the predicted sign of curvature is opposite. The assumption that intra- and interband matrix elements are comparable (Methods, below Eq. 4) should be checked against the ab initio matrix elements in Ni3In.

9. The quantitative criterion T ≈ Δ/3 is used to infer a FB about 0.1 eV below E_F in CoSn (Fig. 5e), but the onset of deviation from linearity is not defined. The dashed line's fit range and the criterion for "deviation" (e.g. a threshold in residual) are not given. The same inference would follow from anharmonic or thermal-expansion contributions, which also give superlinear high-T ρ(T). An error bar on the onset temperature, and therefore on Δ, should be given and compared with the ARPES FB position for CoSn. Measurements along the ab direction would also test whether the onset is direction-independent, as the mechanism (phase space, not velocities) predicts.

10. The Ni3In data are inconsistent between figures. In Fig. 4a (polycrystal, this work) ρ − ρ₀ of Ni3In at 300 K is about 50 µΩ cm and reaches only about 75 µΩ cm at 860 K. In Fig. 5a (literature single crystal, ref. [34]), ρ at 300 K is about 115 µΩ cm. The fit in Fig. 5a stops at 300 K, although the authors' own data extend to 860 K. The factor-of-two magnitude difference (geometry, anisotropy, polycrystal averaging) should be discussed. Fig. 5a should use, or at least add, the authors' own Ni3In data to 860 K, where the sublinear curvature that the paper is about is most visible.

11. The specific-heat support needs checking. The value γ ≈ 0.03 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² for Pd3Pb (p. 6, Fig. 5c) is about two orders of magnitude below typical metals, and even below Cu per atom. It would correspond to a DOS of order 0.01 states eV⁻¹ f.u.⁻¹. Please confirm the value and units, show the C/T versus T² data and fit (Fig. S4), and compare with the DFT DOS at E_F for Pd3Pb. If correct, Pd3Pb is effectively a semimetal. That weakens its use as a "good metal" test of the electron-phonon picture and brings thermally activated carriers into play as an alternative cause of curvature. Fig. 5c also compares γ values from different sources without comparing them to the DFT D(E_F), which would make the consistency argument quantitative.

12. Thermopower is available but not used. The conclusion proposes the Seebeck coefficient as a test of whether the FB lies above or below E_F. The ZEM3 setup used for the high-T resistivity measures S simultaneously. Including S(T) for the measured compounds, especially across the Ni3In1−xSnx series where the sign of dσ/dE should change at x ≈ 0.5, would be an inexpensive independent check of the σ(E) reconstruction. It would also make the claim stronger than a single curvature criterion.

C. Minor

13. On p. 6 the text says the FB "is not placed right at but above E_F in ... Pd3Pb", while Fig. 5c labels Pd3Pb as "FB edge below E_F" and the next sentences invoke E_F > E_edge. Please make the wording consistent.

14. The dashed line on the Ni3In curve in Fig. 4a is not explained in the caption. The caption says the low-T slope varies "by nearly an order of magnitude", while the text (p. 4) says "several times larger". Please reconcile.

15. Fig. 4c: specify which FB edge "E_edge" refers to in Ni3In (the caption says "upper FB edge"). State the temperature used for the BTE-RTA curve, and explain the plateau normalisation of the three model curves.

16. Fig. 3e: define how the "≈1/3" positions were determined (e.g. a fixed percentage deviation from BG). Note that the factor depends on m_FB and n_CB; it is not universal.

17. Methods: the 2PB model fixes Θ_D = 400 K and n = 3 for all of Fig. 3. Please justify n = 3 at all temperatures (the Wilson T³ law applies only at low T), and show that the sub/superlinear distinction survives n = 5 and other values of Θ_D.

18. The Methods say Θ_D can be taken "e.g. from specific heat"; please state which Θ_D values were used in Fig. 5a and their sources.

19. The supplementary figures S1 to S4 are cited for key evidence (finite-bandwidth model, ab initio ρ(T), CoSn high-T data, specific heat). They should be provided with the main text. Several of the concerns above may be partly answered there.

20. Typos: "temprature" (p. 5), "intial" (Methods); "Bloch-Grüneisen" and "Bloch–Grüneisen" are both used.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a "negative control" panel: the same analysis (curvature, Eq. 1 fit, T ≈ Δ/3 onset) for one or two well-characterised metals with a steep d-band DOS edge but no flat band, and for one with a smooth DOS. State explicitly what distinguishes a flat band in the transport signature.
2. Turn the method into a demonstrated measurement. Fit E_F − E_edge as a free parameter for every compound, report it with uncertainties in a table, and compare it with ARPES (CoSn) and with the substitution-series crossing (Ni3In). A blind prediction for a compound whose FB position is not yet known would show the screening value directly.
3. Use the Ni3In1−xSnx series more fully. Add S(T) and specific-heat γ(x) across the series, so that the step in σ(E) is checked against an independent DOS probe and Θ_D(x) is known.
4. Show the raw high-T data with heating and cooling, the overlap between setups, and before/after low-T curves, so readers can judge whether the curvature at 400 to 860 K is intrinsic.
