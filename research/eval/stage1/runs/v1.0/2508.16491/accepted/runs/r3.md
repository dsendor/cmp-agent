Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors propose that the curvature of the resistivity at high temperature shows where a flat band (FB) sits relative to E_F. In their picture, phonon-mediated interband scattering into the FB makes the relaxation time strongly energy dependent (τ ∝ 1/D(E)). That gives sublinear ρ(T) when E_F lies inside the FB and superlinear ρ(T) when E_F lies just outside it. The evidence is a two-parabolic-band (2PB) Boltzmann model, an ab initio electron–phonon calculation for Ni3In, a Ni3In1−xSnx substitution series, and fits of a "modified Bloch–Grüneisen" formula with one free parameter to six intermetallics. A cheap transport diagnostic for FB proximity would be useful. However, the physics (Mott's s–d scattering, with τ ∝ 1/D(E) and the resulting curvature of ρ(T)) is well established. The novelty therefore rests on showing that the diagnostic picks out FBs uniquely and can locate them quantitatively. In its present form the paper does not show either.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A simple, physically clear model, set out transparently in Methods (Eqs. 2–7). It predicts a characteristic onset scale T ≈ Δ/3 (Fig. 3e), which is a concrete and testable statement.
- The Ni3In vs Ni3Sn contrast (Fig. 4a) and the Ni3In1−xSnx series (Fig. 4b) offer chemical tuning of E_F across a known FB, in principle a strong test.
- New high-temperature (up to 860 K) data on CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb, plus a first-principles electron–phonon transport calculation (BTE-RTA, Phoebe) for comparison.
- The low-temperature T^5 law in CoSn (n = 4.99(2), Fig. 5d) is a clean check that ordinary electron–phonon scattering dominates in that compound.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The fingerprint is not shown to be specific to flat bands, and the obvious alternative explanations are not excluded quantitatively. The central claim (Abstract; Conclusion) is that a sublinear or superlinear high-T ρ(T) is a "clear and universal fingerprint" of FB proximity. But the mechanism used (τ ∝ 1/D(E), thermal smearing across a steep DOS feature) applies to any sharp DOS structure: a d-band edge, as in Mott's classic explanation of the sublinear ρ(T) of Pd and Pt, a van Hove singularity, or a pseudogap. Other mechanisms give the same curvature too. The Discussion (p. 6) dismisses some of them only qualitatively:
   - (i) Resistivity saturation. The bending in Ni3In, Ca(Rh,Ru)2 and Ni3Ge sets in at 40–100 μΩ cm (Fig. 5a). That is not far below typical saturation values of 100–200 μΩ cm for transition-metal intermetallics. The statement that the compounds are "well below the MIR limit" needs a number: an estimate of ρ_sat from DFT plasma frequencies, and a parallel-resistor fit compared with Eq. 1 on the same data.
   - (ii) Spin-fluctuation scattering. Ni3In is a near-ferromagnetic, strange-metal compound, and Ni3Ge belongs to the nearly-ferromagnetic Ni3X family. Paramagnon scattering gives a ρ(T) that crosses over from T² through linear to saturating, which looks like the "sublinear" signature. The paper does not mention this mechanism. Susceptibility data, or an argument based on the spin-fluctuation temperature, are needed.
   - (iii) Thermal expansion and phonon softening at constant pressure up to 860 K. These change Θ_D and the electron–phonon coupling, and produce curvature of a few per cent, comparable to the CoSn deviation in Fig. 5e.

   The authors should do three things. First, measure a null-control compound under matched conditions: isostructural and of similar resistivity, but with a featureless DOS near E_F. Ni3Sn partly serves; an explicit no-FB Cu3Au-type or CoSn-type reference would be better. Second, compare Eq. 1 quantitatively against the saturation and spin-fluctuation forms. Third, reword the claim as "proximity of a steep DOS feature" unless FB specificity can be shown.

2. The paper never demonstrates that ρ(T) locates the FB; the fits take the FB position from DFT. The title and Abstract claim the method "tracks" and "locates" FBs and gives |E_F − E_edge|. But every fit in Fig. 5a uses Eq. 1 with the DFT DOS taken as input, presumably at the DFT E_F, with only the prefactor C free. These fits therefore test whether the DFT DOS is consistent with ρ(T). They do not extract E_F − E_edge. This is circular for the paper's main selling point, especially since the authors themselves say DFT is "unavailable or unreliable … for FBs" (p. 6). Eq. 7, the free-position fit, is derived but never applied to any data set in the PDF. The authors should:
   - fit each compound with E_F (or a rigid shift of the DFT DOS) as a free parameter, using Eq. 7 or Eq. 1, and report the extracted E_F − E_edge with uncertainties and parameter correlations;
   - show the χ² landscape against the shift, to show how sharply ρ(T) constrains the FB position;
   - compare with a standard BG fit (also one free parameter at fixed Θ_D) and give residuals, to show that the DOS term is needed at all. For nearly linear data (CoSn, Pd3Pb, Pt3Sn in Fig. 5a), a one-parameter fit will look good with almost any DOS;
   - test the method blind against an independent determination: ARPES FB positions for CoSn (refs. 29, 30), or the composition at which the edge crosses E_F in Ni3In1−xSnx.

3. The best available test, the high-temperature ρ(T) of the intermediate Ni3In1−xSnx compositions, is not shown. The model's main predictions concern the high-T curvature and its onset at T ≈ Δ/3 (Fig. 3e), and how both evolve as |E_F − E_edge| changes. Yet Fig. 4b shows the substitution series only from 2 to 100 K. Fig. 4a shows 2–860 K data only for the two end members, x = 0 and x = 1. Using the low-T slope at 100 K as a proxy for σ(E) does not test the high-T curvature claim. The authors should show ρ(T) up to 860 K for all x (or at least x = 0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6). They should then show that the curvature changes sign where the edge crosses E_F (x ≈ 0.5) and that the deviation onset scales as Δ/3, with Δ from the alloy-DFT mapping. Without this, the substitution series supports the energy dependence of τ but not the proposed high-T fingerprint.

B. Should address

4. The reconstruction of σ(E) in Fig. 4c rests on several assumptions that are not stated.
   - Absolute ρ is compared across different polycrystalline samples. Geometric-factor errors (porosity, microcracks, contact spacing) of 10–20 % feed directly into (ρ − ρ0)^−1. The paper gives no uncertainties for these errors.
   - Subtracting ρ0 assumes Matthiessen's rule. In concentrated alloys with large ρ0 there are known deviations from it.
   - At 100 K (k_BT ≈ 9 meV, below Θ_D) the phonon factor depends on Θ_D(x), which changes from In to Sn. So (ρ − ρ0)^−1 at a fixed T is not a measure of σ(E) unless Θ_D(x) is constant.
   - The experimental points are in "arb. u.", so the step height, which is the quantity compared with the 2PB and DFT models, was evidently scaled to theory. The scaling should be stated.
   - The x → E mapping ("alloy-averaged DFT", ref. 39) should be described here (VCA, CPA or supercell) and given an uncertainty. Disorder broadening in the alloy also smears the edge.

   The authors should give error bars on both axes, state the normalization, and show the result at more than one temperature.

5. The physical approximations in the 2PB model are not justified for the parameter range used.
   - (i) Quasi-elastic scattering. τ(E) ∝ 1/D(E) evaluated at the carrier energy ignores the phonon energy. With Θ_D = 400 K (k_BΘ_D ≈ 34 meV) and Δ = 20–100 meV (Fig. 3), interband transitions sample D(E ± ħω) and smear the edge on a scale comparable to Δ itself.
   - (ii) Chemical potential. With a huge, asymmetric DOS at the edge (m_FB = 100 m0), μ shifts appreciably with T at fixed carrier number, possibly by tens of meV at 1000 K. The Methods do not say whether μ was held fixed or N conserved. This matters directly for the Δ/3 onset and for the non-metallic hump in Fig. 3c.
   - (iii) Factorization. The temperature dependence is factorized into a BG term multiplied by an energy integral (Eqs. 5–7). That is exact only if the phonon-occupation factor does not depend on electron energy across the window.

   The authors should quantify each of these, for example by evaluating Eq. 3 with the full inelastic kernel and with N conserved for one parameter set from Fig. 3. They should also explain how the absolute scale of ρ in Fig. 3 (e.g. 500 μΩ cm at the peak in Fig. 3c) was fixed.

6. The claim of independence from microscopic details is supported only weakly. The statement that microscopic details "do not vary significantly" (Fig. 4 caption, p. 4) rests on the normalized σ(E) of a single compound, Ni3In. There, the BTE-RTA curve oscillates strongly around the 2PB and D_DFT^−1 curves (Fig. 4c). The authors should show directly from the Phoebe calculation that τ_km^−1 tracks D(E), and that v²(E) is smooth across the edge. They should also compare absolute ρ(T) at high T, not only normalized shapes. The supporting figure (Fig. S2) is referenced but is not in the PDF under review. Related points:
   - DFT setup. The calculation uses nonmagnetic PBE without stated spin–orbit coupling, and no correlation correction for a compound with documented strong correlations. The sensitivity of the FB position to these choices should be stated.
   - Anisotropy. Ni3In and CoSn are quasi-2D kagome systems with strongly anisotropic velocities. The isotropic 2PB model is compared with polycrystal (Ni3In1−xSnx) and c-axis-only (CoSn) data, and the paper does not discuss the consequences of mixing directions. CoSn should also be measured in the ab plane, where the kagome FB dispersion is relevant.

7. The specific-heat argument (Fig. 5c) does not discriminate between scenarios as presented.
   - Normalization. γ values for compounds with 2, 3 and 4 atoms per formula unit are compared without saying whether they are per mole of formula units or per mole of atoms.
   - Ni3In. Its γ ≈ 51 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² includes correlation and spin-fluctuation mass enhancement, so it is not a bare DOS measure.
   - Pd3Pb. A value of γ ≈ 0.03 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² is exceptionally small for a compound containing Pd. It needs checking (fit range, Schottky or nuclear contributions, units). Such a value says more about semimetallic low carrier density than about FB position.

   The authors should compare γ with the DFT D(E_F) for each compound and discuss the enhancement factors. Without that, Fig. 5c is consistent with, but does not support, the FB placement.

8. The CoSn evidence for a FB about 0.1 eV below E_F rests on a small deviation from a hand-drawn linear reference (Fig. 5e). The size of the superlinear excess depends on the range used to define the dashed line (it appears to be about 200–400 K). Thermal expansion could produce similar curvature (Concern 1). The authors should state how the reference line was fitted and give the deviation with an uncertainty. Ideally they should also give the onset temperature extracted from an Eq. 7 fit, and compare Δ with the FB energy measured by ARPES in CoSn rather than with Δ_DFT only.

9. Sample characterization and the reliability of the high-temperature measurements are not documented. No XRD, phase-purity, composition or L12-ordering data are given for Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb or CoSn. Antisite disorder in Cu3Au-type compounds strongly affects both ρ0 and the phonon spectrum. Two things need to be ruled out for measurements to 860 K:
   - irreversible changes (Sn/Pb loss, oxidation, ordering or annealing during the run): show heating and cooling curves and ρ0 before and after;
   - artefacts at the join between the helium-bath and ZEM3 data sets (rescaling or offset at about 300 K): explain how the two were joined.

   The paper should also say how many samples of each compound were measured and give the geometric-factor uncertainty.

C. Minor

10. Fig. 5a legend lists "Ca(Rh,Ru)2", while the caption, text and Fig. 5c give "CaRh2". Clarify which material and composition were used and with which DFT DOS. Also, the Ni3In curve in Fig. 5a uses data from ref. 34 only up to about 300 K, although the authors' own Ni3In data extend to 860 K (Fig. 4a). Fitting the latter would be the more stringent test.

11. Give a table of fit inputs and outputs for Fig. 5a: Θ_D and its source, the exponent n (3 or 5) used for each compound, C, the fit window, ρ0, and the DFT DOS reference. The statement that the fits have "a single free parameter" also depends on fixing n, and the choice of n for each material is not stated.

12. Fig. 4a: the caption says the low-T slopes differ by "nearly an order of magnitude", while the text says "several times larger"; make these consistent. Fig. 4c is labelled "m_FB = 10 m0" and the text says "m_FB ≈ 10 m_CB"; state that m_CB = m0 here.

13. Methods, p. 9: the justification "σ_FB ≪ σ_CB … since we only consider very small |E_F − E_edge|" does not follow. The relevant reasons are the effective-mass ratio and the carrier density in each band.

14. Methods, p. 11: the Fermi-window threshold "∂f/∂T = 1 × 10^−10" has no units. Give k-point and q-point convergence tests for the 75 × 75 × 75 mesh, and the smearing used.

15. Figs. S1–S4 and the boxcar-DOS discussion are cited in support of several statements: the finite-bandwidth results, the ρ(T) from first principles, CoSn high-T, and Pd3Pb specific heat. These figures are not included in the PDF reviewed, so those statements could not be checked here. Make sure they accompany the paper.

16. Abstract and p. 3: the sign rule should be stated in terms of the slope of σ(E) at E_F, with a note on the electron-like case. As written, readers may infer that sublinear always means E_F below E_edge.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Turn the method into a demonstrated measurement. Fit E_F − E_edge freely (Eq. 7) for every compound, report uncertainties, and compare blind with an independent FB position (ARPES for CoSn; the crossing composition in Ni3In1−xSnx).
- Measure the full 2–860 K ρ(T) across the Ni3In1−xSnx series. Plot the deviation from BG against T/Δ for all x, to test the predicted collapse and the Δ/3 onset of Fig. 3e directly.
- Add a no-FB reference compound measured in the same setups, and show quantitatively that resistivity saturation, spin-fluctuation scattering and thermal expansion cannot reproduce the curvature, or narrow the claim to "steep DOS features".
- Use the Phoebe calculation to show τ_k^−1 against D(E), and extend it with the inelastic kernel and a temperature-dependent μ to fix the validity range of the simple model (Concern 5).
