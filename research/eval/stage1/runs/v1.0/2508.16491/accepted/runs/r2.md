Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript proposes that the curvature of the high-temperature resistivity tells you where a flat band (FB) sits relative to E_F. Sublinear ρ(T) would mean E_F lies inside the FB states, and superlinear ρ(T) would mean it lies outside them. The mechanism is phonon-mediated interband scattering whose phase space follows the density of states (DOS). The authors support this with a two-parabolic-band Boltzmann model, a DOS-weighted ("modified") Bloch-Grüneisen formula, ab initio electron-phonon transport for Ni3In, a reconstruction of σ(E) across the Ni3In1-xSnx series, and 2-860 K resistivity of several intermetallics (Ni3In, Ca(Rh,Ru)2, Ni3Ge, Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb, CoSn). A cheap transport-based screen for flat bands would be useful. However, the physical mechanism is essentially Mott's s-d interband scattering picture. The new element is turning it into a diagnostic for flat bands, and whether that diagnostic is specific and quantitative is the central question below.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The paper offers a clear, physically transparent picture: the scattering phase space follows the DOS, so a DOS edge near E_F produces a step in σ(E). The model gives a simple energy scale (deviations from BG start near T ≈ Δ/3, Fig. 3e).
- The Ni3In1-xSnx solid solution is a good tuning experiment. The reconstructed σ(E) in Fig. 4c is compared against three levels of theory: the two-parabolic-band model, τ ∝ D_DFT^-1, and BTE-RTA.
- The resistivity data extend to 860 K, a regime rarely reported for these flat-band compounds. The new CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb data add to the literature.
- The model is simple enough for others to apply.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **No test of specificity: the "fingerprint" is never checked on materials without flat bands.**
   - **What the claim rests on.** The abstract and conclusion present a sublinear high-T ρ(T) as a "clear and universal fingerprint" of a flat band at E_F. By the paper's own model, however, the only requirement is a steep DOS edge near E_F (Methods, Eqs. 5-7; Fig. S1).
   - **Why that is not specific.** Sublinear or saturating high-T resistivity is common in transition metals and compounds with a sharp d-band DOS but no flat band in the topological or kagome sense. Examples:
     - Pd and Pt, the classic Mott/Wilson s-d cases;
     - A15 compounds such as Nb3Sn and V3Si;
     - many Heusler compounds and skutterudites (refs. 53-57 by the same group).
   - **What the paper shows.** Every material in Figs. 4-5 was chosen because it is already known or predicted to host a flat band. There is no compound with a van Hove singularity, a d-band edge or a pseudogap but no flat band. There is also no "blind" case where the diagnosis was made before the band position was known.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Apply the same analysis (curvature criterion and Eq. 1 fit) to at least one or two reference metals with a sharp non-flat-band DOS feature near E_F, and to one simple metal with a featureless DOS.
     - Show what the method returns for each.
     - Reword "flat band" to "DOS edge near E_F" wherever the evidence does not go further.

2. **Other explanations for high-T curvature are dismissed qualitatively, not excluded quantitatively.**
   - **Saturation near the Mott-Ioffe-Regel limit.** Page 6 says the compounds are "well below the MIR limit" but gives no numbers. Ni3In in Fig. 5a reaches about 120 μΩ cm by 300 K, and polycrystalline Ni3In in Fig. 4a keeps rising to about 80 μΩ cm above ρ0 at 860 K. This is close to the typical ρ_sat of 100-200 μΩ cm, where the parallel-resistor form 1/ρ = 1/ρ_ideal + 1/ρ_sat is known to produce exactly this sublinearity.
   - **Other effects that bend ρ(T) between 300 and 860 K but are not discussed:**
     - thermal expansion and phonon softening, which change both Θ_D and the bandwidth;
     - temperature-dependent shifts of the chemical potential when a large DOS feature lies near E_F;
     - Debye-Waller effects.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Estimate the mean free path at the highest temperature for each compound, using DFT Fermi velocities and plasma frequencies.
     - Fit each dataset with a conventional BG plus parallel-resistor (saturation) model having the same number of free parameters as Eq. 1, and compare fit quality, e.g. χ² or residual plots.
     - Comment on thermal-expansion corrections to Θ_D.
   - **Why it matters.** Until these alternatives are shown to fail where Eq. 1 succeeds, the agreement in Fig. 5a does not single out interband scattering.

3. **The paper never demonstrates that ρ(T) alone can locate the flat band; the fits in Fig. 5a rely on DFT.**
   - **The headline claim** is that transport can "locate FBs" and give |E_F − E_edge|, for example where DFT is unreliable (p. 6, Eq. 7).
   - **What was actually fitted.** All fits in Fig. 5a use Eq. 1 with the DFT DOS fixed and only a prefactor C free. That tests consistency with DFT; it does not determine the flat-band position from the data.
   - **Eq. 7** is advertised as DOS-free, with C, m2^{3/2}/D1 and E_F − E_edge free, plus Θ_D and n. It is never applied to any measured dataset, and no extracted E_F − E_edge or uncertainty is reported.
   - **The only quantitative location given** is CoSn's "≈0.1 eV below E_F", read from a deviation near 400 K together with the factor Δ/3 from Fig. 3e (Fig. 5e). That factor came from one model setting (m_FB = 10 m0, parabolic bands). The finite-bandwidth results in Fig. S1 suggest it depends on m_FB and W.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Fit Eq. 7 to Ni3In1-xSnx, CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb.
     - Report E_F − E_edge with confidence intervals and parameter correlations, showing whether E_F − E_edge, the mass ratio and C can be separately identified from a smooth ρ(T) curve.
     - Compare the results against independent determinations: ARPES for CoSn (refs. 29, 30, 45) and the x-dependence for Ni3In1-xSnx.
     - Give an uncertainty for the Δ/3 rule across m_FB and W.

B. Should address

4. **The σ(E) reconstruction in Fig. 4c rests on several unverified assumptions.**
   - **Assumptions in the procedure:**
     - (a) Matthiessen's rule holds across a concentrated, disordered alloy, so that subtracting ρ0 isolates electron-phonon scattering. Deviations from Matthiessen's rule are large exactly when ρ0 and the DOS vary strongly.
     - (b) A rigid-band, alloy-averaged DFT mapping converts x into E_F − E_edge. Neither the method (VCA or supercell) nor its uncertainty is given.
     - (c) Geometric factors, grain-boundary contributions, density or porosity, and the polycrystalline averaging of the strongly anisotropic Ni3In are the same for every x.
     - (d) The experimental points are put on the same "arb. u." scale as the theory curves. How that normalization was done is not stated, so the visual agreement may include a free scale factor.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - State how many samples per composition were measured and give the geometric-factor uncertainty.
     - Show error bars on both axes of Fig. 4c.
     - Specify the normalization.
     - Test how robust the result is to the choice of reference temperature (100 K versus, e.g., 200 K or 300 K).
   - **Testing D^-1 against D^-2.** Distinguishing D^-1 from the D^-2 expected for electron-electron scattering (p. 4) needs a quantitative comparison, such as log σ_exp against log D_DFT across x. Visual agreement is not enough.

5. **The fitting procedure in Fig. 5a is underspecified, so "single free parameter" cannot be judged.**
   - **Missing details for each compound:**
     - the value and source of Θ_D;
     - the exponent n used (3 or 5);
     - whether ρ0 was subtracted or fitted (the y-axis is ρ, not ρ − ρ0);
     - the temperature window;
     - how the DFT DOS was broadened;
     - the fitted C and residuals.
   - **Missing comparison.** The paper should show that a standard BG fit, with the same free parameters plus Θ_D, fits these curves noticeably worse.
   - **Why it matters.** Without this comparison, the statement that Eq. 1 gives "good agreement with all the FB materials" has no discriminating power.

6. **Ab initio support for "independent of microscopic details" is narrow.**
   - **What exists.** First-principles electron-phonon calculations are presented only for Ni3In (Figs. 4c and S2), with Ni3Ge taken from ref. 36.
   - **What is claimed.** The abstract states the effect emerges "independently of microscopic details, such as the electron-phonon coupling strength, as confirmed by ab initio calculations."
   - **Caveats on the Ni3In calculation.** The BTE-RTA σ(E) in Fig. 4c fluctuates strongly near E_edge. It is not stated at which temperature it was computed, nor how sensitive it is to smearing and to the 75³ mesh. RTA versus full iterative BTE is not discussed.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Either narrow the claim, or add a calculation for a structurally different case (e.g. CoSn, where the flat band is below E_F and the kagome d-orbital character differs).
     - Quantify how much |g|² and the velocities vary across the edge.

7. **Using DFT and specific heat as cross-checks needs care.**
   - **DFT position of the flat band.** DFT-PBE is known to misplace flat bands in kagome compounds by tens of meV, and ARPES data exist for CoSn. Since Fig. 5b anchors the interpretation, the authors should compare it with ARPES or state the expected error.
   - **Sommerfeld coefficients (Fig. 5c).**
     - γ includes electron-phonon and correlation mass enhancement.
     - γ is normalized per mole of formula units that contain different numbers of atoms (Ni3In: 4; CaRh2: 3; CoSn: 2).
     - The quoted γ ≈ 0.03 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² for Pd3Pb is far below even a free-electron estimate for a Pd-based metal. The Pd3Pb bar in Fig. 5c also appears larger than 0.03 on the plotted scale.
   - **What the authors should do.** Check this value, state the units per atom, and compare γ against the DFT bare γ_band to separate mass renormalization from DOS.

8. **The novelty relative to Mott/Wilson s-d scattering and to the authors' own prior work is not delineated.**
   - The model in Methods (τ ∝ 1/D_tot, Eqs. 2-7) is close to the classic Mott s-d picture (ref. 33) and to the authors' earlier analyses (refs. 39, 53-58).
   - Ref. 39 (PRX 15, 021054) already studies Ni3In1-xSnx tuning of the flat band with the same first author.
   - **What the authors should do.** State explicitly which results here are new: data, the reconstruction in Fig. 4c, the Δ/3 criterion, Eq. 7. This determines the significance assessment.

9. **The measurement details do not support quantitative slope comparisons.**
   - **Why slopes need extra care.** The key observable is the slope and curvature of ρ(T), and the comparison in Fig. 4a is between slopes of different compounds.
   - **What is not reported:**
     - (a) how the low-T bath-cryostat data and the high-T ZEM3 data were joined, and whether a scale factor was applied in the overlap region;
     - (b) whether heating and cooling cycles to 860 K were reproducible, given possible Sn or Pb loss, oxidation or contact degradation;
     - (c) geometric-factor uncertainties;
     - (d) how many samples were measured per compound.
   - **Anisotropy.** CoSn was measured only along c, although the kagome flat bands are mainly in-plane. In-plane ρ(T) would test whether the curvature criterion depends on transport direction. The authors note that Ni3In in-plane data are strange-metal-like (n = 1).

10. **Ni3In's correlated (strange-metal) behavior is not separated from electron-phonon scattering.**
   - Ni3In shows T-linear, non-Fermi-liquid resistivity at low T, attributed to correlations at the flat band (ref. 23).
   - Using (ρ − ρ0)^-1 at 100 K as the electron-phonon σ(E) assumes that correlation-driven scattering is negligible there.
   - The Kondo argument on p. 6 (T* ≈ 50 K) covers only one kind of correlation physics.
   - **What the authors should do.** Discuss how a correlation contribution would distort Fig. 4c near x = 0, and whether the reconstruction changes when a higher reference temperature is used.

C. Minor

11. **The Supplementary Information was not in the submission reviewed.** Figs. S1-S4 are cited for key points: the finite-bandwidth model, the ab initio ρ(T) for Ni3In (S2), high-T CoSn (S3) and Pd3Pb specific heat (S4). The Data Availability statement points to the SI. These figures need to be available to assess the claims.

12. **Semiclassical validity of the model curves.**
   - Fig. 3c (m_FB = 100 m0) gives a peak near 500 μΩ cm, and Fig. 3e extends above 10³ μΩ cm.
   - Both are well beyond where Boltzmann transport is valid. Fig. 3e's T/Δ up to 10 corresponds to T > Θ_D, beyond what the formula represents.
   - The authors should note this limitation, or restrict the plotted range.

13. **Naming is inconsistent:** "Ca(Rh,Ru)2" in Fig. 5a versus "CaRh2" in Fig. 5c and the text. Please make it consistent and state the composition actually measured in ref. 35.

14. **Fig. 5a uses literature Ni3In data (Gim et al., up to about 300 K)** rather than the authors' own Ni3In data, which run from 2 to 860 K (Fig. 4a). Fitting the wider-range data would be a stronger test of Eq. 1.

15. **Fig. 4b:** the abrupt change "around x = 0.5" should be quantified, for example as slope at a fixed T against x, with uncertainties.

16. **Methods details:**
   - Define the "Fermi window width ∂f/∂T = 1×10⁻¹⁰" (units, and its relation to an energy window).
   - Give the electron and phonon grids used for the Wannier interpolation.
   - Low-T data were taken while the sample warmed spontaneously, so state the thermometry lag.

17. **Typos and wording:** "temprature" (p. 5), "intial" (Methods), and "Mott's early work" should cite the 1936 paper specifically. "Arb. u." axes in Fig. 2 should state the normalization.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Add a blind or negative test.** Apply the curvature criterion and Eq. 7 to materials whose band position is known independently (including non-flat-band metals with sharp DOS features), and report how often the method gets it right.
2. **Report the Seebeck coefficient.** The ZEM3 measures S(T) at the same time as ρ(T). The conclusion already notes that the sign of S indicates which side of E_F the flat band extends to, and Ni3In1-xSnx data were analyzed this way in ref. 39. S(T) for CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb would be an independent check at no extra measurement cost.
3. **Provide a fit table.** For every compound, give the fitted parameters with uncertainties for Eq. 1, Eq. 7, standard BG and BG with saturation, so readers can judge whether the method discriminates between them.
4. **Quantify where the method stops working.** Use the finite-bandwidth model (Fig. S1) to give the minimum bandwidth W, step contrast and |E_F − E_edge| that produce a detectable curvature. State which of the moiré and kagome systems mentioned in the conclusion would fall inside that range.
