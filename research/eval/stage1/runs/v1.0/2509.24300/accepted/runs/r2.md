Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports transport on Ca2RuO4 nanoflakes (exfoliated from bulk crystals, 10-35 nm) and on MBE films. Some samples show a logarithmic rise in resistance, R ~ ln(1/T), between roughly 100 and 220 K, and their beta-function shows a "vertical flow". The authors read both as signatures of a Bose glass of localized Cooper pairs at up to about 220 K. They also extract two "quantum critical" sheet resistances, about (5/2)h/e^2 and (5/2)h/4e^2, and propose a phase diagram in which the ground state (Mott insulator, Fermi glass, Bose glass or superconductor) is set by a localization-strength parameter tied to RuO6 octahedral distortion. If the claim held, preformed and localized Cooper pairs at 220 K in a correlated oxide would be very significant. The evidence, however, is almost entirely the curvature of R(T) in a window under one decade wide, with no magnetic-field, structural or direct pairing evidence on the devices measured.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The material and the regime are interesting. Ca2RuO4 changes from a Mott insulator in bulk to a low-resistivity, sometimes superconducting state in nanoflakes, and the paper documents this across several flakes and two kinds of sample (exfoliated flakes and MBE films).
- The beta-function approach used for cuprate and Pb films is applied consistently to samples spanning Mott-insulating, weakly localized and superconducting behaviour, and the data are placed alongside bulk literature data (Figs. 4b, 5a).
- The empirical observation that the high-temperature resistance change, Delta rho between 280 and 120 K, sorts samples by their low-temperature ground state (Fig. 4a) is simple and potentially useful as a screening criterion, whatever its microscopic origin.
- The flakes and the MBE films show qualitatively similar ln(1/T) behaviour (Fig. 3c, Fig. S2), which hints that the effect is not specific to one fabrication route.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. No evidence beyond the shape of R(T) that the 100-220 K regime involves Cooper pairs. The headline claims (abstract; Results p. 2; Conclusion) are that "signatures of Cooper pairs persist up to high temperatures of ~220 K" and that "localized Cooper pairs and moving vortices" exist at 220 K for sample 4. The only transport evidence is that R ~ ln(1/T) fits better than G ~ ln T over 101-220 K (sample 4) or 105-183 K (sample 5) (Fig. 3a,b), plus the sign of beta' derived from the same curves (Fig. 6a). Neither is unique to bosons. The decisive test is the magnetic field.
   - For localized pairs, the magnetoresistance in the ln(1/T) window should be positive, with a field scale set by the flux quantum h/2e through the localization area. A fermionic weak-localization (Fermi glass) regime should give negative magnetoresistance with the standard Hikami-Larkin-Nagaoka form.
   - A field-tuned transition should show a separatrix with finite-size scaling.
   - Please measure R(T, B) for samples 4 and 5 (and 6/7) in perpendicular and parallel field through both the "BG" and "FG" windows. State the bosonic and fermionic predictions for the sign and scale of the magnetoresistance, and show which one the data follow.
   - If it can be done, an h/2e-periodic signal in a patterned ring, or a Nernst signal, at 150-200 K would be direct evidence of pairing.
   - Without such data, the claim should be reduced to "a logarithmic-in-1/T resistance regime of unknown origin".

2. The Bose glass versus Fermi glass criterion reduces to the curvature of ln G against ln T, which ordinary mechanisms also produce. From Eqs. (1)-(4), the sign of beta'_EXP is just the sign of the second derivative of ln g with respect to ln T, so the "independent criterion" of Fig. 5b and Fig. 6a is not independent of the fits in Fig. 3. In samples 4 and 5 the "BG" window ends at a resistance maximum near 90-100 K, followed by a superconducting drop (Fig. 1c, Fig. 3a,b). Any R(T) that rises on cooling and then rolls over must bend in this way just above the maximum.
   - Plausible mundane causes include an Aslamazov-Larkin or Maki-Thompson fluctuation conductance growing on cooling, a parallel metallic or superconducting minority channel shunting an insulating matrix (see concern 4), and a temperature-dependent geometric current redistribution in an inhomogeneous flake.
   - The authors should fit the 100-290 K data of samples 4 and 5 with these alternatives (for example, a G ~ ln T Fermi-glass term plus an AL fluctuation term with T_c0 near 30-90 K, or a two-channel parallel resistor) and compare them with the ln(1/T) form quantitatively. Report residuals, reduced chi^2 or information criteria, and how sensitive the results are to the fitting-window edges.
   - The window edges marked by the arrows and dashed lines in Figs. 2 and 3 appear to be chosen by eye. Please define them objectively, for example as the temperature where the residuals exceed a stated threshold.

3. The two "quantum critical resistances" are not shown to be critical points, and their values depend on assumptions. R^BG-FG = 65.7 kOhm appears to come from reading the Vollhardt-Wolfle curve at beta_c = -1.6 (Fig. 5a gives ln g of about -0.9 there, i.e. g of about 0.39, i.e. about 66 kOhm), not from the measured resistance at the crossover. This creates several problems.
   - beta_EXP carries a prefactor 2/p, with p = 1 assumed (Eq. 1). Taking p = 2 or 3, more typical of electron-phonon dephasing at 100-300 K, scales beta_c by a factor of 2-3 and changes the inferred resistance completely.
   - The measured resistances at the marked BG-FG crossovers are about 80 kOhm per layer for sample 4 (Fig. 3a, ln T of about 5.4) and about 32 kOhm per layer for sample 5 (Fig. 3b, ln T of about 5.2). These differ by a factor of about 2.5 and bracket 65.7 kOhm rather than matching it, which is not what a universal critical resistance would give.
   - beta_c = -1.6 is a horizontal line separating two groups of samples in Fig. 6a. It is not a point where beta' changes sign within a single sample.
   - In Fig. 6b, the 65.7 kOhm and 16.5 kOhm lines mark where the highest-current curve levels off and roughly where curves meet at low T. No temperature-independent separatrix and no scaling collapse in the tuning parameter (current, thickness or Delta rho) against T is shown.
   - "Per layer" normalization requires the thickness, from tilted SEM, and the layer spacing. Neither the definition nor the uncertainty is given, and the "5/2" factor scales directly with both.
   - The authors should state exactly how each critical resistance was obtained and propagate the uncertainties in thickness, geometry and p. They should also either show a crossing or collapse that defines a critical point, or call these characteristic crossover resistances. The "5/2", 8/pi and Pfaffian comparisons (p. 6-7) should be removed or clearly labelled as speculative unless the error bar is well below the differences between those numbers.

4. The measured flakes are not characterized, so parallel conduction through impurity phases or a different polytype cannot be excluded.
   - The source crystals contain about 5% CaRuO3 and 6% S-phase Ca2RuO4 (Methods). Flakes are told apart from CaRuO3 only by "crystal shape" in SEM.
   - The flakes' resistivity is 3-4 orders of magnitude below bulk (Fig. 1a), and the paper attributes this to relaxed octahedral distortion. However, no structural measurement (Raman, micro-XRD, TEM/STEM, EELS/EDX) was made on any measured flake. The lattice parameters quoted are for the bulk powder and the MBE films.
   - A metallic CaRuO3 inclusion, an interfacial layer on SiO2, Ru-rich or oxygen-deficient regions, or a percolating minority phase would all lower rho and |Delta rho|. They would also produce the curvature discussed in concern 2 and could account for the "superconducting" drop.
   - The superconductivity itself is shown in this paper only as resistance drops in Fig. 1b,c. There is no field dependence, no I-V critical current and no diamagnetic response on these devices, and samples 6/7 never reach zero resistance.
   - Because the Bose glass interpretation needs a superconducting phase next to it, the authors should provide, for at least samples 4 and 5: a device-level structural and chemical identification (Raman phonon modes distinguish L/S Ca2RuO4 and CaRuO3; cross-sectional TEM would show the layering and any second phase), and R(T, B) and V(I) through the superconducting transition.

B. Should address

5. The sign and magnitude of beta for samples 4 and 5 appear inconsistent with their own R(T).
   - In the 100-290 K window, both samples have dR/dT < 0, so g increases with T and Eq. (1) gives beta < 0. From Fig. 3a (sample 4), G per layer goes from about 0.8 x 10^-5 to 1.6 x 10^-5 S between ln T = 4.6 and 5.65, so beta should be about -1.3.
   - Fig. 6a instead shows the sample 4 and 5 curves (blue) near 0 to +1 over 100-270 K. Fig. 5b places these samples at ln g of about +0.3 to +0.8, whereas 120 kOhm per layer corresponds to g of about 0.2 (ln g of about -1.5).
   - It seems that g in Figs. 5 and 6 is defined differently (total sheet conductance rather than per layer?) or that the sign convention or temperature range differs from Eq. (1).
   - Please state precisely how g was computed for each sample, including the bulk 3D data of Nakatsuji 1 and 2 in Fig. 5a, and which temperature range enters each panel. Then check that Figs. 3, 5 and 6 agree. Because the vertical-flow argument and beta_c rest on these plots, this needs to be resolved.

6. The same sample has different resistance values in different figures, and transport is non-ohmic.
   - The sample 4 curves in Fig. 6b peak at about 40 kOhm per layer (left axis), and the raw resistance peaks at about 4 kOhm (right axis). Fig. 3a gives a peak of about 125 kOhm per layer for the same sample, and Fig. 1b (rho of about 7 x 10^-3 Ohm cm, 10 nm) is consistent with Fig. 3a, not Fig. 6b.
   - Please say whether Fig. 6b comes from a different cooldown, a different contact configuration or a different conversion, and show whether R(T) survives thermal cycling.
   - Fig. 6b also shows a strong current dependence from 1 nA to 5 uA up to at least 100 K, which overlaps the claimed BG window. Ca2RuO4 is known for current-induced and Joule-heating artifacts (Refs. 28, 34, 35). Please give the excitation current used in Figs. 1-3, show I-V curves at several temperatures in the 100-220 K window to confirm ohmic response, and estimate the self-heating at the largest currents. Please also say whether the "bias-current-induced SIT" survives correction for heating.

7. The role of octahedral distortion and the "no disorder" claim are inferred, not measured.
   - The paper states that thinning "does not introduce disorder" and that the ground state is set by octahedral distortion (Results p. 2 and 4-5; Conclusion). The only argument is that resistivity goes down rather than up. That does not exclude disorder, which can coexist with an independent metallizing effect, and it does not show that the distortion changed.
   - For the MBE films, the c-axis (11.592 A) is shorter than in either bulk phase. In bulk, shorter c goes with stronger flattening and the S (insulating) phase, which seems to contradict "reduced distortion". Compressive epitaxial strain on LaAlO3 is an obvious alternative, and the statement that this "cannot be explained by simple elastic (Poisson) strain" needs a calculation.
   - The y-axis of the phase diagram (Fig. 4a), Delta rho = rho(120 K) - rho(280 K) divided by 160 K, depends on thickness and geometry errors and on any parallel channel. Using it as the "localization strength" partly restates the observation that less-insulating samples become superconducting.
   - Please add Raman or diffraction measurements on representative flakes to tie Delta rho to an octahedral parameter (tilt, rotation, flattening), give error bars on Delta rho from thickness uncertainty, and soften the causal language where the data are only correlational.

8. The 2D weak-localization framework is applied without justification of its preconditions.
   - Eq. (1) assumes L_phi ~ T^(-p/2) with p = 1 (electron-electron dephasing in 2D), and the ln T and ln(1/T) forms assume 2D transport. At 100-300 K, electron-phonon dephasing usually dominates (p of about 2-3), and L_phi is likely only a few nm, smaller than the 10-35 nm film thickness.
   - The per-layer treatment also assumes decoupled RuO2 layers conducting in parallel.
   - Please estimate L_phi, or the localization length, in this temperature range (for example from magnetoresistance, see concern 1) and compare it with thickness. Please also show how the BG/FG classification and beta_c change for p = 2 and 3.
   - Fig. 2a applies 2D VRH (T^(-1/3)) to 25-35 nm flakes. Please compare it with 3D Mott VRH, Efros-Shklovskii VRH and Arrhenius fits, because the Mott-FG crossover temperatures in Fig. 4a depend on this choice.

9. Supporting magnetization evidence is weak and partly retracted. The discussion on p. 2 cites diamagnetism from about 140 K in powders of nanoscale crystals and from about 220 K in current-biased bulk (Refs. 14, 15, 34). It describes these as "indirect but consistent support". Ref. 34 has been retracted, and Ref. 35 attributes such signals to Joule heating. The powder measurement is not on the transport devices, and the ferromagnetic transition at 180 K complicates any diamagnetic reading. This paragraph should either be supported with magnetization data whose background subtraction and ZFC/FC protocol are stated, or be cut back so it does not appear to corroborate pairing at 220 K.

10. Few samples show the headline behaviour, and how samples were selected is unclear.
   - The combination of a BG window and superconductivity appears in two flakes (samples 4 and 5) and in "sample 9". Sample 9 appears only in Fig. 4a and is not described anywhere (thickness, R(T), fits).
   - The text says samples of comparable thickness behave differently.
   - Please give a table of all devices measured, including any that were excluded, with thickness, geometry, rho(300 K), Delta rho, the presence of a resistance maximum or drop, and the fitted crossover temperatures with uncertainties. Please also show R(T) for sample 9, so readers can judge how reproducible the phase diagram is.

11. The universality claimed in Fig. 5 is overstated.
   - The text says "all of the data fall on a single universal curve". The plot combines bulk 3D resistivities (Nakatsuji 1 and 2), whose conversion to a dimensionless 2D g is not explained, with thin-film per-layer data.
   - The Vollhardt-Wolfle curve contains a scale that sets where beta crosses zero, and how it was fixed (fitted or taken from theory) is not stated.
   - Samples 1-3 scatter visibly around it in Fig. 5b.
   - Please give the VW expression and parameters used, explain the g conversion for each dataset, and describe the agreement quantitatively.

C. Minor

12. Terminology. "Quantum critical point" and "quantum critical resistance" are used for finite-temperature crossovers seen as temperature is lowered in fixed samples. "Characteristic crossover resistance" or similar would be more accurate unless a T -> 0 tuning study is shown. In the same spirit, "Fermi glass" and "Bose glass" labels at 200-300 K should be described as transport regimes.

13. Notation. The quantity written "drho(=rho(120 K) - rho(280 K))/dT" (Fig. 1b, Fig. 4) is a finite difference over 160 K. Writing Delta rho / Delta T and giving the sign convention explicitly would avoid confusion with a derivative, given that Fig. 4 labels both "drho/dT < 0" regions and plots |drho/dT|.

14. Several sample descriptions are inconsistent.
   - The Fig. 1a caption groups the 300 nm sample 8 with the "nanoscale thin film single crystals".
   - The bulk structural (Mott) transition is given as 354 K. The commonly cited value is about 357 K; please check it or give its source.
   - The flake lattice parameters in Methods (c = 12.278 A) are described as "nanoscale single crystal" values, but they come from powder XRD of the bulk crystals before exfoliation.

15. Figures and references need small fixes.
   - Fig. 5a has a stray "(i)" label.
   - "Ca2RuO4nanofilms" in the Fig. 1 title is missing a space, and "cooper pairs" should be capitalized consistently.
   - Ref. 27 has an incomplete citation ("Nature Physics 1-7 (2025)").
   - The Fig. 6 caption should state the temperature range and current used in panel (a).

16. Data availability. Data are available only "upon reasonable request". Given that the analysis rests on derivatives of R(T) curves, depositing the raw R(T), R(T, I) and geometry and thickness data would let readers repeat the beta-function analysis.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a magnetotransport dataset (R(T, B) in perpendicular and parallel field through the 100-220 K window and through the superconducting transition) for samples 4 and 5. This is the most direct way to tell bosonic from fermionic localization and to confirm superconductivity.
2. Make the beta-function analysis reproducible and robust. Define g per sample explicitly, show the results for p = 1, 2 and 3, include alternative R(T) models (fluctuation conductivity, two-channel conduction), and report fit statistics and window sensitivity.
3. Characterize the measured flakes directly (Raman and/or cross-sectional TEM with EDX) to rule out CaRuO3 or other second phases and to tie Delta rho to a measured octahedral distortion.
4. Provide a complete device table, including sample 9, with thickness uncertainties propagated to rho, per-layer resistance and the characteristic resistances. Show reproducibility under thermal cycling and ohmic response at the measurement currents.
