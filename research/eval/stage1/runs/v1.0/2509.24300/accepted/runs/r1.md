Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports transport on exfoliated Ca2RuO4 nanoflakes (samples 1-5, 8) and MBE films (samples 6, 7). It claims that the thinner, less-resistive samples pass from a weakly localized "Fermi glass" (sigma ~ ln T) into a "Bose glass" (rho ~ ln(1/T)) below about 220 K, and then become superconducting with onset near 100 K. The evidence offered is a change in the logarithmic form of R(T) and a "vertical flow" in a beta-function plot. If it held, localized Cooper pairs at around 220 K in a correlated oxide, with distortion of the RuO6 octahedra as the tuning parameter, would be striking and broadly interesting. At present, though, the bosonic interpretation rests entirely on the functional form of zero-field R(T) over limited temperature windows. There is no independent evidence that the charge carriers are pairs.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The material system is interesting. Nanoflakes of Ca2RuO4 conduct 3 to 4 orders of magnitude better than the bulk Mott insulator, and the paper places them on a common plot with literature data for doping, pressure and field tuning (Fig. 4b).
- Several samples from two growth routes (exfoliated flakes and MBE films) are compared. The MBE films come with X-ray lattice parameters (Supplementary Table S1).
- The beta-function framework (Eqs. 1-4) is laid out clearly, and the sign of beta' is proposed as a simple operational criterion for telling sigma ~ ln T apart from rho ~ ln(1/T).
- The Fermi glass to VRH crossover temperatures obtained from the two analyses in Fig. 2a and 2b agree with each other, which is a reasonable internal consistency check.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. No measurement tests whether the carriers are pairs. Every Bose glass assignment (Figs. 3, 5, 6) comes from zero-field R(T). Weak localization of charge-2e bosons has signatures that weak localization or interaction corrections of electrons do not share:
   - a magnetoconductance whose characteristic field is set by the superconducting flux quantum h/2e;
   - a large positive magnetoresistance in the Bose glass regime that turns over at a field related to pair breaking;
   - a field-tuned insulator transition with a crossing point.

   Without magnetic-field data, the statements in the abstract and conclusion that "signatures of Cooper pairs persist up to ~220 K" are not established. The authors should measure R(T, B) on samples 4 and 5 (and ideally on 1-3 as fermionic references) with B perpendicular and parallel to the film, from about 220 K down through the superconducting onset. They should show that the magnetoresistance changes qualitatively at the claimed Fermi glass to Bose glass temperature (about 220 K for sample 4, 183 K for sample 5), and that it does not change in samples 1-3 at the same temperatures.

2. The data do not convincingly distinguish rho ~ ln(1/T) from sigma ~ ln T. The two forms differ only at second order in the logarithmic correction.
   - Sample 5 (Fig. 3b) changes resistance by only about 25% (roughly 32 to 40 kOhm) across the 105-183 K Bose glass window. Over a factor of 1.7 in T, a straight line in R vs ln T and a straight line in G vs ln T are nearly indistinguishable at that level.
   - For sample 4 (Fig. 3a), the text says the data "align well" with R ~ ln(1/T) "rather than" G ~ ln T, but no residuals, chi-squared, or fit-window sensitivity are given.
   - The boundaries of the Bose glass windows (dashed lines in Fig. 3) are chosen by eye, and the fit ranges quoted (101-220 K and 105-183 K) define the very crossover temperatures that are then plotted in Fig. 4a.

   The authors should fit both forms, plus a power law and an Altshuler-Aronov form, over identical windows and report the residuals and goodness of fit. They should show that the preferred form is robust when the window edges move, and quote uncertainties on T^{BG-FG}.

3. The "vertical flow" in the beta function is presented as an "independent criterion" (Fig. 5 caption). In fact it carries the same information as the ln T plots, and it can arise trivially.
   - Eqs. (2)-(4) show that the sign of beta' is just the curvature of R(T) on a logarithmic temperature axis. It is not independent of Fig. 3.
   - More importantly, any sample with a resistance maximum gives d ln g / d ln T = 0 at the maximum. Near that point beta sweeps through zero while g barely changes, which draws a vertical line in beta versus ln g whatever the transport mechanism. Samples 4 and 5 have such maxima near 100 K (Fig. 1c), and the vertical segments in Fig. 5b extend to positive beta. Positive beta corresponds to dR/dT > 0, which is the superconducting-fluctuation side of the maximum, not the Bose glass window.
   - The signs also look inconsistent. In the claimed Bose glass regime R rises on cooling, so with beta = -(2/p) d ln g / d ln T the beta function should be negative. Fig. 6a, however, appears to show sample 4 at positive beta (about +0.3 to +0.5) across 100-270 K. Estimating from Fig. 3a (R_layer goes from about 60 to 120 kOhm as ln T goes from 5.6 to 4.6) gives beta of about -1.4.

   The authors should clarify which temperature range each vertical segment in Fig. 5b comes from, and mark the resistance maximum on it. They should reconcile the sign and magnitude of beta for samples 4 and 5 in Fig. 6a with Fig. 3. They should also show that the vertical flow persists when only the data above the resistance maximum are used.

4. The evidence for superconductivity, and for Cooper pairs above T_c^onset, in these specific devices is limited. Fig. 1c shows a drop to near zero for samples 4 and 5, but the paper does not show:
   - the I-V characteristics and critical current;
   - the field dependence of the transition;
   - the resistance floor on a linear scale with noise level;
   - any diamagnetic response from these devices.

   The supporting magnetization argument (p. 2) relies on unshown powder data and on ref. 34, which has been retracted and is attributed to Joule heating. The MBE films (samples 6 and 7) never reach zero resistance, yet they are still placed in the Bose glass region. The authors should present the superconducting characterization of samples 4 and 5 in the paper or the SI: R(T) at several fields, I-V curves, and ideally a local or device-level diamagnetic measurement. They should remove the magnetization paragraph or replace it with data on the measured material.

5. Several mundane explanations would produce the same R(T) shapes and are not ruled out:
   - Phase inhomogeneity. The starting crystals contain about 5% CaRuO3 (a metal) and about 6% S-Pbca phase (Methods). The nanoflakes are 3 to 4 orders of magnitude less resistive than bulk, so a small fraction of metallic or less-distorted regions percolating through the flake is a natural alternative. A granular metal or granular superconductor (Efetov-type sigma ~ ln T, or Josephson-coupled grains) gives logarithmic R(T), a resistance maximum, and incomplete or broad superconducting transitions.
   - Oxygen off-stoichiometry (Ca2RuO4+delta is metallic).
   - Surface damage from sonication in dichloroethane and from electron-beam lithography.

   CaRuO3 is distinguishable from Ca2RuO4 in SEM only by crystal shape (Methods), which does not exclude intergrowths. The authors should provide structural and chemical characterization of the measured flakes themselves, for example TEM or electron diffraction, Raman spectroscopy (which is sensitive to octahedral distortion in Ca2RuO4), and EDX or EELS for stoichiometry. They should also discuss explicitly why a percolating mixed-phase picture cannot account for Figs. 1-3.

B. Should address

6. The central structural claim has no structural evidence for the nanoflakes. The claim is that thinning relieves the RuO6 tilt, rotation and flattening and that this, "rather than ... impurity-induced disorder", sets the ground state (abstract, Fig. 1a schematic, p. 4, conclusion).
   - The only lattice parameters given for the exfoliated material (a = 5.350, b = 5.343, c = 12.278 Å) are powder data on the parent bulk crystals, which match the bulk L phase.
   - The MBE lattice parameters are for epitaxially strained films on LaAlO3, and the text concedes that "X-ray diffraction measurements do not directly determine the octahedral geometry."
   - Disorder is dismissed but never quantified.

   Without a structural or disorder metric for each device, the vertical axis of Fig. 4a cannot be attributed to octahedral distortion. The authors should either measure distortion on the devices (for example by Raman phonon shifts, or STEM on cross-sections) or soften the causal language.

7. The phase diagram in Fig. 4a is partly circular, and its axis depends on geometry.
   - Both coordinates come from the same R(T) curve. The horizontal coordinate is a crossover temperature read off the ln T fits, and the vertical coordinate is |rho(120 K) - rho(280 K)|/dT.
   - That vertical metric is an absolute resistivity difference, so it scales with any error in thickness (estimated from tilted SEM images) and with the contact geometry.
   - Sample 9 appears only in Fig. 4a, with no R(T) data shown anywhere.
   - Each phase is represented by two or three devices. Device-to-device spread at nominally equal thickness, and reproducibility after thermal cycling, are not shown.

   The authors should use a dimensionless measure such as rho(120 K)/rho(280 K), state thickness uncertainties for each sample, and include sample 9's data. They should also report how many devices were measured in total, and how many showed each behavior.

8. The "quantum critical resistances" are not determined the way critical points normally are.
   - R^{BG-FG} = 65.7 kOhm is taken where beta' changes sign (beta_c = -1.6), which is a crossover temperature in a single sample. It is not the crossing point of isotherms across a tuning parameter, and no finite-size or temperature scaling collapse (with exponent zv) is shown.
   - The value depends on the per-layer normalization (layer count inferred from the SEM thickness), on the choice p = 1, and on which sample is used. From Fig. 3a, sample 4's R_layer at about 220 K appears closer to 80 kOhm than to 65.7 kOhm.
   - 65.7 kOhm also differs from (5/2)h/e^2 = 64.5 kOhm, and the paper offers two unrelated explanations for the factor of 2.5: 8/pi from ref. 42 and the 5/2 Pfaffian state.

   The authors should state which sample and temperature gives each critical resistance, propagate the thickness and layer-count uncertainty, show whether different samples give the same value, and either provide a scaling analysis or describe these as characteristic crossover resistances. The Pfaffian analogy should be removed or clearly marked as a numerical coincidence.

9. The assumptions behind Eq. (1) are not justified for this temperature range.
   - Setting p = 1 (electron-electron dephasing) is assumed for 100-300 K, where electron-phonon dephasing (p = 2-3) usually dominates in oxides. Since beta scales as 1/p, both the absolute beta values in Figs. 5 and 6 and the "critical" beta_c depend on this choice.
   - The variable g is defined as (h/e^2)sigma. For samples 1-3, sigma is a 3D conductivity in S/cm, so ln g has units and is offset arbitrarily relative to samples 4 and 5, which use sheet conductance per layer. This undermines the claimed collapse onto the Vollhardt-Wölfle curve in Fig. 5a.
   - Whether 25-35 nm films are 2D on the scale of the dephasing length at 200 K is not discussed.

   The authors should state exactly which conductance (sheet, per layer, or 3D) enters g for each sample, show how Figs. 5 and 6 change with p, and estimate the dephasing length.

10. The size of the logarithmic corrections is not compared with theory. For fermionic weak localization, sigma_1 per layer should be of order (alpha p) e^2/(pi h). For bosonic localization (ref. 14), the coefficient rho_1 has a corresponding expected scale. Fitted values of sigma_1 and rho_1 per layer, in quantum units, are not reported. If the slopes were many times larger than the quantum correction, a weak-localization interpretation of either kind would be in doubt. The authors should tabulate sigma_0, sigma_1, rho_0 and rho_1 for all samples in units of e^2/h and compare them with the theoretical prefactors.

11. The ~220 K scale appears in two places. The Fermi glass to Mott/VRH crossover of samples 1-3 (224, 233 and 208 K) and the Fermi glass to Bose glass crossover of sample 4 (about 220 K) fall at essentially the same temperature. This hints at a common origin unrelated to bosons, such as a structural or orbital crossover of the lattice (which these flakes inherit from the Ca2RuO4 phase diagram), or an extrinsic feature of the substrate or setup. The authors should discuss this coincidence, and check for anomalies near 220 K in the lattice (temperature-dependent XRD or Raman on the MBE films).

12. Fig. 6b (current-induced transition) is open to heating and is internally inconsistent.
   - Ca2RuO4 is notorious for current-driven metallization and Joule heating (refs. 34 and 35). Bias currents up to 5000 nA through a 10 nm flake at kOhm to 100 kOhm resistances can raise the electron temperature substantially. The separatrix of a family of R(T) curves at different currents is not a quantum critical point.
   - The low-current curves are plotted against a right-hand axis of 2000-6000 Ohm. Yet Fig. 3a gives sample 4 an R_layer of up to about 125 kOhm, and it is unclear whether Fig. 6b mixes total and per-layer resistance.
   - The measurement current used for Figs. 1-3 is not stated.

   The authors should state the bias current for all R(T) data, estimate the dissipated power and temperature rise, show I-V curves, and harmonize the axes of Fig. 6b with Fig. 3a.

C. Minor

13. The paragraph citing ref. 34 (retracted) together with unpublished powder magnetization should be removed, or the data should be shown with sample mass, field and background subtraction.
14. "Fermi glass" conventionally denotes an Anderson-localized insulator with a finite density of localized states. Applying it to a weakly localized sigma ~ ln T conductor is non-standard, and the usage should be defined.
15. In the strong-localization limit, 2D Mott VRH gives beta ~ (2/3) ln g, not the slope of 1 of the Vollhardt-Wölfle curve. The authors should comment on how closely the samples 1-3 and bulk data in Fig. 5a actually follow the reference curve.
16. Fig. 1a's legend and caption list samples 1-5 and 8 as "nanoscale" films, but sample 8 is 300 nm thick. Fig. 1b says "samples 1-7". The figure title "Ca2RuO4nanofilms" is missing a space. Fig. 4a includes sample 9, which is not introduced.
17. The abstract states 220 K as the Bose glass onset without noting that sample 5 gives 183 K and the MBE films give 67-81 K. Please quote the range.
18. Methods: please give device images, contact layout and the dimensions used to convert R to rho and to R per layer. Please also give the thickness uncertainty from the tilted-SEM method, the thermometer location, and the excitation current.
19. Making the raw R(T) data available in a repository, rather than "upon reasonable request", would let readers repeat the fit comparisons in concerns 2 and 3.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add magnetotransport across the claimed Fermi glass to Bose glass crossover for samples 4 and 5, with samples 1-3 as fermionic controls. This is the single most decisive experiment for the bosonic interpretation.
2. Replace the by-eye functional-form assignments with a quantitative model comparison (residuals and window sensitivity), and report the logarithmic prefactors in units of e^2/h.
3. Characterize the measured flakes structurally (Raman or TEM for octahedral distortion and phase purity, and stoichiometry), so that the claim that distortion rather than disorder sets the ground state has direct support.
4. Recast the "quantum critical resistances" as characteristic crossover resistances unless a scaling collapse across samples or a tuning parameter can be shown, and drop the Pfaffian analogy.
