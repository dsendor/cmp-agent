Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports resistance-versus-temperature data on exfoliated Ca2RuO4 "nanofilm" crystals (samples 1-5, 8), MBE films on LaAlO3 (samples 6, 7) and digitised bulk data. It claims that samples which go superconducting at low temperature (4 and 5) show rho ~ ln(1/T) between about 100 and 220 K. The authors read this, together with an upturn ("vertical flow") in the beta-function, as weak localization of preformed Cooper pairs, a "Bose glass", persisting up to about 220 K. They also extract two critical sheet resistances, both about 5/2 times the usual quantum values, and propose that thinning tunes electronic correlation by relieving RuO6 octahedral distortion. If the claims held, Cooper pairs near 220 K in a ruthenate would be highly significant. As presented, however, the conclusion rests on telling apart two logarithmic forms over a narrow temperature window, in a few samples, without the field-dependent, structural or thermodynamic evidence such a claim needs.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Ca2RuO4 thin crystals with resistivity three to four orders of magnitude below bulk, some going superconducting (building on Ref. [20]), are an intriguing system. Systematic transport data on them are of interest.
- The paper applies a simple, published analysis (the sign of the temperature derivative of beta, Eqs. 2-4, following Ref. [11]) uniformly to several sample types, including MBE films and literature bulk data.
- Figure 4(b) gathers literature data on Ca2RuO4 under doping, pressure, strain and field in one plot, which some readers may find useful.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The rho ~ ln(1/T) versus sigma ~ ln T distinction is not established statistically or physically over the windows used. In Fig. 3(a) the "BG" window for sample 4 spans 101-220 K, i.e. Delta(ln T) of about 0.8. Over this window R changes by about 50%, so 1/R and R differ only by weak curvature. For sample 5 (Fig. 3(b)) the window 105-183 K is Delta(ln T) of about 0.55, and the data scatter visibly. The "FG" windows are narrower still: about 220-300 K (Delta(ln T) of about 0.3) for samples 4 and 5, and about 210-300 K for samples 1-3 (Fig. 2(b)). Over such ranges almost any smooth monotonic function is indistinguishable from a straight line in either variable. The paper gives no goodness of fit, residuals, fit uncertainties or objective criterion for the window boundaries, which appear to be chosen by eye at the points where one fit departs from the other. Physically, quantum interference corrections between 100 and 300 K are hard to sustain, because the dephasing length there is expected to be comparable to microscopic lengths. Phonon scattering, thermally activated carriers, parallel conduction through secondary phases, electron-electron interaction (Altshuler-Aronov) corrections and Kondo-like magnetic scattering are all candidate sources of logarithmic or near-logarithmic behaviour. Magnetic scattering is especially relevant given the 180 K ferromagnetic transition the authors cite from Ref. [20]. None of these is considered. The authors should:
   - report chi-squared or residuals for both fits, with and without a phonon or background term;
   - show that the preferred form holds over at least a decade in T, or is robust to moving the window;
   - explain why weak-localization physics should apply at 100-300 K in this material;
   - estimate the dephasing length.

2. There is no direct evidence that Cooper pairs exist between Tc_onset and about 220 K. The headline claim (abstract, p. 5, Conclusion) is that localized Cooper pairs and moving vortices exist up to about 220 K in sample 4 and 183 K in sample 5. The only support is the functional form of R(T) and the beta' sign. The supporting diamagnetism (p. 5) is neither shown nor quantified. It is attributed to Ref. [20] at about 140 K, not 220 K. It is argued alongside Ref. [27], which has been retracted, and Ref. [28], which attributes the 220 K diamagnetism to Joule heating. The measurement that would test a bosonic, vortex-mediated resistive state directly is missing: magnetoresistance in the 100-220 K window and its comparison with the 220-300 K window. A pair-based mechanism predicts a characteristic field dependence, for example positive magnetoresistance scaling with a pair-breaking or vortex field. Fermionic weak localization predicts negative magnetoresistance with a well-defined Hikami-Larkin-Nagaoka form. Other useful tests:
   - Nernst signal or diamagnetic susceptibility of the same flakes, with a measured temperature dependence;
   - the field dependence of the "vertical flow" temperature;
   - current-voltage linearity in the putative BG window.
   Without at least one of these, the 220 K Cooper-pair statement should be withdrawn or explicitly stated as a speculation.

3. The "vertical flow" and the sign of beta' are generic features of a resistance maximum, not specific to bosons. By Eqs. (1)-(2), beta = -2 d ln g / d ln T. In samples 4 and 5 this changes sign and diverges where R(T) has its maximum near Tc_onset (Fig. 1(c), about 80-110 K). The sign of beta' depends only on the curvature of R against ln T. Any resistance that rises on cooling and then turns over because of superconducting fluctuations will produce beta' < 0 and an upturn in Figs. 5(b) and 6(a), whether or not localized bosons exist. Aslamazov-Larkin and Maki-Thompson fluctuation conductivity alone produces exactly this curvature above Tc_onset. A crossover between two conduction channels does too. The paper also assumes L^2 = D T^(-p) with p = 1 fixed (p. 7), which ties length scale to temperature in a way that is not justified at these temperatures. The authors should:
   - subtract a fluctuation-conductivity contribution (with the fitted Tc and normal-state background stated) and show whether beta' < 0 survives;
   - show how the result depends on p;
   - show a non-superconducting sample with a resistance maximum of similar shape as a null case for "vertical flow".
   The MBE samples 6 and 7 never superconduct yet are also assigned beta' < 0 (Fig. 6(a)). This needs a separate explanation: their beta values hardly vary with T in Fig. 6(a), and their arrow in Fig. 5(b) points left rather than up.

4. The two critical sheet resistances, and their 5/2 ratio, depend on undefined conversions and on choices made by eye. Four problems:
   - (a) "Sheet resistance per layer" is never defined: which interlayer spacing, how many layers, and whether parallel conduction of all layers is assumed. The quoted critical values scale directly with this choice, so a common factor of 2.5 for both critical points could come from the conversion.
   - (b) For sample 4, Fig. 3(a) shows R_sq/layer peaking near 125 kOhm, while Fig. 6(b) shows the same sample peaking near 41 kOhm, with an unexplained right-hand axis "R (Ohm)" running to 6000. These cannot both be correct, and the 65.7 kOhm and 16.5 kOhm values are read off Fig. 6(b).
   - (c) beta_c = -1.6 is a horizontal line drawn between groups of samples in Fig. 6(a), not a separatrix found from the data. The text then calls it "g_c = -1.6" and converts it to 65.7 kOhm without showing how. With g = (hbar/e^2) sigma, R = 65.7 kOhm corresponds to g of about 0.063 and ln g of about -2.8, so the link between a beta value and a resistance needs to be stated.
   - (d) The SC-BG value of 16.5 kOhm (16.8 kOhm in the text) is taken from a family of curves tuned by bias current up to 5 microamps in Fig. 6(b). Current-driven transitions and Joule heating are well documented in Ca2RuO4 (Refs. [19, 20, 28]). Current is not a thermodynamic tuning parameter, and the flat low-T curves at 65.7 kOhm and 16.5 kOhm may simply be heating or depinning plateaus.

   Also, 65.7/25.8 is about 2.55 and 16.5/6.45 is about 2.56, closer to Fisher's 8/pi than to 5/2, so the Pfaffian analogy is not supported by the numbers. The authors should define the conversion, reconcile Figs. 3(a) and 6(b), and derive beta_c from a crossing or scaling analysis. They should show critical resistances from a thermodynamic tuning parameter (thickness series, gate or magnetic field), check heating (estimated current density and power, and the sample-temperature rise at each current), and quote uncertainties.

5. The proposed mechanism, thinning relieving RuO6 distortion, is not supported by structural data, and phase purity is not demonstrated.
   - Distortion: no structural characterisation of the measured flakes is given (no Raman, TEM, electron diffraction or lattice parameters of individual flakes). The quoted "nanoscale single crystal" c = 12.278 Angstrom appears to come from XRD on the parent material.
   - Thickness: thickness does not order the ground state. Sample 4 (10 nm) and sample 5 (30 nm) superconduct, samples 1-3 (25, 25, 35 nm) do not, and the 300 nm sample 8 is still about three orders of magnitude less resistive than bulk (Fig. 1(a)). The figure title "Thickness-tuned superconductor-insulator transition" is therefore not supported.
   - MBE films: by the authors' own argument the MBE films have a shorter c-axis and stronger distortion (p. 6, Methods), yet they show "Bose glass". This contradicts the stated rule that weak distortion produces bosons.
   - Phase purity: CaRuO3 is excluded only by crystal shape in SEM. Metallic CaRuO3, Ru metal inclusions and oxygen non-stoichiometry are common in Ca2RuO4, and any of them could explain both the dramatically lowered resistivity and inhomogeneous or filamentary superconductivity. The statement that disorder "cannot explain this phenomenon" (p. 6, Conclusion) is asserted, not shown.

   The authors should provide:
   - Raman or TEM, with EDS, on the measured flakes, or on flakes from the same batch, to show phase and octahedral distortion;
   - a resistivity check against CaRuO3 reference values;
   - a thickness series measured under identical conditions.

B. Should address

6. The phase diagram in Fig. 4(a) is partly circular, and some of its contents are undocumented. The vertical axis, d rho/dT = (rho(120 K) - rho(280 K))/dT, is computed from the same R(T) curves whose features define the transition temperatures plotted against it, so the trend "smaller d rho/dT favours BG/SC" is partly built in. The quantity is a finite difference divided by "dT" (presumably 160 K) and its sign convention is unclear (the panel says d rho/dT < 0 while plotting |d rho/dT|). Sample 9 appears in Fig. 4(a) but is not described anywhere else, sample 8 does not appear in Fig. 4, and the shaded phase boundaries are drawn with no data points defining most of them. The bulk point at 354 K is a structural transition, not a Fermi glass-Mott transition. The authors should define the axis precisely, document sample 9, mark which boundaries are guides to the eye, and use an independent measure of localization or correlation (for example rho(300 K) normalised to the Mott value, or a structural parameter).

7. The number of samples is small and reproducibility is not shown. The central Bose-glass claim rests on two superconducting flakes, samples 4 and 5, and the critical resistances on one, sample 4. There is no information on how many flakes were measured in total and how many showed each behaviour. Contact geometry and the uncertainty of resistivity in irregular flakes, the uncertainty of the SEM-tilt thickness, the excitation current and ac/dc mode for Figs. 1-3, and thermal cycling or ageing are also not reported. The authors should give these numbers, and show at least one more superconducting flake reproducing the ln(1/T) window and the beta' sign.

8. The VRH assignment for samples 1-3 is not tested against alternatives. Figure 2(a) fits 2D Mott VRH, exp[(T0/T)^(1/3)], to 25-35 nm films over about 23-125 K. Sample 1 departs from the fit at low T. Neither T0 nor the hopping length is given, and 3D VRH, Efros-Shklovskii and Arrhenius forms are not compared. Calling these films "Mott insulators" on the basis of VRH also mixes two different physics (correlation-driven Mott gap versus Anderson-localized hopping). The authors should give T0, check the hopping-length-to-thickness ratio that justifies 2D, and compare the alternative exponents with a derivative (Zabrodskii) analysis.

9. The universal collapse in Fig. 5(a) needs a stated method. The paper does not say how g is computed for bulk crystals (Nakatsuji data in Ohm cm) and for 25-300 nm films, which are not 2D, so that all data can be plotted against one 2D Vollhardt-Wolfle curve. For a VRH conductance, beta is linear in ln g with slope 2/3 by construction, so agreement in the strongly localized region is partly built in. The authors should give the conversion for each data set, show the expected slope, and state what agreement or disagreement would have meant.

10. The terminology departs from standard usage in ways that affect interpretation. In the Fisher framework the Bose glass is an insulating, compressible phase with zero T = 0 conductivity. The abstract's "finite conductivity" and "bosonic order" follow the Das-Doniach usage of weakly localized bosons. "Mott insulator" in boson SIT language also differs from the fermionic Mott insulator of Ca2RuO4. Readers will be misled unless the paper states which definitions it uses and what experimental property distinguishes the phases. In particular, the Bose glass in Fig. 4(a) is described as a phase with finite resistance at T > 0, so it is not clear what it would be at T = 0.

11. The supplementary Aslamazov-Larkin analysis of digitised 2.6 GPa bulk data (Fig. S2) does not support the main claims and is internally inconsistent. The 2D AL formula requires a sheet resistance, but R_N is given as 44 microOhm cm. Tc0 = 12 K and R_N are fixed by hand. A ferromagnetic-metal resistance drop is not excluded, and that is the interpretation of the original Ref. [19]. Either remove the analysis, or redo it with correct units, free parameters, and a comparison against a normal-state (ferromagnetic-metal) fit.

C. Minor

12. The SC-BG critical resistance is 16.8 kOhm in the text (p. 8) and 16.5 kOhm in Fig. 6(b) and its caption. Please reconcile.

13. The Fig. 6(a) caption calls beta_c = -1.6 the critical value "between Bose glass and Mott insulating phases", the text calls it the BG-FG transition, and the abstract calls it BG-Mott. Use one assignment throughout.

14. The Fig. 3 caption says sample 5 "fits well with the R ~ ln T fitting"; this should be ln(1/T).

15. The text (p. 6) gives Bose-glass onsets of 67 K and 81 K for samples 6 and 7, but the dashed lines in Fig. 3(c) and Fig. S1 are both at ln T of about 4.2 (about 67 K). Please check.

16. p. 8: "estimated from g_c = -1.6" should refer to beta_c, and the conversion should be given (see concern 4).

17. Eq. (1) defines g with hbar/e^2, while the critical resistances are quoted in units of h/e^2 and h/4e^2. Keep the factors of 2 pi consistent when relating beta_c to resistance.

18. Sample 8 appears in Figs. 1 and 5 but nowhere in the analysis. Either discuss it or remove it.

19. Typos and wording: "Supplementaty" (p. 6); "more assertive" (p. 9, presumably "more pronounced"); "fermi glass"; unequal spacing in "Ca2RuO4 is".

20. The Pfaffian analogy (p. 8) has no physical link to this system and should be removed or justified.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add magnetoresistance (both field orientations) across the 100-300 K range for samples 4 and 5, and for an insulating flake. This is the most direct way to tell bosonic from fermionic localization in the window where the paper claims the crossover.
2. Measure a controlled thickness series from a single parent crystal, with structural characterisation (Raman or TEM) of each measured flake. This would put the distortion-relief mechanism on an experimental footing and make the phase diagram depend on an independent variable.
3. Redo the logarithmic fits and beta-function analysis with stated statistics, after subtracting superconducting-fluctuation and phonon contributions, and present the window-sensitivity of the conclusions.
4. Retitle or reframe the 220 K Cooper-pair statement and the 5/2 quantum-resistance claim as hypotheses, unless the additional measurements above support them.
