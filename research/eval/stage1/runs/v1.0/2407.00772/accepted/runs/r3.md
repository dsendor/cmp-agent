Review outline:

Note on scope: this review is based on the eight-page article PDF supplied. The Methods and Supplementary Notes it cites (Supplementary Notes 1, 2, 7, 8 and 9, Supplementary Equations (1) and (9), Supplementary Figs. 5, 9, 12 and 17) were not available to me. Where a concern may already be answered there, I say so; in those cases the authors should bring the key numbers into the main text.

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use few-femtosecond broadband XUV transient absorption (UBXAS) at the Ti M2,3 and Se M4,5 edges of 1T-TiSe2. They follow the initial build-up time, τ_int, of Se 4p holes, read out at Se M5 peak 3, as a function of fluence and temperature. From this they make two claims. First, a 1/√F scaling of τ_int both below and above Tc shows that short-range excitonic correlations persist in the normal state. Second, at low fluence τ_int has a sharp minimum at Tc, which they interpret as a diverging excitonic susceptibility. A bulk-sensitive, element-resolved probe of excitonic fluctuations above Tc would matter for the long-running exciton-versus-Jahn-Teller debate in TiSe2, and the instrument is impressive. However, both headline inferences rest on an indirect, model-dependent reading of a single timescale. The paper needs stronger controls and a quantitative consistency check before the conclusions follow.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The technique is new and capable. Few-fs, broadband, cryogenic XUV transient absorption covers both Ti and Se edges at once, with the static spectral assignments backed by first-principles and TDDFT calculations (Fig. 1).
- The choice of Se peak 3 as a reporter is well reasoned. It lacks A1g phonon modulation (Fig. 2e), which removes a major confound in reading out early-time electronic dynamics.
- The data set is systematic. It covers fluence (about 0.2 to 3 mJ cm^-2) at three temperatures and temperature (about 23 to 350 K) at two fluences, and the low- and high-fluence trends cross-check each other (Fig. 4d,e).
- The static temperature dependence in Fig. 3d,e shows a clear order-parameter-like onset at Tc. This establishes that the XUV observables are sensitive to the transition.
- The question addressed, excitonic correlations above Tc where ARPES and tr-ARPES lose contrast, is important and currently open.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The high-fluence data contradict the model of Eq. (1). Eq. (1) writes n_tot ∝ F[χ0 + χex(T)], and the paper uses τ_int = τ0 + A/√n_tot with τ0 = 25 fs (Fig. 4d). Under this model the enhancement at Tc is multiplicative in F, so it should appear at high fluence too. A rough estimate from Fig. 4e:
   - At low fluence, the variable part of τ_int falls from about 165 fs (190 fs at 23 K) to about 45 fs (about 70 fs at 190 K). That implies n_tot rises roughly 13-fold at Tc.
   - The same factor applied to the high-fluence data (about 75 fs at 23 K, variable part about 50 fs) predicts τ_int of about 25 + 14, or roughly 40 fs, at Tc.
   - The measured high-fluence τ_int stays flat at about 70 to 80 fs at all temperatures (red points and line in Fig. 4e).

   The data fit a floor of about 70 fs much better than the stated τ0 = 25 fs. The same floor shows up in Fig. 4d, where no data point at any temperature or fluence falls below about 65 fs. On this reading, the "dip" at Tc is the low-fluence τ_int reaching the same floor that high fluence reaches. That is a saturation effect, and with a floor in place the divergence exponent α and the claim of a diverging χex cannot be determined.

   The authors should:
   - fit all of Fig. 4d,e together with one model, giving τ0, A, α and Tc as free or fixed and quoting their uncertainties;
   - state the fitted α and Tc in the main text (they are currently only implied by the blue curve in Fig. 4e);
   - explain the origin of the about 70 fs floor and compare it with the IRF in Supplementary Fig. 5;
   - show that the data favour a diverging susceptibility over saturation of a non-divergent quantity.

2. The 1/√F scaling has not been shown to be specific to excitonic correlations. The argument (p. 1792) is that τ_int ∝ 1/ω_pl ∝ 1/√n_tot reflects screening-driven exciton break-up. But many density-dependent processes speed up with carrier density in a similar way: carrier–carrier scattering and thermalization, screening-limited relaxation of hot carriers into the 4p hole states, and Coulomb-driven intraband redistribution. None of these need pre-existing electron–hole pairing. Two further problems:
   - The 23 K and 300 K data fall on the same curve (Fig. 4d), even though the equilibrium state differs qualitatively. This sits uneasily with the claim that the scaling diagnoses the ordered versus the fluctuating state.
   - Over the roughly one decade of fluence covered, 1/√F with a free offset is hard to tell apart from other monotonic forms such as 1/F, a logarithm, or a power law with a free exponent.

   The authors should:
   - fit with a free exponent and report it with its uncertainty at each temperature;
   - include a null reference, either a closely related compound or condition where excitonic correlations are expected to be absent or strongly suppressed, measured at the same edge under matched fluences. Examples are a heavily electron-doped or intercalated TiSe2 (such as Cu_xTiSe2 beyond the CDW dome), or a non-excitonic isostructural dichalcogenide with a comparable Se or chalcogen 4p reporter state;
   - discuss at least one non-excitonic screening mechanism quantitatively. Supplementary Note 8 is said to exclude alternatives; its main conclusions and the specific mechanisms considered should be summarized in the main text.

   Without these, "short-range excitonic correlations above Tc" (abstract, Fig. 4g) is one possible interpretation, not a demonstrated one.

3. The minimum at Tc rests on very few temperature points, and non-excitonic causes of a temperature-dependent n_tot have not been excluded. The low-fluence curve in Fig. 4e has about eight temperatures. One point, at about 190 K, defines the minimum, and its neighbours at about 150 K and 250 K carry error bars of about ±15 to 20 fs. The minimum is therefore set by a single point near Tc. At least three equilibrium properties change across Tc and could change n_tot at fixed incident fluence without any excitonic susceptibility:
   - **Absorbed fluence.** The optical absorption and reflectivity at 750 nm (1.65 eV) change across Tc, so the absorbed dose changes even when the incident fluence is fixed.
   - **Equilibrium carriers.** The density of thermally excited free carriers, which also screen, changes strongly with temperature in TiSe2, as the known resistivity anomaly near 150 to 200 K shows.
   - **Soft modes.** The CDW amplitude mode softens toward Tc and the lattice fluctuations grow there (refs. 40, 44). These can speed up carrier redistribution.

   The phonon argument in the Discussion (20% renormalization giving a 20% change in τ_int) addresses only the fluence-induced hardening of one phonon at low temperature. It does not address softening near Tc.

   The authors should:
   - add temperatures within about ±20 K of Tc at low fluence, ideally in a separate run or on a second sample;
   - normalize to absorbed fluence using measured or literature temperature-dependent optical constants at 750 nm;
   - estimate and discuss the contributions of equilibrium carrier density and soft-mode fluctuations to τ_int near Tc.

B. Should address

4. The effective sample temperature at high fluence is not given. Pumping at 2 mJ cm^-2 at the experiment's repetition rate can raise the steady-state temperature appreciably, and the 350 K high-fluence point had to be taken at 1.1 mJ cm^-2 because the sample degraded (Fig. 4 caption). Both points suggest heating is not negligible. If the true lattice temperature at high fluence is offset by tens of kelvin, the comparison of high- and low-fluence traces at the same nominal temperature (Fig. 4c–e), and the location of Tc in each data set, become uncertain. The authors should:
   - give the repetition rate, the sample thickness, the substrate or membrane and its thermal anchoring;
   - give an estimate of the steady-state temperature rise at each fluence, ideally checked directly, for example by the onset of the Fig. 3e signal measured at high fluence;
   - state whether degradation was checked by re-measuring a reference condition after the high-fluence runs.

5. The integration window in Fig. 3c may mix a peak shift with state filling. The 25 K and 310 K traces in Fig. 3c have a dispersive, derivative-like shape, with a positive lobe near 53.4 eV and a negative lobe near 53.1 eV. That pattern suggests an edge shift or broadening, for example from core-hole screening or band renormalization, rather than a pure loss of absorption from Se 4p hole filling. The window [53.04, 53.33] eV sits on the slope of this structure. A shift and a hole population can have different fluence and time dependences, so τ_int extracted from the integrated window may not be the 4p hole build-up time the interpretation assumes. The authors should:
   - decompose the transient lineshape at peak 3 into amplitude, shift and width components as a function of delay, fluence and temperature;
   - show that τ_int from the amplitude component alone reproduces Fig. 4d,e.

   The text says Supplementary Note 7 tests robustness to the window; the decomposition is a different test and is still needed.

6. Number of samples and reproducibility. The main text does not say how many crystals or flakes were measured, whether the Fig. 4e minimum was reproduced on more than one sample or in more than one temperature sweep, or whether results survived thermal cycling. Because the central claim rests on one point near Tc (concern 3), reproducing it on at least two samples is essential. Please also state sample thickness and how thin specimens were prepared for transmission. Thickness inhomogeneity and strain in exfoliated flakes can broaden or shift Tc, and that bears directly on where the minimum falls.

7. How τ_int is defined and fitted. The "initial response time" comes from Supplementary Equation (1), which is not shown in the main text. Its relation to the IRF of a few fs (Fig. 4a, Supplementary Fig. 5), the treatment of time zero, and whether the error bars include scan-to-scan variation or only the fit covariance are not stated. With τ_int near about 70 fs and coherent oscillations in neighbouring windows with a period of about 167 fs, these choices matter. The authors should:
   - state the fit function, the free parameters and the fit window in the main text or Methods;
   - give error bars that include repeat-scan spread;
   - show that a model-independent measure, such as the 10–90% rise time or the time to half-maximum, reproduces the trends of Fig. 4d,e.

8. The low-fluence sign reversal in the Ti many-body continuum is left unexplained. Fig. 2b,d presents this sign reversal as evidence of "an additional dynamical process that is distinct from simple carrier excitation" that is possibly excitonic. The paper then never explains it or links it to the Se peak 3 analysis. The low-fluence window (ii) signal is about 0.1 × 10^-3, close to the noise in Fig. 2d. The authors should:
   - show its fluence dependence and the threshold fluence at which the sign changes;
   - show its temperature dependence across Tc;
   - either use it as independent support for the excitonic picture or soften the statement.

9. Short-range correlations versus generic Coulomb effects above Tc. Even granting that the 300 K response depends on screening, the paper does not separate "short-range excitonic correlations", meaning incipient pairing at the CDW wavevector, from ordinary electron–hole Coulomb attraction, which is present in any semimetal or small-gap semiconductor and is screened by photocarriers. Some momentum-selective or wavevector-specific evidence would make the claim more specific. Examples are diffuse scattering at the M point, the backfolded spectral weight above Tc in Supplementary Fig. 9, or a link to the correlation length. Fig. 4g implies such a correlation length, which could be estimated from the magnitude of χex(300 K).

C. Minor

10. Fig. 3d,e: the error bars are the standard deviation over 30 spectra taken during a continuous 5 K ramp, so they mix noise with the real temperature change within each bin. Please state the ramp rate and the thermometry position relative to the sample, and estimate the thermal lag between thermometer and sample.

11. The 350 K high-fluence point in Fig. 4e was taken at 1.1 mJ cm^-2 rather than 2.0 mJ cm^-2, yet it is plotted with the high-fluence set and included in the "high-fluence average". Please mark it differently or exclude it from the average.

12. Eq. (1) and ω_pl ∝ √n_tot assume a constant effective mass and a single carrier species, but TiSe2 has electron and hole pockets with different masses. A short justification of the assumed proportionality n_tot ∝ F would help, given the band reconstruction across Tc.

13. Fig. 1c: please name the exchange-correlation functional, whether a +U or hybrid correction was used, and whether the CDW structure was relaxed, so readers can judge the assignment of peaks 1 to 5. Also state in the main text how core-hole effects are treated in the TDDFT M-edge calculation.

14. The fit curve in Fig. 4e reaches its minimum at exactly the measured 190 K point. Please state whether Tc was fixed, at about 200 K from Fig. 3d,e, or fitted.

15. The phrase "a threefold reduction in the measured initial response time" (Discussion) should name the fluence range and temperature it refers to, since at 190 K there is essentially no reduction.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Fit all of the τ_int data in one global analysis with a single explicit model: a free power-law exponent in F, a free or physically motivated floor, and α and Tc with uncertainties. Report whether a non-divergent saturation model fits Fig. 4e equally well.
2. Add a null reference, a doped or non-excitonic analogue measured at the same edge and fluences, to show that 1/√F scaling and a minimum at Tc are absent when excitonic correlations are absent.
3. Measure the low-fluence temperature dependence more densely around Tc, on at least two samples. Normalize to absorbed fluence and give a heating estimate, so that the "enhanced susceptibility" claim does not rest on one point.
4. Use the Ti-edge low-fluence sign reversal (Fig. 2b,d) and the lineshape decomposition of Se peak 3 as independent channels. If they show the same temperature anomaly at Tc, the case for an excitonic origin would be much stronger than one rise time can make on its own.
