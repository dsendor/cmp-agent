Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors use few-femtosecond broadband XUV transient absorption (UBXAS) at the Ti M2,3 and Se M4,5 edges of 1T-TiSe2. They track how fast Se 4p holes appear after photoexcitation as a function of fluence and temperature. Two results are claimed. First, the initial response time follows τ_int ∝ 1/√F both below and above Tc, which they read as short-range excitonic correlations persisting in the normal state. Second, at low fluence τ_int has a cusp-like minimum at Tc, which they read as an enhanced (diverging) excitonic susceptibility. A local, element-specific probe of excitonic correlations above Tc, where ARPES and diffraction lose contrast, would be a useful addition to the long debate on the excitonic versus Jahn-Teller/electron-phonon origin of the CDW in TiSe2. As presented, however, the key observables are not shown to be specific to excitons, and the phenomenological model does not fit all of the data it is meant to explain.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- Technically strong data. Broadband, element-resolved transient spectra with few-fs resolution, covering both Ti and Se edges at once, at cryogenic and elevated temperatures (Figs. 2 and 3).
- A sensible choice of spectral observable. The Se M5 peak ③ (window iii) shows no A1g phonon modulation (Fig. 2e), and its 0.2 ps amplitude has an order-parameter-like onset at Tc ≈ 200 K (Fig. 3e).
- A systematic fluence-by-temperature dataset (Fig. 4a–e). The qualitative contrast is clear: τ_int depends strongly on fluence at 23 K and 300 K and is roughly flat at 190 K.
- The question is timely and relevant to other excitonic insulator candidates (Ta2NiSe5, Ta2Pd3Te5).

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **Equation (1) does not reproduce the high-fluence and 190 K data it is meant to explain.**
   - Where: Eq. (1) (p. 1793) and Fig. 4d,e.
   - The model writes n_tot ∝ F[χ0 + χex(T)] with τ_int ∝ 1/√n_tot + τ0. Because n_tot is linear in F, the temperature dependence enters only as a multiplicative factor. The model therefore predicts that the 2.0 mJ cm⁻² data follow the same temperature dependence as the 0.2 mJ cm⁻² data, with (τ_int − τ0) scaled by √(0.2/2.0) ≈ 0.32.
   - High-fluence prediction: with τ0 = 25 fs and the low-fluence values in Fig. 4e (about 190 fs at 23 K, about 70 fs at 190 K), the 2.0 mJ cm⁻² curve should fall from about 77 fs at 23 K to about 40 fs at Tc. The measured red points are flat at about 72 fs at all temperatures.
   - 190 K prediction: the model gives a 1/√F dependence with a smaller prefactor at Tc. The measured τ_int is roughly fluence-independent at about 70–85 fs from 0.2 to 2.3 mJ cm⁻² (Fig. 4c,d).
   - Offset: the text attributes the offset τ0 to the time to create mobile holes without excitonic interactions. Yet at Tc, where excitons should be easiest to break, τ_int does not approach τ0 = 25 fs. It sits at the same ~70 fs value that the high-fluence data reach at every temperature.
   - Why it matters: this ~70 fs floor, common to all temperatures and to the Tc minimum, points to a limiting process that is not in the model. It could be intrinsic carrier relaxation into the probed Se 4p states near EF, or an effective resolution limit of the fitting procedure. If so, the "minimum at Tc" may be the low-fluence curve reaching that floor rather than evidence for a diverging χex.
   - What to do: extend the model to include the floor (for example, a saturating or additive-rate form) and refit both fluence series and the 190 K fluence series jointly with a single parameter set. Report Tc, α, χ0 and the prefactor with uncertainties, and state explicitly which features of Fig. 4e the model does and does not capture.

2. **Neither signature (1/√F scaling, cusp at Tc) is shown to be specific to excitonic correlations.**
   - Where: Fig. 4d,e; pp. 1792–1793; Discussion.
   - 1/√F: the 1/√n scaling is read as plasmon-limited screening of bound electron–hole pairs. But any density-dependent electronic process at the Se 4p states near EF can give a sublinear power law in F over a decade of fluence. Examples are carrier–carrier scattering and the cascade of hot carriers into states near EF, screening-induced renormalization of the core-level or valence bands, and Auger-type processes. The text says Supplementary Note 8 rules out alternatives, but the main text gives no quantitative argument.
   - Cusp at Tc: in TiSe2 the electronic and lattice order parameters are bilinearly coupled. Any response tied to the CDW will show critical behaviour at Tc whether the instability is driven by excitons or by electron–phonon coupling, as the diffuse-scattering phonon-softening analogy the authors themselves invoke (ref. 40) shows. A cusp in a response time at Tc is therefore expected in both scenarios. As written, it cannot distinguish an "excitonic instability" from a CDW transition with a coupled electronic response.
   - What to do:
     - Provide a reference measurement where excitonic correlations should be absent, under matched fluence and spectral conditions. Candidates are an isostructural non-excitonic compound (for example 1T-TiS2 or 1T-ZrSe2 at the corresponding edge), heavily doped or intercalated TiSe2 with the CDW suppressed, or TiSe2 well above Tc (400–500 K). In such a reference the 1/√F dependence should vanish or change.
     - Show the fluence exponent as a free parameter (log–log plot of τ_int − τ0 against F) at each temperature, so readers can see how sharply the data select 1/2.
     - Discuss explicitly what feature of the Tc anomaly would differ between exciton-driven and phonon-driven transitions.

3. **What the window-(iii) signal and τ_int measure is not established.**
   - Where: Figs. 2e and 3c; τ_int is defined via Supplementary Eq. (1).
   - The interpretation needs the transmission decrease in window (iii) to be a state-filling measure of Se 4p hole population. The spectra in Fig. 3c are derivative-like (positive near 52.7 eV, negative near 53.2 eV, with differences between 25 K and 310 K). This is the shape expected from a transient edge shift or broadening, for example from changes in core-hole screening (refs. 37, 38) or band renormalization, which themselves scale with carrier density.
   - Why it matters: if the window-(iii) rise is dominated by a screening-induced shift, τ_int reflects screening build-up in general, not exciton dissociation specifically.
   - What to do: decompose the Se M5 transient at each delay into state-filling, shift and broadening contributions (for example by fitting the time-dependent spectra with a model lineshape) and show which component carries the fluence- and temperature-dependent rise time. Also state the functional form of Supplementary Eq. (1), how the IRF is included, and whether τ_int is a rise time, a delay or a 10–90% time.

**B. Should address**

4. **The Tc minimum rests on very few temperatures and many free parameters.**
   - Where: Fig. 4e.
   - The low-fluence series has about eight temperatures between 23 K and 350 K. The cusp is defined by essentially one point at 190 K, flanked by 150 K and 250 K, and the fit has at least four free parameters (Tc, α, χ0, prefactor; possibly τ0).
   - What to do: measure full delay traces at 5–10 K steps between about 160 K and 230 K, on both sides of Tc, ideally on more than one sample. Report the fitted Tc and compare it with the Tc from Fig. 3d,e and from transport on the same crystals.

5. **Fluence is used as a proxy for carrier density without calibration.**
   - Where: Eq. (1), Fig. 4d, and the plasmon argument on p. 1792.
   - Absorbed density: the 1/√F argument assumes n_tot ∝ F and spatially uniform excitation. The paper does not give sample thickness, pump penetration depth at 750 nm, or absorbed fraction and its temperature dependence across Tc. In transmission, the XUV probe averages over the whole depth profile of the excitation.
   - Nonlinear absorption: with a 3.4 fs pump at 2 mJ cm⁻², the peak intensity is about 6 × 10¹¹ W cm⁻², so multiphoton absorption and saturation need to be ruled out. One check is linearity of the peak ① amplitude with F.
   - Equilibrium carriers: these are substantial in semimetallic TiSe2 and differ strongly between 23 K and 300 K. Yet one 1/√F curve with the same τ0 fits both temperatures.
   - Magnitude: the paper should estimate whether the measured τ_int (70–190 fs) is consistent with the inverse plasmon frequency for the estimated photoexcited plus equilibrium density, using realistic ε∞ and effective masses.

6. **Sample temperature, heating and characterization are not documented in the main text.**
   - Heating: the cusp at 190 K and the 5 K temperature steps make accurate sample temperature essential. The paper needs an estimate of steady-state heating at each fluence and repetition rate, particularly 2.0 mJ cm⁻², and of thermal lag during the continuous ramp used for Fig. 3.
   - Sample information: the number of samples measured, their thickness and preparation (exfoliated flake or membrane), and stoichiometry or Ti self-intercalation level. Tc in TiSe2 varies with these.
   - Reproducibility: whether Figs. 3 and 4 come from the same sample, and whether the result reproduces after thermal cycling.
   - Degradation: the 350 K high-fluence point had to be taken at 1.1 mJ cm⁻² "to prevent sample degradation". The authors should show that earlier low-temperature traces were reproduced after the high-temperature and high-fluence runs.

7. **The argument excluding a phonon contribution is too thin.**
   - Where: Discussion, p. 1793.
   - The argument takes a 20% phonon frequency renormalization from ref. 28, measured under different conditions, and assumes τ_int scales with the phonon period. Neither step is justified.
   - τ_int (70–190 fs) is a fraction of the A1g period (~167 fs) and of the CDW amplitude-mode period. The zone-boundary soft mode is strongly temperature dependent near Tc (ref. 40). A lattice contribution would therefore not appear as a simple renormalization of one frequency.
   - What to do: give a quantitative estimate of the lattice timescale relevant to the window-(iii) rise (for example from the CDW amplitude mode and soft-mode frequencies at each temperature). Show that its fluence and temperature dependence cannot reproduce Fig. 4d,e.

8. **"Short-range" and "quasi-2D" are inferred, not measured.**
   - Where: title, abstract, Fig. 4g and Discussion.
   - Core-level absorption is local and gives no correlation length or dimensionality. The data at most show that the fluence dependence persists above Tc.
   - What to do: either temper the wording or connect the 300 K result quantitatively to independent correlation lengths, for example diffuse X-ray scattering above Tc. Also show how high in temperature the 1/√F behaviour persists (the 350 K low-fluence point in Fig. 4e is still ~145 fs).

9. **The Ti many-body continuum sign reversal is under-supported.**
   - Where: Fig. 2b,d.
   - The low-fluence sign reversal near 40 eV at 200 fs is presented as evidence for a process "distinct from simple carrier excitation" and possibly excitonic. It is shown at two fluences and one temperature, inside a weak, ×2-magnified signal.
   - What to do: show the fluence at which the sign changes, its temperature dependence, and whether it disappears above Tc. Otherwise present it as tentative.

10. **Fit robustness and error bars.**
    - Where: Figs. 3e and 4d,e.
    - The window-(iii) amplitude decreases by more than a factor of two from 23 K to Tc (Fig. 3e), so the signal-to-noise of the near-Tc traces is lower.
    - The error bars in Fig. 4d,e are only fit uncertainties.
    - What to do: show that τ_int does not correlate with signal amplitude or SNR, for example by fitting noise-matched synthetic data. Give scan-to-scan or day-to-day repeatability for at least the 190 K and 23 K low-fluence points.

**C. Minor**

11. Fig. 4e mixes 2.0 mJ cm⁻² data with a 1.1 mJ cm⁻² point at 350 K inside the "high fluence" average line. Mark this point distinctly or exclude it from the red average.
12. Report the fitted α, Tc and χ0/χex ratio in the main text, not only in Supplementary Note 9. Comment on the physical meaning of α in relation to known CDW critical exponents of TiSe2.
13. The low-fluence 190 K point in Fig. 4d has a large error bar and lies above its neighbours. Comment on whether the 190 K fluence series is truly flat within uncertainties.
14. Fig. 3: give the ramp rate, the ramp direction (heating or cooling), and whether hysteresis was checked. Explain why the temperature dependence was taken at a single delay (0.2 ps), given that τ_int itself changes with temperature and so affects the 0.2 ps amplitude.
15. The main text has no Methods section. Add the key experimental parameters: pump spectrum and duration, spot sizes, repetition rate, XUV probe resolution, sample geometry and thickness, and the IRF width shown in Fig. 4a.
16. Define "excitonic susceptibility" more carefully. χex in Eq. (1) is a response of the photoinduced mobile-carrier yield, not a thermodynamic susceptibility. State how the two are expected to relate.
17. Fig. 1d,e: state the photon energy and resolution of the ARPES data. The two maps are at 260 K and 40 K, while the XUV data use 300 K and 23 K; clarify that this mismatch is immaterial.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a matched null measurement: a non-excitonic isostructural compound, or TiSe2 far above Tc. This is the most direct way to show that the 1/√F scaling and the Tc cusp are specific to excitonic correlations.
2. Replace Eq. (1) with a model that reproduces the ~70 fs floor, the flat high-fluence data and the fluence-independent 190 K series simultaneously. Fit all datasets jointly.
3. Spectrally decompose the Se M5 transient into state-filling and shift/broadening components, and show which one carries the rise-time dynamics.
4. Densify the temperature grid around Tc. Add sample characterization (transport Tc, thickness) and heating estimates, so the location and sharpness of the minimum can be trusted.
