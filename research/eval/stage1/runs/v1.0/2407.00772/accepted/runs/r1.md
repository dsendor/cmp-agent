Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use tabletop few-femtosecond broadband XUV transient absorption (UBXAS) at the Ti M2,3 and Se M4,5 edges of 1T-TiSe2. They track the initial rise time of a Se 4p hole feature (peak ③) as a function of pump fluence and temperature. They claim two things: (i) a 1/√F scaling of the rise time persists above Tc, which they take as evidence of short-range excitonic correlations in the normal state; and (ii) at low fluence the rise time has a sharp minimum at Tc, which they take as an enhanced excitonic susceptibility. Separating excitonic from lattice contributions in TiSe2 is a long-standing problem, and a time-domain, element-resolved probe that works above Tc, where time-resolved ARPES loses contrast, would be a valuable advance. The experiment is technically impressive. However, the step from "the rise time depends on fluence and temperature in this way" to "excitonic correlations and an excitonic susceptibility" depends on a simple plasmon-screening scaling argument and a phenomenological susceptibility model. In the current version neither is tested against alternative explanations.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new experimental capability: cryogenic UBXAS with few-fs pump pulses covering both Ti and Se edges at once (Figs. 1a, 2a,b). It gives element- and orbital-specific access to electronic dynamics above Tc, where ARPES band folding is absent.
- Sensible choice of observable. Peak ③ (window iii) shows no A1g coherent-phonon modulation (Fig. 2e versus 2c,d,f), so its initial response is not contaminated by displacive phonon oscillations.
- Clear order-parameter-like temperature dependence of the transient signal at low fluence (Fig. 3d,e). The sign reversal of the Ti many-body continuum at low fluence (Fig. 2b,d; Fig. 3b,d) is striking.
- Systematic fluence and temperature datasets (Fig. 4a–e). The non-monotonic low-fluence τint(T) with a minimum near Tc, against a temperature-independent high-fluence τint, is a qualitatively new observation.
- The authors give a quantitative argument against a dominant phonon-hardening explanation of the fluence dependence (Discussion, 20% versus threefold).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The 1/√F scaling does not uniquely indicate excitonic correlations, especially above Tc (Fig. 4b,d; Results, "Persistent excitonic fluctuations in the normal state").
   - The central claim of persistent short-range excitonic correlations at 300 K rests on two observations: τint decreases with fluence, and the decrease follows 1/√F plus a constant offset τ0 = 25 fs.
   - A faster rise of a hole population at higher excitation density is generic in a photoexcited semimetal. Carrier–carrier scattering, thermalization and screening-driven renormalization all speed up with density. None of these needs bound electron–hole pairs.
   - The plasmon argument also neglects the equilibrium carrier density. TiSe2 is a semimetal or small-gap semiconductor with substantial intrinsic carriers above Tc. Screening should then scale as 1/√(n_eq(T) + cF), not 1/√F + τ0. The constant offset is introduced without derivation and absorbs the difference.
   - The fluence range spans little more than one decade (about 0.2–3 mJ cm⁻²), and the scatter in Fig. 4d is large. So 1/√F cannot be distinguished from 1/F, a logarithmic dependence, or a saturating form.
   - What the authors should do:
     (a) Fit alternative functional forms, including one with the equilibrium density, and report the fitted parameters, uncertainties and fit-quality comparison.
     (b) Show a null case in which excitonic correlations should be absent but carrier-density-dependent dynamics are present. Options: a much higher temperature where the scaling should disappear, a non-excitonic reference compound measured at the same edge and under matched conditions, or a microscopic calculation of the density-dependent hole-generation time without excitonic binding.
     (c) Move the alternatives currently dismissed in Supplementary Note 8 into the main text, with quantitative comparisons.
   - Without such a discriminating test, the headline claim that short-range excitonic fluctuations "emerge before long-range-order formation" is not established.

2. The susceptibility model (Eq. 1) is not quantitatively consistent with the high-fluence data, and the minimum at Tc rests on very few points (Fig. 4d,e; Eq. 1; Supplementary Note 9).
   - Eq. 1 gives n_tot ∝ F[χ0 + χex(T)]. Combined with τ ∝ 1/√n_tot + τ0, the model predicts a 1/√F fluence dependence at every temperature, with only the prefactor changing.
   - Two predictions follow:
     (i) Near Tc, where χex diverges, τint should approach τ0 ≈ 25 fs. The measured minimum is about 70 fs, the same as the high-fluence floor (red line, Fig. 4e).
     (ii) At 2.0 mJ cm⁻², the model predicts residual temperature dependence: the low-fluence variation (about 190 → 70 fs), reduced by about √10. The high-fluence data are flat.
   - The data instead suggest a temperature-independent floor near 70 fs that both fluences reach at Tc. A rate-limiting process unrelated to χex, or a resolution limit, would produce the same pattern.
   - The low-fluence fit has at least three free parameters (χ0, α and the prefactor, plus possibly Tc) for seven data points. The dip itself is set essentially by the single point at 190 K.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Fit both fluences and the fluence series (Fig. 4d) globally with one parameter set, and report all parameter values, uncertainties, and which are fixed (in particular Tc and whether α is the same on both sides).
     - Explain the 70 fs floor.
     - Add temperatures between about 170 and 230 K at fine spacing at low fluence. This would show that the minimum is a cusp at Tc and not a single low point.

B. Should address

3. Enhanced susceptibility near Tc does not by itself distinguish an excitonic from a lattice-driven instability (Results, "Enhanced excitonic susceptibility at Tc"; Discussion).
   - In any coupled electron–lattice CDW transition, the electronic susceptibility at the ordering wavevector is enhanced near Tc, whichever degree of freedom drives it.
   - Near Tc the CDW amplitude mode and the zone-boundary L-point phonon soften strongly (ref. 40), and the CDW gap closes. Each could change how fast Se 4p holes are created.
   - The phonon argument in the Discussion uses the 20% fluence-induced phonon hardening. It addresses the fluence dependence at low temperature, not the temperature dependence near Tc.
   - The authors should state what in the observed τint(T) would differ if the transition were primarily phonon-driven. They should also explain why a faster, rather than critically slowed, response is expected near a continuous transition.
   - Otherwise "consistent with excitonic instability" should be framed as compatible with, not evidence for, an excitonic mechanism.

4. It is not shown that the window (iii) signal measures Se 4p hole population (Figs. 2a,e, 3c; Supplementary Note 7).
   - Core-level transients in transition-metal compounds often include edge shifts, broadening and core-exciton or screening changes (refs. 37, 38), not just state filling.
   - Fig. 3c shows a derivative-like line shape around 53 eV, positive on the low-energy side and negative above, which suggests a spectral shift as well as an amplitude change.
   - A rise time from an integrated window that mixes shift and filling may track screening dynamics, which also depend on density. That would feed directly into concern 1.
   - The authors should decompose the transient spectra at representative delays, temperatures and fluences into shift, broadening and amplitude components. They should then show that the extracted τint is carried by the amplitude (hole population) component.

5. The effective sample temperature and the Tc calibration are not established near Tc (Fig. 3d,e; Fig. 4c,e).
   - The minimum is placed at 190 K with Tc ≈ 200 K and 5 K temperature bins during a continuous ramp. Average heating from the pump at the laser repetition rate, and the temperature gradient between sensor and sample, matter at this scale, especially at 2.0 mJ cm⁻².
   - The authors should report:
     - the repetition rate;
     - the estimated steady-state heating at each fluence;
     - how Tc was determined independently for these samples, for example by resistivity or from the onset in Fig. 3d,e at the same fluence.
   - They should also state the stoichiometry and self-intercalation level, since Ti excess shifts Tc in TiSe2.

6. Time-zero determination and extraction of τint are not described in the main text (Fig. 4a–e; Supplementary Note 2, Equation (1)).
   - The τint differences at stake are a few tens of femtoseconds. Drift in time zero between temperature or fluence scans, or correlation between τint and t0 in the fit, could produce or hide trends of this size.
   - The authors should state:
     - whether t0 is fixed by an independent reference recorded simultaneously (for example a fast feature at the Ti edge);
     - whether t0 is fixed or free in each fit;
     - the covariance between t0 and τint.
   - They should also show that the error bars in Fig. 4d,e include t0 uncertainty, not only fitting error.

7. Excitation inhomogeneity and fluence calibration are not addressed (Fig. 4d; Methods/SI).
   - The XUV probe is transmitted through the whole sample thickness, while the 750 nm pump is absorbed over a finite penetration depth. The probe therefore averages over a range of excitation densities. This smears and could bias a density-scaling analysis.
   - The analysis uses incident fluence, but absorbed fluence changes with temperature because the optical constants change across the CDW transition. That would affect both the Fig. 3d,e amplitudes and the low-fluence τint(T).
   - The authors should give the sample thickness and pump penetration depth and estimate the depth profile of n_tot. They should also show that the temperature dependence of pump absorption at 750 nm is small or correct for it.

8. Sample count, reproducibility and degradation are not reported (Figs. 3, 4; Fig. 4 caption).
   - The caption says the 350 K high-fluence point needed a reduced fluence (1.1 mJ cm⁻²) to prevent degradation, so damage is a real issue at the fluences used.
   - The authors should state:
     - how many samples were measured;
     - whether the key results (1/√F scaling at 300 K and the τint minimum near Tc) were reproduced on more than one sample and after thermal cycling;
     - how degradation was monitored, for example by repeating a reference temperature and fluence before and after each series.
   - Fig. 4d (190 K) and Fig. 4e come from different datasets (Supplementary Fig. 17). Comparing τint at the same nominal conditions across the two would be a useful reproducibility check.

9. Several claims go beyond the temperature range and data shown (Abstract; Discussion).
   - The abstract says short-range fluctuations "emerge before" long-range order, but the highest temperature measured (350 K) still shows the effect. No onset temperature is identified.
   - The Discussion says the correlations above Tc are "likely confined in-plane". No measurement here bears on dimensionality.
   - The authors should either measure to higher temperature to find where the 1/√F dependence or the χex contribution disappears, or reword these statements as interpretation. They could also connect to equilibrium probes of short-range order above Tc, such as diffuse X-ray scattering or ARPES spectral weight at M above Tc (Fig. 1d, 260 K).

10. The interpretation of the Ti many-body continuum is only qualitative (Fig. 2b,d; Fig. 3b,d).
   - At low fluence, the sign reversal of the transient around 38–41 eV is presented as "an additional dynamical process distinct from simple carrier excitation" and linked to excitonic correlations.
   - The authors already have TDDFT that reproduces the static continuum (Supplementary Fig. 12). They should use it, or a simple screening model, to show which change in the valence states (for example gap closing versus carrier filling) produces a positive versus negative transient at this energy.
   - Otherwise this observation does not support the excitonic interpretation, and the order-parameter-like onset in Fig. 3d is equally explained by CDW melting.

C. Minor

11. The high-fluence average in Fig. 4e (red line) includes the 350 K point taken at 1.1 mJ cm⁻² instead of 2.0 mJ cm⁻². By the authors' own 1/√F relation this point should be slower. Either exclude it from the average or correct for the fluence difference.

12. Fig. 4d: say what τ0 = 25 fs physically represents, and why it is shorter than any measured τint (≥ about 65 fs). Also state the IRF width relative to the shortest τint (the green IRF curve in Fig. 4a), so readers can judge whether the 70 fs floor is close to the resolution limit.

13. Fig. 3e is described as showing a "clear onset at Tc". The data change fairly gradually from 25 K to about 200 K. A fit to an order-parameter form with a fitted Tc, plus a comparison with the Ti window in Fig. 3d, would make the claim quantitative.

14. In the plasmon-frequency argument, state which carriers (electron and hole pockets, effective masses) and which dielectric background enter ω_pl. Also state whether the low-temperature phase, with its gapped bands, changes the proportionality constant between n_tot and F.

15. Define "initial response time" in the main text, not only via Supplementary Equation (1). Readers comparing to tr-ARPES rates (ref. 20) need to know whether it is a rise time, a 10–90% time or an exponential constant.

16. Fig. 4f,g are schematic. Make it explicit in the caption that they are illustrations and not derived from data or calculation.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a discriminating control for the excitonic interpretation of the fluence scaling: a non-excitonic reference compound, a much higher temperature, or a microscopic calculation of density-dependent hole generation without excitonic binding. Compare it directly with Fig. 4d.
2. Do a single global fit of all τint(F, T) data with Eq. 1 and the screening relation. Report all parameters, and add finer low-fluence temperature points around Tc to show the cusp.
3. Decompose the Se M5 transient line shape into shift, broadening and amplitude components. Show that τint reflects hole population.
4. Give the experimental details that bear directly on the claims in the main text or an Extended Data table: number of samples, thickness, absorbed fluence versus T, repetition rate and heating, time-zero referencing, and Tc characterization.
