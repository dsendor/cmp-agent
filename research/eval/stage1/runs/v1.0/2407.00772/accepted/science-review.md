A. Must address

1. The 1/√F scaling is not shown to be specific to excitonic correlations, especially above Tc (Fig. 4b,d; Results, "Persistent excitonic fluctuations in the normal state", p. 1792; Supplementary Note 8). The claim that short-range excitonic correlations persist at 300 K rests on two observations: τ_int decreases with fluence, and the decrease follows 1/√F plus a constant offset τ0 = 25 fs, read as plasmon-limited screening (τ_int ∝ 1/ω_pl ∝ 1/√n_tot) of bound electron–hole pairs. But a faster rise of a hole population at higher excitation density is generic in a photoexcited semimetal. Carrier–carrier scattering and thermalization, the cascade or screening-limited relaxation of hot carriers into the Se 4p states near EF, Coulomb-driven intraband redistribution, screening-induced renormalization of core-level or valence bands, and Auger-type processes all speed up with density, and none needs pre-existing electron–hole pairing. Over the roughly one decade of fluence covered (about 0.2–3 mJ cm⁻²), and given the scatter in Fig. 4d, 1/√F with a free offset cannot be distinguished from 1/F, a logarithm, a saturating form or a power law with a free exponent. The plasmon argument also neglects the equilibrium carrier density, which is substantial in semimetallic TiSe2 and differs strongly between 23 K and 300 K. Screening should then scale as 1/√(n_eq(T) + cF), not 1/√F + τ0; the offset τ0 is introduced without derivation and absorbs the difference. That the 23 K and 300 K data fall on one curve with the same τ0, although the equilibrium states differ qualitatively, sits uneasily with the claim that the scaling diagnoses the ordered versus the fluctuating state. The authors should: (a) fit alternative functional forms, including one with the equilibrium density, and a free fluence exponent at each temperature (for example a log–log plot of τ_int − τ0 against F), reporting fitted parameters, uncertainties and a fit-quality comparison; (b) add a null reference measured at the same edge under matched fluence and spectral conditions where excitonic correlations should be absent or strongly suppressed, such as an isostructural non-excitonic compound (for example 1T-TiS2 or 1T-ZrSe2), heavily electron-doped or intercalated TiSe2 with the CDW suppressed (for example CuxTiSe2 beyond the CDW dome), or TiSe2 well above Tc (400–500 K), or else a microscopic calculation of the density-dependent hole-generation time without excitonic binding; (c) move the alternatives considered in Supplementary Note 8 into the main text with quantitative comparisons. Without such a discriminating test, "short-range excitonic correlations above Tc" that "emerge before long-range-order formation" (abstract, Fig. 4g) is one possible interpretation, not a demonstrated one.

2. The susceptibility model of Eq. (1) does not reproduce the high-fluence and 190 K data, and an unexplained ~70 fs floor may account for the minimum at Tc (Eq. 1, p. 1793; Fig. 4d,e; Supplementary Note 9). Eq. (1) gives n_tot ∝ F[χ0 + χex(T)], and with τ_int = τ0 + A/√n_tot the temperature dependence enters only as a multiplicative factor, so the model predicts a 1/√F dependence at every temperature with only the prefactor changing. Three predictions fail. (i) At low fluence the variable part of τ_int falls from about 165 fs (190 fs at 23 K) to about 45 fs (about 70 fs at 190 K), implying n_tot rises about 13-fold at Tc; scaling (τ_int − τ0) by √(0.2/2.0) ≈ 0.32, the 2.0 mJ cm⁻² curve should fall from about 75–77 fs at 23 K to about 40 fs at Tc, yet the measured red points are flat at about 70–80 fs at all temperatures. (ii) At 190 K the model predicts 1/√F with a smaller prefactor, but the measured τ_int is roughly fluence-independent at about 70–85 fs from 0.2 to 2.3 mJ cm⁻² (Fig. 4c,d). (iii) Near Tc, where χex diverges and excitons should be easiest to break, τ_int should approach τ0 = 25 fs; instead it sits at the same ~70 fs value that the high-fluence data reach at every temperature, and no point in Fig. 4d at any temperature or fluence falls below about 65 fs. The data fit a ~70 fs floor far better than τ0 = 25 fs. That floor could be a rate-limiting process absent from the model (for example intrinsic carrier relaxation into the probed Se 4p states near EF) or an effective resolution limit of the fitting procedure. On this reading the "dip" at Tc is the low-fluence τ_int reaching the same floor as high fluence, a saturation effect, and with a floor in place neither the divergence exponent α nor a diverging χex can be determined. The fit curve in Fig. 4e reaches its minimum exactly at the measured 190 K point, and α, Tc and χ0/χex are only implied by the blue curve. The authors should: extend the model to include the floor (for example a saturating or additive-rate form) and fit the two temperature series and the fluence series of Fig. 4d jointly with one parameter set; report τ0, A, α, χ0 (and the χ0/χex ratio) and Tc in the main text with uncertainties, stating which are fixed (in particular whether Tc was fixed at about 200 K from Fig. 3d,e or fitted, and whether α is the same on both sides of Tc); explain what τ0 physically represents and why it is shorter than any measured τ_int; explain the origin of the ~70 fs floor and compare it with the IRF width (green curve in Fig. 4a, Supplementary Fig. 5); show that the data favour a diverging susceptibility over saturation of a non-divergent quantity; comment on the physical meaning of α relative to known CDW critical exponents of TiSe2; and state explicitly which features of Fig. 4e the model does and does not capture.

4. The anomaly at Tc does not by itself distinguish an excitonic from a lattice-driven instability, and the argument excluding phonons does not address it (Results, "Enhanced excitonic susceptibility at Tc"; Discussion, p. 1793). In TiSe2 the electronic and lattice order parameters are bilinearly coupled, so in any coupled electron–lattice CDW transition the electronic susceptibility at the ordering wavevector, and any response tied to the CDW, shows critical behaviour at Tc whichever degree of freedom drives it, as the diffuse-scattering phonon-softening analogy the authors invoke (ref. 40) itself shows. Near Tc the CDW amplitude mode and the zone-boundary L-point phonon soften strongly and lattice fluctuations grow (refs. 40, 44), and the CDW gap closes; each could change how fast Se 4p holes are created. The phonon argument in the Discussion takes a 20% phonon frequency renormalization from ref. 28, measured under different conditions, and assumes τ_int scales with the phonon period; neither step is justified, and it addresses only the fluence-induced hardening of one phonon at low temperature, not softening near Tc. τ_int (70–190 fs) is a fraction of the A1g period (~167 fs) and of the amplitude-mode period, so a lattice contribution would not appear as a simple renormalization of one frequency. The authors should give a quantitative estimate of the lattice timescale relevant to the window-(iii) rise (from the amplitude-mode and soft-mode frequencies at each temperature) and show that its fluence and temperature dependence cannot reproduce Fig. 4d,e; state what in τ_int(T) would differ if the transition were primarily phonon-driven; and explain why a faster, rather than critically slowed, response is expected near a continuous transition. Otherwise "consistent with excitonic instability" should be framed as compatible with, not evidence for, an excitonic mechanism.

5. It is not established that the window-(iii) signal and τ_int measure Se 4p hole population (Figs. 2a,e, 3c; Supplementary Note 7). The interpretation needs the transmission decrease in window (iii), [53.04, 53.33] eV, to be a state-filling measure of Se 4p holes. Core-level transients in transition-metal compounds often include edge shifts, broadening and core-exciton or core-hole screening changes (refs. 37, 38). The 25 K and 310 K spectra in Fig. 3c are dispersive, derivative-like around 53 eV (lobes of opposite sign near 52.7–53.1 eV and 53.2–53.4 eV), the shape expected from an edge shift or broadening from core-hole screening or band renormalization, and the window sits on the slope of this structure. Shift and hole population can have different fluence and time dependences, and a screening-induced shift itself scales with density, so a rise time from an integrated window that mixes the two may track screening build-up in general, not exciton dissociation, which feeds directly into concern 1. The window-robustness test of Supplementary Note 7 is a different test. The authors should decompose the Se M5 transient at peak ③ into amplitude (state-filling), shift and width components as a function of delay, fluence and temperature (for example by fitting a model lineshape), and show that τ_int from the amplitude component alone reproduces Fig. 4d,e.

6. Incident fluence is used as a proxy for carrier density without calibration (Eq. 1; Fig. 4d; Fig. 3d,e; p. 1792). The 1/√F argument assumes n_tot ∝ F and uniform excitation. The XUV probe is transmitted through the whole sample, while the 750 nm (1.65 eV) pump is absorbed over a finite penetration depth, so the probe averages over a range of excitation densities, which smears and can bias a density-scaling analysis. Absorbed fluence also changes with temperature because the optical absorption and reflectivity change across the CDW transition, which would alter n_tot at fixed incident fluence without any excitonic susceptibility and affect both the Fig. 3d,e amplitudes and the low-fluence τ_int(T). With a 3.4 fs pump at 2 mJ cm⁻² the peak intensity is about 6 × 10¹¹ W cm⁻², so multiphoton absorption and saturation must be ruled out. The authors should give the sample thickness, pump penetration depth and absorbed fraction and estimate the depth profile of n_tot; normalize to absorbed fluence using measured or literature temperature-dependent optical constants at 750 nm, or show that their temperature dependence is small; and check linearity of excitation, for example of the peak ① amplitude with F.

7. The effective sample temperature and the Tc calibration are not established near Tc (Fig. 3d,e; Fig. 4c–e). The minimum is placed at 190 K with Tc ≈ 200 K and 5 K bins. 8. The definition and extraction of τ_int, the treatment of time zero and the error bars are not described in the main text (Fig. 4a–e; Supplementary Note 2, Supplementary Eq. (1)). The τ_int differences at stake are a few tens of femtoseconds, τ_int sits near 70 fs, and neighbouring windows carry coherent oscillations with a ~167 fs period. Drift in time zero between scans, or correlation between τ_int and t0 in the fit, could produce or hide trends of this size. The window-(iii) amplitude drops by more than a factor of two from 23 K to Tc (Fig. 3e), so near-Tc traces have lower signal-to-noise, and the error bars in Fig. 4d,e are only fit uncertainties. The authors should state in the main text or Methods the fit function, how the IRF is included, the free parameters and fit window, and whether τ_int is a rise time, a delay, a 10–90% time or an exponential constant (needed for comparison with tr-ARPES rates, ref. 20); state whether t0 is fixed by an independent simultaneously recorded reference (for example a fast Ti-edge feature) or free, and its covariance with τ_int; give error bars that include t0 uncertainty and scan-to-scan or day-to-day spread, at least for the 23 K and 190 K low-fluence points; show that τ_int does not correlate with signal amplitude or SNR (for example with noise-matched synthetic data); and show that a model-independent measure (10–90% rise time or time to half-maximum) reproduces the trends of Fig. 4d,e.

10. "Short-range", "emerge before long-range order", "quasi-2D" and "confined in-plane" go beyond what is measured (title; abstract; Fig. 4g; Discussion). 12. The plasmon-screening argument is not made quantitative (p. 1792; Eq. 1). ω_pl ∝ √n_tot assumes a single carrier species with constant effective mass, but TiSe2 has electron and hole pockets with different masses, and the band reconstruction across Tc may change the proportionality between n_tot and F. The authors should state which carriers (pockets, effective masses) and which dielectric background ε∞ enter ω_pl, justify n_tot ∝ F in the gapped low-temperature phase, and estimate whether the measured τ_int (70–190 fs) is consistent with the inverse plasmon frequency for the estimated photoexcited plus equilibrium density.
