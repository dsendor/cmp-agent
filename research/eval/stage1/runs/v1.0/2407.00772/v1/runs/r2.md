Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript uses a cryogenic attosecond broadband XUV transient absorption setup (ABXAS) on exfoliated 1T-TiSe2 flakes. It makes three claims. First, static core-level photoemission and XUV absorption show no feature at the CDW transition (Tc ≈ 200 K). Second, transient absorption at a fixed delay of 200 fs has an order-parameter-like onset at Tc in two spectral windows: the Ti many-body continuum (window ii) and the Se M5 peak ② (window iii). Third, the rise time in window iii scales as 1/√F both below and above Tc, which the authors take as time-domain evidence for short-range excitonic correlations in the normal state. Showing that time-resolved core-level absorption can track a low-energy ordering transition would be of interest. The excitonic-fluctuation claim would bear on a long-standing debate, but in its present form it rests on indirect and non-unique evidence.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The instrument is technically impressive: a 3.4 fs pump, a 5.4 fs IRF, broadband coverage of both the Ti M2,3 and Se M4,5 edges in one shot, and cryogenic operation down to about 20 K with drift correction and pointing stabilisation.
- The temperature scans are dense (about 5 K steps, Fig. 3a). The onset in both windows (Fig. 3d,e) coincides with the ARPES Tc (Fig. 1e,f) measured on the same material.
- Combining synchrotron ARPES, core-level XPS and static XAS on one batch gives a useful benchmark (Fig. 1).
- The authors separate spectral windows dominated by the A1g phonon (i, iv) from those free of it (iii). This is a sensible step for isolating electronic dynamics (Fig. 2e, Fig. 4c–e).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The 1/√F scaling of the rise time does not uniquely identify excitonic correlations (Fig. 4e; pp. 9–10; Discussion, p. 11).
   - **What the claim rests on.** The normal-state excitonic claim rests on fitting the "initial response time" of window iii to τ = a/√F + τ0, using about four fluences per temperature. The exponent is fixed and τ0 = 26 fs is free. The argument runs: τ follows the plasmon period, the plasmon frequency goes as √n, and n is proportional to F.
   - **Plasmon argument.** In a semimetal such as TiSe2, the screening that sets exciton breaking depends on the total carrier density: equilibrium carriers plus photoexcited ones. The equilibrium density is strongly temperature dependent, and above Tc it is substantial. The paper does not explain why the photoexcited density alone should set the timescale at 300 K. It also does not explain why the prefactor would be the same at 23 K and 300 K.
   - **Alternative explanations.** Fluence-dependent shortening of the rise is generic. It is expected from:
     - electron–electron thermalisation, whose rate grows with excitation density;
     - state filling and Pauli blocking;
     - saturation of the absorption change;
     - the phonon-free window simply following the build-up of the hot-carrier distribution.

     None of these needs excitons.
   - **Fit statistics.** With about four points per temperature, two free parameters, and a trend set largely by the lowest-fluence point (about 190 fs at 0.2 mJ/cm², with the largest error bar), the data cannot distinguish 1/√F from 1/F, a logarithmic form, or a saturating form. The 23 K point at 2.9 mJ/cm² (about 80 fs) and the 300 K point at 1.9 mJ/cm² (about 87 fs) sit visibly above the curve.
   - **Requested work.**
     - Define precisely which fit parameter is the "initial response time" (ω in Eq. 1, or something else) and explain how ω, t0 and τ are decorrelated.
     - Report the goodness of fit for competing functional forms.
     - Add a control in which excitonic correlations should be absent or much weaker, measured and analysed the same way. Options include a non-excitonic semimetal or semiconductor with a comparable Se or Ti M edge, TiSe2 well above 300 K, or a heavily doped or intercalated TiSe2 in which the excitonic instability is suppressed.
     - Give a quantitative model linking the measured τ to an estimated plasmon frequency, including equilibrium carriers.
   - **If this cannot be done**, the abstract and conclusion ("direct time-domain evidence for incoherent excitonic correlations") should be softened to "consistent with".

2. The order-parameter-like onset in Fig. 3d,e may come from temperature-dependent extrinsic factors (pp. 8–9; Fig. 3).
   - **Fixed delay inside a coherent oscillation.** The data are taken at a single delay of 200 fs. Window ii shows a strong 6 THz A1g oscillation (Fig. 2d; period about 167 fs). The value at 200 fs therefore samples a phonon phase. In TiSe2 the phonon amplitude, frequency and damping all change across Tc, so a snapshot can show an onset at Tc even if the underlying electronic response does not change.
   - **Absorbed energy changes across Tc.** The optical response of TiSe2 changes across Tc in the visible and near-infrared. The absorbed energy density for a fixed incident fluence of 0.2 mJ/cm² is therefore temperature dependent, and the authors do not correct for it.
   - **Edge shift.** The static edges shift continuously with temperature (Fig. 1o–q). A fixed integration window then samples a different part of the edge at each temperature.
   - **Derivative lineshape in Se peak ②.** The transient lineshape of Se peak ② in Fig. 3c is derivative-like: a negative lobe at about 53.2 eV and a positive lobe at about 53.4 eV at 25 K. That shape is typical of a shift or broadening of a peak whose static width narrows on cooling. A larger magnitude at 25 K may then reflect a sharper static peak rather than "additional Se 4p holes from broken excitons".
   - **Requested work.**
     - Show full delay traces at several temperatures across Tc, for example 25, 150, 190, 210 and 300 K, and plot fitted amplitudes (incoherent and phonon parts separately) against temperature.
     - Normalise the signal by the absorbed fluence, using the measured or literature temperature-dependent absorbance of the flake at the pump spectrum.
     - Fit the Se ② transient lineshape to a model of peak shift, broadening and amplitude change, to separate state filling from lineshape effects.

3. The "absence" of a static signature is not established at a sensitivity comparable to the transient measurement (pp. 5–6; Fig. 1j–l, o–q).
   - **Mismatched analyses.** The static data are reduced to fitted peak positions, with scatter of about 10–20 meV. The transient effect is detected as an integrated transmission change of order 10⁻³ in specific windows. For window ii, the static absorption above and below Tc is never compared over the same energy window at the same signal-to-noise. The headline contrast ("equilibrium measurements … showed no observable signals") is therefore partly a contrast between analyses, not between techniques.
   - **Requested work.**
     - Show static difference spectra A(E,T) − A(E,310 K), integrated over windows ii and iii, against temperature, with error bars.
     - Report peak widths and integrated intensities against temperature, not only positions.
     - For XPS, show the Ti 3p and Se 3d lineshapes (widths, asymmetry) across Tc.
   - **If the static integrated windows also show an onset**, the claim that time resolution is required must be revised.

B. Should address

4. Sample heating and the true sample temperature are not quantified (Methods, pp. 18–20).
   - **Cumulative heating.** The sample is a free-standing flake under 100 nm thick on a Cu TEM grid. It is pumped at 1 kHz with up to 2.9 mJ/cm², and radiation shielding was deliberately omitted. The pre-time-zero signal is "uniformly subtracted" with the statement that "millisecond cumulative heating effects … do not change the conclusions". That is asserted, not shown.
   - **Why it matters.** The temperature-dependent and fluence-dependent claims (Figs. 3 and 4) both need the actual steady-state temperature of the pumped spot. At 23 K and high fluence the spot may sit far above the base temperature. A spot above Tc would directly undermine the comparison between "below Tc" and "above Tc" in Fig. 4e.
   - **Requested work.**
     - Report the magnitude of the pre-time-zero signal against fluence and temperature.
     - Estimate the steady-state temperature rise from the flake's thermal link.
     - Check that the Tc inferred from Fig. 3 does not shift with fluence; for example, repeat the temperature scan at a second fluence.

5. Fluence calibration and the proportionality F ∝ n are not justified (pp. 7–8, 10; Methods p. 18).
   - **Nonlinear absorption.** With a 3.4 fs pulse, 1.7–2.9 mJ/cm² corresponds to peak intensities of order 10¹² W/cm². At these intensities multiphoton absorption and saturation are plausible, and they would break the assumed linearity n ∝ F. Linearity sits at the heart of the √n argument.
   - **Threshold comparison.** Thresholds from the literature (refs. 14, 30–34) were measured at different wavelengths, on different sample thicknesses and in different geometries. Comparing incident fluences across these studies is not meaningful.
   - **Inconsistent wording.** The phrase "0.2 mJ/cm² … commensurate with a fluence regime where the CDW order and excitonic correlations are close to be fully suppressed" (p. 8) is confusing. If 0.2 mJ/cm² already nearly suppresses the order, it is unclear why it counts as the "low-fluence" regime that reveals the ordering.
   - **Requested work.**
     - Give absorbed energy density per formula unit (accounting for flake thickness, reflectivity and the transmission through a free-standing flake).
     - Show the amplitude of the incoherent signal in each window against fluence, to demonstrate linearity or quantify the deviation.
     - Restate the fluence regimes in terms of absorbed energy.

6. Reproducibility across samples and the effect of exfoliation are not documented (Methods p. 16; throughout).
   - **Missing information.** The paper does not say how many flakes were measured for Figs. 2–4, whether the onset in Fig. 3 and the scaling in Fig. 4 were reproduced on more than one flake, or whether thermal cycling changes the results.
   - **Possible thickness and handling effects.** The flakes are under 100 nm thick, PDMS-exfoliated and ethanol-transferred. Their Tc and CDW quality may differ from the bulk crystals measured by ARPES. Fig. 3 suggests a similar Tc, but this should be stated and supported, for example by Raman or transport on a sister flake.
   - **Requested work.** Report the number of flakes and flake thicknesses, and show the Fig. 3d/e and Fig. 4e results for at least a second flake.

7. The fluences used below and above Tc are not matched (Fig. 4a,b,e).
   - At 23 K the fluences are 0.2, 0.6, 1.8 and 2.9 mJ/cm²; at 300 K they are 0.2, 0.8, 1.5 and 1.9 mJ/cm².
   - The statement that the two temperatures "reproduce the trend … within experimental uncertainties" therefore relies on interpolating the fit.
   - The 300 K data at 0.2 mJ/cm² are also visibly noisier (Fig. 4b), with large uncertainty on the key low-fluence point.
   - **Requested work.** Measure both temperatures at the same fluences, add intermediate temperatures (for example 150 K and 250 K), and show how τ0 and the prefactor vary with temperature.

8. The inference that excitons, rather than phonons, drive the short-range in-plane order goes beyond the data (pp. 5, 9, 11).
   - **What the paper sets out to do.** It frames the open question as whether short-range order above Tc is "purely phononic" or also has an excitonic contribution.
   - **What the data show.** Even accepting the 1/√F argument, the data show at most that some local electron–hole correlation exists above Tc.
   - **What is concluded.** The Discussion goes further: "exciton correlations … are instrumental in forming the short-range 2D order in-plane before the long-range CDW is formed". The measurement has no momentum resolution and no structural sensitivity. It cannot establish that these correlations are tied to the 2×2 short-range order rather than being generic bound or correlated electron–hole pairs in a small-gap semimetal.
   - **Requested work.** Either provide a link to the short-range order, for example a temperature dependence of τ0 or the prefactor that tracks diffuse-scattering intensity, or restrict the conclusion.

9. The calculations are underspecified and used only qualitatively (pp. 5–6; Methods p. 20; Fig. 1g).
   - **Missing description.** The Methods describe only ground-state PBE DFT. The TDDFT used to assign the "many-body continuum" (Fig. S3, Supplementary Note 1) is not described in the main text: core-hole treatment, kernel, and whether semicore Ti 3p is included in the propagation.
   - **Known PBE limitation.** PBE is known to misdescribe the gap and band overlap of TiSe2, and on its own it does not stabilise the CDW gap seen in ARPES.
   - **Unclear CDW structure.** The CDW-phase DOS in Fig. 1g needs its atomic displacements stated (relaxed or experimental) and the size of the gap it gives.
   - **Comparison with data is qualitative.** No calculated transient or temperature-dependent spectrum is compared with Fig. 2 or Fig. 3.
   - **Requested work.**
     - Describe the TDDFT in the main Methods.
     - State the CDW structure used and test sensitivity to the functional (for example a hybrid functional or DFT+U).
     - Ideally, compute the change in absorption in windows ii and iii between normal and CDW structures, or for a photoexcited carrier distribution, to support the window assignments.

10. The interpretation of Se peak ② needs more support (pp. 8–9).
    - **What is assumed.** The signal is assigned to "Se 4p holes below the Fermi level". This assumes the window probes occupied Se 4p states made empty by excitation.
    - **The tension.** The paper itself assigns peaks ②–⑤ to transitions into unoccupied Se 4p states 0–2 eV and 2.3–4 eV above EF (p. 5).
    - **Requested work.** Clarify the energy of window iii [53.04, 53.33] eV relative to the Se 3d5/2 binding energy plus EF, and show quantitatively that the window samples states just below EF that the pump empties. Also explain why the hole-creation picture predicts an absorption increase of the measured size.

11. The positioning relative to prior XUV transient absorption work on TiSe2 is incomplete (Introduction; Discussion p. 11).
    - **Prior work.** Ref. 22 (Heinrich et al., PRX 2023) already reported "electronic and structural fingerprints of CDW excitations" in XUV transient absorption of TiSe2 at the Ti M edge. Ref. 23 reported temperature-dependent hybridisation in core-level spectroscopy.
    - **Overlapping claim.** The claim that this is "the first demonstration where an attosecond core-level technique … can simultaneously probe low-energy phase transitions" overlaps with this prior work.
    - **Requested work.** Explicitly state what the present data add beyond ref. 22, for example sub-10 fs resolution, simultaneous Se edge coverage and temperature scans through Tc, and moderate the priority claim.

12. The low-fluence sign reversal in the Ti continuum is shown for one delay and one data set (Fig. 2b, green box; p. 8).
    - **Requested work.**
      - Show a fluence series of window ii at fixed delay to locate the crossover fluence.
      - Show whether the sign reversal tracks the CDW suppression threshold in absorbed fluence.
      - Explain why carrier excitation and the proposed many-body process should have opposite signs.
    - **Why it matters.** This is the basis for choosing 0.2 mJ/cm² and 200 fs for Fig. 3.

C. Minor

13. On p. 8 the spectral weight of the folded Se 4p band is cited as Fig. 1f; it is Fig. 1e.

14. Fig. 3d,e use a colour gradient (blue to red) on the markers, and Fig. 3 has "blue curves as guides to the eye". Neither the meaning of the colour nor the absence of visible blue curves in panel d is explained.

15. Eq. 1 calls ω "the intrinsic system response time" while Fig. 4e plots the "initial response time". Use one name and state explicitly whether ω is deconvolved from the IRF. Note also that the IRF (5.4 fs) is much shorter than all the extracted times.

16. Fig. 4e, caption: give the functional form with explicit fitted prefactor and uncertainty, and say whether the blue band is a confidence interval.

17. The Fig. 4 caption is missing its final full stop. The main text has various language issues, for example "time-resolve probes", "Dramatic plasmon response o the" (ref. 16), and "necessitate an access to".

18. State the XUV spectral resolution and energy-calibration uncertainty. They set the floor on the static peak-shift sensitivity in Fig. 1o–q.

19. The data availability statement refers to the Supplementary Information, which was not available with this submission. Several key points depend on it (Figs. S1, S3–S5; Supplementary Notes 1–3). The main text should be self-contained on the IRF determination and the TDDFT.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a non-excitonic reference measurement, analysed with the same Eq. 1 fitting and fluence series as Fig. 4. This is the most direct way to show that the 1/√F rise-time scaling is specific to excitonic correlations.
2. Replace the single-delay temperature scan with fitted amplitudes from full delay traces at a few temperatures, normalised to absorbed fluence. Present them next to matched static difference spectra integrated over the same windows.
3. Report absorbed energy density and fluence linearity, together with an estimate of steady-state heating at 1 kHz, so that the "below Tc" and "above Tc" labels in Fig. 4 are secure.
4. Narrow the Discussion's conclusion about the role of excitons in forming the short-range 2D order unless momentum- or structure-resolved evidence is added. Add a clear comparison with ref. 22.
