Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript uses a cryogenic attosecond broadband XUV transient absorption setup (ABXAS) on exfoliated 1T-TiSe2 flakes. It reports three findings. Static core-level photoemission and absorption show no signature at Tc. The transient signal at 0.2 ps in two spectral windows (the Ti "many-body continuum", window ii, and Se peak 2, window iii) has an order-parameter-like onset at Tc. The fluence dependence of the initial response time in window iii follows tau ~ 1/sqrt(F) + tau0 both below (23 K) and above (300 K) Tc, which the authors read as evidence for short-range excitonic correlations in the normal state. The question is important: whether excitonic correlations exist above Tc in TiSe2 is a real, open debate, and a broadband cryogenic attosecond probe that can follow it with temperature would be a useful advance. However, the central excitonic claim rests on a single scaling argument applied to a small data set, and the novelty relative to earlier XUV transient absorption work on the same material (ref. 22) is not established.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Technically demanding experiment: few-fs pump, 5.4 fs IRF, broadband coverage of both Ti M2,3 and Se M4,5 edges, cryogenic sample stage with drift correction, and dense temperature scans (~5 K steps, 20-320 K) in transient XUV absorption.
- The static benchmark is useful and thorough. Synchrotron ARPES valence observables (gap, folded-band spectral weight), core-level XPS and static XUV absorption are measured on the same footing (Fig. 1). This makes a clear case that equilibrium core-level spectra are, at most, weakly sensitive to the transition.
- The temperature-dependent transient data in Fig. 3d,e show a clear and reasonably sharp change at ~200 K in two element-specific windows.
- Identifying a spectral window (Se peak 2) without visible A1g coherent-phonon modulation, and comparing it with phonon-dominated windows (Fig. 4c-e), is a sensible way to separate electronic from lattice timescales.
- The question addressed (excitonic correlations above Tc) is timely, given the conflicting M-EELS results (refs. 10, 16).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The 1/sqrt(F) scaling in Fig. 4e does not uniquely identify excitonic correlations, and the fit is underconstrained.
   - What the claim rests on. The normal-state excitonic claim rests entirely on the fluence dependence of the window-iii initial response time: four fluences at 23 K and four at 300 K, fitted with tau ~ 1/sqrt(F) + tau0 with tau0 = 26 fs.
   - Alternative explanations. Several mechanisms without excitons predict a faster rise at higher excitation density:
     - Electron-electron scattering and cascade times that shorten with excited-carrier density. The pump photons (500-1000 nm) create carriers far from E_F, and these must relax into the Se 4p hole states near Gamma.
     - Density-dependent screening of the free-carrier plasma itself.
     - State-filling saturation of the probed window.
     - A shift of t0 with fluence that is absorbed into the rise parameter.
   - The offset. tau0 is an extra free parameter introduced ad hoc ("time needed for creating mobile Se 4p holes"). It largely removes the distinction between 1/sqrt(F), 1/F, logarithmic or linear-decreasing forms over a fluence range of only about one decade.
   - The data scatter. The 300 K points are non-monotonic (1.5 mJ/cm2 at ~65 fs, 1.9 mJ/cm2 at ~87 fs). The 23 K point at 2.9 mJ/cm2 (~81 fs) lies above the curve. The 0.2 mJ/cm2 points carry error bars of ~±20-30 fs.
   - Inconsistency with the screening picture. In a semimetal the plasma frequency that screens the electron-hole attraction includes the equilibrium carriers, whose density differs above and below Tc. The proposed mechanism would therefore predict different curves at 23 K and 300 K, not the same one.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Report the fit with and without tau0, and compare it with alternative functional forms (chi-squared or information criteria).
     - Add more fluences, especially below 0.6 mJ/cm2, where the forms differ most.
     - Measure tau at fixed low fluence as a function of temperature well above Tc (e.g., up to 320 K and beyond, if possible). If excitonic correlations weaken with temperature, tau(T) should show it.
     - Provide a null comparison: a window, or a reference material without excitonic correlations (e.g., a related TMD such as TiS2 or a metallic dichalcogenide), measured under matched pump conditions, showing that the free-carrier rise time does not scale this way.
     - Quantitatively show that the scale of tau (100-200 fs at 0.2 mJ/cm2) is consistent with the inverse plasma frequency for the estimated excited density, as in ref. 40.
   - Without these steps the abstract's "direct time-domain evidence for incoherent excitonic correlations" is not supported.

2. Window iii is used both as a probe of long-range order (Fig. 3e) and as a probe blind to long-range coherence (Fig. 4). These two uses contradict each other.
   - Fig. 3. Page 9 states that the Se peak 2 transient amplitude "shows a clear onset right at Tc", and Fig. 3e is described as "quasi-constant above Tc". This is presented as a probe of long-range order.
   - Fig. 4. On page 10 the same feature is said to be "agnostic about the existence of long-range phase coherence among the excitons", because it reflects localized dipole transitions. This is what justifies interpreting the 300 K fluence dependence as short-range correlations.
   - The tension. If window iii were sensitive to local excitonic correlations, which the paper argues persist above Tc (and which the ARPES fluctuation tail in Fig. 1e,f shows), its amplitude should not be flat above Tc. If its amplitude is set by long-range order, the paper must explain why its kinetics would be insensitive to it.
   - What the authors should do. Reconcile these two statements with a concrete model of what the window-iii amplitude and rise time each measure. Show explicitly how the amplitude and the rise time behave together as functions of T at fixed fluence. The excitonic interpretation in Section 3 depends on this distinction.

3. Novelty relative to existing XUV transient absorption on TiSe2 (ref. 22) is not established.
   - The paper claims "the first demonstration where an attosecond core-level technique ... can simultaneously probe low-energy phase transitions" (page 11). It also presents the time-resolved sensitivity to CDW order as its main technical result.
   - Ref. 22 (Heinrich et al., PRX 2023) reports electronic and structural fingerprints of CDW excitations in XUV transient absorption of 1T-TiSe2, at the same Ti and Se M edges. The present manuscript also relies on ref. 22 for the static temperature shift and the many-body continuum assignment.
   - What the authors should do. State precisely what ref. 22 observed (including any temperature dependence across Tc, coherent amplitude-mode signatures, or Ti-edge changes) and what is new here: few-fs resolution, temperature resolution, or the normal-state fluence dependence. Revise the "first demonstration" and "unambiguously" wording to match.
   - Similarly, transient optical and photoemission probes are well known to show order-parameter-like onsets at Tc in TiSe2. The authors should explain what core-level element specificity adds beyond those probes.

B. Should address

4. The sample temperature and Tc of the measured flakes are not independently established.
   - The XUV data come from free-standing exfoliated flakes (<100 nm, ~400 µm wide) on Cu TEM grids. They are pumped at 1 kHz with fluences up to ~3 mJ/cm2, in a cryostat without radiation shielding, with a base temperature of ~20 K. Thermal contact through a TEM grid is poor, and the steady-state heating is not quantified.
   - The pre-time-zero signal is subtracted, and the authors state that the cumulative millisecond heating "does not change the conclusions" (page 18). However, a heating offset would shift the apparent Tc in Fig. 3d,e. It would also make the "23 K" and "300 K" labels in Fig. 4 uncertain, particularly across the fluence series in Fig. 4a,b, where heating increases with fluence.
   - The Tc and quality of the flakes are inferred from bulk ARPES (a different sample and preparation). Exfoliated TiSe2 is air- and solvent-sensitive, and the flakes were released in ethanol. Thin and strained TiSe2 can have a modified Tc.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Give the flake thickness and the number of flakes.
     - Characterize Tc on the flake itself, e.g., Raman or electron diffraction of superlattice peaks, or the static XUV thermometry they already have.
     - Estimate the steady-state and per-pulse temperature rise (absorbed energy, heat capacity, conduction to the grid).
     - Show the pre-time-zero signal versus temperature and fluence.
     - Check the Fig. 3d,e onset for warming and cooling ramps.

5. The fluence regime in Figs. 2-3 is ambiguous, and the low-fluence sign reversal rests on two fluences.
   - The text describes 0.2 mJ/cm2 as "commensurate with a fluence regime where the CDW order and excitonic correlations are close to be fully suppressed" (page 8). It describes 1.7 mJ/cm2 as an order of magnitude above the melting threshold (page 7).
   - It is therefore unclear whether the 0.2 mJ/cm2 data in Fig. 3 are in a perturbative regime, which is what is needed to read Fig. 3d,e as a probe of equilibrium order.
   - The sign reversal in the Ti window ii (Fig. 2b, green box, data ×2) is identified from only two fluences. Its signal is a few ×10^-4.
   - What the authors should do. Give the absorbed fluence and the excited-carrier density. Show a fluence series of the window-ii and window-iii signals at 0.2 ps at base temperature, to locate any threshold or crossover. Quote the signal-to-noise of the window-ii sign change.

6. The temperature dependence in Fig. 3 could partly reflect temperature-dependent pump absorption.
   - The optical constants of TiSe2 in the 1.2-2.5 eV pump range change across Tc. The absorbed energy density, and hence every transient amplitude, could therefore change at Tc for reasons unrelated to the specific excitonic picture invoked on page 9 ("additional Se 4p holes created via the breaking of condensed excitons").
   - What the authors should do. Normalize the Fig. 3d,e amplitudes to the absorbed fluence at each temperature, using measured or literature reflectivity and transmission. Show that a non-specific window, e.g., an incoherent carrier-dominated region, does not display the same onset.

7. The static "null" result in Fig. 1o-q is based only on fitted peak positions.
   - The claim that static core-level absorption is blind to the transition is supported by peak-position fits.
   - Changes in lineshape, width, or integrated intensity, particularly in the broad Ti 38-53 eV region that the paper later identifies as most sensitive, are not analysed. No sensitivity estimate is given.
   - What the authors should do. Show static difference spectra A(E,T) - A(E,300 K) in windows ii and iii, with noise levels. Fit or differentiate the peak-position curves (Fig. 1o-q) for slope changes at Tc. Fig. 1o in particular appears to show a change in slope in the 150-200 K range that should be checked.
   - A quantified upper bound on static changes would make the contrast with the transient data meaningful.

8. The assignment of the Ti "many-body continuum", and the TDDFT supporting it, are under-documented.
   - The broad 38-53 eV feature is assigned to valence-electron excitations accompanying the core transition. This rests on TDDFT (Supplementary Note 1, Fig. S3) and ref. 22.
   - The delayed, broadened Ti M2,3 edge in early 3d metals is also commonly attributed to 3p-3d exchange and multiplet effects (a giant resonance with super-Coster-Kronig decay). Under that reading, transient changes in window ii would reflect local 3d occupation or screening rather than CDW-related valence excitations.
   - The Methods describe only ground-state PBE DFT. Missing information:
     - TDDFT details: whether Ti 3p is treated as valence in the pseudopotential, the real-time propagation parameters, and the kernel (adiabatic LDA/PBE?).
     - Treatment of spin-orbit coupling.
     - Whether the 24-atom CDW cell is relaxed, and with what distortion amplitude.
   - PBE is known to misdescribe the TiSe2 band overlap.
   - What the authors should do. Provide these details in the main text or Methods, discuss the multiplet alternative, and, if possible, compute the transient (or normal-versus-CDW) change in the Ti continuum to compare with Fig. 3b.

9. The absence of the A1g phonon in window iii needs quantification.
   - The choice of Se peak 2 as a phonon-free reporter (pages 7, 10) is based on visual absence of 6 THz oscillations in Fig. 2e.
   - What the authors should do. Give an upper bound on the oscillation amplitude relative to window iv (Fourier transform of the residuals). Show that the window-iii rise time is not partly set by a small displacive phonon contribution.
   - This matters because the high-fluence window-iii rise times (~65-85 fs) approach the phonon-limited value (~50 fs, gray band in Fig. 4e).

10. Statistics and reproducibility are not documented.
    - The paper does not state how many flakes were measured, whether the Fig. 3 onset and the Fig. 4 scaling were reproduced on more than one flake or after thermal cycling, or how many repeats underlie the "1 s.d. of repeated measurements" in Fig. 3d,e.
    - What the authors should do. Report these numbers and show at least one repeat of the key Fig. 3d,e and Fig. 4e results on an independent flake.

C. Minor

11. Notation in Eq. (1) and Fig. 4e is inconsistent. In Eq. (1), omega is the "intrinsic system response time" and tau the relaxation time. Fig. 4e plots an "initial response time" and the caption calls it tau. State which fit parameter is plotted (omega, tau, or a 10-90% rise derived from both). State how strongly it is correlated with t0 in the fits.
12. Page 8 cites "the spectral weight of the folded Se 4p band (Fig. 1f)". This should be Fig. 1e.
13. Pump parameters differ between sections:
    - Fluence: 1.7 mJ/cm2 in Fig. 2 versus 1.8 mJ/cm2 in Fig. 4a.
    - Central wavelength: 750 nm in the Fig. 2 caption versus 790 nm (before broadening) in the Methods.
    - Pulse duration: "sub-4-fs" pump versus 3.4 fs FWHM.
    Clarify these, and give the pump spot size and the pump/probe spot-size ratio.
14. Fig. 3d,e are described as "quasi-constant above Tc". The ARPES observables in Fig. 1e,f, by contrast, show a fluctuation tail. Comment on this difference (see also concern 2).
15. Wording: "unambiguously shows" (page 8), "direct time-domain evidence" (abstract), and "resolve the debate" (page 10) overstate the evidence. Typos: "spectroscopes" (abstract), "necessitate an access" (abstract), "time-resolve probes" (page 11), "close to be fully suppressed" (page 8).
16. The 6.0 THz mode is assigned to A1g. In the CDW phase, the zone-folded amplitude mode lies near 3.4 THz. State whether it is observed in any window, since it is a direct marker of the long-range order the paper discusses.
17. Several key elements are only in the Supplementary Information (Figs. S1-S5, Notes 1-3), including the IRF determination and TDDFT. The main text should summarize them enough to be self-contained.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a temperature series of the window-iii rise time at fixed low fluence, from 20 K to the highest accessible temperature, alongside the amplitude in Fig. 3e. Together these would directly test whether the kinetics track short-range correlations while the amplitude tracks long-range order.
2. Measure a reference compound without an excitonic instability, e.g., TiS2 or a metallic 1T dichalcogenide, under matched pump conditions, to calibrate the free-carrier fluence dependence of the Se/S M-edge rise time.
3. Characterize the measured flake directly (thickness, Tc, oxidation state) and quantify the sample temperature under illumination. The static XUV peak shifts in Fig. 1o-q could serve as an in-situ thermometer.
4. Put the work in context with ref. 22 and with transient optical and photoemission studies, and frame the main contribution around what element-specific, few-fs core-level data uniquely add.
