A. Must address

1. The regime in which the expansion is valid is not stated, and every parameter set used to demonstrate it sits at or beyond its edge. Eq. (13) expands in √ke at fixed ko, "to linear order in ke" (text above Eq. (3)). The corrections that carry the physics, however, are O(ke·ko): the slip and rarefaction terms and the 4ke ko 𝒴₁ term in Eq. (3), the ke ko term in Eq. (5), and the 3ko²/(2G²) term in Eqs. (15b) and (7). The layer width is √(ke·ko) (the variable χ in Eq. (16)), and the layer terms scale as ke^{3/2} ko^{1/2}. The expansion therefore needs ke·ko ≪ 1 (i.e. ℓo ≪ L²/ℓe), not only ke ≪ 1, and the paper never says so. In Fig. 1(d),(e) (ke = 0.1, ko = 2), √(ke·ko) ≈ 0.45 against a channel half-width of 0.5, so the layers from the two walls span the channel and overlap; the construction h = h_B + h_T with independent layer corrections at each wall (Methods) assumes they do not. In Fig. 1(c) and the ΔRxy example (ke = 0.025, ko = 5), √(ke·ko) ≈ 0.35 and the O(ke·ko) slip term (≈ 0.125) is about 40% of the leading 1/3, hardly a small correction; the zero-field conductance correction is roughly 45%. Conversely, when ke·ko ≪ 1 Eq. (5) itself says the corrections are small, so the headline effects ("non-equilibrium effects penetrate significantly deeper", Stokes–Ohm "strongly under-estimated" and "inapplicable" in the degenerate regime) are large only where control is lost. The physical motivation (Discussion: T ≲ 0.1 T_F, ℓo/ℓe ~ (T_F/T)² ≳ 100, ℓo ~ (T_F/T)⁴) is the limit ko ≫ 1, which together with ℓe ≪ L leaves a narrow window; as ko → ∞ (the strongly tomographic, superdiffusive regime of Ref. [16]) Eq. (5) grows without bound. The statement that "excellent agreement is observed, with similar agreement for other parameter values" (p. 5) is not documented, and the good agreement at the shown parameters is not explained. The authors should state the distinguished limit (e.g. ke → 0 at fixed ko or at fixed ke·ko), the condition for the wall layers not to overlap, and the formal remainder (O(ke²), O(ke² ko²) or O((ke ko)^{3/2})); show the asymptotic-minus-numerical error against ke at fixed ko and against ko at fixed ke up to ke·ko ~ 1, at zero field and at Rc ~ ℓo, confirming the expected residual scaling, ideally as a validity map in the (ke, ko) plane with rc as a third parameter (for example a 5% error contour) on which real materials are marked; give the parameter window in (T/T_F, L) for a real material where the theory applies; and qualify the general conclusions in the Introduction and Discussion accordingly.

3. The non-monotonic magnetoconductance is called an "unequivocal macroscopic signature" of tomographic flow (p. 3, Fig. 1(c), Eq. (5)), but no null comparison is shown. For ℓo ≳ L the tomographic minimum at Rc ≈ 3ℓo falls at Rc ~ L, the same field range as the familiar boundary-scattering magnetoresistance peak of ballistic or quasi-ballistic channels with diffuse edges (maximum near W/Rc ≈ 0.55; Refs. [12, 36] and the classic 2DEG literature) and as viscous negative magnetoresistance and viscous-to-ballistic crossovers (Ref. [34]). A conventional single-rate kinetic model with diffuse walls also gives negative then positive magnetoresistance in narrow channels. Without a comparison, the claim that both ℓe and ℓo can be extracted from the magnetoconductance is not established. The authors should overlay on Fig. 1(c) the numerical G_ch(1/rc) for the conventional model with ko = ke and the same diffuse boundaries and matched ℓe and ℓMR, a quasi-ballistic reference with ℓe comparable to L, and the no-slip hydrodynamic baseline; identify which feature is specific to ℓo ≫ ℓe (the position ratio of the two features, the depth scaling as ke·ko, the ko dependence, or the lineshape); and state whether a fit of Eq. (5) to such a curve can recover ℓe and ℓo without degeneracy with a conventional model.

4. Eq. (2) is a two-rate relaxation model, but the conclusions are stated for degenerate Fermi liquids in general. Eq. (2) relaxes all even modes m ≥ 2 with one length ℓe and all odd modes m ≥ 3 with one length ℓo. The microscopic and exact-diagonalization studies the authors cite (Refs. [14–18]) find odd-mode rates that grow strongly with m (roughly as m⁴ log m at low temperature), and that m-dependence is what produces the superdiffusion and scale-dependent viscosity of Ref. [16]. The diffuse-wall boundary layer is populated by high harmonics: the histograms in Fig. 1(d) show substantial weight up to m ≈ 5, exactly the modes the two-rate model misrepresents. The layer functions 𝒴₀, 𝒴₁, 𝒯₀, 𝒯₁, the single layer width √(ℓeℓo), the slip coefficient 32/(15π) in Eq. (17), the factor 3 in 3ko/rc (which reflects the m = 3 harmonic), and whether ℓo in Eq. (5) is ℓ₃ or a weighted average may all change quantitatively or qualitatively. Meanwhile the Introduction and Discussion call a "dual relaxation-time description" "fundamentally limited" and recommend Eq. (2) as "the appropriate starting point", although Eq. (2) is itself a dual (even/odd) relaxation-time model; Ref. [31], cited as going "beyond the dual-relaxation time approximation", is not available to the reader. The authors should redo at least the numerical Boltzmann benchmark (velocity profile, Fig. 1(c) and ΔRxy) with an m-dependent odd spectrum (e.g. ℓ_m = ℓ₃(3/m)⁴, or γ_m ∝ m⁴ normalized to a given ℓo), show which of the four effects survive and what "ko" the magnetoconductance would then measure, and either justify the two-rate reduction (for example as an effective ℓo for the slowest odd mode) or narrow the claims to the two-rate model.

5. The high-field part of Eq. (5) is inserted by hand, and the claim of validity "for ke ≲ 1 at arbitrary magnetic field strength" (p. 5) is not derived. The upturn at Rc ~ 2ℓe comes from a prefactor 1 + (2ke/rc)² that the authors "also include"; it does not follow from integrating Eq. (3). At fixed rc it is O(ke²), beyond the order kept, and it becomes O(1) only when rc ~ ke (1/rc ~ 40 in Fig. 1(c)). In that regime the 1/rc terms in Eq. (2) and in 𝒥₄ of Eq. (15b) are O(1/ke), so the Lorentz term is of the same order as the even-mode collision term and the expansion, in which rc enters only as O(1) through ko/rc, is reordered; the Stokes–Ohm leading order itself should be modified, consistent with the viscosity reduction the authors invoke, and the first-order slip coefficient 32/(15π) in Eq. (17), which has no field dependence, should be modified at Rc ~ ℓe. Eq. (5) therefore combines results from two orderings, and Fig. 1(c) extends to 1/rc ≈ 10², i.e. Rc down to ℓe/2.5. Since measuring ℓo and ℓe from the two field scales is the paper's main experimental proposal, the authors should derive a uniformly valid composite result for rc = O(ke), or state that Eq. (5) is heuristic, say which terms are valid where, and give its numerical error across the full 1/rc axis, explaining the agreement up to 1/rc ≈ 50. The numerical points in Fig. 1(c) are also sparse in the decreasing branch (1/rc ≈ 0.05–1), where the tomographic suppression is the claimed signature; more points there would show whether the Lorentzian form 1/(1 + (3ko/rc)²) is quantitatively right.
