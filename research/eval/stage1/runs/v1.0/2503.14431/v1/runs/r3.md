Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript solves the linearized kinetic equation for a two-dimensional Fermi liquid with separate even-mode and odd-mode relaxation lengths (ℓ_e ≪ L ≲ ℓ_o) in a channel with diffuse edges. It uses a matched asymptotic expansion in √k_e (k_e = ℓ_e/L) and gives closed-form velocity and Hall-field profiles (Eqs. 3–4) and a channel conductance (Eq. 5). The reported effects are: a "tomographic boundary layer" of width √(ℓ_e ℓ_o); a slip velocity of order ℓ_e ℓ_o/L that raises the conductance; rarefaction corrections in the bulk; and fast suppression of all three by a magnetic field, which gives a non-monotonic magneto-conductance. The authors also argue that tomographic corrections spoil existing ways of extracting the Hall viscosity. An analytical treatment of boundary-driven tomographic flow would be a useful addition to the electron-hydrodynamics literature. The strength of the claims, however, depends on whether the expansion holds in the regime it targets and whether the proposed signatures belong to the tomographic regime alone.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A systematic matched-asymptotic treatment of the two-rate kinetic equation with diffuse boundaries. It gives closed-form expressions (Eqs. 3–5, 17–18) where previous work used no-slip conditions or purely numerical solutions.
- The physical picture is clear. The leading order recovers Stokes–Ohm flow. At O(k_e) there is a second-order-type slip condition (Eq. 17) of the same form as in rarefied-gas dynamics, plus a boundary layer described by universal functions 𝒴_0, 𝒴_1, 𝒯_0, 𝒯_1 (Fig. 2).
- The asymptotic profiles and conductance agree well, by eye, with direct numerical solutions of the linearized Boltzmann equation (Fig. 1c–e). The Hall resistance also agrees in one case (ΔR_xy = 0.20 analytic against 0.19 numerical).
- The paper makes testable predictions: magneto-conductance features at R_c ≈ 3ℓ_o and R_c ≈ 2ℓ_e, and a warning about Hall-viscosity protocols. Both are relevant to current imaging and transport experiments.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Regime of validity of the expansion in the tomographic limit. Eq. (13) expands in √k_e with k_o held at O(1). The layer width is √(k_e k_o), and the corrections in Eq. (3) scale as k_e k_o and k_e^{3/2} k_o^{1/2}. Eq. (1), however, defines the regime as L ≲ ℓ_o, and the physics that motivates the paper (Discussion: ℓ_o ∼ (T_F/T)^4 ≫ ℓ_e) is the limit k_o ≫ 1. There √(k_e k_o) and k_e k_o need not be small, and the expansion presumably breaks down. In every parameter set shown, the layer is not thin compared with the channel: √(k_e k_o) ≈ 0.45 for Fig. 1(d,e) (k_e = 0.1, k_o = 2) and ≈ 0.35 for Fig. 1(c) and the ΔR_xy example (k_e = 0.025, k_o = 5). The channel half-width is 0.5, so the two layers overlap. That conflicts with the bulk-plus-independent-layer decomposition in Methods. The good agreement with numerics is encouraging, but it is not explained and it is not mapped out. This matters because the headline statements ("non-equilibrium effects penetrate significantly deeper", "Stokes–Ohm ... does not describe electron flows in the degenerate regime") are made about tomographic flow in general, while the theory is controlled only for √(k_e k_o) ≪ 1. The authors should:
   - state the validity condition explicitly, for example √(k_e k_o) ≪ 1 together with k_e ≪ 1, and say how large k_o may be;
   - show the error between Eq. (3) or Eq. (5) and the numerics as a function of k_e at fixed k_o, and as a function of k_o at fixed k_e, confirming the expected scaling of the residual (O(k_e^{3/2}) or O(k_e²));
   - show where the expansion fails as k_o → ∞, and relate that limit to the Gurzhi et al. R_c^{-1/3} prediction, which the text dismisses as "qualitative" (p. 5) but which may simply describe the opposite limit, k_o ≫ 1/√k_e.

2. Internal inconsistencies in the main result, Eq. (3). Several signs and coefficients in the central formula do not match the Methods or the figures.
   - (i) The O(k_e⁰) term is written as G²[cosh(2x/G)/cosh(1/G) − 1], which is negative for |x| < 1/2. The Poiseuille-type solution plotted in Fig. 1(d) (centre value ≈ 0.35 for G = 1) and the hydrodynamic conductance of 1/3 in Eq. (5) both correspond to G²[1 − cosh(2x/G)/cosh(1/G)].
   - (ii) Eq. (15c) sets ℐ_0 = 0. Eq. (14) then has no driving term at n = 0, and its no-slip solution is identically zero. The electric-field source (−2k_e v_y in Eq. 2) must appear somewhere, presumably as ℐ_0 = 1.
   - (iii) The boundary-layer term in Eq. (3) has coefficient +4k_e k_o 𝒴_1. Eq. (18a) gives k_e(k_o/2)·∂²u^(0)/∂x²·𝒴_1. At the wall ∂²u^(0)/∂x² = −4, so the coefficient is −2k_e k_o 𝒴_1: different in both sign and magnitude. A rough evaluation using the values in Fig. 2(a) suggests that only the latter brings the velocity close to zero at the wall, as Fig. 1(d) shows. The 𝒴_0 term, by contrast, is consistent between Eqs. (3) and (18a).
   - (iv) The "Bulk (with slip)" curve in Fig. 1(d) peaks at ≈ 0.50. Evaluating the bulk terms of Eq. (3) (hydrodynamic plus slip plus rarefaction) at x = 0 for k_e = 0.1, k_o = 2, G = 1 gives ≈ 0.47; the hydrodynamic and slip terms alone give ≈ 0.51. It is therefore unclear which terms the curve contains.

   Because Eq. (3) is presented as "the main result of this work" and readers will use it directly, the authors should check every sign and prefactor in Eqs. (3), (4), (14)–(18) against the code that produced Fig. 1, and say exactly which terms each curve in Fig. 1(d,e) includes.

3. Is the non-monotonic magneto-conductance a unique tomographic signature, and is its high-field part within the theory? The proposed "unequivocal macroscopic signature" (p. 3, Fig. 1c, Eq. 5) has two problems.
   - (a) The upturn at R_c ∼ 2ℓ_e comes from the prefactor 1 + (2k_e/r_c)², which the authors "also include" (p. 5). It does not follow from integrating Eq. (3). It is formally O(k_e²) unless 1/r_c ∼ 1/k_e, and in that limit the Lorentz term in Eq. (2) is of the same order as the even-mode collision term, which the expansion does not treat (r_c appears only as O(1) through k_o/r_c). Eq. (5) therefore combines results from two different orderings. The agreement with numerics up to 1/r_c ≈ 50 needs to be explained, or the composite formula derived.
   - (b) A conventional single-rate kinetic model (k_o = k_e) with diffuse walls, or ballistic boundary scattering when ℓ ∼ L, also gives negative and then positive magnetoresistance in narrow channels. Examples are the ballistic peak near W/R_c ∼ 0.5 and viscous negative magnetoresistance. Since ℓ_o ≳ L, the predicted drop at R_c ≈ 3ℓ_o falls at the same field scale as these geometric effects.

   The authors should overlay on Fig. 1(c) the numerical G_ch(1/r_c) for k_o = k_e (no tomographic effect) and for a purely ballistic channel at comparable ℓ, and identify which features (position, depth, scaling with k_o) distinguish the tomographic case. Without that comparison, the claim that both ℓ_e and ℓ_o can be extracted from magneto-conductance is not established.

B. Should address

4. The two-rate collision model limits the claim to go "beyond the dual relaxation-time approximation". Eq. (2) gives all odd modes m ≥ 3 one rate (1/k_o) and all even modes m ≥ 2 another (1/k_e). The Fermi-liquid odd-mode rates the paper cites [14–18] depend strongly on m; for example, γ_m ∝ m⁴ (up to logarithms) at low m, which underlies the superdiffusion of Ref. [16]. The boundary layer is populated by high harmonics: the histograms in Fig. 1(d) show weight up to m ≈ 5. Its width, √(k_e k_o), and the slip coefficients (32/15π, and the factor 3 in 3k_o/r_c, which reflects the m = 3 harmonic) may therefore change substantially with m-dependent rates. The Discussion's conclusion that Eq. (2) is "the appropriate starting point" for degenerate electrons is stronger than the evidence. The authors should either show, with at least a numerical calculation using m-dependent odd rates, that the layer width, slip and magneto-conductance dip persist qualitatively, or narrow the claim to the two-rate model.

5. The Hall-resistance conclusion rests on one parameter point and lacks a non-tomographic reference. The statement that ΔR_xy "does not provide an accurate avenue" to the Hall viscosity (pp. 6–7) uses one parameter set (k_e = 0.025, k_o = 5, 1/r_c = 0.02, G → ∞).
   - Eq. (11) carries an explicit factor of r_c, so its weak-field behaviour depends on how 𝒯_0 and 𝒯_1 vanish as k_o/r_c → 0. This should be shown.
   - The authors should plot ΔR_xy against 1/r_c, and for several k_o, alongside the Hall-viscosity prediction of Eq. (9).
   - They should also compute the same quantity for k_o = k_e. Boundary (Knudsen-layer) contributions to the Hall field already exist in conventional kinetic theory (cf. their Ref. [23]), and it should be clear whether the failure of the Scaffidi et al. protocol is specific to the tomographic regime or generic to any boundary kinetic layer.

6. Only fully diffuse boundaries are treated. Eq. (12) assumes fully diffuse reflection. The slip coefficient, the layer functions and the sign of ΔR_xy are all boundary-controlled quantities, and real edges (etched graphene, hBN-encapsulated devices, PdCoO₂ channels) are partially specular. A Maxwell-type accommodation coefficient, or at least a numerical check at partial specularity, would show whether the four "distinguishing features" survive. The statement that the effects "persist for an arbitrary device geometry" (p. 3) is supported only by a reference to unpublished work [31]. It should be removed or backed up here.

7. The experimental feasibility and parameter-extraction claims need support. The Discussion states that the regime of Eq. (1) "is readily accessible" and that ℓ_e and ℓ_o can be inferred from G_ch(B), but no material numbers are given.
   - For a specific system (GaAs, graphene, PdCoO₂), the authors should estimate ℓ_e, ℓ_o, ℓ_MR and L at T ≲ 0.1 T_F, and the corresponding fields 3ℓ_o and 2ℓ_e.
   - Eq. (5) is given only for G → ∞. Real devices have finite ℓ_MR, which adds a third unknown and also affects the field dependence (Eq. 3 contains G in the slip and rarefaction terms). The authors should give G_ch for finite G.
   - They should demonstrate on synthetic numerical data that ℓ_e and ℓ_o can be recovered with reasonable uncertainty when G is also unknown.
   - A comparison with existing channel magnetotransport data, even qualitative, would strengthen the paper considerably.

8. The numerical benchmark is undocumented and not quantified. The "direct numerical solutions to the linearized Boltzmann equation" are the main validation of Eqs. (3)–(5), yet the method is not described. The authors should give the angular discretization (number of harmonics), the spatial grid near the walls, how the diffuse condition is imposed, and convergence tests. Agreement should also be quantified, for example as the maximum and integrated deviation for each panel. The visible offsets in Fig. 1(e), both in the velocity at the centre and in the Hall inset (asymptotics above numerics by several per cent), should be discussed and attributed to a specific omitted order.

9. Novelty relative to prior work and strength of the claims. The magnetic-field suppression of tomographic transport was the subject of Ref. [20], which shares a co-author. The matched-asymptotic method for near-hydrodynamic channel flow is in Refs. [23, 24]. Ref. [16] already derived a tomographic channel equation of identical structure (Eq. 16). The authors should state precisely what is new relative to these works. They should also tone down statements such as "the conventional ... description ... is fundamentally limited and does not describe electron flows in the degenerate regime". Their own result recovers Stokes–Ohm at leading order, with corrections controlled by k_e k_o.

10. The scope of the Holder et al. protocol result is narrow. The tomographic correction in Eq. (7) is proportional to 1/G², so it vanishes in clean channels (G → ∞), which are exactly the systems where tomographic effects are expected to be strongest. The authors should state the range of G and k_o over which this correction is comparable to the Hall-viscosity term, using realistic numbers. They should also explain why the first term of Eq. (7) lacks the 1/(1 + (2k_e/r_c)²) factor present in Eq. (9).

C. Minor

11. The Fig. 1(c) caption calls the quantity "Magnetoconductivity" and scales it by e²nℰL³/(m v_F ℓ_e). Elsewhere it is a channel conductance (Eq. 5), and a conductance scale should not contain the driving field ℰ. The axis labels should also use the same symbol as Eq. (5).

12. Notation and typographical issues:
    - "ℓ_0" in the Discussion should be ℓ_o.
    - "the '1' in Eq. (15c)" on p. 8 should refer to Eq. (15b).
    - "Fig 1(b)" and "Fig 1(c)" lack periods.
    - On p. 6, "this tomographic finite-wavelength such a correction" is garbled.

13. Eq. (5) is quoted to "+o(k_e)", but integrating the boundary-layer terms of Eq. (3) gives O(k_e^{3/2} k_o^{1/2}) contributions. For the parameters of Fig. 1(c), these are not negligible next to the retained O(k_e) terms. Please state the size of the omitted term.

14. The momentum-conserving term in Eq. (2) restores only u_y. This is fine for the channel, where u_x = 0, but since the authors claim generality to other geometries, the conserving term for u_x should be noted.

15. A short appendix giving the 2D derivation of the slip coefficient 32/(15π), and tabulated values or fits of 𝒴_{0,1} and 𝒯_{0,1}, would make Eqs. (3)–(4) usable by experimentalists without re-solving Eq. (16).

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a phase diagram in the (k_e, k_o) plane showing where the asymptotic solution is accurate (for example, a 5% error contour against numerics) and where the tomographic corrections exceed the hydrodynamic Hall-viscosity and slip effects.
- Use the predicted temperature scalings (ℓ_e ∝ T⁻², ℓ_o ∝ T⁻⁴) to give an experimental fingerprint: how the minimum of G_ch(B) should move with temperature, which a single-rate model would not reproduce.
- Provide the velocity and Hall-field profiles in dimensional units for one realistic device, for direct comparison with scanning-probe imaging (Refs. [5, 7]).
- Release the numerical solver, or tabulate the layer functions, so others can reproduce Fig. 1 and test the expressions.
