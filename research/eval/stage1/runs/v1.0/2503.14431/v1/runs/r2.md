Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript solves the linearized Fermi-liquid kinetic equation for flow in a channel with diffuse edges by matched asymptotics. The collision model has separate even-mode (ℓe) and odd-mode (ℓo) relaxation lengths in the "tomographic" ordering ℓe ≪ L ≲ ℓo, and includes a perpendicular magnetic field. It reports four results: a boundary layer of width √(ℓeℓo), an extra velocity slip of order ke·ko, a bulk rarefaction correction that flattens the profile, and fast suppression of all three by a weak field. Together these give a non-monotonic magnetoconductance, Eq. (5), and a Hall-resistance deviation, Eq. (11), dominated by the boundary layer rather than by the Hall viscosity. A closed-form, numerically checked description of how a tomographic Fermi liquid flows in a bounded geometry would be useful, because published analyses use either no-slip hydrodynamics or brute-force numerics. The main open questions are: does the expansion hold where the effects are large; is the result robust to a realistic tomographic collision operator; and is the proposed magnetoconductance signature unique to tomographic flow?

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The analytical result is explicit and compact. Eqs. (3)–(5) separate the Stokes–Ohm term, the boundary-layer term, the slip term and the rarefaction term, so each mechanism can be identified physically.
- The asymptotic profiles are compared directly with numerical solutions of the linearized Boltzmann equation (Fig. 1(c)–(e)), and the agreement shown is good.
- The prediction is experimentally relevant. The two field scales in Fig. 1(c), Rc ≈ 3ℓo and Rc ≈ 2ℓe, would in principle give a transport route to both mean free paths, which is hard to obtain otherwise.
- The finding that ΔRxy has the opposite sign to the Hall-viscosity prediction, and is parametrically larger (O(ke^{3/2}) against O(ke^2)), is a sharp warning for the Hall-viscosity protocols of Refs. [1, 36], if it holds up.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The expansion's range of validity does not match the regime where the effects are large. The expansion is in √ke at fixed ko (Eq. (13)). However, the tomographic terms enter as ke·ko (the slip and rarefaction terms in Eq. (3) and Eq. (5)) and as a layer of width √(ke·ko) (the variable χ in Eq. (16)). The parameter that actually controls the expansion therefore seems to be ke·ko, not ke alone. The paper never states that ke·ko ≪ 1 is required, nor what happens otherwise.
   - In Fig. 1(d) (ke = 0.1, ko = 2), √(ke·ko) ≈ 0.45. Each layer then spans almost half the channel, so the layers from the two walls overlap. A matched expansion with independent wall layers is not obviously valid there.
   - The headline effects are large only when ke·ko is not small. The text calls the Stokes–Ohm solution "strongly under-estimated", and the zero-field conductance correction in Fig. 1(c) is roughly 45%. When ke·ko ≪ 1, Eq. (5) itself says the corrections are small.
   - The statement that "excellent agreement is observed, with similar agreement for other parameter values" (p. 5) is not documented.

   Requested:
   - State the formal ordering, for example ke → 0 at fixed ke·ko or at fixed ko, and the condition for the two wall layers not to overlap.
   - Show the relative error of Eqs. (3) and (5) against the Boltzmann numerics over a grid in (ke, ko), at zero field and at Rc ~ ℓo, so readers can see where the closed form can be trusted.
   - Qualify the conclusion in the Discussion that the Stokes–Ohm description is "inapplicable" accordingly.

2. The collision model is a two-rate model, while the tomographic physics the paper invokes is mode-dependent. Eq. (2) relaxes every even harmonic m ≥ 2 at one rate (1/ℓe) and every odd harmonic m ≥ 3 at another (1/ℓo). In the microscopic theory cited (Refs. [14–18]), the odd-mode rates grow strongly with m (roughly as m^4 log m at low temperature). That m-dependence is what produces superdiffusion and the scale-dependent viscosity of Ref. [16].
   - The histograms in Fig. 1(d) show substantial weight in m ≥ 3 harmonics within the boundary layer. These are exactly the modes whose rates the two-rate model misrepresents.
   - The layer functions 𝒴0, 𝒴1, 𝒯0 and 𝒯1, the layer width √(ℓeℓo), and the slip coefficient in Eq. (17) may therefore depend quantitatively, and perhaps qualitatively, on this simplification.
   - Ref. [31], cited as going "beyond the dual-relaxation time approximation", is not available to the reader. Meanwhile the Discussion recommends Eq. (2) as "the appropriate starting point" for electrons at low temperature.

   Requested: at minimum, solve the numerical Boltzmann problem with an m-dependent odd-mode spectrum (for example γ_m ∝ m^4 for odd m ≥ 3, normalized to a given ℓo) and show how much G_ch(B) and the velocity profile change. Then either justify the two-rate reduction (for instance, as an effective ℓo for the slowest odd mode) or narrow the claims to this model.

3. The magnetoconductance minimum is not shown to be unique to tomographic flow. The Introduction calls the non-monotonic magnetoconductance an "unequivocal macroscopic signature". For ℓo ≳ L, however, the tomographic minimum sits at Rc ≈ 3ℓo ~ L. That is the same field range as the familiar boundary-scattering magnetoresistance peak of ballistic or quasi-ballistic channels with diffuse edges, the maximum near W/Rc ≈ 0.55 (Refs. [12, 36] and the classic 2DEG literature). It is also the range where hydrodynamic negative magnetoresistance (Ref. [34]) acts.

   A non-monotonic G_ch(B) with a minimum at Rc ~ L therefore does not by itself distinguish tomographic from conventional quasi-ballistic transport. Requested additions to Fig. 1(c):
   - the conventional kinetic result with the same diffuse boundaries and ℓo = ℓe (single-rate electron–electron collisions);
   - a quasi-ballistic reference with ℓe comparable to L.

   The authors should then identify which feature is specific to ℓo ≫ ℓe: the position ratio of the two features, the depth scaling as ke·ko, or a lineshape. They should also state whether a fit of Eq. (5) to such a curve could recover ℓe and ℓo without degeneracy with a conventional model.

B. Should address

4. The strong-field factor in Eq. (5) is inserted by hand, and the "arbitrary magnetic field strength" claim is not derived. Eq. (5) multiplies the result by [1 + (2ke/rc)^2] ("where we also include as a prefactor"), but this factor does not appear in Eq. (3).
   - At fixed rc this factor is O(ke^2), beyond the stated order. It becomes O(1) only in the distinguished limit rc ~ ke, which the expansion with rc held fixed does not treat.
   - The figure covers 1/rc up to 10^2 with ke = 0.025, that is Rc down to ℓe/2.5. In that range the Stokes–Ohm leading order itself should be modified, consistent with the viscosity reduction the authors invoke.
   - The agreement with numerics at large 1/rc in Fig. 1(c) is encouraging but is not a derivation.

   The authors should either derive the uniformly valid result for rc = O(ke), or state that Eq. (5) is a composite (heuristic) formula and give its expected accuracy. "For ke ≲ 1 at arbitrary magnetic field strength" (p. 5) should be qualified.

5. The numbers in Fig. 1(c) need checking against Eq. (5). With ke = 0.025, ko = 5 and G → ∞, Eq. (5) gives:
   - G_ch ≈ 1/3 + 0.034 + 0.125 ≈ 0.49 at zero field;
   - a minimum near 1/3 + 0.034 ≈ 0.37 around 1/rc ~ 1, where the ko term has dropped out and the prefactor is still ≈ 1.

   I read the vertical axis as logarithmic, since the ticks 0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2 are unevenly spaced. On that reading, the plotted curve near its minimum appears to lie below 0.5 by more than the difference between 0.37 and 0.5, possibly below the no-slip hydrodynamic value of 1/3. That would contradict the statement that tomographic flow enhances the conductance.

   The solid curve also seems to have a small shoulder near 1/rc ≈ 0.1–0.3. A Lorentzian in 3ko/rc times a monotonic prefactor cannot produce that.

   Requested:
   - Mark the scale as logarithmic.
   - Add the hydrodynamic baseline 1/3 (and its field-dependent version) as a reference line.
   - Confirm that the solid curve is exactly Eq. (5).
   - Correct the normalization in the caption, which contains the driving field ℰ ("e^2 n ℰ L^3/(m vF ℓe)") although a conductance should not depend on it.

6. The Hall-resistance result rests on a single parameter point and lacks the conventional reference. The claim that ΔRxy is "dominated by near-boundary kinetic effects" with the opposite sign to Eq. (9) is supported by one point: ke = 0.025, ko = 5, 1/rc = 0.02, giving ΔRxy = +0.20 (asymptotic) against +0.19 (numerical). Three things are missing:
   - ΔRxy as a function of 1/rc and of ko, from Eq. (11) and from numerics, with the Hall-viscosity prediction of Eq. (9) on the same plot. This would show where, if anywhere, the Hall viscosity term recovers dominance, for example at small ko or when Rc ≲ ℓe.
   - The value of ΔRxy in conventional kinetic theory (ℓo = ℓe) with the same diffuse boundaries. Without it, one cannot tell whether the O(ke^{3/2}) boundary-layer contribution is specific to tomographic flow or is a generic Knudsen-layer effect already present in Refs. [23, 36]. The authors' own Ref. [23] treats the near-hydrodynamic Hall field, and the difference from it should be stated explicitly.
   - The value of the integral in Eq. (11) at the quoted parameters, so the reader can reproduce +0.20.

7. The numerical benchmark and the boundary-layer structure are not described. The numerical solution of the linearized Boltzmann equation is the paper's only independent validation, yet no method is given: the angular-harmonic truncation, the spatial discretization, how the diffuse condition in Eq. (12) is imposed, and the convergence checks. The method used to solve the "tomographic equation" (16) for 𝒴0,1 and 𝒯0,1, and its boundary conditions at χ = 0 and χ → ∞, are also not given.

   There is a related structural question. Eq. (16) is second order in χ, so it cannot by itself satisfy the kinetic condition in Eq. (12) for every incoming angle. Is there an inner Knudsen layer of thickness ℓe at the wall, and does it contribute at O(ke)? The 32/(15π) coefficient in Eq. (17) and the 64/(15π) term in Eq. (5) look like classical Knudsen-layer slip. The authors should explain the layer structure (outer bulk, √(ke·ko) layer, and any inner Knudsen layer) and where each coefficient comes from. All of this can go in a Supplement.

8. Fully diffuse edges and the generality claim. All results assume fully diffuse scattering (Eq. (12)). Slip lengths and boundary-layer amplitudes in kinetic theory depend strongly on the specularity parameter, and real edges (etched graphene, PdCoO2 bars) are partly specular. The size of the slip term, and therefore the depth of the magnetoconductance minimum, will depend on it. The authors should show at least how the slip coefficient in Eq. (17) and G_ch change with a Maxwell-type specularity parameter.

   The statement that these phenomena "persist for an arbitrary device geometry" (p. 3) cannot be checked from this manuscript and should be removed or supported.

9. Experimental feasibility and finite momentum relaxation. The experimental window ℓe ≪ L ≲ ℓo ≪ ℓMR together with G ≳ 1 is asserted (Discussion) but not quantified for any real system. The authors should give concrete estimates (ℓe, ℓo, ℓMR, L, T/TF) for at least one platform, for example hBN-encapsulated graphene or GaAs, using the Fermi-liquid scalings they quote and the prefactors from Refs. [17, 18]. Two further points:
   - The required condition ℓo ≪ ℓMR, with ℓo ~ (TF/T)^4, is demanding because ℓMR in the best graphene devices is tens of µm.
   - The corresponding field scales should be stated. For example, Rc = 3ℓo ~ 10 µm corresponds to about 10 mT at n ~ 10^12 cm^-2, which places the feature in the range of weak-localization and ballistic magnetoresistance.

   Eq. (5) is also given only for G → ∞. Since momentum relaxation has its own field dependence, a finite-G version of the conductance should be provided for comparison with experiments.

10. Extracting ℓe and ℓo from G_ch(B) needs to be shown to work. The paper states that the non-monotonic magnetoconductance "can be used to measure both the even- and odd-mode mean free paths" (Abstract, Discussion), but no inversion is demonstrated. The authors should:
    - fit Eq. (5) (or its finite-G version) to synthetic numerical data, with realistic noise and an unknown G;
    - report the uncertainties and correlations of the recovered ℓe and ℓo;
    - state how the depth of the dip (∝ ke·ko) and its field position (∝ 1/ko) jointly constrain ko when ke is also free.

C. Minor

11. Fig. 1(d) caption: "slip conditions (dash-dotted purple line, see Eq. (3))". The slip conditions are Eq. (17); Eq. (3) is the full solution.
12. Terminology: Fig. 1(c) is labelled "magnetoconductivity" in the caption but "channel conductance" on the axis and in the text. Please use one quantity consistently.
13. "Slip of order O(ℓeℓo)" (p. 3) is not a velocity or a length. Please state the dimensionless order (ke·ko) or the dimensional slip length (ℓeℓo/L).
14. Discussion: "ℓe ≃ ℓ0" should read ℓo. On p. 6, "this tomographic finite-wavelength such a correction must be taken into account" is garbled.
15. The comparison with Gurzhi et al. (G ∝ Rc^{-1/3}) is attributed to "the qualitative nature of their discussion". Please give a more concrete reason, for example the different ordering of ℓo relative to L, so readers can tell whether the two results apply to different regimes.
16. The novelty relative to the authors' Ref. [20] (magnetic suppression of tomographic transport in bulk, with a non-monotonic magneto-response) and to Ledwith et al. [16] (the same tomographic equation structure, Eq. (16)) should be stated explicitly in the Introduction.
17. The model assumes a circular Fermi surface and isotropic relaxation, while PdCoO2 and WTe2 are cited as motivating systems. A sentence on the expected effect of Fermi-surface anisotropy would help.
18. Fig. 2: please state the range of χ over which the functions were computed and the asymptotic decay rate (exponential or algebraic) of 𝒴0,1 and 𝒯0,1. Note also that 𝒯0 changes sign for ko/rc = 10.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add to Fig. 1(c) the conventional kinetic curve (ℓo = ℓe), the no-slip hydrodynamic baseline and a quasi-ballistic reference. This would make the tomographic signature visibly distinct, and is the most direct way to support the "unequivocal signature" claim.
2. Provide a Supplement with the full derivation, the numerical method and convergence tests, and a (ke, ko) error map of the asymptotic formulae against numerics.
3. Test the robustness of the main results to an m-dependent odd-mode relaxation spectrum and to partially specular boundaries, or state these as explicit limitations.
4. Give a worked experimental estimate for one material (lengths, temperatures, field scales), and show G_ch(B, T) curves using the Fermi-liquid temperature scalings of ℓe and ℓo. This would turn the proposal into a concrete experimental protocol.
