Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript gives a matched asymptotic solution of a linearized kinetic equation for electron flow in a channel with diffuse walls. The equation has separate even-mode and odd-mode relaxation lengths (ℓe ≪ L ≲ ℓo), which mimic the tomographic odd-even effect of degenerate 2D Fermi liquids. Closed-form expressions are obtained for the velocity profile, the Hall field and the conductance, Eqs. (3)-(5). From these the authors identify a boundary layer of width √(ℓeℓo), a second-order slip of order ℓeℓo, bulk rarefaction corrections, and the suppression of all three by a weak magnetic field. That suppression gives a non-monotonic magnetoconductance with features at Rc ~ ℓo and Rc ~ ℓe. The paper also argues that two proposed routes to the Hall viscosity are contaminated by tomographic corrections. An analytical treatment of tomographic boundary effects in a confined geometry would be useful to the electron-hydrodynamics community. How significant it is depends on how wide the regime of validity is and on whether the conclusions survive a realistic collision spectrum and realistic boundaries. The manuscript does not yet establish either.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A systematic expansion (in √ke, with a boundary-layer "tomographic equation" Eq. (16)) of a problem that has so far mostly been treated numerically or with heuristic no-slip or slip conditions. The connection to second-order slip in rarefied-gas theory (Eq. (17)) is instructive.
- An experimentally testable prediction: a non-monotonic channel magnetoconductance whose two field scales, (3ko)⁻¹ and (2ke)⁻¹, are said to give both mean free paths (Eq. (5), Fig. 1(c)).
- The asymptotic results are compared with direct numerical solutions of the kinetic equation, Fig. 1(c)-(e), and agree well at the parameters shown.
- A pointed, checkable statement that the Hall-resistance protocol for the Hall viscosity is dominated by O(ke^{3/2}) boundary-layer terms. At ke = 0.025, ko = 5 these even reverse the sign of ΔRxy (+0.20 against the hydrodynamic −6ke² prediction).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The regime in which the expansion is valid is not stated, and the parameters used to demonstrate it sit at or beyond its edge.
   - The expansion is formally in √ke at fixed ko, "to linear order in ke" (text above Eq. (3)). The corrections that carry the physics, however, are O(ke ko): the slip, the rarefaction terms and the 4ke ko 𝒴₁ term in Eq. (3), the ke ko term in Eq. (5), and the 3ko²/(2G²) term in Eq. (15b) and Eq. (7). The layer width is √(ke ko). The expansion therefore needs ke ko ≪ 1, i.e. ℓo ≪ L²/ℓe, and not only ke ≪ 1.
   - In Fig. 1(d),(e) (ke = 0.1, ko = 2), √(ke ko) ≈ 0.45 against a half-width of 0.5. The two "boundary layers" span the whole channel and overlap. The construction h = h_B + h_T, with independent layer corrections added at each wall (Methods), assumes they do not overlap.
   - In Fig. 1(c) (ke = 0.025, ko = 5), √(ke ko) ≈ 0.35. The O(ke ko) slip term (≈0.125) is about 40% of the leading 1/3, so it is hardly a small correction.
   - The Discussion places the tomographic regime at T ≲ 0.1 T_F, where ℓo/ℓe ~ (T_F/T)² ≳ 100. Together with ℓe ≪ L, this leaves a narrow window in which ke ko is also small. As ko → ∞, which is the strongly tomographic limit and the superdiffusive regime of Ref. [16], Eq. (5) grows without bound.
   - The authors should:
     - state the distinguished limit and the formal error (is the remainder O(ke²), O(ke² ko²) or O((ke ko)^{3/2})?);
     - show the asymptotic-minus-numerical error against ke at fixed ko, and against ko at fixed ke, up to ke ko ~ 1;
     - give the parameter window in (T/T_F, L) for a real material where the theory applies.

2. The model is a two-rate relaxation model, but the conclusions are stated for degenerate Fermi liquids in general.
   - Eq. (2) relaxes all even modes m ≥ 2 with one length ℓe and all odd modes m ≥ 3 with one length ℓo. The exact-diagonalization studies the authors cite (Refs. [16-18]) find odd-mode rates that depend strongly on m (growing roughly as m⁴, with logarithmic factors). That m-dependence is what produces the scale-dependent viscosity and superdiffusion of Ref. [16].
   - The Introduction and Discussion state that a "dual relaxation-time description" is "fundamentally limited" and that Eq. (2) is "the appropriate starting point". Eq. (2) is itself a dual (even/odd) relaxation-time model, only a different one.
   - The diffuse-wall boundary layer generates high harmonics; the insets of Fig. 1(d) show m up to about 5. Whether the boundary layer keeps a single width √(ℓeℓo), and whether ℓo in Eq. (5) is ℓ₃ or some weighted average, is exactly what an m-dependent spectrum could change.
   - The authors should either redo at least the numerical benchmark with an m-dependent odd spectrum (e.g. ℓ_m = ℓ₃ (3/m)⁴) and show which of the four effects survive and what "ko" the magnetoconductance would measure, or narrow the claims to the two-rate model.

3. The main formula, Eq. (3), and the equations it is derived from contain inconsistencies that stop a reader from reproducing or using it. Since Eq. (3) is called "the main result of this work", these must be fixed.
   - Sign of the leading term. G²(cosh(2x/G)/cosh(1/G) − 1) is ≤ 0 for |x| ≤ 1/2. Fig. 1(d) plots a positive hydrodynamic profile with maximum 1 − 1/cosh 1 ≈ 0.35 at G = 1. For G → ∞ the printed term tends to −2(1/4 − x²). Its integral, −1/3, has the opposite sign to the 1/3 in Eq. (5).
   - Missing forcing. Eq. (15c) sets 𝓘₀ = 0. With Eq. (14) at n = 0 this leaves a homogeneous problem with no-slip walls, whose only solution is u = 0. The driving term from 2ke v_y in Eq. (2) must appear (𝓘₀ = 1 is consistent with the Poiseuille term).
   - Coefficient of 𝒴₁. Eq. (18a) gives the layer correction as ke[∓u′𝒴₀ + (ko/2)u″𝒴₁]. For a positive Poiseuille-like profile, u″ = −4 at the wall when G = 1, so the 𝒴₁ term at x = −1/2 is −2ke ko 𝒴₁. Eq. (3) prints +4ke ko 𝒴₁.
   - Check against Fig. 1(d). With 𝒴₁(0) ≈ 0.5 from Fig. 2(a), the printed version would give a wall velocity of about 0.6, whereas Fig. 1(d) shows u ≈ 0 at the walls. The −2ke ko 𝒴₁ version brings the wall value close to zero.
   - The 𝒴₀ term and the slip term do check out against Eq. (17), so these look like transcription errors. But Eqs. (3), (14)-(18) are the whole technical content and must be internally consistent. Please correct them, and state the normalization of 𝒴₀, 𝒴₁, 𝒯₀ and 𝒯₁ explicitly.

4. The non-monotonic magnetoconductance (Eq. (5), Fig. 1(c)) is presented over a field range the expansion is not shown to cover uniformly.
   - The upturn at 1/rc ~ (2ke)⁻¹ comes from a prefactor 1 + (2ke/rc)² that the authors "also include". This prefactor is O(ke²/rc²), formally beyond the O(ke) order kept, and is relevant only when rc ~ ke, i.e. 1/rc ~ 40 in Fig. 1(c).
   - In that regime the 1/rc terms in Eq. (2) and in 𝒥₄, Eq. (15b), are O(1/ke). This reorders the expansion: the Lorentz term is no longer O(1) relative to the collision terms.
   - The first-order slip coefficient 32/(15π) in Eq. (17) carries no field dependence, although at Rc ~ ℓe the Knudsen-scale slip should be modified too.
   - Measuring ℓo and ℓe from the two field scales of a single curve is the paper's main experimental proposal, so a uniformly valid composite expression is needed. Failing that, an explicit statement of which terms are valid where, and the numerical error of Eq. (5) across the full 1/rc axis, should be given.
   - The numerical points in Fig. 1(c) are sparse in the decreasing branch (1/rc ≈ 0.05-1), where the tomographic suppression is the claimed signature. More points there would show whether the Lorentzian form 1/(1 + (3ko/rc)²) is quantitatively right.

B. Should address

5. All results assume fully diffuse walls, Eq. (12).
   - The tomographic layer, the O(ℓeℓo) slip and the sign change of ΔRxy are boundary effects, so their size and sign should depend on the specularity parameter.
   - Edges in hBN-encapsulated graphene and in PdCoO₂ are not fully diffuse; Refs. [5, 7, 26] discuss partial slip.
   - The authors should give Eq. (5) and ΔRxy for a Maxwell-type boundary with specularity p, or at least show numerically how the ko term and ΔRxy change with p. Otherwise ℓo inferred from the magnetoconductance is degenerate with the boundary condition.

6. The claim that the non-monotonic magnetoconductance is an "unequivocal macroscopic signature" of tomographic flow lacks a null comparison.
   - Negative magnetoresistance at weak fields in channels also comes from ballistic boundary scattering (Rc ~ L) and from viscous-to-ballistic crossovers (Ref. [34]). Here ℓo ~ L, so Rc ~ ℓo coincides with Rc ~ L.
   - The authors should show the same channel computed with ko = ke (conventional single-time model) and with matched ℓe and ℓMR, and identify which feature of the curve (field scale, lineshape, ko dependence) separates the tomographic case.
   - They should also state how ℓe and ℓo would be extracted independently of ℓMR and of the boundary condition when fitting real data.

7. The numerical benchmark, the only independent check of the asymptotics, is not described.
   - The manuscript does not say how the linearized Boltzmann equation was solved: angular truncation (number of harmonics), spatial discretization near the walls, treatment of the diffuse boundary, or convergence tests.
   - Figure 2's functions come from "solving the resulting equation numerically" with no details.
   - Agreement is shown for only two parameter sets.
   - Fig. 1(e) shows a visible discrepancy at the channel center at 1/rc = 1: the asymptote lies above the numerics. This is neither commented on nor quantified.
   - The authors should add a Methods paragraph (or SI) on the numerics, a convergence check, and a table of errors over a grid of (ke, ko, rc, G). Tabulated 𝒴 and 𝒯 functions or code would make Eq. (3) usable.

8. The Hall-viscosity conclusions rest on a single parameter point and a possibly singular weak-field limit.
   - ΔRxy = +0.20 (theory) against +0.19 (numerics) is shown only at ke = 0.025, ko = 5, 1/rc = 0.02.
   - Eq. (11) scales as ke^{3/2} rc ko^{1/2}. At these parameters the prefactor rc ≈ 50 makes this nominally higher-order term O(10⁻¹), about 50 times the Hall-viscosity term −6ke² ≈ −0.004. Whether Eq. (11) stays asymptotic as rc → ∞, and how ΔRxy depends on field, needs to be shown.
   - The authors should plot ΔRxy against 1/rc for several ko, together with the numerics and the hydrodynamic Eq. (9). They should also say whether the sign reversal persists at the parameters of the experiments that measured Hall viscosity (Ref. [5] and related graphene measurements).
   - Eq. (7) likewise adds a correction ∝ ke² ko²/G². Its size relative to the first term at realistic parameters should be stated.

9. The statement that the signatures are "readily detectable in present-day experiments" (Discussion) is not quantified.
   - Please convert the predictions to physical units for at least one material: for example graphene at a stated density, T and L, using the ℓe, ℓo estimates of Refs. [17, 18]. Give the field B at Rc = 3ℓo (likely in the mT range), the fractional conductance dip, and the predicted ΔRxy.
   - Please comment on competing weak-field effects in that range, such as weak localization and residual ballistic magnetoresistance.

10. The novelty relative to the authors' and others' closely related work should be stated more sharply.
    - Ref. [20] (same senior author) already reports magnetic-field suppression of tomographic transport and non-monotonic magneto-response in bulk flow.
    - Refs. [23, 24] develop the near-hydrodynamic matched expansion.
    - Ref. [16] contains the same tomographic boundary equation (acknowledged after Eq. (16)).
    - Ref. [31] is said to treat arbitrary geometry.
    - A short paragraph saying precisely what is new here would help: the √ke structure, the O(ke ko) slip, the channel magnetoconductance formula and the ΔRxy result. The dismissal of Gurzhi et al.'s Rc^{-1/3} law as "likely due to the qualitative nature of their discussion" should be replaced by a concrete identification of the regime (ko → ∞?) in which that law applies and why Eq. (5) differs.

C. Minor

11. Fig. 1(c) has inconsistent labels.
    - The axis is labelled "channel conductance G_ch" but the caption calls it "magnetoconductivity".
    - The scale e²nℰL³/(mv_Fℓe) contains the field ℰ, so it is a current rather than a conductivity or conductance.
    - The weak-field asymptote in Fig. 1(c) is hard to read on the log axis against Eq. (5)'s value (≈0.49 at ke = 0.025, ko = 5). A linear inset would help.

12. Notation and wording.
    - "ℓe ≃ ℓ0" in the Discussion should be ℓo.
    - "To linear order in ke" (above Eq. (3)) should state that ko is held fixed and that terms of order ke ko are kept.
    - Eq. (5) ends with "+ o(ke)" while Eq. (3) uses O(ke²); please reconcile.
    - "[see Fig 1(b)]" and similar bracket and capitalization slips appear throughout.

13. Eq. (2) and boundary conditions.
    - Eq. (2) subtracts only the y component of momentum, 2v_y u_y, in the odd relaxation term. This relies on u_x = 0 in channel flow, which should be stated.
    - Eq. (12) applies "v_x ≷ 0" with upper and lower signs to the two walls. Writing the incoming and outgoing directions explicitly for each wall would avoid ambiguity.

14. Eqs. (6)-(7) and the Hall-field term.
    - Please state the normalization of η_H^sp/(v_F L) in Eq. (6).
    - Please confirm that the term identified as "the usual Hall viscosity" in Eq. (15b) reduces to η_H = v_Fℓe²/(2Rc) in the dimensional form quoted.

15. Fig. 1(d),(e) insets. The Fermi-surface deformations are "exaggerated" by an unstated factor, and the histogram axes have no scale. Please give the amplification factor and normalize the mode amplitudes so the panels can be compared quantitatively.

16. Fig. 2. Please give the asymptotic decay of 𝒴₀,₁ and 𝒯₀,₁ for large χ, e.g. the decay rate as a function of ko/rc. This supports the stated layer width and the statement that the layer is "increasingly confined" with field.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a validity map in the (ke, ko) plane, with rc as a third parameter, showing the relative error of Eqs. (3) and (5) against the numerics, and mark where physical materials sit on it.
2. Release code, or tabulate the tomographic layer functions 𝒴₀, 𝒴₁, 𝒯₀ and 𝒯₁, so that experimentalists can fit Eq. (3) to imaged current and Hall-field profiles.
3. Test robustness by repeating the key comparison (Fig. 1(c) and ΔRxy) with an m-dependent odd-mode spectrum and with partially specular walls. If the qualitative features survive, the paper's claims become much stronger.
4. Fit Eq. (5) or Eq. (3) to an existing data set, such as imaged Poiseuille or Hall-field profiles (Refs. [5, 7]) or channel magnetoresistance in graphene or PdCoO₂. Alternatively, give a worked numerical example in physical units showing the expected size of the effects.
