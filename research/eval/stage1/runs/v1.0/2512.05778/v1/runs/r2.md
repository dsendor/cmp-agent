Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript studies polycrystalline La0.275Pr0.35Ca0.375MnO3 (LPCMO, y = 0.35). It uses laser-power-driven temperature cycles between a base temperature T_B ≈ 38 K and a target temperature T_T, and records Raman spectra at T_B after each cycle. The JT-mode intensities at T_B rise when T_C < T_T < T_MI and fall back ("reset") once T_T reaches T_COO. The authors relate this to analogous cryostat-driven thermal cycles of AC magnetization and two-probe resistivity, and present it as a "previously unidentified regime of structural irreversibility" and as a thermally or laser-driven non-volatile resistive switch. History dependence and thermal memory in phase-separated LPCMO are long established (hysteresis across the first-order FMM/COO transition, kinetic arrest, aging, minor-loop memory). The likely new element is therefore a local Raman readout of that memory. As written, the paper does not show that what it reports goes beyond known minor-loop hysteresis of the first-order transition, nor that the laser and cryostat experiments probe the same phenomenon.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Raman spectroscopy is a suitable probe of JT-distorted (COO) regions in a phase-separated manganite. The contrast between the as-cooled spectra and the spectra after cycling (Fig. 1d–h) is clear and large, up to about a two-fold intensity change.
- The non-monotonic dependence on T_T (gain between T_C and T_MI, reset above T_COO; Fig. 3c–e) is seen in two modes (AS-JT and S-JT). It is qualitatively mirrored in M and ρ (Fig. 4c,d).
- The supplement documents the laser-heating calibration (Table S1, Figs. S1–S5), the spectral fitting (Fig. S6) and phase purity by synchrotron PXRD (Fig. S7, Table S2). This makes the method transparent and open to scrutiny.
- An all-optical, local way to write and read the phase-separated state would interest the oxide-electronics community if it were established.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The Raman and bulk experiments use different perturbations, and the matched control is missing for each.** Raman cycling is done only by laser heating: a 50 µm spot, up to 150 mW at 532 nm, "almost instantaneous" heating and cooling, T_B = 38 K. Magnetization and resistivity cycling is done only with the PPMS: global heating at 5 K/min, T_B = 30 K. Yet the Discussion and Conclusions state that the work demonstrates "laser-induced thermal cycling to induce non-volatile structure/resistive-switching" and "controlling phase separation and resistive properties through localized laser heating". No transport or magnetic measurement was ever made after laser cycling, and no Raman measurement was made after cryostat cycling. Visible light is known to photo-induce melting of charge-orbital order and insulator-metal switching in Pr–Ca–Mn–O based manganites. The local Raman effect could therefore have a non-thermal, photo-induced part, or a part from strong thermal gradients and strain, that the cryostat experiments do not share. The central claim that the local structural memory is the same phenomenon as the bulk M/ρ memory, and that it is thermally driven, does not follow without matched controls. The authors should:
   - repeat the Raman T_T series using the cold-finger heater, with the probe fixed at 10 mW (or lower), at the same T_B, ramp rate and dwell as the PPMS runs, and compare directly with Fig. 3;
   - or, conversely, measure the resistance of a laser-cycled region (for example a narrow bar or a patterned two-terminal gap illuminated by the beam).

   Without one of these, the laser-related claims in the abstract and Conclusions should be removed.

2. **The laser-heating temperature scale is partly circular and its uncertainty is underestimated.** T_T for every laser cycle comes from Table S1. That table was built by "visual inspection and comparison" of power-dependent spectra with temperature-dependent spectra, using mainly the width and intensity of the 480 and 614 cm⁻¹ JT structures (Fig. S1: inflections at 75, 100, 125, 150 and 175 K matched to 40, 50, 60, 70 and 80 mW). These are the same JT intensities whose T_T dependence is then reported, and whose thresholds are said to coincide with T_C, T_MI and T_COO. The coincidence of the Raman thresholds with the transition temperatures (Fig. 3) is therefore partly built in by the calibration. Further problems:
   - Below about 75 K (40 mW) the spectra are admitted to be indistinguishable. T_B = 38(4) K is therefore pure extrapolation of a linear T(P) fit.
   - The first fit has its intercept fixed to zero (footnote, p. 12). A constant 21.8 K offset is then added at all temperatures, although the thermal conductivity of LPCMO changes strongly across T_C and T_COO (metallic versus insulating percolation), so linearity in P is not expected.
   - The quoted ±4–8 K errors are fit-propagation errors only.

   The authors should determine the local temperature independently, for example from the Stokes/anti-Stokes intensity ratio of the 250 cm⁻¹ (or 485 cm⁻¹) mode. Anti-Stokes spectra appear to have been recorded but were absorbed into the background (Fig. S6). This is needed both at T_B under 10 mW and at each T_T. The authors should then re-plot Fig. 3 on that independent scale.

3. **The protocol cannot separate a new "irreversible regime" from ordinary minor-loop hysteresis, kinetic arrest and relaxation of the first-order transition.** The cycles are cumulative and the T_T values increase monotonically (Fig. 1b; Methods). The state before cycle n is therefore the state after cycle n−1, and the effect of T_T is confounded with the accumulated history (Fig. 4c makes this explicit: M_i(n) ≈ M_f(n−1)). The observation itself is what one expects from a first-order transition. On warming above T_C^W ≈ 91 K, FMM converts to COO. On recooling from inside the hysteresis window (T_T < T_COO), part of that COO is retained (kinetically arrested or supercooled) at T_B. Warming above T_COO erases the memory. This is the textbook minor-loop and kinetic-arrest behaviour of LPCMO and related compounds, and the paper cites several of these works (Ghivelder2005, Sacanell, Kundhikanjana2015, Ward2011) without saying how the present result differs. The authors should:
   - use a non-cumulative protocol, recooling from above T_COO (or from 300 K) before each T_T, and compare with the cumulative one;
   - run the T_T sequence in decreasing order;
   - record the time dependence at T_B (Raman intensity and ρ for at least tens of minutes after each cycle), since LPCMO at 30–40 K is known to relax slowly. A 5-minute wait before a 5-minute acquisition (Methods) is short compared with the known relaxation times;
   - state explicitly what is new relative to the established minor-loop and kinetic-arrest literature.

   Without this, "previously unidentified regime" and "new form of nonequilibrium phase dynamics" are not supported.

B. Should address

4. **Reproducibility and possible laser modification of the surface are not shown.** The same spot is repeatedly irradiated up to 150 mW on a 50 µm spot (roughly 7–8 kW/cm²) and probed over more than 15 cycles. Only one T_T series is shown. The Methods say "different spots and pieces of the sample were measured with the same protocol", but no spread between spots or pieces is shown, and no repeat of the full series on the same spot after a reset. An unidentified peak at about 670 cm⁻¹ (red dashed line, Fig. 2a) is attributed to "local oxygen vacancies" without evidence. A band near this frequency is also the fingerprint of laser-induced Mn3O4-like surface decomposition in manganites. It grows strongly near 180–210 K in Fig. 2b and is not tracked through the cycles. The authors should:
   - show Fig. 3c/e for at least two further spots and one other piece;
   - repeat the full sequence on the same spot after a reset above T_COO;
   - track the 670 cm⁻¹ band through all cycles;
   - check the irradiated spot afterwards (optical image or micro-Raman map).

5. **Normalized JT intensity is used as a proxy for COO phase fraction without validation.** All intensities are divided by the ~250 cm⁻¹ rotational mode (Figs. 2d and 3c). Fig. S5d shows that this mode's area itself varies by about a factor of four with temperature and peaks at the same place as the JT modes. The ratio therefore mixes changes in both. The fits use a 7th-degree polynomial background plus several broad Lorentzians (FWHM 50–120 cm⁻¹). The background shape changes markedly between conditions (compare Figs. S6b, e and a), and fit quality was "judged based on visual inspection". The authors should:
   - show the raw (unnormalized) fitted areas of the JT and rotational modes at T_B versus T_T;
   - test the robustness of Fig. 3c–e against the choice of background (lower polynomial order, or a physically motivated electronic background) and against the number of Lorentzians;
   - state how the error bars in Figs. 2c,d and 3b–e were obtained.

   Ideally they would also link JT intensity to P2₁/m phase fraction independently, for example with low-temperature synchrotron XRD after a cryostat cycle, which their PAINEIRA access would allow.

6. **The "magnetization" is AC susceptibility and is not a measure of FMM fraction.** The Methods state that M–T was measured in ACMS mode with a 10 Oe, 11 Hz AC field and no DC field. In a ferromagnet, the zero-field AC response is dominated by domain-wall dynamics and demagnetizing effects, and it is not proportional to the FM volume fraction. The values of about 10⁻² µB/Mn (Fig. 4a) are two orders of magnitude below the saturation moment. There is also a scale inconsistency: the virgin cooling curve in Fig. 4a reaches about 1.8×10⁻² µB/Mn at 30 K, whereas M_i at 30 K in Fig. 4c starts at about 3.3×10⁻² µB/Mn. The authors should report χ′ (and χ″) as susceptibility, explain the Fig. 4a/4c discrepancy, and add DC magnetization at a small fixed field (or M(H) loops at T_B) to estimate the FM fraction after each cycle.

7. **Two-probe resistivity and the "non-volatile resistive switching" claim.** Resistance was measured with two probes, using silver paint on a polycrystalline pellet. History-dependent contact and grain-boundary resistance therefore cannot be separated from bulk percolation. Calling the effect "non-volatile resistive switching" also requires evidence that is not given: retention at T_B over time, repeated set/reset cycles (T_C < T_T < T_MI followed by T_T > T_COO, several times), and the energy and time cost of a cycle. The authors should repeat the key ρ data with four-probe contacts and add a retention trace and a repeated write/erase test, or else tone the claim down to "history-dependent resistance".

8. **The quantitative correspondence between techniques is overstated.**
   - The Discussion states "clear agreement" between Figs. 3d,e and 4d. Yet the Raman ΔI/I_i is negative for T_MI < T_T < T_COO (Fig. 3d), where M shows no matching feature (Fig. 4d, left).
   - The Raman reset is placed at T_T ≈ 188 K (p. 2), whereas M and ρ reset only at T_T ≥ 210 K.
   - The resistivity gain is called "two-fold" on p. 7, but "about three times" in the Discussion, and Fig. 4d (right) shows ΔF/F₀ ≈ 2.2.
   - The base temperatures differ (38 K versus 30 K), as do the effective cooling rates.
   - Fig. S8a labels a T_N ≈ 188 K that is never discussed, although it coincides with the Raman reset temperature.

   The authors should compare the techniques at the same T_B, the same rates and the same T_T grid, overlay them on one axis, and discuss T_N.

9. **The kinetics of the laser cycle are not specified.** The dwell time at T_T, the time for the spot to reach T_T and to cool back, and the number of spectra per cycle are not given. The claim of "almost instantaneous heating and subsequent freezing" (Methods) is not quantified, yet the cooling rate through T_C is exactly what controls how much COO is retained. The authors should give these times, and ideally measure the dependence of the retained intensity on dwell time at T_T and on how fast the power is reduced.

10. **The Raman probe samples the surface, while M and ρ sample the bulk.** At 532 nm the optical penetration in LPCMO is tens of nm, and a freshly broken surface of a polycrystal is probed. Surface strain and grain-boundary effects, which are known to shift phase balance in LPCMO, may differ from the bulk. The laser also produces in-plane and depth temperature gradients across the 50 µm spot, so the Raman signal averages over a range of temperatures. The authors should discuss these effects and estimate the gradient (for example with a simple heat-diffusion model using the known thermal conductivity).

11. **Some interpretive claims go beyond the data.** Examples are the statement that thermal cycling "changes the dimensionality of the percolation paths", "for the first time, it is demonstrated how the resistive switching phenomenon can be associated with the local structure", "manipulate the balance… at will", and the neuromorphic and quantum-device framing. None of these is supported by a measurement in the paper. Fig. 5 is schematic. The authors should either support these statements (for example with spatial mapping, or percolation analysis of ρ versus M) or remove them.

C. Minor

12. The citations appear as raw BibTeX keys (for example [Sacanell2004], [Kim], [Uehara]) and there is no reference list, so the cited literature cannot be checked.

13. There are several figure and label errors:
    - The Fig. 1 caption says "Figures d - f" for five spectra; this should be d–h.
    - The text cites "figure 2 e", which does not exist (Fig. 3e is meant).
    - The Methods say the cyclic procedure is "depicted in figure 1 a"; it is in 1b.
    - Figs. S4 and S5 use T_FPS and T_OCO, while the main text uses T_C and T_COO.
    - The S5 text cites "S4 (c) and (f)", but S4 has no panel f.
    - T_IM and T_MI are used inconsistently.

14. The T_T grid for M/ρ is given as 50, 60, …, 250 K in the Methods, but as 70, 90, 100, 150 and 210 K in the Fig. 4 caption. Fig. 4c shows roughly 10 K steps. Please state the grid actually used, and the T_T values of the Raman cycles shown in Fig. 3.

15. The Fig. S1 caption states that the Bose–Einstein factor was not removed. This affects the temperature dependence in Fig. 2 and the power-to-temperature matching, though not the comparisons made at T_B. Please correct the spectra for it or justify leaving it in.

16. Error bars are missing from the M and ρ points in Fig. 4c,d. Please add them, for example from repeat measurements.

17. Table S1: please explain why the intercept of the first fit was fixed at 0 K rather than at the cold-finger temperature, and why a temperature-independent 21.8 K offset was used.

18. The phrase "highly-correlated" in the title is non-standard ("strongly correlated" is usual). The language also needs editing in places (for example "It was observed for the that…", "shows an clear agreement").

19. Room-temperature PXRD (Fig. S7) confirms the Pnma phase only at 278 K. A statement on oxygen stoichiometry (for example from iodometric titration or TGA) would help, given that the 670 cm⁻¹ band is attributed to oxygen vacancies.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Make the core comparison like-for-like. Do Raman with cold-finger cycling, and Raman with laser cycling, on the same spot, with the same T_B, dwell and rates as the PPMS cycles, and add Stokes/anti-Stokes thermometry. This one addition would settle whether the effect is thermal and whether the laser adds anything.
2. Add non-cumulative and reversed-order T_T protocols and retention measurements at T_B. Use them to place the result explicitly relative to minor-loop hysteresis and kinetic arrest in LPCMO, and state clearly what is new.
3. Strengthen the phase-fraction readout. Report raw mode areas and test robustness to the background model. Calibrate JT intensity against COO fraction from low-temperature diffraction or DC magnetization after identical cycles.
4. If the device angle is kept, demonstrate repeated write/erase of resistance with four-probe contacts, ideally driven by the laser itself, and give retention and energy figures.
