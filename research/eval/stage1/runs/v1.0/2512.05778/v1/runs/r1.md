Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript uses laser-power cycling of a polycrystalline La0.275Pr0.35Ca0.375MnO3 (LPCMO) pellet. The laser locally heats the probed spot from a base temperature T_B (about 38 K) to a target temperature T_T and back. Raman spectra taken at T_B then show that the Jahn-Teller (JT) mode intensities depend on T_T. They grow when T_C < T_T < T_COO and return to their initial values once T_T exceeds T_COO. The authors relate this to analogous bulk thermal-cycling changes in magnetization and two-probe resistance, and call the behaviour a structural "thermal memory" and a thermally driven, non-volatile resistive switching. Connecting a local lattice probe with the history dependence of phase separation is worthwhile. However, memory and rejuvenation under thermal cycling are well documented in phase-separated LPCMO. The new part is therefore mainly the Raman/JT read-out and the laser-heating protocol. The strongest claims, laser-based phase engineering and resistive switching tied to local structure, are not yet supported by the data.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A simple, contactless protocol: laser power serves as the temperature knob and Raman as the read-out. The same instrument heats and probes.
- Fig. 3 gives a clear qualitative result. The JT-mode intensity at T_B depends non-monotonically on T_T, and the gain is erased for T_T above about T_COO. Figs. 3d,e and 4d show qualitatively similar windows in Raman, M and ρ.
- The Supplementary Information compares heater-driven and laser-driven spectra (Figs. S1–S5) and gives a synchrotron PXRD/Rietveld characterization of phase purity (Fig. S7, Table S2).
- The topic (metastability and history dependence in phase-separated oxides) is of broad interest.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The target temperature T_T, the x-axis of the central result, is not independently measured, and the calibration risks being circular. The laser-heating temperature (Table S1, Figs. S1–S3) comes from "visual inspection and comparison" of spectra. The heater-driven and laser-driven spectra were matched mainly on the width and relative intensity of the 250, 480 and 614 cm⁻¹ structures. These are exactly the JT-mode intensities and widths whose history dependence is the claimed effect. A T(P) relation is then imposed: linear, with the intercept fixed, using a 25 K matching grid. The quoted uncertainties of ±4–8 K come only from the linear fit and understate the real uncertainty. Three further problems:
   - The optical absorption and thermal conductivity of LPCMO differ strongly between the FMM and AFMI-COO phases. The temperature reached at a given power should therefore depend on the phase state of the spot, which by the paper's own claim changes from cycle to cycle.
   - The laser spot has strong lateral and depth temperature gradients, so "T_T" is a distribution, not a single value.
   - The spectra are recorded down to about −250 cm⁻¹ (Fig. S2), so anti-Stokes signal is available, but Stokes/anti-Stokes thermometry is not used.

   Why it matters: whether the windows in Fig. 3 coincide with T_C, T_MI and T_COO depends entirely on this calibration. The authors should:
   - determine the spot temperature independently (Stokes/anti-Stokes ratios of the 250 cm⁻¹ mode at each power, before and after cycling);
   - give a realistic uncertainty that includes the matching-grid resolution;
   - estimate the gradient within the 50 µm spot and optical penetration depth;
   - repeat a few key cycles with the cryostat heater instead of the laser, under otherwise identical acquisition, to show that the Raman memory follows temperature and not illumination.

2. The protocol has no controls that separate thermal history from cumulative illumination, relaxation or laser-induced modification. The cycles are cumulative: T_T increases monotonically and the same spot is probed throughout (Methods). The response at a given T_T is therefore confounded with all earlier cycles, with total illumination dose, and with elapsed time at low temperature. Phase-separated LPCMO is known to relax slowly in the dynamic phase-separation regime. The authors also note a "spurious peak, possibly due to local oxygen vacancies" near 660 cm⁻¹ (Fig. 2a), which raises the question of photo-induced or thermally induced oxygen loss at up to 150 mW in a 50 µm spot. The following controls are needed:
   - Waiting-time control: repeated spectra at T_B, at 10 mW, over the duration of a full cycle sequence with no cycling, to measure intrinsic relaxation.
   - Non-cumulative cycles: a fresh cool-down from 300 K followed by a single cycle to one T_T, for several T_T values.
   - Repeated cycles to the same T_T, to show whether the effect saturates or accumulates.
   - A decreasing-T_T sequence.
   - A spot that was never illuminated at high power but went through the same cryostat history.
   - The evolution of the 660 cm⁻¹ feature across the cycles.
   - Spectra after a final warm-up to room temperature and re-cooling, to show the spot is not permanently altered.

   The Methods state that "different spots and pieces of the sample were measured with the same protocol", but no such data or spread are shown. Error bars in Figs. 2c,d and 3b–e appear to be fit errors only. Results from at least two spots or pieces should be presented, and the spot-to-spot spread should be included in the uncertainties.

3. The headline conclusions (laser-induced non-volatile resistive switching, laser-assisted phase engineering, and switching "associated with the local structure for the first time") are not demonstrated by the measurements shown.
   - Different protocols: magnetization and resistance (Fig. 4) were measured on the whole pellet in a PPMS with bulk thermal cycling at 5 K/min and T_B = 30 K. The Raman cycles use local laser heating with "almost instantaneous" cooling and T_B ≈ 38 K. In phase-separated manganites the cooling rate strongly affects the FMM fraction, so rates differing by orders of magnitude are not equivalent histories. The T_T grids also differ (Raman: 59, 81, 102, ... K; bulk: 50, 60, ... K).
   - No combined measurement: no resistance was measured on a laser-cycled region, and no Raman spectrum was taken after bulk cycling. The "correlation" between structure and transport therefore rests on qualitative similarity between two different experiments.
   - Non-volatility untested: retention of the high-resistance state at T_B over time is not measured. Erasing requires heating above about 205 K, and reading requires staying near 30–40 K. These operating conditions should be stated plainly if neuromorphic relevance is claimed.

   The authors should do one of two things. Either measure transport or magnetization on the same specimen after laser cycling (for example a narrow bar or thin region heated by the beam, or two-terminal resistance across a laser-addressed constriction), or take Raman spectra after bulk cryostat cycling with the PPMS rates. Otherwise the claims in the Abstract, Discussion and Conclusions should be reduced to a qualitative correlation between two separate experiments.

B. Should address

4. The intensity normalization and background subtraction may produce or distort the reported trends. The JT intensities are normalized to the ~250 cm⁻¹ rotational mode. Fig. S5d shows that this mode's area itself changes about fourfold with temperature, peaking near 120 K. The normalization therefore shifts the apparent maximum: Fig. S1 and Fig. S5e,f place the JT maximum near 100–125 K, while Fig. 2d places it near T_MI ≈ 150 K. In Fig. 3c the reference mode at T_B could also depend on history.

   Backgrounds were fitted with a fixed 7th-degree polynomial, and fit quality was "judged based on the visual inspection". Fig. 1e,f show the post-cycling background visibly higher than the as-cooled one, and Fig. S6 shows the background shape changing strongly between conditions.

   The authors should:
   - show the absolute (un-normalized) JT and rotational intensities at T_B versus T_T;
   - show the results with at least one alternative background model (for example a lower-order polynomial or an electronic-continuum form) and with the background left in;
   - report how laser focus and collection efficiency were kept constant between cycles.

5. JT-mode intensity is used as a measure of AFMI-COO phase fraction without quantitative support. The statement that "the strength of the COO phase achieved at T_T is retained" rests on comparing intensities measured at temperature T (Fig. 2d) with intensities measured at T_B after visiting T_T (Fig. 3c). Phonon intensities and widths also depend intrinsically on temperature, through Bose factors, polaron dynamics and electronic screening. Some evidence linking intensity at T_B to phase fraction is needed. Options:
   - low-temperature diffraction after cycling (the authors have synchrotron access, but Fig. S7 is at 278 K only) to quantify the P2₁/m versus Pnma fraction or superlattice intensity;
   - a calibration of JT intensity against phase fraction, for example using magnetic field to convert COO to FMM at T_B.

   A structural probe would also support the claim that the irreversibility is "structural" and not purely electronic.

6. The transition temperatures are inconsistent, and the windows are assigned loosely.
   - Values used: T_C is 63 K (cooling) or 91 K (warming) from M (Fig. S8a), about 93–100 K in the Raman figures, and "T_FPS" in Figs. S4/S5. T_MI is 120 K (cooling) or 153 K (warming) from resistance. T_COO is about 205 K.
   - Fig. S8a also marks T_N ≈ 188 K, which is never discussed. Yet the text says M_f − M_i starts increasing again at T_T ≈ 190 K.
   - It is not stated which branch (cooling or warming) defines each dashed line in Figs. 3 and 4, nor whether the Raman and bulk samples share the same transitions.

   Given the ±5 K (in practice larger) uncertainty in T_T, the statement that the windows "coincide" with T_C, T_MI and T_COO needs a careful definition. The role of T_N should also be addressed.

7. The paper is not clearly positioned relative to the existing literature on thermal memory in phase-separated manganites. History dependence, memory and erasure by heating above the charge-ordering temperature are well known in LPCMO and related compounds. Examples include thermal-cycling memory and relaxation studies cited by the authors (Ghivelder2005, Sacanell, Ward2011, Kundhikanjana2015) and the extensive kinetic-arrest/glass-like-arrest work (CHUF protocols). Calling the effect a "previously unidentified regime" and a "new form of nonequilibrium phase dynamics" requires a direct comparison with these results. The authors should state what is new beyond the Raman read-out, for example a quantitative difference in the T_T dependence or a structural signature absent from transport.

8. The text and figures disagree quantitatively on the transport and magnetic changes.
   - Resistance change: the Results say a "two-fold resistivity gain" between T_C and T_COO, while the Discussion says "a maximum increase … of about three times". In Fig. 4d (right) ΔF/F₀ reaches about 2, which is a threefold change.
   - Magnetization explanation: the text attributes the smaller relative change in M to "the AFM character of the insulating phase". It should explain why a phase-fraction change would give asymmetric relative changes in M and ρ in a percolating system, where ρ is strongly nonlinear in the FMM fraction.
   - Fig. 3c versus Fig. 2d: "quantitative correspondence for T_T < T_C" is claimed between these two figures, which are measured at different temperatures. This needs qualification (see concern 5).

9. The transport and magnetic measurements have technical limitations. Resistance was measured with two probes and silver-paint contacts on a polycrystalline pellet with probes about 0.7 mm apart. Contact resistance and its own temperature or history dependence are therefore included, and the conversion to resistivity (Ω mm) needs a stated geometry. A four-probe measurement, or at least a check of contact resistance before and after cycling, is needed to attribute the change to the bulk.

   The "magnetization" is an AC-mode moment at 10 Oe and 11 Hz in zero DC field. This is essentially low-field AC susceptibility, sensitive to domain-wall dynamics as well as FM phase fraction. The quantity should be labelled as such. Ideally, DC magnetization in a moderate field, which better measures FMM fraction, should be added at selected T_T.

10. The difference between local and bulk heating is not discussed. A laser-heated spot is mechanically constrained by a cold surrounding matrix. Phase separation in LPCMO is strongly strain-sensitive, as the authors' own introduction notes. Thermal stress from local heating may therefore drive part of the effect, and it is absent in uniform PPMS heating. In addition, Raman probes only the top tens of nanometres of a freshly broken polycrystalline surface, while M and ρ are bulk-averaged. These differences limit how directly Raman and bulk can be compared, and they should be discussed and, where possible, estimated.

C. Minor

11. The reference list is missing. Citations appear as unresolved BibTeX keys (for example [Sacanell2004], [Uehara], [Diego24]), so the cited claims cannot be checked.

12. Figure and caption errors:
   - The Fig. 1 caption describes panels "d–f" for six T_T values, but the panels run d–h.
   - The text refers to "figure 2 e", which does not exist (presumably Fig. 3e).
   - Figs. S4/S5 use T_FPS and T_OCO, while the main text uses T_C and T_COO.
   - The Supplementary text cites "S4 (c) and (f)", which does not exist.
   - The ρ axis exponent in Fig. 4b,c is hard to read and should be stated in the caption.
   - The Fig. 1c inset labels the modes A_g and B_2g, but the scattering polarization configuration is never given. For a polycrystal, state that the modes are mixed.

13. The Methods list T_T = 50, 60, 70, … 250 K for the bulk cycles, but the Fig. 4 insets and caption show only 70, 90, 100, 150 and 210 K. The Methods should also give:
   - the waiting time at T_B before each M/ρ reading;
   - the laser power at the sample (rather than at the source) and the power density;
   - the acquisition time per spectrum;
   - how the "same spot" was maintained while the cryostat contracted thermally.

14. Fig. 5 is described as "semi-quantitative" but has no quantitative axis. T_T¹–T_T⁷ are not mapped to actual temperatures, and the FMM fractions drawn are not tied to any measured quantity. Either link it to a measured quantity (for example an FMM fraction estimated from M) or call it a schematic.

15. Language and style:
   - "highly-correlated" in the title is better written "strongly correlated".
   - Typos include "an clear agreement", "Figure a show", "It was observed for the that", "it's the adjusted".
   - "vide figures" is unusual.
   - The Introduction on ultrafast optical control (coherent phonon driving and similar) is not relevant to a quasi-static laser-heating experiment and may mislead readers about the mechanism.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Make the thermometry independent: use Stokes/anti-Stokes ratios at each power, and cross-check a subset of cycles with heater-only cycling under identical optics. Report realistic uncertainties in T_T.
2. Add the controls that separate memory from artifacts: waiting time at T_B, single non-cumulative cycles from a fresh cool-down, repeated cycles to the same T_T, and a never-illuminated reference spot. Show the reproducibility across at least two spots or pieces.
3. Connect the structure and transport measurements on the same specimen and protocol. Measure resistance or magnetization after laser cycling, or Raman after PPMS-rate bulk cycling. Use four-probe transport and DC magnetization, and measure how long the switched state is retained at T_B.
4. Quantify the phase fraction directly with low-temperature synchrotron diffraction after selected cycles. Present absolute and alternatively normalized Raman intensities, so that the link between JT-mode intensity and COO fraction is demonstrated rather than assumed.
