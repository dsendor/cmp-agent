A. Must address

1. The diffraction does not establish AA stacking, yet the magnetic mechanism depends on it. The P-3m1 phase is refined in a 3.64 Å cell (a_R-3/√3) with a single Cr site at 1a with 2/3 occupancy (Table S3). This is the averaged CdI2-type subcell expected when honeycomb layers sit at random among the three possible lateral positions, so the in-plane √3 honeycomb order is lost on averaging. The authors state on p. 6 that they "are unable to distinguish" randomized occupancies from randomized A/B/C stacking. Yet pp. 10-12, the abstract and the Conclusions treat the high-pressure phase as "AA-stacked", and Fig. 6a and the Monte Carlo model use DFT interlayer couplings for an ideal AA bilayer, which change sign above about 2 GPa. A random A/B/C sequence produces mostly AB-type (rhombohedral-like) and monoclinic-like neighbour pairs, not AA pairs; according to the bilayer calculations in refs. [25, 49] these have different signs and magnitudes of J_L, and a random or turbostratic sequence would give a broad distribution of interlayer couplings, not the AA value. The two cases predict different diffraction. True ordered AA stacking (honeycomb directly above honeycomb; an ordered one-layer cell such as P-31m with Cr fully on 2c and 1a empty, or a √3 × √3 supercell with c ≈ 6.1 Å) keeps sharp honeycomb superstructure reflections (h − k ≠ 3n) at integer L, while random stacking turns them into diffuse rods along c*. The paper's own observation that the "1/3" reflections fade and vanish by 8.4 GPa (Fig. 3c, histograms) on its face fits loss of interlayer registry better than an ordered AA phase. The authors should refine an ordered AA model against the high-pressure data and compare it with the disordered P-3m1 model; analyse the intensity along the 10L and 11L rods (sharp peaks versus diffuse streaks) at 0, 6.4 and 8.4 GPa, at 8.4-21.5 GPa, and after decompression, reporting whether any diffuse intensity remains; and consider other evidence of registry such as specific (h0l) intensities or the Br positions in the P-3m1 refinement. If ordered AA cannot be shown, J_L should be computed for the registries actually compatible with the data (random A/B/C shifts, AB, and so on), the magnetic model rebuilt on stacking-averaged couplings, and the "AA" label dropped from the abstract and conclusions.

2. The Monte Carlo collapse of T_C may be an artefact of how the stacking disorder is placed, of the order parameter used, and of the simulation cell. Stacking faults act layer pair by layer pair. If each pair of adjacent layers is coupled uniformly by J_L(ABC) > 0 or J_L(AA) < 0, the stack is an unfrustrated one-dimensional chain with a collinear Mattis-type ground state: flipping the spins of whole layers maps every sequence onto a ferromagnet with couplings |J_L|. The ordering temperature should then stay finite, set by J_1 (still about 1.4-2 meV, Fig. 6a), the anisotropy and |J_L|; only the net moment is compensated. The authors themselves note (p. 12) that a CrBr3 monolayer, with no interlayer coupling, orders at about 70% of bulk T_C (22-27 K), so interlayer sign disorder alone cannot plausibly drive T_C to zero; the measured T_C of about 4 K at 5.6 GPa would also require intralayer exchange or anisotropy to collapse, which the DFT (J_1 down by about 30% and the MAE by a factor of two by 8 GPa) does not show. A collapse to T_C = 0 in Fig. 6b and Fig. S8 can therefore only arise from (i) in-plane frustration created by per-atom randomisation (p. 11: "atoms from these phases are randomly distributed"), which whole-layer stacking cannot produce; (ii) taking T_C from the uniform magnetization or susceptibility, which reads a compensated layered order as "T_C = 0"; or (iii) finite-size and boundary effects: the cell is only 10 layers (20×20×1 unit cells) with periodic interlayer boundaries, so an odd number of AFM bonds around the periodic stack frustrates the chain artificially, only three disorder realizations are averaged, and the cell is shorter than the 10 ± 2 nm (about 16-layer) blocks from the paracrystal analysis. The authors should state exactly how AA and ABC are assigned (per atom, per bond or per layer pair) and which order parameter defines T_C; repeat the simulation with layer-wise, block-structured disorder (block lengths from the paracrystal analysis) in cells thicker than one block, with open boundaries or many more layers and many more realizations, plus a finite-size check; and report the layer-resolved (gauge-transformed staggered) order parameter and the specific heat alongside the net magnetization. They should state explicitly whether the predicted state above p_c is ordered (a compensated AFM) or disordered; if it is ordered, the claim must change from "collapse of magnetic order" to "loss of net moment through interlayer compensation", and the gap between the monolayer argument and the measured T_C should be discussed.

3. The decompression results contradict the proposed structure-magnetism link and do not discriminate between scenarios. Fig. S1b shows full recovery of T_C and M_sat after decompression from 10 GPa. Yet Table S4 gives an R-3 fraction of −0.1(7) at 8.4 GPa, the abstract says R-3 is "undetectable above 8.4 GPa", the 1/3 reflections are extinct at 8.4 GPa (Fig. 3c), and P-3m1 is retained after release from 21.5 GPa (Fig. 3d). If R-3 is gone by 8.4 GPa and the transformed phase is metastable, a crystal released from 10 GPa should not regain the full ferromagnetic response. The Conclusions resolve this by saying "the P-3m1 phase was not yet stabilized at 10 GPa while some volume fraction of R-3 persisted", which no structural data at or after 10 GPa support. Moreover, Fig. 6a gives J_L(AA) slightly positive (FM) at ambient pressure, so a recovered P-3m1/AA crystal would be ferromagnetic anyway and recovery says little about which phase is present. Without further controls, the magnetic collapse may be reversible and unrelated to the irreversible stacking change, leaving hydrostatic compression of intralayer exchange and anisotropy as a live explanation. The authors should measure XRD on a crystal decompressed from about 10 GPa, and ambient-pressure M(T) and M(H) (to 5-7 T) on the crystal recovered from 21.5 GPa (single-phase P-3m1 per Table S4), or magnetization on a crystal decompressed from above 15-20 GPa. If the AA phase carries antiparallel interlayer coupling, the recovered crystal should show strongly reduced net moment and anomalous M(H); if it is a normal ferromagnet, the scenario fails. This is the most decisive test within reach of the existing setup.

4. The magnetic state above p_c is not characterized, although the proposed state has a measurable signature. All DAC field sweeps stop at 1 T (Fig. 1a), "M_sat" is the 1 T value at 2 K, and T_C comes from 0.01 T (Fig. 1b), so "collapse of ferromagnetism" here means only that the 1 T moment and the low-field anomaly vanish. In the proposed scenario, layers keep their moments and couple antiparallel across AA interfaces with J_L(AA) ≈ −0.15 to −0.25 meV per Cr (Fig. 6a). In a mixture where only some interfaces are AFM, 1 T along the easy axis should already flip much of the compensated structure, and even a fully A-type arrangement would show a large linear susceptibility and a spin-flip or metamagnetic step at about 1-3 T, recovering most of 3 μB/Cr; M(1 T) should then not fall to zero at 6.24 GPa. A genuine loss of moment, a glassy state or suppressed intralayer order would instead give a paramagnetic, unsaturated response. Further, at 5.24 and 5.60 GPa T_C is about 13 K and 4 K, so the moment at 2 K is reduced simply because the measurement is close to T_C; the "identical trend" of T_C and M_sat (p. 3) does not show a reduced Cr moment or a growing antiparallel fraction. Already at 1.92 GPa, M_sat is about 2 μB/f.u. while T_C is nearly unchanged (Fig. 1 inset a), which an intact saturated ferromagnet should not show and which suggests a calibration or background problem or a non-ferromagnetic fraction the stacking model does not explain. The authors should measure M(H) to the maximum available field (5-7 T) at 2 K just below and above p_c (for example 5.6 and 6.2 GPa or higher); measure M(T) at several fields (0.1-1 T and above the expected spin-flip field), field-cooled and zero-field-cooled, above p_c to look for a Néel-type cusp and a Curie-Weiss regime confirming that local moments survive, including a closer look at the 6.24 GPa M(T) in Fig. 1b; compare moments at a fixed reduced temperature T/T_C; and show the raw sample signal against the empty-cell background at each pressure. The 1 T value should not be called a saturation moment unless saturation is shown.

5. The quantitative link between model and data is weak and partly circular. The "stacking variation model" in Fig. 6b assumes an AA fraction that rises linearly "up to a critical pressure" (p. 12); its slope and end point are not given, and if they were chosen to reproduce p_c the agreement is built in. When the measured fractions are used, the picture weakens: Fig. S8 needs about 73% AA for T_C to vanish, but Table S4 gives about 50-58% P-3m1 at 5.7-6.4 GPa, and since the R-3 fraction is stated to be a lower bound (p. 8) this AA fraction is an upper bound; p. 12 says FM is lost at "45% of P-3m1". At the measured fractions the model predicts T_C ≈ 25-30 K, not zero. The crystals also start with 20-40% P-3m1 at ambient pressure (Table S4, Methods) and are fully ferromagnetic, but the model starts at 0% AA. The concentration dependence uses exchange parameters fixed at 5 GPa (p. 11), and it is unclear whether Fig. 6b uses pressure-dependent J values; relatedly, the text gives T_C = 39 K for pure ABC (Fig. S8, p. 11) whereas Fig. 6b starts both models at about 34 K. The authors should state every free parameter and how the slope and critical pressure were chosen; drive the simulation with the measured fractions of Table S4, with their uncertainties and including the ambient P-3m1 fraction, mapped to the magnetization pressures; use pressure-dependent J_1, J_L and MAE in the mixed-phase simulation; report the resulting T_C(p) with no adjustable critical pressure; and clarify which exchange set each figure uses.

6. Structure and magnetism are measured on different crystals, under different conditions, so their correlation is assumed rather than measured. All XRD and Raman data are at room temperature (Methods, Fig. 2e, Fig. 3, Fig. 5), while the magnetization is at 2-40 K. Cr trihalides have stacking transitions on cooling (CrBr3 near 420 K at ambient, CrI3 near 220 K), the boundary may move with pressure, and stacking energetics depend on temperature, so room-temperature phase fractions need not match low-temperature ones. The pressures also do not line up: the 1/3 reflections are still visible at 6.4 GPa and gone by 8.4 GPa, while ferromagnetism is already gone at 6.24 GPa in a different cell, medium and crystal. Methods say the P-3m1 fraction varies from crystal to crystal and with handling ("somewhat a matter of chance" among 30+ crystals; the XRD crystal began at about 80% R-3), but the initial phase content of the magnetization and Raman crystals is not given. If p_c tracked the initial P-3m1 fraction across runs, that would support the mechanism; if not, it would argue against it. The authors should report XRD on the magnetization and Raman crystals (or crystals cut from the same piece) before loading and discuss whether p_c tracks the initial fraction; add low-temperature diffraction, or at least intensities of the 1/3 reflections near T_C, at one or two pressures around p_c, or justify why room-temperature stacking carries over; and plot the Table S4 phase fractions on the same pressure axis as T_C and M_sat in a main-text figure, with uncertainties. Without these, "the T_C decline correlates with the AA fraction" (p. 11) is an assumption.

7. The pressure at the magnetization measurement point is uncertain, and overshoot matters for a metastable transformation. Pressure is set and read with ruby at room temperature only (p. 14, Methods), but T_C is measured at 2-35 K, and turnbuckle Be-Cu cells can change pressure on cooling. The horizontal error bars in Fig. 1 are one-sided and represent overpressure during loading, which in the first run reaches several GPa (Fig. S1a; up to about 14 GPa at the last point). If the stacking change is irreversible, the magnetization at each point reflects the maximum pressure reached, not the nominal one. The two runs give p_c values about 1 GPa apart, and run 1 retains a finite T_C near 6.3 GPa, yet the main text quotes only 5.8 GPa and Fig. 1 does not say which run it shows. The authors should state the run and sample for each data set, report p_c for every run, quote p_c as a range with an uncertainty covering both runs, and measure or estimate the low-temperature pressure (for example from ruby at low temperature or the superconducting transition of Pb or Sn in the cell).

8. A non-hydrostatic, shear-induced origin for the structural change is not excluded. The paper shows that cutting, bending and mounting alone create P-3m1 (pp. 5-6, 14, Methods, Fig. 4c-h), so non-hydrostatic stress is a plausible driver of both the growth of P-3m1 and its retention on decompression. The methanol-ethanol-water medium is hydrostatic only to about 10.5 GPa (SI p. 9), yet the claim that P-3m1 becomes stable rests on points at 11.8-21.5 GPa and on decompression from 21.5 GPa; the medium in some experiments is described only as "liquid", and many common liquids become non-hydrostatic between about 3 and 10 GPa, the range of interest. The growth of the disordered component, the 10 nm block size and the broadened spots after release from 21.5 GPa (Fig. 3d) could all reflect shear. The change in Raman slopes "around 10 GPa" (Fig. 5) also coincides with solidification of the medium (Fig. 6 of [40] reports 15 GPa). The authors should name the medium in each of the three DAC experiments with its hydrostatic limit, report ruby linewidths or pressure gradients at each point, and if possible do one diffraction (or Raman) run in helium or neon, or show that the onset does not depend on the medium. "Pressure-induced phase transformation" in the title and abstract should be qualified accordingly.

10. The claims of a "so-far unreported" phase and of "intrinsic trimorphism" are overstated. The monoclinic C2/m phase is not observed in this work, so trimorphism relies on the literature, yet the abstract and conclusions present it as an experimental finding. Methods report that as-grown crystals were R-3 and that P-3m1 appeared after cutting; p. 6 says the fraction is "likely unintentionally modified" by mounting; the RSM "deformed" state was produced by an unspecified external force; and the best description is a paracrystal of R-3 and P-3m1 blocks about 10 nm long (Fig. 4h). This points to deformation-induced stacking faults with a P-3m1 average rather than a distinct thermodynamic polymorph. Such average structures from stacking disorder are well documented for CrCl3, CrI3, VI3 and α-RuCl3, and the paper itself notes similar residual density on the Cr2 site in VI3 and VBr3 [38, 39]. The authors should show data from an undeformed as-grown crystal (for example the Fig. 4 RSM before any cutting), describe the deformation used for Figs. 4d and 4g quantitatively, describe the phase in the abstract and conclusions as a stacking-disordered modification with a P-3m1 average (or AA-type only if concern 1 is resolved), state that the monoclinic phase is only cited, cite the stacking-fault literature and make clear what is new relative to earlier observations.

12. The Raman phase assignment is not supported by symmetry analysis or the paper's own data, and has unaddressed alternatives.
