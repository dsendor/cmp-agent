Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports that hydrostatic pressure suppresses ferromagnetism in bulk single-crystal CrBr3, measured by SQUID magnetometry in a diamond anvil cell (DAC). Tc falls from 33 K to about 6 K at 6.4 GPa, and the extrapolated critical pressure is about 6.5 GPa. Room-temperature single-crystal XRD under pressure shows the 1/3 superstructure reflections of R-3 disappearing between 6.4 and 8.4 GPa, leaving a P-3m1 subcell with 2/3 Cr occupancy. The authors attribute the magnetic suppression to A-A stacking with antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling, and support this with DFT exchange constants and atomistic Monte Carlo. Direct DC magnetization of a vdW magnet up to 9.2 GPa is valuable, and the decrease of Tc in a localized-moment insulator is an interesting puzzle. However, the link between the structural transition and the magnetic suppression is asserted rather than shown. The structural data are taken at a different temperature, do not by themselves identify A-A stacking, and do not track the magnetic data as a function of pressure.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- DC magnetization of CrBr3 in a DAC is technically demanding. The data, M(T) up to 6.4 GPa (plus 9.2 GPa) and M(H) up to 9.2 GPa, extend well beyond the earlier 0 to 3 GPa range (Figs. 1 to 3). Fig. 3 combines several runs.
- Single-crystal XRD to 21.5 GPa (Table S1, Figs. 4 and 5) gives a clear observation: the 1/3 reflections are lost between 6.4 and 8.4 GPa, and the change is irreversible on unloading.
- The authors document how sensitive CrBr3 is to handling. Cut crystals contain a stacking-disordered component (30+ samples tested), and the significant Cr2 (3a) occupancy in the R-3 refinement (Table 2) is a useful caution for the community.
- The pressure medium was chosen carefully, with photographic evidence of the sample's integrity (Fig. S1).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The P-3m1 subcell does not establish A-A stacking, and the magnetic mechanism rests on that identification. Tables 1 and 3 give a CdI2-type cell (a = 3.64 Å, Z = 1) with Cr disordered over a single 1a site at 2/3 occupancy. This average structure means the in-plane √3×√3 honeycomb superstructure reflections are absent from the integrated data. That is what one expects for random stacking among the three equivalent vacancy registries, or for in-plane Cr/vacancy disorder. It is not what one expects for ordered A-A stacking. Ordered A-A stacking of honeycomb CrBr3 layers would keep the a ≈ 6.3 Å in-plane cell, give a P-31m structure with c ≈ 6.1 Å, and keep the honeycomb reflections at h − k ≠ 3n as sharp Bragg peaks. The text itself (p. 11) describes "randomized" placement of adjacent layers, yet then concludes (p. 12, p. 17, Conclusions) that A-A stacking is "the only viable option." The DFT/MC interlayer coupling JL = −0.35 meV is computed for ordered A-A stacking, so the magnetic interpretation depends on this point.
   - Analyse the diffuse scattering along the h − k ≠ 3n rods, in the DAC data and in the high-resolution maps of Fig. 7. State whether the honeycomb reflections are absent, diffuse or sharp.
   - Test explicitly whether A-A (P-31m, a ≈ 6.3 Å), random stacking, or in-plane Cr disorder best fits the data. Stacking-fault modelling (DIFFaX-type) of the rod profiles would settle this.
   - Reconcile the Conclusions statement of "1/3 occupation of the Cr 1a site" with the 2/3 in Table 3.

2. The structural evolution, measured at room temperature, does not track the magnetic suppression, measured below 40 K. The mechanism requires Tc to fall as the A-A (P-3m1) fraction grows.
   - Fig. 5b shows the R-3 fraction essentially constant at about 0.5 to 0.55 from 0.7 GPa to about 5.7 GPa, dropping only near 6 to 8 GPa. Over that same range Tc falls from 33 K to about 19 K (Fig. 3) and M at 2 T falls from 2.8 to about 0.9 μB/f.u. (Fig. 2). Most of the magnetic suppression therefore happens with no measured change in phase fraction.
   - The starting sample already contains about 45 to 50% P-3m1 (Fig. 5b; the text on p. 4 says about 80% R-3, which also needs reconciling). Yet at ambient pressure the authors observe Tc = 33 K, a nearly full moment of 2.8 μB/f.u., and no antiferromagnetic contribution (p. 2). By their own MC (Fig. 13b), 45% AA would give a Tc about 11 K lower than the pure AB value.
   - Fig. 3 combines several runs, presumably on different samples whose P-3m1 fractions were never measured, yet they fall on one curve. That suggests Tc is insensitive to the initial stacking-fault content.
   - No structural data exist at low temperature under pressure. Both the stacking energetics and the R-3/P-3m1 boundary may change on cooling, especially given the first-order R-3/monoclinic transition at 420 K.
   - Do the following: determine the phase fraction of the magnetization samples; give low-temperature structural data under pressure (or argue quantitatively why the RT boundary applies at 10 K); and plot Tc and Ms against the measured AA fraction instead of against pressure.

3. There is no direct evidence that the phase above pc is antiferromagnetic. The abstract and Conclusions propose an "antiferromagnetic phase emerging beyond pc," but the 9.2 GPa data show a featureless M(T) at 0.01 T (Fig. 1) and a linear M(H) reaching about 0.5 μB/f.u. at 2 T and 10 K (Fig. 2). A Cr3+ (S = 3/2) Curie paramagnet at 10 K and 2 T gives about 0.6 to 0.7 μB, so these data are equally consistent with a paramagnet, a spin glass, or a phase with strongly reduced intralayer exchange.
   - The proposed state predicts specific signatures: (i) a cusp at T_N in M(T); (ii) a spin-flip or metamagnetic step in M(H), estimated at a few tesla for |JL| = 0.35 meV per Cr; and (iii) a Curie-Weiss θ whose sign and size reflect dominant intralayer FM exchange.
   - Weak interlayer AFM coupling between still-ferromagnetic layers should produce A-type order near the intralayer-set ordering temperature. It should not remove all ordering down to 2 K, as Fig. 1 at 9.2 GPa appears to show.
   - Measure M(H) up to 7 T at several temperatures above 6.5 GPa, measure M(T) at higher fields, and fit the high-temperature susceptibility to Curie-Weiss.
   - Table S1 shows that the P-3m1 phase survives unloading. Magnetization of a decompressed, fully P-3m1 crystal at ambient pressure would therefore be a decisive and accessible test of whether this stacking is itself non-ferromagnetic, separated from the effects of compression.

4. The calculations do not reproduce the loss of ferromagnetism, so they cannot "corroborate" it.
   - The MC (Fig. 13) stops at 45% AA, where Tc = 28 K. It never shows Tc → 0 or an AFM ground state.
   - It gives 39 K for pure AB stacking, against 33 K measured.
   - It distributes AA "atoms" randomly per site (p. 16), whereas stacking faults are a property of whole layer pairs.
   - It keeps the intralayer J1 to J3 fixed at literature ambient values (Ref. 73), although the authors' own DFT predicts pressure changes in exchange (p. 2).
   - No calculation shows that pressure favours A-A over ABC stacking.
   - Provide the enthalpy difference between the stacking variants as a function of pressure, JL(p) for both stackings, and pressure-dependent intralayer J. Extend the MC to 100% AA with a layer-wise fault model, and report the resulting ordered state and its ordering temperature.

B. Should address

5. The absolute normalization and background subtraction of the DAC magnetization need quantifying. Ms at 2 T drops from 2.8 to about 2.0 μB/f.u. by 1.1 GPa (Fig. 2), while Tc changes by only about 1 to 2 K (Fig. 3). That combination is hard to reconcile with any homogeneous mechanism and could instead reflect mass or background errors.
   - State how the sample mass was determined and its uncertainty.
   - State whether the ambient-pressure curve is the same crystal in the DAC or a different crystal measured by VSM (Methods mentions both).
   - Show the raw sample and empty-cell signals.
   - Address demagnetization for the out-of-plane plate geometry.
   - The field is given as 0.1 T in the Fig. 1 caption but as 0.01 T on the figure and in Fig. 3. Clarify which is correct.

6. Pressure calibration and hydrostaticity at low temperature. Ruby was read in the Be-Cu turnbuckle cell, but the paper does not say at which temperature. Pressure in such cells typically changes on cooling, and the methanol-ethanol-water medium is a glass at low temperature.
   - Report the low-temperature pressures, or the estimated correction.
   - The three critical pressures are not reconciled: 6.5 GPa from magnetization at low T, 6.4 to 8.4 GPa from XRD at RT, and about 10 GPa from Raman at RT. Discuss whether the differences come from temperature, sample or calibration.

7. The Raman assignment needs a factor-group analysis.
   - R-3 has point group C3i, whose modes are Ag and Eg, so the "A2g" label for the 181.8 cm−1 mode is inconsistent.
   - An ideal CdI2-type P-3m1 cell has only two Raman-active modes (A1g + Eg), yet four "hard" modes (Ph1, Ph6, Ph7, Ph8) are assigned to P-3m1 (Fig. 10).
   - Several modes evolve continuously through pc with only a slope change (Ph5→Ph8, Ph4→Ph7). That fits a single phase stiffening, not a superposition of two phases.
   - Provide the mode count for each candidate structure (including P-31m for true A-A stacking), polarization-resolved spectra, and a comparison of as-grown and cut crystals at ambient pressure. The latter would test whether the "hard" modes scale with the XRD-determined P-3m1 fraction.

8. The ambient-pressure "trimorphism" may be a sample-preparation artefact, and the phase-fraction method is undocumented.
   - The authors report that as-grown crystals showed pure R-3 and that P-3m1 appeared after cutting (p. 3). The high-resolution maps of an as-grown crystal (Fig. 7) "cannot exclude" P-3m1 but do not detect it.
   - Calling P-3m1 a third polymorph (Conclusions) is therefore not supported. The data show handling-induced stacking disorder.
   - Explain how the R-3 fraction in Fig. 5b was obtained: from which reflections, the scale-factor model, and the origin of the error bars (the 8.4 GPa point has ±0.6 and a negative value).
   - Fig. 9 claims powder diffraction cannot distinguish the phases. However, R-3 has weak superstructure reflections, and stacking faults produce characteristic Warren-type asymmetric broadening. Show the low-intensity region of the measured powder pattern on a log scale.

9. Fitting of Tc(p).
   - Eq. (1) is written as (1 − p/pc)^(−1/3), and the same exponent appears on Fig. 3. With a negative exponent Tc diverges at pc, so the sign must be +1/3.
   - State whether the exponent was fixed or free, and give the uncertainty on pc. Only two points (5.9 and 6.4 GPa) lie above 5.1 GPa.
   - At about 3 GPa the AC-susceptibility Tc (Fig. S2, about 23 K at 3.1 GPa) differs from the DC Tc (Fig. 3, about 27 K at 2.9 GPa). The paper presents the AC data as a comparable extrapolation (p. 6), so the discrepancy needs explaining.

10. The structural data contain features that are not discussed.
   - Table S1 gives c = 19.0(2) Å at 0.7 GPa, larger than the ambient 18.382 Å, while Fig. 5a shows about 18.2 Å.
   - Across the transition the volume per formula unit drops by about 3% (88.3 Å3 at 6.4 GPa to 85.7 Å3 at 8.4 GPa), and the interlayer spacing drops from 5.61 to 5.41 Å, while a stays nearly continuous. This suggests a first-order collapse of the interlayer distance, which bears directly on the change in interlayer exchange and on whether the transition is "gradual."
   - Overlay the DFT lattice parameters (Fig. 11) on the measured values.
   - Compute the A-A and random-stacking c parameters for comparison with experiment.

11. The DFT robustness is not demonstrated.
   - JL, and especially its sign for A-A stacking, depends on Ueff and the vdW treatment. Only Ueff = 2 eV with D3 is reported. Show the sign of JL over a range of U, for example 1 to 4 eV.
   - State which structure (R-3 or A-A) and which pressure the JL values and the DOS of Fig. 12 refer to.
   - The text treats a 1.39 eV gap at 10 GPa as consistent with metallization above 20 GPa (p. 15) without justification. Either justify it or drop it.

C. Minor

12. Inconsistencies in captions, tables and equations.
   - The Fig. 4 caption is garbled ("a) a lower b) and c) lower than the transition temperature"; pressure is meant).
   - The Fig. 7 caption gives P-3m1 as space group 150. P-3m1 is no. 164; no. 150 is P321.
   - The Table 1 caption says 289 K, but the P-3m1 column lists 296 K.
   - Eq. (1) number is used for both the Hamiltonian and the Tc fit.
   - The text calls P-3m1 "rhombo" (p. 11).
   - The paper has a "Figure Figure 13a" duplication, and "Burker", "badges" (batches) and "invented the identical procedure" (adopted).
   - Ref. [48] is a placeholder DOI, and Refs. [62] and [75] are duplicates.

13. The novelty claim, "first direct experimental evidence" of the suppression, should be stated precisely relative to Refs. [18], [20] and [39], which already measured the decrease of Tc under pressure. The new element is the measurement through pc and the loss of the spontaneous moment.

14. The loss of Raman intensity above about 11 GPa is attributed to "metallization." With 514.5 nm (2.41 eV) excitation and a gap of 1.4 to 1.7 eV, changes in resonance and absorption with pressure are a more immediate explanation and should be discussed.

15. Fig. 1 does not include the 5.1 GPa run used in Figs. 2 and 3. State which sample and run each curve in Figs. 1 to 3 comes from, so that device-to-device spread can be judged.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Measure the magnetization of a decompressed crystal that Table S1 shows is fully P-3m1. This one experiment tests directly whether the stacking change, rather than compression, removes ferromagnetism.
2. Measure M(H) to 7 T and Curie-Weiss susceptibility above pc to establish whether the high-pressure state is A-type antiferromagnetic, paramagnetic or glassy.
3. Model the diffuse scattering along h − k ≠ 3n rods to distinguish ordered A-A stacking from random stacking or in-plane disorder, and plot Tc against the measured fault fraction.
4. Add DFT enthalpies of the stacking variants versus pressure, and extend the MC to 100% faulted stacking with pressure-dependent intralayer exchange, so the calculation can be compared quantitatively with Fig. 3.
