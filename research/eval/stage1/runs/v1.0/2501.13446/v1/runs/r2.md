Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports SQUID magnetization of CrBr3 single crystals in a diamond anvil cell up to 9.2 GPa. Tc and the 10 K moment fall steadily, and ferromagnetism is reported lost near 6.5 GPa. Room-temperature single-crystal X-ray diffraction to 21.5 GPa shows the 1/3-type reflections of the rhombohedral R-3 phase disappearing between 6.4 and 8.4 GPa, which leaves a subcell P-3m1 description with 2/3 Cr occupancy. The authors attribute the loss of ferromagnetism to pressure-generated "A-A" stacking with antiferromagnetic interlayer exchange, using DFT and atomistic spin simulations. A direct measurement of ferromagnetism being lost under pressure in a CrX3 compound, with a structural explanation based on stacking, would interest the 2D-magnet community. However, the link between structure and magnetism currently rests on room-temperature structural data, a phase fraction that does not follow the magnetic evolution, and simulations that do not reproduce the loss of order.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- High-pressure DC magnetization of a vdW ferromagnet up to 9.2 GPa is technically demanding. Fig. 3 combines several independent pressure runs, and they agree well.
- Single-crystal diffraction under pressure covers a wide range (Table S1, to 21.5 GPa) and includes an unloaded measurement. This shows the R-3 to P-3m1 change is irreversible at room temperature.
- The paper makes a useful methodological point: cutting and handling produce stacking disorder (30+ crystals tested), and refined Cr occupancy on the nominally vacant 3a site (0.244) shows that "ideal" R-3 models of CrBr3 are oversimplified.
- The hydrostaticity checks are welcome (photographs of the sample in different media, Fig. S1).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Structure and magnetism are compared at mismatched temperatures, and there is no structural information at the temperatures where the magnetism is measured. All diffraction (Figs. 4, 5, Tables 1 and S1) and Raman data (Fig. 10) are at room temperature. The magnetization (Figs. 1 to 3) is at 2 to 60 K. The paper itself notes that the R-3 / monoclinic stacking of CrX3 depends on temperature, and stacking transitions in CrI3 are known to shift and show hysteresis on cooling. The central claim, that the P-3m1 / A-A fraction present at low temperature and a given pressure controls Tc, therefore assumes that the room-temperature phase fractions hold unchanged at 10 to 30 K. The pressure loading and cooling sequence is also not stated for any experiment. The magnetic "pc" (6.5 GPa at low T), the X-ray transition (6.4 to 8.4 GPa at RT) and the Raman "pc" (~10 GPa at RT) are then compared as if they were the same quantity. The authors should provide low-temperature diffraction under pressure (synchrotron single-crystal or powder; neutron diffraction would also give the magnetic structure) at a few pressures across 3 to 9 GPa. At minimum, they should state the thermal and pressure history of each magnetization run and discuss how the stacking fractions might change on cooling.

2. The measured R-3 phase fraction does not track the magnetic suppression, and the ambient state already contradicts the mechanism. Fig. 5b gives an R-3 volume fraction of about 0.55 that stays essentially flat from ambient to 5.7 GPa and drops only above ~6 GPa. Over the same range Tc falls from 33 to ~17 K (Fig. 3), and the 10 K moment falls from 2.8 to <1 µB/f.u. at 5.1 GPa (Fig. 2). In the proposed scenario, a growing A-A concentration drives the suppression, so the paper's own structural data contradict it below 6 GPa. In addition:
   - The ambient sample already contains ~45% P-3m1 by Fig. 5b. That is the largest A-A concentration in the simulation (Fig. 13b, Tc ≈ 28 K), yet at ambient pressure Tc = 33 K and the moment is nearly saturated (2.8 µB).
   - Section 2 says the best sample had "~80% volume fraction" of R-3 at the start of the high-pressure experiment. This disagrees with the ~0.55 plotted in Fig. 5b.

   The authors should reconcile these numbers and explain how the phase fraction is extracted (which reflections, how stacking-fault scattering is separated, why the 8.4 GPa error bar is ±0.6). They should show quantitatively that the A-A concentration rises across 0 to 6 GPa, or else revise the mechanism.

3. The nature of the high-pressure magnetic state, an "antiferromagnetic phase" with intact Cr moments, is asserted rather than measured. The evidence is a flat low-field M(T) at 9.2 GPa (Fig. 1) and a nearly linear M(H) to 2 T at 10 K reaching ~0.5 µB/f.u. (Fig. 2). Neither distinguishes an A-type antiferromagnet from a paramagnet, a spin glass, or a state with reduced moments.
   - With strong intralayer ferromagnetic exchange and JL = -0.35 meV, an A-type stacked antiferromagnet should order at a temperature set mainly by the intralayer exchange (tens of K). It should then show a cusp in M(T), and a spin-flip or spin-flop at a field set by JL, which the authors could estimate. Neither is seen.
   - The claim that "the individual Cr ion moments remain intact" is never tested.

   The authors should measure M(T) at several fields (for example 0.1, 1 and 5 T) at 9.2 GPa, and M(H) to the maximum available field (7 T) at 2 K and above any candidate ordering temperature. They should state whether a Néel anomaly exists, estimate the expected spin-flip field, and test whether the moment recovers toward 3 µB at high field. Element-specific probes (XMCD under pressure) would directly test whether the moments are intact.

4. The structural model for the high-pressure phase does not uniquely give "A-A stacking", and the DFT exchange is computed for a structure that has not been established. The P-3m1 cell (a = 3.64 Å, c = 6.12 Å, Cr at 1a with occupancy 2/3) is the average subcell of a honeycomb layer. It describes any stacking in which the position of the honeycomb vacancy is uncorrelated from layer to layer, that is, random or turbostratic shifts among the three equivalent registries. A well-ordered A-A stacking of complete honeycomb layers would keep the √3 × √3 in-plane superstructure, giving P-31m-type cells with a ≈ 6.3 Å and sharp superstructure reflections. The text on pp. 11 and 12 goes back and forth between "random stacking" and "the only viable option is the A-A type stacking fault", and the Conclusions call the phase "A-A layer stacking faults ... with 1/3 occupation of the Cr 1a site", which conflicts with Table 3 (occupancy 0.667). The authors should analyse the diffuse scattering along the honeycomb superstructure rods (h - k ≠ 3n, plotted along L) at high pressure and in the unloaded crystal. They should model it with a stacking-fault program (for example DIFFaX-type calculations) with A-A, ABC and random-shift probabilities as parameters, and state which stacking the DFT value JL = -0.35 meV actually corresponds to.

5. The atomistic simulations do not reproduce the effect they are said to "corroborate". Fig. 13 shows Tc falling only from 39 K to 28 K at the largest A-A fraction simulated (45%). Ferromagnetism is never lost, and the experimental pc and the T = 6 K point at 6.4 GPa are not approached. Several modelling choices make the comparison qualitative at best:
   - J1 to J3 are ambient-pressure values from Ref. [73]. Yet the Introduction says DFT predicts the intralayer exchange increases under pressure, which would raise Tc.
   - JL is taken at zero pressure.
   - "Atoms from the AA and AB phases" are distributed at random site by site. Stacking, however, is a property of whole interfaces between layers, so a site-random sign of the interlayer coupling is not the physical disorder.

   Given that intralayer exchange dominates, the authors should explain physically how antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling on some interfaces can drive Tc toward zero rather than produce a ferrimagnetic or A-type state with Tc near the intralayer scale. They should:
   - redo the simulations with pressure-dependent J1 to J3 and JL computed from DFT at the experimental lattice parameters;
   - model the disorder at the level of interfaces;
   - extend the A-A fraction to 100%;
   - compare the simulated M(T) and M(H) at 2 T with Figs. 1 and 2 directly.

B. Should address

6. No calculation supports the statement in the abstract that the P-3m1 / A-A phase "becomes stable" with pressure. The DFT section gives only R-3 lattice parameters (Fig. 11) and the DOS (Fig. 12). The authors should compute the enthalpy H = E + pV against pressure for ABC (R-3), A-A, monoclinic and AB' stackings at the same level of theory, and say whether a crossover near 6 to 8 GPa is predicted.

7. The Raman analysis has internal inconsistencies.
   - Group theory: a P-3m1 cell with Cr on 1a and Br on 2d has only A1g + Eg Raman-active modes. Yet five modes (Ph1, Ph3, Ph6, Ph7, Ph8) are assigned to P-3m1 in Fig. 10.
   - "A2g" is listed as a Raman mode at 181.8 cm⁻¹, but in R-3 (C3i) the Raman-active modes are Ag and Eg.
   - The Raman "pc" of ~10 GPa disagrees with the X-ray value of 6.4 to 8.4 GPa, and the conclusions quote both without reconciliation.
   - The loss of intensity is attributed to "gradual metallization", while the authors' own DFT gives a 1.39 eV gap at 10 GPa.

   The authors should provide a factor-group analysis for both structures (and for the expected local A-A stacking), fit peak positions and widths with uncertainties instead of relying on guide lines on a colour map, and either support the metallization interpretation (for example absorption or resistance) or remove it.

8. The magnetization pressures need low-temperature calibration, and the frozen medium may itself create the stacking faults. In the turnbuckle Cu-Be cell, pressure usually changes on cooling, and the 16:3:1 methanol-ethanol-water medium is a glass at 2 to 60 K. The authors' central thesis is that CrBr3 stacking responds to "minute" shear (Section 2, Fig. S1a). Non-hydrostatic stress that develops on cooling could then create stacking faults independently of pressure, and could differ between runs. The authors should state where and at what temperature the ruby pressure was read for each magnetization point. They should also give the ruby linewidth as a measure of hydrostaticity, and discuss whether runs with different loadings and cooling protocols give the same Tc(p). Fig. 3 suggests they might, which would be a useful argument.

9. The determination of pc and the fit form need correction and uncertainties. Eq. (1) on p. 6 and the label in Fig. 3 write Tc = Tc(0)(1 - p/pc)^(-1/3). With a negative exponent Tc diverges at pc, so the exponent must be +1/3. The authors do not justify the exponent, give no fit uncertainties, and do not say how Tc is defined from the 0.01 T M(T) curves (onset, inflection, dM/dT minimum). "Loss of ferromagnetism at ~6.5 GPa" rests on an extrapolation from the 6.4 GPa point (Tc = 6 K, near the 2 K base temperature) and on the 9.2 GPa curve, with no data in between. The authors should report pc with an error bar, compare it with a linear or other fit, define Tc, and add at least one pressure between 6.5 and 9 GPa if feasible. Without that, they should temper the claim to "Tc < 2 K above ~6.5 GPa".

10. The absolute moment values under pressure need error estimates, and the 10 K moments do not track Tc. At 1.1 GPa the 2 T moment falls by ~25% (2.8 to ~2.1 µB/f.u., Fig. 2), while Tc changes by only ~2 K. At T/Tc ≈ 0.3 thermal reduction cannot explain this, so the authors should consider sample-mass normalization, incomplete background subtraction of the cell, or partial loss or fracture of the sample. The 0.01 T M(T) values (~0.08 µB/f.u.) are governed by domains and demagnetization, not by the ordered moment, so their pressure dependence in Fig. 1 does not measure Ms. The authors should state how the sample mass in the DAC was determined and its uncertainty. They should show the raw signal next to the cell background for the highest pressures, and, if possible, re-measure ambient pressure after release to check that the moment normalization holds.

11. The authors do not report the magnetization of the decompressed P-3m1 sample, which is the most direct test of their mechanism. Table S1 shows that the crystal stays P-3m1 after unloading, with no 1/3 satellites. If P-3m1 / A-A stacking carries antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling, the unloaded crystal should show no spontaneous moment, or a strongly reduced one, at ambient pressure. The authors should measure M(T) and M(H) of a pressure-cycled crystal at ambient pressure, and state whether the magnetization in the DAC recovers on releasing pressure.

12. Sample provenance and the claim of "trimorphism" need clarification. Different crystals were used for magnetization, diffraction and Raman, and the P-3m1 content of the magnetization samples is unknown. Section 2 says P-3m1 appears after cutting, while the reciprocal-space maps of an as-grown crystal "cannot exclude" P-3m1 (Fig. 7 and text on p. 13). The Conclusions nevertheless state that a third phase coexists with R-3 at room temperature, "verified ... on dozens" of crystals. The authors should state which batches and pieces went into each figure. They should show whether uncut, as-grown crystals contain P-3m1 (for example by measuring rods with h - k ≠ 3n). They should frame P-3m1 as a description of handling-induced stacking disorder unless there is evidence for a distinct thermodynamic phase.

13. Several lattice-parameter values look anomalous or inconsistent.
   - In Table S1, c at 0.7 GPa (19.0(2) Å) is larger than at ambient (18.382 Å), and V is essentially unchanged (631 vs 632.8 Å³).
   - The P-3m1 parameters in Table 1 (a = 3.6439 Å, T = 296 K) differ from the unloaded values in Table S1 (a = 3.6498 Å), so it is unclear which crystal Table 1 comes from.
   - Fig. 5a should show error bars.
   - The authors should fit an equation of state and compare quantitatively with the DFT curves in Fig. 11. That comparison is currently only stated as "reproduce well".

14. The DFT exchange constants need sensitivity checks. Uₑff = 2 eV is fixed, and the sign and size of the interlayer exchange in CrX3 are known to depend on U, on the vdW treatment, and on whether lattice parameters are relaxed or experimental. The methods also call DFT-D3 a "non-local vdW functional", which it is not. The authors should give JL for AB/ABC and A-A at a few U values (for example 1 to 4 eV) and with experimental lattice parameters, and state the magnetic configurations and supercells used for J1 to J3. Those values are currently taken from an external reference rather than computed consistently.

C. Minor

15. The caption of Fig. 1 says the field is 0.1 T, while the panel legend and Fig. 3 say 0.01 T (100 Oe). Please correct it.

16. The caption of Fig. 4 is garbled ("a) ambient pressure, and a) a lower b) and c) lower than the transition temperature"). It should refer to pressures (0, 6.4 and 8.4 GPa). The histogram panels also need axis labels.

17. Two different equations are both numbered (1): the Hamiltonian on p. 5 and the Tc fit on p. 6. Refs. [62] and [75] are the same paper.

18. In Table 3 the Cr occupancy is given as 0.667 without an uncertainty. Please state whether it was fixed or refined.

19. The calling the ABC (R-3) stacking the "AB phase" in the simulation section is confusing. Please use consistent stacking labels throughout, and state the per-Cr convention for JL against the per-bond J values.

20. Fig. 9a inset shows a split or broadened reflection near 29.2° in the single-crystal pattern that is not discussed. Please explain it.

21. The novelty statement "first direct experimental evidence" should state precisely what is new. Earlier studies (Refs. [18], [20], [39]) already reported a decrease of Tc under pressure; the new element here is reaching the suppressed state.

22. Language and typos: "Burker" (Bruker), "badges" (batches), "subscribed" (ascribed), "rhombo P-3m1", "We have invented the identical procedure", "rate of Tc vs. p fall gradually accelerates", "X-diffraction", and "Figure Figure 13a".

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Do low-temperature diffraction under pressure (synchrotron X-ray, or neutron for the magnetic structure as well) at three or four pressures across 3 to 9 GPa. This gives the stacking fractions where the magnetism is measured and tests directly whether the state above pc is antiferromagnetic.
2. Measure the magnetization of a crystal that has been pressurized into P-3m1 and released, at ambient pressure. Together with high-field M(H) to 7 T at 2 K above pc, this would test both the stacking-AFM mechanism and the "intact moment" claim with modest extra effort.
3. Replace the site-random Monte Carlo with a model of stacking disorder at the level of interfaces, using pressure-dependent J1 to J3 and JL from DFT. Back it with enthalpy calculations of the competing stackings, and compare simulated M(T) and M(H) directly with Figs. 1 and 2.
4. Analyse the diffuse scattering along the rods with h - k ≠ 3n using stacking-fault modelling, to decide between true A-A stacking and random interlayer shifts. Then rewrite the structural sections and conclusions so the P-3m1 average structure, the stacking model, and the structure used in DFT all agree.
