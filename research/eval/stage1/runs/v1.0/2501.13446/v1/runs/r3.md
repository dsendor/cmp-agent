Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports SQUID magnetization of CrBr3 single crystals in a diamond anvil cell up to 9.2 GPa. It finds that T_C and the magnetization fall, faster above about 3 GPa, and that ferromagnetism is lost near 6.5 GPa. Room-temperature single-crystal XRD and Raman spectroscopy show that a P-3m1 average structure, with 2/3 Cr occupancy, grows at the expense of the R-3 phase and becomes the only phase between 6.4 and 8.4 GPa. The authors argue that pressure-generated A-A stacking with antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling causes the suppression, and DFT and Monte Carlo calculations support this. A direct high-pressure magnetization study of CrBr3 beyond 3 GPa is valuable. A pressure-driven stacking change that controls bulk ferromagnetism in a vdW magnet would interest a broad audience. In its present form, however, the link between the structural and magnetic data is asserted more than demonstrated.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Demanding DC magnetization measurements in a DAC up to 9.2 GPa (Figs. 1-3), with T_C followed over several runs, extend earlier data that stopped near 3 GPa (Refs. 18, 20, 39).
- Single-crystal XRD under pressure up to 21.5 GPa (Table S1, Figs. 4-5), with full refinements at ambient pressure for both phases (Tables 1-3), gives more structural information than earlier powder studies. The observation that cutting a crystal produces the P-3m1 phase, seen in more than 30 crystals, is a practically important warning for the field.
- The authors document the sample's integrity in different pressure media (Fig. S1) and give the Raman data in full (Fig. S4).
- The paper combines magnetization, structure, Raman, DFT and atomistic spin simulations in one study.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The structural phase fraction does not track the magnetic suppression, and the two are measured at very different temperatures. Fig. 5b shows an R-3 fraction of about 0.5-0.55, nearly constant from ambient pressure to 5.7 GPa. Over the same range T_C falls from 33 K to about 18 K (Fig. 3), and the 10 K moment falls from 2.8 to about 0.85 μB/f.u. (Fig. 2). Taken at face value, about 45% of the sample is already "AA" (P-3m1) at ambient pressure. Yet the ambient crystal is a fully saturated ferromagnet with T_C = 33 K. Your own simulation (Fig. 13b) gives T_C ≈ 28 K for 45% AA, and Fig. 13a gives a reduced, not full, ordering tendency. The Methods (p. 4) also state that the best sample had ~80% R-3 at the start, which is inconsistent with the ~0.55 in Fig. 5b. In addition, all structural data are taken at room temperature (Table 1: 289/296 K), while the magnetic suppression occurs below 35 K. The cell pressure and the stacking fractions on cooling are unknown. As it stands, the central causal claim (stacking change leads to loss of FM) is a correlation between two different pressure scales: 6.5 GPa at low T from magnetization, and 6.4-8.4 GPa (XRD) or about 10 GPa (Raman) at room temperature. The authors should:
   - reconcile Fig. 5b with the Methods statement and with the full ferromagnetism at ambient pressure;
   - give the initial phase fraction of each crystal used for magnetization;
   - ideally determine the stacking state at low temperature under pressure, by low-T XRD in a DAC or at minimum low-T Raman;
   - if this cannot be done, restate the conclusion as a hypothesis.

2. There is no direct evidence for the proposed antiferromagnetic state, and the moment reduction is not reproduced by the model. The model keeps the intralayer ferromagnetism intact and reverses only the interlayer coupling (J_L = -0.35 meV for AA). Such an A-type, or partly A-type, state has two clear signatures. It should show a Néel transition with a susceptibility cusp on a temperature scale set by the intralayer exchange, comparable to the former T_C. It should also show a metamagnetic (spin-flip or spin-flop) transition that restores the full ~3 μB/Cr. From |J_L| the field of that transition is estimated at the order of a few tesla. The 9.2 GPa M(T) (Fig. 1) shows no transition, and the isotherms (Fig. 2) stop at 2 T at a single temperature of 10 K, so neither prediction is tested. Moreover, Fig. 13a shows M/M0 → 1 at low T for every AA fraction, so the simulation does not account for the large drop of the saturated moment in Fig. 2 (2.8 → 2.0 μB/f.u. already at 1.1 GPa). The authors should:
   - measure M(H) to the highest field available (7 T in an MPMS) at 2 K and at several pressures;
   - measure M(T) at 9.2 GPa in a larger field to look for a Néel anomaly;
   - show whether the full moment is recovered;
   - compute the field-dependent magnetization of their spin model for comparison.
   Without this, the abstract's statement of "an antiferromagnetic phase emerging" is not supported.

3. The absolute calibration of the magnetization in the DAC is not described, and the trend in moment versus pressure depends on it. Fig. 2 compares absolute moments (μB/f.u.) across pressures. The sample in a turnbuckle DAC weighs at most micrograms. The background is subtracted using a separate empty-cell run, and Fig. 3 states that "several different runs" were combined. The ambient curve in Fig. 1 (smooth, green) appears to come from a different measurement, possibly the PPMS VSM. The 30% drop in moment at 1.1 GPa, with T_C nearly unchanged (31 K, Fig. 3), is surprising and could arise from errors in mass or volume, from background subtraction, or from a sample change between runs. The authors should state, for each curve in Figs. 1-2:
   - which sample and run it comes from;
   - how the sample mass or volume was determined and with what uncertainty;
   - whether the ambient reference was measured on the same crystal inside the loaded cell;
   - how large the cell background is relative to the sample signal at 2 T.
   Error bars on M_S, or normalized curves from a single sample across pressures, are needed before a pressure-induced moment reduction can be claimed.

4. The structural model (A-A stacking) does not follow from the refinement, and the paper describes it in two incompatible ways. The P-3m1 cell (a = 3.64 Å) with 2/3 Cr occupancy at 1a is a subcell in which the honeycomb order is averaged away. Page 11 states that such a model arises when "the placement of the adjacent layer is randomized along the c-axis", which describes random stacking. The text then concludes that "the only viable option is the A-A type stacking fault". Ordered A-A stacking of honeycomb layers would keep the in-plane √3×√3 honeycomb reflections as sharp Bragg peaks. Random stacking would turn them into diffuse rods along c*. Neither case is discussed, and the reciprocal-space images in Fig. 4 are too small to judge. This distinction is central, because the DFT value J_L = -0.35 meV is computed for one specific ordered (AA) stacking, while random stacking would mix several interlayer registries with different signs of coupling (Ref. 36). The authors should:
   - analyse intensity along the honeycomb superstructure rods (h - k ≠ 3n), including diffuse scattering;
   - fit a stacking-fault model (for example DIFFaX-type, or a refined fraction of each stacking vector);
   - state explicitly which ordered supercell was used in DFT to represent "the AA phase";
   - explain how a structure with fractional Cr occupancy was mapped onto it.

5. Hydrostaticity and pressure calibration are critical for a material the authors themselves describe as damaged by "any kind of physical incursions", and they are not established. Methods (p. 4) quote a hydrostatic limit of ~14 GPa for methanol-ethanol-water 16:3:1. Refs. 45-46 place it near 10 GPa, which coincides with the Raman p_c (Fig. 10). For the magnetization runs, the medium is frozen at all temperatures below ~200 K. Pressure in turnbuckle cells also typically changes on cooling, and the paper does not say whether the ruby was read at room or low temperature. If deviatoric stress, rather than hydrostatic pressure, generates the stacking faults (as cutting does, p. 3), then the result describes stress-induced damage, not a pressure-driven phase. The authors should:
   - state where and at what temperature each pressure was determined, with uncertainty;
   - report ruby linewidths, or the R1-R2 splitting, versus pressure;
   - correct the hydrostatic limit;
   - ideally repeat key points with a different medium (for example Daphne 7575 or a gas medium) or report a pressure gradient across the culet.

B. Should address

6. The fit of Eq. (1) has the wrong sign in the exponent and an unjustified functional form, and it determines the headline p_c. As written (p. 6 and the label in Fig. 3), T_C = T_C(0)(1 - p/p_c)^(-1/3) diverges at p_c. Presumably +1/3 is meant. The exponent 1/3 is not motivated, and with T_C(0) fixed the value of p_c = 6.5 GPa is set almost entirely by the single 6.4 GPa point (T_C ≈ 6 K). The authors should:
   - correct the equation;
   - justify or free the exponent;
   - give p_c with a fit uncertainty;
   - say that "loss of FM" means T_C below the base temperature reached at 9.2 GPa.
   The comparison with the 8.4 GPa linear extrapolation of Ref. 20 should be framed accordingly.

7. The Monte Carlo model does not test the proposed mechanism quantitatively. Several assumptions limit it:
   - Each Cr atom is assigned at random to the AA or AB "phase" (p. 16), whereas stacking faults act on whole layer interfaces.
   - The intralayer J1-J3 are taken from ambient-pressure neutron data (Ref. 73) and held fixed, although p. 2 cites DFT predicting that the exchange increases with pressure.
   - Pure AB gives T_C = 39 K, against 33 K measured.
   - Even 45% AA only lowers T_C to 28 K, so the simulation does not reach suppression of ferromagnetism and does not reproduce the moment loss (see Concern 2).
   The authors should:
   - assign the sign of the interlayer coupling per interface;
   - include the pressure dependence of all J's from their own DFT at each pressure;
   - extend the AA fraction to 100%;
   - compare T_C(p) and M(p) directly with Figs. 2-3, using the measured phase fractions as input.

8. The DFT calculations do not address whether pressure favours AA stacking, and the key exchange values are reported without sensitivity tests. The paper claims the P-3m1/AA phase "becomes stable" under pressure but reports no enthalpy comparison of the ABC, AB and AA stackings versus pressure. Such a comparison would directly test the proposed driving force. J_L (0.83 and -0.35 meV) is given for a single U_eff = 2 eV with D3. Interlayer exchange in CrX3 is known to depend on U and on the vdW treatment. The authors should provide:
   - stacking-resolved enthalpies from 0 to 10 GPa;
   - J_L for AA and AB versus pressure;
   - a check of the sign and magnitude of J_L against U (for example 1-4 eV).

9. The Raman assignment is not supported by symmetry analysis. Several modes are assigned to P-3m1 (red lines: Ph1, Ph3 and Ph6-Ph8 in Fig. 10) on the basis of their pressure slopes alone. In the P-3m1 average structure, Br sits at 2d and Cr at 1a, which gives only A1g + Eg Raman-active modes. R-3 gives 4Ag + 4Eg. The mode labelled A2g at 181.8 cm^-1 (p. 14) is not Raman active in either group. Spectra are described as polarized, but no polarization analysis is shown that would separate the Ag and Eg modes. The authors should:
   - give the factor-group analysis for both structures, or for an ordered AA supercell;
   - use parallel and crossed polarization to assign symmetries;
   - explain why the Raman p_c (~10 GPa) is higher than the XRD p_c (6.4-8.4 GPa) on the same type of sample at the same temperature.
   They should also reconsider attributing the loss of intensity to metallization: their DFT gap at 10 GPa is still 1.39 eV, and with 2.41 eV excitation a change in resonance conditions is a more direct explanation.

10. Reversibility is not tested, although the paper itself sets up a decisive experiment. Table S1 shows that the crystal remains P-3m1 after unloading (a = 3.6498 Å, no 1/3 satellites). If the stacking model is right, this recovered crystal, or one recovered after a magnetization run, should show reduced ferromagnetism or antiferromagnetic behaviour at ambient pressure. The authors should measure M(T) and M(H) of a recovered crystal. They should also state whether the magnetization at low pressure is recovered after decompression.

11. The number of samples and the spread between them for the magnetic data are unclear. Fig. 3 combines "several different runs", identified by symbol colour, but the paper does not say how many crystals were used, what their initial R-3/P-3m1 fractions were, or whether the same pressure gives the same T_C in different crystals. Because the starting phase fraction varies from crystal to crystal (p. 3: "a matter of chance"), the authors should list each crystal with its initial phase fraction and show whether T_C(p) and M_S(p) depend on it. Such a dependence would itself be a test of the model.

12. The lattice parameters in Table S1 and Fig. 5a disagree, and the transition looks first order. Table S1 lists c = 19.0(2) Å at 0.7 GPa, larger than the ambient value of 18.382 Å, while Fig. 5a plots about 18.3 Å. The parameter a drops by 1.8% between 0 and 0.7 GPa and then does not change up to 3 GPa. Between 6.4 and 8.4 GPa the volume per R-3-equivalent cell drops from 530 to about 514 Å^3 (9 × 57.1), a collapse of about 3%, and c/3 drops from 5.61 to 5.41 Å. This contradicts the description on p. 7 of parameters decreasing "proportionally" and the picture of gradual fault generation. The authors should:
   - resolve the discrepancy at 0.7 GPa;
   - fit an equation of state to each phase;
   - compare the bulk moduli with the DFT results in Fig. 11;
   - discuss the volume discontinuity.

13. The method for deriving the R-3 fraction in Fig. 5b is not given. The authors should explain how the fraction was obtained (ratio of satellite to main reflections, or a two-phase refinement) and how the error bars were computed. They should comment on the point at 8.4 GPa, which is negative with an error bar of about ±0.6. The dotted sigmoid curve should be identified as a guide to the eye or as a fit.

C. Minor

14. Fig. 1 gives the field as 0.1 T in the caption, while the panel says B = 0.01 T and Fig. 3 says 100 Oe. Please also state the cooling protocol (ZFC or FC). The 5.9 GPa curve rises below about 17 K and then falls again below about 13 K. This non-monotonic behaviour should be explained, for example as domain effects, a second transition or an artifact.

15. Several text inconsistencies need correcting:
   - The Conclusions state "1/3 occupation of the Cr 1a site", whereas Table 3 and the text give 2/3.
   - The Fig. 4 caption refers to "lower than the transition temperature" where pressure is meant, and labels two panels "a)".
   - Two different equations are both numbered "(1)" (p. 5 and p. 6).
   - The Fig. 6 caption cites the in-plane views b) and d) for stacking sequences that are visible only in a) and c).
   - "We have invented the identical procedure" (p. 3) should be "We used".
   - "Burker" should be "Bruker" and "badges" should be "batches".

16. Fig. 11 is said to "reproduce well the experimentally observed evolution", but the experimental points are not overlaid. Please plot the measured a and c (Table S1) together with the calculated values.

17. The claim of the "first direct experimental evidence" of pressure-induced suppression should be qualified relative to Refs. 18, 20 and 39, which already measured a decrease of T_C under pressure. What is new is the measurement beyond 3 GPa and to the point of disappearance.

18. References: Refs. 62 and 75 are duplicates. Refs. 41 and 48 have placeholder DOIs. The CrI3 data in Fig. S3, described as preliminary, show T_C still at 41 K at 6 GPa. The statement that CrI3 and CrBr3 behave in a "striking" similar way (p. 6-7) should be toned down or supported.

19. Fig. S2: the arrows that mark T_C and the extrapolation described in the text are hard to follow. Please state the criterion used to define T_C from χ'(T), and show the AC-derived T_C(p) in Fig. 3 for comparison.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Measure magnetization at ambient pressure on a crystal recovered from high pressure (P-3m1 according to Table S1) and on deliberately cut or deformed crystals with known P-3m1 fractions. This separates the effect of stacking from the effect of pressure without a DAC, and it is the most direct test of the proposed mechanism.
2. Extend the isotherms to 7 T at 2 K under pressure and look for the spin-flip transition and the Néel anomaly that the proposed A-type interlayer AFM requires.
3. Obtain structural information at low temperature under pressure: low-T single-crystal XRD in the DAC, or at least low-T Raman. This places the structural and magnetic critical pressures on the same footing.
4. Replace the site-random Monte Carlo model with a layer-resolved stacking-fault model that uses pressure-dependent exchange constants from DFT. Support it with DFT enthalpies showing that AA (or random) stacking is favoured under pressure.
