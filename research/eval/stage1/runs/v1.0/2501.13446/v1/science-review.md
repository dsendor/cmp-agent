A. Must address

1. The measured R-3 phase fraction does not track the magnetic suppression, and the ambient state already contradicts the proposed mechanism. The mechanism requires T_C to fall as the A-A (P-3m1) fraction grows. Fig. 5b, however, gives an R-3 volume fraction of about 0.5 to 0.55 that stays essentially flat from ambient pressure (0.7 GPa) to about 5.7 GPa and drops only near 6 to 8 GPa. Over that same range T_C falls from 33 K to about 17 to 19 K (Fig. 3), and the 2 T / 10 K moment falls from 2.8 to below 1 μB/f.u. (about 0.85 to 0.9 μB/f.u. at 5.1 GPa, Fig. 2). Most of the magnetic suppression therefore happens with no measured change in phase fraction. Taken at face value, about 45 to 50% of the starting sample is already P-3m1. That is the largest A-A concentration simulated (Fig. 13b), which gives T_C ≈ 28 K, about 11 K below the pure-AB value. Yet at ambient pressure the authors observe T_C = 33 K, a nearly saturated moment of 2.8 μB/f.u. and no antiferromagnetic contribution (p. 2). In addition, the Methods/Section 2 text (p. 4) states that the best sample had about 80% R-3 at the start of the high-pressure experiment, which disagrees with the ~0.55 plotted in Fig. 5b. The authors should reconcile Fig. 5b with the Methods statement and with full ferromagnetism at ambient pressure, plot T_C and M_S against the measured A-A fraction rather than against pressure, and show quantitatively that the A-A concentration rises across 0 to 6 GPa, or else revise the mechanism or restate it as a hypothesis.

2. Structure and magnetism are measured at very different temperatures, and there is no structural information where the magnetism is measured. All diffraction (Figs. 4, 5, Tables 1 and S1; Table 1 at 289/296 K) and Raman data (Fig. 10) are at room temperature, while the magnetization (Figs. 1 to 3) and the suppression occur at 2 to 60 K. The paper itself notes that the stacking of CrX3 depends on temperature (first-order R-3 / monoclinic transition at 420 K), and stacking transitions in CrI3 are known to shift and show hysteresis on cooling. The central claim therefore assumes that room-temperature phase fractions and the R-3/P-3m1 boundary hold unchanged at 10 to 30 K. The pressure-loading and cooling sequence is not stated for any experiment. As a result, three different critical pressures are compared as if they were the same quantity without reconciliation: 6.5 GPa from magnetization at low T, 6.4 to 8.4 GPa from XRD at room temperature, and about 10 GPa from Raman at room temperature. The causal claim is then a correlation between different pressure scales. The authors should provide low-temperature structural data under pressure at three or four pressures across 3 to 9 GPa (low-T single-crystal or powder synchrotron XRD in a DAC; neutron diffraction would also give the magnetic structure; at minimum low-T Raman), or argue quantitatively why the room-temperature boundary applies at 10 K. At minimum they should state the thermal and pressure history of each magnetization run, discuss how stacking fractions might change on cooling, and discuss whether the differences between the three critical pressures come from temperature, sample or calibration.

3. The P-3m1 subcell does not establish ordered A-A stacking, and the magnetic mechanism and DFT exchange rest on that identification. Tables 1 and 3 give a CdI2-type average cell (a = 3.64 Å, c = 6.12 Å, Z = 1) with Cr disordered over a single 1a site at 2/3 occupancy (Br at 2d). In this subcell the in-plane √3×√3 honeycomb superstructure is averaged away. That is what one expects for random or turbostratic stacking among the three equivalent vacancy registries, or for in-plane Cr/vacancy disorder; random stacking would turn the honeycomb reflections into diffuse rods along c*. Ordered A-A stacking of complete honeycomb layers would instead keep the a ≈ 6.3 Å in-plane cell (a P-31m-type cell with c ≈ 6.1 Å) and keep the h − k ≠ 3n honeycomb reflections as sharp Bragg peaks. The text on p. 11 describes the model as arising when "the placement of the adjacent layer is randomized along the c-axis", yet pp. 12 and 17 and the Conclusions state that "the only viable option is the A-A type stacking fault". The Conclusions also describe the phase as having "1/3 occupation of the Cr 1a site", which conflicts with the 0.667 in Table 3. The reciprocal-space images in Fig. 4 are too small to judge. This matters because the DFT value J_L = −0.35 meV is computed for one specific ordered A-A stacking, whereas random stacking would mix several interlayer registries with different signs of coupling (Ref. 36). The authors should analyse the intensity, including diffuse scattering, along the h − k ≠ 3n rods (plotted along L) in the DAC data at high pressure, in the unloaded crystal and in the high-resolution maps of Fig. 7, and state whether the honeycomb reflections are absent, diffuse or sharp. They should fit a stacking-fault model (for example DIFFaX-type, with A-A, ABC and random-shift probabilities as parameters) to decide between A-A, random stacking and in-plane disorder; state which ordered supercell was used in DFT for "the AA phase" and how a structure with fractional Cr occupancy was mapped onto it; and correct the 1/3 versus 2/3 occupancy so that the average structure, the stacking model and the DFT structure agree.

4. There is no direct evidence that the phase above p_c is antiferromagnetic with intact Cr moments. The abstract and Conclusions propose "an antiferromagnetic phase emerging" beyond p_c, but the evidence is a featureless low-field (0.01 T) M(T) at 9.2 GPa (Fig. 1) and a nearly linear M(H) reaching about 0.5 μB/f.u. at 2 T and 10 K (Fig. 2). A Cr3+ (S = 3/2) Curie paramagnet at 10 K and 2 T gives about 0.6 to 0.7 μB, so these data are equally consistent with a paramagnet, a spin glass, a phase with strongly reduced intralayer exchange, or a state with reduced moments. The proposed model keeps the intralayer ferromagnetism intact and reverses only the interlayer coupling (J_L = −0.35 meV). An A-type state with weak interlayer AFM coupling between ferromagnetic layers should order at a temperature set by the intralayer exchange (tens of K, comparable to the former T_C), not lose order down to 2 K as Fig. 1 at 9.2 GPa appears to show. It predicts specific signatures: (i) a Néel cusp in M(T); (ii) a spin-flip, spin-flop or metamagnetic step in M(H) at a field of order a few tesla for |J_L| = 0.35 meV per Cr, restoring the full ~3 μB/Cr; and (iii) a Curie-Weiss θ whose sign and size reflect dominant intralayer FM exchange. The isotherms stop at 2 T at the single temperature of 10 K, so none of these is tested, and the claim that "the individual Cr ion moments remain intact" is never tested. The authors should measure M(H) to the maximum field available (7 T in an MPMS) at 2 K and at several temperatures and pressures above 6.5 GPa, measure M(T) at 9.2 GPa at several fields (for example 0.1, 1 and 5 T), fit the high-temperature susceptibility to Curie-Weiss, estimate the expected spin-flip field, and test whether the moment recovers toward 3 μB at high field. Element-specific probes (XMCD under pressure) would directly test whether the moments are intact. Without this, the statement of an emerging antiferromagnetic phase is not supported.

5. The atomistic simulations do not reproduce the loss of ferromagnetism or the moment reduction, so they cannot "corroborate" them. The Monte Carlo (Fig. 13) stops at 45% AA, where T_C = 28 K; it never shows T_C → 0, never approaches the experimental p_c or the T_C = 6 K point at 6.4 GPa, and never shows an AFM ground state. It gives 39 K for pure AB stacking against 33 K measured. Fig. 13a shows M/M0 → 1 at low T for every AA fraction, so the model does not account for the large drop of the saturated moment in Fig. 2 (2.8 → 2.0 μB/f.u. already at 1.1 GPa). Several modelling choices make the comparison qualitative at best: AA and AB "atoms" are distributed randomly site by site (p. 16), whereas stacking faults are a property of whole interfaces between layers, so a site-random sign of the interlayer coupling is not the physical disorder; the intralayer J1 to J3 are fixed at ambient-pressure literature values (Ref. 73, neutron data), although the Introduction (p. 2) cites DFT predicting that intralayer exchange increases with pressure, which would raise T_C; and J_L is taken at zero pressure. Given that intralayer exchange dominates, the authors should explain physically how AFM interlayer coupling on some interfaces can drive T_C toward zero rather than produce a ferrimagnetic or A-type state with T_C near the intralayer scale. They should redo the simulations with disorder assigned per interface, with pressure-dependent J1 to J3 and J_L from their own DFT at the experimental lattice parameters, extend the AA fraction to 100%, report the resulting ordered state and its ordering temperature, use the measured phase fractions as input, and compare simulated T_C(p), M(T) and field-dependent M(H) at 2 T directly with Figs. 1 to 3.

B. Should address

6. Hydrostaticity and pressure calibration at low temperature are not established, and the frozen medium may itself create the stacking faults. Ruby was read in the Be-Cu turnbuckle cell, but the paper does not say where or at what temperature; pressure in such cells typically changes on cooling. The 16:3:1 methanol-ethanol-water medium is a glass at 2 to 60 K, and its hydrostatic limit is quoted in the Methods (p. 4) as ~14 GPa, whereas Refs. 45-46 place it near 10 GPa, coinciding with the Raman p_c (Fig. 10). The authors' central thesis is that CrBr3 stacking responds to "minute" shear and "any kind of physical incursions" (Section 2, p. 3, Fig. S1a). If non-hydrostatic stress developing on cooling, rather than hydrostatic pressure, generates the faults (as cutting does), the result describes stress-induced damage, not a pressure-driven phase, and it could differ between runs. The authors should state where and at what temperature each pressure was determined, with its uncertainty or the estimated low-temperature correction; report ruby linewidths or R1-R2 splitting versus pressure; correct the hydrostatic limit; discuss whether runs with different loadings and cooling protocols give the same T_C(p) (Fig. 3 suggests they might, which would be a useful argument); and ideally repeat key points with a different medium (for example Daphne 7575 or a gas medium) or report the pressure gradient across the culet.

7. The magnetization of a decompressed P-3m1 crystal is not reported, although it is the most direct test of the mechanism. Table S1 shows that the crystal remains P-3m1 after unloading (a = 3.6498 Å, no 1/3 satellites). If P-3m1 / A-A stacking carries antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling, a recovered crystal should show no spontaneous moment, or a strongly reduced one, at ambient pressure. This separates the effect of stacking from the effect of compression without a DAC. The authors should measure M(T) and M(H) at ambient pressure on a pressure-cycled crystal (ideally one recovered after a magnetization run, and also deliberately cut or deformed crystals with known P-3m1 fractions), and state whether the magnetization in the DAC recovers on releasing pressure.

8. The absolute normalization and background subtraction of the DAC magnetization are not described, and the moment-versus-pressure trend depends on them. Fig. 2 compares absolute moments (μB/f.u.) across pressures for a sample of at most micrograms, with the background subtracted from a separate empty-cell run, and Fig. 3 combines "several different runs". The 2 T moment drops by ~25 to 30% from 2.8 to about 2.0 to 2.1 μB/f.u. by 1.1 GPa, while T_C changes by only about 1 to 2 K (31 K, Fig. 3). At T/T_C ≈ 0.3 thermal reduction cannot explain this, and it is hard to reconcile with any homogeneous mechanism; it could arise from mass or volume errors, incomplete background subtraction, or partial loss or fracture of the sample. The ambient curve in Fig. 1 (smooth, green) appears to come from a different measurement, and the Methods mention both the DAC SQUID and a PPMS VSM. The 0.01 T M(T) values (~0.08 μB/f.u.) are governed by domains and demagnetization, so their pressure dependence in Fig. 1 does not measure M_S. The authors should state, for each curve in Figs. 1 and 2, which sample and run it comes from, how the sample mass or volume was determined and with what uncertainty, and whether the ambient reference was measured on the same crystal inside the loaded cell; show the raw sample signal next to the empty-cell background, especially at 2 T and the highest pressures; address demagnetization for the out-of-plane plate geometry; give error bars on M_S or normalized curves from a single sample across pressures; and, if possible, re-measure at ambient pressure after release to check the normalization.

9. The T_C(p) fit has the wrong exponent sign and an unjustified form, and it alone determines the headline p_c. Eq. (1) (p. 6) and the label in Fig. 3 write T_C = T_C(0)(1 − p/p_c)^(−1/3), which diverges at p_c; the exponent must be +1/3. The exponent is not motivated, no fit uncertainties are given, and it is not stated whether the exponent was fixed or free. 14. The Raman assignment and interpretation are not supported by symmetry analysis. A P-3m1 CdI2-type cell with Cr at 1a and Br at 2d has only A1g + Eg Raman-active modes, and R-3 (C3i) gives 4Ag + 4Eg; yet four or five modes (Ph1, Ph3, Ph6, Ph7, Ph8) are assigned to P-3m1 in Fig. 10 on the basis of pressure slopes alone, and the 181.8 cm−1 mode labelled "A2g" (p. 14) is not Raman active in either group. Several modes evolve continuously through p_c with only a slope change (Ph5→Ph8, Ph4→Ph7), which fits a single phase stiffening rather than a superposition of two phases. Spectra are described as polarized, but no polarization analysis is shown. The Raman p_c (~10 GPa) disagrees with the XRD value (6.4 to 8.4 GPa) on the same type of sample at the same temperature, and the Conclusions quote both. The loss of intensity above about 11 GPa is attributed to "gradual metallization", although the authors' own DFT gap at 10 GPa is still 1.39 eV and the link to metallization above 20 GPa (p. 15) is unjustified; with 514.5 nm (2.41 eV) excitation and a gap of 1.4 to 1.7 eV, changes in resonance and absorption are a more immediate explanation. The authors should give the factor-group mode count for each candidate structure (R-3, P-3m1, and P-31m or an ordered A-A supercell), use parallel and crossed polarization to assign symmetries, fit peak positions and widths with uncertainties instead of relying on guide lines on a colour map, compare as-grown and cut crystals at ambient pressure to test whether the "hard" modes scale with the XRD P-3m1 fraction, explain the Raman/XRD p_c difference, and either support metallization (for example by absorption or resistance) or remove it.
