Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors parametrically pump a 2.1 µm out-of-plane magnetized YIG film at 7.8 GHz through a microstrip antenna. They detect the signal near the bottom of the spectrum (about 3.3 GHz, k ≈ 0) with an IQ mixer locked to the same 10 MHz reference, so each shot is recorded in the time domain with its phase. From 1000 single-shot traces they report two things. First, a single-shot "coherence value" (a normalized integral of the 100 ns windowed autocorrelation) rises after pump-off. Second, the phase at fixed times relative to the local oscillator is uniformly distributed across shots. They interpret both as the spontaneous, pump-independent emergence of a random condensate phase. If substantiated, a phase-resolved, single-shot measurement of a magnon condensate relative to an external reference would be a useful new tool. However, the central inference (a uniform phase histogram at a fixed delay implies a spontaneously chosen condensate phase) needs controls the manuscript does not yet contain. The novelty relative to earlier observations of spontaneous coherence in magnon BEC (ref. 35) also needs to be stated more carefully.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The detection scheme is technically attractive. A phase-locked chain (pump, LO and pulse generator share a 10 MHz reference) and an IQ mixer give the amplitude and phase of the k ≈ 0 precession in single shots, with no averaging over repetitions (Fig. 2, Extended Data Fig. 6b).
- The single-shot traces in Fig. 2 are compelling raw data. Before condensation the phase is erratic; after pump-off it advances linearly at the expected intermediate frequency (about 130 MHz, consistent with 3.33 GHz minus the 3.2 GHz LO).
- The IQ-derived spectrogram is cross-checked against an independent spectrum-analyser spectrogram (Extended Data Fig. 6a vs 6c). The time–frequency evolution (relaxation from about 3.42 GHz to 3.33 GHz after pump-off) agrees.
- The power series (Fig. 4) shows a threshold-like onset of both amplitude and coherence between 21 and 22 dBm.
- The out-of-plane (forward-volume) geometry gives condensation at k ≈ 0, which couples efficiently to the antenna. This is what makes the direct inductive phase readout possible.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. A uniform phase histogram at a fixed delay does not show that the initial phase is random. The phase histograms in Fig. 2a (insets at t = 0.10, 0.90 and 1.25 µs) are taken at fixed times after the pump trigger. The text (pp. 5–6) and the Discussion infer from them that "the initial phase state of the condensate must be independent of the external pumping". But the measured phase at t = 1.25 µs is φ0 plus the integral of 2π[f(t′) − f_LO] dt′ from the moment of formation. The condensate frequency is strongly time- and density-dependent: it chirps from about 3.42 to 3.33 GHz after pump-off (Fig. 3a, Ext. Data Fig. 6a,c). The amplitude, and hence the magnon density, varies shot to shot by a factor of 2–3 (compare the three traces in Fig. 2a at 1.25 µs). A shot-to-shot frequency jitter of only a few MHz accumulated over the 0.3–1 µs between formation and the histogram time adds several radians of phase. That alone would wash out the histogram even if φ0 were deterministically set by the pump. Uniformity is therefore expected whether or not the phase is chosen spontaneously, and the histogram at 0.10 µs, where only noise is present, is trivially uniform. The authors should:
   - (a) determine the phase as close as possible to the onset of condensation in each shot (for example at the first time the amplitude crosses a threshold), or reference it to a phase-tracked fit of each shot's frequency evolution, rather than to a fixed laboratory time;
   - (b) show the shot-to-shot spread of the instantaneous frequency and of the accumulated phase ∫Δf dt, to estimate how much randomization the chirp alone produces;
   - (c) condition the histogram on amplitude, or show phase against amplitude, to test for hidden correlations with the pump;
   - (d) most importantly, measure a deterministic control under the same chirp conditions. One option is a pulsed direct (linear or weakly nonlinear) excitation at the condensate frequency with a nonlinear frequency shift comparable to the BEC's. Another is the parametrically pumped magnons at f_p/2 = 3.9 GHz, whose phase should be locked to the pump modulo π and give a bimodal histogram; this needs the LO retuned, since the mixer bandwidth is 500 MHz. Either control would show whether the analysis can detect a non-random phase when one is present.

2. The phase stability of the detection chain is asserted but not shown. The argument that "all externally induced factors are stable within a few degrees of phase shift" (pp. 5–6, Supplement 7.2) is the main basis for attributing the phase randomness to the condensate. Yet the caption of Extended Data Fig. 5 says "the oscilloscope was excluded due to technical difficulties, but phase stability of the setup as a whole was confirmed in preliminary measurements". No such data appear. The relevant risks are:
   - trigger jitter of the pulse generator and the oscilloscope time base relative to the 3.2 GHz LO and the 7.8 GHz pump;
   - the start phase of the pulsed SG2 relative to the CW LO;
   - drift over the acquisition of 1000 shots.
   The paper already contains a usable reference: Extended Data Fig. 6c shows a constant spurious tone at 3.77 GHz in the IQ data. Its single-shot phase histogram at the same delays, or that of a deliberately injected reference tone derived from SG2 or SG1, should be shown and should be narrow. Without this control, a uniform histogram cannot be distinguished from a timing or synchronization artifact.

3. Random phase and narrow linewidth are not unique to a thermodynamic BEC, and the BEC identification itself is not evidenced here. Any self-oscillation that builds up from noise breaks the U(1) phase symmetry spontaneously and acquires a random phase. Examples are a laser above threshold, a classical-wave (Rayleigh–Jeans) condensation, or a kinetic instability that transfers magnons directly to the bottom of the spectrum. The abstract nonetheless says the results "confirm all postulated fundamental properties of quasiparticle condensates", and the Discussion says the independence of the phase from external sources proves "the formation of a true BEC". The manuscript gives no evidence that the magnon gas thermalizes, for example:
   - a frequency-resolved occupation compatible with a Bose–Einstein distribution;
   - a chemical potential reaching the band bottom;
   - a critical density compared with Eq. (1) for this film's dispersion.
   The spectrum-analyser data are said only to "demonstrate the agreement with previous works [41]". Refs. 30–31 also indicate that the interpretation of condensation in perpendicularly magnetized YIG is debated. The authors should either supply evidence of thermalization (and quantify the threshold against Eq. (1)), or reframe the claim as spontaneous phase selection of the k ≈ 0 coherent state and discuss explicitly which observations would differ between BEC and kinetic-instability or laser-like scenarios.

B. Should address

4. The coherence metric is not calibrated, and the text contradicts the figures. Section 1.3 and the caption of Fig. 3 state that "zero denotes perfect white noise", but the pre-pump baseline in Figs. 3b, 4a and Ext. Data Fig. 6d is about 0.17–0.18. The maximum reached is about 0.75–0.8, never 1. Because the metric is a normalized integral of |autocorrelation| in a 100 ns window, it depends on the signal-to-noise ratio, on frequency chirp within the window, and on the 100 ns window, which limits frequency resolution to about 10 MHz. The text attributes the late-time decay of coherence to falling SNR (p. 7). Yet the caption of Fig. 4 claims that coherence "does not depend on the absolute amplitude" and that the BEC "doesn't lose its coherent properties during decay". The p. 8 statement that coherence remains "clearly detectable" at 3.5 µs, when the amplitude is below noise, rests on a residual of about 0.22 vs 0.18. The authors should:
   - compute the coherence value of synthetic signals (a pure tone, and a tone with the measured chirp) plus measured noise as a function of SNR, giving a calibration curve;
   - compare the data with that curve, so that genuine linewidth broadening or dephasing can be separated from SNR effects;
   - give the exact normalization formula (integration range of Δt, treatment of the boxcar envelope) and how the standard-deviation bands were computed.

5. Within-shot dephasing and phase diffusion are not measured, so the claim of no dephasing is not supported. The claim that "the BEC does not undergo dephasing" (pp. 7–8) is based on a 100 ns sliding-window metric, which cannot test phase stability over microseconds. The single-shot data allow a direct test. After removing each shot's deterministic frequency evolution (for example a fitted f(t)), compute the phase-difference variance ⟨[φ(t+τ) − φ(t)]²⟩ as a function of τ up to the 1–2 µs condensate lifetime, or equivalently the single-shot linewidth against time. This would turn a qualitative statement into a measured coherence time, and it would also bear on concern 1.

6. The condensation threshold is not separated from the parametric instability threshold. Fig. 4 shows no signal at 20 dBm, a weak one at 21 dBm and condensation at 22 dBm, and the caption calls 21 dBm "a condensation threshold". If the parametric instability threshold itself lies near 20–21 dBm, then Fig. 4 shows the onset of pumping, not a density-driven condensation threshold. The authors should:
   - show the population of parametric magnons at f_p/2 and of the thermalized gas (spectrum analyser or BLS) at 20–25 dBm;
   - state the parametric threshold;
   - state where the quoted power is referenced (generator output, after Amp 1 with +50 dB, or at the antenna, given the stub tuner and circulator in Ext. Data Fig. 5).

7. The phase statistics are presented only qualitatively. The uniformity in Fig. 2 rests on three polar histograms "with no apparent clustering". For 1000 shots the authors should report a quantitative test (Rayleigh or Kuiper test, mean resultant length |⟨e^{iφ}⟩| with its 1/√N noise floor) as a continuous function of time across pumping, condensation and free evolution. This is especially needed during pumping (0–1 µs), when pump-induced phase locking would be most likely to appear. A time trace of |⟨e^{iφ}⟩| would also expose any brief locking near pump-off that three snapshots could miss.

8. The scope is limited to one sample, field and pump configuration. All data come from one YIG film at 281 mT, one pump frequency (7.8 GHz) and one pulse length (1 µs). The generality of the claim would be strengthened by:
   - a second field or pump frequency;
   - a different pump duration, for example a shorter pulse so that condensation occurs entirely after pump-off, which would separate the pump's influence on the phase more cleanly;
   - repeated datasets on different days, showing the result is reproducible.

9. The physical interpretation of the frequency drift and of the coherence dip near pump-off is incomplete. On p. 7 the downward frequency shift and the dip in coherence near 1 µs are attributed to a nonlinear shift from magnon–magnon interactions, citing ref. 39, which concerns Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction formalism. The caption of Fig. 3, by contrast, attributes the drift to recovery of the net magnetization. Microwave heating at 23–25 dBm, which reduces M_s, and its subsequent relaxation could contribute as well. For the k = 0 forward-volume mode, f ≈ γ(B − μ0M_s), so the expected size of the shift is easy to estimate. The authors should estimate the shift from the magnon density and from heating and compare with the observed ~90 MHz. This also matters for concern 1, because the shift is what drives shot-to-shot phase accumulation.

C. Minor

10. Eq. (2) reads φ(t) = arctan(I + iQ). This should be φ = arg(I + iQ) = atan2(Q, I).

11. The text (p. 5) says the insets show histograms "at t0" of 1000 measurements, while Fig. 1b uses t0 for pump start and the insets are at 0.10, 0.90 and 1.25 µs. Please harmonize the notation. The radial axis ("percentage of measurements in this cluster") and the bin width should also be given.

12. In Fig. 2b the lower y-axis tick appears as "π/2" rather than "−π/2". In Fig. 3a the "Pump on" label overlaps the first green band and is hard to read.

13. Extended Data Fig. 6c shows a band at 3.77 GHz and a "shadow" at 3.2–3.3 GHz, attributed to IQ artifacts. Please state their origin (LO leakage, image from IQ amplitude or phase imbalance, a spur) and whether IQ imbalance was calibrated. Uncorrected imbalance distorts φ(t) within each IF cycle.

14. Sample parameters needed to reproduce the work are missing: substrate, saturation magnetization, FMR linewidth and magnon lifetime, the antenna–film geometry beyond the 100 µm width, and the repetition rate (needed to confirm that the system relaxes fully between shots).

15. The component list in Ext. Data Fig. 5 gives the switch as "5138DU1", while the Methods give "S138BDU1". The Methods also call the oscilloscope "Teledyne LeCroy HDO6054" while the figure says "LECROY HDO6054"; please make these consistent.

16. Data and code are "available at reasonable request". Given that the conclusion rests on a custom coherence metric and phase statistics, depositing the 1000-shot datasets and analysis scripts would materially help reproducibility.

17. Several statements are stronger than the evidence warrants: "for the first time", "confirm all postulated fundamental properties", and "the coherent state phase ... is equivalent to the quantum mechanical phase". Ref. 35 (Demidov et al., PRL 100, 047205) already reported spontaneous coherence of a magnon BEC via its microwave emission. The introduction should state precisely what this work adds: single-shot phase relative to an external reference, and the statistics of that phase across shots.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Build the phase-randomness argument around a deterministic control measured through the identical chain and analysis. For example, use the 3.77 GHz spur or an injected reference tone, and a directly excited or f_p/2 parametric magnon signal. Show side by side that the control gives a narrow (or bimodal) histogram and the condensate a uniform one.
2. Replace fixed-delay phase histograms with the phase extracted at the onset of condensation in each shot. Show the shot-to-shot distribution of accumulated chirp phase, so readers can see that the randomness is in φ0 and not in ∫Δf dt.
3. Calibrate the coherence value against SNR and chirp with synthetic signals, and add a single-shot phase-diffusion or coherence-time analysis over the full free-evolution window.
4. Add spectrally resolved evidence of thermalization, or soften the BEC-specific language. Discuss explicitly how BEC, kinetic instability and laser-like self-oscillation would differ in the observables measured here.
