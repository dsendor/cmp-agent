Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors parametrically pump a 2.1 µm YIG film, magnetized out of plane, at 7.8 GHz through a microstrip antenna. With the same antenna and a phase-locked IQ mixer (3.2 GHz local oscillator) they record single-shot, time-domain I/Q traces of the magnon signal near the bottom of the spectrum (~3.33 GHz). They report three things. In each shot the condensate shows a linear phase evolution. Across 1000 shots the phase relative to the external reference is uniformly distributed (Fig. 2 insets). An autocorrelation-based "coherence value" rises above a pump-power threshold (~21 dBm) and stays high while the amplitude decays (Figs. 3, 4). Measuring the absolute phase of a magnon condensate shot by shot against an external reference would be a useful addition to the magnon-BEC toolbox. Showing that this phase is not set by the pump would address a long-standing question about whether the coherence is pump-induced. As presented, however, the uniform phase histogram is the paper's only evidence for spontaneity. That evidence is not yet separated from simpler explanations: an unverified reference-phase chain, and shot-to-shot frequency jitter. The broader claims ("for the first time", "confirm all postulated fundamental properties") go beyond what is shown.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A simple, all-electronic, single-shot detection scheme (Ext. Data Fig. 5). It gives amplitude and phase of the k ≈ 0 magnon signal with ~ns sampling and no averaging over shots. This is a real advance over Brillouin light scattering, which integrates over many shots and gives no phase.
- The single-shot phase traces (Fig. 2b) are clear: phase is erratic during early pumping and evolves linearly after pump-off.
- The question asked is the right one: is the condensate phase inherited from the pump or chosen spontaneously? An experiment that answers it cleanly would interest the magnonics and the wider BEC community.
- Spectrum-analyser and IQ-derived spectrograms are cross-checked (Ext. Data Fig. 6). The pump-power series (Fig. 4) shows a threshold-like onset.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. No positive control shows that the reference chain can detect a pump-locked phase. Main text and Extended Data contradict each other on phase locking.
   - Where: Section 1.2 (p. 5–6) states that "all generation and detection equipment in the experimental setup is phase-locked" and that external factors are "stable within a few degrees of phase shift". The caption of Ext. Data Fig. 5 says the oscilloscope "was excluded [from the shared 10 MHz reference] due to technical difficulties, but phase stability of the setup as a whole was confirmed in preliminary measurements." Those preliminary measurements are not shown.
   - Why it matters: the central inference is "uniform histogram → phase not set externally". That inference holds only if a signal whose phase *is* set by the drive would give a narrow histogram under identical acquisition: same trigger chain, same oscilloscope, same analysis, same time after the trigger. Without that control, a uniform histogram could equally come from jitter in the pump-pulse start relative to the LO, oscilloscope trigger and time-base jitter, or an unlocked instrument.
   - What to do: show the phase histogram for at least one deterministic reference signal recorded with the same chain. Options:
     - A weak, phase-locked pulse at ~3.33 GHz from a synthesizer locked to the same 10 MHz, sent through the antenna or a bypass, and triggered by the same pulse generator.
     - The constant 3.77 GHz band visible in Ext. Data Fig. 6c, if it originates from a locked source.
     - The linear FMR response at 3.3 GHz driven directly below the parametric threshold.

     Report the circular standard deviation of that histogram next to the condensate histogram. Resolve the contradiction between the main text and the Ext. Data Fig. 5 caption, and state the trigger jitter and the pump–LO phase relation at pulse onset.

2. Shot-to-shot frequency jitter could randomize the phase measured at a fixed lab time, even if the condensate were seeded deterministically.
   - Where: Fig. 2 insets, Section 1.2, Section 7.2. The phase histogram is evaluated at a fixed time after the trigger (t = 1.25 µs for the condensate). Fig. 3a shows that the bottom-of-band frequency drifts by ~80–100 MHz between ~0.8 and ~1.3 µs as the magnon density falls (nonlinear shift).
   - Why it matters: the measured phase is the initial phase plus the integral of (ω_BEC − ω_LO) over time.
     - The nonlinear shift depends on magnon density, and density fluctuates from shot to shot. A spread of only ~1 MHz in the frequency trajectory over the ~0.5 µs between condensate formation and the evaluation time gives a phase spread of ~3 rad, which fills the circle.
     - A uniform histogram at t = 1.25 µs therefore cannot by itself distinguish a random initial phase from a deterministic initial phase plus accumulated frequency noise.
     - Similarly, the pump fixes only the sum of the phases of each ±k magnon pair. What phase information could pass to the k ≈ 0 state through four-magnon scattering is a theoretical question. The paper does not discuss it.
   - What to do: analyse the phase where it is set, not far downstream.
     - Fit each shot's phase trajectory in the free-evolution window (frequency and chirp as free parameters). Extrapolate back to the onset of coherence. Show the histogram of these extrapolated phases and how its width compares with the fit uncertainty.
     - Show the histograms as a continuous function of time from the first rise of the coherence value (~0.6–0.7 µs at 23 dBm), together with the mean resultant length R(t). For N = 1000 shots, R ≈ 0.03 is expected for a uniform distribution.
     - Show the shot-to-shot spread of the fitted condensate frequency. Compare the phase spread it implies with the observed one.
     - Add a short theoretical estimate of which pump-phase memory, if any, is expected to survive to the k ≈ 0 mode, including the two-fold (φ_p/2, φ_p/2 + π) degeneracy of parametric pairs. Then say what histogram a pump-locked scenario would predict (bimodal, or peaked).

3. A random phase from shot to shot is not unique to a BEC, yet the paper treats it as proof of a "true BEC". The thermodynamic evidence for condensation in this geometry is not shown.
   - Where: Abstract ("confirm all postulated fundamental properties"), Fig. 2 caption ("strong indication … result of true BEC"), Discussion ("necessary feature to prove the formation of a true BEC").
   - Why it matters: any instability that grows out of thermal or quantum noise also picks a random phase in each realization. Examples are a laser or parametric oscillator above threshold, a kinetic instability of the magnon gas, and Suhl-type spin-wave instabilities. Random phase is therefore necessary but not sufficient.
   - The paper itself contains no data showing thermalization: no energy distribution of the gas near the band bottom, no chemical potential or density estimate, and no comparison with a Bose–Einstein distribution. The authors cite Ref. 31, a comment disputing BEC claims in perpendicularly magnetized YIG, but do not engage with it. The out-of-plane geometry here is different from the in-plane geometry of most previous magnon-BEC work, so this matters.
   - What to do:
     - Either supply evidence for thermalized condensation in this sample and geometry: for example, time-resolved spectra of the gas near the band bottom across threshold, or an estimate of the critical density compared with the injected density.
     - Or rely explicitly on, and quantitatively match, the conditions of Ref. 41 (same film, field, pump). Discuss Ref. 31.
     - Reframe the phase randomness as consistent with spontaneous symmetry breaking rather than as proof of BEC. Discuss how a kinetic-instability or other noise-seeded scenario would differ.

B. Should address

4. The "coherence value" is not separated from signal-to-noise ratio, yet it supports the claim that "the BEC does not undergo dephasing".
   - Where: Section 1.3, Section 3.2, Figs. 3b and 4a, and p. 7–8 (the claim that coherence persists at t = 3.5 µs after the amplitude has fallen below the noise threshold).
   - The problems:
     - The metric is the integral of |autocorrelation| of the "normalized complex signal" in a 100 ns window. It is lowered by additive noise, by frequency chirp within the window (the authors invoke this for the dip near 0.9 µs), and by phase diffusion, and it cannot tell these apart.
     - The noise floor sits at ~0.17, not 0 as the text states. At 3.5 µs the value (~0.22) is only slightly above that floor.
     - It is unclear how "normalized" is defined: per window, or globally? Whether DC offsets, LO leakage and the 3.2–3.3 GHz "shadow" and 3.77 GHz band artifacts (Ext. Data Fig. 6c) are removed before the computation is also unstated. A constant tone or DC offset in I/Q raises the metric without any magnon coherence.
   - What to do:
     - Calibrate the metric. Compute it for a synthetic pure tone (with the measured chirp) plus the measured noise, over the range of measured amplitudes. Plot the expected coherence value against SNR, then place the data on that curve. If the late-time decay is fully explained by falling SNR, the no-dephasing statement is supported; if not, it is not.
     - Independently, report the single-shot phase residuals from a linear (or chirped) fit, or the linewidth from the single-shot spectra, as a function of time.
     - State which filtering is applied before the phase and coherence analyses.
     - Correct the claim that coherence is "clearly detectable" when the amplitude is "below the noise threshold". Averaging 1000 amplitude traces also detects the signal, so the comparison should be made at equal effective averaging.

5. One sample, one field and one pump configuration; the threshold is weakly defined.
   - Where: Methods 3.1 (2.1 µm film, 281 mT, 7.8 GHz, 1 µs pulse) and Fig. 4.
   - Why it matters:
     - The claimed spontaneity and the threshold at 21 dBm are shown for a single condition in a single sample.
     - At 21 dBm the amplitude and coherence changes in Fig. 4 are barely visible, so a "threshold of 21 dBm" rests on very little.
     - The coherence saturates at ~0.75 from 23 to 25 dBm. Whether this ceiling is set by noise, by chirp or by the condensate itself is unclear.
   - What to do:
     - Show peak condensate amplitude and late-time coherence against pump power on a finer grid, and define the threshold from a fit.
     - Repeat the phase-histogram result at a second field or pump-pulse duration, ideally in a second film or after remounting, so that it does not depend on one configuration.
     - State the pumping threshold for parametric instability itself, to separate it from the condensation threshold.

6. The novelty is overstated relative to prior work on spontaneous coherence in magnon BECs.
   - Where: Abstract ("Until now, the BEC phase was only revealed in phenomena depending on the spatial phase difference"), Main text p. 2, Discussion ("For the first time…", "no direct proof of randomness … has yet been provided").
   - Why it matters: Ref. 35 (Demidov et al., PRL 2008) is titled "Observation of spontaneous coherence in BEC of magnons". In the paper it is cited only for in-plane geometry. Snoke & Girvin (Ref. 24) and Rezende (Ref. 10) address the onset of phase coherence.
   - What to do: state precisely what is new: single-shot absolute phase against an external reference, and the shot-to-shot phase distribution. Explain how this differs from, and goes beyond, linewidth- and spectral-narrowing evidence of spontaneous coherence. Temper "for the first time" and "confirm all postulated fundamental properties" accordingly.

7. The detection is selective in wavevector and frequency, and this is not accounted for in the coherence rise.
   - Where: Section 1.2 ("detection path is not sensitive to the parametrically introduced magnons, but only to the thermal gas near the bottom"), Section 7.2 (500 MHz IQ bandwidth), and Ext. Data Fig. 5 (100 µm microstrip).
   - Why it matters: the antenna couples efficiently only to small wavevectors (k ≲ 2π/100 µm), and the filters plus IQ bandwidth pass only ~3.2–3.7 GHz. The rise in the coherence value may therefore partly reflect magnon population moving into the narrow detected window, where any single mode looks more monochromatic. It need not be a growth of coherence within a fixed population.
   - What to do:
     - Give the antenna's k-space and frequency transfer function.
     - Compute the dispersion at 281 mT with the film parameters (Ms, anisotropy), and compare the predicted k = 0 frequency with the observed 3.33 GHz.
     - Discuss whether the detected mode is the film's uniform precession or a mode confined under or near the antenna.
     - Give the sample's lateral size, substrate, Ms and damping.

8. The phase histograms are not quantified, and the below-threshold comparison is missing.
   - Where: Fig. 2 insets (no radial scale, no statistic; "at t0" in the text versus "at the beginning of each frame" in the caption).
   - Why it matters: the t = 0.10 µs inset contains only noise, so its uniformity is trivial and is not a control. The informative comparisons are:
     - the condensate at several times;
     - a below-threshold power (20 dBm) at the same times;
     - the deterministic reference from concern 1.
   - What to do: give the mean resultant length and a Rayleigh or Kuiper test p-value for each histogram. Add a radial scale. Show the histograms at matched times for the different powers in Fig. 4.

C. Minor

9. Eq. (2) reads φ(t) = arctan(I + iQ). It should be arg(I + iQ), i.e. atan2(Q, I).

10. Section 1.3 states that the coherence value "has the same time resolution as the original signal". With a 100 ns sliding window, the effective resolution is ~100 ns. Please correct this.

11. "0 denotes perfect white noise" (Section 1.3, Fig. 3 caption) is inconsistent with the ~0.17 baseline in Figs. 3b and 4a. Explain the finite-window noise floor.

12. The text alternates between "condensation occurs after the pump is switched off" (p. 5) and Fig. 1b/Fig. 3, where coherence and the band-bottom signal appear from ~0.7 µs, during pumping. Please make the description of when condensation starts consistent.

13. Fig. 3a: the "Pump on"/"Pump off" labels overlap the green time-slice markers and are hard to read. The dotted "parametrically pumped magnons" line at ~3.9 GHz is outside the IQ band. Please clarify whether it is detected or only indicated.

14. Fig. 4b: the amplitude at 25 dBm peaks during pumping (~0.6–0.9 µs), while the coherence at 25 dBm is similar to that at 23 dBm. Please comment on how the 25 dBm condensate forms during pumping, compared with 23 dBm.

15. Section 7.2 says the IQ downshift "has no influence on either the phase coherence or the phase randomness". Please justify this with the LO phase-noise specification and the IF frequency.

16. Data availability "at reasonable request" falls short of current journal standards for the central dataset. Please deposit the raw I/Q traces used for Figs. 2–4.

17. Small textual issues: "inlays" → "insets"; consistent capitalization in the title; the external field of 281 mT should be distinguished from the internal field; "Thermalisation section" → "thermalization stage".

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Build the paper around a side-by-side comparison under identical acquisition: (a) a phase-locked reference tone, which should give a narrow histogram; (b) the below-threshold signal; (c) the condensate's back-extrapolated formation phase, which should give a uniform histogram. Report circular statistics for all three. This single figure would make the central claim much stronger.
2. Replace or supplement the autocorrelation "coherence value" with physically interpretable quantities: single-shot linewidth or phase-residual variance against time, and a first-order coherence g(1)(τ) computed from the complex traces, both calibrated against SNR with synthetic signals.
3. Add a minimal model, for example a stochastic Gross–Pitaevskii or kinetic simulation, or even a noise-seeded growth model. It should predict the phase distribution under pump-inherited versus spontaneous scenarios, so that the measured histogram discriminates between them.
4. Exploit the single-shot capability further. Correlations between shot-to-shot amplitude and frequency (and the phase they imply) would directly test the frequency-jitter explanation, and would themselves be a new observable of condensate fluctuations.
