Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors pump magnons parametrically in an out-of-plane magnetized 2.1 µm YIG film. They detect the k ≈ 0 precession at about 3.3 GHz on a single shot with an IQ mixer whose local oscillator is phase-locked to the pump. From this they report that (i) the precession phase is uniformly distributed over 1000 repetitions and (ii) an autocorrelation-based "coherence value" rises from noise to about 0.75 once the condensate forms. They present this as the first direct proof of spontaneous phase selection in a magnon BEC. A single-shot, phase-referenced electrical readout of a room-temperature magnon condensate would be a useful tool. Whether the data show *spontaneous* symmetry breaking, rather than a pump-set phase scrambled by shot-to-shot fluctuations, is not yet established. The novelty claim also needs to be placed against earlier phase-sensitive observations of magnon condensates and of other quasiparticle condensates.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The experiment asks a sharp and relevant question: does the condensate phase carry memory of the pump? The phase-locked heterodyne readout is a natural way to ask it.
- Single-shot I/Q traces (Fig. 2, Extended Data Fig. 6b) with 1000 repetitions per setting give real access to shot-to-shot statistics, which spectrum-analyser or BLS integration cannot provide.
- The spectrogram reconstructed from IQ data agrees with the spectrum-analyser spectrogram (Extended Data Fig. 6a,c). This supports the claim that the IQ channel sees the same condensate signal.
- The power series (Fig. 4) shows a threshold-like onset of both amplitude and coherence near 21–22 dBm.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **A uniform phase distribution at t = 0.90 µs and 1.25 µs does not show that the initial phase is random. Phase diffusion caused by shot-to-shot frequency fluctuations has not been excluded.** (Fig. 2 insets; Sec. 1.2, p. 5–6: "the uniform phase distribution must be an intrinsic property of the BEC")
   - The signal frequency chirps from about 3.42 GHz to 3.33 GHz after the pump is switched off. The text attributes this to the density-dependent nonlinear shift (Fig. 3a, p. 7). The phase measured at time t is therefore φ(t) = φ₀ + 2π∫ f(t′) dt′, and the integral depends on the magnon density trajectory of each shot.
   - Shot-to-shot jitter of a few MHz in the instantaneous frequency is enough to randomize φ over a few hundred nanoseconds, even if φ₀ were rigidly fixed by the pump. Over the roughly 300–600 ns between onset and the histogram times, a few MHz of jitter gives about 2π of spread. A frequency jitter of this size needs only about 3% density fluctuation, given the about 90 MHz total shift.
   - The same holds for jitter in the condensation onset time, which itself depends on pump power and initial thermal population.
   - A uniform histogram is therefore expected under both the spontaneous and the pump-locked hypotheses. As presented, it does not discriminate between them.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Show the circular statistics (mean resultant length or Rayleigh-test p-value over the 1000 shots) as a continuous function of time from the very first appearance of signal above noise (about 0.55–0.65 µs at 23 dBm and 25 dBm). Do this at times earlier than those where a few MHz of jitter could accumulate 2π.
     - Correct each shot for its own measured instantaneous frequency and onset time, and back-propagate the phase to a common reference point, for example the time the amplitude crosses a fixed threshold. Then test whether the resulting φ₀ distribution is uniform.
     - Run the decisive control: drive the k ≈ 0 mode coherently and directly, as a pulsed linear FMR excitation near 3.3–3.4 GHz at amplitudes large enough to produce a comparable nonlinear shift, or as a weak coherent seed at the band bottom during parametric pumping. Show that the same analysis then returns a narrow phase histogram at matched delays and amplitudes.
   - Without one of these, the central claim (spontaneous, pump-independent phase) does not follow.

2. **Shot-to-shot phase stability of the detection chain is asserted but not shown.** (Sec. 1.2, p. 5: "all externally induced factors are stable within a few degrees"; Extended Data Fig. 5 caption: "The oscilloscope was excluded due to technical difficulties, but phase stability of the setup as a whole was confirmed in preliminary measurements")
   - The oscilloscope is not on the shared 10 MHz reference. The phase at a fixed time t therefore depends on trigger and time-base jitter relative to the 100–130 MHz IF: 1 ns of jitter corresponds to about 0.7 rad at 110 MHz IF.
   - The relation between the 7.8 GHz pump-pulse onset, the DG645 trigger and the 3.2 GHz LO is also not described.
   - The "few degrees" figure is the quantitative basis for attributing the uniform histogram to the condensate, yet no data support it.
   - The authors should show, under the measurement conditions, a reference whose phase should be fixed: for example a pulsed 3.3 GHz tone from a locked generator, triggered by the same pulse generator, sent through the diplexer, amplifier and mixer chain and recorded on the oscilloscope. Its phase histogram over 1000 shots and its circular width should be reported.
   - The constant spur at 3.77 GHz visible in Extended Data Fig. 6c might serve as an in-situ reference within the same shots, if it is derived from a locked source.

3. **Spontaneous phase selection plus temporal coherence is necessary for BEC but not sufficient. The paper offers no evidence of thermalization or of the condensate's position at the true band bottom, and the out-of-plane case is disputed.** (Abstract: "These findings confirm all postulated fundamental properties of quasiparticle condensates"; Discussion, p. 8)
   - Other processes also produce a monochromatic precession with a random phase, seeded by thermal noise: auto-oscillation or kinetic instability of the secondary magnon population, four-magnon parametric processes into the k ≈ 0 mode, or any self-oscillation above threshold.
   - What would separate BEC from these is evidence that the magnon gas thermalizes to a quasi-equilibrium distribution with μ reaching the band bottom, and that the coherent peak sits at the minimum of the calculated spectrum for these parameters.
   - The paper shows only the IQ band (500 MHz bandwidth) and a spectrum-analyser spectrogram restricted to 3.2–3.9 GHz. It gives no dispersion calculation, no frequency-resolved occupation and no comparison with a Bose–Einstein distribution.
   - The BEC interpretation for perpendicularly magnetized YIG films has been explicitly challenged in the literature the authors cite ([31]). The manuscript does not engage with that critique.
   - The authors should:
     - give the calculated FVSW dispersion and band-bottom frequency for 281 mT, using the film's measured M_s and anisotropy;
     - show the frequency-resolved magnon population (spectrum analyser or BLS) before, during and after condensation, and how it compares with a thermal distribution;
     - discuss [31] directly;
     - otherwise, temper the abstract and discussion claims ("confirm all postulated fundamental properties", "proof of randomness", "true BEC").

**B. Should address**

4. **The "coherence value" is not calibrated, is SNR-dependent, and is limited by its 100 ns window. The claims "no dephasing" and "remanence of coherence" therefore do not follow.** (Sec. 1.3, Sec. 3.2, Fig. 3b,c, Fig. 4a, p. 7–8)
   - The metric is stated to be 0 for white noise, but the measured noise baseline is about 0.17–0.2 (Fig. 3b, Fig. 4a). The text says the late-time decrease in coherence is caused by falling SNR. Yet the Fig. 4 caption says coherence "does not depend on the absolute amplitude", and p. 7–8 says the BEC "does not undergo dephasing". With an SNR-dependent metric these statements cannot be separated from dephasing.
   - A 100 ns boxcar resolves only about 10 MHz. Any spectral feature narrower than that, including a narrowband incoherent population near the band bottom, scores close to perfect coherence.
   - It is not stated how the complex signal is "normalized" (per point, or per window), or whether the reference f(t) = e^{iω₀t} uses the measured or a fixed ω₀. A frequency chirp within the window also lowers the score, which may explain the dip at about 1.0 µs.
   - The authors should:
     - calibrate the metric with simulated or measured signals of known linewidth and SNR, for example a locked tone attenuated to the same amplitudes;
     - report an SNR-corrected first-order coherence g¹(τ), or the condensate linewidth from longer windows during free evolution, compared with the Fourier and decay limits;
     - quantify the "clear remanence" at 3.5 µs (Fig. 4a: about 0.22 versus a baseline of about 0.17) with a significance estimate, and show that it is not caused by the 3.77 GHz spur or by mixer offsets.

5. **The onset of coherence is not compared with the parametric instability threshold.** (Fig. 4; p. 7, caption: threshold "21 dBm")
   - If coherence appears at the same power at which parametric magnons first appear, the result points to a direct instability-driven process rather than to condensation beyond a critical density.
   - The setup contains a microwave detector on the reflected path (Extended Data Fig. 5), so the parametric threshold should be available.
   - The authors should:
     - report it;
     - add finer power steps between 20 and 23 dBm;
     - plot peak coherence and the condensate amplitude against pump power relative to that threshold.
   - The statement that increasing power above 23 dBm raises amplitude but not coherence should be shown quantitatively.

6. **The timing of condensation is described inconsistently, and this matters for the phase argument.** (p. 5: "After the pump is switched off, condensation occurs"; Fig. 1b; Fig. 3b and Fig. 4a)
   - Fig. 3b and Fig. 4a show coherence rising to about 0.5 at 0.6–0.85 µs, while the pump is still on (25 dBm and 23 dBm). This is also where any pump-imprinted phase would be most visible.
   - The authors should state clearly when the condensate forms, explain the coherence maximum at about 0.85 µs and the dip at about 1.0 µs, and present the phase histograms at the earliest coherent time (see concern 1), not only at 0.90 µs and 1.25 µs.

7. **The novelty claim does not account for prior phase-sensitive work.** (Abstract and Discussion: "For the first time…", "no direct proof of randomness … has yet been provided")
   - In superfluid ³He-B, the homogeneously precessing domain and magnon-BEC "time crystal" states are detected inductively, with phase relative to a reference (the authors cite [19]).
   - Demidov et al. ([35]) already reported spontaneous coherence of a magnon BEC.
   - Earlier microwave-emission measurements of magnon condensates exist. Phase-resolved studies of photon and polariton condensates have addressed spontaneous symmetry breaking and phase randomness over repetitions.
   - The authors' own Ref. [41] treats the same out-of-plane geometry "toward a coherent BEC".
   - The authors should state precisely what is new relative to these works: single-shot phase referenced to the pump, in a solid-state magnon system. They should also revise the "first" and "proof" language accordingly.

8. **The result rests on one sample, one field (281 mT), one pump frequency (7.8 GHz), one pulse length (1 µs) and one antenna. The repetition rate is not given.**
   - Residual magnons or heating from the previous pulse could seed or bias the phase. Correlations between consecutive shots should be checked, and the repetition period and the number of independent data sets stated.
   - Showing phase randomness and the coherence onset at a second field or pump frequency, or on a second film, would rule out a configuration-specific artifact.
   - The pump powers (20–25 dBm) should be specified as the power at the antenna, given the +50 dB amplifier and attenuators in Extended Data Fig. 5.
   - Microwave heating of the 2.1 µm film should be estimated, since it shifts M_s and hence the band bottom.

9. **IQ-mixer imperfections that bias phase extraction are not characterized.** (Extended Data Fig. 6c: "constant band at 3.77 GHz and a shadow between 3.2 GHz and 3.3 GHz")
   - I/Q amplitude and phase imbalance and DC offsets distort the extracted phase and can produce or hide non-uniformity in phase histograms. The "shadow" may be an image response of the condensate line.
   - The authors should report the mixer calibration (imbalance, offsets), state whether it was corrected before computing φ, and identify the origin of both artifacts.

**C. Minor**

10. Eq. (2) should read φ(t) = arg(I + iQ) = atan2(Q, I). arctan of a complex number is not the intended quantity.
11. The Fig. 2 insets are said to be at "t₀" (text, p. 5) and "at the beginning of each frame" (caption). They are labelled 0.10, 0.90 and 1.25 µs, while t₀ in Fig. 1 denotes pump onset. Please make these consistent. Give the radial scale of the polar histograms ("percentage of measurements in this cluster") and the bin width.
12. Fig. 4b: the peak amplitude at 25 dBm appears lower than at 23 dBm near t ≈ 1 µs. This contradicts the caption's "a higher amplitude can be observed" above 23 dBm. Please check or explain.
13. Fig. 3a and Extended Data Fig. 6a: the rotated "Pump on" label is partly unreadable. Mark the parametric-magnon frequency (f_p/2 = 3.9 GHz) explicitly, and say whether it falls inside the F2 (< 4 GHz) passband.
14. Sample details are missing: substrate, lateral dimensions, M_s, damping and anisotropy of the YIG film, and the distance of the film from the microstrip.
15. "Full dataset and algorithms … available at reasonable request" (Sec. 7.1): please deposit the raw I/Q traces and the coherence-analysis code. The central claim rests on a statistical analysis that readers should be able to reproduce.
16. The "quantum–classical correspondence" argument (p. 2, p. 8) equates the classical precession phase with the condensate's quantum phase. A sentence on its limits would help, since random-phase onset is also generic for classical self-oscillators.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add the pump-locked reference control (concern 2) and a coherently driven k ≈ 0 control with a comparable nonlinear chirp (concern 1). Analyse both with the same phase-histogram pipeline. This is the most direct way to make the "spontaneous" claim convincing.
2. Replace the qualitative polar histograms with a time trace of the inter-shot mean resultant length |⟨e^{iφ}⟩| over 1000 shots, with its expected value for a uniform distribution (about 1/√N), starting from the first detectable signal.
3. Add a frequency-resolved population measurement and the calculated dispersion, to tie the coherent peak to the thermalized band bottom and to answer the critique in [31].
4. Recast the coherence metric as a calibrated, SNR-corrected g¹(τ) or linewidth, and restate the "no dephasing" conclusion on that basis.
