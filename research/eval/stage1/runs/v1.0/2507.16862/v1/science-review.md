**A. Must address**

1. **A uniform phase histogram at a fixed delay does not show that the initial phase is random. Phase diffusion from shot-to-shot frequency and onset-time jitter has not been excluded.** (Fig. 2a insets at t = 0.10, 0.90 and 1.25 µs; Sec. 1.2, pp. 5–6: "the uniform phase distribution must be an intrinsic property of the BEC"; Sec. 7.2; Discussion.) The histograms are taken at fixed laboratory times after the trigger, and the text infers from them that the initial phase is independent of the pump. But the measured phase is φ(t) = φ₀ + 2π∫[f(t′) − f_LO] dt′ from the moment of formation. The condensate frequency chirps by about 80–100 MHz, from about 3.42 to 3.33 GHz, after pump-off (Fig. 3a, Ext. Data Fig. 6a,c). The text attributes this to the density-dependent nonlinear shift. The amplitude, and hence the density, varies shot to shot by a factor of 2–3 (compare the three traces in Fig. 2a at 1.25 µs). A frequency jitter of only about 1 to a few MHz, which needs only about 3% density fluctuation given the ~90 MHz total shift, accumulates about 2π or more over the 0.3–1 µs between formation and the histogram times. Jitter in the condensation onset time, which depends on pump power and the initial thermal population, has the same effect. A uniform histogram is therefore expected whether φ₀ is chosen spontaneously or fixed by the pump. The 0.10 µs histogram contains only noise and is trivially uniform. The paper also does not discuss what pump-phase information could pass to the k ≈ 0 state through four-magnon scattering: the pump fixes only the sum of the phases of each ±k pair, with a two-fold (φ_p/2, φ_p/2 + π) degeneracy. The authors should: (a) extract the phase where it is set, either at the first time each shot's amplitude crosses a fixed threshold, or by fitting each shot's phase trajectory in the free-evolution window (frequency and chirp as free parameters) and back-extrapolating to the onset of coherence, then test whether this φ₀ distribution is uniform compared with the fit uncertainty; (b) show the shot-to-shot spread of the fitted instantaneous frequency and of the accumulated phase ∫Δf dt, and compare the phase spread it implies with the observed one; (c) condition the histogram on amplitude, or plot phase against amplitude, to test for hidden correlations with the pump; (d) most importantly, measure a deterministic control through the same chain and analysis under comparable chirp conditions. One option is a pulsed linear or weakly nonlinear FMR excitation of the k ≈ 0 mode near 3.3–3.4 GHz, at amplitudes large enough to give a comparable nonlinear shift, or a weak coherent seed at the band bottom during parametric pumping; this should return a narrow histogram at matched delays and amplitudes. Another is the parametric magnons at f_p/2 = 3.9 GHz, whose phase should be locked to the pump modulo π and give a bimodal histogram; this needs the LO retuned, since the mixer bandwidth is 500 MHz. (e) Add a short theoretical estimate, or a minimal noise-seeded growth, kinetic or stochastic Gross–Pitaevskii model, of which pump-phase memory is expected to survive to the k ≈ 0 mode and what histogram (bimodal or peaked) a pump-locked scenario predicts. Without such analysis and a control, the central claim of a spontaneous, pump-independent phase does not follow.

3. **Random phase selection plus temporal coherence is necessary for BEC but not sufficient, and the paper gives no evidence of thermalization or of condensation at the true band bottom in this disputed geometry.** (Abstract: "These findings confirm all postulated fundamental properties of quasiparticle condensates"; Fig. 2 caption: "strong indication … result of true BEC"; Discussion, p. 8: "necessary feature to prove the formation of a true BEC".) Any instability that grows out of thermal or quantum noise breaks U(1) symmetry and picks a random phase in each realization: a laser or parametric oscillator above threshold, classical-wave (Rayleigh–Jeans) condensation, a kinetic instability that transfers magnons directly to the bottom of the spectrum, Suhl-type instabilities, four-magnon processes into the k ≈ 0 mode, or any self-oscillation above threshold. What would distinguish BEC is a thermalized magnon gas with μ reaching the band bottom and a coherent peak at the minimum of the calculated spectrum. The paper shows only the 500 MHz IQ band and a spectrum-analyser spectrogram limited to 3.2–3.9 GHz, said only to "demonstrate the agreement with previous works [41]". It gives no dispersion calculation, no frequency-resolved occupation, no chemical potential or density estimate, no comparison with a Bose–Einstein distribution, and no critical density compared with Eq. (1). Refs. 30–31 show that BEC in perpendicularly magnetized YIG is debated (Ref. 31 is a comment disputing such claims), and the manuscript does not engage with that critique; this matters because the out-of-plane geometry differs from most earlier magnon-BEC work. The authors should either supply evidence of thermalized condensation in this sample and geometry: the calculated forward-volume dispersion and band-bottom frequency at 281 mT from the measured M_s and anisotropy, compared with the observed 3.33 GHz; time- and frequency-resolved population (spectrum analyser or BLS) before, during and after condensation compared with a thermal distribution; and the critical density against the injected density. Alternatively they should rely explicitly on, and quantitatively match, the conditions of Ref. 41 (same film, field, pump). In either case they should discuss Ref. 31 directly, say which observables would differ between BEC and kinetic-instability or laser-like scenarios, and otherwise reframe the result as spontaneous phase selection of the k ≈ 0 coherent state, tempering "confirm all postulated fundamental properties", "proof of randomness" and "true BEC".

6. **The "coherence value" is not calibrated, depends on SNR, chirp and its 100 ns window, and the text contradicts the figures.** (Sec. 1.3, Sec. 3.2, Fig. 3b,c, Fig. 4a, Ext. Data Fig. 6d, pp. 7–8.) The metric is stated to be 0 for white noise ("zero denotes perfect white noise"), but the measured baseline is about 0.17–0.2 and the maximum about 0.75–0.8, never 1. As an integral of |autocorrelation| of a normalized signal in a 100 ns window, it is lowered by additive noise, by frequency chirp within the window (which may explain the dip near 0.9–1.0 µs) and by phase diffusion, and cannot tell them apart. The 100 ns window resolves only about 10 MHz, so any narrower feature, including a narrowband incoherent population near the band bottom, scores close to perfect coherence. A constant tone or DC offset in I/Q raises the metric without any magnon coherence. The text attributes the late-time decrease to falling SNR, yet the Fig. 4 caption says coherence "does not depend on the absolute amplitude" and the BEC "doesn't lose its coherent properties during decay". The claim that coherence remains "clearly detectable" at 3.5 µs when the amplitude is "below the noise threshold" rests on about 0.22 against a 0.17–0.18 baseline, and averaging 1000 amplitude traces would also detect the signal. The authors should give the exact normalization (per point, per window or global; integration range of Δt; treatment of the boxcar envelope), whether the reference e^{iω₀t} uses a measured or fixed ω₀, and how the standard-deviation bands were computed; explain the finite-window noise floor; state which filtering (DC offsets, LO leakage, the 3.77 GHz band and 3.2–3.3 GHz shadow) is applied before the analysis; calibrate the metric with synthetic signals (pure tone and tone with the measured chirp, plus measured noise) or an attenuated locked tone over the measured SNR range and place the data on that curve; and quantify the "remanence" at 3.5 µs with a significance estimate, compared at equal effective averaging, showing it is not caused by the 3.77 GHz spur or mixer offsets.

7. **Within-shot dephasing and phase diffusion are not measured, so "the BEC does not undergo dephasing" is not supported.** (pp. 7–8.) A 100 ns sliding-window metric cannot test phase stability over microseconds, and the single-shot data allow a direct test. After removing each shot's deterministic frequency evolution (a fitted linear or chirped f(t)), the authors should compute the phase-residual variance ⟨[φ(t+τ) − φ(t)]²⟩ against τ up to the 1–2 µs condensate lifetime, an SNR-corrected first-order coherence g¹(τ), or the single-shot linewidth against time from longer windows during free evolution, compared with the Fourier and decay limits. This would replace a qualitative statement with a measured coherence time and also bears on concern 1.

9. **The novelty claim does not account for prior work on spontaneous coherence and phase-sensitive detection of condensates.** (Abstract: "Until now, the BEC phase was only revealed in phenomena depending on the spatial phase difference"; p. 2; Discussion: "For the first time…", "no direct proof of randomness … has yet been provided".) Ref. 35 (Demidov et al., PRL 100, 047205, 2008), "Observation of spontaneous coherence in BEC of magnons", reported spontaneous coherence via microwave emission, yet is cited only for the in-plane geometry. In superfluid ³He-B, the homogeneously precessing domain and magnon-BEC "time crystal" states are detected inductively with phase relative to a reference (Ref. 19). Snoke & Girvin (Ref. 24) and Rezende (Ref. 10) address the onset of phase coherence; the authors' own Ref. 41 treats the same out-of-plane geometry "toward a coherent BEC"; and phase-resolved studies of photon and polariton condensates have addressed spontaneous symmetry breaking and phase randomness over repetitions. The authors should state precisely what is new (single-shot phase referenced to the pump in a solid-state magnon system, and the shot-to-shot statistics of that phase), explain how this goes beyond linewidth and spectral-narrowing evidence, and revise "for the first time", "proof" and "confirm all postulated fundamental properties".
