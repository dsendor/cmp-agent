A. Must address

1. **A uniform phase histogram at a fixed lab time does not show that the phase is chosen spontaneously when the condensate forms; shot-to-shot jitter in onset time or frequency would give the same result.** The paper infers spontaneous phase formation from the uniform histogram at t = 1.25 µs (Fig. 2a insets, Fig. 2b, "Phase evolution of the BEC" section; p. 1433, "the uniform phase distribution must be an intrinsic property of the BEC"). The phase is read against a 3.2-GHz LO at a fixed time, 0.25 µs after pump-off and roughly 0.3–0.5 µs after the signal first appears. Over that interval the condensate frequency chirps by about 100 MHz, from roughly 3.42–3.45 GHz down to 3.32 GHz between about 0.9 and 1.4 µs (Fig. 3a; Extended Data Fig. 2a,c), through the nonlinear shift set by the magnon number. The phase at time t is therefore φ(t) = φ0 + 2π∫f(t′)dt′. A shot-to-shot spread of only δf ≈ 2–4 MHz in the frequency trajectory (from pump-power noise, magnon-number fluctuations, sample temperature) or an onset-time jitter of about 10 ns would spread φ(1.25 µs) over more than 2π, even if φ0 were fully deterministic and pump-locked. Such variation is plausible near threshold, given the large ensemble spread in amplitude in Fig. 4b. The pump's narrow grey sector does not rule this out, because the pump has a fixed frequency and no chirp. "Deterministic phase scrambled by jitter" would not be spontaneous U(1) symmetry breaking. The authors should: (a) report the shot-to-shot distribution of instantaneous frequency, amplitude and onset time at onset and at 1.25 µs, and estimate the phase spread that jitter alone would produce; (b) show the phase distribution, or the mean resultant length R, as a function of time from the onset of the coherent signal (about 0.6–0.8 µs at 23 dBm, where the coherence value first exceeds baseline in Fig. 3b) through the plateau — under deterministic-plus-jitter R starts near 1 and decays, under spontaneous formation it is near 1/√N from the first coherent cycles; (c) for each shot, extract f(t) from the IQ data, subtract 2π∫f dt from a common reference point (for example the moment the amplitude first crosses a fixed threshold), and show the histogram of the residual phase; (d) plot the phase against each shot's onset time or integrated amplitude, since jitter-set phase would correlate with these; (e) report phase statistics and the onset-time distribution at each pump power across threshold (the Fig. 4 powers), to show that phase randomness appears together with condensation and is not produced by onset jitter near threshold; (f) most convincingly, add a positive control in which a weak coherent seed at the condensate (k ≈ 0) frequency, phase-locked to the reference, is injected before or during pumping at several seed powers, showing a narrow histogram that broadens as the seed is reduced — this establishes both that the analysis detects a locked phase and where spontaneous selection takes over. A comparison with a numerical model of the pumped magnon gas (stochastic Landau–Lifshitz or kinetic/Gross–Pitaevskii simulations with thermal noise), predicting random phase selection and how frequency jitter accumulates into phase, would also help.

2. **The phase stability of the reference chain is asserted but not shown, and the pump-phase reference is unexplained.** The text says "all externally induced factors are stable within a few degrees of phase shift", but Extended Data Fig. 1 states that the oscilloscope was not locked to the shared 10-MHz reference "due to technical difficulties" and that phase stability "was confirmed in preliminary measurements", which are not shown. The grey "pump phase" sectors in Fig. 2a, against which the BEC phase is called random, are not explained: the 7.8-GHz pump is removed by the diplexer and the 5-GHz and <4-GHz low-pass filters (F1, F2) and lies outside the 3.2-GHz IQ band, so it is unclear how its phase was measured, and why the bars appear only in the first two insets. The grey sectors also look several tens of degrees wide, at odds with "a few degrees". The paper does not say whether the pulse generator's gating of SG2 is synchronous with the 7.8-GHz carrier, or what the oscilloscope trigger jitter is relative to the roughly 120-MHz IF period (about 8 ns). If the reference jitters by tens of degrees or more, the uniformity claim loses its footing. The authors should describe the pump-phase measurement in the Methods and give its spread numerically, and show a reference measurement: a CW or pulsed tone near 3.3 GHz, derived from the locked synthesizers and sent through the identical path (antenna, diplexer, filters, amplifier, IQ mixer, unsynchronised oscilloscope with its triggering) with the same pulse timing and repetition rate, acquired over 1,000 shots, giving a narrow phase histogram with its width in degrees at the same delay as the BEC measurement.

3. **There is no direct evidence that the coherent k ≈ 0 state is a thermalised BEC rather than another noise-seeded instability, and a random phase alone does not single out a BEC.** A noise-seeded random phase is generic to any self-oscillation growing from fluctuations: a laser above threshold, parametric self-oscillation, kinetic instability or auto-oscillation. The paper itself describes the kinetic instability and cascade four-magnon scattering (p. 1432), which feed the band bottom directly and may carry phase correlations with the pump pairs. The signatures offered for BEC — frequency at the band bottom, a threshold and a narrow line — are shared by these processes. The paper shows only the signal in the antenna's narrow window near the band bottom: no magnon distribution over energy or wavevector, no Bose–Einstein-like distribution with μ reaching the band bottom, and no thermalisation timescale compared with theory. This matters especially in the perpendicular geometry, where ref. 32 questions BEC and spin superfluidity in YIG (on the sign of the effective interaction and the stability of the uniform mode); the paper cites it without discussing its arguments. The claims in the title, abstract and Discussion that random phase "confirms" a central prediction of BEC theory and is "required … to prove the formation of a true BEC" therefore rest on an identification the paper does not make. The authors should either add evidence of thermalisation in this sample and geometry — for example time-resolved, spectrally and k-resolved BLS occupation on the same film showing a thermalised distribution feeding the k ≈ 0 peak, with a delay consistent with four-magnon scattering rates, or a pump-duration and power dependence showing condensation follows the gas density rather than the instantaneous pump — or narrow the claim to the spontaneous phase of a coherent band-bottom state. They should explicitly address ref. 32, including the sign of the nonlinear frequency shift in this geometry (the upward shift at high density implied by Fig. 3a is relevant); compare the roughly 0.6-µs delay before any signal appears (Fig. 3a) with expected thermalisation and kinetic-instability timescales; if refs 27, 28 establish thermalisation under the same conditions, state the matching parameters (film, field, pump frequency and power); at minimum state what would distinguish the two scenarios in their data; and temper the language: random phase is necessary but not sufficient.

4. **The coherence value does not distinguish a coherent condensate from a narrowband incoherent (thermal-like) population, so the emergence of a coherent state is not established.** As defined in the Methods, the coherence value C_w(t) is the integral of the normalised |autocorrelation| over a 100-ns window, relative to a pure tone. It measures spectral purity within one shot: any signal with linewidth well below 1/(100 ns) = 10 MHz approaches 1, including a single-mode thermal (chaotic) field or a band-bottom mode populated incoherently but spectrally narrowed. The rise in Figs. 3b and 4a may therefore track spectral narrowing (already known from BLS) and the signal emerging above noise. The authors should use the single-shot data they already have to test field statistics: over the 1,000 shots at fixed t, report the amplitude or |z|² distribution and the intensity correlation g^(2)(0) = ⟨|z|^4⟩/⟨|z|^2⟩^2 after subtracting the noise contribution, in the pre-condensate, onset and plateau windows. A coherent condensate gives a narrow (Rician/Poissonian) amplitude distribution with g^(2)(0) → 1; a thermal mode gives a Rayleigh/exponential intensity distribution with g^(2)(0) ≈ 2. They should also compare the measured condensate linewidth with the 10-MHz window resolution so readers can see what a high coherence value actually excludes.

B. Should address

5. **The coherence value is uncalibrated: it depends on SNR, chirp and amplitude modulation, and its baseline is not zero.** Pure noise gives about 0.18, not 0 as the Fig. 3b caption implies. A perfectly phase-coherent signal that chirps by tens of MHz within 100 ns, or whose amplitude changes within the window, gives a value below 1 without any loss of phase memory; the authors themselves attribute the dip near 1.0–1.1 µs at pump-off (Figs. 3b, 4a) to the nonlinear frequency shift. SNR dependence is evident in the paper: the low-amplitude 22-dBm curve saturates at about 0.45 rather than about 0.75, and the Fig. 4 caption states both that "the coherence of the BEC does not depend on the absolute amplitude" and that the 21-dBm increase "is not very pronounced" because of low SNR, which also contradicts the text attributing the late decay to SNR. These need reconciling. The authors should calibrate the metric with synthetic signals processed identically, using the 100-ns window and the actual sampling rate: (i) a pure tone, (ii) a narrowband Gaussian-noise (thermal) field of the measured linewidth, (iii) a tone with the measured chirp, and (iv) a tone with the measured amplitude envelope, each added to the recorded noise floor at the measured SNR, plus pump-off or no-pump traces. They should show which model reproduces the data, whether the plateau value of about 0.75 rather than 1 comes from noise, chirp or finite linewidth, rescale or subtract the noise baseline, and plot coherence against SNR across pump powers to separate intrinsic coherence changes from detection limits. A complementary standard measure, such as the within-shot phase-noise spectrum or linewidth after removing the chirp, or g1(τ) of the demodulated, chirp-corrected signal, would help.

6. **IQ-mixer artifacts lie in the condensate band.** Extended Data Fig. 2c shows "a constant band at 3.77 GHz and a shadow between 3.2 GHz and 3.3 GHz"; the condensate sits at about 3.32 GHz, next to the shadow, and the 3.2-GHz LO puts the IQ image near 3.08 GHz. A stationary spurious tone is itself coherent: it would raise the coherence value and could bias phases toward a fixed value, while a phase-unstable spur could add phase noise, and IQ gain/phase imbalance can distort phase histograms. The authors should identify the origin of these features, quantify their amplitude relative to the condensate signal, correct for IQ imbalance, and state whether they set the 0.18 baseline. They should report the coherence value and phase histogram after notching or filtering the artifacts, and for pump-off or sub-threshold (20 dBm) traces computed in the same windows, showing that the baseline and plateau values do not change.

7. **The uniformity of the phase histograms is not tested statistically.** The polar histograms alone are not enough. The authors should apply a stated test (Rayleigh or Kuiper, with p-value) at each time slice and pump power, report the mean resultant length |⟨e^{iφ}⟩| against its 1,000-shot null expectation of about 0.03, give the same statistic for the pump phase, and state the bin count. A two-fold (φ, φ + π) structure, as expected for phase locking to a parametric pump, should be explicitly tested for and excluded, for example with the second circular moment.

8. **Shot independence and the repetition protocol are not documented.** The repetition period of the 1,000 shots, the waiting time against the magnon lifetime, and evidence that the system fully relaxes between shots are not given. Residual coherent precession or magnons from a previous shot could seed the next, and heating or thermal memory from 1-µs pulses at 23–25 dBm (about 0.3 W at 25 dBm) could correlate successive shots, which bears directly on the phase statistics. The authors should state the repetition rate, show the correlation of phases between consecutive shots (for example an autocorrelation of φ_n against shot index), and check whether the result survives a change in duty cycle.

9. **The claims that "the BEC does not undergo dephasing" and that coherence persists after the signal is below noise are not supported.** The Fig. 4 discussion (p. 1434) concludes the condensate is "phase-stable until the signal vanishes", but the coherence value only probes 100-ns windows, and its decrease from about 1.8 µs follows the falling amplitude, as both SNR loss and true dephasing would. The authors should overlay the expected coherence-against-SNR curve for an ideal tone, computed from the measured amplitude decay, on Fig. 4a, and show whether the data lie on it or fall below. Long-time phase coherence within a shot should be quantified directly, for example g¹(Δt) for delays of 0.1–1 µs after removing the per-shot chirp, or the residual phase deviation from a smooth phase model over the whole plateau, tracking φ(t) − 2π∫f dt from onset to decay, and compared with the magnon lifetime and with the condensate linewidth from the spectrum analyser (Fig. 3a). The "clear remanence in coherence" at t = 3.5 µs, when the 25-dBm curve is within a few hundredths of the 0.18 baseline, needs a significance estimate against pump-off traces processed the same way and against the artifact level (concern 6). Otherwise these claims should be removed.

10. **The argument that the parametric magnons carry no phase reference rests on a non-observation the detection chain may be unable to make.** The text (p. 1432) states that no coherent signal at f_p/2 = 3.9 GHz is observed and takes this as evidence of phase randomisation among the parametric pairs. But 3.9 GHz lies at the 4-GHz diplexer crossover and at the edge of the F2 "< 4 GHz" low-pass filter (Extended Data Fig. 1); the antenna suppresses the 18-µm waves by an estimated factor of 18; and 3.9 GHz mixed with the 3.2-GHz LO gives a 700-MHz IF that should be checked against the IQ mixer IF port and oscilloscope bandwidth. Pairs are phase-locked to the pump through φ_k + φ_−k = φ_p, so this also bears on concern 1. The authors should give the measured transfer function of the full receive chain (F2 roll-off, amplifier gain) at 3.3 and 3.9 GHz and estimate the expected f_p/2 signal level if the pairs were coherent, turning the non-observation into a quantitative bound, or qualify the statement so readers can tell absence from suppression. A phase-sensitive measurement at f_p/2 synchronous with the pump would address this directly. They should also say whether the dotted line labelled "Parametrically pumped magnons" at about 3.9 GHz in Fig. 3a, where no signal is visible, is a marker or detected power.

11. **The early-time data are receiver noise, so the paper does not observe the incoherent stage it says it tracks.** The abstract says the measurement reveals "how an initially incoherent magnon gas thermalizes and undergoes a spontaneous transition". Before about 0.6 µs the detected signal (Fig. 2a at t = 0.10 µs, Fig. 3a, Fig. 4b) is indistinguishable from noise, since the parametric magnons lie outside the detection band and are suppressed by the antenna. The low early coherence is therefore the noise baseline, and the uniform histograms at 0.10 µs and 0.90 µs are expected trivially and add nothing on the condensate; presenting them alongside 1.25 µs implies a comparison between two physical states. 14. **The novelty statements are stronger than the literature supports.** The introduction says observing spontaneous coherence "has been impossible" and that the BEC phase "has only been revealed through phenomena dependent on spatial phase differences"; the Discussion says "no direct proof of randomness in the established condensate phase has yet been provided". 16. **The explanation of the coherence oscillations is speculative.** The plateau oscillations (period about 0.2–0.3 µs, 1.2–2 µs, Figs. 3b and 4a) are attributed on p. 1434 to incoherent magnons reflected from the edges of the 9.5 × 2.7 mm film, which the authors call "still a subject of investigation". Because they sit in the plateau used to argue for full coherence, the hypothesis should be tested or clearly labelled as conjecture. The authors should report the period at each pump power and compare it with the group-velocity round-trip time to the edges; the mechanism predicts the period changes with antenna-to-edge distance and with field, so moving the antenna or adding absorbing edges would test it. Alternatives to consider include beating between the condensate and a nearby mode, beating with residual chirp in the 100-ns window, and relaxation oscillations between the pump-fed reservoir and the condensate.
