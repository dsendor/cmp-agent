Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors pump a perpendicularly magnetized 2.1-µm YIG film parametrically through a 100-µm microstrip antenna at 7.8 GHz. They detect the k≈0 magnon signal single-shot with an IQ mixer that is phase-referenced to the same 10 MHz clock as the pump. From this they claim to see (i) the emergence of temporal coherence as the pumped gas thermalizes into a magnon BEC near 3.32 GHz and (ii) a condensate phase that is uniformly distributed over 1,000 repetitions while the pump phase is fixed, which they present as spontaneous U(1) symmetry breaking. Single-shot, phase-resolved access to a quasiparticle condensate's macroscopic phase would be a real step beyond Brillouin light scattering, which averages over shots, and it addresses a long-standing objection that coherence in pumped magnon BECs might be inherited from the pump. The central inference, though, rests on a uniform phase histogram and on an autocorrelation "coherence value". Both admit simpler explanations that the paper does not yet exclude.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The experimental idea is simple and new. A single antenna does both pumping and inductive detection of the k≈0 condensate in an out-of-plane geometry, and a phase-locked IQ chain gives the single-shot complex amplitude z(t) = I + iQ (Fig. 2, Extended Data Fig. 2b).
- The spectrogram from the spectrum analyser and the spectrogram reconstructed from the IQ data agree (Extended Data Fig. 2a,c). This supports the claim that the IQ channel records the same magnon dynamics.
- The pump-power series (Fig. 4) shows a clear threshold between 20 and 22 dBm. Above it a high-coherence plateau appears after the pump is switched off, which fits a thermalization-driven process rather than direct excitation.
- The data are deposited on Zenodo, so readers can reanalyse them.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The uniform phase histogram does not by itself show a spontaneously chosen phase; it is equally expected from shot-to-shot jitter in the condensate frequency. (Fig. 2a insets at t = 1.25 µs; "Phase evolution of the BEC" section.) The phase is read relative to a 3.2 GHz LO at a fixed lab time, 0.25 µs after pump-off and roughly 0.5 µs after the signal first appears. Over that interval the condensate frequency moves by more than 100 MHz, from about 3.45 to 3.32 GHz (Fig. 3a), and the drift depends on the nonlinear shift and therefore on the magnon number. A shot-to-shot spread of only δf ≈ 2–4 MHz in that frequency trajectory, from pump-power noise, magnon-number fluctuations, sample temperature or the exact condensation onset time, gives a phase spread 2π·δf·Δt larger than 2π. The distribution would then be uniform even if each condensate formed deterministically with a phase set by the pump-pair dynamics. The pump's narrow grey sector does not rule this out, because the pump has a fixed frequency and no chirp. To support spontaneous symmetry breaking, the authors should:
   - (a) Plot the circular spread of the phase (mean resultant length R) against time from the onset of the coherent signal (about 0.6–0.8 µs at 23 dBm) through the plateau. Under the deterministic-plus-jitter scenario, R starts near 1 and decays as phase diffusion accumulates. Under spontaneous formation it is near 1/√N from the very first coherent cycles.
   - (b) For each shot, extract the instantaneous frequency and onset time, and show that removing the accumulated phase ∫2πf(t)dt from a common reference time still leaves a uniform distribution.
   - (c) Ideally, run a positive control: inject a weak coherent seed at the k≈0 frequency before or during pumping. It should lock the condensate phase and give a narrow histogram, which shows that the analysis can detect a non-random phase when one exists.

2. The "coherence value" does not separate a coherent condensate from a narrowband incoherent (thermal-like) population at the band bottom. (Fig. 3b,c; Methods equation for C_w(t).) The measure is the normalized integral of |autocorrelation| of the single-shot signal over a 100-ns window. It measures spectral purity within one shot. A narrowband Gaussian field, such as a thermalized gas piled up in a band-bottom mode with linewidth well below 1/(100 ns) = 10 MHz, also gives a large value. Since the headline is the emergence of a coherent state, the authors should use the single-shot amplitudes they already have to test field statistics. Over the 1,000 shots at fixed t, they should give the amplitude histogram or the intensity correlation g^(2)(0) = ⟨|z|^4⟩/⟨|z|^2⟩^2 after subtracting the noise contribution. The expected signature is a crossover from Rayleigh, or g^(2) = 2, before threshold or early in the pulse, to Rician or Poissonian, g^(2) → 1, in the plateau. They should also compare the measured condensate linewidth with the 10-MHz window resolution so readers can see what a high coherence value actually excludes.

3. The phase stability of the reference chain is asserted but not shown. (Extended Data Fig. 1 caption: "The oscilloscope was excluded due to technical difficulties, but phase stability of the setup as a whole was confirmed in preliminary measurements"; Fig. 2 grey sectors.) The random-phase conclusion depends entirely on the pump-to-LO-to-acquisition phase being reproducible from shot to shot. The paper does not explain how the grey "pump phase" distribution was measured when the pump is removed by the diplexer and F1/F2 filters. It also does not say whether the pulse generator's gating of SG2 is synchronous with the 7.8 GHz carrier, or what the oscilloscope trigger jitter is relative to the roughly 120 MHz IF. The authors should describe the pump-phase measurement and give its spread numerically. They should also show a reference measurement: a CW or pulsed tone near 3.3 GHz, injected through the same chain with the same triggering and acquired over 1,000 shots, giving a narrow phase histogram at the same delay as the BEC measurement.

B. Should address

4. The coherence values are not corrected for signal-to-noise ratio, so the claim that "the BEC does not undergo dephasing" is not established. (Fig. 4 and its caption; Discussion of decay after 2 µs.) The coherence value of a perfect tone plus additive noise depends on SNR, and its noise-only baseline is about 0.18. The decrease after about 1.8 µs follows the falling amplitude, which is what both SNR loss and true dephasing would produce. The caption's statement that coherence "does not depend on the absolute amplitude of the signal" also contradicts the text, which attributes the decay to SNR. The authors should compute the calibration curve C_w(SNR) for an ideal tone in the measured noise, using the 100-ns window and the actual sampling rate. Plotting the measured C_w against the measured SNR would then show whether the data lie on the pure-tone curve, which would mean no dephasing, or fall below it. The same calibration would show whether the plateau value of about 0.75 rather than 1 comes from noise, chirp or finite linewidth.

5. The paper does not show that the coherent k≈0 signal comes from thermalization into a BEC rather than from direct nonlinear population of the band bottom. (Sections "Magnon BEC properties" and "Phase evolution", which mention kinetic instability.) The text itself mentions kinetic instability and cascade four-magnon scattering, which feed the band bottom directly and may carry phase correlations with the pump pairs. The signatures offered for BEC are frequency at the band bottom, threshold and narrow linewidth, and all three are shared with these processes. Ref. 32 also questions BEC claims in perpendicularly magnetized YIG. The authors should add evidence that the gas thermalizes: for example, a spectrally resolved occupation (BLS, or the antenna response across the band) showing a Bose-Einstein-like distribution with μ approaching the band bottom, or a dependence on pump duration and power showing that condensation follows the gas density rather than the instantaneous pump. At minimum, they should state what would distinguish the two scenarios in their data.

6. IQ-mixer artifacts lie in the condensate band. (Extended Data Fig. 2c caption: "a constant band at 3.77 GHz and a shadow between 3.2 GHz and 3.3 GHz".) The condensate sits at about 3.32 GHz, next to the "shadow", and the LO at 3.2 GHz puts the IQ image near 3.08 GHz. A coherent artifact tone such as the 3.77 GHz line, or IQ imbalance, contributes to both the autocorrelation and the phase. The authors should identify the origin of these features and correct for IQ gain and phase imbalance. They should also report the coherence value and phase histogram after removing or filtering the artifacts, and show that the 0.18 baseline and the plateau value do not change.

7. Shot independence and quantitative uniformity are not documented. (Fig. 2 insets; "1,000 measurements".) The repetition period, the magnon lifetime against it, and any heating of the YIG film by the 1-µs, 23–25 dBm pulses are not given. Residual magnons or thermal memory from the previous shot could correlate successive phases. The authors should state the repetition rate and report a formal uniformity test with its p-value, such as Rayleigh or Kuiper, at each time slice and pump power. They should also show the correlation of phases between consecutive shots.

8. The results come from one sample, one field (281 mT) and one pump frequency (7.8 GHz). The headline would be more convincing if it were reproduced in a second film or a second antenna, or at a second field or pump frequency, so that the threshold, the coherence plateau and the random phase are seen to be generic rather than specific to one configuration. At least, the authors should state whether the 23 dBm results were reproduced over separate cooldowns or mountings, given that the stub tuner has to be retuned.

9. The oscillations of coherence in the plateau are explained by speculation. (Fig. 3b and Fig. 4a, 1.2–2 µs.) The authors propose that incoherent magnons reflected from the sample edges (9.5 × 2.7 mm) cross the antenna. This mechanism predicts that the oscillation period changes with the antenna-to-edge distance and with the group velocity, and so with field. The period at each pump power should be reported and compared with the expected round-trip time, or the attribution should be clearly marked as tentative. An alternative explanation is beating between the condensate and the residual chirp in the 100-ns window.

10. The claim that the parametric magnons are incoherent rests on not seeing a signal at f_p/2, but the detection sensitivity at that frequency is not shown. ("Phase evolution" section: amplitude reduced by "an estimated factor of 18"; F2 is a <4 GHz low-pass and the detection window is "several hundred megahertz" above the Kittel frequency.) The text says no coherent signal appears at 3.9 GHz. The authors should give the chain's transmission at 3.9 GHz, together with F2 roll-off and amplifier gain, and the expected signal level, to show that a coherent signal at f_p/2 would have been detectable. Otherwise the statement that the parametric magnons have no global phase is not tested. Pairs are phase-locked to the pump through φ_k + φ_−k = φ_p, so clarifying this also bears on Concern 1.

11. The pump power and condensation threshold are not calibrated against the parametric threshold. (Fig. 4: threshold "21 dBm in this particular set-up".) Only the generator power is given, apparently before Amp 1 (+50 dB) and the stub tuner. The authors should give the power delivered to the antenna, or the microwave field h at the film, and the parametric instability threshold measured with the microwave detector. Expressing the BEC threshold relative to the parametric threshold would make the results comparable with other work.

C. Minor

12. The quantum-mechanical framing goes beyond what the data show. Phrases such as "directly probe the coherence of the BEC state", "equivalent to the quantum mechanical phase" and "spontaneous symmetry breaking", in the abstract and Discussion, describe the measurement of a classical macrospin precession phase. The authors should also comment on whether the YIG Hamiltonian in this geometry is U(1)-symmetric, given dipolar terms and anisotropy, and whether the pump, by breaking it to a discrete symmetry at f_p/2, matters for a condensate at a different frequency.
13. The x-axis tick labels in Fig. 2 repeat because of rounding ("0.10, 0.10, 0.11, 0.12, 0.12"; "1.25, 1.25"). More decimal places are needed.
14. "Standard derivation" should read "standard deviation" in the captions of Fig. 4 and Extended Data Fig. 2, and "Suppementary" should read "Supplementary" (p. 1433).
15. In the Methods expression for C_w, the "bar denotes vector normalization" is ambiguous. The authors should state whether each window is normalized by its own RMS amplitude, since that choice decides whether C_w is amplitude-independent. They should also give the oscilloscope sampling rate, which sets the number of points per 100-ns window.
16. In Fig. 3a, the dotted line labelled "Parametrically pumped magnons" sits at about 3.9 GHz, at the edge of the plotted range, where no signal is visible. The authors should say whether this is a marker or detected power.
17. The t = 0.10 µs inset in Fig. 2a is a uniform distribution of pure noise and says nothing about the condensate. The caption should say so, to avoid implying a comparison between two physical states.
18. Optical access is not involved, but the film temperature under 1-µs pulses at up to 25 dBm (+50 dB amplification) is not discussed, even though the BEC frequency and thermalization depend on it. The authors should give a brief estimate.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one figure on phase statistics against time since condensation onset. It should show the mean resultant length R(t) with the 1/√N level marked, a demodulated phase histogram after per-shot frequency correction, and the same analysis for a reference tone through the chain. This addresses Concerns 1, 3 and 7 together.
2. Add the amplitude-statistics and g^(2)(0) analysis of the 1,000 single shots, before and after threshold. This would be the most direct evidence that the state is coherent rather than a narrowband thermal population.
3. Provide the SNR calibration of the coherence value and replot Fig. 4a as coherence against SNR, so that intrinsic coherence is separated from detection limits.
4. A seeded-condensation experiment, in which a weak injected tone at the k≈0 frequency locks the BEC phase, would give a clear contrast between stimulated and spontaneous phase selection. It would turn the random-phase observation into a controlled test.
