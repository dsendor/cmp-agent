Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors pump a 2.1-µm YIG film parametrically at 7.8 GHz in the out-of-plane (forward-volume) geometry. A single 100-µm microstrip both pumps the film and picks up the k ≈ 0 condensate signal, which is read out single-shot with an IQ mixer phase-referenced to the pump chain. They report three findings. Over 1,000 shots the condensate phase at a fixed time is uniformly distributed. Within a shot the phase evolves linearly once the condensate forms. A windowed autocorrelation "coherence value" rises from a noise baseline (about 0.18) to about 0.75 as the condensate forms, above a pump threshold of about 21 dBm. Single-shot, phase-resolved electrical access to a room-temperature magnon condensate is a useful experimental advance. However, the central claim, direct observation of spontaneous U(1) symmetry breaking in a magnon BEC, needs more support than the present data give. The uniform phase histogram and the first-order coherence measure have simpler explanations that the paper does not exclude.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clean, all-electrical, single-shot, phase-resolved readout of a pumped magnon gas. This has clear advantages over ensemble-averaged Brillouin light scattering for questions about coherence and phase.
- The time-resolved data are rich: spectrogram, IQ traces, coherence evolution and power dependence. The spectrum-analyser and IQ spectrograms agree (Extended Data Fig. 2). The data are publicly deposited.
- The out-of-plane geometry puts the condensate at k ≈ 0, where inductive detection is efficient. Other groups can readily adopt the approach.
- The question addressed, whether the condensate phase is chosen spontaneously or imprinted by the drive, is fundamental and of broad interest across BEC systems.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **A uniform phase histogram at a fixed time does not show that the phase is selected spontaneously at condensation.** Figure 2a's insets and the "Phase evolution of the BEC" section infer spontaneous phase formation from the uniform histogram at t = 1.25 µs. However, the condensate frequency is not fixed. It chirps by roughly 100 MHz between about 0.9 and 1.4 µs (Fig. 3a; Extended Data Fig. 2a,c) through the density-dependent nonlinear shift, and it is about 100 MHz away from the 3.2-GHz LO. Small shot-to-shot fluctuations in magnon number therefore give shot-to-shot frequency differences. A spread of only a few MHz, accumulated over the about 0.3 µs between condensate onset and the 1.25 µs slice, is enough to spread the phase over 2π. This would happen even if the phase at formation were fully deterministic and pump-locked. The histogram therefore cannot by itself separate "random phase at formation" from "deterministic phase plus frequency jitter".

   The 0.10 µs and 0.90 µs histograms add no information on this point: at those times the bottom-of-band signal is noise-dominated (Fig. 2a, left panels; Fig. 4b), so a uniform distribution is expected trivially. The authors should do the following:
   - Report the shot-to-shot distribution of instantaneous frequency (and amplitude) at onset and at 1.25 µs. Then estimate the phase spread that frequency jitter alone would produce.
   - For each shot, extrapolate the measured phase trajectory back to a common reference point (for example, the moment the amplitude first crosses a fixed threshold) and show the histogram of that phase.
   - Show the histogram as a function of time from onset, not only at three fixed times.
   - Most convincingly, add a positive control. Inject a weak coherent seed at the condensate frequency, phase-locked to the reference, during the pumping interval. Show that the same chain and analysis then give a narrow phase histogram, and that the histogram broadens as the seed is reduced. This would establish both the sensitivity of the method to a locked phase and the threshold at which spontaneous selection takes over.

2. **The phase stability of the reference chain is asserted but not shown.** The text says that "all externally induced factors are stable within a few degrees of phase shift". However, Extended Data Fig. 1 states that the oscilloscope was not locked to the shared 10-MHz reference "due to technical difficulties", and that phase stability "was confirmed in preliminary measurements". Those measurements are not shown. The grey "pump phase" bars in Fig. 2a are not explained: the paper does not say how the 7.8-GHz pump phase was measured through a chain that filters out the pump (F1, F2, diplexer), nor why the bars appear only in the first two insets.

   Uniform BEC-phase histograms are meaningful only if a phase-locked test signal at about 3.3 GHz, sent through the identical path (diplexer, filters, amplifier, IQ mixer, unsynchronised oscilloscope with its trigger jitter), produces a narrow histogram over 1,000 triggers at the same repetition rate. The authors should show this histogram, with its width in degrees, and describe the pump-phase measurement in the Methods.

3. **The coherence value measures first-order (spectral) coherence within a 100-ns window. It does not distinguish a coherent condensate from a narrowband incoherent mode, and it is entangled with signal-to-noise ratio.** As defined in the Methods, the coherence value is the integral of the normalised autocorrelation over a 100-ns window, relative to a pure tone. Any signal whose linewidth is well below 1/(100 ns) = 10 MHz approaches 1. This includes a single-mode thermal (chaotic) field, or a mode populated incoherently but spectrally narrowed. The rise in Figs. 3b and 4a therefore mainly tracks spectral narrowing and the signal emerging above noise. That spectral narrowing is already known from BLS. The measure is also lowered by chirp, which the authors themselves invoke for the dip near 1.0–1.1 µs.

   That the measure depends on SNR is evident in the paper itself. The 22-dBm curve, with low amplitude, saturates at about 0.45 rather than about 0.75. The Fig. 4 caption states both "Remarkably, the coherence of the BEC does not depend on the absolute amplitude" and that the 21-dBm increase "is not very pronounced" because of the low SNR. These statements need reconciling.

   The authors should do two things:
   - Calibrate the coherence value against synthetic signals. Use (i) a pure tone, (ii) a narrowband Gaussian-noise (thermal) field of the measured linewidth, and (iii) a chirped tone matching the observed chirp, each added to the measured noise floor at the observed SNR. Then show which model reproduces the data.
   - Use the single-shot data they already have to test the photon-number statistics, which separate coherent from thermal states. Report the distribution of |z|² over shots, and/or the intensity correlation g²(0) within the plateau. A coherent condensate should give a narrow amplitude distribution with g²(0) ≈ 1; a thermal mode gives an exponential intensity distribution with g²(0) ≈ 2.

4. **There is no direct evidence that the observed state is a thermalised BEC rather than a nonequilibrium instability, so the random phase does not single out BEC.** A random phase seeded by noise is generic to any self-oscillation that grows from fluctuations, such as a laser above threshold, parametric or kinetic instabilities, or auto-oscillation. The paper itself names the kinetic instability as a channel that directly populates the spectral minimum. The evidence for condensation here is a narrow line at the band bottom above a pump threshold. The paper shows no magnon distribution over energy or wavevector, no evidence of a quasi-equilibrium (Bose–Einstein) distribution with μ reaching the band bottom, and no thermalisation timescale compared with theory.

   This matters especially in the perpendicular geometry. Here the authors cite a published comment (ref. 32) that questions BEC and spin superfluidity in perpendicularly magnetised YIG, but they do not discuss its arguments. These concern the sign of the effective interaction and the stability of the uniform mode. The authors should do the following:
   - Provide spectrally (and ideally k-) resolved population data, for example time-resolved BLS on the same sample. Show a thermalised distribution feeding the k ≈ 0 peak, with a delay after pump onset consistent with four-magnon scattering rates.
   - Explicitly address ref. 32, including the sign of the nonlinear frequency shift in this geometry. The upward shift at high density implied by Fig. 3a is relevant here.
   - Temper the abstract and Discussion claims that random phase "confirms" BEC and that it is "required ... to prove the formation of a true BEC". Random phase is necessary but not sufficient.

B. Should address

5. **The argument that the parametric magnons carry no phase reference rests on a non-observation that the detection chain may simply be unable to make.** The text states that no coherent signal at f_p/2 = 3.9 GHz is observed, and takes this as evidence of phase randomisation among the parametric pairs. However, 3.9 GHz lies at the 4-GHz crossover of the diplexer and at the edge of the F2 low-pass filter ("< 4 GHz", Extended Data Fig. 1). The antenna itself suppresses 18-µm waves by about 18 times (stated in the text). In addition, 3.9 GHz mixed with the 3.2-GHz LO gives a 700-MHz IF, which should be checked against the analog bandwidth of the IQ mixer IF port and of the oscilloscope.

   The authors should give the measured transfer function of the full receive chain at 3.3 and 3.9 GHz. They should also estimate the expected f_p/2 signal level if the pairs were coherent, so that the non-observation becomes a quantitative bound. A phase-sensitive measurement at f_p/2 synchronous with the pump would address this directly.

6. **The IQ artifacts overlap the condensate band.** Extended Data Fig. 2c shows a constant band at 3.77 GHz and a "shadow between 3.2 GHz and 3.3 GHz", which is where the condensate sits (about 3.32 GHz). A stationary spurious tone would raise the coherence value and could bias phases toward a fixed value. Conversely, a phase-unstable spur could add phase noise. The authors should quantify the artifacts' amplitude relative to the condensate signal and state whether they set the baseline of about 0.18. They should also show the phase histogram and coherence value of pump-off (or sub-threshold, 20 dBm) traces computed in the same windows.

7. **The threshold and power dependence are only coarsely characterised.** Only five pump powers in 1-dB steps are shown (Fig. 4). The "condensation threshold of 21 dBm" rests on a coherence increase that is barely above baseline, while the text describes the "strong increase" at 22 dBm. The authors should do three things:
   - Show the plateau amplitude and the integrated spectral weight at the band bottom versus pump power, with finer steps. Identify the parametric threshold separately from the condensation threshold.
   - Plot coherence against SNR across powers, to separate an intrinsic coherence change from an SNR effect (see concern 3).
   - Comment on the decline of coherence and amplitude at 25 dBm relative to 23 dBm late in time. This could indicate heating or different thermalisation pathways.

8. **"The BEC does not undergo dephasing" is not demonstrated beyond 100 ns.** The Fig. 4 discussion concludes that the condensate is "phase-stable until the signal vanishes". However, the coherence value only probes 100-ns windows. Long-time phase coherence within a shot should be quantified directly. For example, compute g¹(Δt) for delays of 0.1–1 µs after removing the deterministic, per-shot chirp, or report the residual phase deviation from a smooth phase model over the whole plateau. Then compare the resulting coherence time with the magnon lifetime. The claim that coherence "still shows a clearly detectable" remanence at 3.5 µs when the amplitude is below noise should be checked against the artifact level (concern 6) and the noise-only baseline over the same ensemble.

9. **The work uses a single sample and a single operating point, and the repetition protocol is unspecified.** All data come from one YIG film, one antenna, one field (281 mT) and one pump frequency (7.8 GHz). A field or pump-frequency dependence would show that the condensate frequency tracks the band bottom and that the phase behaviour is robust. A second sample or antenna would show reproducibility. The repetition period of the 1,000 shots, and evidence that the system fully relaxes between shots (no residual magnons or cumulative heating), are not given. Residual coherent precession from a previous shot could in principle seed the next one.

10. **The explanation of the coherence oscillations is speculative.** The plateau oscillations (Figs. 3b and 4) are attributed to magnons reflected from the sample edges. The authors note this is "still a subject of investigation". Because these oscillations sit in the plateau used to argue for full coherence, the paper should either test the hypothesis or clearly label it as conjecture. Possible tests include comparing the oscillation period with group-velocity round-trip times over the 9.5 × 2.7 mm film, moving the antenna, or adding absorbing edges. Alternatives include beating between the condensate and a nearby mode, or relaxation oscillations between pump-fed reservoir and condensate.

11. **The novelty and the quantum-classical framing are overstated.** The introduction states that the BEC phase "has only been revealed through phenomena dependent on spatial phase differences". The Discussion states that "no direct proof of randomness in the established condensate phase has yet been provided". These statements overlook the following:
    - Atomic BEC interference between independently prepared condensates already shows a random relative phase (ref. 14).
    - Photon-BEC work has studied temporal phase coherence and phase jumps of a condensate coupled to a reservoir.
    - Polariton condensates have shown spontaneous polarisation (symmetry-breaking) build-up from shot to shot.

    The authors should position the work relative to these studies. Separately, the statement that the classical precession phase is "equivalent to the quantum mechanical phase" should be phrased more carefully. A macroscopic classical order parameter with noise-seeded random phase is what is measured. No quantum-mechanical (for example, number-phase) property is tested.

C. Minor

12. **Figure 1b is inconsistent with the text.** In Fig. 1b the pumped region at 3.9 GHz spans |k| up to about 5 rad µm⁻¹. The text gives the parametric magnons a wavelength of about 18 µm (k ≈ 0.35 rad µm⁻¹), which is consistent with a forward-volume dispersion for d = 2.1 µm. If Fig. 1b is schematic, say so in the axis or caption; otherwise correct the k scale.

13. **The field and band-bottom calibration should be stated.** With μ₀M_s ≈ 0.175 T for YIG, the out-of-plane Kittel frequency at 281 mT is about 3.0 GHz, whereas the condensate settles at about 3.32 GHz. The authors should state M_s, any anisotropy and the field calibration, or show a low-power FMR measurement locating the k = 0 frequency. They should also discuss whether pump heating (which lowers M_s and raises f in this geometry) contributes to the frequency drift that is attributed purely to magnon-number decrease.

14. **The Methods definition of the coherence value needs more detail.** Please specify the following:
    - which ω is used in the reference e^{iωt}, and whether it matters;
    - whether the coherence value is computed per shot and then averaged, or from the averaged autocorrelation;
    - whether the shaded bands are standard deviations across shots or standard errors.

    Include the noise-only baseline as a dashed line in Figs. 3b and 4a.

15. **The uniformity of the phase histograms should be tested statistically.** Quantify uniformity with a Rayleigh or Kuiper test, and give the bin count. A two-fold (φ, φ + π) structure, as expected for phase locking to a parametric pump, should be explicitly tested for and excluded. This could be done with the second circular moment.

16. **Typographical and labelling issues:**
    - The Fig. 2 time-axis tick labels are rounded to duplicates (for example "0.10, 0.10, 0.11, 0.12, 0.12"); use more digits or relative time.
    - "standard derivation" should be "standard deviation" (Fig. 4 and Extended Data Fig. 2 captions).
    - "Suppementary" should be "Supplementary" (p. 1433).
    - "ensemble average" versus "average of 1,000 measurements" is used inconsistently.
    - Specify in the Fig. 2 caption what the colour scale of the histogram represents.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a coherent-seed experiment at the condensate frequency, varying the seed amplitude. Show the transition from a pump- or seed-locked phase to a spontaneously chosen phase. This single experiment would answer concerns 1, 2 and 5 together.
2. Use the existing single-shot data to compute amplitude and intensity statistics (|z|² distributions, g²) in the pre-condensate, onset and plateau windows. Compare them with coherent and thermal expectations.
3. Add time-resolved BLS (or another energy- or k-resolved probe) on the same film to show thermalisation into a Bose–Einstein distribution. Discuss the perpendicular-geometry objections of ref. 32.
4. Compare the onset time, threshold and phase statistics with a numerical model of the pumped magnon gas (for example, stochastic Landau–Lifshitz or kinetic/Gross–Pitaevskii simulations with thermal noise). The model should show random phase selection at condensation and predict how shot-to-shot frequency jitter accumulates into phase.
