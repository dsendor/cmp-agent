Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper uses SI-STM on Sn-doped CsV3Sb5-xSnx (x ≈ 0.1, 0.33, 0.68) and Ti-doped CsV3-yTiySb5 (y = 0.2) to ask whether rotational symmetry breaking in AV3Sb5 is inherited from the 2×2 CDW. In samples where the 2×2 CDW peaks are absent, the authors report nanoscale regions with unidirectional ~½Q_Bragg modulations (Sn) or anisotropic Bragg-peak amplitude (Ti). From these they conclude that electronic nematicity is pervasive, unrelated to the CDW, and the "parent state" of kagome superconductors. The question matters, and imaging CDW-free compositions is a sensible way to separate the two orders. However, the evidence is qualitative: hand-selected few-nanometre boxes in strongly disordered alloys, at one temperature. As written, it does not support the strong title and abstract claims.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- It addresses a central, widely debated question (nematicity versus 2×2 CDW in AV3Sb5) with a well-chosen strategy: suppress the CDW chemically on two different lattice sites, then look for residual C6 breaking.
- The data are of high quality. The STM data clearly confirm that the long-range 2×2 CDW and 4×1 stripes are absent at x ≥ 0.33 and at y = 0.2 (Fig. 1g–k, Fig. 4b inset).
- The ~½Q_Bragg feature at x = 0.33 is energy dependent and broad, and the DFT/autocorrelation picture of M-pocket scattering (Fig. 2i,j) gives a plausible alternative to residual CDW.
- Within one field of view, scanned with one tip, the domains point along all three lattice directions. This is a useful argument against a tip-induced anisotropy.
- The observation is timely given related reports (Ref. 44) and the long-range nematicity reported in CsTi3Bi5.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The local anisotropy is never quantified, and the regions are hand-picked.** The central evidence is three boxes per image in Fig. 3a,c (each a few nm across) and two zooms in Fig. 4c,d. Each was selected because it "seemingly" shows modulations along one direction. In a random alloy with x = 0.33–0.68, dopant-induced QPI is intrinsically inhomogeneous. Any few-nm window will show uneven intensity among the three ½Q directions purely by chance, even if the underlying electronic state is C6 symmetric and only the impurity configuration is random. The authors should:
   - define a local nematic order parameter. One option is the normalized difference between the three ½Q (or Bragg) Fourier amplitudes, computed by lock-in/Lawler–Fujita demodulation with a stated filter length. Map it over the full field of view in Fig. 3a,c and Fig. 4a.
   - report the distribution of the order parameter and its spatial correlation length, along with the fraction of area that is "nematic" and the domain size.
   - compare against a null model: the same analysis applied to simulated QPI from randomly placed isotropic scatterers, using the DFT band structure (or a C6-symmetric tight-binding model) at the measured dopant density, and/or to phase-randomized or symmetrized versions of the data.

   Only if the measured anisotropy and its correlation length clearly exceed this null does the data show a nematic electronic state rather than random speckle from disorder. This is the key result, and at present it rests on visual inspection.

2. **The finite-q unidirectional modulation is presented as "q = 0 nematicity" without showing a q = 0 signal (Sn samples).** The Discussion states "short-range q = 0 electronic nematicity". However, all Sn-sample evidence is a unidirectional modulation at ~½Q_Bragg (Fig. 3b,d), i.e. a finite-wavevector, locally 1Q pattern. Unidirectional ½Q correlations are exactly what short-range, locally 1Q 2×2 CDW fluctuations, or strain-pinned remnants of the CDW, would look like. The paper's arguments against residual CDW (p. 4) are qualitative:
   - absence of the peaks at x ≈ 0.1;
   - appearance at "some energies";
   - "slight dispersion".

   The authors should show a q = 0 measure of C6 breaking in the Sn samples, for example the anisotropy of the three Bragg-peak amplitudes or of the local lattice/intra-unit-cell signal within each domain, as was done for Ti in Fig. 4e,f. They should also show that it is spatially correlated with the orientation of the ½Q modulation. They should further quantify the q(E) dispersion of the ½Q feature (Extended Data Fig. 2, cited but not provided in this version) against the DFT CEC prediction across energies, and test whether the feature's contrast inverts or shifts across E_F, as expected for QPI but not for a static charge modulation. Without these, "residual short-range 1Q 2×2 correlations nucleated by disorder" remains a live alternative, and it would undercut "unrelated to the 2×2 CDW".

3. **For Ti-doped samples, an electronic origin is not separated from a structural one, and the evidence is very limited.** Fig. 4 rests on one ~8 nm topograph taken at a single bias (+100 mV, 100 pA), plus one more boundary in Extended Data Fig. 3. The domains in the amplitude maps (Fig. 4e,f) are ~1–3 nm across, comparable to the Ti–Ti spacing at y = 0.2 (about one Ti per 15 V sites). A locally stronger Bragg modulation along one direction can come from:
   - local lattice distortion or strain around Ti clusters;
   - local variations in apparent height;
   - setpoint effects;
   - piezo creep or drift in small-area scans.

   None of these is electronic nematicity. The authors should:
   - show the same areas at several biases, both polarities, and in dI/dV maps (a structural distortion should be largely bias independent, and the setpoint effect needs to be addressed);
   - perform a displacement-field (Lawler–Fujita) analysis to check for local uniaxial strain;
   - give the Fourier filter width used for Fig. 4e,f;
   - show statistics from several larger fields of view and more than one tip or cleave.

   Since the claim of a "generic" phenomenon rests on agreement between Sn and Ti, the Ti evidence needs to stand independently.

4. **The headline claims of "pervasive" nematicity, a "parent state", nematicity "in all samples", and a CDW-independent origin go beyond what was measured.**
   - All data are taken at 4.5 K. No temperature dependence is shown, so the paper cannot say where the "nematic" state sets in. Yet the schematic phase diagram (Fig. 1b) shades "Electronic nematicity" across x = 0–1 and up to 100 K.
   - Fig. 1b includes the pristine compound and regions where no data exist (above 4.5 K; x between 0.68 and 1).
   - The paper's own text says that at x ≈ 0.1 no ½Q_Bragg peaks are seen (p. 4, Extended Data Fig. 1). Under the paper's own criterion, nematic domains are therefore not demonstrated at x ≈ 0.1, which conflicts with "persists in all samples".
   - "Parent state" implies that the same order exists in pristine CsV3Sb5 above T_CDW and gives rise to the CDW and superconductivity. Nothing presented connects the short-range domains in heavily doped alloys to the pristine compound.

   The authors should either (a) add variable-temperature SI-STM on at least one CDW-free composition, showing that the anisotropy measure from concern 1 vanishes or weakens at higher temperature, and address x ≈ 0.1 explicitly, or (b) restrict the title, abstract, Fig. 1b and Discussion to what is shown: short-range local C6 breaking at 4.5 K in CDW-free Sn- and Ti-doped samples.

B. Should address

5. **The DFT comparison is qualitative and for a different composition.**
   - The CECs and autocorrelations are computed for x ≈ 0.25 (p. 5), while the data are at x = 0.33 and 0.68.
   - The calculation uses linear interpolation of Wannier hoppings between CsV3Sb5 and CsV3Sb4Sn, with Sn on the in-plane Sb site. This is effectively a virtual-crystal approach, which leaves out disorder broadening and possible rigid band shifts. It is also unclear how the hole doping and chemical potential at x = 0.25 versus 0.33 are handled.
   - Only k_z = 0 CECs are shown, although the data integrate over k_z.

   The authors should compute at the measured compositions and show the predicted ½Q-feature intensity versus energy alongside the measured profile in Fig. 2d. This would test whether the energy window where the feature appears (the red ellipse) matches the M-pocket picture. They should also state the energy alignment between DFT and experiment. Note that the autocorrelation is C6 symmetric by construction. It supports the origin of the wavevector but says nothing about the anisotropy, and the text should make this clear.

6. **The mirror-symmetry statements are not substantiated.** The paper states that each domain "breaks two in-plane mirror symmetries but appears to generally preserve" the third (p. 5, Discussion). This is asserted from small boxes whose FTs span only a few pixels (Fig. 3b,d). The authors should give a quantitative mirror test, such as the correlation between each domain image and its mirror image about each of the three axes, with uncertainties from the null model in concern 1. Alternatively, they should drop the claim. This matters because the Discussion builds on it (a possible chiral state at low temperature, and the comparison with CsTi3Bi5).

7. **Compositions and dopant sites are not characterized.**
   - Sn content is given as x = 0.15 in the Results text (p. 3) but as x = 0.1 or ~0.1 in Fig. 1 and elsewhere.
   - Methods describe growth only for x ≈ 0.33 and 0.68, apparently with nominal ratios.
   - The Ti sample is called y = 0.2 in the text and figure, but "x = 0.2" in Methods, with nominal loading Cs20V12Ti3.5Sb120.

   The authors should state how the actual compositions were determined (EDS/WDS, refinement, Tc or CDW transitions) and with what uncertainty, and report transport or magnetization characterization of the measured batches (absence of the CDW anomaly, Tc). They should also identify the dopants in topographs: Sn on in-plane Sb1 versus out-of-plane Sb2, and Ti on V sites. Ideally they should report whether domain orientation or boundaries correlate with dopant positions. This bears directly on whether disorder or dopant clustering is the source of the anisotropy.

8. **The number of samples, fields of view, tips and cleaves is not reported.** For each composition, the authors should state how many crystals, cleaves, fields of view and tips were used, and in how many of them the domains were seen. The "same tip" argument (p. 7) rules out a global tip anisotropy within one image. It does not establish reproducibility across samples, or that the domain-size statistics are representative.

9. **Domain size and its doping dependence are not reported.** The Discussion attributes the short range to dopant disorder. If so, the domain size should decrease with x from 0.33 to 0.68, or scale with mean dopant spacing. Reporting the correlation length from concern 1 versus composition, for both Sn and Ti, would test this. It would also help decide between intrinsic nematicity broken up by disorder and anisotropy created by disorder.

10. **The relation to Ref. 44 needs discussion.** Ref. 44 is noted as related work on Ti-doped CsV3Sb5, reporting a "chiral-nematic" parent state. The authors should compare the observations directly: domain sizes, wavevectors, mirror symmetry and chirality. This would make clear what is new and whether the two studies agree.

C. Minor

11. **Extended Data Figs. 1–3 are cited but not included in this version.** These figures carry key arguments: the x ≈ 0.1 null, the dispersion, and the second Ti boundary. They should be provided.

12. **Fig. 3 labels the domain wavevectors Q^A_CDW, Q^B_CDW and Q^C_CDW.** This contradicts the text's argument that these are not CDW wavevectors. A neutral label, for example q_½, would avoid confusion.

13. **Fig. 1b:**
   - The caption refers to "four blue circles", but the symbols are diamonds.
   - The temperature axis appears to use a broken or mixed scale; this should be stated.
   - The shaded nematic region should be marked as schematic or speculative (see concern 4).

14. **The Ti-doped compound's formula is written inconsistently.** Variants include "CsV3-ySb5Tiy" (Fig. 4 caption), "CsV3-xTixSb5" (p. 5, Methods) and y_c ≈ 0.05. A single notation should be used.

15. **Fig. 2 has incomplete and inconsistent details.**
   - Panels d–h use a 20 mV excitation and a ±600 meV range with only coarse energy sampling. The energy resolution and step should be stated.
   - The CEC energy reference should be given.
   - The text says the ½Q peaks span "~20% of Q_Bragg"; this should be shown as a fitted width with uncertainty.

16. **Measurement temperature relative to Tc.** Measurements at 4.5 K are close to Tc for some compositions on the dome in Fig. 1b. The authors should state whether any sample was superconducting at the measurement temperature, and give the effective electron temperature.

17. **Data are available only "upon reasonable request".** Depositing the raw dI/dV maps would allow independent tests of the anisotropy analysis. This is particularly valuable given concern 1.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Build the paper around one quantitative, full-field local nematic order-parameter map per composition, with a disorder-only simulated null. Report the domain size and the nematic area fraction versus x, and versus y for Ti.
2. Add variable-temperature SI-STM (for example at 4.5, 30 and 80+ K) on one CDW-free composition. This is the most direct way to support "parent state" language and the phase diagram in Fig. 1b.
3. For both dopant types, show a q = 0 lattice-scale anisotropy (Bragg-peak amplitude ratios and displacement fields) together with the finite-q modulation. Make the data bias dependent, to separate electronic from structural anisotropy.
4. Temper the title and abstract to "short-range rotational symmetry breaking persists in CDW-free doped CsV3Sb5" unless the additional data above are provided. Correct the composition labels and notation throughout.
