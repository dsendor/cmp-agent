Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript studies La2-xBaxCuO4 at x = 0.125 under in-plane uniaxial compression applied 45 degrees to the Cu-O bond. It combines in situ ac susceptibility and ZF/weak-TF muSR on one crystal with in-plane resistivity under stress on x = 0.115, 0.125 and 0.135. The headline results are a rise of the 3D Tc,mid from about 3 K to about 35 K by about 0.15-0.23 GPa, a 2.5-fold drop in the static magnetic volume fraction while Tso and Bint change only modestly, suppression of the resistive "LTT peak", and a zero-resistance Tc of 37 K (onset about 46 K) at 0.56 GPa. The authors argue that the 1/8 composition, the one with the strongest stripes, reaches the highest Tc under stress, and that static stripe order limits phase coherence rather than pairing. Uniaxial-stress muSR at exactly x = 1/8 is a natural and worthwhile extension of the group's earlier x = 0.115 and 0.135 work, and the size of the Tc enhancement is striking. Several of the central inferences, however, rest on assumptions about stress homogeneity, superconducting volume, structural phase and cross-sample comparisons that the paper does not yet test.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A very large, clearly visible stress-induced enhancement of 3D superconductivity at x = 1/8 (Fig. 2b), measured in situ with the same probe at each stress.
- Superconducting (susceptibility) and magnetic (muSR) response measured on the same crystal in the same stress apparatus, which lets the reader set the SC transition against the magnetic volume fraction (Figs. 2b, 2d, 4a, 4b).
- Resistivity under stress for three dopings around 1/8 places the commensurate composition in context (Fig. 3).
- Uniaxial stress is a clean, symmetry-selective tuning parameter, and the data will interest people working on stripes, PDW order and intertwined order in cuprates.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Stress homogeneity in the muSR/susceptibility geometry is not established, and an inhomogeneous stress field would produce exactly the "phase-separated" response the paper interprets as intrinsic. The muSR crystal is a 7 x 5.1 x 0.3 mm^3 plate glued to a Ti holder with Stycast, with a free length of only about 4 mm between the jaws (Fig. 2a; Methods A). The free length is shorter than the 5.1 mm width, and the plate is thin. In this geometry the stress near the epoxy-bonded ends, the free edges and the centre is expected to differ strongly, and a 0.3 mm plate under compression can bend or buckle. The stress is derived as force divided by the nominal 1.53 mm^2 cross-section, using a force-sensor calibration taken from earlier work (Refs. 6, 41) on different samples. Fig. 2d supports the worry: at 0.16-0.23 GPa, VM(T) does not saturate at low temperature and has a long, gradual tail up to about 35 K. A distribution of local stresses would look like this. If so, the "reduction of the magnetic volume fraction by a factor 2.5", the coexistence of LTT and LTLO domains, and the percolation argument (Sec. II, p. 7) could reflect regions under high and low stress rather than an intrinsic mixed state at a given stress. The authors should give a finite-element calculation of the stress (and strain) distribution for this exact sample, holder and epoxy geometry, weighted by the area the muon beam sees through the hematite masks. Ideally they should also measure the strain directly (strain gauge or digital image correlation), and show that VM recovers on releasing the stress, with any hysteresis.

2. The bulk superconducting volume fraction is never measured, so the claims of "bulk", "spatially uniform" and "optimal 3D" superconductivity, and the picture in which superconductivity lives in the non-magnetic regions, are untested. The in situ ac susceptibility in Fig. 2b appears to be normalized to -1 for every stress. Shielding measures screening by a connected superconducting path, not a volume fraction, and a thin percolating shell can give full shielding. The data also strain the paper's own percolation argument. At 0.09 GPa, VM is about 0.84 (Fig. 2d), so only about 16% of the volume is non-magnetic, yet the sample already shows a sharp, complete diamagnetic transition with Tc,mid of about 27 K (Fig. 2b). The text (p. 7) says that a non-magnetic fraction of about 20% "is likely insufficient for percolation". Either superconductivity coexists microscopically with static order in the magnetic regions, or the shielding is carried by a minority path. These two cases lead to opposite conclusions about competition. The authors should:
   - give the absolute susceptibility (or the normalization procedure and the raw signal versus stress);
   - measure the superfluid density and superconducting volume fraction with TF-muSR in a field above Hc1 at several stresses, which their apparatus allows;
   - discuss the 0.09 GPa data against the percolation argument.

3. Suppression of the LTT phase at x = 0.125 is inferred only from the height of a resistive hump, which is not a clean structural measure. The "LTT peak" in Fig. 3b sits at about 35-40 K, well below T_LTT (about 55 K). Its height is set both by the resistive upturn below T_LTT and by the superconducting-fluctuation downturn below it. Because stress raises the superconducting onset to about 46 K, the hump is cut off by superconductivity, and ΔR_LTT (Figs. 3d-f, 4b) would fall even if the LTT fraction were unchanged. The paper also does not show whether the kink at T_LTT itself shifts or vanishes. The correlation of VM with the "LTT peak" in Fig. 4b, and the statement that "suppression of the LTT state is essential" for optimal superconductivity, depend on this proxy. The authors should provide direct structural evidence at x = 0.125 under comparable stress, for example the LTT-specific (100)/(010) superlattice reflections by X-ray or neutron diffraction. At minimum they should track the T_LTT anomaly in dR/dT against stress and compare explicitly with the stress-dependent XRD at x = 1/8 in Ref. 30. The LTLO boundary drawn in Fig. 1d at 0.23 GPa should also be marked as measured here or as taken from elsewhere.

4. The central claim of the title, that x = 1/8 "hosts optimal superconductivity", rests on comparisons made with different metrics, stresses, apparatus and crystals. The highest numbers quoted for x = 0.125 (Tc,zero = 37 K, onset 46 K) were reached at 0.56-0.58 GPa in the Razorbill FC100 cell. For x = 0.115 and 0.135 the highest stresses were only 0.23 and 0.29 GPa (Figs. 3d, 3f). At matched stress (about 0.2-0.3 GPa), Fig. 3 gives Tc,zero of about 33-34 K for x = 0.125 against about 31 K and 30 K, a much smaller margin, and the x = 0.125 curve is still rising. For the susceptibility comparison, Tc,mid of 35 K for x = 0.125 here is set against about 30 K for x = 0.115 and 0.135 from earlier papers (Refs. 6, 19), which used different crystals, a different stress calibration and possibly different growth batches. The abstract further merges the 3D susceptibility onset at zero stress (5 K) with the 2D zero-resistance Tc at 0.5 GPa (37 K) into one "5 K to 37 K" statement. Fig. 4a puts the muSR-cell and FC100-cell stresses on a single axis with no evidence that the two calibrations agree. The authors should:
   - extend the x = 0.115 and 0.135 resistivity measurements to 0.5-0.6 GPa, or compare all dopings at matched stress and with the same Tc definition;
   - state the uncertainty of each stress scale and show that the two cells agree, for example by measuring one crystal in both;
   - state the 3D comparison (Tc,mid) separately from the 2D one (Tc,zero) throughout, including in the abstract and title.

B. Should address

5. The extraction of VM from weak-TF muSR in the presence of a large cell background needs to be documented. Methods A states that only about 50% of implanted muons stop in the sample. Yet VM reaches about 0.97 at 0 GPa (Fig. 2d), so a background fraction must have been determined and subtracted, and Eq. (5), VM = 1 - P_TF(0,T), as written assumes no background. The paper does not say how the background was fixed, whether it was assumed independent of stress (the sample and mask positions can shift slightly as force is applied), or how "baseline asymmetry shifts" were "corrected" (p. 9). Because the factor-2.5 drop in VM is a primary result, the authors should give the fit model with the background term, the parameters held fixed, the background fraction at each stress, and a cross-check of VM from the ZF fits (Eq. 3) against the wTF values. They should also state whether the 0 GPa muSR point was measured in the cell after mounting.

6. The claim of the "highest bulk Tc reported in the 214 cuprate family" (p. 7; Fig. 4d) is not supported as stated. Tc,zero from in-plane resistance is the paper's own measure of 2D/filamentary superconductivity, not bulk; the bulk measure, Tc,mid from susceptibility, is 35 K, below optimal LSCO. The onset of about 46 K is taken from a resistive downturn whose definition is not given. The comparison is with LSCO data from Ref. 32 (1989), with no stated matching of onset criteria. Higher Tc values have been reported in the 214 family, for example in compressively strained LSCO films (about 45-49 K) and in oxygen-intercalated La2CuO4+δ, and should be discussed. The authors should define the onset criterion, use the same criterion for the LSCO reference data, and restrict "bulk" to the susceptibility (and, ideally, superfluid density) results.

7. The zero-stress reference states are uncertain:
   - Tc,zero at "0 GPa" for x = 0.125 is about 23-25 K (Figs. 3e, 4a), noticeably higher than the in-plane zero-resistance temperatures usually reported for LBCO-1/8. This suggests residual strain from epoxy mounting and differential thermal contraction in the cell.
   - For x = 0.115 and 0.135 the "Peak (%)" scale is normalized to a tensile point rather than to zero stress (Figs. 3d, 3f), which suggests the nominal zero is not strain-free.
   - The zero-stress susceptibility curve in Fig. 2b (Tc,mid of about 3 K) was measured ex situ in a dc field of 0.5 mT before mounting, while all other curves are in situ ac data. Mounting strain could therefore account for part of the large step between "0" and 0.09 GPa.

   The authors should show in situ zero-force susceptibility and resistivity, explain how zero stress was established in each cell, and compare the zero-stress Tc,zero with the same crystals measured free-standing.

8. Several interpretive statements go beyond what is measured:
   - "Spin-stripe order remains long range" (p. 4): muSR coherent oscillations show a well-defined local field distribution, not a long correlation length. Neutron diffraction under stress, or appropriate softening of the wording, is needed.
   - Non-magnetic regions host "dynamically fluctuating stripes": no dynamical probe is reported. The relaxation rate λnm of the non-magnetic fraction versus stress, or LF-muSR, would at least constrain this.
   - Stress "enhances pairing" and works "primarily at the level of phase coherence" via restored interlayer Josephson coupling and a PDW-to-d-wave crossover: no c-axis transport, no measure of anisotropy, and no gap or pairing-scale measurement is presented. c-axis resistivity under stress, or the ratio of resistive to susceptibility transitions, would test the interlayer-frustration scenario directly.
   - The title speaks of the "stripe state", but charge order is not measured at all.

   The authors should either add the relevant measurements or recast these points as hypotheses.

9. Reproducibility and stress protocol are not described:
   - Each doping and technique appears to rest on a single crystal, with no second sample and no reversibility or hysteresis data on releasing stress.
   - The paper does not state at which temperature the stress was changed. Stress applied above T_LTT selects twin and LTLO domains differently from stress applied at base temperature.
   - It does not say whether the crystals were thermally cycled between stress points.

   Given the strong sensitivity to small stresses and the possibility of plastic deformation or epoxy creep, the authors should state the protocol, show at least one stress-release (return-to-zero) measurement, and ideally reproduce the key x = 0.125 result on a second crystal.

10. The Introduction (p. 2-3) states that muSR, ac susceptibility and resistivity were "performed on the same crystal". The Methods describe a 7 x 5.1 x 0.3 mm^3 crystal in the muSR device and separately mounted samples in the FC100 cell, which must have different dimensions. The authors should clarify which crystal(s) were used for each measurement, give their dimensions and growth batches, and give the uncertainty in the stress for the resistivity samples (width, thickness, epoxy strain transfer).

C. Minor

11. The captions of Figs. 2b and 2d say "up to 0.32 GPa", but the legends stop at 0.23 GPa. Fig. 2c shows ZF spectra only up to 0.18 GPa, while the text says coherent oscillations persist "up to the maximum applied stress of 0.23 GPa", and Methods give a maximum of 0.32 GPa. The authors should state which stresses were measured with which technique and show the 0.23 (and 0.32) GPa ZF spectra.

12. The quoted numbers disagree in places:
   - The abstract says VM is reduced by "about a factor of two"; the text says 2.5.
   - The abstract says Tc rises "from 5 K to 37 K under 0.5 GPa"; the text says Tc,mid rises from 3 K to 35 K.
   - Tso is "approximately 40 K" at ambient in the text but about 42 K in Fig. 4a.
   - "From 40 K to 33 K within 0.15 GPa" (p. 6) conflicts with 33 K being reached at 0.23 GPa (p. 3).
   - The susceptibility onset is quoted as 37 K in the text but appears near 38 K in Fig. 4d.

   The authors should make all of these consistent.

13. The Fig. 3 caption states that a hump of "a few percent" remains at the highest stress for x = 0.125 and 0.135, but "it was set to zero". Measured values should be plotted as measured, with uncertainty. The abstract's statement that the peak is "fully suppressed across all dopings" should be qualified accordingly.

14. The text calls the rise of Tc,mid "nearly linear" (p. 3), but Figs. 2b and 4a show a jump of about 24 K between 0 and 0.09 GPa followed by saturation above about 0.15 GPa. The wording should match the data.

15. Error bars on Tc, Tso, VM and stress are not explained. The authors should state how they were obtained (fit uncertainties, transition-width criteria) and include the uncertainty of the force-to-stress conversion (1 μV ≈ 30 N).

16. Editorial points:
   - A citation is missing after "x=0.115?" (p. 2), and a figure reference after "ZF-μSR signals shown in were" (p. 9).
   - Supplementary Notes 1-3 and Supplementary Fig. S1 are referenced but not provided with the submission.
   - "Tc,zero" is typeset inconsistently.
   - "Data available upon request" falls short of current journal norms; the authors should deposit the muSR histograms and transport data.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a finite-element map of the stress in the muSR sample geometry and a stress-release measurement. Together these would separate intrinsic phase coexistence from stress gradients (concerns 1, 9).
2. Measure TF-muSR superfluid density at two or three stresses to establish bulk superconducting volume and test whether superconductivity coexists with, or is separated from, the static magnetic regions (concern 2).
3. Extend resistivity on x = 0.115 and 0.135 to the same maximum stress as x = 0.125, measured in the same cell. Present Tc,zero and Tc,mid against stress for all three dopings on a common, calibrated axis (concern 4).
4. Add a direct structural probe of the LTT superlattice under stress at x = 0.125, or a careful dR/dT analysis of the T_LTT anomaly, so that LTT suppression is shown rather than inferred from a hump entangled with superconducting fluctuations (concern 3).
