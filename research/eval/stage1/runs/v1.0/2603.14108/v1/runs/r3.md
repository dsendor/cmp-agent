Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript applies in-plane uniaxial stress at 45 degrees to the Cu-O bond to La2-xBaxCuO4 at x = 0.125, the composition where static stripe order is strongest and bulk Tc is lowest. It measures zero-field and weak-transverse-field muSR, in situ ac susceptibility and in-plane resistivity, and adds resistivity at x = 0.115 and 0.135. The headline results are a rise of Tc,mid from about 3 K to about 35 K by 0.23 GPa, a 2.5-fold drop in the static magnetic volume fraction while Tso and Bint change little, and in-plane zero resistance at 37 K (onset 46 K) by 0.56 GPa. If the results are robust, combining x = 1/8 with a local magnetic probe under stress is a real step beyond the authors' earlier x = 0.115 and 0.135 studies, and it bears directly on the PDW and interlayer-frustration picture. As the paper stands, though, the strongest claims ("optimal superconductivity", "highest bulk Tc in the 214 family", LTT suppression as the cause) rest on measurements whose geometry, stress calibration and structural assignment are not yet secure.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Muon-spin rotation under uniaxial stress at x = 1/8 is technically demanding and new. The ZF spectra (Fig. 2c) and wTF volume fractions (Fig. 2d) give a microscopic, volume-resolved view that bulk probes cannot.
- The paper separates the ordering temperature and local moment (nearly unchanged, Fig. 4a,c) from the ordered volume fraction (strongly reduced, Figs. 2d, 4b). This is a non-trivial, testable statement about how stress acts on stripe order.
- The stress scale is very small (about 0.1-0.5 GPa) compared with hydrostatic pressure (14 GPa for about 18 K), and the effect on Tc is very large. Both points are interesting in their own right.
- Resistivity at three dopings (Fig. 3) gives a consistent qualitative picture: the normal-state hump is suppressed while Tc,zero rises.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The 0 GPa and finite-stress susceptibility data come from different field geometries, so the "elevenfold enhancement of 3D superconductivity" is not established.
   - Where: Section II and Fig. 2b; Methods A.
   - The text says the ambient-pressure curve in Fig. 2b was measured before mounting, in a dc field parallel to the CuO2 planes, "such that the induced shielding currents flow between the layers", and that this is what makes it sensitive to 3D superconductivity. The in situ ac excitation under stress is said to be "predominantly along the c-axis". That drives in-plane shielding currents, which respond to 2D superconductivity as well. The authors' own resistivity puts 2D superconductivity near 20-25 K at zero stress (Figs. 3e, 4a).
   - Why it matters: part of the jump from Tc,mid = 3 K at 0 GPa to 28 K at 0.09 GPa may come from switching from an interlayer-sensitive to an in-plane-sensitive measurement, not from restored 3D coherence. The same problem applies to the "3D SC" region of Fig. 4a and to the "Tc,3D" line in Fig. 1d.
   - What to do:
     - Show the in situ ac susceptibility at 0 GPa in the stress cell, with the same coil and excitation, after cooldown with the sample glued.
     - Ideally also measure with the field in the plane under stress, or measure c-axis resistivity under stress.
     - State explicitly whether each curve in Fig. 2b is normalized to -1. If so, give the unnormalized shielding signal, so readers can judge whether the shielding fraction is bulk at every stress.

2. The stress in the muSR cell is unlikely to be uniform, and that alone could explain the partial loss of magnetic volume fraction.
   - Where: Fig. 2a; Methods A.
   - The crystal is 7 x 5.1 x 0.3 mm3, glued with Stycast along the stress axis, and only a 4 x 5.1 mm2 area is exposed. The free length is therefore shorter than the width. In that geometry the stress is strongly inhomogeneous across the sample and especially near the jaws, and the force-over-area value (480 N / 1.53 mm2 = 0.32 GPa) is at best an average. The force calibration ("1 microvolt corresponds to about 30 N") carries no stated uncertainty, and the stress values have no error bars anywhere.
   - Why it matters: a spread of local stresses would naturally give the central muSR observation. Some regions lose static order, others keep it with an almost unchanged Tso and Bint (Figs. 2d, 4c). Read this way, the result is an apparatus effect, not the intrinsic "mixed static/dynamic stripe state" or LTT/LTLO phase coexistence the paper builds its interpretation on (Section II, p. 7). It would also explain the factor of about 2 between the stress scales seen in the muSR cell and in the FC100 cell (concern 3).
   - What to do:
     - Provide finite-element modelling of the stress distribution over the exposed area for this geometry and glue thickness.
     - Quote the fraction of the muon-illuminated volume within, say, 20% of the nominal stress.
     - Estimate the stress uncertainty.
     - Ideally, check the result with a thinner, longer sample, or with a measurement whose stress distribution is known (for example a strain gauge or digital image correlation on a test sample).

3. The correlation between LTT suppression and the magnetic volume fraction is drawn across two apparatus, two samples and two stress calibrations, and has no direct structural evidence behind it.
   - Where: Figs. 3, 4a, 4b; abstract; Conclusion.
   - Fig. 4b plots VM from the piezoelectric muSR cell on the same stress axis as the resistive "LTT peak" from the Razorbill FC100. These are different mountings, and presumably different pieces of crystal. The introduction says the measurements were "performed on the same crystal"; this should be clarified. The resistive hump is a signature of charge-stripe pinning, not a structural measurement. No diffraction under stress is shown for x = 0.125, although the authors cite stress-dependent X-ray work at x = 1/8 (Ref. 30) and their own diffraction at x = 0.135.
   - The data also show a tension. Tc,mid saturates near 35 K by about 0.15-0.23 GPa (Figs. 2b, 4a), while the resistive LTT peak at x = 0.125 is still 50-70% of its ambient value at those stresses and vanishes only near 0.5 GPa (Fig. 3e).
   - Why it matters: the conclusion that "suppression of the LTT phase is essential" and that the enhanced Tc lives in a "stress-induced LTLO phase" (p. 7) needs (i) a structural probe and (ii) a common stress scale. The tension above argues against bulk 3D superconductivity requiring full LTT suppression.
   - What to do:
     - Provide structural evidence (X-ray or neutron superlattice peaks distinguishing LTT from LTLO) for x = 0.125 under stress, or compare quantitatively with Ref. 30.
     - Cross-calibrate the two cells, for example by measuring Tc,mid or the resistive hump of the same sample in both, and justify plotting them on one axis.
     - Discuss explicitly why Tc,mid saturates while the hump is still large.

4. The claims that x = 0.125 "develops the highest Tc" and reaches "the highest bulk Tc reported in the 214 family" are not supported at the needed level.
   - Where: title, abstract, Fig. 4d, p. 5 and p. 7.
   - (i) The 46 K value is a resistive onset and the 37 K value is in-plane zero resistance. Neither is a bulk measure. The bulk Tc,mid is 35 K, and it was followed only to 0.23 GPa.
   - (ii) The doping comparison uses one sample per doping, measured over different stress ranges: x = 0.125 up to 0.58 GPa, x = 0.115 up to 0.23 GPa, x = 0.135 up to 0.29 GPa. The comparison of Tc,mid also leans on the authors' earlier experiments in a different cell (Refs. 6, 19). The differences are a few kelvin, the same order as plausible sample-to-sample and mounting spread.
   - (iii) Fig. 4d compares LBCO onsets with LSCO data from a single 1989 study (Ref. 32). It does not mention other 214 cuprates with Tc at or above about 40 K: oxygen-intercalated La2CuO4+y, and compressively strained LSCO films.
   - What to do:
     - Measure at least one further sample of each doping over matched stress ranges and show the spread.
     - Base the doping comparison on bulk (susceptibility) Tc.
     - Soften or properly reference the "highest Tc in the 214 family" and "optimal" statements, and state the onset criterion used for the LSCO comparison.

B. Should address

5. The extraction of VM depends on background and normalization choices that are not shown.
   - Where: Methods A, C, D.
   - About 50% of muons stop in the sample. The rest stop in hematite masks, which depolarize quickly and so look like "magnetic" volume in wTF, or in the Ti holder, which behaves as non-magnetic.
   - Methods D says that "baseline asymmetry shifts were corrected by allowing PTF(t) to be adjusted for each temperature". This is exactly the kind of freedom that can move VM.
   - Why it matters: the 2.5-fold reduction of VM is a central result. A small change in sample position or beam overlap as the cell deforms would change the background split.
   - What to do:
     - Give the fitted background fractions and asymmetries for each stress, and state which parameters were fixed and which were free in Eqs. (3)-(5).
     - Show raw (not A0-normalized) wTF amplitudes above and below Tso.
     - Show that the background fraction does not depend on stress, for example from high-temperature calibration at each stress.

6. The percolation picture conflicts with the data at 0.09 GPa, and the superconducting volume is not measured.
   - Where: p. 7.
   - The text argues that (1 - VM) of about 20% at 0.1 GPa "is likely insufficient for percolation" and that Tc saturates only when (1 - VM) reaches about 40%. Yet Fig. 2b shows Tc,mid of about 28 K already at 0.09 GPa, where VM is 0.84 (Fig. 4b), so most of the sample is still magnetic.
   - Why it matters: either superconductivity coexists with static order inside the magnetic regions, or the susceptibility is dominated by filamentary paths. Both possibilities undercut the picture of uniform d-wave patches confined to non-magnetic regions.
   - What to do: since the muSR set-up is available, measure the TF-muSR superfluid density, or the field-cooled diamagnetic shift, versus stress. This would show directly which volume is superconducting and whether 1/lambda^2 tracks (1 - VM).

7. Several interpretive statements go beyond what was measured.
   - "Spin-stripe order remains long range" (abstract, p. 4): muSR shows well-defined local fields, not a correlation length. Long-range order needs neutron or X-ray data.
   - The introduction promises to track "the evolution of the charge stripe order", but no charge-order measurement is presented. The resistive hump is an indirect proxy.
   - Conversion of static stripes to dynamic ones, and enhanced pairing by stripe interactions, are inferred from the absence of signal ("invisible to muSR on its characteristic timescale"). That is equally consistent with the stripes simply melting.
   - "Shifts the balance from a putative PDW-dominated regime" is not tested by any measurement here.
   - What to do: rephrase these as hypotheses, or add evidence that discriminates them, for example ZF/LF muSR relaxation rates in the non-magnetic fraction as a sign of slow fluctuations, or a diffraction correlation length.

8. Reproducibility, reversibility and the zero of stress are not documented.
   - For each doping, give the number of samples measured.
   - Show whether Tc, VM and the resistive hump return to their initial values when the stress is released. Plastic deformation, cracking or slipping of the epoxy at 0.5-0.6 GPa would mimic an irreversible change.
   - Explain how "0 GPa" is defined after cooldown. Differential thermal contraction against the Ti holder or the cell would give a built-in offset, and Fig. 3 already shows small tensile values (-0.03, -0.01 GPa).
   - Give the dimensions of the resistivity samples and the contact geometry. Current crowding and strain at the contacts matter for an in-plane hump measured under stress.

9. The hump amplitude at the highest stresses was set to zero.
   - Where: Fig. 3 caption.
   - For x = 0.125 and 0.135 "a small hump remains visible … amounting to only a few percent. However … it was set to zero."
   - This replaces a measured value with zero, and it is what supports the claim of "full suppression" (abstract: "fully suppressed across all dopings") and the zero points in Fig. 4b.
   - What to do: plot the measured values with uncertainties, and change the wording to "suppressed to a few percent".

10. The zero-stress Tc,zero at x = 0.125 looks high and should be explained.
    - Figs. 3e and 4a give Tc,zero of about 23-25 K at 0 GPa. Published in-plane zero-resistance temperatures for LBCO x = 1/8 crystals are typically lower (about 10-16 K).
    - A higher value would point to residual mounting strain, a slightly different composition, or a different resistance criterion, and any of these would affect the stress-axis comparison.
    - What to do: give the criterion used for Tc,zero (threshold or noise floor) and the ambient-pressure resistivity of the free-standing crystal.

11. The ordering temperature and internal field are not fully supported at the highest stresses.
    - The text says "coherent oscillations persist up to the maximum applied stress of 0.23 GPa", but Fig. 2c stops at 0.18 GPa, and Bint in Fig. 4c also stops at 0.18 GPa.
    - At 0.18 and 0.23 GPa the VM(T) curves in Fig. 2d tail off gradually to about 40 K, so "Tso decreases to about 33 K" depends on how Tso is defined. The definition should be stated, for example as an onset or as the midpoint of a fit.
    - What to do: show the 0.23 GPa ZF spectrum and its Bint, and give the Tso extraction method with uncertainties.

12. The stressed phase diagram in Fig. 1d is presented as if it were measured, but it is largely schematic.
    - At 0.23 GPa only x = 0.125 has muSR data in this work. Doping coverage from 0.095 to 0.165 and the "Tc,3D ≈ Tso" line are extrapolations, or come from other studies.
    - What to do: mark the measured points and label the rest as schematic.

C. Minor

13. The numbers in the abstract do not match the main text.
    - Tc rises "from 5 K to 37 K under 0.5 GPa" in the abstract. The main text gives Tc,mid from 3 K to 35 K at 0.23 GPa, with 37 K as the susceptibility onset, and 37 K as Tc,zero only at 0.56 GPa.
    - The abstract reduces VM by "about a factor of two", the text by 2.5.
    - The abstract mixes bulk and in-plane quantities in one sentence. Align these.

14. The Fig. 2b and 2d captions say "up to 0.32 GPa", but the legends stop at 0.23 GPa. Either the data at higher stress are missing or the captions are wrong.

15. There are reference and text problems.
    - A broken citation ("x=0.115?") on p. 2.
    - "The ZF-muSR signals shown in were analyzed" in Methods C is missing its figure reference.
    - "Work at Brookhaven is is supported".
    - Supplementary Notes 1-3 and Fig. S1 are cited but were not available with the submission.

16. Eq. (3): describing Bint as "the maximum internal field associated with the Overhauser distribution", with a J0 Bessel lineshape, should be justified for the LBCO stripe structure, with a reference. The fraction f_alpha should also be reported versus stress, since it bears on the claimed robustness of local order.

17. Report the sample temperature calibration in the piezo-driven cell at 0.7 K, where piezo actuation can cause heating. Also give the excitation amplitude and frequency of the in situ ac coil.

18. Fig. 4d: state the onset criterion for both data sets. Use consistent symbols for LBCO "under stress", and state which stress value is plotted for each doping.

19. Data availability "upon request" is weak for a paper of this kind. Consider depositing the muSR histograms (for example via the PSI data archive) and the resistivity curves.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Do one experiment that removes the main ambiguities together: in situ ac susceptibility at 0 GPa in the cell, plus TF-muSR superfluid density versus stress on the same mounted crystal. This would establish the bulk 3D superconducting fraction and how it is shared between magnetic and non-magnetic regions.
2. Add a structural measurement under stress at x = 0.125, or a quantitative comparison with Ref. 30, together with finite-element stress maps for both cells. Then the VM-versus-LTT correlation can be plotted on a common, calibrated stress axis with error bars.
3. Repeat the resistivity at x = 0.115 and 0.135 to the same maximum stress as x = 0.125, on at least two samples per doping, including a stress-release cycle. Only then make the doping-selective "optimal Tc" claim.
4. Restructure the discussion so that what is measured (VM, Tso, Bint, Tc,mid, Tc,zero, the resistive hump) is clearly separated from what is inferred (dynamic stripes, PDW-to-d-wave crossover, pairing enhancement). Tone down the title and abstract accordingly.
