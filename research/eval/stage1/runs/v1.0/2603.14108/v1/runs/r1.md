Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports in-plane uniaxial stress, applied at 45° to the Cu–O bonds, on La2-xBaxCuO4 at x = 0.125, the composition where stripe order is most stable and bulk Tc is most strongly suppressed. It combines in-situ AC susceptibility and ZF/weak-TF μSR on one crystal with resistivity under stress on x = 0.115, 0.125 and 0.135. The headline results are: (i) the 3D Tc,mid of x = 0.125 rises from about 3 K to 35 K by about 0.23 GPa; (ii) the static magnetic volume fraction V_M falls by about 2.5 times while T_so and B_int change only modestly; (iii) the resistive LTT anomaly is suppressed; (iv) zero resistance is reached at 37 K and the resistive onset at about 46 K at 0.56 GPa. Extending earlier stress work on x = 0.115 and 0.135 to x = 1/8 itself is a natural and valuable step, and the μSR volume-fraction data under stress at 1/8 are new. However, the broader claims (stripe interactions enhance pairing, LTT suppression is essential, the highest bulk Tc in the 214 family) currently rest on indirect or cross-apparatus evidence.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The stress-induced rise of the 3D Tc at x = 1/8 is large, from about 3 K to 35 K (Fig. 2b). It is measured in situ in the same cell and on the same crystal as the μSR data, so Tc and V_M can be compared directly.
- Volume fraction and ordered moment are tracked separately (Fig. 2c,d; Fig. 4b,c). They show that stress mainly reduces the static-order volume, not the local moment or T_so. This is an informative microscopic distinction that diffraction alone does not give.
- Resistivity on three dopings under stress gives a consistent picture: the normal-state anomaly is suppressed as Tc,zero rises (Fig. 3). This places x = 1/8 next to its neighbours.
- The topic (intertwined orders, PDW and interlayer frustration at 1/8) is of broad interest. A strain-tuned route from the most suppressed to a near-optimal Tc in LBCO is an attractive result.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The bulk character of the stress-induced superconductivity, and where it sits relative to the magnetic volume, is not established, yet the percolation picture depends on it. At 0.09 GPa, V_M at base temperature is about 0.84 (Fig. 2d), so only about 16% of the sample is non-magnetic. Yet χ_AC at 0.09 GPa reaches full shielding (≈ −1) with Tc,mid ≈ 27 K (Fig. 2b), already roughly 80% of the final enhancement. Just below Tc,mid at this stress, V_M(27 K) is still about 0.75. The text (p. 7) states that at 0.1 GPa the non-magnetic fraction (≈20%) "is likely insufficient for percolation", and that Tc only saturates once 1 − V_M approaches 40%. This is not consistent with the near-complete enhancement already seen at 0.09 GPa. Full diamagnetic shielding can come from a thin percolating network or a surface layer, so it does not measure superconducting volume. As it stands, the data cannot tell apart:
   - (a) superconductivity confined to the non-magnetic regions (the authors' picture);
   - (b) superconductivity coexisting microscopically with static stripe order;
   - (c) filamentary superconductivity.

   What the authors should do:
   - Measure a volume-sensitive probe of the superconducting state: TF-μSR in a field above Hc1, field-cooled, at several stresses (0, 0.09, 0.23 GPa), giving the superfluid density and the superconducting volume fraction. The same cell and beamline already allow this.
   - At a minimum, report the absolute (unnormalised) shielding fraction at each stress.
   - Rewrite the percolation argument so that it matches the 0.09 GPa data.

2. Data from two different stress apparatuses and different crystals are combined on one stress axis without cross-calibration, and the manuscript describes the sample basis inconsistently.
   - The Introduction (pp. 2–3) states that μSR, AC susceptibility and resistivity were "performed on the same crystal under uniaxial stress". The Methods describe something else:
     - μSR and ACS used a piezoelectric cell with a 7 × 5.1 × 0.3 mm³ crystal glued to a Ti holder, with stress inferred from a force-sensor calibration (1 µV ≈ 30 N).
     - Resistivity used a Razorbill FC100 in a PPMS with separately mounted crystals, whose dimensions are not given.
   - Fig. 4a overlays T_so and Tc,mid (μSR cell) with Tc,zero (FC100). Fig. 4b plots V_M and the LTT peak height on a common σ axis and states they are "directly correlated".
   - Nominal stress (force divided by cross-section) can differ substantially from the stress the sample actually carries. Causes include epoxy compliance, different free lengths and aspect ratios, and differential thermal contraction between Ti, the epoxy and the sample. So the apparent tracking in Fig. 4b, and the convergence of Tc,zero with Tc,mid at about 0.15 GPa in Fig. 4a, may partly reflect calibration rather than physics.

   What the authors should do:
   - State unambiguously which crystal was measured in which cell.
   - Give the resistivity-sample dimensions, epoxy thickness and free length.
   - Provide a common reference for the two stress scales, for example Tc,mid or the resistive Tc measured on one crystal in both cells, or a direct strain measurement (strain gauge, capacitive displacement or X-ray lattice parameter) in each.
   - Include an estimate of the stress-transmission efficiency, for example from finite-element modelling.

3. LTT suppression and the formation of an "LTLO" phase are inferred only from the size of a resistive hump. No structural measurement is made on x = 0.125 under stress, and the hump data have been modified.
   - The abstract and conclusion state that stress suppresses the LTT phase and stabilises an LTLO phase. Fig. 1d labels the whole 0.23 GPa phase diagram "LTLO".
   - The only evidence presented for x = 0.125 is the height of the normal-state resistivity peak (Fig. 3b,e). In LBCO near 1/8 the LTT transition coincides with charge-stripe ordering. A reduced hump is equally consistent with weaker charge-order pinning or a smaller charge-ordered volume inside a structure that is still LTT, with a shift of T_LTT, or with a change of the transport anisotropy under stress.
   - The Fig. 3 caption states that for x = 0.125 and 0.135 "a small hump … remains visible even at the highest applied stress, amounting to only a few percent. However, in panels (e) and (f), it was set to zero." The 0% points in Fig. 3e,f and Fig. 4b, and the abstract's statement that the LTT peak is "fully suppressed across all dopings", rest on this choice.
   - It is also unclear how ΔR_LTT is defined: the arrow in Fig. 3a runs from a dashed line at the zero-stress peak to the curve.

   What the authors should do:
   - Report the measured residual hump with its uncertainty, not zero, and correct the abstract.
   - Give an explicit definition of ΔR_LTT and of the normalisation to 100%.
   - Show T_LTT as a function of stress.
   - Either support the LTT → LTLO assignment for x = 0.125 with diffraction under stress (Ref. 30 is cited as having done stress-dependent X-ray diffraction at x = 1/8, and its findings should be compared explicitly), or phrase the conclusion in terms of the resistive anomaly.

4. The headline Tc claims mix 2D and 3D criteria and resistive and magnetic onsets, and the comparison with the rest of the 214 family is overstated.
   - The abstract says Tc rises "from 5 K to 37 K under 0.5 GPa". However:
     - The 5 K is the ambient onset or midpoint from susceptibility (3D). The text gives 3 K for Tc,mid.
     - The 37 K is the zero-resistance temperature at 0.56 GPa. The authors themselves describe in-plane Tc,zero as reflecting the 2D transition (p. 5).
     - The highest 3D Tc,mid actually measured is about 35 K at about 0.23 GPa, because susceptibility was not measured above that stress.
   - The 46 K "onset" at 0.56 GPa has no stated criterion. In Fig. 3b the downturn begins on the shoulder of the residual LTT/charge-order hump, and at ambient pressure LBCO-1/8 already shows a resistive drop setting in near 40 K from 2D correlations. The onset is therefore sensitive to how the normal-state background is defined.
   - Fig. 4d compares this resistive onset with LSCO data from a 1989 reference (Ref. 32), using a different criterion and sample type.
   - The statement (p. 7) that this is "the highest bulk Tc reported in the 214 family" does not account for oxygen-doped La2CuO4+δ (Tc of about 40–45 K) or epitaxially strained LSCO films (Tc of about 45–50 K).

   What the authors should do:
   - Quote stress-matched numbers with a stated criterion: 3D Tc,mid from χ, and Tc,zero and onset from ρ, each with its stress.
   - Define the onset criterion, for example the intersection with an extrapolated normal-state background or a fixed percentage drop.
   - If possible, measure χ_AC at 0.5 GPa, or soften "bulk" and "highest in the 214 family".
   - Compare with the relevant literature values.

B. Should address

5. The geometry of the in-situ susceptibility does not obviously measure the 3D transition, and the normalisation of χ_AC is not stated.
   - Page 3 explains that the pre-mounting DC measurement used H ∥ CuO2 planes, so that shielding currents flow between layers and the signal is sensitive to 3D superconductivity.
   - For the in-situ AC measurement, however, "the excitation field was applied predominantly along the c-axis". Shielding currents then flow within the planes, which 2D superconductivity can carry.
   - All curves in Fig. 2b appear normalised to −1. It is unclear whether the 0 GPa curve is the pre-mounting DC curve or an in-situ AC curve.

   What the authors should do:
   - Specify the coil geometry and field direction relative to the crystal axes.
   - State which curve comes from which measurement.
   - Give the normalisation, for example to the zero-stress low-temperature value with a demagnetisation correction.
   - Justify identifying Tc,mid from this configuration with the 3D transition, for example by showing that at zero stress the in-situ midpoint matches the DC H ∥ ab value of about 3 K.

6. Several aspects of how V_M is extracted under stress are not specified, and they directly affect the central μSR result: the 0.97 → 0.38 reduction.
   - Only about 50% of muons stop in the sample (Methods A). The rest stop in hematite (rapid depolarisation), the Ti holder and the epoxy (non-magnetic, precessing at the applied field).
   - V_M(T) = 1 − P_TF(0,T) (Eq. 5) is only correct after subtracting the non-magnetic background. The description that "baseline asymmetry shifts were corrected by allowing P_TF(t) to be adjusted for each temperature" suggests a free parameter that varies with temperature.
   - Any change of the background or the stopping fraction with stress (sample or epoxy displacement, cell deformation) would appear as a change in V_M.

   What the authors should do:
   - Give the background fraction and how it was determined, for example from a run above T_so or with a dummy holder.
   - State whether it was fixed across temperature and stress, and list which parameters in Eqs. 3–4 were free.
   - Show raw weak-TF asymmetries for 0 and 0.23 GPa.
   - Check that V_M from the ZF fits (Eq. 3) agrees with V_M from weak TF.

7. Stress inhomogeneity is not considered, yet it offers an alternative explanation for the "inhomogeneous state". The crystal is a wide, thin plate (5.1 mm × 0.3 mm cross-section) glued along a 7 mm length, and μSR samples the central 4 × 5.1 mm². Epoxy-mounted plates of this aspect ratio typically show strong gradients of stress along the length and across the thickness, and they risk bending. A distribution of local stress would on its own produce a fractional V_M and broad magnetic transitions (the 0.18 and 0.23 GPa curves in Fig. 2d have long tails), with no intrinsic phase separation. The authors interpret V_M < 1 as coexisting LTT and LTLO domains (p. 7). They should:
   - provide a finite-element estimate of the stress distribution over the region the muons see;
   - check for bending;
   - discuss whether the fractional volume could reflect a distribution of stress rather than intrinsic phase coexistence.

8. The zero-stress reference states differ from known ambient behaviour, which suggests residual mounting strain or a different criterion.
   - In Fig. 3d–f the zero-stress Tc,zero is about 21 K (x = 0.115), about 23 K (x = 0.125) and about 20 K (x = 0.135). Fig. 4a puts x = 0.125 at about 25 K.
   - So at "0 GPa" the 1/8 sample has the highest Tc,zero of the three, which contradicts its description as the "lowest ambient-pressure Tc" composition. It is also well above the in-plane zero-resistance temperature usually reported for unstressed LBCO-1/8 (about 16 K).
   - Differential thermal contraction between sample, epoxy and holder can apply stress at nominally zero force.

   What the authors should do:
   - Show ρ(T) for each crystal before mounting or in a free-standing configuration.
   - State the criterion for Tc,zero.
   - Discuss the uncertainty in the zero of stress in both cells.

9. The comparison across dopings, which underpins "stripe-related interactions enhance pairing", is made at mismatched stresses and with mixed probes.
   - x = 0.125 was taken to 0.58 GPa in resistivity. x = 0.115 and 0.135 were taken only to 0.23 and 0.29 GPa.
   - The values of about 30 K for x = 0.115 and 0.135 combine present resistivity with susceptibility from earlier papers (Refs. 6, 19) on different crystals and cells.
   - At matched stress (about 0.2–0.23 GPa), Fig. 3d,e gives Tc,zero of about 31 K (x = 0.115) and about 33 K (x = 0.125). A 2–3 K difference measured on single crystals in different mounts does not support a strong mechanistic claim.
   - Without stripe-related physics, the underlying Tc dome of LBCO would anyway be expected to lie close to its maximum near x ≈ 0.125–0.15.

   What the authors should do:
   - Extend x = 0.115 and 0.135 to comparable stresses, or compare at equal stress.
   - Give error bars that reflect variation between samples.
   - Present the pairing-enhancement interpretation as a hypothesis.

10. Dynamic stripe correlations, PDW physics and d-wave patches are invoked but not measured. The Discussion (p. 7) and the Abstract state that stress converts static stripes into dynamic ones and moves the system from a PDW-dominated state to a uniform d-wave state. None of the measurements is sensitive to dynamic stripes, PDW order or the pairing symmetry, as the authors themselves note for μSR. The authors should do one of the following:
   - point to data that bear on these claims: the stress dependence of λ_L/λ_T or of the relaxation rate of the non-magnetic fraction, which could indicate slow fluctuations, or loss of the 2D/3D separation as a PDW signature;
   - reframe them as possible interpretations, and remove "demonstrate" from the abstract.

11. Reproducibility, reversibility and the stress range of the μSR data need to be documented.
   - Each result rests on one crystal per doping and per technique.
   - There is no evidence that Tc, V_M or the resistive hump recover when stress is released, so plastic deformation, cracking or epoxy creep cannot be ruled out (a cracked sample could also contribute to a resistive drop).
   - The text states that "coherent oscillations persist up to the maximum applied stress of 0.23 GPa" (p. 3). However, ZF spectra (Fig. 2c) and B_int (Fig. 4c) are shown only up to 0.18 GPa, and the last point has a large error bar.

   What the authors should do:
   - Show an unloaded or partially unloaded measurement after the highest stress.
   - Show the ZF spectrum at 0.23 GPa.
   - If possible, repeat the key x = 0.125 resistivity result on a second crystal.

12. T_so for the broad high-stress transitions is not defined. In Fig. 2d the V_M(T) curves for 0.18 and 0.23 GPa decay gradually and remain non-zero up to about 35–40 K, yet T_so is quoted as 33 K (Fig. 4a). The conclusion that T_so "decreases only modestly" depends on how a broad transition is defined. The authors should state the criterion (onset, midpoint or fit to a distribution) and apply it uniformly.

C. Minor

13. The Fig. 2b and 2d captions state "up to 0.32 GPa", but the legends end at 0.23 GPa. The Methods derive a maximum of 0.32 GPa. Please make these consistent, or show the 0.32 GPa data.

14. The abstract says V_M is reduced "by about a factor of two". The text says "a factor of 2.5" (0.97 → 0.38). The abstract also says the ambient Tc is 5 K, while the text gives Tc,mid = 3 K.

15. Page 5 says the LTT anomaly of x = 0.135 is suppressed at about 0.15 GPa. Fig. 3f still shows a peak of about 10–20% at 0.1 and 0.2 GPa, which reaches zero only at 0.29 GPa, the point the caption says was set to zero.

16. Fig. 1d presents an entire phase diagram at 0.23 GPa (T_LTO line, LTLO region, Tc,3D ≈ T_so) for 0.09 < x < 0.17. Please state which data points it is built from. Otherwise label it schematic.

17. Further minor errors in the text and methods:
   - A citation is broken on p. 2 ("x=0.115?").
   - Methods C says "The ZF-μSR signals shown in were analyzed", with the figure reference missing.
   - The notation alternates between V_m and V_M.
   - "is is supported" appears in the Acknowledgments.

18. The resistivity Methods do not give the following:
   - sample dimensions;
   - contact geometry;
   - the direction of the current relative to the stress axis;
   - the temperature sweep rate;
   - thermometer placement relative to the sample in the FC100.
   These matter for the onset and Tc,zero values and for comparison across dopings.

19. Fig. 4d should state the criterion used for each "onset" and the sample form (single crystal or polycrystal) for the LSCO reference data.

20. Data are available only "upon request". Given the central role of the fitted μSR parameters, depositing the raw μSR histograms and the resistivity and susceptibility curves would strengthen the paper.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add field-cooled TF-μSR (superfluid density and superconducting volume fraction) at zero, intermediate and maximum stress on the x = 0.125 crystal. This would directly test whether the stress-induced superconductivity is bulk and whether it lives in the non-magnetic volume, which is the core of the proposed picture.
2. Put the μSR and resistivity data on a common, verified stress scale: cross-calibrate the two cells on one crystal, or measure strain directly. Add finite-element estimates of stress homogeneity. Then the correlations in Fig. 4a,b would carry real weight.
3. Obtain, or compare in detail with, structural data under stress at x = 0.125 (for example the stress-dependent X-ray diffraction of Ref. 30). This would separate LTT suppression from weakened charge-order pinning, and is needed before the "LTLO" label in Fig. 1d and the text can stand.
4. Restate the headline numbers with stress-matched, criterion-specific values (3D Tc,mid; 2D Tc,zero; resistive onset). Moderate the claims of a record 214 Tc and of pairing enhancement accordingly.
