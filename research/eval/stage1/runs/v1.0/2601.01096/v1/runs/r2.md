Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports low-temperature STM/STS on the cleaved (100) surface of the candidate excitonic insulator Ta2Pd3Te5. Surface Te vacancies on four inequivalent Te chains produce a pair of resonances near the gap edges. The authors say these resonances are the excitonic analogue of impurity (Yu-Shiba-Rusinov-like) pair-breaking states. Their evidence is that the energies depend on defect type and tip-sample distance, that the electron and hole components share a spatial profile, that the peaks change with temperature, that vacancy dimers hybridize, and that a two-band mean-field model reproduces the spectra. Direct real-space spectroscopy of impurity states in an excitonic insulator, a prediction dating back to 1967, would be a significant result. The data on individual defects are of good quality. As written, however, the paper does not exclude conventional in-gap defect levels in a hybridization-gapped semiconductor. It also overstates what the data establish about the excitonic origin of the gap. A closely related study (Kwon et al., ref. 41) is acknowledged in a "Note added", which reduces the novelty somewhat.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Atomically resolved identification of four vacancy types, with tip decoration to resolve the five Te chains (Figs. 1c, 2a,b, S2). This allows site-specific statistics of the resonance energies (Fig. 2d).
- A clear, reproducible phenomenology: paired resonances whose energy depends on site and on tip-sample distance (Figs. 2c-e, S4/S5), strongly anisotropic spatial profiles (Fig. 3), and dimer splitting (Figs. S10, S11).
- A minimal two-band model with intra- and inter-band scattering. It gives an intuitive link between the scattering parameters (J1, J2) and the asymmetry and energy of the resonances (Fig. 2f, S6-S8).
- The observation of a second, lower-energy pair of in-gap states tied to spatial inhomogeneity (Fig. 4) is new and could be important if its origin is established.
- The topic is timely given the recent experimental activity on Ta2Pd3Te5 (refs. 6, 21-23).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The "subgap" states lie at or beyond the authors' own gap edges. In Fig. 1d the authors define the gap Δ ≈ 85 meV, from a hole-side onset near -35 mV to an electron-side coherence peak near +50 mV. Several numbers then conflict with this:
   - The most probable resonance separation for V_Te3 is 2δ_m = 104 meV, and the histograms extend to about 120 meV (Fig. 2d). This exceeds the full gap.
   - For V_Te2, the hole component E_h is mapped at -45 meV (Fig. 3f, 3g,h). That is below the hole-like onset of -35 mV, so E_h is not inside the gap.
   - In Fig. 2c the defect spectra show a hard, nearly zero-conductance region between the two resonances. Beyond the resonances they show no remnant of the host's +50 mV peak or of the hole-side onset.

   This lineshape looks more like a locally shifted or renormalized gap whose edges are enhanced than like bound states sitting inside an intact gap. Pair-breaking (YSR-type) states should sit at |E| < Δ, with the host coherence features still visible outside them. That is the central premise of the title.

   The authors should do four things. First, overlay defect and defect-free spectra on the same absolute scale over a wide bias range (±200 mV). Second, give E_e, E_h and the local gap edges for each vacancy type. Third, show for every defect that both resonances lie inside the local gap. Fourth, explain how a 104 meV separation fits into an 85 meV gap, or else revise the gap definition. If the answer depends on local doping or gap inhomogeneity (Fig. 4), the defect-free reference spectrum must be taken next to each defect.

2. The data do not distinguish excitonic pair breaking from ordinary impurity states in a hybridization-gapped two-band insulator. The Conclusion states that the results "confirm that the probed insulating gap at impurity-free region arises from the excitonic pairing". Every observation in the paper would also follow from a non-interacting two-band insulator with a static (for example lattice-driven) hybridization of the same size:
   - a pair of bound states with electron-hole mixed character;
   - a shared envelope for E_e and E_h (Fig. 3g,h);
   - energies set by intra- and inter-band scattering;
   - dimer bonding and antibonding splitting.

   In the model of Supplementary Sec. II, Δ0 enters as a fixed parameter, and in Fig. 4f as an imposed tanh profile. The model is therefore mathematically the same as a hybridization-gap model. Whether the Ta2Pd3Te5 gap is excitonic or structural/phonon-driven is precisely the open question in refs. 6, 21-23. Defect-bound states in a topological insulator (Ta2Pd3Te5 has been discussed as a quantum spin Hall and topological excitonic insulator, refs. 22, 25) are a further alternative to address.

   The authors should identify observables that separate the scenarios, and either test them or soften the claim. Options include:
   - a self-consistent calculation in which the order parameter is suppressed around the impurity. This would predict a measurable local gap suppression and healing length, which could be compared with dI/dV maps of the local gap edge around a vacancy;
   - the temperature evolution of the defect states toward the excitonic transition temperature, compared with that of the host gap;
   - the dependence on electron-hole imbalance, which is specific to excitonic order;
   - an explicit comparison with the hybridization-only model, showing which features it fails to reproduce.

   Without such a discriminating test, the title, abstract and conclusion should present pair breaking as one consistent interpretation, not a demonstration.

3. The tunability attributed to the tip electric field is not separated from tip-induced band bending or voltage division. The abstract says the energies are "continuously tunable by tip electric field". However, Fig. 2e (and Fig. 4e) change the setpoint current at fixed V_b = 0.1 V, up to 8 nA and 90 nA. This mainly changes the tip height, and so changes the local field, the tip-induced band bending (TIBB), the possible tip-induced charging of the defect, and possibly the local heating and mechanical tip-sample interaction. In a low-carrier-density gapped material, TIBB and the voltage division across a double-barrier junction (vacuum plus depletion region) shift and broaden localized-state resonances in exactly this way. The authors themselves find the fitted trend "unexpected" (p. 4): |J1,2| decreases as the tip approaches. They explain it post hoc with an opposite-sign tip potential.

   The authors should do the following:
   - vary the setpoint bias at fixed current, and the current at fixed bias, so that field and distance effects can be separated;
   - report whether the host gap edges in a defect-free region shift with I_t over the same range. Under TIBB they should; under a purely local pair-breaking mechanism they should not;
   - give I(z) curves or the apparent barrier height, so that I_t can be converted into tip height;
   - discuss whether the threshold behaviour at ≈25 nA (Fig. 4e) is a charging event.

B. Should address

4. The temperature dependence (Fig. 2g) is weak evidence. The text says the resonances decay faster than the Fermi-convolved 1.6 K spectrum, and attributes this to "local suppression of the excitonic gap" or "quantum pairing poison". Between 1.6 and 20 K, however, the host excitonic gap (of order 100 meV, with a transition well above 20 K) should hardly change. Several mundane effects can also broaden the peaks beyond Fermi convolution: changes in tip state or setpoint with temperature, drift, and a tip temperature that differs from the sample temperature.

   The authors should show:
   - defect-free spectra at the same temperatures, taken with the same tip;
   - the fitted peak width and energy against T, with uncertainties;
   - the lock-in modulation amplitude, and confirmation that the convolution includes it;
   - spectra to higher temperature, where a real link to the excitonic order would show up as a change tracking the host gap.

   The statement that the states "shift toward the gap center" should be quantified.

5. The model parameters are inconsistent from figure to figure and are not tied to the measured gap.
   - Fig. 2f uses Δ0 = 35 meV and μ = 12.25 meV.
   - Fig. 4d uses Δ0 = 45 meV for the higher-energy states and 18 meV for the lower-energy states, and flips the sign of J1 from -15 meV to +20 meV.
   - Fig. 4f uses Δ0 = 30 meV with x_c = 80 b0 (≈30 nm).
   - The measured gap is 85 meV.

   With Δ0, μ, J1 and J2 free for each panel, "qualitative" reproduction of peak positions and asymmetry is not a strong test. The authors should:
   - state which parameters are fixed by independent data (band structure, gap, doping) and which are fitted;
   - explain the factor of about 2 between Δ0 and Δ, and justify the sign change of J1 between regions;
   - justify the 30 nm healing length;
   - state whether the model's quasi-1D band dispersion, multi-orbital character and spin-orbit coupling match Ta2Pd3Te5.

   A single parameter set fitted to all four vacancy types, with J1 and J2 varying and Δ0 and μ fixed from the defect-free spectra, would be far more convincing.

6. The lower-energy subgap states (Fig. 4) have plausible non-excitonic origins. Their appearance above a threshold current (≳25 nA, Fig. 4e) and their low "surviving temperature" (Fig. S12) are also typical of tip-induced charging of a defect level. Charging gives a sharp, threshold-like peak and a characteristic ring in dI/dV maps whose radius depends on bias and tip height. The type-I/type-II classification rests on a shift of about 20 meV in a normal-state feature (Fig. 4b). Without measuring the local gap magnitude in both regions, this cannot be read uniquely as a change in electron-hole imbalance rather than in local band bending.

   The authors should do three things:
   - provide bias- and height-dependent dI/dV maps around a type-II defect, to test for charging rings;
   - report the local gap magnitude and gap center in type-I and type-II regions from many spectra;
   - give statistics on how many type-II defects show the lower-energy states.

7. The defect assignment and the defect-dependence statistics need strengthening. The vacancy assignment (Fig. S2) rests on topographic appearance alone, and V_Te1-V_Te4 are assumed to be vacancies rather than antisites, interstitials or adsorbates. The authors should support the assignment with DFT-simulated STM images or with controlled creation or annealing. For Fig. 2d, they should give the number of defects in each class. The distributions overlap substantially (for example V_Te4 and V_Te2), so "statistically distinct ranges" needs a quantitative test, such as uncertainties on 2δ_m or a two-sample test. They should also state whether the spread within a class is linked to the local type-I/II environment of Fig. 4, which could produce the width of the histograms. The claim that the ordering V_Te2 → V_Te4 → V_Te1 → V_Te3 reflects coupling strength currently has no microscopic support. Linking it to computed vacancy potentials, or to local orbital weight on each chain, would help.

8. The spatial maps may contain setpoint (normalization) effects. The dI/dV maps in Fig. 3d-f and the linecuts in Fig. 3g,h were presumably taken in constant-current mode at a fixed setpoint. With strong in-gap spectral weight at the defect, setpoint normalization alone can do two things. It can suppress the 60 meV map at the defect, which is the "spectral reversal" taken as spectral-weight transfer. It can also make the E_e and E_h profiles look alike. It is also unclear whether the profiles in Fig. 3g,h were normalized before the "collapse onto the same curve" was claimed.

   The authors should do three things. They should state the setpoint and normalization used. They should show constant-height maps, or maps at different setpoint biases. And they should present the raw linecuts, so the claimed locking of E_e and E_h is not an artifact of normalization.

9. The dimer evidence rests on one pair at each separation. Hybridization is inferred from one dimer at 1.3 nm (Fig. S10) and one at about 2 nm (Fig. S11). Coupling between bound states should depend systematically on separation, and, for this quasi-1D material, on orientation (along b versus c). The authors should show several dimers at several separations and orientations, report the splitting against distance, and compare it with the anisotropic decay lengths in Fig. 3g,h.

10. The non-magnetic character of the impurity is not tested. The model assumes a non-magnetic impurity and a singlet EI, and p. 5-6 argues that both magnetic and non-magnetic impurities break excitonic pairs. A Te vacancy may carry a local moment depending on its charge state. Spectra in an applied magnetic field (Zeeman splitting or shift of E_e and E_h) would test this assumption. They would also bear directly on the singlet versus triplet question the authors raise. If no field is available, the authors should at least discuss the expected charge and spin state of the vacancy.

11. Reproducibility across samples and tips is not documented. The main text does not say how many crystals, cleaves and tips were used, or whether the resonance energies for a given vacancy type agree between them. Because tip state affects these spectra strongly (tip decoration is used deliberately), the authors should state that the key spectra and statistics were reproduced with different tips and samples.

C. Minor

12. In Fig. 4b and 4c the legends show "Type-I" as a gray line, while the plotted curves and arrows are red. The colours should match.

13. The Fig. 4a caption says the dashed curve outlines regions of "relatively stronger e-h balance". The text describes type-II regions as having stronger e-h imbalance. The caption should state unambiguously which region is which.

14. The text cites the tip-dependence data as "[Figs. 2(e) and S4]", while the Fig. 2e caption points to "Fig. S5" for the other vacancies. The supplementary figure references should be checked.

15. The gap estimate mixes an onset (hole side) with a peak (electron side), and the result is compared with ARPES at 20 K. The authors should define the gap consistently, give an uncertainty, and say where the gap center lies relative to E_F. That value is also needed to judge whether E_e and E_h are placed symmetrically about the gap center, as a Bogoliubov-like pair would be.

16. Given ref. 41, a short comparison would help readers: what is the same and what differs in the observations, the material, and the interpretation.

17. Several language issues need correcting:
   - "predicated impurity states" should read "predicted";
   - "formulism" should read "formalism";
   - "Yu-Yhiba-Rusinov" in ref. 37 should read "Yu-Shiba-Rusinov";
   - "the strongly electron–hole imbalanced region" needs rephrasing;
   - the claim that BEC in cold atoms occurs at "~μK", while EIs promise ~10^2 K, would benefit from a more careful framing.

18. Fig. 2g: the authors should state how the Fermi convolution was done (sample-only or tip-and-sample broadening, and whether lock-in broadening was included). They should also say whether the 1.6 K reference was taken with the same tip and setpoint.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Build the paper around one discriminating test of excitonic versus hybridization-gap physics. One option is the evolution of the defect states and the host gap across temperature, up toward the reported transition. Another is a self-consistent calculation predicting a local gap suppression and healing length around a vacancy, tested against gap maps near defects.
2. Fit all vacancy types, both inhomogeneity regions, and the tip-height series with a single model whose Δ0 and μ are fixed from the adjacent defect-free spectra. Report which features the model fails to capture.
3. Add TIBB and charging controls (bias-dependent setpoints, I(z), gap-edge shifts in defect-free regions, and charging-ring maps). Then rephrase "tunable by tip electric field" according to what these controls show.
4. Tone down the Conclusion's statement that the results "confirm" the excitonic origin of the gap until the alternatives in concerns 1-3 have been addressed.
