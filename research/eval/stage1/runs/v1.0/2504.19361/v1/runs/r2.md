Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use focused ion beam milling to make micron-scale rings from exfoliated single crystals of Sr2RuO4 with the intrinsic ~1.5 K Tc. They report an intrinsic Josephson effect, which they attribute to superconducting domain walls pinned in the ring arms. The Ic(H) patterns violate Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)|, show a diode effect at zero field and have a central lobe narrower than Phi0. Under in-plane field the devices also show current-switchable bistable Ic states and half-integer Shapiro steps. If the violation is intrinsic, this would be a transport signature of time-reversal symmetry breaking (TRSB) in unstrained Sr2RuO4 with no engineered weak link, and it would bear directly on the debate over a two-component order parameter. That is a significant result. The novelty over the authors' earlier npj Quantum Mater. 2020 work lies mainly in the TRSB test and the in-plane-field phenomenology. These are exactly the parts most exposed to extrinsic explanations.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Device fabrication is demanding. The crystals are ultra-clean (RRR > 100) and keep the intrinsic Tc after FIB structuring. This platform avoids the stochastic domain behaviour of earlier macroscopic Nb/Sr2RuO4 hybrid junctions.
- Clear integer Shapiro steps (Fig. 1c,d; also the single-arm geometry in Fig. S3) establish real Josephson coupling and rule out Little-Parks or kinematic-vortex explanations for the oscillations.
- The Ic extraction procedure is documented (S2). It is tested on a conventional MoGe/Ag SQUID that obeys Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| within error (Fig. S9).
- Ring 3 has an internal temperature control: no significant TRSB at 1200 mK, but TRSB together with a narrowed, skewed central lobe at 1100 mK (Fig. S6).
- The phenomenology is rich and potentially important: current-trained bistable Ic, in-plane-field-dependent fractional Shapiro steps, and field-reversal swapping the role of the training-current polarity (Fig. S13). Even if part of it turns out to be extrinsic, it would interest the Sr2RuO4 and Josephson-device communities.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Extrinsic sources of Ic+(H) ≠ |Ic-(-H)| are not excluded.
   - In Ring 2 (Fig. 2c), ΔIc is a nearly field-independent negative offset of roughly 5–8 µA at all three temperatures, with jumps at the lobe minima. A field-independent offset in ΔIc is what three extrinsic sources would produce:
     - a dc current offset in the bias source or a ground loop (a shift δI gives ΔIc = 2δI at every field);
     - a polarity-dependent voltage background, such as a thermoelectric voltage that is not removed by subtracting the mean voltage near I = 0;
     - a polarity-dependent Ic criterion. The threshold is 0.1 or 0.2 I0RN "depending on the degree of rounding" (S2), and RN is taken from "a representative portion of the dataset".
   - The same signature is what the paper calls a zero-field diode effect "over the entire interference pattern" (Sec. IV).
   - The quoted error bars come from a bootstrap of voltage noise only (S2). They exclude these systematics and the temperature drift that S2 itself identifies as producing apparent diodicity in the control SQUID (Fig. S9, last half-periods).
   - The authors should:
     - show raw IV curves at a few fields for both polarities;
     - show that ΔIc does not depend on the voltage criterion (for example 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 I0RN, and a dV/dI criterion);
     - repeat the measurement with the current leads swapped and with the voltage leads swapped (an extrinsic offset follows the wiring, an intrinsic asymmetry follows the device);
     - report the measured zero-current voltage and current offsets;
     - give a systematic error budget next to the statistical one.

2. The TRSB test does not include training or history dependence, and trapped flux is a competing explanation.
   - The magnitude of Ic+(H) and Ic-(-H) is invariant only if the whole system, including any frozen-in magnetic configuration, is time-reversed. Two kinds of state break this relation without spontaneous TRSB of the order parameter: Abrikosov vortices trapped in the large Sr2RuO4 banks near the arms, and a remanent field of the magnet that depends on sweep history.
   - The c-axis Hc1 of Sr2RuO4 is only a few mT, and the sweeps in Fig. 2 reach ±10 mT, so vortex entry into the banks is likely. A vortex near a weak link is known to produce exactly this kind of asymmetric, offset Ic(H) pattern.
   - The single global shift H0 (Eq. 1) cannot correct for sweep-direction-dependent remanence of a superconducting magnet.
   - Conversely, the domain wall picture makes a testable prediction. If the TRSB comes from a spontaneously chosen domain configuration, its time-reversed partner should also occur: the sign of ΔIc should reverse when the sample is cooled through Tc in +H versus -H (as in Kerr training). Across thermal cycles in zero field, the sign should be random or set by pinning.
   - The authors should report:
     - ΔIc(H) after zero-field cooling and after field cooling in ±small fields;
     - over several thermal cycles;
     - for both field-sweep directions and for different maximum field excursions (minor loops).
   - A sign reversal of ΔIc under opposite field cooling would be strong evidence for intrinsic TRSB. A ΔIc that depends on field excursion would point to trapped flux.
   - Section V mentions that field-cooled and zero-field-cooled results are identical for the bistable Ic states. That statement should be extended to, and shown for, the TRSB data.

3. The paper does not show that the junctions are domain walls rather than extrinsic weak links.
   - Shapiro steps (Fig. 1, Fig. S3) show that a Josephson weak link exists somewhere in series between the voltage probes. They do not show where it is or that it is a chiral domain wall. Plausible alternatives are:
     - FIB-damaged regions at the necks of the arms (TEM limits damage to about 10 nm at the edges, but the arms are only 0.25–0.7 µm thick, and Ga implantation into the surfaces is not quantified);
     - local inhomogeneity;
     - weak links in the contact or bank regions.
   - The two arguments given against trivial weak links are:
     - the high symmetry of the pattern (Sec. III);
     - the absence of oscillations in 3K-phase rings (cited from earlier work, refs. 38 and 41).
   - The first argument is qualitative. The second is not demonstrated in this manuscript.
   - The authors should:
     - show, in this paper, null-control devices of identical geometry and FIB process made from a single-component superconductor or from 3K-phase Sr2RuO4, measured in the same setup;
     - report whether these controls show Shapiro steps, SQUID oscillations, TRSB or bistability;
     - if possible, add a geometry-dependence test (for example, how Ic and the junction characteristics scale with arm width, or whether the weak link moves when the necks are re-milled).
   - Ring 2's four-stage milling (Fig. S2) is a good start. The authors should state what each stage tells us about junction location.

B. Should address

4. The narrow central lobe and the domain wall motion toy model.
   - The central lobe is 0.73 Phi0 in Ring 1 and 0.74 Phi0 in Ring 3, against about 1.00 Phi0 for the neighbouring lobes. Before attributing this to domain wall motion, the authors should exclude non-linearity of the superconducting magnet near zero field (remanent flux reversal). The control SQUID (Figs. S8, S9) spans only ±2 mT with a ~1.6 mT period, so it does not test the field range where the rings show the effect. The authors should calibrate the field near zero with a Hall sensor, or measure the control SQUID over ±10 mT in the same magnet.
   - The toy model (S3) is phenomenological:
     - The sigmoid in Eq. 4 is not derived from theory.
     - The coupling in Eq. 3, α(Ψ+B − Ψ−B), is not a gauge-invariant free-energy term as written. A coupling of B to |Ψ+|² − |Ψ−|² (orbital moment) would be.
     - The fits (Fig. S10) have Phi_int^max, phi_m, a, αI, βL and a Gaussian width as free parameters for about five lobes.
     - The fits do not reproduce the skew of the central lobe.
   - There is also a conceptual tension. If the domain walls move reversibly with field, as the lack of dependence on sweep direction implies, then H → -H should mirror the domain configuration. This would tend to restore Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| rather than break it.
   - The authors should explain how a reversible, field-driven domain wall position coexists with the observed TRSB. They should state the fitted parameter values with uncertainties and say whether the model makes any prediction that can be tested independently (for example, the temperature dependence of the central lobe width).

5. In-plane-field phenomena (bistable Ic, half-integer and 1/3 Shapiro steps) have conventional explanations that are not excluded.
   - A 20 mT in-plane field threading an arm cross-section of about 0.5 × 0.5 µm² gives several Phi0. Each junction is therefore strongly field-modulated, as the Fraunhofer-like pattern in Fig. S15 shows.
   - The argument that the Josephson penetration depth exceeds the junction width (Sec. VI) dismisses Abrikosov vortex flow, but not two other mechanisms:
     - current-driven switching between Josephson-vortex (fluxon) configurations in a wide junction, a known source of bistable Ic;
     - vortex entry into the banks at the training currents of about 110–120 µA.
   - A misalignment of 1° turns 20 mT in-plane into about 0.35 mT out-of-plane, roughly 0.08 Phi0 through the ring. Half-integer steps in a two-junction SQUID with finite βL near half flux are standard and need no unusual CPR.
   - The authors should:
     - state the field alignment accuracy;
     - show whether the bistability and fractional steps survive when the out-of-plane component is compensated or deliberately varied;
     - give βL for the rings;
     - show the dependence on in-plane field magnitude of the switching thresholds and of the Ic difference between the two states.
   - The irreproducibility after resetting the field (Fig. S14) and the sample-dependent "optimal" angle (Fig. S16; 20° in Ring 1, 55° in Ring 2) should be discussed as possible signs of an extrinsic, pinning-controlled mechanism.

6. The temperature dependence of the TRSB signal is not compared with the bulk TRSB onset.
   - Kerr and µSR place the TRSB onset at Tc in unstrained Sr2RuO4.
   - Here, Ring 3 shows no significant TRSB at 1200 mK and TRSB at 1100 mK, with Tc = 1.5 K (Fig. S6).
   - In Ring 2 (Fig. 2c), ΔIc stays at about −5 to −8 µA while Ic roughly doubles from 1000 to 400 mK, so the relative asymmetry falls on cooling.
   - The authors should show ΔIc(T), and ΔIc/Ic, from base temperature to Tc for at least one ring, and discuss whether the onset agrees with the bulk TRSB transition or with a junction-specific scale.

7. The "unstrained" and "strain-free" claims are asserted rather than shown.
   - The argument rests on Tc never exceeding 1.5 K (Sec. III). However, the devices' Tc values (1.3–1.5 K, Table S1) lie at or below bulk.
   - Differential thermal contraction between the flake, the SrTiO3 substrate and the Ti/Au and SiOx/Au caps can produce strains that split Tc and TRSB without raising Tc to 3 K.
   - The Discussion uses this claim to reject the accidental-degeneracy and strain-texture scenarios (refs. 52–56).
   - The authors should either estimate the residual strain, for example from the Tc width or from thermal-mismatch calculations, or soften the argument.

8. Sample statistics and device identification are incomplete.
   - The text says the features are "universally observed in a large selection of samples" and that "all devices" show the zero-field diode effect. Only Rings 1–3 are shown with TRSB, and the Ring 4 data (Fig. S5) have no TRSB analysis.
   - Ring 2 appears twice in Table S1 (RN = 53 and 112 mΩ) and went through four milling stages. It is not stated which stage produced Fig. 2c, Fig. S10c or the bistability data.
   - The authors should give a table of every device measured, including those that showed no effect, listing for each: whether it shows Shapiro steps, TRSB, the sign of ΔIc, central lobe narrowing and bistability, plus the number of cooldowns.

9. The remanent-field correction and its effect on the TRSB metric need clarification.
   - As written, Eq. 1 minimizes ∫[Ic+(H+H0) − Ic−(H+H0)]², which compares the two branches at the same field rather than at ±H. The authors should state whether Ic− means |Ic−| and whether the minimization is over Ic+(H) versus |Ic−(−H)|.
   - Choosing H0 to minimize the very asymmetry later reported as TRSB biases ΔIc towards zero. That is conservative, but the size of H0 for each dataset should be reported and compared with the magnet's known remanence.
   - The authors should also state whether H0 differs between sweep directions.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a training experiment for the TRSB itself. Cool through Tc in ±small out-of-plane fields, then measure Ic(H) after returning to zero field. Show that the sign of ΔIc follows the training field and is reproducible over thermal cycles. This is the single most convincing test that the asymmetry comes from a spontaneously chosen, time-reversal-partnered state.
2. Measure a same-geometry, same-process null device, for example from a conventional superconductor or 3K-phase Sr2RuO4, in the same cryostat with the same wiring and over the same field range. Show its Ic+(H) versus |Ic-(-H)| with identical analysis, so that the wiring, magnet and analysis systematics are controlled.
3. Provide an error budget for ΔIc that includes the Ic criterion, current and voltage offsets, field remanence and temperature drift. Show that ΔIc is unchanged when the current and voltage leads are swapped.
4. Separate the in-plane-field results into what can and cannot be explained by conventional junction physics (Josephson vortices, misalignment flux, βL). Quantify the field alignment and give the in-plane flux per arm cross-section, so that readers can judge how unusual the bistability and fractional steps are.
