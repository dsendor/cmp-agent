Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors FIB-structure exfoliated single-crystal Sr2RuO4 into micron-scale rings. They report that Josephson coupling appears without any deliberate weak link, which they attribute to chiral domain walls pinned at the ring necks. The headline claim is that the critical-current interference pattern violates Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)|, with a zero-field diode effect, in the unstrained 1.5 K phase. They present this as direct transport evidence for TRSB in Sr2RuO4. They also report a narrowed central SQUID lobe, current-switchable bistable Ic states under in-plane field and half-integer Shapiro steps. If the TRSB signature is shown to be intrinsic, it would be a valuable device-level complement to the muSR and Kerr results. It would also bear on the two-component order-parameter debate. The work builds on the authors' earlier report of spontaneous Josephson junctions in such rings (Ref. 38), so the novelty lies mainly in the TRSB test and the in-plane-field phenomenology.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Testing Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| is a clean, model-independent symmetry criterion. Applying it to an intrinsic, unproximitized Sr2RuO4 device is a sensible and timely idea.
- Shapiro steps are clear and quantized (Fig. 1c,d), including in a singly connected geometry (Fig. S3). This supports Josephson coupling in the device rather than pure Little-Parks behaviour.
- The authors set out an analysis pipeline (offset subtraction, bootstrap errors, remanent-field symmetrization; Sec. S2) and test it on a conventional FIB-made SNS SQUID (Figs. S8, S9, S11). That is good practice.
- The phenomenology is rich and possibly new: bistable Ic states switched by a training current with field-polarity-reversed roles (Fig. 3, S13), and a state-dependent fractional Shapiro response (Fig. 4).
- The asymmetry is seen in three rings (Rings 1-3) and at several temperatures in Ring 2 (Fig. 2c).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Extrinsic sources of broken time-reversal symmetry in the measured state are not excluded (Sec. IV, Fig. 2, Fig. S6). The Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| identity holds only if the whole device, including its flux state, is time-reversal symmetric apart from the applied field. Several ordinary effects would give an apparent violation in a conventional ring:
   - Abrikosov or Pearl vortices pinned in the large Sr2RuO4 banks or lead arms close to the junctions.
   - Flux trapped during cooldown through a remanent magnet field.
   - Hysteresis in the field sweep, since H and -H are reached with different sweep histories.

   Each of these acts as an internal "magnetization" that does not reverse with H. It produces an inhomogeneous phase bias that a single uniform shift H -> H + H0 (Eq. 1) cannot remove. Such flux can also give a finite Ic+ - |Ic-| at nominally zero field. The banks here are wide, roughly µm-scale. The measurements go to about 10 mT out of plane, comparable to fields where vortices enter wide Sr2RuO4 films. The paper does not say whether the data were taken after zero-field cooling with the magnet degaussed, or how the field was swept.

   The decisive experiments are missing:
   - (a) Thermal cycling above Tc with zero-field cooling, repeated several times. A spontaneous chiral domain state should give the sign of the zero-field diode either reproducibly or randomly (the paper says only that earlier macroscopic devices changed on cycling).
   - (b) Field cooling in small +Hz and -Hz. If the asymmetry comes from chirality, Kerr-type training (cited in S3) should fix or reverse the sign of Delta Ic. Trapped vortices would give a different, field-magnitude-dependent signature.
   - (c) Ic+(H) and Ic-(-H) measured in both field-sweep directions.
   - (d) Recovery of the symmetric pattern after warming to just above Tc and cooling in zero field.

   Without these, the TRSB attributed to the order parameter cannot be separated from TRSB of the flux configuration of the device.

2. The statistical and systematic uncertainty on Delta Ic is underestimated, and the analysis procedure is ambiguous (Sec. S2, Eq. 1, Fig. 2b,c).
   - Error bars come only from propagating voltage noise via a parametric bootstrap. The text says this "only accounts for the uncertainty in measured voltage". They do not include switching-current fluctuations, slow drifts, or sweep-to-sweep reproducibility.
   - The authors' own control (Fig. S9) shows a drift-induced Delta Ic of about 1 µA, larger than its error bars.
   - In the Sr2RuO4 rings, Delta Ic is about -5 µA on Ic of 60-140 µA (Fig. 2c). Much of it is a roughly field-independent offset, and that is exactly the form a slow drift or a polarity-dependent systematic would take. Examples are a thermoelectric offset, the order in which polarities are swept, or heating after switching at Ic of about 100 µA.
   - The Ic criterion is "0.1 or 0.2 I0RN depending on the degree of rounding", but the paper does not say which was used for each dataset.
   - Eq. 1 as written minimizes [Ic+(H+H0) - Ic-(H+H0)]^2 at the same field, not at reversed fields. Taken literally, this does not implement the symmetrization described.

   The authors should:
   - correct Eq. 1 and state the H0 found for each dataset;
   - show the raw zero-field IVs for both polarities, with the sweep order reversed;
   - estimate error bars from repeated independent field sweeps;
   - show that Delta Ic survives both threshold choices and an independent criterion such as a dV/dI peak.

   Without this, the "statistically significant" TRSB (Figs. 2, S6) is not established at the level needed for the headline claim.

3. There is no matched null control (Sec. III-IV, Figs. S7-S11). The only control is a MoGe/Ag SNS SQUID with FIB line-cut junctions. Its material, junction type, contact geometry, Ic, inductance and lead layout all differ from the Sr2RuO4 rings. It therefore does not test the artifacts that matter here: flux in thick single-crystal banks, Ti/Au contacts on Sr2RuO4, the self-field of about 100 µA bias currents in this lead geometry, and heating.

   The paper itself notes two natural nulls:
   - Ring 3 at 1200 mK shows no TRSB (Fig. S6a). The paper does not discuss whether this is expected, given that bulk TRSB sets in at Tc, or whether it reflects the reduced signal-to-noise near Tc.
   - 3K-phase rings show no SQUID oscillations, so they cannot serve.

   A ring of identical geometry made by the same exfoliation, contact and FIB process from a conventional single-crystal superconductor would give a direct null. So would a Sr2RuO4 ring in which a deliberate constriction dominates the junctions. Either should show Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| within error under identical measurement protocols. Alternatively, the authors should show the temperature dependence of Delta Ic/Ic in one ring all the way to Tc and argue quantitatively that it tracks the onset of superconductivity rather than noise.

B. Should address

4. The evidence that the junctions are domain walls rather than FIB- or geometry-defined weak links is indirect (Sec. III, Fig. 2a, Fig. S1).
   - The argument that retrapping equals critical current excludes constriction junctions is not decisive. Constrictions shunted by a large normal-state channel, or operated at Ic Rn far below Delta/e, are overdamped.
   - From Fig. S1 (Ic of about 45-80 µA, Rn of about 19-112 mOhm), Ic Rn is only about 1-8 µV. That is orders of magnitude below Delta/e of about 0.2-0.3 mV. Rn is the whole-device resistance, so the junction value is unknown.
   - The argument that high junction symmetry rules out accidental weak links is circular. Symmetric FIB-milled necks would naturally give symmetric constriction junctions.
   - Shapiro-like rf-locked voltage plateaus also occur at phase-slip centres in narrow superconducting strips. So Fig. 1c,d alone does not single out a domain-wall Josephson junction.

   The authors should:
   - discuss the junction Ic Rn and its temperature dependence (Ambegaokar-Baratoff or KO-type against phase-slip behaviour);
   - compare rings with different neck widths, which would test whether the junction follows the necks;
   - temper the "cannot be explained by accidental weak links" statement.

5. The narrow central lobe and its domain-wall-motion interpretation rest on a heuristic model with many free parameters (Sec. IV, Fig. 2a, Sec. S3, Fig. S10). The toy model introduces an ad hoc linear coupling (Eq. 3), a sigmoid with free phi_m and a (Eq. 4), a free Phi_int^max (Eq. 5) and a Gaussian envelope. It still misses the lobe amplitudes in Fig. S10b,c. Lobe widths are also normalized to pi r_in r_out, so they depend on an assumed effective area.

   Conventional alternatives would also make the central lobe differ from the others:
   - screening with field-dependent kinetic inductance;
   - flux focusing from the banks;
   - vortex entry into the banks at |H| of about 2-3 mT.

   Ring 3 shows the effect only below 1200 mK (Fig. S6), which needs an explanation within the model. The authors should:
   - report the fitted parameters with uncertainties and their temperature dependence;
   - show whether the central-lobe width depends on field-sweep range or history, which would distinguish reversible domain-wall motion from vortex entry;
   - present the model as illustrative rather than as support for the domain-wall picture.

6. The in-plane field results need the field alignment and the vortex alternative addressed (Sec. V, Figs. 3, 4, S13-S16).
   - The SQUID period is about 4 mT. A 1-3 degree misalignment of a 10-20 mT in-plane field gives an out-of-plane component of 0.2-1 mT, a significant fraction of a flux quantum. The paper gives no alignment procedure or out-of-plane calibration, for example from compensating the SQUID pattern shift.
   - The bistability symmetry fits a conventional mechanism. Reversing either the current polarity or the field direction swaps the roles, which is the symmetry of a J x B force on vortices lying parallel to the ab plane or trapped at the necks, which a training current could push in or out.
   - The optimal angle differs between rings (20 degrees and 55 degrees; Fig. S16) with no link to the crystal axes. That is also more naturally explained by sample-specific pinning.

   The authors should:
   - quantify the misalignment;
   - show the Ic(Hz) pattern of each Ic state, to test whether the two states differ by a flux offset;
   - test whether the bistability persists at Hx below the in-plane vortex entry field;
   - report the crystal-axis orientation of each ring.

7. Half-integer Shapiro steps are not unique to a non-sinusoidal domain-wall current-phase relation (Sec. VI, Fig. 4, Figs. S14, S15).
   - In a dc SQUID biased near half a flux quantum, or with finite beta_L, half-integer steps arise from the circuit dynamics alone. With the misalignment of Concern 6, the low-Ic state could simply be the more frustrated one.
   - The authors themselves call the fractional steps at the Ic(B) minima trivial (Fig. S15).
   - The steps are not reproduced after a field reset (Fig. S14).
   - No microwave-power dependence of the half-step width is shown, which would distinguish a second CPR harmonic from dynamical locking.

   The authors should:
   - show the power dependence of the half-step width;
   - show that half steps occur when the out-of-plane flux is tuned to an integer value;
   - present the "different current-phase relation" conclusion more cautiously.

8. Device statistics and temperature dependence are thin for a "universal" claim (Sec. III, Figs. 2, S1, S6).
   - The paper says the features are "universally observed in a large selection of samples", but TRSB data are shown for three rings.
   - Ring 3 shows TRSB only at 1100 mK and not at 1200 mK.
   - In Ring 2, Delta Ic is about -5 µA at 400, 800 and 1000 mK even though Ic doubles, so Delta Ic/Ic is not constant.
   - Ring 1 has positive Delta Ic at zero field (Fig. 2b) and Ring 2 negative (Fig. 2c).

   A table would help, listing for every measured device, including those not shown, whether TRSB was observed, the zero-field Delta Ic/Ic with uncertainty and its sign. A plot of Delta Ic/Ic(T) up to Tc for at least one ring would also help. The sign statistics across devices and cooldowns would directly test the spontaneous-domain picture.

9. The discussion of pairing symmetry goes beyond the data (Sec. III and VII). The paper cites Tc ≤ 1.5 K and residual resistivity ratio >100 to call the devices "ultra-pure" and "strain-free". On that basis it dismisses the strain- or dislocation-induced TRSB scenarios (Refs. 55, 56) and favours Eg pairing.
   - Tc and RRR are bulk-averaged. They do not exclude local strain, Ga implantation or amorphization at the milled sidewalls and necks, which are where the junctions are proposed to sit.
   - The cited TEM (Ref. 43) is from other devices.
   - The same data are consistent with any two-component order parameter with degenerate time-reversed partners, including accidental degeneracies.

   The authors should either characterize the neck regions directly (cross-sectional TEM or local strain mapping on a measured device) or confine the conclusion to "a TRSB two-component state" without discriminating between Eg and accidental scenarios.

C. Minor

10. Figure references and captions contain errors:
   - The main text cites "Figure 1d" for the IV curves (they are in 1c) and "Figure 1e" for the power dependence (that is 1d, and there is no 1e).
   - The Fig. 2 caption reads "plotted in in the inset".
   - The Fig. S10 caption refers to a panel (d) that does not exist.
   - Fig. S10b is labelled Ring 1 at 800 mK, but it appears to show the same data as Fig. 2a (50 mK), with lobe widths of 0.71 against 0.73 Phi0.
   - The S13 caption refers to "Figure 4 of the main text" (it is Fig. 3) and to "I+ and I+".
   - The S14 caption mentions a "yellow arrow" (it is orange).
   - The S9 caption says "absence of no TRSB".
   - The double and single asterisks in the Fig. S1 table are not explained.

11. Delta Ic is never defined explicitly, as Ic+(H) - |Ic-(-H)| or the reverse. The sign convention for "diodicity", and whether the 50 mK data in Fig. 2a/b were symmetrized with the same H0 procedure, should be stated.

12. Sec. S4 sets beta_c = 0.001 and assumes a sinusoidal CPR and a symmetric inductance. The control fit (Fig. S11) does not report the fitted alpha_I, beta_L or Gaussian width. Please give these values, and explain why an inductance asymmetry plus a higher-harmonic CPR, which can produce non-identical skewed lobes at finite field, was not included before concluding that RCSJ "cannot be applied".

13. Eqs. 3 and 5 are dimensionally and physically unclear. It is not defined what Psi+ B means as a free-energy term, or why Phi_int scales as (phi_D + pi/2)/(pi/2). Please define the quantities or label them explicitly as phenomenological.

14. The dimensions of Ring 4 are marked with an asterisk, and its geometry, two parallel bars (Fig. S5), is used to rule out contact-geometry effects. This would be stronger if Ring 4's Ic+(H) and |Ic-(-H)| were also compared.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a chirality-training experiment: field-cool in ±Hz, then measure the zero-field Delta Ic. Repeat over several zero-field thermal cycles to show whether its sign is set by spontaneous domain formation or trainable. This would make the TRSB claim far more compelling than the present single-cooldown datasets.
2. Measure a process-matched null ring, made with the same fabrication and contacts from a conventional superconductor or with an engineered constriction, using the identical protocol. Report repeated-sweep error bars for both devices side by side.
3. Calibrate the in-plane field alignment, and show the full Ic(Hz) pattern in each bistable state to separate domain-wall reconfiguration from flux offsets or trapped vortices.
4. Merge the temperature dependence and device statistics into one figure: Delta Ic/Ic at zero field against T/Tc for all rings, with the sign for each cooldown.
