A. Must address

1. The field origin is fitted by minimising the asymmetry being tested, and the comparison is not made under time-reversed protocols (Fig. 2b, 2c; Supplementary Notes S2, S7, Eqs. 9 and 10). The identity Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| holds only when the two branches are taken under protocols related by time reversal. If the device has any history dependence, the time-reverse of an up-sweep at -H is a down-sweep at +H. Possible sources include trapped flux or vortices in the large Sr2RuO4 banks and leads, fluxoid-state hysteresis, magnet hysteresis and the mobile domains the authors themselves invoke. The paper does not say whether Ic+ and Ic- at each field come from the same IV trace or the same field sweep, or what the sweep direction of each was. ΔI_TRSB is not shown for both sweep directions, and the statement that the narrow lobe is independent of sweep direction (p. 8) is not backed by data. H0 is chosen by minimising ∫[Ic+(H) − |Ic-(-H)|]², and the remaining asymmetry is treated as TRSB. This removes any uniform shift by construction, but it cannot remove a sweep-dependent offset, a field-dependent flux-focusing nonlinearity or magnet hysteresis. In Fig. 2b and 2c, ΔI_TRSB spikes and has sawtooth jumps at the lobe minima, where |dIc/dH| is largest. In Ring 2 it ramps monotonically across each lobe, which is what a residual field-dependent displacement between the two branches produces (ΔI ≈ δH·dIc/dH). The fit has two further consequences. First, a φ0-like phase offset in the domain-wall junctions (Supplementary Note S5, Fig. S13b) shifts the whole pattern the same way for both polarities and cannot be told apart from H0. The analysis is therefore blind to the φ0 component that the Discussion relies on, and the remaining skew and offset are compared with a mechanism whose main prediction was subtracted away. Second, an asymmetric SQUID has an ordinary field-induced diode at any non-zero true field, so misplacing zero turns an ordinary diode into an apparent zero-field one. The fitted H0 values, their uncertainty and their variation between sweeps, rings and cooldowns are not reported for any Sr2RuO4 dataset. The authors should state the sweep protocol. They should show ΔI_TRSB from up-sweep Ic+(H) against down-sweep |Ic-(-H)| and vice versa, as well as for each direction separately. They should plot ΔI_TRSB against dIc/dH, or fit it to a·dIc/dH + b plus a field-dependent offset, and show what residual remains. They should report H0 with its confidence interval for every dataset, propagate it into ΔI_TRSB and η, and show the uncorrected data alongside the corrected data. Ideally H0 would be fixed independently of the data under test, for example with a Hall sensor, a conventional TRS-preserving reference SQUID on the same chip analysed identically and in the same cooldown and field history, or a magnet reset or quench protocol. Without this, the "zero-field" diode is defined relative to a fitted origin rather than measured.

2. Trapped Abrikosov vortices and other extrinsic TRS-breaking objects are not excluded as the source of the diode and of the point-symmetry violation, and the sign of the effect has not been tracked across cooldowns or trained (Fig. 2, Figs. S5, S6). A vortex pinned in a Sr2RuO4 bank, neck or arm near a weak link breaks the device's time-reversal symmetry without any TRSB in the order parameter. It is a known mechanism for field-free Josephson diodes and for distortions of Ic(H) with Ic+(H) ≠ |Ic-(-H)|. It is not a uniform field offset, so the H0 symmetrisation of S7 does not remove it. This is plausible here. S7 states that the remanent field of the superconducting magnet is neither nulled nor measured, so the samples are cooled through Tc in an unknown field. The banks are several micrometres across (Figs. 1b, S1), so a fraction of a millitesla is enough to trap vortices. Field scans span ±10 mT, and in-plane fields of 10–70 mT are applied in other experiments (Figs. 3, 4, S16, S17). The magnetic history before each Ic(H) dataset is not recorded: whether it was zero-field cooled, the previous maximum field, or whether the magnet was reset. Magnetic moments in the Ga-damaged edge layer or the SiOx cap are a second, smaller possibility. In a chiral-domain picture, the sign of ΔI_TRSB and η(H = 0) is set by which chirality sits on which side of the wall. It should therefore be random across thermal cycles above Tc with roughly fixed magnitude, and trainable by field-cooling in ±Hz, as reported for the Kerr signal and assumed in the toy model of S3. The text says the bulk configuration is random but the wall is deterministically pinned, yet never says whether the diode sign survives thermal cycling. The sign is already positive for Ring 1 (Fig. 2b, ΔI_TRSB(0) ≈ +4 µA) and negative for Ring 2 (Fig. S6). The authors should null the cooling field independently. They should repeat zero-field cooldowns after warming above Tc many times and report the sign and magnitude of η(0) and ΔI_TRSB for each cooldown and device. They should cool in small controlled ±Hz and show how η depends on the cooling field. Vortex trapping sets in near Φ0 divided by the bank area, whereas field-cooling that flips the sign of η would be strong, direct evidence for chirality-related TRSB. They should also show that η is unchanged after a field excursion below Tc. Random signs set at Tc would support spontaneous domain formation; history-dependent signs, or an effect that vanishes when the cooling field is nulled, would point to trapped flux.

3. The error budget for ΔI_TRSB and η(0) counts only voltage noise, and several unquantified systematics could produce an asymmetry of the observed size (Figs. 2b, 2c, S5b, S6; Eq. 1; Supplementary Note S2). The signal is small: ΔI_TRSB is a few µA on Ic of 60–140 µA, and |η| is 2–3.7% in Ring 2 from three points without error bars (Fig. S6). In Ring 2 the violation is largely a field-independent vertical offset of about 5 µA. S2 states that the bootstrap "only accounts for the uncertainty in measured voltage". It omits switching-current stochasticity, the uncertainty in H0 and in the field axis (largest on the steep flanks near the Ic minima, where ΔI_TRSB spikes), temperature drift, and the choice of threshold. The threshold is 0.1 or 0.2 I0RN "depending on the degree of rounding", and the paper does not say which value was used for each panel. With RN ≈ 20–100 mΩ this threshold is only about 0.1–1 µV, applied to visibly rounded IV curves (Fig. 1c). Temperature drift alone gave the control SQUID an apparent asymmetry of about 1 µA on about 78 µA, roughly 1.3% (Fig. S10). That is comparable to the 2–4% diode in Ring 2, which is measured at up to 1000 mK, close to Tc ≈ 1.35 K, where dIc/dT is large. There is also a specific candidate artifact. The bias runs through Ti/Au leads, and the offset correction subtracts only the mean voltage within a few µA of zero. Any voltage even in current, such as a thermoelectric voltage from I² heating of leads or contacts, survives that correction. It adds to the junction voltage on one polarity and subtracts on the other, shifting Ic+ and |Ic-| oppositely by a field-independent amount, which is the pattern in Fig. 2c. Finally, if the current runs 0 → +I → 0 → −I, switching on one polarity can change the state before the other polarity is measured. The authors should repeat the same field scan several times to give an empirical spread. They should show the even-in-current component [V(I) + V(−I)]/2 below Ic. They should show that ΔI_TRSB(H) and η(0) are unchanged for both thresholds and for a threshold-free definition, such as the dV/dI peak or a fit of each branch to a rounded-RSJ form. They should state the polarity order and show that reversing it leaves ΔI_TRSB unchanged, report thermometer stability during each map, and fold all systematic contributions into the error bars of Fig. 2 and Fig. S6.

4. No null device has been measured under matched conditions, so none of the signatures is shown to be specific to domain walls of a multi-component order parameter (Figs. 2–4, S8–S10; Discussion). The MoGe/Ag SNS control SQUID (Fig. S8) is a different material and junction type, with line-cut junctions in a bilayer, a different geometry and thickness, and no wide banks or necks. It was used only for the out-of-plane Ic(H) symmetry test, not with in-plane fields, training currents or microwaves. It is therefore not a matched null for flux focusing, vortex entry, constriction weak links or bistability. The 3K-phase rings that are meant to show that geometry and FIB processing alone do not create junctions are cited from earlier work (refs. 23, 47). They are not repeated here under matched conditions and analysis, and because they lack SQUID oscillations altogether they cannot serve as a null for the interference analysis. The authors should fabricate a ring with identical ring, neck and bank geometry and comparable thickness and λ from a conventional superconductor such as Nb, MoGe or Al, ideally with a matched FIB/SiOx process. They should run the full protocol on it: the same remanent-field cooling, the ΔI_TRSB analysis, in-plane fields and training currents, and microwaves. They should show that it displays none of the four anomalies, ideally measuring it in the same cooldown so it also serves as the H0 reference of concern 1. A further useful test is to apply the full TRSB and diode analysis to the singly connected Ring 2 (Figs. S2–S3), which appears to show unequal Ic+ and |Ic-| at zero field. Having no loop, it removes the inductance, fluxoid and flux-focusing ambiguities and would be a cleaner test of an intrinsic φ0-like junction.

B. Should address

5. Conventional flux focusing and vortex entry in the Sr2RuO4 banks can produce the narrowed, skewed central lobe followed by recovery of Φ0 periodicity, and the link between the narrow lobe and TRSB is weakened by the authors' own data (Fig. 2a, Figs. S5, S11). The rings are attached to large banks of 250–700 nm thick Sr2RuO4 in perpendicular field (Fig. 1b, Table S1). At low field the Meissner banks focus flux into the loop, which enlarges the effective area and narrows the lobes. Once vortices enter the banks, which can happen at a few mT given the small Hc1 and strong demagnetisation of a flake in perpendicular field, the focusing changes and the period relaxes. Pinned vortices also make the pattern history-dependent, which feeds concern 1. In Ring 3 at 1200 mK (Fig. S5a), described as showing "normal SQUID behavior" without significant TRSB, the central lobe is already narrowed to 0.82 Φ0 against 0.92–0.93 Φ0 for its neighbours. Narrowing can therefore occur without measurable TRSB, which weakens the argument that both come from chirality-driven domain-wall motion. The authors should show Ic(H) for both sweep directions and show how the central-lobe width and ΔI_TRSB depend on the maximum field of the preceding sweep (minor loops). Vortex entry into the banks is hysteretic, whereas domain-wall motion confined by the necks should be largely reversible. They should tabulate central-lobe width against temperature for each ring together with ΔI_TRSB, and fit a model that includes flux focusing as an alternative.

6. The domain-wall toy model is weakly constrained, internally inconsistent, does not produce the TRSB, and implies spontaneous fields that conflict with scanning-SQUID results (Supplementary Note S3, Eqs. 3–5, Fig. S11). As written, φD(H) is odd in H about π/2 (Eq. 4), so H → −H with chirality exchange is a symmetry of the model. It preserves Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| and gives neither the skew of the central lobe, nor ΔI_TRSB, nor the zero-field diode. It accounts only for the narrow lobe, which has conventional alternatives (concern 5), even though the paper presents the narrow lobe and the TRSB as having a common origin. Eq. 5 gives Φ = Φext + 2Φ_int^max at H = 0 (φD = π/2), contradicting the statement that domain contributions cancel at zero field; presumably (φD − π/2)/(π/2) was intended. Eq. 3 couples order-parameter amplitudes linearly to the field in a heuristic, dimensionally loose way, without a stated GL functional. The fit has many free parameters for about five lobes: φm, a, Φ_int^max, the RCSJ α_I and βL, the Gaussian envelope width and the amplitude. Φ_int^max is poorly determined (0.16 +0.13/−0.06 Φ0), the other parameters and their posteriors are not listed, and the fits visibly miss the lobe heights. The fitted Φ_int^max of 0.16–0.24 Φ0 through a 1 µm ring implies mean spontaneous fields of order 1 mT and chiral currents that the scanning-SQUID searches of Sr2RuO4 mesostructures (refs. 28–30) should have detected. The authors should state which rings were fitted and with how many free parameters, and report all parameters with uncertainties and correlations. They should fit both current branches simultaneously, and either extend the model to generate the skew, ΔI_TRSB(H) and the diode or state that it does not. They should also discuss consistency with the scanning-SQUID bounds. A direct local probe such as scanning SQUID or MFM of the ring, ideally in the low- and high-Ic states, would locate the junctions and any trapped vortices, and even a null result with stated sensitivity would constrain Φ_int^max.

7. The half-integer Shapiro steps have conventional explanations, some acknowledged in the paper, that are not excluded for Fig. 4, so they are not shown to reflect a changed current-phase relation of an individual junction (Fig. 4, Figs. S15, S16). The device is a two-junction SQUID. Near half a flux quantum the first harmonic of the effective current-phase relation is suppressed and half-integer steps appear, and Fig. S16 states exactly this for steps near the Ic(B) minima. The low-Ic state may simply sit closer to a SQUID minimum. One route is a perpendicular component from misalignment: 1° of the 10 mT in-plane field gives about 0.2 mT, a sizeable fraction of the 4–5 mT period of Ring 1. Alignment accuracy is not stated anywhere. Another route is a different trapped-flux configuration in the banks. In addition, 10–20 mT in-plane threads roughly one flux quantum through the arm cross-section (thickness 0.25–0.7 µm times an effective junction length of about 2λ plus the wall width), so the junctions are Fraunhofer-modulated. S6 places nodes near 4.5 and 9.9 mT in Fig. S16, and Fig. 4 is taken at 10 mT, close to such a node though at a different field angle. The dynamical regime also matters. At 144 MHz, hf/2e ≈ 0.3 µV against IcRN ≈ 1.4 µV for Ring 1 gives a reduced frequency of about 0.2, where capacitive or inductive dynamics can generate subharmonic steps even for a sinusoidal current-phase relation. The claim that λJ exceeds the arm width is asserted, not calculated. The steps are also fragile: after the field is reset they vanish from one polarity and an integer step goes missing (Fig. S15). The temperature, frequency and power at the sample for Fig. 4 are not given; only S15 gives 145 MHz and 50 mK. The authors should give the perpendicular-field calibration and alignment procedure with its uncertainty. They should map Ic(Hz) at fixed in-plane field for both Ic states to locate each on the SQUID pattern, and map the fractional-step amplitude against in-plane field magnitude and angle, perpendicular trim field, microwave power and frequency, for both states. They should compare integer and half-integer step widths against RSJ and RCSJ calculations for a SQUID with a sinusoidal current-phase relation, finite βL and the estimated flux bias, and give the λJ estimate. They should show that the half-steps persist where the first harmonic is not suppressed, i.e. away from SQUID minima and from nodes of the in-plane modulation. Until then, "a different current-phase relation between the two Ic states" should be presented as one possibility. A direct current-phase-relation measurement, for example embedding the singly connected Ring 2 geometry in an rf-SQUID or asymmetric interferometer, would test for the φ0 offset and second harmonic predicted for a chiral domain wall independently of the Shapiro interpretation.

8. The bistable Ic states are not distinguished from vortex or flux trapping driven by the training current, or from fluxoid transitions (Fig. 3, Figs. S14, S17). A supercritical training current exerts a Lorentz force J × B on in-plane vortices, or on out-of-plane vortices from misalignment, in the banks and arms. The force reverses with either the current or the field, which matches the observed rule that the roles of the training polarities swap when Hx is reversed (S14). Different pinned configurations then give different Ic. Sr2RuO4 is strongly anisotropic and the crystals are 0.25–0.7 µm thick, so 10–20 mT in-plane penetrates substantially. Fig. S17 shows no hysteresis below 10 mT, consistent with a vortex-entry threshold. A 1° tilt at 20 mT gives about 0.35 mT along c, a sizeable fraction of the roughly 4.6 mT lobe period. The angular map (S17b) is sparse and speckled because each pixel is a single IV trace. The optimal angle differs between samples (20° for Ring 1, 55° for Ring 2) with no correspondence to geometry, which fits pinning or misalignment better than an order-parameter property. If βL is not small, current-induced transitions between fluxoid states of the loop could also give two Ic branches; the about 35% modulation depth in Fig. 2a requires either strong junction asymmetry or sizeable βL. The Josephson-penetration-depth argument (p. 11) addresses only the half-integer steps, not the bistability. The authors should quantify the field alignment and out-of-plane component, and estimate the in-plane lower critical and entry fields of banks and arms. They should report the fitted or estimated βL and junction asymmetry. They should show the switching-threshold current against in-plane field magnitude and temperature, the out-of-plane tilt dependence, whether each state persists when the field is removed and re-applied, and whether bistability survives in the singly connected Ring 2. They should map Ic(Hz) in both states, since domain-wall reconfiguration and an added vortex should shift the interference pattern differently. They should repeat the S17 map to establish whether the optimal angle is reproducible, and explain why the vortex scenario is excluded.
