Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors study FIB-structured single-crystal Sr2RuO4 rings that have only normal-metal Ti/Au contacts, so any Josephson coupling must be intrinsic to the crystal. They report four things: (i) the critical-current interference pattern violates Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)|, giving a zero-field diode effect of a few percent; (ii) the central SQUID lobe is narrowed to about 0.73 Phi0 and skewed; (iii) an in-plane field produces bistable critical-current states that a training current can switch; (iv) half-integer Shapiro steps appear in one of those states. They interpret all of this as evidence for chiral domain-wall junctions of a two-component order parameter. If the time-reversal-symmetry-breaking (TRSB) signal holds up as intrinsic, it would be the first device-scale, phase-sensitive transport evidence for spontaneous TRSB in unstrained Sr2RuO4, which scanning-SQUID searches have so far failed to find. That would bear directly on the pairing-symmetry debate and on field-free Josephson diodes. The main weakness is that the key observables have conventional, extrinsic alternatives that the paper does not yet exclude: trapped vortices, a residual-field correction that is fitted to the very symmetry being tested, voltage-criterion artifacts, and vortex or SQUID dynamics under misaligned in-plane fields.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The device concept is clean. Sr2RuO4 is the only superconductor in the circuit, which avoids the proximity and interface ambiguities of the Nb/Ru/Sr2RuO4 hybrid junctions.
- The paper tests a well-defined symmetry relation, Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)|, instead of relying only on Ic(H) asymmetry. It also applies the full extraction pipeline to a control SNS SQUID made of MoGe/Ag (Figs. S8 to S10, S12), where the relation holds within error.
- The phenomenology is rich and was obtained on several rings. Ring 2 was re-milled through four geometries, and Ring 3 provides an internal temperature control: no resolvable TRSB at 1200 mK, TRSB at 1100 mK (Fig. S5).
- The bistable, current-programmable Ic states and the polarity-selective fractional Shapiro steps are striking. If they are intrinsic, they are interesting for superconducting memory and diode devices.
- The data are deposited in a public repository.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Trapped Abrikosov vortices and other extrinsic TRS-breaking objects are not excluded as the source of the diode effect and of the point-symmetry violation (Fig. 2, Fig. S5, Fig. S6).
   - The problem: a vortex pinned in a Sr2RuO4 bank or arm near one of the weak links is itself a TRS-breaking object. It is well known to produce field-free Josephson diode behaviour and to distort Ic(H) so that Ic+(H) ≠ |Ic-(-H)|. A trapped vortex is not equivalent to a uniform field offset, so the H0 shift of Supplementary Note S7 does not remove it.
   - Why it is likely here: Supplementary Note S7 states that the remanent field of the superconducting magnet is neither nulled nor measured. The samples are therefore cooled through Tc in an unknown field. The banks are several micrometres across (Figs. 1b, S1), so a remanent field of a fraction of a millitesla is enough to trap vortices. Magnetic moments in the Ga-damaged edge layer or the SiOx cap are a second, smaller extrinsic possibility.
   - Why it matters: the paper's headline claim is intrinsic, spontaneous TRSB. That claim needs the trapped-flux scenario to be ruled out.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Null the cooling field independently, for example with an on-chip Hall sensor or a co-mounted reference SQUID, or by using a fresh magnet or a magnet with a quench heater.
     - Repeat zero-field cooldowns many times and report the sign and magnitude of eta(H=0) and ΔI_TRSB for each cooldown. Under the chiral-domain picture, the sign should be random from cooldown to cooldown with the magnitude roughly fixed. Under trapped vortices, the effect should vanish when the cooling field is nulled.
     - Cool in small controlled out-of-plane fields of both signs and show how eta depends on the cooling field. Vortex trapping sets in at a characteristic threshold of order Phi0 divided by the bank area.
   - The text says the bulk configuration is random but the wall is deterministically pinned. It never states whether the sign of the diode effect survived thermal cycling.

2. The extraction of ΔI_TRSB and eta(0) has unquantified systematic errors that could produce an offset of the observed size (Figs. 2b, 2c, S6; Supplementary Note S2).
   - The size of the effect: in Ring 2 the violation is mostly a field-independent vertical offset of roughly 5 µA out of 60 to 140 µA, giving eta ≈ −2% to −3.7%. Ic is defined with a voltage threshold of 0.1 or 0.2 I0RN. With RN ≈ 20 to 100 mΩ, that threshold is only about 0.1 to 1 µV, applied to IV curves that are visibly rounded (Fig. 1c).
   - The suspected artifact: the bias current runs through Ti/Au leads, and the offset correction subtracts only the mean voltage within a few µA of zero. Any voltage that is even in the current, such as a thermoelectric voltage from I² heating of the leads or contacts, survives that correction. Such a voltage adds to the junction voltage on one polarity and subtracts on the other. That shifts Ic+ and |Ic-| in opposite directions by an amount that does not depend on field, which is exactly the pattern in Fig. 2c.
   - Error bars and drift: the error bars include only voltage noise (bootstrap). They exclude the uncertainty in H0, temperature drift and threshold choice. The control SQUID itself showed a drift-induced ΔI of about 1 µA (Fig. S10).
   - The threshold rule is not stated: the text says 0.1 or 0.2 I0RN is chosen "depending on the degree of rounding", but not which value was used for each panel.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Show the even-in-current component [V(I) + V(−I)]/2 below Ic.
     - Show that ΔI_TRSB(H) and eta(0) do not change under different thresholds, and under a threshold-free definition such as the dV/dI peak or a fit of each branch to a rounded-RSJ form.
     - State the order in which the two polarities are acquired. If the current runs 0 → +I → 0 → −I, the switching event on one polarity can alter the state before the other is measured. Show that reversing the order leaves ΔI_TRSB unchanged.
     - Show ΔI_TRSB for both field-sweep directions.
     - Fold the systematic contributions into the error bars in Fig. 2 and Fig. S6.

3. The residual-field correction is fitted by minimising the very asymmetry being tested, so it cannot separate a field offset from TRSB physics (Supplementary Note S7, Eqs. 9 and 10).
   - The procedure chooses H0 to minimise the integral of [Ic+ − |Ic-|]² and treats whatever asymmetry remains as TRSB. This creates two problems.
   - First, it removes by construction the clearest signature of the physics the authors invoke. A φ0-like phase offset in the domain-wall junctions (Supplementary Note S5, Fig. S13b) shifts the whole interference pattern in the same direction for both polarities, which is indistinguishable from H0. The analysis is therefore blind to the φ0 component that the Discussion relies on. The remaining ΔI_TRSB, a central-lobe skew plus a vertical offset, is then compared with a mechanism whose main prediction was subtracted away.
   - Second, the zero-field diode claim depends on where H = 0 is placed. In an asymmetric SQUID, a field-induced diode Ic+(H) ≠ |Ic-(H)| exists at any non-zero true field. Mislocating zero therefore turns an ordinary diode into an apparent zero-field one.
   - Minor inconsistency: Eq. 10 is written with Ic-(H + H0) rather than |Ic-(−H − H0)|, so the quantity actually minimised needs to be stated exactly.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Calibrate H0 independently, for example from a TRS-preserving reference SQUID on the same chip and in the same field history, or from a Hall probe.
     - Report the H0 values found by the fit for each dataset, and say whether they agree between sweeps and between rings measured in the same cooldown.
     - Report the uncorrected data alongside the corrected data.

4. None of the signatures is shown to be unique to domain walls of a multi-component order parameter, and there is no null device measured under matched conditions (Figs. 2 to 4; Discussion).
   - What the paper claims: that these features "cannot originate from conventional s-wave superconductivity".
   - What the control actually covers: the MoGe/Ag SNS control is a different material and junction type, made with line-cut junctions in a bilayer. It was used only for the out-of-plane Ic(H) symmetry test, not with in-plane fields, training currents or microwaves.
   - Arms and necks as weak links: the ring arms are about 250 to 350 nm wide, a few times ξ0 = 66 nm. ξ(T) grows near Tc, so the necks and arms can act as geometric constriction weak links. The phrase "Shapiro steps can only originate from the Josephson effect" does not exclude this, because phase-slip centres and Dayem-bridge constrictions also give steps at V = n Phi0 f.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Fabricate a ring with identical geometry, including the necks and banks, from a conventional superconductor such as Nb, MoGe or Al, ideally with a matched FIB/SiOx process.
     - Run the full protocol on it: cooling in the same remanent field, the ΔI_TRSB analysis, the in-plane field and training current, and microwaves.
     - Show that it shows none of the four anomalies.
   - The 3K-phase rings are not a usable null: they lack SQUID oscillations altogether.

B. Should address

5. The bistable Ic states (Fig. 3, Figs. S14, S17) have a conventional vortex explanation that the paper does not discuss.
   - The observed behaviour: the states are switched by a large bias current, and the current polarity that selects each state reverses when the in-plane field reverses (Fig. S14).
   - The conventional reading: this is the signature of Lorentz-force-driven flux motion. The force J × B reverses with either factor, so a training current can push an in-plane (or, through misalignment, out-of-plane) vortex into or out of a weak-link region or bank, producing two metastable Ic values.
   - Why vortices are plausible here: Sr2RuO4 is strongly anisotropic and the crystals are 0.25 to 0.7 µm thick. An in-plane field of 10 to 20 mT therefore penetrates substantially and can create in-plane vortices.
   - Field misalignment: a 1° tilt at 20 mT gives about 0.35 mT along c, a sizeable fraction of the roughly 4.6 mT lobe period. The strong, sample-specific dependence on field angle (optimum at φ = 20° for Ring 1 and 55° for Ring 2, with a sparse and noisy map in Fig. S17b) also fits pinning-dominated vortex physics.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Quantify the field alignment and the out-of-plane component.
     - Show how the switching threshold current depends on the in-plane field magnitude and on temperature.
     - Map Ic(Hz) in both bistable states. Domain-wall reconfiguration and an added vortex should change the interference pattern differently.
     - Explain why the vortex scenario is excluded.

6. The half-integer Shapiro steps (Fig. 4, Figs. S15, S16) are not shown to reflect a change in the current-phase relation of an individual junction.
   - The device is itself a two-junction SQUID, and an out-of-plane flux component from imperfect in-plane alignment frustrates it. The authors themselves note in Fig. S16 that half- and third-integer steps appear near the Ic(B) minima, where the first harmonic is suppressed. That is the ordinary SQUID or 0–π-crossover mechanism, not a domain-wall φ-junction.
   - The data are fragile: the fractional steps are not reproducible after the field is reset, since the pattern changes and even integer steps go missing (Fig. S15).
   - The dynamical regime has not been considered: at 144 MHz, hf/2e ≈ 0.3 µV is small compared with IcRN ≈ 1.4 µV for Ring 1, giving a reduced frequency of about 0.2. In that regime, capacitive or inductive dynamics can generate subharmonic steps even when the current-phase relation is sinusoidal.
   - The claim that λJ exceeds the arm width is asserted, not calculated.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Show how the integer and half-integer step widths depend on microwave power at fixed in-plane field, compared with RSJ and RCSJ calculations for a SQUID with a sinusoidal current-phase relation, finite βL and the estimated flux bias.
     - Show how the half-steps depend on frequency.
     - Give the estimate of λJ.
     - State the temperature, frequency and power for Fig. 4, which its caption omits.

7. The domain-wall toy model for the narrow, skewed central lobe is weakly constrained, does not account for the TRSB itself, and has consequences that conflict with other data (Supplementary Note S3, Fig. S11).
   - Fit constraints: the fit uses φm, a and Φint^max, plus α_I, βL and a Gaussian envelope, all fitted to about five lobes. Φint^max is poorly determined (0.16 +0.13/−0.06 Phi0). Eq. 3 couples the order parameter linearly to B in a heuristic, dimensionally loose way.
   - The model does not produce the TRSB: H → −H with chirality exchange is a symmetry of the model, so it gives a narrow central lobe but neither ΔI_TRSB nor the zero-field diode. The paper presents the two as having a common origin.
   - Consistency with other data: a spontaneous flux of about 0.16 to 0.24 Phi0 through a 1 µm ring implies chiral currents that the cited scanning-SQUID studies of Sr2RuO4 mesostructures (Refs. 28 to 30) should have detected. This needs discussion.
   - An alternative for the narrow central lobe: field-dependent flux focusing by the large Sr2RuO4 banks, which are in the Meissner state at low field and admit vortices above a few mT, could also produce it.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Show Ic(H) for both sweep directions and for minor loops. Vortex entry into the banks is hysteretic, whereas domain-wall motion confined by the necks would be largely reversible.
     - State which rings were fitted and with how many free parameters.
     - Fit a model with flux focusing included, as an alternative.

8. The anomalous-period claim depends on a normalization of Phi0 that is not clearly derived from the geometry (Fig. 2a, Fig. S11).
   - The stated area convention looks inconsistent: the caption normalises lobe widths to a flux quantum through the area π r_in r_out, but then defines r_in and r_out as "diameters".
   - The numbers do not match: using the Ring 1 dimensions in Table S1, π r_in r_out with radii gives a period of about 5.4 mT. The outer lobes in Fig. 2a, labelled 1.00 Phi0, appear to be about 4.5 to 4.7 mT wide. The same Ring 1 lobes are labelled 0.95 and 0.91 Phi0 in Fig. S11b.
   - Why it matters: the claim of departure from Phi0 periodicity, as distinct from a central lobe narrower than its neighbours, needs an absolute calibration that includes flux focusing.
   - What the authors should do: state the effective area used, compare it with the geometric and flux-focused estimates, and use the same normalisation throughout.

9. Sample statistics and reproducibility are not quantified.
   - The text gives inconsistent counts: "three rings" in the main text, "four different devices" in Supplementary Note S1, a "large selection of samples", and the bistability observed "in all samples".
   - Which sample is shown is sometimes unclear: Table S1 lists Ring 2 twice, for two FIB stages, and it is not stated which stage produced Fig. 2c.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Give the total number of devices measured, and for each signature (diode, point-symmetry violation, narrow lobe, bistability, half-steps) the number of devices in which it appeared. Report the sign of eta for each device.
     - Show whether the signals survive thermal cycling, for example as a table.
   - Why it matters: if the sign of the TRSB is random across devices and cooldowns while the magnitude stays similar, that would itself support a spontaneous origin.

10. The temperature dependence of the TRSB signal is not discussed in light of the proposed mechanism (Fig. 2c, Figs. S5, S6).
   - The magnitude of eta in Ring 2 grows from about 2% at 400 mK to 3.7% at 1000 mK, which needs an explanation within the domain-wall model.
   - In Ring 3, the "absence" of TRSB at 1200 mK is measured where Ic is only about 15 µA and the scatter is large. The data therefore set a weaker upper bound, not a demonstrated onset.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Give eta(T) on a finer temperature grid with full error bars.
     - Report the sensitivity limit at each temperature.
     - Compare the onset with Tc and with the muSR result that the TRSB and superconducting transitions coincide in unstrained crystals.

11. The pairing-symmetry discussion rests on the statement that the devices are strain-free, which the evidence does not establish.
   - The paper's argument: Tc ≤ 1.5 K "demonstrates the absence of any edge-dislocations or strain" (Methods), and this is used to reject accidental-degeneracy scenarios (Refs. 61 to 65), which require strain inhomogeneity.
   - Why Tc does not settle it: the absence of the 3K phase only bounds the strain from below. The devices are FIB-milled, capped with SiOx or Au, and sit on SrTiO3, so differential thermal contraction during cooling to 50 mK can leave inhomogeneous strain of order 0.1%. That would barely move Tc but matters in those theories. Measured Tc values of 1.3 to 1.4 K, below the bulk value (Table S1), may even indicate disorder or strain.
   - What the authors should do: soften the claim, or support it with strain measurements or estimates such as Raman, X-ray diffraction or finite-element thermal-contraction modelling.
   - On d_xz + i d_yz: the paper itself notes that heat-capacity data conflict with this pairing. Its argument in favour should be presented as one possibility among others.

C. Minor

12. The Shapiro-step evidence (Fig. 1c, d) would be stronger with the step widths plotted against microwave power and fitted to Bessel-function or RSJ forms.
   - The power axis gives the source power (up to 20 dBm), not the power at the sample. Ic is barely suppressed at "maximum power", which suggests weak coupling.
   - Comment on microwave heating and on the effective electron temperature.

13. Several captions and labels need correcting:
   - The Fig. S11 caption refers to a panel (d) that does not exist.
   - The Fig. S14 caption refers to "Figure 4 of the main text" instead of Fig. 3, writes "I+ and I+" for I+ and I−, and gives −20 mT for both panels.
   - The Fig. S10 caption reads "absence of no TRSB".
   - The Fig. 2 caption calls r_in and r_out diameters.
   - Ref. 62 contains a stray "textit".

14. Contact resistances and the measurement circuit are not described: filtering, the four-probe configuration within the ring, and the current-source and voltmeter setup.
   - These matter for judging the size of any even-in-current voltage (concern 2) and the heating at currents above 100 µA.

15. The bias directions on the device need to be stated. The definition of φ in Fig. S17 (the "polar angle in the xy-plane") should be shown on the SEM image of each ring.
   - The magnitude of the in-plane field should be reconciled between the main text (10 mT for Fig. 4, 20 mT for Fig. 3) and Fig. S17b, which shows no hysteresis below 10 mT.

16. The claim that Cai et al.'s resistance oscillations (Refs. 48, 49) "should be interpreted" as Josephson critical-current oscillations is too strong.
   - The authors' own devices differ in geometry and thickness. It should be phrased as a possibility unless the authors can show the same Shapiro response in comparable structures.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add an on-chip TRS-preserving reference, such as a conventional-superconductor ring of identical geometry or the MoGe/Ag SQUID, measured in the same cooldown. Use it to calibrate H0 independently and to run every protocol in the paper as a null control.
2. Report cooldown statistics: eta(0) and ΔI_TRSB over many zero-field cooldowns with the field nulled, and after cooling in controlled ±Hz. Present these as the main discriminant between spontaneous chiral domains and trapped flux.
3. Map Ic(Hz) in both bistable states, and map how the switching thresholds depend on the in-plane field magnitude, angle and temperature. This would separate domain-wall reconfiguration from Lorentz-force-driven vortex motion and would give the toy model independent constraints.
4. Show the robustness checks on the raw data in the main text or a dedicated supplementary figure: ΔI_TRSB against voltage threshold, polarity order, sweep direction and the even-in-current voltage component. This makes clear that the few-percent effect is not an extraction artifact.
