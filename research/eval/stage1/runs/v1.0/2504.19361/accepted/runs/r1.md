Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports transport on FIB-structured single-crystal Sr2RuO4 rings that have no superconducting contacts. It claims that the spontaneously formed Josephson junctions in these rings break time-reversal symmetry, based on four signatures: a violation of Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)|, a zero-field Josephson diode effect of a few percent, a narrowed and skewed central SQUID lobe, and current-switchable bistable Ic states with half-integer Shapiro steps under in-plane field. The authors interpret all of these as superconducting domain walls of a two-component order parameter. A phase-sensitive, contact-free test of TRSB on the microscale in Sr2RuO4 would matter, both for the pairing debate and for field-free Josephson diodes. At present, however, the TRSB inference rests on a symmetry test whose main conventional loopholes (field history, flux focusing and vortex entry in the large Sr2RuO4 banks, and the fitted remanent-field offset) are not closed.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Microstructures contacted only with normal-metal leads, so any Josephson effect comes from Sr2RuO4 itself. Shapiro steps appear in rings and in the cut, singly connected Ring 2 (Fig. 1c, Fig. S2).
- The data-analysis pipeline is described explicitly: offset subtraction, threshold criterion, bootstrap and remanent-field symmetrisation (S2, S7). It is applied to a conventional MoGe/Ag SNS SQUID that passes the same test (Fig. S10, S12).
- The phenomenology is rich and reproduced across several devices: the anomalous central lobe in Rings 1–3, the bistable Ic with a field-reversal rule (Fig. 3, S14), and the angle map of the hysteresis (S17).
- The data are openly deposited.
- If the main claim holds, the result links bulk μSR and Kerr TRSB to a device-level, phase-sensitive observable.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The TRSB test is not performed under time-reversed protocols, and the remanent-field offset is fitted by minimising the very asymmetry being tested (Fig. 2, S2, S7).
   - The identity Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| holds only when the two branches are taken under protocols related by time reversal. If the system has any history dependence, the time-reverse of an up-sweep at -H is a down-sweep at +H. Sources of history dependence include trapped flux or vortices in the large Sr2RuO4 banks and leads, fluxoid-state hysteresis, and the mobile domains the authors themselves invoke.
   - The paper does not state whether Ic+ and Ic- at each field come from the same field sweep or from separate sweeps, nor the sweep direction of each. It does not show ΔI_TRSB for both sweep directions; the only statement is that the central-lobe width is independent of sweep direction.
   - H0 is obtained by minimising ∫[Ic+(H) − |Ic-(-H)|]² (Eq. 9/10). This removes any uniform shift by construction, but it cannot remove a sweep-dependent offset, a field-dependent flux-focusing nonlinearity, or magnet hysteresis. The fitted H0 values, their uncertainty and their variation between cooldowns are not reported for any Sr2RuO4 dataset.
   - In Fig. 2b and 2c, ΔI_TRSB has spikes and sawtooth jumps concentrated at the lobe minima, where |dIc/dH| is largest. This is what a residual field offset or a sweep-direction-dependent field error would produce.
   - Requested:
     - ΔI_TRSB computed from up-sweep Ic+(H) against down-sweep |Ic-(-H)|, and vice versa.
     - A plot of ΔI_TRSB against dIc/dH to show it is not proportional to the slope.
     - The fitted H0 for every dataset.
     - An independent zero-field reference in the same cooldown, for example a conventional SQUID or Hall sensor on the same chip, or a magnet reset/quench protocol. Without this, the "zero-field" diode at the fitted H = 0 is defined relative to a fitted origin rather than measured.

2. Conventional flux focusing and vortex entry in the Sr2RuO4 banks can produce a narrowed, skewed central lobe followed by recovery of Φ0 periodicity, and this is not excluded (Fig. 2a, S5, S11).
   - The rings are attached to large superconducting banks of 250–700 nm thick Sr2RuO4 in perpendicular field (Fig. 1b, Table S1). At low field the Meissner banks focus flux into the loop, which enlarges the effective area and narrows the lobes. Once vortices enter the banks, which can happen at a few mT given the small Hc1 and the strong demagnetisation of a flake in perpendicular field, the focusing changes and the period relaxes. Pinned vortices also make the pattern history-dependent, which feeds concern 1.
   - The outer-lobe periods are normalised to "1.00 Φ0" using an assumed area πr_in·r_out. The absolute periods in mT and the effective area that reproduces them are not given, so the flux-focusing factor cannot be judged.
   - The authors' own data weaken the link between the anomalous lobe and TRSB. Ring 3 at 1200 mK is described as showing "normal SQUID behavior" and no significant TRSB (text p. 6 of SI, Fig. S5a), yet its central lobe is 0.82 Φ0 against 0.92–0.93 Φ0 for its neighbours.
   - The MoGe/Ag control SQUID (Fig. S8) has a different geometry and thickness and lacks the wide banks and necks. It is therefore not a matched null device for these effects.
   - Requested:
     - A conventional-superconductor replica with the same ring, neck and bank geometry and comparable thickness and λ, measured with the same protocol and analysis.
     - The dependence of the central-lobe width and of ΔI_TRSB on the maximum field of the preceding sweep (minor loops).
     - Absolute periods and effective areas.

3. Sign, reproducibility and error budget of the TRSB and zero-field diode are not established (Fig. 2, S5, S6, S2).
   - In a chiral-domain picture, the sign of ΔI_TRSB and of η(H = 0) should be set by which chirality occupies which side of the wall. The sign should therefore be trainable by field-cooling in ±Hz, and either random or deterministic across thermal cycles above Tc. None of these tests is shown. Field-cooling with opposite Hz flipping the sign of η would be strong, direct evidence for chirality-related TRSB.
   - The quoted uncertainties come from a parametric bootstrap of voltage noise only (S2). This bootstrap "only accounts for the uncertainty in measured voltage". It omits:
     - switching-current stochasticity;
     - sensitivity to the choice of the 0.1 versus 0.2 I0R_N threshold;
     - the uncertainty in H0;
     - temperature drift.
   - Temperature drift alone produced a 1 µA apparent asymmetry in the control SQUID (Fig. S10). This is comparable to the 2–4% diode in Ring 2 (Fig. S6), whose measurements are taken at up to 1000 mK, close to Tc ≈ 1.35 K, where dIc/dT is large.
   - Requested:
     - Repeated sweeps giving an empirical spread of ΔI_TRSB.
     - η(0) for both threshold choices.
     - Thermometer stability during each map.
     - The sign of η across devices and across cooldowns.

4. The half-integer Shapiro steps have a trivial explanation that the paper itself acknowledges, and that explanation is not excluded for Fig. 4 (Fig. 4, S15, S16).
   - In a two-junction loop biased near half a flux quantum, the first harmonic of the effective current-phase relation is suppressed and half-integer steps appear. Fig. S16 states exactly this for the steps near the Ic(B) minima.
   - The low-Ic state in Fig. 4 may simply sit closer to a SQUID minimum. Two mechanisms could put it there: a perpendicular field component from small misalignment of the 10 mT in-plane field (1° gives ~0.2 mT, a sizable fraction of the ~5 mT period), or a different trapped-flux configuration in the banks.
   - The steps are also not reproducible. After resetting the field, they disappear from one polarity and an integer step goes missing (Fig. S15).
   - The temperature and the microwave power at the sample for Fig. 4 are not given.
   - Requested:
     - The perpendicular-field calibration and alignment procedure.
     - Ic(Hz) maps at fixed in-plane field for both Ic states, to locate each state on the SQUID pattern.
     - Shapiro maps against power and Hz for both states.
     - Evidence that the half-integer steps persist where the first harmonic is not suppressed, i.e. at SQUID maxima.

B. Should address

5. The bistable Ic states are not distinguished from vortex or flux trapping driven by the training current (Fig. 3, S14, S17).
   - A supercritical training current exerts a Lorentz force on vortices in the banks and arms. Its direction depends on the sign of both the current and the field, which matches the observed rule that the roles of the training polarities swap when Hx is reversed (S14). Different pinned vortex configurations then give different Ic.
   - The hysteresis map (S17b) is noisy. The optimal angle differs between samples (20° vs 55°), and the authors find no correspondence with sample geometry. This fits a misalignment-dependent perpendicular component better than a property of the order parameter.
   - Requested:
     - Out-of-plane tilt dependence.
     - Persistence of each state when the field is removed and re-applied.
     - The temperature dependence of the switching threshold.
     - The threshold current as a function of Hx.
     - Whether the two states map onto two different positions on the Ic(Hz) SQUID pattern.

6. The toy model does not address the headline TRSB, and its form is inconsistent (S3, Fig. S11).
   - As written, the domain-wall angle φD(H) is odd in H about π/2 (Eq. 4). The model therefore preserves Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| and produces no zero-field diode. It accounts only for the narrow central lobe, which (concern 2) has conventional alternatives.
   - Eq. 5 gives Φ = Φext + 2Φ_int^max at H = 0 (φD = π/2). This contradicts the statement that the domain contributions cancel at zero field. Presumably (φD − π/2)/(π/2) was intended.
   - Eq. 3 mixes order-parameter amplitudes and fields without a stated GL functional.
   - The fit in Fig. S11 involves many parameters on five lobes: φm, a, Φ_int^max, the RCSJ αI and βL, and the Gaussian envelope width. The parameters and their posteriors are not listed, and the lobe heights are visibly missed.
   - The caption refers to a panel (d) that does not exist.
   - Requested:
     - Report all parameters.
     - Fit both current branches simultaneously.
     - Show whether any model variant reproduces the measured ΔI_TRSB(H).

7. The inference about pairing symmetry and the exclusion of strain-driven scenarios go beyond the evidence (Discussion pp. 14–15, Methods).
   - The claim that Tc ≤ 1.5 K demonstrates "the absence of any edge-dislocations or strain" is not justified. Resistive Tc only shows that no percolating 3K-phase path exists. Strains well below those that raise Tc can split the TRSB and superconducting transitions [25]. Several sources of strain are not quantified: thermal-contraction mismatch with the SrTiO3 substrate, the Au/SiOx cap, and Ga-implanted edges.
   - The Tc values span 1.3–1.5 K across devices (Table S1), which suggests disorder varies from device to device.
   - The text itself notes that the favoured dxz+idyz state conflicts with heat-capacity and elastocaloric results [59, 60, 66, 67].
   - The statement that these features "cannot originate from s-wave superconductivity" should be restricted to what has been excluded.
   - Requested:
     - Temper the pairing conclusions.
     - Estimate the cool-down strain.
     - Discuss whether strain-inhomogeneity models [64, 65] could produce mobile walls.

8. The number of devices and the selection are unclear (main text p. 5, S1).
   - The main text says data come from "three rings" and that the features are "universally observed in a large selection of samples". S1 says four devices. TRSB is shown for Rings 1–3 only, Ring 3 only below 1100 mK, and Ring 2 across several FIB stages.
   - Requested: a table of every device measured, its geometry, whether Shapiro steps, TRSB, the anomalous lobe and bistability were observed, and the sign of η. Without this, "universal" cannot be assessed, and the device-to-device spread cannot be judged.

9. Where the junctions are and what they are is not established (Fig. 1, S2, S3).
   - The arms and necks are a few ξ0 wide. Near Tc, ξ(T) grows, so the necks may act as conventional constriction (Dayem-type) weak links or host phase-slip centres. Both show Shapiro steps.
   - The argument for intrinsic domain-wall junctions rests on the earlier comparison with 3K-phase rings [23], not on data in this paper.
   - The Gaussian Ic(B) of the cut ring (S3) is admitted to be unexpected for the clean limit.
   - Fig. 1c supports frequency scaling with only two frequencies. The power dependence in Fig. 1d is shown as a histogram without the expected Bessel-function step-width analysis, and the power is given at the source rather than at the sample.
   - Requested:
     - Step widths against microwave amplitude, compared with RSJ.
     - Ic·R_N per junction.
     - A geometric control: a ring of the same material without necks, or necks of varying width.

10. The temperature dependence does not test the expected link to the TRSB transition (Fig. 2c, S5, S6).
    - η(0) grows from −2.0% at 400 mK to −3.7% at 1000 mK, from only three points (S6). Ring 3 shows TRSB at 1100 mK but not at 1200 mK.
    - If the effect reflects bulk TRSB, which μSR places at Tc, the onset temperature relative to the resistive Tc and the behaviour as T → Tc are diagnostic. A growth towards Tc is also the trend expected from drift artefacts (concern 3).
    - Requested: η(T) and ΔI_TRSB(T) on a fine grid up to Tc, with error bars, for at least two devices.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Field-cool in +Hz and −Hz, and thermally cycle above Tc several times, recording the sign of η(0) and ΔI_TRSB each time. Sign reversal under training is the most direct link between the transport asymmetry and chirality.
2. Fabricate an Nb or MoGe replica with the same geometry, including banks, necks and thickness, measure it with identical protocols and analysis, and report it as the null device for the central-lobe anomaly, ΔI_TRSB and the bistability.
3. Image the device with scanning SQUID or another local magnetic probe, ideally at fields across the bistable states, to locate the junctions and any trapped vortices in the banks.
4. Measure the current-phase relation directly (for example by embedding the singly connected Ring 2 geometry in an rf-SQUID or an asymmetric interferometer) to test for the φ0 offset and the second harmonic predicted for a chiral domain wall, independently of Shapiro-step interpretation.
