Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors study FIB-structured single-crystal Sr2RuO4 rings that have no superconducting contacts, where Josephson coupling appears intrinsically. They report that the interference pattern violates Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)|, that there is a zero-field Josephson diode effect of a few percent, that the central lobe is anomalously narrow (about 0.73 Phi0), and that under in-plane fields there are current-switchable bistable Ic states and half-integer Shapiro steps. They interpret all of this as chiral superconducting domain walls of a two-component order parameter. If the time-reversal-symmetry-breaking (TRSB) signal is shown to be intrinsic, this would be a phase-sensitive, microscopic transport probe of TRSB in Sr2RuO4, complementing muSR and Kerr. It would also be a route to field-free Josephson diodes without proximity engineering, which would interest both the Sr2RuO4 community and the superconducting-diode community. The novelty depends heavily on whether extrinsic sources of broken time-reversal symmetry in the measurement and in the sample's magnetic history can be excluded. The present manuscript does not yet do this convincingly.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The point-symmetry test Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| is the right symmetry criterion. Using it is better than reporting a diode at finite field.
- The analysis pipeline (offset subtraction, threshold criterion, bootstrap errors, remanent-field symmetrisation) is described in detail. It is validated on a conventional MoGe/Ag SQUID (Figs. S8–S10, S12) and on synthetic data (Fig. S18).
- The devices have no superconducting contacts (Ti/Au leads), so all superconductivity comes from Sr2RuO4. This removes the interface ambiguity of earlier Nb/Sr2RuO4 hybrid studies.
- The same ring (Ring 2) is re-milled several times (Fig. S1), and a singly connected version still shows Shapiro steps (Fig. S2). This is a useful, if partial, geometric control.
- The phenomenology is rich and potentially useful (switchable Ic states, polarity-selective fractional steps), and the data are deposited in a public repository.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The TRSB and zero-field diode signals are small, and the error budget behind them counts only voltage noise. Examples: Fig. 2b–c, Fig. S5b, Fig. S6, and Eq. (1) of the main text.
   - Size of the signal. ΔI_TRSB is a few µA on Ic of 60–140 µA, and |η| is 2–3.7% (Fig. S6, three points, no error bars).
   - What the error bars include. Section S2 states that the bootstrap "only accounts for the uncertainty in measured voltage". It does not include the uncertainty in H0, the field-axis uncertainty (which matters most on the steep flanks near the Ic minima, exactly where ΔI_TRSB in Fig. 2b spikes), temperature drift, or the choice of voltage threshold (0.1 or 0.2 I0RN "depending on the degree of rounding").
   - Drift is comparable to the signal. The authors' own control SQUID (Fig. S10) shows a systematic ΔI of up to about 1 µA on about 78 µA (about 1.3%), which they attribute to temperature drift. That is the same order as the diode reported for Ring 2.
   - The shape of ΔI_TRSB(H). In Fig. 2c (Ring 2), ΔI_TRSB(H) ramps monotonically across each lobe and jumps at the Ic minima. This sawtooth is what a residual, field-dependent displacement between the two compared branches produces (ΔI ≈ δH·dIc/dH). One global H0 cannot remove such a displacement, for example a magnet or flux-trapping offset that depends on |H| or on the sweep history.
   - Sweep direction. Time reversal also reverses the direction of the field sweep. The correct comparison is therefore Ic+(H) on an up-sweep against |Ic-(-H)| on a down-sweep, and vice versa. The manuscript does not say how sweeps were ordered or whether both directions were measured. The statement that the narrow lobe is independent of sweep direction (p. 8) is not shown.

   The authors should:
   - state the sweep protocol and whether Ic+ and Ic- come from the same IV trace;
   - show ΔI_TRSB for up- and down-sweeps and for the time-reversed pairing of the two;
   - repeat the same field scan several times to give a run-to-run spread;
   - propagate the H0 uncertainty (report H0 and its confidence interval for each dataset) and the threshold choice into ΔI_TRSB and η, showing that η(H=0) holds for both thresholds and across the H0 interval;
   - fit ΔI_TRSB(H) to a·dIc/dH + b plus a field-dependent offset and show the residual that remains.

   Ideally they would also fix H0 independently of the data being tested, with a Hall sensor or a conventional reference SQUID mounted alongside in the same cooldown, analysed identically, and shown to give ΔI_TRSB = 0. Without these steps the central claim that the junctions break time-reversal symmetry is not established beyond extrinsic asymmetry.

2. Sample-level TRS breaking by trapped flux has not been ruled out. The rings sit between large Sr2RuO4 banks (Figs. 1b, S1). The field scans span ±10 mT, and in-plane fields of 10–70 mT are applied in other experiments (Figs. 3, 4, S16, S17). Abrikosov vortices or flux pinned in the banks, the necks or the arms would break time-reversal symmetry of the device without any TRSB in the order parameter. A vortex near a junction is a known mechanism for zero-field Josephson diodes and for non-shift distortions of Ic(H). Its contribution is not a uniform offset, so the H0 symmetrisation does not remove it. The manuscript does not record the magnetic history before each Ic(H) dataset (zero-field-cooled or not, the previous maximum field, magnet reset).

   The authors should:
   - report ΔI_TRSB and η for zero-field-cooled states after warming above Tc, over several thermal cycles;
   - give the sign and magnitude statistics of η across cycles and devices. The sign is positive for Ring 1 (Fig. 2b, ΔI_TRSB(0) ≈ +4 µA) and negative for Ring 2 (Fig. S6). Random signs set at Tc would support spontaneous domain formation; history-dependent signs would point to trapped flux;
   - test whether field-cooling in small ±Hz trains the sign of η, as reported for the Kerr signal and assumed in the toy model of S3;
   - show that η is unchanged after a field excursion below Tc, which would rule out a vortex-pinning origin.

B. Should address

3. Device statistics are not given, so "universal" cannot be judged.
   - Coverage. The main text says the data come from three rings, while S1 says four devices. TRSB is shown for Ring 1 at 50 mK, Ring 2 at 400–1000 mK and Ring 3 only at 1100 mK. The bistable Ic state is shown for Ring 3 in the main text and Ring 1 in the SI.
   - Unquantified statements. "Universally observed in a large selection of samples" (p. 5) and "observed in all samples, irrespective of device geometry" (p. 11) are not quantified.
   - What to add. A table giving, for every device measured (including those not shown), whether it shows Shapiro steps, a narrow central lobe, significant ΔI_TRSB (with value and sign), a zero-field diode and bistability, and at which temperatures. Devices that showed none of these should be included. This would make the claims checkable, and the reproducibility of the TRSB signal across devices is a key part of the argument.

4. The half-integer Shapiro steps (Fig. 4) have conventional explanations that are not excluded.
   - Flux through the junction cross-section. An in-plane field of 10–20 mT threads roughly one flux quantum through the arm cross-section (thickness 0.25–0.7 µm times an effective junction length of about 2λ plus the wall width). The junctions are therefore modulated in a Fraunhofer-like way. Near a node the first harmonic is suppressed and fractional steps appear. The authors acknowledge this in S6 and find nodes near 4.5 and 9.9 mT in Fig. S16. Fig. 4 is taken at 10 mT, which is close to such a node, although at a different field angle.
   - Misalignment. A misalignment of a few degrees gives a perpendicular component of a fraction of a mT. This is a sizeable fraction of the about 4–5 mT SQUID period of Ring 1, and an asymmetric two-junction SQUID biased near half a flux quantum also produces half-integer steps. Field alignment accuracy is not stated anywhere.
   - Reproducibility. Fig. S15 shows that re-applying the same field changes the step structure.

   The authors should:
   - state the alignment procedure and its uncertainty;
   - map the fractional-step amplitude against in-plane field magnitude and angle and against perpendicular trim field;
   - show that the half-steps in the low-Ic state are not at a node of the in-plane modulation or at a half-flux SQUID bias;
   - report frequency, power and temperature for Fig. 4 (only S15 gives 145 MHz and 50 mK);
   - show the power dependence of the half-integer step widths.

   Until then, "a different current-phase relation between the two Ic states" should be presented as one possibility.

5. The bistable Ic states (Fig. 3, S14, S17) also have conventional explanations.
   - In-plane vortices. Polarity-selective switching that reverses when the field is reversed is what one expects from in-plane vortices in the banks or arms that are moved by the Lorentz force of the bias current. That force reverses with either I or H. Fig. S17 states that no hysteresis appears below 10 mT, consistent with a vortex-entry threshold. The argument based on the Josephson penetration depth (p. 11) addresses only the half-integer steps, not the bistability.
   - Fluxoid states. If the ring inductance parameter β_L is not small, current-induced transitions between fluxoid states of the loop could also give two Ic branches.

   The authors should:
   - estimate the in-plane lower critical field and entry field of the banks and arms;
   - report the fitted or estimated β_L and junction asymmetry. The modulation depth of about 35% in Fig. 2a requires either strong asymmetry or a sizeable β_L;
   - show how the training-current threshold depends on in-plane field magnitude and temperature;
   - show whether bistability survives in the singly connected Ring 2;
   - repeat the S17 angular map, which looks speckled because each pixel is a single IV trace, to establish that the optimal angle (20° for Ring 1, 55° for Ring 2) is reproducible.

6. Neither the narrow central lobe nor the toy model is tied uniquely to TRSB.
   - Narrowing without TRSB. In Fig. S5a (Ring 3, 1200 mK) the central lobe is already narrowed to 0.82 Phi0, yet no statistically significant TRSB is present. The narrowing can therefore occur without measurable TRSB, which weakens the argument that both come from chirality-driven domain-wall motion.
   - Free parameters. The model in S3 is phenomenological: a sigmoid with φ_m, a and Φ_int^max as free parameters, plus envelope and amplitude.
   - Symmetry of the model. As written (Eqs. 4–5), it is symmetric about H = 0. It produces neither the skew of the central lobe nor ΔI_TRSB nor the diode.
   - Fit quality. The fits in Fig. S11 miss the lobe heights noticeably.
   - Size of Φ_int^max. The fitted values of 0.16–0.24 Phi0 imply mean spontaneous fields of order 1 mT through the loop. These should be compared with the null results of scanning-SQUID searches for edge currents in Sr2RuO4 mesostructures (refs. 28–30).
   - Alternatives. Field-dependent effective area from Meissner screening and flux focusing by the large banks, and vortex entry into the banks near the first-lobe field, can also make lobes non-uniform.

   The authors should:
   - tabulate central-lobe width against temperature for each ring, together with ΔI_TRSB;
   - extend the model so that it generates the skew and the diode, or state that it does not;
   - give parameter uncertainties and correlations;
   - discuss the consistency with the scanning-SQUID bounds.

7. Two links in the junction argument are not tested in this work.
   - Shapiro steps. Steps at V = nΦ0f (Fig. 1c–d) show phase-locked phase slips. They do not by themselves show a localised domain-wall junction: phase-slip centres and constriction (Dayem-like) weak links of width a few ξ(T) also give them. The arms are about 0.35 µm wide, roughly 5ξ0, and ξ(T) grows near Tc. Fig. 1d shows step occupancy against power but not the Bessel-function dependence of the step widths, which would test whether the junction is RSJ-like.
   - Material null. The control showing that geometry and FIB processing alone do not create these junctions (3K-phase rings without oscillations) is cited from earlier work (refs. 23, 47) and is not repeated here under matched conditions and analysis.

   The authors should:
   - add the Bessel analysis of the step widths;
   - show at least one ring of identical geometry, made by the same FIB process from a conventional single-crystal superconductor or a 3K-phase sample, measured with the same Ic(H) and TRSB analysis, in which the effects are absent.

8. The temperature dependence of the TRSB signal, and the strain-free assumption behind the pairing discussion, are not addressed.
   - Temperature dependence. In Ring 3, TRSB is absent at 1200 mK and present at 1100 mK (Fig. S5), and in Ring 2 |η| grows from 2% to 3.7% between 400 and 1000 mK (Fig. S6). An onset of TRSB below the junction Tc is what split-transition (strain or accidental-degeneracy) scenarios predict. That is the scenario the Discussion (pp. 14–15) argues against.
   - Tc and strain. The argument that "Tc never exceeds 1.5 K" proves the absence of strain (pp. 5, 16) is not sufficient. Modest strain from differential thermal contraction against SrTiO3, or from the SiOx or Au capping, changes Tc only slightly but can lift a degeneracy. Table S1 already shows Tc spread from 1.3 to 1.5 K.
   - Which Tc. Table S1 lists Tc = 1.5 K for Ring 3, while the text calls 1200 mK "very close to Tc".

   The authors should:
   - present ΔI_TRSB and η against T up to the junction Tc for at least one device, with error bars;
   - state which Tc is meant;
   - temper the conclusions that disfavour accidental-degeneracy scenarios and favour d_xz + i d_yz accordingly.

9. The scope of the conclusions should match the evidence.
   - "Cannot originate from an s-wave superconductor." The abstract and Conclusion say the combination of features cannot originate from an s-wave superconductor. Individually, the in-plane-field features have conventional counterparts (concerns 4 and 5). Only the zero-field TRSB is decisive, and it depends on concerns 1 and 2.
   - Multiband alternative. The manuscript itself cites multiband TRSB mechanisms for diodes (refs. 38–40). It should explain why these do not apply to a single-material ring, rather than dismissing them only on the basis of Phi0-periodicity (ref. 50).
   - Novelty. The claim that such effects have so far been realised only in proximity weak links should acknowledge the similar intrinsic-junction results in CsV3Sb5 (refs. 53–56) that the authors cite.

C. Minor

10. Fig. 2 caption: r_in and r_out are called "diameters", but the area is written as π r_in r_out. With the Table S1 dimensions for Ring 1 (D_in = 0.486 µm, D_out = 1.01 µm), π r_in r_out gives a period of about 5.4 mT. The outer lobes in Fig. 2a appear to be about 4.5–4.8 mT wide yet are labelled 1.00 Phi0. Please state exactly how lobe widths are converted to Phi0 (geometric area or normalisation to the outer period) and give the effective area for each ring.
11. Eq. (10) in S7 drops the field reflection and the absolute value that appear in Eq. (9). Eq. (1) is reused for η in the SI. Please correct the equations.
12. Caption inconsistencies:
   - Fig. S10: the caption says "first three peaks" while the text says "central four periods", and "absence of no TRSB" is a double negative.
   - Fig. S11: the caption refers to a panel (d) that does not exist.
   - Fig. S14: the caption refers to "Figure 4 of the main text" (should be Fig. 3), writes "I+ and I+", and says the data are at -20 mT although panel (a) is at +20 mT.
   - Fig. S5: the caption refers to panels (a) and (b), which are unlabelled.
13. Fig. 1c gives the RF level as "maximum applied power (20 dBm)". Please give the estimated power at the sample, and say whether RF heating changes the effective temperature (compare zero-RF Ic at different source powers).
14. Fig. 2c: the bottom-panel y-axis label reads "I_c^-". The main text says Ring 2 is shown "at three temperatures" without giving the field-sweep range or step. Please add the measurement parameters (sweep step, IV sweep rate, threshold criterion used) to every Ic(H) figure caption.
15. "Multiple experimental setups" (p. 8): please name them and say which dataset was taken in which.
16. The SI analysis of the singly connected Ring 2 (Fig. S3) fits Gaussian envelopes, while the main text argues that the junctions are not in the diffusive limit. The physical meaning of the Gaussian decay should be stated more carefully, or presented as an empirical fit only.
17. Typos: "parings" (p. 15), "proprieties" (S4), "to larger extend" (S2).

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Apply the full TRSB and diode analysis to the singly connected Ring 2 (Figs. S2–S3), which appear to show unequal Ic+ and |Ic-| at zero field. A single junction without a loop removes the inductance, fluxoid and flux-focusing ambiguities and would be a cleaner test of an intrinsic φ0-like junction.
2. Add one consolidated table: for each device and temperature, give H0 with its uncertainty, ΔI_TRSB(0), η, central-lobe width, sweep direction and cooldown index. This would let readers judge reproducibility at a glance.
3. Report an in-plane-field Ic(Hx) pattern and the alignment trim procedure alongside Figs. 3–4. This would anchor the in-plane experiments relative to the Fraunhofer modulation of the junction cross-section.
4. Consider a direct local probe of the proposed domain walls, such as scanning SQUID or MFM of the ring in the low- and high-Ic states. Even a null result with stated sensitivity would constrain Φ_int^max.
