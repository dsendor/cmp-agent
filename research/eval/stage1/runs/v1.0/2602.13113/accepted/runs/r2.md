Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use time-resolved resonant diffuse scattering (tr-RDS) at the Cu L3 edge of the collinear antiferromagnet CuO to follow, with momentum resolution, the low-energy diffuse intensity that appears around the (1/2 0 -1/2) magnetic Bragg peak after 400 nm excitation. They combine it with tr-RXD and time-resolved non-resonant diffuse scattering (tr-NRDS), plus Hubbard/quantum-Boltzmann simulations, and propose a three-stage picture: near-instantaneous creation of non-thermal magnons, then picosecond magnon-magnon quasi-thermalization towards the Γ-point gap, then nanosecond recovery limited by anomalous magnon-phonon scattering and a dispersion-mismatch bottleneck. Momentum-resolved access to low-energy magnon populations on femtosecond-to-nanosecond timescales would be a real methodological advance. The central interpretation rests on attributing a fitted, energy-integrated diffuse "upturn" to a non-thermal magnon population, and the paper does not yet exclude the main alternatives.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new and potentially general observable: energy-integrated, resonant diffuse magnetic scattering with ~60 fs time resolution and fine momentum resolution near a magnetic Bragg peak. It has much higher count rates than tr-RIXS.
- Several checks support a magnetic origin of the upturn: resonant enhancement at the Cu L3 edge (Fig. 2e), π/σ polarization dependence (Fig. 2g), a fluence threshold tied to demagnetization (Fig. 2f), symmetry of the amplitude about q_RXD along b* (Fig. 3f) and anisotropy between b* and [h0h].
- Coverage of three timescales and two subsystems (spin via tr-RDS/tr-RXD, lattice via tr-NRDS), with a paramagnetic-phase comparison for the NRDS (Fig. 4d).
- A microscopic kinetic model (magnon-magnon QBE plus magnon-phonon scattering channels) rather than only a multi-temperature phenomenology. Momentum-dependent rates (Fig. 3i) and a self-similar scaling exponent are compared with theory.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Thermal (heated) critical spin fluctuations are not excluded as the origin of the diffuse upturn. All tr-RDS data are taken at 190 K, only ~23 K below T_N1 = 213 K, and the incommensurate phase lies just above (213-230 K) (Methods, Fig. S5). A rough estimate for 3 mJ/cm² absorbed within the ~450 nm penetration depth gives an energy density of order 50-70 J/cm³. With a CuO heat capacity of roughly 3 J/(cm³·K), that is a near-surface temperature rise of order 15-20 K once spins and lattice equilibrate, which brings the probed 18 nm layer to or near T_N1. Near T_N, thermally populated spin fluctuations produce exactly this kind of critical diffuse scattering around the magnetic Bragg peak. It would be resonant, π-enhanced and symmetric about q_RXD, rise as the order parameter falls, and recover as the order recovers. The fluence dependence in Fig. 2f fits this picture as well: an onset with demagnetization, then a decrease at high fluence, which the text itself attributes to approaching T_N. The paper's distinctive claim is a *non-thermal* magnon population that later quasi-thermalizes, so it has to separate the transient signal from an equilibrium hot-spin state. The temperature estimate is in Supplementary Note 3, and the main text should state the resulting spin and lattice temperatures at 4.5 ps and at 1-10 ns. Requested: static (pump-off) RDS q-profiles at equilibrium temperatures between 190 K and T_N1 (and through the incommensurate phase), measured in the same geometry, and compared directly with the transient profiles at matched RXD suppression. Also wanted: a demonstration that the transient q-dependence (Fig. 3e,f) differs from the equilibrium critical lineshape at the same order-parameter reduction. Measurements at a lower base temperature, where heating stays well below T_N, would test whether the upturn and its picosecond dynamics persist away from criticality.

2. The separation of "RDS" from "RXD tail" signals depends on the model, and the key timescales are at the edge of the measurement window. The upturn component, its amplitude A_RDS and time constant τ_RDS (Fig. 3a,d,f) come from fitting each trace with two exponentials. Near q_RXD the RXD tail dominates: the q = 0.010 trace drops to about -5 a.u. while the upturn is of order +0.2 a.u. (Fig. 3a inset). The Bragg-peak top is saturated (Fig. 2c) and the central region is excluded (hatched, Fig. 3c-f). Meanwhile τ_RDS reaches ~3-4 ps at small |q| (Fig. 3f), comparable to the 4 ps delay window of Fig. 3a. The linear, V-shaped 1/τ_RDS(q) (Fig. 3i), which is the main evidence for momentum-dependent redistribution, therefore rests on the points least constrained by the fit. The authors should:
   - show the full fit residuals and parameter correlations (amplitude-τ covariance) for the inner q points;
   - test whether alternative decompositions change τ_RDS(q). For example, model the RXD contribution from the measured, unsaturated static Bragg lineshape scaled by the RXD time trace, rather than fitting it freely;
   - extend the delay range at small |q| so that τ ≈ 4 ps is actually sampled to several time constants;
   - state whether the line in Fig. 3i is a fit (through the origin or not) and what functional form the QBE predicts for 1/τ(q).

   The claim that the "slight deviation from the symmetric behavior... is due to a small error in the definition of the origin" (Fig. 3a caption) should be quantified. An origin error of the order of the quoted 5×10⁻³ r.l.u. resolution is half the smallest |q| used.

3. The comparison between the simulation and the data is not shown quantitatively, and the simulation's momentum grid looks too coarse for the q range measured. The QBE is computed on a 32×48×32 k-grid in the magnetic Brillouin zone (Methods). This means a grid spacing of order 1/48 ≈ 0.02-0.03 r.l.u. along b*, while the measured tr-RDS spans |q| = 0.010-0.080 r.l.u. (Fig. 3a). The whole experimental window therefore covers only a few grid points, yet Fig. 3g,h are drawn as smooth maps. The excitation parameters were also "chosen to be consistent with the experiment" (Methods), so it is unclear which features are predicted rather than tuned. Fig. 3g (simulation) and Fig. 3e (data) are only compared by eye, on different colour scales and time ranges (0-6 ps vs 0-4 ps). The authors should:
   - state how Fig. 3g,h are obtained on this grid (interpolation, a finer grid near Γ, smoothing);
   - show convergence of τ(q) with grid density near Γ;
   - list which parameters are fitted and which are fixed;
   - overlay the simulated and measured τ_RDS(q) and A_RDS(q) on the same axes (Fig. 3f,i).

   There is also an inconsistency to resolve. The text says Fig. 3j-n is the "calculated" magnon distribution, but the caption says it is "derived from tr-RDS data using Eq. (2)". If the latter is true, explain how a 3D distribution f_μ(q,t) and its energy axis are inverted from a 1D energy-integrated cut.

4. The nanosecond recovery mechanism (anomalous magnon-phonon scattering, dispersion bottleneck) is inferred from a timescale correlation across two experiments with mismatched conditions, and lattice cooling or heat transport is not excluded. Concerns:
   - tr-NRDS was measured at ESRF with ~100 ps pulses, a 1 kHz pump (versus 100 Hz at SwissFEL), an 88° pump incidence (penetration ~500 nm) and a 1.5 µm X-ray probe depth. tr-RDS probes ~18 nm. NRDS therefore averages over a strongly inhomogeneous depth profile that includes unexcited bulk, and the effective absorbed fluence and quasi-static heat build-up at 1 kHz are not stated.
   - The ~2 ns NRDS enhancement and the ~7 ns RXD/RDS recovery (Fig. 4d) are different timescales, and the NRDS data are sparse with large error bars. "Indicating phonon creation during the magnon annihilation process" is a qualitative correlation.
   - The RXD in Fig. 4d recovers only partially by 12 ns. The residual suppression, and possibly part of the 7 ns component, could reflect lattice temperature and heat redistribution in the near-surface layer rather than intrinsic magnon-phonon annihilation. Supplementary Note 11 addresses the microsecond phonon timescale, but the nanosecond component needs the same treatment.
   - The PM reference temperature is not stated in the main text, and the phonon population and scattering rates there differ from 190 K.

   Requested: a quantitative estimate of the lattice temperature evolution in the 18 nm RDS probe depth from 0.1 to 20 ns (including heat diffusion); the PM measurement conditions; and either a direct comparison of the simulated magnon-phonon decay rate with the measured 7 ns recovery without extra free parameters, or a statement of which parameter is fitted. The effective coupling of -59 µeV is "estimated from the magnon relaxation timescale". That makes it an output of the model fitted to one number, so it cannot independently confirm the anomalous-process mechanism. A test that changes the predicted bottleneck would be needed to support it, for example base-temperature dependence of the nanosecond recovery (the gap and phonon occupation change with T) as a check of the predicted trend.

B. Should address

5. The resonance of the upturn differs from that of the RXD. In Fig. 2e the RDS energy dependence peaks near ~931.5-932 eV, close to the XAS maximum, while the RXD intensity peaks near ~930.5 eV and dips at the XAS maximum. The authors attribute the difference to reduced self-absorption from a near-surface magnon density. However, photodoping (doublons and holons) and heating can shift or broaden the L3 resonance and change the refractive index. A transient change in absorption or resonance position would alter the diffuse tail intensity at a fixed photon energy, even without any change in spin correlations. Please show the transient XAS (or an equivalent fluorescence yield measurement) at 3 mJ/cm² and quantify how much of the upturn could come from a change in self-absorption or resonance shift. Model the expected energy dependence of a purely magnetic upturn, including the depth profile.

6. The arguments against a correlation-length change or a phase transition are made only in the SI and depend on saturated data. The main text says the RXD peak width "remains nearly constant" (Fig. S4), which excludes changes in long-range-order correlation length, Kibble-Zurek coarsening and an incommensurate transition (Supplementary Note 4). The peak top is saturated in the shown profiles (Fig. 2c). The authors should state how the width and centre were extracted in the presence of saturation (attenuated data? the Jungfrau detector?), and give the upper bounds on a transient change in width and peak position. A shift towards the incommensurate wavevector (0.506, 0, -0.483) by a fraction of the resolution would produce asymmetric diffuse changes. The authors should show quantitatively that the measured symmetry (Fig. 3f) excludes this.

7. Only one sample, one base temperature and essentially one fluence for the momentum-resolved data. All momentum-resolved tr-RDS (Fig. 3) is at 3 mJ/cm² and 190 K on a single crystal. The quasi-thermalization picture predicts that the rates depend on the initial magnon density (fluence) and on the spin gap and thermal background (temperature). Please show τ_RDS(q) for at least one additional fluence and, if possible, another temperature. State whether the momentum-resolved results were reproduced on a second sample, a second sample position or a second beamtime.

8. The self-similarity exponent is presented as validation, but its support is thin. The power-law exponent 2.28 ± 0.08 (Fig. S10b) is compared with 2.33 from a 2D Heisenberg calculation, yet CuO is quasi-1D/3D with strong exchange anisotropy, a gap, and here a finite pump duration. Please state how many q points and delays enter the scaling collapse, how the uncertainty was obtained, and what exponent the authors' own QBE simulation gives on the same q window. Otherwise the agreement cannot discriminate between mechanisms.

9. The anisotropy and polarization analysis would be stronger with quantitative use of Eq. (1). Eq. (1) predicts specific polarization prefactors for the transverse and longitudinal correlation components. Fig. 2g reports only that π > σ "similar to" the static magnetic profile. Please compare the measured transient π/σ ratio with the static RXD π/σ ratio and with the ratio predicted for transverse magnon fluctuations. The [h0h] versus b* anisotropy (Supplementary Note 6, Fig. S7) is cited as support for the dispersion, so please compare it with the simulated anisotropy from the same spin model.

10. The generalization and priority claims are broader than the data. The abstract and Discussion say the framework "generalizes across antiferromagnetic insulators" and to "any gapped magnetic insulators, including ferromagnets", but only CuO is measured. Statements such as "has not yet been experimentally demonstrated" and "first direct visualization" should be placed against earlier time-resolved resonant scattering studies of transient magnetic correlations and diffuse magnetic scattering in pumped magnets, and against tr-RIXS of transient magnons (e.g., ref. 40). Please narrow the claims to what is shown, or add a second material.

11. The magnetic model parameters and the dispersion used for the bottleneck argument. The simplified orthorhombic cell uses J_AFM = 120 meV, J_FM = -15 meV, J_out = -18 meV and D = 0.025 meV, and is said to reproduce the neutron dispersion (Fig. S1c). The phase-space argument in Fig. 4a-c,e,f depends on how the acoustic magnon and lowest optical phonon dispersions cross. Please show the magnon and phonon dispersions together along the relevant directions in the main text or the SI, with the crossing points marked. State whether the monoclinic distortion, which is neglected, would change these crossings. Also reconcile the quoted 1.3 meV acoustic gap with the gap produced by D = 0.025 meV in the model.

C. Minor

12. Fig. 3 caption, panels j-n: the "derived from tr-RDS data" wording conflicts with the main text (see concern 3). Also, Fig. 3h and Fig. 3j-n share a colour scale ending at 13 without units or a stated normalization.
13. Fig. 2d: state the fit result (time constants with uncertainties) for the upturn and the fast drop in the main text. Currently they are only described qualitatively ("much longer time constant").
14. Fig. 4d: the "AFM RXD fit" visibly departs from the data between 2 and 8 ns. Please give the fitted time constants with uncertainties and the functional form. State that the RDS point (0.47, 0, -0.53) is displaced along [h0h], not along b* as in Fig. 3, and explain why.
15. Methods: the quoted momentum resolution (~5×10⁻³ r.l.u.) should be shown to be consistent with a 2.25° angular acceptance of the avalanche photodiode at ~930 eV. As written, an acceptance that large seems inconsistent with sub-0.01 r.l.u. resolution unless additional slits are used.
16. Methods, tr-NRDS: the sample temperature "controlled between 190 K and 300 K". Give the exact temperatures used for the AFM and PM traces in Fig. 4d and Fig. S11.
17. Eq. (4): the carrier "frequency ω = 3.1 eV" should be written as ħω. Define t₀ and E₀ and the field amplitude used.
18. Several approximate values print as "-70 fs", "-7 ns", "-59 µeV" and similar, where "~" appears to have been rendered as a minus sign. Please check, especially since -59 µeV and -6.2 meV are signed couplings.
19. The doublon lifetime (~200 fs) is cited from other cuprates (refs. 30, 31). If possible, give a CuO-specific estimate or the value used in the simulation.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add an equilibrium reference: static RDS q-profiles versus temperature up to and through T_N1/T_N2, compared directly with the transient profiles at matched RXD suppression. This is the most direct way to show that the picosecond dynamics are non-thermal.
2. Present a single figure overlaying measured and simulated τ_RDS(q), A_RDS(q) and the nanosecond recovery, with the free parameters listed and grid convergence demonstrated.
3. Add a fluence and base-temperature series for τ_RDS(q) and for the nanosecond recovery. The model predicts how both should scale, and this would turn the bottleneck argument from a correlation into a tested prediction.
4. Report the estimated spin and lattice temperatures of the probed layer versus delay (0.1 ps to 20 ns) for both the SwissFEL and ESRF conditions, including heat accumulation at 1 kHz and the different probe depths, so readers can judge how comparable the tr-RDS and tr-NRDS results are.
