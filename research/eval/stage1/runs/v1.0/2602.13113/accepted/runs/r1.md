Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use time-resolved resonant diffuse scattering (tr-RDS) at the Cu L3 edge to look at the diffuse intensity next to the (1/2 0 -1/2) magnetic Bragg peak of CuO after 400 nm excitation. They read the slow, momentum-dependent rise of this intensity as a direct, momentum-resolved view of non-thermal magnons relaxing toward the Gamma point (the zone centre, where the spin gap sits) by magnon-magnon scattering within picoseconds. Together with tr-RXD (time-resolved resonant X-ray diffraction), tr-NRDS (non-resonant diffuse scattering, a probe of the lattice) and quantum Boltzmann simulations, they then build a picture in which the order recovers over nanoseconds through "anomalous" magnon-phonon scattering, where two magnons merge into one phonon. If the diffuse signal really is magnetic, this would be the first momentum-resolved tracking of low-energy magnon populations on femtosecond-to-nanosecond scales, and an energy-integrated complement to tr-RIXS. That would interest a broad ultrafast-magnetism audience. The main risk is the step that assigns the diffuse upturn to magnon populations, and the quantitative link between the data and the microscopic model.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The tr-RDS approach is new. The signal is collected at the same time as the Bragg intensity, and the authors separate it from the Bragg-tail reduction using two-exponential fits (Fig. 3a-f).
- The magnetic origin is supported by several observations. The upturn is resonantly enhanced at the Cu L3 edge (Fig. 2e). It is larger for π than for σ incident polarization (Fig. 2g). It appears only above a fluence threshold (Fig. 2f). The Bragg peak width does not change (Fig. S4).
- The time constant depends clearly on momentum, with 1/τ_RDS rising roughly linearly with |q| (Fig. 3i). This is a distinctive observation that phonon-free or heating-only pictures do not obviously predict.
- The study combines three X-ray techniques with a microscopic quantum-kinetic model rather than a phenomenological multi-temperature model. The AFM/PM comparison in tr-NRDS (Fig. 4d) is a useful control.
- The data are deposited in a public repository.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **A transient change in absorption or in the resonant scattering factor has not been ruled out as the cause of the "RDS" upturn.**
   - What the data show: the upturn is strongest at about 931.8-932 eV, which is exactly where the static RXD shows a self-absorption dip (Fig. 2e). The authors attribute this to a depth profile of photo-created magnons near the surface. Photoexcitation of a charge-transfer insulator is also expected to change the Cu L3 absorption itself, through doublon/holon creation, edge shifts and bleaching. A pump-induced reduction of self-absorption, or a change in the real part of the resonant scattering factor, would raise the scattered intensity everywhere on the peak profile. Away from q = 0, where the multiplicative RXD loss is small in absolute terms, this could look like an "upturn".
   - Why the existing tests do not settle it: the amplitude profile A_RDS(q) in Fig. 3f follows the shape of the Bragg tail closely, which is what a multiplicative, q-independent change in the scattering factor would produce. The π/σ dependence (Fig. 2g) does not separate the two pictures either, because the Bragg tail itself has the same polarization dependence.
   - What the authors should show:
     - The ratio (I_ON - I_OFF)/I_OFF as a function of q at fixed delays (for example 0.5, 1.5 and 4 ps). A magnon redistribution predicts a q-dependent change in this ratio; a change in absorption or form factor predicts a ratio that does not depend on q.
     - Time-resolved XAS, or the full energy dependence of the signal at q_RXD and at the diffuse positions, at several delays, to measure the transient edge directly.
     - Diffuse intensity at a position where magnetic diffuse scattering should be absent but the same absorption path applies, for example a charge Bragg tail at the same energy or far from any magnetic reflection.
   - Without these, the central identification of tr-RDS with f_μ(q,t) is not secure.

2. **There is no static calibration linking diffuse intensity to magnon population, and no test of the equilibrium alternative.**
   - What is missing: the paper interprets I_RDS through Eqs. (1)-(2) and converts it to f_μ(q,t). It does not show the equilibrium diffuse intensity around (1/2 0 -1/2) as a function of temperature, for example from 150 K to just below T_N1 = 213 K, measured in the same geometry.
   - What this measurement would give:
     - a direct test that the diffuse tail scales with the thermal magnon population, as Eq. (2) predicts;
     - a calibration from which an effective magnon temperature can be read at each delay;
     - a test of whether the state at 4 ps (and at 4.5 ps in Fig. 2f) is simply a hotter, quasi-equilibrium magnet rather than the non-thermal distribution claimed.
   - Why it matters: the claims of "non-thermal magnons" and of "quasi-thermalization fulfilling Bose-Einstein statistics" both need this comparison. The authors should also fit the extracted f_μ(q, t ≥ 2 ps) to a Bose-Einstein form with free temperature and chemical potential, and report the result. A magnon-number-conserving process should give a non-zero chemical potential.

3. **The data and the quantum Boltzmann model are compared only qualitatively, and the free parameters are not stated.**
   - The comparison: Fig. 3g (simulation) and Fig. 3e (data) are shown in arbitrary units on different colour scales. The data show 1/τ_RDS rising linearly with |q| (Fig. 3i), but the solid lines there are "guides to the eye". There is no overlay of simulated and measured τ_RDS(q) or A_RDS(q).
   - Parameters not explained:
     - The initial doublon density and the "excitation parameters were chosen to be consistent with the experiment" (Methods). It is not said which observable they were tuned to.
     - The magnon-phonon coupling of -59 µeV is "estimated from the magnon relaxation timescale". This means the ~7 ns recovery is used as an input, so the model's agreement with that timescale is not an independent test.
   - Model structure that is not checked:
     - The model uses a simplified orthorhombic cell with one Cu per layer. It is not shown that this cell has both the acoustic (~1.3 meV) and optical (~23.2 meV) magnon branches that the text uses to rule out optical-magnon contributions.
     - The 30% order-parameter suppression at 3 mJ cm^-2 (Fig. 2d) implies a large magnon density. It is not shown that leading-order scattering beyond linear spin-wave theory is still valid there.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Overlay simulated and measured τ_RDS(q) and A_RDS(q) on common axes.
     - List every parameter and say whether it is fitted or fixed independently.
     - Show how the calculated rates depend on the doublon density and the coupling constants.
   - Why it matters: claims such as "fully quantum-mechanical calculations provide the first direct visualization" and "transcends phenomenological models" depend on this comparison being quantitative.

B. Should address

4. **The comparison behind the claim of nanosecond recovery via phonons mixes facilities and conditions.**
   - What is compared: the claim rests on matching the tr-NRDS enhancement at (0 0 -2), measured at ESRF, to the RXD/RDS recovery, measured at SwissFEL (Fig. 4d).
   - How the two measurements differ:
     - The tr-NRDS probe depth (~1.5 µm) is about three times the pump penetration depth (~450-500 nm), while tr-RDS probes about 18 nm. Most of the NRDS signal therefore comes from weakly excited or unexcited material.
     - The pump pulses are 35 fs versus about ps long, the repetition rates are 100 Hz versus 1 kHz (so cumulative heating may differ), and the pump incidence is about 88° grazing in NRDS. A quoted "3 mJ cm^-2" may therefore correspond to very different absorbed energy densities.
   - Mismatched timescales: the text says the NRDS enhancement appears "around 2 ns, a similar timescale to RDS signals", but the RXD/RDS recovery constant is about 7 ns. The authors should state the fitted NRDS rise and decay constants, with uncertainties, and explain why 2 ns and 7 ns count as matching.
   - Mismatched temperatures: the PM reference is taken in a different temperature window (the cryostream range is 190-300 K). If the PM data are at 300 K, the phonon population and heat capacity differ from those at 190 K. A PM reference just above T_N2 (about 235-240 K) would be the cleaner null control.
   - What the authors should do: report absorbed energy density per unit volume for both setups, and a depth-weighted estimate of the excited fraction seen by NRDS.

5. **The nanosecond recovery time is poorly constrained and has no error bar.**
   - The "~7 ns" constant comes from roughly 8-10 delay points out to 12 ns. At 12 ns the RXD still shows about 30% of its maximum suppression (Fig. 4d). No uncertainty is given, and the fitted function is not stated.
   - Because the magnon-phonon coupling of -59 µeV is derived from this timescale (concern 3), its uncertainty carries directly into the coupling.
   - The authors should give the fit model and uncertainty, and if possible add data out to about 50 ns.

6. **The proposed mechanisms are tested at only one temperature.**
   - All tr-RDS data are taken at 190 K, only about 23 K below T_N1.
   - Both proposed mechanisms have a characteristic temperature dependence:
     - Magnon-magnon thermalization rates depend on the magnon density.
     - The anomalous magnon-phonon process depends on the thermal phonon occupation.
   - Measuring τ_RDS(q) and the RXD recovery time at a second, lower temperature (for example 100-150 K), and comparing with the QBE prediction at that temperature, would turn a consistency argument into a test.
   - The temperature closeness also matters for the fluence dependence (Fig. 2f). The authors attribute the high-fluence decrease of the upturn to heating close to T_N. Supplementary Note 3 should give the estimated lattice temperature at 3 mJ cm^-2. They should also state explicitly whether any part of the excited ~18 nm layer crosses T_N1 into the incommensurate phase.

7. **It is unclear whether Figs. 3j-n are measured or simulated, and how they could be either.**
   - The caption says the panels are "derived from tr-RDS data using Eq. (2)". The main text says "our simulations reveal ... (Figs. 3j-n and S8)".
   - Problems if the panels come from the data:
     - The data cover |q| ≤ 0.08 r.l.u. and are excluded near q = 0 by detector saturation (the hatched region), yet the panels show f_μ from -0.1 to 0.1 r.l.u., including q = 0.
     - Inverting Eq. (2) requires the prefactors A_μ(q) and an absolute scale, and neither is specified.
   - The authors should state clearly which panels are simulated and which are extracted from data. For any extracted distribution they should show the inversion and its uncertainties.

8. **Background separation and the choice of momentum origin affect τ(q) at small q.**
   - The RDS and RXD components are separated by a two-exponential fit to each trace (Supplementary Note 2), with τ_RXD around 50-100 fs. This is comparable to the 60 fs time resolution (Fig. 3d).
   - The authors should state whether the fit was convolved with the instrument response. A misestimated fast component leaks directly into the amplitude of the slow upturn.
   - The positive/negative q asymmetry in Fig. 3a is attributed to "a small error in the definition of the origin". With points as close as 0.010 r.l.u. and a momentum resolution of about 5 × 10^-3 r.l.u., this offset directly affects the small-q values of 1/τ_RDS in Fig. 3i, which carry the linear-in-|q| claim.
   - The authors should treat the q origin as a fit parameter, give its value and uncertainty, and show that the linear scaling of 1/τ survives the correction.

9. **The ~0.5 eV energy integration needs to be shown to leave only magnetic contributions.**
   - The integration window includes elastic charge-disorder scattering, phonons coupled through resonant processes, and possibly bimagnon and multimagnon weight.
   - The argument from the phase factor at the half-integer position (Eq. 1) suppresses ordinary phonon diffuse scattering. It does not obviously suppress pump-induced magnetoelastic or orbital/charge disorder that carries the magnetic periodicity.
   - The σ-polarized upturn is small but non-zero (Fig. 2g). The authors should compare the measured π/σ ratio with the ratio expected for pure spin-spin correlations from Eq. (1) in this scattering geometry.

10. **The text and Figure 2 do not match on the measurement positions.**
    - The text says the RDS was "slightly detuned from the Bragg point" at (0.49, 0.01, -0.51). That point is displaced mainly along [h0-h], not along b*.
    - Fig. 2e uses (1/2, -0.08, -1/2), and Fig. 3 scans along b*.
    - The text states that the amplitude along [h0h] differs markedly from that along b* because of anisotropic dispersion (Fig. S7). This implies that signals at the different positions in Figs. 2d, 2e, 2g and 3 are not directly comparable.
    - The authors should state which direction and position each figure panel uses, and note how the strong anisotropy affects the Fig. 2 conclusions.

11. **The generality claims go beyond the evidence.**
    - The abstract and Discussion claim the framework "generalizes across antiferromagnetic insulators" and establishes "design principles". This rests on a single material, a single sample and a single temperature.
    - The authors should either temper these statements or support them with simulations for at least one other gapped antiferromagnet, using its own dispersions.

C. Minor

12. **Phonon branches in the anomalous process.** The text names the "lowest-energy optical phonons" as the partner in the anomalous two-magnon-to-one-phonon process (Fig. 4e). Magnons quasi-thermalized near the ~1.3 meV gap would need phonons of only a few meV. The authors should state the energies of the phonon branches involved and the momentum region where energy and momentum are conserved.

13. **Self-similarity exponent.** The exponent 2.28 ± 0.08 is compared with 2.33 (Fig. S10b). The q and t ranges used should be stated, together with how sensitive the exponent is to them. The theory being compared applies to two-dimensional Heisenberg systems, whereas CuO is quasi-one-dimensional and three-dimensionally ordered, so the agreement should be framed cautiously.

14. **Excluding magnon-electron scattering.** This is justified by femtosecond electron thermalization and doublon lifetimes of about 200 fs "in copper-based oxides". A direct measure of the photocarrier lifetime in this CuO sample would help, for example the RXD recovery or optical reflectivity, because long-lived in-gap or trapped carriers would also scatter magnons.

15. **Methods details.** The Methods should give:
    - the pump and X-ray spot sizes and footprints, which matter especially at the grazing angles used;
    - the absorbed fraction;
    - how each q point was acquired, whether sequentially or interleaved, and how FEL drifts between q points were controlled.

16. **Single sample and cycling.** The paper should state how many samples and beamtimes contributed, whether the key tr-RDS traces were reproduced on another crystal or surface spot, and whether the signal changed after cumulative exposure (surface degradation under the FEL and pump).

17. **Short-lived NRDS feature.** The ~160 ps NRDS feature (Fig. 4d) is close to the ~100 ps X-ray pulse length. The authors should note that this constant is limited by the time resolution.

18. **Small asymmetry in Fig. 2c.** The difference between I_ON and I_OFF appears larger for negative q than for positive q, consistent with the asymmetry in Fig. 3a. A brief comment in the caption would avoid confusion with the statement that the amplitude is symmetric.

19. **Wording.** "Momentum-selective" magnon-phonon scattering (abstract) and "transcends phenomenological models" are stronger than the data warrant (see concern 3). Typographical issues include a stray period in affiliation 9 and inconsistent capitalization of chemical formulae in the reference list.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one decisive control for the magnetic assignment:
   - normalized ΔI/I versus q at several delays;
   - time-resolved XAS at the Cu L3 edge;
   - a static temperature series of the diffuse tail used to calibrate magnon population and effective temperature.
2. Put data and simulation on common axes: measured versus calculated τ_RDS(q), A_RDS(q) and RXD recovery, with every model parameter listed as fitted or fixed.
3. Measure tr-RDS and the nanosecond recovery at a second base temperature. Give the tr-NRDS comparison in terms of matched absorbed energy density and probe depth, with a paramagnetic reference just above T_N2.
4. Narrow the generality claims to what CuO shows, or support them with model calculations for another gapped antiferromagnet.
