A. Must address

1. **A transient change in absorption, resonance position or the resonant scattering factor has not been ruled out as the cause of the "RDS" upturn.** In Fig. 2e the upturn peaks at about 931.5-932 eV, close to the XAS maximum, which is exactly where the static RXD shows a self-absorption dip; the RXD itself peaks near ~930.5 eV. The authors attribute this difference to reduced self-absorption from a near-surface depth profile of photo-created magnons. But photoexcitation of a charge-transfer insulator is expected to change the Cu L3 absorption itself through doublon/holon creation, edge shifts, broadening and bleaching, and heating can also change the refractive index. A pump-induced reduction of self-absorption, or a change in the real part of the resonant scattering factor, would raise the scattered intensity everywhere on the peak profile at fixed photon energy, without any change in spin correlations. Away from q = 0, where the multiplicative RXD loss is small in absolute terms, this could look like an "upturn". The existing tests do not settle it: the amplitude profile A_RDS(q) in Fig. 3f follows the shape of the Bragg tail closely, which is what a multiplicative, q-independent change in the scattering factor would produce, and the π/σ dependence (Fig. 2g) does not separate the two pictures because the Bragg tail has the same polarization dependence. The authors should show: (i) the ratio (I_ON - I_OFF)/I_OFF versus q at fixed delays (for example 0.5, 1.5 and 4 ps), since magnon redistribution predicts a q-dependent ratio and an absorption or form-factor change predicts a q-independent one; (ii) transient XAS at 3 mJ cm^-2 (or an equivalent fluorescence-yield measurement), or at least the full energy dependence of the RXD and RDS signals at q_RXD and at the diffuse positions at several delays, to measure the transient edge directly; (iii) diffuse intensity at a position where magnetic diffuse scattering should be absent but the same absorption path applies (for example a charge Bragg tail at the same energy or far from any magnetic reflection), or a non-resonant or below-edge control in the same geometry; and (iv) a model of the expected energy dependence of a purely magnetic upturn including the depth profile, with a quantitative estimate of how much of the upturn could come from self-absorption change or resonance shift. Without these, the central identification of tr-RDS with f_μ(q,t) is not secure.

2. **The transient state is not compared with an equilibrium hot-spin state, and there is no static calibration linking diffuse intensity to magnon population.** All tr-RDS data are taken at 190 K, only ~23 K below T_N1 = 213 K, with the incommensurate phase just above (213-230 K) (Methods, Fig. S5). A rough estimate for 3 mJ cm^-2 absorbed within a ~450 nm penetration depth gives an energy density of order 50-70 J cm^-3; with a CuO heat capacity of roughly 3 J cm^-3 K^-1 that is a near-surface temperature rise of order 15-20 K once spins and lattice equilibrate, bringing the probed ~18 nm layer to or near T_N1. Near T_N, thermally populated critical spin fluctuations produce exactly this kind of diffuse scattering around the magnetic Bragg peak: resonant, π-enhanced, symmetric about q_RXD, rising as the order parameter falls and recovering with it. The fluence dependence in Fig. 2f (onset with demagnetization, decrease at high fluence that the text itself attributes to approaching T_N) fits this picture too. The paper interprets I_RDS through Eqs. (1)-(2) and converts it to f_μ(q,t), but never shows the equilibrium diffuse intensity around (1/2 0 -1/2) versus temperature in the same geometry. The claims of "non-thermal magnons" and of "quasi-thermalization fulfilling Bose-Einstein statistics" both need this comparison. The authors should: measure static (pump-off) RDS q-profiles at temperatures from about 150 K through T_N1 and the incommensurate phase, in the same geometry, to test that the diffuse tail scales with thermal magnon population as Eq. (2) predicts and to calibrate an effective magnon temperature at each delay; compare these directly with the transient profiles at matched RXD suppression, and show that the transient q-dependence (Fig. 3e,f) differs from the equilibrium critical lineshape at the same order-parameter reduction, in particular whether the state at 4 ps (and at 4.5 ps in Fig. 2f) is simply a hotter quasi-equilibrium magnet; fit the extracted f_μ(q, t ≥ 2 ps) to a Bose-Einstein form with free temperature and chemical potential and report the result (a magnon-number-conserving process should give a non-zero chemical potential); state in the main text the spin and lattice temperatures from Supplementary Note 3 at 4.5 ps and at 1-10 ns; and measure at a lower fluence or a lower base temperature where heating stays well below T_N1, to test whether the upturn and its picosecond dynamics persist away from criticality.

3. **A transient incommensurate (AF2) phase, short-range AF2 correlations, or a change in the Bragg profile position or lineshape is not convincingly excluded.** A photo-induced collinear-to-spiral transition in CuO has been reported (ref. 23). The AF2 propagation vector (0.506, 0, -0.483) lies within a few hundredths of r.l.u. of the commensurate peak, the same scale as the q-range of Fig. 3. The RDS detector sits at (0.49, 0.01, -0.51), and Fig. 4d uses (0.47, 0, -0.53), which also deviates in h and l. Transient incommensurate satellites, short-range AF2 correlations, or a peak shift from lattice expansion or strain would give a growing, resonant, π-enhanced diffuse signal at these positions, consistent with Figs. 2e-g, without any magnon redistribution; a shift towards the incommensurate wavevector by only a fraction of the resolution would produce asymmetric diffuse changes. The exclusion rests on the statement that the RXD peak width "remains nearly constant" (Fig. S4) and on Supplementary Note 4, but the peak top is saturated in the profiles shown (Fig. 2c). The authors should: state how the width and centre were extracted in the presence of saturation (attenuated data, the Jungfrau detector?); show full transient q-scans through the Bragg peak at several delays giving position, width and integrated intensity (extending Fig. S4), with upper bounds on transient changes in width and position; show quantitatively that the measured symmetry (Fig. 3f) excludes a shift towards AF2; explain in the main text why the measured q points and their time dependence rule out AF2 satellites; state whether any part of the excited ~18 nm layer crosses T_N1 into the incommensurate phase at 3 mJ cm^-2; and, as the most direct test, give a temperature series of tr-RDS at fixed fluence and the RDS response at a lower fluence where the estimated temperature stays well below T_N1.

4. **The separation of the RDS upturn from the Bragg-tail reduction is model-dependent and least constrained exactly where the key result sits.** Everything in Fig. 3 rests on splitting each trace into an RXD-reduction term and an RDS-upturn term with two exponentials (Fig. 3a, c-f; Supplementary Note 2). Near q_RXD the tail dominates: the q = 0.010 trace drops to about -5 a.u. while the upturn is of order +0.2 a.u. (Fig. 3a inset), the Bragg top is saturated (Fig. 2c) and the central region is excluded (hatched, Fig. 3c-f). The points at |q| = 0.010-0.020 r.l.u., only 2-4 times the stated 5×10^-3 r.l.u. resolution, produce the slowest τ_RDS (~3-4 ps, comparable to the 4 ps delay window of Fig. 3a) and the near-zero rate in Fig. 3i, so the linear, V-shaped 1/τ_RDS ∝ |q| law that is the main evidence for momentum-dependent redistribution rests on the least constrained fits. In addition, τ_RXD is about 50-100 fs, comparable to the 60 fs time resolution (Fig. 3d), so a misestimated fast component leaks directly into the slow-upturn amplitude. The positive/negative q asymmetry in Fig. 3a is attributed to "a small error in the definition of the origin", but an origin error of the order of the resolution is half the smallest |q| used and directly affects the small-q values of 1/τ_RDS. The authors should: state whether the fit was convolved with the instrument response; show full fit residuals and the covariance between fitted A and τ for the inner q points; test alternative decompositions, for example modelling the RXD contribution from the measured, unsaturated static Bragg lineshape scaled by the RXD time trace instead of fitting it freely; extend the delay range at small |q| so that τ ≈ 4 ps is sampled over several time constants; treat the q origin as a fit parameter, give its value and uncertainty, explain why its sign produces the asymmetry seen in Fig. 3a, and show that the linear scaling of 1/τ survives the correction; and state whether the line in Fig. 3i is a fit (through the origin or not) and what functional form the QBE predicts for 1/τ(q).

5. **Heat diffusion and lattice cooling are not ruled out as the cause of the ~7 ns recovery, and the magnon-phonon coupling is fitted to that same timescale.** The paper attributes the ~7 ns recovery of RXD and RDS (Fig. 4d) to an intrinsically inefficient anomalous magnon-phonon process and a dispersion-mismatch bottleneck, and extracts from this time constant an effective coupling of ~59 µeV. Heat leaving the excited layer into the cold bulk takes roughly d²/D; for a ~45 nm layer and a thermal diffusivity of order 10^-6 m² s^-1 this is a few nanoseconds, the same order as the observed recovery. (The reviews quote different pump penetration depths for the tr-RDS geometry, ~45 nm and ~450 nm; the authors should state the value, since the diffusion time scales with its square.) If magnons and lattice are near local equilibrium by ~1 ns, depth diffusion alone would restore the RXD on this timescale whatever the coupling. The RXD also recovers only partially by 12 ns, and the residual suppression, and possibly part of the 7 ns component, could reflect lattice temperature and heat redistribution in the near-surface layer; Supplementary Note 11 treats the microsecond phonon timescale but the nanosecond component needs the same treatment. Because the -59 µeV coupling is "estimated from the magnon relaxation timescale", the model's agreement with 7 ns is not an independent test of the anomalous-process mechanism. The authors should: give an explicit estimate of the lattice-temperature evolution in the ~18 nm probe depth from 0.1 to 20 ns for the actual excitation profile, including heat diffusion with literature values of CuO thermal conductivity at 190 K; show that diffusion-limited recovery would be clearly faster or slower than 7 ns, or model the depth-resolved recovery with both diffusion and the QBE magnon-phonon channel and refit the coupling; either compare the simulated magnon-phonon decay rate with the measured recovery without extra free parameters, or state which parameter is fitted; and test a dependence that discriminates the pictures, such as recovery time versus fluence, probe depth (X-ray incidence angle or energy across the edge) or base temperature (the gap and phonon occupation change with T). Without this, neither the 59 µeV coupling nor the "design principle" for lifetimes follows.

8. **The tr-NRDS and tr-RDS experiments are combined across mismatched conditions, which weakens the phonon-creation timeline in Fig. 4d.** The claim of phonon creation during magnon annihilation rests on matching the tr-NRDS enhancement at (0 0 -2), measured at ESRF, to the RXD/RDS recovery, measured at SwissFEL.
