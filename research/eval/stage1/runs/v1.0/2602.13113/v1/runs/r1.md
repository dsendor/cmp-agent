Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports what it presents as the first time-resolved resonant diffuse scattering (tr-RDS) measurement at the Cu L3 edge. Photoexcitation of the collinear antiferromagnet CuO produces a slowly rising diffuse intensity next to the (1/2 0 -1/2) magnetic Bragg peak, which the authors assign to non-thermal magnons. The rise time depends on momentum, which they read as picosecond magnon quasi-thermalization by magnon-magnon scattering. The ~7 ns recovery of order is attributed to anomalous (two-magnon to one-phonon) magnon-phonon scattering, supported by tr-NRDS at ESRF and by quantum Boltzmann calculations. If the magnetic origin of the signal and the momentum-resolved dynamics hold up, an energy-integrated, momentum-resolved probe of low-energy magnons on femto- to nanosecond timescales would be a useful complement to tr-RIXS. The broader claims, a "complete" and "generalizable" framework and "design principles", go well beyond what one material at one temperature and one fluence can establish.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new and potentially general technique (tr-RDS) is demonstrated at an XFEL. Several checks support a resonant magnetic origin of the upturn: it vanishes off resonance (Fig. 2E), it is much larger for π than σ (Fig. 2G), it has a fluence threshold (Fig. 2F), it is symmetric in ±q (Fig. 3F), and it differs between b* and [h 0 h] (Fig. S9).
- The data span 60 fs to microseconds, and three X-ray techniques probe different degrees of freedom of the same crystal.
- The magnon kinetic theory is substantial: it couples photodoped carriers to magnons (Eqs. S5–S13), includes the quartic magnon-magnon vertex (S16–S20), and treats normal and anomalous magnon-phonon processes (S24–S27). It gives a plausible microscopic picture of why recovery of the gapped magnetic order is slow.
- The paper explicitly considers and argues against the transient incommensurate phase as the origin (Fig. S7 and Supplementary Text).
- The data are deposited in a public repository.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Lattice and thermal origins of the tail increase have not been excluded, and the "constant peak width" argument rests on a saturated profile.
   - What the paper argues: the rise in intensity next to q_RXD comes from magnons and not from a change of the magnetic peak shape, because "the (1/2 0 -1/2) RXD peak width remains nearly constant while the tail intensity rises (Fig. 2C)". The Fig. 2C caption itself says "the top of the diffraction peak is missing due to saturation of the detector", so neither the width nor the integrated intensity can be determined from that figure.
   - Why strain is a strong candidate: the X-ray probe depth is ~18 nm and the pump penetration depth is ~450 nm. Thermal and magnetoelastic strain therefore builds up in the probed layer within roughly (probe depth)/(sound velocity), a few picoseconds, which is the timescale of the RDS rise. An inhomogeneous strain gradient broadens the magnetic peak symmetrically in ±q. That would reproduce both the symmetric amplitude profile (Fig. 3F) and a rise that is faster far from q_RXD than close to it.
   - Why heating is a candidate: the authors' own estimate puts the transient lattice temperature up to ~20 K above 190 K. That is within a few kelvin of T_N1 (214.5 K in Fig. S3), where equilibrium critical diffuse scattering around the magnetic peak grows quickly. This is relevant especially to the ns-scale RDS signal in Fig. 4D.
   - What is needed:
     - Attenuated, unsaturated reciprocal-space maps of (1/2 0 -1/2) (θ–2θ and rocking, or a 2D detector such as the Jungfrau already used for Fig. S7) versus delay at 3 mJ/cm². These would give peak position, width and integrated intensity on the same timescale as the RDS rise.
     - The equilibrium temperature dependence (190–215 K) of the diffuse intensity at the q points of Fig. 3A, to calibrate how much of the transient tail a hot lattice would produce.
   - Without these, the central identification of the signal as magnon scattering is not secure.

2. The comparison between the magnon-magnon kinetic theory and the momentum-resolved data is qualitative, and the theory is not set up for the stated experimental conditions.
   - No quantitative comparison:
     - The evidence for picosecond quasi-thermalization is the side-by-side visual comparison of Figs. 3E and 3G, plus a statement that 1/τ_RDS is "proportional to momentum". In Fig. 3I, however, the lines are described as "guide to the eyes".
     - The simulated rise times are never extracted with the same two-exponential procedure and overlaid on Fig. 3F/3I.
     - Simulated and measured RDS are never compared on a common scale with a single overall scale factor.
   - The excitation amplitude: B0Δn(t) was chosen "to reproduce the experimentally observed reduction of the AFM order by approximately 50%" (p. S13). Fig. 2D shows a ~28% drop in RXD intensity at 3 mJ/cm². Because intensity scales as m², that corresponds to roughly a 15% reduction of the sublattice moment. If the simulation is overdriven by a factor of ~3, the magnon density, and with it the quartic scattering rate, is overestimated.
   - The magnon branches: the model has two degenerate magnon branches (p. S13). The main text, by contrast, assigns the signal to the acoustic branch because its gap (1.3 meV) differs strongly from the optical one (23.2 meV). The model therefore cannot test that assignment or the role of optical-magnon decay invoked in the Conclusions and Fig. S8.
   - Data or simulation in Figs. 3J–N: the caption says the magnon distributions are "derived from tr-RDS data using Eq. 2", but the text (p. 7) presents them as simulations (with Fig. S15). If they come from the data, the inversion from energy-integrated intensity to f(q,t) needs to be described, since A_μ(q) and the absolute scale are not otherwise determined. The same applies to the quoted hot-magnon temperature of ~500 K at (0.5, 0.1, -0.5) (p. S9).
   - What the authors should do:
     - Rerun the QBE with the excitation calibrated to the measured RXD drop.
     - Fit the simulated traces exactly as the data are fitted.
     - Overlay τ_RDS(q) and A_RDS(q) from theory and experiment, along both b* and [h 0 h].
     - State clearly what Figs. 3J–N show.

3. The nanosecond recovery mechanism and the "dispersion-mismatch bottleneck" are inferred, not tested, and other channels are not excluded.
   - The coupling constant is assumed, not derived: in the magnon-phonon calculation, c^x = 1 eV/Å is fixed for both the normal and anomalous couplings (p. S17). The effective coupling of ~59 μeV is then back-derived from the measured ~7 ns time constant (p. 9). The claim that the coupling is small because of phase-space restriction rather than a small microscopic matrix element is therefore not demonstrated. The comparison with the 6.2 meV spin-phonon coupling from Raman (ref. 44) compares quantities defined differently.
   - What the calculation needs:
     - An independent estimate of the anomalous vertex, for example ∇J from DFT+U under frozen-phonon displacements of the lowest optical modes, or from the measured spin-phonon shifts.
     - A demonstration that this vertex, inserted into Eq. S27, gives nanosecond rates.
     - The full time-evolved recovery curve from the QBE, not only the initial rate Γ(q) = S/f at t = 0, compared with the RXD and RDS traces of Fig. 4D.
   - Other recovery channels:
     - Long-lived photocarriers or trapped charges: the paper assumes doublons decay within ~200 fs using values from other cuprates (refs. 30, 31). No measurement on CuO is shown. Elsewhere the authors invoke defect-related electronic thermal conductivity in this very sample (p. S10).
     - Thermal recovery of the order: the probed 18 nm layer stays hot for ≫10 ns.
   - Controls that would discriminate between channels:
     - Transient optical reflectivity or absorption on the same crystal and fluence, to give the carrier lifetime.
     - Comparison of the RXD level at 10–12 ns with the equilibrium RXD intensity at the estimated lattice temperature.

B. Should address

4. The tr-NRDS evidence for phonon creation by magnon annihilation is weak as a control.
   - Different conditions: the tr-NRDS data (Fig. 4D top) come from a different facility with:
     - a picosecond 1 kHz pump instead of a 35 fs 100 Hz pump;
     - ~100 ps X-ray pulses;
     - a 1.5 μm probe depth that extends well beyond the ~500 nm pump depth;
     - a different pump incidence angle.
   - Timescales do not match: the AFM enhancement peaks at ~2 ns, while the RDS and RXD recover with ~7 ns. If phonons were produced by magnon annihilation, their population should grow on the magnon decay timescale, not peak earlier.
   - The AFM/PM comparison is made at 190 K versus 300 K, where phonon populations, heat capacity, absorption and thermal conductivity all differ. Magnetostriction specific to the AFM phase is another possible source of AFM-only lattice diffuse changes.
   - The region of interest in Fig. S10C was chosen by hand to avoid a Bragg-peak shift that is visible at 0.15 and 0.6 ns. The ~160 ps feature is close to the time resolution.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Add a PM reference just above T_N2 (~235–240 K) and an AF2 reference.
     - Show the sensitivity of the NRDS trace to the choice of region of interest.
     - Quantify the deposited energy at each temperature.
     - Give fit uncertainties for the 2 ns and 160 ps time constants.

5. Detector linearity and the two-exponential decomposition need validation, because the momentum dependence of τ_RDS is the key observable.
   - Detector linearity: the largest RDS amplitudes and longest τ_RDS occur closest to q_RXD (Fig. 3F; Fig. S9E). Those are the APD channels nearest the saturated region (hatched in Fig. 3). Single-shot XFEL pulses can drive avalanche photodiodes non-linear. A linearity calibration (attenuator series at those q) is needed.
   - The fit model as written: Eq. S3, A exp(-t/τ), decays to zero at long times. It cannot describe a step-like drop or a rising plateau, so the actual function used (presumably including (1 - e^(-t/τ)) and convolution with the 60 fs resolution) must be stated.
   - Fit window: the fitted window is 4 ps, while τ_RDS reaches ~4 ps (Fig. 3F) and ~13 ps with A_RDS ≈ 18 (Fig. S9E). At these q, amplitude and time constant are strongly correlated.
   - Model adequacy: Fig. S8 shows that the dynamics are not monotonic (maximum at ~5 ps), so a single-exponential RDS term may bias τ.
   - What the authors should report: the A–τ covariance, fits over longer delay windows where they exist, and whether a global fit with the RXD time constant shared across q (Fig. 3D shows it to be q-independent) changes τ_RDS(q).

6. The energy dependence of the RDS upturn follows the absorption maximum, not the magnetic RXD resonance (Fig. 2E).
   - The RDS upturn peaks at ~931.7 eV, coinciding with the XAS maximum, whereas the RXD peaks near 930 eV and dips at 933 eV from self-absorption.
   - Magnon-related resonant scattering is governed by the same dipole matrix elements as the magnetic Bragg intensity. The difference in line shape needs an explanation, for example a calculated resonance profile for the inelastic channel.
   - It also needs a test ruling out a pump-induced change in absorption, fluorescence or self-absorption, which would scale with the local tail intensity. Time-resolved XAS in fluorescence yield at the same fluence would do this.

7. The polarization test does not distinguish transverse magnon fluctuations from longitudinal (Bragg-like) magnetic scattering.
   - The text argues that the π/σ ratio of the upturn (Fig. 2G) is "similar to the magnetic RXD profile" (Fig. S5B), and takes this as proof of magnetic origin.
   - Magnons are transverse fluctuations perpendicular to the b-axis moments, so for a given geometry Eq. 1 predicts a σ/π ratio different from the elastic Bragg ratio. The authors already have the geometric tensor F (Eq. S37).
   - They should compute the expected σ and π RDS intensities from transverse S^xx and S^zz correlations, and the expected Bragg ratio from S^yy, and compare both with Figs. 2G and S5B. Agreement would be a far stronger signature than similarity to the Bragg ratio.

8. Momentum-resolved data exist only at one temperature (190 K) and one fluence (3 mJ/cm²), so the proposed scattering hierarchy is not tested through its predicted dependences.
   - Magnon-magnon scattering rates scale with magnon density, so τ_RDS(q) should shorten with fluence. The anomalous magnon-phonon rate depends on the occupation of the lowest optical phonons, so the ~7 ns recovery should depend on temperature in a calculable way.
   - The Bose factor in Eq. 2 also makes the equilibrium RDS baseline temperature dependent.
   - Measuring τ_RDS(q) at two or three fluences, and the RXD/RDS recovery at a lower base temperature (for example 100–150 K, away from T_N1), would turn qualitative consistency into a test of the mechanism.

9. Quoted RXD suppression, fluence scale and sample temperature are inconsistent with one another.
   - Fig. 2D shows ΔI/I ≈ -0.28 at 3 mJ/cm². In Fig. 2F the axis ends near log f ≈ 3.4 (about 2.5 mJ/cm² if log10 in μJ/cm²), yet ΔI/I ≈ -0.5 is already reached there.
   - The simulations assume a ~50% reduction of the order (concern 2).
   - The authors should:
     - state the log base and units;
     - mark 3 mJ/cm² on Fig. 2F;
     - say whether fluences are incident or absorbed and whether they are corrected for footprint at 63.5° incidence;
     - reconcile these numbers.
   - With ΔT ≈ 20 K from 190 K, the surface layer probed (18 nm, where absorbed energy density is highest) sits at or near T_N1 = 214.5 K. This needs explicit discussion, since it bears on both the incommensurate-phase argument and concern 1.

10. The paper reports a single crystal and does not quantify its twin domains.
    - All measurements are on one floating-zone crystal at a single sample position. Reproducibility on a second crystal or spot is not reported.
    - Fig. S4 shows a dense set of twin-domain stripes across the face. The text describes this as "a minimal volume of twin domains".
    - Static diffuse scattering from domain or twin boundaries near q_RXD, and pump-induced changes of it, should be estimated. A twin volume fraction should be given.

11. The generality claims are not supported by the evidence.
    - The abstract and Conclusions state that the framework "transcends phenomenological models and generalizes across materials" and applies to "any gapped magnetic insulators". They also state that tr-RDS enables "attosecond-scale investigations".
    - Only CuO is studied, and the magnon-phonon calculation uses an assumed coupling (concern 3).
    - These statements should be reframed as hypotheses. The prediction that "strongly overlapping dispersions … exhibit faster recovery" should be presented as a testable prediction, not an established principle.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Acquire unsaturated 2D maps of the magnetic peak versus delay, together with an equilibrium temperature series of the diffuse tail. These are the single most direct way to show that the upturn is magnon scattering rather than strain broadening or heating.
2. Measure τ_RDS(q) versus fluence and the ns recovery versus base temperature, and compare each with QBE predictions computed for the same conditions. This tests the magnon-magnon and anomalous magnon-phonon assignments through their characteristic scalings.
3. Present theory and experiment on common axes: τ_RDS(q), A_RDS(q), the RXD recovery curve and polarization ratios. Calibrate the excitation to the measured RXD drop and use an independently estimated magnon-phonon vertex.
4. Add a transient optical (reflectivity or absorption) measurement on the same crystal to fix the photocarrier lifetime. Where possible, take a tr-NRDS PM reference just above T_N2 under matched pump conditions.
