Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports pump-induced changes in resonant soft X-ray scattering intensity at the Cu L3 edge, measured slightly away from the (1/2 0 -1/2) magnetic Bragg peak of CuO. The authors interpret these changes as time- and momentum-resolved "resonant diffuse scattering" (tr-RDS) from photo-created magnons. They combine this with tr-RXD, ns-scale non-resonant diffuse scattering (tr-NRDS) and quantum Boltzmann simulations to propose a hierarchy: femtosecond spin disorder, picosecond magnon-magnon quasi-thermalization that piles magnons up near the zone centre, and nanosecond recovery through anomalous (two-magnon to one-phonon) scattering, followed by phonon dissipation on microsecond timescales. If the magnon assignment holds, energy-integrated tr-RDS would be a useful complement to tr-RIXS for low-energy magnons, which it cannot resolve. A complete picture of energy flow in a photoexcited antiferromagnetic insulator would also interest the ultrafast magnetism community. The novelty rests almost entirely on the assignment of the upturn signal to magnons and on the quantitative link between experiment and simulation. Both need more support.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The approach is new: diffuse intensity near a magnetic Bragg peak, followed at XFEL time resolution over a range of momentum offsets along two directions (Fig. 3, Fig. S9).
- Several diagnostics were taken simultaneously or on the same crystal. These include RXD and RDS on one detector tower, X-ray energy, fluence and incident polarization dependences (Fig. 2E–G), a long-delay trace (Fig. S8), and ns-to-µs NRDS in the AFM and PM phases (Fig. 4D, Fig. S11).
- The theory chain is microscopic. Its magnon-magnon vertex follows from exchange constants fixed by neutron dispersion, not from free fitting. Done carefully, this could give a genuinely parameter-light comparison.
- The momentum dependence of the upturn time constant (Fig. 3F, 3I) is symmetric about q_RXD and differs between b* and [h 0 h]. These are suggestive features that a simple heating artefact would not obviously produce.
- Data are deposited in a public repository (ref. 50).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The upturn signal is not shown to come uniquely from magnons rather than from a change in the shape of the magnetic Bragg peak.** The evidence for magnon origin (p. 4–5; Fig. 2E–G, 3F) is as follows:
   - resonance at the Cu L3 edge;
   - a π > σ polarization dependence "similar to the magnetic RXD profile (Fig. S5)";
   - a fluence threshold;
   - ±q symmetry along b*;
   - the statement that the RXD peak width "remains nearly constant" (Fig. 2C).

   The first three are equally expected for any pump-induced redistribution of magnetic Bragg intensity into its tails, for example:
   - a transient shift or broadening of the peak from strain;
   - changes in mosaic or domain structure;
   - a reduced magnetic correlation length.

   In particular:
   - (a) The width claim cannot be checked from Fig. 2C, because the caption states that the peak top is missing due to detector saturation, and no unsaturated profile versus delay is shown.
   - (b) The X-ray probe depth is about 18 nm (Methods) while the pump depth is about 450 nm. An acoustic strain front crosses 18 nm in a few picoseconds, the same timescale as the upturn rise (Fig. 3F, τ_RDS up to about 4 ps). The surface normal (1 0 -1) is close to the q_RXD direction, so a strain-induced peak shift is most relevant along the [h 0 h]-type path. That path is measured on the negative-q side only (Fig. S9A), so the symmetry test is unavailable exactly where it matters. Fig. S9 also shows the largest upturns, which grow almost linearly up to 4 ps.
   - (c) For RXD, the measured π/σ ratio in Fig. 2G is about 6 at 4 ps. This is close to the static RXD ratio in Fig. S5B (about 3.1/0.5). Magnon diffuse scattering comes from fluctuations transverse to the ordered moment along b, so its polarization factor should generally differ from that of the ordered moment. The authors have the geometric tensor F_αα' (Eq. S37) needed to predict the π/σ ratio for transverse magnon fluctuations versus a longitudinal Bragg tail. They should compute both and compare them with Fig. 2G. As it stands, the agreement with the RXD profile arguably favours a Bragg-tail origin.
   - (d) In Fig. 2E the RDS energy profile peaks near 931.7 eV, whereas RXD peaks near 930 eV. The authors should explain this using self-absorption corrections for each geometry.

   Requested work:
   - Unsaturated, attenuated θ–2θ and rocking profiles at several delays (0, 1, 4, 20 ps), or 2D Jungfrau maps at 3 mJ/cm² rather than only at high fluence.
   - Both signs of q along [h 0 h].
   - A temperature null control: the same detuned momentum just above T_N2 and well below T_N1. This tests whether the upturn and its q-dependent time constant follow the magnon population.
   - The predicted versus measured π/σ ratio.

2. **The separation into "RXD" and "RDS" components, and the extracted τ_RDS(q), are not robust as presented.**
   - The functional form in Eq. S3, A exp(−t/τ), decays to zero rather than describing a step-like drop and a rise. The form actually fitted, including the convolution with the 60 fs resolution and the time-zero handling, is not given.
   - The fits use a 4 ps window (Fig. 3A, Fig. S9A), yet the extracted τ_RDS reaches about 4 ps near q = 0.01 (Fig. 3F) and about 13 ps along [h 0 h] (Fig. S9E). Where τ exceeds the window, amplitude and time constant are strongly correlated and poorly determined. The near-Γ points carry the linear 1/τ ∝ q claim (Fig. 3I), so this matters.
   - The decomposition assumes the magnon contribution is zero just after excitation. The authors' own model (Fig. S14B, Fig. 3J) creates magnons across the zone instantaneously, which would add a prompt positive RDS step that the fit would absorb into the negative "RXD" term.
   - The interpretation of the "RXD tail" also depends on the momentum resolution, which is not stated. From Fig. S5 the radial and transverse Bragg widths correspond to about 10⁻³ r.l.u. along b*, yet Fig. 2C shows substantial intensity at 0.025–0.075 r.l.u. Is this the detector acceptance, an equilibrium critical or thermal magnetic diffuse signal at 0.9 T_N, or static disorder?

   Requested work:
   - State the APD angular acceptance in r.l.u. and fold it into the analysis.
   - Show the fitted model with parameter covariances.
   - Use longer delay scans (as in Fig. S8) at several q to pin down τ_RDS.
   - Test whether τ(q) survives a fit that allows a prompt RDS step.

3. **The simulation–experiment comparison that underpins the "quasi-thermalization via magnon-magnon scattering" claim is qualitative and partly inconsistent with the measurement.**
   - (a) The QBE is solved on a 32 × 48 × 32 grid over the magnetic zone, a spacing of about 0.02–0.03 r.l.u. The experimental points are at q = 0.010, 0.015, 0.020 and 0.025 r.l.u. The δ-function is broadened with a 3.0 meV kernel (p. S15), comparable to the acoustic magnon energies at these q (spin gap of about 1.3 meV, Fig. S13C). The simulated momentum dependence near Γ in Fig. 3G/H and Fig. S16 is therefore set largely by interpolation and broadening. Convergence with grid density and kernel width must be shown.
   - (b) The excitation amplitude B₀Δn is chosen to "reproduce the experimentally observed reduction of the AFM order by approximately 50%" (p. S13). Fig. 2D at the same 3 mJ/cm² shows a RXD intensity reduction of about 28%, which is about a 15% reduction of the sublattice moment if I ∝ M². The initial magnon density, and hence the magnon-magnon rates, may be strongly overestimated.
   - (c) Fig. 3G and 3E are compared only by eye, on different colour scales and delay ranges. No simulated τ(q) is overlaid on Fig. 3I.
   - (d) The Fig. 3 caption says J–N are "derived from tr-RDS data using Eq. 2", but the main text (p. 7) presents them as simulation results. The colour scale matches the simulated panel H, and absolute occupations (up to 13) cannot be obtained from intensities in arbitrary units.

   Requested work:
   - Rerun with the demagnetization matched to Fig. 2D.
   - Extract τ(q) from the simulated traces with the same fit as the data and overlay it on Fig. 3I.
   - Show convergence.
   - Correct the provenance of Fig. 3J–N.

B. Should address

4. **The agreement between the computed anomalous magnon-phonon rate and the 7 ns recovery is set by an arbitrary constant.** On p. S17 the coupling is fixed at c^x = 1 eV/Å for both processes. The scattering rate scales as |c^x|². The absolute rates in Fig. 4E/F and Fig. S17 (about 0.2 ns⁻¹) therefore do not test the 7 ns recovery independently. Only the relative phase-space structure is a prediction. The "effective magnon-phonon coupling strength ~59 µeV" (p. 9) is not derived anywhere in the text or SI. Comparing it with the 6.2 meV spin-phonon coupling from Raman (ref. 44) compares quantities with different definitions. The authors should:
   - obtain ∇J independently, for example from frozen-phonon DFT+U, which they already run;
   - state the derivation of the 59 µeV value;
   - show how sensitive the conclusion "anomalous scattering dominates recovery" is to the ratio c^anomalous/c^normal, which is assumed equal;
   - say whether the omitted optical magnon branch (23 meV gap), absent in the degenerate-branch orthorhombic model, opens other decay channels.

5. **The NRDS evidence for phonon creation during magnon annihilation (Fig. 4D) comes from a different experiment that is not matched to the RXD/RDS conditions, and its kinetics do not obviously fit the proposed sequence.**
   - **Mismatched conditions.** The NRDS data differ from the RXD/RDS data in four ways:
     - facility, and pump and probe pulse durations (100 ps X-rays);
     - repetition rate (1 kHz vs 100 Hz), with possible average heating;
     - geometry, with pump incidence near 88°;
     - probe depth, about 1.5 µm against a pump depth of about 500 nm, versus 18 nm for RXD/RDS.

     The absorbed energy density and lattice temperature sampled therefore differ substantially.
   - **Unmatched control.** The PM control is at 300 K rather than just above T_N2, so phonon populations, heat capacity and Debye–Waller factors also change.
   - **What NRDS detects.** NRDS within 0.1 r.l.u. of (0 0 −2) is dominated by low-energy acoustic phonons (intensity ∝ n/ω²). The anomalous process is said to emit the lowest optical phonons, so the observable is at best indirect.
   - **Kinetics.** Phonons created at the magnon annihilation rate (about 7 ns) and dissipated over about 0.7 µs (Fig. S11) should accumulate monotonically over about 10 ns. Instead NRDS peaks near 2 ns and then decays.
   - **Magnetostriction.** Strain from the recovering order in this strongly spin-lattice coupled material would broaden (0 0 −2) inhomogeneously and could raise intensity in the annular ROI of Fig. S10C even without a peak shift.

   Requested work:
   - A coupled rate-equation model fitted jointly to the RXD, RDS and NRDS traces.
   - A PM-phase control at about 235–250 K.
   - An estimate of steady-state heating at 1 kHz.
   - A test of strain broadening, for example from radial versus transverse NRDS cuts.

6. **The sample temperature and energy balance are marginal and not checked against an equilibrium calibration.** The supplement estimates a temperature rise below 20 K at 190 K (p. S7), which puts the photoexcited surface at up to about 210 K, against T_N1 = 213 K in the text or 214.5 K in Fig. S3. The 18 nm probed layer sits where the deposited energy is largest. Near T_N1 the equilibrium RXD intensity changes steeply with temperature. Yet at 12 ns (Fig. 4D) the RXD has recovered to about −10%, after the energy should have passed to the lattice. The authors should:
   - show the static temperature dependence of the (1/2 0 −1/2) intensity on this crystal;
   - use it to convert the 4 ps plateau and the 12 ns value into effective temperatures;
   - show that these agree with the stated heating.

   This calibration also bears on concern 8 (proximity to the ICM phase) and on which magnetic state the main data probe.

7. **The fluence axis in Fig. 2F appears inconsistent with Fig. 2D.**
   - All main-text data are at 3 mJ/cm² (log₁₀ ≈ 3.48 in µJ/cm²), but the Fig. 2F axis ends near log ≈ 3.3, roughly 2 mJ/cm².
   - At that highest plotted fluence the RXD reduction is about −0.5, larger than the about −0.28 in Fig. 2D at a nominally higher fluence and similar delay (4.5 ps vs about 4 ps).
   - The RDS amplitude peaks at log ≈ 2.7–3 and falls above it, so the main data may sit on the falling side, where the authors themselves invoke approach to T_N.

   Please state the logarithm base and whether fluences are incident or absorbed, reconcile the two panels, and give the fluence used for the high-excitation data in Fig. S7.

8. **Contamination from transient incommensurate order and from twin domains is not ruled out quantitatively.**
   - **Incommensurate peak.** The ICM peak at (0.506, 0, −0.483) differs from the measured detuned point (0.49, 0.01, −0.51) by only 0.016–0.027 r.l.u. per component. That is within the measured q range. Fig. S7 shows the ICM signal is spread broadly on the detector and grows with time constants of up to several hundred ps. The statement that no significant upturn was seen at the ICM position "within our experimental precision" (p. S7) needs the actual trace and its uncertainty at 3 mJ/cm².
   - **Twin domains.** The polarized microscope image (Fig. S4) shows many twin lamellae, not obviously a "minimal volume". The authors should show where the twin-domain magnetic reflections, and any domain-wall diffuse scattering, fall relative to the detuned positions.

9. **Excluding magnon–photocarrier scattering rests on literature values from other cuprates.** The main text (p. 6) excludes magnon-electron scattering on the picosecond timescale because "electrons are already thermalized within femtosecond timescales (30, 31)". These references concern other copper oxides. CuO is a charge-transfer insulator with trap-related carrier dynamics, so residual doublons and holons, or trapped carriers, may persist for picoseconds and could redistribute magnons. A direct measurement on this sample at the same fluence would settle the question, for example:
   - transient optical reflectivity;
   - time-resolved Cu L3 XAS (edge shift or bleach) versus delay, which the SwissFEL setup can measure.

10. **The hot-magnon temperatures and absolute magnon occupations are not supported by the data shown.** The SI (p. S9) quotes a hot-magnon temperature of about 500 K at (0.5, 0.1, −0.5). That point lies outside the measured range (|q| ≤ 0.08), and converting intensities in arbitrary units into occupations requires an absolute normalization that is not described. Please give the procedure and the measured point used, or state that this is a simulation result.

11. **The generalization claims go beyond a study of one sample, at one temperature and one fluence for the momentum maps.** Examples include:
    - "generalizes across materials" and "establishing design principles" (abstract);
    - "applicable to any gapped magnetic insulators" (p. 9);
    - the prediction that overlapping dispersions give faster recovery (p. 10);
    - "attosecond-scale investigations" (p. 10).

    None is tested beyond CuO at 190 K, and the dispersion-overlap principle is not checked even within CuO, for example by temperature tuning of phonon populations. Either temper these statements or add a second temperature or material that tests a prediction.

C. Minor

12. The statement that tr-RDS "has not yet been experimentally demonstrated" (p. 3) should be positioned against earlier time-resolved resonant X-ray studies that followed transient magnetic correlations and correlation lengths through diffuse or broadened magnetic scattering, for example photodoped Sr₂IrO₄. The difference in information content should be made explicit.

13. Energy-integrated APD detection also collects pump-modified Cu L3 fluorescence, d–d excitations and phonon inelastic scattering. Please show that the q-independent background, as at |q| = 0.08 and far from any Bragg peak, is pump-insensitive at all delays, and state the effective energy window.

14. The authors invoke "hydrodynamic magnon slowdown" (ref. 41; p. 7). However, Fig. 3I shows 1/τ ∝ q, which is not the q² dependence expected for diffusive relaxation. Please clarify which regime is meant and whether the linear dependence is a fit or the guide to the eye stated in the caption.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add an unsaturated, delay-resolved map of the full magnetic Bragg profile at 3 mJ/cm². Add a matched temperature control above T_N2 for the detuned RDS points. Together these would establish whether the upturn is diffuse magnon scattering or a change in the Bragg peak itself.
2. Fit the RXD, RDS and NRDS traces with one coupled rate model whose magnon-magnon and magnon-phonon rates come from the QBE. The coupling should come from an independent ∇J estimate, and the excitation density should be matched to the measured demagnetization. The comparison would then be quantitative rather than visual.
3. Report τ_RDS(q) from longer delay scans, with resolution-folded simulations overlaid on Fig. 3I and both signs of q along [h 0 h]. The momentum-dependent quasi-thermalization would then rest on well-determined numbers.
4. Narrow the generality claims to what CuO at 190 K supports. Alternatively, add one test of the dispersion-overlap prediction, such as a different temperature or a second gapped antiferromagnet.
