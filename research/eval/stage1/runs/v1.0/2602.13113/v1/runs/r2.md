Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports time-resolved resonant diffuse scattering (tr-RDS) at the Cu L3 edge near the (1/2 0 -1/2) magnetic Bragg peak of the antiferromagnetic insulator CuO. The authors read this signal as a momentum-resolved probe of photo-generated low-energy magnons. They combine it with tr-RXD, tr-NRDS (non-resonant diffuse scattering from phonons), and quantum-Boltzmann simulations of magnon–magnon and magnon–phonon scattering. The result is a three-stage picture: femtosecond spin disorder, picosecond magnon quasi-thermalization towards the zone centre (Γ), and nanosecond recovery through anomalous two-magnon-to-one-phonon scattering. If the diffuse signal is shown to come from magnons, the technique would be a real advance: it gives time- and momentum-resolved access to meV magnons that energy-resolved tr-RIXS cannot reach. The microscopic picture of the ns bottleneck would also be of broad interest to ultrafast magnetism.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A new and potentially general measurement: energy-integrated resonant diffuse scattering at an X-ray free-electron laser (XFEL), used as a time-resolved probe of the spin–spin correlation function at small q around a magnetic Bragg peak.
- Several checks point to a magnetic, resonant origin of the upturn signal:
  - resonance at the Cu L3 edge (Fig. 2E);
  - π > σ polarization dependence (Fig. 2G);
  - a fluence onset that tracks demagnetization (Fig. 2F);
  - an upturn amplitude symmetric about q_RXD along b* (Fig. 3F);
  - anisotropy between b* and [h 0 h] (Fig. S9).
- The upturn time constant depends strongly on momentum (Fig. 3F, 3I), while the RXD drop time does not (Fig. 3D). This is a clean, model-independent observation that the diffuse and Bragg components evolve differently.
- Three X-ray probes (RXD, RDS, NRDS) cover the fs-to-µs dynamics on the same crystal, with a paramagnetic (PM) comparison for the NRDS.
- A microscopic theory chain (DFT → Wannier → spin–charge model → QBE → scattering cross-section) is set out in reproducible detail in the Supplement, and the data are deposited.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **It is not shown that the "RDS" upturn is diffuse magnon scattering rather than a pump-induced change in the Bragg-peak lineshape or in quasi-elastic static scattering.**
   - *Where the data sit.* All tr-RDS points lie 0.01–0.08 r.l.u. from a Bragg peak whose top saturates the detector (Fig. 2C caption; hatched region in Fig. 3).
   - *Why the width argument fails.* The text says the peak width "remains nearly constant" (p. 4), but the profile in Fig. 2C has its top missing. Neither the width nor the position can be judged from it.
   - *Alternative lineshape effects.* Each of the following would raise the intensity in the tails and could produce a delayed, q-dependent rise:
     - transient strain propagating into the ~18 nm probe depth (pump penetration ~450 nm, so strain fronts cross the probed layer on the ps timescale of the upturn);
     - a change of the magnetic correlation length or mosaic;
     - a small shift of the magnetic propagation vector.
   - *What the polarization and energy checks do and do not show.* The π/σ dependence (Fig. 2G) and the resonance (Fig. 2E) show that the signal is magnetic, not that it is diffuse rather than a magnetic Bragg tail. The text itself notes that the polarization dependence mirrors the RXD (Fig. S5).
   - *Detector integration.* The avalanche photodiodes integrate over all energies. The signal therefore also contains quasi-elastic scattering from static short-range magnetic disorder and from domain walls; see also concern 11.
   - *Requested:*
     - unsaturated profiles through the full peak (attenuated, or with the 0.5M Jungfrau already used for Fig. S7) at several delays (0, 1, 4, 100 ps, 1 ns);
     - fitted peak position, width and integrated intensity versus delay;
     - a test of whether the tail increase can be reproduced by any combination of broadening and shift;
     - a comparison of the measured q-profile of the upturn with the profile Eq. 1–2 predicts for magnon populations (e.g. the expected weighting by A_µ(q)/ω(q)).

2. **The interpretation as non-thermal magnons that quasi-thermalize lacks an equilibrium calibration.**
   - *Proximity to T_N1.* The sample sits at 190 K. The authors' own estimate of the average heating is up to ~20 K (p. S7), which places the probed surface layer within a few kelvin of T_N1 (213 K in the text, 214.5 K in Fig. S3).
   - *Critical scattering.* Near T_N, equilibrium critical magnetic diffuse scattering at small |q| grows steeply with temperature. A delayed rise peaked at small |q| with a q-dependent rise time is what one would expect if the probed layer were simply heating towards T_N1.
   - *The temperature figure.* The "hot-magnon temperature ~500 K" at (0.5, 0.1, -0.5) (p. S9) is given without the method or calibration used to obtain it.
   - *Requested:*
     - static (unpumped) RDS intensity at the same q points versus temperature from ~150 K through T_N1 and T_N2;
     - the corresponding RXD intensity versus temperature;
     - with these, a test of whether the pumped late-time (4–100 ps) RDS and RXD values match a single equilibrium temperature, which would mean thermal magnons, or not, which would support a non-thermal distribution;
     - a stated procedure for converting RDS intensity to a q-dependent "temperature".
   - This is the central test of the "non-thermal → quasi-thermal" claim. Without it the ps dynamics could equally be read as fast local heating plus critical fluctuations.

3. **The magnon–phonon attribution of the ns recovery rests on cross-setup comparisons and on a coupling constant that was not determined independently.**
   - *(a) Mismatched NRDS conditions.* The NRDS data (Fig. 4D, top) were taken under conditions very different from the RXD/RDS data:
     - a different laser (ps Ti:sapphire at 1 kHz versus 35 fs at 100 Hz);
     - pump incidence ~88°;
     - probe depth ~1.5 µm, against ~500 nm pump penetration and 18 nm for the soft X-ray probe;
     - 100 ps time resolution;
     - fluence definition not given.
     The "similar timescale" between the NRDS maximum at ~2 ns and the RDS decay is therefore a comparison between different depth-averaged volumes and excitation densities.
   - *(b) The NRDS shape does not obviously match the proposed energy flow.* In a simple energy-flow picture, if magnons decay with τ ≈ 7 ns, phonon creation should rise over ~7 ns. The data instead show a maximum at ~1–2 ns followed by decay. The authors should fit RXD, RDS and NRDS jointly with a rate-equation model that includes magnon → phonon transfer and phonon redistribution, and should check the energy budget: magnon energy lost against phonon population gained.
   - *(c) The PM control is 110 K warmer.* At 300 K the phonon occupation, Debye–Waller factor and thermal expansion all differ. A PM-phase or AF2-phase measurement just above T_N2 (e.g. 235–240 K) would isolate the magnetic contribution far better.
   - *(d) The predicted rate is not a prediction.* In the theory the normal and anomalous coupling constants are both fixed at c^x = 1 eV/Å (p. S17) with no justification. The absolute anomalous rate (Fig. 4F, ~0.2 ns⁻¹) therefore scales with an arbitrary c², so its agreement with the measured ~7 ns is not a prediction.
   - *(e) The 59 µeV coupling is unexplained.* The "effective magnon–phonon coupling ~59 µeV" (p. 9) has no stated derivation. Comparing it with a 6.2 meV spin–phonon coupling from Raman (ref. 44) mixes quantities with different definitions.
   - *Requested:* fix c^x from an independent source, for example DFT exchange gradients ∇J, the measured spin–phonon shifts (ref. 44) or magnetostriction, and then show that the ns timescale follows. Also give the formula and inputs behind the 59 µeV.

4. **Long-lived photocarriers are not excluded as the cause of the persistent demagnetization and its ns recovery.**
   - *What the paper assumes.* Magnon–electron scattering is excluded, and the ns recovery assigned to magnons, on the grounds that doublons decay within ~200 fs (refs. 30, 31). Those references concern cuprate superconductors, not CuO.
   - *Why CuO may differ.* In a 1.4–1.5 eV charge-transfer insulator, photo-holes and electrons can localize (polarons, trapping) and survive for ns. Through the same spin–charge coupling invoked in Eq. S5 they would keep the order suppressed and could set the 7 ns recovery through recombination.
   - *Requested:*
     - an independent measurement of photocarrier lifetime in CuO under the same excitation, e.g. transient optical reflectivity/absorption on this crystal, or tr-XAS at the Cu L3 / O K edge across ps–ns;
     - evidence that the carriers have decayed long before the RXD recovers.
   - Without this, step (3) of the conclusions, recovery via anomalous magnon–phonon scattering, is not uniquely supported.

**B. Should address**

5. **The two-exponential decomposition and the extracted τ_RDS(q) need firmer footing.**
   - *Form of the fit function.* Eq. S3 as written, A·exp(−t/τ) with no step function or (1 − exp) form and no convolution with the ~60 fs instrument response, cannot produce the rising curves in Figs. S6C/F. Please give the actual function fitted, including how time zero and the instrument response were handled.
   - *Fit window versus fitted times.* Close to q_RXD the fitted τ_RDS reaches ~4 ps (Fig. 3F) and ~13 ps (Fig. S9E), comparable to or longer than the 4 ps window. Amplitude and time constant are then strongly correlated, and the error bars from the fit may understate the uncertainty.
   - *Non-exponential traces.* Fig. S8 shows the upturn is non-monotonic (maximum ~5 ps, plateau after ~10 ps), so a single exponential is not an adequate description.
   - *Requested:*
     - extend the delay range at the small-|q| points;
     - report parameter correlations;
     - state the basis for "the scattering rate is proportional to momentum" (Fig. 3I has only guide-to-the-eye lines); a fit with its uncertainty is needed.

6. **The comparison between simulation and data is qualitative, and the model's excitation strength does not match the measured suppression.**
   - *Side-by-side maps only.* Fig. 3G (simulation, 0–6 ps, colour scale 0–800) and Fig. 3E (data, 0–4 ps, arbitrary units) are compared only by eye.
   - *Requested overlays and statements:*
     - simulated τ_RDS(q) on Fig. 3I;
     - simulated and measured time traces at matched q, with a single overall scale factor;
     - a clear statement of whether Figs. 3J–N are simulation (as the main text implies) or "derived from tr-RDS data using Eq. 2" (as the caption says). Inverting energy-integrated data into f(q, t) would itself need justification.
   - *Excitation strength.* B₀Δn was tuned to reproduce "a reduction of the AFM order by approximately 50%" (p. S13). Fig. 2D shows a ~28% drop in RXD intensity; since intensity scales as M², this corresponds to roughly a 15% reduction of the sublattice moment. Please reconcile.
   - *Branch structure.* The simplified orthorhombic model has two degenerate magnon branches (p. S13). The main-text argument relies on distinct acoustic (~1.3 meV) and optical (~23 meV) gaps, so please state which gap the model has and how the optical branch, whose decay is invoked in the conclusions and in Fig. S8, enters.
   - *Chemical potential.* Because quartic magnon–magnon scattering conserves magnon number, the "quasi-thermalized" state should be a Bose–Einstein distribution with a non-zero chemical potential. Please show this explicitly, since it is the physical origin of the claimed bottleneck.

7. **The fluence calibration is inconsistent between figures.**
   - *Range.* Fig. 2F spans log f ≈ 0–3.3, i.e. up to ~2 mJ/cm², and the RDS upturn is already falling at its high end. All momentum-resolved and ns data were taken at 3 mJ/cm², which lies outside the scan.
   - *Values.* Fig. 2F shows ~−50% RXD change at ~2 mJ/cm², whereas Fig. 2D at 3 mJ/cm² shows ~−28%.
   - *Requested:*
     - the definition of fluence (incident or absorbed, spot sizes of pump and probe, angle of incidence, reflectivity);
     - an explanation of the difference between datasets (different runs, footprint, or sample position?);
     - the heating estimate redone for the 18 nm probed surface layer rather than the average.
   - *ICM transition.* Fig. S7 shows the transient incommensurate (ICM) phase appearing at higher fluence, so the margin between 3 mJ/cm² and the ICM transition matters. The statement that no upturn is seen at the ICM position at moderate fluence "within our experimental precision" (p. S7) should be backed by the trace.

8. **There is a single temperature, a single sample and a single fluence for the momentum maps.**
   - *The model makes testable predictions.* The magnon–magnon and anomalous magnon–phonon rates depend on the thermal magnon and phonon occupations, so they predict a clear temperature dependence of τ_RDS(q) and of the ns recovery.
   - *Requested:*
     - tr-RDS and long-delay RXD at one or two lower temperatures (e.g. 100 K and 150 K), which would test the framework directly and would also move the measurement away from T_N1 (concern 2);
     - confirmation that the key momentum dependence (Fig. 3F) reproduces on a second crystal or after thermal cycling, with the spread stated.

9. **The NRDS analysis needs more support.**
   - *The enhancement and its interpretation.* The AFM enhancement at ~2 ns (Fig. 4D, Fig. S10C) rests on a handful of delays with large error bars. The ~160 ps peak sits at the 100 ps resolution limit, yet is read as the "phonon quasi-thermalization timescale".
   - *Thermal expansion is not clearly excluded.* The ROI between ellipses (Fig. S10C) is said to exclude Bragg-peak shifts. However, panels 2 and 4 of Fig. S10 show clear dipolar (shift-like) patterns. CuO also has strong magnetoelastic coupling, so the thermal expansion and magnetostriction in the AFM phase differ from those in the PM phase.
   - *Requested:*
     - radial and azimuthal profiles versus delay;
     - the fitted Bragg-peak position versus delay in both phases;
     - a demonstration that the ROI signal is insensitive to the ROI choice.

10. **Twin and antiferromagnetic domains and their walls as a source of diffuse intensity.**
    - *Domain content.* Fig. S4 shows many twin stripes across the crystal, which seems at odds with "a minimal volume of twin domains". Magnetic domains are not characterised.
    - *Why it matters.* Domain walls and finite domain size give quasi-elastic diffuse intensity near the magnetic peak, and this intensity can change on photoexcitation.
    - *Requested:*
      - the X-ray footprint relative to the domain size;
      - the equilibrium (pump-off) diffuse intensity at the measured q points;
      - an argument that pump-induced domain-wall dynamics do not contribute.

11. **The claims of generality and novelty go beyond the evidence.**
    - *Generality.* The abstract and conclusions state that the framework "generalizes across materials", is valid for "any gapped magnetic insulator", "establishes design principles", and enables "attosecond-scale investigations".
    - *What the evidence covers.* The evidence comes from one material at one temperature, over |q| ≤ 0.08 r.l.u. along two directions. The proposed rule, that dispersion overlap controls recovery speed, is not tested by varying the overlap.
    - *Novelty.* The statement that tr-RDS "has not yet been experimentally demonstrated" should be checked against prior time-resolved studies of magnetic diffuse or critical scattering and of transient magnetic peak profiles.
    - Please temper these statements or add the supporting tests.

**C. Minor**

12. Fig. S11 has four delay points per phase with large error bars. These do not establish 0.1 µs versus 0.7 µs time constants, nor the conclusion that electronic thermal conductivity "significantly contributes" through oxygen excess or Cu vacancies. Stage (4) in the conclusions should be stated as tentative.
13. The Néel temperatures are inconsistent: 213/230 K in the text versus 214.5/230.5 K in Fig. S3.
14. The "D point" (Figs. S6, S9) is not defined in the main text. "q_RDS" on p. S8 should presumably be q_RXD.
15. The Fig. 3 caption says J–N are "derived from tr-RDS data", which conflicts with the main text (see concern 6). Figs. 3G/H span 0–6 ps while the data span 0–4 ps. Fig. 2E uses (1/2, −0.08, −1/2) while the other RDS data use (0.49, 0.01, −0.51); please explain why.
16. In Eq. S37 the last off-diagonal element should be F_zy. Please also clarify whether the outgoing polarization is summed while the incoming polarization is fixed to π.
17. Methods should give the following, and how the error bars were obtained:
    - pump and probe spot sizes;
    - the X-ray footprint;
    - the number of shots per point;
    - the detector linearity and saturation handling for the APDs;
    - the time-zero determination.
18. Refs. 63 and 71 are duplicates. There are typos: "Ultrafasst" (ref. 69), "Sacttering" (p. S18), "Assmining" (p. S15), "La0.5Sr1.5MnO4" formatting (ref. 22), and the stray comma before Eq. S22.
19. Please state whether the "Hydrodynamic magnon slowdown … (Fig. S16)" is a quantitative feature of the simulation or a qualitative remark. Fig. S16 does not show it directly.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add an equilibrium temperature series of the RXD and the diffuse intensity at the measured q points (150–240 K). Use it to calibrate the pumped signal into an effective temperature or occupation, which directly separates thermal from non-thermal magnons.
2. Record full, unsaturated two-dimensional profiles of the magnetic peak versus delay with the area detector. Show that the diffuse upturn survives after the peak position, width and integrated intensity are fitted and subtracted.
3. Fix the magnon–phonon coupling constants from independent input (DFT ∇J or measured spin–phonon shifts). Then fit RXD, RDS and NRDS jointly with one rate-equation/QBE model, including an energy-conservation check.
4. Measure the photocarrier lifetime in this crystal (transient reflectivity or tr-XAS) and repeat the momentum-resolved tr-RDS at a lower base temperature. Both would test the proposed hierarchy far more stringently than the single 190 K dataset.
