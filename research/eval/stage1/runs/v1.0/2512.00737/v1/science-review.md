A. Must address

1. The data do not show anything specific to "bi-altermagnetism" beyond what the known magnetic structure already fixes (Abstract; p. 1 right column; final paragraph of "CD in RIXS and bi-altermagnetism", p. 4). The central claim is that the Oh sublattice alone and the Td sublattice alone are each altermagnetic, and that this is a new form of altermagnetism. Four problems stand in the way. First, the paper gives only the relativistic magnetic point group (6'm'm' / 6'mm') and no spin-group (non-relativistic) analysis, although altermagnetism is defined through spin groups. It does not give the Wyckoff positions of each sublattice or the operation (6_3 screw or c-glide, not translation or inversion) that relates the up and down sites within each sublattice. Second, both sublattices order at a single T_N and are locked together by the strong Oh-Td exchange. Once the crystal has the 6'mm' symmetry, CD is allowed on every excitation of every site, including a nonmagnetic or weakly polarised site. CD on the Td feature therefore follows from the global symmetry breaking and does not show that the Td sublattice is an independent altermagnet. The authors' own model describes the Td site as Van Vleck-type: spin-orbit coupling gives a singlet, and the moment is induced by the Weiss field (p. 5; SI p. 7). Its staggered moment is therefore slaved to the exchange field and is not an independent order parameter. As written, any altermagnet with two inequivalent magnetic Wyckoff positions would be "bi-altermagnetic", and that describes many known compounds. Third, in the single-ion model a moment along c breaks the m mirrors at any site whether the sublattice is compensated or not. The paper does not show that the CD would be absent or different if a sublattice were ferromagnetically aligned, as in the field-induced or Zn-doped ferrimagnetic phases of this family (Refs. 35, 36). Fourth, the up and down sublattice contributions are summed (Methods; SI Sec. 2) but never shown separately, so it is unclear whether they add or cancel, and therefore whether the observable is sensitive to the compensated, alternating arrangement that defines an altermagnet. The authors should: (i) give the spin-group analysis for each sublattice; (ii) state an operational definition of bi-altermagnetism that differs from "an altermagnet with two inequivalent magnetic sites"; (iii) show the computed CD for each sublattice separately and for alternative ordering patterns (parallel spins within the Oh sublattice, only one site ordered, L in the ab plane), and say which observed features rule out each alternative; (iv) identify an observable that would differ between a bi-altermagnet and an altermagnet whose second sublattice is merely polarised (for example distinct temperature dependences of CD on features A and B, a quantitative CD ratio between sites that depends on the sublattice order parameters, or independently controllable sublattice orders), and say whether the data test it; (v) explain what the RIXS-CD reveals that neutron diffraction has not already established. If the CD cannot make these distinctions, the claim should be reframed as sublattice-resolved detection of mirror-symmetry breaking by the Néel order, with bi-altermagnetism inferred from the known structure rather than "unveiled".

2. The magnetic domain state is not characterised or controlled (RIXS measurements, Methods; Figs. 2, 3). The m operation maps L onto -L and RCP onto LCP in this geometry, so the two time-reversed antiferromagnetic domains give CD of opposite sign (the basis of the MnTe domain imaging in Refs. 40, 41). A beam footprint with equal domain populations would show no net CD, so the observed CD implies a dominant domain after what appears to be zero-field cooling. The paper does not say how this was obtained (field cooling, magnetoelectric cooling, strain, natural domain size against spot size) and gives no beam size, no mapping over several sample positions and no repeated cooldowns. Fe2Mo3O8 is also polar, and the CD sign may depend on the relative orientation of P and L, so the polar domain state matters too. Without this, the observed CD magnitude is set by an unknown domain imbalance, the magnitude comparison with the model (Fig. 2a against 2b,c) is uncontrolled, and the sign agreement between experiment and simulation has no meaning, because the simulated sign depends on the chosen L. The authors should report the cooling protocol, the spot size, and the number of thermal cycles; show the CD sign and magnitude at several sample positions and after repeated cooling through T_N; and ideally reverse L deliberately (magnetoelectric cooling with parallel and antiparallel E and H, opposite-sign field cooling, or field cooling through the ferrimagnetic phase) and show that the CD at A and at B reverses sign together. A common sign flip at both sites would be the strongest evidence the paper could offer that both sublattice signals come from one Néel order parameter.

3. The CD and its absence are never quantified, and the instrumental controls are not shown (Figs. 2a, 3, S1–S3). Every CD statement rests on visual comparison of overlaid RCP and LCP curves, with no difference spectra, no normalised asymmetry (I_R − I_L)/(I_R + I_L), no error bars and no counting statistics. Key statements, that the CD "vanished within the experimental uncertainty" above T_N (p. 3) and "vanishes within the present experimental accuracy" in the m-plane geometry (p. 4, Fig. S1), cite an uncertainty that is never given. Yet in the m geometry the elastic peaks differ by about 8% (about 95 against 88, Fig. S1 lower panel), with small residual differences at B and near 1 eV; at 70 K there is a visible RCP/LCP difference on the elastic line (Fig. 3d, Fig. S2g). The normalisation is not described (incident flux, elastic line, or high-energy dd weight); normalising to a feature that itself carries CD would bias the result. Absolute intensities also change between temperatures (feature B about 20 at 32 K and about 14 at 80 K; all dd features 25–30% weaker at 80 K in Fig. S3h), and the shape of the feature A shoulder changes across T_N. The temperature dependence cannot exclude helicity-dependent flux or beam-position artifacts on its own, because the paramagnetic and ordered measurements also differ in time and thermal history. The authors should: show difference or normalised CD spectra with counting-statistics error bars for both geometries and all temperatures; give the statistical error per energy bin; state the normalisation procedure (within and across temperatures), the degree of circular polarisation for each helicity, and beam-position stability on helicity switching; give the m-plane residual CD as a number with uncertainty next to the m'-plane CD; quote CD magnitudes at A and B (for example as percentages) so that readers can compare with MnTe and CrSb; and perform a geometric sign-reversal test in the ordered phase (the mirror-conjugate geometry, for example ±angle about the specular condition or a 180° rotation about c), which should reverse the CD.

4. The assignment of the CD at feature A to the Oh site rests mainly on the model, and the energy region is crowded (p. 3; Fig. 4; Figs. S4, S6). The Oh half of the bi-altermagnet claim depends on feature A, which sits on the tail of the elastic line at 45 meV total resolution. The fits in Fig. S4 place two components at about 35 (31–38) and 70 meV, the first partly inside the elastic line. The Td spin-orbital and exchange-split levels extend to roughly 20–45 meV for Weiss fields of 3–5 meV (Fig. S6a), and magnons (10–15 meV, Ref. 44) and phonons also fall in this range. Feature A is defined as a "broad structure centered at about 70 meV", but in Figs. 2a, 3a and S2a the CD spans roughly 0.03–0.15 eV and so overlaps both fitted components and the Td and magnon region. The statement (p. 3) that the Td spin-orbit excitation "lies well below 50 meV and does not yield significant CD" is a calculated statement, and the Td simulations in Fig. S6 are not discussed at low energy. The paper itself notes (p. 2; Figs. 1e, 1f) that Td and Oh resonate at different incident energies, which gives a direct test. The authors should measure the CD of feature A as a function of incident energy (an RCP − LCP map over 703–710 eV matching the RCP map of Fig. 4b,c) and compare it with computed Oh and Td CD maps, to show that the dichroic weight resonates at the Oh energy. They should also fit the RCP and LCP spectra separately with the Fig. S4 model to show which component carries the dichroism; give the energy-integrated CD of A in a window that excludes the region below 50 meV; show the calculated Td-site CD explicitly in the 0–0.15 eV window; and state the elastic-line CD.

B. Should address

5. The comparison between model and experiment is qualitative, and the one comparison that does not depend on the domain state is not shown (Fig. 2a against 2b,c; Figs. S6, S7; Methods). Measured and calculated difference spectra are never plotted, and the Oh and Td contributions are shown separately, never as a weighted sum convolved with the 45 meV resolution and overlaid on the data. The relative CD magnitudes at A and B and the CD line shape (sign changes within feature B) are not compared. The relative sign of the CD at A and B is fixed, independently of domains, by the antiparallel Oh and Td moments within a layer, so it is a domain-independent prediction. In the data (Fig. 2a, Fig. S1) LCP exceeds RCP at both A (around 0.07 eV) and B (around 0.43 eV), but at the plotted scale the model's relative sign cannot be read from Fig. 2b,c. The paper also does not say how the Oh-up/Td-down pairing of the real Néel structure, the orientation of the local axes of the up and down sublattices (related by the 6_3 screw, which does not preserve the scattering geometry), the incident and scattered angles and the Q of Fig. 2 enter the simulation. The Weiss field acts only on spin (SI p. 6), although the paper stresses an unquenched orbital moment, and the intermediate-state broadening Γ = 0.3 eV is fixed; the SI says only that the "presence or absence" of CD is insensitive to ζ. Without these, "the simulations reproduce the experimentally observed CD" amounts to "both show some CD", which symmetry alone guarantees. The authors should overlay measured and resolution-convolved calculated difference spectra for features A, B and C on the same axes; state the relative A/B sign in theory and experiment with the sublattice orientation specified explicitly; give the ratio CD(A)/CD(B); show the combined simulation for each geometry, including the m-plane geometry where it should vanish; and show how CD magnitudes and line shapes depend on the Weiss field, Γ and ζ.

6. The temperature dependence of the CD is not quantified (Fig. 3; Figs. S2, S3). The claim that the CD tracks the altermagnetic order rests on overlaid spectra judged by eye. The CD weakens sharply between 50 and 55 K and is nearly gone at 55 K (Figs. S2d, S3d), below the nominal T_N = 60 K, and the lowest temperature, 32 K, is not in the saturated regime. The authors should plot the integrated CD at A and at B against temperature, with error bars from counting statistics and repeat scans, and compare it with the sublattice magnetisation from neutron diffraction or susceptibility, or with (1 − T/T_N)^(2β), since the RIXS-CD is expected to be odd in L and to first order linear in it. They should say whether the Oh and Td signals have the same temperature dependence, as the single-order-parameter picture requires; separate temperature dependences would also test the site-resolved claim. They should state the sample-temperature calibration and estimate X-ray beam heating, which could explain the apparent onset below T_N.

7. The Weiss field is chosen after the fact (SI p. 7: "around 3–5 meV, which appears to be a reasonable value"). The size and line shape of the calculated CD, and the induced Van Vleck moment at the Td site, depend strongly on this parameter (Fig. S6). Mean-field estimates from T_N = 60 K and the known magnon bandwidth (Ref. 44) suggest exchange fields well above this range. The authors should tie the Weiss field to independent information (published exchange constants or the inelastic neutron magnon bandwidth, or a mean-field estimate from T_N), compare the resulting model moments (for example (S_c, L_c) = (1.67, 0.35) at Td for 3 meV) with the Oh and Td ordered moments from neutron diffraction, and show how the integrated CD at A and B scales with the Weiss field in the allowed range, compared with the measured magnitude. Constraining the trigonal distortion with independent neutron or THz data would likewise turn the CD magnitudes into a quantitative test.

8. A ferrimagnetic or net-moment contribution is not ruled out for the measured crystal (Methods, crystal growth). Fe2Mo3O8 lies close to a ferrimagnetic phase induced by modest c-axis fields or small changes in composition (Refs. 35, 36), with a net moment and an ordering temperature similar to T_N. In that phase conventional XMCD-type dichroism would also appear below about 60 K. The null result in the m geometry argues against this, because in a ferrimagnetic 6m'm' state all vertical mirrors become primed and CD would appear in both geometries, but that control exists only at 32 K and one Q and is not quantified. The authors should show M(T) and M(H) for the measured crystal to confirm a compensated ground state, give the Fe:Mo stoichiometry from EDX or refinement, and ideally show circularly polarised XAS in the same geometry, where 6'mm' requires zero XMCD.

11. The 2:3 energy-ratio argument for the J_eff assignment of feature A is inconsistent with the paper's own level scheme and fits (p. 5 left column; Fig. 4a; Fig. S4d). The text says the two low-energy components have an "energy ratio close to 2:3—consistent with the prediction of the zero-order approximation". In the first-order scheme of Fig. 4a, the intervals are ζ/2 and 3ζ/4 (a 2:3 ratio of intervals), which puts transitions from the J_eff = 1 ground state at ζ/2 and 5ζ/4, a 2:5 ratio of energies. The atomic calculation in the Fig. 4 caption places the quintets at about 23.7–25.6 meV and the septets at about 58.1–64.8 meV (ratio about 0.4, consistent with 2:5). The fitted peaks in Fig. S4d (about 31–38 and 68–73 meV) give an energy ratio of about 1:2 and intervals of about 1:1. Neither matches 2:3. The main text uses ζ = 52 meV (Fig. 4), while the CD simulations use ζ = 65 meV (SI, criterion C2). The fit itself (a free Voigt elastic peak plus two Lorentzians at 45 meV resolution) has many free parameters for a structureless shoulder, the ~35 meV component sits within one resolution width of the elastic line, and no parameter uncertainties are given. Peak 1 also disperses by about 7 meV between L = 0.7 and 0.9 (Fig. S4d), although the SI calls this "minimal momentum dependence". The text says "zero-order approximation" while Fig. 4a shows first- and second-order levels. The authors should state which ratio is meant and correct the statement, give the fitted energies with uncertainties and constraints, compare them with the full calculation including Weiss field and trigonal distortion (Fig. S7a), say whether the data favour ζ = 52 or 65 meV and use one value throughout, state how robust the two-peak decomposition is, correct the dispersion statement, make the terminology consistent, and avoid calling the agreement quantitative.

The authors state that the CD "arises predominantly from the wave-vector dependence of the polarization (dipole) operators rather than momentum dependence of the final-state wave functions", but all CD data are taken at a single, nearly specular Q, (0.05,0,0.65) or (0.03,0,0.66). 15. The claim of rare "active orbital magnetism" is overstated and untested (p. 5, end of results). The paper calls Fe2Mo3O8 "a rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism". Rutile-type FeF2 and CoF2, widely discussed as altermagnets, host Fe2+/Co2+ ions with unquenched orbital moments. The orbital moment of about 20% of the spin moment, and the Van Vleck-type magnetism at the Td site (SI p. 7), come only from the ionic model with an assumed Weiss field; nothing measured here isolates the orbital moment. The SI quotes the 20% for the Td site, which is not consistent with how the main text uses it, and the p. 5 paragraph moves from the Td site to "a singlet ground state A1" in a way that is hard to follow. The authors should temper the claim, state which site each statement and the 20% figure refer to, and either check the model moments against independent evidence (literature g-factors from ESR or THz work, neutron ordered moments, susceptibility anisotropy, XMCD sum rules in the field-induced ferrimagnetic phase) or present the statement as a model inference.

17. SI Sec. 3 has derivation problems in the effective-orbital-moment treatment of the Oh Fe2+ ion. The single determinant used in Eqs. (2)–(5) has M_L = −1 and M_S = 2 and is not the stretched J = 3 state used in Eq. (9); the statement "J = |L + S| = 3" should make clear that this is the top of the multiplet, not the ground state. The authors should cite a standard treatment (for example Abragam–Bleaney) or redo the derivation consistently and recheck the sign and size of the moment.
