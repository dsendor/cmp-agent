Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports circular dichroism (CD) in Fe L3-edge RIXS on the polar antiferromagnet Fe2Mo3O8. The CD appears below T_N = 60 K in a scattering geometry whose plane is an m' (mirror x time-reversal) plane. It is absent in a geometry whose plane is a true mirror m. It appears in excitations that the authors assign to the octahedral (Oh, spin-orbital feature A near 70 meV) and tetrahedral (Td, dd feature B near 0.45 eV) Fe sites. Ionic multiplet calculations with a staggered Weiss field reproduce the CD qualitatively. On this basis the authors propose that Fe2Mo3O8 is a "bi-altermagnet", with two independently altermagnetic sublattices. RIXS-CD as a site-selective probe of compensated magnets is timely, and observing the CD switch on at T_N is cleaner than in earlier MnTe/CrSb work, where the paramagnetic phase was out of reach. The new concept, "bi-altermagnetism", is asserted more than it is tested, and the comparison with theory is qualitative.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The symmetry-based choice of geometry is a good one. In the m-plane geometry the CD is forbidden in all phases. In the m'-plane geometry it is forbidden only in the paramagnetic phase. The paper shows CD in the second geometry and none in the first (Fig. 2a inset, Fig. S1).
- A dense temperature series (32 to 80 K, Figs. 3, S2, S3) shows the CD disappearing at or just below T_N in both feature A and feature B. This links the signal to magnetic order and not to the static polar structure.
- The ionic model was optimised on peak positions only, without using the CD (SI Sec. 2, criteria C1 to C3). It reproduces the main dd and spin-orbital features (Fig. 1e,f; Fig. 2b,c). The CD that comes out of the model is therefore not tuned by construction.
- Fe2Mo3O8 has two inequivalent magnetic Fe sites with very different crystal-field scales (about 0.5 eV and 1 eV). This makes it a natural test case for site-resolved dichroism, and the multiferroic context broadens the interest.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. What "bi-altermagnetism" means, and what observation could have ruled it out (Abstract; p. 1 right column; final paragraph of "CD in RIXS and bi-altermagnetism"). The paper says each sublattice can "individually support altermagnetic order", but gives no explicit analysis: no spin group for the Oh-only and Td-only sublattices, and no statement of which operation links the up and down sites within each sublattice (6_3 screw or c-glide, not translation or inversion). This analysis should appear in the paper. The experimental logic is the bigger problem. Both sublattices order at a single T_N and are locked together by the strong Oh-Td exchange, so the whole crystal has one magnetic point group, 6'm m'. Once that group is realised, symmetry allows CD in every excitation, including those of a nonmagnetic or weakly polarised site. CD at the Td feature therefore follows from the global symmetry breaking. It does not show that the Td sublattice taken alone is altermagnetic. As written, any altermagnet with two inequivalent magnetic Wyckoff positions would be "bi-altermagnetic". The authors should:
   (i) give the sublattice spin-group analysis;
   (ii) state what experimental result would distinguish a bi-altermagnet from an ordinary altermagnet whose second site is merely polarised by the first, for example a quantitative CD ratio between the sites that depends on the sublattice order parameters;
   (iii) or reframe the claim as site-resolved detection of altermagnetic order in a material with two magnetic sites.

2. The comparison with theory is qualitative, and the one comparison that does not depend on the domain state is not shown (Fig. 2a vs 2b,c; Fig. S6; Methods). The paper shows RCP and LCP curves overlaid but never the difference spectra (I_RCP − I_LCP), measured or calculated. The sign of the CD in a single domain reverses with L, so the absolute sign cannot be compared without knowing the domain state (see concern 3). The relative sign and magnitude of the CD at feature A (Oh) and feature B (Td), however, are fixed by the known relative orientation of the Oh and Td moments, which is antiparallel within a layer. That makes them a domain-independent prediction of the model. In the data (Fig. 2a, Fig. S1), LCP exceeds RCP at both A (around 0.07 eV) and B (around 0.43 eV). Reading the theory panels, I cannot tell whether the model gives the same relative sign. The Oh and Td ionic models are also computed separately and summed. The paper does not say how the Oh-up/Td-down pairing of the real Néel structure, and the geometry (incident and scattered angles, the Q of Fig. 2), enter the simulation. The authors should:
   - plot the measured and calculated CD difference spectra on the same axes, including the CD at feature C;
   - state the relative sign of the CD at A and B and show that the model reproduces it, with the sublattice orientation specified explicitly;
   - give the ratio CD(A)/CD(B) and its dependence on the Weiss field.
   Without this, "the simulations reproduce the experimentally observed CD" amounts to "both show some CD", which symmetry alone guarantees.

3. The magnetic domain state is not characterised (RIXS measurements, Methods; Figs. 2, 3). RIXS-CD in an altermagnet has opposite sign for the two T-related domains (L and −L) (Refs. 40, 41). The paper observes a net CD, so the beam spot must have been close to single-domain after zero-field cooling. The manuscript gives no reason for this: no beam size, no field or magnetoelectric cooling, no mapping over several spots, and no repeated cooldowns. Fe2Mo3O8 is also polar, and the sign of the CD may depend on the relative orientation of P and L, so the polar domain state matters too. The authors should state how the domain population was set or characterised. The decisive test is to reverse L, for example by magnetoelectric cooling in parallel or antiparallel E and H, or by field cooling through the ferrimagnetic phase, and show that the CD at A and at B reverses sign together. Failing that, they should at least show the CD sign and magnitude over repeated cooldowns and sample positions. A sign flip that is common to both sites would be the strongest evidence the paper could offer that the CD at both sites comes from a single altermagnetic order parameter.

B. Should address

4. The assignment of feature A to the Oh site depends on the model, and the energy region is crowded (p. 3; Fig. 4; Fig. S4). The resolution is 45 meV FWHM. The fit in Fig. S4 places peak 1 at 31 to 38 meV, partly inside the elastic line. The text also says the Td spin-orbital excitations lie "well below 50 meV", and magnons reach this range as well. Feature A is defined as a "broad structure centered at about 70 meV", but the CD in Figs. 2a, 3a and S2 extends from about 0.03 to 0.15 eV and overlaps the Td and magnon region. The authors should give the energy-integrated CD of A in a window that excludes the region below 50 meV. They should also show the calculated Td-site CD in that same window (Fig. S6b–d), to put a number on how much of the A-region CD could come from Td or magnon excitations.

5. The 2:3 energy ratio is inconsistent with the paper's own level scheme and fits (p. 5, left column; Fig. 4a; Fig. S4d). In the first-order scheme of Fig. 4a, transitions from the J_eff = 1 ground state to the quintet and septet lie at ζ/2 and 5ζ/4, a ratio of 2:5. Only the successive intervals (ζ/2 and 3ζ/4) are in the ratio 2:3. The caption of Fig. 4a gives quintet at 23.7 to 25.6 meV and septet at 58.1 to 64.8 meV, a ratio of about 0.4. The fitted peaks in Fig. S4d (about 31–38 and 68–73 meV) give a ratio of about 0.5. Neither number is 2:3. The sentence also calls the result "zero-order", while Fig. 4a uses first- and second-order approximations. The authors should correct the statement, give the fitted energies with uncertainties, and say whether the data are compatible with ζ = 52 meV (atomic) or ζ = 65 meV (the value adopted, SI C2). Given that peak 1 sits inside the elastic tail, they should also state how robust the two-peak decomposition is.

6. The size of the Weiss field is not justified (SI p. 7: "around 3–5 meV, which appears to be a reasonable value"). The size and line shape of the calculated CD, and the Van Vleck moment at the Td site, depend on this parameter (Fig. S6). The authors should tie it to independent information: the exchange constants or magnon bandwidth from inelastic neutron scattering (Ref. 44), or a mean-field estimate from T_N. They should compare the resulting ordered moments, for example (S_c, L_c) = (1.67, 0.35) at Td for 3 meV, with the Oh and Td moments measured by neutron diffraction. They should also show how the CD magnitude at A and B scales with the Weiss field in the range allowed by these constraints.

7. There is no quantitative temperature dependence of the CD (Fig. 3; Figs. S2, S3). The claim that the CD tracks the altermagnetic order rests on overlaid spectra judged by eye. The CD is already nearly gone at 55 K (Fig. S2d, S3d), while T_N = 60 K. The authors should plot integrated |CD| at A and at B against T, with error bars from counting statistics and repeat scans. They should compare it with the sublattice magnetisation from neutron data or susceptibility, since the RIXS-CD is expected to be odd in L and to first order linear in it. They should also say whether the Oh and Td signals have the same temperature dependence, as the single-order-parameter picture requires. Beam heating should be estimated, because it would shift the apparent onset.

8. The m-plane null control is not quantified, and the normalisation is not described (Fig. 2a inset; Fig. S1 lower panel). In the m-plane geometry the elastic peaks for RCP and LCP differ visibly (about 95 vs 88 in Fig. S1), and small differences remain at B and near 1 eV. In the m'-plane data the elastic intensities match. The authors should state how the RCP and LCP spectra were normalised (incident flux, elastic line, total dd weight), the degree of circular polarisation of each helicity, and the Q and sample azimuth of the m-plane measurement. Q is given only for the m' geometry, and in hexagonal notation the ac plane does not contain (h,0,l). They should then give the residual CD in the m-plane geometry as a number, with its uncertainty, next to the m'-plane CD. The same normalisation question applies across temperatures: the intensities at 80 K in Fig. S3h are about 25% lower than at other temperatures.

9. The explanation for the missing CD at feature C is ad hoc (p. 4, "Note that feature C …"). The calculation in Fig. 2b and Fig. S6f–h shows visible CD at the about 1 eV Oh feature, and the experiment shows none. The authors attribute this to lifetime broadening, but the final-state broadening used in the simulation is not given; only Γ = 0.3 eV for the intermediate state is quoted. Since feature C belongs to the same Oh ion as feature A, this discrepancy bears on the site-resolved interpretation. The authors should show calculations with a physically justified final-state broadening for the 5E and 3T1 states and check whether the CD at C then falls below the experimental noise.

10. The claim of rare "active orbital magnetism" is overstated (p. 5, end of results). The paper calls Fe2Mo3O8 "a rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism". Rutile-type FeF2 and CoF2, which are widely discussed as altermagnets, host Fe2+/Co2+ ions with unquenched orbital moments. In addition, the 20% orbital-to-spin ratio comes only from the ionic model with an assumed Weiss field and is not measured. The authors should temper the claim, state which site the 20% refers to (the SI numbers are for Td), and cite independent evidence, for example XMCD sum rules in the field-induced ferrimagnetic phase or g-factors from ESR or THz work.

11. The number of samples and reproducibility are not reported (Methods). The paper does not say how many crystals or cleaved or polished surfaces were measured, or whether the m- and m'-geometry data come from the same crystal remounted. The authors should report this, and show the key CD at both A and B in at least a second crystal or cooldown. This overlaps with concern 3.

C. Minor

12. Q is given as (0.05, 0, 0.65) for Fig. 2 and Fig. S1, but as (0.03, 0, 0.66) for Fig. 3 and Figs. S2, S3. Please explain why it differs and whether the CD magnitude depends on Q in this range. Fig. S4 uses Q = (0, 0, L) with RCP only; it would help to say whether CD was measured there.

13. The CD calculation for the paramagnetic phase is "not shown" (p. 4). Even though symmetry forces it to vanish, a panel would show that the numerical setup respects the m' symmetry of the geometry.

14. SI Sec. 3: the effective-orbital-momentum treatment of the t2g manifold (T–P equivalence, l_eff = −l, with the covalency factor) is not discussed. The resulting g-factor (5/2) and moment (5/2 μB) for J_eff = 1 should be checked against the standard treatment of octahedral Fe2+. The statement "J = |L + S| = 3" for the state used in Eq. (9) should make clear that this is the top of the multiplet, not the ground state.

15. The Methods should give the incident and scattering angles, the beam footprint, the circular-polarisation degree of the source for each helicity, the acquisition time per spectrum, and whether self-absorption differs between geometries. The simulation should state whether outgoing polarisations are summed and how the experimental geometry enters.

16. In Fig. 1b, the m' planes are drawn as orange lines and the m planes as light-blue lines, but the text and caption call them "three vertical mirror planes … m'" and "glide planes m". Please say explicitly which are mirrors and which are glides in P6_3mc. The m plane is called a "mirror (glide) plane" on p. 4 and an "m glide symmetry plane" in the caption of Fig. 2.

17. "Data available upon reasonable request": please consider depositing the RCP/LCP spectra and model parameters.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one main-text figure with measured and calculated CD difference spectra for features A, B and C. Include the integrated CD against temperature with error bars, and the m-plane residual for comparison.
2. Reverse the altermagnetic domain, for example by magnetoelectric cooling, and show that the CD at the Oh and Td features flips sign together. This is the most direct way to tie both site signals to one order parameter.
3. Reframe or sharpen "bi-altermagnetism" with an explicit spin-group analysis of each sublattice and a stated observable that separates it from an ordinary altermagnet with two inequivalent magnetic sites.
4. Constrain the Weiss field and the trigonal distortion with independent neutron or THz data, so that the CD magnitudes become a quantitative test of the model rather than a qualitative check.
