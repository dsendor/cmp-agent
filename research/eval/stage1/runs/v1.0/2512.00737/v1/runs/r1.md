Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors measure Fe L3-edge RIXS with circularly polarized X-rays on the polar collinear antiferromagnet Fe2Mo3O8. In a scattering geometry where the scattering plane coincides with an m' (mirror times time reversal) plane, they find circular dichroism (CD) below T_N = 60 K. The CD appears on a ~70 meV spin-orbital feature (A, assigned to the octahedral Oh site) and on the ~0.45 eV dd feature (B, assigned to the tetrahedral Td site). It vanishes above T_N and is absent in an m-plane geometry. Single-ion multiplet models with a staggered Weiss field reproduce it. From this the authors conclude that both Fe sublattices are individually altermagnetic, which they call "bi-altermagnetism", and they propose RIXS-CD as a sublattice-resolved probe of compensated magnets. Using the energy separation of site-specific local excitations to resolve symmetry breaking on each sublattice is a useful idea, and observing the CD switch on at T_N in a material with an accessible ordering temperature is a real step beyond the MnTe and CrSb RIXS-CD work. The case for a "new form" of altermagnetism is less developed than the spectroscopy. As presented, the CD confirms that the known magnetic order breaks the m mirrors at both sites. It does not yet show a property that is specific to the proposed bi-altermagnetic classification.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The geometry follows a clear symmetry argument: m' plane against m plane, with left and right circular polarization mapped onto each other by the mirror. The paramagnetic phase serves as an internal null control for helicity-dependent instrumental artifacts.
- The temperature series (Fig. 3, Figs. S2 and S3, 32 to 80 K) shows the CD on both features disappearing between 50 and 60 K, in line with T_N.
- The Td and Oh dd excitations are well separated in energy (about 0.5 eV against 1.0 eV). Feature B is therefore a convincing Td-site probe.
- The multiplet model is tuned only on peak positions; the CD was not used in the tuning (SI Sec. 2). The model reproduces the presence of CD at A and B and the near-absence of CD at C. The trigonal-distortion check (Fig. S7) adds robustness.
- RIXS-CD may be one of the few probes of sublattice-resolved symmetry breaking in compensated magnets, which is of broad interest.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Magnetic domains and the sign of the CD are not discussed. The m operation maps the Néel vector L onto -L and RCP onto LCP in this geometry, so the two antiferromagnetic domains (L and -L) must give CD of opposite sign. This is the basis of the MnTe domain imaging in Ref. 40. A sample with equal domain populations within the beam footprint would show no net CD. The paper does not say how a single domain, or a dominant one, was obtained in Fe2Mo3O8: field cooling, magnetoelectric cooling, natural domain size against spot size, or anything else. It also does not say whether the CD sign and magnitude are reproducible after warming above T_N and cooling again, or at other positions on the sample. This matters for the main claim because the size of the observed CD is set by an unknown domain imbalance. Comparing its magnitude with the model (Fig. 2a against 2b,c) is then not meaningful unless the domain state is known. The authors should:
   - report the cooling protocol, spot size and the number of thermal cycles;
   - show CD at several sample positions and after repeated cooling through T_N;
   - ideally, field-cool or magnetoelectric-cool with opposite signs and show that the CD reverses at both A and B together.

   Reversal of both features together would be a strong test that both sublattice signals come from one Néel order parameter.

2. The CD does not yet distinguish "bi-altermagnetism" from what follows from the known magnetic structure. The central claim is that each sublattice (Oh alone and Td alone) is altermagnetic, and that this is a new form of altermagnetism.
   - The paper gives the relativistic magnetic point group (6'm'm') but no spin-group (non-relativistic) analysis. Altermagnetism is defined through spin groups. The Wyckoff positions of each sublattice, and the operation (6_3 screw or c-glide) that relates the up and down sites of that sublattice, should be given explicitly.
   - The observed CD shows that the ordered moments break the m mirrors at the Oh and Td sites. In the single-ion model, a moment along c on any site does this whether the sublattice is compensated (altermagnetic) or not. It is not shown that the CD would be absent, or different, if a sublattice were ferromagnetically aligned, as in the field-induced or Zn-doped ferrimagnetic phases of this family (Refs. 35, 36).
   - The authors sum the up and down sublattices (Methods; SI Sec. 2) but never show the two contributions separately. It is unclear whether the CD of the two sublattices adds or cancels, and therefore whether the observable is sensitive to the alternating, compensated arrangement that defines an altermagnet.

   The authors should show the computed CD for each sublattice separately and for alternative ordering patterns: parallel spins within the Oh sublattice; only one site ordered; L in the ab plane. They should state which observed features would rule out each alternative. If the CD cannot distinguish them, the claim should be reframed as "sublattice-resolved detection of mirror-symmetry breaking by the Néel order", with bi-altermagnetism inferred from the known structure rather than "unveiled".

3. The Oh-site assignment of the CD at feature A rests mainly on the model. The Oh half of the bi-altermagnet claim depends on feature A, which sits on the tail of the elastic line at 45 meV total resolution.
   - The fits in Fig. S4 place two components at about 35 and 70 meV.
   - The Td spin-orbital and exchange-split levels are computed to extend to roughly 20 to 45 meV for Weiss fields of 3 to 5 meV (Fig. S6a).
   - Magnons at 10 to 15 meV (Ref. 44) and phonons are also present.
   - In Fig. 3a and Fig. S2a the CD spans roughly 0.03 to 0.15 eV, so it overlaps both fitted components.

   The text (p. 3) says the Td spin-orbit excitation "lies well below 50 meV and does not yield significant CD", but this is a calculated statement. The paper itself notes (p. 2) that the Td and Oh resonances occur at different incident energies. The direct experimental test is the incident-energy dependence of the CD at feature A: a CD map (RCP minus LCP) over 703 to 710 eV, matching the RCP map of Fig. 4b,c, compared with the computed Oh and Td CD maps. The authors should also fit the RCP and LCP spectra separately with the Fig. S4 model to show which component carries the dichroism. They should state the elastic-line CD as well, which in Fig. S1 and Fig. S2g differs visibly between helicities.

B. Should address

4. The CD and its absence are not quantified. Every CD statement is made from overlaid RCP and LCP curves, without difference spectra, error bars or statistics. Key statements are "vanished within the experimental uncertainty" above T_N (p. 3) and "vanishes within the present experimental accuracy" in the m-plane geometry (p. 4, Fig. S1). Yet in Fig. S1 (lower panel) the elastic peaks differ by about 8% (about 95 against 88), and in Fig. S2g (70 K) there is a visible difference at the elastic line.
   - The authors should show difference spectra (RCP minus LCP), or normalized CD, with counting-statistics error bars.
   - They should give the integrated CD of features A and B against temperature with error bars, and compare it with the sublattice magnetization from neutron diffraction. Separate temperature dependences for the Oh and Td features would also test the site-resolved claim.
   - The normalization should be stated: incident flux, the degree of circular polarization for each helicity, and beam-position stability on helicity switching. Absolute intensities change between temperatures (feature B is about 20 at 32 K and about 14 at 80 K in Fig. S3), so the normalization matters.

5. The comparison between model and experiment is qualitative, and the Weiss field is chosen after the fact. The CD is reproduced for an exchange field of "around 3–5 meV, which appears to be a reasonable value" (SI p. 7). This value is not tied to the known exchange constants or to T_N.
   - The relative CD magnitudes at A and B and the CD line shape (sign changes within feature B) are not compared quantitatively.
   - The relative sign of the CD at A and at B is fixed, independently of domains, by the antiparallel Oh and Td moments within a layer. The paper does not discuss it, and at the plotted scale it cannot be read from Fig. 2b,c.
   - The Weiss field acts only on spin (SI p. 6), although the paper stresses an unquenched orbital moment.
   - The lifetime broadening Γ = 0.3 eV is fixed.

   The authors should overlay computed and measured difference spectra for both features and state the relative A/B sign in theory and experiment. They should justify the Weiss-field magnitude, for example from a mean-field estimate using published exchange constants, and show how CD magnitudes and line shapes depend on Weiss field, Γ and ζ (the SI says only that the "presence or absence" of CD is insensitive to ζ).

6. The 2:3 energy-ratio claim does not match the paper's own fits. On p. 5 the two low-energy components are said to have "energy ratio close to 2:3—consistent with the prediction of the zero-order approximation".
   - Fig. S4d gives peak energies of about 31–38 meV and 68–73 meV. The ratio of energies is then about 1:2, and the intervals (Jeff=1→2 and 2→3) are about 1:1.
   - The first-order prediction (Fig. 4a) gives intervals of ζ/2 and 3ζ/4, a 2:3 ratio of intervals, which puts the levels at ζ/2 and 5ζ/4 (a 2:5 ratio of energies).
   - The atomic calculation in the Fig. 4 caption places the quintets at about 24–26 meV and the septets at about 58–65 meV.
   - The simulations use ζ = 65 meV, not the atomic 52 meV.

   The authors should state clearly which ratio is meant. They should compare the fitted energies with the full calculation that includes the Weiss field and trigonal distortion (Fig. S7a), and avoid calling the agreement quantitative. The fit itself (Voigt elastic peak plus two Lorentzians at 45 meV resolution) needs parameter uncertainties and constraints, since the ~35 meV component sits within one resolution width of the elastic line.

7. The momentum and geometry dependence of the CD is not measured. On p. 4–5 the authors state that the CD "arises predominantly from the wave-vector dependence of the polarization (dipole) operators rather than momentum dependence of the final-state wave functions". All CD data are taken at a single, nearly specular Q, either (0.05,0,0.65) or (0.03,0,0.66). The local-model interpretation predicts a specific dependence of the CD on incident and scattered angles within the m' plane. Measuring the CD at several incident angles (or several L at fixed in-plane geometry), and comparing with the model, would test this statement and the local-excitation picture. It would also help exclude specular or reflectivity contributions to the dichroism near the elastic line.

8. The "active orbital magnetism" claim is not tested by the data. The conclusion that Fe2Mo3O8 is "a rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism" (p. 5) comes from the ionic model: an orbital moment of about 20% of the spin moment, and Van Vleck-type magnetism at the Td site (SI p. 7). No measurement here isolates the orbital moment. The authors should either point to experimental evidence (literature g-factors, ordered moments from neutrons, magnetic susceptibility anisotropy) and check the model moments against it, or present the statement as a model inference. The main-text paragraph on p. 5 also moves from the Td site to "a singlet ground state A1" in a way that is hard to follow, and should say explicitly which site each statement refers to.

9. Sample count and reproducibility are not stated. The paper does not say how many crystals were measured, whether the m and m' geometries used the same crystal (which needs a 30° azimuthal remount) or the same surface preparation, or whether the Fig. 2 data (Q = (0.05,0,0.65)) and Fig. 3 data (Q = (0.03,0,0.66)) come from different mounts. The authors should report this and show the key CD result on at least a second crystal or mount. They should also estimate X-ray beam heating at 32–55 K: the CD weakens sharply between 50 and 55 K, below the nominal T_N, which could reflect a difference between sample and sensor temperature.

10. A site-substitution control is missing. Zn in Fe2Mo3O8 substitutes preferentially on the Td site (Ref. 36 and related work). A (Fe,Zn)2Mo3O8 or Zn-rich sample would be a direct experimental test of site specificity: the CD at B should be suppressed while the CD at A stays, provided the Oh sublattice remains compensated. This control, or the field-induced ferrimagnetic phase (see concern 2), would turn the sublattice assignment from model-based into experimental.

C. Minor

11. The Q values differ between Fig. 2 and Fig. S1 ((0.05,0,0.65)) and Fig. 3 and Figs. S2–S3 ((0.03,0,0.66)) with no explanation. The Q for the m-plane geometry (Fig. 2a inset, Fig. S1 lower panel) is not given, and should be stated in the hexagonal reciprocal-lattice basis.

12. SI Sec. 3 has derivation problems.
    - The single determinant used in Eqs. (2)–(5) has M_L = −1 and M_S = 2. It is not the stretched J = 3 state used in Eq. (9).
    - The sign of the effective coupling under the T–P equivalence (l_eff = −l) is not handled. The standard result is λ = −ζ/4 for the orbital operator of the T2 manifold.
    - The Landé factor g = 5/2 in Eq. (11) is obtained with an orbital g of +1. This conflicts with the statement that the orbital contribution is antiparallel to the spin.

    The authors should cite a standard treatment (for example Abragam–Bleaney) or redo the derivation consistently.

13. On p. 5 the text refers to the "zero-order approximation", while Fig. 4a shows first- and second-order levels. The terminology should be made consistent.

14. Fig. 1b refers to "three vertical mirror planes ... m′" and to "glide planes m". The text calls m a "mirror (glide) plane". The authors should state clearly which are pure mirrors and which are glides in P6_3mc, since the m-plane control depends on this.

15. The statement that XMCD and AHE "generally do not have sublattice resolution" (p. 4) is too strong. XMCD at the L edge often separates inequivalent sites by multiplet energy, as in mixed-valence and spinel oxides. The authors should qualify it.

16. Fig. 2a and Fig. 3a–d use different vertical scales and normalizations for the same feature. The text quotes no CD magnitude (for example a percentage at A and B). Stating one would help readers compare with MnTe and CrSb.

17. The XAS in Fig. 1d is shown without self-absorption correction. The authors should say whether the RCP and LCP RIXS spectra were corrected for self-absorption, which can differ between helicities near the L3 peak.

18. Data are available "upon reasonable request". Depositing the RIXS spectra and the model input files in a public repository would allow the CD analysis to be reproduced.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add one figure of RCP minus LCP difference spectra with error bars for both geometries and all temperatures, together with the integrated CD of A and B against temperature, overlaid on the neutron order parameter.
- Measure the incident-energy-resolved CD map across the L3 edge to separate Oh and Td contributions to feature A experimentally, and compare it with the computed site-resolved CD maps.
- Demonstrate domain control (opposite field or magnetoelectric cooling) and show that the CD at A and B reverses together. This also anchors the sign comparison with theory.
- Recast the "bi-altermagnetism" claim with an explicit spin-group analysis and model calculations for alternative ordering patterns. Make clear which part of the conclusion the RIXS-CD establishes and which part is inferred from the known magnetic structure.
