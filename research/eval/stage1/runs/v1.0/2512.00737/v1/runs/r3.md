Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports circular dichroism (CD) in Fe L3-edge RIXS from the polar antiferromagnet Fe2Mo3O8. The CD appears below T_N = 60 K when the scattering plane is an m' plane, and is absent in a glide-plane (m) geometry. It is resolved on two groups of excitations: a low-energy spin-orbital feature (A, about 70 meV) assigned to the octahedral (Oh) Fe site, and the e -> t2 crystal-field feature (B, about 0.45 eV) assigned to the tetrahedral (Td) Fe site. From this the authors argue that both Fe sublattices are separately altermagnetic, and they introduce the term "bi-altermagnetism". The symmetry-controlled experiment (m' against m geometry, and above against below T_N) is a clean and useful demonstration of RIXS-CD as a sublattice-resolved probe of compensated order. It goes beyond earlier RIXS-CD work on MnTe and CrSb because the paramagnetic phase can be reached. What the experiment shows that was not already fixed by the known magnetic structure is less clear, and so is how firmly the CD of each feature can be attributed to one sublattice.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A well-chosen symmetry test. In the m' geometry the paramagnetic mirror forbids CD, and the Néel order removes that mirror. The CD switches on and off across T_N in two independent excitation windows (Fig. 3, Figs. S2 and S3, eight temperatures from 32 to 80 K).
- A null geometry (the m glide plane, inset of Fig. 2a and Fig. S1) that is preserved in the ordered phase and in which the CD is much weaker or absent.
- Energy separation of Td and Oh crystal-field excitations, supported by a multiplet model whose crystal-field parameters are fitted to the RIXS spectra and not to the CD (SI Sec. 2, Fig. S5).
- A broader methodological point: RIXS-CD can be finite where XMCD and the anomalous Hall effect are forbidden by the magnetic point group 6'mm'. This makes it a probe for compensated magnets of low symmetry.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. What "bi-altermagnetism" adds beyond the known magnetic structure, and what the data actually discriminate (abstract, introduction p. 1, RIXS-CD section p. 4). The spin-group and magnetic-point-group classification follows from the published magnetic structure: both Fe sites carry ordered, antiparallel moments along c, with point group 6'mm'. Once the whole crystal has 6'mm' symmetry, CD is symmetry-allowed on every excitation of every site, so its observation on a Td-derived feature does not by itself show that the Td sublattice is an "independent" altermagnet. The authors' own model in fact describes the Td site as Van Vleck-type: SOC gives a singlet, and the moment is induced by the Weiss field (p. 5; SI p. 7). Its staggered moment is therefore slaved to the exchange field and is not an independent order parameter. The manuscript should:
   (i) state an operational definition of bi-altermagnetism that differs from "an altermagnet with two inequivalent magnetic Wyckoff sites", which describes many known compounds;
   (ii) identify an observable that would differ between a bi-altermagnet and an altermagnet whose second sublattice is merely polarized, and say whether the data test it. Examples are distinct temperature dependences of the CD on features A and B, or independently controllable sublattice orders;
   (iii) explain what the RIXS-CD reveals that neutron diffraction has not already established.
   Without this, the headline claim reduces to a new label for a known magnetic structure. The data would still be valuable, but as a demonstration of the method.

2. The CD is not quantified, and the instrumental controls are not shown (Figs. 2a, 3, S1 to S3). Every CD statement in the paper rests on visual comparison of overlaid red and blue curves. There are no difference spectra, no normalized asymmetry (I_R - I_L)/(I_R + I_L), no error bars, no counting statistics, and no statement of how the RCP and LCP spectra were normalized (to incident flux, to the elastic line, or to the high-energy dd spectral weight). Normalizing to a feature that itself has CD would bias the result. The text says the CD "vanished within the experimental uncertainty" above T_N (p. 3), but that uncertainty is never given. At 70 K there are visible RCP/LCP differences on the elastic line (Fig. 3d, Fig. S2g), and in the m geometry the elastic peak differs by about 8% (Fig. S1, lower panel). The authors should:
   - plot the integrated CD of features A and B against temperature with error bars, and compare it with an order parameter such as the neutron sublattice magnetization or (1 - T/T_N)^(2β);
   - give the degree of circular polarization for each helicity and the normalization procedure;
   - give the statistical error per energy bin;
   - show a geometric sign-reversal test. Measuring the mirror-conjugate geometry (for example ±H about the specular condition, or rotating the sample 180° about c) should reverse the CD in the ordered phase. This would rule out helicity-dependent flux or beam-position artifacts, which the temperature dependence alone cannot exclude, because the paramagnetic and ordered measurements also differ in time and thermal history.

B. Should address

3. Magnetic domains and reproducibility (whole RIXS-CD section). The two time-reversed domains (L and -L) give CD of opposite sign. An unbalanced domain population within the beam footprint is therefore required to see any CD at all. The paper does not say how a single domain or a dominant domain was obtained: zero-field cooling, magnetoelectric cooling (Fe2Mo3O8 is a polar magnetoelectric), or strain. It also does not compare beam size with domain size. Without this:
   - the sign agreement between experiment and simulation in Figs. 2b and 2c has no meaning, because the simulated sign depends on the chosen L;
   - the magnitude comparison is uncontrolled.
   The authors should report:
   - the number of crystals and cool-downs measured;
   - whether the sign and size of the CD survive thermal cycling through T_N and a change of beam spot;
   - ideally, a deliberate domain selection that reverses the CD.
   All the data appear to come from one crystal.

4. Ruling out a ferrimagnetic or net-moment contribution (Methods, crystal growth). Fe2Mo3O8 lies close to a ferrimagnetic phase. That phase is induced by modest c-axis fields or by small changes in composition (refs. 35, 36), and it has a net moment and an ordering temperature similar to T_N. In that phase conventional XMCD-type dichroism would also appear below about 60 K. The null result in the m geometry argues against this, because in a ferrimagnetic 6m'm' state all vertical mirrors become primed and CD would appear in both geometries. However, that control exists only at 32 K and one Q, and it is not quantified. The authors should:
   - show M(T) and M(H) for the measured crystal, confirming a compensated ground state;
   - give the Fe:Mo stoichiometry from EDX or refinement;
   - ideally, show circularly polarized XAS in the same geometry, where 6'mm' requires zero XMCD.

5. Assignment and analysis of feature A (pp. 3 and 5, Fig. 4, Fig. S4). The claim that the Oh sublattice is altermagnetic rests on feature A, which has several problems.
   (a) The two fitted components at about 35 and 70 meV (Fig. S4d) lie within, or close to, the 45 meV resolution and are fitted together with a free elastic Voigt. The fit therefore has many free parameters for a structureless shoulder, and no parameter uncertainties are given.
   (b) The text states that the energy ratio of the two components is "close to 2:3", consistent with the gaps ζ/2 and 3ζ/4. That ratio describes successive level spacings. Transitions from the J_eff = 1 ground state would be at ζ/2 and 5ζ/4, a ratio of 2:5; the calculated levels in the Fig. 4a caption (about 25 and about 62 meV) agree with 2:5. The fitted 35 and 70 meV give 1:2. The quantitative support for the J_eff assignment is therefore inconsistent as written.
   (c) The main text uses ζ = 52 meV (Fig. 4), while the CD simulations use ξ_3d = 65 meV (SI, criterion C2).
   (d) Peak 1 disperses by about 7 meV between L = 0.7 and 0.9 (Fig. S4d), yet the SI text calls this "minimal momentum dependence".
   (e) Excitations below about 50 meV can also come from the Td site. With a Weiss field of 3 to 5 meV, Td levels lie up to about 45 meV (Fig. S6a). Magnons and phonons also fall in this range.
   The authors should show the CD of feature A as a function of incident energy, using the different Oh and Td resonance energies visible in Figs. 1e and 1f, to show that the dichroic weight resonates at the Oh energy. They should also correct the energy-ratio argument and use one SOC value throughout.

6. The comparison with theory is qualitative, and part of it disagrees with the data (Figs. 2b, 2c, S6, S7).
   - The Oh simulation predicts a visible CD on feature C at about 1.0 eV (Fig. 2b and Figs. S6f to S6h), which is not observed. The explanation given, "large lifetime broadening", is not tested in a calculation with the observed broadening.
   - The Oh and Td contributions are shown separately and never as a weighted sum convolved with the 45 meV resolution and overlaid on the data, together with the difference spectra.
   - The Weiss field of 3 to 5 meV is described as "reasonable" without justification. Mean-field estimates from T_N = 60 K and the known magnon bandwidth (ref. 44) suggest exchange fields well above this. Fig. S6 shows that the induced Td moment and the CD depend strongly on this parameter.
   - The paramagnetic (zero-field) calculation, in which the CD should vanish, is "not shown".
   - The paper does not specify how the local axes of the up and down sublattices are oriented in the ionic model. The CD in the m' geometry depends on the two sublattices being related by the 6_3 screw, which does not preserve the scattering geometry.
   The authors should:
   - present a combined, resolution-convolved simulation of the CD for each geometry, including the m-plane geometry, which the model should also predict to be zero;
   - show the Weiss-field dependence of the integrated CD and compare it with the measured magnitude;
   - justify the field scale using the known ordered moments and exchange constants.

7. The m-geometry control is narrow (inset of Fig. 2a, Fig. S1). It was measured at one temperature and one Q. The Q given for the ac-plane geometry, (0.05, 0, 0.65), is the same as for the a*c* geometry, although a momentum transfer lying in the ac plane cannot have indices (H, 0, L) in hexagonal reciprocal-lattice units. The authors should give the correct Q, state whether the same crystal and beam spot were used, and add a quantitative CD for the m geometry (see concern 2). If possible, they should also give the m-geometry CD at a second temperature below T_N, to show it stays zero while the m'-geometry CD is finite.

C. Minor

8. Sign of the orbital moment (SI Sec. 3, main text p. 5). The magnetic moment of the J_eff = 1 states is computed with g = 5/2, using μ = -μ_B(L_eff + 2S). This does not apply the T-P equivalence (l_eff = 1 corresponds to a real orbital moment of about -L_eff), and it gives an orbital moment antiparallel to the spin. For high-spin d6 Fe2+ (more than half-filled), Hund's third rule and the established cases of FeO-like compounds give parallel spin and orbital moments and a moment above the spin-only value. Please recheck the sign and the resulting moment (about 3.5 μ_B rather than 2.5 μ_B). Also check the consistency of the "about 20% orbital contribution" statement, which in the SI is quoted for the Td site, with the main-text discussion of active orbital magnetism.

9. Intensity normalization across temperature. In Fig. S3h (80 K), all dd features are about 25 to 30% weaker than at 70 K. In Figs. S2 and 3, the shape of the feature A shoulder changes across T_N. Please state the normalization used across temperatures, and separate changes in total spectral weight from changes in CD.

10. Two different momentum transfers are used at 32 K: (0.05, 0, 0.65) in Fig. 2a and (0.03, 0, 0.66) in Fig. 3. Please explain why, and confirm that the CD is the same at both.

11. Statements in the main text are inconsistent with the SI. The main text says the SOC excitations of the Td site lie "well below 50 meV" and "do not yield significant CD". Fig. S6a shows levels up to about 45 meV at a Weiss field of 5 meV, and the Td simulations in Fig. S6 are not discussed at low energy. Please show the Td-site CD in the 0 to 0.15 eV window explicitly.

12. The lowest measured temperature is 32 K, where the order parameter is not saturated. Please state the sample-temperature calibration and any beam heating, given that the CD closes between 50 and 60 K.

13. Data availability "upon reasonable request" is weak for a claim that rests on small differences between spectra. Please deposit the RIXS spectra.

14. Abstract: "break symmetry under the combined operations of parity and time reversal" is loose for a polar, non-centrosymmetric compound. Please state the defining symmetry (no translation or inversion combined with T relating the sublattices).

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a figure with the normalized CD spectra (difference/sum, with error bars) for features A and B in the m' and m geometries, and the integrated CD against T overlaid on the sublattice magnetization from neutron diffraction.
2. Measure a CD map against incident photon energy across the L3 edge. Td-derived and Oh-derived dichroism should resonate at different incident energies (Figs. 1e and 1f), which would make the sublattice attribution direct rather than model-based.
3. Perform a domain-reversal experiment, for example magnetoelectric cooling with opposite E·H, or a second crystal with the opposite domain. It should flip the sign of the CD on both features together, which would tie the CD to L itself.
4. Reframe the conceptual claim. Either show a measurable distinction between "bi-altermagnetic" order and two-site altermagnetic order, or present the result mainly as sublattice-resolved RIXS-CD of an altermagnet, which the data support well.
