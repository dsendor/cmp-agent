Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports Fe L3-edge RIXS with circularly polarized incident X-rays on the polar magnet Fe2Mo3O8. Circular dichroism (CD) appears below T_N in the crystal-field (dd) and low-energy spin-orbital excitations when the scattering plane lies in an m' plane. It is absent above T_N and absent, within the stated accuracy, when the scattering plane lies in an m (glide) plane. Because the CD-carrying features can be assigned to the tetrahedral (feature B, ~0.45 eV) and octahedral (feature A, ~70 meV) Fe sites, the authors argue that both magnetic sublattices are individually altermagnetic ("bi-altermagnetism"). An ionic multiplet model with a staggered Weiss field reproduces the presence of the CD. Using site-selective excitations to read sublattice-resolved time-reversal symmetry breaking in a compensated magnet, where XMCD is forbidden, is an attractive idea with broad use for altermagnet research. The strength of the conclusion depends on how quantitatively the symmetry selection rules and the domain state have been established.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear symmetry-based experimental design: the m' versus m scattering-plane comparison is chosen so that CD is forbidden in the paramagnetic phase in both geometries and allowed in the ordered phase in only one. This turns the onset of CD into a symmetry test, not just a signature of magnetic order.
- Site selectivity: the Td e→t2 excitation (~0.45 eV) and the Oh t2g→eg excitation (~1 eV) are well separated in energy and incident-photon energy (Fig. 1e,f; Fig. S6). This allows CD to be attributed to individual sublattices, which is not possible with ARPES or transport.
- The temperature series (Fig. 3, Figs. S3, S4) shows the CD of features A and B disappearing between 50 and 60 K, consistent with the literature T_N.
- The model parameters (crystal fields 0.49 and 0.95 eV, J_H = 0.75 eV, ξ = 65 meV) were fixed from the RIXS line positions without using the CD (SI Sec. 4). This makes the reproduction of CD in the m' geometry and its absence in the m geometry a non-trivial check.
- The simulations of the competing ferrimagnetic state (Fig. S9) give a concrete selection-rule contrast (CD allowed in the m geometry for the ferrimagnet, forbidden for the altermagnet).
- Source data are deposited on Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The selection-rule test that underpins the main claim is not quantified, and the m-plane "null" is not clean in the data shown.
   - What is wrong: the paper states that the m-plane CD "vanishes within the present experimental accuracy" (p. 4) and that the m'/m contrast, "rather than the mere onset of CD below T_N, identifies the observed signal as a signature of bi-altermagnetism". No accuracy is ever given.
   - What the figures show: no difference spectra, no error bars, no integrated CD values. In the lower panel of Fig. S2 (m plane, 32 K), LCP and RCP differ visibly at the quasi-elastic peak (about 95 versus 88 counts). That difference is comparable to the m'-plane CD at feature A. There are also small differences around 0.5 eV. By contrast, the inset of Fig. 2a, described as the corresponding m-plane spectra, shows essentially overlapping curves. It is not clear whether the inset and Fig. S2 are the same data, or why they look different.
   - Why it matters: the ferrimagnetic state predicts a larger CD that is allowed in the m geometry (Fig. S9). The size of any residual m-plane CD therefore decides whether the data distinguish the bi-altermagnetic state from a ferrimagnet or a mixed state.
   - What to do:
     - Show I_RCP − I_LCP difference spectra with statistical error bars (counting statistics plus the spread over repeated acquisitions) for both geometries at 32 K and above T_N.
     - Report the integrated CD of features A and B, normalized to their summed intensity, with uncertainties, for m' and m.
     - Explain the quasi-elastic difference in the m geometry.
     - Plot the integrated CD versus temperature (all eight temperatures in Figs. S3, S4) against the sublattice magnetization or T_N of the same crystal. At present the link to the order parameter is made only by eye.

2. The magnetic domain state, and hence the sign and size of the CD, is not controlled or discussed.
   - What is missing: in 6'mm' the two time-reversed domains (L and −L) must give CD of opposite sign. The same point was made explicitly for MnTe in Ref. 40, which the authors co-authored.
   - Why it matters:
     - The observed CD magnitude depends on the unknown domain imbalance within the ~400 × 30 µm² beam spot (SI Sec. 2).
     - The sign agreement between experiment and simulation (Fig. 2a versus 2b,c) carries no information unless the domain is known.
     - Reproducibility across cool-downs has not been shown: Fig. 2a and Fig. 3a are both at 32 K, but at different Q and θ, and it is not stated whether they come from separate cool-downs or crystals.
   - What to do:
     - Report the number of cool-downs, crystals and beam positions measured, and whether the CD sign and magnitude were the same each time.
     - Fe2Mo3O8 has a large linear magnetoelectric effect (Refs. 9, 35, 36). Magnetoelectric field cooling (parallel E and H along c) should select one L domain. Showing that the CD reverses sign for the opposite field-cooling sign would directly tie the signal to the Néel vector. It would also exclude geometric or instrumental artifacts and give a fixed sign to compare with theory.
     - If this cannot be done, the sign comparison with theory should be removed and the possibility of domain averaging discussed.

3. Ferrimagnetic contamination of the probed volume is not excluded by characterization of the measured crystal.
   - What is missing: the sample is characterized only by powder XRD and Laue diffraction (SI Sec. 1). T_N = 60 K is taken from the literature. No magnetization, susceptibility or specific-heat data are shown for the crystal that was measured.
   - Why it matters:
     - Fe2Mo3O8 sits close to a ferrimagnetic phase. Small Zn substitution, off-stoichiometry or modest fields stabilize it (Refs. 35, 36).
     - Soft-X-ray RIXS probes the near-surface region (~100 nm) of an HCl-cleaned, ex-situ surface, where off-stoichiometry or strain could locally favour ferrimagnetism.
     - The authors' own simulations show that the ferrimagnetic CD is "significantly stronger" than the altermagnetic one (Fig. S9). A small ferrimagnetic volume fraction could therefore produce a sizable part of the m'-plane CD.
   - What to do:
     - Show M(T) and M(H) along c for the same crystal, which bound any net moment, and a T_N determination.
     - Measure XAS-XMCD at the Fe L edge in the same geometry and temperature. XMCD is forbidden for 6'mm' but allowed for the ferrimagnet, so this is a cheap, direct test of ferrimagnetic contamination at the relevant depth.
     - Combine this with the quantitative m-plane bound from concern 1 to put an upper limit on the ferrimagnetic fraction.

B. Should address

4. The assignment of feature A to Oh spin-orbital excitations, and the quoted 2:3 level spacing, are not supported quantitatively.
   - The 2:3 spacing does not follow from the fit: the main text (p. 5) says the two-component fit gives "an energy spacing [that] follows a 2:3 ratio (see Fig. S5)". The peaks in Fig. S5d are at ~31–39 meV and ~68–73 meV. The spacings between them (~33 and ~37 meV) are close to 1:1, not 2:3. The ratio is only near 2:3 if the first spacing is measured from the elastic line, which assumes the J_eff = 1 ground level is unsplit.
   - The SOC constant is used inconsistently: Fig. 4a uses ζ = 52 meV, giving levels at 24–26 and 58–65 meV, while the simulations use ξ = 65 meV (SI C2). With 65 meV, ζ/2 + 3ζ/4 ≈ 81 meV, above the observed ~70 meV.
   - The fit itself is weak: both "peaks" lie within, or are comparable to, the 45 meV resolution. They sit on an unresolved quasi-elastic line containing electromagnons (4–10 meV), magnons (11–14 meV) and phonons. The fit has a Voigt, two Lorentzians and a linear background, and the authors themselves call it a "constrained minimal decomposition" (SI Sec. 3.2).
   - Why it matters: chiral magnons in altermagnets also carry RIXS-CD (Refs. 26, 27, 45). The paper notes CD differences at the quasi-elastic peak (Fig. S3a,b). Without a firmer assignment, the attribution of the low-energy CD to localized Oh spin-orbital excitations, rather than to collective modes, remains uncertain.
   - What to do:
     - State the ζ used consistently.
     - Report fitted widths and parameter correlations.
     - Show the incident-energy dependence of the CD of feature A across the L3 edge (Fig. 4b,c are RCP only) and compare it with the impurity-model prediction. A collective mode and a local Oh multiplet should resonate differently.
     - Rephrase the 2:3 statement to match the numbers.

5. The theory comparison is qualitative, and the Weiss field and lifetime are free choices.
   - What is wrong:
     - The Weiss field is set to "3–5 meV, which appears to be a reasonable value" (SI p. 6) without connection to known exchange constants or T_N.
     - It is restricted to a spin-only form along c.
     - Γ = 0.3 eV is fixed. The CD in RIXS arises from the iΓ term (p. 3), so its magnitude should depend on Γ.
     - No CD magnitude or A:B ratio is compared between experiment and calculation.
   - An unexplained discrepancy: the Oh calculation (Fig. 2b; Figs. S7f–h, S9h) gives a visible CD at the ~1.0 eV feature C, which the experiment does not show. The authors attribute this to "large lifetime broadening" (p. 4) but give no calculation that demonstrates it.
   - What to do:
     - Show the calculated CD of A, B and C as a function of Weiss field and Γ.
     - Justify the Weiss-field scale from mean-field exchange (for example from neutron-derived J values, Ref. 47).
     - Overlay calculated and measured difference spectra on a common, broadened scale.
     - Show the incident-energy dependence of the CD in both theory and experiment.

6. The Td orbital moment and Van Vleck claim rests on an unconstrained model.
   - What is claimed: the statements that the Td moment is Van Vleck-type, that its orbital moment is "about 20% of the spin moment", and that Fe2Mo3O8 is "a rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism" (pp. 5–6) all come from the ionic model at a chosen Weiss field. In the SI, (S_c, L_c) varies from (1.29, 0.30) to (1.80, 0.35) between 1 and 5 meV.
   - Why it matters: no measurement in the paper constrains the orbital moment, and the CD itself was not fitted to obtain it.
   - What to do: compare the model's total Td and Oh moments with published neutron ordered moments. Either give independent support (for example, sum-rule analysis of linear or magnetic dichroism, if available) or present the orbital-moment statements as model predictions.

7. Only two scattering geometries were tested; the strongest symmetry test is missing.
   - What is missing: in 6'mm', a 60° azimuthal rotation about c maps one m' plane onto another via 6' (which contains T). The CD should therefore reverse sign under 60° rotation and be unchanged under 120° rotation. In the ferrimagnetic group (6m'm') no such reversal is expected.
   - Why it matters: this test is specific to altermagnetic order and largely insensitive to domain or normalization artifacts. It would strengthen the "symmetry-dependent response" argument far beyond a single m'/m pair.
   - What to do: measure the CD of features A and B at azimuths φ and φ + 60° (and ideally a full azimuthal scan), within a single domain region.
   - Related point: Fig. 2 (Q = (0.05,0,0.65), θ = 26.26°) and Fig. 3 (Q = (0.03,0,0.66), θ = 30°) use different geometries without comment. State how the CD depends on θ at fixed scattering plane.

8. It is unclear what observation distinguishes "bi-altermagnetism" from an ordinary altermagnet with two magnetic Wyckoff sites.
   - What is unclear: the global magnetic point group 6'mm' already constrains both sites. It is not explained what alternative magnetic state would give CD at only one site's excitations, or the same total m'/m contrast but a different sublattice distribution.
   - Why it matters: the novelty claim in the title and abstract relies on the sublattice decomposition.
   - What to do: spell out which alternative ordered states the site-resolved data exclude (for example, Td moments disordered, or Td moments parallel within the unit while Oh moments are antiparallel). Show, with the model, that these states would give a measurably different CD pattern at A and B in the two geometries.

9. Sample temperature and the CD temperature dependence are not established.
   - What is unclear: CD is already strongly reduced at 55 K (Figs. S3d, S4d), while small LCP/RCP differences remain at 60–70 K (Figs. S3e–g). Beam heating at 10^15 photons/s, and the thermometer position relative to the spot, are not discussed.
   - Why it matters: this affects whether the CD follows the order parameter.
   - What to do: state the thermometry and any heating check (for example, a flux-dependence test), and present the CD(T) curve requested in concern 1 against the crystal's own T_N.

C. Minor

10. Normalization.
    - LCP and RCP spectra are normalized to the 5–7 eV loss region (SI Sec. 3.1). In the ordered phase this region could itself carry dichroism through fluorescence.
    - In Fig. S4h (80 K) the dd intensities are ~30% lower than at all other temperatures, which suggests a change in normalization or sample position.
    - Please show raw I0-normalized spectra as well, and explain the 80 K data.

11. SI Sec. 5 derivation.
    - The SOC expectation value is computed for a single Slater determinant, mixing real orbital labels (xy, yz, zx) with an m_l basis.
    - The sign convention for l_eff in the t2g manifold (the T–P equivalence l_eff = −l) and the "ignore the e_g electrons" step are not justified.
    - The g = 5/2 moment estimate omits the orbital reduction factor.
    - The final ζ/2 and 3ζ/4 intervals agree with the standard result (λ = −ζ/4 for high-spin d⁶), so a citation (for example Abragam–Bleaney) plus a brief, correct derivation would suffice.

12. Sample and surface details. State how many crystals were measured, the surface preparation immediately before measurement (ex-situ HCl cleaning versus in-situ cleaving), the probing depth, and an XAS lineshape check that the surface is Fe²⁺ and not partly Fe³⁺.

13. Cross-references and labels.
    - p. 3 refers to "Fig. S6(a,e)" for the Td spin-orbital excitation lying below 50 meV, but that information is in Fig. S7a.
    - In Fig. 2a, the inset Q and temperature should be given.
    - In Fig. S6, the experimental panel's intensity axis runs right to left without a note.
    - In Fig. 1e/f, state the RCP versus LCP choice in the calculation.

14. Code availability "upon reasonable request" limits reproducibility of the key simulations. Please deposit the input parameters and the model Hamiltonians with the Zenodo data.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a single summary figure of the integrated CD (with error bars) of features A and B versus temperature for both scattering planes, overlaid with the sublattice magnetization or T_N of the measured crystal. Add the calculated CD for the altermagnetic and ferrimagnetic states.
2. Use magnetoelectric field cooling to prepare a single Néel domain and demonstrate CD sign reversal. Combine this with a 60° azimuthal rotation showing the sign change expected from 6'. Together these would turn the symmetry argument into a direct test.
3. Measure Fe L-edge XAS-XMCD on the same crystal and geometry as a null control against ferrimagnetism.
4. Map the CD as a function of incident photon energy across the L3 edge for features A and B, and compare it with the impurity model. This would strengthen the site assignment and help separate local multiplet CD from possible magnon contributions.
