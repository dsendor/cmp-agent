Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors measure circular dichroism (CD) in Fe L3-edge RIXS on the polar antiferromagnet Fe2Mo3O8 (magnetic point group 6'mm', Néel vector L parallel to c). They report CD in the m' scattering geometry below T_N = 60 K, both in a low-energy (~70 meV) spin-orbital feature attributed to the octahedral (Oh) Fe site and in the ~0.45 eV dd feature attributed to the tetrahedral (Td) Fe site. CD is absent above T_N and in the m-plane geometry. An ionic multiplet model with a staggered Weiss field reproduces the observation. The paper presents this as evidence for "bi-altermagnetism", meaning two independently altermagnetic sublattices. Using site-selective dd excitations to resolve the symmetry breaking on each magnetic sublattice is a new and useful way to apply RIXS-CD to compensated magnets. Fe2Mo3O8 is also a good test case because its T_N is low enough that the paramagnetic null can be measured. Earlier RIXS-CD work on MnTe, CrSb and α-Fe2O3 could not do this.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The symmetry logic is clear. The m' geometry forbids CD in the paramagnetic phase, independent of the time-reversal subtleties of RIXS. Measuring above T_N gives a built-in null, and the m-plane geometry gives a second, symmetry-based null in the ordered phase.
- The CD appears in spectrally separated features (A near 70 meV, B near 0.45 eV), and the multiplet analysis assigns these to different Fe sites. This gives a direct handle on the magnetic order of each sublattice.
- The temperature series (Fig. 3, Figs. S3 and S4, 32 to 80 K) shows CD in both features fading as T_N is approached. The m-plane comparison (Fig. 2a inset, Fig. S2) is a sensible control.
- The ionic model is constrained by the RIXS line positions alone, without using the CD (Supplement section 4, C1 to C3). Robustness checks are given against trigonal distortion (Fig. S8) and Weiss-field amplitude (Fig. S7). A ferrimagnetic comparison calculation (Fig. S9) makes the selection-rule argument concrete.
- The data are deposited on Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **Magnetic domain population is not discussed, and the sign of the CD depends on it.** In 6'mm' with L parallel to c, the two Néel domains (L and −L) are related by time reversal, so they should give CD of opposite sign in the m' geometry. An equal domain population over the beam footprint (about 386 µm × 30 µm, Supplement section 2) would cancel the net CD.
   - **What is missing:** a finite CD therefore means one domain is preferred, but the paper never says why. It does not say whether the sample was cooled in a field, in an electric field, or in zero field. It does not say whether the sign and magnitude of the CD repeat after warming above T_N and cooling again, or at other beam positions or on other crystals. The number of samples measured is not stated.
   - **Why it matters:** the whole argument rests on the presence of CD, and on its sign matching the calculation (Fig. 2b,c). This holds up only if the domain state is controlled or at least characterized. If the domain balance is set by chance on cooling, the magnitude of the CD, and the temperature dependence in Fig. 3, could reflect domain fractions rather than the order parameter.
   - **What to do:**
     - State the cooling protocol and the number of crystals and cooldowns.
     - Show repeat measurements of the 32 K m'-plane CD after thermal cycling through T_N and at two or more sample positions.
     - Discuss how the polar axis (±c) combines with the Néel domain to set the CD sign.
     - If possible, show that cooling in a field (or a magnetoelectric field combination) reverses or saturates the CD.
   - **On the sign comparison with theory:** quote the relative sign of the CD on features A and B. This sign does not depend on the domain, so comparing it with the model is a real test.

2. **The CD is never shown as a quantity with uncertainties, so the claims that it "vanishes" cannot be judged.** Every CD figure (Figs. 2, 3, S2, S3, S4) overlays RCP and LCP spectra. No difference spectrum I_RCP − I_LCP, no counting-statistics error bars, and no integrated CD against temperature are given.
   - **Where "within experimental uncertainty" fails to hold up:** the text says the CD vanishes "within the experimental uncertainty" above T_N and "within the present experimental accuracy" in the m geometry, but that uncertainty is never defined. In the lower panel of Fig. S2 (m plane, 32 K), the elastic/quasi-elastic peak visibly differs between LCP (about 95) and RCP (about 88). Small differences are also visible near 0.45 eV and 1.6 to 1.9 eV. These are comparable to the 0.45 eV CD in the m' geometry.
   - **Why it matters:** the m-plane null is the main evidence that separates the proposed state from a ferrimagnetic state (Fig. S9). That distinction is lost if the "null" contains differences of similar size to the signal.
   - **What to do:**
     - Add difference spectra with Poisson error bars for both geometries.
     - Add integrated CD of features A and B against T, with error bars, compared with an order-parameter form or with the sublattice moment from neutron data.
     - State the repeat-to-repeat scatter of the CD within one polarization, for example by measuring RCP, LCP, RCP in alternation.
     - Explain the elastic-line difference in the m geometry.

B. Should address

3. **How strongly the data separate "bi-altermagnetism" from the alternatives should be stated more carefully.**
   - **Ferrimagnetic alternative:** the main experimental discriminator is the m-plane null, but it was measured at only one temperature (32 K) and one Q (Fig. S2). A small ferrimagnetic fraction is a natural contaminant in Fe2Mo3O8, which sits close to a ferrimagnetic phase that field or doping stabilizes. It could give m-plane CD below the sensitivity reached here.
   - **What "bi-altermagnetism" means experimentally:** that each sublattice pair is altermagnetic is a symmetry property of the known collinear structure. The measurement shows that the Oh and Td sites both carry ordered, compensated moments that break the m' mirror. It is not clear which magnetic structure, consistent with 6'mm' and zero net moment, would *not* count as bi-altermagnetic yet would produce the same data.
   - **What to do:**
     - State explicitly which candidate orders are excluded, and by which measurement.
     - Measure the m-plane CD at several temperatures and give a quantitative upper bound.
     - If available, measure a ferrimagnetic reference (for example Zn-substituted (Fe,Zn)2Mo3O8, or the field-induced phase) under matched conditions. This would test the predicted change in selection rules (m-plane CD appearing; larger amplitude, Fig. S9).
   - **On the wording "identifies":** the main-text sentence that the symmetry-dependent response "identifies the observed signal as a signature of bi-altermagnetism" should be softened. The response is consistent with the 6'mm' magnetic point group, which also follows from earlier neutron results.

4. **The comparison with the model is qualitative, and the Weiss field is a free parameter.**
   - **Weiss field:** the field is set at 3 to 5 meV as "a reasonable value" (Supplement p. 6) without independent justification. Examples would be matching the known exchange constants, the magnon energies of 10 to 15 meV, or the ordered moments from neutron diffraction.
   - **Amplitudes and model choices:** the CD amplitude, especially of the Td Van Vleck moment (Sc going from 1.29 to 1.80 between 1 and 5 meV), depends on this field. It also depends on the choice to couple the field only to spin and only along c.
   - **Feature C:** in Fig. 2b the Oh calculation gives visible CD on the 1.0 eV feature C, which is absent in the data. This is attributed to "large lifetime broadening" and to wave-function character, but the model does not include that broadening.
   - **What to do:**
     - Give the measured and calculated CD ratios, (I_R − I_L)/(I_R + I_L) for A, B and C, side by side.
     - Fix or bound the Weiss field from independent data.
     - Compare the calculated ordered moments on Oh and Td with the neutron values.
     - Show the calculated CD at C with a realistic final-state broadening, to test the lifetime explanation.

5. **The incident-energy dependence of the CD is not shown, although the site assignment relies on resonance energy.** The text argues that Td and Oh features resonate at different incident energies (Fig. 1e,f), yet all CD data are taken at a single incident energy of 706 eV.
   - **What to do:** show a CD map against incident energy (RCP − LCP across the L3 edge), compared with the calculated CD maps for each site. This would strongly support the "sublattice-specific" claim. It would also rule out CD from a nominal Td feature that actually carries Oh weight, or the reverse.

6. **The assignment and fitting of the low-energy feature A contain internal inconsistencies.**
   - **The 2:3 ratio:** the main text says the fitted spacing "follows a 2:3 ratio (see Fig. S5)", consistent with ζ/2 and 3ζ/4. But the fitted peaks in Fig. S5d are at about 31 to 38 meV and 68 to 73 meV. That makes the successive spacings about 35 and 35 meV (roughly 1:1), not 2:3. Read as absolute positions, the ratio is about 1:2, also not 2:3. By contrast, the 2:3 spacing in Fig. 4a puts the levels near 25 and 62 meV.
   - **Spin-orbit coupling constant:** Fig. 4a uses ζ = 52 meV, while the RIXS-CD simulation uses ξ = 65 meV (Supplement C2). With 65 meV the first-order Jeff levels would sit near 33 and 81 meV.
   - **Fit quality:** the (2+1)-component fit has a free linear background and free Lorentzian widths, at 45 meV resolution. The authors themselves describe it as a "constrained minimal decomposition", so it can hardly decide between level schemes.
   - **Trigonal distortion and dispersion:** the trigonal distortion (Δtri = ±30 meV) is comparable to the spin-orbit splitting, so the Jeff picture is only approximate. Dispersion was measured only along (0,0,L) (Fig. S5), so the claim of small dispersion, used to exclude collective modes, does not cover in-plane momentum.
   - **What to do:**
     - Correct or explain the 2:3 statement and reconcile ζ = 52 meV with ξ = 65 meV.
     - Give the fit parameters and which ones were fixed.
     - Either measure in-plane dispersion or limit the claim.
     - Address whether magnons or electromagnons (10 to 15 meV, 4 to 10 meV) could contribute CD to the shoulder through the resolution tail.

7. **The sample is not characterized magnetically.** Supplement section 1 gives XRD and Laue only. No magnetization or susceptibility data are given to confirm T_N = 60 K and the absence of a ferrimagnetic component in this crystal. The paper also does not show a measured null XMCD at the Fe L edge at 32 K, which would be the most direct check, by the same technique, of zero net magnetization over the probed volume.
   - **What to do:** add M(T) under low field and an XMCD null measured in the RIXS geometry (or explain why one was not taken).

8. **The temperature normalization and the polarization normalization need justification.**
   - **Normalization method:** spectra are normalized to the 5 to 7 eV loss region (Supplement section 3.1), and the LCP/RCP flux ratio is 1.1.
   - **Intensity changes:** the overall intensity changes noticeably between temperatures. For example, the elastic peak is about 80 at 50 K (Fig. S3c) against about 95 at other temperatures, and the 0.45 eV peak is about 15 at 80 K (Fig. S4h) against about 20 to 22 elsewhere.
   - **What to do:**
     - Show that the 5 to 7 eV region is free of polarization-dependent or temperature-dependent spectral weight, such as charge-transfer or fluorescence tails.
     - Explain the intensity changes between temperatures.
     - Confirm that the CD is unchanged if the spectra are normalized to the dd intensity or to I0 instead.

9. **The claim of active orbital magnetism goes beyond the data.** The Discussion says Fe2Mo3O8 is a "rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism", with an orbital moment of about 20% of the spin moment. That number comes from the ionic model with a Weiss field acting only on spin, set to an assumed value. It is not measured.
   - **What to do:** either support the claim with a measurement, for example XMCD sum rules in the field-induced ferrimagnetic phase or comparison with a published g-tensor or neutron form factor, or present it as a model prediction.

C. Minor

10. **Different Q values without comment.** Fig. 2 uses Q = (0.05, 0, 0.65) with θ = 26.26°, and Fig. 3 uses Q = (0.03, 0, 0.66) with θ = 30°. The 32 K spectra in Figs. 2a and 3a,e are therefore different measurements. State this and comment on any Q dependence of the CD.

11. **Glide-plane geometry not explained.** The m planes are glide planes. Explain briefly why the translation part does not affect the CD selection rule for local dd excitations at finite Q. Also clarify how the m-plane geometry (described as the "ac plane") was reached, for example by an azimuthal rotation, and how accurately the scattering plane was aligned to each mirror plane.

12. **Ambiguous XMCD phrasing.** The text says "three mirror (glide) planes m that also prohibit the anomalous Hall effect". Please state which operations forbid the net moment and XMCD in 6'mm', so the reader does not have to infer it.

13. **No counting details in Methods.** Give the counting time, the number of repeats per polarization, and the order in which the polarizations were acquired. These matter for judging drift as a source of CD.

14. **Incomplete sentence in the Supplement.** In Supplement section 5, "or the eg level, both orbitals are filled..." is a fragment. It should also say whether the single-determinant estimate leading to ζ' = ζ/4 is consistent with the multiplet results used in Fig. 4a.

15. **Code availability.** The multiplet code is available "upon reasonable request". Given how much the conclusions rest on the model, please deposit the input parameters and Hamiltonians with the Zenodo data.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Show a CD-reversal experiment. Either cool through T_N under conditions that select the opposite Néel domain and show the CD on both A and B change sign together, or map the CD sign across the sample surface. This would settle the domain issue and show that the CD is magnetic.
2. Present one summary figure: integrated CD of A and B against temperature with error bars, overlaid on the neutron sublattice order parameter, with the m-plane upper bound on the same axes.
3. Measure the CD at symmetry-related azimuths. A rotation of 120° about c should give identical CD. Rotations involving the primed 6-fold (6_3 combined with time reversal) give a further prediction. Also measure at grazing incidence against grazing emission. These would test the 6'mm' selection rules beyond a single geometry.
4. Add a short paragraph on what new physics "bi-altermagnetism" predicts, for example separate spin-split bands or anomalous responses from each sublattice. That would give the terminology a role beyond a classification label.
