## A. Must address

1. **The CD and the "null" results are never quantified, and the m-plane null is not clean in the data shown.** The paper says the CD "vanished within the experimental uncertainty" above T_N (p. 4) and is absent "within the present experimental accuracy" in the m geometry. It argues that the m'/m contrast, "rather than the mere onset of CD below T_N, identifies the observed signal as a signature of bi-altermagnetism". No uncertainty is ever defined. Every CD figure (Figs. 2, 3, S2, S3, S4) only overlays RCP and LCP spectra. There are no I_RCP − I_LCP difference spectra, no counting-statistics error bars, no repeat scans and no integrated CD values. The panels themselves raise doubts. In the lower panel of Fig. S2 (m plane, 32 K), the elastic/quasi-elastic peak differs by about 8% between LCP (about 95 counts) and RCP (about 88). Visible RCP/LCP differences also appear near 0.45 to 0.55 eV and at 1.6 to 2.0 eV. These differences are comparable to the m'-plane CD on feature A (Fig. 2a, Fig. 3a) and on the 0.45 eV feature. By contrast, the inset of Fig. 2a, described as the corresponding m-plane spectrum, shows essentially overlapping curves. It is not clear whether the inset and Fig. S2 are the same data or were processed differently. The m-plane null is also shown only at 32 K and at one Q, and that Q is not given. This matters because the m-plane null is the main evidence separating the proposed state from a ferrimagnetic state. In Fig. S9 the ferrimagnetic state gives a larger CD that is allowed in the m geometry. Without a margin between "allowed" and "forbidden", the data do not discriminate. The link between the CD and the order parameter is also made only by eye. The authors should:
   - show I_RCP − I_LCP difference spectra with Poisson error bars, plus the spread over repeated acquisitions (for example alternating RCP, LCP, RCP), for both geometries at the same Q and temperature, at 32 K and above T_N;
   - report the integrated CD, or the asymmetry (I_R − I_L)/(I_R + I_L), of features A and B with uncertainties for both m' and m;
   - measure the m-plane CD at several temperatures and give a quantitative upper bound;
   - plot the integrated CD at all eight temperatures (Figs. S3, S4) against an order-parameter form, the neutron sublattice moment, or the T_N of the same crystal;
   - explain the quasi-elastic difference in the m geometry and the apparent difference between the Fig. 2a inset and Fig. S2;
   - give the Q of the m-plane data.

2. **The Néel-domain state is not controlled or characterized, and no test shows that the CD is odd in the order parameter.** In 6'mm' with L parallel to c, the two Néel domains (L and −L) are related by time reversal, equivalently by the primed 6-fold operation. They must therefore give CD of opposite sign in the m' geometry. The same point was made explicitly for MnTe in Ref. 40. An equal domain population within the roughly 386 × 30 µm² beam footprint (SI Sec. 2) would cancel the CD. A finite CD in Figs. 2a and 3 therefore requires a domain imbalance, but the paper never says what sets it. It does not give the cooling protocol: zero field, magnetic field or magnetoelectric field. It does not say whether the CD sign and magnitude repeat after thermal cycling through T_N, at other beam positions or on other crystals, or how many crystals and cool-downs were used. All data appear to come from one crystal (Fig. S1). Figs. 2a and 3a are both at 32 K but at different Q and θ, and it is not stated whether they come from separate cool-downs. Reaching the m and m' geometries requires an azimuthal rotation, so the two geometries may probe different spots with different domain populations. This matters in three ways. First, the sign agreement between experiment and simulation (Fig. 2a versus 2b,c) carries no information unless the domain is known. Second, the CD magnitude and its temperature dependence (Fig. 3) could reflect domain fractions rather than the order parameter. Third, without a reversal there is no direct proof that the dichroism is magnetic rather than instrumental. The authors should:
   - state the cooling protocol, the number of crystals, cool-downs and beam positions, and whether the CD sign and size were the same each time;
   - repeat the 32 K m'-plane measurement after several thermal cycles and at two or more sample positions, and ideally show the key m'/m contrast on a second crystal or mounting;
   - discuss how the polar axis (±c) combines with the Néel domain to set the CD sign;
   - ideally select a single domain by magnetoelectric field cooling (parallel E and H along c, below the metamagnetic field; Fe2Mo3O8 has a large linear magnetoelectric effect, Refs. 9, 35, 36), and show that the CD on A and B reverses sign together when the field-cooling sign is reversed;
   - quote the relative sign of the CD on features A and B and compare it with the model, which ties it to the antiparallel Oh and Td moments. This comparison does not depend on the domain;
   - if domain control is not possible, remove the absolute-sign comparison with theory and discuss domain averaging.

   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

3. **Ferrimagnetic contamination of the probed volume is not excluded.** Fe2Mo3O8 sits close to a ferrimagnetic phase that small Zn substitution, off-stoichiometry or modest fields stabilize (Refs. 35, 36). Soft-X-ray RIXS probes only about the top 100 nm of an ex-situ, HCl-cleaned surface, where off-stoichiometry or strain could locally favour ferrimagnetism. The authors' own simulations show that the ferrimagnetic CD is "significantly stronger" than the altermagnetic one (Fig. S9). A small ferrimagnetic volume fraction could therefore produce a sizable part of the m'-plane CD, and an m-plane CD below the sensitivity reached here. Yet the measured crystal is characterized only by powder XRD and Laue diffraction (SI Sec. 1). T_N = 60 K is taken from the literature, and no magnetization, susceptibility or specific-heat data are shown for this crystal. The Fig. S9 comparison also uses a single 3 meV Weiss field, so the claim that the ferrimagnetic CD is "significantly stronger" depends on that choice. The authors should:
   - show M(T) under low field and M(H) along c for the same crystal, which bound any net moment and fix its T_N;
   - measure Fe L-edge XAS-XMCD (for example fluorescence yield with both helicities) in the RIXS geometry at 32 K. XMCD is forbidden for 6'mm' but allowed for the ferrimagnet, so this is a cheap, direct test at the relevant depth;
   - combine this with the quantitative m-plane bound from concern 1 to put an upper limit on the ferrimagnetic fraction;
   - show that the m-geometry CD expected for a ferrimagnetic state lies above that bound for every Weiss field in the plausible range;
   - if available, measure a ferrimagnetic reference, such as Zn-substituted (Fe,Zn)2Mo3O8 or the field-induced phase, under matched conditions, to test the predicted appearance of m-plane CD.

   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

4. **The evidence for symmetry breaking on the Oh sublattice rests on feature A, the least clean feature, while the clean Oh feature C does not show the CD the model predicts.** Feature A sits at about 70 meV, about 1.5 resolution widths from the elastic line at 45 meV resolution. That region also contains the elastic line, electromagnons (4 to 10 meV), magnons (11 to 14 meV) and phonons (SI Sec. 3.2). It also contains the Td spin-orbital levels, which Fig. S7a places at about 5 to 40 meV for Weiss fields of 3 to 5 meV. The fitted peak_1 in Fig. S5 lies at 30 to 38 meV. Chiral magnons in altermagnets also carry RIXS-CD (Refs. 26, 27, 45), and the paper notes CD differences at the quasi-elastic peak (Fig. S3a,b). The argument that collective modes are "unlikely" (p. 5) rests on RCP-only fits along (0,0,L) with error bars of about ±10 meV (Fig. S5d). The CD itself was measured at in-plane momentum components of only 0.03 to 0.05 r.l.u., so in-plane dispersion has not been tested. Meanwhile, the one well-separated Oh excitation, feature C at about 1 eV, shows no measurable CD (p. 4). The model predicts clear CD there (Fig. 2b; Figs. S7f–h, S9h). The explanation offered, "large lifetime broadening and the character of the corresponding wave functions", is not demonstrated by any calculation. The paper therefore has one ambiguous Oh feature where the model agrees with the data and one clean Oh feature where it does not. This undercuts the claim that both sublattices are seen to break the symmetry. The authors should:
   - fit the RCP and LCP spectra of feature A with the same constrained decomposition as in Fig. S5, with the elastic line fixed from the carbon-tape reference, and show which component carries the CD;
   - show the model's Td contribution to the CD in the 0 to 100 meV window, so that a Td origin of the CD in the A region can be excluded;
   - address whether magnons or electromagnons could contribute CD to the shoulder through the resolution tail;
   - either measure the dispersion and CD at larger in-plane Q or soften the statement about collective modes;
   - recompute the Oh CD at feature C with a final-state broadening matched to the measured width of C, and show whether the predicted CD falls below the noise.

   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

## B. Should address

5. **"Bi-altermagnetism" needs an operational definition, and it is unclear what the RIXS data add beyond the known magnetic structure.** The m/m' selection rules are properties of the global magnetic point group 6'mm'. They would hold for any compensated order with this group, and the group already constrains both Wyckoff sites. In a non-centrosymmetric crystal, a compensated pair of moments related by a rotation or screw (here 6_3) cannot be related by inversion. Each compensated Wyckoff orbit is therefore altermagnetic by construction. "Bi-altermagnetism" thus seems to follow from the neutron magnetic structure and the space group, not from the RIXS measurement. In addition, the authors' own model makes the Td moment a Van Vleck moment induced entirely by the exchange (Weiss) field (p. 5, SI p. 6). It is then unclear in what sense the Td sublattice is "independently" altermagnetic. The novelty claim in the title and abstract relies on this sublattice decomposition. The authors should:
   - define bi-altermagnetism in spin-group terms;
   - state explicitly which alternative ordered states the site-resolved data exclude, and by which measurement. Examples are Td moments disordered, Td moments parallel within the unit while Oh moments are antiparallel, or only one sublattice compensated by a rotation;
   - show with the model that these states would give a measurably different CD pattern at A and B in the two geometries;
   - soften "identifies the observed signal as a signature of bi-altermagnetism" and "the realization of bi-altermagnetism". The response is consistent with 6'mm', which also follows from earlier neutron results. The defensible claim is site-resolved detection of time-reversal symmetry breaking at both Fe sites;
   - optionally, add a short paragraph on what new physics bi-altermagnetism predicts, for example separate spin-split bands or anomalous responses from each sublattice, so that the term does more than classify.

   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

6. **Polarization-switching artifacts are not bounded.** The CD is obtained by switching the EPU between helicities. The paper quotes a phase error of about 3.5°, a polarization degree above 98% and an LCP/RCP flux ratio of 1.1, which is normalized away. Two artifacts could produce a temperature-dependent "CD". The first is a residual linear component that differs between the two modes, multiplied by magnetic linear dichroism. Magnetic linear dichroism switches on at T_N and could be strong in this hexagonal polar crystal. The second is a change in beam position or footprint between the modes. The paramagnetic null does not exclude the first, because linear dichroism is also absent above T_N. The authors should:
   - measure LH and LV RIXS at 32 K and above T_N in the m' geometry, and estimate the leakage into RCP − LCP;
   - record XAS with both helicities in the same geometry at 32 K. A vanishing XMCD would validate the helicity switching, as well as serve the ferrimagnetism test in concern 3.

   Raised by: r3

7. **The comparison with the Weiss-field model is qualitative, and its key parameters are free choices.** Page 5 says "the measured CD is quantitatively reproduced by an atomic (impurity) model", but the comparison is only qualitative:
   - The Weiss field is set to "3–5 meV, which appears to be a reasonable value" (SI p. 6), with no link to known exchange constants, magnon energies (10 to 15 meV), ordered moments or T_N. The value actually used in Figs. 1f, 2b and 2c is not stated.
   - The field is restricted to a spin-only form along c, although the authors note that it could be more general (SI p. 5).
   - The core-hole broadening Γ = 0.3 eV is fixed without justification. The CD in RIXS arises from the iΓ term (p. 3), so its magnitude should depend on Γ.
   - The CD amplitude depends on the Weiss field (Fig. S7). For the Td site, S_c changes from 1.29 to 1.80 between 1 and 5 meV.
   - No CD magnitude, A:B ratio or overlay of calculated and measured difference spectra is given.
   - The final-state broadening and the incident energy used in the simulations are not stated.

   The authors should:
   - state all of these parameters and the Weiss field used in each figure;
   - fix or bound the Weiss field independently, for example from mean-field exchange consistent with T_N = 60 K or from neutron-derived J values (Ref. 47);
   - show the calculated CD of A, B and C as a function of Weiss field and Γ;
   - compare the calculated Oh and Td ordered moments with the neutron values;
   - give the measured and calculated asymmetries for A, B and C side by side, and overlay the difference spectra on a common, broadened scale;
   - otherwise replace "quantitatively" with "qualitatively".

   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **The incident-energy dependence of the CD is not shown, although the site assignment relies on resonance energy.** The text argues that the Td and Oh features resonate at different incident energies (p. 3; Fig. 1e,f; Fig. S6). Yet all CD data are taken at a single incident energy of 706 eV, and Fig. 4b,c are RCP only. A CD map against incident energy and energy loss would give site selectivity that does not depend on the energy-loss assignment. It would rule out CD from a nominal Td feature that actually carries Oh weight, or the reverse. It would also help separate local multiplet CD from collective modes, which should resonate differently. The authors should measure RCP − LCP across the L3 edge for features A and B, an RCP/LCP version of Fig. 1e and Fig. 4c, and compare it with the calculated CD maps for each site.

   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

9. **Only two scattering geometries were tested; the strongest symmetry test is missing.** In 6'mm', a 60° azimuthal rotation about c maps one m' plane onto another through the primed 6-fold operation, which contains time reversal. The CD should therefore reverse sign under a 60° rotation and be unchanged under 120°. No such reversal is expected in the ferrimagnetic group (6m'm'). This test is specific to altermagnetic order and largely insensitive to normalization artifacts. It would strengthen the "symmetry-dependent response" argument far beyond a single m'/m pair, and it also constrains the domain population. The authors should measure the CD of features A and B at azimuths φ and φ + 60°, within a single domain region, and compare with the model's prediction of reversal or invariance. Ideally they should also measure φ + 120°, a full azimuthal pattern between neighbouring m' and m planes, and grazing incidence against grazing emission.

   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

10. **The normalization, and the intensity changes between temperatures, need justification.** LCP and RCP spectra are normalized to the 5 to 7 eV loss region (SI Sec. 3.1). In the ordered phase this region could itself carry dichroism, or temperature-dependent weight such as charge-transfer or fluorescence tails. The overall intensity also changes noticeably between temperatures. The elastic peak is about 80 at 50 K (Fig. S3c) against about 90 to 97 elsewhere. The 0.45 eV feature B drops from about 21 at 70 K (Fig. S4g) to about 15 at 80 K (Fig. S4h), about 30% below the other temperatures. This suggests changes in normalization, alignment or sample position larger than the CD itself. The authors should:
    - show that the 5 to 7 eV window carries no polarization- or temperature-dependent signal;
    - show raw I0-normalized spectra;
    - confirm that the CD is unchanged when the spectra are normalized to the dd intensity or to I0;
    - explain the intensity changes, in particular the 80 K data.

    Raised by: r1, r2, r3

11. **The sample temperature, and hence the CD temperature dependence, is not established.** The CD is already strongly reduced at 55 K (Figs. S3d, S4d), while small LCP/RCP differences remain at 60 to 70 K (Figs. S3e–g). The sample is an insulator mounted with carbon tape on a Cu plate under about 10^15 photons/s. Beam heating and the thermometer position relative to the spot are not discussed. This affects whether the CD follows the order parameter and the comparison with T_N. The authors should state the thermometry, estimate the sample temperature under the beam (for example with a flux-dependence test), and present the CD(T) curve from concern 1 against the crystal's own T_N.

    Raised by: r1, r3

12. **The spin-orbital assignment of feature A and the quoted 2:3 level spacing do not match the numbers.** The main text (p. 5) says the two-component fit gives "an energy spacing [that] follows a 2:3 ratio (see Fig. S5)", consistent with ζ/2 and 3ζ/4. The fitted peaks in Fig. S5d are at about 31 to 39 meV and 68 to 73 meV. The successive spacings, ground state to peak_1 and then peak_1 to peak_2, are about 34 and 36 meV. That is close to 1:1, not 2:3, and read as absolute positions the ratio is about 1:2. The ratio approaches 2:3 only if the J_eff = 1 ground level is assumed to be unsplit. The spin-orbit constant is also used inconsistently. The Fig. 4a caption uses ζ = 52 meV, with quintet levels at 23.7 to 25.6 meV and septet levels at 58 to 65 meV, neither of which matches 35 and 70 meV. The simulations use ξ = 65 meV (SI C2), which gives first-order levels near 33 and 81 meV, above the observed ~70 meV. The trigonal distortion (Δ_tri = ±30 meV, Fig. S8a) is comparable to the spin-orbit splitting, so the J_eff picture is only approximate. The fit uses a Voigt, two Lorentzians with free widths and a free linear background, all within about the 45 meV resolution. The authors themselves call it a "constrained minimal decomposition" (SI Sec. 3.2), so it can hardly decide between level schemes. The authors should:
    - correct or rephrase the 2:3 statement to match the fitted energies, including the effect of the Weiss field and trigonal distortion on the J_eff levels;
    - state which ζ applies where, and reconcile 52 and 65 meV;
    - report the fit parameters, which ones were fixed, their widths and correlations, or compare models with one, two and three components.

    Raised by: r1, r2, r3

13. **The claims of a Van Vleck Td moment and "active orbital magnetism" rest on the model alone.** Three statements come from the ionic model at an assumed, spin-only Weiss field: that the Td moment is Van Vleck-type, that its orbital moment is "about 20% of the spin moment", and that Fe2Mo3O8 is "a rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism" (pp. 5–6, end of Results). In the SI, (S_c, L_c) varies from (1.29, 0.30) to (1.80, 0.35) between 1 and 5 meV. No measurement in the paper constrains the orbital moment, and the CD was not fitted to obtain it. The authors should either give independent support or present these statements as model predictions. Possible support includes XMCD sum rules in the field-induced ferrimagnetic phase, published g-tensor or neutron form-factor data, and comparison of the model's total Td and Oh moments with published neutron ordered moments.

14. **Different Q and θ in Figs. 2 and 3 without comment.** Fig. 2 uses Q = (0.05, 0, 0.65) with θ = 26.26°, and Fig. 3 uses Q = (0.03, 0, 0.66) with θ = 30°. The 32 K spectra in Figs. 2a and 3a,e are therefore different measurements. The authors should state this, comment on any Q or θ dependence of the CD at a fixed scattering plane, and give the Q and temperature of the Fig. 2a inset.

15. **The glide-plane selection rule and the geometry alignment need explanation.** In the m geometry the relevant symmetry is a glide with a c/2 translation (Fig. 1b, p. 4). The statement that it forbids CD at finite Q_c = 0.65 relies on the RIXS signal being an incoherent sum of local site contributions. The authors should:
    - state this assumption explicitly, and note whether it holds for any coherent (dispersive) contribution within feature A;
    - explain how the m-plane ("ac plane") geometry was reached, for example by azimuthal rotation;
    - say how accurately the scattering plane was aligned to each mirror plane.

18. **The XMCD phrasing is ambiguous.** The text says "three mirror (glide) planes m that also prohibit the anomalous Hall effect". The authors should state which operations of 6'mm' forbid the net moment and XMCD, so the reader does not have to infer it.

    Raised by: r2

19. **The derivation in SI Sec. 5 is heuristic and partly inconsistent.** The problems are:
    - The SOC expectation value is computed for a single Slater determinant and mixes real orbital labels (xy, yz, zx) with an m_l basis. The configuration is written as xy-down, but the down electron is then labelled m_l = −1.
    - The e_g electrons are ignored without justification.
    - The sign change of l on projection onto the t2g manifold (T–P equivalence, l_eff = −l) is not included.
    - "or the eg level, both orbitals are filled..." is a sentence fragment.
    - The g = 5/2 moment estimate, attributed to an "anti-parallel" orbital moment, omits the orbital reduction factor and is doubtful. For Fe²⁺ the real orbital moment is expected to be parallel to the spin.

    The final ζ/2 and 3ζ/4 intervals agree with the standard result (λ = −ζ/4 for high-spin d⁶). A citation such as Abragam and Bleaney, a brief correct derivation, and a check that ζ' = ζ/4 is consistent with the multiplet results in Fig. 4a would suffice.
