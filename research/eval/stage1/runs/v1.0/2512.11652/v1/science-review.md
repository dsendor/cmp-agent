A. Must address

1. **The claim that the nuclear spin is driven "irrespective of hybridization" is not supported, and conflicts with standard ENDOR physics and with the paper's own supplementary statements.** The Abstract ("the nuclear spin can be driven efficiently irrespective of its hybridization with the electron spin"), the Introduction ("we unambiguously demonstrate NMR driving independent of electron-nuclear spin hybridization") and the Conclusion ("hybridization ... is not necessary to drive NMR") all rest on ENDOR features persisting from 200 to 1400 mT. At these fields the flip-flop admixture |⟨↓,−3/2|ψ⟩|² is small, about 10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵ (SFig. 1b). But small is not zero. The transverse hyperfine terms still mix electron character into each nuclear state with amplitude of order A_⊥/(2 f_ESR), roughly 10⁻³–10⁻² for A_⊥ of tens of MHz and f_ESR = 2–11.5 GHz. The electron moment exceeds the 47Ti nuclear moment by about g_e μ_B/(g_N μ_N) ~ 10³–10⁴ (with g_e,z ≈ 0.56 and g_N ≈ 0.315). Any transverse oscillating field, or exchange/tip-field modulation, acting on the electron therefore gives a hyperfine-enhanced Δm_I = ±1, Δm_s = 0 matrix element of order (g_e μ_B/g_N μ_N)(A/h f_ESR) g_N μ_N B1. That is plausibly 10–100 times larger than direct coupling to the bare nucleus across the whole field range, and it falls as about 1/B, the same scaling as the hybridization coefficient in SFig. 1b. There is also an internal inconsistency. Supplementary Note 1 states that the tip field is mostly exchange and "cannot couple directly with the nuclear spin". Yet the Discussion proposes an "effective oscillating magnetic field" from the ESR-STM mechanisms (piezoelectric displacement in the tip field, barrier modulation, spin torque), all of which act on the electron spin. By the authors' own reasoning, such a drive reaches the nucleus only through the hyperfine admixture, which is the hybridization the paper says is unnecessary. The contrast with Fig. 1d does not settle this. The single-tone signal vanishing at 200 mT reflects the readout requirement (Δm_s ≠ 0), not the drive efficiency, so the ENDOR features show that readout no longer needs hybridization, not that the drive matrix element is independent of it. Nor does the paper show that drive efficiency is independent of B: the ENDOR amplitude changes non-monotonically with field, and this is attributed to V_ESR transmission (Supplementary Note 2), which leaves open whether the NMR Rabi rate falls with B as hyperfine-mediated driving predicts. "Driven efficiently" is likewise not backed by any measure of drive strength (Rabi rate, saturation power), and the statement that the equalized first two ESR peaks in Fig. 1e "imply that we drive the nuclear spin sufficiently to saturate" comes from one spectrum. What the authors should do: (i) Compute, from the eigenstates of Eq. 1 including the anisotropic A (A_⊥), the tip-field tilt (φ ≈ 5°) and the measured g-tensor, the Δm_I = ±1 transition matrix elements (Rabi rates) as a function of B for each candidate drive: a transverse field on the bare nucleus only; a transverse field on electron and nucleus together; modulation of B_tip/exchange on the electron; modulation of the anisotropic hyperfine terms; and modulation of Q. Give the predicted field dependence and m_s dependence for each. (ii) Measure the drive strength independently of the V_ESR readout calibration: the V_NMR dependence of the ENDOR dip depth and linewidth (saturation parameter, power broadening) for transitions I and II at two or three widely separated fields with matched V_ESR (for example 200 and 450 mT, where V_ESR = 20 mV is available at both, or 300 mT and 1.07 T with V_ESR set to the same lower value), or normalize each ENDOR dip to the corresponding ESR peak height in SFig. 4a. Ideally also measure below 200 mT, where the admixture is large, with the same tip. (iii) Compare the intensities of the ↑ and ↓ transitions (I vs II, III vs IV), which have opposite hyperfine-enhancement factors, with the predictions. If the drive proves to be hyperfine-enhanced, the result still stands, but the headline claim should become what is shown, for example "driving and readout do not rely on electron-nuclear flip-flop transitions (Δm_s ≠ 0)" or "readout independent of hybridization, with drive persisting up to 1.4 T". The Abstract, Introduction and Conclusion should be worded consistently with what is measured.

B. Should address

2. **The arguments that eliminate hyperfine and quadrupole modulation as drive mechanisms are incomplete, so the conclusion that the drive is "dominantly an effective oscillating magnetic field" is not supported (Discussion; Supplementary Note 3; Fig. 2b; SFig. 8).** The Discussion argues that modulating the hyperfine interaction drives only flip-flop transitions at GHz frequencies. That holds only for the isotropic or S_x I_x + S_y I_y part. With an anisotropic or tilted hyperfine tensor, and with the electron quantization axis tilted by the tip field (φ ≈ 5°, Supplementary Note 1), modulation produces off-diagonal terms such as A_zx S_z I_x, which drive Δm_s = 0, Δm_I = ±1 transitions at exactly the observed MHz frequencies, with an m_s-dependent sign. This is the hyperfine-Stark mechanism of electrically driven NMR in donors (refs. 16, 17) and single-molecule magnets (ref. 7), and a tip-induced change of the electric field or a piezoelectric displacement of the atom could modulate exactly these terms. Quadrupole modulation is dismissed on two grounds, neither decisive. (i) The absence of Δm_I = ±2 lines in SFig. 8: this is a single sweep with noise of about ±3 fA, in which even transitions I and III are barely above the noise; moreover, the relative strengths of the Δm_I = ±1 terms ({I_z, I_x}) and Δm_I = ±2 terms (I_x² − I_y²) depend on which electric-field-gradient components are modulated, and the expected ±2 amplitude is never estimated. (ii) A "slight signal" at the −1/2 ↔ +1/2 transitions V/VI in Fig. 2b (f_ESR ≈ 4.0–4.1 GHz), which the text says only "may be discerned" and then uses in both directions in the argument; its significance is not quantified. The authors should write the modulated terms explicitly, with selection rules and expected relative intensities for transitions I–VI and for the Δm_I = ±2 lines; state the amplitude ratio needed to see Δm_I = ±2 at the SNR of SFig. 8, and repeat the wide-range sweep with enough averaging that the upper bound is meaningful relative to the I/III amplitude; show line cuts and Lorentzian fits with significance for V/VI, ideally from a dedicated measurement with f_ESR on the m_I = −1/2 peak, or else treat V/VI as not resolved; and use the m_s dependence of the line amplitudes (I/III against II/IV) as a discriminant. Without a quantitative discrimination, the mechanism should be presented as an open question, and the sentence claiming applicability to other nuclear spin systems "not dependent on A or Q" should be removed.

6. **Reading the I/II amplitude ratio as the electron-spin population ratio in Fig. 4 needs justification.** P↑/P↓ is taken from the normalized dip amplitudes of transitions I and II, which equals the m_s population ratio only if the two transitions are driven equally strongly, saturated to the same degree and read out with equal efficiency. If the drive is hyperfine-enhanced (concern 1), the effective nuclear moments differ between the ↑ and ↓ manifolds (enhancement roughly 1 ± A/2ν_n in opposite senses), and so do the saturation levels. Continuous ESR driving of |↓,−5/2⟩ ↔ |↑,−5/2⟩ also mixes exactly the two states being compared. The inset rests on three V_ESR values with large error bars, and the normalization is not defined. The authors should state the normalization, correct for or bound the matrix-element ratio using the model from concern 1, add more V_ESR values if possible, and compare the 6 mV ratio (about 0.63) with the thermal Boltzmann ratio at 0.4 K and 450 mT (exp(−h·4 GHz/k_B·0.4 K) ≈ 0.62) as a consistency check. Since the ↑/↓ assignment is already supported by the opposite field slopes of I and II in Fig. 3b, the authors could lean on that rather than saying Fig. 4 "removes the ambiguity". The same caveat applies to the explanation of the field dependence of II and IV in Fig. 3a.

8. **The nuclear g-factor extraction neglects second-order hyperfine (pseudo-nuclear Zeeman) shifts (SFig. 5).** g_N = 0.37 ± 0.04 is obtained from a straight-line fit of transition I against B_z, compared with 0.315 in bulk (about 17%, 1.4σ). The frequency of |↓,−5/2⟩ ↔ |↓,−3/2⟩ contains a field-dependent second-order term of order A_⊥²/(4 h f_ESR), about 1–2 MHz at 200 mT (f_ESR ≈ 2 GHz) and falling roughly as 1/B toward 1.4 T (f_ESR ≈ 11.5 GHz). This is comparable to the total shift of about 3 MHz in SFig. 5, whose points indeed look flat from 200 to 450 mT and steeper above. A linear fit therefore biases g_N and probably explains the discrepancy, which argues against presenting it as a measured deviation from the bulk value. In addition, the tip field (B_tip ≈ 67 mT) is included in the electron Zeeman term but not in the nuclear Zeeman term, and Supplementary Note 1 fixes g_N at the literature value "given the discrepancy", which is circular if the discrepancy comes from the linear fit. For 47Ti g_N is negative, and the sign convention should be stated. The authors should fit all four transitions at all fields with the full Hamiltonian, with g_N free and A_z, A_⊥ (stated, fixed or free) and Q shared, report whether any discrepancy with 0.315 remains, use the same g_N in the main text, compute the grey lines in Fig. 3b from that full calculation, and state whether the pseudo-nuclear Zeeman term is included.

10. **The NMR linewidths and the coherence outlook are not discussed.** The Lorentzian fits (Fig. 3a, Fig. 4) give ENDOR lines several MHz wide, far broader than any nuclear dephasing compatible with the seconds-scale T1. Possible causes are power broadening by V_NMR = 20 mV; electron-spin flips under continuous ESR drive and tunnelling, which switch the nuclear frequency by ~A_z = 132 MHz so that the m_s-resolved lines have a lifetime set by the electron T1; mixing of |↓,−5/2⟩ and |↑,−5/2⟩ by the continuous ESR drive; and tip-height or tip-field noise. This bears directly on the Conclusion's statement that coherence times could "potentially" reach "the same order of magnitude" as T1: under tunnelling and ESR drive the electron spin flips at kHz–MHz rates and would dephase the nucleus far faster than T1. The authors should report the fitted widths against field, V_NMR, V_ESR (which Fig. 4 already contains) and I_set, discuss what sets the linewidth, and justify or tone down the coherence statement.
