Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use an ENDOR scheme in ESR-STM to drive and detect nuclear spin transitions of a single 47Ti atom on MgO/Ag(100). One RF tone in the MHz range drives the nucleus and a second, chopped tone in the GHz range reads out the population of one nuclear state through the hyperfine-split ESR peaks. Earlier STM work (Veldman 2024; Stolte 2025) saw only "NMR-like" transitions, which need electron-nuclear hybridization for readout. This work separates drive from readout, resolves quadrupole-split transitions I to IV (and possibly V/VI), follows them from 200 to 1400 mT, and proposes that the drive is an effective oscillating magnetic field. Continuous-wave NMR on a single surface atom with atomic-scale control is a worthwhile step toward coherent nuclear control by STM. The headline claim, that driving is "independent of hybridization", is not yet established quantitatively.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear ENDOR demonstration on a single atom. Fig. 1e shows the first two ESR peaks equalizing when f_NMR is on resonance. Fig. 2a/b shows NMR features that appear only at the ESR frequencies of particular m_I states. The controls are sensible: f_ESR set off resonance (grey trace, Fig. 2a), and an I = 0 Ti atom measured with the same tip (SFig. 2).
- The quadrupole splitting is resolved and the transitions can be addressed one at a time. The 2D map (Fig. 2b) matches the spin-Hamiltonian frequencies, and A_z = 132.1 ± 0.4 MHz agrees with the literature.
- Transitions I to IV persist from 200 mT to 1.4 T (Fig. 3), well into the regime where single-tone NMR-like features vanish (Fig. 1d), and their positions follow the nuclear Zeeman term.
- The V_ESR dependence (Fig. 4) is a sensible extra handle for assigning transitions II and IV to the m_s = ↑ manifold.
- The setup is described in detail (SFig. 9, Methods), and the authors say data and code will be deposited.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The central claim, that nuclear driving is "irrespective of hybridization" and "without hybridization with the electron spin" (Abstract, Introduction, Conclusion), is not supported quantitatively, and the experiment as designed does not separate drive from hybridization.
   - Fig. 1d versus Fig. 3: the single-tone features vanish above about 100 mT because the readout needs Δm_s ≠ 0. That is a statement about readout, not about drive. ENDOR changes the readout, so features persisting in Fig. 3 show that readout no longer needs hybridization. They do not show that the drive matrix element is independent of it.
   - In ENDOR the nuclear transition rate is generally dominated by hyperfine enhancement. An RF field B1 (or an effective field from tip-height modulation acting on the strongly tilted tip field) acts on the electron moment, and through the hyperfine term (A/2 ≈ 65 MHz, much larger than the nuclear Zeeman energy of a few MHz) it produces a nuclear matrix element of order (g_e μ_B / g_N μ_N) × (A / h f_ESR) × g_N μ_N B1.
   - With g_e ≈ 0.56, g_N ≈ 0.315, A ≈ 132 MHz and f_ESR = 2 to 11.5 GHz, this electron-mediated term still exceeds direct nuclear Zeeman driving by roughly an order of magnitude or more across 200 to 1400 mT. It falls as about 1/B, the same scaling as the hybridization coefficient in SFig. 1b.
   - So the data are consistent with a drive that is itself mediated by electron-nuclear admixture, just weaker in amplitude.
   - The authors should calculate the transition matrix elements of the Eq. 1 eigenstates for (i) a direct transverse field on the nucleus only, (ii) a transverse field on electron and nucleus together, (iii) modulation of B_tip, and (iv) modulation of the anisotropic hyperfine and quadrupole terms. They should then compare the predicted field dependence and the ratio of transition I to transition II with the data.
   - Experimentally, the drive strength must be extracted at each field independently of the ESR-readout amplitude. Options: V_NMR power dependence of the ENDOR depth and linewidth (saturation parameter, power broadening), or an estimate of the NMR Rabi frequency. The V_ESR-related transmission problem (Supplementary Note 2) can be avoided by setting V_ESR to the same lower value at all fields, or by normalizing each ENDOR dip to the corresponding ESR peak height in SFig. 4a.
   - Without this, "driven efficiently irrespective of its hybridization" should be reworded to what is shown: readout independent of hybridization, with drive persisting up to 1.4 T.

2. The discussion of the drive mechanism does not rule out the alternatives it dismisses, so the conclusion that the drive is "dominantly an effective oscillating magnetic field" is not supported.
   - Hyperfine modulation: the Discussion says modulating the hyperfine interaction would drive only flip-flop transitions at GHz frequencies. That holds only for the isotropic or transverse-transverse part. Modulating an anisotropic off-diagonal term (A_zx S_z I_x) drives Δm_I = ±1, Δm_s = 0 transitions at exactly the observed MHz frequencies, with an m_s-dependent sign. This is the hyperfine-Stark mechanism of electrically driven NMR in single-molecule magnets (the authors' Ref. 7). A tilted tip field (φ ≈ 5°, Supplementary Note 1) and a piezoelectric displacement of the atom make such a term plausible.
   - Quadrupole modulation is dismissed on two grounds, and neither is decisive. (i) A "slight signal" at the −1/2 ↔ +1/2 transitions (V/VI) in Fig. 2b; its statistical significance is not quantified and the text only says it "may be discerned". (ii) The absence of Δm_I = ±2 lines in a single noisy sweep (SFig. 8). Whether ±2 transitions appear at all depends on which components of the electric field gradient are modulated, and their strength relative to ±1 lines is not estimated.
   - The authors should (a) quantify V/VI with a fit and a signal-to-noise estimate, ideally from a dedicated measurement with f_ESR on the m_I = −1/2 peak; (b) estimate the expected Δm_I = ±2 amplitude under quadrupole modulation relative to the observed ±1 lines, given the noise in SFig. 8; and (c) use the m_s dependence of the line amplitudes as a discriminant, since transitions I and II share Δm_I but have opposite hyperfine-enhancement factors.
   - Otherwise the mechanism should be presented as an open question, and the sentence claiming applicability to other nuclear spin systems "not dependent on A or Q" should be removed.

B. Should address

3. The Fig. 1d versus Fig. 3 comparison is made under mismatched conditions. Fig. 1d was taken with a different microtip (Methods) and at different setpoints (I_set = 2.5 to 3 pA, V_bias = 70 to 90 mV, versus 4 pA and 80 mV). No ENDOR data are shown at 60 or 100 mT, and no single-tone data with the ENDOR tip. The contrast between "vanishes beyond 100 mT" and "persists to 1.4 T" is a main pillar of the narrative. The authors should show single-tone and ENDOR spectra with the same tip, setpoint and V_NMR at at least one low field (≤ 100 mT) and one high field.

4. Reproducibility across atoms and tips is not documented. All main-text ENDOR data come from one microtip and apparently one 47Ti atom. The authors should state how many 47Ti atoms and tips showed ENDOR features, and show at least one more atom (ideally with a second tip) reproducing transitions I and II at one field, with the fitted A_z and Q. This also bears on the tip-dependent Q and g_N deviations discussed below.

5. The g_N extraction (SFig. 5) uses a linear fit that leaves out second-order hyperfine shifts. Transition frequencies in the m_s = ↓ manifold include field-dependent second-order terms of order A_⊥²/(4 h f_ESR), which are about 1 to 2 MHz at 200 mT and fall roughly as 1/B. These terms are comparable to the total frequency change of about 3 MHz over the range. A linear slope therefore does not measure g_N and may explain the 0.37 versus 0.315 discrepancy (about 17%, 1.4σ).
   - The authors should fit the four transitions at all fields with the full Hamiltonian, g_N free and A_z, A_⊥ and Q shared.
   - The g_N used in the main text should be consistent with that procedure. At present Supplementary Note 1 fixes g_N at the literature value "given the discrepancy", which is circular if the discrepancy comes from the linear fit.
   - The grey lines in Fig. 3b ("calculated from Eq. (1)") should be checked against the outliers: IV at 1.07 T (about 72 MHz) and II at 1.4 T (about 90 MHz).

6. The precision claimed for Q does not support the comparison made. Q = −2.8 ± 0.8 MHz carries a 30% uncertainty, so the "deviation of ~5%" from previous reports cannot be resolved, and attributing it to a tip-dependent electric field gradient is speculative. The authors should also state which parameters were free in the final fit (A_z and κ, with B_tip, φ and g_e fixed), how many transition frequencies entered it, and whether A_⊥ was fixed and at what value. A sensitivity check is needed: how A_z and Q change when B_tip, φ, g_e,z and g_N are varied within their uncertainties. The assumption η = 0 should be justified with a note on how a tilted tip or nearby atoms would break the four-fold symmetry.

7. Interpreting amplitudes as populations in Fig. 4 assumes equal drive matrix elements for transitions I and II. P↑/P↓ is taken as the ratio of the normalized dip amplitudes of I and II. However, the effective NMR matrix element differs between the m_s = ↓ and ↑ manifolds (hyperfine enhancement goes as about 1 ± A/2ν_n in opposite senses), and so does the degree of saturation. The amplitude ratio therefore mixes electron population with drive strength. The trend with V_ESR is suggestive, but it rests on three points with large error bars. The authors should state the normalization explicitly, correct for or bound the matrix-element ratio using the model from concern 1, and add more V_ESR values if possible. The same caveat applies to the explanation of the field dependence of II and IV in Fig. 3a.

8. The peaks in the m_I = −3/2 trace (red, Fig. 2a) are not convincing. The text says population is driven out (dips, blue) and in (peaks, red). In Fig. 2a the red trace shows little visible structure, and in Fig. 2b the column at about 3.9 GHz mainly shows decreases at III/IV. The column-mean background subtraction in Fig. 2b could also shift apparent signs. The authors should show the fitted amplitudes with uncertainties for both traces, and check the sign of the I and II features at the −3/2 readout against a rate-equation model of spin pumping (as sketched in Fig. 3c). If peaks are absent where the model predicts them, this matters for the population-transfer picture.

9. Possible artifacts from the MHz drive and heating are not fully characterized.
   - Supplementary Note 2 says transfer functions exist for the "full frequency range", but only GHz values are shown (SFig. 6). The MHz transfer function over 20 to 170 MHz, and hence the actual V_NMR at the junction across each sweep, should be shown, since features are 5 to 10 MHz wide.
   - Supplementary Note 2 mentions heating effects at high RF power. The authors should state whether adding V_NMR = 20 mV changes the DC current, the tip height or the ESR linewidth.
   - The authors should show at least one ENDOR spectrum at reduced V_NMR (for example 10 mV) to confirm that line positions do not shift with drive amplitude.

10. The NMR linewidths and their implications are not discussed. The Lorentzian fits (Fig. 3a, Fig. 4) give dips several MHz wide. The authors should report the fitted widths against field, V_ESR and V_NMR, and discuss the broadening: power broadening, tunnelling-current-induced electron spin flips that change the hyperfine field (so that the m_s-resolved lines have a lifetime set by electron T1), or tip-field fluctuations. This also bears on the Conclusion's suggestion that coherence times could reach "the same order of magnitude" as the seconds-long T1. Given MHz-wide lines and continuous electron spin flips under tunnelling, that expectation needs justification or should be toned down.

C. Minor

11. The word "unambiguously" (Introduction) overstates the case given concerns 1 and 2. Similarly, "driven efficiently" is not supported by any measure of drive efficiency (Rabi rate, saturation power).

12. The value of f_NMR for transition I at 450 mT differs between figures: 49.3 MHz in Fig. 1e versus about 48.5 MHz in SFig. 5. The authors should reconcile the two or note that they agree within the linewidth.

13. Fig. 3b caption says "grey dashed lines", but the lines in the figure are solid. Fig. 1c caption refers to "yellow lines", which appear orange; the main text also says "yellow" for the orange spectrum in Fig. 1b. The SFig. 6 caption refers to "Fig. S3 a", which should be SFig. 4a.

14. The text says hybridization is "reduced by approximately a factor of 10" between 200 and 1400 mT. SFig. 1b uses a logarithmic axis whose tick labels are hard to read, so the factor cannot be checked from the figure. The authors should give the values explicitly and state whether the plotted quantity is an amplitude or a probability (|⟨↓,−3/2|ψ⟩|²).

15. Lock-in chopping at 271 Hz sits close to the preamplifier's 300 Hz low-pass cutoff. The authors should state the resulting attenuation and phase, and whether they matter for comparing amplitudes across datasets.

16. References duplicated: Ref. 20 and Ref. 29 are the same paper (Yang et al., PRL 119, 227206), as are Refs. 35 and 38 (Yang et al., Nat. Nanotech. 13, 1120).

17. The Data availability statement contains a placeholder "[URL]".

18. Throughout, "the nuclear spin can be driven efficiently irrespective of its hybridization" and "NMR driving without hybridization" should be phrased consistently with what is measured (see concern 1). The magnetic field values (200 mT in Fig. 1d versus "beyond B_z = 100 mT" in the text) should be used consistently.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a quantitative drive model: the transition matrix elements of the Eq. 1 eigenstates under each candidate drive term (direct B1 on the nucleus, B1 on electron and nucleus, B_tip modulation, anisotropic-hyperfine modulation, quadrupole modulation). Compare the predicted field dependence and the I/II and I/III amplitude ratios with Figs. 3 and 4. This would turn the Discussion from a hypothesis into a test.
2. Measure the V_NMR dependence of the ENDOR depth and linewidth for transitions I and II at two widely separated fields (for example 300 mT and 1.07 T), with matched V_ESR. Saturation curves give a drive-strength estimate that does not depend on the readout and would directly address whether drive efficiency tracks hybridization.
3. Fit all transition frequencies at all fields globally with the full Hamiltonian (g_N, A_z, A_⊥, Q free; B_tip and g_e from the ESR data). Report the correlation matrix, and drop the separate linear g_N fit.
4. Show ENDOR on at least one more 47Ti atom (and ideally a second tip), including a single-tone versus ENDOR comparison at the same low field. This establishes reproducibility and removes the mismatch between Fig. 1d and Fig. 3.
