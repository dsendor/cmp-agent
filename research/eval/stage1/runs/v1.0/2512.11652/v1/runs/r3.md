Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use an STM to run an electron-nuclear double resonance (ENDOR) experiment on a single 47Ti atom (I = 5/2) on MgO/Ag(100). Continuous MHz driving changes the relative heights of the hyperfine-resolved ESR peaks, and the authors read this as a measure of nuclear spin population. They resolve at least four quadrupole-split Δm_I = ±1 transitions, follow them from 200 to 1400 mT, and fit A_z and Q. Earlier STM work saw only NMR-like transitions at low field, where the electron and nuclear spins are strongly hybridized. Separating nuclear driving from electron-spin readout in a scanning probe is therefore a useful and timely step toward coherent nuclear control in STM. The headline interpretive claim is that the nuclear spin is driven "irrespective of its hybridization with the electron spin", by an effective oscillating magnetic field. The data do not yet establish that claim.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First clear ENDOR spectra on a single surface atom in an STM. Driving (MHz) and readout (ESR peak heights) are separated, and the scheme is easy to follow (Fig. 1e, Fig. 2).
- Good controls on whether the signal is genuinely nuclear. With f_ESR set off resonance there is no f_NMR dependence (Fig. 2a, grey). The I = 0 isotope shows no response at its own ESR peak (SFig. 2). The resonances move when f_ESR selects a different m_I (Fig. 2b) and when B changes (Fig. 3b). A transmission-line artifact would do none of these.
- The transition frequencies agree quantitatively with the spin Hamiltonian. Transitions I to IV sit at about 48, 57, 75 and 85 MHz at 450 mT. These values match A_z/2 ≈ 66 MHz combined with an effective axial quadrupole term of about 1.5Q ≈ −4.2 MHz.
- The ENDOR features persist up to 1.4 T (Fig. 3a), whereas the single-tone NMR-like features vanish near 200 mT (Fig. 1d). This makes the method usable in the high-field regime, where the nuclear T1 is long.
- The V_ESR dependence of the I/II amplitude ratio (Fig. 4) is a sensible extra check on the electron-spin assignment.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The claim that driving is "irrespective of hybridization" conflicts with the authors' own mechanism and with standard hyperfine enhancement. The abstract, introduction ("we unambiguously demonstrate NMR driving independent of electron-nuclear spin hybridization") and conclusion all say that the nuclear spin is driven without needing hybridization. The evidence offered is that the ENDOR features persist at fields where the flip-flop admixture |⟨↓,−3/2|ψ⟩|² is reduced to about 10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵ (SFig. 1b). Three problems follow.
   (i) At these fields the transverse hyperfine term still mixes a small electron-spin component into each nuclear state, with amplitude of order A⊥/(2 f_ESR). Any transverse oscillating field on the electron then drives the Δm_I = ±1, Δm_s = 0 transition through this admixture. The electron moment is about 10³ times larger than the 47Ti nuclear moment (g_e,z μ_B versus g_N μ_N). Even a ~1% admixture therefore gives a "hyperfine-enhanced" drive far larger than the bare nuclear Zeeman coupling. This is the well-known hyperfine enhancement of nuclear Rabi frequencies in ENDOR and in donor systems.
   (ii) The proposed mechanism is an effective field from piezoelectric displacement in the tip field, from barrier modulation or from spin torque. In Supplementary Note 1 the authors themselves state that the tip field is mostly exchange "and hence cannot couple directly to the nuclear spin". By their own reasoning, this field can reach the nucleus only through the electron spin and the hyperfine coupling, which is the hybridization the paper says is unnecessary.
   (iii) The paper does not show that the driving efficiency is independent of B. The ENDOR amplitude changes non-monotonically with field, and the authors attribute this to the V_ESR transmission (Supplementary Note 2). That leaves open whether the NMR Rabi rate falls with B, as hyperfine-mediated driving predicts (amplitude ∝ A⊥/f_ESR).
   What the authors should do:
   - Compute the expected Rabi rates for the Δm_s = 0 transitions from the full Hamiltonian (Eq. 1, including A⊥) as a function of B. Do this for (a) a bare transverse field acting only on the nucleus and (b) a transverse field or exchange modulation acting on the electron.
   - Compare both with a measured quantity that is independent of the V_ESR readout calibration. Options are the saturation V_NMR, or the power-broadened NMR linewidth versus V_NMR, measured at two or three fields with V_ESR held fixed (for example 200 and 450 mT, where V_ESR = 20 mV is available at both).
   - If driving turns out to be mediated by the electron, reword the claim to "driving does not rely on electron-nuclear flip-flop transitions (Δm_s ≠ 0)". The data may support that narrower statement. They do not support "irrespective of hybridization".

2. The key contrast between single-tone and ENDOR detection is not a matched comparison. The argument that ENDOR reveals driving that the earlier scheme cannot rests on Fig. 1d (single tone; features at 60 and 100 mT, absent at 200 mT) set against Fig. 3a (ENDOR; features from 200 mT upward). The two datasets differ in several ways at once:
   - They were taken with different microtips (Methods: "except for Fig. 1d, which was taken with a different microtip"), so the tip fields and spin polarizations differ.
   - The setpoints differ (I_set = 2.5–3 pA and V_bias = 70–90 mV in Fig. 1d, against 4 pA and 80 mV in Fig. 3a).
   - They cover different field ranges and overlap only at 200 mT.
   Because the authors cannot distinguish "no driving" from "no readout" in single-tone mode at 200 mT, the comparison does not show that the hybridization-dependent features and the ENDOR features come from different driving processes. The authors should, with one tip and one setpoint:
   - measure single-tone NMR and ENDOR on the same atom at a low field (60–100 mT) and at a high field (≥ 200 mT);
   - show that the ENDOR resonances at low field coincide with, or differ from, the single-tone features, and check how their amplitudes scale relative to SFig. 1b.
   Without this, the "unambiguously" wording in the introduction is too strong.

B. Should address

3. The argument that excludes hyperfine and quadrupole modulation is incomplete.
   - Hyperfine modulation (Discussion) is ruled out because it "would drive flip-flop transitions … in the GHz range". That is true only for modulation of the S_x I_x + S_y I_y part. Modulating off-diagonal anisotropic components such as A_zx S_z I_x does drive Δm_I = ±1, Δm_s = 0 transitions at the NMR frequencies measured here. This is the hyperfine-Stark or electric-dipole-spin-resonance-type nuclear drive known from donor work (refs. 16, 17). The tip-induced change of the electric field and of the adsorption geometry could modulate exactly these terms.
   - Quadrupole modulation is excluded because no Δm_I = ±2 lines appear in SFig. 8, and because a weak response is seen at V/VI. SFig. 8 is a single sweep with noise of about ±3 fA, and the expected Δm_I = ±2 amplitude relative to Δm_I = ±1 is never estimated. The V/VI response in Fig. 2b is described only as "may be discerned".
   The authors should estimate the relative matrix elements of each mechanism for transitions I–VI and for the Δm_I = 2 lines. They should then state the amplitude ratio that would be needed to see Δm_I = 2 at the SNR of SFig. 8, and present the mechanism as an open question unless a quantitative discrimination is available. Comparing the relative strengths of transitions I/III against II/IV, which hyperfine-enhanced driving predicts to differ between the ↑ and ↓ manifolds, could help separate the mechanisms.

4. Drive strength is not characterized, so "driven efficiently" and "saturate" are untested.
   - All NMR data use V_NMR = 20 mV. There is no V_NMR dependence of the dip depth or the linewidth, and no MHz transfer function. Supplementary Note 2 and SFig. 6 cover only the GHz ESR line.
   - The statement that the first two ESR peaks "equalize" (Fig. 1e) and that this "implies that we drive the nuclear spin sufficiently to saturate" comes from one spectrum.
   - The NMR linewidths in Fig. 3a are several MHz. That is far broader than any nuclear dephasing time compatible with the seconds-scale T1 invoked in the Conclusion. Possible causes include power broadening, the electron spin lifetime under tunnelling (which switches the m_s-dependent hyperfine field), and mixing of the |↓,−5/2⟩ and |↑,−5/2⟩ states by the continuous ESR drive.
   The authors should give:
   - the V_NMR dependence of the dip depth and width for transition I;
   - the calibrated MHz amplitude at the junction across 30–170 MHz;
   - the dependence of linewidth on V_ESR, which Fig. 4 already contains and only needs extracting;
   - a short discussion of what sets the linewidth.
   This also bears on the Conclusion's suggestion that coherence times could be "in the same order of magnitude" as T1. That suggestion is not supported by anything measured here and should be softened.

5. Reproducibility across atoms and tips is not shown. "All data presented in the main text were taken with the same microtip" and, as far as one can tell, on a single 47Ti atom. The fitted A_z, Q and g_N, the transition assignments, and the persistence of ENDOR at high field are therefore each demonstrated once. The authors also attribute the 5% shift in Q to "a different electric field gradient dependent on the tip shape", which is itself a claim of tip dependence. The authors should report ENDOR spectra, with at least transitions I and II, on a second 47Ti atom and preferably with a second tip, and quote the spread of A_z and Q. Measuring Q at two tip heights on one atom would test the tip-EFG explanation directly.

6. The nuclear g-factor extraction ignores second-order hyperfine (pseudo-nuclear Zeeman) shifts. SFig. 5 fits transition I against B with a straight line and gets g_N = 0.37 ± 0.04, compared with 0.315 in bulk. The frequency of |↓,−5/2⟩ ↔ |↓,−3/2⟩ also contains a field-dependent second-order term of order A⊥²/f_ESR. That term changes by roughly a MHz between 200 mT (f_ESR ≈ 2 GHz) and 1.4 T (f_ESR ≈ 11.5 GHz), which is comparable to the total shift of about 3 MHz in SFig. 5. The first four points (200–450 mT) in SFig. 5 also look flat, which is not well described by a straight line. Separately:
   - the tip field (B_tip ≈ 67 mT) is included in the electron Zeeman term but not in the nuclear Zeeman term;
   - the paper then reverts to the literature g_N "given the discrepancy".
   The authors should fit the field dependence of I–IV with the full Hamiltonian (with A⊥ stated and either fixed or free) and g_N as a free parameter. They should then report whether any discrepancy with 0.315 remains. The grey lines in Fig. 3b should come from that full calculation, and the text should say whether the pseudo-nuclear Zeeman term is included.

7. The Hamiltonian fit is under-specified. Supplementary Note 1 fits A_z and κ with B_tip, φ, g_e,z and g_N fixed, and sets η = 0 by symmetry. Several things are missing:
   - The values used for A_x = A_y and g_e,x/y are not given, although they control the second-order shifts and the mixing in SFig. 1.
   - The paper does not say which transitions and fields enter the fit, so the data-to-parameter ratio cannot be judged.
   - It is unclear how the ±0.4 MHz and ±0.8 MHz uncertainties were obtained (covariance only, or including tip-field uncertainty).
   - Q = −2.8 ± 0.8 MHz is called a "~5% deviation" from the literature, but the error bar is about 30%, so the deviation is not significant. The convention also matters: with Eq. S2 the observed splittings correspond to an effective axial coefficient of 1.5Q, and readers comparing with refs. 32 and 33 need this stated.
   - The η = 0 assumption sits uneasily with an EFG that depends on the tip. A tilted tip (φ ≈ 5°) breaks the four-fold symmetry.
   The authors should list all fixed parameters with their sources, give a residuals table of measured against calculated frequencies for every transition and field, and state the convention for Q explicitly.

8. Reading the I/II amplitude ratio as an electron-spin population ratio needs justification (Fig. 4). P↑/P↓ is taken from the normalized amplitudes of transitions I and II. This equals the m_s population ratio only if the two transitions are driven equally strongly and read out with equal efficiency. If driving is hyperfine-enhanced (Concern 1), the effective nuclear moments differ between the ↑ and ↓ manifolds, and so do the saturation levels. In addition, continuous ESR driving of the |↓,−5/2⟩ ↔ |↑,−5/2⟩ transition mixes exactly the two states being compared. The inset has three points with large error bars, and the normalization is not defined. Three things would help:
   - state the normalization;
   - compare the 6 mV ratio (about 0.63) with the thermal Boltzmann ratio at 0.4 K and 450 mT (exp(−h·4 GHz/k_B·0.4 K) ≈ 0.62). This agreement would be a useful consistency check if it holds;
   - acknowledge the matrix-element caveat.
   The ↑/↓ assignment is already supported by the opposite field slopes of I and II in Fig. 3b. The authors could lean on that rather than saying Fig. 4 "removes the ambiguity".

9. The spin-pumping explanation for transitions III/IV and the field dependence of the amplitudes is qualitative. The authors explain why driving III/IV (m_I = −3/2 ↔ −1/2) reduces the m_I = −5/2 population with the rate scheme of Fig. 3c. They also claim that III/IV "become less pronounced" at higher field because flip-flop rates are reduced. In Fig. 3a, however, III and IV remain clearly visible at 660 and 1070 mT, and the amplitude changes are confounded by the changing V_ESR (Supplementary Note 2). There are also bookkeeping questions:
   - Fig. 2a's text describes "peaks (red)" for the m_I = −3/2 readout, but the red trace shows no clear peaks at 50 or 85 MHz.
   - In Fig. 2b, the m_I = −3/2 column should show an increase (blue in the colour scale) at I/II if population is pumped from −5/2 to −3/2.
   A minimal rate-equation model would let the authors check whether the observed sign and relative size of each feature, in each m_I readout column, are consistent with the proposed pumping network. The model would include spin-polarized scattering, flip-flops weighted by SFig. 1b, ESR driving and NMR driving. It would also turn the field-dependence argument into a prediction.

10. Signal changes with field are attributed to the RF line without separating V_NMR effects. Supplementary Note 2 attributes the weaker high-field ENDOR signal to reduced V_ESR above 5 GHz. The MHz amplitude reaching the junction is not calibrated, and the frequency dependence of the MHz line over 30–170 MHz is not shown (SFig. 6 covers only the GHz range). Because the I and II amplitudes at different f_NMR are compared quantitatively (Fig. 4, Fig. 3 discussion), a frequency-dependent V_NMR would bias those comparisons. The authors should give the MHz transfer function, or at least show that V_NMR at the junction is constant between 45 and 90 MHz.

C. Minor

11. The paper says V/VI "may be discerned" between 4.0 and 4.1 GHz in Fig. 2b and then uses this "slight signal" in both directions in the mechanism discussion. The authors should show a line cut with a fit, or treat V/VI as not resolved.

12. The Fig. 3b caption says the calculated lines are "grey dashed"; they appear solid. The 1070 mT point for IV (about 72 MHz) lies well off the calculated line. Comment on whether the four-Lorentzian fit at 1070 and 1400 mT, where SNR is low, is reliable, and give fit uncertainties for all points.

13. Text and caption inconsistencies:
   - Fig. 1b is called "yellow" in the text and "orange" in the caption, and SFig. 2 uses both words.
   - The SFig. 6 caption refers to "Fig. S3 a" where SFig. 4a is meant.
   - "Rhode & Schwarz" should read "Rohde & Schwarz".
   - "converge" in Supplementary Note 1 should read "convergence".

14. Reference duplicates: refs. 20 and 29 are the same paper (Yang et al., PRL 119, 227206), and refs. 35 and 38 are the same paper (Yang et al., Nature Nanotech. 13, 1120). Ref. 29 is presumably meant to be a different ESR-STM paper.

15. The data availability statement still contains the placeholder "[URL]".

16. In Methods, state the DC bias polarity and the tip-sample distance for the ENDOR data. The text attributes Fig. 2a/2b differences to "different tip heights" and a "~10 fA" current offset, but the tip height is not otherwise reported.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a quantitative driving model: Rabi rate as a function of B for each candidate mechanism, computed from the full Hamiltonian. Test it with V_NMR-dependent saturation or linewidth measurements at two or more fields. This is the single addition that would make the central claim defensible, and it would decide between "independent of hybridization" and "independent of flip-flop transitions".
2. Make one matched single-tone versus ENDOR comparison (same tip, same atom, same setpoint) across the crossover field.
3. Repeat the key ENDOR spectrum on a second 47Ti atom or with a second tip, and fit all field-dependent transition frequencies with the full Hamiltonian to obtain g_N, A_z and Q with properly propagated uncertainties.
4. Add a simple rate-equation model of the steady-state nuclear populations under spin pumping, ESR driving and NMR driving. It would account for the sign and size of every ENDOR feature in Figs. 2–4.
