Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors implement continuous-wave electron-nuclear double resonance (ENDOR) in an ESR-STM. They use the hyperfine-resolved ESR peak heights of a single 47Ti atom on MgO/Ag(100) as a readout of nuclear populations while a second, MHz-frequency RF tone drives Delta m_I = ±1 transitions. The quadrupole interaction resolves several nuclear transitions, and the transitions are followed from 200 to 1400 mT, a field range where the single-tone "NMR-like" features of earlier STM work (which need electron-nuclear mixing for readout) are absent. Separating nuclear drive from electron-spin readout in an atom-by-atom platform is a real and useful step. However, the broader claims, that the nuclear spin is driven "irrespective of its hybridization" and "mainly through an oscillating magnetic field", are not yet supported by the analysis presented.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First ENDOR-type spectroscopy of a single on-surface nuclear spin. Driving and readout are separated, and transitions are resolved by both nuclear and electron spin state (Fig. 2b, Fig. 3a).
- Sensible controls. There is no f_NMR dependence when f_ESR is off resonance (Fig. 2a, grey) and no feature on an I = 0 Ti atom (SFig. 2). The feature frequencies follow the probed m_I (Fig. 2b) and the external field (Fig. 3).
- The transition frequencies agree with a spin-Hamiltonian model at the few-MHz level. The model gives A_z and Q values close to the literature, and the m_s assignment of transitions I/II is supported by the V_ESR dependence (Fig. 4).
- The approach is general and could be applied to other hyperfine-coupled adatoms and to pulsed nuclear control.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The claim that the nuclear spin is driven "irrespective of hybridization" contradicts standard ENDOR physics and the paper's own supplementary statements.**
   - **Where the claim is made:** Abstract ("the nuclear spin can be driven efficiently irrespective of its hybridization with the electron spin"), Introduction ("NMR driving independent of electron-nuclear spin hybridization") and Conclusion ("hybridization ... is not necessary to drive NMR").
   - **What the evidence actually shows:** NMR features persist in ENDOR at 200–1400 mT, where the flip-flop admixture |<↓,−3/2|ψ>|² is small (SFig. 1b). But a small admixture is not zero, and in ENDOR the nuclear transition moment is normally dominated by it.
   - **Order-of-magnitude estimate:**
     - The transverse hyperfine terms mix electron character into the nuclear states with amplitude ε ~ A_⊥/(2 f_ESR). For A_⊥ of tens of MHz and f_ESR of 2–11 GHz, ε is roughly 10⁻³–10⁻².
     - The electron-spin moment exceeds the 47Ti nuclear moment by about μ_B g_e / (μ_N |g_N|) ~ 10³–10⁴.
     - So the electron-mediated ("hyperfine-enhanced") contribution to the Delta m_I = ±1 matrix element is plausibly 10–100 times larger than direct coupling to the bare nucleus, across the whole field range studied.
   - **Internal inconsistency:** Supplementary Note 1 states that the tip field is mostly exchange and "cannot couple directly with the nuclear spin". Yet the Discussion proposes a drive via an "effective oscillating magnetic field" analogous to the ESR-STM mechanisms (piezoelectric displacement in the tip field, barrier modulation, spin torque). All of these act on the electron spin. A nuclear drive produced by them necessarily passes through the hyperfine admixture, which is the hybridization the paper says is not needed.
   - **The comparison with Fig. 1d does not settle this.** The single-tone signal vanishing at 200 mT reflects the readout requirement (Delta m_s ≠ 0), not the driving efficiency. Fig. 1d was also taken with a different microtip and different V_bias/I_set at each field.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - (i) Compute the Delta m_I = ±1 transition matrix elements for each candidate drive term from the full Hamiltonian, including the anisotropic A, the tip-field tilt (φ ≈ 5°) and the measured g-tensor. The candidates are: an oscillating field on the bare nucleus; a modulated tip/exchange field on the electron; modulation of A; and modulation of Q. Give the field dependence and m_s dependence predicted for each.
     - (ii) Measure the drive strength directly. Use the V_NMR dependence of ENDOR depth and linewidth (saturation and power broadening) at several fields with matched V_ESR (200–450 mT, where V_ESR = 20 mV is constant, is sufficient). Ideally also measure at fields below 200 mT, where the admixture is large.
     - (iii) Compare the intensities of the ↑ and ↓ transitions (I vs II, III vs IV) with the predicted hyperfine-enhancement factors.
   - **If the drive is shown to be hyperfine-enhanced, this does not undermine the result.** It would change the headline claim from "independent of hybridization" to "nuclear drive and readout without relying on hybridized readout". Either way, the wording in the abstract and conclusion must follow what the data show.

B. Should address

2. **The arguments that eliminate hyperfine and quadrupole modulation are incomplete (Discussion; Supplementary Note 3; SFig. 8).**
   - **Hyperfine modulation:** The paper argues that modulating the hyperfine interaction only drives flip-flop transitions at GHz frequencies. This holds only for the S_x I_x + S_y I_y part. With an anisotropic or tilted hyperfine tensor, and with an electron quantization axis tilted by the tip field (φ = 5°, Suppl. Note 1), modulation produces S_z I_x-type terms. These drive Delta m_s = 0, Delta m_I = ±1 transitions at exactly the observed MHz frequencies. This is the mechanism used for electrically driven NMR on donors (ref. 16).
   - **Quadrupole modulation:** For a quadrupole drive, the relative strengths of the Delta m_I = ±1 terms ({I_z, I_x}) and Delta m_I = ±2 terms (I_x² − I_y²) depend on which electric-field-gradient components are modulated. Absent Delta m_I = ±2 lines therefore do not exclude a quadrupole drive.
   - **SNR of the supporting data:** In SFig. 8 even transitions I and III are barely above the noise. The "slight" V/VI response in Fig. 2b (f_ESR ≈ 4.0–4.1 GHz) is not quantified.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Write the modulated terms explicitly, with selection rules and expected relative intensities.
     - Show line cuts and Lorentzian fits with significance for transitions V/VI.
     - Repeat the wide-range sweep with enough averaging that the Delta m_I = ±2 upper bound is meaningful relative to the I/III amplitude.

3. **Only one atom and one microtip are shown.**
   - All main-text ENDOR data (Figs. 1e, 2, 3, 4) come from the same microtip and, apparently, the same 47Ti atom. Fig. 1d was taken with a different tip.
   - The fitted Q differs from the literature, and the authors attribute this to a tip-dependent field gradient. The ENDOR amplitudes also depend on tip polarization and height (as the ~10 fA offset argument in the Fig. 2 discussion shows).
   - **What the authors should do:** Show the ENDOR spectrum (at least the 450 mT spectrum, as in Fig. 3a) on a second 47Ti atom and, preferably, with a second tip. Report the spread in line positions, A_z and Q.

4. **No population model explains the sign, amplitude and assignment of the ENDOR features.**
   - **Signs and features:**
     - The text says the populations show "dips (blue) and peaks (red)" in Fig. 2a. The red (m_I = −3/2) trace shows no clear peak, and in Fig. 2b all features in the −3/2 column appear as decreases.
     - The assignment of III/IV as features of the m_I = −5/2 readout relies on a qualitative spin-pumping cascade (Fig. 3c).
   - **Field-dependence interpretation:** The amplitude changes with field (inner features weakening because of reduced flip-flop rates; ↑ features weakening at higher field) are interpreted without a model. They are also confounded with the V_ESR changes above 500 mT.
   - **Fig. 4:**
     - P↑/P↓ is equated with the m_s population ratio. That assumes equal NMR drive matrix elements in the two m_s manifolds, which does not hold if the drive is hyperfine-enhanced (concern 1).
     - The trend rests on three V_ESR values, with large error bars.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Provide a rate-equation (or Lindblad) model with spin-polarized scattering, flip-flop rates, ESR and NMR drive. Show that it reproduces the signs and relative amplitudes of I–IV in Fig. 2a/b and Fig. 3a, and the P↑/P↓ trend.
     - State which parameters are fitted and which are fixed.

5. **Possible instrumental contributions to the 2D ENDOR map (Fig. 2b, SFig. 3, Suppl. Note 2).**
   - **Extent along f_ESR:** In Fig. 2b, feature II near 83 MHz extends along f_ESR over roughly 3.72–3.82 GHz, wider than the ESR linewidth in Fig. 1b. In the raw map (SFig. 3), dark horizontal lines near 48 and 83 MHz appear to run across a wide range of f_ESR, including between ESR peaks.
   - **Background subtraction:** Subtracting the column average can both create and suppress such structure.
   - **Unmeasured RF inputs:** The junction amplitude of the MHz tone as a function of f_NMR is not shown; the transfer functions in SFig. 6 cover only the GHz tone. The two tones are combined passively and may mix in the nonlinear junction.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Show line cuts of the raw data at f_ESR values between ESR peaks.
     - Show the calibrated V_NMR(f_NMR) at the junction.
     - Confirm that the feature width along f_ESR matches the ESR linewidth.
   - The off-resonance trace in Fig. 2a and the I = 0 control are taken at a single f_ESR each, so they do not cover this.

6. **The fit parameters and their uncertainties are inconsistent (Suppl. Note 1, main text p. 4, Fig. 3b).**
   - **Q uncertainty:** κ* = −56.7 ± 0.8 MHz with Q = κ/[2I(2I−1)] = κ/20 gives Q = −2.84 ± 0.04 MHz, not ± 0.8 MHz.
   - **"5% deviation":** With the quoted ±0.8 MHz (about 30%), the stated "~5% deviation" from the literature is not meaningful. Either the uncertainty or the comparison needs correcting.
   - **Missing fixed values:** A_x, A_y (and g_x, g_y) are fixed but their values are not given.
   - **η = 0 assumption:** η = 0 is assumed from the four-fold site symmetry, while the authors attribute the change in Q to the tip, which breaks that symmetry.
   - **Fig. 3b deviations:** At 1070 mT, transition IV (~72 MHz) lies several MHz below the calculated line. At 1400 mT, transition II (~90 MHz) lies above it. These deviations exceed the "MHz precision" claimed.
   - **B_tip,z:** The quoted B_tip,z = 65.64 ± 0.02 mT from a seven-point linear fit seems implausibly precise.
   - **What the authors should do:** Provide a table of fixed and free parameters with uncertainties, and the residuals of the line positions at every field.

7. **The nuclear g-factor extraction neglects second-order hyperfine shifts (SFig. 5).**
   - g_N = 0.37 ± 0.04 is obtained from a linear fit of transition I against B_z.
   - Second-order terms of order A_⊥²/f_ESR shift the nuclear transition frequencies by an amount that scales roughly as 1/B. Across 0.2–1.4 T this change is comparable to the ~3 MHz total shift in SFig. 5. The points in SFig. 5 indeed look flat below 0.45 T and steeper above.
   - A linear fit therefore biases g_N, and this probably explains the 0.37 vs 0.315 discrepancy. That would argue against presenting it as a measured deviation from the bulk value.
   - **Sign:** For 47Ti g_N is negative, and the sign convention used should be stated.
   - **What the authors should do:** Fit the field dependence with the full Hamiltonian, with g_N free.

8. **Linewidths and the coherence outlook.**
   - The ENDOR lines in Fig. 3a are several MHz wide, yet their origin is not discussed.
   - Possible causes:
     - power broadening by V_NMR = 20 mV;
     - broadening from electron-spin flips under continuous ESR drive and tunnelling, which switch the nuclear frequency by ~A_z = 132 MHz;
     - tip-height noise.
   - This matters for the Conclusion's statement that coherence times "potentially" reach seconds. Under tunnelling and ESR drive, the electron spin flips at kHz–MHz rates and would dephase the nucleus far faster than T1.
   - **What the authors should do:** Analyse the linewidth against V_NMR, V_ESR and I_set, and temper the coherence statement accordingly.

C. Minor

9. **Fig. 1d is not a like-for-like comparison.** It uses a different tip and different V_bias/I_set at each field (75, 70, 90 mV). If it is used to contrast with ENDOR, matched conditions, or at least the same tip, would be needed.
10. **"Unambiguously demonstrate" (Introduction) is too strong** given concerns 1–2. The "~10 fA tunnelling current offset" explanation for amplitude differences between Fig. 2a and 2b should be backed by data or removed.
11. **Supplementary Note 1 assumes a Boltzmann nuclear distribution** in the ESR fit, while the main text stresses strong non-equilibrium spin pumping toward m_I = −5/2. Please clarify what is actually assumed.
12. **Notation:**
    - Eq. (S1) omits Planck's constant.
    - η is used both as a scalar asymmetry parameter and as a tensor in Eq. (1).
    - The definition of Q differs from the common convention 3e²qQ/[4I(2I−1)h]. State the convention when comparing with refs. 32 and 33.
13. **Figure details:**
    - The Fig. 3b caption mentions grey dashed lines, but the lines appear solid.
    - Fig. 1e's "blue" curve appears teal.
    - The SFig. 6 caption refers to "Fig. S3 a" but means SFig. 4a.
    - SFig. 8 uses V_ESR = 19 mV versus 20 mV elsewhere. Confirm.
14. **References and data availability:** Refs. 20 and 29 are duplicates, as are refs. 35 and 38. The Data availability section contains a placeholder "[URL]".
15. **Literature context:** The statement that NMR "without hybridization has not yet been shown" should be limited to STM platforms. Electrically and magnetically driven NMR of single nuclei is established in donors and NV centres, which are cited.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a quantitative drive-mechanism analysis: calculated matrix elements for each candidate mechanism, compared with V_NMR saturation curves at several fields and with the ↑/↓ intensity ratio. This would turn the Discussion's hypothesis into a test.
2. Extend ENDOR below 200 mT, into the strongly hybridized regime, with the same tip. A direct comparison of drive efficiency against the calculated admixture is the cleanest way to address the hybridization question.
3. Add a rate-equation model of the steady-state populations that reproduces Figs. 2–4 with stated parameters.
4. Pulsed ENDOR (a nuclear Rabi oscillation read out through the ESR peak) on the same system would directly give the drive strength. It would also substantiate the coherence outlook, even if done at a single field.
