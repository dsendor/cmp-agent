Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors synthesise spin-1 triangulene Haldane chains (N = 3 to 14) on ~25-30 ML Au(111) films grown on Nb(110), which keep the catalytic chemistry of gold and carry a proximity-induced gap with de Gennes-Saint James (dGSJ) resonances. With superconducting tips they report an even-odd alternation at the chain termini. Odd-N chains show in-gap Yu-Shiba-Rusinov (YSR) states. Even-N chains show only extra-gap singlet-triplet (ST) replicas. They explain this with a zero-bandwidth two-spin model in which the inter-edge exchange J renormalises the substrate coupling J_K. On-surface synthesis of a Haldane chain on a superconducting platform is new and technically valuable. The parity-dependent YSR response is a clear demonstration of a ground-state-dependent coupling to a condensate. As presented, the case for the mechanism rests mainly on the qualitative presence or absence of in-gap peaks, not on a quantitative test.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First demonstration that triangulene chains can be built by Ullmann coupling and cyclodehydrogenation on a proximitised gold film. The herringbone reconstruction, the Shockley state and the superconducting gap survive the anneal (Fig. 1, Fig. 2b, Fig. S1).
- A complete length series N = 3 to 14 with bond-resolved images and terminal spectra for every length (Figs. S3-S14). The parity alternation is visible directly in Fig. 3b.
- The edge exchange |J| from ST/TS energies agrees well with a BLBQ model whose parameters (J0 ~ 18 meV, beta ~ 0.09) are taken from earlier work on bulk Au(111), not refitted (Fig. 3e). This is a genuinely independent cross-check.
- The tip-induced dehydrogenation experiment (Figs. S15, S16) is a strong in-situ control. On an N = 8 chain, passivating one end removes the ST peaks and makes a YSR state appear at the other end, which directly links the absence of YSR to the partner edge spin.
- Spatial maps of the YSR, TS and collective-mode intensities (Figs. 2f, 4b, 4c, S5d, S11c) localise the edge and bulk excitations.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Is the absence of YSR states in even-N chains real, or is it limited by resolution and statistics? The central experimental claim is that even chains with N ≤ 10 have "no sub-gap YSR signal" (p. 6, p. 7; Fig. 3b, 3d). Yet several YSR binding energies in odd chains are only ~0.1-0.3 meV. The authors say "many YSR states overlap with the dGSJ peaks" and need two-Lorentzian fits to separate them (SI S2, last paragraph). Fig. 3c gives J_K*/J_K ~ 0.3-0.4 at |J|/J_K ~ 1.7, which is the N = 10 case (|J| ~ 1.0 meV, J_K ~ 0.6 meV). That implies a YSR binding energy of order 0.1-0.2 meV, comparable to the dGSJ linewidth in Fig. S10b. The paper does not:
   - state how many chains and termini were measured for each N;
   - say what fraction of each parity shows an in-gap peak;
   - give a quantitative detection limit for a YSR peak merged with the dGSJ peak.

   The text also contradicts itself on where even-chain YSR appears. The caption of Fig. 3d says it appears "only for N ≥ 14", while p. 7 says it is "already detected for N = 12 (Fig. S12)". The authors should:
   - provide a table listing, per N, the number of chains and termini, the δE_YSR or upper bound, and E_ST/E_TS;
   - fit even-chain spectra with the same tip-DOS deconvolution used in Fig. S2, with and without a YSR component, and report the largest YSR binding energy consistent with each even-chain spectrum;
   - plot that bound against the model's red band in Fig. 3d.

   Only then does "absent" become a quantitative statement that can discriminate the model.

2. The model is not tested quantitatively, and the claim that it is "independently validated" is stronger than the evidence. The model compared in Fig. 3d uses a single averaged J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV. Its distinctive prediction for odd chains is that δE_YSR should grow from 2J_K/3 toward J_K as N increases and |J| falls. That trend is hidden by a chain-to-chain spread in J_K that the authors themselves describe as fluctuating. The flagship N = 11 chain of Fig. 2 has δE_YSR ≈ 0.8 meV (SI S2). This lies outside the plotted range of Fig. 3d (caption: "a few cases were found outside the mapped range") and implies J_K ≈ 1.1 meV in the J → 0 limit, outside the quoted ±0.2 meV range.

   A within-chain test is available with the tools already in the paper. Measure δE_YSR on one end of a given chain before and after dehydrogenating the other end, and compare the ratio with the model's J_K*/J_K at that chain's measured |J|. For odd chains with large |J| (N = 3, 5, 7), the model predicts an increase in binding energy by up to ~3/2 after the cut. For even chains it predicts emergence from zero. So far only one N = 8 and one N = 14 chain are shown (Figs. S15, S16). "Several chains" were passivated to obtain J_K, but the before and after values are not given. The authors should report these pairs for all passivated chains, including at least a few odd chains, and show them against the curves in Fig. 3c. This would turn the qualitative parity argument into a quantitative test of the J_K* renormalisation.

B. Should address

3. There is no direct evidence for the claimed ground-state spin. The abstract and p. 7 assign odd chains an S = 1 (triplet) ground state and even chains an S = 0 ground state. The only evidence is inferred from the YSR pattern and the relative ST/TS intensities. There is no magnetic-field dependence, and no normal-state reference (for example above Tc, or with a normal tip at fields that quench the film's superconductivity) in which the TS/ST excitations would show the Zeeman splitting expected for each multiplicity. In addition, the tip-approach test for the weak-coupling regime (Fig. S15c) was done only on a dehydrogenated N = 14 end. The assignment of odd-chain YSR peaks to excitations from a free-spin ground state, rather than from a screened one, should be checked on intact odd chains, preferably at several N. The authors should add field-dependent or normal-state spectra on at least one odd and one even chain, and tip-approach series on intact odd chains.

4. The model's assumptions are not matched to this substrate, and its parameters are model-dependent. Eq. (S7) is a zero-bandwidth "single-site superconductor" with a BCS-like gap Δ. The substrate, however, is a proximitised film whose low-energy spectrum is dominated by dGSJ resonances, and the authors themselves say a Dynes function fails for it (SI S2). Several points need justifying or discussing:
   - Δ in Eqs. (S8, S9) is identified with E_dGSJ.
   - J_K is quoted in meV as if it were a physical exchange constant, although in a zero-bandwidth model it is an effective parameter.
   - J_K1 = J_K2 is assumed (Ref. 48), although the two ends of the same chain show clearly different YSR energies (e.g. Fig. S7b, S11a, S13b).

   The authors should do three things. First, show how δE_YSR depends on J and J_K when J_K1 ≠ J_K2. Second, state which conclusions survive if J_K is treated only as an effective parameter. Third, ideally benchmark the parity effect against an NRG or finite-bandwidth calculation that includes a proximitised DOS (Refs. 27, 38).

5. The claim of "topological origin" is overstated. The text says the YSR maps "establish the topological origin of the observed YSR states" (p. 5) and that the edge states "remain robust in a superconducting environment" (p. 3, p. 9). A YSR state localised on the terminal triangulene with zigzag-edge shape (Fig. 2f) is what any unpaired spin on a terminal unit would give. The spin-1/2 fractional character is supported mainly by the ST/TS energies (Fig. 3e) and by the two-spin model, not by the YSR maps. Also, J_K ≪ Δ ≪ J0 (0.6 meV against 18 meV) means survival of the edge states is expected rather than tested. The authors should:
   - rephrase these claims;
   - add the natural reference structures, an isolated triangulene (N = 1, S = 1, expected to show YSR) and an N = 2 dimer (singlet with large J, expected to show none), if present on the surface (the 3:1 precursor ratio should produce them);
   - compare the measured YSR decay length along the chain with the edge-state localisation length expected from the BLBQ model.

6. The collective-mode claims are qualitative. The claims that the bulk collective spin (CS) modes are "decoupled from the superconductor by the much larger Haldane gap" (abstract, p. 9) and "perfectly reproduced" by the BLBQ model (p. 9) are not supported by a quantitative comparison. Fig. 4c overlays circles on the data without a fit metric. No energies or linewidths are compared with the same chains on bulk Au(111) (Ref. 13), which would be the natural test of whether the superconductor renormalises the modes. The CO frustrated-translation mode at ~35 meV overlaps the predicted CS energies (Fig. 4a, 4d, S8c, N = 3 in Fig. 4d), so some assignments near 30-35 meV are ambiguous. The authors should:
   - tabulate measured and calculated CS energies for each N;
   - compare them with published bulk-Au(111) values;
   - confirm the assignments near 35 meV with a metal (non-CO) tip.

7. Peak extraction and uncertainties are not reported. δE_YSR, E_ST and E_TS in Figs. 3d and 3e are taken from raw peak separations. This was justified by a single deconvolution (N = 11, δE ≈ 0.8 meV, error < 3%; SI S2), but the NDR-induced shift is likely to differ for YSR peaks lying within the dGSJ linewidth. Figs. 3d and 3e have no error bars. Some data points have no spectrum shown:
   - The N = 3 TS energy (~12 meV in Fig. 3e) is not shown anywhere; Fig. S3 covers only ±5 mV.
   - N = 12 (E_ST ~0.5 meV) and N = 13 (E_TS ~0.5 meV) are not monotonic as exponential decay would require.

   The authors should apply the deconvolution of Fig. S2 to every spectrum, or at least to those with δE < 0.3 meV or E_ST/TS < 1 meV. They should then add error bars and provide the missing spectra.

8. The attribution of J_K variations to the herringbone reconstruction (p. 6, Ref. 45) is speculative. No correlation is shown between YSR energy and the adsorption site of the terminal unit (fcc, hcp or elbow). Film thickness and local gap variations across the Au film are not quantified, and the number of film preparations is not given. Because the J_K spread is what masks the model test in concern 2, the authors should correlate δE_YSR with the local herringbone position and the local dGSJ energy for the chains they measured.

9. The explanation for TS peaks being weaker than one third of the ST intensity (SI S4: a "stronger interaction J_K* of the triplet states with the substrate") is speculative. It is used to support the parity picture without a calculation. The authors should either compute TS and ST cross-sections including J_K in the two-site model, or label this explanation as conjecture.

C. Minor

10. On p. 3 the herringbone reconstruction is attributed to "Fig. 1a"; it is shown in Fig. 1c.
11. Section references do not match the Supplementary Information. The main text cites "Section S6" for the BLBQ model and spin models (p. 5, Fig. 3e caption, p. 9) and "Section S4" for chains with a single edge state (Fig. 3d caption). The SI has only S1-S5: BLBQ is in S4 and the passivation data are in S3.
12. The Fig. 3d caption says "Symbols and shaded regions show model predictions", while the text calls the green dots experimental data. Please clarify which symbols are data and which are model.
13. SI S2 states that δ^sim E_YSR = Δ_sample − ε_YSR is "independent of the Δ_sample value used". As written, the expression depends explicitly on Δ_sample. Please explain, and explain how Δ_sample = 1.2 meV was "precalibrated". In Fig. S2c it is unclear which orange peaks correspond to the YSR state.
14. The bias axis of Fig. S1b is labelled "mV", but the surface-state onset is at −0.5 V. The SI uses T = 1.3 K (S4) while the main text and methods use 1.2 K.
15. In Fig. S17a the red dashed curve, described as an exponential decay of the edge excitations, levels off near ~2.5 meV. The measured E_ST continues to decrease to ~1 meV at N = 10 and ~0.5 meV at N = 12. Please correct or explain the curve.
16. SI S5 calls the J < 0 limit (an S = 1 coupled to two channels) a "two-channel Kondo scenario" and cites Jones and Varma (a two-impurity Kondo paper). Two-channel Kondo usually refers to the overscreened S = 1/2 case. Please reword.
17. Typos: "ends f the" (Fig. 2 caption); "Spin model of for the" (S4 title); "Phy. Rev. B" (Ref. 43). SI Refs. [3] and [6] are duplicates.
18. Fig. 3c has an inverted y axis and a decreasing x axis (|J|/J_K from 10^1 to 10^-1), which is hard to read. Consider standard orientation, or mark the N values of the measured chains on the curve.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a summary table covering all chains measured: N, the number of chains and termini, δE_YSR at each end (with uncertainty, or an upper bound for even N), E_ST or E_TS, and the BLBQ prediction. Base Fig. 3d on the full dataset with error bars.
2. Use the dehydrogenation technique as a systematic within-chain test of J_K*/J_K for both parities and several lengths, and plot the results directly on Fig. 3c.
3. Add normal-state or magnetic-field spectra on representative odd and even chains to confirm the triplet and singlet ground states directly. Compare the CS-mode energies and linewidths with bulk Au(111) data to support the claim that the modes are decoupled.
4. Present the two-site model with asymmetric couplings and a proximitised substrate DOS, or benchmark it against NRG. Recast the "topological" and "robust" language so it matches what the YSR data alone can establish.
