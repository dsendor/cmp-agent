Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors make spin-1 triangulene Haldane chains (N = 3 to 14) on a Au(111) film of about 25 to 30 ML grown on superconducting Nb(110). With superconducting-tip STS they report an even-odd alternation at the chain ends. Odd-N chains show sub-gap Yu-Shiba-Rusinov (YSR) resonances. Even-N chains instead show extra-gap singlet-triplet (ST) excitations and no YSR state. The authors explain this with a two-site quantum-spin model in which the inter-edge exchange J renormalizes the substrate coupling J_K to an effective J_K*. The main advances are the on-surface synthesis of graphene-based spin chains on a proximitized substrate and the length-parity switching of the YSR response. The model builds on the authors' earlier work on dimers and two-impurity YSR states. The qualitative even-odd observation is convincing. The quantitative model test, the statistics behind the parity "rule" and the topology-related wording need firmer support.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The synthesis works on a proximitized superconductor. Bond-resolved images confirm intact chains of known N from 3 to 14, and the herringbone reconstruction and Shockley surface state are preserved (Fig. 2, Fig. S1, Figs. S3 to S14).
- The even-odd alternation is visible across a full length series at both termini (Fig. 3a,b). It is paired with ST/TS energies that decrease monotonically with N and agree with a BLBQ model whose parameters (J0 ~ 18 meV, beta ~ 0.09) come from earlier Au(111) work (Fig. 3e).
- There is a direct control on the same chain. Dehydrogenating one end of an N = 8 chain removes the ST peaks and creates a YSR state at the other end (Fig. S16). This shows that the edge spins of even chains are not intrinsically decoupled from the substrate.
- The tip DOS is deconvolved with an Arnold-type model to check that the NDR-shifted peak positions are reliable (Section S2, Fig. S2).
- The collective spin modes are mapped in real space with node patterns that grow with energy (Fig. 4b,c, Fig. S5d), which is attractive in its own right.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The parity "rule" rests on one or two chains per length, and the statistics are not given. Main text p. 7 states that "all odd-length chains host bound YSR states ... while even-length chains show no sub-gap YSR signal". However, Figs. S3 to S14 show one chain per N, except N = 11 (two chains, Fig. S11) and N = 8 (two chains for the CS modes only, Fig. S8c). Fig. 3d has several green points for some odd N, so more chains were measured, but how many is never stated. The caption also says "a few cases were found outside the mapped range". The central claim is a deterministic rule across N, so the reader needs to know how often it holds. The authors should add a table listing, for each N:
   - the number of chains and termini measured;
   - the number with a resolvable YSR peak, an ST/TS peak, or neither;
   - the binding energies at each end;
   - the chain conformation (the chains have different kink sequences, e.g. Figs. S10 and S13) and the herringbone domain (fcc/hcp/ridge) of each terminus.
   Every exception should be reported, including any odd chain without a resolvable YSR state and any even chain with one.

2. The quantitative test of the two-site model (Fig. 3d) cannot separate the proposed renormalization from site-to-site scatter in J_K. The model predicts a binding energy of 3J_K/4 for a free spin-1/2 and J_K/2 for the S = 1 (odd, |J| >> J_K) limit. That is a factor of 2/3. The input J_K = 0.6 +/- 0.2 meV already has a spread of about 33%. The blue shaded band in Fig. 3d therefore covers most of 0.1 to 0.5 meV, and the odd-chain points sit inside it whether or not the renormalization operates. The text says the model "reproduces the experimental evolution well", but this agreement is not discriminating.
   The decisive experiment is available with the authors' own tool: dehydrogenate one end of several odd chains and compare the YSR binding energy at the opposite end before and after. On the same terminus, with the same J_K and adsorption site, the model predicts that the binding energy rises from about J_K/2 to 3J_K/4 for short chains. The ratio should approach 1 for long chains where |J| ~ J_K. The same before/after protocol on several even chains (only N = 8 is shown, Fig. S16; N = 14 in Fig. S15 is a different regime) would give J_K* versus |J| chain by chain, free of the J_K scatter.
   Also needed:
   - how many chains entered the J_K = 0.6 +/- 0.2 meV average;
   - the formula used to convert binding energy into J_K;
   - whether J_K is a dimensionless product with nu0 absorbed or an energy.
   For reference, Fig. S15 gives a binding energy of 0.36 meV, which implies J_K ~ 0.48 meV under the 3J_K/4 relation.

B. Should address

3. The absence of YSR states in longer even chains is limited by resolution, and the text contradicts itself on where YSR states reappear. For N = 10 (|J| ~ 1 meV, |J|/J_K ~ 1.7), Fig. 3c gives J_K*/J_K of a few tenths. That implies a binding energy of order 0.1 meV, which is below the width of the dGSJ peaks in Figs. S10 and S12. A YSR state merged into the dGSJ peak cannot be told apart from an absent one by eye. The authors should apply the Section S2 deconvolution to the even-chain spectra and quote upper bounds on the binding energy for N = 4 to 12, so the even-chain points in Fig. 3d carry error bars.
   On the contradiction: the main text (p. 7) says emergent YSR signal is "already detected for N = 12 (Fig. S12)". The Fig. 3 caption says it "appears only for N >= 14 (Fig. S14)". Fig. S12 itself only describes a "particle-hole asymmetry ... attributed to incipient emergence". These statements need to be reconciled.

4. The ground-state spin of the odd chains (S = 1 triplet) is inferred rather than measured. The evidence is the weak TS peaks (Figs. S7, S9, S11, S13). The authors note that these are "frequently below one third" of the ST intensity, which is weaker than the factor of 3 expected from thermal sharing over the triplet. This shortfall is then attributed post hoc to a stronger J_K* of the triplet. The two-site model also simply assumes that J changes sign with parity (Section S5, "J is positive ... for even chains and negative ... for odd chains").
   The authors should either:
   - give an independent check of the triplet ground state, for example the Zeeman response of the TS step or YSR state in a field below the Nb critical field, or normal-state spectra above it showing a Kondo or Zeeman signature on odd chains only; or
   - show that the full BLBQ calculation of the edge-state sign and magnitude of J (Section S4) reproduces the TS/ST intensity ratios quantitatively, including J_K*.

5. The model is applied outside its stated range. Section S5 assumes J_K << Delta, but J_K = 0.6 meV against Delta_sample = 1.2 meV (Section S2) is not small. The binding energies in Fig. 3d reach 0.5 meV out of about 1.2 meV. The only evidence for weak coupling is the tip-approach shift in Fig. S15c, on a single dehydrogenated N = 14 chain. That interpretation also assumes the tip reduces J_K rather than, for example, changing the junction or adding tip-induced potential scattering.
   The authors should:
   - show how the J_K* curves in Fig. 3c and the bands in Fig. 3d change when J_K/Delta ~ 0.5 is used, or cite the NRG benchmark (refs. 27, 28 of the SI) at comparable parameters;
   - repeat the tip-approach test on at least one undehydrogenated odd chain;
   - state explicitly what Delta is in the model: the Nb gap, the dGSJ energy, or Delta_sample = 1.2 meV.

6. The topology and "decoupling by the Haldane gap" language goes beyond the data. The abstract and conclusion say fractionalization "survives" and the edge spins "remain robust", and that the collective modes are "decoupled from the superconductor by the much larger Haldane gap". The data show localized spin-1/2-like end moments with parity-alternating coupling. That is consistent with the AKLT picture, but it is not a test of symmetry protection or robustness against perturbation.
   For the CS modes, the only evidence of decoupling is qualitative agreement with a free-chain BLBQ model ("perfectly reproduced", p. 9). The authors should do one of two things:
   - Compare the CS and ST energies and linewidths on Au/Nb quantitatively with the same chains on bulk Au(111) (ref. 13), give a numerical residual between the measured and calculated mode energies in Fig. 4c,d, and state whether any CS mode shows a substrate-induced shift or broadening.
   - Soften these statements.

7. The attribution of the J_K scatter to the herringbone reconstruction (p. 6, "probably due to the Au(111) herringbone reconstruction") is untested. The two ends of the same N = 11 chain, and two different N = 11 chains (Fig. S11a,b), give clearly different binding energies. The authors should correlate the binding energy with the local herringbone site, or with the terminal-unit orientation and conformation, using the topographs they already have. This scatter is what limits the quantitative test in concern 2.

8. The extracted energies need a single table with uncertainties and a consistent extraction rule. Section S2 says peak positions are used directly, with dGSJ-referenced energies, but some values are hard to reconcile with the figures:
   - Fig. S13 quotes E_TS ~ 0.5 meV, while the marker sits near 2.9 mV, about 0.8 meV above the dGSJ peak.
   - Fig. 3e has an experimental |J| for N = 14 (and N = 3), but Fig. S14 shows no ST feature, and Fig. S3 shows only the low-bias window for N = 3.
   - The N = 5 TS energy cannot be read precisely from Fig. S5c.
   Please tabulate E_ST/E_TS, binding energy and their uncertainties for every chain, and state for each whether it was taken from peak positions or from a deconvolved fit. Also state that the BLBQ parameters in Fig. 3e are taken from ref. 13 and not refitted, if that is the case. If so, it strengthens the comparison.

9. The superconducting platform after synthesis is documented only by citation. The statement that "the reconstruction and the superconducting gap are unaltered by the annealing procedure [36]" relies on a PhD thesis. Delta_sample = 1.2 meV is "precalibrated" without explanation. The authors should show in the SI:
   - substrate spectra before and after the 200 and 340 C anneals;
   - the spread of the dGSJ energy across terraces near the measured chains;
   - how the film thickness (25 to 30 ML) was determined.
   Variations in dGSJ energy across the surface feed directly into the binding energy defined relative to the dGSJ peak.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Make single-end dehydrogenation on the same chain the centrepiece of the quantitative test, using several odd and even chains. Plot each chain's J_K* (before) against J_K (after) as a function of |J|. This removes the J_K scatter that currently limits Fig. 3d.
2. Add a statistics table covering all measured chains (N, both ends, YSR/ST/TS energies, conformation, herringbone site), and report the success rate of the parity rule.
3. Give an independent ground-state spin check for the odd chains, through a magnetic-field dependence or normal-state spectra, and compare TS/ST intensity ratios quantitatively with the model.
4. Compare the CS-mode energies and widths quantitatively with the same chains on bulk Au(111), to support or temper the "decoupled by the Haldane gap" statement.
