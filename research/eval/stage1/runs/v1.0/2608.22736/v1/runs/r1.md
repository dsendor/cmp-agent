Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors grow triangulene spin-1 (Haldane) chains on thin Au(111) films proximitized by Nb(110). With superconducting tips they report that odd-length chains show Yu-Shiba-Rusinov (YSR) states at their ends, while even-length chains show only singlet-triplet (ST) excitations above the gap. They explain this alternation with a zero-bandwidth two-site model in which the inter-edge exchange J renormalizes the substrate coupling J_K. On-surface synthesis of Haldane chains on a superconducting substrate is new and technically valuable. Seeing the S=0/S=1 ground-state parity of a molecular spin chain through the presence or absence of YSR states is an appealing result. The headline mechanism, however, is supported mostly qualitatively. The quantitative check of the model is weak.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The platform is new. The Au/Ag/Nb(110) films keep the herringbone reconstruction, the Au(111) Shockley state (Fig. S1) and the catalytic activity needed for Ullmann coupling and cyclodehydrogenation, together with a hard proximity gap with sharp dGSJ peaks (Figs. 1d, 2b). This is likely to be used by others.
- The length series is broad: N = 3 to 14 with bond-resolved images, plus wide-bias and low-bias spectra for each length (Figs. S3-S14).
- The parity alternation is visible directly in the raw spectra: YSR peaks inside the gap for N = 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 and 13, and extra-gap ST peaks for N = 4, 6, 8 and 10 (Fig. 3b).
- The tip-induced dehydrogenation of one end of an N=8 chain (Fig. S16) is a strong single-chain control. The ST peaks vanish and a YSR state appears at the other end of the same chain.
- The experimental |J|(N) agrees well with the BLBQ model using literature parameters (Fig. 3e). The spatial maps of the collective modes (Fig. 4) add a real-space view of the bulk excitations.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The quantitative test of the two-site mechanism is weak, and the flagship chain does not fit the stated parameters.**
   - *Where.* Fig. 3d, the main-text paragraph on "validated this picture", Section S2, Fig. S11.
   - *Why the model is barely constrained.* With J_K = 0.6 meV, the model's binding energy (δE_YSR = Δ − (E_i − E_GS)) is about J_K/2 ≈ 0.3 meV for odd chains with |J| ≫ J_K and 3J_K/4 ≈ 0.45 meV for free spins. The ±0.2 meV band on J_K maps to a shaded region roughly 0.15-0.5 meV wide. That region covers almost the whole range where odd-chain points fall. The scatter of the odd-chain points (0.15-0.55 meV in Fig. 3d) is as large as the predicted odd-chain effect (the factor 2/3 renormalization). The authors themselves attribute this scatter to site-dependent J_K. So the comparison cannot tell "J_K* = 2J_K/3" apart from "no renormalization".
   - *The flagship chain is outside the model.* The N=11 chain in Fig. 2 and Section S2 has δE_YSR ≈ 0.8 meV, and Fig. S11a is listed as "outside the mapped range". In this model an odd chain with |J| ≈ 0.8 meV ~ J_K can only reach about 0.45 meV unless J_K ≈ 1.6 meV or more. That is far outside 0.6 ± 0.2 meV.
   - *What this means for the claim.* The statement that the mechanism is "independently validated" and that "the model reproduces the experimental evolution well" rests on the presence or absence of YSR states. It does not rest on quantitative agreement.
   - *What to do.* Test the renormalization chain by chain, as in Fig. S16, but on odd chains. Measure δE_YSR at one end, dehydrogenate the other end, and measure again. The model predicts the binding energy rises by about 50% (from J_K/2 to 3J_K/4) in short odd chains and by much less in long ones. Also report all chains in Fig. 3d, including the out-of-range ones, and state how many there are.

2. **"Absence" of YSR states in even chains has to be turned into an upper bound.**
   - *What the model predicts.* In the model, even chains do not lose their YSR states. The states move to the gap edge, since E_i − E_GS → Δ as J/J_K grows. So the experimental claim is really that δE_YSR is below the resolution set by the dGSJ peak width.
   - *Signs of hidden weight.* In Figs. S6, S8b, S10 and S12 the dGSJ peaks on even-chain ends are visibly lower and reshaped compared with the substrate reference. This may be unresolved YSR weight, as the authors themselves argue for N=12 and N=14 (Fig. S14 inset).
   - *Where the model and data may disagree.* Fig. 3d plots even-chain experimental points at δE ≈ 0, apparently without an error bar or a fitting procedure. The red model band for N = 10-14 predicts finite binding energies (about 0.1-0.25 meV) that are comparable to those of some odd chains.
   - *What to do.* For every even-N end, apply the same two-peak (dGSJ + YSR) lineshape fit used for odd chains (Section S2) and give either an extracted δE or a quantified upper bound. Plot these with error bars against the model band. This decides whether the even-chain data agree with the model or only with "no YSR".

3. **Statistics of the parity rule are not given.**
   - *What is shown.* Each length is represented by one chain in the SI, two for N=8 and N=11. Fig. 3d shows several odd-chain points per N but no count of chains or ends.
   - *What is missing.* The paper does not say how many chains of each length were measured, how many ends each, and whether any odd chain lacked YSR states or any short even chain (N ≤ 10) showed them. The main claim is a parity alternation that holds "for all lengths". Its strength depends on these numbers and on whether chains with defects, kinks or ends at particular herringbone sites were excluded.
   - *What to do.* Add a table: N, number of chains, number of ends, YSR yes/no, δE_YSR, E_ST/E_TS, tip used. State the exclusion criteria.

B. Should address

4. **Mapping the proximitized film onto a single-site BCS model.**
   - *Where the model's Δ comes from.* The two-site model (Eq. S7-S9) couples each edge spin to a single BCS site with gap Δ. The experiment uses a ballistic proximitized film whose spectrum is dominated by dGSJ states below Δ_Nb (Fig. 1d). The binding energy is measured from E_dGSJ. The authors' own earlier work (ref. 38) shows that YSR states in proximitized films hybridize with dGSJ states and are pushed away from them near the gap edge. That is exactly where the small even-chain and long-chain binding energies lie.
   - *J_K is a model parameter.* The single-site model is described as "qualitative", yet it is used quantitatively in Figs. 3c and 3d. J_K = 0.6 meV is a parameter of this model, not a physical exchange.
   - *What to do.* State which gap plays Δ in the model. Check the J_K*/J_K curves of Fig. 3c against NRG, or a model with a proximitized normal layer, for at least the parameters relevant here (|J|/J_K ≈ 1-20, J_K/Δ ≈ 0.5). Discuss how dGSJ-YSR hybridization affects the extracted δE.

5. **The weak-coupling regime is established on only one end.**
   - *Where.* The tip-approach test (Fig. S15c) is shown for one dehydrogenated N=14 end, a free spin-1/2.
   - *What it supports.* The model and the interpretation of all odd chains assume the weak-coupling (free-spin ground state) regime for the S=1 triplet as well. In a two-channel S=1 case the screening and the position of the quantum phase transition differ.
   - *A tension.* J_K ≈ 0.6 meV against Δ ≈ 1.2 meV is not "J_K ≪ Δ", as stated in Fig. S15 and Section S5.
   - *Also.* Fig. S15c shows the YSR state merging into the dGSJ peaks as the tip approaches. This is also how a YSR state hybridizing with dGSJ might behave.
   - *What to do.* Repeat the approach on several odd-chain ends (including the 0.8 meV N=11 end) and on a passivated end with larger binding energy.

6. **The passivation control needs more detail and more statistics.**
   - *How many chains.* J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV is "averaged from chains with a single edge state", but only N=14 (δE = 0.36 meV, which implies J_K ≈ 0.48 meV via 3J_K/4) and N=8 (Fig. S16) are shown. The main text points to "Section S4" for this, but S4 is the BLBQ model.
   - *Spin counting.* Dehydrogenating one site of a terminal S=1 triangulene leaves, at most, an S=1/2 unit. The text should say explicitly why this quenches the fractional edge spin at that end rather than leaving a residual spin.
   - *Bonding to Au.* Give evidence that the σ-radical bonding to Au does not change the adsorption, and hence J_K, of the opposite end. A before/after comparison of a passivated odd chain would help here.
   - *What to do.* State the number of passivated chains and the spread of J_K.

7. **The topological and fractionalization claims go beyond the evidence.**
   - *What the claims are.* The abstract and main text state that the YSR maps "establish the topological origin of the observed YSR states" and that "topological edge states … remain robust in a superconducting environment".
   - *What the evidence shows.* End localization and the even-odd alternation of the ground-state spin follow from Lieb-Mattis for any finite antiferromagnetic chain on a bipartite lattice. They do not on their own establish symmetry-protected topological order or robustness. No perturbation (defect, substrate site, chain kink) is used to test protection.
   - *The S=1/2 assignment.* The spin-1/2 character of each end is inferred from the model, not measured.
   - *What to do.* Either soften the wording, or add evidence specific to the Haldane phase. For example, show that a defect inside the chain creates new edge spins with YSR signatures, or show the N-dependence of the edge-state localization length.

8. **The claim that the collective modes are decoupled from the superconductor is not tested.**
   - *What is shown.* Fig. 4 and its text state that the CS modes are "decoupled from the superconductor by the much larger Haldane gap", and that their spatial patterns are "perfectly reproduced" by the BLBQ model. No measurement addresses the decoupling. The comparison with the model is visual (circles and stars).
   - *Energy axis.* In SIS spectra all inelastic thresholds are shifted by Δ_tip + Δ_sample ≈ 2.1 meV. Fig. S8c uses V − Δ/e, but Figs. 3a, 4a, 4b and 4d are labelled in sample bias, and Fig. 4c is labelled "Energy (meV)". State which correction was applied before comparing with the model.
   - *What to do.* Give a table of measured CS and ST energies against the BLBQ values (J0 = 18 meV, β = 0.09 taken from ref. 13, not fitted; say so explicitly) and against the values reported on bulk Au(111) in ref. 13. Agreement there is the evidence for decoupling.

9. **The J_K variations are attributed to the herringbone, and chain conformation is not considered.**
   - *Where.* The text attributes the scatter in δE_YSR to "probably … the Au(111) herringbone reconstruction" without showing any correlation.
   - *Conformation may matter.* The chains have different cis/trans kink sequences (e.g. Figs. 2c, S10a and S13a). These may change both J (through the end-unit geometry) and J_K.
   - *A within-chain test.* The two ends of the same chain sometimes differ markedly (Figs. S7b and S9b), which bears directly on this.
   - *What to do.* Correlate δE_YSR with end-unit location (fcc, hcp or soliton region) and with conformation. Report both ends of each chain in Fig. 3d.

10. **The |J| values from weak triplet-singlet features in long odd chains carry unknown uncertainty.**
    - *Where.* For N = 11 and 13, E_TS ≈ 0.5-0.8 meV is read from weak shoulders that sit on the dGSJ negative-differential-resistance dips (Figs. S11 and S13). These values set the |J| used in Fig. 3e and as model input in Fig. 3d.
    - *What to do.* Give error bars for all |J| in Fig. 3e, describe how the shoulders were located, and propagate these uncertainties into the model band of Fig. 3d.

C. Minor

11. **Arithmetic in Section S2.** It states δ^sim E_YSR = Δ_sample − ε_YSR = 1.2 − 0.47 = 0.78 meV, but 1.2 − 0.47 = 0.73 meV. The claim that the result is "independent of the Δ_sample value used" also needs explaining. Fig. S2b shows "Δ_s and Δ_t" cut off in the panel title.
12. **Cross-references.**
    - The main text refers to "Section S6" for the BLBQ model and to "Section S4" for single-edge chains. The SI has S4 (BLBQ) and S5 (two-site), and the passivation data are in S3.
    - "Supplementary Fig. 5" should be Fig. S5.
    - The herringbone is described as shown in "Fig. 1a"; it is Fig. 1c.
    - The SI repeats ref. [3] as ref. [6].
13. **Fig. 2e caption.** It says the YSR resonances lie "at energy J_K* below the dGSJ peaks". The binding energy is not J_K*. Use δE_YSR.
14. **Fig. S1b units.** The bias axis is labelled mV with range ±1, but the surface-state onset is quoted at −0.5 V.
15. **Temperature.** The base temperature is 1.2 K in the Methods and 1.3 K in Section S4. State which was used for the Boltzmann weights.
16. **Modulation amplitude.** The lock-in modulation is given as "a few mV" for wide-bias spectra. Give the actual values for Figs. 3a and 4, since they set the resolution of the CS and low-N ST features.
17. **Inverted axes.** Figs. 3c and 3d use inverted axes (x decreasing, y increasing downward). This makes them hard to read. Consider standard orientation or a clear note.
18. **Fig. 2f orbital shape.** The YSR map is said to "resemble radical states at zigzag edges". A computed edge-state LDOS (tight-binding or DFT) for the terminal unit would make this quantitative.
19. **TS/ST intensity ratio.** The suggestion in Section S4 that TS peaks are below one third of ST because of a stronger J_K* for triplets is speculative. Either support it with the two-site model or label it as conjecture.
20. **Typos.** "Only the ends f the" (Fig. 2 caption); "Spin model of for" (S4 title); "Phy. Rev. B" (ref. 43); "can couple with each other … an exponentially decaying correlation length" (p. 5) is garbled.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Passivate one end of several odd chains of different lengths and track δE_YSR before and after at the opposite end. This is the most direct, within-chain test of the 2J_K/3 renormalization and would make the mechanism quantitative.
2. Give a complete statistics table (concern 3) and a version of Fig. 3d that pairs each odd chain's measured |J| with its own passivated-end J_K, if available, instead of a global J_K band.
3. Back the single-site model with NRG or a proximitized-film calculation for the relevant |J|/J_K and J_K/Δ. Fit the full SIS lineshapes for the even chains to put an upper bound on hidden YSR weight.
4. Compare the ST, TS and CS energies directly with the bulk Au(111) data of ref. 13 for the same N. Agreement would support the decoupling claims for the bulk modes; a systematic shift would measure the substrate's renormalization of the chain.
