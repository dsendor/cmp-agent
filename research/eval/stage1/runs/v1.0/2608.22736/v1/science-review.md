A. Must address

1. **The quantitative test of the two-site model cannot tell the proposed J_K renormalization apart from scatter in J_K, and the flagship chain does not fit the stated parameters.** In the model, the binding energy (δE_YSR = Δ − (E_i − E_GS)) is about 3J_K/4 for a free spin-1/2 and about J_K/2 for an odd chain with |J| ≫ J_K, a factor of 2/3. The input J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV already has a spread of about 33%, so the blue shaded band in Fig. 3d covers roughly 0.1-0.5 meV. The odd-chain points (0.15-0.55 meV) sit inside it whether or not the renormalization operates, and their scatter, which the authors attribute to site-dependent J_K, is as large as the predicted effect. The main-text statements that the mechanism is "independently validated" and that the model "reproduces the experimental evolution well" therefore rest on the presence or absence of YSR states, not on quantitative agreement. In addition, the N = 11 chain of Fig. 2 and Section S2 has δE_YSR ≈ 0.8 meV and is listed as "outside the mapped range" (Fig. S11a; Fig. 3d caption: "a few cases were found outside the mapped range"). An odd chain with |J| ≈ 0.8 meV ~ J_K can reach at most about 0.45 meV unless J_K is about 1.1-1.6 meV, well outside 0.6 ± 0.2 meV. The J_K average itself is under-documented: it is "averaged from chains with a single edge state", but only the N = 14 chain (Fig. S15, δE = 0.36 meV, implying J_K ≈ 0.48 meV via 3J_K/4) and the N = 8 chain (Fig. S16) are shown, and "several chains" were passivated without before/after values. The authors should make a within-chain test the centrepiece: on several odd and even chains of different lengths, measure δE_YSR at one end before and after dehydrogenating the other end, and plot each chain's J_K* (before) against J_K (after) as a function of its measured |J| on the curves of Fig. 3c. On the same terminus and adsorption site, the model predicts a rise of about 50% (J_K/2 to 3J_K/4) for short odd chains (N = 3, 5, 7), a ratio approaching 1 for long odd chains where |J| ~ J_K, and emergence from zero for even chains. They should also report all chains in Fig. 3d, including the out-of-range ones, with their number; state how many chains entered the J_K average and its spread; give the formula used to convert binding energy into J_K; and state whether J_K is an energy or a dimensionless product with ν0 absorbed.

2. **The "absence" of YSR states in even chains must be turned into a quantitative upper bound, and the text contradicts itself on where even-chain YSR states reappear.** In the model, even chains do not lose their YSR states; the states move toward the gap edge as J/J_K grows (E_i − E_GS → Δ). For N = 10 (|J| ≈ 1.0 meV, |J|/J_K ≈ 1.7), Fig. 3c gives J_K*/J_K ≈ 0.3-0.4, implying a binding energy of order 0.1-0.2 meV, comparable to the dGSJ linewidth in Figs. S10b and S12 and to binding energies of some odd chains. The red model band for N = 10-14 in Fig. 3d predicts finite binding energies of about 0.1-0.25 meV, yet the even-chain experimental points are plotted at δE ≈ 0 without error bars or a stated fitting procedure. The authors themselves note that "many YSR states overlap with the dGSJ peaks" and need two-Lorentzian fits for odd chains (Section S2). In Figs. S6, S8b, S10 and S12 the dGSJ peaks at even-chain ends are visibly lower and reshaped compared with the substrate reference, which may be unresolved YSR weight, as the authors argue for N = 12 and 14 (Fig. S14 inset). On where the YSR signal reappears, p. 7 says it is "already detected for N = 12 (Fig. S12)", the Fig. 3 caption says it "appears only for N ≥ 14 (Fig. S14)", and Fig. S12 itself mentions only a "particle-hole asymmetry ... attributed to incipient emergence". The authors should fit every even-chain end (N = 4 to 12) with the same tip-DOS deconvolution and two-peak (dGSJ + YSR) lineshape used in Section S2 and Fig. S2, with and without a YSR component, report either an extracted δE or the largest binding energy consistent with each spectrum, plot these with error bars against the red band in Fig. 3d, and reconcile the N = 12 versus N ≥ 14 statements. Only then can the even-chain data discriminate the model from simple "no YSR".

3. **The statistics behind the parity rule are not given.** The main text (p. 7) states that "all odd-length chains host bound YSR states ... while even-length chains show no sub-gap YSR signal", a deterministic rule across N. Yet Figs. S3-S14 show one chain per length, except N = 11 (two chains, Fig. S11) and N = 8 (two chains, for the CS modes only, Fig. S8c). Fig. 3d has several points for some odd N, so more chains were measured, but the number is never stated, and the caption concedes that some cases fall outside the mapped range. The strength of the claim depends on how often it holds and on whether chains with defects, kinks or ends at particular herringbone sites were excluded. The authors should add a table listing, for each N: the number of chains and termini measured; the number with a resolvable YSR peak, an ST/TS peak, or neither; δE_YSR (or an upper bound) at each end; E_ST/E_TS; the tip used; the chain conformation (kink sequence, e.g. Figs. S10, S13); and the herringbone domain (fcc, hcp, ridge) of each terminus. They should state the exclusion criteria and report every exception, including any odd chain without a resolvable YSR state and any short even chain (N ≤ 10) with one, and give the success rate of the rule.

B. Should address

4. **The single-site BCS model is not matched to the proximitized film, and it is used outside its stated range.** The two-site model (Eqs. S7-S9) couples each edge spin to a zero-bandwidth single BCS site with gap Δ, whereas the substrate is a ballistic proximitized film whose spectrum is dominated by dGSJ states below Δ_Nb (Fig. 1d), for which the authors say a Dynes function fails (Section S2). The binding energy is measured from E_dGSJ, and the authors' own earlier work (ref. 38) shows that YSR states in proximitized films hybridize with dGSJ states and are pushed away from them near the gap edge, which is exactly where the small even-chain and long-chain binding energies lie. Section S5 and Fig. S15 assume J_K ≪ Δ, but J_K ≈ 0.6 meV against Δ_sample ≈ 1.2 meV is not small, and binding energies in Fig. 3d reach 0.5 meV out of about 1.2 meV. The model is called "qualitative" yet used quantitatively in Figs. 3c and 3d, and J_K is quoted in meV as if it were a physical exchange although it is an effective parameter of this model. The model also assumes J_K1 = J_K2 (footnote/ref. 48), although the two ends of the same chain show clearly different YSR energies (e.g. Figs. S7b, S11a, S13b). The authors should state which gap plays Δ in the model (the Nb gap, E_dGSJ or Δ_sample = 1.2 meV); show how the J_K*/J_K curves in Fig. 3c and the bands in Fig. 3d change for J_K/Δ ≈ 0.5 and |J|/J_K ≈ 1-20, ideally benchmarked against NRG or a finite-bandwidth model with a proximitized normal layer (SI refs. 27, 28, 38); discuss how dGSJ-YSR hybridization affects the extracted δE; show how δE_YSR depends on J and J_K when J_K1 ≠ J_K2, including the effect on the even-chain prediction; and state which conclusions survive if J_K is treated only as an effective parameter.

5. **The weak-coupling (free-spin ground state) regime is established on only one end.** The tip-approach test (Fig. S15c) is shown for a single dehydrogenated N = 14 end, a free spin-1/2. The interpretation of all odd chains assumes the weak-coupling regime for the S = 1 triplet as well, but for an S = 1 coupled to two channels the screening and the position of the quantum phase transition differ. The interpretation also assumes the tip reduces J_K rather than changing the junction or adding tip-induced potential scattering, and the YSR state merging into the dGSJ peaks on approach is also how a YSR state hybridizing with dGSJ states might behave. The authors should repeat the tip-approach series on several intact odd-chain ends at different N (including the 0.8 meV N = 11 end) and on a passivated end with larger binding energy, and discuss the alternative explanations.

6. **Extracted energies lack uncertainties, a consistent extraction rule, and in some cases the underlying spectra.** δE_YSR, E_ST and E_TS in Figs. 3d and 3e come from raw peak separations, justified by a single deconvolution (N = 11, δE ≈ 0.8 meV, error < 3%; Section S2), but the NDR-induced shift is likely to differ for YSR peaks lying within the dGSJ linewidth. Figs. 3d and 3e have no error bars. For N = 11 and 13, E_TS ≈ 0.5-0.8 meV is read from weak shoulders on the dGSJ negative-differential-resistance dips (Figs. S11, S13), and these values set the |J| used in Fig. 3e and as model input in Fig. 3d. Some values are hard to reconcile with the figures: Fig. S13 quotes E_TS ≈ 0.5 meV while the marker sits near 2.9 mV, about 0.8 meV above the dGSJ peak; Fig. 3e has experimental |J| for N = 14 although Fig. S14 shows no ST feature; the N = 3 TS energy (~12 meV in Fig. 3e) appears in no spectrum, since Fig. S3 covers only ±5 mV; the N = 5 TS energy cannot be read precisely from Fig. S5c; and N = 12 (E_ST ≈ 0.5 meV) and N = 13 (E_TS ≈ 0.5 meV) are not monotonic as exponential decay would require. The authors should apply the Fig. S2 deconvolution to every spectrum, or at least to those with δE < 0.3 meV or E_ST/TS < 1 meV; describe how shoulders were located; tabulate E_ST/E_TS and δE_YSR with uncertainties for every chain, stating for each whether it came from peak positions or a fit; add error bars to Figs. 3d and 3e and propagate the |J| uncertainties into the model band of Fig. 3d; and provide the missing spectra. They should also state explicitly that the BLBQ parameters in Fig. 3e (J0 = 18 meV, β = 0.09) are taken from ref. 13 and not refitted, which strengthens the comparison.

7. **The attribution of the J_K scatter to the herringbone reconstruction is untested, and chain conformation is not considered.** The text attributes the scatter in δE_YSR to "probably ... the Au(111) herringbone reconstruction" (p. 6, ref. 45) without showing any correlation. The chains have different cis/trans kink sequences (e.g. Figs. 2c, S10a, S13a), which may change both J (through the end-unit geometry) and J_K. The two ends of the same chain sometimes differ markedly (Figs. S7b, S9b, S11a), as do two different N = 11 chains (Fig. S11a,b). Film thickness and local gap variations across the film are not quantified, and the number of film preparations is not given. Because this scatter is what masks the model test in concern 1, the authors should use the topographs they already have to correlate δE_YSR with the end-unit adsorption site (fcc, hcp, elbow or soliton region), the terminal-unit orientation and conformation, and the local dGSJ energy, and report both ends of each chain in Fig. 3d.

8. **The ground-state spin of odd (S = 1) and even (S = 0) chains is inferred rather than measured.** The assignment rests on the YSR pattern and on weak TS peaks (Figs. S7, S9, S11, S13), and the two-site model simply assumes that J changes sign with parity (Section S5: "J is positive ... for even chains and negative ... for odd chains"). There is no magnetic-field dependence and no normal-state reference in which the TS/ST excitations would show the Zeeman splitting expected for each multiplicity. The authors should add field-dependent spectra below the Nb critical field, or normal-state spectra (above Tc or at fields that quench the film's superconductivity) showing a Zeeman or Kondo signature on odd chains only, on at least one odd and one even chain; alternatively, show that the full BLBQ calculation of the sign and magnitude of the edge J (Section S4) quantitatively reproduces the observed TS/ST intensity ratios.

9. **The topological, fractionalization and robustness claims go beyond the evidence.** The abstract and main text state that the YSR maps "establish the topological origin of the observed YSR states" (p. 5), that fractionalization "survives", and that topological edge states "remain robust in a superconducting environment" (p. 3, p. 9). End localization and the even-odd alternation of the ground-state spin follow from Lieb-Mattis for any finite antiferromagnetic chain on a bipartite lattice; a YSR state localized on the terminal triangulene with zigzag-edge shape (Fig. 2f) is what any unpaired spin on a terminal unit would give; and the spin-1/2 character of each end is inferred from the ST/TS energies (Fig. 3e) and the model, not measured. With J_K ≪ Δ ≪ J0 (0.6 meV against 18 meV), survival of the edge states is expected rather than tested, and no perturbation (defect, substrate site, kink) is used to test symmetry protection. The authors should soften this wording or add evidence specific to the Haldane phase: for example, show that a defect inside the chain creates new edge spins with YSR signatures; compare the measured YSR decay length along the chain, and its N-dependence, with the edge-state localization length of the BLBQ model; and add the natural reference structures, an isolated triangulene (N = 1, S = 1, expected to show YSR) and an N = 2 dimer (singlet with large J, expected to show none), which the 3:1 precursor ratio should produce on the surface.

The energy axis is ambiguous: in SIS spectra all inelastic thresholds are shifted by Δ_tip + Δ_sample ≈ 2.1 meV; Fig. S8c uses V − Δ/e, but Figs. 3a, 4a, 4b and 4d are labelled in sample bias and Fig. 4c in "Energy (meV)". The CO frustrated-translation mode at ~35 meV overlaps the predicted CS energies (Figs. 4a, 4d, S8c; N = 3 in Fig. 4d), so some assignments near 30-35 meV are ambiguous. The authors should state which energy correction was applied before comparing with the model; tabulate measured and calculated CS and ST energies for each N with a numerical residual; compare energies and linewidths with the same chains on bulk Au(111) (ref. 13) and state whether any mode shows a substrate-induced shift or broadening, since agreement there is the evidence for decoupling and a systematic shift would measure the substrate's renormalization; confirm the assignments near 35 meV with a metal (non-CO) tip; or else soften these statements.
