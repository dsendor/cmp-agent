Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports polarization-dependent THz emission from bulk centrosymmetric Cr2Ge2Te6 under 1.2 eV excitation. It attributes the emission to magnetic dipole radiation from a magnetization induced at second order by a resonant nonlinear Edelstein effect. That effect is said to act on the Cr moments as an "Edelstein–Zeeman field". A symmetry analysis for point group -3, a toy tight-binding calculation showing that the interband nonlinear Edelstein response survives Brillouin-zone integration, and a mean-field Heisenberg/Landau model of the fluence dependence support this reading. If it were established, optical control of a ferromagnet through a light-induced, polarization-tunable effective field in a globally centrosymmetric crystal would be of broad interest. At present, however, the magnetic origin of the signal and the claimed "manipulation of ferromagnetism" rest on indirect arguments.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear, symmetry-based phenomenology (Eqs. 2–3, Table S1) that predicts a fourfold |cos(2φ−2δ)| pattern not tied to a crystal axis. The normal-incidence data in Fig. 2d,e match this pattern well, with nodes close to zero and no visible isotropic background.
- A striking fluence-driven 90° rotation and polarity reversal of the two-fold pattern at 45° incidence (Fig. 3b,c), with a minimum in spectral weight at intermediate fluence (Fig. 4e). This is a nontrivial observation whatever its origin.
- The SI gives a careful, self-contained derivation showing that the inversion-even quadrupolar (A⊗A) term generates a net spin density in a locally non-centrosymmetric but globally centrosymmetric lattice (Eqs. IV.47–IV.55). This is a useful theoretical contribution.
- The topic is timely: hidden spin texture, nonlinear Edelstein effect and ultrafast control of van der Waals magnets.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **Symmetry cannot tell the proposed mechanism from non-magnetic emission mechanisms, and the two exclusion arguments are not decisive.**
   - In point group -3, an in-plane axial vector (M_x ± iM_y) and the in-plane response to E⊗E both belong to E_g (Table S1).
   - At the surface, the symmetry drops to point group 3. There an in-plane polar response (surface optical rectification or a surface photocurrent, P or J ∝ E⊗E) also transforms as E. It therefore gives the same |cos(2φ−2δ)| fourfold pattern, with a non-universal δ, at normal incidence.
   - Bulk higher-multipole mechanisms are allowed in centrosymmetric crystals and would also produce in-plane emission with this angular form. Examples are the electric-quadrupole/photon-drag current, which is proportional to the photon wavevector, and electric-quadrupole radiation from the very intra-cell quadrupoles invoked in SI Fig. S6b.
   - The paper excludes the surface contribution on two grounds (p. 2): (i) it would not track magnetic order, and (ii) it would scale linearly with fluence.
   - Argument (i) is weak because the signal is ~85% of its maximum at 200 K, deep in the paramagnetic phase (Fig. 2f). The amplitude also changes smoothly with temperature, which could come from band-gap, absorption or carrier-lifetime changes. Cr2Ge2Te6 is known to show such changes across T_c through magneto-elastic and band effects.
   - Argument (ii) is weak because sublinear, saturating fluence dependence is common for photocurrent and rectification emitters, through absorption saturation, screening and carrier-density effects.
   - The authors should show measurements that respond to magnetism specifically and that symmetry alone cannot supply:
     - Emission versus an applied magnetic field, in-plane and out-of-plane, including field reversal. The OptiCool system they used has a magnet.
     - A comparison of emission from opposite faces of a crystal, or from samples of different thickness.
     - An isostructural or non-magnetic reference measured under matched conditions.
     - Full vector THz polarimetry (both E_x and E_y), together with an azimuthal sample rotation at fixed pump polarization, to confirm that the pattern is fixed to the crystal.

2. **No direct evidence that the magnetization of the Cr moments is changed, as the title and abstract claim.**
   - The only observable is far-field THz emission. No measurement shows ΔM, its direction, or its relation to the ferromagnetic order.
   - The signal persists at nearly full strength at 80 K and 200 K (Fig. 2e,f). So even within the authors' picture, most of the effect is a light-induced magnetization of a paramagnet, not "manipulation of ferromagnetism".
   - The authors should add a direct magnetic probe of the pumped state with the same pump polarization dependence: time-resolved Kerr/Faraday rotation or ellipticity, or time-resolved magnetic linear dichroism.
   - They should also estimate the magnitude of ΔM needed to radiate the observed THz field from the excited volume, and the corresponding H^EZ in tesla. This should be compared with the anisotropy field of Cr2Ge2Te6 and with the photocarrier density at the fluences used.
   - Without these, the statement that the field "pull[s] [the magnetization] completely into the plane" (p. 4, Fig. 4d) is a model output, not an observation.

3. **The 45° interpretation depends on a net out-of-plane magnetization, which a soft ferromagnet in zero field should not have, and the model compares static M with what is a pump-induced signal.**
   - Fig. S2 shows a soft ferromagnet with vanishing coercivity. In zero applied field, a 1.9 mm spot should cover many up/down domains with net ⟨M_z⟩ ≈ 0.
   - The paper does not state whether a bias field was applied during the 45° measurements.
   - If no field was applied, the "frozen easy-axis component" that competes with M_y (p. 6) averages to zero, and the proposed M_y/M_z interference cannot occur.
   - THz emission is set by the pump-induced change ΔM(t) or its time derivative, not by the static M(I_0).
   - Fig. 4d plots the full static |M_z|, |M_y| and |M_y − M_z|. M_z starts at 1 at zero fluence, so the green curve's minimum comes from subtracting a large equilibrium M_z that does not itself radiate.
   - Recomputed with ΔM_y and ΔM_z = M_z(I_0) − M_z(0), which have opposite signs, the destructive interference and the minimum may not survive. The relative weights of the two components would also have to include the 45° radiation projection and Fresnel factors.
   - The authors should:
     - state the field and domain condition of the measurement;
     - repeat the 45° fluence series with the sample magnetized ±z by a small bias field, and show that the pattern rotation follows the sign of M_z;
     - recompute Fig. 4d using ΔM with the correct geometric weights.

4. **Heating, both steady-state and transient, is not quantified, and it could produce the saturation and temperature trends that the model is fitted to.**
   - Steady-state heating:
     - The normal-incidence data use 50 kHz (25 kHz after chopping) and up to 330 mW average pump power on a ~1.9 mm spot, in an exchange-gas cryostat at a nominal 7 K (SI Sec. II).
     - The 45° data use up to 90 mW at a lower repetition rate.
   - Transient heating, from my rough estimate:
     - An absorbed fluence of ~0.3 mJ/cm² deposited within an optical penetration depth of a few tens of nm gives an energy density of order tens of J/cm³.
     - Literature heat-capacity values suggest this is comparable to or larger than the enthalpy needed to heat Cr2Ge2Te6 from 7 K to above T_c = 67 K.
   - Heating would naturally give saturation in Fig. 4c and non-monotonic behavior in Fig. 4e. It also means the effective spin temperature is not the 7 K assumed in the model (T = 0.104 T_c^0, Fig. S9).
   - The authors should:
     - give the absorbed fluence and penetration depth at 1.2 eV;
     - estimate steady-state and per-pulse temperature rise;
     - test for steady-state heating by varying the repetition rate or chopping at fixed pulse energy;
     - show that the low-fluence pattern is unchanged after high-fluence exposure (no cumulative damage or surface change).

5. **The mean-field model treats the response as quasi-static equilibrium, and this is not justified for sub-picosecond dynamics.**
   - The model minimizes F(M) in the presence of H^EZ (Eqs. 4, V.9) and identifies the emitted amplitude with the resulting equilibrium M.
   - The emitted pulses are ~1 ps long (Figs. 2c, 3b,d), while H^EZ lasts about as long as the photocarrier coherence.
   - Rotating localized Cr moments toward a new equilibrium is governed by precession and damping, which in a soft ferromagnet take place on GHz–sub-THz timescales. Reaching the equilibrium M of Eq. (4) within the pulse is therefore not expected.
   - The model also gives no account of the waveform or spectrum. For example, the spectral peak moves from ~0.4 to ~1 THz with fluence in Fig. 3d.
   - The authors should solve a time-dependent model in which the localized spins are driven by a pulse-like H^EZ(t), for example an LLG or Bloch equation with the experimental pulse duration. They should then show that the polarization, fluence and waveform trends survive. Alternatively, they should argue explicitly why the adiabatic limit applies.

B. Should address

6. **The "quantitative" agreement with the model is overstated.**
   - Neither Fig. 4b,c nor Fig. 4d,e states which parameters were fitted. Likely candidates are the scale mapping model I_0 (in units of T_c^0) to mJ/cm², the vertical scale, κ, χ_{x,∥}, χ_{z,xx}, and the choice of plotting |M_y − M_z| with equal weights.
   - With a free horizontal and vertical scale, any saturating function will fit Fig. 4c.
   - The authors should:
     - list all parameters and which ones are fixed independently, for example κ from the measured anisotropy and T_c from Fig. S1;
     - overlay model and data on common axes for both geometries with a single parameter set;
     - add the model prediction to the temperature dependence in Fig. 2f, which currently has no model curve. Fig. S7b and Fig. S9 (right) suggest a different trend from the data.
   - Otherwise, "quantitatively captured" in the abstract should be softened.

7. **The temperature dependence in Fig. 2f does not match the text or the model.**
   - The text says the signal "decreases gradually with cooling and then undergoes a more rapid reduction upon crossing T_c" (p. 2). In the figure, the amplitude is roughly flat from ~40 K to above T_c, and the steep drop occurs below ~30–40 K, well inside the ordered phase.
   - The model predicts that the in-plane susceptibility is constant below T_c and peaks near T_c (Eq. V.23, Fig. S7b). That is not what Fig. 2f shows.
   - The y-axis is "Spectral Weight", while the text says |S_THz|max is plotted.
   - The error bars are large below T_c and their origin is not given.
   - The authors should:
     - define the plotted quantity and the error bars;
     - add finer polarization maps across T_c, not just 7, 80 and 200 K;
     - explain why the change is concentrated far below T_c;
     - compare with the model at the same fluence.

8. **The emitted field amplitudes are inconsistent across the paper, and their absolute magnitude is not used.**
   - The main text says "several V/cm" (p. 2).
   - Fig. 2c peaks at ~0.8 V/cm and Fig. 3b at ~0.4 V/cm.
   - SI Sec. II.C estimates "on the order of 0.1 V/cm".
   - The calibration should be reconciled, including collection efficiency and detector response.
   - The authors should then use the calibrated value to estimate the radiating ΔM (see concern 2). A comparison with a reference emitter such as ZnTe under the same conditions would help.

9. **The microscopic model is generic and is not connected quantitatively to Cr2Ge2Te6.**
   - The SI model (Fig. S5, Eq. III.3) is a two-site Dirac model with Rashba-type SOC of opposite sign on inversion partners stacked along z. It establishes that χ^(2) ≠ 0 but gives neither its magnitude nor its sign, nor the angle δ, for the real compound.
   - The real 1.2 eV transitions involve Te-p/Cr-d bands on a honeycomb lattice, and the bands are spin-split below T_c, which is not the Kramers-degenerate setting of the model.
   - The s-d coupling J_sd, the photocarrier density and the coherence lifetime (regularized by δ⁺ → 0) set the size of H^EZ, but none is estimated.
   - The authors should provide at least an order-of-magnitude estimate of H^EZ for realistic parameters (ideally from a DFT-based calculation of the interband nonlinear spin response at 1.2 eV), and of the ratio χ_{z,xx}/χ_{x,∥} that the 45° interpretation needs.
   - The paper's mechanism involves dynamic intra-cell electric quadrupoles (SI Sec. IV.B.4). The authors should also explain why these quadrupoles do not themselves contribute comparable radiation.

10. **The fluence-driven pattern rotation at 45° has other generic explanations.**
    - Any two contributions with different signs and different fluence scaling give a 90° rotation of a two-fold pattern and a minimum at the crossover. One example is a term linear in fluence, such as surface rectification or a photocurrent, plus a saturating term.
    - Fig. 3c shows only two fluences, with sparse points, and the magnitudes are plotted without sign.
    - The authors should show signed polarization maps at several fluences through the minimum (~0.1–0.4 mJ/cm²), and fit them with the full Eq. (3) at oblique incidence, including the E_z terms.
    - They should also show that the fitted coefficients evolve as the M_y/M_z competition predicts, rather than as a linear + saturating sum of independent contributions.

11. **The pattern shape is not checked against the saturation the model invokes.**
    - If M_y saturates near 0.4 mJ/cm² (Fig. 4b,c), the model's |M_y(φ)| = f(I_0|cos(2φ−2δ)|) should flatten the petals at high fluence, broadening them toward a square shape.
    - Fig. 2d instead shows the same lobe shape at 0.1 and 0.4 mJ/cm².
    - The authors should compare normalized patterns at several fluences with the model prediction.
    - They should also reconcile the angle of maximum emission: φ ≈ 60° in the main text versus δ_∥ ≈ 50° in the SI.

12. **Samples, surface and setup details are missing.**
    - The paper does not state:
      - how many crystals were measured;
      - sample thickness;
      - whether surfaces were freshly cleaved, and how exposure to air was controlled (Cr2Ge2Te6 surfaces degrade);
      - how the a-axis direction defining φ was determined (Laue, XRD?);
      - whether results reproduce after thermal cycling.
    - The normal-incidence and 45° data were taken in different cryostats, at different repetition rates (50 kHz vs 3 kHz) and with different chopping schemes. So the two geometries are not directly comparable.
    - The authors should report these details and show the key normal-incidence and 45° results on at least a second crystal.

13. **The polarization purity of the pump inside the cryostat is not verified.**
    - The half-wave plate sits before the cryostat window, and strain-induced birefringence in the window at low temperature can add ellipticity that depends on φ.
    - A circular component would drive helicity-dependent responses (inverse Faraday effect, circular photocurrents), which would mix into the pattern.
    - The authors should measure the polarization state after the window, or show that the emission is unchanged under a quarter-wave-plate test, that is, as a function of helicity.

C. Minor

14. **Fluence calibration (SI Sec. II.B).**
    - Eq. II.2 states f = 3 kHz, but the prefactor 0.017 mJ/cm² per mW corresponds to f = 1.5 kHz, which would be the chopped rate.
    - It is also unclear whether P is measured before or after the chopper.
    - The quoted fluences are incident, not absorbed. Reflection losses differ between 0° and 45°, which matters when comparing Fig. 4c and 4e.

15. **Magnetization characterization (Figs. S1–S2).**
    - The applied field for M(T) is not stated.
    - The saturation moment is ~1.45 μB/f.u. in Fig. S1 but ~3.2 μB/f.u. in Fig. S2. Neither equals the ~6 μB expected per Cr2Ge2Te6 formula unit with two Cr3+ (S = 3/2), so the unit should be clarified.
    - The text calls M(H) "nearly linear", but the low-T curves are step-like.
    - The Fig. S2 caption contains "300 14K".

16. **Undefined or inconsistent quantities and notation.**
    - "Spectral weight" (Figs. 2f, 3, 4c,e) needs a definition: integration range, and whether it is the amplitude or the power spectrum.
    - T_c is given as both 66 K and 67 K.
    - S_THz and E_THz are used interchangeably.
    - J (Eq. 5) and J_sd (Eq. V.32) are the same quantity under two names.
    - "Kramer's" should be "Kramers'".
    - "Sub-valent Cr3+ band" is unclear.

17. **The Fig. 4a caption is hard to follow.** It mixes "in-plane" and "planar" Edelstein–Zeeman fields and does not say which panels are above or below T_c. The axes of panels b and d are labeled "Fluence I_0 ∝ |E_0|²" without units or a mapping to experiment.

18. **Data availability "upon request" is weak for a claim of this kind.** Depositing the raw waveforms and the model code would let readers check the fits.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Use the OptiCool magnet to measure emission versus in-plane and out-of-plane field, including reversal, at 7 K and above T_c, at both incidence angles. This is the most direct way to show that the emission comes from the magnetization and that M_z takes part in the 45° rotation.
2. Add polarization-resolved time-resolved MOKE or Faraday measurements with the same pump. These would show ΔM directly, its direction, and its 2φ dependence, and would give an absolute scale for H^EZ.
3. Replace the static free-energy minimization with a driven LLG or Bloch simulation that uses ΔM. Then fit both geometries and the temperature dependence with one set of parameters, each stated as fixed or fitted.
4. Add a control set: emission from both faces or from several thicknesses, a non-magnetic or isostructural reference, a repetition-rate test for heating, and an azimuthal sample rotation. Together these would exclude surface rectification, photon drag and thermal artifacts.
