Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports pump-polarization-dependent THz emission from the centrosymmetric van der Waals ferromagnetic semiconductor Cr2Ge2Te6 under 1.2 eV excitation. It attributes the emission to magnetic-dipole radiation from Cr magnetization driven by a light-induced "Edelstein-Zeeman field" generated by the second-order nonlinear Edelstein effect. If this holds, it would be an experimental demonstration of a non-thermal, polarization-controlled, time-reversal-even route to steer magnetic order in a centrosymmetric material. That would interest the ultrafast magnetism, nonlinear optics and spintronics communities. The polarization-symmetry analysis is clean. The step from "anomalous THz emission with a magnetic-symmetry-allowed polarization dependence" to "manipulation of ferromagnetism" rests on indirect evidence and a qualitative Landau model.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A robust, sizeable (several V/cm) THz signal from a nominally centrosymmetric crystal, with a fourfold |cos(2φ − 2δ)| pump-polarization dependence that stays stable across fluence and temperature (Fig. 2d, e).
- A symmetry analysis for point group 3 (Eqs. 1–3) that predicts the allowed nonlinear magneto-electric tensor components and the fourfold pattern at normal incidence, and explains why δ is not locked to a crystal axis.
- A striking fluence-driven polarity reversal and 90° rotation of the twofold pattern at 45° incidence (Fig. 3b–d). An intermediate-fluence trace resembles the sum of the low- and high-fluence traces, which suggests two competing contributions.
- A simple, physically transparent Landau picture (Eq. 4, Fig. 4a) of competition between an easy-axis anisotropy and an in-plane effective field. It qualitatively reproduces the saturation at normal incidence and the non-monotonic spectral weight at 45°.
- The proposed mechanism is general, and the idea of a nonlinear-Edelstein-derived effective field acting on slower subsystems may inspire further work.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **No evidence that the Cr ferromagnetic moments, rather than the photoexcited carriers themselves, are the radiating magnetization.** The title and abstract claim "manipulation of ferromagnetism". However, the emission pattern and amplitude are essentially unchanged across Tc. In Fig. 2e the fourfold pattern persists at 80 K and 200 K, and in Fig. 2f the amplitude is largest in the paramagnetic phase, roughly flat from 60 to 200 K and only falling below ~50 K. A transient spin or orbital density of the photocarriers (the nonlinear Edelstein δM itself, Eq. 1) would also radiate as a magnetic dipole, with the same φ dependence and without any involvement of the Cr sublattice. On the ps timescale of the emitted pulse (spectrum 0.5–1.5 THz, Fig. 3d), it is not shown that the Cr moments can respond at all. Their precession and anisotropy frequencies in Cr2Ge2Te6 are far lower.
   - The authors should separate "carrier magnetization" from "Cr magnetization driven by H^EZ". Direct probes would do this: time-resolved MOKE or Faraday rotation at a Cr-sensitive or longer-lived window, with the same pump-polarization dependence, or the XMCD they propose.
   - At minimum they should give an estimate of the Cr response time and of the induced δM.
   - Without this, the headline claim should be reframed as THz emission from a light-induced nonlinear Edelstein magnetization, with ferromagnetic involvement inferred only from the 45° data.

2. **The 45° analysis assumes a single-domain M_z, but no magnetic field is applied and the domain state is not characterized.** The interpretation of Fig. 3 and Fig. 4d, e rests on a competition between M_y and the "frozen" easy-axis component M_z.
   - In zero field, a bulk Cr2Ge2Te6 crystal below Tc is generally multidomain, with up and down M_z averaging to near zero over a macroscopic pump spot. The M_z contribution to E_THz ∝ (M × n) would then largely cancel.
   - The Methods mention a magneto-optic cryostat, yet no field-dependent data are shown.
   - The authors should report the magnetic history (zero-field-cooled or field-cooled).
   - They should measure the 45° waveforms at low and high fluence under ±B along c, and after field cooling in ±B. If the M_z-derived contribution is real, it must reverse sign with the field and the polarity-reversal fluence must shift.
   - Field dependence at normal incidence (in-plane and out-of-plane) would also test whether the emission couples to the ordered moment.

3. **Alternative emission mechanisms are not excluded by the tests presented.** The arguments against surface electric-dipole rectification have three weaknesses:
   - The temperature argument does not exclude it. Surface depletion fields, carrier densities and surface χ(2) in a semiconductor are also temperature dependent, and Fig. 2f shows a smooth crossover rather than an order-parameter-like onset.
   - The linear-fluence argument does not exclude it. Surface rectification and photo-Dember emission also saturate at high fluence.
   - Symmetry does not exclude it. The paper itself notes that a surface without mirror symmetry would give the same unlocked fourfold pattern, so the polarization dependence at normal incidence does not separate magnetic-dipole from electric-dipole or electric-quadrupole emission.

   Also, at 1.2 eV the optical penetration depth is likely only tens of nm, so "bulk" and "surface" are not cleanly separated. Decisive tests are needed:
   - (a) Rotating the sample azimuth at fixed pump polarization, to establish how the lobes relate to the crystal axes (and to report δ).
   - (b) Detecting both orthogonal THz polarization components. For a magnetic dipole M_y ∝ cos(2φ − 2δ), the orthogonal component should follow the partner tensor component with a definite phase relation, and the sign of E_THz should alternate between adjacent lobes.
   - (c) Comparing emission from the front and back faces, or in transmission versus reflection. Magnetic-dipole and electric-dipole radiation flip sign differently under sample flip.
   - (d) Measuring a thickness or excitation-wavelength dependence.
   - (e) Measuring a non-magnetic isostructural or reference crystal under identical conditions.

4. **The fluence-driven reversal at 45° could be thermal or carrier-density driven rather than an Edelstein-Zeeman competition.** The reversal occurs between 0.1 and 0.75 mJ/cm² at 7 K (Fig. 3b–d). Cr2Ge2Te6 has a very small heat capacity at 7 K, and 0.75 mJ/cm² at 1.2 eV with tens-of-nm absorption could transiently heat, or even steadily heat, the excited volume toward or above Tc (66 K). That would suppress any M_z-related contribution and leave the in-plane one, giving the same phenomenology as Fig. 4d.
   - The authors should state the repetition rate, spot size and absorbed energy density.
   - They should estimate the transient and average lattice and spin temperatures.
   - Crucially, they should show the 45° fluence series at T > Tc (e.g., 80 K). If the reversal is due to competition with the ordered M_z, it must vanish above Tc. If it persists, the interpretation fails.
   - A temperature series at fixed fluence across Tc at 45° would likewise test whether the low-fluence (M_z-dominated) component follows the order parameter.

B. Should address

5. **A static free-energy minimization is compared with a radiated field that depends on the time derivative of M.** E_THz is proportional to ∂²M/∂t² (or ∂M/∂t in the near field), not to the static M obtained by minimizing Eq. 4 under a constant H^EZ.
   - The rise time, decay time and spectral content of δM(t) set both the amplitude and the shape of the emitted waveform. The waveform and spectrum change markedly with fluence (Fig. 3d; the spectral peak moves from ~0.5 THz to ~1 THz). The adiabatic static picture cannot capture this, and the "spectral weight" comparison in Fig. 4c, e implicitly assumes it is fixed.
   - The authors should model the dynamics (e.g., an LLG or time-dependent Landau equation driven by a pulsed H^EZ(t) with the IR-pulse envelope and carrier lifetime). Alternatively, they should explicitly justify why the spectral weight should be proportional to the static |M_y| or |M_y − M_z|.

6. **The "quantitative" agreement claimed in the abstract is not demonstrated.**
   - Fig. 4b, d are plotted against a dimensionless I_0 ∝ |E_0|², while Fig. 4c, e are plotted against fluence in mJ/cm². The mapping between them, the values of r_z, r_0, u_z, g and J, and which were fitted are not given in the main text.
   - The grey curve in Fig. 4c is not identified (is it the model or an empirical fit?).
   - No model curve is shown for the temperature dependence (Fig. 2f) or for the polarization patterns, although the abstract says all three dependences are "quantitatively captured".
   - Mean-field theory would predict an enhanced in-plane response near Tc (paramagnetic susceptibility ∝ 1/(T − Tc) above Tc, crossing over to the transverse susceptibility set by the anisotropy below Tc). The data show no feature near Tc, only a gradual change. The authors should overlay the model on Fig. 2f with stated parameters, and either tone down "quantitatively" or provide a global fit with parameter tables.

7. **The saturation at normal incidence has simpler explanations.** The sublinear growth in Fig. 4c is read as Zeeman-like saturation of M_y. Absorption saturation, Pauli blocking, photocarrier screening, or a fluence-dependent carrier lifetime would produce similar curves.
   - The authors should measure the fluence dependence of absorbed power or transient reflectivity in the same range.
   - If the THz amplitude saturates while the excited carrier density remains linear, that supports the magnetic interpretation.

8. **The superposition test in Fig. 3d is under-specified.** The 0.4 mJ/cm² trace is compared with E_0.1 + E_0.75 without stated weights. If the two contributions scale differently with fluence, an unweighted sum of the end-point traces has no obvious reason to reproduce the intermediate trace.
   - The authors should state whether any scaling, time shift or normalization was applied.
   - They should give a quantitative goodness of agreement.
   - Ideally they should decompose all five fluence traces into two fixed temporal basis functions with fluence-dependent coefficients, and show that the coefficients follow the predicted M_y and M_z curves.

9. **Samples, characterization and reproducibility are not documented.**
   - The paper does not state how many crystals were measured, their thickness, or whether the fourfold pattern, its δ, and the 45° reversal were reproduced in more than one sample or spot.
   - No magnetization, Tc or anisotropy characterization of the measured crystals is shown, although Tc is quoted as 66 K in the text and 67 K in Fig. 2f.
   - Surface quality and degradation are not discussed (cleaved? exposure to air?). Te-related surface states could matter for surface emission.
   - The authors should report these details and show a second sample for the key results (Fig. 2d and Fig. 3c).

10. **The magnitude of the effect is not estimated.**
    - The paper does not estimate the δM required to produce several V/cm of THz field, nor the size of H^EZ implied by the observed saturation.
    - Nor does it estimate the nonlinear Edelstein susceptibility expected for Cr2Ge2Te6 at 1.2 eV.
    - Order-of-magnitude numbers would let readers judge whether the Cr moments can plausibly be rotated "completely into the plane" (as Fig. 4a and the text suggest) at sub-mJ/cm² fluence. They would also show whether the carrier spin density alone could account for the signal (see concern 1).

11. **The error bars do not reflect measurement uncertainty.** In Fig. 2f and Fig. 4c, e they are the spread from varying the Fourier-transform window (2–3.5 ps in Fig. 2f; 3–4 ps in Fig. 4). This captures analysis sensitivity, not shot-to-shot, scan-to-scan or spot-to-spot variation.
    - Several conclusions rely on small differences: the "more rapid reduction upon crossing Tc" in Fig. 2f, and the depth of the minimum in Fig. 4e.
    - Statistical uncertainties from repeated scans should be included.
    - The window should be the same across figures, or the difference justified.

12. **The excitation conditions and their link to the mechanism need justification.**
    - The pump is described as "near-resonant with the direct gap at Γ". The relevant interband transitions at 1.2 eV, the absorption coefficient and the probed depth should be documented.
    - The tensor analysis assumes the bulk point group 3 throughout the excited volume, and a near-surface excitation makes that assumption less safe.
    - Residual pump ellipticity from the half-wave plate (especially at 45° incidence) could add an inverse-Faraday M_z contribution. Its sign would depend on φ and might mimic part of the twofold pattern. A circular-polarization control, or a measured polarization purity, would close this.

C. Minor

13. The text says "we fix the pump polarization … and plot |S_THz|max with temperature in Fig. 2f", but the axis of Fig. 2f is "Spectral Weight". Please make the plotted quantity consistent and define "spectral weight" (the integration range in frequency, and whether it is |E(ω)| or |E(ω)|²).

14. Eq. (3) writes M_y in terms of α^(2)_{x,xy}, α^(2)_{x,xx} and α^(2)_{x,yz}. The first index appears to label a different magnetization component from the one on the left-hand side. Please check the indices against Eq. (2) and define δ explicitly in terms of the tensor components.

15. The polar plots (Figs. 2d, e and 3c) show amplitudes without sign. Since electro-optic sampling is phase-sensitive, please indicate the sign of the peak field per lobe (e.g., by colour) to show the sign alternation expected for cos(2φ − 2δ).

16. The low-fluence datasets in Fig. 2d and Fig. 3c are multiplied by 3 for display. Please also give absolute values, so the fluence scaling can be read directly.

17. The Methods should list the repetition rate, spot sizes of pump and THz collection, the method used to determine the crystal a-axis, and the sample thickness. The emitted-field calibration (V/cm) should also be explained.

18. The shift of the spectral peak with fluence in Fig. 3d (~0.5 THz at low fluence, ~1 THz at 0.75 mJ/cm²) is not discussed. It may carry information on the dynamics relevant to concern 5.

19. References 32 and 33 are rendered as "\mmfnt Dover Publications". Please fix the formatting.

20. The discussion of Fig. 2f says the crossover rather than sharp criticality occurs "since the system cannot break time-reversal symmetry again". Please rephrase. The explicit symmetry breaking by H^EZ smears the transition, which is the standard field-smearing argument, and this would be clearer stated that way.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a direct, element- or order-parameter-sensitive probe of the light-induced magnetization (time-resolved MOKE or Faraday rotation, or XMCD), with the same pump-polarization dependence. This would show that the Cr moments, not only the carriers, are rotated, and on what timescale.
2. Use the available magneto-optic cryostat for field-dependent and field-cooled measurements at both normal and 45° incidence. Repeat the 45° fluence series above Tc. Together these would establish or refute the M_y/M_z competition.
3. Add symmetry-discriminating geometry tests: sample azimuth rotation, both THz polarization components with sign, front versus back face, and a non-magnetic reference. These would separate magnetic-dipole emission from surface electric-dipole and quadrupole emission.
4. Replace the static Landau comparison with a time-dependent model driven by a pulsed H^EZ(t), state all parameters, and overlay it on Figs. 2f, 4c and 4e. Then either justify or soften the "quantitatively captured" claim.
