A. Must address

1. The assignment of both skyrmion families to the CoFeB sublattice, with CoB "laterally uniform", is not established independently of a fitted parameter (Abstract; Fig. 3, Eqs. 1–2; "Element-resolved magnetometry"; Methods "Soft X-ray techniques"; DPC section). The square CoB loop in Fig. 3d is I_CoB = I_Co − a·I_Fe with a = 0.35, and Methods say a was "determined empirically by adjusting a until the reconstructed total loop matched the SQUID-VSM loop" with a closed central area. The squareness of I_CoB therefore follows from that choice. The two uses of a also conflict. Eq. 1 can be rewritten as I_total = I_Co + (1 − a)·I_Fe, which reproduces the net moment only if diode current converts to moment with the same factor at the Co and Fe edges. Nothing justifies that, because absorption cross-sections, elemental fractions (Co68B32 vs Co40Fe40B20) and transmitted backgrounds differ. The a that makes Eq. 1 match SQUID-VSM need not equal the ratio r of the CoFeB Co-edge signal to its Fe-edge signal, which is what Eq. 2 needs to isolate CoB. The CoFeB loop (Fig. 3b) has large non-hysteretic linear slopes beyond ±30 mT, exactly where the textures appear, so any error in a adds or removes a fraction of that slope in the reconstructed CoB loop and could hide or create CoB reversal. The fitting target is also weak: the SQUID-VSM loop was measured on Si/SiO2, the XMCD on a sister sample on a 200-nm Si3N4 membrane, whose switching fields and slopes need not match. No other technique settles which sublattice hosts the textures. Because Ms·t is equal for both sublattices by design (8.85 × 10⁻⁸ A m² each), full reversal of all ten CoB layers would give the same 13.6 T nm DPC step as full reversal of all ten CoFeB layers; the 12.8 ± 0.7 T nm result shows that one sublattice reverses locally while the other stays uniform, not which one, and the paper should say so explicitly. The DPC zero reference is moreover "set by assumptions informed primarily by the XMCD I(H) loops" (Methods), so DPC is not independent of this decomposition. MFM and SAXS are not element-selective as presented. The authors should (i) fix a independently, by measuring Co L3 and Fe L3 loops or XMCD amplitudes on the CoFeB-only reference multilayer already used for Keff in the same geometry, or from saturated XMCD amplitudes with the known equal sublattice moments, or from tabulated cross-sections plus the Co content; (ii) show the CoB loop for a range of a (for example 0.25–0.45) with an uncertainty on a, and give an explicit upper bound on the fraction of CoB reversed at 45, 80 and 120 mT given the noise in Fig. 3d; (iii) ideally provide element-resolved real- or reciprocal-space data at both edges in the conventional, maze and inverse states: resonant SAXS at the Fe L3 versus Co L3 edge normalized by the respective XMCD amplitudes (if the textures are in CoFeB only, the Co-edge magnetic scattering should scale with the Co content of CoFeB alone), or STXM/ptychography/holography at both edges (for example at the COSMIC beamline). The photon energy or edge of the SAXS in Fig. 2f–j is not stated; if both edges were recorded, the existing data may already settle the question. Without one of these, "we unequivocally attribute the observed textures to the CoFeB sublattice" and "all textures reside solely in the CoFeB layers" are not demonstrated.

B. Should address

2. The micromagnetic support for confinement to CoFeB rests on free and altered parameters, and the simulations the Discussion relies on are not shown (Methods "Micromagnetic modelling"; Extended Data Table 2; Fig. 4; Discussion). D = 1.10 (CoFeB) and 1.00 (CoB) mJ m⁻² are free parameters, with no statement of how they were chosen or how sensitive the result is to them, and A = 8 pJ m⁻¹ is assumed for both layers. With these values κ = πD/(4√(A·Keff)) is about 0.92 for CoFeB and about 0.59 for CoB, so CoFeB is placed close to the domain-wall instability threshold and the outcome that only CoFeB hosts textures is largely set by this choice. CoB is given the CoFeB thickness and Ms = 0.81 MA m⁻¹ (measured 0.63), with Ku rescaled to keep Keff. This removes the thickness and Ms asymmetry that the Discussion credits for the larger Deff/Keff (Deff = D/t) in CoFeB, and changes CoB self-demagnetization and wall energy, so the simulated selectivity tests essentially only the Keff asymmetry. The Discussion states that D_eff is "comparable" in both layers, but D was never measured. The statements that "reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers, whereas stronger coupling localizes the non-collinear state" are presented as simulation results but not shown. The authors should present a state diagram of which sublattice hosts textures versus D_CoB, D_CoFeB, the Keff asymmetry and J_RKKY, including the RKKY-strength simulations quoted in the text; simulate the real CoB Ms and thickness or show they do not matter; and, since D is the parameter the conclusion depends on, measure D for each sublattice, for example by Brillouin light scattering on the reference films or by the domain-period method of Baćani et al. (ref. 48).

3. The simulated stacking order is inverted relative to the sample, and the outer layers, which MFM is most sensitive to, are not treated (Fig. 4e versus Fig. 1a and Methods; Results discussion of Fig. 4). Experimentally each repeat is Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB from the bottom up, so the topmost magnetic layer is CoB and the bottommost is CoFeB. Fig. 4e shows Pt/CoB/Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru, with CoFeB on top, and the text discusses the "very top CoFeB layer ... coupled to CoB on one side only, with vacuum above", a configuration the sample does not have; in the real sample that boundary CoFeB layer is on the substrate side. The real top CoB layer has a CoFeB neighbour on one side only, so its RKKY field is about half that of interior CoB layers, and by the authors' own argument it is the CoB layer most likely to host textures. Because MFM is weighted towards the near-surface layers, this bears both on the "solely in CoFeB" claim and on the reading of MFM contrast. The authors should repeat the simulations with the experimental stacking order and the real 1.5 nm Ru/Pt spacer thicknesses (or justify why the order is irrelevant), correct the top-layer discussion, and include the outer layers in the XMCD sensitivity estimate of Concern 1; each contributes 1/10 of its sublattice signal, which should be resolvable.

4. The effective-field mechanism (Fig. 6) is argued qualitatively but not tested against the measured field windows (Discussion; Fig. 6). The model says CoFeB behaves as an ordinary CoFeB skyrmion multilayer in an effective field H − H_RKKY. With the stated parameters, the RKKY field on an interior CoFeB layer from its two CoB neighbours is 2|J|/(Ms·t) ≈ 125 mT (about 63 mT per neighbour), so the effective field would vanish near 125 mT, not near the 80 mT maze state. The authors should compute H_eff, including the stack's demagnetizing/stray field, for each observed state (conventional skyrmions at +120 mT, maze at +80 mT, inverse skyrmions at +45 mT) and show that the predicted zero crossing and window boundaries agree with the data. The CoFeB-only and CoB-only reference multilayers already exist (used for Keff); imaging them by MFM across a field sweep and plotting all three systems against H − H_RKKY would give the natural control. If the CoB-only multilayer also hosts skyrmions in some window, the authors must explain why the RKKY field suppresses them in the SAF; if the CoFeB windows do not map onto the SAF windows after the shift, the simple picture is incomplete (for example, dipolar coupling between tubes would matter).

5. The RKKY coupling strength is extracted with a formula that does not fit this stack, and the same value feeds the simulations (Methods "Sample fabrication"; Extended Data Tables 1 and 2). J_RKKY = μ0·Hs·Ms·t_FM/2 with μ0Hs = 125 mT is the bilayer spin-flop expression. In a [ ]x10 stack interior layers couple on both sides, which changes the prefactor by about a factor of two (to about /4). For perpendicular layers measured with an out-of-plane field, saturation also proceeds through domain, maze and skyrmion states (Fig. 1c, Fig. 2, up to about 130–170 mT), so Hs contains nucleation, DMI, wall and dipolar energies, not only uniform exchange. The authors should justify the formula for a multilayer or determine J_RKKY independently, for example from an in-plane hard-axis loop, minor-loop shifts, the plateau-edge field or a reference bilayer SAF with the same spacer; give its uncertainty propagated from Hs; and state how the simulated field windows (−130, −80, −50 mT) change with J.

6. The novelty relative to known AF/FM phase coexistence in perpendicular SAFs, and the skyrmion character of the inverse-polarity textures, need to be established (Introduction; Discussion "form of phase coexistence"; Fig. 2; Supplementary Note 4). In any SAF a local switch between FM and AF states reverses exactly one sublattice, so AF bubbles in an FM background and FM bubbles in an AF background are both expected along the descending branch; closely related coexistence was reported in [Co/Pt]/Ru multilayers by Hellwig et al. (refs 30–32), Hauet et al. (ref. 32), Barker et al. (ref. 33, PRB 2024) and Darwin et al. (ref. 35), and the authors themselves describe the states as AFM–FM phase coexistence. The paper should state explicitly what is new beyond those works (sublattice selectivity, the same sublattice hosting both families, Néel chirality, size scale). Evidence for Néel chirality is only in the SI, and the main text does not show whether the in-plane wall components reverse with the core as a fixed DMI requires. The authors should show, in the main text or Extended Data, tilt-dependent Lorentz/Fresnel contrast or DPC wall-vector maps for both families (especially the inverse-polarity tubes), demonstrate uniform Néel chirality through all ten CoFeB layers without Bloch lines, give the topological charge per layer from the simulations, and state whether the inverse-polarity textures are chiral skyrmions or achiral bubbles.

7. The "general materials-design principle" is claimed from a single stack with no control sample (Discussion; Outlook). All results come from one composition and thickness set; sister samples were "deposited together", and no second growth run or second piece is shown to reproduce the field windows. No sample tests whether the selectivity follows the engineered asymmetry. The authors should measure at least one control stack with the same XMCD protocol: a symmetric CoFeB/CoFeB or CoB/CoB SAF with matched moments, a stack with a thicker Ru spacer (weaker RKKY), or one with the Keff or thickness asymmetry reversed, which should move the textures into CoB. They should also state whether the result is reproduced on more than one sample. Failing that, the claim should be narrowed to this stack.

8. The correlative comparison combines different samples, substrates, field geometries, calibrations and temperatures without checking their equivalence (Methods; Figs. 2 and 3). MFM and SQUID-VSM were on Si/SiO2, Lorentz TEM on 25-nm Si3N4 membranes, and XMCD and SAXS on 200-nm Si3N4 membranes; membranes can change strain and anisotropy, so the field windows need not coincide. Matched states occur at different fields: conventional +120 (MFM) versus −131 mT (DPC), maze +80 versus −89 mT, inverse +45 versus −49 mT. The DPC images are taken at a fixed 17° tilt with only the out-of-plane field quoted, so an in-plane field of about 0.3·Hz (about 40 mT at −131 mT) is present; in an AF-coupled stack with a soft sublattice (Keff = 0.11 MJ m⁻³) this can cause canting or spin-flop and bias, distort or nucleate textures, and Fig. 2h shows strongly anisotropic SAXS for the maze state. The three field calibrations (Hall sensor in the TEM holder, ALS electromagnet, MFM coil) are not cross-checked, and the sample temperature in the cryocooled XMCD/SAXS electromagnet is not stated. The authors should overlay out-of-plane loops on all three substrates (for example the XMCD total loop and a Lorentz field series against SQUID), cross-check the field calibrations, state the temperature for every technique, and quantify the in-plane field effect by comparing tilts or simulating with the tilted field.

9. The return-point-memory analysis does not support the mechanistic conclusion drawn from it (Fig. 5; "Return-point memory" section; Supplementary Note 5). High within-family and low cross-family RPM (0.03–0.05, Fig. 5c) show that the two families use different sites, but not that one is "external-field-driven" and the other "RKKY-driven". Defects favouring a down core in an up background need not favour an up core in a down background, and along a continuous descending sweep (path 2 → 4) the inverse-polarity skyrmions are the last unreversed CoFeB regions, so they would naturally sit between former conventional sites; low overlap then follows from geometry. No chance baseline is given, although the families differ in density, size and MFM contrast; if chance level is well above 0.03, the families are anti-correlated, which is a different and interesting result. The authors should report the RPM for randomly placed skyrmions at the measured densities or for rigidly shifted/rotated maps, state how bright and dark features are matched given the contrast inversion, give the number of areas and samples studied, add a spatial cross-correlation (for example the nearest-neighbour distance from each inverse skyrmion to former conventional sites), and soften or separately support the mechanistic interpretation.

10. The MFM tip may perturb the textures (MFM methods; Figs. 2 and 5). The Co-coated, high-Q tip sits about 20 nm above a stack whose soft sublattice has low Keff and whose total moment nearly cancels, so tip-induced nucleation or displacement could affect imaged densities and the RPM statistics, especially near the maze-to-skyrmion transitions, since the same region is rescanned after each cycle. The authors should show that repeated scans at fixed field, scan-direction comparisons, larger lift heights or cycles without scanning give the same positions and densities.
