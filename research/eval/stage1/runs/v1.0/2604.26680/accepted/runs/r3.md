Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors build a compensated synthetic antiferromagnet (SAF), [Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]x10, in which the two sublattices have matched M_s·t but different anisotropy. They report two families of textures within one field sweep. At high field, "conventional-polarity" skyrmions form (cores antiparallel to the field, AF tubes in an FM background). At low field, "inverse-polarity" skyrmions form (cores parallel to the field, FM tubes in an AF background). On the basis of element-resolved XMCD loops, DPC quantification and micromagnetics, they argue that both families live only in the CoFeB sublattice and are driven by the RKKY field from a uniform CoB sublattice. If the sublattice selection holds up, it is a useful design idea for layer-selective 3D spin textures. The phenomenology of AF/FM phase coexistence in perpendicular SAFs is not new, however (Hellwig et al., Hauet et al., and Barker et al. PRB 2024 from the same groups). What is new is mainly the sublattice selectivity, and that rests on one decomposition step, discussed below.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The data are well correlated across techniques: MFM, resonant SAXS, Lorentz DPC-STEM, element-resolved XMCD, SQUID-VSM and XRR. Fig. 2 shows the same sequence of states (FM, conventional skyrmions, maze, inverse skyrmions, SAF) in real and reciprocal space.
- The DPC is quantitative. A tilt-corrected step of 12.8 ± 0.7 T·nm against 13.6 ± 0.2 T·nm expected from the measured M_s·t is a strong check that one full sublattice reverses locally.
- The sample metrology is careful: XRR thicknesses with uncertainties, cross-section STEM, and a compensated m(H) with about 1% remanence.
- The return-point-memory experiment (Fig. 5) is a reasonable attempt to separate nucleation pathways and gives clear within-family reproducibility.
- The design rule is stated clearly (strong AF RKKY, moment compensation, sublattice asymmetry in K_eff and D_eff) and could be tested by others.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The headline claim that "all textures reside solely in the CoFeB layers" while "the CoB sublattice remains laterally uniform" rests on a decomposition with one free parameter, fixed by fitting.
   - Where: Fig. 3d (the "fully square" CoB loop) is I_Co − a·I_Fe, with a = 0.35. Methods say a was "determined empirically by adjusting a until the reconstructed total loop matched the SQUID-VSM loop".
   - Why this matters: the CoFeB loop (Fig. 3b) has large non-hysteretic linear slopes beyond ±30 mT, exactly where the textures appear. Any error in a adds or removes a fraction of that slope in the reconstructed CoB loop. Whether the CoB loop is square, rather than also slanted, is therefore set by the choice of a, not measured independently.
   - The fitting target is weak. The SQUID-VSM loop was measured on the sample grown on oxidized Si. The XMCD was measured on a sister sample on a 200 nm Si₃N₄ membrane, whose switching fields and slopes need not be identical.
   - The other techniques cannot settle which sublattice hosts the textures. The DPC result (12.8 versus 13.6 T·nm) only shows that one sublattice reverses. Because M_s·t is the same for CoB and CoFeB by design (Methods), DPC cannot say which one. MFM and SAXS are likewise not element-selective as presented.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Determine a independently, for example by Co- and Fe-edge XMCD on the CoFeB-only reference multilayer already used for K_eff, or from tabulated cross-sections plus the known Co content.
     - Show the CoB loop for a range of a (for example 0.25 to 0.45), with an explicit upper bound on the fraction of CoB reversed at 45, 80 and 120 mT.
     - Ideally, provide element-resolved real-space or reciprocal-space evidence: Fe- versus Co-edge resonant SAXS intensities (the edge used for Figs. 2f–j is not stated), or STXM/holography at both edges at the COSMIC beamline. If the textures are in CoFeB only, the Co-edge magnetic scattering should scale with the Co content of CoFeB alone.
   - Without one of these, "unequivocally attribute" (Introduction) and "solely" (Abstract) are stronger than the evidence.

B. Should address

2. The novelty relative to the known AF/FM phase coexistence in perpendicular SAFs is not stated clearly.
   - In any SAF, a local switch between the FM and AF states reverses exactly one sublattice. So an AF bubble in an FM background ("conventional") and an FM bubble in an AF background ("inverse") are both expected along the descending branch. Hellwig et al. (refs 30–32), Hauet et al. (ref. 32) and Barker et al. (ref. 33) reported closely related coexistence.
   - The genuinely new element is that the same sublattice (CoFeB) hosts both families, together with the evidence that these are chiral Néel skyrmions rather than achiral bubbles.
   - The authors should state this explicitly against refs 30–33, and should show the Néel and chirality evidence (Supplementary Note 4) for both families, not only one. A brief discussion of topological charge per layer would also help, since "skyrmion" is used for both families.

3. There is no control sample, so the "general materials-design principle" is not tested.
   - Only one stack composition is measured.
   - The Discussion states that the asymmetry in D_eff/K_eff is decisive, and that "reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers". The latter is said to be confirmed by simulation, but no simulation is shown.
   - A symmetric SAF (CoFeB/CoFeB or CoB/CoB with matched moments), or a sample with altered Ru thickness, measured with the same XMCD protocol, would show whether the selectivity follows the asymmetry as claimed. At minimum, the simulations varying J_RKKY and K_eff should be shown, for example as a state diagram.

4. The micromagnetic model departs from the experimental structure in ways that bear on the layer-selectivity claim.
   - (i) The stacking order is reversed. Experimentally, CoFeB sits above Ru/Pt and CoB is the topmost FM layer (Fig. 1a; Methods). In Fig. 4e, CoB is the bottom FM layer and CoFeB the top. The text then discusses a "topmost CoFeB layer" coupled on one side only. This end-layer effect, and any comparison with the surface-weighted MFM, therefore refers to a stack that differs from the measured one.
   - (ii) CoB is modelled with the CoFeB thickness and M_s (0.81 instead of 0.63 MA m⁻¹), with K_u rescaled to keep K_eff. This removes the thickness and M_s asymmetry that the Discussion credits for the D_eff balance. As a result, the simulated selectivity tests essentially only the K_eff asymmetry.
   - (iii) D is a free parameter (1.00 versus 1.10 mJ m⁻²), and it is not said how it was chosen or how sensitive the result is to it.
   - (iv) Each state is relaxed from a random configuration at fixed field, at T = 0, in a 400 × 400 nm box with unstated boundary conditions. The experiments are history-dependent field sweeps (Fig. 5a).
   - What the authors should do: simulate the experimental stacking order and the real CoB M_s and thickness, or show these do not matter. Report how D was chosen and how sensitive the result is to it. Simulate a field sweep and compare the simulated total and sublattice loops with Figs. 1c, 3b and 3d. Compare simulated and measured skyrmion diameters against field.

5. The effective-field picture is qualitative and needs a quantitative check.
   - J_RKKY = −0.034 mJ m⁻² is obtained from μ0H_s M_s t_FM/2 with H_s = 125 mT, which is a bilayer spin-flip expression.
   - In a 10-repeat stack, interior layers couple on both sides. The approach to saturation in Fig. 1c also proceeds through domain states, not uniform spin-flip, so demagnetizing energy contributes to H_s.
   - The prefactor and the resulting H_RKKY on CoFeB (about 125 mT if both neighbours count) should be justified. The authors should then show that the fields at which the effective field "changes sign" (the maze state near 80 mT) and at which each family appears agree quantitatively with H_ext − H_RKKY plus the stray/demagnetizing field. As written, the effective field would vanish near 125 mT, not near 80 mT.

6. The interpretation of the return-point-memory results is not unique.
   - Cross-type RPM of 0.03–0.05 (Fig. 5c) is taken as evidence for "distinct pinning/formation mechanisms".
   - Along a continuous descending sweep (path 2 → 4), inverse-polarity skyrmions are the last CoFeB regions not yet reversed, so they would naturally sit between the former conventional-skyrmion sites. Low overlap then follows from geometry, without any separate mechanism.
   - The authors should:
     - state how conventional (bright) and inverse (dark) features are matched given the contrast inversion;
     - give the RPM expected for randomly placed skyrmions at the measured densities;
     - add a spatial cross-correlation, for example the nearest-neighbour distance from each inverse skyrmion to the former conventional sites, to separate anticorrelation from independence.

7. The correlative comparison combines different samples, substrates and field calibrations.
   - MFM and SQUID were measured on Si/SiO₂, Lorentz on 25 nm Si₃N₄, and XMCD/SAXS on 200 nm Si₃N₄.
   - Matched states occur at different fields in different techniques: conventional +120 (MFM) versus −131 mT (DPC); maze +80 versus −89 mT; inverse +45 versus −49 mT.
   - In Lorentz TEM the sample is tilted by 17°, so an in-plane field of about 0.3·H_z is present. Quoted fields are the out-of-plane component only. In a soft SAF sublattice (K_eff = 0.11 MJ m⁻³), an in-plane field can bias the textures, and Fig. 2h shows strongly anisotropic SAXS for the maze state.
   - The sample temperature for the cryocooled XMCD/SAXS electromagnet is not stated.
   - What the authors should do: report out-of-plane loops for the membrane samples (XMCD total loop versus SQUID), state the temperatures for every technique, and discuss the in-plane field component in TEM and the anisotropy seen in SAXS.

8. Possible MFM tip influence.
   - The Co-coated tip sits about 20 nm above a stack whose soft sublattice has low K_eff and whose total moment nearly cancels.
   - Tip-induced nucleation or displacement could affect both the imaged densities and the RPM, since the same region is rescanned after each cycle.
   - The authors should show that images taken at larger lift height, or after a cycle without scanning, give the same skyrmion positions and densities.

C. Minor

9. Discussion, final paragraph on the mechanism: "at zero external field, the strong AFM RKKY interaction saturates the CoFeB layers in the up state". CoB stays up (Fig. 3d) and the RKKY field on CoFeB is negative, so this should presumably read "down state". Please check against Fig. 6.

10. The spacer between CoFeB and CoB is Ru(0.6 nm)/Pt(0.9 nm), not Ru alone (Fig. 1a; Methods). The repeated phrase "across the 0.6 nm ruthenium spacers" should mention the Pt insertion, which affects J_RKKY.

11. Methods say the 6 Å discretization equals "the experimental value for CoFeB", but XRR gives 6.69 ± 0.07 Å. Please state how the thickness mismatch was handled in M_s·t and J_RKKY/t.

12. Skyrmion diameters differ between MFM and DPC (81 versus 58 nm; 64 versus 48 nm). This is attributed to MFM broadening, but the measurements were also made at different fields and on different substrates. A diameter-versus-field comparison at matched fields would make the size trends in Supplementary Note 3 more convincing.

13. Fig. 3: the y axes are "inverted diode current (a.u.)" with different offsets. Please state the normalization and confirm that the linear relation between transmitted intensity and m holds for the absorption of this 10-repeat stack at both edges.

14. In Fig. 3a, the text says the Co loop is "dominated by" CoB on the basis of the 9 nm versus 7 nm thicknesses. The Co atomic content (about 5.9 versus 2.7 nm Co-equivalent) and the different Co moments in the two alloys are the relevant numbers and should be given.

15. Extended Data Table 2 lists J_RKKY = −0.03 mJ m⁻² for both sublattices. For clarity, note that this is a single interlayer parameter, and give its uncertainty as propagated from H_s.

16. The Abstract and Introduction use "unequivocally" and "solely". Please temper these to match the bounds obtained in concern 1.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add element-resolved real-space imaging (STXM or holography at the Fe and Co L3 edges) at one field from each family. This would turn the sublattice assignment from an inference into a direct observation.
2. Add a symmetric-SAF or variable-asymmetry control series to support the claimed general design rule, together with simulated state diagrams in J_RKKY and K_eff.
3. Rerun the micromagnetics with the experimental stacking order and the real CoB parameters as a field sweep, and compare total and per-sublattice loops quantitatively with Figs. 1c and 3.
4. Strengthen the RPM analysis with random-placement baselines and spatial cross-correlation, so that "distinct nucleation mechanisms" is separated from the geometric consequence of a single reversal sequence.
