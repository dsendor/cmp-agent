Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors build a moment-compensated synthetic antiferromagnet (SAF), [Ru/Pt/CoFeB(0.67 nm)/Ru/Pt/CoB(0.87 nm)]x10. In it they report two skyrmion families with opposite core polarity, appearing in different field windows. One is conventional-polarity: antiferromagnetic tubes in a ferromagnetic background, near +120 mT. The other is inverse-polarity: ferromagnetic tubes in an antiferromagnetic background, near +45 mT. They argue that both families live only in the CoFeB sublattice and come from competition between the applied field and the RKKY field of the CoB layers. Confining 3D textures to one chosen sublattice of an asymmetric SAF, with core polarity set by the field regime, is a clear and potentially useful design idea. The correlative approach (MFM, resonant SAXS, Lorentz DPC, element-resolved XMCD, micromagnetics, return-point memory) is broad. The weak point is the central attribution: that the textures sit in CoFeB and not in CoB. It rests mainly on one element-resolved loop decomposition with an empirically tuned parameter, and on simulations with a free DMI parameter.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A well-characterized compensated stack: XRR thicknesses, cross-sectional STEM (Fig. 1b), SQUID-VSM loop with a 1% Ms remanence plateau (Fig. 1c), and sublattice parameters (Extended Data Table 1). These are internally consistent; for example, Keff = Ku - mu0 Ms^2/2 holds for both sublattices.
- The same field sequence (FM, conventional skyrmions, maze, inverse skyrmions, SAF) appears in three independent probes: MFM, SAXS and DPC (Fig. 2).
- The DPC integrated-induction step (12.8 +/- 0.7 T nm) agrees quantitatively with full reversal of one sublattice across all ten repeats (13.6 T nm). This is a convincing argument that one sublattice is laterally uniform.
- The Fe-edge loop (Fig. 3b) directly shows that the CoFeB moment evolves continuously outside the +/-30 mT plateau.
- The cross-type RPM analysis (Fig. 5c) shows the two families use different pinning sites. This supports their being distinct textures rather than one texture seen at different fields.
- The proposed design principle is simple and could be transferred to other stacks: strong AF coupling, moment compensation and sublattice asymmetry in Keff and D.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The assignment of the textures to CoFeB rather than CoB is not established independently of fitted or tuned quantities (Fig. 3, Eqs. 1-2, Methods "Soft X-ray techniques", DPC section).
   - **DPC cannot tell the sublattices apart.** The layers are moment-compensated by design (Ms t equal, Methods; 8.85e-8 A m^2 each). Full reversal of all ten CoB layers would give exactly the same 13.6 T nm induction step as full reversal of all ten CoFeB layers. So the DPC result shows that one sublattice switches while the other stays uniform, but not which one. The sentence "the DPC results suggest that the CoFeB and CoB sublattices exhibit two different magnetization states" is fair. The implied attribution to CoFeB is not. In addition, the DPC zero reference is "set by assumptions informed primarily by the XMCD I(H) loops" (Methods), so DPC is not independent of the XMCD analysis.
   - **The XMCD decomposition depends on a scaling factor fixed by the very result it is used to show.** The attribution therefore rests on Eqs. 1-2. The factor a = 0.35 is chosen "empirically" so that the reconstructed total matches the SQUID-VSM loop with a closed central area. The squareness of I_CoB (Fig. 3d) then follows from that choice.
   - **Different samples.** The SQUID-VSM loop was measured on Si/SiO2, while the XMCD loops were measured on a 200-nm Si3N4 membrane. Matching the two assumes the loops on the two substrates are identical.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Show how I_CoB(H) changes as a is varied over a plausible range, and give an uncertainty on a.
     - Fix a independently. One route is from the saturated XMCD amplitudes together with the known equal sublattice moments. Another is from tabulated Co and Fe L3 cross-sections and the Co content of Co40Fe40B20 relative to Co68B32.
     - Give a quantitative upper bound on how much of the CoB net moment could be reversed in the maze and skyrmion states, given the noise in Fig. 3d.
   - **Most direct test:** element-resolved real-space or reciprocal-space data at both edges. Options are resonant SAXS at the Fe L3 edge compared with the Co L3 edge, normalized by the respective XMCD amplitudes, or STXM/ptychography images at both edges. The caption of Fig. 2f-j does not state which edge the SAXS patterns were taken at. If both edges were recorded, the intensity ratio alone would largely settle the question.
   - Without one of these, "we unequivocally attribute the observed textures to the CoFeB sublattice" and "all textures reside solely in the CoFeB layers" (abstract) are not yet demonstrated.

B. Should address

2. The micromagnetic support for confinement to CoFeB depends on a free DMI parameter, and the simulation cell is near the exchange and wall length scales (Methods "Micromagnetic modelling"; Extended Data Table 2; Discussion).
   - **D was free.** D = 1.10 (CoFeB) and 1.00 (CoB) mJ m^-2 were treated as free parameters, with A = 8 pJ m^-1 fixed for both layers.
   - **CoFeB sits near the instability threshold.** With these values, kappa = pi D / (4 sqrt(A Keff)) is about 0.92 for CoFeB and about 0.59 for CoB. CoFeB is therefore placed close to the domain-wall instability threshold, and the outcome that textures form only in CoFeB is largely set by this choice.
   - **The Discussion's argument is not modelled.** The Discussion attributes the asymmetry to a larger Deff/Keff in CoFeB, where Deff = D/t. But the simulation gives CoB the CoFeB thickness and Ms, so the thickness dependence invoked there is not in the model. Real CoB has lower Ms, which changes its self-demagnetization and wall energy; the rescaled Ku keeps Keff but not these.
   - **Claimed simulations are not shown.** The text states that "reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers, whereas stronger coupling localizes the non-collinear state". These simulations are not presented.
   - **Resolution.** The lateral cells are 4 nm, against an exchange length of about 4.4 nm and a wall parameter sqrt(A/Keff) of about 8.5 nm for CoFeB. The small CoB perturbations near CoFeB walls (Fig. 4) are exactly the feature the mechanism relies on, and they are only a few cells wide.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Present a phase map of which sublattice hosts textures versus D_CoB, D_CoFeB (or Keff_CoB) and J_RKKY.
     - Show the RKKY-strength simulations quoted in the text.
     - Run a mesh-convergence check at 2 nm cells.
     - If possible, measure D for both sublattices, for example by BLS on reference films or from the domain-period analysis of ref. 48, since D is the parameter the conclusion depends on.

3. The simulated stack order is inverted relative to the experimental stack, and the top-layer discussion follows from the inverted order (Fig. 4e versus Fig. 1a and Methods).
   - In the experiment each repeat is Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB from the bottom up, so the topmost magnetic layer is CoB and the bottommost is CoFeB.
   - Fig. 4e shows Pt/CoB/Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru, with CoFeB on top.
   - The text then discusses the "very top CoFeB layer ... coupled to CoB on one side only, with vacuum above". In the real sample that boundary layer is at the bottom (on the substrate side), and the top layer is CoB.
   - This matters because MFM is most sensitive to the topmost layers. It may also affect the claim that CoB stays uniform, since the outermost CoB has only one CoFeB neighbour and the weakest RKKY pinning.
   - The authors should repeat the simulations with the experimental stacking order, or justify why it is irrelevant, and correct the top-layer discussion.

4. The effective-field mechanism is argued qualitatively but not tested quantitatively against the measured field windows (Discussion, Fig. 6; Methods J_RKKY).
   - The model predicts that the CoFeB layers behave as an ordinary CoFeB skyrmion multilayer in an effective field H - H_RKKY.
   - From Extended Data Table 1, the RKKY field on an interior CoFeB layer from its two CoB neighbours is 2|J|/(Ms t), about 125 mT (about 63 mT per neighbour). The authors should compute H_eff for each observed state: conventional skyrmions at +120 mT, maze at +80 mT, inverse skyrmions at +45 mT. They should then show that these H_eff values map onto the conventional skyrmion, maze and skyrmion windows of a CoFeB-only reference multilayer, with signs reversed as appropriate.
   - The CoFeB-only and CoB-only reference multilayers already exist; they were used for Keff. Imaging them by MFM across a field sweep would provide the natural reference control:
     - If the CoB-only multilayer also hosts skyrmions in some field window, the authors need to explain why the RKKY field suppresses them in the SAF.
     - If the CoFeB windows do not map onto the SAF windows after the H_RKKY shift, the simple effective-field picture is incomplete. For example, dipolar coupling between tubes would matter.

5. The J_RKKY extraction is approximate, and the mechanism depends on it (Methods; Extended Data Table 1).
   - J = mu0 Hs Ms t / 2 with mu0 Hs = 125 mT is a bilayer expression. In a [ ]x10 stack the interior layers couple to two neighbours, which changes the prefactor by about a factor of two.
   - In a perpendicular SAF, the saturation field reached through domain and maze states (Fig. 1c, saturation near 150-170 mT) also contains domain-wall and dipolar energy, not only RKKY.
   - The same J (-0.03 mJ m^-2) is then used in the simulations.
   - The authors should justify the formula for a multilayer or extract J independently, for example from a hard-axis loop, from the field of the plateau edge, or from a bilayer reference. They should also state how the simulated field windows (-130, -80, -50 mT) change with J.

6. The probes used different substrates, field geometries and calibrations, and the comparison assumes they are equivalent (Methods; Figs. 2 and 3).
   - **Substrates.** MFM and SQUID-VSM were measured on Si/SiO2, Lorentz TEM on 25-nm Si3N4 and soft X-ray on 200-nm Si3N4. Membranes can change strain and anisotropy, so the field windows need not coincide. The authors should show hysteresis loops (for example the XMCD total loop and a Lorentz field series) demonstrating that the plateau and saturation fields match across substrates.
   - **Tilt in Lorentz DPC.** The DPC images are taken at a fixed 17 deg tilt, with "field values ... the out-of-plane component". The in-plane component is then about 0.3 times that, around 40 mT at -131 mT. In an AF-coupled stack, an in-plane field can cause canting or spin-flop and can distort or nucleate textures. The authors should quantify this effect, for example by comparing tilts or simulating with the tilted field.
   - **Field calibration.** The three field calibrations (Hall sensor in the TEM holder, ALS electromagnet, MFM coil) should be cross-checked.

7. The RPM conclusions about "external-field-driven" versus "RKKY-driven" nucleation go beyond what the RPM data show (Fig. 5; Supplementary Note 5).
   - **No chance baseline.** Cross-family RPM values of 0.03-0.05 are given without a chance-level baseline. The two families have different densities and sizes and opposite MFM contrast. The authors should report the RPM for maps rigidly shifted or rotated relative to each other, and state how opposite-contrast maps were overlaid.
   - **Possible tip effects.** The MFM tip (Co coated, about 20 nm lift, high Q) may perturb textures in the low-Keff CoFeB layers, especially near the maze-to-skyrmion transitions. The authors should test for tip-induced nucleation or displacement, for example by repeated scans at fixed field or by scan-direction comparisons.
   - **Overreach.** High within-family and low cross-family RPM show distinct pinning sites. They do not by themselves identify the driving force for nucleation, so this conclusion should be softened or supported separately.

8. The relation to earlier work on AF/FM phase domains in perpendicular SAFs, and the topological characterization of the inverse-polarity textures, need sharpening (Introduction; Discussion "form of phase coexistence"; Supplementary Note 4).
   - Field-driven FM bubbles in an AF background, and AF regions in an FM background, were reported in [Co/Pt]/Ru multilayers (refs. 30-33, especially Hellwig et al. 2003 and Barker et al. 2024). The authors themselves describe the states as AFM-FM phase coexistence.
   - The paper should state explicitly what is new beyond those works: sublattice selectivity, Néel chirality, size scale.
   - It should show that both families, and in particular the inverse-polarity tubes, have uniform Néel chirality through all ten CoFeB layers and no Bloch lines. That would justify calling them skyrmions rather than bubble or phase domains.
   - Fresnel tilt-series images at both polarities and an estimate of the topological charge per layer from the simulations would help.

9. A general design principle is claimed from a single stack (Discussion; Outlook).
   - All results come from one composition and thickness set; sister samples were "deposited together". The paper does not show reproducibility across depositions, device-to-device spread, or a second stack in which the asymmetry is reversed. Swapping the Keff ordering or the thicknesses should move the textures into CoB.
   - Even one additional stack, with RKKY or Keff varied, would turn the "general materials-design principle" from an extrapolation into a demonstration. Failing that, the claim should be stated more narrowly.

C. Minor

10. Units, Results ("Element-resolved magnetometry"): the text says "9 nm Co68B32 versus 7 nm Co40Fe40B20", but the layers are 0.9 nm and 0.7 nm (9 Å and 7 Å).

11. Fig. 2f-j, SAXS:
    - State the photon energy or edge and the helicity used for each pattern.
    - Give the q of the ring maximum and the corresponding period, and compare it with the MFM periods.
    - Explain the strongly one-sided crescent in Fig. 2h (maze state), when the MFM maze in Fig. 2c looks isotropic. Is it an artifact of the beam-stop or saturated-state subtraction, or a sign of in-plane anisotropy from field history?

12. Skyrmion diameters are compared across techniques at mismatched fields: MFM at +120 mT and DPC at -131 mT; MFM at +45 mT and DPC at -49 mT. The conventional skyrmions differ by about 30% (64 versus 48 nm). A size-versus-field plot for both families, with the MFM tip-convolution estimate in the main text, would make the "opposite trends" statement checkable.

13. The DPC value (12.8 +/- 0.7 versus 13.6 +/- 0.2 T nm) should state:
    - how the tilt correction and its uncertainty (tilt-angle error) enter the error bar;
    - whether stray fields above and below the tilted film are accounted for.

14. Micromagnetics details:
    - Give the boundary conditions of the 400 x 400 nm box, which holds only a few skyrmions.
    - State how the Ru and Pt spacers are represented in z (number of empty cells) and whether interlayer dipolar coupling is computed fully.
    - Explain the negative field convention, which is opposite to the positive-branch experimental fields in Fig. 2.
    - The simulations are at T = 0 while the experiments are at room temperature. Note this, and say whether the input parameters are room-temperature values.

15. Extended Data Table 2:
    - The caption reads "for of each sublattice".
    - The CoB row (Ms = 0.81, Ku = 0.63) should be flagged as an effective, rescaled description rather than the measured CoB values.
    - Extended Data Table 1 gives J_RKKY = -0.034 mJ m^-2 while Table 2 uses -0.03; state the rounding.

16. Wording:
    - "Unequivocally", "solely" and "the CoB sublattice remains laterally uniform" sit uneasily with the simulations, which show CoB perturbations at CoFeB walls (Fig. 4). Phrase the claim as "the net CoB moment remains saturated to within X%".
    - Fig. 3: the axis label "inverted diode current" and the statement that I(H) is linear in m(H) need a sentence on why the linear approximation of the exponential transmission holds for this XMCD contrast.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Settle the sublattice attribution directly with element-resolved data: resonant SAXS or STXM at both the Fe and Co L3 edges in the conventional-skyrmion, maze and inverse-skyrmion states. Also fix the XMCD scaling factor a without reference to the SQUID loop.
2. Image the existing CoFeB-only and CoB-only reference multilayers across a field sweep, and plot all three systems against the effective field H - H_RKKY. This would turn the mechanism in Fig. 6 into a quantitative prediction.
3. Redo the micromagnetics with the experimental stacking order, a finer mesh, and a scan over D, Keff and J_RKKY. Include the RKKY-variation results already mentioned in the text.
4. Add a second stack with the sublattice asymmetry reversed or the RKKY coupling changed, to demonstrate the claimed design principle rather than infer it.
