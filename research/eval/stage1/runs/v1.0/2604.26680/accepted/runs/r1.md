Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors build a moment-compensated synthetic antiferromagnet (SAF), [Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]x10, from two chemically distinct sublattices. They report two families of skyrmions with opposite core polarity in separate field windows. Conventional-polarity skyrmions (AFM-aligned tubes in an FM background) appear near 120 mT, and inverse-polarity skyrmions (FM-aligned tubes in an AFM background) appear near 45 mT, with a maze state between them. The authors attribute both families to the CoFeB sublattice alone, driven by an effective field equal to the external field plus the RKKY field from CoB. Confining textures to one chosen sublattice and changing their polarity with field is a new idea for 3D spin textures in SAFs, and the multi-technique dataset (MFM, resonant SAXS, Lorentz DPC, element-resolved XMCD, micromagnetics) is substantial. The central new claim is that the CoB layers stay uniform. In the paper this rests mainly on one empirically scaled subtraction of XMCD loops.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Correlated real-space (MFM, DPC) and reciprocal-space (SAXS) imaging across the whole field sweep, with consistent contrast reversal between the two families (Fig. 2).
- A quantitative DPC check: 12.8 ± 0.7 T nm measured against 13.6 ± 0.2 T nm expected for lateral variations confined to a single sublattice. A conventional SAF tube, with both sublattices reversed, would give no net-induction contrast (Fig. 2n).
- The Fe L3 loop (Fig. 3b) directly shows gradual CoFeB reversal outside the ±30 mT plateau. The texture-hosting role of CoFeB is therefore measured directly, not inferred.
- Return-point-memory analysis (Fig. 5) gives repeatable statistics that separate the nucleation sites of the two families.
- The micromagnetic simulations reproduce the sequence of states with parameters close to the measured ones (Fig. 4, Extended Data Tables 1 and 2).
- The magnetic parameters in Extended Data Tables 1 and 2 are internally consistent: Keff = Ku − μ0Ms²/2 holds for every entry.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The evidence that CoB stays uniform depends on an empirically chosen scaling factor whose physical meaning is not established (Fig. 3, Eqs. 1–2, Methods "Soft X-ray techniques").
   - What is wrong: the claim that CoB "remains laterally uniform" (abstract, Fig. 3d, "Element-resolved magnetometry") rests on I_CoB = I_Co − a·I_Fe with a = 0.35. The value of a was chosen so that the reconstructed total in Eq. 1 matches the SQUID-VSM loop.
   - Why the two uses of a conflict: Eq. 1 can be rewritten as I_total = I_Co + (1 − a)·I_Fe. It reproduces the net moment only if diode current converts to moment with the same factor at the Co and Fe edges. Nothing justifies that, since absorption cross-sections, elemental fractions (Co68B32 vs Co40Fe40B20) and transmitted backgrounds differ. The a that makes Eq. 1 match SQUID-VSM therefore need not equal the ratio r of the CoFeB Co-edge signal to its Fe-edge signal, which is what Eq. 2 needs to isolate CoB.
   - Why it matters: if a ≠ r, the "fully square" CoB loop (Fig. 3d) is partly a product of the fit. A CoB contribution to the sloped regions at |μ0H| > 30 mT could be hidden or created. DPC cannot settle this, because Ms·t is the same for both sublattices (8.85 × 10⁻⁸ A m² each), so the 13.6 T nm expectation is identical for CoB-only and CoFeB-only textures. MFM is also not element-specific. The assignment to CoFeB rather than to both layers therefore rests on this decomposition and on the simulations.
   - What to do:
     - Measure r directly: record Co L3 and Fe L3 loops (or XMCD amplitudes) on the CoFeB-only reference multilayer used for Keff, in the same geometry.
     - Show the CoB loop for a range of a around the chosen value (for example 0.25–0.45). State how large a CoB reversal (fraction of area) would still be consistent with Fig. 3d within the noise.
     - If possible, give element-resolved scattering or imaging. Resonant SAXS at the Co L3 edge vs the Fe L3 edge, at the same fields, would show whether the Co-edge scattering exceeds what the CoFeB Co content explains. Co vs Fe XMCD imaging (STXM) of the same area would answer the question directly. The SAXS photon energy is not stated in Methods, so the existing data may already allow this comparison.

B. Should address

2. The RKKY coupling strength is extracted with a formula that does not fit this stack, and the effective-field picture is never tested quantitatively (Methods, "Sample fabrication"; Discussion; Fig. 6).
   - What is wrong with the extraction: J_RKKY = μ0·Hs·Ms·t_FM/2 with μ0Hs = 125 mT is the bilayer spin-flop expression. In a 20-layer stack, interior layers are coupled on both sides, which changes the prefactor (to about /4 for interior layers). More importantly, for perpendicular-anisotropy layers measured with an out-of-plane field, saturation runs through domain and skyrmion states (as Fig. 2 itself shows up to about 130 mT). The saturation field is then set by nucleation, DMI and dipolar energies, not by uniform exchange alone.
   - Why it matters: this value is fed directly into the simulations (Extended Data Table 2) and underlies the effective-field mechanism.
   - What to do:
     - Determine J_RKKY independently, for example from an in-plane hard-axis loop, from minor-loop shifts, or from a reference bilayer SAF with the same spacer.
     - Make the mechanism quantitative. With the stated parameters, the RKKY field on an interior CoFeB layer is about 2|J|/(Ms·t) ≈ 125 mT. Compare the predicted zero crossing of the CoFeB effective field, including the stack's demagnetizing field, with the observed maze window near 80 mT and with the boundaries of the two skyrmion windows.

3. The micromagnetic model differs from the real stack in ways that bear on the claims, and several stated simulation results are not shown (Fig. 4, Methods "Micromagnetic modelling", Extended Data Table 2, Discussion).
   - Stacking order is reversed:
     - Fig. 4e has CoB as the bottom FM layer and CoFeB as the top one, with vacuum above it. The experimental stack (Fig. 1a, Methods) has CoFeB at the bottom and CoB at the top.
     - The text's discussion of the "topmost CoFeB layer" with "vacuum above" therefore describes a configuration the sample does not have.
   - Model parameters depart from experiment:
     - CoB is given Ms = 0.81 MA m⁻¹ (measured 0.63) and the CoFeB thickness, with Ku rescaled.
     - D is a free parameter (1.0 and 1.1 mJ m⁻²), and A = 8 pJ m⁻¹ is assumed for both layers.
     - The thickness of the nonmagnetic spacers and the lateral boundary conditions are not given, although interlayer dipolar coupling depends on the real 1.5 nm Ru/Pt separation.
   - The comparison with experiment is qualitative only:
     - The simulated inverse-polarity skyrmion in Fig. 4c looks far smaller than the measured 58–81 nm diameters.
     - No simulated m(H) loop or skyrmion size versus field is compared with Fig. 1c or Supplementary Note 3.
   - Claims stated but not shown: "reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers" and "stronger coupling localizes the non-collinear state" (Discussion).
   - What to do:
     - Simulate the experimental stacking order with the real spacer thicknesses.
     - Show sweeps in J_RKKY, in the Keff asymmetry and in the D asymmetry, to identify which asymmetry confines the textures to CoFeB.
     - Compare simulated m(H) and skyrmion sizes with the data.

4. The outer layers are not treated, and the top layer is the one MFM is most sensitive to (Results, Fig. 4 discussion).
   - What is wrong: in the real stack the topmost FM layer is CoB. It has a CoFeB neighbour on one side only, so its RKKY field is about half that of interior CoB layers. By the authors' own argument, it is the CoB layer most likely to host textures. The bottom CoFeB layer is similarly coupled on one side only.
   - Why it matters: MFM is weighted towards the near-surface layers, so the outer layers affect both the "solely in CoFeB" claim and the reading of the MFM contrast.
   - What to do: address the outer layers explicitly, in simulations with the correct stacking order and in the XMCD sensitivity estimate from Concern 1. They contribute 1/10 of each sublattice signal, which should be resolvable.

5. The claim of a "general materials-design principle" rests on a single stack composition, with no experimental control, and the role of DMI is asserted rather than measured (Discussion, Outlook).
   - What is wrong: the Discussion attributes the confinement to CoFeB to a larger D_eff/K_eff in CoFeB and states that D_eff is "comparable" in both layers, but D was never measured. The claims that reducing RKKY restores textures in both layers, and that the design transfers to other compensated SAFs, are not tested on any sample.
   - What to do:
     - Measure D for each sublattice, for example by the Baćani et al. method (ref. 48) on the reference multilayers, or by Brillouin light scattering.
     - Measure at least one control stack under matched conditions: a symmetric CoFeB/CoFeB or CoB/CoB SAF, a stack with a thicker Ru spacer (weaker RKKY), or a stack with the anisotropy asymmetry reversed. If space does not allow this, narrow the claim to this stack.

6. The return-point-memory analysis does not support the mechanistic conclusion drawn from it (Fig. 5, "Return-point memory" section).
   - What is wrong: low cross-family RPM (0.03–0.05) shows that the two families use different sites. It does not show that one family is "external-field-driven" and the other "RKKY-driven". Defects that favour nucleating a down core in an up background need not favour an up core in a down background.
   - What is missing:
     - The chance-level RPM for two uncorrelated maps with the measured skyrmion densities. If chance level is well above 0.03, the families are anti-correlated (for example, inverse skyrmions sitting at interstitial sites of the conventional lattice), which is a different and interesting result.
     - How maps with opposite MFM contrast are overlaid.
     - How many areas and samples were studied.
     - Whether the MFM tip affects nucleation, given the low CoFeB Keff.
   - What to do: report the chance baseline and a spatial cross-correlation between the two families' positions, and rephrase the mechanistic interpretation.

7. The relation to earlier work, and whether the inverse-polarity textures are skyrmions, need clarifying (Introduction, Fig. 2, Supplementary Note 4).
   - Prior work: FM-aligned stripe and bubble domains in an AFM background, and phase coexistence in AFM-coupled perpendicular multilayers, are well known (Hellwig et al., refs 30–32; Barker et al., ref. 33; Darwin et al., ref. 35). The manuscript should state precisely what is new beyond confinement to one sublattice.
   - Topological character: evidence for the Néel chirality of both families is only in the SI, and the main text does not show whether the in-plane wall components reverse with the core in the way that a fixed DMI requires.
   - What to do: show tilt-dependent Lorentz contrast, or DPC wall-vector maps, for both families in the main text or Extended Data. State whether the inverse-polarity textures are chiral skyrmions or achiral bubbles.

8. Comparisons across samples and setups are not checked (Methods; Fig. 2 caption).
   - Different substrates and holders: MFM and SQUID-VSM were done on Si/SiO2, Lorentz TEM on 25-nm Si3N4 membranes, and XMCD and SAXS on 200-nm Si3N4 membranes.
   - Tilted field in the TEM: the 17° tilt adds an in-plane field of about 40 mT at the −131 mT image. The DPC fields (−131, −89, −49 mT) differ from the MFM fields (+120, +80, +45 mT) by about 10%.
   - Reproducibility not shown: no second growth run or second piece of the same growth run is shown to reproduce the field windows.
   - What to do: overlay the out-of-plane loops of the films on all three substrates. Discuss how the in-plane field component affects the textures. State whether the result is reproduced on more than one sample.

9. The sublattice saturation magnetizations are derived by assuming compensation (Methods; Extended Data Table 1).
   - What is wrong: Ms(CoB) and Ms(CoFeB) are obtained by dividing an "identical magnetic moment" of 8.85 × 10⁻⁸ A m² by each layer's thickness. Compensation is therefore assumed, not measured. Both the Ku values and the simulation inputs depend on this split.
   - What to do: measure Ms directly on the CoB and CoFeB reference multilayers already used for Keff. Report the resulting degree of compensation and its uncertainty.

C. Minor

10. The Discussion contradicts Fig. 6. It states that "at zero external field, the strong AFM RKKY interaction saturates the CoFeB layers in the up state". In Fig. 6 (SAF saturation) and by the authors' own logic, CoB is up and CoFeB is down at zero field, and the inverse-polarity skyrmions are up cores in down CoFeB. Please correct this.

11. SAXS needs more detail (Fig. 2f–j, Methods).
    - Give the photon energy and the exact fields.
    - Explain the strongly one-sided intensity in Fig. 2h. A centrosymmetric pattern would be expected from a purely charge-like magnetic contrast. Possible causes are a dichroic or chirality effect, the beamstop subtraction, or stripe alignment.
    - Quantify the peak q for each state. With the 0.031 nm⁻¹ scale bar the rings imply periods of roughly 150 nm, while the text says "length scales below 100 nm". Compare with the MFM spacings.

12. Diameter statistics (64 ± 4, 81 ± 4, 48 ± 5, 58 ± 6 nm) lack the number of skyrmions measured, a statement of whether the ± is s.d. or s.e.m., and the fitting procedure. MFM and DPC are also compared at different fields. Please add these details.

13. DPC quantification: say explicitly that the 13.6 T nm benchmark cannot tell the two sublattices apart, because Ms·t is equal. Explain how the zero reference and the 17° tilt correction were validated, for example with images at two tilt angles.

14. XMCD: justify that transmitted intensity is linear in m for this absorber thickness. State whether the Fe- and Co-edge loops were measured on the same spot and with the same field history. Comment on possible beam-induced heating during the loops.

15. Wording:
    - "0.6 nm ruthenium spacers": the coupling acts across Ru(6 Å)/Pt(9 Å), about 1.5 nm, which is relevant to the weak J.
    - "unequivocally attribute": too strong given Concern 1.
    - The links to computing in the abstract and Outlook are not supported by any device-level demonstration.

16. Fig. 4 is simulated at negative fields while Fig. 2 MFM is at positive fields. State the sign convention so the m_z colour bar can be compared directly with the MFM contrast.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Settle the CoB uniformity question with a reference-sample calibration of the Co/Fe edge ratio, plus element-resolved SAXS or STXM at both edges for the conventional, maze and inverse states.
2. Add a predictive, quantitative effective-field analysis, with a properly determined J_RKKY and the demagnetizing field, that locates the maze and polarity-reversal fields. Compare it with simulated m(H) for the real stacking order.
3. Add at least one control stack, either symmetric sublattices or weaker RKKY, to show experimentally that the second skyrmion family and the single-sublattice confinement come from the engineered asymmetry.
4. Turn the RPM result into a spatial statement with a chance baseline and a cross-correlation of the two families' positions, and link it to the pinning-site picture.
