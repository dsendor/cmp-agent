Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors study a moment-compensated synthetic antiferromagnet, Ta/[Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]x10, whose two sublattices are chemically different. They report two families of small (<100 nm) textures in one field sweep. At high field, conventional-polarity skyrmions appear (core antiparallel to the field). At low field, inverse-polarity skyrmions appear (core parallel to the field). The authors argue that both families live only in the CoFeB layers, driven by the competition between the applied field and the RKKY field from the CoB layers. Correlative use of MFM, resonant SAXS, Lorentz DPC-STEM, element-resolved XMCD, return-point memory (RPM) and micromagnetics on a single stack is a strength. Layer-selective textures with a field-selectable polarity would interest the skyrmion and SAF communities. However, the core physics overlaps strongly with known domain physics in AFM-coupled perpendicular multilayers: ferromagnetic-phase nucleation and tubes of one phase inside the other (Refs. 30-33, including the authors' own PRB 2024). The paper needs to state more sharply what is new beyond that work, and its two central claims rest on evidence that is currently partly indirect: (i) confinement to CoFeB and (ii) the topological (skyrmion) character of the inverse family.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A carefully grown, structurally well-characterised compensated SAF: XRR thicknesses with uncertainties, cross-section ADF-STEM (Fig. 1b), and a 1% remanence plateau (Fig. 1c).
- Real-space (MFM, DPC) and reciprocal-space (SAXS) data at a series of fields consistently show a contrast reversal between the high-field and low-field textures (Fig. 2).
- Quantitative DPC: the integrated-induction step of 12.8 ± 0.7 T nm agrees with the 13.6 ± 0.2 T nm expected from SQUID and XRR. This shows that only one sublattice reverses across a domain boundary.
- Element-resolved XMCD loops (Fig. 3) show that the gradual high-field reversal is carried by the Fe-containing (CoFeB) layers.
- The RPM data (Fig. 5) are a useful and uncommon statistical test of pinning: high same-family return-point memory (~0.85) and very low cross-family overlap (~0.04).
- The design concept (moment compensation, asymmetric anisotropy, interlayer exchange) is simple and could be reused.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The assignment of all textures to CoFeB, with CoB uniform, rests on a decomposition with an empirically tuned parameter. No element-specific spatial measurement tests it directly.
   - **What the paper does.** The CoB loop (Fig. 3d, Eq. 2) is obtained by subtracting a·I_Fe from I_Co. The value a = 0.35 is chosen "by adjusting a until the reconstructed total loop matched the SQUID-VSM loop" (Methods). The SQUID loop in Fig. 3c is itself "rescaled", which adds a free scale and offset.
   - **The weighting is not justified.** Eq. 1 adds transmitted intensities measured at two different edges. These have different non-magnetic backgrounds (~4,700 vs ~7,000 counts) and different XMCD magnitudes per unit moment, yet they are summed with unit weight for the Fe term.
   - **The result can be shaped by the choice of a.** As presented, the squareness of Fig. 3d (and hence "CoB remains laterally uniform") may depend on a. Fig. 3d also is not perfectly flat above 30 mT: the positive-field branch rises from ~2,420 to ~2,500, and the two branches do not coincide at high field.
   - **Why it matters.** MFM and DPC cannot tell which sublattice reverses. Because the moments are compensated, a CoB-hosted texture on a uniform CoFeB background gives the same MFM contrast and the same 13.6 T nm DPC step. Confinement to CoFeB is the headline claim.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - (a) Estimate a independently, from composition (Co content of 6.69 Å Co40Fe40B20 vs 8.69 Å Co68B32) and tabulated L3 XMCD cross-sections, or from a Co-edge loop of a CoFeB-only reference film. Compare this with 0.35.
     - (b) Show Fig. 3d for a range of a (e.g. 0.25-0.45) with an uncertainty on a.
     - (c) Turn the residual high-field slope of Fig. 3d into an upper bound on the CoB area fraction that could be reversed at 120 mT and 45 mT. Compare it with the core area fraction in Figs. 2b and 2d.
     - (d) Most directly, compare resonant SAXS intensities (or STXM/holography images) at the Co and Fe L3 edges at the same fields. The scattered intensity ratio should follow the CoFeB-only expectation if CoB carries no texture. State the photon energy used for Fig. 2f-j, which the paper does not currently give.

2. The skyrmionic, topological character of both families, and the claim that the two families carry opposite topological charge, are not shown in the main text.
   - **Where.** The text says the Néel nature "was verified experimentally using Lorentz microscopy as shown in Supplementary Note S4". It does not say whether this holds for both families or with which handedness.
   - **Why it matters.** The title ("Beyond conventional skyrmions") and the "inverse-polarity skyrmion" label depend on the low-field objects being homochiral Néel skyrmions with Q opposite to the high-field ones. They should not merely be up-magnetised CoFeB bubbles in an AFM background. Such ferromagnetic bubbles and tubes in AFM-coupled perpendicular multilayers are known (Refs. 30-33).
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Show tilt-series Fresnel or DPC data for both families in the main text, with the sign of the wall chirality for each.
     - Show that the chirality is the same and the core polarity opposite, so that Q is reversed.
     - Discuss explicitly how the inverse-polarity state differs from the FM-phase bubble domains of Refs. 30-33. This includes the authors' own PRB 109, 134437 (2024), which reports AFM/FM phase coexistence in a SAF.
   - The statements about skyrmion Hall angles and opposite transverse deflection rest on simulations that are "not shown". Either show them, or present the statements as predictions.

B. Should address

3. The micromagnetic model is described as "parameter-matched", but it departs from the experimental structure in ways that matter for the claims it supports.
   - **Inverted layer order.** Fig. 4e places CoFeB on top of each repeat and CoFeB as the topmost magnetic layer. The text confirms this ("very top CoFeB layer ... CoB below, vacuum above"). The real stack (Fig. 1a and Methods) is Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB, so CoB is topmost. The paper's own top-layer deviation result, and the layer nearest the MFM tip, therefore refer to a different structure.
   - **CoB layer changed.** CoB is modelled at 6 Å thickness with Ms = 0.81 MA/m and a rescaled Ku = 0.63 MJ/m³. The measured values are 8.69 Å, 0.63 MA/m and 0.47 MJ/m³. This changes the RKKY field on CoB (∝ J/Ms t) and the interlayer dipolar fields.
   - **Other choices not given or not justified:**
     - The CoFeB cell thickness is stated as "6 Å (same as the experimental value)", whereas the measured value is 6.69 Å.
     - The non-magnetic spacer thicknesses, which set the dipolar coupling, are not given.
     - D is a free parameter (1.0 and 1.10 mJ/m²), but the observable used to fix it is not stated.
     - Boundary conditions for the 400 nm × 400 nm box are not given. With ~50 nm skyrmions and periodic or open edges, the box size can set the texture density and phase boundaries.
   - **Unshown simulations.** Several supporting simulations are "not shown": the symmetric stack, the current-driven Hall angle, and the J_RKKY dependence quoted in the Discussion ("reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers").
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Simulate the actual layer order and thicknesses (or show that the results are insensitive to them).
     - State how D was fixed and give the spacer thicknesses and boundary conditions.
     - Add a simulated field sweep (m_z of each sublattice versus H) to compare directly with Figs. 3b and 3d.
     - Include the J_RKKY and K_eff-ratio phase map that underpins the "general design principle".

4. The interlayer exchange and the effective-field mechanism are described qualitatively and partly inconsistently.
   - **Spacer.** The Discussion and Fig. 6 caption refer to "the 0.7 nm Ru spacer(s)". The Methods give 6.03 Å Ru, and the actual spacer between each CoFeB and CoB is Ru(6 Å)/Pt(9 Å), ~1.5 nm.
   - **"Strong" coupling.** The design rules call the exchange "strong", but J_RKKY = −0.034 mJ/m² is an order of magnitude below typical Ru-only values.
   - **Extraction of J.** J is obtained from μ0Hs = 125 mT. In a loop where the slope above 30 mT comes from domain and skyrmion formation, the saturation field includes dipolar and nucleation contributions, not only exchange. Fig. 1c also appears to saturate nearer 150 mT than 125 mT.
   - **Quantitative check.** In the simple effective-field picture, H_eff(CoFeB) = H − 2|J|/(Ms t). With the stated parameters this crosses zero near ~125 mT, so the maze state should be centred there. Experimentally, the maze sits at ~80 mT, between conventional skyrmions at 120 mT and inverse skyrmions at 45 mT. The midpoint of the linear part of Fig. 3b is also ~90-95 mT.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Give H_eff for each imaged state.
     - Reconcile the window of texture fields with J, including the stack dipolar field. Alternatively, extract J independently, for example from the field at which the CoFeB m_z crosses zero in Fig. 3b, or from a minor-loop or FMR measurement.
     - Correct the spacer description.

5. The RPM analysis supports uncorrelated pinning sites, but not yet "independent nucleation pathways" with distinct mechanisms.
   - **Missing baseline.** The cross-family RPM of 0.03-0.05 is described as "negligible", but no random baseline is given. Compute the RPM between a map and laterally shifted or rotated versions of itself, or randomised maps at the same density and overlap tolerance.
   - **Possible anti-correlation.** Report whether the cross-family value is below the random level. That would indicate that inverse cores avoid conventional-core sites, which would be informative about the pinning.
   - **Registration.** Explain how drift between maps taken at +120 mT and +40 mT was registered.
   - **Overreach.** Labelling the two channels "external-field-driven" and "RKKY-driven" goes beyond what RPM measures. Either support it, for example with nucleation statistics versus field-cycling amplitude or a link to specific defect sites, or soften it in the abstract.

6. The effective-field picture makes a clear, untested prediction: the two families should change size in opposite directions with field. Supplementary Note S3 reports this only in simulation.
   - **What to measure.** Measure skyrmion diameter and density versus field for both families (MFM and DPC), and compare quantitatively with the simulated sizes. Fig. 4 provides simulated sizes that are never compared with the 81/58 nm and 64/48 nm measurements.
   - **Size discrepancy.** MFM and DPC diameters differ by ~30% for both families. Broadening by the MFM transfer function is one cause. Others are the different substrates (Si/SiO2 vs 25 nm Si3N4), fields (+120 vs −130 mT) and the tilt. Deconvolve the MFM data with the tip transfer function, or compare the two techniques at matched conditions.

7. The correlative comparison spans different samples, substrates, field signs and geometries. These differences are not controlled.
   - **Sample differences.** MFM, SQUID and XRR used Si/SiO2; LTEM used 25 nm Si3N4; XMCD and SAXS used 200 nm Si3N4. The MFM fields are positive; the DPC fields are negative and differ in magnitude (−131, −89, −49 vs +120, +80, +45 mT).
   - **In-plane field in LTEM.** The fixed 17° tilt puts an in-plane field of ~30-40 mT on the sample at −131 mT, which can distort Néel textures and shift phase boundaries.
   - **Missing context.** Temperatures for the MFM, XMCD (cryo-cooled magnet) and LTEM measurements are not stated. How many samples and regions show each state is also not stated.
   - **What the authors should do.** Give a magnetometry or XMCD loop for the membrane samples (to show that the field windows transfer). Report the measurement temperature for each technique. State whether both families were observed in more than one sample, region, or after re-cycling.

8. The generality claim ("general materials-design principle", "strategy is general and transferable") rests on a single stack, with no control in which the effect should be absent.
   - Ideally, measure a reference SAF with identical sublattices (e.g. CoB/CoB or CoFeB/CoFeB at compensation). There, mirrored tube textures in both sublattices, or a single family, would be expected. A sample with a different spacer (different J) would also serve.
   - At minimum, the simulated phase diagram requested in concern 3 should show the boundaries in (J, K_eff ratio, D ratio) space.
   - The three design criteria in the Discussion should be stated as hypotheses until such a test is shown.

9. The DMI argument is not supported by measurement.
   - **What the paper argues.** The Discussion says CoFeB has "potentially reduced intrinsic DMI" offset by its smaller thickness, "yielding comparable D_eff". It calls the "enhanced ratio D_eff/K_eff in CoFeB" the decisive asymmetry.
   - **What is measured.** No DMI value is measured. In the simulations D is a free parameter, set to 1.0 and 1.1 mJ/m², so the modelled asymmetry is almost entirely in K_eff.
   - **Notation.** "D_eff = D/t" mixes interfacial (pJ/m) and volume (mJ/m²) DMI conventions.
   - **What the authors should do.** Either measure D for the reference CoB and CoFeB multilayers (BLS, or domain-wall-energy analysis from MFM as in Ref. 48), or present the DMI argument as conjecture. Also clarify which quantity the simulations use.

10. The depth profile ("three-dimensional spin textures", textures "in every CoFeB layer", top-layer deviation) is inferred from transmission-integrated probes and simulations; layer-resolved evidence is absent.
   - The DPC ratio 12.8/13.6 ≈ 0.94 is consistent with nearly all CoFeB layers reversing within the cores. Make this argument explicitly, with its uncertainty.
   - Avoid implying direct observation of the vertical profile.

C. Minor

11. **Fig. 2 caption.** The caption says the DPC line profile in panel n is "taken from the marked position in g". Panel g is a SAXS pattern; the text says the profile comes from Fig. 2l (blue box). Also explain the dashed and dotted lines in panel k.

12. **Fig. 1c axis.** The label reads "m (×10^8 Am²)" and should read ×10^-8. Saturation at ~±17.7 × 10^-8 Am² is consistent with 2 × 8.85 × 10^-8 Am², which is worth noting.

13. **Discussion refers to Fig. 6a-d,** but Fig. 6 has no panel letters.

14. **Table 2.** "A (pJm-1)" should be pJ/m. Give the spacer thicknesses and the cell size along z.

15. **SAXS details.**
   - State the photon energy (edge) and helicity for Fig. 2f-j.
   - Give the ring q values and the corresponding real-space periods, so they can be compared with the MFM spacing.
   - Explain why the 80 mT maze pattern shows a strongly anisotropic arc.

16. **XMCD measurement.** Loops were measured with a single fixed helicity. Say whether helicity-reversed loops or beam-intensity normalisation were used to remove drift; the branch splitting at high field in Figs. 3a and 3d suggests some drift.

17. **DPC zero reference.** It is "set using assumptions informed primarily by the XMCD I(H) loops". Describe these assumptions concretely, since they link the DPC and XMCD conclusions.

18. **Typos and placeholders:**
   - "negligble" (Introduction)
   - "gemeotry" (Methods)
   - "whose" and "Several sister samples" sentence structure
   - "Data Availability ... [doi will be inserted later]"
   - "J_RKKY (= −0.034 ...)" is quoted as −0.03 in Table 2. State whether the simulations used −0.034.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add element-resolved imaging or scattering, comparing Co- and Fe-edge SAXS intensity or STXM images at the key fields. This would make the single-sublattice claim direct rather than rely on the a-dependent loop decomposition.
2. Rerun the simulations with the actual layer order, thicknesses and Ms values. Add simulated sublattice-resolved m(H) curves and a phase map in (J_RKKY, K_eff ratio), and show the currently "not shown" results.
3. Quantify the effective-field mechanism with measured size-versus-field and density-versus-field curves for both families, and an independently determined J. This would turn the qualitative Fig. 6 into a tested model.
4. Sharpen the novelty statement relative to FM-phase nucleation and domain coexistence in AFM-coupled perpendicular multilayers (Refs. 30-33), and show chirality for both families. This would establish that the inverse-polarity objects are skyrmions of opposite topological charge.
