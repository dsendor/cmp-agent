Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript studies a compensated synthetic antiferromagnet (SAF), Ta/[Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]x10, in which the two sublattices are chemically different. It reports that in a single field sweep the CoFeB sublattice alone hosts two families of Néel skyrmions with opposite core polarity: conventional-polarity skyrmions near FM saturation and inverse-polarity skyrmions near the SAF state. The evidence combines MFM, resonant SAXS, Lorentz DPC-STEM, element-resolved XMCD loops, return-point-memory (RPM) MFM and micromagnetics. The correlative, multi-probe characterisation of one stack is a real strength. The conceptual novelty is less clear than the framing ("beyond conventional skyrmions") suggests. Bubbles of the FM phase in an AF matrix and AF bubbles in an FM matrix, produced by reversal of one sublattice during the AF-FM transition of perpendicular layered antiferromagnets, are established physics (refs. 30-33, including the authors' own ref. 33). In the CoFeB layer's own frame, the "inverse-polarity" skyrmion is an ordinary skyrmion in a background aligned with its local effective field.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A carefully grown, well-characterised compensated SAF (XRR thicknesses with uncertainties, cross-section STEM, SQUID-VSM), with the moment balance between the two sublattices designed in.
- An unusually broad correlative data set on one material system: MFM (H), SAXS (M) and DPC (B), plus element-specific XMCD. The DPC integrated-induction value (12.8 ± 0.7 Tnm after tilt correction) agrees with the 13.6 Tnm expected for full reversal of one sublattice. This is a quantitative and convincing check for the maze state.
- The RPM methodology applied separately to two texture families is a useful approach to nucleation and pinning in multilayers.
- The idea of choosing which sublattice hosts textures through asymmetric anisotropy is relevant to the design of 3D spin textures and is clearly explained in Fig. 6.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Novelty relative to known AF-FM phase coexistence in layered antiferromagnets is not established, and the "beyond conventional" framing is not justified (Introduction, Discussion, Fig. 6, title).
   - Hellwig et al. (refs. 30-31) and Hauet et al. (ref. 32) showed that, in perpendicular [Co/Pt]/Ru layered antiferromagnets, the field-driven AF-to-FM transition proceeds through domains in which a subset of layers reverses. This produces FM-phase domains or bubbles in an AF matrix and the reverse. The authors' own ref. 33 (Barker et al., PRB 2024) is titled "Phase coexistence and transitions between antiferromagnetic and ferromagnetic states in a synthetic antiferromagnet".
   - The sequence in Fig. 6 (FM, AF bubbles in FM, maze, FM bubbles in AF, AF) is the same phase-coexistence sequence. Here the reversing sublattice is preselected by its lower K_eff, and DMI fixes the wall chirality.
   - In the CoFeB layer's own frame, the inverse-polarity texture is a conventional skyrmion whose core is antiparallel to a background that is saturated along the local effective field (H_ext minus the RKKY field). The Discussion (pp. 12-13) effectively says this.
   - What to do:
     - State explicitly what is observed here that was not observed or not possible in refs. 30-33. Candidates: homochiral Néel walls in both families, nanoscale (<100 nm) size, or the opposite topological charge at fixed chirality.
     - Show directly that the textures are skyrmions rather than achiral bubble domains. For both families separately, give the Fresnel tilt-series evidence for Néel character with the same chirality (currently only cited as Suppl. Note S4) and a size versus field dependence.
     - Without these, the title and abstract overstate the conceptual advance.

2. The claim that textures reside "only in the CoFeB layers" and that CoB stays "laterally uniform" rests on a decomposition with a fitted parameter (Eqs. 1-2, Fig. 3, Methods p. 17). The abstract and p. 4 call the attribution "unequivocal".
   - The isolated CoB loop in Fig. 3d is I_Co − a·I_Fe, with a = 0.35 chosen empirically so that the reconstructed total matches the SQUID loop "with a closed central area".
   - Eq. 1 also implicitly fixes the relative weight between the Fe-edge and Co-edge signals to unity. That is a second, unstated free parameter.
   - Because the non-hysteretic linear slope above 30 mT sits in both I_Co and I_Fe, the flatness of the reconstructed CoB loop outside the plateau, which is the key evidence, depends directly on a. Some of the result is therefore built into the fit.
   - The other probes do not settle the question for the skyrmion states. DPC is quantified only for the maze state (Fig. 2n). MFM cannot separate the sublattices. SAXS is not reported as element-resolved.
   - What to do:
     - Estimate a independently. From the XRR thicknesses and nominal compositions, the ratio of Co atoms in CoFeB to Co atoms in CoB is about (0.40 × 6.69)/(0.68 × 8.69) ≈ 0.45. Compare this with the fitted 0.35, and discuss per-atom Co XMCD differences between the two alloys.
     - Show how the slope of the reconstructed CoB loop above 30 mT changes for a in the range of about 0.3 to 0.45, and give an upper bound on the CoB reversed fraction at 45 and 120 mT.
     - Report resonant SAXS at both the Co and Fe L3 edges in the two skyrmion states, and state which edge was used for Fig. 2f-j. If CoB is uniform, the magnetic scattering at the Co edge should be reduced by a predictable factor relative to the Fe edge.
     - Provide DPC line-profile quantification for the conventional- and inverse-polarity states (Fig. 2k, m), not only the maze.

3. The RPM analysis does not yet support "independent nucleation pathways" or the assignment of "external-field-driven" versus "RKKY-driven" mechanisms (Fig. 5, pp. 10-12, abstract).
   - Cross-family RPM values of 0.03-0.05 are described as "uncorrelated", but no random-placement baseline is given. For skyrmion densities like those in Fig. 2b, d and any reasonable matching tolerance, chance overlap would probably be well above 0.03-0.05. If so, the cross-family result is an anticorrelation rather than independence.
   - Anticorrelation is what a shared pinning landscape would predict. A site that favours a down-magnetised CoFeB core at 120 mT is also the last place to keep down magnetisation at low field. There it becomes part of the inverse-state background, not an inverse-polarity core.
   - The data may therefore point to the same defects acting with opposite roles, rather than distinct mechanisms.
   - What to do:
     - Give the matching criterion, the number of skyrmions per map, the map area and the expected RPM for randomly placed skyrmions at the measured densities, for example by shuffling positions.
     - Compute a spatial cross-correlation between inverse-polarity core positions and conventional-polarity core positions, and also with the interstitial positions of the conventional lattice.
     - Remove or justify the mechanism labels, which are not tested by any measurement shown.
   - The MFM tip is a 6.5 nm Co coating at about 20 nm distance over a layer with K_eff = 0.11 MJ/m³. Show that repeated scans at fixed field do not move or nucleate textures, since tip-assisted nucleation at the same sites would inflate the same-family RPM.

B. Should address

4. The effective-field picture is qualitative and leaves out magnetostatics. The J_RKKY extraction it relies on is also questionable (Discussion pp. 12-13, Methods p. 16).
   - J_RKKY = μ0·H_s·M_s·t_FM/2 is the bilayer expression. Interior layers in a [A/B]x10 stack couple to two neighbours, which changes the prefactor.
   - In a PMA multilayer that forms domains, the out-of-plane saturation field is set largely by dipolar energy, not by uniform spin-flip against RKKY. The linear m(H) between 30 and 125 mT reflects domain growth.
   - Taking J = 0.034 mJ/m² and M_s·t = 5.4 × 10⁻⁴ A for CoFeB, the RKKY field from two CoB neighbours on an interior CoFeB layer is 2J/(M_s·t) ≈ 125 mT. The simple picture then puts the sign change of the effective field near 125 mT, not at the maze state near 80 mT.
   - The average FM-state magnetisation of the stack is about 0.27 MA/m (μ0M ≈ 0.34 T). Demagnetising fields are therefore at least comparable to the RKKY field.
   - What to do:
     - Use the simulations to give layer-resolved averages of the RKKY, demagnetising and external fields on the CoFeB layers at each state in Fig. 4. Show where the net field changes sign.
     - Obtain J independently, for example from the plateau-edge field or from an in-plane loop, or justify the formula used.

5. The micromagnetic model is described as "parameter-matched", but it differs from the experiment in ways relevant to the claims (Fig. 4, Methods pp. 19-20, Table 2).
   - The stacking order is inverted. Fig. 1a has CoFeB as the bottom magnetic layer and CoB on top, whereas Fig. 4e and p. 10 have CoFeB on top. This matters because the authors discuss a top-layer effect, and MFM probes the top CoB layer.
   - CoB is modelled at 6 Å rather than 8.69 Å, with M_s raised from 0.63 to 0.81 MA/m. This changes the magnetostatic energy of CoB.
   - The spacer thicknesses, which set interlayer dipolar coupling, are not given. Lateral boundary conditions for the 400 × 400 nm box are not stated.
   - D is a free parameter (1.0 and 1.10 mJ/m²) with no stated fitting criterion.
   - States are obtained by zero-temperature "field-cooling" from random states. The experiment is a hysteretic field sweep, as the RPM history dependence shows.
   - The statement that all initial conditions relax to the same state is surprising for a system with maze/skyrmion metastability.
   - Several supporting claims are "not shown": the symmetric-stack test, the J-dependence that "restores texture formation in both layers", and the Hall-angle simulations.
   - What to do:
     - Simulate a field sweep in the experimental stacking order and compare the simulated total and layer-resolved m(H) directly with Figs. 1c and 3.
     - Compare simulated and measured skyrmion diameters and field windows.
     - Show a sensitivity map (for example D_CoB, K_eff,CoB and J) marking where textures stay confined to one sublattice.
     - Include the "not shown" results in the SI.

6. The "general materials-design principle" (pp. 13-14) rests on one composition and has no control sample.
   - No SAF with identical sublattices (for example CoB/CoB), and no stack with the anisotropy asymmetry reversed, is shown. Such controls would show mirrored textures in both sublattices or textures moving to CoB.
   - K_eff for each sublattice comes from separate single-material reference multilayers. These do not have the Ru/Pt-coupled partner layer, so the in-SAF anisotropy may differ.
   - The sublattice M_s values in Table 1 are derived by assuming equal moments (exact compensation), not measured independently.
   - What to do: add at least one control stack (symmetric, or with the asymmetry inverted) and measure the per-sublattice M_s in the reference films. Otherwise, narrow the generality claims.

7. Claims about topology and dynamics are made without supporting data (p. 10, abstract).
   - The paper states that the two families have opposite topological charge and "deflect in opposite transverse directions under current", supported by simulations that are "not shown". It also calls the textures "topologically protected".
   - What to do: show the simulated trajectories and Hall angles together with the computed topological charge per layer and for the stack, or remove these statements.

8. The cross-technique comparison mixes substrates, field geometries and possibly temperatures (Fig. 2, Methods).
   - MFM and SQUID used Si/SiO2, XMCD and SAXS used 200 nm Si3N4, and LTEM used 25 nm Si3N4.
   - The LTEM fields come from a 17°-tilted objective-lens field. At −131 mT out-of-plane, this implies an in-plane component of about 40 mT, which can cant a SAF and distort the textures. The DPC tilt correction assumes purely out-of-plane domains.
   - The XMCD endstation uses a cryo-cooled electromagnet. The sample temperature for XMCD, SAXS and MFM is not stated, and J and K are temperature-dependent.
   - The size differences (81 vs 58 nm and 64 vs 48 nm) are attributed to probe broadening, but sample and geometry differences may contribute.
   - What to do: state the temperatures, show hysteresis loops (or XMCD/Fresnel equivalents) for the membrane samples, and discuss the effect of the in-plane field on the DPC states and their quantification.

C. Minor

9. The Fig. 2n caption says the profile is "taken from the marked position in g". Panel g is a SAXS pattern; the text (p. 7) indicates panel l.
10. p. 13 states that at zero field the RKKY interaction "saturates the CoFeB layers in the up state". Fig. 6 and the rest of the argument require CoFeB to be down (antiparallel to CoB) in the SAF state.
11. The Fig. 6 caption gives a 0.7 nm Ru spacer, whereas the stack is 0.6 nm. The coupling acts across a Ru(6 Å)/Pt(9 Å) composite spacer, not Ru alone. Describing J = −0.034 mJ/m² as "strong" coupling, and Ru 6 Å as chosen "to maximize" it, needs qualifying given the Pt insertion.
12. The D_eff = D/t argument (p. 13) is confusing. D in Eq. 5 and Table 2 (mJ/m²) is already a volume-normalised constant, and the interfacial quantity is D_s = D·t. No DMI measurement supports "comparable D_eff in both layers". Clarify, or cite or measure (for example by BLS).
13. In Fig. 3d, the CoB switching appears near ±10-20 mT rather than "at the edges of the SAF plateau at ±30 mT" (p. 8), and the two branches do not overlap at +160 mT. Describing the loop as "fully square" should be qualified.
14. SAXS (Fig. 2f-j) gives a characteristic periodicity, not a diameter. Quote the q values and corresponding periods. Comment on the two-lobed, anisotropic pattern for the maze state (Fig. 2h), which suggests stripe alignment, for example from an in-plane field or from history.
15. In Fig. 5, explain how the standard-deviation error bars (±0.005 to ±0.03) are obtained for each overlay. The RPM probes pinning reproducibility, not "thermodynamic stability" (p. 10).
16. Table 2 rounds J to −0.03 mJ/m² against −0.034 measured, and the CoFeB cell thickness is 6 Å against the measured 6.69 Å. State whether these differences matter. Simulated fields are negative while MFM fields are positive; note the symmetry explicitly.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add element-resolved spatial evidence: Co-edge versus Fe-edge SAXS, or STXM/XMCD-PEEM if available, in both skyrmion states. This would make the single-sublattice claim independent of the fitted scaling factor a.
2. Rework the RPM analysis with a random-placement baseline and a cross-family spatial correlation, and reframe the nucleation interpretation accordingly.
3. Run field-sweep micromagnetics in the experimental stacking order. Report the layer-resolved field balance and compare simulated loops, sizes and field windows quantitatively with Figs. 1c, 2 and 3.
4. Place the work explicitly relative to layered-AF phase coexistence (refs. 30-33) and add a control stack, so that the "design principle" is demonstrated rather than asserted.
