Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors build a compensated synthetic antiferromagnet (SAF), [Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]x10, in which the two sublattices are chemically different. In a single field sweep they report two families of Néel skyrmions with opposite core polarity: conventional-polarity skyrmions near 120 mT and inverse-polarity skyrmions near 45 mT. They assign all the textures to the CoFeB sublattice, with CoB staying uniform, and explain the field sequence by an RKKY bias field from CoB that competes with the applied field. Confining textures to a chosen sublattice of an SAF, with polarity selected by field, is an attractive idea for three-dimensional spin-texture engineering. The correlative MFM, SAXS, Lorentz DPC and XMCD dataset is broad. However, the central sublattice assignment currently rests on laterally averaged and empirically scaled XMCD loops. The physics is also close to known ferromagnetic/antiferromagnetic phase coexistence in perpendicular SAFs, and the paper does not yet draw that distinction sharply.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- One sample is studied with several complementary probes: MFM sensitive to H, resonant SAXS to M and DPC to B. The real-space images in Fig. 2a-e clearly show a contrast inversion between the high-field and low-field textures within one branch.
- The DPC quantification is a good internal consistency check. The tilt-corrected step in integrated induction, 12.8 ± 0.7 Tnm, agrees with the 13.6 ± 0.2 Tnm expected from SQUID-VSM and XRR for a change between local SAF and local FM alignment. This excludes "SAF-tube" textures, which would give near-zero contrast, and it excludes reversal of the whole stack.
- The element-resolved loops (Fig. 3) are a sensible route to sublattice resolution. The Fe-edge loop isolates CoFeB without any modelling.
- The return-point memory dataset (Fig. 5) is systematic: four paths, ten repetitions each, and cross-path comparisons.
- The micromagnetic model reproduces the qualitative sequence: FM, then conventional skyrmions, maze, inverse skyrmions and finally SAF.
- The idea of a design rule (moment compensation, strong AFM RKKY coupling and asymmetric K_eff between sublattices) is attractive and testable.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The assignment of all textures to CoFeB, with CoB laterally uniform, is not directly demonstrated.
   - What the claim rests on. The abstract and Introduction claim to "unequivocally attribute the observed textures to the CoFeB sublattice only". The evidence is:
     - (i) the DPC magnitude (Fig. 2n, p. 7);
     - (ii) the reconstructed CoB loop in Fig. 3d.
   - Why (i) does not decide it. In a compensated SAF with equal sublattice moments (Table 1), flipping CoFeB locally from SAF to FM gives exactly the same induction step as flipping CoB locally. So the 12.8 Tnm value shows that one sublattice varies, but not which one. The text on p. 7 concedes only that the two sublattices "exhibit two different magnetisation states". The sign is set "using assumptions informed primarily by the XMCD I(H) loops" (Methods, p. 18), so the DPC is not independent of (ii).
   - Problems with (ii).
     - Fig. 3d is obtained as I_Co - a·I_Fe, with a = 0.35 "determined empirically by adjusting a until the reconstructed total loop matched the SQUID-VSM loop" (p. 17). Whether the CoB loop comes out square, with a flat slope above 30 mT, depends directly on the chosen a. The squareness is therefore partly a construction, not an independent observation.
     - The loops are spatial averages. They cannot exclude CoB lateral textures whose net m_z nearly cancels, or small partial textures under CoFeB walls. The authors' own simulations show such perturbations (p. 10).
     - Fig. 3a and 3d show visibly separated ascending and descending branches at |μ0H| > 30 mT. In Fig. 3d they are offset by roughly 30 out of 400 a.u. This residual hysteresis in the supposedly saturated CoB is not discussed. It may be real CoB behaviour in the textured field range, or it may be beam or detector drift. Loops at a single helicity, without I0 or opposite-helicity normalisation, are vulnerable to drift.
   - What to do.
     - Give a physically motivated estimate of a: Co atomic density in each layer and the relative L3 XMCD cross-sections, or a measurement on the CoFeB-only reference multilayer at both edges. Show how Fig. 3d changes for a within, say, ±30% of 0.35.
     - Add helicity-reversed or I0-normalised loops.
     - Most importantly, provide element-resolved spatial evidence. The COSMIC beamline was already used: compare resonant SAXS taken at the Fe L3 and Co L3 edges at the same field. The magnetic scattering intensity at the Co edge, after accounting for the Co content of CoFeB, should then contain only the CoFeB contribution. Alternatively, use element-resolved STXM or ptychography at both edges. The paper also does not say at which edge the SAXS data in Fig. 2f-j were taken; this must be stated.

2. The effective-field mechanism, and the J_RKKY it rests on, are not quantitatively consistent with the field windows observed.
   - How J_RKKY was obtained. The value J_RKKY = -0.034 mJ m^-2 comes from setting the out-of-plane saturation field μ0H_s = 125 mT equal to an RKKY spin-flip field (p. 16).
   - Why that is questionable. Between about 30 and 125 mT the moment does not rise by coherent spin-flip. It rises through maze domains and skyrmions in CoFeB, which is the paper's own result. So H_s also contains the dipolar (stripe-domain) saturation field and the DMI-dependent annihilation field. The resulting J is then fed into the simulations (Table 2), so the model inherits this ambiguity.
   - Inconsistency with the qualitative picture. With 2|J|/(μ0 M_s t) ≈ 125 mT acting on an interior CoFeB layer, the net bias on CoFeB would vanish near 125 mT, where the FM state is observed. Yet the maze state, which the Discussion (p. 13) associates with near-cancellation of the two fields, is centred near 80 mT. The two skyrmion families sit roughly symmetrically about 80 mT (120 and 45 mT).
   - What to do.
     - Plot H_eff = H - H_RKKY (including the stack demagnetising field) for each observed state. Show that the conventional and inverse windows are mirror images about H_eff = 0, or explain why not.
     - Obtain J_RKKY independently, for example from the CoFeB minor-loop shift in the Fe-edge XMCD, from a non-PMA or in-plane-saturated reference, or from a stack with varied Ru thickness.
     - Show how sensitive the simulated field windows are to J_RKKY and D.
   - Unshown simulations. The Discussion states that "reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers, whereas stronger coupling localizes the non-collinear state" (pp. 13-14). This is the key mechanistic test, but no data are shown. It should be presented as a phase diagram, J_RKKY versus field, with the layer each texture occupies marked.

B. Should address

3. The return-point memory analysis does not establish "distinct nucleation mechanisms: external-field-driven … and RKKY-driven" (pp. 11-12).
   - Missing baseline. Cross-family RPM values of 0.03-0.05 need a chance-level reference: the expected overlap of two random (or randomly shifted) skyrmion maps with the measured densities and the same overlap tolerance. Without it, "negligible" cannot be read as "uncorrelated". If the values lie below chance, the sites are anti-correlated, which is a different statement.
   - Geometric explanation. Paths 2 and 4 both start from the maze state. If the maze is itself reproducible, conventional skyrmions will form from the segments of one contrast and inverse skyrmions from segments of the other. Low cross-overlap then follows from geometry, not from different mechanisms.
   - Pinning explanation. Site-selective pinning whose sign depends on core polarity, for example local anisotropy or thickness variations that favour up or down cores differently, would also give family-specific sites with a single nucleation mechanism.
   - Wording. RPM does not probe "thermodynamic stability" (p. 10).
   - What to do.
     - Add the random baseline.
     - Report the maze-state RPM.
     - Show conventional-skyrmion and inverse-skyrmion position maps overlaid on the same region, to test anti-correlation.
     - State the detection threshold and overlap tolerance, and what the standard-deviation error bars are taken over.
     - Moderate the mechanistic wording.

4. The paper does not establish its novelty relative to known FM/AFM phase coexistence in perpendicular SAFs.
   - The inverse-polarity textures are FM-aligned tubes in an AFM background, and the conventional ones are AFM-aligned tubes in an FM background. Both are closely related to the FM-phase domains in AFM-coupled [Co/Pt]/Ru multilayers (refs 30-32) and to the authors' own earlier phase-coexistence study (ref. 33).
   - One could view CoFeB as a ferromagnetic multilayer with an exchange bias field from a rigid CoB sublattice. In that view the "two families" are ordinary bubble skyrmions on either side of zero effective field, with polarity defined relative to the applied field rather than to the bias.
   - The genuinely new element appears to be sublattice selectivity, driven by the K_eff asymmetry. The title ("Beyond conventional skyrmions") and the abstract should be framed around that.
   - The paper should state explicitly what ref. 33 observed in a related SAF, and what is new here.

5. The "general materials-design principle" (pp. 13-14) is claimed from a single stack composition.
   - Only one multilayer design is reported. The sister samples share one deposition, on three different substrates.
   - What to do. Add at least one control stack where the principle predicts different behaviour:
     - a symmetric SAF (CoFeB/CoFeB or CoB/CoB), where mirrored SAF textures or sublattice-degenerate FM-phase domains are expected;
     - or a stack with altered K_eff asymmetry or Ru thickness, where textures should appear in both sublattices.
   - Otherwise, restrict the claim to this system and present the rule as a hypothesis supported by simulations. Those simulations must then be shown (see concern 2).

6. The micromagnetic model departs from the real structure in ways that bear on the conclusions.
   - CoB layer. CoB is modelled with CoFeB's thickness and M_s: 6 Å and 0.81 MA m^-1, against the measured 8.69 Å and 0.63 MA m^-1. Its K_u is rescaled to 0.63 MJ m^-3 (Table 2), against the 0.47 MJ m^-3 measured (Table 1). The cell thickness of 6 Å is described as "same as the experimental value for CoFeB", but XRR gives 6.69 Å. These changes alter interlayer dipolar fields and how hard CoB is relative to CoFeB, which is exactly the quantity the selectivity argument depends on.
   - Stack order. The simulated stack (Fig. 4e) has CoB at the bottom and CoFeB on top. The real sample, Ta/[Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]x10/Ru/Pt, has CoFeB lowest and CoB as the topmost magnetic layer. The discussion of the "very top CoFeB layer (CoB below, vacuum above)" (p. 10) therefore does not describe the real sample. Because MFM is surface-weighted and the real top layer is CoB, this also matters for interpreting Fig. 2a-e.
   - DMI. D is a "free parameter", with 1.00 and 1.10 mJ m^-2 for the two sublattices, but the quantity it was fitted to is not stated. The Discussion's argument that D_eff/K_eff is the decisive asymmetry (p. 13) is then built on these fitted values, not on measured ones.
   - History. States are obtained by field-cooling from random configurations at T = 0 at each field, and alternative initial conditions reportedly converge to the same state. The experiment is strongly history-dependent: hysteretic switching at ±30 mT, and RPM paths that differ by history. A quasi-static field sweep would be a more faithful comparison.
   - What to do.
     - Simulate the real stack order and real CoB parameters, or show that the results are insensitive to them.
     - State what D was fitted to (skyrmion diameter? field windows?) and give its uncertainty.
     - Give the boundary conditions, damping, convergence criteria and simulation box size. A 400 nm box holds only a few 50-80 nm skyrmions.
     - Show the symmetric-stack simulation currently marked "not shown".

7. The topological character and skyrmion Hall claims are under-supported in the main text.
   - The Néel character is deferred to Supplementary Note S4. The skyrmion Hall angle and the opposite deflection of the two families are stated on the basis of simulations "not shown" (p. 10).
   - The DMI was not measured. With D near 1 mJ m^-2 and appreciable interlayer dipolar fields across 10 repeats, hybrid or thickness-dependent chirality is possible (refs 19-21). If chirality is not uniform, the topological-charge argument for opposite Hall deflection fails.
   - What to do.
     - Bring the tilt-series Fresnel evidence for uniform Néel chirality into the main text or summarise it there.
     - Show the current-driven simulations, or remove the Hall-angle statements.
     - Ideally, give an independent DMI estimate for each sublattice, for example from Brillouin light scattering (BLS) on the reference multilayers or from domain-period analysis.

8. The cross-technique correlation mixes samples, field branches and field geometries without a demonstration that they match.
   - MFM uses an SiO2/Si substrate. LTEM uses 25 nm Si3N4 membranes and XMCD/SAXS use 200 nm Si3N4 membranes. Substrate roughness and strain can shift K_u and pinning.
   - The DPC images are on the negative branch at -131, -89 and -49 mT, at a fixed 17° tilt. That tilt adds an in-plane field of about 40 mT at -131 mT, which can distort Néel textures in a soft, compensated sublattice.
   - The sizes differ substantially (inverse 81 vs 58 nm; conventional 64 vs 48 nm), and the comparison is deferred to Note S3.
   - What to do.
     - Show the m(H) loop, or the Fe-edge XMCD loop, for each membrane sample used, against the SQUID loop.
     - Discuss the effect of the in-plane field component.
     - State how the diameter is defined in each technique, and compare sizes at matched |H| on the same branch.
     - Address the weak DPC contrast in Fig. 2k and 2m. Skyrmions are hard to discern in panel k, and the main text should include a processed or averaged image or the identified features.

C. Minor

9. SAXS (Fig. 2f-j).
   - State the photon energy and helicity for each pattern.
   - Explain the strong single-sided crescent at 80 mT (Fig. 2h), which implies aligned stripes, whereas the MFM maze in Fig. 2c is isotropic. It may reflect field history, the different substrate, or an artefact of the saturated-state subtraction and low-pass filtering.
   - Give the extracted periods in the main text.

10. MFM tip perturbation. A Co-coated tip at about 20 nm height over a compensated, low-K_eff sublattice may nucleate or displace textures. This is relevant to the RPM statistics and to the 45 mT state. Comment on tip stray-field magnitude, or compare scans taken with reversed tip magnetisation or at different heights.

11. Ru thickness. Fig. 6 caption and the Discussion (p. 12) refer to a "0.7 nm Ru spacer", but the stack has 0.6 nm Ru (XRR: 6.03 Å).

12. Fig. 2 caption. Panel n is said to be "taken from the marked position in g", but g is a SAXS pattern. The marked rectangle is in panel l.

13. Fig. 1c axis label reads "×10^8 Am²". It should be ×10^-8 Am², consistent with 8.85×10^-8 Am² per sublattice.

14. Table 1 and the extraction of M_s.
   - The sublattice M_s values are derived by assuming equal moments (compensation), not by measuring each sublattice. State this explicitly, and say how the 1% remanence bounds any imbalance.
   - K_u for CoFeB is 0.53 MJ m^-3 in Table 1 and 0.52 in Table 2. Using μ0M_s²/2 with 0.81 MA m^-1 gives 0.52.
   - The reference CoB and CoFeB multilayers used for K_eff are not described (interfaces, repeats). Their interfaces may differ from those in the SAF, where each layer sits on Pt and under Ru.

15. Measurement temperatures. The XMCD/SAXS endstation is cryo-cooled. State the sample temperature for XMCD, SAXS, LTEM and MFM, since K_eff and J_RKKY are temperature-dependent and the comparison assumes room temperature throughout.

16. The DPC zero reference is "set by using assumptions informed primarily by the XMCD I(H) loops". State these assumptions explicitly, field by field.

17. Wording. Replace "unequivocally" (p. 4) and "confirm" (abstract, RPM) with language that matches the evidence. Typos: "negligble" (p. 3), "gemeotry" (p. 17).

18. Fig. 4 needs layer-resolved m_z line profiles through a skyrmion for each state. In the present cross-section colour maps it is hard to verify FM versus SAF background in each panel, and to see the CoB perturbations mentioned in the text.

19. Data availability gives a placeholder DOI. The RPM maps and detection code should be included.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add element-resolved imaging, such as resonant SAXS or STXM/ptychography at both the Fe and Co L3 edges at identical fields. This would turn the sublattice assignment from inference into direct observation, and it is the single most valuable addition.
2. Quantify the effective-field picture. Use an independently determined J_RKKY to plot the observed states against H - H_RKKY, and present the simulated phase diagram (J_RKKY versus field, with the hosting sublattice marked) that currently appears only in prose.
3. Test the design rule experimentally with one control stack: a symmetric SAF, or a sample with altered K_eff asymmetry.
4. Recast the RPM section with a random-overlap baseline and an anti-correlation analysis. Position the results explicitly against refs 30-33, so that the novelty (sublattice-selective, polarity-switchable textures) is clear.
