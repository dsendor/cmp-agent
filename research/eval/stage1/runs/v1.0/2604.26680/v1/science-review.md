A. Must address

1. The assignment of all textures to the CoFeB sublattice, with CoB laterally uniform, is not directly demonstrated, although the abstract and Introduction (p. 4) say the textures are "unequivocally" attributed to CoFeB only. The claim rests on two pieces of evidence. The first is the DPC step (Fig. 2n, p. 7). In a compensated SAF with equal sublattice moments (Table 1), a CoB-hosted texture on a uniform CoFeB background gives the same MFM contrast and the same 13.6 T nm induction step as a CoFeB-hosted one. The 12.8 ± 0.7 T nm value therefore shows that one sublattice reverses, but not which one. The DPC zero/sign is also set "using assumptions informed primarily by the XMCD I(H) loops" (Methods, p. 18), so it is not independent of the second piece of evidence, and the DPC step is quantified only for the maze state, not for the two skyrmion states (Fig. 2k, m). The second is the reconstructed CoB loop (Fig. 3d, Eqs. 1-2), obtained as I_Co − a·I_Fe with a = 0.35 "determined empirically by adjusting a until the reconstructed total loop matched the SQUID-VSM loop" (Methods, p. 17), where the SQUID loop in Fig. 3c is itself rescaled (an extra scale and offset). Eq. 1 also implicitly sums Fe-edge and Co-edge transmitted intensities with unit weight, although they have different non-magnetic backgrounds (~4,700 vs ~7,000 counts) and different XMCD magnitudes per unit moment; this is a second, unstated free parameter. Because the non-hysteretic linear slope above 30 mT is present in both I_Co and I_Fe, the flatness and squareness of the CoB loop outside the plateau, which is the key evidence, depends directly on a, so the result is partly built into the fit. Fig. 3d is also not perfectly flat above 30 mT (the positive branch rises from ~2,420 to ~2,500). Finally, loops are spatial averages and cannot exclude CoB lateral textures whose net m_z nearly cancels, or partial CoB textures under CoFeB walls, which the authors' own simulations show (p. 10). Since MFM and DPC cannot tell which sublattice reverses, and confinement to CoFeB is the headline claim, the authors should: (a) estimate a independently, from composition and XRR thicknesses (Co atoms in 6.69 Å Co40Fe40B20 vs 8.69 Å Co68B32 give roughly (0.40 × 6.69)/(0.68 × 8.69) ≈ 0.45, compared with the fitted 0.35) together with per-atom Co L3 XMCD differences between the alloys, or from a Co-edge and Fe-edge measurement on the CoFeB-only reference multilayer; (b) show Fig. 3d for a range of a (e.g. 0.25-0.45, or ±30% of 0.35) with an uncertainty on a; (c) convert the residual high-field slope of Fig. 3d into an upper bound on the reversed CoB area fraction at 120 mT and 45 mT, and compare it with the core area fractions in Figs. 2b and 2d; (d) most directly, provide element-resolved spatial evidence, by comparing resonant SAXS at the Co and Fe L3 edges at the same fields in both skyrmion states (the Co-edge magnetic scattering should be reduced by a predictable factor if CoB is uniform), or element-resolved STXM/ptychography/holography at both edges; (e) state which photon energy (edge) was used for Fig. 2f-j; (f) give DPC line-profile quantification for the conventional- and inverse-polarity states, not only the maze; and (g) replace "unequivocally" with wording that matches the evidence until such data are shown.

2. The skyrmionic, topological character of both families, and the claim that the two families carry opposite topological charge, are not shown in the main text. The text says the Néel nature "was verified experimentally using Lorentz microscopy as shown in Supplementary Note S4", but does not say whether this holds for both families or with which handedness. The title ("Beyond conventional skyrmions") and the "inverse-polarity skyrmion" label depend on the low-field objects being homochiral Néel skyrmions with Q opposite to the high-field ones, rather than achiral bubble domains or up-magnetised CoFeB bubbles in an AFM background. The DMI is not measured, and with D near 1 mJ/m² and appreciable interlayer dipolar fields across 10 repeats, hybrid or thickness-dependent chirality is possible (refs 19-21); if chirality is not uniform, the topological-charge argument fails. The authors should show tilt-series Fresnel or DPC evidence for each family separately in the main text, with the sign of the wall chirality for each, demonstrate that the chirality is the same and the core polarity opposite so that Q is reversed, give the computed topological charge per layer and for the stack from the simulations, and add a size-versus-field dependence for each family (see concern 12) as further evidence of skyrmion rather than bubble behaviour.

3. Novelty relative to known AF-FM phase coexistence in perpendicular layered antiferromagnets is not established, and the "beyond conventional" framing of the title and abstract is not justified (Introduction, Discussion, Fig. 6). Hellwig et al. (refs 30-31) and Hauet et al. (ref. 32) showed that in perpendicular [Co/Pt]/Ru layered antiferromagnets the field-driven AF-to-FM transition proceeds through domains in which a subset of layers reverses, producing FM-phase bubbles in an AF matrix and the reverse. The authors' own ref. 33 (Barker et al., PRB 109, 134437 (2024)) reports "Phase coexistence and transitions between antiferromagnetic and ferromagnetic states in a synthetic antiferromagnet". The sequence in Fig. 6 (FM, AF bubbles in FM, maze, FM bubbles in AF, AF) is the same phase-coexistence sequence, with the reversing sublattice preselected by its lower K_eff and the wall chirality fixed by DMI. In the CoFeB layer's own frame, the inverse-polarity texture is a conventional skyrmion whose core is antiparallel to a background saturated along the local effective field (H_ext minus the RKKY field); equivalently, CoFeB can be viewed as a ferromagnetic multilayer exchange-biased by a rigid CoB sublattice, so the "two families" are ordinary bubble skyrmions on either side of zero effective field with polarity defined relative to the applied rather than the bias field. The Discussion (pp. 12-13) effectively says this. The authors should state explicitly what ref. 33 and refs 30-32 observed and what is new here (candidates: sublattice selectivity driven by the K_eff asymmetry, homochiral Néel walls in both families, sub-100 nm size, opposite topological charge at fixed chirality), and reframe the title and abstract around that advance.

B. Should address

4. The effective-field mechanism, and the J_RKKY it rests on, are qualitative and not quantitatively consistent with the observed field windows (Discussion pp. 12-13, Methods p. 16). J_RKKY = −0.034 mJ/m² is obtained by equating μ0H_s = 125 mT to an RKKY spin-flip field using the bilayer expression J = μ0H_s·M_s·t_FM/2; interior layers in an [A/B]x10 stack couple to two neighbours, which changes the prefactor. Between about 30 and 125 mT the moment rises through maze domains and skyrmions in CoFeB (the paper's own result), so H_s also contains dipolar (stripe-domain) saturation and DMI-dependent annihilation contributions; Fig. 1c also appears to saturate nearer 150 mT than 125 mT. The resulting J is fed into the simulations (Table 2), which inherit the ambiguity. With J = 0.034 mJ/m² and M_s·t = 5.4 × 10⁻⁴ A for CoFeB, the RKKY field from two CoB neighbours on an interior CoFeB layer is 2|J|/(M_s t) ≈ 125 mT, so the simple picture H_eff(CoFeB) = H − 2|J|/(M_s t) puts the zero of the effective field near 125 mT, where the FM state is observed. Experimentally the maze state, which the Discussion associates with near-cancellation, sits near 80 mT, with the two skyrmion families roughly symmetric about it (120 and 45 mT), and the midpoint of the linear part of Fig. 3b is ~90-95 mT. The average FM-state magnetisation of the stack is about 0.27 MA/m (μ0M ≈ 0.34 T), so demagnetising fields are at least comparable to the RKKY field and are omitted from the picture. The authors should plot H_eff = H − H_RKKY (including the stack demagnetising field) for each imaged state and show whether the conventional and inverse windows are mirror images about H_eff = 0; use the simulations to give layer-resolved averages of the RKKY, demagnetising and external fields on the CoFeB layers at each state in Fig. 4 and show where the net field changes sign; and obtain J independently (from the field at which the CoFeB m_z crosses zero in Fig. 3b, the CoFeB minor-loop shift in the Fe-edge XMCD, the plateau-edge field, an in-plane loop, FMR, or a stack with varied Ru thickness), or justify the formula used.

5. The micromagnetic model is described as "parameter-matched", but it departs from the experiment in ways that bear on the claims it supports (Fig. 4, Methods pp. 19-20, Table 2). The stacking order is inverted: Fig. 4e and p. 10 place CoFeB on top of each repeat and as the topmost magnetic layer ("very top CoFeB layer ... CoB below, vacuum above"), whereas the real stack Ta/[Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]x10/Ru/Pt (Fig. 1a, Methods) has CoFeB lowest and CoB topmost; the top-layer deviation result and the layer nearest the surface-weighted MFM tip therefore refer to a different structure. CoB is modelled at 6 Å with M_s = 0.81 MA/m and a rescaled K_u = 0.63 MJ/m³, against the measured 8.69 Å, 0.63 MA/m and 0.47 MJ/m³; this changes the RKKY field on CoB (∝ J/M_s t), the interlayer dipolar fields and how hard CoB is relative to CoFeB, which is exactly what the selectivity argument depends on. The CoFeB cell is described as "6 Å (same as the experimental value)" whereas XRR gives 6.69 Å. Spacer thicknesses (which set dipolar coupling), lateral boundary conditions, damping and convergence criteria are not given; a 400 nm × 400 nm box holds only a few 50-80 nm skyrmions, so box size and edges may set the texture density and phase boundaries. D is a free parameter (1.0 and 1.10 mJ/m²) with no stated fitting criterion. States are obtained by T = 0 "field-cooling" from random configurations, while the experiment is a hysteretic field sweep (switching at ±30 mT, history-dependent RPM paths), and the statement that all initial conditions relax to the same state is surprising for a system with maze/skyrmion metastability. Several supporting simulations are "not shown": the symmetric stack and the J_RKKY dependence quoted in the Discussion ("reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers, whereas stronger coupling localizes the non-collinear state", pp. 13-14), which is the key mechanistic test. The authors should simulate a quasi-static field sweep in the experimental stacking order with the real CoB parameters (or show insensitivity to them), compare simulated total and sublattice-resolved m_z(H) directly with Figs. 1c, 3b and 3d and simulated diameters and field windows with experiment, state what D was fitted to and its uncertainty, give spacer thicknesses, boundary conditions and box-size checks, and include the "not shown" results, in particular a phase diagram in (J_RKKY, K_eff ratio, D ratio) or J_RKKY versus field with the hosting sublattice marked.

6. The return-point memory analysis supports reproducible, family-specific sites but not "independent nucleation pathways" or the assignment of "external-field-driven" versus "RKKY-driven" mechanisms (Fig. 5, pp. 10-12, abstract). Cross-family RPM values of 0.03-0.05 are called "negligible" or "uncorrelated", but no chance-level baseline is given; for the densities in Figs. 2b, d and any reasonable matching tolerance, random overlap may well exceed 0.03-0.05, in which case the result is anticorrelation, not independence. Anticorrelation could arise from a shared pinning landscape acting with opposite roles (a site favouring a down CoFeB core at 120 mT is the last to keep down magnetisation at low field, becoming part of the inverse-state background), from polarity-dependent site pinning (local anisotropy or thickness variations), or from geometry, since paths 2 and 4 both start from the maze, so if the maze is reproducible conventional and inverse skyrmions form from segments of opposite contrast. The authors should compute the expected RPM for randomly placed, shuffled, shifted or rotated maps at the measured densities and tolerance; give the matching criterion, detection threshold, number of skyrmions per map and map area; explain how drift between maps at +120 mT and +40 mT was registered; report the maze-state RPM; overlay conventional and inverse core positions on the same region and compute their spatial cross-correlation, including with the interstitial sites of the conventional lattice; explain what the standard-deviation error bars (±0.005 to ±0.03) are taken over; replace "thermodynamic stability" (p. 10), which RPM does not probe; and remove or support the mechanism labels, for example with nucleation statistics versus field-cycling amplitude or a link to specific defect sites.

9. The DMI argument is not supported by measurement. The Discussion (p. 13) says CoFeB has "potentially reduced intrinsic DMI" offset by its smaller thickness, "yielding comparable D_eff", and calls the "enhanced ratio D_eff/K_eff in CoFeB" the decisive asymmetry. No DMI is measured, and in the simulations D is a free parameter set to 1.0 and 1.10 mJ/m², so the modelled asymmetry is almost entirely in K_eff. The notation "D_eff = D/t" also mixes conventions: D in Eq. 5 and Table 2 (mJ/m²) is already a volume-normalised constant, and the interfacial quantity is D_s = D·t (pJ/m). The authors should measure D for the reference CoB and CoFeB multilayers (BLS, or domain-wall-energy/domain-period analysis from MFM as in Ref. 48) or present the DMI argument as conjecture, and clarify which quantity the simulations use.
