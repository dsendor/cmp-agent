Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report a low-energy (0.3 to 0.6 eV) dispersive feature in boron K-edge RIXS on MgB2, taken at the sigma-hole pre-edge resonance (187 eV) with in-plane momentum transfer below 0.17 Å^-1. They identify it with the intra-band particle-hole (Lindhard) continuum, using BSE (OCEAN) simulations, a tight-binding Lindhard/RPA calculation and a sum-rule argument for why EELS cannot see it. If the identification holds, momentum-resolved access to the intra-band continuum of a metal would be a useful new capability for RIXS and of broad interest. As submitted, however, the kinematics of the observed feature, the way momentum components covary in the geometry, and the convergence of the simulations do not yet establish the intra-band assignment.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new, clearly resolved low-energy RIXS feature in a well-characterised metal, with systematic momentum and geometry dependence (Fig. 3a,b).
- Several independent calculations (ab initio BSE RIXS, tight-binding Lindhard function, orbital-weighted RPA) that reproduce the energy scale and the trend of the feature (Figs. 3c-e, 4).
- A simple, transparent argument (sum rules, Eqs. 1, 34-39 and Fig. S4) for why orbital-selective probes can see particle-hole weight that the total charge response suppresses as q^4. This is pedagogically valuable and testable.
- The proposed use of low-q RIXS continua as falsifiable tests of theories of correlated metals is well motivated.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The feature's energy is kinematically inconsistent with in-plane sigma intra-band excitations. For intra-band excitations at small q, the continuum is bounded by omega <= v_F q (plus a small curvature term). The paper itself takes v_F(sigma) ≈ 2 eVÅ (p. 2). At q_par = 0.016 Å^-1 (Fig. 2b), this bounds the intra-band sigma continuum at about 0.03 eV, yet the feature peaks at about 0.3 eV. At q_par = 0.05 Å^-1 (Fig. 3e) the bound is about 0.1 eV, but omega_exp ≈ 0.4 eV. Over the whole range in Fig. 3e the data lie well above v_F q, and the dashed linear fit extrapolates to a finite intercept of about 0.3 eV at q_par → 0, not to zero as an intra-band continuum must. The quoted "speed ≈ 1.4 eVÅ" also does not obviously match the dashed line, whose slope reads closer to 2 eVÅ between 0.05 and 0.16 Å^-1. As it stands, the central identification ("directly map out the Lindhard continuum") does not follow from the dispersion data. The authors should:
   - plot the kinematic boundaries omega = v_F^(i) q for each Fermi sheet (sigma and pi, with the full 3D q) on Fig. 3b-e;
   - state the fitted slope and intercept with uncertainties;
   - explain how a feature with a finite q → 0 intercept, including the clear 0.3 eV peak at q_par = 0.016 Å^-1, can be intra-band.

2. In-plane momentum, out-of-plane momentum, incidence angle and polarization all change together, so the "dispersion" is not isolated. The experiment varies theta_in at (apparently) fixed scattering angle. In Fig. 3a, q_perp falls from 0.14 to 0.02 Å^-1 as q_par rises from 0.05 to 0.16 Å^-1, and the orbital projection of the pi-polarized beam rotates from in-plane to out-of-plane at the same time. A shift of the peak with q_par could therefore come from changing q_perp (to which the 3D pi sheets are sensitive) or from a changing sigma/pi matrix-element weighting of several unresolved contributions, rather than from a true in-plane dispersion. The listed pairs also do not correspond to a constant |q| ((0.05,0.14) gives 0.149, (0.10,0.08) gives 0.128, (0.16,0.02) gives 0.161 Å^-1), so the geometry is unclear. The authors should:
   - give a table of theta_in, 2theta, q_par, q_perp and |q| for every spectrum;
   - state whether the Lindhard and RPA calculations in Figs. 3d and 4 use the full 3D q or only q_par;
   - ideally, show spectra at a second scattering angle, so that q_par can be varied at roughly fixed polarization projection, or q_perp at fixed q_par;
   - show the Gamma-K data, which the SI (Sec. A) says were measured. An in-plane-isotropic sigma response would be a useful internal check.

3. Plausible alternatives are not excluded, and the band decomposition that is meant to exclude them is ambiguous. Two mechanisms produce low-energy weight at finite energy as q → 0 and would naturally explain the finite intercept in Concern 1. The first is inter-band transitions between the light- and heavy-hole sigma bands, which are degenerate along Gamma-A and split away from it. The second is pi intra-band excitations driven by the large q_perp. Fig. 4c is meant to settle this, but:
   - The legend ("sigma-sigma, intra", "sigma-sigma, inter", "pi-pi, intra", "pi-pi, inter") does not match the caption ("Inter-band excitations between the sigma and pi bands are negligible") or the text. It is unclear whether transitions between the two sigma bands count as "intra" or "inter".
   - At q_par = 0.088 Å^-1, the "pi-pi, intra" curve extends to about 1.5 eV and "sigma-sigma, intra" to about 0.6 eV. These imply velocities of roughly 17 and 7 eVÅ, far above any Fermi velocity in MgB2 (including the paper's 2 eVÅ for sigma). Either q is much larger than q_par, or these channels contain inter-band (sub-band) transitions.

   The authors should define the decomposition precisely (band index a → b for each of the eight TB bands), show each Fermi-surface-crossing band pair separately, and check each channel against its kinematic bound. If sigma-light → sigma-heavy transitions carry the weight, the paper's title and main conclusion change.

4. The BSE simulations are probably not converged at the relevant q and energy scale, so their agreement with the data cannot support the intra-band assignment. SI Sec. C uses:
   - a 36 × 36 × 28 k-grid, whose in-plane spacing (about 2.35/36 ≈ 0.065 Å^-1) is larger than or comparable to the measured q_par (0.016 to 0.16 Å^-1);
   - Fermi-Dirac smearing of 0.02 Ry ≈ 0.27 eV, comparable to the feature energy itself;
   - only 12 bands, with the first 4 occupied bands removed.

   With these settings, intra-band transitions at q smaller than the grid spacing are not resolved, and the smearing alone can generate a low-energy peak at a few hundred meV. The agreement of omega_BSE with experiment in Fig. 3e, and the band projection in Fig. 2c (used to say the transition is "localized within the sigma bands ... close to the BZ center"), therefore need convergence tests. The authors should repeat the RIXS BSE calculation with a denser (or shifted or interpolated) k-mesh and much smaller smearing, show that the low-energy feature and its dispersion are stable, and state explicitly how a momentum transfer smaller than the k-spacing is represented.

B. Should address

5. A peak fit is used to describe a continuum, and the lineshape is not compared. The feature is described as "markedly asymmetric" (p. 2), yet its position is extracted from a symmetric Voigt (Fig. S2) alongside an elastic line whose tail overlaps it strongly at the lowest q (Fig. 3a). A Lindhard continuum has no unique "peak velocity": its maximum depends on band weighting and broadening, while its defining features are the onset at omega → 0 and the upper edge near v_F q. Specifically:
   - Fitted peak positions are biased by the elastic and quasi-elastic subtraction and by the assumed lineshape.
   - The origin of the error bars in Fig. 3e is not stated.
   - Electron-phonon coupling to the E2g mode in the sigma bands, and phonon or multiphonon contributions near the elastic line, are not considered in the fit.

   The authors should:
   - fit with an asymmetric or model-based lineshape (for example the broadened, weighted chi of Fig. 4a convolved with resolution);
   - report the extracted upper edge or leading edge as well as the maximum;
   - give the elastic-line treatment and how the error bars were obtained.

6. Incident-energy, polarization and off-resonance controls are missing. The assignment relies on the pre-edge resonance selecting sigma and pi states near E_F, but only one incident energy is shown.
   - An incident-energy map across the 187 eV pre-edge would show that the feature stays at constant energy loss (Raman-like) rather than tracking the incident energy (fluorescence-like or core-exciton decay). This is needed to interpret it as a valence excitation.
   - Spectra at the main edge (about 192 to 194 eV) and below the edge would provide a reference where the predicted orbital selectivity differs.
   - Data with sigma incident polarization at the same q would test the predicted orbital weighting directly. Under Eq. 32 the p_x and p_y weights would change while q is unchanged, which is the cleanest way to separate matrix-element effects from dispersion (see Concern 2).

7. The novelty claims are overstated relative to existing probes of the particle-hole continuum. The abstract and introduction say the Lindhard continuum "has remained experimentally elusive" and that "no feature resembling the textbook illustration ... has been reported". Three bodies of earlier work cut against this:
   - Non-resonant inelastic X-ray scattering on simple metals (for example Li, Na, Al) has long measured S(q, omega) beyond the plasmon cutoff, where the particle-hole continuum dominates.
   - Electronic Raman scattering probes intra-band particle-hole excitations in metals at small q.
   - Momentum-resolved EELS has studied low-energy continua, including Ref. 23.

   The sum-rule argument (Eq. 1) also applies only at small q, below the plasmon cutoff. The authors should cite and discuss this literature and recast the claim more precisely: observation of the small-q, below-cutoff intra-band continuum via an orbital-selective probe.

8. The RPA and orbital-weighting model is heuristic, and its role is unclear.
   - Eq. 32 weights orbital-resolved RPA susceptibilities with cos^4, sin^4 and cross terms of theta_in only. It does not account for the unanalyzed outgoing polarization or the scattering angle, and its off-diagonal combination (chi_{py,pz} + chi_{pz,py}) is not derived from the RIXS cross section.
   - The Coulomb term V(q) = U/q^2 is quoted with "U = 2 eVÅ", which is dimensionally inconsistent: for q in Å^-1, U would carry units of eVÅ^3 per volume normalization. The calibration against a GPAW plasmon near 3 eV is not shown.
   - The SI states "the low-energy spectrum is unaffected by the choice of the parameter U", yet the main text says "the combination of interactions and orbital sensitivity shifts the spectral weight" (Fig. 4a vs 4b).

   The authors should:
   - show the orbital-weighted chi^0 alone (no RPA) next to Fig. 4a, so the reader can see what interactions actually do;
   - derive or justify the weights from the K-edge dipole RIXS cross section, including the outgoing channel;
   - fix the units and show the U dependence.

9. Sample information and reproducibility are thin. Sample growth is delegated to Ref. 24 (HPCVD thin films), and the paper gives no:
   - film thickness, substrate or orientation;
   - Tc or residual resistivity;
   - surface preparation;
   - number of samples or spots measured.

   MgB2 surfaces oxidize readily. The experimental XAS (Fig. 2a) shows a strong peak near 194 eV that exceeds the BSE prediction and is close to the B2O3 boron pi* resonance, so a surface oxide or borate contribution is possible. An oxide would not produce a dispersing metallic continuum, but it could add to the background under the feature and alter the apparent q dependence through changing probing depth with theta_in. The authors should:
   - give sample characterization;
   - discuss the 194 eV excess;
   - state whether the low-energy feature was reproduced on a second sample or spot, or after re-cleaning.

C. Minor

10. Numbers in the text: check the quoted sigma Fermi velocity (about 2 eVÅ). DFT literature values for MgB2 sigma and pi sheets are generally higher (roughly 3 to 5 eVÅ), and the value matters for Concern 1. Also reconcile the quoted 1.4 eVÅ slope with the dashed line in Fig. 3e.

11. Fig. 2b is labelled q_par = 0.016 Å^-1, outside the 0.05 to 0.16 Å^-1 range of Fig. 3. State the corresponding q_perp and theta_in, or correct the label. Fig. 3e has its omega axis on the right with only two ticks, which makes the slope hard to read; add a left axis or more ticks.

12. Presentation and typographical issues:
   - "Florescence" (Fig. 2 caption); "the the" (p. 4); "motive" for "motivate" (p. 5); "fact that" for "fact that EELS" (p. 1).
   - Broken cross-references "S0 D", "S0 G" and "Figure S0 G". The functional comparison is the unlabelled XAS panel on p. S8, not Sec. G; give it a figure number.
   - An empty equation number (38) on p. S5.
   - Fig. 4c legend colours are hard to distinguish ("sigma-sigma, inter" vs "pi-pi, intra" are both red).

13. The tight-binding model (Fig. S3) deviates visibly from DFT along A-L-H and for one sigma band near Gamma-A close to E_F. Comment on the effect on the Lindhard function at the q range studied. The data availability statement ("upon reasonable request") is weak for a claim of this kind; deposit the spectra and fit results.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Put the kinematics at the centre of the paper. Overlay the per-sheet v_F q boundaries, computed with the full 3D q, on the measured intensity maps, and show directly where the measured weight lies relative to them. This would answer Concerns 1 to 3 in one figure.
2. Decouple q_par, q_perp and polarization projection, with a second scattering angle, Gamma-K data and sigma-polarized spectra. Then show that the feature follows the predicted in-plane dispersion at fixed orbital weighting.
3. Replace the peak-position comparison with a full lineshape comparison: data versus resolution-convolved, orbital-weighted chi (with and without RPA) at every measured (q_par, q_perp), on an absolute or common intensity scale.
4. Add an incident-energy RIXS map through the pre-edge, and an off-resonance or main-edge reference, to establish the Raman-like character of the feature and the resonance selectivity the argument relies on.
