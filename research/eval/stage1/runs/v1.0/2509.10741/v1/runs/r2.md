Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report ultra-soft (B K-edge, ~187 eV) RIXS on MgB2 and identify a low-energy (~0.3-0.7 eV), dispersive, asymmetric feature as the intra-band particle-hole (Lindhard) continuum. They support this with OCEAN BSE simulations, a tight-binding Lindhard/RPA calculation, and a sum-rule argument for why EELS cannot see this continuum at small q while an orbital-selective probe can. If the assignment holds, the paper would show that low-q, orbital-selective RIXS can access intra-band charge dynamics in metals. That would matter well beyond MgB2, for example as a benchmark for continua in correlated metals. As written, the kinematics, the momentum geometry and the resonance behaviour do not yet establish the assignment, and the claim to be the first direct observation is broader than the argument supports.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new, clearly resolved low-energy RIXS feature in a well-characterized metal, measured at very small in-plane momentum transfer (q_par < 0.17 A^-1) with ~100 meV resolution (Figs. 2b, 3a,b).
- An unusually complete theory comparison for a soft x-ray RIXS paper. It includes a full-geometry BSE simulation of XAS and RIXS (Figs. 2a,b, 3c), exciton projections onto the band structure (Fig. 2c) and a tight-binding Lindhard/RPA calculation (Figs. 3d, 4).
- The orbital-selectivity argument (Eqs. 34-39, Fig. S4) is simple and instructive: partial (orbital-projected) susceptibilities escape the q^4 suppression that screening imposes on the total charge response. This gives a physical reason why RIXS and EELS should differ.
- The outlook is useful: continuum lineshapes and q-omega scaling as falsifiable tests for theories of correlated metals.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The "dispersion" is measured at nearly constant |q|, with q_perp and polarization changing together with q_par. Fig. 3a labels the end points (q_par, q_perp) = (0.05, 0.14) and (0.16, 0.02) A^-1. The total |q| is therefore ~0.149 and ~0.161 A^-1, nearly constant, as expected for a fixed scattering angle at 190 eV, where the photon momentum is ~0.096 A^-1. Rocking theta_in mostly rotates q from near the c axis into the plane, and at the same time changes the polarization projection and self-absorption. The shift of the peak from ~0.4 to ~0.65 eV (Fig. 3e) is therefore an angular (anisotropy plus matrix-element) dependence at fixed |q|, not a dispersion in q_par at fixed q_perp. The "nearly linear dispersion" and the velocity "~1.4 eV A" extracted against q_par alone (p. 3) do not have the meaning given to them. A particle-hole continuum depends on the full 3D q through v_F·q. For MgB2 this is strongly anisotropic, because the sigma cylinders have very small v_z.

The authors should:
- state the full (q_par, q_perp) for every spectrum;
- confirm that the Lindhard and RPA calculations in Figs. 3d, 3e and 4 use the actual 3D q of each spectrum, not q_par with q_perp = 0;
- show the Gamma-K data mentioned in the SI (Sec. A) but not shown;
- ideally, add data at a second scattering angle, so that |q| actually changes at fixed direction.

A test that separates the momentum dependence from the polarization and geometry dependence is needed before the feature can be called "dispersive" in the sense of a continuum.

2. The feature's energy scale and its finite intercept do not obviously satisfy the kinematic bound of an intra-band continuum. An intra-band continuum is bounded by omega <= v_F·q and must collapse to zero energy as q -> 0. In Fig. 3e the experimental peak sits at ~0.4 eV at q_par ~ 0.05 A^-1. A linear extrapolation of the dashed line gives a finite intercept of ~0.3 eV at q_par = 0. With |q| ~ 0.15 A^-1 and v_F ~ 2 eV A for the sigma bands (p. 2), v_F·|q| is ~0.3 eV, and smaller still for the c-axis component, where the sigma bands are nearly flat.

The theory shows similar tension. In Fig. 4c, at q_par = 0.088 A^-1, the "sigma-sigma, intra" weight extends to ~0.55 eV. That requires v_F ~ 6 eV A, three times the quoted sigma velocity. The "pi-pi, intra" weight extends to ~1.5 eV, which would need v_F ~ 17 eV A, unphysical for MgB2. Either the "intra-band" label in the decomposition (Eq. 29) includes transitions between different bands (for example, misassignment of band indices where the 6 sigma and 2 pi tight-binding bands cross), or the momentum used is not the stated one.

The velocity numbers are also inconsistent with each other. The quoted 1.4 eV A is called "around half" of ~2 eV A. Read off Fig. 3e, the slope of the experimental points is closer to ~2.2 eV A (about 0.43 eV at 0.055 A^-1 to 0.66 eV at 0.155 A^-1).

Because the identification as the Lindhard continuum depends on these kinematics, the authors should:
- give the relevant band velocities, in-plane and along c, for each sigma and pi Fermi sheet;
- plot the computed upper edge v_F·q of each sheet for the experimental q on Fig. 3e;
- explain the finite intercept;
- check the intra/inter classification in Fig. 4c, for example by verifying that the "intra" weight vanishes above max(v_F·q).

If the weight above v_F·q comes from transitions between the two nearly degenerate sigma bands near Gamma-A (light and heavy hole), the feature is inter-band. That would directly contradict the paper's central conclusion.

3. The Raman nature of the feature and the role of the intermediate state are not tested. All RIXS data are taken at a single incident energy, tuned to the sharp pre-edge peak at ~187 eV (Fig. 2a). That pre-edge peak is a sigma-hole and core-exciton-like resonance. In metals, RIXS near threshold commonly contains a fluorescence-like (non-Raman) component, and its apparent energy loss changes with detuning and with absorption geometry. An asymmetric feature at a few hundred meV that rises from the elastic line and has a long high-energy tail (Fig. 3a) is just what threshold fluorescence or intermediate-state relaxation would also produce.

The authors should:
- show an incident-energy map (RIXS versus Omega_i across the pre-edge, at fixed q) to show that the feature stays at constant energy loss;
- report whether the BSE RIXS feature survives when the core-hole potential is switched off, or in a non-interacting (independent-particle) RIXS calculation.

This would establish whether the measured spectrum is proportional to an orbital-projected charge susceptibility, which is the key assumption linking RIXS to chi^0 in Figs. 3d-4.

B. Should address

4. The sigma-versus-pi attribution is inconsistent between the BSE and the Lindhard analysis. The BSE projection (Fig. 2c) and the text attribute the low-energy weight to sigma states near Gamma/A. However, Fig. 4c shows the pi-pi intra-band channel dominating -Im chi^0 from ~0.5 to 1.5 eV, with sigma-sigma intra weight confined below ~0.55 eV. The experimental peak (0.4-0.65 eV) therefore sits where, according to Fig. 4c, the pi contribution is comparable to or larger than the sigma contribution.

The text also refers to "two sigma bands" (p. 4), while the tight-binding model has six sigma bands (SI Sec. F), most of them far from E_F.

The authors should show the decomposition of Fig. 4c at several q, with the orbital weighting of Eq. 32 applied. They should also state which sigma and pi bands cross E_F, and reconcile the orbital character of the computed RIXS-relevant weight with the BSE projection.

5. The comparison with the Lindhard and RPA calculations is not quantitative. In Fig. 3e, chi^0 deviates from experiment by up to ~0.25 eV over roughly half of the q range (q_par < 0.10 A^-1). This is more than "closely except at smaller q". The RPA result, which the text says "matches the RIXS data" (p. 4), is shown only as a colour map (Fig. 4a) and is not included in Fig. 3e. Several modelling choices also need justification or clarification:
- The orbital weighting in Eqs. 32-33 (cos^4, sin^4 and cos^2 sin^2 factors) is an ad hoc ansatz rather than a RIXS cross-section, and ignores the outgoing polarization.
- The interaction is written as V(q) = U/q^2 with q in A^-1 and "U = 2 eV A" (SI Sec. E). These units are not consistent with each other, and the normalization (unit-cell volume, epsilon_inf) should be stated.
- The SI states that "the low-energy spectrum is unaffected by the choice of U", while the main text credits "the combination of interactions and orbital sensitivity" for shifting the weight. It should be stated explicitly which of the two does the work.

The authors should add the RPA peak positions, and the computed continuum edges, to Fig. 3e. They should also compare lineshapes and widths, not only peak positions, at two or three representative q.

6. The novelty claim needs to be narrowed and placed in context. The title and abstract present this as the first direct observation of the Lindhard continuum, which has "remained experimentally elusive". The sum-rule argument (Eq. 1) concerns only the small-q regime below the plasmon cutoff. Above the cutoff, the electron-hole continuum of simple metals has been measured directly by non-resonant inelastic x-ray scattering and momentum-resolved EELS, where the dynamic structure factor follows the Lindhard form, with known corrections. Electronic Raman scattering also probes intra-band particle-hole excitations in metals.

Ref. [21] reports monochromated EELS on MgB2 that "distinguish intra-band and inter-band transitions". The paper should explain what that work saw and why the present result differs, rather than citing it only for the inter-band features.

The claim should be restated as the first observation at q well below the plasmon cutoff, or as the first observation by RIXS, unless the authors can argue otherwise.

7. The sum-rule argument should be connected quantitatively to MgB2. The statement that "EELS ... cannot see low-energy intra-band excitations" (p. 5) is derived for a single-component system. MgB2 is a multiband metal with sigma and pi carriers of different velocities. Out-of-phase sigma-pi density fluctuations are not fully screened, and can carry continuum or acoustic-plasmon-like weight in the total response. The cited ab initio loss function (Ref. [22]) and the related low-energy charge-excitation work (Ref. [12]) address exactly this.

The authors should show the computed total loss function for MgB2 at the experimental q, from their RPA or GPAW calculation. They should also quantify the ratio of continuum weight in the total response to that in the orbital-projected response, so that the MgB2-specific claim rests on a number rather than on the toy model of Fig. S4.

8. The peak extraction and the error bars need to be described. The dispersion in Fig. 3e is built from Voigt-fit peak positions of a "markedly asymmetric" feature (Figs. 3a, S2). For an asymmetric continuum, a symmetric Voigt peak position depends on the fitting window, the background and the elastic-line model, and the high-energy tail is absorbed into another component (Fig. S2). The authors should state:
- how the error bars in Fig. 3e were obtained;
- which parameters were free and which were fixed;
- how the elastic line and the 3 eV feature were constrained;
- how omega_BSE and the chi^0 points were extracted (peak maximum, first moment, or fit).

For a continuum, the onset and the upper edge are the physically meaningful quantities. An edge- or moment-based analysis, applied identically to data and theory, would be more convincing.

9. The BSE setup may not resolve sub-eV intra-band physics. The DFT ground state uses a Fermi-Dirac smearing of 0.02 Ry (~0.27 eV; SI Sec. C). That is comparable to the energy of the feature itself, and blurs the occupations that define the intra-band window. The 36x36x28 k-grid gives an in-plane spacing of ~0.065 A^-1, about the size of the smallest q_par measured. The authors should show convergence of the low-energy BSE RIXS feature with respect to smearing and k-mesh.

The authors should also address two points of agreement:
- The BSE spectrum in Fig. 2b has almost no weight between ~0.6 and 2.5 eV, where the experiment shows a large tail, and its low-energy structure has two sharp peaks (~0.15 and ~0.35 eV) not seen in experiment.
- SCAN was chosen because it best reproduces the XAS (uncaptioned functional comparison on SI p. S8). Is the low-energy RIXS result also functional-dependent?

10. The sample and its reproducibility are not documented. The sample is an HPCVD thin film (Ref. [24]). The paper does not give the substrate, thickness, orientation or mosaicity, surface preparation, or the number of samples or spots measured. All data are at a single temperature (77 K). Boron oxide surface species and substrate contributions (for example SiC, if used) can add B K-edge and low-energy spectral weight. Angle-dependent self-absorption also changes the relative intensity across the theta_in series.

The authors should:
- specify the film;
- show the key spectrum on a second sample or spot;
- comment on surface contamination as seen in XAS;
- state whether the spectra were corrected for self-absorption before comparing intensities with the BSE (Fig. 3b,c).

C. Minor

11. Several cross-references are broken or placeholders: "S0 D", "S0 G" and "Figure S0 G" (pp. 4-5, SI Sec. C). The XAS functional comparison (SCAN/GGA/LDA, SI p. S8) has no figure number or caption.

12. Fig. 4c: two curves appear to share a red colour ("pi-pi, intra" and "sigma-sigma, inter"), and the curve colours are hard to match to the legend. The y-axis label "-Im chi" should state whether the plot shows chi^0 (as the caption implies) and whether any orbital weighting is applied.

13. Fig. 3: the colour scales of panels (b) and (c) are both labelled 0-2.5 "arb. units". The text should state how the experimental and BSE intensities were normalized relative to each other, since the intensity suppression at grazing incidence is used as a point of agreement.

14. The Lindhard and RPA calculations are done at T = 0 with delta = 0.01 eV, while the data are at 77 K with 100 meV resolution. This is harmless, but should be stated next to Fig. 3d, together with the broadening applied in Fig. 3d versus Fig. 4.

15. Typos and wording: "Florescence" (Fig. 2 caption); "arises from that fact" (p. 1); "the the" (p. 4); "motive" for "motivate" (p. 5); "voigt" for "Voigt" (Fig. S2).

16. The data availability statement ("upon reasonable request") is weak for a paper whose main claim rests on a reanalysis of peak positions. The authors should consider depositing the RIXS spectra and the tight-binding/RPA code.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add an incident-energy-detuning RIXS map at one or two q values, and a BSE run without the core-hole potential. Together these establish that the feature is a Raman-like, susceptibility-proportional response.
2. Re-plot Fig. 3e against the full 3D q, or against v_F·q per Fermi sheet. Overlay the computed continuum upper edges and the RPA peak positions, and include the Gamma-K data and, if possible, a second scattering angle.
3. Project the BSE exciton density onto band pairs (same band versus different band), not only onto individual bands. This would give a direct intra/inter decomposition of the measured-geometry RIXS signal, independent of the tight-binding model.
4. Compute the full MgB2 loss function at the experimental q, and if possible compare it with EELS on the same film. This would turn the sum-rule argument into a quantitative, material-specific contrast between RIXS and EELS.
