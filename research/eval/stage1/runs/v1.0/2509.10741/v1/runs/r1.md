Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report a low-energy (~0.3–0.6 eV) feature in boron K-edge RIXS of MgB2 thin films, measured at the 187 eV pre-edge resonance with in-plane momentum transfers up to ~0.17 Å⁻¹. They interpret it as the intraband particle–hole (Lindhard) continuum. They support this with OCEAN/BSE RIXS simulations, a tight-binding Lindhard function with an orbitally weighted RPA, and a sum-rule argument for why the continuum is invisible in EELS but visible in an orbital-selective probe. A momentum-resolved view of the intraband continuum in a metal would be of broad interest, and the case that orbital selectivity evades the screening sum rule is conceptually attractive. However, the measured energies, the scattering geometry and the band decomposition do not yet fit together well enough to establish that the feature is the Lindhard continuum. The novelty framing also leaves out earlier observations of single-particle excitation continua.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new, clear low-energy RIXS feature in a well-understood metal. It is resolved from the elastic line, and its lineshape is reproducible across many incident angles (Fig. 3a).
- Agreement between the data and first-principles BSE RIXS simulations made with the full experimental geometry (Figs. 2b, 3c), including the drop in intensity at grazing incidence.
- A clear, general argument (Eq. 1, SI Sec. G, Eqs. 34–39, Fig. S4) for why a probe of an orbitally projected density can carry O(1) intraband weight, while the total charge response suppresses it as q⁴. This could guide future RIXS work on metals.
- The prospect of ultra-soft, low-q RIXS as a momentum-resolved probe of fermiology, which is of interest well beyond MgB2.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The measured energies lie outside the kinematic bound of the intraband continuum at small in-plane momentum. The intraband continuum of a band with Fermi velocity v_F extends only to ω ≈ v_F·q and must vanish as q → 0. The paper gives the σ-band Fermi velocity as "roughly 2 eVÅ" (p. 2). The σ sheets are nearly kz-independent cylinders (Fig. 1c), so for them only q_∥ matters.
   - In Fig. 2b (q_∥ = 0.016 Å⁻¹), the low-energy feature peaks at ~0.3 eV. The σ intraband bound there is ~0.03–0.05 eV, an order of magnitude lower.
   - In Fig. 3e, the experimental peak is at ~0.40 eV at q_∥ ≈ 0.055 Å⁻¹, so ω/q ≈ 7 eVÅ. Extrapolating the dashed linear fit to q_∥ = 0 gives a finite intercept of roughly 0.25–0.3 eV. An intraband continuum cannot have a finite energy at zero momentum.
   - The BSE projection (Fig. 2c, †) still assigns the feature to σ states near Γ.
   - The quoted "speed of ≈1.4 eVÅ, around half of the Fermi velocity of the σ bands" is also internally inconsistent. Half of "roughly 2 eVÅ" is 1 eVÅ. Reading Fig. 3e directly, the slope is closer to ~2 eVÅ.
   - The χ⁰ peak positions in Fig. 3e (triangles) do extrapolate toward zero. They disagree with the data precisely at the smallest q, where the Lindhard picture is most diagnostic. The text dismisses this as spectral weight being "split between different features".

   What the authors should do:
   - State the fit form, the intercept with its uncertainty, and the resulting velocity.
   - Explain how a feature at 0.3–0.4 eV at q_∥ ≤ 0.055 Å⁻¹ can be an intraband excitation of the σ bands.
   - Alternatively, identify which bands (π, with their kz dispersion and larger velocities) could support it at the actual total momentum transfer. This is the central issue: the paper's identification rests on the dispersion, and the dispersion as presented does not have the defining property of the intraband continuum.

2. The "dispersion" is measured by rotating the momentum transfer at roughly fixed |q|, which mixes momentum dependence with polarization and orbital selection. The labels in Fig. 3a run from (q_∥, q_⊥) = (0.05, 0.14) to (0.10, 0.08) to (0.16, 0.02) Å⁻¹. So |q| stays near 0.15–0.16 Å⁻¹ while the incident angle θ_in, and with it the polarization, changes. The authors note that grazing geometry changes the σ/π orbital sensitivity (p. 3–4), and Fig. 4a shows that the orbital weighting alone moves spectral weight between dispersive features. A peak shift with θ_in therefore does not by itself show momentum dispersion. It could equally reflect a changing σ/π mixture, with π contributions sensitive to q_⊥ through their kz dispersion. It is also unclear whether the Lindhard and RPA calculations (Figs. 3d, 3e, 4) used the actual (q_∥, q_⊥) of each measurement or only q_∥ along Γ–M. The axes suggest the latter.

   What the authors should do:
   - Recompute χ⁰ and χ along the experimental (q_∥, q_⊥) trajectory.
   - Show data that separate momentum from geometry. Options include:
     - a second scattering angle, or a second incident energy within the resonance, giving different |q| at the same θ_in;
     - spectra at the same q_∥ reached at different θ_in, which would give different polarization;
     - the Γ–K data mentioned in SI Sec. A but never shown. Near Γ the σ continuum should be nearly isotropic in-plane, so Γ–M versus Γ–K is a useful consistency check.

3. Alternative origins of the feature are not ruled out.
   - (i) Acoustic plasmon. Ref. [12] (Silkin et al., PRB 80, 054521), which the authors cite, predicts a low-energy acoustic plasmon in MgB2 from out-of-phase σ and π carrier oscillations. It disperses roughly linearly with a velocity between the σ and π Fermi velocities, in the energy and momentum window studied here. The introduction even names acoustic plasmons, yet this collective mode is not discussed as an alternative. A linearly dispersing peak (rather than a broad continuum) is arguably closer to that prediction. The orbitally resolved RPA (Fig. 4a) should either contain such a mode or show that it is absent or overdamped. The authors should state which.
   - (ii) Resonance and fluorescence effects. The data are taken at a single incident energy on a sharp, exciton-like pre-edge resonance (Fig. 2a). Over the relevant energy range, the unoccupied σ states near Γ/A extend only ~0.5–0.8 eV above E_F (Fig. 2c, S1). That is the same energy scale as the feature. A process tied to the σ hole-pocket depth, or a fluorescence-like contribution, could produce a feature in this range.
   - (iii) Interband σ1–σ2 transitions. The two σ bands are nearly degenerate at Γ, so transitions between them also produce low-energy, q-dependent weight.

   What the authors should do: show an incident-energy map across the 187 eV pre-edge at fixed geometry, to demonstrate a constant (Raman-like) energy loss and resonance behaviour consistent with the BSE. Then discuss (i)–(iii) explicitly, using their calculations to rule them out.

4. The intra/interband decomposition in Fig. 4c, which carries the claim that the analysis "conclusively demonstrates" intraband origin, looks unphysical as presented.
   - At q_∥ = 0.088 Å⁻¹, the "ππ intra" component extends to ~1.5 eV and the "σσ intra" component to ~0.5 eV. These imply ω/q ≈ 17 eVÅ and ≈ 6 eVÅ. Both are far above the Fermi velocities in MgB2 (≲ 5–6 eVÅ for π, ~2–3 eVÅ for σ).
   - Possible causes include insufficient k-point sampling relative to q, a mislabelled q (for example a large implicit q_⊥), or an "intraband" label assigned by band index ordering that mixes bands across crossings.
   - SI Sec. F states "an N×N×N grid ... where N=512". If this means 512 points in total (8×8×8), the grid spacing (~0.3 Å⁻¹ in-plane) exceeds the entire measured q range, and intraband transitions at q ≤ 0.17 Å⁻¹ cannot be resolved with δ = 0.01 eV.
   - How "intraband" is defined for the two σ bands, which are degenerate at Γ, should be stated. The bands are labelled by energy ordering, by orbital character or by continuity, and the choice changes the decomposition.
   - The Fig. 4 caption says interband σ–π excitations are negligible, but the legend shows only σσ-inter and ππ-inter, with no σπ curve.

   What the authors should do:
   - Clarify the k-mesh and show convergence of χ⁰ with respect to mesh and δ at the measured q.
   - Overlay the v_F·q bounds of each band on Figs. 3d and 4.
   - Give the σπ interband component.

B. Should address

5. The BSE RIXS simulations may not be converged at these momenta. The OCEAN calculation uses a 36×36×28 k-mesh (SI Sec. C), which gives an in-plane spacing of ~0.06–0.07 Å⁻¹. The measured q_∥ range is 0.05–0.16 Å⁻¹, only about one to three grid spacings, and Fig. 2b is at 0.016 Å⁻¹, well below the spacing. Combined with 0.1 eV broadening, it is unclear how the BSE resolves intraband transitions at these momenta, and whether the agreement in Figs. 2b, 3c and 3e is robust. The authors should explain how sub-mesh momentum transfers are treated and show convergence with a denser mesh, at least at one q. They should also show that the low-energy feature in the BSE is absent or changed when transitions are restricted (for example, when intraband σ transitions are excluded). That would be a far more direct test of the "intraband" assignment than the projection in Fig. 2c.

6. The comparison uses only peak positions; the lineshape and intensity scaling expected of a continuum are not tested.
   - The intraband continuum is a bounded continuum, not a peak. For a 2D cylinder, Im χ⁰ ∝ ω/√(v_F²q² − ω²) up to v_F·q. Its integrated weight should grow with q at small q.
   - The extracted peak positions (Fig. 3e) come from Voigt fits to a feature the authors describe as "markedly asymmetric" (Fig. 3a, S2), so the "peak" depends on the fit model.
   - The error bars in Fig. 3e are not defined.

   What the authors should do:
   - Compare full lineshapes, for example the leading and trailing edges or the first moment, with χ⁰/χ convolved with the resolution at each (q_∥, q_⊥).
   - Show how the integrated low-energy intensity scales with q once normalized for the geometry-dependent cross-section and self-absorption.
   - State how the error bars were obtained and which fit parameters were free.

7. Mapping the RIXS cross-section to an orbitally weighted RPA susceptibility needs justification and internal consistency.
   - Eqs. 32–33 weight only the p_y,p_y, p_z,p_z and mixed components with cos⁴/sin⁴ factors. They omit s and p_x contributions and the core-hole intermediate-state physics that the BSE includes. The authors should justify this (for example, an ultrashort core-hole lifetime approximation) or show that the conclusions do not depend on it.
   - The paper says the combination of "interactions and orbital sensitivity" shifts the weight (Figs. 4a vs 4b). SI Sec. E, however, states that "the low-energy spectrum is unaffected by the choice of the parameter U". If so, the shift comes from orbital weighting, not interactions, and the RPA adds little. The authors should clarify this.
   - The units of V(q) = U/q² with "U = 2 eVÅ" do not give an energy without a volume normalization. This should be fixed.

8. The novelty claims need qualification.
   - The abstract and introduction state that the Lindhard continuum "has remained experimentally elusive" and that "no feature resembling the textbook illustration ... has been reported". However, the intraband single-particle-excitation continuum, dispersing with v_F·q, has long been observed in resonant inelastic light scattering from 2D electron gases in semiconductor heterostructures. That work relied on the same resonance-enabled escape from Coulomb screening that the authors invoke.
   - Particle–hole continua have also been discussed and compared to Lindhard-type calculations in RIXS and momentum-resolved EELS of metallic oxides.
   - The claim should be narrowed, for example to "first momentum-resolved observation by RIXS in a three-dimensional multiband metal", and the relation to these works discussed.
   - The sum-rule argument (Eq. 1) shows that the continuum carries small weight in L(q,ω), not zero weight. The statement that EELS "cannot see" intraband excitations should be softened accordingly.

9. Sample characterization and reproducibility are missing. The sample is cited only by reference to the growth paper [24]. The paper does not give:
   - film thickness, substrate or orientation;
   - surface preparation;
   - how many samples or sample positions were measured, or whether the feature reproduces.

   At ~190 eV the probing depth is tens of nm, and MgB2 surfaces readily form Mg and B oxides. The B–O π* resonance of borates lies near 194 eV, which coincides with the strong TFY peak that the BSE does not fully reproduce (Fig. 2a, S3). The authors should:
   - report the film details;
   - assess surface oxidation, for example by comparing TEY and TFY or with XPS;
   - state whether self-absorption corrections were applied across θ_in;
   - show the feature on at least a second sample or spot.

C. Minor

10. Fig. 4c: the legend lacks a σπ-interband curve, although the caption refers to σ–π interband excitations. The y-axis label (−Im χ) should specify whether χ⁰ is plotted.

11. Fig. 3a: give the θ_in for each spectrum, explain the normalization, and specify the Voigt components used (elastic, low-energy feature, background) and their constraints. Fig. 3b/3c: state whether the colour maps are normalized identically. Fig. 2b: state the θ_in and q_⊥ for q_∥ = 0.016 Å⁻¹.

12. The text quotes "the regime below 0.2 Å⁻¹" (p. 2) and "up to ≈ 0.17 Å⁻¹" (p. 3). The plotted range in Fig. 3 ends at ~0.16 Å⁻¹. Please make these consistent.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a figure overlaying the v_F·q bounds of each σ and π band, computed at the actual (q_∥, q_⊥) of each spectrum, on the experimental map (Fig. 3b). This lets readers see directly whether the feature lies inside the intraband continuum.
2. Measure an incident-energy RIXS map across the pre-edge, and spectra at a second scattering angle or at matched q_∥ with different θ_in. Together these separate genuine momentum dispersion from resonance and polarization effects.
3. Run a "switch-off" test in the BSE or tight-binding calculations: remove σ intraband (or all intraband) transitions and show that the low-energy feature disappears. Discuss the predicted acoustic plasmon of Ref. [12] explicitly.
4. Reframe the novelty in relation to prior observations of single-particle continua by resonant inelastic light scattering and momentum-resolved EELS, and emphasize what is new here: orbital- and momentum-resolved access in a multiband 3D metal.
