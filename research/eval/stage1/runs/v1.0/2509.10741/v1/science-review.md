A. Must address

1. The measured energies violate the kinematic bound of an intra-band continuum. An intra-band particle-hole continuum is bounded by ω ≤ v_F·q (plus a small curvature term) and must collapse to zero energy as q → 0. The paper takes v_F(σ) ≈ 2 eVÅ (p. 2), and because the σ sheets are nearly kz-independent cylinders (Fig. 1c), only q_∥ matters for them. At q_∥ = 0.016 Å⁻¹ (Fig. 2b) the σ intra-band bound is ~0.03–0.05 eV, yet the feature peaks at ~0.3 eV. At q_∥ ≈ 0.05–0.055 Å⁻¹ (Fig. 3e) the bound is ~0.1 eV, but ω_exp ≈ 0.4 eV (ω/q ≈ 7 eVÅ). Over the whole range in Fig. 3e the data lie well above v_F·q, and the dashed linear fit extrapolates to a finite intercept of ~0.25–0.3 eV at q_∥ = 0, which an intra-band continuum cannot have. Even using |q| ~ 0.15 Å⁻¹, v_F·|q| is only ~0.3 eV, and smaller for the c-axis component, where the σ bands are nearly flat. The BSE projection (Fig. 2c, †) nevertheless assigns the feature to σ states near Γ. The velocity numbers are also internally inconsistent: the quoted "≈1.4 eVÅ, around half of the Fermi velocity of the σ bands" implies 1 eVÅ if v_F ≈ 2 eVÅ, while the slope of the experimental points in Fig. 3e reads closer to ~2–2.2 eVÅ (about 0.43 eV at 0.055 Å⁻¹ to 0.66 eV at 0.155 Å⁻¹). The quoted σ Fermi velocity itself should be checked, since DFT literature values for the MgB2 sheets are generally higher (roughly 3–5 eVÅ), and the value matters for this argument. The χ⁰ peak positions in Fig. 3e (triangles) do extrapolate toward zero, but they disagree with the data precisely at the smallest q, where the Lindhard picture is most diagnostic; the text dismisses this as spectral weight being "split between different features". Because the identification as the Lindhard continuum rests on the dispersion, and the dispersion as presented lacks the defining property of an intra-band continuum, the central claim does not yet follow. The authors should state the fit form, the slope and intercept with uncertainties; give in-plane and c-axis band velocities for each σ and π Fermi sheet; overlay the per-sheet upper edges ω = v_F^(i)·q, computed with the full 3D q of each spectrum, on Figs. 3b–e; and explain how a feature at 0.3–0.4 eV at q_∥ ≤ 0.055 Å⁻¹, including the clear 0.3 eV peak at q_∥ = 0.016 Å⁻¹, can be an intra-band σ excitation, or else identify which bands (for example π, with their kz dispersion and larger velocities) could support it at the actual total momentum transfer.

2. The "dispersion" is measured by rotating q at nearly constant |q|, so in-plane momentum, out-of-plane momentum, incidence angle and polarization all change together. The Fig. 3a labels run from (q_∥, q_⊥) = (0.05, 0.14) through (0.10, 0.08) to (0.16, 0.02) Å⁻¹, i.e. |q| ≈ 0.149, 0.128 and 0.161 Å⁻¹, roughly constant as expected for a fixed scattering angle at 190 eV (photon momentum ~0.096 Å⁻¹), though the middle point does not quite fit, so the geometry is unclear. Rocking θ_in mostly rotates q from near the c axis into the plane while changing the polarization projection (π-polarized beam rotating from in-plane to out-of-plane orbital sensitivity) and the self-absorption. The authors themselves note that grazing geometry changes σ/π sensitivity (pp. 3–4), and Fig. 4a shows that orbital weighting alone moves spectral weight between dispersive features. The peak shift from ~0.4 to ~0.65 eV (Fig. 3e) could therefore be an angular (anisotropy plus matrix-element) dependence at fixed |q|, or a changing q_⊥ to which the 3D π sheets are sensitive, rather than an in-plane dispersion; the "nearly linear dispersion" and velocity extracted against q_∥ alone (p. 3) do not have the meaning given to them, since a particle-hole continuum depends on the full 3D q through v_F·q, which is strongly anisotropic in MgB2. It is also unclear whether the Lindhard and RPA calculations (Figs. 3d, 3e, 4) used the actual (q_∥, q_⊥) of each spectrum or only q_∥ along Γ–M with q_⊥ = 0; the axes suggest the latter. The authors should give a table of θ_in, 2θ, q_∥, q_⊥ and |q| for every spectrum; recompute χ⁰ and χ along the experimental (q_∥, q_⊥) trajectory; and show data that separate momentum from geometry, for example a second scattering angle or second incident energy within the resonance (different |q| at the same θ_in or direction), spectra at the same q_∥ reached at different θ_in, σ-polarized incident spectra at the same q (which under Eq. 32 change the p_x/p_y weights at fixed q and so isolate matrix-element effects), and the Γ–K data mentioned in SI Sec. A but never shown (near Γ the σ continuum should be nearly isotropic in-plane, so Γ–M versus Γ–K is a consistency check).

The intra/inter-band decomposition in Fig. 4c, which carries the claim that the analysis "conclusively demonstrates" an intra-band origin, looks unphysical and is ambiguously defined, so it does not exclude inter-band alternatives. At q_∥ = 0.088 Å⁻¹ the "σσ intra" weight extends to ~0.5–0.6 eV and the "ππ intra" weight to ~1.5 eV, implying ω/q ≈ 6–7 and ≈ 17 eVÅ, far above any Fermi velocity in MgB2 (≲ 5–6 eVÅ for π, ~2–3 eVÅ for σ). Possible causes include insufficient k-point sampling relative to q (SI Sec. F states "an N×N×N grid ... where N=512"; if this means 512 points in total, i.e. 8×8×8, the in-plane spacing of ~0.3 Å⁻¹ exceeds the whole measured q range and intra-band transitions at q ≤ 0.17 Å⁻¹ cannot be resolved with δ = 0.01 eV), a mislabelled q (for example a large implicit q_⊥), or an "intra-band" label assigned by band-index ordering (Eq. 29) that mixes bands across crossings of the 6 σ and 2 π tight-binding bands. Inter-band transitions between the light- and heavy-hole σ bands, which are degenerate along Γ–A and split away from it, would naturally give low-energy, q-dependent weight at finite energy as q → 0 and explain the finite intercept of Concern 1; if they carry the weight, the feature is inter-band and the title and main conclusion change. The legend (σσ intra, σσ inter, ππ intra, ππ inter) also does not match the caption's statement that σ–π inter-band excitations are negligible: no σπ curve is shown, and it is unclear whether transitions between the two σ bands count as intra or inter. The authors should define the decomposition precisely (band index a → b for each of the eight TB bands, and whether bands are labelled by energy ordering, orbital character or continuity), show each Fermi-surface-crossing band pair separately including the σπ component, verify that each "intra" channel vanishes above its max(v_F·q), clarify the k-mesh and show convergence of χ⁰ with respect to mesh and δ at the measured q, and overlay the v_F·q bounds of each band on Figs. 3d and 4.

4. The Raman nature of the feature and the role of the intermediate state are not tested. All RIXS data are taken at a single incident energy on the sharp, exciton-like σ-hole pre-edge resonance at ~187 eV (Fig. 2a). In metals, RIXS near threshold commonly contains a fluorescence-like (non-Raman) component whose apparent energy loss changes with detuning and absorption geometry; an asymmetric feature at a few hundred meV that rises from the elastic line with a long high-energy tail (Fig. 3a) is what threshold fluorescence or intermediate-state relaxation would also produce. Moreover, the unoccupied σ states near Γ/A extend only ~0.5–0.8 eV above E_F (Figs. 2c, S1), the same energy scale as the feature, so a process tied to the σ hole-pocket depth could also generate it. This matters because the link between RIXS and χ⁰ in Figs. 3d–4 assumes the spectrum is proportional to an orbital-projected charge susceptibility. The authors should show an incident-energy RIXS map across the pre-edge at one or two fixed q to demonstrate a constant (Raman-like) energy loss and resonance behaviour consistent with the BSE; add off-resonance references (below the edge, and at the main edge ~192–194 eV, where the predicted orbital selectivity differs); and report whether the BSE RIXS feature survives when the core-hole potential is switched off or in an independent-particle RIXS calculation.

5. The BSE RIXS simulations are probably not converged at the relevant momenta and energy scale, so their agreement with the data cannot yet support the intra-band assignment. SI Sec. C uses a 36×36×28 k-mesh with in-plane spacing ~0.065 Å⁻¹ (about 2.35/36), comparable to or larger than the measured q_∥ (0.05–0.16 Å⁻¹ in Fig. 3, and 0.016 Å⁻¹ in Fig. 2b, well below the spacing); Fermi-Dirac smearing of 0.02 Ry ≈ 0.27 eV, comparable to the feature energy itself and blurring the occupations that define the intra-band window; 0.1 eV broadening; and only 12 bands with the first 4 occupied bands removed. With these settings, intra-band transitions at sub-mesh q are not resolved, and the smearing alone could generate a low-energy peak at a few hundred meV. The agreement of ω_BSE with experiment (Figs. 2b, 3c, 3e) and the band projection in Fig. 2c (used to say the transition is "localized within the σ bands ... close to the BZ center") therefore need convergence tests. The BSE also disagrees with the data in places: in Fig. 2b it has almost no weight between ~0.6 and 2.5 eV, where the experiment shows a large tail, and its low-energy structure has two sharp peaks (~0.15 and ~0.35 eV) not seen in experiment; SCAN was chosen because it best reproduces the XAS (uncaptioned comparison on SI p. S8), leaving open whether the low-energy RIXS is functional-dependent. The authors should state how momentum transfers smaller than the k-spacing are represented; repeat the RIXS BSE with a denser (or shifted or interpolated) mesh and much smaller smearing, at least at one q, and show the feature and its dispersion are stable; check the functional dependence; and run a direct test of the assignment, either a "switch-off" calculation excluding σ intra-band (or all intra-band) transitions, or a projection of the BSE exciton density onto band pairs (same band versus different band) to give an intra/inter decomposition of the measured-geometry signal independent of the tight-binding model.

6. A predicted collective mode in the same window is not considered. Ref. [12] (Silkin et al., PRB 80, 054521), which the authors cite, predicts a low-energy acoustic plasmon in MgB2 from out-of-phase σ and π carrier oscillations, dispersing roughly linearly with a velocity between the σ and π Fermi velocities in the energy and momentum range studied here. The introduction names acoustic plasmons, yet this mode is not discussed as an alternative, and a linearly dispersing peak with a finite apparent velocity is arguably closer to that prediction than to a broad continuum. The orbitally resolved RPA (Fig. 4a) should either contain such a mode or show it is absent or overdamped. The authors should state which, and discuss the acoustic plasmon explicitly as an alternative assignment.

B. Should address

7. A peak fit is used to describe a continuum, and the lineshape and intensity scaling expected of a continuum are not tested. The feature is "markedly asymmetric" (p. 2; Figs. 3a, S2), yet its position is extracted from a symmetric Voigt alongside an elastic line whose tail overlaps it strongly at the lowest q, while the high-energy tail is absorbed into another component (Fig. S2). The peak position then depends on the fitting window, background and elastic/quasi-elastic model. A Lindhard continuum has no unique peak velocity: for a 2D cylinder Im χ⁰ ∝ ω/√(v_F²q² − ω²) up to v_F·q, its defining features are the onset at ω → 0 and the upper edge, and its integrated weight should grow with q at small q. Electron-phonon coupling to the E2g mode in the σ bands and phonon or multiphonon contributions near the elastic line are not considered in the fit. The error bars in Fig. 3e are not defined, and it is not stated how ω_BSE and the χ⁰ points were extracted. The authors should state how the error bars were obtained, which fit parameters were free or fixed, which Voigt components were used (elastic, low-energy feature, 3 eV feature, background) and their constraints; fit with an asymmetric or model-based lineshape (for example the broadened, weighted χ of Fig. 4a convolved with resolution); report leading and upper edges or the first moment, applied identically to data and theory; compare full lineshapes and widths with resolution-convolved χ⁰/χ (with and without RPA) at each measured (q_∥, q_⊥), on a common intensity scale; and show how the integrated low-energy intensity scales with q after normalizing for the geometry-dependent cross section and self-absorption.

11. The sum-rule argument needs to be connected quantitatively to MgB2. The statement that EELS "cannot see" low-energy intra-band excitations (p. 5) is derived for a single-component system, and Eq. 1 shows only that the continuum carries small, not zero, weight in L(q, ω). MgB2 is a multiband metal with σ and π carriers of different velocities; out-of-phase σ–π density fluctuations are not fully screened and can carry continuum or acoustic-plasmon-like weight in the total response, as the cited ab initio loss function (Ref. [22]) and Ref. [12] address. The authors should compute the total loss function of MgB2 at the experimental q from their RPA or GPAW calculation, quantify the ratio of continuum weight in the total versus orbital-projected response so the claim rests on a material-specific number rather than the toy model of Fig. S4, ideally compare with EELS on the same film, and soften the "cannot see" wording accordingly.

12. The novelty claims are overstated. The abstract and introduction say the Lindhard continuum "has remained experimentally elusive" and that "no feature resembling the textbook illustration ... has been reported". Against this: the intra-band single-particle continuum dispersing with v_F·q has long been observed by resonant inelastic light scattering from 2D electron gases in semiconductor heterostructures, relying on the same resonance-enabled escape from screening; non-resonant inelastic x-ray scattering and momentum-resolved EELS on simple metals (for example Li, Na, Al) have measured S(q, ω) beyond the plasmon cutoff, where it follows the Lindhard form with known corrections; electronic Raman scattering probes intra-band particle-hole excitations in metals at small q; and particle-hole continua have been compared with Lindhard-type calculations in RIXS and momentum-resolved EELS of metallic oxides, including Ref. 23. Ref. [21] reports monochromated EELS on MgB2 that "distinguish intra-band and inter-band transitions", but is cited only for inter-band features. The sum-rule argument applies only at small q, below the plasmon cutoff. The authors should cite and discuss this literature, explain what Ref. [21] saw and how the present result differs, and narrow the claim, for example to the first momentum-resolved observation of the small-q, below-cutoff intra-band continuum by RIXS, or via an orbital-selective probe, in a three-dimensional multiband metal.
