Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use ultra-soft RIXS at the boron K pre-edge (about 187 eV) on HPCVD MgB2 thin films. They report an asymmetric low-energy feature between about 0.35 and 0.6 eV that disperses with in-plane momentum transfer q|| (0.05 to 0.16 Å^-1) at about 1.4 eV·Å. They assign it to the intraband particle-hole (Lindhard) continuum, using OCEAN/BSE RIXS simulations, an eight-band tight-binding Lindhard function, an orbitally weighted RPA, and a sum-rule argument that orbital-selective probes escape the q^4 screening suppression that hides the continuum from EELS. If the assignment holds, the result is significant: it would be the first momentum-resolved spectroscopic view of the small-q intraband continuum in a metal, and it would open RIXS as a probe of fermiology. Whether the data show the non-interacting continuum, and not an interaction-renormalized or interband feature, is not yet settled by what is presented.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear, previously unreported dispersive low-energy feature in a well-understood metal. It is measured at two beamlines (ALS qRIXS and the BESSY II meV-RIXS endstation) and along both Γ–M and Γ–K.
- Several layers of theory (BSE RIXS, tight-binding χ0, orbital-resolved RPA), each reproducing parts of the data. The BSE simulation captures the dispersion and the intensity suppression at grazing geometry (Fig. 3b,c,e).
- A physically appealing sum-rule argument (Eq. 1, Eqs. 4–5) for why the small-q intraband continuum is invisible in total-charge probes but can appear in an orbitally selective one. This is a useful conceptual point beyond MgB2.
- The data have been deposited and the Lindhard code is public.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The feature does not extrapolate to zero energy as q goes to zero, which an intraband continuum must do. The intraband continuum is bounded by ω ≤ v_F q, so its weight must collapse towards zero energy as q goes to zero. In Fig. 3e the experimental peak is already at about 0.35 eV at q|| ≈ 0.05 Å^-1, and the linear guide extrapolates to roughly 0.25–0.3 eV at q|| = 0. With the quoted σ Fermi velocity of about 2 eV·Å, the σ continuum at q|| = 0.05 Å^-1 extends only to about 0.1 eV. In Fig. 2b, taken at q|| = 0.016 Å^-1, the dagger feature already sits at a few tenths of an eV. At that q||, any in-plane σ intraband response would lie inside the 100 meV elastic line. The tight-binding χ0 points in Fig. 3e (blue triangles) do head towards low energy at small q||, and the text concedes they deviate from experiment there ("split between different features"). So on the paper's own evidence, the non-interacting Lindhard function does not reproduce the small-q end of the dispersion; only the BSE simulation does.

   The authors should:
   - state explicitly what happens to the observed feature as q goes to zero;
   - explain the finite-energy offset (out-of-plane momentum, band anisotropy, interactions, an interband admixture);
   - fit the data with a functional form that has a physically motivated offset, or with none, and report the intercept with its uncertainty;
   - if possible, add data at smaller total |Q|, for example at smaller 2θ.

   Without this, "linear dispersion with velocity comparable to v_F" is not enough to identify the Lindhard continuum.

2. Out-of-plane momentum changes together with q||, and the q labels are hard to reconcile with the geometry. q|| is varied by rotating θin at fixed 2θ, so q⊥ falls as q|| rises. The Fig. 3a labels go from (0.05, 0.14) to (0.16, 0.02), so the total |q| is nearly constant across the "dispersion". The σ sheets are quasi-2D, but the π sheets are 3D, and Fig. 4c assigns a large share of the low-energy weight to ππ intraband transitions, which disperse with q⊥. The apparent dispersion against q|| could therefore partly reflect the changing q⊥. That would also bear on concern 1: at q|| = 0.016 Å^-1, q⊥ is near its maximum.

   I also could not reconcile the labels with the stated geometry. With hν ≈ 187–190 eV, |Q| = 2k sin(θ/2) is about 0.18 Å^-1 at 2θ = 150° and about 0.13 Å^-1 at 2θ = 90°. Neither matches sqrt(q||² + q⊥²) for both (0.16, 0.02) and (0.05, 0.14). The units of the labels (Å^-1 or r.l.u.) and the 2θ used for each dataset are not stated.

   The authors should:
   - give, for every spectrum in Fig. 3, the full (q||, q⊥), its units, the 2θ, the beamline and the sample temperature;
   - state whether the χ0, RPA and BSE calculations in Figs. 3c–e and 4 use the full three-dimensional q of each spectrum or only q|| along Γ–M;
   - ideally, add a measurement at fixed q|| and different q⊥ (using the two 2θ values available) to separate the two dependences.

3. The data may show an interaction-renormalized or collective response rather than the non-interacting continuum. The title and abstract claim "quantitative agreement with simulations of the non-interacting charge susceptibility". But Fig. 3e shows χ0 failing at small q||. The calculation said to reproduce the data in energy and momentum is the interacting, orbitally weighted RPA (Fig. 4a). Comparing Fig. 4a with Fig. 4b, interactions visibly move the weight to higher energy.

   In a two-component (σ/π) metal with the Coulomb interaction coupled to total density, the unscreened orbital-projected channel of Eq. 5 is exactly the out-of-phase channel that hosts the acoustic plasmon predicted for MgB2 (refs. 15, 26). The acoustic plasmon is dismissed only by a reference to Supplementary Section F ("expected at a smaller energy range"), and the main text gives no quantitative comparison.

   The authors should:
   - plot, in Fig. 3e, the RPA peak dispersion alongside χ0, BSE and experiment;
   - show whether the orbital-resolved RPA response has any pole or sharp mode in the measured window, and where the acoustic-plasmon branch would fall in the same (q||, q⊥) coordinates;
   - say what in the data (lineshape, width growing with q, a high-energy edge scaling with v_F q) distinguishes a continuum from a damped collective mode or from a renormalized continuum.

   If the best description is an interaction-dressed, orbitally weighted response, the title and abstract should say so.

B. Should address

4. The σ versus π assignment is inconsistent across the paper. Three passages disagree:
   - The Results text says the pre-edge resonance is mainly planar px,py (σ) holes, and that the BSE projection places the low-energy transition in the σ bands near Γ (Fig. 2c).
   - The Methods say the incident energy was tuned to "the π*-like resonance".
   - Fig. 4c shows ππ intraband weight dominating the spectrum from about 0.4 eV to 1.5 eV at q|| = 0.088 Å^-1, while σσ intraband weight peaks near 0.3–0.4 eV.

   The quoted velocity (1.4 eV·Å, "around half" the σ Fermi velocity) is also compared only with σ. The authors should state which band(s) produce the observed peak at each q||, give the tight-binding Fermi velocities of each sheet along Γ–M and along c, and reconcile the Methods wording with the Results.

5. The observed velocity is below v_F without explanation. For a 2D cylindrical sheet, Im χ0 at fixed q rises towards, and peaks at, the upper edge ω = v_F q. A peak tracking about 0.7 v_F therefore needs explaining. Possible causes are averaging over the hexagonally warped σ sheets, the q⊥ contribution, matrix elements, or convolution with the elastic tail. The authors should show the upper edge or onset of the continuum against q, which is a sharper test of the Lindhard bound than the peak maximum, and compare it with v_F q from the tight-binding model.

6. The peak-extraction model and its uncertainties are not described in the main text. Peak positions come from asymmetric Voigt fits (Supplementary Fig. S4), and the error bars come only from the covariance matrix. The feature lies just 0.25–0.5 eV above an elastic and quasi-elastic line of about 100 meV resolution, and its lineshape is intrinsically asymmetric. The "peak position" of a continuum therefore depends on the background, the elastic-line model and the asymmetry parameter. The authors should state which parameters are free or fixed, and how the elastic line and the high-energy background (the tail of the 3 eV feature) were modelled. They should test robustness by refitting with alternative background and lineshape models, and include this systematic spread in the Fig. 3e error bars.

7. Raman behaviour of the low-energy feature is not shown. All low-q data are at one incident energy (the 187 eV pre-edge). At a sharp core resonance, fluorescence-like or intermediate-state features can mimic constant-loss features. The authors should show in the main text, or point explicitly to, a detuning series across the pre-edge at fixed q. It should confirm that the 0.3–0.6 eV feature stays at constant energy loss and give its resonance profile. It is not clear whether Supplementary Fig. S2 covers the low-energy feature or only the >2 eV interband features.

8. Sample characterization is missing and the number of samples is unclear. The paper reports no film thickness, substrate, Tc, residual resistivity ratio, XRD or surface preparation, and does not say how many films were measured. The TFY XAS in Fig. 2a has strong structure at about 192–196 eV. The region near 194 eV is where the B2O3 π* resonance appears, so surface oxidation, or a boron-oxide or hydroxide overlayer, should be ruled out. This matters because RIXS at a 1–2° grazing geometry is more surface-sensitive. Data were also taken at 77 K (ALS) and 28 K (BESSY II); 28 K is below Tc for typical HPCVD films. The authors should:
   - give the film characterization;
   - state which figure uses which sample, beamline and temperature;
   - show that the dispersion is the same in at least two films, or at both beamlines and both temperatures, overlaid in one panel;
   - comment on the oxide.

9. The comparison between calculations and data is mostly qualitative, and several model parameters are tuned. The evidence for "quantitative agreement" is mainly visual comparison of colour maps (Fig. 3b,c,d; Fig. 4a,b) and peak positions (Fig. 3e).
   - In the RPA, U = 2 eV·Å is calibrated against the 3 eV feature. That feature is then itself interpreted as a Landau-damped collective excitation.
   - The orbital weights cos^4 θin and sin^4 θin are ad hoc (the Fig. 4 caption calls them "sinusoidal factors").
   - The tight-binding parameters are fitted to DFT.

   The authors should:
   - show lineshape overlays at several q|| (data against BSE, χ0 convolved with the resolution, and RPA);
   - justify the polarization weights from the actual scattering geometry and dipole matrix elements;
   - show how the RPA peak moves when U varies within a reasonable range.

10. The sum-rule argument and the novelty claim are stated more broadly than the paper supports.
    - The claim that the Lindhard continuum "has remained experimentally elusive" should be limited to the small-q regime, q much less than k_F, where the plasmon saturates the screening sum rule. Non-resonant IXS has measured particle-hole continua in simple metals at q of order k_F and above, and ref. 29 (IXS on MgB2) treats the single-particle/collective crossover.
    - Eqs. 4–5 come from a two-band toy RPA with orbital-diagonal coupling. The conditions under which χ_aa keeps O(1) weight should be stated, including the ratio of orbital susceptibilities and the role of the core-hole potential in the intermediate state.
    - The authors should also check that the full BSE RIXS calculation (which includes the core-hole potential and the polarization dependence) shows the same lack of screening, for example by comparing RIXS-weighted and total-density (EELS-like) BSE or RPA responses at the same q.

11. The intensity normalization across angles is not described. The suppression of the feature at grazing geometry is attributed to the out-of-plane component of π polarization. Changing θin also changes the probing depth, self-absorption and footprint, and that matters for intensity comparisons in Fig. 3b and for the polarization analysis in Supplementary Fig. S5. The authors should state how the spectra were normalized against θin and whether self-absorption corrections were applied.

C. Minor

12. In Eq. 1, the loss function is written with "J" where Im is presumably meant. V(q) = U/q² with U given in eV·Å has inconsistent dimensions; please define V(q) and its units properly.

13. Figure 3e has an unlabeled left axis, with the energy axis on the right. The q|| ranges differ between panels: the colour maps start at about 0.06 Å^-1 and the points at 0.05 Å^-1. Please harmonize them.

14. The statement that the Γ–M and Γ–K directions show "no difference" should be supported by an overlay in the main text or a clearly referenced supplementary figure, since anisotropy of the σ Fermi velocity is a natural test of fermiology.

15. In Fig. 2c, the markers projecting XAS and RIXS weight onto the bands are hard to read. Please state the momentum transfer and broadening used for the projection, and make clear which band each symbol refers to.

16. "Weakly correlated" should be qualified, given MgB2's strong σ–E2g electron-phonon coupling. The authors could comment on whether phonon renormalization of the σ velocity near E_F (a kink at about 70 meV) affects the comparison with the bare tight-binding v_F.

17. Please state the momentum resolution, including the angular acceptance and the uncertainty in θin, and add it as horizontal error bars in Fig. 3e.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a single summary figure that plots, against the full momentum transfer, the experimental feature (onset, peak and upper edge) with the χ0 band of allowed intraband excitations (0 ≤ ω ≤ v_F q for each sheet), the RPA peak, the BSE peak and the expected acoustic-plasmon branch. This would make the identification, and its limits, transparent.
2. Use the two available 2θ values to map the feature against q⊥ at fixed q||, and against q|| at fixed q⊥. This separates the σ (2D) and π (3D) contributions and directly tests the intraband scaling.
3. Show a complete resolution-convolved lineshape comparison (data against χ0, RPA and BSE) at three representative q||, with the fit residuals. Use the high-energy edge of the continuum, not only the peak, as the quantitative test.
4. Report film characterization and show reproducibility across films and beamlines in the main text or Methods. Include a check for a surface boron-oxide layer.
