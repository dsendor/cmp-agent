Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report ultra-soft RIXS at the boron K pre-edge (~187 eV) on HPCVD MgB2 films. They find a low-energy (~0.3-0.6 eV), asymmetric feature that disperses with in-plane momentum (q_par ~0.05-0.16 Å^-1). Using BSE (OCEAN) simulations, an eight-band tight-binding Lindhard function and an orbitally resolved RPA, they assign it to the intraband (mainly sigma-band) particle-hole continuum, i.e. the Lindhard continuum. They also give a sum-rule argument for why EELS and non-resonant IXS cannot see this continuum while an orbital-selective probe can. If the assignment holds, this is a conceptually attractive result: it would establish soft-X-ray RIXS as a momentum-resolved probe of intraband particle-hole excitations in metals, and the sum-rule argument would apply beyond MgB2.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The question is clear and fundamental, and the argument for why RIXS, unlike EELS, should see the continuum (non-conserved, orbital-projected density; Eqs. 1, 4, 5) is physically sensible and well presented.
- The material and absorption edge are well chosen. MgB2 is a weakly correlated metal with well-known DFT bands. The B K edge gives small momentum transfers with good momentum resolution, and the strong interband features sit above ~2 eV.
- Three theory approaches (BSE RIXS, TB Lindhard and orbital RPA) are compared with the data, and the BSE reproduces the measured peak positions in Fig. 3e quite closely.
- Data were taken at two facilities (ALS qRIXS and BESSY II meV-RIXS), along two in-plane directions and with two polarizations. Data and code are made available.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The measured dispersion does not appear to go to zero as q -> 0, which an intraband continuum must. The defining property of the Lindhard continuum is that its upper edge (and, for a cylindrical sigma sheet, the peak of Im chi0, which sits near omega ~ v_F q) goes to zero linearly with q. The data in the paper do not seem to show this.
   - In Fig. 3e, by my reading, omega_exp rises from ~0.42 eV at q_par ~0.055 Å^-1 to ~0.6 eV at ~0.155 Å^-1. A linear extrapolation then has an intercept of roughly 0.3 eV at q_par = 0.
   - Fig. 2b, taken at q_par = 0.016 Å^-1, shows the low-energy feature at ~0.2-0.3 eV. For any v_F in the range quoted (1.4-2 eV Å), v_F q there is ~0.02-0.03 eV.
   - At q_par = 0.088 Å^-1 the measured peak is ~0.5 eV, so omega/q ~ 5-6 eV Å. That is much larger than the 1.4 eV Å "speed" quoted in the text, which must therefore be a slope with a large offset.
   - The chi0 points in Fig. 3e (triangles) extrapolate much closer to the origin, and the text acknowledges disagreement "at smaller q_par".

   A gapped, linearly dispersing mode with a ~0.3 eV offset fits a collective or interband excitation, or a resolution/elastic-tail artifact, at least as naturally as the Lindhard continuum. This is the central test of the headline claim, and the "quantitative agreement" in the abstract does not hold for it.

   The authors should:
   - fit omega(q_par) with a free intercept and report the intercept with its uncertainty;
   - show the low-q spectra (including the q_par = 0.016 Å^-1 data) with the elastic line and the fitted components;
   - explain how a q -> 0 intercept of order 0.3 eV is consistent with an intraband continuum, or narrow the claim accordingly.

   That BSE reproduces the offset (red squares) is not enough by itself. BSE includes core-hole and matrix-element effects, so its agreement with experiment does not show that the feature is the ground-state chi0 continuum.

2. The change in q_par is tied to changes in polarization projection, orbital weighting and q_perp, so the apparent dispersion may come partly from these changes. q_par is varied only by rotating theta_in at fixed 2theta.
   - This rotation also changes the projection of the incident polarization onto in-plane (sigma, p_x,p_y) versus out-of-plane (pi, p_z) orbitals. The authors use exactly this mechanism to explain the intensity suppression and the cos^4/sin^4 weights in the RPA.
   - The authors also report that the low-energy peak lies at higher energy in pi than in sigma polarization (Supplementary Fig. S5), and Fig. 4c shows pi-intraband weight at higher energy than sigma-intraband weight.
   - So the peak could shift upward towards grazing incidence because the pi/sigma weighting changes, even with no q dependence. The momentum labels in Fig. 3a also show that q_perp falls from 0.14 to 0.02 while q_par rises, so |q| is nearly constant (~0.15-0.16) across the series.

   The authors should separate the two effects:
   - Compare spectra at the same q_par taken with different theta_in. The 90 degree geometry at BESSY and the 150 degree geometry at ALS should make this possible.
   - Alternatively, compare spectra at the same theta_in, with the same polarization projection, but different q_par.
   - Fit the dispersion separately in sigma and pi incident polarization.

   Without such a control, the "dispersion" in Fig. 3a-b and 3e cannot be attributed to momentum alone.

3. The paper does not show that the low-energy feature is a Raman-like valence excitation rather than a feature produced by the core hole or near-threshold fluorescence. The feature is resonantly enhanced at a sharp pre-edge, excitonic-like resonance (Fig. 2a) in a metal.
   - In this regime, the core-hole potential is known to produce particle-hole shake-up excitations in the intermediate state (x-ray-edge-singularity physics), and these appear as a low-energy, asymmetric RIXS tail.
   - Fluorescence-like contributions near threshold can also give features whose energy depends on detuning rather than being fixed in energy loss.
   - The main text cites the incident-energy dependence (Supplementary Fig. S2) only for the features above 2 eV.

   The authors should:
   - show incident-energy maps across the pre-edge at fixed q for the low-energy feature, demonstrating constant energy loss;
   - discuss how much of the low-energy intensity in the OCEAN BSE calculation comes from core-hole shake-up and how much from the bare intraband channel (for example by switching off or reducing the core-hole potential).

   The projection of the BSE exciton density (Eq. 2, Fig. 2c) identifies which states take part in the absorption step. It does not on its own establish that the final state is an intraband sigma-sigma pair described by the ground-state chi0.

B. Should address

4. The Fermi velocities quoted, the energy scales in Fig. 4c and the measured slope do not agree with one another.
   - The text gives sigma-band Fermi velocities of "roughly 2 eV Å" and calls the measured 1.4 eV Å "around half" of the Fermi velocity. 1.4/2 is 0.7, and the abstract says "comparable".
   - In Fig. 4c (q_par = 0.088 Å^-1), the sigma-sigma intraband weight peaks at ~0.35 eV and ends near ~0.5 eV. This corresponds to velocities of ~4-6 eV Å.
   - The pi-pi intraband weight extends to ~1.5 eV, which implies ~15 eV Å. That is well above the pi-band Fermi velocities expected from DFT.

   The authors should:
   - state the in-plane v_F of each sheet in their TB model;
   - overlay the analytic continuum boundaries (omega = v_F q_par for each band) on Figs. 3b-d;
   - state the broadening and the q_perp used in Figs. 3d and 4c;
   - explain why the calculated continuum extends to these energies.

   This is needed before "quantitative agreement" can be judged.

5. The paper does not test the lineshape and width against the Lindhard form. A continuum differs from a mode in its lineshape and in a width that grows with q. For a 2D cylindrical sheet, Im chi0 ~ omega/sqrt(v_F^2 q^2 - omega^2) below omega = v_F q, with the weight concentrated just below a sharp edge.
   - The experimental peak positions come from asymmetric Voigt fits, and the text mentions only qualitatively that the feature broadens "towards grazing incidence".
   - The authors should fit, or at least compare, the measured spectra with the TB chi0 (or the sigma-intraband part) convolved with the 100 meV resolution, using a free amplitude and an elastic line.
   - They should report the fitted width against q_par, and show whether it scales linearly with q_par as the continuum predicts. Its direction of asymmetry should be compared with the Lindhard prediction.

   This would directly back the identification, which the peak position alone cannot do.

6. The acoustic-plasmon alternative is dismissed only qualitatively. MgB2 has carriers with two different Fermi velocities (sigma and pi), and an acoustic plasmon in such a system disperses linearly, with a velocity between the two Fermi velocities. That is the same phenomenology as the observed feature. The text dismisses this with "expected at a smaller energy range" and a pointer to Supplementary Section F.
   - The orbital RPA in Fig. 4a is the natural tool here, but the paper does not say whether its intensity maximum is a pole of the RPA response or a reweighted continuum.
   - The authors should show the eigenvalues of [1 - V chi0] (or the loss function) for their RPA model across the measured q range.
   - They should state where the acoustic-plasmon branch lies relative to the data, using the published ab initio values (refs. 15, 26) for comparison.

7. Several RPA choices are unexplained, and the RPA comparison depends on them.
   - V(q) = U/q^2 with "U = 2 eV Å" has inconsistent units for a 3D Coulomb kernel.
   - U is calibrated to the ~3 eV feature, which the paper itself describes as a Landau-damped collective excitation and which is anisotropic in a layered material.
   - The geometric weights cos^4 theta_in and sin^4 theta_in for the p_y and p_z channels are stated without derivation, and the cross-orbital terms are not discussed.
   - The full RPA intensity (Fig. 4a) is about ten times smaller than chi0 (Fig. 4b), and the reason is not discussed.

   The authors should give the kernel with units, derive or reference the polarization weights, and show how sensitive the Fig. 4a peak position is to U.

8. The film samples are hardly characterized, and reproducibility is not shown. The Methods give the growth method but not:
   - film thickness, substrate or orientation;
   - Tc, residual resistivity or X-ray diffraction;
   - how many films were measured.

   Surface condition matters especially at 190 eV, where the probing depth is tens of nm. MgB2 surfaces readily form MgO and boron oxides, and boron oxide has strong B K-edge features near 194 eV.

   The authors should also state which spectra come from which facility and sample (ALS at 77 K versus BESSY at 28 K), and whether the low-energy dispersion was reproduced on both instruments and on more than one film.

9. The word "conclusively" goes beyond what the decomposition in Fig. 4c can show. The intraband/interband split there is a property of the TB chi0. It does not test the measurement independently, and the interband sigma1-sigma2 channel near Gamma (light and heavy hole sigma bands) could give low-energy weight that depends on matrix elements the model leaves out. Polarization dependence, the measured lineshape (concern 5) and the q -> 0 behaviour (concern 1) are the experimental handles. The language should be tempered, or the claim tied explicitly to these tests.

10. The prior literature on the intraband continuum is incomplete. The introduction says no feature resembling the textbook continuum has been reported. However:
    - Electronic Raman scattering detects intraband particle-hole continua at q ~ 0 through non-conserved (unscreened) vertices, which is the same mechanism invoked here. It has been measured in MgB2 itself, around the superconducting gaps.
    - Non-resonant IXS on MgB2 (ref. 29) addressed single-particle versus collective excitations.

    The authors should discuss how the present observation differs: momentum resolution, and access to the dispersing edge rather than q ~ 0 weight.

11. The fitting procedure is under-described.
    - The paper does not state which parameters of the asymmetric Voigt are free.
    - It does not say how the elastic line and the background from the >2 eV features are modelled, or whether the Gaussian width is fixed to the resolution.
    - Error bars come only from the covariance matrix. Systematic uncertainty from the elastic-line model, which matters for peaks at 0.3-0.4 eV with 100 meV resolution, is not included.

    The authors should give the full fit model in the main text or Methods and show how stable the low-q peak positions are under reasonable changes to it.

C. Minor

12. The Methods say the incident energy was tuned to "the pi*-like resonance", but the Results assign the pre-edge feature mainly to planar p_x, p_y (sigma) hole states. Please make these consistent.

13. The momentum conventions need clarifying. The Methods say momenta are given in r.l.u., but the Fig. 3a labels such as (0.16, 0.02) seem to be in Å^-1, and the axes use Å^-1. The text says Gamma-M was measured "up to ≈ 0.17 Å^-1", but Fig. 3 stops at ~0.155. Please add a table giving, for each spectrum: theta_in, 2theta, photon energy, q_par, q_perp, facility and temperature. Also give the momentum resolution, and show x error bars in Fig. 3e.

14. The BESSY data were taken at 28 K, below the films' Tc. This is not important at 100 meV resolution, but it should be stated, and any spectra combined across temperatures should be identified.

15. Fig. 3e places the energy axis on the right without a left-hand scale, which makes the points hard to read. Please add a linear fit line with its parameters (slope and intercept) and the corresponding Lindhard edge.

16. The Gamma-K data said to show "no difference" from Gamma-M should be shown, at least in the Supplement, with peak positions overlaid on Fig. 3e.

17. In the Discussion, the statement that L_p-h "has no contribution to the first moment because it is kinematically constrained to omega <= v_F q" should be made quantitative: give the order in q of its contribution. The claim that the total charge response has a q^4 suppression (Eq. 4) should be checked against the measured or calculated chi in Fig. 4a.

18. The colour scales in Figs. 3b-d and 4a-b use different arbitrary units with different maxima. A common normalization, or line cuts with all calculations and the data on one plot, would make the comparison clearer.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a figure showing the low-q end explicitly: spectra at q_par = 0.016-0.06 Å^-1 with the elastic line, the fitted components, and the analytic omega = v_F q edges for the sigma and pi sheets, together with the intercept analysis.
2. Use both scattering geometries (90 and 150 degrees) to measure at least two pairs of spectra with the same q_par and different theta_in, and the reverse. This separates momentum dispersion from polarization and orbital reweighting.
3. Fit the data directly with the resolution-convolved TB chi0 (sigma- and pi-intraband parts with free amplitudes), and report the width against q. This would turn the qualitative agreement into a quantitative test of the Lindhard lineshape.
4. Show incident-energy maps of the low-energy feature across the pre-edge, and a BSE calculation with the core-hole potential reduced. Together these would separate the ground-state intraband continuum from core-hole shake-up.
