Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report a low-energy (about 0.3 to 0.6 eV), dispersive, asymmetric RIXS feature in MgB2 thin films, measured at the boron K pre-edge resonance (187 eV). They identify it as the intraband particle-hole (Lindhard) continuum, using three comparisons: OCEAN/BSE RIXS simulations, an eight-band tight-binding Lindhard function and an orbitally resolved RPA. They also argue from sum rules that orbital-selective RIXS, unlike EELS or non-resonant IXS, avoids the screening suppression of the intraband continuum at small q. If the assignment holds, the result is significant: RIXS would become a momentum-resolved probe of intraband charge dynamics, and the sum-rule argument is general. The weak point is the assignment itself. As written, the kinematics of the feature do not obviously match an intraband continuum, and the analysis does not rule out the main alternatives.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The measurement is new. Ultra-soft RIXS at the B K-edge reaches small momentum transfers (q below 0.2 Å^-1) with about 100 meV resolution, and the paper shows a clear, previously unreported low-energy feature that changes systematically with q_par (Fig. 3a, b).
- The theory is broad. Three different calculations (BSE RIXS, tight-binding χ0, orbital RPA) all reproduce a dispersing low-energy feature. The BSE projection (Fig. 2c) places the relevant transitions on the σ bands near the Γ–A Fermi surface.
- The sum-rule argument (Eqs. 1, 4, 5) is a clear, simple explanation of why the intraband continuum is nearly invisible in the screened longitudinal response yet can carry order-one weight in an orbital-projected response. It is likely to be useful beyond MgB2.
- The polarization dependence (Supplementary Fig. S5) and the agreement between Γ–M and Γ–K are useful checks, and the data are deposited publicly.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The energy and momentum of the feature are not shown to lie inside the intraband continuum, and the dispersion has a finite intercept. For an intraband continuum at small q, Im χ0 is bounded by roughly ω ≤ v_F·q and its peak should go to zero linearly as q → 0. The paper's own numbers do not fit this picture:
   - The text gives σ-band Fermi velocities of "roughly 2 eV Å".
   - At q_par ≈ 0.05 to 0.06 Å^-1 the experimental peak in Fig. 3e is at about 0.37 eV. That is ω/q ≈ 6 to 7 eV Å, three times the stated σ velocity.
   - In Fig. 2b (q_par = 0.016 Å^-1) the low-energy peak (dagger) is still near 0.3 eV, where a σ intraband edge would sit below about 0.05 eV.
   - Read from Fig. 3e, the dashed linear fit extrapolates to about 0.25 eV at q = 0, not to the origin.
   - In Fig. 4c (q_par = 0.088 Å^-1) the σσ intraband weight extends to about 0.5 eV and the ππ intraband weight to about 1.5 eV. These imply edge velocities of about 6 and 17 eV Å, inconsistent with the quoted σ velocity and with literature in-plane π velocities.

   One possible resolution is that the out-of-plane component q_perp matters. At 2θ = 150° and 187 eV, |Q| ≈ 0.18 Å^-1 is nearly fixed, so varying θ_in trades q_perp against q_par. Fig. 3a labels the spectra with (q_par, q_perp) pairs such as (0.05, 0.14), so q_perp is large exactly where q_par is small. If so, the "in-plane dispersion" is really a rotation of a fixed-length Q, and the σ-band picture (2D cylinders) and the slope-based velocity comparison need to be reworked. The headline identification depends on resolving this. The authors should:
   - give the full 3D Q (units stated) for every spectrum and for every χ0, RPA and BSE calculation;
   - state the in-plane and out-of-plane Fermi velocities of each band in their tight-binding model;
   - overlay the continuum boundaries ω = v_F·q for each band on Figs. 3b–d and 4c;
   - explain the finite intercept of the experimental dispersion. If possible, they should also use the 2θ = 90° BESSY data, where q_par and q_perp combine differently, to separate the two dependences.

2. Interband σ–σ transitions are excluded using the wrong response function. The paper's main argument is that RIXS measures an orbital-projected response (χ_{px px}, χ_{py py}, χ_{pz pz}), not the total density response (Eq. 5, Methods). Yet the decomposition that is said to "conclusively" exclude interband transitions (Fig. 4c) is a decomposition of the total charge χ0. In the total density vertex, transitions between the two nearly degenerate σ bands near Γ–A are suppressed at small q by band-orthogonality (coherence) factors. They are not suppressed in a px- or py-projected vertex, which is what a linearly polarized B 1s → 2p transition selects. Such σ1 → σ2 transitions near k_F have finite energy as q → 0, set by the σ-band splitting at the Fermi surface, which is a few hundred meV. They would naturally produce the finite intercept and weak dispersion noted in concern 1. The BSE projection in Fig. 2c places the weight on the σ bands, which fits either picture. The authors should repeat the Fig. 4c intra/inter decomposition for the orbital-projected χ0_aa and χRPA_aa that they argue RIXS measures, for both polarizations. If possible they should also decompose the BSE RIXS spectrum itself into intraband and interband final states. Without this, "conclusively demonstrates" (p. 4) is not supported.

B. Should address

3. A continuum has not been distinguished from a collective mode, and "quantitative agreement" is overstated. The paper compares only peak positions, extracted with a phenomenological asymmetric Voigt profile (Fig. 3e). A two-component metal like MgB2 has a predicted out-of-phase σ–π acoustic plasmon (refs. 15, 26), and the orbitally resolved RPA (Fig. 4a) visibly moves spectral weight relative to χ0 (Fig. 4b). Interactions therefore matter for exactly the feature under discussion. Supplementary Section F is cited to exclude the acoustic plasmon on energy grounds, but the main text gives no numbers. The tests that would distinguish a continuum from a mode are:
   - the lineshape: the low-ω onset (Im χ0 ∝ ω), a sharp upper edge, and a width that grows linearly with q;
   - the integrated intensity versus q, compared with the χ0 and RPA predictions.
   
   The authors should fit the spectra directly with resolution-convolved χ0_aa and χRPA_aa (with the elastic line as the only extra component), report width versus q, and state the predicted acoustic-plasmon dispersion in the main text. Fig. 3e also does not support "quantitative agreement" (abstract) for χ0. At q_par ≈ 0.055 Å^-1 the χ0 peak (about 0.25 eV) is about 0.1 eV below the data, and the text says the χ0 intensity peaks at larger q than in the experiment. The claim should either be backed by a lineshape-level comparison or toned down.

4. There is no incident-energy (Raman versus fluorescence) test of the low-energy feature. In a metal, a resonant feature near the elastic line can include fluorescence-like contributions whose loss energy follows the incident energy. Supplementary Fig. S2 is cited only for the features above 2 eV. The authors should show detuning-dependent spectra (for example 186.5 to 188 eV in small steps through the pre-edge peak) at a fixed q, showing that the 0.3 to 0.6 eV feature stays at constant loss and that its intensity follows the pre-edge resonance. The BSE detuning dependence should be shown for comparison.

5. Sample characterization, surface state and reproducibility are not documented. The main text describes the HPCVD growth only qualitatively. It does not give:
   - film thickness, substrate, c-axis orientation, Tc or RRR;
   - how many films were measured;
   - which datasets (ALS at 77 K or BESSY at 28 K) appear in Figs. 2 and 3;
   - whether the feature and its dispersion agree between the two instruments.
   
   MgB2 surfaces oxidize readily. B-oxide species have strong B K-edge resonances in the 192–194 eV region, where the TFY spectrum in Fig. 2a has structure the BSE does not fully reproduce. The authors should report the film parameters, compare TEY with TFY to assess surface contamination, and show the key dispersion from more than one film or from both beamlines.

6. The convergence of the BSE and RPA calculations at small q is not documented. To resolve q_par down to 0.05 Å^-1 (Γ–M ≈ 1.18 Å^-1), the BSE needs a dense k-mesh near the Fermi surface. The OCEAN k-mesh, the broadening and the screening parameters are not given in the main text. The BSE also uses SCAN wavefunctions, whose σ-band velocities may differ from those of the tight-binding model. For the RPA, V(q) = U/q² with U = 2 eV Å is dimensionally inconsistent as written, it is tuned to a 3 eV plasmon, and the orbital weights are a crude cos^4 θ_in / sin^4 θ_in. The authors should state the k-meshes and show convergence of the low-energy peak position with mesh. They should also correct the units of U and show how sensitive the Fig. 4a result is to U and to the orbital weighting.

7. The novelty and generality claims need qualifying. The intraband particle-hole continuum has been probed before by other means:
   - non-resonant IXS in simple metals beyond the plasmon cutoff;
   - electronic Raman scattering;
   - momentum-resolved EELS (M-EELS) on correlated metals;
   - ref. 24, which reports separating intraband from interband transitions in MgB2 with EELS.
   
   The statement that the continuum "has remained experimentally elusive" should be limited to the small-q, plasmon-dominated regime, and these works should be discussed. Likewise, the Discussion says RIXS "will generically capture the Lindhard continuum". Eq. 5 relies on there being at least two orbital channels and treats the RIXS cross-section as an orbital-projected χ, neglecting core-hole and intermediate-state effects. These assumptions should be stated, with a brief comment on when they fail, for example in single-orbital metals or away from the pre-edge resonance.

C. Minor

8. The velocity numbers are inconsistent with each other:
   - the abstract says the velocity is "comparable to the Fermi velocity";
   - the text gives 1.4 eV Å and calls it "around half" of a σ velocity it quotes as "roughly 2 eV Å", but 1.4/2 = 0.7;
   - the slope read from Fig. 3e looks closer to 2 eV Å.
   
   Please state how 1.4 eV Å was obtained (fit range, whether the intercept was free, uncertainty) and make the three statements agree.

9. The momentum labels in Fig. 3a are ambiguous. The units of the (q_par, q_perp) labels are not stated. The top label has q_par = 0.16, which is outside the Fig. 3e axis. The implied |Q| is not constant, as it should be at fixed 2θ. Please state the units and check the values against the scattering geometry.

10. The fitting is not described well enough. Please state which parameters of the asymmetric Voigt plus elastic model were free, how the elastic line and the high-energy background were constrained, and whether the peak position is stable against the choice of fit function. Error bars from the covariance matrix alone (Fig. 3e) underestimate systematic uncertainty from the elastic-tail subtraction.

11. Fig. 3d is broadened to the 100 meV resolution, but Figs. 4a and 4b are not, while the text compares both with the data. Please use the same broadening (or show both) so that the comparison of peak positions in Fig. 4 with Fig. 3e is like for like.

12. The Discussion says the continuum "has no contribution" to the f-sum at small q and that EELS "cannot see" intraband excitations. The q^4 argument gives strong suppression, not zero weight. Please phrase it that way and give an estimate of the relative weight for MgB2 parameters.

13. One dataset (28 K) is below the bulk Tc of MgB2 (about 39 K). The superconducting gap is irrelevant at 0.5 eV, but stating this, and confirming that spectra at 28 K and 77 K agree, would remove a reader's question.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a single figure that overlays the experimental peak positions and widths on the ω = v_F·q continuum boundaries of each band, computed for the full 3D Q of each spectrum. This would show directly whether the feature lies inside the intraband continuum.
2. Decompose the orbital-projected (px, py, pz) χ0 and χRPA into σσ-intra, σσ-inter (σ1 ↔ σ2), ππ and σπ parts for both polarizations. Show the decomposition next to the polarization-resolved data in Supplementary Fig. S5.
3. Replace peak-position fitting with direct lineshape fits to resolution-convolved model susceptibilities, and report width and integrated intensity versus q. This is the clearest way to tell a continuum from a Landau-damped collective mode.
4. Measure at a second scattering angle (the BESSY 90° geometry) at matching q_par values. This would separate the in-plane and out-of-plane momentum dependence and strengthen the link to the two-dimensional σ Fermi surface.
