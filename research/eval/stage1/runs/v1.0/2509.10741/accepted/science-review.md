A. Must address

1. The measured dispersion does not go to zero as q → 0, which an intraband continuum must do. For the Lindhard continuum at small q, Im χ0 is bounded by ω ≤ v_F·q, and for a cylindrical σ sheet its peak sits near ω ≈ v_F·q, so the feature must collapse linearly towards zero energy as q → 0. The paper's own data do not show this. In Fig. 3e the experimental peak is already at about 0.35-0.42 eV at q_par ≈ 0.05-0.06 Å^-1 and rises to about 0.6 eV at about 0.155 Å^-1; the dashed linear guide extrapolates to roughly 0.25-0.3 eV at q_par = 0. At q_par ≈ 0.05-0.06 Å^-1, ω/q ≈ 6-7 eV Å, about three times the quoted σ Fermi velocity ("roughly 2 eV Å"), whereas a σ continuum at q_par = 0.05 Å^-1 would extend only to about 0.1 eV. At q_par = 0.088 Å^-1 the measured peak is about 0.5 eV (ω/q ≈ 5-6 eV Å), so the quoted 1.4 eV Å "speed" must be a slope with a large offset. In Fig. 2b (q_par = 0.016 Å^-1) the low-energy feature (dagger) still sits at about 0.2-0.3 eV, where any in-plane σ intraband response (v_F q ≈ 0.02-0.05 eV) would lie inside the 100 meV elastic line. The tight-binding χ0 points in Fig. 3e (blue triangles) do extrapolate towards the origin, and the text concedes they deviate from experiment at smaller q_par ("split between different features"); only the BSE (red squares) reproduces the offset. That BSE agreement is not sufficient by itself, since BSE includes core-hole and matrix-element effects, so it does not show that the feature is the ground-state χ0 continuum. A gapped, linearly dispersing feature with a 0.25-0.3 eV offset fits a collective or interband excitation, or a resolution/elastic-tail artefact, at least as naturally as the Lindhard continuum, so "linear dispersion with velocity comparable to v_F" does not identify it, and the "quantitative agreement" in the abstract does not hold for this test. The authors should state explicitly what happens to the feature as q → 0; fit ω(q_par) with a free intercept (or with a physically motivated offset) and report the intercept with its uncertainty; show the low-q spectra, including the q_par = 0.016 Å^-1 data, with the elastic line and fitted components; explain the offset (out-of-plane momentum, band anisotropy, interactions, interband admixture; see concerns 2-4) or narrow the claim accordingly; and, if possible, add data at smaller total |Q|, for example at smaller 2θ.

2. The in-plane "dispersion" is confounded with changes in out-of-plane momentum and in polarization/orbital projection. q_par is varied only by rotating θ_in at fixed 2θ. At 2θ = 150° and 187 eV, |Q| ≈ 0.18 Å^-1 is nearly fixed, so the rotation trades q_perp against q_par: the Fig. 3a labels run from (0.05, 0.14) to (0.16, 0.02), so |q| is nearly constant (about 0.15-0.16) across the series and q_perp is largest exactly where q_par is smallest (including at q_par = 0.016 Å^-1, which bears on concern 1). The σ sheets are quasi-2D but the π sheets are 3D, and Fig. 4c assigns much of the low-energy weight to ππ intraband transitions, which disperse with q_perp. The same rotation also changes the projection of the incident polarization onto in-plane (σ, p_x,p_y) versus out-of-plane (π, p_z) orbitals, the very mechanism the authors use to explain the intensity suppression at grazing incidence and the cos^4/sin^4 weights in the RPA. Since the low-energy peak lies higher in π than in σ polarization (Supplementary Fig. S5) and Fig. 4c puts ππ intraband weight above σσ, the peak could shift upward towards grazing incidence purely through changing π/σ weighting, with no q dependence. If so, the "in-plane dispersion" is partly a rotation of a fixed-length Q, and the σ-band (2D cylinder) picture and slope-based velocity comparison must be reworked. The authors should give the full 3D Q (with units) for every spectrum and state whether every χ0, RPA and BSE calculation (Figs. 3c-e, 4) uses that full 3D q or only q_par along Γ-M; compare spectra at the same q_par taken at different θ_in, using the 2θ = 90° BESSY and 150° ALS geometries, and spectra at the same θ_in and polarization projection but different q_par; map the feature against q_perp at fixed q_par and vice versa; and fit the dispersion separately in σ and π incident polarization. Without such a control the dispersion in Figs. 3a, 3b and 3e cannot be attributed to in-plane momentum alone.

3. Interband σ1-σ2 transitions are excluded using the wrong response function, so "conclusively demonstrates" (p. 4) is not supported. The paper's central argument is that RIXS measures an orbital-projected response (χ_{px px}, χ_{py py}, χ_{pz pz}; Eq. 5, Methods), not the total density response. Yet the intra/inter decomposition said to "conclusively" exclude interband transitions (Fig. 4c) is a decomposition of the total charge χ0, a property of the tight-binding model rather than an independent test of the measurement. In the total-density vertex, transitions between the two nearly degenerate σ bands near Γ-A (light- and heavy-hole) are suppressed at small q by band-orthogonality (coherence) factors; in a p_x- or p_y-projected vertex, which is what a linearly polarized B 1s → 2p transition selects, they are not, and they may depend on matrix elements the model leaves out. Such σ1 → σ2 transitions near k_F have finite energy as q → 0, set by the σ-band splitting at the Fermi surface (a few hundred meV), and would naturally produce the finite intercept and weak dispersion of concern 1. The BSE projection in Fig. 2c, which places weight on the σ bands, fits either picture. The authors should repeat the Fig. 4c decomposition (σσ-intra, σ1↔σ2 inter, ππ, σπ) for the orbital-projected χ0_aa and χRPA_aa they argue RIXS measures, for both polarizations, shown next to the polarization-resolved data of Supplementary Fig. S5; if possible, decompose the BSE RIXS spectrum itself into intraband and interband final states; and either temper "conclusively" or tie the claim explicitly to the experimental handles (polarization dependence, lineshape, q → 0 behaviour).

4. The data may show an interaction-renormalized or collective response, including the predicted acoustic plasmon, rather than the non-interacting continuum claimed in the title and abstract. The abstract claims "quantitative agreement with simulations of the non-interacting charge susceptibility", but Fig. 3e shows χ0 failing at small q_par: at q_par ≈ 0.055 Å^-1 the χ0 peak (about 0.25 eV) lies about 0.1 eV below the data, and the text says the χ0 intensity peaks at larger q than in experiment. The calculation said to reproduce the data in energy and momentum is the interacting, orbitally weighted RPA (Fig. 4a), and comparing Fig. 4a with Fig. 4b, interactions visibly move spectral weight to higher energy for exactly this feature. MgB2 is a two-component (σ/π) metal whose predicted out-of-phase σ-π acoustic plasmon (refs. 15, 26) disperses linearly with a velocity between the two Fermi velocities, the same phenomenology as the observed feature; moreover, with the Coulomb interaction coupled to total density, the unscreened orbital-projected channel of Eq. 5 is exactly the out-of-phase channel that hosts this mode. The acoustic plasmon is dismissed only with "expected at a smaller energy range" and a pointer to Supplementary Section F, with no numbers in the main text, and the paper does not say whether the RPA intensity maximum is a pole of the response or a reweighted continuum. The authors should plot the RPA peak dispersion in Fig. 3e alongside χ0, BSE and experiment; show the eigenvalues of [1 − Vχ0] (or the loss function) of their RPA model across the measured q range to establish whether there is any pole or sharp mode in the measured window; state in the main text where the acoustic-plasmon branch falls in the same (q_par, q_perp) coordinates, compared with the published ab initio values (refs. 15, 26); and say what in the data distinguishes a continuum from a damped collective mode or a renormalized continuum (see concern 7). If the best description is an interaction-dressed, orbitally weighted response, the title and abstract should say so, and "quantitative agreement" should either be backed by a lineshape-level comparison or toned down.

5. The paper does not show that the low-energy feature is a Raman-like valence excitation rather than a fluorescence-like or core-hole-induced feature. It is resonantly enhanced at a sharp, excitonic-like pre-edge (Fig. 2a) in a metal. In this regime, fluorescence-like contributions near threshold can give features whose loss energy follows the incident energy, and the core-hole potential produces particle-hole shake-up in the intermediate state (x-ray-edge-singularity physics), which appears as a low-energy asymmetric RIXS tail. All low-q data are taken at one incident energy (187 eV), and the main text cites the incident-energy dependence (Supplementary Fig. S2) only for the features above 2 eV; it is unclear whether S2 covers the low-energy feature. The projection of the BSE exciton density (Eq. 2, Fig. 2c) identifies the states involved in the absorption step, not that the final state is an intraband σσ pair described by the ground-state χ0. The authors should show detuning-dependent spectra or incident-energy maps at fixed q across the pre-edge (for example 186.5 to 188 eV in small steps), demonstrating that the 0.3-0.6 eV feature stays at constant loss and that its intensity follows the pre-edge resonance, with the BSE detuning dependence for comparison; and quantify how much of the low-energy intensity in the OCEAN BSE comes from core-hole shake-up versus the bare intraband channel, for example by switching off or reducing the core-hole potential.

B. Should address

6. The quoted Fermi velocities, the measured slope and the energy scales of the calculated continuum do not agree with one another. The abstract says the velocity is "comparable to the Fermi velocity"; the text gives 1.4 eV Å and calls it "around half" of a σ velocity quoted as "roughly 2 eV Å", but 1.4/2 = 0.7, and the slope read from Fig. 3e looks closer to 2 eV Å. In Fig. 4c (q_par = 0.088 Å^-1) the σσ intraband weight peaks at about 0.35 eV and extends to about 0.5 eV, and the ππ intraband weight to about 1.5 eV, implying edge velocities of about 4-6 and 15-17 eV Å, inconsistent with the quoted σ velocity and with DFT/literature in-plane π velocities. Further, for a 2D cylindrical sheet Im χ0 at fixed q rises towards and peaks at the upper edge ω = v_F q, so a peak tracking about 0.7 v_F needs explaining (warping of the σ sheets, q_perp, matrix elements, convolution with the elastic tail). The authors should state how 1.4 eV Å was obtained (fit range, whether the intercept was free, uncertainty) and make the three statements agree; state the in-plane and out-of-plane Fermi velocities of each sheet in their tight-binding model along Γ-M and along c; overlay the analytic continuum boundaries ω = v_F q for each band, computed for the full 3D Q, on Figs. 3b-d and 4c together with the experimental peak positions and widths; state the broadening and q_perp used in Figs. 3d and 4c and explain why the calculated continuum extends to these energies; and track the upper edge or onset of the feature against q, a sharper test of the Lindhard bound than the peak maximum.

7. The lineshape, width and intensity have not been tested against the Lindhard form, and only peak positions are compared. Peak positions are extracted with a phenomenological asymmetric Voigt (Fig. 3e), and the broadening "towards grazing incidence" is mentioned only qualitatively; the evidence for agreement is otherwise visual comparison of colour maps (Figs. 3b-d, 4a,b). A continuum differs from a mode in its lineshape and width: for a 2D cylindrical sheet Im χ0 ∝ ω/sqrt(v_F² q² − ω²) below ω = v_F q, with a low-ω onset (Im χ0 ∝ ω), weight concentrated below a sharp upper edge, and a width that grows linearly with q. The authors should fit the spectra directly with resolution-convolved (100 meV) χ0_aa (or its σ- and π-intraband parts with free amplitudes) and χRPA_aa, with the elastic line as the only extra component; show lineshape overlays with residuals at several representative q_par (data against BSE, convolved χ0 and RPA); report the fitted width against q_par and whether it scales linearly; compare the direction of asymmetry with the Lindhard prediction; and compare the integrated intensity versus q with the χ0 and RPA predictions.

8. The σ versus π assignment is inconsistent across the paper. 9. The peak-extraction model and its uncertainties are under-described. The paper does not state which parameters of the asymmetric Voigt plus elastic model are free or fixed, whether the Gaussian width is fixed to the resolution, or how the elastic and quasi-elastic line and the high-energy background (the tail of the >2 eV / 3 eV features) were modelled (Supplementary Fig. S4). The feature lies only 0.25-0.5 eV above a 100 meV-resolution elastic line and is intrinsically asymmetric, so the "peak position" of a continuum depends on the background, elastic-line model and asymmetry parameter; error bars from the covariance matrix alone (Fig. 3e) underestimate this systematic uncertainty. The authors should give the full fit model in the main text or Methods, refit with alternative background, elastic-line and lineshape models to show the stability of the low-q peak positions, and include the systematic spread in the Fig. 3e error bars.

10. Sample characterization, surface state and reproducibility are not documented. The main text describes HPCVD growth only qualitatively, with no film thickness, substrate, c-axis orientation, Tc, residual resistivity (RRR), XRD or surface preparation, and does not say how many films were measured, which datasets (ALS at 77 K or BESSY at 28 K) appear in Figs. 2 and 3, or whether the feature and its dispersion agree between instruments. MgB2 surfaces readily form MgO and boron oxides/hydroxides, and B2O3 has a strong B K-edge π* resonance near 194 eV, where the TFY spectrum in Fig. 2a has structure (about 192-196 eV) that the BSE does not fully reproduce; this matters for RIXS at 1-2° grazing geometry, which is more surface-sensitive, even though the probing depth at 190 eV is tens of nm. The authors should report the film parameters, compare TEY with TFY to assess surface contamination and comment on the oxide, state which figure uses which sample, beamline and temperature, and show the key dispersion from more than one film and from both beamlines/temperatures, overlaid in one panel.

11. Several RPA model choices are unexplained or ad hoc, and the Fig. 4a comparison depends on them. V(q) = U/q² with "U = 2 eV Å" is dimensionally inconsistent for a 3D Coulomb kernel. U is calibrated to the ~3 eV feature, which the paper then itself interprets as a Landau-damped collective excitation, and which is anisotropic in a layered material. The geometric weights cos^4 θ_in and sin^4 θ_in for the p_y and p_z channels (called "sinusoidal factors" in the Fig. 4 caption) are stated without derivation, and cross-orbital terms are not discussed. The full RPA intensity (Fig. 4a) is about ten times smaller than χ0 (Fig. 4b), without explanation, and the tight-binding parameters are themselves fitted to DFT. The authors should define the kernel with correct units, derive the polarization weights from the actual scattering geometry and dipole matrix elements (or cite a derivation), explain the intensity ratio, and show how sensitive the Fig. 4a peak position is to U over a reasonable range and to the orbital weighting.

12. The convergence of the BSE and χ0 calculations at small q is not documented. Resolving q_par down to 0.05 Å^-1 (Γ-M ≈ 1.18 Å^-1) requires a dense k-mesh near the Fermi surface, but the OCEAN k-mesh, broadening and screening parameters are not given in the main text. The BSE also uses SCAN wavefunctions, whose σ-band velocities may differ from those of the tight-binding model used for χ0 and RPA. 13. The novelty claim and the generality of the sum-rule argument are stated more broadly than the paper supports. The statement that the continuum "has remained experimentally elusive" (and that no feature resembling the textbook continuum has been reported) ignores prior probes: non-resonant IXS in simple metals at q of order k_F and above, beyond the plasmon cutoff; ref. 29 (non-resonant IXS on MgB2), which treats the single-particle/collective crossover; electronic Raman scattering, which detects intraband continua at q ≈ 0 through non-conserved (unscreened) vertices, the same mechanism invoked here, and which has been measured in MgB2 around the superconducting gaps; momentum-resolved EELS (M-EELS) on correlated metals; and ref. 24, which reports separating intraband from interband transitions in MgB2 with EELS. The claim should be limited to the small-q (q ≪ k_F), plasmon-dominated regime, and the differences (momentum resolution, access to the dispersing edge rather than q ≈ 0 weight) discussed. Likewise, the Discussion's statement that RIXS "will generically capture the Lindhard continuum" rests on Eqs. 4-5, a two-band toy RPA with orbital-diagonal coupling that requires at least two orbital channels and treats the RIXS cross-section as an orbital-projected χ, neglecting core-hole and intermediate-state effects. The authors should state these assumptions and the conditions under which χ_aa keeps O(1) weight (including the ratio of orbital susceptibilities), comment on when they fail (for example single-orbital metals or away from the pre-edge resonance), and check that the full BSE RIXS calculation shows the same lack of screening, for example by comparing RIXS-weighted and total-density (EELS-like) BSE or RPA responses at the same q.
