## A. Must address

1. **Strain and other extrinsic symmetry lowering are not excluded as the source of the rotated l = 2 distortion.** The distortion invoked (Fig. 2e; p. 8) is a d-wave term η cos(2θ + φ) with φ = −π/6, a two-fold (nematic) deformation whose director lies 15° away from both the σv and σv' mirror lines. Any in-plane uniaxial or shear strain along a non-high-symmetry direction produces exactly this symmetry class: three unequal |q| along A–H_I, A–H_II and A–H_III and a cyclic "handedness". Possible sources include cleave-induced strain, mounting, differential thermal contraction against the sample plate, step or terrace gradients, local strain around Ti clusters, or a non-random Ti distribution. The effect is small (average 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg|, Supplementary Table 1), so modest strain combined with the large electronic nematic susceptibility reported for AV3Sb5 could account for it. The global affine transformation that forces the Bragg peaks onto a perfect hexagon (Methods; Figs. 2b, 4d) removes the lattice signature of a real strain by construction, but leaves its electronic response, which would appear as a mirror-breaking Fermi surface; the analysis may therefore discard the very evidence that would identify strain. Domains of opposite handedness (±15° director, Fig. 2g) are also what strain varying on a ~100 nm scale would produce, and the T-matrix argument (Supplementary Figs. 18–20) addresses defects but not strain. The authors should: report, for every map, the raw uncorrected Bragg vectors, the affine-correction parameters and the implied strain tensor (magnitude and principal-axis angle); test whether the sign and size of φ and η track the strain axis, including across the opposite-handedness domains of Fig. 2g; use DFT to estimate the strain needed to produce the observed η and compare it with the uncorrected lattice distortion; and report the director angle for several independently mounted crystals. A director pinned at ±π/6 in every domain, with a ~30° rotation across a domain boundary, would favour intrinsic order; a scattered angle would favour strain. A strain-tuning experiment would be stronger still.

2. **Measurement artifacts have not been excluded at the level needed for a percent-scale, few-pixel effect.** The distinction between nematic and chiral-nematic rests on q-differences of about 0.01–0.03 |Q_Bragg| between the A–H_m directions (Figs. 2c, 3c, d), comparable to the plotted error bars, which include a pixel term ε_pix. For the roughly 70–100 nm fields of view of Fig. 1d–g, one FT pixel is of order 0.5–1% of |Q_Bragg|, so the signal is a few pixels. A global affine correction removes only linear distortion; it cannot remove non-linear or spatially varying distortions within the field of view such as piezo creep, thermal drift along the slow-scan axis, residual non-orthogonality, or fast- versus slow-scan asymmetry. Each has a fixed orientation relative to the scan frame and can therefore produce an apparent handedness. Tip anisotropy (a multi-atom or asymmetric tip) convolves the LDOS with an anisotropic kernel, redistributing intensity within the QPI arcs and shifting apparent peak positions in line cuts, which could also distort the apparent "triangle tilt" of q3. The opposite-handedness region of Fig. 2g is the most persuasive evidence so far, but it lies "several hundred nanometres away" and was taken at a different setpoint (1000 pA, 0.3 mV lock-in, against 500 pA, 0.5 mV), and the paper does not say whether the same tip, scan angle, session and field-of-view size were used. The authors should: image the same area with the scan frame rotated (for example by 30°, 60° or 90°) and with opposite fast-scan directions, showing that φ stays locked to the lattice rather than the scan frame; repeat the analysis with a local (Lawler–Fujita-type) drift correction and show the q-vector asymmetries are unchanged; show the same handedness with independently prepared tips and the opposite handedness with the same tip; state how many tips were used; state the FOV, pixel number and q-pixel size for every map (including in the captions of Figs. 1, 2 and 4) and give the distortion in q-pixels; show that the inter-direction differences in Supplementary Table 1 exceed the combined uncertainty from fitting, pixelation and the affine-correction residual; and calibrate the systematic floor of the distortion metric on a quantity that should be symmetric, such as a reference surface measured with the same scanner and pipeline (for example the Au(111) surface-state ring or a known hexagonal metal), the inversion-related q1 ring of the α pocket, or the Bragg-peak shapes before correction. Ideally a domain boundary should be imaged within a single field of view with one tip, where tip and drift are common to both handednesses.

3. **The data do not yet show that all mirrors are broken, as opposed to an ordinary mirror-preserving nematic, and η and φ are chosen rather than fitted.** A nematic state that keeps one mirror makes two of the three A–H_m directions equivalent, so the chiral claim requires that all three pairwise differences in q1, q2, q3, d12, d13, d23 and the velocities are significant. The line profiles in Fig. 2c, d carry no error bars, and in Fig. 3c, d the error bars of the three directions overlap over much of the energy range. The phase φ = −π/6 appears to be imposed (p. 8: "used for our QPI data analysis"), the value of η used in the simulation of Fig. 2f is not given, and the agreement with the data is described as "very well captured" without any quantitative comparison of simulated and measured d12, d13, d23 or q_i(θ). The authors should give uncertainties for every q, test pairwise significance, and fit η and φ directly to the extracted q_i(E) along the three directions or to the full QPI map, stating the number of data points and free parameters and giving confidence intervals and residuals. They should show that φ is many standard deviations from every mirror-preserving value (see concern 6 for which values those are), and whether a pure nematic (φ = 0) plus uniform strain, or a C3-breaking term alone, fits equally well. φ (or the equivalent ordering of d12, d13, d23) should be reported for every dataset, doping level and domain (Supplementary Table 1, Supplementary Figs. 3–5, 16), with the result reproduced in independent fields of view and samples.

4. **The "parent state" claim and T* ≈ 167 K rest on no temperature-dependent data.** The title and abstract present the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid as the parent of the chiral CDW and of superconductivity, emerging at T* above T_CDW, and Fig. 4f draws a T* line above T_CDW at all doping levels. Yet all QPI is at 0.4 K, plus a 4 K comparison around Tc; Ti doping is used as a stand-in for temperature. T* ≈ 167 K is obtained by converting a mean energy splitting of 14.4 meV, derived at x = 0.15 from the δ-pocket dispersions through the renormalized velocities, directly into a temperature (apparently E/kB with no prefactor). No justification is given for equating a band-splitting scale with a transition temperature; mean-field ratios alone change the estimate by a factor of order unity. The result is then compared with T_CDW ≈ 90 K of the undoped compound, mixing compositions, and with the 130 K torque anomaly of ref. 28, which concerns undoped samples, a bulk probe, and is numerically inconsistent with 167 K. The resistivity shows no anomaly at T* (p. 8). Suppressing the CDW by doping does not show that the state found at x = 0.15 and 0.4 K exists above T_CDW in the undoped compound. The authors should measure the q1/q2/q3 anisotropy at x = 0.15 from 0.4 K to elevated temperatures (for example 20, 50 and 100 K, ideally above 100 K) and show where it vanishes, or measure QPI on x = 0 between T_CDW and the claimed T*, or use a bulk probe on the same x = 0.15 crystals that is sensitive to rotation of the principal axes away from the mirrors (polarization-resolved optical birefringence, elastoresistance, or polarized Raman mixing of the Eg channels). They should give the formula and assumptions behind the 14.4 meV to 167 K conversion. If no data are added, the title, abstract, T* estimate and statements such as "already present above the CDW transition" should present the parent-state relationship as a hypothesis.

5. **The causal link between the chiral-nematic QPI and the CDW chirality at small x is asserted, not demonstrated.** At x = 0.05 the paper shows mirror-breaking QPI (Fig. 4c–e) coexisting with 2a0 CDW peaks whose intensity ratio is 2.6 : 1.2 : 1.0. For the claim that the CDW-triggered states descend from the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid, the CDW handedness should be locked to the QPI handedness; coexistence alone does not establish parentage. The authors should show, across several domains of each handedness at x = 0 or 0.05, that the sense of the CDW intensity ratio flips together with the QPI chirality, using intensity ratios shown to be robust to tip and setpoint (see concern 18).

## B. Should address

6. **The symmetry analysis of the order parameter is incomplete, and the Methods contain errors.** A single two-component l = 2 (E2g) nematic order parameter on a hexagonal lattice has an allowed cubic (Z3) Landau term ∝ cos 3α that pins the director to σv or σv', preserving one mirror. A director at an intermediate angle, such as φ = −π/6 (principal axis at 15°, halfway between the mirror types), therefore needs either a second, independent order parameter that breaks all vertical mirrors (A2g-like; the authors propose intra-unit-cell sublattice charge order, Eqs. 2–5) or an external symmetry-breaking field such as strain (concern 1). The paper should say which is assumed, what sets the 15° offset, and what each predicts. In addition, the Methods state that mirror symmetry survives only for φ = 0, ±π, but cos(2θ + φ) with φ = ±π/3 or ±2π/3 is symmetric about a σv' or σv line; with Eqs. (3)–(5), φ = π/3 gives n1 = n3 ≠ n2, so mirror-preserving configurations occur at every φ = nπ/3. The statement that for φ = ±π/2 "the nematic component vanishes and the charge order becomes purely chiral" is inconsistent, since n1 = n0 ≠ n2 ≠ n3 breaks C3: within this parametrization any δn ≠ 0 is nematic and φ only sets the angle between the director and the mirrors. The authors should correct these statements and restate the claim as an E2g director at a non-symmetric angle together with whatever additional order parameter is required.
Raised by: r1, r3

7. **Sample statistics, reproducibility and domain structure are not reported.** The paper says the chirality is reproducible across doping levels, energies and terminations (Discussion; Supplementary Figs. 3–7, 14, 16), but does not state how many crystals, cleaves, tips and fields of view were measured at each x, how often each handedness was seen, whether any region showed no distortion, or how large the domains are. Fig. 4g shows the handedness changing sign between compositions, consistent with domains, but the domain picture rests on one other region several hundred nanometres away (Fig. 2g, at a different setpoint). The authors should give a table listing, for each map, x, sample, termination, tip, scan angle, setpoint, field of view, φ with uncertainty (or the ordering of d12/d13/d23 or of |q2| along the three directions) and handedness. Large-area or multi-FOV mapping showing the domain size and the nature of a domain wall, ideally with the director rotating by about 30° rather than an arbitrary angle, would substantially strengthen the claim of a spontaneous electronic order.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **Dopant-induced or structural symmetry breaking is not excluded, and sample characterization is thin.** The proposed microscopic origin is unequal charge on the three V sublattices (Supplementary Fig. 10b), and Ti substitutes on exactly these sites. Preferential sublattice occupation, short-range Ti ordering, directional clustering, or Ti alignment along a step or growth direction would produce the same symmetry breaking structurally. The T-matrix simulations (Supplementary Figs. 18–20) treat random point defects with "representative" strong-scattering parameters, and the statement that there is "no dominant or correlated defect population" (Supplementary Fig. 13) is qualitative. The authors should tabulate the sublattice assignment of the Ti features in the topographs (for example Fig. 1d) for each FOV and test whether occupancy is equal across sublattices; quantify the Ti pair-correlation function and orientational distribution in the topographs used for the QPI maps; state the range of scattering strengths and sublattice choices explored in the T-matrix study; and report whether diffraction on x = 0.15 crystals shows any monoclinic or orthorhombic distortion. Because the Sb surface is prepared by sweeping Cs adatoms away with the tip (Methods), they should show that the residual Cs arrangement has no preferred orientation correlated with φ. The Ti concentrations are nominal or cited (ref. 29); the measured composition of the crystals used, the local Ti density for each map, and transport or susceptibility confirming the absence of CDW and the Tc at x = 0.15 should be given.

9. **Whether the effect is a surface or bulk property is not addressed.** STM probes the Sb- and Cs-terminated surfaces of a crystal cleaved at 13 K. Surface reconstruction, incomplete Cs coverage and subsurface Ti layers can all lower the local symmetry, yet the paper claims a bulk Fermi-liquid phase underlying the bulk CDW. The Cs-terminated data (Supplementary Fig. 14) help, but the authors should state whether the handedness is the same on the two terminations in the same region, discuss surface effects explicitly, and either add a bulk probe at x = 0.15 (torque magnetometry, elastoresistance, polarization-resolved optics, or comparison with the bulk measurements of ref. 28 on doped samples) or argue that the surface layer reflects the bulk symmetry.

10. **The proposed q = 0 sublattice charge order is not looked for directly.** A modulation with n1 ≠ n2 ≠ n3 (Eqs. 2–5) is a q = 0 order and should be visible as an anisotropy of the Bragg-peak intensities in topography and dI/dV at x = 0.15, or as inequivalent V-site contrast in real-space images. The authors should report the Bragg-peak intensity ratios, analogous to the CDW peak ratio of 2.6 : 1.2 : 1.0 quoted for x = 0.05 (Fig. 4c), and correlate their sense with the QPI handedness.

11. **The direction-dependent velocities and mass renormalizations are less robust than their presentation suggests.** The text and Supplementary Table 2 quote v_{1,F} = (4583.84, 5107.30, 4121.17) meV/|Q_Bragg| and v_DFT = 6074.72 to six significant figures with no uncertainties, fit window or fit quality, although the chirality argument uses the orderings of these values. The α-pocket spread is about 24% and the δ-pocket masses range from 1.45 to 2.33 (for example 1.53, 1.95, 2.33), a 60% spread; a Pomeranchuk deformation with η of about 2% of |Q_Bragg| changes Fermi-surface shape but would not normally produce 25–60% velocity anisotropy. The α-band ordering (v^II > v^I > v^III) has the opposite cyclic sense to the δ-band ordering (v^I > v^II > v^III), which is not commented on although both are offered as evidence of one handedness. For the δ pockets, q2 and q3 connect inequivalent pockets not related by inversion, so v_F ≈ 2 v_s is justified only "to leading order in the chiral-nematic distortion" (p. 6), yet the resulting direction dependence of m*/m is of the same order as the distortion. The authors should state the fitting windows used to avoid the kinks and the sensitivity of the velocities to them; give uncertainties on all v_s, v_F and m*/m values and the significance of inter-direction differences; round to meaningful precision and give velocities in physical units (meV·Å); quantify the error of the v_F ≈ 2 v_s approximation, for example by computing dq/dE from the distorted model Fermi surface of Fig. 2e; explain the opposite cyclic orderings; and discuss whether a single cos(2θ + φ) deformation can produce anisotropies of this size. If it cannot, the velocity anisotropy should not be presented as independent confirmation of the same order parameter.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

12. **The chiral-nematic superconducting state is overstated.** The claim that superconductivity emerges directly from the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid with the same handedness (Abstract; Discussion; Supplementary Figs. 8, 9, 17) rests on the qualitative resemblance of subgap Bogoliubov QPI to the normal-state pattern. This shows that the paired quasiparticles live on a distorted Fermi surface, as expected for an anisotropic band structure of any origin, but not that the superconducting order parameter itself breaks mirror symmetry or any constraint on pairing symmetry. Chirality is said to be seen "both below (0.4 K) and above (4 K)" Tc = 3.5 K, but 4 K is barely above Tc, and the gap at x = 0.15 is a fraction of a meV while the electron temperature is 650 mK and lock-in modulation in the main-text maps is 0.3–0.5 mV. The authors should report the modulation and electron temperature for the B-QPI data and show that the subgap maps are dominated by Bogoliubov quasiparticles. Stronger support would come from a same-area, same-tip comparison of subgap B-QPI against normal-state QPI with superconductivity suppressed by a field above Hc2 (the 11 T magnet is available), with a quantitative comparison of q-vector misalignments; from gap anisotropy Δ(k) along mirror-related directions; from a field- or temperature-dependent change in B-QPI distinct from the normal state; or from vortex-core shapes with the same handedness. Otherwise the claim should read "the normal-state anisotropy persists into the superconducting state".

13. **"Chirality" is used inconsistently and is defined only late.** Only in the Discussion (p. 8) is "chiral" said to mean the breaking of all vertical mirrors without time-reversal breaking. The proposed state preserves inversion (Methods), and combined with the in-plane mirror σh, which the measurement does not test, its point group is at most C2h, achiral in three dimensions; the handedness is a planar property of an in-plane director at a non-high-symmetry angle. The abstract and introduction, however, link the state to the "chiral CDW" (refs. 19, 20, 24), whose proposed handedness is different and is associated with TRS breaking and field-switchable handedness, and speak of "chiral superconductivity", which most readers will take to mean TRSB pairing. The authors should define the term in the abstract and introduction, state the full point group of the proposed state, and explain how it would connect to the 3D chirality and TRSB claimed for the CDW phase. Testing whether the QPI handedness responds to out-of-plane field training with the available 11 T magnet would bear directly on the link to the field-tunable CDW chirality.

14. **Normalization and setpoint treatment differ between figures and may affect the q-vectors.** Fig. 2 uses raw dI/dV maps while Fig. 3 uses Feenstra-normalized L = (dI/dV)/(I/V) (Eq. 1), and Figs. 2a and 2g are at different currents. The normalization is ill-conditioned near E = 0, exactly where the red arrows in Fig. 3a highlight the direction dependence, and setpoint effects can shift QPI intensity in an energy-dependent way. The authors should show that the q-vector anisotropy (for example at −10 mV) and the extracted q(E), including the kinks and near-E_F differences, are the same in raw dI/dV, in L(r, E), and at two setpoints.

15. **The DFT reference and QPI simulation rely on approximations whose effect on percent-level q-shifts is not assessed.** DFT uses the virtual-crystal approximation without spin–orbit coupling at fixed kz = π, and the simulated QPI is a JDOS autocorrelation of a single kz = π contour with no matrix elements or tunnelling form factor. A Ti-induced shift of the chemical potential moves the δ band relative to the van Hove points near M and L, changing v_DFT, and kz dispersion projected onto the surface could produce direction-dependent apparent q-vectors. The authors should discuss whether kz projection or the doping-induced Fermi-level shift could mimic the anisotropy, and state the expected size of SOC-induced changes near the δ pockets and the L point (Fig. 3b) relative to the 1–2% distortions being interpreted.
