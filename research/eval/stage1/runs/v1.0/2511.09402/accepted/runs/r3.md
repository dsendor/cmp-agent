Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use spectroscopic-imaging STM on Ti-doped CsV3Sb5. At x = 0.15 no long-range CDW is present, yet the quasiparticle interference (QPI) patterns break all in-plane mirror and rotational symmetries. The authors read this as a "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid": a parent state from which the chiral CDW and the superconducting states of AV3Sb5 descend. If correct, it would be an important result for the kagome field, because it would place the mirror-symmetry breaking of the CDW phase in a translation-invariant electronic instability (a phase-shifted d-wave Pomeranchuk distortion). The QPI data are of high quality and the observation is interesting. But the size of the claimed symmetry breaking (about 1.4 to 2.2% of |Q_Bragg|) is small, the data come from one temperature, and the term "parent state" implies a temperature hierarchy that the measurements do not test. So the central claim currently rests more on interpretation than on direct evidence.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The QPI maps are clean and high-resolution at x = 0.15 (Figs. 1f,g and 2a). The q1, q2 and q3 channels are assigned to the Sb pz α pocket and the in-plane V d δ pockets in a way that is consistent with the authors' earlier orbital-resolved work.
- The analysis goes beyond a single map. It includes energy-resolved dispersions along the three A–H directions (Fig. 3) and direction-dependent velocity and mass-renormalization ratios (Supplementary Table 2). A region with the opposite handedness is shown (Fig. 2g). The same phenomenology is followed across x = 0, 0.04, 0.05, 0.15 and 0.27, on Sb- and Cs-terminated surfaces, and into the superconducting state through Bogoliubov QPI.
- A simple phenomenological model, k_F(θ) = k_F^0(θ) + η cos(2θ + φ) with φ ≠ 0, ±π, reproduces the qualitative features of the distorted QPI (Fig. 2e,f). The sublattice charge-density picture (Eqs. 2–5) gives a concrete microscopic hypothesis that can be tested.
- The paper tries to exclude defect-induced anisotropy with T-matrix simulations (Supplementary Figs. 18–20).
- The raw data are deposited publicly.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The paper does not rule out uniaxial strain or another extrinsic anisotropy at a generic angle as the source of the "chiral-nematic" signal. The proposed distortion, η cos(2θ + φ) with φ = −π/6, is a two-fold (nematic) deformation whose director is rotated 15° away from both the σ_v and σ_v' mirror lines. Any uniaxial strain whose axis does not lie along a high-symmetry direction produces exactly this symmetry class: three unequal |q| values along A–H_I, A–H_II and A–H_III, and a cyclic "handedness". This could be cleave-induced strain, strain from differential thermal contraction against the sample holder, or local strain around Ti clusters. The affine correction applied to the Bragg peaks (Methods, Figs. 2b and 4d) removes the lattice distortion from the image, but not the electronic response to a real lattice strain. Because the effect is only 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg| (Supplementary Table 1), a modest strain combined with the large electronic nematic susceptibility reported for AV3Sb5 could account for it. The authors should:
   - report the raw (uncorrected) Bragg-peak positions and the lattice strain tensor extracted from the topographs in every map used, including its magnitude and principal-axis angle;
   - show whether the direction of the QPI distortion tracks the strain axis or is independent of it;
   - compare the domains of opposite handedness (Fig. 2g) and test whether the handedness correlates with local strain.

   A strain-tuning experiment, or at least maps from several independently mounted crystals with the director angle reported for each, would make the intrinsic interpretation much stronger.

2. Measurement artifacts have not been excluded at the level needed for a percent-scale effect. The distinction between "nematic" and "chiral-nematic" rests on differences of about 0.01–0.02 |Q_Bragg| in peak positions (Figs. 2c and 3c,d). These are close to the stated pixel resolution, which enters the error bars in Fig. 3. Several things could produce a small distortion that the affine correction leaves behind: non-linear piezo drift or creep, slow-scan versus fast-scan asymmetry, and tip anisotropy, which redistributes intensity within the QPI arcs and could distort the apparent "triangle tilt" of q3. The authors should:
   - show that the sense and size of the distortion stay the same when the scan frame is rotated relative to the lattice, for example by 60° and 90°, in the same area with the same tip;
   - show that the distortion survives a deliberate tip change;
   - ideally, show both handedness domains within a single map with one tip, rather than in maps taken "several hundred nanometres away" (Fig. 2g), so that tip and drift are common to both.

   They should also state the FT pixel size in units of |Q_Bragg| and the field of view for each map, and show that the inter-direction differences in Supplementary Table 1 exceed the combined uncertainty from fitting, pixelation and the residual of the affine correction.

3. The paper does not demonstrate a "parent state" above T_CDW; the temperature hierarchy is inferred. The title and abstract call this the parent state of the chiral CDW, and the phase diagram (Fig. 4f) draws a T* line above T_CDW at all doping levels. However, all normal-state QPI data are taken at 0.4 K, plus 4 K in the superconducting comparison. Ti doping is used as a stand-in for temperature. The estimate T* ≈ 167 K comes from converting an average energy splitting of 14.4 meV to a temperature (Discussion). This conversion assumes an unstated ratio between the order-parameter energy scale and the transition temperature, and has no independent support. Doping suppresses the CDW, but that does not show that the state found at x = 0.15 and T = 0.4 K exists above T_CDW in the undoped compound. The authors should either:
   - measure the temperature dependence of the q1/q2/q3 anisotropy at x = 0.15 up to, for example, 20–100 K, and show that it persists or vanishes at a characteristic temperature; or
   - substantially soften "parent state", the T* estimate and the T* line in Fig. 4f, presenting them as hypotheses.

   The comparison with the 130 K torque anomaly (ref. 28) concerns undoped samples and a bulk probe. It does not directly support a surface QPI observation at x = 0.15.

B. Should address

4. The direction-dependent velocities and masses are hard to reconcile with a distortion of only about 2% of k_F. The text and Supplementary Table 2 give v_{1,F} = (4583.84, 5107.30, 4121.17) meV·|Q_Bragg|⁻¹. This is about a 24% spread across directions for the α pocket. The δ-pocket masses range from 1.45 to 2.33, a 60% spread. A Pomeranchuk deformation with η of about 2% of |Q_Bragg| changes Fermi-surface shape, but would not normally produce a 25–60% anisotropy in Fermi velocity. In addition:
   - The ordering of the α-band velocities (v^II > v^I > v^III) has the opposite cyclic sense to the δ-band ordering (v^I > v^II > v^III). The paper does not comment on this, although both orderings are offered as evidence of a single handedness.
   - For the δ pockets, the relation v_F ≈ 2 v_s is asserted "to leading order". But q2 and q3 connect inequivalent pockets that are not related by inversion, so the velocities at the two ends of the scattering vector need not be equal.

   The authors should give uncertainties on all v_s and m*/m values (from the linear fits in Fig. 3c,d), with the energy window used. They should justify the v_F ≈ 2 v_s approximation for inter-pocket scattering, explain the opposite cyclic orderings, and discuss whether a single cos(2θ + φ) deformation can produce velocity anisotropies of this size. If it cannot, velocity anisotropy should not be presented as independent confirmation of the same order parameter.

5. The model comparison is qualitative, and its parameters are not stated. The simulated QPI (Fig. 2f) uses DFT contours at k_z = π, computed in the virtual-crystal approximation without spin-orbit coupling (Methods), with η and φ chosen by hand. The text calls the agreement "very well captured". However:
   - the value of η used is not given;
   - φ = −π/6 is not obtained from a fit;
   - no quantitative comparison of simulated and measured d12, d13, d23 or q_i(θ) is shown.

   The authors should fit η and φ to the measured q_i along the three directions, with the number of data points and free parameters stated, and report the uncertainties. They should also state whether a pure nematic (φ = 0) plus uniform strain, or a C3-breaking term, fits equally well. That would show that the phase twist is required by the data rather than chosen.

6. There is no symmetry argument for why the nematic director is pinned at a generic angle. In a hexagonal lattice, the Landau free energy of a two-component E2g nematic order parameter contains a cubic (Z3) anisotropy term. That term pins the director to high-symmetry directions, which preserve one mirror plane. A director at a generic angle such as φ = −π/6 needs either a second order parameter (the authors propose intra-unit-cell sublattice charge order, Eqs. 2–5) or an external field such as strain (see concern 1). Two points should be discussed explicitly:
   - What sets the 15° offset, and is the same φ found in all doping levels and domains? Supplementary Table 1 and Figs. 16, 3–5 should report φ, or the equivalent ordering of d12, d13 and d23, for every dataset.
   - The proposed sublattice charge order (n1 ≠ n2 ≠ n3) is a q = 0 modulation and should be directly visible. It should appear as an anisotropy of the Bragg-peak intensities in topography and dI/dV at x = 0.15, or as inequivalent V-site contrast in real-space images. Please report the Bragg-peak intensity ratios, analogous to the CDW peak ratio of 2.6:1.2:1.0 quoted for x = 0.05 (Fig. 4c).

7. The statistics on handedness and reproducibility are not reported. The paper states that the chirality is reproducible across doping levels, energies and terminations (Discussion; Supplementary Figs. 3–7, 14, 16). It does not state:
   - how many crystals, cleaves, tips and fields of view were measured at each x;
   - how many showed each handedness;
   - whether any region showed no distortion.

   A table listing, for each map, x, termination, setpoint, field of view, the extracted ordering of d12/d13/d23 or of |q2| along the three directions, and the handedness would let the reader judge whether this is a robust bulk-derived property or depends on the region. The domain size and the nature of the boundary between opposite-handedness regions would also bear directly on the order-parameter interpretation.

8. Whether the effect is surface or bulk is not addressed. STM probes the Sb-terminated (and Cs-terminated) surface of a crystal cleaved at 13 K. Surface reconstructions, incomplete Cs coverage and subsurface Ti layers can all lower the local symmetry. The claim of a bulk "Fermi liquid" phase that underlies the bulk CDW therefore needs either a bulk probe at x = 0.15 or an argument that the surface layer reflects the bulk symmetry. Examples of bulk probes are torque magnetometry, elastoresistance, polarization-resolved optics, or a comparison with the bulk measurements of ref. 28 on doped samples. The Cs-terminated data (Supplementary Fig. 14) help. The authors should state whether the handedness is the same on the two terminations in the same region, and discuss surface effects explicitly.

9. The defect-based explanation is excluded only for the configurations simulated. The T-matrix calculations (Supplementary Figs. 18–20) use "representative" strong-scattering parameters. The statement that no correlated defect population is visible (Supplementary Fig. 13) is qualitative. Ti substitution is random only on average; short-range ordering, or Ti alignment along a step or growth direction, could select a preferred orientation. The authors should quantify the Ti-dopant pair correlation function and orientational distribution in the topographs used for the QPI maps. They should also state the range of scattering strengths and sublattice choices explored in the T-matrix study.

10. The claim of a chiral-nematic superconducting state rests on limited evidence. The direct transition into a "chiral-nematic SC" (Abstract; Discussion; Supplementary Figs. 8, 9, 17) is based on Bogoliubov QPI at subgap energies that "closely resembles" the normal-state QPI. This shows that the normal-state anisotropy survives below Tc, which would be expected if the band structure is anisotropic for any reason. It does not show that the superconducting order parameter itself breaks mirror symmetry. To support the claim, the authors should show:
    - a gap anisotropy (Δ(k) along mirror-related directions) or a field- or temperature-dependent change in the Bogoliubov QPI that is distinct from the normal-state QPI;
    - vortex-core shapes, if available, that reflect the same handedness.

    Otherwise the superconducting claim should be phrased as "the chiral-nematic normal-state anisotropy persists into the SC state".

11. Consistency with the authors' earlier QPI study at x = 0.15 is not discussed. Ref. 22 (Huang et al., PRL 134, 56001) by the same group reported orbital-resolved QPI at the same composition. The authors should state whether the mirror-symmetry breaking is visible in those earlier data, and if it was not reported, why: resolution, field of view, or different samples. This affects both the novelty and the reproducibility of the effect.

C. Minor

12. The phrase "chirality" for in-plane mirror breaking without time-reversal breaking needs a precise definition. The Discussion clarifies that chirality here means the breaking of all mirrors and does not imply TRSB. The abstract and introduction, however, link it to the TRSB "chiral CDW" and "chiral superconductivity" literature. A one-sentence definition early in the paper, noting that a nematic director at a non-high-symmetry angle is enough, would prevent misreading.

13. Figure 4f is a schematic, but it looks like a measured phase diagram. The flat T* line, the "nematic order" region and the "mirror-breaking SC" region are not based on data in this paper. Please label the figure as schematic and mark which boundaries are measured (Fig. 1b, ref. 29) and which are conjectured.

14. The Feenstra normalization (Eq. 1) is used to remove setpoint effects. Its use in the dispersion analysis near E = 0, where the red arrows in Fig. 3a highlight the differences, should be checked against unnormalized dI/dV FTs, so that the "kink" features and the near-E_F differences are not normalization artifacts.

15. Methods states that line profiles are averaged over six neighbouring lines. Please give the angular width this corresponds to in q-space, and confirm that the averaging window does not straddle the q1/q2 overlap region along A–H_I (Fig. 2c), where the separation is the key observable.

16. The DFT uses VCA without SOC, at fixed k_z = π. The paper should state the expected size of the SOC-induced changes near the δ pockets and the L point (Fig. 3b), compared with the 1–2% distortions being interpreted.

17. The temperature estimate "T* ~ 167 K" should state the conversion used. It appears to be 14.4 meV divided by k_B, with no prefactor.

18. Several symbols need defining in the main text: v_{i,s} versus v_{i,F}, and the "energy splitting" of −14.4 meV (sign and definition). The intensity ratio 2.6:1.2:1.0 in Fig. 4c should include uncertainties.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Run the decisive symmetry controls in a single session:
   - rotate the scan frame;
   - change the tip;
   - image a boundary between opposite-handedness domains in one field of view;
   - report raw lattice strain and director angle for every map.

   Together these would separate an intrinsic order parameter from strain and instrument effects.
2. Use the 11 T magnet and variable temperature already available. Track the anisotropy at x = 0.15 as a function of temperature, which tests the "parent state" claim and T*. Track it as a function of out-of-plane field, which tests whether the handedness can be trained or switched as reported for the chiral CDW.
3. Fit the phenomenological model quantitatively, with η and φ as free parameters, to all datasets, and report φ for every doping level and domain. A universal φ would strongly support an intrinsic electronic origin. A scattered φ would point to extrinsic pinning.
4. Look directly for the proposed q = 0 sublattice charge order: inequivalent Bragg-peak intensities and V-site contrast at x = 0.15. Correlate its sense with the QPI handedness.
