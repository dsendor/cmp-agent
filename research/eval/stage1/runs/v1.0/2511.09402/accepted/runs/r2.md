Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use spectroscopic-imaging STM on Ti-doped CsV3Sb5. At x = 0.15 the long-range CDW is absent. They report that the low-energy QPI patterns (q1 from the Sb pz alpha pocket, q2 and q3 from the V d delta pockets) break all in-plane mirror symmetries as well as C6 rotation. They interpret this as a "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid" and propose it as the parent state from which the chiral CDW (small x) and the superconducting states inherit their handedness. If the result holds, it would reframe the origin of chirality in AV3Sb5. It would move the symmetry breaking from the CDW to a translation-invariant, Pomeranchuk-type quadrupolar instability, which is a significant claim. The central observation, however, is a percent-level anisotropy in q-space, and its interpretation as a bulk, spontaneous electronic order with a high onset temperature needs stronger support than the paper gives.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The paper reaches a regime the field has not yet examined: a CDW-free kagome metal whose orbital-resolved QPI can be compared directly with the CDW phase. QPI branches are tracked across x = 0, 0.04, 0.05, 0.15 and 0.27, and on Sb- and Cs-terminated surfaces.
- The symmetry analysis is careful and visual. It uses side-by-side comparisons after 60 and 120 degree rotations (Fig. 2b, 4d), line cuts in two independent directions (cut#1 and cut#2; Fig. 2c, d), and energy-resolved dispersions along the three A–H directions (Fig. 3).
- A region of opposite chirality is reported a few hundred nm away (Fig. 2g, Supplementary Fig. 1). This is the right kind of evidence that the handedness is a property of the sample and not of the measurement.
- A simple, falsifiable phenomenology is given: a phase-shifted d-wave distortion k_F(θ) = k_F0(θ) + η cos(2θ + φ) with φ = −π/6. It reproduces the qualitative QPI distortions (Fig. 2e, f). The paper also gives a microscopic picture: an intra-unit-cell sublattice charge order (Eqs. 2–5).
- The authors make a reasonable effort to exclude a defect origin, using T-matrix calculations with sublattice-selective and anisotropic defects (Supplementary Figs. 18–20).
- The raw data are deposited publicly.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The size of the symmetry breaking is close to the q-resolution and to residual image-distortion effects, and the paper does not show that it survives the controls that would rule these out. The average chiral distortion is quoted as 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg| (Supplementary Table 1). The q-differences between the A–H_m directions in Fig. 3c, d are about 0.01–0.03 |Q_Bragg| at most energies, comparable to the plotted error bars, which include a pixel term ε_pix.
   - For the field of view in Fig. 1d–g (roughly 70–100 nm), one FT pixel is of order 0.5–1% of |Q_Bragg|. The signal is therefore a few pixels.
   - The affine transformation applied to restore a perfectly symmetric Bragg lattice (Methods; Figs. 2b, 4d) removes linear distortion only. It cannot remove nonlinear piezo creep, thermal drift along the slow scan axis, or residual non-orthogonality. These produce exactly the kind of direction-dependent, mirror-breaking misalignment of q-vectors claimed here. Each of them has a fixed orientation relative to the scan frame, so it can produce an apparent handedness.
   - The opposite-chirality region (Fig. 2g) is the key control, but it was taken at a different setpoint (1000 pA, 0.3 mV lock-in, against 500 pA, 0.5 mV). The paper does not state whether it used the same tip, the same scan angle and the same field-of-view size.

   The authors should:
   - show the same area imaged at two or three scan-frame rotations (for example 0, 60 and 90 degrees), and at opposite fast-scan directions, demonstrating that the handedness stays locked to the crystal and not to the scan frame;
   - state the FOV, pixel number and resulting q-pixel size for every map, and give the chiral distortion in units of q-pixels;
   - show that the two opposite-chirality regions in Fig. 2g were taken with the same tip and scan frame;
   - apply a control analysis to a symmetric quantity that should not be distorted. One option is the q1 ring of the alpha pocket, which the paper says remains inversion-related; another is the Bragg-peak shapes before correction. This calibrates the residual distortion floor.

   Without this, the central observation could be an artifact.

2. The paper does not separate spontaneous electronic order from extrinsic symmetry lowering, especially strain. The form used in the analysis, η cos(2θ + φ) with φ = −π/6, is a quadrupolar (nematic) distortion whose principal axis lies 15 degrees away from the high-symmetry directions. Any uniaxial strain along a generic in-plane direction produces the same symmetry class. So would a cleave-induced step or terrace gradient, or a non-random Ti distribution.
   - The affine correction of the Bragg peaks removes the lattice signature of such strain from the images but not its electronic response. The analysis may therefore discard the very evidence that would identify strain.
   - Domains of opposite handedness (±15 degree director) are also what strain varying on a ~100 nm scale would give.

   The authors should:
   - report the uncorrected Bragg-peak positions and the lattice strain they imply for each map, and check whether the sign and size of φ and η correlate with them;
   - give domain statistics: how many regions, cleaves and samples, what fraction of each handedness, and the typical domain size, ideally with a large-scale map across a domain boundary showing that the director rotates by about 30 degrees, not by an arbitrary angle;
   - state whether φ = −π/6 is a fit result or an assumption, and whether every domain and doping fits the same |φ|. If the angle is pinned at ±π/6, this favours an intrinsic order. If it varies, it favours strain.

   The symmetry-based T-matrix argument (Supplementary Figs. 18–20) addresses defects but not strain or tip anisotropy.

3. The "parent state" claim and T* ≈ 167 K rest on no temperature-dependent data. All QPI is taken at 0.4 K, plus a 4 K comparison around Tc for the superconducting state.
   - T* is obtained by converting a mean energy splitting of 14.4 meV, derived from the delta-pocket dispersions (Supplementary Table 1), into a temperature. This conversion assumes a mean-field relation between splitting and onset temperature that is not stated or justified. The splitting itself depends on how the q-differences are converted into energy through the renormalized velocities.
   - That this T* exceeds T_CDW ≈ 90 K is the basis for calling the state a "parent" of the CDW (Fig. 4f phase diagram). The comparison with the 130 K torque anomaly (ref. 28) is suggestive but numerically inconsistent with 167 K.

   The authors should:
   - measure the QPI anisotropy at a few elevated temperatures (for example 20, 50 and 100 K at x = 0.15), or else clearly label T* and the phase diagram in Fig. 4f as a conjecture;
   - give the formula and assumptions behind the 14.4 meV to 167 K conversion;
   - temper statements such as "already present above the CDW transition", which the data do not test.

4. The causal link between the chiral-nematic QPI and the CDW chirality at small x is asserted, not demonstrated. At x = 0.05 the paper shows mirror-breaking QPI (Fig. 4c–e) coexisting with CDW peaks whose intensity ratio is 2.6:1.2:1.0. For the claim "CDW-triggered exotic states descend from the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid", one expects the CDW handedness to be locked to the QPI handedness. The authors should:
   - show, across several domains of each handedness at x = 0 or 0.05, that the sense of the CDW intensity ratio flips together with the QPI chirality;
   - show that the CDW peak-intensity ratios are robust to setpoint and tip. Intensity ratios of 2a0 peaks in AV3Sb5 are known to be sensitive to both.

   Without the correlation, coexistence does not establish parentage.

B. Should address

5. The quantitative velocity and mass-renormalization analysis needs uncertainties and a significance test. Supplementary Table 2 and the text quote velocities to six significant figures, for example v_F = (4583.84, 5107.30, 4121.17) meV/|Q_Bragg| and v_DFT = 6074.72. No uncertainties are given, yet the chirality claim rests on the ordering v_I < v_III < v_II (and the analogous ordering for q2 and q3).
   - The fits use the linear range "away from the kink", but neither the window nor the sensitivity of the velocities to it is specified.
   - For the delta pocket, v_F ≈ 2 v_s is acknowledged to hold only to leading order, because the scattering is not inversion-related.

   The authors should give fit windows, uncertainties and the significance of the inter-direction differences, and round to meaningful precision. They should also state the velocities in physical units (meV·Å), not per |Q_Bragg|.

6. The DFT and simulated QPI comparison is qualitative, and its parameters are not independent.
   - DFT uses the virtual crystal approximation without spin-orbit coupling. The QPI simulation is a JDOS autocorrelation of a single kz = π contour with no matrix elements or tunneling form factor.
   - η and φ seem to be chosen to match the data, not fitted.

   The authors should report how η was determined, and compare simulated and measured q-vector positions quantitatively along each A–H_m direction, with residuals. They should also discuss whether kz dispersion, or the Ti-induced shift of the Fermi level relative to the van Hove points near M, could produce direction-dependent apparent q-vectors when the three-dimensional FS is projected.

7. The chiral-nematic superconducting state is weakly supported.
   - The claim that superconductivity "emerges directly" from the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid with the same handedness rests on the qualitative similarity of Bogoliubov QPI at subgap energy to the normal-state pattern (Supplementary Fig. 8).
   - Chirality is said to be observed "both below (0.4 K) and above (4 K)" Tc = 3.5 K. However, the electronic temperature is quoted as 650 mK, and at 4 K the system is barely above Tc.

   A same-area comparison would be convincing: subgap B-QPI against normal-state QPI with superconductivity suppressed by a field above Hc2 (the system has 11 T), using the same tip. It should include a quantitative comparison of the q-vector misalignments. Otherwise the conclusion should be narrowed to "the normal-state anisotropy persists in the superconducting state".

8. The setpoint and normalization treatment differs between figures. Fig. 2 uses raw dI/dV maps; Fig. 3 uses Feenstra-normalized L(r, E). Setpoint effects can shift QPI intensity distributions in an energy-dependent way. The authors should show that the q-vector anisotropy at −10 mV is the same in raw dI/dV, in L(r, E) and at two setpoints. Fig. 2a and 2g are themselves at different currents, which matters for concern 1.

9. Tip anisotropy is not considered as an alternative. A multi-atom or asymmetric tip convolves the LDOS with an anisotropic kernel. This can redistribute FT intensity between directions and shift apparent peak positions in line cuts. The authors should state how many tips were used. Ideally they should show the same chirality with independently prepared tips on the same sample, and the opposite chirality with the same tip.

10. The meaning of "chiral" should be made precise up front. Only in the Discussion do the authors state that "chirality" here means the breaking of all mirrors and does not imply time-reversal breaking. Because the CDW literature they cite uses "chiral" in connection with TRS breaking and field-switchable handedness, the abstract and title invite that reading. The authors should state this in the abstract or introduction. They could also test whether the QPI handedness responds to field training, using the available 11 T magnet. This would bear directly on the link to the field-tunable CDW chirality they cite.

11. Sample characterization and dopant distribution are thin. The Ti concentrations are nominal or cited (ref. 29). The authors should give the measured composition of the crystals used, the local Ti density from topography for each map, and transport or susceptibility confirming the absence of CDW and the Tc at x = 0.15. They should also check for directional clustering of Ti dopants, since the defect-population argument relies on "no dominant or correlated defect population" (Supplementary Fig. 13).

C. Minor

12. The order of panels differs between figures: A–H_I, A–H_II, A–H_III in Fig. 2b against A–H_I, A–H_III, A–H_II in Fig. 4d. Please make it consistent, or state why it differs.

13. In the Methods text following Eq. (2), the site angles appear to be garbled ("θ = 0, ±π/, ±2π/, π"). Please correct them to 0, ±π/3, ±2π/3, π.

14. "T*-167 K", "T_CDW-90 K" and similar strings presumably mean "≈". Please fix the typography.

15. The text says the extracted mass ratios "fall within the range 1.29–1.71 predicted by DFT+DMFT", but the delta-band values reach 1.88 and 2.33. Please rephrase.

16. Fig. 1b: the temperature axis has a break and mixed scale. Please state this, and mark where the x = 0.04 and 0.05 data were taken.

17. Please state the FOV and pixel counts in the captions of Figs. 1, 2 and 4. This also supports concern 1.

18. Fig. 4c: please state how the CDW peak intensities (2.6:1.2:1.0) were obtained (integrated or peak value, and background subtraction) and give their uncertainties.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a dedicated "artifact controls" supplementary section. It should include scan-rotation and fast/slow-axis tests on one area, uncorrected Bragg-peak strain analysis, same-tip opposite-domain data, and the q-pixel resolution for each map. This is the most effective way to make the central observation convincing.
2. Map a domain boundary at large scale and report domain statistics across samples and cleaves. Show that the director angle is pinned at ±π/6, the signature that would distinguish a spontaneous order from strain.
3. Even a few temperature points of the QPI anisotropy at x = 0.15 above T_CDW of the parent compound would turn the "parent state" from a conjecture into an observation.
4. Correlate QPI chirality with CDW chirality, domain by domain, at x = 0 or 0.05, and test response to magnetic-field training. This would directly support the proposed genealogy in Fig. 4f.
