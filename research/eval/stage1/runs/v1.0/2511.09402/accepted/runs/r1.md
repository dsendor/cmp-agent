Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The paper reports spectroscopic-imaging STM on CsV3-xTixSb5. At x = 0.15, where the 2a0 × 2a0 and 4a0 stripe charge orders are absent, the quasiparticle interference (QPI) patterns of the Sb pz-derived α pocket and the V d-derived δ pockets break C6 rotation and every vertical mirror. The authors call this a "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid". They propose that it is the parent state of the chiral CDW, the nematic order and the superconductivity of the AV3Sb5 family. They support this with residual chiral QPI at x = 0, 0.04, 0.05 and 0.27, with Bogoliubov QPI in the superconducting state, and with a phenomenological d-wave Pomeranchuk distortion rotated off the mirror lines. Mirror-breaking electronic structure that persists without CDW would be an important result for a heavily studied family. However, the effect is small (1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg|). It is shown at one temperature and mainly in one compound region. Its "parent" status is inferred, not measured. As a result, the significance rests on controls that the manuscript does not yet fully provide.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The QPI data are high quality. At x = 0.15 the q1, q2, q3 features are well defined, and the CDW is absent (Fig. 1d–g).
- The authors use a clear orbital-resolved assignment of the scattering channels, with an explicit caveat about the kz = π approximation.
- Mirror-breaking QPI is reported across four Ti concentrations (Fig. 4g), on both Sb- and Cs-terminated surfaces, and in the superconducting state. A region of opposite handedness is shown (Fig. 2g), which argues against a fixed instrumental handedness.
- The energy-resolved dispersions (Fig. 3) give direction-dependent velocities and mass renormalizations that can be compared with DFT, DFT+DMFT and ARPES.
- The phenomenological description kF(θ) = kF0(θ) + η cos(2θ + φ) is simple and reproduces the qualitative QPI distortions (Fig. 2e, f).
- The raw data are deposited publicly.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **Extrinsic sources of a rotated l = 2 distortion are not excluded.**
   - *What the data show.* The Fermi-surface distortion invoked (Fig. 2e; p. 8) is a d-wave term η cos(2θ + φ) whose principal axis lies off the mirror lines. This is exactly the form produced by an in-plane uniaxial or shear strain along a non-high-symmetry direction. It is also the form produced by an imperfectly corrected linear scan distortion: residual piezo anisotropy, creep or drift.
   - *Why the correction cannot settle it.* The effect is small: an average distortion of 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg| (Supplementary Table 1). It is extracted after a global affine transformation that forces the Bragg peaks onto a perfect hexagon (Methods; Figs. 2b, 4d). A global affine correction removes the lattice distortion by construction. It cannot correct non-linear or spatially varying distortions within the field of view. If the lattice is genuinely strained (from cleaving, mounting, or differential thermal contraction against the sample plate), the correction hides the strain while leaving the electronic response to it. That response would appear as a mirror-breaking Fermi surface.
   - *Requested controls:*
     - (a) For each dataset, report the raw, uncorrected Bragg vectors and the affine-correction parameters, i.e. the apparent strain magnitude and axis.
     - (b) Repeat the analysis with a local (Lawler–Fujita-type) drift correction and show that the q-vector asymmetries are unchanged.
     - (c) Image the same area with the fast-scan direction rotated, for example by 30° or 90°. Show that the distortion axis φ stays locked to the lattice and does not follow the scan frame.
     - (d) Measure, with the same scanner and analysis pipeline, a reference whose QPI should be mirror-symmetric (for example a Au(111) surface-state ring or a known hexagonal metal). This establishes the systematic floor on the "distortion" metric.
     - (e) Use DFT to estimate the strain needed to produce η and compare it with the uncorrected lattice distortion.
   - *What would be decisive.* The opposite-handedness region in Fig. 2g is the most persuasive evidence so far. The authors should state whether it was taken with the same tip, scan angle and session. Ideally they should image a domain boundary within one field of view, where an instrumental origin is impossible.

2. **The data do not yet distinguish "chiral-nematic" (all mirrors broken) from an ordinary mirror-preserving nematic.**
   - *The distinction.* A nematic state that keeps one mirror makes two of the three A–Hm directions equivalent. The chiral claim therefore requires that all three pairwise differences in q1, q2, q3, d12, d13, d23 and the velocities are significant.
   - *What the paper shows.* The line profiles in Fig. 2c, d carry no error bars. In Fig. 3c, d the error bars of the three directions overlap over much of the energy range. The velocities quoted on p. 6 (for example v1,F = 4583.84, 5107.30, 4121.17 meV/|Q_Bragg|) carry no uncertainties, fitting window or fit quality. The FT pixel size in units of |Q_Bragg| is not stated, so a 1–2% shift cannot be compared with the q resolution.
   - *Why φ matters.* The phase φ = −π/6 appears to be imposed rather than fitted (p. 8: "used for our QPI data analysis").
   - *Requested analysis:*
     - Give uncertainties for every q and velocity, and test pairwise significance.
     - Fit η and φ directly to the extracted q(E) or to the full QPI map, with confidence intervals. Show that φ is many standard deviations away from the mirror-preserving values (see concern 4 for which values those are).
     - Show the same result in independent fields of view and samples.

3. **The "parent state" claim is not measured.**
   - *What is missing.* The title and abstract claim that the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid is the parent of the chiral CDW and of superconductivity, emerging at T* above T_CDW. All QPI shown is at 0.4 K, with some supplementary data near 4 K. No temperature dependence is presented.
   - *Why the T* estimate is weak.* T* ≈ 167 K is obtained by converting a mean energy splitting of 14.4 meV, measured at x = 0.15, directly into a temperature (E/kB). This is then compared with T_CDW ≈ 90 K of the undoped compound. There is no justification for equating the band-splitting scale with a transition temperature. Mean-field ratios alone would change the estimate by a factor of order unity. The comparison also mixes compositions.
   - *Why the doping argument is weak.* The resistivity shows no anomaly at T* (p. 8). Within the paper, the parent-state argument therefore rests on (i) features persisting across doping at 0.4 K, and (ii) agreement with torque data from another composition.
   - *Requested data.* Temperature-dependent QPI on x = 0.15 from 0.4 K up to, ideally, above 100 K, showing whether and where the asymmetry vanishes. Alternatively, QPI on x = 0 between T_CDW and the claimed T*. A bulk probe on the same x = 0.15 crystals sensitive to the rotation of the principal axes away from the mirrors would also serve: polarization-resolved optical birefringence, elastoresistance, or polarized Raman mixing of the Eg channels.
   - *If no data are added.* The title and abstract should present the parent-state relationship as a hypothesis.

**B. Should address**

4. **The symmetry analysis of the order parameter is incomplete, and the Methods contain an error.**
   - *Landau theory.* A single l = 2 (E2g) order parameter on a hexagonal lattice has an allowed cubic term ∝ cos 3α, which pins the director to σv or σv'. A director at an intermediate angle, such as the φ = −π/6 used here (principal axis at 15°, halfway between the mirror types), therefore needs either a second, independent order parameter that breaks all vertical mirrors (A2g-like) or an external symmetry-breaking field such as strain (see concern 1). The paper should discuss which one is assumed and what it predicts.
   - *The Methods error.* The text says mirror symmetry survives only for φ = 0, ±π. But cos(2θ + φ) with φ = ±π/3 or ±2π/3 is symmetric about a σv' or σv line. With Eqs. (3)–(5), φ = π/3 gives n1 = n3 ≠ n2. So mirror-preserving configurations occur at every φ = nπ/3.
   - *An inconsistent statement.* The text says that for φ = ±π/2 "the nematic component vanishes and the charge order becomes purely chiral". Yet n1 = n0 ≠ n2 ≠ n3, which breaks C3. Within this parametrization, any δn ≠ 0 is nematic. φ only sets the angle between the director and the mirrors.
   - *Requested changes.* Correct these statements, and restate the claim as an E2g director at a non-symmetric angle, together with whatever additional order parameter is required.

5. **"Chirality" is used inconsistently.**
   - The proposed state preserves inversion (Methods, "Because inversion symmetry is preserved…"). Combined with the in-plane mirror σh, which the measurement does not test, the point group is at most C2h, which is achiral in three dimensions. The handedness here is therefore a planar (2D) property of the in-plane director.
   - This matters because the paper links the state to the "chiral CDW" (refs. 19, 20, 24), whose proposed handedness is different. The abstract also speaks of "chiral superconductivity", which most readers will take to mean time-reversal-symmetry-breaking pairing. The note on p. 8 helps.
   - The authors should define the term in the abstract and introduction, state the full point group of the proposed state, and explain how it would connect to the 3D chirality and TRSB claimed for the CDW phase.

6. **Structural or dopant-induced symmetry breaking is not excluded.**
   - The proposed microscopic origin is unequal charge on the three V sublattices (Supplementary Fig. 10b). Ti substitutes on exactly these sites. A preferential sublattice occupation of Ti, or short-range Ti ordering, would produce the same symmetry breaking structurally rather than electronically.
   - The T-matrix simulations address random point defects, but not correlated sublattice occupancy.
   - The authors should tabulate the sublattice assignment of the Ti features in the topographs (for example Fig. 1d) for each FOV and test whether the occupancy is equal across the three sublattices. They should also report whether diffraction on x = 0.15 crystals shows any monoclinic or orthorhombic distortion.
   - The Sb surface is prepared by sweeping Cs adatoms away with the tip (Methods). The residual Cs arrangement should be shown not to have a preferred orientation correlated with φ.

7. **Sample statistics and domain structure are not reported.**
   - The main text does not state how many crystals, cleaves, tips and fields of view were measured at each x. Nor does it say how often each handedness was seen, or how large the domains are.
   - Fig. 4g shows the handedness changing sign between compositions, consistent with domains. However, the domain picture is supported only by one other region "several hundred nanometers away" (Fig. 2g, recorded at a different setpoint).
   - The authors should give a table of datasets with x, sample, tip, scan angle, φ (with uncertainty) and handedness. Large-area or multi-FOV mapping showing the domain size and a domain wall would substantially strengthen the claim of a spontaneous electronic order.

8. **The velocities and mass renormalizations are less robust than their presentation suggests.**
   - *δ-pocket approximation.* For the δ pockets, q2 and q3 connect different pockets. The relation v_F ≈ 2 v_s is justified only "to leading order in the chiral-nematic distortion" (p. 6). Yet the resulting direction dependence of m*/m (1.53, 1.95, 2.33) is of the same order as the distortion. The error introduced by this approximation should be quantified, for example by computing dq/dE from the distorted model Fermi surface of Fig. 2e.
   - *DFT reference.* The reference velocities come from VCA-DFT without spin–orbit coupling at fixed kz = π. A shift of the chemical potential with Ti doping moves the δ band relative to the van Hove point near L and changes v_DFT.
   - *Fit details.* The fitting window used to avoid the kinks should be stated.
   - *Precision.* Velocities quoted to 0.01 meV/|Q_Bragg| (six significant figures) imply far more precision than the data support. Give values with uncertainties.

9. **The superconducting-state conclusion is overstated.**
   - Bogoliubov QPI that inherits the normal-state Fermi-surface anisotropy (Supplementary Fig. 8) shows that the quasiparticles which pair live on a distorted Fermi surface. It does not by itself establish a "chiral-nematic SC ground state" or constrain the pairing symmetry.
   - The SC gap at x = 0.15 is a fraction of a meV. The lock-in modulation (0.3–0.5 mV in the main-text maps) and the electron temperature (650 mK) should be reported for the B-QPI data and compared with the gap, to show that the subgap maps are indeed dominated by Bogoliubov quasiparticles.
   - The claim should be phrased as a normal-state anisotropy inherited by the superconducting state unless order-parameter-sensitive evidence is added.

**C. Minor**

10. **Panel order in Fig. 4d, e.** The panels and legend are ordered A–H_I, A–H_III, A–H_II, but the caption lists A–H_I, A–H_II, A–H_III. Harmonize the order, and the colour coding, with Fig. 2b, c.

11. **Feenstra normalization near E = 0.** The normalization L = (dI/dV)/(I/V) is ill-conditioned near E = 0, which is exactly where Fig. 3a highlights the direction dependence (red arrows). Show that the extracted q(E) near E_F agree with those from unnormalized dI/dV maps.

12. **CDW intensity ratio.** The 2.6 : 1.2 : 1.0 intensity ratio of the 2a0 CDW peaks (Fig. 4c) is cited as evidence of handedness. FT peak intensities depend on tip shape and setpoint. Give an uncertainty across several FOVs and tips.

13. **"Fermi liquid" label.** The label is assumed, not tested. If the energy dependence of the QPI linewidths is available, showing ∝ ω² behaviour near E_F would support it. Otherwise use "metallic normal state".

14. **Schematic T* lines.** The flat T* line in Fig. 4f and the dashed curve in Fig. 1b are not measured. Mark them as conjectural in the captions.

15. **Sign of the splitting.** The quoted "average energy splitting of −14.4 meV" carries a sign that has no meaning for a temperature scale. Define how the splitting and its spread are computed.

16. **Comparison with RbV3Sb5.** The 1.4–2.2% distortion is compared with the "3% |Q_Bragg|" reported for RbV3Sb5 (ref. 31). Clarify whether the same metric is used in both cases.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Image a chirality domain wall within a single field of view, and repeat a dataset with a rotated scan frame. Together these would remove most doubts about instrumental or strain origins.
2. Make η and φ fitted parameters with uncertainties, extracted from every dataset and composition. Present the results as a single figure or table of φ against x, sample and surface termination.
3. Add temperature-dependent QPI, even over a limited range (for example 0.4 K to 50–80 K at x = 0.15), or a bulk polarization-resolved probe on the same crystals, to test the parent-state and T* assertions directly.
4. Recast the symmetry discussion in terms of an E2g director together with any required secondary order parameter, and correct the Methods statements about which φ preserve mirrors. This will make the proposal sharper and testable.
