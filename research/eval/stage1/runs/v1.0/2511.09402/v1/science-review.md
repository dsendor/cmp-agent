A. Must address

1. Scanner, drift and tip artifacts are not ruled out at the size of the claimed effect. Where: Figs. 2a–d, 2g, 3a, 3c–d, 4d; Methods; Supplementary Table 1. The whole claim rests on differences between q1, q2 and q3 along the three A–H_m directions, and the reported distortion is only 1.4–2.2 % of |Q_Bragg|. For the field of view of Fig. 1d–g (roughly 80–90 nm), one Fourier pixel is 2π/L ≈ 0.08 nm⁻¹ against |Q_Bragg| ≈ 13 nm⁻¹, i.e. about 0.5–0.6 % of |Q_Bragg|. The effect is therefore only 2–4 FT pixels. Piezo calibration error, creep, scanner nonlinearity and non-orthogonality of the scan axes, thermal drift over a multi-hour dI/dV map, and layer-to-layer drift in the multi-energy dataset of Fig. 3 routinely distort FT images by 1–3 %. The Methods say a single affine transformation is applied to the Bragg peaks "to restore a perfectly symmetric lattice", but only for Figs. 2b and 4d; it is not stated whether the maps behind Fig. 2a/2c, Fig. 3 and Supplementary Table 1 were corrected. An affine correction removes only uniform, linear distortion, and once the Bragg peaks are forced to be symmetric they can no longer serve as an internal symmetry reference. An asymmetric or multi-apex tip can also produce direction-dependent QPI intensity and apparent peak shifts. Fig. 3c makes the problem concrete: near E = 0 the q1 data for the three directions differ by about 0.01 |Q_Bragg|, while the horizontal error bars are about 0.005 |Q_Bragg|. The opposite-handedness region (Fig. 2g) was taken "several hundred nanometers away" at a different setpoint (1000 pA, 0.3 mV against 500 pA, 0.5 mV), and it is not said whether the tip or scan angle changed, so it does not yet exclude a tip or scan-frame origin. The key test is whether the handedness stays fixed to the crystal rather than to the scan frame or the tip. The authors should: state the field of view, pixel count and q-resolution of every map and express the effect in FT pixels; report the raw Bragg-peak positions, the affine-correction parameters and the residual Bragg-peak asymmetry after correction for each dataset; show the raw, uncorrected FT next to the symmetrized one; apply a full Lawler–Fujita drift correction rather than only an affine one; give fit uncertainties (confidence intervals) for every q_i^m at E_F and for d12, d13 and d23, and show that the three directional values differ beyond them; repeat the same area with the scan frame rotated (for example by 60° or 90°), compare forward and backward scans, and separately repeat after a deliberate tip change; state whether Figs. 2a and 2g used the same tip and scan angle; ideally image a domain boundary between the two handednesses within one field of view with one tip; and analyse a reference surface known to be C6/C3 symmetric (for example Au(111) QPI) with the same scanner and pipeline to show it yields no comparable "chiral" asymmetry. A dedicated systematics figure collecting these controls would answer the most likely first objection directly.

2. The observed symmetry cannot be told apart from an ordinary nematic distortion, or from extrinsic uniaxial strain, along a generic in-plane direction. Where: Fig. 2e–f, Fig. 2a vs 2g, Discussion, Extended Fig. 6a, Methods (affine transformation). The authors' own model, k_F(θ) = k_F⁰(θ) + η·cos(2θ+φ) with φ = π/2 (i.e. −η·sin 2θ), is a pure l = 2 distortion whose principal axes lie at 45° and 135° to the crystalline direction. Any two-fold distortion whose axes avoid the mirror lines θ = nπ/6 breaks all vertical mirrors and keeps C2, and the two "chiralities" are then simply the two mirror-related director orientations. A uniaxial strain along a generic direction, from cleaving, mounting with differential thermal contraction against the holder or epoxy, or local Ti inhomogeneity, produces exactly this symmetry: three unequal q_i along the three A–H_m directions and two mirror-related "domains". A shear of order 0.5 % could plausibly shift pocket boundaries by the reported 1.4–2.2 % of |Q_Bragg|. Opposite handedness a few hundred nanometres away also fits a director set by random local strain. The affine correction discards the lattice distortion that would reveal strain, since strain and drift both appear as Bragg-peak distortion. The headline claim is spontaneous electronic symmetry breaking (an "intrinsic correlated Pomeranchuk state"), and a symmetry argument alone cannot separate it from strain. The authors should: extract the local lattice strain tensor (magnitude and principal axis) from the uncorrected Bragg peaks or Lawler–Fujita displacement fields in every field of view, and estimate the residual strain that drift cannot account for; compare the strain axis with the fitted director and test for correlation; compile director angle and handedness over many fields of view, samples, cleaves and mountings, and say whether the local Bragg peaks differ in the opposite-handed domain; calculate with DFT under strain (or the tight-binding model) what strain, at what angle, would reproduce the observed q-vector distortions, and compare with the measured strain; explain physically and experimentally what distinguishes "chiral-nematic" from "nematic with a generic director"; and, if possible, apply deliberate uniaxial strain (strain-tuned STM) to see how the handedness responds. A distribution of fitted φ clustered away from the mirror directions and independent of strain and mounting would support an intrinsic state.

The claim that the CDW and the superconductivity "descend from" the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid is causal, while the data are at best correlational, and the handedness of the CDW and of the residual QPI is not compared. Where: Abstract ("demonstrating that the CDW-triggered exotic states descend from…"), Fig. 4a–e, Discussion, Extended Figs. 3–4. 5. The phenomenological model is imposed rather than fitted, has internal inconsistencies, and lacks a theoretical justification for a generic director angle. Where: Fig. 2e–f, Discussion, Extended Fig. 6. The phase φ = π/2 is "used for our QPI data analysis" rather than fitted, η is never reported, and the agreement between Fig. 2f and Fig. 2a (and the simulation in general, "well describe") is judged by eye. The text writes k_F = k_F⁰ + η·sin(2θ) in one place and η·cos(2θ+φ) with φ = π/2 in another; these differ by a sign, so the handedness convention is ambiguous. It is unclear whether one η captures both the α (Sb pz) and δ (V d) pockets; the sublattice charge-order picture would predict different couplings for the two orbitals. A pure shift of k_F changes no velocities, yet the Fermi velocities in Fig. 3 differ by up to about 45 % between directions (for example v2,F = 1554 against 1074 meV/|Q_Bragg|), so the model does not account for the velocity anisotropy and no mechanism is given; the claim that the δ-band renormalization "reinforces" the symmetry breaking holds only if the anisotropic renormalization has the same C2 pattern as the k_F distortion. In addition, for a hexagonal two-component E2g (d-wave) Pomeranchuk order parameter, the symmetry-allowed cubic (three-state Potts) Landau term pins the director to high-symmetry directions, so a director locked at a generic angle (15° from both σv and σv′) needs a microscopic reason or an explicit symmetry-breaking field; the same applies to the proposed intra-unit-cell sublattice charge order with three unequal V densities (Extended Fig. 6b). The authors should: fit η and φ, per pocket or jointly, to the measured q_i^m(θ, E) in all three directions with uncertainties; show quantitatively that φ = 0 (pure nematic, or φ at a multiple of π/6), a C6-symmetric model (η = 0) and a strain model are rejected; use one sign convention throughout; overlay simulated and measured q positions or show residuals; explain or model the velocity anisotropy; address the Landau pinning argument; and present the sublattice charge-order mechanism explicitly as a hypothesis with testable predictions (for example site-resolved LDOS contrast on the three V sublattices in the existing topographs).

9. Setpoint and normalisation effects are not excluded. Where: Figs. 1f, 2, 3, Methods. The dI/dV maps are taken in constant-current mode at a single setpoint per map (for example −10 mV, 500 pA). 13. The Bogoliubov QPI does not establish a "chiral-nematic superconducting ground state". Where: Abstract, p. 11, Discussion, Extended Fig. 5, Fig. S4. Bogoliubov QPI at subgap or near-gap energies inherits the normal-state Fermi-surface geometry, so if the normal state is already distorted the B-QPI will be too, whether or not the gap function breaks mirrors. A U-shaped, fully gapped spectrum gives no information on the angular anisotropy of the gap; moreover, in such a spectrum subgap QPI should be weak, and the origin of the subgap signal in Extended Fig. 5b (residual normal carriers, impurity bound states, broadening) is not discussed, nor is the subgap energy compared with Δ and the 650 mK electron temperature. The authors should show anisotropy in the gap itself, for example direction-dependent gap magnitude or coherence-peak position from phase-resolved B-QPI near Δ, or gap maps with a C2 pattern on a non-symmetric axis. Otherwise the claim, including "direct transition from the parent state to a chiral-nematic superconducting state" in the abstract, should be restated as "the normal-state anisotropy persists into the superconducting state".

14. "Chiral" is used for a state that keeps inversion, and the symmetry classification is not stated. Where: throughout; Introduction, Fig. 1a, Discussion. The proposed state keeps inversion and C2z, and presumably σh, so its point group is C2h, which is achiral in the usual 3D sense; the handedness is a planar chirality of the in-plane Fermi-surface projection, equivalently a nematic director at a non-high-symmetry angle. The phrase "C6 symmetric (C3 plus inversion)" is also imprecise: for a 2D Fermi surface this is in-plane C2 ⊕ C3, and the bulk point group is D6h. Readers will easily confuse this with the chiral (possibly time-reversal-breaking) CDW of Refs. 19, 20 and 24, whose chirality was defined differently. The authors should define the term precisely early on, state the parent and residual point groups explicitly, relate the state to the chiral CDW of those references, and discuss whether "low-symmetry nematic" describes it more accurately.
