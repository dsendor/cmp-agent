Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports spectroscopic-imaging STM quasiparticle interference (QPI) on Ti-doped CsV3Sb5. At x = 0.15, where long-range CDW order is absent, the authors find QPI patterns whose wavevectors differ along the three A–H directions and break all vertical mirror planes. They interpret these patterns as a "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid", modelled by a phase-shifted d-wave Pomeranchuk distortion of the DFT Fermi surfaces. They argue that this state is the "parent" of the chiral CDW and of the superconductivity in AV3Sb5. If the result holds, it matters: the origin of the rotational and mirror symmetry breaking in kagome metals is an open question, and finding a point-group-breaking electronic state without translation symmetry breaking would change how the CDW and the superconducting phases are viewed. The claims rest, however, on wavevector differences of about 1–2% of |Q_Bragg| in Fourier-transformed STM maps, measured at T ≤ 4 K. The "parent state above T_CDW" claim is inferred, not measured.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The topic is timely. The data cover a doping series (x = 0, 0.05, 0.15, 0.27) and come from a group with an established QPI track record on this material family.
- Energy-resolved QPI dispersions are extracted along each of the three A–H directions separately (Fig. 3), so the mirror-breaking claim is tested in energy as well as in a single constant-energy map.
- The authors report a region with the opposite handedness (Fig. 2g). This is the kind of domain evidence an intrinsic symmetry-breaking state should produce, and it partly argues against a fixed instrumental distortion.
- The analysis is consistent across normal-state QPI, Bogoliubov QPI and residual QPI in the CDW phase.
- The simple kF(θ) = kF0(θ) + η·cos(2θ+φ) parametrisation is a transparent, testable framework.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The size of the effect is close to typical STM scanner and Fourier-analysis systematics, and the controls that would exclude these are missing (Figs. 2, 3, 4; Methods; Supplementary Table 1). The chiral distortion is quoted as 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg|. Piezo calibration error, thermal drift, creep and scanner nonlinearity routinely distort FT images by about 1–3%. The Methods state that an affine (Bragg-peak) correction is applied only to Figs. 2b and 4d. It is not stated whether the maps behind Fig. 2a/2c, Fig. 3 and Supplementary Table 1 were corrected. An affine correction removes only linear distortion: nonlinear drift over a long dI/dV map, or energy-layer-to-layer drift in the multi-energy dataset of Fig. 3, can still produce direction-dependent wavevector shifts of exactly this size. Fig. 3c makes the problem concrete. Near E = 0 the q1 data for the three directions differ by roughly 0.01 |Q_Bragg|, while the horizontal error bars are about 0.005 |Q_Bragg|. The authors should:
   - (i) state the field of view, pixel number and resulting q-resolution for every map, and express the 1.4–2.2% effect in FT pixels;
   - (ii) report the residual Bragg-peak asymmetry after correction, and the raw Bragg-peak distortion before it, for each dataset;
   - (iii) give uncertainties on q1, q2, q3, d12, d13 and d23 in each direction, and show that the three values are mutually distinct beyond those uncertainties;
   - (iv) repeat a map on the same area with the fast-scan axis rotated (for example by 60° or 90°), and show that the handedness stays fixed to the lattice and not to the scan frame;
   - (v) ideally, image a domain boundary between the two handednesses in one field of view with one tip. This would rule out tip- or scan-related origins much more convincingly than Fig. 2g, which was taken in a different area with a different set point (1000 pA, 0.3 mV against 500 pA, 0.5 mV).

2. The observed symmetry breaking cannot be told apart from a nematic distortion pinned by extrinsic strain or by an anisotropic defect arrangement (Figs. 1d/1f, 2; Discussion). In the 2D sense used here, a state that breaks all vertical mirrors but keeps inversion is a twofold nematic whose director lies at an angle that is not a high-symmetry direction. A uniaxial strain along a generic in-plane direction produces exactly this symmetry, for example from cleaving, from mounting with differential thermal contraction, or from local Ti inhomogeneity. The paper's own model (φ = π/2, i.e. the director 45° from a crystal axis) fits this description. Several observations also point to extrinsic pinning:
   - The opposite handedness a few hundred nanometres away fits a director set by random local strain.
   - The dI/dV map in Fig. 1f shows elongated dark features that appear to share one orientation, which suggests anisotropic or oriented defects.
   - The affine correction discards the very lattice distortion that would reveal strain.

   The authors should:
   - (i) extract the local lattice strain tensor (magnitude and principal axis) from the uncorrected Bragg peaks, or from Lawler–Fujita displacement fields, in every field of view;
   - (ii) compare the strain axis with the fitted nematic director φ and test for correlation;
   - (iii) compile the director angle and handedness over many fields of view and samples;
   - (iv) characterise the spatial distribution of Ti, Cs vacancies and line-like defects;
   - (v) estimate how much strain the observed kF distortion would need, using DFT under strain, and compare it with the measured strain.

   Without this, the "intrinsic correlated Pomeranchuk state" interpretation does not follow from the data.

3. The "parent state" claim, a state existing above T_CDW at T* ≈ 174 K, is not supported by any measurement above 4 K (Discussion, Fig. 4f, Abstract). The data contain no temperature dependence that would show a T*. T* ≈ 174 K is obtained by converting an estimated "energy splitting" of about 15 meV (Supplementary Table 1) directly through k_B T. No mean-field or other relation between a Fermi-surface distortion energy and an ordering temperature justifies this step. Fig. 4f draws a T* line that was not measured. The causal direction claimed at x = 0.05 and x = 0 ("the CDW descends from the chiral-nematic FL") is also not established: in samples that already have a chiral CDW and 4a0 stripes, the chiral CDW itself could imprint handedness on the residual Fermi surface. At x = 0.15, short-range chiral CDW correlations around Ti sites have not been excluded either. The authors should:
   - (i) measure QPI at elevated temperatures on x = 0.15 (and ideally on x = 0 above T_CDW ≈ 94 K), and track the distortion η(T) to show where it vanishes;
   - (ii) quantify any diffuse intensity at the 2×2 and 1×4 wavevectors in the x = 0.15 FT (for example line cuts through Fig. 1e/1g at Q_2a), giving an upper bound on short-range CDW correlations;
   - (iii) at x = 0.05, report whether the handedness of the CDW peak intensities (2.6:1.2:1.0) and the handedness of the residual QPI are locked together in sign across domains.

   Without (i), the title and abstract claim of a "parent" state should be reduced to a low-temperature observation.

B. Should address

4. The theoretical model is imposed, not fitted, and it does not account for the velocity anisotropy (Fig. 2e–f, Discussion, Extended Fig. 6). The phase is set to φ = π/2 "for our QPI data analysis". The value of η is not reported, and the agreement in Fig. 2f is judged by eye. There are also two internal problems.
   - kF(θ) = kF0(θ) + η·cos(2θ+φ) only shifts Fermi momenta, by about 2% of |Q_Bragg|. Yet the Fermi velocities extracted in Fig. 3 differ by up to about 45% between directions (v2,F = 1554 against 1074 meV/|Q_Bragg|). The model does not produce such velocity anisotropy, and no mechanism for it is given.
   - In a hexagonal crystal, an E2g (d-wave) Pomeranchuk order parameter has a symmetry-allowed cubic Landau term (the three-state Potts form). This term pins the director along the mirror directions, so a director at a generic angle such as φ = π/2 is not the natural outcome of a correlation-driven instability. The same applies to the proposed intra-unit-cell sublattice charge order (Extended Fig. 6b): three unequal V densities with a generic ratio are not favoured unless something else breaks the symmetry.

   The authors should:
   - fit η and φ (with uncertainties) jointly to the measured q_i^m in all three directions;
   - compare the fit quality with a pure nematic model (φ = 0 or a multiple of π/6) and with a C6-symmetric model;
   - explain the velocity anisotropy;
   - address the Landau-theory pinning argument.

5. The extraction of the velocities and mass renormalizations needs justification and error analysis (Fig. 3c–d, Supplementary Table 2, pp. 7–8).
   - No uncertainties are given for v_s or m*/m_DFT, yet the conclusion (v^III < v^I < v^II, and so on) depends on differences of 5–20%.
   - The energy window of the linear fits is not stated. The authors attribute curvature outside it to an electron–phonon kink, but they give no analysis supporting that, and the window choice directly affects the slope.
   - The relation v_F = 2v_s is exact only for scattering between inversion-related points (q1). For the inter-pocket vectors q2 and q3, the q-dispersion depends on the velocity projections at both end points. Calling v_F ≈ 2v_s a "good estimate" "to leading order in the chiral-nematic distortion" is not shown to hold, and the δ-band mass ratios of 1.63–2.35 depend on it.
   - Fig. 3a says Gauss-like backgrounds were subtracted, while the text says peak positions come from Lorentzian fits (Fig. S1). The effect of the background model on the peak positions should be shown.
   - The label "Fermi liquid" is asserted, not tested. Showing quasiparticle lifetime or linewidth scaling, for example the QPI peak width against energy, would support it.

   Please provide fit windows, slope uncertainties and a sensitivity analysis.

6. The Bogoliubov QPI is overinterpreted as "strong evidence for a chiral-nematic SC ground state" (p. 11, Extended Fig. 5). Bogoliubov QPI that resembles the normal-state QPI shows that the superconducting quasiparticles inherit the normal-state band structure. It says nothing about whether the pairing order parameter itself breaks mirror symmetry. Moreover, with a U-shaped, fully gapped spectrum, the subgap QPI should be weak, and the origin of the subgap signal used in Extended Fig. 5b (residual normal carriers, impurity bound states, broadening) is not discussed. To support a mirror-breaking superconducting state, the authors would need direction-dependent gap magnitudes, for example from the energy dependence of B-QPI or from directional gap maps. Otherwise the claim should be reduced to "superconductivity coexists with the distorted Fermi surface".

7. Surface relevance is not established (Methods, Figs. 1–2). All data are taken on Sb-terminated surfaces prepared by moving Cs adatoms away with the tip. The authors' own earlier work (ref. 21) reports symmetry-breaking surfaces in this family. A surface-specific relaxation or reconstruction, or residual Cs ordering, could reduce the point-group symmetry seen by STM. The authors should:
   - show the chirality on a surface not prepared by tip manipulation, or on a different termination;
   - discuss how consistent a bulk mirror-breaking Fermi surface at x = 0.15 is with existing bulk probes (ARPES Fermi maps, elastoresistance, torque, optical birefringence) of Ti-doped samples;
   - say whether a bulk measurement is planned.

8. Reproducibility statistics are missing.
   - For each x, the paper should state how many crystals, cleaves, fields of view and tips were measured, and in how many the chiral pattern was seen (including any that looked C6-symmetric).
   - It should state the typical domain size and the fraction of each handedness.
   - The x = 0.27 and x = 0 evidence sits only in Extended Figs. 2–4, which are not included in the submitted file. The same holds for Extended Figs. 1, 5 and 6, Figs. S1–S4 and Supplementary Tables 1–2. Supplementary Tables 1 and 2 hold the core quantitative evidence, so they need to be available for review.

9. Possible set-point and normalisation effects should be excluded (Figs. 1f, 2, 3). dI/dV maps taken in constant-current mode carry a set-point effect: spatial variations of the integrated LDOS are imprinted onto all energy layers. Combined with anisotropic defects, this can produce anisotropic FT features. The authors should show that the q-positions and handedness are unchanged in L(r,V) = (dI/dV)/(I/V) maps, or in maps taken with a different set-point bias.

10. The terminology and symmetry classification should be made precise (Introduction, Fig. 1a, Discussion). The state described keeps inversion, and presumably σh, so its point group is C2h, which is not chiral in the usual 3D sense. The "chirality" is planar handedness of a nematic director at a generic angle. The authors should state the residual point group explicitly and say how this state relates to the "chiral CDW" of refs. 19, 20 and 24, whose chirality was defined differently. The discussion of time-reversal symmetry already handles part of this point; a group-theoretic statement would remove ambiguity about what "chiral-nematic" means.

C. Minor

11. Fig. 2c caption says the line profiles are taken "in (a)", but the text says they are along cut#1 of Fig. 2b. Please clarify whether the profiles use affine-corrected data.

12. Fig. 4: the caption lists panel (d) as A–H_I, A–H_II, A–H_III, but the panels are labelled A–H_I, A–H_III, A–H_II. The caption also says "1a0 × 4a0 stripes" where the text says "4a0 × 1a0". In addition, the order of q1–q2 separation at x = 0.05 (I < III < II) differs from x = 0.15 (I < II < III); please state explicitly what this means for the handedness.

13. The comparison of the 1.4–2.2% distortion with the "approximately 3%" optically and magnetically induced distortions in RbV3Sb5 (ref. 29) should say whether the same quantity is being compared.

14. DFT details are incomplete. The k-mesh is not given. The cutoff of 300 eV is low for V with semicore states; please confirm convergence. Spin-orbit coupling is omitted, so please comment on its effect on the Sb pz pocket and the δ pockets. Please also justify using kz = π contours for comparison with a surface probe that integrates over kz.

15. Citations:
   - Refs. 17 and 34 are identical.
   - Ref. 37 (a roton superconductivity preprint) is cited for the crystal structure.
   - Ref. 43 (UTe2 PDW) is cited for the drift-correction algorithm; the original Lawler–Fujita method should be cited.
   - Ref. 13 is cited for the QPI autocorrelation algorithm.

   Please check these.

16. The electron temperature (650 mK) is stated, but the 4 K normal-state data (Figs. S2–S3) are referred to only briefly. Please state the energy resolution and lock-in settings for every dataset used in Fig. 3.

17. Fig. 3a (middle panel) shows strong horizontal banding between energy layers. This suggests normalisation or acquisition differences between bias layers that could bias the peak extraction along A–H_II.

18. Language such as "unprecedented", "discovery" and "establishes" should be moderated to match the evidence, especially given concerns 1–3.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a dedicated "systematics" figure. It should contain: raw and corrected Bragg-peak positions; a scan-angle-rotated map of the same area; the q-resolution; uncertainties on all q_i^m and d_ij; and a single field of view containing both handednesses. This would answer the most likely first objection directly.
- Measure QPI at several temperatures between 4 K and about 200 K on x = 0.15, and on x = 0 across T_CDW, and plot the fitted η(T). This is the most direct test of a parent state at T*.
- Map the local strain tensor and the nematic director over many fields of view, and show whether or not they are correlated. If the director turns out to be independent of strain and locked to the CDW handedness at x = 0.05, the intrinsic interpretation becomes much stronger.
- Fit η and φ quantitatively, extend the model so that it also captures the measured velocity anisotropy, and give a Landau-theory argument for why the director is not pinned to a mirror direction.
