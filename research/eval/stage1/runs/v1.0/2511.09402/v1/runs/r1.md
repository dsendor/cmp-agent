Review outline:

Note on scope: the submission I received is the 23-page main text with Figs. 1–4 and Methods. The Extended Data figures (Ext. Figs. 1–6), Supplementary Figs. S1–S4 and Supplementary Tables 1–2 that the text cites are not included. Several key results depend on them: the opposite-chirality domain, the x = 0.27 and x = 0 data, the Bogoliubov QPI, the temperature series across Tc, and every tabulated velocity and distortion value. I could not check those results, and the comments below say where that matters.

1. Significance and novelty

The paper uses spectroscopic-imaging STM on Ti-doped CsV3Sb5. It reports that at x = 0.15, where long-range CDW order is absent, the quasiparticle interference (QPI) patterns break rotational symmetry and all vertical mirror symmetries. The authors interpret this as a "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid" that is the parent state of the chiral CDW and of the superconductivity in AV3Sb5. If the symmetry breaking is intrinsic, spontaneous and present in the bulk above T_CDW, the result would matter to the kagome field, because it would identify the symmetry-breaking state that precedes the CDW. As presented, though, the evidence is a percent-level anisotropy measured at 0.4 K (and 4 K) on one surface termination. The claimed onset temperature T* is never measured, and the main extrinsic explanations (strain, drift and tip effects, aligned defects) are not ruled out.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The data quality is high. QPI is resolved at x = 0.15, 0.05 (and, the authors say, 0.27 and 0) with orbital assignments (Sb pz α pocket; in-plane V d δ pockets) that connect to the authors' earlier PRL.
- Fig. 2b–d makes the comparison concrete. Rotating the three A–H directions onto each other with aligned Bragg peaks and comparing line cuts is the right way to test for broken symmetry.
- The study goes beyond static FT images: it resolves energy dispersions along all three directions (Fig. 3) and gives a phenomenological model with a phase-shifted l = 2 distortion (Fig. 2e–f) that reproduces the qualitative pattern.
- The text reports domains of opposite handedness. If these are documented with the same tip and scan frame, they would be a strong internal control.
- The question matters: whether the parent state above T_CDW already breaks point-group symmetry is open, and torque data (Ref. 35) give independent motivation.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Strain is not excluded, and a uniaxial strain along a generic in-plane direction produces exactly the reported symmetry. Where: Fig. 2e–f, Discussion (Extended Fig. 6a and the cos(2θ+φ) model), Methods (affine transformation). The authors' own model shows that the "chiral-nematic" distortion is an l = 2 deformation whose director is not along a crystalline mirror (φ ≠ 0, π/2 relative to σv, σv′). That is the symmetry of any C2-preserving perturbation applied at a generic angle. Residual uniaxial strain from thermal contraction against the sample holder or epoxy, or from cleaving, is common in STM samples and does this without any electronic instability. A shear of order 0.5 % could plausibly move pocket boundaries by the reported 1.4–2.2 % of |Q_Bragg| (Supplementary Table 1). The Methods state that an affine transformation is applied to the Bragg peaks "to restore a perfectly symmetric lattice" (Figs. 2b, 4d). This removes the one signature in the data that would reveal a real lattice distortion, since drift and strain both show up as Bragg-peak distortion. Why it matters: the headline claim is spontaneous electronic symmetry breaking, and a symmetry argument alone cannot tell it apart from extrinsic strain. What to do:
   - Report the raw Bragg-peak positions and the affine-correction parameters for each data set, and estimate the residual lattice strain that drift cannot account for. Compare forward and backward scans, and maps taken at different scan angles.
   - Use DFT (or the tight-binding model) to calculate how much uniaxial strain, and at what angle, would reproduce the observed q-vector distortions.
   - Show whether the handedness and magnitude change between cleaves or mountings. The opposite-handed domain is a start; the paper should say whether its local Bragg peaks differ.

2. Measurement artifacts are not ruled out at the level of the effect. Where: Fig. 2a–d, Supplementary Table 1, Methods. The quoted distortion is 1.4–2.2 % of |Q_Bragg|. For the field of view of Fig. 1d–g (about 90 nm), one FT pixel is about 2π/L ≈ 0.5 % of |Q_Bragg|, so the effect is only about 3–4 pixels. Tip anisotropy, nonlinear piezo creep, thermal drift that a single affine transform cannot correct, and the setpoint effect (maps taken at V_bias = −10 mV under constant-current feedback) can all distort FT features at this level and are not addressed. The key test is whether the handedness stays fixed to the crystal lattice, rather than to the scan frame or the tip. What to do:
   - State the FT pixel size and give fit uncertainties for every q_i^m in Fig. 2c and Supplementary Table 1.
   - Repeat the x = 0.15 measurement with the scan frame rotated (for example by 60° or 90°) and with at least one different tip.
   - Show the raw, uncorrected FT next to the symmetrized one.
   - Show that the handedness does not depend on setpoint bias and current, or use a normalized quantity (dI/dV divided by I/V, or the L-map) that suppresses setpoint effects.
   - State explicitly whether Fig. 2a and Fig. 2g (opposite handedness) used the same tip and scan angle. If so, include them prominently in the main text. Ideally, image a domain boundary within one field of view.

3. The central claim that the chiral-nematic state is a parent state forming at T* ≈ 174 K above T_CDW is not measured. Where: Discussion ("T* ~ 174 K"), Fig. 4f, Abstract. All QPI data are taken at 0.4 K (with a brief comparison at 4 K, Figs. S2–S3). T* is obtained by converting an average energy splitting of about 15 meV directly to temperature (15 meV/k_B ≈ 174 K), and that conversion has no physical basis. The claim that the state "emerges at a characteristic temperature T*" above T_CDW, and the phase diagram in Fig. 4f, therefore rest on extrapolation. A low-temperature anisotropy at x = 0.15 does not show that the same order exists above T_CDW at x = 0. What to do:
   - Measure temperature-dependent QPI at x = 0.15 (for example 4, 20, 50, 100 K and above) and track the distortion parameter η (or the q_i^m differences) against temperature.
   - Or tone down the title, abstract and phase diagram, and call T* a speculative scale.
   - Probe the undoped compound above T_CDW (≈ 94 K) directly if possible.

4. The claim that the CDW and the superconductivity "descend from" the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid is causal, and the data are only correlational. Where: Fig. 4a–e, Discussion, Abstract ("demonstrating that the CDW-triggered exotic states descend from…"). At x = 0.05 the surface carries a unidirectional 4a0 stripe order (Fig. 4b), which already breaks rotational symmetry and is known to reconstruct QPI. The anisotropy in the residual q1–q3 features could therefore be inherited from the stripe and CDW order rather than the other way round. The 2a0 × 2a0 peak intensity ratio of 2.6:1.2:1.0 comes from one bias (−5 mV). Earlier work (Ref. 20) shows that CDW peak intensity ratios and the apparent handedness depend on bias. What to do:
   - Show how the handedness of the residual QPI relates to the stripe direction and the CDW handedness over several fields of view.
   - Give the CDW peak intensity ratios at several biases, both polarities.
   - Compare the size of the q-vector distortion at x = 0.05 with x = 0.15. If the parent-state picture holds, it should be similar.
   - Reword "demonstrating … descend from" unless such a relationship is shown.

B. Should address

5. The model comparison is qualitative, and the distortion parameters are neither stated nor fitted. Where: Fig. 2e–f, Discussion. The main text never gives a value for η. The phase φ = π/2 is "used" rather than fitted. The agreement between Fig. 2f and Fig. 2a is judged by eye. The text writes k_F = k_F⁰ + η·sin(2θ) in one place and η·cos(2θ+φ) with φ = π/2 in another; these differ by a sign, so the handedness convention is ambiguous. What to do:
   - Fit η and φ (for each pocket, or jointly) to the measured q_i^m(θ) and report uncertainties.
   - Show that φ = 0 (pure nematic) and η = 0 are rejected quantitatively.
   - Use one sign convention throughout.
   - Explain why one η captures both the α (Sb p) and δ (V d) pockets, or whether separate η values are needed. The sublattice charge-order picture would predict different couplings for the two orbitals.

6. The velocities and mass renormalizations are given with spurious precision and without uncertainties, and the assumptions behind them are not tested. Where: Fig. 3c–d, text on pp. 8–9, Supplementary Table 2.
   - Velocities are quoted to six significant figures (for example 4928.84, 5115.95 and 4350.17 meV/|Q_Bragg|) with no errors, yet the chirality claim rests on their ordering (v_III < v_I < v_II). Fig. 3c shows energy error bars and q scatter comparable to the separation between directions.
   - The linear fitting window is not given. Deviations away from E_F are attributed to electron–phonon kinks without evidence.
   - For the δ pockets, v_F ≈ 2v_s assumes equal velocities at the two connected momenta. The authors note this breaks in the distorted state, so m*/m_DFT = 1.63–2.35 has an unknown systematic error.
   - The DFT reference is VCA, without spin–orbit coupling, at a single k_z = π, and it is not said whether the Fermi level was shifted to match the data.

   What to do: report fit windows and the standard errors of each velocity, test whether the three directions differ beyond those errors, and discuss k_z integration and the Fermi-level alignment of the DFT comparison.

7. The evidence for a "chiral-nematic superconducting ground state" is indirect. Where: Discussion, Extended Fig. 5, Fig. S4. In a fully gapped (U-shaped) superconductor, the subgap B-QPI looks like the normal-state QPI. That is expected if the normal-state anisotropy carries over, and it says nothing about the pairing symmetry. The electronic temperature (650 mK) and the subgap energy relative to Δ are not compared. What to do: show whether the gap magnitude or the coherence peaks are anisotropic along the three A–H directions (for example from gap maps or B-QPI at energies near Δ). Otherwise, restate the result as "the normal-state anisotropy persists into the superconducting state".

8. "Chiral" is used for a state that keeps inversion. Where: throughout. The proposed state keeps inversion and C2z. Its 3D point group then contains the horizontal mirror and inversion (C2h-like), so it is achiral in the usual 3D sense. The handedness is a planar (2D) chirality of the in-plane FS projection, or equivalently a nematic director at a non-high-symmetry angle. Readers will easily confuse this with the chiral (and possibly time-reversal-breaking) CDW in Refs. 19, 20 and 24, which the paper also invokes. What to do: define the term precisely early in the paper, state the point group of the proposed state, and discuss whether "low-symmetry nematic" describes it more accurately.

9. Sample statistics and reproducibility are not reported. Where: Methods, Figs. 2 and 4, Discussion. The paper does not say how many crystals, cleaves, tips and fields of view were measured at each x. It does not give the fraction of regions showing each handedness or the size of the domains. The claim of a "robust and universal" feature at all doping levels rests on x = 0.27 and x = 0 data that appear only in Extended Figs. 2–4. What to do: tabulate the number of samples, cleaves and fields of view per doping, the handedness and distortion magnitude for each, and the domain size. Bring at least one x = 0 data set with the same quantitative line-cut analysis into the main text.

10. Oriented defects could account for the intensity asymmetry. Where: Fig. 1f. The dI/dV map shows elongated dark defects that look preferentially oriented. A set of anisotropic scatterers with one orientation imprints its form factor on the QPI intensity, and the text uses "spectral intensity" as part of the mirror-breaking argument. What to do: identify the defects (Ti substituents, Sb vacancies, residual Cs), give their orientation statistics, and check whether the intensity anisotropy persists in maps or masked regions with few such defects. Base the symmetry claims on peak positions, not intensities.

11. The surface may not represent the bulk. Where: Methods (Cs adatoms removed by the tip to expose a large Sb surface). Tip-cleaned Sb surfaces may carry local strain, residual Cs or surface relaxation, any of which could set the symmetry breaking. The bulk claim (parent state of the bulk CDW, comparison with bulk torque, Ref. 35) needs an argument that this is not a surface effect. What to do: compare as-cleaved areas with tip-cleaned areas, and discuss the surface sensitivity of the α (Sb pz) and δ pockets.

12. The quoted distortion is compared with an unrelated quantity. Where: p. 6, comparing 1.4–2.2 % with the "optically- and magnetically-induced distortions of approximately 3 %" in RbV3Sb5 (Ref. 29). The physical meaning of the 3 % figure in Ref. 29 is not explained, and it is unclear that it measures the same thing. Either explain the equivalence or remove the comparison.

C. Minor

13. Fig. 2a, 2g and 4c are 3D perspective renderings, which distort the in-plane distances (d12, d13, d23) that the text uses as evidence of handedness. Show the quantitative spacings on the 2D FT with error bars, and keep the 3D views as illustrations only.

14. Fig. 4d shows the panels in the order A–H_I, A–H_III, A–H_II, while the caption and Fig. 2b use I, II, III. The text for x = 0.05 also reverses the ordering of separations relative to x = 0.15 without saying whether this reflects opposite handedness. Make the order consistent and state the handedness of each data set.

15. The caption of Fig. 4b says "1a0 × 4a0 stripes" and the text says "4a0 × 1a0". Use one form.

16. Ref. 37 is cited for the CsV3Sb5 crystal structure ("forming a simple hexagonal lattice"), but it is a preprint on roton superconductivity. Refs. 17 and 34 are duplicates. Ref. 43 (UTe2 PDW) is cited for the affine-transformation method; describe the algorithm in the Methods.

17. The text says Lorentzian fits (Fig. S1), and the Fig. 3a caption says "Gauss-like backgrounds subtracted". Explain how the background subtraction affects the extracted peak positions, especially where q1 and q2 overlap along one direction.

18. The mass renormalizations are said to "fall within" the DFT+DMFT range of 1.29–1.71, but m*/m = 1.19 and 1.23 for the α band lie below it. Correct the wording.

19. The tabulated quantities that the conclusions rely on (Supplementary Tables 1–2, Figs. S1–S4, Extended Figs. 1–6) must be included with the submission for the claims to be assessed.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a temperature series of the distortion parameter at x = 0.15, from 0.4 K up to at least 100–150 K. This is the single measurement that would turn "parent state at T*" from inference into observation.
2. Build a control set against extrinsic causes:
   - repeat with the scan frame rotated and with different tips;
   - give raw and corrected Bragg peaks and the strain estimate for each map;
   - image a domain boundary showing reversed handedness within one field of view;
   - if possible, apply deliberate uniaxial strain to show how the handedness responds.
3. Replace the visual model comparison with a global fit of η and φ to all measured q_i^m(θ, E), with uncertainties, and compare it with pure nematic and strain models.
4. Narrow the language of the title and abstract ("discovery", "direct evidence", "descend from", "chiral-nematic SC ground state") to what the 0.4 K data establish, and define "chiral" precisely as planar mirror breaking with inversion kept.
