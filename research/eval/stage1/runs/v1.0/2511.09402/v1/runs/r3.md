Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript uses spectroscopic-imaging STM to study CsV3-xTixSb5. At x = 0.15, where long-range CDW order is absent, the authors report quasiparticle interference (QPI) patterns that break all vertical mirror planes as well as six-fold rotation. They read this as a "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid" and propose it as the parent state of the chiral CDW and of the superconductivity in AV3Sb5. If the result holds, it would matter: rotational and mirror symmetry breaking would come from the Fermi liquid itself, not from the CDW, and the community would have to rethink the origin of chirality in the kagome metals. As presented, though, the central observation is a direction-dependent change of about 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg| in QPI wave vectors. That is small enough that apparatus and surface artifacts, and a conventional nematic or strain explanation, are not yet excluded. The "parent state" and "T*" claims rest on inference, not on measurement.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The question is timely and important. Whether point-group symmetry breaking in AV3Sb5 exists without translational symmetry breaking bears directly on how the CDW and the superconductivity arise.
- Ti substitution suppresses the CDW and gives access to the low-temperature, translation-invariant electronic structure. The orbital-resolved QPI assignment (q1 from the Sb pz alpha pocket; q2 and q3 from the in-plane V d delta pockets) builds on the authors' earlier work and is plausible.
- The analysis goes beyond a single map. Energy-resolved dispersions along three directions are extracted (Fig. 3), the same signature is followed across x = 0, 0.05, 0.15 and 0.27, above and below Tc, and in the Bogoliubov QPI.
- The phenomenological description, a d-wave Fermi-surface distortion η·cos(2θ+φ) on the C6-symmetric DFT surface, is simple and gives a concrete, testable parametrization.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. No controls exclude tip, scanner and drift artifacts at the 1–2% level of the claimed distortion. The whole claim rests on differences between q1, q2 and q3 along the three A–H_m directions (Figs. 2b–d, 3a, 3c–d; Supplementary Table 1). The reported mean distortion is 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg|. Fig. 1d shows a field of view of roughly 80 nm. The Fourier pixel is then 2π/L ≈ 0.08 nm⁻¹, against |Q_Bragg| ≈ 13 nm⁻¹, so one pixel is about 0.6% of |Q_Bragg|. The claimed effect is therefore only 2–4 Fourier pixels.

   The Methods say a single affine transformation is applied to the Bragg points "to restore a perfectly symmetric lattice" (Figs. 2b, 4d). This causes two problems:
   - an affine correction removes only uniform distortion; piezo creep, non-linear drift during a multi-hour dI/dV map, and non-orthogonality of the scanner axes can still distort QPI wave vectors anisotropically at the percent level;
   - once the Bragg peaks are forced to be symmetric, they can no longer serve as an internal reference for symmetry.

   An asymmetric or multi-apex tip can also give direction-dependent QPI intensity and apparent shifts of peak positions. The opposite-chirality region (Fig. 2g) was taken at a different setpoint (1000 pA, 0.3 mV) "several hundred nanometers away". Without information on whether the tip changed, this region does not rule out a tip effect.

   The authors should:
   - state the field of view, pixel count and q resolution of each map;
   - apply a full Lawler–Fujita drift correction, not only an affine correction;
   - repeat the same area with the scan frame rotated (for example by 60° or 90°) and, separately, after a deliberate tip change, and show that the direction-dependent q_i follow the crystal and not the scan frame or the tip;
   - measure a reference hexagonal surface known to be C6/C3 symmetric (for example Au(111) QPI, or a C6 material) with the same scanner and protocol, to show that the analysis yields no comparable "chiral" asymmetry;
   - give confidence intervals for each q_i^m at E_F, so that it is clear that all three values differ significantly from one another.

2. A "chiral-nematic" state cannot be told apart from an ordinary nematic or a strain field with a director at a non-symmetric angle, and the director angle is fixed, not fitted. The model used (main text; Extended Fig. 6a; Fig. 2e–f) is k_F(θ) = k_F^0(θ) + η·cos(2θ+φ), with φ = π/2, i.e. −η·sin 2θ. This is a pure l = 2 nematic distortion whose principal axes lie at 45° and 135° to the crystalline direction. Any two-fold distortion whose axes avoid the twelve mirror lines θ = nπ/6 breaks all vertical mirrors and retains C2 (point group C2h). The two "chiralities" are then the two mirror-related director orientations. A uniaxial strain along a generic in-plane direction, for example from cleaving, mounting or thermal contraction against the sample holder, produces exactly this symmetry: three unequal q_i along the three A–H_m directions and two mirror-related "domains" (Fig. 2a vs 2g).

   Two further gaps follow:
   - in Landau theory for the hexagonal two-component E2g nematic order parameter, the cubic (three-state Potts) term pins the director to high-symmetry directions; a director locked at 45° (15° from both the σv and σv' lines) needs a microscopic reason or an explicit symmetry-breaking field;
   - φ = π/2 is "used", and the value of η is not reported, so it is unclear whether φ and η were fitted and with what uncertainty.

   The authors should:
   - fit φ and η freely to the measured q_i^m(E_F) for all three pockets, with uncertainties, and show that φ is significantly different from 0 and π/2 modulo the crystal mirrors (i.e. different from a conventional nematic);
   - explain what distinguishes "chiral-nematic" from "nematic with a generic director", physically and experimentally;
   - address strain directly, for example by measuring the local lattice-constant anisotropy from the uncorrected Bragg peaks across many fields of view, or by comparing samples mounted differently; strain-tuned STM would be the most direct test.

3. The "parent state" and T* above T_CDW are inferred, not measured. The title, abstract and Fig. 4f claim that the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid emerges at T* above T_CDW and is the parent of the CDW in undoped AV3Sb5. The data, however, are at 0.4 K, with some at 4 K (Figs. S2, S3), all far below any proposed T*. T* ≈ 174 K is obtained by converting an average energy splitting of about 15 meV through E = k_B·T*. This has no theoretical justification: no mean-field ratio, and no reason the band splitting at T = 0 sets the ordering temperature. Observing a symmetry-broken state at x = 0.15 and 0.4 K also does not show that the same state exists above T_CDW at x = 0.

   The authors should do one of two things:
   - measure the temperature dependence of the QPI anisotropy at x = 0.15 (or 0.27) up to and beyond the proposed T*, or at least well above 4 K (for example 20, 50, 100 K), and show where it vanishes;
   - otherwise, substantially soften the "parent state" language throughout, including the title, and describe T* as a speculative estimate.

   The torque result cited (ref. 35, about 130 K) gives context but is not evidence from this work.

B. Should address

4. The number of samples, fields of view and domain statistics are not reported. The paper says chiral QPI is "reproducible" across dopings and that opposite-chirality domains exist (Fig. 2g, Extended Fig. 1). It does not say:
   - how many crystals per doping were cleaved;
   - how many independent fields of view were analysed;
   - what fraction showed each handedness or none.

   No domain wall is imaged. The authors should tabulate samples, cleaves, fields of view and the extracted (η, φ) for each. They should image a boundary between opposite-chirality regions with the same tip in one map. That would also strongly address concern 1.

5. The surface may break the symmetry, rather than the bulk electronic state. The measurements are on the Sb-terminated surface after the tip has removed Cs adatoms. The authors' own earlier work (ref. 21) reports "symmetry-breaking surfaces" of this material. Surface relaxation, residual Cs order, tip-induced modification of the cleaved surface, or Ti clustering at the surface could set the director. The authors should:
   - show that the anisotropy is independent of the Cs-removal procedure, for example by comparing areas with differing residual Cs coverage;
   - report the Ti distribution and density seen in topography;
   - discuss why the effect should be bulk.

   A bulk-sensitive cross-check, even a literature comparison with ARPES Fermi-surface maps of Ti-doped samples, would help.

6. Short-range or fluctuating CDW at x = 0.15 is not excluded. The absence of 2a0×2a0 and 4a0×1a0 peaks in Fig. 1e rules out long-range order in that field of view. It does not rule out short-range correlations or pinned CDW puddles near Ti dopants, which could be oriented by local strain and could reconstruct the QPI anisotropically. The authors should show:
   - the FT with logarithmic intensity scale around 2a0 and 4a0;
   - dI/dV maps at several energies looking for local charge modulations;
   - the same QPI analysis at x = 0.27 (Extended Fig. 2) at matching quality, since there the CDW is further from the phase boundary.

7. The Fermi velocities and mass renormalizations lack uncertainties, and their derivation for q2 and q3 is approximate.
   - Velocities v_{1,F}^m = (4928.84, 5115.95, 4350.17) meV/|Q_Bragg| and the corresponding m*/m^DFT ratios are given without error bars. The DFT values are quoted to six significant figures. The authors should give fit uncertainties and the energy window used for the linear fits in Fig. 3c–d, and show that the three directional velocities differ beyond those uncertainties.
   - Fig. 3a and 3c–d appear to cover the range −70 to +60 meV. The "kink" attributed to electron–phonon coupling is not quantified, and the choice of linear range affects v_s.
   - The relation v_F ≈ 2v_s for the inter-pocket q2 and q3 assumes the two connected states have equal and antiparallel velocity projections. The text says this "still holds to leading order", but the size of the correction should be estimated.
   - The pure η·cos(2θ+φ) shift of k_F used in Fig. 2e–f changes no velocities. The direction-dependent velocities therefore need their own model, or the authors should state that the phenomenological model does not account for them.
   - The claim that the δ-band renormalization "reinforces" symmetry breaking is not justified: an anisotropic renormalization can only reinforce it if it has the same C2 pattern as the k_F distortion.

8. The Bogoliubov QPI does not establish a "chiral-nematic SC ground state". Below Tc, Bogoliubov QPI at subgap or near-gap energies inherits the normal-state Fermi-surface geometry. If the normal state already has a distorted Fermi surface, the B-QPI (Extended Fig. 5) will be distorted too, whether or not the gap function itself breaks mirrors. The U-shaped gap (Fig. S4, Extended Fig. 5d) gives no information on the gap's angular anisotropy. To claim a mirror-breaking order parameter, the authors would need to show anisotropy in the gap itself, for example:
   - a direction-dependent gap magnitude or coherence-peak position from phase-resolved B-QPI;
   - gap maps showing a C2 pattern with a non-symmetric axis.

   Otherwise the statement should be restricted to "the SC state forms out of the chiral-nematic normal state". The phrase "direct transition from the parent state to a chiral-nematic superconducting state" in the abstract likewise needs this support.

9. The handedness of the residual QPI and of the CDW is not compared in the CDW samples. At x = 0.05 (Fig. 4) the 2a0×2a0 peak intensities follow 2.6 : 1.2 : 1.0 and the QPI distortion has a handedness. There is also a unidirectional 1a0×4a0 stripe order. That stripe order alone breaks rotation and could orient a nematic distortion. The authors should:
   - state the stripe direction relative to the distortion axes;
   - say whether the QPI handedness is locked to the CDW handedness (the sense of the intensity ordering) across domains;
   - give the ordering of q1–q2 separations for x = 0.05, which reads H_I < H_III < H_II, and how it compares with x = 0.15 (H_I < H_II < H_III) after accounting for crystal orientation.

   If the two handedness definitions are not correlated over several domains, the claim that the CDW "inherits" its chirality from the Fermi liquid is unsupported. For x = 0, q3 is "faint but still detectable" (Extended Figs. 3, 4), and the evidence there should be quantified in the same way as at x = 0.15.

10. The DFT and QPI simulation inputs limit the quantitative comparison.
   - The DFT uses the virtual crystal approximation without spin-orbit coupling.
   - The comparison uses only k_z = π.
   - The QPI simulation is a JDOS autocorrelation of only the α and δ contours, with no matrix elements or k_z integration.

   STM integrates over k_z. The authors should justify the k_z = π cut, for example by showing that k_z = 0 contours give q values inconsistent with the data. They should check that SOC and VCA do not alter the pocket shapes at the 1–2% level relevant here. They should also compare simulation and data quantitatively, for example with overlaid q positions or residuals, not only by eye ("well describe").

11. Normalization and setpoint effects. The dI/dV maps are taken at a single setpoint per map (for example −10 mV, 500 pA). Setpoint effects can shift and redistribute QPI intensity, and the intensity asymmetry is part of the claim ("in terms of both momentum locations ... and the spectral intensity"). The authors should repeat the key analysis on L(r,E) = (dI/dV)/(I/V) or on maps taken at a different setpoint bias, and show that the asymmetry is unchanged.

12. The supporting data are missing from the submission. The submission refers to Extended Figs. 1–6, Supplementary Figs. S1–S4 and Supplementary Tables 1–2, which carry key quantitative results: the distortion magnitudes, the velocities, the Lorentzian fits, the x = 0 and x = 0.27 data and the B-QPI. None of these is included in the submitted file, so the central numbers cannot be checked. They should be provided with the manuscript.

C. Minor

13. "C6 symmetric (C3 plus inversion)". For a 2D Fermi surface this is in-plane C2 ⊕ C3. The point group of the bulk is D6h. Please state the symmetry operations precisely, and say which subgroup (C2h?) the proposed state retains.

14. Ref. 34 duplicates ref. 17. Ref. 37 (a loop-current preprint) is cited for the statement that "Sb1 atoms ... form a simple hexagonal lattice", which seems incorrect. Ref. 43 (UTe2) is cited for the affine drift correction; the original drift-correction method should be cited.

15. Fig. 3b inset and Fig. 1c label the pockets α and δ. Please also label the green out-of-plane V d contours, and state the colour code in the caption.

16. Fig. 2c: label the y-axis offsets. Explain the "dotted curves" that are said to indicate chirality, because the panel shows dashed vertical lines.

17. Fig. 4 caption: "1a0 × 4a0" in the caption and "4a0 × 1a0" in the text; please make these consistent.

18. The lock-in modulation (0.2–0.5 mV) and the electron temperature (650 mK) are fine for the normal state. Please state the energy resolution of the Fig. 3 dispersions and the energy step of the map sequence.

19. Quantities like v = 3037.36 meV/|Q_Bragg| are given to more precision than is meaningful. Please round, and give velocities also in eV·Å for comparison with ARPES (ref. 30).

20. The schematic phase diagram (Fig. 4f) places "Mirror breaking SC" across all doping and "Chiral PDW, Chiral SC" at low doping. Mark which regions are supported by data in this paper and which are conjecture.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a dedicated artifact-control figure:
   - the same area scanned at two scan-frame orientations;
   - the same area after a deliberate tip change;
   - one map that crosses a domain boundary between opposite handedness;
   - a reference C6 surface analysed with the identical pipeline.

   Together these would turn the central observation from suggestive to convincing.
2. Fit the distortion model with η and φ free across many fields of view and samples, and report histograms of φ. A φ distribution clustered away from 0 and π/2 (mod crystal mirrors) and independent of mounting would separate an intrinsic chiral-nematic state from strain or a conventional nematic.
3. Measure the temperature dependence of the q_i anisotropy above Tc, up to as high a temperature as the instrument allows. This would directly test the proposed T* and the "parent state" interpretation.
4. Recast the B-QPI and phase-diagram claims to match the evidence. Present the superconducting result as inheritance of the normal-state Fermi-surface distortion, unless gap anisotropy is shown directly. Present the sublattice charge-order mechanism (Extended Fig. 6b) explicitly as a hypothesis, with predictions (for example a site-resolved LDOS contrast on the three V sublattices) that the existing topographs could test.
