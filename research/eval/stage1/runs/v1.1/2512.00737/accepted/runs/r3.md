Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper uses circularly polarized Fe L3-edge RIXS on the polar antiferromagnet Fe2Mo3O8 to show circular dichroism (CD) that appears below T_N = 60 K. The CD shows up in two site-resolved excitations: the ~70 meV spin-orbital feature A at the quasi-octahedral (Oh) site and the ~0.5 eV dd feature B at the tetrahedral (Td) site. It appears in the m′ scattering geometry and is absent in the m (glide) geometry. The authors read this as evidence that both magnetic sublattices are individually altermagnetic ("bi-altermagnetism"), and an ionic model with a staggered Weiss field reproduces the CD. The idea is new and useful: RIXS-CD on localized dd excitations gives a probe of altermagnetic symmetry breaking that resolves the sublattice, works where XMCD and the anomalous Hall effect are forbidden, and avoids the ambiguities of magnon CD. The contrast with RIXS-CD studies of MnTe, CrSb and α-Fe2O3, whose high T_N prevents a clean on/off comparison across the transition, is well argued.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The m′ and m geometries are a clean symmetry test. The selection rule (CD forbidden in the paramagnetic phase in both geometries, allowed only in m′ in the altermagnetic phase, allowed in both in the ferrimagnetic phase) is worked out explicitly and backed by simulations (Fig. S9).
- The temperature series is dense across T_N (32, 45, 50, 55, 60, 65, 70, 80 K; Figs. 3, S3, S4), and the CD disappears at both feature A and feature B.
- Site assignment is clear: the Td and Oh crystal-field splittings (0.49 and 0.95 eV) differ enough that CD can be attributed to each sublattice. The crystal-field model is fixed on the RIXS line positions alone, before the CD is computed (SI Sec. 4).
- The simulated CD for the Oh and Td sites (Fig. 2b,c) matches the measured CD qualitatively at A and B, and the result is checked against the unknown trigonal distortion of the Oh site (Fig. S8).
- The data are publicly deposited.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None.

B. Should address

1. The CD is never quantified, and the null geometry shows comparable differences on the elastic line. Every CD statement in the paper rests on overlaid RCP and LCP spectra (Figs. 2a, 3, S2, S3, S4). Phrases such as "vanished within the experimental uncertainty" (p. 4) and "vanishes within the present experimental accuracy" (inset of Fig. 2a) are never backed by a stated uncertainty. In the m-plane control (Fig. S2, lower panel), the elastic line differs between LCP and RCP by about 8% (~95 vs ~88). The signal claimed for feature A in the m′ geometry is a shoulder difference of ~10–15%, sitting on the flank of that same elastic line. Above T_N the elastic peaks also differ by a few percent (Fig. S3e–g). The feature-B CD (~15% at the peak) is clearly larger, but the Oh-site half of the bi-altermagnetism claim rests on feature A.
   - Show difference spectra I_RCP − I_LCP (or the normalized asymmetry) with statistical error bars for both geometries and all temperatures.
   - Plot the integrated CD of A and of B against temperature, with error bars.
   - Explain the elastic-line difference in the m geometry, for example specular or diffuse reflectivity that depends on polarization, or slight misalignment of the scattering plane from the glide plane.

2. The Oh-site evidence rests on a single feature near the resolution limit, and the model predicts CD at feature C that is not seen.
   - Feature A sits at ~70 meV with 45 meV resolution, and the two-Lorentzian decomposition is admitted to be a "constrained minimal decomposition" (SI Sec. 3.2). Td spin-orbital excitations (<50 meV, Fig. S7a), magnons (10–15 meV) and electromagnons all lie within the same window.
   - The simulations show visible CD at the Oh 1.0 eV 5E feature in the m′ geometry (Fig. 2b; Fig. S9h). Experimentally, feature C shows negligible CD, which the paper attributes to "large lifetime broadening" (p. 4).
   - The authors should test that explanation in the model by broadening the 1–1.3 eV final states and showing that the predicted CD at C falls below the experimental noise.
   - They should also show that the Td contribution at A's energies carries no CD for the Weiss fields used. Fig. 2c suggests this, but no quantitative statement is given.
   - Without these checks, the sublattice-specific Oh claim is weaker than the Td claim.

3. It is unclear what the measurement establishes beyond the known magnetic structure. The magnetic structure of Fe2Mo3O8 (antiparallel Oh–Td within a layer, compensated overall, L ∥ c) is known from neutron diffraction. Given that structure, it follows from symmetry that each Oh pair and each Td pair is related by the 6_3 screw and so satisfies the altermagnet definition. The paper itself says (p. 4) that CD below T_N "does not, by itself, uniquely identify bi-altermagnetism." The m/m′ selection rule identifies the global magnetic point group 6′mm′, not whether each sublattice is "independently" altermagnetic. The authors should state what a non-bi-altermagnetic but compensated alternative would look like in their data, and show which observation excludes it. Two concrete options:
   - Use the relative sign of the CD at A and at B. It is fixed by whether the Oh and Td moments in a unit are parallel or antiparallel, so it is a sublattice-resolved check of the magnetic structure beyond the point group. Show explicitly that the model reproduces this relative sign.
   - Compare the temperature dependence of the CD at A and at B with each other and with the published sublattice magnetizations.
   Either would make "bi-altermagnetism" a measured statement rather than a classification.

4. The domain state and a sign-reversal test are not addressed. The CD is odd under time reversal, so the two altermagnetic domains (L and −L) give opposite CD. A finite net CD therefore means one domain dominates within the ~386 × 30 µm² beam footprint on a 1.26 mm crystal. The paper does not say:
   - how the sample was cooled (zero field, or magnetoelectric field cooling, which is natural in this polar, magnetoelectric compound);
   - whether the CD was checked at several positions on the sample;
   - whether the CD sign reverses for the opposite domain or for the mirror-related geometry (scattering plane reflected through m′).
   A sign reversal under domain or geometry reversal would be the most direct proof that the signal is magnetic. Domain information also matters for comparing the CD magnitude with the model, which assumes a single domain.

5. The robustness of the normalization is not shown. LCP and RCP spectra are normalized to the slit current and then to the integrated weight at 5–7 eV energy loss (SI Sec. 3.1). The source flux ratio is 1.1, so the normalization changes the raw data by ~10%, which is comparable to the CD. The authors should show that the CD at A and B, and its absence at C and in the m geometry, survives alternative normalizations, for example total integrated intensity or the weight of feature C.

6. The spin-orbital analysis is internally inconsistent.
   - The text (p. 5) says the two fitted components follow "a 2:3 ratio" compatible with J_eff splittings of ζ/2 and 3ζ/4. The fitted peaks in Fig. S5d are at ~31–38 meV and ~68–73 meV, so the intervals are ~35 and ~35 meV (ratio ≈ 1, not 2:3), and the excitation energies are in a ratio of ≈ 0.5 (first order predicts 2:5).
   - Fig. 4a uses ζ = 52 meV, while the CD simulations use ξ_3d = 65 meV (SI C2).
   - Fig. 4a predicts levels at 24–26 and 58–65 meV, below both fitted peaks.
   The authors should restate the comparison with one consistent SOC value, including second-order and trigonal (Fig. S8a) and Weiss-field (Fig. S7e) shifts, and make clear how much of the J_eff picture the data actually constrain at this resolution.

7. The model parameters and the orbital-magnetism claim are under-constrained.
   - The Weiss field (3–5 meV) is chosen as "reasonable" (SI p. 6), not derived from the known exchange constants or T_N.
   - The Weiss field acts only on spin, along L.
   - The comparison with data is qualitative; no CD magnitudes are compared.
   - The ordered moments behind the "Van Vleck-type" Td magnetism and the "about 20%" orbital contribution (p. 5–6), which supports the claim that this is "a rare example" of an altermagnet with active orbital magnetism, come entirely from this model. Those moments, (S_c, L_c) = (1.67, 0.35) at the Td site and 5/2 µB for the Oh J_eff = 1 state, should be compared with the moments measured by neutron diffraction, and with the CD magnitude as a function of Weiss field.
   - Otherwise the orbital-magnetism statement should be framed as a model result.

C. Minor

8. Results from only one crystal are shown, and thermal-cycle reproducibility is not mentioned. State how many samples and cooldowns the CD was seen in.

9. The sample's effective temperature under the focused beam (>10^15 photons/s into ~30 µm vertically) is not discussed. This matters because the CD is already strongly reduced by 55 K (Fig. S3d), close to T_N.

10. Figure consistency:
    - Fig. 2 (Q = (0.05,0,0.65), θ = 26.26°) and Fig. 3 (Q = (0.03,0,0.66), θ = 30°) use different geometries, and the low-energy intensities differ by ~1.6×. Say why the temperature series was taken at a different Q.
    - At 80 K (Fig. S4h) all dd intensities drop by ~30% compared with 70 K. Explain this, since it bears on the normalization (item 5).
    - In Fig. S6 the caption refers to the "vertical bars" that mark optimal values; the panel labels give ξ_3d in meV while the axis of panel (b) says ξ_3d = 0.0 eV.

11. SI Sec. 5 needs tidying.
    - The derivation of ζ′ = ζ/4 mixes real orbitals (↑xy, ↓xy) with m_l eigenstates.
    - It drops the e_g electrons without justification.
    - It does not discuss the sign of the effective coupling under the T–P correspondence, which sets the level ordering (J_eff = 1 lowest).
    - Cite a standard reference (e.g., Abragam–Bleaney) or give the projection properly.

12. The low-energy dispersion (Fig. S5) is measured only along (0,0,L) at three points, whereas the CD is measured at (H,0,L). The statement that "the observed dispersion within the measured momentum range is small" should be limited to this direction.

13. The statement that XMCD and the anomalous Hall effect are forbidden in 6′mm′ (p. 4) would be clearer with a one-line symmetry argument: the 6′ operation reverses M_z.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a figure of CD difference spectra, with error bars, for both geometries, and the integrated CD of A and B against temperature, set beside the sublattice order parameter.
2. Measure, or state if already measured, the CD sign under reversal of the scattering geometry through m′ and in a magnetoelectrically field-cooled sample of opposite domain.
3. Use the relative sign of the A and B dichroism as an explicit sublattice-resolved test of the magnetic structure, and compare it with the model.
4. Unify the SOC constant and the J_eff level analysis between Fig. 4, Fig. S5 and the simulations, and compare the model's moments with neutron values.

5. Verdict: minor revision
