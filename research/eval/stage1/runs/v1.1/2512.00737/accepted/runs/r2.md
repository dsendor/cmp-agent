Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors measure circular dichroism (CD) in Fe L3-edge RIXS on the polar, compensated magnet Fe2Mo3O8. They report that CD appears below T_N = 60 K in the crystal-field (dd) and spin-orbital excitations of the tetrahedral (feature B, ~0.5 eV) and octahedral (feature A, ~70 meV) Fe sites. They argue that this site-resolved CD, seen for a scattering plane in the m′ mirror and absent for the m (glide) plane, is a signature of "bi-altermagnetism": two interpenetrating altermagnetic sublattice pairs. Using a zero-magnetization, symmetry-forbidden-XMCD system and the site selectivity of dd excitations to tell the sublattices apart is a new use of RIXS-CD, and it adds to current work on how to detect altermagnetic order. The experiment is clean in concept. The weight of the conclusion rests on symmetry selection rules (m′ against m, below against above T_N) and on an ionic-model simulation, so how well those selection rules are tested in the data is the central question.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The geometry is well chosen. In the m′-plane geometry, symmetry forces CD to vanish in the paramagnetic phase whatever the iΓ term in the Kramers–Heisenberg amplitude does. The measurements at 60, 65, 70 and 80 K (Fig. 3, Figs. S3–S4) therefore act as a built-in null under matched conditions.
- The CD on feature B (~0.45 eV, T_d site) is clearly visible at 32, 45 and 50 K (Fig. S4a–c, about 15% peak intensity difference) and is gone at 60 K and above. The onset coincides with T_N.
- The site assignment of features A, B and C rests on a reasonably constrained multiplet model (Fig. S6). Crystal-field and Hund's parameters were fixed from peak positions without using the CD.
- The simulations of the ferrimagnetic state (Fig. S9) give a concrete, falsifiable prediction: CD in the m-plane geometry for the ferrimagnetic phase but not for the altermagnetic one. This sharpens the claim beyond "magnetic order gives CD."
- The source data are deposited on Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The m-plane null, which the authors present as what identifies bi-altermagnetism, is not quantified, and the data shown have visible residual differences. On p. 4 the authors state that the onset of CD below T_N "does not, by itself, uniquely identify bi-altermagnetism" and that "this symmetry-dependent response [m versus m′] … identifies the observed signal as a signature of bi-altermagnetism." The m-plane evidence is a single RCP/LCP overlay at 32 K and one Q (inset of Fig. 2a; Fig. S2 lower panel), described as vanishing "within the present experimental accuracy." No accuracy is given. In Fig. S2 (lower panel), the m-plane spectra differ at the elastic/quasi-elastic peak (LCP about 95 against RCP about 88, a difference as large as or larger than the feature-A CD in the m′ geometry). They also differ slightly on the high-energy side of feature B (~0.5 eV). These residuals could be counting noise, an imperfect flux normalization (the LCP/RCP flux ratio is 1.1 and is corrected using slit current and the 5–7 eV region, SI §3.1), a footprint or self-absorption change on azimuthal rotation, or polarization-dependent specular/diffuse elastic scattering. Until they are quantified, the reader cannot judge whether the m-plane CD is zero to within an error much smaller than the m′-plane CD. The authors should:
   (i) plot the difference spectra (I_LCP − I_RCP), or the normalized asymmetry, for the m′ and m geometries on the same axes, with error bars from counting statistics and from repeated polarization switching;
   (ii) give integrated CD values with uncertainties for features A and B in both geometries;
   (iii) show, ideally, the m-plane spectra above T_N as well, so that any residual is shown to be temperature-independent and therefore instrumental;
   (iv) state how many RCP/LCP cycles were averaged, and whether the polarization was alternated during the scans.

B. Should address

2. CD amplitude against temperature is shown only as overlaid spectra, without error bars. The statement on p. 4 that "the CD vanished within the experimental uncertainty" above T_N, and the "direct link" to the magnetic order, would be much stronger with the integrated CD of features A and B plotted against T (32–80 K, from Figs. S3–S4), with uncertainties, and compared with the order-parameter dependence (for example the sublattice magnetization from neutron data, or the Weiss-field dependence of the model in Fig. S7). This would also show whether the CD at A and B scales in the same way, as expected if both sublattice pairs are driven by the same Néel order. As it stands, the CD at 55 K (Figs. S3d, S4d) is already hard to distinguish from the 60–80 K data. Note also that the overall dd intensity at 80 K (Fig. S4h, peak ~14) is about 30% lower than at 70 K (Fig. S4g, ~22) on the same axes. The authors should explain this, since the CD analysis relies on the same normalization.

3. Altermagnetic domains, sample count and reproducibility are not discussed. The two time-reversed Néel domains (L and −L) give CD of opposite sign. With a beam footprint of about 386 × 30 µm² on a ~1.3 mm crystal (SI §2, Fig. S1), a multi-domain state would reduce the CD and could make its sign depend on the cooling history and the beam position. The paper does not state:
   - how many crystals or beam spots were measured;
   - whether the sign and size of the CD reproduced after thermal cycling through T_N;
   - whether any poling (magnetic or electric field cooling, possible in this magnetoelectric polar magnet) was used.
   The sign comparison with the simulation (Fig. 2a against Figs. 2b, 2c) is only meaningful if the domain is known. Otherwise the agreement should be described as agreement in magnitude and line shape, not sign. A reproducibility statement, and a sign comparison after field-cooling in opposite fields if feasible, would settle this.

4. The comparison between the model and the data is qualitative, though the text calls it quantitative. Section "Spin-orbital excitations" states that "the measured CD is quantitatively reproduced by an atomic (impurity) model." However:
   - the Weiss field is a free parameter set to "around 3–5 meV, which appears to be a reasonable value" (SI p. 6) without independent justification, for example from T_N, the measured magnon energies or published exchange constants;
   - at the T_d site, the ordered moment itself depends strongly on this field (Van Vleck magnetism; (S_c, L_c) changes from (1.29, 0.30) to (1.80, 0.35) between 1 and 5 meV);
   - the Weiss field acts only on spin, which the SI admits is a simplification for a ground state with active orbital moments.
   The authors should overlay the measured and calculated CD difference spectra for features A and B (after resolution broadening) for the chosen Weiss field, report the ratio of CD to intensity in each case, and show how that ratio changes with the Weiss field. Otherwise they should soften "quantitatively reproduced" to "qualitatively reproduced."

5. The assignment of the CD at feature A to O_h spin-orbital excitations rests on a fit that sits close to the elastic line. Feature A (~35 and ~70 meV) lies within roughly 1.5 resolution widths (45 meV FWHM) of the elastic peak. The (2+1)-component fit (Fig. S5, SI §3.2) is performed for RCP only, at Q = (0,0,L), not at the Q used for the CD data. The CD itself is never decomposed. The authors should fit RCP and LCP separately at the CD momentum transfer, with the elastic width fixed to the carbon-tape reference, and show that the dichroic weight sits in the inelastic components and not in the quasi-elastic line. This matters more given the elastic-line difference seen in the m-plane data (concern 1).
   Separately, the claim that "the resulting energy spacing follows a 2:3 ratio" (p. 5) is not borne out by Fig. S5d. Peak energies of ~31–38 meV and ~68–73 meV imply spacings of roughly 33 and 37 meV (close to 1:1). The Landé 2:3 pattern would require peak 1 at about 28 meV if peak 2 is at 70 meV. SI p. 6 instead attributes the two features at 35 and 70 meV to a trigonal distortion Δ_tri = ±30 meV. The authors should reconcile these two explanations and state which one they adopt.

6. What the measurement establishes about "bi-altermagnetism" should be stated more precisely. The magnetic point group 6′m′m′ and the collinear Néel structure with L ∥ c are known from earlier neutron and magnetization work. Given that structure, the altermagnetic character of each sublattice pair follows from symmetry. The new experimental content is that the CD appears in excitations localized on each site and obeys the m/m′ selection rule. Two points deserve discussion:
   - Any compensated collinear order with the same magnetic point group would give the same selection rules, so the data test the magnetic symmetry, not "bi-altermagnetism" as a separate physical property.
   - Within a single sublattice pair, CD at a given site could also arise from the exchange field induced by the other sublattice. This is especially relevant at the T_d site, whose moment is of Van Vleck type and is induced by the Weiss field.
   The authors partly acknowledge this on p. 4. The abstract ("support the realization of bi-altermagnetism") and the closing of the CD section ("supporting the realization of bi-altermagnetism") would read more accurately as evidence for symmetry breaking at both sublattices that is consistent with the bi-altermagnetic magnetic point group.

7. There is no experimental test against the ferrimagnetic alternative. The simulations (Fig. S9) predict sizable m-plane CD in the ferrimagnetic phase, which can be reached in Fe2Mo3O8 with a few-tesla field along c or by Zn doping. A measurement in that phase, even at one temperature, would provide a positive control of the m-plane selection rule with the same sample and geometry. It would also show that the m-plane null in the altermagnetic phase is not simply a lack of sensitivity in that geometry. If this is beyond the present study, the authors should state it as a limitation.

C. Minor

8. The value of the spin-orbit constant is inconsistent. The caption of Fig. 4a uses ζ = 52 meV (atomic value), while SI C2 adopts ξ_3d = 65 meV for the CD simulations at both sites. The text should state which value is used where, and why the T_d fine structure requires a 25% enhancement that is then also applied to the O_h site.

9. The momentum transfers and geometries differ between figures: Q = (0.05, 0, 0.65) with θ = 26.26° (Figs. 1e, 2, S2) against Q = (0.03, 0, 0.66) with θ = 30° (Figs. 3, S3, S4), both with ω = 70°. The 32 K spectra in Fig. 3a and Fig. S3a, nominally at the same Q, differ slightly at the elastic peak (equal intensities in Fig. 3a; LCP > RCP in Fig. S3a). The authors should state whether these are the same scans and why the geometry was changed.

10. Data normalization (SI §3.1) uses the 5–7 eV energy-loss region. The authors should state explicitly that this region has no dichroism (that is, that fluorescence-like weight carries no CD, consistent with the symmetry-forbidden XMCD), and give the size of the normalization correction applied.

11. The derivation of ζ′ = ζ/4 (SI §5) evaluates ⟨l·s⟩ on a single Slater determinant written in real-orbital labels (↓_xy) while assigning m_l = −1. The authors should use the complex-orbital basis consistently, and comment on the T–P equivalence sign that sets the J_eff = 1 ground state. The main text quotes an orbital moment "about 20% of the spin moment" without saying which site and which Weiss field this refers to (the SI values for T_d give 23% at 1 meV and 19% at 5 meV).

12. The CD at the O_h feature C is said to be negligible because of lifetime broadening and wave-function character (p. 4), but the simulated CD at C (Fig. 2b; Fig. S7f–h) should be compared with the data to show that this agrees with the model and is not a discrepancy.

13. The code is available "upon reasonable request." Given that the conclusions lean on the simulations, the authors should consider depositing the model Hamiltonian parameters and the input files for the CD calculations with the Zenodo data.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a main-text figure of the CD difference spectra (m′ and m geometries) with error bars, plus a panel of integrated CD for features A and B against temperature from 32 to 80 K.
- Overlay the experimental and simulated CD difference spectra, with the simulated ones broadened to resolution, and show how sensitive they are to the Weiss field and to Δ_tri.
- Report reproducibility on a second crystal or beam spot and after thermal cycling, and discuss the domain state. If possible, field-cool in ±B to reverse the CD sign.
- If feasible, measure the field-induced ferrimagnetic phase in the m-plane geometry as a positive control of the selection rule.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
