Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use circular dichroism in Fe L3-edge RIXS (RIXS-CD) to look for altermagnetic symmetry breaking in Fe2Mo3O8. The material has two magnetic Fe2+ sublattices: quasi-octahedral (Oh) and tetrahedral (Td). The key observations are these:
- In the m' scattering geometry, a CD appears below T_N = 60 K on the Td crystal-field excitation (feature B, about 0.5 eV) and on a low-energy spin-orbital feature attributed to Oh (feature A, about 70 meV).
- The CD is absent in the m (glide) geometry, as the 6'mm' magnetic point group requires.
- An ionic multiplet model with a staggered Weiss field reproduces the main CD features.

The method gives site-resolved access to symmetry breaking in a compensated magnet where XMCD and the anomalous Hall effect are symmetry-forbidden. Measuring RIXS-CD on dd excitations across T_N in a low-T_N altermagnet is new and broadly interesting. The label "bi-altermagnet" is mostly a symmetry statement about a known magnetic structure. The experimental contribution is to show the corresponding dichroism resolved by site.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The experimental design rests on a clear symmetry argument. In the m' geometry, CD is forbidden in the paramagnetic phase even though RIXS-CD is not constrained by time reversal alone. The m-geometry measurement in the ordered phase is a real null control (Fig. 2a inset, Fig. S2 lower panel).
- The temperature series across T_N, from 32 to 80 K for both the low-energy and dd regions (Figs. 3, S3, S4), shows the CD switching on below T_N.
- The dd features are separated by site (Td about 0.5 eV, Oh about 1.0 eV), so dichroic contributions can be assigned to sublattices.
- The model parameters (crystal field, J_H, SOC) are fixed from the RIXS spectra without using the CD (SI Sec. 4). The ferrimagnetic comparison (Fig. S9) gives a falsifiable selection-rule difference.
- The data are deposited on Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The central claim (magnetic-order-induced, symmetry-allowed RIXS-CD on both sublattices, absent in the m geometry) is supported well enough for the conclusions drawn. The items below would sharpen it.

B. Should address

1. **The evidence for symmetry breaking on the Oh sublattice rests on one unresolved shoulder, and the model's Oh-site CD prediction is not borne out at feature C.**
   - The "bi" claim needs CD on both sublattices. The Td side is carried by feature B. The Oh side is carried only by feature A, a shoulder at roughly 50–150 meV that sits within about one resolution width (45 meV FWHM) of the elastic line.
   - Several other contributions overlap this window. Fig. S7(a) shows Td-site levels reaching about 20–40 meV for Weiss fields of 3–5 meV, inside the window of peak 1 in Fig. S5. Electromagnons and magnons (whose chiral splitting in altermagnets gives its own CD; Refs. 44, 45) and phonons also lie here.
   - The only Oh dd feature that is resolved, C at 1.0 eV, shows no measurable CD. The authors' own Oh simulation predicts a visible CD there (RCP above LCP at 1.0 eV in Fig. 2b and Fig. S7(f–h)). The text attributes the absence to "large lifetime broadening" but does not demonstrate this.

   What the authors should do:
   - (i) Show a simulation of feature C with a realistic final-state broadening that removes the CD at C while keeping the CD at A and B.
   - (ii) Show the CD of feature A as a function of incident photon energy across the L3 edge, compared with the calculated Oh and Td resonance profiles. Fig. 1e/f already shows that the two sites resonate at different incident energies.
   - (iii) Show difference spectra (LCP − RCP) in the 0–0.3 eV window and compare them with the Oh-only and Td-only calculated difference spectra.

   Without this, the Oh-site CD is model-assigned rather than experimentally site-resolved, and the abstract and conclusion should say so.

2. **The CD is never quantified, so "vanishes within experimental uncertainty" cannot be judged.**
   - All CD data are overlays of normalized RCP and LCP spectra (Figs. 2, 3, S2–S4). There are no difference spectra, no integrated asymmetry and no error bars.
   - The m-geometry null in Fig. S2 (lower panel) shows a visible LCP/RCP difference at the elastic line (about 95 vs 88) and small differences near 0.5 eV. Its size relative to the m'-geometry CD on features A and B needs to be stated.
   - Normalization is two-step: slit current, then the 5–7 eV loss weight with an LCP/RCP flux ratio of 1.1 (SI Sec. 3.1). Yet absolute intensities vary noticeably between temperatures. For example, feature B is about 14 at 80 K (Fig. S4h) against about 21 at 70 K (Fig. S4g), and the elastic line is lower at 50 K (Fig. S3c).

   What the authors should do:
   - Plot an integrated CD asymmetry for features A and B against temperature (32–80 K), with uncertainties from repeated polarization switching and counting statistics.
   - Include the same quantity for the m geometry, for feature C, and for the paramagnetic spectra as a measured systematic floor.
   - Compare the temperature dependence with the sublattice magnetization from the literature. The CD is already largely suppressed at 55 K (Figs. S3d, S4d), where the order parameter should still be substantial. Possible reasons include beam heating, domain effects or a sample-temperature offset; say which applies and whether the sample temperature was calibrated under the beam.
   - State whether the RCP and LCP spectra were interleaved, and over what time.

3. **Magnetic domains, number of samples and reproducibility.**
   - The two altermagnetic domains (L and −L) should give CD of opposite sign. A finite CD over a beam footprint of about 386 × 30 µm therefore requires a strong domain imbalance. The paper does not discuss this, and the model assumes a single domain.
   - Only one crystal is shown (Fig. S1). It is not stated whether the CD was reproduced on other spots, after thermal cycling through T_N, or on another sample.

   What the authors should do:
   - Report reproducibility across spots, cooling cycles and samples.
   - Discuss why one domain dominates, for example coupling to polar domains or to the cooling history.
   - Ideally, show a sign reversal of the CD for the opposite domain. Options include magnetoelectric field cooling, or an azimuthal rotation equivalent to L → −L under 6'mm' (for example 180° about c, which maps through the 2' operation).

   A sign reversal would also be the most direct evidence that the CD is odd in the Néel vector. It would make the comparison between the measured and calculated CD magnitudes meaningful.

4. **The model has a free Weiss-field amplitude, and the comparison with data is only qualitative.**
   - The staggered field is restricted to −S·B_eff along c. Its magnitude of 3–5 meV is described as "reasonable" (SI p. 6) without independent justification.
   - At the Td site the moment depends strongly on this field: (S_c, L_c) runs from (1.29, 0.30) to (1.80, 0.35) for 1 to 5 meV. That dependence underlies both the Van Vleck interpretation and the CD amplitude.
   - The simulations use ξ_3d = 65 meV (SI C2), while Fig. 4a and the main text quote ζ = 52 meV.

   What the authors should do:
   - Compare the model's sublattice moments with neutron-diffraction values.
   - State which Weiss field was used in Fig. 2b,c.
   - Give the calculated against measured CD amplitudes for A and B as numbers. The calculated CD scales with the Weiss field, so a mean-field temperature dependence could be compared directly with the item-2 curve.
   - Use one SOC value consistently, or explain the difference.

5. **The claimed 2:3 ratio of the J_eff spacings is not what the fit gives.**
   - The main text states that the two-component fit of feature A "follows a 2:3 ratio" (Fig. S5). Fig. S5(d), however, gives peak 1 at about 31–38 meV and peak 2 at about 68–73 meV. The spacings (J1→J2 about 33 meV, J2→J3 about 37 meV) are close to 1:1.
   - A 2:3 ratio would place peak 1 at about 28 meV for peak 2 at 70 meV. With ζ = 52 meV, first-order theory gives 26 and 65 meV. With the 65 meV used in the simulations, it gives about 33 and 81 meV.
   - The SI states that the decomposition is a "constrained minimal" one and not evidence of resolved components. Section 4 also invokes a trigonal distortion of ±30 meV to match features at 35 and 70 meV, which would itself break the simple J_eff ladder.
   - The fits are at Q = (0,0,L) with RCP only, and no temperature is given.

   What the authors should do:
   - Soften the statement to "consistent with a J_eff manifold split by SOC and a small trigonal field".
   - Report the fit parameters, including widths, with the temperature.
   - Remove the 2:3 claim, or show that it survives the fitting uncertainties.

6. **What RIXS-CD establishes about altermagnetism, and the orbital-magnetism claim.**
   - RIXS-CD on dd excitations is a relativistic probe. It tests the magnetic point group (breaking of m but not of m'), not the nonrelativistic spin-group classification that defines altermagnetism.
   - The bi-altermagnetic assignment therefore relies on the known collinear Néel structure from neutron scattering. The data exclude the ferrimagnetic state through the m-geometry null, but not other compensated orders with the same magnetic point group.
   - It also remains open whether the Td site is "independently" altermagnetic or carries a moment induced by the exchange field of the Oh sublattice. The paper itself describes Van Vleck-type magnetism at Td.
   - The statement that Fe2Mo3O8 is "a rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism", with an orbital moment of about 20% of the spin moment, comes from the ionic model, not from a measurement.

   What the authors should do:
   - Spell out the spin-group and site-symmetry analysis showing that each Wyckoff orbit separately satisfies the altermagnetic criteria.
   - State explicitly which alternative magnetic states the m/m' selection rules exclude.
   - Phrase the orbital-moment statement as a model result.

C. Minor

7. **Wrong figure reference.** Main text p. 3 cites "Fig. S6(a,e)" for the Td spin-orbital excitation lying below 50 meV. The relevant panels appear to be Fig. S7(a,e).

8. **Measurement conditions for the m-geometry data.** Fig. S2 (lower panel) and the inset of Fig. 2a should state Q, θ and ω, and whether the same sample spot was used. This is needed to judge whether the null is a matched comparison.

9. **Residual linear polarization.** The EPU phase error is about 3.5°, giving a circular polarization degree above 98% (SI Sec. 2). Magnetic linear dichroism also switches on at T_N. Briefly estimate the upper bound on any apparent CD arising from residual linear polarization that differs between the two helicities.

10. **SI Section 5 derivation.** The text is garbled ("or the e_g level, both orbitals are filled…"). It assigns real t2g orbitals (xy, yz, zx) to m_l eigenstates without stating the T–P equivalence and its sign of the effective orbital momentum. The g = 5/2 estimate ignores the orbital reduction or projection factor. Notation switches between ^5T_2 and ^5T_2g. The final splittings (ζ/2, 3ζ/4) agree with standard results, but the derivation should be cleaned up or replaced by a citation.

11. **Fig. 1e/f colour scales.** The scales differ (0–80 against 0–150), and the calculated map overweights feature B relative to the elastic line. Note this, or use matched normalization, so readers do not over-read the agreement.

12. **Main-text wording.** "We have checked that the presence or absence of CD is irrelevant with varying the spin–orbit coupling" (SI C2) should be reworded, for example to "the CD does not depend qualitatively on ξ in this range".

13. **Code availability.** The calculations use an in-house code available "upon reasonable request". Providing input files and parameter sets alongside the Zenodo data would let others reproduce the simulated CD.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Present the CD as LCP − RCP difference spectra with error bars, plus an integrated-asymmetry-versus-temperature panel for features A, B and C and for the m geometry, in the main text.
2. Map the CD as a function of incident photon energy across the L3 edge and compare it with the site-resolved simulations. This would make the site assignment of feature A experimental rather than model-based.
3. Attempt domain control (magnetoelectric cooling or azimuthal rotation) to demonstrate a sign reversal of the CD with the Néel vector.
4. If feasible, measure in the field-induced ferrimagnetic state. Fig. S9 predicts a finite CD in the m geometry there, which would directly test the selection rule that distinguishes the two states.

5. Verdict: minor revision
