A. Must address

1. **The CD is never quantified, and the m-plane null that is meant to identify bi-altermagnetism shows visible residual differences.** Every CD statement in the paper rests on overlays of normalized RCP and LCP spectra (Figs. 2a, 3, S2, S3, S4). There are no difference spectra, no integrated asymmetry and no error bars, so phrases such as "vanished within the experimental uncertainty" (p. 4) and "vanishes within the present experimental accuracy" (inset of Fig. 2a) cannot be judged. This matters most for the m-plane control, which the paper itself presents as what turns "CD below T_N" (which "does not, by itself, uniquely identify bi-altermagnetism", p. 4) into "a signature of bi-altermagnetism". That control is a single RCP/LCP overlay at 32 K and one Q (inset of Fig. 2a; Fig. S2 lower panel). In it, the elastic/quasi-elastic line differs by about 8% (LCP ~95 against RCP ~88), as large as or larger than the ~10–15% shoulder difference claimed for feature A in the m′ geometry, which sits on the flank of that same elastic line; there are also small differences on the high-energy side of feature B (~0.5 eV). Above T_N the elastic peaks also differ by a few percent (Fig. S3e–g). These residuals could be counting noise, imperfect flux normalization, a footprint or self-absorption change on azimuthal rotation, polarization-dependent specular or diffuse elastic scattering, or a slight misalignment of the scattering plane from the glide plane. Until they are quantified, the reader cannot tell whether the m-plane CD is zero to within an error much smaller than the m′-plane CD. The authors should: (i) plot difference spectra (I_LCP − I_RCP) or the normalized asymmetry for the m′ and m geometries on the same axes and at all temperatures, with error bars from counting statistics and from repeated polarization switching; (ii) give integrated CD values with uncertainties for features A, B and C in both geometries, and for the paramagnetic spectra as a measured systematic floor; (iii) ideally show the m-plane spectra above T_N as well, so that any residual is shown to be temperature-independent and instrumental; (iv) explain the elastic-line difference in the m geometry; and (v) state how many RCP/LCP cycles were averaged, whether the helicities were interleaved and over what time.

B. Should address

2. **The evidence for symmetry breaking on the Oh sublattice rests on one unresolved shoulder near the elastic line, and the model's Oh-site CD prediction is not seen at feature C.** The "bi" claim needs CD on both sublattices. The Td side is carried by feature B (~0.5 eV, CD ~15% at the peak). The Oh side is carried only by feature A, a shoulder at roughly 35–150 meV that lies within about 1–1.5 resolution widths (45 meV FWHM) of the elastic line. Several other contributions overlap this window: Td spin-orbital levels (below 50 meV, reaching ~20–40 meV for Weiss fields of 3–5 meV, Fig. S7a, inside the window of peak 1 in Fig. S5), magnons (10–15 meV, whose chiral splitting in altermagnets gives its own CD; Refs. 44, 45), electromagnons and phonons. The two-component fit of A (Fig. S5, SI Sec. 3.2) is admitted to be a "constrained minimal decomposition", is done for RCP only, and at Q = (0,0,L) rather than at the Q of the CD data; the CD itself is never decomposed. Meanwhile the only resolved Oh dd feature, C at ~1.0 eV, shows no measurable CD, although the authors' own Oh simulation predicts visible CD there (RCP above LCP at 1.0 eV in Fig. 2b; Figs. S7f–h, S9h); the text attributes the absence to "large lifetime broadening" (p. 4) without demonstrating it. The authors should: (i) simulate feature C with realistic final-state broadening of the 1–1.3 eV states and show that the predicted CD at C falls below the experimental noise while the CD at A and B survives; (ii) fit RCP and LCP separately at the CD momentum transfer, with the elastic width fixed to the carbon-tape reference, and show that the dichroic weight sits in the inelastic components and not in the quasi-elastic line; (iii) show quantitatively that the Td contribution at A's energies carries no CD for the Weiss fields used (Fig. 2c suggests this); (iv) compare the measured LCP − RCP difference in the 0–0.3 eV window with the Oh-only and Td-only calculated difference spectra; and (v) ideally, measure the CD of feature A as a function of incident photon energy across the L3 edge and compare it with the calculated Oh and Td resonance profiles, since Fig. 1e/f shows that the two sites resonate at different incident energies. Without this, the Oh-site CD is model-assigned rather than experimentally site-resolved, and the abstract and conclusion should say so.

3. **Magnetic domains, number of samples and reproducibility are not addressed, and no sign reversal is shown.** The CD is odd under time reversal, so the two altermagnetic domains (L and −L) give CD of opposite sign. A finite CD over a beam footprint of about 386 × 30 µm² on a ~1.26–1.3 mm crystal (SI Sec. 2, Fig. S1) therefore requires a strong domain imbalance, and a multi-domain state would reduce the CD and could make its sign depend on cooling history and beam position. The model assumes a single domain. Only one crystal is shown, and the paper does not state how many crystals or beam spots were measured, whether the sign and size of the CD reproduced after thermal cycling through T_N, or how the sample was cooled (zero field, or magnetic or electric field cooling, which is natural in this polar magnetoelectric compound). The sign comparison with the simulation (Fig. 2a against Figs. 2b, 2c) and any comparison of CD magnitudes are only meaningful if the domain state is known; otherwise agreement should be described as agreement in line shape and magnitude, not sign. The authors should report reproducibility across spots, cooldowns and samples, discuss why one domain dominates (for example coupling to polar domains or cooling history), and ideally show a reversal of the CD sign for the opposite domain, by magnetoelectric field cooling in opposite fields or by a geometry related through the 6′mm′ operations (for example a 180° rotation about c via 2′, or reflection of the scattering plane through m′). A sign reversal would be the most direct evidence that the signal is odd in the Néel vector.

4. **What the measurement establishes about "bi-altermagnetism" should be stated more precisely.** The collinear Néel structure with L ∥ c and the magnetic point group are known from earlier neutron and magnetization work; given that structure, the altermagnetic character of each Oh pair and each Td pair (related by the 6_3 screw) follows from symmetry. RIXS-CD on dd excitations is a relativistic probe: the m/m′ selection rule tests the global magnetic point group, not the nonrelativistic spin-group classification that defines altermagnetism, nor whether each sublattice is "independently" altermagnetic. The m-geometry null excludes the ferrimagnetic state, but any compensated collinear order with the same magnetic point group would give the same selection rules. It also remains open whether the CD at the Td site reflects its own altermagnetic order or a moment induced by the exchange field of the Oh sublattice, which the paper itself describes as Van Vleck-type. The authors should spell out the spin-group and site-symmetry analysis showing that each Wyckoff orbit separately satisfies the altermagnetic criteria, state explicitly which alternative magnetic states the m/m′ selection rules exclude and what a non-bi-altermagnetic compensated alternative would look like in their data, and consider a sublattice-resolved test beyond the point group: the relative sign of the CD at A and at B, which is fixed by whether the Oh and Td moments are parallel or antiparallel, shown to be reproduced by the model. Otherwise the abstract ("support the realization of bi-altermagnetism") and the close of the CD section should be rephrased as evidence for symmetry breaking at both sublattices consistent with the bi-altermagnetic magnetic point group.

5. **The model has a free Weiss-field amplitude, the comparison with data is qualitative though called quantitative, and the orbital-magnetism claim is a model result.** The text says "the measured CD is quantitatively reproduced by an atomic (impurity) model", but no CD magnitudes are compared. The staggered field acts only on spin (−S·B_eff along c), a simplification for a ground state with active orbital moments, and its size of 3–5 meV is called "a reasonable value" (SI p. 6) without justification from T_N, magnon energies or published exchange constants. At the Td site the ordered moment depends strongly on this field ((S_c, L_c) from (1.29, 0.30) at 1 meV to (1.80, 0.35) at 5 meV), which underlies both the Van Vleck interpretation and the CD amplitude. The statement that Fe2Mo3O8 is "a rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism", with an orbital moment "about 20% of the spin moment", comes entirely from this model and does not say which site or Weiss field it refers to (the SI gives 23% at 1 meV and 19% at 5 meV for Td). The authors should state which Weiss field was used in Fig. 2b,c; compare the model's sublattice moments ((S_c, L_c) = (1.67, 0.35) at Td, 5/2 µB for the Oh J_eff = 1 state) with neutron-diffraction values; overlay measured and calculated CD difference spectra for A and B (broadened to resolution) and give the CD-to-intensity ratio in each case and its dependence on the Weiss field; and either support "quantitatively reproduced" or soften it to "qualitatively reproduced" and phrase the orbital-moment statement as a model result.

6. **The temperature dependence of the CD is not compared with the order parameter, and its early suppression is unexplained.** The "direct link" to magnetic order would be much stronger with the integrated CD of A and B plotted against temperature from 32 to 80 K (from Figs. 3, S3, S4) with uncertainties, compared with the sublattice magnetization from neutron data and with the Weiss-field dependence of the model (Fig. S7). The CD is already strongly suppressed and hard to distinguish from the 60–80 K data at 55 K (Figs. S3d, S4d), where the order parameter should still be substantial. Possible causes include beam heating under the focused beam (>10^15 photons/s into ~30 µm vertically), domain effects or a sample-temperature offset; the authors should say which applies and whether the sample temperature was calibrated under the beam. The comparison would also show whether the CD at A and at B scale in the same way, as expected if both sublattice pairs follow the same Néel order.

7. **The spin-orbital (J_eff) analysis is internally inconsistent: the claimed 2:3 ratio is not what the fit gives, and two SOC values are used.** The main text (p. 5) says the two-component fit of feature A "follows a 2:3 ratio" compatible with J_eff splittings of ζ/2 and 3ζ/4. Fig. S5d gives peak 1 at ~31–38 meV and peak 2 at ~68–73 meV, so the intervals are ~33 and ~37 meV (close to 1:1) and the excitation energies are in a ratio of ~0.5 (first order predicts 2:5); a 2:3 ratio would place peak 1 at ~28 meV for peak 2 at 70 meV. Fig. 4a and the main text use ζ = 52 meV, predicting levels at 24–26 and 58–65 meV, below both fitted peaks, while the CD simulations at both sites use ξ_3d = 65 meV (SI C2), which gives ~33 and ~81 meV; the reason for a 25% enhancement needed for the Td fine structure and then applied also to Oh is not given. SI Sec. 4 instead attributes the features at 35 and 70 meV to a trigonal distortion Δ_tri = ±30 meV, which itself breaks the simple J_eff ladder. The fits are RCP only, at Q = (0,0,L), with no temperature stated. The authors should reconcile the two explanations and state which they adopt; use one SOC value consistently or state which value is used where and why; include second-order, trigonal (Fig. S8a) and Weiss-field (Fig. S7e) shifts; report the fit parameters including widths and temperature; and either remove the 2:3 claim or show it survives the fitting uncertainties, softening to "consistent with a J_eff manifold split by SOC and a small trigonal field".

8. **The robustness of the LCP/RCP normalization is not shown.** Spectra are normalized to slit current and then to the integrated weight at 5–7 eV energy loss, with an LCP/RCP source flux ratio of 1.1 (SI Sec. 3.1), so the normalization changes the raw data by ~10%, comparable to the CD itself. Absolute intensities also vary noticeably between temperatures: feature B is ~14 at 80 K (Fig. S4h) against ~21–22 at 70 K (Fig. S4g) on the same axes, a ~30% drop across all dd intensities, and the elastic line is lower at 50 K (Fig. S3c). The authors should state explicitly that the 5–7 eV region carries no dichroism (consistent with symmetry-forbidden XMCD), give the size of the correction applied, explain the temperature-dependent intensity changes, and show that the CD at A and B, and its absence at C and in the m geometry, survive alternative normalizations (for example total integrated intensity or the weight of feature C).

9. **There is no experimental positive control of the m-plane selection rule.** The simulations (Fig. S9) predict sizable m-plane CD in the ferrimagnetic phase, which in Fe2Mo3O8 can be reached with a few-tesla field along c or by Zn doping. A measurement in that phase, even at one temperature and in the same sample and geometry, would directly test the selection rule that distinguishes the two states and show that the m-plane null in the altermagnetic phase is not a lack of sensitivity in that geometry. If this is beyond the present study, the authors should state it as a limitation.
