A. Must address

1. The CD and both null tests are judged by eye from overlaid RCP/LCP spectra, with no stated normalization, no difference spectra and no uncertainties. The paper says the CD "vanished within the experimental uncertainty" above T_N (p. 3) and that the m-plane CD "vanishes within the present experimental accuracy" (p. 4), but no uncertainty is given anywhere. Visible helicity differences appear where CD should be absent: in the m-plane null (Fig. S1, 32 K) the elastic/low-energy peak differs by about 8% between RCP and LCP (about 95 vs 88), with a slight difference also at the 0.42 eV peak; small differences remain at 60–65 K near 0.45–0.5 eV (Fig. S3e,f) and at the elastic line at 70 K (Fig. 3d, Fig. S2g); and the overall dd intensity drops from about 21 to about 15 at 80 K (Fig. S3h). These residuals are of the same order as part of the claimed CD at feature B (Fig. 2a, Fig. 3e). Possible artifacts are not addressed: the normalization (incident flux, counting time, elastic line, total dd weight), the degree of circular polarization of each helicity and whether residual linear components differ between them (magnetic linear dichroism also switches on at T_N, so a helicity-dependent linear admixture could mimic a T_N-locked "CD"), how helicity switching is done at beamline 41A (undulator phase or other) and the time between RCP and LCP acquisitions, and beam position or footprint changes on switching. Since the positive claim and both null claims rest on these comparisons, the authors should state the normalization and polarization degree; plot I_RCP − I_LCP (or the asymmetry) with statistical error bars for the m' geometry at each temperature, above and below T_N, and for the m geometry, showing the m-plane and paramagnetic residuals are consistent with zero; state the number of scans averaged and the scan-to-scan reproducibility; and plot the integrated CD of features A and B separately against temperature on one figure, with errors, compared with the sublattice magnetization from neutron data or with the authors' own magnetization, susceptibility or specific-heat determination of T_N on the same crystal (none is shown). An order-parameter-like CD(T) for both A and B vanishing at the same T_N would be the strongest support for the central claim, and different temperature dependences for the Oh and Td features would be informative about the "bi-" character.

2. The Oh half of the claim rests on assigning feature A to Oh spin-orbital excitations, and this assignment is established only by the model. The only Oh-specific dd feature with a clear experimental signature, feature C (about 1.0 eV, ⁵E/³T1), shows negligible CD, which the authors attribute to lifetime broadening (p. 4); so Oh-sublattice CD comes from feature A alone, a broad shoulder at about 30–150 meV on the tail of the elastic line at 45 meV total resolution. In that window lie Td-site excitations extending up to about 40–45 meV once the Weiss field is on (Fig. S6a), magnons (chiral magnon splitting was itself the source of RIXS-CD in MnTe, Refs. 42, 43), and phonons and Td orbital excitations known from THz/IR work (Ref. 37). The statement that Td "does not yield significant CD" there is a model result (Fig. S6b–d). The S4 fit (elastic Voigt plus two free Lorentzians, no error bars, few constraints) is underdetermined at this resolution, and its lower peak at ~35 meV sits close to the elastic line and to the Td and magnon range. The authors should: measure the incident-energy dependence of the CD of feature A across the Oh and Td resonances, which fall at different incident energies (Fig. 1e,f; Td lower), and show that it follows the Oh resonance, comparing with calculated site-resolved CD maps; show the simulated Oh and Td contributions to the 0–150 meV CD separately, convolved with the 45 meV resolution and on the same intensity scale as Fig. 2a; show the CD separately for the 35 and 70 meV components, with fit constraints (fixed widths? fixed elastic width?) and parameter uncertainties; and discuss magnon contributions to the CD at A. Without this, the data establish CD at the Td sublattice but not independently at the Oh sublattice.

3. The CD is not shown to be specific to altermagnetic order of each sublattice, which the title and abstract say it "unveils". In the m'-plane geometry CD appears whenever magnetic order breaks the pure mirror m present above T_N; any order with moments along c does this, including order with a net moment such as the ferrimagnetic stacking Fe2Mo3O8 adopts under field or Zn doping (Refs. 35, 36). That each sublattice is altermagnetic (opposite-spin sites related by the 6_3 screw rather than by translation or inversion) follows from the known neutron magnetic structure and symmetry analysis, not from the RIXS-CD; as written ("identify a new form of altermagnetism", "compelling evidence for bi-altermagnetism"), the paper implies a discrimination it has not shown, and the claim is not tested by any measurement that could have come out otherwise. The simulation sums local contributions from two opposite-spin sublattices per site class (Methods; SI p. 6) but never shows the per-sublattice CD or explains why the up- and down-sublattice contributions add rather than cancel, which is exactly where rotation-related order, as opposed to a translation- or PT-related antiferromagnet, would enter. The authors should show the CD of each sublattice and its sum for each site class; compute the CD for at least one non-altermagnetic reference configuration (for example ferromagnetically stacked Oh layers as in the ferrimagnetic phase, a sublattice whose partners are related by inversion, which should give cancelling CD, or two opposite-spin sites with identical local orientation); and state which observable features (sign pattern between A and B, Q dependence, sign reversal between sublattices) are unique to the altermagnetic arrangement. Otherwise the claim should be narrowed to: magnetic order breaks the m mirror at both Fe sites, consistent with the bi-altermagnetic structure inferred from the known magnetic symmetry.

B. Should address

4. The magnetic domain state, the CD sign and reproducibility are not discussed. Opposite Néel domains (L ∥ +c and −c) give CD of opposite sign, so a net CD requires domain imbalance in the illuminated spot, and its size depends on that imbalance. The paper does not say how the sample was cooled (zero field, residual or applied field and its direction, or magnetoelectric/polar selection, possible since Fe2Mo3O8 is polar and magnetoelectric), the beam spot size, whether the CD sign and magnitude reproduce over thermal cycles, spots and crystals, how many crystals were measured, or whether the m-plane control (Fig. S1) was taken on the same crystal, spot and cool-down as the m'-plane data. A decisive test would be CD sign reversal for both A and B after reversing L, by field cooling in opposite fields or spatial mapping (as in the MnTe RIXS-CD work, Ref. 40); alternatively, since 6' (C6 combined with T) relates neighboring m' planes, a 60° azimuthal rotation about c should reverse the CD in a single-domain region. Either would rule out instrumental helicity artifacts more strongly than the m-plane null, test the predicted locking of the Oh and Td signs, and, with a known domain, allow the measured CD signs at A and B to be compared with the calculation. At a minimum, report the cooling protocol, number of samples and cool-downs, and reproducibility of the sign.

5. The model–experiment comparison is qualitative and the Weiss field is set by hand. The Weiss field acts only on spin (H = −S·B_eff, SI p. 6), and its size (1, 3, 5 meV; Fig. S6) is called "reasonable" without justification; a mean-field estimate from T_N = 60 K or the exchange constants from neutron scattering (Ref. 44) should fix it. The Oh ground state carries unquenched orbital moment, so a spin-only exchange field is a real approximation whose effect on the CD line shape should be checked. Fig. 2b,c and Fig. S6 show only RCP/LCP overlays, and the main text says only that the calculation "reproduce[s] the experimentally observed CD". The authors should overlay calculated and measured CD difference spectra on a common energy axis and scale after resolution convolution; compare, feature by feature, the sign and relative magnitude of the CD at A and B and the lineshape within B (the experimental double structure at 0.43/0.5 eV, Fig. 2a, against Fig. 2c); show whether the model reproduces the absence of CD at C and the m-plane null; state the scattering angles used in the simulation and confirm they match Q = (0.05, 0, 0.65); and state how the CD sign maps onto the sign of L and whether the relative sign of A and B is fixed by the model. A sign mismatch between sites would challenge the interpretation; a match would strengthen it. The statement that the agreement "demonstrates that the excitations exhibit a localized character" (p. 4) is circular, since the model assumes localization, and should be toned down.

6. The energy-ratio argument for the J_eff assignment of feature A does not match the numbers. The text (p. 5) says the fitted quintet and septet energies have a ratio "close to 2:3—consistent with the prediction of the zero-order approximation (see Fig. S4)". Fig. S4d gives peak 1 at about 31–38 meV and peak 2 at about 68–73 meV, a ratio of about 1:2. The first-order prediction for excitations from the J_eff = 1 ground triplet (Fig. 4a) is ζ/2 : 5ζ/4 = 2:5; 2:3 is the ratio of successive splittings (ζ/2 : 3ζ/4), and the measured successive splittings (about 35 and 35 meV) are about 1:1. The absolute energies also lie above the multiplet values in the Fig. 4 caption for ζ = 52 meV (23.7–25.6 and 58.1–64.8 meV), while the SI simulations use ξ_3d = 65 meV. This may reflect the Weiss field, Δ_tri and the larger SOC, but the authors should state which ratio is meant, give fitted energies with uncertainties, reconcile the ζ values, and compare with the model including the exchange field rather than claim agreement with 2:3. This matters because the Oh assignment of feature A (concern 2) rests on this spin-orbital interpretation.

7. "Bi-altermagnetism" as a distinct concept needs a sharper definition. In P6_3mc the Oh and Td Fe ions each form a single Wyckoff orbit whose two members are related by the 6_3 screw and c-glide, so any compensated collinear order with L ∥ c that is antiparallel within each orbit makes each orbit altermagnetic by symmetry. The Oh and Td moments order at a single T_N, are exchange-locked, and belong to one magnetic point group 6'mm', which is a property of the whole crystal rather than of each sublattice; the magnetic structure on both sites is already known from neutron diffraction (Refs. 35, 36). "Bi-altermagnet" thus describes any altermagnet with magnetic ions on two inequivalent orbits, which many known altermagnets have. The authors should define what distinguishes a bi-altermagnet from an ordinary altermagnet with two magnetic orbits and what physical consequence follows (observables depending on the relative sign or magnitude of the two sublattice order parameters, separately tunable spin splittings, possible independent ordering under doping, distinct contributions to the spin splitting); state which alternative scenario the RIXS-CD data exclude; and discuss the ferrimagnetic character of each Fe layer (Oh and Td moments of different size, antiparallel within a layer) and the nearby ferrimagnetic phase under Zn doping or field. Unless a stronger distinction is shown, the title and abstract should present the result as site-resolved confirmation of altermagnetic symmetry breaking on both sublattices.

8. The momentum dependence of the CD is not shown. CD is reported only at Q = (0.05, 0, 0.65) (Fig. 2) and (0.03, 0, 0.66) (Fig. 3); the dispersion in Fig. S4 is measured at (0, 0, L) with RCP only. The authors should show the CD at several L values, and at ±H if the geometry allows, and its azimuthal dependence at fixed temperature; this would support the local-excitation interpretation, help separate magnon contributions from feature A, and could turn the symmetry argument into a positive test of the characteristic angular dependence of altermagnetic CD.

9. Ground-state and orbital-magnetism claims rest on the model, and the SI derivation of the Oh moment appears incorrect. The claims of "active orbital magnetism" (orbital moment about 20% of spin) and that Fe2Mo3O8 is a "rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism" (p. 5) are model outputs with no measurement. The "Van Vleck-type" Td moment rests on an ionic calculation in which a 1 meV Weiss field already induces S_c = 1.29 (SI p. 7) and depends strongly on the assumed field; it should be checked against the measured Td ordered moment and the Td-site SOC splitting the paper places "well below 50 meV". SI Sec. 3 treats L_eff = 1 for t2g with orbital g-factor +1, obtaining g = 5/2, an Oh moment of 5/2 μB (Eq. 11) and an orbital moment antiparallel to spin; under the T–P equivalence the t2g effective orbital moment carries a factor −1 (about −3/2 with covalency), giving g ≈ 3.5 for the J_eff = 1 triplet with the physical orbital moment parallel to the spin, as known for Fe2+ oxides. The authors should correct the SI derivation, compare computed (S_c, L_c) with the ordered moments of both sites from neutron diffraction and with any XMCD-sum-rule or g-factor data, and present the orbital-magnetism statements as model predictions.
