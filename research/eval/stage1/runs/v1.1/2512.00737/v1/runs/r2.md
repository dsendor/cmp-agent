Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper uses circular dichroism (CD) in Fe L3-edge RIXS on the polar antiferromagnet Fe2Mo3O8 to show that site-selective excitations of both the quasi-octahedral (Oh) and tetrahedral (Td) Fe2+ sublattices become dichroic below T_N = 60 K, in a scattering geometry (m' plane) where the paramagnetic symmetry forbids CD, while CD stays absent in the m-plane geometry. Using the separation of crystal-field excitations at the two sites, the authors argue that each sublattice on its own breaks the mirror symmetry in the way an altermagnet does, and they call the compound a "bi-altermagnet". Site-resolved detection of altermagnetic symmetry breaking in a compensated magnet, with a paramagnetic reference and a symmetry-protected null geometry, is a useful extension of the RIXS-CD method recently applied to MnTe and CrSb. The conceptual novelty of "bi-altermagnetism" as a distinct form of altermagnetism is less clearly established than the experimental result.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clean symmetry design: the m'-plane geometry forbids CD in the paramagnetic phase, and the m-plane geometry forbids it in the ordered phase as well. The data follow both expectations (Fig. 2a and its inset, Fig. S1).
- Temperature dependence across T_N at eight temperatures for both the spin-orbital (feature A) and dd (feature B) regions (Fig. 3, Figs. S2, S3). Unlike the high-T_N MnTe and CrSb, this compound allows a direct paramagnetic reference.
- Site selectivity of feature B (Td 5E→5T near 0.45 eV) is well grounded, because the Oh multiplet has no states near 0.5 eV (Fig. S5d), so CD at B is a convincing Td-sublattice signal.
- The ionic-model parameters are fixed from the RIXS energies alone, without using the CD (SI Sec. 2), and the model then reproduces CD at A and B with only the sublattice Weiss field added. Robustness checks against SOC and trigonal distortion are given (Fig. S7).
- It shows a practical route to sublattice-resolved magnetic symmetry information in compensated magnets, where XMCD and the anomalous Hall effect are forbidden.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The CD and its absence are judged only by eye from overlaid RCP/LCP curves, with no stated normalization, no difference spectra and no uncertainties. Section "CD in RIXS and bi-altermagnetism" says the CD "vanished within the experimental uncertainty" above T_N, and that the m-plane CD "vanishes within the present experimental accuracy". Neither uncertainty is given. The normalization matters here. In the m-plane null spectrum (Fig. S1 lower panel) the elastic and low-energy peak differs by about 8% between RCP and LCP (about 95 vs 88), and small RCP/LCP differences remain at 60–65 K near 0.45–0.5 eV (Fig. S3e,f) and at 70 K at the elastic line (Fig. 3d, Fig. S2g). The positive claim and both null claims rest on these comparisons, so the reader needs to know what size of CD is significant. The authors should:
   - state how the RCP and LCP spectra are normalized (incident flux, total spectral weight, or a specific feature) and how polarization switching and degree of circular polarization are handled;
   - show the difference spectra (RCP − LCP), or CD asymmetry, with statistical error bars for the m' geometry at each temperature and for the m geometry;
   - plot integrated CD for feature A and feature B separately versus temperature, with errors, ideally against the known sublattice magnetization or neutron order parameter;
   - state the number of scans averaged and the scan-to-scan reproducibility.

   With this, "appears below T_N" and "absent in the m plane" become quantitative statements rather than visual ones.

B. Should address

2. The magnetic domain state and the sign of the CD are not discussed. CD from altermagnetic order reverses sign between L and −L domains, so a net CD over the beam footprint requires an imbalanced domain population. The paper does not say how the domain population was set (zero-field cooling? any residual field? polar/magnetoelectric selection?), whether the CD sign and magnitude are the same on different spots, on different cooldowns and on more than one crystal, or how many crystals were measured. A demonstration that the CD reverses sign for the opposite domain (field cooling through T_N, or spatial mapping as in the MnTe RIXS-CD work, Ref. 40) would tie the signal directly to L. Without the domain state, the experimental CD signs at A and B cannot be compared with the calculation either (concern 4). At a minimum, the number of samples and cooldowns, and the reproducibility of the sign, should be reported.

3. The data do not distinguish "bi-altermagnetism" from ordinary altermagnetic order in a compound with two magnetic Wyckoff sites. Both sublattices order at a single T_N in one magnetic point group, 6'mm'. Given that point group, every magnetic site whose up/down partners are related by the 6_3 screw/m' operation, rather than by translation or inversion, must show the same mirror-symmetry breaking. Site-resolved CD therefore confirms that both sublattices carry the ordered moment in the established structure, which is valuable. It does not show two independent altermagnetic orders, and the abstract's "new form of altermagnetism" is not tested by any measurement that could have come out otherwise. The authors should:
   - state what observable would distinguish a "bi-altermagnet" from a conventional altermagnet with two inequivalent magnetic sites, and what a negative outcome would look like (for example a sublattice whose partners are related by inversion, which should give cancelling CD);
   - otherwise phrase the conclusion as site-resolved confirmation of altermagnetic symmetry breaking on both sublattices.

   A comparison of the CD temperature dependence of A and B (whether both sublattices follow the same order parameter) would add content here.

4. The comparison between the calculated and measured CD is qualitative only. The Weiss field is a free parameter (1, 3, 5 meV; Fig. S6), and the main text says only that the calculation "reproduce[s] the experimentally observed CD". The authors should:
   - compare, feature by feature and in the same units, the sign and relative magnitude of the CD at A and at B, and the CD lineshape within B (the experimental double structure at 0.43/0.5 eV, Fig. 2a, against Fig. 2c);
   - state the scattering angles used in the simulation and confirm that they match the experiment at Q = (0.05, 0, 0.65);
   - state whether the relative sign of the A and B dichroism is fixed by the model for a given L.

   A sign mismatch between sites would challenge the interpretation, and a match would substantially strengthen it.

5. The assignment of feature A to the Oh site alone, and hence "sublattice-specific" CD at A, rests on the model and on a resolution-limited fit. Feature A lies within about 0.1 eV of the elastic line at a total resolution of 45 meV. Fig. S6a shows Td-site excitations extending up to about 45 meV for Weiss fields of 3–5 meV, and magnons also lie in this window. Chiral magnon splitting has itself been the source of RIXS-CD in MnTe (Refs. 42, 43). The statement that Td does "not yield significant CD" in this window is a model result. The authors should:
   - show the simulated Td-site contribution to the A region on the same intensity scale as Fig. 2a;
   - discuss magnon contributions to the CD at A;
   - show the CD at A at more than one Q, given that only RCP Q-dependence is shown in Fig. S4.

6. The energy-ratio argument for the J_eff assignment does not match the numbers shown. The main text says the fitted quintet and septet energies have a ratio "close to 2:3—consistent with the prediction of the zero-order approximation". Fig. S4d gives peak 1 at about 31–38 meV and peak 2 at about 68–72 meV, a ratio of about 1:2. The first-order prediction for excitation energies from the J_eff = 1 ground state is ζ/2 : 5ζ/4 = 2:5, and 2:3 is the ratio of the successive splittings (J=1→2 against J=2→3). By the same token the measured successive splittings, about 35 and 35 meV, are about 1:1. The absolute energies (about 35 and 70 meV) also differ from the atomic-multiplet values quoted in the Fig. 4 caption (23.7/25.6 and 58–65 meV). This may reflect the Weiss field, the trigonal distortion and the 65 meV SOC used in the simulation, but the text should state which ratio is meant, give fitted energies with uncertainties, and compare them with the model including the exchange field, rather than claim agreement with 2:3. The two-Lorentzian fit next to a Voigt elastic line at 45 meV resolution (Fig. S4a–c) is also underdetermined. The fit constraints (fixed widths? fixed elastic width?) and the parameter uncertainties should be given.

7. The orbital-moment claims are model-based, and the SI derivation of the Oh moment appears incorrect. SI Sec. 3 treats L_eff = 1 for the t2g manifold with an orbital g-factor of +1, obtains g = 5/2 and an Oh moment of 5/2 μB, and says the orbital moment is antiparallel to the spin. Under the T–P equivalence the t2g effective orbital moment carries a factor −1 (or about −3/2 with covalency). For high-spin d6 Fe2+ in Oh this gives g ≈ 3.5 for the J_eff = 1 triplet, with the physical orbital moment parallel to the spin, as is known for Fe2+ oxides. The main text says the orbital moment is about 20% of the spin and highlights "active orbital magnetism", yet no measurement of the orbital moment is presented. The "Van Vleck-type" Td moment (S_c, L_c in SI Sec. 2) also depends strongly on the assumed Weiss field. The authors should:
   - correct the SI derivation;
   - check that the simulated moments are consistent with the ordered moments known from neutron diffraction on Fe2Mo3O8;
   - present the orbital-magnetism statements as model predictions.

8. A direct check that XMCD in absorption is zero in the same sample and geometry is missing. A small net moment from an uncompensated ferrimagnetic minority (Fe2Mo3O8 is close to a field- and doping-induced ferrimagnetic phase, Refs. 35, 36), or from surface or defect regions, would give CD in both geometries. The m-plane null argues against this, but it is the measurement with the uncertainty noted in concern 1. TEY/TFY XAS with RCP and LCP below T_N, showing zero XMCD within error, would confirm compensation in the probed volume directly. The XAS of Fig. 1d is shown without self-absorption correction, so the probing depth and any surface-layer contribution should also be discussed.

9. The temperature calibration and beam heating are not addressed. At 55 K the CD at both A and B is already much reduced (Figs. S2d, S3d) relative to 50 K, while T_N = 60 K. This may be the order-parameter behaviour, or a sample temperature a few kelvin above the sensor reading under the beam. The thermometer location and an estimate of beam heating should be given, or T_N should be checked in situ (for example the temperature dependence of a magnetic feature in the same run).

C. Minor

10. Fig. 2 uses Q = (0.05, 0, 0.65) and Fig. 3 uses Q = (0.03, 0, 0.66). The reason for the change should be stated, along with whether the 32 K spectra in Fig. 2a and Fig. 3a,e come from the same scan.

11. Fig. 2a and Fig. S1 duplicate essentially the same data. The inset of Fig. 2a shows only the low-energy m-plane data, while the m-plane dd region, which is the key Td-site null, appears only in the SI. Bringing the full m-plane spectrum into the main figure would help.

12. The Fig. 4 caption gives ζ = 52 meV, while the simulations use ξ_3d = 65 meV (SI C2). The main text should state which value enters each comparison.

13. In "Spin-orbital excitations", the sentence after "The tetrahedral site ... " says the Weiss field "generates a large magnetic moment, indicating Van Vleck-type magnetism at the Td site" right after a discussion of the Oh singlet ground state ("While SOC leads to a singlet ground state A1"). This is confusing: the A1 singlet belongs to the Td 5E manifold, not to the Oh 5T2 manifold, whose ground state is the J_eff = 1 triplet. The sentence should be clarified.

14. The statement that the broad lineshape of feature C "is not implied by symmetry, but stems from large lifetime broadening" would benefit from a short quantitative justification, or a pointer to the simulated CD at C.

15. Fig. 1e/f: the calculated map has much narrower features than the data. The broadening used for the maps should be stated, and the text's claim that the Td resonance window is "significantly broader" should be supported by a quantitative comparison (for example a CIE cut at each feature).

16. The "Data availability" statement ("upon reasonable request") is weak for a paper whose key claims are small spectral differences. Depositing the RCP/LCP spectra would be helpful.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a figure of RCP−LCP difference spectra with error bars for the m' and m geometries, and an integrated-CD vs. temperature panel for features A and B overlaid with the sublattice order parameter.
- Demonstrate the CD sign reversal for the opposite altermagnetic domain (field cooling through T_N or spatial mapping), and use the known domain to compare the signs of the CD at A and B with the calculation.
- Overlay calculated and measured CD (difference spectra) on the same energy axis, including the separate Oh and Td contributions in the 0–0.15 eV window.
- Scope the "new form of altermagnetism" language to what the data show, namely site-resolved altermagnetic symmetry breaking on both Fe sublattices, unless a distinguishing observable can be identified.

5. Verdict: major revision
