Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors measure circular dichroism (CD) in Fe L3-edge RIXS on the polar, compensated magnet Fe2Mo3O8. They use the different crystal-field energies of the tetrahedral (Td) and quasi-octahedral (Oh) Fe2+ sites to resolve the signal by site. CD appears below T_N in a scattering geometry whose mirror plane (m') is broken by the Néel order. It is absent in the geometry whose mirror plane (m) is preserved. An ionic multiplet model with a staggered Weiss field reproduces the CD at a low-energy feature (A, assigned to the Oh site) and a dd feature (B, assigned to the Td site). From this the authors argue for "bi-altermagnetism", meaning two altermagnetic sublattices that are not related by crystal symmetry. Site-resolved RIXS-CD as a probe of compensated magnetic order is timely and of broad interest. The temperature dependence through T_N and the m/m' symmetry control go beyond earlier RIXS-CD work on α-MnTe and CrSb. The claim that this is a new form of altermagnetism needs both a sharper conceptual definition and firmer evidence on the Oh side.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First RIXS-CD study of an altermagnet candidate in which the CD is followed through T_N and disappears in the paramagnetic phase (Fig. 3, Figs. S2–S3). This ties the signal to the magnetic order rather than to the crystal structure.
- A well-chosen symmetry null control: CD in the m'-plane geometry and no CD in the m-plane geometry, measured at the same temperature (Fig. 2a inset, Fig. S1).
- The Td dd excitation (feature B, about 0.45 eV) lies well apart from the Oh excitations. Its CD therefore gives a clean, site-specific signature for the Td sublattice.
- The multiplet parameters were fixed from the RIXS peak positions without using the CD (SI Sec. 2). This makes the later CD comparison a partly independent test. The robustness check against trigonal distortion (Fig. S7) is useful.
- The method extends to other compensated magnets with inequivalent magnetic sites.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The Oh half of "bi-altermagnetism" rests entirely on assigning feature A to Oh spin-orbital excitations, and this assignment is not established experimentally.
   - **Where the evidence comes from.** The only Oh-specific dd feature with a clear experimental signature, feature C (about 1.0 eV, ⁵E/³T1), shows negligible CD (p. 4). The authors attribute this to lifetime broadening, not to symmetry. So the CD from the Oh sublattice comes from feature A alone: a broad shoulder at about 30–150 meV on the tail of the elastic line, measured with 45 meV total resolution.
   - **What else sits in that window.** The Td site has low-energy excitations up to about 40 meV once the Weiss field is on (Fig. S6a). Magnons extend into the same window. Phonons and Td-site orbital excitations known from THz/IR work (Ref. 37) are also nearby. The assignment of A to Oh, and the statement that Td "does not yield significant CD" there, come only from the ionic model (Fig. S6 b–d).
   - **The fit does not match the proposed scheme.** The two-component fit in Fig. S4 gives peaks at about 35 and 70 meV, an energy ratio of about 1:2. Transitions from the J_eff = 1 ground triplet to the quintet and septet should give about 2:5 in first order (24–26 and 58–65 meV for ζ = 52 meV, Fig. 4a caption). The text says the ratio is "close to 2:3" (p. 5), but neither the transition energies nor the level spacings in Fig. S4d give 2:3. As it stands, the evidence that A is the Oh J_eff manifold is weak.
   - **What the authors should do.**
     - The Oh and Td resonances fall at different incident energies (Fig. 1e,f; Td at lower energy). Measure the incident-energy dependence of the CD of feature A. Show that it follows the Oh resonance and not the Td resonance, and compare with the calculated site-resolved CD maps.
     - Show the CD as a difference spectrum (RCP − LCP), with the calculated Oh and Td contributions to the 0–150 meV CD overlaid separately after convolution with the 45 meV resolution.
     - Reconcile the fitted energies with the J_eff scheme. This includes the change from ζ = 52 meV (Fig. 4) to ξ = 65 meV (SI, CD simulations) and the effect of Δ_tri.
     - Without this, the data establish CD at the Td sublattice, but not independently at the Oh sublattice.

B. Should address

2. The CD is shown only as overlaid RCP/LCP spectra, with no difference spectra, no uncertainties and no stated normalization. The statement that the CD "vanished within the experimental uncertainty" above T_N (p. 3) cannot be checked.
   - **Normalization.**
     - In Fig. S1 (m plane, 32 K), the elastic peaks for RCP and LCP differ by about 8% (about 95 vs 88).
     - At 70 K (Fig. 3d, Fig. S2g), the elastic peak again shows an RCP/LCP difference, while the inelastic shoulder does not.
     - The overall intensity of the dd spectra drops from about 21 to about 15 at 80 K (Fig. S3h).
     - It is therefore unclear whether spectra are normalized to incident flux, acquisition time, or the elastic or dd intensity. It is also unclear how differences in circular polarization degree or beam footprint between helicities are handled.
   - **Requested.**
     - State the normalization procedure.
     - Plot RCP − LCP with statistical error bars for each temperature and for the m-plane control.
     - Plot the integrated CD of features A and B against temperature on one figure.
     - Compare this with the sublattice magnetization, either from neutron data or from the authors' own magnetization or specific-heat measurement of T_N on the same crystal; no sample T_N characterization is shown. An order-parameter-like CD(T) for both A and B, vanishing at the same T_N, would be the strongest experimental support for the paper's central claim.

3. The altermagnetic domain state and reproducibility are not discussed.
   - **Why this matters.** The two Néel domains (L and −L) give CD of opposite sign. The measured CD therefore requires a net domain imbalance in the illuminated spot, and its magnitude depends on that imbalance.
   - **What is missing.**
     - The paper does not say whether the sample was field-cooled or electric-and-magnetic-field-cooled. Fe2Mo3O8 is a magnetoelectric, so such cooling is possible.
     - It does not say whether the CD sign and size reproduce over thermal cycles, spots or crystals.
     - It does not say whether the m-plane control (Fig. S1) was taken on the same spot and in the same cooling cycle as the m'-plane data.
     - The number of crystals measured is not given.
   - **A decisive test.** The 6' operation (C6 combined with T) implies that rotating the sample by 60° about c, from one m' plane to the next, is equivalent to reversing L. The CD should then reverse sign in a single-domain region. Alternatively, domain selection by field cooling should reverse the CD. Either would rule out instrumental helicity artifacts in a way that the m-plane null alone does not, and would be unique to the proposed magnetic symmetry.

4. The concept of "bi-altermagnetism" and its testable content need a sharper statement.
   - **Why the concept is in question.** In P6_3mc, the Oh and Td Fe ions each form a single Wyckoff orbit whose two members are related by the 6_3 screw and the c-glide. Any compensated collinear order with L ∥ c in which each orbit is antiparallel therefore makes each orbit "altermagnetic" by symmetry. The magnetic structure, including order on both sites, is already known from neutron diffraction (Refs. 35, 36). The magnetic point group 6'mm' is a property of the whole crystal, not of each sublattice.
   - **Requested.**
     - Define precisely what distinguishes a bi-altermagnet from an ordinary altermagnet with two inequivalent magnetic orbits. For example, are there observables that depend on the relative sign or magnitude of the two sublattice order parameters?
     - State which alternative scenario the RIXS-CD data actually exclude.
     - Discuss the ferrimagnetic character of each Fe layer (Oh and Td moments of different size, antiparallel within a layer) and the nearby ferrimagnetic phase under Zn doping or field (Refs. 35, 36), and say how these fit the "two altermagnets" picture.
     - The abstract and title should then present the RIXS-CD as site-resolved confirmation of the symmetry breaking on each sublattice, unless a stronger distinction is shown.

5. The comparison between model and experiment is qualitative, and the Weiss field is set by hand.
   - **Choice of Weiss field.** The Weiss field acts only on spin (H = −S·B_eff, SI p. 6). Its size, 3–5 meV, is called "reasonable" without justification. A mean-field estimate from T_N = 60 K, or the exchange constants from neutron scattering (Ref. 44), should fix it. The Oh ground state carries unquenched orbital moment, so a spin-only exchange field is a real approximation; its effect on the CD line shape should be checked.
   - **Comparison with data.** Fig. 2b,c and Fig. S6 show RCP/LCP overlays only. Please show:
     - the calculated and measured CD difference spectra on the same energy axis, after resolution convolution;
     - the relative CD amplitude at A and B;
     - whether the model reproduces the absence of CD at C;
     - the calculated m-plane null;
     - how the sign of the CD maps onto the sign of L.
   - **What the agreement shows.** The statement that agreement "demonstrates that the excitations exhibit a localized character" (p. 4) is circular, because the model assumes localization.

6. Several ground-state statements go beyond what the data or the calculations support.
   - **Ambiguous text.** On p. 5, "While SOC leads to a singlet ground state A1, the Weiss field ... generates a large magnetic moment, indicating Van Vleck-type magnetism at the T_d site" mixes the Oh and Td sites. Fig. 4a gives a T1 (J_eff = 1) ground state for Oh, while SI p. 7 assigns the SOC singlet to Td.
   - **The Van Vleck claim.** "Van Vleck-type magnetism" at Td rests on an ionic calculation in which a 1 meV Weiss field already induces S_c = 1.29 (SI p. 7). This needs to be compared with the measured Td ordered moment. It should also be checked against the Td-site SOC splitting, which the paper itself places "well below 50 meV".
   - **The orbital-moment claim.** The orbital moment of about 20% of the spin moment is a model output and is not measured. Both claims should be clarified and presented as model results, or supported with data (for example XMCD sum rules in a field, or literature g-factors).

7. The momentum dependence of the CD is not shown.
   - **What is shown.** The CD is reported at Q = (0.05, 0, 0.65) in Fig. 2 and at (0.03, 0, 0.66) in Fig. 3. The dispersion in Fig. S4 is measured at (0, 0, L) with RCP only.
   - **Why it matters.** The paper argues that the CD comes from the polarization dependence of local dipole operators, not from momentum dependence (pp. 4–5). The CD should then be nearly Q-independent within the m' plane.
   - **Requested.** Show the CD at several L values, and at ±H if the geometry allows. This would support the local-excitation interpretation and help separate magnon contributions from the A feature.

C. Minor

8. The SOC parameters are inconsistent: ζ = 52 meV in Fig. 4a and the main text, ξ_3d = 65 meV in the CD simulations (SI C2). State which value is used in Fig. 2b,c and Fig. 4.

9. The momentum transfer differs between Fig. 2 (0.05, 0, 0.65) and Fig. 3 (0.03, 0, 0.66). The Fig. 3 caption should also state that the 32 K panels in Fig. 3 are the same data as Figs. S2a/S3a, if they are.

10. In Fig. 1e the color scale is labeled, but the incident energy chosen for Figs. 2–3 (706 eV) and the Td/Oh resonance positions could be marked on the map. That would make the site-selectivity argument easier to follow.

11. The XAS in Fig. 1d is not corrected for self-absorption. Fluorescence-yield self-absorption differs between geometries, so state whether the m and m' geometries used the same incidence angle and footprint.

12. The degree of circular polarization at beamline 41A and its uncertainty should be given, together with how helicity switching was done (undulator phase or other) and the time between RCP and LCP acquisitions.

13. SI Sec. 3 has typos: "or the e_g level", and S6 is labeled "(a)(e)". Eq. (9) also uses J = 3 to evaluate L_eff·S for a state built from single-electron m_l m_s products. A short justification of why that product state is the J_eff = 3 stretched state would help readers.

14. The phrase "a magnetic point group 6'mm' ... permits both symmetric and antisymmetric spin splittings" (p. 1) and the A/S-type classification should be briefly explained for non-specialists.

15. Data are available only "upon reasonable request". Depositing the RIXS spectra, including the RCP/LCP raw counts and normalization, would let others check the CD analysis.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one figure with the integrated CD of features A and B against temperature, with error bars, compared to the sublattice order parameter. Include the m-plane null integrated over the same windows.
2. Measure the incident-energy dependence of the CD across the Td and Oh resonances. This would give a model-independent site assignment of the CD, especially for feature A.
3. Perform an azimuthal 60° rotation, or a field-cooling domain reversal, to show that the CD sign follows L. This is the test most specific to the proposed altermagnetic symmetry.
4. Recast the "bi-altermagnetism" concept with a precise definition and a statement of which observables, here or in future experiments, distinguish it from an ordinary altermagnet with two magnetic Wyckoff orbits.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
