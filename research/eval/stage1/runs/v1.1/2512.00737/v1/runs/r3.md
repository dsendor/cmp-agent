Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The paper reports Fe L3-edge RIXS with circularly polarized X-rays on the polar compensated antiferromagnet Fe2Mo3O8. It finds circular dichroism (CD) in low-energy spin-orbital excitations (feature A, ~70 meV, assigned to the quasi-octahedral Oh site) and in the crystal-field excitation at ~0.45 eV (feature B, assigned to the tetrahedral Td site). The CD appears only below T_N = 60 K and only when the scattering plane is an m' plane, not an m (glide) plane. The authors take this as evidence for "bi-altermagnetism", meaning that the Oh and Td sublattices are each altermagnetic. The idea of using site-selective dd excitations to resolve sublattice symmetry breaking in a compensated magnet is attractive and timely. Seeing CD switch on at T_N is a real step beyond earlier RIXS-CD work on α-MnTe and CrSb, where the paramagnetic phase could not be reached.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The CD has a clear temperature dependence: present at 32, 45 and 50 K, weak at 55 K, and absent at 60, 65, 70 and 80 K (Fig. 3, Figs. S2 and S3). This ties the dichroism to the magnetic order, which earlier RIXS-CD studies could not do.
- There is a geometry control. With the scattering plane in the m plane, whose glide symmetry survives in the ordered phase, the CD is strongly suppressed (Fig. 2a inset, Fig. S1). This supports the symmetry argument.
- The dd excitations of the two Fe sites are well separated in energy (Td e→t2 at ~0.5 eV, Oh t2g→eg at ~1 eV), which gives a plausible route to site resolution.
- The ionic-model parameters are fixed from the RIXS peak positions without using the CD (SI Sec. 2), and the model then reproduces CD at features A and B (Fig. 2b,c). The CD result is also stable against the unknown trigonal distortion at the Oh site (Fig. S7).
- The low-energy spin-orbital (J_eff) excitations of Fe2+ in Fe2Mo3O8 are interesting in their own right.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The CD is not shown to be specific to altermagnetic order of each sublattice, which is what the title and abstract claim it "unveils".**
   - *What the measurement shows.* In the m'-plane geometry, CD appears whenever magnetic order breaks the pure mirror m that is present above T_N. Any magnetic order with moments along c does this, including order with a net moment: ferrimagnetic stacking, for example, which Fe2Mo3O8 adopts under field or with Zn doping (Refs. 35, 36).
   - *Where the altermagnetic character comes from.* That each sublattice is altermagnetic, with opposite-spin Oh (or Td) sites related by the 6_3 screw rather than by translation or inversion, follows from the known neutron magnetic structure and the symmetry analysis. It does not come from the RIXS-CD itself. As written ("identify a new form of altermagnetism", "compelling evidence for bi-altermagnetism"), the paper implies the CD discriminates altermagnetism from other orders. It has not shown this.
   - *What is missing in the model.* The simulation sums local contributions from two opposite-spin sublattices per site class (Methods; SI p. 6), but the paper never shows the per-sublattice CD or explains why the up- and down-sublattice contributions add rather than cancel. That mechanism is exactly where altermagnetic (rotation-related) order, as opposed to a translation- or PT-related antiferromagnet, would enter.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Show the CD of each sublattice separately, and its sum, for each site class.
     - Compute the CD for at least one non-altermagnetic reference configuration: for example, an Oh sublattice with ferromagnetically stacked layers (the ferrimagnetic phase), or a hypothetical arrangement in which the two opposite-spin sites have identical local orientation.
     - State which observable features (sign pattern between A and B, Q dependence, sign reversal between sublattices) are unique to the altermagnetic arrangement.
   - *If they cannot.* The claim should be narrowed: the CD shows that magnetic order breaks the m mirror at both Fe sites, which is consistent with the bi-altermagnetic structure inferred from the known magnetic symmetry.

2. **The CD and the null tests are judged by eye from overlaid RCP and LCP spectra, with no difference spectra, uncertainties or normalization procedure.**
   - *Unsupported statements.* Statements such as "the CD vanished within the experimental uncertainty" (p. 3) and "the CD for this geometry vanishes within the present experimental accuracy" (p. 4) carry the main argument, but no uncertainty is given anywhere.
   - *Visible differences where CD should be absent.*
     - In Fig. S1 (m plane, 32 K), LCP exceeds RCP by several percent at the elastic peak (~95 vs ~88) and slightly at the 0.42 eV peak.
     - In Fig. S2g (70 K, above T_N), the elastic peak also differs between helicities.
     - These differences are of the same order as part of the claimed CD at feature B (Fig. 2a, Fig. 3e).
   - *Possible artifacts not addressed.*
     - How the RCP and LCP spectra are normalized (incident flux, counting time, elastic line, total dd weight).
     - The degree of circular polarization of each helicity, and whether the residual linear components differ between them. Magnetic linear dichroism also switches on at T_N, so a helicity-dependent linear admixture could mimic a T_N-locked "CD".
     - Beam position or footprint changes on helicity switching.
   - *What the authors should do.*
     - Plot the difference spectra I_RCP − I_LCP (or the asymmetry) with statistical error bars, for both geometries and above and below T_N.
     - Integrate the CD over features A and B as a function of temperature, with error bars.
     - State the normalization and the polarization degree of each helicity, and show that the m-plane and paramagnetic residuals are consistent with zero.

**B. Should address**

3. **Magnetic domains are not discussed.** Opposite Néel-vector domains (L ∥ +c and L ∥ −c) give opposite CD. A finite CD therefore needs a domain imbalance within the beam footprint.
   - The paper does not say how the sample was cooled (zero field or in field, and the field direction), how large the beam spot is, or whether the CD sign is reproducible on repeated cooling or at different spots.
   - Because Fe2Mo3O8 is polar and magnetoelectric, the domain population may be set by the polar domain together with stray fields. That would be worth stating.
   - The most direct test linking the CD to L is reversal of the CD sign for both features A and B after the Néel vector is reversed, for example by field cooling in opposite fields (as in the MnTe domain work of Ref. 40). It would also test the predicted locking of the Oh and Td signs.
   - At a minimum, the authors should report the cooling protocol and whether the CD sign and magnitude reproduce over thermal cycles and spots.

4. **The temperature dependence is qualitative.** The CD is shown at eight temperatures (Figs. S2 and S3), but it is not reduced to a number and compared with the sublattice magnetization (from neutron data or susceptibility) or with the calculated CD as a function of Weiss field (Fig. S6).
   - Since a central argument is that the CD tracks the order parameter, a plot of integrated CD(T) for features A and B separately, compared with the known order parameter, would strengthen it.
   - Different temperature dependences for the Oh and Td features would also be informative about the "bi-" character.

5. **The 2:3 energy ratio of the spin-orbital excitations does not match the fitted peak energies.**
   - The text (p. 5) says the fitted peaks have an "energy ratio close to 2:3, consistent with the prediction of the zero-order approximation (see Fig. S4)". Fig. S4d gives peak 1 at ~31–38 meV and peak 2 at ~68–73 meV, a ratio of about 1:2.
   - The first-order prediction in Fig. 4a puts J_eff = 1→2 at ζ/2 and J_eff = 1→3 at 5ζ/4, a ratio of 2:5. Only the interval ratio (ζ/2 : 3ζ/4) is 2:3.
   - With ζ = 52 meV, the multiplet energies quoted in the Fig. 4 caption (23.7–25.6 and 58.1–64.8 meV) also lie noticeably below the fitted 35 and 70 meV. The SI instead uses ξ_3d = 65 meV.
   - The authors should state which ratio is meant, give the fitted energies with uncertainties, and reconcile the ζ values used in the main text and the SI.
   - This matters because the assignment of feature A to the Oh site, and therefore the "Oh half" of the bi-altermagnetism claim, rests on this spin-orbital interpretation.

6. **The assignment of feature A to the Oh site alone needs firmer support.**
   - Feature A sits at ~70 meV, and the instrumental resolution is 45 meV FWHM. The S4 fit uses an elastic Voigt plus two free Lorentzians, with no error bars and few constraints. The lower peak at ~35 meV lies close to the elastic line and to the energy range of the Td excitations ("well below 50 meV") and of magnons.
   - The assignment of the CD to the Oh site rests entirely on the model (Fig. 2b, Fig. S6).
   - The authors should:
     - show the CD separately for the 35 and 70 meV components;
     - give the fit uncertainties;
     - discuss whether magnon or Td-site contributions near 30–50 meV could carry part of the dichroism. The Td site does develop a Van Vleck moment and low-lying states in the Weiss field (Fig. S6a).

7. **"Bi-altermagnetism" as a distinct concept is not justified.** The Oh and Td moments order at a single T_N and are exchange-locked, so "bi-altermagnet" describes any altermagnet whose magnetic ions occupy two inequivalent Wyckoff orbits. Many known altermagnets have this property.
   - The authors should state what physical consequence, beyond site-resolved spectroscopy, follows from both sublattices being individually altermagnetic. Examples would be separately tunable spin splittings, possible independent ordering under doping, or distinct contributions to the spin splitting.
   - Otherwise the new term risks overstating the novelty.

8. **The claim of active orbital magnetism rests on the model alone.** The claim that Fe2Mo3O8 is a "rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism" (p. 5) rests on the ionic model with a spin-only Weiss field whose amplitude (1–5 meV) is chosen by hand. That model gives an orbital moment of ~20% of the spin moment and a Van Vleck-type Td moment.
   - The authors should compare the computed (S_c, L_c) with the measured ordered moments of the two sites from neutron diffraction, and with any XMCD or g-factor data.
   - They should either justify the Weiss-field amplitude, for example from a mean-field estimate using T_N and the known exchange constants, or present the orbital-moment statement as a model prediction.

9. **The model's limitations are presented as evidence.** The statement that "the agreement with the experimental data therefore demonstrates that the excitations exhibit a localized character" (p. 4) is circular, because the model assumes localization.
   - The agreement with the data is also only shown visually. The paper does not compare signs or relative magnitudes of the CD at A and B between theory and experiment.
   - The authors should overlay measured and calculated CD difference spectra on a common scale, state whether the signs agree at A and B, and tone down the "demonstrates" wording.

10. **Sample statistics are not reported.** The paper does not say how many crystals were measured, whether the CD was reproduced on more than one crystal or more than one cool-down, or whether the m- and m'-geometry data come from the same crystal and cool-down. Please state this.

**C. Minor**

11. Different momentum transfers are used in different figures: Q = (0.05, 0, 0.65) in Fig. 2 and S1, and Q = (0.03, 0, 0.66) in Fig. 3, S2 and S3. The Q for the m-plane inset is not given.
    - The Methods should describe how the m-plane geometry was reached (azimuthal rotation), the angular misalignment tolerance with respect to the mirror plane, and the incidence and scattering angles.
    - A small misalignment of the scattering plane from the m' plane breaks the symmetry argument in the paramagnetic phase, so the alignment accuracy should be quoted.

12. The SI derivation of ζ' = ζ/4 (Sec. 3, Eqs. 2–10) has problems:
    - It equates the expectation value of ζΣl·s in a single product state with M_L = −1, M_S = 2 (so M_J = 1) to the J = 3 value of L·S. That product state is not the stretched J = 3 state.
    - The description of the filling ("three t2g orbitals with spin-up electrons and one spin-down", then a basis labeled by m_l = ±1, 0) is confusing.
    - The sign of the effective orbital g-factor under the T–P equivalence is not stated, yet it underlies the 5/2 μB moment in Eq. 11.
    - The magnitude ζ/4 is standard, but the derivation should be corrected or replaced by a reference.

13. Fig. 2a: the main text says the inset shows the m-plane spectra, but the inset covers only −0.2 to 0.3 eV. Fig. S1 shows the full range. Please mention Fig. S1 in the caption, and give the m-plane temperature and Q in the caption.

14. Fig. 1f: the intensity scale and broadening of the calculated map differ from Fig. 1e. Please state the broadening used and whether self-absorption was included, since the XAS in Fig. 1d is uncorrected.

15. p. 4: "the magnetic symmetry of Fe2Mo3O8 forbids XMCD ... from the presence of three mirror (glide) planes m". The 6' operation already forbids M_z. Please state precisely which elements forbid each component.

16. The trigonal-distortion discussion (SI p. 8) says Δ_tri = ±30 meV is "consistent with the experimental constraints", but no criterion is given. Please state what in Fig. S4 constrains it.

17. Data are available only "upon reasonable request". Depositing the RCP/LCP spectra and model input files would help reproducibility.

18. Minor typographical points:
    - The magnetic point group is written "6'mm'" in the text; confirm the notation is consistent with Fig. 1b.
    - "altermagnetism–has" (p. 1) has a missing space or dash.
    - SI eq. 2 context reads "configuration. or the e_g level".

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Present all CD results as difference or asymmetry spectra with error bars. Add an integrated CD(T) plot for features A and B against the sublattice order parameter.
2. Demonstrate the link to the Néel vector directly by reversing the CD sign through field-cooled domain selection, or at least by documenting reproducibility over cool-downs and beam spots.
3. Add calculations for non-altermagnetic reference orders, and show the per-sublattice CD decomposition. This would show which features of the data actually single out the bi-altermagnetic arrangement.
4. Measure the Q (and azimuthal) dependence of the CD at fixed temperature. Altermagnetic CD should have a characteristic angular dependence that a generic magnetic-order effect would not share. This would turn the symmetry argument into a positive test.

**5. Verdict**

Verdict: major revision
