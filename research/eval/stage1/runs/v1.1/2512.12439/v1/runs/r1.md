Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript introduces an all-dry route to few-layer Ta2NiSe5 flakes: Au or ALD Al2O3 is deposited directly on a cleaved crystal, then transferred by Au cold-welding. The authors use polarized Raman spectroscopy (ac channel) to follow the orthorhombic-to-monoclinic transition and the Fano/quasi-elastic signatures of the electronic transition in flakes on Al2O3 (7L, 4L) and on Au (18L, 6L, 4L). The central result is that 4L flakes on Au show a transition lowered to T_c^avg ≈ 220 ± 40 K and broadened, while 4L on Al2O3 stays sharp at about 280 K. The authors attribute this to an interfacial charge-transfer/screening gradient that suppresses excitonic interactions, and they use it to argue that about 100 K of the bulk 326 K T_c is excitonic in origin. Substrate engineering of a candidate excitonic insulator is a timely and attractive idea. The fabrication method may be useful beyond this material, and the side-by-side Raman tracking of structural and electronic signatures in 4-layer flakes is technically impressive.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new, solvent-free exfoliation and transfer protocol (Fig. 1C) that reliably produces 10–100 µm few-layer flakes of a rippled vdW compound, with possible general use.
- Careful symmetry assignment of the phonons in a 4L flake (Fig. 2) and a sensible choice of the ac channel to suppress substrate signal (Fig. 2F,G).
- The structural signal (A_g(m4)/A_g(m5) onset, Fig. 3) and the electronic signals (Fano −1/q and χ''(10 cm⁻¹), Fig. 4) are followed in the same flakes. The two give consistent T_c values for the three sharp samples.
- The size of the effect is large: a ≥100 K suppression on Au against about 40 K on Al2O3 at the same nominal thickness. This is much larger than earlier thickness-only tuning on SiO2 (Ref. 45).
- 18L on Au and 7L on Al2O3 recover the bulk T_c, which provides internal reference points.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The Au–Al2O3 comparison does not isolate "conducting versus insulating", and each condition rests on a single flake.** The paper's key inference (pp. 13–15) is that the broadened, suppressed transition on Au is caused by charge transfer and screening. The two substrates differ in many other ways:
   - Au is thermally evaporated directly onto the Ta2NiSe5 surface, which raises the possibility of hot-adatom damage, Au–Se bonding or intermixing at the first layer, and roughness from conforming to the ripples (p. 4). Al2O3 is grown by ALD at 100–150 °C with TMA/H2O, a different chemistry and thermal history.
   - The two stacks also differ in thermal-expansion mismatch and in the strain transmitted on cooling. A 50–100 nm amorphous ALD oxide clamps the flake differently from a 20–50 nm Au film welded to Au/Cr.
   - The authors' own group has shown that uniaxial strain strongly tunes T_c and the excitonic fluctuations in Ta2NiSe5 (Ref. 17). Strain-induced suppression and broadening is therefore a leading alternative explanation, and it is not ruled out.
   - The Al2O3 sample excludes oxidation (p. 15) but none of the Au-specific alternatives.

   In addition, the 6L and 4L flakes on Au appear to be one flake each, and the headline 4L/Au result rests on one flake with signal about 50× weaker than the reference (Fig. 3E, 4E).

   The authors should do the following:
   - Show the key result on at least one more 4L/Au flake, and ideally on a second cooldown of the same flake.
   - Use the room-temperature phonon frequencies of all five flakes as an internal strain gauge, compared with bulk and with the uniaxial-strain coefficients of Ref. 17.
   - Ideally, add a control that separates conductivity from interface chemistry, for example a few-nm Al2O3 or hBN spacer between flake and Au, or a different metal with a different work function.
   - Without such evidence, the attribution to charge transfer/screening should be stated as a hypothesis.

2. **The interfacial-gradient model is not consistent with the measured onsets, and the optical depth sensitivity on a metal mirror is not considered.** The model (p. 13–14, Fig. 5B inset) says the layers nearest Au have a T_c suppressed by more than 100 K, the surface layers have "T_c closer to room temperature", and the Au effect is confined to one or two layers.

   **(a) Onset temperatures.** If two or three of the four layers in 4L/Au were near-bulk, or at least like 4L/Al2O3 (about 280 K), the first kink and the onset of A_g(m4)/A_g(m5) should appear near 280–300 K. That onset should be followed by a tail from the interfacial layers. Instead, the first kink is at about 220 K (4L/Au) and about 250 K (6L/Au) (Fig. 3S,T). These values are 60 K and 30 K below 4L/Al2O3, which indicates that all layers are suppressed. The 6L/Au flake should have four or five unperturbed layers, yet its onset is about 30 K below that of the thinner 4L/Al2O3 flake.

   **(b) Depth sensitivity.** For a few-nm flake directly on an optically thick Au film, the standing-wave field at 633 nm has a near-node at the metal surface. Raman sensitivity is then weakest for exactly the interfacial layers that the model says are perturbed. The Al2O3 flakes sit on a 50–100 nm dielectric spacer, which changes this weighting entirely. The roughly 50× lower signal of 4L/Au relative to 4L/Al2O3 (Fig. 3E, 4E against 3B, 4B) is suggestive of this. The statement that "Raman scattering from the interfacial Ta2NiSe5 layers becomes detectable" (p. 13–14) needs a quantitative basis.

   The authors should:
   - Compute the layer-resolved Raman weighting (transfer-matrix) for each stack.
   - Show that a layer-dependent T_c profile reproduces the measured onset, width and spectral evolution.
   - Otherwise, revise the microscopic picture. A uniform suppression of the whole flake, or lateral inhomogeneity within the laser spot, are alternatives. Spatial Raman maps across the 4L/Au flake would test lateral inhomogeneity.

3. **The excitonic attribution is not unique, and the key low-energy evidence in 4L/Au is at the noise and artifact level.**

   **(a) Uniqueness.** The conclusion that "the strong sensitivity ... to conducting Au ... establishes an excitonic component" and that about 100 K of T_c is excitonic (pp. 15–16, abstract) assumes that carrier doping and screening suppress only the excitonic channel. A Q = 0 hybridization-gap/band-Jahn-Teller instability (Refs. 11–13, 36) is also sensitive to band filling and to the electrostatic environment. Doping moves the chemical potential away from the hybridization point and weakens the electronic energy gain. A T_c reduction under doping therefore does not discriminate between the two scenarios without a quantitative model. A strain origin (concern 1) would discriminate even less.

   **(b) The quasi-elastic peak (QEP) argument.** This argument for selective suppression of excitonic fluctuations (p. 15) has three problems:
   - The 4L/Au low-energy signal (Fig. 4T, ×50) is extremely weak. It appears comparable in magnitude to the bare Au/Cr ac background (Fig. 2G), although units across figures are not stated to be comparable.
   - A spurious Rayleigh leak at 15 cm⁻¹ was removed by interpolation in exactly the 6L and 4L Au spectra (Methods, p. 18). The quantity used is χ''(10 cm⁻¹), close to the notch/edge cutoff of a single-grating spectrometer.
   - "Undetectable" may therefore reflect sensitivity rather than physics.

   **(c) Fano comparison.** The comparison that the electron–phonon (Fano) channel is "not strongly reduced" rests on −1/q falling from 0.46 to 0.38. That is a 17% reduction, and the authors themselves attribute it to broadening. Fano q in a 20–80 cm⁻¹ window is also sensitive to the continuum shape and to the leaked A_g(o1)* mode.

   The authors should:
   - Show the raw (unscaled, same-units) low-energy ac spectra of 4L/Au next to bare Au/Cr at the same temperatures, with noise levels, and the spectra before Rayleigh removal.
   - Demonstrate that a QEP of the size expected from a broadened transition would have been detectable.
   - Either provide a model calculation that distinguishes excitonic from hybridization/lattice suppression under doping, or soften the abstract and conclusions ("manifesting the presence of excitonic interactions", "establishes an excitonic component") to a consistent-with statement.

B. Should address

4. **Laser heating and the effective sample temperature are not documented.** The Methods (p. 17) state that about 1 mW was used and that "the actual temperature" was checked from the Stokes/anti-Stokes ratio, but no values are given. Thin flakes on 50–100 nm amorphous Al2O3 and on welded Au films will have very different thermal anchoring and absorption. In particular, the Au stack absorbs at 633 nm, and the flake–Au interface may be imperfect. A local temperature offset translates directly into an apparent T_c shift and, if it varies across the spot, into an apparent broadening. The authors should report the Stokes/anti-Stokes-derived temperature against nominal temperature for each sample. They should also show a power dependence (for example 0.25, 0.5 and 1 mW) of the A_g(m4)/m5 onset or of the second-kink temperature for 4L/Au.

5. **The T_c^avg values and the ±40 K width for the Au flakes rest on an ad hoc rule.** The broadening is inferred by assuming that the first-to-second-kink separation (60 K in sharp flakes) is intrinsic, and that any excess (100 K) represents symmetric broadening of ±40 K (p. 10). The physical origin of the second kink is not explained, and it is not obvious that it should shift rigidly with T_c or that broadening is symmetric about the first kink. Indeed, T_c^avg = 220 K is set equal to the first kink of 4L/Au, while the broadening is taken to extend 40 K above it. This implies an onset at about 260 K, which the data do not show (Fig. 3T). A more robust approach would be a global fit of the A_g(m5) integrated intensity (an order-parameter proxy) or of the m4 peak position against T, using a distribution of T_c values. This would yield a mean and width with stated uncertainties, applied identically to all five flakes. Supplementary Note 2 is referred to but was not available for this review.

6. **Measurements above and below 300 K appear to use different setups, right across the transition region.** "For measurements below 300 K, the samples were loaded into a cryostat equipped with a sapphire viewport" (p. 17), with a calibration factor applied. Data extend to 340–400 K (Fig. 3, 4), so the higher-temperature points were presumably taken differently. T_c for the Al2O3 and 18L flakes (280–330 K) and the maximum of −1/q and of χ''(10 cm⁻¹) all lie near this boundary. The authors should describe the high-temperature setup and show that the spectra overlap at a common temperature in both configurations. Intensity-based quantities (Fig. 4P–T) are especially sensitive to such a splice.

7. **There is no direct evidence of charge transfer or of its sign and magnitude.** The inset of Fig. 5B draws electrons in Au, but neither the direction nor the density of transferred charge is estimated, for example from work functions or from a phonon-frequency shift. Doping-sensitive phonon shifts or linewidths at 295 K could serve as a check, comparing 4L/Au with 4L/Al2O3 in Fig. 2/3. Kelvin-probe or other local measurements would also help, as would a simple electrostatic estimate of the induced carrier density per layer versus distance from Au. Such an estimate would test the claim that the effect is confined to one or two layers.

8. **The 15% reduction in 4L/Al2O3 is not discussed.** The paper describes 4L/Al2O3 as showing "minimal influence from the substrate" (p. 4) and "nearly bulk-like properties" (abstract), yet T_c drops from 326 to about 280–287 K (Fig. 5A). This 40 K shift is larger than the error bars and larger than the 9% reduction reported for 5L on SiO2 (Ref. 45). Whether it comes from thickness, ALD-induced strain or interface chemistry matters for the baseline used to isolate the Au effect. If it is a thickness effect, a 6L/Al2O3 flake would allow a matched comparison with 6L/Au.

9. **The relationship to Ref. 60 needs more discussion.** The authors note that a gate-tuning study of atomically thin Ta2NiSe5 in hBN "arrive[s] at a different conclusion" (p. 16) but do not say what that conclusion is or why the results differ. Gate doping is the cleanest test of the "charge transfer suppresses T_c" hypothesis. A substantive comparison is needed, for example of the carrier densities reached and the T_c shifts observed. If gating at a comparable density does not suppress T_c by 100 K, this directly bears on concerns 1 and 3.

10. **The layer count on Au, and its uncertainty, are not shown in the main text.** Thickness is determined by AFM (Supplementary Note 3). For flakes on evaporated, rippled-conforming Au, the step height may include roughness or a gap. Because the interpretation hinges on 4L against 6L against 18L, the main text or a figure should state the measured heights, the layer thickness assumed per layer and the uncertainty. Optical contrast or ultra-low-frequency interlayer modes, if accessible, would be an independent check.

C. Minor

11. Fig. 4 caption: "The dashed curves in (H) and (J) are guides to the eye", but the dashed curves appear in (H) and (I). Correspondingly, the text on p. 11 says "compare dashed guides in Fig. 4, H and I".

12. Fig. 4K–O: the error bars on −1/q are stated to be smaller than the symbols, but these are fit-statistical only. Please state which Fano parameters were free and whether the background or the A_g(o1)* leak was included in the fit. Please also show fits for 4L/Au at low temperature, where the signal is weakest.

13. The 15%-of-maximum criterion used to define the electronic T_c and its width (Fig. 4P–S) is arbitrary. Please show that the conclusions do not change for, for example, 10% or 25%.

14. The structural T_c is assigned from where the A_g(o3)* leak "begins to decrease" (p. 8). The leak arises from polarizer misalignment, so its intensity and position may vary from sample to sample. Please state the misalignment for each sample, or use an intrinsic ac-channel mode.

15. The abstract states the 4L/Au T_c is "reduced by over 100 K", whereas T_c^avg = 220 ± 40 K is consistent with a reduction anywhere from about 66 to 146 K relative to 326 K. Please phrase the size of the reduction consistently with the uncertainty.

16. The statement that the 18L "fraction of interfacial layers is too small to contribute to the Raman signal" (p. 13) should be backed by the same depth-sensitivity calculation requested in concern 2.

17. The Data availability statement says data "will be made available"; please provide a repository link at publication.

18. Typo on p. 3: "exctonic".

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a spacer-control sample (thin hBN or few-nm Al2O3 between flake and Au) and at least one more 4L/Au flake. Together these are the most direct way to show that the effect is electronic (conductivity/screening) and reproducible, rather than chemical or strain-induced.
2. Carry out a transfer-matrix calculation of the depth-resolved Raman sensitivity for each stack, and fit the temperature evolution of the A_g(m5) intensity with a distribution of T_c values. This would replace the kink-difference heuristic and put the "gradient" picture on a quantitative footing.
3. Report the laser-heating calibration (Stokes/anti-Stokes temperatures and a power series) and room-temperature phonon frequencies for all flakes, to rule out heating and strain as sources of the shift.
4. Soften or support the excitonic-fraction conclusion with a model that contrasts how doping and screening affect a pure excitonic versus a hybridization/lattice-driven transition. Compare quantitatively with the gate-tuned results of Ref. 60.

5. Verdict: major revision
