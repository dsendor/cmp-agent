Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper introduces an all-dry evaporation/cold-welding exfoliation route that yields large (10–100 µm) four-layer Ta2NiSe5 flakes on Au and Al2O3, and uses polarized Raman spectroscopy (onset of the monoclinic Ag(m4)/Ag(m5) modes in the ac channel, the Fano asymmetry of B2g(o1), and the low-energy quasi-elastic response) to track the structural and electronic transition. The main finding is that 4L Ta2NiSe5 on Au shows a strongly lowered and broadened transition (T_c^avg ≈ 220 ± 40 K), whereas 4L on Al2O3 shows a sharp transition about 40 K below bulk. The authors interpret this as an interfacial T_c gradient produced by screening and charge transfer from Au, and use it to argue that excitonic interactions account for up to ~150 K of the 326 K bulk T_c. Substrate-tuning a candidate excitonic insulator in the few-layer limit is new and of broad interest. The fabrication method could also be useful well beyond this material.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The fabrication protocol is new and practical. It gives large, uniform few-layer flakes of a rippled, hard-to-exfoliate vdW compound, under inert conditions throughout (Fig. 1).
- The Raman methodology is careful. Symmetry assignments are made with azimuthal dependence. Bare-substrate controls show that the ac channel is free of substrate signal (Fig. 2f, g). The second-derivative analysis makes the onset of the monoclinic modes visible even in very weak signals (Fig. 3k–t).
- The structural (phonon kink) and electronic (Fano −1/q, quasi-elastic peak) T_c estimates are independent and agree for the sharp-transition flakes (7L/Al2O3, 4L/Al2O3, 18L/Au; Fig. 5).
- Internal references support the comparison. The 18L/Au flake reproduces the bulk T_c, so the Au-transfer process alone does not destroy the transition. The 4L/Al2O3 flake shows a sharp transition at the same thickness as the most affected Au flake.
- Data are deposited publicly (Zenodo).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Strain, chemical reaction or Au interdiffusion are not ruled out as causes of the Au-specific suppression and broadening. The paper attributes the effect on Au to screening and charge transfer (pp. 13–15, Fig. 5c, abstract) by elimination: the effect is "absent" on insulating Al2O3, and the sharp 4L/Al2O3 transition "serves as a control to exclude a degradation effect" (p. 15). However, the two substrates differ in more than conductivity:
   - Au is thermally evaporated directly onto the freshly cleaved Ta2NiSe5 surface. Al2O3 is grown by ALD at 100–150 °C with TMA/H2O. The interfacial chemistry, any Au–Se reaction or Au interdiffusion into a rippled surface, and the deposition-induced stress are all different.
   - Ta2NiSe5's T_c is known to be very sensitive to uniaxial strain and pressure (refs. 14–17). Au and Al2O3 have quite different thermal expansion coefficients. A flake rigidly bonded to evaporated Au therefore experiences different, and possibly inhomogeneous, thermal-mismatch strain on cooling from 400 K. Strain that varies through the layers or laterally would also lower and broaden the transition.
   - The 4L/Al2O3 sample therefore does not test degradation or strain at the Au interface.

   The headline mechanism needs evidence that distinguishes these alternatives. The authors should:
   - Compare high-temperature (orthorhombic, e.g. 340–400 K) phonon frequencies and linewidths of 4L/Au, 6L/Au, 4L/Al2O3 and 18L/Au. Strain and doping shift the Ag(o) modes differently, and a reference to bulk shifts would bound the strain.
   - Add an estimate of the thermal-mismatch strain and compare it with the known dT_c/dε.
   - Include, if possible, a control that keeps conduction but changes interface chemistry or stiffness (e.g. a thin hBN or Al2O3 spacer of one or a few nm on Au, which would also directly test the claimed screening length), or a cross-sectional or XPS check for Au–Se reaction.

   Without such evidence, the interface effect should be described as Au-specific, with screening and charge transfer presented as a hypothesis.

2. Each condition is a single flake, and the central result rests on one 4L/Au and one 4L/Al2O3 flake. The study has five samples, each a distinct thickness/substrate combination (p. 7). It shows no repeat flake for any condition, no spot-to-spot variation within a flake, and no thermal cycling. The conclusions depend on a comparison between two individual flakes (4L/Au vs 4L/Al2O3) and on the thickness trend 18L→6L→4L on Au, so flake-to-flake variation (local thickness errors, partial adhesion, wrinkles, local damage) could produce the differences attributed to the substrate. In particular, the 40 K reduction for 4L/Al2O3 versus the bulk-like 7L/Al2O3 has no independent replicate. The authors state that flakes can be "regularly" isolated with this method (p. 4), so the following should be feasible:
   - at least one more 4L (or 3–5L) flake on each substrate, measured over a reduced temperature set;
   - T_c and its width at several positions on the 4L/Au flake;
   - a statement of how many flakes were measured and whether any were excluded.

B. Should address

3. The vertical-gradient picture is inferred, not tested, and the optical depth weighting is not considered. The claim that the broadening is "perpendicular to the layers" (abstract; pp. 13–14; Fig. 5b inset) is supported by the toy model in Supplementary Note 4, but the main text does not rule out a lateral distribution of T_c within the laser spot. On a highly reflective Au surface, the standing-wave field of the 632.8 nm excitation has a node near the metal. The interfacial layers of a ~2.5 nm flake should therefore contribute much less Raman intensity than the top layers. Fig. 3e (×50) against Fig. 3b shows the total intensity of 4L/Au to be roughly two orders of magnitude below 4L/Al2O3. The two configurations also differ optically: the Al2O3 flakes sit on 50–100 nm Al2O3 over Au, an interference stack. The authors should:
   - compute the depth-dependent Raman weighting for both stacks and include it in the toy model, to show whether one or two interfacial layers could produce the observed broadened signal;
   - present spatial Raman maps at a few temperatures within the broadened range, to separate vertical from lateral inhomogeneity.

   This also bears on the claim that the interface effect is confined to "one or two layers" (pp. 13–14). 6L/Au and 4L/Au show nearly the same ±40 K broadening with only a 30 K shift, which is hard to reconcile with a 1–2 layer effect that should be diluted in 6L. That consistency should be shown quantitatively.

4. The excitonic versus lattice partition of T_c (~150 K excitonic, ~180 K lattice) rests on weak evidence. The discussion (pp. 15–16) infers that Au weakens electron–hole interactions rather than electron–lattice coupling. The basis is that the quasi-elastic peak at 10 cm⁻¹ disappears for 4L/Au, while the maximum of −1/q drops by only 16%. Several issues weaken this:
   - The 4L/Au signal is weak (×50 scaling in Fig. 4e, t). For the Au flakes only, a spurious Rayleigh-leakage peak at 15 cm⁻¹ was removed by interpolation (p. 19). The "undetectable" quasi-elastic peak at 10 cm⁻¹ is therefore at the edge of what the data can resolve. Noise levels and a detection limit relative to the 6L/18L peak heights should be given.
   - The bare-substrate ac controls in Fig. 2g start at ~30 cm⁻¹. A temperature-dependent ac control at 10 cm⁻¹ on Au is needed.
   - Screening and doping also renormalize the phonon and the hybridization gap. A modest change in the maximum of −1/q does not show that electron–lattice coupling is unaffected.
   - The 4L/Au electronic T_c^avg (220 ± 46 K) is obtained by copying the 6L width and shifting by ~30 K (p. 12). It is not an independent measurement.

   Reduced T_c on a conducting substrate is consistent with excitonic physics. The quantitative statement that excitonic mechanisms account for "up to ~150 K" should be presented as an upper-bound estimate within a stated model, or toned down.

5. Laser heating is not quantified. About 1 mW at 632.8 nm is focused on 4–7L flakes whose thermal anchoring differs: directly on Au/Cr versus on 50–100 nm Al2O3 over Au. Differential heating would shift the apparent T_c between substrates by an amount comparable to the 40 K differences discussed. The Methods state that Stokes/anti-Stokes ratios were checked (p. 18), but give no values. The authors should report the Stokes/anti-Stokes-derived temperatures against nominal temperature for each flake, or a laser-power dependence of T_c for at least 4L/Au and 4L/Al2O3.

6. The T_c^avg and width extraction for the Au flakes is heuristic. The ±40 K broadening comes from assuming that the second (low-temperature) kink of Ag(m4) sits a fixed 60 K below T_c, as in the sharp flakes. The excess separation (100 K versus 60 K) is then read as broadening (pp. 9–10). This assumption is not motivated physically: the second kink's origin is not discussed. The peak positions also come from local maxima of a smoothed, interpolated second derivative (Gaussian smoothing of 10–15 K in temperature, p. 19), which itself broadens temperature features by an amount comparable to the 10 K data spacing. Please explain the origin of the second kink, show raw peak positions from direct fits (e.g. two-Lorentzian fits of the 112–143 cm⁻¹ region) alongside the derivative-based ones, and state the effect of the smoothing on the extracted widths.

7. The ~40 K reduction for 4L on Al2O3 is not explained, and it confounds the substrate comparison. The 4L/Al2O3 T_c (280–287 K) is well below 7L/Al2O3 (320–330 K). The authors call this "moderately perturbed" with "minimal influence from the substrate" (p. 3), yet offer no mechanism: intrinsic dimensional effect, ALD-related surface modification, or strain. It is therefore not clear which part of the 4L/Au suppression is substrate-specific and which comes from thickness alone. A thickness series on Al2O3 (e.g. 3L, 5L) or a 4L flake on a third insulator such as hBN would separate these effects, as would a discussion of the 8L/SiO2 result (ref. 45).

8. There is no estimate of the size of the charge transfer. The mechanism invokes electron transfer from Au but gives no expected carrier density. One could be estimated from work functions (the paper quotes ≈5.4 eV for Ta2NiSe5; evaporated amorphous Au is typically lower) and from the density of states. An estimate of the transferred density and its decay length, compared with K-dosing densities that collapse the gap (refs. 25–28), would make the screening/charge-transfer picture quantitative. Doping-induced phonon shifts, if present in the Ag modes, could serve as an experimental check.

9. The Fano fits for a mixed-phase signal need care. If the 4L/Au signal is a superposition of orthorhombic and monoclinic layers, as proposed, then a single Fano function (Eq. 1) fitted to the sum gives effective parameters whose meaning is unclear. This affects the interpretation of the broadened −1/q in Fig. 4n, o and the "16% reduction" argument. The authors should either fit a weighted two-component model consistent with the toy model, or note this limitation explicitly.

C. Minor

10. Measurements below 300 K used a cryostat with a sapphire window and a calibration factor (p. 18). The data extend to 340–400 K, near T_c, but the text does not say how those temperatures were reached, nor whether the same setup was used across 300 K. A change of setup in the 280–330 K window where the transition occurs could introduce discontinuities. Please clarify.

11. On p. 12, "see Fig. 3o" should apparently be Fig. 4o.

12. The asymmetric error bars on the structural T_c (e.g. 320 +10/−14 K) are defined as half-spacings to neighbouring points. The electronic T_c errors use a 15% threshold. Since Fig. 5 plots the two on the same axes, state clearly that they are resolution and width measures, not statistical uncertainties.

13. Layer numbers rest on AFM (Supplementary Notes 5, 6). In the main text, briefly state the step height used per layer and its uncertainty on the rough amorphous Au, since an error of one layer matters for the 4L versus 6L argument.

14. "Atomic-scale interface effect" (abstract) and "confirm the existence of excitonic effects" (p. 16) are stronger than the evidence shown (see items 1, 3 and 4). Consider more measured wording.

15. Fig. 5b: the Au panel shows two error bars at 4L and 6L (structural and electronic). The legend could state more explicitly that the 4L electronic point is derived from the 6L width rather than measured.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Separate screening/charge transfer from strain or chemistry with phonon-frequency analysis at high temperature and, ideally, a thin-spacer-on-Au control. A spacer of variable thickness would also measure the interface decay length directly.
- Add replicate flakes and spatial maps for the 4L conditions, and include a depth-resolved optical weighting for the Au and Al2O3/Au stacks in the toy model.
- Report laser-heating checks quantitatively, and fit peak positions directly in addition to the smoothed second-derivative maps.
- Present the excitonic/lattice partition of T_c as a model-dependent estimate, with the quasi-elastic peak detection limit for the Au flakes stated.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
