Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors develop an all-dry route (evaporating the substrate material onto a cleaved crystal, then transferring by Au cold welding) to make few-layer Ta2NiSe5 flakes on Au and on Al2O3. With polarized Raman they track the orthorhombic-to-monoclinic and charge-gap transition as a function of thickness and substrate. The central finding is that 4L and 6L flakes on Au show a strongly lowered and broadened transition, while 4L on Al2O3 shows a sharp transition lowered by about 40 K. They read this as an interfacial Tc gradient caused by screening and charge transfer from Au, and from it they estimate the excitonic share of the 326 K transition. The substrate-tuning approach to the excitonic-insulator question is new and the fabrication method is likely to be useful beyond this material. The step from the observed broadening to a layer-resolved, screening-driven mechanism, and from there to an excitonic-versus-lattice decomposition, is not yet secured.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A practical, solvent-free exfoliation and transfer protocol that gives 10–100 µm few-layer flakes of a hard-to-exfoliate rippled compound on several substrates (Fig. 1).
- Careful symmetry assignment of the Raman modes in the 4L flake. The ac channel is shown to exclude substrate contributions (Fig. 2f, g), and the orthorhombic-to-monoclinic change in selection rules is demonstrated at four layers.
- Two independent markers of the transition (phonon onset and kinks in Fig. 3, Fano asymmetry and quasi-elastic weight in Fig. 4) are tracked on the same flakes and agree for the sharp samples.
- The qualitative contrast between Au and Al2O3 at the same thickness (4L) is clear in the raw data (Figs. 3b, e; 4b, e).
- The open data deposit and the supplementary toy model of layer-dependent Tc.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Alternative origins of the broadening on Au are not excluded: deposition damage, chemical reaction and strain. The two substrate types are made by different processes. On Au, the metal is thermally evaporated directly onto the freshly cleaved Ta2NiSe5 (Methods, p. 17). On Al2O3, the first layer is ALD Al2O3 grown at 100–150 °C with TMA/H2O precursors, and Au is evaporated only afterwards. Hot Au adatoms on a Se-terminated surface can intermix, form Au–Se bonds, create Se vacancies or roughen the interface. The text itself says the adatoms "conform better to the surface ripples and bind more strongly" (p. 4). Any of these would produce a disordered, chemically modified or strained interfacial layer with a suppressed and smeared Tc, without any role for free-carrier screening. Strain is a specific risk: uniaxial strain is known to shift Tc strongly (refs. 7, 14–17), and amorphous evaporated Au bonded conformally to the rippled surface, with a different thermal contraction, could impose strain that varies with depth on cooling. The Al2O3 sample "serves as a control to exclude a degradation effect" (p. 15) only for oxidation. It does not control for metal-deposition damage, because no metal ever touches that flake. The conclusion that "charge transfer and electrostatic screening from a conducting substrate are the likely culprit" (p. 15) therefore rests on an uncontrolled process difference. The authors should do one or more of the following:
   - Measure a flake placed on a pre-formed metal surface without evaporation onto Ta2NiSe5, for example the Au(111)/mica ribbons of Fig. 1b or a flake stamped onto Au with an hBN-free dry transfer.
   - Measure a flake with an ultrathin insulating spacer (about 1–2 nm Al2O3 or hBN) between Ta2NiSe5 and Au. This keeps the deposition chemistry but removes charge transfer.
   - Compare the high-temperature (orthorhombic) phonon frequencies and linewidths of the A_g(o) modes across all five flakes, to bound strain and disorder.
   - Discuss whether XPS or cross-sectional TEM of the Au/Ta2NiSe5 interface is feasible.

   Without at least one such control, the interface effect is established but its attribution to screening and charge transfer, and everything built on that attribution in the Discussion, is not.

B. Should address

2. Each condition rests on a single flake. Five flakes are measured, one per thickness and substrate combination, and no flake is measured twice or after thermal cycling. The headline contrast (4L/Al2O3 sharp at about 280 K, 4L/Au broad and ending near 180 K) is thus two samples. The 6L/Au flake partly replicates the Au result. The 40 K reduction for 4L/Al2O3 has no replicate, and the 8L/SiO2 value of 309 K from ref. 45 suggests sizable sample-to-sample spread. Please add at least one more 4L flake on each substrate, or show the spread of Tc across flakes in the Supplementary Information. Thermal-cycling reproducibility of the 4L/Au data would also help.

3. Depth weighting of the Raman signal near a metal is not addressed. The interpretation needs the Ta2NiSe5 layers adjacent to Au to contribute appreciably to the ac signal (p. 13: "Raman scattering from the interfacial Ta2NiSe5 layers becomes detectable"). For a flake a few nm thick on a good metal at 632.8 nm, the tangential electric field nearly vanishes at the metal surface, so the layer touching Au should be the least weighted. The overall intensity drop (×25 and ×50 scaling for 6L and 4L on Au versus ×3 for 18L, Figs. 3, 4) is consistent with this. A transfer-matrix calculation of the layer-resolved Raman weight for each stack (Ta2NiSe5/Au/Cr and Ta2NiSe5/Al2O3/Au) should be added and folded into the Supplementary Note 4 toy model. The model should be tested against whether a strongly suppressed bottom layer can still produce the observed broadening, or whether the suppression must extend further into the flake than "one or two layers" (p. 14).

4. The sample temperature and laser heating are not documented. Spectra were taken at about 1 mW from a focused HeNe spot on flakes a few nm thick. The Methods state that the temperature was "checked" with the Stokes/anti-Stokes ratio but give no values. Heating differs between the stacks: Ta2NiSe5 on 50–100 nm ALD Al2O3 is thermally isolated from the Au heat sink, whereas Ta2NiSe5 directly on Au is well sunk. A few tens of kelvin of heating on Al2O3 would mimic part of the 40 K Tc reduction attributed to 4L/Al2O3. Please:
   - report the anti-Stokes/Stokes temperatures for each flake, at least near Tc;
   - show a power dependence (for example 0.25, 0.5 and 1 mW) of the transition marker for 4L/Al2O3 and 4L/Au;
   - explain how temperatures above 300 K, up to 400 K, were reached, since the Methods mention the cryostat only for measurements below 300 K.

5. The Tc and width extraction for the Au flakes is ad hoc, and the "150 K" headline depends on it. The ±40 K broadening comes from assuming that the separation between the first kink and a second, lower kink of A_g(m4) (60 K in sharp samples, about 100 K for the Au flakes) is a measure of the transition width (p. 9–10). Nothing establishes why the second kink should track the transition width rather than, say, a separate low-temperature crossover. For 4L/Au, the electronic Tc_avg = 220±46 K is not measured. It is obtained by shifting the 6L curve by about 30 K and borrowing its width (p. 12). The statement that the transition "finishes roughly 150 K below the bulk Tc" (abstract) therefore rests on two layers of inference. The authors should:
   - fit the temperature dependence of an order-parameter proxy, such as the A_g(m5) integrated intensity or the A_g(o3)*/A_g(m4) shift in Fig. 3p–t, to a distribution of local Tc values (for example the Supplementary Note 4 model) and extract a centre and width with uncertainties;
   - state that the lower bound near 180 K is model-dependent.

6. The excitonic-versus-lattice decomposition is overstated. The Discussion concludes that Au mainly weakens electron-hole rather than electron-lattice interactions. This rests on two observations: −1/q peaks at 0.38 versus 0.45 (a 16% reduction), and the quasi-elastic peak disappears in 4L/Au. From these the paper assigns about 150 K of the 326 K to "excitonic mechanisms" and about 180 K to the lattice (p. 16). There are several problems:
   - The quasi-elastic signal at 10 cm⁻¹ in 4L/Au is measured at a ×50 reduced intensity. It sits right next to a Rayleigh leakage peak at 15 cm⁻¹ that was "removed … and replaced via interpolation" (Methods). Its non-detection may be a sensitivity limit, so a detection limit relative to the 18L peak should be given.
   - Metallic screening also renormalizes electron-phonon coupling and the Fano continuum, so −1/q is not a clean electron-lattice measure.
   - The maximal local suppression of the interfacial layer is not measured. It is inferred from the end of a broadened transition, so it bounds the excitonic contribution only within a particular model.

   This part should be recast as a hypothesis consistent with the data, and the abstract's "We discuss the role of excitonic physics" kept at that level. Alternatively, the authors could support it with a quantitative comparison, for example the expected Tc suppression from image-charge screening of the exciton binding energy versus distance from Au.

7. Lateral inhomogeneity has not been ruled out. The gradual onset could also arise from in-plane variation of Tc within the laser spot, caused by Au roughness, wrinkles, bubbles or variation in contact quality on amorphous evaporated Au, rather than from a vertical gradient. Raman maps across the 4L/Au flake at one or two temperatures inside the broadened window (for example the A_g(m5)/background ratio at 220 K) would test this. If the response is uniform at the spot scale, the vertical picture becomes more compelling.

8. The thickness series is not matched across substrates. Al2O3 has 7L and 4L, and Au has 18L, 6L and 4L. With only one matched thickness, the substrate effect and the intrinsic thickness effect cannot be separated cleanly. For example, the "intrinsic" Tc at 6L, without Au, is unknown. Adding a 6L/Al2O3 and ideally a 2L flake on either substrate would make Fig. 5 a real phase diagram.

C. Minor

9. Page 12: "By comparing −1/q of the 6L and 4L flakes on Au on the same plot (see Fig. 3o)" should refer to Fig. 4o.

10. The structural Tc criterion uses the kink of the A_g(o3)* mode, which appears in the ac channel only through polarizer misalignment (p. 7). The amount of leakage may differ between flakes and could bias the kink determination. Please state the extinction ratio for each sample, or confirm the kinks against the A_g(m5) onset alone.

11. The second-derivative maps (Fig. 3k–o) are computed from data smoothed with 10–15 K Gaussian kernels in temperature (Methods). This smoothing broadens features in T and is comparable to the step size near Tc. Please confirm that the peak positions in Fig. 3p–t come from the unsmoothed spectra, and state the smoothing used for the Fano fits, if any.

12. Fig. 4k–o: the text says fit error bars are smaller than the symbols, but parameter correlations between q, γ and the background in Eq. (1) over 20–80 cm⁻¹ are likely significant. Please state which parameters were free, whether a background term was included, and give uncertainties that include correlations.

13. Fig. 5a/b uses the full ±40 K and ±46 K as vertical error bars. These represent a physical width rather than an uncertainty, so a different symbol, such as a shaded range, would avoid conflating the two.

14. Page 15: "Fig. 4r, t" for the quasi-elastic peak omits the 6L panel (s), which is the one showing the broadening. Please check the panel references.

15. Charge-transfer direction: the argument uses a Ta2NiSe5 work function of about 5.4 eV. Please give the assumed work function of the amorphous evaporated Au with a Cr underlayer, which may differ from Au(111), and say whether the sign and magnitude of the transferred charge are expected to be significant over a 1–2 layer depth.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a metal-without-evaporation control and a thin-spacer control (Concern 1). This is the single experiment that would turn the screening and charge-transfer interpretation into a demonstrated result.
- Include an optical transfer-matrix calculation of layer-resolved Raman weights in the toy model, and fit the measured temperature dependences to a distribution of local Tc values (Concerns 3, 5).
- Document laser heating with anti-Stokes/Stokes temperatures and a power series (Concern 4).
- Moderate the excitonic/lattice partition in the Discussion and abstract to match what the data can bear (Concern 6).

5. Verdict: major revision
