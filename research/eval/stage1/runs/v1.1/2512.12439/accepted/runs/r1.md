Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors present an all-dry evaporation and Au cold-welding transfer that gives few-layer Ta2NiSe5 flakes on amorphous Au and on ALD Al2O3. Using ac-channel polarized Raman, they track the orthorhombic-to-monoclinic transition and the electronic transition (Fano asymmetry of B2g(o1), quasi-elastic intensity at 10 cm^-1) in five flakes. A 4L flake on Al2O3 shows a sharp transition near 280-287 K. 6L and 4L flakes on Au show transitions that are lower and broader (Tc^avg about 250 K and 220 K, each ±40 K). The authors read this as an interface Tc gradient caused by screening and charge transfer from Au, and use it to estimate the excitonic share of the 326 K Tc. Substrate-controlled charge tuning of an excitonic-insulator candidate in the few-layer limit is new and of broad interest. The fabrication method may also prove useful for other rippled van der Waals compounds.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The transfer technique is new, practical and general. It routinely gives 10-100 µm few-layer flakes of a compound that is hard to exfoliate (Fig. 1), and it is done entirely in Ar.
- The Raman methodology is careful:
  - phonon symmetries are assigned by azimuthal dependence;
  - bare-substrate spectra are measured in both channels (Fig. 2f, g), which justifies using the ac channel;
  - structural markers (Ag(m4)/Ag(m5) onset, the kink of Ag(o3)*) and electronic markers (-1/q, χ''(10 cm^-1)) are tracked together.
- The 18L/Au and 7L/Al2O3 flakes reproduce the bulk Tc, which anchors the method. The structural and electronic Tc values agree for the sharp-transition flakes.
- The qualitative contrast in Figs. 3 and 4 between the sharp transitions (7L and 4L on Al2O3, 18L on Au) and the broadened, lowered ones (6L and 4L on Au) is clear.
- The data are deposited on Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The Au-versus-Al2O3 comparison changes several things besides metallicity, so the attribution to screening and charge transfer is not yet established.
   - **Claim at stake.** The abstract and Discussion (pp. 13-15) attribute the lowered, broadened transition on Au to electrostatic screening and charge transfer that create a Tc gradient perpendicular to the layers. The only evidence is the contrast with 4L on Al2O3. The authors argue this rules out degradation and leaves metallicity as the difference (p. 15).
   - **What else differs between the two interfaces:**
     - (i) Formation. On Au, the interface is made by thermally evaporating Au directly onto the rippled Ta2NiSe5 surface. Hot metal adatoms are known to create defects and intermixing (for example Au-Se bonding) in chalcogenide top layers. On Al2O3, the interface is made by ALD with TMA/H2O at 100-150 °C.
     - (ii) Strain. A conformal metal film bonded to the ripples (the authors say Au "conforms better to the surface ripples and binds more strongly", p. 4) will impose different strain, and a different thermal-contraction mismatch on cooling, than amorphous Al2O3. The authors' own uniaxial-pressure work (Ref. 16) shows Tc in Ta2NiSe5 is very strain-sensitive. Laterally inhomogeneous strain from a rough amorphous Au film would broaden the transition within the ~1 µm laser spot, with no vertical gradient at all.
     - (iii) Optics. The optical environment differs (see item 4).
   - **What is missing.** No measurement in the paper separates a vertical electronic gradient from strain or interface damage. The evidence that the broadening is vertical rather than lateral is the thickness dependence (broadening in 6L and 4L, none in 18L). That trend would also follow from a strain or defect layer near the interface.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Compare room-temperature and low-temperature phonon frequencies (for example Ag(o1)-Ag(o8), and Ag(m4)/Ag(m5) at 100 K) between 4L/Al2O3 and 4L/Au, as a strain indicator.
     - Map the transition (or the 100 K Ag(m5) frequency and intensity) across a flake on Au, to test lateral homogeneity.
     - Ideally, add a flake on Au with a thin insulating spacer (1-2 nm Al2O3 or hBN) made by the same evaporation route. This keeps the strain and optical environment largely unchanged but blocks charge transfer.
     - If none of these can be done, recast the screening/charge-transfer picture and the "Tc gradient perpendicular to the layers" in the abstract as one hypothesis among others, and discuss strain and interface damage explicitly.

B. Should address

2. Each condition rests on a single flake.
   - **Where.** The central comparison (4L/Al2O3 at ~280 K against 4L/Au at 220±40 K) and the Fig. 5 phase diagram use one flake per thickness and substrate.
   - **Why it matters.** Tc in thin flakes on insulators is known to vary (the authors cite 309 K for 8L on SiO2). Without repeats, the ~40 K reduction for 4L/Al2O3, and the gap between it and 4L/Au, cannot be separated from flake-to-flake spread.
   - **What to do.** Add at least a second 4L (or 4-6L) flake on each substrate, even if only the Ag(m5) onset is measured. State how many flakes were screened.

3. The quasi-elastic peak in 4L/Au may have been missed for technical reasons, which weakens the inference that Au mainly suppresses excitonic fluctuations.
   - **The inference (p. 15).** Au "suppress[es] its magnitude beyond detection", which "affirms" the excitonic nature of the quasi-elastic peak. Because -1/q keeps 84% of its maximum, the authors conclude that Au weakens electron-hole rather than electron-lattice interactions (pp. 15-16, abstract).
   - **Reasons the peak could be undetectable regardless of any change in excitonic fluctuations:**
     - The 4L/Au signal is about 50 times weaker (scaling factors in Figs. 3e and 4e).
     - A Rayleigh leakage peak at 15 cm^-1 was removed and interpolated for exactly these flakes (Methods, p. 19), 5 cm^-1 from the 10 cm^-1 evaluation point.
     - A Tc spread of ±40 K across layers would by itself smear a peak that is only ~30-40 K wide in the sharp flakes (Fig. 4p-r) into a plateau.
   - **Assumptions not justified.** Comparing -1/q (a ratio, insensitive to overall intensity) with the absolute quasi-elastic intensity is not like for like. The argument also assumes that an amplitude reduction of -1/q could only come from weaker electron-phonon coupling.
   - **What to do:**
     - Show the raw low-frequency spectra for 6L and 4L/Au, before Rayleigh removal and Bose correction, with the noise level.
     - Simulate the quasi-elastic response expected from the layer-resolved Tc model of Supplementary Note 4, to test whether broadening alone explains the non-detection.
     - Temper the electron-hole versus electron-lattice conclusion.

4. The depth weighting of the Raman signal near a metal is not discussed, yet the layer-resolved interpretation depends on it.
   - **The issue.** At a metal surface the tangential optical field is strongly reduced. With in-plane polarizations at 632.8 nm, the Ta2NiSe5 layer next to Au therefore contributes less Raman signal than the top layer. This is consistent with the ~50× intensity drop of 4L/Au relative to 4L/Al2O3 (Figs. 3b, e). In the Al2O3 case, the 50-100 nm Al2O3 spacer on Au gives a different interference condition.
   - **Why it matters.** The claim that the interfacial layers are "detectable in the overall signal" (p. 13) and the ~150 K local suppression assigned to them (p. 13, Fig. 5b inset) both depend on how much the bottom layers are weighted.
   - **What to do.** Include a transfer-matrix estimate of the layer-resolved Raman efficiency for 4L and 6L on Au and on Al2O3/Au. Use it in the toy model of Supplementary Note 4. If the bottom layer is strongly down-weighted, the ~150 K local suppression would need revising (likely upward), or the picture would need to change.

5. The broadening estimates are ad hoc, and the structural and electronic Tc^avg for 4L/Au are not independent.
   - **Structural broadening.** The ±40 K width comes from assuming that the separation between the first and second kinks of Ag(m4) should be 60 K, as in the sharp flakes. Any excess (100 K for 6L and 4L/Au) is then read as broadening (pp. 9-10). The second kink has no stated physical origin, and its separation from Tc could itself depend on strain or doping.
   - **Electronic Tc for 4L/Au.** It is obtained by shifting the 6L -1/q curve by 30 K and borrowing the 6L width (p. 12). The claimed agreement between the structural and electronic 220 K values is therefore partly built in.
   - **What to do.** Fit the temperature dependence of the Ag(m4)/Ag(m5) integrated intensity (which scales with the monoclinic order parameter squared), and -1/q, with a model that has an explicit Tc distribution. Report the fitted centre and width with uncertainties. State plainly that the 4L/Au electronic Tc is an estimate from the 6L data, not an independent measurement.

6. The quantitative excitonic/lattice split (~150 K excitonic, ~180 K lattice) overreaches.
   - **Where.** p. 16.
   - **Why.** The split assumes that Au removes the excitonic contribution in the interfacial layers and leaves lattice and electron-lattice contributions intact. It also measures the suppression from the bulk 326 K. The 4L/Al2O3 flake already sits at ~280-287 K, so the Au-specific suppression at equal thickness is closer to 100 K. Even the lower edge of the 4L/Au range (~180 K) need not mean the excitonic part has been fully quenched.
   - **What to do.** Measure the Au effect against 4L/Al2O3. Present the 150 K as an upper bound that depends on these assumptions, or remove the numerical partition.

7. The charge-transfer scenario has no direct evidence and no magnitude estimate.
   - **What is missing.** The paper gives no estimate of the induced carrier density from the Au/Ta2NiSe5 work-function difference. Amorphous Au on Cr is likely near 5.0-5.1 eV, against ≈5.4 eV for Ta2NiSe5, so the expected transfer is modest. Nor is there a comparison with the Tc versus density measured by gating in atomically thin flakes (Ref. 50) or by K dosing (Refs. 25-28).
   - **What to do.** An order-of-magnitude estimate of the transferred density, and of the Tc shift it implies from those references, would show whether charge transfer or image-charge screening can plausibly give ~100 K. Any direct probe (doping-sensitive phonon shifts, Kelvin probe or XPS on a Au-supported flake) would strengthen the claim.

8. Laser heating is not documented, and it could differ between substrates.
   - **Where.** At ~1 mW and 632.8 nm, the Methods (p. 18) state that the temperature was checked by the Stokes/anti-Stokes ratio, but no data are shown.
   - **Why it matters.** Heat sinking differs between a flake on 20-30 nm Au and a flake on 50-100 nm ALD Al2O3. Heating would lower the apparent Tc, so it bears directly on the ~40 K reduction of 4L/Al2O3 and on the Au-Al2O3 difference.
   - **What to do.** Show the Stokes/anti-Stokes temperatures against nominal temperature for each flake, or a power dependence of the Ag(m5) onset for one flake per substrate. Clarify how temperature is calibrated for measurements above 300 K, which are made outside the cryostat according to p. 18, since every Tc lies close to that crossover.

C. Minor

9. On p. 12, "see Fig. 3o" should read "Fig. 4o" (the -1/q comparison of 6L and 4L on Au).

10. Layer counts on amorphous Au rely on AFM (Supplementary Notes 5-6). Rough Au and interfacial adsorbates make ±1 layer errors possible, and these matter for 4L against 6L. Low-frequency interlayer shear or breathing modes, or optical contrast, would independently confirm the counts. Please state the AFM step-height uncertainty in the main text.

11. The bare-substrate spectra in Fig. 2f and 2g are labelled with different temperatures for Au/Cr (250 K and 260 K) and are compared with flake spectra at 295 K and 100 K. Please state why these temperatures were chosen, or show matched temperatures.

12. Wording of the local Tc values:
    - p. 4 and p. 13 say the surface layers have "a local Tc closer to room temperature", yet the upper edge of the 4L/Au range (~260 K) is below the 280-287 K of 4L/Al2O3. Please make the wording consistent with the numbers.
    - "Finishes roughly 150 K below the bulk Tc" in the abstract would read more clearly as the lower edge of a ±40 K estimate.

13. The -1/q error bars in Fig. 4k-o are described as smaller than the symbols, but these are statistical fit errors only. Please show how -1/q depends on the fit window (20-80 cm^-1) and on whether a background term is included. This matters most for the weak 4L/Au spectra, where the Ag(o1)*/Ag(m1) peak and residual Au background may enter the fit.

14. The samples were heated to ~400 K. State whether any flake was remeasured after the temperature cycle, and whether Tc or the line shapes changed. ALD at 100-150 °C with H2O could also alter the Al2O3 interface; a comment would help.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add one decisive control that separates electronic from mechanical or chemical interface effects. The best option is a thin insulating spacer between the Au and Ta2NiSe5, made by the same evaporation and cold-welding route. A Raman map of the transition across a Au-supported flake would also help.
- Replace the kink-separation broadening estimate with a forward model: a layer-resolved Tc profile plus layer-resolved Raman efficiency from a transfer-matrix calculation. Fit it simultaneously to the Ag(m5) intensity, -1/q and the quasi-elastic intensity for 4L and 6L on Au.
- Measure a second 4L flake on each substrate. Consider a thicker intermediate flake on Au (for example 8-10L) to test the predicted crossover from interface-dominated to bulk-like behaviour.
- Move key supporting information into the main text or a short summary: the Stokes/anti-Stokes temperature check, the AFM thickness determination and the toy-model result.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
