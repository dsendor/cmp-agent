Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors synthesise [3]-triangulene spin-1 chains (N = 3–14) on thin Au(111) films grown on Nb(110), so that the chains sit on a proximitized superconductor. With a superconducting tip they observe a clear even–odd alternation at the chain ends: odd-N chains show sub-gap YSR resonances, while even-N chains show extra-gap singlet–triplet excitations and no resolvable YSR state. They explain the alternation with a two-site, zero-bandwidth model of exchange-coupled edge spins. The main advances are the platform (on-surface Ullmann/cyclodehydrogenation chemistry on a superconducting substrate) and a clean, length-resolved demonstration that inter-edge exchange gates the coupling of fractional edge spins to the condensate. Both will interest the molecular-magnetism, YSR and Haldane-chain communities. The physical mechanism itself (a singlet does not bind a YSR state; a triplet does, with a renormalized coupling) is largely expected, so the novelty lies mainly in the experimental realisation.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First on-surface synthesis of extended triangulene Haldane chains on a proximitized superconductor, with bond-resolved images showing intact chains (Fig. 2c,d; Figs. S3–S14) and a superconducting gap that survives the 340 °C anneal.
- A systematic data set over a wide range of lengths (N = 3–14), with spectra from both termini of each chain in the SI.
- The dehydrogenation control (Figs. S15, S16) is a strong, direct test. Passivating one end of an N = 8 chain removes the ST peaks and creates a YSR state at the other end. This shows that the absence of YSR in even chains comes from inter-edge correlation, not from a weakly coupled or non-magnetic terminus.
- The tip-approach series (Fig. S15c) places the edge spins in the weak-coupling regime (J_K ≪ Δ), which justifies the free-spin-ground-state picture used in the model.
- The extracted inter-edge |J|(N) agrees well with BLBQ theory (Fig. 3e). Mapping the collective spin modes (Fig. 4) adds a bulk Haldane-chain signature on the same platform.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The central claim, a parity-dependent presence or absence of YSR states set by the inter-edge exchange, is supported by the length series and, above all, by the dehydrogenation control in Fig. S16.

B. Should address

1. Statistics behind the parity rule are not reported. Except for N = 8 and N = 11 (Figs. S8c, S11), the SI shows one chain per length. The main text never says how many chains, ends or sample preparations were measured for each N, or whether any chain broke the rule. Fig. 3d shows several green points per N but does not say what they are (different chains? both ends?). The caption also admits that "a few cases were found outside the mapped range". Since the headline claim is a binary rule (YSR for odd N, none for even N), readers need to know how general it is. The authors should add a table, in the SI or the main text, listing for each N: the number of chains measured, the number of ends with and without a resolvable YSR peak, the δE_YSR values, and E_ST/E_TS. They should also state explicitly whether any even chain with N ≤ 10 showed a sub-gap YSR state, or any odd chain lacked one.

2. The headline N = 11 example lies outside the plotted range, and this affects the claimed model agreement. SI Section S2 gives δE_YSR ≈ 0.78–0.8 meV for the Fig. 2 chain. The δE_YSR axis of Fig. 3d stops at 0.5 meV, and the odd-chain model band (J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV) tops out near 0.4–0.5 meV. The free-spin limit of the model gives a binding energy of 3J_K/4 ≈ 0.45 meV. The representative chain of the main text therefore needs J_K well above the quoted range, and points like it are left out of Fig. 3d. The statement "the model reproduces the experimental evolution well" is then hard to judge. Please:
   - plot all measured points, extending the axis or marking out-of-range points;
   - give the J_K needed for each outlier;
   - discuss whether the spread in J_K (attributed to the herringbone, refs. 44, 45) can be tested, for example by correlating δE_YSR with adsorption site relative to fcc/hcp/soliton regions in the images you already have.

3. The comparison does not test the parity-specific renormalization. The quantitative signature of the model is J_K*/J_K → 2/3 for odd chains (an effective spin-1 impurity) and → 1 as |J| → 0. Yet the odd-chain data in Fig. 3d scatter by roughly ±0.2 meV at every N, which hides any systematic increase of binding energy with N. On the even side, the model predicts a small but non-zero J_K* (Fig. 3c), not exactly zero. The predicted binding energies for N = 6–10 (about 0–0.15 meV) appear comparable to the dGSJ linewidth. So "no YSR in even chains" may partly be a resolution limit rather than a strict absence. Two analyses would make the comparison more than qualitative:
   - (a) Fit the even-chain spectra with the same two-Lorentzian procedure used for odd chains and the N = 14 inset of Fig. S14. Report upper bounds on any YSR component for N = 4–10 and compare them with the model's red band.
   - (b) Compare like with like at the same chain end. For chains where the dehydrogenation was done, δE_YSR before and after passivation gives J_K*/J_K directly, without assuming a site-averaged J_K. Fig. S15 (N = 14, before and after) is already such a measurement and should be compared quantitatively with Fig. 3c. A similar comparison for at least one odd chain would test the 2/3 factor directly.

   Also state how many passivated chains enter J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV. Only N = 14 and N = 8 are shown, and the N = 14 value (δE_YSR = 0.36 meV) implies J_K ≈ 0.48 meV through 3J_K/4.

4. The claim that "fractionalization survives the coupling to the superconductor" is stronger than the test performed. The abstract poses this as an open question. However, the measured J_K ≈ 0.6 meV is far below both J_0 ≈ 18 meV and the Haldane gap. In that regime the edge states are expected to be essentially unchanged by the substrate. The data show they are, but this is not a demanding test. Likewise, the YSR map of Fig. 2f shows weight on the terminal triangulene with a radical-like shape. That is consistent with a fractional edge spin but does not by itself "establish the topological origin" (p. 5): a single unpaired radical on a terminal unit would look similar. Two things would support the topological reading more directly:
   - map the decay of the edge-state/YSR weight over the first two or three units, and compare it with the BLBQ correlation length (Fig. S17b already gives the theoretical distribution);
   - compare E_ST(N) and the CS energies with those of the same chains on bulk Au(111) (ref. 13), to show quantitatively that the superconducting substrate leaves the spin Hamiltonian unchanged.

   Otherwise, please tone down the "open question/survives" and "establishing the topological origin" wording.

5. The statement that the collective modes are "decoupled from the superconductor by the much larger Haldane gap" is asserted, not tested. Fig. 4 shows that the CS energies and nodal patterns match the BLBQ model, using J_0 ≈ 18 meV and β ≈ 0.09 taken from ref. 13. It is not stated whether these values were refitted for the Au/Nb system. It is also unclear what measurable sign of coupling to the superconductor (shift, broadening, or sub-gap replica) is being ruled out. Please:
   - state whether J_0 and β were fixed or fitted;
   - compare the CS energies and linewidths quantitatively with ref. 13 on bulk Au(111);
   - replace "perfectly reproduced" (p. 9) with a quantitative measure of agreement.

6. The weak-coupling regime is established on one chain end only. The tip-approach evidence for J_K ≪ Δ (Fig. S15c) comes from a single passivated N = 14 chain. The model's ground-state identification depends on this regime holding for all chains, including the outliers of concern 2, which have the largest binding energies and are the most likely to approach the quantum phase transition. A similar approach series on one or two odd chains with large δE_YSR (such as the N = 11 chain of Fig. 2) would close this gap.

C. Minor

7. Text and caption conflict on where YSR first appears in even chains. The text (p. 7) says YSR "can be already detected for N = 12 (Fig. S12) and N = 14". The Fig. 3d caption says "Incipient YSR signal in even chains appears only for N ≥ 14". Please reconcile.

8. SI cross-references are off. The main text refers to "Section S6" for the BLBQ model and "Section S5" for the two-site model. The Fig. 3d caption cites "Section S4" for J_K from single-edge chains. The SI contains only S1–S5: BLBQ is S4, the two-site model is S5, and the passivation data are in S3. The SI also mentions "Supplementary Fig. 5" for N = 5 node patterns (it is Fig. S5d).

9. Arithmetic in SI S2. Δ_sample − ε_YSR = 1.2 − 0.47 = 0.73 meV, not 0.78 meV. The statement that the result is "independent of the Δ_sample value used" also needs explaining, since the formula depends on Δ_sample explicitly.

10. Scaling description on p. 5. The ST gap is said to scale "inversely with the chain length, an exponentially decaying correlation length". Inverse and exponential scaling are different; please state which the model predicts and which the data in Fig. 3e follow.

11. Fig. 2e caption. It states the YSR resonances lie "at energy J_K* below the dGSJ peaks", which conflates a coupling constant with a binding energy. The binding energy is a non-linear function of J_K* (Eq. S6 and Eqs. S8–S9).

12. Fig. 3e error bars. No uncertainties are given for the experimental |J|, especially for the odd-chain points taken from weak TS shoulders (e.g. E_TS ≈ 0.5 mV for N = 13, labelled "faint"). Please add error bars and mark which points come from ST and which from TS.

13. TS/ST intensity ratio (SI S4). The argument that TS peaks are "frequently below one third" of ST peaks because of a stronger J_K* is qualitative. Either quantify the ratio across N, or present it as a hypothesis.

14. Temperature. The temperature is given as 1.2 K (Methods, Fig. 2) and 1.3 K (SI S4). The effective electron temperature is not stated, and it matters for the thermal triplet population of the longest even chains, where E_ST ≈ 0.5 meV.

15. Small errors:
    - p. 3: "(Fig. 1a)" should be Fig. 1c for the herringbone.
    - Fig. 2f caption: "ends f the".
    - Fig. S1b: the axis is labelled "Bias (mV)" while the text quotes −0.5 V.
    - SI S4 title: "Spin model of for the".
    - Ref. 43 is given as "Phy. Rev. B".
    - SI refs 3 and 6 are duplicates.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a per-length statistics table (chains, ends, YSR yes/no, δE_YSR, E_ST/E_TS) and show every data point in Fig. 3d, including outliers.
- Use before/after-passivation pairs on the same terminus to measure J_K*/J_K directly for both parities, and compare these with Fig. 3c. This would turn the qualitative model agreement into a quantitative test of the 2/3 renormalization.
- Put upper bounds on hidden YSR components in even chains by fitting them with the same dGSJ+YSR line-shape procedure used for N = 14.
- Compare edge (ST/TS) and bulk (CS) excitation energies directly with the bulk-Au(111) data of ref. 13, to support the claim that the superconducting substrate leaves the fractional edge states and Haldane gap intact.
