Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors synthesize triangulene spin-1 chains (N = 3–14) on ~25–30 ML Au(111) films grown on Nb(110). They report a clear even/odd alternation in how the chain-end states couple to the proximity-induced superconducting gap. Odd chains show Yu–Shiba–Rusinov (YSR) resonances. Even chains show only extra-gap singlet–triplet (ST) inelastic replicas, and YSR signal appears only once the inter-edge coupling |J| falls to about J_K. The platform result is significant in itself: on-surface Ullmann/cyclodehydrogenation chemistry on a superconducting surface with an intact, sharp proximity gap. The parity-dependent YSR fingerprint of Haldane edge spins is new and of broad interest to the molecular-magnetism and spin–superconductor hybrid communities. The theoretical side (a zero-bandwidth two-impurity model and a BLBQ model with literature parameters) is mostly qualitative.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A technically impressive platform. The herringbone reconstruction and Au(111) Shockley state survive on the Au/Nb films (Fig. S1), the proximity gap with dGSJ peaks survives the 340 °C annealing, and bond-resolved imaging confirms intact chains (Fig. 2c,d; Figs. S3–S14).
- A systematic data set across every length from N = 3 to 14. The parity alternation is visible length by length (Fig. 3a,b), with supporting per-chain figures for each length.
- A good mechanistic control: tip-induced dehydrogenation of one terminus. In an N = 8 chain it removes the ST peaks and creates a YSR state at the opposite end (Fig. S16). In an N = 14 chain it shifts the YSR state deeper into the gap (Fig. S15). This directly shows that the inter-edge coupling, not a lack of edge magnetism, suppresses YSR states in even chains.
- The extracted |J|(N) from ST/TS energies agrees with the BLBQ model (Fig. 3e). The spatial maps of the collective spin modes (Fig. 4b,c) add a consistent bulk picture.
- Careful treatment of the SIS convolution and negative differential resistance (NDR) using Arnold-type densities of states (Section S2, Fig. S2).

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **Number of chains measured per length, and how universal the parity rule is.** The central claim is categorical: "all odd-length chains host bound YSR states … while even-length chains show no sub-gap YSR signal" (p. 7, abstract). For most lengths, however, the paper shows one chain per N (Figs. S3–S14). The exceptions are two N = 8 chains (Fig. S8c, long-range spectra only) and two N = 11 chains (Fig. S11). The paper itself stresses that J_K varies strongly from site to site, attributing this to the herringbone (p. 6; the Fig. 3d caption notes "a few cases were found outside the mapped range"). The two N = 11 chains already differ visibly in δE_YSR. With J_K spread this large, an odd chain at a weakly coupled site could have its YSR state hidden in the dGSJ peak. Likewise, an even chain at a strongly coupled site could show a sub-gap state. The universality of the alternation is therefore not established by the data shown.
   - Report, for each N, how many chains (and how many termini) were measured, how many show resolvable YSR states, and the full distribution of δE_YSR. For example, plot every terminus as a point in Fig. 3d, not one value per N.
   - State whether any chains violated the parity rule, and how chains were selected: straight vs. kinked, crossing herringbone elbows, defects.
   - Give the energy resolution used to declare a YSR state "absent", i.e. the minimum δE separable from the dGSJ peak given its width.

**B. Should address**

2. **The showcase N = 11 chain lies outside the regime the model assumes.** Section S2 extracts δE_YSR ≈ 0.78–0.8 meV for the N = 11 chain of Fig. 2. In the two-site model with J_K* → 2J_K/3, this implies J_K ≈ 1.6 meV. That exceeds Δ_sample = 1.2 meV, contradicting the weak-coupling assumption J_K ≪ Δ used throughout Section S5. It also implies about three times the J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV used for the model curves in Fig. 3d. The second N = 11 chain (Fig. S11b) has a smaller δE.
   - Discuss how such large J_K values fit the weak-coupling picture.
   - Say whether the tip-approach test of Fig. S15c, done on only one dehydrogenated N = 14 terminus, also holds for chains with large δE.
   - Consider showing a more representative odd chain in Fig. 2.

3. **The quantitative comparison with the two-site model is weak.** The text says that "the model reproduces the experimental evolution well" (p. 7). The odd-N data in Fig. 3d scatter by roughly as much as the predicted N-dependence, and J_K is a fixed average taken from other chains. As a result, the test reduces to the binary YSR/no-YSR distinction. In addition, the limits stated in Section S5 imply δE = J_K/2 ≈ 0.30 meV (odd, |J| ≫ J_K) and 3J_K/4 ≈ 0.45 meV (J = 0) for J_K = 0.6 meV. The odd-N model curve in Fig. 3d appears to sit closer to ~0.4–0.5 meV. Please check the plotted curve against these formulas.
   - A much sharper test would be a same-terminus comparison on odd chains: measure δE_YSR before and after dehydrogenating the opposite end. For |J| ≫ J_K the model predicts a ratio of about 2/3, independent of the local J_K.
   - The text says that "several chains" were passivated, but only N = 8 and N = 14 are shown. Please present all passivation results, including odd chains, with before/after δE values.

4. **Tip–molecule interaction during spectroscopy.** Section S2/S3 states that attractive forces of the CO tip are largest at junction resistances of 10–20 MΩ, and Fig. S15c shows δE_YSR shifting with tip approach. The low-energy spectra used for Fig. 3d were taken at 5 mV / 500 pA = 10 MΩ, inside this window. Measured δE_YSR values (and hence J_K) therefore carry a setpoint-dependent tip perturbation. This could add to the scatter attributed to herringbone sites.
   - Show δE_YSR versus junction resistance for a few odd chains.
   - Either extrapolate to the far-tunnelling limit or confirm that all chains were measured at a strictly identical setpoint. Include the tip effect in the error budget for J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV.

5. **The ground-state spin and spin-1/2 character are inferred, not measured directly.** That odd chains have an S = 1 ground state, and that the YSR states come from spin-½ edge spins, rests on two inputs: the model, and the spatial shape of the YSR maps resembling "radical states at zigzag edges" (p. 5). The map shape does not distinguish a fractional spin-½ edge state from other local moments on the terminal triangulene. The phrase "thus establishing the topological origin of the observed YSR states" is too strong for this evidence.
   - Useful controls on the same substrate would be YSR spectra of an isolated triangulene monomer (S = 1) and of a dimer (N = 2), measured with the same tip.
   - If feasible, a magnetic-field measurement of the ST/TS excitations in the normal state would confirm the spin multiplicity directly.
   - Otherwise, temper the wording.

6. **Evidence that the bulk modes are decoupled from the substrate.** The claim that the collective spin modes are "decoupled from the superconductor by the much larger Haldane gap" (abstract, p. 9) rests on agreement with a free-chain BLBQ model using parameters from Ref. 13 (J0 ≈ 18 meV, β ≈ 0.09), taken on bulk Au(111).
   - State whether these parameters were refitted.
   - Compare the CS and ST energies quantitatively with those measured on bulk Au(111) (Ref. 13). This is the most direct test that the substrate leaves the chain's magnetism unchanged.
   - Note that "perfectly reproduced" (p. 9) is not quantified. Give energy deviations or overlap measures for Fig. 4c.

7. **The model's level of description.** The two-site Hamiltonian (Eq. S7) is a zero-bandwidth (single-site superconductor) model. Within it, J_K is a model parameter, not the physical exchange coupling ν0 J_K of Eq. S6. Extracting "J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV" and then comparing energies quantitatively mixes these meanings.
   - Clarify what J_K means in the model.
   - State how the ±0.2 meV was obtained: spread across chains, or fit uncertainty.
   - Ideally, benchmark the specific parameter range (|J|/J_K ~ 1–20) against NRG, as is referenced for related systems.
   - The model also omits potential (Coulomb) scattering, which the authors invoke to explain particle–hole asymmetry in Fig. S12. Discuss how it affects the even-N "incipient YSR" identification.

8. **Even-N "incipient YSR" at N = 12/14 is weakly supported and inconsistently stated.** The main text says YSR signal "can be already detected for N = 12 (Fig. S12) and N = 14 (Fig. S14)". The Fig. 3d caption says it "appears only for N ≥ 14". The Fig. 3c caption refers to "absence of YSR states in shorter chains". The N = 12 evidence is only a particle–hole asymmetry of the dGSJ peaks, and N = 14 relies on a decomposition (Fig. S14 inset) whose fit procedure is not described.
   - Reconcile these statements.
   - Describe the two-Lorentzian fitting used to separate YSR from dGSJ (Section S2 end), including uncertainties.

**C. Minor**

9. **Cross-references.** The main text refers to "Section S6" for the BLBQ model and finite-chain spin models (pp. 5, 6, 9), but the SI has only S1–S5; the BLBQ model is S4. The Fig. 3d caption cites "Section S4" for J_K averaged from single-edge chains; that material is in S3. The SI also refers to "Supplementary Fig. 5" for N = 5 maps. Please correct.
10. **Temperature.** The experiment is quoted at 1.2 K (S1, Fig. 2), while the model population uses T = 1.3 K (S4). Unify these, and state the effective electron temperature or energy resolution.
11. **Fig. 2 labels.** Fig. 2e shows a "J*_K" label next to the even-N (red) ST peak; this appears misplaced. Fig. 2f is labelled dI/dV (1 mV), while the YSR peak of this chain in Fig. S11a is at ≈1.3–1.4 mV and Fig. 4b uses 1.5 mV. Clarify which bias was mapped.
12. **Fig. 3d.** The ordinate is inverted. The caption defines δE_YSR = E_dGSJ − E_YSR, while Section S2 uses |E_dGSJ − E_YSR|. Make the sign convention and axis orientation explicit. Also state whether the green points are tip-deconvolved or raw peak differences.
13. **Gap and dGSJ parameters.** Section S2 uses Δ_sample = 1.2 meV as "precalibrated" without saying how. Give the calibration, along with film thickness and dGSJ energy for the films used.
14. **Section S3, J_K statement.** "Systematically shows YSR states at lower energies" (p. 7) is ambiguous: lower absolute energy, or larger binding energy? Rephrase in terms of δE_YSR.
15. **Typos and wording.** "Only the ends f the" (Fig. 2 caption), "with with" (Fig. S9), "Spin model of for the" (S4 title), "comparable J_K" (S5 end). Unify the reference [6] duplicate in the SI (Refs. 3 and 6 are identical).

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a statistics figure: a histogram or scatter of δE_YSR and E_ST/TS for all measured termini versus N, with the number of chains per length. This would make the parity rule quantitative.
2. Perform and show odd-chain one-end dehydrogenation with before/after δE on the same terminus. This is the cleanest parameter-free test of the J_K* → 2J_K/3 renormalization.
3. Add a direct comparison table of ST/TS/CS energies on Au/Nb versus bulk Au(111) (Ref. 13), to substantiate the decoupling and platform-equivalence claims.
4. Tone down "topological origin"/"topologically protected" phrasing, or add a test that directly probes the protection (e.g. robustness of edge-state YSR signatures to bulk defects or kinks in the chain).

**5. Verdict**

Verdict: major revision
