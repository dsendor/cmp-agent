Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors grow spin-1 triangulene (Haldane) chains on a thin Au(111) film that the Nb(110) substrate below makes superconducting by proximity. With a superconducting Nb tip they show that the fractional spin-1/2 edge states couple to the condensate in a way that alternates with chain parity. Odd-N chains show Yu–Shiba–Rusinov (YSR) states at their ends. Even-N chains show only singlet–triplet (ST) excitations above the gap, and YSR states appear in them only once the inter-edge exchange J drops to about J_K (N ≈ 12–14) or one edge is removed by dehydrogenation. As far as I know, this is the first on-surface synthesis of a correlated nanographene spin chain on a superconductor. It also links YSR physics to the Haldane edge-state problem in a clean, length-tunable way, which should interest the molecular-magnetism, YSR and quantum-spin-chain communities. The Haldane phenomenology itself (edge states, the ST/TS alternation, collective modes, BLBQ parameters) is already established on bulk Au(111) (Ref. 13). The new elements are the superconducting platform and the YSR readout of parity.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Synthesis technique: atomically precise triangulene chains are made on a superconducting surface while the herringbone reconstruction, the Shockley state (Fig. S1) and a hard proximity gap with dGSJ peaks (Fig. 1d) are all preserved.
- Systematic dataset: chain lengths N = 3–14, with a bond-resolved image and end spectra for each length (Figs. S3–S14). The odd/even alternation in YSR presence holds over twelve consecutive lengths.
- Direct control experiments: removing one edge spin by tip-induced dehydrogenation (N = 8 in Fig. S16, N = 14 in Fig. S15) turns an even chain with ST excitations and no YSR state into one with an in-gap YSR state. This is strong evidence that the inter-edge singlet, and not weak substrate coupling, suppresses the YSR state.
- Independent parameters: |J| vs N from the ST/TS energies agrees with a BLBQ model whose J0 and β are taken from the literature rather than fitted (Fig. 3e). The spatial structure of the collective modes is reproduced (Fig. 4c, Fig. S17b).
- The tip-approach series in Fig. S15c places the edge spins in the weak-coupling regime, which is the regime the two-site model assumes.
- The SIS deconvolution procedure is documented (Section S2, Fig. S2).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The parity alternation, its link to the edge-spin ground state and the passivation controls together support the main claims. The items below would make the result more quantitative and more robust.

B. Should address

1. **Statistics: chains per length, and how many follow the alternation.** For most lengths the SI shows one chain (two for N = 8 and N = 11). The text says J_K varies from site to site by ±0.2 meV out of 0.6 meV (about 30%) because of the herringbone (p. 6). If so, whether an individual chain shows a resolvable YSR state could depend on where it sits as well as on its parity. Fig. 3d appears to contain several green points for some N, but the number of chains measured per N is never given. Neither is the fraction of odd chains with YSR states and of even chains (N ≤ 10) without them. The statement "all odd-length chains host bound YSR states" (p. 7) needs these numbers behind it. Please give, for each N, the number of chains measured and the number that follow the alternation. Please also report any exceptions, including the out-of-range cases mentioned in the Fig. 3d caption.

2. **Detection threshold for "no YSR" in even chains.** The two-site model curves in Fig. 3d predict a non-zero binding energy in even chains that grows with N: roughly 0.05–0.1 meV around N = 8–10, rising to about 0.25 meV at N = 14. A shift of 0.1 meV is comparable to the width of the dGSJ peak in Figs. 2e and S10. So for intermediate N, "absence" of a YSR state may mean "below resolution", and plotting even-chain points at δE_YSR = 0 in Fig. 3d hides this. Please:
   - fit the even-chain spectra (N = 6, 8, 10) with the same tip-DOS plus Lorentzian procedure used in Section S2 and the Fig. S14 inset;
   - plot the result as an upper bound on δE_YSR;
   - compare that bound with the model prediction for each N.

3. **How J_K\* maps to the plotted δE_YSR, and whether the curves are consistent.** Eqs. S8–S9 give, for J_K1 = J_K2 = J_K:
   - binding energy 3J_K/4 at J = 0 (about 0.45 meV for J_K = 0.6 meV);
   - binding energy J_K/2 for ferromagnetic |J| ≫ J_K (about 0.30 meV).

   For N = 3, |J| ≈ 12 meV, so |J|/J_K ≈ 20 and the odd-chain prediction should be close to 0.3 meV. The blue model points in Fig. 3d look closer to about 0.4 meV, though the inverted axis makes this hard to read. Please state explicitly how the δE_YSR curves in Fig. 3d were computed from Eqs. S8–S9:
   - which equation and J_K value were used;
   - whether |J| is the measured value or the BLBQ value;
   - how the shaded bands propagate the ±0.2 meV spread.

   Please also state how J_K itself was obtained from the passivated-chain binding energies (for example, J_K = 4δE/3?). For instance, the 0.36 meV shift for N = 14 in Fig. S15 would give J_K ≈ 0.48 meV. The claim that the model "reproduces the experimental evolution well" (p. 7) currently rests on a comparison whose construction cannot be checked.

4. **The test of the mechanism mixes chains with different J_K.** The model is compared with data using a population-averaged J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV, while J (from ST/TS energies) and δE_YSR come from individual chains. Given the site-to-site J_K variation, the odd-chain band in Fig. 3d is wide enough to fit most outcomes. The text calls the mechanism "independently validated" (abstract). A sharper test is available within the data: on the chains where both quantities were measured on the same chain, plot δE_YSR (or J_K\*/J_K, using J_K from the passivated end) against the measured |J|/J_K. N = 8 and N = 14 qualify, as would any further passivated odd chains. Even two or three such per-chain points on the Fig. 3c curve would test the parity-dependent renormalization much more directly than the ensemble comparison does. Please also state how many chains were passivated ("several", p. 7) and list their binding energies.

5. **Validity of the two-site model in the regime used.** The model assumes J_K ≪ Δ (Section S5), but the extracted J_K = 0.6 meV is about half of Δ_sample = 1.2 meV. In addition:
   - the single-site (zero-bandwidth) model's J_K is a model parameter rather than the physical exchange; and
   - the edge spin in the real chain is spread over the terminal triangulene and its neighbour, so its coupling to the substrate is not that of a point impurity.

   Please discuss how sensitive the predicted parity contrast (J_K\* → 0 vs J_K\* → 2J_K/3) is to these approximations. The NRG benchmarks in Refs. 27–28 could support this if they cover the relevant J_K/Δ and J/J_K range. The parity contrast itself is a robust ground-state effect, but the quantitative agreement claimed in Fig. 3d depends on these approximations.

6. **J values near the resolution limit and the N = 3 point.** For N ≥ 11 the J values in Fig. 3e come from TS/ST features of about 0.5–0.8 meV. These are weak shoulders that overlap the YSR and dGSJ peaks (Figs. S11–S13). Please give error bars for the experimental |J| in Fig. 3e and explain how the inelastic thresholds were located when YSR resonances are present. In odd chains the YSR channel may itself shift or broaden the TS step. Fig. 3e also shows an experimental point at N = 3 (about 12 meV), but Fig. S3 shows no TS feature in the low-bias window. Please say which spectrum it comes from.

7. **Platform integrity after synthesis.** The claim that the reconstruction and superconducting gap are "unaltered by the annealing procedure" (p. 5) is supported only by a reference to a PhD thesis (Ref. 36). The films are 25–30 ML of Au grown with an Ag surfactant and annealed to 340 °C, so Nb/Ag/Au intermixing could change Δ_sample, the dGSJ energies and the local J_K. Please show substrate spectra and Shockley-state spectra taken on the same sample before and after the synthesis anneal. Please also state the variation of Δ_dGSJ across the surface; Fig. 3b mentions "tip-dependent gap variations" that were aligned out.

C. Minor

8. Fig. 3b: the dGSJ peaks are said to be aligned to remove tip-dependent gap variations. Please describe the alignment: was the bias rescaled by Δ, or shifted? Please also list which tips were used for which chains, since a tip change between chains could correlate with N.

9. Inconsistent statements about when YSR states appear in even chains. Page 7 says emergent YSR signal is detected for N = 12 (Fig. S12) and N = 14. The Fig. 3d caption says incipient YSR signal "appears only for N ≥ 14". Fig. S12 attributes the particle–hole asymmetric dGSJ peaks to incipient YSR states. Please reconcile these.

10. Cross-references to the SI are wrong in several places. The main text refers to "Section S6" for the BLBQ model and to "Section S4" for the passivation-based J_K, but the SI has the BLBQ model in S4, the two-site model in S5, the passivation data in S3, and no S6. Page 3 cites "Fig. 1a" for the herringbone, which is Fig. 1c. The SI text refers to "Supplementary Fig. 5" for the N = 5 maps.

11. Fig. S1: the bias axis is labelled mV, but the caption gives the onset at −0.5 V.

12. Fig. S4 (N = 4): the caption gives the peak at about 8.5 meV and E_ST = 6.3 mV. Please state the subtraction used (Δ_tip + Δ_sample) for all ST/TS energies.

13. The temperature is given as 1.2 K in the experiment (S1) and 1.3 K in the thermal-population model (S4). Please use one value.

14. Section S4 states that TS peaks are often weaker than one third of the ST peaks and attributes this to stronger J_K\* for triplet states. This is plausible but untested. It could be checked by comparing the TS intensity with δE_YSR across odd chains.

15. The proposed origin of the J_K variation (the herringbone, p. 6) could be checked by noting the fcc/hcp/elbow position of each chain end alongside its δE_YSR.

16. The concluding sentences ("new mechanism for encoding and detecting spin correlations", "topologically protected molecular spin qubits") go beyond what was shown, since no coherent control or readout is demonstrated. Please tone them down.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a table (main text or SI) giving, for every chain measured, N, the end-to-end J (ST or TS), δE_YSR at each end, and the tip used. Plot these per-chain points on Fig. 3c/3d.
- Passivate one or two odd chains as well (for example N = 5 and N = 9). Comparing δE_YSR before and after would test the predicted J_K\*/J_K ≈ 2/3 → 1 change for a ferromagnetic J directly, complementing the even-chain passivations.
- Where possible, measure the same chain's ends with a normal-metal tip, or at fields that suppress the tip gap but not the sample gap. This would give the ST/TS and YSR energies without SIS convolution and would help for the weak features at N ≥ 11.
- Discuss the crossover regime N = 12–14 (|J| ~ J_K) more explicitly. It is where the two-site model makes its most distinctive prediction: the YSR binding energy in even chains rises continuously with N.
