B. Should address

1. **Statistics behind the parity rule.** The central claim is categorical: "all odd-length chains host bound YSR states … while even-length chains show no sub-gap YSR signal" (p. 7, abstract). For most lengths, though, the SI shows one chain per N (Figs. S3–S14). The exceptions are two N = 8 chains (Fig. S8c, long-range spectra only) and two N = 11 chains (Fig. S11), and the two N = 11 chains already differ visibly in δE_YSR. The paper itself says J_K varies from site to site by ±0.2 meV out of 0.6 meV (about 30%) because of the herringbone (p. 6), and the Fig. 3d caption admits that "a few cases were found outside the mapped range". With a spread this large, an odd chain at a weakly coupled site could have its YSR state hidden in the dGSJ peak, and an even chain at a strongly coupled site could show a sub-gap state. So whether a given chain shows a resolvable YSR state could depend on where it sits as well as on its parity. Fig. 3d shows several green points for some N but does not say what they are (different chains? both ends?). The authors should add a table, in the SI or the main text, listing for each N: the number of chains, termini and sample preparations measured; the number of ends with and without a resolvable YSR peak; the δE_YSR values at each end; E_ST/E_TS; and the tip used. They should plot every terminus as its own point in Fig. 3d. They should state explicitly whether any even chain with N ≤ 10 showed a sub-gap YSR state or any odd chain lacked one, and how chains were selected (straight vs. kinked, crossing herringbone elbows, defects).

2. **Detection threshold for "no YSR" in even chains.** The two-site model (Fig. 3c,d) predicts a small but non-zero J_K\* in even chains, and the binding energy grows with N: roughly 0–0.15 meV for N = 6–10 (about 0.05–0.1 meV around N = 8–10), rising to about 0.25 meV at N = 14. A shift of about 0.1 meV is comparable to the width of the dGSJ peak in Figs. 2e and S10. For intermediate N, "absence" of a YSR state may therefore mean "below resolution", and plotting the even-chain points at δE_YSR = 0 in Fig. 3d hides this. The authors should state the energy resolution used to declare a YSR state "absent", i.e. the minimum δE that can be separated from the dGSJ peak given its width. They should fit the even-chain spectra (N = 4–10) with the same tip-DOS plus Lorentzian (dGSJ + YSR) procedure used for odd chains in Section S2 and for the Fig. S14 inset, plot the result as an upper bound on δE_YSR, and compare that bound with the model's red band for each N.

3. **How the model curves in Fig. 3d were built, and whether they match the stated formulas.** For J_K1 = J_K2 = J_K, Eqs. S8–S9 give a binding energy of 3J_K/4 at J = 0 (about 0.45 meV for J_K = 0.6 meV) and J_K/2 for ferromagnetic |J| ≫ J_K (about 0.30 meV). For N = 3, |J| ≈ 12 meV, so |J|/J_K ≈ 20 and the odd-chain prediction should be close to 0.3 meV. The odd-N model curve in Fig. 3d appears to sit at about 0.4–0.5 meV instead, though the inverted axis makes this hard to read. The claim that the model "reproduces the experimental evolution well" (p. 7) currently rests on a comparison whose construction cannot be checked. The authors should check the plotted curves against these formulas and state explicitly: which equation and J_K value were used; whether |J| is the measured or the BLBQ value; and how the shaded bands propagate the ±0.2 meV spread. They should also state how J_K was obtained from the passivated-chain binding energies (for example J_K = 4δE/3?); the 0.36 meV shift for N = 14 in Fig. S15 would give J_K ≈ 0.48 meV. Finally, they should say how many passivated chains enter J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV, and whether ±0.2 meV is the spread across chains or a fit uncertainty.

4. **The showcase N = 11 chain lies outside the model's regime and outside the plotted range.** Section S2 gives δE_YSR ≈ 0.78–0.8 meV for the N = 11 chain of Fig. 2. In the two-site model with J_K\* → 2J_K/3 this implies J_K ≈ 1.6 meV. That is about three times the J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV used for the Fig. 3d curves. It also exceeds Δ_sample = 1.2 meV, against the weak-coupling assumption J_K ≪ Δ used throughout Section S5. The δE_YSR axis of Fig. 3d stops at 0.5 meV, so points like this one are left out of the comparison. The second N = 11 chain (Fig. S11b) has a smaller δE. The authors should plot all measured points, extending the axis or marking out-of-range points. They should give the J_K each outlier requires, discuss how such large values fit the weak-coupling picture, and consider showing a more representative odd chain in Fig. 2.

5. **A same-chain test of the parity-dependent renormalization.** The model is compared with data using a population-averaged J_K, while J (from ST/TS energies) and δE_YSR come from individual chains. Given the site-to-site J_K variation, the odd-chain data scatter by roughly ±0.2 meV at every N, about as much as the predicted N-dependence. This hides any systematic increase of binding energy with N, and the odd-chain band is wide enough to fit most outcomes. The test therefore reduces to the binary YSR/no-YSR distinction, yet the abstract calls the mechanism "independently validated". The quantitative signature of the model is J_K\*/J_K → 2/3 for odd chains (an effective spin-1 impurity) and → 1 as |J| → 0. A same-terminus measurement tests this without assuming a site-averaged J_K. Fig. S15 (N = 14, before and after passivation) is already such a measurement and should be compared quantitatively with Fig. 3c, as should N = 8. The authors should also passivate one or two odd chains (for example N = 5 and N = 9) and compare δE_YSR on the same terminus before and after. For |J| ≫ J_K the model predicts a ratio of about 2/3, independent of the local J_K. They should plot these per-chain points (δE_YSR or J_K\*/J_K against the measured |J|/J_K) on Fig. 3c. The text says "several chains" were passivated (p. 7), but only N = 8 and N = 14 are shown. All passivation results should be listed with before/after δE values.

6. **Validity and meaning of the two-site model in the regime used.** The model assumes J_K ≪ Δ (Section S5), but the extracted J_K = 0.6 meV is about half of Δ_sample = 1.2 meV. Eq. S7 is a zero-bandwidth (single-site superconductor) model. Within it, J_K is a model parameter, not the physical exchange ν0 J_K of Eq. S6, so extracting "J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV" and then comparing energies quantitatively mixes the two meanings. In the real chain the edge spin is spread over the terminal triangulene and its neighbour, so its coupling to the substrate is not that of a point impurity. The model also omits potential (Coulomb) scattering, which the authors invoke to explain the particle–hole asymmetry in Fig. S12. The parity contrast (J_K\* → 0 vs J_K\* → 2J_K/3) is a robust ground-state effect, but the quantitative agreement claimed in Fig. 3d depends on these approximations. The authors should clarify what J_K means in the model and discuss how sensitive the predictions are to these approximations. Ideally they should benchmark the parameter range used (|J|/J_K ~ 1–20, J_K/Δ ~ 0.5) against NRG; Refs. 27–28 may help if they cover it. They should also discuss how potential scattering affects the identification of "incipient YSR" in even chains.

7. **Weak coupling is established on one chain end only.** The tip-approach evidence for J_K ≪ Δ (Fig. S15c) comes from a single passivated N = 14 terminus. The model's ground-state identification depends on this regime holding for all chains. That includes the outliers of concern 4, which have the largest binding energies and are the most likely to be near the quantum phase transition. The authors should repeat the approach series on one or two odd chains with large δE_YSR, such as the N = 11 chain of Fig. 2.

8. **Tip–molecule interaction at the spectroscopy setpoint.** Sections S2/S3 state that attractive forces of the CO tip are largest at junction resistances of 10–20 MΩ, and Fig. S15c shows δE_YSR shifting with tip approach. The low-energy spectra used for Fig. 3d were taken at 5 mV / 500 pA = 10 MΩ, inside this window. The measured δE_YSR values, and hence J_K, therefore carry a setpoint-dependent tip perturbation. This could add to the scatter that is attributed to herringbone sites. The authors should show δE_YSR versus junction resistance for a few odd chains. They should then either extrapolate to the far-tunnelling limit or confirm that all chains were measured at an identical setpoint, and include the tip effect in the error budget for J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV.
Raised by: r2

9. **Fractional and topological character is inferred rather than tested.** That odd chains have an S = 1 ground state, and that the YSR states come from fractional spin-½ edge states, rests on two inputs: the model, and the resemblance of the YSR map (Fig. 2f) to "radical states at zigzag edges" (p. 5). A single unpaired radical on a terminal unit, or another local moment on the terminal triangulene, would look similar, so the phrase "thus establishing the topological origin of the observed YSR states" is too strong. Likewise, the abstract poses as an open question whether "fractionalization survives the coupling to the superconductor". With J_K ≈ 0.6 meV far below J_0 ≈ 18 meV and the Haldane gap, the edge states are expected to be essentially unchanged by the substrate, so this is not a demanding test. Possible direct tests are:
   - map the decay of the edge-state/YSR weight over the first two or three units and compare it with the BLBQ correlation length (Fig. S17b gives the theoretical distribution);
   - measure YSR spectra of an isolated triangulene monomer (S = 1) and a dimer (N = 2) on the same substrate with the same tip;
   - if feasible, confirm the spin multiplicity from the magnetic-field dependence of the ST/TS excitations in the normal state.

   Otherwise the authors should tone down the "open question/survives" and "establishing the topological origin" wording.
Raised by: r2, r3

10. **Decoupling of the bulk modes from the superconductor is asserted, not tested.** The claim that the collective spin modes are "decoupled from the superconductor by the much larger Haldane gap" (abstract, p. 9) rests on agreement with a free-chain BLBQ model. That model uses J0 ≈ 18 meV and β ≈ 0.09 from Ref. 13, measured on bulk Au(111). The paper does not say whether these parameters were refitted for Au/Nb, or which measurable sign of coupling to the superconductor (shift, broadening, sub-gap replica) is being ruled out. The authors should state whether J0 and β were fixed or fitted. They should compare E_ST(N) and the CS energies and linewidths quantitatively with the same chains on bulk Au(111) (Ref. 13), for example in a table. That is the most direct test that the substrate leaves the chain's spin Hamiltonian unchanged. They should also replace "perfectly reproduced" (p. 9) with a quantitative measure of agreement for Fig. 4c, such as energy deviations or overlaps.
Raised by: r2, r3

11. **Uncertainties of |J| near the resolution limit, and the N = 3 point.** For N ≥ 11 the J values in Fig. 3e come from TS/ST features of about 0.5–0.8 meV. These are weak shoulders that overlap the YSR and dGSJ peaks (Figs. S11–S13), for example E_TS ≈ 0.5 mV for N = 13, labelled "faint". No uncertainties are given for the experimental |J|. In odd chains the YSR channel may itself shift or broaden the TS step. The authors should add error bars to Fig. 3e, mark which points come from ST and which from TS, and explain how the inelastic thresholds were located when YSR resonances are present. Measuring the same ends with a normal-metal tip, or at fields that suppress the tip gap but not the sample gap, would remove the SIS convolution for these weak features. Fig. 3e also shows an experimental point at N = 3 (about 12 meV), but Fig. S3 shows no TS feature in the low-bias window. Please say which spectrum it comes from.

12. **Platform integrity and gap calibration after synthesis.** The claim that the reconstruction and superconducting gap are "unaltered by the annealing procedure" (p. 5) is supported only by a reference to a PhD thesis (Ref. 36). The films are 25–30 ML of Au grown with an Ag surfactant and annealed to 340 °C. Nb/Ag/Au intermixing could therefore change Δ_sample, the dGSJ energies and the local J_K. Section S2 also uses Δ_sample = 1.2 meV as "precalibrated" without saying how. The authors should show substrate and Shockley-state spectra taken on the same sample before and after the synthesis anneal. They should describe the Δ_sample calibration and give the film thickness and dGSJ energy for the films used. They should also state the variation of Δ_dGSJ across the surface, since Fig. 3b mentions "tip-dependent gap variations" that were aligned out.

13. **Even-N "incipient YSR" at N = 12–14 is weakly supported and inconsistently stated.** The main text (p. 7) says YSR signal "can be already detected for N = 12 (Fig. S12) and N = 14 (Fig. S14)". The Fig. 3d caption says incipient YSR signal "appears only for N ≥ 14", and the Fig. 3c caption refers to the "absence of YSR states in shorter chains". The N = 12 evidence is only a particle–hole asymmetry of the dGSJ peaks, which Fig. S12 attributes to incipient YSR states but which potential scattering could also produce (concern 6). N = 14 relies on a decomposition (Fig. S14 inset) whose fit procedure is not described. The authors should reconcile these statements. They should describe the two-Lorentzian fit used to separate YSR from dGSJ (end of Section S2), including uncertainties. They should also discuss the N = 12–14 crossover (|J| ~ J_K) more explicitly, since the model's most distinctive prediction is there: the even-chain binding energy rises continuously with N.
