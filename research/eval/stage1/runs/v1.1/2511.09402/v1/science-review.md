A. Must address

1. Instrumental and data-processing artifacts are not excluded for a distortion of about 2% in q. The central claim rests on differences of 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg| between q1, q2 and q3 along the three A–H_m directions (p. 6; Fig. 2b–d; Supplementary Table 1), and on a small "tilt" of the q3 segment. For a field of view of order 100 nm, one FT pixel is roughly a0/L ≈ 0.5% of Q_Bragg, so the effect spans only a few pixels. Residual piezo nonlinearity, creep, hysteresis, thermal drift, non-orthogonal scan axes, anisotropic piezo calibration and asymmetric tips all shift q-vectors by amounts of this size, and differently along different crystal directions. The Methods (p. 18) say an affine transformation is applied to the Bragg points "in Figs. 2b and 4d" only, "to restore a perfectly symmetric lattice". It is not stated whether the line profiles (Figs. 2a, 2c,d, 4e), the dispersions (Fig. 3) and the velocity and mass values were extracted from corrected or uncorrected data. An affine correction removes only linear distortion, not nonlinear drift within a frame, which can produce exactly this kind of direction-dependent error. No FT pixel size, no uncertainty on the Bragg-peak positions and no error bars on the q-differences, d12/d13/d23 or the 1.4–2.2% figure are given, and the error bars in Fig. 3c,d overlap between directions over much of the energy range, particularly for q1. Intensity differences between directions, which the text also cites as evidence of chirality (p. 5, "in terms of both momentum locations … and the spectral intensity"), are routinely produced by tip anisotropy and setpoint effects. The opposite-handedness region "several hundred nanometers away" (Fig. 2g) is the right kind of control but is not decisive: it was taken at a different setpoint (1000 pA and 0.3 mV against 500 pA and 0.5 mV), apparently in a separate session, and the tip, scan angle and drift state are not stated; setpoint effects can themselves alter apparent QPI shapes. The authors should: (a) state the q-space pixel size and field of view for each map and give a full error budget for each |q_i^m| (fit uncertainty, pixel size, spread over independent maps), so that the 1.4–2.2% differences can be compared with the resolution; (b) show the raw, uncorrected Bragg-peak positions, widths and amplitudes for every map, the size of the affine correction, and which correction was applied to which figure; (c) re-map the same area with the fast-scan direction rotated (for example 0°, 60° and 90°), and compare forward and backward scans and at least two independent tips, showing that the handedness stays fixed to the lattice, not to the scan frame; (d) show the handedness reversal of Fig. 2g with the same tip, setpoint and scan angle, ideally as a single field of view, or a continuous series with the same tip, that crosses a boundary between opposite-handedness domains; (e) drop intensity anisotropy as evidence, or support it with tip-change controls.

2. Strain, or a nematic director at a generic angle, is not ruled out, and in the model it is the same thing as the "chiral-nematic" distortion. The model k_F(θ) = k_F^0(θ) + η cos(2θ + φ) with φ = π/2 (p. 12) is an ordinary l = 2 (E2g-type) nematic distortion whose director does not lie along a crystallographic mirror. In 2D, any nematic director off a mirror line breaks all vertical mirrors, and the authors themselves define "chirality" here (p. 11) as only that. Landau theory for a two-component (three-state-Potts) nematic on a hexagonal lattice pins the director to the mirror directions through the sixth-order cos 6φ term, so a director at a generic angle needs either an additional order parameter or an external symmetry-breaking field. In-plane uniaxial or shear strain along a low-symmetry direction is the obvious candidate. Such strain could come from sample mounting, cleaving at 13 K, differential thermal contraction or local Ti clustering, and a strain texture would naturally vary over hundreds of nanometres, producing opposite "handedness" in different regions (Fig. 2g). Because the Fermi surface lies near van Hove singularities, a strain of order 0.1% could plausibly produce a Fermi-surface distortion of order 1%. The affine correction that forces the Bragg lattice to be symmetric would also remove from the images exactly the lattice signature of a real strain while leaving its electronic consequence in the QPI. Without addressing this, "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid" as an intrinsic correlated state is not distinguished from a strained or locally anisotropic Fermi surface; the novelty over a nematic Fermi liquid rests on showing that φ is set by the crystal and the electronic order, not by an extrinsic field. The authors should: quantify the native lattice distortion from the uncorrected Bragg peaks or real-space lattice fits in every field of view and for each handedness, and compare its size and axis with the fitted director φ; estimate from their DFT the Fermi-surface distortion per unit shear strain and compare it with η; map the spatial extent of the chiral domains and correlate handedness and η with local strain and with the Ti distribution (Ti sites are often visible in topography or dI/dV); and explain theoretically why the director is not pinned to a mirror if the order is intrinsic, for example how the proposed intra-unit-cell sublattice charge order (Extended Fig. 6b) yields a director at a generic angle.

3. The "parent state above T_CDW" claim (T* ≈ 174 K) is inferred, not measured. The title, abstract and phase diagram (Fig. 4f) present the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid as the parent of the CDW and superconducting phases, setting in at T* above T_CDW ≈ 90 K. All data are taken at 0.4 K, plus 4 K for one comparison (Figs. S2, S3). T* ≈ 174 K is obtained by converting an average energy splitting of about 15 meV directly into k_B T* (p. 10), with no uncertainty. No mean-field ratio, order-parameter model or measurement justifies equating a band-splitting energy with an ordering temperature; by that logic many CDW gaps would imply ordering temperatures far above the measured ones. The x = 0.15 sample has no CDW at any temperature, so a symmetry-broken state there is a low-temperature state of a different composition, not evidence of a phase above T_CDW in x = 0. The x = 0 and x = 0.05 data are taken deep inside the CDW phase, where the CDW (and at x = 0.05 the 4a0 stripe) is already known to break rotation and mirror symmetry (refs. 19, 20, 24), so residual chiral QPI there may be a consequence of the CDW rather than of a precursor, and the argument cannot separate parent from descendant. The torque result (ref. 35) is suggestive but is not this paper's evidence, and it reports about 130 K in undoped CsV3Sb5, not 174 K. The authors should either (a) measure QPI at elevated temperatures, for x = 0.15 at several temperatures up to and above the proposed T* (for example 10, 20, 30, 60, 100 K and beyond), or for x = 0 or 0.05 between T_CDW and T* (for example 95–130 K at x = 0), and show that the distortion persists and then vanishes; or (b) remove the T* estimate, "parent", "emerges at T*" and "already present above the CDW transition" from the title, abstract, text and Fig. 4f, and present the result as a low-temperature chiral-nematic Fermi surface in Ti-doped CsV3Sb5 that is compatible with ref. 35, clearly separating what is measured from what is proposed.

4. A surface origin of the symmetry breaking is not addressed. STM probes the Sb-terminated surface after Cs adatoms have been swept away with the tip (Methods), and the authors' own earlier work (ref. 21) reports symmetry-breaking surfaces in this material family. Surface termination, relaxation or reconstruction, residual Cs or Cs-vacancy order, tip-swept areas and surface band bending can all lower the local symmetry, so a 1–2% anisotropy at the surface does not by itself establish a bulk Fermi-liquid property. The comparison with bulk DFT at k_z = π also does not discuss k_z projection or surface weighting. The authors should show that the effect does not depend on surface preparation (as-cleaved Cs-covered areas against tip-cleaned areas, different Cs-clearing histories, different distances from Cs remnants, other terminations if accessible); discuss slab calculations or surface-relaxation effects; and cite or provide independent bulk evidence for rotation and mirror breaking at x = 0.15 (for example polarized optics or Kerr rotation, elastoresistance, transport anisotropy or torque on the same crystals). If none can be supplied, the claims should be narrowed to the surface electronic structure.

5. The model comparison is qualitative and its parameters are chosen by hand. The simulated QPI is said to "well describe" the data (Fig. 2e,f), but the comparison is by eye: η is never given, φ = π/2 is fixed ("used for our QPI data analysis") rather than fitted, and no residuals or peak-position comparison is shown. The DFT k_F^0(θ) is taken at k_z = π only, although STM QPI integrates over k_z, and for the δ pockets centred at H it is not stated whether θ is measured from the zone centre or the pocket centre, which changes what "d-wave" means for those pockets. In addition, φ = π/2 turns cos(2θ + φ) into −sin 2θ (p. 12), while p. 6 writes +η sin(2θ). The authors should fit η and φ to the measured q_i^m(θ, E) in every field of view and at every doping, report them with uncertainties, give a goodness-of-fit compared with the symmetric (η = 0) and pure-nematic (φ = 0) cases, specify the Fermi-surface parameterization, the θ origin and the k_z choice, and make the sign convention consistent. A φ that is the same across cleaves and uncorrelated with strain would strongly support an intrinsic origin (see concern 2).

6. The velocities, mass renormalizations and the handedness orderings derived from them lack uncertainties. Any three distinct numbers define a cyclic order, so statements such as v_1,s^III < v_1,s^I < v_1,s^II and v_2,s^I > v_2,s^II > v_2,s^III show handedness only if the differences exceed the uncertainties. The Fermi velocities are quoted to six significant figures (4928.84, 5115.95, 4350.17 meV/|Q_Bragg|, p. 8) and the mass ratios ((1.23, 1.19, 1.40), (1.63, 1.97, 2.35), (1.25, 1.62, 1.89)) to two decimals, with no error bars (Supplementary Table 2 is not in the reviewed version). In Fig. 3c,d the A–H_I and A–H_II points overlap within their error bars over much of the energy range. The linear-fit energy window is not stated, and the text says the dispersion is bent by an electron-phonon kink (refs. 30, 31), which is asserted without analysis. For the δ band, v_F ≈ 2v_s is assumed "to leading order" although the scattering states are not related by inversion in the distorted state, yet the extracted m*/m^DFT values up to 2.35 are used to argue for "an exotic state". The statement that "most bands" fall within the DFT+DMFT range 1.29–1.71 also needs care, since all three α-band values (1.19–1.40) and several δ values lie outside or at the edge of that range. The authors should give fit windows and standard errors for every v_s, test the significance of the three-direction differences (for example by bootstrap over line-cut averaging or over independent maps), show robustness to the energy window, test the 2v_s approximation within their own model Fermi surface, and state conclusions only at the significance the data support.

7. The "chiral-nematic superconducting state" claim goes beyond the data. The abstract claims "the direct transition from the parent state to a chiral-nematic superconducting state", and the Discussion (p. 11) calls the similarity of Bogoliubov QPI to normal-state QPI (Extended Fig. 5) "strong evidence for a chiral-nematic SC ground state". That similarity is what one expects when any gap opens on an already-distorted Fermi surface; it shows the normal-state band distortion persists below Tc, not that the pairing order parameter breaks the mirrors. The reported U-shaped, full gap (Fig. S4) is if anything consistent with a nodeless, near-isotropic gap, and leaves few subgap states, so it should be explained what "subgap" B-QPI images and at which energy relative to the gap. The 4 K "above Tc" comparison is only about 0.5 K above Tc = 3.5 K, and no data are shown in the main figures. The authors should either show a direction-dependent gap magnitude that breaks the mirrors independently of the normal state (for example from B-QPI at several subgap energies, gap maps, the angular dependence of the coherence peak, or anisotropic vortex cores), or rephrase this as "superconductivity develops on the chiral-nematic Fermi surface" and adjust the abstract accordingly.

9. Handedness of the CDW inferred from peak intensities is tip-sensitive and confounded by the stripe order. The 2.6:1.2:1.0 ratio of the three 2a0 × 2a0 peaks (p. 10, Fig. 4b,c) is used to call the CDW handed. Relative FT peak intensities depend on tip shape, setpoint, bias and drift, and an intensity ratio alone shows only that rotation symmetry is broken; it does not distinguish a chiral CDW from a nematic one with domains. The unidirectional 4a0 stripe order also makes the three directions inequivalent. The authors should show the energy dependence of the ratio, at both bias polarities and several setpoints, confirm the sense of the ordering in a second field of view or after a tip change, rule out the stripe as the source of the ordering, and state whether the CDW handedness matches that of the residual QPI in the same map, which is the key link the paper wants to draw.

10. At x = 0.05 and x = 0 the residual-Fermi-surface distortion is not separated from anisotropy imposed by the CDW. At x = 0.05 (Fig. 4) the q1, q2 and q3 features coexist with CDW-reconstructed features, and both the 2a0 × 2a0 CDW and the unidirectional 4a0 stripe break rotation symmetry and reconstruct the Fermi surface; the stripe in particular is a C2-breaking field that could order the residual pockets. At x = 0, q3 is described as "faint but still detectable". The authors should quantify the q-length differences at x = 0.05 and x = 0 with the same resolution and uncertainty analysis asked for in concern 1, separate the residual-FS distortion from the anisotropy imposed by the 2×2 and 4a0 orders, and explain why it is not simply set by the stripe direction. Otherwise the statement that the distortion is "independent of the CDW" is not established at these dopings.

12. The DFT reference used for the "symmetric" Fermi surface and the mass renormalizations needs justifying and fuller description. The reference is a VCA, PBE, no-SOC calculation with a 300 eV cutoff, at k_z = π only (Methods; Figs. 1c, 3b). The k-mesh, structural input (relaxed or experimental, and at what x), the treatment of Ti 3d states in VCA and the Fermi-level alignment used to match the data are not given. STM QPI integrates over k_z with surface-weighted matrix elements, and the δ pockets and van Hove region are sensitive to SOC, k_z and the treatment of Ti disorder; Ti doping also shifts the chemical potential and pocket sizes, which would change the inferred "distortion" in q2 and q3. Because the claimed effect is a small deviation from C6, and the m*/m_DFT values (especially the δ-band 1.63–2.35 said to exceed the DFT+DMFT range and "reinforce" an exotic state) depend on this reference, the authors should state these parameters, show the k_z dispersion of the relevant pockets, test the effect of SOC and of the Fermi-level alignment, and explain why k_z = π is the right comparison. The renormalization argument is also not specific to mirror breaking: an enhanced mass does not by itself indicate broken symmetry, and that sentence should be softened.
