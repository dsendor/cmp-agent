Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors use spectroscopic-imaging STM on Ti-doped CsV3-xTixSb5 (mainly x = 0.15, where long-range CDW is absent) to report quasiparticle interference (QPI) patterns whose wavevectors differ along the three symmetry-equivalent A–H directions in a way that breaks all vertical mirrors. They model this as an l = 2 (d-wave) Pomeranchuk distortion with a phase twist, k_F(θ) = k_F0(θ) + η cos(2θ + φ), extract direction-dependent Fermi velocities and mass renormalizations, report similar features at x = 0.05, 0.27 and x = 0, and propose that a "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid" is the parent state of the chiral CDW and superconductivity in AV3Sb5, with onset T* ≈ 174 K. If it holds, a point-group-breaking normal state that exists without translation-symmetry breaking would matter a great deal for the kagome field. As presented, though, the evidence is a percent-level anisotropy in QPI wavevectors at 0.4–4 K. It does not yet separate an intrinsic electronic phase from strain, surface or analysis artifacts, and it does not show a state above T_CDW.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The question matters: whether the normal state above the CDW already breaks point-group symmetry is an open and widely debated issue in AV3Sb5. Torque (Ref. 35) and other probes hint at symmetry breaking above T_CDW.
- Ti doping is a clean route to suppress the CDW and image the translation-invariant electronic structure at low temperature. The orbital assignment of q1 (Sb pz α pocket) and q2/q3 (in-plane V d δ pockets) builds on the authors' earlier work (Ref. 22).
- The analysis is multi-pronged: side-by-side rotated FT panels, line cuts, energy-resolved dispersions along each direction, a simple phenomenological model that reproduces the qualitative pattern, and a region with opposite handedness (Fig. 2g).
- The same qualitative features recur across several dopings (x = 0, 0.05, 0.15, 0.27), which argues against a single bad dataset.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The size of the chiral distortion has no uncertainty, and instrumental and processing artifacts are not excluded at the required level.** The headline effect is a difference of 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg| between the q_i along the three A–H_m directions (p. 6, Supplementary Table 1). For a field of view of order 100 nm, one FT pixel is roughly a0/L ≈ 0.5% of Q_Bragg, so the effect spans only a few pixels. Residual piezo nonlinearity, creep, thermal drift, non-orthogonal scan axes or anisotropic piezo calibration all shift q-vectors by amounts of this size, and they do so differently along different crystal directions. The Methods say an affine transformation is applied "in Figs. 2b and 4d" only, to "restore a perfectly symmetric lattice". So:
   - (i) It is unclear whether the line profiles (Figs. 2c, 2d, 4e), the dispersions (Fig. 3) and the velocity and mass values were extracted from corrected or uncorrected data.
   - (ii) An affine correction removes linear distortion but not nonlinear drift within a map, which can produce exactly this kind of direction-dependent misalignment.
   - (iii) No error bars are given for the q-differences, d12/d13/d23 or the 1.4–2.2% figure.
   - (iv) Intensity differences between directions, which the text also cites as evidence of chirality (p. 5, "in terms of both momentum locations … and the spectral intensity"), are routinely produced by tip anisotropy and setpoint effects.

   The opposite-handedness region (Fig. 2g) helps, but it was taken at a different setpoint (1 nA, 0.3 mV vs 500 pA, 0.5 mV), and the scan angle and tip state are not stated. The authors should:
   - give a full error budget for each q_i^m: fit uncertainty, FT pixel size, and the spread over independent maps;
   - show that the handedness is fixed to the sample and not to the scan frame, by re-mapping the same area with the fast-scan direction rotated by 60° or 90°, or by reversing the scan direction;
   - show Bragg-peak positions and widths before and after correction for every map used;
   - show the handedness reversal of Fig. 2g with the same tip, setpoint and scan angle;
   - drop intensity anisotropy as evidence, or support it with tip-change controls.

2. **Strain or a nematic director at a generic angle has not been ruled out, and in the model it is the same thing as the "chiral-nematic" distortion.** The fitted form η cos(2θ + φ) with φ = π/2 (p. 12) is an ordinary l = 2 nematic distortion whose director does not lie along a crystallographic mirror. Any uniaxial strain applied along a low-symmetry in-plane direction produces a deformation of this kind. Such strain could come from sample mounting, cleaving at 13 K, differential thermal contraction, or local Ti inhomogeneity, and it also breaks all vertical mirrors. Domains of opposite "handedness" several hundred nm apart (Fig. 2g) are equally what one expects from a strain texture. The affine correction that forces the Bragg lattice to be symmetric would also hide any lattice strain that is present. The authors need to:
   - report the uncorrected lattice distortion, from Bragg peaks or real-space lattice fits, in every field of view, and compare its orientation with the fitted director φ;
   - show the handedness and magnitude in more than one cleave and sample at each x, with domain statistics;
   - state whether φ and η were fitted (with uncertainties) or chosen. The text says "φ = π/2 used for our QPI data analysis", and η is never quoted.

   Without these, "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid" cannot be preferred over "strained or locally anisotropic Fermi surface". Note too that "chirality" here, as the authors say on p. 11, means only the absence of all vertical mirrors in 2D, a property that any off-axis nematic director has. The novelty over a nematic Fermi liquid therefore rests on showing that φ is fixed by the crystal and the electronic order, not by an external or extrinsic field.

3. **The "parent state" claim, a phase that forms at T* above T_CDW, is not measured.** All data were taken at 0.4 K, plus 4 K for one comparison (Figs. S2, S3). The x = 0.15 sample has no CDW at any temperature, so a symmetry-broken state there is a low-temperature state of a different composition, not evidence of a phase above T_CDW in x = 0. The x = 0 and x = 0.05 data are taken deep inside the chiral CDW phase, where the CDW itself is already known to be chiral (Refs. 19, 20, 24). Residual chiral QPI there can therefore be a consequence of the CDW rather than of a precursor state, so the argument is circular. The T* ≈ 174 K estimate (p. 10) simply converts a ~15 meV "energy splitting" into k_B T*. No mean-field or other relation justifies equating a band-splitting energy with an ordering temperature, and its uncertainty is not given. The authors should either:
   - measure QPI at temperatures above T_CDW (for example 95–130 K in x = 0, or the x = 0.15 pattern as a function of T up to tens of K to show it weakens toward some T*); or
   - remove "parent state", T*, and the inference "already present above the CDW transition" from the title, abstract and phase diagram (Fig. 4f), and present the result as a low-temperature chiral-nematic Fermi surface in Ti-doped CsV3Sb5 that is compatible with Ref. 35.

4. **A surface origin is not addressed.** STM probes the Sb-terminated surface after Cs adatoms have been swept away by the tip (Methods). The authors' own earlier work (Ref. 21) reports symmetry-breaking surfaces in this material. Surface relaxation, residual Cs or Cs-vacancy order, tip-swept areas and surface band bending can all lower the local symmetry, so a 1–2% anisotropy at the surface does not by itself establish a bulk Fermi-liquid property. The paper also compares with bulk DFT at k_z = π without discussing k_z projection. The authors should:
   - show that the effect does not depend on the surface preparation (as-cleaved areas vs tip-cleaned areas, and different distances from Cs remnants);
   - discuss slab calculations or surface-relaxation effects;
   - cite independent bulk evidence for C6 → mirror-less symmetry breaking in Ti-doped samples, if any exists (for example elastoresistance, torque or optical rotation on x = 0.15).

   If none can be supplied, narrow the claims to the surface electronic structure.

**B. Should address**

5. **The B-QPI result does not test the superconducting order parameter.** On p. 11, the similarity of Bogoliubov QPI to normal-state QPI is taken as "strong evidence for a chiral-nematic SC ground state". That similarity is what one expects when any gap opens on an already-distorted Fermi surface, so it says nothing about the symmetry of the gap itself. To claim a chiral-nematic superconductor, the authors would need direction-resolved gap magnitudes, for example from B-QPI octet-like analysis, gap maps or the angular dependence of the coherence peak, that break the mirrors independently of the normal state. Otherwise, rephrase this as "superconductivity develops on the chiral-nematic Fermi surface". The same narrowing applies to the abstract's "direct transition … to a chiral-nematic superconducting state".

6. **The velocities and mass renormalizations lack uncertainties, and the δ-band analysis rests on an approximation.** Velocities are quoted to six significant figures (p. 8, e.g. 4928.84 meV/|Q_Bragg|), and the mass ratios (1.23, 1.19, 1.40), (1.63, 1.97, 2.35) and (1.25, 1.62, 1.89) carry no error bars. In Figs. 3c,d the error bars on individual points are comparable to the separation between directions, and the fit energy window is not stated. For the δ pocket, v_F ≈ 2v_s is assumed to hold "to leading order" although the scattering states are not related by inversion. The authors should:
   - give the fit windows and standard errors;
   - test how sensitive the results are to the window, given the phonon kink;
   - test the 2v_s approximation within their own model Fermi surface.

   The statement that "most bands" fall within the DFT+DMFT range of 1.29–1.71 (p. 8) also needs care: all three α-band values (1.19–1.40) and several δ values lie outside or at the edge of that range.

7. **Sample and field-of-view statistics are missing.** The paper does not state how many samples, cleaves and fields of view were measured at each x, how many showed chiral QPI, or how the handedness is distributed. Fig. 2g shows one reversed domain. Domain size and the fraction of each handedness bear directly on both the strain alternative and the claim of a spontaneously broken symmetry. A table of all maps (x, FOV size, setpoint, handedness, distortion magnitude) would resolve this.

8. **The CDW handedness from peak intensities is tip-sensitive.** The 2.6:1.2:1.0 intensity ratio of the 2a0 × 2a0 peaks (p. 10, Fig. 4c) is used to call the CDW handed. Relative FT peak intensities depend on tip shape, setpoint and drift. A ratio alone shows that rotation symmetry is broken; handedness requires the sense of the ordering, ideally confirmed in a second FOV or with a tip change. Also state whether the handedness of the CDW matches that of the residual QPI in the same map, which is the key link the paper wants to draw.

9. **The DFT reference has unstated parameters.** The calculations omit spin-orbit coupling, use a 300 eV cutoff and use the virtual crystal approximation for Ti. The structural input (relaxed or experimental, and at what x) and the k-mesh are not given. Because the claimed effect is a small deviation from C6, the reference k_F0(θ) should be shown to be robust. Ti doping in particular shifts the chemical potential and pocket sizes, and a different pocket size would change the inferred "distortion" in q2 and q3. The authors should state the Fermi-level alignment used to match the data and show how sensitive the analysis is to it.

10. **The handedness ordering differs between dopings without comment.** At x = 0.15, q1 and q2 overlap along A–H_I and separate most along A–H_III (p. 5). At x = 0.05, they separate most along A–H_II (p. 9). The velocity orderings also differ between the α and δ bands. The authors should explain how the handedness is defined consistently across samples and bands, and whether these differences are consistent with a single order parameter φ.

**C. Minor**

11. Extended Figs. 1–6, Figs. S1–S4 and Supplementary Tables 1–2 are cited throughout but are not in the submission. Several claims (x = 0.27, x = 0, B-QPI, 4 K data, the mass tables) cannot be checked without them.
12. On p. 7, "the mirror symmetries (Fig. 2c) require q_i(E) to be the same" appears to point to the wrong panel; Fig. 1a or 2e is probably meant.
13. The stripe order is written "4a0 × 1a0" in the text (p. 4, p. 9) but "1a0 × 4a0" in the Fig. 4b caption.
14. Refs. 17 and 34 are the same paper (Yu et al., PRB 104, L041103).
15. Ref. 37 is cited for the crystal structure (p. 4), but it is a theory preprint on roton superconductivity. Ref. 43 (UTe2 PDW) is cited for the affine drift correction; the original Lawler–Fujita method should be cited.
16. On p. 12, φ = π/2 turns cos(2θ + φ) into −sin 2θ, while p. 6 writes +η sin(2θ). Please make the sign and the definition of θ consistent and give the value of η.
17. Fig. 3a caption: state how the "Gauss-like backgrounds" were fitted and whether the subtraction can shift the fitted peak positions. The text says Lorentzian fits; please reconcile the two.
18. The paper calls the observation "unprecedented" (p. 4, p. 10), says it "genetically engenders" chirality (p. 4), and calls it "the discovery" (abstract). This language goes beyond what the low-temperature STM data establish and should be toned down.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

- Make the scan-frame-independence test the centrepiece: re-map the same FOV at two or more scan angles, include a Bragg-peak and lattice-strain analysis for every map, and add an error budget showing that the 1.4–2.2% effect lies well above systematic uncertainty.
- Fit η and φ freely, with uncertainties, in every FOV and at every doping. Then show φ against the local lattice-strain axis and against domain location. A φ that is the same across cleaves and uncorrelated with strain would strongly support an intrinsic electronic origin.
- Add temperature-dependent QPI, even at a few temperatures (for example 4, 20, 40 and 80 K at x = 0.15, or above T_CDW at x = 0), to give the "parent state" claim direct support.
- Separate clearly what is measured (low-temperature, surface, mirror-breaking QPI) from what is proposed (a bulk parent phase at T*, sublattice charge order, a chiral-nematic superconductor), in the abstract and in Fig. 4f.

**5. Verdict**

Verdict: major revision
