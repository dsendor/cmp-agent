Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports spectroscopic-imaging STM on Ti-doped CsV3-xTixSb5. At x = 0.15, where long-range CDW order is absent, the quasiparticle-interference (QPI) patterns are said to break the in-plane rotation symmetry and all vertical mirror symmetries. The authors interpret this as a "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid", which they propose as the parent state of the chiral CDW and of chiral superconductivity in AV3Sb5. If the result holds up, it matters: it would move the origin of the much-debated rotation and mirror symmetry breaking in kagome metals from the CDW to a translation-invariant electronic instability above T_CDW. As presented, however, the central observation is a direction-dependent change of about 1–2% of |Q_Bragg| in QPI wavevectors, measured on a surface, at 0.4 K, after an affine lattice correction. The leap from that observation to a bulk parent phase below T* ≈ 174 K is not yet supported.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- Doping-tuned suppression of the CDW is a well-chosen route to the translation-invariant electronic structure, and the orbital assignment of q1 (Sb pz, α pocket) and q2/q3 (in-plane V d, δ pockets) builds on the authors' earlier work (ref. 22).
- The QPI data at x = 0.15 are of high quality. The analysis is systematic: side-by-side views after aligning the Bragg peaks (Fig. 2b), line cuts (Fig. 2c,d) and energy-resolved dispersions along three directions (Fig. 3).
- The authors report an opposite-handedness region in the same sample (Fig. 2g). This is the kind of internal control the claim needs.
- The same qualitative distortion is reported across several dopings (x = 0, 0.05, 0.15, 0.27) and in the superconducting state. That points to something robust, whatever its origin.
- A simple phenomenological model, a phase-shifted l = 2 distortion of the DFT Fermi surfaces, captures the main qualitative features of the patterns.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **Error budget for the 1.4–2.2% effect against instrumental distortion.** The whole symmetry-breaking claim rests on differences between q1, q2 and q3 along the three A–H_m directions. The paper puts the mean distortion at 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg| (p. 6). Methods (p. 18) say that in Figs. 2b and 4d "an affine transformation algorithm is applied to the Bragg points to restore a perfectly symmetric lattice". It is not stated whether the same correction was applied to Figs. 2a, 2c,d, 3 and 4e, from which the quantitative values are extracted.
   - An affine correction removes linear drift and piezo miscalibration. It does not remove nonlinear drift, creep or hysteresis within a frame. Those can easily produce direction-dependent errors of order 1% in q.
   - The paper gives no FT pixel size in units of |Q_Bragg|, no uncertainty on the Bragg-peak positions, and no comparison of the q-differences with the Lorentzian-fit uncertainties.
   - The error bars in Fig. 3c,d visibly overlap between directions over much of the energy range, particularly for q1.

   The authors should:
   - report the raw, uncorrected Bragg-peak geometry for every map;
   - state which corrections were applied to which figure;
   - give the q-resolution and fit uncertainties alongside the inter-direction differences (Supplementary Table 1 is cited but not included in the version reviewed);
   - show that the effect survives the standard STM controls: the same area imaged at several scan angles (for example 0°, 60° and 90° fast-scan direction), forward against backward scans, at least two independent tips, and a check that the handedness does not follow the scan frame.

   The opposite-chirality region (Fig. 2g) is helpful only if it was imaged with the same tip, scan angle and setpoint as Fig. 2a. The listed parameters differ (1000 pA and 0.3 mV against 500 pA and 0.5 mV), which suggests a separate session. A map across the boundary between the two handedness domains, in a single field of view, would be the most convincing control.

2. **"Chirality" here is equivalent to an l = 2 nematic with its director at a non-symmetric angle, and strain is not excluded.** The model in the Discussion is k_F(θ) = k_F^0(θ) + η cos(2θ + φ), with φ = π/2 used for the analysis. This is an ordinary d-wave (E2g-type) nematic distortion whose director lies at 45° from the crystalline axis, between the mirror lines at 30° and 60°. In two dimensions, any nematic director that is not along a mirror line breaks all vertical mirrors. Landau theory for a three-state-Potts nematic on a hexagonal lattice pins the director to the high-symmetry axes. A director at a generic angle therefore needs either an additional order parameter or an external symmetry-breaking field, and in-plane shear strain at a generic angle is the most obvious candidate.

   This matters doubly here. The affine correction "restores a perfectly symmetric lattice", so it would remove from the images exactly the lattice signature of a real strain while leaving its electronic consequence in the QPI. In these materials the Fermi surface lies near van Hove singularities, so a strain of order 0.1% could plausibly produce a Fermi-surface distortion of order 1%. Cleave-induced strain, or strain from Ti inhomogeneity, would also naturally vary over hundreds of nanometres, which is the scale of the reported "chiral domains".

   The authors should:
   - quantify the native lattice distortion from the uncorrected Bragg peaks, in each region and for each handedness;
   - estimate, from their DFT, the Fermi-surface distortion per unit of shear strain, and compare it with η;
   - discuss why the director is not pinned to a high-symmetry axis if the order is intrinsic;
   - ideally, show whether the handedness or the magnitude of η correlates with local strain across several fields of view.

   Without this, "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid" as an intrinsic correlated state is not distinguished from a strain-oriented Fermi-surface distortion.

3. **The "parent state above T_CDW" (T* ≈ 174 K) is inferred, not measured.** The title, abstract and phase diagram (Fig. 4f) present the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid as the parent of the CDW and superconducting phases, with T* > T_CDW. All the data, however, are taken at 0.4 K, plus one temperature of 4 K (Figs. S2, S3). T* ≈ 174 K is obtained simply by converting an average "energy splitting" of about 15 meV to a temperature with k_B (p. 10). No mean-field ratio, order-parameter model or measurement justifies equating a band-splitting energy with an ordering temperature; by that logic, many CDW gaps would imply ordering temperatures far above the measured ones.

   The argument that the effect is "independent of the CDW" at x = 0 and x = 0.05 has a further problem. In those samples the CDW and the 4a0 stripe order are present and already break rotation symmetry, so a residual anisotropy in q1 is expected there regardless of any parent state.

   The authors should either:
   - measure QPI at elevated temperatures (for example 20–100 K at x = 0.15, and above T_CDW at x = 0 or 0.05) and show that the distortion persists and vanishes near a T*; or
   - substantially soften the claims: "parent", "emerges at T*", and the T* ≈ 174 K value in Fig. 4f and the text.

   The torque result (ref. 35) is cited as support. It reports about 130 K in undoped CsV3Sb5, which is not the same as 174 K, and it concerns a different sample composition.

**B. Should address**

4. **Surface versus bulk.** The measurements are on the Sb-terminated surface after Cs adatoms were cleared with the tip (Methods). The authors' own earlier work (ref. 21) discusses symmetry-breaking surfaces in this family. A surface-specific rotation and mirror distortion, from termination, reconstruction or residual Cs, could produce the same QPI anisotropy without any bulk phase. The bulk claim needs at least:
   - a discussion of this possibility;
   - checks on areas prepared differently (different Cs-clearing histories, or other terminations if accessible);
   - ideally, reference to a bulk probe of rotation and mirror breaking at x = 0.15 (transport anisotropy, elastoresistance, optical birefringence or Kerr rotation).

5. **The model fit is qualitative, and its parameters are unclear.** Fig. 2e,f compares a simulated joint density of states with the data by eye. Several points need clarifying:
   - The fitted η and its uncertainty are not given.
   - The DFT k_F^0(θ) is taken at kz = π only, although STM QPI integrates over kz.
   - For the δ pockets centred at H, it is not clear whether θ is measured from the zone centre or from the pocket centre. This choice changes what "d-wave" means for those pockets.
   - φ = π/2 appears to be chosen rather than fitted.

   The authors should provide a quantitative comparison: fitted q_i^m against model q_i^m, η and φ with uncertainties, and a goodness-of-fit compared with the symmetric (η = 0) and pure-nematic (φ = 0) cases. They should also specify the Fermi-surface parameterization and the kz choice.

6. **The velocities and mass renormalizations need uncertainties.**
   - The scattering and Fermi velocities are quoted to six significant figures (for example v_1,F = 4928.84, 5115.95, 4350.17 meV/|Q_Bragg|), and the mass ratios to two decimals, with no uncertainties in the text. Supplementary Table 2 is not in the reviewed version.
   - The linear-fit energy window is not stated.
   - In Fig. 3c the three directions differ by amounts comparable to the error bars.
   - For the δ band, the relation v_F ≈ 2v_s is acknowledged not to hold exactly in the distorted state, yet the extracted m*/m^DFT values (up to 2.35) are used to argue for "an exotic state".
   - The attribution of the deviations from linearity to an electron-phonon kink (refs. 30, 31) is asserted without analysis.

   The authors should give fit windows and uncertainties, and state the conclusions only at the significance the data support.

7. **The chiral-nematic superconductivity claim goes beyond the data.** The abstract claims "the direct transition from the parent state to a chiral-nematic superconducting state". The Discussion calls the broken symmetries of the Bogoliubov QPI "strong evidence for a chiral-nematic SC ground state". Bogoliubov QPI that resembles the normal-state QPI shows only that the normal-state band distortion persists below Tc. It says nothing about whether the pairing order parameter itself breaks rotation or mirror symmetry. The reported U-shaped gap (Fig. S4) is if anything consistent with a nodeless, near-isotropic gap.

   Evidence for symmetry breaking in the pairing would require, for example, a direction-dependent gap magnitude from Bogoliubov QPI at several subgap energies, or anisotropic vortex cores. Without such evidence the superconducting-state claim should be rephrased as superconductivity emerging from a normal state with a distorted Fermi surface.

8. **Statistics and reproducibility are not reported.** The paper does not say:
   - how many crystals and cleaves were measured at each x, or how many fields of view and tips were used;
   - the fraction of regions that show each handedness, or any region with no detectable distortion;
   - the size of a typical domain.

   The x = 0.27 and x = 0 evidence (Extended Figs. 2–4) and the opposite-chirality region (Extended Fig. 1) are not contained in the version reviewed, so they cannot be assessed. Please report these numbers and include the Extended Data and Supplementary material.

9. **Mirror breaking inferred from the CDW peak intensities (p. 10, Fig. 4b,c).** The 2.6:1.2:1.0 ratio of the three 2a0 × 2a0 peaks shows rotational anisotropy. Using an intensity ordering to infer handedness requires two things:
   - the ordering must be robust against tip anisotropy, setpoint and bias, and should be shown at several energies, including both polarities;
   - the unidirectional 4a0 stripe order must be ruled out as the source of the ordering, since it also makes the three directions inequivalent.

   Please show the energy dependence of the ratio and discuss the role of the stripe order.

10. **Interpretation at x = 0.05 and x = 0.** The q1, q2 and q3 features coexist with CDW-reconstructed features (Fig. 4b). The residual-FS assignment and its distortion should be separated from the anisotropy imposed by the 2×2 and 4a0 orders, which break rotation symmetry on their own. Otherwise the statement that the distortion is "independent of the CDW" is not established at these dopings.

11. **DFT details.** The calculations use VCA, no spin-orbit coupling and a 300 eV cutoff. No k-mesh, no structural relaxation details, and no assessment of how VCA treats the Ti 3d states at x = 0.15 are given. The quantitative mass-renormalization comparisons depend on the DFT velocities, so these choices should be stated and their sensitivity checked, at least for SOC.

**C. Minor**

12. The Extended Data Figs. 1–6, Supplementary Figs. S1–S4 and Supplementary Tables 1–2 are cited throughout but are not in the reviewed document.
13. Fig. 4d orders its panels A–H_I, A–H_III, A–H_II, while the caption lists A–H_I, A–H_II, A–H_III. Fig. 2b uses I, II, III. Please make them consistent.
14. Fig. 4 caption: "1a0 × 4a0 stripes order" against "4a0 × 1a0" in the text.
15. p. 8: "v^I_2,s > v^II_2,s > v^III_3,s". The last term should presumably be v^III_2,s.
16. Refs. 17 and 34 are the same paper.
17. Ref. 43 (UTe2 pair density wave) is cited for the affine drift-correction algorithm. Please cite the original Lawler–Fujita method or describe the algorithm.
18. p. 5: "6-fold rotation (C3 plus inversion)". In the in-plane projection, C6 = C3 combined with C2. Please clarify which inversion is meant.
19. "Stochiometric" (p. 9) should be "stoichiometric". "Two sets of mirror plane ... its two mirror arises" (p. 12) needs grammatical correction.
20. The comparison of the 1.4–2.2% distortion with the "approximately 3% |Q_Bragg|" reported in RbV3Sb5 (ref. 29) should specify that both quantities are defined the same way.
21. Fig. 1b: the temperature axis has a break and non-uniform scale that is easy to misread. Please indicate T_CDW and Tc values explicitly.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Present one fully controlled dataset: the same field of view at two scan angles and with two tips, with uncorrected and corrected FTs, and a domain wall between opposite handedness imaged in a single map. This would turn the headline observation from suggestive into convincing.
2. Recast the model explicitly as an E2g nematic order parameter with a director angle. Discuss pinning by the hexagonal anisotropy and the role of strain, and report the fitted director angle and η with uncertainties.
3. Add at least a few temperature points at x = 0.15 (for example 10, 30, 60 and 100 K) to test the T* scenario directly.
4. Tone down "discovery", "unprecedented", "establish" and "direct transition to a chiral-nematic superconducting state" until the evidence above is in hand.

**5. Verdict**

Verdict: major revision
