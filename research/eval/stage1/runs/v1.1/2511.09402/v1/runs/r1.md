Review outline:

Note on scope: the file reviewed contains the main text, Methods and Figs. 1-4. The Extended Data figures (Extended Figs. 1-6), the Supplementary Figures (S1-S4) and Supplementary Tables 1-2 are cited but were not included. Where a concern depends on material that may be in those items, I say so.

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use spectroscopic-imaging STM on Ti-doped CsV3Sb5 (x = 0.15, where long-range CDW is absent) and report quasiparticle interference (QPI) wavevectors whose lengths and shapes differ between the three A-H directions and break all vertical mirrors. They interpret this as a "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid", model it as a d-wave Pomeranchuk distortion with a phase offset, and propose that it is the parent state from which the chiral CDW and superconductivity of AV3Sb5 descend. If the symmetry breaking is intrinsic and bulk, finding rotational and mirror symmetry breaking without the CDW would matter a great deal for the long-running debate on chirality in kagome metals. The experimental core is a small distortion, about 1.4-2.2% of |Q_Bragg| in QPI wavevectors. The paper currently treats that distortion as more firmly established, and more clearly linked to a high-temperature parent state, than the data show.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- It addresses an important open question: whether point-group symmetry breaking in AV3Sb5 needs the CDW or comes before it.
- The QPI data at x = 0.15 are of high quality. Several distinct scattering branches (q1 from the Sb pz alpha pocket, and q2 and q3 from the V d delta pockets) are resolved and tracked in energy (Fig. 3a, c, d).
- The data contain several internal consistency checks: opposite handedness in a second region (Fig. 2g), similar distortions at x = 0.05 (Fig. 4), x = 0.27 and x = 0 (Extended Figs. 2-4, as described), and in the Bogoliubov QPI below Tc (Extended Fig. 5).
- The phenomenological description, k_F(theta) = k_F^0(theta) + eta cos(2 theta + phi), is simple and gives a concrete, testable picture. The simulated QPI (Fig. 2f) reproduces the qualitative features of the data.
- The paper extracts direction-dependent Fermi velocities and mass renormalizations and compares them with DFT, DFT+DMFT and ARPES.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Instrumental and data-processing artifacts are not excluded for a distortion of about 2% in q. The central claim rests on differences of 1.4-2.2% of |Q_Bragg| in the lengths of q1, q2 and q3, and on a small "tilt" of the q3 segment (Fig. 2b-d; Supplementary Table 1). Distortions of this size and type are what an asymmetric tip, nonlinear piezo response, thermal drift or residual non-orthogonality of the scanner produce. The Methods say that an affine transformation was applied to the Bragg peaks in Figs. 2b and 4d "to restore a perfectly symmetric lattice". That corrects only linear distortions, and the paper does not say whether it was also applied to the data behind Figs. 2a, 2c-d, 3 and Supplementary Tables 1-2. The opposite chirality in a region "several hundred nanometers away" (Fig. 2g) is the right kind of control, but it is not decisive. That map used different setpoints (1000 pA and 0.3 mV against 500 pA and 0.5 mV), and the paper does not say whether the tip, scan angle or drift state were the same. The authors should:
   (a) state the q-space pixel size and field of view for each map, and the uncertainty on each |q_i^m| in Supplementary Table 1, so that a 1.4-2.2% difference can be compared with the resolution;
   (b) show the raw, uncorrected Bragg-peak positions and amplitudes for each map, and the size of the affine correction applied;
   (c) repeat a map of the same area with the scan direction rotated (for example by 60° or 90°) and show that the handedness stays fixed to the lattice, not to the scan frame;
   (d) ideally, show both handednesses within one field of view, or across a domain boundary imaged with the same tip, or with a tip change between maps of the same area. This would be the most convincing control against tip shape;
   (e) say how many tips, cleaves and samples gave the chiral pattern, and how often a symmetric pattern was found, if ever.

2. Extrinsic sample origins of mirror breaking (strain, surface, dopant inhomogeneity) are not addressed. A d-wave (l = 2) Fermi-surface distortion whose axis is rotated away from the crystal mirrors, which is what eta cos(2 theta + phi) with phi = pi/2 describes, is also what a uniaxial or shear strain along a low-symmetry direction produces in a hexagonal crystal. A strain from crystal mounting, thermal contraction or local Ti clustering would give exactly this "chiral-nematic" Fermi surface, and differently oriented strain domains would give opposite "handedness" in different regions (Fig. 2g). The surface is also a concern. The Sb surface was prepared by moving Cs adatoms away with the tip (Methods), and ref. 21 (by the same group) reports symmetry-breaking surfaces in this material family. The surface layer may therefore break symmetries that the bulk does not. The claim of a bulk "Fermi liquid" phase needs these possibilities to be addressed. The authors should:
   (a) quantify the local lattice strain in each region from the raw Bragg peaks (before the affine correction) and compare its size and axis with the electronic distortion axis. Point (b) of concern 1 also bears on this;
   (b) map the spatial extent of the chiral domains and correlate them with the Ti distribution (Ti sites are often visible in topography or in dI/dV);
   (c) discuss why surface termination and Cs removal by the tip cannot produce the effect, or measure on as-cleaved, Cs-covered areas;
   (d) if possible, give a bulk-sensitive check, for example polarized optics, elastoresistance or torque on the same x = 0.15 crystals. Alternatively, state plainly that the evidence is surface-sensitive and narrow the claims accordingly.

3. The "parent state above T_CDW" claim is not supported by temperature-dependent data. The title, abstract and phase diagram (Fig. 4f) present the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid as a parent state that sets in at T* above T_CDW, about 90 K. The only temperatures measured are 0.4 K and 4 K (Figs. S2-S3). T* ≈ 174 K is obtained by converting an average energy splitting of about 15 meV directly into a temperature (Discussion). No theory justifies that conversion: in a mean-field or Landau picture the ratio of order-parameter energy to T* is not 1, and an energy splitting is not a transition temperature. The x = 0.15 sample has no CDW at all, so measurements there cannot show that the state exists above T_CDW in the x = 0 compound. The x = 0 data (Extended Figs. 3-4) are below T_CDW, where the CDW itself is known to break rotation and mirror symmetry, so they cannot separate parent from descendant either. Torque data from another group (ref. 35) are suggestive but are not this paper's evidence. The authors should either:
   (a) measure QPI at elevated temperatures, for x = 0.15 up to and above the proposed T*, or for x = 0 or 0.05 between T_CDW and T*, and show that the distortion persists and then vanishes; or
   (b) remove the T* estimate and recast "parent state" and Fig. 4f as a hypothesis, with the title and abstract changed to match.

B. Should address

4. The handedness assignments from three velocities lack statistical significance. Any three distinct numbers define a cyclic order. Statements such as v_1,s^III < v_1,s^I < v_1,s^II and v_2,s^I > v_2,s^II > v_3,s^III therefore show handedness only if the differences exceed the uncertainties. The quoted Fermi velocities (4928.84, 5115.95 and 4350.17 meV/|Q_Bragg|) carry six significant figures and no error bars. In Fig. 3c the A-H_I and A-H_II points overlap within their horizontal error bars over much of the energy range. The fits also cover an energy window that the text says is bent by electron-phonon kinks. The authors should give fit uncertainties and fit windows for every v_s, test whether the three-direction differences are significant (for example by bootstrap over line-cut averaging or over independent maps), and show how robust the results are to the choice of energy window.

5. The model comparison is qualitative, and the chosen distortion is fixed by hand. The text says the simulated QPI "well describe[s]" the data (Fig. 2f), but:
   - the value of eta is never given;
   - phi is fixed at pi/2 instead of being fitted;
   - no quantitative comparison (residuals, peak positions) is shown.
   
   phi = pi/2 means the nematic director lies 15° from a crystal mirror. In a Landau theory of a two-component E2g nematic order parameter on a hexagonal lattice, the sixth-order cos 6 phi anisotropy pins the director to the mirror directions. An intermediate angle therefore needs either an additional symmetry-breaking field (such as strain, which feeds back into concern 2) or a coupling to another order that the paper should specify. The authors should fit eta and phi to the measured q_i^m(theta) and report the uncertainties. They should also explain theoretically why the director is not pinned to a mirror, or say how the proposed intra-unit-cell sublattice charge order (Extended Fig. 6b) produces a director at a generic angle.

6. The claim of a "chiral-nematic superconducting state" from Bogoliubov QPI is overstated. Extended Fig. 5 is said to show that the B-QPI "closely resemble[s]" the normal-state QPI, including the chiral features, and the paper calls this "strong evidence for a chiral-nematic SC ground state". Normal-state band-structure distortions inherited by the Bogoliubov quasiparticles do not show that the superconducting order parameter itself breaks the mirrors. A full gap with a U-shaped spectrum also leaves few subgap states, so it should be explained what "subgap" B-QPI images and at which energy relative to the gap. The 4 K "above Tc" comparison is only about 0.5 K above Tc = 3.5 K, and no data are shown in the main figures. The authors should either show gap anisotropy or a gap-structure signature tied to the mirror breaking, for example a direction-dependent gap magnitude obtained from B-QPI, or reword this as "the superconducting state inherits the distorted normal-state Fermi surface". The abstract's "direct transition from the parent state to a chiral-nematic superconducting state" should be adjusted to match.

7. The DFT reference used for the mass renormalizations needs justifying. The "symmetric" reference is a VCA, PBE, no-SOC calculation at k_z = pi only (Methods; Figs. 1c, 3b). STM QPI integrates over k_z with surface-weighted matrix elements, and the delta pockets and van Hove region are sensitive to SOC, k_z and the treatment of Ti disorder. The m*/m_DFT values, and especially the conclusion that the delta band's renormalization of 1.63-2.35 exceeds the DFT+DMFT range and so "reinforces" an exotic state, depend on this reference. The authors should show the k_z dispersion of the relevant pockets, test the effect of SOC, and explain why k_z = pi is the right comparison. The renormalization argument is also not specific to mirror breaking: an enhanced mass does not by itself indicate broken symmetry, and this sentence should be softened.

8. The CDW-state evidence at x = 0.05 and x = 0 is hard to separate from known CDW nematicity. At x = 0.05 (Fig. 4), a unidirectional 4a0 stripe and a 2a0 × 2a0 CDW are present. Both are known to break rotation symmetry and to reconstruct the Fermi surface, and the stripe is the C2-breaking field that orders the residual pockets. The 2.6:1.2:1.0 CDW peak-intensity ratio is quoted as evidence of handedness. FT peak intensities are, however, sensitive to the tip, the setpoint and the bias, and unequal intensities alone do not distinguish a chiral CDW from a nematic one with domains. For x = 0, q3 is described as "faint but still detectable". The authors should show the energy and setpoint dependence of the 2a0 peak ratios, and quantify the q-length differences at x = 0.05 and x = 0 with the same resolution and uncertainty analysis asked for in concern 1. They should also explain why the residual-FS distortion is not simply set by the stripe direction. The ordering at x = 0.05 (q1 and q2 coalesce along A-H_I and split most along A-H_II) differs from that at x = 0.15 (most split along A-H_III). This should be discussed with respect to the claimed common origin.

9. Sample statistics and reproducibility are not stated. The text gives one x = 0.15 sample with two regions, one x = 0.05 map, one x = 0.27 map and two x = 0 samples. The authors should give, for each doping, the number of crystals, cleaves, tips and fields of view examined, and the fraction that showed each handedness. They should also report the domain size if it can be estimated. This links to concern 1(e). Without these numbers it is hard to judge "robust and universal".

10. The "Fermi liquid" label is not tested. The paper establishes a distorted band dispersion and renormalized velocities. It does not test Fermi-liquid properties, for example the quasiparticle lifetime (QPI peak width) scaling as omega^2, or a Landau-parameter analysis. Either give such evidence, for instance from the energy dependence of the Lorentzian widths in Fig. S1, or describe the state more neutrally as a "chiral-nematic metal or electronic state".

C. Minor

11. Some figure cross-references are wrong. On p. 7, "the mirror symmetries (Fig. 2c) require q_i(E) to be the same" and, on p. 8, "affected by the chiral-nematic distortion (Fig. 2c)" both appear to mean Fig. 2e or Fig. 1a.

12. The stripe notation is inconsistent: the text says "4a0 × 1a0" and the Fig. 4 caption says "1a0 × 4a0".

13. The text says q_i was extracted by Lorentzian fits (Fig. S1). The Fig. 3a caption says "Gauss-like backgrounds" were subtracted. The full fitting procedure and the treatment of the background should be stated in the Methods.

14. Refs. 17 and 34 are the same paper (Yu, F. H. et al., PRB 104, L041103). Ref. 37, a theory preprint on roton superconductivity, is cited for the crystal structure statement that the V kagome lattice is coordinated by Sb1 "forming a simple hexagonal lattice". Please check this citation.

15. The Methods say the line profiles are averaged over "six neighboring lines". Please state the width of the averaging window in units of |Q_Bragg|, and confirm that the same window was used in all three directions.

16. Supplementary Table 1 is described as giving "1.4%-2.2% of |Q_Bragg|", and the Discussion as giving "an average energy splitting of ~15.0 meV". Please explain in the main text how the q differences were converted into an energy splitting, including which velocities were used.

17. The comparison with the "approximately 3%" distortions in RbV3Sb5 (ref. 29) should say which quantity is being compared, since ref. 29 concerns CDW-state, field- or optically-trained effects.

18. Fig. 1b has a broken temperature axis and no data points. Please state whether it is schematic or what data it is based on (ref. 28).

19. Only one sample at x = 0.15 was set at 1000 pA (Fig. 2g). Setpoint effects can alter apparent QPI shapes, so please show both handednesses at the same setpoint.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- The most persuasive single addition would be one field of view, or a continuous series of fields of view with the same tip, that crosses a boundary between opposite-handedness domains. Raw Bragg-peak analysis on both sides would show that the lattice is undistorted where the electronic handedness reverses.
- Temperature-dependent QPI at x = 0.15, from 0.4 K up to 100-200 K, would turn the "parent state" from an inference into a measurement and would test T* directly.
- Fit eta and phi quantitatively to q_i^m(theta, E), with uncertainties, and give a symmetry analysis (Landau theory or the sublattice charge-order model of Extended Fig. 6b) explaining why the director is not pinned to a mirror.
- Put the uncertainty budget (q-pixel size, drift correction, fit errors, tip-to-tip spread) in one table, so that readers can judge directly whether the 1-2% effects and the velocity orderings are significant.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
