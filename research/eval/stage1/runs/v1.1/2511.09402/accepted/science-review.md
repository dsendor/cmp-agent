A. Must address

1. Extrinsic causes of the mirror-breaking distortion are not excluded: strain, scanner distortion and its affine correction, and tip shape. The headline claim rests on direction-dependent QPI wavevectors that differ by only 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg| (p. 3; Supplementary Table 1), together with direction-dependent dispersions and Fermi velocities along the three A–H_m directions (Fig. 3c,d). The fitted form, k_F(θ) = k_F^0(θ) + η cos(2θ + φ) with φ = −π/6, is a two-fold (l = 2) distortion whose director lies between the σ_v and σ_v′ mirror planes. A uniform in-plane uniaxial or shear strain produces exactly this when its principal axis is not along a crystal mirror: it breaks all vertical mirrors and C3 while keeping C2, and the two mirror-related strain orientations give the two "handednesses". Strain of a few tenths of a percent is common from cleaving at 13 K, from differential thermal contraction against the sample plate, or from Ti-induced local stress. Because AV3Sb5 has a large electronic nematic susceptibility and the V d bands near the van Hove points respond strongly to strain, such strain could deform the Fermi surface at the reported level. The opposite-handedness region "several hundred nanometers away" (Fig. 2g) argues against a fixed scanner artefact, but it is equally consistent with strain domains. The paper does not say whether Fig. 2g was taken with the same tip and in the same scan frame as Fig. 2a. The Discussion (p. 8) and the T-matrix study (Supplementary Figs. 18–20) address point defects only, and strain is not discussed anywhere. The analysis makes this worse. The affine transformation applied to the Bragg points "to restore a perfectly symmetric lattice" (Methods, p. 9; used for Figs. 2b and 4d) removes the lattice signature of uniform strain and of residual piezo non-orthogonality, and it rescales the q-space metric in which the 1–2% differences are then measured. With fields of view of about 50–80 nm, one FT pixel is roughly 0.5–1% of |Q_Bragg|. The direction-to-direction differences in q1 near E ≈ 0 in Fig. 3c are about 0.01–0.02 |Q_Bragg|, so only one to a few pixels. At that size, nonlinear scanner distortion, creep and slow-scan drift matter. Fast-scan versus slow-scan anisotropy in particular has a fixed orientation in the scan frame and can mimic a twofold distortion at a generic angle. An asymmetric tip also convolves the LDOS with a non-symmetric kernel and can redistribute FT intensity anisotropically. The authors should do the following. (a) Report the uncorrected Bragg-peak positions and the affine matrix (size and orientation of the correction) for every dataset, and compare the correction with the 1.4–2.2% effect. (b) Give a rigid-band DFT estimate of the Fermi-surface distortion that the residual strain could produce. (c) Test whether the QPI director and handedness correlate with the residual strain axis or with the fast-scan direction across all datasets (x = 0, 0.04, 0.05, 0.15, 0.27, the Cs surface, the Fig. 2g region). (d) Re-image the same field of view with the scan frame rotated (for example by 60° or 90°) and after a deliberate tip change, showing that the handedness stays locked to the lattice. State which datasets were taken with independently prepared tips. (e) Report the measured handedness and director angle φ for each sample, to show whether φ is pinned to −π/6 (pointing to an intrinsic origin) or varies (pointing to an extrinsic one). (f) Ideally, image a boundary between opposite-handedness domains within one uncorrected field of view with the same tip, with the local Bragg geometry on both sides. A sharp reversal of φ with no change in lattice geometry would settle the issue. Without these controls, an extrinsic origin remains plausible and the claim that the electronic state itself breaks all mirrors is not established.

2. The "parent state" claim and T* ≈ 167 K are not supported by temperature-dependent data. The title and abstract present the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid as a parent state that emerges at T* > T_CDW and from which the chiral CDW and superconductivity "descend". All normal-state QPI, however, is taken at 0.4 K, with Bogoliubov comparisons near 3.5–4 K (Supplementary Fig. 4). T* is obtained by dividing an average energy splitting of −14.4 meV of the δ-pocket dispersions (Supplementary Table 1) by k_B (p. 8). The paper does not explain why this splitting should set an ordering temperature, and a mean-field relation would carry a numerical prefactor. The torque onset near 130 K cited in support (ref. 28) does not match 167 K, yet the two are presented as mutually supporting. Within the paper, the "parent" status rests on doping: the state persists where the CDW is absent (x = 0.15), and the residual QPI at x = 0.05 resembles that at x = 0.15. Showing that the state precedes the CDW in x = 0 would require showing that it exists above T_CDW (about 90 K), and no transition is tracked. Fig. 4f places T* above T_CDW at all dopings with no measured points. The authors should measure QPI at x = 0.15, and ideally at x = 0, at several elevated temperatures (for example 20, 50, 100, 150 K, and above T_CDW for x = 0). They should track the anisotropy measures (d12/d13/d23, the q-vector differences, the q3 tilt) and show that the anisotropy persists and then vanishes. Failing that, they should narrow the title, abstract and Fig. 4f to a low-temperature chiral-nematic Fermi surface in the CDW-free regime that is consistent with a parent-state scenario. They should then either remove T* or present it as an order-of-magnitude energy scale with a stated prefactor, comment on the difference from the 130 K torque onset, and label Fig. 4f as a conjectured schematic.

B. Should address

3. The statistical significance of the direction dependence is not established. The velocities v^m_{i,s} in the three directions (p. 6; Supplementary Table 2), for example 4583.84, 5107.30 and 4121.17 meV/|Q_Bragg| for the α pocket, and the mass ratios (1.33, 1.19, 1.47) are quoted to six significant figures with no uncertainties. In Fig. 3c,d the error bars of the three directions overlap at many energies, especially for q1, so the ordering v^III < v^I < v^II depends on the linear-fit window around the "kink". That window is stated only as "away from these kink features". The error bars combine Lorentzian-fit errors with pixel resolution (Fig. 3 caption). They do not include the uncertainty from the affine correction (concern 1) or from the choice of cut position. The line profiles are averages of six neighbouring lines (Methods), and it is not said whether this is done in the affine-corrected frame. The authors should state the energy window for each fit and give uncertainties on every velocity and mass ratio in the main text. They should show that the velocity ordering survives reasonable changes of window, and give a significance test, for example a χ² comparison of one common velocity against three separate velocities. They should also compare the pixel resolution ε_pix with the 1.4–2.2% effect.

4. The phenomenological model is compared only qualitatively, its parameters are not given, and it does not obviously explain the large velocity anisotropy. The values of η and φ used in Fig. 2e,f are not stated, and it is unclear whether they were fitted, with what uncertainty, or chosen by eye. There is no quantitative comparison of predicted and measured d12, d13, d23 or q-length differences. For the δ band, the renormalisation ratios 1.53, 1.95 and 2.33 (p. 6) imply velocities differing by up to about 50% between directions related by C3 in the parent lattice, whereas the Fermi-wavevector distortion is only about 2% of |Q_Bragg|. A single-parameter η cos(2θ + φ) distortion does not obviously produce this. Several of these velocities come from q2 and q3, which connect δ-pocket states that are not related by inversion. For these, v_F ≈ 2 v_s is invoked only "to leading order in the chiral-nematic distortion", which is the least controlled step. The resulting values exceed the 1.29–1.71 DFT+DMFT range that the text cites as agreement, and the direction dependence is then read as further evidence for the exotic state. The authors should fit η and φ globally to the extracted q_i(E) in all three directions, with confidence intervals. This would make "breaks all mirror symmetries" a quantitative statement (φ significantly different from 0 and ±π/2). They should state the Fermi-surface distortion the fitted η implies and compare it with the 1.4–2.2% figure. They should also compute q2,3(E) from the fitted distorted contours to propagate the 2v_s approximation into the quoted renormalisations, and say whether the velocity anisotropy follows from the same η and φ. Otherwise they should state that the model reproduces only the Fermi-surface shape and temper the interpretation of the direction-dependent δ-band renormalisation.

5. The evidence for a "chiral-nematic superconducting state" is thin and sits in the Supplementary Information. The claim (Abstract; p. 8, called "strong evidence") rests on Bogoliubov QPI at a subgap energy that "closely resembles" the normal-state chiral pattern (Supplementary Figs. 8, 9, 17). A conventional gap on a Fermi surface that is distorted for any reason would give the same pattern. With a U-shaped gap, subgap B-QPI resembling the normal state could also reflect normal-state scattering at the gap edge, or tip-induced broadening. The observation therefore constrains the Fermi surface, not the pairing symmetry. The authors should show a gap-anisotropy analysis that itself breaks the mirrors, for example the angular dependence of the gap magnitude from B-QPI, or a comparison of B-QPI at E < Δ, E ≈ Δ and E > Δ. Otherwise they should restate the result as superconducting quasiparticles inheriting the chiral-nematic Fermi surface.

6. The statistics of samples, tips, handedness and domains are not reported. The text calls the chirality "reproducible", and Fig. 2g shows one opposite-handedness region. The paper does not say how many samples, cleaves, fields of view and tips were measured at each doping, how often each handedness occurred, what the typical domain size is, or whether a domain wall was ever imaged. A short table would let readers judge universality. It would also show whether the domain populations behave as expected for spontaneous symmetry breaking (roughly equal populations of both handednesses without a symmetry-breaking field), which bears directly on concern 1.

7. The claim that the CDW at x = 0.05 is chiral rests on three CDW peak intensities. The 2a0 × 2a0 peak intensity ratio 2.6 : 1.2 : 1.0 (Fig. 4c) is used as evidence of chirality. FT-STM peak intensities depend on tip shape, set point and Feenstra normalisation. An unequal-amplitude triple-Q state is chiral only if the ordering of the amplitudes has a sense of rotation that is stable against these factors. The authors should show the ratio at several biases of both signs, at several set points, with several tips or in several regions, and give its uncertainty. They should also state how the CDW handedness relates to the handedness of the QPI distortion in the same field of view.

8. A surface or dopant-ordering origin is not addressed. The Sb-terminated surface is produced by cleaving and by moving Cs adatoms with the tip (Methods). Surface reconstruction, residual Cs, Ti ordering on particular V sublattices, or a surface-only orbital order could break the mirrors without any bulk Fermi-liquid instability. The DFT reference is mirror-symmetric by construction: it uses the virtual-crystal approximation, without spin–orbit coupling, at a single k_z = π (Methods, p. 9). Yet the claim rests on deviations from it of only a few percent. The Cs-terminated data (Supplementary Fig. 14) address the surface question only partly, and no bulk probe is provided. The authors should discuss explicitly whether the effect could be surface-confined. They should add a Ti dopant-site histogram over the three V sublattices from topography, since an imbalance would itself select a chirality. They should also confirm that SOC, k_z integration, the real Ti distribution and surface relaxation cannot lower the symmetry of the contours, so that the mirror-symmetric reference is a strict constraint and not an approximation.

9. The proposed microscopic origin has a direct consequence that is not tested. The sublattice charge order n1 ≠ n2 ≠ n3 (Eqs. 2–5; Supplementary Fig. 10b) is translation invariant. It should therefore show up as a C2, mirror-breaking pattern in the Bragg-peak intensities and positions, or as an intra-unit-cell contrast in topography. The affine correction does not remove Bragg-peak intensity asymmetries. The authors should analyse the Bragg-peak intensities, or do an intra-unit-cell sublattice analysis, for the x = 0.15 maps. A positive result would strongly support an intrinsic origin and would also bear on concern 1.

C. Minor

10. "Chiral" here means planar breaking of all vertical mirrors with C2 retained, which is equivalent to a nematic director rotated away from a mirror axis. It does not mean 3D structural chirality or time-reversal-symmetry breaking. The authors say this on p. 8, but it should be stated in the abstract and introduction, because the kagome literature (for example refs. 19, 20, 24) uses "chiral CDW" for time-reversal-breaking order. They should also state which point-group elements remain and why the term "chiral" is used rather than "rotated nematic".
