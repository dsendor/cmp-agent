Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper uses spectroscopic-imaging STM on Ti-doped CsV3Sb5 to argue that, with long-range CDW suppressed (x = 0.15), the low-energy quasiparticle interference (QPI) already breaks all in-plane mirror symmetries and C6 rotation symmetry. The authors call this a "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid" and propose that it is the parent state of the chiral CDW and of the chiral superconductivity reported in AV3Sb5. If it holds, this matters: it would move the origin of kagome "chirality" from the CDW itself to an electronic Fermi-surface instability (a d-wave Pomeranchuk distortion whose director is rotated off the mirror lines). The QPI is of good quality, and the analysis across directions, dopings and terminations is careful. However, the effect is small (QPI-vector differences of 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg|), it is resolved only after drift correction, and the claim that it is a "parent" state rests on low-temperature data alone.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- High-quality, orbital-resolved QPI on a CDW-free kagome surface (x = 0.15). The q1 (Sb pz α pocket) and q2/q3 (V d δ pockets) channels are cleanly identified (Figs. 1c, 1g, 3a–b).
- The mirror-symmetry breaking is shown in several independent ways: q1/q2 separation and q3 tilt in three directions (Fig. 2a–d), the energy-dependent dispersions along the three A–H_m directions (Fig. 3c–d), and similar features at x = 0, 0.04, 0.05, 0.15 and 0.27, on the Cs-terminated surface, and in Bogoliubov QPI (Fig. 4g; Supplementary Figs. 4–8, 14, 16).
- Two controls already point away from a fixed scan artifact: opposite handedness in another region of the same sample (Fig. 2g), and T-matrix simulations arguing that random point defects do not produce the distorted pattern (Supplementary Figs. 18–20).
- A simple phenomenological model, k_F(θ) = k_F^0(θ) + η cos(2θ − π/6), reproduces the main QPI distortions (Fig. 2e–f). It gives a concrete framework that others can test.
- Raw data are deposited publicly.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Extrinsic sources of a few-percent, all-mirror-breaking distortion are not quantitatively excluded. The headline observation is a distortion of order 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg| (Supplementary Table 1), plus differences in q-vector lengths and dispersion slopes between the three A–H_m directions. Three non-electronic mechanisms produce exactly this symmetry class:
   - Piezo nonlinearity, creep or thermal drift, together with the affine transformation applied "to the Bragg points to restore a perfectly symmetric lattice" (Methods; used for Figs. 2b and 4d).
   - An asymmetric tip, which convolves the LDOS with a non-symmetric kernel.
   - Uniaxial strain along a low-symmetry direction, from cleaving or mounting. Such strain breaks every mirror and gives a "nematic director at a generic angle", which is exactly the η cos(2θ + φ) form with φ ≠ 0, π/2 used in the paper.

   An electronically driven distortion and a strain-driven band deformation (V d bands near van Hove points respond strongly to strain) would look the same in Figs. 2–3. The opposite-handedness region in Fig. 2g helps against a fixed scanner artifact, but it does not rule out strain domains. The paper also does not say whether Fig. 2g was taken with the same tip and in the same scan frame.

   Please provide:
   - (i) The raw, uncorrected Bragg-peak positions for every dataset: magnitude and direction of the lattice distortion that the affine correction removes, with that correction's size compared with the 1.4–2.2% effect.
   - (ii) A test of whether the QPI distortion axis and handedness correlate with the residual lattice-strain axis or with the fast-scan direction across all datasets (x = 0, 0.04, 0.05, 0.15, 0.27, Cs surface, Fig. 2g region).
   - (iii) The same field of view re-imaged with the scan frame rotated (for example by 60° or 90°), and after a deliberate tip change. The handedness should stay locked to the lattice, not to the scan frame or the tip.
   - (iv) A map of a chiral domain boundary, or at least the number of regions, cleaves and tips examined and the fraction showing each handedness.

   Without these, the central claim that the electronic state itself breaks all mirrors is not established beyond a plausible but unexcluded extrinsic origin.

2. The "parent state" and the T* ≈ 167 K claim are not supported by temperature-dependent data. The title and abstract present the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid as the parent state that emerges at T* > T_CDW and "genetically engenders" the chiral CDW. All normal-state QPI, however, is taken at 0.4 K, with Bogoliubov comparisons near 3.5 K and 4 K (Supplementary Fig. 4). The estimate T* ~ 167 K is a direct conversion of an average energy splitting (−14.4 meV) into temperature by E/k_B, with no mean-field or other justification (page 8). At x = 0.15 the state coexists with superconductivity at low T and has no CDW, but showing that it precedes the CDW in the x = 0 compound requires showing that it exists above T_CDW (~90 K). The torque measurements cited for support (ref. 28) report an onset near 130 K, which does not match 167 K.

   The authors should do one of two things:
   - Measure QPI at x = 0.15, and ideally at x = 0, at several elevated temperatures (for example 20, 50, 100 K and above T_CDW for x = 0), and track the anisotropy measures (d12/d13/d23, the q-vector differences, the q3 tilt) as a function of T.
   - Otherwise, narrow the title, abstract and Fig. 4f to "a chiral-nematic Fermi surface in the CDW-free regime at low temperature, consistent with a parent-state scenario". In that case, either remove the T* estimate or present it clearly as an order-of-magnitude scale with a stated prefactor.

B. Should address

3. Uncertainties on the symmetry-breaking quantities are not shown in the main text, and the overlap in Fig. 3c–d is substantial. The chirality ordering v^III_{1,s} < v^I_{1,s} < v^II_{1,s} (velocities 4583.84, 5107.30, 4121.17 meV/|Q_Bragg|) and the mass ratios (1.33, 1.19, 1.47) are quoted to six significant figures without errors. Yet the error bars in Fig. 3c overlap substantially between directions at most energies, and the linear-fit energy window is not specified. Please:
   - give fit uncertainties for every velocity and mass ratio in the main text;
   - state the fit window and show that the ordering survives reasonable changes to it;
   - state the statistical significance of the 1.4–2.2% distortion, including how pixel resolution (ε_pix) compares with the effect;
   - state whether the six-line averaging along symmetry directions (Methods) is done in the affine-corrected frame.

4. The model comparison is qualitative, and its parameters are not stated.
   - The value of η used in Fig. 2f is not given, and neither is whether φ = −π/6 was fitted or chosen.
   - The use of the k_z = π CEC is called "an approximation", but there is no quantitative comparison (for example predicted versus measured d12, d13, d23 or q-length differences).
   - The relation v_F ≈ 2v_s for inter-pocket δ scattering is assumed even though the two δ pockets connected by q2/q3 have different velocities in the distorted state. That assumption feeds directly into the m*/m_DFT values (1.53, 1.95, 2.33), which exceed the DFT+DMFT range the text cites as agreement.

   Please give the fitted η, φ and their uncertainties, compare the predicted anisotropies quantitatively with the measured ones, and propagate the 2v_s approximation into the quoted renormalizations.

5. The microscopic attribution is largely conjectural, and the alternative of surface symmetry breaking is not addressed.
   - The Sb-terminated surface is produced by cleaving and by tip-moving Cs adatoms (Methods). Surface reconstructions, residual Cs, Ti ordering on particular sublattices, or a surface-only orbital order could break mirrors without any bulk Fermi-liquid instability.
   - The Cs-terminated data (Supplementary Fig. 14) partly address this, but a bulk probe is not provided.

   Please discuss explicitly whether the effect could be surface-confined, and add a statement on Ti site and sublattice distribution from topography (for example a dopant-site histogram over the three V sublattices). An imbalance there would itself select a chirality.

6. The claim of a chiral-nematic superconducting state rests only on Bogoliubov QPI in the Supplementary (Figs. 8, 9, 17), stated in the main text as "strong evidence". Bogoliubov QPI at a subgap energy that "closely resembles" the normal state may simply inherit the normal-state band distortion and need not reflect any symmetry property of the order parameter.

   Please either show a gap-anisotropy measurement (for example angle-resolved gap magnitude from Bogoliubov QPI versus energy) that independently breaks the mirrors, or soften "chiral-nematic SC ground state" to "the superconducting quasiparticles inherit the chiral-nematic Fermi surface".

7. The claim of a chiral CDW intensity ratio at x = 0.05 is not robust as stated. The 2a0×2a0 peak intensity ratio 2.6:1.2:1.0 (Fig. 4c) is used to claim that the CDW is chiral. FT peak intensities depend on setpoint, tip and the Feenstra normalization. Please show that the ratio is stable across setpoints, energies and fields of view, and give its uncertainty.

C. Minor

8. The handedness labels across dopings (red at x = 0 and 0.15, blue at 0.05 and 0.27 in Fig. 4g) presumably reflect random domains. State this explicitly, so that readers do not infer a doping-dependent handedness.
9. The text says the CEC at k_z = π shows "good quantitative agreement with the experimental data", while also calling it "only a qualitative reference". Reconcile these statements.
10. "Chirality" here means planar breaking of all mirrors, not time-reversal breaking. The paper states this on page 8, but it should be clear in the abstract and introduction, because the kagome literature commonly uses "chiral CDW" for time-reversal-breaking order.
11. Fig. 3a: specify the q-pixel size, the FOV size and the energy step. Also specify the Lorentzian fit model (number of peaks, background) used for Supplementary Fig. 2.
12. Fig. 4f is a schematic phase diagram that places T* above T_CDW for all doping without any measured points. Label it as conjectural.
13. Small typesetting issues: "Fermi velocities v^m_{1,F} = 2v^m_{1,s} = (…)" lacks units in one instance; in Methods, "Nb crystal" calibration for an electronic temperature of 650 mK should cite the fitting procedure.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a dedicated "artifact controls" figure or Supplementary section: raw versus affine-corrected Bragg peaks, rotated-scan-frame and tip-change datasets, and a correlation of handedness with strain axis and scan direction across every dataset.
- Track one simple scalar order parameter (for example (d23 − d12)/d13, or the q2 length difference) versus temperature, doping, region and energy, with error bars. This would make the chiral-nematic state quantitatively testable and would directly address T*.
- Map a chiral domain wall in real space by spatially resolved (windowed) FT QPI. Showing an abrupt handedness reversal not tied to topographic features would be strong evidence for an intrinsic order parameter.
- Consider a strain-tuning control (for example a sample on a piezo strain cell or a deliberately bent substrate) to test whether external uniaxial strain selects or rotates the chiral-nematic director.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
