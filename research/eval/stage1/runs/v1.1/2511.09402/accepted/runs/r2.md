Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper uses spectroscopic-imaging STM on Ti-doped CsV3Sb5 to argue that, with long-range CDW order suppressed (x = 0.15), the low-energy quasiparticle interference already breaks every vertical mirror and the C6/C3 rotations of the kagome lattice. The authors call this a "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid" and propose it as the parent state from which the chiral CDW and the unconventional superconductivity of the AV3Sb5 family descend. If the result holds, it would move the origin of the much-debated rotation and mirror symmetry breaking in kagome metals from the CDW to the normal-state Fermi liquid, which would be a significant conceptual step. The evidence, though, is a small (about 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg|) direction-dependent distortion of QPI wavevectors measured at 0.4 K, and the paper does not yet rule out the most ordinary sources of such a distortion.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The QPI data at x = 0.15 are of high quality. They resolve three distinct scattering channels (q1, q2, q3) and follow their dispersion over about ±60 meV in three symmetry-related directions (Figs. 2 and 3).
- The broken-symmetry signature is seen in several independent ways: the q1/q2 separation, the tilt of the q3 segment, the inter-triangle spacings d12, d23 and d13, and the direction-dependent Fermi velocities. They are all consistent with one d-wave Pomeranchuk-type distortion, k_F(θ) = k_F^0(θ) + η cos(2θ − π/6) (Fig. 2e,f).
- Opposite handedness is seen in a second region of the same sample (Fig. 2g). This points to domains rather than a fixed instrumental bias.
- The same distortion appears across dopings (x = 0, 0.04, 0.05, 0.15, 0.27), on both Sb- and Cs-terminated surfaces, and in the superconducting state (Fig. 4g; Supplementary Figs. 5, 6, 7, 8, 14, 16). That consistency supports universality.
- The authors already address one alternative explanation (anisotropic or correlated strong point defects) with T-matrix simulations (Supplementary Figs. 18–20).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **Extrinsic sources of an l = 2 mirror-breaking distortion (strain, scanner drift and its correction, tip) are not excluded.** The headline claim rests on direction-dependent QPI wavevectors that differ by only 1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg| (p. 3; Supplementary Table 1), with Fermi-velocity differences between the three A–H_m directions (Fig. 3c,d).
   - The proposed form, η cos(2θ + φ) with φ = −π/6, is exactly what a uniform in-plane uniaxial or shear strain produces when its principal axis does not lie along a crystal mirror. Strain is an l = 2 tensor. When its axis sits between σ_v and σ_v′, it breaks all vertical mirrors and C3 while keeping C2, and the two mirror-related strain orientations give the two "handednesses." Cleaving at 13 K and differential thermal contraction against the sample holder routinely produce strain of this order. AV3Sb5 is also known to have a large electronic nematic susceptibility, so even modest strain could give a percent-level Fermi-surface distortion.
   - The Methods (p. 9) say an affine transformation is applied to the Bragg points "to restore a perfectly symmetric lattice." That step removes exactly the information that would reveal lattice strain or residual scanner non-orthogonality, and it rescales the q-space metric in which the 1–2% differences are then measured.
   - The paper does not report (i) the uncorrected Bragg-peak positions and the size and orientation of the affine correction for each map; (ii) whether the inferred nematic director is correlated with the strain or correction axis; (iii) whether the same field of view, imaged at a different scan angle or with the fast-scan axis rotated, gives a distortion that stays fixed to the lattice; or (iv) whether Fig. 2a and Fig. 2g were taken with the same tip and scan geometry. If they were, the opposite handedness is a strong control against tip and scanner artifacts and should be stated explicitly.
   - The T-matrix study (Supplementary Figs. 18–20) addresses defects only. Strain is not discussed anywhere.
   - What to do: report the raw lattice distortion and the affine parameters for every map in the paper. Show that the QPI distortion axis and handedness do not follow the scan direction or the strain axis (for example, maps at rotated scan angles, or domains of opposite handedness within a single uncorrected map). Give a quantitative estimate of how much distortion the residual strain could produce. If both chiralities coexist in one strain-free field of view, separated by a domain wall, that would settle the issue.

B. Should address

2. **The statistical significance of the direction dependence is not established.**
   - The q differences are about 0.004–0.007 |Q_Bragg| (from 1.4–2.2%). Fig. 3c,d show many energies where the error bars of the three directions overlap, especially for q1.
   - The error bars combine Lorentzian-fit errors with pixel resolution (Fig. 3 caption), but they do not include the uncertainty from the affine correction (concern 1) or from the choice of cut position. The line profiles are also averages of six neighbouring lines (Methods).
   - The extracted velocities and mass ratios depend on the energy window chosen for the linear fit, which is stated only qualitatively ("extracted away from these kink features").
   - What to do: give the fit windows, the uncertainties on each v_s^m in Supplementary Table 2, and a significance test (for example, a χ² comparison of one common velocity against three separate velocities). Show that the ordering of v^I, v^II and v^III survives reasonable changes of the window.

3. **The Fermi-velocity anisotropy is much larger than the Fermi-surface distortion, and the paper does not discuss this.** For the δ band, the renormalization ratios are 1.53, 1.95 and 2.33 in the three directions (p. 6), so the velocities differ by up to about 50% between directions related by C3 in the parent lattice. The Fermi-wavevector distortion, by contrast, is about 2% of |Q_Bragg|.
   - The single-parameter Pomeranchuk form η cos(2θ + φ) used for the simulations does not obviously give such a large velocity anisotropy.
   - Some of the velocities come from q2 and q3, which connect states that are not related by inversion. The relation v_F ≈ 2 v_s is invoked "to leading order" for these, which adds an uncontrolled factor.
   - What to do: show whether the velocity anisotropy follows quantitatively from the same η and φ that fit the wavevectors, or say that the model reproduces only the Fermi-surface shape. Moderate the quantitative mass-renormalization claims accordingly.

4. **The "parent state" and T* rest on low-temperature data and an order-of-magnitude conversion.**
   - All QPI is taken at 0.4 K, and a few superconducting-crossing maps at 4 K. T* ≈ 167 K is obtained by converting an average energy splitting of 14.4 meV directly with k_B (p. 8). That is not a measured transition temperature, and the conversion has no justified prefactor.
   - The torque data cited in support (ref. 28) show an onset near 130 K, so the two estimates do not agree well.
   - The claim that the CDW "descends from" this state is inferred from the similarity of the residual QPI at x = 0.05 to that at x = 0.15. It is not shown by tracking a transition.
   - What to do: either measure the x = 0.15 QPI distortion at one or more elevated temperatures (for example 20–80 K, where QPI is often still resolvable) to show that it persists or weakens, or present T* explicitly as a rough energy-scale estimate. In the latter case, soften "parent state" and "demonstrating that the CDW-triggered exotic states descend from" to wording that reflects a phase-diagram correlation.

5. **The case for a "chiral-nematic superconducting state" is weak and sits entirely in the Supplementary Information.** It rests on Bogoliubov-QPI maps at subgap energy resembling the normal-state QPI (Supplementary Fig. 8). With a U-shaped gap, subgap B-QPI similar to the normal state could reflect normal-state scattering at the gap edge, or tip-induced broadening, rather than a symmetry property of the condensate.
   - What to do: show a gap-symmetry or gap-anisotropy analysis (for example, angular dependence of the gap, or a comparison of B-QPI at E < Δ, E ≈ Δ and E > Δ). Otherwise, restrict the claim to the observation that the normal-state symmetry breaking persists below T_c.

6. **The chirality claim for the CDW at x = 0.05 rests on the intensities of three CDW peaks.** The 2a0 × 2a0 peak intensities are given as 2.6 : 1.2 : 1.0 (Fig. 4c). Peak intensity ratios in FT-STM depend on tip shape, setpoint and normalization, and an unequal-amplitude triple-Q state is not chiral unless the ordering of amplitudes has a sense of rotation that is stable against these factors.
   - What to do: show the ratio at several biases (including both signs), with several tips or in several regions, and say how it relates to the handedness of the QPI distortion in the same field of view.

7. **The proposed microscopic origin has a direct, untested consequence.** The sublattice charge order n1 ≠ n2 ≠ n3 (Eqs. 2–5; Supplementary Fig. 10b) is translation invariant, so it should appear as a C2, mirror-breaking pattern of intensities and positions in the Bragg peaks, or as an intra-unit-cell contrast in topography.
   - The affine correction does not remove Bragg-peak intensity asymmetries.
   - What to do: analyse the Bragg-peak intensities, or perform an intra-unit-cell sublattice analysis, for the x = 0.15 maps. A positive result would strongly support an intrinsic origin, and it would also bear on concern 1.

8. **Samples, tips and domains are not stated.**
   - The text says the chirality is "reproducible," but it does not give how many cleaves, samples, tips and fields of view were measured at each x.
   - It also does not give how often each handedness occurred, the typical domain size, or whether a domain wall was ever imaged.
   - What to do: add a table of these numbers. They would support universality and help with concern 1.

C. Minor

9. **The terminology needs clarification.** "Chiral" here means the absence of every vertical mirror while C2 is retained, which is equivalent to a nematic director rotated away from a mirror axis. Say explicitly which point-group elements remain (C2 only?), why this is called chiral rather than a rotated nematic, and how it differs from the 3D chirality and time-reversal-symmetry-breaking chirality discussed in refs. 19, 20 and 24.

10. **The DFT reference has limits.** It is computed without spin–orbit coupling, with the virtual-crystal approximation, at a fixed k_z = π, and is used as the bare reference for the mass enhancements. Comment on how SOC and k_z dispersion would change v_F^DFT, especially for the δ band.

11. **The parameter η in the simulation (Fig. 2e,f) is not given.** State its value, say whether it was fitted or chosen, and give the Fermi-surface distortion it implies, so it can be compared with the 1.4–2.2% figure.

12. **The comparison with ARPES is qualitative.** Lower mass enhancement than ARPES is attributed to differences in doping, reference band and surface sensitivity (p. 6). A one-line estimate of the size of each effect would make the comparison more useful.

13. **Several results are located only in the Supplementary Information.** These include the undoped and x = 0.04 and x = 0.27 data, the B-QPI data and the T-matrix simulations. They carry key parts of the universality argument, so give a one-sentence quantitative summary of each in the main text, for example the measured distortion at each x.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a strain and scanner-artifact control section: raw versus corrected Bragg peaks, a rotated-scan-angle map of the same area, and, ideally, an opposite-chirality domain wall in a single field of view.
- Use the 11 T magnet and the temperature control to test the state. A temperature series up to tens of kelvin at x = 0.15, and a test of whether the domain handedness responds to field training or to deliberately applied uniaxial strain, would distinguish a spontaneous electronic order from an extrinsic distortion.
- Quantify the distortion as a single order parameter (η, φ) for each map, with uncertainties, and plot it against doping and energy. That gives a compact test of the claimed universality.
- Recast T* and the "parent state" wording so that they match what low-temperature data can establish.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
