Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript compares Ni and Cu L3-edge RIXS on an undoped but superconducting infinite-layer nickelate film (PrNiO2, PNO) and on the infinite-layer cuprate CaCuO2 (CCO), measured under matched conditions on the same beamline. It reports three main results. First, a linear-spin-wave (LSW) fit gives J1 ≈ 46 meV for PNO against 82 meV for CCO, with comparable interlayer couplings J⊥ (6 and 7 meV). Second, PNO magnons are much less damped than magnons in doped Bi2201. Third, and newest, the dxy orbiton in PNO disperses with the opposite sign to that in CCO; the authors attribute this to nearest-neighbour (NN) orbital superexchange in the nickelate and next-nearest-neighbour (NNN) superexchange in the cuprate. A direct side-by-side comparison of spin and orbital excitations in the two infinite-layer families is useful. The orbiton-dispersion result would be a new piece of physics if it holds up.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- Both materials were measured on the same instrument along the same Brillouin-zone paths, with polarization analysis of the scattered photons. This gives a cleaner comparison than one assembled from the literature.
- Clear, dispersive magnons in a superconducting, nominally undoped nickelate film are an informative data set. The fitted J1 for PNO agrees with earlier nickelate reports.
- Polarization-resolved dd spectra are compared with single-ion cross-section calculations (Fig. 4). This gives the orbital assignments a check beyond energy alone.
- The opposite sign of the dxy dispersion, if confirmed, gives a concrete and testable link between the magnetic environment and how orbitons propagate.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The dispersion formula used to extract J^orb is the NNN form, but the PNO result is interpreted as NN superexchange.** Eq. (1), ε_k = 2J^orb cos(kx)cos(ky), is the dispersion for hopping between diagonal (next-nearest) neighbours; Ref. 43 used it for that reason. NN orbital hopping gives ε_k ∝ 2J(cos kx + cos ky).
   - The two forms make different predictions along the measured paths. In the NNN form, X = (0.5,0) and (0.25,0.25) differ by 2J and the [H,H] dispersion is cos²-like. In the NN form, X and (0.25,0.25) are degenerate and the bandwidth along [H,0] is half the full Γ–M bandwidth.
   - Section II and Table III nonetheless conclude that "the experimental J^orb agrees with the calculated orbital NN coupling". That conclusion rests on a J^orb extracted with the NNN functional form.
   - The quoted bandwidths also do not obviously match Eq. (1). With J^orb = 14 meV, Eq. (1) gives at most about 56 meV between Γ and X along [H,0], and about 20 meV between Γ and (0.34,0.34). Fig. 5(a) marks 62 and 90 meV. For CCO, J = −11 meV gives about 44 and 22 meV, while Fig. 5(c) marks 56 and 34 meV.
   - The authors should:
     - fit the PNO (and CCO) dxy dispersion with the NN form, the NNN form, and a combined J_NN + J_NNN form, and report which one the data distinguish, for example through the relative energies at X and (0.25,0.25);
     - explain how the 62/90/34/56 meV markers relate to the fitted curves;
     - restate the comparison with the theory values in Table III using the matching functional form.

   Without this, the central "NN vs NNN" interpretation is not supported by the fit actually performed.

2. **The PNO dxy dispersion (tens of meV) is not shown to be robust against fitting and geometry systematics.** The PNO dd region (Fig. 2c) is broad. The dxy component has a width of roughly 0.5 eV, and it overlaps the 0.7 eV "RE peak", the dxz/yz peak and a charge continuum. The claimed dispersion is a few tens of meV, comparable to the 40 meV resolution and a small fraction of the peak width.
   - With a fixed scattering angle, sweeping H from negative to positive moves the geometry continuously from grazing incidence to grazing emission. Fig. 4 shows that the polarization character, and hence the relative cross-sections of dxy, dxz/yz and the 0.7 eV feature, changes strongly between ±H in PNO; the text itself notes that the dxy character "is very different at positive H". A smooth, geometry-driven change in the relative weights of overlapping components can shift a fitted centroid by tens of meV and could mimic a dispersion.
   - Self-absorption, which also changes with geometry, is a further candidate.
   - The authors should:
     - state which fit parameters (widths, positions of the other components) were fixed or free across momenta;
     - show the dispersion at equivalent momenta measured in both grazing-incidence and grazing-emission geometries, or show that the fitted energies at ±H agree;
     - show the result from a model-free estimator, such as the first moment over a fixed window or the position of the maximum, alongside the multi-peak fit;
     - report how the extracted dispersion changes when the 0.7 eV and continuum components are treated differently.

   The CCO dd peaks are sharp, so this concern is mainly about PNO. However, the claim that the two materials have *opposite* dispersions depends on the PNO result being robust.

3. **J⊥ and the "three-dimensional AFM" conclusion rest on near-Γ magnon energies taken at uncontrolled L, in a region dominated by elastic and phonon signal.** The Methods state that 2θ was fixed and "the value of the out-of-plane momentum component L was not fixed", and add that "this is not an issue" because in-plane couplings dominate. Yet J⊥ is extracted from exactly the L dependence of the gap near Γ ("when approaching the Γ point at non-integer L values, the single-magnon energy remains large").
   - In PNO the claimed in-plane-zone-centre magnon energy (60–80 meV) coincides with the ~80 meV phonon included in the fit.
   - It lies within about two resolution widths of a very strong specular/elastic line (Fig. 1e).
   - The near-Γ points in Fig. 3(a) carry the largest error bars.
   - An alternative explanation for a finite energy near Γ is the fit misattributing phonon or elastic tails. Magnetic anisotropy, which the paper mentions (Ref. 59), is another.
   - The authors should:
     - list the L value of each point used in the SpinW fit, and confirm that the fit uses those L values rather than a fixed L;
     - show raw spectra and fit decompositions at the near-Γ momenta for PNO;
     - test whether a model with J⊥ = 0 plus an anisotropy gap, or a 2D model with free phonon parameters, fits the data equally well;
     - ideally, measure the magnon energy as a function of L at fixed in-plane Q, for example by varying 2θ or the photon energy within the resonance.

   The statements that "both materials support a three-dimensional antiferromagnetic order" (abstract) and that J⊥/J1 = 13% in PNO need this support. This is all the more so because bulk IL nickelates show no long-range order (Introduction).

**B. Should address**

4. **The microscopic model comparison in Table III is tuned, so agreement with experiment is weak validation.** The PNO hopping integrals are set to "80% of their corresponding CCO values", and Δ = 8 eV was chosen as a value "sufficiently larger than U also providing reasonable results". U = 3.2 eV for Ni is also at the low end of common estimates. With Δ selected for its outcome, the match J_NN = 14.5 meV against 14 ± 4 meV is not independent evidence. The authors should show how J^orb_NN and J^orb_NNN vary over plausible ranges of Δ, U and t_pd. They should state which conclusions, in particular the near-vanishing of J_NNN in PNO, are insensitive to these choices.

5. **The damping comparison with Bi2201 does not isolate self-doping as the cause.**
   - Fig. 3(b) compares PNO damping (from this work, at 40 meV resolution, using the authors' fitting) with Bi2201 values taken from Ref. 49. Those values come from different instrument conditions and possibly a different fitting protocol.
   - The PNO broadening of ~100 meV could also come from film disorder, incomplete or inhomogeneous reduction, strain or finite thickness (a ~5 nm film), and not from self-doping.
   - The conclusion that self-doping "endow[s] IL nickelates with a non-disruptive way to achieve superconductivity which is totally absent in copper oxides" goes well beyond this one comparison.
   - The authors should:
     - state whether the Bi2201 data were refitted with the same model and resolution;
     - discuss the extrinsic sources of broadening;
     - ideally, compare with a non-superconducting or differently reduced PNO film, or with Sr-doped PNO measured in the same session;
     - otherwise, soften the claim.

6. **"Coexistence of superconductivity and sharp spin excitations" is inferred from measurements above Tc.** RIXS was done at 20 K. Zero resistance is reached only below 4 K, with onset slightly below 11 K. The data show that sharp magnons exist in a film that superconducts at lower temperature; they do not show coexistence in the superconducting state. The text in Section II and the comparison with muon data (Ref. 39) should be worded accordingly. Alternatively, a spectrum below Tc should be added, if that is technically possible.

7. **Sample characterization and reproducibility are not documented in this manuscript.**
   - One film of each material appears to have been measured.
   - No transport, XRD or reduction-quality data for the measured PNO film are shown; only Ref. 40 is cited. It is unclear whether the RIXS film is the same one whose Tc is quoted.
   - The statement that the comparable J⊥ values "give a proof of the fully occurred topotactic reduction of the PNO samples and the absence of interstitial oxygen atoms" is not justified. Magnon dispersion is not a direct probe of residual apical oxygen. XAS line shape or XRD c-axis values would be more direct evidence.
   - The authors should show XRD and ρ(T) for the measured film, give XAS evidence of Ni¹⁺, state the number of samples, and remove or justify the "proof" statement.

8. **The four-J LSW fit may not be unique, and its applicability to PNO is assumed.** Five couplings (J1–J4, J⊥) are fitted to dispersions along two cuts. J2, J3 and J4 are known to be strongly correlated with each other and with J1 in such fits. The quoted uncertainties (e.g. J1 = 45.8 ± 0.3 meV) look small for that situation. LSW also assumes long-range order, which PNO is said to lack. The authors should report the parameter covariance or confidence regions. They should check whether a simpler model (J1, J2 plus J⊥, or an effective one-band Hubbard parametrization) fits equally well, and comment on whether LSW is adequate for a disordered or short-range-correlated state.

9. **The PNO dxy peak assignment and its polarization anomaly remain speculative.** The 0.7 eV "RE peak" is excluded from the analysis. The dxy peak's mixed polarization character, and its σ-incident intensity being stronger than the single-ion calculation predicts, are attributed to Pr 5d or interstitial-s hybridization without a test. Because this peak is the one whose dispersion carries the main new claim, the authors should at least show the single-ion fit quality quantitatively. They should also discuss whether a component from the 0.7 eV feature or the continuum could contribute to the measured "dxy" peak and its momentum dependence.

**C. Minor**

10. The Fig. 5 caption says the continuous line "represents the charge transfer model fitting". The text says the dispersion was fitted with Eq. (1). Please make these consistent.

11. The Methods give the CCO "sheet resistance ... at room temperature" as 2 mΩ, which is implausible for an insulating film, and also give a resistivity of ~6000 mΩ·cm. Please check the units and values.

12. Table I: the PNO d_z² energy (2.96 ± 0.58 eV) is essentially unconstrained. The text's statement that it is "possibly larger" than in the cuprate should acknowledge this.

13. Fig. 2(a) is a sum over H = 0.4–0.475. State whether the magnon and phonon parameters shown there are representative of the single-momentum fits used in Fig. 3.

14. The [H,H] anomalies (maximum at (0.2,0.2), elastic increase beyond (0.25,0.25)) and the stripe-order speculation are given without supporting data in the paper. Either show the elastic intensity versus Q, or shorten this paragraph.

15. The Supplementary Information and the Zenodo link are placeholders ([52], "URL to be inserted"). Fitting details referred to the SI could not be assessed.

16. Typos and wording: "Aand", "out-to-in-plane", "tridimensional", "infiite-layer" (Ref. 19), the duplicated "Acknowledgments" heading, and the Fig. 1 caption, which mentions "dark blue arrows" on metal sites.

17. The closing speculation that the lower Tc is "mainly due to the overall smaller energy of spin fluctuations", and that Mott-Hubbard character implies weaker electron–phonon coupling and no CDW, is not tested here. It should be labelled clearly as speculation or shortened.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Refit the orbiton dispersions with the NN, NNN and combined forms, and show the model-free peak positions at ±H in both geometries. This one analysis would firm up, or correct, the paper's most novel claim.
2. Add an L scan at the in-plane zone centre for PNO, or at minimum the per-point L values and near-Γ fit decompositions, to support the J⊥ value.
3. Add a sensitivity map of the calculated J^orb_NN and J^orb_NNN against Δ, U and t_pd, so the theory comparison carries weight.
4. Include characterization of the measured PNO film (XRD, ρ(T), XAS) in the main text or SI, and moderate the claims about self-doping and coexistence.

**5. Verdict**

Verdict: major revision
