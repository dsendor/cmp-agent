Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript compares, with the same spectrometer and comparable analysis, Ni L3 RIXS on an undoped superconducting PrNiO2 film and Cu L3 RIXS on an insulating CaCuO2 film, covering the magnon dispersion and damping, polarization-resolved spin and dd excitations, and the momentum dispersion of the dxy orbital excitation. The new results are (i) a linear-spin-wave fit for the nickelate that includes an interlayer coupling J⊥, (ii) a dxy orbiton dispersion in PNO whose sign is opposite to that recently reported for CCO, and (iii) an interpretation of that sign change as nearest-neighbour rather than next-nearest-neighbour orbital superexchange. A side-by-side comparison of infinite-layer nickelates and cuprates is timely. The headline interpretations, however, rest on parameter extractions whose robustness is not yet demonstrated.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The two infinite-layer compounds are measured on the same beamline (ID32), at the same temperature and with similar resolution (40/42 meV), so the comparison is direct.
- Both materials have full momentum maps along M–Γ–X–M (Fig. 1d,e), and the analysis is applied in parallel to both.
- Polarization-resolved RIXS with single-ion cross-section calculations (Fig. 4) is a useful check on the dd assignments and on the magnon/bimagnon character in both systems.
- The opposite sign of the dxy dispersion (Fig. 5) is visible in the raw maps and stacks, and if it holds up it is a new observation for nickelates.
- The microscopic estimate of J_NN^orb and J_NNN^orb (Tables II–III) tries to tie the orbital dispersion to a model rather than leaving it phenomenological.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The dispersion form used for the PNO orbiton does not match the nearest-neighbour mechanism it is used to support.** Eq. (1), ε_k = 2J^orb cos kx cos ky, is the momentum dependence of a diagonal (next-nearest-neighbour) hop: cos kx cos ky = ½[cos(kx+ky) + cos(kx−ky)]. A nearest-neighbour hop of a single orbital excitation on a square lattice gives ∝ (cos kx + cos ky), which has a different momentum pattern. In that form the energy at (0.5,0) equals the energy at (0.25,0.25), and the [H,H] bandwidth is larger than the [H,0] one. Yet the paper fits PNO with Eq. (1), takes J^orb = +14 ± 4 meV, and sets it against the calculated J_NN^orb = 14.5 meV (Table III) as evidence that NN superexchange dominates (pp. 6–7, abstract). The fitted value and the annotated bandwidths also disagree. With J^orb = 14 meV, Eq. (1) predicts a drop of about 56 meV from Γ to (0.475,0) and about 20 meV from Γ to (0.34,0.34). Fig. 5(a) marks 90 meV and 62 meV. For CCO, J^orb = −11 meV gives about 44 and 22 meV, against the marked 56 and 34 meV in Fig. 5(c). The authors should:
   - state which dispersion relation the charge-transfer model of Ref. 43 gives for NN and for NNN orbital superexchange;
   - fit the PNO data with both the NN and NNN forms (and their combination), giving residuals;
   - reconcile the fitted J^orb with the bandwidths marked in Fig. 5.

   If the PNO data are in fact better described by the cos kx cos ky form with positive sign, the NN interpretation in the abstract needs revisiting.

2. **The PNO dxy "dispersion" may come from changing weights of overlapping peaks rather than a true shift.** In PNO the dxy feature is broad (FWHM ≈ 0.5 eV in Fig. 2c). It overlaps the 0.7 eV "RE" peak on one side, the dxz/yz peak on the other, and the self-doping continuum underneath. The proposed dispersion is 50–90 meV, a small fraction of that width. At fixed 2θ = 149.5°, scanning H changes the incidence and emission angles. Fig. 4 shows, and the text stresses (p. 6), that the polarization and cross-section of the 1.29 eV peak in PNO depend strongly on geometry and on the sign of H. A momentum-dependent change in the relative weight of these overlapping components would move the fitted dxy centroid even if no excitation disperses. The fact that the measured σ-polarized dxy intensity at H = 0.4 departs from the single-ion calculation (p. 6) adds to the concern. Needed:
   - the fit constraints used for Fig. 5(a), in the main text: which widths, positions and background terms are free or fixed across Q;
   - a test showing that the extracted dxy energy is unchanged when the widths of dxy, dxz/yz and the 0.7 eV peak are fixed;
   - a comparison of ±H points at equal |Q| (grazing incidence against grazing emission) to separate geometric weight changes from genuine dispersion;
   - a simulation with the single-ion model showing how far the apparent centroid moves from cross-section changes alone, if feasible.

   The opposite sign relative to CCO is the central orbital result, so it must survive these checks.

3. **The claim that J⊥ and "three-dimensional antiferromagnetic order" are present in PNO is not established by the data shown.** The abstract and p. 4 conclude that J⊥ is comparable in PNO and CCO, "indicating that both materials support a three-dimensional antiferromagnetic order". The only evidence is the finite magnon energy near Γ (60–80 meV) in Fig. 3(a), fitted with an LSW model whose ordered ground state is assumed. Several points are open.
   - L is not controlled. The Methods state that L was "not fixed" at fixed 2θ, and the L values at each point are not given; by my estimate L ≈ 0.43 at Γ for PNO. No L dependence at fixed in-plane momentum is shown, and that is the measurement that would separate an interlayer-exchange gap from other origins.
   - A finite zone-centre energy also arises from exchange or single-ion anisotropy (the paper itself cites Ref. 59), from a damped paramagnon whose peak is pushed up by damping, or from the fit of an excitation that sits on the elastic tail and the ~60–80 meV phonon. Near Γ the PNO damping (~100 meV, Fig. 3b) exceeds the fitted energy, the resolution is 40 meV, and the error bars near Γ in Fig. 3(a) are the largest in the data set.
   - "Order" conflicts with the literature cited in the introduction (Refs. 13, 19, 20, 38): bulk infinite-layer nickelates show no long-range order and show spin-glass behaviour. An LSW fit to paramagnon dispersions measures spin correlations, not order.
   - A J⊥ of 5.9 ± 1.1 meV from five free exchange parameters fitted to one dispersion per cut needs the parameter correlations and the sensitivity of χ² to J⊥ = 0 with an anisotropy gap instead.

   The authors should do one of two things. They could measure or show the L dependence at Γ (or at a fixed H) to demonstrate interlayer dispersion, give the L value for each point, and compare fits with J⊥ against fits with an anisotropy gap and J⊥ = 0. Otherwise they should restate the claim as "the data are consistent with a finite J⊥" and drop "three-dimensional antiferromagnetic order" for PNO. The related statement on p. 5 that the result gives "a proof of the fully occurred topotactic reduction … and the absence of interstitial oxygen atoms" is not supported by a magnon fit. It should be removed, or backed by structural or XAS evidence.

B. Should address

4. **The damping comparison and the conclusion drawn from it are overstated.** Fig. 3(b) sets PNO damping (~100 meV) against Bi2201 from Ref. 49. On that basis the text concludes that self-doping gives IL nickelates "a non-disruptive way to achieve superconductivity which is totally absent in copper oxides" (p. 4). Several issues:
   - The self-doped hole density of PNO is not quantified, so the comparison with Bi2201 at known dopings is not at matched carrier density.
   - Damping in a 5 nm reduced film can also come from structural disorder, stacking faults, residual apical oxygen and capping-layer or interface effects, independent of self-doping.
   - The Bi2201 values come from a different fitting procedure and dataset, and the paper notes that the single-peak fit overestimates damping near (0.5,0) in CCO.
   - Near Γ the PNO magnon energy (~80 meV) is below its damping (~100 meV), so describing the excitation as "sharp" (pp. 2–3) is too strong along part of the path.
   - The measurement is at 20 K, above the reported Tc (onset ~11 K), so "coexistence of superconductivity and sharp spin excitations" refers to the normal state of a superconducting film.

   The authors should give the ratio of damping to energy, discuss non-doping sources of damping, and tone the conclusion down accordingly.

5. **The agreement between calculated and measured J^orb depends on tuned parameters (Tables II–III).** For PNO:
   - all hoppings were scaled ad hoc to 80% of the CCO values;
   - Δ = 8 eV was chosen "sufficiently larger than U also providing reasonable results";
   - U = 3.2 eV and J_H come from different references.

   The agreement 14.5 against 14 ± 4 meV is presented as validation. Since Δ was picked partly by the outcome, the comparison is not independent. Please show how J_NN^orb and J_NNN^orb vary over plausible ranges of Δ (for example 4–9 eV), U and the hopping scale. The sign and magnitude of J_NN − J_NNN should be shown to be robust, not only the single point quoted. The abstract's "markedly different Δ" should also be identified as a model input, not a quantity determined here.

6. **The exchange constants rest on a five-parameter model with weakly constrained J2–J4; the comparison with the literature needs care.** For PNO, J2 = −8.3, J3 = 4.2 and J4 = 8.0 meV are fitted alongside J1 and J⊥, with uncertainties as small as ±0.3 meV on J1. In Ref. 48-type models the definitions mix ring exchange and longer-range terms, so "J1 about half of CCO" depends on the definitions. The PNO dispersion also has its [H,H] maximum at (0.2,0.2) rather than at the zone boundary (p. 5), and Fig. 3(a) does not make clear whether the LSW fit reproduces this. Please:
   - give the parameter correlation matrix, or fits with fewer parameters;
   - report a model-independent quantity, such as the zone-boundary dispersion E(0.5,0) − E(0.25,0.25) or the bandwidth, for both compounds;
   - state explicitly how the J1 values compare with earlier nickelate RIXS fits (Refs. 12, 47, 57) using the same convention.

7. **The orbital assignments and peak energies in PNO are weakly constrained.** Table I gives the PNO d_z² at 2.96 ± 0.58 eV, which is essentially undetermined. The 0.7 eV peak is assigned to RE 5d hybridization, with the alternative of interstitial s states. The single-ion calculations in Fig. 4(g–l) do not list their crystal-field parameters (10Dq, Ds, Dt or equivalent), nor whether these were fitted to the data. Please give the parameters and state how they were chosen. Please also discuss whether the 0.7 eV feature could instead be a low-lying dd or a charge-transfer/continuum contribution, since that choice affects the dxy fits in concern 2.

8. **Samples and reproducibility are not documented.** The paper does not state how many PNO and CCO films were measured. It does not say whether the dxy dispersion and the magnon fits were reproduced on more than one film, or whether the RIXS film is the one whose transport (Tc onset 11 K, zero resistance below 4 K) is quoted. The CCO film is on NGO and the PNO film is on STO with an STO cap, so substrate strain differs (in-plane a = 3.94 Å against 3.86 Å). The text attributes the dxy energy difference partly to this, but does not separate strain from intrinsic chemistry. Please give sample numbers and, ideally, a second PNO film for the dxy dispersion.

C. Minor

9. The conclusion (p. 8) states that IL nickelates lack "charge density waves … ubiquitous in hole doped cuprates", while the authors' own Ref. 45 reports charge order in NdNiO2 that depends on the capping layer. Please reconcile, or qualify the statement. The speculation that the lower Tc is "mainly due to the overall smaller energy of spin fluctuations" is not tested by the data and should be flagged as such.
10. The Fig. 5 caption says the continuous line is "the charge transfer model fitting", but the text says it is a fit of Eq. (1). Please correct the caption.
11. The text (p. 5) says CCO polarimetric spectra were taken at H = ±0.5 r.l.u., but Fig. 4(d) is labelled Q = (−0.45,0).
12. For the dxz/yz comparison, "2.05 eV for the center of mass of the two features of CCO" is quoted, while the simple mean of 1.95 and 2.29 eV is 2.12 eV. Please state the weighting.
13. Fig. 2 caption: please give the fitted magnon energy and damping for the summed spectra shown, and state the H range for CCO consistently. The fitting details are deferred to a Supplementary Information that is not available with the submission. The key constraints for Figs. 2, 3 and 5 should be summarized in the main text.
14. Fig. 3(a): the CCO points near Γ and (0.25,0.25) have large scatter and some sit well off the fit. Please comment on whether the fit is weighted by the error bars, and give reduced χ² for both fits.
15. Please give the L value at representative points along each cut (for example in the Fig. 1 or Fig. 3 caption).
16. Typos and wording: "Åand" (twice, p. 8); "out-to-in-plane"; "tridimensional"; a duplicated "ACKNOWLEDGMENTS" heading; the data-availability URL is a placeholder.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a figure giving the PNO dxy fit at three or four representative momenta, with all components shown and the fit constraints stated. Overlay the NN (cos kx + cos ky) and NNN (cos kx cos ky) dispersion forms on the extracted points for both compounds.
- Add an L scan at fixed in-plane momentum near Γ for PNO, or at least an analysis of the existing points against their computed L values, to support or replace the J⊥ claim.
- Give a sensitivity plot of J_NN^orb and J_NNN^orb against Δ and the hopping scale for the nickelate parameters.
- Compare the damping using a dimensionless measure (Γ/E) and against nickelate RIXS data at known Sr doping, which would test the self-doping argument more directly than a comparison with Bi2201.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
