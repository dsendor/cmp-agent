Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper compares Ni L3 and Cu L3 RIXS on an undoped but reportedly superconducting infinite-layer PrNiO2 film and an insulating infinite-layer CaCuO2 film, measured with the same spectrometer and analysed the same way. It extracts in-plane and out-of-plane exchange couplings, magnon damping, the energies and polarization character of the dd excitations, and the momentum dispersion of the d_xy orbital excitation. The most novel claims are a finite interlayer coupling J_perp in the nickelate, of size comparable to the cuprate's, and a d_xy orbiton dispersion whose sign is opposite to CaCuO2's. The paper attributes the opposite sign to nearest-neighbour rather than next-nearest-neighbour orbital superexchange. A side-by-side comparison of this kind is useful to the nickelate community. How much it adds beyond existing RIXS work on infinite-layer nickelates depends on how robust the J_perp and orbiton-dispersion results are.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Both compounds were measured on the same beamline (ID32), at the same temperature and scattering angle and with similar resolution (40 and 42 meV), so the comparison of magnon dispersions and dd spectra is cleaner than comparisons across different studies.
- Polarization analysis of the scattered photons (Fig. 4) on a nickelate is rare. Comparing it with single-ion cross-sections adds information on the symmetry of the dd features beyond energy fitting alone.
- The nickelate magnon is well defined across the [H,0] and [H,H] cuts (Figs. 1e, 3a), and its fitted damping is nearly independent of momentum (Fig. 3b). This is a useful dataset for undoped, self-doped infinite-layer material.
- The opposite-sign d_xy dispersion (Fig. 5) is a potentially interesting observation. Pairing it with a microscopic estimate of the nearest- and next-nearest-neighbour orbital superexchange (Tables II, III) is a sensible way to interpret it.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The paper says both that PrNiO2 has three-dimensional antiferromagnetic order and that it does not, and the J_perp extraction that supports the first statement is not constrained by the data shown. The abstract says the similar out-of-plane couplings indicate "that both materials support a three-dimensional antiferromagnetic order", and Fig. 1a draws an ordered AFM lattice for both compounds. Page 6, however, rests the orbiton interpretation on the opposite premise: "the nickelate, unlike the cuprate, does not present evidence of long-range AFM order", so the magnetic string effect "is expected to be absent or much weaker". Both statements cannot carry the argument.

   On top of this, J_perp = 5.9 +/- 1.1 meV is inferred from the magnon energy near Gamma (60-80 meV; Fig. 3a), but the Methods state that L was not fixed because 2theta was held at 149.5 deg while H was scanned. An L-scan at fixed in-plane momentum is never shown. Near Gamma, the PNO magnon sits within one or two resolution widths of a very strong elastic line and has the largest error bars in Fig. 3a. A finite energy at the in-plane zone centre could come from J_perp, from an exchange anisotropy gap (which the paper itself mentions, ref. 59), from the 40 meV resolution combined with elastic subtraction, or from short-range correlations without order. These alternatives make different predictions for the L dependence. The Methods also say the varying L "is not an issue since the in-plane Js are still dominating the out-of-plane J_perp", which contradicts using that same L variation to determine J_perp.

   This matters because the 3D-order statement is a headline claim of the abstract and the conclusions. The paper also uses the 3D character as "a proof of the fully occurred topotactic reduction" (p. 5).

   The authors should:
   - state clearly whether they claim long-range order in PNO and reconcile p. 6 with the abstract;
   - give the actual L for every point in Fig. 3a and show that the LSW fit used it;
   - ideally show an L dependence of the magnon energy at fixed small in-plane Q (for example by rotating the sample at two or more scattering angles);
   - if that is not possible, fit an anisotropy-gap model alongside the J_perp model and show that the data distinguish them;
   - otherwise reduce the claim to "consistent with a finite interlayer coupling".

2. The sign and size of the PNO d_xy dispersion, the paper's most novel result, are not shown to be robust, and the quoted bandwidths do not match the fitted J_orb. The d_xy feature in PNO is broad (Fig. 2c). It overlaps a 0.7 eV peak assigned to rare-earth states and a broad d_xz/yz peak, and it sits on a charge continuum. The relative weights of all these change with momentum because the geometry changes along the cut: grazing incidence for negative H and grazing emission for positive H, with the switch at Gamma, where PNO has its dispersion maximum. Fig. 4 shows that the d_xy polarization character in PNO is "very different" between positive and negative H, and at H = 0.4 it carries more weight than the single-ion calculation predicts. A momentum-dependent shift of spectral weight among overlapping components can therefore produce an apparent dispersion of a few tens of meV in a peak with a width of several hundred meV.

   There is also a quantitative inconsistency. With epsilon_k = 2J cos(kx)cos(ky) and J_orb = 14 meV, the predicted change from Gamma to (0.34,0.34) is about 2J(1 - 0.29), roughly 20 meV, and from Gamma to (0.475,0) about 56 meV. Fig. 5a quotes 62 meV and 90 meV for these branches. For CCO (J = -11 meV) the corresponding predictions of about 22 and 44 meV are closer to the quoted 34 and 56 meV. Either the dashed-line values in Fig. 5a do not mean what the caption says, or Eq. (1) does not describe the PNO data. In either case the agreement with the calculated J_NN = 14.5 meV (Table III) is not yet established.

   The authors should:
   - show the PNO d_xy energy from several fitting schemes: free versus fixed widths, with and without the 0.7 eV peak and the continuum, and the d_xz/yz position free versus fixed;
   - compare data taken at +H and -H, and with sigma as well as pi polarization, to show the dispersion is independent of geometry;
   - overlay Eq. (1) with the fitted J on both branches with the data, and report the residuals and any constant offset;
   - give the reduced chi-squared for a dispersionless model.

B. Should address

3. The microscopic estimate in Tables II-III has adjustable inputs, and it is not shown that the agreement with experiment is independent of how they were chosen. For PNO the hoppings were scaled to 80% of the CCO values. Delta = 8 eV was "chosen sufficiently larger than U also providing reasonable results", which reads as tuning. U = 3.2 eV and J_H = 0.65 eV were taken from the literature. The match J_NN = 14.5 meV against the experimental 14 +/- 4 meV, and the two-orders-of-magnitude suppression of J_NNN (about -0.1 meV), may depend strongly on Delta and t_pd. The authors should:
   - show how J_NN and J_NNN vary over a plausible range of Delta (for example 4-9 eV) and of the hopping scaling;
   - explain physically why J_NNN collapses in PNO.

   For CCO, the model gives J_NN = 22 meV, which is not observed. The argument that the magnon string effect suppresses it is external to the model, so the comparison in Table III is only quantitative for the PNO NN term. The text should say so.

4. The claim that self-doping leaves spin order nearly intact while chemical doping destroys it rests on a cross-material, cross-study comparison. Fig. 3b compares PNO damping with Bi2201 damping from ref. 49: a single-layer cuprate with apical oxygen, measured in a different experiment with different resolution and fitting, and hole-doped to a known p. The number of self-doped carriers in PNO is not estimated, so "much milder impact" cannot be stated per carrier. "A non-disruptive way to achieve superconductivity which is totally absent in copper oxides" also overlooks electron-doped cuprates and the persistent paramagnons in hole-doped cuprates. The authors should:
   - add an estimate of the self-doped hole density (for example from O K-edge or Ni L-edge XAS, or from Hall data on these films);
   - compare against doped infinite-layer nickelates, such as their own Nd1-xSrxNiO2 data (ref. 46), and if possible doped infinite-layer cuprate data;
   - temper the "totally absent" statement.

5. The fit of five exchange parameters to one dispersion needs justification. J1-J4 and J_perp are fitted to roughly 40 points along a single path, with a ferromagnetic J2 = -8.3 meV and J4 > J3 in PNO. The definition of J4 appears only in the Fig. 3a inset, and the ring-exchange and higher-neighbour terms are known to trade off against each other in cuprates. Please:
   - give the parameter correlation matrix, or show that simpler models (J1-J2-J3 + J_perp, or J1 + ring exchange) fit significantly worse;
   - state whether a quantum renormalization factor Z_c was applied consistently with the literature values quoted (refs. 12, 24, 46);
   - comment on the larger magnon width near (0.5,0) in CCO, which the paper says the single-peak fit misrepresents, and on whether this biases J1 for CCO.

6. Sample characterization and provenance are thin for a comparison that depends on the PNO film being superconducting and fully reduced. The Methods give the Tc of "the resulting samples" and refer to ref. 40, but they do not say:
   - whether the RIXS film is the one measured in transport;
   - how many films of each compound were measured;
   - whether the key spectra were reproduced on a second film.

   Fully completed reduction is asserted from the spin dynamics (p. 5) rather than shown directly. The authors should give the XRD and Ni L-edge XAS of the measured film in the SI and state how many samples were measured. They should also check that the elastic and "RE peak" features are not affected by residual NiO3-x or Ruddlesden-Popper-type secondary phases.

7. Epitaxial strain differs between the two films, and this is not discussed when the exchange constants are compared. PNO is on SrTiO3 with a = 3.94 Å, and CCO is on NdGaO3 with a = 3.86 Å. Strain is known to change J in infinite-layer nickelates (ref. 57). Part of the J1 ratio and the d_xy energy difference, which p. 6 partly attributes to "different ... in-plane lattice constants", may reflect the substrate. A short quantitative estimate, for example J1 proportional to a^(-n) with a typical n, would show how much of the factor of two is intrinsic.

8. The assignment and interpretation of the 0.7 eV feature and the d_xy anomaly in the polarimetric data are speculative and not consistent with the stated agreement with single-ion theory. The Introduction says polarization-resolved RIXS shows "a better agreement of the PNO orbital excitations with single-ion cross-section calculations". Yet the PNO crossed and non-crossed spectra in Fig. 4b differ visibly from the calculation in Fig. 4h: the calculation has the crossed channel dominating near 2 eV, while the data show comparable channels. The d_xy intensity at H = 0.4 is also attributed to Pr 5d or interstitial s states without a test. The authors should:
   - quantify the agreement in Fig. 4 (for example crossed/total ratios per peak, data versus calculation);
   - list the crystal-field and other single-ion parameters used;
   - either test the rare-earth hybridization hypothesis (for example through the incident-energy dependence of the 0.7 eV and d_xy features across the Ni L3 resonance) or present it as conjecture.

C. Minor

9. The phonon is said to be at "~80 meV" (p. 3), but the phonon component in Fig. 2a is centred near 55-60 meV. The incident energy is given as 852.48 eV in the Fig. 2 caption and 852.4 eV in the Methods. Please make these consistent.

10. The Fig. 5 caption calls the continuous line "the charge transfer model fitting", but the text describes it as a fit of the phenomenological Eq. (1). Please also give the Fig. 3 and Fig. 5 values (0.95 versus 0.68 confidence) and the d_z2 energy in Table I (2.96 +/- 0.58 eV) with consistent uncertainty conventions. The statement that d_z2 has "a possibly larger energy in the nickelate" is not supported by an uncertainty of 0.58 eV.

11. The incommensurate elastic intensity near (0.2,0.2) and the speculation about stripe order (p. 5) are not supported by any momentum scan of the quasi-elastic line, any temperature dependence, or any check against specular or substrate and capping-layer scattering. Either add such data or shorten this to a brief observation.

12. The closing paragraph's statements, that lower Tc comes from a lower spin-fluctuation energy and that the Mott-Hubbard character implies weaker electron-phonon coupling and no charge density waves, go beyond the data. Charge order has been reported in some infinite-layer nickelate films (ref. 45, by the same authors). These should be clearly marked as speculation or removed.

13. The Data Availability and SI references are placeholders ("URL to be inserted"). The fitting details on which items 2 and 5 depend are deferred to an SI that was not available for this review. Typographical issues: "out-to-in-plane", "Åand", "tridimensional", and a duplicated "ACKNOWLEDGMENTS" heading.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add an L-dependent magnon measurement near the in-plane zone centre in PNO, or at least an explicit table of (H, K, L) for each point, so that J_perp is constrained directly rather than through a fixed-2theta path.
- Present the d_xy dispersion analysis with a sensitivity study across fitting models and scattering geometries, plus a plot of Eq. (1) against the data on both branches, so readers can judge whether the sign reversal is robust.
- Include a sensitivity map of the calculated J_NN and J_NNN against Delta and the hopping scaling, so the agreement in Table III can be read as a test rather than a fit.
- Strengthen the damping comparison with doped infinite-layer nickelate data from the same group's previous work, measured and fitted the same way.

5. Verdict: major revision
