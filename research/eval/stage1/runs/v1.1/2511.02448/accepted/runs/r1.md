Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper compares Ni and Cu L3-edge RIXS on thin films of the nominally undoped but superconducting infinite-layer nickelate PrNiO2 (PNO) and of the infinite-layer cuprate CaCuO2 (CCO), measured on the same instrument. It covers spin waves (four in-plane couplings J1-J4 plus an interlayer J_perp), magnon damping, polarization-resolved dd excitations and, newly, the momentum dispersion of the Ni dxy orbital excitation. The most novel result is the claim that the PNO dxy orbiton disperses with the opposite sign to that in CCO and through nearest-neighbour rather than next-nearest-neighbour orbital superexchange. A Kugel-Khomskii polaronic model with a Hund's-exchange-assisted free-hopping channel supports this claim. A same-beamline comparison of this kind between the two infinite-layer families is useful to the community. The spin-wave conclusion that J1 is smaller in the nickelate is consistent with the literature. The orbital-dispersion and three-dimensionality claims are the new elements, and they need the most support.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The two families are compared directly on the same spectrometer, at about 40 meV resolution, the same temperature and the same momentum paths. This removes many cross-study systematics.
- The magnon data are dense in momentum along three high-symmetry directions. Damping is given with confidence intervals and set against published Bi2201 values (Fig. 3).
- Polarimetric RIXS on both compounds (Fig. 4) is set side by side with single-ion cross-section calculations. This gives an orbital assignment that goes beyond energy position alone.
- A microscopic model (Tables II-III, SM section I) gives a nearest-neighbour orbiton hopping of t1^orb ≈ 13.7 meV, close to the fitted 12.5 ± 3.8 meV. It also supplies a physical mechanism, Hund's-exchange-assisted orbital-spin flip, for orbiton motion that does not disturb the antiferromagnetic background.
- The supplementary material is open about the fitting choices: the low-H phonon removal, the two-peak against three-peak dd fits, the parameter cross-correlations and the J_perp scan. The data are deposited on Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The opposite-sign dxy dispersion in PNO has not been shown to survive changes in the fit model or in the scattering geometry (Fig. 5a-b, Table I, Eq. 1, abstract). The reported PNO dispersion is 62-90 meV. It is extracted from a dxy peak with a width of several hundred meV (Fig. 2c, Fig. 7). That peak overlaps strongly with a very broad dxz/yz component (2.96 ± 0.58 eV for dz2 shows how loosely the high-energy part is constrained) and with the 0.7 eV "RE" feature. The text says the RE feature "becomes progressively enhanced towards the Brillouin zone edge", so its weight changes along the same path on which the dxy centre is tracked. Two further effects can shift the apparent centre of an unresolved multi-component lineshape without any true dispersion:
   - The incident and emission angles change along each momentum cut at fixed 2θ = 149.5°, which changes the single-ion cross-sections of dxy relative to dxz/yz.
   - The polarimetry in Fig. 4 and Fig. 12 shows that the dxy feature in PNO has a mixed, geometry-dependent polarization character that the single-ion model does not reproduce.

   The shift is only about one fifth of the linewidth, and the two branches give different amplitudes (62 against 90 meV). The headline claim therefore needs explicit robustness tests. The authors should:
   - refit the full momentum series with and without the RE component, with dxy and dxz/yz widths fixed against free, and with the two-peak model of Fig. 7, and show that the sign and the approximate size of the dxy dispersion survive each variant;
   - give a model-independent measure, such as the first moment or the peak maximum of the raw spectra in a fixed window around 1.3 eV, plotted against Q;
   - use the single-ion cross-sections already calculated for Fig. 4(g-l) to estimate how much the geometry-induced change in the dxy/dxz,yz weight ratio alone shifts the fitted dxy centre along the path;
   - ideally, show that the dispersion is the same at the two equivalent momenta ±H measured in grazing incidence and grazing emission, since a geometry artifact would not be symmetric in this way.

   Without these tests, the "opposite dispersion" in the abstract, and the theory comparison built on it (Table III), may rest on a fitting artifact.

2. The three-dimensional antiferromagnetic correlations in PNO (abstract; Results, page 5; Conclusions) rest on a parameter that was fixed rather than fitted, at the momentum where the data are least certain. J_perp = 5.9 meV for PNO was fixed, and the only evidence for it is the finite magnon energy near Γ (Fig. 13; SM section F). Three things weaken this evidence:
   - Near Γ the fitted magnon energies have 95% confidence intervals of tens to about 100 meV (Fig. 10b, Fig. 13). The magnon there is a weak shoulder on a specular elastic peak much larger than itself (Fig. 9), and the phonon was removed from the fit for Q < 0.175 r.l.u.
   - The Methods state that L was not fixed in the in-plane scans. The L values at the points near Γ, which set the interlayer contribution to the gap, are not reported.
   - A gap at Γ can equally come from exchange anisotropy, as the authors themselves note for ref. 65, or from finite-L dispersion in a quasi-2D system with a much smaller J_perp. An LSW fit with J_perp = 0 and an easy-plane or Ising anisotropy term is not shown.

   The conclusion that "both materials support three-dimensional antiferromagnetic correlations" and the sentence "our results give a proof of the fully occurred topotactic reduction ... and the absence of interstitial oxygen atoms" therefore go beyond the evidence. The authors should report the L value of each spectrum and fit J_perp as a free parameter with its uncertainty. They should test an anisotropy-gap model against the J_perp model on the same data. Preferably they should measure the L dependence of the magnon energy at fixed small in-plane Q by rotating the sample at fixed 2θ, which is the direct test of interlayer coupling. If that is not possible, the abstract and conclusions should be restated as a gap compatible with, but not proof of, an interlayer coupling, and the topotactic-reduction "proof" should be removed.

B. Should address

3. The theoretical orbiton hopping depends sharply on parameters that were chosen rather than determined (Table II, Methods, SM section I), and this sensitivity is not shown. t1^orb = (j_HS − j_LS)/2 · J̄1^orb with j_HS = 1/(1 − 3J_H/U) is strongly nonlinear in J_H/U. Taking J_H = 1.0 eV in place of 1.2 eV at U = 6.5 eV lowers (j_HS − j_LS)/2 from about 0.50 to about 0.34, and J̄1^orb changes with U at the same time. The inputs come from different sources:
   - the hoppings are 80% of the CCO values, with no independent justification;
   - Δ is taken from DFT on LaNiO2;
   - U is taken from the trilayer La4Ni3O8, which has a different Ni valence;
   - J_H is set close to the atomic value on nephelauxetic arguments.

   The SM (section H) also states that "our estimate of the Mott energy U is exclusively based on the orbital dispersion". This contradicts the Methods, where U is taken from ref. 29. If U was in fact adjusted to fit the dispersion, then the agreement between theory and experiment in Table III is a fit, not a prediction. The authors should say which parameters were tuned, and give t1^orb (and J1^orb, J2^orb) over plausible ranges of U (for example 5-8 eV), J_H (0.8-1.3 eV) and the hopping scale. They should also remove the contradiction between the Methods and SM section H. In addition, the fitted t3^orb = 5.4 ± 1.7 meV is statistically significant and about half of t1. Neglecting it in the model weakens the claim of "good agreement", and this should be stated as a limitation.

4. The magnon-damping comparison does not support the inference that "self-doping has a much milder impact on spin order than chemical doping" without qualification (Fig. 3b, page 4). PNO is compared with Bi2201, a different structural family, fitted in another study. The PNO broadening (about 80-100 meV) can also come from sources other than doping:
   - film inhomogeneity in a 5 nm topotactically reduced film;
   - unresolved multimagnon weight;
   - the unpolarized continuum the authors themselves see in Fig. 4(c);
   - the summation over several momenta used for the fitted spectra (Fig. 2a caption, H = 0.4-0.475).

   The amount of self-doping in PNO is not estimated. A more relevant comparison would be with Sr-doped infinite-layer nickelates measured by RIXS (for example ref. 12 and ref. 51 data). The authors should name the PNO broadening mechanisms they cannot exclude and, if possible, compare with doped (Nd,Sr)NiO2 or (Pr,Sr)NiO2 damping extracted with the same damped-harmonic-oscillator model. They should present the self-doping interpretation as one possibility.

5. The PNO linear-spin-wave fit is poorly constrained and misses part of the data (Fig. 3a, Fig. 13). Four free in-plane couplings plus a fixed J_perp are fitted to a single dispersion with large uncertainties near Γ. The fit lies systematically above the data near (0.25, 0.25), by roughly 15-30 meV in Fig. 13. The text also notes that the PNO dispersion maximum along (H,H) sits at about (0.2, 0.2), not at the zone boundary, which a commensurate Néel LSW model cannot produce. The resulting J3 = 4.2 and J4 = 8.0 meV (with J4 > J3) may absorb this misfit and the correlations between couplings, rather than reflect real longer-range exchange. The authors should:
   - give the parameter covariance or correlation matrix for the J fit;
   - show a reduced model (J1, J2 plus an effective ring exchange, or J1-J2-J3) for comparison;
   - comment on what the misfit near (0.25, 0.25) and the off-boundary maximum imply.

   For the comparison with literature values (around 64 meV in NdNiO2 from ref. 12), an effective J derived from the zone-boundary energies would make "in agreement with the literature" quantitative.

6. Sample characterisation for the film measured by RIXS is unclear (Methods; title). The paper does not say how many PNO films were measured, or whether the RIXS film itself showed the superconducting transition (Tc onset about 11 K, zero resistance below 4 K) or whether those numbers come from a sister sample in ref. 42. This matters because "superconducting" is in the title and the coexistence of sharp magnons with superconductivity is a stated conclusion. The authors should state the number of films, give transport for the measured film (or say it is a sister film), and say whether the key magnon and dd results were reproduced on more than one film.

7. The strain argument for an intrinsic J1 difference is internally inconsistent (pages 5-6). The text states that compressive strain generally reduces exchange, and then concludes that "strain would tend to increase J1 in PNO", which is under compression. The "+0.9%" is also labelled compressive, although (3.905 − 3.94)/3.94 is about −0.9%. Finally, a_PNO = 3.94 Å is given as the measured film lattice parameter (Methods). If the film is relaxed to 3.94 Å, it is not strained to STO, and the strain argument changes accordingly. The authors should give the measured in-plane lattice parameter of the reduced film (for example from reciprocal-space maps), correct the sign convention and the direction of the expected effect, and keep the order-of-magnitude argument, which on its own probably suffices.

8. The concluding statements go well beyond what was measured (page 9). Examples are "IL nickelates are indeed mimicking the essential physics of cuprates, including the mechanisms for superconducting pairing" and "the lower Tc ... is mainly due to the overall smaller energy of spin fluctuations". So is the inference of a smaller electron-phonon interaction and of the absence of charge-density waves from a Mott-Hubbard character. None of these is tested by the data. These should be rephrased as speculation or removed.

C. Minor

9. Figure 6 / SM section A: the caption energy window reads "[−0.1; 0−1] eV" while the panel says (−0.1; 0.1) eV; please correct. The search for charge order is done only at the in-plane momenta of one scan, with L varying along it. Stating the L range and the incident energy (on or off the resonance where ref. 57 observed the peak) would make the null result more convincing. The error bars come from counting statistics only.

10. Fig. 2a fits a spectrum summed over H = 0.4-0.475 r.l.u., where the magnon still disperses by tens of meV. This broadens the magnon and may bias the damping shown as representative. State whether the damping in Fig. 3b comes from single-momentum fits.

11. In Fig. 5a,c the annotated extrema (62/90 meV for PNO, 61/70 meV for CCO) do not obviously match the bandwidths implied by the fitted hoppings. For Eq. 1, E(Γ) − E(X) = 4t1 + 6t2 ≈ 51 meV; for Eq. 2, 4|t2| ≈ 54 meV. Please explain how the annotated values were obtained (data or fit), and why the two PNO branches differ.

12. The dxy peak in PNO is assigned a mixed polarization, explained by proximity to the 0.7 eV peak or by Pr 5d / interstitial s hybridization (page 6). These are reasonable hypotheses. A calculation that adds a rare-earth-hybridized component, or a statement of how large an admixture would reproduce Fig. 4(c), would make the argument testable.

13. The orbital cross-correlation analysis (Eq. 5, Figs. 8 and 11) uses a shift index n over concatenated momenta. This is unusual and hard to read. A standard parameter correlation matrix from the fit covariance would be clearer.

14. Typos and wording: "Superconductive IL phase", "ar rather large value" (SM page 19), "decribes" (SM page 17), "intra-unit-cell coupling of bi-layer cuprates like YBCO" (please give the ratio being compared), and "the fully occurred topotactic reaction".

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a supplementary figure showing the PNO dxy centre against Q for several fit models, with a model-free first-moment analysis and the geometry-induced shift predicted by the single-ion cross-sections. This would settle concern 1 directly.
2. Measure, or if already available report, the L dependence of the low-Q magnon in PNO at fixed in-plane momentum, and compare a J_perp model with an anisotropy-gap model.
3. Give a sensitivity map of t1^orb against U and J_H (for example a two-dimensional colour plot with the experimental band 12.5 ± 3.8 meV overlaid). This would show how much of parameter space reproduces the observed dispersion.
4. Compare the magnon damping with hole-doped infinite-layer nickelates analysed with the same model, so that the self-doping and chemical-doping comparison is made within one family.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
