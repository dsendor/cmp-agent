Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper compares Ni and Cu L3-edge RIXS on a nominally undoped but superconducting infinite-layer PrNiO2 film and on insulating CaCuO2, measured with the same spectrometer, and extends the analysis to polarization-resolved dd excitations and orbiton dispersion. The most novel results are (i) a dispersing Ni dxy orbital excitation whose dispersion is opposite to that of Cu dxy, interpreted with a spin-orbital model as nearest-neighbour orbiton hopping enabled by Hund's exchange, and (ii) a spin-wave analysis that includes an interlayer coupling J⊥, giving three-dimensional antiferromagnetic correlations in the nickelate. The side-by-side dataset is valuable. However, the headline orbiton and J⊥ results rest on small effects extracted from broad, overlapping features, and their robustness is not yet demonstrated.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The two infinite-layer compounds are compared directly, with matched instrumentation, temperature and analysis, so differences in J1, damping and dd energies can be read with fewer systematic caveats than in cross-paper comparisons.
- The magnon dispersion of PNO is measured along M–Γ–X–M with good statistics, and the fit parameters with 95% confidence intervals are given (Fig. 3a).
- Polarimetric RIXS of the dd region in a superconducting nickelate is new, and it is compared with single-ion cross-section calculations under matched geometries (Fig. 4).
- The orbiton dispersion is backed by a microscopic theory (Table III, SM Sec. I) that gives a quantitative t1^orb comparable to experiment and explains why CCO and PNO differ.
- Useful supporting material: the absence of quasi-elastic intensity at Q ≈ (1/3,0) (Fig. 6), low-Q fits (Fig. 9), cross-correlation of fit parameters (Figs. 8, 11), and open data on Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The opposite-sign dxy orbiton dispersion in PNO is not yet shown to be robust against the multi-peak fitting. In PNO the dxy feature at 1.29 eV is broad (Gaussian FWHM of several hundred meV; Fig. 2c, Fig. 7). It overlaps both the 0.7 eV "RE peak", whose intensity grows strongly toward the zone edge (p. 3), and the very broad dxz/yz component. The claimed dispersion is only 62–90 meV (Fig. 5a), a fraction of the linewidth, and the map in Fig. 5a shows little visible curvature by eye. A momentum-dependent change in the weight of the 0.7 eV peak or of dxz/yz, or the change of incidence angle along the cut (self-absorption and polarization factors change the relative weight of the dd components, Fig. 4), could shift the fitted dxy centroid by tens of meV without any real orbiton motion. The cross-correlation analysis in the SM (Fig. 8) covers only dxz/yz and dz2, not dxy against the RE peak or against dxz/yz, even though the latter is exactly the correlation that matters here. The 95% confidence intervals in Fig. 5a are statistical fit errors and do not include this model dependence. The fitted t3^orb = 5.4 ± 1.7 meV, about half of t1^orb, also suggests the functional form of Eq. (1) is being stretched to follow the data. Because the abstract and the theory section are built on this dispersion, the authors should: (a) show a model-independent measure of the peak position versus Q, such as the first moment or the maximum of the raw spectrum in a window around 1.1–1.5 eV, or the energy of the steepest leading edge; (b) repeat the fit with the RE-peak and dxz/yz widths and energies fixed across Q, and with the RE peak removed or replaced by a broad background, and show that the sign and size of the dxy dispersion survive; (c) give the dxy–RE and dxy–dxz/yz cross-correlations; (d) show that the dispersion is the same along [H,0] and [H,H] and symmetric for ±H where data exist, which also rules out a geometric (incidence-angle) origin; (e) if possible, show the same dispersion in a second PNO sample.

2. The out-of-plane coupling J⊥ in PNO, and the claims that rest on it, are not constrained by the data. J⊥ for PNO was fixed at 5.9 meV rather than fitted (Fig. 3a; SM Sec. F). The only evidence for a nonzero value is the magnon energy at the three or four points closest to Γ. There the magnon sits on the specular elastic tail, its energy is comparable to the 40 meV resolution, and its fitted error bars are ±20–50 meV (Fig. 10b, Fig. 13). In addition:
   - L is not fixed along the cuts (Methods, p. 10). The value of L at the Γ-adjacent points is not stated, so the L-dependent J⊥ gap cannot be checked.
   - Other origins of a finite apparent gap are not considered: easy-plane or exchange anisotropy, which the authors themselves note was used in earlier work (Ref. 65); the damping of an overdamped paramagnon, since the damped-harmonic-oscillator pole shifts when damping (~100 meV) is comparable to the energy; and the phonon being removed from the fit only below Q = 0.175 r.l.u.
   - The LSW framework presupposes long-range order, whereas the authors describe PNO as lacking long-range order with spin-glass behaviour.

   Despite this, the abstract states that "the out-of-plane values are similar, indicating that both materials support three-dimensional antiferromagnetic correlations". The conclusions say "We can safely state that the exchange interaction between different planes clearly confers a certain degree of tridimensionality". Page 5 says the results "give a proof of the fully occurred topotactic reduction of the PNO samples and the absence of interstitial oxygen atoms". These statements go beyond what a fixed parameter with a stated range of 0–10 meV can support. The authors should give the L values along the cuts. They should fit J⊥ as a free parameter with its confidence interval, and also fit a model with a single-ion or exchange anisotropy gap and J⊥ = 0, and report whether the data can distinguish them. Unless one of these fits gives a significant lower bound on J⊥, they should rephrase the abstract and conclusions as consistent with a small interlayer coupling and drop the "proof" of complete topotactic reduction. A direct L-scan at fixed in-plane Q near Γ would settle the issue.

B. Should address

3. The theoretical t1^orb agreement depends on parameters chosen rather than determined, and its sensitivity is not shown. Table II takes the PNO hoppings as 80% of the CCO values, Δ = 4.4 eV from DFT, U = 6.5 eV from a trilayer nickelate with a different Ni valence, and J_H = 1.2 eV from a reduced atomic value. t1^orb scales as (j_HS − j_LS) J1^orb and depends steeply on J_H/U and on Δ, through J1^orb ∝ t^4/Δ^2-type terms. Agreement within the 30% experimental uncertainty (12.5 ± 3.8 meV vs 13.7 meV) is therefore only meaningful if it holds over a plausible parameter range. The authors should show t1^orb and J2^orb as functions of Δ, U and J_H over reasonable ranges (for example Δ 2–5 eV, U 5–8 eV, J_H 0.8–1.2 eV). They should also say whether the sign and NN dominance survive across that range. That conclusion is the robust part of the claim, rather than the numerical match.

4. Several stated inputs and derived quantities for the orbiton model conflict with each other.
   - SM Sec. H states that "our estimate of the Mott energy U value is exclusively based on the orbital dispersion", whereas Methods says U was taken from the trilayer nickelate La4Ni3O8. If U was tuned to reproduce the observed dispersion, then the "good agreement" in Table III is partly a fit, not a prediction.
   - In SM Sec. I, J̄1^orb = U J1^orb/(U − 2J_H) with J1^orb = 21.6 meV, U = 6.5 eV and J_H = 1.2 eV gives ≈34 meV, not the quoted ≈27 meV. The quoted t1^orb = 13.7 meV follows from 27 meV. Either the formula or the number needs correcting, and the effect on t1^orb should be stated.
   - The model keeps only NN processes, while experiment gives t3^orb ≈ 0.4 t1^orb. This should be acknowledged when the agreement is claimed.

5. The interpretation of the PNO magnon damping as evidence that "self-doping has a much milder impact on spin order than chemical doping" (p. 4) compares a ~5 nm capped thin film with Bi2201 data from another paper, analysed with a different fit. The ~100 meV damping in PNO could equally come from the spin-glass state, film inhomogeneity (partial reduction, secondary phases, interface layers), or the multi-magnon continuum near the zone boundary. The paper notes the last of these overestimates damping in CCO near (0.5,0). The claim should be scoped as a comparison of line widths. The authors should discuss these alternatives and state the self-doping level (or an estimate of it) the comparison assumes. A Q-independent damping is also worth discussing, since it differs from the Q-dependent damping in the doped cuprates.

6. The assignment of the 0.7 eV peak to Ni 3d–Pr 5d hybridization is adopted, but the authors concede an extrinsic origin cannot be ruled out, since the peak is stronger in samples with impurity-phase diffraction (Ref. 49). That assignment then feeds the explanations of the mixed polarization of the dxy peak and its excess intensity versus the single-ion calculation (p. 6), and it affects the dxy fit (concern 1). The authors should report x-ray diffraction or other phase-purity checks for this specific sample. They should also either compare the 0.7 eV intensity with a sample of known impurity content or present the dxy-polarization discussion as conditional on that assignment.

7. Only one PNO film (and one CCO film, re-analysed from earlier work) appears to have been measured. The paper does not state whether any of the key results (J1, damping, dxy energy and dispersion, polarization character) were reproduced on a second film or after thermal cycling, or whether the beam caused any time-dependent change in these reduced, metastable films. A statement on sample number and reproducibility should be added. Comparison with the earlier PNO RIXS data (Refs. 12, 47, 52) for J1 and the 0.7 eV feature would also help.

8. The magnon fit for PNO uses four in-plane couplings plus J⊥ (fixed), on a dispersion that does not reach X (the scan stops at 0.475,0) and shows no data between (0,0) and about 0.12 r.l.u. along [H,0]. The parameter correlations between J1, J2, J3 and J4 are not reported. J4 (8.0 meV) is larger than J3 (4.2 meV) in PNO, which is unusual and may signal degeneracy. The statement that J1 = 46 meV is "in agreement with the literature" is also not quantified; earlier RIXS on NdNiO2 reported J1 ≈ 64 meV. The authors should give the parameter covariance or show that a simpler J1–J2(–J3) model, or a fit with the Hubbard-model constraint between J's, describes the data equally well. They should also compare quantitatively with published infinite-layer values.

9. The abstract's final sentence attributes to PNO a larger Δ, smaller spin-fluctuation energy and stronger localization of doped charge. Of these, the paper measures only the smaller J1 and the lower dd energies; Δ is taken from DFT (Table II). The concluding speculation on electron-phonon coupling and the absence of CDWs is not tested. These statements should be labelled as interpretation, or linked to a specific measured quantity.

C. Minor

10. The strain discussion (p. 5–6) calls the PNO strain "compressive" with "+0.9%" and the CCO strain "tensile" with "−0.1%". The sign convention is reversed from the usual one. The capping STO layer and possible partial relaxation in a 5 nm film are not discussed, and no reciprocal-space map confirming coherent strain is shown.

11. The Fig. 6 caption gives the integration range as "[−0.1;0−1] eV", while the panel says (−0.1; 0.1) eV. Eq. (1) has "cos(2πky)" without the subscript. The SM refers to the "RE hybridization peak at 0.6 eV", while the main text says 0.7 eV.

12. Table I gives dz2 = 2.96 ± 0.58 eV for PNO. With this uncertainty, the text's statement that dz2 lies at "slightly larger energy than in the cuprate" (2.68 eV) is not supported and should be softened.

13. Fig. 2(a) is a sum of spectra between H = 0.4 and 0.475, while Fig. 2(b) is a single spectrum at H = 0.465. The comparison of relative magnon and elastic intensities between the two panels should note this. The normalization to the dd integral (1–3.5 eV, Fig. 1 caption) also includes the 0.7 eV peak in PNO.

14. The incommensurate maximum of the [H,H] dispersion at (0.2,0.2) and the elastic increase beyond the BZ boundary (p. 6) are mentioned but not analysed. Either give a brief quantitative account or move them to the SM.

15. The temperature, 20 K, is above Tc (4 K onset, near 11 K). This is fine for the purpose, but it should be stated explicitly when calling the sample "superconducting" in the context of spin excitations.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a single figure in the main text that shows the dxy dispersion in PNO extracted three ways (constrained fit, free fit, model-free first moment), for both [H,0] and [H,H].
- Add an L-dependent measurement, or at minimum state L at each in-plane point. Fit J⊥ freely and compare it with an anisotropy-gap model, then scope the 3D-correlation statement to what this supports.
- Add a parameter-sensitivity panel for the orbiton theory (t1^orb versus J_H/U and Δ), and reconcile the U provenance and the J̄1^orb formula.
- Report the PNO sample's structural characterization and the number of films measured, and compare the extracted J1 and the 0.7 eV feature with previous RIXS on other RNiO2 films.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
