Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper compares Ni and Cu L3-edge RIXS on two infinite-layer films measured on the same spectrometer: a nominally undoped but superconducting PrNiO2 (PNO) film and an insulating CaCuO2 (CCO) film. It covers the magnon dispersion and damping, polarization-resolved dd excitations, and the momentum dispersion of the dxy orbital excitation. The new results are three. First, a linear spin-wave fit that includes an interlayer coupling J⊥ for the nickelate. Second, polarimetric dd spectra of a nickelate compared with single-ion calculations. Third, and most notable, the report that the dxy orbiton in PNO disperses opposite to that in CCO, which the authors explain with a spin-orbital (Kugel-Khomskii) model in which nearest-neighbour orbital superexchange and Hund's coupling allow free orbiton hopping. A side-by-side comparison of a nickelate and its parent cuprate analogue is useful to the community. The orbiton result would be of broad interest if it proves robust.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Both materials were measured with the same instrument at comparable resolution (40 and 42 meV), along the same Brillouin-zone paths, so the J1 comparison (46 vs 82 meV) is direct and agrees with earlier reports.
- The polarization-resolved dd spectra of a superconducting infinite-layer nickelate are new, and the comparison with single-ion cross-sections (Fig. 4) is a sensible framework.
- The orbiton-dispersion analysis connects experiment to a microscopic mechanism. Table III compares experimental and theoretical hopping amplitudes quantitatively, and the Supplement gives the derivation of the spin-orbital model in reasonable detail.
- The Supplement shows a good share of the fitting checks: the low-Q fits (Fig. 9), parameter trends (Fig. 10), cross-correlations (Figs. 8 and 11), the two-peak alternative (Fig. 7) and J⊥ sensitivity (Fig. 13). The data are deposited publicly.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Robustness of the "opposite" dxy orbiton dispersion in PNO (Fig. 5(a,b), Eq. 1, Table III; abstract).
   - **The problem.** The headline orbital result is a dispersion of 62–90 meV, with the maximum at Γ. It sits on a dxy feature whose width is several hundred meV in Fig. 2(c), which lies between two other features:
     - the 0.7 eV "RE peak", which the text says "becomes progressively enhanced towards the Brillouin zone edge" and may be extrinsic (impurity phases, ref. 49);
     - a very broad dxz/yz feature, with cross-correlated fit parameters (Fig. 8).
   - **Why it matters.** As the 0.7 eV peak grows towards the zone edges, its high-energy tail, or a mis-modelled lineshape, would pull the fitted dxy energy down at exactly the momenta where the paper reports the dxy minimum. That would mimic the reported dispersion.
   - **Other sources of apparent dispersion.** The paper itself reports two:
     - the dxy peak has a mixed crossed/parallel polarization character in PNO that changes with geometry (Fig. 4(a–c)), so the weight of its sub-components can shift with the scattering angle;
     - L is not fixed along the cuts (Methods).
   - **What is missing.** The cross-correlation analysis in Fig. 8 covers only the dxz/yz and dz2 parameters, not the dxy energy against the RE-peak intensity and energy.
   - **Requested.** Please show that the dxy dispersion survives each of the following:
     - (i) different treatments of the 0.7 eV feature: fixed width, free width, or a different lineshape;
     - (ii) a model-independent measure, such as the first moment of the spectrum over a fixed window around 1.3 eV or the position of the maximum of the raw spectra, versus Q;
     - (iii) a cross-correlation of the dxy energy with the RE-peak and dxz/yz parameters.
   - **If possible.** Show the dispersion for σ and π polarization separately, or in more than one scattering geometry or sample.
   - **Because the Δ ≈ 60–90 meV effect is a fraction of the linewidth**, the claim needs this level of support before the theoretical interpretation can be built on it.

2. The claim that the out-of-plane exchange is "similar" and that PNO has three-dimensional AFM correlations (abstract; Fig. 3(a); Supplement F, Fig. 13).
   - **How J⊥ was obtained.** J⊥ = 5.9 meV for PNO was not fitted; it was fixed. Its only constraint is the magnon energy at the few points closest to Γ, which in Fig. 3(a) and Fig. 10(b) carry error bars of roughly ±50 meV. In that region the magnon merges with the specular elastic line, and the phonon was removed from the model by hand (Supplement D).
   - **The paper's own statements conflict.**
     - Supplement F concludes that J⊥ lies "between 0 and 10 meV". This range includes zero, yet the same section says J⊥ = 0 "sensibly worsens the fitting".
     - The main text uses the same parameter to claim "comparable" out-of-plane coupling, 3D correlations and even "a proof of the fully occurred topotactic reduction ... and the absence of interstitial oxygen atoms".
   - **Unexamined alternatives.**
     - An apparent gap at in-plane Γ could also come from exchange or single-ion anisotropy (mentioned via ref. 65 but not modelled).
     - It could also come from the dependence of the damped-harmonic-oscillator peak energy on damping when the damping (~100 meV) exceeds the mode energy.
     - It could come from the uncontrolled L: the Methods state that L varies along the cuts because 2θ is fixed, while J⊥ enters precisely through the L dependence.
   - **Requested.** Please state whether the SpinW fit used the actual L of each point. Show the quality of fit with a free J⊥ and its confidence interval, and compare a J⊥ model with an anisotropy-gap model.
   - **Better still.** Measure the magnon near Γ at two or more L values, for example by changing the incident energy across the L3 resonance width or by using a different scattering angle. In a 3D-coupled system the Γ-point energy should depend on L; with an anisotropy gap it should not.
   - **Otherwise,** reduce the abstract and conclusion to "the data are consistent with a finite J⊥ of up to ~10 meV". Remove the inference about topotactic reduction and interstitial oxygen.

B. Should address

3. Independence of the theoretical orbiton hopping from the data (Table II, Table III, Supplement H and I).
   - **Possible tuning of U.** The Methods state that U = 6.5 eV was taken from La4Ni3O8 (ref. 29), but Supplement H states that "our estimate of the Mott energy U value is exclusively based on the orbital dispersion". If U was adjusted to reproduce the orbiton bandwidth, the "good agreement" between t1orb = 13.7 meV (theory) and 12.5 ± 3.8 meV (experiment) is partly built in.
   - **Sensitivity to other inputs.** The prediction is very sensitive to the parameters, because t1orb = (jHS − jLS)/2 · J̄1orb with jHS = 1/(1 − 3JH/U):
     - for JH = 1.2 eV and U = 6.5 eV, jHS − jLS ≈ 1.0;
     - for the CCO-like values JH = 1 eV and U = 8 eV it is ≈ 0.46, roughly halving t1orb.
     - Further inputs are the hoppings, set to 80% of the CCO values, and JH, which is argued from a reduced nephelauxetic effect.
   - **Requested.**
     - Clarify how U was chosen.
     - Give the sensitivity of t1orb and J2orb to U, JH and Δ over plausible ranges.
     - Check whether the same three-band parameter set reproduces the measured spin exchange J1 = 46 meV. That would be an independent consistency test.
     - Explain the difference between "Theory J1orb = 21.6 meV" in Table III and "J̄1orb ≈ 27 meV" in Supplement I.
     - Say whether the sign of t1orb is a prediction or an input.
     - Comment on the experimentally significant t3orb = 5.4 ± 1.7 meV, which the theory does not address.

4. The strain argument is internally inconsistent (pp. 5–6).
   - **The contradiction.** The text says compressive strain "generally reduc[es]" the exchange, and that PNO on STO is under compressive strain. It then says that "strain would tend to increase J1 in PNO", which contradicts the previous sentence. Logically, the stated trend would reduce J1 in PNO and so partly account for the observed difference rather than oppose it.
   - **Sign conventions.** These are reversed as well: a "compressive strain of about +0.9%" and a "tensile strain of about −0.1%".
   - **Is the film strained at all?** The Methods give a = 3.94 Å for the PNO film, which equals the bulk-like value used to compute the mismatch. If this is the measured in-plane parameter of the film, the film is relaxed and the strain argument does not apply.
   - **Requested.** State the measured in-plane lattice parameter of the film (from reciprocal-space maps), fix the logic and signs, and quote the expected size of a strain-induced change in J1 from refs. 68–71. Only then can the claim that the J1 reduction "must be intrinsic" stand.

5. Interpretation of the magnon damping (Fig. 3(b), p. 4).
   - **What the paper claims.** The paper attributes the ~80–100 meV damping in PNO to self-doping and concludes that self-doping has a "much milder impact on spin order than chemical doping", endowing nickelates with "a non-disruptive way to achieve superconductivity".
   - **The comparison is indirect.**
     - It uses Bi2201 values taken from another work (ref. 54), obtained with a different resolution and fitting procedure.
     - The self-doping level of PNO is not quantified.
     - Extrinsic broadening in a 5 nm, capped, topotactically reduced film is not separated out. Possible sources are disorder, inhomogeneous reduction, the spin-glass behaviour (refs. 13, 40) and L variation.
   - **Requested.**
     - Estimate the effective hole concentration from self-doping (Hall data or literature).
     - Compare with damping in hole-doped infinite-layer nickelates or doped CCO measured in a similar way (for example ref. 51 from the same group).
     - Discuss the extrinsic contributions.
     - Otherwise, tone down the conclusion.

6. Polarization analysis of the dxy peak and its interpretation as Pr 5d hybridization (Fig. 4(a–c), Fig. 12, p. 6).
   - **What the paper claims.** The mixed crossed/parallel character of the 1.29 eV peak at H > 0 is read as a sign of Pr 5d (or interstitial-s) hybridization.
   - **What is missing.** The PNO polarimetric spectra are noisy, and the paper does not describe:
     - how the polarimeter efficiency was corrected;
     - how the unpolarized continuum and the 0.7 eV peak were subtracted;
     - how the overlap with the broad dxz/yz peak was handled.
   - **The alternative is not excluded.** The authors themselves raise the overlap with the 0.7 eV peak as an alternative, and it would explain the discrepancy without any hybridization.
   - **Requested.** Give the polarimeter calibration and the crossed and parallel fractions of each fitted component with uncertainties. Show whether the anomaly persists when the 0.7 eV component is fitted separately in each polarization channel. Present the hybridization interpretation as one possibility among others.

7. Samples and reproducibility.
   - **What is not stated.** The number of PNO films measured is not given. Nor is it said whether the key results (J1, damping, the dxy dispersion, the polarization anomaly) were reproduced on a second film or after thermal cycling.
   - **Characterization.** No structural or transport data for the measured film are shown; the reader is referred to ref. 42.
   - **Mixed data sources.** The CCO data are a re-analysis of earlier datasets (refs. 45, 55), possibly from different beamtimes.
   - **Requested.** State the number of samples, show the resistivity and XRD of the measured film (in the Supplement is fine), and indicate which CCO spectra come from which experiment.

8. Spin-wave fit uniqueness and quality (Fig. 3(a), Fig. 13).
   - **Parameters.** Five exchange constants are fitted, with J4 = 8.0 meV (17% of J1) and a ferromagnetic J2 = −8.3 meV. The confidence intervals are very small (J1 = 45.8 ± 0.3 meV), and no parameter correlations are shown.
   - **Fit quality.** Along (H,H) the PNO data near (0.25,0.25) fall noticeably below the fit, around 130 vs ~150 meV. The text also notes that the dispersion maximum is at (0.2,0.2) and that there is an unexplained elastic increase beyond the zone boundary.
   - **Requested.**
     - Show fits with fewer parameters, such as J1–J2–J3 or J1 plus a ring exchange, and their χ².
     - Give the correlation matrix.
     - Discuss whether the (H,H) deviation, possibly linked to the incommensurate elastic feature, biases J1 and J⊥.

9. Speculative conclusions in the abstract and summary.
   - **What the data do not support.** Several statements go beyond what was measured:
     - the lower Tc stems from smaller spin-fluctuation energy;
     - the Mott-Hubbard character implies a smaller electron-phonon interaction and no CDW;
     - nickelates "mimic the essential physics of cuprates, including the mechanisms for superconducting pairing";
     - the "larger charge-transfer energy Δ", in the abstract, which is a DFT input (Δ = 4.4 eV, ref. 6), not a quantity measured here.
   - **Requested.** Label these as speculation or remove them from the abstract.

C. Minor

10. Charge-order statement (Supplement A, Fig. 6). The quasielastic integral was measured along a single (H,0) cut at uncontrolled L, in a capped film; ref. 50 shows that capping itself suppresses CO. The conclusion "confirming ... the fully occurred topotactic reaction" does not follow. Absence of CO at this L is all the data show. Also fix the caption's energy range "[−0.1; 0−1] eV".

11. The dz2 energy in PNO is 2.96 ± 0.58 eV (Table I), and Fig. 7 shows the spectrum can be fitted without this peak. The statement that it lies at "slightly larger energy than in the cuprate" (2.68 eV) is not supported within the error bar.

12. Single-ion calculations (Supplement G).
    - The spin direction (in-plane, 45° from a) is assumed for PNO, which has no established long-range order. Show how sensitive the results are to this choice.
    - The Lorentzian widths, and for CCO the separate nsf/sf widths of the xz and yz components, are tuned by hand. Make clear that the agreement in Fig. 4 concerns relative polarization weights, not lineshapes.

13. Measurements were made at 20 K, above the PNO Tc (onset ~11 K). Statements about the "coexistence of superconductivity and sharp spin excitations" should say that the magnons were measured in the normal state of a superconducting sample.

14. Fig. 3(b): the text states that the PNO damping is ~100 meV, but most points lie at 80–90 meV. The text also says the CCO single-peak fit overestimates the damping near (0.5,0); flag that region in the figure.

15. Notation and typos:
    - Fig. 2(d) legend lists "dxz/yz" twice; label the two CCO components distinctly.
    - Eq. (1) has "cos(2πky)".
    - Supplement G uses "z²" and "dz²" inconsistently with "d3z²−r²".
    - Table II lists ε_xy = 0.70 eV vs the measured dxy excitation at 1.29 eV; a sentence explaining the difference between these quantities would avoid confusion.
    - "Superconductive IL phase" should read "superconducting".
    - "Pr" vs "RE" peak labels are used inconsistently.

16. The Fig. 5 caption states that the dashed lines quote the maximum and minimum energies "of each branch". Give the corresponding numbers with uncertainties in the text, since 62 and 90 meV are the central result of the orbital section.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a model-independent display of the dxy dispersion in PNO: peak maximum or first moment from the raw spectra versus Q, with uncertainties, overlaid on the fitted values in Fig. 5(a).
2. Measure, or at least analyse, the magnon near Γ at more than one L to separate J⊥ from anisotropy. If that is not possible, present J⊥ as an upper bound.
3. Give a short table showing how the theoretical t1orb and J2orb depend on U, JH and Δ, and check that the same parameters reproduce the spin J1.
4. Put the spin-wave, damping and dd analyses on a common footing with a second PNO film, or with a hole-doped infinite-layer nickelate measured in the same setup, so that the self-doping argument rests on a like-for-like comparison.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
