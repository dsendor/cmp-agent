A. Must address

1. The opposite-sign dxy orbiton dispersion in PNO has not been shown to be robust against the multi-peak fitting or the scattering geometry (Fig. 5(a,b), Eq. 1, Table I, Table III; abstract). The reported PNO dispersion is 62–90 meV, with the maximum at Γ. It is extracted from a dxy feature at 1.29 eV whose Gaussian width is several hundred meV (Fig. 2(c), Fig. 7), so the shift is only about one fifth of the linewidth, and the map in Fig. 5(a) shows little visible curvature by eye. The feature overlaps two others: the 0.7 eV "RE peak", which the text says "becomes progressively enhanced towards the Brillouin zone edge" and which may be extrinsic (impurity phases, ref. 49); and a very broad dxz/yz component with cross-correlated fit parameters (Fig. 8; dz2 at 2.96 ± 0.58 eV shows how loosely the high-energy part is constrained). As the 0.7 eV peak grows towards the zone edge, its high-energy tail or a mis-modelled lineshape would pull the fitted dxy energy down at exactly the momenta where the paper reports the dxy minimum. Further non-dispersive sources of an apparent shift are reported in the paper itself: the incident and emission angles change along each cut at fixed 2θ = 149.5°, which changes the single-ion cross-sections, self-absorption and the relative weight of dxy and dxz/yz; the dxy feature in PNO has a mixed, geometry-dependent polarization character that the single-ion model does not reproduce (Fig. 4(a–c), Fig. 12); and L is not fixed along the cuts (Methods). The cross-correlation analysis in Fig. 8 covers only dxz/yz and dz2, not the dxy energy against the RE-peak or dxz/yz parameters, which is exactly the correlation that matters here. The 95% confidence intervals in Fig. 5(a) are statistical and do not include this model dependence. The two PNO branches give different amplitudes (62 against 90 meV), and the fitted t3^orb = 5.4 ± 1.7 meV, about half of t1^orb, suggests Eq. (1) is being stretched to follow the data. Because the abstract and the theory comparison (Table III) rest on this dispersion, the authors should: (a) give a model-independent measure versus Q, such as the first moment or the maximum of the raw spectra in a fixed window around 1.1–1.5 eV, or the energy of the steepest leading edge, overlaid on the fitted values; (b) refit the full momentum series with and without the RE component (or with it replaced by a broad background), with the RE-peak and dxz/yz widths and energies fixed across Q against free, with a different RE lineshape, and with the two-peak model of Fig. 7, and show that the sign and approximate size of the dxy dispersion survive each variant; (c) give the dxy–RE and dxy–dxz/yz cross-correlations; (d) use the single-ion cross-sections already calculated for Fig. 4(g–l) to estimate how much the geometry-induced change in the dxy/dxz,yz weight ratio alone shifts the fitted dxy centre along the path; (e) show that the dispersion is the same along [H,0] and [H,H] and symmetric at ±H measured in grazing incidence and grazing emission, which a geometric artifact would not be; (f) if possible, show the dispersion for σ and π polarization separately, or in a second PNO sample.

2. The interlayer coupling J⊥ in PNO, and the three-dimensionality and topotactic-reduction claims built on it, are not constrained by the data (abstract; Fig. 3(a); Results p. 5; Conclusions; SM Sec. D and F, Figs. 9, 10(b), 13). J⊥ = 5.9 meV for PNO was fixed rather than fitted. The only evidence for a nonzero value is the magnon energy at the three or four points nearest Γ, where the magnon is a weak shoulder on a much larger specular elastic peak (Fig. 9), its energy is comparable to the 40 meV resolution, its 95% confidence intervals span tens to about 100 meV (Fig. 10(b), Fig. 13), and the phonon was removed from the fit by hand for Q < 0.175 r.l.u. The paper's own statements conflict: SM Sec. F places J⊥ "between 0 and 10 meV", a range that includes zero, yet says J⊥ = 0 "sensibly worsens the fitting". Because 2θ is fixed, L varies along the cuts (Methods, p. 10), and J⊥ enters precisely through the L dependence, but the L values at the Γ-adjacent points are not reported, nor is it stated whether the SpinW fit used the actual L of each point. Alternative origins of a finite apparent gap are not modelled: exchange or single-ion (easy-plane or Ising) anisotropy, which the authors themselves mention via ref. 65; finite-L dispersion with a much smaller J⊥; and the shift of the damped-harmonic-oscillator peak energy when the damping (~100 meV) is comparable to or exceeds the mode energy. The LSW framework also presupposes long-range order, while the authors describe PNO as lacking it, with spin-glass behaviour. Despite this, the abstract says the out-of-plane values "are similar, indicating that both materials support three-dimensional antiferromagnetic correlations", the conclusions say "we can safely state that the exchange interaction between different planes clearly confers a certain degree of tridimensionality", and p. 5 says the results "give a proof of the fully occurred topotactic reduction ... and the absence of interstitial oxygen atoms". The authors should report L for each spectrum, fit J⊥ as a free parameter with its confidence interval, and fit a J⊥ = 0 model with an anisotropy gap on the same data, reporting whether the data distinguish them. Preferably they should measure the magnon near Γ at two or more L values at fixed in-plane Q (by rotating the sample, changing the scattering angle or the incident energy within the resonance width): with interlayer coupling the Γ-point energy depends on L, with an anisotropy gap it does not. Unless a fit gives a significant lower bound on J⊥, the abstract and conclusions should be restated as "consistent with a finite J⊥ of up to ~10 meV", and the topotactic-reduction and interstitial-oxygen "proof" removed.

B. Should address

3. The theoretical orbiton hopping depends sharply on parameters that were chosen rather than determined, its sensitivity is not shown, and U may have been tuned to the data (Table II, Table III, Methods, SM Sec. H and I). The Methods state that U = 6.5 eV was taken from La4Ni3O8 (ref. 29), a trilayer with a different Ni valence, but SM Sec. H states that "our estimate of the Mott energy U value is exclusively based on the orbital dispersion". If U was adjusted to reproduce the orbiton bandwidth, the agreement between t1^orb = 13.7 meV (theory) and 12.5 ± 3.8 meV (experiment) is partly a fit, not a prediction. The other inputs come from different sources: the hoppings are set to 80% of the CCO values without independent justification, Δ = 4.4 eV comes from DFT on LaNiO2, and J_H = 1.2 eV is set near the atomic value on nephelauxetic arguments. Because t1^orb = (j_HS − j_LS)/2 · J̄1^orb with j_HS = 1/(1 − 3J_H/U), and J̄1^orb scales like t^4/Δ^2, the result is strongly nonlinear: J_H = 1.0 instead of 1.2 eV at U = 6.5 eV lowers (j_HS − j_LS)/2 from about 0.50 to about 0.34, and the CCO-like values J_H = 1 eV, U = 8 eV give j_HS − j_LS ≈ 0.46 instead of ≈ 1.0, roughly halving t1^orb. Agreement within the 30% experimental uncertainty is only meaningful if it holds over a plausible parameter range. The authors should state which parameters were tuned and remove the contradiction between the Methods and SM Sec. H; give t1^orb, J1^orb and J2^orb over plausible ranges (for example Δ 2–5 eV, U 5–8 eV, J_H 0.8–1.3 eV, and the hopping scale), ideally as a map with the experimental band overlaid; say whether the sign of t1^orb and its nearest-neighbour dominance, the robust part of the claim, survive across that range, and whether the sign is a prediction or an input; and check whether the same three-band parameters reproduce the measured spin exchange J1 = 46 meV as an independent consistency test.

4. Numbers in the orbiton model are internally inconsistent, and the model omits an experimentally significant term (Table III, SM Sec. I). Table III gives theory J1^orb = 21.6 meV, while SM Sec. I uses J̄1^orb ≈ 27 meV; but J̄1^orb = U J1^orb/(U − 2J_H) with J1^orb = 21.6 meV, U = 6.5 eV and J_H = 1.2 eV gives ≈ 34 meV, not 27 meV. The quoted t1^orb = 13.7 meV follows from 27 meV. Either the formula or the number needs correcting, and the effect on t1^orb should be stated. In addition, the model keeps only nearest-neighbour processes, while the fit gives t3^orb = 5.4 ± 1.7 meV ≈ 0.4 t1^orb, which is statistically significant. This limitation should be stated wherever "good agreement" is claimed.

5. The magnon-damping comparison does not support, without qualification, the conclusion that "self-doping has a much milder impact on spin order than chemical doping" and offers "a non-disruptive way to achieve superconductivity" (Fig. 3(b), p. 4). PNO, a ~5 nm capped, topotactically reduced film, is compared with Bi2201 values from another work (ref. 54), a different structural family fitted with a different resolution and procedure. The ~80–100 meV PNO broadening could also come from film inhomogeneity (partial reduction, secondary phases, interface layers, disorder), the spin-glass state (refs. 13, 40), unresolved multimagnon weight near the zone boundary (which the paper says overestimates the CCO damping near (0.5,0)), the unpolarized continuum seen in Fig. 4(c), L variation along the cuts, or summation over momenta in the fitted spectra. The self-doping level of PNO is not estimated. The Q-independent damping in PNO, unlike the Q-dependent damping in doped cuprates, is also not discussed. The authors should estimate the effective hole concentration (Hall data or literature), discuss which broadening mechanisms they cannot exclude, compare if possible with hole-doped infinite-layer nickelates ((Nd,Sr)NiO2 or (Pr,Sr)NiO2, e.g. refs. 12, 51) or doped CCO analysed with the same damped-harmonic-oscillator model, and otherwise present the self-doping interpretation as one possibility and scope the claim as a comparison of line widths.

6. The PNO linear-spin-wave fit is poorly constrained, misses part of the data, and the literature comparison is not quantified (Fig. 3(a), Fig. 13, p. 6). Four in-plane couplings plus a fixed J⊥ are fitted to one dispersion that does not reach X (the scan stops at (0.475,0)), has no data between Γ and about 0.12 r.l.u. along [H,0], and has large uncertainties near Γ. The confidence intervals are nonetheless very small (J1 = 45.8 ± 0.3 meV), and no parameter correlations are given. The result has a ferromagnetic J2 = −8.3 meV and J4 = 8.0 meV larger than J3 = 4.2 meV, which is unusual and may reflect parameter degeneracy or absorbed misfit rather than real longer-range exchange. Along (H,H) the data near (0.25,0.25) lie 15–30 meV below the fit (about 130 against ~150 meV), and the dispersion maximum sits at about (0.2,0.2) rather than at the zone boundary, which a commensurate Néel LSW model cannot produce; the unexplained elastic increase beyond the zone boundary is also mentioned but not analysed. The authors should give the parameter covariance or correlation matrix; show reduced models (J1–J2–J3, J1–J2 plus ring exchange, or a fit with the Hubbard-model constraint between the J's) with their χ²; discuss whether the (H,H) misfit and the off-boundary maximum, possibly linked to the incommensurate elastic feature, bias J1 and J⊥, or move a brief account to the SM; and make "in agreement with the literature" quantitative, e.g. against J1 ≈ 64 meV for NdNiO2 (ref. 12), possibly via an effective J from the zone-boundary energies.

7. The interpretation of the 0.7 eV peak as Ni 3d–Pr 5d hybridization, and of the mixed polarization of the dxy peak that relies on it, is not secured (Fig. 4(a–c), Fig. 12, p. 6). The authors concede that the 0.7 eV peak may be extrinsic, since it is stronger in samples with impurity-phase diffraction (ref. 49), yet the assignment feeds the explanation of the mixed crossed/parallel character and the excess intensity of the 1.29 eV peak at H > 0 relative to the single-ion calculation, and it affects the dxy fit (concern 1). The PNO polarimetric spectra are noisy, and the paper does not describe how the polarimeter efficiency was corrected, or how the unpolarized continuum, the 0.7 eV peak and the overlap with the broad dxz/yz peak were handled. Overlap with the 0.7 eV peak, which the authors raise themselves, would explain the discrepancy without any hybridization. The authors should report XRD or other phase-purity checks for this specific sample, and if possible compare the 0.7 eV intensity with a sample of known impurity content; give the polarimeter calibration and the crossed and parallel fractions of each fitted component with uncertainties; show whether the anomaly persists when the 0.7 eV component is fitted separately in each polarization channel; make the hybridization explanation testable, e.g. by a calculation with a rare-earth-hybridized component or an estimate of the admixture needed to reproduce Fig. 4(c); and present the hybridization interpretation as one possibility, conditional on the assignment of the 0.7 eV peak.

8. Sample characterisation and reproducibility are not documented (Methods; title). The paper does not say how many PNO films were measured, whether the RIXS film itself showed the superconducting transition (Tc onset about 11 K, zero resistance below 4 K) or whether those numbers come from a sister sample in ref. 42, and no structural or transport data for the measured film are shown. It is not stated whether the key results (J1, damping, dxy energy and dispersion, polarization anomaly) were reproduced on a second film or after thermal cycling, or whether the beam caused any time-dependent change in these reduced, metastable films. The CCO data are a re-analysis of earlier datasets (refs. 45, 55), possibly from different beamtimes. This matters because "superconducting" is in the title and the coexistence of sharp magnons with superconductivity is a stated conclusion. The authors should state the number of films, show resistivity and XRD of the measured film (the SM is fine) or say it is a sister film, state which results were reproduced, indicate which CCO spectra come from which experiment, and compare J1 and the 0.7 eV feature quantitatively with earlier PNO/RNiO2 RIXS (refs. 12, 47, 52).

9. The strain argument for an intrinsic J1 difference is internally inconsistent (pp. 5–6). The text states that compressive strain generally reduces the exchange and that PNO on STO is under compression, then concludes that "strain would tend to increase J1 in PNO"; by the stated trend strain would instead reduce J1 in PNO and partly account for the observed difference. The sign conventions are reversed: a "compressive strain of about +0.9%", although (3.905 − 3.94)/3.94 ≈ −0.9%, and a "tensile strain of about −0.1%" for CCO. The Methods give a = 3.94 Å for the PNO film, the bulk-like value used to compute the mismatch; if this is the measured in-plane parameter, the film is relaxed and the strain argument does not apply. The STO capping layer and possible partial relaxation of a 5 nm film are not discussed, and no reciprocal-space map is shown. The authors should give the measured in-plane lattice parameter of the reduced film (from reciprocal-space maps), correct the signs and the direction of the expected effect, and quote the expected size of a strain-induced change in J1 from refs. 68–71 before claiming that the J1 reduction "must be intrinsic"; the order-of-magnitude argument may suffice on its own.
