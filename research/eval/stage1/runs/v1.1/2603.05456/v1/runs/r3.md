Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports THz emission from the centrosymmetric van der Waals ferromagnet Cr2Ge2Te6 under 1.2 eV linearly polarized excitation. The authors interpret it as magnetic-dipole radiation from Cr magnetization driven by an "Edelstein–Zeeman" field, which they attribute to a resonant interband nonlinear Edelstein effect (NLEE) acting on the hidden spin texture of the locally non-centrosymmetric Cr sites. If established, a non-thermal, polarization-programmable effective field acting on the order parameter of a centrosymmetric magnet would interest both the ultrafast-magnetism and nonlinear-response communities. As presented, however, the evidence is THz emission patterns whose symmetry is not unique to the proposed mechanism, together with a mean-field model compared to the data only qualitatively. No direct probe of the magnetization is included.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Topical idea: a hidden-spin-texture route to rectified spin density in a globally centrosymmetric magnet, linked to the order parameter through s–d exchange.
- Clean, systematic THz emission data: polarization maps at two fluences and three temperatures (Fig. 2d,e), a fine temperature scan across Tc (Fig. 2f), and fluence series at normal and 45° incidence (Figs. 3, 4c,e).
- The fluence-driven 90° rotation and polarity reversal of the twofold pattern at 45° incidence (Fig. 3b–d, 4e) is an interesting, non-trivial observation.
- A symmetry analysis for point group 3̄ (Eq. 2–3, Table S1, Eq. IV.62) correctly shows that the dyad E⊗E and the axial vector M share the Ag ⊕ Eg representations. This explains why the petal orientation is not locked to a crystal axis.
- A detailed SI: a model Hamiltonian, perturbative density-matrix formalism, and a mean-field Heisenberg treatment.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The magnetic-dipole (NLEE) origin of the emission is not separated from other second-order sources with the same symmetry.**
   - *Where:* p. 2, right column; p. 3, left column.
   - *What the paper shows:* The main evidence is the d-wave polarization pattern at normal incidence (|S| ∝ |cos(2φ−2δ)|, Fig. 2d,e).
   - *Why it is not unique:* For point group 3 (C3, no mirrors), the in-plane surface electric-dipole rectification tensor has exactly this form: Px = a(Ex²−Ey²) − 2bExEy, Py = −b(Ex²−Ey²) − 2aExEy. So a surface or interface χ(2), a near-surface strain gradient, or a stacking-fault region would produce the same fourfold amplitude pattern, with a non-universal phase δ. Bulk electric-quadrupole and magnetic-dipole optical rectification are also allowed in centrosymmetric crystals, and their tensor form is the same as Eq. (2).
   - *Why the two arguments offered for excluding a surface origin do not settle it:*
     - (i) "Tracking the bulk magnetic order" (Fig. 2f). The signal is largest deep in the paramagnetic phase (200 K in Fig. 2e; rising up to ~180 K in Fig. 2f). The feature at Tc is a modest change in slope, with error bars of ±0.1–0.15 on a ~0.5–0.9 scale. A surface χ(2) that changes with temperature through the band structure or magnetostriction would also show a kink at Tc.
     - (ii) Linear-versus-saturating fluence scaling. Saturation with fluence is generic for photocarrier-driven emission (screening, absorption bleaching, heating). It does not discriminate between mechanisms.
   - *What the authors should do:* At least two of the following discriminating measurements.
     - (a) Applied-field dependence of amplitude, polarity and petal orientation. The OptiCool cryostat used for the 45° data is a magneto-optic system. Examples: an out-of-plane field that saturates Mz, an in-plane field that pulls M into the plane, and field reversal. A magnetic-dipole mechanism makes sharp predictions here (for example, a field that saturates M along y should quench the dM_y response); a surface χ(2) would be nearly field-independent.
     - (b) Detection of the orthogonal THz polarization component (Ey). Eq. (IV.63)–(IV.64) predicts that it is phase-shifted by 45° in φ relative to Ex, with equal amplitude.
     - (c) Signed (polarity-resolved) amplitude versus φ, showing the predicted sign change under φ → φ + 90°.
     - (d) Thickness dependence, or front-versus-back excitation, to separate bulk from surface.
     - (e) A non-magnetic or isostructural reference measured under the same conditions.

2. **No direct evidence that the Cr magnetization is changed ("manipulation of ferromagnetism"), and the dynamics are not modelled.**
   - *Where:* title, abstract, pp. 4–6.
   - *Missing direct probe:* The only observable is the far-field THz waveform. No time-resolved Kerr or Faraday rotation, or other magnetization-sensitive probe, shows that M of the Cr³⁺ sublattice rotates or grows.
   - *Alternative source not excluded:* Even if the emission is magnetic-dipole in nature, the radiating moment could be the transient itinerant spin density of the photocarriers itself (the NLEE spin, Eq. IV.55), not the localized Cr moments. The paper does not distinguish these.
   - *Quasi-static model applied to picosecond emission:* The model in Fig. 4 and Sec. V minimizes a static free energy. The emitted field, however, is set by ∂²M/∂t² on a ~1 ps timescale, comparable to the 160 fs pump plus detection response. Rotating an ordered moment substantially within ~1 ps requires precession frequencies of order 1 THz. The paper never explains how the adiabatic mean-field M(I0) of Fig. 4b,d maps onto a radiated spectral weight.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Provide a time-resolved magneto-optical measurement under the same conditions (fluence, polarization, temperature), showing a polarization-dependent ΔM with the d-wave symmetry.
     - Provide a dynamical model (e.g., LLG or time-dependent mean-field driven by a pulsed H^EZ(t)). It should give the THz waveform and spectrum, and show that the relevant response is that of the Cr moments rather than of the carriers.

3. **The claimed "quantitative" agreement with the model is not shown, and the temperature dependence appears to contradict the low-fluence model.**
   - *Where:* abstract, Fig. 4, SI Sec. V.C.
   - *Qualitative comparison only:* The fluence axes in Fig. 4b,d are in arbitrary units of I0 with no mapping to mJ/cm². The parameters are chosen rather than fitted or independently fixed: κ = 0.2, χ_x,∥ = χ_z,xx = 0.5/Tc⁰, I0 = 5Tc⁰, and δ∥ = 50° "chosen to match". The gray line in Fig. 4c is not identified.
   - *Arbitrary weighting at 45°:* The 45° comparison uses |My − Mz| with equal weights. The detected p-polarized field at 45° weights My and Mz differently, through the emission geometry, refraction and Fresnel factors, and the location of the minimum depends on this choice.
   - *Temperature dependence:* In the low-field regime the model gives an in-plane susceptibility that is constant below Tc and decreases above it as 1/(T − Tc/(1+κ)) (Eq. V.23; Fig. S7b). The data in Fig. 2f instead show the signal increasing from Tc up to ~180 K. Agreement is obtained only in the strong-field limit (I0 = 5Tc⁰, Fig. S8/S9). In that limit the effective field is of order the exchange scale, i.e. tens to ~100 T.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - (a) Fit the normal-incidence and 45° fluence dependences and the temperature dependence simultaneously, using one calibrated mapping I0 ↔ fluence and stated free parameters, with uncertainties.
     - (b) Estimate H^EZ in tesla from the photocarrier density at the experimental fluence (≈10²⁰ cm⁻³ at 0.1 mJ/cm² for a ~50 nm absorption depth), the computed χ(2), and J_sd. Show that it is large enough to reach the saturation regime the model needs.
     - (c) Check that the radiated field expected from the implied ΔM, through the magnetic-dipole radiation formula, matches the measured ~0.1–1 V/cm.

B. Should address

4. **The theoretical demonstration that ⟨S^(2)⟩ ≠ 0 is incomplete.**
   - *Where:* SI Sec. IV.B.4.
   - *Why the argument is incomplete:* The conclusion rests on showing that individual trace terms are even under inversion (Eq. IV.48, IV.52–IV.55). The constraint from time reversal on a T-odd spin density rectified from linearly polarized (T-even) light is not analysed. For real absorption in a Kramers-degenerate system with linear light, contributions at ±k with opposite spin cancel. A net result must therefore come from a specific dissipative coherence term, analogous to the shift current. The paper does not identify which term this is.
   - *Missing calculation:* No Brillouin-zone integral is actually evaluated, numerically or analytically.
   - *Model–structure mismatch:* The model places the inversion partners along z (Fig. S5). In Cr2Ge2Te6 the inversion-related Cr pair sits within the buckled honeycomb layer.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Evaluate χ(2) numerically for the model, and ideally for a DFT-derived Wannier model of Cr2Ge2Te6.
     - Explicitly verify T-parity and survival under Brillouin-zone integration.
     - Report its magnitude and its dependence on photon energy. Pump-photon-energy dependence is also a natural experimental test of the claimed interband resonance; data at a second pump energy would strengthen the "resonant" claim.

5. **Polarization-dependent absorption at 45° incidence is not controlled.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 3c, 4e.
   - *Why it matters:* At oblique incidence the absorbed fluence and internal field differ for s and p polarization, which by itself produces a twofold modulation. Photo-Dember and surface-field emission are polarization-independent in mechanism, but they are not polarization-independent in amplitude at 45°, and they radiate p-polarized THz. The fluence-driven 90° rotation could then come from a fluence-dependent competition between such an isotropic, absorption-weighted term and the anisotropic term. This would not require an My/Mz competition.
   - *What the authors should do:* Correct for Fresnel absorption, report absorbed rather than incident fluence, and fit the 45° polar plots to a model that includes an isotropic term.

6. **Heating and effective sample temperature are not addressed, although the mechanism is claimed to be "non-thermal".**
   - *Where:* SI Sec. II.A–B; Methods.
   - *The issue:*
     - At normal incidence the average power reaches 330 mW at 50 kHz (half after chopping) on a 1.9 mm spot at a nominal 7 K, which is a substantial steady-state heat load.
     - The two geometries use different cryostats (Janis exchange gas versus OptiCool closed-cycle) and repetition rates (50 kHz versus 3 kHz).
     - Because the signal grows with temperature (Fig. 2f), steady-state heating directly affects both the fluence and temperature dependences.
   - *What the authors should do:* Estimate or measure the steady-state and per-pulse temperature rise (e.g., repetition-rate dependence at fixed fluence), and show that the fluence saturation is not a heating artefact.

7. **The anisotropy and static magnetic characterization are insufficient to constrain the model.**
   - *Where:* Fig. S1, S2; Sec. V.
   - *Gaps in the characterization:*
     - The field direction in Fig. S2 is not stated.
     - In-plane and out-of-plane M(H) are not compared, although they would fix κ (taken as 0.2 without justification).
     - M(T) saturates at 1.45 μB/f.u. while M(H) saturates at ~3.2 μB/f.u. The measuring field and formula-unit convention are not given.
   - *Conflicting descriptions:* The SI describes the sample as a soft ferromagnet with "nearly linear response", which is hard to reconcile with the step-like low-temperature loops in Fig. S2 and with the main-text "weakly uniaxial" description.
   - *What the authors should do:* Provide easy-axis and hard-axis M(H), extract the anisotropy field, and use it to fix κ.

8. **Sample statistics and reproducibility are not stated.**
   - *Missing information:* The paper does not say how many crystals were measured, the sample thickness, whether the normal- and oblique-incidence data come from the same crystal, or how the surface was prepared (cleaved in air? Cr2Ge2Te6 surfaces degrade).
   - *What the authors should do:* Show the key polarization pattern and fluence dependence on at least a second sample. Report whether δ is sample-independent: a surface or strain origin would likely make it vary from sample to sample.

9. **The waveform and spectral changes with fluence are not analysed.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 3b,d.
   - *The issue:*
     - In Fig. 3d the spectral peak shifts from ~0.4 THz at low fluence to ~1 THz at high fluence.
     - The high-fluence waveform in Fig. 3b is more oscillatory, not simply polarity-reversed as stated in the text.
     - A frequency shift may indicate a different emission process, or a changing magnetic resonance, at high fluence. The single "spectral weight" number does not capture this.
     - The definition of "spectral weight" (integration band, normalization) is not given.
   - *What the authors should do:* Define the spectral weight and discuss the spectral shift within the model.

C. Minor

10. **The quoted THz field strength is inconsistent.** The main text says "several V/cm" (p. 2), Fig. 2c shows ~0.8 V/cm peak, and SI Sec. II.C says "on the order of 0.1 V/cm". Give the calibrated value, including the EOS response function and collection efficiency.

11. **The phase convention is inconsistent.** Main-text Eq. (3) discussion gives |My| ∝ |cos(2φ − δ)|, whereas SI Eq. (IV.64)/(V.40) use cos(2φ − 2δ). The main text places the maximum near φ = 60° (p. 2; Fig. 2f inset), while the SI uses δ∥ ≈ 50° "obtained" from the main text. Reconcile these, and give the fitted δ with its uncertainty.

12. **The 45° fluence formula does not reproduce its own coefficient.** Eq. (II.2) with f = 3 kHz and d = 1.90 mm gives ≈0.0083·P mJ/cm² per mW, not 0.017·P. The stated 0.017 is reproduced only for a 1.5 kHz post-chopping rate. Clarify the repetition and chopping rate for the oblique geometry.

13. **Tc is quoted inconsistently.** It is given as ~66 K (p. 2) and 67 K (Fig. 2f, SI).

14. **"p-polarized" is used at normal incidence.** Fig. 3a and p. 2 refer to p-polarized emission at normal incidence; "x-polarized (along â)" is the accurate term there. "Fourfold symmetry" of |cos 2φ| would also be better described as a d-wave (twofold-signed) pattern, which is what the SI says.

15. **Fig. 4 caption.** The caption of panel (a) is incomplete ("…above Tc and in the presence of the planar Edelstein-Zeeman field H_y^EZ"). Panel (b)'s legend lists |My − Mz| although only one curve appears. Panel (c)'s gray line is unexplained.

16. **Pump photon energy relative to the gap.** The statement that 1.2 eV is "near-resonant with the direct gap at Γ" should be supported with a band-structure or absorption reference, given reported Cr2Ge2Te6 gaps of a few tenths of an eV.

17. **Fig. S2 caption typo.** "300 14K" should be corrected.

18. **Data availability.** "Upon request" is weak for a top-tier journal; deposit the waveforms and analysis code.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add field-dependent THz emission (out-of-plane and in-plane fields, both field polarities) in the existing magneto-optic cryostat. This is the most direct way to tie the emission to M.
- Add a time-resolved MOKE or Faraday measurement with the same pump, showing a d-wave, linear-polarization-dependent ΔM. This would establish the "control of ferromagnetism" claim directly.
- Measure both THz polarization components and the signed amplitude versus φ, and fit Eq. (IV.62) globally across fluence and temperature with calibrated fluence and stated free parameters.
- Evaluate χ(2) numerically for a realistic Cr2Ge2Te6 tight-binding/Wannier model. Estimate H^EZ in tesla at the experimental fluence and the expected radiated field, to show that the effect has the right order of magnitude.

5. Verdict: major revision
