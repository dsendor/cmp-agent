Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports THz emission from bulk, centrosymmetric Cr2Ge2Te6 under 1.2 eV excitation. The emission at normal incidence varies with pump polarization as |cos(2φ − 2δ)|, and the authors attribute it to magnetic-dipole radiation from a magnetization that light induces at second order through a "nonlinear Edelstein–Zeeman field". They support this with a symmetry analysis for point group 3̄, a tight-binding argument that a second-order spin density survives Brillouin-zone integration, and a mean-field Heisenberg/Landau model of how that field acts on the Cr moments. If it were established, optical control of magnetization in a centrosymmetric magnet through a nonlinear spin–orbit pathway would be of broad interest. As it stands, however, the experiments do not separate this mechanism from conventional second-order emission that has the same symmetry. Several observations, including the temperature dependence, the 45° data and the radiation geometry, also sit uneasily with the proposed picture.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear and striking observation: strong THz emission from a nominally centrosymmetric van der Waals ferromagnet, with a clean four-lobed pump-polarization pattern (Fig. 2d,e) that stays the same across fluence and temperature.
- The fluence-driven change at 45° incidence is unusual and interesting: polarity reversal (Fig. 3b), a 90° rotation of the twofold pattern (Fig. 3c), a minimum in spectral weight near 0.2 mJ/cm² (Fig. 4e) and a shift of spectral content from about 0.5 to 1 THz (Fig. 3d).
- The supplementary theory is careful and explicit. It shows that equilibrium spin vanishes, that first-order spin cancels after zone integration, and that the second-order quadrupolar (A⊗A) term survives (Eqs. IV.26–IV.55). The symmetry-reduced tensor for point group 3̄ (Eq. IV.62, Table S1) is useful in its own right.
- The idea that a hidden spin texture from locally non-centrosymmetric Wyckoff sites can be converted into a net, light-tunable effective field acting on localized moments is conceptually appealing and could apply generally.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The magnetic-dipole origin of the emission is not shown, and the polarization dependence cannot discriminate it.** The central claim is that the THz field is radiated by a light-induced M_y. The paper's evidence is the fourfold |cos(2φ − 2δ)| pattern plus two exclusion arguments (p. 2).
   - **Symmetry does not discriminate.** At the surface, point group 3̄ reduces to 3. There an in-plane polar vector transforms as the same E doublet as an in-plane axial vector. Surface optical rectification or a surface shift/injection photocurrent J_x therefore gives P_x ∝ cos(2φ − 2δ′), the same pattern as Eq. (3).
   - **Bulk-allowed alternatives with the same pattern.** In centrosymmetric bulk, the photon-drag current j_i ∝ T_izkl q_z E_k E_l and electric-quadrupole rectification are both allowed. Both give in-plane sources with this form, and the authors' own theory invokes intra-cell electric quadrupoles (Fig. S6b).
   - **"Surface" versus "bulk" is not sharply defined here.** At 1.2 eV the optical penetration depth in Cr2Ge2Te6 is only tens of nanometres. The "bulk" region that is pumped therefore lies within the near-surface layer where surface fields and band bending act.
   - **Argument (ii) is weak.** Surface emitters (surface-field screening, photo-Dember, shift currents) routinely saturate at fluences of 0.1–1 mJ/cm² through carrier screening and absorption saturation. Saturation in Fig. 4c is therefore not specific to a magnetic mechanism. Argument (i) is addressed in concern 2.
   - **No direct magnetic test.** No measurement probes the magnetic character of the source, even though the 45° data were taken in a magneto-optical cryostat (OptiCool, SI Sec. II.A).

   The authors should:
   - Measure the emission versus applied magnetic field. An out-of-plane field changes the transverse susceptibility and the domain state. An in-plane field along y saturates M_y and should strongly suppress the dM_y response if the source is magnetic. Pure electric-dipole sources should not depend on field.
   - Measure the cross-polarized component E_y. The model predicts it should follow sin(2φ − 2δ).
   - Pump from the opposite face, or compare crystals of different thickness. This separates photon drag (odd in q) and surface contributions from a bulk magnetization.
   - Use circularly polarized pump light. The symmetric E⊗E term in Eq. (2) predicts no in-plane M_y at normal incidence for circular light.

   Without at least the field dependence, the headline claim of magnetic-dipole radiation from a light-induced magnetization is unsupported.

2. **The temperature dependence runs opposite to what the proposed mechanism predicts.**
   - **What the data show.** In Fig. 2e,f the emission at 100 µJ/cm² is *largest* in the paramagnetic phase: at 200 K (about 3T_c) it is roughly twice its 7 K value. It decreases smoothly on cooling, and the change at T_c is modest given the error bars.
   - **What the model predicts.** If the signal is M_y = χ_∥(T)·H^EZ, the paramagnetic response should fall as the susceptibility falls above T_c. The authors' own M(H) data (Fig. S2) show the low-field slope at 150 K and 300 K is far smaller than at 80 K. The low-fluence mean-field result (Fig. S7b) also predicts η_y largest below T_c and decaying above.
   - **The high-fluence explanation does not apply to these data.** To explain the increase with T, the SI invokes the high-fluence regime (I_0 = 5T_c⁰, Figs. S8–S9). But 100 µJ/cm² lies in the nearly linear part of the measured fluence dependence (Fig. 4c), so this explanation does not cover the data in Fig. 2f.
   - **A magnetism-independent emitter fits the data.** The four-lobe orientation and shape are unchanged at 7, 80 and 200 K (Fig. 2e). A source that does not depend on magnetism, modulated by temperature-dependent absorption, carrier lifetime or THz refractive index, explains these data at least as naturally.

   The authors should:
   - Give T-dependent data at several fluences, including the lowest available.
   - Compare the emitted amplitude quantitatively with the measured χ(T) and M(T) from Figs. S1–S2.
   - Explain why the paramagnetic-state signal is larger than the ferromagnetic one.

   As written, argument (i) for excluding surface mechanisms is not supported by the data.

3. **The 45° interpretation (competition between M_y and M_z) conflicts with magnetic-dipole selection rules and with the domain state.** The polarity reversal and 90° pattern rotation (Figs. 3, 4d,e) are presented as key evidence of fluence-driven reorientation of the magnetization. Three problems arise.
   - **Selection rules.** Fig. 3a and SI Fig. S3 state that p-polarized THz is detected (the wire-grid polarizer selects lab-x, in the plane of incidence). A magnetic dipole radiates E ∝ n × m̈. For emission in the plane of incidence, m along z (and m along x) radiates purely s-polarized light, and only m_y radiates p. An M_z contribution therefore cannot interfere with M_y in the detected channel. The model quantity |M_y − M_z| in Fig. 4d, with equal weights and no Fresnel or radiation factors, has no basis.
   - **Domain state.** Cr2Ge2Te6 is a soft ferromagnet with no hysteresis (Fig. S2). At zero field the 1.9 mm spot averages over many out-of-plane domains, so the net M_z, and any response odd in M_z, should largely cancel. Figs. 4d and S9 assume a single domain starting from M_z = 1. The paper does not say whether a field was applied.
   - **Refraction and absorption effects.**
     - With n ≈ 3–4 at 1.2 eV, the internal angle for 45° incidence is only about 10–15°, so the E_z² terms in Eqs. (3), (V.38) and (V.39) are strongly reduced.
     - At oblique incidence the absorbed fluence itself depends on φ, because s and p Fresnel coefficients differ. This gives a twofold modulation independent of mechanism.
     - The pronounced change in spectrum with fluence (Fig. 3d) points to two sources with different dynamics rather than one rotating moment.

   The authors should:
   - State the field conditions and the detected polarization.
   - Measure s and p separately.
   - Repeat the fluence series in a saturating out-of-plane field of both signs.
   - Correct the oblique-incidence analysis for internal angle and polarization-dependent absorption.
   - Model the detected field with the correct radiation geometry.

B. Should address

4. **The "quantitative" agreement with the mean-field model has free scales, and the size of the effect is not checked for plausibility.**
   - **Free scales.** The abstract says the polarization, fluence and temperature dependences are "quantitatively captured". In Fig. 4b–e the fluence axis of the model (I_0 in units of T_c⁰) is mapped onto mJ/cm² by a free factor. The susceptibilities are set by hand (χ = 0.5/T_c⁰), and κ = 0.2 is not tied to the measured anisotropy of Cr2Ge2Te6.
   - **Mismatched quantities.** "Spectral weight", presumably an integral of |E(ω)|², is compared directly with |M|, not with a quantity linear in the radiated field.
   - **Implausibly large fields.** At I_0 = 5T_c⁰ the Zeeman energy is about 30 meV, of order 100 T for a 3 µ_B moment. No estimate links such a field to the absorbed photon density at 0.1–1 mJ/cm² and a realistic J_sd.
   - **No ΔM estimate.** The paper does not estimate the ΔM (fraction of M_s) needed to radiate the observed field.

   The authors should give these order-of-magnitude estimates, define the spectral weight, compare quantities with the same scaling, and soften "quantitatively".

5. **A static equilibrium model is used for sub-picosecond dynamics.**
   - **What the model computes.** The model minimizes F(M) (Eq. 4, Sec. V) and treats the induced M as following H^EZ adiabatically. The radiated far field is ∝ ∂²M/∂t², and H^EZ lasts about as long as the 160 fs pulse or the interband coherence or carrier lifetime.
   - **Precession is omitted.** For a ferromagnet with M ∥ z, the leading response to a short in-plane field pulse is precessional (δM ∝ M × H). That response is rotated by 45° in φ relative to the longitudinal response assumed here and is odd in M_z.
   - **The paramagnetic case is unexplained.** In the paramagnetic phase, building up a longitudinal M within about 1 ps requires spin relaxation that the model does not include.

   A time-dependent treatment (for example LLG or Bloch equations driven by H^EZ(t)) is needed to connect the model to the waveforms and spectra in Figs. 2c, 3b and 3d. It should state which timescale sets H^EZ(t).

6. **The effective sample temperature and steady-state heating are unknown.**
   - **Heating could mimic saturation.** At normal incidence, 0.46 mJ/cm² corresponds to 330 mW average power in a 1.9 mm spot (SI Eq. II.1), with the sample at 7 K in exchange gas. Steady-state heating toward or above T_c could produce the sub-linear fluence dependence in Fig. 4c and blur the T-dependence in Fig. 2f.
   - **Calibration is needed.** The authors should estimate or measure the temperature rise. For example, they could compare fluence series at several base temperatures, vary the repetition rate or chopping duty cycle at fixed fluence, or track a temperature-sensitive signature.

7. **Established opto-magnetic mechanisms are not considered.** Below T_c, linearly polarized pulses act on ferromagnets through the inverse Cotton–Mouton effect, impulsive stimulated Raman excitation of magnons, photo-induced changes of magnetic anisotropy and ultrafast demagnetization. Some of these produce polarization-dependent torques with the same E⊗E symmetry. The paper should discuss them and explain how the data separate them from the proposed mechanism.

   It should also state clearly what is new relative to:
   - Ref. 8 (nonlinear-optics-driven spin reorientation in ferromagnets);
   - Refs. 9 and 15 (hidden spin polarization probed optically and by THz emission in centrosymmetric materials).

8. **The polarization data lack sign and crystal-axis registration.**
   - **Sign.** Fig. 2d,e plot only magnitudes. The d-wave prediction (Eq. IV.64) requires the emitted field to change sign between adjacent lobes. The signed peak amplitude, or waveforms at φ and φ + 90°, should be shown.
   - **Axis registration.** The paper does not say how the â axis was determined (Laue, cleavage edges?). Because δ is "material-dependent" and "not locked to a crystalline axis", rotating the sample azimuthally is needed to show that the pattern rotates with the crystal rather than with the lab frame (for example a waveplate or setup birefringence artifact).
   - **Inconsistent maxima.** The text gives maxima near φ = 60°, but the SI fits δ_∥ ≈ 50°.

9. **Samples and reproducibility are not reported.** The paper does not give:
   - how many crystals were measured;
   - crystal thickness;
   - whether surfaces were freshly cleaved, given that Cr2Ge2Te6 surfaces degrade in air;
   - whether the normal-incidence (Janis, 50 kHz) and 45° (OptiCool, 3 kHz) data come from the same crystal;
   - any spread between samples or after thermal cycling.

   Showing the fourfold pattern and the 45° crossover on at least a second crystal would strengthen the claims.

10. **The theory is generic and not connected to Cr2Ge2Te6.**
    - **Generic model.** The tight-binding model (Sec. III) is a two-site Rashba model with generic C_n symmetry, not the electronic structure of Cr2Ge2Te6. The SI notes that the itinerant bands lie 2–3 eV from the Cr³⁺ states, but transitions at 1.2 eV involve hybridized Cr d / Te p states, so the split into itinerant and localized sectors in the s–d coupling (Eq. V.28) is a strong assumption.
    - **Nothing is computed for the real material.** None of the six χ⁽²⁾ components, their ratios (which set δ and the 45° crossover) or their magnitudes is computed or estimated for Cr2Ge2Te6.

    Even a DFT-based or symmetry-adapted estimate of the dominant components and of δ would make the theory–experiment link testable.

C. Minor

11. **Emitted field strength is quoted inconsistently.** The main text says "several V/cm", Fig. 2c peaks at about 0.8 V/cm, and SI Sec. II.C says "on the order of 0.1 V/cm". Please reconcile these. Give the source of r_41 for CdTe at 1030 nm, and state whether Fresnel transmission and collection losses are included.

12. **Fluence calibration has several gaps.**
    - Eq. II.2 states f = 3 kHz, but the prefactor 0.017 corresponds to 1.5 kHz (after chopping).
    - Please state whether P is measured before or after the chopper.
    - Please state whether quoted fluences are average or peak; for a Gaussian with a 1/e² diameter, peak is twice average.
    - Please give absorbed as well as incident fluence.

13. **Labels and quoted values are inconsistent.**
    - T_c is given as about 66 K in the text and 67 K in Fig. 2f and S1.
    - Fig. 2f plots "Spectral weight" while the text describes |S_THz|_max.
    - The text uses S_THz, but the figures use E_THz and E^p_THz.
    - Fig. S9 uses T = 0.104 T_c⁰, but the text says 0.107 T_c⁰.

14. **Panel references on p. 6 are off by one.** "Agreement between theory and experiment in Figs. 4a and 4b" should be 4b and 4c. "As shown in Fig. 4c and Fig. 4d" (45°) should be 4d and 4e.

15. **"p-polarized" at normal incidence.** On p. 2, "only the M_y component generates p-polarized THz radiation ... at normal incidence" uses a term that is undefined at normal incidence. Please state the detected lab-frame component (E_x).

16. **Typos and wording.**
    - Fig. S2 caption: "300 14K".
    - "Kramer's" should be "Kramers'" (throughout).
    - "explicitly" is misspelled in SI p. S-20.
    - Fig. 4a caption is garbled ("above the Curie temperature T_c and in the presence of the planar ...").
    - Data are "available upon request"; depositing the waveforms would be preferable.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add field-dependent THz emission (in-plane and out-of-plane, both signs) at normal and 45° incidence. This is the most direct test of a magnetic-dipole source and can be done in the existing OptiCool setup.
2. Map the full polarization (E_x and E_y, signed) against pump polarization and sample azimuth, and include circular pump polarization and front/back illumination. These separate the Edelstein–Zeeman mechanism from surface rectification, photon drag and quadrupolar emission.
3. Replace the static minimization with a time-dependent spin model driven by H^EZ(t). Anchor it with physical estimates of H^EZ, the induced ΔM and the radiated field, and compare it with the measured waveforms and spectra.
4. Re-measure the temperature dependence at the lowest usable fluence with a heating calibration. Compare it directly with χ(T) and M(T) from Figs. S1–S2.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
