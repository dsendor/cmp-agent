A. Must address

1. No direct evidence that the THz emission comes from the magnetization, or that the Cr magnetization changes at all. The title and abstract claim "manipulation of ferromagnetism" and that the authors "directly observe magnetic dipole radiation arising from optically driven magnetization dynamics". The model also relies on competition at 45° incidence between a "frozen" out-of-plane M_z and an induced in-plane M_y (pp. 4–6, Fig. 4d). Yet no measurement in the paper varies M directly. The evidence is indirect: the shape of the polarization patterns, a modest change of slope near Tc in Fig. 2f (about 30–40% suppression), and the fluence dependence at 45° (Figs. 3, 4c,e). The Methods list a magneto-optic cryostat (OptiCool), but no field-dependent data are shown. The magnetic history of the sample (zero-field-cooled or field-cooled) and the domain state within the beam spot are not stated. In zero field an easy-axis ferromagnet such as Cr2Ge2Te6 is generally multidomain (labyrinth or stripe domains with ±M_z), so the net M_z averaged over the spot should be close to zero. The n_y M_z term in E_THz ∝ M × n that the model needs at 45° would then largely cancel, as would the canted equilibrium M_z of Fig. 4a, unless the sample is poled. The authors should show: (i) THz waveforms at 45° incidence after field-cooling in +B_z and −B_z, and in-field at ±B along c. The M_z-derived component should reverse sign with the poling direction, while the in-plane-driven M_y term at normal incidence and any non-magnetic contribution should not. (ii) Waveforms under an in-plane field (along the a-axis), which should alter the M_y channel and the anisotropy competition that the model invokes; the crossover fluence at 45° should shift with field-tuned anisotropy. (iii) The same tests above Tc, where the model predicts no M_z contribution. (iv) Ideally, a time-resolved Kerr or Faraday measurement in the same pump geometry showing a light-induced δM that follows the pump polarization as |cos(2φ − 2δ)| (the authors mention XMCD only as future work). Without at least (i) and (ii), the attribution to the ferromagnetic order parameter rests only on the polarization symmetry, which does not single out magnetism (concern 2), and the central claim is not established.

2. Other second-order emission mechanisms with the same symmetry are not excluded, and the arguments given do not separate them from bulk magnetic-dipole radiation. (a) Polarization pattern: for in-plane fields in a threefold-symmetric bulk or surface, any response quadratic in E (polar or axial) has the form ∝ cos(2φ − 2δ) with unconstrained δ; for example a surface or interface electric dipole or shift current on a cleaved surface of reduced C3 symmetry gives P_y ∝ χ1(Ex² − Ey²) + χ2(2ExEy). Because E⊗E → Ag ⊕ 2Eg, the fourfold |cos(2φ − 2δ)| pattern of Fig. 2d,e is what a surface electric dipole, a bulk electric quadrupole (photon-drag-like, q-dependent) or a magnetic dipole response would all produce. The argument on p. 3 that the surface lacks a mirror, and so would not lock surface dipole emission to the crystal axes, applies equally to the bulk axial-vector response. Also, the pattern orientation being unchanged with fluence and temperature does not discriminate. (b) Electric quadrupole: bulk electric-quadrupole radiation is the same multipole order as magnetic-dipole radiation and is also allowed in centrosymmetric R3̄. The authors themselves invoke "intra-cell electric quadrupole transitions" (p. 5 and SI) as part of the microscopic mechanism, but never separate quadrupolar from magnetic-dipole emission. Shift- or injection-type currents at the surface or in depletion fields within the absorption depth, and carrier-density gradients along c, are also possible. (c) Temperature: the emission is largest in the paramagnetic phase, keeps the same pattern at 80 K and 200 K, is nearly flat from ~70 K to 200 K and changes only gradually across Tc (Fig. 2f). Cr2Ge2Te6 has strong magneto-elastic and magneto-optical changes at Tc (band-edge shift, magnetostriction), so any non-magnetic rectification resonant near 1.2 eV would also change across Tc. (d) Fluence: the claim that a surface contribution "would scale linearly with pump intensity" (p. 2) is not a discriminator. Absorption bleaching, carrier screening, two-photon absorption and saturation of interband coherence make any above-gap rectification or photocurrent sublinear at 0.1–1.5 mJ/cm², and the normal-incidence data in Fig. 4c are close to linear below ~0.2 mJ/cm² anyway. The authors should provide at least one discriminating test: sample rotation about the surface normal at fixed pump polarization; detection of both orthogonal THz components (full THz polarimetry), to check that E_THz transforms as M × n of an axial vector with the M_x/M_y ratio and relative phases predicted by the α coefficients in Eq. 2; front- versus back-side excitation, transmission versus reflection geometry, a thickness dependence, or a comparison of freshly cleaved and aged surfaces, with the optical penetration depth at 1.2 eV, THz absorption and phase-matching (coherence) length, and crystal thickness stated; a circular-polarization and helicity test to bound inverse-Faraday and injection contributions; a non-magnetic isostructural or closely related reference measured under matched conditions; and the field tests of concern 1. Alternatively, an order-of-magnitude estimate should show that magnetic-dipole radiation from the inferred δM can account for the observed "several V/cm" while surface and quadrupole terms cannot.

3. The time scale of the proposed dynamics, the static Landau model and the size of the effect are not reconciled. The Introduction, Fig. 1 and Discussion describe H_EZ as driving the slower dynamics of the Cr³⁺ moments, yet the emitted waveforms are single-cycle pulses ~1 ps long with spectral weight at 0.3–1.5 THz (peaks 0.5–1.2 THz, Fig. 3d), close to the pump duration and detection bandwidth and far above the magnon/FMR frequencies of Cr2Ge2Te6 (tens of GHz, anisotropy fields ~0.5 T). Magnetic-dipole radiation requires d²M/dt² on that time scale, so the localized moments would have to tilt within the pump pulse; reorientation by a Zeeman-like torque proceeds by precession at γH_eff, and rotating M "completely into the plane" (p. 5) within ~1 ps would require fields of order tens to hundreds of tesla. The model, however, obtains M by minimizing a static free energy F(M) (Eq. 4) at each fluence, assuming the moments follow H_EZ adiabatically. The paper gives no estimate of J, χ(2) or α(2) (nor a comparison with theoretical values for related materials, refs. 4–9), of H_EZ in tesla, or of the δM (in μB per Cr, or as a tilt angle or fraction of M_s) needed to radiate the measured field. The authors should (a) estimate H_EZ and δM from the measured THz amplitude; (b) replace or support the static minimization with a Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert or time-dependent Landau calculation showing that the Cr moments respond on the ps time scale, and state what sets the emission bandwidth; (c) say whether any slower, GHz-scale component appears at longer delays (the traces end at about 2 ps); and (d) consider whether the radiating moment is instead the itinerant photocarrier spin/orbital density. That alternative would also explain the weak temperature dependence, but it would not amount to "manipulation of ferromagnetism".

B. Should address

4. The temperature dependence is not compared with the model and appears to contradict it. In Fig. 2f the THz spectral weight decreases on cooling and drops further below Tc, while emission at 200 K, deep in the paramagnetic phase, is the largest. In the Landau picture the induced in-plane moment above Tc is δM_y ≈ χ_⊥ H_EZ with χ_⊥ ∝ 1/(T − Tc) (r_z ∝ T − Tc), so the response should show a Curie-Weiss enhancement near Tc and fall off on warming; the data show the opposite. The text calls this a "crossover" (p. 5) but computes no temperature dependence. The authors should overlay the model's prediction at fixed H_EZ, with stated parameters, on Fig. 2f, and correct or normalize for the temperature dependence of the 1.2 eV absorption, the gap, carrier lifetime, THz refractive index and absorption and out-coupling (or show these are flat). Otherwise they should state which ingredient makes the emission grow at high T; if absorption changes dominate, the temperature dependence cannot be used as evidence for a magnetic origin.

5. Heating, absorption saturation and the "non-thermal" assignment are not controlled. Fluences up to 1.5 mJ/cm² at 1.2 eV (above gap) are used at 7 K with Tc ≈ 66 K. Transient electron, lattice and spin temperatures within the penetration depth may approach or exceed Tc, especially at high fluence where the 45° pattern rotates. Thermal demagnetization of M_z would produce the same "crossover from M_z-dominated to M_y-dominated" emission and mimic the reversal in Fig. 3; absorption bleaching, two-photon absorption and photocarrier screening could produce the normal-incidence saturation (Fig. 4c). The authors should give the absorbed energy density, the estimated electron and lattice temperature rise per pulse (from heat capacity and absorption depth), the repetition rate and steady-state heating; measure transmitted or reflected pump fluence to check for absorption saturation; and show whether the polarity reversal persists when the base temperature is raised at low fluence, or that the fluence dependence above Tc (no M_z) differs qualitatively from that at 7 K.

6. The model–data comparison is qualitative, but the abstract says the dependences are "quantitatively captured". Fig. 4b,d use arbitrary units for M and fluence (I0 ∝ |E0|²); r_0, r_z, u_z, g, J, χ(2) and the fluence-to-H_EZ scaling are not given in the main text; the gray curve in Fig. 4c is not specified (fit or prediction, which parameters free), and Fig. 4e has no model overlay. A saturating function fits many sublinear mechanisms, so the normal-incidence agreement alone is weak support. The assumption that THz spectral weight is proportional to |M_y| or a linear combination of M_y and M_z is not justified. The authors should list the parameters and how they were fixed (for example from the known anisotropy field and Tc), relate the model fluence axis to experimental fluence, overlay the model on Fig. 4c and 4e with the same parameters, and test whether one parameter set reproduces Fig. 4c, Fig. 4e and Fig. 2f together; in particular, whether the 45° crossover fluence (~0.2 mJ/cm²) follows from parameters constrained by the normal-incidence saturation. Otherwise "quantitatively" should be softened.

7. The two-component superposition test in Fig. 3d is not quantitatively justified. The 0.4 mJ/cm² waveform is compared with the unscaled sum E_0.1 + E_0.75 of waveforms measured at other fluences. In the proposed model the M_y and M_z contributions each scale with fluence in their own way (Fig. 4d), so the intermediate trace should be a fluence-weighted combination; the close match may be coincidental. The authors should fit the full fluence series with two fixed temporal basis waveforms and fluence-dependent amplitudes, aE_A(t) + bE_B(t) (for example by SVD or global fitting), and compare the extracted a(F) and b(F) with M_y(F) and M_z(F) in Fig. 4d.

8. Polarization-independent backgrounds and competing non-magnetic channels at 45° incidence. Ultrafast demagnetization of any net M_z, photo-Dember or surface-field currents, or an out-of-plane surface photocurrent accessible through E_z at oblique incidence give p-polarized emission that is isotropic or differently scaled in fluence. An isotropic term competing with a cos(2φ) term produces a two-lobed pattern whose apparent orientation changes as their relative weight changes with fluence, mimicking Fig. 3c without H_EZ rotating M; two non-magnetic mechanisms with different fluence scaling could likewise produce the polarity reversal. The authors should fit the 45° patterns in Fig. 3c (and at all fluences in Fig. 3d) to a Fourier decomposition A0 + A2 cos(2φ − 2δ2) + A4 cos(4φ − …), report each component versus fluence and field, and combine this with the field dependence of concern 1.

9. The out-coupling weights of M_y and M_z at 45° incidence are not derived. The interpretation of Figs. 3 and 4d,e relies on E_THz,x ∝ n_z M_y − n_y M_z. For a high-index material (n ≈ 3–4 at 1.2 eV) the refracted pump angle inside the crystal is only ~12–15°, and out-coupling of THz radiated by M_z also depends on the THz refractive index and Fresnel factors. The weights used to construct |M_y − M_z| in Fig. 4d should be derived from these; as plotted, the zero crossing depends on an unstated choice of weights and fluence scaling.

10. "Resonant" is asserted but not tested. The abstract and text call the effect a "resonant nonlinear Edelstein effect" (1.2 eV "near-resonant with the direct gap at Γ"), but only one photon energy is used. A pump photon-energy dependence (two or three wavelengths, including sub-gap), or at least a comparison with the absorption spectrum, is needed to show the response tracks interband absorption as the Edelstein mechanism requires; otherwise the wording should be changed.

11. The nonlinear Edelstein effect is not distinguished from other second-order magneto-optical routes. The inverse Cotton-Mouton effect and impulsive stimulated Raman excitation of magnons also couple E⊗E to the magnetization. The paper says the inverse Cotton-Mouton effect "requires time-reversal symmetry breaking" (pp. 1–2), yet the signal persists well above Tc. The authors should explain which observable separates a dissipative, interband-coherence-driven nonlinear Edelstein response from these alternatives, for example the sign and phase of α(2), the photon-energy dependence, or the temporal profile relative to the pump envelope.
Raised by: r3

12. The crystal-axis reference and polarization artifacts are not controlled. The pump polarization is rotated with a half-wave plate; small changes in transmitted power, ellipticity or beam pointing with wave-plate angle can produce twofold or fourfold modulations. Rotating the sample azimuth at fixed polarization would control for this and show whether the pattern is tied to the crystal axes (δ) or the lab frame. The Methods do not say how the a-axis was determined (Laue, XRD, edge facets). The authors should give the relation between δ and the ratio α_x,xx/α_x,xy (Eq. 3) explicitly, and report the fitted δ with its uncertainty at each temperature and fluence, to support the statement that the orientation is unchanged.

13. Samples, reproducibility and characterization. The paper does not state how many crystals or spots were measured, their thickness relative to the optical penetration depth, whether surfaces were freshly cleaved and how long they were exposed to air (Cr2Ge2Te6 surfaces degrade), or whether the fourfold pattern (Fig. 2d), the 45° reversal and 90° rotation (Fig. 3c) and the temperature dependence (Fig. 2f) were reproduced on more than one sample or after thermal cycling.
