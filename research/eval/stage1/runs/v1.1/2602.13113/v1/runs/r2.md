Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports time-resolved resonant diffuse scattering (tr-RDS) at the Cu L3 edge near the (½ 0 −½) magnetic Bragg peak of CuO and presents it as a new momentum-resolved, energy-integrated probe of non-equilibrium low-energy magnons. The authors combine it with tr-RXD, ESRF tr-NRDS of phonons near (0 0 −2), and a quantum-Boltzmann treatment of magnon–magnon and magnon–phonon scattering. From these they build a three-stage picture of sublattice demagnetization: femtosecond spin disorder, picosecond magnon quasi-thermalization towards Γ, and nanosecond recovery through anomalous (two-magnon → one-phonon) scattering limited by dispersion mismatch. If the diffuse signal is shown to be magnon scattering, the technique is a real advance that complements tr-RIXS at low energy, and the hierarchy would interest the ultrafast-magnetism community. As the paper stands, the identification of the signal and the assignment of the nanosecond recovery mechanism are less secure than the text says.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- A new experimental observable: a pump-induced, momentum-dependent increase of resonant scattering in the tails of a magnetic Bragg peak, with a rise time that depends on momentum (Fig. 3F, 3I). Few probes give this kind of momentum-resolved access to the low-energy magnon population.
- Several checks are consistent with a magnetic origin: resonance at Cu L3 (Fig. 2E), π > σ polarization dependence (Fig. 2G), a fluence threshold (Fig. 2F), amplitude symmetric about q_RXD (Fig. 3F), and anisotropy between b* and [h 0 h] (Fig. S9). The incommensurate-phase alternative is tested explicitly (Fig. S7, Supplementary Text).
- The quantum-kinetic modelling is microscopic: magnon excitation through doublon spin–charge coupling, a QBE with magnon–magnon vertices from fourth-order Holstein–Primakoff, and magnon–phonon normal and anomalous channels. It reproduces the qualitative trend that the RDS rise slows as q approaches Γ (Figs. 3G/H, S16).
- Three complementary probes are applied to the same crystal, covering 50 fs to µs.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **A change in the Bragg-peak lineshape is not ruled out as the source of the "RDS" upturn.** The diffuse signal is measured 0.01–0.08 r.l.u. from a magnetic Bragg peak whose top saturates the detector (Fig. 2C caption; hatched region in Fig. 3). The paper excludes a change in correlation length because "the RXD peak width remains nearly constant" (p. 4, Fig. 2C). With the peak top missing, however, the width and integrated intensity cannot be determined from Fig. 2C. A second mechanism is also plausible. The pump penetrates ~450 nm while the resonant probe samples only ~18 nm (Methods), so a strongly inhomogeneous strain field is launched at the surface and crosses the probed layer in a few picoseconds. Strain and lattice-parameter gradients, or mosaic changes, broaden the magnetic peak and move intensity from the core into the tails. That redistribution would be resonant and would show the same π/σ ratio as the magnetic peak (Fig. 2G vs. Fig. S5B). It would also appear only above the demagnetization threshold (Fig. 2F), be roughly symmetric about q_RXD, differ between b* and [h 0 h], and rise on picosecond times that depend on q. Every control in Fig. 2 is therefore also consistent with a lineshape change of the magnetic Bragg peak. To make the main claim stand, the authors should:
   - (a) measure the unsaturated magnetic peak profile (with attenuation, or with the Jungfrau detector that was already used at high fluence) before and after the pump, and test whether the tail gain is matched by a narrowing or broadening of the core, i.e. whether integrated intensity is conserved;
   - (b) take the same q-resolved traces around a structural (charge) Bragg reflection, or off resonance on a charge peak, to test for strain-induced tails;
   - (c) show that the upturn does not appear at an equivalent offset from the Bragg position when the magnetic peak is absent (T > T_N2), or give a quantitative argument against strain broadening at the observed amplitude.

2. **The nanosecond recovery is assigned to magnon–phonon anomalous scattering without excluding thermal control by heat transport.** The RXD and RDS recover with τ ≈ 7 ns (Fig. 4D). The paper attributes this to an intrinsic magnon–phonon bottleneck and extracts an "effective magnon-phonon coupling ~59 µeV" from it (p. 9). A simpler alternative is that, after picosecond magnon–phonon quasi-equilibration, the magnetic order in the 18 nm probed layer follows the local lattice temperature, which relaxes as heat diffuses out of a ~450 nm excited layer that is strongly graded within the probe depth. That would also give nanosecond recovery, and the paper does not test it. The supporting tr-NRDS evidence is indirect:
   - it was taken at a different facility with ~100 ps pulses, 1 kHz repetition rate, an 800 nm-derived pump, and a ~1.5 µm probe depth versus 18 nm for RXD;
   - the "AFM vs PM" contrast compares 190 K with 300 K, which also changes the phonon population, heat capacity and thermal conductivity, not only the magnetic state;
   - the enhancement peaks near 2 ns while the magnon annihilation it is supposed to reflect has τ ≈ 7 ns, and the fit function for the blue curve in Fig. 4D is not given.

   The authors should: estimate the lattice temperature in the probed layer against delay from the absorbed fluence, heat capacity and thermal diffusivity of CuO, and compare the expected thermal (quasi-equilibrium) suppression of the RXD intensity with Fig. 4D; specify the NRDS fit model and its parameters; and either measure tr-NRDS near T_N under conditions matched to the RXD (fluence, repetition rate, probe depth, or a temperature just above T_N1 rather than 300 K) or soften the claim that the NRDS feature shows phonon creation by magnon annihilation. Unless this is done, the "recovery bottleneck from dispersion mismatch" should be presented as a hypothesis consistent with the data.

**B. Should address**

3. **The model's demagnetization amplitude does not match the data.** The excitation amplitude B0Δn(t) was "chosen to reproduce the experimentally observed reduction of the AFM order by approximately 50%" (p. S13). At the fluence used for Figs. 3 and 4 (3 mJ/cm²), Fig. 2D shows an RXD intensity reduction of ~28%. Because RXD intensity scales roughly as the square of the sublattice magnetization, that corresponds to a ~15% reduction of the order parameter. The authors should state which quantity was matched, recalibrate if necessary, and show how sensitive the simulated magnon distribution and RDS time constants are to this amplitude.

4. **The comparison between theory and experiment is qualitative.** Figs. 3E and 3G are compared only by eye; their colour scales differ (experimental ±1.7 a.u. against simulated 0–800). Fig. 3I calls its solid lines "guide to the eyes", and simulated τ(q) values are not overlaid, even though the text claims the scattering rate is proportional to momentum. The authors should overlay simulated and measured 1/τ_RDS(q) and normalized A_RDS(q) along both b* and [h 0 h], with any free parameters (Dirac-delta kernel width, B0Δn) stated.

5. **The origin of Fig. 3J–N is unclear.** The caption says these magnon distributions are "derived from tr-RDS data using Eq. 2", but the text (p. 7) and Fig. S15 present them as simulations, and they extend to ω ≈ 20 meV and |q| = 0.1 r.l.u., beyond the measured range. Inverting energy-integrated data with Eq. 2 would also need the A_μ(q) prefactors, an absolute normalization, and the separation of the two branches. The authors should clarify whether these panels are data or simulation. If they are data, they should describe the inversion and its assumptions, including how the ~500 K "hot-magnon temperature" (p. S9) is obtained from arbitrary-unit intensities.

6. **The separation into RXD and RDS components may not be robust near the Bragg peak.** Eq. S3 writes both components as A·exp(−t/τ), which describes a decay; a rising RDS component needs a (1 − e^{−t/τ}) form. The fitted τ_RDS also reaches ~4 ps in Fig. 3F and ~13 ps in Fig. S9E, while the data window is only 4 ps. Near q_RXD the RXD component is several times larger than the RDS component, so the two amplitudes are likely to be strongly covariant. Fig. S8 additionally shows that the RDS overshoots and partly decays by ~10 ps, which a single exponential cannot describe. The authors should:
   - state the exact fit function and whether τ_RXD was shared across q;
   - give parameter correlations or uncertainties;
   - show that the linear 1/τ ∝ |q| trend survives longer delay windows.

   The authors should also state whether the avalanche photodiodes (APDs) respond linearly at the count rates close to the peak. A saturated detector would distort I_ON − I_OFF exactly where the largest RDS amplitudes are reported.

7. **The model rests on assumptions that need justification.**
   - It uses a spin-1/2 linear spin-wave plus QBE description at 190 K, about 0.9 T_N1, and reports hot-magnon temperatures of ~500 K, above T_N. Magnon damping and renormalization are strong in this regime. The authors should discuss the validity of the quasiparticle and Boltzmann treatment there.
   - The two magnon branches are degenerate in the simplified orthorhombic model (p. S13), yet the main text uses the 1.3 meV acoustic and 23.2 meV optical gaps to argue that the signal comes from the acoustic branch (p. 6). The model as described (D = 0.025 meV) does not contain that branch splitting, so this argument rests on literature values rather than on the calculation.

8. **The magnon–phonon rates are not quantitative.** The coupling constants were fixed at c^x = 1 eV/Å for both the normal and the anomalous channels, all masses were replaced by a single Cu mass, and only the initial rate at t = 0 was computed (pp. S17–S18). Absolute rates scale as c², and the normal/anomalous ratio depends on assuming equal c^x. The paper says the ~59 µeV effective coupling was "estimated from the magnon relaxation timescale" but does not give the procedure. The authors should explain that estimate, state its relation to c^x, and give the uncertainty of the conclusion that anomalous scattering with the lowest optical phonon dominates, for example under variation of c^anomalous/c^normal.

9. **Only one sample, temperature and fluence condition is shown for the momentum-resolved data.** All tr-RDS data are from one crystal at 190 K, and the q-resolved maps are at one fluence. The magnon picture predicts that the RDS amplitude and the q-dependence of the rise time vary with the equilibrium Bose population and with fluence. q-scans at a second temperature (e.g. ~100–150 K) or a second fluence would test it directly. The paper should also state whether the effect was reproduced on a second sample or a second spot, given the twin domains shown in Fig. S4.

10. **The scope of the claims exceeds the evidence.** The abstract and conclusions state that the framework "transcends phenomenological models and generalizes across materials", applies "to any gapped magnetic insulators", and enables "attosecond-scale investigations" (pp. 2, 9, 10). Only CuO is studied, and the recovery mechanism is not yet tested against thermal alternatives (concern 2). These statements should be reworded as outlook. The claim that tr-RDS of magnons "has not yet been experimentally demonstrated" should be supported by a discussion of prior time-resolved magnetic diffuse or small-angle scattering work.

**C. Minor**

11. Fig. 2 has no full caption heading in the figure itself (the caption continues on p. 13). Fig. 2E states that the RDS energy scan was taken at (½, −0.08, −½), a momentum where Fig. 3F shows A_RDS close to zero. Please confirm the momentum and explain why that point was chosen.
12. Fig. 4D NRDS: specify the ROI and the fit function, give the number of independent delays, and explain the "short-lived ~160 ps peak" given the ~100 ps time resolution.
13. Fig. S11 supports the claim of µs phonon dissipation dominated by electronic thermal conductivity (conclusion item 4) with four points per phase and error bars comparable to the signal. Mark this claim as tentative.
14. Fig. S6 caption refers to "the D point (see main text)", but the main text never defines a D point. Also, the right half of Fig. S6 uses the axis label [½+q 0 −½+q], whereas the main text writes [h 0 h].
15. The sample temperature during the tr-RDS measurement (190 K) is close to T_N1 = 213–214.5 K. Give the estimated average temperature rise at 3 mJ/cm² explicitly in the main text, since it bears on Fig. 2F and on the incommensurate-phase argument.
16. Typographical issues: "Sacttering" (p. S18), "Assmining" (p. S15), "indidcated" (Fig. S16), a stray comma before Ĥ_mag−ph in Eq. S22, the repeated F_xy label (should be F_zy) in Eq. S37, the duplicated Refs. 63/71, "Mentink1" in Ref. 29, and the malformed chemical formula in Ref. 22.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add one figure that rules out a Bragg-lineshape change directly (concern 1): unsaturated peak profiles before and after the pump, an integrated-intensity balance, and a charge-peak control measured under the same conditions.
2. Add a thermal-transport model of the probed 18 nm layer and overlay its predicted RXD recovery on Fig. 4D, so the magnon–phonon bottleneck can be distinguished from heat diffusion.
3. Overlay simulated and measured τ_RDS(q) and A_RDS(q) along both crystallographic directions, using a demagnetization amplitude matched to Fig. 2D.
4. Extend the q-resolved tr-RDS data to longer delays and to a second temperature, so that the rise times are constrained and the dependence on the Bose factor is tested.

**5. Verdict**

Verdict: major revision
