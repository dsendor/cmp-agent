Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors present time-resolved resonant diffuse scattering (tr-RDS) at the Cu L3 edge as a new way to follow low-energy magnon populations with momentum resolution after photoexcitation, and apply it to the collinear antiferromagnet CuO at SwissFEL. They combine it with tr-RXD, with tr-NRDS at ESRF, and with a quantum Boltzmann (QBE) treatment of magnon-magnon and magnon-phonon scattering. From this they build a three-stage picture: femtosecond spin disorder from doublons, picosecond magnon quasi-thermalization towards the zone centre, and nanosecond recovery through anomalous magnon-phonon scattering limited by phase space. If the RDS signal is shown to be magnon diffuse scattering and not a change in the Bragg-peak profile, a momentum-resolved, energy-integrated probe of non-equilibrium magnons would be a useful and broadly applicable addition to ultrafast X-ray methods.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First reported attempt to use energy-integrated resonant diffuse scattering around a magnetic Bragg peak as a time- and momentum-resolved probe of magnon populations. The photon-count advantage over tr-RIXS is real and is argued well.
- The paper offers several signatures that the upturn signal is magnetic: resonant enhancement at Cu L3 (Fig. 2E), a π/σ ratio like that of the magnetic Bragg peak (Fig. 2G and Fig. S5B), a fluence threshold that coincides with the onset of demagnetization (Fig. 2F), amplitudes symmetric in ±q (Fig. 3F), and anisotropy between b* and [h 0 h] (Fig. S9).
- The momentum-dependent rise time (Fig. 3F, 3I) is a clear experimental observation, and the RXD time constant stays the same at every q (Fig. 3D). Together these separate global demagnetization from a q-dependent redistribution.
- Three X-ray probes across fs to µs time scales give a broad view of where the energy goes. The AFM-versus-PM comparison in tr-NRDS (Fig. 4D) is a sensible attempt at a null control.
- The theory is microscopic: a spin-charge coupled excitation, a QBE with a fourth-order Holstein-Primakoff magnon-magnon vertex, and normal and anomalous magnon-phonon terms. It is set out in detail in the Supplementary Text.
- The incommensurate-phase alternative is examined explicitly with 2D detector data (Fig. S7) and is distinguished well.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The RDS upturn has not been separated from a pump-induced change in the magnetic Bragg-peak profile.** This matters because the headline novelty is that tr-RDS measures magnon diffuse scattering.
   - The resonance (Fig. 2E) and polarization (Fig. 2G) tests show only that the extra intensity is magnetic scattering. Any change in the shape of the (1/2 0 −1/2) peak itself would inherit the same Cu L3 resonance and the same π/σ ratio. Examples are inhomogeneous strain, a lattice-parameter change, a change in the absorption length (which sets the peak width for an 18 nm probe depth), or a depth profile of the order parameter. So these tests do not discriminate between the two pictures.
   - The only direct argument against a profile change is that "the RXD peak width remains nearly constant" (Fig. 2C). But the top of the peak in Fig. 2C is missing because the detector saturated (caption). The profile is shown at a single delay (3 ps) and only along b*.
   - Picosecond strain is a specific worry. The pump deposits energy within about 450 nm. The probe depth is about 18 nm, which a strain front crosses in a few ps at typical CuO sound velocities. That is the same time scale as the RDS rise. Strain broadens the peak mainly along the surface normal, close to the specular (1/2 0 −1/2) direction. That direction is not scanned, and the APD acceptance may integrate over it.
   - The linear-in-q rate (Fig. 3I) has a slope of 1/τ ≈ 5 ps⁻¹ at q ≈ 0.075 r.l.u. along b* (≈0.14 Å⁻¹). This is about 4 km/s if 1/τ is taken as an angular frequency, a value in the range of acoustic sound velocities. It is about 24 km/s if a factor 2π is included, closer to the magnon velocity of the model dispersion (Fig. S13C). The authors should state which mechanism the slope is consistent with.
   - What to do:
     - Use the 0.5M Jungfrau detector, which the authors already used for high-fluence RXD (Methods; Fig. S7), or an attenuated APD scan, to show the full unsaturated peak profile around (1/2 0 −1/2) along b*, [h 0 h] and the specular direction, at several delays from 0 to 10 ps at 3 mJ/cm².
     - Report the fitted peak position, width and integrated intensity against delay.
     - State the momentum acceptance of each APD in r.l.u. along all three directions.
     - Estimate the strain-induced profile change expected from the known sound velocities and the pump and probe depths, and show that it is small compared with the RDS amplitudes in Fig. 3F.

B. Should address

2. **The theory and the data are compared only qualitatively.** The text says the simulated tr-RDS (Fig. 3G) "reproduces" the measured map (Fig. 3E), and that the scattering rate "is proportional to momentum" (Fig. 3I). However:
   - The line in Fig. 3I is described as a guide to the eye. No simulated 1/τ(q) is overlaid.
   - Fig. 3G is on a different intensity scale (0–800) and a longer time window (6 ps).
   - The excitation strength B₀Δn was chosen to give a "reduction of the AFM order by approximately 50%" (Supplementary Text, below Eq. S13). Fig. 2D shows a ≈28% drop in RXD intensity at the fluence used. Since the intensity scales roughly as the square of the sublattice moment, this is a moment reduction of about 15%. The model may therefore inject several times too many magnons. That would change the magnon-magnon scattering rates, which depend strongly on occupation.

   What to do:
   - Re-run the simulation with the excitation set by the measured RXD drop.
   - Convolve with the experimental momentum resolution.
   - Fit the simulated traces with the same two-component model (Eq. S3).
   - Overlay the simulated amplitudes and 1/τ(q) on Fig. 3F and 3I, along b* and along [h 0 h] (Fig. S9E).

   It would also help to say whether the linear-in-q dependence is expected from the model. The authors invoke hydrodynamic slowdown (ref. 41), but diffusive relaxation would give 1/τ ∝ q², not q.

3. **The evidence that recovery goes through anomalous magnon-phonon scattering into the lowest optical phonons is mostly from the model, and the experimental link is indirect.**
   - The tr-NRDS null control compares the AFM phase at 190 K with the PM phase at 300 K (Fig. 4D, Fig. S11). These differ in thermal phonon population, heat capacity, thermal expansion and Bragg intensity, not only in magnetic order.
   - tr-NRDS probes about 1.5 µm with a 100 ps probe at 1 kHz. The pump penetrates about 500 nm. tr-RDS probes about 18 nm at 100 Hz. The two measurements therefore average over very different excitation densities, and Methods describes the tr-NRDS pump source as a picosecond laser.
   - The "broad enhancement around 2 ns" rests on a handful of delays (≈1, 3, 5, 7 and 10 ns in Fig. S10C).
   - In the model, all mode-specific couplings are replaced by one constant, c^x = 1 eV/Å, for both the normal and anomalous terms (Supplementary Text, after Eq. S25). The result that the lowest optical phonons dominate (Fig. 4E–F, Fig. S17) then reflects phase space only. The real polarization-vector and exchange-gradient factors could reorder the channels.
   - The method used to extract the effective coupling of ≈59 µeV from the 7 ns time constant is not given. Its comparison with the 6.2 meV spin-phonon coupling from Raman (ref. 44) compares different quantities.

   What to do:
   - Measure a PM reference just above T_N2 (about 235 K), or show the AFM tr-NRDS at a fluence below the demagnetization threshold.
   - Add tr-NRDS delays between 1 and 5 ns.
   - Show that a single rate-equation model with magnon decay rate 1/τ ≈ 1/7 ns reproduces the tr-NRDS rise and decay together.
   - Show how the choice between optical and acoustic channels changes when c^x varies by mode.
   - Give the formula behind the 59 µeV value.

4. **The data do not show "non-thermal magnons throughout reciprocal space", and the RDS signal is not converted into magnon occupations.**
   - The abstract states the non-thermal, zone-wide initial distribution as an observation. The data cover only |q| ≤ 0.08 r.l.u. around q_RXD, excluding the hatched region near the peak. The zone-wide distribution comes from the model (Fig. S14B).
   - The caption of Fig. 3J–N says the magnon density was "derived from tr-RDS data using Eq. 2". The main text attributes the same panels to the simulations ("Our simulations reveal… (Figs. 3J–3N…)"). The q range of ±0.1 also goes beyond the measured range.
   - The Supplementary Text quotes hot-magnon temperatures of about 500 K at (0.5, 0.1, −0.5). That point lies outside the Fig. 3 data, and no calibration from RDS intensity to f(q) is described.

   What to do:
   - Measure the static RDS intensity against temperature (for example 100–210 K) at the same q points. This would calibrate intensity against equilibrium magnon occupation and would show whether the transient state at a given q matches any equilibrium temperature. That comparison is the direct test of "non-thermal".
   - State plainly which panels are data and which are simulation.
   - Reword the abstract to reflect what is measured.

5. **The sample sits close to T_N1, and the fluence calibration is internally inconsistent.**
   - All RDS data are taken at 190 K. The paper puts T_N1 at 213 K (Methods) or 214.5 K (Fig. S3). It estimates the heating at 3 mJ/cm² as "below 20 K". The excited region may therefore be within a few kelvin of the transition, where equilibrium critical magnetic diffuse scattering changes rapidly with temperature.
   - Fig. 2F gives an RXD reduction of about −0.5 at log f ≈ 3.3 (≈2 mJ/cm²) at 4.5 ps. Fig. 2D gives about −0.28 at 3 mJ/cm² at 4 ps. The RXD drop should not shrink as fluence rises.
   - The explanation that the RDS signal falls at high fluence "because the RXD intensity reduction is larger at temperatures closer to the AFM transition temperature" is hard to follow without numbers.

   What to do:
   - Reconcile the fluence scales. If the data come from different beamtimes, say so and give the spot size and absorbed fluence for each.
   - Give the heating estimate with the reflectivity and heat capacity used.
   - Repeat the momentum-resolved RDS at a lower base temperature, for example 100–150 K. Magnon-magnon scattering rates depend strongly on the thermal magnon density, so the predicted temperature dependence of τ_RDS(q) is a sharp test of the quasi-thermalization mechanism. It would also move the measurement away from critical fluctuations.

6. **Some fitted time constants lie well beyond the measured window.**
   - Fig. 3 uses a 4 ps window. Yet τ_RDS near q = 0.01 r.l.u. reaches the top of the axis in Fig. 3F, and in Fig. S9E it reaches about 13 ps.
   - Fig. S8 shows that the trace at (0.48, 0.01, −0.52) is not monotonic: it peaks at about 5 ps and plateaus after about 10 ps. A single rising exponential cannot capture that.
   - As a result, the small-q points in Fig. 3I, which carry the "∝ q" claim, are extrapolations.

   What to do:
   - Show fits over a longer window wherever such data exist (as in Fig. S8).
   - Report how stable τ_RDS is when the fit window changes.
   - Mark points whose τ exceeds the window.

7. **Long-lived photocarriers are excluded only by citing cuprate measurements.** Magnon-electron scattering is ruled out "since the electrons are already thermalized within femtosecond timescales (30, 31)", but both references concern cuprates. In CuO, charge-transfer excitations may persist as polarons, excitons or trapped carriers for picoseconds or longer. The Supplementary Text itself invokes significant electronic thermal conductivity from defects. Continued magnon generation by carrier recombination would also give a picosecond rise in RDS. Transient optical reflectivity or absorption on the same crystal at 400 nm pump and 190 K would show how long the carriers live.

8. **The simplified model is asked to explain features it does not contain.**
   - The calculation uses an orthorhombic cell with one Cu per layer (Fig. S13A).
   - The geometric factors F_αα′ (Eq. S37) assume spherically symmetric Cu sites. The Cu²⁺ d(x²−y²) hole makes the L3 scattering tensor strongly anisotropic.
   - Even so, the main text attributes the much larger RDS amplitude along [h 0 h] than along b* (Fig. S9E against Fig. 3F) to "anisotropy in magnon dispersion as expected from the symmetry".

   What to do:
   - Show whether the model reproduces the measured amplitude ratio between the two directions.
   - Discuss how the spherical-site approximation affects the comparison of q dependence.

9. **The generality claims rest on one crystal, one temperature and one compound.** Examples are "transcends phenomenological models and generalizes across materials", "generalizable to any gapped magnetic insulators", "establishing design principles", and "in principle, attosecond-scale investigations". All RDS data come from one CuO crystal at 190 K, and the "faster recovery with overlapping dispersions" principle is not tested. Either tone these statements down or add a comparison: a second sample, a second temperature, or a material with a different overlap between the magnon and phonon dispersions. The authors should also state how many crystals and beamtimes contributed. Fig. S4 shows twin domains, so it should be confirmed that the RDS results were reproduced at a second spot or on a second sample.

C. Minor

10. As written, Eq. S3 is a sum of decaying exponentials, A exp(−t/τ). That does not describe a step drop followed by a rise. Give the actual model used, presumably a step convolved with the time resolution times [1 − exp(−t/τ)] terms, and state which parameters were free and which fixed.
11. The transition temperatures are quoted inconsistently: T_N1 = 213 K and T_N2 = 230 K in Methods, against 214.5 K and 230.5 K in Fig. S3. The supplement also quotes a "bulk quasi-equilibrium limit of ~210 K".
12. In Fig. 2F, write the axis as log₁₀ and mark the 3 mJ/cm² fluence used elsewhere. Figs. 2 and 3 carry their captions on the following page with only a title under the figure. Fig. 3G uses an arbitrary scale (0–800) that should be explained.
13. The text says the scattering rate "is proportional to momentum" (Fig. 3I), while the caption calls the lines a guide to the eye. Either fit the data or soften the statement.
14. Typos and slips:
    - "time-resonant diffuse scattering" (Methods)
    - "q_RDS" for q_RXD in the Supplementary Text on the incommensurate state
    - "Sacttering", "Assmining", "supression", "indidcated"
    - a stray comma before Ĥ_mag−ph in Eq. S22
    - "F_xy" written twice in Eq. S37, where the last row should read F_zy
    - refs. 63 and 71 are duplicates
    - ref. 48 has a garbled author list
    - ref. 29 reads "Mentink1"
    - "La0.5Sr1.5MnO4" in ref. 22 is badly formatted
15. In Fig. S10, panels 2 and 4 (0.15 and 0.6 ns) show a dipolar red/blue pattern from a Bragg-peak shift. The text says the region of interest avoids it, but the early tr-NRDS peak (the ~160 ps component) sits at the instrument resolution of about 100 ps. A quoted 160 ps time constant should be described as limited by the resolution.
16. The XAS in Fig. 2E is measured in total fluorescence yield and is distorted by self-absorption. Note this, since the comparison of the RDS resonance with the XAS peak position supports the magnetic assignment.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Use the 2D detector data already in hand to show the delay-dependent, unsaturated profile of the (1/2 0 −1/2) peak in three directions. This is the strongest available way to establish that tr-RDS measures magnon diffuse scattering, and it would also characterize the momentum resolution.
2. Add an equilibrium temperature series of the RDS intensity at the measured q points. This calibrates intensity against magnon occupation and turns the "non-thermal" and "hot-magnon temperature" statements into measured quantities.
3. Put the simulated 1/τ(q) and amplitudes on the same axes as the data (Fig. 3F, 3I and Fig. S9E), using an excitation strength fixed by the measured RXD drop.
4. Take the momentum-resolved tr-RDS at a second base temperature, and add a PM tr-NRDS reference just above T_N2. Both test the proposed scattering hierarchy through its predicted temperature dependence.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
