Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper introduces time-resolved resonant diffuse scattering (tr-RDS) at the Cu L3 edge as a momentum-resolved probe of low-energy magnon populations after photoexcitation. It applies the method to the antiferromagnetic insulator CuO, together with tr-RXD, tr-NRDS (phonons) and quantum-Boltzmann simulations. The authors propose a three-stage picture: femtosecond spin disorder from doublon creation, picosecond magnon quasi-thermalization by magnon-magnon scattering toward the Γ point, and nanosecond recovery by anomalous (two-magnon to one-phonon) scattering, which is held back by the mismatch between the magnon and phonon dispersions. If the diffuse signal is shown to be magnon scattering, this is a real methodological advance: it complements tr-RIXS at low energy, and the combined spin-lattice picture is of broad interest.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First demonstration, as far as I know, of a pump-induced diffuse signal around a magnetic Bragg peak at an XFEL. It is collected at the same time as the Bragg intensity (Fig. 2D), which allows a direct comparison.
- Several checks point to a magnetic, resonant origin of the upturn: the resonance profile (Fig. 2E), π/σ polarization dependence close to that of the magnetic Bragg peak (Fig. 2G and Fig. S5), a fluence threshold that matches demagnetization (Fig. 2F), and amplitude symmetric about q_RXD along b* (Fig. 3F).
- The time constants depend strongly on momentum (Fig. 3F and Fig. S9E), while the RXD time constants do not (Fig. 3D). Taken at face value, this is a direct signature of redistribution in reciprocal space.
- The microscopic modelling is extensive. Photodoping is mapped onto magnon creation (Eqs. S5–S13), there is a magnon-magnon QBE with a quartic vertex (S15–S20), magnon-phonon normal and anomalous terms (S21–S27), and a cross-section derivation (S30–S36).
- Data cover femtoseconds to microseconds, and the AFM is compared with the PM phase for the phonon channel (Fig. 4D and Fig. S11).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The upturn is not separated from a thermal (critical) increase in diffuse scattering, or from a change in the Bragg line shape.** The central claim is that the upturn measures a non-thermal magnon distribution that then quasi-thermalizes. The paper does not exclude simpler explanations.
   - (a) **Heating close to T_N1.** The main data are taken at 190 K and 3 mJ/cm². The authors' own estimate of the temperature rise is "below 20 K" (Supplementary Text, "Transient incommensurate state"). With T_N1 = 213 K (or 214.5 K in Fig. S3), the probed 18 nm surface layer, where excitation is strongest, therefore ends up within a few kelvin of T_N1. Near T_N1, equilibrium critical diffuse scattering around (½ 0 −½) grows steeply. It would be resonant, π-enhanced and symmetric about q_RXD, which are exactly the signatures used in Figs. 2E–G and 3F to argue for magnons. The fall of the upturn above 3 mJ/cm² (Fig. 2F) is itself attributed to the approach to T_N, so heating is already part of the authors' reading.
   - (b) **Line-shape changes.** A small broadening or shape change of the magnetic Bragg peak would also raise the tails symmetrically. Possible causes are inhomogeneous strain from the pump-induced stress within the 18 nm probe depth (pump depth about 450 nm), or a reduced correlation length. The argument that the width "remains nearly constant" (Fig. 2C) is weak. The peak top is saturated, and the region within about ±0.0125 r.l.u. is not measured (hatched in Fig. 3), so the width cannot be extracted from these data.

   What to do:
   - Measure the static diffuse intensity around (½ 0 −½) as a function of temperature from 190 K to above T_N1, along both b* and [h 0 h], at the same energy and polarization. Compare it quantitatively with the pumped tails at late delays (10–100 ps, Fig. S8). If the system has quasi-thermalized, the late-delay q-profile should match an equilibrium profile at some elevated temperature. Matching or not matching at early delays is what would show the distribution is non-thermal. The same measurement would also give an absolute calibration for converting RDS intensity into f(q,t) (see concern 4).
   - Give the instrumental momentum resolution along b* and [h 0 h]. The APD acceptance is not specified. Show that points at |q| = 0.01–0.02 r.l.u. are not dominated by the resolution tail of a saturated Bragg peak.
   - Provide a null measurement: tr-RDS at the same q in the PM phase, above T_N2, or in the ICM phase at the commensurate position, where the commensurate magnon picture predicts no upturn.

2. **The evidence linking magnon annihilation to phonon creation on nanoseconds is indirect, and its timescales do not obviously agree.** The headline "nanosecond recovery via momentum-selective magnon-phonon scattering" rests on four pieces of evidence. None of them establishes the mechanism quantitatively.
   - (a) **Mismatched experiments.** The tr-NRDS data (Fig. 4D, top) come from a different experiment: ESRF, 1 kHz, about 100 ps resolution, 8.7 keV, grazing incidence, probe depth about 1.5 µm against a pump depth of about 500 nm. The X-ray probe therefore averages over a strongly inhomogeneous and partly unexcited volume. The RXS probe depth is 18 nm. The excitation densities and fluence calibration of the two measurements need to be matched explicitly.
   - (b) **Kinetics.** The NRDS enhancement peaks at about 1–2 ns and has largely decayed by 10 ns. The RDS and RXD recover with τ ≈ 7 ns, and the phonon dissipation is said to take about 0.7 µs (Fig. S11). If phonons are fed by a 7 ns magnon decay and removed on microseconds, the phonon diffuse signal should keep rising over about 7 ns, not peak at 2 ns. Some intermediate process, such as phonon redistribution out of the region of interest, is needed but is not discussed.
   - (c) **PM control.** The PM control is at 300 K, not just above T_N2. The phonon populations, Debye-Waller factor and absorbed energy all differ, so the absence of the bump at 300 K does not isolate the magnetic channel. A measurement at about 235–250 K would.
   - (d) **Theory.** The simulation fixes c^x = 1 eV/Å for both normal and anomalous coupling (p. S17). So the absolute rates in Fig. 4E/F, and any agreement with the ~7 ns timescale, follow from that choice. Only the momentum structure of the rates is a genuine prediction. The "~59 µeV effective coupling" (p. 9) has no stated definition or derivation.

   What to do:
   - Build a simple rate-equation model with magnon, phonon-in-ROI and bath reservoirs that fits RXD, RDS and NRDS together, using the measured excitation densities.
   - Add an NRDS temperature point just above T_N2.
   - Derive or bound c^x independently, for example from the magnetoelastic or spin-phonon couplings of Ref. 44. Show the sensitivity of the predicted recovery time to it, and give the definition and calculation of the 59 µeV value.
   - Estimate quantitatively the lattice-cooling and heat-diffusion contribution to the RXD recovery, so that recovery limited by thermal transport is excluded.

B. Should address

3. **The two-exponential decomposition is underdetermined where it matters most.**
   - Eq. S3 as written is a sum of decaying exponentials. It cannot describe a step-like drop plus a rising component, so the fitted form is presumably different (for example 1 − exp, convolved with the 60 fs resolution and a step at t₀). Please state the actual function, which parameters were free, and how t₀ and the resolution were handled.
   - At small |q|, τ_RDS reaches about 4 ps along b* (Fig. 3F) and about 13 ps along [h 0 h] (Fig. S9E), while the fit window is 4 ps. Over such a window the rise is close to linear, so only A/τ is constrained and A and τ are strongly correlated.
   - Fig. S8 shows the trace is non-monotonic, with a maximum around 5 ps, so a single rising exponential is not the right model beyond the window.
   - This bears directly on the claim that "the scattering rate (1/τ_RDS) is proportional to momentum" (p. 7, Fig. 3I). The caption calls the lines a "guide to the eyes", and the outermost points have large error bars.
   - Please give fit covariances, refit with longer delay windows where data exist, and either fit 1/τ ∝ |q| with a stated uncertainty or soften the statement.

4. **The magnon distributions in Fig. 3J–N need their derivation shown.** The caption says these are "derived from tr-RDS data using Eq. 2". Several things are unclear:
   - The panels are smooth, extend to ±0.1 r.l.u. and ω ≈ 20 meV, cover the unmeasured hatched region, and share the colour scale (0–13) of the simulated f in Fig. 3H.
   - RDS intensities are in arbitrary units. How was the absolute occupation obtained?
   - How were A_µ(q) and Ā_µ(q) evaluated, and with which polarization factors (Eq. S37)?
   - How was the static (f + 1) term handled when taking ON − OFF?
   - What was done in the gap at |q| < 0.0125?

   If the panels are in fact model output constrained by the data, say so. Otherwise add the raw-to-f conversion with uncertainties, and plot measured points only where data exist.

5. **Model parameters and consistency with experiment.**
   - The doublon-magnon coupling amplitude is chosen to "reproduce the experimentally observed reduction of the AFM order by approximately 50%" (p. S13). Fig. 2D shows a 28–30% drop in Bragg intensity at 3 mJ/cm², which corresponds to only about 15% sublattice-magnetization reduction if I ∝ M². Please reconcile the two numbers, since this sets the initial magnon density that drives the QBE.
   - The spin gap of the model with D = 0.025 meV is not stated. Compare it with the 1.3 meV acoustic gap invoked in the main text.
   - The δ-function broadening of 3.0 meV (p. S15) is larger than the acoustic gap, which is the scale that controls accumulation at Γ and the claimed bottleneck. Show that the results converge as the broadening is reduced.
   - The model's two branches are degenerate (p. S13). It therefore cannot represent the optical magnon branch (gap about 23 meV) that the main text uses to argue that acoustic magnons dominate, nor the decays from high-energy branches invoked in the Conclusions.
   - The comparison between Fig. 3E (experiment) and Fig. 3G (simulation) is visual only. A side-by-side plot of τ_RDS(q) and A_RDS(q) from experiment and theory would make the agreement, or disagreement, quantitative.

6. **Exclusion of other channels in the picosecond redistribution.** The text argues that magnon-electron scattering can be excluded because "the electrons are already thermalized within femtosecond timescales". The support is a 200 fs doublon lifetime from other copper oxides (Refs. 30, 31), not CuO. Photo-doped O 2p holes and polarons could persist for picoseconds. A test internal to the data would be the fluence dependence of τ_RDS(q). For magnon-magnon scattering the rate should increase with magnon density, roughly with fluence below saturation, while relaxation dominated by phonons or carriers would scale differently. Fig. 2F already gives the fluence dependence of the amplitude at 4.5 ps; extending it to the time constants would strengthen the assignment considerably.

7. **Claims beyond the measured range and system.**
   - The abstract and Conclusions state that magnons are generated "throughout reciprocal space" (citing Fig. 3J). The experiment covers |q| ≤ 0.08 r.l.u. along two directions, and the Brillouin-zone-wide distribution comes from the model alone (Fig. S14).
   - "Hot-magnon temperatures ... reach ~500 K" (p. S9) is given without the procedure, the assumed dispersion, or an uncertainty.
   - "Generalizes across materials", "design principles", "complete hierarchical energy transfer pathways", "transcends phenomenological models", and the suggestion of "attosecond-scale investigations" go beyond a single-temperature study of one compound.

   Please mark which statements are measured and which are modelled, and limit the generality claims accordingly.

8. **Sample and condition coverage.** All RXS data come from one crystal at one temperature, 190 K. The crystal has visible twin domains (Fig. S4). The statement that other domains do not contribute is plausible for the Bragg peak but not demonstrated for diffuse intensity within ±0.08 r.l.u. Repeating the key momentum scan at a lower base temperature (for example 100 K) would test the assignment. It would increase the margin to T_N1 (relevant to concern 1) and change the equilibrium magnon population in a predictable way. A second sample or sample position would also help.

C. Minor

9. **Wording of the phonon-dissipation interpretation (Fig. S11).** The claim that electronic thermal conductivity due to oxygen excess and Cu vacancies dominates the microsecond decay rests on four points per phase with large error bars. Please phrase it as a possibility.

10. **Undefined and inconsistent labels.**
    - Figs. S6 and S9 refer to "the D point (see main text)", but the main text does not define it.
    - Page S8 refers to q_RDS where q_RXD is meant.
    - T_N1 is 213 K in the text but 214.5 K in Fig. S3; T_N2 is 230 K in the text and 230.5 K in the figure.

11. **Eq. S37.** The last row lists F_xy where F_zy is meant. The text also does not say how the s and p contributions are weighted for the π-polarized measurements.

12. **Figure captions.**
    - Figs. 2 and 3 have only a title in bold on the figure page, with the panel descriptions on the following page; consider merging them.
    - Fig. 4E/F use the same colour-bar label Γ_avg(q) for phonon and magnon rates with opposite signs. State what the sign means.
    - Fig. 3I's caption ("guide to the eyes") conflicts with the quantitative statement in the text.

13. **References.** Ref. 71 duplicates Ref. 63. Ref. 22 has a garbled formula (La0.5Sr1.5MnO4). Ref. 29 has a stray "1" in "Mentink1". Ref. 51 lacks a DOI.

14. **Typos.** "time-resonant diffuse scattering" (p. S2), "an another", "supression" (S10), "Assmining" (S15), "Sacttering" (S18), "indidcated" (Fig. S16), "Ultrafasst" (Ref. 69). There is also a stray leading comma before Ĥ_mag−ph in Eq. S22.

15. **Inelastic channels in the energy-integrated signal.** The APDs integrate all scattered photons within the 0.5 eV incident bandwidth and the detector acceptance, including dd excitations and fluorescence. Please state explicitly that pump-induced changes in these largely q-independent channels are removed, for example by referencing to the large-|q| points, and show that the q = 0.08 traces fix this background.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one figure that compares the pumped RDS q-profile at several delays with the equilibrium diffuse profile measured at a set of temperatures. This single comparison would establish or refute both the non-thermal initial state and the quasi-thermalization.
2. Present a joint rate-equation fit of the RXD, RDS and NRDS kinetics on a common time axis, with matched excitation densities. Report the extracted magnon-to-phonon transfer rate next to the QBE prediction and its dependence on c^x.
3. Measure the fluence dependence of τ_RDS(q). It is the most direct internal test that the picosecond redistribution comes from magnon-magnon scattering.
4. Reframe the abstract and Conclusions to distinguish what is measured (|q| ≤ 0.08 r.l.u., one compound, one temperature) from what is modelled. Move the cross-material and device-design statements to an outlook sentence.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
