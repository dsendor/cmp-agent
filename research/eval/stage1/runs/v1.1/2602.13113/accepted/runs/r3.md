Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The paper introduces time-resolved resonant diffuse scattering (tr-RDS) at the Cu L3 edge as a momentum-resolved, energy-integrated probe of low-energy magnon populations after photoexcitation. It applies the method to the collinear antiferromagnet CuO, combined with tr-RXD, tr-NRDS and quantum-Boltzmann simulations. The authors argue for a three-stage hierarchy: sub-100 fs spin disorder via spin–charge coupling, picosecond magnon–magnon quasi-thermalization toward the zone centre, and ~7 ns recovery of order through magnon–phonon (anomalous, two-magnon → one-phonon) scattering. Directly measuring the momentum-dependent redistribution of low-energy magnons on picosecond timescales is new, and the technique is potentially broadly useful. The long-timescale mechanism and the generality claims rest more heavily on simulation than on measurement.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- New experimental capability: momentum-resolved tracking of low-energy (few-meV) magnon populations near magnetic Bragg peaks with ~60 fs resolution, which tr-RIXS cannot reach because of its energy resolution.
- Several independent checks on the magnetic origin of the diffuse upturn: resonance at the Cu L3 edge (Fig. 2e), π/σ polarization dependence (Fig. 2g), a fluence onset tied to sublattice demagnetization (Fig. 2f), symmetry of the amplitude about q_RXD (Fig. 3f), and a constant Bragg-peak width (Fig. S4).
- A clear momentum dependence of the RDS time constant (Fig. 3f, i), qualitatively reproduced by a microscopic magnon–magnon QBE calculation (Fig. 3g, h). The self-similar scaling exponent (2.28 ± 0.08) is close to the inverse-cascade value.
- The complementary tr-NRDS comparison between the AFM and PM phases (Fig. 4d) gives a direct lattice-side signature that correlates with magnon annihilation.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

None. The magnon origin of the RDS upturn and the picosecond quasi-thermalization are supported by evidence a reasonable referee would accept. The weaker points are listed under B.

**B. Should address**

1. **The momentum selectivity of the nanosecond magnon–phonon recovery is shown only in simulation.** The abstract and Discussion state that order recovers "via momentum-selective magnon-phonon scattering", with the anomalous process dominant (Fig. 4e, f). Experimentally, the nanosecond RDS decay is shown at a single momentum, (0.47, 0, −0.53) (Fig. 4d, orange). The choice of channel (iii) over (i) and (ii) rests on the QBE rates computed in a simplified orthorhombic cell, with phonon modes taken from Ref. 70 and a coupling derived from the Heisenberg model. The calculation predicts a distinctive q-dependence of the decay rate (Fig. 4e, f), which tr-RDS could test directly.
   - What to do: show RDS time traces out to ~12 ns at several |q| (along b* and along [h0h]) and compare the extracted decay rates with the calculated Γ_avg(q). If this is not possible, describe the momentum selectivity in the abstract and Discussion as a model prediction rather than an observation.

2. **A purely thermal explanation of the nanosecond recovery is not ruled out.** After magnons and phonons equilibrate, the residual reduction of the order parameter should follow the equilibrium M(T) at the local lattice temperature. Recovery would then be set by heat transport out of the excited layer (pump penetration ~450 nm, probe depth ~18 nm), not by magnon–phonon coupling. This alternative needs particular attention because the base temperature of 190 K is only ~23 K below T_N1 = 213 K, where M(T) is steep. In Fig. 4d the RXD has not fully recovered at 12 ns. The estimated coupling of −59 µeV is "estimated from the magnon relaxation timescale" and so assumes the 7 ns constant is intrinsic.
   - What to do: compare the RXD reduction on the nanosecond plateau with the equilibrium RXD intensity at the lattice temperature estimated in Supplementary Note 3, and estimate the heat-diffusion timescale out of the probed volume. Ideally, show how the ~7 ns constant depends on base temperature or fluence. A magnon–phonon bottleneck and thermal transport predict different trends.

3. **The tr-NRDS and tr-RDS/RXD experiments are compared across mismatched conditions.** The lattice-side evidence (Fig. 4d, top) comes from ESRF ID09. That setup has a ~100 ps X-ray pulse, a picosecond 800→400 nm pump at 1 kHz, pump penetration ~500 nm and probe depth ~1.5 µm. The resonant data come from SwissFEL, with a 35 fs pump at 100 Hz and probe depth ~18 nm. The NRDS therefore averages over a volume most of which is weakly excited, possibly with more cumulative heating at 1 kHz. The text says the ~2 ns NRDS enhancement occurs on "a similar timescale to the RDS signals", but the RDS/RXD recovery constant is ~7 ns, and Fig. 4d shows the NRDS AFM signal peaking at ~1–2 ns and then decaying.
   - What to do: state the base temperatures of the AFM and PM NRDS data and the effective fluence in the probed volume. Reconcile the 2 ns and 7 ns timescales, for example by modelling the depth-weighted NRDS response. Show that the AFM/PM contrast does not arise from different deposited energy, heat capacity or thermal diffuse scattering background at the two temperatures.

4. **The explanation for the missing self-absorption dip in the RDS energy dependence conflicts with the stated depths.** For Fig. 2e, the text says the RDS lacks the RXD self-absorption dip "likely" because a larger magnon density is created near the surface, "limiting the self-absorption effect". But the pump penetration (~450 nm) is ~25 times the X-ray probe depth (~18 nm, Methods), so the excitation should be nearly uniform over the probed depth. The energy dependence is one of the main arguments for the resonant magnetic origin of the upturn, so its lineshape should be understood.
   - What to do: give a quantitative model of the RXD and RDS energy dependences that includes self-absorption, the depth profiles of both beams, and any photoinduced change in the absorption near 931–932 eV. Or remove the speculative explanation and report the difference as unexplained.

5. **Momentum resolution and tail subtraction at small |q| bear directly on the "accumulation at Γ" claim.** The quoted momentum resolution is ~5 × 10⁻³ r.l.u. The smallest measured offsets are 0.010 and 0.015 r.l.u., and the region nearer q_RXD is excluded because of saturation. At these offsets the RXD tail dominates (Fig. 3a, d). The RDS component is isolated by a two-exponential fit whose separation relies on the different time constants. The Fig. 3 caption also notes an error in the definition of q = 0, which shifts all |q| values. Yet the near-Γ points carry the claim that 1/τ_RDS ∝ |q| (Fig. 3i) and that magnons accumulate at Γ.
   - What to do: show how the resolution function and the uncertainty in the origin propagate into τ_RDS and A_RDS at |q| ≤ 0.02. Report the correlation between the fitted RXD and RDS parameters at these points. Test whether a fit that convolves the model with the resolution changes the slope or intercept in Fig. 3i.

6. **Linear spin-wave and QBE treatment of a strongly disordered state.** At 3 mJ/cm² the RXD intensity falls by ~30% within ~70 fs (Fig. 2d), and by more at higher fluence (Fig. 2f), at a base temperature near T_N1. Eqs. (1)–(3) describe the response through magnon occupations f_μ(q, t) on a linear spin-wave background, with corrections only at leading order beyond it. The initial-state parameters were "chosen to be consistent with the experiment" (Methods, Supplementary Note 1). So the theory reproducing the data is partly built in.
   - What to do: say which simulation parameters were fitted to the tr-RDS or tr-RXD data and which were fixed independently. Show the quantitative comparison directly, not side by side: overlay the calculated τ_RDS(q) on Fig. 3i and compare simulated and measured RDS traces at matched q on a common scale. Briefly discuss whether a magnon picture is still valid at the demagnetization levels reached.

7. **Single base temperature, single sample.** All resonant data are taken at 190 K, and Fig. 4 adds only one PM-phase comparison. Thermal magnon populations, spin gaps and phonon occupations all depend on temperature, so the quasi-thermalization time and the nanosecond bottleneck predicted by the model should shift with T. It is also not stated whether the key RDS results were reproduced on more than one crystal or sample spot.
   - What to do: if beamtime data exist, add RDS time traces at a second temperature in the commensurate AFM phase. State how many samples, spots or beamtimes the Fig. 2–3 results represent.

8. **The generality claims go beyond the data.** The abstract says the framework "generalizes across antiferromagnetic insulators, establishing design principles", and the Discussion predicts that materials with "strongly overlapping dispersions" recover faster. Only CuO is measured, and CuO's channel selection depends on its particular spin gap and phonon spectrum.
   - What to do: present these statements as hypotheses. Give at least one concrete, testable prediction for another material, or state which features of CuO the conclusions depend on.

**C. Minor**

9. Two different momentum directions are used for the RDS data. Figs. 2–3 measure the RDS along b*, at (0.49, 0.01, −0.51) and along [½ q −½]. Fig. 4d measures it at (0.47, 0, −0.53), an offset along [h0h], which the text and Supplementary Note 6 say is strongly anisotropic. State why this direction was chosen for the nanosecond data, and whether the picosecond behaviour at that point matches Fig. 3.
10. The Fig. 3j–n caption says the magnon distributions are "derived from tr-RDS data using Eq. (2)", while the main text presents Fig. 3h and 3j–n as simulated. Clarify whether these panels are calculated or inverted from the data.
11. Fig. 2e shows the RXD maximum near ~930.5 eV and the RDS maximum near ~932 eV, and the rocking curve is at 929.9 eV. State the photon energy used for the Fig. 2d, 3 and 4 time traces.
12. The text says 1/τ_RDS "is proportional to momentum" (Fig. 3i), but the solid lines are guides to the eye, and the data near q = 0 do not obviously extrapolate to zero. Give the fit form and intercept.
13. The −59 µeV effective magnon–phonon coupling is compared with a −6.2 meV spin–phonon coupling from Ref. 46. Explain whether these are the same quantity, so that the comparison of magnitudes is meaningful.
14. The RDS axes in Figs. 2d, 2f and 4d are in arbitrary units of I_ON − I_OFF, while RXD is normalized by I_OFF. Use a common normalization, or explain how the relative RDS amplitudes can be compared across fluence and q.
15. The claim that Fourier-transform independence "enables, in principle, attosecond-scale investigations" (Discussion) has no support in the paper. Remove it or qualify it.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add nanosecond RDS traces at several momenta and overlay the predicted Γ_avg(q). This would turn the momentum-selective magnon–phonon channel from a model result into a measurement.
2. Add a short quantitative "thermal baseline" analysis: the estimated lattice temperature, the equilibrium M(T) at that temperature, and the heat-diffusion time, to separate intrinsic magnon–phonon recovery from heat transport.
3. Put theory and experiment on common axes: τ_RDS(q) in Fig. 3i, and RDS traces at matched q in Fig. 3. List clearly which model parameters were fitted.
4. Add a short schematic or table of excitation and probe depths, pulse durations and repetition rates for the three techniques, so readers can judge how comparable the datasets are.

**5. Verdict**

Verdict: minor revision
