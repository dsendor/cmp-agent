Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper presents time-resolved resonant diffuse scattering (tr-RDS) at the Cu L3 edge as a momentum-resolved probe of low-energy, non-equilibrium magnons. It applies the method to photoexcited CuO, together with tr-RXD, tr-NRDS and quantum-Boltzmann simulations. The authors report three stages: fs spin disorder via spin–charge coupling, ps magnon–magnon quasi-thermalization with a momentum-dependent rate (1/τ ∝ q), and ns recovery of AFM order limited by magnon–phonon (anomalous) scattering with a dispersion-mismatch bottleneck. The first demonstration of energy-integrated, momentum-resolved magnon population dynamics close to a magnetic Bragg peak is a real methodological advance. The proposed hierarchy is a useful, testable microscopic picture that goes beyond multi-temperature models.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new experimental capability: tr-RDS measured simultaneously with tr-RXD on one detector tower, giving the order-parameter and fluctuation channels under identical conditions (Fig. 2d, Fig. 3a–f).
- Several checks tie the upturn signal to magnetism: resonance at the Cu L3 edge (Fig. 2e), π ≫ σ polarization dependence matching the magnetic RXD profile (Fig. 2g, Fig. S18), symmetry about q_RXD along b* (Fig. 3f), a fluence threshold tied to demagnetization (Fig. 2f), and an unchanged RXD peak width (Fig. S4) that rules out a change in correlation length.
- The momentum dependence of the RDS rise time (Fig. 3f, i) is the key observation. A uniform rise in spin temperature would not produce it, and it points directly to redistribution within the magnon branch.
- The ps and ns timescales come with microscopic calculations (QBE magnon–magnon and magnon–phonon scattering integrals) rather than phenomenological fits. The comparison of AFM and PM phases in tr-NRDS (Fig. 4d) is a sensible control.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The main experimental observations are supported by the controls shown. The concerns below would sharpen or narrow the interpretation.

B. Should address

1. Thermal alternative for the late-time RDS and the ns recovery is not quantitatively excluded (Fig. 4d, Supplementary Note 3, Methods).
   - The sample is held at 190 K, only ~23 K below T_N1 = 213 K, and the main data use 3 mJ cm⁻² at 400 nm with ~450 nm penetration.
   - A rough estimate (absorbed ~67 J cm⁻³, C ≈ 3.4 J cm⁻³ K⁻¹) gives a lattice temperature rise of order 15–20 K. After equilibration that would put the probed volume close to T_N1, where thermal critical diffuse scattering around (1/2 0 −1/2) is strong and the order parameter is suppressed.
   - Part of the RDS upturn and RXD suppression at ps–ns times could therefore be an equilibrium spin response at a raised lattice temperature, not a non-thermal magnon population held up by a magnon–phonon bottleneck. The fact that the RDS signal decreases nonlinearly at high fluence "at temperatures closer to the AFM transition" (p. 4) points the same way.
   - The strongest argument for a bottleneck is that RXD recovers in ~7 ns while the lattice stays hot for µs (Fig. S13). That argument needs to be made quantitatively.
   - Requested: (i) state the estimated final lattice temperature in the main text; (ii) compare the RXD reduction and RDS intensity at the longest delays (10–12 ns and later) with static RXD/RDS measured at that temperature (static T-dependence data appear to exist, Supplementary Note 3); (iii) show the asymptotic value of the 7 ns fit.
   - If the long-time plateau matches the static values at the lattice temperature, the bottleneck interpretation is much stronger. If it does not, the 7 ns component needs re-examination.

2. The comparison of experiment with the quantum-kinetic simulation is qualitative, and part of the figure is ambiguous about what is measured and what is calculated (Fig. 3g–n, p. 4–5).
   - Fig. 3g (simulated I_RDS) is shown beside Fig. 3e only as colour maps, on different time ranges (0–6 ps against 0–4 ps) and different intensity scales.
   - The central quantitative observation, 1/τ_RDS linear in q (Fig. 3i), is plotted with "guides to the eye", not against the simulated rate.
   - The excitation parameters "were chosen to be consistent with the experiment" (Methods), so it is unclear what the simulation predicts and what is tuned.
   - The Fig. 3j–n caption says the magnon distribution is "derived from tr-RDS data using Eq. (2)", but the text calls it "the calculated time-dependent magnon distribution". The panels also extend to |q| = 0.1 r.l.u. and ω = 20 meV, beyond the measured range (|q| ≤ 0.08, with the region near q = 0 masked).
   - Requested: overlay simulated and measured τ_RDS(q) and the RDS amplitude in Fig. 3f/i with no rescaling beyond one stated global factor; list which parameters were adjusted; correct the Fig. 3j–n caption, or, if the panels really are inverted from data, explain how the energy-integrated signal was inverted to f_µ(q) and what A_µ(q) was used.

3. "Quasi-thermalization … fulfilling Bose–Einstein statistics" and "non-thermal magnons throughout reciprocal space" go beyond what is measured (abstract, p. 4–5).
   - The experiment covers a small window, |q| ≲ 0.08 r.l.u. along b* around one magnetic reflection. Population at the zone boundary, and the Bose–Einstein form of the late-time distribution, come from the model only.
   - If magnon number is (approximately) conserved by the leading-order magnon–magnon scattering, the quasi-equilibrium state should have a finite chemical potential. The accumulation near Γ (f up to ~13, Fig. 3h, n) suggests this, but no effective temperature or chemical potential is given or tested against the data.
   - Requested: report the effective T and µ of the simulated late-time distribution, and test whether a Bose–Einstein form with these parameters, convolved with A_µ(q) and the momentum resolution, fits the measured RDS q-profile at 3–4 ps. Then word the abstract so it separates what is measured from what is modelled.

4. The tr-NRDS phonon evidence was taken under conditions that do not match the tr-RDS data, and the timescales quoted as "similar" differ by a factor of ~3 (Fig. 4d, p. 6, Methods).
   - The text says the NRDS enhancement at ~2 ns is "a similar timescale to the RDS signals", but the RDS/RXD recovery is ~7 ns.
   - The NRDS experiment differs in several ways:
     - ~100 ps X-ray pulses;
     - a pump incidence angle of ~88°, which if measured from the surface normal greatly lowers the absorbed fluence per unit area compared with the nominal 3 mJ cm⁻²;
     - an X-ray probe depth of ~1.5 µm, much larger than the ~500 nm pump penetration, so unexcited bulk contributes;
     - a different cryostat.
   - The excitation density in Fig. 4d (top) may therefore be quite different from that in Fig. 4d (bottom).
   - The PM-phase temperature for the control trace is not stated, and the equilibrium phonon population is different there.
   - Requested: state how the absorbed energy density in the probed volume compares between the two experiments; give the PM temperature; fit both NRDS and RDS with a common model, or explain the 2 ns against 7 ns difference.

5. The magnon–phonon channel assignment depends on a fitted coupling (p. 6–7, Fig. 4e, f, Supplementary Note 10).
   - The effective coupling of −59 µeV is "estimated from the magnon relaxation timescale". The calculation therefore fixes the absolute rate to the data and only ranks the three channels (Fig. 4a–c).
   - The conclusion that anomalous scattering dominates, and the claim of an "unexpectedly small" effective coupling compared with the −6.2 meV spin–phonon coupling, both depend on how each vertex is derived from the Heisenberg Hamiltonian and on the phonon modes included.
   - Requested: give the calculated rates for all three processes with the same microscopic vertices and no fitted rescaling, showing what is parameter-free; show the sensitivity to the phonon branches included; explain why channel (i) is negligible, beyond the qualitative phase-space argument.

6. The ns RDS data were taken along a different momentum direction from the ps analysis (Fig. 4d, Fig. 2d).
   - The ps momentum analysis runs along b* (Fig. 3), but the ns RDS trace was taken at (0.47, 0, −0.53), along [h0h]. The paper itself states this direction shows strong magnon anisotropy (Supplementary Note 6, Fig. S7).
   - Fig. 2d uses (0.49, 0.01, −0.51), which is displaced along all three axes.
   - The two directions probe different magnon energies, so the ns recovery cannot be directly compared with the ps redistribution along b*.
   - Requested: justify the choice of direction, or show that the ns behaviour does not depend on direction.

7. Generalization claims rest on one material, one temperature and one main fluence (abstract, Discussion).
   - All tr-RDS data are at 190 K, and the momentum-resolved data are at 3 mJ cm⁻², on a single crystal.
   - The statements that the framework "generalizes across antiferromagnetic insulators" and "establishes design principles" are not tested within the paper.
   - Requested: either add RDS rate data at a second base temperature or fluence, since the predicted 1/τ(q) and the ns bottleneck should change in a calculable way with the initial magnon density and the phonon population; or tone the generalization down to a hypothesis.

8. Separating the RXD tail from the RDS upturn depends on the model (Fig. 3a–f, Supplementary Note 2).
   - The RDS component is obtained by fitting each time trace with two exponentials, labelled RXD reduction and RDS upturn.
   - Near q_RXD the RXD tail dominates; the hatched region is excluded because of saturation.
   - The authors attribute the asymmetry between ±q to an error in the definition of the origin, which shows the result is sensitive to the exact reference point.
   - The fitted τ_RDS at the smallest |q| (~3–4 ps), and hence the low-q end of the linear 1/τ ∝ q law, is the part most exposed to this decomposition.
   - Requested: show residuals for representative q; show how τ_RDS changes when the RXD time constant is fixed at its global value and when it is left free; show the fit after re-centring the origin.

9. Possible magneto-vibrational and phonon contributions to the resonant diffuse signal.
   - Photoexcited phonons, seen directly in tr-NRDS (Fig. 4d), could produce diffuse intensity around a magnetic Bragg peak through magneto-vibrational scattering.
   - That signal would be resonant and would follow the magnetic π/σ selection, so neither Fig. 2e nor Fig. 2g excludes it.
   - Requested: estimate its size relative to the measured upturn, for example from the measured NRDS amplitude scaled by the ratio of magnetic to charge structure factors.

C. Minor

10. Inconsistent depth arguments for the self-absorption dip (Fig. 2e, p. 3).
    - The dip in the RDS energy dependence is attributed to a larger magnon density near the surface with a depth profile.
    - But the X-ray probe depth (~18 nm) is much smaller than the pump penetration (~450 nm), so excitation should be almost uniform within the probed volume.
    - Please make the self-absorption argument quantitative, or reconsider it.

11. Missing error and resolution information.
    - "Error bars represent standard error": please state over what (shots, scans, delay bins) and how many shots per point.
    - Give the q-resolution (~5 × 10⁻³ r.l.u.) next to the smallest measured q (0.010 r.l.u.) in Fig. 3, since at q = 0.010 the resolution is half the offset.

12. Self-similarity exponent (Fig. S10, p. 4).
    - The 2.28 ± 0.08 against 2.33 comparison should state the fitted time and q windows, how robust the fit is to their choice, and whether the uncertainty includes the systematic error from the decomposition in concern 8.

13. Timescale of the initial drop (Fig. 2d).
    - The step-like RXD drop "within ~70 fs" is close to the ~60 fs combined time resolution.
    - Please state that the drop is resolution-limited, or give a deconvolved time constant.

14. Missing details in Fig. 4d and the Methods.
    - The Fig. 4d caption should give the PM measurement temperature.
    - The Methods should state whether the stated pump fluence is in the beam plane or on the sample surface, for both experiments.

15. Clarity of Eq. (2).
    - In Eq. (2), please define A_µ and Ā_µ explicitly in the main text, and state which branch(es) µ contribute in the measured q range.
    - At present the reader cannot tell how strongly the 1/ω weighting near q_RXD shapes the RDS q-profile, as distinct from f_µ itself.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add one figure that overlays measured and simulated τ_RDS(q), RDS amplitude(q) and the 3–4 ps q-profile, with the adjusted parameters listed. This would turn the qualitative agreement into a quantitative test of the magnon–magnon thermalization picture.
- Compare the long-delay RXD/RDS levels with static temperature-dependent data at the estimated lattice temperature, to separate the non-thermal magnon bottleneck from simple heating close to T_N1.
- A second base temperature (for example well below 190 K) or a fluence series of τ_RDS(q) would test the predicted dependence of the redistribution and recovery rates on the initial magnon density and the phonon population.
- Rephrase the generalization to other antiferromagnetic insulators as a prediction, and state explicitly which parts of the hierarchy (zone-wide magnon population, Bose–Einstein form, channel dominance) come from the model rather than the data.

5. Verdict

Verdict: minor revision
