Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports time-resolved resonant diffuse scattering (tr-RDS) at the Cu L3 edge around the (1/2 0 -1/2) magnetic Bragg reflection of CuO. It uses this, together with tr-RXD, tr-NRDS and quantum-Boltzmann simulations, to follow photoexcited magnons in momentum and time. If the assignment holds, it is the first momentum-resolved, energy-integrated tracking of low-energy magnon populations on femtosecond-to-nanosecond timescales. The proposed hierarchy is quasi-instantaneous spin disorder from doublon creation, then picosecond magnon-magnon quasi-thermalization towards the spin gap, then nanosecond recovery limited by magnon-phonon "anomalous" scattering. This picture would interest the ultrafast magnetism community, and the technique could be used widely. The novelty rests mainly on the method and on the momentum-dependent picosecond dynamics. The generalization to "antiferromagnetic insulators" is argued, not demonstrated.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new observable: the resonant diffuse signal near a magnetic Bragg peak, measured at the same time as the Bragg intensity on a stacked photodiode/APD tower, so RXD and RDS share identical excitation conditions (Fig. 2d, Methods).
- Several checks that the upturn signal is magnetic and resonant: resonant enhancement at the Cu L3 edge (Fig. 2e), stronger signal for pi than for sigma polarization, matching the magnetic RXD polarization dependence (Fig. 2g, Fig. S18), a fluence threshold matching sublattice demagnetization (Fig. 2f), and a constant RXD peak width (Fig. S4), which argues against domain coarsening or loss of long-range order.
- A clear experimental fingerprint of redistribution: the RDS time constant depends strongly on q and is symmetric in ±q (Fig. 3f, i), while the RXD-reduction time constant does not depend on q (Fig. 3d).
- A microscopic model (Hubbard-derived spin model, magnon-magnon and magnon-phonon collision integrals) that reproduces the qualitative momentum-delay map (Fig. 3e vs 3g) and gives a physical reason for the nanosecond bottleneck (phase space for anomalous scattering, Fig. 4e, f).
- Complementary tr-NRDS showing a ~2 ns phonon-diffuse enhancement in the AFM phase that is absent in the PM phase (Fig. 4d, top).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The main experimental claims (a magnetic, resonant diffuse signal whose rise time depends on momentum, and nanosecond recovery of the order) are supported by evidence a reasonable referee would accept. The items below concern how firmly the microscopic interpretation is pinned down.

B. Should address

1. The simulation is compared with the data only qualitatively. The magnon-magnon thermalization mechanism and the "non-thermal" initial distribution are inferred mainly from the agreement between Fig. 3e (data) and Fig. 3g (simulation). The two maps are in arbitrary units on different delay ranges (0–4 ps measured, 0–6 ps simulated). The line in Fig. 3i is described as a guide to the eye, not as the model prediction. Methods says the excitation parameters "were chosen to be consistent with the experiment" (Supplementary Note 1), so some agreement is built in. As presented, the reader cannot tell whether the model predicts the measured 1/τ_RDS(q), including the roughly linear-in-|q| rate and its absolute scale (~0.3–5 ps^-1), or only the trend. Please overlay the simulated 1/τ_RDS(q) (fitted with the same two-exponential procedure as the data) on Fig. 3i. Please also state which model parameters were fixed independently (J values from the INS dispersion, ref. 38) and which were adjusted (doublon density, ramp time).

2. Non-thermal magnons versus a heated thermal magnon population. The claim of "non-thermal magnons throughout reciprocal space within femtoseconds" (abstract, Fig. 3j) comes from the model; the data show a magnetic diffuse increase that develops over picoseconds. A simpler picture also predicts a q-dependent rise and an increased diffuse magnetic intensity: fast electron-to-spin energy transfer that raises a thermal magnon population, at 190 K, only ~23 K below T_N1 = 213 K. Two things should be shown explicitly: how the data at the earliest delays (<0.5 ps) favour a broad non-thermal distribution over a hot Bose-Einstein one, and whether the measured q-dependence of τ_RDS differs from that expected for a heated thermal population relaxing through the same collision integral. A run of the model with a thermal (elevated-T) initial state, compared with the data in Fig. 3a/i, would settle this.

3. The two-exponential separation of RDS from the RXD tail is central and is least reliable where the physics happens. All RDS amplitudes and time constants (Fig. 3d–f, i) come from a two-exponential decomposition (Supplementary Note 2). The resolution is ~5×10^-3 r.l.u. and the smallest q shown is 0.010 r.l.u. Near q_RXD the RXD-tail reduction is 10–20 times larger than the upturn (Fig. 3a), and the authors note a slight ±q asymmetry from an error in the origin. The fastest and slowest τ_RDS values, which set the |q|-linear trend in Fig. 3i, come from exactly these points. Please show the correlation between fitted τ_RDS and the RXD-tail parameters for the |q| ≤ 0.02 points. Please state whether the RXD time constant was shared or free across q, and show a fit with τ_RXD fixed to its q-independent value (Fig. 3d) to show the τ_RDS(q) trend does not depend on this choice.

4. The AFM-versus-PM tr-NRDS comparison, the main evidence for magnon-to-phonon energy transfer, is not made under matched conditions. The tr-NRDS data (Fig. 4d, top) were taken at ESRF with ~100 ps X-ray pulses, picosecond 400 nm pump pulses at 1 kHz (versus 35 fs at 100 Hz for tr-RDS), and a ~1.5 µm probe depth (versus ~18 nm for tr-RDS). The PM data are necessarily at a different base temperature (>230 K). The temperature, fluence and absorbed energy density for the AFM and PM traces should be stated. The authors should discuss whether the difference in equilibrium phonon population and specific heat between the two temperatures, rather than the presence of order, could explain the missing ~2 ns enhancement. A tr-NRDS trace in the AFM phase at a second temperature, or at a fluence below the demagnetization threshold of Fig. 2f, would strengthen this link. Also, the depth sampled by NRDS (~1.5 µm) exceeds the pump penetration (~500 nm), while the RDS depth (~18 nm) is near the surface. This mismatch should be discussed when the two timescales are compared.

5. Energy budget and lattice heating for the nanosecond recovery. Supplementary Note 3 estimates the temperature, but the main text does not say what equilibrium temperature magnons and lattice would reach at 3 mJ cm^-2 after full thermalization. Nor does it say whether the residual RXD reduction at 12 ns (Fig. 4d, ~-0.1) matches the static order parameter at that temperature. This check would show that the ~7 ns recovery reflects magnon annihilation into phonons, not partial equilibration followed by slower cooling. It would also connect to the microsecond phonon-relaxation stage claimed in the Discussion (Fig. S13). Please give the estimated temperatures in the main text and compare the long-delay RXD level with the equilibrium M(T)^2.

6. Self-absorption argument is inconsistent with the stated depths. Fig. 2e shows an RXD dip at resonance due to self-absorption, while the RDS signal peaks at 931.9 eV without a dip. The authors attribute this to a larger magnon density near the surface. But the pump penetration (~450 nm) is ~25 times the X-ray probe depth (~18 nm), so the excitation should be nearly uniform over the probed depth. Please give a quantitative explanation. One possibility is different scattering geometries or path lengths at the off-Bragg position; another is that the RDS intensity is a difference signal rather than an absolute intensity. If no explanation holds, soften the statement.

7. Generality claims exceed the evidence. The abstract and Discussion say the framework "transcends phenomenological models and generalizes across antiferromagnetic insulators" and that "materials with strongly overlapping dispersions of magnons and phonons will exhibit faster recovery". All data are from one CuO crystal at one base temperature (190 K) and, for the momentum-resolved data, one fluence (3 mJ cm^-2). No other material or tuning parameter tests the dispersion-overlap principle. Please reword these as predictions, or add a test such as the temperature dependence of the ns recovery, since the magnon gap and phonon populations change with T.

8. Temperature dependence of the picosecond dynamics. Magnon-magnon scattering rates should depend strongly on the thermal magnon background, which is large at 190 K (k_BT ≈ 16 meV, comparable to the magnon energies probed, Fig. 3j–n). Eq. (2) uses f̄ = f + 1, but it is not stated whether the simulated initial state includes the thermal population at 190 K. Please state this. If possible, show τ_RDS(q) at a second base temperature or a second fluence; either would test the magnon-magnon assignment directly.

9. Model simplifications relative to the real structure. The spin model uses a simplified orthorhombic cell with one Cu per layer and neglects the monoclinic distortion and interlayer complexity (Methods, Fig. S1b). CuO is strongly quasi-one-dimensional along the J_AFM = 120 meV chains, and the paper reports strong anisotropy between b* and [h0h] (Fig. S7). Please show how well the model reproduces the measured RDS anisotropy between b* and [h0h], not only the b* cut. For the self-similarity analysis (Fig. S10), please justify the comparison with the 2D Heisenberg exponent 2.33 given this quasi-1D character.

C. Minor

10. Different momentum points are used for "RDS" in different figures: (0.49, 0.01, -0.51) in Fig. 2d, (1/2, -0.08, -1/2) in Fig. 2e, (0.47, 0, -0.53) in Fig. 4d, and [1/2 q -1/2] in Fig. 3. The off-b* points have different magnon energies and different RXD-tail contamination. Please state the |q| and direction of each point and whether the traces can be compared directly.

11. The "step-function-like" decrease "within ~70 fs" (Fig. 2d) is comparable to the stated ~60 fs combined time resolution. Please quote it as an upper bound or give the deconvolved value with uncertainty.

12. The effective magnon-phonon coupling of -59 µeV is "estimated from the magnon relaxation timescale". Please give the procedure and uncertainty in the main text or Methods, since it is then compared with the -6.2 meV spin-phonon coupling to argue for phase-space restriction.

13. Fig. 3i: please state whether the open circles at large |q| with large error bars (|q| = 0.075) are included in the trend, and how the error bars were obtained (fit covariance or repeated scans).

14. The tr-NRDS pump uses picosecond pulses, longer than the ~200 fs doublon lifetime cited. The argument that this does not matter (Methods) would be stronger with a measured tr-RXD trace taken with a stretched pump, or a reference.

15. Twin and AF2 domains: the conclusion that a photoinduced transition to the incommensurate AF2 phase (ref. 23) is excluded rests on Supplementary Note 4. A sentence in the main text on the search window (for example, whether the (0.506, 0, -0.483) position was monitored) would help readers.

16. Fig. 2c is labelled with the top of the diffraction peak missing due to saturation, and Fig. 3 hatches the saturated region. Please state the detector linearity range used to choose the q window.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a figure or panel that compares simulation and data quantitatively: the simulated and measured 1/τ_RDS(q) and RDS amplitude A_RDS(q) on common axes, plus a run with a thermal initial state as the null model for "non-thermal" magnons.
- Add a matched-condition control for the nanosecond stage: tr-NRDS and tr-RXD/RDS recovery at a second temperature within the AFM phase, or below the demagnetization fluence threshold, with stated absorbed energy densities.
- Give an explicit energy budget (absorbed energy, estimated electron, magnon and lattice temperatures versus delay) and relate the long-delay RXD level to the static order parameter.
- Tone down the generality statements in the abstract and Discussion, or support them with a second material or a tunable dispersion-overlap test.

5. Verdict

Verdict: minor revision
