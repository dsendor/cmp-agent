Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper studies La2-xBaxCuO4 at exactly x = 0.125 under in-plane uniaxial stress applied along [110]_t. It combines in situ ac susceptibility, zero-field and weak-transverse-field muSR and resistivity, and adds transport at x = 0.115 and 0.135. The main reported results are a rise of the susceptibility midpoint Tc from about 3 K to 35 K by about 0.15-0.23 GPa; a drop of the static spin-stripe volume fraction to about 0.4 while T_so and B_int change only slightly; and suppression of the resistive LTT anomaly by about 0.5 GPa, with Tc,zero = 37 K and a resistive onset of about 46 K. The muSR volume-fraction data at the 1/8 composition under stress are new and useful. The Tc enhancement at 1/8 under stress is partly anticipated by earlier work, including Ref. 30 and the authors' own Refs. 6 and 19. The main conceptual claims are that x = 1/8 reaches the "highest bulk Tc in the 214 family" and that stripe correlations promote pairing. These go beyond what the data establish.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Three probes on the commensurate 1/8 composition under the same type of symmetry-selective stress: superconductivity (ac susceptibility and resistivity), static magnetism (ZF and wTF muSR) and a structural proxy (the LTT resistance anomaly). This is a natural and long-awaited extension of the x = 0.115 and 0.135 studies.
- The split between a strongly reduced magnetic volume fraction (Fig. 2d, 0.97 to about 0.37) and a nearly unchanged ordered moment and ordering temperature (Figs. 2c, 4c) is a clear and informative result. It supports a picture of phase separation rather than uniform weakening of the order.
- The doping comparison (Fig. 3) shows that the critical stress is systematically larger at 1/8, consistent with the greater stability of stripe order there.
- The resistivity data are clean, and Fig. 3d-f summarises the anticorrelation between Tc,zero and the LTT anomaly clearly.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The zero-stress reference for the "eleven-fold enhancement of 3D superconductivity" was measured in a different geometry from the stressed data, and the stressed geometry is not specifically sensitive to 3D coherence.
   - Where: Section II (p. 3) and Fig. 2b. The 0 GPa curve was measured "prior to mounting" as a dc magnetisation in 0.5 mT applied parallel to the CuO2 planes, so the shielding currents must cross the layers. The in situ ac data under stress were taken with the excitation field "predominantly along the c-axis". In that geometry the shielding currents flow in the planes, so the signal also responds to 2D (decoupled-layer) superconductivity.
   - Why it matters: the authors' own resistivity puts the in-plane (2D) transition of x = 0.125 at Tc,zero of about 23-25 K at zero stress (Figs. 3e, 4a). This is close to the 0.09 GPa in situ Tc,mid of about 25 K. As it stands, the jump from 3 K to 25-35 K in Fig. 2b, and the Tc,mid^AC points labelled "3D" in Fig. 4a, may partly reflect the change of measurement geometry rather than the onset of interlayer coherence. The central claim that stress converts 2D into 3D superconductivity at x = 1/8 depends on this comparison.
   - What to do:
     - Show an in situ ac susceptibility curve at zero (or minimal) force after mounting, in the same coil geometry as the stressed data.
     - Explain why an H || c ac shielding signal measures 3D rather than 2D superconductivity, or add a probe that does measure 3D coherence: interlayer (c-axis) resistivity under stress, ac susceptibility with H || ab, or TF-muSR superfluid density under stress.
     - Report the absolute shielding fraction rather than χ normalised to -1 at every stress. Normalisation hides any change in shielded volume and weakens the word "bulk".

2. The claims that the resulting Tc exceeds the optimal Tc of LSCO and is "the highest bulk Tc reported in the 214 cuprate family" are not supported, and are partly contradicted by the literature.
   - Where: Abstract; p. 3 ("We essentially reach the optimal Tc of LSCO"); p. 5 (46 K "slightly exceeds the optimal Tc reported for LSCO"); p. 7 ("representing the highest bulk Tc reported in the 214 cuprate family"); Fig. 4d.
   - Problems with the comparison:
     - It rests on a resistive onset of about 46 K. The onset criterion is not defined, and the onset lies in the temperature range of the residual LTT/stripe hump and of 2D fluctuation superconductivity.
     - The LSCO points in Fig. 4d are taken from a 1989 study (Ref. 32), and their onset criteria may differ.
     - The bulk measures in this paper (Tc,mid = 35 K and Tc,zero = 37 K) are at or below the optimal Tc of LSCO (about 38-40 K), not above it.
     - Higher Tc values have been reported in the 214 family, for example in oxygen-intercalated La2CuO4+δ and in epitaxially strained LSCO films.
   - What to do:
     - Define the onset criterion (for example a fixed fraction of the normal-state resistance, or the deviation from a normal-state fit), and show it on the curves.
     - Compare like with like: zero resistance with zero resistance, and susceptibility midpoint with susceptibility midpoint.
     - Restate the claim as "comparable to optimally doped LSCO", and remove "highest bulk Tc in the 214 family" or qualify it against the literature.
     - The title phrase "hosts optimal superconductivity" should be revised to match.

B. Should address

3. The direct correlation between V_M and LTT suppression (Fig. 4b), and the percolation picture built on it, combine two different apparatuses, possibly different samples, and an LTT proxy that is not a volume fraction.
   - Where: Fig. 4b and p. 7.
   - The two quantities come from different setups:
     - V_M comes from the piezo muSR cell: a 7 x 5.1 x 0.3 mm³ plate with only 4 mm free length, and stress taken from force divided by the full cross-section.
     - ΔR_LTT comes from the Razorbill FC100 transport cell, with sample dimensions not given.
   - The text says the measurements were "performed on the same crystal". Please state whether the same piece was used, and how the two stress scales were cross-calibrated, given strain transmission through Stycast and the very different aspect ratios.
   - Plotting "LTT peak (%)" on the same axis as V_M implies that the resistance hump scales linearly with the LTT volume fraction. This is an assumption: the hump reflects stripe pinning and percolation of the transport paths, and need not be linear in volume.
   - V_M is measured only up to 0.23 GPa (and is nearly flat between 0.18 and 0.23 GPa), while the LTT peak continues to fall until about 0.5 GPa. The correlation is therefore tested only over the lower half of the stress range.
   - Please extend the muSR data to higher stress (Methods state that 0.32 GPa was reached), show stress-homogeneity estimates (for example finite-element modelling of the plate in the muSR cell), and soften "directly correlated".

4. The LTT to LTLO conversion is inferred, not measured.
   - Where: Section II, p. 7, and the LTLO labels in Fig. 1d.
   - The resistance anomaly is used as the structural probe. Low-temperature structural data at x = 0.125 under stress exist (Ref. 30, and the authors' earlier diffraction at x = 0.135).
   - Please compare the critical stresses quantitatively with Ref. 30, or add diffraction on these crystals. Otherwise, phrase the LTLO assignment as an inference.
   - Fig. 1d presents a full doping-temperature phase diagram at 0.23 GPa, including "Tc,3D ≈ T_so" across 0.095-0.165. It is based on three compositions, and the 0.125 data at 0.23 GPa do not even show full LTT suppression (Fig. 4b). Mark it as schematic and indicate where the measured points are.

5. The mechanistic conclusions go beyond the data.
   - Claims in question:
     - "stripe-related interactions enhance pairing strength";
     - stress converts static stripes into dynamic ones;
     - the non-magnetic regions support fluctuating stripes.
   - None of these is measured: no inelastic neutron or x-ray scattering, no charge-order measurement and no pairing-gap probe under stress.
   - The doping argument is also weak. A higher saturated Tc at 1/8 than at 0.115 and 0.135 (37 vs about 31 and 30 K) is a difference of a few kelvin, between single samples, measured with different maximum stresses (0.23 and 0.29 GPa vs 0.58 GPa). It could also arise from sample quality, strain inhomogeneity or criterion differences.
   - Please:
     - show that x = 0.135 has saturated (Fig. 3f still appears to rise at 0.29 GPa);
     - give sample-to-sample spread or measure a second crystal at x = 0.125;
     - present the pairing and dynamic-stripe interpretation as a hypothesis rather than a demonstrated result, in the Abstract, the Conclusion and the title.

6. The muSR volume-fraction extraction needs more detail and checks.
   - Missing details:
     - The magnitude of the weak transverse field is not given.
     - The wTF analysis says that "baseline asymmetry shifts were corrected by allowing P_TF(t) to be adjusted for each temperature". This is vague, and it could bias V_M(T), especially in the long gradual tails at 0.18-0.23 GPa (Fig. 2d).
     - Only about 50% of muons stop in the sample. It is not explained how the sample, hematite and cell contributions were separated and fixed.
   - Consistency checks:
     - The ZF-derived V_m (Eq. 3) and the wTF-derived V_M should agree at base temperature. Please show this.
     - The ZF spectra (Fig. 2c) and B_int (Fig. 4c) stop at 0.18 GPa. The text nevertheless says that coherent oscillations "persist up to the maximum applied stress of 0.23 GPa". Show the 0.23 GPa ZF spectrum or correct the statement.
   - Define how T_so is extracted from the broadened V_M(T) curves under stress. The quoted 33 K at 0.23 GPa is not obvious from Fig. 2d, where the signal extends to about 40 K.
   - Please give the fit parameters with uncertainties (in the supplement, which was not available with this submission).

7. The residual LTT hump was set to zero in the plotted data.
   - Where: the Fig. 3 caption states that for x = 0.125 and 0.135 "a small hump remains visible even at the highest applied stress, amounting to only a few percent. However, in panels (e) and (f), it was set to zero."
   - Why it matters: whether the LTT anomaly is fully suppressed is a stated result (the Abstract says "fully suppressed across all dopings").
   - Please plot the measured residual values with uncertainties, and revise "fully suppressed" accordingly.

8. The stress protocol and history are not described.
   - It is not stated whether stress was changed at base temperature, below T_LTT (about 55 K), or above it before cooling. Domain selection and pinning in the LTT phase could make the results history-dependent.
   - The same applies to whether the data at each stress were taken on warming, and whether the effects are reversible on releasing stress or survive thermal cycling.
   - The negative (tensile) points at -0.03 and -0.01 GPa in Fig. 3d and 3f need an explanation of how tension was applied and how the zero of force was determined.
   - For transport, please give the current direction relative to the stress axis and the measurement current. Heating matters near a sharp transition.

9. The ambient-pressure Tc,zero of x = 0.125 (about 23-25 K, Figs. 3e and 4a) is high compared with commonly reported in-plane zero-resistance temperatures for LBCO-1/8 crystals.
   - Please compare with literature values (for example Refs. 36 and 37).
   - Comment on whether the epoxy mounting introduces residual strain at "zero" force, or whether the doping differs slightly from 0.125. This bears directly on the baseline of the claimed enhancement.

C. Minor

10. Inconsistent numbers between the Abstract, the main text and the figures:
   - The Abstract gives Tc rising "from 5 K to 37 K under 0.5 GPa". The text gives Tc,mid rising from 3 to 35 K by about 0.23 GPa in susceptibility, and Tc,zero reaching 37 K at 0.56 GPa in resistivity.
   - The Abstract says V_M is reduced "by about a factor of two"; the text says 2.5.
   - T_so at zero stress is given as 40 K in the text but appears at about 42 K in Fig. 4a.
   - Please harmonise these and state in each case which criterion and which apparatus a number refers to.

11. The Fig. 2 caption says data go "up to 0.32 GPa", but the legends in Fig. 2b and 2d stop at 0.23 GPa. Either show the 0.32 GPa data or correct the caption.

12. In Fig. 3d-f the axis assignment is indicated only by small arrows. Use colour-matched axis labels. Also define "Peak (%)" explicitly (relative to which baseline and which normalisation).

13. In Fig. 4a the T_so dashed line extends to 0.6 GPa, although muSR data end at 0.23 GPa. Mark the extrapolated part.

14. Typos and references:
   - There is a broken citation on p. 2 ("x = 0.115?").
   - Section IV.C reads "The ZF-muSR signals shown in were analyzed"; the figure number is missing.
   - "Is is supported" appears in the Acknowledgments.
   - The Supplementary Notes 1-3 are cited but were not included.

15. The statement that 14 GPa of hydrostatic pressure raises Tc to only about 18 K needs a citation.

16. Specify the stress-transmission assumptions for the muSR cell. The conversion assumes that 480 N is distributed uniformly over 1.53 mm², through epoxy, into a plate whose free length (4 mm) is shorter than its width (5.1 mm). Give an uncertainty on the stress values.

17. Data availability "upon request" is weak for a study of this kind. Consider depositing the muSR and transport data.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one measurement that probes interlayer coherence directly under stress at x = 0.125. Possible choices are c-axis resistivity, H || ab ac susceptibility, or TF-muSR superfluid density, which would also give the superconducting volume fraction and test the percolation picture.
2. Take ac susceptibility and muSR on the same crystal across the full stress range up to 0.3 GPa or more, to test whether V_M keeps falling where the LTT anomaly disappears. Include a release-and-reapply cycle to check reversibility.
3. Present Fig. 4d as a like-for-like comparison (same criteria) and add the published uniaxial-stress Tc values for x = 0.115, 0.135 and Ref. 30 to place the 1/8 result in context.
4. Separate measured facts (Tc, V_M, B_int, T_so, ΔR_LTT versus stress) from interpretation (LTLO domains, dynamic stripes, PDW, pairing enhancement) in the Abstract and the Conclusion.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
