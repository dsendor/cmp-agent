Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript studies La2-xBaxCuO4 at exactly x = 1/8 under in-plane uniaxial compression applied 45° to the Cu–O bonds. It combines in situ ac susceptibility and ZF/weak-TF μSR on one crystal with resistivity under stress for x = 0.115, 0.125 and 0.135. The headline results are a rise of the 3D transition (Tc,mid) from ~3 K to ~35 K by about 0.2 GPa; a roughly 2.5-fold drop of the static spin-stripe volume fraction while Tso and Bint change only slightly; and suppression of the LTT-related resistivity peak, with Tc,zero ≈ 37 K and a resistive onset near 46 K at 0.56 GPa. Measuring superconductivity and the magnetic volume fraction together under stress at the commensurate composition is new and useful. The broader claims are less well supported: that stripe interactions enhance pairing, and that the stressed x = 1/8 crystal beats optimal LSCO and reaches "the highest bulk Tc reported in the 214 family."

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The Tc enhancement at x = 1/8 is large and seen in two independent probes: in situ ac susceptibility (Fig. 2b) and in-plane resistivity (Fig. 3b,e).
- μSR under uniaxial stress is technically demanding. Tracking VM(T, σ) at the composition where stripe order is strongest (Fig. 2d) is a real experimental step beyond the earlier x = 0.115 and 0.135 work.
- The two effects separate cleanly. The ordered volume fraction falls strongly while Tso and Bint stay nearly fixed (Figs. 2c,d; 4a,c). This is a concrete, falsifiable constraint on models of intertwined order.
- Resistivity is measured at three dopings with the same protocol, so the LTT-peak suppression and the Tc,zero rise can be compared across the 1/8 anomaly (Fig. 3).

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The claim that the stripe-stable composition reaches the highest Tc, and so that "stripe-related interactions enhance pairing," rests on a weak cross-doping comparison.**
   - *Where:* Abstract; Sec. II (pp. 3, 5, 7); Conclusion.
   - *What is wrong:* The evidence is Tc,mid ≈ 35 K for x = 0.125 against ~30 K for x = 0.115 and 0.135, and Tc,zero ≈ 37 K against ~31 K and ~30 K.
     - The susceptibility values for x = 0.115 and 0.135 come from earlier studies (Refs. 6, 19), done on different crystals and with different stress setups.
     - In the resistivity series there is one crystal per doping, and the stress ranges differ: up to 0.58 GPa for x = 0.125, but only 0.23 and 0.29 GPa for the other two (Fig. 3d–f).
     - No uncertainty on the doping level or on the stress calibration is given, and the reproducibility of the saturated Tc on a second crystal is not shown.
   - *Why it matters:* A 5–7 K difference between single samples does not by itself establish a doping trend. Even if the trend is real, it does not show that stripe interactions enhance pairing. An equally simple reading is that the underlying "LTT-free" Tc(x) dome of LBCO simply peaks near x ≈ 0.12–0.13. The paper does not test this alternative, and nothing in the data probes pairing strength or dynamic stripe correlations. μSR is, as the authors note, blind to fluctuating stripes.
   - *What to do:*
     - (a) Measure x = 0.115 and 0.135 up to the same maximum stress as x = 0.125, or show that their Tc,zero has truly saturated.
     - (b) Show a second x = 0.125 crystal, or a stress-release cycle on the same one.
     - (c) State the doping and stress uncertainties.
     - (d) Otherwise reword the pairing inference as a hypothesis. "Indicates that stripe-related interactions enhance pairing" should become "is consistent with."

2. **"Exceeds optimal LSCO" and "highest bulk Tc in the 214 family" rest on mismatched definitions and on a normalized susceptibility.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 4d; p. 5; pp. 6–7.
   - *Mismatched Tc definitions:* The 46 K value is a resistive onset in a stressed crystal whose transition is ~9 K wide (46 K → 37 K). The comparison is with LSCO onsets taken from a single 1989 study (Ref. 32). The susceptibility points in Fig. 4d show LBCO-1/8 (~38 K) comparable to optimal LSCO, not above it.
   - *Normalized susceptibility:* χAC in Fig. 2b is normalized to −1 at every stress. It therefore gives no shielding or volume fraction, yet "bulk" 3D superconductivity is claimed. That matters all the more because μSR shows ~40% of the muons still in magnetically ordered regions at 0.23 GPa.
   - *Internal inconsistency:* The abstract quotes "5 K to 37 K under 0.5 GPa" and a zero-resistance state at 37 K. The susceptibility rise is 3 K → 35 K and was reached by 0.23 GPa (Fig. 2b), while 37 K is the resistive Tc,zero at 0.56 GPa in a different cell.
   - *Literature context missing:* Other 214 Tc values are not discussed, for example oxygen-doped La2CuO4+δ and epitaxially strained LSCO films.
   - *What to do:*
     - (a) Compare like with like: the same criterion (onset, midpoint or zero), the same probe, and LSCO data with stated criteria.
     - (b) Give the unnormalized χ′ with a geometry/demagnetization-corrected shielding fraction, or a field-cooled Meissner fraction, at 0 and 0.23 GPa.
     - (c) Make the abstract numbers match the figures.
     - (d) Temper "highest bulk Tc in the 214 family" unless it can be backed with consistent criteria.

**B. Should address**

3. **The picture of the stressed state contradicts itself, and stress inhomogeneity is not ruled out as the source of the partial magnetic volume fraction.**
   - *Where:* p. 7; abstract.
   - *The contradiction:*
     - The discussion (p. 7) describes "an inhomogeneous state under stress, with coexisting LTT domains hosting static stripe order and LTLO domains supporting dynamic stripes," with percolation once 1−VM ≈ 40%.
     - The abstract and introduction speak of "spatially-uniform superconductivity" and "a homogeneous d-wave superconducting state."
   - *The untested alternative:* The μSR sample is a 7 × 5.1 × 0.3 mm³ plate glued with epoxy. The stress is likely non-uniform along the length, near the glued ends, and across the 5.1 mm width, and a thin wide plate may also bend. A stress gradient would naturally give a partial VM, with highly stressed regions non-magnetic and less stressed regions still LTT and ordered. That would mimic "intrinsic" phase coexistence and the percolation threshold.
   - *What to do:*
     - Give a finite-element stress map for both the μSR and the Razorbill mounts, and the fraction of the muon-exposed 4 × 5.1 mm² area within, say, ±20% of the nominal stress.
     - Discuss whether a gradient reproduces the gradual VM(σ).
     - Make the abstract match the inhomogeneous picture that the discussion actually supports.

4. **Stress scales from two different devices are put on one axis without cross-calibration.**
   - *Where:* Figs. 4a,b; Methods.
   - *The problem:*
     - Fig. 4a overlays Tc,zero (Razorbill FC100) and Tc,mid/Tso (piezo μSR cell). Fig. 4b plots VM (μSR cell) and the LTT peak (FC100) against a common σ.
     - The μSR stress is inferred from a force-sensor calibration (1 μV ≈ 30 N), and the Methods say the sample is "fixed to a Grade-2 Ti sample holder." It is unclear whether any force is shunted through the holder or epoxy.
     - The "convergence" of Tc,zero and Tc,mid at 0.15 GPa (Fig. 4a) and the VM–ΔR_LTT correlation (Fig. 4b) both depend on the two scales matching.
     - The introduction states the measurements were "performed on the same crystal," but the Methods describe a separate cell, and apparently separate crystals, for resistivity.
   - *What to do:* Cross-check one observable in both devices, for example Tc or the LTT anomaly in the μSR cell, or an ac-susceptibility Tc in the FC100. Clarify which crystals were used where, and give the stress uncertainty.

5. **LTT suppression is inferred only from the resistivity hump, and ΔR_LTT is treated as an LTT volume fraction.**
   - *Where:* p. 5; Fig. 4b ("LTT peak (%)"); Conclusion ("suppressing the LTT lattice symmetry").
   - *The problem:* The peak height reflects charge-stripe pinning and scattering, not directly the structural fraction. It also depends on the normalization R/R(100 K), which may itself change with stress.
   - *What to do:* Support the structural interpretation with diffraction at x = 1/8 under comparable stress (Ref. 30 is cited but not compared quantitatively), or state clearly that ΔR_LTT is a transport proxy.
   - *Data handling:* For x = 0.125 and 0.135 the residual hump "was set to zero" in Fig. 3e,f and then plotted as 0 in Fig. 4b. The actual residual values should be plotted with error bars instead.

6. **VM is not measured above 0.23 GPa, so the high-stress state is not characterized magnetically.**
   - *Where:* Fig. 4b; p. 7.
   - *The problem:* The resistive LTT signal keeps falling to ~0.5 GPa, and Tc,zero keeps rising to 0.56 GPa. Whether static order disappears where the LTT peak vanishes, or saturates near VM ≈ 0.35–0.4, decides between the "coexistence" and "suppression" readings. Also, the Methods give a maximum stress of 0.32 GPa and the Fig. 2 caption says data run "up to 0.32 GPa," yet no 0.32 GPa curve is shown.
   - *What to do:* Show the 0.32 GPa data if they exist, or say why they are omitted. Discuss what is expected above 0.23 GPa.

7. **The μSR background and normalization procedure needs to be spelled out.**
   - *Where:* Methods.
   - *The problem:* Only ~50% of the muons stop in the sample; the rest go into the hematite masks and the Ti holder. Yet VM(0 GPa) ≈ 0.95. The Methods say "baseline asymmetry shifts were corrected by allowing PTF(t) to be adjusted for each temperature." Without a fixed, independently determined background, a stress-dependent shift in sample illumination or cell position could change the apparent VM.
   - *What to do:* Give the fitted background fraction versus stress and the absolute asymmetries. Show that the high-T asymmetry is unchanged with stress. Add a fit-quality example for the Eq. (3) model at 0 and 0.18 GPa.

8. **Reversibility and the zero-stress reference are not shown.**
   - *Where:* Figs. 3–4.
   - *The problem:* At "0 GPa" in the FC100, x = 0.125 already shows Tc,zero ≈ 23–25 K (Figs. 3e, 4a). Thermal-contraction mismatch with the cell and epoxy can strain the crystal at nominal zero force. Stresses up to 0.58 GPa may also cause plastic deformation or cracking.
   - *What to do:* Show a stress-release curve returning to the zero-stress R(T) and χ(T). Compare with a free-standing crystal from the same boule.

9. **The mechanistic narrative goes beyond the data.**
   - *Where:* Introduction and Discussion.
   - *The problem:* The data contain no direct evidence for dynamic stripe correlations in the non-magnetic regions, for PDW-to-uniform d-wave conversion, or for restored interlayer Josephson coupling. Examples are the c-axis resistivity or Josephson plasma resonance, or inelastic scattering.
   - *What to do:* Frame these points as interpretation. Name the measurement that would test each one, for example ρc(T, σ) or neutron/RIXS on the stressed state.

**C. Minor**

10. **The claim that spin-stripe order "remains long range" is not shown by μSR.** Coherent ZF oscillations show a well-defined local field, not a correlation length. Rephrase, or cite diffraction.
11. **Figure and text disagree in several places.**
    - Fig. 2c shows ZF spectra and Fig. 4c gives Bint only up to 0.18 GPa, while the text says coherent oscillations persist to 0.23 GPa.
    - The Fig. 2b and 2d captions say "up to 0.32 GPa," but the legends stop at 0.23 GPa.
    - The abstract says "about a factor of two," while the text says "a factor of 2.5."
12. **Fig. 1d is drawn as a full T–x phase diagram at 0.23 GPa** (x ≈ 0.095–0.165, "LTLO" only), but it rests on three dopings and different stresses. At 0.23 GPa the x = 0.125 LTT peak is still ~50%. Mark it as schematic, and show the data points it is based on.
13. **Fig. 4d axis labels are unclear.** "LSCO_R" and "LBCO_Susceptibility" should be defined in the caption, and the stress value of each LBCO point should be given.
14. **Definitions are missing or misplaced.**
    - Define Tc,ons in Fig. 2b and say how it is extracted.
    - Define the tensile-stress sign convention.
    - The ZF-μSR section says "The ZF-μSR signals shown in were analyzed" with the figure reference missing.
    - The Methods cite Supplementary Notes 1–3 and Fig. S1, which are not provided.
15. **A reference in the introduction is broken** ("x=0.115?"). The Ref. 1 entry and the "is is" typo in the acknowledgments need correcting.
16. **The 14 GPa hydrostatic comparison (p. 3) has no reference.**

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a control with stress applied along the Cu–O bond ([100]_t). Under the authors' picture this should couple differently to the LTT tilt pattern. It would test whether the effect is symmetry-selective rather than a generic strain effect.
2. Measure c-axis resistivity, or ac susceptibility with the field in-plane, under stress. That would directly test the claim that stress restores interlayer coherence, the key mechanistic step.
3. Give the stress-dependent transition widths, and the Tc,zero–Tc,mid difference, as a measure of inhomogeneity alongside VM.
4. Deposit the μSR and transport data publicly rather than "upon request."

**5. Verdict**

Verdict: major revision
