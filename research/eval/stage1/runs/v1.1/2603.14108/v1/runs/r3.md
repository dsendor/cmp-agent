Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript studies La2-xBaxCuO4 at the commensurate x = 0.125 composition under in-plane uniaxial stress applied 45° to the Cu–O bond. It combines zero-field and weak-transverse-field μSR, in situ ac susceptibility and in-plane resistivity, and adds resistivity on x = 0.115 and 0.135. The headline results are a rise of the diamagnetic Tc,mid from ~3 K to ~35 K by 0.23 GPa, with only a modest drop of Tso (40 → 33 K) and of Bint but a 2.5-fold reduction of the static magnetic volume fraction, plus zero resistance at 37 K with a resistive onset of ~46 K at 0.56 GPa. Tuning the most stripe-stable composition into the highest-Tc state of the 214 family would be a striking result, and it bears on the question of whether stripes compete with or support pairing. The Tc enhancement near 1/8 under in-plane stress has, however, been reported before (Refs. 6, 19, 21, 30, including Ref. 30 at x = 1/8). The new elements are mainly the μSR volume-fraction data at x = 1/8 and the three-doping resistivity comparison.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Microscopic magnetic data (ZF and wTF μSR) and a bulk-sensitive superconducting probe (ac susceptibility) are taken in situ on the same stressed crystal. This lets VM, Bint, Tso and Tc,mid be tracked against one stress axis (Fig. 2, Fig. 4a–c).
- The separation between a robust ordering temperature and local moment (Tso, Bint) and a strongly reduced ordered volume fraction (VM) is a clear, informative observation (Fig. 2c,d; Fig. 4b,c).
- Resistivity on three dopings in one force-controlled cell gives a consistent picture: the LTT-related resistive hump decreases while Tc,zero increases (Fig. 3).
- The size of the effect (Tc,mid rising more than tenfold at a few tenths of a GPa) is large and robust to modest calibration errors.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The "bulk" high-Tc and "highest bulk Tc in the 214 family" claims rest on probes that do not establish bulk superconductivity at the stresses where those Tc values are reported. The claims appear in the abstract, p. 5 and p. 7 ("representing the highest bulk Tc reported in the 214 cuprate family"), and Fig. 4d.
   - The 37 K zero-resistance and ~46 K onset values come from four-probe in-plane resistivity at 0.56–0.58 GPa (Fig. 3b,e). Resistive zero needs only one filamentary path between the voltage contacts, and in a clamped, epoxy-mounted crystal that path can run through the most strained region.
   - The only bulk-sensitive probe, ac susceptibility, stops at 0.23 GPa and gives Tc,mid ≈ 35 K. That is at or below the LSCO optimum.
   - Even there, χAC is normalized to −1 and measures shielding, not superconducting volume. A minority superconducting shell can give full shielding.
   - The paper's own numbers show this ambiguity. At 0.09 GPa, Tc,mid is already ~28 K (Fig. 2b), while VM at 28 K is still ~0.8 (Fig. 2d) and the non-magnetic fraction at base temperature is only ~16%. The text itself says on p. 7 that ~20% is "likely insufficient for percolation".
   - The comparison in Fig. 4d sets resistive onsets under stress against 1989 LSCO data. It does not discuss other 214 systems with Tc of ~40–45 K, such as oxygen-intercalated La2CuO4+δ and compressively strained LSCO films. So "highest bulk Tc in the 214 family" is not established.

   What the authors should do:
   - Provide a measure of the superconducting volume or superfluid density under stress. TF-μSR below Tc at the stresses already reached is the obvious option with this setup; field-cooled Meissner fraction or an absolutely calibrated χ' would also help.
   - Measure susceptibility at stresses up to ≥0.5 GPa, for example with a coil in the FC100 cell.
   - Otherwise, restate the 37/46 K values as resistive (possibly filamentary) transitions and drop the "highest bulk Tc in the 214 family" wording.

2. The stress magnitude and homogeneity in the μSR cell are not established, yet the main interpretation depends on them. That interpretation includes the 3× larger critical stress at x = 0.125, the convergence of Tc,zero and Tc,mid at 0.15 GPa in Fig. 4a, and the picture of coexisting LTT and LTLO domains on p. 7.
   - Geometry: the crystal is 7 × 5.1 × 0.3 mm³ (Methods), with an exposed length of 4 mm against a width of 5.1 mm (Fig. 2a). It is glued with Stycast to a Ti holder.
   - Such a low length-to-width ratio is far from the regime where the stress in the central region is uniform and uniaxial. Epoxy transfer, end effects and differential thermal contraction can make the stress strongly non-uniform.
   - Calibration: the stress is inferred from a force-sensor calibration "reported previously" (30 N/μV). No in situ check is given, and the zero-stress state at low temperature is not characterized.
   - Effect on the VM result: a strain gradient across the muon-illuminated area would naturally produce what is observed in Fig. 2d. Part of the sample would be strained enough to lose static order while the rest keeps Tso and Bint nearly unchanged, and VM(T) at 0.18–0.23 GPa would develop a broad, almost linear temperature dependence. So the large VM reduction with nearly constant Tso and Bint, which the paper reads as intrinsic phase coexistence, could equally reflect the stress distribution.
   - Cross-cell comparison: Fig. 4a,b put μSR-cell data (one crystal, Ti holder, sensor calibration) on the same stress axis as FC100 resistivity data (different mounting, possibly a different crystal). This makes sense only if both stress scales are accurate.

   What the authors should do:
   - Provide finite-element modelling of the strain distribution in the μSR geometry, weighted by the muon stopping profile.
   - Give the uncertainty of the stress calibration, and if possible an independent cross-check, for example a known stress-sensitive transition or the Tc,mid(σ) of the same crystal measured in both cells.
   - Discuss explicitly whether the reduction of VM could be a strain-gradient effect.

B. Should address

3. The claim that the LTT phase is "fully suppressed across all dopings" is not supported as stated, and the correlation in Fig. 4b mixes two experiments.
   - The caption of Fig. 3 says that for x = 0.125 and 0.135 "a small hump just above Tc remains visible even at the highest applied stress … However, in panels (e) and (f), it was set to zero." Setting a residual signal to zero creates the abrupt drop from ~35% at 0.35 GPa to 0 at 0.48 GPa in Fig. 3e and Fig. 4b, and it contradicts the abstract's "fully suppressed".
   - The resistive hump is used as the only measure of LTT volume. How its height maps onto an LTT volume fraction is not shown.
   - Fig. 4b overlays VM (μSR cell, ≤0.23 GPa) and hump height (FC100 cell, to 0.58 GPa) and calls them "directly correlated" (p. 7), but the two series hardly overlap in stress range.

   What the authors should do:
   - Plot the measured residual hump with its uncertainty.
   - Compare quantitatively with the stress-dependent diffraction at x = 1/8 in Ref. 30: the stress at which the LTT superlattice peaks vanish.
   - Soften "fully suppressed" and "directly correlated" accordingly.

4. The data do not support the inference that "stripe-related interactions enhance pairing" (abstract, Sec. II, Conclusion).
   - The argument is that x = 0.125 reaches a higher optimal Tc than x = 0.115 and 0.135. But the three crystals were taken to very different maximum stresses: 0.23, 0.58 and 0.29 GPa respectively (Fig. 3).
   - Tc,zero at x = 0.125 is still rising at 0.58 GPa. Additional lattice compression unrelated to stripes, which raises Tc in LSCO films, could account for the extra few kelvin.
   - There is one crystal per doping, so sample-to-sample variation in oxygen content or Ba homogeneity at the few-kelvin level is not excluded.
   - No pairing-sensitive quantity (gap, Hc2, superfluid stiffness) is measured, so the conclusion that stress acts on phase coherence rather than pairing is interpretation, not result.

   What the authors should do: compare Tc at matched stress and at matched LTT-hump suppression for all three dopings, add a second crystal at x = 0.125 if possible, and present the pairing-versus-coherence statement as a hypothesis.

5. The percolation and competition argument on p. 7 is not quantitatively consistent with the figures.
   - The text says Tc saturates only when 1 − VM approaches 40%. But Fig. 2b shows Tc,mid ≈ 28 K at 0.09 GPa, where 1 − VM ≈ 16% at 700 mK and less near Tc.
   - Beyond ~0.15 GPa, Fig. 4a shows Tc,mid at or above Tso: at 0.23 GPa, Tc,mid ≈ 35 K against Tso ≈ 33 K. So static order then develops inside the superconducting state, and VM at 0.23 GPa grows from ~0.05 at 35 K to 0.37 at base temperature (Fig. 2d). If static order and superconductivity compete, VM(T) should show an anomaly or reduced growth below Tc.

   What the authors should do: analyse VM(T) and Bint(T) across Tc at each stress, and discuss Fig. 1d (Tc,3D ≈ Tso) in light of this.

6. Sample identity, reproducibility and reversibility are unclear.
   - The introduction (p. 2) says the measurements were "performed on the same crystal". The Methods instead describe a 7 × 5.1 × 0.3 mm³ μSR crystal and separate FC100 samples for x = 0.115, 0.125 and 0.135.
   - The paper should state which crystals were used for which panel and their dimensions in the FC100 cell.
   - It should show whether Tc, VM and the hump recover on releasing stress, since piezo-driven stress at 0.5 GPa can plastically deform or crack a crystal. It should also show whether results repeat on a second mounting or after thermal cycling.

7. The novelty relative to prior work needs to be stated concretely.
   - Ref. 30 reported Tc and stress-dependent X-ray diffraction at x = 1/8. Refs. 6 and 19 reported μSR and susceptibility under stress at x = 0.115 and 0.135.
   - The manuscript should say what Tc, critical stress and structural results Ref. 30 obtained, and how the present Tc and stress scale compare.
   - Otherwise readers cannot tell which of the abstract's findings are new.

8. The definitions and extraction of Tso, Bint and VM need more detail.
   - At 0.18–0.23 GPa, VM(T) has no clear onset (Fig. 2d), so Tso ≈ 33 K depends on the criterion. That criterion should be stated, with how the error bars in Fig. 4a were obtained.
   - For the ZF fits (Eq. 3), the paper should state which parameters were free and which were shared or fixed (fα, λT, λL, λnm), and how the hematite and holder background was treated.
   - It should also explain why Bint (Fig. 4c) and ZF spectra (Fig. 2c) stop at 0.18 GPa while VM extends to 0.23 GPa.

9. No control is given for the claim of symmetry selectivity.
   - The paper calls 45° stress "symmetry-selective" and attributes the effect to destabilizing the LTT tilt pattern.
   - Resistivity or susceptibility with stress along the Cu–O bond, at matched magnitude, would test whether the enhancement is specific to this direction or a generic effect of compression.
   - Citing earlier data on bond-direction stress would partly suffice.

10. The ac susceptibility method is under-described. The paper should give the excitation frequency and amplitude, the coil geometry relative to the sample, and how χ was normalized to −1 under stress (demagnetization, filling factor). This matters because Tc,mid is the paper's main bulk measure.

C. Minor

11. The numbers in the abstract and text do not match.
    - Abstract: Tc rises "from 5 K to 37 K" (onset values) and VM is "reduced by about a factor of two".
    - Text: Tc,mid rises from 3 K to 35 K, and VM drops by a factor of 2.5 (0.95 → 0.37).
    - Use one consistent definition in each place.
12. The caption of Fig. 2 says stress was applied "up to 0.32 GPa" (panels b and d), but the data shown stop at 0.23 GPa, and panel c stops at 0.18 GPa.
13. Several references and cross-references are broken or missing:
    - p. 2: "x=0.115?" has a broken citation.
    - p. 9: "The ZF-μSR signals shown in were analyzed" is missing the figure reference.
    - Supplementary Notes 1–3 and Figure S1 are cited but not provided.
    - The claim that "even a much larger hydrostatic pressure of 14 GPa increases Tc only to approximately 18 K" (p. 3) needs a reference.
14. The stress direction "45° to the Cu–O bond direction (denoted as [100])" is confusing next to Fig. 1b, which uses tetragonal indices. Give the direction explicitly in both tetragonal and orthorhombic notation.
15. Fig. 3d,f include tensile points (−0.01 to −0.03 GPa). Explain how the zero of stress is defined at low temperature, given thermal-contraction mismatch with the epoxy and the cell.
16. Fig. 1d is a schematic phase diagram "under 0.23 GPa", but at that stress the x = 0.125 LTT hump is only about half suppressed (Fig. 3e). Mark the figure as schematic and reconcile it with the data.
17. Fig. 4d: label the axes and legend consistently ("LBCO_R", "LSCO_Susceptibility"), and state how onset is defined for each data set.
18. Calling the stress "pressure" (p. 3) is confusing next to the hydrostatic-pressure comparison. Use "stress" throughout.
19. Data availability "upon request" is weak for a study whose key quantities are fitted. Consider depositing the μSR histograms and the resistivity and susceptibility data.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add TF-μSR superfluid-density measurements under stress at x = 0.125. They would turn the "bulk high-Tc" claim from a shielding signal into a volume-resolved result and would directly test the competition between static order and superconductivity.
- Provide finite-element strain maps of both stress cells and, if possible, measure one crystal in both cells, so that Figs. 4a and 4b share a reliable stress axis.
- Present the doping comparison at matched stress, and include the residual LTT hump rather than setting it to zero.
- Separate the measured results (Tc,mid, VM, Tso, hump suppression) from the interpretation (PDW frustration, dynamic stripes promoting pairing) in the abstract and conclusion.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
