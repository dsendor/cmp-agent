A. Must address

1. The claims that stressed x = 1/8 exceeds the optimal Tc of LSCO and reaches "the highest bulk Tc reported in the 214 cuprate family" are not supported by the probes and criteria used. These claims appear in the Abstract, p. 3 ("We essentially reach the optimal Tc of LSCO"), p. 5 (46 K "slightly exceeds the optimal Tc reported for LSCO"), p. 7 and Fig. 4d, and the title phrase "hosts optimal superconductivity" rests on them. The comparison depends on a resistive onset of about 46 K at 0.56–0.58 GPa. The onset criterion is not defined, the transition is about 9 K wide (46 K to 37 K), and the onset lies in the temperature range of the residual LTT/stripe hump and of 2D fluctuation superconductivity. The LSCO points come from a single 1989 study (Ref. 32) whose criteria may differ. Resistive zero needs only one filamentary path between the voltage contacts, and in a clamped, epoxy-mounted crystal that path can run through the most strained region. The only bulk-sensitive probe, ac susceptibility, stops at 0.23 GPa and gives Tc,mid ≈ 35 K. The susceptibility points in Fig. 4d (LBCO-1/8 about 38 K) and Tc,zero = 37 K are at or below the LSCO optimum (about 38–40 K), not above it. Even the susceptibility does not establish bulk superconductivity: χAC in Fig. 2b is normalized to −1 at every stress, so it measures shielding, not superconducting volume, and a minority superconducting shell can give full shielding. This matters because μSR still shows about 40% of muons in magnetically ordered regions at 0.23 GPa, and at 0.09 GPa Tc,mid is already about 28 K while the non-magnetic fraction is only about 16%, which the text itself (p. 7) calls "likely insufficient for percolation". Higher Tc values in the 214 family (oxygen-intercalated La2CuO4+δ, compressively strained LSCO films, about 40–45 K) are not discussed. The authors should define the onset criterion and show it on the curves; compare like with like (zero with zero, midpoint with midpoint, same probe, LSCO data with stated criteria); give an unnormalized χ′ with a geometry/demagnetization-corrected shielding fraction or a field-cooled Meissner fraction at 0 and 0.23 GPa; ideally measure the superconducting volume or superfluid density under stress (TF-μSR below Tc with the existing setup) and extend susceptibility to ≥0.5 GPa (for example a coil in the FC100 cell). Otherwise they should restate the 37/46 K values as resistive (possibly filamentary) transitions, describe the result as "comparable to optimally doped LSCO", drop or qualify "highest bulk Tc in the 214 family" against the literature, and revise the title accordingly.

2. The zero-stress reference for the "eleven-fold enhancement of 3D superconductivity" was measured in a different geometry from the stressed data, and the stressed geometry is not specifically sensitive to 3D coherence. In Section II (p. 3) and Fig. 2b, the 0 GPa curve was measured "prior to mounting" as dc magnetization in 0.5 mT applied parallel to the CuO2 planes, so the shielding currents must cross the layers. The in situ ac data under stress were taken with the excitation field "predominantly along the c-axis", where shielding currents flow in the planes, so the signal also responds to 2D (decoupled-layer) superconductivity. The authors' own resistivity puts the in-plane (2D) transition of x = 0.125 at Tc,zero ≈ 23–25 K at zero stress (Figs. 3e, 4a), close to the in situ Tc,mid of about 25–28 K at 0.09 GPa. The jump from 3 K to 25–35 K in Fig. 2b, and the Tc,mid points labelled "3D" in Fig. 4a, may therefore partly reflect the change of measurement geometry rather than the onset of interlayer coherence, yet the central claim that stress converts 2D into 3D superconductivity depends on this comparison. The authors should show an in situ ac susceptibility curve at zero (or minimal) force after mounting, in the same coil geometry as the stressed data; explain why an H || c ac shielding signal measures 3D rather than 2D superconductivity, or add a probe of interlayer coherence under stress (c-axis resistivity, ac susceptibility with H || ab, Josephson plasma resonance, or TF-μSR superfluid density); and report the absolute shielding fraction rather than χ normalized to −1.

3. The magnitude and homogeneity of the stress in the μSR cell are not established, and a stress gradient could produce the main new μSR result. The crystal is a 7 × 5.1 × 0.3 mm³ plate glued with Stycast to a Grade-2 Ti holder, with a free length of 4 mm against a width of 5.1 mm (Methods, Fig. 2a). The stress is taken as 480 N divided uniformly over the full 1.53 mm² cross-section, using a force-sensor calibration "reported previously" (about 30 N/μV), with no in situ check, no stated uncertainty, no discussion of whether force is shunted through the holder or epoxy, and no characterization of the low-temperature zero-stress state. Such a low length-to-width ratio is far from the regime where the stress in the central region is uniform and uniaxial; epoxy transfer, end effects, differential thermal contraction and bending of a thin wide plate can make it strongly non-uniform. A stress gradient across the muon-illuminated area would naturally give what Fig. 2d shows: highly stressed regions lose static order while less stressed regions keep LTT order with nearly unchanged Tso and Bint, and VM(T) at 0.18–0.23 GPa develops a broad, almost linear temperature dependence. The paper instead reads the large VM reduction with constant Tso and Bint as intrinsic coexistence of LTT and LTLO domains with a percolation threshold (p. 7), and the 3× larger critical stress at x = 0.125 and the convergence of Tc,zero and Tc,mid at 0.15 GPa (Fig. 4a) also rest on the stress scale. The picture is also internally inconsistent: the Discussion describes "an inhomogeneous state under stress", while the Abstract and Introduction speak of "spatially-uniform superconductivity" and "a homogeneous d-wave superconducting state". The authors should provide finite-element stress maps for the μSR (and Razorbill) mounts, weighted by the muon stopping profile, with the fraction of the 4 × 5.1 mm² exposed area within, say, ±20% of nominal stress; give an uncertainty on the stress values; discuss explicitly whether a gradient reproduces the gradual VM(σ); and make the Abstract consistent with the inhomogeneous picture.

B. Should address

4. The inference that "stripe-related interactions enhance pairing" rests on a weak cross-doping comparison (Abstract; Sec. II, pp. 3, 5, 7; Conclusion; title). The evidence is Tc,mid ≈ 35 K at x = 0.125 against about 30 K at x = 0.115 and 0.135, and Tc,zero ≈ 37 K against about 31 and 30 K. The susceptibility values for the other dopings come from earlier studies (Refs. 6, 19) on different crystals and stress setups. In the resistivity series there is one crystal per doping, and the maximum stresses differ: 0.58 GPa at x = 0.125 versus 0.23 and 0.29 GPa at x = 0.115 and 0.135, and Fig. 3f still appears to rise at 0.29 GPa. Tc,zero at x = 0.125 is still rising at 0.58 GPa, so extra lattice compression unrelated to stripes (which raises Tc in LSCO films) could account for the few kelvin. Sample quality, oxygen content or Ba homogeneity, strain inhomogeneity, and criterion differences are not excluded, and no doping or stress uncertainties are given. Even if the trend is real, an equally simple reading is that the LTT-free Tc(x) dome of LBCO peaks near x ≈ 0.12–0.13, and no pairing-sensitive quantity (gap, Hc2, superfluid stiffness) is measured, so the statement that stress acts on phase coherence rather than pairing is interpretation. The authors should take x = 0.115 and 0.135 to the same maximum stress or show that they have saturated; compare Tc at matched stress and at matched LTT-hump suppression; show a second x = 0.125 crystal; state doping and stress uncertainties; and otherwise present the pairing inference as a hypothesis ("is consistent with" rather than "indicates").

5. Stress scales from two devices, and possibly different crystals, are placed on one axis without cross-calibration. Fig. 4a overlays Tc,zero (Razorbill FC100) and Tc,mid/Tso (piezo μSR cell), and Fig. 4b plots VM (μSR cell) and the LTT peak (FC100) against a common σ. The "convergence" of Tc,zero and Tc,mid at 0.15 GPa and the VM–ΔR_LTT correlation both depend on the two scales matching, given strain transmission through Stycast and very different aspect ratios. The Introduction (p. 2) says the measurements were "performed on the same crystal", but the Methods describe a separate cell and apparently separate samples for resistivity, whose dimensions are not given. The authors should state which crystal was used for which panel and its dimensions in the FC100, cross-check one observable in both devices (for example Tc or the LTT anomaly in the μSR cell, or ac susceptibility in the FC100, ideally on the same crystal), and give the uncertainty of each stress scale.

6. The "direct correlation" between VM and LTT suppression in Fig. 4b, and the percolation picture built on it, is tested over only part of the stress range, and the high-stress state is not characterized magnetically. VM is measured only up to 0.23 GPa (and is nearly flat between 0.18 and 0.23 GPa), while the LTT peak keeps falling to about 0.5 GPa and Tc,zero keeps rising to 0.56 GPa, so the two series hardly overlap. Whether static order disappears where the LTT peak vanishes, or saturates near VM ≈ 0.35–0.4, decides between the "coexistence" and "suppression" readings. The Methods state that 0.32 GPa was reached and the Fig. 2 caption says data run "up to 0.32 GPa", yet no such curve is shown. The authors should show the 0.32 GPa data or explain their omission, extend μSR to higher stress if possible, discuss what is expected above 0.23 GPa, and soften "directly correlated".

7. The LTT suppression and the LTT-to-LTLO conversion are inferred from the resistivity hump alone, and ΔR_LTT is treated as an LTT volume fraction (p. 5, p. 7, Fig. 4b "LTT peak (%)", the LTLO labels in Fig. 1d, Conclusion "suppressing the LTT lattice symmetry"). The hump reflects charge-stripe pinning, scattering and percolation of transport paths, need not be linear in structural volume, and depends on the normalization R/R(100 K), which may itself change with stress. Low-temperature diffraction at x = 1/8 under stress exists (Ref. 30, and the authors' earlier diffraction at x = 0.135) but is not compared quantitatively. The authors should compare their critical stresses with the stress at which the LTT superlattice peaks vanish in Ref. 30, or add diffraction on these crystals; otherwise state clearly that ΔR_LTT is a transport proxy and present the LTLO assignment as an inference.

9. The percolation and competition argument on p. 7 is not quantitatively consistent with the figures. The text says Tc saturates only when 1 − VM approaches 40%, but Fig. 2b shows Tc,mid ≈ 28 K at 0.09 GPa, where 1 − VM ≈ 16% at 700 mK and less near Tc. Beyond about 0.15 GPa, Fig. 4a shows Tc,mid at or above Tso (at 0.23 GPa, Tc,mid ≈ 35 K against Tso ≈ 33 K), so static order develops inside the superconducting state, with VM growing from about 0.05 at 35 K to 0.37 at base temperature (Fig. 2d). If static order and superconductivity compete, VM(T) should show an anomaly or reduced growth below Tc. The authors should analyse VM(T) and Bint(T) across Tc at each stress and reconsider Fig. 1d (Tc,3D ≈ Tso) in this light.

10. The μSR extraction of VM, Tso and Bint needs more detail and consistency checks. The weak transverse field magnitude is not given. Only about 50% of muons stop in the sample, the rest in the hematite masks and Ti holder, yet VM(0 GPa) ≈ 0.95–0.97, and the statement that "baseline asymmetry shifts were corrected by allowing PTF(t) to be adjusted for each temperature" is vague; without a fixed, independently determined background, a stress-dependent shift in illumination or cell position could change the apparent VM, especially in the long tails at 0.18–0.23 GPa. For the ZF fits (Eq. 3), it is not stated which parameters (fα, λT, λL, λnm) were free, shared or fixed. The ZF-derived Vm and wTF-derived VM should be shown to agree at base temperature. The ZF spectra (Fig. 2c) and Bint (Fig. 4c) stop at 0.18 GPa, although the text says coherent oscillations "persist up to the maximum applied stress of 0.23 GPa". Tso at 0.18–0.23 GPa depends on the criterion, since VM(T) has no clear onset; the quoted 33 K at 0.23 GPa is not obvious from Fig. 2d, where signal extends to about 40 K. The authors should give the fitted background fraction and absolute asymmetries versus stress, show that the high-T asymmetry is unchanged with stress, add fit-quality examples at 0 and 0.18 GPa, show the 0.23 GPa ZF spectrum or correct the statement, define the Tso criterion and how the Fig. 4a error bars were obtained, and tabulate fit parameters with uncertainties.

11. The zero-stress baseline of the resistivity is questionable. At nominal zero force in the FC100, x = 0.125 already has Tc,zero ≈ 23–25 K (Figs. 3e, 4a), which is high compared with commonly reported in-plane zero-resistance temperatures for LBCO-1/8 crystals (for example Refs. 36, 37). Thermal-contraction mismatch with the cell and epoxy could strain the crystal at "zero" force, or the doping could differ slightly from 0.125; either bears directly on the baseline of the claimed enhancement. The authors should compare with literature values, compare with a free-standing crystal from the same boule, and discuss residual mounting strain and doping uncertainty.

12. The stress protocol, history and reversibility are not described. It is not stated whether stress was changed at base temperature, below T_LTT (about 55 K), or above it before cooling; domain selection and pinning in the LTT phase could make the results history-dependent. It is not stated whether data were taken on warming, whether Tc, VM and the hump recover on releasing stress, or whether results repeat after thermal cycling or remounting; stresses up to 0.58 GPa may plastically deform or crack the crystal. The tensile points at −0.01 to −0.03 GPa in Fig. 3d,f need an explanation of how tension was applied, the sign convention, and how the zero of force is defined at low temperature. For transport, the current direction relative to the stress axis and the measurement current (heating near a sharp transition) should be given. The authors should describe the protocol and show a release-and-reapply cycle returning to the zero-stress R(T) and χ(T).

13. The mechanistic narrative goes beyond the data. Stress converting static into dynamic stripes, the non-magnetic regions supporting fluctuating stripes, PDW-to-uniform d-wave conversion, and restored interlayer Josephson coupling are not measured: there is no inelastic neutron or x-ray scattering, no charge-order measurement, no pairing-gap probe, and no c-axis transport under stress, and μSR is blind to fluctuating stripes. The authors should separate measured results (Tc, VM, Bint, Tso, ΔR_LTT versus stress) from interpretation in the Abstract, Conclusion and title, frame these points as hypotheses, and name the measurement that would test each (for example ρc(T, σ), neutron or RIXS on the stressed state).
