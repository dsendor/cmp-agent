Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors measure focused-ion-beam-structured single-crystal Sr2RuO4 rings with normal-metal contacts, where Josephson coupling appears without any external superconductor. They report a violation of Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)|, a zero-field Josephson diode effect of a few percent, a narrowed and skewed central interference lobe, current-switchable bistable Ic states in in-plane field, and half-integer Shapiro steps. They interpret all of these as evidence for mobile chiral domain walls of a two-component, time-reversal-symmetry-breaking order parameter. A phase-sensitive, microscopic transport signature of TRSB in Sr2RuO4 would matter a great deal, given the conflict between muSR/Kerr and scanning-SQUID results. The novelty over the group's earlier npj Quantum Materials work (Ref. 23) is the explicit TRSB test and the in-plane-field phenomenology. However, the TRSB signal is small, and the main alternative explanations have not yet been excluded.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- There is no superconducting proximity contact, so every Josephson effect has to come from Sr2RuO4 itself. This avoids the main ambiguity of the earlier Nb/Sr2RuO4 hybrid experiments.
- The analysis pipeline for Ic extraction and remanent-field removal is documented (Supplementary S2, S7). It is validated on synthetic data (Fig. S18) and on a conventional MoGe/Ag SNS SQUID that shows no TRSB (Figs. S8-S10, S12b).
- Shapiro steps confirm the Josephson nature of the coupling in rings and in a singly connected geometry (Fig. 1c,d; Fig. S2). This rules out a pure Little-Parks reading of the oscillations.
- The phenomenology is rich and appears in several devices: Rings 1-3 for the TRSB analysis and Ring 3 for the bistable state, with temperature series on Ring 2. The data are openly deposited.
- The bistable-state switching depends on field polarity in a way consistent with symmetry (Fig. 3, Fig. S14).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The headline TRSB/zero-field-diode signal has not been separated from a constant current offset or from field-history and temperature-drift artifacts. In Fig. 2c (Ring 2), ΔI_TRSB has a roughly field-independent negative background of about -5 µA at 1000, 800 and 400 mK. Combining Fig. S6 (η = -2.0, -3.0, -3.7 %) with the Ic values in Fig. 2c (about 130, 100 and 70 µA) gives a zero-field |Ic+ - |Ic-|| of about 5 µA at all three temperatures. The absolute asymmetry is therefore nearly temperature-independent while Ic changes by a factor of two. That is the signature of a DC offset in the bias current, or of an asymmetric threshold or thermoelectric effect, not of an intrinsic diode, which should scale with Ic and vanish at Tc. Supplementary S2 corrects voltage offsets only, and the bootstrap error bars propagate voltage noise only ("this method only accounts for the uncertainty in measured voltage"). The authors' own control (Fig. S10) also drifts at larger fields, which they attribute to temperature drift, so the same apparatus can produce a ΔI of order 1 µA without TRSB.

   Beyond offsets, the decisive test of spontaneous TRSB in a multi-component superconductor is missing: the sign of ΔI_TRSB and of η should be set by the domain configuration. The authors should:
   - report η and ΔI_TRSB over several zero-field thermal cycles through Tc, which should randomize the sign or keep it fixed if pinned, and after field cooling in +Hz and -Hz, which should train the sign;
   - repeat the measurement with the bias-current leads swapped, or show the current-source offset directly, and with the order of the positive and negative current sweeps reversed;
   - show ΔI_TRSB for both field-sweep directions;
   - state how many devices and cooldowns were measured, and how many showed a significant ΔI_TRSB, with a systematic error budget (threshold choice, field step, H0 uncertainty).

   If the sign is fixed by geometry across thermal cycles, the authors need to explain how that is compatible with spontaneous symmetry breaking. As it stands, the claim in the abstract and conclusion rests on a few-µA asymmetry whose temperature scaling points to an extrinsic origin.

2. The bistable Ic states and the half-integer Shapiro steps have conventional explanations that the paper does not exclude. The Discussion and Conclusion state that these features "cannot originate from an s-wave superconductor."
   - **Flux through the junction cross-section.** In-plane fields of 10-20 mT on arms 0.25-0.7 µm thick (Table S1) thread flux of order one Φ0 through each junction's cross-section: for example 20 mT × 0.45 µm × (2λ + d) ≈ 0.4 µm gives about 1.7 Φ0. The authors' own Fig. S16 shows a Fraunhofer-like in-plane pattern with minima near 4.5 and 9.9 mT, with fractional steps near those minima, which the text itself attributes to suppression of the first harmonic. Multivalued Ic states from trapped Josephson vortices in extended junctions, which can be switched by bias current and whose roles swap under field reversal, are a standard conventional effect. So is a fluxoid change in a SQUID loop with βL ≳ 1.
   - **Half-integer steps from SQUID dynamics.** Half-integer Shapiro steps arise in an ordinary dc SQUID at non-integer flux bias with finite inductance. A small misalignment of the in-plane field gives an out-of-plane component (1° at 20 mT ≈ 0.35 mT, a sizable fraction of the ~3 mT period in Fig. 2a).
   - **Dismissal of vortex flow.** The paper rules out vortex flow by comparing the Josephson penetration depth with the arm width. This does not address the flux threading the junction cross-section, which is set by the thickness. The half-integer steps are also not reproducible after a field reset (Fig. S15).

   The authors should:
   - estimate the in-plane flux per junction, λJ and βL for each ring, and the field-misalignment angle;
   - show that the bistability and the fractional steps do not follow the in-plane Fraunhofer minima of Fig. S16;
   - otherwise, restrict the claim to "consistent with" and remove "cannot originate from s-wave superconductivity" for these features.

B. Should address

3. The toy model for the narrow central lobe has three free parameters per curve, and its fitted magnitude has not been checked against existing limits. In Supplementary S3, φm, a and Φint^max are all free and fitted separately for each dataset (Fig. S11). The fitted Φint^max = 0.24 Φ0 (Ring 1) and 0.16 Φ0 (Ring 2) imply a spontaneous flux from a single chiral domain far larger than the edge-current magnetization bounded by scanning SQUID (Refs. 28-30, cited in the Introduction).
   - **Conflict with scanning-SQUID limits.** The authors should estimate the flux expected from chiral edge currents in a ring of these dimensions and compare it with the fitted values. If they disagree, this contradiction should be stated.
   - **Conventional alternatives for the narrow lobe.** These should be modelled: the field-dependent Gaussian envelope of the individual junctions combined with junction asymmetry and inductance, and vortex entry into the large banks at a few mT, which changes flux focusing. The latter is especially relevant given the discrete jumps in ΔI_TRSB in Fig. 2b,c.
   - **Effective area.** The effective-area normalization (π r_in r_out) should be justified, since the ring width is comparable to λ and the banks are large.

4. The control device does not match the Sr2RuO4 measurement conditions closely enough. The only no-TRSB control (Figs. S8-S10, S12b) is a MoGe/Ag SNS SQUID with a different geometry and resistance. Its TRSB comparison window covers just about ±2 mT, and the text and caption disagree on how much of it overlaps ("central four periods" in the text against "first three peaks" in the caption).
   - **Better references.** Stronger references would be a FIB-structured ring from a conventional superconductor with the same lead and bank geometry, or a 3K-phase Sr2RuO4 ring (which the authors say lacks a Josephson effect). Either should be measured in the same cryostat and wiring and run through the same ΔI_TRSB and η analysis, including the current-offset check in item 1.
   - **Field range.** The control comparison should cover the same ±10 mT range as Fig. 2a.

5. The temperature and device dependence of the TRSB signal is inconsistent and is not discussed.
   - **η grows toward Tc.** |η| increases with temperature, from 2 % at 400 mK to 3.7 % at 1000 mK (Fig. S6). An order-parameter effect would be expected to vanish at Tc.
   - **Ring 3 at a single temperature.** Ring 3 shows significant TRSB at 1100 mK but not at 1200 mK (Fig. S5), and no lower-temperature data are given.
   - **Ring 1 at a single temperature.** Ring 1 is analysed only at 50 mK.
   - **Narrow lobe without TRSB.** In Fig. S5a, Ring 3 at 1200 mK is called "normal SQUID behavior," yet its central lobe is already 0.82 Φ0 against 0.92-0.93 Φ0 for its neighbours. This means a narrowed central lobe does not by itself indicate TRSB.

   The authors should show η(T) and ΔI_TRSB(T) for all rings down to base temperature and up to Tc, and reconcile these trends with the domain-wall picture.

6. The Ic definition and the significance threshold are not specified per dataset. Ic is defined with a 0.1 or 0.2 I0RN threshold "depending on the degree of rounding" (Supplementary S2), but the paper does not say which threshold applies to which figure. The IVs in Fig. 3 are strongly rounded and asymmetric in shape between polarities, so a threshold criterion can by itself produce a polarity asymmetry. Please:
   - state the threshold used for each dataset;
   - show that η and ΔI_TRSB are robust to the threshold choice;
   - quote η and ΔI_TRSB with significance in units of the total (statistical plus systematic) uncertainty.

7. The sample statistics and the "universal" claims are not quantified. The text says the features are "universally observed in a large selection of samples" and that bistability appears "in all samples, irrespective of device geometry," but it shows three rings in the main text and four devices in the Supplement. A table of all devices measured should report, for each one:
   - Shapiro steps;
   - the narrow central lobe;
   - ΔI_TRSB;
   - η;
   - bistability.

   It should also cover how these properties change after FIB re-milling (Ring 2, Fig. S1) and after thermal cycling.

8. The case for the Josephson origin of the oscillations needs tightening. The main text argues that Shapiro steps "can only originate from the Josephson effect," which rules out kinematic vortices and phase-slip centres. However, phase-slip lines and kinematic vortices can also phase-lock to RF and produce voltage steps at Φ0 f. The Shapiro evidence is strong (Fig. 1d histogram), but the wording should be moderated, or the step-power dependence should be compared with RCSJ Bessel-function behaviour.

9. Several materials-quality statements go beyond the evidence. The Methods conclude from Tc ≤ 1.5 K that the devices have no edge dislocations or strain, and state that "neither the exfoliation nor the focused ion beam structuring reduces the quality of the crystal." Yet Tc ranges from 1.3 to 1.5 K across devices (Table S1), and the cited TEM damage depth (about 10 nm) comes from a different study (Ref. 48). Because the authors argue against strain-texture-stabilized accidental-degeneracy scenarios (Discussion, Refs. 64-65) on the basis of "strain-free crystals," this point needs direct support, such as a TEM or strain characterization on these devices. Otherwise it should be softened.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a dedicated figure of η and ΔI_TRSB across repeated zero-field cooldowns and ±Hz field cooldowns for at least two rings, together with a current-lead-reversal check. This would turn the TRSB claim from suggestive into direct.
- Measure the in-plane Fraunhofer pattern of each ring at the fields used for Figs. 3 and 4, and overlay the bistability map of Fig. S17 on it, to separate domain-wall physics from junction-flux effects.
- Give quantitative estimates of the spontaneous flux expected from chiral domains in these rings, and confront the fitted Φint^max with the scanning-SQUID bounds.
- Measure a FIB-processed ring of a conventional superconductor, or a 3K-phase Sr2RuO4 ring, with identical geometry and wiring as a null reference for the full analysis chain.

5. Verdict: major revision
