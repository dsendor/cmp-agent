Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors study Josephson junctions that form spontaneously in FIB-structured single-crystal Sr2RuO4 rings, where no other superconductor is present. They report a violation of Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)|, a zero-field Josephson diode effect of a few percent, a narrower central SQUID lobe (about 0.73 Φ0), current-switchable bistable Ic states under in-plane field, and half-integer Shapiro steps. They interpret all of these as chiral domain-wall junctions of a two-component order parameter. If the TRSB signal is shown to be intrinsic, it would be the first phase-sensitive, device-level evidence of TRSB in pristine Sr2RuO4 without proximity contacts. That would matter both for the pairing-symmetry debate and for field-free Josephson diodes.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The platform is clean: no external superconductor, RRR above 100, Tc ≤ 1.5 K with no 3K-phase. Shapiro steps (Fig. 1c,d; Fig. S2) firmly establish Josephson coupling in rings, in a singly connected geometry and in a straight-bar geometry (Fig. S4).
- The TRSB test Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| is the right symmetry criterion. Unlike a plain diode asymmetry, it is not fooled by ordinary geometric or inductance asymmetries. The authors back this with RCSJ modelling (S4) and a control SNS SQUID (Figs. S9, S10, S12).
- The remanent-field correction (S7) minimizes the asymmetry. That makes it conservative: the reported ΔI_TRSB is close to a lower bound on the residual asymmetry, not an artifact of the shift.
- The effect appears in several devices (Rings 1–3), and in Ring 3 it switches on between 1200 and 1100 mK (Fig. S5), which suggests it is tied to the superconducting state.
- The phenomenology is rich and new for an intrinsic junction: an anomalous central lobe, bistable Ic states whose switching polarity reverses with field direction (Fig. 3, Fig. S14), and polarity-selective half-integer steps (Fig. 4). Data are openly deposited.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Extrinsic TRSB from trapped flux and field history has not been excluded. This is the central gap for the headline claim. The only extrinsic source the analysis removes is a uniform remanent field, as a rigid shift H → H + H0 (S2, S7). A different, well-known source breaks Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| in an otherwise conventional SQUID and produces a zero-field diode: an Abrikosov vortex or other trapped flux pinned in the wide Sr2RuO4 banks or arms near one junction. Such flux imposes a fixed phase offset on that junction, which acts like a φ0 shift. The lobe-width corrections are not uniform in H, so they cannot be removed by a single H0.

   Several features of the data point to history-dependent flux rather than a smooth intrinsic signal:
   - ΔI_TRSB for Ring 2 (Fig. 2c, all three temperatures) shows abrupt, step-like jumps of about 10 µA at specific fields, followed by sawtooth-like recovery. This is the signature of discrete flux entry or exit events.
   - The control SQUID itself develops a systematic ΔIc drift of about 1 µA at higher fields (Fig. S10). The reported effects in the rings are of order 2–8 µA.
   - Time reversal also reverses the field-sweep direction. A comparison of Ic+(H) on one sweep with Ic-(-H) from the same sweep therefore compares states with different magnetic histories. The paper does not say which sweep directions enter Figs. 2b,c and S5.

   The authors should:
   (i) compare Ic+(H) from the up-sweep with |Ic-(-H)| from the down-sweep, and show both sweep directions for each dataset;
   (ii) report the zero-field diode η after zero-field cooling in a compensated field (Hall-probe or SQUID-calibrated), repeated over several thermal cycles above Tc;
   (iii) field-cool in ±Hz, then measure at H = 0. An intrinsic chiral-domain origin predicts trainable sign reversal of η and ΔI_TRSB, by analogy with the Kerr training the authors cite in S3. Trapped vortices predict a different dependence, scaling with the cooling field.
   A table of the sign and size of η across cool-downs and devices would turn the present observation into a test of the claim.

2. The in-plane-field results (bistable Ic, half-integer steps) are attributed to domain walls without excluding out-of-plane misalignment and in-plane vortices. Figs. 3 and 4 and S14–S17 use in-plane fields of 10–20 mT, and up to 70 mT in Fig. S17. The SQUID period is about 4.5 mT for out-of-plane field (Fig. 2). A misalignment of only 1° at 20 mT gives about 0.35 mT out of plane, a sizable fraction of a lobe. The paper states no alignment procedure or misalignment estimate.

   The arms are also 0.25–0.7 µm thick (Table S1). An in-plane field therefore threads the junction cross-sections, and Fig. S16 indeed shows a Fraunhofer-like Ic(H) with about 5 mT period. The authors concede that the 1/3- and 1/2-steps near its minima are trivial. In-plane Abrikosov vortices can also enter the banks and arms at these fields, since the in-plane Hc1 of Sr2RuO4 is small. Vortices pinned near a junction and pushed by the Lorentz force of the bias current naturally give two Ic states, switched by current polarity and with roles exchanged on field reversal, exactly as in Fig. 3 and Fig. S14.

   The argument on p. 11, that the Josephson penetration depth exceeds the junction width, addresses Josephson vortices but not Abrikosov vortices in the electrodes. Because these features are presented as further evidence for domain walls (Discussion; Conclusion), the authors should:
   - state and quantify the field alignment, for example by nulling the SQUID oscillation shift with a small Hz trim at each in-plane field;
   - show the bistability and the half-integer steps with the out-of-plane component compensated;
   - discuss and test the in-plane vortex scenario, for example through the dependence of the switching threshold on in-plane field magnitude, and whether bistability survives warming to just below Tc and re-cooling at fixed Hx.

B. Should address

3. There is no geometry-matched null device. The only control is a MoGe/Ag SNS SQUID (Fig. S8), which differs in material, Hc1, pinning, junction type and geometry. Showing that the analysis pipeline returns ΔI_TRSB = 0 for a conventional device is valuable, but it does not test whether FIB-made constriction rings with wide banks generate extrinsic asymmetries. The authors cite earlier 3K-phase rings that show no SQUID oscillations (refs 23, 47), so they cannot serve. A ring of the same geometry milled from a conventional superconductor, such as Nb or MoGe film, or a conventional Dayem-bridge SQUID of matching dimensions, measured and analysed identically, would directly address concerns 1 and 4.

4. Alternative origins of the narrow central lobe. The 0.73 Φ0 central lobe (Fig. 2a) is called irreconcilable with established mechanisms (p. 8). Other possibilities are not discussed:
   - the effective area depends on field because of Meissner screening and flux focusing in the wide banks;
   - vortex entry into the banks or leads above a threshold field changes the effective area;
   - field-dependent kinetic inductance.
   Each could make the first lobe differ from the rest. The lobe widths also depend on the chosen area convention π r_in r_out. The authors should show that the anomaly is independent of sweep direction (the text says so; please show the data), give its temperature dependence, and test whether it changes after field excursions large enough to trap vortices in the banks.

5. The domain-wall toy model does not account for the TRSB it is meant to explain. In the S3 model, the domain fluxes cancel at zero field ("At zero external field, where the domain wall is centered, the contributions of the domains cancel out", p. 13). So it does not by itself produce the zero-field diode or ΔI_TRSB, which the Discussion instead attributes to the Sigrist–Agterberg φ0-like energy (S5). The sigmoid in Eq. 4 is ad hoc. The fits in Fig. S11 have at least three free domain parameters plus the RCSJ asymmetry, screening and envelope parameters, and they visibly miss the peak heights. Eq. 3 couples the complex order parameters linearly to B without specifying the gauge-invariant form.

   The authors should:
   - fit Ic+ and Ic- jointly, so that the model has to reproduce ΔI_TRSB(H) and η;
   - list which parameters are free and which fixed;
   - compare with an RCSJ fit that includes a φ0 shift in one junction and a uniform H0, as a minimal non-domain alternative;
   - give the Fig. S11d panel the caption announces.
   Without this, "consistent with domain walls" rests on qualitative agreement.

6. The temperature dependence is sparse and partly counterintuitive. η is shown at only three temperatures for one device (Fig. S6). Its magnitude grows from about 2% at 400 mK to about 3.7% at 1000 mK, increasing toward Tc. Meanwhile Ring 3 shows TRSB at 1100 mK but none at 1200 mK (Fig. S5), roughly 0.3 K below its stated Tc of 1.5 K (Table S1). Neither trend is discussed against the expectation for an order-parameter-driven φ0 junction. Finer temperature steps up to Tc, for η and the integrated ΔI_TRSB in at least two devices, would test whether the TRSB onset tracks Tc (as μSR finds) or appears at a separate lower temperature, which would bear on the pairing discussion on pp. 14–15.

7. The device statistics and the "universal" statements are not quantified. The main text says data come from "three rings" and the features are "universally observed in a large selection of samples" (p. 5). S6 says bistability is "a universal property". S1 refers to four devices, one of which (Ring 2) was re-milled four times. The number of devices measured and the fraction that show (a) Shapiro steps, (b) TRSB above error, (c) the narrow central lobe and (d) bistability are not given. Neither are the sign and magnitude of η per device. Please add such a table. It matters for the claim that the junctions are deterministic domain walls fixed by confinement (Fig. 1a).

8. The error bars cover only voltage noise. The bootstrap uncertainty on Ic "only accounts for the uncertainty in measured voltage" (S2, p. 11). The uncertainty on the fitted H0, thermal drift (shown to be about 1 µA in Fig. S10), and the choice of voltage criterion (0.1 or 0.2 I0RN "depending on the degree of rounding") are not propagated.

   The criterion matters especially here. The IV curves differ in shape between polarities and states (Figs. 3, S7, S14), so a fixed voltage threshold can produce an apparent Ic asymmetry when the rounding differs between polarities. The authors should:
   - show that ΔI_TRSB and η are insensitive to the criterion, for example by giving them at 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 I0RN;
   - include the H0 uncertainty in the error bars of Fig. 2;
   - state H0 for each dataset.

9. Spontaneous junctions as domain walls rather than constriction weak links. Shapiro steps prove Josephson coupling but not its origin. The arm widths are about 250–380 nm (Table S1), only a few ξ0 = 66 nm, and the necks are narrower still. Phase-slip or Dayem-bridge behavior, possibly helped by FIB-damaged edges (about 10 nm), is a candidate. The argument rests mainly on the earlier work (ref. 23) and the absence of the effect in 3K-phase rings. The Gaussian Ic(H) of the singly connected device (Fig. S3) is acknowledged to be characteristic of long diffusive junctions, which the authors say they do not expect. A brief, quantitative comparison of the expected Dayem-bridge or phase-slip response for these dimensions with the observed Ic, IcRN and Ic(T) (Fig. S1) would strengthen the domain-wall assignment.

C. Minor

10. Eq. 10 minimizes [Ic+(H+H0) − Ic-(H+H0)]², which compares the two polarities at the same field, a diode-type comparison. Eq. 9 instead uses |Ic-(-H)|. Please state which expression was actually used. If Eq. 10 as written was used, the correction would partly remove genuine TRSB.

11. The Fig. 2 caption calls r_in and r_out the inner and outer "diameter". Given the area πr_in r_out, they should be radii. Please also state explicitly the area used to normalize the Φ0 lobe widths for each ring, since Table S1 lists diameters.

12. Fig. S10 caption: "showing the absence of no TRSB" is a double negative. The caption says "first three peaks" while the text says "central four periods". Please reconcile.

13. Fig. S14 caption: "roles of I+ and I+" should read I+ and I-. The caption refers to "Figure 4 of the main text" where the bistable IVs are in Figure 3. It says the field is "-20 mT", while panel (a) is +20 mT.

14. The angle conventions are inconsistent. In Fig. S16, φ is measured from the y-axis (30°); in Fig. S17, φ = 0 lies along x, "the direction along the ring". The S17 caption also says no hysteresis occurs below 10 mT, but the map starts at 10 mT. Please unify the conventions and extend or annotate the map.

15. The figures do not consistently state the measurement temperature and the device. Examples: the Fig. 2b temperature; Fig. 4 at 50 mK but at what frequency (S15 says 145 MHz, Fig. 1 uses 144 MHz); the ring used for Fig. S5. Please list ring, temperature, frequency and RF power in every caption.

16. The main text claims a "complete breakdown of the RCSJ model" (p. 5). Strictly, what is shown is that a two-junction sinusoidal-CPR RCSJ model with a uniform field cannot reproduce the data. Please phrase it accordingly.

17. Fig. S11's caption promises a panel (d) that is absent. Ref. 62 contains a LaTeX artifact ("textitSuperconducting").

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a dedicated TRSB-robustness figure for one ring: ΔI_TRSB(H) and η for both sweep directions, after zero-field cooling and after ±Hz field cooling, over several thermal cycles, with H0 and the criterion dependence included in the error bars.
- Calibrate and report the in-plane field alignment, and repeat a key bistability and half-integer-step measurement with the out-of-plane component nulled.
- Measure a geometry-matched conventional-superconductor ring made by the same FIB process, as a null device for the full set of claimed anomalies.
- Extend the phenomenological model to fit Ic+ and Ic- jointly and reproduce the zero-field diode, and compare it with a minimal φ0-plus-trapped-flux SQUID model so the reader can see what the domain-wall picture explains that the simpler alternative does not.

5. Verdict: major revision
