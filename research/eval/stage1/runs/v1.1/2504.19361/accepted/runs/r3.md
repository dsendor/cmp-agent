Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports transport on FIB-structured single-crystal Sr2RuO4 rings and bars with normal-metal (Ti/Au) contacts, so any Josephson coupling has to be intrinsic to the crystal. The authors report a violation of Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)|, a zero-field Josephson diode effect of a few percent, a narrowed central SQUID lobe (≈0.73 Φ0), bistable critical-current states that a training current switches under in-plane field, and half-integer Shapiro steps. They interpret all of these as Josephson coupling across chiral superconducting domain walls of a two-component order parameter. If the time-reversal symmetry breaking (TRSB) holds up, it would be the first device-level, phase-sensitive transport evidence for TRSB in unstrained Sr2RuO4 without a proximity interface. That would bear directly on the long-running pairing-symmetry debate and would point to junctions that are intrinsic to the material. The central TRSB claim, though, rests on a small asymmetry (a few μA out of 60–130 μA). Its extraction involves a fitted field offset and field-sweep protocols that are not fully described.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The device concept is clean. Sr2RuO4 is the only superconductor in the circuit, so proximity-induced TRSB, which complicates hybrid Nb/Sr2RuO4 junctions, is excluded.
- The data cover several independent signatures: Shapiro steps in doubly and singly connected devices (Fig. 1c,d; Supp. Fig. S2), Ic(H) point-symmetry tests on three rings (Fig. 2; Supp. Fig. S5), bistable Ic (Fig. 3; Supp. Figs. S14, S17) and fractional Shapiro steps (Fig. 4; Supp. Figs. S15, S16).
- The analysis pipeline for extracting Ic and testing point symmetry is documented, and it was checked on a conventional MoGe/Ag SNS SQUID that shows no TRSB within error (Supp. Figs. S8–S10, S12). This control is useful.
- Ring 3 shows symmetric patterns at 1200 mK and asymmetric ones at 1100 mK (Supp. Fig. S5). This is a temperature-onset internal control.
- The authors are open about tensions with the chiral d-wave picture (the missing second heat-capacity jump), and the data are publicly deposited.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The point-symmetry test is not shown to be protocol-symmetric, in a system the authors themselves describe as history-dependent.
   - **What is missing.** Time reversal maps a field sweep from −H to +H onto a sweep from +H to −H, and it also reverses the order in which current polarities are applied. The paper never states how Ic+(H) and Ic−(H) in Fig. 2 and Supp. Fig. S5 were acquired: the field-sweep direction, whether both polarities come from the same IV sweep, and the order within each IV.
   - **Why it matters.** The central claims are that domain walls move with field (Discussion; Supp. Note S3) and that Ic is bistable and set by bias history (Fig. 3). Comparing Ic+ at +H with Ic− at −H taken from one sweep direction therefore compares states with different histories. A fully time-reversal-symmetric but hysteretic system would violate Ic+(H) = |Ic−(−H)| under such a protocol.
   - **The jumps in Fig. 2c.** The abrupt steps in ΔI_TRSB in Fig. 2c (for example about 10 μA near ±2–3 mT at 1000 mK) look like history-dependent switching, not a smooth intrinsic asymmetry.
   - **What to do.** State the protocol explicitly. Show up- and down-sweeps separately. Perform the time-reversal-correct comparison: Ic+(H) on the up-sweep against |Ic−(−H)| on the down-sweep, with the current-polarity order reversed as well. Show that ΔI_TRSB survives that comparison beyond the sweep-to-sweep scatter of repeated identical sweeps.

2. Extrinsic sources of broken time-reversal symmetry (trapped flux, remnant-field offset) are not excluded by cooldown and field-history tests, and the error budget does not cover them.
   - **The remnant-field correction.** The remnant field H0 is not measured. It is found by minimising the asymmetry integral (Supp. Note S7, Eqs. 9–10), which assumes a single constant offset. It does not account for:
     - vortices trapped in the large Sr2RuO4 banks or leads (Hc1 along c is only a few mT, and demagnetisation in thin flakes lowers it further);
     - field-dependent offsets from magnet hysteresis;
     - flux focusing that changes with the field.
     Each of these produces Ic+(H) ≠ |Ic−(−H)| and a "zero-field" diode at the fitted H = 0 in an ordinary asymmetric SQUID with finite βL.
   - **The error budget.** The quoted uncertainty comes from a bootstrap over voltage noise only (Supp. Note S2). It does not include sweep-to-sweep reproducibility, uncertainty in H0, or thermal drift. The control SQUID itself shows a drift-induced ΔI of about 1 μA (Supp. Fig. S10), comparable to the 2–4 μA asymmetries in Ring 3 (Supp. Fig. S5b).
   - **What a chiral-domain origin predicts.** Within the authors' own picture, the sign of the zero-field diode and of ΔI_TRSB should depend on the domain configuration chosen at Tc. It should therefore be random, or trainable, across thermal cycles through Tc, and it should be reversible by field-cooling in ±H_z. Trapped flux in the magnet or banks would instead give a sign tied to the magnet history.
   - **What to do.** Show ΔI_TRSB and η at H = 0 over several zero-field cooldowns, after field-cooling at opposite signs, and after degaussing or quenching the magnet. Report H0 for each dataset with its uncertainty, and propagate that uncertainty into ΔI_TRSB and η. Without these tests, the main TRSB claim cannot be separated from conventional flux-trapping artifacts.

B. Should address

3. The bistable Ic states (Fig. 3; Supp. Figs. S14, S17) have a conventional SQUID explanation that is not addressed.
   - **The alternative.** In a SQUID with screening parameter βL ≳ 1, Ic(Φ) is multivalued. Each fluxoid state has its own Ic, and driving the bias past Ic in one polarity switches the fluxoid state. Polarity-alternating switching follows naturally from this. Reversing the field exchanges the roles of the two current polarities (Supp. Fig. S14b), which is exactly what time-reversal symmetry requires; it is not evidence against it.
   - **Why it is plausible here.** The applied field is nominally in-plane (10–20 mT). A misalignment of about 1° gives about 0.2–0.35 mT out of plane, which is a sizeable fraction of a flux quantum for these ring areas. Kinetic inductance with λ ≈ 190 nm in sub-μm arms may not be small.
   - **What to do.**
     - Estimate βL for each ring (geometric plus kinetic) from the measured Ic and dimensions, or from the modulation depth of Ic(H).
     - Quantify the field misalignment.
     - Show whether bistability persists in the singly connected (cut) Ring 2 device or in other singly connected devices, where fluxoid states do not exist. The text says it appears "irrespective of device geometry", but no singly connected data are shown.
   - **The angle map.** The Supp. Fig. S17b map is noisy and shows features near φ ≈ 0°, 180° and 360°. Showing repeatability of this map would help.

4. The half-integer Shapiro steps (Fig. 4; Supp. Figs. S15, S16) do not uniquely indicate a non-sinusoidal current-phase relation (CPR) of the junction.
   - **Conventional origins.** An asymmetric two-junction interferometer with finite inductance at non-zero flux has an effective non-sinusoidal CPR and shows half-integer steps. The authors themselves note fractional steps at the Ic(B) minima (Supp. Fig. S16). Here, too, a small out-of-plane component of the "in-plane" field supplies flux.
   - **Drive regime.** At 144–145 MHz, Ω = f/(2eIcRn/h) is roughly 0.2. In this low-frequency regime, subharmonic structure is sensitive to capacitance, noise and inductance.
   - **Reproducibility.** The steps change after the field is merely reset (Supp. Fig. S15, which also has missing integer steps). That suggests sensitivity to an uncontrolled flux state.
   - **What to do.** Model the measured response with RCSJ including βL and asymmetry. Show the half-integer steps in a singly connected device, and their dependence on drive power and frequency (a map like Fig. 1d for the low-Ic state). Only then attribute them to the φ0/φ-junction energy of Sigrist and Agterberg (Supp. Note S5).

5. The narrow central lobe (0.73 Φ0) has non-domain explanations that are not excluded.
   - **What the comparison rests on.** The lobe widths are normalised to an assumed area π r_in r_out (Fig. 2 caption). The comparison is therefore really with the outer lobes.
   - **The alternative.** A field-dependent effective area would also give a narrow central lobe flanked by Φ0-periodic lobes. For example:
     - Meissner flux focusing by the large banks at low field;
     - vortex entry into the banks at a few mT, which changes focusing above that field.
   - **The toy model.** The model in Supp. Note S3 has free parameters φm, a and Φint^max. It fits Ring 1 and Ring 2 (Supp. Fig. S11) but makes no independent prediction.
   - **What to do.**
     - Compare devices with different bank sizes.
     - Check whether the anomaly scales with bank geometry or with ring geometry, using Ring 4's straight-arm geometry.
     - Show the field-sweep-direction data that the text says were checked.
     - Report how many free parameters the fit has relative to the number of independent data features.

6. Sample statistics are thin, and "universal" is not quantified.
   - **What is claimed.** The text says the features are "universally observed in a large selection of samples" (p. 5), yet TRSB data are shown for three rings and bistability mainly for Rings 1 and 3.
   - **What to report.**
     - How many devices were measured in total, and how many showed each signature: Shapiro steps, ΔI_TRSB above error, the narrow central lobe, bistability, and half-integer steps.
     - The sign and magnitude of η across devices.
     - Whether any device with Tc ≈ 1.5 K lacked these features.
   - **Why it matters.** It distinguishes an intrinsic property from a device-specific one, and it is the natural null-control population.

7. The temperature dependence of the TRSB is sparse and in places counter-intuitive.
   - **What the data show.** η in Ring 2 grows in magnitude from −2% at 400 mK to −3.7% at 1000 mK, from only three points (Supp. Fig. S6). In Ring 3, TRSB appears at 1100 mK but is absent at 1200 mK (Supp. Fig. S5), although Tc is about 1.5 K. Zero-field μSR and Kerr data place the TRSB onset at Tc in unstrained material.
   - **What to do.** Provide denser η(T) and ΔI_TRSB(T) data up to Tc on at least one device. Discuss whether the onset below Tc and the growth of η with T fit a domain-wall scenario or rather point to a thermally activated or extrinsic origin.

8. The step from Shapiro steps to "junctions spontaneously appear in both arms" is overstated.
   - **The statement.** Page 7 says Shapiro steps "can only originate from the Josephson effect", ruling out kinematic vortices.
   - **Why it is overstated.** Phase-slip centres and kinematic vortex flow in narrow superconducting bridges are well known to phase-lock to microwaves and produce Shapiro-like steps.
   - **Weak links from fabrication.** The narrow necks and the FIB-damaged edges (≈10 nm) are natural weak links. The earlier work [23] is cited against constriction junctions, but this paper should briefly restate why constriction or phase-slip weak links, which are themselves time-reversal symmetric, cannot produce the step structure. The answer should cover Rn, the step widths against power, and the IcRn values (e.g. 80 μA × 19 mΩ ≈ 1.5 μV for Ring 1).
   - **Why it matters.** The domain-wall interpretation depends on the junctions not being geometric weak links.

9. The strain and order-parameter arguments in the Discussion and Methods are stronger than the evidence.
   - **The strain claim.** Tc ≤ 1.5 K is said to demonstrate "the absence of any edge-dislocations or strain" (p. 16). It shows only the absence of the 3K-phase. It does not exclude:
     - differential thermal-contraction strain from the SrTiO3 substrate;
     - inhomogeneous strain near the FIB-cut edges;
     - strain at the constrictions.
     These are exactly what the accidental-degeneracy scenarios [64, 65] require.
   - **Why it matters.** That argument is used to favour a symmetry-protected chiral state over accidental degeneracy. Given that the chiral d_xz + id_yz state conflicts with heat-capacity data [59, 60], the conclusion should be phrased as "consistent with a multi-component order parameter" rather than as discriminating between candidate states.

C. Minor

10. **Supp. Eq. 10** writes Ic−(H + H0) without the sign flip or absolute value, unlike Eq. 9 and the stated test Ic+(H) = |Ic−(−H)|. Please correct it, and state the integration window used for the minimisation.

11. **Cross-references are inconsistent.**
    - Main text p. 7 cites "Supplementary Figure S3" for the singly connected Shapiro steps; these are in Fig. S2.
    - Main text p. 13 cites "Supporting Figure S5" for a doubly connected device without constricted leads; Fig. S5 shows Ring 3 TRSB data, and that device does not appear to be shown.
    - Methods cites "Supporting Figure S2" for re-milled Tc values; this is Fig. S1.
    - The Supp. Fig. S14 caption refers to "Figure 4 of the main text" (it should be Fig. 3) and writes "I+ and I+".
    - The Supp. Fig. S11 caption describes a panel (d) that is absent.
    - The Supp. Fig. S10 caption says "first three peaks" and "absence of no TRSB", while the text says "central four periods".

12. **Dimensions and definitions do not match.**
    - The Fig. 2 caption defines r_in and r_out as "diameters" in an area formula.
    - The main text gives a typical outer diameter of 1.2 μm, while Table S1 lists 1.01 μm for Ring 1.
    - Please state which dimensions are used for the Φ0 normalisation in Fig. 2a.

13. **Heating and contacts.** Currents of about 100 μA flow through Ti/Au contacts to the crystal at 50 mK base temperature. Please give the contact resistances and confirm four-probe geometry. Also state whether the effective temperature at the 50 mK data points is known; Ic still varies strongly between 400 mK and 50 mK in some panels.

14. **Missing scale and parameter information.**
    - Fig. 1d should state the microwave power at the sample, or at least the attenuation, since 0–20 dBm is the source power.
    - Fig. 2 should state the voltage criterion (0.1 or 0.2 I0Rn) used for each dataset.

15. **The Supp. Note S3 free energy (Eq. 3)** couples the field linearly to Ψ± without specifying the order-parameter quantity (for example angular momentum or magnetisation) that carries the coupling. A sentence relating it to a physical coupling would help.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one compact "TRSB robustness" figure for a single device. It should show ΔI_TRSB and η at H = 0 for up- and down-sweeps, across several zero-field cooldowns, and after ±H_z field-cooling, with error bars that include H0 uncertainty and sweep-to-sweep scatter.
2. Measure a singly connected Sr2RuO4 bridge of the same geometry for the zero-field diode, the bistability and the half-integer steps. This separates junction-intrinsic effects from SQUID/fluxoid effects.
3. Quantify βL and the field misalignment for each ring, and include them in an RCSJ simulation of the Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 measurements. That would show directly whether the conventional SQUID model can or cannot reproduce them.
4. Report device statistics in a table (total devices measured and the fraction showing each signature), with the sign of η per device and per cooldown.

5. Verdict: major revision
