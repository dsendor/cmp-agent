Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports that FIB-structured microrings of unstrained, intrinsic-phase Sr2RuO4 (Tc about 1.5 K) host Josephson junctions that the authors attribute to pinned superconducting domain walls. The central claim is that these junctions violate Ic+(H) = Ic-(-H) and show a zero-field supercurrent diode effect, which the authors take as direct transport evidence of time-reversal symmetry breaking (TRSB) in Sr2RuO4. Secondary claims are a central SQUID lobe narrower than Phi0 (attributed to field-driven domain-wall motion), current-switchable bistable Ic states under in-plane fields, and half-integer Shapiro steps. If the TRSB signal is shown to be intrinsic, this would be an important result: it would give a device-level TRSB probe that complements muSR and Kerr in one of the most debated unconventional superconductors.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The Josephson character of the rings is well documented. Integer Shapiro steps with the correct height hf/2e appear at two frequencies (Fig. 1c), the power dependence is shown (Fig. 1d), and Shapiro steps persist in a singly connected arm (Fig. S3).
- The Ic(H) analysis is described in detail (Sec. S2): offset subtraction, a threshold criterion, bootstrap errors and remnant-field symmetrization. It is benchmarked on a conventional MoGe/Ag SQUID that preserves Ic+(H) = Ic-(-H) within error (Fig. S9). The symmetrization shifts both branches equally, so it cannot create a zero-field diode.
- The asymmetry appears in three rings (Rings 1, 2 and 3; Fig. 2 and Fig. S6) and at several temperatures in Ring 2 (Fig. 2c). Ring 3 shows the asymmetry switching on between 1200 and 1100 mK.
- The narrowed central lobe (about 0.73 to 0.86 Phi0) is reproduced in three devices and does not depend on sweep direction.
- The bistable Ic states are switched deterministically by current (Fig. 3), and the roles of the two current polarities exchange when the in-plane field is reversed (Fig. S13). This is a clean, reproducible phenomenology.
- The approach studies domain-wall Josephson physics without a hybrid interface. It removes the stochastic switching that limited earlier Nb/Sr2RuO4 experiments.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Extrinsic, history-dependent sources of broken time-reversal symmetry are not excluded, and the test specific to chiral domains is missing. Ic+(H) ≠ Ic-(-H) shows that the device's state is not time-reversal invariant. It does not by itself show that the superconducting order parameter breaks TRS. Any trapped magnetic flux does the same: an Abrikosov vortex pinned in the 250 to 700 nm thick arms or banks, flux trapped in the leads during field sweeps, or field history in the magnet. All of these produce Ic+(H) ≠ Ic-(-H), skewed lobes and a zero-field diode in a conventional two-junction loop. The paper does not state the cooling protocol for the Fig. 2 data (zero-field cooled, field cooled, compensated field). It does not report whether the ΔIc(H) pattern survives thermal cycling above Tc, or whether its sign is fixed or random from one cooldown to the next. In the authors' own picture, the domain-wall positions are fixed by the geometry, but which side is Psi+ and which is Psi- should be set at random on each cooldown, or by the sign of a cooling field. That is the decisive test that separates intrinsic chiral domains from trapped flux. The authors should:
   - field-cool through Tc in small +Hz and -Hz (and in a carefully nulled zero field) and show that ΔIc(H) reverses sign, or maps onto itself under H → -H, as chiral-domain training predicts;
   - repeat zero-field cooldowns and report the sign and shape of ΔIc(H) for each;
   - estimate the field at which vortices enter the arms and banks, and show that the field ranges in Fig. 2 (±10 mT) stay below it, or that the asymmetry does not depend on the maximum field reached before the sweep.
   Without this, the title claim of "direct evidence of TRSB in unstrained Sr2RuO4" is not separated from extrinsic flux trapping.

2. In Ring 2 the asymmetry looks like a polarity offset, and the paper has not shown that it belongs to the device rather than the measurement. In Fig. 2c, ΔIc stays negative at about -5 to -10 µA over almost the whole field range at 400, 800 and 1000 mK. The text confirms this: the sign changes only twice, and the "diodicity persists over the entire range of measured fields". A nearly field-independent ΔIc is what one expects from:
   - a dc current offset of a few µA in the bias source;
   - a polarity-dependent thermoelectric or offset voltage combined with the 0.1 to 0.2 I0RN threshold;
   - asymmetric rounding of the positive and negative branches.
   It is not obviously what a phi0 or phi junction in a nearly symmetric SQUID would give, since that mainly shifts or skews the pattern. The control SQUID (Fig. S9) was measured on a different device, and it is not stated that the same wiring and cryostat were used. It also shows a small systematic negative ΔIc (about -0.3 to -0.8 µA) that grows at the edges and is attributed to temperature drift, so drift and offsets in this setup are not negligible. The authors should:
   - swap the current (and voltage) leads, or reverse the wiring, on Ring 2 and show that ΔIc keeps its sign relative to the device;
   - report the measured zero offset of the current source;
   - show ΔIc(H) for several threshold criteria (for example 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 I0RN) and for a switching-current definition;
   - give the spread of Ic± over repeated IV sweeps at fixed field, not only the bootstrap error from voltage noise;
   - measure the control SQUID in the same setup as Ring 2.
   The zero-field diode effect, which the authors cite as a key signature, depends on this point.

B. Should address

3. The bistable Ic states have a conventional alternative that is not discussed: in-plane Abrikosov vortices. The arms are 0.25 to 0.7 µm thick, comparable to or larger than 2λ. An in-plane field of 10 to 20 mT along x can therefore put vortices parallel to the film into the arms and banks. The bias current exerts a Lorentz force J × B on these vortices that reverses with either the current or the field. That matches what is observed:
   - a positive training current selects one state and a negative one the other (Fig. 3c,f);
   - the roles swap when the field is reversed (Fig. S13);
   - nothing happens below about 10 mT (Fig. S16), consistent with a vortex-entry threshold;
   - the angle and magnitude needed vary from sample to sample.
   Fluxoid-state switching of the loop should also be excluded with an estimate of βL; the kinetic inductance of the arms may be non-negligible. The authors argue against in-plane vortex flow only for the half-integer steps (Josephson penetration depth larger than the junction width), not for the bistability. The paper presents the bistable states as "compelling evidence for the presence of superconducting domains" (Conclusion), so the authors should:
   - estimate Hc1 parallel to ab for the actual cross-sections;
   - show the dependence of the bistability on the field component perpendicular to the current, compared with the component parallel to it;
   - report how well the field is aligned in-plane and the residual Hz.
   Alternatively, they should soften the claim.

4. The half-integer Shapiro steps have several trivial explanations that are not excluded, and the steps do not reproduce reliably. Half-integer and fractional steps arise in a two-junction loop with finite inductance when the loop is frustrated near half a flux quantum. They also arise near Fraunhofer nodes of the individual junctions, which the authors acknowledge for the 1/3 steps in Fig. S15. A misalignment of only 1 to 2 degrees at 10 mT in-plane field gives an Hz of about 0.2 to 0.35 mT, an appreciable fraction of the roughly 4.4 mT period. After the field was reset, the half-integer steps disappeared from one polarity, and an integer step went missing (Fig. S14). The authors should:
   - report the out-of-plane field component, and how it was nulled, for Fig. 4 and Fig. S14;
   - show the half-integer steps as a function of microwave power (a map like Fig. 1d) to separate them from rounding and subharmonic features;
   - show that the half steps survive when the SQUID is biased at integer flux.
   Until then, "a different current-phase relation between the two Ic states" should be presented as tentative.

5. The domain-wall origin of the junctions rests mostly on inference and earlier work. Shapiro steps establish Josephson coupling, but phase-slip centres and FIB-damaged constrictions also give Shapiro steps. The statement that the steps in the singly connected arm "confirm the presence of a chiral domain wall" (Fig. S3 caption) is not justified by that measurement alone. The main arguments are:
   - the IV curves are non-hysteretic;
   - the two arms are highly symmetric;
   - in an earlier paper, 3K-phase rings did not oscillate.
   A null device made with the same FIB process, capping and geometry from a conventional superconductor, or from a 3K-phase or deliberately strained Sr2RuO4 ring that does show oscillations, would directly test whether FIB weak links alone give Ic+(H) = Ic-(-H). Measuring this device in the same setup and showing symmetry would strengthen both the domain-wall and the TRSB claims considerably. The MoGe/Ag SQUID differs in material, junction type and loop size.

6. The central-lobe narrowing is described by an underconstrained phenomenological model, and the "complete breakdown of the RCSJ model" is overstated.
   - The toy model (Sec. S3, Eqs. 4 and 5) uses a sigmoid that, as the authors say, "does not directly derive from theory". It adds φm, a and Φint^max to the RCSJ parameters (αI, βL and a Gaussian envelope).
   - The fits in Fig. S10 miss the lobe heights noticeably, and no goodness of fit or parameter correlations are given.
   - The RCSJ comparison in Sec. S4 is restricted to a sinusoidal current-phase relation with symmetric resistances.
   - A SQUID made of phi0 or phi junctions, or of junctions with a second harmonic (the very physics proposed in Sec. S5), is not tried, so a lumped-element model with such junctions could well capture the data.
   Other conventional explanations of a field-dependent effective area should also be discussed, such as flux focusing by the thick banks or vortex entry into the banks after the first lobe. The authors should fit a generalized RCSJ model with phi0 or second-harmonic junctions. They should report the fitted parameters, uncertainties and the data-to-parameter ratio, and state what the toy model predicts that could be tested independently (for example, how the narrowing depends on temperature).

7. How many devices were measured, and the temperature dependence, are under-reported. The text says the features are "universally observed in a large selection of samples", but data are shown for four rings. TRSB is shown for three of them, and only Ring 2 has a temperature series. Ring 4 (Fig. S5) is shown only for positive Hz, so the TRSB test cannot be done on it. The authors should:
   - state how many devices were measured, how many show ΔIc ≠ 0, and the sign and size of the effect in each;
   - plot ΔIc/Ic (and the zero-field diode efficiency) against T up to Tc for at least one ring.
   The onset matters: in Ring 3 the asymmetry appears between 1100 and 1200 mK, while the table in Fig. S1 gives Tc = 1.5 K. muSR and Kerr place TRSB onset at Tc, so whether the device signal follows Tc or a lower temperature bears directly on its interpretation.

8. The remnant-field correction and the error bars need clarifying.
   - H0 is chosen by minimizing the integral of [Ic+(H+H0) - Ic-(H+H0)]^2 (Eq. 1). In a device that genuinely breaks TRS, this minimization absorbs part of the signal and depends on the cutoff HR. The authors should report the H0 values, compare them with an independent remnant-field calibration (a Hall probe, or the control SQUID measured in the same magnet sequence), and show that the size of ΔIc is robust to HR.
   - The error bars in Fig. 2 come only from voltage noise, which the authors state, and so understate the real uncertainty. Earlier Sr2RuO4 hybrid devices showed telegraph switching, so the error bars should include repeat-sweep scatter.

9. The discussion goes beyond the data. Several statements are not supported:
   - "Our experiments confirm the long-standing question of TRSB";
   - the claim that the crystals are "ultra-pure and strain-free", used to rule out accidental-degeneracy scenarios;
   - the stated preference for Eg pairing.
   Strain in the devices is not measured. Flakes on SrTiO3 with Ti/Au contacts and SiOx or Au caps experience differential thermal contraction, and FIB leaves a damaged edge layer. Tc not exceeding 1.5 K excludes the 3K phase but not weak or inhomogeneous strain. Domain-wall mobility, which is used against strain-pinned domains, is inferred from the model and not observed directly. The text also says that a dxz + idyz state should produce a second heat-capacity anomaly that is not seen, which cuts against the stated preference. The authors should temper these conclusions, or add a strain estimate such as Tc homogeneity or a calculation of thermal-mismatch strain.

C. Minor

10. Figure references and captions:
   - The text cites "Figure 1d" for the IV curves and "Figure 1e" for the power dependence; these are panels 1c and 1d.
   - The Fig. 2 caption says "plotted in in the inset".
   - The Fig. S10 caption describes a panel (d) that does not exist.
   - The Fig. S13 caption gives "-20 mT" for both panels, refers to "Figure 4 of the main text" (it should be Fig. 3), and writes "I+ and I+".
   - The Fig. S14 caption mentions a "yellow arrow" (the arrow is orange).
   - The Fig. S9 caption says "absence of no TRSB".

11. Inconsistent numbers:
   - Fig. S10b is labelled Ring 1 at 800 mK, but its Ic range (about 50 to 80 µA) and periods (0.71 vs 0.73 Phi0) resemble the 50 mK data in Fig. 2a.
   - Fig. S10c is labelled Ring 2 at 800 mK, but Ic of about 108 to 135 µA matches the 400 mK curve in Fig. 2c, not the 800 mK one (about 80 to 110 µA).
   - The Fig. S6 caption calls 1200 mK "very close to Tc" for Ring 3, while the table in Fig. S1 gives Tc = 1.5 K.
   - The asterisks in the table in Fig. S1 ("80**", "0.475*", "1.38*") are not explained.
   - The main text cites Supporting Figure S2 for residual resistivity ratios above 100 and for Tc after re-milling, but Fig. S2 shows no RRR. RRR values per device should be added.

12. The normalization of the period to πr_in r_out (Fig. 2a) should be stated explicitly, together with the uncertainty in the effective area and in each lobe width. Without these, the 0.73 Phi0 central lobe cannot be compared with the 0.97 to 1.00 Phi0 side lobes.

13. The Gaussian envelope in Sec. S4 is written exp(-H^2/2^2). The authors should state whether the width is fixed (and in what units) or fitted.

14. The notation is inconsistent: Ic+, Ic-, I_C^+ and -I_C(-H) are all used across the figures. The sign convention should be fixed once.

15. Typos: "resonanse" (Fig. 1 caption), "standard standard" (Sec. VI), "Strikingly, We" (Sec. VI), "proprieties" (Sec. S4).

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a cooldown-statistics figure. Show ΔIc(H) for several zero-field and ±Hz field-cooled cooldowns of one ring, in the same wiring and with the leads swapped. Such a figure would by itself make the TRSB claim much more convincing.
2. Build a null device with the identical FIB process and geometry, from a conventional superconductor or a 3K-phase crystal, and measure it in the same setup to show Ic+(H) = Ic-(-H).
3. Replace or supplement the sigmoid toy model with a SQUID model made of the phi0 or phi junctions described in Sec. S5. Test whether one parameter set accounts for the skewed central lobe, the narrowed central lobe and the zero-field diode together.
4. Map the bistable state and the half-integer steps against the field component perpendicular and parallel to the current, with Hz calibrated. This would separate a domain-wall-orientation mechanism from in-plane vortex and loop-frustration mechanisms.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
