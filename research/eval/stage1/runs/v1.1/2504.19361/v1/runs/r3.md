Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors FIB-structure single-crystal Sr2RuO4 into micron-scale rings. They report that Josephson junctions form spontaneously in these rings, which they attribute to superconducting domain walls pinned by the geometry. Their headline claim is that the critical-current interference pattern violates Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| in three rings, including a zero-field diode effect, and that this gives transport evidence for intrinsic time-reversal symmetry breaking (TRSB) in unstrained Sr2RuO4. They also report SQUID lobes narrower than Φ0 near zero field, bistable critical-current states switched by a training current under in-plane field, and half-integer Shapiro steps. A device-level TRSB test in a bulk-intrinsic Sr2RuO4 junction, with no deliberately made weak link, would matter for the Sr2RuO4 pairing debate. Whether it is convincing depends on excluding extrinsic routes to broken time-reversal symmetry at the device level.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Shapiro steps at the expected height Φ0·f in several geometries, including the singly connected cut ring (Fig. 1c,d; Fig. S3), establish Josephson coupling in nominally weak-link-free crystals.
- The authors take care with Ic extraction: voltage-offset removal, a bootstrap error estimate, and remnant-field re-symmetrization. They also show a conventional MoGe/Ag SQUID null test that passes Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| (Figs. S8, S9, S11).
- The TRSB signature appears in more than one ring (Rings 1, 2, 3) and in Ring 2 at three temperatures (Fig. 2c). Ring 3 shows the onset of TRSB between 1200 and 1100 mK (Fig. S6).
- The phenomenology is rich and new for this material (a central lobe of about 0.73 Φ0, switchable Ic branches, polarity-selective half-integer steps), and it may motivate theory on domain-wall junctions.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Extrinsic, history-dependent origins of Ic+(H) ≠ |Ic-(-H)| are not excluded (Section IV, Fig. 2, Fig. S6, Section S2).
   - **What is missing.** The error bars come only from a parametric bootstrap of voltage noise. S2 states that "this method only accounts for the uncertainty in measured voltage". There is no estimate of run-to-run reproducibility and no comparison of field-sweep directions.
   - **Why it matters.** In a single monotonic field sweep, Ic+(H) and Ic-(-H) are recorded on opposite sides of zero field, at different points in the sample's magnetic history. The large Sr2RuO4 banks and leads can trap Abrikosov vortices as the field passes ±10 mT. A trapped vortex near one arm breaks time reversal at the device level and produces exactly this kind of asymmetry, without any intrinsic TRSB. The remnant-field correction only shifts the field axis. It cannot remove history-dependent flux trapped in the sample.
   - **A second artifact to rule out.** In Ring 2 (Fig. 2c) ΔIc is an almost field-independent offset of about −5 μA, with sawtooth jumps at the lobe minima. A constant ΔIc is what an offset in the dc current source, a leakage path, or a polarity-dependent voltage threshold would produce. The authors correct the voltage offset but say nothing about the current offset.
   - **Measurement history.** The paper does not say whether Ic+ and Ic- come from the same bipolar IV sweep at each field, or in which order the fields were visited.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - (i) Show Ic±(H) for both field-sweep directions, and for zero-field-cooled and field-cooled starts, and show that ΔIc(H) is unchanged.
     - (ii) Report the scatter of ΔIc over repeated sweeps under nominally identical conditions, and use that scatter as the error bar.
     - (iii) Calibrate the current offset independently, for example with the ring in the normal state or with a reference resistor.
     - (iv) Thermally cycle above Tc and report whether the sign and shape of ΔIc(H) reproduce. A genuinely chiral, degenerate domain configuration should pick either sign at random from cooldown to cooldown, or be selectable by cooling in a small ±Hz.

   Tests (iv) and (i) also probe the intrinsic interpretation directly (see concern 2). Without these controls, the central claim of "direct evidence of TRSB" rests on asymmetries of a few μA that could come from the measurement history.

B. Should address

2. The step from device-level TRSB to intrinsic chiral order in Sr2RuO4 is not established (Abstract, Sections IV, VII, VIII).
   - **What the data show.** A violation of Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| shows that the device's ground state breaks time reversal. Besides intrinsic chiral domains, that could also come from trapped flux (concern 1) or from magnetic inclusions near a junction. Ferromagnetic SrRuO3 intergrowths are a known defect in floating-zone Sr2RuO4, and so are Ga-implanted FIB edges.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Report whether the crystals were checked for SrRuO3 inclusions, for example through a magnetization or resistance anomaly near 160 K.
     - Show whether the TRSB onset coincides with the junction Tc in all rings. In Ring 3 it appears only below 1200 mK, while Tc is about 1.35–1.5 K.
     - Show field-cooling training of the sign of ΔIc, which would tie the effect to a chirality that a field can select.
   - **Overreach in the Discussion.** Favouring an Eg (dxz + idyz) state goes beyond what Ic(H) data can discriminate. The data do not distinguish between two-component TRSB candidates. The authors also dismiss accidental-degeneracy scenarios because the crystals are "ultra-pure and strain-free", but Tc ≤ 1.5 K only excludes the 3K phase. It does not exclude small residual strain from the SiOx/Au capping or the SrTiO3 substrate. The authors should quantify the residual strain or soften the claim.

3. The claim that the interference patterns are incompatible with a conventional SQUID is asserted rather than shown quantitatively (Section IV; Section S4; Fig. S10).
   - **Modulation depth.** The patterns are shallow: Ic_min/Ic_max is about 0.65 in Ring 1 (Fig. 2a) and about 0.85 in Ring 2 at 400 mK (Fig. 2c). For a sinusoidal-CPR SQUID that requires either a large screening parameter βL or strong junction asymmetry. This sits uneasily with the claim of "a high degree of symmetry between the weak links". If βL is large, Ic(Φ) can be multivalued, which feeds directly into concerns 1 and 4.
   - **Fit not attempted.** Section S4 says the RCSJ model "cannot be applied" to the rings, but no fit is attempted.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Estimate βL from the geometric inductance and the kinetic inductance (λ, arm cross-section).
     - Fit the RCSJ model with αI and βL free, and with a non-sinusoidal CPR and/or a φ0 offset allowed.
     - Show where the fit fails, beyond the narrow central lobe and the skew.
   - **Alternatives for the narrow central lobe.** A field-dependent effective area could produce the 0.73 Φ0 central lobe: vortex entry into the bulk banks at specific fields changes flux focusing. The authors should address this alternative.
   - **The toy model.** The model in S3 uses a phenomenological sigmoid with three free parameters (φm, a, Φint^max). It shows that a moving domain wall could produce the narrow lobe, but its agreement with the data does not support domain-wall motion over these alternatives. The fits in Fig. S10 also miss the outer lobe heights noticeably.

4. The bistable Ic states have a conventional alternative that is not addressed (Section V, Fig. 3, Figs. S13, S16).
   - **The alternative.** A training current that switches between two Ic branches, with the role of the current polarity reversing when the field is reversed, is also what one expects from switching between fluxoid states of an asymmetric loop with finite inductance, or from a current-driven vortex entering or leaving one arm. An "in-plane" field with a small misalignment adds an out-of-plane component. At 1° misalignment, 20 mT gives about 0.35 mT, which is a sizeable fraction of a period of roughly 4.5 mT in Ring 1.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Report how well the in-plane field is aligned and how they calibrated it.
     - Measure Ic(Hz) in both states at fixed Hx and report whether the two branches are related by a rigid flux shift of the SQUID pattern, which would point to fluxoid or vortex states, or differ in modulation shape and depth, which would point to a change of the junction itself.
     - Explain why no switching occurs below 10 mT (Fig. S16).

5. The half-integer Shapiro steps are not tied uniquely to a change in the domain-wall current-phase relation (Section VI, Fig. 4, Figs. S14, S15).
   - **Alternatives.** Half-integer steps arise generically in two-junction SQUIDs with finite inductance near half-flux bias. They also arise at Ic(B) minima, where the first harmonic is suppressed, as the authors acknowledge for the 1/3 steps in S15, and they can come from RF-induced heating. If the low-Ic state corresponds to a different flux point of the SQUID (concern 4), half-steps there would be expected without any domain-wall physics.
   - **Reproducibility.** The result is not robust: after the field is reset, the half-steps disappear on one polarity and an integer step goes missing (Fig. S14).
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - Show Shapiro maps against microwave power for both Ic states, at more than one frequency.
     - State where each state sits in the out-of-plane SQUID pattern.
     - Compare with RCSJ-SQUID simulations that include inductance.

6. The location and origin of the junctions are inferred, not shown (Section III).
   - **What is shown.** The Shapiro steps prove Josephson coupling somewhere between the voltage probes. They do not prove that the coupling sits at a domain wall rather than at the necks where the ring joins the leads, or at FIB-damaged or Ga-implanted edges. The arms are only about 0.26–0.35 μm wide, and the authors estimate about 10 nm of damage.
   - **The current argument.** The argument against trivial constriction junctions is non-hysteretic IVs plus symmetry. Non-hysteretic IVs are also typical of overdamped extrinsic weak links, and the symmetry argument is weakened by concern 3.
   - **What the authors should do.** A same-geometry control made with the same FIB process from a conventional superconductor (Nb, or Al films of comparable thickness) would show whether FIB processing alone produces Josephson behaviour. The MoGe/Ag SQUID is a bilayer with deliberately cut junctions, so it does not test this. A systematic dependence on arm width would also help. Alternatively, the authors should state clearly that the domain-wall origin rests on their earlier TDGL comparison (Ref. 38).

7. Sample statistics are not reported (Section III; Section S1).
   - **What is missing.** The text says the features are "universally observed in a large selection of samples" and that bistability appears "in all samples". However, TRSB data are shown for three rings, and the main text says "three rings" while S1 says "four different devices". The paper does not say how many devices were measured, how many showed SQUID oscillations, how many showed a statistically significant ΔIc, and how many showed none.
   - **Why it matters.** Without these numbers, selection bias cannot be judged.
   - **What the authors should do.** Add a table of all devices measured, with outcomes for each.

8. The Ic extraction and remnant-field correction need to be specified and shown to be robust (Section S2, Eq. 1).
   - **Eq. 1.** As written, Eq. 1 compares Ic+(H+H0) with Ic-(H+H0) at the same field. Since Ic- < 0, this is not the TRS comparison Ic+(H) vs |Ic-(-H)|. The authors should state exactly which quantity is minimized.
   - **The fitted H0.** H0 is chosen to minimize the asymmetry, so the residual ΔIc is a lower bound under that procedure. The authors should report the fitted H0 for each dataset and compare it with an independent measure of the magnet's remanence, for example from a reference sample or a Hall probe.
   - **Voltage criterion.** The criterion is 0.1 or 0.2 I0RN "depending on the degree of rounding". The authors should state which criterion was used for Figs. 2 and S6, and show that ΔIc(H) and the zero-field diode effect do not change with the criterion. Polarity-dependent rounding would otherwise turn directly into a ΔIc.

9. The effective temperature under microwaves is not established (Fig. 1c,d; Fig. 4; Fig. S15).
   - **What is missing.** The Shapiro data at 50 mK use up to 20 dBm at the source, and Fig. 1c is taken "at maximum applied power". The authors do not report the electron temperature, or how much Ic is suppressed by heating rather than by the RF drive.
   - **Why it matters.** This bears on the reading of the fractional steps and on comparisons across temperatures.
   - **What the authors should do.** Report Ic and RN under RF at zero step amplitude, or compare them with the temperature dependence of Ic.

    - Fig. S10c is labelled Ring 2 at 800 mK, with Ic of about 110–135 μA. This matches the 400 mK data in Fig. 2c; at 800 mK, Fig. 2c gives about 80–108 μA.

    The authors should check that the datasets are correctly labelled.

12. Flux axis and Table S1:
    - **Flux normalization.** The flux axis is "normalized with respect to πr_in r_out", but the period is not given in mT. The authors should give the period in mT and the effective area implied by the outer lobes, compared with the geometric area. Flux focusing by the banks matters here.
    - **Table S1 entries.** The table uses undefined asterisks ("80**", "0.475*", "1.38*"). Ring 2 appears twice with different RN (53 and 112 mΩ); the authors should state which FIB stage the data in Fig. 2c come from.

13. Diode effect description:
    - **"Sign changes only twice".** The statement that "the sign of this asymmetry only changes twice" over the field range in Ring 2 is hard to reconcile with Fig. 2c, where ΔIc stays mostly negative and touches zero at each lobe minimum.
    - **Quantification.** The authors should quantify the diode efficiency (Ic+ − |Ic-|)/(Ic+ + |Ic-|) at zero field for each ring and temperature.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Make a field-cooling training experiment the centrepiece of the TRSB claim: cool in ±Hz, then measure ΔIc(H) after removing the field. If the sign of ΔIc follows the cooling field, and is random or reproducible across zero-field cooldowns in a documented way, this would separate intrinsic chiral domains from trapped-flux artifacts far more convincingly than a single pattern.
- Add a quantitative RCSJ-SQUID comparison: fit with inductance, junction asymmetry, a second harmonic and a φ0 offset, and report which features remain unexplained. This would turn "cannot be captured by the conventional RCSJ model" from an assertion into a result.
- For the bistable states, measure the full Ic(Hz) pattern in each state. That single measurement would show whether the states differ by a fluxoid or vortex shift or by a change in the junction.
- Narrow the Discussion's conclusions about pairing symmetry to what the transport data can support, and present the Eg preference as one consistent possibility rather than as favoured by these experiments.

5. Verdict: major revision
