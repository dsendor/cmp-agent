Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports critical-current interference patterns in FIB-structured single-crystal Sr2RuO4 rings (no deliberately made weak links) that violate Ic+(H) = Ic−(−H). It also reports a zero-field supercurrent diode effect, a narrowed central SQUID lobe, current-switchable bistable Ic states under in-plane fields, and half-integer Shapiro steps. All of these are attributed to Josephson coupling across chiral superconducting domain walls pinned by the ring geometry. A transport-based, device-level signature of TRSB in unstrained Sr2RuO4 would be a significant addition to the μSR/Kerr evidence, and the domain-wall-junction concept is attractive. The central inference, however, is that an observed Ic+(H) ≠ Ic−(−H) is "unequivocal evidence" of intrinsic TRSB of the order parameter. That inference needs controls the paper does not yet give.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Clear Josephson phenomenology in nominally homogeneous crystals. Shapiro steps appear at the expected Φ0f heights at several frequencies (Fig. 1c,d), including in a singly connected geometry (Fig. S3). The IV curves are non-hysteretic, and SQUID oscillations are seen down to 50 mK.
- The Ic+(H) versus −Ic−(−H) comparison is a well-chosen, model-independent test. The authors describe their Ic extraction, error propagation and remnant-field symmetrization (S2), and validate them on a conventional MoGe/Ag SQUID that shows no violation (Fig. S9).
- The violation appears in three rings (Fig. 2b,c; Fig. S6) and at several temperatures in Ring 2. Its magnitude (5–10 μA in Ring 2) is well above the quoted error bars and above the ~1 μA drift seen in the control.
- The bistable Ic states (Fig. 3) are striking and well documented, including field reversal (Fig. S13) and a field-angle map (Fig. S16). Their link to fractional Shapiro steps (Fig. 4) is an interesting observation.
- Repeated FIB modification of one device (Ring 2: disk → ring with island → ring → single arm, Fig. S2) is a useful internal comparison.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Extrinsic, history-dependent sources of Ic+(H) ≠ Ic−(−H) are not excluded, and the TRSB is not shown to be reproducible across cooldowns.

Where: Sec. IV, Fig. 2b,c, Fig. S6, S2. The text calls any deviation from Ic+(H) = Ic−(−H) "unequivocal evidence of TRSB" (p. 5). That holds only if the device contains no time-reversal-odd frozen-in object other than the superconducting order parameter. Two such objects are plausible here and are not ruled out.

(i) Trapped flux. Abrikosov vortices or flux trapped in the wide Sr2RuO4 banks, in the arms, or in the superconducting magnet impose a phase offset that is not uniform across the two arms. A rigid shift H → H + H0, the only correction applied (Eq. 1), cannot remove such an offset. It yields exactly a φ0-like shift, a zero-field diode effect and unequal lobe skewing. These are the signatures reported.

(ii) Current-driven state switching. The authors' own Fig. 3 shows that bias currents above a threshold switch the device between metastable states, and that switching depends on current polarity. If, during an Ic(H) sweep, the large current excursions at one polarity leave the device in a different metastable state (domain-wall position, trapped vortex or fluxoid number) than the other polarity, then Ic+ and Ic− are measured in different states. The resulting asymmetry is set by the measurement protocol, not by an equilibrium TRSB order parameter.

The nearly field-independent offset ΔIc ≈ −5 to −10 μA in Ring 2 (Fig. 2c) is what one would expect from such a polarity-dependent state difference.

Why it matters: this is the headline claim of the paper.

What to do:
- (a) State the measurement protocol explicitly: sweep order of current polarities, whether both branches come from the same IV trace, and field sweep direction. Repeat the Ic(H) measurement with the current-polarity order reversed, and with only one polarity swept per field point.
- (b) Report how many cooldowns through Tc were performed per ring and show ΔIc(H) for at least two independent cooldowns. If chiral domains are chosen spontaneously, the sign of the diode and of ΔIc might be expected to randomize between cooldowns. Report whether it does.
- (c) Field-cool through Tc in small ±Hz. Show whether the sign of ΔIc follows the cooling field, and how this compares with what deliberately trapping a vortex in the banks produces.
- (d) Give the H0 values from Eq. 1 for each dataset and compare them with an independent remnant-field calibration in the same cooldown.

Without (b) and at least one of (a) or (c), the data show a nonreciprocal device state, not intrinsic TRSB.

2. The inductive state of the "SQUIDs" is not characterized, so standard multistable-SQUID physics is not excluded as the origin of the narrow central lobe, the bistable Ic states and the half-integer Shapiro steps.

Where: Sec. III–VI, Fig. 2a, Fig. 3, Fig. 4, S4.

- The Ic modulation depth in Fig. 2a is only ~35% (Ic,min ≈ 50 μA, Ic,max ≈ 80 μA), even though the authors stress that the two junctions are highly symmetric. For a symmetric SQUID, a shallow modulation implies a sizable screening parameter βL = 2LI0/Φ0.
- With βL ≳ 1, the loop has several fluxoid states. A bias current above a threshold can switch between them, giving two stable Ic branches; which polarity switches which way reverses with the field direction (Fig. 3, S13). Fractional Shapiro steps appear near half-flux bias.
- The text treats the fractional steps at the Ic(B) minima as "trivial" (S6) but does not apply the same reasoning to the low-Ic branch in Fig. 4.
- S4 says the RCSJ model "cannot be applied" to the Sr2RuO4 rings, but gives no fitted or estimated βL and αI for them.

What to do:
- Estimate the kinetic plus geometric inductance of each ring from λ_ab, λ_c and the arm dimensions in Table S1.
- Fit the outer lobes with the S4 model to extract βL.
- Show that fluxoid multistability cannot produce the two Ic branches of Fig. 3, the lobe narrowing of Fig. 2a, or the half-steps of Fig. 4.
- Address the 20 mT in-plane field directly. A misalignment of ~1° gives ~0.35 mT out of plane, a sizable fraction of the ~5 mT period. The field also threads the arm cross-section (t = 0.25–0.7 μm).

If βL is small, this alternative is ruled out, and the paper should say so with numbers.

B. Should address

3. There is no same-process null device.

Where: Sec. III, S2. The only TRS control is a MoGe/Ag SQUID with line-cut junctions. It differs in material, junction type, fabrication (no Au/SiOx capping on a crystal, no SrTiO3 substrate) and current scale. A device made by the identical exfoliation, Ti/Au contact, capping and Ga-FIB process from a conventional single crystal would test whether the procedure itself introduces TRS-odd artifacts. A Sr2RuO4 ring measured above the temperature where TRSB disappears would also serve, measured in the same cooldown and with the same analysis. Ring 3 at 1200 mK (Fig. S6a) is a partial example, but its Ic is only ~15 μA and the scatter is large.

What to do: provide such a control, or a systematic ΔIc(T) through Tc on one ring.

4. The temperature dependence of the TRSB signal is not explained and appears to conflict with bulk TRSB onset at Tc.

Where: Fig. 2c, Fig. S6. In Ring 3 the violation is absent at 1200 mK and present at 1100 mK, while Tc = 1.5 K (Table S1). In Ring 2 the offset |ΔIc| stays roughly 5–10 μA while Ic doubles between 1000 and 400 mK. μSR and Kerr place TRSB onset at Tc in unstrained crystals.

What to do: show the diode efficiency (Ic+ − |Ic−|)/(Ic+ + |Ic−|) at zero field versus T up to Tc for at least one ring. Discuss whether the onset reflects the bulk TRSB transition, a domain-wall-pinning crossover, or resolution. As it stands, a signal that switches on well below Tc fits the extrinsic scenarios of item 1 at least as well.

5. The "unstrained" and "intrinsic phase" claims rest only on Tc ≤ 1.5 K.

Where: Sec. III and VII. The Discussion rules out accidental-degeneracy, strain-induced TRSB scenarios [52–56] because the crystals are "ultra-pure and strain-free". Tc rises only weakly with small or inhomogeneous strain. The devices are capped, clamped to SrTiO3 and FIB-milled with Ga implantation in edge layers ~10 nm thick. The 3K-phase argument excludes dislocation-rich crystals, not percent-level local strain.

What to do: estimate the thermal-mismatch strain from the substrate and capping layer, and give the width of the resistive transition as a bound on Tc inhomogeneity. Soften "confirm the long-standing question of TRSB in the unstrained intrinsic phase" and "contradictory to our ultra-pure and strain-free crystals" accordingly.

6. The location and existence of the domain walls are inferred, not observed.

Where: Fig. 1a, Sec. II–III, S1. The Josephson coupling is assigned to pinned domain walls in the arms. The evidence is Shapiro steps, junction symmetry and the earlier TDGL work [38]. The single-arm Ring 2 (Fig. S3, S4) still shows Shapiro steps and a Gaussian Ic(B), which is equally consistent with a FIB-related weak link at a neck. The text also notes that a Gaussian pattern usually signals diffusive junctions, while the mean free path exceeds the ring size.

What to do: discuss how a domain-wall junction differs from a constriction or edge weak link in a testable way. For example: a dependence of Ic and of the TRSB on the arm width or neck geometry across the rings in Table S1, or the absence of SQUID oscillations when the ring is made too small to host two walls. If spatial probes (scanning SQUID or Kerr) are out of reach, say so and frame the domain-wall picture as the leading interpretation.

7. The narrowed-central-lobe model has many free parameters and only qualitative agreement.

Where: S3, Fig. S10. Eq. 4 is a phenomenological sigmoid with free φm, a and Φint^max, added to the RCSJ parameters. The fits in Fig. S10b,c miss the lobe heights. A narrower central lobe can also come from field focusing by the banks combined with field-dependent flux entry.

What to do:
- Report all fitted parameters with uncertainties and the parameter count against the number of data points.
- Test whether Φint^max (0.24 and 0.16 Φ0) is consistent with the expected spontaneous edge-current flux of a chiral domain, which is known to be small in Sr2RuO4.
- Show that a model with flux entry but no domain-wall motion fits worse.

8. The period calibration is unclear.

Where: Fig. 2a caption ("normalized with respect to π r_in r_out"). With the Ring 1 dimensions (D_in = 0.486 μm, D_out = 1.01 μm), π r_in r_out ≈ 0.385 μm² gives 5.4 mT per Φ0. The outer lobes in Fig. 2a have a period of about 4.6–4.7 mT, which would be ≈ 0.87 Φ0, yet they are labeled 1.00 Φ0. The paper claims a period "deviating from Φ0", so how Φ0 is fixed matters.

What to do: state whether the labels are normalized to the outer lobes or to the geometric area, and give the absolute periods in mT for each ring.

9. The half-integer Shapiro steps are dismissed with an incorrect argument.

Where: Sec. VI (p. 7). The argument that Abrikosov vortex flow cannot give half-integer steps because "the Josephson penetration depth is far larger than the junction width" does not address the relevant alternatives: SQUID loop inductance near half-flux (see item 2), a second harmonic from a near-0–π configuration, rf-induced flux modulation of the loop, and the history dependence documented in Fig. S14, where steps disappear after a field reset.

What to do:
- Show the half-steps versus rf power (a Bessel-type step-width map like Fig. 1d) for both Ic branches.
- Show their dependence on out-of-plane flux at fixed in-plane field.
- Fit an RSJ model with a second harmonic to extract the harmonic content.

10. The bistable-Ic symmetry is not specific to domains.

Where: Sec. V, Fig. S13. Swapping the roles of the current polarities when the in-plane field is reversed is what any mechanism obeying combined current-and-field reversal must show. A Lorentz-force (J × B) vortex-entry mechanism behaves the same way. The authors also note that the required field angle varies between samples with no relation to geometry (Sec. VI, S16: 20° for Ring 1, 55° for Ring 2).

What to do: present this symmetry as a consistency check, not as support for domains. Add a test that separates the two pictures, such as whether the switching threshold scales with Ic or with field magnitude in the way vortex entry predicts.

11. The diode-effect statements do not match the figures and are not quantified.

Where: Sec. IV, p. 5. The text says "the sign of this asymmetry only changes twice". In Fig. 2c, ΔIc stays mostly negative and approaches zero only near some cusps. In Ring 1 (Fig. 2b), ΔIc changes sign several times: positive near −7 mT and near 0, negative elsewhere.

What to do: give the zero-field diode efficiency with an uncertainty for each ring and temperature. Correct the description so it is device-specific.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a reproducibility matrix for the TRSB signature: rings × cooldowns × temperature × current-sweep protocol, giving the sign and magnitude of the zero-field diode efficiency and of ΔIc in each case. This addresses items 1, 3, 4 and 11 together.
2. Report βL and αI for each Sr2RuO4 ring from fits of the outer lobes, and use them to bound the conventional multistable-SQUID explanations of the bistability, the narrow lobe and the half-steps (items 2, 7, 9).
3. Include a null device made with the identical fabrication process (a conventional single crystal, or Sr2RuO4 above the TRSB onset), analyzed with the same S2 pipeline.
4. Reframe the domain-wall picture as the leading interpretation, and state explicitly which observations would falsify it, for example how TRSB depends on the presence of a second arm. The Ring 2 milling sequence already allows a comparison of ΔIc before and after cutting one arm.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
