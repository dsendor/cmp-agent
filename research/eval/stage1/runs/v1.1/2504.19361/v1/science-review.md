A. Must address

1. Extrinsic, history-dependent sources of Ic+(H) ≠ |Ic-(-H)| are not excluded, and the TRSB signal is not shown to reproduce across cooldowns (Sec. IV, Fig. 2b,c, Fig. S6, Sec. S2). A violation of Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| shows that the device's state is not time-reversal invariant; it does not by itself show that the superconducting order parameter breaks TRS, yet the text calls any deviation "unequivocal evidence of TRSB" (p. 5). At least two other time-reversal-odd, frozen-in objects are plausible here. (i) Trapped flux: Abrikosov vortices pinned in the 250–700 nm thick arms or the wide banks and leads, flux trapped as the field passes ±10 mT, or remanence in the magnet. In a single monotonic field sweep, Ic+(H) and Ic-(-H) are recorded on opposite sides of zero field, at different points of the magnetic history. Such flux imposes a phase offset that is not uniform across the two arms, and the only correction applied (a rigid shift H → H + H0, Eq. 1) cannot remove it. It produces exactly a φ0-like shift, unequal lobe skewing and a zero-field diode effect in a conventional two-junction loop. (ii) Current-driven state switching: Fig. 3 shows that bias currents above a threshold switch the device between metastable states in a polarity-dependent way. If the large current excursions at one polarity during an Ic(H) sweep leave the device in a different domain-wall, vortex or fluxoid state than the other polarity, Ic+ and Ic- are measured in different states, and the asymmetry is set by the measurement protocol, not by an equilibrium TRSB order parameter. The nearly field-independent ΔIc ≈ −5 to −10 µA in Ring 2 (Fig. 2c) is what either scenario would give. The paper does not state the cooling protocol for the Fig. 2 data (zero-field cooled, field cooled, compensated field), whether Ic+ and Ic- come from the same bipolar IV trace, the order of current polarities, the field-sweep direction or the order in which fields were visited, or how many cooldowns through Tc were performed per ring. In the authors' own picture the domain-wall positions are fixed by geometry, but which side is Psi+ and which Psi- should be chosen at random on each cooldown, or by the sign of a cooling field; this is the decisive test separating intrinsic chiral domains from trapped flux. The authors should: (a) state the measurement protocol explicitly, and repeat Ic(H) with the current-polarity order reversed and with only one polarity swept per field point; (b) show Ic±(H) for both field-sweep directions; (c) repeat zero-field cooldowns (thermal cycling above Tc) and report the sign and shape of ΔIc(H) for each, for at least two independent cooldowns per ring; (d) field-cool through Tc in small +Hz and −Hz (and in a carefully nulled zero field), measure after removing the field, and show whether ΔIc(H) reverses sign or maps onto itself under H → −H as chiral-domain training predicts, compared with what deliberately trapping a vortex in the banks produces; (e) estimate the vortex-entry field of the arms and banks and show that the ±10 mT sweep range stays below it, or that the asymmetry does not depend on the maximum field reached before the sweep. Without cooldown statistics and at least one of the protocol or field-cooling tests, the data show a nonreciprocal device state, not intrinsic TRSB, and the title claim of "direct evidence of TRSB in unstrained Sr2RuO4" is not separated from extrinsic artifacts.

2. The Ring 2 asymmetry looks like a polarity offset, and it has not been shown to belong to the device rather than the measurement (Fig. 2c, Sec. S2). In Fig. 2c, ΔIc stays negative at about −5 to −10 µA over almost the whole field range at 400, 800 and 1000 mK, with sawtooth jumps at the lobe minima; the text confirms that "diodicity persists over the entire range of measured fields". A nearly field-independent ΔIc is what one expects from a dc current offset of a few µA in the bias source, a leakage path, a polarity-dependent thermoelectric or offset voltage combined with the 0.1–0.2 I0RN threshold, or asymmetric rounding of the positive and negative branches. It is not obviously what a φ0 or φ junction in a nearly symmetric SQUID would give, since that mainly shifts or skews the pattern. The authors correct the voltage offset but say nothing about the current offset. S2 says the threshold is 0.1 or 0.2 I0RN "depending on the degree of rounding" without stating which was used for each dataset in Figs. 2 and S6; an asymmetric threshold choice or polarity-dependent rounding would add directly to ΔIc. The error bars come only from a parametric bootstrap of voltage noise ("this method only accounts for the uncertainty in measured voltage") and so understate the real uncertainty, especially since earlier Sr2RuO4 hybrid devices showed telegraph switching. The control SQUID (Fig. S9) was measured on a different device, it is not stated that the same wiring and cryostat were used, and it itself shows a small systematic negative ΔIc (about −0.3 to −0.8 µA) growing at the edges and attributed to temperature drift, so drift and offsets in this setup are not negligible. The authors should: swap the current (and voltage) leads or reverse the wiring on Ring 2 and show that ΔIc keeps its sign relative to the device; calibrate the current-source zero offset independently (for example with the ring in the normal state or with a reference resistor); show ΔIc(H) and the zero-field diode effect for several threshold criteria (e.g. 0.05, 0.1, 0.2 I0RN) and for a switching-current definition; report the scatter of Ic± and ΔIc over repeated IV sweeps at fixed field under nominally identical conditions and use it as the error bar; and measure the control SQUID in the same setup as Ring 2. The zero-field diode effect, cited as a key signature, depends on this point.

3. The inductive state of the rings is not characterized, so conventional multistable-SQUID physics is not excluded as the origin of the narrow central lobe, the bistable Ic states and the half-integer Shapiro steps, and the claimed incompatibility with the RCSJ model is asserted rather than shown (Secs. III–VI, Fig. 2a, Figs. 3 and 4, Secs. S3–S4, Fig. S10). The modulation is shallow: Ic,min/Ic,max is about 0.65 in Ring 1 (Ic ≈ 50 to 80 µA, Fig. 2a) and about 0.85 in Ring 2 at 400 mK (Fig. 2c). For a sinusoidal-CPR SQUID this requires either a sizable screening parameter βL = 2LI0/Φ0 or strong junction asymmetry, and the authors stress "a high degree of symmetry between the weak links". With βL ≳ 1 the loop has several fluxoid states; a bias current above a threshold can switch between them, giving two stable Ic branches whose polarity roles reverse with field direction (Fig. 3, Fig. S13), Ic(Φ) can become multivalued (which feeds back into concern 1), and fractional Shapiro steps appear near half-flux bias. The text treats fractional steps at Ic(B) minima as "trivial" (S6) but does not apply the same reasoning to the low-Ic branch in Fig. 4. Sec. S4 says the RCSJ model "cannot be applied" to the Sr2RuO4 rings, but gives no fitted or estimated βL or αI, and the only RCSJ comparison is restricted to a sinusoidal current-phase relation with symmetric resistances. A SQUID made of φ0 or φ junctions, or of junctions with a second harmonic (the very physics proposed in Sec. S5), is not tried, and could well capture the data. The kinetic inductance of the thin arms may be non-negligible, and the 20 mT in-plane field both threads the arm cross-section (t = 0.25–0.7 µm) and, at ~1° misalignment, adds ~0.35 mT out of plane, a sizable fraction of the ~4.5–5 mT period. The authors should estimate the kinetic plus geometric inductance of each ring from λab, λc and the arm dimensions in Table S1; fit the outer lobes with the S4 model with αI and βL free, and also with a non-sinusoidal CPR, second harmonic and/or φ0 offset allowed; report βL and αI per ring; and show, with numbers, where such fits fail beyond the narrow central lobe and the skew, and that fluxoid multistability cannot produce the two Ic branches of Fig. 3, the lobe narrowing of Fig. 2a or the half-steps of Fig. 4. If βL is small, this alternative is ruled out and the paper should say so quantitatively.

B. Should address

4. There is no same-process null device. The only TRS control is a MoGe/Ag SQUID with line-cut junctions, which differs in material, junction type, fabrication (no Au/SiOx capping on a crystal, no SrTiO3 substrate), loop size and current scale. It therefore tests neither whether the FIB process alone produces Josephson behaviour nor whether it introduces TRS-odd artifacts. A device made with the identical exfoliation, Ti/Au contact, capping, Ga-FIB process and geometry from a conventional superconductor (a Nb or Al single crystal or film of comparable thickness), or from a 3K-phase or deliberately strained Sr2RuO4 ring that does show oscillations, measured in the same setup and analyzed with the same S2 pipeline, would directly test whether FIB weak links alone give Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)|. A Sr2RuO4 ring measured above the TRSB onset in the same cooldown would also serve; Ring 3 at 1200 mK (Fig. S6a) is a partial example, but its Ic is only ~15 µA and the scatter is large. The authors should provide such a control, or at least a systematic ΔIc(T) through Tc on one ring.

5. The temperature dependence of the TRSB signal is under-reported and appears to conflict with bulk TRSB onset at Tc (Fig. 2c, Fig. S6). In Ring 3 the violation is absent at 1200 mK and present at 1100 mK, while Table S1 gives Tc = 1.5 K (and the Fig. S6 caption calls 1200 mK "very close to Tc"). In Ring 2, |ΔIc| stays roughly 5–10 µA while Ic doubles between 1000 and 400 mK, and only Ring 2 has a temperature series. μSR and Kerr place the TRSB onset at Tc in unstrained crystals, so a signal that switches on well below Tc fits the extrinsic scenarios of concern 1 at least as well. 6. The bistable Ic states have conventional alternatives that are not addressed, and their symmetry is not specific to domains (Sec. V, Fig. 3, Figs. S13, S16). The arms are 0.25–0.7 µm thick, comparable to or larger than 2λ, so an in-plane field of 10–20 mT along x can put vortices parallel to the film into the arms and banks. The bias current exerts a Lorentz force J × B on them that reverses with either current or field. That matches what is observed: a positive training current selects one state and a negative one the other (Fig. 3c,f); the roles swap when the field is reversed (Fig. S13); nothing happens below about 10 mT (Fig. S16), consistent with a vortex-entry threshold; and the required field angle and magnitude vary from sample to sample with no relation to geometry (20° for Ring 1, 55° for Ring 2). Switching between fluxoid states of a loop with finite inductance (concern 3), aided by the out-of-plane component from a small misalignment, gives the same phenomenology. Swapping the polarity roles under field reversal is what any mechanism obeying combined current-and-field reversal must show, so it is a consistency check, not support for domains. The authors argue against in-plane vortex flow only for the half-integer steps, not for the bistability, yet the Conclusion calls the bistable states "compelling evidence for the presence of superconducting domains". The authors should estimate Hc1 parallel to ab for the actual cross-sections; report the in-plane field alignment, its calibration and the residual Hz; show the dependence of the bistability on the field components perpendicular and parallel to the current; measure the full Ic(Hz) pattern in both states at fixed Hx and report whether the two branches are related by a rigid flux shift (pointing to fluxoid or vortex states) or differ in modulation shape and depth (pointing to a change of the junction); test whether the switching threshold scales with Ic or with field magnitude as vortex entry predicts; and explain the absence of switching below 10 mT. Otherwise the claim should be softened.

7. The central-lobe narrowing is described by an underconstrained phenomenological model, and alternative explanations of a field-dependent effective area are not discussed (Sec. S3, Eqs. 4–5, Fig. S10). The toy model uses a sigmoid that, as the authors say, "does not directly derive from theory", adding free φm, a and Φint^max to the RCSJ parameters (αI, βL and a Gaussian envelope). The fits in Fig. S10b,c noticeably miss the lobe heights, including the outer lobes, and no goodness of fit, uncertainties or parameter correlations are given. S5 accounts for the skewed central lobe, the narrowed central lobe and the zero-field diode together; and state what the model predicts that can be tested independently (for example the temperature dependence of the narrowing). 8. The half-integer Shapiro steps have trivial explanations that are not excluded, are dismissed with an argument that does not address them, and do not reproduce reliably (Sec. VI, Fig. 4, Figs. S14, S15). Half-integer and fractional steps arise generically in a two-junction loop with finite inductance near half-flux bias, near Ic(B) minima where the first harmonic is suppressed (as the authors acknowledge for the 1/3 steps in Fig. S15), from a second harmonic near a 0–π configuration, from rf-induced flux modulation of the loop, and from rf heating. If the low-Ic state sits at a different flux point of the SQUID (concerns 3 and 6), half-steps there are expected without domain-wall physics. A 1–2° misalignment at 10–20 mT in-plane field gives Hz ≈ 0.2–0.35 mT, an appreciable fraction of the ~4.4 mT period. The argument that Abrikosov vortex flow cannot produce half-steps because "the Josephson penetration depth is far larger than the junction width" does not address these alternatives. After the field was reset, the half-integer steps disappeared from one polarity and an integer step went missing (Fig. S14). The authors should report the out-of-plane field component and how it was nulled for Fig. 4 and Fig. S14; show the half-steps against microwave power (a Bessel-type map like Fig. 1d) for both Ic branches and at more than one frequency; state where each state sits in the out-of-plane SQUID pattern and show the dependence on out-of-plane flux at fixed in-plane field, including that the half-steps survive at integer-flux bias; fit an RSJ model with a second harmonic and compare with RCSJ-SQUID simulations including inductance. Until then, "a different current-phase relation between the two Ic states" should be presented as tentative.

9. The location and existence of the domain-wall junctions are inferred, not shown (Fig. 1a, Secs. II–III, Sec. S1, Figs. S3, S4). Shapiro steps prove Josephson coupling somewhere between the voltage probes, but phase-slip centres, the necks where the ring joins the leads, and FIB-damaged or Ga-implanted edges (about 10 nm, on arms only 0.26–0.35 µm wide) also give Shapiro steps. The Fig. S3 caption's statement that steps in the singly connected arm "confirm the presence of a chiral domain wall" is not justified by that measurement alone; the single-arm Ring 2 still shows Shapiro steps and a Gaussian Ic(B), equally consistent with a weak link at a neck, and the text itself notes that a Gaussian pattern usually signals diffusive junctions while the mean free path exceeds the ring size. The remaining arguments (non-hysteretic IVs, which are also typical of overdamped extrinsic weak links; arm symmetry, which is weakened by concern 3; and the earlier observation that 3K-phase rings do not oscillate and the TDGL work of Ref. 38) are indirect. The authors should state how a domain-wall junction differs testably from a constriction or edge weak link, for example through the dependence of Ic and of the TRSB on arm width or neck geometry across the rings in Table S1, the absence of oscillations when a ring is too small to host two walls, or a comparison of ΔIc before and after cutting one arm in the Ring 2 milling sequence. If spatial probes (scanning SQUID, Kerr) are out of reach, they should say so and frame the domain-wall picture as the leading interpretation resting on Ref. 38, stating which observations would falsify it.

10. The remnant-field correction needs to be specified and shown not to bias ΔIc (Sec. S2, Eq. 1). As written, Eq. 1 minimizes the integral of [Ic+(H+H0) − Ic-(H+H0)]² at the same field; since Ic- < 0, this is not the TRS comparison Ic+(H) vs |Ic-(-H)|, and the authors should state exactly which quantity is minimized. Because H0 is chosen to minimize the asymmetry, in a device that genuinely breaks TRS it absorbs part of the signal and depends on the cutoff HR; the residual ΔIc is then a lower bound under that procedure. The authors should report the fitted H0 for each dataset, compare it with an independent remnant-field calibration in the same cooldown and magnet sequence (a Hall probe, or the control SQUID), and show that the size of ΔIc is robust to HR.

12. The Discussion goes beyond the data on strain and pairing symmetry (Secs. III, VII, VIII). The "unstrained" and "intrinsic phase" claims rest only on Tc ≤ 1.5 K, which excludes the 3K phase and dislocation-rich crystals but not weak, percent-level local or inhomogeneous strain, to which Tc responds only weakly. 13. Magnetic inclusions are not excluded as a source of device-level TRSB (Sec. IV). Besides intrinsic chiral domains and trapped flux, a broken time-reversal ground state of the device could come from magnetic inclusions near a junction: ferromagnetic SrRuO3 intergrowths are a known defect in floating-zone Sr2RuO4, and Ga-implanted FIB edges are another candidate. The authors should report whether the crystals were checked for SrRuO3 inclusions, for example through a magnetization or resistance anomaly near 160 K.
