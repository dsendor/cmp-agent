A. Must address

1. **The claim that the nucleus is driven "irrespective of" or "independent of" hybridization is not established, and the leading alternative, a hyperfine-enhanced drive acting through the electron spin, is not considered.** The claim appears in the Abstract ("the nuclear spin can be driven efficiently irrespective of its hybridization with the electron spin"), the Introduction ("we unambiguously demonstrate NMR driving independent of electron-nuclear spin hybridization") and the Conclusion ("hybridization of the nuclear spin with the electron spin is not necessary to drive NMR"). The evidence offered is that transitions I–IV remain visible from 200 to 1400 mT (Fig. 3a), while the flip-flop admixture |⟨↓,−3/2|ψ⟩|² computed in SFig. 1b falls by about a factor of 10. A factor of 10 in probability is only a factor of about 3 in amplitude, and the admixture never reaches zero. ENDOR separates readout from drive, but it says nothing about how the Δm_I = ±1 transition is driven. In standard high-field ENDOR, an oscillating field acting on the electron tilts the electron quantization axis at f_NMR, and through the transverse hyperfine coupling this produces an effective transverse field at the nucleus (the hyperfine enhancement of the effective nuclear g-factor). The relevant admixture is this adiabatic tilt, of order A_⊥/(2hf_e), not the flip-flop coefficient plotted in SFig. 1b, so SFig. 1b is the wrong measure of "hybridization" for this argument. The numbers favour the electron-mediated path: with γ_e/γ_N ≈ (0.56 μ_B)/(0.315 μ_N) ≈ 3×10³ and an admixture amplitude of about 132 MHz / 7.6 GHz ≈ 0.02 at 450 mT (equivalently an enhancement of order m_s·A/ν_N with A/2 ≈ 66 MHz and ν_N ≈ 2.4 MHz/T × B), the electron-mediated term exceeds direct nuclear Zeeman driving by about 50–60 at 450 mT and still by 10–20 at 1.4 T. The tilted tip field (φ ≈ 5°, Suppl. Note 1) and anisotropic A add pseudo-secular S_z′I_x terms that make this worse. The ESR-STM driving fields proposed in the paper (piezoelectric displacement in the tip field, barrier modulation, spin torque; refs. 28, 42–45) act on the electron, and Suppl. Note 1 itself states that B_tip "is caused by exchange interaction and hence cannot couple directly with the nuclear spin", so they can reach the nucleus only through the hyperfine interaction. The data cannot separate the scenarios: no quantity tracks drive strength against field. All ENDOR data use V_NMR = 20 mV, with no saturation curve, no power broadening of the 3–5 MHz NMR linewidths, and no V_NMR at which the ESR peaks equalize (Fig. 1e, "saturate the two nuclear spin populations"). The lines appear saturated, and the Fig. 3a amplitudes are confounded by the V_ESR changes the authors acknowledge (20 → 8–12 mV; Suppl. Note 2, SFig. 6b), so a drive scaling roughly as 1/B (hyperfine-mediated) cannot be told apart from a field-independent (direct) one. The authors should (a) measure ENDOR amplitude and linewidth against V_NMR at three or more fields (e.g. 200, 450 and 1070 mT) with matched, calibrated V_ESR and ideally two setpoint currents, and extract an effective nuclear Rabi rate or saturation parameter, or a lower bound on it; (b) compute, as a function of B_z in the full eigenbasis of Eq. (1) including the fitted tip-field tilt, the Δm_s = 0, Δm_I = ±1 matrix elements of the candidate drive operators (transverse B_1 on S and on I; modulation of B_tip magnitude and direction; modulation of A and Q), and compare their predicted field dependence with the measured one; (c) estimate the B_1 that direct nuclear driving would need to saturate the transition against the spin-pumping rate at I_set = 4 pA, and say whether that is plausible in the junction. If the data cannot separate the scenarios, or follow the hybridization-mediated prediction, the claim should be restated as what is shown: Δm_s = 0 NMR transitions driven and detected without relying on flip-flop hybridization for readout, followed to fields where that hybridization is reduced by an order of magnitude. The statements about the drive being independent of electron-nuclear coupling should be removed.

B. Should address

2. **The arguments that exclude hyperfine and quadrupole modulation are incomplete, and the proposed "effective oscillating magnetic field" mechanism is not tied to any estimate** (Discussion; Suppl. Note 3; SFig. 8). Hyperfine modulation is ruled out because it "would drive flip-flop transitions … in the GHz range", which holds only for the S_xI_x + S_yI_y part (isotropic S·I in the high-field basis). Modulating the anisotropic hyperfine, the pseudo-secular S_zI_x terms present because of the ~5° tip-field tilt, or the electron quantization axis (for example through piezo-induced motion in the tip-field gradient, the ESR-STM mechanism itself) drives Δm_I = ±1 at exactly the observed MHz frequencies with m_s conserved. The "oscillating magnetic field" and "hyperfine modulation" pictures are therefore not cleanly separable, and since the tip field is an exchange field that cannot act on the nucleus directly (Suppl. Note 1), the proposed mechanism is the hyperfine-mediated path the paper sets out to exclude. The case against quadrupole modulation is also weak. The absence of Δm_I = ±2 lines in a single trace (SFig. 8) is not meaningful without an expected amplitude: its noise (~2–3 fA) is comparable to the strongest single-quantum dips, and the junction V_NMR at 100–170 MHz is not given. Moreover, a vertical or lateral tip displacement mainly modulates V_zz (no transition) and V_xz, V_yz (Δm_I = ±1 only); Δm_I = ±2 requires modulating V_xx − V_yy. The weak response at the −1/2 ↔ +1/2 positions V/VI (Fig. 2b), offered as evidence against quadrupole driving, is in fact what V_xz-type driving predicts, since its matrix element is proportional to (2m+1) and vanishes for that transition. A discriminating test exists: the squared-matrix-element ratio of transition I (−5/2 ↔ −3/2) to III (−3/2 ↔ −1/2) is about 0.6 for magnetic-dipole driving and about 2.5 for V_xz quadrupole driving, measurable from the V_NMR-dependent saturation of I and III. The authors should add this comparison; give the predicted Δm = 2 versus Δm = 1 amplitude under quadrupole modulation; quantify V/VI with fitted amplitudes and signal-to-noise; estimate the B_1 needed to explain the observed saturation and compare it with plausible sources; and add a tip-height (setpoint) dependence of drive efficiency, since piezo, barrier-modulation and tip-field-gradient mechanisms scale differently with it. Short of this, the mechanism should be stated as a hypothesis or an open question in the Discussion and Conclusion.

4. **The central contrast between low-field single-tone features and high-field ENDOR is not a matched comparison.** The Introduction and the field study contrast the disappearance of single-tone NMR-like features above about 100–200 mT (Fig. 1d) with the persistence of ENDOR features up to 1400 mT (Fig. 3, "observed for all fields"). But Fig. 1d was taken "with a different microtip" (Methods), at different setpoints (I_set = 2.5–3 pA, V_bias = 70, 75 and 90 mV, versus 4 pA and 80 mV) and with a different readout scheme, while the ENDOR data start only at 200 mT. Since tip field and spin-pumping rate both affect these signals, the contrast mixes tip, setpoint and readout. The authors should take single-tone NMR at 200 and 450 mT and ENDOR at 60–100 mT, both with the ENDOR tip, atom and setpoint. This also bears on concern 1: if the ENDOR drive is hybridization-assisted, ENDOR amplitude at fixed V_NMR should grow strongly towards 60 mT.

6. **The outlook on seconds-scale coherence is not supported by the measurement conditions.** The Conclusion suggests that, with a relaxation time "in order of seconds" (ref. 34), coherence times may be "in the same order of magnitude". Under the measurement conditions the electron spin is continuously flipped by ESR driving and spin-polarized tunnelling, and each flip shifts the nuclear transition frequency by about 35 MHz (I at ~48 MHz, II at ~85 MHz), so nuclear coherence is limited by the electron flip rate rather than nuclear T1. The observed linewidths of 3–5 MHz (Figs. 3a, 4) imply T₂* ≲ 100 ns and are consistent with this, but the paper does not say whether the width is power broadening, lifetime broadening from electron flips, or something else. The authors should analyse the linewidth against V_ESR (Fig. 4 already has three values), V_NMR and setpoint current, state the dephasing time it implies, and either temper the statement or describe the conditions (current off or tip retracted, electron in its ground state) under which long coherence could be expected.

7. **The Hamiltonian fit and the nuclear g-factor extraction are fragile** (Suppl. Note 1; SFig. 5; Fig. 3b). Only z-components of the hyperfine tensor are fitted and the A_x, A_y values used are not stated. Second-order hyperfine terms of order A_⊥²/(4ν_e) shift the NMR lines by an amount falling as 1/B; between 0.2 T (ν_e ≈ 2 GHz) and 1.4 T (ν_e ≈ 11.5 GHz) this can be of order MHz, comparable to the ~3 MHz nuclear Zeeman change. A linear fit of line I against B (SFig. 5) therefore mixes the nuclear Zeeman term with pseudo-nuclear-Zeeman and second-order shifts (and, per concern 1, hyperfine enhancement), which may explain g_N = 0.37 ± 0.04 versus the bulk 0.315. The slope is also set mainly by the two highest-field points (1070 and 1400 mT), the noisiest spectra in Fig. 3a, while the points below 450 mT are flat within error. A and Q are then fitted with g_N fixed to 0.315 "given the discrepancy". In Fig. 3b, IV at 1.07 T (~72 MHz versus ~78 MHz expected) and II at 1.4 T (~90 MHz versus ~86 MHz) lie well off the calculated lines, and most points lack error bars although SFig. 5 has them. The authors should state all fixed parameters, fit the line I and II positions (ideally all lines globally) with the full Hamiltonian with A_⊥ and g_N treated explicitly, report correlated uncertainties, add error bars to Fig. 3b, discuss the outliers in terms of signal-to-noise, and either discuss the g_N discrepancy in terms of second-order effects or present g_N = 0.37 ± 0.04 as an effective quantity consistent with the bulk value within ~1.4σ rather than as a deviation.
