Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors combine continuous-wave ESR-STM with a second, MHz-range RF tone (ENDOR) to drive and read out nuclear spin transitions of a single 47Ti atom on MgO/Ag(100), at fields of 200 to 1400 mT. In earlier STM work, single-tone "NMR" features exist only at low field and involve electron-nuclear flip-flops. Here the readout is separated from the drive and pure Δm_s = 0 nuclear transitions are resolved. The quadrupole splitting allows individual transitions to be addressed selectively. Bringing ENDOR into STM is a real methodological advance and a natural step towards coherent nuclear-spin control on surfaces. The broader claim, that the driving works "irrespective of hybridization" and through a mechanism independent of A and Q, is less well supported than the spectroscopy itself.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First demonstration of ENDOR detection of Δm_s = 0 nuclear transitions on a single surface atom. The spectra are clean and assignable: Fig. 1e shows the first two ESR peaks equalizing in height, and Figs. 2a and 2b show NMR lines that depend on f_ESR.
- Sensible controls. With f_ESR off resonance, f_NMR has no effect (Fig. 2a, grey), and an I = 0 Ti atom shows no ENDOR features (SFig. 2).
- The line positions are consistent with a spin Hamiltonian over a sevenfold range of field (Figs. 2b and 3b). The fitted A_z = 132.1 ± 0.4 MHz and Q = -2.8 ± 0.8 MHz agree with earlier values.
- The V_ESR dependence (Fig. 4) gives an independent way to assign transitions I and II to the m_s = ↓ and ↑ manifolds.
- The technique is general and directly relevant to the high-field, long-T1 regime reported by the same group (ref. 34).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The claim that driving is "irrespective of hybridization" is not established, and textbook ENDOR physics points the other way.** This claim appears in the abstract, the introduction ("we unambiguously demonstrate NMR driving independent of electron-nuclear spin hybridization") and the conclusion.
   - **What the evidence shows.** The evidence is that transitions I to IV remain visible from 200 to 1400 mT (Fig. 3a), while the flip-flop admixture |⟨↓,−3/2|ψ⟩|² computed in SFig. 1b falls. ENDOR separates readout from driving, but it says nothing about how the Δm_I = ±1 transition is driven.
   - **The hyperfine-enhancement argument.** In ENDOR at high field, an oscillating field acting on the electron spin tilts the electron quantization axis adiabatically at f_NMR. Through the hyperfine coupling, this produces a transverse field at the nucleus. The resulting enhancement over direct nuclear Zeeman driving is of order m_s·A/ν_N.
   - **The numbers here.** With A/2 ≈ 66 MHz and ν_N ≈ 2.4 MHz/T × B, the enhancement is about 60 at 450 mT and still about 20 at 1.4 T. So the electron-mediated (hybridization-mediated) drive is expected to dominate across the whole field range studied. This admixture is the adiabatic transverse tilt of the electron spin. It is not the flip-flop coefficient plotted in SFig. 1, so SFig. 1b is the wrong measure of "hybridization" for this argument.
   - **The ESR-STM driving fields act on the electron.** The proposed mechanisms (piezoelectric displacement in the tip field, barrier modulation, spin torque; refs. 28 and 42 to 45) act on the electron spin. Supplementary Note 1 itself states that B_tip "is caused by exchange interaction and hence cannot couple directly with the nuclear spin". Such fields can reach the nucleus only through the hyperfine interaction.
   - **The data cannot separate the scenarios.** The ENDOR amplitude in Fig. 3a cannot tell a drive strength that scales roughly as 1/B (hyperfine-mediated) from one that is independent of field (direct). The lines are saturated (equalized peaks in Fig. 1e), and V_ESR changes with field (8 to 20 mV; SFig. 6b). The authors themselves attribute the change in signal to V_ESR.
   - **What the authors should do.**
     - Measure the nuclear drive strength as a function of field at fixed, calibrated V_ESR. The ENDOR linewidth and depth against V_NMR (power broadening and onset of saturation) at three or more fields would give an effective nuclear Rabi rate, or a lower bound on it.
     - Compare that field dependence quantitatively with (i) direct nuclear Zeeman driving and (ii) the hyperfine-enhanced drive of an electron-acting field.
     - Estimate the oscillating field B1 that direct nuclear driving would need in order to saturate the transition against the spin-pumping rate at I_set = 4 pA, and say whether that field is plausible in the junction.
     - If the data cannot separate the two scenarios, restate the claim. The defensible claim is that NMR (Δm_s = 0) transitions can be driven and detected without relying on flip-flop hybridization for readout. That is different from saying the drive is independent of electron-nuclear coupling.

B. Should address

2. **The arguments that exclude quadrupole and hyperfine driving are incomplete, and the proposed "oscillating magnetic field" mechanism is not tied to any estimate** (Discussion; Supplementary Note 3; SFig. 8).
   - **Hyperfine modulation.** Only modulation that drives flip-flops (|m_s, m_I⟩ ↔ |m_s ± 1, m_I ∓ 1⟩) is considered. Two other routes give Δm_s = 0, Δm_I = ±1 transitions at MHz frequencies: modulation of pseudo-secular terms (S_z I_x type), and modulation of the electron quantization axis. The tip field is tilted by φ ≈ 5° (Supplementary Note 1), which breaks the C4v symmetry that would otherwise suppress these terms.
   - **Quadrupole modulation.** The absence of Δm_I = ±2 lines (SFig. 8) does not exclude quadrupole driving. A vertical or lateral tip displacement mainly modulates V_zz (no transition) and V_xz, V_yz (Δm_I = ±1 only). Δm_I = ±2 requires modulation of V_xx − V_yy.
   - **The weak V/VI response fits quadrupole driving.** The weak response at the −1/2 ↔ +1/2 positions (Fig. 2b) is what V_xz-type quadrupole driving predicts: the matrix element is proportional to (2m+1), which vanishes for this transition. It is therefore not evidence against that mechanism.
   - **A test is available.** Magnetic-dipole and quadrupole driving predict different ratios of drive strength for −5/2 ↔ −3/2 (I) against −3/2 ↔ −1/2 (III). The predicted squared-matrix-element ratios are about 0.6 for magnetic-dipole driving and about 2.5 for V_xz quadrupole driving. These ratios are measurable from V_NMR-dependent saturation of I and III.
   - **What the authors should do.** Add this comparison. Add a tip-height (setpoint) dependence of the drive efficiency, since tip-field-gradient mechanisms predict a strong dependence. Soften the Discussion accordingly.

3. **One atom and one microtip.**
   - All main-text ENDOR data come from a single microtip. The paper does not say how many 47Ti atoms were measured.
   - B_tip (about 67 mT, tilted about 5°) shapes the spectrum, and probably the driving mechanism as well, so reproducibility matters.
   - Fig. 1d, the low-field single-tone comparison, was taken with a different microtip and at different setpoints (V_bias = 70, 75 and 90 mV). Its contrast with Fig. 3 ("features vanish at 200 mT" against "observed for all fields") therefore mixes tip, setpoint and readout scheme.
   - What the authors should do: show ENDOR on at least one more 47Ti atom and/or tip. Ideally, also take ENDOR at 60 to 100 mT with the same tip used for Fig. 3, so that the two regimes can be compared directly.

4. **Calibration and artifacts of the MHz line.**
   - A transfer function is shown only for the GHz line (SFig. 6). V_NMR is quoted as 20 mV at the junction from 20 to 170 MHz, but no MHz transfer function is shown. Resonances in the MHz line could change the effective drive, or produce features at fixed f_NMR.
   - Faint horizontal structure at about 48 and about 83 MHz may be present in the raw map (SFig. 3). After column-wise background subtraction (Fig. 2b), it is not possible to judge whether those features are confined to the ESR peak columns.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Show the MHz transfer function.
     - Show raw line cuts of SFig. 3 at f_ESR between the hyperfine peaks.
     - State whether the feedback loop is open during the f_NMR sweeps, since a continuous MHz tone of 20 mV could shift tip height or rectified current.

5. **Hamiltonian fitting and the nuclear g-factor** (Supplementary Note 1; SFig. 5).
   - **Transverse hyperfine values are missing.** Only the z-components are fitted, and the A_x and A_y values used are not stated. Second-order hyperfine terms of order A_⊥²/(4ν_e) shift the NMR lines by an amount that falls as 1/B. Between 0.2 T (ν_e ≈ 2 GHz) and 1.4 T (ν_e ≈ 11.5 GHz), this can be a shift of order MHz, comparable to the nuclear Zeeman change of about 3 MHz.
   - **The g_N fit is affected.** A linear fit of line I against B (SFig. 5) therefore mixes the nuclear Zeeman term with pseudo-nuclear-Zeeman and second-order shifts. This may account for g_N = 0.37 ± 0.04 against the bulk value of 0.315.
   - **The Boltzmann assumption contradicts the main text.** The ESR fits assume a Boltzmann distribution of peak intensities, whereas the main text stresses non-thermal, pumped populations.
   - **B_tip uncertainty is implausibly small.** B_tip,z = 65.64 ± 0.02 mT is quoted from a single global fit, although the authors say tip height varied between measurements (Fig. 2 discussion).
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - State all fixed parameters.
     - Fit the line I and II positions from the full Hamiltonian with g_N free.
     - Report realistic uncertainties.
     - Discuss the g_N discrepancy in terms of second-order hyperfine effects.

6. **Fig. 4 population analysis.**
   - Reading the ratio of dip amplitudes of I and II as P↑/P↓ assumes that the two transitions are driven equally and to the same degree of saturation. It also assumes the dip amplitude is linear in population.
   - The inset rests on three V_ESR points with error bars of about 0.1 to 0.15, and the trend is described as "approaching 1".
   - What the authors should do: state these assumptions, show how the error bars were obtained, and ideally add V_ESR points or a simple rate-equation model of the expected ratio.

7. **Assignment of the weaker transitions.**
   - The text says the authors "resolve at least two pairs" and that "another pair may be discerned" for V/VI (−1/2 ↔ +1/2) between 4.0 and 4.1 GHz. In Fig. 2b these features are close to the noise.
   - In Fig. 2a, the predicted peaks in the red (m_I = −3/2) trace are barely distinguishable from the noise.
   - What the authors should do: provide line cuts with fits and signal-to-noise for V/VI and for the red trace, or state explicitly that these transitions are tentative. The V/VI splitting should equal 2ν_N plus second-order corrections, so check that the observed positions agree with this.

C. Minor

8. **Overstated wording.** "Unambiguously demonstrate" in the introduction is too strong; see concern 1. The suggestion that coherence times may be "in the same order of magnitude" as T1 of seconds is speculative, so either justify it or remove it.

9. **Fig. 3b.**
   - The caption describes "grey dashed lines", but the lines appear solid.
   - The figure has no error bars, although SFig. 5 has them.
   - The outliers are not discussed: IV at 1.07 T (about 72 MHz against about 78 MHz expected) and II at 1.4 T (about 90 MHz against about 86 MHz).

10. **Quadrupole convention.**
    - With the tensor of Eq. S2, the effective coefficient of I_z² is 3Q/2, not Q. It is this coefficient that sets the roughly 35 MHz I–II splitting. Please make the convention explicit in Eq. 1.
    - Quote the literature Q value behind the "~5% deviation". A 5% difference is well inside the ±0.8 MHz (about 30%) uncertainty.

11. **Hybridization factor.** The text says hybridization falls "by approximately a factor of 10" between 200 and 1400 mT. Please state whether this refers to an amplitude or a probability, and make sure it agrees with the logarithmic axis of SFig. 1b.

12. **Colour naming.**
    - Fig. 1b and SFig. 2 call the same trace "yellow" in places and "orange" in others.
    - The Fig. 1c caption mentions "yellow lines", but the lines in the panel appear orange.

13. **Reference and file errors.**
    - SFig. 6 caption cites "Fig. S3 a" where SFig. 4a is meant.
    - References 20 and 29 are the same paper, and so are 35 and 38.
    - The Data availability statement contains a placeholder "[URL]".
    - Typos: "Rhode & Schwarz" should be "Rohde & Schwarz"; "converge" should be "convergence" in Supplementary Note 1.

14. **Missing acquisition details.** Please give the time per sweep point, the lock-in time constant, the number of sweeps per spectrum (Fig. 2b, SFig. 8), and the feedback condition. Without these, signal-to-noise cannot be compared across figures.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Measure ENDOR line depth and width against V_NMR at three or more magnetic fields, with matched V_ESR. Fit with a rate-equation model to extract an effective nuclear Rabi rate. This directly tests whether the drive is hyperfine-enhanced or direct (concerns 1 and 2).
2. Add a tip-height (setpoint current) series of ENDOR drive efficiency, together with an estimate of the junction B1 field that direct nuclear driving would need. This would discriminate among the proposed driving mechanisms.
3. Fit all observed NMR lines globally with the full spin Hamiltonian, with A_⊥ and g_N treated explicitly, and report correlated uncertainties.
4. If feasible, add a pulsed ENDOR or nuclear Rabi measurement at one high field (for example 1.4 T). Even a coarse Rabi frequency would anchor the drive-mechanism discussion and the outlook on coherent control.

5. Verdict: major revision
