Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports electron–nuclear double resonance (ENDOR) in an ESR-STM on a single 47Ti atom on MgO/Ag(100). A second, MHz-range RF tone drives Δm_I = ±1 transitions, and the populations are read out through the hyperfine-split ESR peak heights. Because the quadrupole term lifts the degeneracy, up to six NMR lines are resolved, and they are followed from 200 to 1400 mT. Earlier STM work (Veldman 2024, Stolte 2025) showed only hybridization-mediated "NMR-like" flip-flop signals at low field. ENDOR detection of transitions that conserve m_s, and that persist at high field, is a clear technical step and of broad interest to the atomic-spin-on-surfaces and spin-qubit communities. The stronger claims are that the nucleus is driven "irrespective of its hybridization" and that the drive is an effective oscillating magnetic field. These are less well supported than the spectroscopy itself.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First ENDOR (pure-NMR-type, Δm_s = 0) spectroscopy on a single surface atom. The detection scheme separates driving from readout (Fig. 1e, Fig. 2).
- Good controls for the existence of the signal: f_ESR off resonance gives no dependence on f_NMR (Fig. 2a, grey), and an I = 0 Ti isotope under ENDOR gives no features (SFig. 2).
- Several transitions are resolved and assigned (I–VI). The assignment is checked in three ways: the 2D map (Fig. 2b), the sign of the nuclear Zeeman shift with field (Fig. 3b, I/III decreasing and II/IV increasing), and the V_ESR dependence of the ↑/↓ manifold amplitudes (Fig. 4).
- A_z = 132.1 ± 0.4 MHz and Q = −2.8 ± 0.8 MHz are extracted with MHz precision. A_z agrees with earlier studies.
- The data and code are said to be deposited.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The claim that driving is "irrespective of hybridization" or "independent of hybridization" is not demonstrated.** This appears in the Abstract, in the Introduction ("we unambiguously demonstrate NMR driving independent of electron-nuclear spin hybridization") and in the Conclusion ("hybridization … is not necessary to drive NMR").
   - **What the paper shows.** ENDOR lines are still seen at 1400 mT, where the flip-flop admixture is about 10× smaller than at 200 mT (SFig. 1b). This shows that the transitions are detectable with a readout that does not rely on hybridization. It does not show that the drive does not rely on hybridization.
   - **Why the admixture still matters at high field.** In standard ENDOR the effective nuclear transition moment includes a hyperfine-enhancement term. The drive acts on the electron, and its admixture ε ~ A_⊥/(2hf_e) mixes that into the nuclear transition. Here f_e ≈ 4–11 GHz and A/2 ≈ 65 MHz, so ε is about 10⁻²–10⁻³. The ratio of electron to nuclear moments for Ti on MgO is about μ_B g_e,z/(μ_N g_N) ≈ 0.56 × 1836/0.315 ≈ 3×10³. The electron-mediated matrix element can therefore exceed the bare nuclear one by one to two orders of magnitude, even at 1.4 T. A 10× drop in |⟨↓,−3/2|ψ⟩|² across the studied range is a modest change, not a regime in which hybridization is absent.
   - **No measure of drive strength.** The paper gives no quantity that tracks drive efficiency against field. The ENDOR amplitudes in Fig. 3a are confounded by the V_ESR changes the authors acknowledge (20 → 8–10 → 12 mV; Suppl. Note 2, SFig. 6). No V_NMR dependence is shown at any field: no saturation curve, no power broadening of the NMR linewidth, and no V_NMR at which the ESR peaks equalize.
   - **What the authors should do:**
     - (a) Measure ENDOR amplitude and linewidth against V_NMR at two or more fields with V_ESR held equal, e.g. 200 and 1070 mT. Extract a saturation parameter or power-broadening slope, which is a proxy for the nuclear Rabi rate. Compare its field dependence with the scaling expected for a hyperfine-enhanced drive (∝ ε(B)) and for a direct nuclear drive (field-independent).
     - (b) Estimate, with the fitted Hamiltonian, the size of the electron-mediated term relative to a direct nuclear-Zeeman drive at each field.
   - If neither test is done, the wording should be reduced to "NMR transitions detected without relying on hybridization for readout, at fields where hybridization is reduced by an order of magnitude". The claims about driving should be removed.

B. Should address

2. **The driving-mechanism argument (Discussion; Suppl. Note 3) is incomplete and partly self-contradictory.**
   - (i) Hyperfine modulation is ruled out because it "would drive flip-flop transitions … in the GHz range". This only holds for the S_xI_x + S_yI_y part. Suppl. Note 1 fits a tilted tip field (φ ≈ 5°) and A is anisotropic, so in the quantization frame there are pseudo-secular S_zI_x terms. An RF modulation of these (e.g. by piezo displacement, which changes the adsorption geometry) drives Δm_I = ±1 at exactly the observed MHz frequencies, with no electron flip.
   - (ii) The proposed "effective oscillating magnetic field" is modelled on the ESR-STM tip-field mechanism. Suppl. Note 1, however, states that the tip field "is caused by exchange interaction and hence cannot couple directly with the nuclear spin". An oscillating exchange field acts on the nucleus only through the electron, which is the hyperfine-mediated path the paper sets out to exclude.
   - (iii) Quadrupole modulation is argued against on two grounds. The first is "some response" at the −1/2 ↔ +1/2 lines V/VI. In Fig. 2b this is the weakest feature and is not quantified. The second is the absence of Δm_I = ±2 lines in SFig. 8. That spectrum has a noise level (~2–3 fA) comparable to the strongest single-quantum dips, and no expected Δm = 2 amplitude is given.
   - **Requested:**
     - Signal-to-noise and fitted amplitudes for V/VI.
     - A predicted Δm = 2 amplitude relative to Δm = 1 under quadrupole modulation, to show the null result is meaningful.
     - The B_1 amplitude needed to explain the observed saturation, compared with plausible sources.
     - Dependences that discriminate the mechanisms, such as tip height or setpoint, which change piezo and tunnelling-barrier modulation differently.

   Short of this, the mechanism should be stated as an open question.

3. **The outlook on coherence is not supported by the conditions of the experiment** (Conclusion: "relaxation time is in order of seconds … potentially resulting in coherence times in the same order of magnitude").
   - Under the measurement conditions the electron spin is continuously flipped by ESR driving and spin-polarized tunnelling. Each flip shifts the nuclear transition frequency by ~35 MHz (I at ~48 MHz, II at ~85 MHz). The nuclear coherence is therefore limited by the electron flip rate, not by nuclear T1.
   - The observed NMR linewidths of several MHz (Fig. 3a, Fig. 4) are consistent with this.
   - The authors should:
     - analyse the linewidth against V_ESR (Fig. 4 already has three values) and against setpoint current;
     - say what dephasing time the linewidths imply;
     - temper the statement, or describe the conditions (current off, electron in its ground state) under which long coherence could be expected.

4. **The low-field and high-field comparison is not matched.**
   - The argument that hybridization-dependent features vanish while ENDOR persists compares two datasets with different conditions. Fig. 1d was taken "with a different microtip" (Methods), at different setpoints (I_set = 2.5–3 pA, V_bias = 70–90 mV) and with single-tone detection. The ENDOR data (Figs. 1e, 2–4) start only at 200 mT.
   - Single-tone NMR at 200–450 mT and ENDOR at 60–100 mT, both with the ENDOR tip and atom, would let the reader see directly how the two detection schemes compare as hybridization grows.
   - This would also support concern 1: if the ENDOR drive is hybridization-assisted, ENDOR amplitude at fixed V_NMR should grow strongly toward 60 mT.

5. **Reproducibility.**
   - All main-text ENDOR data come from one microtip and, as far as can be told, one 47Ti atom.
   - The paper does not state how many 47Ti atoms or tips showed ENDOR, or whether the line positions (A_z, Q) and the presence of III/IV and V/VI reproduce.
   - This matters most for Q, which the authors themselves suggest depends on tip shape.
   - ENDOR spectra from at least one more atom and/or tip, even at a single field, should be shown.

6. **Field-dependent amplitudes in Fig. 3a are interpreted beyond what the data support.**
   - The text attributes the weakening of III/IV with field to "reduced hybridization decreas[ing] the efficiency of spin pumping". It attributes the reduction of II/IV to lower ↑ occupation.
   - V_ESR changes from 20 to 8–12 mV across this series (caption, SFig. 6). Fig. 4 shows that V_ESR itself changes the ↑/↓ balance and the overall amplitude.
   - The authors should either compare amplitude ratios that are insensitive to the overall scale (III/I, IV/II) against field together with a model for each ratio, or repeat a subset of fields at a common V_ESR (e.g. 8 mV at 200, 450 and 1070 mT).

7. **The nuclear g-factor extraction (SFig. 5; Fig. 3b) is fragile.**
   - The fitted slope of line I is set mainly by the two highest-field points (1070 and 1400 mT). These are the noisiest spectra in Fig. 3a. Below 450 mT the points are flat within error.
   - The linear fit ignores second-order hyperfine shifts (∝ A_⊥²/f_e), which vary with field and bias the apparent slope.
   - In Fig. 3b, IV at 1.07 T (~72 MHz) and II at 1.4 T (~90 MHz) lie well off the calculated lines. No error bars are shown for most points.
   - The authors should fit g_N inside the full Hamiltonian, with the second-order terms included, and give error bars for all points in Fig. 3b. Alternatively, they should report g_N = 0.37 ± 0.04 only as consistent with the bulk value within ~1.4σ, not as a deviation.

C. Minor

8. **Frequencies of transitions III and IV.** The text says the second pair appears "at ~65 MHz and ~75 MHz (labelled III and IV)". Fig. 2b and Figs. 3a,b place III near 56–57 MHz.

9. **Uncertainty on B_tip,z.**
   - In Suppl. Note 1 the linear fit gives B_tip,z = 65.64 ± 0.02 mT, while g_e,z = 0.56 ± 0.02. The intercept and slope are strongly correlated, so a 3.6% uncertainty in g implies an uncertainty of order mT in B_tip,z.
   - The later B*_tip = 67.3 ± 2.1 mT is consistent with that.
   - Please give the uncertainty with correlations taken into account.

10. **Comparison of Q with earlier work.** Q = −2.8 ± 0.8 MHz is described as "a deviation of ~5%" from earlier reports, and the difference is attributed to tip-dependent field gradients. The uncertainty is about 30%, so the values agree. Please quote the literature value and drop or soften the explanation.

11. **Sign of g_N.** 47Ti has a negative nuclear moment. Please state the sign convention for g_N, which sets which manifold's lines rise with field. Its consistency with Fig. 3b is part of the assignment argument.

12. **Calibration of V_NMR.** The MHz transfer function is mentioned (Suppl. Note 2) but not shown. Please show the V_NMR delivered to the junction over 30–170 MHz. Frequency-dependent V_NMR would distort the relative amplitudes of I–IV and the Δm = 2 search in SFig. 8.

13. **Figs. 2a and 2b.**
    - The text says the −3/2 readout (red, Fig. 2a) shows peaks at ~50 and ~85 MHz. These are hard to see above noise; a fitted amplitude with error would help.
    - In Fig. 2b, column-mean background subtraction can create or suppress features where a column contains a strong line. A row-independent baseline or the raw-data cuts (SFig. 3) at V/VI should be shown.
    - The explanation of the amplitude difference between Figs. 2a and 2b ("different tip heights … ~10 fA offset") should be made concrete.

14. **Independence of the "calculated" markers.** The calculated transitions in Fig. 2b use A_z and Q fitted to these same NMR frequencies. "Consistent with our eigenenergy calculations" (Abstract) should make clear that the agreement is a fit with two free parameters, and state how many line positions constrain it.

15. **Fig. 4.** Please state how the P↓ and P↑ amplitudes are normalized and how the inset error bars were obtained. With three V_ESR points, "approaching 1" should be stated cautiously.

16. **Editorial.**
    - The Data availability statement has a placeholder ("[URL]").
    - The SFig. 6 caption refers to "Fig. S3 a" where SFig. 4a seems meant.
    - Refs. 20 and 29 are duplicates, as are refs. 35 and 38.
    - "Rhode & Schwarz" should read "Rohde & Schwarz".
    - Typos: "converge" should read "convergence", and "ESR frequency send" should read "sent".

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a V_NMR power series, giving saturation and linewidth, at two fields with matched V_ESR. This one dataset would address concerns 1, 2 and 3 together.
2. Use the fitted Hamiltonian to compute, for each field, the expected Δm_I = ±1 matrix elements under three candidate drives: a direct transverse B_1 on the nucleus, an electron-mediated transverse field (hyperfine enhancement) and modulated pseudo-secular hyperfine terms. Compare the predicted relative intensities of I–VI with the measured ones.
3. Show ENDOR on a second 47Ti atom and/or with a second tip, and extend ENDOR down to 60–100 mT with the same tip for a matched comparison with Fig. 1d.
4. Rephrase the Abstract, Introduction and Conclusion so that what is demonstrated (ENDOR detection of Δm_s = 0 nuclear transitions with readout independent of hybridization, followed to 1.4 T) is separated from what is proposed (drive mechanism, hybridization independence of the drive, long coherence).

5. Verdict: major revision
