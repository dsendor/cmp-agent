Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use an ESR-STM electron-nuclear double resonance (ENDOR) scheme to detect Δm_s = 0 nuclear spin transitions of a single ⁴⁷Ti atom on MgO/Ag(100). The ESR peak heights serve as the readout of the nuclear populations, and a second, MHz-range RF tone drives the nucleus. The quadrupole splitting resolves individual Δm_I = ±1 transitions, which are tracked from 200 to 1400 mT and assigned to the electron-spin manifolds by varying the ESR drive. Separating NMR drive from readout on a single surface atom in an STM is a real step beyond earlier hybridization-assisted (Δm_s ≠ 0) "NMR-like" transitions on Ti and Cu. However, the stronger claim that the nucleus is driven "independent of" or "irrespective of" its hybridization with the electron spin is not established quantitatively.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The double-resonance scheme decouples drive and readout and is described clearly (Fig. 1e, Fig. 2a inset, SFig. 9).
- Two sensible controls: no f_NMR dependence with f_ESR parked off any ESR line (grey trace in Fig. 2a), and no feature on an I = 0 Ti isotope (SFig. 2).
- A 2D (f_ESR, f_NMR) map (Fig. 2b, raw data in SFig. 3) ties each NMR feature to the m_I population being probed.
- Transitions I–IV are followed over a sevenfold range of field (Fig. 3). Their slopes have opposite signs for the two m_s manifolds, consistent with the nuclear Zeeman term.
- The V_ESR series (Fig. 4) gives an independent way to assign features I/II to m_s = ↓/↑. At the lowest V_ESR, P↑/P↓ ≈ 0.63, close to the Boltzmann factor exp(−hf_ESR/k_BT) ≈ 0.6 at 3.8 GHz and 0.4 K, which supports the assignment.
- Raw data and code are to be deposited.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The claim of driving "independent of hybridization" is not supported quantitatively, and the leading alternative, hyperfine-enhanced drive through the electron spin, is not considered.
   - Where the claim is made: the abstract ("the nuclear spin can be driven efficiently irrespective of its hybridization with the electron spin"), the Introduction ("we unambiguously demonstrate NMR driving independent of electron-nuclear spin hybridization") and the Conclusion ("hybridization of the nuclear spin with the electron spin is not necessary to drive NMR").
   - The evidence offered: ENDOR features persist from 200 to 1400 mT, a range over which the relevant admixture |⟨↓,−3/2|ψ⟩|² falls by only about a factor of 10 (SFig. 1b).
   - Why this is not enough: a factor of 10 in probability is only a factor of about 3 in amplitude, and the admixture never reaches zero. More importantly, any oscillating magnetic (or effective magnetic) field also acts on the electron spin. Through the small transverse-hyperfine admixture, that field produces a Δm_s = 0, Δm_I = ±1 matrix element. This matrix element is the standard hyperfine enhancement of the effective nuclear g-factor in ENDOR.
   - Order of magnitude: with γ_e/γ_N ≈ (0.56 μ_B)/(0.315 μ_N) ≈ 3×10³ and an admixture amplitude of about A/(2hf_ESR) ≈ 132 MHz / 7.6 GHz ≈ 0.02 at 450 mT, the electron-mediated term exceeds the bare nuclear Zeeman coupling by roughly 50. Even at 1.4 T it is still more than 10 times larger.
   - Consequence: the observed NMR driving could be dominated by hybridization at every field studied. Signal persisting at high field therefore does not show that hybridization is "not necessary". The tilted tip field (φ ≈ 5°, Suppl. Note 1) makes this worse, because it adds pseudo-secular S_z′I_x terms.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Compute, as a function of B_z, the Δm_I = ±1, Δm_s = 0 matrix elements of the candidate drive operators (transverse B_1 on S and on I; modulation of B_tip magnitude and direction; modulation of A and Q) in the full eigenbasis of Eq. (1), including the fitted tip-field tilt.
     - Compare the predicted field dependence of the drive strength with the measured one. That comparison requires the ENDOR amplitude or linewidth versus V_NMR at several fields, normalised by the calibrated junction V_ESR and V_NMR (concern 3).
     - If the measured drive follows the hybridization-mediated prediction, reword the claims to what the data show: NMR driving with a drive that does not require Δm_s ≠ 0 readout.

B. Should address

2. The case against hyperfine modulation, and the conclusion that the drive is an oscillating magnetic field, is incomplete.
   - The Discussion rules out hyperfine modulation on the grounds that it would only drive GHz flip-flops. That holds only for modulation of the isotropic S·I term in the high-field basis.
   - Modulation of the anisotropic hyperfine, or of the electron quantization axis, contains S_z I_x components that drive Δm_I = ±1 at MHz with m_s conserved. The axis can be modulated by piezo-induced motion in the tip-field gradient, the mechanism invoked for ESR-STM.
   - In other words, the proposed "effective oscillating magnetic field" and "hyperfine modulation" are not cleanly separable here.
   - The argument against quadrupole modulation rests on two weak observations:
     - weak, marginal features V/VI in Fig. 2b;
     - the absence of Δm_I = ±2 lines in a single noisy trace (SFig. 8), for which neither the expected relative amplitude nor the junction V_NMR at 100–170 MHz is given.
   - The authors should:
     - estimate expected Δm_I = 2 versus Δm_I = 1 intensities for quadrupole modulation;
     - show the V_NMR transfer function across 30–170 MHz;
     - state the mechanism conclusion as a hypothesis, as the Discussion already partly does ("we hypothesize"), rather than as the Conclusion's expectation.

3. No V_NMR dependence and no linewidth analysis are shown.
   - All ENDOR data use V_NMR = 20 mV. The NMR lines are about 3–5 MHz wide (Figs. 3a, 4).
   - The paper does not say whether this width is power broadening, lifetime broadening from tunnelling-induced electron spin flips (which move the nuclear frequency by about 35 MHz between manifolds), or something else.
   - The amplitude and width versus V_NMR is the most direct measure of the drive strength, and therefore of "efficient" driving (Fig. 1e, "saturate the two nuclear spin populations"). It is also what concerns 1–2 need.
   - The authors should show the ENDOR amplitude and width versus V_NMR, ideally at two fields and two setpoint currents, and extract a drive rate.

4. The claim of seconds-scale coherence in the Conclusion is not supported.
   - The Conclusion suggests coherence times "in the same order of magnitude" as the seconds-scale relaxation time of Ref. 34.
   - The measured linewidths imply T₂* ≲ 100 ns under the measurement conditions, most likely limited by electron spin flips from tunnelling electrons and ESR driving.
   - The authors should discuss which conditions would give long coherence (for example, the tip retracted during the drive) and temper the statement.

5. Reproducibility across atoms and tips is not documented.
   - The Methods state that all main-text data come from one microtip, apart from Fig. 1d. The number of ⁴⁷Ti atoms measured is not given.
   - The authors should state how many atoms and tips showed the ENDOR features and give the spread in A_z, Q and the line positions. Q in particular is attributed to "a different electric field gradient dependent on the tip shape", which is only testable with more than one tip.

6. The contrast between Fig. 1d and Fig. 3 is not a matched comparison.
   - Both the Introduction and the field study contrast the disappearance of single-tone NMR-like features above about 100–200 mT (Fig. 1d) with the persistence of the ENDOR features. However, Fig. 1d was taken with a different microtip and different setpoints (2.5–3 pA, 70–90 mV, versus 4 pA, 80 mV).
   - Because tip field and spin-pumping rate both affect these signals, the authors should repeat single-tone NMR at 200 and 450 mT with the ENDOR tip and setpoint. This is the matched control for the central contrast of the paper.

7. The spin-pumping account of features III/IV is qualitative only.
   - The paper explains why transitions III/IV (−3/2 ↔ −1/2) appear when probing the m_I = −5/2 population, and why they weaken with field (Fig. 3c, "reduced hybridization decreases the efficiency of spin pumping"), only qualitatively.
   - A rate-equation model with the computed flip-flop rates (SFig. 1b), ESR drive and spin-polarized scattering should reproduce:
     - the sign of III/IV;
     - their amplitudes relative to I/II;
     - the field dependence of those amplitudes;
     - the P↑/P↓ trend of Fig. 4.
   - Such a model would strengthen both the assignments and the population-readout interpretation.

8. The analysis of the 2D map (Fig. 2b) needs checking.
   - In Fig. 2b the band at II (~84 MHz) appears to extend in f_ESR beyond the width of the first ESR line, towards about 3.86 GHz. Background subtraction (minus each column's mean) can also create or suppress weak features such as V/VI.
   - The authors should show line cuts from the raw data (SFig. 3) at f_ESR between ESR peaks, together with the signal-to-noise of V/VI, to confirm that the features are confined to on-resonance f_ESR.

9. The fitted parameters and their uncertainties are inconsistent.
   - From Eq. (S2) with I = 5/2, Q = κ/20. Then κ = −56.7 ± 0.8 MHz gives Q = −2.84 ± 0.04 MHz, not ± 0.8 MHz. The κ uncertainty appears to have been carried over unchanged.
   - Saying the result is "a deviation of ~5% compared to previous reports" needs the previous value stated.
   - g_N = 0.37 ± 0.04 is extracted from the field slope (SFig. 5), but A and Q are then fitted with g_N fixed to the bulk value 0.315 "given the discrepancy".
   - Second-order and hyperfine-enhancement corrections make the effective nuclear Zeeman slope differ from g_Nμ_N. The authors should fit g_N within the full Hamiltonian, or state explicitly that the linear-slope value is an effective quantity.
   - In Fig. 3b, the outliers lie several MHz from the calculated lines (IV at 1.07 T, about 72 MHz; II at 1.4 T, about 90 MHz). They should be discussed in terms of the low signal-to-noise at these fields.

10. The frequency dependence of the drive amplitude affects the amplitude comparisons.
    - The P↑/P↓ ratio in Fig. 4 compares features at about 48 and 84 MHz. Any difference in the junction V_NMR between these two frequencies biases the absolute ratio, and with it the claim that the ratio approaches 1.
    - Likewise, the field-dependent ENDOR amplitudes are attributed to the delivered V_ESR (SFig. 6), but the low-field points use the same V_ESR and still vary.
    - The authors should give the delivered V_NMR at the transition frequencies and correct the ratios if needed. The Fig. 4 inset rests on three points with sizeable error bars; more V_ESR values would help.

C. Minor

11. The wording "unambiguously demonstrate" in the Introduction should be moderated in line with concern 1.
12. Fig. 1 caption: "yellow lines indicate ESR transitions" refers to orange curves.
13. Fig. 3b caption: "grey dashed lines" are drawn as solid lines.
14. In Eq. (1), the Zeeman term "μ_N g_N(B_i) Î_i" lacks a clear bracket, and the meaning of η as a tensor versus a scalar asymmetry parameter (Eq. S2) should be harmonised.
15. The statement "We assign this slight variation … to different tip heights, potentially resulting in a non-negligible tunnelling current offset (~10 fA)" (comparing Fig. 2a and 2b) is unclear and should be explained or removed.
16. Duplicate references: 20 = 29 and 35 = 38. The Data availability statement has a placeholder "[URL]".
17. The ESR-spectrum fitting assumes peak intensities follow a Boltzmann distribution (Suppl. Note 1). This conflicts with the strong non-thermal nuclear polarization reported in the main text and should be justified or removed.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a table, or a figure versus B_z, of calculated drive matrix elements for each candidate mechanism (including the electron-mediated, hyperfine-enhanced magnetic drive). Compare it with measured drive strengths extracted from V_NMR series.
2. Include a rate-equation simulation of the ENDOR spectra (all features I–VI, their signs and relative amplitudes) to back the population-readout model.
3. Perform matched single-tone versus ENDOR measurements with the same tip and setpoint at two or three fields.
4. Show reproducibility on at least one more ⁴⁷Ti atom or tip, and report how the fitted Q varies.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
