A. Must address

1. Each contact width has one device and no normal-state characterisation, so the W_sc trends cannot be told apart from device-to-device spread (Figs. 2a, 2b, 2c, 3, 4b, 4c; abstract). The headline statements, "an increase in both critical current and diode efficiency with increasing contact width" and "a linear trend ... with respect to inverse contact width", rest on one junction per W_sc (0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.2 µm and "∞"), five data points in all. The main text gives no normal-state resistance R_n, I_cR_n product, excess current, induced gap from multiple Andreev reflection, or transparency estimate for any device. I_c depends on transparency, number of modes and the actual contact separation. Junctions with a nominal 80 nm gap made by selective Al wet etching commonly vary by tens of percent in I_c and R_n through gap width, etch undercut and interface quality. A smaller I_c in the narrow-contact devices could therefore reflect a larger R_n (for example a slightly wider gap after the etch) rather than reflection of Andreev bound states at the rear interface. W_sc and the gap were checked only on "nominally identical proxy devices" (Sec. II), with no uncertainty given. Undercut matters most for the 0.3 µm device, which anchors the fit in Fig. 2b, and the 1/ln[I_c^∞/(I_c^∞ − I_c)] transform amplifies errors as I_c approaches I_c^∞. Spread comparable to the effect is already visible: max|η| is non-monotonic (≈ 10% at 0.9 µm versus ≈ 8.5% at 1.2 µm, Fig. 4b); the ∞ device has about twice the efficiency of the 1.2 µm device although its I_c is only slightly larger; and at V_g = 0 the ∞ device has B_* ≈ 22 mT against ≈ 7 mT for the others (Figs. 2c, 3). The authors should report R_n, I_cR_n and, if available, excess current or MAR features for every device (and versus gate voltage), and show that I_cR_n is width-independent or follows Eq. (1), or normalise I_c by it. They should give the SEM-measured W_sc and gap length of the measured junctions, or the spread across proxies, with an uncertainty for each. They should add at least one repeat device at one or more widths, or data from a second chip or cooldown, to show the scatter in I_c, max|η| and B_η. Without this the W_sc dependences, and the coherence-length interpretation built on them, are not established.

B. Should address

2. The 1/W_sc fit of B_η (Fig. 4c) is not the Eq. (3) model, and the scaling does not single out an orbital mechanism. Eq. (3), B_∥W_sc d = Φ, predicts a line through the origin, but the fitted line has an intercept of ≈ 25 mT at W_sc⁻¹ = 0. The fit thus has two parameters for four points, and the intercept has no physical meaning in the stated model. A zero-intercept line with the same slope would put the 1.2 µm point near 60 mT instead of the observed ≈ 95 mT; a zero-intercept fit gives a noticeably larger slope (currently ≈ 72 mT·µm), shifting Φ/Φ₀ ≈ 0.36–0.61 and d = 10–17 nm. The 1.2 µm point (W_sc⁻¹ ≈ 0.83 µm⁻¹) sits on the dashed (2ξ)⁻¹ line where saturation is said to begin, yet is included in the linear fit, and the claim that B_η "saturates for W_sc ≳ 2ξ" rests on the single ∞ device. The finite-momentum resonance condition the authors cite, 2q(2W_sc + W_n) = (2n+1)π/2, gives B ∝ 1/(2W_sc + W_n), nearly indistinguishable from 1/W_sc over 0.3–1.2 µm for W_n = 80 nm; with q = q_M it predicts ≈ 50 mT at W_sc = 1 µm from the paper's own q_M/B, comparable to the data. So the 1/W_sc law does not discriminate orbital flux from spin-orbit or Meissner finite-q mechanisms. Further, the sign changes of η occur in a 400–600 mT window for all devices that show one, roughly independent of W_sc, whereas a flux mechanism with Φ = B W_sc d would shift them by a factor of two between 1.2 and 0.6 µm (with d ≈ 13 nm the 0.6 µm device reaches Φ ≈ 2Φ₀ at 500 mT, and the expected Φ₀-periodic sign changes are not seen). A W-independent scale points rather to something like the Rashba–Zeeman field (estimate 690 mT). The authors should state the fit model and free parameters, fit with and without an intercept and quote the uncertainty on Φ/Φ₀, compare with the 1/(2W_sc + W_n) resonance form, plot the sign-change field against W_sc⁻¹ alongside B_η, and state which alternatives the data exclude. If they cannot be distinguished, the orbital conclusion and the saturation statement should be tempered, or devices with W_sc ≈ 2–5 µm added to test saturation directly.

3. The extracted coherence length and its "good agreement" with the dirty-limit estimate depend on unjustified choices (Sec. III, Eq. (1), Fig. 2b). Eq. (1) says the reflection probability is proportional to the amplitude ψ ∝ exp(−W_sc/ξ); a probability would normally go as |ψ|² ∝ exp(−2W_sc/ξ), which gives ξ ≈ 1.17 µm from the same data, double the estimate. The estimate itself carries choices comparable to the claimed 3% agreement: √(ξ_BCS ℓ) versus √(ħD/Δ) with D = v_Fℓ/2 in 2D (≈ 0.71 µm, a ≈ 25% difference) and the 0.85 BCS dirty-limit prefactor; Δ is the bulk Al gap (≈ 250 µeV) rather than the typically smaller induced gap that sets the ABS decay under the Al; n and ℓ = 130 nm come from magnetotransport on Al-free 2DEG; and Eq. (1) assumes τ → 1 while Eq. (2) and Fig. 3 treat τ as finite and gate-tunable, for which ξ_ABS > ξ. I_c^∞ is fixed from the single ∞ device, which therefore sits at zero in Fig. 2b by construction and is not an independent point; for the wide contacts (I_c/I_c^∞ ≈ 0.8–0.9) a few-percent error in I_c^∞ shifts the 0.9 and 1.2 µm points substantially. Fig. 2b has no error bars and ξ = 586 nm has no uncertainty. Other explanations for I_c rising with W_sc are not discussed: a reduced induced gap in narrow contacts from inverse proximity with bare 2DEG behind the contact, which changes the boundary condition of Eq. (1), and current crowding or a series contribution in the narrow 10 nm Al strips of the T geometry. The authors should derive Eq. (1) or cite a calculation and fix the exponent; fit with I_c^∞ free (with the ∞ device as a constraint) and propagate I_c uncertainties to a confidence interval on ξ; state in Fig. 2b that the ∞ point is zero by construction and give ξ as a fit result with uncertainty; use or bound the induced gap; state the rear-edge boundary condition; discuss the alternatives; and present 586 versus 570 nm as order-of-magnitude consistency rather than a quantitative match.

4. The assignment of B_η to orbital effects and B_* to spin–orbit coupling is not supported quantitatively, and the relation between the two field scales is not addressed (Sec. IV, Conclusion). The paper's own orbital estimate puts the efficiency maximum at B_∥^max ≈ 4 mT (q_orb/B ≈ 2.6×10⁷ m⁻¹T⁻¹) and the Meissner estimate at 13 mT: both far below the measured B_η ≈ 90–270 mT and close to the measured B_* ≈ 5–40 mT. The only spin–orbit/Zeeman estimate, 690 mT, is the one nearest B_η. The flux-quantisation reading (Φ/Φ₀ ≈ 0.36–0.61) is a separate "orbital" picture with a free effective separation d, and the text does not reconcile it with orbital finite-momentum pairing. B_* and B_η differ by an order of magnitude and depend on W_sc and V_g in opposite ways, yet both are presented as finite-momentum signatures without explaining why one q(B) gives extrema at such different fields; an orbital or Meissner term proposed for B_η would also shift I_c(B) and so contribute to B_*. Sec. IV calls the extrema "qualitatively consistent with multiple SDE mechanisms" without saying which observations exclude any mechanism. The authors should explain what each quantity measures in the model of Ref. [8], compare both scales as functions of W_sc and V_g with a single model including finite contact width, Rashba SOC, Zeeman and the vector potential (e.g. a tight-binding or BdG calculation in the spirit of Refs. [8, 19, 47] with the real geometry), and state which mechanisms the data exclude. Failing that, they should say plainly that the assignment is tentative, that the estimates point the other way for B_*, and that the mechanism of B_η remains undetermined.

5. The gate dependence of B_* is attributed to α and τ, but neither is measured and Eq. (2) depends on other gate-dependent quantities (Sec. III, Eq. (2), Figs. 2c, 3). Eq. (2) has B_* ∝ (1−τ)^{1/4}(k_so/k_F)E_T/g*, and a gate changes k_so, k_F, E_T (through v_F), τ and possibly g*. Raising V_g on a near-surface InAs well normally raises the density, k_F and usually the transparency, which push B_* in different directions; the text picks the direction (larger α, smaller τ) that matches the data, and the "α ↑", "τ ↓" arrows on Fig. 3 assert these directions without data for these devices. Ref. [45] is on a similar heterostructure, not these devices. No V_g-to-density conversion, I_c(V_g), R_n(V_g), α(V_g) or τ(V_g) is given. The "V_g < 0" points are taken at different voltages for different devices (about −5 to −2 V in Fig. 3) yet plotted as one series against W_sc in Fig. 2c, so devices are compared at mismatched densities. The authors should give n(V_g) from Hall-bar data or the gate capacitance, α(V_g) from weak antilocalisation on a co-fabricated Hall bar or from Ref. [45], and τ(V_g) from I_cR_n, excess current or MAR; test whether Eq. (2) reproduces the measured increase of B_* with these inputs at least in order of magnitude; and plot B_* against density or compare devices at matched density.

6. The extraction of B_* is not described, and values of a few mT are comparable to magnet remanence (Sec. III, Figs. 2c, 3, 4c). B_* is obtained "from low-field fits to Eq. (1) of Ref. [8]", but the fit function, fit window, free parameters and origin of the error bars in Figs. 2c, 3 and 4c are not given. A superconducting vector magnet can trap several mT after excursions to ±1 T, and trapped flux in the Al leads can offset the effective zero. The authors should describe the fit explicitly, show that B_* does not depend on field-sweep direction, and calibrate the zero of B_∥, for example from the symmetry point of I_c+ + |I_c−|, the Fraunhofer pattern or a reference junction.

7. Negative B_* values are not discussed (Figs. 2c, 3). At the most negative gate voltages the 1.2 µm and 0.9 µm devices show B_* < 0 (≈ −5 mT and ≈ −1.5 mT in Fig. 3), and Fig. 2c shows ≈ −3 mT. In Eq. (2) the sign of B_* follows the sign of the spin–orbit term, so a reversal would imply a sign change of the effective α or a competing contribution such as the Meissner term (≈ 13 mT). The authors should say whether these values differ significantly from zero given how the error bars were obtained, and discuss what a sign change would mean.

11. Measurement temperature is not stated and no temperature dependence is shown. ξ, η and the candidate mechanisms all depend on temperature: in the dirty limit ξ ∝ 1/√Δ(T), Meissner and orbital finite-q scales go through q₀ ∝ Δ/v_F, and the Rashba–Zeeman scale behaves differently. The authors should give the mixing-chamber and estimated electron temperatures and any check of heating at switching. Measuring I_c(W_sc) at two or three temperatures would test whether the fitted length follows the induced coherence length, and a coarse temperature series of η(B_∥) or B_η for the narrowest and widest contacts would help discriminate mechanisms.

12. I_c+ and I_c− are each taken as the extremum of their own Fraunhofer branch (Sec. II), so they may correspond to different out-of-plane fields. This removes a class of extrinsic effects but changes η relative to a fixed-field definition and may suppress or add W_sc-dependent contributions, for example through flux focusing by contacts of different widths; a relative shift of the extrema is itself a diode signature. The authors should report the out-of-plane field of each extremum as a function of B_∥ and device, and for at least one device compare η with a fixed-B_z definition.
