Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript studies five symmetric, T-shaped Al/InAs planar Josephson junctions, co-fabricated on one chip, that differ only in the longitudinal width W_sc of the superconducting contacts (0.3, 0.6, 0.9 and 1.2 µm, plus a "W_sc → ∞" device). It reports how the critical current and the in-plane-field superconducting diode effect depend on W_sc and on gate voltage. The contact width has received little systematic study, even though theory for finite-momentum pairing and topological phases in planar junctions depends on it, so a controlled geometric study is useful. The main results are: I_c(W_sc) saturates exponentially with a length ξ ≈ 586 nm, close to an estimated dirty-limit coherence length; the peak diode efficiency grows with W_sc; the field B_η of maximum efficiency scales roughly as 1/W_sc, which the authors read as an orbital (flux) effect; and the field B_* of maximum I_c± depends on gate voltage, which they read as a spin–orbit effect. The experimental idea is sound and timely. The quantitative links between the data and the proposed mechanisms are still loose.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- Contact width is varied cleanly while everything else is held fixed: all devices are co-fabricated on a 1.6 mm × 1.6 mm area of one wafer, with a common 80 nm separation and 4 µm width.
- The trends in I_c (Fig. 2a,b) and in B_η (Fig. 4c) with W_sc are clear and large.
- Field and gate are both varied, so field scales that are sensitive to geometry (B_η) can be told apart from field scales that are sensitive to the gate (B_*). This separation is a useful experimental observation in its own right.
- Taking the maximum of each Fraunhofer branch at every in-plane field and gate setting is a sensible way to reduce the extrinsic nonreciprocity that comes from out-of-plane field components.
- The paper is honest that its simple estimates (Eq. 5: 4, 13 and 690 mT) do not reproduce the measured field scales. It also discusses the limits of the single-mode picture.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **One device per contact width, and no device characterization beyond I_c, so a coherence-length origin of the I_c(W_sc) trend cannot be separated from device-to-device variation.** All the W_sc trends (Fig. 2b, Fig. 2c, Fig. 3, Fig. 4b, Fig. 4c) rest on one junction per width, five data points in total. The main text gives no normal-state resistance R_n, I_cR_n product, excess current or transparency estimate for any device. W_sc is confirmed only on "nominally identical proxy devices" (Sec. II), not on the measured junctions. This matters most at small W_sc: lateral wet-etch undercut of 10 nm Al could shift the 0.3 µm device's real width by a sizeable fraction, and the fit in Fig. 2b is most sensitive to that point. Spread is already visible in the data. Fig. 4b is non-monotonic: the 1.2 µm device has max|η| ≈ 8.5% and the 0.9 µm device ≈ 10%. In Fig. 2c and Fig. 3, the W_sc → ∞ device has B_* ≈ 22 mT at V_g = 0, against ≈ 7 mT for the others. Without R_n and I_cR_n for each device, the authors cannot show that the I_c(W_sc) trend reflects contact length rather than variation in contact transparency or interface quality between devices. I_cR_n should be roughly W_sc-independent if only I_c^∞ scaling is involved, or should follow Eq. 1 if the induced gap is what changes. **Request:** report R_n, I_cR_n and (if available) excess current or MAR features for every device, and the SEM-measured W_sc of the measured junctions themselves or an uncertainty for each value. Ideally, add a second device at one or more widths, or nominally identical devices measured elsewhere on the chip, so that device-to-device spread can be compared with the size of the trend.

2. **Eq. 1 is a heuristic with an ambiguous exponent, and the agreement between ξ = 586 nm and the 570 nm estimate does not test it.** Sec. III says the reflection probability "is proportional to the amplitude ψ ∝ exp(−W_sc/ξ)". A probability would normally scale with |ψ|², i.e. exp(−2W_sc/ξ). With that choice the same data give ξ ≈ 1.17 µm, double the dirty-limit estimate. The estimate of ξ itself has several choices whose effects are comparable to the 3% agreement claimed:
   - (i) the dirty-limit prefactor: √(ξ_BCS ℓ) versus √(ħD/Δ) with D = v_Fℓ/2 in 2D gives ≈ 0.71 µm, and the BCS dirty-limit expression has a 0.85 prefactor;
   - (ii) Δ is taken as the Al gap, 250 µeV, whereas the decay length of ABSs into the proximitized region is set by the induced gap in the InAs under the Al, which in such heterostructures is typically smaller;
   - (iii) n and ℓ = 130 nm come from magnetotransport on Al-free 2DEG, while carrier density and scattering under the Al differ.

   The fit also has, in effect, one parameter and four free points. In Fig. 2b, I_c^∞ is taken from the W_sc → ∞ device, so that point sits at zero by construction and the line is forced through the origin. No uncertainty on ξ is given. Other explanations for I_c rising with W_sc are not discussed:
   - a reduced induced gap in narrow contacts from inverse proximity with the uncovered 2DEG behind the contact (Fig. 1a suggests the region behind each Al strip is bare semiconductor rather than vacuum, which changes the boundary condition assumed in Eq. 1);
   - current crowding or a series contribution when current is fed along narrow 10 nm Al strips in the T geometry.

   **Request:** derive Eq. 1, or cite a calculation, and fix the exponent. Give ξ with a confidence interval from a fit that propagates the I_c uncertainties, with I_c^∞ either fixed (state this) or free. Use the induced gap, or bound it, in the comparison. State the boundary condition at the rear edge of the contact. If possible, add I_c(W_sc) at elevated temperature: if ξ is the induced coherence length, the fitted ξ should grow as Δ(T) falls, which would test Eq. 1 directly.

**B. Should address**

3. **The assignment of B_η to orbital effects and of B_* to spin–orbit coupling is not supported quantitatively, and the paper's own estimates argue against it.** The Conclusion says the W_sc dependence of B_η "invites an orbital interpretation", while the gate tunability of B_* "lends credence to a spin–orbit coupling based interpretation". But the paper's orbital estimate (Sec. IV) puts the efficiency maximum at B_∥^max ≈ 4 mT and the Meissner estimate at 13 mT. Both are well below the measured B_η ≈ 90–270 mT (Fig. 4c) and close to the measured B_* ≈ 5–40 mT (Figs. 2c, 3). The only spin–orbit/Zeeman estimate, 690 mT, is the one closest to B_η. The flux-based reading (Φ/Φ_0 ≈ 0.36–0.61) is a separate argument. It involves a free effective separation d, and the expected Φ_0-periodic sign changes are not seen. **Request:** compare both field scales, as functions of W_sc and V_g, with a single model that includes the finite contact width and the vector potential, e.g. a tight-binding or BdG calculation in the spirit of Refs. [8, 19, 47] with the real geometry. Alternatively, state plainly that the assignment is tentative and that the order-of-magnitude estimates point the other way for B_*.

4. **The gate dependence of B_* is attributed to α and τ, but neither is measured, and Eq. 2 depends on several other gate-dependent quantities.** Eq. 2 has B_* ∝ (1−τ)^{1/4} (k_so/k_F) E_T / g^*. A gate voltage changes the carrier density, and with it k_F, E_T (through v_F) and possibly g^*, as well as α and τ. The right-hand axis of Fig. 3 ("increasing α", "decreasing τ") asserts directions that the paper does not show for these devices. Further points:
   - B_* is extracted from "low-field fits to Eq. (1) of Ref. [8]" (Sec. III), but the main text does not describe the fit or its free parameters.
   - Several B_* values are a few mT and some are negative (Fig. 2c, red; Fig. 3 at V_g < 0). That is comparable to typical remanent fields and to the hysteresis of superconducting magnets.
   - The "V_g < 0" series combines different negative voltages for different devices (Fig. 3: about −5, −4 and −3 V), yet Fig. 2c plots them as one series against W_sc.
   - Sec. III says Eq. 2 is valid only for W_sc → ∞ and that B_* is therefore not compared across devices, yet the same paragraph reports a "nontrivial dependence on W_sc" from Fig. 2c.

   **Request:** report the density (and ideally α from weak antilocalization) against V_g for this gate stack, and R_n or I_cR_n against V_g as a proxy for τ. Describe how B_* is extracted and with what uncertainty. Show that B_* does not depend on the field-sweep direction, and calibrate the zero of B_∥ (e.g. from the symmetry point of I_c+ + |I_c−|). Compare devices at matched V_g, or at matched density.

5. **The 1/W_sc law for B_η and its saturation rest on few points and one free intercept, and a resonance mechanism predicts a similar dependence.** In Fig. 4c the fitted line is labeled as a fit to Eq. 3, which predicts B_η = Φ/(W_sc d), passing through the origin. The plotted line instead has an intercept of ≈ 25 mT at W_sc^{-1} = 0. So the fit has two parameters (slope and intercept) and four points, and the intercept is not explained. The claim that B_η "saturates for W_sc ≳ 2ξ" rests on one device, W_sc → ∞, beyond the dashed line at (2ξ)^{-1}. The 1.2 µm device sits essentially on that line. The finite-momentum resonance condition the authors cite, 2q(2W_sc + W_n) = (2n+1)π/2 (Sec. IV), also predicts B_η ∝ 1/(2W_sc + W_n), close to 1/W_sc for W_sc ≫ 80 nm. So the W_sc scaling does not by itself pick out the orbital flux picture. **Request:** state the fit model and parameters exactly. Fit both Φ/(W_sc d) and the resonance form, and compare. Temper the saturation statement, or add a device with W_sc between 1.2 µm and the wide-contact limit.

6. **The diode efficiency is reported mainly at V_g = 0, and its definition mixes out-of-plane fields.** Fig. 4b shows max|η| against W_sc only at V_g = 0, and the trend is not monotonic (concern 1). Because I_c+ and I_c− are each taken as the extremum of their own Fraunhofer branch (Sec. II), the two may correspond to different out-of-plane fields. This choice removes a class of extrinsic effects, but it also changes η relative to a fixed-field definition and may suppress or add contributions that depend on W_sc (for example, through flux focusing by contacts of different widths). **Request:** show max|η| against W_sc at the other gate voltages (the data appear to exist in Sec. S.IV). Report the out-of-plane field at which each extremum occurs, as a function of B_∥ and of device. Show for at least one device how η compares with a fixed-B_z definition.

7. **The device geometry and current path are not described well enough to interpret W_sc.** It is not clear from Fig. 1a and Sec. II what lies behind each Al contact (bare 2DEG, mesa edge, or vacuum), how current reaches the Al strips in the T geometry, or what the "W_sc → ∞" device is physically (contact width, and whether the Al joins the bond pads directly). These details set the boundary condition in Eq. 1 (concern 2), the current distribution along the 4 µm junction, and the possibility of Josephson vortices or kinetic-inductance effects in the narrow strips, which Sec. IV invokes. **Request:** add a schematic of each device's footprint, including the W_sc → ∞ case, and state where the leads connect.

8. **The I_c criterion, switching statistics and electron temperature are not given.** The main text does not state the voltage or resistance criterion for I_c, whether switching or retrapping is hysteretic, how many sweeps are averaged, or the base and electron temperature. With efficiencies of 5–16% and B_* values of a few mT, switching-current fluctuations and heating on switching set the noise floor. Fig. 2a and Fig. 4b have no error bars, and the shaded bands in Fig. 4a are not defined. **Request:** give the I_c definition, the number of repetitions and the resulting standard errors on I_c± and η, and the mixing-chamber and estimated electron temperatures.

**C. Minor**

9. Fig. 4a's shaded bands and the error bars in Figs. 2c, 3 and 4c are not defined in the captions.
10. "Eq. (1) of Ref. [8]" in Sec. III is easily confused with this paper's Eq. 1. Name the model explicitly, e.g. "the finite-momentum model of Ref. [8]".
11. Fig. 2b: state that the W_sc → ∞ point lies at zero by construction of the y-axis and so is not an independent data point. Make the legend "ξ = 586 nm" a fit result with an uncertainty.
12. The Al thickness d_sc = 10 nm is inferred from the in-plane critical field (Sec. II). It enters the Meissner estimate (q_M, via λ_L → d_sc/2) and the effective separation d. Cross-check it with the growth calibration or TEM, if available.
13. Key procedures (Fraunhofer-based extraction of I_c±, the B_* fit, the gate-dependent data) are deferred to Sections S.I–S.IV. A short summary of each in the main text would make the paper self-contained.
14. Sec. III says the Rashba strength "increases with increasing gate voltage due to the increased structural inversion asymmetry". The sign depends on the heterostructure and the gate geometry. Cite the specific measurement on this stack or show it.
15. The arrows for α and τ on the right axis of Fig. 3 assign physical meaning to the V_g axis without data. Remove them, or move them into the caption as an interpretation.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Measure I_c(W_sc) at two or three temperatures, and at finite B_∥, to test whether the fitted length follows the induced coherence length (ξ ∝ 1/√Δ(T) in the dirty limit). This would turn the agreement in Sec. III into a test.
2. Add a modeling section, even a minimal numerical BdG or tight-binding calculation for the finite-W_sc geometry with Rashba SOC, Zeeman and the orbital vector potential. It should predict B_* and B_η against W_sc and against density, and be compared directly with Figs. 2c, 3 and 4c.
3. Use gate-dependent R_n and I_cR_n (already available in principle from the same measurements) to put the transparency axis in Fig. 3 on a measured footing, and plot B_* against density rather than V_g, so that devices can be compared.
4. Consider a geometry in which the orbital area changes independently of W_sc, e.g. a different Al thickness, or a different 2DEG depth as the Conclusion suggests. Even a single such device would separate the flux mechanism from the length-resonance mechanism.

**5. Verdict**

Verdict: major revision
