Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript studies how the longitudinal width W_sc of the superconducting contacts in symmetric Al/InAs planar Josephson junctions affects the critical current and the superconducting diode effect under an in-plane field perpendicular to the current. All five devices were co-fabricated on one chip. Contact width is a design parameter that is often ignored, so a systematic study of it is useful for the planar-junction and diode-effect communities. The data show three trends: I_c rises with W_sc in a saturating way (ξ ≈ 586 nm), the peak diode efficiency rises with W_sc, and the field B_η of maximum efficiency scales roughly as 1/W_sc. The mechanistic conclusions are hedged ("invites an orbital interpretation", "lends credence to a spin–orbit coupling based interpretation"). As the paper stands, these conclusions rest on one device per width and on qualitative comparisons with theory.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The devices are co-fabricated on a 1.6 mm × 1.6 mm area with nominally identical 80 nm gaps. This removes much of the wafer-to-wafer variation that undermines comparisons across studies.
- I_c± are extracted at the extrema of the Fraunhofer central lobe at each in-plane field and gate voltage. This is a sensible way to suppress diode signals produced by an out-of-plane field component.
- The I_c(W_sc) data are described by a simple saturating form, and the fitted length (586 nm) is of the same order as an independent estimate built from transport parameters.
- The paper separates two field scales: B_* (extremum of I_c, gate-tunable, 5–40 mT) and B_η (extremum of η, depends on width, ~90–270 mT). It also attempts to link each to a different mechanism. Separating the two scales is a useful observation for the field.
- The numerical estimates in Section IV are internally consistent. I checked v_F ≈ 3×10⁶ m/s, ξ_BCS ≈ 2.5 µm, ξ_dirty ≈ 0.57 µm, q_M/B, q_RZ/B and the three B^max values, and they follow from the stated parameters.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Every width trend rests on a single device per W_sc, with no measure of device-to-device spread and no normalization that would separate a width effect from variation in junction quality. Figs. 2(a,b), 4(b) and 4(c) each have one device per width (0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.2 µm and "∞"). Junctions with the same nominal 80 nm gap made by selective wet etching of Al commonly vary by tens of percent in I_c and normal resistance R_n, through gap-width variation, etch undercut and interface transparency. The paper confirmed the gaps only on "proxy devices" (Sec. II), not on the measured ones. A spread of that size would substantially change the extracted ξ. The fit has effectively four free data points, with I_c^∞ fixed by a single other device, and the 1/ln transform in Fig. 2(b) amplifies errors as I_c approaches I_c^∞. Fig. 4(b) already shows a non-monotonic step: max|η| is ≈ 8.5% at W_sc = 1.2 µm but ≈ 10% at 0.9 µm. This shows that device-level scatter is comparable to the trend being claimed. Why it matters: the abstract's central statements ("an increase in both critical current and diode efficiency with increasing contact width"; "a linear trend ... with respect to inverse contact width") cannot be distinguished from device-to-device variation without some estimate of that variation. What to do:
   - Report R_n, I_cR_n and, if available, excess current or multiple-Andreev-reflection features for each device. Show that these are width-independent, or normalize I_c by them (I_cR_n against W_sc instead of I_c).
   - Report SEM-measured gap lengths for the measured devices, or at least the spread across the proxies.
   - Ideally, add a second device at one or two widths, or a repeat cooldown of the existing devices, to give an error bar on the width dependence.

2. The 1/W_sc scaling of B_η is presented as evidence for an orbital mechanism via Eq. (3). However, the fit in Fig. 4(c) is not Eq. (3), and the paper's own data and estimates are in tension with that interpretation.
   - (a) Eq. (3), B_∥W_sc d = Φ, predicts a line through the origin in Fig. 4(c). The plotted black line instead has an intercept of ≈ 25 mT at W_sc⁻¹ = 0. Its slope (≈ 72 mT·µm, giving Φ/Φ₀ = 0.036 nm⁻¹ × d) therefore depends on a free offset with no physical meaning in the stated model. A zero-intercept fit to the same three or four points gives a noticeably larger slope. The extracted Φ/Φ₀ ≈ 0.36–0.61 and the d = 10–17 nm range then both shift.
   - (b) If the extremum is set by flux through W_sc d, the sign changes of η should also scale as 1/W_sc. Sec. IV instead reports sign changes in a narrow 400–600 mT window for all devices that show one. Over the 1.2 to 0.6 µm range, the orbital picture predicts a factor-of-two shift. A W-independent sign-change field points instead to a W-independent scale such as the Zeeman/Rashba one, whose estimate is B^max_RZ ≈ 690 mT.
   - (c) The paper's own single-mode orbital estimate (q_orb/B ≈ 2.6×10⁷ m⁻¹T⁻¹, B^max ≈ 4 mT) is more than an order of magnitude below the observed B_η ≈ 90–270 mT. The text does not reconcile the two "orbital" pictures (flux quantization through W_sc d, and orbital finite-momentum pairing).
   - (d) The paper notes that the resonance condition 2q(2W_sc + W_n) = (2n+1)π/2 gives a W-dependence for any finite-q mechanism. With q = q_M ∝ B this condition gives B ∝ 1/(2W_sc + W_n), which is almost indistinguishable from 1/W_sc over 0.3–1.2 µm. It also predicts ≈ 50 mT at W_sc = 1 µm using the paper's q_M/B, a magnitude comparable to the data. The 1/W scaling is therefore not specific to an orbital-flux mechanism.

   Why it matters: the conclusion that the "contact width dependence of the field of maximum nonreciprocity invites an orbital interpretation" is one of the paper's two main interpretive results. What to do:
   - Fit Eq. (3) as written (zero intercept), or justify the offset physically, and quote the uncertainty on Φ/Φ₀.
   - Plot the sign-change field against W_sc⁻¹ alongside B_η and discuss the result.
   - Compare the data quantitatively with the finite-q resonance scaling (using 2W_sc + W_n), and with the orbital single-mode estimate, and state which of these alternatives the data exclude.
   - If they cannot be distinguished, rephrase the conclusion accordingly.

B. Should address

3. The extracted coherence length and its "good agreement" with the dirty-limit value depend on choices the paper does not justify.
   - (i) Sec. III states that the reflection probability is proportional to the amplitude ψ ∝ exp(−W_sc/ξ). A probability would normally go as |ψ|² ∝ exp(−2W_sc/ξ), which changes the fitted ξ by a factor of two (≈ 1.17 µm). Either choice is heuristic, but the quoted agreement with 570 nm depends on it.
   - (ii) Different conventions for the dirty-limit length (√(ξ_BCS ℓ) against √(ħD/Δ) with D = v_Fℓ/2) differ by ≈ 25%.
   - (iii) The estimate uses the bulk Al gap (1.764k_BT_c ≈ 250 µeV) rather than the induced gap. It also uses ℓ and n from magnetotransport of the bare 2DEG, whereas the Andreev bound state decays in the 2DEG under the Al.
   - (iv) Eq. (1) assumes τ → 1, yet Eq. (2) and Fig. 3 treat τ as finite and gate-tunable. For τ < 1, ξ_ABS > ξ.
   - (v) I_c^∞ is fixed from the "W_sc → ∞" device rather than fitted, and no uncertainty on ξ is given.

   The authors should fit with I_c^∞ free, quote confidence intervals, and justify the exponent. They should present the 586 versus 570 nm comparison as order-of-magnitude consistency rather than as a quantitative match.

4. The spin–orbit attribution of the gate dependence of B_* (Figs. 2(c), 3) rests on assumed, not measured, gate dependences of α and τ. Fig. 3 attaches arrows "α ↑" and "τ ↓" to increasing V_g. However, increasing V_g on a near-surface InAs well normally raises the carrier density, and with it often I_c and the transparency. This would push B_* the opposite way in Eq. (2). k_F also enters Eq. (2) and changes with V_g. The paper gives no I_c(V_g), R_n(V_g), density, α(V_g) (for example from weak antilocalization on a Hall bar from the same chip) or τ(V_g) (for example from I_cR_n or MAR). The "V_g < 0 V" values also differ between devices (Fig. 3 shows roughly −5 to −2 V), so the red points in Fig. 2(c) are compared at mismatched gate voltages and probably mismatched densities. Several B_* values are negative (≈ −5 mT for W_sc = 1.2 µm at negative V_g; ≈ −3 mT at V_g < 0 in Fig. 2(c)). This is a sign reversal of the I_c-extremum shift, and the text does not comment on it. The authors should provide independent gate calibrations, compare devices at matched density, and discuss the sign of B_*.

5. B_* values of a few mT are close to the scale of magnet remanence and hysteresis, and the extraction method is not specified. B_* is obtained "from low-field fits to Eq. (1) of Ref. [8]", but the fit function, the fit window, which parameters are free, and the origin of the error bars in Figs. 2(c), 3 and 4(c) are not given. A superconducting vector magnet can trap a remanent field of several mT after excursions to ±1 T, and trapped flux in the Al leads can offset the effective zero. The authors should show that B_* does not depend on the direction of the field sweep, or calibrate the field zero, for example from the symmetry point of the Fraunhofer pattern or from a reference junction. They should also describe the fit explicitly.

6. The device geometry beyond the contact, and the identity of the "W_sc → ∞" device, are unclear. They matter for the reflection model of Eq. (1) and for the B_η fits.
   - In Fig. 1(a), the regions outside the blue Al strips are labelled "semiconductor (green)". This suggests that the rear interface of each Al strip borders bare 2DEG rather than an etched edge. If so, the rear interface is an S/N interface with Andreev rather than normal reflection, and possibly series resistance and quasiparticle relaxation.
   - The text should state what lies beyond the Al, how current is injected, and where the voltage probes sit.
   - The "W_sc → ∞" device is plotted at W_sc⁻¹ = 0 and used to fix I_c^∞. It is then excluded from the B_η fit because of "length-limiting effects". Its actual Al extent and geometry, including gate coverage, should be given. It should then be plotted at its real W_sc⁻¹. Plotted there, it may fall well off the Fig. 4(c) line.
   - The claim that B_η "saturates for W_sc ≳ 2ξ" rests essentially on this single point. The W_sc = 1.2 µm point (W_sc⁻¹ ≈ 0.83 µm⁻¹) lies close to the fitted line.

7. No temperature dependence is shown, and the measurement temperature and the method of extracting I_c are not stated in the main text. The candidate mechanisms predict different temperature dependences of B_*, B_η and max|η|. Meissner and orbital finite-q effects scale with Δ(T) through q₀ ∝ Δ/v_F, and the Rashba–Zeeman scale has its own temperature behaviour. Even a coarse T-dependence of B_η for two widths would help discriminate the mechanisms. The authors should also give the base temperature, the switching-current statistics (number of sweeps, how I_c is defined) and the uncertainty bands in Fig. 4(a).

8. The trend in diode efficiency against width (Fig. 4(b)) has no model and no error bars. Apart from the "∞" device (16.5%), the values span only ≈ 5–10% and are not monotonic. The text concedes that the dependence "does not follow a clear exponential trend". The authors should add error bars and state whether max|η| simply tracks I_c, ξ or W_sc. They should also say whether any of the discussed mechanisms predicts the observed magnitude, for example using the single-mode q₀ result of Eq. (5) together with the multimode considerations of Ref. [19].

C. Minor

9. The main text relies on a supplement (Sections S.I–S.IV) for the Al thickness, the SEM confirmation of W_sc, the field-alignment procedure and the field traces at other gate voltages. This supplement was not part of the submission reviewed here. The key items should be summarized in the main text, or the supplement included.

10. The caption of Fig. 2(c) and the text refer to "V_g < 0 V" without values. List the actual negative gate voltage for each device (they differ, per Fig. 3).

11. The legend of Fig. 4(c), "Φ/Φ₀ = 0.036 nm⁻¹ × d", is confusing. State the fitted slope in mT·µm with its uncertainty, and give the derived Φ/Φ₀ for a specified d separately.

12. The assumption λ_L → d_sc/2 for the Meissner estimate, the choice |g*| = 10, and n = 6×10¹² cm⁻² (a high density for a near-surface InAs well) each need a source or justification. B^max_RZ and the ordering q_orb > q_M > q_RZ depend on them.

13. Sec. IV says the diode-efficiency extrema "are qualitatively consistent with multiple SDE mechanisms". It would help to state explicitly which observations, if any, exclude a given mechanism. At present every observation is described as consistent with something.

14. State whether the I_c+ maximum and the I_c− minimum on the Fraunhofer central lobe occur at the same out-of-plane field. A relative shift is itself a diode signature, and reporting it would show that the procedure in Sec. II fully separates out-of-plane contributions.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add R_n and I_cR_n for every device, and if possible a second device at one or two widths. This turns the width trends from suggestive into convincing.
- Plot the sign-change field and B_η together against W_sc⁻¹. Overlay the predictions of the orbital-flux, finite-q resonance and Rashba–Zeeman pictures, using the paper's own parameters, to see which (if any) the data select.
- Pair the gate sweeps with independent measurements of density and spin–orbit coupling (Hall and weak antilocalization on a co-fabricated Hall bar), and with I_cR_n against V_g, so that the α and τ arrows in Fig. 3 are measured rather than assumed.
- A short temperature series of η(B_∥) for the narrowest and widest contacts would give a mechanism-discriminating test at little extra cost.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
