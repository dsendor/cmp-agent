Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors measure five symmetric planar Al/InAs Josephson junctions, co-fabricated on one chip, that differ only in the longitudinal width W_sc of the superconducting contacts (0.3, 0.6, 0.9 and 1.2 µm, plus a nominally infinite contact). They track the critical current, the superconducting diode efficiency η, and two characteristic in-plane fields as functions of W_sc and gate voltage. Using contact width as a control knob to separate diode-effect mechanisms is a useful idea, and the observation that two characteristic fields depend differently on geometry and gate is interesting. In its current form, though, the work is mostly descriptive. Each geometry is a single device. None of the candidate mechanisms is matched quantitatively: the paper's own estimates of the field of maximum nonreciprocity (4 mT, 13 mT, 690 mT) all miss the observed 90 to 270 mT. The conclusions about mechanisms stay qualitative.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The device design is clean and systematic. All junctions are co-fabricated within 1.6 mm × 1.6 mm with the same 80 nm separation and 4 µm width, and only W_sc is varied. This isolates a geometric parameter that most planar-JJ diode experiments leave fixed.
- Ic grows smoothly with W_sc (Fig. 2a,b). It fits the simple form Ic∞(1 − e^{−W_sc/ξ}) with ξ = 586 nm, close to the dirty-limit estimate of about 570 nm built from independently measured n, ℓ and Tc.
- The gate dependence of B* (Fig. 3) is qualitatively the same in all five devices: B* increases monotonically with V_g. This is a reproducible trend across devices.
- The two characteristic fields behave in contrasting ways. B* is strongly gate-tunable and only weakly geometry-dependent; B_η depends strongly on geometry and weakly on gate (Fig. 4c). This contrast is a useful phenomenological result for theory.
- Taking Ic± from the extrema of a narrow out-of-plane Fraunhofer scan at each in-plane field is a sensible way to suppress artifacts from field misalignment.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. One device per geometry, and no normal-state normalisation, so the W_sc trends cannot be separated from device-to-device spread (Figs. 2a, 2b, 4b; abstract). Both headline trends, "increase in both critical current and diode efficiency with increasing contact width", rest on a single junction at each W_sc. Nothing in the main text shows how large the spread between nominally identical junctions is.
   - The η data already suggest the spread is comparable to the effect. max|η| is non-monotonic (about 10% at 0.9 µm versus about 8.5% at 1.2 µm). The ∞ device has about twice the efficiency of the 1.2 µm device even though its Ic is only slightly larger.
   - The Ic trend has the same problem. Ic depends on transparency, number of modes and the actual contact separation, and none of these is reported per device. The normal-state resistance R_n, the IcR_n product, the excess current, or the induced gap from multiple Andreev reflection are not given for any device. Without them, a smaller Ic in the narrow-contact devices could reflect a larger R_n, for example from a slightly wider gap after the selective Al wet etch, rather than reflection of Andreev bound states at the rear interface.
   - W_sc itself was checked only on "nominally identical proxy devices" (Sec. II). No uncertainty in W_sc or in the 80 nm separation is given, and wet-etch undercut matters most for the 0.3 µm device that anchors the fit.

   The authors should:
   - report R_n (and ideally IcR_n and the excess current) for every device and gate voltage, and show that IcR_n follows the same exponential law;
   - give the measured or estimated uncertainty of W_sc and of the junction length for each device;
   - add at least one repeat device for one or more widths, or data from a second chip or cooldown, to show the size of the device-to-device scatter in Ic, max|η| and B_η.

   Without this, the W_sc dependences, and the coherence-length interpretation built on them, are not established.

B. Should address

2. The linear fit of B_η against 1/W_sc (Fig. 4c) is not the Eq. (3) model, and the scaling does not single out an orbital mechanism.
   - Eq. (3) at fixed flux Φ predicts B_η = Φ/(W_sc d), a line through the origin. The fitted line in Fig. 4c has an intercept of about 25 mT, which can be read off where it meets W_sc^{-1} = 0. A line through the origin with the same slope would put the 1.2 µm point near 60 mT instead of the observed ~95 mT. The text does not say whether the intercept was free or what it means physically.
   - The fit uses four points. The 1.2 µm point (W_sc^{-1} ≈ 0.83 µm^{-1}) lies right at the dashed (2ξ)^{-1} line where the text says saturation sets in, yet it is included in the linear fit.
   - The stated saturation "for W_sc ≳ 2ξ" rests on a single device, the ∞ one.
   - The finite-momentum resonance condition the authors cite themselves, 2q(2W_sc + W_n) = (2n+1)π/2, also gives B ∝ 1/(2W_sc + W_n). For W_sc ≫ W_n = 80 nm this is close to 1/W_sc. So the 1/W_sc scaling does not discriminate orbital from spin-orbit or Meissner finite-q mechanisms.
   - The sign changes of η occur at 400 to 600 mT for all devices, roughly independent of W_sc. An orbital mechanism tied to Φ = B W_sc d would make them scale with 1/W_sc. With d ≈ 13 nm, the 0.6 µm device reaches Φ ≈ 2Φ0 at 500 mT.

   The authors should:
   - state the fit model and the free parameters, and fit with and without an intercept;
   - compare the data with the 1/(2W_sc + W_n) resonance form;
   - plot the zero-crossing fields against 1/W_sc;
   - temper the wording on saturation and on orbital effects. Devices with W_sc of 2 to 5 µm would test the saturation directly.

3. The gate dependence of B* is attributed to spin-orbit coupling only qualitatively (Sec. III, Eq. (2), Fig. 3).
   - Eq. (2) involves k_so, k_F, τ, E_T and g*, and a gate changes all of them.
   - Raising V_g raises the density. That increases k_F and v_F (and so E_T) and usually also raises the transparency. These changes push B* in different directions, and the text picks the direction (larger α, smaller τ) that matches the data.
   - The paper gives no V_g-to-density conversion and no α(V_g) or τ(V_g) for these samples. Ref. [45] is on a similar heterostructure, not these devices.
   - The devices are compared at different negative gate voltages (about −2.5 to −4.5 V in Fig. 3), which do not necessarily correspond to equal densities.

   The authors should:
   - give n(V_g) from Hall-bar data or the gate capacitance;
   - estimate τ(V_g) from the excess current or IcR_n;
   - use α(V_g) from Ref. [45] or weak antilocalisation, and test whether Eq. (2) reproduces the measured increase of B* with these inputs at least in order of magnitude;
   - plot B* against density rather than V_g.

4. Negative B* values are not discussed (Figs. 2c and 3). At the most negative gate voltages, the 1.2 µm and 0.9 µm devices show B* < 0 (about −5 mT and −1.5 mT in Fig. 3), and Fig. 2c shows B* ≈ −3 mT. In Eq. (2) the sign of B* follows the sign of the spin-orbit term. A sign reversal would therefore imply a change of sign of the effective α, or a competing contribution such as the Meissner term estimated at about 13 mT. Either would matter for the interpretation. The authors should say whether these values differ significantly from zero, given how the error bars were obtained, and discuss what a sign change would mean.

5. The relation between the two characteristic fields is not addressed. B* (0 to 40 mT) and B_η (90 to 270 mT) differ by an order of magnitude, and they depend on W_sc and V_g in opposite ways. Both are presented as signatures of finite-momentum pairing, yet the paper never discusses why a single q(B) should give extrema at such different fields. The proposed orbital interpretation of B_η also has a Meissner-like or orbital term that would shift Ic(B) and so contribute to B*.
   - The authors should explain what each quantity measures in the model of Ref. [8] and whether a combined model (spin-orbit plus orbital finite q) can produce both scales.
   - Since none of the order-of-magnitude estimates in Sec. IV matches the 90 to 270 mT range, they should state plainly that the mechanism of B_η remains undetermined.

6. The coherence-length fit should give its uncertainty and its sensitivity to Ic∞ (Fig. 2b).
   - Ic∞ is taken from the single ∞ device, which therefore sits at the origin of Fig. 2b by construction.
   - The quantity 1/ln[Ic∞/(Ic∞ − Ic)] is very sensitive to Ic∞ for the wide-contact devices, where Ic/Ic∞ ≈ 0.8 to 0.9. A few-percent error in Ic∞ shifts the 1.2 µm and 0.9 µm points substantially.
   - Fig. 2b has no error bars, and ξ = 586 nm is quoted without an uncertainty.
   - The 3% agreement with the dirty-limit estimate is partly fortuitous. The prefactor of the dirty-limit expression is of order unity and depends on convention: sqrt(ξ_BCS ℓ), as written, versus sqrt(ħD/Δ) with D = v_F ℓ/2 in 2D.
   - The estimate also uses ℓ from magnetotransport on bare InAs and the Al gap for Δ, rather than the induced gap and the mean free path under the Al.

   The authors should:
   - fit with Ic∞ as a free parameter (with the ∞ device as a constraint);
   - report the confidence interval of ξ;
   - state the uncertainty of the dirty-limit estimate, including the choice of induced gap versus Al gap.

7. Error bars are missing or unexplained.
   - Fig. 4b (max|η|) and Fig. 4c (B_η) have no error bars.
   - Fig. 3 has error bars, but the main text does not say how they were obtained. "Low-field fits to Eq. (1) of Ref. [8]" leaves the fit window and free parameters unstated.
   - The shaded bands in Fig. 4a are not defined in the caption.
   - Near the η extrema and sign changes Ic± are small, so the noise on η grows. A stated uncertainty is needed before the positions of the sign changes or the non-monotonic max|η| are interpreted.

   The authors should define the uncertainty for every extracted quantity and propagate it to ξ and Φ/Φ0.

8. Device geometry beyond the contacts and the measurement configuration are unclear (Fig. 1a, Sec. II). The colouring in Fig. 1a leaves open what lies on the far side of each W_sc-wide Al strip: bare 2DEG, or Al pads. That boundary sets the "rear interface" reflection on which Eq. (1) rests.
   - If it is normal 2DEG, the leads are N–S interfaces with their own proximity and inverse-proximity effects, and lead resistance appears in series.
   - The paper also does not say whether Ic is a switching current from a four-terminal measurement.
   - It does not say what physically realises the "W_sc → ∞" device, or whether its gate overlap matches the other devices.

   A schematic of the full device layout and wiring for each device type is needed to interpret Eq. (1) and to compare the ∞ device with the rest.

9. Measurement temperature and its effect are not stated in the main text. ξ and the diode efficiency both depend on temperature. The fridge temperature, any check of electron heating at the switching point, and whether any of the extracted quantities were followed in temperature should be stated. A temperature dependence of ξ, fitted from Ic(W_sc) at two or three temperatures, would strongly support the coherence-length interpretation, because ξ should scale roughly as 1/sqrt(Δ(T)).

C. Minor

10. Figs. 2c and 3 present the same data but do not agree everywhere when read off the plots.
    - At V_g = 10 V, the 0.9 µm device is about 17 mT in Fig. 2c but about 20 mT in Fig. 3.
    - The V_g < 0 point of the 1.2 µm device is about −3 mT in Fig. 2c but about −5 mT in Fig. 3.
    - The "V_g < 0" series in Fig. 2c mixes different gate voltages across devices.
    - The V_g = 10 V series has no 0.3 µm point; the reason should be stated.

    Please check the figures and state the V_g used for each point.

11. Eq. (2) is derived for W_sc → ∞, and the text says B* is not compared across W_sc for that reason. Yet Fig. 2c plots exactly that comparison and the text calls the dependence "nontrivial". Either discuss the W_sc dependence of B*, including its non-monotonic upturn at 0.3 µm, or justify showing it.

12. The Meissner estimate replaces λ_L by d_sc/2 for a 10 nm film. The authors should justify this replacement, cite it, and say how q_M changes if the thin-film effective penetration depth is used instead.

13. The fitted d = 10 to 17 nm for Φ/Φ0 = 0.36 to 0.61 is circular: d is assumed and Φ is then inferred. State which quantity is constrained independently. If d is taken from the layer stack (Fig. 1b), give the resulting Φ/Φ0 with its uncertainty and compare it with the theoretical flux at maximum η for the orbital mechanism of Ref. [15].

14. |g*| = 10 for the Rashba–Zeeman estimate and m* = 0.024 m_e should be sourced or measured for this heterostructure.

15. Fig. 1b: state whether the arrows on the Fermi-surface sketch and the labels q ± k follow a consistent sign convention with Eq. (5) and the definition of q. Also, the inset label "Φ" sits inside the dashed box, which suggests flux through the Al region rather than the region between Al and 2DEG described in Eq. (3).

16. Several references are given only as DOIs or arXiv numbers with "type: article" artefacts (e.g. Refs. [1], [7], [9], [13]). Please clean these up.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a table of the device parameters: W_sc (measured), R_n, IcR_n, excess current and induced gap from MAR, density at V_g = 0. Use it to normalise Figs. 2 and 4 so that geometry is separated from device variation.
- Fill the gap between 1.2 µm and ∞ with devices at W_sc ≈ 2 to 5 µm. This would test both the exponential saturation of Ic and the claimed saturation of B_η at W_sc ≈ 2ξ.
- Give Ic(W_sc) at several temperatures to show that ξ(T) follows the expected dependence on the induced gap. This would turn the coherence-length interpretation from a fit into a test.
- Plot B* and B_η against carrier density, and compare them quantitatively with Eq. (2) and with a model that includes both orbital and spin-orbit finite-q terms, for example the multimode treatment of Ref. [19]. This would turn the mechanism discussion from qualitative consistency into a test.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
