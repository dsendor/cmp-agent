A. Must address

1. The W_sc trends in η and B_η rest on one device per width, and the end point is a structurally different, differently processed device. Each W_sc value is represented by a single junction, and the paper reports no device-to-device spread at fixed W_sc. The abstract nonetheless says that "the diode efficiency increase[s] with contact width". Across the four finite-width devices max η is non-monotonic (Fig. 5b: ≈4.7–5% at 0.3 µm, ≈5.5% at 0.6 µm, ≈10% at 0.9 µm, ≈8.5% at 1.2 µm), and the text itself says the behaviour "does not follow a clear exponential trend". The total range is comparable to what fabrication variation in the 80 nm gap, the Al etch edge or the local gate coupling could produce. The increase is therefore carried mostly by the W_sc → ∞ device (≈16.5%). That device differs in more than W_sc: its leads are unetched, its nearest SC–insulator interface is tens of microns away, the current-feed geometry, in-plane field penetration of a wide Al film and possible vortex entry into the large Al area all differ, and it is the only device whose data were smoothed before extraction (Results; supplementary section on data smoothing), which could change the extracted peak η, for example through the kernel's effect on a sharp peak near ±0.1 T. The same device also fixes I_c^∞ in the linearised fit of Fig. 3b, where it sits at the origin by construction and sets the ordinate of every other point; because 1/ln[I_c^∞/(I_c^∞ − I_c)] is very sensitive to I_c^∞ when I_c is close to it (the 1.2 µm device), the fitted ξ depends directly on whether this device is otherwise equivalent to the others (gate coverage, current injection, junction gap). The authors should: (a) state whether additional junctions of any width were measured, and show a replicate for at least two W_sc values, or the same devices after a thermal cycle (Methods says the chip was cooled several times), to give a device-to-device or cooldown-to-cooldown spread for I_c, max η and B_η; (b) show that the ∞ device's max η and B_η do not change when the smoothing is removed or varied; (c) give the uncertainty of ξ when I_c^∞ is a free fit parameter (two parameters, four finite-W_sc points) rather than fixed to the ∞ device; (d) failing (a)–(b), reword the abstract and conclusion so the η(W_sc) statement is a tentative observation ("tends to increase", or what the finite-width devices support), not a trend. A finite-W_sc device large compared with ξ (for example 3–5 µm) with etched leads would be a better saturation reference.

B. Should address

2. The material parameters behind the coherence-length comparison are inconsistent, so the stated agreement is not established. On p. 2–3 the dirty-limit estimate ξ = sqrt(ξ_BCS ℓ) ≈ 570 nm uses n = 6 × 10¹² cm⁻² and ℓ = 130 nm "from magnetotransport", giving v_F ≈ 3 × 10⁶ m/s and ξ_BCS ≈ 2.5 µm. The Methods report, from a van der Pauw sample on a different piece of the same wafer, n = 8.14 × 10¹¹ cm⁻², µ = 9.86 × 10³ cm²/Vs and ℓ_e = 147 nm. The densities differ by a factor of about 7; with the Methods density, v_F ≈ 1.1 × 10⁶ m/s, ξ_BCS ≈ 0.9 µm and ξ ≈ 0.35–0.37 µm, about 40% below the fitted 586 nm. A density of 6 × 10¹² cm⁻² is unusually high for a 4 nm InAs well, and v_F ≈ 3 × 10⁶ m/s is unusually high for InAs, where band nonparabolicity would lower it. The estimate also uses the bulk Al gap (Δ = 1.764 k_B T_c ≈ 250 µeV) with InAs v_F and ℓ, without saying which medium's coherence length governs the decay; the induced gap in the 2DEG under the Al is usually smaller, which raises ξ. The fitted ξ has no uncertainty quoted. The authors should state where each set of transport parameters comes from (Al-covered region, Al-removed Hall bar on this chip, different wafer piece, gate voltage), say which applies to the 2DEG under the Al and justify it, quote an uncertainty on the fitted ξ, and give the dirty-limit estimate with an uncertainty range reflecting these choices; otherwise the agreement should be presented as order-of-magnitude consistency.

3. The exponent in Eq. (2) is ambiguous, which makes the ξ agreement non-diagnostic (p. 2). The text states that the "probability of reflection is proportional to the amplitude ψ ∝ exp(−W_sc/ξ)". A probability would normally scale as |ψ|² ∝ exp(−2W_sc/ξ); with that form the fitted decay length in Fig. 3b becomes ≈1.17 µm rather than 586 nm, and the agreement with the ≈570 nm estimate disappears. The authors should justify the exponent, for example from a quasiclassical or tight-binding calculation of I_c versus contact length for this geometry, or present the agreement as order-of-magnitude only.

4. Alternative explanations for the suppression of I_c at small W_sc are not excluded (Fig. 3a,b, Eq. 2). Eq. (2) is phenomenological, and narrower contacts could lower I_c through: (i) the kinetic inductance of the narrow Al strips — current enters each 4 µm-long contact from the T stem and runs along a strip of width W_sc and thickness 10 nm, about 13 squares of thin Al at W_sc = 0.3 µm, so with I_c of a few µA the phase drop along the strip could reach a sizable fraction of 2π, making the supercurrent distribution non-uniform with a W_sc dependence that need not be exponential (and possibly feeding self-field nonreciprocity); (ii) wet-etch damage near the Al edge, which matters most for the narrowest contacts; (iii) lower interface transparency or a different junction length from device to device, both of which vary with wet etching; (iv) a reduced induced gap under narrow Al strips (inverse proximity effect). The authors should tabulate R_n, I_c R_n and the excess current for every device (and versus V_g), and plot I_c R_n against W_sc alongside I_c — if R_n is independent of W_sc and I_c R_n shows the same saturation, the coherence-length reading is much stronger. They should estimate the strip kinetic inductance and phase drop at I_c, and show whether the Fraunhofer patterns stay regular for the narrowest W_sc (Fig. 1c is for one representative device). A further discriminator is to fit Eq. (2) at each V_g using the supplementary traces and report ξ(V_g), testing whether the decay length tracks the 2DEG properties as the Andreev picture predicts.

5. The fit in Fig. 5c is not the proportionality Eq. (4) implies, and its parameters and uncertainties are not stated (p. 4, Fig. 5c). Eq. (4), B_∥ W_sc d = Φ, predicts B_η ∝ 1/W_sc through the origin, but the solid line has a sizable positive intercept (≈25–30 mT at 1/W_sc = 0), and the finite-width points are not proportional to 1/W_sc (B_η·W_sc ≈ 115 mT·µm at 1.2 µm but ≈80 mT·µm at 0.3 µm). The slope (and hence Φ/Φ₀ ≈ 0.36–0.61 for an assumed d = 10–17 nm) comes from a line through four points, one per device; the 0.3 µm point dominates the leverage, yet its η is the smallest and broadest of the set (Fig. 5a), so its extremum is the least well defined. Fig. 5c shows no uncertainties on B_η. The claimed saturation for W_sc ≳ 2ξ ≈ 1.15 µm rests essentially on the 1.2 µm point and the structurally different ∞ device (concern 1). The authors should state the fit function and its free parameters, say whether a zero-intercept fit is acceptable, explain physically what any intercept represents (for example a W_sc-independent contribution from W_n = 80 nm or the junction region), give uncertainties on B_η and on the slope, and rephrase the saturation as a deviation of the ∞ device from the linear trend, or check it quantitatively by replacing W_sc with an effective width ≈ min(W_sc, 2ξ) in Eq. (4).

6. The orbital interpretation of B_η is in tension with the authors' own estimates and with missing signatures (pp. 4–5, abstract, Conclusion). The Conclusion says the W_sc dependence of B_η "invites an orbital interpretation", but: the orbital picture predicts a sign change at Φ = Φ₀ and Φ₀-periodic oscillations of η, and the sign change occurs at larger flux while the oscillations are not seen; none of the single-mode estimates of Eq. (6) (≈13 mT Meissner, ≈4 mT orbital tunnelling, ≈690 mT Rashba–Zeeman) matches the observed 90–270 mT; and the observed sign changes occur where the induced gap is strongly suppressed (I_c falls by more than a factor of 2 by 0.5 T, Fig. 3a). The authors should state the result as a consistency of the 1/W_sc scaling with a flux-through-contact length scale, list the failed predictions alongside it, and say in the abstract and conclusion that no model tested reproduces the measured field scale. Two tests are available from existing data: plot the 400–600 mT sign-change field against 1/W_sc on the same axes as B_η (a shared Φ/d slope would support a flux-set scale; no scaling would argue against it), and use the Fraunhofer period to estimate whether the effective flux-focusing area scales as W_sc·d. Better still, a multi-mode tight-binding or quasiclassical calculation with finite W_sc, the 80 nm junction, the actual layer thicknesses and the Meissner, orbital and Rashba–Zeeman terms would make the comparison quantitative.

7. The gate dependence of B_* is attributed to spin–orbit coupling without independent calibration, and parts of the B_* data are unexplained (pp. 3–4, Figs. 3c and 4, Eq. 3). Eq. (3), B_* ∝ (1 − τ)^{1/4}(k_so/k_F)E_T/g*, depends on τ, k_so, k_F and E_T, all of which change with V_g. The account requires α to increase and τ to decrease with V_g; the latter is counterintuitive, since higher density usually raises transparency, and neither is measured. No n(V_g) calibration, no α(V_g) (for example weak antilocalisation on a gated Hall bar from the same wafer, as in ref. 46) and no per-device transparency or Thouless energy (excess current, multiple Andreev reflection, I_c R_n versus V_g) is shown, so the trend cannot discriminate α from τ or k_F effects. Several B_* values are negative (Fig. 4: ≈ −6 mT for 1.2 µm and negative for 0.9 µm at V_g ≈ −3 V, and the V_g < 0 series in Fig. 3c), which Eq. (3) cannot produce and the text does not discuss. Eq. (3) was derived for W_sc → ∞, so the strong, non-monotonic W_sc dependence of B_* at V_g = 0 (Fig. 3c) is left uninterpreted. The "V_g < 0" points in Figs. 3c and 4 are taken at different gate voltages (about −4 V to −3 V) for different devices, so devices are compared at mismatched conditions. Finally, spin–orbit coupling is invoked both for the strong gate dependence of B_* and for the weak gate dependence of B_η ("consistent with a spin–orbit coupling based mechanism"); the authors should state which observation would have argued against a spin–orbit origin. Requested: n(V_g) and α(V_g) from a gated reference structure, I_c R_n and R_n versus V_g per device, B_* plotted against density or α, the exact V_g of each point with comparison at matched density or normal-state conductance, an explanation of the sign convention and negative B_*, and a comment on the W_sc dependence of B_*.

8. The values of W_sc carry no measured uncertainty. W_sc was confirmed by SEM on proxy devices, not on the measured junctions, and both W_sc and the 80 nm gap are defined by wet etching of Al (Transene D), which undercuts laterally by up to tens of nm — a large fraction of 0.3 µm. This moves the rightmost points in Figs. 3b, 3c and 5c. The authors should give the measured spread of W_sc (and W_n) on the proxy devices, add horizontal error bars to the plots against 1/W_sc, and check that the fitted ξ and Φ/Φ₀ are robust to that spread.

9. The uncertainties on I_c±, η, B_* and B_η, and the zero of B_∥, are not fully described. Methods says I_c± comes from a local fit to the Fraunhofer extremum with uncertainty from the RMS residual, but does not state how many I–V sweeps set each switching current or the width of the switching distribution compared with |I_c+| − |I_c−| (for a 2 µA device, η = 5% is ≈100 nA). B_* is only 0–40 mT, and a few mT at negative gate, comparable to possible remanent fields in the 6 T vector magnet. B_* is defined as argmax I_c+ = −argmin I_c−; averaging the two branches cancels a common B_∥ offset, but a single branch does not. The authors should report the switching-histogram width or repeatability of I_c± at fixed field; state whether B_* and B_η are averages of the two branches; say how the B_∥ zero was calibrated (remanence, sweep direction, symmetry of I_c±(B_∥)); state whether all five devices were measured in the same cooldown and whether any was remeasured after a thermal cycle; and specify the field window and fitted parameters (τ, E_T fixed or free) of the low-field fits to Eq. (1) of ref. 8 that yield B_* and its error bars. Then the sign of B_* and the error bars in Figs. 3c, 4 and 5c have a stated basis.

10. The extraction protocol removes part of the nonreciprocity some theories predict (Eq. 1, p. 2). Taking the global extremum of each branch over B_⊥ discards any diode contribution that appears as a relative shift of the I_c+ and I_c− interference patterns; some in-plane-field mechanisms (Meissner-current and orbital mechanisms with a φ₀ shift) generate exactly such shifts, and the B_⊥+ ≠ B_⊥− offsets noted on p. 2 are discarded. The authors should report ΔB_⊥ = B_⊥+ − B_⊥− versus B_∥ for each W_sc, which also tests whether the self-field explanation of the offset is consistent with its W_sc scaling, and note in the comparison with Eq. (6) that the measured η is a pattern-maximum quantity, not a single-phase-bias one.
