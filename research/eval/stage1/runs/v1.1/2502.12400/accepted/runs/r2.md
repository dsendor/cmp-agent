Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper varies one geometric parameter, the longitudinal width W_sc of the superconducting contacts, across five planar Al/InAs Josephson junctions co-fabricated on one chip. It reports that I_c rises toward saturation with W_sc on a length scale (ξ ≈ 586 nm) close to the dirty-limit coherence length. It also reports that the in-plane field B_η of maximum diode efficiency scales roughly as 1/W_sc and saturates for W_sc ≳ 2ξ, while the field B_* of maximum critical current is strongly gate-tunable. A controlled geometric study like this is useful, because diode-effect experiments in planar junctions are hard to interpret mechanistically, and a scaling with a device dimension is a cleaner discriminant than a single-device field sweep. The novelty is mainly experimental and phenomenological. The mechanism remains open, as the authors acknowledge.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clean, systematic design: five devices with the same junction length (80 nm), width (4 µm), heterostructure and gate stack, differing only in W_sc, all co-fabricated on one chip.
- A careful protocol against out-of-plane-field artifacts: each branch is extremized over a narrow B_⊥ window around the central Fraunhofer lobe at every (B_∥, V_g) point (Eq. 1, Fig. 1d–f). The θ = 0 null control (Fig. 1h) shows η ≈ 0 when B_∥ is parallel to the current, and η(B_∥ = 0) ≈ 0 for all devices.
- A large, monotonic I_c(W_sc) trend (Fig. 3a,b), with a simple one-parameter description whose ξ agrees with an independent estimate.
- An appropriately hedged discussion. The order-of-magnitude estimates for the Meissner, orbital and Rashba–Zeeman finite-q mechanisms are given explicitly, and the authors state that none of them matches the observed B_η.
- Raw data and analysis scripts are deposited on Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The central observations, the I_c(W_sc) saturation, the W_sc-dependence of B_η and the gate dependence of B_*, are supported well enough by the data shown for a phenomenological paper, provided the interpretation is kept at the level of the B items below.

B. Should address

1. Only one device per contact width, and the W_sc → ∞ reference device is structurally different (pp. 2, 4; Figs. 3, 5). Each W_sc value is represented by a single junction, and the paper gives no estimate of device-to-device spread at fixed W_sc.
   - This matters most for the abstract's claim that "the diode efficiency increase[s] with contact width." Across the four finite-width devices, max η is non-monotonic (Fig. 5b: ≈8.5% at 1.2 µm, ≈10% at 0.9 µm, ≈5.5% at 0.6 µm, ≈4.7% at 0.3 µm), and the text itself says the trend "does not follow a clear exponential trend." The clearest increase comes from the W_sc → ∞ device (≈16.5%).
   - That device has unetched leads, its nearest SC–insulator interface is tens of microns away, and its data were smoothed differently ("see supplementary section on data smoothing"). It therefore differs in more than W_sc: the current feed geometry, the in-plane field penetration of a wide Al film, and the possible vortex entry into the large Al area are all different.
   - The same device also fixes I_c^∞ in the linearized fit of Fig. 3b, where it sits at the origin by construction. Its I_c therefore sets the ordinate of every other point. Because 1/ln[I_c^∞/(I_c^∞ − I_c)] is very sensitive to I_c^∞ when I_c is close to it (the 1.2 µm device), the fitted ξ depends strongly on this one device.
   - The authors should do the following:
     (a) state whether additional junctions of any width were measured, and show them if so;
     (b) give the uncertainty of ξ when I_c^∞ is a free fit parameter rather than fixed to the W_sc → ∞ device;
     (c) narrow the abstract's wording on η to what the finite-width devices support, or show a repeat device.

2. Ambiguity in the decay model makes the ξ agreement non-diagnostic (Eq. 2, p. 2). The text states that the "probability of reflection is proportional to the amplitude ψ ∝ exp(−W_sc/ξ)." A probability would normally scale as |ψ|² ∝ exp(−2W_sc/ξ). With that form, the fitted decay length in Fig. 3b would become ≈1.17 µm instead of 586 nm, and the stated agreement with the dirty-limit estimate (≈570 nm) would disappear.
   - The authors should justify the exponent in Eq. (2), for example from a quasiclassical or tight-binding calculation of I_c versus contact length for this geometry, or present the agreement as order-of-magnitude only.
   - The 570 nm estimate itself uses the Al bulk gap (Δ = 1.764 k_B T_c ≈ 250 µeV) together with the InAs Fermi velocity and mean free path. It is not stated which medium's coherence length governs the decay: the proximitized InAs under the Al, with an induced gap that may be smaller than Δ, or the Al. The authors should specify this.

3. Inconsistent density and mean free path used for the ξ estimate (p. 2 vs Methods). The main text uses n = 6 × 10¹² cm⁻² and ℓ = 130 nm to obtain v_F ≈ 3 × 10⁶ m/s and ξ ≈ 570 nm. The Methods report n = 8.14 × 10¹¹ cm⁻² and l_e = 147 nm from a van der Pauw sample on a different piece of the wafer.
   - These densities differ by a factor of about 7. With the Methods density, v_F ≈ 1.1 × 10⁶ m/s, ξ_BCS ≈ 0.9 µm, and the dirty-limit ξ ≈ 0.35–0.37 µm, which no longer matches the fitted 586 nm.
   - If the higher density refers to the 2DEG under the Al (for example, from charge transfer or from the gated junction magnetotransport in the supplement), the text should say so, explain where each number comes from, and propagate the uncertainty into the ξ comparison.

4. Fig. 5c and Eq. (4): the plotted fit is not the proportionality Eq. (4) implies (p. 4, Fig. 5c).
   - Eq. (4), B_∥ W_sc d = Φ, predicts B_η ∝ 1/W_sc through the origin. However, the solid line in Fig. 5c has a sizable positive intercept (≈25–30 mT at 1/W_sc = 0). The finite-width points are also not proportional to 1/W_sc: B_η·W_sc ≈ 115 mT·µm at 1.2 µm but ≈ 80 mT·µm at 0.3 µm.
   - The slope (≈0.036 nm⁻¹ × d in Φ/Φ₀ units) is therefore extracted from a two-parameter line through four points, one per device. The 0.3 µm point dominates the leverage, and its η is the smallest and broadest of the set (Fig. 5a), so its extremum is the least well defined.
   - Fig. 5c also shows no uncertainties on B_η.
   - The authors should do the following:
     (a) state the fit function and its free parameters;
     (b) explain physically what the intercept represents (for example, a W_sc-independent contribution from W_n = 80 nm or from the junction region itself);
     (c) give uncertainties on B_η and on the slope;
     (d) add horizontal error bars for W_sc. The actual widths come from SEM of proxy devices, and the lateral undercut of the Transene D wet etch can be tens of nm, which matters most at 0.3 µm.

5. The orbital interpretation of B_η is in tension with the authors' own estimate (pp. 4–5, Conclusion). The Conclusion says the W_sc dependence of B_η "invites an orbital interpretation," yet the single-mode orbital estimate gives B_η ≈ 4 mT, against observed values of 90–270 mT.
   - The effective flux at the extremum, Φ/Φ₀ ≈ 0.36–0.61, is consistent with a phase-winding picture. However, the expected Φ₀-periodic sign changes and oscillations are not seen, and the observed sign changes occur at fields where the induced gap is strongly suppressed (I_c falls by more than a factor of 2 by 0.5 T in Fig. 3a).
   - The authors should either develop the orbital argument quantitatively, for example with a multi-mode model including W_sc, or soften the Conclusion to say that the 1/W_sc scaling is compatible with a flux-through-contact length scale without identifying the mechanism. A Fraunhofer-period estimate of the effective flux-focusing area could also test whether the relevant area scales as W_sc·d.

6. The gate dependence of B_* is attributed to spin–orbit coupling without independent calibration (pp. 3–4, Fig. 4, Eq. 3).
   - Eq. (3) contains (1−τ)^{1/4}, k_so, k_F and E_T, and all of these change with V_g. The paper's account requires α to increase and τ to decrease with increasing V_g. The second is counterintuitive, since higher density usually raises transparency, and it is not measured.
   - No gate-voltage-to-density calibration, no α(V_g) (for example from weak antilocalization on a gated Hall bar from the same wafer, as in ref. 46), and no per-device transparency (from excess current or multiple Andreev reflection) are shown. Without these, the B_* trend cannot discriminate α from τ or k_F effects.
   - Fig. 4 also shows B_* changing sign for V_g < 0 in several devices (for example, ≈ −6 mT for 1.2 µm at V_g ≈ −3 V). Eq. (3), as written, gives a positive B_*, and the paper does not discuss this sign change.
   - The paper also states that the weak gate dependence of B_η is "consistent with a spin–orbit coupling based mechanism." Together with item 6's B_* argument, spin–orbit coupling is then invoked both for a strong gate dependence and for a weak one. The authors should state which observation would have argued against a spin–orbit origin.
   - Requested: I_c R_n and R_n versus V_g for each device, a density or α calibration from a gated reference, and a discussion of the sign change.

7. Normal-state resistance and junction-length variation are not reported (Fig. 3a,b). I_c in an 80 nm InAs weak link is sensitive to the actual gap length and the interface transparency, both of which vary with wet etching.
   - Without R_n for each device, part of the I_c(W_sc) trend could come from device-to-device variation in the junction itself rather than from W_sc.
   - The authors should tabulate R_n, I_c R_n and the excess current for every device, and plot I_c R_n against W_sc alongside I_c. If I_c R_n shows the same exponential saturation, the coherence-length interpretation becomes much stronger.

8. The extraction protocol removes part of the nonreciprocity that some theories predict (Eq. 1, p. 2). Taking the global extremum of each branch over B_⊥ removes any diode contribution that appears as a relative shift of the I_c+ and I_c− interference patterns in B_⊥. That is a reasonable way to suppress artifacts. But some in-plane-field mechanisms, such as Meissner-current and orbital mechanisms with a φ₀ shift, generate exactly such shifts, and the B_⊥+ ≠ B_⊥− offsets mentioned on p. 2 are discarded.
   - The authors should report ΔB_⊥ = B_⊥+ − B_⊥− versus B_∥ for each W_sc. This also tests whether the self-field explanation offered for the offset is consistent with its W_sc scaling.
   - They should also note in the comparison with Eq. (6) that the measured η is a pattern-maximum quantity, not a single-phase-bias quantity.

C. Minor

9. Uncertainties are missing from Figs. 3b and 5c. The caption of Fig. 3 explains the error bars for panels b and c, but none are visible in Fig. 3b, and Fig. 5c has none at all. Please add them, or state that they are smaller than the symbols.

10. Thermal cycling and field history. The chip was cooled down several times. Please state whether all five devices were measured in the same cooldown, whether any device was remeasured after a thermal cycle, and how the zero of B_∥ was established for the vector magnet, including its remanence and sweep direction. B_* is only 0–40 mT, so a few-mT offset matters. Averaging the I_c+ and I_c− extrema, as in the definition of B_*, removes a common offset, but please confirm that this was done.

11. Gate voltages differ between devices. In Figs. 3c and 4, the "V_g < 0" points are taken at different gate voltages for different devices. Please give the exact V_g for each point, and if possible compare devices at matched density or matched normal-state conductance rather than matched V_g.

12. The fit range is not specified. Please state the field range used for the "low-field fits to Eq. (1) of ref. 8" that yield B_*, and whether the fit model assumes τ or E_T fixed.

13. Notation. The notation for the critical in-plane field ("2.7 T") and the use of both ℓ and l_e for the mean free path should be harmonized. In Fig. 1h, η is defined with I_c+ unsigned and I_c− in absolute value, whereas Eq. (5) uses |I_c+|. Please make these consistent.

14. The statement that B_η "saturates for W_sc ≳ 2ξ ≈ 1.15 µm" rests on a single point at 1/W_sc = 0, which comes from the structurally different device in item 1. Please rephrase it as a deviation of the W_sc → ∞ device from the linear trend.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a second chip, or at least repeat devices at two widths, to establish device-to-device spread for I_c, I_c R_n, max η and B_η.
2. Fabricate a finite-W_sc device large compared with ξ (for example, 3–5 µm) with etched leads, to replace the structurally different W_sc → ∞ device as the saturation reference.
3. Measure α(V_g) and n(V_g) on a gated Hall bar or a weak-antilocalization structure from the same wafer, and plot B_* against density and α rather than against V_g.
4. Compare the data quantitatively with a multi-mode tight-binding or quasiclassical calculation that includes finite W_sc, the Meissner, orbital and Rashba–Zeeman terms, and the actual layer thicknesses. This would turn the qualitative "multiple mechanisms coexist" conclusion into a testable decomposition.

5. Verdict

Verdict: minor revision
