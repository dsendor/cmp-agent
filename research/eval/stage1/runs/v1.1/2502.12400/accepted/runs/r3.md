Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper varies one geometric parameter, the longitudinal width W_sc of the superconducting contacts, across five co-fabricated T-shaped Al/InAs planar Josephson junctions. It tracks how the critical current, the diode efficiency η, and the two characteristic in-plane fields (B*, where I_c is largest, and B_η, where η is largest) depend on W_sc and on gate voltage. Few experiments vary this parameter systematically, and it can help separate orbital mechanisms of the superconducting diode effect from spin–orbit/Zeeman ones. The methodological step of taking the extremum over a narrow B⊥ sweep at every (B∥, V_g) point is sound and useful to others. The mechanistic conclusions are interpretive: none of the paper's quantitative estimates reproduces the measured B_η.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clean, systematic geometric study: all devices are on one chip with the same 80 nm junction length and 4 µm width, and only W_sc is varied, along with gate voltage.
- A careful protocol against B⊥-induced artefacts. The extrema of the positive and negative branches are taken separately over B⊥ (Eq. 1, Fig. 1d–f). Switching currents rather than retrapping currents are compared. The θ = 0 control in Fig. 1h shows η ≈ 0 when B∥ is parallel to the current.
- The I_c(W_sc) data in Fig. 3a,b vary by more than a factor of two and follow a simple one-parameter model. The extracted ξ ≈ 586 nm is consistent in order of magnitude with a dirty-limit estimate.
- Two field scales behave differently: B_η has a strong W_sc dependence and a weak gate dependence, while B* is the reverse. This is a useful empirical discriminator, and the authors present it without overclaiming.
- Raw data and analysis scripts are deposited on Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The W_sc trends in η and B_η rest on one device per geometry, and the end point is measured and processed differently. The abstract claims that "the diode efficiency increase[s] with contact width" and that B_η has "a linear trend ... with respect to inverse contact width ... which saturates". Each W_sc value is one device, and no device-to-device spread is reported. Without the W_sc → ∞ device, max η in Fig. 5b is 5% (0.3 µm), 5.5% (0.6 µm), 10% (0.9 µm) and 8.5% (1.2 µm): non-monotonic, with a total range comparable to what fabrication variation in the 80 nm gap, the Al etch edge or the local gate coupling could produce. The paper itself says the behaviour "does not follow a clear exponential trend". The "increase" is therefore carried mostly by the W_sc → ∞ point (16.5%). That device differs in kind: its leads are unetched, and its data were smoothed before extraction (Results; supplementary section on data smoothing). Both could change the extracted peak η, for example through the smoothing kernel's effect on a sharp peak near ±0.1 T. The B_η line in Fig. 5c is fixed by only four finite-W_sc points at V_g = 0, and "saturation" is inferred from the 1.2 µm and ∞ points together. I_c can tolerate this, because its trend is large and monotonic. η and B_η are the claims that bear on mechanism, and for them the authors should do one of the following:
   - (a) show a replicate device for at least two W_sc values, or the same devices after a thermal cycle (Methods says the chip was cooled several times), to give a device-to-device or cooldown-to-cooldown spread for max η and B_η;
   - (b) show that the ∞ device's max η and B_η do not change when the smoothing is removed or varied;
   - (c) failing (a)–(b), reword the abstract and conclusion so that the η(W_sc) statement is a tentative observation, not a trend.

B. Should address

2. The coherence-length "agreement" depends on parameter choices that the paper states inconsistently. The text takes n = 6 × 10¹² cm⁻² and ℓ = 130 nm "from magnetotransport". From these it gets v_F ≈ 3 × 10⁶ m/s, ξ_BCS ≈ 2.5 µm and ξ ≈ 570 nm, which it compares with the fitted 586 nm. Methods reports n = 8.14 × 10¹¹ cm⁻² and ℓ_e = 147 nm from a van der Pauw sample on the same wafer. With the Methods density, v_F ≈ 1.1 × 10⁶ m/s, ξ_BCS ≈ 0.9 µm and the dirty-limit ξ ≈ 0.37 µm, which disagrees with the fit by about 40%. Further points:
   - The authors should say which measurement gives 6 × 10¹² cm⁻² (presumably the Al-covered region) and justify using it for ξ.
   - Δ is the parent Al gap from T_c, not the induced gap that sets the proximity coherence length in the 2DEG under the Al.
   - The fitted ξ has no uncertainty quoted.
   - v_F ≈ 3 × 10⁶ m/s is unusually high for InAs; band nonparabolicity at this density would lower it.

   The close numerical agreement should be presented as order-of-magnitude consistency unless these points are resolved.

3. Other explanations for the I_c(W_sc) suppression are not excluded. Eq. (2) is a phenomenological exponential. Fig. 3b's linearisation fixes I_c^∞ from the W_sc → ∞ device, which has a different lead geometry. Narrower contacts could lower I_c through:
   - a lower interface transparency or a larger normal-state resistance R_n;
   - etch damage near the Al edge, which matters most when the edge is close to the junction;
   - a reduced induced gap under narrow Al strips (inverse proximity effect).

   The authors should report R_n and I_c R_n for each device. If R_n is independent of W_sc while I_c falls, the proximity-length interpretation is supported. They should also state whether I_c^∞ is fixed or fitted, and give the fit uncertainty. A fit with I_c^∞ free (two parameters, four finite-W_sc points) would show how robust ξ is.

4. The orbital interpretation of B_η is only partly consistent with the data, and the quantitative estimates do not match it. The fit of Eq. (4) gives Φ/Φ₀ ≈ 0.36–0.61, depending on an assumed d of 10–17 nm. Several features argue against or remain untested for the orbital picture:
   - The sign change of η, expected at Φ = Φ₀, occurs at a larger flux.
   - The expected Φ₀-periodic oscillations are not seen.
   - The three single-mode estimates give B_η ≈ 13 mT (Meissner), 4 mT (orbital tunnelling) and 690 mT (Rashba–Zeeman), while the measured values are 90–270 mT.

   The Discussion is candid about this, but the abstract and conclusion ("The W_sc-dependence of the field of maximum nonreciprocity invites an orbital interpretation") should say that no model tested reproduces the measured field scale. A direct, unused test is in Fig. 5a: if the sign-change fields at 400–600 mT scale as 1/W_sc with the same Φ/d slope as B_η, that supports a flux-set scale. If they do not scale, that argues against it. Plotting the sign-change field against W_sc⁻¹ would strengthen or falsify the orbital reading. The saturation at W_sc ≳ 2ξ should also be checked quantitatively, by replacing W_sc with an effective width of about min(W_sc, 2ξ) in Eq. (4) and comparing with the ∞ device's B_η.

5. Attributing the gate dependence of B* to spin–orbit coupling does not separate the competing gate effects. Eq. (3) gives B* ∝ (1 − τ)^{1/4} (k_so/k_F) E_T/g*. The gate changes k_F, τ, E_T and k_so together, and none is calibrated against V_g: there is no gate-dependent density, no weak-antilocalisation α, and no Thouless energy or transparency from the I–V characteristics. Several devices in Fig. 4 have B* < 0 at negative gate (for example, 1.2 µm at V_g ≈ −3 V and 0.9 µm at V_g ≈ −3 V). Eq. (3) does not produce this sign reversal, and the text does not discuss it. The "V_g < 0" points in Figs. 3c and 4 are also taken at different gate voltages (about −4 V to −3 V) for different devices, so Fig. 3c compares devices at mismatched conditions. The authors should:
   - measure n(V_g), or at least I_c R_n and excess current against V_g, to bound how much of the B* change comes from k_F and τ rather than α;
   - explain the negative B* values;
   - in Fig. 3c, compare devices at a common V_g or a common density.

6. The uncertainties on η and on the small fields B* and B_η are not fully described. Methods says I_c± comes from a local fit to the Fraunhofer extremum, with uncertainty given by the RMS residual. It does not state:
   - how many I–V sweeps set each switching current;
   - the switching-current distribution width compared with |I_c+| − |I_c−|. For a 2 µA device, η = 5% corresponds to a difference of about 100 nA.

   B* is only a few mT at negative gate, comparable to possible remanent fields in the 6 T vector magnet. The authors should report the switching-histogram width or the repeatability of I_c± at fixed field, and say how the B∥ zero was calibrated, for example from the symmetry of I_c±(B∥) or from a sweep in both directions. Then the sign of B* and the error bars in Figs. 3c, 4 and 5c have a stated basis.

7. The θ = 0 null control appears for one representative device only (Fig. 1h). The W_sc trends are the headline, so η(B∥) at θ = 0 should be shown, at least in the supplement, for the narrowest (0.3 µm) and widest devices. That would show that no W_sc-dependent extrinsic asymmetry, such as lead self-field or a T-geometry current-path asymmetry, contributes. The paper should also state which device appears in Fig. 1. From the I_c scale it appears to be W_sc = 0.6 µm.

C. Minor

8. Fig. 5c: Eq. (4) predicts B_η ∝ 1/W_sc with zero intercept, but the plotted line appears to cross W_sc⁻¹ = 0 at a finite field. State whether the intercept was fixed at zero. Write the ranges in the text properly ("Φ/Φ₀ ≈ 0.36–0.61", "d = 10–17 nm"; currently "0.36 − −0.61", "10 − −17 nm").

9. The Meissner estimate takes λ_L → d_sc/2 for 10 nm Al. In such thin films the effective (Pearl) penetration depth far exceeds d_sc, and the screening-current momentum shift would be smaller still. Say that 13 mT is a lower bound, or justify the choice. Likewise, |g*| = 10 for the Rashba–Zeeman estimate should be referenced or bounded.

10. Fig. 3b: give the y-axis quantity explicitly in the caption. State whether max I_c+ is taken at B* or at B∥ = 0; the text says "max I_c+(B∥)", which is the B* value. Also say how averaging the positive and negative branches enters the fit.

11. Fig. 1g: give the V_g range over which the junction is not depleted and the leakage current, because the "V_g < 0" points sit close to where I_c falls steeply.

12. Fig. 5b: η depends on gate voltage, and the main text shows max η only at V_g = 0. A short statement of how max η changes with V_g for each device would support the claim that B_η is weakly gate-dependent while η's magnitude may not be.

13. References contain merged words and formatting artefacts (for example refs. 7, 9, 10, 13, 15, 18, 19, 40, 44, 45, which have missing spaces such as "AndreevBound", "PlanarJosephson"). Ref. 1 has a stray "type: article". Ref. 53 is incomplete.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add an R_n and I_c R_n table for all devices at each gate voltage. This addresses concerns 3 and 5 together and costs almost nothing given the existing data.
- Plot the field of the first η sign change against W_sc⁻¹ on the same axes as B_η (Fig. 5c). A shared 1/W_sc scaling would be the strongest geometric evidence for an orbital/flux mechanism.
- Model η(B∥) quantitatively with a multimode or tight-binding BdG calculation that includes finite W_sc, the 80 nm junction and d. Use it to test whether a finite-W_sc orbital model reproduces both B_η ≈ 90–270 mT and the saturation near 2ξ, rather than relying on single-mode estimates that miss by large factors.
- If more devices can be fabricated, add a second chip or replicate W_sc values. Narrower contacts (about 0.1–0.2 µm) would test the 1/W_sc law of B_η over a wider range.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
