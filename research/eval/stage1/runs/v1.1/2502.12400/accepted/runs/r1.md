Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper varies one geometric parameter, the longitudinal width W_sc of the superconducting contacts, in five co-fabricated symmetric T-shaped Al/InAs planar Josephson junctions. It tracks the critical current, the superconducting diode efficiency and two characteristic in-plane fields (B_* and B_η) as W_sc and gate voltage change. Most diode-effect studies vary only field, gate or angle, so a controlled geometric series is a useful addition. The exponential saturation of I_c with W_sc, and the different gate and W_sc dependences of B_* and B_η, give experimental handles for separating spin-orbit and orbital contributions. The mechanistic interpretation stays qualitative, and the authors say so.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- All devices are on one chip with nominally identical heterostructure, junction gap and width. This removes much of the wafer-to-wafer variation that complicates comparisons in the diode-effect literature.
- The extraction protocol is careful. For each (B_par, V_g) point the global extrema of the central Fraunhofer lobe are found separately for each current polarity (Eq. 1, Fig. 1d-f). This suppresses diode signals from an out-of-plane field component or from trapped vortices, and switching rather than retrapping currents are used.
- There is a null check. At θ = 0 (field parallel to the current), η ≈ 0 over the whole field range (Fig. 1h, black circles), and η ≈ 0 at zero field within uncertainty. Both are what a symmetric junction with the in-plane field as the only time-reversal-breaking agent should give.
- I_c(W_sc) follows the one-parameter form of Eq. (2) closely (Fig. 3b). The fitted ξ = 586 nm is close to a dirty-limit estimate.
- The contrast is clear and potentially diagnostic. B_η depends strongly on W_sc and weakly on gate (Fig. 5c), while B_* depends strongly on gate (Fig. 4).
- Raw data and analysis scripts are deposited on Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The paper's central observations (I_c and the field scales depend systematically on W_sc and V_g) are supported by the data shown. Its mechanistic statements are framed as interpretations, not conclusions. The concerns below would make those interpretations firmer but are not preconditions for the observations.

B. Should address

1. The material parameters behind the coherence-length comparison are inconsistent. On p. 2-3 the dirty-limit estimate ξ = sqrt(ξ_BCS ℓ) ≈ 570 nm uses n = 6 × 10^12 cm^-2 and ℓ = 130 nm, giving v_F ≈ 3 × 10^6 m/s and ξ_BCS ≈ 2.5 µm. The Methods report van der Pauw values from the same wafer of n = 8.14 × 10^11 cm^-2, µ = 9.86 × 10^3 cm^2/Vs and ℓ_e = 147 nm. With the Methods density, v_F ≈ 1.1 × 10^6 m/s, ξ_BCS ≈ 0.9 µm and ξ ≈ 0.37 µm, which is well below the fitted 586 nm. The close agreement highlighted in the text therefore depends on which density is used, and a density of 6 × 10^12 cm^-2 is unusually high for a 4 nm InAs well. The authors should:
   - state where each set of transport parameters comes from (Al-removed Hall bar on this chip, a different wafer piece, a different gate voltage);
   - say which is appropriate for the 2DEG under the Al;
   - give the dirty-limit estimate with an uncertainty range that reflects this.
   They should also note that the gap used is the bulk Al value 1.764 k_B T_c ≈ 250 µeV. The induced gap in such structures is usually somewhat smaller, which raises ξ.

2. The coherence-length interpretation of I_c(W_sc) has not been separated from alternatives. Two other effects could also reduce I_c as W_sc shrinks:
   - Kinetic inductance of the narrow Al strips. Current enters each 4 µm-long contact from the T stem and must run along a strip of width W_sc and thickness 10 nm. For W_sc = 0.3 µm that is about 13 squares of thin Al. With I_c of a few µA, the phase drop along the strip can plausibly reach a sizable fraction of 2π. That would make the supercurrent distribution across the junction non-uniform and lower the measured switching current, with a W_sc dependence that need not be exponential.
   - Edge damage from the wet etch, which matters most in the narrowest contacts.
   Both can also feed into self-field contributions to nonreciprocity. The authors should estimate the strip kinetic inductance and the resulting phase drop at I_c. They should also show whether the Fraunhofer patterns (Fig. 1c is for one representative device) stay regular for the narrowest W_sc. The cleanest discriminator is the gate or temperature dependence of the fitted ξ. Fitting Eq. (2) at each V_g in the supplementary traces and reporting ξ(V_g) would test whether the decay length tracks the 2DEG properties, as the Andreev picture predicts, or not.

3. Each width is represented by a single device, and the reference device is structurally different. The trends in Figs. 3-5 rest on one junction per W_sc, so device-to-device spread cannot be judged. This matters most for the non-monotonic diode efficiency: max η is about 8.5% at W_sc = 1.2 µm and about 10% at 0.9 µm (Fig. 5b). The abstract nonetheless states that the diode efficiency increases with contact width.
   Separately, the W_sc → ∞ device has unetched Al leads. It fixes I_c^∞ in Fig. 3b, it gives the largest η (about 16.5%), and it is the only device whose data were smoothed (Results, end of first section). Because I_c^∞ is taken from this one device, the fitted ξ depends directly on whether it is otherwise equivalent to the others (gate coverage, current injection, junction gap).
   The authors should:
   - soften the abstract statement on η to "tends to increase";
   - quantify how the smoothing affects max η and B_η for that device;
   - show the sensitivity of ξ to treating I_c^∞ as a free parameter instead of fixing it to that device's value;
   - if a second device at any width exists, show it.

4. The values of W_sc lack measured uncertainties. W_sc was confirmed by SEM on proxy devices, not on the measured junctions, and both W_sc and the 80 nm gap are defined by wet etching of Al, which undercuts. An undercut of a few tens of nm is a large fraction of 0.3 µm and moves the rightmost points in Figs. 3b, 3c and 5c. The authors should give the measured spread of W_sc (and of W_n) on the proxy devices and add horizontal error bars to the plots against 1/W_sc, or check that the fitted ξ and Φ/Φ_0 are robust to that spread.

5. The orbital reading of B_η rests on few points and an ambiguous fit.
   - Eq. (4) predicts B_η ∝ 1/W_sc through the origin. The solid line in Fig. 5c appears to have a sizable positive intercept (about 25 mT at 1/W_sc = 0). The text does not say whether an intercept was fitted, or how a range Φ/Φ_0 ≈ 0.36-0.61 follows from a single slope by assuming d = 10-17 nm. The authors should state the fit model and its free parameters, give the slope with its uncertainty, and say whether a zero-intercept fit is acceptable.
   - The claimed saturation for W_sc ≳ 2ξ rests on essentially two points (1.2 µm and ∞).
   - The orbital picture predicts sign changes near Φ = Φ_0 and Φ_0-periodic oscillations of η, and neither is seen (p. 4).
   - None of the Eq. (6) estimates (13 mT Meissner, 4 mT orbital, 690 mT Rashba-Zeeman) matches the observed 90-270 mT.
   The conclusion that the W_sc dependence "invites an orbital interpretation" should therefore be stated as a consistency, with the failures listed alongside it. Better still, the authors could include a calculation that keeps finite W_sc (for example a tight-binding or quasiclassical model of the actual geometry), which would make the comparison quantitative.

6. The spin-orbit interpretation of the gate dependence of B_* is not tested independently.
   - Eq. (3) depends on τ, k_so, k_F and E_T, all of which change with V_g. The paper attributes the rise of B_* with V_g to increasing α and decreasing τ but measures neither on these devices. A gate-dependent α from weak antilocalization on the same chip (or a cited calibration of this wafer), and a τ estimate from I_c R_n or excess current versus V_g, would let Eq. (3) be checked semi-quantitatively.
   - Several B_* values are negative (Fig. 4: W_sc = 1.2 µm and 0.9 µm at V_g < 0, and the V_g < 0 series in Fig. 3c). Eq. (3) gives only positive B_*. The sign convention and the meaning of a negative B_* should be explained.
   - Eq. (3) was derived for W_sc → ∞, so the strong W_sc dependence of B_* in Fig. 3c (non-monotonic at V_g = 0) has no interpretation. The authors should at least comment on it.

7. The temperature dependence is missing, though it is the most direct test of the Andreev-bound-state picture. All data are at base temperature. Eq. (2) predicts that the W_sc-suppression of I_c weakens or strengthens with temperature as ξ and the induced gap change. The orbital and finite-q pictures predict different temperature dependences of η and B_η. Even I_c(W_sc) at two or three temperatures would strengthen the central claim considerably.

C. Minor

8. The averaging and error bars for B_* need stating. B_* is defined as argmax I_c+ = −argmin I_c−, and Fig. 3b uses the average of the two branches. Please say explicitly whether B_* (and B_η) are also averages of the two branches. Averaging cancels any offset in the zero of B_par from magnet remanence; a single branch does not. This matters because B_* is only a few mT for several devices. The same applies to the error bars from the ref. 8 model fit: say what is fitted and over what field window.

9. The sign changes at 400-600 mT are attributed to the collapse of the induced gap (p. 4-5). Fig. 3a shows I_c still finite there. A brief justification would help, for example the field scale for gap closing estimated from g* and Δ, or the B_par dependence of the gap from the supplementary data.

10. The θ = 0 null measurement is shown only for a representative device (Fig. 1h). Please state whether it was done for all widths, or include it for the narrowest and widest devices in the supplement.

11. On p. 5, q_M uses λ_L → d_sc/2. Please justify this substitution for a 10 nm film, where the Pearl length is the relevant screening length, or cite where it comes from.

12. Typographical and editorial points:
    - "Φ/Φ_0 ≈ 0.36−−0.61" and "d = 10−−17 nm" have doubled dashes.
    - Many reference entries are missing spaces between words (for example refs. 7, 9, 10, 13, 16, 18, 19, 38) and mix arXiv and journal formats.
    - The Fig. 1 caption says the uncertainty includes a "2° misalignment"; please state whether this enters only the θ = 0 data.
    - Fig. 3c and Fig. 4 show the same data. Consider merging them or saying so in the Fig. 3 caption.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Reconcile the density and mean-free-path values between the main text and the Methods, and rederive the dirty-limit ξ with uncertainties (concern 1).
2. Fit Eq. (2) at each gate voltage, and ideally at a few temperatures, to show that the decay length behaves as an induced coherence length should. Add an estimate of the kinetic-inductance phase drop along the narrow Al strips to rule out a current-distribution origin of the I_c suppression (concerns 2 and 7).
3. Give the fit model behind Fig. 5c explicitly, with free parameters, intercept and uncertainties. List the orbital-model predictions that the data do not show (Φ_0 periodicity, sign change at Φ_0) next to those they do (concern 5).
4. Measure α(V_g) and τ(V_g) independently on the same chip to make the Eq. (3) interpretation of B_* testable (concern 6).

5. Verdict

Verdict: minor revision
