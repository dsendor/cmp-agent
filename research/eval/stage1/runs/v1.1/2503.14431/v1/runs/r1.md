Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper derives a matched asymptotic solution, in powers of sqrt(k_e), of a linearised kinetic equation for electron flow in a channel with diffuse edges. The equation has separate even-mode (l_e) and odd-mode (l_o) relaxation lengths, the "tomographic" regime. Closed forms are given for the velocity profile (Eq. 3), the Hall field (Eq. 4) and the channel conductance (Eq. 5), and they are checked against numerical solutions of the same kinetic equation. The four reported features are a boundary layer of width sqrt(l_e l_o), a second-order velocity slip, bulk rarefaction corrections and fast suppression of all three by a magnetic field. The last of these predicts a non-monotonic magnetoconductance, and the paper argues that tomographic corrections change two proposed protocols for extracting the Hall viscosity. An analytic treatment of boundary effects in the tomographic regime would be a useful addition to the electron-hydrodynamics literature, which so far has relied on heuristic boundary conditions or numerics.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A systematic, controlled expansion with explicit boundary-layer functions (Y_0, Y_1, T_0, T_1 in Fig. 2) replaces ad hoc no-slip or slip boundary conditions. The structure of the expansion, a non-analytic sqrt(k_e) series with a separate layer equation (Eq. 16) of the same form as the tomographic equation of Ledwith et al., is physically transparent.
- The asymptotic results are checked against direct numerical solutions of the kinetic equation for the velocity profile (Fig. 1d), the profile and Hall field in a field (Fig. 1e), the conductance over four decades of 1/r_c (Fig. 1c) and Delta R_xy (0.20 against 0.19). Agreement is good.
- The internal algebra holds where I could check it. In the G -> infinity limit the bulk profile 1/2 - 2x^2, together with the slip condition Eq. (17), gives exactly the 1/3 + 64 k_e/(15 pi) + k_e k_o/(1 + (3k_o/r_c)^2) of Eq. (5).
- The result has a direct experimental angle: the magnetic-field scales of the conductance minimum, Fig. 1(c), give a route to both mean free paths, and the paper warns that Hall-viscosity extractions can be contaminated by kinetic corrections.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The model for odd-mode relaxation is not tested, and the physical claims depend on it. Eq. (2) relaxes all odd harmonics m >= 3 at one rate 1/l_o. The microscopic results the paper cites for the tomographic effect ([15-18]) instead give odd-mode rates that depend strongly on m (roughly as m^4, with logarithms, in Ledwith et al.). That dependence is what produces superdiffusion and the scale-dependent viscosity of Ref. [16]. The layer equation, Eq. (16), contains v_x^2 d^2/dchi^2 acting on the whole odd sector, so the width and shape of the "tomographic boundary layer" and the O(k_e k_o) slip may change qualitatively if the odd rates are mode-dependent. The layer could become multi-scale rather than having a single width sqrt(l_e l_o).
   - This bears directly on the headline statements. The Discussion says the right starting point is "the generalized Boltzmann equation (2)" and not "a dual-relaxation time description". Yet Eq. (2) is itself a relaxation-time model with one more rate. The conductance minimum is proposed as a measurement of "the odd-mode mean free path", which has no single meaning if the odd rates depend on m.
   - What the authors should do: either (i) solve the channel problem numerically with an m-dependent odd spectrum taken from Refs. [17, 18] (or Ledwith's form), and show that the four features, and the location of the conductance minimum near R_c ~ l_o, survive, stating which effective l_o is measured; or (ii) reframe the claims explicitly as results of a three-rate model and drop the statements about real tomographic Fermi liquids that the model cannot support.

2. There is no comparison with the conventional (non-tomographic) kinetic model, so the "distinguishing" and "unequivocal signature" claims are not shown.
   - The abstract and introduction call the four effects "new distinguishing features" and the non-monotonic magnetoconductance "an unequivocal macroscopic signature". Several ingredients are also present in a conventional single-rate kinetic description with diffuse edges:
     - The O(k_e) slip 64 k_e/(15 pi) in Eq. (5) does not depend on k_o.
     - Kinetic (Knudsen-layer) slip is suppressed by a field at R_c ~ l_e.
     - At finite G or with l_e ~ L, ballistic and Ohmic channels show negative magnetoresistance (Refs. [1, 34, 36]).
   - None of the figures shows the k_o = k_e (or small-k_o) case under the same conditions, solved numerically since the expansion assumes k_o = O(1). That curve would show that the minimum in Fig. 1(c), and the +0.20 sign of Delta R_xy in the Hall-viscosity section, are specific to k_o >> k_e and not generic to diffuse-boundary kinetic flow.
   - What the authors should do: add to Fig. 1(c), and to the Delta R_xy comparison, numerical curves for k_o = k_e and for one or two intermediate k_o at fixed k_e. Also show at least one finite-G case, and state how much of the minimum's depth and position scales with k_o.

B. Should address

3. The regime of validity of the expansion does not match the regime claimed as tomographic.
   - The expansion is in sqrt(k_e) with k_o held at O(1). Terms such as k_e k_o and the layer width sqrt(k_e k_o) are counted as small only if k_e k_o << 1, that is, sqrt(l_e l_o) << L.
   - The Discussion motivates the regime by l_e ~ (T_F/T)^2 and l_o ~ (T_F/T)^4 at T <~ 0.1 T_F. That implies l_o/l_e ~ (T_F/T)^2 >~ 100, and Fig. 1(a) labels the regime l_o >> l_e. When l_o >> L the layers fill the channel and the expansion is no longer controlled.
   - The examples sit at the edge: k_e k_o = 0.2 in Fig. 1(d,e), where sqrt(k_e k_o) = 0.45 against a half-width of 0.5, and 0.125 in Fig. 1(c).
   - The high-field end of Fig. 1(c) (1/r_c up to about 100, so R_c < l_e) is also outside the ordering used to derive the viscosity reduction factor (1 + (2k_e/r_c)^2).
   - What the authors should do: state the double inequality the result needs (including sqrt(l_e l_o) << L). Give an error map of asymptotics against numerics over the (k_e, k_o) plane, for example the relative conductance error. Say where the closed forms fail.

4. Estimates for real materials are missing. The paper says the signatures "should be readily detectable in present-day experiments" but gives no numbers. The authors should take a representative graphene or GaAs channel (density, width, temperature), quote estimated l_e, l_o and l_MR from Refs. [17, 18], and give:
   - the resulting k_e, k_o and G;
   - the fields B corresponding to R_c = l_o and R_c = l_e;
   - the predicted depth of the conductance minimum.

   This matters most because finite G (phonons, impurities) acts on the same field scales, while Fig. 1(c) is computed for G -> infinity.

5. Eq. (3) and the bulk equations appear to contain sign and forcing errors.
   - With the stated scaling, the leading term G^2(cosh(2x/G)/cosh(1/G) - 1) is negative in the channel; for G = 1 it is -0.35 at x = 0. The dashed hydrodynamic curve in Fig. 1(d) is +0.35, which corresponds to G^2(1 - cosh(2x/G)/cosh(1/G)).
   - Likewise, Eq. (14) with I_0 = 0 and no-slip boundaries has only the trivial solution u = 0. The electric-field drive (the -2 k_e v_y term in Eq. 2) must appear as a source at n = 0. Since Eq. (5) is consistent with u = 1/2 - 2x^2, I take these to be typographical errors.
   - The authors should correct them and check signs throughout Eqs. (3), (4), (14)-(18).

6. The Methods are too brief to reproduce the main result.
   - Neither the derivation of Eqs. (14)-(15) nor the numerical solution of the layer equation, Eq. (16), is given. That solution is a two-dimensional (chi, theta) boundary-value problem, and the paper does not state its far-field matching conditions, angular truncation or how T_0, T_1 were obtained.
   - The "direct numerical solutions to the linearized Boltzmann equation" used as the benchmark are not described: discretisation in x and theta, number of angular harmonics, treatment of the diffuse boundary, convergence checks.
   - Because the numerics are the paper's only validation, these details (in a supplement if needed) are needed to judge the agreement in Fig. 1 and the 0.20 against 0.19 comparison.

7. The rarefaction claim depends on G. Feature (iii), that bulk rarefaction corrections reduce the curvature of the profile, comes from I_2, which is proportional to the fourth derivative of u^(0). That derivative vanishes for the Poiseuille profile, so the third bracket in Eq. (3) goes to zero as G -> infinity. The effect therefore exists only when momentum-relaxing scattering is significant, while the Discussion stresses ultraclean devices. The abstract and introduction should say this, and the authors should show how large the term is as a function of G.

8. Boundary specularity is fixed. All results assume fully diffuse edges, Eq. (12). Both the slip coefficients and the tomographic-layer functions are expected to depend on the specularity parameter, and real graphene or GaAs edges are partially specular. The authors should at least discuss how the slip coefficient 32/(15 pi) and the k_o term in Eq. (17) change with partial specularity, or show one numerical case. The magnetoconductance route to l_o, l_e would otherwise be confounded by an unknown edge parameter.

9. The Hall-viscosity analysis rests on one data point.
   - The conclusion that Delta R_xy does not track eta_H rests on one parameter set (k_e = 0.025, k_o = 5, 1/r_c = 0.02, G -> infinity). There, 2k_e/r_c = 0.001, so the hydrodynamic Hall-viscosity term is about -0.004 against the tomographic +0.20.
   - A plot of Delta R_xy against 1/r_c (and k_o), asymptotic and numerical, overlaid with Eq. (9), would show where, if anywhere, the Hall viscosity could still be extracted.
   - The same applies to Eq. (7). Show the size of the second term relative to the first for realistic G, since it vanishes for G -> infinity.

C. Minor

10. Fig. 1(c): the caption says "Magnetoconductivity ... scaled by the hydrodynamic conductivity e^2 n E L^3/(m v_F l_e)", but the axis is "channel conductance G_ch", and a conductance scale should not contain the driving field E. Please make these consistent.

11. Fig. 1(c) at small 1/r_c: the asymptotic curve has a small bump near 1/r_c ~ 0.03-0.1 and the numerics are sparse there. Please add numerical points in that range or comment on whether the bump is real.

12. The text says the results "persist for an arbitrary device geometry", deferring to Ref. [31] (in preparation). Either support this or soften it.

13. Typos and garbled sentences:
   - "l_e ≃ l_0" should read l_o (Discussion).
   - "i.e, the channel" (Channel conductance).
   - "this tomographic finite-wavelength such a correction must be taken into account" (end of the Hall-field subsection).
   - Fig. 1 caption: "(d) and (e): Flow in a channel driven ...".

14. Eq. (14) is introduced as holding "up to n = 2 for u and n = 4 for delta mu", but J_n and I_n are listed for different ranges. Please state explicitly which orders are used for which result.

15. The comparison with Gurzhi et al.'s R_c^{-1/3} scaling is dismissed as probably due to "the qualitative nature of their discussion". A sentence on which regime (k_o -> infinity?) that scaling would apply to, and whether the present expansion can reach it, would be more informative.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a phase-diagram-style figure in (k_e, k_o, 1/r_c) showing where the asymptotic forms are accurate, where the tomographic layer fills the channel, and where the conventional (k_o = k_e) limit is recovered.
- Recast Eq. (5) as a fitting formula: ratios of the minimum position and depth to the high-field rise. Show, with synthetic noisy data or realistic parameters, that l_e and l_o can be extracted independently and with what uncertainty.
- Provide the tabulated layer functions Y_0, Y_1, T_0, T_1 (or a short code) so that experimentalists can use Eqs. (3)-(4) directly.
- Briefly discuss how thermal smearing and the temperature dependence of l_e and l_o would appear in measurements of the conductance minimum at fixed B as T varies.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
