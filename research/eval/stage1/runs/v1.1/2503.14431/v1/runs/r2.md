Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript solves the linearized Fermi-liquid kinetic equation for flow in a 2D channel with diffuse edges, using a relaxation model with separate even-mode (ℓe) and odd-mode (ℓo) mean free paths plus momentum relaxation (ℓMR), in the ordering ℓe ≪ L ≲ ℓo ≪ ℓMR. A matched asymptotic expansion in √ke gives closed expressions for the velocity profile, Hall field and conductance (Eqs. 3–5). The expressions contain a "tomographic boundary layer" of width √(ℓeℓo), a slip correction of order ℓeℓo, bulk rarefaction terms, and strong suppression of all of these once Rc ≲ ℓo. That suppression makes the magnetoconductance non-monotonic. The authors also argue that two published Hall-viscosity protocols are contaminated by tomographic corrections. An analytical treatment of tomographic flow with real boundaries is new and useful, since earlier work used no-slip conditions or purely numerical solutions. The advance builds directly on the authors' own near-hydrodynamic asymptotics (Refs. 23, 24) and their bulk magnetic-suppression study (Ref. 20).

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A systematic, controlled expansion with a non-analytic small parameter √ke. It identifies a boundary layer much thicker than the classical Knudsen layer, and the resulting slip condition has the same form as the second-order slip of rarefied gases (Eq. 17). The physics is clear.
- The asymptotic results are checked against direct numerical solutions of the linearized Boltzmann equation: velocity (Fig. 1d,e), Hall field (Fig. 1e inset), conductance across five decades of field (Fig. 1c), and ΔRxy (0.20 vs 0.19). The agreement is good even where the small parameter is not very small.
- The non-monotonic magnetoconductance has two characteristic fields, (3ko)⁻¹ and (2ke)⁻¹. It is a concrete, experimentally oriented prediction, and the Hall-resistance result (Eq. 11, ΔRxy ∝ ke^{3/2}, opposite in sign to the Hall-viscosity prediction) is a useful caution for experiments.
- The paper is compact and the Methods section is clear about the structure of the expansion.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. A single odd-mode relaxation length is assumed for all odd harmonics, and the main quantitative claims depend on it. Eq. (2) relaxes every odd harmonic m ≥ 3 at the single rate 1/ℓo, and every even harmonic m ≥ 2 at 1/ℓe. The microscopic studies the paper cites (Refs. 15–18) find that odd-mode rates depend strongly on m; for low m they grow roughly as m⁴ in the tomographic regime, which is what produces the superdiffusion and scale-dependent viscosity of Ledwith et al. (Ref. 16). Higher odd modes therefore relax much faster than m = 3. The boundary layer is exactly where higher harmonics matter: the histograms in Fig. 1(d,e) show substantial weight up to m ≈ 5 at x = −0.5 and −0.45. Several results depend directly on the single-ℓo assumption:
   - the width √(keko) of the tomographic layer;
   - the ko/4 coefficient in the slip condition (Eq. 17);
   - the Lorentzian scale 3ko/rc that sets the low-field feature of the magnetoconductance;
   - the claim that ℓo can be read off from the conductance.
   With m-dependent rates, "ℓo" may not be a well-defined single length, and the field (3ko)⁻¹ could map onto a different combination of rates. The authors should do one of the following:
   - (a) solve the kinetic equation numerically with m-dependent odd rates (for example γ_m ∝ m⁴ for odd m, or the exact-diagonalization rates of Refs. 17, 18) and show how far the velocity profile, slip, layer width and Fig. 1(c) change; or
   - (b) give a controlled argument for why only the slowest odd mode controls the O(ke) results, and state which effective ℓo is measured.
   Without this, the statement that the conductance "can be used to measure both the even- and odd-mode mean free paths" is not supported for real Fermi liquids. The Discussion's claim that Eq. (2) is "the appropriate starting point" is also not established.

B. Should address

2. The novelty claims need a comparison with the conventional kinetic description (ko = ke), not only with no-slip Stokes–Ohm. In Fig. 1(d,e) and in the Hall-resistance discussion, the comparison is with the no-slip hydrodynamic solution. A classical gas with diffuse walls (ko = ke, as in Refs. 23, 24) already has a Knudsen layer, an O(ke) slip and rarefaction corrections. "Distinguishing" features (i)–(iv) should therefore be shown against that baseline. Two items are needed:
   - An extra curve in Fig. 1(d) and Fig. 1(c): the kinetic solution with ko = ke at the same ke, so readers can see how much of the enhanced conductance and of the negative magnetoconductance is specifically tomographic.
   - For Eq. (11), the value of ΔRxy in the conventional kinetic description. Ref. 23 already reports that the near-hydrodynamic Hall field departs from the Hall-viscosity prediction. Is the positive sign of ΔRxy unique to ko ≫ ke?

3. The range of validity of the asymptotics is not quantified, and the chosen parameters push it. The layer width √(keko) is 0.45 for Fig. 1(d,e) (ke = 0.1, ko = 2) and 0.35 for Fig. 1(c) (ke = 0.025, ko = 5). Both are comparable to the channel half-width of 0.5, so the two layers overlap, which matched asymptotics with separated layers does not obviously allow. Agreement with numerics is good at these points, but small deviations are visible near the centre in Fig. 1(e). The authors should show the error between Eq. (3) or Eq. (5) and the numerics as a function of ke and ko, for example the conductance at B = 0 and the minimum value against √(keko). They should also state where the formulas fail, since an experimentalist fitting Eq. (5) needs this.

4. The magnetic-field prefactor in Eq. (5) is inserted, not derived. The text says "where we also include as a prefactor 1 + (2ke/rc)²" to describe the reduction of viscosity at Rc ~ ℓe. In the expansion, rc is treated as O(1), so the regime Rc ~ ℓe (1/rc ~ 1/ke) lies outside the derived result. The upturn, half of the non-monotonic signature, therefore rests on a heuristic factor applied to all terms, including the slip and rarefaction terms. Fig. 1(c) shows that the numerics agree, which is reassuring. The authors should either derive the uniformly valid form (for example a distinguished limit rc ~ ke) or clearly label the high-field part as an interpolation, and show the size of its error in Fig. 1(c).

5. The parameter hierarchy may not be reachable experimentally. The paper says the regime is "readily accessible" provided devices are clean. However:
   - the figures use ko/ke = 20 (Fig. 1d,e) and 200 (Fig. 1c), while ℓo ≪ ℓMR must also hold;
   - by the scalings quoted in the Discussion (ℓe ~ (TF/T)², ℓo ~ (TF/T)⁴), large ratios require T ≪ 0.1 TF;
   - at such temperatures ℓe itself becomes long, so ℓe ≪ L becomes harder to satisfy.
   The authors should give numbers for at least one real system (for example graphene at a stated density and temperature, or GaAs). These should include the expected ℓe, ℓo and the phonon/impurity ℓMR, show that ℓe ≪ L ≲ ℓo ≪ ℓMR can be met, and give the predicted dip depth in Fig. 1(c) at those values. The conductance is also given only for G → ∞ (Eq. 5). The finite-G version, which the full Eq. (3) contains, should be given or plotted, since real devices have G ~ 1–10.

6. The claim that ℓe and ℓo can be extracted is not tested as an inverse problem. The low-field feature is a broad Lorentzian in 3ko/rc multiplied by keko, and the upturn is set by 2ke/rc. Other sources of non-monotonic magnetoresistance in channels exist: the ballistic peak at W/Rc ≈ 0.5 (Ref. 12) and the finite-G Gurzhi crossover. The authors should show that fitting Eq. (5), or its finite-G form, to synthetic numerical data recovers ke and ko with stated uncertainty. They should also discuss how the tomographic dip is distinguished from these other mechanisms in the same field range.

7. Fully diffuse boundaries are assumed throughout (Eq. 12). Slip and boundary layers in kinetic theory depend strongly on the accommodation coefficient, and graphene edges are known to be partly specular. The slip coefficient 32/(15π), the ko/4 second-order term and the functions 𝒴, 𝒯 will change with specularity. The authors should at least state how the results depend on a Maxwell-type specularity parameter, or give one numerical example, since the prediction of measurable ℓe and ℓo depends on it.

8. The Hall-viscosity conclusions rest on a single parameter point. The statement that ΔRxy "does not provide an accurate avenue" for the Hall viscosity is supported by one evaluation (ke = 0.025, ko = 5, 1/rc = 0.02). ΔRxy should be plotted against 1/rc, and ideally against ko, together with the Hall-viscosity prediction of Eq. (9). This would show where, if anywhere, the Hall-viscosity term dominates (for example when Rc ≲ ℓo suppresses the tomographic layer). For the Holder et al. protocol (Eq. 7), the extra term is of order ko²/G² and vanishes for clean samples. Please quantify it for realistic G so readers can judge whether the correction matters in practice.

9. The numerical Boltzmann solutions are not described. The validation of every main result relies on "direct numerical solutions to the linearized Boltzmann equation", but the method is not given anywhere: angular discretization or harmonic cutoff, spatial grid, treatment of the diffuse boundary, and convergence tests. The cutoff matters especially near the boundary, where many harmonics contribute. Please add this to the Methods section.

C. Minor

10. The Discussion says the description is "in closed analytical form". The tomographic layer functions 𝒴0, 𝒴1, 𝒯0, 𝒯1 are, however, obtained by numerically solving the half-space problem, Eq. (16) ("solving the resulting equation numerically"). Please say so in the abstract or Discussion, and provide the functions in a usable form (table, fit or code), since Eqs. (3), (4) and (11) cannot be evaluated otherwise.
11. Fig. 1(c): the y-axis appears logarithmic (unequal spacing of the ticks 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2). Please state this. Please also state the B = 0 value predicted by Eq. (5) for these parameters (1/3 + 64ke/15π + keko ≈ 0.49) so readers can check the low-field limit against the figure.
12. The caption of Fig. 1(c) calls the quantity "Magnetoconductivity" while the axis and text say "channel conductance". The text on p. 5 says Eq. (5) holds "for ke ≲ 1", while the derivation requires ke ≪ 1.
13. Equation references: p. 8 says "the '1' in Eq. (15c)" and "the term of O(ko²) in Eq. (15c)"; both refer to Eq. (15b). The caption of Fig. 1(d) says the slip conditions are given in "Eq. (3)"; the slip condition is Eq. (17).
14. Discussion: "ℓe ≃ ℓ0 ~ (TF/T)²" should read ℓo. The odd-mode scaling "ℓo ~ (TF/T)⁴" omits the logarithmic factors found in Refs. 17, 18. Please qualify it.
15. Eq. (5) ends with "+ o(ke)" while Eqs. (3), (4) end with O(ke²). Please state the actual order of the first neglected term in the conductance (presumably O(ke^{3/2}) from integrating the tomographic layer, the same order that dominates Eq. 11).
16. The statement that the phenomena "persist for an arbitrary device geometry" is deferred to Ref. 31 and is not supported here. Please soften it or give a brief argument.
17. The Hall field is identified with the gradient of the electrochemical potential δμ. A sentence on how this relates to the voltage measured by gated or ungated probes, or by local imaging (Refs. 5, 7), would help experimental readers.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add one figure that compares, at matched ke, the kinetic solutions for ko = ke (classical), ko ≫ ke with a single odd rate (this work) and m-dependent odd rates. Showing the velocity profile and the magnetoconductance would address concerns 1 and 2 together.
- Provide a phase diagram in the (1/rc, ko) or (ke, ko) plane showing where Eq. (5) is accurate to within a stated tolerance, and where ΔRxy is dominated by the Hall viscosity versus the tomographic layer.
- Give a worked experimental estimate for one material (density, temperature, channel width, expected dip depth and the two characteristic fields), with ℓMR included.
- Release the numerical tomographic-layer functions and the Boltzmann solver, or tabulate the functions, so that Eqs. (3)–(5) and (11) can be used directly.

5. Verdict: major revision
