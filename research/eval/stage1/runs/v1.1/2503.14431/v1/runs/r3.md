Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript derives a matched asymptotic solution of a linearized Fermi-liquid kinetic equation for flow through a channel with diffuse edges. The equation has separate even-mode (ℓe) and odd-mode (ℓo) relaxation lengths plus momentum relaxation. The solution predicts a "tomographic boundary layer" of width O(√(ℓe ℓo)), a slip velocity of order ℓe ℓo, finite-wavelength corrections to the bulk equations, rapid magnetic-field suppression of all three, and therefore a non-monotonic channel magnetoconductance with features at Rc ~ 3ℓo and Rc ~ 2ℓe. The authors also argue that two proposed Hall-viscosity protocols are contaminated by tomographic corrections. A closed-form, numerically checked description of tomographic channel flow, including its boundary physics, would be useful to the electron-hydrodynamics community. Novelty against the authors' own bulk magneto-response work [20] and against Ledwith et al. [16] lies mainly in the treatment of the boundary.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Systematic matched asymptotic expansion (with the non-analytic √ke structure identified) rather than heuristic boundary conditions. The resulting second-order slip condition (Eq. 17) and tomographic-layer equation (Eq. 16) connect cleanly to the rarefied-gas literature and to Ref. [16].
- The velocity profiles (Fig. 1d,e) and the magnetoconductance (Fig. 1c) are checked against direct numerical solutions of Eq. (2), with good agreement shown.
- A concrete, experimentally oriented prediction: a non-monotonic G_ch(B) whose two characteristic fields correspond to ℓo and ℓe.
- A potentially important caveat for Hall-viscosity extraction (Eqs. 7, 11): the computed ΔRxy has the opposite sign to the hydrodynamic prediction.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The regime of validity of the expansion is not stated, and the parameters used in the figures, as well as the physically motivated limit, sit at or beyond its edge. The expansion is in ke with ko held fixed (Methods, Eq. 13), but the results contain ke·ko and √(ke ko) as effective small parameters:
   - the layer width is √(ke ko) in units of L;
   - the slip term in Eq. (5) is ke ko/(1+(3ko/rc)²);
   - the tomographic-layer contributions to the integrated conductance are of order ke·√(ke ko), and are dropped from Eq. (5) as "o(ke)".

   In Fig. 1(d) (ke = 0.1, ko = 2), √(ke ko) ≈ 0.45, comparable to the channel half-width. The layers then overlap and the bulk/layer separation underlying Eq. (16) (semi-infinite χ) is not obviously valid. In Fig. 1(c) (ke = 0.025, ko = 5), √(ke ko) ≈ 0.35, so the dropped terms are a ~35% correction to the O(ke) terms kept.

   Moreover, Eq. (1) assumes ℓo ≳ L, and the Discussion stresses ℓo ~ (TF/T)⁴ growing much faster than ℓe ~ (TF/T)². In the deep tomographic regime the product ke ko therefore grows and eventually exceeds unity. There the expansion fails, and the slip term in Eq. (5), which grows linearly in ko, cannot be trusted.

   This matters because the headline claims rest on Eqs. (3)–(5): the enhanced conductance, the depth of the magnetoconductance minimum, and the extraction of ℓo from it. The authors should:
   - state the distinguished limit explicitly (for example ke → 0 with ke ko → 0), along with the size of the neglected terms;
   - show the error of Eq. (5) against the numerical solution as a function of ke ko at fixed ke, up to ke ko ~ 1;
   - say what happens to the conductance when ℓe ℓo ≳ L², for example by showing numerically whether the ko-linear enhancement saturates.

B. Should address

2. The quantitative predictions depend on a single odd-mode rate. Eq. (2) relaxes all odd modes (except the conserved current) at one rate 1/ℓo. The microscopic odd-mode spectrum [16–18], which the paper uses to motivate the work, has strongly m-dependent rates (∝ m⁴ at low T in Ref. [16]). That dependence produces the scale-dependent viscosity and superdiffusion of Refs. [15, 16]. The layer width √(ℓe ℓo), the slip coefficient k_o/(4(1+(3k_o/r_c)²)) in Eq. (17), and the field scale (3ko)⁻¹ in Eq. (5) all inherit the single-ℓo assumption. With an m-dependent spectrum, the "ℓo" extracted from the magnetoconductance minimum may be an effective, model-dependent average rather than a well-defined material parameter.

   The Discussion states that Eq. (2), rather than a "dual-relaxation time description", is the appropriate starting point, yet Eq. (2) is itself a relaxation-time model with one odd rate. The authors should either show (numerically, for at least one m-dependent odd spectrum) that the layer structure and the two-feature magnetoconductance survive, or state which effective odd-mode rate enters, and narrow the claim about measuring ℓo.

3. Treatment of the magnetic field in Eq. (5). The prefactor 1 + (2ke/rc)² is added by hand ("where we also include as a prefactor") from the known field-dependent viscosity. The expansion as stated does not derive it. It also multiplies the slip terms, which have their own field dependence. In Fig. 1(c) it is exercised up to 1/rc ≈ 50, where 2ke/rc ≈ 2.5. The authors should:
   - state how rc is ordered relative to ke and ko in the expansion;
   - derive the uniformly valid form, or justify the multiplicative combination;
   - show the residual between Eq. (5) and the numerics across the whole field range in Fig. 1(c), particularly in the crossover region around the minimum, where the claimed extraction of ℓe and ℓo would be made.

4. Uniqueness and observability of the magnetoconductance signature.
   - Non-monotonic magnetoresistance in channels also arises from purely ballistic/Knudsen physics with diffuse edges (the ballistic peak near W/Rc ~ 0.5) and from conventional hydrodynamics with Knudsen-layer slip. The paper does not show how the tomographic feature at Rc ~ 3ℓo differs from these. A comparison with the conventional case (ko = ke, same diffuse boundary) computed with the same machinery would make the "unequivocal macroscopic signature" claim testable.
   - Eq. (5) is given only for G → ∞, whereas realistic devices have G ~ 1 (as in Fig. 1d,e). The magnitude of the dip at finite G should be shown.
   - The Discussion states the regime is "readily accessible" but gives no numbers. Provide an estimate for one material, for example graphene or GaAs at a stated density and T/TF: ℓe, ℓo, ℓMR, a channel width L satisfying Eq. (1), the fields corresponding to Rc = 3ℓo and 2ℓe, and the expected fractional conductance change.

5. Hall-resistance conclusion rests on one parameter point. The statement that ΔRxy "does not provide an accurate avenue" for the Hall viscosity relies on a single comparison (ke = 0.025, ko = 5, G → ∞, 1/rc = 0.02; ΔRxy = +0.20 against +0.19 numerically). Two things are needed:
   - Show ΔRxy as a function of 1/rc and of ko (including ko → ke), against both Eq. (11) and the numerics. This would establish over what range the k_e^{3/2} boundary-layer term dominates the O(ke²) Hall-viscosity term.
   - Clarify whether the contamination is specifically tomographic. Conventional Knudsen-layer corrections with ko = ke (Refs. [23, 36]) may also exceed O(ke²). If so, the problem with the Scaffidi protocol is generic to diffuse boundaries rather than a tomographic effect, and the text should say so.

6. Claim (iii) of the abstract is not generic. The finite-wavelength term in Eq. (3), the third square bracket, is proportional to ke ko/G² for large G, because the source I₂ in Eq. (15c) involves ∂⁴u⁽⁰⁾, which vanishes for Poiseuille flow. Likewise, the extra term in Eq. (7) vanishes as G → ∞. The "bulk rarefaction correction that decreases the curvature" therefore exists only through momentum relaxation and is absent in the cleanest devices. The abstract and introduction should state this dependence on G, and the size of the effect at experimentally relevant G should be given.

7. Boundary condition. Only fully diffuse scattering (Eq. 12) is treated. The slip length and the layer amplitude are expected to depend strongly on edge specularity, which varies between devices (for example etched vs. encapsulated graphene edges). At minimum, discuss how a specularity parameter would enter Eqs. (16)–(17) and whether the ko-dependent slip survives partially specular edges. Otherwise the magnitude predictions are tied to an idealized boundary.

8. Reproducibility of the numerical and asymptotic results. The Methods give the structure of the expansion but not:
   - the intermediate O(√ke) problem;
   - the numerical method for Eq. (16), including the angular truncation and the treatment of χ → ∞;
   - the method, discretization and convergence of the "direct numerical solutions" of Eq. (2) used as the benchmark throughout.

   Because the agreement in Fig. 1 is the main evidence that the asymptotics are correct, the numerics should be described (angular modes, spatial grid, convergence test), for example in a supplement. Several derivations are deferred to unpublished work [31]. The statement that the effects "persist for an arbitrary device geometry" relies only on [31] and should be removed or supported.

C. Minor

9. Sign convention. Eq. (3) at O(ke⁰) gives G²(cosh(2x/G)/cosh(1/G) − 1) ≤ 0, while Fig. 1(d,e) plots positive u_y (for G = 1 the hydrodynamic curve peaks at +0.35 = 1 − 1/cosh 1). Either the figure shows −u_y or Eq. (3) has a sign slip. Please make them consistent with the stated driving field in the negative y-direction.

10. Fig. 1(c): the caption calls the quantity "magnetoconductivity", while the axis and text call it "channel conductance" G_ch. The curve's small structure at 1/rc ~ 0.03–0.1 is not obviously captured by the Lorentzian in Eq. (5); please check that the plotted line is Eq. (5) as written. Also add the hydrodynamic-only curve (1 + (2ke/rc)²)/3 for reference.

11. Fig. 1(e): the asymptotic curve visibly overshoots the numerics near the channel center and in the Hall-field inset. Quantify the discrepancy and attribute it to the O(ke²) terms or otherwise.

12. Eq. (5) is written with "+ o(ke)" while Eqs. (3)–(4) use "O(ke²)". State the actual order of the neglected terms (see concern 1).

13. Text after Eq. (15c): "the '1' in Eq. (15c)" and "the term of O(ko²) in Eq. (15c)" should refer to Eq. (15b).

14. The Discussion writes "ℓe ≃ ℓ0"; this should be ℓo. "such a correction must be taken into account" (p. 6) is missing words in the preceding clause.

15. The comparison with Gurzhi et al.'s Rc^{-1/3} scaling is dismissed as being "likely due to the qualitative nature" of that discussion. A sentence identifying which regime (ke ko ≫ 1?) would reproduce that scaling would be more informative, and connects to concern 1.

16. State the relation to Ref. [20] (same authors, magnetic suppression of tomographic transport in bulk) explicitly in the introduction, so that the new content specific to the channel boundary is clear.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a plot of the relative error of Eq. (5) and of the profile, Eq. (3), against the numerics as a function of ke ko, which defines where the analytic results can be used for fitting data.
2. Add a side-by-side calculation for the conventional hydrodynamic case (ko = ke) with the same boundary condition. This would isolate which features (layer width, ko-linear slip, the low-field dip in G_ch, the sign of ΔRxy) are specifically tomographic.
3. Include one realistic material estimate showing the fields and conductance changes an experiment would see, at finite G.
4. Test the robustness of the main predictions with an m-dependent odd-mode relaxation spectrum, even for one case.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
