Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript uses a phase-locked IQ heterodyne chain to record, shot by shot, the complex microwave signal from a parametrically pumped magnon gas in an out-of-plane magnetized 2.1 µm YIG film. From this it reports (i) a uniform distribution of the condensate phase relative to the reference over 1000 repetitions, taken as evidence that the phase is chosen spontaneously, and (ii) an autocorrelation-based "coherence value" that rises when the k ≈ 0 condensate forms. A single-shot, time-resolved measurement of the phase of a magnon condensate against an external reference is an attractive capability, and a convincing demonstration of spontaneous U(1) symmetry breaking in a room-temperature quasiparticle condensate would interest a broad audience. As presented, though, the central inference from a uniform phase histogram to a spontaneously chosen phase is not yet secured. The paper also does not explain how its result goes beyond earlier evidence of spontaneous coherence in magnon BEC (e.g. Ref. [35]).

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The experimental idea is simple and general. All sources share a 10 MHz reference, the condensate signal is down-converted with a phase-locked LO, and both amplitude and phase are recorded in single shots without averaging (Fig. 2, Extended Data Fig. 6b).
- The out-of-plane (FVSW) geometry puts the condensate at k ≈ 0, where the same microstrip antenna can detect it inductively. This avoids the optical (BLS) detection used in most earlier magnon-BEC work.
- The spectrogram from the spectrum analyser and the spectrogram reconstructed from the IQ data agree qualitatively (Fig. 3a, Extended Data Fig. 6a,c), which supports the detection chain.
- The data show a clear power threshold (Fig. 4) and the expected relaxation of the frequency towards the band bottom as the magnon density falls after the pump is switched off (Fig. 3a).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. A uniform phase histogram at a fixed laboratory time does not show that the initial phase is random, and the paper has no positive control showing the chain would detect a fixed phase.
   - What is claimed: the inset histograms in Fig. 2a (1000 shots, at the start of each frame, e.g. t = 1.25 µs) and the text on p. 5–6 argue that, because all equipment is phase-locked, "the uniform phase distribution must be an intrinsic property of the BEC."
   - Why it does not follow: the phase at time t relative to the LO is φ(t) = φ0 + ∫(ω_BEC(t') − ω_LO)dt'. Fig. 3a shows the condensate frequency chirping by about 100 MHz between 0.8 and 1.3 µs through the density-dependent nonlinear shift. The magnon density, and so the chirp, will vary from shot to shot. A frequency jitter of only 1 MHz accumulated over about 1 µs already gives about 2π of phase spread. A condensate whose initial phase is locked to the pump would therefore still give a uniform histogram at t = 1.25 µs. The uniformity of the histograms does not distinguish the two cases.
   - The control is missing too. Extended Data Fig. 5 states that the oscilloscope was not on the shared 10 MHz reference "due to technical difficulties" and that phase stability "was confirmed in preliminary measurements." These data are not shown, and the timing chain is decisive for this claim.
   - What the authors should do:
     - (a) Positive control: inject a phase-locked tone at about 3.3 GHz (or drive the k = 0 mode directly with a weak, phase-locked pulse at the BEC frequency) through the same antenna, filters, amplifier, mixer and oscilloscope with the same triggering. Show that the identical analysis gives a sharply clustered histogram, and quantify its width.
     - (b) Remove the accumulated dynamical phase shot by shot. Two ways: reference the phase to the moment coherence first appears in that shot, or fit φ(t) for each shot and report the distribution of the extrapolated phase at condensation onset.
     - (c) Report the shot-to-shot spread of the instantaneous BEC frequency, so readers can judge how much randomisation the chirp alone would produce.
     - (d) Give a circular statistic (for example a Rayleigh test, or the mean resultant length with its expected value for N = 1000) rather than a visual impression of uniformity.

2. The "coherence value" is not validated as a measure of condensate coherence, and its stated scale does not match the data.
   - How it is defined (Sec. 1.3, Sec. 3.2, Fig. 3c): the integral of |autocorrelation| of the "normalized" complex signal in a 100 ns window, divided by the same integral for an ideal tone.
   - Points the authors need to resolve:
     - (i) The text says 0 is perfect white noise, but every curve sits at about 0.17–0.18 with no signal (Figs. 3b and 4a). The no-signal baseline therefore has to be explained and subtracted, or the scale restated.
     - (ii) A 100 ns window resolves frequencies only to about 10 MHz. Any signal narrower than about 10 MHz will score high, including spectrally narrowed incoherent (thermal-like) noise. So the metric measures narrowband content, not phase coherence in the BEC sense.
     - (iii) The value also depends on SNR, on the amplitude envelope within the window and on the chirp. The paper itself attributes the dip near 1 µs to the chirp and the late decrease to SNR.
     - (iv) It is not stated how the signal is normalized, or how ω0 of the reference tone is chosen.
   - Why it matters: the title claim, "emergence of phase coherence," rests on this curve.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Calibrate the metric with synthetic and injected signals: a pure tone plus noise at the measured SNR at each time, a chirped tone, and Gaussian noise filtered to the measured BEC linewidth.
     - Add a discriminator that separates a coherent state from narrowband incoherent occupation. The single-shot amplitude statistics would do this: a Rician or Poissonian-like distribution against a Rayleigh distribution, or equivalently the normalized intensity correlation g2(0) of the IQ signal over the 1000 shots, as a function of time and pump power.

B. Should address

3. A random phase is necessary for a condensate but does not by itself identify one.
   - Other processes also leave the phase of a single mode undetermined:
     - kinetic instability, where secondary magnon groups are created in pairs and only their summed phase is fixed by the parametric magnons;
     - four-magnon parametric processes that feed the k = 0 mode;
     - classical self-oscillation of a nonlinear uniform-precession mode.
   - The identification of the signal as a BEC formed through thermalization rests on earlier work [41] and on the spectrogram. The paper shows no frequency-resolved population distribution and no estimate of the chemical potential reaching the band bottom. Nor does it separate the condensation threshold from the parametric instability threshold.
   - The perpendicular-YIG "BEC" interpretation has been disputed in the literature (Ref. [31] is cited but not discussed).
   - What the authors should do:
     - Discuss these alternatives explicitly.
     - Show the full spectrum (spectrum analyser) at 20, 21 and 22 dBm to establish where the parametric threshold lies relative to the coherence threshold.
     - Explain why the coherent signal that appears during pumping (0.6–1.0 µs, at about 3.42 GHz) is a condensate and not a kinetic-instability product.

4. The novelty relative to earlier work is not established, and several statements overreach.
   - Demidov et al., PRL 100, 047205 (2008) [Ref. 35], already reported "spontaneous coherence" of a magnon BEC from its microwave emission, including its independence from the pump. The manuscript does not say what the present measurement adds beyond that.
   - Overstated wording:
     - "it has been impossible to observe its spontaneous emergence" (p. 2);
     - "For the first time…" (p. 8);
     - "These findings confirm all postulated fundamental properties of quasiparticle condensates" (abstract).
   - What the authors should do: compare their result explicitly with Ref. [35] and Ref. [10], and moderate these claims.

5. The claims that "the BEC does not undergo dephasing" and that coherence persists after the amplitude falls below noise are not supported.
   - Where: p. 7–8 and the Fig. 4 caption.
   - Why the data do not show this:
     - In Fig. 4a at t = 3.5 µs the coherence value is only about 0.2–0.25, against a no-signal baseline of about 0.17. Without error analysis this is not a "clear remanence."
     - In Fig. 3b the coherence falls steadily from 1.8 µs onward, and the paper assigns the fall to SNR without showing it.
     - Phase stability over the condensate lifetime is a statement about long-delay temporal coherence, which a 100 ns sliding window cannot test.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Compute, per shot, the phase difference φ(t2) − φ(t1) after removing the mean frequency evolution, for delays up to about 1.5 µs. Report its variance over shots as a function of delay; this gives a phase-diffusion time.
     - Compare the measured coherence decay with what the measured amplitude and noise alone predict for a perfectly coherent tone.

6. The power-dependence text and Fig. 4 are inconsistent in places, and the threshold is ambiguous.
   - The caption first says that at 21 dBm "an increase in coherence is observed", then that at 22 dBm a "strong increase" occurs, and finally names 21 dBm as the threshold.
   - The text says the coherence "does not depend on the absolute amplitude." Yet the 22 dBm curve peaks at about 0.5, against about 0.75 at 23 and 25 dBm, so the coherence clearly depends on power near threshold, at least through SNR.
   - It also says a higher amplitude is observed above 23 dBm. In Fig. 4b, however, the 25 dBm trace peaks during pumping, at about 0.7 µs, and after the pump is switched off it is not obviously larger than the 23 dBm trace.
   - What the authors should do: give the threshold with an uncertainty, plot peak coherence (baseline-subtracted) and peak amplitude against pump power, and correct the text to match the figure.

7. Only one sample, one field and one pump duration are shown, and the operating conditions are thinly documented.
   - Repeatability across days or samples is not addressed, and neither is the sample temperature or heating under pumping at 23–25 dBm (amplified).
   - A field series would be a useful test. Moving the condensate frequency relative to the fixed 3.2 GHz LO and the filter edges should leave the phase statistics unchanged if they are intrinsic.
   - Basic sample parameters are missing: substrate, saturation magnetization, the linear FMR frequency at 281 mT, and a check that about 3.33 GHz is the band bottom. With nominal YIG parameters, γ(B − μ0Ms) gives roughly 3.0 GHz, so the difference needs an explanation (anisotropy, field calibration).

8. The artifacts of the IQ technique are not characterized with respect to the phase and coherence analysis.
   - Extended Data Fig. 6c shows a constant band at 3.77 GHz and a "shadow" between 3.2 and 3.3 GHz, both attributed to the IQ technique.
   - A constant, phase-stable spur inside the analysis band would add a fixed-phase component and could raise the baseline coherence value. IQ gain and phase imbalance would distort φ(t) and produce image components.
   - What the authors should do: state whether these components are filtered out before the phase and coherence analysis, and quantify the IQ imbalance and its effect on the phase histograms.

C. Minor

9. Eq. (2), φ = arctan(I + iQ), is not correct as written. It should be φ = arg(I + iQ) = atan2(Q, I).

10. Sec. 1.3 states that the coherence value "has the same time resolution as the original signal." With a 100 ns boxcar window, the effective resolution is about 100 ns. Please correct this, and state the window overlap used.

11. Fig. 2: the text says the histograms are taken "at t0," while the caption says "at the beginning of each frame." Please make these consistent. Also give the number of bins and define the radial scale ("Amplitude denotes the percentage…") with numbers.

12. Fig. 3a and Extended Data Fig. 6a appear to show the same spectrogram. State the power calibration of the colour scale (dBm at which reference plane), and say whether the spectra were taken in a separate run from the IQ data.

13. The Extended Data Fig. 5 caption should give the trigger jitter of the oscilloscope relative to the pulse generator. At an IF of about 130 MHz, 1 ns of jitter corresponds to about 0.8 rad of phase.

14. "The full dataset and algorithms … are available at reasonable request." Most top-tier journals now expect the raw IQ traces and analysis code to be deposited. Doing so would also let readers check concerns 1 and 2 directly.

15. Some wording is loose: "inlays" should be "insets," "thermalisation section" should be "thermalisation stage," and "coherency." The schematic in Fig. 1b places the onset of thermalization before the end of pumping. That matches the data, but the text on p. 5 implies condensation occurs only after the pump is switched off, while Fig. 3a shows a coherent component appearing during pumping. Please make these consistent.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Build the phase-randomness claim around a positive control, a phase-locked tone injected through the identical chain, and around the phase extrapolated to each shot's own condensation onset. Present both together with a Rayleigh statistic in one figure.
2. Replace or supplement the 100 ns autocorrelation metric with the single-shot amplitude statistics (or g2(0)) of the IQ signal, and with a delay-resolved phase-diffusion analysis over the condensate lifetime. These directly test a coherent state and a stable phase.
3. Add a field series and a finer power series around threshold, with spectrum-analyser data, to tie the coherence threshold to the condensation threshold and not to the parametric threshold.
4. Set the result explicitly against Demidov et al. (2008) and against alternative, non-BEC routes to a random-phase coherent k = 0 mode, and scale the conclusions to what the data show.

5. Verdict: major revision
