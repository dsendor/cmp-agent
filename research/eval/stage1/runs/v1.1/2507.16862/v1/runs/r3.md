Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors pump magnons parametrically in an out-of-plane magnetized 2.1 µm YIG film. They detect the k ≈ 0 signal with the same microstrip antenna through a phase-locked IQ mixer, which gives single-shot, time-resolved amplitude and phase of the magnetization precession relative to an external reference. Their main claims are that (i) the phase of the condensate is uniformly distributed over 1000 repetitions, so it forms spontaneously rather than being imposed by the pump, and (ii) a windowed-autocorrelation "coherence value" rises sharply above a pump-power threshold and stays high while the amplitude decays. Direct, single-shot phase access to a magnon condensate is attractive and could become a useful tool. The novelty of the headline claim, however, depends on showing that the random phase and the high coherence value are specific to condensation and are not produced by the apparatus or by any narrowband, kinetically populated mode. The manuscript does not yet show this.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The experimental concept is clean and simple: pumping and detection with one antenna, IQ down-conversion phase-locked to the pump chain, and single-shot records with no averaging over repetitions (Fig. 2, Extended Data Fig. 6b).
- The spectrogram from the IQ data agrees with the spectrum-analyser data (Extended Data Fig. 6a vs 6c), which supports the detection chain at the level of frequency content.
- The power series (Fig. 4) shows a threshold-like onset of the coherence value between 21 and 22 dBm, and the coherence plateau (~0.75) is the same at 23 and 25 dBm even though the amplitudes differ.
- The phase traces in Fig. 2b show linear phase evolution with the same slope across shots in the condensed regime, in contrast to the erratic phase during early pumping. This is a direct visual signature of a narrowband state forming.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The phase stability of the detection chain is asserted, not shown, and the oscilloscope is not locked to the reference.** The central claim rests on a uniform phase histogram being "an intrinsic property of the BEC" because "all externally induced factors are stable within a few degrees of phase shift" (p. 5–6) and because the pump starts "with a constant phase-offset" (p. 5). But the caption of Extended Data Fig. 5 states that "the oscilloscope was excluded [from the shared 10 MHz reference] due to technical difficulties, but phase stability of the setup as a whole was confirmed in preliminary measurements." No such measurement is shown.
   - Why this matters: the IF here is roughly 100–200 MHz (BEC near 3.3–3.4 GHz, LO at 3.2 GHz). At that IF, a trigger or timebase jitter of a few nanoseconds between the pulse generator and the free-running oscilloscope is enough to randomize the measured phase completely, which would give exactly the uniform histograms of Fig. 2a. Nothing in the paper excludes this.
   - What the authors should do: show a positive control under identical acquisition conditions that yields a clustered phase histogram. Options:
     - (a) a CW or pulsed tone from SG1/SG2 injected at ~3.3 GHz into the detection path;
     - (b) the linear (sub-threshold, directly driven) FMR response of the same film at the band bottom, excited by a phase-locked pulse at ~3.3 GHz;
     - (c) the leaked pump, or a pump harmonic, recorded through the same IQ/oscilloscope path.
   - For each, quote the circular standard deviation of the phase over 1000 shots at the same delay (t ≈ 1.25 µs). Without this, a uniform phase distribution cannot be attributed to the magnon system.

2. **A uniform phase at a fixed late time is expected even with a deterministic initial phase, because of shot-to-shot frequency jitter.** Fig. 3a shows the condensate frequency drifting from ~3.42 to ~3.33 GHz between pump-off and ~1.2 µs. The paper attributes this to the density-dependent nonlinear shift, so the drift will fluctuate from shot to shot along with the injected magnon number.
   - The size of the effect: the accumulated phase at t is φ(t) = φ0 + 2π∫f(t′)dt′. A shot-to-shot spread of only ~3 MHz in the mean frequency over ~0.3 µs gives a phase spread of ~2π. The histograms at t = 1.25 µs (Fig. 2a, right) therefore cannot distinguish a random initial phase from a fixed initial phase smeared by frequency or timing jitter. The "Measurement 1–3" traces in Fig. 2b also show visibly different slopes before ~1.25 µs.
   - What the authors should do: separate the initial phase from the accumulated phase. Options:
     - extract the instantaneous frequency of each shot, subtract its integrated phase, and histogram the residual phase at the onset of coherence;
     - histogram the phase at the earliest time the coherence value crosses a set level, for each shot;
     - show that the shot-to-shot spread of ∫f dt is much smaller than 2π.
   - The analysis should also report the onset time of condensation for each shot and its spread.

3. **The two signatures used (random phase across shots, high single-shot temporal coherence) are not unique to a Bose–Einstein condensate.**
   - The random phase is not BEC-specific: the k ≈ 0 mode is populated by four-magnon scattering out of the parametric pairs, so its phase is not fixed by the pump whether or not a condensate forms. A random phase is the generic outcome for any mode populated kinetically, and Fig. 2a's histograms at 0.10 and 0.90 µs are uniform too.
   - The high coherence value is not BEC-specific: over a 100 ns window (≈10 MHz resolution), any single mode with a linewidth below a few MHz gives a high autocorrelation integral, even if it is populated incoherently (thermal or amplified-noise statistics). The YIG band-bottom mode is intrinsically narrow.
   - So the observations in Figs. 2–4 are also consistent with a strongly overpopulated but incoherent (Gaussian, chaotic) occupation of the lowest mode.
   - What would discriminate: the amplitude statistics, which the authors already have from 1000 single shots. A chaotic single-mode field gives a Rayleigh-distributed |z| (g⁽²⁾(0) = 2). A coherent condensate gives a narrow distribution about a finite mean (g⁽²⁾(0) → 1).
   - What the authors should do: show the histogram of A(t) = |I + iQ| at several delays, and the derived intensity correlation g⁽²⁾(0) = ⟨A⁴⟩/⟨A²⟩², as a function of time and pump power, after correcting for the amplifier noise floor. They should also explain what in their data distinguishes the condensate from a narrowband incoherent occupation. As written, the claim that these results "confirm all postulated fundamental properties of quasiparticle condensates" (abstract) is not supported.

B. Should address

4. **The coherence metric is not calibrated, and its baseline contradicts its stated definition.**
   - The text says "0 denotes perfect white noise and 1 denotes a perfectly coherent signal" (p. 6 and the Fig. 3 caption), but the pure-noise baseline in Figs. 3b and 4a is ≈0.18. A metric with a non-zero noise floor and a strong SNR dependence underpins three claims: the threshold, the statement that coherence "does not depend on the absolute amplitude", and the "clear remanence in coherence" at 3.5 µs when the amplitude is below the noise.
   - At 3.5 µs, Fig. 4a shows ≈0.22–0.25 against a baseline of ≈0.18. The significance of this difference is not given, and the shaded bands are described as standard deviations, not standard errors.
   - What the authors should do: calibrate the metric with synthetic and injected signals, i.e. a pure tone plus measured detector noise, swept in SNR and in Lorentzian linewidth. Then use that calibration to (i) convert the coherence value into a linewidth or coherence time, (ii) show whether the late-time decrease is fully explained by SNR (the "no dephasing" claim, p. 7), and (iii) state the statistical significance of the 3.5 µs remanence.
   - The authors should also state precisely what is integrated (|autocorrelation| over which lag range; whether the signal is normalized per window) and whether a frequency chirp inside the 100 ns window lowers the value. This last point bears on the dip near pump-off.

5. **The condensation threshold is not separated from the parametric-instability threshold.** Fig. 4 places the condensation threshold at 21 dBm, but the parametric threshold of the 7.8 GHz pump in this geometry is not reported.
   - Why this matters: if 20 dBm is below the parametric threshold, the coherence onset may simply track the onset of parametric injection, not a critical density for condensation.
   - What the authors should do: report the parametric threshold, e.g. from the reflected pump power on the microwave detector shown in Extended Data Fig. 5, or from the 3.9 GHz parametric-magnon line. Then show coherence and phase statistics at finer power steps between the two thresholds, plotted against the injected magnon density or the peak amplitude.

6. **Single sample, single field, single temperature.** All data come from one 2.1 µm YIG film at 281 mT and room temperature (p. 8), and only one power series (Fig. 4) is shown.
   - Why this matters: a dependence predicted by the BEC interpretation would strengthen the claim considerably. Examples are a field dependence of the band-bottom frequency matched by the condensate line, a pump-duration dependence of the onset time, and a second film or antenna.
   - What the authors should do: add at least one such dependence and a repeat on a second sample or a later cooldown/remounting. Also show the phase histograms at 21, 22 and 25 dBm, not only 23 dBm.

7. **IQ artifacts overlap the condensate band.** The caption of Extended Data Fig. 6 notes "a constant band at 3.77 GHz and a shadow between 3.2 GHz and 3.3 GHz", which is adjacent to the 3.33 GHz condensate line. LO leakage or image components add a fixed-phase or broadband contribution that biases both the phase histograms and the coherence baseline.
   - What the authors should do: identify these artifacts (image rejection, LO feedthrough, DC offsets of I/Q), state whether they are removed before analysis (IQ imbalance correction, DC subtraction), and show that the results are unchanged when they are.

8. **The novelty statement overreaches prior work.** The paper says that "no direct proof of randomness in the established condensate phase has yet been provided" and "for the first time, we are able to directly measure the spontaneous emergence of coherence" (p. 8). But ref. [35] (Demidov et al., PRL 2008) is titled "Observation of spontaneous coherence in Bose–Einstein condensate of magnons", and refs. [24, 31, 41] discuss onset of coherence and out-of-plane condensation.
   - What the authors should do: state precisely what the phase-resolved single-shot measurement adds beyond those works, and engage with the points raised in the Demokritov comment [31] on out-of-plane magnon condensation.

C. Minor

9. **Fig. 2 histograms.**
   - The text says they are "at t0" (p. 5), while the caption says "at the beginning of each frame". State the exact delays.
   - Add a radial scale.
   - Give a quantitative uniformity test (Rayleigh test, circular variance) next to the corresponding control value.

10. **Band-bottom frequency.** For a 281 mT perpendicular field, a simple Kittel estimate f = γ(B − μ0Ms) gives ≈3.0 GHz, while the condensate sits at ≈3.33 GHz. Give the calculated FVSW dispersion, including anisotropy and the effective magnetization, to confirm that the 3.33 GHz line is the k ≈ 0 band bottom.

11. **Phase definition.** Eq. (2) should read φ = arg(I + iQ) (or atan2(Q, I)), not arctan(I + iQ).

12. **Inconsistent statements about detection.** "The detection path is not sensitive to the parametrically introduced magnons" (p. 5) sits awkwardly with the 3.9 GHz "parametrically pumped magnons" line marked in Fig. 3a. Fig. 3 also says signal "is not registered at all" early on (p. 7), yet Fig. 3b shows a coherence bump at ~0.85 µs during pumping. Clarify both.

13. **Wording.**
   - "Inlays" should be "insets".
   - "Thermalisation section" should be "thermalisation stage".
   - The Methods state that "Supplementary Information is available", but the supplementary section is part of this document. Reconcile the numbering of Extended Data and Section 7.

14. **Data availability.** "Available at reasonable request" (Sec. 7.1) is weak for a claim that rests on statistics over 1000 single shots. Depositing the raw IQ traces would let others repeat the phase and amplitude-statistics analysis.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add one figure that puts side by side the phase histograms (with Rayleigh statistics) for: a phase-locked injected tone, the linear sub-threshold FMR response, and the condensate at the onset of coherence. This single figure would turn the central claim from an assertion into a demonstration.
- Use the existing 1000-shot dataset to compute amplitude histograms and g⁽²⁾(0) versus time and pump power. This is the most direct way to distinguish a condensate from an incoherently overpopulated band-bottom mode.
- Replace or calibrate the coherence value with a physically interpretable quantity, such as single-shot linewidth or first-order coherence time, using synthetic signals with the measured noise.
- Track the instantaneous frequency of each shot, remove the deterministic chirp, and report the residual phase distribution and the spread of the onset time.

5. Verdict: major revision
