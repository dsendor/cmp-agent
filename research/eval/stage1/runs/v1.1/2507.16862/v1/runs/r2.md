Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors pump a 2.1 µm out-of-plane magnetized YIG film parametrically at 7.8 GHz and record the microwave signal near the bottom of the spin-wave band (~3.3 GHz) single-shot with an IQ mixer referenced to a local oscillator. They claim two results. First, the phase of the condensate relative to an external reference is uniformly distributed over repeated shots, which they read as direct evidence of spontaneous symmetry breaking. Second, an autocorrelation-based "coherence value" rises above a pump-power threshold and persists after the pump is switched off. Measuring the order-parameter phase of a quasiparticle condensate shot by shot, in the time domain, is an attractive idea and could be a useful experimental tool. In its present form, however, the central phase-randomness claim lacks the reference controls needed to separate it from instrumental or deterministic dephasing, and the coherence metric is not calibrated.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Single-shot, phase-resolved detection of the band-bottom magnon signal is shown in Fig. 2 and Extended Data Fig. 6b. Signal amplitude and phase are available within each shot, so no averaging over repetitions is needed.
- The IQ-based spectrogram (Extended Data Fig. 6c) is compared with spectrum-analyser data (Extended Data Fig. 6a). This is a useful consistency check of the detection chain.
- A clear pump-power threshold appears in both amplitude and coherence (Fig. 4: no change at 20 dBm, onset at 21–22 dBm, saturation of the coherence value at about 23 dBm).
- The question is well posed. Prior magnon-BEC experiments showed coherence through interference, supercurrents or Josephson oscillations, but did not test whether the phase is set spontaneously or imprinted by the pump. A clean test of that would interest the community.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The phase reference and the phase stability of the detection chain are not demonstrated. The main claim (Fig. 2 insets; Sec. 1.2, p. 5–6; Discussion) is that the uniform phase histogram at t = 1.25 µs reflects a random condensate phase, because "all externally induced factors are stable within a few degrees of phase shift". No data support this. Extended Data Fig. 5 states that the oscilloscope "was excluded [from the shared 10 MHz reference] due to technical difficulties, but phase stability of the setup as a whole was confirmed in preliminary measurements."
   - Why trigger timing matters: with a 3.2 GHz LO and a condensate at ~3.33 GHz, the IF is about 130 MHz. The phase extracted at a fixed oscilloscope time sample therefore depends on trigger timing. A trigger or sampling jitter of about 1 ns between acquisitions shifts the phase by about 0.8 rad, and a few nanoseconds of jitter alone would produce a near-uniform histogram.
   - What is needed is a null control measured with the same chain, trigger and analysis: a deterministic signal at ~3.3 GHz whose phase should be fixed shot to shot. Candidate signals are a pulsed tone from a generator on the shared 10 MHz reference, or pump leakage, or a linearly driven ferromagnetic-resonance (FMR) response of the film at the band-bottom frequency. The authors should show that its phase histogram over 1000 shots is narrow (a few degrees, as claimed).
   - The width of that reference histogram, together with the trigger/sampling scheme and the IF frequency, should be reported in the paper. Without this control the uniform histograms cannot be attributed to the condensate.

2. A random phase at a fixed lab time does not establish a random initial phase. The insets of Fig. 2 show the phase distribution at fixed times (0.10, 0.90, 1.25 µs), so each value is the phase accumulated since pump onset.
   - Why deterministic dynamics can mimic randomness: the condensate frequency drifts strongly in time (Fig. 3a: ~3.4 GHz during pumping, relaxing to ~3.33 GHz after pump-off) through the nonlinear shift, which depends on magnon density. A shot-to-shot spread of only ~1 MHz in mean frequency, integrated over ~1 µs, gives a phase spread of 2π. Small pump-power or density fluctuations could produce such a spread. A fully deterministic, pump-seeded process would therefore also give a uniform histogram at t = 1.25 µs.
   - What would separate the two:
     - (i) Show the phase-difference distribution between two closely spaced times within a shot, and the phase at condensation onset (the earliest time with adequate SNR, ~0.6–0.8 µs), not only at 1.25 µs.
     - (ii) Report the shot-to-shot spread of the instantaneous frequency and amplitude, and test whether the phase correlates with them. Alternatively, de-chirp each shot using its measured frequency trajectory and examine the residual initial phase.
     - (iii) Apply a quantitative uniformity test (for example a Rayleigh test, or the circular mean resultant length with its expected value for N = 1000) instead of visual inspection of the rose plots.
     - (iv) Compare with a case where phase locking is expected. Directly parametrically excited magnons at ωp/2 should show the two-fold (φ, φ+π) phase degeneracy relative to the pump. Showing that this bimodal structure is visible in the same setup, and disappears for the condensate, would be a convincing positive control.

B. Should address

3. The coherence value is uncalibrated against signal-to-noise ratio, which undermines the claims about coherence during decay. The metric (Sec. 1.3, Sec. 3.2) is the integral of |autocorrelation| over a 100 ns window, normalized to that of a perfect tone.
   - Inconsistent zero: the text says 0 corresponds to white noise, but the noise-only baseline in Figs. 3b and 4a is about 0.18.
   - Ceiling below 1: the maximum (~0.75–0.8) stays well below 1 even at the highest amplitudes.
   - The metric mixes SNR, linewidth within the window and frequency chirp. The authors themselves attribute the dip near 1 µs to chirp.
   - The claims "the coherence of the BEC does not depend on the absolute amplitude" and "BEC doesn't lose its coherent properties during decay" (Fig. 4 caption; p. 7–8) are not supported as stated. In Fig. 4a the coherence value falls steadily from ~0.75 at 1.8 µs to ~0.22 at 3.5 µs, close to the 0.18 noise floor. "Clear remanence in coherence" at 3.5 µs then means a ~0.04 excess, with no statistical significance given.
   - Requested: calibrate the metric with a synthetic or measured tone of known linewidth plus the measured noise, at the SNRs of the experiment. This gives coherence value versus SNR for a perfectly coherent signal, so the decay in Fig. 4a can be compared with the pure-SNR expectation.
   - Also requested: report the condensate linewidth or coherence time directly (e.g. from g1(τ) out to τ > 100 ns, or from the per-shot spectral width), and state the significance of the 3.5 µs excess.

4. Identification of the coherent state as a BEC, as opposed to other noise-seeded coherent states, is not argued in this paper.
   - A random phase arising from noise is necessary for a BEC but not unique to it. Any instability that grows from thermal fluctuations has the same property: a parametric oscillator above threshold (for the non-degenerate part of the phase), kinetic instability, or lasing-like accumulation in a mode.
   - The paper cites a comment (ref. 31) disputing BEC in perpendicularly magnetized YIG, but does not address it.
   - Evidence the BEC interpretation rests on in this paper is limited to spectrograms showing accumulation at the band bottom (Fig. 3a, Extended Data Fig. 6a), plus reference to earlier work (ref. 41).
   - Requested: give an estimate of the magnon density relative to the critical density at the 21 dBm threshold. State the parametric instability threshold compared with the condensation threshold. Present evidence for thermalisation, such as the populated band shape before condensation, or the time delay of the band-bottom signal after pump onset and its power dependence.
   - Alternatively, scope the claims (title, abstract "confirm all postulated fundamental properties", Discussion "proof of the formation of a true BEC") to the spontaneous phase of the band-bottom coherent state.

5. Calibration of the IQ mixer and the effect of the band-limited detection on the phase statistics.
   - IQ gain and phase imbalance and DC offsets distort the extracted phase and can bias the angular histogram toward particular angles. Extended Data Fig. 6c shows mixer artefacts: a constant band at 3.77 GHz and a "shadow" between 3.2 and 3.3 GHz.
   - The authors should describe how I/Q imbalance and offsets were measured and corrected. They should also show that the shadow band (where the image of the condensate could fall) does not affect the phase extraction.
   - The detection band is filtered to < 4 GHz and limited to 500 MHz. The authors should state which frequency range enters the 100 ns window analysis at each time, because during pumping the signal at 3.4 GHz may contain contributions from the non-condensed gas.

6. Only one sample, one bias field (281 mT), one pump frequency and one pulse duration are reported.
   - Whether the phase randomness and the coherence threshold reproduce on another film, or another position on the same film, is not stated.
   - The repetition rate of the 1000 shots and the waiting time between pulses are not given. Residual magnons, or heating from 25 dBm pulses, could carry over between shots.
   - Requested: give these parameters. At minimum, show that the phase result (Fig. 2) holds at a second pump power above threshold (e.g. 25 dBm) and preferably at a second field.

7. The novelty relative to earlier work is not stated precisely.
   - Ref. 35 (Demidov et al., PRL 2008) is titled "Observation of spontaneous coherence in Bose–Einstein condensate of magnons", and ref. 24 treats the onset of phase coherence in condensates. Both are cited, but the Main text says the spontaneous emergence of coherence "has been impossible to observe".
   - The authors should explain specifically what earlier experiments could not establish (e.g. phase relative to the pump) and what this measurement adds. They should temper "For the first time" and "no direct proof of randomness... has yet been provided" accordingly.

C. Minor

8. Eq. (2) defines φ(t) = arctan(I + iQ). This should be arg(I + iQ) or atan2(Q, I).
9. The Fig. 2b phase axis labels read "π/2" twice. One should be "−π/2".
10. Fig. 2 caption and text disagree on when the histograms are taken: "at t0" (p. 5) versus "at the beginning of each frame". The caption sentence "Amplitude denotes the percentage of measurements in this cluster" should say what bin width was used.
11. Figs. 3b and 4: state whether the shaded band is the standard deviation across shots of a per-shot coherence value, or a standard error. Also state whether the coherence value is computed per shot and then averaged.
12. Sec. 4 says "Supplementary Information is available", but the supplementary material appears as Sec. 7 of the same document. Sec. 3.1 calls the IQ-mixer section "supplementary material Section 7.2".
13. The statement that the oscilloscope was excluded from the shared clock (Extended Data Fig. 5) should appear in Methods, not only in a figure caption, because it bears directly on the main claim (see concern 1).
14. Give basic sample and measurement parameters in Methods: film growth and substrate, sample temperature, the microstrip and film dimensions (Extended Data Fig. 5 gives a 100 µm strip), and the pump power actually reaching the antenna, as opposed to generator output power.
15. Sec. 1.2 says that "after the pump is switched off, condensation occurs". This conflicts with the earlier statement that thermalisation and condensation begin during pumping, and with Fig. 3a, where band-bottom accumulation is visible before 1 µs. The authors should clarify.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a single figure combining the reference-signal phase histogram (null control), the condensate phase histogram at onset, and a quantitative uniformity statistic. This would make the central claim self-contained.
- Use the per-shot frequency trajectories to separate the random initial phase from accumulated phase. Plotting phase versus instantaneous frequency for 1000 shots would show directly whether the randomness is intrinsic.
- Calibrate the coherence value against SNR and replace the qualitative statements about coherence during decay with a coherence time or linewidth.
- Show the parametrically pumped ωp/2 magnons in the same phase-resolved scheme as a phase-locked counterpart to the condensate.

5. Verdict: major revision
