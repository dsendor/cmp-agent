Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors pump a 2.1-µm YIG film magnetized out of plane through a 100-µm microstrip antenna at 7.8 GHz. They record the inductive signal of the k ≈ 0 magnon condensate in single shots, phase-referenced through an IQ mixer to a 3.2-GHz local oscillator that shares a 10 MHz reference with the pump. From 1,000 repetitions they show that the condensate phase is uniformly distributed from shot to shot while each shot becomes monochromatic, and they read this as direct evidence of spontaneous U(1) symmetry breaking in a quasiparticle BEC. Single-shot, phase-resolved electrical access to a magnon condensate is new and useful. Measuring the shot-to-shot phase distribution answers a real question in the magnon-BEC literature: whether the coherence is imposed by the pump. Whether the data show that the phase is set spontaneously *at condensation* depends on a control that is currently missing (concern 1).

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- It is an experimental method new to this field: single-shot, phase-locked detection of the condensate's precession, with the IQ data cross-checked against a spectrum analyser (Extended Data Fig. 2).
- It addresses a long-standing objection to magnon BEC: that coherence could be inherited from the coherent pump. The out-of-plane geometry puts the condensate at k ≈ 0, where an antenna can detect it.
- The pump's phase distribution is shown alongside the condensate's (grey sectors, Fig. 2a), giving a reference for the stability of the set-up.
- The pump-power series (Fig. 4) gives a threshold of about 21 dBm, and coherence persists after the pump is switched off.
- The data are deposited on Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **A uniform phase histogram at a fixed lab time does not, by itself, show that the phase was chosen at random when the condensate formed.**
   - *What the paper shows.* The central evidence (Fig. 2a insets; Discussion) is that the measured phase is uniform at t = 1.25 µs across 1,000 shots. The condensate oscillates at about 3.3–3.35 GHz, with a frequency set by magnon density through the nonlinear shift. The text itself attributes the post-pump downward drift (Fig. 3a) to the decaying density.
   - *Why it is not enough.* Shot-to-shot fluctuations in pump power, in the onset time of condensation or in the density all change the instantaneous frequency. A frequency jitter of only about 1 MHz, accumulated over the roughly 0.5–1 µs between condensation onset and the analysis time, smears the phase over 2π. That happens even if every shot's phase were fixed deterministically by the pump (for example through the pair-phase relation of parametric pumping and four-magnon scattering).
   - *Why the existing check does not settle it.* Pump phase stable "within a few degrees" rules out drift in the apparatus. It does not rule out this mechanism, because the frequency of the condensate, unlike the pump's, depends on the state of the sample.
   - *What to do:*
     - (i) Extract the instantaneous frequency of each shot (the data in Fig. 2b already allow this). Report its spread across the ensemble, and estimate the phase dispersion it would produce over the relevant time.
     - (ii) Remove the frequency effect. Either extrapolate each shot's phase back to the onset of condensation, or compute the phase relative to the integrated phase ∫ω(t)dt, or condition on shots with matched frequency and onset time. Then show that the residual phase is still uniform.
     - (iii) Show the histogram at the earliest time at which the coherence is clearly above baseline (around 0.8–0.9 µs), when little phase has had time to accumulate.
     - (iv) Quantify uniformity with a Rayleigh or Kuiper test, and give the value expected for a deterministic phase plus the measured jitter.

   Without this analysis, the headline claim of spontaneous phase selection is not separated from a deterministic phase blurred by frequency fluctuations.

B. Should address

2. **The coherence value is confounded with signal-to-noise ratio, so the threshold in Fig. 4a may be partly a detection threshold.**
   - *What the paper says.* The coherence metric (Methods, equation for 𝒞_w) has a finite noise baseline of about 0.18. The paper itself attributes the late-time fall in coherence to "deterioration of the signal-to-noise ratio".
   - *Why it matters.* At 20–22 dBm the signal amplitude is a few mV (Fig. 4b), so low coherence there may reflect noise rather than the absence of a condensate.
   - *What to do.* Calibrate the metric against SNR: compute 𝒞_w for a synthetic perfectly coherent tone with the measured noise added, at each amplitude. Then compare the powers at matched amplitude. The statement that coherence "does not depend on the absolute amplitude" (Fig. 4 caption) would then be quantitative.

3. **Temporal coherence is not the same as condensation.**
   - *What the metric measures.* The 100-ns autocorrelation measures how monochromatic the signal is within a window. Its frequency resolution is about 10 MHz, so any narrowband oscillation scores high: for example, ringing of the Kittel mode after the abrupt pump switch-off, or a kinetic-instability-driven bottom mode.
   - *Why it matters.* A spontaneously chosen phase is also expected for any classical self-oscillation seeded by noise.
   - *What to do.* Give evidence specific to BEC. For example, show that the onset tracks a critical density rather than a pump-power threshold for an instability, or show the spectral occupation thermalizing toward the band bottom before the coherence rises. Alternatively, temper the wording "confirming a central prediction of Bose–Einstein condensation theory".

4. **The claim that the BEC "does not undergo dephasing" until it disappears is not tested.**
   - *What the paper claims.* Section text near Fig. 4: coherence is "still detectable" at 3.5 µs, below the noise threshold.
   - *Why it matters.* Given the nonzero baseline, this needs a statistical comparison with the pre-pump baseline.
   - *What to do.* Give a significance level. Separately, compare the decay of coherence with the amplitude decay predicted from SNR (see concern 2).

5. **IQ artefacts sit at the condensate frequency.**
   - *What the paper shows.* Extended Data Fig. 2c shows a constant band at 3.77 GHz and a "shadow between 3.2 GHz and 3.3 GHz" from the IQ technique. The latter overlaps the condensate frequency.
   - *What to do.* Show that this background does not raise the coherence values or bias the phase histograms. For example, give 𝒞_w and the phase distribution for pump-off or below-threshold shots after the same filtering.

6. **The timing chain has an unresolved gap.**
   - *What the paper shows.* The oscilloscope is not locked to the shared 10 MHz reference, and phase stability "was confirmed in preliminary measurements" (Extended Data Fig. 1 caption) that are not shown.
   - *What is unclear.* It is not stated how the pump phase (grey sectors, Fig. 2a) is measured, given the 5-GHz low-pass filter before the mixer. It is also not stated whether that measurement goes through the same acquisition and trigger path as the condensate signal.
   - *What to do.* Describe this measurement, and show the phase distribution of a reference CW tone near 3.3 GHz injected through the same path. That would bound trigger jitter at the relevant beat frequency.

7. **There is a single sample at a single field and temperature, with no reproducibility data.**
   - *What the paper shows.* All data come from one YIG film at 281 mT and room temperature.
   - *What to do.* State whether the result was reproduced after re-mounting or in a second film. A second bias field, which moves the band bottom, would also show that the signal follows the dispersion minimum.

8. **The oscillations in the coherence plateau are not explained.**
   - *What the paper shows.* Fig. 3b and 4a show oscillations in the coherence plateau. Fig. 3c(ii) shows side lobes at 0.9 µs, suggesting several frequency components. The authors' suggestion of reflected magnons is speculative.
   - *Why it matters.* The antenna probes only about 100 µm of a 9.5 mm × 2.7 mm film, so the measured coherence is local. A multi-domain or spatially varying condensate cannot be excluded.
   - *What to do.* Discuss this limitation. Test the reflection hypothesis by varying the antenna position or the sample size.

C. Minor

9. The Fig. 2 time-axis tick labels repeat because of rounding ("0.10, 0.10, 0.11, 0.12, 0.12…"; "1.25, 1.25, 1.26…"). Use more digits.
10. "standard derivation" should read "standard deviation" in the captions of Fig. 4 and Extended Data Fig. 2. "Suppementary" in the main text should read "Supplementary".
11. Methods: define the "vector normalization" (the overbar) precisely. State whether 𝒞_w is computed shot by shot and then averaged, or on averaged quantities.
12. Fig. 2a inset: state the bin width and the expected per-bin fluctuation for 1,000 shots, so readers can judge uniformity.
13. The phrase "direct proof of randomness" (Discussion) should be "direct evidence", in line with the abstract.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Present the per-shot frequency spread, together with the phase histograms after removing the frequency effect (concern 1). This is the single analysis that would make the central claim convincing.
- Add an SNR calibration curve for 𝒞_w, using synthetic coherent signals with the measured noise.
- Add a control with a deterministic phase, such as a weak resonant 3.3-GHz seed applied before condensation. Showing that a seed locks the phase while its absence gives a uniform distribution would demonstrate that the method can detect a phase reference when one exists.
- Show a second bias field or a second sample.

5. Verdict: major revision
