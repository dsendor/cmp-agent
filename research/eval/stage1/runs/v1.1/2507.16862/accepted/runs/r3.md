Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use inductive, phase-referenced (IQ-mixer) single-shot detection of a parametrically pumped magnon gas in an out-of-plane magnetized YIG film. The out-of-plane geometry puts the condensate at k ≈ 0, where a 100-µm microstrip antenna can pick it up. With this set-up they follow the phase and a windowed-autocorrelation "coherence value" of the k ≈ 0 precession through pumping, thermalization and free decay. The headline result is that the condensate's phase is uniformly distributed over 1,000 shots while the pump phase stays fixed (Fig. 2a), which the authors read as spontaneous U(1) symmetry breaking. Tracking a quasiparticle condensate's phase shot by shot in real time is new and of broad interest. The experimental idea is simple and well suited to the question.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A direct, single-shot phase measurement of a magnon condensate, without the ensemble averaging that limits Brillouin light scattering. The out-of-plane geometry makes k ≈ 0 condensation accessible to inductive detection.
- A large ensemble (1,000 shots per condition) with phase histograms at several stages (Fig. 2a), and a reference sector for the pump phase measured in the same run.
- The single-shot data show clear behaviour: noise-like phase before condensation and a linear phase ramp afterwards (Fig. 2b). Spectrum-analyser and IQ data are cross-checked against each other (Extended Data Fig. 2).
- A pump-power series (Fig. 4) shows a threshold between 20 and 21 dBm and a coherence plateau that persists while the amplitude decays.
- Data and analysis code are available through Zenodo.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The uniform phase histogram is not yet shown to reflect a random condensate phase, as opposed to shot-to-shot variation in frequency or timing, and the method is not shown to detect a non-random phase. The phase in Fig. 2 is measured at a fixed lab time relative to a 3.2-GHz local oscillator. The condensate frequency is 120–250 MHz above the LO and drifts from about 3.45 to 3.32 GHz during condensation (Fig. 3a, Extended Data Fig. 2a,c). So the phase at t = 1.25 µs is φ0 + ∫ω(t′)dt′, which integrates the whole frequency history of that shot. Suppose condensation onset or the nonlinear frequency shift varies by only ~10 ns, or by a few MHz over ~1 µs, from shot to shot, as is likely given the noise-seeded kinetics and pump-power fluctuations. The accumulated phase then spreads by several radians, and the histogram would be uniform even if the condensate's initial phase were fixed relative to the pump. The grey "pump phase" sectors show that the reference chain is stable at the pump frequency. They do not address this mechanism, and the text does not explain how the pump phase is measured after the pump has been filtered out. The oscilloscope is also not locked to the shared 10-MHz reference (Extended Data Fig. 1 caption), and phase stability is only "confirmed in preliminary measurements" that are not shown. The paper's central claim (abstract; Discussion: "show independence of the established condensate phase from any external sources") rests on this histogram, so the authors should:
   - (a) Quantify the per-shot spread of condensation onset time and instantaneous frequency (both available from the single-shot IQ data). From these, estimate the phase dispersion that the frequency history alone would produce at t = 1.25 µs.
   - (b) Determine each shot's phase at a reference point tied to the condensate itself, for example extrapolated from the linear phase ramp back to the onset time or to the pump-off time. Show that this phase is also uniform.
   - (c) Give a positive control through the identical chain and oscilloscope settings: a CW or pulsed tone at about 3.3 GHz derived from the locked sources should give a narrow histogram at the same delays. Better still, seed the k ≈ 0 mode with a weak phase-locked tone before or during pumping, and show the condensate phase distribution becoming non-uniform as the seed grows.
   - (d) Quote a uniformity statistic (Rayleigh or Kuiper test, mean resultant length) for each histogram in Fig. 2a.

B. Should address

2. A random phase is necessary for a condensate but does not identify one. Any instability that grows from noise is expected to give a random phase: a laser or optical parametric oscillator above threshold, the parametric instability itself, or the kinetic instability that directly populates the band bottom. In parametric pumping, the pump fixes only the sum φ_k + φ_−k (as the paper notes on p. 1432). Four-magnon scattering into k ≈ 0 again fixes only phase sums, so randomness of φ0 follows even without thermalization. The paper calls the result "evidence of spontaneous symmetry breaking … confirming a central prediction of Bose–Einstein condensation theory" and proof of a "true BEC" (abstract, Discussion). It offers no evidence that the gas thermalizes towards a Bose–Einstein distribution in this geometry, beyond the spectrogram settling at the band bottom (Fig. 3a). Condensation in perpendicularly magnetized YIG has been disputed (ref. 32). The authors should either support the thermalized-condensate interpretation more directly or state the narrower claim: the coherent k ≈ 0 state forms with a spontaneously chosen phase. Direct support could be the population distribution over energy (for example BLS on the same film), the time scale of thermalization against the four-magnon scattering time, or the dependence of the threshold on the pumped density. In the narrower version, the paper should explain what would distinguish a BEC from kinetic-instability generation within this measurement.

3. The "coherence value" mixes signal-to-noise ratio with coherence, and its window limits what it can show. The metric is the normalized |autocorrelation| integrated over a 100-ns boxcar (Methods). Its noise baseline is about 0.18, and it falls as the amplitude decays (Figs. 3b, 4a). The paper uses it both for "emergence of coherence" and for claims that need it to be independent of amplitude: "the coherence of the BEC does not depend on the absolute amplitude" (Fig. 4 caption); "the BEC does not undergo dephasing"; "a clear remanence in coherence" at 3.5 µs (p. 1434). These statements pull against each other. Two further limits apply. A 100-ns window cannot detect dephasing on longer time scales. The metric is also lowered by the known frequency chirp after pump-off (p. 1434), independently of phase coherence. The authors should:
   - calibrate the metric against synthetic sinusoids, with and without chirp, plus the measured noise at the SNR of each curve;
   - give a significance level for the late-time values in Fig. 4a (for example about 0.25 against the 0.18 baseline);
   - report a window-independent single-shot quantity, such as linewidth, phase-noise spectrum or coherence time from the phase ramps in Fig. 2b, to support "does not undergo dephasing".

4. The parameter range is narrow and only one sample is reported. All results come from one 2.1-µm YIG film at one field (281 mT), one pump frequency (7.8 GHz) and room temperature. Only pump power is varied (Fig. 4). Field alignment is not given, although the k ≈ 0 band minimum depends on the field being normal to the film; a small tilt moves the minimum to finite k. The parametric instability threshold is not given either, so it is unclear whether 21 dBm marks condensation or simply the onset of parametric pumping. The authors should report:
   - the parametric threshold, measured with the microwave detector already in the set-up;
   - the field tilt and its uncertainty;
   - at least one further field, or a second sample, showing the phase histograms and the coherence onset.

5. The behaviour of the parametric magnons in the detection window is not fully explained. The text says magnons at about 18 µm would be detected with roughly 18-fold reduced amplitude, and that "no coherent signal at the parametric frequency is observed" (p. 1432). However:
   - Fig. 3a labels a feature at about 3.9 GHz as "parametrically pumped magnons".
   - The receive chain contains a diplexer that splits at about 4 GHz and low-pass filters (Extended Data Fig. 1), so suppression by the filters near f_p/2 = 3.9 GHz is a competing explanation.
   - The IQ spectrogram (Extended Data Fig. 2c) shows an artefact band at 3.77 GHz.

   The authors should show the transfer function of the receive chain around 3.3–4 GHz. They should also say whether the absence of a coherent f_p/2 signal comes from the physics or from the filtering, since the paper uses it to argue that the pumped magnons carry no global phase.

6. The oscillations in the coherence plateau are left unexplained. The roughly 0.3-µs oscillations in coherence and amplitude (Figs. 3b, 4) are attributed speculatively to magnons reflected from the sample edges. This could be tested from the sample dimensions and group velocities, or by moving the antenna or changing the film size. As it stands, it is also unclear whether these oscillations affect the phase histograms at particular delays.

C. Minor

7. Heating. A 1-µs pump at up to 25 dBm can heat the film locally; ref. 27 shows that temperature shifts the band frequency. Please give the repetition rate and duty cycle. Please also comment on whether part of the downward frequency shift after pump-off (Fig. 3a caption) is thermal rather than due to the falling magnon number.

8. Fig. 2. The time-axis tick labels repeat ("0.10 0.10 0.11 0.12 0.12 0.13", and similarly in the other panels) because of rounding; use more digits or relative time. State the histogram bin width. Explain how the pump phase (grey sectors) was obtained, since the pump is said to be filtered out.

9. Fig. 3c and the Methods. State whether the "perfect coherence" reference is computed with the same sampling and window as the data. Explain why the measured autocorrelation at t_i = 0.1 µs has a central peak of 1 even for pure noise; this follows from the normalization, but it affects how the baseline should be read.

10. Statistics. Figs. 3 and 4 show the mean ± s.d. over 1,000 shots. The standard error, or a bootstrap confidence interval on the mean, would be more appropriate for comparing curves. "Standard derivation" should read "standard deviation" (Fig. 4 and Extended Data Fig. 2 captions).

11. The audio rendering of the waveform (p. 1433, Supplementary Section 3) is illustrative only and could move entirely to the Supplementary.

12. Typos: "Suppementary" and "osicllator" (p. 1433). "Standard derivation" appears in several captions.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a seeded-condensate experiment: a weak, phase-locked tone injected at the band-bottom frequency. Showing the phase histogram change from uniform to peaked as the seed grows would make the spontaneity claim much stronger than any argument based on the equipment.
- Report a per-shot phase at the onset of condensation, together with the distribution of onset times and frequencies. This separates spontaneous phase choice from phase accumulated through frequency jitter.
- Replace or supplement the windowed autocorrelation with single-shot linewidth or phase-noise analysis of the condensate, normalized for SNR.
- Frame the conclusion as spontaneous phase selection of the k ≈ 0 coherent state, and set out what additional evidence ties it to a thermalized Bose–Einstein condensate rather than a kinetic instability.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
