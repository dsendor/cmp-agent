A. Must address

1. The uniform phase histogram does not yet show that the condensate phase is chosen spontaneously when the condensate forms, and the method is not shown to detect a non-random phase. The central claim (abstract; Discussion: "show independence of the established condensate phase from any external sources") rests on the phase histograms at t = 1.25 µs over 1,000 shots (Fig. 2a insets). That phase is measured at a fixed lab time against a 3.2-GHz local oscillator. The condensate sits 120–250 MHz above the LO and its frequency drifts from about 3.45 to about 3.32 GHz during formation (Fig. 3a; Extended Data Fig. 2a,c) through the density-dependent nonlinear shift, so the measured phase is φ0 + ∫ω(t′)dt′ and integrates the whole frequency history of each shot. A 1% shot-to-shot spread in magnon density changes the frequency by about 1 MHz; over the 0.3–1 µs between onset and the analysis time this spreads the phase by ~2 rad or more. Jitter in the condensation onset time does the same (a 1.6 ns jitter at the ~100 MHz intermediate frequency gives about 1 rad; ~10 ns gives several radians). Pump-power fluctuations and the noise-seeded kinetics make such jitter likely. A uniform histogram would therefore result even if each shot's phase were fixed deterministically by the pump, for example through the pair-phase relation of parametric pumping and four-magnon scattering. The grey pump-phase sectors, stable "within a few degrees", rule out drift of the apparatus at the pump frequency but not this mechanism, because the condensate frequency, unlike the pump's, depends on the state of the sample. The timing chain also has gaps: the oscilloscope is not locked to the shared 10 MHz reference "due to technical difficulties", and phase stability "was confirmed in preliminary measurements" that are not shown (Extended Data Fig. 1 caption). Nor does the paper say how the 7.8 GHz pump phase was measured, given that the diplexer and the 5-GHz low-pass filters remove the pump before the IQ mixer, or whether that measurement uses the same acquisition and trigger path as the condensate signal. The authors should: (i) extract each shot's onset time and instantaneous frequency from the single-shot IQ data (Fig. 2b), report their spread across the ensemble and estimate the phase dispersion the frequency history alone would produce at 1.25 µs; (ii) remove the dynamical phase — reference each shot's phase to the onset of coherence or the pump-off time (for example by extrapolating the linear phase ramp), subtract ∫ω(t)dt using the per-shot instantaneous frequency, or condition on shots with matched frequency and onset — and show that the residual phase is still uniform; (iii) show histograms at the earliest time the coherence is clearly above baseline (about 0.8–0.9 µs); (iv) add a positive control through the identical chain and oscilloscope settings, such as a CW or pulsed tone near 3.3 GHz derived from the locked sources (ideally chirped like the condensate signal) or a directly excited non-parametric linear mode, and show that its histogram is sharply peaked; (v) ideally, seed the k ≈ 0 mode with a weak phase-locked tone during thermalization and show the condensate phase distribution becoming peaked as the seed grows; (vi) quantify uniformity for each histogram with a Rayleigh or Kuiper test or the mean resultant length with confidence intervals, and give the value expected for a deterministic phase plus the measured jitter; (vii) describe the pump-phase measurement. Histograms of phase differences between two times within the same shot would further separate single-shot phase rigidity from randomness across shots.

B. Should address

2. The identification of the coherent k ≈ 0 state as a thermodynamic BEC, and of the random phase as BEC-specific, goes further than the data (abstract; pp. 1431–1432; Discussion). The evidence for condensation is the spectrogram settling at the band bottom (Fig. 3a) and a pump-power threshold; the magnon population across k or energy is not shown. A random phase is expected for any instability that grows from noise — a laser or optical parametric oscillator above threshold, a classical self-oscillator, the parametric instability itself, or the kinetic instability that the paper names as a redistribution channel and which can populate the band bottom directly without thermalization. Since the pump fixes only φ_k + φ_−k (p. 1432) and four-magnon scattering into k ≈ 0 again fixes only phase sums, randomness of φ0 follows even without a thermalized gas. The 100-ns autocorrelation metric, with ~10 MHz resolution, would also score any narrowband oscillation highly, for example Kittel-mode ringing after the abrupt pump switch-off or a kinetic-instability-driven bottom mode. Condensation in perpendicularly magnetized YIG films is also contested (ref. 32). The statements that the work confirms "a central prediction of Bose–Einstein condensation theory" and shows a "true BEC" therefore need either more support or narrower wording. The authors should add BEC-specific evidence — population distribution over energy (for example BLS on the same film or prior work in the same film), the thermalization time against the four-magnon scattering time, an onset that tracks a critical density rather than a pump-power instability threshold, the power dependence of the onset time and of phase uniformity near threshold (21–22 dBm), or a kinetic or stochastic (Gross–Pitaevskii / Landau–Lifshitz) simulation reproducing onset, coherence and phase statistics. Otherwise they should restate the claim as spontaneous phase selection of a noise-seeded coherent k ≈ 0 state and explicitly discuss what would distinguish a BEC from kinetic-instability generation in this measurement.

3. The coherence value is not calibrated and is confounded with signal-to-noise ratio, so the threshold in Fig. 4a may partly be a detection threshold (Methods "Data analysis", equation for 𝒞_w; Figs. 3b, 4a). The metric, the normalized |autocorrelation| integrated over a 100-ns boxcar, measures spectral narrowness within one window and should fall as SNR drops; the paper itself attributes the late-time fall to "deterioration of the signal-to-noise ratio". Yet the Fig. 4 caption says coherence "does not depend on the absolute amplitude". Fig. 4a partly contradicts this: peak coherence at 22 dBm is about 0.45 against about 0.77 at 23 and 25 dBm, and at 20–22 dBm the amplitude is only a few mV (Fig. 4b). The noise baseline of about 0.18 and the plateau at about 0.75 rather than 1 are not explained, and the metric is also lowered by the known frequency chirp after pump-off (p. 1434) independently of phase coherence. The authors should calibrate 𝒞_w with synthetic or measured signals of known linewidth, chirp and SNR (including a perfectly coherent tone plus the measured noise at each curve's amplitude) passed through the same chain, compare powers at matched amplitude, explain the 0.18 floor and the 0.75 plateau, and report how the results change with window length (for example 50, 100 and 200 ns).

4. IQ artefacts lie at or next to the condensate frequency (Extended Data Fig. 2c). The IQ technique produces a constant tone at 3.77 GHz and a "shadow between 3.2 GHz and 3.3 GHz"; the condensate is at about 3.32 GHz. A constant tone is itself coherent and could raise the coherence baseline or bias the phase histograms. The authors should show 𝒞_w and the phase distributions with the artefacts filtered out or subtracted, and for pump-off or below-threshold shots processed identically.

5. The claims that the condensate "does not undergo dephasing" until it disappears and keeps "a clear remanence in coherence" at 3.5 µs are not supported by the analysis (Fig. 4; pp. 1434–1435). A 100-ns window cannot detect dephasing on longer timescales. At 3.5 µs Fig. 4a shows about 0.2–0.25 against a noise floor of about 0.18, with overlapping standard-deviation bands at some powers, and the amplitude is below the noise floor there. The authors should test the late-time values statistically against signal-free (pre-pump) traces processed identically and give a significance level, compare the coherence decay with the decay expected from SNR alone, and report a window-independent single-shot phase-coherence measure — g1(τ) over microsecond delays, the linewidth from long windows, a phase-noise spectrum, or a coherence time from the phase ramps in Fig. 2b — compared with the amplitude decay time.

6. The absence of a coherent response at f_p/2 is used to argue that the pumped magnons carry no global phase, but the detection sensitivity there is not shown (p. 1432; Fig. 3a; Extended Data Fig. 1). 3.9 GHz lies at the edge of the diplexer's "< 4 GHz" port and of the F2 filter band, the paper itself estimates an ~18-fold suppression of 18-µm magnons by the antenna, Fig. 3a labels a feature near 3.9 GHz as "parametrically pumped magnons", and an artefact band sits at 3.77 GHz. Filtering is thus a competing explanation for the missing signal. The authors should show the receive-chain transfer function over 3.3–4 GHz (for example with a calibration tone), give an upper bound on any coherent f_p/2 component, and state whether its absence is physical or instrumental.

7. All data come from one 2.1-µm YIG film at one field (281 mT), one pump frequency (7.8 GHz) and room temperature, with only pump power varied (Methods; Fig. 4). There are no reproducibility data. The authors should state whether the result was reproduced after re-mounting or in a second film or antenna, and show the phase histograms and coherence onset at a second bias field (which moves the band bottom and would show the signal follows the dispersion minimum), pump frequency or sample — or explain why this was not done.

8. Several operating parameters needed to interpret the results are missing (Methods). The pulse repetition rate and duty cycle are not given, yet they determine whether the 1,000 shots are independent: residual magnons or heating from earlier pulses could seed or bias later shots. Local heating by a 1-µs pulse at up to 25 dBm is not discussed, although temperature shifts the band frequency (ref. 27) and could contribute to the downward frequency shift after pump-off (Fig. 3a caption). The reference plane of the quoted pump power (generator, amplifier output or antenna) is not stated. The parametric instability threshold is not given, so it is unclear whether ~21 dBm marks condensation or simply the onset of parametric pumping. The field tilt from the film normal is not given, though a small tilt moves the band minimum to finite k. The authors should report the repetition rate, power at the antenna, the parametric threshold (measurable with the microwave detector already in the set-up), the field tilt and its uncertainty, and comment on heating.

9. The ~0.3-µs oscillations in the coherence and amplitude plateau (Figs. 3b, 4a), together with side lobes in Fig. 3c(ii) at 0.9 µs suggesting several frequency components, are attributed speculatively to magnons reflected from the sample edges. Since the antenna probes only about 100 µm of a 9.5 mm × 2.7 mm film, the measured coherence is local and a multi-domain or spatially varying condensate cannot be excluded. The authors should estimate the round-trip time from the group velocity and the 2.7 or 9.5 mm dimensions and compare it with the oscillation period, or test the hypothesis by moving the antenna or changing the film size; discuss the locality limitation; and state whether the oscillations affect the phase histograms at particular delays.
