Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports low-temperature STM/STS on cleaved Ta2Pd3Te5, a proposed excitonic insulator (EI). Surface Te vacancies produce a pair of in-gap resonances (E_e, E_h). The authors interpret these as excitonic pair-breaking states, the EI analogue of Yu-Shiba-Rusinov states, and support this with their dependence on defect type, tip current and temperature, their spatial maps, dimer splitting, and a mean-field two-band model. A second, lower-energy pair of subgap states in more electron-doped regions is assigned to pair breaking where the order parameter is locally suppressed. Atomic-scale spectroscopic evidence of impurity pair breaking in an EI would be new and of broad interest, since it would give a local probe of the excitonic condensate. However, the present data do not yet separate this interpretation from ordinary defect levels in a narrow-gap semiconductor that are shifted by the tip. A closely related study (Ref. 41) also appeared during review.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The spectroscopy is of high quality. Decorated-tip imaging resolves the Te1–Te5 chains, and the vacancies are classified as V_Te1–V_Te4 with defect-type statistics for the subgap splitting 2δ (Fig. 2d).
- Several kinds of measurement are brought together: the dependence on tip–sample distance (Fig. 2e), temperature (Fig. 2g), real-space maps along b and c (Fig. 3), dimer splitting (Figs. S10–S11) and spatial inhomogeneity (Fig. 4).
- The reversal of contrast between in-gap maps and the 60 meV continuum map (Fig. 3d–f), and the matching spatial profiles of E_e and E_h (Fig. 3g,h), are interesting observations in their own right.
- A minimal model of an EI with an impurity, including intra- and inter-band scattering, qualitatively reproduces the asymmetry of the subgap peaks and their trends.
- The section contrasting EIs with superconductors (spectral asymmetry, orbital content, impurity sensitivity) is a useful discussion.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The data do not rule out ordinary vacancy-bound states in a gapped band structure.** The paper's central inference is that the vacancy-induced in-gap resonances are *excitonic pair-breaking* states. The Conclusion goes further: the results "in turn confirm that the probed insulating gap at impurity-free region arises from the excitonic pairing" (p. 6). Yet a Te vacancy in any narrow-gap semiconductor or hybridization-gap semimetal generically binds in-gap donor- or acceptor-like states. Each piece of evidence offered is also expected in that picture:
   - Defect-type dependence of the bound-state energy (Fig. 2d) is expected for any defect level.
   - Two peaks on either side of E_F can come from one localized level seen in resonant tunnelling at one polarity and in a tip-induced charging/ionization step at the other. They can also come from two levels of the vacancy (for example, different charge states).
   - E_e and E_h having the same spatial profile (Fig. 3g,h) is expected if both come from the same localized orbital. "Locking" is therefore not specific to Bogoliubov-like e–h mixing.
   - The suppressed continuum near the defect (Fig. 3d) can arise from local band shifts or from the setpoint normalization of the maps (see concern 6).
   - Dimer bonding/antibonding splitting (Fig. S10) is generic for any pair of coupled bound states (cf. Ref. 36, Mn in GaAs).

   The authors themselves note that the EI states show no e–h symmetry, no clear orbital structure, and no selectivity for magnetic over nonmagnetic impurities (pp. 5–6). This removes the main fingerprints that make YSR identification convincing in superconductors.

   To support the claim, the authors need at least one observable that separates the two scenarios, and should state what an ordinary defect level would predict for it. Candidates:
   - a DFT supercell calculation of each V_Te configuration without excitonic order, showing whether the single-particle in-gap levels match the measured 2δ ordering (V_Te2 < V_Te4 < V_Te1 < V_Te3);
   - temperature tracking of the subgap states up to a sizeable fraction of the excitonic transition temperature, where the order parameter collapses but a single-particle defect level would survive (see concern 4);
   - a quantitative correlation, defect by defect, between the local gap Δ and E_e,h, as the pair-breaking picture predicts.

   Without such a test, the conclusion should be narrowed to "consistent with" pair breaking. The statement that the results "confirm" the excitonic origin of the gap should be removed.

2. **Tip-induced band bending and charging could explain the tunability and the "secondary pair breaking".** The abstract states that the subgap energies "are continuously tunable by tip electric field". The authors' model only reproduces the shift with tip approach (Fig. 2e) if |J1,2| *decreases* as the tip approaches, which they call "unexpected" (p. 4). In a semiconducting gap at 1.6 K, the standard explanation for setpoint-dependent defect-state energies is tip-induced band bending (TIBB) together with charging of the defect level, which shifts and broadens the resonances. Two observations point the same way:
   - The lower-energy subgap state appears only above a threshold I_t ≳ 25 nA (Fig. 4e; up to 90 nA at V_b = 0.1 V, i.e. junction resistance around 1 MΩ). Thresholds of this kind are characteristic of tip-induced charging or ionization, not of a change in the order parameter. The assignment of this state to a "secondary pair-breaking effect" with a different order parameter, a headline claim of the abstract, rests largely on this behaviour.
   - Near-contact tunnelling also raises the possibility of local heating and of tip–sample interaction modifying the surface.

   The authors should:
   - separate the effect of tip–sample distance from that of setpoint bias, by measuring at fixed I_t with varying V_b, and at fixed height with varying V_b;
   - show whether the continuum gap edges move with I_t, which TIBB would cause;
   - look in the maps for charging rings or rim features around the vacancy;
   - estimate the magnitude of TIBB from the work functions, the carrier density and the gap.

   If TIBB is significant, the "tunable impurity scattering" and "secondary pair breaking" claims must be reframed.

**B. Should address**

3. **The model is only qualitative, and its parameters are not consistent with the data or with each other.**
   - From Fig. 1(d) the authors extract an experimental gap Δ ≈ 85 meV (and cite ~100 meV from ARPES).
   - The simulations use Δ0 = 35 meV (Fig. 2f), 45 and 18 meV (Fig. 4d) and 30 meV (Fig. 4f), with a chemical potential μ = 12.25 meV.
   - The signs of J1 change between Fig. 2f (J1 < 0) and the lower-energy case in Fig. 4d (J1 = +20 meV).
   - The model is a two-band singlet EI. It has none of the multi-band, spin–orbit-coupled, quasi-1D band structure of Ta2Pd3Te5, which has been discussed as a topological/Dirac system (Refs. 6, 22).

   With Δ0, μ, J1 and J2 all free for each panel, qualitative agreement has limited discriminating power. The authors should:
   - state which parameters are fitted and which are fixed;
   - explain why Δ0 differs from the measured gap by a factor of about two;
   - show that a single parameter set can describe Fig. 2(c–f) for at least one defect type;
   - show the same model's prediction for a non-excitonic (Δ0 = 0, band-gap) reference, so readers can see which features require excitonic order.

4. **The temperature-dependence argument is weak at the temperatures measured.** Fig. 2g extends only to 20 K (k_BT ≈ 1.7 meV), whereas the gap is tens of meV and is reported to persist to far higher temperatures (Refs. 6, 21). "Thermal suppression of excitonic gap" at 20 K should therefore be negligible. The extra broadening beyond Fermi-function convolution is attributed to local gap suppression or "quasiparticle poisoning" without a quantitative estimate. Other contributions are not considered: temperature-dependent tip DOS, lock-in modulation broadening, phonon and lifetime broadening of a localized level, and thermal drift of the setpoint.

   The authors should either extend the measurement to temperatures where Δ itself is appreciably reduced (100 K or above, if feasible) and show the subgap energies following Δ(T), or tone down the claim. They should also state the modulation amplitude and include it in the convolution. Similarly, the "far lower surviving temperature" of the lower-energy states (Fig. S12) should be quantified in the main text.

5. **The vacancy identification and the 2δ statistics need more support.**
   - Vacancies are assigned to Te1–Te4 sites from decorated-tip images alone (Fig. 2b, S2). No simulated STM images (DFT) of each vacancy type are shown, and the text does not explain why Te5 vacancies are absent.
   - The histograms in Fig. 2d overlap strongly, for example V_Te4 (76 meV) and V_Te2 (69 meV). The number of defects per type, the number of tips and samples or cleaves, and the setpoint used are not given, even though 2δ depends on setpoint (Fig. 2e).

   The authors should report N per type, use a fixed setpoint for all histogram spectra (or correct for setpoint), give uncertainties on 2δ_m, and test whether the defect-type ordering is statistically significant. The claim that coupling strength depends on defect configuration depends on this.

6. **The setpoint effect in the dI/dV maps may cause the "spectral-weight transfer".** The contrast reversal between in-gap maps and the 60 meV map (Fig. 3d–f) is offered as evidence of spectral-weight transfer across the gap, likened to superconductors. Constant-current dI/dV maps are normalized by the integrated LDOS up to the setpoint bias. A defect with a large in-gap LDOS will therefore suppress dI/dV at other energies purely through normalization. The authors should give the map setpoints and show the 60 meV suppression either at constant height or with a setpoint above 60 meV. Otherwise this observation should not be used as evidence for pair breaking.

7. **The spatial-inhomogeneity analysis in Fig. 4 is not well quantified.**
   - Type-I and type-II regions are separated by a small shift in the energy of a normal-state feature (arrows in Fig. 4b), and the size of that shift is not stated.
   - The text says type-II regions are more e–h imbalanced, but the Fig. 4a caption says the dashed curve outlines regions with "relatively stronger e–h balance". It is unclear which region is which.
   - The model in Fig. 4f uses Δ(x) = Δ0 tanh(x/x_c) with x_c = 80 b0 ≈ 30 nm. This is a long length scale for a defect-induced suppression, and it is not connected to measured gap maps.

   The authors should provide a map of the local gap (or of the normal-state peak energy) over the area of Fig. 4a and correlate it, defect by defect, with the presence and energy of the lower-energy subgap states.

8. **The magnetic character of the vacancy is not tested.** The discussion (pp. 5–6) argues that nonmagnetic impurities break excitonic pairs regardless of the spin structure of the pairing. However, the magnetic state of a Te vacancy is not determined. Spectra in an applied magnetic field would show whether the subgap peaks Zeeman-split or shift. This would constrain whether the model's non-magnetic impurity is appropriate, and would bear on the spin-singlet versus spin-triplet question the authors raise. If a field-capable system was used (a high-field centre is listed), even one field series would help.

9. **Reproducibility across samples and tips is not reported.** The main text does not say how many crystals, cleaves and tips were used, or whether the defect-type dependence, the tip-approach behaviour and the lower-energy states were reproduced on more than one sample. Fig. S5 is cited for reproducibility of the tip approach for other vacancy types, which is helpful. A brief statement of these numbers should be added.

10. **The relation to Ref. 41 should be discussed.** Ref. 41 reports YSR-like bound states in an exciton condensate. The authors should state briefly how their observations and interpretation agree or differ, so that the novelty claim is clear.

**C. Minor**

11. Gap definition (p. 2): the hole-like edge is taken as the "onset" of non-zero dI/dV (about −35 mV), but the electron-like edge as the coherence peak (about 50 mV). This mixes two criteria. Please use a consistent definition, give an uncertainty, and comment on why the gap is centred below E_F.
12. In Fig. 3g the 10 mV and −45 mV linecuts visibly differ in the tails (|x| > 0.3 nm), even though the text says they "collapse onto the same curve". Please state the normalization used and quantify the agreement, for example with fitted widths for each energy.
13. The abstract says tunability is "by tip electric field", whereas the experiment varies I_t at fixed V_b. Please describe the control parameter as tip–sample distance unless the field is estimated.
14. The Fig. 2e colour bar runs from 0.2 to 8 nA, and Fig. 4e from 5 to 90 nA. Please give the corresponding junction resistances and the lock-in modulation amplitudes in the captions.
15. Fig. 2f legend: give the J1 and J2 values for every curve, not only the endpoints, and state the broadening used in the simulation.
16. Typos and wording: "predicated impurity states" (p. 5, presumably "predicted"); "tends to emerge" → "tend to emerge"; "formulism" → "formalism"; "Nat. Phys, 21" punctuation in Ref. 22.
17. Several key supporting results appear only in the Supplementary Information (dimer, Figs. S10–S11; temperature dependence of the lower-energy states, Fig. S12; spatial simulations, Fig. S8). Consider moving at least the dimer result into the main text, since it is cited as one of the lines of evidence.
18. The statement that the crystal-field splitting is "~100 times smaller than the energy of excitonic gap" (p. 5) needs a reference or a number. Crystal-field splittings in transition-metal tellurides are usually not of order 1 meV.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

- Add a discriminating control against an ordinary defect level: DFT of each vacancy type without excitonic order, and/or temperature tracking of the subgap states up to where Δ is reduced.
- Characterize tip-induced band bending and charging systematically (dependence on setpoint bias, height and current; behaviour of the gap edges; search for charging rings) before attributing the tip-dependent shifts and the lower-energy states to changes in pair-breaking strength.
- Constrain the model with a single, physically motivated parameter set tied to the measured gap, and show the corresponding Δ0 = 0 prediction.
- Correlate local gap, local e–h imbalance and subgap energies defect by defect over a large field of view.

**5. Verdict**

Verdict: major revision
