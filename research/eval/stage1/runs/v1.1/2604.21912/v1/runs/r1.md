Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript introduces "cryogenic shock exfoliation", a method in which graphite on tape is heated with the chip and then plunged into liquid nitrogen. The authors report that it roughly triples the non-Bernal fraction and the area of exfoliated multilayer graphene flakes (Fig. 1g,h). Combined with a low-pressure PC/PDMS micro-cavity transfer, it gives rhombohedral devices above 1000 µm² with a transfer yield of about 90% (Fig. S4b). One 13-layer device is then characterized by capacitance, nanoSQUID magnetization imaging, transverse magnetic focusing (TMF) and current imaging. From these the authors infer long mean free paths and a size-driven crossover from Poiseuille to porous hydrodynamic flow. If robust, the method removes a real materials bottleneck for rhombohedral graphene physics and would be widely adopted. The fabrication advance is the main novelty. The hydrodynamic results are presented as a further signature of quality.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A simple, low-cost and plausibly transferable exfoliation protocol with a large practical effect. The geometric-mean flake area rises from 1200 to 3100 µm², the non-Bernal fraction rises from 12% to 32%, and there is a long tail of single-stacking domains above 1000 µm² (Fig. 1g,h, Fig. S1).
- The standard-exfoliation control is reasonable: same substrate area, same 110 °C heating step, and baseline values that match literature crystallographic ratios.
- Transfer statistics are given as counts (Fig. S4b), and the transfer membrane and procedure are described in enough detail to reproduce (Methods, Fig. S4a, video).
- The real-space nSOT magnetization map (Fig. 2c) is direct evidence that a single isospin transition is crossed uniformly over the 10 × 10 µm² square. Few uniformity tests are this strong.
- The TMF data are clean: up to six focusing orders on all four edges (Fig. 3c), and the ballistic, diffusive-like and hydrodynamic-like current images (Fig. 4a,c,e) are visually compelling.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The TMF analysis does not measure the disorder mean free path, yet the abstract claims "a disorder mean free path exceeding 200 µm".
   - Where: Eqs. (4)–(6) in Methods normalize the peak area to its low-temperature limit, A(T)/A0, and convert it to ℓmfp = −(πd/2)/ln(A/A0). By construction ℓmfp → ∞ as T → 0, and the Methods state that A(T) saturates below 15 K (Fig. S7).
   - Why it matters: any temperature-independent loss, whether impurity scattering, diffuse boundary scattering or contact transmission, is absorbed into A0 and is invisible to the analysis. The statement "ℓ0 ≥ 200 µm below 30 K" therefore reflects only the fact that the temperature-dependent suppression is a few percent. With d ≈ 5 µm, ℓ = 200 µm corresponds to ln(A/A0) ≈ −0.04, which is close to the scatter of the normalization itself. The method bounds only the temperature-dependent scattering, not the residual disorder.
   - Two related problems:
     - The suppression of a focusing peak is caused by both momentum-relaxing and momentum-conserving (electron-electron) scattering. The extracted quantity is therefore a total quasiparticle mean free path, yet the Methods later use it as the range for ℓmr ("2–200 µm from the TMF measurements").
     - The specularity p = 0.5 is "empirically chosen" to match the decay of successive focusing peaks. That decay depends jointly on p and on the residual mean free path, so fixing p this way presupposes a long ℓ.
   - What to do: give an absolute rather than normalized estimate of the residual momentum-relaxing length. Options:
     - Fit the absolute amplitudes and the order-by-order decay of the focusing peaks (1R–6R) with the Boltzmann simulation, treating p and ℓmr jointly. Show how well ℓmr is constrained, for example ℓmr = 20, 50, 100 and 200 µm.
     - Compare the measured negative bend resistance (≈ −6 Ω at low T, Fig. S6c) quantitatively with the simulated map in Fig. S9a.
     - Report Hall or field-effect mobility in the large square.
   - If these cannot be done, the abstract and main text should be reworded to say that the temperature-dependent scattering length exceeds 200 µm below 30 K, and the comparison with "best-in-class" graphene devices should be qualified accordingly.

2. All electronic evidence for uniformity and quality comes from a single device, while the headline claims concern a fabrication method across many devices and areas.
   - Where: the abstract and introduction state "highly uniform devices exceeding 1300 µm²" with "yields of ~90%". The 1330 µm² device (Fig. S5d–f) and the other devices in Fig. S5 are characterized only by optical and IR images. Capacitance, nSOT and TMF are shown for one 13-layer device whose total rhombohedral area is 740 µm², and the electrically probed region is the ~100 µm² square plus a 0.5 µm channel.
   - Why it matters: IR contrast after transfer does not establish electronic uniformity. Twist-free stacking faults, strain, bubbles and sub-diffraction Bernal domains could all survive in the larger devices. For a methods paper, device-to-device reproducibility of electronic quality is the core deliverable.
   - What to do: show at least one more device with a different layer number and/or a larger active area, made by the same protocol. Give penetration-field capacitance maps, or transport phase diagrams with a characteristic isospin transition, and TMF or bend resistance. Ideally include a large-area (>30 µm) electrically probed region to support "uniform over 1300 µm²".
   - Report how many completed devices were cooled down and how many showed the expected rhombohedral phase diagram. This distinguishes the post-fabrication yield from the transfer yield.

B. Should address

3. The Poiseuille-to-porous crossover rests on two geometries measured in different configurations, at mismatched densities, displacement fields and temperatures.
   - Different configurations: the channel resistance (Fig. 4g) is a local four-terminal measurement, whereas the square (Fig. 4j, Fig. S6b,c) is a bend or nonlocal configuration. In the square, the resistance is negative at low T because of ballistic focusing into the voltage probe. A monotonic R(T) in the square therefore does not by itself show that boundary friction is absent; it is dominated by the ballistic-to-diffusive change in the bend signal.
   - Mismatched conditions:
     - The nSOT "hydrodynamic" image (Fig. 4e) is at (6.5 × 10¹² cm⁻², 0.41 V/nm) and T ≈ 1.6 K.
     - The Gurzhi minimum appears at 15–30 K and at (2.5, 0.85), (3, 0.51) and (4, 0.51).
     - The simulations fix ℓee = 50 nm, taken from bilayer graphene [25] rather than fitted.
   - Internal tension: if ℓee were already 50 nm at 1.6 K, the 0.5 µm channel would be deep in the Poiseuille regime at base temperature. It is then unclear why the resistance minimum, which should mark ℓee ≈ W, occurs only at 15–30 K. The Fig. S8i simulations also show that the y_max profile constrains ℓee only to ≲ 1–10 µm, not to 50 nm.
   - Alternative explanation: in the half-metal and quarter-metal regime, isospin-ordering transitions and spin or valley fluctuation scattering at tens of kelvin can also produce dR/dT < 0 and a resistance minimum.
   - What to do:
     - Measure the channel R(T) at the (n, D) of Fig. 4e.
     - Add a control at (10¹³ cm⁻², D = 0), where the flat-band interaction enhancement is absent, and show whether the minimum disappears.
     - Show the width dependence with more than two widths, or at least the scaling expected for Gurzhi (the depth and position of the minimum versus W).
     - If possible, provide temperature-dependent nSOT current images across the crossover.
     - State how ℓee(T) is constrained, rather than treating ℓee⁻¹ simply "as a proxy for temperature".

4. The exfoliation statistics need more detail before the factor-of-three improvement and the proposed mechanism can be assessed.
   - Where: Fig. 1g plots the non-Bernal fraction (1 − A_Bernal)/A_total, while the text calls it "rhombohedral". IR contrast and sMIM distinguish Bernal from non-Bernal but may not separate ABC stacking from mixed sequences (for example ABCB or twinned stacking faults), which are common in flakes above about 10 layers. Raman 2D lineshape or IR spectroscopy on a subset of domains would confirm that the counted regions are rhombohedral.
   - Missing information:
     - How many exfoliation runs, chips and source crystals contribute to each distribution.
     - Whether the thickness distributions of the two methods are matched. The rhombohedral fraction depends on thickness, and the cryogenic method may produce thicker flakes.
     - The fraction as a function of layer number.
     - A statistical test of the difference in means.
   - Mechanism: the text proposes "partial conversion of Bernal to rhombohedral stacking" by thermal stress, but the data do not separate conversion from preferential cleavage around pre-existing rhombohedral regions. Either state this as a hypothesis, or test it, for example by checking the source crystal or tape residue after cryogenic shock, or by applying the cryogenic step to already-exfoliated Bernal flakes.

5. The yield comparisons in Fig. S4b are uncontrolled, and the yield is defined only at the transfer stage.
   - The statistics were "collected by seven co-authors of varying experience". Each bar varies one technique, but other factors (operator, period, flake thickness, combined techniques) are not held fixed. This matters especially for "pressureless transfer" (7/24 vs 50/62) and "LN2 exfoliation" (18/24 vs 32/36), which are the comparisons behind the headline claims.
   - What to do:
     - State how a transfer was judged successful (IR after pickup, after full stack, after fabrication?).
     - Say whether the "technique not applied" cases came from earlier periods.
     - Ideally give a cross-tabulation, or yields with confidence intervals.
     - The cumulative 24/26 should be tied to verification after nanofabrication (see item 2).

6. The effective-mass extraction is weaker than the text implies.
   - Uncertainty: the stated m* = 0.075 ± 0.05 mₑ has a 67% uncertainty, so "excellent agreement" with the tight-binding value of 0.078 mₑ is not a discriminating test.
   - Model issues:
     - The analysis assumes a Lorentzian kernel, which visibly misfits the tails (Fig. S7a).
     - It assumes parabolic bands at E_F = 160 meV, whereas the Fermi surface in Fig. 3a is trigonally warped.
     - It assumes a temperature-independent lineshape.
   - Fit quality and validity: the fit in Fig. S7b deviates systematically from the data over much of the temperature range. The validity condition k_BT/E_F < w/d is invoked without stating the collector width w.
   - What to do: give the uncertainty's origin, show the sensitivity to the lineshape choice, and soften the claim accordingly.

7. The title and abstract emphasize "ultrahigh mobility", but no mobility value is reported anywhere. Give a Hall or field-effect mobility, or a transport mean free path from the large square, at the relevant densities, so that the claim can be compared directly with the literature cited in refs. [44–46].

8. There is no matched device from standard exfoliation.
   - The claim that cryogenic shock does not degrade microscopic quality rests on ref. [39] and on the exclusion criteria, not on a device comparison. Thermal shock could introduce cracks, strain or point defects.
   - A same-geometry device from a conventionally exfoliated flake, or a direct comparison with published rhombohedral graphene TMF or bend-resistance data at matched density, would support the quality claim.
   - The same holds for any comparison with literature values of ℓ in rhombohedral graphene; the introduction states these are "limited only by the sample size".

C. Minor

9. Fig. 3 panel labels i and j are swapped relative to the caption and text. In the figure, ℓmfp(T) is panel j (top right) and V_peak/A_peak is panel i. The caption and text ("shown in Fig. 3i", "Fig. 3j" for m*) assign them the other way round.

10. The ℓmr used for the near-neutrality "diffusive" profile is inconsistent: 10 µm in the main text and Fig. 4d, 20 µm in the Fig. S8k caption, and "approximately the device size" in the Methods. Please reconcile these values.

11. Fig. 4j and Fig. S6 report the square's signal as "R_s (Ω/sq)". For a bend or nonlocal configuration a sheet resistance is not defined. Label it as a resistance and state the contact configuration explicitly. Also clarify whether Fig. 4j and Fig. S6c show the same quantity.

12. The ballistic nSOT image (Fig. 4a) differs noticeably from the simulation (Fig. 4b), with crossing jets and fine structure. The difference is attributed to "details of the current injection". A simulation with the actual contact geometry, or with different injection angular distributions, would strengthen the ballistic assignment. The p = 0.5 specularity fit mentioned in the Methods should also be shown.

13. The extrapolation ℓmfp(300 K) ≈ 2 µm lies beyond the measured range (≤ 250 K), and at that point ℓ < d. It is compared with mono- and bilayer graphene at different densities. Please state the density used for the comparison and mark the extrapolation as such.

14. Fig. 2b: state what features a Bernal inclusion would be expected to contribute to C_P, and the minimum area fraction it would need in order to be detectable. Likewise, give the nSOT sensitivity to a non-switching minority region in Fig. 2c, so that "uniform" has a quantitative meaning.

15. The Matthiessen exponent α = 1.56 is reported without interpretation. Comment on whether it reflects phonon scattering, electron-electron scattering or a mixture, given the point in item 1.

16. Typos and formatting:
    - "First TMF peak for at T=2.0K" (Fig. 3 caption).
    - "m* = .075mₑ" (Fig. 3 caption).
    - "an ℓee varied" (Fig. S8i caption).
    - Ref. [1] contains "iSBN"; ref. [22] contains "version Number: 4"; ref. [36] has an all-caps author name.
    - "µm²" is typeset inconsistently in places.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Replace or supplement the normalized TMF analysis with an absolute constraint on ℓmr. Simulate the full TMF spectrum, including higher-order peaks, and the low-temperature negative bend resistance as functions of ℓmr and p, and quote the resulting bound.
2. Add a second (and ideally third) electrically characterized device, including one with a much larger active area, to support "uniform devices exceeding 1300 µm²" and the post-fabrication yield.
3. For the hydrodynamic section, measure channel R(T) at the same (n, D) as the nSOT image, add a non-flat-band control, and, if feasible, a third channel width. This would turn the Poiseuille-to-porous crossover into a quantitative test rather than a qualitative consistency check.
4. Report the exfoliation statistics per layer number, with the number of runs and crystals, and confirm the stacking assignment by Raman on a subset of domains.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
