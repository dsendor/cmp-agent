A. Must address

1. The headline claim, that ρ(T) lets one infer where the flat band (FB) sits relative to E_F (title, abstract, conclusion: "a simple, broadly applicable method for detecting flat bands"), is not demonstrated quantitatively or independently of DFT. In the fits in Fig. 4(a), the FB position enters only through the DFT DOS used as fixed input to Eq. 1; only the prefactor C is adjusted. The fits therefore test whether the DFT DOS is compatible with the data; they do not infer E_edge from transport. The inverse procedure, the fit formula said to give the FB position and normalized effective mass without DOS input (Eq. 6 in the SM), is mentioned but never applied in the main text, and no extracted E_F − E_edge or m_FB/m0 with uncertainties is reported for any compound. The only quantitative inference is CoSn (Fig. 4(e)): a gradual deviation "at around T ≈ 400 K ≈ Δ_DFT/3" (FB ≈ 0.1 eV below E_F), read off against a hand-placed dashed linear extrapolation over roughly 200–800 K. The onset criterion and the fit window are not defined, no uncertainty is given, the factor 1/3 comes from Fig. 3(e) for one specific choice (m_FB = 10 m0, Δ = 100 meV) with no shown dependence on m_FB or edge sharpness, and the result is compared only with the DFT value the authors already had, which may not coincide with the ARPES FB energy. The authors should: (i) apply the DOS-free fit to every compound in Fig. 4(a) and tabulate E_F − E_edge and m_FB/m0 with error bars, ideally as fit-quality maps in (E_F − E_edge, m_FB); (ii) for the DFT-based fits, rigidly shift the DOS (or E_F within it) by ±20–100 meV and show fit quality versus shift, so that a clear minimum near zero with a stated width demonstrates that ρ(T) actually constrains the FB position (if shifts of tens of meV fit equally well, the method cannot "pinpoint" the FB); (iii) compare the inferred positions with independent spectroscopic values, i.e. ARPES on CoSn (refs. 38, 39), Ni3In (ref. 23) and CaRh2 (ref. 32), ideally as a blind test in which the transport estimate is made before the ARPES comparison; (iv) for CoSn, state the linear-reference window, show the sensitivity of the deviation temperature to it and to the choice of n and Θ_D, mark Δ_DFT/3 with its uncertainty in Fig. 4(e), and show the residuals of the Eq. 1 fit with and without the FB feature in the DOS.

2. The sub- or superlinear signature is not shown to be specific to flat bands, and competing explanations are not excluded. Within Eq. 1 and the two-parabolic-band model (m_FB = 10 or 100 m0), any steep DOS feature within a few k_BT of E_F produces the same curvature: an ordinary d-band edge, a van Hove singularity, or the d-band shoulder that makes ρ(T) of Pd, Pt and A15 compounds sublinear (Mott's original s–d case, ref. 29). Superlinearity in particular only requires D(E) to rise away from E_F, which is generic. Nothing in the model uses the defining features of a kagome or pyrochlore FB. Several other mechanisms also bend ρ(T) at hundreds of kelvin: resistivity saturation near the Mott–Ioffe–Regel limit (the parallel-resistor form), directly relevant for Ni3In and CaRh2 at about 100–160 μΩ cm in Fig. 4(a); thermal expansion (constant-pressure vs constant-volume resistivity); phonon softening, a temperature-dependent Θ_D and anharmonicity; a temperature-dependent chemical potential; and spin fluctuations in nearly magnetic Ni- and Co-based compounds. The authors themselves note (p. 3, refs. 45–47) that similar ρ(T) features in Heusler compounds and skutterudites arise from other energy-dependent scattering. There is no null control and no competing fit, so the agreement in Fig. 4(a) does not discriminate between mechanisms. The authors should: (i) fit each dataset with the standard Bloch–Grüneisen law, Bloch–Grüneisen plus a parallel-resistor saturation term, and Eq. 1, with the same number of free parameters, and report goodness of fit; (ii) quantify or bound thermal expansion, μ(T) and spin-fluctuation contributions for the measured compounds; (iii) analyse in exactly the same way at least one reference metal with a pronounced but dispersive DOS feature near E_F and no FB, ideally isostructural (for example a Cu3Au-type compound for the Ni3Ge family) or elemental Pd/Pt, measured under the same conditions; (iv) discuss when the FB interpretation can be distinguished from saturation using ρ magnitudes relative to the Ioffe–Regel limit. A doping or pressure series in one family (e.g. Ni3(In,Sn) or In/Sn substitution in CoSn) showing the crossover move as predicted would be the most convincing demonstration. Without this, the paper supports "ρ(T) tracks DOS structure near E_F" rather than "ρ(T) detects flat bands", and "universal" and "detecting flat bands" must be reframed accordingly.

B. Should address

3. The statement that the prefactor C is "the only free parameter" (p. 3) cannot be checked and appears incomplete. Eq. 1 also contains Θ_D and the exponent n (5 or 3), and the curves in Fig. 4(a) have finite T = 0 intercepts, so a residual resistivity ρ0, absent from Eq. 1, must also enter. For no compound does the paper say whether Θ_D was fixed (and from what source, e.g. specific heat) or fitted, which n was used and why, how ρ0 was handled (subtracted or fitted), or what temperature window was fitted. If Θ_D is adjusted, the one-parameter agreement is overstated, since Θ_D strongly controls the curvature between 50 and 300 K. The SM (ref. 30, link a placeholder "xxx") is not available. The authors should give a table per compound of C, Θ_D with its source, n, ρ0, the residual resistivity ratio, the temperature window and a goodness-of-fit measure with residuals, state explicitly which parameters were free, compare any fitted Θ_D with calorimetric values, and bring the key SM content (model equations, fit formula, parameter table) into the main text. Since the agreement in Fig. 4(a) is the main validation of the framework, its strength depends on this.

4. Eq. 1 rests on approximations that are neither stated nor justified. It takes σ(E) ∝ 1/D_DFT(E), dropping the N(E)v²(E) weighting of σ(E) = N(E)v²(E)τ(E) and the band-resolved velocities of the initial dispersive states, and assuming the FB states carry no current. It multiplies a Bloch–Grüneisen phonon integral by a Fermi-window average of 1/D(E) evaluated at E rather than E ± ħω, although phonon energies up to k_BΘ_D (tens of meV) are comparable to the Δ values and FB widths considered (20–100 meV), so inelastic smearing should matter. It uses the total DOS of all orbitals rather than the final-state DOS of the FB reachable by phonons. The main text does not state the σ(E) actually used for the DFT-based fits. The authors should state these approximations, test Eq. 1 against the full two-band model (e.g. apply Eq. 1 to the model DOS of Fig. 2 and compare with the exact model ρ(T) of Fig. 3), estimate the effect of using the DFT transport function or band velocities for at least one compound, say whether the sign of the curvature survives, and make the claimed consistency with first-principles RTA calculations (refs. 33, 43) quantitative by overlaying those curves.

5. The temperature dependence of the chemical potential is not discussed. With a large, asymmetric DOS within ~50–100 meV of E_F (e.g. m_FB = 100 m0) and temperatures up to 862 K (k_BT ≈ 74 meV), μ(T) shifts appreciably, alters ρ(T) by the same mechanism the paper invokes, and could change the position or even the existence of the resistivity maximum in Fig. 3(c), or move E_F across the edge. Thermal expansion over this range also shifts bands. The authors should state whether μ(T) is computed self-consistently from particle conservation in the model (Figs. 2–3) and in Eq. 1, and if not, include it and show how much it changes Fig. 3 and the fits in Fig. 4(a).
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

6. The model is a phase-space-only picture with a constant electron–phonon matrix element, an isotropic Debye spectrum and two isotropic parabolic bands. Real kagome and pyrochlore FBs are dispersionless over only part of the Brillouin zone, are k_z-dependent, touch dispersive bands at quadratic points, and have strong orbital, sublattice and momentum-dependent matrix elements; interband electron–phonon coupling depends on orbital overlap, not only on phase space, and some scattering channels may be suppressed. The authors should discuss why a constant-coupling model is expected to hold for these materials and present the agreement as qualitative.
Raised by: r1, r2

7. Anisotropy and sample form are mixed while the model is isotropic: CoSn single crystals are measured along c, Ni3In in the ab plane, and Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb are polycrystalline. Kagome FBs are strongly two-dimensional, so interband scattering rates and velocities can differ strongly between ab and c, and grain-boundary contributions in polycrystals can distort the temperature dependence of the absolute resistivity. The authors should discuss whether the CoSn c-axis result reflects the in-plane FB, ideally measure ρ_ab(T) of CoSn, and address grain-boundary effects in the polycrystals.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. Ni3In is the paper's main sublinear example, yet its ρ_ab is T-linear (n = 1, strange-metal) down to the lowest temperatures (p. 4, refs. 23, 31), electron–electron scattering is explicitly excluded from the model (p. 2), and the paper itself cites correlation-driven transport in FB systems (refs. 53–55). Fig. 4(a) nevertheless shows a fit over the whole range. An electron–electron or quantum-critical contribution, or its crossover, can also be sublinear or saturating, so the high-T agreement could be coincidental. The authors should state the temperature window used for Ni3In, show the residuals, and show that subtracting a plausible correlation contribution leaves the conclusion unchanged.

9. The reliability of the new high-temperature data (to 862 K) on Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb and CoSn is not documented. The main text gives no information on the high-T apparatus and atmosphere, contact geometry and geometric-factor uncertainty, sample characterization (XRD, composition), possible oxidation, Sn/Pb loss or annealing, warming/cooling hysteresis, or the number of samples per compound. Because C scales with absolute ρ and the claimed effects are curvature changes at the highest temperatures (including the superlinear CoSn upturn above ~400 K, the key quantitative datum, which a slow irreversible change would also produce), the authors should show heating and cooling curves, residual resistivity before and after the high-temperature run, and reproducibility on at least a second sample of each compound.

10. The fits and the classification depend on DFT, which is known to misplace FBs, and on DOS settings that are not given. The FB positions come from GGA-PBE; the authors attribute the worst fit (CaRh2) to the coarse Materials Project DOS and argue that a finer grid would give "more pronounced sublinear behavior", which shows that the outcome depends on DOS broadening; and Ni3In's FB position is sensitive to correlations. The authors should give the DFT details (code, k-mesh, energy grid and smearing, spin–orbit coupling, important for the Pt and Pb compounds, magnetism for CoSn and Ni3In, lattice parameters, any DFT+U-type correction) and the sensitivity of the fits to them, show the DOS for all compounds, and compute a converged DOS for CaRh2 and refit, which would turn an excuse into a test; if agreement does not improve, that is informative about the model's limits.

12. The Sommerfeld-coefficient comparison in Fig. 4(c) needs checking. Values are given per mole of formula unit across compounds with 2 to 4 atoms per formula unit, and γ includes mass enhancement beyond the band DOS. The Pd3Pb value γ ≈ 0.03 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² is extraordinarily small for a Pd-containing intermetallic, about 20 times below Cu and roughly two orders of magnitude below typical intermetallic values. The authors should check units, normalization and fit range, normalize per atom, compare with the bare DFT D(E_F) (band γ) for all compounds, and show the C/T vs T² data and fit (Fig. S2) in the main text or SM.

13. The low-temperature exponent argument is weak. The CoSn fit giving n = 4.99(2) with r² = 0.991 (Fig. 4(d)) is used to support E_F lying away from the FB, but the signal ρ − ρ0 is below ~0.2 μΩ cm over 1.5–55 K, so n is strongly correlated with ρ0 and the fit window, and the quoted error is presumably statistical only. Wilson-type interband n = 3 behaviour can also be suppressed at low T by the minimum phonon wavevector needed to bridge two Fermi surfaces. The authors should state ρ0 and the RRR of the CoSn crystal, report how n depends on fit range and ρ0, and avoid treating n = 5 as evidence on the FB position.
