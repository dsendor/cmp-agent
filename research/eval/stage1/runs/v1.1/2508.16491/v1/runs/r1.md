Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper proposes that the high-temperature electrical resistivity of a metal can be used to locate a flat band (FB) relative to E_F. The argument is that phonon-mediated interband scattering into the FB makes the scattering rate follow the density of states, tau_ep^-1(E) ~ D(E). This makes rho(T) sublinear when the FB edge lies just above E_F and superlinear when it lies below. The authors support this with a two-parabolic-band Boltzmann model (Figs. 2-3) and with fits of a DOS-weighted Bloch-Grüneisen formula (Eq. 1) to rho(T) data up to 862 K on five intermetallics (Fig. 4). They measured three of these compounds themselves (Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb and CoSn) and took the other two from the literature (Ni3In and CaRh2). A cheap transport diagnostic for FBs would be useful. However, the physical mechanism is Mott's s-d interband scattering picture for transition metals (ref. 29), so the novelty lies in applying it to FB compounds and in the claim that it can locate the FB. The paper has not yet demonstrated that second claim quantitatively.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The paper asks a timely and practical question: can a routine measurement such as rho(T) up to high temperatures constrain the position of the FB in the many new kagome, pyrochlore and Cu3Au-type FB candidates?
- The model calculations in Figs. 2-3 are clear and pedagogical. They show how an asymmetric sigma(E) produces sub- or superlinear rho(T), and that the crossover scale is T ~ Delta/3 (Fig. 3e), a useful rule of thumb.
- The authors present new rho(T) data over a wide temperature range (1.5-862 K) on Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb and CoSn. They also report specific heat for Pd3Pb.
- Several material classes are compared within a single framework. The qualitative ordering against the Sommerfeld coefficient gamma (Fig. 4c) is suggestive.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The headline claim, that rho(T) lets one infer where the FB sits, is not tested independently of DFT. In the fits in Fig. 4(a), the FB position enters through the DFT DOS used as input to Eq. 1; only the prefactor C is fitted. So the fits show that the DFT DOS is consistent with rho(T). They do not show that rho(T) determines E_edge. The text mentions a fit formula (Eq. 6 in the SM) that extracts the FB position and normalized effective mass without DFT input, but the main text applies it to no compound and reports no extracted value or uncertainty. The evidence for the location claim is therefore the qualitative sub- vs superlinear sorting plus the CoSn kink in Fig. 4(e).

   The authors should:
   - apply the DFT-free fit to every compound in Fig. 4(a) and report E_edge and m_FB with uncertainties;
   - compare these values with independent determinations, since ARPES exists for CoSn (refs. 38, 39), Ni3In (ref. 23) and CaRh2 (ref. 32);
   - test sensitivity directly by rigidly shifting the DFT DOS by +/-25, 50 and 100 meV and showing how the fit quality of Eq. 1 degrades.

   If shifts of tens of meV fit equally well, the method cannot "pinpoint" the FB as the abstract and conclusion claim.

2. The paper does not show that the signature is specific to flat bands. Within Eq. 1, any steep DOS feature within a few k_B T of E_F gives the same sub- or superlinear behavior. Examples are an ordinary d-band edge, a van Hove singularity, or the d-band shoulder that makes rho(T) of Pd and Pt sublinear (the original Mott case). Superlinearity in particular only requires D(E) to rise away from E_F, which is generic.

   Several other mechanisms also bend rho(T) at hundreds of kelvin:
   - resistivity saturation near the Ioffe-Regel limit (the parallel-resistor form). This is relevant for Ni3In and CaRh2, which reach about 100-150 microOhm cm (Fig. 4a).
   - thermal expansion (constant-pressure vs constant-volume resistivity)
   - phonon anharmonicity
   - a temperature-dependent chemical potential
   - spin fluctuations in nearly magnetic Ni- and Co-based compounds

   The authors should show that these alternatives fit worse or are quantitatively small, for example by fitting a parallel-resistor model to Ni3In and CaRh2. They should also include a reference compound with a pronounced but dispersive DOS feature near E_F and no flat band, to show that rho(T) distinguishes the two cases. Without this, the paper supports "rho(T) tracks DOS structure near E_F" rather than "rho(T) detects flat bands", and the claimed universality is not established.

3. The "single-parameter fit" statement (p. 3) cannot be checked, and it appears to be incomplete. Eq. 1 also contains Theta_D and the exponent n (5 or 3), and the curves in Fig. 4(a) have finite T = 0 intercepts, so a residual resistivity rho_0 must also enter. The paper does not say for any compound whether Theta_D was fixed (and from what source), which n was used, or how rho_0 was set.

   The authors should provide a table of all parameters for each compound (C, Theta_D with its source, n, rho_0, temperature window), with fit residuals, plus an explicit statement of which parameters were free. Since the agreement in Fig. 4(a) is the main validation of the framework, its strength depends on this.

B. Should address

4. The transport model rests on approximations that are not justified for the materials studied:
   - tau_ep^-1 ~ D(E), with a constant matrix element and an isotropic Debye phonon spectrum;
   - in Eq. 1, the transport function reduces to D^-1(E). This drops the N(E)v^2(E) weighting in sigma(E) = N(E)v^2(E)tau(E) and assumes the FB itself carries no current;
   - the chemical potential is fixed in temperature.

   For a heavy band with m_FB = 100 m_0 near E_F, mu(T) shifts appreciably at 500-1000 K. This could change the position and even the existence of the resistivity maximum in Fig. 3(c). Kagome FBs are also strongly anisotropic and k_z-dependent. The CoSn data are along c, while the Ni3In data are in the ab plane, yet the model is isotropic.

   The authors should include mu(T) self-consistently in the two-band model, state in the main text the sigma(E) actually used for the DFT-based fits, and discuss the anisotropy, ideally by measuring CoSn in-plane as well.

5. Electron-electron scattering is excluded (p. 2). Yet Ni3In shows strange-metal, T-linear behavior down to the lowest temperatures (p. 4, ref. 23), and the paper itself cites correlation-driven transport in FB systems (refs. 53-55). For Ni3In, attributing the high-T curvature purely to electron-phonon interband scattering needs justification. One option is to show that subtracting a plausible correlation contribution leaves the conclusion unchanged.

6. The high-temperature data on the new samples need more support. Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb are polycrystalline and were taken to 862 K. The authors should show:
   - characterization (XRD, composition, and whether Pb/Sn evaporates or oxidizes at high temperature);
   - heating and cooling curves demonstrating reversibility;
   - a second sample of each compound to show sample-to-sample reproducibility;
   - how the geometric factor was determined. Fig. 4 is based on absolute resistivity values, and grain-boundary contributions in polycrystals can distort the temperature dependence.

7. Pt3Sn appears in Fig. 4(a) and in the methods, but the text never says whether its rho(T) is sub- or superlinear, what its DFT DOS shows, or how it fits the classification. It is also missing from Fig. 4(c). Either discuss it or explain why it is omitted.

8. CaRh2 gives the worst fit, which the authors attribute to the coarse Materials Project DOS. This is testable. The authors already perform DFT for CoSn, so they should compute a converged DOS for CaRh2 and refit. If the agreement does not improve, that is informative about the limits of the model.

9. The CoSn evidence for a FB about 0.1 eV below E_F (Fig. 4e) is a gradual deviation from a straight line fitted over a chosen window, at T ~ 400 K. The authors should:
   - state the window and show the sensitivity of the "deviation temperature" to it;
   - show the residuals of the Eq. 1 fit with and without the FB feature in the DOS;
   - compare the inferred position with the ARPES FB energies for CoSn, which may not coincide with the DFT value in Fig. 4(b).

10. The Sommerfeld coefficients in Fig. 4(c) are compared per mole of formula unit across compounds with 2 to 4 atoms per formula unit, and gamma includes mass enhancement beyond the band DOS. The authors should normalize per atom and compare with the DFT band-structure values D(E_F). The Pd3Pb value of gamma ≈ 0.03 mJ mol^-1 K^-2 is unusually small for an intermetallic metal, about 20 times smaller than Cu. Please check it and show the C/T vs T^2 data and fit in the main text or SM.

C. Minor

11. The CoSn data appear to end near 720 K in Figs. 4(a) and 4(e), whereas the text gives 1.5-862 K for Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb and CoSn. Please state the actual range for each compound.

12. The resistivity in Fig. 3 is given in microOhm cm, but the prefactor C is called a free constant. State the value of C used, or label the axes in arbitrary units.

13. Fig. 3 uses n = 3 at low temperature (grey T^3 line in Fig. 3e), while CoSn shows n = 4.99 (Fig. 4d). Clarify when each exponent applies within the model, and which n is used in the Eq. 1 fits for each compound.

14. Give explicit definitions of the DOS and sigma(E) normalization behind Fig. 2, and of how E_edge is defined for a band with finite width.

15. Words such as "universal", "strikingly similar", "perfectly" and "robust" (abstract, pp. 1, 2 and 4) overstate a phenomenological comparison of five intermetallics. Please temper them.

16. Small typos: "we do not included" (p. 2), the "Ca(Rh,Ru)2" label in the Fig. 4(a) legend versus "CaRh2" in the text, and the missing volume/page details for ref. 9.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Run a blind test. Fit rho(T) with the DFT-free formula for a compound whose FB position is known from ARPES (for example CoSn or Ni3In), and only afterwards compare the result with ARPES.
- Strengthen the case by tuning the FB position with chemical doping, for example in a CoSn or Ni3(In,Sn) series. Showing that the sub/superlinear crossover moves as predicted would be the most convincing demonstration.
- Measure thermopower on the authors' own samples (Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb, CoSn). The paper already suggests its sign as a discriminator, and the same sigma(E) should then describe both rho(T) and S(T).
- Discuss explicitly how the present framework relates to Mott's s-d model and to the authors' earlier two-parabolic-band work (refs. 45-48), so that the new contribution is clear.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
