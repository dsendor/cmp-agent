Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper proposes that the curvature of the resistivity well above the Debye-like regime shows where a flat band (FB) sits relative to E_F. Sublinear ρ(T) means E_F lies in the FB, superlinear means it lies away from it, and the temperature of the departure from linearity (about Δ/3) gives the offset. The physical idea, an energy-dependent electron-phonon scattering phase space τ⁻¹(E) ∝ D(E), descends from Mott's s-d picture. The contribution here is to frame it as a quick FB screening tool and test it on a series of modern FB compounds: a two-parabolic-band model, a DFT-DOS-modified Bloch-Grüneisen (BG) formula, ab initio electron-phonon transport for Ni3In, a Ni3In1-xSnx substitution series, and new 2-860 K data on Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb and CoSn. A transport-only route to FB proximity would be useful to the community if it can be shown to be discriminating and quantitative.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A simple, physically transparent mechanism. The model behaviour (Figs. 2, 3) is clearly explained, including the T/Δ scaling in Fig. 3e and the finite-bandwidth check in the Methods and Fig. S1.
- A first-principles electron-phonon (Phoebe/BTE-RTA) calculation for Ni3In gives the same step-like σ(E) as the DOS-only models (Fig. 4c). This is real support for the claim that the phase space dominates over matrix-element details, at least in this compound.
- The Ni3In1-xSnx solid solution (Fig. 4b) is a well-chosen experimental knob that moves E_F through the FB edge within one structure family.
- New high-temperature data over a wide range (up to 860 K) on several compounds, plus consistency with Sommerfeld coefficients (Fig. 5c).
- The paper is honest about other mechanisms (Kondo, semimetal activation, MIR saturation) and about the limits of a narrow FB (Methods).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The qualitative claim, that the sign of the high-T curvature correlates with whether E_F sits in the FB, is supported by the model, by the Ni3In ab initio calculation, by the substitution series, and by consistency with DFT and γ across the compounds shown. The items below concern how far the claims extend, especially the quantitative "locating" claim and the uniqueness of the fingerprint.

B. Should address

1. The method is never used to locate a flat band quantitatively.
   - Where: abstract ("the temperature evolution of dρ/dT encodes information about the energy offset"), Methods Eq. 7, and the Conclusion ("our method provides an efficient way to determine the position of FB states").
   - What is missing: Eq. 7 has E_F − E_edge as a free parameter, but it is never fitted to any measured ρ(T). The Fig. 5a fits instead fix the energy alignment from the DFT DOS and leave only C free. The one quantitative extraction, CoSn in Fig. 5e (deviation at about 400 K, read as Δ_DFT/3 ≈ 0.1 eV), uses a "Δ/3" rule taken from the parabolic model of Fig. 3e. That rule is not shown to hold for other m_FB, other bandwidths W (Fig. S1) or a non-parabolic DOS.
   - Why it matters: this is the headline application.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Fit Eq. 7, or Eq. 1 with the DFT DOS rigidly shifted by an offset δ left free, to the Ni3In, CoSn, Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb and Ni3In1-xSnx data. Report the best-fit E_F − E_edge with an uncertainty (χ² versus δ).
     - Compare these values with independent determinations, in particular ARPES for CoSn (refs. 29, 30) and for Ni3In (ref. 23).
     - Show how sensitive the onset criterion is to m_FB and W.
   - If ρ(T) only weakly constrains δ (for example, if ±50 meV shifts fit equally well), the claim should be narrowed to a qualitative screening criterion.

2. The fingerprint is not shown to be specific to flat bands.
   - Where: p. 6, the paragraph on other mechanisms.
   - The issue: the curvature criterion responds to any steep variation of D(E) near E_F. A steep d-band edge from an ordinary dispersive band, which is Mott's classic case for Pd and Pt, gives the same sublinear ρ(T). Several other mechanisms also bend high-T ρ(T):
     - MIR saturation;
     - thermal expansion and phonon anharmonicity, which commonly produce mild superlinearity in simple metals. This bears on the CoSn interpretation in Fig. 5e.
   - The MIR exclusion is asserted ("good metals well below the MIR limit") without numbers. Yet the Gim et al. Ni3In data in Fig. 5a reach about 120 µΩ cm at 300 K, and Ca(Rh,Ru)2 and Ni3Ge reach about 160 µΩ cm, which is close to typical ρ_sat values of 100-200 µΩ cm.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Give the estimated ρ_sat (or k_F ℓ) for each compound.
     - Show that a parallel-resistor (saturation) fit with a linear ideal term does measurably worse than Eq. 1, at least for Ni3In and Ca(Rh,Ru)2.
     - Estimate the thermal-expansion contribution to the superlinearity in CoSn and Pd3Pb.
     - Ideally, add a reference compound measured over the same range with a smooth D(E) at E_F and no FB, showing near-linear ρ(T).
     - Otherwise, reword "fingerprint of a FB" as "fingerprint of a steep DOS edge near E_F", which may or may not be a FB.

3. The σ(E) reconstruction from Ni3In1-xSnx (Fig. 4c) depends on several unstated assumptions.
   - What the paper does: it reads σ(E) from (ρ − ρ0)⁻¹ at 100 K and maps x to energy using alloy-averaged DFT.
   - Problems with this:
     - Matthiessen's rule is known to fail substantially in concentrated alloys.
     - 100 K is not the high-temperature regime of the BG law. The BG prefactor at 100 K depends on Θ_D(x), which presumably changes across the series.
     - Absolute ρ of polycrystalline, hexagonal (anisotropic) samples carries geometric-factor and grain-boundary uncertainties of order 10-20%, while the conductivities are compared across x.
     - Sn substitution changes the band structure and phonons, not just E_F (it is not a rigid-band shift).
     - The σ axis is in arbitrary units, and the paper does not say how the experimental points were normalized to the model curves. If the points were scaled to match, the agreement is partly by construction.
   - Why it matters: Fig. 4c is the main direct experimental test of the σ(E) step.
   - What the authors should do:
     - State the normalization and the x → E mapping explicitly, with its uncertainty.
     - Add error bars on both axes.
     - Show that the reconstruction is stable when made at other temperatures (e.g., 200 and 300 K, using the high-T data).
     - Give Θ_D(x), or at least argue that it is nearly constant.

4. The "single free parameter" in Eq. 1 hides fixed inputs, and the fit results are not reported.
   - What is missing: for each compound in Fig. 5a, the paper does not state the Θ_D used and its source, the exponent n (3 or 5), the fitted C, the fit range or a goodness of fit. It also does not show how the DFT DOS was broadened or aligned.
   - Why it matters: DFT FB positions are often off by tens of meV, as the authors themselves note ("when the DFT-calculated DOS is unavailable or unreliable, as is often the case for FBs"). Good fits with a fixed DFT DOS therefore test DFT as much as they test the mechanism.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Provide a table of parameters and residuals.
     - Show the fits with the DOS shifted by ±25 and ±50 meV to show discriminating power (see item 1).

5. The temperature dependence of the chemical potential is not discussed.
   - The issue: a large, asymmetric FB DOS near E_F shifts μ(T) appreciably at 300-900 K at fixed carrier number. This shift is comparable to the Δ ≈ 20-100 meV offsets considered.
   - Whether the 2PB calculations (Fig. 3) and Eq. 1 keep μ fixed or conserve particle number is not stated. A shift of μ toward or away from the edge would change both the onset temperature (the Δ/3 rule) and the curvature.
   - What the authors should do: state the choice made, and show the effect of computing μ(T) self-consistently.

6. The case for "microscopic details do not matter" rests on a single compound and ignores anisotropy.
   - The issue: the ab initio support is for Ni3In (Fig. 4c, S2) and Ni3Ge (ref. 36) only. The kagome compounds are strongly anisotropic, yet:
     - CoSn was measured along c, while the kagome FB states are largely in-plane, with small v_z;
     - the Ni3In data in Fig. 5a come from another group's single crystal, and the current direction is not stated;
     - the Ni3In1-xSnx samples are polycrystals.
   - Why it matters: a τ ∝ D⁻¹ model with isotropic velocities may not capture the direction-dependent transport function.
   - What the authors should do: discuss which current direction the analysis applies to and, if possible, show ρ(T) for CoSn in the ab plane as well.
   - A related question: why does Fig. 5a use the Gim et al. Ni3In data (to about 300 K) rather than the authors' own Ni3In data, measured to 860 K (Fig. 4a)? The latter is the stronger test of the high-T regime.

7. Sample reproducibility and high-temperature artifacts are not documented.
   - Missing basics: one sample per compound appears to have been measured, with no reproducibility, heating/cooling hysteresis or thermal-cycling data shown above 300 K.
   - Joining the two setups: the low-T (He bath) and high-T (ZEM3) data come from different instruments, and the paper does not say how the two segments were joined (scaling, overlap region).
   - CoSn flux inclusions: flux-grown CoSn commonly contains Sn inclusions, and Sn melts at 505 K, which lies inside the measured range. Melting or redistribution of inclusions, or surface reaction above about 500 K, could alter the high-T slope that Fig. 5e interprets.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Show heating and cooling curves.
     - Report the overlap between the setups.
     - Confirm that the CoSn results are reproducible on a second crystal after high-T cycling.

C. Minor

8. Pd3Pb Sommerfeld coefficient. The value γ ≈ 0.03 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² quoted for Pd3Pb (p. 6, Fig. 5c) is implausibly small for a metal: about 20 times smaller than for Cu. Please check the units and normalization (per formula unit or per atom), and the fit in Fig. S4.
9. Pd3Pb wording. On p. 6, Pd3Pb is described as having its FB "not placed right at but above E_F". This reads as the opposite of the conclusion drawn, that E_F > E_edge gives superlinear ρ(T). Please clarify.
10. CaRh2 labeling. The compound appears as "Ca(Rh,Ru)2" in the Fig. 5a legend but as "CaRh2" in the text, the caption and Fig. 5c. State the actual composition of the ref. 35 sample.
11. DOS form in Eqs. 2, 6 and 7. These use (E − E_edge)^{1/2}, which describes an electron-like band above E_edge. The FB is described as hole-like, occupying E < E_edge (Fig. 2). Use (E_edge − E)^{1/2}Θ(E_edge − E) or equivalent, and state the convention.
12. Missing error bars. Fig. 4c shows no error bars on the experimental σ(E) points, and Fig. 5e does not state the Δ_DFT value used (it can only be read off Fig. 5b as roughly 0.1 eV).
13. FB conductivity statement. The Methods statement that σ_FB is "10 and 100 times weaker … from the higher effective mass alone" ignores the different carrier densities of the two bands. Please give the carrier density assumed for the FB.
14. Ab initio Fermi window. "Fermi window width of ∂f/∂T = 1×10⁻¹⁰" needs units and an energy-window equivalent.
15. Fig. 3 rendering. In Fig. 3c the BG and m_FB = 100 m0 curves are hard to tell apart near the peak, and the "activation of conductivity edge" annotation should point to the specific E_F − E_edge curve.
16. Data and code availability. The code is available only on request. Given the proposed use as a screening tool, consider releasing a short script implementing Eq. 1/Eq. 7.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Turn the method into a demonstrated quantitative tool. Fit with E_F − E_edge free, report it with uncertainties, and benchmark against ARPES for CoSn and Ni3In.
2. Add a discriminating control: a compound with no FB and a smooth D(E), measured in the same high-T setup, together with explicit parallel-resistor and thermal-expansion comparisons.
3. Strengthen Fig. 4c with σ(E) reconstructed at several temperatures, explicit normalization, error bars and Θ_D(x).
4. Provide a parameter table for all Eq. 1 fits, including Θ_D, n, C, the fit range and residuals.

5. Verdict
