Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper argues that the curvature of the high-temperature resistivity, sublinear when E_F lies inside flat-band (FB) states and superlinear when E_F lies just outside them, is a general transport signature of how close a flat band is to E_F. The authors attribute it to phonon-mediated interband scattering whose phase space follows the DOS. They support this with a two-parabolic-band Boltzmann model, ab initio electron-phonon (Phoebe) calculations for Ni3In, a Ni3In1-xSnx composition series used to reconstruct σ(E), and a one-parameter modified Bloch-Grüneisen (BG) formula fitted to six compounds. The physics is Mott's s-d scattering picture applied to the current flat-band materials. The new part is the proposal to use it as a fast screening tool, and the Ni3In1-xSnx reconstruction of the conductivity edge is the strongest new experimental result.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Simple, physically transparent idea with a clear practical goal: screening FB candidates using only ρ(T).
- Ni3In1-xSnx series (Fig. 4b,c): transport through a doping-tuned FB edge is mapped systematically, and the experimental σ(E) agrees with three models of increasing complexity (2PB, τ ∝ 1/D_DFT, BTE-RTA).
- The ab initio electron-phonon calculation supports the claim that the scattering is governed mainly by phase space (the DOS) rather than by the matrix elements.
- Wide temperature range (2-860 K) across several structural families: kagome, pyrochlore, Cu3Au-type.
- New high-temperature data on CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb. The n = 4.99(2) low-temperature exponent of CoSn is a clean check that intraband electron-phonon scattering dominates at low T.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The curvature criterion has not been shown to be diagnostic of a flat band, as opposed to other causes of curvature in ρ(T). The headline claim (abstract, Conclusion) is that sublinear/superlinear ρ(T) is a "clear and universal fingerprint" of FB proximity. The alternatives are dismissed in one paragraph (p. 6) without quantitative tests.
   - (a) Resistivity saturation. Ni3In reaches ρ ≈ 120 µΩ cm by 300 K in Fig. 5a, and Ca(Rh,Ru)2 reaches ≈ 160 µΩ cm at 860 K. These values are close to the usual Mott-Ioffe-Regel or parallel-resistor saturation scale of transition-metal compounds (~100-200 µΩ cm). Calling the compounds "good metals (well below the MIR limit)" needs a mean-free-path estimate using the DFT Fermi velocities and carrier density, together with a parallel-resistor fit for comparison.
   - (b) Sublinearity is generic. Sublinear ρ(T) is textbook behaviour for Pd, Pt and many transition-metal alloys, where a steep d-band DOS at E_F does the same job. The signature therefore tracks a large dD/dE near E_F, not a flat band as such.
   - (c) Superlinearity is generic. Superlinear ρ(T) at constant pressure is common in metals with no nearby FB, through thermal expansion, phonon softening and anharmonicity. These are not discussed, although Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb and CoSn are assigned to "E_F above the FB edge" on exactly this basis.

   What to do: fit a saturation (parallel-resistor) model for comparison. Discuss or estimate the thermal-expansion contribution at 300-860 K. Most importantly, show at least one null-control compound measured on the same setup: an isostructural or chemically similar metal whose DFT DOS is smooth near E_F. If the method is meant for screening, the rate of false positives matters as much as the true positives.

2. The method's ability to locate a flat band is not demonstrated independently of DFT. The paper says the approach "determine[s] the position of FB states" and replaces doping studies or ARPES. However:
   - In Fig. 5a the DFT DOS is fixed, Θ_D and n are fixed, and only the prefactor C is fitted. These fits therefore test whether the DFT DOS is consistent with ρ(T); they do not extract E_F − E_edge from the data.
   - Eq. 7 in Methods does allow E_F − E_edge and m2^{3/2}/D1 to be fitted, but it is never applied to any data set.
   - In the Ni3In1-xSnx series, the conversion from composition to E_F − E_edge also comes from DFT (alloy-averaged DOS, ref. 39).

   What to do: apply Eq. 7, or Eq. 1 with a rigid shift of E_F as a free parameter, to the measured ρ(T) of each compound. Report the fitted E_F − E_edge with uncertainties and parameter correlations, and compare with independent determinations: ARPES for CoSn (refs. 29, 30, FB ≈ 0.1 eV below E_F) and for Ni3In, and the Sommerfeld coefficients in Fig. 5c. If the curvature alone cannot pin down the FB position (for example because C and the FB weight are correlated), say so and narrow the claim to a qualitative sign test.

B. Should address

3. The reconstruction σ(E) ∝ (ρ − ρ0)^-1 at 100 K (Fig. 4c) rests on assumptions that are not stated.
   - Θ_D, the phonon spectrum and the conduction-band velocities are taken to be the same across the In/Sn series.
   - Matthiessen's rule is taken to hold in a substitutional alloy, where ρ0 is large and itself depends on composition.
   - Geometric-factor errors in bar-shaped polycrystals are ignored, which is relevant given that the panel is in arbitrary units and the normalisation to the models is not explained.

   What to do: give ρ0, RRR and the estimated geometry uncertainty for each x. State how the experimental points were scaled onto the model curves. Show that the step survives a reasonable variation of Θ_D(x) (for example from specific-heat data). Two of the compositions (x = 0.4, 0.6) were measured by a different person (author contributions); please confirm that the setup and contact geometry were the same.

4. Several key Fig. 5a curves come from single samples, and some are polycrystals of anisotropic materials.
   - Ni3In ab-plane single-crystal data show strange-metal behaviour at low T (ref. 23), and the text notes that the contrast with Ni3Sn is "even stronger in single crystals". The Ni3In1-xSnx data are polycrystalline averages over a strongly anisotropic kagome system.
   - CoSn is measured only along c.

   What to do: discuss how anisotropy and polycrystalline averaging affect the curvature. Ideally show CoSn in-plane ρ(T), and at least one repeat sample for the new compounds.

5. The low-temperature and high-temperature measurements use different setups: a helium bath cryostat, and a ZEM3 furnace up to 860 K. The paper does not say how the two data sets are matched (scaling, offset) at the overlap, or whether the samples changed during heating. Annealing, Sn or Pb loss, and oxidation can all alter ρ(T) above 600 K. Please show the overlap region and the heating/cooling reproducibility, and state whether any scaling was applied. The high-temperature curvature is the quantity being interpreted, so this directly affects the main observable.

6. Universality of the T ≈ Δ/3 crossover (Fig. 3e, Fig. 5e). The Δ/3 onset is derived for one parabolic model (m_FB = 10 m0). It is then used to infer Δ_DFT ≈ 0.1 eV for CoSn from a deviation from linearity at ≈ 400 K, judged against a dashed linear fit over an arbitrary window (Fig. 5e). Please show how the onset ratio depends on m_FB, on the FB bandwidth (boxcar model, Fig. S1) and on n. Also show that the inferred Δ is robust to the choice of linear-fit window.

7. The model leaves out temperature-dependent shifts of the chemical potential and of the bands. With a strongly asymmetric DOS, μ(T) shifts appreciably at k_B T ~ 50-75 meV, and thermal expansion moves the FB edge. Either effect changes E_F − E_edge in exactly the regime used for the diagnosis. Please estimate μ(T) from the DFT DOS, at minimum for Ni3In and CoSn.

8. The quasiparticle-broadening and electron-electron scattering issues are acknowledged but not quantified. Ni3In is a strongly correlated, non-Fermi-liquid metal at low T, and the text itself notes that strong electron-phonon scattering smears the conductivity edge. An estimate of the scattering rate ħ/τ at 300-800 K, compared with the edge sharpness and with Δ, would show whether the semiclassical picture holds where the paper applies it.

9. The fits in Fig. 5a need more detail:
   - which exponent n (3 or 5) was used for each compound;
   - the Θ_D values and their sources;
   - the fitted C values and their uncertainties;
   - the fit ranges, and whether ρ0 was subtracted;
   - residuals, or a goodness-of-fit measure.

   Comparing the fitted C against an independent estimate (from λ_ep and v_F) would also test the "single free parameter" claim more strongly.

10. The statement that the experimental σ(E) "depends inversely on the DOS and does not follow a D^-2 dependence" (p. 4) is not shown in Fig. 4c. Please add the D_DFT^-2 curve so readers can judge whether the data discriminate between the two.

C. Minor

11. Pd3Pb Sommerfeld coefficient. The text gives γ ≈ 0.03 mJ mol^-1 K^-2, which would be extraordinarily small for a metal. The Pd3Pb bar in Fig. 5c appears visibly larger than that. Please check the value and its units, and show the C/T data (Fig. S4) clearly.

12. Fig. 4a and its text. The text says the Ni3In and Ni3Sn slopes "converge" at high temperatures. In Fig. 4a, Ni3In flattens at high T to a slope that appears smaller than that of Ni3Sn. Please quantify both slopes, for example dρ/dT at 700-860 K.

13. Material label. Fig. 5c is labelled "CaRh2", while Fig. 5a and the text use Ca(Rh,Ru)2 data. Please make the material and the γ value consistent.

14. Data provenance. Fig. 5a mixes data taken from the literature (Ni3In, Ca(Rh,Ru)2, Ni3Ge) with new data. Please state which Ni3In data set was used (polycrystal or single crystal, and in which direction), since Ni3In ρ appears in both Fig. 4a and Fig. 5a with different magnitudes.

15. Methods, sample characterisation. Report residual resistivities, RRR values and phase-purity checks (XRD, EDX) for Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb and CoSn. Some Cu3Au-type phases have order-disorder transitions, so please state whether L12 order is retained after the 1173 K anneal and during the 860 K measurements.

16. Wording of the sign rule (p. 2-3). The text says the rule reverses for an electron-like FB. Please restate the criterion in one unambiguous sentence (E_F inside vs. outside the high-DOS region), so that "above/below the edge" does not confuse readers.

17. Eq. 7 and the parameter count. Eq. 7 is described as having "three independent fit parameters" once Θ_D is known, but n is also a choice. Please list it explicitly.

18. Fig. 3 consistency. The ρ values in Fig. 3c (hundreds of µΩ cm) are far above the MIR scale, which is not mentioned. The caption and text should note that the m_FB = 100 m0 case is illustrative.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a null-control compound with a smooth DOS near E_F, measured on the same setups, and a saturation (parallel-resistor) analysis. Together these would make the "fingerprint" claim much more convincing.
- Carry out a blind extraction of E_F − E_edge from ρ(T) alone, using Eq. 7 or Eq. 1 with a rigid E_F shift, and compare it with ARPES (CoSn, Ni3In) and with specific heat.
- Add thermopower for the same samples. The authors note that the sign of S indicates on which side of E_F the FB lies, and it would be an inexpensive independent check of the assignments in Fig. 5.
- Turn the Fig. 3e analysis into a practical recipe: the onset ratio T*/Δ as a function of FB mass and bandwidth, with uncertainties, so that others can apply the screening method.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
