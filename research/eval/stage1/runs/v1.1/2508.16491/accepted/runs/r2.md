Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper argues that the sign of the curvature of ρ(T) at elevated temperatures (sublinear when E_F lies inside a flat band, superlinear when E_F lies just outside it) is a simple transport fingerprint of how close a flat band (FB) is to E_F. The cause is the energy dependence of the phonon-mediated interband scattering phase space, τ⁻¹(E) ∝ D(E). The authors support this with a two-parabolic-band Boltzmann model, a modified Bloch-Grüneisen (BG) formula that uses the DFT density of states, one ab initio electron-phonon calculation for Ni3In, a Ni3In1−xSn_x substitution series, and high-temperature (up to 860 K) resistivity on several intermetallics. A cheap screening tool for FB materials would be useful. However, the physics is the Mott s-d interband scattering picture that has long been used for transition metals and their alloys. The novelty rests mainly on recasting it as a diagnostic for FB proximity, so the diagnostic's specificity and quantitative power need to be shown.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The model is clear and physically transparent. Fig. 3e gives an appealing scaling statement: deviations from BG behaviour set in at T ≈ Δ/3, where k_BΔ = |E_F − E_edge|.
- The Ni3In1−xSn_x series (Fig. 4b) tunes E_F continuously across the FB edge in one structural family. The reconstructed (ρ − ρ0)⁻¹ step in Fig. 4c agrees in shape with the 2PB model, the D_DFT⁻¹ model and the BTE-RTA calculation, which is the strongest piece of evidence.
- The authors present new high-temperature data (2–860 K) on CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb. CoSn is the cleanest case: its low-T exponent is n = 4.99(2) (Fig. 5d), and its high-T resistivity deviates from linearity near Δ_DFT/3 (Fig. 5e).
- The trend of the curvature sign with the Sommerfeld coefficient (Fig. 5c) is a sensible independent consistency check.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The paper never demonstrates that ρ(T) can actually locate a flat band. The abstract and conclusion claim the method lets one "locate FBs via transport" and "determine the position of FB states" without ARPES or doping studies. In practice, every real-material fit uses Eq. 1 with the DFT DOS held fixed and only the prefactor C free. That tests whether the DFT-placed FB is consistent with ρ(T), but it does not extract E_F − E_edge from transport. The Methods introduce Eq. 7 for exactly that purpose, with C, m2^{3/2}/D1 and E_F − E_edge as free parameters, but it is never applied to data.
   - The only quantitative "location" result is CoSn: a deviation from linearity at T ≈ 400 K ≈ Δ_DFT/3 (Fig. 5e). That number depends on the window chosen to define the linear reference, and it is compared only with DFT.
   - What the authors should do: fit Eq. 7, or Eq. 1 with a rigid shift of E_F as a second free parameter, to at least the CoSn and Ni3In data. They should report the extracted E_F − E_edge with an uncertainty, including the covariance with C and the sensitivity to the choice of Θ_D and n. They should then compare the result with an independent determination: ARPES places the CoSn flat band roughly 0.1–0.2 eV below E_F (their refs. 29, 30), and specific heat or doping data exist for Ni3In.
   - Without such a test, the claim should be narrowed to "the curvature sign is consistent with the DFT-predicted FB position".

2. The fingerprint has not been shown to be specific to flat bands, and the competing explanations are dismissed too quickly.
   - Sublinear high-temperature ρ(T) is generic. It arises from resistivity saturation (parallel-resistor behaviour), from any sharp structure in the DOS (the classic s-d mechanism in Pd, Pt and Ni alloys, which is essentially this model), and from thermal expansion or anharmonic softening of Θ_D. Superlinear ρ(T) above ~400 K is also common in simple metals, through thermal expansion at constant pressure and Debye-Waller effects.
   - Page 6 rules out MIR saturation because the compounds are "well below the MIR limit". Yet Fig. 5a shows Ni3In approaching ~120–150 μΩ cm and CaRh2 ~160 μΩ cm, within a factor of ~1–2 of typical intermetallic saturation values. Fig. 4a likewise shows ρ − ρ0 ≈ 75 μΩ cm for Ni3In with clear flattening.
   - What the authors should do:
     - (i) Fit the same high-T data with a standard BG plus parallel-resistor (saturation) model, and with a BG model using an energy-independent DOS plus thermal expansion. Compare goodness of fit with the same number of free parameters.
     - (ii) Show at least one null reference measured in the same high-T setup: an isostructural compound with no FB within ~0.3 eV of E_F, where the model predicts nearly BG-linear ρ(T) up to 860 K. A Cu3Au-type compound without FB features would be a natural choice alongside Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb.
     - (iii) Show at least one case where the model predicts the curvature sign before the data are examined.

   Without these, the word "universal" in the abstract and the claim that the curvature "directly reveals" FB proximity are not supported.

B. Should address

3. The experimental σ(E) reconstruction (Fig. 4c) has unquantified systematic errors.
   - The plotted quantity is (ρ − ρ0)⁻¹ at 100 K from polycrystals. Absolute resistivity of polycrystalline bars carries geometric-factor, porosity and grain-boundary errors that can easily reach 10–30% between samples. The step in Fig. 4c spans a factor of ~5, but the error bars are not shown, and it is not stated how the arbitrary-unit experimental points were normalised onto the theory curves.
   - The x → E_F − E_edge mapping rests on alloy-averaged DFT (ref. 39). That mapping is itself the quantity the method claims to determine, so the agreement is partly circular.
   - Matthiessen's rule is assumed, although ρ0 in the mid-composition alloys is disorder-dominated and the alloy disorder also broadens the FB itself.
   - What the authors should do: give the error bars, including geometric-factor uncertainty, and the normalisation procedure. They should show the σ(E) reconstruction at a second temperature (e.g. 50 K and 200 K) to test whether it is temperature-independent, as the model requires. They should also state the uncertainty of the x-to-energy mapping.

4. The evidence that the effect is "independent of microscopic details" rests on one compound. Only Ni3In has a full BTE-RTA calculation (Fig. 4c, Fig. S2); Ni3Ge is from earlier work.
   - It is also unclear how the BTE-RTA σ(E) was computed as a function of E. Was the chemical potential shifted rigidly in a fixed band structure with fixed phonons? Is the smooth line a transport function at fixed T?
   - PBE is known to misplace flat bands in kagome compounds. The calculations use no Hubbard U or spin-orbit settings, and these are not discussed.
   - What the authors should do: either add a BTE-RTA calculation for CoSn, where ARPES gives an independent FB position, or soften the generality claim. They should also specify the rigid-band procedure, the spin-orbit treatment and the sensitivity of E_edge to the functional.

5. Fit protocol details needed to judge the "single-parameter" fits in Fig. 5a are missing.
   - The values of Θ_D and n used for each compound are not given. The text says n is "either 5 or 3", while CoSn shows n = 4.99 at low T.
   - The temperature window, the residuals and a goodness-of-fit measure are not reported.
   - The main text says the fit has a single free parameter, while the Methods say Eq. 7 has three. This needs reconciling.
   - What the authors should do: add a table listing each compound's Θ_D (with its source), n, the fit range, C, the residual and whether ρ0 was subtracted. They should also show how the fit quality changes if the DFT DOS is replaced by a constant.

6. Sample and measurement reproducibility at high temperature is not documented.
   - The 2–300 K (helium cryostat) and 300–860 K (ZEM3) data come from different setups, and the matching procedure (scaling or offset) is not stated.
   - Heating to 860 K can anneal, oxidise or change the stoichiometry of the samples. CoSn crystals are grown from Sn flux, and any Sn inclusions melt at 505 K, close to the 400 K onset of the claimed CoSn anomaly. Changes in sample state would directly alter the curvature being analysed.
   - Only one sample per compound appears to have been measured.
   - What the authors should do: show heating and cooling curves and repeat a low-T measurement after the high-T run. They should state how the two datasets were joined and, for CoSn, show that flux inclusions are absent (e.g. no Sn superconducting transition at 3.7 K).

7. The anisotropy and sample form differ across the comparison set. CoSn was measured along c on single crystals, whereas Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb, Ni3In1−xSn_x and Ni3Ge are polycrystals. For Ni3In, in-plane single crystals show strange-metal linear-in-T behaviour at low T. The interband phase-space argument is isotropic in the model, but real velocity anisotropy can change the curvature.
   - What the authors should do: discuss whether the curvature sign is expected to depend on current direction, and show in-plane CoSn data if available.

8. The Sommerfeld coefficient γ ≈ 0.03 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² quoted for Pd3Pb (page 6, Fig. 5c) is implausibly small for a metal with 3 Pd per formula unit. It is more than an order of magnitude below Cu. The value should be checked; it may be a units or per-atom/per-f.u. error, or a fit artifact. The C/T vs T² data and the fit range should be shown in Fig. S4. Because γ is used as the independent confirmation of the FB position, an error here affects the consistency argument.

C. Minor

9. Pt3Sn and Ni3Ge appear in Fig. 5a, but their FB position, their γ and their agreement with the curvature-sign rule are not discussed in the text. Either add them to Fig. 5c and the discussion or explain why they are omitted.

10. Fig. 3c shows model resistivities up to ~500 μΩ cm with dρ/dT < 0, well beyond the MIR limit, where Boltzmann theory is not valid. The text should state that the "semiconductor-like" regime of the m_FB = 100 m0 scenario lies outside the model's range of validity.

11. The Δ/3 onset criterion (Fig. 3e) is derived for a sharp parabolic edge. The authors should state how it changes for a smeared edge (the m_FB = 10 m0 case, the box-car model in Fig. S1, or the real DFT DOS in Fig. 5b) before it is applied to CoSn.

12. The page 2 statement that electron-electron scattering would give τ_ee⁻¹ ∝ D² is followed on page 4 by the claim that the experimental σ(E) "does not follow a D⁻²(E) dependence". The authors should show this comparison explicitly (overlay D_DFT⁻² in Fig. 4c) rather than only asserting it.

13. Typos: "temprature" (p. 5), "intial" (Methods), and the opening quotation mark in "burns" (Methods).

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a benchmark figure comparing the FB position extracted from ρ(T) fits (with uncertainty) against ARPES or doping-derived positions for CoSn and Ni3In. This would turn the method from a qualitative indicator into a tool.
- Measure the thermopower S(T) on the same samples, as the conclusion itself suggests. The Mott formula links S to d ln σ/dE, so it would give a second, independent handle on the same σ(E) step and break the degeneracy with saturation models.
- Include one or two FB-free reference compounds in Fig. 5a, measured in the same high-T setup.
- Add a parameter table for all fits and the BTE-RTA settings (spin-orbit, smearing, rigid-band procedure).

5. Verdict: major revision
