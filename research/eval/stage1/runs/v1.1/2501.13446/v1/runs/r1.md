Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports SQUID magnetization of CrBr3 single crystals in a diamond anvil cell up to 9.2 GPa. It shows T_C falling from 33 K to about 6 K at 6.4 GPa, with ferromagnetism lost near 6.5 GPa. Room-temperature single-crystal X-ray diffraction and Raman spectroscopy under pressure show the rhombohedral R-3 stacking giving way to an averaged trigonal P-3m1 structure (Cr0.67Br2, one layer per cell), and a P-3m1 fraction is reported already at ambient pressure. The authors propose that pressure generates A-A stacking with antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling and that this destroys bulk ferromagnetism, supported by DFT interlayer exchange values and Monte Carlo simulations. A direct magnetization measurement through the suppression of ferromagnetism in a vdW insulator, together with a stacking-based explanation, would interest the 2D-magnet community. As presented, however, the structural and magnetic data are not linked tightly enough to establish the mechanism.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- High-pressure SQUID magnetization on CrBr3 up to 9.2 GPa is demanding. The T_C(p) phase diagram (Fig. 3) combines several independent pressure runs, which agree with each other and with the AC susceptibility below 3 GPa (Fig. S2).
- Single-crystal diffraction under pressure (Fig. 4, Table S1) shows clearly that the 1/3-type satellite reflections disappear between 6.4 and 8.4 GPa, and that the change is irreversible on unloading. The diffraction also contains the useful practical observation that cutting or handling the crystals produces stacking faults (more than 30 samples were checked).
- The authors took care over hydrostaticity: they compared pressure media (Fig. S1) and checked that the crystals do not degrade in the methanol-ethanol-water mixture.
- The paper raises a practical point: powder diffraction cannot separate the R-3 and P-3m1 stackings (Fig. 9), which bears on earlier powder studies.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The claimed antiferromagnetic high-pressure state is not tested by any magnetic measurement. In the abstract, the Discussion (pp. 17–18) and the Conclusions, the authors assert that above p_c an "antiferromagnetic phase" with AFM-coupled layers and intact Cr moments replaces ferromagnetism. The only data above p_c are an M(T) curve at 9.2 GPa in 0.01 T (Fig. 1), which is featureless, and an M(H) loop to 2 T at 10 K (Fig. 2), which is linear and reaches about 0.5 μB/f.u.
   - In an A-type state (ferromagnetic layers coupled antiferromagnetically), the intralayer order would survive. M(T) should then show a Néel anomaly at a temperature comparable to T_C, because the intralayer J dominates.
   - M(H) should show a spin-flip or spin-flop transition toward about 3 μB/f.u. at a field set by J_L. With the authors' J_L = −0.35 meV per Cr and S = 3/2, the interlayer saturation field is several tesla, so the 2 T range in Fig. 2 cannot tell this scenario apart from a paramagnet or from a reduced Cr moment.

   The authors should:
   - measure M(H) at 9.2 GPa (and at about 6–7 GPa) up to the 7 T limit of the MPMS at several temperatures;
   - analyse dM/dT, or the 9.2 GPa M(T) at higher fields, for a Néel-type anomaly;
   - compare the high-field slope and any saturation with the moment expected from J_L.

   Without this, "loss of ferromagnetism" is supported, but "emergence of an antiferromagnetic phase with intact moments" is not. If the measurement is not possible, the claim should be reduced to a hypothesis in the abstract and conclusions.

2. The structure and the magnetism are compared at different conditions, and where they can be compared quantitatively they do not agree.
   - (a) All diffraction and Raman data were taken at room temperature (Methods; Table 1; Fig. 5). All magnetic data are below 50 K, in a different cell (Be/Cu turnbuckle DAC) and on different crystals. Stacking energetics in CrX3 depend strongly on temperature: CrBr3 itself has the 420 K structural transition, and CrI3 is known to retain monoclinic stacking at low temperature. The pressure at which R-3 converts at 300 K therefore need not apply at 10 K.
   - (b) Fig. 5b gives an R-3 volume fraction of about 0.55 that is nearly constant from 0.7 to 5.7 GPa and drops only at 6.4–8.4 GPa. Over the same range T_C falls from 33 K to about 15 K (Fig. 3), and M at 10 K and 2 T falls from 2.8 to about 0.85 μB/f.u. by 5.1 GPa (Fig. 2). The gradual magnetic suppression therefore occurs while the measured stacking fraction is essentially unchanged. This contradicts the statement on p. 17 that the process "gradually decreases the Curie temperature".
   - (c) By Fig. 5b, ambient-pressure crystals already contain about 45% P-3m1, and up to about 20% even in the best sample. Yet the ambient magnetization shows a sharp ferromagnetic transition and about 2.8 μB/f.u. saturation, with "no antiferromagnetic contribution" (p. 2). If P-3m1 regions carry AFM interlayer coupling, the ambient isotherms should reveal them.
   - (d) In the Monte Carlo results (Fig. 13a), M/M0 at T = 0 stays at 1 for every AA fraction up to 45%, and T_C falls only from 39 K to 28 K. The simulation therefore reproduces neither the reduction of the low-temperature moment in Fig. 2 nor a T_C collapse toward zero.

   The authors should:
   - determine the stacking state at low temperature, ideally under pressure (low-temperature high-pressure XRD or neutron diffraction), or at least at ambient pressure at low temperature on a crystal with a known phase fraction;
   - characterise the phase fraction of the actual crystals used for magnetization;
   - plot T_C(p) and M_s(p) against the measured P-3m1 fraction on a common axis;
   - reconcile point (c) explicitly.

   As it stands, the central claim that stacking variation causes the suppression rests on qualitative coincidence.

3. The step from "R-3 satellites vanish and an averaged P-3m1 cell with 2/3 Cr occupancy fits" to "A-A stacking" is not demonstrated, yet the sign of the coupling depends on it. The P-3m1 model with randomly placed 2/3-occupied Cr is the average structure of any stacking sequence in which the honeycomb vacancy positions of successive layers are uncorrelated (p. 11). Losing the 1/3 reflections means losing ABC order; it does not identify A-A registry. Ref. [36] and Ref. [74] show that the sign of the interlayer coupling differs between stacking types, so a random mixture does not necessarily give net AFM coupling.

   The authors should analyse the diffuse scattering along the rods affected by stacking (h − k ≠ 3n): streak intensity profiles along L, or the histograms in Fig. 4, which are not explained. This would distinguish random stacking from a correlated A-A sequence, and would allow the probability of each stacking vector to be estimated (for example with a DIFFaX/FAULTS-type model). The c-axis spacing should also be compared with the DFT interlayer distance for AA versus ABC stacking. If only random stacking can be established, the magnetic model in Fig. 13, where all faulted atoms are AFM, should be revised to use the correct distribution of stacking-dependent couplings.

B. Should address

4. The theory does not address the pressure dependence that the mechanism requires. The DFT gives J_L for AB (0.83 meV) and AA (−0.35 meV), apparently at ambient volume. It does not show:
   - that A-A stacking becomes energetically favoured over ABC under pressure, which needs the enthalpy difference versus p;
   - how J_L and J1–J3 change with pressure;
   - how sensitive the sign and magnitude of J_L are to U_eff (fixed at 2 eV) and to the vdW correction.

   The intralayer J1–J3 are taken from Ref. [73] (ambient) rather than computed. Fig. 11 is said to "reproduce well" the experimental lattice parameters, but there is no overlay with Table S1 or Fig. 5a. A rough comparison suggests the calculated c at 10 GPa (about 16.4 Å) is close to the measured c at 6.4 GPa (16.84 Å) only if the pressure scales differ.

   The authors should add the stacking enthalpies versus pressure, J_L(p) for both stackings, a U-dependence check, and a direct overlay of calculated and measured lattice parameters.

5. The Monte Carlo model is not physically matched to stacking faults. Assigning AFM interlayer coupling to randomly chosen atoms (p. 16) is unlike a stacking fault, which flips the sign of the coupling across an entire interface. Interface-wise faults in a ten-layer stack can produce compensated or ferrimagnetic layer sequences, with very different bulk moments and transition temperatures. The simulated ambient T_C (39 K) also exceeds the measured 33 K, and fractions above 45% are not simulated. The authors should simulate interface-wise faults up to 100% AA, report the T = 0 moment, and compare normalized T_C/T_C(0) with Fig. 3.

6. The pressure determination and hydrostaticity in the SQUID cell are not specified. The text does not say whether the ruby pressure was read at low temperature, or only at room temperature, for each point in Figs. 1–3. The methanol-ethanol-water medium solidifies on cooling, and the cell pressure often changes by several tenths of a GPa between 300 K and 10 K. The paper itself shows that shear from cutting is enough to create P-3m1 (p. 3). The transformation is also irreversible on unloading (Table S1). Both points suggest that deviatoric stress, not hydrostatic pressure alone, may drive the stacking change. The authors should:
   - state how and at what temperature pressure was determined;
   - give the pressure uncertainties as horizontal error bars in Fig. 3;
   - discuss the role of non-hydrostatic stress, including for the XRD points above the roughly 10 GPa hydrostatic limit.

7. The T_C(p) fit and the value of p_c are not well founded. Eq. (1) on p. 6, and the label in Fig. 3, use the exponent −1/3, which would make T_C diverge at p_c; presumably +1/3 is meant. The choice of a 1/3 exponent is not justified physically. p_c = 6.5 GPa depends almost entirely on the single 6.4 GPa point (T_C about 6 K, from a noisy upturn in Fig. 1), with nothing measured between 6.4 and 9.2 GPa. The authors should:
   - state which parameters were free and give fitted uncertainties;
   - show the sensitivity of p_c to the functional form, for example linear plus quadratic, or a free exponent;
   - define how T_C was extracted from the M(T) curves (inflection, onset, or dM/dT minimum).

   The 5.9 GPa curve in Fig. 1 also needs comment: M rises to a maximum near 13 K and then decreases on further cooling. That shape is unusual for a ferromagnet and could indicate competing AFM coupling or a background problem.

8. The magnetization background and normalization need documentation. The empty-cell reference was measured separately (p. 4). At 9.2 GPa the residual signal is small and linear (Fig. 2), which is what an imperfect background subtraction would leave. The authors should:
   - show raw sample-plus-cell and empty-cell moments for at least one high-pressure field sweep;
   - state the sample mass and how it was determined in the DAC;
   - explain the 10% drop of M_s between 0 and 0.4 GPa (2.8 versus about 2.5 μB/f.u., Fig. 2). This may reflect different samples or instruments (VSM versus SQUID) rather than physics.

9. The method behind Fig. 5b is not explained, and it conflicts with the Methods. The text does not say how the "volume fraction of R-3" was obtained from single-crystal data: from 1/3-reflection intensities, from refined Cr2 occupancy, or from twin or phase scale factors. Fig. 5b shows about 55% R-3 at the lowest pressure, while p. 4 says the sample started at about 80% R-3. The 8.4 GPa point (about −0.1 ± 0.6) carries no information, and the sigmoid guide is drawn through it. The authors should describe the procedure, reconcile the starting fraction, and state whether the same crystal was used throughout.

10. The Raman analysis is interpretive and gives a third value of p_c. Assigning soft modes to R-3 and hard modes to P-3m1 (p. 14, Fig. 10) rests only on slope differences. No phonon calculation for either stacking and no symmetry analysis of the Raman-active modes is given. The averaged P-3m1 cell with fractional Cr is not a real periodic structure, so its selection rules are not obvious. The mode at 181.8 cm^−1 is labelled A2g, which is not Raman active in D3d or C3i.

    The intensity loss above about 11 GPa is attributed to a change in dielectric constant and incipient metallization. However, the DFT gap at 10 GPa is still 1.39 eV, and pressure-driven changes in resonance with the 2.41 eV excitation (CrBr3 Raman is known to be resonant, Ref. [37]) are a more direct candidate.

    The Raman p_c (about 10 GPa) also differs from both the XRD p_c (6.4–8.4 GPa) and the magnetic p_c (6.5 GPa), although all three are presented as one transition. The authors should:
    - compute the Γ-point phonons for both stackings under pressure;
    - give polarization-resolved assignments;
    - discuss the resonance alternative;
    - address the discrepancy between the three critical pressures.

11. The "trimorphism" and "new phase" claims are overstated. The P-3m1 structure is an average over stacking disorder. It appears after mechanical handling of as-grown R-3 crystals and persists after unloading. This fits a metastable faulted state better than a thermodynamically stable third polymorph (Conclusions, p. 18; abstract, "becomes stable"). The authors should rephrase or provide thermodynamic evidence, such as DFT enthalpies or evidence of reversibility.

12. The priority claim needs qualification. The abstract and pp. 3 and 18 claim "the first direct experimental evidence" of the suppression. Refs. [18], [20], [24] and [39] already report pressure-dependent T_C or magnetization of CrBr3. The novelty is the measurement to and beyond the collapse, and the wording should say so and compare quantitatively with the dT_C/dp of those works.

19. Fig. 5a plots unit-cell volumes for cells with different Z, which creates an apparent factor-of-9 jump. Plotting the volume per CrBr3 formula unit would show whether there is any real volume discontinuity at the transition (from Table S1 it is about 3%).

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Take one crystal through the whole sequence: characterise its stacking fraction by XRD at ambient pressure, then measure its magnetization under pressure. Even a single such crystal would anchor the structure–magnetism correlation far better than comparing different crystals.
2. Measure ambient-pressure magnetization (M(H) to high field, and the low-field M(T) shape) on deliberately cut or deformed crystals with a large P-3m1 fraction, next to as-grown R-3 crystals. If stacking faults carry AFM coupling, this is a simple ambient-pressure test of the mechanism.
3. Recast the DFT and Monte Carlo section around the pressure dependence: stacking enthalpies versus p, J_L(p) for each stacking, and interface-wise fault statistics up to 100%. Present T_C/T_C(0) and M(0) against fault fraction alongside the experimental Fig. 3 and Fig. 2.
4. Shorten the introduction's general vdW material, and expand the Methods for the SQUID DAC: low-temperature pressure determination, sample mass, and background subtraction.
