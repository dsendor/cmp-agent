Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports SQUID magnetization of CrBr3 single crystals in a diamond anvil cell up to 9.2 GPa. It finds that Tc falls from 33 K to about 6 K at 6.4 GPa and that the high-field moment drops. Room-temperature single-crystal XRD and Raman are used to argue that the rhombohedral R-3 phase converts to a trigonal P-3m1 average structure (Cr0.67Br2, 2/3 Cr occupancy), which the authors read as A-A stacking with antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling. A pressure-driven loss of ferromagnetism in a localized-moment vdW insulator, tied to a change in stacking, would interest the 2D-magnet community. The magnetization data under pressure are the most valuable new result. The structural identification and the causal link to the magnetism are much less secure than the abstract and title state.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Direct DC magnetization of a vdW magnet in a DAC up to 9.2 GPa is technically demanding. The Tc(p) data are consistent across several runs (Fig. 3) and extend the earlier AC-susceptibility work, which went to about 3 GPa (Fig. S2).
- The single-crystal XRD under pressure (Fig. 4, Table S1) shows a clear loss of the "1/3" reflections between 6.4 and 8.4 GPa. The transformation is retained after unloading, which is a useful and somewhat surprising observation.
- The observation that cutting CrBr3 crystals produces a P-3m1-like component (30+ samples) is a practical warning for others working on this material.
- The theme (stacking-dependent interlayer exchange in bulk CrX3 under pressure) is timely, and the attempt to combine magnetization, structure, Raman and DFT/atomistic modeling is welcome.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The structural phase fractions and the authors' own model do not account for the magnetic data. Fig. 5b gives an R-3 volume fraction of only about 0.55 near ambient pressure, about 0.5 at 3 and 5.7 GPa, and about 0.42 at 6.4 GPa. That is, roughly 45% of the sample is already the "antiferromagnetic A-A" P-3m1 phase at ambient pressure and the fraction barely changes up to 5.7 GPa. Over the same range, Tc falls from 33 K to about 19 K (Fig. 3) and the 10 K, 2 T moment falls from 2.8 to about 0.9 µB/f.u. (Fig. 2, 5.1 GPa). Meanwhile:
   - (i) The ambient samples show the full bulk Tc = 33 K and Ms ≈ 2.8 µB.
   - (ii) The Introduction states that no antiferromagnetic contribution is seen in CrBr3 isotherms at ambient pressure, and the 0 GPa loop in Fig. 2 indeed shows no metamagnetic step.
   - (iii) The Tc values of the different runs in Fig. 3 agree, although cutting is said to produce varying P-3m1 fractions from sample to sample.
   - (iv) The atomistic simulations (Fig. 13) give only a reduction from 39 K to 28 K at 45% AA, and the normalized low-temperature M/M0 is not reduced at all.

   Read together, the data suggest that the P-3m1 fraction measured by room-temperature XRD does not control Tc. That directly undercuts the central claim that ferromagnetism is suppressed "due to pressure-induced layer stacking variation". The text also quotes about 80% R-3 for the best sample (Sec. 2), which disagrees with Fig. 5b.

   The authors should:
   - state the R-3/P-3m1 fraction of the actual samples used for magnetization;
   - explain how the Fig. 5b fractions were obtained (which reflections, which model, how the error bars were derived);
   - reconcile the near-flat phase fraction below 6 GPa with the strong drop in Tc and moment;
   - extend the simulations to the AA fractions actually required (up to 100%) and compare simulated Tc and net moment quantitatively with Figs. 2 and 3.

   If the reconciliation fails, the mechanism should be presented as a hypothesis, not as the explanation.

2. The identification of the P-3m1 phase as A-A stacking is not supported by the diffraction analysis. The refined P-3m1 cell (a = 3.644 Å, c = 6.116 Å, Cr 2/3 on 1a; Tables 1 and 3) is a CdI2-type subcell with a = a(R-3)/√3. In that cell the honeycomb Cr/vacancy order is averaged away. That average arises when the honeycomb registry varies randomly from layer to layer (random stacking among the three in-plane positions), as the authors themselves describe on p. 11 ("the placement of the adjacent layer is randomized..., which would lead to seemingly 2/3 occupancy"). A coherent A-A stacking of honeycomb layers would instead keep the in-plane √3×√3 honeycomb reflections. It would index in a P-31m-type cell with a ≈ 6.3 Å and c ≈ 6.1 Å, with sharp superstructure reflections at integer l. The step on p. 12, "only one viable option which is the A-A type stacking fault", therefore does not follow from the data.

   This matters because the sign of the interlayer exchange (JL = −0.35 meV) used for the whole magnetic scenario is computed specifically for A-A stacking. Random stacking would mix AB, AC, AA and other registries, which [36] shows have different exchange signs.

   The authors should:
   - report whether reflections or diffuse rods at h−k ≠ 3n (in the 6.3 Å setting) are present above 8.4 GPa and in the unloaded sample, and whether they are sharp or streaked along c*;
   - refine or simulate the competing models (A-A P-31m, random stacking, mixed R-3/A-A faulting) against the same data;
   - give DFT enthalpies of ABC, AB and AA stackings as a function of pressure, to support the claim that A-A "becomes stable".

3. There is no direct evidence that the moments stay intact and are antiferromagnetically coupled above pc. The text states several times that ferromagnetism is lost "despite the individual Cr ion moments remaining intact" and that the high-pressure state is "generally antiferromagnetic". The only data above pc are a featureless low-field M(T) at 9.2 GPa (0.01 T, Fig. 1) and a linear M(H) up to 2 T at 10 K, giving about 0.5 µB/f.u. (Fig. 2). For an A-type interlayer antiferromagnet with |JL| ~ 0.35 meV per Cr, a spin-flip or spin-flop transition would be expected at several tesla, above which the moment recovers toward 3 µB. A Néel anomaly should also be visible in M(T) at higher field.

   These are within reach of the existing setup:
   - M(H) to 7 T at 2–5 K at the highest pressures;
   - M(T) at 0.5–1 T to look for TN.

   A cheap and decisive test is also available. Table S1 shows the P-3m1 phase is retained after unloading, so the authors can measure the magnetization of a fully transformed, decompressed P-3m1 crystal at ambient pressure with standard SQUID/VSM, with no cell background. They should also report whether ferromagnetism recovers on decompression in the magnetization runs. Without some such data, the paper establishes a suppression of the ferromagnetic signal, not its mechanism or the nature of the high-pressure magnetic state.

B. Should address

4. The magnetization normalization and procedures under pressure are not documented well enough to support the quantitative moment reduction (2.8 → 0.9 → about 0.5 µB/f.u.). The paper should state:
   - how the sample mass in the DAC was determined, and its uncertainty;
   - the size of the sample signal relative to the subtracted cell background at each pressure;
   - whether the curves in Fig. 2 come from one sample in one loading or from different samples or runs (Fig. 3 mentions "several different runs");
   - which pressures were measured on increasing and which on decreasing pressure.

   The 0 GPa curve in Fig. 1 is smooth and clearly from a different setup (presumably VSM outside the cell); this should be said. The 5.9 GPa M(T) curve is non-monotonic, peaking near 14 K and falling below 12 K, which suggests a background or temperature artifact. The criterion used to define Tc from M(T) (onset, inflection or dM/dT minimum) should be stated, with an error bar for each Tc. Low-field M(T) at 0.01 T is dominated by domains and demagnetization, so it should not be read as tracking the bulk moment.

5. The structure is measured at room temperature but the magnetism below 50 K, and the effect of the frozen medium is not addressed. All XRD and Raman are at room temperature, while magnetization is at ≤50 K. The methanol-ethanol-water medium freezes on cooling and is not hydrostatic at low temperature. The authors themselves stress that CrBr3 converts to the P-3m1 component under mild mechanical stress (cutting) and disintegrates in non-hydrostatic media (Fig. S1a). Non-hydrostatic stress on cooling could therefore change the stacking distribution in the magnetization samples relative to the room-temperature XRD. The authors should:
   - say whether ruby pressure was read at low temperature in the SQUID cell or only at room temperature, and give the pressure uncertainty and gradients;
   - discuss the stacking state at low temperature (ideally low-temperature XRD at one or two pressures);
   - state which medium was used for the magnetization runs ("liquid pressure medium" is not specific).

6. The critical pressures from the three probes disagree, and the Raman phase assignment is weak. The magnetic pc is about 6.5 GPa (fit), the XRD transition lies between 6.4 and 8.4 GPa, and the Raman pc is about 10–11 GPa (Fig. 10; Conclusions "≈10 GPa"). The text says the Raman intensity loss above 11 GPa "corresponds to the critical pressure pc detected by X-ray diffraction", which it does not. The modes are assigned to R-3 or P-3m1 solely by their pressure slopes. No factor-group analysis or polarization selection rules are used, and no calculated phonons.

   The CdI2-type P-3m1 average structure with one formula unit has only A1g + Eg Raman modes, whereas four or five "red" modes are attributed to P-3m1. Conversely, if the local structure is honeycomb, the average-structure mode count does not apply and the phases may not be separable by Raman at all. The authors should:
   - give the mode counts for both structures;
   - use the polarized spectra they say were recorded to check symmetries;
   - compare with DFT phonon frequencies and pressure slopes;
   - discuss why the Raman transition sits about 3 GPa above the XRD transition.

7. The "trimorphism" claim, that P-3m1 coexists with R-3 at ambient pressure, rests on cut or processed crystals. On p. 3 the P-3m1 component appears after the crystals were cut down for the DAC. The high-resolution reciprocal-space maps on an as-grown crystal (Figs. 7 and 8) only confirm R-3 and "cannot exclude" P-3m1. The evidence therefore supports a mechanically induced faulted or disordered phase, not a third equilibrium polymorph. The Conclusions ("We have observed trimorphism in CrBr3") should be narrowed unless an as-grown, uncut crystal is shown to contain P-3m1, for example via the h−k ≠ 3n rods discussed in item 2. Separately, the R-3 refinement puts 24% occupancy on the nominally vacant Cr2 (3a) site (Table 2). That is itself a signature of stacking faults within R-3 and should be connected quantitatively to the phase fractions in Fig. 5b.

8. The construction of the theory model needs to be specified and tested. Specific gaps:
   - The pressure at which JL(AB) = 0.83 meV and JL(AA) = −0.35 meV were computed is not given, nor how JL varies with pressure.
   - "AB" is used for the R-3 phase, whose stacking is ABC.
   - The intralayer J1–J3 are taken from ambient-pressure Ref. [73] and held fixed, although the Introduction cites DFT predicting that J1 grows with pressure. That is the very effect the authors say should raise Tc.
   - The sensitivity of JL to Ueff (2 eV only) is not shown.
   - Stacking faults are planar, yet the simulation assigns AA or AB interlayer bonds to randomly chosen atoms. A random per-site sign of JL is not the same physics as AFM-coupled interfaces between FM slabs. In a layered magnet with |JL| much smaller than the intralayer J, switching interface signs mostly produces compensated A-type order with a similar ordering temperature, not a collapse of the ordering temperature.
   - The simulated Tc at 0% AA (39 K) exceeds experiment (33 K), and the runs stop at 45%.

   The authors should simulate planar-fault configurations across the full concentration range, include pressure-dependent intralayer exchange, and report the ordering temperature and the net moment separately.

9. The Tc(p) fit function is incorrect as written and its pc is not robust. Eq. (1) on p. 6 and the label in Fig. 3 give Tc = Tc(0)(1 − p/pc)^(−1/3). That expression diverges at pc rather than vanishing; the plotted curve evidently uses +1/3. No justification is given for the exponent. Because the data stop at 6.4 GPa with Tc ≈ 6 K, pc = 6.5 GPa is essentially fixed by the last point and the chosen exponent. The authors should correct the sign, justify or free the exponent, and give pc with an uncertainty. They should also tone down the contrast with the 8.4 GPa linear extrapolation of [20], since a linear fit to 0–3 GPa data would be expected to overestimate pc when the decrease accelerates.

C. Minor

10. "First direct experimental evidence of the pressure-induced suppression of ferromagnetism" should acknowledge earlier Tc(p) measurements on CrBr3 ([18], [39], [20]); what is new is following it to near-complete suppression.
11. Fig. 1: the caption says 0.1 T, but the panel and Fig. 3 say 0.01 T (100 Oe). State which, and whether the data are ZFC or FC.
12. Fig. 4 caption: "a) ambient pressure, and a) a lower b) and c) lower than the transition temperature" should refer to pressures 0, 6.4 and 8.4 GPa.
13. Fig. 7 caption: P-3m1 is not rhombohedral and is space group 164, not 150 (150 is P321). Fig. 5a labels the phase "F-3m1".
14. Raman labels: "A2g" (181.8 cm⁻¹) is not an irrep of point group C3i (R-3), whose Raman-active modes are Ag and Eg. Check all mode symmetries.
15. Conclusions: "1/3 occupation of the Cr 1a site" contradicts the 2/3 occupancy in Table 3 and the text.
16. Table S1: c = 19.0(2) Å at 0.7 GPa exceeds the ambient value of 18.382(3) Å, which is not physical for compression; check this point. In Fig. 5a, plot a·√3 and 3c for the P-3m1 phase so the two phases can be compared on the same scale. The apparent jump in a from 6.0 to 3.5 Å is just the change of cell setting.
17. Fig. 11 is said to "reproduce well" the experimental lattice parameters, but no experimental points are overlaid. Add Table S1 data to the plot. State which structure (R-3 or AA) was used for the 10 GPa DOS in Fig. 12.
18. Two equations are both numbered (1) (Hamiltonian, p. 5; Tc fit, p. 6). Refs. [62] and [75] are duplicates. Table 1 lists T = 289 K and 296 K for the two phases, whereas the title says 289 K. "Badges" should be "batches", "Burker" should be "Bruker", and "invented the identical procedure" should be "adopted". In Fig. 13a, the legend "AFM (%)" should match the "AA" terminology of the text.
19. The ambient Ms of about 2.8 µB/f.u. is below the 3 µB expected for Cr3+; comment on whether this reflects normalization or the faulted fraction.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Measure the magnetization of the unloaded, fully P-3m1 crystal at ambient pressure, and extend the high-pressure M(H) to 7 T. These two measurements would directly test whether the high-pressure stacking carries antiferromagnetically coupled, intact Cr moments.
- Analyse the h−k ≠ 3n superstructure and diffuse scattering in the existing high-pressure and unloaded XRD data to distinguish A-A stacking from random stacking, and support it with DFT stacking enthalpies versus pressure.
- Determine the phase or fault fraction of the samples actually used for magnetization (before loading, and after unloading), and present a single figure that puts Tc, Ms and the R-3 fraction against pressure on the same axis.
- Recast the atomistic model with planar faults, pressure-dependent intralayer exchange and the full range of AA fraction, and compare simulated Tc and net moment directly with experiment.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
