Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports SQUID magnetization of CrBr3 single crystals in a diamond anvil cell up to 9.2 GPa. It shows that the Curie temperature and the moment measured at 2 T fall with pressure, and that ferromagnetism is lost near 6.5 GPa. Room-temperature single-crystal XRD shows the R-3 stacking giving way to a one-layer P-3m1 average structure (2/3 Cr occupancy) between 6.4 and 8.4 GPa. The authors attribute the loss of ferromagnetism to pressure-generated "A-A" stacking faults that couple the layers antiferromagnetically, and they support this with DFT interlayer exchange and Monte Carlo simulations. Direct high-pressure magnetization of a vdW ferromagnet up to full suppression of order is useful, and the link between stacking and magnetism matters for the whole CrX3 family. However, the causal link between stacking and magnetism, which is the paper's main interpretive claim, is not yet established by the data shown.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Hard high-pressure SQUID data on a vdW magnet. Tc(p) is reproduced across several pressure runs (Fig. 3) and is consistent with the AC susceptibility below 3 GPa (Fig. S2).
- Single-crystal (rather than powder) XRD under pressure. It shows clearly that the 1/3 reflections disappear between 6.4 and 8.4 GPa and that the P-3m1 state is retained on unloading (Table S1).
- The useful practical observation that cutting and handling introduce stacking disorder into CrBr3 crystals (30+ samples). This bears on how earlier powder studies should be read (Fig. 9).
- Several techniques (magnetization, XRD, reciprocal-space maps, Raman, DFT, atomistic spin simulations) applied to one material.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The structural phase fraction does not follow the magnetic evolution, and the structure and magnetism were measured on different samples at different temperatures.
   - The central claim (abstract, pp. 17–18) is that a growing concentration of A-A stackings progressively lowers Tc and the bulk moment. The paper's own data do not show this.
   - Fig. 5b gives an R-3 volume fraction of about 0.5–0.55 that is essentially flat from ambient to 5.7 GPa. Over the same range Tc falls from 33 K to about 17 K (Fig. 3), and the moment at 2 T falls from 2.8 to about 2.1 µB/f.u. by 1.1 GPa (Fig. 2).
   - Conversely, a sample that is already about 45% P-3m1 at ambient pressure would, on the authors' model, have a strongly reduced moment and Tc. Yet the ambient values (33 K, 2.8 µB/f.u.) are those of ideal CrBr3.
   - The text also says the best sample had "~80% volume fraction" of R-3 at the start (p. 4), which conflicts with the ~0.55 in Fig. 5b.
   - The XRD was done at room temperature on one crystal. The magnetization was done at 2–50 K on other crystals that had been cut to fit the cell, a step the authors themselves say generates P-3m1 (pp. 3–4). The stacking state of the magnetization samples, and its evolution on cooling under pressure, is unknown.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Report the phase fraction of the actual magnetization samples (before and after loading).
     - Explain how the Fig. 5b fractions were obtained and reconcile them with the 80% figure.
     - Ideally, obtain low-temperature diffraction under pressure.
     - Most directly, measure magnetization after decompression. Table S1 shows the unloaded crystal remains P-3m1, so the model predicts that the recovered sample should no longer be a bulk ferromagnet with 3 µB/f.u. That is a decisive and feasible test.

2. The proposed mechanism does not account for suppression of the ordering temperature, and the moment reduction has not been tested against field.
   - Interlayer coupling of either sign is small (J_L = 0.83 and −0.35 meV, p. 16) compared with the intralayer J1–J3.
   - In a quasi-2D magnet, a change of interlayer sign should mainly change the relative orientation of ferromagnetic layers. Each layer would still order near the 2D/bulk ordering temperature: pure A-A stacking should give an A-type antiferromagnet with T_N comparable to the ambient Tc, not loss of order.
   - The authors' own simulation shows this: at 45% "AFM" fraction, Tc is still 28 K (Fig. 13b). Simulations stop at 45%, so the model has not been shown to reach the regime the experiment probes (Tc = 6 K at 6.4 GPa, no transition at 9.2 GPa).
   - In addition, the AA/AB character was assigned at random to individual atoms (p. 16). A stacking fault is a property of a whole layer interface, so this assignment does not represent the structure being proposed.
   - On the experimental side: if the missing moment at 2 T (2.1, 0.85 and 0.5 µB/f.u. at 1.1, 5.1 and 9.2 GPa, Fig. 2) came from antiparallel-coupled layers, it should be recovered by a spin-flop/spin-flip field set by |J_L|. That field is of order a few tesla for −0.35 meV. Only fields up to 2 T are shown, and only at 10 K.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Extend the simulations to 100% A-A, with the coupling assigned per interface, and report both the net moment and the ordering temperature (for example, via the staggered layer magnetization).
     - Include the DFT pressure dependence of the intralayer exchange, which the introduction says increases.
     - Measure M(H) to the highest available field (7 T in an MPMS) at several pressures, to check whether the moment recovers.
     - Look for a Néel-type anomaly above pc (for example, in M(T) at higher field).
   - Without these, the experimental Tc collapse may need an additional cause, such as weakened intralayer exchange or nonhydrostatic stress, and the stacking scenario is at most part of the explanation.

3. The structural data do not identify A-A stacking specifically.
   - The P-3m1 refinement (Table 3: a = 3.64 Å, c = 6.12 Å, Cr 1a occupancy 2/3) is the average structure expected when the honeycomb vacancy positions are uncorrelated from layer to layer, that is, random lateral stacking.
   - Pure A-A stacking would keep the honeycomb registry between layers. It would give an ordered one-layer cell with a ≈ 6.3 Å (P-31m type) and sharp in-plane superstructure reflections, not a 2/3-occupied 1a site in a 3.64 Å cell.
   - The argument on pp. 11–12 that "the only viable option" is A-A stacking moves from "randomized" stacking to A-A without testing the alternatives. Those alternatives include random shifts, monoclinic-type (C2/m) faults (which are also known to favor AFM coupling in CrI3), and mixtures.
   - This matters because the AFM interlayer coupling invoked from Ref. [36] and from the DFT is specific to the A-A registry.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Analyse the intensity along the h−k ≠ 3n rods (diffuse streaks against sharp superstructure peaks), both in the DAC data and in the reciprocal-space maps.
     - Compare calculated diffraction for A-A, random and monoclinic-fault models.
     - State which stacking the DFT J_L = −0.35 meV refers to.

B. Should address

4. Quantification and protocol of the high-pressure magnetization.
   - Moments are given in µB/f.u., but the paper does not state how the mass of the micro-sample in the DAC was determined, or how uncertain it is.
   - It does not say whether the sample stayed intact (Fig. S1a shows fragmentation in some media), or how large the empty-cell background is compared with the signal at 6.4–9.2 GPa (about 0.01 µB/f.u. at 0.01 T).
   - The 0 GPa curves in Figs. 1–2 look like PPMS VSM data on a different sample. If so, the pressure dependence of the moment mixes two samples and two instruments.
   - The paper does not state whether the M(T) data are ZFC or FC, or how Tc was defined (onset, inflection, dM/dT). Arrows appear only for the AC data in Fig. S2.
   - Low-field (0.01 T) magnetization depends on domains and demagnetization, so its decrease is not a measure of Ms.
   - The 5.9 GPa curve in Fig. 1 rises at about 16 K and then falls below about 12 K. This re-entrant shape is not discussed and could signal competing interlayer order.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Give the normalization procedure with error bars.
     - Show raw signal against background at the highest pressures.
     - Show the same-sample ambient reference.
     - Define Tc.
     - Comment on the 5.9 GPa curve.

5. Pressure calibration at low temperature, and hydrostaticity.
   - The proposed mechanism is stress-induced stacking faulting, so stress conditions are central to the claim.
   - The paper does not say whether the ruby pressure in the turnbuckle Be–Cu cell was read at room temperature or at low temperature. Pressure in such cells typically changes on cooling.
   - The stated hydrostatic limit of 16:3:1 methanol–ethanol–water of "~14 GPa" (p. 4) is higher than the commonly quoted ~10 GPa for these mixtures at room temperature. The medium also freezes on cooling.
   - The structural transition (6.4–8.4 GPa) and the Raman pc (~10 GPa) lie close to where the medium becomes nonhydrostatic.
   - What the authors should do:
     - State how and at what temperature pressure was determined.
     - Report pressure gradients, for example from several rubies or ruby linewidths.
     - Ideally, repeat a key XRD or magnetization point with a gas medium (He or Ne), to show that the transition is driven by hydrostatic pressure rather than by deviatoric stress.

6. Determination and consistency of pc.
   - The fit equation has a negative exponent, Tc = Tc(0)(1 − p/pc)^(−1/3) (p. 6 and the Fig. 3 label), which diverges at pc. Presumably +1/3 is meant.
   - The paper does not say whether the exponent was fixed or free. pc = 6.5 GPa is essentially fixed by the single 6.4 GPa point, and no data lie between 6.4 and 9.2 GPa.
   - The three probes give different critical pressures: magnetization ~6.5 GPa (low T), XRD 6.4–8.4 GPa (300 K), Raman ~10 GPa (300 K). The conclusions present these as consistent but do not discuss the differences.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Correct the formula and state the fit parameters with uncertainties.
     - Add data near 7–8 GPa if possible.
     - Discuss the spread in critical pressure, including the different temperatures.

7. Raman phase assignment and interpretation.
   - The split of modes into "hard" (P-3m1) and "soft" (R-3) groups (pp. 14–15, Fig. 10) rests only on slopes. No phonon calculation for either stacking supports it.
   - The P-3m1 structure is an average over disorder, so its Raman selection rules are not obvious.
   - The loss of intensity above ~11 GPa is attributed to "further gradual metallization". The authors' own DFT gives a 1.39 eV gap at 10 GPa, and the 514.5 nm (2.41 eV) excitation will move in and out of resonance as the gap shrinks.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Compute Γ-point phonons and their pressure coefficients for the R-3 and A-A structures.
     - Show the spectra after decompression.
     - Consider resonance effects rather than metallization.

8. DFT details behind J_L.
   - The paper does not state at which lattice parameters and pressure J_L(AB) = 0.83 and J_L(AA) = −0.35 meV were obtained, or how sensitive they are to U (fixed at 2 eV) and to the vdW scheme.
   - D3 is a pairwise dispersion correction, not the "non-local vdW functional" stated on p. 4, and Ref. [54] is a different method.
   - The simulations take J1–J3 from an ambient-pressure neutron study (Ref. [73]). The simulated Tc of 39 K for pure AB is 6 K above experiment.
   - What the authors should do: give J_L(p) for both stackings, test sensitivity to U and functional, and state the DOS structure used at 10 GPa (R-3 or P-3m1).

9. Internal inconsistencies in the structural data.
   - Table S1 gives c = 19.0(2) Å at 0.7 GPa, larger than the ambient 18.382 Å. Fig. 5a plots about 18.3 Å at this pressure.
   - a drops by 1.8% between 0 and 0.7 GPa, then does not change between 0.7 and 3 GPa (6.189 against 6.1897 Å).
   - The ambient a differs between Table 1 (6.3091 Å) and Table S1 (6.3049 Å).
   - The 8.4 GPa phase-fraction point in Fig. 5b is negative, with an error bar spanning almost the full range.
   - These points affect the compressibility and the location of the transition. What the authors should do: check and correct them, and explain the error estimates.

10. Overstatement of "trimorphism" and of a "new trigonal phase".
   - By the authors' own account, the P-3m1 component appears after cutting, bending or exfoliating as-grown R-3 crystals. The reciprocal-space maps of an as-grown crystal "cannot exclude" it (p. 13).
   - It also survives unloading (Table S1).
   - This behavior is that of a mechanically induced, metastable stacking-disordered state, not a third equilibrium polymorph.
   - What the authors should do: rephrase the claims in the abstract, p. 3 and the conclusions accordingly, or provide evidence (for example, from unstrained as-grown crystals) that P-3m1 is thermodynamically stable.

C. Minor

11. The Fig. 1 caption gives the field as 0.1 T, while the legend, the Fig. 3 caption and the Fig. 3 label give 0.01 T (100 Oe). Please make these consistent.

12. Two different equations are both numbered (1) (pp. 5–6).

13. The Fig. 4 caption is garbled ("a) ambient pressure, and a) a lower b) and c) lower than the transition temperature"). It should refer to pressures below and above the transition.

14. The conclusions state that the Cr 1a site has "1/3 occupation", but Table 3 gives 2/3. The sentence "However, the high-resolution reciprocal lattice maps have shown the almost ideal spacing…" is unclear.

15. Notation:
   - Calling the ABC (R-3) stacking the "AB phase" and labelling phases "(or FM?)" and "(or AFM?)" (p. 16) is confusing.
   - "rhombo P-3m1" (p. 11) contradicts "trigonal".

16. Fig. 10: the text speaks of "red a green guiding lines" and blue lines, and the caption says "green ones (blue)". Please make the colors and mode labels (Ph1–Ph8) consistent between the text and the figure.

17. The CrI3 data in Fig. S3 are called "preliminary", and no experimental details are given. Either add the details or remove the comparison from the main argument.

18. Typos and wording:
   - "invented the identical procedure" (p. 3)
   - "Burker D8"
   - "badges" for batches (p. 18)
   - "Figure Figure 13a"
   - Refs. [62] and [75] are duplicates.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Measure magnetization on the decompressed (P-3m1-retaining) crystal, and characterize the stacking of the magnetization samples themselves. Together these would test the central causal claim directly.
- Extend the spin simulations to the full range of A-A fraction, with per-interface couplings and pressure-dependent intralayer exchange. Compare the simulated net moment and ordering temperature quantitatively with Figs. 2–3.
- Perform a diffuse-scattering analysis that separates A-A, random and monoclinic-type stacking.
- Do high-field M(H) at a few pressures, and at least one run with a gas pressure medium.

5. Verdict: major revision
