A. Must address

1. The diffraction data do not establish AA stacking, yet the whole magnetic mechanism depends on it (pp. 5–6, Figs. 2c,d and 3; pp. 10–12; abstract "the AA-stacked trigonal phase"; conclusions p. 13 "This phase is characterized by AA stacking"). The refined P-3m1 model is the small subcell (a_P-3m1 = a_R-3·tan 30°) with a single Cr site at 2/3 occupancy. That is exactly the average structure produced by honeycomb layers stacked at random over the three in-plane registries, and the authors concede on p. 6 that they "are unable to distinguish" ordered AA stacking from randomized stacking of A, B and C planes. A genuinely AA-stacked crystal, in which every layer has the same honeycomb, keeps a √3×√3 in-plane cell and gives the h−k ≠ 3n ("1/3") reflections at every integer l; in-plane domains of the three honeycomb variants would not remove them. Fig. 3 instead shows these reflections vanishing (8.4 GPa) or smearing into a broad, nearly continuous intensity along c* after decompression (Fig. 3d, lowest histogram), the signature of random or turbostratic stacking (a mixture of ABC, ACB, AA and AB/monoclinic-like shifts). Only ordered AA stacking yields the negative J_L(AA) of Fig. 6a; in a random-stacking state most new interlayer contacts would be AB- or BA-type registries whose coupling may be ferromagnetic, since interlayer exchange changes sign and size between stacking types (refs. 25, 49). AA stacking of a vdW halide, with Br directly above Br, is also normally high in energy, and no calculation shows it becomes favourable under pressure. The authors should (i) analyse the intensity distribution along l of the h−k ≠ 3n (10L/01L) rods at each pressure and after decompression, with an explicit stacking-fault model separating AA, AB/monoclinic-shift, twin and random registries (the paracrystal framework of Fig. 4 is a natural starting point), and state which sequences are compatible; (ii) compute DFT enthalpies H(p) of ABC, AA and other common stackings over 0–10 GPa; (iii) compute J_L at pressure for every registry that actually occurs and repeat the Tc simulation with those. If AA registry cannot be shown, the phase should be recast as stacking-disordered and the abstract, conclusions and "so-far unreported phase" wording should present AA stacking as a hypothesis, not the explanation.

2. The causal link between the stacking change and the collapse of ferromagnetism is not established by the data shown, and part of the data points the other way (abstract "This behavior is attributed to the increasing number of AFM-coupled Cr moments in AA stackings"; pp. 3–6, 13–14; Figs. 1, 3, S1). (a) Different samples and conditions: the structural evolution (Figs. 2e, 3, Table S4) and the Raman data (Fig. 5) were measured at room temperature on crystals starting from about 80% R-3 (Methods, p. 14), while the magnetization was measured at 2–35 K on other crystals whose starting phase content is not reported, although the paper itself says the P-3m1 fraction depends on cutting, mounting and handling (pp. 5–6, 14) and that pc is sample-dependent (Fig. S1a). No structural data exist at low temperature under pressure, even though stacking in Cr trihalides is temperature dependent and R-3 itself forms only on cooling through 420 K. In the model about 45% P-3m1 is needed at pc ≈ 5.8 GPa, whereas at room temperature R-3 is still clearly present at 6.4 GPa. (b) Reversibility contradicts the structural story: FM recovers with "unaffected Tc and Msat" after release from 10 GPa (Fig. S1b), yet R-3 is undetectable above 8.4 GPa (Fig. 3c) and P-3m1 is retained after release from 21.5 GPa (Fig. 3d). The explanation that "P-3m1 was not yet stabilized at 10 GPa while some volume fraction of R-3 persisted" (p. 13) is post hoc, conflicts with the diffraction result and is backed by no measurement after release from about 10 GPa; and by Fig. 6a, where J_L(AA) is near zero or positive at ambient, FM recovery at ambient does not by itself discriminate retained P-3m1 from reverted R-3. The authors should do at least one decisive test: measure magnetization on a crystal recovered from above 10–20 GPa (ideally the 21.5 GPa sample, with retained high-pressure stacking confirmed) and recompress it, which by their model should show strongly reduced FM or a much lower pc; and/or measure XRD (at least the 1/3 reflections) on a crystal released from about 10 GPa. They should also report the initial phase content of the magnetization crystals (XRD on the loaded crystal or a sibling piece) or correlate the two runs' different pc with their initial P-3m1 content, and ideally add a low-temperature high-pressure diffraction point (for example 5–7 GPa at about 10 K). Without such tests the AA mechanism should be presented as a hypothesis consistent with the data.

3. The magnetization data do not distinguish the proposed state with antiferromagnetically coupled, internally ordered layers from loss or reduction of the Cr moment or of intralayer order, and the 1 T data look quantitatively inconsistent with the calculated coupling (Fig. 1a,b; Fig. 6a; pp. 3, 12). Msat is defined as M(1 T) and the loops go only to ±1 T. By Fig. 6a, J_L(AA) is only about −0.1 to −0.25 meV at 5–8 GPa while J1 stays near 1.5–2 meV, so each layer remains a robust ferromagnet. The Zeeman energy of 1 T on S = 3/2 Cr (about 0.35 meV) is comparable to or larger than this interlayer energy, so a layered antiferromagnet with such coupling should show a spin-flip (or spin-flop) transition at or below about 1 T and a large M(1 T), as in few-layer CrI3; a vanishing 1 T moment is hard to reconcile with the model unless J_L is much larger than calculated or something else suppresses the moment. Conversely, if 1 T does not overcome the coupling, a drop in M(1 T) cannot separate interlayer AFM correlations from moment loss. Likewise, layers ordered internally should leave an anomaly in the 0.01 T susceptibility at the intralayer ordering temperature (bulk- or monolayer-like, about 20–30 K), but Fig. 1b shows none at 6.24 GPa. The claim that "the localized ionic magnetic moments are preserved" (p. 12) therefore needs: M(H) at 2 K up to the full SQUID field (5–7 T) at 5.2, 5.6 and 6.24 GPa and below pc, looking for a field-induced recovery towards ~3 μB/Cr; full-range, background-subtracted χ(T) above pc with uncertainties, looking for an AFM cusp; the expected spin-flip field from the calculated J_L; and a discussion of frustration, spin-glass behaviour or loss of intralayer order as alternatives. If background subtraction limits the field range, say so, state that "Msat" at 1 T may not be the saturated moment, and state in the conclusions which claims await a microscopic probe (neutron diffraction, XMCD or µSR under pressure).

B. Should address

4. The simulated "Tc → 0" (Fig. 6b) is not shown to be the counterpart of the experiment (pp. 10–12, 15–16; Figs. 6b, S8). With J1 ≈ 1.8 meV still ferromagnetic at 5–6 GPa and finite single-ion anisotropy (Fig. S7), each layer should still order at a temperature of order the monolayer Tc (about 27 K; the authors note on p. 12 that monolayers order at ~70% of bulk Tc). Random-sign interlayer coupling with 45% AA bonds removes the net moment but should give a layered ferro/antiferromagnetic or glassy state with finite ordering temperature, as the p. 12 statement that the high-pressure state is "generally AFM with zero bulk moment" implies. With only ten layers (20×20 cells per layer, three runs averaged), the net moment of each realisation is dominated by a few AFM bonds, and a Tc taken from the uniform susceptibility can fall to zero just because layer moments cancel. The authors should state the order parameter used; report layer-resolved or staggered (layer-sign-gauged) magnetization and the specific heat versus T; check convergence with number of layers, cell size and disorder realisations near pc where Tc collapses steeply; state whether the model predicts loss of order or only loss of net moment; and compare the experimental quantities of Fig. 1 (M at 0.01 T and 1 T) with the corresponding simulated quantity.

6. Pressure determination and hydrostaticity are not addressed, although the paper's own observations make them central (Methods pp. 13–14; Fig. 1). The P-3m1 fraction grows with cutting, bending and deformation (Fig. 4; pp. 6, 14), so deviatoric stress in the DAC is a leading alternative driver of the stacking change and of the sample dependence of pc. The "liquid pressure medium" is not named in any of the three DAC experiments; several common media freeze or become non-hydrostatic below 6–10 GPa, at room temperature or on cooling. Pressure was also set and read by ruby at room temperature, but Tc and Msat were measured at 2–35 K, and pressure in Be–Cu turnbuckle cells typically changes on cooling; the one-sided "overpressure" error bars in Fig. 1 are not explained quantitatively. The authors should name each medium and its hydrostatic limit, report ruby linewidths, compare the hydrostaticity of the XRD, Raman and SQUID loadings, give a low-temperature pressure calibration (or an in situ manometer such as Pb Tc) or an estimate of the change on cooling, explain the asymmetric error bars, and discuss whether the pc variation in Fig. S1a correlates with loading conditions.

7. Several choices in the DFT and spin model need justification and robustness checks (pp. 10–11, 15–16). J_L(AA) changes sign near 2 GPa and stays small (≤ 0.25 meV), so its sign could depend on the functional. Structures are relaxed with PBE+U (U_eff = 2.8 eV) + D3, but exchange is computed with r2SCAN+rVV10, and it is not stated whether U is applied there. The authors should show J_L(ABC) and J_L(AA) for at least one other U value or functional; state supercell sizes and the number of magnetic configurations in the fits; say whether AA cells were relaxed separately at each pressure or built from the ABC geometry, and how J_L was obtained for the partially occupied P-3m1 average structure; give the spin normalization and Hamiltonian convention, since J2 and J3 are fixed to ambient neutron values from ref. 47 which uses a different form; compare DFT J1 (≈ 2.1 meV) and ambient J_L(ABC) (≈ 0.4 meV) with the inelastic neutron/spin-wave values (ref. 47) or bilayer data; say whether the 34 K ambient Tc required rescaling; and discuss why the single-stacking model gives Tc rising from 34 to 39 K (Fig. 6b), opposite to the measured low-pressure slope (−2 to −3 K/GPa) where little stacking change is expected, since this bears on how far the calculated J(p) can be trusted.

9. The "intrinsic trimorphism" and "new phase" claims are overstated (abstract, conclusions p. 13). The monoclinic C2/m phase is the known high-temperature phase above 420 K and was not observed in this work. The Methods (p. 14) report that as-grown crystals showing pure R-3 develop P-3m1 after cutting, and its abundance is "likely unintentionally modified" by mounting (p. 6); together with the single-site 2/3 occupancy, this fits a mechanically induced, stacking-fault-rich state better than a distinct thermodynamic polymorph. The authors should either show P-3m1 in an untouched as-grown crystal (Fig. 4 shows only that it "cannot be excluded") or reword so the abstract does not suggest three intrinsic phases were found.

10. The Raman two-phase assignment is weakly constrained (pp. 8–9, Fig. 5). Assigning "hard" modes to P-3m1 and "soft" modes to R-3 by pressure slope alone is ad hoc: both structures share the same CrBr6 layers, so intralayer modes should have nearly identical frequencies and slopes, and the data look like single branches whose slope changes near 10 GPa, consistent with a compressibility crossover (ref. 40); coexisting phases should make each mode appear twice. The Raman crossover (~10 GPa) also differs from the XRD transition (6.4–8.4 GPa) and from the magnetic pc. The intensity loss attributed to "progressive metallization" conflicts with the authors' own DFT gap of 1.39 eV at 10 GPa (metallization only above 20 GPa); with 514.5 nm (2.41 eV) excitation, pressure-tuned resonance is more likely. The authors should show raw spectra at selected pressures with multi-peak fits demonstrating coexisting branches, support the assignment with group-theory and calculated Γ-point phonon frequencies and slopes for ABC and AA (or disordered) stacking, or present the Raman data as consistent with, not evidence for, two phases.
