Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript combines DAC SQUID magnetometry, single-crystal and high-resolution X-ray diffraction, Raman spectroscopy, DFT and atomistic spin simulations to argue that ferromagnetism in bulk CrBr3 collapses near 5.8 GPa because pressure converts the ABC (R-3) layer stacking into an "AA-stacked" trigonal P-3m1 phase with antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling. Direct magnetization data on bulk CrBr3 above 3 GPa, showing both Tc and the 2 K moment going to zero, are new and useful, and linking stacking order to interlayer exchange in bulk Cr trihalides under pressure is of broad interest to the vdW-magnet community. The weak point is the structural step of the argument: the identification of the high-pressure state as AA stacking, which the whole magnetic explanation rests on, is not established by the diffraction data shown, and the structure–magnetism link is made only qualitatively, across different samples, temperatures and pressure points.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First magnetization measurements of bulk CrBr3 through the full loss of the ferromagnetic signal (Fig. 1), with Tc from both AC susceptibility and DC magnetization, a second run (Fig. S1a) and recovery after decompression (Fig. S1b).
- Single-crystal diffraction to 21.5 GPa (Figs. 2, 3) showing loss of the "1/3" superstructure reflections and an irreversible change after decompression from 21.5 GPa, a structural observation that powder studies missed (Fig. S3).
- Useful ambient-pressure evidence that as-grown and handled CrBr3 crystals contain substantial stacking disorder (Cr2 3a occupancy 0.244, reciprocal-space maps in Fig. 4, sensitivity to cutting and mounting), relevant for anyone working on this material.
- A physically reasonable mechanism (stacking-dependent interlayer exchange, as known for CrI3 and CrBr3 bilayers) supported by explicit DFT values of J1 and JL for two stackings (Fig. 6a).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The diffraction data do not show AA stacking; they show loss of interlayer registry of the honeycomb, and the magnetic model depends on the distinction. (Pages 5–6, Figs. 2c,d and 3; conclusions p. 13 "This phase is characterized by AA stacking".) The P-3m1 model is the small subcell (a_P-3m1 = a_R-3·tan 30°) with a single Cr site at 2/3 occupancy. A genuinely AA-stacked crystal, in which every layer has the same honeycomb, has a √3×√3 in-plane cell and gives the h−k ≠ 3n ("1/3") reflections at every integer l; in-plane domains of the three honeycomb variants would not remove them. What Fig. 3 shows is that these reflections vanish (8.4 GPa) or smear into streaks (Fig. 3d, after decompression). That is the signature of random or turbostratic stacking (a mixture of ABC, ACB, AA, AB-type shifts), which averages to 2/3 occupancy on one site. The authors acknowledge on p. 6 that they "are unable to distinguish" randomized Cr occupancies from randomized stacking of A, B, C planes, but both readings mean the layers lack AA registry, and the paper then uses AA as the defining structure of the phase, in the DFT (Fig. 6a, JL(AA)) and in the magnetic explanation (abstract, p. 12). Because interlayer exchange changes sign and size between stacking types (ref. [25, 49]), a random-stacking state is not equivalent to an AA state with JL < 0. What the authors should do: (i) analyse the intensity distribution along l of the h−k ≠ 3n rods (diffuse streaks vs. sharp spots at integer l) in the high-pressure and decompressed data, and state which stacking sequences are compatible; (ii) either show positive evidence for AA registry or recast the phase as stacking-disordered and redo the magnetic modelling with interlayer couplings for the stacking types actually allowed (ABC, ACB-twin, AA, and the monoclinic-like shift), each computed by DFT at pressure; (iii) compute the DFT enthalpy of the competing stackings vs. pressure, which would show whether pressure is expected to favour AA at all. The terms "AA-stacked trigonal phase" in the abstract and "so-far unreported phase" should follow from the outcome.

2. The structure–magnetism link is qualitative and partly self-contradictory. (Pp. 3–6, 11–13; Figs. 1, 3, 6b; Table S4.) (a) Structure was measured at room temperature on a crystal that was ~80% R-3 at the start (p. 14); magnetization was measured at 2–35 K on different crystals, whose initial phase content is not reported, and the authors note that the P-3m1 fraction changes with handling and that pc is sample-dependent (Fig. S1a). (b) The pressure dependence of the phase fraction is not shown in the main text (only 0, 6.4, 8.4 GPa in Fig. 3) and is not used in the model: the AA fraction in Fig. 6b is "a linearly increasing function up to a critical pressure", which is a free choice that sets where simulated Tc vanishes, so the agreement with Fig. 1 holds by construction. (c) Reversibility is inconsistent with the structural story as told. FM with "unaffected Tc and Msat" recovers after decompression from 10 GPa (Fig. S1b), while the R-3 reflections are "undetectable above 8.4 GPa". The conclusion (p. 13) explains this by saying "some volume fraction of R-3 persisted" at 10 GPa, which contradicts the diffraction result. (d) No account is given of whether the room-temperature stacking survives cooling to <35 K under pressure; stacking in Cr trihalides is temperature dependent (the 420 K transition, p. 2). What the authors should do: plot the R-3/P-3m1 fraction from Table S4 against pressure next to Tc(p); use the measured fractions, with their stated lower-bound character, as input to the MC instead of a linear ansatz; measure diffraction on decompression from ~10 GPa, or explain the recovery; and either report the initial phase content of the magnetization crystals (for example, XRD on the loaded crystal or a sibling piece) or correlate the two runs' different pc with their initial P-3m1 content. The most direct test of the proposed mechanism would be to re-measure magnetization on a crystal decompressed from >10–20 GPa, where the authors say P-3m1 is retained. By their own Fig. 6a, JL(AA) is near zero or positive at ambient and turns negative above ~2 GPa, so such a crystal should lose FM at much lower pressure on recompression. Note also that, by Fig. 6a, FM recovery at ambient does not by itself discriminate between retained P-3m1 and reverted R-3.

3. The simulated "Tc → 0" (Fig. 6b) is not a meaningful counterpart of the experiment as presented. (Pp. 10–12, 15–16; Figs. 6b, S8.) With J1 ≈ 1.8 meV still ferromagnetic at 5–6 GPa and a finite single-ion anisotropy (Fig. S7), each layer should still order at a temperature of order the monolayer Tc. The authors themselves note (p. 12) that monolayers order at ~70% of bulk Tc. Random-sign interlayer coupling with 45% AA bonds removes the net moment, but it should give a layered ferro/antiferromagnetic or glassy state with a finite ordering temperature, not Tc = 0. The p. 12 statement that the high-pressure state is "generally AFM with zero bulk moment" implies the same. The MC uses only ten layers (20×20×1 cells per layer, three runs averaged), so with random interlayer signs the net moment of each realisation is dominated by which few bonds are AFM, and Tc "determined from the susceptibility" of the uniform magnetization is ill-defined. What the authors should do: report the order parameter used; compute per-layer (or staggered, layer-sign-gauged) magnetization and specific heat vs. T alongside the uniform susceptibility; check convergence with the number of layers and with disorder realisations; and state clearly whether the model predicts loss of order or only loss of net moment. The experimental quantity in Fig. 1 (M in 0.01 T and at 1 T) should then be compared with the corresponding simulated quantity, not with an ordering temperature.

B. Should address

4. No experimental evidence distinguishes AFM-coupled layers from loss or reduction of the Cr moment. (Fig. 1a; pp. 3, 12.) "Msat" is read at 1 T. For |JL(AA)| ≈ 0.2–0.3 meV per Cr (Fig. 6a), 1 T may not overcome the interlayer AFM coupling, so the drop of the 1 T moment cannot by itself separate the two scenarios. The claim that "the localized ionic magnetic moments are preserved" (p. 12) needs M(H) up to the full MPMS field (5–7 T) at 2 K above and below pc, looking for a field-induced (metamagnetic or spin-flop-like) recovery towards ~3 μB/Cr, and low-field M(T) above pc looking for an AFM cusp. If background subtraction limits this, state the field range over which the data are reliable.

5. Pressure determination and hydrostaticity. (Methods pp. 13–14; Fig. 1.) Pressure was set and read by ruby at room temperature, but the magnetization was measured at 2–35 K; pressure in turnbuckle Be–Cu cells typically changes on cooling. The one-sided "overpressure" error bars in Fig. 1 are not explained quantitatively. The liquid pressure medium is not named in any of the three methods, and its solidification and hydrostatic limit matter here, because the authors themselves show that mechanical shear (cutting, bending, mounting) creates the P-3m1 stacking. Non-hydrostatic stress could therefore drive the stacking change and the sample dependence of pc. Please name the media, give low-temperature pressure calibration (or an in situ manometer such as Pb Tc), and discuss whether the XRD, Raman and SQUID media are comparable in hydrostaticity.

6. Magnetization normalisation and the "identical trend" claim. (P. 3, Fig. 1.) The moment in μB/f.u. requires the mass of a sub-mm crystal in a DAC; how was it obtained (weighing, scaling to the ambient value)? Ambient Msat is quoted as ~2.8 μB/f.u. rather than ~3. Error bars on M after empty-cell subtraction are not shown, and the 6.24 GPa loop (green) is small and offset, so its signal-to-background ratio matters for "no FM signal". The text says Tc and Msat "decrease ... in an identical trend", but in Fig. 1 Msat falls by ~25% at 1.92 GPa while Tc falls by ~12%. Please quantify the uncertainties and reword, or explain the faster Msat decrease. The AC susceptibility measurements that supply most of the low-pressure Tc points are not described in the Methods (cell type, frequency, amplitude).

7. DFT/exchange methodology needs consistency checks. (Pp. 10–11, 15–16.) Structures are relaxed with PBE+U (Ueff = 2.8 eV)+D3, while exchange is computed with r2SCAN+rVV10; it is not stated whether U is applied in the latter, nor whether the AA cells were relaxed separately at each pressure or built from the ABC geometry. J2 and J3 are fixed to ambient neutron values; JL(ABC) ≈ 0.4 meV at ambient should be compared with the experimental interlayer coupling (from spin-wave data, ref. [47], or bilayer measurements). The single-stacking model gives Tc rising from 34 to 39 K (Fig. 6b), opposite in sign to the measured low-pressure slope (−3 K/GPa) where little stacking change is expected; this mismatch deserves discussion because it bears on how far the calculated J(p) can be trusted.

8. The Raman assignment is not established. (Pp. 8–9, Fig. 5.) Assigning "hard" modes to P-3m1 and "soft" modes to R-3 by compressibility alone is ad hoc. Both structures share the same CrBr6 layers, so intralayer modes should be nearly identical in frequency and pressure slope. The soft-to-hard crossover is placed at ~10 GPa, while XRD finds no R-3 above 8.4 GPa. The intensity loss is attributed to "progressive metallization", yet the authors' DFT gives a 1.39 eV gap at 10 GPa and metallization above 20 GPa; with 514.5 nm (2.41 eV) excitation, changing resonance conditions are a more likely cause. Please support the assignment with calculated Γ-point phonons for ABC and AA (or disordered) stacking under pressure, or present the Raman data as consistent with, not evidence for, two coexisting phases.

9. The phase-fraction and paracrystal analysis is model-dependent and partly undefined. (Pp. 7–8, Fig. 4, Fig. S4.) The "deformation by external force" applied between Figs. 4c,f and 4d,g is not described (uniaxial press, bending, cutting?), so the comparison cannot be reproduced. The R-3 end-member used for phase fractions already carries 0.244 occupancy on the 3a site, i.e., it is itself faulted, so the extracted "volume fractions" mix two kinds of disorder. Please define the deformation, give the paracrystal fit parameters and their uncertainties for the 10±2 nm block size, and state the phase fractions in Table S4 with their model assumptions in the main text.

C. Minor

10. "Intrinsic trimorphism" (abstract, conclusions): the monoclinic phase is not observed in this work, and the "trigonal phase" is an averaged description of stacking disorder (see item 1). Please reword.

11. Fig. 5 caption: the dashed line at ~10 GPa is labelled "pc", while pc denotes the magnetic critical pressure of 5.8 GPa elsewhere. Use a distinct symbol.

12. Introduction (p. 2) frames the Tc decrease as a puzzle because DFT predicts J1 to increase with pressure; the authors' own J1 (Fig. 6a) decreases above ~1 GPa. Reconcile the two statements.

13. P. 12 states that the simulation reaches "a state with 45% of P-3m1" at pc, while p. 12 (top) describes the AA fraction as a linear function up to a critical pressure, and Fig. S8 uses 5 GPa exchange parameters. State explicitly which J's and which fraction enter each point of Fig. 6b.

14. P. 5: "the shrinking of the lattice parameter c saturates above p ~ 11.8 GPa", which is not visible in Fig. 2e; point to the data or remove. Fig. 2e mixes experimental and calculated quantities on three axes; label which symbols are calculated in the legend more clearly.

15. Editorial: "Table 4S" (p. 8) vs. "Tab. S4"; "pc ≈ 5.8" lacks units (p. 13); two equations are numbered (1); references [15]/[29] and [17]/[37] are duplicates; ref. [28] "onolayers"; ref. [32] has a placeholder DOI; "We have invented the identical procedure" (p. 13) should read "adopted"; Ehrenfest relation (Eq. 1) should state which compressibility and expansion coefficients (volume vs. linear) are meant.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Make the diffraction carry the stacking claim: show l-scans along h−k ≠ 3n rods at each pressure and after decompression, and model them with explicit stacking-fault probabilities (ABC, twin, AA, monoclinic shift), as is standard for CrCl3/CrI3/RuCl3.
- Close the loop experimentally: measure magnetization on a crystal recovered from >10 GPa (with a confirmed retained high-pressure stacking) and recompress it; a strongly reduced pc would be compelling support for the mechanism.
- Add high-field M(H) at 2 K above pc to show whether the Cr moment is recoverable by field, which directly tests the "moments intact, AFM interlayer" picture.
- Feed the measured phase fractions (Table S4) into the spin simulations and report per-layer order parameters, so that theory and experiment are compared on the same observable.

5. Verdict: major revision
