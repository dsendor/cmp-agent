Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper measures the magnetization of bulk CrBr3 in a diamond anvil cell (DAC) up to about 6.2 GPa and finds that ferromagnetism vanishes near pc ≈ 5.8 GPa, well below the 8.4 GPa extrapolated from earlier work. Single-crystal and high-resolution XRD identify a trigonal P-3m1 polymorph, assigned to AA stacking with 2/3 Cr occupancy, that coexists with the R-3 phase at ambient pressure, grows under pressure and is retained after decompression from 21.5 GPa. DFT plus atomistic spin simulations attribute the loss of ferromagnetism to antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling in AA-stacked layers. A direct measurement of the magnetic collapse in a prototypical vdW insulator, combined with a stacking-based explanation, would interest the 2D-magnet and high-pressure communities. As it stands, however, the structural assignment and the structure-magnetism link are less firmly established than the abstract suggests.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The DAC SQUID magnetometry (Fig. 1) gives a direct measurement, not an extrapolation, of the suppression of Tc and the 2 K moment up to 6.24 GPa, together with recovery after decompression (Fig. S1b) and a second run (Fig. S1a).
- Single-crystal reciprocal-space reconstructions (Fig. 3) follow the 1/3 reflections across the transition and after decompression from 21.5 GPa. The paper explains clearly why powder XRD (Fig. S3) could not have resolved the P-3m1 component.
- The high-resolution reciprocal-space maps before and after deformation (Fig. 4c–h), with the paracrystal analysis, give a quantitative block size (10 ± 2 nm) and show that mechanical stress promotes the stacking change.
- The DFT results for interlayer coupling in the two stackings (Fig. 6a) give a concrete mechanism that can be tested, and the paper is open that the phase-fraction input is not determined experimentally.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The causal link between the AA (P-3m1) stacking and the collapse of ferromagnetism is not established by the data shown, and some of the data point the other way. This is the central interpretive claim in the abstract ("This behavior is attributed to the increasing number of AFM-coupled Cr moments in AA stackings") and in the Conclusions. Four problems:
   - (a) Different conditions. The structural evolution (Figs. 2e, 3, Table S4) and the Raman data (Fig. 5) were measured at room temperature on different crystals, starting from about 80% R-3 (Methods, p. 14). The magnetization was measured at 2–40 K on another crystal whose starting phase fraction is not given, and the paper itself says (pp. 5–6, 14) that the phase fraction depends on the sample and on handling. No structural data exist at low temperature under pressure, even though R-3 itself forms only on cooling through 420 K.
   - (b) Inconsistency with recovery. FM fully recovers, with unchanged Tc and Msat, after decompression from 10 GPa (Fig. S1b; Conclusions, p. 13). Yet R-3 is already undetectable at 8.4 GPa (Fig. 3c), and P-3m1 is retained on decompression from 21.5 GPa (Fig. 3d). The explanation offered, that "P-3m1 was not yet stabilized at 10 GPa while some volume fraction of R-3 persisted", is not supported by any diffraction measurement after release from about 10 GPa.
   - (c) AA versus random stacking. The paper concedes (p. 6) that XRD cannot distinguish ordered AA stacking from randomized ABC stacking, which gives the same 2/3 average occupancy. Only ordered AA stacking yields the negative J_L(AA) of Fig. 6a. Locally random stacking would include ABC, BA and other registries with different J_L. The diffuse c* intensity after decompression (Fig. 3d, lowest histogram) looks more like stacking disorder than an ordered AA phase.
   - (d) The TC(p) curve from the "stacking variation model" (Fig. 6b) uses an AA fraction that rises linearly up to a chosen critical pressure. The agreement with Fig. 1 is therefore largely built in, not a prediction.

   To support the attribution, the authors should do at least one of the following:
   - measure XRD (or at least the 1/3 reflections) on a crystal released from about 10 GPa, to reconcile it with Fig. S1b;
   - measure magnetization on a crystal recovered from above 10 GPa, ideally the 21.5 GPa sample, which by their model should show strongly reduced or absent FM at ambient pressure; this is a decisive and feasible test;
   - analyse the diffuse intensity along the (10L) rods to separate ordered AA from random stacking.

   Without these, the abstract and conclusions should present the AA mechanism as a hypothesis consistent with the data, not as the explanation.

B. Should address

2. There is no direct evidence that the state above pc has antiferromagnetically coupled layers rather than reduced intralayer order. The M(H) loops (Fig. 1a) go only to ±1 T, and Msat is defined as M(1 T). If AA-stacked layers couple antiferromagnetically with |J_L| up to about 0.25 meV (Fig. 6a), the corresponding spin-flip fields are on the order of 1 T or more. A drop in M(1 T) could then reflect interlayer AFM correlations, not a loss of moment, and a field-induced metamagnetic step would directly support the scenario of Fig. 6. The authors should measure M(H) to the full SQUID field range (5–7 T) at 5.2, 5.6 and 6.24 GPa, and should state that "Msat" at 1 T may not be the saturated moment.

3. Hydrostaticity and shear are not addressed, although the paper's own observation makes them central. The P-3m1 fraction grows with cutting, bending and deformation (Fig. 4; pp. 6, 14), so deviatoric stress in the DAC is a leading alternative driver of the stacking change and of pc. The "liquid pressure medium" is not named in any of the three DAC experiments. Its hydrostatic limit at room temperature and at the measurement temperature matters, because several common media freeze or become non-hydrostatic below 6–10 GPa. The authors should name the medium and its limit, report ruby linewidths, and discuss whether the sample dependence of pc (Fig. S1a) correlates with loading conditions.

4. Pressure at low temperature is not calibrated. Pressure was set and read at room temperature (p. 14), but Tc and Msat were measured at 2–40 K. In Be-Cu turnbuckle cells the pressure can change on cooling. The one-sided "overpressure" error bars in Fig. 1 are not explained quantitatively. The authors should state how the low-temperature pressure was estimated, or give the expected change on cooling, and explain the asymmetric error bars.

5. The Methods do not describe the AC susceptibility data (blue circles in Fig. 1): which cell, which pressure medium and calibration, and which sample. Since these points set the low-pressure part of the phase diagram, they need the same documentation as the DAC data.

6. The text and Fig. 1 disagree in places.
   - The text (p. 3) says that Tc and Msat "decrease in an identical trend". Yet in Fig. 1 Msat at about 2 GPa is already about 20% below its ambient value while Tc has fallen by only about 12%. The visual overlap depends on the choice of the right-hand axis scale.
   - Some M(1 T) values read from the inset loops (for example the 3.57 GPa curve) do not obviously match the orange squares at the same pressure.
   - The 6.24 GPa loop (green) seems to retain a small field-dependent moment, while its Msat square is plotted at zero.
   - Please tabulate M(1 T), Tc and the Tc criterion (minimum of dχ/dT) for each pressure, state the noise floor after subtracting the empty-cell background, and explain how the sample mass was obtained to convert to μB/f.u.

7. The "intrinsic trimorphism" claim conflicts with the evidence on the origin of P-3m1. The abstract and conclusions call P-3m1 an intrinsic third phase. Yet the Methods (p. 14) report that as-grown crystals showing pure R-3 develop P-3m1 after cutting, and that its abundance is "likely unintentionally modified" by mounting (p. 6). Together with the single-site 2/3 occupancy, this fits a stacking-fault-rich, mechanically induced state better than a distinct thermodynamic polymorph. The monoclinic phase was not measured in this work. The authors should either show P-3m1 in an untouched as-grown crystal (Fig. 4 is the natural place, but it shows only that P-3m1 "cannot be excluded"), or soften "intrinsic trimorphism" and "new phase".

8. The phase-fraction data needed for the model are not quantified. Table S4 gives R-3 volume fractions, which the paper says are lower bounds (p. 8), but they are not plotted against pressure or compared with the AA fraction assumed in Fig. 6b. The text (p. 12) quotes "45% P-3m1" at pc. The authors should overlay the measured lower-bound fractions on the linear model used for Fig. 6b, and test how sensitive the simulated pc is to the assumed fraction-versus-pressure law.

9. Several choices in the DFT and spin model need justification and checks.
   - Structures were relaxed with PBE+U (U_eff = 2.8 eV) plus D3, but exchange was computed with r2SCAN+rVV10. State whether U was used in the exchange calculations and how sensitive J1 and J_L are to U. J_L(AA) changes sign at about 2 GPa and stays small (≤ 0.25 meV), so its sign could depend on the functional.
   - J2 and J3 are fixed at experimental values from ref. 47, while J1 is computed. Give the spin normalization and Hamiltonian convention, since ref. 47 uses a different form, and say whether DFT J1 (≈ 2.1 meV) agrees with the inelastic neutron scattering value.
   - Say whether the 34 K ambient Tc in Fig. 6b required any rescaling.
   - Say whether AA-stacked supercells were relaxed separately under pressure, and how J_L was obtained for the partially occupied P-3m1 average structure, which cannot itself be computed.
   - Report finite-size checks of the 10-layer 20×20 cell near pc, where Tc collapses steeply.

10. The thermodynamic (Ehrenfest) argument on p. 3 is dismissed without numbers. Using the measured anomalies in thermal expansion and compressibility (ref. 34), the authors should state what dTc/dP the relation predicts and compare it with the measured −2 K/GPa at low pressure. This separates the magnetoelastic contribution from the stacking mechanism. Also check the form of Eq. (1); the standard Ehrenfest relation is dTc/dP = Δβ/Δα (or TcVΔα/ΔCp), and the factor of 3 needs explaining.

11. The Raman interpretation is weakly constrained.
   - Assigning modes to the two phases by pressure slope alone (Fig. 5) is not supported by a group-theory or DFT phonon analysis of the two stackings.
   - The vanishing intensities are attributed to "progressive metallization", but the paper's own DFT gives a 1.39 eV gap at 10 GPa (p. 10). With 514.5 nm excitation (2.41 eV), pressure-tuned resonance is a more likely cause.
   - The Raman crossover (about 10 GPa) differs from the XRD crossover (6.4–8.4 GPa); the paper should discuss this beyond "sample state".
   - Computed phonon frequencies and slopes for R-3 and for an AA cell would make the assignment testable.

C. Minor

12. Fig. 4a caption: P-3m1 is space group 164, not 150 (which is P321).

13. The 181.8 cm⁻¹ mode is labelled A2g (p. 8). A2g modes are Raman-inactive in D3d/C3i point groups; please check the symmetry label.

14. References need correcting.
   - Refs. 15 and 29 are the same paper, as are refs. 17 and 37.
   - "the ideally vacant 3a site centers [34]" (p. 4) cites the Kozlenko thermal-expansion paper, which looks like the wrong reference.
   - The statement "Another study predicted that a different magnetic order would occur at 17 GPa" (p. 2) has no citation.
   - Refs. 32, 41, 42 and 53 have placeholder DOIs ("https://doi.org/").

15. "pc ≈ 5.8" in the Conclusions lacks a unit. "Table 4S" (p. 8) should read "Table S4". Fig. 2 caption: the panel order (b/d) in the description is confusing.

16. Fig. 5 caption: "the green ones (blue)" is unclear, and the dashed line "tentatively marks the pc", but pc in this paper is the magnetic critical pressure (5.8 GPa), not the 10 GPa Raman crossover. Please use a different symbol.

17. Fig. 4: state which sample (and its prior handling) was used for the "pristine" maps, and the type and magnitude of the "deformation by an external force".

18. Ambient Tc in Fig. 6b (34 K) and the "39 K for the pure ABC phase" (p. 11, Fig. S8) are both quoted. Clarify that these correspond to 0 GPa and 5 GPa exchange parameters respectively.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Measure magnetization on the crystal recovered from 21.5 GPa (P-3m1 dominant, per Fig. 3d). Of all the tests listed here, this one most directly checks the AA-AFM mechanism.
- Extend the M(H) loops at and above 5 GPa to 5–7 T to look for interlayer spin-flip transitions.
- Model the diffuse intensity along (10L) using the paracrystal framework already developed, to distinguish ordered AA from random stacking, and give the resulting fractions against pressure.
- Plot the Table S4 phase fractions against pressure in the main text, next to Fig. 1 and the assumed AA fraction of Fig. 6b, so readers can judge the correlation directly.

5. Verdict: major revision
