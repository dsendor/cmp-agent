Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper combines high-pressure SQUID magnetometry in a diamond anvil cell, single-crystal and high-resolution X-ray diffraction, Raman spectroscopy, DFT and atomistic spin simulations to argue that ferromagnetism in bulk CrBr3 collapses near 5.8 GPa, and that the cause is a pressure-driven change in layer stacking: the rhombohedral R-3 (ABC) structure gives way to a trigonal P-3m1 structure, read as AA stacking, whose interlayer coupling is antiferromagnetic. The magnetization data under pressure fill a real gap (earlier work stopped near 3 GPa and extrapolated), and connecting stacking to magnetic order is timely for the CrX3 family. The novelty depends on two things: whether the P-3m1 "phase" really is AA stacking rather than an average over random stacking, and whether the magnetic data actually tell layered antiferromagnetism apart from other ways of losing the net moment.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Direct magnetization measurements of Tc(p) and the 1 T moment up to 6.24 GPa (Fig. 1), with a critical pressure well below the earlier extrapolated 8.4 GPa, and full recovery of the ferromagnetic state after release from 10 GPa (Fig. S1b).
- Careful single-crystal diffraction showing extra density on the nominally vacant 3a Cr site at ambient pressure (occupancies 0.878/0.244). The paper also gives a useful explanation of why powder diffraction cannot see the P-3m1 component (Fig. S3), and reports testing more than 30 crystals.
- Reciprocal-space maps around 10-1.13 and 30-3.15 before and after deformation, with a paracrystal analysis (Fig. 4, Fig. S4). This is a good way to measure how stacking disorder responds to mechanical stress.
- A physically motivated model linking stacking-dependent interlayer exchange to Tc, set against a single-stacking calculation that fails to reproduce the suppression (Fig. 6b).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The assignment of the high-pressure P-3m1 structure to AA stacking is not established, yet the whole magnetic mechanism rests on it (pp. 5–6, Fig. 2c,d, Fig. 3d; pp. 10–12). The refined P-3m1 model has one Cr site at 2/3 occupancy. That is exactly the average structure produced by honeycomb layers stacked at random over the three possible in-plane registries. The authors say as much on p. 6: they "are unable to distinguish" ordered AA stacking from randomized stacking of A, B and C planes. Fig. 3d (after decompression) also shows a broad, nearly continuous intensity distribution along c*, which looks like diffuse rod scattering from stacking disorder rather than sharp Bragg peaks from an ordered AA sequence. The magnetic model, however, assumes that the non-ABC layer pairs are specifically AA pairs with J_L(AA) < 0 (Fig. 6a). If the stacking is random, most new interlayer contacts would be AB- or BA-type registries (for example monoclinic-like or other shifted pairs), whose interlayer coupling may well be ferromagnetic. In that case the mechanism does not follow. AA stacking of a vdW halide, with Br sitting directly above Br, is also normally high in energy, and the paper gives no calculation showing it becomes favorable under pressure.
   What to do: (i) analyse the diffuse intensity along the 10L/01L rods at high pressure and after decompression, using a stacking-fault model that separates AA, AB and random registries (the paracrystal model of Fig. 4 is a natural starting point); (ii) compute DFT enthalpies H(p) of ABC, AA and other common stackings (AB/monoclinic-like) over 0–10 GPa; (iii) compute J_L for each registry that actually occurs in a random-stacking scenario and repeat the Tc simulation with those. If AA cannot be established, the text and abstract should present AA stacking as a hypothesis, not as "the AA-stacked trigonal phase".

2. The magnetization data do not tell the proposed interlayer-AFM state apart from other ways of losing the bulk moment, and the 1 T data look quantitatively inconsistent with the proposed coupling strength (Fig. 1a, Fig. 6a, p. 12). Msat is "read as the 1 T magnetization value", and it falls to zero by 6.24 GPa. According to Fig. 6a, though, the AFM interlayer coupling for AA stacking is only about −0.1 to −0.25 meV in the 5–8 GPa range, while the intralayer J1 stays near 1.5–2 meV, so each layer remains a robust ferromagnet. A Zeeman energy of 1 T on an S = 3/2 Cr ion (about 0.35 meV) should be comparable to or larger than the interlayer AFM energy. A layered antiferromagnet with this coupling should therefore undergo a spin-flip (or spin-flop) transition at or below 1 T and show a large magnetization at 1 T, as in few-layer CrI3. A vanishing 1 T moment is hard to reconcile with this unless J_L is much larger than calculated or something else suppresses the moment. In the same vein, if layers stay ferromagnetically ordered internally, the 0.01 T susceptibility above pc should still show an anomaly at the intralayer ordering temperature (bulk-like or monolayer-like, about 20–30 K). Fig. 1b shows no such anomaly at 6.24 GPa.
   What to do: show M(H) at 2 K above pc up to the highest field the SQUID/DAC setup allows (5–7 T), and look for a spin-flip field. Show χ(T) above pc over the full temperature range, with the background-subtracted signal and its uncertainty. State the expected spin-flip field from the calculated J_L. Discuss whether the data instead point to frustration or spin-glass behaviour, or to loss of order inside the layers.

B. Should address

3. The structural and magnetic data come from different samples, at different temperatures, with different initial phase content, so the correlation between stacking and Tc is qualitative only (pp. 3–5, 14). XRD and Raman were done at room temperature, magnetization at 2–35 K. The XRD crystal started at about 80% R-3, while the initial P-3m1 fraction of the magnetization crystal(s) is unknown. The authors report that pc is "sample-dependent" (Fig. S1a) and that the P-3m1 fraction changes when crystals are cut and mounted. In the model, about 45% P-3m1 is needed at pc, but at room temperature R-3 is still clearly present at 6.4 GPa and disappears only by 8.4 GPa. Pressure in the magnetization DAC was loaded and measured at room temperature, so the low-temperature pressure is not known. Please (i) quantify the R-3/P-3m1 fraction versus pressure from the high-pressure XRD (even as the lower bound mentioned in Table S4) and plot it next to Tc(p); (ii) give both pc values from Fig. S1a and the number of crystals measured magnetically; (iii) where possible, characterize the phase fraction of the magnetization crystals before loading; and (iv) state how the low-temperature pressure was determined, or estimate its uncertainty.

4. Reversibility does not fit the structural picture (p. 5, p. 13). Ferromagnetism with unchanged Tc and Msat comes back after release from 10 GPa. Yet P-3m1 is metastable after release from 21.5 GPa, and at 8.4 GPa R-3 was already undetectable. The explanation that P-3m1 "was not yet stabilized at 10 GPa" is post hoc and untested. Either XRD after release from about 10 GPa, or magnetization after release from above 15–20 GPa, would test the central claim directly: if the metastable P-3m1 state recovered from 21.5 GPa is non-ferromagnetic (or weakly ferromagnetic), that would strongly support the mechanism.

5. In the stacking-variation simulation, the AA fraction is put in by hand, so agreement with experiment is partly built in (pp. 11–12, Fig. 6b). The AA fraction is taken as "a linearly increasing function up to a critical pressure", but neither the critical pressure nor the endpoint fraction is stated. Exchange parameters are fixed at their 5 GPa values for the concentration dependence (Fig. S8). Please state every input to the Tc(p) curve and show how sensitive it is to the assumed fraction-versus-pressure law. Ideally, use the XRD-derived fraction from concern 3 rather than a free function.

6. The MC "Tc" should be defined and shown to reflect ordering, not just cancellation of the net moment (pp. 11–12, 15–16). With J1 about 1.5–2 meV and random signs of the weak interlayer coupling, the layers should still order individually, as monolayer CrBr3 does near 27 K, as the authors themselves note on p. 12. A Tc taken from the uniform susceptibility of a ten-layer cell can fall to zero just because layer moments cancel. Please report layer-resolved order parameters, or the staggered magnetization, and the specific heat from the simulations. That would also show what experimental signature to expect above pc (see concern 2).

7. The sign of the interlayer exchange depends on small energy differences, and no robustness check is reported (pp. 10–11, 15). J_L(AA) changes sign near 2 GPa and reaches only about −0.25 meV. The Methods use PBE+U (U_eff = 2.8 eV) + D3 for structures and r2SCAN+rVV10 for exchange, and it is unclear whether a U was applied in the r2SCAN step. Please show J_L(ABC) and J_L(AA) for at least one other U value or functional, and state the supercell sizes and the number of magnetic configurations used in the fits. Please also compare the calculated ambient interlayer coupling with experimental estimates, such as the spin-wave dispersion along c in ref. 47.

8. The Raman two-phase assignment is not compelling (pp. 8–9, Fig. 5). Intralayer phonons of the same CrBr3 layer stacked in different registries should have nearly identical frequencies and pressure slopes. The data in Fig. 5 look like single branches whose slope changes near 10 GPa, consistent with a compressibility crossover (ref. 40). If two phases coexisted below 10 GPa, each mode should appear twice, as a soft and a hard branch. The 10 GPa dashed line, labelled pc in the caption, also conflicts with the magnetic pc of 5.8 GPa and with the XRD transition between 6.4 and 8.4 GPa. Please show raw spectra at selected pressures with multi-peak fits demonstrating coexisting branches, or tone the Raman interpretation down to a slope change. The "progressive metallization" invoked to explain the loss of intensity also conflicts with the authors' own DFT gap of 1.39 eV at 10 GPa.

9. Magnetization under pressure lacks quantitative details (Fig. 1, p. 14). Please state the sample mass and dimensions in the DAC, and explain how M was normalized to µB/f.u. at each pressure. Show the size of the empty-cell background relative to the sample signal at 6.24 GPa, along with error bars on Msat. Name the pressure-transmitting medium and say whether it is hydrostatic up to 6 GPa at room temperature and on cooling. Non-hydrostatic stress is especially relevant here, because the authors show the stacking is very sensitive to mechanical deformation. The loops in Fig. 1a also seem to be vertically offset or not centered on zero. Please plot them unshifted, or state the offsets.

10. The "trimorphism" claim is overstated (abstract, p. 13). The monoclinic C2/m phase is the known high-temperature phase above 420 K, and it was not observed in this study. The ambient-condition diffraction shows R-3 plus a stacking-disordered component. Please rephrase so the abstract does not suggest that three phases were found in the measured crystals.

C. Minor

11. Fig. 1 caption: the "low-pressure AC susceptibility" data (blue circles, up to about 4.5 GPa) are not described in the Methods (cell, frequency, amplitude). Please add this.
12. Fig. 2e: the experimental R-3 a and c values and the calculated values use different conventions ("a·tan(30)", "c/3"). Please define these clearly. The pressure axis ends at 16 GPa, while the text quotes data up to 21.5 GPa.
13. Equation numbering: both the Ehrenfest relation (p. 3) and the Hamiltonian (p. 16) are labelled (1).
14. References [15] and [29] are duplicates. Reference [32] (Supporting Information) and several other DOI links are incomplete. "Table 4S" (p. 8) should read "Table S4".
15. "Rombohedral" in Fig. 2a. On p. 9, "the green ones (blue)" in the Fig. 5 caption is unclear. "sizes of the blocks ... width" (p. 8): the paracrystal block size of (10 ± 2) nm comes from the deformed sample. Please say whether pristine crystals give a different value.
16. Fig. 3 caption: give the actual pressures for panels b and c rather than "lower/higher than the transition pressure" (they are 6.4 and 8.4 GPa).
17. p. 2: "Another study predicted that a different magnetic order would occur at 17 GPa" needs a citation.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Plot Tc(p), Msat(p) and the XRD phase fraction (or diffuse-scattering measure of stacking disorder) against pressure on one figure, so the reader can judge the correlation directly.
2. Use DFT stacking enthalpies versus pressure to motivate why AA (or random) stacking appears under compression.
3. A low-temperature high-pressure diffraction point (for example 5–7 GPa at about 10 K) would remove the temperature mismatch between the structural and magnetic data.
4. Say explicitly in the conclusions that a microscopic magnetic probe (neutron diffraction, XMCD, or µSR under pressure) would distinguish layered AFM from other non-ferromagnetic states, and make clear which of the current conclusions depend on that.

5. Verdict: major revision
