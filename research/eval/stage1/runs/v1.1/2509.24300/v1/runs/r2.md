Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports resistance measurements on exfoliated Ca2RuO4 nanoflakes (samples 1–5, 8) and MBE films on LaAlO3 (samples 6, 7). From these it claims a "Bose glass" of weakly localized Cooper pairs that sets in near 220 K, far above the superconducting onset (~100 K) that the same group reported in Ref. [20]. It also claims two quantum critical sheet resistances of ≈(5/2)h/e² and ≈(5/2)h/4e², and that thinning relieves the RuO6 octahedral distortion and so tunes the electron correlation. If the claims held, they would matter a great deal: preformed pairs at 220 K in a ruthenate, and non-universal critical resistances, would be striking. But every claim rests on the functional form of ρ(T) over narrow temperature windows and on a β-function construction derived from that same ρ(T). No independent probe of pairing, structure or criticality is given.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The resistivity of the nanoflakes is 3–4 orders of magnitude lower than in bulk, with a crossover from hopping-like to metallic/superconducting-like behaviour across samples (Fig. 1). If it is intrinsic, this is an interesting observation in a much-studied Mott insulator.
- Plotting nanoflakes, MBE films and bulk literature data on one β(ln g) plot (Fig. 5) and one dρ/dT-vs-T diagram (Fig. 4) is a compact, if qualitative, way to organize a varied set of samples.
- The Ref. [11] criterion, the sign of β′ (σ ~ ln T against ρ ~ ln(1/T)), is applied systematically to every sample, and Eqs. (3)–(4) are derived correctly.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The ρ ~ ln(1/T) vs σ ~ ln T distinction cannot carry the weight placed on it (Figs. 2(b), 3, 6(a); Eqs. 2–4).**
   - *The windows are too narrow and the comparison is not quantitative.* The "Bose glass" identification rests on ρ ~ ln(1/T) fitting "better" than σ ~ ln T over 101–220 K (sample 4) and 105–183 K (sample 5), roughly a factor of two in T. The "Fermi glass" identification uses 220–290 K, only a factor of ~1.3 (ln T from 5.4 to 5.67 in Fig. 2(b), Fig. 3(a,b)). Over such windows, and with ρ changing by tens of percent, the two forms differ only at second order. No residuals, χ² values or fit-range sensitivity are given.
   - *The β′ criterion is a curvature test.* The sign of β′ (Eqs. 3–4) is the sign of the curvature of ln g against ln T. Any ρ(T) that approaches a maximum, here the maximum near 90–110 K before the superconducting drop (Fig. 1(c)), necessarily has β′ < 0 on its high-temperature side, whatever the mechanism. Examples are a crossover to a metallic or ferromagnetic state (Ref. [20] reports ferromagnetism at 180 K in these flakes), a parallel conducting channel, or a percolating metallic minority phase. So the upturns in Fig. 6(a) for samples 4–7 do not by themselves discriminate bosons from fermions.
   - *Weak localization at 100–220 K needs a quantitative check.* Interpreting any ln T term as a quantum interference correction at 100–220 K requires a phase-coherence length longer than the elastic mean free path at those temperatures. It also requires a prefactor of the expected order (~e²/πh per conducting layer for fermions, the analogous scale for bosons). Neither the fitted ln T coefficients nor these estimates are reported.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Report the fitted coefficients per layer and compare them with the theoretical prefactors.
     - Give quantitative goodness-of-fit comparisons with residuals over the stated ranges.
     - Test alternative descriptions (a two-channel model, thermally activated plus metallic parallel conduction, and so on).
     - Show the ln T behaviour over a wider range of temperature or tuning parameter.

2. **There is no direct evidence for Cooper pairs at 180–220 K (Abstract; p. 5; Conclusion).**
   - The claim that "bosons—Cooper pairs—persist up to high temperatures around 220 K" is supported only by the curvature criterion of concern 1 and by the "vertical flow" in Fig. 5(b). The vertical flow is equally generic. Where ρ(T) passes through a maximum, d ln g/d ln T passes through zero and changes sign while g is almost constant. β then sweeps vertically at fixed ln g for any mechanism that produces a resistance maximum.
   - None of the standard tests for pairing above T_c are presented for samples 4–7:
     - magnetoresistance in the 100–220 K window (the orbital response of pairs or vortices against the weak-localization or weak-antilocalization magnetoconductance of fermions);
     - field dependence of the temperature at which the ln(1/T) regime starts;
     - diamagnetic or Nernst signals on these samples;
     - paraconductivity (fluctuation-conductivity) analysis near T_c.
   - The magnetization argument (p. 5) cites diamagnetism setting in near 140 K in Ref. [20], which does not match 220 K. It also leans on Refs. [27, 28], where the 220 K diamagnetism was attributed to Joule heating and Ref. [27] was retracted. That argument does not support the claim.
   - *What the authors should do:* Add field-dependent transport (at least R(T, B) for samples 4 and 5 up to several tesla, in both orientations) and, if possible, magnetization on the same flakes. Alternatively, restrict the claim to "ρ(T) consistent with the ln(1/T) form proposed for weakly localized bosons."

3. **The two "quantum critical sheet resistances" are not established as critical points, and their values are internally inconsistent (Fig. 6; p. 8; Conclusion).**
   - *R_BG–FG.* β_c = −1.6 is a dashed line drawn between the low-temperature plateaus of samples 1–3 and those of samples 4–7 in Fig. 6(a). It is not a fixed point found by any crossing or scaling analysis. The text then says the resistance is "estimated from g_c = −1.6", but g is a dimensionless conductance and cannot be negative. The route from β_c to 65.7 kΩ is not given and cannot be reproduced. On the VW curve, β = −1.6 corresponds to ln g ≈ −1.5 to −2, i.e. R□/layer of order 20–30 kΩ, not 65.7 kΩ. Fig. 6(a)'s caption calls β_c the boundary "between the Bose glass and Mott insulating phases", whereas the text calls it the Bose glass–Fermi glass transition.
   - *R_SC–BG.* The 16.5/16.8 kΩ value is read from a bias-current series on sample 4 (Fig. 6(b)), with currents up to 5 µA. Current is not a quantum tuning parameter, and Ref. [28], cited by the authors, documents Joule heating dominating current-induced phenomena in Ca2RuO4. A separatrix in such a family of curves therefore cannot be called a quantum critical resistance without a heating analysis: estimates of dissipated power and sample temperature, and dV/dI against T.
   - *Magnitudes disagree between figures.* In Fig. 3(a), sample 4 peaks at R□/layer ≈ 125 kΩ, consistent with ρ ≈ 7×10⁻³ Ω cm at 10 nm in Fig. 1(b) and ~16 layers. In Fig. 6(b), the same "sample 4" peaks at ≈ 41 kΩ, a factor of ~3 lower and closer to sample 5's values in Fig. 3(b). Either the per-layer normalization or the sample labelling differs, and the quoted 16.8 kΩ and 65.7 kΩ depend directly on it.
   - *No uncertainties.* None are given, yet the R□/layer normalization relies on SEM-tilt thicknesses and an assumed number of layers. The claimed 5/2 ratios (64.5 vs 65.7 kΩ; 16.1 vs 16.8 kΩ) agree only at the 2–4 % level. The thickness uncertainty alone is likely ≥10 %.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Explain the conversion from β_c to R.
     - Resolve the Fig. 3(a)/6(b) discrepancy.
     - Give error budgets.
     - Either show a genuine quantum-critical analysis (isotherm crossing and finite-size scaling in T with a tuning parameter such as field or gate) or present these as phenomenological crossover resistances.

4. **The claim that thinning relieves RuO6 distortion and tunes correlations "rather than impurities" has no structural or compositional data on the measured flakes (pp. 4, 6, 9; Abstract; Conclusion).**
   - *Phase identity.* Phase is identified by crystal shape in SEM (Methods), and the c-axis value of 12.278 Å for "nanoscale single crystals" appears to come from XRD of the parent crystals. No Raman, TEM/electron diffraction, EDX or XRD is shown for the flakes that were measured.
   - *Alternative explanations for low resistivity.* The flakes' resistivity is 3–4 orders lower than bulk. That is the observation all the subsequent physics rests on, and it is equally consistent with parallel conduction through a metallic minority phase (CaRuO3, Ru metal, Sr- or Ru-rich inclusions), a conducting surface or interface layer, or oxygen nonstoichiometry. Such a percolating metallic path would also naturally produce the resistance maxima and the non-zero-resistance "SIT" of samples 6 and 7.
   - *Thickness does not order the ground states.* Samples 1 and 2 (25 nm) and 3 (35 nm) are Mott/VRH, sample 5 (30 nm) is superconducting, and sample 8 (300 nm) has low resistivity but is not discussed. So the data do not show the systematic thickness control the abstract claims.
   - *Unsupported statement.* "The effect of impurity-induced disorder in thin films cannot explain this phenomenon" (p. 6) is asserted without evidence.
   - *What the authors should do:* Provide structural characterization (Raman octahedral modes, or TEM with diffraction) and composition data on the measured flakes. Show resistivity against thickness for all flakes. If this is not available, remove or soften the distortion-relief and "not disorder" conclusions.

**B. Should address**

5. **The β-function construction and the "universal curve" in Fig. 5(a) have limited discriminating power.**
   - L² = DT^{−p} with p = 1 is fixed by hand, and β scales as 1/p.
   - g is computed per RuO2 layer for flakes 10–300 nm thick and for bulk crystals, which assumes fully decoupled 2D layers. This is not justified, especially for samples 3D in thickness, and it is not stated how the bulk data of Ref. [26] were converted.
   - For any hopping law ln g ≈ −(T0/T)^α, β = −(2/p) d ln g/d ln T is automatically linear in ln g. The collapse of the strongly localized data onto a line of slope ~1 is therefore largely built in.
   - The authors should show how the collapse depends on p and on the layer normalization, and should not present Fig. 5(a) as evidence that weak-localization scaling theory applies to Ca2RuO4.

6. **Few samples, with gaps in the record.**
   - The Bose-glass behaviour rests on two flakes (4, 5) and two MBE films (6, 7).
   - "Sample 9" appears in Fig. 4(a) (T^SC–BG and T^BG–FG) but is described nowhere, and no data are shown.
   - Sample 8 appears in Figs. 1 and 5 without discussion.
   - Missing details: no statement of measurement currents for the R(T) curves, of whether results survive thermal cycling, or of contact configuration and geometry (needed for ρ and R□).
   - Please describe every plotted sample, list thickness, lateral dimensions and measurement current for each in a table, and show reproducibility.

7. **The superconductivity of samples 4 and 5 is assumed rather than shown here.**
   - The whole picture (SC–BG–FG) requires samples 4 and 5 to be superconductors. Fig. 1(c) shows a resistance drop, and Fig. 6(b) a near-zero value at 1 nA for one sample. No field dependence of the drop, no I–V characteristics, and no Meissner response are shown in this manuscript.
   - The authors call samples 6 and 7 "near the quantum critical point" only because they do not reach zero resistance.
   - At minimum, include R(T, B) and I–V curves through the transition for samples 4 and 5. This also bears on concern 4 (filamentary metallic paths against bulk superconductivity).

8. **The Mott/VRH and Fermi-glass assignments for samples 1–3 (Fig. 2) are weakly supported.**
   - A 2D Mott-VRH exponent (1/3) is applied to 25–35 nm films without justification or comparison with Efros–Shklovskii (1/2), Arrhenius or 3D (1/4) forms. A reduced activation energy plot, w = d ln ρ/d ln T against T, would discriminate them.
   - The deviation points (arrows, ~208–233 K) are what define the "Fermi glass–Mott transition" temperatures plotted in Fig. 4(a), yet they come from where a straight line visually departs from the data. No criterion or uncertainty is given.

9. **The phase diagram in Fig. 4 is qualitative and partly hand-drawn.**
   - dρ/dT ≡ [ρ(120 K) − ρ(280 K)]/ΔT is used as a "localization strength". It depends directly on the thickness normalization (concern 4) and on whether the sample is already ordered by 120 K. The bulk points of Fig. 4(b) come from systems tuned by La doping, pressure, electric field and Sr substitution, measured with different geometries.
   - The SC, BG and FG region boundaries appear to be sketched rather than determined.
   - Please state how the boundaries were drawn, add error bars, and avoid language such as "High-temperature superconductivity appears through the mediation of the Bose glass phase" (p. 6), which the diagram cannot establish.

10. **The role of the 180 K ferromagnetic transition is not discussed.**
    - Ref. [20] reports ferromagnetism at 180 K in these nanofilms, inside the claimed Bose-glass window (101–220 K for sample 4, 105–183 K for sample 5).
    - Spin-disorder scattering changing across a magnetic transition is a ready alternative source of changes in curvature of ρ(T).
    - The authors should show whether the BG–FG "transition" temperatures correlate with the magnetic transition in the same samples.

11. **The bulk "superconductivity" analysis in Fig. S2 is not sound as presented.**
    - A 2D Aslamazov–Larkin form, which needs a sheet resistance and gives the universal prefactor e²/16ħ, is applied to the 3D resistivity (in µΩ cm) of a bulk crystal under 2.6 GPa from Ref. [19].
    - T_c0 = 12 K and R_N = 44 µΩ cm are chosen by hand, and the slope appears free.
    - It is concluded that "superconductivity coexisting with ferromagnetism may also occur in bulk crystals", contradicting the original authors' interpretation, from a single digitized curve.
    - Either use a dimensionally consistent 3D AL form with the prefactor fixed, or drop the claim from the main text (p. 7) and Fig. 4(b).

**C. Minor**

12. Fig. 3 caption: for sample 5 it says the data fit "R□/layer ~ ln T", whereas the text and fit say ln(1/T).
13. R_SC–BG is 16.8 kΩ in the text (p. 8) and 16.5 kΩ in the Fig. 6(b) label and caption.
14. "g_c = −1.6" (p. 8) should presumably be β_c. The caption/text mismatch on which phases β_c separates (concern 3) should also be fixed.
15. The comparison of 5/2 with Fisher et al.'s 8/π ≈ 2.55 and with the ν = 5/2 Pfaffian state (p. 8) is numerological. The Pfaffian has no evident connection to this system. Please remove the Pfaffian analogy, or state clearly that it is speculation.
16. Definitions: a Bose glass is conventionally an insulating, compressible phase with vanishing dc conductivity at T = 0. Here it is used for "weak localization of bosons" with "finite conductivity" (Abstract). Please define the term as used, and distinguish it from the anomalous/Bose metal (Refs. [8, 9]).
17. Fig. 4(a) is labelled "dρ/dT < 0" while the axis is |dρ/dT|. The text's definition dρ(= ρ(120 K) − ρ(280 K))/dT mixes a finite difference with a derivative notation; write Δρ/ΔT explicitly.
18. Methods: state how R□/layer is computed (layer spacing c/2, number of layers) and give the uncertainty of the SEM-tilt thickness measurement.
19. Typos: "Supplementaty" (p. 6), "more assertive" (p. 9, presumably "more pronounced"), "fermi glass" (p. 5), "Cooper pairs condensate" (p. 3).

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Measure magnetotransport R(T, B) on samples 4 and 5 (and on a Mott-like flake as a control) across 100–220 K. This is the single most informative addition for separating bosonic from fermionic localization and for testing pairing above T_c.
2. Characterize the measured flakes themselves (Raman, TEM/SAED, EDX) to establish phase purity, and to test directly the claimed relief of octahedral distortion against metallic inclusions.
3. Replace "fits better" with quantitative model comparison (residuals, information criteria, fit-window sensitivity). Report the fitted ln T coefficients in units of e²/h per layer.
4. Recast the "quantum critical resistances" either as a proper scaling analysis with a quantum tuning parameter, or as phenomenological crossover values, and give an explicit error budget.

**5. Verdict**

Verdict: major revision
