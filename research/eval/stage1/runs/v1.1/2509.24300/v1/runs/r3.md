Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports transport on exfoliated/dispersed Ca2RuO4 nanoflakes (samples 1–5, 8) and MBE films on LaAlO3 (samples 6, 7), and argues that (i) the thin samples are 3–4 orders of magnitude less resistive than bulk because thinning relieves RuO6 distortion, (ii) samples 4–7 show a Fermi glass to Bose glass crossover marked by ρ ~ ln(1/T) and a "vertical flow" of the β-function, implying localized Cooper pairs up to ~220 K, and (iii) two critical sheet resistances, 65.7 kΩ ≈ (5/2)h/e² and 16.8 kΩ ≈ (5/2)h/4e², bound the Bose glass phase. If established, preformed, weakly localized Cooper pairs at ~220 K in a correlated ruthenate would be a striking result. As presented, however, the boson claim rests almost entirely on the curvature of R(T) over narrow temperature windows in two superconducting flakes, and the structural and normalization assumptions behind the headline numbers are not demonstrated.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The material system is interesting: a Mott insulator whose ground state is very sensitive to lattice distortion, with earlier reports from the same group of superconductivity in nanoflakes (Ref. [20]).
- The data set covers both insulating (1–3) and superconducting (4, 5) flakes, a thick reference flake (8) and MBE films (6, 7), and places them next to bulk literature data (Fig. 1(a), Fig. 4(b)).
- The β-function framework (Eqs. 1–4) gives a compact, testable criterion (the sign of β′) for separating ln T behaviour in σ from ln(1/T) behaviour in ρ, and it is applied the same way to every sample.
- Fig. 3(c) and Fig. S1 for the MBE films show ρ ~ ln(1/T) over more than a decade in temperature with a factor of ~3 change in ρ. That is a much more discriminating range than the flake data.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The existence of Cooper pairs and a Bose glass up to ~220 K is inferred only from the functional form of R(T), over windows too narrow to discriminate, and no independent test of pairing is given. For sample 4 the "BG" window is 101–220 K (ln T ≈ 4.6–5.4, Fig. 3(a)), where R□/layer changes by about 50%. For sample 5 it is 105–183 K (ln T ≈ 4.65–5.2, Fig. 3(b)), where R changes from ~32 to ~40 kΩ, about 25%. When the relative change is this small, ρ = ρ0 + ρ1 ln(1/T) and σ = σ0 + σ1 ln T are the same to first order and differ only in curvature, which is also sensitive to the choice of window, to background (for example phonon) contributions and to any parallel channel. No goodness-of-fit numbers, residuals or sensitivity to the window endpoints are given. The β′ sign test (Eqs. 2–4, Fig. 6(a)) is the same curvature information re-expressed, so it is not independent evidence. The "vertical flow" in Fig. 5(b) is what any sample with a resistance maximum produces: β = −2 d ln g/d ln T passes through zero and changes sign at nearly constant g. Superconducting fluctuations of a fermionic normal state, a magnetic or structural anomaly (the authors themselves cite a ferromagnetic transition near 180 K in Ref. [20]), or a parallel metallic path would all do the same. Without a measurement that probes pairing, the 220 K pair-formation claim is not supported. The authors should:
   - measure magnetoresistance at several temperatures in the claimed FG, BG and SC regimes, in perpendicular and parallel field. Positive, pair-breaking MR whose scale tracks a pairing field, against negative weak-localization MR for fermions, is the standard discriminator;
   - report the I–V characteristics in the BG window;
   - give fit residuals or reduced χ² for both functional forms over each window, and show how the inferred crossover temperatures move when the window endpoints change;
   - if possible, measure diamagnetism or Nernst signal on the same flakes;
   - at minimum, restate the 220 K claim as consistent with, not evidence of, preformed pairs.

2. The two quantum critical resistances, a headline result of the abstract and conclusion, depend on an undocumented normalization, internally inconsistent data and an unclear extraction.
   - R□/layer requires the length, width and thickness of each flake and an assumed layer spacing. Thickness comes from tilted SEM images, and neither the geometry nor its uncertainty is reported. A 20–30% error in thickness or effective width alone moves the critical values by more than the difference between 5/2 and, say, 2 or 3.
   - For the same sample 4, Fig. 3(a) shows the R□/layer maximum at ≈125 kΩ (≈60 kΩ at room temperature, consistent with Fig. 1(a)–(c)), while in Fig. 6(b), at the lowest bias currents, the maximum is ≈40 kΩ. A factor of ~3 between two figures of the same sample, with no stated change of cooldown, contacts or normalization, directly undermines the 16.5/16.8 kΩ and 65.7 kΩ values read off Fig. 6(b).
   - It is not explained how a critical β value, β_c = −1.6 (called "g_c = −1.6" on p. 8), maps to 65.7 kΩ.
   - In Fig. 6(b), the 65.7 kΩ dashed line marks the low-T level of the 5000 nA curve. It is not a temperature-independent separatrix between families of curves, which is what a critical resistance at a quantum phase transition requires. The 16.5 kΩ marker likewise sits at no evident crossing point.
   - Bias currents up to 5 µA through a flake on SiO2 at a few kelvin can cause significant Joule heating. The paper itself cites the history of heating artifacts in current-driven Ca2RuO4 (Refs. [27, 28]), but no electron-temperature check, pulsed-current measurement or dependence on dissipated power is shown.

   The authors should tabulate geometry with uncertainties, explain the Fig. 3(a)/6(b) discrepancy, describe explicitly how each critical resistance is extracted, show crossing or separatrix behaviour in R(T) at fixed tuning parameter, and rule out heating, for example by plotting R against dissipated power, comparing with the R(T) calibration, or using pulsed bias. Without this, the factor 5/2 and the comparisons to 8/π and the ν = 5/2 Pfaffian should be removed.

3. The central physical interpretation, that thinning relieves RuO6 distortion and so tunes correlation rather than disorder, has no structural or compositional measurement on the flakes that were measured.
   - The only lattice parameter for the "nanoscale single crystals" (c = 12.278 Å) appears to come from XRD of the parent crystals before sonication and dispersion, not from the flakes. No TEM, electron diffraction, Raman (which is sensitive to the L/S phases and octahedral tilts in Ca2RuO4), EDS or thickness-dependent structural data are shown.
   - The authors' own data do not show a thickness trend. The superconducting samples are 10 nm (sample 4) and 30 nm (sample 5), while Mott-like samples 1–3 are 25, 25 and 35 nm.
   - The MBE films have the shorter c axis (11.592 Å), which the authors say means stronger distortion, yet they are placed in the Bose glass region of Fig. 4(a) rather than the Mott region. This contradicts the stated rule that strong distortion gives a Mott transition near 220 K (p. 6).
   - The 3–4 orders of magnitude drop in resistivity relative to bulk (Fig. 1(a)) could equally come from metallic inclusions or surface layers: CaRuO3, which forms in the same growth and which the authors distinguish from Ca2RuO4 only by crystal shape in SEM, Ru or RuO2 from sonication damage, or oxygen off-stoichiometry. Parallel conduction through such a path would also produce resistance maxima and shifted "transition" temperatures. The statement that "impurity-induced disorder in thin films cannot explain this phenomenon" (p. 6) is asserted, not tested.

   The authors should provide structural (Raman, TEM/SAED) and compositional (EDS/EELS) characterization of measured flakes, ideally the same flakes after transport, and address the non-monotonic thickness dependence directly. Otherwise they should withdraw the claim that thickness is the control parameter.

B. Should address

4. Sample statistics are thin for the claims made. The Bose glass and superconducting phases rest on two flakes (4, 5). Fig. 4(a) also contains a "Sample 9" (T_SC–BG and T_BG–FG symbols) that is not described anywhere in the text, Methods or Fig. 1. The authors should add a table listing each sample's thickness, lateral dimensions, contact geometry, room-temperature ρ, dρ/dT, and the transition temperatures used in Fig. 4. They should also state how many flakes were measured in total (including any that did not fit the classification) and whether the curves are reproducible on thermal cycling.

5. The Fermi glass interpretation above ~220 K is not quantitatively tested. Weak-localization corrections at 220–300 K require a phase-coherence length larger than the elastic mean free path. For a 2D description per RuO2 layer, they also require the layers to be effectively decoupled. Neither is discussed. The authors should compare the fitted ln T slopes with the expected per-layer prefactor (of order e²/πh per conducting sheet), and state the number of layers assumed for each flake. For samples 1–3, Fig. 2(b) gives σ in S/cm, so this comparison cannot be made as presented. Electron–electron interaction corrections, which also give σ ~ ln T, should also be considered. Similarly, assigning 2D VRH with exponent 1/3 (Fig. 2(a)) over about a decade of ρ should be compared with exponents 1/4, 1/2 and 1 (activated), for example with a Zabrodskii log-derivative plot.

6. The β-function scaling relies on the unjustified choice p = 1 in L² = DT^−p (p. 7). The "single universal curve" in Fig. 5(a) is partly built in. For VRH, ln g ∝ −(T0/T)^(1/3), so β_EXP = (2/3p) ln g, linear in ln g whatever the sample, and the slope matching β_VW depends on p. The authors should show how the collapse and the critical β_c change with p (for example p = 1, 2, 3), and state which features of Fig. 5 are independent of p.

7. The superconducting endpoint of the phase diagram needs stronger characterization. Fig. 1(c) and Fig. 6(b) show broad transitions with T_c^onset ~100 K. Zero resistance (within the noise floor) and the temperature at which it is reached are not stated for sample 5, and the low-T tail of sample 4 is not shown on a linear scale. No field dependence or Meissner data on these flakes are presented. Because the SC–BG transition temperature (triangles in Fig. 4(a)) and R_SC–BG depend on where superconductivity sets in, the criterion for T_SC–BG should be defined and the zero-resistance state documented.

8. The localization-strength axis of Fig. 4 is close to circular and is compared across inconsistent definitions. dρ/dT = [ρ(120 K) − ρ(280 K)]/ΔT is itself a property of the R(T) curves being classified. It is plotted as |dρ/dT| while the region is labelled "dρ/dT < 0". Literature points in Fig. 4(b) come from different temperature windows (and include data below structural transitions). The authors should state how the literature values were computed, use the same window for every point, and acknowledge that the diagram is a re-plotting of R(T) rather than an independent tuning parameter.

9. The diamagnetism argument (pp. 5–6) links the ~220 K scale to diamagnetism seen in bulk under current (Ref. [27], retracted, attributed to Joule heating, see Ref. [28]) and to magnetization of different nanoscale samples with an onset near 140 K, not 220 K. This does not support the Bose glass assignment in samples 4 and 5. Either measure magnetization on comparable flakes or remove the argument.

10. The Aslamazov–Larkin analysis of bulk 2.6 GPa data (Supplementary Fig. S2, p. 7) applies a 2D AL formula to a bulk resistivity in µΩ cm, with T_c0 = 12 K and R_N = 44 µΩ cm set by hand. The 2D AL prefactor e²/16ħ requires a sheet resistance, so the units in the formula as written do not match. The deviation at small T_c0/(T − T_c0) is not discussed. The resulting suggestion that bulk Ca2RuO4 superconducts under pressure is speculative and tangential to the paper. It should either be done properly (3D AL, free parameters with uncertainties, comparison with the ferromagnetic-metal explanation of Ref. [19]) or removed.

C. Minor

11. Internal inconsistencies:
    - The Fig. 3 caption says sample 5 fits "R□/layer ~ ln T"; the text says ln(1/T).
    - R_SC–BG is 16.8 kΩ in the text (p. 8) and 16.5 kΩ in Fig. 6(b).
    - The Fig. 6 caption calls β_c the boundary "between Bose glass and Mott insulating phases"; the text calls it the BG–FG boundary.
    - p. 8 writes "g_c = −1.6" for β_c.
    - The text gives BG onsets of 67 and 81 K for samples 6 and 7, while Fig. 3(c) shows sample 7 ending near ln T ≈ 4.2 (≈67 K) and Fig. S1 shows sample 6 ending near ≈70 K.
    - Note that (5/2)h/e² = 64.5 kΩ and (5/2)h/4e² = 16.1 kΩ. The quoted values deviate by 2–4%, which should be stated alongside the uncertainty.

12. Methods are missing several items: the excitation current used for each R(T) curve in Figs. 1–3 (relevant given the strong bias dependence in Fig. 6(b)), how the layer thickness entering R□/layer is defined, the thermometer location and calibration in the ³He insert, the contact geometry (four-terminal arrangement on flakes of irregular shape), and the MBE film thickness determination.

13. The comparisons with the 8/π factor of Fisher et al. and with the ν = 5/2 Pfaffian state (p. 8), and the closing remarks on nickelates, are speculative and unconnected to the data. They should be shortened or clearly marked as speculation.

14. Presentation:
    - "Supplementaty" (p. 6).
    - The axis label "ln T (K)" (Figs. 2(b), 3, S1).
    - The [10^−?] multipliers on the G□/layer axes in Fig. 3 are hard to read.
    - Some yellow/light-green symbols in Figs. 4 and 5 are hard to distinguish.
    - Fig. 6(b) has two y-axes (R□/layer and R) without a stated conversion factor.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add magnetoresistance (perpendicular and parallel field) across 2–300 K for samples 4 and 5 and an insulating flake. This is the most direct way to separate pair-breaking from fermionic weak-localization physics in the 100–220 K window.
2. Characterize the measured flakes structurally and chemically (Raman for L/S phase and octahedral distortion, TEM/SAED, EDS) and plot transport parameters against a measured structural quantity rather than against thickness or dρ/dT.
3. Provide a per-sample table (geometry, layer count, uncertainties, measurement current) and redo the critical-resistance extraction with error bars and explicit heating checks, reconciling Figs. 3(a) and 6(b).
4. Measure more superconducting flakes, so the Bose glass window and the critical resistances can be shown to be reproducible rather than resting on samples 4 and 5.

5. Verdict: major revision
