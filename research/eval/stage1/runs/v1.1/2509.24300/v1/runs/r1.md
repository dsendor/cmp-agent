Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports transport on sonication-exfoliated Ca2RuO4 flakes (10-300 nm) and MBE films on LaAlO3. It argues that a resistance upturn between roughly 220 K and the superconducting onset (about 100 K) follows rho ~ ln(1/T) rather than sigma ~ ln T, and that this marks a Bose glass of weakly localized Cooper pairs above 200 K. It also reports a "vertical flow" in the beta-function, and two critical sheet resistances of about (5/2) h/e^2 and (5/2) h/4e^2. If correct, preformed and localized pairs near 220 K in a ruthenate would be a striking result. As presented, however, the evidence is almost entirely the functional form of R(T) over a narrow temperature window, and the paper offers no direct test of pairing.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The resistivity of the nanoflakes is several orders of magnitude below that of bulk Ca2RuO4, and some flakes superconduct. Building on the authors' earlier report (Ref. [20]), this is an unusual and potentially important observation.
- The paper is systematic in one respect. It collects nine samples of two growth types and compares them with a broad set of bulk literature data (Fig. 4(b)). It applies one framework (the beta-function and the sign of beta') to all of them.
- The phenomenological map of |drho/dT| against transition temperature (Fig. 4) is a compact way to organize where insulating and superconducting ground states appear.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The ln(1/T) versus ln T discrimination cannot carry the claim of localized Cooper pairs at 220 K. For sample 4 the "Bose glass" window is 101-220 K (ln T from 4.6 to 5.4). For sample 5 it is 105-183 K (ln T from 4.65 to 5.2). Each is less than a factor of two in temperature.
   - Over such a window, R linear in ln(1/T) and G linear in ln T differ only by curvature. Figs. 3(a,b) show visual fits with no residuals, no goodness-of-fit comparison and no uncertainty on the boundary temperatures. The boundaries (220 K, 183 K, the dashed lines in Fig. 3) appear to be chosen by eye.
   - The Das-Doniach weak-localization picture (Ref. [13]) and the beta-function framework are low-temperature, quantum-interference theories. At 100-220 K the phase-coherence length in an oxide with rho ~ 10^-3 to 10^-2 Ohm cm should be at most a few lattice spacings. The paper gives no estimate of the dephasing length, the diffusion constant D or k_F l to show that a weak-localization regime exists there.
   - Several mundane mechanisms give a smooth resistance upturn with slightly concave curvature on a log-T axis: the ferromagnetic transition near 180 K that the authors themselves cite for these flakes (Ref. [20]), spin-disorder scattering, a gradual or partial structural (L/S-like) transition, and phonon or polaron hopping. Any of these could produce the feature called the FG-BG transition near 180-220 K.
   - The paper contains no measurement that is sensitive to pairing. What is needed: magnetoresistance in the 100-220 K window and its comparison with the expected boson or fermion weak-localization forms; field dependence of the upturn and of the "transition" temperature; and ideally Nernst, tunneling or magnetization evidence that the upturn regime holds pairs. In addition, the authors should provide quantitative fit comparisons (residuals, reduced chi^2, the effect of moving the window edges) for both functional forms in every sample.

   Without these, the abstract's statements are not supported by the data shown. These are that bosons persist to about 220 K, that vortices are "found" to be dual to localized pairs, and that a Bose glass separates the SC and Mott phases.

2. The "vertical flow" in the beta-function (Fig. 5(b), Fig. 6(a)) is not specific to bosons.
   - beta = -(2/p) d ln g / d ln T goes through zero and becomes positive wherever R(T) has a maximum. Every sample with a superconducting downturn (4, 5) or a resistance peak will therefore show beta rising "perpendicular" to the Vollhardt-Wolfle curve as T approaches the peak.
   - In the strongly localized region, any activated or VRH conduction gives beta roughly linear in ln g. The collapse onto beta_VW in Fig. 5(a), including the bulk crystals at ln g ~ -20, is therefore close to automatic. It does not show that the scaling theory applies.
   - beta also scales as 1/p, and p = 1 is set without justification.
   - It is unclear which g is used for the 25-300 nm flakes and the bulk crystals. Their transport is not two-dimensional, yet g = (hbar/e^2) sigma is formed from a per-layer sheet conductance or from a 3D conductivity; the paper does not say which.
   - The authors should state exactly how g is constructed for each sample, show how the collapse and beta_c depend on p, and explain what in the beta-function would distinguish their scenario from a resistance maximum caused by superconducting fluctuations of an ordinary (fermionic) normal state.

3. The phase identity and stoichiometry of the measured flakes are not established, yet the main interpretation depends on them.
   - The flakes are separated from CaRuO3 "by observing the crystal shape" in SEM (Methods). No diffraction, Raman, EDX or TEM is reported on the flakes that were measured.
   - The quoted nanocrystal c = 12.278 Å is not attributed to a specific measurement on flakes; the Methods mention powder XRD with a Miniflex. It is also close to the bulk metallic L-phase value.
   - The flake resistivities (about 10^-3 to 10^-2 Ohm cm, Fig. 1(a)) are 3-4 orders of magnitude below bulk. They are in the range expected for CaRuO3, for oxygen-excess Ca2RuO4+delta, or for current paths through metallic inclusions or grain boundaries.
   - The paper's central inference is that "thinning relieves RuO6 distortion" and that "impurity-induced disorder cannot explain this phenomenon" (p. 6 and Conclusion). It rests on this undocumented structural assumption; no octahedral distortion is measured on any nanoflake.
   - The authors should provide structural and compositional characterization of the measured devices, or of flakes from the same batch at the same thickness. Raman of the octahedral modes, TEM/SAED or micro-XRD, and EDX/EELS for Ca:Ru and oxygen would each help. They should also discuss explicitly how CaRuO3 or Ru inclusions and off-stoichiometry are excluded.

4. The two "quantum critical resistances" are not determined in a way that supports quantum criticality, and the figures are inconsistent with each other.
   - (i) R^{BG-FG} = 65.7 kOhm is obtained from "beta_c = -1.6" (Fig. 6(a)). The text then writes "estimated from g_c = -1.6" (p. 8), conflating beta and g. The mapping from beta_c to a resistance (presumably through beta_VW(g)) is not given. The value depends on p, on the layer thickness used for "per layer" and on the VW curve.
   - (ii) R^{SC-BG} is read off a family of R(T) curves taken at different bias currents on one sample (Fig. 6(b)). Bias current, up to 5 uA, is not a quantum tuning parameter in the usual sense; the paper shows no separatrix with temperature-independent R or scaling collapse; and heating and current-driven effects are well known in Ca2RuO4 (Refs. [19, 20, 28]). The 65.7 kOhm value in Fig. 6(b) is simply the low-T value of the 5000 nA curve.
   - (iii) The sheet resistance of sample 4 in Fig. 6(b) peaks near 40 kOhm (right axis R about 4 kOhm). In Fig. 3(a) the same sample's R_sq/layer peaks near 125 kOhm, which is also what rho about 7 x 10^-3 Ohm cm in Fig. 1 gives for a 0.6 nm layer. Either the normalization or the sample differs.
   - (iv) The text quotes 16.8 kOhm; the figure and its caption give 16.5 kOhm.
   - (v) Neither 65.7 nor 16.8 kOhm equals 5/2 times the quantum unit (64.5 and 16.1 kOhm). No uncertainty is given, so the "5/2" identification, and the links to 8/pi (Ref. [34]) and to the Moore-Read Pfaffian (Ref. [35]), are numerology.

   The authors should reconcile the normalizations, define R_sq/layer (which layer thickness, and how flake thickness and geometry errors propagate), and give error bars. Either they should demonstrate critical behaviour (a temperature-independent separatrix and finite-size or dynamical scaling) or they should drop the quantum-critical language.

B. Should address

5. The thickness dependence does not follow the "thickness-tuned SIT" narrative of the Fig. 1 title.
   - Sample 5 (30 nm) superconducts, while samples 1-3 (25, 25 and 35 nm) are Mott-like. Sample 4 (10 nm) superconducts and sample 8 (300 nm) is insulating.
   - The ground state is therefore not set by thickness. The authors instead order the samples by |drho/dT|, which is itself the observed transport.
   - The definition drho = (rho(120 K) - rho(280 K))/dT is written ambiguously; the paper should state that it is divided by 160 K and give its sign convention. It also mixes intrinsic behaviour with geometric-factor errors from SEM thickness estimates.
   - Fig. 4(a) is therefore closer to a correlation between how insulating a sample is above 120 K and whether it superconducts than to a tuning parameter.
   - The authors should retitle Fig. 1, show the ground state against measured thickness, and give the thickness uncertainty from the 70-degree-tilt SEM method.

6. Sample 9 appears in Fig. 4(a) (BG-FG and SC-BG transition temperatures) but nowhere else: not in Fig. 1, the text or the Methods. Its thickness, data and analysis should be shown, or it should be removed.

7. The MBE films are hard to reconcile with the narrative. Samples 6 and 7 have the shorter c-axis (11.592 Å), which the authors read as more distortion, which their own argument links to Mott localization. Yet they are placed in the Bose glass phase below 67-81 K without zero resistance.
   - Their rho ~ ln(1/T) regime (Fig. 3(c), Fig. S1) extends over about 4-67 K. At these temperatures and resistivities, Kondo scattering and electron-electron interaction corrections are standard alternatives that also give logarithmic behaviour. They should be addressed, for example with magnetoresistance and field-dependent slopes.
   - Epitaxial strain on LaAlO3 (Ref. [30]) also alters the octahedra, so the c-axis alone is not a measure of distortion.
   - The paper should report whether the MBE films are phase-pure and how their thickness and strain state were characterized.

8. Measurement current, heating and linearity are not reported for Figs. 1-3. Ca2RuO4 is notoriously current-sensitive (Refs. [19, 20, 27, 28]), and Fig. 6(b) shows strong current dependence in sample 4.
   - The authors should state the excitation current and current density for each sample. They should show that R(T) in the 100-220 K window is independent of current (I-V linearity), and estimate self-heating for the high-bias curves in Fig. 6(b).

9. Only two flakes (samples 4 and 5) carry the main Bose glass claim in the superconducting family, and the paper shows no thermal cycling and no repeat measurement. The authors should state how many flakes were measured in total and what fraction superconduct. They should also say whether the 180-220 K feature is reproducible on cooling and warming; hysteresis would point to a structural or magnetic origin.

10. The magnetization argument (p. 5) leans on diamagnetism "from around 220 K" in bulk Ca2RuO4 (Refs. [27, 28]). This is the result of a retracted paper and was attributed to Joule heating. The nanoflake diamagnetism quoted from Ref. [20] starts at 140 K, well below the claimed 220 K pair-formation scale. This paragraph does not support the claim. It should be removed or replaced with magnetization data on the present samples, with background subtraction and sample mass or volume stated.

11. The reanalysis in Fig. S2 of another group's 2.6 GPa bulk data with a 2D Aslamazov-Larkin form has problems. T_c0 = 12 K and R_N = 44 uOhm cm are chosen by hand; the formula mixes a resistivity R_N with the 2D sheet-resistance prefactor e^2/16hbar; and the sample is a bulk 3D crystal. The fit does not discriminate superconducting fluctuations from the ferromagnetic-metal interpretation of Ref. [19]. The authors should either fit with 3D AL and free parameters, alongside a comparison with a non-superconducting model, or soften the statement that superconductivity may occur in bulk pressurized Ca2RuO4.

12. The vortex statements in the abstract and introduction ("vortices are found to have a dual relationship with the localized Cooper pairs, enabling their motion") rest on no vortex-sensitive measurement. Examples would be field-dependent R(T), flux-flow analysis or Nernst. They should be framed as interpretation.

C. Minor

13. The Fig. 3 caption says sample 5 "fits well with the R_sq/layer ~ ln T fitting"; it should read ln(1/T). The Fig. 6 caption calls beta_c the boundary "between the Bose glass and Mott insulating phases", whereas the text calls it the Bose glass-Fermi glass transition.

14. Fig. 6(b) has two y-axes (R_sq/layer on the left, R on the right) with no stated conversion. The 1 nA curve label overlaps the data, and the dashed critical lines are not explained.

15. Fig. 1 does not state the superconducting samples' T_c^onset and zero-resistance temperatures, nor the zero-resistance criterion. Sample 5 in Fig. 1(a) shows scattered points below 30 K. The SC phase in Fig. 4 uses these values.

16. In Fig. 4(b) the vertical dotted lines connect drho/dT "before and after the structural transition", but the paper does not explain which data points come from which side.

17. Typos and wording: "Supplementaty", "more assertive" (presumably "more pronounced"), "fermi glass", "Ichimua", "Ca2RuO4 is" (missing space). The Methods name a 3He refrigerator but give no base temperature or the temperature range of each dataset.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Measure magnetoresistance and the field dependence of R(T) between 100 and 250 K on the superconducting flakes. Compare with the boson and fermion weak-localization predictions, and with a non-superconducting Mott-like flake as a reference. This is the most direct way to test pairing above T_c.
2. Characterize the measured flakes structurally and compositionally (Raman of the octahedral modes, TEM/SAED, EDX/EELS). This would back up the "released distortion" mechanism and exclude CaRuO3 or off-stoichiometric paths.
3. Replace the visual ln T / ln(1/T) fits with a quantitative model comparison over variable windows, including the competing magnetic and structural scenarios. Report the beta-function analysis with its dependence on p and on the definition of g.
4. Reorganize the phase diagram around a measured control parameter (thickness with uncertainty, or a structural metric) rather than |drho/dT|. Document every sample in it, including sample 9.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
