Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports transport on exfoliated Ca2RuO4 nanoflakes (samples 1–5, plus a 300 nm reference) and MBE films on LaAlO3 (samples 6, 7). It argues that a regime with R ~ ln(1/T) between roughly 100 and 220 K is a Bose glass of localized Cooper pairs, sitting between a Fermi-glass (σ ~ ln T) regime and the superconducting state. It extracts two "quantum critical" sheet resistances, 65.7 kΩ ≈ (5/2)h/e² and 16.5 kΩ ≈ (5/2)h/4e², and proposes a phase diagram in which the room-temperature-to-120 K resistivity slope dρ/dT stands in for "localization strength" set by octahedral distortion. If correct, finding Cooper-pair signatures up to ~220 K in a ruthenate would be striking. As presented, though, the bosonic assignment rests almost entirely on the functional form of R(T) over less than a decade in temperature and on a β-function analysis derived from that same R(T).

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The material system is new for Bose-glass physics. Nanoflakes and MBE films both show a non-monotonic R(T) and, in two flakes, a resistive transition to near zero (Fig. 1c), which is interesting on its own.
- The data are organized in a single framework (β-function, Fig. 5) that covers strongly localized VRH, weakly localized and superconducting samples, and compares them with bulk data from the literature (Fig. 4b).
- Bias-current-tuned R(T) curves for sample 4 (Fig. 6b) show a separatrix-like structure that is worth reporting.
- The MBE films come with X-ray lattice parameters (c = 11.592 Å, against 12.278 Å for the flakes). This gives a structural handle on why thin samples avoid the Mott state.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The bosonic (Cooper-pair) origin of the ln(1/T) regime up to ~220 K is not established, and the β-function "vertical flow" does not add independent evidence.
   - What the claim rests on. The headline claim in the abstract and conclusion ("signatures of Cooper pairs persist up to high temperatures of ~220 K") is supported by three things:
     - (i) R□/layer fitting ln(1/T) better than G ~ ln T over 101–220 K for sample 4, 105–183 K for sample 5, and a similar range for sample 7 (Fig. 3);
     - (ii) the sign of β′ and the "vertical flow" in Figs. 5b and 6a;
     - (iii) diamagnetism seen in powder samples near 140 K, and in bulk work under current that the paper notes has been retracted (ref. 34).
   - Why (i) is weak. The windows span only Δln T ≈ 0.6–0.8. Over a window where R changes by 20–40%, R ~ ln(1/T) and G ~ ln T differ only in curvature. No quantitative model comparison is given: no residuals, χ², or sensitivity to the window. Other mechanisms also give logarithmic or near-logarithmic corrections: electron–electron interaction (Altshuler–Aronov), Kondo-like or spin-disorder scattering in a correlated oxide close to magnetic order, granular-metal conduction, and a parallel conduction channel from minority phases (see concern 3). None of these is discussed or excluded.
   - Why (ii) is not independent. β′_EXP is a second logarithmic derivative of the same R(T), so Eqs. (3)–(4) reduce exactly to asking whether σ or ρ is linear in ln T. That is the same test as Fig. 3, not an independent criterion as the Fig. 5 caption states. In addition, a vertical segment in a β-versus-ln g plot arises wherever g is stationary while d ln g/d ln T changes. That happens generically at a resistance maximum or plateau, such as the maxima near ~100 K in Fig. 1c and Fig. 3a,b. So the vertical flow is not unique to bosonic localization.
   - Why (iii) is weak. It comes from different samples, it is indirect, and its main bulk precedent has been retracted.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Provide a direct test of pairing in the 100–220 K window. The most accessible is magnetoresistance: perpendicular versus parallel field, with the magnitude and sign expected for localized pairs versus single-electron weak localization or antilocalization, and with field-dependent suppression of the ln(1/T) slope.
     - If possible, add independent signatures: Nernst, noise, or local diamagnetism on the same devices.
     - Give a quantitative comparison of the competing functional forms.
     - Rewrite the β-function argument so that it does not present the vertical flow as independent evidence.
     - Without such tests, scope the claims to "Bose-glass-like transport phenomenology".

2. The two "quantum critical resistances" are not reproducibly defined and do not match the paper's own data.
   - Where they come from. The 65.7 kΩ value is obtained from the β′ sign change at β_c = −1.6 (Fig. 6a), but the paper does not say how β_c is converted to a resistance. With g = (h/e²)σ and the Vollhardt–Wölfle form β ≈ −1/g, β_c = −1.6 gives about 41 kΩ, not 65.7 kΩ.
   - Mismatch with Fig. 3. At the BG–FG crossover marked in Fig. 3, R□/layer is about 80 kΩ for sample 4 (at ln T ≈ 5.4) and about 32 kΩ for sample 5 (at ln T ≈ 5.2). Neither is 65.7 kΩ, and they differ from each other by a factor of 2.5. A sample-independent critical resistance would require them to coincide.
   - Mismatch between figures for the same sample. Sample 4 in Fig. 6b has R□/layer ≈ 40 kΩ near 100 K, while Fig. 3a shows ≈ 125 kΩ at the same temperature. This suggests a different cooldown, contact configuration or normalization, which is not stated.
   - Normalization. "Per layer" normalization assumes N independent parallel RuO2 layers, with N taken from an SEM-tilt thickness estimate. The uncertainty in N, and so in R□/layer, is not given. It needs to be well below 10% for prefactors of 5/2 or 8/π to be distinguished.
   - Conceptual problem. A sign change of β′ at a finite temperature of 180–220 K is a crossover, not a quantum critical point. Calling it a QCP needs a T → 0 separatrix in a tuning parameter, which only Fig. 6b, for a single sample, approaches.
   - What the authors should do:
     - State the β_c → R conversion explicitly.
     - Give the layer count and its error bar for each sample.
     - Reconcile the Fig. 3 and Fig. 6b normalizations for sample 4.
     - Show the critical resistance for every sample that shows the crossover (4, 5, 6, 7, 9).
     - Either demonstrate a common value with error bars or drop the 5/2 and 8/π comparisons.

B. Should address

3. The phase and structure of each measured flake are not verified.
   - The source crystals contain about 5% CaRuO3 (a metal) and 6% S-phase Ca2RuO4 (Methods). Flakes are separated from CaRuO3 only by "crystal shape" in SEM.
   - Samples 4 and 5, the only superconducting ones, have ρ ≈ 10⁻³ Ω cm. That is several orders of magnitude below bulk L-phase Ca2RuO4 and closer to metallic ruthenates. A CaRuO3 inclusion or intergrowth would give a parallel metallic or weakly localized channel and would change both the ln T analysis and the per-layer normalization.
   - The relaxation of RuO6 distortion invoked to explain the low resistivity is inferred for the flakes, not measured. Only the MBE films have lattice parameters, and even there the octahedral tilt and rotation are not determined.
   - What the authors should do: provide per-device phase identification (Raman, EDX, cross-sectional TEM, or micro-diffraction on the measured flakes), or at least on flakes from the same exfoliation, plus any direct measure of octahedral distortion.

4. The phase diagram's control parameter is circular, and the "not disorder" claim is untested.
   - Fig. 4a uses dρ/dT between 120 and 280 K as "localization strength" and then shows that samples with small dρ/dT reach BG or SC states. Because dρ/dT is taken from the same R(T) whose low-temperature form defines the phase, the correlation is partly built in.
   - The statements that "the effect of impurity-induced disorder in thin films cannot explain this phenomenon" and that thinning "does not introduce disorder" are not tested. No residual-resistance ratio, Hall density or mobility, or independent disorder metric is given.
   - Samples with similar thickness (1, 2: 25 nm; 5: 30 nm; 3: 35 nm) end up in different phases. That is equally consistent with uncontrolled sample-to-sample disorder or phase content.
   - What the authors should do: add an independent sample characterization (Hall effect, Raman linewidths of the octahedral modes, lattice parameters) and plot it against the phase.

5. The β-function construction relies on an unverified length scale.
   - Eq. (1) assumes L² = D T⁻ᵖ with p = 1 for all samples and all regimes (VRH, FG, BG). The absolute value of β_EXP, and hence β_c = −1.6 and the collapse onto β_VW in Fig. 5a, scales with 2/p.
   - p is not measured. Magnetoresistance would give the dephasing exponent.
   - A Fermi-liquid VRH regime and a bosonic regime need not share p.
   - The "universal curve" agreement is then partly a choice of p.
   - What the authors should do: show how Figs. 5 and 6a change for p = 1/2 to 3/2. Make clear that β_VW is drawn for fermions and is used only as a reference.

6. Reproducibility and sample count.
   - Superconductivity and the full SC/BG/FG sequence are shown for two flakes (4 and 5). Sample 9 appears in Fig. 4a but is described nowhere in the text, methods or Fig. 1.
   - No repeated cooldowns, thermal cycling, or contact-configuration swaps (to test for current-path effects in irregular flakes) are reported.
   - What the authors should do: state the total number of devices fabricated and measured, include sample 9's data, and show at least one repeated cooldown for a superconducting flake.

7. Possible heating in the bias-current data.
   - The bias-current SIT in Fig. 6b uses DC currents up to 5 μA through a nanoflake with R of a few kΩ at low temperature in a ³He system. The excitation current for the R(T) data in other figures is not stated.
   - Joule heating or self-heating at contacts could shift the 16.5 kΩ separatrix.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Give the excitation currents.
     - Show I–V curves or dV/dI, and check heating, for example by comparing resistance at fixed current with R(T).
     - State the base temperature reached.

8. The VRH fit and the crossover temperatures are under-determined.
   - Fig. 2a fits 2D Mott VRH (T^(−1/3)) over a limited range. Efros–Shklovskii (T^(−1/2)) and 3D Mott (T^(−1/4)) are not compared, although the flakes are 25–35 nm thick.
   - The crossover temperatures (224, 233 and 208 K), placed close to the claimed BG onset in samples 4 and 5, are read from where two fits deviate. They have no uncertainty.
   - What the authors should do: add a reduced activation-energy analysis (w = d ln ρ / d ln T against ln T) and give error bars on the crossover temperatures in Fig. 4a.

9. The magnetization evidence is weak.
   - The discussion of diamagnetism near 140–220 K relies on powder samples (ref. 29) and on bulk current-induced diamagnetism that the paper acknowledges has been retracted (refs. 34, 35).
   - What the authors should do: present this as context, not as "supporting evidence", or measure it on the films. The MBE films are large enough for SQUID magnetometry.

10. The numerical comparisons are numerology.
   - The comparisons to 8/π (ref. 42) and to the 5/2 Pfaffian state (ref. 43) have no stated mechanism linking them to this system. Given concern 2, the prefactor is not known well enough to support them.
   - What the authors should do: remove these comparisons or clearly label them as speculative.

C. Minor

11. "Sample 8 (300 nm)" is grouped with the nanoscale samples in Fig. 1a. The caption says samples 1–5 and 8 are nanoscale single crystals, but the text never discusses sample 8 as such.
12. The notation "dρ(=ρ(120 K) − ρ(280 K))/dT" (Fig. 1b, Fig. 4 caption, text) is a finite difference with no stated divisor. State it explicitly as Δρ/ΔT with ΔT = −160 K, and give units consistent with Fig. 4's |dρ/dT| axis in Ω cm K⁻¹.
13. Units are inconsistent across Fig. 3: panels (a) and (b) plot R□/layer in kΩ and in Ω respectively, while (c) plots ρ in Ω cm and σ in S/cm. Since the β-function uses dimensionless g, state how g is computed for samples 6 and 7, which have no per-layer normalization.
14. The Fig. 4b legend uses filled and open symbols for dρ/dT > 0 and < 0, but the vertical dotted lines and the "structural transition" band are not explained clearly enough for the reader to check which literature points belong to which regime.
15. In Eqs. (3)–(4), σ0, σ1, ρ0, ρ1 > 0 is assumed. State the fitted values and their uncertainties for each sample.
16. Fig. 6a quotes β_c = −1.6, but the curves cross β′ = 0 at visibly different β for different samples. Mark where each sample's β′ sign change occurs.
17. Reference 26 is incomplete ("Nature Physics 1–7 (2025)"). The Supplementary Fig. S3 Aslamazov–Larkin fit uses another group's 2.6 GPa data (ref. 28), which should be stated clearly in the main text.
18. The Methods describe two lattice-parameter sets in two places with slightly different wording. Consolidate them, and specify the MBE film thicknesses (50 nm in the legend) and the substrate-induced strain.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add perpendicular- and parallel-field magnetotransport across 2–250 K on samples 4 and 5 (and on the MBE films). This is the single most decisive test of whether the ln(1/T) regime involves pairs. It would also fix the dephasing exponent p in the β-function.
2. Recast the β-function section so that it is presented as a reparametrization of the same data, not a separate signature. Show the robustness of β_c and of the vertical flow to p, to smoothing, and to the fitting window.
3. Tabulate, for every sample: thickness and layer count with errors, dρ/dT, the crossover temperatures, the critical resistances, and the structural characterization. This would let readers judge the phase diagram's systematics.
4. Use the MBE films, which are larger and structurally characterized, for complementary probes: Hall effect, SQUID magnetization, and Raman of the octahedral modes. These would connect lattice distortion to localization directly.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
