Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports transport on exfoliated Ca2RuO4 nanoflakes (samples 1–5, 10–35 nm) and MBE films (samples 6, 7). It argues that a Bose glass of localized Cooper pairs exists between a Fermi glass and a superconducting phase, up to about 220 K. The evidence is a crossover from σ ~ ln T to ρ ~ ln(1/T), a sign change of the temperature derivative of the experimental β-function, and two "critical" sheet resistances, 65.7 kΩ and 16.5 kΩ, each about 5/2 times a quantum resistance. A bosonic regime at 220 K in a correlated oxide would be a striking result for SIT physics and for the debate on preformed pairs in cuprates and nickelates. As presented, however, the bosonic interpretation rests almost entirely on the functional form of R(T) over a narrow temperature window. No measurement in the paper directly probes charge 2e or pairing.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A comparison of many samples spanning Mott/VRH, Fermi-glass-like and superconducting-onset behaviour (Figs. 1, 2, 4a), including two independently grown MBE films.
- A clear, reproducible empirical observation: in the samples that later superconduct, the high-temperature logarithmic behaviour changes its curvature below about 180–220 K (Fig. 3a,b). Samples that stay insulating do not show this change.
- A single scaling plot (Fig. 5a) that places strongly and weakly localized data on a common β(g) curve. It also places the nanofilms next to the bulk Ca2RuO4 literature (Fig. 4b).
- The topic is timely. If the pairing interpretation held, it would be broadly interesting.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. No direct evidence that the carriers between about 100 and 220 K are Cooper pairs. The headline claim, "signatures of Cooper pairs persist up to high temperatures of ~220 K" (abstract; p. 2, "Samples 4 and 5…"), is inferred only from R ∝ ln(1/T) in Fig. 3. No measurement in the paper tests pairing in that window: no perpendicular-field magnetoresistance, no field dependence of the BG–FG crossover temperature, no Nernst signal, no shot noise, no h/2e periodicity. The supporting magnetization comes from powder samples (p. 2). The text itself links the 220 K bulk diamagnetism to a retracted paper (ref. 34) and to Joule heating (ref. 35).
   Why it matters: an R = R0 − a ln T form is not unique to localized bosons. It also arises from Kondo scattering off local Ru moments, from Altshuler–Aronov interaction corrections in the fermionic channel, and from crossovers near a structural or orbital transition, which Ca2RuO4 is known to have.
   What to do: the decisive test is magnetotransport across the BG window. Field suppression of pairing should give positive magnetoresistance and shift or remove the ln(1/T) regime. Fermionic weak localization gives negative magnetoresistance with a characteristic HLN form. Show R(T) at several perpendicular fields for samples 4 and 5, and fit the magnetoresistance. Failing that, discuss and exclude the Kondo and interaction alternatives explicitly.

2. Two concerns about the analysis.

   (a) Over the fitted windows, ρ ~ ln(1/T) and σ ~ ln T are not shown to be statistically distinguishable. In Fig. 3a (sample 4) the BG window is 101–220 K, a factor of 2.2 in T. R changes from about 125 to 80 kΩ there. For sample 5 (105–183 K), R changes by about 20%. To leading order the two forms are identical, and they differ only through curvature. The visible deviation of G from the ln T line in Fig. 3a sits at the low-T end, next to the resistance maximum at about 90 K. That maximum is the onset of the superconducting drop, so superconducting fluctuations are an equally natural cause of the curvature. A 2D Aslamazov–Larkin correction, e²/(16ħ)·Tc/(T−Tc), is about 10⁻⁵ S at T ≈ 2Tc, the same order as G_□/layer in Fig. 3a. The authors apply an AL analysis themselves in Supplementary Fig. S3.

   (b) The β-function analysis does not add independent evidence. By Eqs. (2)–(4), the sign of β'_EXP is simply the sign of the curvature of σ versus ln T. The "vertical flow" in Fig. 5b is where d ln g/d ln T passes through zero, which is the resistance maximum preceding the superconducting onset. Neither feature is a signature specific to a Bose glass.

   What to do: report fit residuals and χ² (or AIC) for ln T versus ln(1/T) in each window, and include fermionic WL + AL/MT fluctuation models fitted with Tc fixed from Fig. 1c. Show how the crossover temperatures (220 K, 183 K) move when the fit windows change. Present the β-function as a re-expression of the R(T) curvature rather than as an "independent criterion" (Fig. 5 caption).

3. The weak-localization framework is applied far outside its regime of validity, and the sheet-resistance normalization is not defined. Bosonic (and fermionic) weak-localization logarithms are perturbative corrections valid for g ≫ 1, that is, R_□ ≪ h/4e² = 6.45 kΩ for pairs. In Fig. 3a, sample 4 has R_□/layer = 60–125 kΩ, about 10–20 times h/4e² and above h/e². Sample 5 (28–40 kΩ) is also above h/e². These values lie in the strongly localized regime for both carrier types, so attributing a ln(1/T) dependence there to "weak localization of bosons" is internally inconsistent.
   In addition, "R_□/layer" is never defined. The paper does not say how many conducting layers are assumed (thickness/(c/2)?), how the tilted-SEM thickness uncertainty propagates, or why the layers can be treated as decoupled 2D sheets in 10–35 nm films. The inelastic length is not compared with the film thickness. Every quantitative statement depends on this normalization: g, Fig. 5, β_c, and the critical resistances.
   What to do: state the normalization and its uncertainty, and estimate L_φ relative to the thickness. Explain how a weak-localization description is justified at these g values, or reframe the regime.

4. The phase identity and homogeneity of the measured flakes are not established. The Methods report CaRuO3 (about 5%) and Ca3Ru2O7 (about 6%) impurity phases in the source crystals. Both are metallic over much of 100–300 K. Measured flakes were selected only by "crystal shape" in SEM. The nanofilm resistivity is 3–4 orders of magnitude below bulk (Fig. 1a), which the authors attribute to relaxed RuO6 distortion.
   A percolating metallic CaRuO3 or Ca3Ru2O7 inclusion, or a surface/interface conducting layer, would give the same low resistivity and logarithmic temperature dependences. It would undercut both the Mott → FG → BG narrative and the per-layer sheet-resistance analysis.
   What to do: provide phase identification on the measured devices, or on flakes from the same batch. Options are Raman (which distinguishes S/L-Ca2RuO4, CaRuO3 and Ca3Ru2O7), TEM/EDX, or micro-XRD. Report the device geometries.

B. Should address

5. The claim about universal critical resistances is weakly supported and leans on numerology.
   - It is not explained how β_c = −1.6 (Fig. 6a) is converted to R^BG–FG = 65.7 kΩ. Samples 4–7 cross over at very different resistances, for example about 80 kΩ in sample 4 and about 32 kΩ in sample 5 in Fig. 3. A single critical value therefore depends entirely on the β-to-g mapping and on the per-layer normalization (concern 3).
   - In Fig. 6b the "65.7 kΩ" and "16.5 kΩ" levels are drawn as dashed lines. A clear crossing point of the current-tuned curves is not demonstrated.
   - The comparison with 8/π (ref. 42) and with the 5/2 Pfaffian state is suggestive at best.

   Please give uncertainties for both values, propagated from thickness and fitting. Show the derivation sample by sample, and temper the interpretation.

6. Bias-current dependence and heating.
   - The current used for Figs. 1–3 is not stated.
   - Fig. 6b shows R(T) changing by more than an order of magnitude between 1 nA and 5 µA. Ca2RuO4 is notorious for current-induced metallic states and for Joule-heating artifacts (refs. 28, 34, 35).

   Please state the excitation current and the estimated current density for each sample. Show I–V linearity at the measurement current at 100–220 K. Discuss heating for the high-current curves in Fig. 6b before using them to infer a critical resistance.

7. The structural mechanism is asserted, not measured on the transport samples. The abstract states "we demonstrate" that the ground state is set by RuO6 distortion, and the conclusion states that thinning "does not introduce disorder". Lattice parameters are given only for the powder and for the MBE films. No structural data come from the exfoliated flakes, and no disorder measure is given (residual resistivity, mobility, TEM). The text also says that samples of comparable thickness behave differently, which contradicts the abstract's statement that thickness "acts as an effective control parameter".
   Please provide structural characterization of flakes, such as Raman octahedral-rotation modes. Otherwise present the distortion picture as a hypothesis, and reconcile the abstract with p. 4.

8. Choice of p = 1 in the β-function. The inelastic exponent is fixed at p = 1 (electron–electron dephasing) without justification. At 100–300 K, electron–phonon dephasing (p ≈ 2–3) is normally dominant. The sign of β' does not depend on p, but the magnitudes in Fig. 5 do, and so do the agreement with β_VW and the value β_c = −1.6.
   Please justify p, show Fig. 5 for p = 2 and 3, or extract p independently from magnetoresistance.

9. Phase-diagram construction (Fig. 4a).
   - The localization parameter "dρ/dT" = ρ(120 K) − ρ(280 K) divided by "dT" is defined loosely. For samples 4 and 5 the 120 K endpoint lies close to the resistance maximum preceding the superconducting onset, so the parameter is not independent of the superconductivity it is used to predict.
   - The crossover temperatures carry no uncertainties, although they are read from where fits "deviate".
   - Sample 9 appears in Fig. 4a (SC–BG and BG–FG points) but nowhere else in the paper: no thickness, no data.

   Please define the parameter precisely and check its robustness to the choice of endpoints. Add error bars on the crossover temperatures and include the sample 9 data.

10. The superconducting phase assignment relies on prior work. "SC" regions in Fig. 4 and "high-temperature superconductivity" (Fig. 1c caption) rest on a resistance drop in samples 4, 5 and 9. This paper shows no field dependence, critical current or diamagnetic response for these devices.
    The BG interpretation requires a genuine superconducting ground state on the low-T side. Please show at least R(T) in a field, or the zero-resistance criterion and the residual resistance, for samples 4 and 5. Also state whether the results survive thermal cycling.

C. Minor

11. Eqs. (3)–(4) appear to give d ln σ/d ln T rather than β'_EXP. With β = −2 d ln g/d ln T, the definition in Eq. (2) yields β' = 2σ1²/σ² for σ = σ0 + σ1 ln T, and β' = −2ρ1²σ² for ρ = ρ0 + ρ1 ln(1/T). The signs are as stated but the expressions are not. Please correct them.

12. Sign convention for "dρ/dT". As defined, ρ(120) − ρ(280) is positive for insulating-like behaviour, but Fig. 4a labels the region "dρ/dT < 0" and the axis uses |dρ/dT|. Please unify the convention.

13. Bulk magnetic phase (p. 5). The text says that "in bulk Ca2RuO4 crystals, a ferromagnetic metal transition occurs at approximately 20 K". At ambient pressure bulk Ca2RuO4 is an antiferromagnetic Mott insulator, and the ferromagnetic metal appears under pressure. Please clarify which conditions are meant.

14. Text and figures on samples 6 and 7. The text gives BG onsets of 67 and 81 K for samples 6 and 7, but only sample 7 is shown in the main text (Fig. 3c). For sample 7 the "BG" line spans ln T ≈ 2–4.2 (7–67 K), a different temperature range from the exfoliated flakes. Please comment.

15. Methods: DC four-terminal measurements at elevated T should use current reversal to remove thermoelectric offsets. Please state whether this was done, and give the contact resistances.

16. Reference 26 is incomplete ("Nature Physics 1–7 (2025)"). Fig. 1 legend "Nakatsuji 1/2" would be clearer as "bulk S/L phase (ref. 16)".

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add perpendicular- and parallel-field magnetotransport across 100–250 K for samples 4 and 5. This is the most direct way to show that charge-2e physics underlies the ln(1/T) regime (concern 1).
2. Replace visual line fits with a quantitative model comparison: fermionic WL plus SC fluctuations versus bosonic localization versus Kondo. Give residuals, fit windows and their sensitivity (concern 2).
3. Characterize the measured flakes structurally and by phase (Raman/TEM). Define and propagate the per-layer sheet-resistance normalization (concerns 3, 4, 7).
4. Recast the critical-resistance discussion with uncertainties, and moderate the 8/π and 5/2 comparisons (concern 5).

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
