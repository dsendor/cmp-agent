Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper uses laser power as a local heater to cycle a polycrystalline La0.275Pr0.35Ca0.375MnO3 pellet between a base temperature T_B ≈ 38 K and a series of rising target temperatures T_T. It reports that the Jahn-Teller (JT) Raman modes measured back at T_B carry a memory of T_T: they gain intensity for T_C < T_T < T_MI and reset for T_T ≥ T_COO. Separate cold-finger/PPMS cycling of magnetization and two-probe resistivity shows the same windows. A structural (Raman) view of thermal-history memory in phase-separated LPCMO, and the idea of writing and reading it optically, are of interest. The novelty over the existing literature on thermal-history, minor-loop and memory effects in LPCMO (several of these papers are cited in the introduction) is less clear than the abstract's "previously unidentified regime" suggests. The central optical-heating protocol also lacks the controls it needs.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The Raman, magnetic and transport measurements were all run under a cyclic protocol, and the three probes give consistent T_T windows set by T_C, T_MI and T_COO (Figs. 3d,e and 4d).
- The heating-power calibration is laid out in some detail (Figs. S1–S5, Table S1). Mode positions, widths and areas from laser heating and from cold-finger heating agree reasonably well.
- The sample is characterized structurally (synchrotron PXRD and Rietveld refinement, Fig. S7, Table S2), and the transition temperatures come from derivatives of M and R (Fig. S8).
- The Raman memory map at T_B (Fig. 3a) closely resembles the direct temperature map (Fig. 2b). This is a clear, easy-to-read presentation of the effect.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. No cold-finger cycling control separates thermal history from laser-specific effects, and the cooling (quench) rate is undefined. The memory effect in Raman (Figs. 1, 3) comes only from laser-power cycling: the power steps from 10 mW to P(T_T) and back, described as "almost instantaneous heating and subsequent freezing" (Methods). Three issues follow.
   (i) Manganites, LPCMO in particular, respond to light non-thermally: the authors themselves cite photo-induced phase changes (Deng2024, Lin2018). High power densities on a 50 µm spot (up to 150 mW) could also cause photo-oxidation or oxygen loss at the surface. The red "spurious peak" attributed to oxygen vacancies (Fig. 2a) makes this concern concrete.
   (ii) The cooling rate after the power step is unknown but presumably far faster than the 5 K/min used for the M and ρ cycles. In the DPS regime of LPCMO, phase fractions depend strongly on cooling rate, so the Raman "quench" and the PPMS cycles are different thermal histories.
   (iii) The heated volume has a steep gradient. The optical penetration depth is far smaller than the spot size and the cold-finger contact goes through silver paint, so a single "T_T" does not describe the probed region.
   The direct test is cheap and decisive. Repeat the Fig. 3 protocol with the cold-finger heater (laser fixed at 10 mW) cycling between 38 K and the same T_T values, and record Raman at T_B. If Fig. 3d,e is reproduced, the effect is thermal; if not, it is laser-specific, and the interpretation (and the "laser-assisted phase engineering" framing) changes accordingly. The authors should also report the cooling rate or time constant after a power step, and check that the as-cooled spectrum is recovered after the T_T ≥ T_COO cycles at the same spot. Fig. 1h suggests it is, which partly addresses irreversible sample damage.

2. The temperature calibration is partly circular and its uncertainty is underestimated. T_T is assigned by "visual inspection and comparison of spectra" (Table S1, Figs. S1–S2). The comparison uses the very features under study, the intensity and width of the ~480 and ~614 cm⁻¹ JT bands, and it is matched in coarse 25 K steps. A linear T(P) is then imposed with the intercept fixed to zero (footnote 1) and offset by a 21.8 K self-heating at 10 mW. The quoted ±4–7 K uncertainties come only from the linear-fit statistics, not from the matching itself.
   Because the memory windows are defined relative to T_C, T_MI and T_COO, this matters. The T_T axis of Fig. 3 is calibrated by matching JT features to the same transition-related intensity changes that Fig. 3 then reports, so agreement between the Fig. 3 windows and the transition temperatures is partly built in. Fig. S4 also shows that the S-JT position under power heating departs from the cold-finger curve between T_C and T_COO, which is exactly the window of interest.
   The authors should calibrate T_T independently of the JT intensities, for example with the Stokes/anti-Stokes ratio of the 250 cm⁻¹ mode (anti-Stokes data are recorded, Fig. S2). They should propagate a realistic matching uncertainty into the T_T axis, or else state that the window edges are uncertain at the ~10–20 K level.

3. Reproducibility and statistics of the memory effect are not shown. Fig. 3 appears to be a single cumulative sequence at a single spot. The Methods say "different spots and pieces of the sample were measured with the same protocol", but none of those data are shown, and the error bars are fit errors only. The single-cycle increments in Fig. 3d rest largely on one or two points: the AS-JT ΔI/I_i spike to ~1.1 at T_T ≈ 100 K, and the negative values near T_MI–200 K. Without repeats, these could be spot-to-spot or time-dependent fluctuations.
   The authors should show the result for at least two further spots or pieces, with the spread, and repeat at least one T_T in the T_C–T_MI window to show the cycle-to-cycle response is reproducible.

4. The resistive-switching and "laser-induced" claims go beyond the data. The Discussion and Conclusions state that "the study demonstrates the potential of laser-induced thermal cycling to induce non-volatile structure/resistive-switching" and "introduces a novel method for controlling phase separation and resistive properties through localized laser heating".
   - Resistivity was never measured under laser heating. It was measured with two probes over a whole pellet cycled in a PPMS at 5 K/min, with T_B = 30 K rather than 38 K and a different T_T grid.
   - The Raman spot (50 µm, near-surface) and the transport path (~0.7 mm between probes, bulk percolation) probe different volumes.
   - "Non-volatile" needs a retention measurement: R or Raman intensity at T_B versus waiting time after a cycle. None is shown. LPCMO in the DPS regime is known for slow relaxation, and only a 5-minute wait is used.
   The authors should either measure resistance under local laser cycling, or rephrase the conclusions so that the Raman (optical) and transport (thermal) results are described as parallel observations under different protocols. They should also add time-dependence data at T_B, or drop "non-volatile".

B. Should address

5. Cumulative protocol versus single-cycle memory. Each cycle in Figs. 1 and 3 starts from the state left by the previous, lower T_T. The I_0-referenced change (Fig. 3e) therefore mixes the dependence on T_T with accumulated history. To support a "thermal-memory" picture in which the state encodes the maximum temperature reached, the authors should show at least a few single cycles from a freshly as-cooled state (cooled from >T_COO) to T_T in each window, and compare them with the cumulative result.

6. Intensity normalization and background subtraction. JT intensities are normalized to the ~250 cm⁻¹ rotational mode. This mode's area changes by a factor of ~4 across the transitions (Fig. S5d), and its width changes strongly too. Whether it stays constant at T_B across cycles is not shown. The background is a "fixed predefined 7th degree polynomial", and fit quality is judged visually (Fig. S6). The electronic/polaronic continuum visibly changes between cycles (Fig. 1e,f, higher background at large shift), so the extracted JT intensities could partly track the continuum.
   The authors should show raw (unnormalized) JT and rotational-mode areas at T_B versus T_T, and test how sensitive ΔI/I to the background model is (for example, a lower-order polynomial or a fixed-shape continuum).

7. Interpreting Raman intensity as COO phase fraction. The text equates increased JT intensity at T_B with a larger AFMI-COO fraction. In a phase-separated sample, though, Raman intensity also depends on the optical penetration depth and on electronic screening: metallic FMM regions screen and reduce the probed volume. A change in FMM connectivity near the surface could therefore change the JT intensity without changing the COO fraction. Mode frequencies at T_B versus T_T would help, as would some estimate linking the intensity change to phase fraction, for example via the measured ρ or M changes. Without these, Fig. 5 and the "phase fraction" language should be presented as hypothesis.

8. The Raman and macroscopic measurements are mismatched. T_B differs (38 K Raman, 30 K PPMS), rates differ (quench versus 5 K/min), the T_T grids differ, and the volumes probed differ. The Discussion's statement that Figs. 3d,e and 4d "show a clear agreement" is qualitative. For example, M shows a large jump at T_T ≈ 210 K (Fig. 4c,d) with no counterpart in Raman. ρ gains about two-fold between T_C and T_COO, while M changes by a factor of ~0.5. A side-by-side overlay on a common T_T axis, with the matched windows marked, would let readers judge the agreement. It should also be stated whether the same pellet was used for both.

9. Relation to prior memory and minor-loop work. Thermal-history-dependent resistivity and magnetization in LPCMO (minor hysteresis loops, cooling-rate dependence, glassy relaxation) are documented in the works the authors cite (Ghivelder2005, Sharma2004, Kundhikanjana2015, Sacanell2004/2018). The M and ρ results in Fig. 4 should be explicitly compared with these. The new element (the structural/JT signature) should be stated precisely, to justify "previously unidentified regime" and "new form of nonequilibrium phase dynamics".

10. Transport and magnetization methods. Resistance is two-probe, with silver paint and gold wires on a polycrystalline pellet. Contact and grain-boundary resistance can carry their own thermal history. The "magnetization" is the AC moment from ACMS at 10 Oe and 11 Hz (values ~10⁻² µB/Mn), that is, a susceptibility-type quantity sensitive to domain dynamics, not the FMM phase fraction directly. A four-probe check of at least one cycle, and DC magnetization at moderate field (or relabelling the quantity as AC susceptibility), would strengthen the link to phase fractions.

11. Transition-temperature assignments. Raman figures mark T_C at ~95–100 K, while magnetization gives T_C^W ≈ 91 K on warming and T_C^C ≈ 63 K on cooling. Fig. S8a labels the ~188 K feature T_N and 205 K as T_COO, but the text does not discuss T_N. The Raman target temperatures are approached on warming but then quenched. The authors should state which (warming or cooling) transition temperature is relevant to each window edge and use them consistently.

C. Minor

12. The text cites "figure 2 e" (p. 6) for the I_0-normalized change; this should be Fig. 3e. The Fig. 1 caption says "d - f" but covers five panels (d–h).
13. Notation is inconsistent: T_FPS and T_OCO in Figs. S4–S5 versus T_C and T_COO in the main text; T_IM versus T_MI. Fig. S8 lacks units on the x-axis of panel a.
14. The ~660–670 cm⁻¹ "spurious peak", attributed "possibly" to oxygen vacancies (Fig. 2a, prominent near 200 K in Fig. 2b), lies close to the S-JT band. Explain how it was handled in the fits and whether it changes with cycling.
15. The Bose–Einstein factor was not removed (Fig. S1 caption). That is harmless for comparisons at fixed T_B but should be stated where Fig. 2 intensities at different temperatures are compared.
16. Fig. 5 is a schematic with no quantitative input. State this explicitly and avoid language implying it is "constructed" from the data.
17. Give the laser power density, and say whether the 10 mW base power (~22 K self-heating) was held throughout the T_B measurements, including the as-cooled spectrum.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add the cold-finger-only cycling Raman experiment (concern 1). It is the single most informative addition and would allow the paper to state cleanly whether the effect is thermal.
- Recalibrate T_T with Stokes/anti-Stokes thermometry and present Fig. 3 with realistic T_T error bars.
- Add one figure with repeats on other spots and pieces, plus a time series at T_B after a cycle in the T_C–T_MI window, to establish reproducibility and retention.
- Overlay the Raman ΔI/I_0 and ρ, M ΔF/F_0 on a single T_T axis, and narrow the resistive-switching and laser-control claims to what each protocol directly shows.

5. Verdict: major revision
