Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript uses laser-power temperature cycling between a base temperature T_B ≈ 38 K and a series of target temperatures T_T, read out by Raman spectroscopy of the Jahn-Teller (JT) stretching modes of La0.275Pr0.35Ca0.375MnO3 (LPCMO). The authors report that the JT mode intensities measured at T_B grow after excursions with T_C < T_T < T_COO, with a maximum near T_MI, and return to the as-cooled values after T_T ≳ T_COO. They read this as a "thermal-memory" of the charge/orbital-ordered phase fraction, and they compare it with magnetization and two-probe resistivity measured after analogous cryostat cycles. Seeing history-dependent phase separation through a local structural probe is a worthwhile addition to the large literature on memory, kinetic arrest and minor hysteresis loops in phase-separated LPCMO. However, the claims of a "previously unidentified regime" and of "laser-induced non-volatile resistive switching" go beyond what the data establish.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The Raman sequence in Fig. 1d–h and Fig. 3 is clear. The JT-mode intensity at T_B rises and then falls as T_T crosses T_C, T_MI and T_COO, a trend that is visible in the raw spectra and not only in the fitted parameters.
- The heat map of spectra at T_B versus T_T (Fig. 3a) looks strikingly like the conventional warming heat map (Fig. 2b). This supports the idea that the structural state reached at T_T is partly frozen in on cooling back to T_B.
- The bulk magnetization and resistivity after cycling (Fig. 4c,d) show non-monotonic dependences on T_T with features at the same characteristic temperatures, and a reset above T_COO. Together the probes give a multi-technique picture.
- The synchrotron XRD refinement (Fig. S7, Table S2) and the extended temperature/power comparison (Figs. S1–S5) document the sample and the method in some detail.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The local temperature under laser heating is not independently known, and no laser-free control establishes that the Raman effect is purely thermal. All Raman cycling (Fig. 1, Fig. 3) uses the laser as heater. T_T is assigned through Table S1, which comes from "visual inspection and comparison of spectra" between cold-finger and laser-power series, followed by a linear fit (Figs. S2, S3) and a 21.8 K offset at 10 mW obtained by extrapolation.
   - The calibration is circular. It matches the very JT-mode intensities and widths that the paper then interprets as history dependent, in a material with large thermal hysteresis. A spectrum that "looks like" 100 K may be a different phase mixture at another temperature.
   - Figs. S4 and S5 themselves show systematic differences between the two heating routes. The mode areas in Fig. S5d–f peak at about 7.5 (temperature) against about 4.5 (power), the rotational FWHM differs above T_C, and the frequencies differ above T_COO. Either the laser-heated spot is not equivalent to a uniformly heated sample (radial and depth gradients under a 50 µm spot at up to 150 mW), or the laser does something beyond heating (photo-induced melting of charge order is well documented in manganites; local oxygen loss is possible, cf. the "spurious" ~660 cm⁻¹ peak the authors attribute to oxygen vacancies).
   - The quoted uncertainties of ±4–8 K on T_T are statistical fit errors and do not include this systematic uncertainty.

   This matters because the mapping of the memory window onto T_C, T_MI and T_COO is the central claim. The authors should:
   - Determine the local temperature independently from the Stokes/anti-Stokes intensity ratio. The spectra already extend to negative shifts (Fig. S2).
   - Most importantly, repeat the T_B → T_T → T_B cycling with the cold finger as heater at fixed low laser power, and show whether the same JT intensity memory and reset appear. Without this control, the result cannot be attributed to thermal history rather than to laser-specific effects.

2. Reproducibility and irreversible sample modification are not demonstrated. The Raman results come from what appears to be a single cumulative cycling sequence on one spot. The Methods state that "different spots and pieces of the sample were measured with the same protocol", but none of these data are shown, and the error bars in Fig. 3 are fitting errors only. Several features carry the interpretation, for example:
   - the jump of the AS-JT FWHM from ~48 to ~87 cm⁻¹ (Fig. 3b);
   - the sign change of ΔI/I_i between ~120 and ~200 K (Fig. 3d);
   - the AS-JT spike at T_T ≈ 100 K.

   These need to exceed spot-to-spot and run-to-run scatter. Repeated high-power exposure (up to 150 mW focused to 50 µm, roughly several kW/cm²) can also alter oxygen stoichiometry or the surface irreversibly. The "reset" above T_COO argues against gross damage but does not exclude partial modification. The authors should:
   - Show the full sequence on at least two further spots or pieces, with the spread.
   - Show that, after the full sequence, a fresh cooldown from 300 K with the cryostat restores the as-cooled spectrum of Fig. 1c, including the ~660 cm⁻¹ feature.
   - Repeat the sequence a second time on the same spot to demonstrate that the memory is a reversible, repeatable property of the material.

3. The claimed laser-induced, non-volatile resistive switching is not measured. The Discussion and Conclusions state that the study "demonstrates the potential of laser-induced thermal cycling to induce non-volatile structure/resistive-switching" and that "for the first time, it is demonstrated how the resistive switching phenomenon can be associated with the local structure". Two gaps:
   - Resistivity was measured only after cryostat cycling at 5 K/min on a whole pellet (Fig. 4b–d). It was never measured during or after laser cycling, so no laser-induced change in resistance is shown.
   - "Non-volatile" is not tested. There is no retention measurement of ρ, M or the Raman intensity at T_B over time. This matters in the dynamic phase-separation regime the authors themselves invoke, where slow relaxation is expected.

   The authors should either measure transport across a laser-cycled region (for example a narrow bar or a device geometry in which the laser-heated volume dominates the resistance) together with a retention time series at T_B, or restrict the claims to thermally induced, history-dependent resistance changes in bulk cycling.

B. Should address

4. The Raman and the bulk measurements use mismatched protocols, so their "correlation" is only qualitative. The Raman cycles have T_B = 38 K, near-instantaneous laser quenches, T_T on the calibrated grid 59–317 K, and a local 50 µm probe. Magnetization and resistivity have T_B = 30 K, 5 K/min ramps, T_T = 50, 60, …, 250 K, and are bulk measurements. Cooling rate is known to change the FMM fraction in LPCMO strongly, and in Fig. S8 the transition temperatures differ by ~30 K between cooling and warming. The claim that Fig. 3d,e and Fig. 4d show "a clear agreement" is therefore weaker than stated. The detailed shapes differ:
   - Raman ΔI/I_i peaks at ~100 K and is negative between ~150 and 200 K;
   - M shows sharp features near 90 K and 200–210 K, with a large positive jump at ≥210 K that has no Raman counterpart.

   The authors should overlay the Raman and the bulk quantities on a common T_T axis, discuss where they disagree, and ideally match the protocols, which the cold-finger Raman control of item 1 would provide.

5. Positioning relative to known memory and kinetic-arrest phenomena in LPCMO. Return-point memory, minor hysteresis loops, cooling-rate dependence and kinetically arrested FMM/COO coexistence in LPCMO and related compounds are established (several of the cited works, e.g. Sacanell2004/2018, Ghivelder2005, Sarma2004, address thermal-history effects). A warming excursion into T_C < T_T < T_COO converts FMM regions to COO, which then remain arrested on fast recooling into the frozen regime. That simple picture appears to account for the observations. The paper should state explicitly what is new beyond observing this physics with a structural probe, and justify the phrases "previously unidentified regime of structural irreversibility" and "a new form of nonequilibrium phase dynamics" or else remove them.

6. Cumulative protocol, aging and relaxation at T_B. Each cycle starts from the state left by the previous one (Fig. 1b), so the effect of a given T_T is convolved with all earlier excursions. The time spent at T_B also grows through the sequence, and the dynamic phase-separation regime relaxes with time. Two changes would show what part of the effect is a property of the maximum temperature reached and what part is cumulative history or aging:
   - A non-cumulative control: re-cool from room temperature before each T_T, for a few representative T_T.
   - A time series at T_B without cycling, over the duration of the full sequence.

7. Robustness of the intensity extraction.
   - Mode intensities come from Lorentzian fits on top of a fixed 7th-order polynomial background that is large, history dependent (raw spectra in Fig. 1e,f differ above ~700 cm⁻¹) and was judged "by visual inspection" (Fig. S6).
   - The intensities are normalized by the ~250 cm⁻¹ rotational mode, whose own area changes by a factor ~4 with temperature (Fig. S5d) and could itself depend on history.

   The authors should show raw, un-normalized integrated intensities of the JT and rotational modes at T_B versus T_T, test the sensitivity of the conclusions to the background model (for example a lower-order polynomial or a fit restricted to 200–750 cm⁻¹), and state how the error bars were obtained.

8. Interpreting the magnetization and resistivity as phase fractions. Magnetization was measured in ACMS mode with a 10 Oe AC field and no DC field. The resulting values (~10⁻² µB/Mn) are an AC moment governed by domain and demagnetization effects, not the FM volume fraction. Resistivity is two-probe with silver-paint contacts on a polycrystalline pellet, so contact and grain-boundary resistances, which can themselves change with thermal cycling, are included. To support the percolation interpretation of Fig. 5, the authors should:
   - Provide DC M(T) at a moderate field, or a calibrated estimate of the FMM fraction at T_B for each T_T.
   - Provide four-probe resistivity, or at least the sample geometry and a check on contact resistance.

   A quantitative estimate of the FMM fraction would turn Fig. 5 from an illustration into a test.

C. Minor

9. Inconsistent reset temperature. The text says the spectra after cycling are "very similar to the as-cooled one" from T_T = 188(5) K, but elsewhere the reset occurs at T_T ≥ T_COO ≈ 205 K. The authors should reconcile these values given the calibration uncertainty.

10. Inconsistent magnitudes.
   - The text states an "almost two-fold" Raman gain and a "two-fold resistivity gain" between T_C and T_COO, but also a "maximum increase of about three times" in resistivity. Fig. 4d (right) shows ΔF/F₀ ≈ 2.1, i.e. about three-fold.
   - The magnetization at T_B in Fig. 4c (~3.4 × 10⁻² µB/Mn) does not match the low-temperature value in the main M–T curve of Fig. 4a (~1.9 × 10⁻² µB/Mn).

11. Notation and labels.
   - Transition temperatures are named inconsistently: T_C/T_FPS, T_COO/T_OCO, T_MI/T_IM. The T_N ≈ 188 K feature labelled in Fig. S8a is never discussed, although it lies close to the reported reset window.
   - T_C is drawn at ~95–100 K in Figs. 2 and 3 but quoted as 91 K (warming) in the text.

12. Figure and caption errors.
   - The Fig. 1 caption says panels "d–f" for five spectra; it should be d–h.
   - The text cites "figure 2 e", which does not exist; it should be Fig. 3e.
   - The Fig. 4 captions and Methods give different T_T lists (70, 90, 100, 150, 210 K versus 50–250 K in 10 K steps).
   - The S4/S5 captions use T_FPS/T_OCO.
   - The supplementary section labels are all "S".

13. Missing experimental details. Please give:
   - the laser power density at the sample, and whether it was measured at the sample or at the source;
   - the absorbed fraction and optical penetration depth at 532 nm;
   - the pellet dimensions and the resistivity geometry factor;
   - the oxygen stoichiometry of the sample;
   - whether the spectra in Fig. 2 were Bose-corrected (Fig. S1 says no). This matters when comparing intensities across temperature.

14. Wording. "For the first time, it is demonstrated…" and the neuromorphic/quantum-device framing in the abstract and conclusions should be toned down to match a single-sample, bulk, cryogenic study.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Use Stokes/anti-Stokes thermometry on the existing spectra to put T_T on an independent footing, and add a cold-finger-cycling Raman series as the key control for "thermal" memory.
- Present a reproducibility panel: the same cycling sequence on several spots and pieces, plus a repeat on the same spot, with the spread shown on Fig. 3c–e.
- Convert the Raman intensities and the magnetization into estimated COO/FMM phase fractions and plot them on one T_T axis with the resistivity. This would make the correlation quantitative and test the percolation picture of Fig. 5.
- Recast the discussion around the known kinetic-arrest and minor-loop framework, and identify what the structural (JT-mode) probe adds, for example whether the AS- and S-JT modes respond differently, as Fig. 3b suggests.

5. Verdict: major revision
