Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors heat a polycrystalline La0.275Pr0.35Ca0.375MnO3 (LPCMO) sample cyclically with the Raman laser, between a base temperature T_B ≈ 38 K and rising target temperatures T_T. They report that the intensity of the Jahn-Teller (JT) stretching modes measured back at T_B grows when T_T lies between T_C and T_MI, and resets when T_T exceeds T_COO. Separate cryostat-driven cycles show a matching drop in magnetization and rise in resistivity. They present this as a thermal-memory effect in the lattice and as a route to laser-induced, non-volatile resistive switching. A local structural probe of history-dependent phase separation is of interest. However, thermal-history and memory effects in the dynamic phase-separation regime of LPCMO are already well documented. The new element is therefore the Raman readout and the laser-heating protocol. That element rests on a laser-temperature calibration and a link to transport that, as presented, need much stronger support.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear, simple protocol (Fig. 1b) that brings history dependence in a phase-separated manganite into the lattice (JT phonon) channel.
- The Raman intensity map at T_B against T_T (Fig. 3a) closely resembles the conventional heating map (Fig. 2b). The window between T_C and T_COO is qualitatively consistent with the macroscopic M and ρ cycles (Fig. 4c,d).
- The reset above T_COO (Figs. 1h, 3e, 4d) argues against a purely cumulative, irreversible change of the probed spot.
- The supplementary material documents the power-temperature calibration in detail (Table S1, Figs. S1-S5). It also gives the fitting procedure (Fig. S6) and a Rietveld refinement confirming single-phase Pnma (Fig. S7, Table S2).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The Raman memory and the transport and magnetization memory come from different protocols, so the central link and the "laser-induced resistive switching" claim are not demonstrated.** The Raman cycles are driven by the laser (Methods, "cyclic procedure/heating"):
   - a hot spot of about 50 µm inside a cold sample;
   - cooling that is "almost instantaneous";
   - T_B ≈ 38 K;
   - a different piece and surface from the transport sample.

   The M and ρ cycles (Fig. 4) differ on every point: the whole pellet is heated in the PPMS, it is cooled at 5 K/min, and T_B = 30 K.

   In LPCMO the dynamic phase-separation state is known to depend strongly on cooling rate (kinetic arrest, glassy relaxation). Agreement between Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 is therefore not evidence that the same reconfiguration occurs. Despite this, the Discussion and Conclusions state that the work "demonstrates the potential of laser-induced thermal cycling to induce non-volatile structure/resistive-switching" and that "for the first time" resistive switching is "associated with the local structure". No resistance was ever measured after a laser cycle.

   The authors should do at least one of the following:
   - (a) Repeat the Raman cycles using the cryostat heater at fixed low laser power, with the same T_B, T_T set and ramp rate as Fig. 4. This test is straightforward, since the standard-heating data of Fig. 2 already use this mode.
   - (b) Measure resistance across a laser-cycled region, for example on a thin film or a narrow bar whose cross-section is dominated by the illuminated volume.

   Failing either, the laser-switching claims in the abstract, Discussion and Conclusions should be removed.

2. **The laser-heating temperature scale is calibrated using the observable under study, with no independent thermometer, and its uncertainty is understated.** Every T_T in Figs. 1 and 3, and their placement relative to T_C, T_MI and T_COO (the core claim that the window coincides with the transitions), comes from Table S1. That table was built by "visual inspection and comparison" of spectra. The comparison relied on:
   - the low-frequency (Bose-factor) region;
   - the widths and intensities of the 250, 480 and 614 cm⁻¹ structures.

   The last two quantities are exactly the JT intensities and widths that the paper shows to be history dependent. The reference curve (Fig. 2) is itself one particular thermal history. The procedure is therefore partly circular.

   The calibration also has these problems:
   - It assumes linearity and fixes the intercept to zero (footnote, p. 12), although the free fit gives -3(5) K.
   - It adds a 21.8 K offset by extrapolation.
   - It quotes uncertainties of ±4-5 K that reflect only the linear-fit statistics, not the visual matching or background differences, which the authors acknowledge (p. 12).
   - It ignores the radial and depth temperature gradient inside a 50 µm Gaussian spot at up to 150 mW. The thermal conductivity of LPCMO changes across T_C, so the gradient is not constant.

   The spectra extend to negative Raman shift (Fig. S2), so the authors should use the Stokes/anti-Stokes intensity ratio of the 250 or 480 cm⁻¹ mode as an independent local thermometer at each target power. They should give realistic uncertainties on T_T, and discuss how a temperature distribution within the probed volume affects the identification of the window T_C < T_T < T_COO.

B. Should address

3. **One cumulative sequence on one spot cannot separate dependence on T_T from dependence on the number of cycles.** T_T only increases (Fig. 1b, Table S1), so each data point in Fig. 3 combines the effect of the current T_T with the whole prior history. The claim that the system "stores" the state reached at T_T needs:
   - non-cumulative cycles, in which a fresh as-cooled state (warm above T_COO, cool to T_B) precedes each single excursion to T_T;
   - repeated cycles to the same T_T, to test whether the change saturates or keeps accumulating;
   - an independent repetition on another spot and another piece.

   Methods states that "different spots and pieces of the sample were measured with the same protocol", but only one data set is shown. The spot-to-spot spread should be shown, for example as overlaid curves in Fig. 3e. The same applies to Fig. 4: is the result reproduced over a second pellet or a second run after thermal cycling to room temperature?

4. **Laser-induced surface modification is not excluded.** Power densities reach about 150 mW on a 50 µm spot, and the same spot is revisited throughout the sequence. Manganites are known to lose oxygen and decompose under such illumination. The strongest Raman line of the decomposition product Mn3O4 lies near 655-660 cm⁻¹, close to the "spurious peak" marked in red in Fig. 2a, which the authors attribute to oxygen vacancies without evidence. Fig. S6f (150 mW) also shows a strongly changed background.

   The reset seen in Fig. S6i is reassuring, but the authors should:
   - track the intensity of the ~660 cm⁻¹ feature at T_B after every cycle;
   - show optical images of the spot before and after the sequence;
   - repeat the full sequence a second time on the same spot to demonstrate that the irreversibility is itself reproducible.

5. **The extracted intensities depend on the background model, and the normalization uses a mode that also changes.** All spectra were fitted with Lorentzians on a "fixed predefined 7th degree polynomial" background, and fit quality was judged visually (Fig. S6). The JT modes are broad (FWHM 50-120 cm⁻¹, Figs. 2c, 3b) and overlap, so their areas are very sensitive to the background. The raw spectra after cycling (Fig. 1e,f) show a clear increase in background at high shift as well.

   Intensities are then normalized to the ~250 cm⁻¹ rotational mode. Yet Fig. S5d shows that the area of this mode itself changes by a factor of about 4 across the same temperature range, and its frequency shifts at T_C (Fig. S4a). The authors should:
   - state how the error bars in Figs. 2c,d and 3b-e were obtained;
   - show un-normalized JT areas and the background level at T_B against T_T;
   - test how robust ΔI/I₀ is to the polynomial order or to a physically motivated background (electronic continuum).

6. **Treating JT intensity as a proxy for the AFMI-COO phase fraction is an assumption.** The Discussion and Fig. 5 read the JT intensity at T_B as the amount of charge- and orbitally-ordered (COO) phase. JT-mode intensity in manganites also reflects:
   - correlated-polaron short-range order;
   - electronic screening in the metallic fraction;
   - resonance conditions at 532 nm, which change with the electronic state.

   In Fig. 2d the JT intensity peaks near T_MI (~150 K), where correlated polarons are strongest, not where the COO fraction is largest. Fig. 5 is described as "semi-quantitative", but no quantity enters it. The authors should justify the mapping and, if possible, convert ΔM at T_B into an estimated ferromagnetic-metal (FMM) fraction. That would require M at a small DC field, compared with the saturated moment.

7. **Non-volatility and time relaxation are not tested.** LPCMO in the dynamic phase-separation regime shows strong time relaxation of M and ρ at low temperature, which is part of the cited literature. The Raman spectra are taken after a 5-minute wait, and M and ρ after a 5 K/min ramp, with no time dependence shown. Calling the effect "non-volatile" resistive switching and "memory" requires M(t) or ρ(t) at T_B after selected cycles (for example T_T = 100, 150 and 210 K) over hours, and Raman spectra repeated over the same interval.

8. **The magnetic and transport measurements need clarification.**
   - "Magnetization" was measured in ACMS mode with a 10 Oe AC field and no DC field (Methods), yet Fig. 4a reports it in µB/Mn. The authors should state whether this is the AC moment, the real part of χ_ac, or something else, and how the PPMS remanent field was handled. The zero-field ferromagnetic signal depends on domain state and remanent field, which could also show cycle dependence unrelated to phase fractions.
   - Resistivity was measured with two probes using silver paint on a pellet at 0.1 µA. In the insulating regime (up to about 10⁵ Ω mm) contact resistance and grain-boundary contributions can dominate and can themselves be history dependent. A four-probe measurement, or at least a check of the contact resistance, is needed before attributing the threefold change in ρ (Fig. 4d) to bulk phase reconfiguration.

9. **The novelty needs positioning against existing work on thermal-history memory in manganites.** History-dependent states and memory after partial thermal cycling in LPCMO and related compounds are established, for example:
   - Ghivelder 2005, Sacanell 2004/2018 and Kundhikanjana 2015, cited here;
   - work on kinetic arrest and on cooling and heating in unequal fields (CHUF protocols), not cited.

   The macroscopic results of Fig. 4 should be compared explicitly with these works. The text should then state what the Raman data add beyond them. Claims such as "previously unidentified regime" and "for the first time" should be moderated accordingly.

C. Minor

10. **The reference list is missing.** All citations appear as unresolved BibTeX keys (for example [Sacanell2004], [Diego24], [GSAS]), so none of the evidence cited from the literature can be checked.

11. **Figure references are wrong in several places:**
    - The Fig. 1 caption says "Figures d-f" for five spectra (d-h).
    - p. 6: "In figure 2 e" should read Fig. 3e.
    - Methods: "cyclic procedure, depicted in figure 1 a" should read Fig. 1b.
    - The text on p. 15 cites "S4 (c) and (f)", but Fig. S4 has no panel f.
    - The captions of Figs. S4 and S5 call the power data "orange", but they are plotted in yellow.

12. **Transition temperatures and their labels are inconsistent.**
    - T_C is about 93-100 K in Figs. 2 and 3, while magnetization gives T_C^W ≈ 91 K and T_C^C ≈ 63 K (Fig. S8a).
    - T_IM = 120 K on cooling and T_MI = 153 K on warming.
    - Figs. S4 and S5 use T_FPS and T_OCO.
    - Fig. S8a marks T_N ≈ 188 K, which is never discussed. It coincides with the upper end of the Raman decrease (T_T = 188 K).

    The authors should state which value of each transition is drawn in each figure, and why the warming values are the relevant ones.

13. **Inconsistencies in the transport description.**
    - The resistivity change is described as "two-fold" (p. 7) but also as "about three times" (Discussion). ΔF/F₀ ≈ 2 corresponds to a threefold increase.
    - The Fig. 4 caption lists T_T = 70, 90, 100, 150 and 210 K, whereas Methods gives 50, 60, 70, ..., 250 K.

14. **The lattice-parameter relation is wrong.** Methods states "a ≈ c ≈ √2 b". With b = 7.678 Å and a ≈ c ≈ 5.44 Å (Table S2), the correct relation is b ≈ √2 a ≈ 2a_p.

    Separately, the Rietveld residuals in Fig. S7c-e show systematic misfits at split reflections. The authors should comment on whether these hint at strain or a minority phase.

15. **Method details are missing.**
    - The laser power is not specified at the sample.
    - The Bose-Einstein factor is not corrected (Fig. S1 caption), although Fig. 2d compares intensities across temperatures. This should be stated in the main text.

16. **The language needs editing.** Examples: "an clear agreement"; "It was observed for the that"; "it's"; and the title hyphenation "thermal - memory".

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a figure that compares directly, at the same T_B and T_T values, the Raman results from cryostat-heater cycling, from laser cycling and from the M and ρ cycles. This would separate the effects of the laser and of local heating from the intrinsic thermal memory.
- Use Stokes/anti-Stokes thermometry in place of the visual-matching calibration. Report T_T with realistic uncertainties and a simple estimate of the temperature profile within the spot.
- Add non-cumulative single-cycle runs, repeated cycles to the same T_T, and measurements at two or more spots and samples. Show the spread between them.
- Add time-dependent measurements at T_B to support the "non-volatile" wording. If a laser-switching device claim is kept, add a transport measurement on a laser-cycled region.

5. Verdict: major revision
