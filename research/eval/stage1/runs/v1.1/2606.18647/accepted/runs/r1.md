Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports a Hall response in Coulomb-coupled MoSe2/WSe2 electron-hole double layers that the authors attribute to equilibrium interlayer trions moving under the Lorentz force. The main signature is an electron-like (negative) Hall resistance, R_xy and R_xy,drag, measured on the hole (W) layer while the electron (Mo) layer is open-circuited, in the region the authors assign to an exciton plus negative-trion mixture (X + X−). This signature disappears when the temperature or the doping density is raised. Showing that a charged three-body bound state carries a Hall response would be a clear step beyond the recent zero-field reports of trion fluids (refs. 38, 39) and towards trion quantum Hall physics. The central question is whether the observed signature is specific to trions.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The material platform and measurement capability are state of the art. The authors measure the W layer in two configurations (open-circuit Hall and Coulomb drag) and add valley-resolved optical reflection contrast on the same type of structure.
- The trion regions are identified in several independent ways. These are the R_xx metal-insulator boundaries (Fig. 2), large R_xx,drag (Fig. 3a), valley-resolved RC showing that positive trions appear only when both hole valleys are occupied (Fig. 3b, Extended Data Figs. 5 and 6), their disappearance at 10 K, and the magnetic-field evolution (Extended Data Figs. 4 and 8).
- The sign structure is clear. In the X + X+ and X + X− regions, R_xy,drag is positive and negative respectively, and R_xy shows the same contrast at low T (Fig. 4a,b). Both vanish continuously with temperature and outside the trion regions (Fig. 4c–e).
- The 4 × 4 conductivity-tensor model (Methods, equations (1)–(8)) is a transparent framework that predicts the observed signs.
- The authors are careful not to over-interpret the resistance oscillations in the X + X+ region (Extended Data Fig. 7).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The negative Hall signal is not shown to be specific to trions, by the paper's own model.**
   - *What the paper claims.* The main text states that the negative R_xy in the X + X− region "unambiguously demonstrates the presence of Coulomb drag and the trion Hall effect".
   - *Why the model does not support this.* In equation (7), take the limit with no mobile trions (σ_T0 → 0, f → 0). The expression reduces to R_xy ≈ −B/(e n_e). A mixture of perfectly dragged neutral excitons and free electrons would therefore also give an electron-like Hall voltage on the open-circuit hole layer. The sign comes from free electrons counterflowing in the Mo layer, which is forced by the open-circuit condition. It does not come from trions experiencing the Lorentz force.
   - *Where trions actually enter.* Trions enter only through the magnitude: the f² term and n_T. For the positive-trion region, equation (8) shows that R_xy measures only free holes, so there the trion contribution appears only in R_xy,drag.
   - *Why this matters.* The headline claim that trions carry the Hall response therefore rests on a quantitative statement the paper does not make. The temperature dependence does not separate the two scenarios either, because the high-T limit in the Methods assumes that excitons and trions dissociate together.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Fit R_xx, R_xy, R_xx,drag and R_xy,drag together across the X + X− region (Fig. 4a,b,f) using equation (7). Use n_e and n_T from the calibrated gate capacitances (the dashed lines in Fig. 4f) and show that a trion term (f > 0, with physically sensible σ_T0/σ_X0) is needed to reproduce the measured Hall density.
   - Show a control in which excitons and free electrons coexist without trions, for example the field range where the authors say trions are destroyed by the valley Zeeman splitting (B ≳ 8 T, Extended Data Fig. 4d,e). Report whether the negative R_xy is absent there and how that fits equation (7).
   - Without one of these, the title claim should be narrowed to a drag-induced Hall response in a bound-state fluid.

2. **The paper does not say whether the Hall data were antisymmetrized in magnetic field, although they were taken in a highly resistive regime.**
   - *What the data show.* In the trion regions, R_xx is 10^4–10^5 Ω at 1.5 K (Fig. 2b; Extended Data Fig. 4). The negative R_xy in the X + X− region is a few kΩ (Fig. 4e) and appears below about 4 K, exactly where R_xx rises steeply.
   - *Why this matters.* Mixing in a few per cent of R_xx through contact misalignment or inhomogeneous current paths would produce a signal of this size and temperature dependence.
   - *What is missing.* The Methods describe the lock-in measurement but do not mention ±B reversal or symmetrization. Extended Data Fig. 4e shows only positive B.
   - *Why this is not settled by the sign contrast.* The opposite signs in the X + X+ and X + X− regions, and the agreement between R_xy and R_xy,drag, argue against a pure artifact. They do not exclude it, because the two regions have different R_xx and the positive-trion region has a large genuine hole Hall signal.
   - *What the authors should do.* State how R_xy and R_xy,drag were obtained. Show the ±B data, or antisymmetrized maps, for Fig. 4a–e, together with the symmetric part. The symmetric part bounds the R_xx admixture.

B. Should address

3. **The drag ratio contradicts the perfect-drag assumption.**
   - The model assumes perfect Coulomb drag for both excitons and trions.
   - Extended Data Fig. 3b shows a drag ratio of about 0.2–0.3 inside both trion regions, compared with about 1 in the X region.
   - In the X + X+ region with the hole layer closed, equation (3) with E_h ≈ 0 predicts a drag ratio of (σ_X + 2σ_T)/(σ_X + σ_T) ≥ 1, because free electrons are neglected there.
   - Either the hole-layer contact and series resistances make E_h ≠ 0, or the assumption fails.
   - The authors should reconcile the measured drag ratio with the model, for example by including the measured hole-layer resistance in the closed-circuit analysis. Otherwise they should relax the perfect-drag assumption and show how this changes equations (7) and (8). This bears directly on concern 1.

4. **The Hall effect is shown in only one device.**
   - Fig. 4 and Extended Data Figs. 3 and 4 all come from device 3 (Δ = 9 V).
   - Device 1 (Δ = 13 V) supplies the R_xx phase diagram, and device 2 (Δ = 11 V) supplies the optics.
   - The text says only that the crossover temperature is "of the same order of magnitude in other devices".
   - The authors should show the negative R_xy and R_xy,drag in the X + X− region for at least one more device, or state that it was not measured. They should also give the device-to-device spread in the crossover temperature and the binding-energy estimates.

5. **Interlayer leakage at the operating bias is not reported.**
   - The channel barrier is 1.5–2 nm of hBN, and V_b is about 0.76–0.9 V.
   - Tunnelling current through such a barrier can produce spurious drag and Hall signals, especially where the layers are resistive.
   - The Methods describe a transformer and potentiometer that suppress a.c. coupling, but they report no d.c. or a.c. interlayer leakage current over the V_b–V_g range of Figs. 2–4.
   - The authors should show interlayer current against V_b, or the drag signal with the bias reversed, to establish that leakage is negligible inside the trion regions.

6. **The trion binding-energy estimates differ, and inputs are inconsistent.**
   - *Three estimates are given:*
     - about 0.7 meV (8 K) from the Hall crossover temperature (main text);
     - about 1.2 meV from the RC injection threshold (Methods);
     - "the scale of millielectronvolts" (introduction).
   - *The hole mass used is inconsistent.* The Methods estimate uses m_h ≈ 0.45 m_0 in C_Q, but Extended Data Fig. 7d obtains a hole cyclotron mass of 0.32 m_e in the pi region of the same type of device.
   - *What the authors should do:*
     - Reconcile the mass used in C_Q.
     - Give an uncertainty for E_T,b.
     - Note that a dissociation crossover temperature in a fluid usually sits well below E_b/k_B, because of entropy, so the 8 K value is a lower bound rather than an estimate.
     - Show that the Zeeman criterion for trion instability (for example around 8–9 T in Extended Data Figs. 4 and 8) is quantitatively consistent with the quoted E_T,b and a stated valley g-factor.

7. **The trion-region boundaries and the gate calibration have no stated uncertainty.**
   - The X + X± regions are drawn as the metal-insulator crossover in R_xx (blue dashed lines). Their lower edges are p = 2n and n = 2p, from gate and interlayer capacitances.
   - Fig. 4f relies on this calibration for the "total" electron and hole densities that are compared with n_H.
   - The authors should give the capacitance values, how they were determined, and their uncertainty, and say how the boundaries were drawn.
   - The proposed cause of the field-induced expansion of the trion regions (binding energy enhanced by magnetic confinement) is presented as an explanation but is not tested. It should be labelled as tentative, or supported, for example by tracking the RC threshold shift with B (Extended Data Fig. 8).

8. **The quantum-oscillation mass analysis is fragile.**
   - In the X + X+ region, the Lifshitz–Kosevich fit (Extended Data Fig. 7d) uses amplitudes normalized at 1.5 K over only about 1.5–3 K, with the reference trace taken at 3.3 K. Neighbouring oscillations also overlap the trion boundary.
   - The LK form also assumes independent quasiparticle Landau levels. That is exactly what the authors argue these oscillations are not: they propose transitions between quantum Hall and trion phases.
   - The authors should either soften the quoted 0.65 m_e to a qualitative statement, or show fits with an uncertainty and with different reference temperatures.
   - They should also explain where the "expected trion mass ≈ 1.4–1.6 m_e" comes from.

C. Minor

9. **The quoted cyclotron energy does not match the quoted masses.**
   - The Conclusion gives the free-hole cyclotron energy at 4 T as about 0.8 meV.
   - With m_h = 0.45 m_0 one obtains ħω_c ≈ 1.0 meV, and with the measured 0.32 m_e about 1.45 meV.
   - The authors should state which mass was used.

10. **Region labels are inconsistent.** The Fig. 3b caption and the main text use "region III" and "region II", while the figure and Extended Data Fig. 6 use (iii) and (ii). The labels should be made consistent. The text says "(X + X+)" in places where the Extended Data captions say "(X + X+/−)". The authors should check that each caption refers to the correct region.

11. **The definition of the crossover temperature is not given.** Fig. 4c,d and the main text quote a crossover temperature of about 8 K that "decreases continuously with increasing trion density". The authors should define how it is extracted, for example as the sign change of R_xy or a fixed fraction of the low-T value, and plot it against the estimated trion density.

12. **Measurement parameters are missing.** The Methods should state:
    - the current through the hole layer in the open-circuit Hall configuration, given the 1 mV excitation and the large sample resistance;
    - whether self-heating was checked by varying the excitation;
    - the input-impedance and filtering limits of the voltage measurement when R_xx reaches 10^5–10^7 Ω (Fig. 2a colour scale).

13. **The grey "cannot be measured" areas need a criterion.** Fig. 4a,b and Extended Data Fig. 4 grey out large areas next to the trion regions. The authors should state the criterion, for example a resistance or phase threshold, so that readers can judge how close to the excitonic insulator the Hall data remain reliable.

14. **The bilayer MoSe2 choice needs justification.** The Methods say that bilayer MoSe2 "behaves effectively like a monolayer" because electrons sit in the layer next to the W layer. A brief justification, such as a reference or a check of the interlayer capacitance, would help, because the trion's spin and valley structure and its binding depend on it.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a figure that overlays the measured n_H(V_g) in the X + X− region (Fig. 4f) with equation (7). Use calibrated n_e and n_T and show two curves: with trions, and with excitons plus free electrons only. This would turn the qualitative sign argument into a quantitative test that only trions pass.
- Use the magnetic-field window where the authors say trions are unstable but excitons survive (Extended Data Fig. 4d,e) as a built-in null control for the Hall signature.
- Plot the crossover temperature of R_xy against the RC-derived binding energy at several densities. This would tie the transport and optical determinations of trion stability together.
- Report antisymmetrized Hall data and interlayer leakage maps in the Extended Data. Both are short additions that would remove the most obvious artifact explanations.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
