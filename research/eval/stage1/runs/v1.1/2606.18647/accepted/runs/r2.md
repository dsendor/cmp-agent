Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports Hall and Coulomb-drag Hall measurements on dual-gated MoSe2/WSe2 electron–hole double layers under a perpendicular field. It maps trion-bearing regions (X + X+ and X + X−) in the Vb–Vg phase diagram with Rxx, Rxx,drag and valley-resolved reflection contrast. The key observation is that, with the electron layer left open, the Hall resistance of the hole layer turns negative (electron-like) in the X + X− region and vanishes as trions dissociate with temperature or doping. The authors read this as a Hall response of mobile negative trions transmitted through perfect Coulomb drag. Transport by charged composite particles in a tunable bilayer is new, and it is a step towards trion quantum Hall physics. The main open question is how uniquely the sign reversal identifies trions, as opposed to bound pairs plus free carriers in general.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The paper reports a clear, qualitatively striking effect: the hole layer's Hall sign reverses (Fig. 4b,d,e) only inside the trion region, and the drag Hall signal (Fig. 4a,c) appears in the same regions.
- Several independent probes identify the trion regions consistently: Rxx and Rxx,drag maps (Figs. 2, 3a), temperature dependence (Fig. 2b,c), and valley- and helicity-resolved RC of the attractive polarons in both layers (Fig. 3b, Extended Data Figs. 5, 6, 8). The RC analysis also gives the spin-singlet (two-valley) character of the trions directly (regions II vs III).
- The binding energy is estimated two ways, from the injection threshold (~1.2 meV) and from the Hall crossover temperature (~0.7 meV), and the field evolution of the phase diagram (Extended Data Figs. 4, 8) fits a singlet trion destroyed by Zeeman splitting.
- A minimal 4 × 4 conductivity-tensor model (Methods, equations (1)–(8)) explains the signs of Rxy and Rxy,drag in both trion regions.
- Three devices are used, and the measurement scheme (transformer-coupled drive, balanced bias) is described in detail.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The central observation (negative hole-layer Rxy confined to the independently identified X + X− region and vanishing on trion dissociation) is supported well enough for the main claim. The items below would strengthen or sharpen it.

B. Should address

1. How specific is the negative Hall signal to trions? In the Methods model, when the electron layer is open, the negative Rxy in the X + X− region comes from bound complexes carrying hole-layer current, which forces a counterflow of free electrons in the electron layer. Equation (7) with f → 0 (no trion conductivity, only perfectly dragged excitons plus free electrons) still gives Rxy ≈ −B/(n_e e) < 0. So, within the authors' own model, a negative hole-layer Hall resistance does not single out mobile trions. It follows from any perfectly dragged neutral or charged pairs coexisting with free electrons, and free hole conduction is dropped by assumption. The statement on p. 4 that "the negative Hall resistance unambiguously demonstrates the presence of Coulomb drag and the trion Hall effect" therefore claims more than the data and model show. The authors should:
   - show which observable carries the trion-specific term, for example the n_T dependence of Rxy in equation (7), or the magnitude of n_H in Fig. 4f compared with the calibrated n_e and the trion density inferred from n − p;
   - compare Rxy quantitatively with equation (7) across the X + X− region using the gate-calibrated densities;
   - state explicitly what would distinguish an exciton-plus-free-electron fluid from a trion fluid, for example the electron-doped side of the exciton insulator where RC shows only one Mo valley occupied (region II, Fig. 3b), or the high-field data in Extended Data Fig. 4d,e where trions are unstable but excitons and free electrons remain. If Rxy stays positive there while bound excitons are still present, that would be strong evidence that the sign reversal needs trions.

2. Perfect-drag assumption against the measured drag ratio. The model assumes "perfect Coulomb drag for excitons and trions" (Methods), but Extended Data Fig. 3b shows a drag ratio of only ~0.2–0.3 inside the trion regions (~1 only in the X region). The paper calls this "strong" drag without reconciling it with the model input. A drag ratio well below 1 implies sizeable free-carrier or frictional contributions, which can change the magnitude and possibly the sign balance in equations (7) and (8). Please discuss how equations (7) and (8) change when trion drag is imperfect, and whether the observed Rxy sign survives for drag ratios of 0.2–0.3.

3. Antisymmetrization and Rxx admixture. In the trion regions Rxx is 10^4–10^6 Ω (Figs. 2a, 3a; Extended Data Fig. 4), while |Rxy| is a few kΩ. The Methods do not say whether Rxy and Rxy,drag were antisymmetrized in ±B. With such a large Rxx, even a small contact misalignment, or a field-symmetric drag contribution, can produce a Hall-like signal of either sign. The sign contrast between X + X+ and X + X− argues against a pure geometric artefact. Even so, please:
   - state the symmetrization procedure;
   - show Rxy(+B) and Rxy(−B) separately for one Vg in each trion region;
   - show Rxy versus B at fixed (Vg, Vb) at low field, demonstrating linear-in-B behaviour of the stated sign. The model is linearized "under a small perpendicular magnetic field", but all Hall data are at 4 T, where hole Landau quantization is already visible.

4. Interlayer leakage and the "open-circuit" condition. The channel barrier is only 1.5–2 nm of hBN and Vb ≈ 0.76–0.88 V. The whole interpretation of the hole-layer Hall data depends on zero net current in the electron layer. Please report the interlayer tunnelling current versus Vb in the relevant range and compare it with the a.c. drive current. Also say whether the electron layer, contacted only through Mo-10 at d.c. potential (Extended Data Fig. 2a), carries any a.c. current through capacitive coupling to ground at 7.33 Hz, given resistances up to MΩ.

5. Excitation level, heating and linear response. A 1 mV r.m.s. a.c. excitation is used. When the sample resistance dominates the circuit, a large share of this drops across the channel, i.e. up to ~1 meV. That is comparable to the trion binding energy (0.7–1.2 meV) and to k_BT at 1.5 K. Please show that Rxy and Rxy,drag in the trion regions do not depend on excitation amplitude, or quote the actual current and the voltage across the channel. Otherwise electron heating or field-induced dissociation could affect both the magnitude of the effect and the extracted crossover temperature.

6. Reproducibility across devices. The Hall phase diagram and its temperature dependence (Fig. 4) come from device 3 only. Device 1 is used for Rxx (Fig. 2) and device 2 for RC (Fig. 3). The text says the ~8 K crossover is "of the same order of magnitude as in other devices studied in this work", but no Hall data from another device are shown. Please add Rxy and Rxy,drag maps, or at least linecuts showing the negative Rxy in the X + X− region, for a second device (device 1 or 2), and state the total number of devices measured.

7. Two temperature scales in the X + X− region. Fig. 4e shows Rxy turning negative only below ~4 K at the chosen Vg, whereas the drag Hall signal and the crossover temperature are quoted as ~8 K. Please explain why the hole-layer Hall sign changes at a much lower temperature than the trion formation scale. For example, does this reflect the competition between σ_h (neglected in equation (7)) and σ_T? Please also discuss whether this changes the binding-energy estimate taken from the Hall crossover.

C. Minor

8. Fig. 4f: inside the X + X− region at 1.5 K, n_H first rises to a positive peak (~+3 × 10^12 cm^−2) near Vg ≈ 0.02 V and then diverges and changes sign. This non-monotonic behaviour, a zero crossing of 1/Rxy inside the trion region, is not discussed. Please comment, for instance on where the free-hole contribution is still relevant.

9. Equation (7) gives Rxy,drag ≈ −B/(n_e e) in the X + X− region, which depends only on the free-electron density and not on trions. Please state this explicitly in the main text, so readers do not take the negative drag Hall signal in Fig. 4a,c as independent trion evidence.

10. Trion binding energy. The injection-threshold estimate (Methods) uses 25 mV in Vb and capacitances with g_s g_v = 2 and m_h ≈ 0.45 m_0. The uncertainty on the 1.2 meV value, and its consistency with the ~0.7 meV from the 8 K crossover and with the ~6 T disappearance of the negative-Rxy region in Extended Data Fig. 4e (Zeeman splitting with the WSe2 g-factor), should be stated quantitatively.

11. Quantum oscillations in the X + X+ region (Extended Data Fig. 7). The cyclotron mass of 0.65 m_e comes from a Lifshitz–Kosevich fit over only 1.5–3 K, with amplitudes normalized at 1.5 K, which makes the fit loosely constrained. The proposed explanation, recurring transitions between hole quantum Hall states and a trion phase, is speculative. Please give fit uncertainties and soften the wording in the Conclusion, or add field-dependence evidence (for example the period in 1/B and its relation to the hole density).

12. Density calibration. The dashed density lines in Fig. 4f use geometric gate capacitances. Please say whether quantum capacitance and the doping offset of the pn threshold (the yellow line) are included, since n_H is compared directly with these lines.

13. Bilayer MoSe2 is said to behave "effectively like a monolayer" because electrons are confined to the layer next to WSe2 at high Δ. Please give supporting evidence, for example the RC spectra of the Mo layer, and say whether Δ = 9, 11 and 13 V in the three devices all satisfy this.

14. The optical measurements use illumination up to 50 nW µm^−2 on a gated, highly insulating heterostructure. Please comment on possible photodoping or photogating shifts of the phase boundaries when overlaying transport-derived boundaries on the RC maps (Extended Data Figs. 5, 6, 8), which come from a different device.

15. Wording: the abstract says "an electron-like Hall effect emerges on the hole layer in the presence of negatively charged trions because of Coulomb drag". This is accurate, but the main text's "unambiguously demonstrates ... the trion Hall effect" should be brought in line with it (see item 1).

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a figure or panel comparing measured Rxy (and n_H) in both trion regions with equations (7) and (8), using calibrated densities and the measured drag ratio. This would turn the qualitative sign argument into a quantitative test.
- Show a control region or condition where bound excitons and free electrons coexist but trions do not (region II of Fig. 3b, or B ≳ 8 T), demonstrating that Rxy remains positive there.
- Present Hall data from a second device, with ±B raw traces and an excitation-amplitude check.
- Discuss the competing free-hole contribution in the X + X− region, to explain the ~4 K versus ~8 K scales and the non-monotonic n_H in Fig. 4f.

5. Verdict

Verdict: minor revision
