Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports Hall and Hall-drag measurements on dual-gated MoSe2/WSe2 electron–hole double layers in the parameter regions where equilibrium trions were recently identified at zero field. It maps how the positive- and negative-trion regions grow into triangles (X + X+, X + X−) under a perpendicular field, using transport and valley-resolved reflection contrast. Its central result is an electron-like (negative) Hall resistance on the hole layer in the X + X− region, which disappears when trions dissociate thermally or at high doping. This is presented as the first trion Hall effect. If the effect is shown to be intrinsic and specific to trions, it is a clear, timely step: it shows that composite charged quasiparticles in these bilayers move as charge carriers and respond to the Lorentz force, and it points toward trion quantum Hall physics.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Hard experiments done carefully: separate contacts to each layer, Bi contacts to the MoSe2, exciton-contact regions, and a transformer and potentiometer scheme that reduces AC coupling in the drag measurement (Extended Data Fig. 2).
- The trion regions are identified in several independent ways: insulating Rxx and large Rxx,drag (Fig. 2, Fig. 3a), valley-resolved reflection contrast showing that holes occupy both valleys in the X + X+ region and electrons occupy both valleys in the X + X− region (Fig. 3b, Extended Data Figs. 5, 6, 8), and the field evolution (Extended Data Figs. 4, 8).
- The negative Rxy on the hole layer in the X + X− region is a counter-intuitive signature. Together with the sign of Rxy,drag in both trion regions (Fig. 4a,b) and its disappearance with temperature (Fig. 4c–e), it makes a compelling qualitative picture.
- A simple four-by-four conductivity-tensor model (Methods, equations (1)–(8)) explains the signs and gives explicit formulas for the Hall responses.
- The authors also check that the resistance oscillations in the X + X+ region are not trion Landau levels: the cyclotron mass of 0.65 me is below the expected trion mass (Extended Data Fig. 7). This shows appropriate caution.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The negative Hall signal needs field-antisymmetrization, or an equivalent check against Rxx mixing. In the X + X− region the hole layer is strongly resistive. Rxx is about 10^4 Ω at 1.5 K and rises on cooling (Fig. 2b, orange region; Fig. 2c). The negative Rxy reaches only about −1 kΩ (Fig. 4e, top) and appears below about 4 K, exactly as Rxx grows. A contact misalignment of a few per cent between the Hall probes (W-4/W-5, Extended Data Fig. 2a) would mix a temperature-dependent longitudinal voltage of this size, and possibly this sign, into Rxy. The same applies to Rxy,drag, measured where Rxx,drag is large. The Methods do not say whether Rxy and Rxy,drag were antisymmetrized in B. All field-dependent data, including the B sweep in Extended Data Fig. 4e, are shown at positive B only. The authors should:
   - show Rxy(V_g) and Rxy,drag(V_g) at +B and −B for the cut in Fig. 4c–f;
   - present antisymmetrized data, or quantify the symmetric component;
   - show that the antisymmetric part changes sign in the X + X− region and is linear in B at low field.
   
   Without this check, the headline electron-like Hall signal could still be a geometric artifact.

2. The model shows that a negative Rxy is not by itself specific to trions, so the claim that the negative Hall resistance "unambiguously demonstrates ... the trion Hall effect" (p. 4) goes beyond the analysis. In equation (7), f = σT0/(σT0 + σX0). If no trions are present (σT0 → 0, f → 0), the model still gives Rxy ≈ −B/(n_e e) and Rxy,drag ≈ −B/(n_e e): perfectly dragged excitons plus a counterflow of free electrons already produce an electron-like Hall response on the hole layer. Only the f²/n_T term in equation (7), and the corresponding term in equation (8), carries trion information. The same holds in the X + X+ region, where Rxy,drag tends to B/(n_h e) without trions. The main text says that including excitons "does not change the picture", but the formulas show the sign comes from bound-pair drag plus excess free carriers, not from trions as such. The authors should:
   - use the calibrated n_e, n_h and the gate-derived trion density (the dashed lines in Fig. 4f) to test quantitatively whether the measured Rxy and Rxy,drag need the trion term. For example, compare n_H extracted from Rxy,drag (which the model says gives n_e directly) with n_H from Rxy, and extract n_T and f.
   - check that the extracted f and n_T agree with the drag ratio in the trion regions (about 0.2–0.3 in Extended Data Fig. 3b).
   
   If this cannot be done, they should reword the claim: the Hall sign shows Coulomb-drag-mediated transport by bound charged complexes, while the trion assignment rests on the reflection-contrast and earlier thermodynamic evidence.

B. Should address

3. The Hall and Hall-drag data come from one device. All Rxy and Rxy,drag data (Fig. 4, Extended Data Figs. 3, 4) are from device 3. Device 1 gives Rxx (Fig. 2, Extended Data Fig. 7), and device 2 gives Rxx,drag and optics (Fig. 3, Extended Data Figs. 5, 6, 8). The text says the roughly 8 K crossover temperature "is of the same order of magnitude as in other devices studied in this work (Methods provides discussions on Rxy,drag)". The Methods contain no such discussion and no Hall data from another device. The authors should show the negative Rxy in the X + X− region, and the sign structure of Rxy,drag, in at least one more device, or state plainly that the Hall result rests on one device. They should also supply the promised Methods discussion.

4. Landau quantization at the measurement field complicates the Hall-density analysis. At 4 T the cyclotron energy of free holes is about 0.8 meV, comparable to the trion binding energy (p. 5), and Rxx oscillates in the trion and pi regions (Extended Data Figs. 3b, 4, 7). Yet the Hall analysis uses the classical small-field formulas: the linearized σ_i in equation (6), and n_H = B/(e Rxy) in Fig. 4f. Kinks in n_H near the X + X− boundary at 1.5 K (Fig. 4f) may come from quantum oscillations rather than trion physics. The authors should:
   - show Rxy versus B inside each trion region at fixed (V_g, V_b) from low field, and check that the slope sign persists at 1–2 T, where oscillations are weak;
   - say whether ω_c τ is small, as the linearization assumes.

5. Interlayer leakage and AC coupling are not reported. The barrier is 1.5–2 nm of hBN and V_b is about 0.76–0.88 V, so some interlayer tunnelling is possible. The model assumes none. For the Rxy measurement the Mo layer is AC-open but DC-connected (Extended Data Fig. 2a), and the voltage transformer is used only in the drag configuration. The authors should report:
   - the DC interlayer leakage current over the V_b range used;
   - the out-of-phase lock-in components in the trion regions, where resistances reach 10^4–10^6 Ω and RC phase shifts at 7.33 Hz may matter;
   
   and they should show these do not affect the sign of Rxy.

6. The trion binding energy estimates are only loosely consistent. The Hall-drag crossover temperature of about 8 K is converted to "0.7 meV" (p. 4). The Methods estimate from the valley-injection threshold is about 1.2 meV and is said to be in "good agreement". Meanwhile Rxy turns negative only below about 4 K (Fig. 4e). Thermal dissociation of a bound state normally happens at k_BT well below E_b, because of entropy, so equating the crossover temperature with E_b is crude. The authors should explain why the Rxy and Rxy,drag crossovers differ by a factor of about two, and give a rough uncertainty for both E_b estimates.

7. The assumptions of perfect drag for trions and negligible frictional drag for free carriers need support. The model assumes perfect drag for excitons and trions and no frictional drag between free electrons and holes (Methods). Extended Data Fig. 3 shows drag ratios of only about 0.2–0.3 in the trion regions. The authors should show that these values follow from the model with perfect trion drag, given the free-carrier fractions. They should also estimate how much frictional Hall drag from free carriers, which can itself produce a Hall-drag signal, could add to Rxy,drag outside the bound-state regions.

C. Minor

8. Gate dielectric thicknesses in the channel, and the capacitances used to convert V_g and V_b into the densities in Fig. 4f, are not given. Only the interlayer value is given (C_2L ≈ 1.55 μF cm^-2). Please list them, together with the uncertainty of the dashed density lines.

9. Device 2's optical image is not shown. Extended Data Fig. 1 shows only devices 1 and 3. Please add a table listing each device: Δ, hBN thicknesses, MoSe2 layer number, and which figures use it.

10. In Fig. 4f the 1.5 K n_H curve diverges near the X + X− boundary (V_g ≈ 0.05 V), producing large positive and negative spikes. Please say whether these points lie where Rxy passes through zero, and mask or comment on them.

11. Fig. 2a (device 1) has V_b of about 0.70–0.80 V and Fig. 4 (device 3) about 0.84–0.92 V. Please explain briefly the device dependence of the pn onset, through Δ and the band offsets, so readers can compare the maps.

12. The p. 3 statement that the X + X− region "is substantially more insulating" because the exciton binding energy is an order of magnitude larger than the trion binding energy is qualitative. A quantitative comparison of Rxx in the two regions at matched |n − 2p| would help.

13. Notation: Fig. 1d,e labels the regions with "n = 2p" and "p = 2n", while the text and figure captions refer to "X+X+/−" (Extended Data Figs. 3, 4). Please use the same notation throughout.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a figure with ±B raw and antisymmetrized Rxy and Rxy,drag along the V_b = 0.88 V cut, and low-field Rxy(B) traces inside each trion region.
- Fit equations (7) and (8) quantitatively to Fig. 4 using the calibrated densities, to show that the trion term is required, and compare the fitted f with the measured drag ratio.
- Show the negative-Rxy result in a second device, and fill the promised Methods discussion of device-to-device variation in the crossover temperature.
- Present the trion binding energy from all three routes (crossover in Rxy,drag, crossover in Rxy, and the valley-injection threshold) in one place, with uncertainties.

5. Verdict: major revision
