A. Must address

1. **The negative Hall signal is not shown to be specific to trions, by the paper's own model.** The main text (p. 4) states that the negative Rxy in the X + X− region "unambiguously demonstrates the presence of Coulomb drag and the trion Hall effect". In equation (7), with f = σT0/(σT0 + σX0), take the limit with no mobile trions (σT0 → 0, f → 0): the model still gives Rxy ≈ −B/(n_e e) and Rxy,drag ≈ −B/(n_e e). A mixture of perfectly dragged neutral excitons and free electrons therefore already produces an electron-like Hall voltage on the open-circuit hole layer. The sign comes from free electrons counterflowing in the Mo layer, forced by the open-circuit condition, not from trions experiencing the Lorentz force; free hole conduction is also dropped by assumption. Trions enter only through the magnitude, via the f² and n_T term in equation (7) and the corresponding term in equation (8). The same holds in the X + X+ region, where equation (8) shows Rxy measures only free holes and Rxy,drag tends to B/(n_h e) without trions. Rxy,drag in the X + X− region depends only on the free-electron density, so the negative drag Hall signal in Fig. 4a,c is not independent trion evidence; the main text should say so. The statement that including excitons "does not change the picture" is not borne out by the formulas, and the temperature dependence does not separate the scenarios because the Methods' high-T limit assumes excitons and trions dissociate together. The authors should: (i) fit Rxx, Rxy, Rxx,drag and Rxy,drag together across the X + X− region (Fig. 4a,b,f) with equation (7), using n_e, n_h and n_T from the calibrated gate capacitances (the dashed lines in Fig. 4f), for example comparing n_H from Rxy,drag (which the model says gives n_e directly) with n_H from Rxy, and show that a trion term (f > 0, with physically sensible σT0/σX0) is required, with two curves: with trions, and with excitons plus free electrons only; (ii) check that the extracted f and n_T agree with the measured drag ratio (about 0.2–0.3, Extended Data Fig. 3b); (iii) show a null control where bound excitons and free electrons coexist without trions, such as the field range where trions are said to be destroyed by valley Zeeman splitting (B ≳ 8 T, Extended Data Fig. 4d,e) or the electron-doped side of the excitonic insulator where RC shows only one Mo valley occupied (region II, Fig. 3b), and report whether Rxy stays positive there and how that fits equation (7). Without this, the claim should be narrowed to a Coulomb-drag-mediated Hall response of a bound-state fluid, with the trion assignment resting on the reflection-contrast and earlier thermodynamic evidence, and the main-text wording brought in line with the more careful abstract.

2. **The Hall data are not shown to be antisymmetrized in magnetic field, although they were taken in a highly resistive regime.** In the trion regions Rxx is 10^4–10^6 Ω at 1.5 K and rises on cooling (Figs. 2a–c, 3a; Extended Data Fig. 4), while the negative Rxy in the X + X− region is only about −1 kΩ to a few kΩ (Fig. 4e) and appears below about 4 K, exactly where Rxx grows steeply. A contact misalignment of a few per cent between the Hall probes (W-4/W-5, Extended Data Fig. 2a), inhomogeneous current paths, or a field-symmetric drag contribution would mix in a temperature-dependent longitudinal voltage of this size, and possibly this sign. The same applies to Rxy,drag, measured where Rxx,drag is large. The Methods describe the lock-in measurement but do not mention ±B reversal or symmetrization, and all field-dependent data, including the B sweep in Extended Data Fig. 4e, are shown at positive B only. The opposite signs in the X + X+ and X + X− regions and the agreement between Rxy and Rxy,drag argue against a pure artifact but do not exclude it, because the two regions have different Rxx and the positive-trion region has a large genuine hole Hall signal. The authors should state how Rxy and Rxy,drag were obtained; show Rxy(Vg) and Rxy,drag(Vg) at +B and −B for the cut in Fig. 4c–f (or antisymmetrized maps for Fig. 4a–e) together with the symmetric part, which bounds the Rxx admixture; and show that the antisymmetric part changes sign in the X + X− region and is linear in B at low field. Without this check the headline electron-like Hall signal could still be a geometric artifact.

B. Should address

3. **The measured drag ratio contradicts the perfect-drag assumption.** The model assumes perfect Coulomb drag for excitons and trions and no frictional drag between free electrons and holes (Methods), but Extended Data Fig. 3b shows a drag ratio of only about 0.2–0.3 inside both trion regions, against about 1 in the X region; the paper calls this "strong" drag without reconciling it with the model. In the X + X+ region with the hole layer closed, equation (3) with E_h ≈ 0 predicts a drag ratio of (σX + 2σT)/(σX + σT) ≥ 1, since free electrons are neglected there. Either hole-layer contact and series resistances make E_h ≠ 0, or the assumption fails; a drag ratio well below 1 implies sizeable free-carrier or frictional contributions that can change the magnitude and possibly the sign balance in equations (7) and (8). The authors should reconcile the measured drag ratio with the model, for example by including the measured hole-layer resistance in the closed-circuit analysis and the free-carrier fractions, or relax the perfect-drag assumption and show how equations (7) and (8) change and whether the observed Rxy sign survives for drag ratios of 0.2–0.3. They should also estimate how much frictional Hall drag from free carriers could contribute to Rxy,drag. This bears directly on concern 1.

4. **The Hall effect is shown in only one device.** All Rxy and Rxy,drag data (Fig. 4, Extended Data Figs. 3, 4) come from device 3 (Δ = 9 V). Device 1 (Δ = 13 V) supplies Rxx (Fig. 2, Extended Data Fig. 7) and device 2 (Δ = 11 V) supplies Rxx,drag and optics (Fig. 3, Extended Data Figs. 5, 6, 8). The text says the ~8 K crossover "is of the same order of magnitude as in other devices studied in this work (Methods provides discussions on Rxy,drag)", but the Methods contain no such discussion and no Hall data from another device. The authors should show the negative Rxy in the X + X− region, and the sign structure of Rxy,drag, in at least one more device (maps or at least linecuts), or state plainly that the Hall result rests on one device; supply the promised Methods discussion; state the total number of devices measured; and give the device-to-device spread in crossover temperature and binding-energy estimates.

5. **Interlayer leakage and AC coupling are not reported, although the interpretation depends on the open-circuit condition.** The channel barrier is 1.5–2 nm of hBN and Vb is about 0.76–0.9 V, so interlayer tunnelling is possible and can produce spurious drag and Hall signals where the layers are resistive; the model assumes none. The whole interpretation of the hole-layer Hall data depends on zero net current in the electron layer. For the Rxy measurement the Mo layer is AC-open but DC-connected through Mo-10 (Extended Data Fig. 2a), and the transformer and potentiometer that suppress AC coupling are used only in the drag configuration. The authors should report the DC and AC interlayer leakage current versus Vb over the Vb–Vg range of Figs. 2–4 (or the drag signal with bias reversed), compared with the AC drive current; state whether the electron layer carries AC current through capacitive coupling to ground at 7.33 Hz given resistances up to MΩ; report the out-of-phase lock-in components in the trion regions, where RC phase shifts may matter; and show that none of these affect the sign of Rxy.

6. **Excitation level, heating and measurement parameters are not given.** A 1 mV r.m.s. excitation is used. When the sample resistance dominates the circuit, up to ~1 meV can drop across the channel, comparable to the trion binding energy (0.7–1.2 meV) and to k_BT at 1.5 K, so electron heating or field-induced dissociation could affect both the magnitude of the effect and the extracted crossover temperature. The Methods should state the actual current through the hole layer in the open-circuit Hall configuration and the voltage across the channel, show that Rxy and Rxy,drag in the trion regions do not depend on excitation amplitude, and give the input-impedance and filtering limits of the voltage measurement when Rxx reaches 10^5–10^7 Ω (Fig. 2a colour scale).
Raised by: r1, r2

7. **Landau quantization at the measurement field is not accounted for in the Hall analysis.** All Hall data are at 4 T, where the free-hole cyclotron energy (about 0.8 meV, p. 5) is comparable to the trion binding energy and Rxx already oscillates in the trion and pi regions (Extended Data Figs. 3b, 4, 7). Yet the analysis uses classical small-field formulas: the model is linearized "under a small perpendicular magnetic field" (equation (6)), and n_H = B/(e Rxy) in Fig. 4f. Kinks in n_H near the X + X− boundary at 1.5 K may come from quantum oscillations rather than trion physics. The authors should show Rxy versus B inside each trion region at fixed (Vg, Vb) from low field, check that the sign of the slope persists at 1–2 T where oscillations are weak, and say whether ω_c τ is small as the linearization assumes.
Raised by: r2, r3

8. **The two temperature scales in the X + X− region are unexplained, and the crossover temperature is undefined.** Fig. 4e shows Rxy turning negative only below ~4 K at the chosen Vg, whereas the drag Hall crossover is quoted as ~8 K and converted to the binding energy. The authors should explain why the hole-layer Hall sign changes at about half the trion formation scale, for example through competition between σ_h (neglected in equation (7)) and σ_T, and discuss whether this changes the binding-energy estimate. They should also define how the crossover temperature in Fig. 4c,d is extracted (sign change of Rxy, or a fixed fraction of the low-T value) and plot it against the estimated trion density, since the text says it "decreases continuously with increasing trion density".

9. **The trion binding-energy estimates differ and use inconsistent inputs.** Three values are given: about 0.7 meV from the 8 K Hall crossover (p. 4), about 1.2 meV from the valley-injection threshold (Methods, said to be in "good agreement"), and "the scale of millielectronvolts" (introduction). The Methods estimate uses 25 mV in Vb, g_s g_v = 2 and m_h ≈ 0.45 m_0 in C_Q, whereas Extended Data Fig. 7d obtains a hole cyclotron mass of 0.32 m_e in the pi region of the same type of device. Thermal dissociation of a bound state in a fluid normally occurs at k_BT well below E_b because of entropy, so the crossover temperature gives a lower bound rather than an estimate. The authors should reconcile the mass used in C_Q, give uncertainties for each E_T,b estimate, present all routes (Rxy,drag crossover, Rxy crossover, injection threshold) together, and show that the Zeeman criterion for trion instability (around 6 T for the disappearance of negative Rxy in Extended Data Fig. 4e, and 8–9 T in Extended Data Figs. 4 and 8) is quantitatively consistent with the quoted E_T,b and a stated valley g-factor.

10. **The gate calibration and trion-region boundaries have no stated uncertainty.** The X + X± regions are drawn as the metal–insulator crossover in Rxx (blue dashed lines), with lower edges p = 2n and n = 2p from gate and interlayer capacitances, and Fig. 4f compares n_H directly with the "total" electron and hole densities from this calibration. Only the interlayer capacitance (C_2L ≈ 1.55 μF cm^−2) is given. The authors should list the gate dielectric thicknesses and capacitances, how they were determined and their uncertainty, say whether quantum capacitance and the doping offset of the pn threshold (yellow line) are included in the dashed lines, and state how the boundaries were drawn. The proposed cause of the field-induced expansion of the trion regions (binding energy enhanced by magnetic confinement) is untested and should be labelled tentative or supported, for example by tracking the RC threshold shift with B (Extended Data Fig. 8).

11. **The quantum-oscillation mass analysis is fragile.** In the X + X+ region the Lifshitz–Kosevich fit (Extended Data Fig. 7d) uses amplitudes normalized at 1.5 K over only about 1.5–3 K, with the reference trace at 3.3 K, and neighbouring oscillations overlap the trion boundary. The LK form assumes independent quasiparticle Landau levels, which is exactly what the authors argue these oscillations are not, since they propose recurring transitions between hole quantum Hall states and a trion phase, an explanation that is itself speculative. The authors should give fit uncertainties and fits with different reference temperatures, or soften the quoted 0.65 m_e to a qualitative statement and soften the Conclusion wording; add field-dependence evidence (period in 1/B and its relation to the hole density); and explain where the "expected trion mass ≈ 1.4–1.6 m_e" comes from.
