Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports Hall and Hall-drag measurements on Coulomb-coupled MoSe2/WSe2 electron-hole double layers. It interprets the signals as a Hall effect of interlayer trions, meaning charged composite particles moving under a Lorentz force. Earlier work from these and other groups established equilibrium trion fluids (refs. 36, 37). Finding a transport signature unique to their net charge would be a natural and interesting next step, and an electron-like Hall response in a hole-only layer is a striking observation. The novelty depends on whether the Hall signals actually require a Lorentz force on trions, rather than following generically from interlayer bound states plus free carriers.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The data set is rich and combines several probes on dual-gated double layers: Rxx, Rxx(drag), Rxy, Rxy(drag), and valley-resolved reflection contrast as functions of Vg, Vb, B and T.
- Valley-resolved RC (Fig. 2b,c; Extended Data Fig. 3) shows a lowered injection threshold for the minority valley inside the (X+X+) and (X+X−) regions. Its B and T evolution (Extended Data Figs. 4, 5) gives independent, spectroscopic evidence for spin-singlet interlayer trions.
- Rxy(drag) is large only inside the trion regions and has opposite signs on the positive and negative sides (Fig. 3b, Fig. 4c,d). Rxy in the hole layer turns negative below about 4 K on the negative-trion side (Fig. 3a, Fig. 4a,b). Both are clear, reproducible-looking features that switch off with temperature (about 8 K), with doping (|Vg| ≳ 0.06 V) and with field (above about 6 T), consistent with the optical phase diagram.
- A conductivity-tensor framework (Methods, Eqs. 1–5) is given for interpreting the multi-component transport.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The Hall signatures, including the "unambiguous" electron-Hall effect, follow from the authors' own model without any trion Lorentz force. The main text (p. 6) states that the negative Rxy in the hole layer "unambiguously demonstrates the presence of a trion Hall effect and trion drags". The Methods expression, however, is Rxy ≈ −(B/e)[(1+f)/n_e + f²(1−σX0/σe0)/n_T]. Its leading term, −(B/e)(1+f)/n_e, is negative even for f → 0. A short calculation with Eq. (1) and only excitons plus free electrons (σT = 0, open-circuit Mo-layer) makes the point. The hole-layer response is the series combination of σX and σe, so Rxy = −B/(n_e e). Perfect exciton drag alone therefore produces an electron-Hall effect in the hole layer, set by the free electrons. Similarly, on the positive side the paper's result Rxy ≈ B/(n_h e) contains no trion property at all. A finite Rxy(drag) also arises from excitons plus free holes: driven excitons drag the bound holes, the free holes counterflow, and that counterflow produces a Hall field. As presented, neither the sign of Rxy nor the existence of Rxy(drag) distinguishes "a Lorentz force on trions" (the claim in the abstract and Fig. 1c) from "neutral excitons perfectly dragging plus free carriers in a magnetic field". This matters for the title claim. The authors should:
   - derive Rxy(drag) from Eqs. (1)–(5), which the Methods do not give although it is the most direct trion probe;
   - compute Rxy, Rxy(drag), Rxx and Rxx(drag) for the exciton-plus-free-carrier model and for the trion model;
   - identify an observable that discriminates between them, such as the magnitude and Vg dependence of Rxy(drag)/Rxx(drag), the value of n_H relative to p − n versus the expectation with trions, or the relation between Rxy and Rxy(drag);
   - fit the data in Fig. 3d and Fig. 4b,d with both models.

   If no such discrimination is possible, the claim should be reframed as a Hall response of a bound-state (exciton/trion) fluid, and "unambiguously" removed.

2. The Hall data are not shown to be antisymmetric in B, and admixture of the very large longitudinal signals is not excluded. Rxx in the trion regions is 10^4–10^6 Ω (Fig. 1e; Extended Data Fig. 7c), and Rxx(drag) reaches 10^4–10^6 Ω (Fig. 2a). The reported Hall signals are about 1–15 kΩ (Figs. 3, 4). A contact misalignment of only 1% would therefore give a spurious "Hall" signal of the same size. The Methods describe no field-reversal antisymmetrization, and Fig. 3c covers only B ≥ 0. Several features look like possible admixture:
   - The most negative Rxy values sit right next to the grey "completely insulating" mask, where Rxx diverges (Fig. 3a; Fig. 4a below about 3 K).
   - In Fig. 3a and Extended Data Fig. 6 the boundary of the masked region carries alternating red/blue speckle.
   - At 10 K both Rxy(drag) line cuts in Fig. 4d settle at the same finite negative value, about −0.2 to −0.3 kΩ, for positive and negative trions. This common offset is not expected for a pure Hall-drag signal.

   The authors should:
   - show Rxy and Rxy(drag) measured at ±B and antisymmetrized, at least for the line cut in Fig. 3a and the temperature sweeps in Fig. 4;
   - show the symmetric part next to Rxx and Rxx(drag);
   - for the drag, check Onsager reciprocity by swapping drive and sense layers, Rxy(drag)(B) versus R_yx(drag)(−B).

   The electron-Hall claim in particular should be shown to survive antisymmetrization.

B. Should address

3. The free-carrier densities in Fig. 3d are not compared quantitatively with any scenario. In the positive region, n_H measures the free holes. With gate-determined densities (dashed lines in Fig. 3d), the expected free-hole density is p − n if every electron is bound in an exciton, and smaller if trions (two holes per electron) form. Fig. 3d has p ≈ 1.5–1.8 × 10^12 cm⁻² and n ≈ 1.1–1.5 × 10^12 cm⁻² across the positive-trion window, so the two scenarios predict clearly different n_H. Plotting p − n and the trion-based expectation on Fig. 3d, with the trion fraction taken from RC if possible, would turn a qualitative statement ("decreases monotonically towards zero") into a test of trion formation. Currently n_H falls to zero at p ≈ n, which as stated is also what pure exciton binding predicts. The 10 K curve shows an "anomaly" near Vg = 0 that reaches about 2.9 × 10^12 cm⁻², roughly double the expected hole density. This is not "weak" and should be explained or measured with field reversal.

4. The Hall results come from a single device. Rxy and Rxy(drag) (Figs. 3, 4; Extended Data Figs. 6, 8) are shown only for device 2, Rxx maps for device 1, and optics for device 3. The paper should show Rxy (ideally also Rxy(drag)) for at least one further device, in particular the electron-Hall sign reversal in the hole layer, and say how many devices were measured in total. The phase-diagram coordinates also differ between devices: the pn onset is at Vb ≈ 0.73 V in device 1 (Fig. 1d,e) and about 0.84–0.86 V in devices 2 and 3 (Figs. 2, 3). The authors should explain how the dashed "Fig. 1f" boundaries were placed in each device and in the RC maps. They should also say whether those boundaries are fitted to each data set or derived independently from electrostatics.

5. The two trion-binding-energy estimates rest on approximate conversions and are not obviously consistent. The 1.2 meV estimate (Methods) uses C_Q with g_v = 2. The threshold in question, however, is for a single valley (the K-valley of the W-layer), so g_v = 1 seems more appropriate and would roughly double E_T,b to about 2.4 meV. The conversion also ignores the gate capacitances and the asymmetric hBN thicknesses, which enter the lever arm from Vb to the chemical potential. Separately, "≈ 8 K (or 0.7 meV)" equates a crossover temperature with the binding energy. For ionization equilibrium (Saha-type), the binding energy is typically several times k_B T*. The authors should:
   - give uncertainties for both estimates;
   - justify the degeneracy and lever-arm choices;
   - fit the T dependence (for example Rxy(drag)(T) in Fig. 4d, or the trion fraction) with an ionization model before claiming "good agreement" with each other and with the Zeeman scale.

   The text should also give the Zeeman energy behind the statement that trions are destabilized above about 6 T, including the g-factors used.

6. Interlayer leakage, AC coupling and the masking criteria are not documented. Drag and single-layer measurements are taken at Vb ≈ 0.85–0.90 V across a 1.5–2 nm hBN barrier, with a 1 mV, 7.33 Hz excitation into channels of 10^5–10^7 Ω, read through 100 MΩ preamplifiers. The paper should:
   - show the DC interlayer leakage current versus Vb in the relevant windows;
   - report the out-of-phase lock-in components;
   - show that the signals are linear in excitation amplitude in the trion regions;
   - state the quantitative criterion behind the grey "completely insulating" mask in Figs. 1, 3, 4 and Extended Data Fig. 6.

   This matters because the strongest Hall signals lie at the edges of these masks.

7. The field dependence does not test Lorentz-force scaling. A Hall effect of charged trions should give Rxy(drag) odd in B and, at low field, roughly linear in B at fixed trion density. Fig. 3c shows Rxy only along one Vb, where the trion region itself appears and disappears with B. At B = 0 there is already a narrow trion region along p = 2n (Fig. 1d). Measuring Rxy and Rxy(drag) versus B (including negative B) at low field along the p = 2n line, or at a fixed point inside a trion region, would separate the Hall response of a fixed trion fluid from the B-induced change in phase composition.

8. The spectroscopic and transport temperatures and samples differ. The optics (device 3, Attodry, 1.6 K, white-light intensity up to 50 nW/μm²) and the transport (devices 1 and 2, TeslatronPT, 1.5 K) are used together to construct one phase diagram and one ionization scale (about 8 K from transport, about 10 K from Extended Data Fig. 5). The paper should either show RC on device 2 or show that the boundary positions and the ionization temperature match across devices. It should also give an estimate of optical heating at the stated intensity.

9. The quantum-oscillation statements in the Conclusion and Extended Data Fig. 8 go beyond the data. The claims that the vertical stripes "demonstrate the survival of exciton binding upon hole-doping" and reflect "recurring transitions between bound trions and quantum Hall states" rest on a qualitative stripe orientation and two coincident dips (Extended Data Fig. 8b). These should be supported, with stripe slopes compared against expected Landau-level filling lines for free holes (density p − n versus p) and their B dependence, or presented as tentative.

C. Minor

10. Extended Data Fig. 7c: the y-axis is labelled "T (K)" but shows Rxx (Ω).

11. Extended Data Fig. 1 caption and Methods disagree on the outline colours: the caption has top gate black dashed, bottom gate red and exciton-contact hBN pink, while the Methods say black/red solid outline the WSe2/MoSe2 layers and dashed lines the gates. Please reconcile them.

12. Methods, optical measurements: "50nW/μm^-2" should read nW/μm². "To further simply the expressions" should read "simplify". Define σe0 in the Rxy expression for electron trions.

13. Fig. 2a and Extended Data Fig. 3a: the inset is labelled "Rxx" although the plotted quantity is Rxx(drag).

14. Methods, Eq. (1): the drag conductivity σD between free carriers is dropped as "negligible at low temperatures". The same model is then used to interpret the 10 K data (Fig. 3d, Fig. 4), where free carriers dominate. Please state the regime of validity. Also state whether the exciton conductivity is taken to have no Hall component for dipolar excitons in a field.

15. Fig. 3d: the 1.5 K curve on the negative side never approaches the orange dashed (electron-density) line before diverging near Vg ≈ 0.06 V. A sentence explaining what n_H should be compared with there would help the reader.

16. The abstract and main text describe trions as "positively charged (for p = 2n)", but the Hall data are taken at p/n ≈ 1–1.6 (Fig. 3d dashed lines). The text should make clear that the Hall regions are exciton-trion mixtures rather than pure trion fluids.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a Methods section that derives Rxy(drag) and solves the full 4×4 problem numerically. Fit Fig. 3d and Fig. 4b,d with physically constrained parameters (densities from gates and RC, mobilities from Rxx and Rxx(drag)), and set the trion-plus-exciton model against an exciton-only model.
- Present field-antisymmetrized Rxy and Rxy(drag) as the primary data in Figs. 3 and 4, with the symmetric component in Extended Data.
- Reproduce the key Hall and Hall-drag signatures in a second device, ideally the same one used for the RC measurements, so the optical trion identification and the Hall signal come from the same sample.
- Fit the temperature dependence with a Saha-type ionization model to extract a single trion binding energy with uncertainty, and compare it with the RC threshold shift and the field at which the trion regions vanish.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
