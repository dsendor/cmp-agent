Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports transport signatures attributed to a Hall effect of interlayer trions in dual-gated MoSe2(bilayer)/hBN/WSe2 electron-hole double layers. Under a perpendicular field (2–6 T), the narrow B = 0 trion line at p = 2n (n = 2p) broadens into two triangular regions. In these regions the authors observe a large Hall-drag resistance whose sign changes between positive and negative trions. They also observe a reduced Hall density in the WSe2 layer and, on the negative-trion side, a negative (electron-like) Hall resistance in the hole layer. All of these vanish above about 8 K or at |Vg| ≳ 0.06 V. Helicity-resolved reflection contrast is used to support the trion assignment. Transport of charged composite particles in an equilibrium Bose-Fermi mixture is a timely topic, and the data set (transport plus valley-resolved optics) is rich. Whether the observations isolate a Lorentz force on trions, as opposed to Hall responses of free carriers transmitted by exciton drag, is the central open question.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- First Hall-type transport study of the recently established equilibrium trion fluid in electron-hole bilayers. It combines single-layer Hall, Hall drag and valley-resolved optics on the same phase diagram.
- Clear, reproducible-looking phenomenology: Rxy(drag) changes sign between the X+X+ and X+X− regions (Fig. 3b). The T-dependence shows a common onset near 8 K in Rxy, Rxy(drag), Rxx and RC (Fig. 4, Extended Data Figs. 5 and 7).
- The striking electron-like Hall response in a hole-doped WSe2 layer (Figs. 3a, 3d, 4b) is a clean qualitative signature of strong interlayer binding.
- The optical data (Fig. 2, Extended Data Figs. 3–5) give an independent, valley-resolved picture of where the bound states form. The threshold shift relative to the non-interacting injection line is a useful binding-energy probe.
- A conductivity-tensor model (Methods) gives a framework for interpreting the signals.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The Hall signals presented as proof of a "trion Hall effect" are not shown to require trions, or a Lorentz force on trions. The authors' own model reproduces them with excitons plus free carriers alone.
   - In Methods, the negative-trion result is Rxy ≈ −(B/e)[(1+f)/n_e + f²(1−σX0/σe0)/n_T] with f = σT0/(σT0+σX0). Setting σT = 0 (f = 0: excitons plus free electrons, no trions) gives Rxy = −B/(n_e e). That is an electron-like Hall response in the hole layer, produced purely by exciton drag of the open-circuit Mo layer.
   - Directly: with J_e = 0 and only excitons, E_h = E_e + σX⁻¹J_h and E_e = σe⁻¹J_h. The W-layer Hall voltage is then the Mo-layer electron Hall voltage. The same reasoning gives a nonzero Hall drag of opposite sign on the two sides of neutrality with excitons plus free holes, or excitons plus free electrons.
   - On the positive side the paper itself states that Rxy ≈ B/(n_h e) is set only by free holes. Neither expression contains the trion cyclotron frequency ω_c,T.
   - So the statements in the abstract and main text that the negative Hall resistance "unambiguously demonstrates the presence of a trion Hall effect and trion drags", and that "the Hall effect arises from a Lorentz force on trions", are not supported by the analysis as presented. The observed signs and the drop of n_H toward zero are what a doped excitonic insulator with perfect exciton drag would also give.
   - A clear discriminator is available from the authors' own data and should be shown: the Hall density in the X+X+ region compared quantitatively with p − 2n (holes bound in trions) versus p − n (holes bound in excitons only). Use the gate-calibrated densities already in Fig. 3d along the Vb = 0.88 V cut, and do the same for n_e against n − 2p on the X+X− side.
   - The authors should also fit Fig. 3d (and Fig. 4b,d) with the full tensor model, with and without trions. They should say which measured quantities depend on ω_c,T τ_T and show that the data constrain it.
   - Without this, the title claim should be narrowed to Hall and Hall-drag signatures of bound states in the doped excitonic insulator, consistent with trion formation.

2. Hall resistances in strongly insulating regions are not shown to be odd in B, so longitudinal admixture is not excluded.
   - The large Rxy and Rxy(drag) features (≈5–15 kΩ, Figs. 3a–c, 4, Extended Data Fig. 6) sit exactly where Rxx and Rxx(drag) are 10⁴–10⁶ Ω (Figs. 1e, 2a; Extended Data Figs. 7, 8). They lie next to grey regions where the signals cannot be measured.
   - At these resistances, a contact misalignment of a few percent in the Hall bar (Extended Data Fig. 1 shows irregular, etched geometries) mixes kΩ-scale Rxx into Rxy.
   - Methods do not say whether Rxy and Rxy(drag) were antisymmetrized with respect to B, and no −B data are shown.
   - The sign change across Vg = 0 argues against pure Rxx admixture, but it does not establish the magnitude of either signal. The negative Rxy on the X+X− side (≈ −3 to −5 kΩ, Fig. 4b), and hence the quantitative n_H in Fig. 3d, depend on it directly.
   - The authors should show Rxy and Rxy(drag) at ±B (at least the Vb = 0.88 V cut and the Fig. 4b,d line cuts) and report antisymmetrized values.
   - For the drag channel, they should also check Onsager reciprocity by swapping drive and sense layers, as far as the contacts allow.
   - It would also help to show Rxy versus B along a cut that stays inside a trion region (for example near the p = 2n line), to test linearity in B. The cut in Fig. 3c crosses into and out of the trion region with B, which mixes field dependence with phase-boundary motion.

B. Should address

3. The key Hall data come from one device, and the phase assignment is transferred across devices.
   - All Rxy and Rxy(drag) data (Figs. 3, 4, Extended Data Figs. 6, 8) are from device 2. The Rxx maps (Fig. 1, Extended Data Fig. 7) are from device 1, and the optics (Fig. 2, Extended Data Figs. 3–5) from device 3.
   - The pn threshold sits at Vb ≈ 0.73 V in device 1, ≈ 0.86 V in device 2 and ≈ 0.84 V in device 3. Yet the trion triangles are drawn "corresponding to those in Fig. 1f" in all of them.
   - The authors should show the Hall and Hall-drag sign change, including the negative Rxy, in at least one more device; device 1 has Hall contacts.
   - They should explain how the phase boundaries (blue dashed triangles) were placed in each device. Ideally, optics and transport should come from the same device, or the paper should show that the triangle positions agree after normalizing to the pn threshold.

4. The field dependence of the trion region is not explained.
   - At B = 0 trions occupy only a narrow line at p = 2n (Fig. 1d). At 2–6 T this broadens into wide triangles before vanishing above 6 T (Figs. 1e, 3c; Extended Data Figs. 4, 6).
   - The text explains the disappearance (valley Zeeman energy exceeding the trion binding energy). It does not explain why a field first enlarges the stability region of spin-singlet trions, which the Zeeman energy should disfavor.
   - Because the field window is central to where the "trion Hall effect" is seen, a quantitative electrostatic or thermodynamic argument for the shape and B-evolution of the triangles is needed. It should use the valley-split bands of Fig. 2d and the binding energy.
   - It would also help to show the B-dependence of the threshold shift in Extended Data Fig. 4.

5. The trion binding energy is estimated three ways with inconsistent results, and the assumptions are not justified.
   - The Methods give E_T,b ≈ 1.2 meV from a 25 mV threshold shift at 6 T. The main text gives ≈ 0.7 meV from an 8 K crossover, and it invokes a Zeeman scale.
   - The conversion uses only the WSe2 quantum capacitance with g_s g_v = 2. At 6 T with valley Zeeman splitting, the relevant degeneracy and the MoSe2 quantum capacitance are not discussed. It also uses ε_r = 3.5 and d = 2 nm, while Methods give d = 1.5–2 nm, a roughly 30% spread.
   - Equating a crossover temperature with the binding energy ignores the entropy of dissociation, which typically puts kT_crossover well below E_b.
   - The authors should give uncertainties, state which number they consider reliable, and check consistency with the valley Zeeman energy at the field where the triangles vanish (≈ 6 T).

6. The conductivity-tensor model is incomplete as presented.
   - Eqs. (1)–(5) are written down, but the solution is only quoted. The free-carrier drag σD is set to zero, and in the X+X− region the expression depends on unknown ratios f and σX0/σe0.
   - The main text describes f as "the ratio of exciton to trion conductivity", but Methods define f = σT0/(σT0+σX0).
   - The authors should give the full inversion, at least in a supplement. They should state which terms survive at the measured ω_c τ, and use the measured Rxx, Rxx(drag), drag ratio (Extended Data Fig. 8) and Rxy(drag) to constrain f and test the model quantitatively. This ties into concern 1.

7. Linear response, heating and interlayer leakage are not documented.
   - The excitation is a 1 mV AC voltage. Where the sample resistance dominates, most of this drops across the channel, an energy scale comparable to the ≈ 0.7–1.2 meV binding energy and well above kT at 1.5 K.
   - The paper does not show that Rxy, Rxy(drag) and the negative Hall response are independent of excitation amplitude, or that the effective electron temperature is 1.5 K.
   - For the drag data, the interlayer DC leakage current at the relevant Vb (0.84–0.92 V across 1.5–2 nm hBN) and the residual AC coupling after potentiometer balancing should be reported. In high-impedance regions, small leakage or capacitive pickup at 7.33 Hz can mimic drag.
   - The authors should also state the lock-in phase criteria, given the 100 MΩ preamplifier input impedance and MΩ-scale sample resistances.

C. Minor

8. Cyclotron energy: with m_h ≈ 0.45 m0 (Methods), ħω_c at 4 T is ≈ 1.0 meV, not ≈ 0.8 meV as stated in the Conclusion. Please reconcile, or state the mass used.

9. The Fig. 4 caption says a sign change in both Rxy and Rxy(drag) around Vg ≈ 0 is observed for T ≲ 8 K. However, Fig. 4b shows the n-type Rxy crossing zero only at ≈ 3.7 K, consistent with the main text ("T ≲ 4K"). Please correct the caption.

10. Fig. 4d: the n-type Rxy(drag) remains at about −0.2 to −0.3 kΩ up to 10 K, while the text says it is "negligible at T ≳ 8K". Please comment on this residual, which may be free-carrier drag or an offset.

11. The criterion for the grey "unmeasurable" regions in Figs. 1–4 (Rxx threshold, lock-in phase, noise) should be stated. The negative-Rxy region on the X+X− side borders these regions and shows speckle near Vb ≈ 0.86 V (Fig. 3a,b).

12. Explain how the gate-calibrated electron and hole densities (dashed lines, Fig. 3d) are computed as functions of Vg at fixed Vb. Near Vg ≈ −0.09 V the 10 K n_H slightly exceeds the total hole density. Please state the calibration uncertainty.

13. Extended Data Fig. 7c: the y-axis is labelled "T (K)" but shows resistance values (10⁴, 10⁵). It should be Rxx (Ω).

14. Methods: "50nW/μm⁻²" should be nW/μm²; "70⁰C" should be 70 °C; "To further simply the expressions" should be "simplify".

15. Extended Data Fig. 1 labels "exciton contact hBN (pink line)", but the text refers to red lines for MoSe2 and the bottom gate. Please make the colour legend consistent between the caption and the Methods text.

16. The statement that the interlayer bound states disappear "due to free carrier screening" at high Vb or Vg is asserted, with references, but not shown here. A short pointer to the relevant data (for example Fig. 2a above Vb ≈ 0.90 V) would help.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a quantitative Hall-density analysis: n_H against p − 2n versus p − n in X+X+, and n_e against n − 2p in X+X−. This would turn the qualitative sign arguments into a direct test of trion versus exciton binding.
- Provide ±B (antisymmetrized) Hall and Hall-drag data, and a drive/sense-reversed drag measurement, for the key cuts.
- Fit the full four-component tensor model to Figs. 3d and 4, using the measured drag ratio and Rxx(drag) as constraints. State explicitly which observable is sensitive to the trion Lorentz force (ω_c,T τ_T).
- Reproduce the Hall and Hall-drag sign change in a second device, and measure optics and transport on the same device, or align them by a stated procedure.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
