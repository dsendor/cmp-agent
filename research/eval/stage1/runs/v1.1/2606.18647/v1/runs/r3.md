Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript maps the phase diagram of a Coulomb-coupled WSe2/hBN/bilayer-MoSe2 electron-hole double layer at finite magnetic field. It finds two triangular regions on either side of the excitonic insulator (EI) where helicity-resolved reflection contrast indicates spin-singlet interlayer trions. In these regions the authors report large Hall drag that changes sign between positive and negative trions, and a negative (electron-like) Hall resistance in the hole-doped WSe2 layer with the MoSe2 layer open-circuited. They interpret both as a "trion Hall effect". If the interpretation holds, this is the first transport signature of a Lorentz force acting on a charged composite in an equilibrium Bose-Fermi mixture, and it builds naturally on the recent observations of equilibrium trion fluids (Refs. 36, 37). The question is whether the Hall data identify trions specifically, rather than bound holes in general.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Transport and helicity-resolved optics are combined. The valley-resolved attractive-polaron maps (Fig. 2c, Extended Data Fig. 3) show both W valleys occupied in the X+X+ region, and a lowered K-valley injection threshold relative to the non-interacting line. This is independent, microscopic support for spin-singlet trion formation.
- The Hall drag R_xy(drag) (Fig. 3b) is large only inside the proposed trion regions and has opposite signs on the two sides of charge neutrality. This is a clean, striking result.
- The electron-like Hall response in a hole layer (Figs. 3a, 3d, 4a,b) is an unusual observation that cannot come from single-particle transport in the W-layer.
- Several independent dependences point the same way: field (Fig. 3c, Extended Data Figs. 4 and 6), temperature (Fig. 4, Extended Data Figs. 5 and 7) and doping. The window of roughly 2-6 T and below about 8 K is consistent across transport and optics.
- The measurement circuits are described carefully (Extended Data Fig. 2), including how interlayer AC coupling is minimized in the drag configuration.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The Hall signatures shown do not single out trions over excitons plus free carriers.** The central claim is that "the negative Hall resistance shows an electron-Hall effect in a hole layer, which unambiguously demonstrates the presence of a trion Hall effect and trion drags" (p. 6).
   - Take the authors' own conductivity-tensor model (Eqs. 1-5) with σ_T = 0: a fluid of neutral excitons plus free electrons in the MoSe2 layer, all holes bound. With the Mo-layer open-circuited (J_e = 0), the W-layer resistivity tensor becomes ρ_h = σ_X⁻¹ + σ_e⁻¹. Its off-diagonal part is the free-electron Hall resistivity, so it is electron-like. In words: perfect exciton drag transfers the electron-layer Hall response into the hole layer.
   - Similarly, with the W-layer open-circuited, exciton drag alone gives a Hall drag with the sign of the free carriers in the driven layer.
   - On the positive side the paper itself says that R_xy ≈ B/(n_h e) measures only free holes, and that the drop in n_H reflects "a loss of free holes to the formation of trions and excitons" (pp. 5-6). Exciton formation alone would produce this.
   - The first term of the negative-side expression, −(B/e)(1+f)/n_e, is itself a free-electron Hall term.
   - So the sign of R_xy and of R_xy(drag) identifies the sign of the excess free carrier once the minority carriers are bound. It does not by itself show that a Lorentz force acts on a bound three-particle complex. The assignment to trions currently rests on the optics (Fig. 2c, from device 3) and on earlier work, not on the Hall data.

   To support the title claim, the authors should do the following:
   - (a) Make the comparison quantitative. Use the gate-calibrated n and p (the dashed lines in Fig. 3d) to compute the n_H(V_g) expected from (i) an exciton + free-carrier mixture, n_H = p − n on the electron-rich side, and (ii) the trion + exciton + free-carrier model. Show which one the 1.5 K data in Fig. 3d follow.
     - For example, at V_g ≈ 0.03 V the measured |n_H| is about 1×10¹² cm⁻². The gate-defined imbalance n − p appears to be considerably smaller. Explaining this mismatch quantitatively would be the discriminating test.
   - (b) Use the drag Eq. 5 to predict R_xy(drag) in both scenarios and compare with Fig. 3b and Fig. 4d.
   - (c) Explain why the Hall drag is confined to the triangles if excitons plus free carriers would give a similar signal, for example by showing that R_xx(drag)/R_xx (the drag ratio) is much lower outside them.
   - If the Hall data cannot discriminate, the claim should be rephrased: a Hall response of a bound-state (trion/exciton) fluid, with trions identified optically.

2. **Hall data are not shown to be antisymmetrized in B, and the measurements are made at very high impedance.** In the trion regions R_xx is 10⁵-10⁶ Ω (Fig. 1e, Extended Data Fig. 7), and higher on the X+X− side (p. 4). R_xx(drag) reaches about 10⁶ Ω (Fig. 2a). The Hall signals of interest are a few kΩ: −3 to −4 kΩ in Fig. 4b and −3 kΩ in Fig. 4d.
   - A longitudinal pickup of a fraction of a percent to a few percent, from contact misalignment or inhomogeneity, would match these magnitudes.
   - The negative-R_xy region in Fig. 3a sits directly against the grey, masked "completely insulating" region, where the map is visibly noisy.
   - Fig. 3c covers only B ≥ 0. The Methods do not mention symmetrization of R_xx or antisymmetrization of R_xy and R_xy(drag).
   - At 7.33 Hz, with source impedances near the 100 MΩ input impedance of the preamplifiers and cable capacitance, phase shifts and loading are also possible.

   The authors should do the following:
   - Show R_xy and R_xy(drag) antisymmetrized between +B and −B. Valley Zeeman physics is symmetric under B → −B with K ↔ K′.
   - Report the quadrature (out-of-phase) component and the two-terminal contact resistances in the trion regions.
   - State how the grey regions were defined.
   - Ideally, confirm Onsager reciprocity for the drag by interchanging the drive and drag layers.

   The near-zero R_xy at low B in Fig. 3c is partly reassuring, but it does not cover the 4 T regime, where R_xx is largest.

B. Should address

3. **Reproducibility across devices.** The results are split across devices:
   - The Hall and Hall-drag results (Figs. 3, 4, Extended Data Figs. 6, 8) come from device 2.
   - The R_xx maps (Fig. 1, Extended Data Fig. 7) come from device 1.
   - The optics and R_xx(drag) at 6 T (Fig. 2, Extended Data Fig. 3) come from device 3. Device 3 is not shown in Extended Data Fig. 1.
   - The device used for Extended Data Figs. 4 and 5 is not stated.

   The negative R_xy in the W-layer and the sign-changing Hall drag therefore appear in only one device. The authors should show the Hall and Hall-drag maps for at least one other device (device 1 has Hall-bar contacts 1-5), or state plainly that the result comes from a single device.

4. **The trion-region boundaries in the transport devices are transplanted, not measured.** The dashed triangles in Figs. 3a,b and 4 and Extended Data Fig. 6 are said to "correspond to those in Fig. 1f", which is a schematic. The optical identification of valley occupation was done on device 3, which has a different V_b scale. The authors should explain how the triangles were placed for device 2 (for example, from R_xx or R_xx(drag) features). Alternatively, they could provide reflection-contrast data on device 2, so that the "inside versus outside the trion region" comparison central to Fig. 3 rests on measured boundaries.

5. **The model in the Methods is incompletely specified and not used quantitatively.**
   - σ_e0 appears in the final R_xy expression but is never defined.
   - The trion and electron tensors in Eq. 3 use the same sign of ω_c for positive and negative charges, so the sign convention for the cyclotron frequencies is unclear.
   - The R_xy expression diverges as n_T → 0, which obscures its relation to the exciton-only limit.
   - The free-carrier frictional drag σ_D is neglected as "negligible at low temperatures". At 4 T, Landau quantization of the free carriers is evident (Extended Data Fig. 8), and Hall drag between Landau-quantized layers can be substantial.

   The authors should do the following:
   - Define all quantities and the sign conventions.
   - Give the full solution of Eqs. 4-5, including the R_xy(drag) predictions.
   - Justify neglecting σ_D at 4 T, for example from the small R_xy(drag) measured outside the trion regions at the same field.
   - Show the model evaluated with plausible parameters against Figs. 3d and 4b,d (see concern 1).

6. **The trion binding energy estimates are loosely connected.**
   - The conversion of the 25 mV threshold shift to E_T,b ≈ 1.2 meV uses only the WSe2 hole quantum capacitance, with g_v = 2. The bilayer-MoSe2 electron quantum capacitance is left out, and so is the fact that the injected holes enter a single (K) valley.
   - It also uses the value at 6 T, where the region is about to vanish.
   - The transport crossover of about 8 K is equated to 0.7 meV. For a binding energy, k_BT and E_b typically differ by an entropy factor.
   - The two values (1.2 and 0.7 meV) are called "in good agreement". So is the valley-Zeeman scale at 6 T, whose g-factor is not given.

   The authors should do the following:
   - State the g-factor and the Zeeman energy at 6 T.
   - Give uncertainties.
   - Derive the threshold shift as a function of B from Extended Data Fig. 4. The prediction is that E_T,b(B) decreases linearly with the Zeeman energy until it closes near 6 T.

7. **There are residual signals at 10 K that the text calls negligible or "weak".**
   - In Fig. 3d, the 10 K n_H has a peak near V_g ≈ 0.005 V of about 2.9×10¹² cm⁻², about twice the total gate-defined hole density. This means R_xy is well below the free-hole value, so some electron-like or drag contribution remains.
   - In Fig. 4d, R_xy(drag) on the n-type side stays at about −0.3 kΩ at 10 K.

   The statement that trions are fully ionized above about 8 K, and that the 10 K curve "follows the expected dependence", should be qualified. These residuals may instead reflect exciton drag persisting above the trion ionization scale, which bears on concern 1.

8. **Optical and transport conditions differ.** The optics use broadband illumination of up to 50 nW/µm² on highly insulating regions with resistive contacts. Photodoping or photo-induced shifts of the gate response could move the thresholds that define the trion regions and the 25 mV shift. The transport-optics comparison is also made across different cryostats (1.5 K and 1.6 K) and different devices. The authors should check that the thresholds in Fig. 2c do not depend on illumination power, or compare them with transport-derived thresholds on the same device.

C. Minor

9. The interpretation of the quantum oscillations in the conclusion ("vertical stripes demonstrate the survival of exciton binding") is asserted rather than analysed. Either support it with a fan diagram or Landau-level indexing, or soften it.
10. Extended Data Fig. 7c: the y-axis is labelled "T (K)" but should be R_xx (Ω).
11. Methods: "50nW/µm⁻²" should be nW/µm². Also "70⁰C" should be 70 °C, and "To further simply" should be "To further simplify".
12. The text gives the RC line cut at V_g ≈ −0.06 V. The blue dashed line in Fig. 2a appears to lie near −0.08 V. Please reconcile.
13. Extended Data Fig. 8 defines a drag ratio from the drag current, with a closed-loop inset. The Methods describe drag only as a voltage measurement with the W-layer open-circuited. Please describe the current-drag configuration.
14. Fig. 3d: the vertical blue line near V_g ≈ 0.06 V is the divergence where R_xy crosses zero. It should be masked or explained in the caption.
15. Notation: σ_+/σ_− in the captions versus σ⁺/σ⁻ in the text. Also X_W(Mo) versus X_W,a in Fig. 2b.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a figure that overlays the measured n_H(V_g) at 1.5 K with the predictions of the exciton + free-carrier model and the trion model, both computed from gate-calibrated densities. This is the single most persuasive addition for the "trion" part of the claim.
- Present the Hall data antisymmetrized in B, with ±B maps in the Extended Data.
- Show Hall and Hall-drag data from a second device, and a reflection-contrast map on the transport device used for Fig. 3.
- Track the trion window and the threshold shift as functions of B and T together (Extended Data Figs. 4 and 5 versus Fig. 3c and Fig. 4). Use this to extract a single, consistent binding-energy and Zeeman-energy picture with uncertainties.

5. Verdict: major revision
