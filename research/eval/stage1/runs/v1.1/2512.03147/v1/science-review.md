A. Must address

1. The assignment of the SdH frequencies to stacking-specific graphene Fermi pockets is not supported, and the agreement with DFT depends on a fitted Fermi-level shift (Electronic properties, p. 5-6; Fig. 2g,h; Fig. 5; Conclusions). This comparison is the paper's main physics link between experiment and theory. Without adjustment the computed pockets match poorly: stage-2-like AB|AB gives 131 and 444 T against a single measured β ≈ 170 T, and the stage-4-like stackings give 70/296 T (ABAB|ABAB) or 28/311/330 T against measured α = 91 T and β ≈ 176 T. The predicted 444 T and ~300 T pockets are absent from the FFTs, although they should be resolvable in a 4-9 T window. Agreement is then sought with a rigid Fermi-level shift of about 0.1 eV, applied independently per structure and only to the stage-4 geometries; its sign (more or less hole doping) is not stated, and no shifted stage-2 values are given. Even after the shift, 27/183 T (ABAB|ABAB) and 126/214 T (ABCA|ABCA) do not reproduce the measured 91/176 T pair. With one free energy shift and two or three pockets per structure, frequency matching is not a discriminating test; the statement that the "inner hole pocket" can be pushed below E_F to explain the missing α in stage 2 shows how flexible the comparison is. Calling the absence of α in S2 "sample-dependent" is circular, because the theory assigns S2 and S4 to different structures. Most strikingly, the dominant β is essentially the same in both stages (170 vs 176 T, within the ~7 T FFT resolution), whereas DFT predicts clearly different pocket sets for the two stagings. This points to a common origin (stage-2 domains within the S4 crystal, the graphene layers bounding a RuCl3 gallery at fixed doping, a surface/bounding-layer contribution, a common impurity phase, or Cl-doped graphite), which the paper does not discuss. The authors should report frequencies for all stackings without a free shift, or apply one stated shift uniformly to both stages and justify it; explain explicitly how a stage-independent β fits a stacking-dependent interpretation; and, unless the frequencies can be tied to a specific stacking, restate the conclusions ("consistent with the experimental SdH frequencies", "the quantum oscillation response is governed by the stacking sequence") as qualitative evidence of heavy hole doping of graphene.

2. The DFT level of theory is not adequate for the charge-transfer magnitude on which the Fermi-pocket comparison depends (Methods p. 7; captions of Figs. 4, 5, S3, S5). All calculations are non-spin-polarised GGA without Hubbard U or spin-orbit coupling. At this level α-RuCl3 is metallic: Fig. S5b,c shows Ru t2g bands crossing E_F, which contradicts the main-text statement that α-RuCl3 remains "rather insulating" (p. 5). The graphene-to-RuCl3 charge transfer, and hence the graphene Fermi-level shift (0.48-0.68 eV) and every pocket area, is then set by pinning to a spurious metallic Ru band rather than by the alignment of the Mott gap/upper Hubbard band with the graphene Dirac point, which depends strongly on U, SOC and magnetic order. This is why the earlier graphene/α-RuCl3 work the authors cite (refs. 12, 13, 35, some from the same group) used GGA+U+SOC with magnetic order. Since the paper itself argues that 0.1 eV shifts decide the agreement with experiment, the method-dependent uncertainty in E_F must be quantified. In addition, the Fermi surfaces in Fig. 5 are projected onto graphene only, so any Ru-derived Fermi sheets, which would themselves produce SdH frequencies, are silently dropped. The authors should repeat at least the A|A or AB|AB and one stage-4 structure in GGA+U(+SOC) with zigzag or ferromagnetic order, at the experimental interlayer spacings; report the charge transfer per graphene layer, the Dirac-point shift and the resulting frequencies with no free shift; and show the full Fermi surface, stating whether RuCl3-derived bands contribute sheets.

3. The SdH oscillations are not experimentally tied to quasi-2D graphene pockets of the intended intercalated phase (Fig. 2, p. 3; Methods). The samples are "highly oriented polycrystalline" with residual graphite (Fig. S1), mixed staging appears at longer reaction times, the stage-4 pattern shows strong graphite reflections, and cutting is said to damage samples; yet the interpretation rests on one field orientation, one temperature and, apparently, one sample per stage. The Cl2-annealed graphite control is shown only as ρ(T) in Fig. 2b, and that curve differs from pristine Kish graphite, so Cl/Cl2 doping of graphite without RuCl3 is a live alternative origin for β. The authors should provide: (a) temperature-dependent SdH amplitudes with Lifshitz-Kosevich effective masses for α, β and the 359 T feature, to compare with the DFT bands and with m* ≈ E_F/v_F² expected for doped Dirac/multilayer bands (a discriminating test, since graphene-like pockets at E_F ≈ 0.5 eV predict specific masses); (b) the field-angle dependence F(θ), to show 2D cylinders (F ∝ 1/cos θ) as assumed in the k_z-independent Onsager analysis and to test for warping from coupling through RuCl3, which bears on the "three-dimensional platform" framing; (c) the Hall data the Methods say were measured and antisymmetrised, with sign and carrier density compared to the Onsager pocket areas and Bader charge transfer (for four-fold degenerate Dirac pockets, 170 T corresponds to about 1.6×10^13 cm^-2); (d) SdH and Hall data on the Cl2-annealed control under the same conditions; (e) (00l) XRD of the same pieces used for transport, or at least the same batch; and (f) the number of crystals grown and measured per stage, whether α and β reproduce across growths and the spread. The "sample-dependent" absence of α needs at least two S4 samples, with and without α, to be credible.

4. The intercalant's identity, the stage assignment and the stacking sequence are inferred rather than measured, so the claim that the crystal structure was "determined" is not supported (Structure analysis p. 2-3; Methods p. 7, assumptions 1-4; Table I; Fig. S1). Only (00l) reflections from powder and (hk0) reflections from single crystals are indexed, and Fig. S1 states that l ≠ 0 reflections cannot be observed in this geometry. The graphene registries (A/B/C) and the stacking across the RuCl3 layer (A|AB|B; AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB) are therefore taken from analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 GICs. Specific problems: (a) the Table I stage-4 sequences as written contain two RuCl3 layers and eight graphene layers, which with the paper's spacings (~3.35-3.54 Å, gallery ~9-10 Å) would give about 39 Å, whereas c = 58.2 Å corresponds to three stage-4 repeats of ~19.4 Å (stage 2, c = 25.4 Å = 2 × 12.7 Å, is consistent with A|AB|B); the authors should state how many intercalant and graphene layers each cell contains and show that it reproduces c, and report the gallery height d_s obtained from c and compare it with other metal-trichloride GICs. (b) Assumption 1 says RuCl3 is "incommensurate with the graphite host", whereas the main text and assumption 2 index a commensurate 12×12 / 5×5 supercell with a = 29.6 Å; the observed reflections (multiples of 5 for the RuCl3 sublattice, or graphite) are equally consistent with an incommensurate structure, so the supercell should be presented as a description of in-plane registry unless commensurability is demonstrated. (c) There is no chemical evidence that the intercalant is RuCl3 in the α honeycomb form, rather than a Ru chloride of different stoichiometry, a Cl-rich species or co-intercalated Cl2, which cannot be assumed at 830-900 C in excess Cl2, nor that Ag/AgCl from the Cl source is absent. Every downstream statement (the α-RuCl3 naming, the Kitaev framing, the choice of DFT geometries) rests on these points. The authors should add compositional analysis (EDX/WDS, ICP, or mass uptake/TGA giving Ru:Cl and C:RuCl3 ratios, plus a check for Ag); Raman spectroscopy (G-band splitting and shift for bounding vs interior layers as a stage and doping diagnostic; RuCl3 phonon modes to identify the intercalant); off-axis (h0l) reflections or a 00l intensity / one-dimensional electron-density analysis to fix the gallery structure; the in-plane epitaxial relation between graphite and RuCl3 reflections; and stage purity, (00l) linewidths and residual-graphite and mixed-stage volume fractions. Otherwise the stacking should be presented as a hypothesis, and "comprehensive characterization" (abstract), "fully characterize" (introduction) and "determined the crystal structure" should be softened.

B. Should address

5. The calculated interlayer geometry is not checked against the measured lattice parameters, and its distances are defined inconsistently (Methods; SI; Fig. S4a). Methods fixes d_Gr-RuCl3 = 3.60 Å as "the average experimental value" / "suggested by X-ray data" and d_Gr-Gr = 3.54 Å, while the SI quotes 3.765 Å and a graphene-graphene distance across RuCl3 of 10.241 Å for A|A, apparently with different definitions. XRD gives only one c-period per stage, so these distances cannot be separated from the data; the measured periods imply d_Gr-Gr close to 3.35 Å and a gallery of about 9.2-9.4 Å. With 3.54 Å inner spacings the model S2 and S4 periods would be about 27 Å and 60.6 Å rather than 25.4 and 58.2 Å, and the larger d_Gr-Gr weakens interlayer hopping, which directly changes the multilayer pocket structure. Fig. S4a shows the charge transfer changing by about 10% over the plausible range of c, and the paper attributes the residual mismatch to interlayer spacing. The authors should define each distance once (to the Cl plane or the Ru plane), explain how 3.60 Å was obtained, give the computed c for each model next to the measured one, constrain the model cells by the measured c, or show the frequency dependence over the experimentally allowed range.

6. The DFT stacking models differ from the proposed structures (p. 4). A|AB|B is replaced by AB|AB, and AB|BCAB|BC by ABAB|ABAB or ABCA|ABCA, on the argument that charge transfer is insensitive to orientation; the DFT stackings also put different graphene registries across the RuCl3 layer, contrary to assumption 4 and Table I. Pocket areas, unlike total charge transfer, are sensitive to interlayer hopping and stacking, which is exactly what the paper says controls the Fermi surface. The authors should show that the actual proposed sequences (for example, mirror registry across the RuCl3 layer) give the same unfolded pockets, or state this as a limitation.

7. The benchmark of the reduced, strained cell is incomplete and the reported numbers conflict. The main text (p. 4) gives about 4% tensile strain on α-RuCl3 in the 2×2 / 5×5 cell, while the SI gives about 1% for the 5×5 / 12×12 cell (the SI geometry, Ru-Ru 3.443 Å, appears to be the unstrained one); the Fermi shift is 0.68 eV in the main text and ≈0.7 eV in the SI. The strained-versus-unstrained benchmark (Fig. S4a) is done only for A|A, but the reduced cell is then used for the multilayer stackings that matter most. The authors should reconcile the values, quantify the strain-induced difference in Dirac-point shift (meV) as a frequency uncertainty, and ideally do a single-point AB|AB calculation in the experimentally indexed 12×12 / 5×5 cell.

8. Several FFT peak assignments are questionable and the FFT procedure is not reported (Fig. 2f-h). In S2 the peak labelled 2β = 359 T differs from 2 × 170 = 340 T by about 19 T, well beyond the ~7 T resolution of a 4-9 T window (in S4, 347 T vs 2 × 176 = 352 T is closer). The 359 T peak may be a separate pocket, possibly related to the higher-frequency pocket DFT predicts for AB|AB, which would change the comparison in concern 1. In Fig. 2f (Kish graphite), 2ν1 = 9.3 T is not twice ν1 = 4.13 T (8.26 T) but coincides with ν1 + ν2 = 9.29 T and should be relabelled. The authors should show the background-subtracted Δρ against 1/B, state the field window, background polynomial order and windowing function, check whether peak positions shift with field range, and give the temperature dependence of each peak so that the reliability of the 91 T and 359 T features can be assessed.

9. The headline framing ("platform for exotic quantum phases", "magnetically active", Kitaev magnetism, flat-band physics, topology) is not tested by any measurement (title, abstract, conclusions). There are no magnetisation, susceptibility or specific-heat data showing whether the intercalated RuCl3 orders (near 7 K in bulk α-RuCl3, or otherwise), and the low-temperature ρ(T) is not examined for anomalies at a magnetic transition. In the authors' own calculation the flat bands lie roughly 0.5 eV from E_F after hole doping (Fig. 4c,d), so the link to rhombohedral flat-band physics is not supported. The authors should add at least χ(T) on the intercalated crystals and a low-temperature ρ(T) close-up, or present the platform as a prospect and temper the title, abstract and conclusions.

10. The resistivity data have internal inconsistencies and undiscussed features (Fig. 2b-e). The zero-field low-temperature resistivity of S2 differs between Fig. 2b (a few μΩ cm) and Fig. 2d (about 12 μΩ cm), possibly from different pieces; the y-axis of Fig. 2c reads mΩ cm while the other panels use μΩ cm. S4 has a residual resistivity of about 150 μΩ cm, roughly ten times S2 and above pristine Kish graphite, which is unusual for an acceptor GIC, and its ρ(T) is not shown in Fig. 2b. S2 shows large, nearly linear, non-saturating magnetoresistance (about 500% at 9 T) that is not discussed. The authors should give sample dimensions, geometric uncertainty and RRR for each sample, quantify the sample-dependent RRR noted on p. 3, check the units, and say whether the higher S4 resistivity reflects intrinsic doping or disorder/mixed phase, which bears on the Dingle temperature and on why fewer pockets are observed; a brief comment on the magnetoresistance is also needed.

13. Air stability and sample provenance are not stated. The paper does not say whether transport, single-crystal XRD and powder XRD were done on the same batch or piece, or how long samples were exposed to air. Chloride GICs can deintercalate at surfaces, producing mixed staging that is relevant to the stage-independent β (concern 1). The authors should state exposure times, storage conditions and which batch each measurement used.
