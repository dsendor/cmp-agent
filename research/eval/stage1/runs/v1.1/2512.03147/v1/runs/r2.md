Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report vapour-transport synthesis of graphite intercalated with RuCl3 (stage 2 and stage 4, "C-RuCl3"), using AgCl photolysis as a Cl2 source. They characterise it by synchrotron and laboratory XRD, by low-temperature magnetotransport with Shubnikov–de Haas (SdH) oscillations (β ≈ 170–176 T in both stages, plus α = 91 T in stage 4), and by non-spin-polarised GGA supercell calculations of charge transfer and unfolded graphene Fermi surfaces. A bulk material in which graphene multilayers alternate with a Kitaev-candidate layer and are heavily hole-doped by it would be a useful extension of the graphene/α-RuCl3 heterostructure work, and the synthesis is new as far as I know. As written, though, the paper is mainly a synthesis report. Its electronic characterisation does not yet tie the observed oscillations to the proposed stacking, and none of the "exotic quantum phases" in the title is probed.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new graphite intercalation compound, made with a simple and reproducible-sounding recipe (two-zone vapour transport, Cl2 from AgCl), with a clear time-controlled route from stage 4 (22 h) to stage 2 (48 h).
- The c-axis repeats (25.4 Å and 58.2 Å) are internally consistent with a single intercalant sandwich of about 9.35 Å plus 3.35 Å graphene spacings (2 × (9.35 + 3.35) ≈ 25.4 Å; 3 × (9.35 + 3 × 3.35) ≈ 58.1 Å). The in-plane a ≈ 29.6 Å matches 5 × a(RuCl3) ≈ 12 × a(graphene), which supports a RuCl3-like intercalant layer.
- The intercalated samples show SdH frequencies about 40 times higher than in the parent Kish graphite, which qualitatively signals heavy doping of the graphene layers. The Cl2-annealed graphite reference is a sensible first control.
- The DFT strain benchmark (5×5/12×12 against 2×2/5×5 cells, Fig. S4a) and the orientation test (Fig. S2b/S3) are careful checks of the reduced-cell approach.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The DFT–SdH comparison does not support the claim that the oscillations are "consistent with" the proposed stackings (Electronic properties, p. 5–6; Conclusions).
   - **The computed pockets match poorly.** Stage-2-like AB|AB gives 131 T and 444 T, and stage-4-like stackings give 70/296 T or 28/311/330 T. The measured values are β ≈ 170 T in stage 2 and α = 91 T, β = 176 T in stage 4. Neither stage gets a quantitative match, and the predicted 444 T and ~300 T pockets are absent from the FFTs (Fig. 2g,h), although they should be resolvable in a 4–9 T window.
   - **The agreement is obtained post hoc.** A rigid Fermi-level shift of 0.1 eV is applied only to the stage-4 geometries, giving 27/183 T and 126/214 T. No corresponding shift is shown for stage 2. A free Fermi-level parameter that brings one of two or three pockets near one measured frequency is not a discriminating test. The authors' own statement that the "inner hole pocket" can be pushed below E_F to explain the missing α in stage 2 shows how flexible the comparison is.
   - **β is nearly identical in both stages.** The measured β is almost the same in stage 2 and stage 4 (170 vs 176 T), whereas the calculations predict quite different pocket sets for the two stagings. This points to a common origin, such as the interface graphene layer adjacent to RuCl3 at a fixed doping, which the paper does not discuss.
   - **The electronic model is questionable for this system.** The calculations are non-spin-polarised GGA without U or SOC. In that limit RuCl3 is metallic, and Fig. S5b,c indeed shows RuCl3 bands crossing E_F. The Fermi level, and therefore the graphene doping that sets every frequency, is pinned by spurious RuCl3 states. Published graphene/α-RuCl3 calculations (refs. 12, 35, which share authors) use GGA+U+SOC with zigzag magnetic order precisely because the charge transfer depends on the Mott gap.
   - **The calculation geometry departs from experiment.** d_Gr–Gr is fixed at 3.54 Å instead of about 3.35 Å, which weakens interlayer hopping and directly changes the multilayer pocket structure. d_Gr–RuCl3 = 3.60 Å in Methods but 3.765 Å in the SI. And the DFT stackings (AB|AB, ABAB|ABAB, ABCA|ABCA) put different graphene registries across the RuCl3 layer, contrary to assumption 4 and the Table I stackings (A|AB|B, AB|BCAB|BC).

   What the authors should do:
   - Repeat at least the A|A and AB|AB calculations with GGA+U (+SOC, zigzag or FM order) at the experimental interlayer spacings.
   - Report the resulting doping and frequencies without a free Fermi shift. If a shift is kept, apply it uniformly to both stages and justify it.
   - Discuss why β is stage-independent.
   - Otherwise, restate the conclusion as qualitative (heavy hole doping of graphene) rather than as identification of the stacking from SdH.

2. Attributing the SdH frequencies to graphene layers in the intended stage is not established experimentally (Fig. 2, p. 3). The samples are described as "highly oriented polycrystalline", with residual graphite (Fig. S1). Mixed staging appears with longer reaction, and the stage-4 pattern shows strong graphite reflections. The argument that the oscillations come from quasi-2D hole pockets in the graphene layers of the given stage rests on one field orientation, one temperature and one sample per stage. Several standard checks are missing:
   - (a) Angle-dependent SdH, F(θ) ∝ 1/cosθ, to show that the pockets are 2D cylinders as assumed in the k_z-independent Onsager analysis.
   - (b) Temperature-dependent amplitudes with a Lifshitz–Kosevich fit to give cyclotron masses. These can be compared directly with the DFT bands, and with the m* ≈ E_F/v_F² expected for doped Dirac/multilayer bands.
   - (c) The Hall data, which the Methods say were measured (antisymmetrised) but are not shown. Their sign and magnitude would confirm hole carriers and give a density to compare with the Luttinger count from α and β.
   - (d) SdH on the Cl2-annealed graphite reference. Fig. 2b shows that this reference's resistivity changes, so the possibility that β comes from Cl-doped graphite regions rather than RuCl3 intercalation must be excluded.
   - (e) The number of samples measured per stage, and whether β is reproduced across growths.

   Without (a)–(d), the statement that the oscillations "indicate substantial modifications of the Fermi surface" of the RuCl3-intercalated phase is plausible but not demonstrated.

B. Should address

3. The intercalant's composition and structure are inferred rather than measured (Structure analysis; Methods assumptions 1–4).
   - There is no elemental analysis (EDX/WDS, ICP or TGA) to give the Ru:Cl ratio and the C:RuCl3 ratio, and to exclude Ag or AgCl incorporation from the Cl source.
   - The stage assignment and stacking sequence (Table I) come from analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 GICs, because only (00l) and (hk0) reflections were accessible. The A|AB|B / AB|BCAB|BC registries and the "α" (honeycomb) character of the intercalant are therefore assumptions.
   - Assumption 1 states that RuCl3 is incommensurate with graphite, while assumption 2 and the indexing use a commensurate 5×5/12×12 supercell. Please reconcile these.
   - Please add composition data, any off-axis (h0l) reflections or Raman evidence for the RuCl3 layer, and an estimate of the residual-graphite and mixed-stage volume fractions. Soften "comprehensive characterization" and "determined the crystal structure" accordingly.

4. The title and abstract claim a "platform for exotic quantum phases", and the conclusion invokes flat bands, Kitaev magnetism and topology. None of these is measured. There are no magnetisation or specific-heat data to show whether the intercalated RuCl3 orders (bulk α-RuCl3 orders near 7 K), nor resistivity or MR anomalies at a magnetic transition. Either add a basic susceptibility measurement on the intercalated crystals, or present the platform as a prospect rather than a result.

5. Resistivity data are hard to interpret quantitatively (Fig. 2b–e). The authors note large geometric uncertainty and that cutting changes the RRR. Stage 4 has a residual resistivity of about 150 μΩ cm, an order of magnitude above stage 2. Please give the sample dimensions and estimated geometric uncertainty. Please also say whether the higher stage-4 resistivity reflects intrinsic doping or disorder and mixed phase, since this bears on the Dingle temperature and on why fewer pockets are observed.

6. The DFT extraction of pocket areas from smeared unfolded spectral weight (Methods) introduces a systematic error described only as "small". Please quantify it, for example by comparing with the dhva-code result available for A|A, and give uncertainties on the computed frequencies. Without these, statements such as "the agreement improves" cannot be judged.

C. Minor

7. Fig. 2f: the peak labelled 2ν1 = 9.3 T is not twice ν1 = 4.13 T (8.26 T). It coincides with ν1 + ν2 = 9.29 T and should be relabelled. Similarly, in Fig. 2g, 2β = 359 T differs from 2 × 170 = 340 T by more than the FFT resolution for a 4–9 T window. Please check the peak positions.
8. The strain on RuCl3 in the reduced cell is given as ≈4% in the main text but ≈1% in the SI. Fermi shifts are 0.68 eV (main) vs ≈0.7 eV (SI). The SI labels the cells "S1 and S2" (Fig. S3), which clashes with the S2/S4 stage notation.
9. The Fig. S1 caption describes (a),(b) as XRD images and (c),(d) as integrated profiles, but the figure shows the reverse. The supplementary figures run S1, S2, S4, S3.
10. The Table I caption reads "α-CuI3", and should read RuCl3. Other typos: "heretostructures", "University of of Tennessee", "Graphte" in Fig. 2a.
11. Please define d_Gr–RuCl3 precisely: to the Cl plane or to the Ru plane? Please also state how the value of 3.60 Å was "suggested by X-ray data", given that only the total sandwich thickness is measured.
12. The Fig. 2 FFTs should state the field window, the background polynomial order and the windowing used, so that the frequency resolution and the reliability of the 91 T peak can be assessed.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add angle- and temperature-dependent SdH (giving F(θ) and m*) together with the Hall density. These are the measurements that would turn the Fermi-surface discussion into a test of the stacking model.
- Recompute the stage-2 and stage-4 Fermi surfaces with GGA+U(+SOC) and magnetic order at the experimental spacings, and compare with experiment without a stage-specific Fermi-level shift.
- Provide composition (EDX/WDS) and phase-fraction data, plus SdH on the Cl2-annealed control.
- Measure magnetic susceptibility of the intercalated crystals to establish whether the RuCl3 layers retain magnetic order. This is the minimum needed to support the title.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
