Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports vapour-transport growth of graphite intercalated with RuCl3 (stage 2 and stage 4, "C-RuCl3"), using UV-decomposed AgCl to supply Cl2. It characterises the samples by X-ray diffraction and Shubnikov-de Haas (SdH) oscillations, and compares the SdH frequencies with DFT Fermi surfaces of graphene multilayers hole-doped by charge transfer to RuCl3. A bulk, three-dimensional analogue of the graphene/alpha-RuCl3 heterostructures, with tunable graphene stacking between Kitaev-candidate layers, would be an attractive materials platform, and the synthesis route is useful in itself. In its current form, however, the work establishes that some intercalation compound with large c-axis expansion and new, higher-frequency quantum oscillations has formed. It does not yet establish the intercalant's identity and structure, the stacking sequences, or the claimed link between stacking, charge transfer and the measured Fermi surface.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new and simple synthesis route (two-zone transport with AgCl as an in situ Cl2 source), with the useful observation that Cl2 is needed for intercalation, and with stage selection by reaction time (22 h for stage 4, 48 h for stage 2).
- Clear c-axis expansion in powder and single-crystal XRD (25.4 and 58.2 Angstrom), with in-plane reflections indexed to a roughly 29.6 Angstrom supercell. This matches 12x12 graphene / 5x5 RuCl3 to within about 1%, a natural commensurate approximant.
- Quantum oscillations in bulk intercalated crystals at 170-176 T, much higher than in pristine Kish graphite (4-5 T). This is direct evidence of strong hole doping of the graphene layers. The Kish-graphite and Cl2-annealed-graphite references are a sensible control for the synthesis environment.
- A systematic DFT study across A|A, AB|AB, ABAB|ABAB and ABCA|ABCA geometries, with band unfolding onto graphene and Bader analysis, showing weak hybridisation and charge transfer that grows with the number of graphene layers per RuCl3 layer.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The intercalant's identity, the stage assignment and the stacking sequence are inferred rather than measured. The claim that the crystal structure was "determined" is not supported. The structure section (p. 2-3, Methods p. 7, Table I, Fig. S1) indexes only (00l) reflections from powder and (hk0) reflections from single crystals; Fig. S1 states that l ≠ 0 reflections cannot be observed in this geometry. The graphene stacking (A/B/C) and the registry across the RuCl3 layer are therefore taken from four assumptions "by analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3" (p. 7) and are not obtained from data. Specific problems:
   - (a) The stage-4 stacking in Table I does not obviously reproduce the measured c. With the paper's own spacings (about 3.35-3.54 Angstrom between graphene layers and an intercalant gallery of about 9-10 Angstrom), one stage-4 repeat is about 19.4 Angstrom, so c = 58.2 Angstrom corresponds to three intercalant layers per cell. The listed sequences "AB|BCAB|BC" or "AB|BABA|AB" contain two RuCl3 layers and eight graphene layers, which would give about 39 Angstrom. For stage 2, by contrast, c = 25.4 Angstrom = 2 x 12.7 Angstrom is consistent with "A|AB|B". The authors should state explicitly how many intercalant and graphene layers each proposed cell contains and show that it reproduces c.
   - (b) Assumption 1 states that RuCl3 is "incommensurate with the graphite host". The (hk0) indexing to a = 29.6 Angstrom with hexagonal supercell reflections (Fig. S1c,d) implies a commensurate 12x12 / 5x5 superlattice. These are different structural statements.
   - (c) No chemical evidence is given that the intercalant is RuCl3, nor that it keeps the alpha-RuCl3 honeycomb, as opposed to a Ru chloride of different stoichiometry, a Cl-rich species, or co-intercalated Cl2. At 830-900 C in excess Cl2 this cannot be assumed. Nor is there evidence that Ag from the AgCl source is absent. The paper also does not report the intercalant gallery height (d_s) obtained from c, which would itself test the RuCl3 identification.

   This matters because every downstream statement (the naming as alpha-RuCl3, the "Kitaev" framing, and the selection of DFT geometries) rests on the intercalant being an alpha-RuCl3 honeycomb layer in a specific stacking. The authors should:
   - add compositional analysis (EDX/WDS on cleaved crystals or mass uptake / TGA giving the C:RuCl3 ratio, plus a check for Ag);
   - add Raman spectroscopy (graphene G-band splitting and shift for bounding vs interior layers is the standard stage and doping diagnostic in acceptor GICs; RuCl3 phonon modes would identify the intercalant);
   - add reflections with l ≠ 0, or a full 00l series analysis (an l-dependent intensity or one-dimensional Fourier/electron-density analysis of the 00l intensities, which is routine for GICs and fixes the gallery structure);
   - quantify the residual-graphite and mixed-stage fractions.

   Without this, the stacking should be presented as a hypothesis rather than as determined.

2. The assignment of the SdH frequencies to stacking-specific graphene Fermi pockets is not supported. This is the paper's main physics link between experiment and theory (p. 5-6, Fig. 2g,h, Fig. 5).
   - (a) Agreement is achieved only after a post hoc Fermi-level shift of about 0.1 eV, applied independently per structure. Even then the match is loose. The measured 91 and about 176 T in S4 are compared with 27/183 T (ABAB|ABAB) or 126/214 T (ABCA|ABCA). The unshifted stage-2 prediction (131 and 444 T) is compared with a single measured 170 T, and no shifted stage-2 values are given. With one free energy shift and two or three pockets per structure, frequency matching is a weak test.
   - (b) Stage 2 and stage 4 show essentially the same dominant frequency (170 vs 176 T, within the roughly 7 T FFT resolution of a 4-9 T window). The DFT predicts clearly different pocket sets for the two stages. The coincidence is more naturally explained by a common phase or region, for example stage-2 domains in the S4 crystal or a surface-bounding-layer contribution. The text does not discuss it.
   - (c) The labelled stage-2 harmonic "2β = 359 T" is not twice β = 170 T (340 T), whereas in S4 2β = 347 T is close to 2 x 176 T. The 359 T peak may be a separate frequency.
   - (d) The text calls the absence of α in S2 "sample-dependent", but the theory assigns S2 and S4 to different structures, so this explanation is circular.

   The authors should:
   - provide temperature-dependent SdH amplitudes with Lifshitz-Kosevich effective masses for each frequency (graphene-like pockets with E_F of about 0.5 eV predict specific masses, so this is a discriminating test);
   - measure the field-angle dependence of the frequencies to show that the pockets are two-dimensional (F proportional to 1/cos θ), as expected for graphene layers separated by RuCl3;
   - show the Hall data that the Methods say were measured, to confirm hole carriers and give a density for comparison with the frequency-derived density;
   - report the FFT window, background-subtraction order and frequency resolution;
   - measure more than one crystal per stage (the number of samples is not stated).

   If the frequencies cannot be tied to a specific stacking, the conclusion that "the quantum oscillation response is governed by the stacking sequence" should be softened.

3. The DFT level of theory is not appropriate for the charge-transfer magnitude on which the Fermi-pocket comparison depends. All calculations use non-spin-polarised GGA with no Hubbard U and no spin-orbit coupling (Methods p. 7). In this approximation alpha-RuCl3 is metallic. Fig. S5c in fact shows RuCl3-derived bands crossing E_F, which contradicts the main-text statement that alpha-RuCl3 remains "rather insulating" (p. 5). The size of the charge transfer, and hence the graphene Fermi-level shift (0.48-0.68 eV) and the pocket areas, is set by where the empty Ru t2g states sit relative to the graphene Dirac point. That position depends strongly on U, SOC and magnetic order, which is why earlier work on graphene/alpha-RuCl3 (including refs. 12 and 35 from the same group) used GGA+U+SOC with magnetic order. Since the paper itself argues that 0.1 eV shifts decide the agreement with experiment, the method-dependent uncertainty in E_F must be quantified. The authors should:
   - repeat at least the AB|AB and the four-layer structures with GGA+SOC+U in the zigzag (or ferromagnetic) state;
   - report the resulting Dirac-point shift and pocket frequencies;
   - state whether RuCl3-derived bands contribute Fermi-surface sheets, which would themselves produce SdH frequencies and should not simply be projected out.

B. Should address

4. The "first time" claim (Conclusions, p. 6) needs reconciling with the literature the paper cites. Ref. 27 (Postnikov et al., J. Struct. Chem. 1977) reports synthesis and structure of graphite compounds with ruthenium chlorides. The authors should explain how the present compounds differ (phase, stage, crystal quality, characterisation) and compare their c-axis values and gallery heights with that work and with other metal-trichloride GICs.

5. The calculated geometries are not checked against the measured lattice parameters. Methods fixes d_Gr-RuCl3 = 3.60 Angstrom and d_Gr-Gr = 3.54 Angstrom. The SI gives a graphene-graphene distance across the RuCl3 layer of 10.24 Angstrom for A|A. The measured c values imply a gallery of about 9.2 Angstrom (stage 2) or less. It is also unclear how "3.60 Angstrom suggested by X-ray data" was obtained, since only c is measured. The authors should give the computed c for each model next to the measured one. Fig. S4a shows that the charge transfer changes by about 10% over the plausible range of c, which matters given concern 2.

6. The benchmark for the reduced, strained cell is incomplete and the strain values conflict. The main text (p. 4) states about 4% tensile strain in the 2x2 RuCl3 / 5x5 graphene cell, while the SI states about 1% for the 5x5/12x12 cell. The strained-versus-unstrained benchmark is done only for A|A, but the reduced cell is then used for the multilayer stackings that matter most. The authors should quantify the difference in Dirac-point shift (meV) between strained and unstrained cells and express it as a frequency uncertainty. The measured cell (12x12 / 5x5, a = 29.6 Angstrom) is the one that was benchmarked, so a single-point calculation of AB|AB in that cell would remove the concern for stage 2.

7. The DFT stacking models are simplified relative to the proposed structures. The text replaces A|AB|B by AB|AB and AB|BCAB|BC by ABAB|ABAB or ABCA|ABCA (p. 4), arguing that charge transfer is insensitive to orientation. Pocket areas, however, are sensitive to interlayer hopping and stacking, which is exactly the property the paper says controls the Fermi surface. The authors should show that the actual proposed sequences (for example, mirror registry across the RuCl3 layer) give the same unfolded pockets, or state this as a limitation.

8. The transport data have internal inconsistencies and features that are not discussed.
   - The zero-field low-temperature resistivity of S2 in Fig. 2b (a few micro-ohm cm) differs from that in Fig. 2d (about 12 micro-ohm cm). This may reflect different pieces; the sample-dependent RRR noted on p. 3 should be quantified.
   - S4 is more resistive than both S2 and pristine Kish graphite (Fig. 2e), which is unusual for an acceptor GIC.
   - S2 shows large, nearly linear, non-saturating magnetoresistance (about 500% at 9 T).
   - The y-axis unit of Fig. 2c (milliohm cm) should be checked against Fig. 2b.

   The authors should state the number of samples, give RRR and geometry uncertainty for each, and comment briefly on the magnetoresistance and on the S4 resistivity.

9. The "exotic quantum phases" framing goes beyond the data. No magnetic measurement is presented (susceptibility, specific heat, or the zigzag transition near 7 K in bulk alpha-RuCl3), nor any evidence of flat-band or correlated behaviour. Even a magnetisation curve showing whether the intercalated RuCl3 orders, and at what temperature, would substantively support the "Kitaev material in a 3D graphite host" motivation. Otherwise the title and abstract should be toned down.

10. Air stability and sample homogeneity are not stated. The paper does not say whether the transport, single-crystal XRD and powder XRD were done on the same batch or piece, or how long samples were exposed to air. Chloride GICs can deintercalate at surfaces, which could produce mixed staging (relevant to concern 2b).

C. Minor

11. The Table I caption refers to the "alpha-CuI3 layer"; this should read alpha-RuCl3.

12. Assumption 1 on p. 7 ("RuCl3 crystallizes in a solid composed of Cl3Ru2Cl3") is unclear and should be rephrased.

13. "Comprehensive characterization" (abstract) and "fully characterize" (introduction) overstate the scope given concern 1.

14. Fig. 2f-h: the FFT x-axis ranges differ between panels and the α peak in Fig. 2h is labelled vertically and is hard to read. Showing the background-subtracted oscillations against 1/B would help.

15. Fig. S3 caption: the labels "S1" and "S2" are used for supercell orientations A and B, which clashes with the S2/S4 stage notation used in the main text.

16. Fig. 4 caption and text: please state which pristine multilayer (AB or ABC, number of layers) the grey reference bands are, and how the "global Fermi energy shift" of 0.68/0.48 eV is defined when bands are not rigidly shifted.

17. The text states that the Fermi pocket boundaries were "taken from the highest intensity points" of smeared unfolded spectral functions. Please give an estimated uncertainty in the calculated frequencies from this procedure.

18. Data availability "upon reasonable request": consider depositing the XRD patterns, magnetotransport data and DFT structures.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add Raman and compositional analysis (EDX/WDS or mass uptake) for each stage. Together with a 00l intensity analysis, this would turn the structural part from inference into determination and fix the gallery height used in DFT.
- Turn the SdH data into a discriminating test: temperature-dependent amplitudes for effective masses, field-angle dependence for dimensionality, and Hall density. Compare masses and densities with the DFT pockets rather than frequencies alone.
- Recompute the key structures at GGA+SOC+U with magnetic order in the experimentally indexed 12x12/5x5 cell, with c set to the measured value. Present the remaining frequency discrepancy with an uncertainty band instead of an ad hoc Fermi-level shift.
- Add one magnetic measurement on the intercalated crystals, to support the claim that the Kitaev layer is retained in the 3D host.

5. Verdict: major revision
