Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors grow graphite intercalated with α-RuCl3 by two-zone chemical vapour transport, using AgCl as an in-situ Cl2 source, and identify stage-2 and stage-4 compounds by XRD (c = 25.4 Å and 58.2 Å). They report Shubnikov–de Haas (SdH) frequencies of about 170–176 T, plus a 91 T frequency in stage 4, both far above those of pristine graphite. GGA calculations on strained-supercell models show hole doping of the graphene layers and stage- and stacking-dependent K-point hole pockets. A bulk, three-dimensional counterpart of the much-studied graphene/α-RuCl3 heterostructures is of real interest, and a reproducible synthesis route is the main contribution. The microscopic link between the measured oscillations and specific stackings is much less firmly established than the text suggests.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A practical synthesis recipe, with the role of excess Cl2 identified (no intercalation without it) and a Cl2-annealed graphite control for the transport data (Fig. 2b).
- XRD c-axis repeats that agree well with simple staging arithmetic: about 9.4 Å gallery + (n−1)×3.35 Å gives about 12.7 Å for stage 2 (25.4/2) and about 19.4 Å for stage 4 (58.2/3).
- Sharp, high-frequency SdH oscillations with visible harmonics, reported as reproducible across pieces of one crystal and across independently grown samples. This points to a fairly ordered, uniformly doped bulk compound.
- A consistent DFT picture (Figs. 3–5) of interfacial charge transfer, screening of the inner graphene layers, and K-point hole pockets. This is a useful starting point for later spectroscopy.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The claimed agreement between the measured SdH frequencies and the stacking-dependent DFT Fermi surfaces is not supported (Fig. 2g,h; Fig. 5; page 5; Conclusions). The text says the calculated Fermi surfaces are "consistent with the experimental SdH frequencies" and that "the quantum oscillation response is governed by the stacking sequence". The numbers do not show this:
   - For stage 2, the AB|AB model gives 131 T and 444 T, while experiment shows one fundamental at 170 T. No shifted calculation is reported for stage 2.
   - For stage 4, the unshifted models give 70/296 T or 28/311/330 T. Rough agreement with 91 T and 176 T appears only after an ad hoc Fermi-level shift of 0.1 eV, which gives 27/183 T or 126/214 T. Even then neither stacking reproduces both observed frequencies, and the shift is a free parameter tuned to the data being explained.
   - The dominant frequency is almost the same in stage 2 (170 T) and stage 4 (176 T). With the field window used (roughly 4–9 T, Δ(1/B) ≈ 0.14 T⁻¹, frequency resolution ~7 T), these two values cannot be distinguished. The calculations instead predict quite different pocket sets for the two stages. A stage-independent β is the observation that most needs explaining. It could point to a common pocket (for example the outer, interface-adjacent graphene layer, whose doping is set locally by the RuCl3 layer) or to a shared phase in both samples, rather than to stacking control.

   What the authors should do: (i) give each experimental frequency with an uncertainty, the field window and the number of periods. (ii) Measure the angular dependence F(θ) to show the pockets are quasi-two-dimensional and come from the graphene layers. (iii) Extract cyclotron masses from Lifshitz–Kosevich fits of the temperature dependence and compare them with the band masses of the candidate pockets; this is a much stronger discriminator than frequencies alone. (iv) If these are not available, reword the conclusions: the calculations show the expected size of the hole pockets, but they do not identify the stacking or establish a one-to-one assignment.

B. Should address

2. The stacking sequences in Table 1 and Fig. 1 are assumed, not measured (Methods, "Structure analysis", assumptions 1–4). Powder XRD gives only 00l (h = k = 0) reflections, and the single-crystal data give only in-plane reflections. Neither set constrains the interlayer registration (AA vs AB vs ABC), which needs mixed h0l/hkl reflections. The sequences are adopted "by analogy" with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 compounds. This matters for the paper's framing: the rhombohedral (ABCA) vs Bernal (ABAB) distinction is what links the material to flat-band physics, and it drives the DFT comparison in concern 1. The stacking should be called a model assumption throughout. Ideally it should be tested, for example with off-axis single-crystal reflections, or by comparing the cross-sectional STEM images with simulated images of each candidate stacking along a zone axis where they differ.

3. Phase purity and staging distribution are not quantified (pages 2–3). The text mentions "a small portion of residual pure graphite". It also says a 48 h reaction gives "mixed stage intercalation and/or disordered compounds", and that the α frequency is absent in some samples ("sample-dependent absence of the α frequency"). The latter conflicts with the statement that "measurements on multiple independently prepared samples reproduced the same FFT peak". Please state how many samples of each stage were measured by XRD and by transport, whether the XRD and transport data come from the same pieces, and the fraction of graphite and of other stages, estimated from integrated 00l intensities. Show the FFT spectra of the additional samples, at least in the Supplementary Material, so that sample-to-sample spread in frequency and amplitude can be judged. The main-text argument that mixed staging "would be expected to broaden or complicate the oscillation spectrum" is weak: SdH selects the highest-mobility regions and does not by itself rule out a second phase.

4. The chemical composition of the intercalant is not established. No EDS or EELS analysis, chemical analysis, or RuCl3 mass uptake is reported. The growth uses AgCl decomposed under UV and an enhanced Cl2 atmosphere, so Cl-only or Ag-containing intercalation, or a Ru-deficient or non-stoichiometric layer, should be ruled out. The STEM evidence is described as "qualitative" with no "fully resolved layer-by-layer intercalation pattern". Elemental mapping across the galleries in the existing cross-sectional STEM data would settle this directly. A matching null control would also help: the SdH spectrum of the Cl2-annealed graphite already used in Fig. 2b, showing that it lacks the 170 T and 91 T frequencies. At present that control is shown only as zero-field resistivity.

5. The Hall density is not reconciled with the SdH pocket areas (page 3). A single-carrier estimate of 7.8×10²⁰ cm⁻³ is quoted without the carrier sign, the sample (stage), the temperature, or whether ρxy is linear in field. A rough Luttinger count from F ≈ 170 T (k_F ≈ 0.072 Å⁻¹; about 1.6×10¹³ cm⁻² per graphene layer including spin and valley) gives about 2.6×10²⁰ cm⁻³ for stage 2 if every graphene layer carries one such pocket. This is a factor of about 3 below the Hall value. The difference may come from additional pockets that are not observed, from multiband compensation, or from inequivalent layers, but the paper should present the comparison. It is a direct, model-light test of how much charge is transferred and could also tie the measurements to the DFT Bader numbers (Fig. 3, Fig. S4). Positive (hole) Hall sign should be shown explicitly, since hole doping is a central DFT prediction.

6. The DFT setup may limit how reliable the charge-transfer magnitude and band positions are (Methods; pages 3–4). The calculations are non-spin-polarized GGA without +U or spin–orbit coupling, on a nonmagnetic RuCl3 layer. In graphene/α-RuCl3 work, the amount of charge transfer and the position of the RuCl3 t2g states relative to the graphene Dirac point depend on U and on magnetic order. The 0.1 eV shift needed in concern 1 is of the size such choices produce. A benchmark of GGA+U(+SOC) with zigzag or ferromagnetic order, on at least the A|A or AB|AB cell, would show how robust the hole-pocket sizes are. In addition, d_Gr–RuCl3 is fixed at 3.60 Å, a value described as "experimental". Please give the c-axis repeat of the relaxed models and compare it with the measured 12.7 Å and 19.4 Å repeats. With a ~2.7–2.8 Å Cl–Ru–Cl slab, the XRD repeats suggest a somewhat smaller graphene–RuCl3 separation, and the paper states that the pockets are "highly sensitive" to this distance.

7. The in-plane lattice description is internally inconsistent (Table 1; Methods). Assumption 1 states that RuCl3 is "incommensurate with the graphite host". Yet the measured a ≈ 29.6 Å is indexed as a commensurate N×M supercell, consistent with 12×12 graphene (29.5 Å) and close to 5×5 RuCl3. Please state which reflections give a, whether superlattice reflections from the RuCl3 layer are seen, and whether the near-coincidence with 5×5 RuCl3 (about 1% strain) or the 2×2/5×5 model with 4% tensile strain is closer to reality. This bears on the strain discussion at the end of the Results.

8. The title and abstract claim more than the data show ("platform for exotic quantum phases", "access to novel correlated and topological states", "fully characterize the samples"). The paper presents no data on the magnetic state of the intercalated RuCl3 (susceptibility, specific heat, or the 7 K ordering transition), and no evidence of flat-band or correlation effects in the graphene layers. The calculations are nonmagnetic. A basic magnetization measurement showing whether, and at what temperature, RuCl3 still orders in the intercalated compound would substantially support the "platform" framing. Without it, the wording should be toned down, and "fully characterize" should be removed.

9. The priority claim needs qualification. The paper states the compound is synthesized "for the first time", but it cites (ref. 27) a 1977 study of layer-type graphite compounds with ruthenium chlorides. Please explain how the present material differs from that earlier work (phase, staging, crystallinity), or remove the claim.

C. Minor

10. Harmonics: 2β is given as 359 T for stage 2, against 2×170 = 340 T, and 2ν1 = 9.3 T for Kish graphite, against 2×4.13 = 8.26 T. Either these lie within the FFT resolution, which should be stated, or the peaks are separate frequencies. The second case matters for stage 2, where DFT predicts a second pocket. The text also calls 2β the "first harmonic"; it is the second harmonic.

11. Fig. 2: state the field window and background-subtraction polynomial order used for each FFT, and say whether the windows were the same for the different samples. Give the zero-field resistivities of Fig. 2c–e and make them consistent with Fig. 2b (Fig. 2c is in mΩ cm, Fig. 2b in μΩ cm). State which stage-4 sample was used in Fig. 2h.

12. The Fermi-surface frequencies come from smeared unfolded spectral weight, with pocket boundaries "taken from the highest intensity points". Please estimate the resulting uncertainty, for example by varying the broadening or threshold. Show the dhva cross-check result for the A|A case, not just mention it.

13. Methods, XRD: "all the observed peaks are for hkl (l = 0)" appears to contradict the earlier "hkl (h = k = 0)". Clarify which data set contains which reflections. Also: "29.5(A)" in the text should read 29.5(4) Å (Table 1); "Graphte" appears in Fig. 2a; and "AgCl 0.03g" in the mass ratio is unclear.

14. Sample stability: say whether the samples degrade in air or under thermal cycling, and how long elapsed between growth and transport measurement. This bears on the RRR changes caused by cutting, which the text mentions.

15. The statement that the 4% strain "does not significantly affect the relevant physical observables" is benchmarked only for A|A (page 3). Please say so, or extend the check to one multilayer stacking.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add LK mass fits and F(θ) for both stages. Together with the Hall data, these would turn the quantum-oscillation section from frequency matching into a quantitative test of the charge-transfer picture.
- Add cross-sectional STEM elemental maps and a stacking-sensitive zone-axis image comparison, to determine composition and interlayer registration directly.
- Measure magnetic susceptibility, or at least M(T) through the bulk RuCl3 ordering range, to show what happens to the Kitaev magnet on intercalation.
- Show the Cl2-annealed graphite SdH spectrum as an explicit null control alongside Fig. 2f–h.

5. Verdict: major revision
