Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports chemical-vapour-transport growth of graphite intercalated with α-RuCl3, using photolysed AgCl as a chlorine source. It assigns stage-2 and stage-4 phases from X-ray diffraction, reports Shubnikov-de Haas (SdH) frequencies of about 91 T and 170-176 T, and compares these with unfolded GGA Fermi surfaces of graphene/RuCl3 superlattices. A bulk, three-dimensional counterpart of the graphene/α-RuCl3 charge-transfer heterostructure would be a useful platform, and the synthesis route is of practical interest. As the manuscript stands, the structural assignment (stage and especially stacking) and the link between the measured oscillations and the computed graphene pockets rest more on assumption than on direct evidence.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A workable and described synthesis route (CVT with an AgCl-generated Cl2 atmosphere, two-zone furnace, reaction time controlling stage) that others could reproduce. The Cl2-annealed graphite control (Fig. 2b) is a sensible check that annealing alone does not explain the transport changes.
- Clear expansion of the c-axis (25.4 Å and 58.2 Å against 6.7 Å for graphite) and an in-plane supercell (a ≈ 29.6 Å ≈ 12 a_graphene ≈ 5 a_RuCl3) that is consistent with a RuCl3 layer inside the graphite.
- Sharp new SdH frequencies (91 T, 170-176 T), far above the graphite frequencies, that are reported to recur on a second piece and on independently prepared samples. This points to a well-defined, hole-doped electronic structure.
- A systematic DFT study of how charge transfer and the unfolded Fermi surface depend on the number of graphene layers between RuCl3 sheets (Figs. 3-5). It reproduces the qualitative picture of charge depletion concentrated in the interface layers, as known from graphene/α-RuCl3 heterostructures.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The stacking sequences and part of the stage assignment are assumed, not measured, yet they underpin the main conclusion. The Methods state that powder data were indexed only on (00l) reflections and that all single-crystal reflections observed were (hk0) "due to the large c-axis and limited sample oscillation angle". Neither set of reflections is sensitive to the lateral registry of the graphene layers (A, B or C positions) or to that of the RuCl3 layers. The stackings in Table 1 (A|AB|B; AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB) and Fig. 1 therefore follow from assumption 4 in the Methods (analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 GICs), not from the data. This matters for the paper's central message. The Conclusions say the authors "determined the crystal structure and showed that several different stackings can be achieved", and the DFT section concludes that "the quantum oscillation response is governed by the stacking sequence". Both statements need stacking-sensitive evidence.
   - The stage assignment itself also needs firmer support. The authors should show the indexed (00l) pattern with the identity period I_c = d_i + (n-1)·3.35 Å worked out explicitly for each stage, the intensity fit (or at least relative intensities) against stage-1, 2, 3 and 4 models, and the fraction of residual graphite and of mixed stages. Fig. S1 is cited for this, but the main text gives only the two c values.
   - What would settle the stacking: off-axis (h0l) or (hkl) reflections, either from a rotation or precession dataset with a larger oscillation range or from cross-sectional STEM along a zone axis where the A/B/C registry is resolved. Otherwise the authors should present the stackings as one possibility among several and remove "determined the crystal structure" and "several different stackings can be achieved".
   - Related: the text reports two stacking options for S4 (Table 1). Fig. 1 labels ABAB|ABAB and ABCA|ABCA as S4, while the DFT replaces the measured sequences with these idealised ones. The authors should state plainly that the S4 stacking is unknown, and that the two computed S4 stackings bracket it only if the stacking-insensitivity argument holds.

2. Whether the SdH oscillations come from the intercalated graphene layers is not established. The quantitative comparison with DFT does not yet support the "consistent with experimental SdH frequencies" statement in the Conclusions.
   - Dimensionality. The oscillations are shown only for B ∥ c at a single temperature (Fig. 2d, e, g, h). Weakly coupled graphene layers between RuCl3 sheets should give quasi-2D pockets with F(θ) ∝ 1/cos θ. Residual graphite, which the XRD shows is present, or a different minority phase would not. Angle-dependent SdH is the standard test and should be added.
   - Lifshitz-Kosevich analysis. No effective masses or Dingle temperatures are given. The cyclotron mass from the temperature dependence of the amplitude, compared with the DFT band mass of each candidate pocket, is the most direct way to tell which computed pocket corresponds to α and which to β. It would also distinguish Dirac-like monolayer-interface pockets from bilayer-like pockets.
   - Same frequency in both stages. β is essentially the same in S2 (170 T) and S4 (176 T). Yet the calculations give 131/444 T for AB|AB and 70/296/28 T or 311/330 T for the four-layer stackings: no common pocket near 170 T, and nothing near 91 T for S2. The paper needs to explain why two different stages give the same dominant frequency. One possibility is that β comes from interface graphene layers with similar hole density in both stages; another is that both samples contain the same majority phase. The authors should say which computed pocket they assign to β in each stage.
   - The 0.1 eV Fermi-level shift. The shift that brings the calculation "into good agreement" is applied only to the ABCA|ABCA case: 27 and 183 T, then 126 and 214 T, against measured 91 and 176 T, which is still a 20-40% mismatch. The S2 result under the same shift is not given. Its sign and size should be tied to an independent quantity, for example the Hall density or a Luttinger count, rather than chosen to match.
   - Harmonic labelling. In Fig. 2g the peak labelled 2β is at 359 T while β = 170 T (2β = 340 T). With a 4-9 T window the frequency resolution is roughly 7 T, so the 19 T discrepancy is outside it. Either the 359 T peak is an independent frequency or the background subtraction or windowing shifts the peaks. The authors should check this and state the field window, the background polynomial order and the frequency uncertainty.
   - What to do: add angle-dependent SdH and T-dependent amplitudes with LK fits on at least one S2 and one S4 sample. Tabulate measured against computed frequencies and masses for both stages, with and without any Fermi-level shift. Moderate the Conclusions accordingly.

B. Should address

3. The DFT treats α-RuCl3 as non-magnetic, without Hubbard U or spin-orbit coupling, yet the paper draws conclusions about its insulating and magnetic character. The Methods specify "assuming a non-magnetic state" and non-spin-polarised GGA. In that approximation α-RuCl3 is metallic, with Ru t2g bands at E_F. The graphene-to-RuCl3 charge transfer, which sets every computed pocket size, is controlled by where these bands sit relative to the Dirac point. The statement that "α-RuCl3 remain[s] rather insulating and Ru atoms keep their magnetic nature" (p. 4) cannot come from this calculation. Earlier work on graphene/α-RuCl3, including refs. 12 and 34 by some of the authors, used GGA+U(+SOC) with magnetic order. The authors should benchmark at least the A|A and AB|AB cases with GGA+U+SOC in a zigzag or ferromagnetic configuration and report how the charge transfer and the pocket frequencies change. Since the paper itself shows that 0.1 eV matters, this is directly relevant to the frequency comparison in concern 2.

4. The chemical identity and stoichiometry of the intercalant are not shown. The paper's evidence for intercalated RuCl3 is the c-axis expansion, the in-plane supercell and a STEM contrast modulation that is described as "qualitative" and lacking "a fully resolved layer-by-layer intercalation pattern" (STEM images are in the SI only). The synthesis also involves Cl2 and Ag/AgCl, and in other GICs metal chlorides can be partially reduced or co-intercalated with Cl. The authors should provide:
   - EDS or EELS line scans across the STEM cross-section showing Ru and Cl in the galleries, with the Ru:Cl ratio and no Ag;
   - a bulk composition measure (mass uptake, EDS or XPS on cleaved surfaces);
   - if possible, Raman spectroscopy, where the graphite G-band splitting is a standard stage diagnostic and RuCl3 phonons would confirm the intercalant's local structure.
   - Separately, the main STEM images belong in the main text, given how much the structural claim relies on them.

5. The "first time" novelty claim needs to be reconciled with the authors' own references. The Conclusions state that graphite was intercalated with α-RuCl3 "for the first time". Ref. 27 (Postnikov et al., 1977, "Synthesis and structure of layer-type graphite compounds with ruthenium, palladium and osmium chlorides") and ref. 30 (Stumpp, 1977) appear to describe RuCl3-graphite compounds. The authors should say what was reported there (stages, c-axis periods) and how the present samples differ, for example in single-crystal quality, quantum oscillations or the stage-4 phase. They should then rephrase the novelty claim.

6. The Hall density is not tied to the SdH pockets or to the DFT charge transfer. The text gives "approximately 7.8 × 10^20 cm^-3 ... from the slope of the transverse resistivity", but does not say which sample, which temperature, or whether ρ_xy is linear in field. In a multi-pocket, partially compensated system a single-slope density is only indicative.
   - A rough Luttinger estimate shows the gap. A 2D pocket of 170 T with four-fold degeneracy gives about 1.6 × 10^13 cm^-2 per graphene layer. With about 6.35 Å per graphene layer in stage 2, that is roughly 2.6 × 10^20 cm^-3, a factor of about 3 below the Hall value.
   - The authors should show ρ_xy(B) for each stage and compare the Hall density with the Luttinger count of the observed pockets and with the Bader or DFT hole count per graphene layer. A two-carrier fit would be appropriate if ρ_xy is nonlinear.

7. The sample statistics and uniformity claims are not documented. The text says measurements "on multiple independently prepared samples reproduced the same FFT peak" and that a second piece of the S2 crystal gave the same frequency. No number of samples, no data and no spread are given, and the S4 reproducibility is not addressed.
   - Fig. 2b shows ρ(T) only for S2, not S4.
   - The zero-field residual resistivities differ by more than an order of magnitude: about 10 μΩ cm for S2 (Fig. 2d) and about 150 μΩ cm for S4 (Fig. 2e). This is larger than the stated geometric uncertainty would seem to allow, and it bears on how uniform and ordered the S4 phase is.
   - The authors should tabulate, in the SI, every sample measured (stage, RRR, frequencies, amplitudes) and add ρ(T) for S4. "Confirming uniform intercalation" should be softened: SdH oscillations come preferentially from the highest-mobility regions and do not exclude minority phases.

8. The control samples are incomplete for the SdH comparison. The Cl2-annealed graphite is shown only in ρ(T). Its magnetoresistance and FFT under the same conditions would show that no 91 T or 170 T frequencies arise from chlorine or AgCl exposure alone, which is a cheap and decisive control. The authors should add it.

9. The Fermi-surface extraction method needs more detail for a quantitative comparison. The unfolded Fermi surfaces are computed on a 6×6×2 k-mesh (Methods). Pocket boundaries are taken "from the highest intensity points" of smeared spectral weight, and the dhva cross-check is done only for A|A. The authors should:
   - give an uncertainty on each computed frequency;
   - extend the dhva or dense-mesh check to AB|AB and the four-layer cases;
   - state at which k_z the extremal orbits are taken. Within a period the graphene bilayers and tetralayers are coupled, so k_z dispersion of the supercell bands may be non-negligible.

C. Minor

10. The DFT lengths should be reconciled with the XRD. The DFT fixes d_Gr-Gr = 3.54 Å, whereas graphite is 3.35 Å, and d_Gr-RuCl3 = 3.60 Å is called the "average experimental value". The authors should say where these experimental values come from and how they match the 9-10 Å gallery height assumed in the XRD analysis and the measured c-axis periods. They should also state whether d_Gr-RuCl3 is measured to the Cl or to the Ru plane, which Fig. 3a leaves ambiguous.

11. The resolution of the Kish graphite FFT is overstated. Fig. 2f resolves peaks at 4.13 T and 5.16 T from oscillations above about 4 T up to 9 T. That window spans about 0.14 T^-1, giving a frequency resolution of about 7 T, so these two peaks cannot be separated by FFT alone. The authors should give the window and method, or fit the oscillations directly.

12. The stacking labels are inconsistent. Fig. 1 caption labels AB|AB as "stage 2: S2", while Table 1 gives A|AB|B for S2. Fig. 1c/d label ABAB|ABAB and ABCA|ABCA as S4, while Table 1 gives AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB. The authors should distinguish clearly between proposed experimental stackings and the simplified computed ones.

13. The Methods describe RuCl3 as "incommensurate with the graphite host" (assumption 1), yet the data are indexed with a commensurate 12×12 graphene / 5×5 RuCl3 supercell (a ≈ 29.6 Å). The authors should clarify which they mean.

14. "Stage 4 ... formed" after 22 h and "stage 2" after 48 h. The authors should state how many growth runs were made and how reproducibly each stage forms. They should also show the evidence for the statement that RuCl3 does not intercalate without excess Cl2.

15. Fig. 2b compares absolute resistivities, while the text cautions that "resistivity values should not be considered absolute". The caveat should go in the caption, or the curves should be normalised to their 300 K values.

16. The Abstract and Introduction say the authors "fully characterize the samples". Given that there is no magnetic, thermodynamic or spectroscopic characterisation and the stacking is only inferred, this wording should be moderated.

17. "First harmonic" is used for the 2ν and 2β peaks. The usual term is "second harmonic", and the wording should be made consistent.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Collect a stacking-sensitive diffraction dataset ((h0l)/(hkl) reflections), or zone-axis cross-sectional STEM with EDS. Use it to fix the stage and stacking rather than inferring them by analogy.
2. Add angle- and temperature-dependent SdH (LK masses, Dingle temperatures) for S2 and S4. Present a single table of measured and computed frequencies and masses per pocket, with the Hall and Luttinger carrier densities alongside.
3. Benchmark the charge transfer and pocket sizes with magnetic GGA+U+SOC for RuCl3, and remove statements about RuCl3's magnetic or insulating state that the non-magnetic calculation cannot support.
4. Since the title advertises a platform for "exotic quantum phases", include at least a magnetisation or specific-heat measurement showing whether the intercalated RuCl3 orders magnetically, or retains the about 7 K zigzag order of bulk α-RuCl3. Alternatively, temper the framing.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
