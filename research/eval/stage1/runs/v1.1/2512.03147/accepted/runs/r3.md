Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports chemical vapour transport growth of graphite intercalated with RuCl3, using AgCl as a Cl2 source. It assigns stage 2 and stage 4 phases from X-ray diffraction, reports Shubnikov–de Haas (SdH) frequencies of about 170 T, plus 91 T in the stage-4 sample, that differ strongly from pristine graphite, and compares them with unfolded DFT Fermi surfaces of graphene/RuCl3 supercells. A bulk, three-dimensional counterpart of the much-studied graphene/α-RuCl3 heterostructures would be a useful materials platform, and the synthesis recipe is the most concrete contribution. However, the identity of the intercalant, the stacking assignment and the link between the measured frequencies and particular stackings are much less firmly established than the abstract and conclusions state ("comprehensive characterization", "we determined the crystal structure", "platform for exotic quantum phases").

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A reproducible-sounding growth route (two-zone CVT with photolysed AgCl for Cl2 overpressure), including the useful negative result that RuCl3 does not intercalate without excess Cl2.
- Large c-axis expansions (25.4 Å and 58.2 Å) that fit stage-2 and stage-4 identity periods for a gallery of about 9.4 Å (12.7 Å × 2 and 19.4 Å × 3). This is good evidence that intercalation occurred.
- Clear SdH oscillations in the intercalated samples, with frequencies 20–40 times those of the Kish graphite reference, which shows a strongly modified Fermi surface. A Cl2-annealed graphite control is included for the resistivity.
- A DFT analysis that captures the qualitative physics: hole doping of graphene, charge depletion concentrated on the interface layers, and a number of K-point hole pockets that depends on stacking.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The intercalant's chemical identity and phase are not shown. The title, abstract and conclusions state that α-RuCl3 is intercalated, "for the first time". The evidence is the c-axis expansion and an in-plane supercell indexed by analogy to FeCl3/CrCl3/AlCl3 GICs (Methods, assumptions 1–4). No measurement of composition is reported: no EDS/EELS in STEM, no XPS, no chemical analysis of Ru:Cl:C, and no check for Ag or AgCl residues from the Cl2 source. The growth runs in a Cl2-rich atmosphere at 830–900 °C, so the guest could plausibly be RuCl3 with extra Cl, another Ru chloride, or a mixture. Calling it the honeycomb α-polytype also needs in-plane evidence: in-plane reflections indexed to the RuCl3 sublattice, or atomic-resolution STEM of the gallery. The supplementary STEM is described as showing only "alternating contrast" and "qualitative evidence", with no fully resolved layer-by-layer pattern. On novelty, Ref. 27 (Postnikov et al., 1977, "graphite compounds with ruthenium … chlorides") seems to describe ruthenium chloride–graphite compounds already. The "for the first time" claim must be reconciled with it.
   What to do: add elemental analysis (STEM-EDS/EELS maps across galleries, or XPS for the Ru oxidation state and Ru:Cl ratio). Give a direct count of carbon layers between guest layers in STEM, or Raman G-band splitting, which is the standard probe of GIC stage. State what distinguishes this work from Ref. 27.

2. The quantum oscillations do not support stacking-specific conclusions, and the DFT agreement depends on a tuned Fermi level. The conclusion that "the quantum oscillations response is governed by the stacking sequence" (p. 5) faces several problems:
   (a) The stage-2 and stage-4 samples show almost the same dominant frequency (β = 170 T and 176 T, Fig. 2g,h), although DFT predicts very different pocket sets for the two stages. One explanation is that both signals come from the same phase, or from a minority phase common to both crystals. XRD itself reports residual graphite, and the paper notes mixed staging at longer growth times.
   (b) For stage 2, the unshifted DFT gives 131 T and 444 T, while experiment shows only 170 T. A 444 T peak would lie well inside the FFT window and is not seen. The text does not resolve this discrepancy, and no Fermi-level shift is reported for AB|AB.
   (c) For stage 4, agreement is reached only after a rigid 0.1 eV shift, chosen after the fact. That shift turns 70/296 T into 27/183 T, and 28/311/330 T into 126/214 T. The statement that "only a limited range of physically reasonable parameters … reproduces the observed frequencies" is not backed by a parameter scan. With one free shift and several candidate stackings, getting two numbers within about 20% is weak discrimination. The 91 T α peak is explained as "sample-dependent", but its absence elsewhere is not documented.
   What to do: report how many samples of each stage were measured, with FFTs for each. Add the field-angle dependence F(θ) to show that the pockets are quasi-2D (F ∝ 1/cos θ), as the graphene-pocket model assumes. Fit Lifshitz–Kosevich temperature dependence to obtain cyclotron masses, and compare them with the DFT masses of each candidate pocket; this is far more discriminating than frequency alone, especially for flat-band ABC stacking. Show F versus Fermi-level shift for all stackings, including AB|AB. Otherwise, reduce the stacking-identification claims to a statement of qualitative consistency.

B. Should address

3. The electronic structure method may not suit RuCl3. The calculations are non-spin-polarized GGA, with no Hubbard U and no spin–orbit coupling, in a "non-magnetic state". Without U, the Ru t2g bands of α-RuCl3 are metallic at E_F, so both the size of the graphene→RuCl3 charge transfer and the position of E_F relative to the graphene Dirac point, which set the pocket sizes and hence the SdH frequencies, may be unreliable. Earlier graphene/α-RuCl3 work (e.g. Refs. 12, 34) used GGA+U(+SOC) with magnetic order for this reason. The text also says Ru atoms keep "their magnetic nature" (p. 4), which a non-magnetic calculation cannot show. Please repeat at least the A|A and one stage-4 calculation with GGA+U+SOC and zigzag or ferromagnetic order, and report how the doping shift (0.68/0.48 eV) and the computed frequencies change. The paper should also state the Bader charge per formula unit in the main text.

4. The structural model is internally inconsistent and some conclusions are stated more firmly than the data allow. Methods assumption 1 says RuCl3 is incommensurate with graphite. Yet the measured a = 29.62 Å is close to both 12 × a_graphene (29.5 Å) and 5 × a_RuCl3 (about 29.9 Å), and the text describes the structure as an N1×M1 / N2×M2 commensurate superlattice. All observed single-crystal peaks are (hk0), and the powder data are (00l). Neither probes the registry of graphene layers relative to each other or to the guest, so the specific stackings in Table 1 (A|AB|B; AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB) follow from assumption 4, not from the measurement. Please state which parts of Table 1 are measured and which are assumed, resolve the commensurate/incommensurate contradiction, and moderate "we determined the crystal structure". Mixed-index (hkl, l ≠ 0) reflections, or STEM along a zone axis that resolves graphene registry, would be needed to fix the stacking.

5. The Hall density is not compared with the SdH pockets or with DFT. The paper reports n ≈ 7.8 × 10^20 cm^-3 from a single-band slope, but does not say which sample it comes from or whether the Hall response is linear in field. For orientation: a 170 T pocket with spin and valley degeneracy holds about 1.6 × 10^13 cm^-2 per graphene layer. With four graphene layers per 25.4 Å in stage 2, this gives about 2.6 × 10^20 cm^-3, roughly three times below the Hall value. Graphite-derived systems also carry electrons and holes. Please show ρxy(B), state the sample, and compare the Hall density with the Luttinger count from the SdH frequencies and with the DFT charge transfer. Such a comparison would directly test the doping level that sets the frequencies.

6. The "platform for exotic quantum phases" claim has no magnetic or low-temperature characterization behind it. The title and abstract promise correlated and topological physics, but no magnetization, susceptibility or specific heat data are shown. The basic question for a Kitaev-material intercalant is whether RuCl3 in the galleries still orders, near 7 K or otherwise. Even M(T) on the stage-2 and stage-4 crystals would anchor the claim. Without such data, the title and conclusions should present the work as a synthesis-and-Fermi-surface study.

7. The uniformity and phase-purity evidence is thin. The argument that a sharp SdH peak implies uniform intercalation (p. 3) is weak, because SdH is dominated by the highest-mobility regions. The paper does not show the residual-graphite fraction, rocking curves, or the volume fractions of mixed stages. Please quantify the phase fractions from the XRD patterns (Fig. S1) and give the number of independently grown batches and crystals measured.

8. The strained-cell approximation is benchmarked only for A|A. The 4% tensile strain on RuCl3 in the 5 × 5 / 2 × 2 cell is validated against the 12 × 12 / 5 × 5 cell only for single-layer A|A, and only through charge transfer. The quantitative frequency comparison relies on small energy differences (0.1 eV shifts produce changes of more than 100 T). Please show that the band shifts of the strained cell agree with the large cell to well below 0.1 eV.

C. Minor

9. Harmonic labels are not self-consistent. 2β is labelled 359 T for β = 170 T (expected 340 T), 2ν1 is 9.3 T for ν1 = 4.13 T (expected 8.26 T), and 347 T is given for β = 176 T. Please state the FFT field window, windowing and frequency resolution, and either fix the values or explain the differences.

10. The Kish graphite frequencies (4.13 T and 5.16 T) are called "in good agreement" with the literature. Please quote the literature values; the commonly cited graphite frequencies are about 4.6–4.7 T and 6.4–6.5 T.

11. Table 1 footnote: "α-CuI3" should read α-RuCl3.

12. Fig. 2b: the RRR and the absolute ρ are acknowledged to be uncertain. Plotting ρ/ρ(300 K) alongside the absolute values would make the comparison between samples cleaner. The Cl2-annealed graphite control should also be shown in magnetotransport (SdH), not only in ρ(T), to rule out Cl-only doping as the source of β.

13. The two-terminal versus four-terminal geometry, contact placement on flaky samples, and the excitation current should be stated, as well as the field-alignment accuracy relative to c.

14. Fig. 3b: the axis label and caption refer to d_Ru, while the text uses d_Gr–RuCl3. Please harmonize them. Also state the dhva computation for stacking geometries other than A|A, or explain why the A|A cross-check is enough.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add STEM-EDS/EELS and Raman (G-band splitting) to pin down the intercalant composition and the stage. These are inexpensive and standard for GICs.
- Measure the angle and temperature dependence of the SdH oscillations to obtain the pocket dimensionality and the cyclotron masses, and compare the masses with the DFT predictions for ABAB versus ABCA stacking.
- Redo the key DFT cases with GGA+U+SOC and magnetic order, and present a systematic frequency-versus-E_F map for all candidate stackings.
- Measure M(T) to test whether RuCl3 magnetism survives in the intercalated galleries, which bears directly on the "exotic quantum phases" framing.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
