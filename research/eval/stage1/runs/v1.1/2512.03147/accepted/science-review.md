A. Must address

1. The data do not support the claimed agreement between the measured SdH frequencies and the stacking-dependent DFT Fermi surfaces, nor the statement that "the quantum oscillation response is governed by the stacking sequence" (Fig. 2g,h; Fig. 5; p. 5; Conclusions, "consistent with experimental SdH frequencies"). The numbers do not match. (a) Stage 2: the AB|AB model gives 131 T and 444 T, while experiment shows one fundamental at 170 T. A 444 T peak would lie well inside the FFT window and is not seen, nothing near 91 T is predicted, and no Fermi-level-shifted calculation is reported for AB|AB. (b) Stage 4: the unshifted models give 70/296 T (ABAB|ABAB) or 28/311/330 T (ABCA|ABCA). Rough agreement appears only after a rigid 0.1 eV Fermi-level shift chosen after the fact, which gives 27/183 T or 126/214 T. Against the measured 91 and 176 T this is still a 20–40% mismatch, and neither stacking reproduces both frequencies. The statement that "only a limited range of physically reasonable parameters … reproduces the observed frequencies" is not backed by a parameter scan. With one free shift and several candidate stackings, matching two numbers to within about 20% discriminates weakly. (c) The dominant frequency is essentially the same in stage 2 (170 T) and stage 4 (176 T). With a field window of roughly 4–9 T (Δ(1/B) ≈ 0.14 T⁻¹, resolution about 7 T) the two cannot be distinguished. Yet DFT predicts quite different pocket sets for the two stages, with no common pocket near 170 T. A stage-independent β is the observation that most needs explaining. It could come from the interface-adjacent graphene layers, whose hole density is set locally by RuCl3 and would be similar in both stages, or from a majority or minority phase common to both crystals (the XRD shows residual graphite, and mixed staging is reported at longer growth times). It does not point to stacking control. (d) The 91 T α peak is called "sample-dependent", but its absence in other samples is not documented. (e) The oscillations are shown only for B ∥ c at a single temperature. The authors should: give each measured frequency with its uncertainty, field window and number of periods; measure F(θ) on at least one stage-2 and one stage-4 sample, to show the pockets are quasi-2D (F ∝ 1/cos θ) as the graphene-pocket model assumes, which residual graphite would not be; extract cyclotron masses and Dingle temperatures from Lifshitz–Kosevich fits and compare the masses with the DFT band masses of each candidate pocket, which discriminates far better than frequencies alone, including between Dirac-like interface pockets, bilayer-like pockets and flat-band ABC stacking; state which computed pocket is assigned to β in each stage; tabulate measured against computed frequencies and masses for both stages, with and without any shift, and show F versus Fermi-level shift for all stackings, including AB|AB; and tie the sign and size of any shift to an independent quantity such as the Hall density or a Luttinger count rather than to the data being fitted. If this cannot be done, reduce the conclusions to qualitative consistency: the calculations give the expected size of the hole pockets, but they do not identify the stacking or assign the frequencies one to one.

2. The chemical identity and stoichiometry of the intercalant are not shown, although the title, abstract and conclusions state that α-RuCl3 is intercalated. The evidence is the c-axis expansion, an in-plane supercell indexed by analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 GICs (Methods, assumptions 1–4), and Supplementary STEM described as showing only "alternating contrast" and "qualitative evidence", with no "fully resolved layer-by-layer intercalation pattern". No composition measurement is reported: no STEM-EDS or EELS, no XPS, no chemical analysis of Ru:Cl:C, no mass uptake, and no check for Ag or AgCl residues from the Cl2 source. Growth runs in a Cl2-rich atmosphere at 830–900 °C, and metal chlorides in other GICs can be partially reduced or co-intercalated with Cl. The guest could therefore be RuCl3 with extra Cl, another Ru chloride, a Ru-deficient layer, a Cl-only or Ag-containing intercalant, or a mixture. Calling it the honeycomb α-polytype also needs in-plane evidence: reflections indexed to the RuCl3 sublattice, or atomic-resolution STEM of the gallery. The authors should add elemental line scans or maps across the galleries in the existing cross-sectional STEM data, showing Ru and Cl, the Ru:Cl ratio and no Ag; a bulk composition measure (mass uptake, EDS or XPS on cleaved surfaces, with the Ru oxidation state from XPS); and, where possible, Raman spectroscopy, where G-band splitting is the standard stage diagnostic and RuCl3 phonons would confirm the guest's local structure. A direct count of carbon layers between guest layers in STEM would also support the stage assignment. The main STEM images belong in the main text, given how much the structural claim relies on them.

3. The stacking sequences in Table 1 and Fig. 1 are assumed, not measured, and the stage assignment is shown only in outline. Powder data were indexed only on (00l) reflections, and all single-crystal reflections were (hk0) "due to the large c-axis and limited sample oscillation angle". Neither set is sensitive to the lateral registry (A, B or C positions) of the graphene or RuCl3 layers. The stackings A|AB|B for stage 2 and AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB for stage 4 therefore follow from assumption 4 (analogy with other chloride GICs), not from the data. Yet the Conclusions say the authors "determined the crystal structure and showed that several different stackings can be achieved", the rhombohedral (ABCA) versus Bernal (ABAB) distinction is what links the material to flat-band physics, and the stacking drives the DFT comparison in concern 1. The DFT also replaces the proposed sequences with idealised ones (ABAB|ABAB and ABCA|ABCA for stage 4). The authors should: show the indexed (00l) pattern with the identity period I_c = d_i + (n−1)·3.35 Å worked out for each stage, relative intensities or an intensity fit against stage-1 to stage-4 models, and the fractions of residual graphite and mixed stages (main text, not only Fig. S1); obtain stacking-sensitive evidence, either off-axis (h0l)/(hkl) reflections from a dataset with a larger oscillation range or cross-sectional STEM along a zone axis where the candidate registries differ, compared with simulated images; and otherwise present the stackings as a model assumption throughout, state plainly that the stage-4 stacking is unknown and that the two computed stackings bracket it only if the stacking-insensitivity argument holds, and remove "determined the crystal structure" and "several different stackings can be achieved". The text should state which parts of Table 1 are measured and which assumed.

B. Should address

4. The DFT treats RuCl3 as non-magnetic, with non-spin-polarised GGA and no Hubbard U or spin–orbit coupling (Methods, "assuming a non-magnetic state"). In this approximation α-RuCl3 is metallic, with Ru t2g bands at E_F. Both the size of the graphene→RuCl3 charge transfer and the position of E_F relative to the graphene Dirac point, which set every computed pocket size and hence the SdH frequencies, depend on U and magnetic order. The 0.1 eV shift needed in concern 1 is of the size such choices produce. Earlier graphene/α-RuCl3 work, including refs. 12 and 34 by some of the authors, used GGA+U(+SOC) with magnetic order for this reason. The statement that "α-RuCl3 remain[s] rather insulating and Ru atoms keep their magnetic nature" (p. 4) cannot come from a non-magnetic calculation and should be removed or backed up. The authors should repeat at least the A|A and AB|AB cells, and one stage-4 cell, with GGA+U+SOC in zigzag or ferromagnetic order, and report how the doping shift (0.68/0.48 eV), the charge transfer and the computed frequencies change. The Bader charge per formula unit should be given in the main text.

5. The Hall density is not reconciled with the SdH pockets or with the DFT charge transfer (p. 3). The single-carrier value of about 7.8 × 10²⁰ cm⁻³ "from the slope of the transverse resistivity" is given without the carrier sign, the sample (stage), the temperature, or whether ρ_xy is linear in field. In a multi-pocket, possibly compensated graphite-derived system a single slope is only indicative. A rough Luttinger count shows the gap: a 170 T pocket (k_F ≈ 0.072 Å⁻¹) with spin and valley degeneracy holds about 1.6 × 10¹³ cm⁻² per graphene layer, which gives about 2.6 × 10²⁰ cm⁻³ for stage 2 (about 6.35 Å per graphene layer) if every layer carries one such pocket, a factor of about 3 below the Hall value. The difference may come from unobserved pockets, multiband compensation or inequivalent layers, but the paper should show ρ_xy(B) for each stage, state the positive (hole) sign explicitly since hole doping is a central DFT prediction, use a two-carrier fit if ρ_xy is nonlinear, and compare the Hall density with the Luttinger count of the observed pockets and with the DFT/Bader hole count per graphene layer (Fig. 3, Fig. S4). This is a direct, model-light test of the charge transfer that sets the frequencies.

6. Phase purity, sample statistics and uniformity are not documented (pp. 2–3). The text says "multiple independently prepared samples reproduced the same FFT peak" and that a second piece of the stage-2 crystal gave the same frequency, but gives no number of samples, no data and no spread, and does not address stage-4 reproducibility. This conflicts with the "sample-dependent absence of the α frequency". The text also mentions "a small portion of residual pure graphite" and "mixed stage intercalation and/or disordered compounds" after 48 h, without quantifying either. Fig. 2b shows ρ(T) only for stage 2, and the zero-field residual resistivities differ by more than an order of magnitude (about 10 μΩ cm for stage 2 in Fig. 2d against about 150 μΩ cm for stage 4 in Fig. 2e), more than the stated geometric uncertainty would seem to allow; this bears on how ordered the stage-4 phase is. The argument that a sharp SdH peak confirms "uniform intercalation", or that mixed staging "would be expected to broaden or complicate the oscillation spectrum", is weak, because SdH comes preferentially from the highest-mobility regions and does not exclude minority phases. The authors should state how many batches and crystals of each stage were measured by XRD and by transport and whether these were the same pieces; estimate the graphite and mixed-stage fractions from integrated 00l intensities (and rocking curves if available); tabulate in the SI every sample measured (stage, RRR, frequencies, amplitudes) with its FFT; add ρ(T) for stage 4; and soften "confirming uniform intercalation".

9. The title and abstract claim more than the data show: "platform for exotic quantum phases", "access to novel correlated and topological states", "fully characterize the samples" and "comprehensive characterization". There is no magnetic, thermodynamic or spectroscopic characterisation, no evidence of flat-band or correlation effects in the graphene layers, the calculations are non-magnetic, and the stacking is only inferred. The basic question for a Kitaev-material intercalant is whether the RuCl3 in the galleries still orders magnetically, near the about 7 K zigzag transition of bulk α-RuCl3 or otherwise. Magnetisation M(T) or specific heat on the stage-2 and stage-4 crystals would substantially support the platform framing. Without such data, the title and conclusions should present the work as a synthesis and Fermi-surface study, and "fully characterize" should be removed.

10. The in-plane lattice description is internally inconsistent (Table 1; Methods). Assumption 1 says RuCl3 is "incommensurate with the graphite host", yet the measured a ≈ 29.6 Å is indexed as a commensurate N1×M1 / N2×M2 supercell, close to both 12 × a_graphene (29.5 Å) and 5 × a_RuCl3 (about 29.9 Å). 11. The DFT interlayer distances are not reconciled with the XRD. The calculations fix d_Gr–Gr = 3.54 Å (graphite: 3.35 Å) and d_Gr–RuCl3 = 3.60 Å, called the "average experimental value" without a source. With a Cl–Ru–Cl slab of about 2.7–2.8 Å, the measured repeats of 12.7 Å (stage 2) and 19.4 Å (stage 4), and the 9–10 Å gallery height assumed in the XRD analysis, suggest a somewhat smaller graphene–RuCl3 separation, and the paper states that the pockets are "highly sensitive" to this distance. The authors should give the source of the experimental values, the c-axis repeat of each relaxed model compared with the measured repeats, and whether d_Gr–RuCl3 is measured to the Cl or to the Ru plane, which Fig. 3a leaves ambiguous.

12. The Fermi-surface extraction needs more detail for a quantitative comparison. The unfolded Fermi surfaces use a 6×6×2 k-mesh, pocket boundaries are taken "from the highest intensity points" of smeared spectral weight, and the dhva cross-check is only mentioned, and only for A|A. 14. Harmonic assignments and FFT details. In Fig. 2g, 2β is at 359 T while 2 × 170 T = 340 T; for β = 176 T the 2β peak is given as 347 T (expected 352 T); and 2ν1 = 9.3 T for Kish graphite against 2 × 4.13 = 8.26 T. With a frequency resolution of about 7 T the 19 T discrepancy in stage 2 is outside the resolution, so the 359 T peak may be an independent frequency, which matters because DFT predicts a second stage-2 pocket, or the background subtraction or windowing shifts the peaks. The authors should check this and state, for each FFT, the field window, windowing function, background polynomial order, frequency uncertainty, and whether the windows were the same for all samples. The 2ν and 2β peaks are second harmonics, not "first harmonic"; the wording should be made consistent.
