A. Must address

1. The momentum used for the "dispersion" is not the momentum the excitation experiences, and the measured kinematics do not obviously match an intraband continuum. In Fig. 3a, (q∥, q⊥) runs from (0.05, 0.14) to (0.16, 0.02) Å⁻¹, so |Q| stays at roughly 0.13–0.16 Å⁻¹ across the series, near the 2k_photon ≈ 0.19 Å⁻¹ limit at 190 eV. The shift plotted against q∥ in Fig. 3b,e is therefore mostly a rotation of Q from near the c axis to near in-plane at nearly fixed |Q|, not an in-plane dispersion at fixed q⊥. This matters for the continuum assignment. The paper's own sum-rule argument (p. 5) confines the intraband continuum to ω ≤ v_F·q, vanishing as q → 0. Yet the extracted peak sits at about 0.4 eV at q∥ = 0.05 Å⁻¹, and the linear fit in Fig. 3e extrapolates to a finite intercept of about 0.3 eV at q∥ = 0. With the σ in-plane Fermi velocity quoted on p. 2 (≈ 2 eV Å), the σ intraband edge at q∥ = 0.05 Å⁻¹ is only about 0.1 eV, a factor of about four below the observed peak. A peak with a finite intercept lying above v_F·q is the typical signature of an interband transition or a collective mode, not a gapless Lindhard continuum. The feature can only be intraband if the large q⊥ component and the strongly c-axis-dispersing π bands (t_pz,z = 1 eV, Eq. 18; Fig. 1c, Fig. S1) are included, in which case the comparison with the σ Fermi velocity is the wrong one. The χ⁰ of Fig. 3d,e, the RPA χ of Fig. 4 and the decomposition of Fig. 4c (labelled q∥ = 0.088 Å⁻¹) appear to be computed for purely in-plane q along Γ–M, and the tight-binding model deviates from DFT along A–L–H (Fig. S3), exactly where k_z matters. The authors should state 2θ and the full Q vector for every spectrum; recompute χ⁰, the RPA χ and the decomposition at the actual (q∥, q⊥) of each spectrum, so that theory and experiment use the same q; replot the measured peak position against |Q| and/or both components; overlay on Fig. 3e the computed continuum boundaries ω = v_F^band(q̂)·|q| for each σ and π Fermi-surface sheet, stating which band and direction set the expected edge, and show that every measured point lies inside the intraband continuum; report the fitted intercept with its uncertainty; and, if the spectrometer allows, add a series at fixed q∥ with different q⊥ (different 2θ) or at fixed momentum direction, to separate geometry from momentum.

2. The apparent dispersion may be a geometry-dependent shift of orbital weight rather than a true q-dependence. Changing θ_in changes q∥ and q⊥, and it also changes the incident polarization and hence the cos⁴θ versus sin⁴θ weighting of pσ and pπ (Eqs. 32–33). The authors' own decomposition (Fig. 4c, q∥ = 0.088 Å⁻¹) shows the σσ intraband weight peaking near 0.35–0.4 eV and the ππ intraband weight near 0.7 eV. As θ_in moves from grazing to normal incidence, the relative weight of these two components shifts, which alone could move the centre of a single Voigt fit from about 0.4 to about 0.6 eV with no intrinsic dispersion in either component. The text attributes the intensity variation to "out-of-plane components of the X-ray polarization" (p. 3) but does not separate this effect from the energy shift. Since the linear dispersion in Fig. 3e is the main experimental evidence for a particle-hole origin, the authors should show, with the BSE or RPA model, what peak shift results from the polarization change alone at fixed Q, and if possible measure at fixed geometry with Q varied by another route (2θ), or use σ versus π incident polarization at fixed θ_in.

3. No signature specific to a continuum has been shown, and alternative assignments are not excluded. The defining properties of the Lindhard continuum are spectral weight extending from ω = 0 (Im χ⁰ ∝ ω at low ω), an upper edge at v_F·q, and a width that grows linearly with q. The data are instead fitted with a single symmetric Voigt peak (Fig. S2), only peak centres are reported, and in Fig. 3a the lineshape looks like a peak of roughly constant width. Two alternatives give a dispersing low-energy peak in this window: (a) the acoustic σ–π plasmon of MgB2 predicted by Silkin et al. (Ref. 12, cited but not discussed), which is near-linearly dispersing, sharper than a continuum, and out of phase between orbital sectors so that an orbital-selective probe would see it; and (b) interband transitions between the two split σ bands near Γ/A, which have finite energy at q → 0 and so match the finite intercept. The "conclusive" intra/interband separation (p. 4, Fig. 4c) is done on the tight-binding χ⁰ at a single momentum, with a bare charge vertex and no RIXS matrix elements, not on the BSE RIXS calculation that actually reproduces the data; the tight-binding χ⁰ itself misses the measured dispersion at low q. The weakness of σσ interband weight may also be an artifact of the labelling problem in concern 5. The projection in Fig. 2c is of the X-ray absorption exciton (the intermediate state, Eqs. 2–3), not the final valence excitation, so it does not identify the final-state particle-hole pairs. The authors should: report the fitted width and, ideally, the low-energy onset after elastic subtraction as a function of Q and compare with the predicted continuum width; fit with a model continuum lineshape (for example Im χ⁰ convolved with the 100 meV resolution) and compare the asymmetric lineshape noted on p. 3 quantitatively with it; perform the intra/interband and σ/π decomposition inside the BSE RIXS calculation, for example by restricting the bands in the intermediate and final states, at several momenta including the lowest; analyse the RPA χ of Fig. 4a for poles and compare explicitly with the acoustic-plasmon dispersion of Ref. 12; and say which observable rules out each alternative. Until then, the statement that the analysis "conclusively demonstrates" an intraband origin (p. 4) is not supported.

B. Should address

4. There is no incident-energy dependence. All data are taken at a single incident energy, 187 eV, on a sharp pre-edge that resonates into unoccupied states just above E_F, and the feature lies within a few times the 100 meV resolution of the elastic line. Fluorescence-like emission or resonance-dependent intermediate-state effects can appear at small loss and shift with incident energy. The BSE agreement partly addresses this, but the authors should show Ω_i–ω RIXS maps across the 187 eV pre-edge to establish that the 0.4–0.6 eV feature is Raman-like, at constant energy loss.

5. The momentum scale or the intra/interband labelling of the tight-binding calculations looks inconsistent with known Fermi velocities. In Fig. 4c (q∥ = 0.088 Å⁻¹), the "σσ,intra" weight extends to about 0.5–0.6 eV and the "ππ,intra" weight to about 1.5 eV, which would require velocities of about 6–7 and 17 eV Å, far above the ≈ 2 eV Å quoted for σ and above typical DFT values for π. Either the "intra/inter" labels follow band-index ordering, so that pairs of different σ bands (light and heavy hole) or crossing bands count as "intra", or the q used in the calculation is not the q∥ on the axis (the Methods say momenta are "given in reciprocal lattice units" while the figures use Å⁻¹, so a 2π or r.l.u. conversion may be off). If the momentum axis of Fig. 3d and Fig. 4 is off by a constant factor, the agreement in Fig. 3e changes. The authors should check the q units and redo the Fig. 4c decomposition with a physical criterion for intra- versus interband, for example eigenvector continuity or overlap between k and k+q, showing the σ–σ interband contribution separately.

6. The theory–experiment comparison is not quantitative, and the wording overstates it. The χ⁰ peak positions (Fig. 3e, blue triangles) sit at about 0.15–0.2 eV at the lowest q versus about 0.4 eV measured, a deviation of up to about 0.2 eV (a factor of two) at the three lowest momenta, and the slopes differ clearly; Fig. 3d is acknowledged to peak "at a larger value of q". The BSE peaks (squares) also fall systematically below the data at low q. Yet the abstract claims "quantitative agreement with simulations of the non-interacting charge susceptibility", the text calls the agreement "close" and the decomposition "conclusive" (p. 4), and the low-q discrepancy is dismissed as a region "where the spectral weight is split". The small-q region is exactly where a gapless continuum and a gapped excitation are most clearly distinguished (concern 1), so it should be discussed explicitly. The authors should give quantitative measures (peak positions, widths and lineshapes at several q overlaid on the data), discuss the residual discrepancies, compare the upper edge and lineshape rather than a Voigt centre, and moderate the abstract claim to the BSE agreement, which is the closer one.

8. The peak extraction and error bars are under-specified. The feature is described as "markedly asymmetric", yet positions come from symmetric Voigt components (Fig. S2), the elastic line and a low-energy shoulder near 0.1 eV (visible in Fig. 2b and Fig. S2) are fitted separately, and in Fig. 3a the red fits visibly miss the rising edge near 0.1–0.3 eV at several momenta. The authors should state which parameters were free or fixed (elastic width, Lorentzian/Gaussian ratio, background), how the elastic line and any phonon contribution (the E2g mode near 70 meV) were handled, and how the error bars in Fig. 3e were obtained (fit covariance, repeated scans, resolution), since they grow strongly with q without explanation. 9. The orbitally weighted RPA model (Fig. 4a, Eqs. 20, 30, 32–33) needs justification. The SI sets V_ab,cd(q) = V(q) for all band indices (Eq. 30), coupling interband densities ρ_ab (a ≠ b) to the long-range Coulomb interaction with full weight although their q → 0 form factors should vanish; this can redistribute low-energy weight artificially. V(q) = U/q² with U = 2 eV Å and q in Å⁻¹ is dimensionally inconsistent for a 3D Coulomb interaction (a volume factor is missing); it is isotropic in an anisotropic material, without local-field effects or c-axis momentum dependence. U is tuned to a GPAW plasmon near 3 eV, yet the statement that "the low-energy spectrum is unaffected by the choice of U" is surprising given that the central argument is that screening suppresses intraband weight, and it is exactly what would distinguish a continuum from an acoustic plasmon (concern 3). The cos⁴/sin⁴ weights include only the incident polarization, not the unanalysed outgoing polarization, and the link between a 1s → 2p RIXS cross-section with a core hole and a partial charge susceptibility is assumed, not derived. The authors should show RPA results for two or three values of U, use proper band-basis Coulomb matrix elements, state the relation of the orbital-weighted χ to the BSE cross-section, and show the RPA–data agreement quantitatively, for example by overlaying RPA peak positions on Fig. 3e.

11. Sample characterization, surface state and reproducibility are not documented. The sample is only described as an HPCVD film grown as in Ref. 24, with no thickness, substrate, c-axis orientation, surface preparation, residual resistivity or Tc given, and only one sample appears to have been measured. B K-edge soft X-rays at grazing geometry are surface-sensitive, and boron oxides give a strong B₂O₃ π* resonance near 194 eV; the strong peak near 193–194 eV in the TFY of Fig. 2a is underestimated by the BSE and all functionals in Fig. S3, and may contain such a contribution. 12. The novelty claim and the EELS argument are framed too strongly. The statements that the intraband continuum "has remained experimentally elusive", that "no feature resembling the textbook illustration has been reported", and that EELS "cannot see" low-energy intraband excitations (p. 5) should be set against existing work: non-resonant IXS and EELS observations of the particle-hole continuum in simple metals at q comparable to or above the plasmon cutoff; Ref. 21, which reports EELS separating intra- and interband features in MgB2; electronic Raman scattering, which probes the intraband continuum at q ≈ 0 and has been studied in MgB2; M-EELS; optical Drude-tail measurements; and RIXS observations of particle-hole continua in doped transition-metal oxides. The sum-rule argument (Eq. 1) is asymptotic in small q, shows that the continuum's weight in the loss function is suppressed rather than absent, weakens as q approaches k_F, and has known exceptions in multiband metals with acoustic modes. The authors should discuss these works, qualify the claims to "at small q, momentum-resolved", and say precisely what is new: the momentum resolution, the intraband origin, or the metal studied.
