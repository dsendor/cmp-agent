Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report a low-energy (≈0.3–0.6 eV) dispersive feature in boron K-edge RIXS of MgB2, taken at the sharp 187 eV pre-edge resonance, and identify it as the intra-band particle-hole (Lindhard) continuum. They support this with OCEAN/BSE RIXS simulations, a tight-binding Lindhard and orbitally weighted RPA calculation, and a sum-rule argument for why EELS cannot see this continuum while orbital-selective RIXS can. If the assignment holds, this is the first momentum-resolved spectroscopic observation of the intra-band continuum in a metal. It would also establish ultra-soft, low-q RIXS as a probe of Fermi-surface kinematics, which is of broad interest. As it stands, the assignment depends on how the momentum transfer is defined and on model decompositions that need tightening.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A new, clearly resolved low-energy RIXS feature in a well-characterised metal, with good statistics (Fig. 3a, Fig. S2) and a systematic shift with momentum (Fig. 3b,e).
- The ab initio BSE simulations of the full RIXS cross-section (Fig. 2b, Fig. 3c) reproduce the energy, the dispersion and the intensity suppression at grazing geometry without free parameters beyond a global energy shift and broadening. This is a strong independent check.
- The projection of the BSE exciton density onto the band structure (Fig. 2c) puts the intermediate states in the σ bands near Γ/A, which makes a σ-derived low-energy excitation physically reasonable.
- The sum-rule and orbital-selectivity argument (Eq. 1, Supplement G, Fig. S4) is simple and pedagogically useful. It explains why the intra-band continuum is hidden in the screened total-charge response and can reappear in a partial (orbital) susceptibility.
- The topic connects to open questions, such as continua in strange metals (Ref. 23), and the paper sets out a falsifiable programme.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The momentum variable used for the "dispersion" is not the momentum the excitation experiences.
   - What is shown: Fig. 3a labels each spectrum with (q∥, q⊥). These run from (0.05, 0.14) to (0.16, 0.02) Å⁻¹. The total |q| is therefore nearly constant, about 0.15–0.16 Å⁻¹, across the whole series. Changing θ_in mainly rotates q from nearly c-axis to nearly in-plane, and at the same time changes the polarization and hence the orbital selectivity.
   - The problem: the shift plotted against q∥ in Fig. 3b,e, and the "speed of ≈1.4 eV Å" compared with the in-plane σ Fermi velocity, are therefore not an in-plane dispersion at fixed q⊥. They are a mix of directional (anisotropy) and polarization effects at roughly fixed |q|.
   - Kinematics: this matters for the continuum assignment. The intra-band continuum must vanish as q → 0, but the linear fit in Fig. 3e extrapolates to a finite intercept of roughly 0.3 eV at q∥ = 0. With v_F^σ ≈ 2 eV Å (p. 2), the intra-band upper edge at q∥ = 0.05 Å⁻¹ is only about 0.1 eV, well below the observed peak at about 0.4 eV. The feature can only be intra-band if the large q⊥ component and the strongly c-axis-dispersing π bands (t_pz,z = 1 eV, Eq. 18) are included. In that case the comparison with "the Fermi velocity of the σ bands" is the wrong one.
   - Calculations: it is not stated whether the χ⁰ in Fig. 3d,e and Fig. 4, and the decomposition in Fig. 4c (labelled q∥ = 0.088 Å⁻¹), include the experimental q⊥ at each point. The axes suggest purely in-plane q.
   - What the authors should do:
     - recompute χ⁰, the RPA χ and the decomposition at the actual 3D momentum of each spectrum;
     - replot the experimental peak position against |q| and/or both components;
     - state which Fermi velocities (band and direction) set the expected continuum edge;
     - ideally, add a series at fixed q⊥ and varying q∥ (for example by varying 2θ or Ω_i), or show that the trend persists when the momentum direction is held fixed.

   Without this, the statement that the feature "follows a nearly linear dispersion" consistent with intra-band particle-hole kinematics is not established.

2. The identification as the continuum, rather than another low-energy excitation, rests on a model decomposition that does not yet exclude the alternatives.
   - What the decomposition shows: the "conclusive" intra- versus inter-band separation (p. 4, Fig. 4c) is done on the tight-binding χ⁰ at a single momentum. It is not done on the BSE RIXS simulation, which is the calculation that actually reproduces the data. The tight-binding χ⁰ in turn misses the measured dispersion at low q (Fig. 3e triangles at about 0.2 eV against about 0.38 eV measured), and the tight-binding bands deviate visibly from DFT along A–L–H and for the π bands (Fig. S3).
   - Alternatives that need ruling out:
     - Acoustic plasmon: MgB2 is a two-carrier (σ/π) system for which a low-energy acoustic plasmon has been predicted (Ref. 12, cited but not discussed). Such a mode is linearly dispersing, sits in this energy window, and is largely out of phase between orbital sectors, so an orbital-selective probe would see it.
     - σσ inter-band transitions: transitions between the two σ bands near Γ–A, which lie close in energy.
   - Tests that would discriminate:
     - perform the intra/inter-band (and σ/π) decomposition inside the BSE calculation, for example by restricting the conduction and valence bands in the RIXS intermediate and final states;
     - show the decomposition at several momenta, including the lowest ones;
     - plot the measured width against q. A continuum should broaden roughly ∝ q and have a lineshape bounded by v_F q, while a collective mode should stay comparatively sharp. The asymmetric lineshape noted on p. 3 could be compared quantitatively with the continuum shape predicted by χ⁰.
     - explicitly discuss and exclude the acoustic plasmon of Ref. 12, for instance by showing whether the orbitally weighted RPA χ (Fig. 4a) contains a pole in this window.

B. Should address

3. There is no incident-energy (detuning) dependence.
   - Why it matters: the feature is measured only at Ω_i = 187 eV. To show that it is a Raman-like energy-loss excitation at fixed ω, rather than a fluorescence-like or resonance-shape artifact that tracks Ω_i, the authors should show spectra at several incident energies across the narrow pre-edge, in Ω_i–ω maps. This is especially relevant because the pre-edge is sharp and the feature lies within a few times the 100 meV resolution of the elastic line.
   - Partial support: the BSE agreement partly addresses this.
   - What to add: experimental detuning data would make the assignment much more robust.

4. The wording "quantitative agreement" and the quoted velocity do not match the figures.
   - Abstract: the abstract claims "quantitative agreement with simulations of the non-interacting charge susceptibility". In Fig. 3e, however, χ⁰ deviates by up to about 0.2 eV at the three lowest momenta, and Fig. 3d is acknowledged to peak "at a larger value of q".
   - Velocity: the stated speed of "≈1.4 eV Å, around half of the Fermi velocity of the σ bands" is internally inconsistent with the quoted v_F^σ ≈ 2 eV Å, which gives a ratio of about 0.7. The dashed line in Fig. 3e also appears to have a slope nearer 2 eV Å (roughly 0.38 → 0.6 eV over 0.05 → 0.155 Å⁻¹).
   - What to do: give the fitted slope and intercept with uncertainties, state which velocity is compared, and moderate the abstract claim to the BSE agreement, which is the closer one.

5. The fit procedure and peak extraction are under-specified.
   - Lineshape: the low-energy feature is described as "markedly asymmetric", yet peak positions come from symmetric Voigt components (Fig. S2). In Fig. 3a the red fits visibly miss the rising edge near 0.1–0.3 eV at several momenta.
   - Missing details: please specify which parameters were free or fixed (elastic width, Lorentzian/Gaussian ratio, background), how the elastic line was constrained, and how the error bars in Fig. 3e were obtained. The bars grow strongly at high q and are not explained.
   - Robustness check: an alternative, model-independent estimator (first moment or half-maximum of the leading edge) would show that the dispersion does not come from the lineshape model.

6. The orbitally weighted RPA (Fig. 4a, Eqs. 32–33) is ad hoc and its parameters are loosely fixed.
   - Weighting: the cos⁴/sin⁴ weights account only for the incident polarization. The outgoing polarization was not analysed and is not included. The connection between the RIXS cross-section at a 1s → 2p resonance and a partial charge susceptibility is assumed, not derived.
   - Coulomb interaction: it is an isotropic U/q², with U = 2 eV Å chosen by matching a GPAW plasmon. MgB2 is anisotropic, and local-field effects are absent.
   - Insensitivity to U: the statement that "the low-energy spectrum is unaffected by the choice of U" should be shown, because it bears directly on whether the feature is a continuum (insensitive to U) or a collective mode (sensitive to U). Showing χ for two or three values of U would serve as a useful diagnostic for concern 2.

7. Only one direction and one sample are shown.
   - Γ–K: Supplement A states that measurements were also made along Γ–K, but only Γ–M is shown. The σ Fermi surfaces are nearly cylindrical, while the π network is strongly anisotropic. A Γ–M versus Γ–K comparison, against the corresponding χ⁰, is a direct and cheap test of the "Fermi-surface geometry" claim and should be included.
   - Samples: please state how many samples or films and how many spots were measured, and whether the feature is reproduced.

8. The EELS argument and the novelty claim are stated more strongly than the evidence supports.
   - Sum rule: the argument (Eq. 1) holds at leading order in small q. It shows that the continuum's weight in the loss function is suppressed, not that it is absent, and the suppression weakens as q approaches k_F.
   - Earlier EELS work: Ref. 21 reports EELS distinguishing intra- and inter-band transitions in MgB2. The relation to that work, and to high-resolution EELS and optical Drude-tail measurements, should be discussed.
   - What to do: rephrase "cannot see" and "has not been reported to date" accordingly.

C. Minor

9. Sample details are minimal: only "Ref. 24" is given. Please state the film thickness, substrate, c-axis orientation, surface preparation and residual resistivity.
   - Oxidation: boron K-edge XAS of air-exposed MgB2 often shows a B₂O₃ π* resonance near 194 eV. The strong peak near 193–194 eV in the TFY of Fig. 2a, which is underestimated by all functionals in Fig. S3, may contain such a contribution. Please comment on surface oxidation and the probing depth at 187 eV.

10. Fig. 4c:
   - The caption says inter-band excitations "between the σ and π bands" are negligible, but the legend shows σσ and ππ inter-band terms. Clarify whether σ–π cross terms are included, given the δ_ad δ_bc structure of Eq. 27.
   - Also state the k-mesh convergence for χ⁰ at q ≈ 0.05 Å⁻¹. N = 512 per direction gives Δk ≈ 0.005 Å⁻¹ in-plane, which is adequate, but the δ = 0.01 eV broadening relative to the k-spacing should be justified.

11. Fig. 3d versus Fig. 4b use different colour scales and broadening for the same quantity. A common normalisation, or a side-by-side panel, would help the comparison.

12. Fig. 3e: the legend's ω_BSE squares are extracted in an unstated way. Specify whether the same Voigt procedure was applied to the simulated spectra.

13. The measurement temperature (77 K, normal state) appears only in the Supplement. Give it in the main text, together with the resolution and the scattering angle 2θ, so that q can be reproduced.

14. Typos: "Florescence" (Fig. 2 caption), "the the" (pp. 4, 5), "motive" → "motivate" (p. 5), "superconducing". A "Data available upon reasonable request" statement is weak for a paper whose main result is a dataset.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Re-express all experimental and theoretical momenta as full 3D vectors. Overlay the measured peak and width on the continuum boundaries ω = v_F^band(q̂)·|q| for each Fermi-surface sheet.
- Add an Ω_i–ω RIXS map across the 187 eV pre-edge, and the Γ–K momentum series that was already measured.
- Perform the intra/inter-band and σ/π decomposition inside the BSE RIXS calculation, and discuss the predicted acoustic plasmon of MgB2 explicitly.
- Consider a temperature series across T_c ≈ 39 K. Opening of the σ gap (about 7 meV) is below the resolution, but a lineshape or low-energy weight change would be an elegant consistency check of the Fermi-surface origin.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
