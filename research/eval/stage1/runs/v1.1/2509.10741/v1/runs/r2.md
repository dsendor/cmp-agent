Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report boron K-edge RIXS on the weakly correlated metal MgB2 at very small momentum transfer (q|| ≈ 0.05–0.16 Å⁻¹). They find a low-energy feature at 0.4–0.6 eV that disperses with q||, and they identify it as the intraband particle-hole (Lindhard) continuum, using OCEAN BSE simulations, a tight-binding Lindhard function and an orbitally weighted RPA. They also argue from the f-sum and screening sum rules that EELS or non-resonant IXS cannot see this continuum at small q, while orbitally selective RIXS can. If it holds up, a momentum-resolved spectroscopic window on the intraband continuum of a metal would matter well beyond MgB2. The headline identification, though, rests on a modest q range, a geometry in which q|| and q⊥ change together, and theory comparisons that are only partly quantitative.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear, well-motivated idea: an orbital-projected, non-conserved RIXS density escapes the small-q screening suppression that hides the continuum in the total charge response. The toy model (Supplementary G, Eqs. 34–39, Fig. S4) makes the point cleanly.
- A new experimental observation: a strong, asymmetric low-energy feature at the B K pre-edge resonance (Fig. 2b, Fig. 3a), measured with 100 meV resolution in an ultra-soft regime that is rarely used for RIXS.
- Full BSE simulations that include the experimental geometry reproduce both the dispersion and the angle-dependent intensity (Fig. 3c, red squares in Fig. 3e). This is a non-trivial check.
- The material is a good choice: a DFT-describable multiband metal, so the theory can in principle be benchmarked against the data.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The momentum transfer is not purely in-plane, and the analysis ignores q⊥. In Fig. 3a, q⊥ falls from 0.14 to 0.02 Å⁻¹ as q|| rises from 0.05 to 0.16 Å⁻¹. Over the measured range the total |Q| stays at roughly 0.13–0.16 Å⁻¹, close to the 2k_photon ≈ 0.19 Å⁻¹ limit at 190 eV. The "dispersion" in Fig. 3b,e is therefore largely a rotation of Q from near-c-axis to near-in-plane at nearly fixed |Q|, not a change of |Q|.

   This matters for two reasons:
   - The π bands disperse strongly along kz (Fig. 1c, Fig. S1). Excitations at q⊥ = 0.14 Å⁻¹ probe a different part of the continuum than excitations at q|| = 0.05 Å⁻¹.
   - Kinematics. The intraband continuum is bounded by ω ≤ v_F·q. With the σ in-plane velocity quoted in the text (≈ 2 eVÅ) and q|| = 0.05 Å⁻¹, the bound is about 0.1 eV, yet the observed peak sits at about 0.4 eV. Read against q|| alone, the line in Fig. 3e also extrapolates to a finite intercept of about 0.3 eV at q|| → 0. A gapless Lindhard continuum cannot do that, while a gapped (interband) or collective excitation could.

   Fig. 3d and Fig. 4 appear to be computed as a function of q|| alone, along Γ–M. The tight-binding model also deviates from DFT along A–L–H (Fig. S3), which is exactly where kz matters.

   The authors should:
   - state 2θ and the full Q vector for every spectrum;
   - recompute χ0, the RPA χ and the decomposition at the actual (q||, q⊥) of each spectrum;
   - present the dispersion against |Q| and against the projection onto the relevant Fermi velocities;
   - show explicitly that the feature energy at every point lies inside the computed intraband continuum, ω ≤ max(v_F·Q).

   If the spectrometer allows, spectra at fixed q|| with different q⊥ (different 2θ, or the Γ–K azimuth that the Methods say was measured) would separate the two dependences directly.

2. The apparent dispersion may be a geometry-dependent shift of orbital weight, not a true q-dependence. Changing θ_in changes q|| and q⊥, and it also changes the polarization: cos⁴θ versus sin⁴θ weighting of pσ and pπ (Eqs. 32–33). The authors' own decomposition (Fig. 4c, q|| = 0.088 Å⁻¹) shows the σσ intraband weight peaking near 0.35–0.4 eV and the ππ intraband weight peaking near 0.7 eV. As θ_in moves from grazing to normal incidence, the relative weight of these two components shifts. That alone would move the peak of a single Voigt fit from about 0.4 to about 0.6 eV, even with no intrinsic dispersion in either component.

   The text attributes the intensity variation to "out-of-plane components of the X-ray polarization" (p. 3) but does not separate this effect from the energy shift. The authors should:
   - show, with the BSE or RPA model, what peak shift results from the polarization change alone at fixed Q;
   - if possible, measure at a fixed geometry with Q varied by another route (2θ), or use σ versus π incident polarization at a fixed θ_in.

   Without this, the linear dispersion in Fig. 3e, which is the main experimental evidence for a particle-hole origin, is not established.

3. No signature specific to a continuum has been shown, and alternatives have not been excluded. The defining properties of the Lindhard continuum are:
   - spectral weight extending from ω = 0 (Im χ0 ∝ ω at low ω);
   - an upper edge at v_F·q;
   - a width that grows linearly with q.

   The data are instead fitted with a single Voigt peak (Fig. S2), and only peak positions are reported. In Fig. 3a the lineshape looks like a peak of roughly constant width, not a continuum whose width scales with q.

   Two alternatives give a dispersing low-energy peak in this energy window:
   - a damped collective mode, in particular the acoustic-like σ–π plasmon predicted for MgB2 by Silkin et al. (Ref. [12]). That paper is cited but not discussed;
   - interband transitions between the two split σ bands near Γ/A.

   Fig. 4c argues that interband terms are negligible, but in the bare charge vertex of a tight-binding χ0, not in the RIXS (BSE) cross-section that actually reproduces the data. The authors should:
   - report the fitted width (and, ideally, the low-energy onset after elastic subtraction) as a function of Q, and compare it with the predicted continuum width;
   - fit with a model continuum lineshape, for example Im χ0 convolved with the 100 meV resolution, rather than a Voigt;
   - discuss the acoustic-plasmon and σ–σ interband scenarios and say which observable rules each out;
   - decompose the BSE spectra themselves into intraband and interband contributions (the exciton projection of Eq. 3 makes this possible), not only the tight-binding χ0.

   Until then, the paper's statement that the analysis "conclusively demonstrates" intraband origin (p. 4) is not supported.

B. Should address

4. The quoted velocity does not appear to match Fig. 3e. The text gives "≈ 1.4 eVÅ, around half of the Fermi velocity of the σ bands". Reading the experimental points and the dashed line in Fig. 3e, ω goes from about 0.42 to 0.65 eV over q|| = 0.05 → 0.155 Å⁻¹, a slope of about 2.2 eVÅ. Please give the fitted slope with its uncertainty and say which axis variable it refers to. Also explain why a continuum feature should disperse at about half v_F rather than at the continuum edge. For a cylindrical σ Fermi surface, Im χ0 piles up near ω ≈ v_F·q.

5. The momentum scale of the tight-binding calculations looks inconsistent with known Fermi velocities. In Fig. 4c (q|| = 0.088 Å⁻¹), the σσ intraband weight cuts off near 0.6 eV and the ππ intraband weight near 1.5 eV. These cutoffs imply in-plane velocities of about 7 and 17 eVÅ, far above the ≈ 2 eVÅ quoted for σ and above typical DFT values for π. Please check the q units, in particular a possible 2π or r.l.u. conversion; the Methods say momenta are "given in reciprocal lattice units" while the figures use Å⁻¹. If the momentum axis of Fig. 3d and Fig. 4 is off by a constant factor, the agreement in Fig. 3e changes.

6. The RPA model is only loosely specified. Eq. 20 writes V(q) = U/q² with q in Å⁻¹ and U = 2 eVÅ. The dimensions do not work for a 3D Coulomb interaction, which needs a volume factor. Momentum dependence along c and local-field effects are also absent. The statement that "the low-energy spectrum is unaffected by the choice of U" is precisely what would distinguish a continuum from an acoustic plasmon (concern 3), so please show it, for example RPA maps for two or three values of U. The cos⁴/sin⁴ orbital weights of Eqs. 32–33 are a heuristic stand-in for the RIXS matrix element. Their relation to the BSE cross-section, which includes the core hole, should be stated, and the RPA–data agreement in Fig. 4a should be shown quantitatively, for example by overlaying the peak positions on Fig. 3e.

7. The low-q χ0 discrepancy is acknowledged but not resolved. In Fig. 3e the χ0 peak (blue triangles) sits at about 0.15–0.2 eV at the lowest q, versus about 0.4 eV in experiment. The text says the agreement holds "except at smaller q where the spectral weight is split". Yet the small-q region is where a gapless continuum and a gapped excitation are most clearly distinguished (concern 1), so this is the regime that tests the claim. Please discuss it explicitly.

8. Incident-energy dependence is missing. At a K pre-edge that resonates into unoccupied states just above E_F, fluorescence-like emission can appear at small loss and shift with incident energy. Please show RIXS maps across the 187 eV pre-edge feature (incident-energy detuning) to show that the 0.4–0.6 eV feature is Raman-like, at constant energy loss.

9. Sample characterization and reproducibility. The Methods state only that the sample is grown as in Ref. [24]: an HPCVD thin film, with no thickness, substrate, orientation, residual resistivity or Tc given. B K-edge soft X-rays at grazing geometries are surface-sensitive, and B oxides contribute near 194 eV. The XAS above about 192 eV (Fig. 2a) is notably stronger in experiment than in BSE. Please give film parameters and surface preparation, state how many samples and spots were measured, and show that the low-energy feature reproduces. All data are at 77 K. This is fine for the claim, but a second temperature would rule out thermally populated or phonon-related contributions to the asymmetric tail.

10. The novelty and the EELS argument are framed too strongly. The particle-hole continuum is routinely observed by non-resonant IXS and EELS at q comparable to or larger than the plasmon cutoff momentum, in simple metals. The paper's argument applies specifically to q ≪ k_F. Ref. [21] also reports EELS separating intra- and inter-band features in MgB2. The abstract ("remained experimentally elusive") and the opening paragraph should be qualified to "at small q, momentum-resolved". The sum-rule argument on p. 5 is asymptotic in small q, and the statement that EELS "cannot see" the continuum should be stated with that limit.

11. Γ–K data. The Methods say the dispersion was measured along both Γ–M and Γ–K, but only Γ–M is shown. In-plane anisotropy, or its absence, compared with the computed χ0 along Γ–K would be a direct, independent test of a Fermi-surface origin. Please include it.

C. Minor

12. Fig. 2b is labelled q|| = 0.016 Å⁻¹, below the 0.05 Å⁻¹ lower limit of Fig. 3, and its peak (about 0.3 eV) differs from the lowest-q peak marked in Fig. 3a (about 0.38 eV). Please check whether this is a typo, and give the full (q||, q⊥).

13. The (q||, q⊥) labels in Fig. 3a give |Q| = 0.161, 0.128 and 0.149 Å⁻¹, which is not constant. This is unexpected at fixed 2θ. Please state the scattering angle and check the labels.

14. Error bars in Fig. 3e grow strongly with q, but their origin (fit covariance, repeated scans, resolution) is not stated. Also explain how the elastic line was subtracted and which Voigt parameters were free.

15. In Fig. 4c two curves (ππ intra and σσ inter) are drawn in similar red colours. Please use distinct colours or line styles.

16. Cross-references in the text, such as "S0 D", "S0 G" and "Figure S0 G", are broken, and the XAS functional comparison and Fig. S2 share an unnumbered figure page. Please number the supplementary XAS comparison figure.

17. Typographical errors: "Florescence" (Fig. 2 caption), "the the charge susceptibility" (p. 4), "motive" → "motivate" (p. 5), "from that fact" → "from the fact" (p. 1).

18. A "data available upon reasonable request" statement is weak for a claim of a first observation. Please consider depositing the RIXS spectra and the tight-binding/RPA code.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Re-plot the main result (Fig. 3e) against the full Q, with the computed intraband continuum boundaries for the σ and π bands overlaid. This one figure would show the reader directly whether the feature lives inside the Lindhard continuum.
- Decompose the BSE RIXS spectra, which reproduce the data, into intra- and inter-band and σ/π channels, instead of relying on a separate tight-binding χ0 for the assignment.
- Add a continuum-specific test: the width or onset versus Q, plus a comparison to Im χ0 convolved with the resolution, rather than peak positions alone.
- Add incident-energy detuning maps and Γ–K data to separate Raman-like intraband excitations from fluorescence and matrix-element effects.

5. Verdict
