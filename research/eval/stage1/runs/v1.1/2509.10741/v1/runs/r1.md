Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report boron K-edge RIXS on MgB2 at very small momentum transfer (q_par of about 0.05 to 0.16 Å^-1). Tuned to the 187 eV pre-edge resonance, the spectra show a low-energy feature at 0.4 to 0.6 eV that disperses with q_par. They identify it as the intraband particle-hole (Lindhard) continuum, using DFT/BSE (OCEAN) RIXS simulations, a tight-binding Lindhard function, an orbitally weighted RPA calculation, and a sum-rule argument for why EELS cannot see this continuum. If the assignment holds, this would be the first momentum-resolved spectroscopic view of the intraband continuum of a metal. It would also open a new use for ultra-soft RIXS, so the result is of broad interest. The case for the assignment is still mainly qualitative, and the kinematics of the observed feature do not obviously match those of an intraband continuum.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The experiment is new and hard: ultra-soft RIXS at the B K-edge with about 100 meV resolution and q_par below 0.2 Å^-1. The new low-energy feature (Fig. 2b, Fig. 3a,b) is clearly resolved and reproducible across the momentum series.
- Theory is brought in at several levels: a first-principles BSE RIXS cross-section that includes the experimental geometry (Fig. 3c), a tight-binding Lindhard function (Fig. 3d), an orbitally resolved RPA (Fig. 4a), and a band-resolved decomposition (Fig. 4c).
- The sum-rule argument and the two-orbital toy model (Eqs. 1, 34-39, Fig. S4) explain clearly why an orbital-selective probe can show particle-hole weight that the total-density response hides. This idea is useful well beyond MgB2.
- MgB2 is a well-characterized, weakly correlated, multiband metal with a reliable DFT band structure. That makes it a sensible test case.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The measured kinematics do not obviously match an intraband continuum. The paper's own sum-rule argument (p. 5) says the intraband continuum is confined to omega <= v_F q, and that it vanishes as q -> 0. In contrast, the extracted peak (Fig. 3e, black circles) sits at about 0.4 eV at q_par = 0.055 Å^-1. The linear fit (dashed line) extrapolates to a finite intercept of about 0.3 eV at q_par = 0. With the σ-band Fermi velocity quoted on p. 2 ("roughly 2 eV Å"), the intraband σ continuum at q_par = 0.055 Å^-1 should end near 0.1 eV, a factor of about four below the observed peak. Three further points are inconsistent:
   (i) The text quotes a velocity of about 1.4 eV Å, "around half" the σ Fermi velocity, which implies v_F of about 2.8 eV Å, not the 2 eV Å stated earlier. The slope that can be read from Fig. 3e (about 0.40 to 0.62 eV over 0.055 to 0.155 Å^-1) is closer to 2 eV Å.
   (ii) In Fig. 4c, at q_par = 0.088 Å^-1, the "σσ,intra" weight extends to about 0.5 eV and the "ππ,intra" weight to about 1.5 eV. These numbers would need velocities of about 6 and 17 eV Å, far above any MgB2 Fermi velocity. This suggests that the "intra/inter" labels follow band-index ordering, so that pairs of different σ bands (light and heavy hole) or crossing bands count as "intra". Alternatively, the q used in the calculation is not the q_par on the axis.
   (iii) The spectra in Fig. 3a carry a large q_perp that varies together with q_par: (0.05, 0.14) to (0.16, 0.02) Å^-1. So |q| stays nearly constant (about 0.13 to 0.16 Å^-1) across the series, while the geometry and polarization change. Any continuum contribution from the π bands, which disperse along k_z, depends on the full 3D q, not on q_par. The apparent "dispersion versus q_par" is therefore confounded with the change of θ_in and polarization.
   Why it matters: the headline claim is that the feature is the Lindhard continuum. A peak with a finite intercept, lying above v_F q, is the typical signature of interband transitions or of a collective mode, not of an intraband continuum.
   What to do:
   - Plot the data and every calculation against the full momentum (q_par, q_perp), with the same q in theory and experiment.
   - Show the expected kinematic boundary omega = v_F·q for the relevant σ and π sheets on Fig. 3e.
   - Report the fitted intercept and slope with uncertainties, and reconcile the velocity numbers.
   - Redo the Fig. 4c decomposition using a physical criterion for intra- versus interband, for example eigenvector continuity or overlap between k and k+q, and show the σ-σ interband contribution separately.
   - If possible, measure at fixed q_par with different q_perp, or at fixed θ_in with different scattering angle, to separate geometry from momentum.

2. Alternative assignments are not ruled out. Three need to be excluded explicitly.
   (a) The acoustic plasmon of MgB2. Out-of-phase oscillation of σ and π carriers was predicted in Ref. 12 (Silkin et al., cited but not discussed) at energies and with a near-linear dispersion of the kind observed here. A collective mode would naturally give a peak that is sharper than a continuum and has a well-defined dispersion. Whether the RPA χ in Fig. 4a contains such a mode is never examined.
   (b) Interband transitions between the two σ bands near the Fermi surface. These have a finite energy at q -> 0, which matches the finite intercept. Their weakness in the χ0 decomposition (Fig. 4c) may be an artifact of the labelling problem in concern 1, and χ0 lacks the RIXS matrix elements.
   (c) Incoherent or fluorescence-like emission from the sharp pre-edge, or a resonance-dependent intermediate-state effect. All data are taken at a single incident energy, 187 eV.
   What to do:
   - Show the incident-energy dependence (a RIXS map across the pre-edge) to establish Raman-like, constant-energy-loss behavior.
   - Analyse the RPA χ (Fig. 4a) for poles: is the intensity maximum a collective mode or continuum weight? Compare it explicitly with the acoustic-plasmon dispersion of Ref. 12.
   - Show that the BSE RIXS intensity (Fig. 3c) at the peak energy comes from final-state particle-hole pairs within the same band. The projection in Fig. 2c is of the X-ray absorption exciton (the intermediate state, Eqs. 2-3), not of the final valence excitation, so it does not identify the final-state transitions.
   - The Γ-K data that the SI says were measured would give an anisotropy test and should be shown.

B. Should address

3. The theory-experiment comparison is not quantitative. The χ0 peak positions (Fig. 3e, triangles) differ from experiment by up to a factor of two at low q (about 0.2 versus 0.4 eV), and the slopes differ clearly. Yet the text calls the agreement "close" and the decomposition "conclusive" (p. 4). The BSE peaks (squares) also fall systematically below the data at low q. Please give quantitative measures, for example peak positions, widths and lineshapes at several q overlaid on the data, and discuss the residual discrepancies. For a continuum, the most informative quantities are the upper edge and the lineshape, not a Voigt peak centre.

4. The RPA model needs justification.
   - The SI sets V_{ab,cd}(q) = V(q) for all band indices a, b, c, d (Eq. 30). This couples interband densities ρ_ab (a ≠ b) to the long-range Coulomb interaction with full weight, whereas their q -> 0 form factors should vanish. That treatment can redistribute low-energy weight artificially.
   - U is tuned so that a GPAW calculation gives a plasmon near 3 eV, yet the text states that "the low-energy spectrum is unaffected by the choice of U". That is surprising, because the paper's central argument is that screening is what suppresses the intraband weight.
   - The units are inconsistent: U = 2 eV Å in V = U/q^2 with q in Å^-1.
   Please show the RPA results for several U values, use the proper band-basis Coulomb matrix elements, and explain why the orbital-weighted χ (Eq. 32) is a fair proxy for the RIXS cross-section at the B K-edge, including the core-hole intermediate state.

5. The novelty claim needs context. The statements that the intraband continuum "has remained experimentally elusive" and that "no feature resembling the textbook illustration has been reported" should be set against existing work:
   - electronic Raman scattering in metals, which probes the intraband continuum at q of about 0 and has been studied in MgB2;
   - M-EELS and IXS studies of metals;
   - RIXS observations of particle-hole continua in doped transition-metal oxides.
   The general claim that EELS "cannot see" low-energy intraband excitations (p. 5) should be qualified. The argument is asymptotic in small q, and multiband metals with acoustic modes are a known exception. Please discuss these works and say precisely what is new: the momentum resolution, the intraband origin, or the metal studied.

6. The paper does not say how the peak positions and their error bars are extracted from an asymmetric feature. A single Voigt component (Fig. S2) is used to describe what is argued to be a continuum, and the elastic line and the low-energy shoulder near 0.1 eV (visible in Fig. 2b and Fig. S2) are fitted separately.
   - Please state which parameters were free, how the elastic line and any phonon contribution (the E2g mode is near 70 meV) were handled, and how the error bars in Fig. 3e were obtained.
   - Please check that the peak positions at the lowest q, where the feature is closest to the elastic line, do not depend on the elastic-line model.

7. Samples and surface. The sample is a hybrid physical-chemical vapor deposition (HPCVD) film (Ref. 24), but its thickness, substrate, orientation, surface preparation and oxidation state are not given. Only one sample appears to have been measured.
   - Boron oxides give a strong resonance near 194 eV, close to the main XAS peak in Fig. 2a. This peak is also where the experiment and the BSE calculation disagree most (Fig. S3).
   - Because the probing depth at 190 eV is shallow, please show that the surface is metallic MgB2, using XAS, XPS, or the absence of the B2O3 π* peak, and state whether the feature was reproduced on a second sample or spot.
   - Intensity comparisons across θ_in (the "suppression at grazing conditions") also need a self-absorption correction or a statement that none was applied.

8. All data are at 77 K. An intraband continuum in a Fermi liquid should not depend strongly on temperature, whereas some alternatives would. A modest temperature series would be a useful, cheap control, though it is not essential.

9. Data availability. "Available upon reasonable request" is weak for a paper whose central claim depends on lineshape and peak extraction. Please deposit the RIXS spectra and the fit results.

C. Minor

10. The figure legends and labels need fixing:
   - Fig. 4c uses similar red tones for "ππ,intra" and "σσ,inter".
   - The text says the low-energy weight comes from "the two σ bands and two π bands", while the SI tight-binding model has six σ bands and two π bands. Please make these consistent.
   - The Fig. 2b caption gives q_par = 0.016 Å^-1, which lies outside the range used elsewhere (0.05 to 0.16). Please check the value and give q_perp.

11. Several cross-references are broken ("S0 D", "S0 G", "Figure S0 G"), and SI Eq. (38) is empty.

12. Fig. 1a would benefit from marking omega = v_F q and the 2k_F scale explicitly. A sketch of the expected MgB2 boundaries on Fig. 3e would also help the reader.

13. Typos: "Florescence" (Fig. 2 caption), "the the charge susceptibility", "arises from that fact that", "motive" (should be "motivate"), "it is evident ... no intensity for L_{p-h}", "Voigt" capitalization.

14. The definitions of θ_in (0° = normal incidence) differ in the SI and the main text. Please state them once, and give θ_in for every spectrum in Fig. 3a.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Re-plot Fig. 3e against the full 3D momentum, with the calculated kinematic edges v_F·q for each Fermi-surface sheet, and fit the upper edge of the feature rather than a Voigt centre.
- Add an incident-energy map across the 187 eV pre-edge, and the Γ-K dispersion, as direct tests of the Raman character and of the Fermi-surface anisotropy.
- Decompose the BSE RIXS final states (not the absorption exciton) into intraband and interband particle-hole pairs. This gives the most direct theoretical support for the assignment.
- Examine the RPA response for an acoustic plasmon and compare it explicitly with Ref. 12, so that a collective mode can be distinguished from continuum weight.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
