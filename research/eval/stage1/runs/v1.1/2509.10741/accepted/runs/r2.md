Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use ultra-soft RIXS at the boron K pre-edge (≈187 eV) of MgB2 thin films and report a low-energy (0.3–0.6 eV), asymmetric, linearly dispersing feature at in-plane momenta below ≈0.17 Å⁻¹. From BSE-RIXS simulations, tight-binding Lindhard and orbitally weighted RPA calculations, and a band decomposition of χ⁰, they identify it as the intraband particle-hole (Lindhard) continuum. They also argue, from the f-sum and screening sum rules, why total-charge probes (EELS, non-resonant IXS) cannot see this continuum at small q while orbital-selective RIXS can. A clean, momentum-resolved spectroscopic observation of the intraband continuum in a simple metal would be significant, both conceptually and as a new use of low-q soft-X-ray RIXS in metals. The sum-rule argument, with the q⁴ suppression of the total response against the unscreened orbital-projected response (Eqs. 4–5), is a useful and clearly stated contribution.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The low-energy feature is new. It is reproduced, including its dispersion and the loss of intensity at grazing incidence, by first-principles BSE calculations that include the experimental geometry (Fig. 3c,e).
- The authors attack the assignment from several sides: projecting the BSE intermediate states onto the bands (Fig. 2c), the tight-binding Lindhard function, orbitally weighted RPA (Fig. 4a), the intra/interband decomposition (Fig. 4c), and linear dichroism (Supplementary Fig. S5).
- The sum-rule discussion explains why the continuum is invisible in EELS. This physical point holds beyond MgB2 and gives the measurement a clear rationale.
- The experimental idea, using a very low-energy absorption edge to reach q ≲ 0.2 Å⁻¹ with good momentum resolution, can be carried over to other metals. The authors are appropriately careful that nothing about MgB2 is uniquely required.
- The data are deposited and the Lindhard code is public.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The kinematics of the feature need to be reconciled with an intraband continuum, including its q → 0 behaviour and the out-of-plane momentum. The Discussion stresses that the intraband continuum is confined to ω ≲ v_F q (and Eq. 1 relies on this). Yet in Fig. 3e the experimental peak sits at ≈0.38–0.40 eV at the smallest q∥ ≈ 0.055 Å⁻¹ and rises to ≈0.6 eV at 0.155 Å⁻¹. A straight line through these points extrapolates to a finite energy of order 0.25–0.3 eV at q∥ → 0, whereas the upper edge of an intraband continuum must collapse to zero energy as q → 0. With the quoted σ-band Fermi velocity of ≈2 eV Å, v_F q∥ at 0.055 Å⁻¹ is only ≈0.1 eV, a factor of about 4 below the observed peak. Several points need clarifying.
   - (a) Which momentum is on the abscissa? At fixed 2θ, varying θ_in trades q∥ for q⊥. The Fig. 3a labels, (0.16, 0.02), (0.10, 0.08) and (0.05, 0.14), show q⊥ growing as q∥ falls, so the total |q| barely changes across the series. The units of these labels are not given (Å⁻¹ or r.l.u.?), and it is not stated which spectrometer (ALS at 150° or BESSY II at 90°) recorded the Fig. 3 data. The π Fermi surface is three-dimensional and the σ cylinders are warped, so the continuum edge for each band depends on the full (q∥, q⊥). The "dispersion" in Fig. 3 is therefore partly a rotation of q at nearly constant |q|.
   - (b) Using the actual band velocities from the tight-binding model, the authors should plot the kinematic upper edge ω_max(q∥, q⊥) of each intraband channel (two σ, two π) for the measured trajectory, overlay it on Fig. 3b,e, and show that the observed spectral weight lies inside it.
   - (c) They should explain the finite intercept. One candidate alternative would naturally give a finite energy at q → 0: interband transitions between the two σ bands, or between σ and π bands, near E_F, which split away from Γ–A. Fig. 4c addresses this only for the unweighted total χ⁰ at a single q∥ = 0.088 Å⁻¹. Moreover, in that panel the σσ-intra peak (≈0.35 eV) and the ππ-intra peak (≈0.75 eV) both lie well above v_F q∥ ≈ 0.18 eV, which itself needs explaining (are q⊥, broadening, or much larger band velocities responsible?).
   - (d) The velocity is described inconsistently. The abstract says it is "comparable to the Fermi velocity", the text gives ≈1.4 eV Å, "around half" the σ Fermi velocity, and the Fig. 3e points look steeper than 1.4 eV Å. Please state the fitted slope, its intercept, the fit range, and uncertainties for both.

   Without this analysis, the central identification rests on agreement in peak position with calculations rather than on the defining kinematic property of a Lindhard continuum.

B. Should address

2. The intra/interband decomposition should be done for the quantity that matches the experiment. In Fig. 3d,e the bare χ⁰ places its intensity at the wrong q∥ and deviates from the data by ≈0.1–0.15 eV at small q∥. The agreement in intensity is recovered only after orbital weighting by cos⁴θ_in and sin⁴θ_in within RPA (Fig. 4a). Yet the band decomposition in Fig. 4c is of the unweighted total χ⁰. Because the RIXS vertex projects onto p_x, p_y and p_z, interband σ–σ (p_x ↔ p_y) and σ–π channels could carry different relative weights in χ_{p_x p_x}, χ_{p_y p_y} and χ_{p_z p_z} than in the total density response. The authors should show the intra/interband decomposition of the orbitally weighted RPA response that is compared with the data in Fig. 4a, at several q∥ including the smallest. They should also show the BSE-RIXS result with interband σ–σ transitions artificially removed, or an equivalent test.

3. Evidence that the feature is a continuum rather than a dispersing collective mode. A Lindhard continuum predicts a characteristic lineshape: weight rising roughly linearly from ω = 0 up to a cutoff near v_F q, with a width that grows in proportion to q. The data are fitted with a single asymmetric Voigt peak (Fig. 3a, Supplementary Fig. S4), and only the peak position is reported. Please report the fitted width and asymmetry as functions of q∥ and compare them with the width and edge of the calculated χ⁰ convolved with the 100 meV resolution. Please also show how robust the extracted parameters are to the treatment of the elastic line and of phonon or quasi-elastic contributions below ≈0.2 eV, where the low-energy side of the continuum must lie. The error bars in Fig. 3e come only from the fit covariance matrix and do not capture systematic uncertainty from the choice of lineshape and background. Two observations would greatly strengthen the "continuum" assignment: an approximately linear growth of the width with q, and spectral weight that extends down to the elastic line. The acoustic-plasmon alternative is dismissed only by referring to Supplementary Section F. The main text should state the predicted acoustic-plasmon energy and velocity for the measured q and say why they are inconsistent with the data.

4. Which bands contribute? According to Fig. 4c, σσ-intra peaks near 0.35 eV and ππ-intra near 0.75 eV at q∥ = 0.088 Å⁻¹, and ππ carries most of the integrated weight. The experiment shows a single feature near 0.5 eV at this q∥, in between. The BSE projection (Fig. 2c) and the main text emphasise σ states, while the dichroism (Supplementary Fig. S5) shows that both polarizations contribute, at slightly different energies. Please state which channel or channels the observed peak is assigned to, and test whether two components with σ- and π-like velocities, constrained by the polarization dependence, describe the data better than one. This matters for the claim that the experiment "directly maps out" the continuum and is related to the abstract's statement about the Fermi velocity.

5. The Raman-like (constant energy-loss) behaviour should be shown in the main text. A particle-hole continuum should appear at constant energy loss as the incident energy is detuned across the 187 eV resonance, whereas fluorescence-like or core-hole-dressed features may shift with it. The text refers to Supplementary Fig. S2 for the incident-energy dependence, but no statement in the main text establishes that the low-energy feature is Raman-like. Please add a sentence, ideally with a small panel, showing the energy of the low-energy feature against incident energy.

6. Sample characterization and reproducibility. The Methods describe HPCVD growth but give no film thickness, substrate, crystalline orientation, T_c, residual resistivity or surface preparation. Data were taken at two facilities, at different temperatures (77 K at ALS, 28 K at BESSY II) and with different scattering geometries. The paper does not say which data come from which, nor whether the low-energy feature and its dispersion are reproduced across instruments and across more than one film. Boron oxide or other surface contamination is a known concern at the B K edge. The TEY/TFY comparison and the absence of oxide features near 194 eV should be documented to rule out a contribution from the surface layer. Please add a sample table and show the dispersion from both instruments, or from two films, on one plot.

7. Assumptions in the RPA orbital model. Eq. 3 uses V(q) = U/q² with U = 2 eV Å, calibrated against the ≈3 eV plasmon. The units of U look inconsistent with a 1/q² interaction (eV Å² would be expected). The geometry is included only through cos⁴θ_in and sin⁴θ_in weights. Please justify these weights from the dipole matrix elements of the actual experimental polarizations (σ and π, with 2θ = 150° and 90°), and state how sensitive the Fig. 4a intensity map is to U and to the weighting form. Since the RPA is what brings the calculated intensity into agreement with the data, its parameters should not be calibrated only loosely.

8. Framing of "first direct observation". The introduction says the intraband continuum has "remained experimentally elusive" and that no feature resembling Fig. 1a has been reported. Other probes have seen intraband particle-hole contributions: the electronic Raman continuum in metals, EELS in low-density metals and semimetals where the plasmon lies at low energy, and RIXS particle-hole continua in other materials (for example refs. 2–3 and work on doped cuprates). The novelty claim should be stated precisely, in terms of momentum resolution of the continuum's dispersion in a good metal at small q where screening hides it from total-charge probes, and set against these earlier observations.

C. Minor

9. Fig. 3: give the units for (q∥, q⊥) in panel a. State the instrument, polarization and temperature for each panel. Define what ω_BSE and ω_χ⁰ in panel e are (peak positions extracted in the same way as the experiment?). In panel e, label the left and right axes consistently: only a right-hand ω axis is shown.

10. Fig. 4: panels a and b are unbroadened while Fig. 3d is broadened, so they cannot be compared directly. Show the broadened RPA map alongside the experiment, or the same map on a common colour scale. In panel c, give the broadening used and confirm that the σσ-intra curve sums both σ bands.

11. Eq. 1: the screening sum rule as written, ∫ L(q,ω)/ω dω = π/2, holds only in the q → 0 limit, where ε(q,0) → ∞. State this explicitly. Clarify that "the plasmon alone saturates the sum rule" is a leading-order statement in q.

12. The BESSY II measurements at 28 K are below the MgB2 T_c of 39 K, while the ALS data at 77 K are in the normal state. The superconducting gaps are far below the 100 meV resolution, but please say so and confirm that no data at the two temperatures are combined in a single dispersion.

13. Small items: "Fermi Surface" should be "Fermi surface" (Fig. 1 caption). "low–energy" has an inconsistent dash. "π*-like resonance" in the Methods should be reconciled with the main text, which assigns the 187 eV pre-edge mainly to σ (p_x, p_y) holes. Give the k-mesh and convergence for the OCEAN/BSE calculations and the broadening used in Fig. 3c.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add one figure that overlays the measured peak positions and widths on the calculated kinematic boundaries of each intraband and interband channel along the actual (q∥, q⊥) trajectory. This would turn the identification from agreement with calculations into a direct kinematic test.
- Measure at a second scattering angle, or with a sample rotation that varies |q| at fixed q⊥. This would separate the in-plane dispersion from the change in q direction and allow the q → 0 limit to be approached more directly.
- Show the orbitally resolved (p_x p_x, p_y p_y, p_z p_z) intra/interband decomposition as a function of q. This would let readers see which channel dominates in each geometry and would make the dichroism argument quantitative.
- Report the fitted width or edge of the feature against q, which is the most distinctive fingerprint of a continuum, alongside its peak position.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
