Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors report a low-energy (~0.4–0.65 eV), asymmetric, dispersive feature in ultra-soft RIXS at the boron K pre-edge (187 eV) of MgB2 thin films. Using BSE simulations, an eight-band tight-binding Lindhard function, an orbitally resolved RPA and a band decomposition of χ0, they identify it as the intraband particle-hole (Lindhard) continuum. They also give a sum-rule argument for why total-charge probes such as EELS and non-resonant IXS suppress this continuum at small q while orbital-selective RIXS does not. If the identification holds, this would be the first momentum-resolved spectroscopic image of the Lindhard continuum in a metal. That would be significant, and it would open ultra-soft, low-q RIXS as a probe of fermiology and of the continua in correlated metals.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The question is clear and of general interest, and the material is well chosen: a weakly correlated metal whose band structure DFT describes well, with an absorption edge whose small photon momentum gives fine q resolution in the 0.05–0.17 Å⁻¹ range.
- Several independent theoretical routes (OCEAN BSE with the experimental geometry, tight-binding χ0, orbitally resolved RPA) each reproduce a dispersive low-energy feature in roughly the right energy window (Fig. 3b–e, Fig. 4a).
- The decomposition of χ0 into σσ/ππ intra- and interband channels (Fig. 4c) and the projection of BSE weight onto the band structure (Fig. 2c) give a concrete microscopic argument, not just phenomenology.
- The sum-rule discussion (Eq. 1, Eqs. 4–5) explains clearly why the continuum has been hard to see with EELS. It is conceptually useful beyond MgB2.
- Data and code are publicly deposited.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The kinematics of the measured feature are not reconciled with an intraband continuum. In Fig. 3e the experimental peak sits at ≈0.45 eV for q∥ ≈ 0.05 Å⁻¹ and at ≈0.62 eV for q∥ ≈ 0.155 Å⁻¹. A linear fit (dashed line) has a slope of ≈1.4–1.7 eV·Å but extrapolates to a large finite intercept, ≈0.35–0.4 eV, at q∥ → 0. An intraband particle-hole continuum is gapless and collapses to ω → 0 as q → 0. Its upper boundary is ≈ v_F q, which for the quoted σ-band v_F ≈ 2 eV·Å (p. 2) is only ≈0.1 eV at q∥ = 0.05 Å⁻¹ and ≈0.3 eV at 0.15 Å⁻¹. The measured energies are therefore several times larger than the textbook bound the paper itself invokes (Fig. 1a). Calling the 1.4 eV·Å slope a "velocity comparable to the Fermi velocity" conflates the slope of an offset line with a phase velocity ω/q, which is ≈4–9 eV·Å here.

   The tight-binding χ0 itself peaks at ≈0.6 eV at q∥ ≈ 0.15 Å⁻¹ (triangles, Fig. 3e). In Fig. 4c at q∥ = 0.088 Å⁻¹, the σσ intraband weight extends to ≈0.6 eV and the ππ weight to ≈1.4 eV. These imply ω/q values well above the σ and π Fermi velocities. They may reflect the out-of-plane momentum (q⊥ up to 0.14 Å⁻¹ per Fig. 3a), kz warping of the σ cylinders, the numerical broadening used in χ0, or the definition of q in the calculation, but the paper does not say. At the lowest q∥, χ0 peaks at ≈0.25 eV against ≈0.45 eV measured. The paper attributes this to split spectral weight, so the agreement holds only at the higher q∥ points.

   Since the headline identification rests on kinematics, the authors should:
   - (i) state exactly which (q∥, q⊥) enter the χ0, RPA and BSE calculations for each experimental point;
   - (ii) overlay on Fig. 3b/3e the calculated lower and upper boundaries of the σ and π intraband continua for those momenta;
   - (iii) explain the finite intercept of the experimental dispersion and how it is compatible with a gapless intraband continuum;
   - (iv) repeat the Fig. 4c decomposition at the lowest and highest measured q∥, not only at 0.088 Å⁻¹, and give the η broadening used in the "unbroadened" χ0 of Fig. 4b.

2. The alternative identification as an acoustic (σ–π out-of-phase) plasmon or "demon" is not excluded in the main text. MgB2 is the classic proposed host of such a mode (refs. 15, 26). Its expected dispersion is linear with a velocity between the σ and π Fermi velocities, which is what is observed. The paper dismisses it in one sentence, deferring to Supplementary Section F ("this branch is expected at a smaller energy range when considering the experimental scattering geometry").

   The issue is sharpened by the paper's own mechanism. Eq. 5 and the orbitally resolved RPA argue that RIXS sees the orbital-projected response χ_aa rather than the total density. In a two-component (σ, π) system with Coulomb coupling, the out-of-phase acoustic mode appears precisely in the orbital-projected channels and is screened out of the total density response. The good agreement of the RPA result (Fig. 4a) with experiment therefore does not by itself distinguish a continuum from a strongly Landau-damped acoustic collective mode living in the same channel.

   The authors should bring the discriminating evidence into the main text:
   - whether χ^RPA_aa in Fig. 4a has a pole or a distinct peak separate from Im χ0_aa, and how the peak positions and widths of χ0 and χ^RPA compare as a function of q;
   - where the acoustic branch would fall for the measured (q∥, q⊥), with the parameters used;
   - which measured observable (line shape, width scaling with q, polarization dependence) favours a continuum over a damped collective mode.

   Without this, "confirming it as the Lindhard continuum" and "conclusively demonstrates" (p. 4) are stronger than the evidence shown.

B. Should address

3. The angle scan changes q∥, q⊥, polarization projection and the σ/π orbital weighting all at once. In Fig. 3a, q⊥ falls from 0.14 to 0.02 as q∥ rises from 0.05 to 0.16. The text and Fig. 4c show that σσ and ππ intraband channels peak at different energies (~0.4 vs ~0.65 eV). The π bands are three-dimensional, so the π contribution depends on q⊥. Part of the apparent dispersion could therefore come from a changing σ/π mixture or q⊥ rather than from q∥ alone. The paper states that measurements were made at two scattering angles (2θ = 150° at ALS, 90° at BESSY II) and along both Γ–M and Γ–K. Showing peak positions from both geometries on a common q∥ axis, together with fixed-polarization (σ and π) dispersions, would show directly that the feature depends on q∥ and would make the dispersion claim much more robust. Currently it is not stated which instrument and temperature Fig. 3 comes from.

4. The incident-energy dependence that establishes Raman-like behaviour is not shown in the main text. The feature is reported only at a single incident energy tuned to a sharp core-exciton pre-edge (Fig. 2a). At such resonances, intermediate-state effects (core-hole lifetime, detuning-dependent shifts, fluorescence-like tails) can create apparent low-energy features. Supplementary Fig. S2 is cited for the incident-energy dependence, but the main text should show that the ~0.5 eV feature stays at constant energy loss, not constant emission energy, across the pre-edge. It should also show how its intensity tracks the resonance, so the feature can be read as an excitation of the ground state.

5. The fitting procedure behind the dispersion is not specified well enough to judge Fig. 3e. The model needs to be stated: the asymmetric Voigt form and its asymmetry parameter, how the elastic line and any phonon or multiphonon tail are treated (MgB2 has a strongly coupled E2g mode near 70 meV, comparable to the 100 meV resolution), the high-energy background from the 3 eV feature, and which parameters are fixed or free. For a continuum, the "peak" of an asymmetric profile is not a uniquely defined quantity. Its position depends on the asymmetry parameter and the background. Error bars taken only from the fit covariance (Fig. 3 caption) do not capture this model dependence. The authors should show that the dispersion survives alternative line-shape models, and ideally also track the high-energy edge of the feature, which is the quantity the Lindhard picture predicts most directly.

6. "Quantitative agreement" (abstract) is stronger than what is shown. The comparison is limited to peak positions in arbitrary-unit maps, and χ0 misses the lowest-q points (concern 1). The RPA ingredients are partly ad hoc:
   - V(q) = U/q² with U = 2 eV·Å is calibrated against a ~3 eV feature that the paper itself describes as a Landau-damped interband collective excitation, not the screened plasma frequency;
   - the orbital weighting by cos⁴θ_in and sin⁴θ_in is a phenomenological stand-in for the RIXS cross section.

   The authors should show how the RPA peak positions depend on U and on the weighting scheme. They should also quantify the agreement, for example by giving residuals between ω_exp, ω_BSE and χ0 in Fig. 3e, or soften the wording.

7. The validity of the orbital-projected-χ picture for RIXS at this resonance (Eqs. 4–5, Discussion) is assumed without justification. RIXS at a core exciton resonance is not in general proportional to an orbital-projected density–density correlator. That mapping holds only in the ultrashort core-hole-lifetime limit, or under specific conditions on the intermediate state. The BSE calculation handles this more completely, and the paper should state when and why the χ_aa description is adequate here, for example by comparing BSE and χ_aa line shapes at the same q. The broader statement that RIXS "will generically capture the Lindhard continuum" in any metal should be qualified accordingly.

8. Sample characterization and reproducibility are thin. For the HPCVD films the paper should give thickness, substrate, c-axis orientation, Tc and resistivity or RRR. It should also show that the surface is free of boron oxide (B2O3 and related species have strong B K-edge features) within the RIXS probe depth, for instance by comparing TEY and TFY. It should state how many films and spots were measured. The ALS (77 K) and BESSY II (28 K, below Tc) data should be compared directly, since the paper states that measurements were made at both facilities but presents results without attributing them.

C. Minor

9. Internal inconsistency in the velocity comparison: the text gives σ-band Fermi velocities of "roughly 2 eV Å" (p. 2), then calls 1.4 eV·Å "around half of the Fermi velocity of the σ bands" (p. 3). Please give the computed in-plane v_F for each σ and π sheet from the tight-binding model and use one consistent comparison.

10. Momentum units: Methods says momenta are given in r.l.u. (a = 3.086 Å, c = 3.524 Å), but the labels in Fig. 3a, e.g. (0.16, 0.02), appear to be in Å⁻¹. In r.l.u., 0.16 would correspond to ~0.33–0.38 Å⁻¹, beyond the maximum momentum transfer at 187 eV (≈0.18 Å⁻¹ at 2θ = 150°). Please clarify and state q⊥ for every point in Fig. 3e.

11. Fig. 2b gives q∥ = 0.016 Å⁻¹ for the overview spectrum. Please state the corresponding q⊥ and geometry, since the total momentum transfer at this energy is much larger.

12. Angle-dependent self-absorption at a strongly resonant pre-edge, especially near grazing incidence, should be corrected or discussed before the intensity suppression with q∥ is attributed to polarization and orbital selectivity (pp. 3–4).

13. Fig. 3d shows χ0 broadened by 100 meV, but no momentum-resolution broadening is mentioned. Please state the momentum resolution of each geometry.

14. Typos and wording: "against the - 3eV plasmon energy" in Methods; units of U in V(q) = U/q²; "weighed by sinusoidal factors" in Fig. 4 caption (weighted); "low–energy" hyphenation is inconsistent.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add one main-text figure that overlays, on the experimental ω–q∥ map, the calculated σ and π intraband continuum boundaries, the acoustic-plasmon branch from the same RPA, and the BSE and χ0 peak positions, all evaluated at the actual (q∥, q⊥) of each measurement.
2. Combine the ALS (2θ = 150°) and BESSY II (2θ = 90°) data, and Γ–M with Γ–K, in one dispersion plot, so that the q∥-only dependence and the reproducibility across instruments and samples are visible.
3. Present polarization-resolved (σ vs π) dispersions in the main text. They give the most direct experimental test of the σσ vs ππ intraband assignment in Fig. 4c.
4. Give the sum-rule argument a short quantitative estimate for MgB2: the predicted EELS intraband weight relative to the plasmon at q = 0.1 Å⁻¹ against the orbital-projected weight. This would turn the qualitative argument into a checkable number.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
