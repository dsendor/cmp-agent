Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use ultra-soft RIXS at the boron K pre-edge (about 187 eV) on MgB2 thin films. They report a low-energy (about 0.3 to 0.6 eV), asymmetric, dispersing feature, which they identify as the intraband particle-hole (Lindhard) continuum, measured at small momentum transfer (q∥ < 0.17 Å^-1). They support this with BSE (OCEAN) RIXS simulations, an eight-band tight-binding Lindhard function, an orbitally resolved RPA, and a sum-rule argument for why EELS and non-resonant IXS cannot see this continuum at small q. If the assignment holds, the result is of broad interest: it would make soft-X-ray RIXS a momentum-resolved probe of intraband charge excitations in metals, and it gives a clear reason why such excitations are hidden in total-charge probes.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The question is clean and fundamental. The physical argument (orbital-selective RIXS operators are not dressed by the long-range Coulomb kernel, whereas EELS measures -Im 1/ε) is well put and could be used widely.
- The experiment is carefully designed. Resonating at the σ/π pre-edge, and using the low photon momentum of the B K edge, gives access to the small-q regime with about 100 meV resolution.
- Several theoretical treatments (BSE, tight-binding χ0, orbital RPA) reproduce the overall dispersion and intensity trends. The linear-dichroism data (σ versus π polarization, Supplementary Fig. S5) give an independent orbital check.
- Data and code are publicly deposited.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The feature's energy at small q∥ is not reconciled with the kinematic limit of intraband excitations, and the momenta used in experiment and theory may not match. The central claim needs both resolved.
   - An intraband particle-hole continuum is bounded by ω ≤ v_F·q. In this experiment the feature does not extrapolate to zero energy as q∥ → 0.
     - Fig. 3e: the experimental peak sits at about 0.42 eV at q∥ ≈ 0.05 Å^-1 and rises to about 0.62 eV at 0.155 Å^-1. The linear fit therefore has an intercept of roughly 0.3 eV.
     - Fig. 2b: at q∥ = 0.016 Å^-1 a strong peak (dagger) still appears at a few tenths of an eV.
     - With the σ Fermi velocity quoted in the text ("roughly 2 eV Å"), the σ intraband continuum ends at about 0.03 eV at q∥ = 0.016 Å^-1 and about 0.1 eV at 0.05 Å^-1. Both are several times below the observed peak. Yet the BSE projection in Fig. 2c assigns this very spectrum to σ states near Γ.
   - The out-of-plane momentum cannot be ignored. With 2θ = 150° and hν ≈ 187 eV, |Q| ≈ 0.18 Å^-1 is fixed, so reducing q∥ increases q⊥, up to about 0.18 Å^-1 near specular. The 2D σ cylinders barely disperse along kz. The π bands do, so a finite-energy response at q∥ ≈ 0 would have to come from q⊥ acting on the π bands or from something other than σ intraband excitations.
   - The theoretical momentum scale seems inconsistent with the stated one. In Fig. 4c (q∥ = 0.088 Å^-1), the σσ intraband χ0 extends to about 0.5 eV and the ππ intraband χ0 to about 1.3 eV. These imply velocities of about 5.7 and about 15 eV Å at the stated q, far above the known MgB2 values and the text's 2 eV Å. The numbers would be plausible if the q axis were in reciprocal lattice units (0.088 × 2.35 Å^-1 ≈ 0.21 Å^-1). The Methods also say "momenta are reported in r.l.u.", while all figures are labelled in Å^-1.
   - The (q∥, q⊥) labels in Fig. 3a, (0.16, 0.02), (0.10, 0.08) and (0.05, 0.14), do not lie on a sphere of constant |Q| in either unit system. A fixed scattering angle requires that they do.
   - If the χ0 maps in Figs. 3d and 4 were computed at momenta 2 to 2.5 times larger than those probed, or with q⊥ omitted, then the agreement in Fig. 3e cannot be used as evidence.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Tabulate, for every spectrum, θ_in, 2θ, hν, q∥ and q⊥ in Å^-1.
     - State explicitly which momentum vector (full 3D Q, or q∥ only) and which units enter the χ0, RPA and BSE calculations.
     - Overlay the band-resolved kinematic boundaries v_F,σ·q∥ and the 3D π-band boundary on Figs. 3b–3e.
     - Explain quantitatively why the feature keeps a finite energy as q∥ → 0. Possible origins include q⊥ through the π bands, σ light-hole/heavy-hole interband transitions, or core-hole shake-up.

B. Should address

2. Alternative origins of the low-energy feature are excluded only qualitatively or at a single momentum.
   - The intra/inter decomposition (Fig. 4c) is shown only at q∥ = 0.088 Å^-1. The σσ interband channel between the two σ bands near Γ is the obvious candidate for a finite-energy response at q → 0. The authors should show the decomposition at the lowest and highest measured momenta, including q∥ = 0.016 Å^-1 (Fig. 2b).
   - The acoustic-plasmon alternative is dismissed in one sentence that points to Supplementary Section F. A two-component (σ + π) metal can support an acoustic mode lying just above the slower continuum, with a velocity between the two Fermi velocities. The main text should state the predicted acoustic-plasmon dispersion for the actual geometry and compare it with Fig. 3e.
   - A Raman-like origin should be demonstrated in the main text. A map of the low-energy spectrum versus incident-energy detuning across the 187 eV resonance would show the feature at constant energy loss. That rules out fluorescence-like or core-hole-induced (X-ray-edge-singularity type) shake-up contributions, which would also give asymmetric low-energy tails in a metal.

3. The quantitative comparison with χ0 is weaker than the text implies.
   - In Fig. 3e the χ0 "peak" sits about 0.15 to 0.2 eV below the experiment over the lower half of the momentum range. The text describes this as matching "closely except at smaller q∥".
   - A continuum has no intrinsic peak. Its maximum after convolution with a 100 meV resolution and a q-dependent orbital weighting depends on the analysis choices. Comparing positions extracted with an asymmetric Voigt fit is therefore not a sharp test.
   - The quoted velocity of about 1.4 eV Å does not obviously match the slope of the dashed line in Fig. 3e, which looks closer to 1.8 to 2 eV Å, and no intercept is given.
   - The error bars come only from the fit covariance matrix, which ignores the correlation with elastic-line and background parameters.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Fit the measured spectra directly with the resolution-convolved, orbitally weighted χ0 (or the BSE spectra), with at most an overall scale and a background.
     - Report the fitted velocity and intercept with uncertainties, or compare spectral first moments.
     - Give the functional form of the asymmetric Voigt and the elastic-line treatment.

4. The screening and orbital-selectivity argument needs to be made quantitative for MgB2.
   - The RPA uses an isotropic V(q) = U/q², with U calibrated to the 3 eV feature. The text itself describes that feature as an interband-damped collective mode rather than a free-electron plasmon, so the calibration needs justification.
   - The RPA weights the orbitals with ad hoc cos⁴θ_in and sin⁴θ_in factors. Outgoing polarization is not analysed, and the layered anisotropy of the Coulomb interaction is not included.
   - Figs. 4a and 4b show the orbital-projected RPA intensity reduced by about an order of magnitude (colour scale 0.0425 versus 0.375). Screening is therefore far from absent.
   - At the B K edge, the B 2p orbitals carry most of the valence charge, so the orbital-projected density is not obviously decoupled from the total density.
   - The authors should:
     - state whether the OCEAN BSE RIXS final state includes long-range (RPA-like) screening of the valence electron-hole pair;
     - compare the predicted ratio of the low-energy feature to the 3 eV feature with the measured ratio;
     - temper the general statement that "RIXS is free from the screening constraint". RIXS operators are not pure orbital densities, and the degree of escape depends on the orbital weights.

5. The novelty is overstated.
   - The paper says the Lindhard continuum "has remained experimentally elusive" and that "no feature resembling the textbook illustration … has been reported". Non-resonant IXS and EELS on simple metals (Li, Na, Al) have long resolved the particle-hole continuum at momenta beyond the plasmon cutoff, with lineshapes compared against Lindhard and local-field theories.
   - Electronic Raman scattering and earlier RIXS on metals (refs. 2–4) also access intraband particle-hole excitations.
   - The real advance is observation at small q (q ≪ k_F), where screening hides the continuum in total-charge probes. The abstract and introduction should say so and cite the large-q IXS work.

6. Sample characterization and reproducibility are thin.
   - The Methods give the growth method but not the following:
     - film thickness and substrate;
     - c-axis and in-plane epitaxy (the claim of no difference between Γ–M and Γ–K relies on the in-plane orientation);
     - Tc and resistivity;
     - how many films were measured.
   - Data come from two instruments (ALS at 2θ = 150° and 77 K; BESSY II at 2θ = 90° and 28 K, which is below Tc), but no figure says which instrument produced it. The authors should show that the dispersion agrees between the two instruments, which also probe different |Q|.
   - Soft-X-ray measurements of MgB2 are sensitive to surface oxidation (MgO, B2O3, whose BO3 π* resonance lies near 194 eV). The authors should comment on surface quality, using the XAS (Fig. 2a) and the TEY/TFY comparison.

7. Self-absorption and geometry are not corrected.
   - The momentum is scanned by rotating θ_in on a strongly absorbing pre-edge resonance. Self-absorption and probing depth therefore change with angle and can distort intensities and asymmetric lineshapes.
   - The text attributes the broadening and intensity loss at grazing incidence (larger q∥) to kinematic widening of the continuum and to polarization. The authors should show that self-absorption corrections do not change the extracted widths and peak positions.

C. Minor

8. Units and notation.
   - The Methods state that momenta are given in r.l.u., but the figures use Å^-1.
   - The Fig. 3a labels lack units.
   - In V(q) = U/q², "U = 2 eV Å" has the wrong dimensions for a 3D Coulomb kernel. Please give U with consistent units and its value in terms of e²/ε∞.

9. Fig. 4c caption. It says "Inter-band excitations between the σ and π bands are negligible", but the plotted interband curves are σσ and ππ. Please clarify and show the σπ channel explicitly.

10. Sum-rule paragraph (Eq. 1). The claim that L_p-h "has no contribution to the first moment" holds only to leading order in q. Please give the q-scaling of the continuum contribution, which is consistent with the q⁴ suppression in Eq. 4.

11. Missing calculation details.
   - BSE: the broadening parameters and core-hole lifetime used in Figs. 2 and 3c.
   - Tight-binding: the parameters and the quality of the fit to the DFT Fermi velocities for the σ and π sheets, in the main text or a table.
   - The k-point convergence of χ0 at these small q. With a 512³ grid, the smallest q steps are close to the measured q∥.

12. Fig. 2c. The dagger and double-dagger projections are hard to read. A zoom near Γ, showing which σ states contribute at the dagger energy, would help with concern 1.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a single figure that overlays the measured peak positions (or first moments) on the band-resolved intraband boundaries ω = v_F·|q|. Compute these boundaries with the full 3D Q of each spectrum, in consistent Å^-1 units, and show the χ0 decomposition at the lowest and highest q.
2. Add an incident-energy detuning map of the low-energy region to the main text to establish constant-loss (Raman) behaviour.
3. Measure, or at least model, a fixed-q∥ series with varying q⊥, for example by combining the ALS and BESSY scattering angles, to separate σ (2D) from π (3D) intraband contributions.
4. Compare intensity ratios as well as dispersions, for example the low-energy feature versus the 3 eV feature as a function of θ_in and polarization, against BSE and orbital RPA. This would test the screening argument directly.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
