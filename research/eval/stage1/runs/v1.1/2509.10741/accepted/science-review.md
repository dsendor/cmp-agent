A. Must address

1. The kinematics of the feature are not reconciled with an intraband continuum, and the momenta used in experiment and theory may not match. An intraband particle-hole continuum is gapless, with upper boundary ω ≈ v_F·q, and must collapse to zero energy as q → 0. The paper invokes this bound itself (Fig. 1a, Eq. 1). In Fig. 3e, however, the experimental peak sits at ≈0.38–0.45 eV at the smallest q∥ ≈ 0.05–0.055 Å⁻¹ and rises to ≈0.6–0.62 eV at 0.155 Å⁻¹. The linear fit therefore extrapolates to a finite intercept of roughly 0.25–0.4 eV at q∥ → 0. In Fig. 2b a strong peak (dagger) still appears at a few tenths of an eV at q∥ = 0.016 Å⁻¹, yet the BSE projection in Fig. 2c assigns this spectrum to σ states near Γ. With the quoted σ Fermi velocity of "roughly 2 eV Å" (p. 2), the σ intraband continuum ends at ≈0.03 eV at q∥ = 0.016 Å⁻¹, ≈0.1 eV at 0.05 Å⁻¹ and ≈0.3 eV at 0.15 Å⁻¹, several times below the observed peaks. The phase velocity ω/q of the data is ≈4–9 eV Å. Calling the ≈1.4 eV Å slope a "velocity comparable to the Fermi velocity" conflates the slope of an offset line with a phase velocity. Several momentum issues compound this. (a) The out-of-plane momentum matters. With 2θ = 150° and hν ≈ 187 eV, |Q| ≈ 0.18 Å⁻¹ is fixed, so lowering q∥ raises q⊥, as the Fig. 3a labels (0.16, 0.02), (0.10, 0.08), (0.05, 0.14) show. The "dispersion" in Fig. 3 is therefore partly a rotation of q at nearly constant |q|. The σ cylinders barely disperse along k_z but the π bands do, so a finite-energy response at q∥ → 0 would have to come from q⊥ acting on the π bands, from k_z warping, or from something other than σ intraband excitations. Fig. 2b also does not give the q⊥ or geometry for its q∥ = 0.016 Å⁻¹ spectrum. (b) The theoretical momentum scale looks inconsistent with the stated one. In Fig. 4c (q∥ = 0.088 Å⁻¹, where v_F q∥ ≈ 0.18 eV) the σσ intraband χ0 peaks near 0.35 eV and extends to ≈0.5–0.6 eV, and the ππ intraband χ0 peaks near 0.75 eV and extends to ≈1.3–1.4 eV. These imply velocities of ≈5.7 and ≈15 eV Å, far above the known MgB2 values. The tight-binding χ0 also peaks at ≈0.6 eV at q∥ ≈ 0.15 Å⁻¹ (Fig. 3e). The numbers would be plausible if the q axis were in reciprocal lattice units (0.088 × 2.35 Å⁻¹ ≈ 0.21 Å⁻¹), and the Methods say "momenta are reported in r.l.u." (a = 3.086 Å, c = 3.524 Å), while all figures are labelled in Å⁻¹. In r.l.u., 0.16 would be ≈0.33–0.38 Å⁻¹, beyond the maximum momentum transfer at 187 eV. The Fig. 3a labels have no units and do not lie on a sphere of constant |Q| in either unit system, as a fixed scattering angle requires. Nor is it stated which spectrometer (ALS at 150° or BESSY II at 90°) recorded Fig. 3. If the χ0, RPA and BSE results in Figs. 3d and 4 were computed at momenta 2 to 2.5 times larger than those probed, or with q⊥ omitted, the agreement in Fig. 3e cannot be used as evidence. Since the headline identification rests on kinematics, the authors should: tabulate θ_in, 2θ, hν, q∥ and q⊥ in Å⁻¹ for every spectrum, including Fig. 2b; state which momentum vector (full 3D Q or q∥ only) and which units enter the χ0, RPA and BSE calculations for each experimental point; use the tight-binding band velocities to overlay the kinematic upper (and lower) boundaries ω_max(q∥, q⊥) of each intraband channel (two σ, two π) along the measured trajectory on Figs. 3b–3e, and show that the observed weight lies inside them; report the fitted slope, intercept, fit range and their uncertainties; and explain quantitatively why the feature keeps a finite energy as q∥ → 0. Candidate origins include q⊥ acting through the π bands, σ light-hole/heavy-hole or σ–π interband transitions, numerical broadening of χ0, and core-hole shake-up. Without this, the identification rests on agreement in peak position with calculations rather than on the defining kinematic property of a Lindhard continuum.

2. The alternative identification as an acoustic (σ–π out-of-phase) plasmon or "demon" is not excluded in the main text. MgB2 is the classic proposed host of such a mode (refs. 15, 26). Its expected dispersion is linear, with a velocity between the σ and π Fermi velocities and lying just above the slower continuum, which resembles what is observed. The paper dismisses it in one sentence, deferring to Supplementary Section F ("this branch is expected at a smaller energy range when considering the experimental scattering geometry"). The paper's own mechanism sharpens the issue. Eq. 5 and the orbitally resolved RPA argue that RIXS sees the orbital-projected response χ_aa rather than the total density. In a Coulomb-coupled two-component system, the out-of-phase acoustic mode appears precisely in the orbital-projected channels and is screened out of the total density response. The agreement of the RPA result (Fig. 4a) with experiment therefore does not by itself distinguish a continuum from a strongly Landau-damped acoustic mode in the same channel. The authors should bring the discriminating evidence into the main text. They should show whether χ^RPA_aa in Fig. 4a has a pole or a distinct peak separate from Im χ0_aa, and how the peak positions and widths of χ0 and χ^RPA compare as a function of q. They should state where the acoustic branch would fall, in energy and velocity, for the measured (q∥, q⊥) with the parameters used, and compare it with Fig. 3e. They should also say which measured observable (line shape, width scaling with q, polarization dependence) favours a continuum over a damped collective mode. Without this, "confirming it as the Lindhard continuum" and "conclusively demonstrates" (p. 4) are stronger than the evidence shown.

B. Should address

3. Interband alternatives are excluded only at a single momentum and only for a quantity that does not match the experiment. The intra/interband decomposition in Fig. 4c is shown only at q∥ = 0.088 Å⁻¹ and only for the unweighted total χ0. Interband transitions between the two σ bands near Γ, or between σ and π bands near E_F (which split away from Γ–A), are the obvious candidates for a finite-energy response at q → 0. In Fig. 3d,e the bare χ0 puts its intensity at the wrong q∥, and intensity agreement is recovered only after cos⁴θ_in/sin⁴θ_in orbital weighting within RPA (Fig. 4a). Because the RIXS vertex projects onto p_x, p_y and p_z, interband σ–σ (p_x ↔ p_y) and σ–π channels may carry different relative weights in χ_{p_x p_x}, χ_{p_y p_y} and χ_{p_z p_z} than in the total density response. The authors should show the intra/interband decomposition of the orbitally weighted RPA response used in Fig. 4a at several q∥, including the lowest and highest measured momenta and q∥ = 0.016 Å⁻¹ (Fig. 2b). They should also show a BSE-RIXS result with σ–σ interband transitions artificially removed, or an equivalent test.

4. The line-shape analysis does not test the continuum assignment, and the fitting procedure behind Fig. 3e is under-specified. A Lindhard continuum has no intrinsic peak. Its weight rises roughly linearly from ω = 0 to a cutoff near v_F q, with a width growing in proportion to q. The data are fitted with a single asymmetric Voigt (Fig. 3a, Supplementary Fig. S4), and only the peak position is reported. That peak depends on the asymmetry parameter, the background, the 100 meV resolution and the q-dependent orbital weighting, so comparing peak positions is not a sharp test. The functional form of the asymmetric Voigt, the treatment of the elastic line and of phonon or multiphonon tails (MgB2 has a strongly coupled E2g mode near 70 meV, comparable to the resolution), the high-energy background from the 3 eV feature, and which parameters are fixed or free are not stated. The low-energy side of the continuum must lie below ≈0.2 eV, where these contributions dominate. The error bars come only from the fit covariance matrix and ignore correlations with elastic-line and background parameters and the choice of lineshape. The authors should give the full fit model and show that the dispersion survives alternative line-shape models. They should report the fitted width, asymmetry and high-energy edge as functions of q∥ and compare them with the calculated χ0 convolved with the resolution. Linear growth of the width with q, and weight extending down to the elastic line, would greatly strengthen the assignment. Ideally they should fit the spectra directly with the resolution-convolved, orbitally weighted χ0 (or BSE spectra) with only an overall scale and background, or compare spectral first moments.

5. The angle scan changes q∥, q⊥, polarization projection and σ/π orbital weighting at once, and the channel assignment of the observed peak is unclear. In Fig. 3a, q⊥ falls from 0.14 to 0.02 as q∥ rises from 0.05 to 0.16. At q∥ = 0.088 Å⁻¹ Fig. 4c places σσ-intra near 0.35–0.4 eV and ππ-intra near 0.65–0.75 eV, with ππ carrying most of the integrated weight, whereas experiment shows a single feature near 0.5 eV in between. The BSE projection (Fig. 2c) and the main text emphasise σ states, while the dichroism (Supplementary Fig. S5) shows both polarizations contributing at slightly different energies. Because the π bands are three-dimensional, part of the apparent dispersion could come from a changing σ/π mixture or from q⊥ rather than from q∥ alone. The authors should state which channel or channels the peak is assigned to. They should test whether two components with σ- and π-like velocities, constrained by the polarization dependence, describe the data better than one. They should show polarization-resolved (σ and π) dispersions in the main text. They should also combine the ALS (2θ = 150°) and BESSY II (2θ = 90°) data, and Γ–M with Γ–K, on a common q∥ axis, or model a fixed-q∥ series with varying q⊥, to separate the in-plane dispersion from the change in q direction.

6. Raman-like (constant energy-loss) behaviour is not established in the main text. The feature is reported at a single incident energy tuned to a sharp core-exciton pre-edge (Fig. 2a). At such resonances, intermediate-state effects (core-hole lifetime, detuning-dependent shifts, fluorescence-like tails, or core-hole-induced shake-up of the X-ray-edge-singularity type) can produce asymmetric low-energy features in a metal. Supplementary Fig. S2 is cited for the incident-energy dependence, but nothing in the main text states the result. The authors should add a detuning map or small panel showing that the ≈0.5 eV feature stays at constant energy loss, not constant emission energy, across the 187 eV resonance, and how its intensity tracks the resonance.

7. The screening-free, orbital-projected picture of RIXS (Eqs. 4–5, Discussion) is assumed rather than shown for MgB2. RIXS at a core-exciton resonance is not in general proportional to an orbital-projected density–density correlator. The mapping holds only in the ultrashort core-hole-lifetime limit or under specific intermediate-state conditions. Screening is also far from absent in the paper's own results: Figs. 4a and 4b show the orbital-projected RPA intensity reduced by about an order of magnitude (colour scale 0.0425 versus 0.375). At the B K edge the B 2p orbitals carry most of the valence charge, so the orbital-projected density is not obviously decoupled from the total density. The authors should state when and why the χ_aa description is adequate here, for example by comparing BSE and χ_aa line shapes at the same q. They should state whether the OCEAN BSE final state includes long-range (RPA-like) screening of the valence electron–hole pair. They should compare the predicted ratio of the low-energy feature to the 3 eV feature, as a function of θ_in and polarization, with the measured ratio. They should give a short quantitative sum-rule estimate for MgB2, such as the EELS intraband weight relative to the plasmon at q = 0.1 Å⁻¹ against the orbital-projected weight. The general statements that "RIXS is free from the screening constraint" and "will generically capture the Lindhard continuum" should be qualified, since RIXS operators are not pure orbital densities and the degree of escape depends on the orbital weights.

9. Self-absorption and probing-depth changes are not corrected. Momentum is scanned by rotating θ_in on a strongly absorbing pre-edge resonance, so self-absorption and probing depth change with angle, especially near grazing incidence, and can distort intensities and asymmetric line shapes. The text attributes the broadening and intensity loss at grazing incidence (larger q∥) to kinematic widening and to polarization and orbital selectivity (pp. 3–4). The authors should correct for self-absorption, or show that the correction does not change the extracted widths, peak positions and intensity trends.

10. Sample characterization and reproducibility are thin. The Methods describe HPCVD growth but give no film thickness, substrate, c-axis and in-plane orientation (the claimed absence of a Γ–M/Γ–K difference relies on in-plane epitaxy), T_c, resistivity or RRR, surface preparation, or the number of films and spots measured. 11. The novelty claim is overstated. The paper says the Lindhard continuum "has remained experimentally elusive" and that "no feature resembling the textbook illustration … has been reported". Non-resonant IXS and EELS on simple metals (Li, Na, Al) have long resolved the particle-hole continuum beyond the plasmon cutoff, with line shapes compared against Lindhard and local-field theories. EELS in low-density metals and semimetals, electronic Raman scattering in metals, and earlier RIXS on metals and doped cuprates (refs. 2–4) have also accessed intraband particle-hole excitations. The real advance is momentum-resolved observation of the continuum's dispersion in a good metal at small q (q ≪ k_F), where screening hides it from total-charge probes. The abstract and introduction should say so and cite the earlier work.
