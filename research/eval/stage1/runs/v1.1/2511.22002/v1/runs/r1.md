Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper uses SI-STM at 4.5 K on CsV3Sb5 with Sn on the Sb site (x = 0.15/0.1, 0.33, 0.68) and Ti on the V site (y = 0.2). It reports that, after the 2x2 CDW and the 4x1 stripes are suppressed, nanoscale regions remain in which the electronic modulations are unidirectional, in three orientations. The authors conclude that electronic nematicity is ubiquitous across the AV3Sb5 phase diagram, is unrelated to the 2x2 CDW, and is the "parent state" of kagome superconductors. Whether rotational symmetry breaking in AV3Sb5 is driven by the CDW or is independent of it is an important open question. Using doping to remove the CDW is a sensible way to separate the two. As presented, though, the data show local anisotropy in strongly disordered alloys, and the paper does not yet show that this anisotropy is a correlated electronic nematic state rather than a direct imprint of the dopant disorder.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The question is timely and important, and the doping strategy addresses it directly. Using two substitution sites (Sb-site Sn and V-site Ti) is a real strength.
- The data cover a wide composition range. Topographic FTs (Fig. 1g-k) clearly show the 2x2 CDW disappearing, consistent with bulk scattering.
- The energy-resolved FT linecuts (Fig. 2b vs 2d) clearly contrast the sharp, non-dispersive 2x2 peaks of the pristine compound with the broad, energy-limited 1/2 Q_Bragg feature at x = 0.33.
- The argument that domains of different orientation under the same tip (Figs. 3, 4) rule out a simple tip-induced anisotropy is sound as far as it goes.
- The DFT-based CEC autocorrelation (Fig. 2i-l) gives a plausible scattering origin for the 1/2 Q_Bragg wave vector at some energies.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The anisotropy is not separated from the local symmetry of the dopant disorder (Figs. 3, 4; Discussion). The central claim is that the doped samples host short-range electronic nematic domains, i.e. a correlated state. The more ordinary explanation is that each region looks anisotropic because the dopants, and the scattering they produce, break C6 locally. For Ti this is especially serious. The V site in the kagome plane has site symmetry mmm, so a single Ti substituent already lowers the local symmetry from C6 to C2, with exactly three orientations. At y = 0.2 (about 7% of V sites) the dopants are roughly 1 nm apart, comparable to the ~2-4 nm "domains" in Fig. 4. Random Ti placement would therefore produce patches of C2 anisotropy in three orientations with boundaries between them, without any electronic nematic order. For Sn the Sb1 site is C6-symmetric, but any finite random configuration of scatterers gives locally anisotropic QPI. The authors themselves attribute the Sn-sample modulations to "anisotropic electron scattering and interference" (p. 3, 5), and a QPI pattern's local anisotropy follows the scatterer configuration. Before the word "nematic" is justified, the authors should do the following:
   - Image dopant positions in the same fields of view (or identify them from topography at suitable bias), and test whether the local anisotropy direction is predicted by the nearby dopant configuration.
   - Show that the nematic correlation length clearly exceeds the mean dopant spacing.
   - Compare against a null model, for example simulated T-matrix or tight-binding QPI for random, uncorrelated dopants on the real lattice, run through the same analysis. Then show that the measured domain size, order-parameter distribution or correlation length is statistically incompatible with uncorrelated disorder.

2. There is no quantitative nematic order parameter, and the domains in Fig. 3 are hand-selected. Fig. 3a,c and Fig. 4 show three or two hand-picked boxes of a few nm, with FTs only a handful of pixels across. The "peak along one direction" in the Fig. 3b,d insets is judged by eye against substantial noise. Selection bias cannot be excluded: in any disordered image one can find small boxes where one direction dominates by chance. The authors should define a local order parameter over the full field of view, for example the anisotropy among the three 1/2 Q_Bragg (or Bragg) Fourier amplitudes computed with a Lawler-Fujita-type demodulation and a stated filter length. They should then show:
   - its spatial map;
   - its histogram, including the fraction of area that is significantly anisotropic;
   - its spatial autocorrelation and correlation length, with the filter length as an explicit lower bound;
   - the same analysis on the pristine sample and on a simulated isotropic-noise or random-disorder image as references.

   The claim in the abstract that this "persists in all samples" also needs this analysis for each composition, including x ≈ 0.1. For x ≈ 0.1 the paper shows only the stripe correlations and states that no 1/2 Q_Bragg features exist (p. 4, Extended Data Fig. 1), yet Fig. 1b marks it inside the nematic region.

3. The energy dependence of the domain pattern, which can tell the two scenarios apart, is not shown. If the modulations are QPI that disperses with energy (p. 4: "the modulation wave vector shows slight dispersion and notably changes in shape"), then a static nematic order and dopant-specific scattering make different predictions. A static nematic order should give a domain map that is largely independent of energy. Pure interference around scatterers should give a pattern that changes with energy. Fig. 3a (x = 0.33) is shown only at -200 mV and Fig. 3c (x = 0.68) only at +50 mV, and the Ti data (Fig. 4) are a single topograph at +100 mV. The authors should compute the order-parameter map from concern 2 at several energies within the same dI/dV map, both inside and outside the window where 1/2 Q_Bragg is strong (Fig. 2d), and report the cross-correlation between the maps. For the Ti sample, bias-dependent topographs or dI/dV maps of the same area are needed to show that the Bragg-amplitude anisotropy is electronic and not a setpoint or tip-height effect.

4. The claims of a "parent state", an unrelated mechanism and a phase diagram are not supported by data at 4.5 K only (title, abstract, Fig. 1b, Discussion). All measurements are at about 4.5 K, yet Fig. 1b shades the whole region up to 100 K as "Electronic nematicity". The title and abstract describe nematicity as the parent state "under which other exotic low-temperature phenomena subsequently emerge". No onset temperature is measured, no link to the pristine-sample nematicity at T* ~ 80-100 K is shown, and the superconducting state of the doped samples is not characterised. The authors should either:
   - measure the anisotropy (with the order parameter of concern 2) at several temperatures, at least for one composition, to establish that it is a thermodynamic state with an onset; or
   - restrict the title, abstract and Fig. 1b to what is measured: local C6 breaking at 4.5 K in doped samples without long-range CDW.

   Relatedly, the evidence that the doped-sample anisotropy is "unrelated to the 2x2 CDW" rests on the absence of long-range 2x2 peaks. The real-space modulations in Fig. 3 are themselves at about 1/2 Q_Bragg = Q_CDW. Short-range, unidirectional 2x2-like correlations pinned by disorder would look much like Fig. 3b,d. The three arguments on p. 4 do not exclude this:
   - absence at x ≈ 0.1 says nothing about x ≥ 0.33;
   - energy-limited visibility is common for CDW contrast in dI/dV;
   - "slight dispersion" is not quantified.

   The authors should plot q(E) for the broad peak with error bars from fits and compare it quantitatively with the DFT joint-DOS prediction. That would show whether the dispersion is resolved beyond the peak width (~20% of Q_Bragg).

B. Should address

5. The text contradicts itself about "q = 0 nematicity" versus finite-q unidirectional modulations. The Discussion (p. 6) describes "short-range q = 0 electronic nematicity", but the Sn-sample evidence is unidirectional modulation at finite q ≈ 1/2 Q_Bragg. That describes a local unidirectional density wave (smectic-like) or anisotropic QPI, not a q = 0 nematic order parameter. Only the Ti data (Bragg-peak anisotropy) are directly q = 0. The authors should state which observable is the nematic order parameter in each sample and why the two signatures belong to the same state.

6. The mirror-symmetry statements are unclear and not quantified (p. 2, 5, 6). The kagome plane has six in-plane mirror lines. A unidirectional cosine modulation along Q_A preserves the mirrors parallel and perpendicular to Q_A. The text says each domain "breaks two in-plane mirror symmetries" and preserves "one", which counts only three. Please specify which mirrors are meant. Also test mirror preservation quantitatively: compare the Fourier amplitudes at mirror-related wave vectors, or the real-space mirror correlation, within each domain, with uncertainties. The comparison with CsTi3Bi5 ("the same local symmetry") and the remark about a possible chiral state both depend on this.

7. The DFT and autocorrelation comparison is qualitative and its assumptions are not justified (Fig. 2i-l, Methods). Several choices need justification or testing:
   - The calculation uses x ~ 0.25 while the data are at x = 0.33.
   - It uses a linear interpolation of Wannier hoppings between CsV3Sb5 and CsV3Sb4Sn, which captures the average band filling but not disorder broadening.
   - CECs are shown only at kz = 0, although the Fermi surface has kz dispersion.
   - The DFT energies are compared directly with experiment without a stated shift or renormalisation.
   - The autocorrelation "assuming all scattering processes are possible" (Fig. 2j, l) shows many features absent from the data.

   Please give the energy alignment used, show the kz dependence or a kz-integrated JDOS, and compare the predicted and measured q(E) of the 1/2 Q_Bragg feature quantitatively (see concern 4). A point that should also be stated: any pocket centred at M produces 1/2 Q_Bragg-type scattering by symmetry, so the match of the wave vector alone does not discriminate between models.

8. Fig. 1i-k and the text disagree about 1/2 Q_Bragg in topographs. Page 4 states that "very broad FT peaks around each 1/2 Q_Bragg position" appear in topograph FTs for x ≥ 0.33 (Fig. 1i,j). Yet Fig. 1k is said to show "the absence of these peaks" for all Sn samples, and its x = 0.33 and 0.68 linecuts look featureless at Q_CDW. Please reconcile this: state the direction and width of the 1k linecuts and show whether a broad hump is present. Also, topographs at +20 mV integrate the LDOS from 0 to 20 mV. Seeing 1/2 Q_Bragg there partly undercuts the argument that the feature appears "only at some energies".

9. Sample characterisation and statistics are missing. The paper gives no measured composition (EDS or WDS) for the STM crystals, only nominal ratios. The x ≈ 0.1 (or 0.15; both values appear) and Ti samples have no growth description for the relevant composition. The paper also gives no Tc or transport and susceptibility data for the doped crystals, and not the number of samples, cleaves, fields of view or tips per composition. Since the claim is "in all samples", please report these and show that the domain phenomenology appears in more than one cleave or tip per composition. Show also that the Sb termination is identified in the doped samples, where the topographs (Fig. 1d-f) are highly disordered and surface Sn segregation is possible.

10. Strain and lattice distortion are not tested as the source of local anisotropy. The authors cite strain (Ref. 30) as an extrinsic factor in the Discussion but do not test it. Lawler-Fujita analysis of the atomic lattice in the same fields of view can map local shear and uniaxial strain. Correlating that map with the nematic order-parameter map would show whether the anisotropy follows local strain from cleaving or dopant-size mismatch. For the Ti data this is essential, since the nematic signature is itself an anisotropy of the Bragg-peak amplitude.

11. The tip-artifact control for the Ti data is incomplete. Domains with different orientations under one tip rule out a uniform tip anisotropy. However, Bragg-amplitude maps such as Fig. 4e,f are sensitive to drift, piezo nonlinearity, scan direction and the Fourier filter width. Please state the filter width (it sets the smallest resolvable domain), show forward and backward scans or rotated scan frames, and show that the domain boundary in Fig. 4a (dashed line) is the same at different setpoints. The Ti field of view in Fig. 4 is only about 8 nm across. Larger fields of view with several domains of all three orientations are needed to support a general statement.

12. Relation to Ref. 44 and to prior pristine-sample nematicity. The note citing concurrent work on Ti-doped CsV3Sb5 (Ref. 44, "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid") should be followed by a short comparison of the observations and symmetry assignments. The Discussion should also explain how the 4.5 K local anisotropy in doped samples relates to the bulk nematic signals in pristine samples above T_CDW (Ref. 38). The "parent state" interpretation depends on that link.

C. Minor

13. Notation is inconsistent:
   - The Methods use CsV3-xTixSb5 (x = 0.2) but the main text uses y = 0.2.
   - The caption of Fig. 4 writes CsV3-ySb5Tiy.
   - The Sn compositions are given as x = 0.15 (p. 3) and x ≈ 0.1 (p. 4, Fig. 1).
   - The DFT uses x ~ 0.25.

14. Extended Data Figs. 1-3 are cited, and ED Fig. 1 carries one of the three arguments against short-range CDW, but the figures are not included in this version. Please include them.

15. Fig. 1b uses a broken temperature axis (1-5 K and 80-100 K) with a continuous green "nematicity" region. It should be marked as schematic, and the nematic shading should be limited to measured points (see concern 4).

16. Fig. 2b and d use different normalisation and setpoints (1.5 GΩ vs 0.6 GΩ, 4 mV vs 20 mV excitation). A large 20 mV excitation limits energy resolution for the claimed dispersion. Please state the effective resolution.

17. Fig. 3 labels the domains Q^A_CDW, Q^B_CDW and Q^C_CDW, while the text argues the modulations are not CDW. A neutral label such as q_A or 1/2 Q^A_Bragg would avoid confusion.

18. Page 5 has the typo "lights up in for". Page 2 has "inter-layer phasing [1]"; a primary reference would be preferable.

19. Data and code are available only "upon reasonable request". Depositing the dI/dV maps would allow independent order-parameter analysis.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Build the whole case around one full-field-of-view nematic order-parameter map per composition. Report its energy dependence, histogram and correlation length, and compare it with a simulated random-dopant QPI null model on the same lattice.
- Add dopant-resolved analysis, especially for Ti, whose V-site symmetry is itself C2. Showing that the domain orientation does not simply follow the local dopant configuration would be the most convincing evidence for a correlated nematic state.
- Measure at least one composition at several temperatures (for example 4.5 K, 20-40 K and 80+ K) to give the "parent state" language an experimental basis, or reframe the title and abstract.
- Add a quantitative q(E) dispersion plot with fitted peak positions and widths, overlaid on the DFT JDOS. This would also strengthen the case that the feature is not a short-range 2x2 CDW.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
