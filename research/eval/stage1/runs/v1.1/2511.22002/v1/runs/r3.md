Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper uses SI-STM on CsV3Sb5 with the 2×2 CDW suppressed by Sn (x ≈ 0.1, 0.33, 0.68) or Ti (y = 0.2) substitution. It reports nanoscale regions in which the ~½Q_Bragg dI/dV modulations (Sn) or the Bragg-peak lattice modulations (Ti) are stronger along one lattice direction. From this it concludes that electronic nematicity is ubiquitous across the AV3Sb5 phase diagram, unrelated to the 2×2 CDW, and is the "parent state" of kagome superconductors. Whether rotational symmetry breaking in AV3Sb5 is separate from the 2×2 CDW is a central open question, so a clean demonstration would matter a great deal. The CDW-free doped samples are a sensible platform for testing it. As written, however, the evidence is mostly qualitative: hand-picked windows, a few images and missing Extended Data. It falls well short of the strength of the headline claim.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The question is important, and the strategy is well chosen: suppress the 2×2 CDW and see whether the rotational symmetry breaking survives. Using two substitution sites (Sb site and kagome V site) adds generality.
- The data show convincingly that long-range 2×2 order is absent at x ≥ 0.1 and y = 0.2 (Fig. 1g–k, Fig. 4b inset), in agreement with bulk studies.
- In Fig. 3 and Fig. 4, domains of different orientation appear within one field of view taken with one tip. This is a reasonable first argument against a pure tip-shape artifact.
- The DFT constant-energy contours and their autocorrelation (Fig. 2i–l) offer a plausible scattering origin for the energy-dependent broad ½Q_Bragg features at x = 0.33.
- The point in the Discussion that a large-field FT can look C6-symmetric even when every domain is locally C2 is useful and correct.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. Short-range, disorder-pinned 2×2 CDW correlations are not excluded as the source of the local anisotropy. The anisotropic modulations in Fig. 3 sit at "about ½Q_Bragg", which is exactly the 2×2 CDW wave vector. Pristine CsV3Sb5 already shows a 2×2 state with a unidirectional component that breaks rotational symmetry. The most economical alternative is therefore that dopants pin short-range, locally unidirectional 2×2 (or 2×2×n) charge correlations, i.e. fluctuating CDW remnants. That would make the anisotropy a disordered descendant of the CDW, not something "unrelated to the 2 × 2 charge density wave" (abstract). The paper rules this out on p. 4 with three arguments, and none is decisive:
   - The ½Q peaks are absent at x ~ 0.1. That evidence is in Extended Data Fig. 1, which is not in the submission.
   - The peaks appear only at some energies. Pinned short-range CDW contrast is also strongly bias dependent and can show contrast inversion.
   - There is "slight dispersion" and a change of shape with energy. This is not quantified, and the supporting Extended Data Fig. 2 is also missing.

   The authors should:
   - include Extended Data Figs. 1–3;
   - plot the peak position against energy, |q|(E), with error bars, for the ½Q feature along each direction, and compare it with the DFT-predicted dispersion and with a non-dispersive CDW;
   - check whether the local real-space modulation phase is locked to the lattice in 2×2 fashion (for example from a phase map in a Lawler–Fujita-type analysis);
   - show the anisotropy at an energy where the ½Q feature is absent, which would separate a Fermi-surface (q = 0) nematic from anisotropic ½Q correlations;
   - if possible, cite or provide bulk diffuse X-ray scattering at x = 0.33/0.68 that bounds short-range 2×2 correlations.

   Without this, the central claim that the anisotropy is "distinct from the symmetry of the 2 × 2 CDW" is not established.

2. Nothing shows quantitatively that the local anisotropy exceeds the random fluctuations expected from isotropic scattering off randomly placed dopants. The evidence for C2 domains is three hand-selected windows per sample (Fig. 3b,d), each about 5–8 nm wide, with FTs a few pixels across. In a heavily disordered alloy (x = 0.33 and 0.68 mean about 7–14% of Sb sites substituted), QPI from a random dopant arrangement will always produce windows where one ½Q direction is randomly stronger than the others, even if the electronic structure is C6-symmetric on average. The authors should:
   - define a local nematic order parameter, e.g. η(r) = [A_A − (A_B + A_C)/2]/ΣA computed with a stated filter length, or the analogous C2 decomposition;
   - map η(r) over the whole field of view;
   - give its histogram, spatial correlation length and the fraction of area in each domain type;
   - compare these with a null model: the same analysis on maps simulated from isotropic scatterers at the measured dopant density, or on the same data after C6 symmetrization or with randomized scatterer positions;
   - show that the domain sizes and anisotropy amplitudes are statistically significant and do not change with filter length.

   They should also state how the "three highlighted regions" were chosen and how many such regions were examined. As it stands, the claim that the state "locally breaks rotational six-fold symmetry" rests on visual inspection of selected areas.

3. The "parent state" claim and the phase diagram in Fig. 1b go beyond the data. All measurements are at about 4.5 K (Methods). No data are shown at temperatures where the "parent" state should exist, above the pristine T_CDW of ~80–100 K. Even so, Fig. 1b shades "Electronic nematicity" in green over the entire x–T plane up to 100 K. The relation to superconductivity is also not studied: no T_c is given for the measured crystals, and no measurement is made below T_c. The title and abstract therefore claim an ordering hierarchy ("parent state … under which other exotic low-temperature phenomena subsequently emerge") with no temperature dependence at all. To support it, the authors should either:
   - measure the domain anisotropy and its order parameter (concern 2) at several temperatures, ideally up to and above 80–100 K, or at minimum at clearly elevated temperatures; or
   - restrict the phase diagram to the measured points and reword the title, abstract and Discussion to "low-temperature short-range electronic anisotropy persists after the 2×2 CDW is suppressed".

   The pristine sample is also measured only inside the CDW phase, so the claim that the same nematicity underlies the pristine compound (p. 7, "all of which may arise from the electronic nematic state imaged here") is not supported by the paper's own data.

B. Should address

4. The Ti-doped evidence (Fig. 4) is a different observable and weaker. For y = 0.2 the anisotropy is in Bragg-peak amplitudes of constant-current topographs at a single bias (100 mV). It is shown over a field of view of only a few nm, with one domain wall in Fig. 4a and one more in the missing Extended Data Fig. 3. Bragg-peak amplitude anisotropy in topographs can arise from:
   - local lattice strain or orthorhombic distortion around Ti clusters;
   - surface reconstruction;
   - setpoint effects;
   - tip anisotropy combined with residual scanner drift or nonlinearity along the fast-scan axis.

   The domain wall shows that the effect is not uniform across the image, but not that it is electronic. The authors should show:
   - bias dependence, including both polarities and dI/dV maps;
   - larger fields of view with domain statistics;
   - the same area scanned with rotated scan direction, or a second tip;
   - lattice-constant (strain) maps from the Lawler–Fujita displacement field, to separate structural from electronic anisotropy.

   The Fourier filter width that sets the apparent domain size in Fig. 4e,f should be given. The statement that the "phenomenology is confirmed in two different types of samples" (p. 6) should be softened unless the Ti data reach the same standard as the Sn data. Note also that the observables differ: ½Q QPI in Sn samples, Bragg-peak modulation in Ti samples.

5. Samples, fields of view and reproducibility are not documented. The paper does not say how many crystals, cleaves, tips and fields of view were studied at each composition. Nor does it say whether domains with the same character appear on independent cleaves, or whether the domain pattern stays put when the bias or tip changes. "Persists in all samples" (abstract) needs a table of samples and maps, with the observation status of the domains in each. For x ≈ 0.1 in particular, the text reports only unidirectional stripe correlations and no ½Q features, so it is unclear what evidence for "nematic domains" exists at that composition, even though Fig. 1b places a data point there.

6. The mirror-symmetry statements are qualitative and internally inconsistent. The Introduction (p. 3) says each domain "appears to break all but one in-plane mirror symmetries". Results (p. 5) says each region "breaks two in-plane mirror symmetries but appears to generally preserve the in-plane mirror symmetry along the direction of the arrows". The Discussion (p. 6) says the domains are "C2-symmetric, with two in-plane mirror lines that are clearly broken, and one that appears to be intact". The kagome Sb surface has six in-plane mirror lines, not three, and a C2 domain derived from C6v would retain two mirrors (C2v), not one. The authors should:
   - state which mirrors are tested;
   - quantify mirror breaking, e.g. by comparing intensities of mirror-related FT peaks within each domain with uncertainties;
   - reconcile this with the possibility, raised in their own Discussion, of a chiral state.

   As written, it cannot be told whether the local symmetry is C2v, C2 or lower. That matters for the comparison with CsTi3Bi5 and with Ref. 44.

7. Composition calibration is missing. The Sn contents x ≈ 0.33 and 0.68 appear to be nominal flux ratios (Methods, 25:18.75:80:40 and 25:18.75:60:60), and Ti y = 0.2 is nominal (Cs20V12Ti3.5Sb120). No EDS, WDS or refinement values are given, and nothing is said about inhomogeneity across a crystal. Measured local dopant density, from counting substitutional defects in topographs, would anchor both the compositions and the null model in concern 2. Whether the CDW is suppressed should also be checked at the actual, not nominal, compositions.

8. The DFT comparison is qualitative and cannot address the anisotropy. The calculation linearly interpolates hoppings between CsV3Sb5 and CsV3Sb4Sn at x ~ 0.25, not the measured 0.33. It shows only k_z = 0 contours and assumes all scattering channels are allowed. By construction the CECs are C6-symmetric, so the theory explains at best why ½Q features appear at some energies, not why they are locally C2. The authors should:
   - say whether the Sn-induced hole doping (Fermi-level shift) is captured by the interpolation;
   - compare the predicted energy window and dispersion of the M-pocket scattering with the measured window (Fig. 2d, ellipse) quantitatively;
   - discuss k_z integration;
   - state explicitly that the calculation is used only to assign the wave vector, not the symmetry breaking.

9. The role of disorder needs to be addressed directly. The Discussion attributes the short-range character to dopant disorder and leaves this for future work. But dopants can also create the anisotropy, through local uniaxial strain from clustered or correlated Sn/Ti arrangements. The authors should correlate the domain map with dopant positions or local topographic height. In particular, they should check whether domain walls follow dopant-rich regions (the bright patches in Fig. 3c at x = 0.68 look like possible clusters) and whether domain orientation correlates with local strain.

C. Minor

10. Composition labels are inconsistent. The text lists x = 0.15, 0.33, 0.68, but the figures and text use x = 0.1 / x ~ 0.1. Methods describe growth only for x ~ 0.33 and 0.68. The Ti sample is "y = 0.2" in the text and Fig. 4 but "x = 0.2" in Methods, and the Fig. 4 title writes "CsV3−ySb5Tiy" while Methods writes "CsV3−xTixSb5". DFT uses x ~ 0.25.
11. Extended Data Figs. 1–3 are cited in the text but not included. They are needed for concerns 1 and 4.
12. Fig. 1k is described as linecuts "along the Q_stripe direction (if present)". For x ≥ 0.33, where no stripe exists, state which Bragg direction was used. Show linecuts along all three directions, since that is the symmetry-relevant comparison.
13. In Fig. 1b, the T axis is broken and the blue diamonds sit at about 4 K. Clarify that they mark 4.5 K, and indicate on the plot that the nematic region at higher T is not measured (see concern 3).
14. The term "q = 0 electronic nematicity" (p. 6) is used for modulations at ½Q_Bragg. Define what q = 0 means here, i.e. the anisotropy of the ½Q modulation amplitudes as a q = 0 order parameter.
15. Setup conditions are missing for Fig. 4e,f (filter width) and for the energy/k_z of the DFT panels beyond those stated. The Fig. 2 caption gives R_tip-sample for some panels and V/I for others; make this uniform.
16. On p. 7, tip anisotropy [41] and strain [30] are named as extrinsic factors to be excluded only for a future chirality study. They are equally relevant to the present C2 claim and should be discussed here.
17. The abstract is numbered as Section I. Minor wording: "lights up in for" (p. 6), "a single crystalline directions" (p. 5).

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Build the analysis around a quantitative local nematic order parameter map with a disorder null model. This one addition would answer concerns 2, 5, 6 and 9 together.
- Add temperature-dependent SI-STM on at least one CDW-free composition, e.g. x = 0.33 at 4.5, 20, 50 and >90 K. This would turn the "parent state" claim from an inference into a measurement.
- Measure the full energy dispersion of the ½Q feature for each domain orientation, and show anisotropy in an energy window free of ½Q scattering, to separate a Fermi-surface nematic from short-range anisotropic 2×2 correlations.
- For the Ti-doped samples, give dI/dV maps, bias dependence and strain maps over larger areas, so that the second substitution route supports the same observable as the Sn data.

5. Verdict: major revision
