Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The paper uses few-femtosecond broadband XUV transient absorption (UBXAS) at the Ti M2,3 and Se M4,5 edges of 1T-TiSe2 to follow photoinduced exciton dissociation as a function of fluence and temperature, including above Tc, where time-resolved ARPES loses its folded-band observable. The two headline claims are (i) short-range excitonic correlations persist in the normal state, inferred from a 1/√F scaling of the initial response time τ_int of Se peak ③ at 300 K that matches the scaling at 23 K, and (ii) an enhanced excitonic susceptibility at Tc, inferred from a cusp-like minimum of the low-fluence τ_int near 190 K. An element- and orbital-selective probe of excitonic correlations through a CDW transition, and above it, would interest a broad audience, and the instrument and dataset are clearly state of the art. The weak point is whether the observables are specific to excitonic correlations rather than to generic carrier screening or lattice-driven critical behaviour.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The technique is new for this question. A 3.4 fs pump and broadband XUV probe resolve sub-100 fs hole generation in Se 4p states with element specificity, and the authors pick a spectral window (Se peak ③, window (iii)) that shows no A1g phonon modulation (Fig. 2e), which removes an obvious confound.
- The temperature scans at fixed delay (Fig. 3d,e) show order-parameter-like changes at both edges near Tc ≈ 200 K. This connects the XUV observables to the phase transition.
- The fluence dependences at 23 K and 300 K (Fig. 4a,b,d) and the contrasting fluence-independent response at 190 K (Fig. 4c) form a qualitatively clear dataset. The low- and high-fluence temperature series (Fig. 4e) are internally consistent with the fluence series.
- The data are deposited (ref. 53).

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **The 1/√F scaling of τ_int is not shown to be specific to excitonic correlations, which is the only evidence for "persistent excitonic fluctuations" above Tc.** (p. 1792, "Persistent excitonic fluctuations in the normal state"; Fig. 4b,d.) The argument goes: τ_int ∝ 1/ω_pl ∝ 1/√n_tot, so a 1/√F trend means holes are released by screening-driven exciton breakup. But a plasmon- or screening-limited timescale for generating mobile Se 4p holes would appear in any photoexcited semimetal or small-gap semiconductor: the rate of carrier redistribution, Auger-like scattering and screening build-up all speed up with density, with or without pre-existing bound pairs. The model's own constant offset τ0 is said to represent hole creation "in the absence of excitonic interactions", yet nothing shows that a non-excitonic process would not also scale as 1/√F. Three further points weaken the inference:
   - The fluence range is about one decade (0.2–3 mJ cm⁻²) with a free offset τ0. Over that range, 1/√F + τ0 is hard to tell apart from other power laws or a saturating form.
   - The 23 K and 300 K data fall on the same curve with the same τ0 and prefactor (Fig. 4d), although the paper itself says the excitonic population differs strongly between the condensed phase and a fluctuating normal state (compare the amplitudes at 25 K and 310 K in Fig. 3c,e). A timescale insensitive to the presence of long-range order and to the size of the excitonic signal reads more naturally as a generic carrier-density effect than as exciton-specific physics.
   - Alternative processes are relegated to Supplementary Note 8 with a one-line summary.

   What to do: (a) fit with the exponent free, τ_int = A·F^(−β) + τ0, at each temperature, and report β with uncertainties and the fit quality compared with alternative forms; (b) bring the discrimination against non-excitonic, density-dependent hole-generation processes into the main text and say which observable each alternative fails to reproduce; (c) ideally, add a reference measurement where excitonic correlations should be absent or strongly weakened under matched conditions (for example a doped or intercalated TiSe2 with CDW suppressed, an isostructural non-excitonic dichalcogenide with a comparable Se M-edge, or a much higher temperature where the fluctuations should have decayed) and show that the 1/√F law, or its prefactor, changes there. Without one of these, the normal-state claim should be worded as "consistent with" rather than as evidence for short-range excitonic correlations.

2. **The cusp in τ_int at Tc (Fig. 4e) is attributed to a diverging excitonic susceptibility, but the paper does not rule out lattice (soft-phonon) critical behaviour or a temperature-dependent melting threshold, and the fit is underdetermined.** (p. 1792–1793, "Enhanced excitonic susceptibility at Tc", Eq. (1); Fig. 4c,e; Discussion p. 1793–1794.) Three issues:
   - *Lattice alternative.* The CDW soft phonon at the L point softens strongly towards Tc (the paper's own ref. 40). The Discussion excludes phonon contributions only through the ~20% photoinduced renormalization of the CDW phonon at low temperature (ref. 28), which addresses the fluence dependence at 23 K but not the temperature dependence near Tc, where lattice susceptibility also diverges. Any order-parameter softening near a continuous transition, excitonic or Peierls/Jahn–Teller-like, would make the ordered state more fragile to a weak perturbation. Nothing in Fig. 4e shows the enhancement belongs to the excitonic rather than the lattice channel.
   - *Threshold alternative.* The fluence needed to fully suppress the ordered-state response falls as T → Tc. At 190 K, 0.2 mJ cm⁻² may already exceed that threshold, so the low-fluence response joins the high-fluence plateau. That would explain both the fluence independence in Fig. 4c,d and the fact that the low-fluence minimum (~70 fs) coincides with the high-fluence level (~75 fs, red line in Fig. 4e) rather than dropping below it. Eq. (1) does not obviously predict this coincidence. The high-fluence data are flat at ~75 fs at all temperatures, which suggests a floor that the model does not contain.
   - *Fit robustness.* The low-fluence curve has about eight temperature points, with one point carrying the cusp. The model has at least χ0, the χex prefactor, Tc, α and the τ0/proportionality constants from Eq. (1) and Fig. 4d. The fitted α, Tc and their uncertainties are not given in the main text, and the curve is labelled only "Fit".

   What to do: report all fitted parameters with uncertainties and say which were fixed (for example τ0 = 25 fs from Fig. 4d); show that a non-divergent form (for example a smooth minimum, or a threshold crossing with a T-dependent threshold) fits the data significantly worse; add low-fluence points between 170 and 230 K so the cusp does not rest on one temperature; measure the fluence threshold for full suppression as a function of temperature (or show the 0.2 mJ cm⁻² transient amplitude across Tc relative to its saturated value) to address the threshold scenario; and discuss explicitly why lattice softening near Tc cannot produce the same dip. If these cannot be separated, describe the result as enhanced susceptibility of the coupled exciton–lattice order near Tc rather than as excitonic susceptibility specifically.

**B. Should address**

3. **Equilibrium carrier density and absorbed fluence change with temperature, but the model uses n_tot ∝ F.** (p. 1792, Eq. (1); Fig. 4d,e; Fig. 3.) Across the CDW transition, 1T-TiSe2 changes its equilibrium free-carrier density (gap opening and band folding, Fig. 1d,e) and its optical constants at 1.65 eV, the pump photon energy. The plasmon frequency that sets τ_int should depend on the total mobile density, equilibrium plus photoexcited, so at 300 K the larger thermal density should shift the 1/√n_tot curve relative to 23 K. Likewise, a temperature-dependent reflectivity or absorption at 750 nm changes the absorbed density at fixed incident fluence, which feeds directly into Fig. 3d,e and Fig. 4e. Please give the temperature-dependent absorbed fluence (or reflectivity at 750 nm), include the equilibrium density in the n_tot model, and show whether the conclusions survive these corrections.

4. **Effective sample temperature and the location of Tc.** (Fig. 3 caption: continuous ramp, 5 K bins of 30 spectra; Fig. 4e.) The cusp argument needs the temperature of the probed volume to be known to a few kelvin. Please state the repetition rate and estimate steady-state laser heating at 0.2 and 2.0 mJ cm⁻²; give the thermal lag during the continuous ramp (and compare up- and down-sweeps, or hold the temperature fixed); and say how the nominal 190 K point in Fig. 4c,e relates to the Tc ≈ 200 K inferred from Fig. 3. A 10 K offset between the probed and nominal temperatures would change how "near Tc" the minimum is.

5. **Samples: number, thickness and reproducibility.** The main text does not say how many samples were measured, how the transmission samples were made (thickness, substrate, exfoliation or microtomy), or whether the key results (the Fig. 2b sign reversal, the Fig. 3 onsets, the 1/√F scaling and the Fig. 4e cusp) were reproduced on more than one sample or after thermal cycling. Thin TiSe2 flakes can show thickness- and strain-dependent Tc and inhomogeneity. The 350 K high-fluence point had to be taken at a reduced 1.1 mJ cm⁻² "to prevent sample degradation" (Fig. 4 caption), which raises the question of whether earlier high-fluence exposures altered the sample. Please report sample count and thickness, and show reproducibility, including a low-fluence measurement repeated after the high-fluence series to confirm reversibility.

6. **Extraction of τ_int and its error bars.** (Fig. 4a–d; Supplementary Eq. (1).) τ_int is the central quantity, but the main text does not define it. It is unclear whether it is a rise time, an exponential constant or an error-function width, how it correlates with the fitted amplitude and delay-zero, and how it is deconvolved from the IRF shown in Fig. 4a. The error bars are 1 s.d. fitting uncertainties only. Please define τ_int in the main text, give its correlation with the other free parameters, and estimate scan-to-scan reproducibility (for example from repeated scans at one condition), since the claimed differences (for example 190 fs against ~75 fs) rest on single fits to traces that, at low fluence, are visibly noisy and have been vertically rescaled.

7. **The low-fluence sign reversal of the Ti many-body continuum is under-characterized.** (p. 1791; Fig. 2b,d.) The sign flip at 200 fs (green dashed box) is presented as "qualitatively different" evidence of a non-carrier process tied to the low-temperature order, and it motivates the choice of window (ii) in Fig. 3d. But it is shown at only two fluences, its amplitude (~0.1 × 10⁻³) is close to the noise in Fig. 2d, and no mechanism is offered for why a reduced fluence reverses the sign. A fluence series showing where the crossover occurs, and a statement of whether the flip is present above Tc, would show whether this feature tracks excitonic order or a different competition, such as state filling against screening.

8. **"Clear onset at Tc" in Fig. 3d,e is asserted, not quantified.** (p. 1792; Fig. 3d,e.) The blue curves are "guides to the eye". In Fig. 3e the Se signal keeps changing above Tc (from about −4 to −3 × 10⁻³ between 200 and 300 K), and in Fig. 3d the Ti signal crosses zero well below Tc. Please fit both with an explicit order-parameter form plus a smooth background and report the onset temperature with uncertainty. The residual temperature dependence above Tc in Fig. 3e is itself relevant to the fluctuation claim and should be discussed.

**C. Minor**

9. The Discussion's claim that normal-state correlations are "likely confined in-plane" (p. 1794) and the schematic in Fig. 4g are not supported by any measurement in this paper. State that this is an interpretation based on ref. 26.

10. At 0.2 mJ cm⁻² and 23 K one would expect the CDW amplitude mode (~3.4 THz) as well as the 6.0 THz A1g mode. Please say whether it is seen in the low-fluence traces (Fig. 2b–f). Its presence or absence is a useful check of whether the low fluence is below the melting threshold.

11. Fig. 4d: the 190 K series has fewer points and larger error bars than the 23 K and 300 K series. "Approximately independent of the pump fluence" should come with a quantitative statement, for example a slope consistent with zero.

12. Fig. 1: the assignment of the 38–53 eV hump to a many-body continuum relies on TDDFT in Supplementary Fig. 12. One sentence in the main text on what the independent-particle calculation fails to reproduce, quantitatively, would help.

13. The closing statement that UBXAS can "establish definitive benchmarks" for other candidates (Ta2NiSe5, Ta2Pd3Te5) is broader than this work supports, given concerns 1 and 2. Consider softening it.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a reference system or condition where excitonic correlations should be absent and show that the τ_int fluence law changes there. This is the single most effective way to make the normal-state claim specific.
2. Densify the low-fluence temperature series around Tc and add an intermediate fluence (for example 0.5 mJ cm⁻²) across temperature. This would turn Fig. 4e from a two-curve comparison into a fluence–temperature map and test the threshold scenario directly.
3. Put the key fit parameters (β, τ0, α, Tc, χ0/χex) and their uncertainties in a small table in the main text or Extended Data.
4. Plot the equilibrium optical constants at 1.65 eV and the absorbed fluence against temperature alongside Fig. 4e.

**5. Verdict**

Verdict: major revision
