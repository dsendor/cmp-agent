Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The paper uses few-femtosecond broadband XUV transient absorption (UBXAS) at the Ti M2,3 and Se M4,5 edges of 1T-TiSe2. It reports three findings. A phonon-insensitive Se 4p feature (peak 3) tracks the CDW/excitonic order. Its initial response time follows a 1/sqrt(F) fluence scaling both below and above Tc, which the authors read as short-range excitonic correlations in the normal state. At low fluence this response time has a cusp-like minimum near Tc, which they read as enhanced excitonic susceptibility. A probe that sees short-range electron-hole correlations above Tc, where ARPES gap and folding signatures are gone, would be a real advance in the long debate over whether TiSe2 is an excitonic insulator. The technique also carries over to Ta2NiSe5 and Ta2Pd3Te5. The novelty rests mainly on how the fluence and temperature dependence of a single timescale is interpreted, so the strength of that interpretation decides the impact.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Technically impressive measurement: 3.4 fs pump, broadband XUV probe covering Ti and Se edges at once, cryogenic operation from 23 K to 350 K, with element and orbital selectivity backed by DFT/TDDFT density-of-states assignments (Fig. 1).
- The spectral window is chosen with care. Peak 3 (window iii) shows no A1g coherent-phonon modulation (Fig. 2e), so phonon contamination of the dynamics is reduced.
- The order-parameter-like temperature dependence of the transient signal in both the Ti continuum and the Se edge (Fig. 3d,e) is clean and fits the assignment.
- The fluence dependence below Tc matches earlier time-resolved ARPES (ref. 20). That cross-check makes the observable credible.
- The non-monotonic low-fluence temperature dependence alongside the temperature-independent high-fluence response (Fig. 4e) is a striking, qualitatively new observation.
- The data are deposited publicly (ref. 53).

3. Concerns

A. Must address

(None. The main observations are supported by the data shown; the issues below concern how far the interpretation can be pushed.)

B. Should address

1. The susceptibility model (Eq. 1) does not appear to account for the high-fluence data or for the 190 K fluence series. Eq. 1 writes n_tot ∝ F[χ0 + χex(T)], and the text sets τ_int ∝ 1/sqrt(n_tot) with offset τ0 = 25 fs (Fig. 4d). Taken literally, the model makes two predictions:
   - (a) A diverging χex at Tc should drive τ_int towards τ0 ≈ 25 fs at every fluence.
   - (b) The relative temperature modulation should be present at high fluence too, only compressed.

   The data show something else:
   - At 190 K, τ_int sits at about 70 to 90 fs and is roughly independent of fluence from 0.2 to 2.5 mJ cm−2 (Fig. 4c,d), not near 25 fs and not following 1/sqrt(F).
   - At 2.0 mJ cm−2, τ_int is flat at about 70 fs across all temperatures (Fig. 4e, red), with no dip at Tc.

   Both regimes look like the same floor of about 70 fs, which points to a limiting timescale (for example a minimum electron-electron or electron-phonon scattering time for generating mobile Se 4p holes) rather than to the plasmon scaling with τ0 = 25 fs. This matters because the claim of "enhanced excitonic susceptibility" rests on Eq. 1 reproducing the cusp in Fig. 4e. The authors should:
   - state explicitly in the main text how the fitted curve in Fig. 4e (Supplementary Equation (9)) handles the floor, and which parameters are free (χ0, prefactor, Tc, α, τ0);
   - check that one parameter set fits Fig. 4d at 23 K, 300 K and 190 K and both curves of Fig. 4e together;
   - report α and the fitted Tc with uncertainties.

   If the minimum at Tc is really a low-fluence curve reaching a saturation floor, the evidence for a divergence is weaker than the text suggests.

2. The cusp at Tc rests on a single temperature point. The low-fluence series in Fig. 4e has seven temperatures spaced 40 to 50 K apart. The minimum is defined by the single 190 K point, and the fitted |T − Tc|^−α form (with χ0, amplitude, α and possibly Tc as free parameters) is barely constrained. To claim a divergent susceptibility, the authors should:
   - add temperatures within ±20 K of Tc, in steps comparable to the 5 K used in Fig. 3;
   - show that the minimum sits at the Tc set by the static and transient order-parameter data (Fig. 3d,e).

   They should also address the effective sample temperature near Tc. The pump repetition rate and the resulting steady-state heating at 0.2 and 2.0 mJ cm−2 should be quantified, because a few kelvin of average heating moves the 190 K point relative to Tc and changes the apparent shape of the cusp.

3. A diverging susceptibility at Tc does not single out an excitonic driving mechanism. In a coupled exciton-phonon system, the electronic (excitonic) susceptibility at the ordering vector diverges at Tc whether the instability is driven by electrons or by the lattice, because both fields are coupled to the same order parameter. The Discussion rules out a phonon contribution to the fluence dependence using the 20% transient hardening of the CDW phonon (ref. 28). That argument does not address the temperature dependence near Tc, where the soft L-point mode and critical lattice fluctuations (ref. 40) also peak. The authors should:
   - discuss explicitly what feature of Fig. 4e, if any, would differ between an exciton-driven and a Jahn-Teller or electron-phonon-driven transition with residual electron-hole coupling;
   - otherwise soften "evidence for the excitonic instability" (end of the "Enhanced excitonic susceptibility" section) to "consistent with", as the abstract already does.

   The same applies to the normal-state 1/sqrt(F) scaling. A screening-controlled timescale for generating Se 4p holes may appear in any semimetal with Coulomb-enhanced electron-hole scattering, not only one with pre-formed excitons.

4. There is no reference material for the 1/sqrt(F) argument. The claim that 1/sqrt(F) scaling indicates excitonic correlations above Tc (Fig. 4b,d,g) would be much stronger with a null control: a compound with similar band structure and edges but no excitonic correlations, measured with the same protocol. Candidates are 1T-TiS2 (a semiconductor with similar Ti 3d/chalcogen p bands), heavily Cu-intercalated or electron-doped TiSe2 where the CDW and excitonic pairing are suppressed, or TiSe2 at a temperature well above any reported fluctuation regime. If the initial response time there were fluence-independent, or followed a different law, the plasmon-screening interpretation would be persuasive. Supplementary Note 8 is said to rule out alternative processes, but the main text should at least summarize which alternatives were tested and why they fail.

5. The fluence-to-density conversion and depth inhomogeneity should be quantified. The scaling τ ∝ 1/sqrt(n_tot) treats n_tot as proportional to F. Two effects complicate that:
   - Above Tc, TiSe2 already has a sizeable equilibrium carrier density (it is a semimetal or small-gap semiconductor). Whether 1/sqrt(F) or a flattening is expected at low F depends on how n_photo at 0.2 mJ cm−2 compares with n_eq, yet the same τ0 = 25 fs is used in both phases.
   - The 750 nm pump penetration depth is likely comparable to or shorter than the transmission sample thickness, while the XUV probe integrates through the whole thickness. The probed region therefore contains a range of photoexcited densities, and the extracted τ_int is a depth-averaged quantity.

   The authors should give the sample thickness, the pump absorption depth, and estimated photoexcited and equilibrium carrier densities for each fluence. They should also show that depth averaging does not distort the fitted exponent. Fitting the exponent as a free parameter (rather than fixing it at −1/2) and reporting its value and uncertainty at 23 K and 300 K would show how specifically the data pick out 1/sqrt(F).

6. The fluence range crosses the order-melting threshold. The text notes that the Fig. 2a conditions exceed the thresholds for melting the excitonic and CDW order, while 0.2 mJ cm−2 produces a qualitatively different Ti-continuum response (the sign reversal in Fig. 2b,d). Below Tc, the fluence series in Fig. 4a,d therefore spans partial and complete quenching. A crossover between these regimes could by itself give a fluence-dependent initial timescale, unrelated to plasmon screening. The authors should mark the melting threshold on Fig. 4d and show that the 1/sqrt(F) trend holds on both sides of it.

7. The Ti continuum sign reversal at low fluence is reported but not explained. The low-fluence sign flip in window (ii) at 200 fs (Fig. 2b,d) is presented as evidence of "an additional dynamical process distinct from simple carrier excitation", and it motivates the claim that the Ti continuum is sensitive to excitonic correlations. Its origin is never identified, and the window is not used in the later fluence and temperature analysis. The authors should either give a tentative assignment (for example a transient increase in local screening, or a coherent response of the order parameter) supported by its fluence and temperature dependence, or present it more cautiously.

8. Sample statistics and reproducibility are not given in the main text. It is not stated how many crystals or flakes were measured, whether the Fig. 4e temperature dependence and the Fig. 4d fluence series come from the same sample, or whether the 190 K behaviour was reproduced on thermal cycling. The 350 K high-fluence point had to be taken at 1.1 mJ cm−2 "to prevent sample degradation". That implies degradation at elevated temperature and fluence, which could also affect other high-temperature points. The authors should state the number of samples, show the key result (the cusp near Tc) on at least a second sample or a repeated cool-down, and describe how degradation was monitored, for example by re-measuring the static spectrum or a reference point.

C. Minor

9. Fig. 3d,e show the transient amplitude at a fixed 200 fs delay. Because τ_int itself depends strongly on temperature (Fig. 4e), the amplitude at 200 fs may mix the hole-population change with the rise time. A brief check that the signal has reached its extremum by 200 fs at all temperatures (or a plot of the peak amplitude instead) would remove this ambiguity.

10. Fig. 3 data come from a continuous temperature ramp, with each point integrated over about 5 K. The ramp rate, the direction (warming or cooling), any thermal lag between sensor and sample, and any hysteresis should be stated. These control where the "onset at Tc" falls relative to 200 K.

11. Fig. 4e plots the 350 K high-fluence point (1.1 mJ cm−2) with the 2.0 mJ cm−2 points and includes it in the red average. Use a distinct marker or leave it out of the average.

12. The error bars in Fig. 4d,e are 1 s.d. fitting uncertainties. Reporting variation across repeated scans would give a more realistic uncertainty, especially for the low-fluence points, where the signal-to-noise is visibly poorer (Fig. 4a–c, top traces).

13. The schematic in Fig. 4f,g pictures "short-range excitonic correlations" as finite domains of correlated pairs. The data constrain the existence of a fluence-dependent screening timescale but not the correlation length. A sentence noting that no length scale is extracted would avoid over-reading the cartoon.

14. The A1g frequency of 6.0 THz is labelled in Fig. 2 as a normal-state unit-cell mode (ref. 36), but the measurement is at 23 K in the ordered phase, where the amplitude mode near 3.4 THz is also usually seen. Please comment on whether the amplitude mode is visible in any spectral window. Its absence or presence bears on how directly each window couples to the order parameter.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Fit Fig. 4d (all three temperatures) and Fig. 4e (both fluences) together with a single model that includes an explicit floor timescale, and report the fitted exponents with uncertainties. This is the most direct way to show that the cusp at Tc reflects a divergent susceptibility rather than saturation.
- Add a dense low-fluence temperature series within about 20 K of Tc, with the steady-state heating quantified.
- Measure a non-excitonic reference compound (for example TiS2 or a strongly doped TiSe2) with the identical fluence protocol, to establish that 1/sqrt(F) scaling of the Se 4p response is not generic.
- Move a short summary of the alternatives tested in Supplementary Note 8 into the main text, and frame the conclusions about the driving mechanism as "consistent with" throughout.

5. Verdict

Verdict: minor revision
