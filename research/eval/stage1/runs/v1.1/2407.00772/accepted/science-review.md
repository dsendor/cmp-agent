## A. Must address

1. The 1/√F scaling of τ_int has not been shown to be specific to excitonic correlations, yet it is the only evidence for "persistent excitonic fluctuations in the normal state" (p. 1792; Fig. 4b,d,g). The argument runs τ_int ∝ 1/ω_pl ∝ 1/√n_tot, and since the same scaling appears at 300 K as at 23 K, excitonic correlations are said to survive above Tc. A build-up time set by the inverse plasma frequency is, however, a generic property of screening in any photoexcited electron–hole plasma or semimetal with Coulomb-enhanced electron–hole scattering. Carrier redistribution, Auger-like scattering, band renormalization, thermalization into Se 4p states and the build-up of screening all speed up with density, whether or not bound or correlated pairs exist. The constant offset τ0 is assigned, without independent support, to "the time needed to create mobile Se 4p holes in the absence of excitonic interactions", but nothing shows that a non-excitonic process would not also scale as 1/√F. Several points weaken the inference further. (a) The fluence range is only about one decade (0.2–3 mJ cm⁻²) with a free offset τ0, and the shape is set mostly by the one or two lowest-fluence points, so 1/√F + τ0 is hard to tell apart from 1/F, other power laws, a logarithmic or a saturating form. (b) The 23 K and 300 K data fall on the same curve with the same τ0 and prefactor (Fig. 4d), although the excitonic population should differ strongly between the condensed phase and a fluctuating normal state (compare the amplitudes at 25 K and 310 K in Fig. 3c,e). A timescale insensitive to long-range order and to the size of the excitonic signal reads more naturally as a generic carrier-density effect. (c) No null case is shown. (d) Alternative processes are relegated to Supplementary Note 8 with a one-line summary. The authors should: fit τ_int = A·F^(−β) + τ0 with the exponent free at each temperature, and report β with uncertainties, the fit quality against alternative forms, and whether 23 K and 300 K share one prefactor; bring the tests against non-excitonic, density-dependent hole-generation processes into the main text and state which observable each alternative fails to reproduce; and, ideally, measure a reference under matched conditions with the same probe and fluence protocol where excitonic correlations should be absent or strongly weakened (1T-TiS2, heavily Cu-intercalated or electron-doped TiSe2 with the CDW suppressed, or TiSe2 at a much higher temperature) and show that the fluence law or its prefactor changes there. A calculation showing that single-particle screening gives a different fluence dependence, or a demonstration that the prefactor carries exciton-specific information, would also serve. Without one of these, the normal-state claim should be worded as "consistent with" short-range excitonic correlations rather than as evidence for them.

2. The susceptibility model (Eq. 1) does not account for the high-fluence data or the 190 K fluence series, and a saturation-floor or melting-threshold explanation of the cusp in Fig. 4e is not excluded. Eq. 1 sets n_tot ∝ F[χ0 + χex(T)] with τ_int − τ0 ∝ 1/√n_tot and τ0 = 25 fs (Fig. 4d). The temperature dependence therefore multiplies F, so (τ_low − τ0)/(τ_high − τ0) should equal √10 ≈ 3.2 at every temperature, and a diverging χex should drive τ_int towards ≈25 fs at every fluence near Tc. At 23 K the data agree: (190 − 25)/(75 − 25) ≈ 3.3. At 190 K, however, τ_int sits at about 70–90 fs and is roughly fluence-independent from 0.2 to 2.5 mJ cm⁻² (Fig. 4c,d), and the low-fluence value (~70 fs) equals the high-fluence value (~75 fs), a ratio of about 1. The model would predict a high-fluence value near 40 fs at Tc. At 2.0 mJ cm⁻², τ_int is flat at about 70–75 fs across all temperatures with no dip at Tc (Fig. 4e, red), and the red line is a plain average not tied to Eq. 1. Both observations point to a floor of about 70 fs, for example a minimum electron–electron or electron–phonon scattering time for generating mobile Se 4p holes, or a saturable excitonic contribution, which the model lacks. A related scenario: the fluence needed to fully suppress the ordered-state response falls as T → Tc, so at 190 K 0.2 mJ cm⁻² may already exceed it, which would make the low-fluence response join the high-fluence plateau and explain both the fluence independence in Fig. 4c,d and the coincidence of the minimum with the high-fluence level. A low-fluence curve reaching a floor or threshold near Tc is not the same as a diverging susceptibility, and the claim of "enhanced excitonic susceptibility" rests on Eq. 1 reproducing the cusp. The fitted Tc, α, χ0 and χex prefactor, and the 1/√F prefactors, are not given in the main text, the curve is labelled only "Fit", and with at least χ0, the χex prefactor, Tc, α and τ0 against about eight temperature points the fit is barely constrained. The authors should: state in the main text how the Fig. 4e fit (Supplementary Equation (9)) handles the floor and which parameters are free or fixed; extend Eq. 1 (for example with a saturable χex or an explicit floor timescale) and fit Fig. 4d at 23, 190 and 300 K and both curves of Fig. 4e jointly with one parameter set; report all fitted parameters with uncertainties and the number of free parameters against data points; show that a non-divergent form (a smooth minimum, or a threshold crossing with a temperature-dependent threshold) fits significantly worse; and measure the fluence threshold for full suppression against temperature, or show the fluence dependence of the transient amplitude (not only of τ_int) at 190 K and 23 K, and the 0.2 mJ cm⁻² amplitude across Tc relative to its saturated value.

3. The cusp at Tc rests on a single temperature point, and the effective sample temperature near Tc is not documented (Fig. 4e; Fig. 3 caption). The low-fluence series in Fig. 4e has its temperatures spaced 40–50 K apart, with nothing between 150 and 250 K except 190 K; the sharp minimum is defined by that one point. A claimed divergence ∝ |T − Tc|^−α cannot be established this way. The authors should add low-fluence points at about 5–10 K spacing between 170 and 230 K, comparable to the 5 K bins of Fig. 3, and show that the minimum sits at the Tc fixed by the static and transient order-parameter data (Fig. 3d,e). They should also state the repetition rate and estimate steady-state laser heating at 0.2 and 2.0 mJ cm⁻², since a few kelvin of heating moves the 190 K point relative to Tc and changes the apparent shape of the cusp, and explain why the minimum sits at 190 K while Tc ≈ 200 K is quoted. For the continuous ramp in Fig. 3 (each point integrated over about 5 K, 30 spectra), they should give the ramp rate, direction, thermal lag between sensor and sample, and any hysteresis between up- and down-sweeps, since these set where the "onset at Tc" falls. Adding an intermediate fluence (for example 0.5 mJ cm⁻²) across temperature would turn Fig. 4e into a fluence–temperature map.

## B. Should address

4. A diverging susceptibility at Tc does not single out an excitonic driving mechanism. In a coupled exciton–phonon system, the electronic susceptibility at the ordering vector diverges at Tc whether the instability is electron- or lattice-driven, and any order-parameter softening near a continuous transition (excitonic, Peierls or Jahn–Teller-like) makes the ordered state more fragile to weak perturbation. The Discussion (p. 1793–1794) excludes phonon contributions only through the ~20% photoinduced hardening of the CDW amplitude mode at low temperature (ref. 28). That addresses the fluence dependence at 23 K, but not the temperature dependence near and above Tc, where the soft L-point phonon is strongly damped, its frequency depends strongly on temperature and critical lattice fluctuations peak (ref. 40), which are exactly the regimes where the excitonic claims are made. The authors should explain, for example by comparison with soft-phonon frequencies and damping from X-ray or electron diffuse scattering (refs. 28, 40, 44), why soft-mode dynamics cannot produce the temperature-dependent τ_int, and state what feature of Fig. 4e would differ between an exciton-driven and a lattice-driven transition with residual electron–hole coupling. Otherwise the result should be described as enhanced susceptibility of the coupled exciton–lattice order, and "evidence for the excitonic instability" softened to "consistent with", as the abstract already does.

5. The model takes n_tot ∝ F, but the equilibrium carrier density and the absorbed fluence change with temperature (Eq. 1; Fig. 4d,e; Fig. 3). Above Tc, TiSe2 has a sizeable equilibrium carrier density, and gap opening and band folding across Tc (Fig. 1d,e) change both this density and the optical constants at 1.65 eV. If n_tot = n_eq + n_photo, the 1/√F form holds only when n_photo ≫ n_eq, and at 300 K the larger thermal density should shift the curve relative to 23 K, yet the same τ0 = 25 fs and prefactor are used in both phases. A temperature-dependent absorption at 750 nm also changes the absorbed density at fixed incident fluence, which feeds into Fig. 3d,e and Fig. 4e. The authors should give absorbed fluence (or reflectivity/absorption at 750 nm against temperature) alongside the incident values, the estimated photoexcited density per formula unit at each fluence (so the results can be compared with time-resolved ARPES, refs. 20, 23), compare n_photo at 0.2 mJ cm⁻² with n_eq at 300 K, include n_eq in the model, and show whether the conclusions survive.

6. Sample thickness and the excitation-depth profile are not given. XUV transmission requires thin samples, but the main text does not give the thickness, substrate or preparation (exfoliation, microtomy), or compare the 750 nm pump penetration depth with the thickness. If the pump is absorbed non-uniformly, the XUV probe integrates over a range of photoexcited densities and τ_int is a depth-averaged quantity, which changes how it relates to F and could distort the fitted exponent (concerns 1 and 2). Thin flakes can also show thickness- or strain-dependent Tc. The authors should report these quantities, show that depth averaging does not distort the fitted exponent, and state the Tc determined in this sample (Fig. 3) compared with bulk.

7. The number of samples, reproducibility and degradation are not reported. The main text does not say how many crystals or flakes were measured, whether the Fig. 4d fluence series and the Fig. 4e temperature series come from the same sample, or whether the key results (the Fig. 2b sign reversal, the Fig. 3 onsets, the 1/√F scaling at 300 K and the cusp near Tc) were reproduced on another sample or after thermal cycling. The 350 K high-fluence point had to be taken at 1.1 mJ cm⁻² "to prevent sample degradation", which implies degradation at elevated temperature and fluence that could affect other high-temperature points or later measurements. The authors should state the sample count, show the cusp on at least a second sample or a repeated cool-down, describe how degradation was monitored (for example by re-measuring the static spectrum or a reference point), and repeat a low-fluence measurement after the high-fluence series to confirm reversibility.

8. τ_int is not defined in the main text, and its error bars understate the uncertainty (Fig. 4a–e; Supplementary Eq. (1)). It is unclear whether τ_int is a rise time, an exponential constant or an error-function width, how it correlates with the fitted amplitude and delay zero, and how it is deconvolved from the IRF in Fig. 4a. The error bars are 1 s.d. fitting uncertainties only, leaving out scan-to-scan and sample-to-sample variation, while the central claims rest on differences of 50–100 fs between single fits to low-fluence traces that are visibly noisy and vertically rescaled. The authors should define τ_int in the main text, give its correlations with the other free parameters, and report the repeatability of τ_int from repeated scans at a fixed condition.

9. Below Tc the fluence series crosses the order-melting threshold. The Fig. 2a conditions exceed the thresholds for melting the excitonic and CDW order, while 0.2 mJ cm⁻² gives a qualitatively different Ti-continuum response (Fig. 2b,d), so the fluence series in Fig. 4a,d spans partial and complete quenching. A crossover between these regimes could by itself give a fluence-dependent initial timescale unrelated to plasmon screening. The authors should mark the melting threshold on Fig. 4d and show that the 1/√F trend holds on both sides of it.

10. It is assumed, not shown, that the transmission decrease in window (iii) measures only the Se 4p hole population (p. 1791–1792). The spectra in Fig. 3c show a derivative-like shape (positive and negative lobes around 52.5–54 eV) at both 25 K and 310 K, the signature of an edge shift or broadening (core-hole screening, band renormalization) rather than pure state filling. If part of the window (iii) signal comes from a shift, τ_int partly measures how fast screening builds up rather than how fast holes are created, which feeds directly into concern 1. The main text refers only to Supplementary Note 7 for robustness to the window choice. The authors should fit the transient spectra at several delays with a state-filling plus edge-shift model and show τ_int for each component.

11. The low-fluence sign reversal of the Ti many-body continuum is under-characterized (p. 1791; Fig. 2b,d). The flip in window (ii) at 200 fs is presented as evidence of "an additional dynamical process distinct from simple carrier excitation" tied to the low-temperature order, and it motivates the claim that the Ti continuum is sensitive to excitonic correlations and the choice of window (ii) in Fig. 3d. But it is shown at only two fluences, its amplitude (~0.1 × 10⁻³) is close to the noise in Fig. 2d, no mechanism is offered, and the window is not used in the later fluence and temperature analysis. The authors should show a fluence series locating the crossover, state whether the flip persists above Tc, link its temperature dependence (Fig. 3b,d) explicitly to the Se-edge results, and offer a tentative assignment (for example a transient increase in local screening, a coherent order-parameter response, or competition between state filling and screening), or present it more cautiously.
