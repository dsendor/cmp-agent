Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use a cryogenic attosecond broadband XUV transient absorption setup (ABXAS) on exfoliated 1T-TiSe2 flakes and show that, while static Ti 3p / Se 3d photoemission and static M-edge absorption peak positions show no anomaly at Tc ≈ 200 K, two transient spectral windows at 200 fs delay (the Ti "many-body continuum" near 38.6–40.9 eV and the Se M5 peak ② near 53.0–53.3 eV) show an order-parameter-like onset below Tc. They further argue, from the pump-fluence dependence of the rise time of the Se peak ② signal at 23 K and 300 K (τ ∝ 1/√F + τ0), that incoherent excitonic correlations persist above Tc. Element-resolved, phonon-free core-level reporters of a CDW transition, combined with sub-10 fs time resolution and temperature control, would be a useful technical advance; the claim of time-domain evidence for normal-state excitonic correlations would, if established, bear on a long-standing debate.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A technically demanding combination (3.4 fs pump, 5.4 fs IRF, 20–320 K cryostat with drift correction, broadband coverage of both Ti M2,3 and Se M4,5 edges) applied to a well-known benchmark material.
- Dense temperature scans (~5 K steps) in Fig. 3a with error bars from repeated measurements, giving a clear onset at Tc in two independent spectral windows (Fig. 3d,e).
- Careful separation of phonon-modulated windows (i, iv, with constant ~50 fs rise time, Fig. 4e red markers) from the phonon-free window iii provides an internal contrast within the same data set.
- The ARPES characterisation (Fig. 1a–f) and static core-level temperature series (Fig. 1j–l, o–q) are measured carefully and give a useful benchmark for the "static spectra show nothing" baseline.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The evidence for normal-state excitonic correlations (the abstract's second headline claim, Section "Persistent short-range excitonic fluctuations", Fig. 4) does not exclude generic, non-excitonic explanations of a fluence-dependent rise time. The argument is: rise time of Se window iii shortens with fluence, the trend follows 1/√F + τ0, √F ∝ plasmon frequency, therefore exciton breaking by screening is occurring above and below Tc. Several links are untested:
   (a) No null control. A fluence-dependent rise time of a hole-population signal is expected in any photoexcited semimetal, because carrier–carrier scattering and the cascade that fills states near EF speed up with excitation density. The paper needs a reference where excitonic correlations should be absent, measured under matched conditions: e.g. the same Se/Ti windows in a non-CDW isostructural compound (1T-TiS2 or 1T-TiTe2), or the temperature dependence of the fluence slope from 300 K upwards, showing that the 1/√F trend weakens as correlations are expected to fade. Only a single temperature above Tc (300 K) is shown.
   (b) The scaling is not established. Fig. 4e has four fluences per temperature with sizeable scatter (at 23 K the 2.9 mJ/cm² point sits above the 1.8 mJ/cm² point; at 300 K the 1.9 mJ/cm² point sits above the 1.5 mJ/cm² point), and the 0.2 mJ/cm² points, which have the largest error bars and the noisiest traces (Fig. 4b), largely set the curve. A two-parameter fit (prefactor and τ0 = 26 fs) to these points cannot distinguish 1/√F from 1/F, log F, or a simple monotonic decrease. Please give the fit with uncertainties, compare alternative functional forms (reduced χ² or similar), and add fluence points, particularly below 0.6 mJ/cm².
   (c) The plasmon argument ignores equilibrium carriers. Above Tc, TiSe2 is a semimetal with an intrinsic free-carrier density; the screening frequency should scale as √(n0 + αF), not √F, and the 300 K and 23 K curves should then differ. Please estimate the photoexcited density per mJ/cm² for the flake thickness and the equilibrium density n0 at 300 K and 23 K, compute the expected plasma periods, and show that the measured τ values (~50–190 fs) and the common curve at both temperatures are quantitatively consistent with this picture.
   (d) "Short-range" is inferred, not measured, and the signature is not shown to be unique to excitonic correlations rather than the ordinary electron–hole Coulomb interaction present in any semimetal. The conclusion that "exciton correlations … are also instrumental in forming the short-range 2D order in-plane before the long-range CDW is formed" (p. 11) has no supporting measurement linking the Fig. 4 time constants to the structural short-range order.
   Without (a)–(c), the second headline claim should be reworded as a fluence-dependent rise time consistent with, but not demonstrative of, excitonic correlations above Tc.

B. Should address

2. The assignment of Se window iii to the Se 4p hole population (p. 8–9) is assumed rather than demonstrated, and it underpins both Fig. 3e and Fig. 4. The 25 K transient in Fig. 3c has a derivative-like shape (negative lobe then positive lobe around 53.2–53.5 eV), which can equally come from an edge shift or broadening (band-gap renormalisation, core-exciton screening change) as from state filling. A temperature-dependent edge shift would also change the amplitude integrated over a fixed window. Please decompose the transient spectra around peak ② into shift, broadening and amplitude contributions (for example, fit the static peak and model ΔA from each), and show that the integrated window-iii signal tracks the amplitude (hole) term. The TDDFT machinery the authors already have could compute ΔA for a hole population at EF compared with a rigid shift.

3. The "no static signature" claim (abstract; Fig. 1o–q) is tested on a different observable from the transient one. Static absorption is tracked only through fitted peak positions, whereas the transient signal that shows the onset is an integrated intensity in window ii (many-body continuum) and window iii. For a fair comparison, show static difference spectra A(T) − A(300 K) integrated over the same windows ii and iii, with their noise level, and state the detection limit relative to the ~10⁻³–10⁻² transient changes in Fig. 3d,e. Otherwise the advantage claimed for time-resolved measurement may come from the observable chosen rather than from the time dimension.

4. The effective sample temperature and steady-state heating are not quantified. The sample is a free-standing flake <100 nm thick on a Cu TEM grid, excited at 1 kHz with up to 2.9 mJ/cm² incident fluence, and the Methods note no special radiation shielding (base ~20 K). Pre-time-zero signals were "uniformly subtracted" and said not to change the conclusions (p. 18), but no data are shown. Please report the pre-time-zero ΔI against fluence and temperature, and estimate the average temperature rise of the illuminated spot. This matters in particular for the 23 K high-fluence points in Fig. 4a,e: if the average temperature approaches Tc, the "below Tc" fluence series is not cleanly in the CDW state.

5. The incident fluence is used as a proxy for excited density (F ∝ n, p. 10), but the absorbed fraction of a sub-100 nm flake at 500–1000 nm depends on thickness and changes across Tc, because the optical constants of TiSe2 change with CDW formation. Please give the flake thickness (from the AFM mentioned in Methods), the absorbed fluence and excited carrier density, and check that the temperature dependence of absorption does not contribute to the onset in Fig. 3d,e.

6. Sample statistics are not reported. The transient data appear to come from one flake (Fig. S1 shows "a measured flake"). State how many flakes were measured, and show that the Fig. 3d,e onset and the Fig. 4e fluence trend reproduce on at least a second flake, and on thermal cycling, as was done for ARPES (p. 16).

7. The interpretation of the Ti window ii sign reversal at low fluence (Fig. 2b, green box; Fig. 3b) as a "separate low-energy many-body dynamical process" related to excitonic correlations is qualitative. The static calculation reproduces the continuum peak, but no transient or CDW-versus-normal-state calculation of the continuum response is given. Either support the assignment with a calculation (e.g. TDDFT absorption in the 24-atom CDW cell against the normal cell, or with a modified occupation), or present it as a phenomenological marker of the CDW without a microscopic assignment.

8. The CDW amplitude mode (~3.4 THz in the CDW phase) is not discussed. At 0.2 mJ/cm² and 23 K one might expect it alongside the 6.0 THz A1g mode. Its presence or absence at low fluence would check where the experiment sits relative to the CDW melting threshold; the text calls 0.2 mJ/cm² a regime where the CDW and excitonic correlations are "close to be fully suppressed" (p. 8), yet the Fig. 4a series spans 0.2–2.9 mJ/cm², which complicates the "CDW ground state" label for Fig. 4a. Please state what fraction of the order is quenched at each fluence and how that affects the below-Tc/above-Tc comparison.

9. The DFT details are incomplete for the CDW-phase DOS in Fig. 1g: was the 24-atom 2×2×2 cell relaxed, and with what distortion amplitude? Was spin–orbit coupling included (relevant to the Se 3d doublet assignment)? PBE without +U or a hybrid functional is known to misplace the Ti 3d–Se 4p overlap in TiSe2. A quantitative comparison of the calculated and measured peak positions (②–⑤ relative to the Se 3d binding energies) would also strengthen the assignment.

C. Minor

10. Define "initial response time" (Fig. 4e) in terms of the parameters of Eq. (1): is it ω, τ, or a 10–90% rise time? Eq. (1) has ω as an "intrinsic system response time" and τ as relaxation; it is unclear which is plotted and how the IRF enters.

11. p. 5: "absent in core-level spectroscopies … (Fig. 1h,m)" — panels h and m are schematics; the relevant panels are j–l and o–q.

12. p. 8: "the spectral weight of the folded Se 4p band (Fig. 1f)" should refer to Fig. 1e.

13. Fig. 2 caption gives the pump centre as 750 nm, whereas Methods describe a 790 nm driver broadened to 500–1000 nm; please state the pump spectrum actually used and clarify the "sub-4-fs" (p. 7) against the 3.4 fs FWHM.

14. Fig. 3d,e: the blue curves are described as "guides to the eye"; if Tc is extracted from these data, give the fit form and the fitted Tc with uncertainty.

15. The Ti and Se fluence sets in Fig. 4 differ (0.9–2.3 mJ/cm² for Ti window i; 0.2–2.9 for Se). Measuring the phonon-dominated windows down to 0.2 mJ/cm² would make the constant ~50 fs reference more convincing at the fluences that drive the 1/√F fit.

16. Many statements depend on the Supplementary Information (Figs. S1–S5, Notes 1–3, Table S1), which should accompany the submission.

17. Minor language issues: "spectroscopes" (abstract), "necessitate an access" (abstract), "close to be fully suppressed" (p. 8), "time-resolve probes" (p. 11), "spectra before the pump pulse arrival was" (p. 8).

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a reference compound without excitonic or CDW instability (1T-TiS2 or 1T-TiTe2) measured with the same windows and fluence series; this one experiment would turn Fig. 4 from suggestive to decisive.
- Measure the window-iii rise time against temperature at two or three fixed fluences from 20 K to 320 K, so that the excitonic contribution (offset from the phonon/thermal baseline) can be followed through Tc and into the normal state.
- Model the window-iii and window-ii transients microscopically (state filling against shift/broadening, possibly with TDDFT on the CDW and normal cells), to tie each window to a specific electronic process.
- Show static difference spectra over the same windows to place the claimed advantage of time resolution on a quantitative footing.

5. Verdict: major revision
