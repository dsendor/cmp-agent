Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use a cryogenic table-top broadband XUV transient absorption setup (ABXAS, Ti M2,3 and Se M4,5 edges, 5.4 fs instrument response) on exfoliated 1T-TiSe2 flakes. They make three claims. First, static core-level photoemission and absorption show no anomaly at Tc (Fig. 1). Second, the transient signal at 200 fs, in the Ti "many-body continuum" (window ii) and in the Se peak 2 (window iii), turns on at Tc like an order parameter (Fig. 3). Third, the rise time of the Se peak 2 signal depends on fluence as 1/sqrt(F) both below and above Tc, which they read as time-domain evidence for short-range excitonic correlations in the normal state (Fig. 4). Showing that a local core-level probe can, in the time domain, report on a low-energy ordering transition that static spectra miss would interest both the attosecond and the correlated-materials communities. The third claim speaks to a long-running debate about the excitonic insulator. As presented, however, it rests on a fluence-scaling argument that does not single out excitons, and it conflicts with the paper's own temperature data.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The instrument is a real technical achievement: a sub-4-fs pump, a continuous XUV spectrum covering both the Ti and Se M edges, and cryogenic temperatures down to about 20 K with drift correction and beam-pointing stabilisation (Methods).
- Static ARPES and static core-level data were taken on the same crystals. These include the band-replica spectral weight, the gap at Gamma (Fig. 1e,f) and dense temperature series of the core-level positions (Fig. 1j–l, o–q). This makes a clean baseline showing that the equilibrium core-level spectra do not respond at Tc.
- Fig. 3d,e show a temperature dependence with about 5 K steps and a clear kink at about 200 K in two independent spectral windows, Ti window ii and Se window iii. This is the strongest result in the paper.
- The authors pick spectral windows with care, for example Se peaks 2 and 3, which show no 6.0 THz A1g modulation (Fig. 2e). This separates electronic dynamics from coherent phonon dynamics. The phonon-dominated windows (Fig. 4e, red markers) serve as an internal reference whose rise time does not depend on fluence.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The fluence dependence of the Se window iii rise time does not uniquely signal excitonic correlations, and the paper has no control in which the effect should be absent (Fig. 4, pp. 9–10, abstract). The claim of "direct time-domain evidence for incoherent excitonic correlations in the normal state" rests on one observation: the rise time tau of the Se peak 2 bleach falls with fluence and fits tau = a/sqrt(F) + tau0 at both 23 K and 300 K. Several non-excitonic processes also speed up with excitation density in a photoexcited semimetal or narrow-gap semiconductor:
   - electron–electron scattering and thermalization;
   - the Auger-like cascade that populates Se 4p holes near E_F after the pump has made carriers up to about 2 eV away;
   - the onset of free-carrier screening, which also scales with the plasma frequency;
   - band-gap renormalization.
   The Se 4p holes probed in window iii are mostly not created directly at the probed energy, so a density-dependent filling time is expected without any excitonic binding. The paper also explicitly finds the 23 K and 300 K responses identical "within experimental uncertainties". That is equally consistent with the response being unrelated to excitonic order. As framed, the argument cannot be falsified: no measurement shown would come out differently if there were no excitonic correlations. The authors should do at least one of the following.
   - Measure the same window at fixed fluence over a wide temperature range, extending as far above Tc as possible, and show whether the rise time and its fluence dependence change as fluctuations die out (the ARPES tails in Fig. 1e,f give an expected scale).
   - Measure a reference system without excitonic correlations under matched conditions, for example the same transition in TiS2, or a heavily electron-doped or intercalated TiSe2 with the CDW suppressed.
   - Model the hole-filling time for a non-interacting semimetal at the relevant densities, and show that it cannot reproduce a/sqrt(F) with the fitted prefactor.
   Without such a test, the conclusion in the Discussion (p. 11) that excitonic correlations "are also instrumental in forming the short-range 2D order in-plane" is not supported.

2. The paper treats Se window iii in two contradictory ways: as a probe of long-range order in Fig. 3e, and as a probe blind to long-range coherence in Fig. 4. On p. 9 and in Fig. 3e, the amplitude of the Se peak 2 bleach is presented as "another sensitive probe of long-range ordering". It is quasi-constant above Tc and onsets sharply at Tc, and it has no visible precursor tail above Tc, unlike the ARPES replica weight and gap in Fig. 1e,f. On p. 10, the same feature is said to be "agnostic about the existence of long-range phase coherence among the excitons", which is what licenses reading its kinetics above Tc as evidence for short-range correlations. Both cannot hold without further argument. If window iii amplitude is a local reporter of excitonic correlations, Fig. 3e implies those correlations are essentially absent or temperature-independent above Tc. It should then show a fluctuation tail like Fig. 1e,f, which it does not. If instead the amplitude tracks long-range order, the authors need to explain why its kinetics would still report short-range correlations. The authors should reconcile these two readings explicitly, for example with a model that separates amplitude and rise time. They should also show the rise time tau(T) at fixed fluence across the Fig. 3 temperature series, since a kink, or no kink, at Tc in tau would directly test which reading is right.

B. Should address

3. Steady-state and per-pulse heating at high fluence may put the "23 K" fluence series partly above Tc (Fig. 4a,e; Methods pp. 18–20). The flakes are free-standing, under 100 nm thick, on Cu TEM grids, pumped at 1 kHz with fluences up to 2.9 mJ/cm², about ten times the CDW-melting threshold. The cryostat has no radiation shielding, and the "effective minimum temperature ... was around 20 K" without saying how this was determined. The pre-time-zero signal, which carries cumulative heating, was subtracted. Methods says this "do[es] not change the conclusions", but its size is never reported. If the high-fluence points at 23 K come from a sample sitting near or above Tc in steady state, then the overlap of the below-Tc and above-Tc rise times in Fig. 4e follows automatically. The authors should use the calibrated temperature shift of the static absorption edges (Fig. 1o–q, roughly 0.2 meV/K) as an on-sample thermometer. With it they should report the steady-state flake temperature for each fluence in Fig. 4 and the size of the subtracted pre-time-zero signal.

4. The 1/sqrt(F) fit is weakly constrained, and its physical premise does not hold across temperatures (Fig. 4e, p. 10). Each temperature has four fluences, and the 300 K trace is noisy (Fig. 4b). The single blue band is drawn through both temperatures with a free prefactor and tau0 = 26 fs, and some points, for example 23 K at 2.9 mJ/cm² and 300 K at 1.9 mJ/cm², lie off it. The premise "plasmon frequency ~ sqrt(n), n ~ F" ignores several things:
   - The equilibrium carrier density differs between the semimetallic 300 K state and the gapped 23 K state. The relevant plasma frequency should go as sqrt(n0(T) + n_exc), not sqrt(F).
   - The effective mass and background dielectric constant change across Tc.
   - Absorption can saturate at the highest fluences.

   The authors should:
   - fit each temperature separately with a free exponent and report its uncertainty;
   - compare 1/sqrt(F) against 1/F and against a constant using a goodness-of-fit criterion;
   - use absorbed fluence, accounting for thin-film interference in a flake under 100 nm thick, instead of incident fluence;
   - explain the physical origin of tau0 = 26 fs, which is about five times the IRF.

5. Possible spectral shift and broadening contributions to the temperature-dependent transient signal (Fig. 3, Fig. 1o–q). The static edges shift monotonically with temperature by tens of meV (Fig. 1o–q). The Se window iii transient in Fig. 3c has a derivative-like lineshape: negative near 53.1–53.3 eV and positive near 53.5 eV. Fig. 3b shows a sign change in Ti window ii. A fixed integration window on a derivative-like signal whose zero crossing moves with temperature can produce a temperature-dependent integral. The kink at Tc argues against a purely smooth artefact. Still, the authors should decompose the transient spectra at each temperature into pump-induced peak shift, broadening and amplitude change, and show which of these carries the order-parameter-like onset. They should also show the static difference spectrum A(25 K) − A(310 K) next to the transient. That would also strengthen the claim that static spectra carry no CDW signature, which currently rests on peak positions only, not on widths or intensities.

6. There is no microscopic explanation of the Ti many-body continuum signal and its sign reversal (Figs. 2b, 3b,d, pp. 7–8). The low-fluence sign reversal at 200 fs in window ii is attributed to "a separate low-energy many-body dynamical process" and later to valence charge excitations that "are most affected by the changing density of states". No calculation backs this up. The authors already have a TDDFT framework that reproduces the static continuum feature (Fig. S3, Supplementary Note 1). They should compute the absorption in the 2×2×2 CDW cell and in the normal cell, or with a modified valence occupation, and test whether the sign and size of the change in window ii follow. Otherwise the result is an empirical correlation without a mechanism. It would also help to show the full delay trace of window ii at low fluence for several temperatures, not only the single 200 fs delay, so that a shift of a dynamical feature in time is not misread as a change of sign.

7. The sample's Tc, sample count and reproducibility are not documented for the absorption measurements (Methods, p. 16). ARPES was done on cleaved bulk crystals, while all ABXAS data come from exfoliated free-standing flakes under 100 nm thick, whose CDW transition can differ from bulk. Tc ≈ 200 K for the flakes is inferred only from the ABXAS data themselves. The paper does not say how many flakes were measured, whether Figs. 2–4 come from one flake, or whether the Fig. 3 onset survives thermal cycling and a second flake. The authors should:
   - state the number of flakes and their thicknesses;
   - show the Fig. 3d,e onset on at least one more flake or after a thermal cycle;
   - characterise the flakes' Tc independently if possible, for example by Raman spectroscopy of the CDW amplitude modes or by resistance.

   Pump penetration depth relative to flake thickness also sets how uniform the excitation is through the probed volume, and should be discussed.

8. The novelty and "attosecond" framing are overstated relative to what the data use and to prior work (title, abstract, p. 11). The temperature-dependent result is taken at a single delay of 200 fs, and the rise times of interest are 40–200 fs. Neither needs attosecond resolution; a ~20–30 fs XUV setup would resolve them. Ref. 22 (Heinrich et al., PRX 2023) already reported electronic and structural CDW fingerprints of TiSe2 in XUV transient absorption, so the statement that this is "the first demonstration where an attosecond core-level technique ... can simultaneously probe low-energy phase transitions" needs to be qualified. The authors should state clearly what is new relative to Ref. 22: the temperature series across Tc, and the absence of static signatures. They should also say which conclusions actually need the broadband coverage or the few-fs resolution.

C. Minor

9. On p. 8, "spectral weight of the folded Se 4p band (Fig. 1f)" should read Fig. 1e. On p. 5, the absence of core-level signatures is attributed to "Fig. 1h,m", but those panels are the schematics; the data are in Fig. 1j–l and o–q.

10. Fig. 2e and the text disagree. The text (p. 8) says low fluence gives a "near-proportional scaling-down", but in Fig. 2e the low-fluence and high-fluence traces of window iii have similar magnitude, and the panel does not say whether it was rescaled (panels c, d and f say "×0.5"). Fig. 4a, by contrast, shows a roughly threefold difference between 0.2 and 1.8 mJ/cm². Please state the scaling used in Fig. 2e.

11. The pump central wavelength is given as 750 nm in the Fig. 2 caption and as 790 nm (amplifier) in Methods, with a 500–1000 nm broadened spectrum. Please give the central wavelength of the compressed pump spectrum and the photon energy used to estimate excitation density.

12. Methods describes only the static PBE calculations. The TDDFT method used for Fig. S3 (treatment of the Ti 3p semicore, exchange-correlation kernel, cell, propagation) should be summarised in the main Methods. The CDW structure used for the density of states in Fig. 1g should also be specified: are the atomic displacements taken from experiment or relaxed in PBE?

13. Eq. (1) calls omega the "intrinsic system response time". Please say whether omega is deconvolved from the IRF, and whether omega and tau (the decay) are correlated in the fits for window iii, where the high-fluence trace overshoots (Fig. 2e, Fig. 4a). The fit covariance should be reported alongside the 1 s.d. error bars in Fig. 4e.

14. Fig. 4e shows horizontal error bars for fluence, but there is no statement of how the spot size was measured at the sample plane or of the pump/probe spot-size ratio. Both are needed to judge the fluence uncertainty.

15. Typos: "o the" in Ref. 16's title as cited; "time-resolve probes" (p. 11); "spectroscopes" (abstract).

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a temperature series of the rise time tau of window iii at fixed fluence, from 20 K to the highest reachable temperature. Together with a non-excitonic reference measurement, this would turn the short-range-correlation claim from an inference into a test.
- Use the static edge shifts in Fig. 1o–q as an on-sample thermometer, so that every transient data point comes with a measured sample temperature.
- Run TDDFT in the CDW and normal cells, or with photo-modified occupations, to explain why the Ti continuum in window ii changes sign at Tc. This would supply the "why" behind the paper's strongest empirical result.
- Reword the conclusions about excitonic correlations to match the evidence. In particular, separate what Fig. 3 shows (a transient core-level signature that turns on at Tc) from what Fig. 4 can and cannot show.

5. Verdict: major revision
