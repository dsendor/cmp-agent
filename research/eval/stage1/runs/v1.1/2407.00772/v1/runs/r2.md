Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors use a cryogenic attosecond broadband XUV transient absorption setup (ABXAS) covering the Ti M2,3 and Se M4,5 edges of exfoliated 1T-TiSe2 flakes. They report three things. Static core-level photoemission and static XUV absorption show no anomaly at the CDW transition. The transient signal at 200 fs in two spectral windows (the Ti "many-body continuum" near 39–41 eV and Se peak ② at the M5 edge) shows an order-parameter-like onset at Tc. The fluence dependence of the initial rise time at Se peak ② is the same above and below Tc, which they take as evidence for short-range excitonic correlations in the normal state. Showing that a high-energy core-level probe picks up a low-energy ordering transition through its transient response is a useful contribution for the attosecond community. The excitonic claim speaks to a long-running debate. As presented, though, it rests on a thin and indirect piece of evidence.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Technically demanding experiment: few-fs instrument response combined with a 20–320 K temperature scan, beam-pointing and sample-drift stabilization, and simultaneous Ti and Se edges.
- A clear, well-organized benchmark. Temperature-dependent ARPES shows the replica band and gap growing at Tc (Fig. 1e,f), and both static core-level probes are flat (Fig. 1j–q). Against that, the transient signal has an onset at Tc (Fig. 3d,e).
- The choice of Se peak ②, which shows no A1g modulation (Fig. 2e), gives a phonon-free spectral reporter. Fig. 4e contrasts it with phonon-dominated windows whose response time does not depend on fluence.
- Energy calibration against 13 autoionizing lines and an IRF measured in He are good practice.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The rise-time fluence dependence does not establish excitonic correlations above Tc (Fig. 4, pp. 9–10, and the abstract). This is the paper's most consequential claim. It rests on these steps:
   - roughly four fluence points per temperature (Fig. 4a,b);
   - one fit of τ = A/√F + τ0, with τ0 = 26 fs, to both data sets together;
   - the argument that a √n plasmon frequency sets the exciton-breaking time.

   Several gaps remain.
   - (i) "Initial response time" is never mapped onto a parameter of Eq. (1). It could be ω, which the Methods call the "intrinsic system response time", or a combination of t0 and ω. The paper should state which parameter, how strongly it correlates with t0 and τ in the fit, and how the error bars were obtained. This matters most for the weak 0.2 mJ/cm² trace at 300 K (Fig. 4b), which anchors the 1/√F curvature and carries the largest error bar.
   - (ii) The data show only a monotonic decrease. The specific exponent is not tested: compare 1/√F with 1/F, a constant plus an exponential, and similar forms, and report the fitted exponent with its uncertainty. At 23 K the 1.8 and 2.9 mJ/cm² points sit around 70–80 fs, above the curve. At 300 K the 1.9 mJ/cm² point (about 87 fs) is higher than the 1.5 mJ/cm² point.
   - (iii) The rise time of a state-filling signal can depend on carrier density without any excitons. Plausible mechanisms include density-dependent electron–electron scattering and thermalization of the hot carriers that fill Se 4p hole states, saturation or Pauli blocking, and a density-dependent cascade from the ~1.65 eV excitation down to the near-EF states. Nothing in the paper rules these out. A control is needed in which excitonic correlations are absent or much weaker but the Se 4p reporter behaves the same, for example TiS2 or a Ti/S-substituted crystal. Alternatively, the authors could show that the fluence dependence disappears at a temperature or doping where short-range correlations are known to be absent.
   - (iv) The plasmon argument is not made quantitative. For the photoexcited densities at 0.2–3 mJ/cm², what plasma frequency do the authors estimate, and is its inverse comparable to the 50–200 fs measured here? A screening time of a few fs would undercut the mechanism.
   - (v) Above-Tc data "reproducing the trend within experimental uncertainties" would also be consistent with any density-dependent but correlation-independent mechanism. Agreement between 23 K and 300 K therefore cannot by itself single out excitonic correlations.

   Without (iii) and (iv), the result supports a narrower statement: "a fluence-dependent rise time that is the same above and below Tc".

2. The comparison behind "no signature in static spectra" does not match like with like (Fig. 1o–q against Fig. 3d,e; abstract; p. 6). The static analysis tracks only fitted peak positions, whereas the transient analysis integrates intensity changes over windows ii and iii. A CDW-induced change in the static spectrum could show up in intensity or line shape within those same windows while leaving peak positions smooth. The static data are already in hand: the transient scans record pump-off spectra at every temperature. The authors should show the static absorption integrated over windows ii and iii, and the static difference spectra A(T) − A(300 K), against temperature, with the same error treatment as Fig. 3d,e. If these are flat at Tc, the headline contrast stands. If not, the claim that the time domain is required must be revised.

B. Should address

3. The Tc onset in the transient signal is shown at one delay and one fluence (Fig. 3: 200 fs, 0.2 mJ/cm²). In the Ti window the sign of the low-temperature signal itself depends on fluence: at 23 K it is negative at 1.7 mJ/cm² and positive at 0.2 mJ/cm² (Fig. 2a against 2b). Full delay traces in window ii at a few temperatures above and below Tc would make the "separate low-energy many-body process" concrete, as would the temperature scan at a second fluence or delay. They would also show whether the onset is a change in amplitude or a change in timing.

4. The exciton-specific reading of the Se peak ② onset is not separated from a generic CDW-gap effect (p. 9, Fig. 3c,e). Below Tc the Se 4p band is gapped and backfolded (Fig. 1c,d,g), so state filling of the near-EF Se 4p states by the same absorbed energy will change at Tc regardless of whether the gap is excitonic or phonon/Jahn–Teller driven. The "additional Se 4p holes from broken condensed excitons" interpretation therefore needs either a calculation of the expected transient change with and without the CDW gap or a stated reason why a Peierls-type gap would give no such onset.

5. Sample temperature and heating are not characterized (Methods, pp. 18–20). The free-standing flake (<100 nm) sits on a Cu TEM grid without radiation shielding, so the "on-target" 20 K and the 1 kHz steady-state heating under pumping are uncertain. The pre-time-zero signal was subtracted on the grounds that cumulative heating "does not change the conclusions", but those data are not shown. Please show the pre-time-zero ΔI against temperature and fluence and estimate the steady-state temperature rise. Also confirm that the transition seen in the flakes occurs at the bulk Tc of about 200 K, given that thin TiSe2 can have a modified Tc. The onset in Fig. 3d,e is a useful internal check, but it should be stated as such.

6. Samples and reproducibility. ARPES and core-level photoemission come from cleaved bulk crystals, and XUV absorption from exfoliated flakes. The paper does not state how many flakes were measured, whether the Fig. 3 onset and the Fig. 4 fluence trend were reproduced on more than one flake, or whether they survive thermal cycling. Please state this, and show the Fig. 4e analysis on a second flake if available.

7. Fluence as a proxy for carrier density (p. 10). The 1/√F argument assumes the excited density scales linearly with incident fluence over 0.2–2.9 mJ/cm². For a sub-100-nm flake under a broadband 3.4 fs pump this needs an absorbed-fluence estimate that accounts for flake thickness and thin-film interference, and a check for absorption saturation. These incident fluences are also compared with literature threshold fluences measured on bulk samples with different pulses. Note too that 0.2 mJ/cm² is described as already near full suppression of the CDW (p. 8), so the whole fluence series lies at or above the threshold.

8. The Discussion goes further than the data (p. 11). The statement that excitonic correlations "are also instrumental in forming the short-range 2D order in-plane before the long-range CDW is formed" is not tested. The measurement is local and gives no information on the in-plane correlation length, its dimensionality, or a causal role. Likewise, "resolve the debate" should be softened to match concern 1.

9. Theory support is incomplete in the main text. The assignment of the 38–53 eV feature as a many-body continuum relies on TDDFT (Supplementary Note 1, Fig. S3), but the Methods describe only the ground-state PBE calculation. Please give the TDDFT method (kernel, core-hole treatment, whether the CDW structure was relaxed) in the Methods. Please also note that PBE does not produce the CDW/excitonic gap of TiSe2, so the CDW DOS in Fig. 1g reflects the imposed distortion rather than excitonic physics.

10. Relation to Ref. 22 (Heinrich et al., PRX 2023). That work already reports XUV transient absorption fingerprints of CDW excitations in TiSe2. The "first demonstration" claim on p. 11 needs a precise statement of what is new: temperature-resolved onset, attosecond IRF, or simultaneous Ti and Se edges.

C. Minor

11. p. 8: "the spectral weight of the folded Se 4p band (Fig. 1f)" should refer to Fig. 1e.

12. Several key supports are in the SI (Figs. S1, S3–S5, Table S1, Supplementary Notes 1–3), including the IRF determination that underpins the 5.4 fs claim. The intro also cites Fig. S5 as critical for the broadband argument. The main text should carry enough of this to stand alone, for example the IRF value and how it was obtained.

13. Fig. 4e: the red markers mix Ti window i and Se window iv at different fluences. Please label which temperature each was taken at and show the underlying fits for the phonon-dominated case.

14. Fig. 3d,e: the "guides to the eye" could be replaced by a fit to an order-parameter form with a free Tc. This would quantify the claim of an onset "right at Tc".

15. The time-zero calibration uses the He 2s2p transient at 60.12 eV, outside the sample windows. Please state the uncertainty in t0 that propagates into the rise times.

16. Wording: "spectroscopes" should be "spectroscopies" (abstract), "o the" should be "on the" (Ref. 16), "time-resolve probes" should be "time-resolved probes" (p. 11), and "spectra ... was also taken" should be "were" (p. 8).

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a non-excitonic reference material (e.g., TiS2) measured with the same Se/S-reporter analysis and fluence series, to show that the √F rise-time scaling is specific to TiSe2.
- Extract the static absorption in windows ii and iii against temperature from the existing pump-off spectra (concern 2). This is a low-cost analysis that directly tests the headline contrast.
- Map the rise time against temperature at fixed fluence through Tc and up to 320 K. If excitonic correlations weaken with temperature above Tc, the rise time should change smoothly, which would be a stronger signature than two temperatures.
- Model the transient state-filling response at Se peak ② with and without the CDW gap, for example with the DFT DOS from Fig. 1g plus a hot-carrier distribution, to separate generic gap effects from exciton breaking.

5. Verdict: major revision
