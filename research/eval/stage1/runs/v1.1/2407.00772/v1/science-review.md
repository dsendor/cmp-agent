A. Must address

1. The fluence dependence of the Se window iii rise time does not single out excitonic correlations, and the paper has no control in which the effect should be absent (Fig. 4, pp. 9–10, abstract). The claim of "direct time-domain evidence for incoherent excitonic correlations in the normal state" rests on one observation: the rise time of the Se peak ② signal falls with fluence and fits τ = a/√F + τ0 at both 23 K and 300 K. A fluence-dependent rise time of a hole-population signal is expected in any photoexcited semimetal or narrow-gap semiconductor, without any excitonic binding. Candidate mechanisms include density-dependent electron–electron scattering and thermalization of the hot carriers, the Auger-like cascade that fills the near-E_F Se 4p holes after the ~1.65 eV pump creates carriers up to ~2 eV away, the onset of free-carrier screening, band-gap renormalization, and saturation or Pauli blocking. The Se 4p holes probed in window iii are mostly not created directly at the probed energy, so a density-dependent filling time is expected anyway. The paper also finds the 23 K and 300 K responses identical "within experimental uncertainties", which is equally consistent with a response that is independent of excitonic order. As framed, no measurement shown would come out differently if excitonic correlations were absent, and nothing shows the signature is specific to excitonic correlations rather than the ordinary electron–hole Coulomb interaction present in any semimetal. The authors should do at least one of the following: (a) measure a reference system without excitonic correlations under matched conditions and with the same windows and fluence series, for example 1T-TiS2, 1T-TiTe2, a Ti/S-substituted crystal, or heavily electron-doped or intercalated TiSe2 with the CDW suppressed; (b) measure the window iii rise time at fixed fluence(s) over a wide temperature range, from 20 K to the highest reachable temperature (at least 320 K), and show whether the rise time and its fluence dependence change as fluctuations die out (the ARPES precursor tails in Fig. 1e,f give an expected scale); only one temperature above Tc (300 K) is currently shown; (c) model the hole-filling time for a non-interacting semimetal at the relevant densities and show that it cannot reproduce a/√F with the fitted prefactor. Without such a test, the claim should be reworded as a fluence-dependent rise time that is the same above and below Tc, consistent with but not demonstrative of excitonic correlations above Tc.

2. The 1/√F scaling is not established by the data, and the plasmon-screening argument behind it is neither quantitative nor consistent with the equilibrium carrier density (Fig. 4e, p. 10). Each temperature has only four fluences, with sizeable scatter: at 23 K the 1.8 and 2.9 mJ/cm² points sit around 70–80 fs, above the curve (the 2.9 point above the 1.8 point), and at 300 K the 1.9 mJ/cm² point (~87 fs) lies above the 1.5 mJ/cm² point. The 0.2 mJ/cm² points, which have the largest error bars and the noisiest traces (300 K, Fig. 4b), largely set the 1/√F curvature. A single two-parameter curve (free prefactor, τ0 = 26 fs) drawn through both temperatures cannot distinguish 1/√F from 1/F, log F, a constant plus exponential, or a generic monotonic decrease. The premise "plasma frequency ∝ √n, n ∝ F" also ignores that the semimetallic 300 K state and the gapped 23 K state have different equilibrium carrier densities: the relevant frequency should scale as √(n0(T) + αF), so the two temperatures would be expected to differ; the effective mass and background dielectric constant also change across Tc. The authors should fit each temperature separately with a free exponent and report it with its uncertainty; compare 1/√F against 1/F, a constant and other forms with a goodness-of-fit criterion (reduced χ² or similar); add fluence points, particularly below 0.6 mJ/cm²; estimate the photoexcited density per mJ/cm² for the flake thickness and the equilibrium density n0 at 23 K and 300 K, compute the resulting plasma periods, and show they are comparable to the measured 50–190 fs (a screening time of a few fs would undercut the mechanism) and consistent with a common curve at both temperatures; and explain the physical origin of τ0 = 26 fs, about five times the IRF.

3. The headline contrast that static spectra show no CDW signature while transient spectra do compares different observables (abstract; p. 6; Fig. 1o–q against Fig. 3d,e). The static absorption is tracked only through fitted peak positions, whereas the transient onset is seen in intensity changes integrated over windows ii (many-body continuum) and iii. A CDW-induced change in the static spectrum could appear in intensity, width or line shape within those windows while leaving peak positions smooth. The data are already in hand, since pump-off spectra are recorded at every temperature. The authors should show the static absorption integrated over windows ii and iii, and the static difference spectra A(T) − A(300 K) (for example A(25 K) − A(310 K) next to the transient), against temperature with the same error treatment as Fig. 3d,e, and state the detection limit relative to the ~10⁻³–10⁻² transient changes. If these are flat at Tc the headline contrast stands; if not, the claim that the time domain is required must be revised.

4. The paper treats Se window iii in two contradictory ways: as a probe of long-range order in Fig. 3e and as a probe blind to long-range coherence in Fig. 4. On p. 9 and in Fig. 3e the amplitude of the Se peak ② bleach is presented as "another sensitive probe of long-range ordering"; it is quasi-constant above Tc, onsets sharply at Tc and shows no precursor tail above Tc, unlike the ARPES replica weight and gap in Fig. 1e,f. On p. 10 the same feature is called "agnostic about the existence of long-range phase coherence among the excitons", which is what licenses reading its kinetics above Tc as evidence for short-range correlations. If the window iii amplitude is a local reporter of excitonic correlations, Fig. 3e implies those correlations are absent or temperature-independent above Tc and should show a fluctuation tail like Fig. 1e,f, which it does not. If the amplitude tracks long-range order, the authors must explain why its kinetics would still report short-range correlations. The authors should reconcile these readings explicitly, for example with a model separating amplitude and rise time, and show the rise time τ(T) at fixed fluence across the Fig. 3 temperature series; a kink or no kink at Tc in τ would directly test which reading holds.

7. Spectral shift and broadening may contribute to the temperature-dependent transient signal, and the assignment of window iii to the Se 4p hole population is assumed rather than demonstrated (Fig. 3b,c; Fig. 1o–q; pp. 8–9). The Se window iii transient at 25 K (Fig. 3c) has a derivative-like shape, negative near 53.1–53.3 eV and positive near 53.5 eV, and Fig. 3b shows a sign change in Ti window ii. Such a shape can come from an edge shift or broadening (band-gap renormalisation, change of core-exciton screening) as well as from state filling, and the static edges shift monotonically with temperature by tens of meV, so a fixed integration window on a derivative-like signal whose zero crossing moves can produce a temperature-dependent integral. The kink at Tc argues against a purely smooth artefact, but the authors should decompose the transient spectra at each temperature into pump-induced peak shift, broadening and amplitude change, and show which carries the order-parameter-like onset and that the integrated window iii signal tracks the amplitude (hole) term. The existing TDDFT machinery could compute ΔA for a hole population at E_F against a rigid shift. This assignment underpins both Fig. 3e and Fig. 4.

8. The exciton-specific reading of the Se peak ② onset is not separated from a generic CDW-gap effect (p. 9, Fig. 3c,e). Below Tc the Se 4p band is gapped and backfolded (Fig. 1c,d,g), so state filling of the near-E_F Se 4p states by the same absorbed energy will change at Tc whether the gap is excitonic or phonon/Jahn–Teller driven. The interpretation as "additional Se 4p holes from broken condensed excitons" needs either a calculation of the expected transient change with and without the CDW gap (for example the DFT DOS of Fig. 1g with a hot-carrier distribution) or a stated reason why a Peierls-type gap would give no such onset.

9. Incident fluence is used as a proxy for excited carrier density (p. 10). The argument assumes the excited density scales linearly with incident fluence over 0.2–2.9 mJ/cm². For a sub-100 nm flake under a broadband 500–1000 nm, 3.4 fs pump, the absorbed fraction depends on thickness and thin-film interference, may saturate at the highest fluences, and changes across Tc because the optical constants of TiSe2 change with CDW formation. The incident fluences are also compared with literature threshold fluences measured on bulk samples with different pulses. The authors should give the absorbed fluence and excited carrier density for each point, check for absorption saturation, and check that the temperature dependence of absorption does not contribute to the onset in Fig. 3d,e.

15. The novelty and "attosecond" framing are overstated relative to the data used and to prior work (title, abstract, p. 11). The temperature-dependent result is taken at a single delay of 200 fs, and the rise times of interest are 40–200 fs; neither needs attosecond resolution, and a ~20–30 fs XUV setup would resolve them. Ref. 22 (Heinrich et al., PRX 2023) already reported electronic and structural CDW fingerprints of TiSe2 in XUV transient absorption, so "the first demonstration where an attosecond core-level technique ... can simultaneously probe low-energy phase transitions" needs qualification.
