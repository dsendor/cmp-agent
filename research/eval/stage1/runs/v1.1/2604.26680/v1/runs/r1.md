Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors grow a moment-compensated synthetic antiferromagnet, [Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]x10, with chemically different sublattices. On the positive field branch they image two field windows of skyrmion-like textures with opposite net-moment contrast: "conventional-polarity" textures near 120 mT and "inverse-polarity" textures near 45 mT. They combine MFM, resonant SAXS, Lorentz DPC-STEM, element-resolved XMCD loops and micromagnetics to argue that all textures live only in the CoFeB layers while the CoB layers stay uniform, and they use return-point memory (RPM) to show that the two families nucleate at uncorrelated sites. Confining textures to one sublattice of a SAF, with polarity selected by field through the RKKY effective field, is a useful and well-documented addition to the SAF skyrmion literature. The conceptual step beyond earlier work on field-driven FM/AFM phase coexistence in perpendicular AF-coupled multilayers, including the authors' own PRB 2024 (Ref. 33) and Refs. 30-32, needs to be stated more precisely.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The correlative data set is unusually complete for one stack. Real-space MFM (Fig. 2a-e), reciprocal-space SAXS (Fig. 2f-j) and quantitative DPC (Fig. 2k-n) all show the same sequence: saturated, conventional skyrmions, maze, inverse skyrmions, uniform SAF.
- The DPC quantification is internally consistent. The tilt-corrected 12.8 ± 0.7 Tnm agrees with the 13.6 Tnm expected from SQUID and XRR for full reversal of one sublattice. I checked that 2 × 10 × μ0 Ms t (CoFeB) ≈ 13.6 Tnm and 3.9/tan 17° ≈ 12.8 Tnm, and both hold. It rules out both sublattices reversing together, which would give about 27 Tnm.
- The Fe-edge XMCD loop (Fig. 3b) directly shows that the CoFeB sublattice goes gradually from fully antiparallel to fully parallel between about 30 and 130 mT, which is the window where the textures appear.
- The RPM analysis (Fig. 5) is a careful way to separate the two families: high self-overlap (0.8-0.9) across nucleation paths and near-zero cross-family overlap (0.03-0.05).
- The micromagnetic parameters (Ms, Keff, J_RKKY) come largely from independent measurements (Table 1). The text says the simulated states do not depend on the initial condition.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The main claims (two field-separated texture families of opposite net polarity, confinement to the CoFeB sublattice, and distinct nucleation sites) are each backed by at least one direct measurement that a referee would accept. The items below would sharpen or narrow them.

B. Should address

1. The CoB-only loop depends on a scaling factor fitted to a different sample (Fig. 3c,d; Methods, "Soft X-ray techniques"). The "fully square" CoB loop in Fig. 3d comes from I_Co − a·I_Fe, with a = 0.35 "determined empirically by adjusting a until the reconstructed total loop matched the SQUID-VSM loop". That SQUID loop was measured on the Si/SiO2 sister sample, not the Si3N4 membrane used for XMCD. As written, the reader cannot tell how much of the squareness, which is the paper's main evidence that "the CoB sublattice remains laterally uniform", is built in by the choice of a. A stronger argument that does not depend on a is available from the paper's own data:
   - Fig. 3b shows that I_Fe spans its full saturation range over 30-130 mT.
   - The two sublattice moments are equal (Table 1).
   - The SQUID moment rises from zero to full saturation over the same window.
   - So the CoB must already be saturated.

   The authors should:
   - state this argument explicitly;
   - show Fig. 3d for a range of a (for example 0.25-0.45) and give the largest CoB reversal fraction consistent with the noise (it looks like roughly 5% of the loop amplitude);
   - compare a = 0.35 with an estimate from composition (Co40Fe40B20 against Co68B32, and the 6.69 vs 8.69 Å thicknesses) and tabulated L3 dichroism;
   - if available, compare SAXS at the Co and Fe edges at the same field. If only the CoFeB scatters, the ratio of Co-edge to Fe-edge magnetic scattering intensity should match the CoFeB Co/Fe XMCD ratio, which would test the "CoFeB only" claim directly in imaging.

   Fig. 2 does not say at which edge the SAXS patterns were taken; please add it.

2. The effective-field mechanism is qualitative, and the value and meaning of J_RKKY are unclear (Discussion; Methods; Table 1; Eq. 4; Fig. 6 caption). The central explanation is that the field on CoFeB, H_ext − H_RKKY, changes sign between the two families. Several points need attention.
   - (a) J_RKKY = −0.034 mJ/m² is obtained from μ0Hs Ms t/2 with μ0Hs = 125 mT. In a perpendicular multilayer whose transition proceeds through domains (Fig. 2), the saturation field also contains magnetostatic and domain-wall contributions. The prefactor also depends on whether each layer has one or two coupled neighbours. The authors should justify the formula or determine J independently, for example from an in-plane hard-axis loop or minor loops of the plateau edge at ±30 mT.
   - (b) With this J, the interlayer field on an interior CoFeB layer is 2|J|/(Ms t) ≈ 125 mT. That would put the sign change of the effective field near 125 mT, not near the maze state at about 80 mT. A short quantitative accounting is needed: RKKY field, the demagnetising field −μ0⟨M⟩ of the whole stack (which changes along the branch), and interlayer dipolar fields. It should place the zero of the effective field where the maze appears and explain why CoFeB never fully reverses until 125 mT.
   - (c) |J| = 0.034 mJ/m² is weak by RKKY standards. The spacer is Ru(6 Å)/Pt(9 Å), about 1.5 nm, not "0.7 nm Ru" as the Fig. 6 caption says. So the repeated description of "strong AFM RKKY" coupling and the Methods statement that 6 Å Ru "maximize[s] the antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling" need qualifying.
   - (d) In Eq. 4, H_RKKY = −J m_j/(Ms t). With J < 0 (Table 2) this field points along the neighbour's magnetisation, which is ferromagnetic coupling, and the μ0 is missing. Please check the sign convention used in the code.

3. Several simulation claims are not shown, and the history differs from experiment (Micromagnetic modelling section; Discussion; Methods; Table 2).
   - (a) D is a free parameter (1.00 and 1.10 mJ/m²). State what it was fitted to: field windows, sizes or Néel character.
   - (b) Each state is obtained by "field-cooling from randomised magnetisation" at a fixed field. The experiment, and the RPM argument, concern states reached along a sweep from saturation or from the SAF state. Please show a simulated descending branch (m(H) and the textures) for direct comparison with Fig. 1c and Fig. 3b, and with the field windows and skyrmion diameters (81/58 nm and 64/48 nm).
   - (c) The CoB is modelled with the CoFeB thickness (6 Å, itself not the 6.69 Å measured) and the CoFeB Ms, with Ku rescaled. Its intralayer dipolar energy and wall energy therefore differ from the real CoB. Say why this does not affect the conclusion that only CoFeB textures.
   - (d) Several results that support the Discussion are "not shown":
     - the symmetric-stack simulation;
     - the statement that weaker RKKY restores textures in both layers and stronger RKKY localises them;
     - the current-driven Hall-angle simulations and the statement that the two families "deflect in opposite transverse directions".

     The J_RKKY dependence underpins the "general design principle" and should go in the SI. The Hall-angle statement should either be shown or removed. Also state the boundary conditions (periodic or open) of the 400 × 400 nm box, which holds only a few textures at these sizes.

4. The RPM interpretation needs a chance baseline (Fig. 5c; Supplementary Note S5). The cross-family RPM of 0.03-0.05 is read as "uncorrelated nucleation sites". For dense arrays of 60-80 nm objects, as in Fig. 2b,d, random placement would already give a finite overlap that depends on the matching tolerance. A value well below that baseline would mean anticorrelation, for example inverse skyrmions sitting in the interstices of the conventional lattice or maze. That is a different physical statement. Please:
   - compute the RPM for randomly shifted or shuffled maps of each family;
   - describe the image registration and drift correction;
   - define the stated error bars (standard deviation over what?).

   The further step from "uncorrelated sites" to "distinct pinning/formation mechanisms: external-field-driven … and RKKY-driven" is not argued. In the CoFeB both families are reversed domains in a background set by the local effective field. Either give a mechanism for why their pinning sites differ, or soften the wording. RPM probes pinning and nucleation, not "thermodynamic stability" as stated at the start of that section.

5. The novelty relative to prior AF-coupled perpendicular multilayers is not set out (Introduction; Discussion). FM tubes in an AFM background and AFM tubes in an FM background along the AFM-FM transition of [Co/Pt]/Ru-type multilayers are discussed in Refs. 30-33, including the authors' own Ref. 33 on phase coexistence in a SAF. Within the CoFeB layer, the "inverse-polarity" skyrmion has its core antiparallel to the local effective field. In that sense it is a conventional skyrmion in a layer whose effective field has reversed, which is how the authors explain it themselves. The title ("Beyond conventional skyrmions") and abstract should state precisely what is new:
   - chemical asymmetry under moment compensation;
   - confinement of the textures to one sublattice;
   - two families with opposite topological charge in one sweep.

   A paragraph explaining what Ref. 33 and Refs. 30-32 did and did not show would help.

6. The "general materials-design principle" rests on one composition (Discussion; Summary). The Discussion lists three requirements: strong AF exchange, moment compensation, and engineered asymmetry, with the Deff/Keff ratio named as decisive. None is varied experimentally, and the DMI values that enter Deff/Keff are simulation inputs, not measurements. The CoB and CoFeB reference multilayers used for Table 1 already exist. MFM field series on them, showing that the CoFeB stack textures readily and the CoB stack does not at comparable effective fields, would directly support the anisotropy argument at little cost. A second SAF with different spacer thickness, and hence different J, would test the predicted J dependence. Otherwise, phrase the generality as a hypothesis supported by simulation.

7. The comparisons across samples and techniques need their conditions stated (Fig. 2; Methods). Field-matched comparisons are made across three substrates (Si/SiO2 for MFM and SQUID, 25 nm Si3N4 for LTEM, 200 nm Si3N4 for XMCD and SAXS). Please:
   - show at least the m(H) or XMCD loop of the membrane samples against the Si/SiO2 SQUID loop, to establish that the field windows transfer between them;
   - state the in-plane field during DPC at 17° tilt (about 40 mT at −131 mT) and say whether it affects the textures and the 25% smaller DPC diameters (58 vs 81 nm, 48 vs 64 nm);
   - explain the strongly anisotropic SAXS arcs at 80 mT (Fig. 2h) against the nearly isotropic MFM maze (Fig. 2c). This suggests a field misalignment or in-plane component in the SAXS setup.
   - give the sample temperature for each technique; the XMCD magnet is "cryo-cooled" and Ms, K and J all depend on temperature.

   MFM tip perturbation should also be addressed. A Co-coated tip 20 nm above a stack whose CoFeB effective field is near zero in the maze window could nucleate or move textures. Repeated scans at fixed field without cycling would show whether it does.

C. Minor

8. Fig. 2 caption, panel n: "line profile taken from the marked position in g". Panel g is a SAXS pattern. The text says the profile is from the maze state, so this should be panel l.
9. The Fig. 6 caption says "0.7 nm Ru spacers". Methods give 6.03 Å Ru plus 8.99 Å Pt between the magnetic layers. Please make them consistent.
10. Fig. 1c axis label reads "m (×10⁸ Am²)". It should read 10⁻⁸, consistent with Fig. 5a and the 8.85·10⁻⁸ Am² per sublattice.
11. The Fig. 2 caption and abstract describe conventional skyrmion cores as "antiparallel to the external field". MFM and DPC sense the thickness-integrated moment. In the thickness average the core is a zero-moment AFM region in an FM background (Fig. 6). The "antiparallel" statement holds only for the CoFeB layers, which is established later by XMCD. Rewording would avoid confusion.
12. "Deff = D/t" is called the "DMI energy density". In the convention of Eq. 5, D in mJ/m² is already the thickness-normalised constant, and the surface DMI is Ds = D·t. Please clarify the notation and say whether any DMI was measured for these layers.
13. Discretisation: 4 nm in-plane cells against wall widths of √(A/Keff) ≈ 8.5 nm (CoFeB) and 6 nm (CoB). Please state the convergence check.
14. XMCD loops are plotted as transmitted intensity. State whether a −ln(I/I0) conversion was tested. Linearity in m is assumed in Eqs. 1-2.
15. The paper uses "unequivocally" and "topologically protected". Neither the topological charge nor the Néel character (SI S4) is shown in the main text. Please give a short main-text summary of the tilt-series evidence for Néel chirality in each family.
16. Typos: "gemeotry" (Methods, SAXS); Table 1 header spacing; the Data Availability DOI placeholder.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Add a schematic or plot of the effective field on CoFeB, from RKKY, demagnetising and Zeeman terms, against applied field, with the measured field windows of the conventional skyrmions, maze and inverse skyrmions marked on it. This would turn Fig. 6 from an illustration into a quantitative argument.
- Move the J_RKKY-dependence simulations and the a-sensitivity analysis of Fig. 3d into the SI, and refer to them where the claims are made.
- Report skyrmion density and size against field for both families from MFM next to the simulated trends (currently in SI S3), so the "parameter-matched" modelling can be judged in the main text.
- If beamtime data at both edges exist, a Co-edge against Fe-edge SAXS or STXM comparison at the same field would be the most direct single-sublattice evidence and worth including.

Verdict: minor revision
