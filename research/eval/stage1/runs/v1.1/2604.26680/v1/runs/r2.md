Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript studies a moment-compensated synthetic antiferromagnet, Ta/[Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]×10, in which the two sublattices are chemically different. It reports that, within one field sweep, two families of Néel skyrmions appear in the CoFeB sublattice only. "Conventional-polarity" skyrmions (cores antiparallel to the applied field) form near 120 mT, and "inverse-polarity" skyrmions (cores parallel to it) form near 45 mT. The CoB sublattice stays laterally uniform throughout. The evidence combines MFM, resonant SAXS, Lorentz DPC-STEM, element-resolved XMCD loops, micromagnetics and return-point-memory (RPM) statistics. Confining textures to one sublattice of a SAF, with core polarity set by the sign of an effective (applied minus RKKY) field, is a useful design idea for layer-selective 3D textures. The idea that a soft layer in an exchange-biased or AF-coupled stack sees a shifted effective field is not new in itself (Hellwig et al., refs. 30–32; the authors' own ref. 33). The contribution lies in the clean, multi-probe demonstration that the textures are skyrmions confined to one sublattice and come in two polarities.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The correlative data set is unusually complete for a multilayer skyrmion paper. MFM (sensitive to H), SAXS (to M) and DPC (to B) agree on both families, and on the field windows for the maze state and the saturated states.
- The DPC quantification is good. The tilt-corrected domain contrast of 12.8 ± 0.7 T nm matches the 13.6 ± 0.2 T nm expected for full reversal of one sublattice across all ten repeats: 2μ0Ms t × 10 with the Table 1 values reproduces 13.6 T nm. This is strong evidence that each texture is a full, vertically coherent reversal of one sublattice.
- The Fe-edge XMCD loop (Fig. 3b) isolates the CoFeB response directly. It shows the gradual CoFeB reversal between ~30 and ~160 mT.
- The micromagnetic model uses measured Ms, Keff and J_RKKY and reproduces the sequence of states and the sublattice selectivity (Fig. 4).
- The RPM data within each family are clean: values of 0.8–0.9 over nine repeats and four paths.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

None. The two headline claims, two polarity families and textures confined to CoFeB, are supported by evidence a reasonable referee would accept. The items below concern how firmly the supporting interpretations are established.

**B. Should address**

1. **The cross-family RPM result is over-interpreted, and it has no baseline (Fig. 5c, p. 11–12; Abstract).** The authors take the cross-family RPM of 0.03–0.05 as showing "uncorrelated nucleation sites" and "distinct pinning/formation mechanisms: external-field–driven for conventional-polarity skyrmions and RKKY–driven for inverse-polarity skyrmions."
   - There are two problems. First, the RPM value expected for two uncorrelated skyrmion maps is not given. It depends on the overlap criterion (Supplementary Note S5) and on the skyrmion area fraction, which in Fig. 2b,d looks like tens of percent. If the chance baseline is well above 0.03–0.05, the two families are anticorrelated, not uncorrelated.
   - Second, anticorrelation is expected on purely geometric grounds. Paths 2 and 4 start from the same maze. Conventional skyrmions are the shrunken remains of the down-CoFeB stripes, and inverse skyrmions are the shrunken remains of the up-CoFeB stripes. They therefore sit in complementary regions of a reproducible maze whatever the nucleation physics.
   - Low cross-family overlap thus does not, on its own, show that one family is "field-driven" and the other "RKKY-driven".
   - The authors should do three things. Report the chance-level RPM for each pair, by randomly shifting or rotating one map, or by comparing maps from disjoint areas. Report the RPM of the maze state itself. Test whether inverse-skyrmion sites fall preferentially on the former up-domains of the maze. The "distinct mechanism" sentence in the Abstract and Results should be narrowed to what the data show: reproducible, family-specific sites.

2. **The CoB-only assignment leans on a scaling factor tuned to the target result (Fig. 3c,d; Eqs. 1–2; Methods p. 17).** a = 0.35 was chosen "until the reconstructed total loop matched the SQUID-VSM loop". The agreement in Fig. 3c is therefore built in, not a validation. The squareness of the reconstructed CoB loop in Fig. 3d also depends on a.
   - The authors already have a stronger, a-independent argument and should make it explicit. Between ~30 and ~160 mT, the net SQUID moment (Fig. 1c) rises by 2m_CoFeB. That is the full CoFeB swing seen in the Fe-edge loop (Fig. 3b). The mean CoB moment therefore cannot change in this range, and since it is saturated at both ends, CoB is uniform.
   - The authors should also show I_CoB(H) for a range of a (say 0.25–0.45), and compare a = 0.35 with an a-priori estimate. The Co content per area is 9 nm Co68B32 versus 7 nm Co40Fe40B20, and the Fe and Co L3 XMCD per atom are known approximately.
   - Note too that the XMCD and SQUID loops come from different sister samples: a 200 nm Si3N4 membrane versus thermally oxidised Si. The SQUID loop is used to calibrate the XMCD decomposition, so the samples' equivalence (switching field, saturation field) should be shown.

3. **Element-resolved SAXS would be direct evidence for CoFeB-only textures; the photon energy is not stated (Fig. 2f–j; Methods p. 17).** The Methods give no photon energy for the SAXS patterns. Resonant scattering at the Fe L3 edge proves directly that CoFeB hosts the lateral modulation. Patterns at the Co L3 edge from the same field states would then test the claim quantitatively: the magnetic scattering at the Co edge should match only the Co in CoFeB, with no extra contribution from CoB. The authors should state the energy used for Fig. 2f–j and, if both edges were recorded, show both. This would turn the spatially averaged XMCD argument into a spatially resolved, element-specific one.

4. **The extracted J_RKKY and the effective-field picture are only qualitative (Methods p. 16; Discussion p. 12–13; Fig. 6).**
   - J_RKKY = −0.034 mJ m⁻² comes from J = μ0 Hs Ms t_FM/2 with μ0Hs = 125 mT. This ignores the dipolar energy of the FM state and the fact that reversal here proceeds through textures, not a uniform spin flip. It also treats the outermost layers, which have only one CoB neighbour, the same as the interior ones.
   - The resulting RKKY field on CoFeB is ~125 mT. On the paper's own picture (Fig. 6), the effective field on CoFeB should then vanish near 125 mT, which is where the maze state should sit. Experimentally the maze is at 80 mT and conventional skyrmions at 120 mT. Both the dipolar shift and the error in J could explain this.
   - Note also that the interlayer spacer is Ru(0.6 nm)/Pt(0.9 nm), not bare Ru. The text and Fig. 6 caption call it a "0.7 nm Ru spacer". Coupling through a 1.5 nm composite spacer is weak, and its value matters for the whole mechanism.
   - The authors should do one of two things. Give an independent determination of J, for example from minor loops of the SAF switching at ±30 mT, or from a CoB/Ru/Pt/CoFeB reference trilayer. Or show quantitatively, using the simulation, where effective field and stack demagnetising field place each state's field window, and how sensitive those windows are to J.

5. **Key supporting simulations are "not shown" (p. 10 and p. 13–14).** Four statements rest on simulations that are not presented:
   - that the top-layer size asymmetry disappears in a symmetric stack;
   - that the skyrmion Hall angle is finite;
   - that the two families deflect in opposite directions;
   - that "reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers, whereas stronger coupling localizes the non-collinear state."

   The last statement is the main support for the "general materials-design principle" in the Discussion and Summary. The authors should show these results, at minimum a J_RKKY (and ideally Keff,CoFeB) phase diagram of where textures form, or remove the claims. The opposite-Hall-deflection statement in particular is presented as a consequence but is untested experimentally. It should be labelled a prediction.

6. **The generality claim rests on one stack (Discussion p. 13; Summary p. 14).** "The mechanism reported here reflects a general materials-design principle rather than a peculiarity of the present stack" and "our results establish a general materials-design principle" are drawn from a single composition, one Ru thickness and one thickness ratio. They are supported only by unshown simulations. The authors should either show at least one further stack, for example a different spacer thickness or a CoB/CoFeB thickness pair, that confirms the predicted trend, or present the principle as a model-based proposal.

7. **Most model parameters are fixed, but the decisive one is fitted, and the comparison with data is qualitative (Methods p. 19–20; Table 2; Discussion p. 13).**
   - D is a free parameter (1.0 and 1.1 mJ m⁻²) and A is fixed at 8 pJ/m. Yet the Discussion attributes the sublattice selectivity to "the enhanced ratio Deff/Keff in CoFeB" and to thickness compensating for the CoFeB's "potentially reduced intrinsic DMI". These statements rest on the fitted D, not on a measurement. The Discussion's Deff = D/t also conflicts with Table 2, where D is already an areal quantity in mJ m⁻².
   - CoB is modelled with CoFeB's thickness and Ms, with Ku rescaled. The cell thickness is given as 6 Å "same as the experimental value for CoFeB", but XRR gives 6.69 Å.
   - The authors should test how the selectivity depends on D and Keff, for example by swapping or equalising the two sublattices' parameters.
   - They should also compare simulated and measured quantities directly: skyrmion diameters per family (64/48 nm and 81/58 nm measured), field windows, and the size-versus-field trends mentioned for Supplementary Note S3.
   - It should be stated whether the 400 × 400 nm box uses periodic boundaries. It holds only a few 50–80 nm skyrmions, which affects densities and the maze.

8. **History dependence in experiment versus field-cooled equilibrium in simulation (p. 9).** In the simulations, each state was obtained by "field-cooling from randomised magnetisation" at a fixed field. In the experiment, the states are reached along a sweep, and the hysteresis and RPM results show that history matters. The SAF flip at ±30 mT is itself hysteretic. The authors should run at least one quasi-static field sweep in the simulation, from saturation down to 0 mT, and show that the same sequence and field windows appear.

9. **Field geometry and sign differences between techniques (Fig. 2, Fig. 4; Methods p. 18).**
   - MFM is shown at positive fields, while DPC and the simulations are at negative fields. Lorentz images are taken at a 17° tilt, with only the out-of-plane component quoted. At −131 mT this implies an in-plane field component of roughly 40 mT. That can distort Néel skyrmions and shift the field windows of a weakly anisotropic CoFeB layer.
   - The authors should state the sweep history for each data set, discuss or bound the effect of the in-plane component, and state the sample temperature for each technique. The XMCD endstation is cryo-cooled with 30–400 K control, and none of the measurement temperatures are given.

10. **Topological character of the inverse-polarity textures.** The Néel character and fixed chirality are said to be verified by tilt-series Fresnel imaging in Supplementary Note S4. The main text should state explicitly that this was done for both families. The claim that the two families have opposite topological charge, and hence opposite Hall deflection, depends on it. The supplementary notes were not available to this reviewer, so this point, the extended DPC series (S2) and the RPM procedure (S5) could not be checked.

**C. Minor**

11. Fig. 2 caption: "DPC line profile taken from the marked position in **g**". Panel g is a SAXS pattern; the profile is presumably from **l**, where the box is drawn, or from k, which carries the dashed line. The text says the profile is for the maze state, i.e. panel l. Please correct this, and say what the dashed line in k marks.

12. p. 13: "at zero external field, the strong AFM RKKY interaction saturates the CoFeB layers in the up state". Fig. 6 and the rest of the argument have CoB up and CoFeB down at zero field. Please correct.

13. Spacer thickness is inconsistent. The Fig. 6 caption and p. 12 say "0.7 nm Ru". Fig. 1a, Methods and XRR give 0.6 nm (6.03 Å). The spacer actually separating CoB from CoFeB is Ru/Pt.

14. Fig. 4a–b: the background in the cross-sections appears as alternating red and blue rows. At −130 mT a conventional-polarity state should have a uniformly blue FM background. Please check the colour scale or the panel. In any case, mark the CoB and CoFeB rows in the cross-sections so readers can read them unambiguously.

15. Fig. 5c legend lists pairs such as "1 with 3" and "3 with 1" without saying whether the comparison is symmetric. The error bars are called "standard deviation", but it is not said over what (skyrmions, sub-areas, image registrations). Please specify. Also state the number of skyrmions per map and the scan area.

16. The MFM tip (6.5 nm Co, ~20 nm lift) produces a stray field near the soft CoFeB layer close to compensation. A sentence on whether repeated scanning perturbs nucleation sites would reassure readers about the RPM data. For example, compare the RPM with and without intermediate scans.

17. "We unequivocally attribute" (p. 4) and "Return-point memory measurements confirm independent nucleation pathways" (Abstract) are stronger than the evidence warrants; see items 1–2.

18. Table 1 Ms values are derived on the assumption that the two sublattice moments are identical. This should be stated as an assumption, supported by the 1% remanence, rather than as a measurement.

19. Typos: "gemeotry" (p. 17), "negligble" (p. 3), "for of each sub-lattice" (Table 2 caption).

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Replace the a-tuned decomposition in Fig. 3 with the a-independent argument, combining the Fe-edge loop amplitude with the SQUID ΔM, and add element-resolved SAXS at both edges. Together these would make the CoFeB-only claim essentially airtight.
2. Add a chance baseline and a maze-registration analysis to the RPM study, and reframe the mechanistic conclusion accordingly.
3. Present the J_RKKY and Keff phase diagram from the simulations, and one quantitative table comparing simulated and measured field windows and diameters per family.
4. Sharpen the novelty statement against refs. 30–33, especially the authors' own PRB 2024 on AFM–FM phase coexistence in a SAF. Say what is new beyond the confinement of textures to one sublattice and the two-polarity sequence.

**5. Verdict**

Verdict: minor revision
