Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors study a moment-compensated synthetic antiferromagnet (SAF), [Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]×10. In this stack the two sublattices have equal Ms·t but different effective anisotropy. Within one field sweep they observe two families of Néel skyrmion tubes with opposite core polarity relative to the applied field: conventional-polarity skyrmions near 120 mT and inverse-polarity skyrmions near 45 mT, separated by a maze state. They attribute all textures to the CoFeB sublattice, with the CoB sublattice staying uniform. Each layer hosts an ordinary chiral skyrmion, so what is new is that the texture sits in a single sublattice and that two polarity families can be reached in a compensated SAF. That is a useful extension of the SAF-skyrmion literature and of the older AF-coupled PMA multilayer work on ferromagnetic domains in an AF background (refs. 30–33). How strong the result is depends mainly on how firmly the single-sublattice assignment is established.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Several independent techniques probe different quantities on sister samples, all with sub-100 nm resolution: MFM (stray field H), resonant SAXS, Lorentz DPC-STEM (induction B), element-resolved XMCD (M) and SQUID-VSM. Together they give a consistent picture of the field sequence FM → conventional skyrmions → maze → inverse skyrmions → SAF.
- The DPC analysis is quantitative. After tilt correction, the integrated-induction step (12.8 ± 0.7 T nm) matches the value expected for full reversal of one sublattice in all ten repeats, 2μ0Ms·t × 10 ≈ 13.6 T nm. This is a strong check that the maze contrast is a single-sublattice reversal, not partial reversal of both.
- The return-point-memory analysis is a clever use of MFM. It shows that each family reappears at the same sites over repeated cycles (RPM 0.8–0.9), but that the two families do not share sites (cross-RPM 0.03–0.05).
- The micromagnetic simulations use mostly measured parameters. They reproduce the field windows and the localisation of textures in CoFeB. The authors also checked robustness against different initial states.
- The design rule is clearly stated and could be useful for layer-selective 3D textures: strong AF exchange, moment compensation, and asymmetric Keff and D across the sublattices.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. **The XMCD sublattice decomposition rests on one empirically tuned parameter that does two physically different jobs** (Eqs. 1–2, Fig. 3c,d, Methods p. 17).
   - **What the paper does.** The CoB loop is obtained as I_CoB = I_Co − a·I_Fe, and the total loop as I_Fe + I_CoB = (1 − a)·I_Fe + I_Co. The value a = 0.35 is "determined empirically by adjusting a until the reconstructed total loop matched the SQUID-VSM loop, with a closed central area."
   - **Why one parameter is not enough.** Two separate physical factors are needed:
     - (i) the fraction of the Co-edge signal that comes from the Co in Co40Fe40B20, relative to the Fe-edge signal of the same layers;
     - (ii) the relative sensitivity (dichroism per unit moment, sample absorption, detector response) of the Fe-edge and Co-edge measurements, needed to put I_Fe and I_CoB on a common moment scale.

     Eq. 1 adds I_Fe and I_CoB with unit weight, which implicitly fixes factor (ii) as 1 − a. As a result, the plateau-matching condition that fixes a is not an independent determination of the Co-in-CoFeB contribution.
   - **Why it matters.** The flat CoB loop above |μ0H| = 30 mT (Fig. 3d) is the only direct, element-resolved evidence that CoB stays uniform while textures exist. That flatness holds only if a also equals the ratio of the high-field slopes of I_Co and I_Fe. In addition, transmitted intensity depends exponentially on absorption, not linearly. The linear relation I(H) ∝ m(H) stated in the Fig. 3 caption needs a brief justification or a log-ratio treatment.
   - **What to do.**
     - Estimate a independently, from composition, thicknesses and tabulated or measured L3 XMCD cross-sections. Ideally, measure it at both edges on the CoB-only and CoFeB-only reference multilayers already grown for the Keff measurements.
     - Introduce a separate normalisation factor for Eq. 1.
     - Show I_CoB(H) for a range of a around the chosen value, and state which features (the high-field flatness, the square switching at ±30 mT) are robust.
     - If the SAXS data exist at both the Fe and Co L3 edges, report whether the Co-edge scattering intensity scales as expected for CoFeB-only textures. This would be a direct element-resolved test of the single-sublattice claim for the skyrmion states themselves, not only for averaged loops.

     Without this, "unequivocally attribute the observed textures to the CoFeB sublattice" (p. 4) is overstated. The DPC step (item 2 of the acceptance points) cannot tell which sublattice reverses, because Ms·t is the same for both.

B. Should address

2. **The J_RKKY extraction and its use as a simulation input** (Methods p. 16, Table 2).
   - The paper uses J_RKKY = μ0Hs·Ms·t_FM/2 with μ0Hs = 125 mT. This is the bilayer expression. In a 20-layer stack, interior layers are coupled on both sides, so coherent saturation would give roughly a factor-of-two smaller |J| (about −0.017 mJ m⁻²).
   - More fundamentally, the paper's own data show that this PMA SAF saturates through domain and skyrmion formation and collapse. The "saturation field" is therefore not a coherent spin-flip field, and the formula's premise does not hold.
   - The quoted Hs (125 mT) also differs from the saturation seen in Fig. 1c and MFM (about 150 mT; skyrmions still present at 120 mT).
   - Because J_RKKY sets the effective field that drives the whole mechanism and is fed directly into the simulations, the authors should:
     - justify the formula, or extract J independently, for example from the switching field at the plateau edge, a minor-loop shift, or fitting the full m(H) with the simulation;
     - show how the simulated field windows change for J within a plausible range.

3. **Which asymmetry drives single-sublattice confinement is not isolated, and key supporting simulations are "not shown"** (pp. 10, 13–14).
   - Ms·t is equal for both sublattices, and the simulations use equal Ms and t. The Zeeman and RKKY effective fields per unit moment are therefore identical for the two sublattices, so the asymmetry must come from Keff (0.22 vs 0.11 MJ m⁻³) and/or D (1.00 vs 1.10 mJ m⁻²). This makes the abstract's "external field and interlayer exchange act asymmetrically on the sublattices" imprecise.
   - D is a free parameter. The text does not say which observable it was tuned to: domain period, skyrmion size, or the field windows.
   - Several claims rest on results that are not shown:
     - that reducing J_RKKY restores textures in both layers while stronger coupling localises them;
     - the symmetric-stack test of the top-layer size deviation;
     - the opposite skyrmion Hall angles.
   - Please include:
     - simulations with D swapped, or set equal, and with Keff equal;
     - a J_RKKY sweep;
     - a statement of how D was chosen.

   These are needed to support the "general materials-design principle" in the Discussion and the Summary.

4. **The RPM data support distinct pinning sites, but not distinct "field-driven" versus "RKKY-driven" nucleation mechanisms** (pp. 10–12, Fig. 5).
   - Cross-RPM values of 0.03–0.05 need a chance-level baseline. Please give the expected overlap for randomly placed skyrmions at the measured densities and sizes, under the same overlap criterion.
   - A value well below chance would suggest anticorrelation, for example inverse skyrmions forming at the gaps between former conventional cores. That is physically informative and should be discussed.
   - Please state how image registration and drift between the +120 mT and +40 mT maps were handled.
   - The labels "external-field–driven" and "RKKY-driven" are interpretive. The CoFeB layer always responds to the sum H + H_RKKY, so a different set of pinning sites alone does not show a different mechanism.
   - The RPM data say nothing about "thermodynamic stability" (p. 10). Please rephrase.

5. **Single sample stack, and a generality claim** (Discussion, Summary).
   - Everything is shown for one composition, imaged on sister samples on three different substrates: SiO2/Si, 25 nm Si3N4 and 200 nm Si3N4.
   - The claimed general design principle would be much stronger with either:
     - an experimental control, such as a symmetric SAF (CoFeB/CoFeB or CoB/CoB) or a stack with different Ru thickness or Keff contrast, showing textures in both sublattices or the loss of one family; or
     - at least a comparison of the field windows (from SQUID or SAXS) across the sister samples, to show that substrate and strain differences do not shift the states.
   - The paper should also state more clearly how these results go beyond the authors' own earlier SAF phase-coexistence work (ref. 33) and the FM-domain-in-AF-background phases of refs. 30–32. The "inverse-polarity skyrmions" are FM-aligned tubes in an AF background, closely related to those phases. What is new is their chirality and their single-sublattice character.

6. **Evidence for conventional-polarity skyrmions and for single-sublattice character in Lorentz DPC** (Fig. 2k, n; p. 7).
   - The −131 mT DPC image (Fig. 2k) shows very little discernible contrast, yet the text describes "bright features in k".
   - The quantitative single-sublattice argument is made only for the maze state (Fig. 2l, n). It is not made for either skyrmion family, and those families are the paper's headline.
   - Please:
     - show a contrast-enhanced or averaged profile across individual skyrmions in k and m, compared with the expected induction step;
     - state how the zero-deflection reference was set. It is "set by using assumptions informed primarily by the XMCD I(H) loops", which makes the DPC quantification partly dependent on the XMCD decomposition criticised in item 1;
     - discuss the in-plane field component, about tan 17° × |H|, present during tilted imaging.

C. Minor

7. **Spacer thickness and stacking.** Fig. 6's caption and p. 12 refer to a "0.7 nm Ru spacer", but the stack has Ru(6 Å), and the full spacer between CoFeB and CoB is Ru(6)/Pt(9). Please make these consistent, and clarify which interfaces set the DMI sign in each layer.

8. **Simulation geometry.**
   - The simulation uses CoFeB thickness 6 Å ("same as the experimental value"), but the XRR value is 6.69 Å.
   - It is not stated how the non-magnetic spacers enter the magnetostatics (layer pitch), or whether periodic boundary conditions are used for the 400 × 400 nm box, which holds only a few skyrmions.
   - The simulations are at T = 0 while the experiments are at room temperature. Please comment on how this affects comparison of the field windows.

9. **Mixed DMI units.** p. 13 discusses D_eff = D/t as an energy density and argues that the thinner CoFeB compensates for lower intrinsic DMI. Table 2, however, gives D in mJ m⁻² for layers of equal thickness, with CoFeB having the larger D. Please reconcile.

10. **Fig. 2 caption.** It says the panel-n line profile is "taken from the marked position in g". It should be l.

11. **Skyrmion diameters.** For the quoted diameters (81 ± 4, 58 ± 6, 64 ± 4, 48 ± 5 nm), state the number of skyrmions measured, whether the uncertainty is a standard deviation or a standard error, and the fitting procedure. Also state the SAXS photon energy and the ring-to-size conversion.

12. **MFM tip.** Comment on possible perturbation of the soft CoFeB textures by the tip, especially near compensation and during RPM imaging. Give the measurement temperature for MFM, XMCD (cryo-cooled magnet) and LTEM.

13. **Terminology.** The inverse-polarity texture is an ordinary skyrmion within the CoFeB layer. The title phrase "beyond conventional skyrmions" and the "polarity" convention (defined relative to the external field rather than the local background) should be explained clearly early in the paper.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Calibrate the XMCD decomposition on the single-sublattice reference multilayers, and add element-resolved SAXS at the Co edge, or element-resolved imaging (for example STXM or holography at both edges). This would make the single-sublattice claim direct rather than inferred.
- Present a simulated phase diagram versus J_RKKY and ΔKeff (or D asymmetry), with the experimental point marked. This would turn the qualitative design principle into a quantitative one.
- Plot the measured skyrmion size versus field for both families against the simulated trends (currently only in the SI). This is a direct, quantitative test of the model.
- Add a random-placement baseline and a spatial cross-correlation analysis to the RPM data, to sharpen the claim about distinct nucleation sites.

5. Verdict

Verdict: major revision
