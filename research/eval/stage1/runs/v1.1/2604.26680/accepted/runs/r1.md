Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors build a compensated synthetic antiferromagnet (SAF) from two chemically different sublattices, [Pt/Co68B32/Ru] and [Pt/Co40Fe40B20/Ru] ×10, with equal Ms·t. They report two families of Néel skyrmions with opposite core polarity, separated by a maze state, and argue that both live only in the CoFeB sublattice. In their picture the effective field on CoFeB, the external field minus the RKKY field from a uniform CoB sublattice, changes sign as the external field is lowered. Confining textures to one sublattice of a compensated SAF, with a polarity set by a tunable effective field, is a new and appealing design idea for 3D spin textures. The correlative dataset (MFM, resonant SAXS, Lorentz DPC, element-resolved XMCD loops, micromagnetics) supports it well. The main limits are that the layer attribution is indirect and that the generality claims rest on simulations the paper does not show.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear, physically intuitive mechanism (effective-field sign reversal on the softer sublattice) that accounts for both skyrmion families and the maze state between them (Fig. 6).
- Strong correlative imaging. MFM (Fig. 2a–e), SAXS (Fig. 2f–j) and DPC (Fig. 2k–m) all show the same sequence of states on sister samples, which also gives some evidence of reproducibility across substrates.
- A quantitative DPC check. The tilt-corrected induction step (3.9 ± 0.2 Tnm / tan 17° = 12.8 ± 0.7 Tnm) matches 2μ0Ms·t of one sublattice (13.6 Tnm). Reversal of both sublattices would give about twice that.
- The Fe L3 loop (Fig. 3b) is a clean, CoFeB-only measurement. It shows that CoFeB reverses gradually over the field windows where textures are imaged.
- Careful structural metrology (XRR thicknesses to ±0.07 Å, cross-section STEM in Fig. 1b) and open data.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The main claims (two opposite-polarity skyrmion families, hosted in the CoFeB sublattice, explained by an effective-field reversal) are supported well enough by the combination of the Fe-edge XMCD loop, the DPC quantification and the imaging. The items below would make them firmer.

B. Should address

1. The assignment of the textures to CoFeB, and of uniformity to CoB, rests on a loop decomposition with an empirically tuned parameter (Fig. 3, Eqs. 1–2, Methods "Soft X-ray techniques").
   - What is wrong: the factor a = 0.35 is chosen so that the reconstructed total matches the SQUID loop "with a closed central area". The square CoB loop (Fig. 3d) is then derived with that same a. Eq. 1 also implicitly gives the CoB and CoFeB contributions equal weight per unit moment, and the SQUID curve is "rescaled". So the decomposition has several adjustable degrees of freedom.
   - Why the DPC check does not resolve it: because Ms·t is equal in the two sublattices (Extended Data Table 1), the 12.8 Tnm step shows that one sublattice reverses but cannot say which.
   - Why it matters: a scenario in which part of the reversal occurs in CoB, scaling with the CoFeB reversal, would be partly absorbed into a.
   - What to do:
     - Show I_CoB(H) for a range of a (for example 0.25–0.45) and give an independent estimate of a from the elemental compositions, thicknesses and L3 cross-sections.
     - Make the moment-accounting argument explicit. If the Fe loop shows full CoFeB reversal between saturation and the plateau (outer-branch change equal to the jump at ±30 mT), then compensation alone requires the average CoB moment to stay saturated.
     - State at which edge the SAXS patterns in Fig. 2f–j were taken. If data exist at both the Fe and Co L3 edges, compare the magnetic scattering intensity ratio with the XMCD ratio. Scattering at the Fe edge is a direct, spatially resolved signature of CoFeB texture, and the ratio would show whether CoB contributes any texture contrast.

2. The return-point-memory analysis is over-interpreted (Fig. 5, "Return-point memory" section).
   - What is wrong: the cross-type RPM values (0.03–0.05) compare maps taken at different fields (+120 mT and +40 mT), with different densities and sizes and opposite MFM contrast. No baseline is given for uncorrelated positions at the same density.
   - Why it matters: for a dense skyrmion lattice such as Fig. 2b, random overlap should be of the order of the area fraction. A value well below that would indicate anticorrelation (inverse skyrmions forming between former conventional sites), not merely "uncorrelated" sites. The paper also infers "external-field-driven versus RKKY-driven" nucleation, which RPM alone does not test.
   - What to do:
     - Give the RPM definition in the main text, including how opposite contrast is handled.
     - Add a randomised-position baseline (shuffled maps at matched density) for both same-type and cross-type comparisons.
     - Compare at least the two families' maps after a field excursion that brings both to a common field, or temper the mechanistic interpretation.

3. The general design-principle claims are not shown (Discussion and Outlook).
   - Unshown simulations: the text states that "micromagnetic simulations confirm that reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers, whereas stronger coupling localizes the non-collinear state". No such simulations are shown.
   - Unmeasured DMI: the "decisive asymmetry", the larger ratio Deff/Keff in CoFeB, rests on DMI values that were free parameters in the model (D = 1.0 and 1.10 mJ m⁻², Extended Data Table 2) and were never measured. With D differing by only 10%, the conclusion seems to be driven mainly by Keff.
   - What to do:
     - Add a simulated phase map (for example, which sublattice hosts textures, and in which field windows, as a function of J_RKKY and Keff(CoFeB)/Keff(CoB), with D varied over a reasonable range).
     - Measure D in each sublattice, for example with the MFM domain-width method of ref. 48 already available to the authors, or with BLS on reference films. Otherwise present the Deff/Keff argument as a hypothesis.
     - The claims of generality would be much stronger with a single experimental control stack, for example with different Ru thickness or with the anisotropy asymmetry reversed. Without one, they should be worded as predictions.

4. The micromagnetic model departs from the real stack in ways that affect the specific claims made from it (Methods "Micromagnetic modelling", Fig. 4).
   - Stacking order:
     - In the sample (Fig. 1a, Methods), each repeat is Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB from the bottom, so the topmost magnetic layer is CoB and CoFeB is the bottom one.
     - The simulated stack (Fig. 4e) has CoFeB on top within each repeat. The Results section discusses the "topmost CoFeB layer ... with vacuum above" as the origin of a size deviation, but that layer does not exist in the sample.
     - This matters for MFM, which senses the top of the stack, where the real sample has the CoB layer claimed to be uniform.
   - Ms and thickness asymmetry removed: CoB is simulated with the Ms and thickness of CoFeB (0.81 MA m⁻¹, 0.6 nm, against the measured 0.63 MA m⁻¹ and 0.869 nm), so the dipolar asymmetry between the sublattices is removed. Simulated Ms·t (about 4.9 × 10⁻⁴ A) is also below the measured 5.4 × 10⁻⁴ A.
   - Field history: states are relaxed from random magnetization at 0 K, not reached by the experimental field sweep. Yet the RPM data (Fig. 5) show that the history matters.
   - Box size: the 400 × 400 nm box holds only a few 50–80 nm skyrmions, which limits comparison of maze periods and densities.
   - What to do:
     - Rerun the key states with the experimental stacking order and the real Ms and thickness of CoB, or justify why the swap is immaterial.
     - Simulate a field sweep from saturation.
     - State the boundary conditions and how the 1.5 nm Ru/Pt spacers enter the dipolar calculation.
     - Compare simulated and measured diameters quantitatively.

5. The RKKY field, the quantity at the centre of the mechanism, needs a consistent estimate (Methods, Extended Data Table 1).
   - Formula: J_RKKY = μ0Hs·Ms·t/2 is the bilayer spin-flop expression. Interior layers of a 20-layer stack couple to two neighbours, which gives μ0Hs·Ms·t/4.
   - Saturation field: in a PMA multilayer that reverses through domains, the saturation field is not the spin-flop field. In addition, Fig. 1c appears to saturate nearer 150 mT than the quoted 125 mT.
   - Field scale: with the quoted J = −0.034 mJ m⁻², the RKKY field on an interior CoFeB layer is about 2|J|/(Ms·t) ≈ 125 mT. With the multilayer formula it is about 63 mT. Only the second lies near the maze-state field (about 80 mT), which in the effective-field picture should mark zero effective field.
   - What to do:
     - Clarify the formula and the definition of Hs, and give an uncertainty that includes the model choice.
     - Test the mechanism quantitatively: plot the skyrmion diameters of both families (Supplementary Note 3) and the boundaries between states against the effective field H − H_RKKY. If the picture is right, the two families should collapse onto one curve, mirrored about zero effective field.

6. The placement relative to prior work and the "unconventional" framing need sharpening (Introduction, Discussion).
   - Prior work: FM-phase bubbles and stripes embedded in an AF background, and the reverse, were reported in AF-coupled [Co/Pt]/Ru multilayers (refs. 30–32), and the authors' own PRB (ref. 33) discusses AFM–FM phase coexistence in a SAF. The paper should state plainly what is new beyond those works. Is it confinement to one sublattice, Néel chirality, or the dual polarity?
   - Framing: in the effective-field frame, both families are ordinary skyrmions in CoFeB with cores antiparallel to the local effective field. "Inverse polarity" is defined relative to the external field only. Saying this explicitly would help readers.
   - Missing evidence in the main text: it contains no evidence that the inverse-polarity textures are chiral skyrmions rather than dipolar bubbles (the Néel character is in Supplementary Note 4). A short main-text statement of the Fresnel tilt result for each family, including the inverse-polarity textures in the maze/SAF background, is warranted, given that the title and abstract stress topological protection.

7. The sublattice Ms values are not independent measurements (Methods, Extended Data Table 1).
   - What is wrong: Ms for CoB and CoFeB is obtained by assuming that each sublattice carries "identical magnetic moment of 8.85 × 10⁻⁸ A m²", that is, by assuming compensation. The paper then cites the equal Ms·t as evidence of compensation.
   - A related gap: Keff comes from in-plane VSM on "separate CoB and CoFeB reference multilayers", whose structure (spacers, interlayer coupling, number of repeats) is not described.
   - What to do: report Ms measured directly on the single-material reference multilayers and describe those references. Agreement with the values in Extended Data Table 1 would turn the compensation into a measured result.

C. Minor

8. Discussion, paragraph on the zero-field state: "at zero external field, the strong AFM RKKY interaction saturates the CoFeB layers in the up state". Fig. 6 and the preceding sentences (CoFeB background down, inverse skyrmions with up cores) imply that CoFeB is down at zero field on the descending branch. Please correct the text.

9. Eq. 4: with J_RKKY < 0 denoting AFM coupling, H = −J_RKKY/(Ms·t)·m_j points along m_j and so favours parallel alignment. Please check the sign convention, and the missing μ0 in the normalised field.

10. The field values differ between techniques: MFM at +120, +80 and +45 mT; DPC at −131, −89 and −49 mT; simulations at −130, −80 and −50 mT. The text also quotes −130 mT for DPC where the caption gives −131 mT.
    - The 17° tilt adds an in-plane field of about 0.3H, roughly 40 mT for the conventional state. Comment on whether this shifts the state windows.
    - Give the tilt-angle uncertainty. A 1° error changes the 1/tan θ conversion by about 6% and should enter the 12.8 ± 0.7 Tnm error.

11. The Fig. 5 caption says that data points are "individual RPM measurements" but carry ±s.d. error bars "calculated from n = 9 measurements for each legend entry". Please clarify what each error bar represents.

12. The spacer is repeatedly called "the 0.6 nm ruthenium spacer", but the coupling acts across a 0.6 nm Ru / 0.9 nm Pt composite. The claim that 6 Å of Ru "maximize[s] the AFM interlayer coupling" should be justified for this composite spacer, with a reference or the authors' calibration.

13. Please state the measurement temperature for MFM, XMCD and LTEM. Please also show, for example in the SI, hysteresis loops of the membrane samples (25 nm and 200 nm Si3N4) next to the Si/SiO2 SQUID loop. The XMCD decomposition is matched to a SQUID loop measured on a different substrate.

14. MFM tip perturbation: CoFeB has low Keff (0.11 MJ m⁻³) and so may be susceptible to the tip. A brief statement would help, for example on the tip moment, or that maps at a different tip–sample distance or with a repeated scan show no changes.

15. The XMCD analysis assumes that the transmitted intensity is linear in m (Fig. 3 caption). A sentence on the size of the dichroic modulation that justifies linearisation would help. The Fig. 3c caption refers to SQUID data "(green)", which should match the legend colour.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Spatially resolved element-specific evidence (dual-edge SAXS intensity ratios, or STXM/ptychography at the Fe and Co L3 edges) would remove the remaining indirectness of the layer attribution.
- Present the data in effective-field coordinates (H − H_RKKY): skyrmion diameter, density and state boundaries for both families on one plot. This would turn the qualitative mechanism into a quantitative test.
- Add a simulated phase map over J_RKKY, the Keff ratio and D, ideally with one experimental control stack, so that the "general materials-design principle" claims are supported rather than asserted.
- Bring the Néel-chirality evidence for both families (Supplementary Note 4) and the RPM definition (Supplementary Note 5) into the main text or Methods.

5. Verdict

Verdict: minor revision
