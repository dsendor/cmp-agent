Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors build a compensated synthetic antiferromagnet (SAF) from two chemically different ferromagnets, [Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB/Ru]×10. Using MFM, resonant SAXS and Lorentz DPC-STEM, they report two families of Néel skyrmions with opposite core polarity in different field windows: conventional-polarity skyrmions near +120 mT and inverse-polarity skyrmions near +45 mT. Element-resolved XMCD loops and micromagnetics are used to argue that all textures sit in the CoFeB sublattice. The CoB sublattice stays uniform and acts on CoFeB as an RKKY effective field whose competition with the external field sets the polarity. Layer-selected texture formation with a field-programmable polarity is a clear and useful step beyond mirrored SAF skyrmion tubes and beyond the symmetric AFM/FM phase coexistence reported earlier in [Co/Pt]/Ru-type SAFs. The design principle is attractive for 3D spin-texture engineering.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The approach is genuinely correlative. Real-space MFM, reciprocal-space SAXS and quantitative DPC tell a consistent story about the field sequence: FM saturation → conventional skyrmions → maze → inverse skyrmions → SAF state (Fig. 2).
- Element-specific transmission XMCD loops (Fig. 3) give direct sublattice-resolved information. The Fe-edge loop, which is CoFeB-only, shows the gradual non-hysteretic change in the field window where textures are imaged.
- The quantitative DPC value (12.8 ± 0.7 Tnm against 13.6 ± 0.2 Tnm expected for full reversal of one sublattice) is a strong semi-independent check that the textures involve one sublattice's worth of moment, not both.
- The return-point-memory experiments (Fig. 5) add a non-trivial observation: the two families nucleate at uncorrelated sites, while each family reproducibly re-nucleates at the same sites from different paths.
- The micromagnetics reproduce the qualitative sequence and the confinement to CoFeB with largely measured parameters (Extended Data Tables 1 and 2).
- The effective-field picture (Fig. 6) is simple, physically transparent and suggests clear design rules.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The central claims are supported at the level a referee at this journal would normally accept. The items below would make the sublattice assignment and the mechanism more quantitative and better bounded.

B. Should address

1. The claim that CoB stays laterally uniform rests on a subtraction with one empirically tuned parameter. This needs a sensitivity analysis and an upper bound (Fig. 3, Eq. 2, Methods "Soft X-ray techniques").
   - What the paper does: the Co-edge loop contains both sublattices. The isolated CoB loop I_CoB = I_Co − a·I_Fe (Fig. 3d) uses a = 0.35, "determined empirically by adjusting a until the reconstructed total loop matched the SQUID-VSM loop, with a closed central area."
   - What the other evidence can and cannot show: DPC cannot tell which sublattice hosts the textures, because M_s·t is identical for CoB and CoFeB by design (Extended Data Table 1). DPC also cannot distinguish "one sublattice fully textured" from "both sublattices partially textured with the same net Δm". MFM and SAXS are not element-specific either.
   - Why it matters: the statement "all textures reside solely in the CoFeB layers" (abstract) therefore hinges on the flatness of Fig. 3d at |μ0H| > 30 mT. In principle that flatness is a non-trivial test: a is fixed by compensation of the central plateau, and the high-field slope of I_CoB is a second, independent condition. But the paper does not present it that way.
   - Requested:
     - (i) Show I_CoB for a range of a, for example 0.25 to 0.45, and state how well a is constrained.
     - (ii) Compare a = 0.35 with the value expected from the Co and Fe content of each layer (Co in CoFeB against Co in CoB, about 0.45:1 in atoms per area from the XRR thicknesses and compositions) and from tabulated L3 XMCD contrasts.
     - (iii) Quantify the residual slope of I_CoB between 30 and 160 mT as an upper bound on the fraction of CoB area that is reversed in the texture window.
   - If resonant SAXS was recorded at both the Fe and the Co L3 edges, compare the scattered intensity of the texture peak at the two edges. That would give an element-resolved real-space check independent of a. The Methods do not say which edge was used for the SAXS patterns in Fig. 2f–j; this should be stated.

2. The effective-field mechanism is shown only qualitatively; a quantitative test with the reference samples is missing (Discussion, Fig. 6, Methods J_RKKY).
   - The picture predicts that CoFeB behaves like an isolated CoFeB multilayer in an effective field H − H_RKKY, with μ0H_RKKY ≈ 125 mT from the J_RKKY extraction.
   - On that basis the CoFeB effective field crosses zero near +125 mT. Yet the maze state, where a conventional multilayer has its zero-effective-field configuration, is observed at +80 mT. The conventional and inverse windows (+120 and +45 mT) are also not symmetric about 125 mT.
   - The authors already have separate CoB and CoFeB reference multilayers (used for K_eff in Extended Data Table 1). Requested:
     - Show their out-of-plane loops, and ideally MFM, at matched conditions.
     - Plot the Fe-edge loop of Fig. 3b against H − H_RKKY on top of the CoFeB reference loop.
     - Show whether a CoB-only multilayer hosts textures in some field window. If it does, the confinement to CoFeB is a genuine RKKY/anisotropy-selection effect. If it does not, the CoB result is less surprising.
   - This is the natural null control for "texture formation is confined to a single sublattice by design". It would also show whether dipolar fields from the uniform CoB layers, or the demagnetizing field of the net moment, shift the effective-field centre away from H_RKKY.

3. The correlation across techniques combines different substrates, field signs and tilt geometries without showing that the magnetic response is the same.
   - SQUID and MFM are on oxidized Si, Lorentz TEM on 25-nm Si3N4 membranes, and soft X-ray work on 200-nm Si3N4 membranes (Methods).
   - The DPC states are at negative fields (−131, −89, −49 mT) with a 17° tilt; MFM states are at positive fields (+120, +80, +45 mT).
   - The reconstructed XMCD total loop (membrane) is matched to the SQUID loop (Si substrate) by construction through a. Comparing the transition fields across substrates is therefore not an independent check.
   - Requested:
     - Report the transition fields (plateau edge, onset of textures, saturation) for each substrate type, for example from XMCD on the membranes against SQUID and MFM on Si.
     - State the number of samples and areas imaged per technique.
     - Indicate whether the two families appear in more than one sample or membrane.

4. The RPM cross-type result needs a random-placement baseline and a clearer metric (Fig. 5c, Supplementary Note 5).
   - What is shown: the cross-type RPM of 0.03–0.05 is interpreted as "distinct pinning/formation mechanisms: external-field-driven for conventional-polarity skyrmions and RKKY-driven for inverse-polarity skyrmions".
   - Why a baseline is needed: the two families are imaged at different fields (+120 and +40 mT), with different densities and opposite MFM contrast. The overlap expected for randomly placed skyrmions at these densities is probably of order the areal coverage, plausibly 0.1–0.3. A value of 0.03 could then indicate anticorrelation, for example inverse skyrmions forming between the former conventional cores, rather than independence.
   - Requested:
     - State how overlap is defined between objects of opposite polarity.
     - Give the random-placement (or shuffled-map) expectation.
     - Report the number of skyrmions and the field of view behind each RPM value. The n = 9 error bars reflect cycle-to-cycle variation in one area, not sample-to-sample spread.
   - The mechanistic labels (external-field-driven against RKKY-driven) are an interpretation, not a direct result of the RPM data, and should be phrased accordingly.

5. The micromagnetic comparison is qualitative, and several modelling choices affect the very asymmetry being explained (Methods, Extended Data Table 2, Fig. 4).
   - The CoB layer is modelled with CoFeB's thickness and M_s (0.81 MA m⁻¹ rather than 0.63), with K_u rescaled to keep K_eff. This removes the thickness asymmetry that the Discussion invokes (D_eff = D/t and the D_eff/K_eff ratio) as part of the design principle.
   - D is a free parameter (1.0 and 1.1 mJ m⁻² for CoB and CoFeB) without independent measurement.
   - States are obtained by field-cooling from random states at each field at T = 0, rather than by following the field history, even though Fig. 5 shows the nucleation path matters.
   - Requested:
     - Give a quantitative comparison with experiment: skyrmion diameters and the field windows of each state, simulated against measured.
     - Show the sensitivity of single-sublattice confinement to D in CoB and to the CoB thickness and M_s treatment.
     - Show the stated result that "reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers", or point to where it is shown. It is not in the main text or Extended Data.

6. The generality of the design principle is asserted on one stack.
   - The Discussion and Outlook claim "a general materials-design principle" based on one composition and one Ru thickness.
   - The role of DMI asymmetry ("comparable D_eff in both layers; the decisive asymmetry therefore arises from the enhanced ratio D_eff/K_eff in CoFeB") rests on D values that were not measured.
   - Requested: either show a second stack, for example a different Ru thickness (and hence J_RKKY) or a different CoFeB/CoB thickness ratio, in which the field windows shift as the effective-field picture predicts; or temper the generality claims and present the D asymmetry as an assumption.

C. Minor

7. The simulated stack order appears inverted relative to the grown stack.
   - The grown stack per Fig. 1a and Methods is Ta/[Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]×10/Ru/Pt, so the topmost FM layer is CoB and the bottom one is CoFeB.
   - Fig. 4e shows Pt/CoB/Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru per repeat, with CoFeB on top.
   - The text discusses "the very top CoFeB layer … coupled to CoB on one side only (below), with vacuum above", which does not correspond to the experimental structure; in the experiment the single-side-coupled CoFeB layer is the bottom one.
   - Please reconcile, and state whether this matters for the predicted depth profile.

8. J_RKKY is extracted with μ0H_s = 125 mT, but the SQUID loop (Fig. 1c), the Fe loop (Fig. 3b) and the FM state in MFM (+150 mT) suggest saturation nearer 150–160 mT. Please define how H_s was taken, for example by extrapolation. The interior-layer formula J = μ0H_s·M_s·t/2 also neglects the domain and dipolar contributions that dominate the approach to saturation in exactly this texture regime; please comment on the resulting uncertainty in J_RKKY.

9. The Lorentz images are taken at 17° tilt, and the quoted fields are the out-of-plane component. The corresponding in-plane component (about 40 mT at −131 mT) can distort Néel textures and shift transition fields. Please state the total and in-plane field for each DPC state and comment on its effect.

10. The diameters differ substantially between techniques: 81 ± 4 nm (MFM) against 58 ± 6 nm (DPC) for inverse-polarity skyrmions, and 64 ± 4 against 48 ± 5 nm for conventional ones. The fields differ as well (+120 against −131 mT). Please summarize in the main text the main reasons: tip convolution, field mismatch, tilt projection over the roughly 45-nm stack thickness (about 14 nm lateral smearing), and different substrates.

11. The relation to earlier work on field-driven AFM/FM phase coexistence in perpendicular SAFs (refs. 30–33, including the authors' own ref. 33) deserves a more explicit discussion: FM-aligned domains in an AFM background, and the reverse. In particular, how do the "inverse-polarity skyrmions" differ from the FM bubble domains in an AFM matrix reported there, beyond the deterministic choice of sublattice? This would sharpen the "unconventional" framing in the title.

12. Small consistency items:
    - Extended Data Table 2 gives J_RKKY = −0.03 mJ m⁻² against −0.034 measured, and K_u(CoFeB) = 0.52 against 0.53 MJ m⁻³. State whether these are deliberate roundings.
    - The CoB M_s of 0.81 MA m⁻¹ in Table 2 should be flagged as a modelling substitution, not a measured value.
    - The Fig. 3 caption should give a = 0.35 and state how the polarity of the inverted diode current was fixed.
    - The linear relation between transmitted intensity and m (Fig. 3 caption) holds only for small dichroic contrast; please give the contrast level.
    - Comment briefly on possible MFM tip perturbation of nucleation sites in the RPM experiments: low-moment tip, about 20 nm lift height.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Turn the XMCD decomposition into a quantitative bound: an a-sensitivity plot, an a value derived from composition, and an upper limit on the fraction of CoB that is reversed. If possible, add Fe- against Co-edge SAXS intensity as an independent element-resolved check.
- Use the existing CoB-only and CoFeB-only reference multilayers as null and reference controls, and test the effective-field picture quantitatively by mapping the CoFeB sublattice loop onto the reference loop shifted by H_RKKY.
- Add a random baseline to the cross-type RPM analysis, and soften the mechanistic labels unless they are tested directly.
- Fix the simulated stack order, show the J_RKKY- and D-dependence simulations in Extended Data, and compare simulated and measured sizes and field windows quantitatively.

5. Verdict

Verdict: minor revision
