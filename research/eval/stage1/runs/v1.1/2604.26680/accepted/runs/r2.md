Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors build a moment-compensated synthetic antiferromagnet (SAF) from two chemically different ferromagnets, Co68B32 and Co40Fe40B20, with equal Ms·t but different anisotropy. They report two skyrmion families of opposite core polarity in separate field windows of a single sweep: conventional-polarity skyrmions near +120 mT and inverse-polarity skyrmions near +45 mT. Using element-resolved XMCD, quantitative DPC and micromagnetics, they attribute all textures to the CoFeB sublattice and explain the polarity reversal as a sign change of the effective field (external field plus RKKY field) acting on that sublattice. Phase coexistence in PMA SAFs has been seen before, including by these authors (refs 33, 35). What is new here is the sublattice selectivity and the layer-selected polarity of the skyrmions. The correlative, multi-technique approach is a strength.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Two skyrmion families of opposite polarity are imaged consistently by three independent probes (MFM, resonant SAXS and Lorentz DPC) across a full field sweep (Fig. 2).
- The Fe-edge XMCD loop (Fig. 3b) probes only CoFeB. It shows the gradual, texture-related change of magnetization, which directly ties the textures to CoFeB without any modelling.
- The DPC quantification (Fig. 2n) gives 12.8 ± 0.7 T nm against an expected 13.6 ± 0.2 T nm for full reversal of one sublattice. This is a nice quantitative check that only one sublattice reverses locally.
- The material parameters are characterized carefully: XRR thicknesses, Ms from SQUID-VSM, and Keff from reference multilayers (Extended Data Table 1). The micromagnetic parameters follow from them, and I checked that the quoted Keff, Ku and Ms values are internally consistent.
- The RPM experiments add a statistical view of nucleation that goes beyond static imaging.
- The design concept, an asymmetric sublattice energy hierarchy under global moment compensation, is clearly stated and relevant to the field.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

None. The main experimental claims are supported by several mutually consistent probes. The items below would make the sublattice assignment and the mechanism more secure, and would bring the generality claims into line with the evidence.

B. Should address

1. **The claim that CoB stays uniform rests on an empirical, unchecked subtraction.**
   - Where: Fig. 3, Eqs. (1)–(2), and Methods ("Soft X-ray techniques"). The scaling factor a = 0.35 was "determined empirically by adjusting a until the reconstructed total loop matched the SQUID-VSM loop".
   - What the reconstruction assumes:
     - The Co-edge signal per unit CoB moment equals the Fe-edge signal per unit CoFeB moment. This is implicit in using I_Fe + (I_Co − a·I_Fe) as the total.
     - Transmitted intensity is linear in m, although Beer–Lambert attenuation is exponential.
     - The SQUID loop, measured on a sample grown on a Si/SiO2 substrate, can calibrate an XMCD loop measured on a sister sample grown on a Si3N4 membrane.
   - Why it matters: The square CoB loop in Fig. 3d is the main experimental support for "the CoB sublattice remains laterally uniform" and for "all textures reside solely in the CoFeB layers". For many values of a, the flatness of I_CoB above ±30 mT depends directly on how the Fe slope is subtracted. The paper does not show how robust the square loop is.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Estimate a independently, from the Co content of each layer (0.40 × 7 Å in CoFeB against 0.68 × 9 Å in CoB) and the edge cross-sections.
     - Show I_CoB(H) for a range of a around 0.35.
     - Use the noise in Fig. 3d to place a quantitative upper bound on the fraction of CoB that could be reversed in the textured states.
   - A more direct test, if the data exist: compare the resonant SAXS intensity of the textured states at the Fe and Co L3 edges. The ratio should match a CoFeB-only origin.
   - Unless such a bound is given, "unequivocally" (Introduction) and "solely" (Abstract) should be softened. The authors' own simulations show "small perturbations" in CoB near CoFeB walls.

2. **The DPC quantification cannot identify which sublattice hosts the textures, and its zero reference partly depends on the XMCD.**
   - The expected 13.6 T nm equals 2 × 10 × μ0·Ms·t for CoFeB. Because Ms·t is identical for CoB and CoFeB by design, textures confined to CoB would give exactly the same value. DPC therefore shows that one sublattice reverses but not which one.
   - The Methods also state that the DPC zero reference "is set by using assumptions informed primarily by the XMCD I(H) loops". Even the difference measurement is therefore not fully independent of point 1.
   - The text ("the DPC results suggest that the CoFeB and CoB sublattices exhibit two different magnetization states") should say this plainly. A short table would help: the expected integrated-induction contrast for (i) textures in CoFeB only, (ii) textures in CoB only, and (iii) AFM-aligned tubes running through both sublattices. Readers could then see what DPC does and does not rule out.
   - The 17° tilt also adds an in-plane field of about 0.3 μ0H, roughly 40 mT at −131 mT. In a SAF this could cant the magnetization or distort the textures. The authors should comment on this, and on why DPC states on the negative branch at −131, −89 and −49 mT are equivalent to the MFM states at +120, +80 and +45 mT.

3. **The J_RKKY estimate and the effective-field picture are not quantitative.**
   - Where: Methods. J_RKKY = μ0·Hs·Ms·t_FM/2, with μ0Hs = 125 mT.
   - Problem 1, the formula: this is the bilayer expression. In a 20-layer stack, every interior magnetic layer is coupled on both sides, so the expression overestimates |J| by up to a factor of two.
   - Problem 2, the saturation field: in a strongly PMA SAF that forms domains, the out-of-plane saturation field marks where the last reversed domains disappear. Domain-wall and dipolar energies affect it, not only the interlayer exchange.
   - Why it matters: |J| sets the RKKY field acting on CoFeB, which is the central quantity in the proposed mechanism. With the quoted J and two CoB neighbours, μ0H_RKKY = 2|J|/(Ms·t) ≈ 125 mT. The effective field H − H_RKKY on CoFeB would then be about −5, −45 and −80 mT at the fields where conventional skyrmions (120 mT), mazes (80 mT) and inverse skyrmions (45 mT) appear. The maze state would not sit near zero effective field, as the Discussion implies. With |J| halved, the windows fit the picture much better.
   - What the authors should do:
     - Give an explicit estimate of H_eff(H) for CoFeB and place the observed windows on it.
     - Justify or correct the J formula, ideally checking it against minor loops or a CoB-only reference SAF.
     - Show the simulation statement "reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers" in the paper. A J–H state diagram in Extended Data would directly support the design-principle claim.

4. **The simulated stack departs from the real stack in ways that bear on the conclusions.**
   - Differences between the model and the sample:
     - CoB is modelled with Ms = 0.81 MA m⁻¹ and 6 Å thickness, against the measured 0.63 MA m⁻¹ and 8.69 Å, with Ku rescaled to match Keff.
     - D is a free parameter, set to 1.00 mJ m⁻² for CoB and 1.10 mJ m⁻² for CoFeB.
     - The layer order is reversed. Fig. 4e has CoB at the bottom of each repeat and CoFeB at the top of the stack. Fig. 1a and the Methods put CoFeB at the bottom of each repeat and CoB as the topmost magnetic layer.
   - Consequences:
     - The discussion of the "topmost CoFeB layer" with "vacuum above" does not describe the measured sample. In the sample, the CoFeB layer coupled on one side only is the bottom one, next to the Ta/Ru/Pt seed.
     - The authors state that D_eff/K_eff is "the decisive asymmetry". Because D is free, they should show that the CoFeB-only localization survives D varied within its literature range and with the measured CoB Ms and thickness.
   - Also state:
     - whether the states in Fig. 4 come from independent relaxations at each field or from a simulated field sweep, since the experiment is history-dependent (Fig. 5a);
     - whether the 400 nm × 400 nm box uses periodic boundaries, given that it holds only a few skyrmions.

5. **The RPM analysis needs a baseline and other interpretations should be considered.**
   - Where: Fig. 5 and the RPM section.
   - A baseline is missing: a cross-type RPM of 0.03–0.05 is interpreted as uncorrelated nucleation sites. The value expected for two random arrangements at the measured skyrmion densities is not given. At the high density of Fig. 2b, chance overlap may be well above 0.05, in which case the low value would mean anti-correlation, which would be a stronger and different statement.
   - Method details are missing:
     - how the opposite MFM contrast of the two families is handled when the maps are overlaid;
     - how many skyrmions each map contains.
   - Possible tip effects: the Co-coated tip in this low-Keff, compensated system might move or nucleate textures. Repeat scans at the same field, or a different tip moment or lift height, would exclude this.
   - The interpretation is a leap: going from "distinct pinning sites" to "external-field-driven" against "RKKY-driven nucleation" is an inference. Opposite-polarity cores in a background of opposite sign could simply prefer different features of the same defect landscape. The authors should either add a test that separates the two readings or present the mechanism as an interpretation.

6. **The generality claim rests on one composition, and cross-sample consistency is not shown.**
   - The Discussion and Outlook say the mechanism "reflects a general materials-design principle rather than a peculiarity of the present stack". Yet only one stack composition is measured.
   - The measurements use sister samples on different substrates: Si/SiO2 for XRR, SQUID and MFM, and Si3N4 membranes for LTEM and XMCD. No evidence is given that their loops agree, for example a SQUID or XMCD total loop of the membrane sample against Fig. 1c.
   - What would help:
     - A comparison with a chemically symmetric reference SAF measured under the same conditions, such as the Co68B32 SAFs of refs 33 and 35. The two-family behaviour should be absent there.
     - Or a second asymmetric stack with a different Keff contrast.
   - Without one of these, the generality should be supported by simulation only and worded that way.

C. Minor

7. **Sign error in the Discussion.** It states "at zero external field, the strong AFM RKKY interaction saturates the CoFeB layers in the up state". On the descending branch CoB stays up down to −30 mT (Fig. 3d), so AFM-coupled CoFeB should be down at zero field. This also matches the inverse-polarity skyrmions having up cores in a down background. Please check and correct.

8. **The spacer description is inconsistent.** The text repeatedly refers to "the 0.6 nm ruthenium spacer". In fact adjacent ferromagnets are separated by Ru(6 Å)/Pt(9 Å). The Methods say 6 Å Ru was chosen "to maximize the AFM interlayer coupling", but the Pt insertion strongly reduces it, consistent with the small |J|. Please describe the composite spacer.

9. **Measurement temperatures are not stated.** No temperature is given for MFM, XMCD (with an in-vacuum cryocooled magnet), SAXS or LTEM, and the simulations are at 0 K. Since Keff and J depend on temperature, please state the sample temperature for each technique.

10. **Skyrmion sizes are compared across mismatched conditions.** The sizes are compared between MFM (+120 and +45 mT, Si/SiO2 substrate) and DPC (−131 and −49 mT, membrane), with tip convolution affecting the MFM values. Please add the simulated diameters at matched fields. The field dependence of size, which the authors say has opposite trends in the two families, would be worth a main-text or Extended Data figure, because it is a direct signature of the effective-field picture.

11. **SAXS conditions are missing for Fig. 2f–j.** Please give the photon energy (Fe or Co L3) and helicity. The crescent in Fig. 2h should also be explained, for example by stripe alignment or drift.

12. **The Néel check is not tied to a family.** Néel character is verified by tilted Fresnel imaging (Supplementary Note 4), but the paper does not say for which family. Please confirm both families have the chirality expected for the fixed DMI sign. This is a simple consistency check on the claim that both reside in the same CoFeB layers.

13. **DMI units are mixed.** The Discussion uses D_eff = D/t, while Extended Data Table 2 gives D in mJ m⁻² (already an effective continuum value) and Eq. (5) uses D directly. Please make the notation and the D_eff/K_eff values explicit, with numbers.

14. **Small table and caption issues.**
    - The Extended Data Table 1 caption says Ku was obtained using "the corresponding saturation magnetisation Ms values of the full CoB/CoFeB multilayer stack". It is unclear how sublattice-specific Ms values come from the full stack. Please clarify.
    - The Fig. 3 caption states "transmission intensity I(H) is linearly related to m(H)". Please state the conditions under which this holds.

15. **The DPC line-profile difference lacks error analysis.** The 3.9 ± 0.2 T nm difference in Fig. 2n and the tilt correction by tan θ need a description of how the error bar was obtained, including the uncertainty in θ and the number of profiles averaged.

4. Suggestions for improvement

- Image one textured state element-resolved, for example by STXM or by SAXS at both the Fe and Co L3 edges on the same membrane. This would establish CoFeB-only localization directly and settle points 1 and 2.
- Plot the observed field windows against the estimated effective field on CoFeB, H − H_RKKY, with a corrected J estimate. Add a simulated J–D–H state diagram showing where the textures move into both sublattices.
- Measure, or at least simulate with the real parameters, a control stack: a symmetric SAF, or an asymmetric SAF with reversed stacking order. This would back the "general design principle" claim.
- Add a random-placement baseline and per-map skyrmion counts to the RPM analysis, so that the cross-type RPM values can be interpreted quantitatively.

5. Verdict

Verdict: minor revision
