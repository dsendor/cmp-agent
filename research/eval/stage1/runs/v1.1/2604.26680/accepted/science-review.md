B. Should address

1. The claim that the CoB sublattice stays laterally uniform, and that "all textures reside solely in the CoFeB layers" (Abstract; "unequivocally" in the Introduction), rests on a loop decomposition with an empirically tuned parameter (Fig. 3, Eqs. 1–2, Methods "Soft X-ray techniques"). The isolated CoB loop I_CoB = I_Co − a·I_Fe (Fig. 3d) uses a = 0.35, "determined empirically by adjusting a until the reconstructed total loop matched the SQUID-VSM loop, with a closed central area". The reconstruction also assumes that the Co-edge signal per unit CoB moment equals the Fe-edge signal per unit CoFeB moment (implicit in using I_Fe + (I_Co − a·I_Fe) as the total), that transmitted intensity is linear in m, and that a SQUID loop measured on a Si/SiO2 sample (and "rescaled") can calibrate an XMCD loop from a sister sample on a Si3N4 membrane. The decomposition therefore has several adjustable degrees of freedom, and a scenario in which part of the reversal occurs in CoB, scaling with the CoFeB reversal, would be partly absorbed into a. The flatness of I_CoB above ±30 mT is in principle a non-trivial second test (a is fixed by compensation of the central plateau, the high-field slope is an independent condition), but the paper does not present it that way, and the authors' own simulations show "small perturbations" in CoB near CoFeB walls. The authors should: (i) show I_CoB(H) for a range of a (for example 0.25–0.45) and state how well a is constrained; (ii) compare a = 0.35 with an independent estimate from the Co content of each layer (0.40 × 7 Å in CoFeB against 0.68 × 9 Å in CoB, about 0.45:1 in atoms per area) and tabulated L3 XMCD cross-sections; (iii) use the residual slope and noise of I_CoB between 30 and 160 mT to place a quantitative upper bound on the fraction of CoB area reversed in the texture window; (iv) make the moment-accounting argument explicit — if the Fe loop shows full CoFeB reversal between saturation and the plateau (outer-branch change equal to the jump at ±30 mT), compensation alone requires the average CoB moment to stay saturated. They should also state at which edge (and helicity) the SAXS patterns in Fig. 2f–j were taken; if data exist at both the Fe and Co L3 edges, the ratio of texture-peak scattering intensities, compared with the XMCD ratio, would give a direct, spatially resolved check independent of a. Unless such a bound is given, "unequivocally" and "solely" should be softened.

2. The DPC quantification (Fig. 2n) cannot identify which sublattice hosts the textures, and its zero reference partly depends on the XMCD. The expected 13.6 Tnm equals 2 × 10 × μ0·Ms·t for CoFeB, but because Ms·t is identical for CoB and CoFeB by design (Extended Data Table 1), textures confined to CoB would give exactly the same value; DPC also cannot distinguish one sublattice fully textured from both partially textured with the same net Δm. The Methods further state that the DPC zero reference "is set by using assumptions informed primarily by the XMCD I(H) loops", so even the difference measurement is not fully independent of concern 1. The text ("the DPC results suggest that the CoFeB and CoB sublattices exhibit two different magnetization states") should say plainly that DPC shows one sublattice's worth of moment reversing but not which one. A short table of expected integrated-induction contrast for (i) textures in CoFeB only, (ii) textures in CoB only and (iii) AFM-aligned tubes through both sublattices would let readers see what DPC does and does not rule out.

3. The RKKY field, the central quantity of the effective-field mechanism, is not estimated consistently, and the mechanism is not tested quantitatively (Methods, Extended Data Table 1, Discussion, Fig. 6). J_RKKY = μ0Hs·Ms·t/2 is the bilayer spin-flop expression; interior layers of a 20-layer stack couple to two neighbours, giving μ0Hs·Ms·t/4, so |J| may be overestimated by up to a factor of two. In a PMA SAF that reverses through domains, the out-of-plane saturation field is set also by domain-wall and dipolar energies, not only by interlayer exchange; and Fig. 1c, the Fe loop (Fig. 3b) and the FM state in MFM (+150 mT) suggest saturation nearer 150–160 mT than the quoted 125 mT. With the quoted J = −0.034 mJ m⁻² and two CoB neighbours, μ0H_RKKY = 2|J|/(Ms·t) ≈ 125 mT, so the CoFeB effective field H − H_RKKY would be about −5, −45 and −80 mT where conventional skyrmions (+120 mT), mazes (+80 mT) and inverse skyrmions (+45 mT) appear: the maze would not sit near zero effective field as the Discussion implies, and the conventional and inverse windows are not symmetric about it. With |J| halved (H_RKKY ≈ 63 mT) the windows fit the picture much better. The authors should define how Hs was taken, justify or correct the J formula with an uncertainty that includes the model choice (ideally checked against minor loops or a CoB-only reference SAF), give an explicit H_eff(H) for CoFeB and place the observed windows on it. A direct test is available: plot skyrmion diameters of both families (Supplementary Note 3) and the state boundaries against H − H_RKKY — the two families should collapse onto one curve mirrored about zero effective field. The separate CoB and CoFeB reference multilayers already used for K_eff offer a natural control: show their out-of-plane loops (and ideally MFM) at matched conditions, overlay the Fe-edge loop of Fig. 3b plotted against H − H_RKKY on the CoFeB reference loop, and show whether a CoB-only multilayer hosts textures in some field window. This would also reveal whether dipolar fields from the uniform CoB layers or the demagnetizing field shift the effective-field centre away from H_RKKY.

4. The micromagnetic model departs from the real stack in ways that bear on the specific claims made from it (Methods "Micromagnetic modelling", Extended Data Table 2, Fig. 4). (a) Stacking order: the grown stack (Fig. 1a, Methods) is Ta/[Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]×10/Ru/Pt, so the topmost magnetic layer is CoB and the single-side-coupled CoFeB layer is the bottom one, next to the seed; Fig. 4e has CoFeB on top within each repeat. The Results discussion of "the very top CoFeB layer … coupled to CoB on one side only (below), with vacuum above" as the origin of a size deviation therefore describes a layer that does not exist in the sample — and MFM senses the top of the stack, where the real sample has the CoB layer claimed to be uniform. (b) CoB is simulated with CoFeB's Ms and thickness (0.81 MA m⁻¹, 0.6 nm, against the measured 0.63 MA m⁻¹ and 0.869 nm), with Ku rescaled to keep Keff; this removes the dipolar and thickness asymmetry that the Discussion invokes (D_eff = D/t and the D_eff/K_eff ratio), and the simulated Ms·t (about 4.9 × 10⁻⁴ A) falls below the measured 5.4 × 10⁻⁴ A. (c) D is a free parameter (1.00 and 1.10 mJ m⁻² for CoB and CoFeB). (d) States are relaxed from random magnetization at each field at 0 K rather than reached by a field sweep, although Fig. 5 shows the history matters. (e) The 400 × 400 nm box holds only a few 50–80 nm skyrmions, limiting comparison of maze periods and densities. The authors should rerun the key states with the experimental stacking order and the real CoB Ms and thickness (or justify why the substitution is immaterial), show that CoFeB-only confinement survives D varied within its literature range, simulate a field sweep from saturation, state the boundary conditions (periodic or not) and how the 1.5 nm Ru/Pt spacers enter the dipolar calculation, and compare simulated and measured skyrmion diameters and field windows quantitatively.

5. The "general materials-design principle" claims (Discussion and Outlook) are not shown. The text states that "micromagnetic simulations confirm that reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers, whereas stronger coupling localizes the non-collinear state", but no such simulations appear in the main text or Extended Data. The "decisive asymmetry", the larger D_eff/K_eff in CoFeB, rests on unmeasured DMI values that were free model parameters differing by only 10%, so the conclusion seems driven mainly by K_eff. And only one stack composition and one Ru thickness are measured. The authors should add a simulated state diagram (which sublattice hosts textures, and in which field windows, as a function of J_RKKY, K_eff(CoFeB)/K_eff(CoB) and D); measure D in each sublattice (for example with the MFM domain-width method of ref. 48, or BLS on reference films) or present the D_eff/K_eff argument as a hypothesis; and ideally add one experimental control — a chemically symmetric reference SAF measured under the same conditions (such as the Co68B32 SAFs of refs. 33 and 35, where the two-family behaviour should be absent), a different Ru thickness or CoFeB/CoB thickness ratio in which the windows shift as predicted, or reversed anisotropy asymmetry or stacking order. Without one, the generality should be worded as a prediction supported by simulation.

6. The return-point-memory analysis is over-interpreted (Fig. 5, "Return-point memory" section, Supplementary Note 5). The cross-type RPM values of 0.03–0.05 compare maps at different fields (+120 and +40 mT), with different densities and sizes and opposite MFM contrast, and are read as uncorrelated nucleation sites. No baseline is given: for a dense lattice such as Fig. 2b, random overlap should be of order the areal coverage (plausibly 0.1–0.3), in which case 0.03 would indicate anticorrelation (inverse skyrmions forming between former conventional cores), a stronger and different statement. The step from distinct sites to "external-field-driven" versus "RKKY-driven" nucleation is an inference that RPM does not test; opposite-polarity cores in backgrounds of opposite sign could simply prefer different features of the same defect landscape. The authors should give the RPM definition in the main text, including how overlap between objects of opposite contrast is handled; add a randomised-position (shuffled-map) baseline at matched density for both same-type and cross-type comparisons; report the number of skyrmions and the field of view behind each value; clarify the Fig. 5 caption, which calls data points "individual RPM measurements" yet gives ±s.d. error bars "calculated from n = 9 measurements for each legend entry" (these reflect cycle-to-cycle variation in one area, not sample-to-sample spread); and either compare the two families after a field excursion that brings both to a common field, or present the mechanistic labels as interpretation.

7. The sublattice Ms values are not independent measurements (Methods, Extended Data Table 1). Ms for CoB and CoFeB is obtained by assuming each sublattice carries an "identical magnetic moment of 8.85 × 10⁻⁸ A m²", i.e. by assuming compensation, and the equal Ms·t is then cited as evidence of compensation. Relatedly, K_eff comes from in-plane VSM on "separate CoB and CoFeB reference multilayers" whose structure (spacers, interlayer coupling, number of repeats) is not described. The authors should report Ms measured directly on the single-material reference multilayers and describe those references; agreement with Extended Data Table 1 would turn compensation into a measured result.

8. The cross-technique correlation combines different substrates, field signs and geometries without showing that the magnetic response is the same. SQUID and MFM are on oxidized Si, Lorentz TEM on 25 nm Si3N4 membranes and soft X-ray work on 200 nm Si3N4 membranes; DPC states are at negative fields (−131, −89, −49 mT, with the text quoting −130 mT where the caption gives −131 mT) under 17° tilt, MFM states at +120, +80, +45 mT, and simulations at −130, −80, −50 mT. Because the reconstructed XMCD total loop is matched to the SQUID loop by construction through a, comparing transition fields across substrates is not an independent check. The authors should show hysteresis loops of the membrane samples next to the Si/SiO2 SQUID loop (for example in the SI), report the transition fields (plateau edge, texture onset, saturation) for each substrate type, explain why the negative-branch DPC states are equivalent to the positive-branch MFM states, state the number of samples and areas imaged per technique, and indicate whether both families appear in more than one sample or membrane.

9. The placement relative to prior work and the "unconventional" framing need sharpening (Introduction, Discussion, title). FM-phase bubbles and stripes in an AF background, and the reverse, were reported in AF-coupled [Co/Pt]/Ru multilayers (refs. 30–32), and the authors' own ref. 33 discusses AFM–FM phase coexistence in a SAF. The paper should state plainly what is new beyond those works — confinement to one sublattice, Néel chirality, or dual polarity — and in particular how the "inverse-polarity skyrmions" differ from FM bubble domains in an AFM matrix beyond the deterministic choice of sublattice. It would also help to say explicitly that in the effective-field frame both families are ordinary skyrmions in CoFeB with cores antiparallel to the local effective field, and that "inverse polarity" is defined relative to the external field only.
