Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors measure magneto-absorption of the yellow 2p and 3p excitons of Cu2O on one crystal with three pulsed-field techniques (non-destructive pulsed magnet to 60 T, single-turn coil to ~150 T, electromagnetic flux compression to 500 T). They reassign the two strong 2p lines as the spin-Zeeman-split orthoexciton states 2p0^(Sz=±1), extract μ* = 0.415 ± 0.01 m_e from a hydrogen-in-field fit, and report that above ~300 T the 2p0^(Sz=+1) linewidth rises steeply and its energy falls below the hydrogen model. They read this as possible Harper broadening, i.e. a breakdown of the effective-mass approximation once ℓ_B nears the lattice constant. Following a Wannier exciton out to 500 T in a bulk crystal is a technically impressive and new data set. If the lattice-scale interpretation held, it would interest a wide audience. As it stands, the Harper interpretation rests on four or five EMFC linewidth points and an order-of-magnitude length comparison.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Unique data: optical spectroscopy of a bulk Wannier exciton up to 500 T, with the same crystal measured by all three field techniques. Where the regimes overlap, the data agree with each other and with the DC data of Artyukhin et al. (Fig. 2, Fig. S1).
- The 2p0^(Sz=+1) line can be followed continuously from 0 to 500 T (Fig. 1b, Fig. 2), so the field dependence of its energy and width can be tracked over a very wide range.
- The global hydrogen-model fit (Fig. 2, Fig. S3, Table I) is a useful, self-consistent parametrisation of the 2p/3p fan up to ~150–300 T.
- The authors are appropriately cautious in the title ("possible evidence").

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. No quantitative estimate shows that Harper broadening could be observable at 300 T, and the paper's own numbers suggest it would be negligible (pp. 8–9, Fig. 4b). The argument is that at 300 T, ℓ_B ≈ 14.8 Å ≈ 3.5 a0 and α = φ/φ0 ≈ 0.013 (I reproduce this value from a0 = 4.27 Å), and that this is "comparable" to the lattice constant. In Harper/Hofstadter physics, however, the periodic potential broadens each Landau level into subbands whose width falls off roughly exponentially in 1/α for small α. At α ≈ 0.013 the Landau-level bandwidth is expected to be vanishingly small, not meV-scale. The observed extra broadening is large: the FWHM goes from ~2.6× to ~6.8× the 2.9 meV zero-field width, i.e. roughly 10–12 meV of additional width between 300 and 500 T. Several further problems compound this:
   - Harper's model applies to a single charged carrier in a tight-binding band. Cu2O's conduction band (Cu 4s) is broad and nearly free-electron-like.
   - The object here is a neutral exciton at K≈0 whose relative-motion size is set by both ℓ_B and the Coulomb length. The paper does not explain how a Harper spectrum of the relative coordinate would show up as an absorption linewidth.
   - Nothing in the model gives a threshold at 300 T. Harper effects should grow smoothly with α.

   The authors should give at least an order-of-magnitude calculation of the expected broadening at 300–500 T for Cu2O parameters (band widths from Ref. 33, actual a0, fcc Cu sublattice), and show that it can reach ~10 meV. Otherwise the interpretation should be reduced to "an unexplained broadening and energy deviation above 300 T".

2. Competing explanations for the broadening and the energy deviation, including one visible in the authors' own Fig. S3, are not ruled out (pp. 7–9, Figs. 2, 4, S3).
   - (i) Overlap or anticrossing with nearby levels. In Fig. S3(a) the calculated 2p_(−1)^(Sz=0) level (grey dashed) approaches 2p0^(Sz=+1) from below over 300–500 T. The measured points at 350–500 T sit between the purple 2p0^(Sz=+1) curve and this grey dashed line, and further levels (2p0^(Sz=0), 2p_(−1)^(Sz=±1)) lie within ~10–20 meV. An unresolved second component, or level mixing at a near-crossing, would at once broaden the apparent line and pull its fitted centre below the hydrogen curve. That is exactly the pair of observations taken as support for Harper broadening.
   - (ii) Phonon-assisted relaxation and magnetopolaron effects. Cu2O has LO phonons at ~19 and ~82 meV. With μ* = 0.415 m_e, ħeB/μ* reaches ~84 meV near 300 T, and the gaps to lower-lying 2p states in Fig. S3 open to tens of meV. Resonant polaron coupling or new decay channels opening could produce both a threshold-like broadening and an energy pinning.
   - (iii) Band nonparabolicity and Γ7+/Γ8+ valence-band mixing, which raise the effective mass at high energy. The authors themselves describe the deviation as "an increase of reduced effective mass".

   The authors should overlay all calculated levels near 2p0^(Sz=+1) on the 300–500 T spectra and fit with the extra components allowed. They should also estimate the LO-phonon resonance fields for the relevant level spacings, and say why each alternative is excluded.

3. Reproducibility and robustness of the linewidth extraction above 300 T are not shown (Figs. 1b, 3, 4).
   - The upturn rests on four or five EMFC points (350–500 T). In Fig. 1(b) the 2p0^(Sz=+1) feature above 300 T is a weak step on a steeply rising background, and the only fit shown (Fig. 3) is at 120 T. Without the band-edge feature at 2.16–2.20 eV, a ~20 meV FWHM at 500 T would be poorly constrained, so the result depends strongly on the "asymmetrical Gaussian" form and the background polynomial.
   - The paper does not say how many EMFC shots were taken, whether the 300–500 T points come from a single shot (destructive technique, one sample), or how the FWHM error bars were obtained.
   - Pulse-specific artefacts are not quantified: the field swept during each streak-camera time slice, and the field inhomogeneity across the 1.8 × 1.8 mm sample inside the imploding liner. Either broadens a line by (dE/dB)·ΔB.

   The authors should:
   - show fits, with backgrounds and residuals, at 300, 400 and 500 T;
   - test whether the FWHM upturn survives reasonable changes of background and line shape;
   - state the number of shots and show a second EMFC shot (or up- versus down-sweep data) reproducing the upturn;
   - give the time window per spectrum, dB/dt and estimated ΔB at each point, and the resulting instrumental broadening;
   - test statistically whether a single smooth superlinear function fits the whole 0–500 T FWHM data as well as "linear + threshold".

4. The argument for reassigning the strong 2p lines as 2p0^(Sz=±1) rests on an incorrect expression for the orbital Zeeman term (p. 6; SI Sec. I, Fig. S2). The text says the 2p±1 splitting is "exactly ħω_c… imposed by time reversal symmetry" and infers μ ≈ 2 m_e if the lines were 2p±1. For an exciton with unequal electron and hole masses, though, the linear term in the relative-motion Hamiltonian is (eħB/2)(1/m_e − 1/m_h)·m, not ħeB/(2μ)·m. With m_e ≈ 0.99 and m_h ≈ 0.58 m_e (Ref. 20), the 2p+1–2p−1 splitting is ~0.7·(ħe/m_0)B ≈ 0.08 meV/T. That is the same order as the measured ~0.11 meV/T (Fig. S2), so the discrimination is much weaker than claimed. Other weaknesses:
   - g = 2 for the spin-Zeeman option is assumed (hole g ≈ 0), not measured independently.
   - The selection rule invoked in SI Sec. II, that σ± "only couples strongly with the spin flip transitions", is inferred from the fit rather than derived from the quadrupole selection rules for O_h with B ∥ [001].
   - The assignment contradicts Refs. 11, 18 and 27, and the paper does not discuss their arguments in detail.

   This matters because the paper presents the assignment as a main result ("correctly assign the quantum numbers"), and the Harper analysis depends on knowing which state is being followed. The authors should redo the comparison with the correct linear term and literature electron and hole masses (and g-factors). They should derive the selection rules for this geometry and confront the alternative assignment of Refs. 11/18 quantitatively over the full 0–150 T fan.

B. Should address

5. The quoted uncertainty on μ* = 0.415 ± 0.01 m_e does not include the systematic choices it depends on (p. 6, Table I).
   - Ry* = 103 meV is fixed from the zero-field 2p–3p spacing assuming a pure 1/n² series. Low-n P excitons in Cu2O are known to deviate from that series (quantum defects and central-cell corrections; Ref. 24), and the literature Ry* ≈ 92–98 meV differs by 5–10%.
   - E_g = 2.1756 eV is also ~3 meV above the commonly quoted value.
   - Because the diamagnetic shift scales strongly with Ry* and μ*, these choices feed directly into μ*.
   - The abstract says the "high field data" provide μ*, yet the highest-field data (>300 T) deviate from the model. The fit range is not stated, nor whether the 300–500 T points were included.

   The authors should state the field range used, how the ±0.01 was obtained, and how μ* changes if Ry* and E_g are free or set to literature values.

6. Temperatures do not match across the data sets combined in Figs. 2 and 4. The NDPM data are at 1.3 K (Fig. 1a; 1.5 K in Fig. S1), the STC sample is cooled to 10 K (Methods), the EMFC data are at 45 K (Fig. 1b), and the Artyukhin data are at 1.2 K. Yet Fig. 2 is labelled "T = 45 K", and Fig. 4 normalises all three to a single zero-field FWHM (~2.9 meV) without saying at which temperature it was taken. Phonon broadening differs at 1.3 and 45 K. Sample heating during a flux-compression shot (shock, radiation, eddy currents in the cryostat) is also not discussed, and a temperature rise near peak field could itself produce a late-pulse broadening. The authors should normalise each data set to its own zero-field width, state how the EMFC sample temperature was measured, and comment on heating near peak field.

7. Other transitions are not used as internal controls (Fig. 4). A breakdown of the effective-mass approximation set by ℓ_B versus a0 should affect all states at a given field, not only 2p0^(Sz=+1). FWHM versus B for 2p0^(Sz=−1), 3p0^(Sz=±1), and any other line measurable at the same fields would test whether the broadening tracks ℓ_B (state-independent onset) or tracks proximity to other levels (state-specific onset). The linear broadening up to 300 T is also left unexplained: the only reference given (Ref. 31) is to quantum-well luminescence. Since the "upturn" is defined relative to that line's extrapolation, a mechanism for it, e.g. growing inhomogeneous or (dE/dB)-weighted broadening, would help.

8. Field calibration and its uncertainty in the STC and EMFC experiments are not described (Methods C–D). This includes the pickup-coil calibration, the estimated error in B at 300–500 T, and the field inhomogeneity over the sample. The energy deviation from the hydrogen model and the B ≈ 300 T threshold both depend on it. The authors should quote ΔB/B at each technique's highest fields and show that it cannot account for the deviation in Fig. 2.

9. The sample was polished to 48 μm and glued to quartz with Stycast. The resulting differential-contraction strain could split or broaden the lines, and the zero-field 2p FWHM of ~2.9 meV is wide compared with high-quality Cu2O. Only one crystal is studied. The authors should comment on strain (e.g. the 2p zero-field line shape compared with unmounted crystals) and say whether any second sample was measured.

C. Minor

10. Broken cross-references: "Fig. ?? (a-b)" on p. 3 and "Fig. ??(a)" in the Fig. S2 caption.
11. The abstract and p. 7 call 2p0 the "ground state transition". The exciton ground state is 1s. "Lowest optically allowed" would be accurate.
12. The maximum field is given as 500 T in the abstract and conclusions but as 520 T on p. 3 and in Methods D.
13. Fig. 1 caption: "Faraady". Fig. 2 legend mixes "2P", "3P" and "3p". Fig. 3 uses "S^Z" rather than S_z. The SI p. 5 text has "p_(−1)^(Sz=+1)", missing the "2".
14. The Fig. S3 caption describes "circular and square symbols", but the figure uses circles and diamonds. The SI text refers to an "insert of Fig. S3" that does not exist.
15. Fig. 3 shows a "3p_(+1)" component in the fit, which is not otherwise discussed. The text says "asymmetrical Gaussian" while the caption says "Gaussian".
16. Fig. 4 shows no error bars on the NDPM and STC points.
17. Fig. 4b labels the top axis in units of a0. The same symbol commonly means the (exciton) Bohr radius, so "lattice constants a" would be clearer.
18. p. 9 explains Harper broadening as an "increase of the degeneracy of the energy levels". The Harper effect actually lifts the Landau-level degeneracy into subbands, so the wording should be corrected.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Repeat the EMFC measurement with B along [110] and [111]. Lattice-periodicity (Harper/Hofstadter) effects depend on the flux through the unit-cell faces normal to B and so on crystal orientation, while level crossings, polaron effects and nonparabolicity largely do not. This would be the most discriminating experimental test.
2. Add a numerical calculation of a hydrogen-like exciton in a periodic potential (or a tight-binding electron–hole model on the Cu2O lattice) in field, predicting the onset and size of lattice-induced broadening for comparison with Fig. 4.
3. Plot the full 300–500 T spectra together with all calculated level positions from Fig. S3, so readers can judge which transitions overlap the 2p0^(Sz=+1) feature.
4. If the Harper reading cannot be made quantitative, recast the paper around the robust results (the 500 T fan, the assignment, μ*) and present the high-field broadening as an observation with candidate explanations.

5. Verdict: major revision
