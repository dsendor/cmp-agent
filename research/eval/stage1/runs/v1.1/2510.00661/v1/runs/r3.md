Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors measure the magneto-absorption of the 2p and 3p yellow excitons of Cu2O on one crystal using three pulsed-field techniques (NDPM to 60 T, single-turn coil to ~120–200 T, EMFC to 500 T). They make three claims: the two strong 2p absorption lines are the spin-Zeeman-split orthoexciton 2p0^{Sz=±1} levels rather than 2p±1; a hydrogen-model fit gives μ* = 0.415 ± 0.01 m_e; and above 300 T the 2p0^{Sz=+1} linewidth rises abruptly and its energy falls below the hydrogen model, which the authors take as "possible evidence for Harper broadening", meaning a breakdown of the effective-mass approximation once ℓ_B approaches a few lattice constants. Excitonic spectra of a bulk crystal at 500 T are rare, and an optical signature of lattice-scale magnetic confinement would be of broad interest. In this version, however, the reassignment rests on a questionable premise, and the Harper interpretation is qualitative and rests on a handful of single-shot points.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Unique data: excitonic absorption in a bulk semiconductor followed to 500 T, with one sample used in all three field-generation systems, so the datasets can be compared directly.
- Below 60 T the data agree well with the DC-field results of Artyukhin et al. (Fig. S1), which is a useful check on the pulsed-field calibration and energy extraction.
- Up to ~300 T a single, compact parameter set (Ry*, μ*, g) describes four transitions (Fig. 2, Fig. S3).
- The question asked (when does the hydrogenic, effective-mass description of a Wannier exciton fail as ℓ_B approaches the lattice scale) is well posed and important.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The case against assigning the strong 2p lines to 2p±1 relies on an incorrect orbital-Zeeman splitting. This matters for the quantum-number assignment, the fitted μ* and the Harper argument, all of which build on it. On p. 6, item (ii), the authors state that "the exactly ħω_c splitting of the 2p±1 states is imposed by time reversal symmetry". In SI Sec. I and Fig. S2 they then show that, if the lines were 2p±1, the splitting would imply "μ_r* = 2 m_e", five times the literature value, and they reject that assignment. For an exciton with zero centre-of-mass momentum, though, the term linear in B in the relative-motion Hamiltonian is (e/2)(1/m_e − 1/m_h) L·B, not (e/2μ) L·B. The 2p+1/2p−1 splitting is therefore ħeB|1/m_e − 1/m_h|, which is much smaller than ħeB/μ when the electron and hole masses are comparable, as they are in Cu2O. With the cyclotron-resonance masses the authors cite (Hodby et al., ref. 20; m_e ≈ 0.99, m_h ≈ 0.69 m_e), |1/m_e − 1/m_h|^-1 ≈ 2.3 m_e. That is essentially the "2 m_e" found in Fig. S2, so the observed splitting is just what the conventional 2p±1 assignment predicts, and the argument used to exclude it falls away. Points (i) and (iii) on pp. 5–6 do not separate the two assignments on their own: σ± resolution is expected for either, and the extra shoulders could be other members of the 2p multiplet.

   The authors should:
   - redo the analysis with the correct linear term, using independent m_e and m_h, or confirm and state that the Makado–McGill / Maude et al. model they use already handles unequal masses;
   - give the quadrupole selection rules for σ± in Faraday geometry along [001] for every 2p_m^{Sz} component;
   - show that a joint fit with the 2p0^{Sz=±1} assignment fits clearly better than one with the 2p±1 assignment, using the same parameters and the diamagnetic curvature up to 300 T, which differs between m = 0 and m = ±1.

   Because the diamagnetic shift of 2p0 differs from that of 2p±1, the value μ* = 0.415 m_e quoted in the abstract depends directly on this assignment.

2. The linewidth upturn above 300 T, the central experimental result, is not shown to be robust or intrinsic. It rests on four EMFC points (350, 400, 450 and 500 T in Fig. 4a). These are presumably one destructive shot or a few, and in Fig. 1(b) the 2p0^{Sz=+1} feature at those fields is a weak dip on a steep, noisy background. Specifically:

   - (a) Overlap with other transitions. The authors' own level diagram (Fig. S3a) puts the 2p−1^{Sz=0} level, and nearby members of the 2p−1 multiplet, crossing or approaching 2p0^{Sz=+1} between ~300 and 500 T. The measured energies (2.155–2.158 eV) lie between the purple 2p0^{Sz=+1} curve and the dashed 2p−1^{Sz=0} curve. Either an unresolved superposition of two lines or an anticrossing would produce both of the observed anomalies, the extra width and the energy falling below the hydrogen curve, without any breakdown of the effective-mass approximation. The authors need to rule this out explicitly, for example with two-component fits, by checking which polarization was used in the EMFC shots, and with model spectra of oscillator strength against field.
   - (b) Fitting systematics. The FWHM comes from "asymmetrical Gaussian" fits after subtracting a background (Fig. 3). That background is not specified at high field, and the asymmetry parameter is not defined. Example fits at 350–500 T should be shown, and the FWHM uncertainty should include the spread from different background choices and line shapes, not only the statistical error.
   - (c) Reproducibility. The number of EMFC shots per field should be stated, and the FWHM from independent shots should be shown, ideally two or more shots that reach ≥ 400 T.
   - (d) Statistics. The paper calls the increase "sudden" and "abrupt" on the strength of a linear fit below 300 T (blue line in Fig. 4a). It should test whether a smooth superlinear form, for example power law or quadratic, fits all points as well as a linear-plus-threshold model. With the error bars shown, the data may not distinguish a threshold.

3. The Harper-broadening interpretation is qualitative and may be quantitatively implausible. Harper/Hofstadter broadening is a property of Landau levels of a charged particle in a periodic potential, and it depends smoothly on the flux per plaquette α. The authors quote α ≈ 0.013 at 300 T. For the low-lying levels of a tight-binding band at such small α, the Harper bandwidth is expected to be extremely small, suppressed roughly exponentially in 1/α. The paper offers no estimate of its magnitude, which here would be ~10–15 meV of extra width (Fig. 4a: the normalized FWHM rises from ~2.5 to ~7 times 2.9 meV). The paper also gives no reason why a smooth function of α should switch on at a threshold near 300 T.

   Further, the object probed is a neutral exciton. Its 2p0 wavefunction along B is still governed by the Coulomb length, and its centre-of-mass motion couples to B differently from a free carrier. Harper's single-band result for free electrons (ref. 26) does not carry over directly. ℓ_B ≈ 3.5 a0 also does not by itself imply that the effective-mass approximation has broken down: a cyclotron orbit of radius 3.5 a0 still spans many unit cells.

   The authors should provide at least an order-of-magnitude calculation of the expected broadening for realistic Cu2O conduction and valence bands at 300–500 T, or else substantially narrow the claim in the title, abstract and conclusions.

B. Should address

4. Alternatives within effective-mass physics to the energy deviation and "mass increase" above 300 T are not discussed. Several mechanisms are known to matter in Cu2O at large energy shifts:
   - conduction- and valence-band nonparabolicity;
   - the anisotropic, Γ7+/Γ8+-mixed hole, including field-induced mixing with the green series ~130 meV higher;
   - exciton–LO-phonon (polaron) corrections, which change as the binding energy changes;
   - central-cell corrections.

   Each would naturally make the hydrogen model with a constant μ* overestimate the shift at high field. An increase in effective mass with energy is the expected signature of nonparabolicity, not specifically of a breakdown of the effective-mass approximation. These alternatives should be estimated or at least discussed (pp. 6–7 and p. 9).

5. Temperature and conditions are mixed across datasets, and the linewidth normalization is unclear. NDPM data are at 1.3 K (Fig. 1a; 1.5 K in Fig. S1), the STC cryostat reaches 10 K (Methods), and the EMFC data are at 45 K. Yet Fig. 2 and Fig. S3 are labelled "T = 45 K" while including all of these datasets. Fig. 4 normalizes every FWHM by a single zero-field value of ≈ 2.9 meV without stating at which temperature, or with which technique, it was measured. Phonon broadening at 45 K and at 1.3 K differ, and the temperature of the sample during a 50 µs EMFC implosion is not monitored. Heating from eddy currents or the shock, or a temperature that changes from shot to shot, could add width that grows with field. The authors should:
   - give the zero-field FWHM for each setup and temperature, and normalize each dataset by its own reference;
   - discuss or bound sample heating in the EMFC shots;
   - correct the temperature labels.

6. Field-related broadening in the EMFC and STC measurements needs bounding. The Methods give no streak-camera exposure or time window, no field-measurement method (pickup coil or Faraday rotation) and no calibration uncertainty. Two effects grow with field:
   - the field change during the exposure, dB/dt × Δt × dE/dB, where dB/dt is largest just before the EMFC peak;
   - spatial inhomogeneity of B across the 1.8 × 1.8 mm sample in the compressed-flux region.

   The authors should estimate both at 300–500 T. They should also state the uncertainty of the field values, since a few-percent error in B moves the EMFC points horizontally in Figs. 2 and 4.

7. The derivation of the fit parameters and their uncertainties is opaque. Table I gives μ* = 0.415 ± 0.01 m_e, Ry* = 103 ± 1 meV and g = 2.0 ± 0.1, with E_g fixed. The paper does not say:
   - which data and field range enter the fit (up to 300 T only?);
   - which parameters are free;
   - how Ry* and μ* are correlated, given that the dielectric constant is implicitly free;
   - how the errors were obtained.

   The value 0.415 m_e lies above the literature range of 0.35–0.41 m_e quoted on p. 6, so it is not, as stated, "close to the high end" of that range. The abstract says that "the high field data provides" μ*, yet the text says μ* appears to increase above 300 T. It should be made explicit which regime defines the quoted value. A fit restricted to ≤ 60 T, which is unaffected by the EMFC issues, would be a useful cross-check.

8. The polarization configuration of the STC and EMFC data is not stated clearly. In Fig. 1(b) only 2p0^{Sz=+1} is tracked, and Fig. 2 shows no 2p0^{Sz=−1} points beyond ~130 T. The authors should state whether EMFC spectra were polarization-resolved. If they were not, they should explain how the line followed to 500 T was identified as Sz = +1 rather than another member of the 2p multiplet. This links to concern 2(a).

9. Only one crystal was measured. A second sample, or a second set of EMFC shots on a fresh piece of the same boule, would show whether the upturn above 300 T is reproducible. The authors themselves raise sample-dependent variation of Ry* (~5%, p. 6).

C. Minor

10. Several cross-references are broken and print as "??". These are "Fig. ?? (a-b)" on p. 3 and "Fig. ??(a)" in the Fig. S2 caption; both appear to refer to Fig. S1.

11. Small inconsistencies:
   - "Faraady" in the Fig. 1 caption;
   - the maximum field is given as 500 T in the abstract and text but as 520 T in the Introduction and Methods;
   - the temperatures 1.2/1.3/1.5 K differ between Fig. 1a, the text and Fig. S1;
   - "p_{-1}^{Sz=+1}" on SI p. 5 lacks the "2";
   - spin labels are written as both "S^z" and "S_z" (Fig. 3 against elsewhere);
   - the Fig. S3 caption describes "circular and square symbols" but the figure shows circles and diamonds.

12. Ref. 15 (Albrecht et al., Hofstadter spectrum in quantum-Hall conductance) is cited on p. 2 for magneto-exciton wavefunction shrinkage in quantum wells, which is not what that paper shows. It would fit better in the Hofstadter discussion on p. 9. On p. 9, "Harper broadening observed in a natural crystal" overstates what the abstract itself calls "possible evidence".

13. Fig. 4(b): the dashed line labelled B = 300 T is drawn at ℓ_B ≈ 14.8 Å. The top axis (ℓ_B/a0) should be checked against a0 = 4.27 Å; 14.8/4.27 ≈ 3.5 appears to align correctly. A statement that error bars are shown only for EMFC points, and why, would help.

14. The Methods omit the spectrometer resolution, the streak-camera time resolution and exposure, the number of EMFC shots, and how absorption was obtained from transmission (reference spectrum, normalization). These are needed to judge whether the measured widths of 3–20 meV are resolution-limited anywhere.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Rebuild the assignment argument using the correct exciton linear-Zeeman term, ∝ (1/m_e − 1/m_h), and present a side-by-side global fit of both assignments with a goodness-of-fit comparison.
2. Show raw EMFC spectra and fits at 300–500 T, including two-line fits that allow for the crossing 2p−1 levels of Fig. S3. Report shot-to-shot reproducibility of the FWHM.
3. Provide a quantitative estimate of Harper/Hofstadter broadening for Cu2O bands at α ≈ 0.01–0.02. Compare it with the observed excess width and with simple nonparabolicity or band-mixing estimates. If the estimate is far below the data, recast the conclusion as an observed deviation from the hydrogenic model, with its origin open.
4. Harmonize temperatures and normalizations across the three techniques, and bound heating and dB/dt broadening in the EMFC shots.

5. Verdict: major revision
