Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors measure magneto-absorption of the yellow 2p and 3p excitons of Cu2O on one crystal, using three pulsed-field techniques (NDPM to 60 T, single-turn coil to about 130 T, electromagnetic flux compression to 500 T). From these data they reassign the dominant 2p lines as spin-Zeeman-split 2p0 orthoexciton states, extract a reduced mass of μ* = 0.415 ± 0.01 me, and report that above 300 T the 2p0(Sz=+1) linewidth rises sharply while its energy falls below the hydrogen-model curve. They interpret this as possible Harper broadening, meaning a breakdown of the effective-mass approximation once the magnetic length is about 3.5 lattice constants. Exciton spectroscopy at 500 T in a bulk crystal is rare and technically impressive, and a lattice-scale breakdown of the effective-mass picture would interest a broad audience. As written, however, the headline interpretation rests on four high-field points with no quantitative theory behind it, and the assignment it relies on uses an incomplete argument.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The data are unique: exciton absorption in Cu2O followed continuously to 500 T, with the same crystal used in all three field-generation techniques. This allows direct cross-checks, and the NDPM data agree well with the DC-field data of Artyukhin et al. (Fig. S1).
- The paper offers a concrete, testable reassignment of the strong 2p lines, backed by polarization-resolved data and a global hydrogen-model fit to many branches (Fig. S3).
- The question is well posed and broadly interesting: whether, and at what field, a Wannier exciton stops behaving as a hydrogen atom with effective masses.
- The hydrogen-model fit to the 2p and 3p branches up to 300 T gives a sensible parameter set. Ry* = 103 meV, μ* = 0.415 me and g = 2 together imply ε ≈ 7.4, consistent with the known dielectric constant of Cu2O.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The Harper-broadening interpretation has no quantitative support, and obvious competing explanations are not excluded (Section II, pp. 8–9; Fig. 4; Fig. S3). The argument is only that ℓB ≈ 14.8 Å ≈ 3.5 a0 at 300 T, with α = φ/φ0 ≈ 0.013. Several problems follow.
   (a) In Harper's problem, Landau levels near a band edge broaden into bands whose width is exponentially small in 1/α at small α. No estimate shows that α ≈ 0.013 can produce the observed broadening of roughly 3 to 7 times 2.9 meV (≈ 10–20 meV), or why the onset should be abrupt at 300 T rather than gradual.
   (b) Harper's result concerns a charged particle in a periodic potential. The 2p exciton at K ≈ 0 is neutral, and the paper does not explain how Harper physics carries over to its relative-motion wavefunction.
   (c) At ℓB ≈ 3.5 a0 the carriers sample wavevectors of order 1/ℓB, roughly 10% of the zone boundary. The more conventional consequences are band nonparabolicity and central-cell corrections: they make the effective mass heavier, as the authors themselves infer from Fig. 2, and they also change linewidths through altered coupling to phonons and the continuum. The paper does not separate "breakdown of the parabolic-band effective-mass approximation" from "Harper/Hofstadter physics".
   (d) The authors' own global fit (Fig. S3a) suggests a plainer explanation. Above about 300 T the measured 2p0(Sz=+1) energies (≈ 2.155–2.158 eV) leave the purple 2p0(Sz=+1) curve and fall close to the predicted 2p−1(Sz=0) branch (grey dashed line), which approaches it at high field. An unresolved second component, or an anticrossing between these branches, would produce both the apparent broadening and the energy deviation at the same field, without any lattice effect.
   (e) Other mechanisms also need to be addressed. The cyclotron energy of the reduced-mass pair at 300 T is ≈ 84 meV, which is in the range of the Cu2O optical phonons, so a magneto-polaron or phonon-assisted resonance is possible. Field-induced mixing with the 2s/3d manifolds and the approaching 3p/continuum edge are further candidates.
   What the authors should do: (i) give at least an order-of-magnitude calculation of the expected Harper broadening for Cu2O band parameters at 300–500 T, ideally a tight-binding or k·p exciton calculation in field; (ii) refit the 350–500 T spectra with two components (2p0(Sz=+1) plus 2p−1(Sz=0)) and show whether a single broadened line is actually required; (iii) discuss and estimate nonparabolicity and magneto-polaron effects. Unless these alternatives are excluded, the conclusion should stay at "a deviation from the hydrogen model of unknown origin".

2. The high-field linewidth data are not shown to be reproducible or free of flux-compression artifacts (Figs. 1(b), 4; Methods D). The whole effect rests on four points (350, 400, 450 and 500 T), all from EMFC.
   (a) The number of EMFC shots is not stated, nor whether the upturn appears in more than one shot. In EMFC the sample is normally destroyed, so it is also unclear how the "same crystal" was used in every technique, and in what order.
   (b) The fields above 300 T are reached in the last microseconds before liner collapse. That is exactly when shock waves, mechanical stress on the sample, heating of the sample and cryostat, and field inhomogeneity across the 1.8 mm crystal are largest. Strain shifts and splits Cu2O exciton lines strongly, so strain alone could produce both the broadening and the energy shift.
   (c) The sample temperature during the EMFC shot (nominally 45 K) is not monitored. The streak-camera spectral resolution and time window, and the corresponding field change ΔB within each spectrum, are not given.
   (d) The 2p feature at 350–500 T is a weak structure on a steep, rising background (Fig. 1(b)), and the FWHM depends directly on the background model. Only the 120 T fit is shown (Fig. 3).
   What the authors should do: report the number of shots and show the upturn in at least two independent shots. Show the Fig. 3-type fits, with residuals, for 300–500 T. State how the FWHM error bars were obtained and how sensitive the FWHM is to the background function. Give the streak-camera resolution and the dB/dt-induced smearing. Use timing to separate field from time-to-collapse: compare up-sweep with down-sweep, or use a shot whose peak field is about 350 T, so that 300 T occurs at a different time relative to collapse. If the broadening tracks B rather than time, that would be strong evidence that it is intrinsic.

3. The argument used to reassign the strong lines as 2p0(Sz=±1) rather than 2p±1 is incomplete (p. 6, point (ii); SI Sec. I, Fig. S2). This assignment underpins μ*, the Fig. 2 fit, and the identification of the line whose width is analysed.
   (a) The paper says the splitting of 2p±1 is "exactly ħωc" by time-reversal symmetry, and concludes that the observed splitting would imply μ ≈ 2 me. For an exciton with unequal electron and hole masses, however, the linear orbital Zeeman term is proportional to (1/me − 1/mh)·m, not 1/μ. It vanishes for me = mh. With the commonly quoted Cu2O masses (me ≈ 1 me, mh ≈ 0.6–0.7 me), this term gives an "effective" mass of order 1.5–2 me. That is precisely the value the authors use to rule out the 2p±1 assignment.
   (b) The hole in Cu2O belongs to the spin-orbit-split Γ7+ band, so pure spin is not a good quantum number for the hole, and the spin/orbit separation in point (ii) needs justification beyond a reference.
   (c) Within this framework the selection rules are hard to follow. The text says the yellow series is "electric quadrupole, therefore only p-states are allowed", but yellow nP excitons are usually described as weakly electric-dipole allowed and only the 1s as quadrupole. The SI also states that σ± couple "only with spin-flip transitions", but the shoulders are then assigned to 2p+1(Sz=−1) and 2p−1(Sz=+1).
   What the authors should do: redo the competing-assignment test with the correct exciton linear Zeeman term, including both electron and hole masses and g-factors. Compare the full field dependence of both assignments up to 500 T, where the quadratic (diamagnetic) terms of 2p0 and 2p±1 differ strongly. Then give a symmetry-based (Oh, Γ7+ ⊗ Γ6+ ⊗ envelope) account of which components are bright in σ+ and σ− in Faraday geometry along [001].

B. Should address

4. Extraction of μ* and its uncertainty (Table I; p. 6). Ry* is fixed by assuming the 2p–3p splitting follows a pure 1/n² law. Low-n yellow P excitons in Cu2O are known to show quantum defects from nonparabolicity and central-cell effects. Forcing a pure Rydberg series raises Ry* (103 meV here versus ≈ 98 meV or less in the literature) and shifts Eg (2.1756 eV here versus ≈ 2.172 eV in the literature). Because the hydrogen-in-field scaling depends on γ = ħωc/2Ry*, this bias passes directly into μ*. The ±0.01 me uncertainty is also not explained: which parameters are free and which fixed, which field range was fitted, and whether the field-calibration uncertainty of the STC/EMFC data is included. Please give this information, and show how μ* changes when Ry* is left free or a quantum defect is allowed.

5. Mixed temperatures and conditions in the combined analysis (Figs. 2, 4). Fig. 2 is labelled T = 45 K, but it combines DC data at 1.2 K, NDPM at 1.3–1.5 K, STC at 10 K and EMFC at 45 K. Fig. 4 normalizes all FWHMs by a single zero-field value (≈ 2.9 meV) without saying at which temperature, or with which instrument, that value was measured. Linewidths and the gap both depend on temperature and spectral resolution. Please normalize each data set by its own zero- or low-field width, or give absolute widths with the instrumental resolution of each setup, and correct or discuss the temperature-dependent gap shift used in the joint fit.

6. The "sudden" upturn and the linear baseline (Fig. 4(a)). The linear increase up to 300 T is not explained (the only reference is to III-V quantum-well work), and the fit to it is not specified. With four points above 300 T and error bars of ±0.5 in normalized units, a smooth superlinear (for example quadratic) dependence may describe the whole range equally well. Please give the fit and compare models statistically, so that the claim of a threshold at 300 T is quantitative. The specific value ℓB ≈ 3.5 a0 depends on this.

7. Polarization and line selection in EMFC (Fig. 1(b), p. 4). The paper does not say whether the EMFC spectra were polarization-resolved. It does not explain how the single surviving line was "later identified" as Sz=+1, or why the strong 2p0(Sz=−1) branch is not followed above about 130 T. Fig. 2 predicts that branch should be well separated (≈ 2.17–2.19 eV at 200–300 T). Its disappearance is relevant both to the assignment and to the possibility that the observed line is a mixture of branches. Please clarify, and show σ+ and σ− EMFC spectra if they exist.

8. The claimed increase in reduced mass (pp. 3, 7, 9) is stated but not quantified. Please give the μ* (or field-dependent effective mass) needed to describe the 350–500 T energies. Please also show whether a nonparabolic-band model with literature parameters reproduces the energy deviation, since this bears directly on concern 1.

9. Only one crystal was measured. A second crystal, or at least a second EMFC shot on a crystal from a different batch or with a different thickness, would show that the broadening is not tied to this sample's strain state, its epoxy mounting (Stycast on quartz, which produces differential thermal strain), or its polishing damage.

C. Minor

10. Broken cross-references: "Fig. ??(a-b)" on p. 3 (presumably Fig. S1) and "Fig. ??(a)" in the Fig. S2 caption.
11. Typos and notation: "Faraady" (Fig. 1 caption); "p−1^{Sz=+1}" should be "2p−1^{Sz=+1}" (SI p. 5); "exhibit" should be "exhibits" (p. 3); the reference "Makado and McGill calculations considers" (p. 6).
12. The Fig. S3 caption says circles and squares represent σ− and σ+, but the figure uses circles and diamonds. The colour coding of the SI figures also differs from the main-text figures.
13. Fig. 3 is described in the text as a fit with "asymmetrical Gaussian functions", but the caption says Gaussians. Please define the line shape and state how the FWHM of an asymmetric profile is defined. Fig. 3(b) also contains a "3p+1" component that is not discussed elsewhere.
14. Field ranges are inconsistent: the STC is described as reaching 200 T but its data stop at about 130 T, and EMFC is described as reaching 520 T while the data stop at 500 T. Please also state how the field was measured in each technique (pickup coil, Faraday rotation) and with what uncertainty.
15. Table I gives Eg without an uncertainty, and the effective g-factor is not clearly defined (exciton g for the Sz = ±1 splitting, or electron g alone). The comparison with literature values in the Fig. 2 inset should include the literature numbers.
16. For α = φ/φ0, please state which area is used (cubic cell a0² of the 6-atom Cu2O cell, or a primitive-cell projection), since this affects the comparison with Hofstadter physics.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add a theory section or appendix with a quantitative model: for example a two-band tight-binding or k·p exciton calculation for Cu2O in a field, giving the expected energy deviation and linewidth versus B. Compare it directly with Figs. 2 and 4, including the alternative of nonparabolicity without Harper physics.
2. Show all high-field EMFC spectra with fits, residuals and background variations in the SI, together with a shot log (peak field, timing, temperature) and the streak-camera resolution, so that readers can judge the robustness of the four points that carry the claim.
3. Recast the assignment discussion in the SI as a quantitative comparison of the two assignments, using the full exciton Hamiltonian (separate me and mh, hole Γ7+ g-factor) over 0–500 T, with the goodness of fit for each.
4. Unless the additional analysis rules out the alternatives, moderate the title and conclusions toward "a deviation from hydrogenic behaviour above 300 T".

5. Verdict: major revision
