A. Must address

1. The Harper-broadening interpretation has no quantitative support, and the paper's own numbers suggest the effect would be negligible (Section II, pp. 8–9; Fig. 4(b)). The only argument given is that at 300 T, ℓ_B ≈ 14.8 Å ≈ 3.5 a0 (a0 = 4.27 Å) and α = φ/φ0 ≈ 0.013, which the authors call "comparable" to the lattice constant. In Harper/Hofstadter physics, the periodic potential broadens Landau levels near a band edge into subbands whose width falls off roughly exponentially in 1/α at small α. At α ≈ 0.013 that width should be vanishingly small, not meV-scale. The observed extra width is large: the normalized FWHM rises from ~2.5–2.6 to ~6.8–7 times the 2.9 meV zero-field width, i.e. ~10–20 meV of additional broadening between 300 and 500 T. Nothing in the model gives a threshold at 300 T either, since Harper effects grow smoothly with α. Harper's result also concerns a single charged carrier in a tight-binding band, whereas Cu2O's conduction band (Cu 4s) is broad and nearly free-electron-like, and the object probed is a neutral exciton at K ≈ 0. Its 2p0 relative-motion wavefunction along B is still set by the Coulomb length, and its centre-of-mass motion couples to B differently from a free carrier. The paper does not explain how a Harper spectrum of the relative coordinate would appear as an absorption linewidth. Finally, ℓ_B ≈ 3.5 a0 does not by itself mean the effective-mass approximation has broken down, because a cyclotron orbit of that radius still spans many unit cells. The authors should give at least an order-of-magnitude calculation of the expected broadening at 300–500 T for realistic Cu2O bands (band widths from Ref. 33, the actual a0, the fcc Cu sublattice), ideally a tight-binding or k·p electron–hole calculation in field, and show that it can reach ~10 meV. A strongly discriminating experimental test would be to repeat the EMFC measurement with B along [110] and [111]: lattice-periodicity effects depend on the flux through the unit-cell faces normal to B, whereas level crossings, polaron effects and nonparabolicity largely do not. Unless the interpretation is made quantitative, the title, abstract and conclusions should be reduced to "an unexplained broadening and energy deviation from the hydrogen model above 300 T".

2. Competing explanations for the broadening and the energy deviation are not ruled out, including one visible in the authors' own level diagram (pp. 7–9; Figs. 2, 4, S3). (i) Overlap or anticrossing with nearby levels. In Fig. S3(a) the calculated 2p−1^(Sz=0) level (grey dashed line) approaches or crosses 2p0^(Sz=+1) from below between ~300 and 500 T. The measured energies at 350–500 T (≈ 2.155–2.158 eV) sit between the purple 2p0^(Sz=+1) curve and this grey dashed curve, and further members of the 2p multiplet (2p0^(Sz=0), 2p−1^(Sz=±1)) lie within ~10–20 meV. An unresolved second component, or level mixing at a near-crossing, would at the same field both broaden the apparent line and pull its fitted centre below the hydrogen curve. Those are exactly the two observations taken as evidence for Harper broadening, and they would need no lattice effect. (ii) Phonon-assisted relaxation and magnetopolaron effects. Cu2O has LO phonons at ~19 and ~82 meV. With μ* = 0.415 m_e the cyclotron energy ħeB/μ* reaches ~84 meV near 300 T, and the gaps to lower-lying 2p states in Fig. S3 open to tens of meV, so a resonant polaron coupling or newly opened decay channels could produce both a threshold-like broadening and an energy pinning. Field-induced mixing with the 2s/3d manifolds and the approaching 3p/continuum edge are further candidates. The authors should overlay all calculated levels near 2p0^(Sz=+1) on the 300–500 T spectra; refit those spectra with two (or more) components, e.g. 2p0^(Sz=+1) plus 2p−1^(Sz=0), and show whether a single broadened line is actually required; model the oscillator strengths against field; estimate the LO-phonon resonance fields for the relevant level spacings; and state why each alternative is excluded.

3. The linewidth upturn above 300 T, the central experimental result, is not shown to be reproducible, robust to the fitting procedure, or free of flux-compression artifacts (Figs. 1(b), 3, 4; Methods D). The effect rests on four EMFC points (350, 400, 450 and 500 T). The paper does not state how many EMFC shots were taken, whether these points come from a single destructive shot, or whether the upturn appears in more than one shot. Since EMFC normally destroys the sample, it is also unclear how the "same crystal" was used in every technique and in what order. In Fig. 1(b) the 2p0^(Sz=+1) feature above 300 T is a weak dip or step on a steep, rising, noisy background. Without the band-edge feature at 2.16–2.20 eV, a ~20 meV FWHM at 500 T would be poorly constrained, so the result depends directly on the background polynomial and on the "asymmetrical Gaussian" line shape, whose asymmetry parameter and FWHM definition are not given. The only fit shown is at 120 T (Fig. 3), and the origin of the FWHM error bars is not stated. Fields above 300 T are reached in the last microseconds before liner collapse, when shock waves, mechanical stress on the sample, and field inhomogeneity across the 1.8 × 1.8 mm crystal are largest. Strain shifts and splits Cu2O exciton lines strongly, so it alone could produce both the broadening and the energy shift. The field swept during each streak-camera time slice and the spatial inhomogeneity of B each broaden a line by (dE/dB)·ΔB, and neither the streak-camera spectral resolution, exposure or time window nor dB/dt is given. The authors should: state the number of shots and show the upturn in at least two independent shots reaching ≥ 400 T; show fits with backgrounds and residuals at 300, 350, 400, 450 and 500 T; include in the FWHM uncertainty the spread from reasonable changes of background and line shape; give the time window per spectrum, dB/dt, the estimated ΔB (temporal and spatial) at each point and the resulting instrumental broadening; and separate field from time-to-collapse, for example by comparing up- and down-sweeps or using a shot that peaks near 350 T, so that 300 T occurs at a different time relative to collapse. If the broadening tracks B rather than time, that would be strong evidence that it is intrinsic.

4. The argument for reassigning the strong 2p lines as 2p0^(Sz=±1) rather than 2p±1 rests on an incorrect orbital-Zeeman term (p. 6, point (ii); SI Sec. I, Fig. S2). The paper states that "the exactly ħω_c splitting of the 2p±1 states is imposed by time reversal symmetry" and infers that the observed splitting would require μ ≈ 2 m_e, five times the literature value. For an exciton at zero centre-of-mass momentum with unequal electron and hole masses, however, the term linear in B in the relative-motion Hamiltonian is (e/2)(1/m_e − 1/m_h) L·B, not (e/2μ) L·B; it vanishes for m_e = m_h. With the cyclotron-resonance masses the authors cite (Hodby et al., Ref. 20; m_e ≈ 0.99, m_h ≈ 0.58–0.69 m_e), |1/m_e − 1/m_h|⁻¹ ≈ 1.5–2.3 m_e, giving a 2p+1–2p−1 splitting of ~0.08 meV/T, the same order as the measured ~0.11 meV/T. The observed splitting is therefore roughly what the conventional 2p±1 assignment predicts, and the exclusion argument falls away. Points (i) and (iii) on pp. 5–6 do not separate the assignments on their own: σ± resolution is expected for either, and the extra shoulders could be other members of the 2p multiplet. Further weaknesses: g = 2 for the spin-Zeeman option is assumed (hole g ≈ 0) rather than measured independently; the hole belongs to the spin-orbit-split Γ7+ band, so pure spin is not a good quantum number and the spin/orbit separation needs more justification than a reference; the statement that the yellow series is "electric quadrupole, therefore only p-states are allowed" conflicts with the usual description of yellow nP excitons as weakly dipole-allowed and only 1s as quadrupole; the SI says σ± couple "only with spin-flip transitions", which appears to be inferred from the fit, yet the shoulders are assigned to 2p+1^(Sz=−1) and 2p−1^(Sz=+1); and the assignment contradicts Refs. 11, 18 and 27 without discussing their arguments in detail. This matters because the paper presents the assignment as a main result, μ* = 0.415 m_e depends on it (the diamagnetic shifts of 2p0 and 2p±1 differ), and the Harper analysis depends on knowing which state is followed. The authors should redo the comparison with the correct linear term, independent literature m_e, m_h and g-factors (or confirm that the Makado–McGill / Maude et al. model already handles unequal masses); derive the selection rules (O_h, Γ7+ ⊗ Γ6+ ⊗ envelope) for σ+ and σ− in Faraday geometry along [001] for every 2p_m^(Sz) component; and present a side-by-side global fit of both assignments over the full field range, using the diamagnetic curvature where m = 0 and m = ±1 differ strongly, with a goodness-of-fit comparison.

B. Should address

5. Alternatives within effective-mass physics for the energy deviation and the "increase of reduced effective mass" above 300 T are neither quantified nor discussed (pp. 3, 6–7, 9; Fig. 2). At ℓ_B ≈ 3.5 a0 the carriers sample wavevectors ~10% of the zone boundary, where conduction- and valence-band nonparabolicity, the anisotropic Γ7+/Γ8+-mixed hole (including field-induced mixing with the green series ~130 meV higher), exciton–LO-phonon (polaron) corrections that change with binding energy, and central-cell corrections all become important. Each would make a hydrogen model with constant μ* overestimate the shift at high field, and a mass increasing with energy is the expected signature of nonparabolicity, not specifically of a breakdown of the effective-mass approximation. The paper does not separate "breakdown of the parabolic-band approximation" from "Harper/Hofstadter physics". The authors should give the μ* (or field-dependent mass) needed to describe the 350–500 T energies, and show whether a nonparabolic-band or band-mixing model with literature parameters reproduces the deviation.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

6. The extraction of μ* and its uncertainty are opaque and omit systematic choices (Table I; p. 6; abstract). Ry* = 103 meV is fixed by assuming the zero-field 2p–3p spacing follows a pure 1/n² law, but low-n yellow P excitons in Cu2O show quantum defects from nonparabolicity and central-cell effects (Ref. 24), and literature values of Ry* (≈ 92–98 meV) differ by 5–10%. E_g = 2.1756 eV is ~3 meV above the commonly quoted ≈ 2.172 eV and is given without uncertainty. Because the hydrogen-in-field scaling depends on γ = ħω_c/2Ry*, these choices pass directly into μ*. The paper does not say which parameters are free, how Ry* and μ* are correlated (the dielectric constant is implicitly free), which field range was fitted, whether the 300–500 T points were included, whether field-calibration uncertainty is propagated, or how ±0.01 m_e was obtained. The abstract says the "high field data" provide μ*, yet the highest-field data deviate from the model. The value 0.415 m_e also lies above, not "close to the high end" of, the literature range 0.35–0.41 m_e quoted on p. 6. The authors should state all of this, show how μ* changes when Ry* and E_g are free, set to literature values, or a quantum defect is allowed, and add a cross-check fit restricted to ≤ 60 T.

7. Temperatures and conditions are mixed across the combined data sets, and the linewidth normalization is unclear (Figs. 1, 2, 4, S1, S3; Methods). The DC data of Artyukhin et al. are at 1.2 K, NDPM at 1.3 K (Fig. 1a; 1.5 K in Fig. S1), STC at 10 K and EMFC at 45 K, yet Figs. 2 and S3 are labelled "T = 45 K". Fig. 4 normalizes every FWHM by a single zero-field value (≈ 2.9 meV) without saying at which temperature or with which setup it was measured, though phonon broadening and instrumental resolution differ between them. The EMFC sample temperature is not monitored during the ~50 µs implosion, and heating from eddy currents, radiation or the shock near peak field, or a temperature that varies from shot to shot, could itself add width late in the pulse. The authors should give the zero-field FWHM and instrumental resolution for each setup and temperature and normalize each data set by its own reference, correct or discuss the temperature-dependent gap shift in the joint fit, state how the EMFC sample temperature was determined and bound heating near peak field, and correct the temperature labels.

8. The "sudden" upturn at 300 T and the linear baseline below it are not justified statistically or physically (Fig. 4(a)). The linear increase up to 300 T is unexplained, its only reference (Ref. 31) being quantum-well luminescence, and the fit (blue line) is not specified. With four points above 300 T and error bars of about ±0.5 in normalized units, a smooth superlinear (power-law or quadratic) dependence may describe the whole 0–500 T range as well as "linear + threshold". Because the upturn is defined relative to this line's extrapolation, and the quoted ℓ_B ≈ 3.5 a0 depends on the threshold, the authors should specify the fit, compare the models statistically, and propose a mechanism for the linear part (e.g. growing inhomogeneous or (dE/dB)-weighted broadening).

11. Field calibration and its uncertainty in the STC and EMFC experiments are not described (Methods C–D). 12. Only one crystal was measured, and its mounting could introduce strain. The sample was polished to 48 μm and glued to quartz with Stycast, so differential thermal contraction or polishing damage could split or broaden the lines; the zero-field 2p FWHM of ~2.9 meV is wide compared with high-quality Cu2O, and the authors themselves note sample-dependent variation of Ry* (~5%, p. 6). The authors should comment on strain (e.g. compare the zero-field 2p line shape with unmounted crystals) and show the upturn in a second sample, or at least in EMFC shots on a fresh piece or a crystal from a different batch or thickness.
