A. Must address

1. **The claim that the nuclear spin is driven "irrespective of hybridization" is not supported, and conflicts with standard ENDOR physics and with the paper's own supplementary statements.** The Abstract ("the nuclear spin can be driven efficiently irrespective of its hybridization with the electron spin"), the Introduction ("we unambiguously demonstrate NMR driving independent of electron-nuclear spin hybridization") and the Conclusion ("hybridization ... is not necessary to drive NMR") rest on the observation that ENDOR features persist from 200 to 1400 mT, where the flip-flop admixture |⟨↓,−3/2|ψ⟩|² is small (SFig. 1b, about 10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵). But the contrast with Fig. 1d shows only that the readout no longer requires Δm_s ≠ 0; it says nothing about the drive matrix element. A small admixture is not zero, and in ENDOR the nuclear transition moment is normally dominated by it. The transverse hyperfine term mixes electron character into each nuclear state with amplitude ε ~ A_⊥/(2 f_ESR), roughly 10⁻³–10⁻² for A_⊥ of tens of MHz (A/2 ≈ 65 MHz) and f_ESR = 2–11.5 GHz. The electron moment exceeds the ⁴⁷Ti nuclear moment by g_e μ_B/(g_N μ_N) ~ 10³–10⁴ (with g_e ≈ 0.56, g_N ≈ 0.315). The electron-mediated ("hyperfine-enhanced") contribution, of order (g_e μ_B / g_N μ_N) × (A / h f_ESR) × g_N μ_N B1, is therefore plausibly 10–100 times larger than direct coupling to the bare nucleus across the whole field range, and falls as about 1/B, the same scaling as the hybridization coefficient in SFig. 1b. The paper is also internally inconsistent: Supplementary Note 1 states that the tip field is mostly exchange and "cannot couple directly with the nuclear spin", yet the Discussion proposes a drive via an effective oscillating field from piezoelectric displacement in the tip field, barrier modulation or spin torque. All of these act on the electron spin, so they can reach the nucleus only through the hyperfine admixture, which is the hybridization the paper says is not needed. Nor does the paper show that the drive efficiency is independent of B: the ENDOR amplitude changes non-monotonically with field and is attributed to V_ESR transmission (Supplementary Note 2), leaving open whether the NMR Rabi rate falls with B as hyperfine-mediated driving predicts. The authors should (i) compute the Δm_I = ±1 transition matrix elements (Rabi rates) from the full Hamiltonian of Eq. 1, including the anisotropic A, A_⊥, the tip-field tilt (φ ≈ 5°) and the measured g-tensor, for each candidate drive: a transverse field on the bare nucleus only, a transverse field on electron and nucleus together, modulation of B_tip/exchange, and modulation of the anisotropic hyperfine and quadrupole terms, and give the predicted field and m_s dependence of each; (ii) measure the drive strength independently of the ESR readout calibration, via the V_NMR dependence of ENDOR depth and linewidth (saturation, power broadening) at two or three fields with matched V_ESR (e.g. 200 and 450 mT, where V_ESR = 20 mV is available, or by setting V_ESR to the same lower value at all fields, or normalizing each ENDOR dip to the ESR peak height in SFig. 4a), ideally also below 200 mT where the admixture is large; (iii) compare the intensities of the ↑ and ↓ transitions (I vs II, III vs IV) with the predicted enhancement factors. If the drive proves hyperfine-enhanced, the result is not undermined, but the headline must be narrowed to what is shown, e.g. "readout independent of hybridization" or "driving does not rely on electron-nuclear flip-flop (Δm_s ≠ 0) transitions", with drive persisting to 1.4 T.

B. Should address

2. **The arguments that exclude hyperfine and quadrupole modulation as drive mechanisms are incomplete, so the conclusion that the drive is "dominantly an effective oscillating magnetic field" is not established (Discussion; Supplementary Note 3; SFig. 8; Fig. 2b).** The Discussion says modulating the hyperfine interaction would only drive flip-flop transitions at GHz frequencies. That holds only for the S_x I_x + S_y I_y part. With an anisotropic or tilted hyperfine tensor, and an electron quantization axis tilted by the tip field (φ ≈ 5°, Suppl. Note 1), modulation produces off-diagonal terms such as A_zx S_z I_x that drive Δm_s = 0, Δm_I = ±1 transitions at exactly the observed MHz frequencies, with an m_s-dependent sign. This is the hyperfine-Stark mechanism of electrically driven NMR in donors and single-molecule magnets (refs. 7, 16, 17), and a piezoelectric displacement of the atom makes it plausible here. Quadrupole modulation is dismissed on two grounds, neither decisive. First, the relative strength of Δm_I = ±1 ({I_z, I_x}) and Δm_I = ±2 (I_x² − I_y²) terms depends on which electric-field-gradient components are modulated, so absent ±2 lines in SFig. 8 do not exclude a quadrupole drive; moreover SFig. 8 is a single sweep with noise of about ±3 fA in which even transitions I and III are barely above the noise, and the expected ±2 amplitude is never estimated. Second, the "slight signal" at V/VI (f_ESR ≈ 4.0–4.1 GHz in Fig. 2b) is said only to "may be discerned", is unquantified, and is used in both directions in the argument. The authors should write the modulated terms explicitly with selection rules and expected relative intensities for I–VI and the Δm_I = ±2 lines; state the amplitude ratio needed to see ±2 lines at the SNR of SFig. 8 and repeat the wide sweep with enough averaging for a meaningful bound; show line cuts and Lorentzian fits with significance for V/VI, ideally from a dedicated measurement with f_ESR on the m_I = −1/2 peak, or treat V/VI as unresolved; and use the m_s dependence of the I/III vs II/IV amplitudes as a discriminant. Otherwise the mechanism should be presented as an open question, and the sentence claiming applicability to other nuclear spin systems "not dependent on A or Q" removed.

3. **The single-tone versus ENDOR contrast (Fig. 1d vs Fig. 3) is not a matched comparison.** The narrative that single-tone features "vanish beyond 100 mT" while ENDOR "persists to 1.4 T" is a main pillar of the paper, but the two datasets differ in several ways at once: Fig. 1d was taken with a different microtip (Methods), so tip fields and spin polarizations differ; setpoints differ (I_set = 2.5–3 pA and V_bias = 70–90 mV varying between fields in Fig. 1d, versus 4 pA and 80 mV); and the field ranges overlap only at 200 mT. No ENDOR data are shown at 60 or 100 mT, and no single-tone data with the ENDOR tip. Because single-tone mode cannot distinguish "no driving" from "no readout" at 200 mT, the comparison does not show that the hybridization-dependent features and the ENDOR features arise from different driving processes. With one tip, one atom and one setpoint (and the same V_NMR), the authors should measure single-tone NMR and ENDOR at a low field (60–100 mT) and at a high field (≥ 200 mT), show whether the low-field ENDOR resonances coincide with the single-tone features, and check how amplitudes scale relative to SFig. 1b.

6. **No population model accounts for the sign, amplitude and assignment of the ENDOR features.** The text says Fig. 2a shows "dips (blue) and peaks (red)", but the red m_I = −3/2 trace shows no clear peaks at 50 or 85 MHz, and in Fig. 2b the −3/2 column (about 3.9 GHz) shows mainly decreases at III/IV, whereas pumping from −5/2 to −3/2 should give increases at I/II; the column-mean subtraction could also shift apparent signs. If peaks are absent where the model predicts them, this matters for the population-transfer picture.

7. **Reading the I/II amplitude ratio as the electron-spin population ratio (Fig. 4) needs justification.** P↑/P↓ is taken from the normalized amplitudes of transitions I and II, which equals the m_s population ratio only if both are driven and read out with equal efficiency. If the drive is hyperfine-enhanced (concern 1), the effective matrix elements differ between the ↓ and ↑ manifolds (enhancement ~1 ± A/2ν_n in opposite senses), and so do saturation levels; continuous ESR driving of |↓,−5/2⟩ ↔ |↑,−5/2⟩ also mixes exactly the two states compared. The trend rests on three V_ESR values with large error bars, and the normalization is not defined. The authors should state the normalization, correct for or bound the matrix-element ratio using the model of concern 1, add V_ESR values if possible, and compare the 6 mV ratio (about 0.63) with the Boltzmann ratio at 0.4 K and 450 mT (≈ 0.62) as a consistency check. Since the ↑/↓ assignment is already supported by the opposite field slopes of I and II in Fig. 3b, the authors could lean on that rather than saying Fig. 4 "removes the ambiguity". The same caveat applies to the explanation of the field dependence of II and IV in Fig. 3a.

8. **The Hamiltonian fit is under-specified and its uncertainties are inconsistent (Suppl. Note 1, main text p. 4, Fig. 3b).** With κ* = −56.7 ± 0.8 MHz and Q = κ/[2I(2I−1)] = κ/20, the uncertainty on Q should be ± 0.04 MHz, not ± 0.8 MHz; with the quoted ± 0.8 MHz (about 30%), the stated "~5% deviation" from the literature is not resolvable and its attribution to a tip-dependent EFG is speculative. Either the uncertainty or the comparison needs correcting, and it is unclear whether the uncertainties are covariance-only or include tip-field uncertainty. The fixed values of A_x = A_y and g_x, g_y are not given, although they control second-order shifts and the mixing in SFig. 1; the transitions and fields entering the fit are not stated, so the data-to-parameter ratio cannot be judged. η = 0 is assumed from four-fold symmetry, while the authors attribute the change in Q to the tip, which (tilted at φ ≈ 5°) breaks that symmetry. 9. **The nuclear g-factor extraction neglects second-order hyperfine (pseudo-nuclear Zeeman) shifts (SFig. 5).** g_N = 0.37 ± 0.04 is obtained from a linear fit of transition I against B_z. Transitions in the m_s = ↓ manifold include second-order terms of order A_⊥²/(4 h f_ESR), about 1–2 MHz at 200 mT and falling roughly as 1/B; their change across 0.2–1.4 T is comparable to the ~3 MHz total shift in SFig. 5, and the first four points (200–450 mT) indeed look flat with a steeper rise above. A linear slope therefore biases g_N and probably explains the 0.37 vs 0.315 discrepancy (about 17%, 1.4σ), which should not be presented as a measured deviation from the bulk value. The tip field (B_tip ≈ 67 mT) is included in the electron Zeeman term but not the nuclear one, and fixing g_N at the literature value "given the discrepancy" is circular if the discrepancy comes from the linear fit. The sign convention should be stated, as g_N for ⁴⁷Ti is negative. The authors should fit I–IV at all fields globally with the full Hamiltonian (g_N free; A_z, A_⊥, Q shared), report the correlation matrix and any remaining discrepancy, drop the separate linear fit, and generate the Fig. 3b lines from that calculation, stating whether the pseudo-nuclear Zeeman term is included.

10. **The NMR linewidths and the coherence outlook are not discussed.** The Lorentzian fits (Figs. 3a, 4) give lines several MHz wide, far broader than any dephasing compatible with seconds-scale T1. Possible causes include power broadening by V_NMR = 20 mV; electron spin flips under tunnelling and continuous ESR drive, which switch the nuclear frequency by ~A_z = 132 MHz (so the m_s-resolved lines have a lifetime set by the electron T1); mixing of |↓,−5/2⟩ and |↑,−5/2⟩ by the ESR drive; and tip-height or tip-field noise. The claim that the first two ESR peaks "equalize" (Fig. 1e) and that this "implies that we drive the nuclear spin sufficiently to saturate" rests on one spectrum. The Conclusion's suggestion that coherence times could reach "the same order of magnitude" as T1 is not supported by anything measured here, since the electron spin flips at kHz–MHz rates under tunnelling. The authors should report fitted widths against field, V_ESR (already in the Fig. 4 data), V_NMR and I_set, show the V_NMR dependence of dip depth and width for transition I, discuss what sets the linewidth, and temper the coherence statement.
