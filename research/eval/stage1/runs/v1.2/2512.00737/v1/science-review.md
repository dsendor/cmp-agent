A. Must address

1. The data do not separate "bi-altermagnetism" from what the known magnetic structure already fixes (Abstract; p. 1 right column; final paragraph of "CD in RIXS and bi-altermagnetism", p. 4). The headline claim is that each Fe sublattice, Oh alone and Td alone, is individually altermagnetic, and that this is a new form of altermagnetism. The paper gives the relativistic magnetic point group (6'mm') but no spin-group (non-relativistic) analysis, although altermagnetism is defined through spin groups. It does not give the Wyckoff positions of each sublattice, or the operation that relates the up and down sites within each sublattice (6_3 screw or c-glide, not translation or inversion). The experimental logic is the larger problem. Both sublattices order at one T_N and are locked together by the strong Oh–Td exchange, so the crystal has a single magnetic point group. Once that group is realised, symmetry allows CD on every excitation of every site, including a site that is only polarised. In the single-ion model, a moment along c breaks the m mirrors on any site whether or not that sublattice is compensated. The authors' own model calls the Td site Van Vleck-type: spin–orbit coupling gives a singlet, and the moment is induced by the Weiss field (p. 5; SI p. 7). Its staggered moment is therefore slaved to the exchange field and is not an independent order parameter. As written, any altermagnet with two inequivalent magnetic Wyckoff positions would be "bi-altermagnetic", and the CD confirms that the known Néel order breaks the m mirrors at both sites rather than showing a property specific to the new classification. The authors sum the up and down sublattices in the model (Methods; SI Sec. 2) but never show them separately, so it is unclear whether the observable is even sensitive to the compensated, alternating arrangement. The authors should: (i) give the spin-group analysis for each sublattice; (ii) state an operational definition of bi-altermagnetism that differs from "an altermagnet with two inequivalent magnetic sites"; (iii) show the computed CD for each sublattice separately and for alternative orderings (parallel spins within the Oh sublattice, as in the field-induced or Zn-doped ferrimagnetic phases of Refs. 35, 36; only one site ordered; L in the ab plane), and say which observed feature rules out each; (iv) identify an observable that would differ between a bi-altermagnet and an altermagnet whose second site is merely polarised (for example distinct temperature dependences of the CD at A and B, a quantitative CD ratio tied to the sublattice order parameters, or independently controllable sublattice orders) and say whether the data test it; (v) say what the RIXS-CD establishes that neutron diffraction has not. If none of this distinguishes the cases, the claim should be reframed as sublattice-resolved detection of mirror-symmetry breaking by the Néel order (site-resolved RIXS-CD of an altermagnet with two magnetic sites), with bi-altermagnetism inferred from the known structure rather than "unveiled".

B. Should address

2. The CD is never quantified and the normalisation is not described (Figs. 2a, 3, S1–S3). Every CD statement rests on overlaid RCP and LCP curves: there are no difference spectra, no normalised asymmetry (I_R − I_L)/(I_R + I_L), no error bars or counting statistics, and no quoted CD magnitude (for example a percentage at A and B, which would also allow comparison with MnTe and CrSb; Fig. 2a and Figs. 3a–d also use different vertical scales). The text says the CD "vanished within the experimental uncertainty" above T_N (p. 3) and "vanishes within the present experimental accuracy" in the m-plane geometry (p. 4, Fig. S1), but the uncertainty is never given. Yet in the m geometry the elastic peaks differ by about 8% (about 95 against 88, Fig. S1 lower panel) and small differences remain at B and near 1 eV, while at 70 K there are visible RCP/LCP differences on the elastic line (Fig. 3d, Fig. S2g). The paper does not say how RCP and LCP were normalised (incident flux, elastic line, or high-energy dd weight; normalising to a feature that itself has CD would bias the result), nor the degree of circular polarisation of each helicity or beam-position stability on helicity switching. Normalisation across temperature also matters: absolute intensities change (feature B is about 20 at 32 K and about 14 at 80 K in Fig. S3; all dd features at 80 K in Fig. S3h are 25–30% weaker), and the shape of the feature-A shoulder changes across T_N (Figs. 3, S2). The authors should show difference or normalised CD spectra with per-bin statistical errors for both geometries and all temperatures, state the normalisation and polarisation degree, separate changes in total spectral weight from changes in CD, and give the m-plane residual as a number with uncertainty beside the m'-plane CD. They should also consider a geometric sign-reversal test (the mirror-conjugate geometry, for example ±angle about specular or a 180° rotation about c), which should reverse the CD in the ordered phase and would exclude helicity-dependent flux or beam-position artifacts that the temperature series alone cannot, since the ordered and paramagnetic measurements differ in time and thermal history.

3. The magnetic domain state is not characterised (RIXS methods; Figs. 2, 3). The two time-reversed domains (L and −L) give CD of opposite sign, as used for domain imaging in MnTe (Refs. 40, 41), so a net CD requires an unbalanced domain population within the beam footprint. The paper does not say how a single or dominant domain was obtained (zero-field cooling, field or magnetoelectric cooling, strain, natural domain size against spot size), and gives no beam size. Because Fe2Mo3O8 is polar, the relative orientation of P and L, and hence the polar domain state, may also set the CD sign. Without this the absolute sign agreement between experiment and simulation (Fig. 2a against 2b,c) has no meaning, since the simulated sign depends on the chosen L, and the magnitude comparison is uncontrolled. The authors should report the cooling protocol, spot size and number of cooldowns; show that the CD sign and magnitude survive repeated cooling through T_N and changes of beam spot; and ideally reverse L deliberately (magnetoelectric cooling with opposite E·H, or field cooling through the ferrimagnetic phase) and show that the CD at A and at B reverses sign together. A common sign flip would be the strongest evidence that both site signals come from one Néel order parameter.

4. The Oh-site assignment of the CD at feature A rests mainly on the model, and the energy region is crowded (pp. 3, 5; Fig. 4; Figs. S4, S6). The Oh half of the bi-altermagnet claim depends on feature A, which sits on the elastic tail at 45 meV total resolution. The Fig. S4 fit places components at about 35 and 70 meV; the calculated Td spin-orbital and exchange-split levels extend to about 20–45 meV for Weiss fields of 3–5 meV (Fig. S6a); magnons at 10–15 meV (Ref. 44) and phonons also lie in this range. Feature A is defined as a broad structure centred near 70 meV, but the CD in Figs. 2a, 3a and S2a spans about 0.03–0.15 eV and overlaps all of these. The statement that the Td spin–orbit excitation "lies well below 50 meV and does not yield significant CD" (p. 3) is a calculated statement, and the Td simulations of Fig. S6 are not shown or discussed at low energy. The paper itself notes (p. 2; Figs. 1e,f) that the Oh and Td resonances occur at different incident energies, which offers a direct test. The authors should measure a CD (RCP − LCP) map over about 703–710 eV, matching the RCP map of Fig. 4b,c, and compare it with computed Oh and Td CD maps, to show that the dichroic weight at A resonates at the Oh energy. They should also fit RCP and LCP separately with the Fig. S4 model to show which component carries the dichroism, give the integrated CD of A in a window excluding energies below 50 meV, show the calculated Td-site CD in the 0–0.15 eV window, and state the elastic-line CD.

5. The comparison between model and experiment is qualitative, and the one domain-independent comparison is not shown (Figs. 2a–c, S6, S7; Methods). Measured and calculated difference spectra are never shown, and the Oh and Td contributions are shown separately, never as a weighted, resolution-convolved sum overlaid on the data. The relative sign and magnitude of the CD at A (Oh) and B (Td) are fixed, independently of domains, by the antiparallel Oh and Td moments within a layer; in the data LCP exceeds RCP at both A (about 0.07 eV) and B (about 0.43 eV), but at the plotted scale it cannot be read from Fig. 2b,c whether the model gives the same relative sign. The line shape (sign changes within B) and the ratio CD(A)/CD(B) are not compared. The paper does not say how the Oh-up/Td-down pairing of the real structure, the orientation of the local axes of the up and down sublattices (related by the 6_3 screw, which does not preserve the scattering geometry), the incident and scattered angles, or the Q of Fig. 2 enter the simulation. The Weiss field acts only on spin (SI p. 6) although the paper stresses an unquenched orbital moment, and the lifetime broadening Γ = 0.3 eV is fixed; the SI says only that the "presence or absence" of CD is insensitive to ζ. Without this, "the simulations reproduce the experimentally observed CD" amounts to "both show some CD", which symmetry alone guarantees. The authors should overlay measured and calculated CD difference spectra for A, B and C on the same axes, for both geometries (the model should also give zero in the m-plane geometry); state the relative A/B sign in theory and experiment with the sublattice orientation specified; give CD(A)/CD(B); and show how CD magnitudes and line shapes depend on Weiss field, Γ and ζ.

6. The size of the Weiss field is chosen after the fact (SI p. 7: "around 3–5 meV, which appears to be a reasonable value"). The magnitude and line shape of the calculated CD and the induced Van Vleck moment at the Td site depend strongly on this parameter (Fig. S6), but it is not tied to the known exchange constants, the magnon bandwidth (Ref. 44) or T_N; a mean-field estimate from T_N = 60 K and the magnon bandwidth suggests exchange fields well above this range. The authors should justify the value from independent data (published exchange constants, a mean-field estimate from T_N), compare the resulting ordered moments (for example (S_c, L_c) = (1.67, 0.35) at Td for 3 meV) with the Oh and Td moments from neutron diffraction, and show how the integrated CD at A and B scales with the Weiss field across the allowed range and compare it with the measured magnitude.

7. The temperature dependence of the CD is not quantified, and the sample temperature is not calibrated (Fig. 3; Figs. S2, S3). The claim that the CD tracks the order parameter rests on overlaid spectra judged by eye. The CD is already nearly gone at 55 K (Figs. S2d, S3d), and weakens sharply between 50 and 55 K, below the nominal T_N = 60 K; this could reflect beam heating or a difference between sample and sensor temperature. The lowest temperature, 32 K, is also not in the saturated regime. The authors should plot the integrated CD of A and of B against T with error bars from counting statistics and repeat scans, compare it with the sublattice magnetisation from neutron diffraction or susceptibility (or (1 − T/T_N)^(2β)), since the CD should be odd and to first order linear in L, and say whether the Oh and Td features follow the same temperature dependence, as a single order parameter requires and as a test of the site-resolved claim. They should state the temperature calibration and estimate beam heating.

8. The 2:3 energy-ratio claim does not match the paper's own level scheme or fits (p. 5 left column; Fig. 4a; Fig. S4d). The text says the two low-energy components have "energy ratio close to 2:3—consistent with the prediction of the zero-order approximation". In the first-order scheme of Fig. 4a the intervals are ζ/2 and 3ζ/4 (2:3), so transitions from the J_eff = 1 ground state lie at ζ/2 and 5ζ/4 (2:5). The Fig. 4a caption puts the quintets at about 24–26 meV and septets at about 58–65 meV (ratio about 0.4, consistent with 2:5). The fitted peaks in Fig. S4d (about 31–38 and 68–73 meV) give an energy ratio of about 1:2, with intervals about 1:1. Neither is 2:3. The text also says "zero-order" while Fig. 4a shows first- and second-order levels, the main text uses ζ = 52 meV (atomic) while the simulations use ζ = 65 meV (SI criterion C2), and the SI calls the momentum dependence "minimal" although peak 1 disperses by about 7 meV between L = 0.7 and 0.9 (Fig. S4d). The fit itself (a free Voigt elastic peak plus two Lorentzians for a structureless shoulder, with the ~35 meV component within one resolution width of the elastic line) has many free parameters and no uncertainties. The authors should state which ratio is meant and correct the argument, compare the fitted energies with the full calculation including Weiss field and trigonal distortion (Fig. S7a), give fit uncertainties and constraints and the robustness of the two-peak decomposition, say whether the data favour ζ = 52 or 65 meV and use one value throughout, make the terminology consistent, and avoid calling the agreement quantitative.

9. The CD calculated at feature C is not observed, and the explanation is untested (p. 4, "Note that feature C …"; Fig. 2b; Figs. S6f–h). The Oh simulation predicts visible CD at the about 1 eV feature, and the experiment shows none. The authors attribute this to large lifetime broadening, but only the intermediate-state Γ = 0.3 eV is quoted and the final-state broadening is not given. Since C belongs to the same Oh ion as A, the discrepancy bears on the site-resolved interpretation. The authors should compute the CD with a physically justified final-state broadening for the 5E and 3T1 states, convolved with the observed resolution, and show whether the CD at C then falls below the experimental noise.

11. A ferrimagnetic or net-moment contribution is not explicitly excluded (Methods, crystal growth). Fe2Mo3O8 lies close to a ferrimagnetic phase induced by modest c-axis fields or small composition changes (Refs. 35, 36), with a net moment and an ordering temperature similar to T_N, in which conventional XMCD-type dichroism would appear below about 60 K. The m-geometry null argues against this, since in a ferrimagnetic 6m'm' state all vertical mirrors become primed and CD would appear in both geometries, but that control exists at only one temperature and Q and is not quantified. The authors should show M(T) and M(H) for the measured crystal to confirm a compensated ground state, give the Fe:Mo stoichiometry (EDX or refinement), and ideally show circularly polarised XAS in the same geometry, where 6'mm' requires zero XMCD.

13. The claim of rare "active orbital magnetism" is model-based and overstated (p. 5, end of results; SI p. 7). The paper calls Fe2Mo3O8 "a rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism". Rutile-type FeF2 and CoF2, widely discussed as altermagnets, host Fe2+/Co2+ with unquenched orbital moments. The orbital moment of about 20% of the spin moment comes only from the ionic model with an assumed Weiss field, and the SI quotes it for the Td site while the main text discusses orbital magnetism generally; no measurement here isolates it. The main-text paragraph on p. 5 also moves from the Td site to "a singlet ground state A1" in a way that is hard to follow. The authors should temper the claim, say which site each statement and the 20% figure refer to, and check the model moments against independent evidence (neutron ordered moments, susceptibility anisotropy, g-factors from ESR or THz, or XMCD sum rules in the field-induced ferrimagnetic phase), or present the statement as a model inference.

14. The momentum and geometry dependence of the CD is not measured (pp. 4–5). The authors state that the CD "arises predominantly from the wave-vector dependence of the polarization (dipole) operators rather than momentum dependence of the final-state wave functions", but all CD data are taken at a single, nearly specular Q. The local-model picture predicts a specific dependence on incident and scattered angles within the m' plane.
