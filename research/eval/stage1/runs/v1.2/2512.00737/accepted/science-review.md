A. Must address

1. **The CD and the "null" results are never quantified, and the m-plane null is not clean in the data shown.** The paper states that the CD "vanished within the experimental uncertainty" above T_N (p. 4) and is absent "within the present experimental accuracy" in the m geometry, and that the m'/m contrast, "rather than the mere onset of CD below T_N, identifies the observed signal as a signature of bi-altermagnetism". No uncertainty is ever defined: every CD figure (Figs. 2, 3, S2, S3, S4) overlays RCP and LCP spectra, with no I_RCP − I_LCP difference spectra, no counting-statistics error bars, no repeat scans and no integrated CD. The panels raise doubts. In the lower panel of Fig. S2 (m plane, 32 K) the elastic/quasi-elastic peak differs by about 8% between LCP (~95 counts) and RCP (~88), and there are visible differences near 0.45–0.55 eV and 1.6–2.0 eV; the elastic-line difference is comparable to the m'-plane CD on feature A (Fig. 2a, Fig. 3a) and the 0.45 eV differences to the m'-plane CD on feature B. By contrast, the Fig. 2a inset, described as the corresponding m-plane spectrum, shows essentially overlapping curves; it is not stated whether it is the same data as Fig. S2 or was processed differently. The m-plane null is also shown only at 32 K and at a Q that is not given. This matters because the m-plane null is the main evidence that separates the proposed state from a ferrimagnetic state, which the authors' own simulations (Fig. S9) predict gives a larger CD that is allowed in the m geometry; if the "null" contains differences of similar size to the signal, the data no longer discriminate between a bi-altermagnet, a ferrimagnet or a mixed state. The authors should: plot difference spectra with error bars (Poisson statistics plus the spread over repeated acquisitions, e.g. alternating RCP, LCP, RCP) for both geometries at the same Q, at 32 K and above T_N; report the integrated CD or asymmetry (I_R − I_L)/(I_R + I_L) of features A and B with uncertainties for m' and m; plot it against temperature for all eight temperatures (Figs. S3, S4) against an order-parameter form, the neutron sublattice moment, or the T_N of the measured crystal, since the link to the order parameter is currently made by eye; give a quantitative upper bound on the m-plane CD, ideally at several temperatures; and explain the elastic-line asymmetry in the m geometry.

B. Should address

2. **The Néel-domain state is not characterized, and no measurement shows that the CD is odd in the order parameter.** In 6'mm' with L ∥ c, the two Néel domains (L and −L) are related by time reversal (and by the 6_3 operation) and must give CD of opposite sign in the m' geometry, as the authors themselves noted for MnTe in Ref. 40. Equal domain populations within the ~386 × 30 µm² beam footprint (SI Sec. 2) would cancel the CD, so a finite CD implies a domain imbalance whose origin is never discussed. The paper does not state the cooling protocol (zero field, magnetic field, magnetoelectric cooling), how many crystals, cool-downs and beam positions were measured, or whether the CD sign and magnitude repeat after thermal cycling through T_N. Fig. 2a and Fig. 3a are both at 32 K but at different Q and θ, and it is not said whether they come from separate cool-downs; all data appear to come from one crystal (Fig. S1), and because the m and m' geometries require an azimuthal rotation, they may probe different spots with different domain populations. Consequences: the CD magnitude, and the temperature dependence in Fig. 3, may reflect domain fractions rather than the order parameter; the sign agreement between experiment and simulation (Fig. 2a versus 2b,c) carries no information without a known domain; and there is no test that the dichroism reverses with the magnetic order, which is the cleanest way to exclude instrumental origins. The authors should state the cooling protocol and the number of crystals, cool-downs and positions; show repeat measurements of the 32 K m'-plane CD after thermal cycling and at two or more sample positions; discuss how the polar axis (±c) combines with the Néel domain to set the CD sign; ideally use magnetoelectric field cooling (parallel E and H along c, below the metamagnetic field; Fe2Mo3O8 has a large linear magnetoelectric effect, Refs. 9, 35, 36) to select a domain and show that the CD on A and B reverses together; and compare with the model the relative sign of the CD on features A and B, which is domain-independent and tied by the model to the antiparallel Oh and Td moments. If domain control is not possible, the absolute sign comparison with theory should be removed and domain averaging discussed.

3. **Ferrimagnetic contamination of the probed volume is not excluded, and the measured crystal is not characterized magnetically.** The sample is characterized only by powder XRD and Laue diffraction (SI Sec. 1); T_N = 60 K is taken from the literature, and no magnetization, susceptibility or specific-heat data are shown for the crystal measured. Fe2Mo3O8 sits close to a ferrimagnetic phase stabilized by small Zn substitution, off-stoichiometry or modest fields (Refs. 35, 36). Soft-X-ray RIXS probes ~100 nm of an HCl-cleaned, ex-situ surface where off-stoichiometry or strain could favour ferrimagnetism locally, and since the ferrimagnetic CD is "significantly stronger" than the altermagnetic one (Fig. S9), a small ferrimagnetic fraction could produce a sizable part of the m'-plane CD while giving m-plane CD below the sensitivity reached here. The authors should show M(T) at low field and M(H) along c for the same crystal, bounding any net moment and fixing T_N; measure Fe L-edge XAS-XMCD (e.g. fluorescence yield, both helicities) in the RIXS geometry at 32 K, which is forbidden for 6'mm' but allowed for the ferrimagnet and so directly tests contamination at the relevant depth (or explain why it was not taken); combine this with the quantitative m-plane bound of concern 1 to put an upper limit on the ferrimagnetic fraction; and, if available, measure a ferrimagnetic reference (Zn-substituted (Fe,Zn)2Mo3O8 or the field-induced phase) under matched conditions to test the predicted change in selection rules.

4. **The evidence for symmetry breaking on the Oh sublattice rests on feature A, the least clean feature, while the clean Oh feature C disagrees with the model.** Feature A (~70 meV) lies on the tail of an elastic line measured at 45 meV resolution, in a region that also contains electromagnons (4–10 meV), magnons (11–14 meV), phonons (SI Sec. 3.2) and the Td spin-orbital levels, which Fig. S7a places at ~5–40 meV for Weiss fields of 3–5 meV; the fitted peak_1 in Fig. S5 lies at 30–38 meV. Chiral magnons in altermagnets also carry RIXS-CD (Refs. 26, 27, 45), and the paper notes CD differences at the quasi-elastic peak (Fig. S3a,b). The argument that collective modes are "unlikely" (p. 5) rests on RCP-only fits along (0,0,L) with ~±10 meV error bars (Fig. S5d), whereas the CD was measured at in-plane components of only 0.03–0.05 r.l.u., so the small-dispersion claim does not cover in-plane momentum. Meanwhile the well-separated Oh feature C (~1.0 eV) shows no measurable CD (p. 4), although the model predicts clear CD there (Fig. 2b; Figs. S7f–h, S9h); the explanation, "large lifetime broadening and the character of the corresponding wave functions", is not demonstrated, and the model does not include that broadening. One ambiguous Oh feature agrees with the model and one clean Oh feature does not, which weakens the claim that both sublattices are seen to break the symmetry. The authors should fit the RCP and LCP spectra of feature A with the same constrained decomposition as in Fig. S5, with the elastic line fixed from the carbon-tape reference, and show which component carries the CD; show the model's Td contribution to the CD in the 0–100 meV window so that a Td origin of the A-region CD can be excluded; address whether magnons or electromagnons could contribute CD through the resolution tail; recompute the Oh CD at C with a final-state broadening matched to the measured width of C and show whether it drops below the noise; and either measure CD at larger in-plane Q or soften the statement about collective modes.

5. **What "bi-altermagnetism" means operationally, and what the RIXS data add to it, is not established.** The m/m' selection rules are properties of the global magnetic point group 6'mm', which already constrains both sites and also follows from earlier neutron results. In this non-centrosymmetric structure a compensated pair of moments related by 6_3 cannot be related by inversion, so each compensated Wyckoff orbit is altermagnetic by construction; "bi-altermagnetism" may therefore follow from the known magnetic structure and space group rather than from the measurement. Moreover the authors' model makes the Td moment a Van Vleck moment induced by the exchange (Weiss) field (p. 5, SI p. 6), so in what sense the Td sublattice is "independently" altermagnetic is unclear. Since the novelty claim in the title and abstract relies on this, the authors should define bi-altermagnetism in spin-group terms; state which alternative ordered states, compatible with zero net moment, the site-resolved data exclude (e.g. Td moments disordered, only one sublattice compensated by a rotation, or Td moments parallel within the unit while Oh moments are antiparallel) and show with the model that these give a measurably different CD pattern at A and B in the two geometries; soften the sentence that the symmetry-dependent response "identifies the observed signal as a signature of bi-altermagnetism" and "the realization of bi-altermagnetism" toward site-resolved detection of time-reversal symmetry breaking at both Fe sites; and, if possible, say what new physics bi-altermagnetism predicts (e.g. separate spin-split bands or anomalous responses per sublattice).

6. **The model comparison is qualitative, although described as quantitative, and the Weiss field and lifetime are free choices.** Page 5 states that "the measured CD is quantitatively reproduced by an atomic (impurity) model", but the Weiss field is set to "3–5 meV, which appears to be a reasonable value" (SI p. 6) without connection to exchange constants, T_N, magnon energies (10–15 meV) or neutron ordered moments; the value used in Figs. 1f, 2b and 2c is not stated; the field is restricted to a spin-only form along c although the authors note it could be more general (SI p. 5); and Γ = 0.3 eV is fixed without justification even though the RIXS CD arises from the iΓ term (p. 3). The CD amplitude depends on the Weiss field (Fig. S7), and the statement that the ferrimagnetic CD is "significantly stronger" (Fig. S9) uses the same 3 meV choice. No CD magnitudes or A:B ratios are compared between experiment and calculation. The final-state broadening and the incident energy used in the simulations are also not given. The authors should fix or bound the Weiss field independently (e.g. mean-field exchange from neutron-derived J values, Ref. 47, consistent with T_N = 60 K) and compare calculated Td and Oh ordered moments with neutron values; show the calculated CD of A, B and C versus Weiss field and Γ; give measured and calculated asymmetries (I_R − I_L)/(I_R + I_L) for A, B and C side by side and overlay difference spectra on a common broadened scale; show that the m-geometry CD expected for a ferrimagnet exceeds the experimental bound for every plausible Weiss field; state all simulation parameters; and otherwise replace "quantitatively" with "qualitatively".

7. **The spin-orbital assignment of feature A and the quoted 2:3 level spacing do not match the numbers.** The main text (p. 5) says the two-component fit gives "an energy spacing [that] follows a 2:3 ratio (see Fig. S5)". The fitted peaks in Fig. S5d are at ~31–39 meV and ~68–73 meV, so the successive spacings (~33–35 and ~35–37 meV) are close to 1:1, and the absolute positions are ~1:2; 2:3 holds only if the first spacing is measured from the elastic line and the J_eff = 1 ground level is assumed unsplit. The SOC constant is used inconsistently: Fig. 4a uses ζ = 52 meV, giving quintet levels at 23.7–25.6 meV and septet levels at 58–65 meV (neither matching ~35 and ~70 meV), while the simulations use ξ = 65 meV (SI C2), which would put the first-order levels near 33 and 81 meV. The fit (a Voigt, two Lorentzians with free widths and a linear background, on a shoulder ~1.5 resolution widths from the elastic line, at 45 meV resolution) is called a "constrained minimal decomposition" by the authors (SI Sec. 3.2) and cannot decide between level schemes; the trigonal distortion (Δ_tri = ±30 meV) is comparable to the SOC splitting, so the J_eff picture is only approximate. The authors should correct or rephrase the 2:3 statement, including the effect of the Weiss field and trigonal distortion on the J_eff levels (Fig. S8a); state which ζ applies where; and report fitted widths, which parameters were fixed, parameter correlations, or a comparison of one-, two- and three-component models.

8. **The incident-energy dependence of the CD is not shown, although the site assignment relies on resonance energy.** The text argues that Td and Oh excitations resonate at different incident energies (p. 3; Fig. 1e,f; Fig. S6), but all CD data are taken at a single incident energy of 706 eV, and Fig. 4b,c are RCP only. A CD map (RCP − LCP versus incident energy across the L3 edge and energy loss), compared with calculated per-site CD maps, would make the site attribution independent of the energy-loss assignment, rule out CD on a nominal Td feature that actually carries Oh weight (or the reverse), show whether the CD in the A region follows the Oh or Td resonance, and distinguish a local multiplet from a collective mode, which should resonate differently. The authors should measure and present this for features A and B.

9. **Polarization-switching artifacts are not bounded.** The CD is obtained by switching the EPU between helicities, with a quoted phase error of ~3.5°, polarization degree above 98% and an LCP/RCP flux ratio of 1.1 that is normalized away. A residual linear component that differs between modes, multiplied by magnetic linear dichroism (which switches on at T_N and could be strong in this hexagonal polar crystal), or a beam position or footprint change between modes, could produce a temperature-dependent "CD"; the paramagnetic null does not exclude the first, since linear dichroism also vanishes above T_N. The authors should measure LH and LV RIXS at 32 K and above T_N in the m' geometry to estimate the leakage into RCP − LCP, and record helicity-resolved fluorescence-yield XAS in the same geometry at 32 K, where a vanishing XMCD (forbidden by 6'mm', p. 4) would validate the helicity switching.

10. **The normalization of the spectra needs justification, and intensities shift between temperatures by more than the CD.** LCP and RCP spectra are normalized to the 5–7 eV loss region (SI Sec. 3.1), which could itself carry polarization- or temperature-dependent weight (fluorescence or charge-transfer tails) in the ordered phase. Overall intensities change between temperatures: the elastic peak is ~80 at 50 K (Fig. S3c) against ~90–97 elsewhere, and feature B drops from ~20–22 to ~15 at 80 K (Fig. S4g→h; dd intensities ~30% lower), suggesting changes in normalization, alignment or sample position larger than the CD itself. The authors should show that the normalization window carries no CD, show raw I0-normalized spectra, explain the intensity changes, and confirm that the CD is unchanged when normalized to the dd intensity or to I0.

11. **Only two scattering geometries were tested; the azimuthal symmetry test is missing.** In 6'mm', a 60° rotation about c maps one m' plane onto another via 6' (which contains T), so the CD should reverse sign under a 60° rotation and be unchanged under 120°, whereas no such reversal is expected for the ferrimagnetic group 6m'm'. This test is specific to altermagnetic order, largely insensitive to normalization artifacts, and also constrains the domain population. The authors should measure the CD of A and B at azimuths φ and φ + 60° within one domain region, and ideally a full azimuthal pattern between neighbouring m' and m planes compared with the model; grazing-incidence versus grazing-emission measurements would further test the selection rules.

13. **The Td Van Vleck moment and "active orbital magnetism" claims rest on the unconstrained model.** The statements that the Td moment is Van Vleck-type, that its orbital moment is "about 20% of the spin moment", and that Fe2Mo3O8 is "a rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism" (pp. 5–6) come from the ionic model with a spin-only Weiss field at an assumed value; (S_c, L_c) varies from (1.29, 0.30) to (1.80, 0.35) between 1 and 5 meV (SI p. 6). No measurement in the paper constrains the orbital moment, and the CD was not fitted to obtain it. The authors should either support the claim independently (compare total Td and Oh moments with published neutron ordered moments, XMCD sum rules in the field-induced ferrimagnetic phase, or literature g-factor or form-factor data) or present it as a model prediction.
