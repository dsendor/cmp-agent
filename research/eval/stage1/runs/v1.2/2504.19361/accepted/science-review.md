A. Must address

1. Trapped flux and the sample's magnetic history are not excluded as the source of the TRSB signal and the zero-field diode (Fig. 2, Figs. S5, S6, Supplementary Note S7). The rings sit between Sr2RuO4 banks several micrometres across (Figs. 1b, S1), 250–700 nm thick, in perpendicular field. Supplementary Note S7 states that the remanent field of the superconducting magnet is neither nulled nor measured, so the samples are cooled through Tc in an unknown field, and a fraction of a millitesla is enough to trap vortices in the banks. Field scans span ±10 mT, and in-plane fields of 10–70 mT are applied in other experiments (Figs. 3, 4, S16, S17), which can also leave pinned flux. A vortex pinned in a bank, neck or arm near a weak link is itself a TRS-breaking object: it is a known source of field-free Josephson diode behaviour and of distortions of Ic(H) that violate Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)|. Its effect is not a uniform field offset, so the H0 symmetrisation does not remove it. Magnetic moments in the Ga-damaged edge layer or the SiOx cap are a second, smaller extrinsic possibility. The manuscript does not record the magnetic history before each Ic(H) dataset (zero-field-cooled or not, previous maximum field, magnet reset), and never says whether the sign of the diode survived thermal cycling, even though the text says the bulk domain configuration is random while the wall is deterministically pinned. The signs already differ between devices: ΔI_TRSB(0) ≈ +4 µA in Ring 1 (Fig. 2b) and η < 0 in Ring 2 (Fig. S6). Because the headline claim is intrinsic, spontaneous TRSB, this scenario must be ruled out. The authors should null the cooling field independently (on-chip Hall sensor, co-mounted reference SQUID, or a magnet reset/quench protocol); repeat zero-field cooldowns from above Tc several times and report the sign and magnitude of η(H = 0) and ΔI_TRSB for each cooldown and each device (random sign with roughly fixed magnitude supports spontaneous chiral domains; a signal that vanishes with a nulled cooling field, or tracks history, points to trapped flux); field-cool in small controlled ±Hz and show how η depends on the cooling field, since sign reversal under training would be the most direct link to chirality, while vortex trapping should set in at a threshold of order Φ0 divided by the bank area; and show that η is unchanged after a field excursion below Tc.

2. The point-symmetry test is not done under time-reversed protocols, and the zero of field is fixed by minimising the very asymmetry being tested (Fig. 2, Supplementary Notes S2 and S7, Eqs. 9–10). The identity Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| holds only if the two branches are taken under protocols related by time reversal; with any history dependence (trapped flux in the banks, fluxoid-state hysteresis, or the mobile domains the authors invoke), the time-reverse of an up-sweep at −H is a down-sweep at +H. The paper does not say whether Ic+ and Ic- at each field come from the same sweep or from separate sweeps, nor the sweep direction of each, and does not show ΔI_TRSB for both directions; the statement that the narrow lobe is independent of sweep direction (p. 8) is not shown. H0 is then chosen to minimise ∫[Ic+(H) − |Ic-(−H)|]², which removes a uniform shift by construction but cannot remove a sweep-dependent offset, a field-dependent flux-focusing nonlinearity, or magnet hysteresis. In Figs. 2b and 2c, ΔI_TRSB ramps across each lobe and spikes or jumps at the lobe minima where |dIc/dH| is largest, which is the sawtooth a residual field displacement between the compared branches produces (ΔI ≈ δH·dIc/dH). The procedure has two further consequences. It is blind by construction to a φ0-like phase offset of the domain-wall junctions (Supplementary Note S5, Fig. S13b), which shifts the whole pattern like H0, so the main prediction of the invoked mechanism is subtracted away before comparison. And because an asymmetric SQUID has an ordinary diode Ic+(H) ≠ |Ic-(H)| at any nonzero true field, a mislocated zero turns a field-induced diode into an apparent zero-field one; the "zero-field" diode is defined relative to a fitted origin rather than measured. The fitted H0 values, their uncertainties and their variation between sweeps, rings and cooldowns are not reported for any Sr2RuO4 dataset. The authors should state the sweep protocol and whether Ic+ and Ic- come from the same IV trace; show ΔI_TRSB for up- and down-sweeps and for the time-reversed pairing (up-sweep Ic+(H) against down-sweep |Ic-(−H)| and vice versa); report H0 with its confidence interval for every dataset and whether it agrees between sweeps and between rings in the same cooldown; show the uncorrected data alongside the corrected data; plot or fit ΔI_TRSB(H) against a·dIc/dH + b (plus a field-dependent offset) and show the residual; and fix H0 independently of the tested data, with a Hall sensor or a TRS-preserving reference SQUID on the same chip and in the same field history, analysed identically and shown to give ΔI_TRSB = 0.

3. The error budget behind ΔI_TRSB and η counts only voltage noise, while known systematics are of the same order as the signal (Fig. 2b–c, Figs. S5b, S6, Supplementary Note S2, Eq. 1). ΔI_TRSB is a few µA on Ic of 60–140 µA, and |η| in Ring 2 is 2–3.7% from three points without error bars (Fig. S6). Section S2 states that the parametric bootstrap "only accounts for the uncertainty in measured voltage"; it omits switching-current stochasticity, the uncertainty in H0 and in the field axis (which matters most on the steep flanks where ΔI_TRSB spikes), temperature drift, and the threshold choice, which is 0.1 or 0.2 I0RN "depending on the degree of rounding" without stating which was used for each panel. With RN ≈ 20–100 mΩ that threshold is only about 0.1–1 µV on visibly rounded IV curves (Fig. 1c). The authors' own control SQUID shows a drift-induced ΔI of about 1 µA on about 78 µA, about 1.3% (Fig. S10), comparable to the Ring 2 diode, which is measured at up to 1000 mK, close to Tc ≈ 1.35 K where dIc/dT is large. The MoGe/Ag control passing the same pipeline does show the analysis does not itself manufacture asymmetry, but the uncertainty on the Sr2RuO4 numbers is still understated. The authors should repeat the same field scan several times to give an empirical run-to-run spread of ΔI_TRSB; show η(0) and ΔI_TRSB(H) for both thresholds and for a threshold-free definition (dV/dI peak, or a fit of each branch to a rounded-RSJ form); propagate the H0 interval; report thermometer stability during each map; and fold these systematics into the error bars of Fig. 2 and Fig. S6.

4. A voltage even in the bias current, or the order in which the two polarities are measured, could produce a field-independent offset between Ic+ and |Ic-| (Figs. 2c, S6, Supplementary Note S2). In Ring 2 the violation is mostly a field-independent vertical offset of roughly 5 µA. The bias current runs through Ti/Au leads, and the offset correction subtracts only the mean voltage within a few µA of zero. Any voltage even in current, such as a thermoelectric voltage from I² heating of leads or contacts at currents above 100 µA, survives that correction; it adds to the junction voltage on one polarity and subtracts on the other, shifting Ic+ and |Ic-| in opposite directions by a field-independent amount, which is the pattern in Fig. 2c. Separately, if the current runs 0 → +I → 0 → −I, the switching event on one polarity can change the state before the other is measured. The measurement circuit (contact resistances, filtering, the four-probe configuration within the ring, current source and voltmeter) is not described, so the size of such effects cannot be judged. The authors should show the even-in-current component [V(I) + V(−I)]/2 below Ic, state the acquisition order and show that reversing it leaves ΔI_TRSB unchanged, and describe the measurement circuit.

6. Conventional flux focusing and vortex entry in the Sr2RuO4 banks can produce the narrowed, skewed central lobe followed by recovery of Φ0 periodicity, and the narrow lobe occurs without TRSB (Fig. 2a, Figs. S5, S11). At low field the Meissner banks focus flux into the loop, enlarging the effective area and narrowing the lobes; once vortices enter the banks, which can happen at a few mT given the small Hc1 and the strong demagnetisation of a flake in perpendicular field, the focusing changes and the period relaxes. Pinned vortices also make the pattern history-dependent. The authors' own data weaken the link between the anomalous lobe and TRSB: Ring 3 at 1200 mK is described as showing "normal SQUID behavior" with no significant TRSB (Fig. S5a), yet its central lobe is already narrowed to 0.82 Φ0 against 0.92–0.93 Φ0 for its neighbours. The authors should measure Ic(H) for both sweep directions and for minor loops with varying maximum field (vortex entry into the banks is hysteretic, whereas domain-wall motion confined by the necks would be largely reversible), tabulate central-lobe width together with ΔI_TRSB against temperature for each ring, and fit a model including flux focusing as an alternative. Applying the full TRSB and diode analysis to the singly connected Ring 2 (Figs. S2–S3), which appears to show unequal Ic+ and |Ic-| at zero field, would remove the inductance, fluxoid and flux-focusing ambiguities.

7. The half-integer Shapiro steps in the low-Ic state have conventional explanations that the paper itself acknowledges but does not exclude for Fig. 4 (Fig. 4, Figs. S15, S16). In a two-junction loop near half a flux quantum the first harmonic of the effective current-phase relation is suppressed and half-integer steps appear; Fig. S16 states this for steps near the Ic(B) minima. Field alignment accuracy is never stated, and a 1° misalignment of the 10 mT in-plane field gives about 0.2 mT along c, a sizeable fraction of the about 4–5 mT period of Ring 1, so the low-Ic state may simply sit nearer a SQUID minimum, or reflect a different trapped-flux configuration. An in-plane field of 10–20 mT also threads roughly one flux quantum through the arm cross-section (0.25–0.7 µm thickness times about 2λ plus the wall width), so the junctions are Fraunhofer-modulated; Fig. S16 finds nodes near 4.5 and 9.9 mT, and Fig. 4 is taken at 10 mT, close to such a node though at a different angle. At 144–145 MHz, hf/2e ≈ 0.3 µV against IcRN ≈ 1.4 µV for Ring 1 gives a reduced frequency of about 0.2, a regime where capacitive or inductive dynamics generate subharmonic steps even with a sinusoidal current-phase relation. The claim that λJ exceeds the arm width is asserted, not calculated. The steps are not reproducible: after resetting the field they disappear from one polarity and an integer step goes missing (Fig. S15). Temperature, frequency and microwave power at the sample for Fig. 4 are not given. The authors should give the perpendicular-field calibration and alignment procedure with its uncertainty; map Ic(Hz) at fixed in-plane field for both Ic states to locate each on the SQUID pattern; map the fractional-step amplitude against in-plane field magnitude and angle, perpendicular trim field, microwave power and frequency, comparing integer and half-integer step widths with RSJ/RCSJ calculations for a SQUID with sinusoidal current-phase relation, finite βL and the estimated flux bias; show that half-steps persist where the first harmonic is not suppressed (at SQUID maxima, away from in-plane nodes); give the λJ estimate; and state the Fig. 4 parameters. Until then, "a different current-phase relation between the two Ic states" should be presented as one possibility.

8. The bistable, current-switchable Ic states are not distinguished from vortex or fluxoid-state switching driven by the training current (Fig. 3, Figs. S14, S17). A supercritical training current exerts a Lorentz force J × B on in-plane vortices (Sr2RuO4 is strongly anisotropic and the crystals are 0.25–0.7 µm thick, so 10–20 mT in-plane penetrates substantially) or, through misalignment, on out-of-plane vortices in the banks and arms; the force reverses with either I or H, matching the observed rule that the roles of the training polarities swap when Hx is reversed (Fig. S14). Fig. S17 shows no hysteresis below 10 mT, consistent with a vortex-entry threshold. The angle map in Fig. S17b is speckled because each pixel is one IV trace; the optimal angle differs between samples (20° for Ring 1, 55° for Ring 2) with no correspondence to geometry, which fits a misalignment-dependent perpendicular component or pinning better than an order-parameter property. If βL is not small, current-induced transitions between fluxoid states of the loop could also give two Ic branches; the modulation depth of about 35% in Fig. 2a requires either strong junction asymmetry or sizeable βL. The λJ argument on p. 11 addresses only the half-integer steps, not the bistability. The authors should quantify field alignment and the out-of-plane component and measure the out-of-plane tilt dependence; estimate the in-plane lower critical and entry fields of banks and arms; report βL and the junction asymmetry; show how the switching threshold depends on in-plane field magnitude and temperature; test persistence of each state when the field is removed and re-applied; map Ic(Hz) in both states (domain-wall reconfiguration and an added vortex should change the pattern differently); test whether bistability survives in the singly connected Ring 2; repeat the angular map to show the optimal angle is reproducible; and explain why the vortex scenario is excluded.

The domain-wall toy model does not produce the headline TRSB, is weakly constrained, has internal inconsistencies and implies spontaneous fields that conflict with other data (Supplementary Note S3, Fig. S11). As written, φD(H) is odd in H about π/2 (Eq. 4), so H → −H with chirality exchange is a symmetry of the model: it gives a narrow central lobe but neither the skew, nor ΔI_TRSB, nor the zero-field diode, although the paper presents these as having a common origin. Eq. 5 gives Φ = Φext + 2Φ_int^max at H = 0 (φD = π/2), contradicting the statement that the domain contributions cancel at zero field; presumably (φD − π/2)/(π/2) was intended. Eq. 3 couples order-parameter amplitudes linearly to B without a stated Ginzburg–Landau functional. The fit uses φm, a, Φ_int^max, αI, βL, a Gaussian envelope width and an amplitude on about five lobes; parameters and posteriors are not listed, Φ_int^max is poorly determined (0.16 +0.13/−0.06 Φ0), and the lobe heights are visibly missed. Fitted Φ_int^max of 0.16–0.24 Φ0 implies mean spontaneous fields of order 1 mT through a 1 µm loop, i.e. chiral currents that scanning-SQUID searches on Sr2RuO4 mesostructures (refs. 28–30) should have detected. 12. Where the junctions are and what kind of weak link they are is not established in this paper (Fig. 1c–d, Figs. S2, S3). Shapiro steps at V = nΦ0f show phase-locked phase slips but not by themselves a localised domain-wall junction: the arms are about 250–350 nm wide, a few times ξ0 = 66 nm, and ξ(T) grows near Tc, so necks and arms can act as Dayem-type constriction weak links or host phase-slip centres, which also give steps; the statement that "Shapiro steps can only originate from the Josephson effect" does not exclude this. The argument for intrinsic domain-wall junctions rests on the comparison with 3K-phase rings in earlier work [23], not on data here. Fig. 1c supports frequency scaling with only two frequencies; Fig. 1d shows step occupancy against power as a histogram without the Bessel-function step-width analysis that would test RSJ-like behaviour. Power is given at the source ("maximum applied power (20 dBm)"), not at the sample; Ic is barely suppressed at maximum power, suggesting weak coupling, and RF heating is not assessed. The Gaussian Ic(B) envelope of the singly connected ring (Fig. S3) is admitted to be unexpected for the clean limit, while the main text argues the junctions are not diffusive. The authors should plot step widths against microwave amplitude with RSJ/Bessel fits, give Ic·RN per junction, estimate the power at the sample and compare zero-RF Ic at different source powers to bound heating, state the physical meaning of the Gaussian envelope or present it as an empirical fit, and ideally add a geometric control (a ring without necks or with necks of varying width). Measuring the current-phase relation directly, for example by embedding the singly connected Ring 2 geometry in an rf-SQUID or asymmetric interferometer, would test the φ0 offset and second harmonic predicted for a chiral domain wall independently of Shapiro-step interpretation.

13. The strain-free assumption behind the pairing-symmetry discussion is not established, and the pairing conclusions go beyond the evidence (Methods; Discussion pp. 14–16; Table S1). The claim that Tc ≤ 1.5 K "demonstrates the absence of any edge-dislocations or strain" only shows that no percolating 3K-phase path exists. The devices are FIB-milled, capped with SiOx or Au, and sit on SrTiO3, so differential thermal contraction on cooling to 50 mK and Ga-implanted edges can leave inhomogeneous strain of order 0.1%, which would barely move Tc but can split the TRSB and superconducting transitions [25] and matters for the accidental-degeneracy scenarios (refs. 61–65) the paper rejects. The Tc spread of 1.3–1.5 K across devices, below the bulk value, may indicate disorder or strain. The authors should soften the strain-free claim or support it with strain estimates or measurements (finite-element thermal-contraction modelling, Raman, X-ray diffraction), discuss whether strain-inhomogeneity models [64, 65] could produce mobile walls, and present dxz + idyz as one possibility among others.
