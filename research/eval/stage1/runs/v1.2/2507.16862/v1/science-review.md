**A. Must address**

1. **A uniform phase histogram at a fixed delay does not show that the initial condensate phase is random; phase diffusion from shot-to-shot frequency and onset jitter has not been excluded.** The phase histograms in Fig. 2a (insets at t = 0.10, 0.90 and 1.25 µs) are taken at fixed times after the trigger, and Sec. 1.2 (pp. 5–6) and the Discussion infer from them that "the uniform phase distribution must be an intrinsic property of the BEC" and that the initial phase "must be independent of the external pumping". But the phase measured at time t is φ₀ + 2π∫[f(t′) − f_LO] dt′ from the moment of formation, and the condensate frequency chirps by about 80–100 MHz, from about 3.42 to 3.33 GHz, after pump-off (Fig. 3a, Ext. Data Fig. 6a,c), which the text attributes to the density-dependent nonlinear shift. The amplitude, and hence the density, varies shot to shot by a factor of 2–3 (compare the three traces in Fig. 2a at 1.25 µs). A spread of only ~1–few MHz in the frequency trajectory over the 0.3–1 µs between formation and the histogram time gives a phase spread of ~2π or more; a few MHz corresponds to only ~3% density fluctuation given the ~90 MHz total shift. Jitter in the condensation onset time, which depends on pump power and the initial thermal population, has the same effect. A uniform histogram is therefore expected whether φ₀ is spontaneous or deterministically set by the pump, and the t = 0.10 µs histogram, where only noise is present, is trivially uniform. The authors should: (a) extract the phase where it is set, for example by fitting each shot's phase trajectory in the free-evolution window (frequency and chirp as free parameters) and back-propagating it to a common reference point such as the time the amplitude crosses a fixed threshold, then test whether the resulting φ₀ distribution is uniform relative to the fit uncertainty; (b) show the shot-to-shot spread of the fitted instantaneous frequency, onset time and accumulated phase ∫Δf dt, and compare the phase spread it implies with the observed one; (c) condition the histogram on amplitude, or plot phase against amplitude and frequency, to test for hidden correlations with the pump; (d) most importantly, run a deterministic control under the same chirp conditions through the same analysis: a pulsed direct (linear or weakly nonlinear) FMR excitation near 3.3–3.4 GHz at amplitudes large enough to produce a comparable nonlinear shift, a weak coherent seed at the band bottom during parametric pumping, or the parametric magnons at f_p/2 = 3.9 GHz (whose phase should be locked to the pump modulo π and give a bimodal histogram; this needs the LO retuned because of the 500 MHz mixer bandwidth). The control should return a narrow (or bimodal) histogram at matched delays and amplitudes. Without one of these, the central claim of a spontaneous, pump-independent phase does not follow.

2. **The shot-to-shot phase stability of the detection chain is asserted but not shown, and the main text contradicts the Extended Data on phase locking.** Section 1.2 (pp. 5–6) and Supplement 7.2 state that "all generation and detection equipment in the experimental setup is phase-locked" and that "all externally induced factors are stable within a few degrees of phase shift"; this is the quantitative basis for attributing the uniform histogram to the condensate. Yet the caption of Ext. Data Fig. 5 says "the oscilloscope was excluded [from the shared 10 MHz reference] due to technical difficulties, but phase stability of the setup as a whole was confirmed in preliminary measurements", and no such data appear. With the oscilloscope off the shared reference, the phase at fixed t depends on trigger and time-base jitter relative to the 100–130 MHz IF: 1 ns of jitter is about 0.7 rad at 110 MHz. Other risks are the start phase of the pulsed pump generator (SG2) relative to the CW 3.2 GHz LO, the unstated relation between the 7.8 GHz pump-pulse onset, the DG645 trigger and the LO, and drift over the acquisition of 1000 shots. Without a positive control, a uniform histogram cannot be distinguished from a timing or synchronization artifact. The authors should show, under identical acquisition (same trigger chain, oscilloscope, analysis and delay), the phase histogram and its circular standard deviation over 1000 shots for at least one signal whose phase is fixed: a weak pulsed ~3.33 GHz tone from a synthesizer locked to the same 10 MHz and triggered by the same pulse generator, sent through the antenna or a bypass, the diplexer, amplifier and mixer chain; the constant 3.77 GHz band in Ext. Data Fig. 6c as an in-situ reference within the same shots, if it originates from a locked source; or the linear FMR response at 3.3 GHz driven directly below the parametric threshold. They should also resolve the contradiction between main text and caption, and state the trigger jitter and the pump–LO phase relation at pulse onset.

**B. Should address**

3. **Random phase and temporal coherence are necessary for a BEC but not sufficient, and the paper gives no direct evidence of thermalization in this geometry.** The abstract says the results "confirm all postulated fundamental properties of quasiparticle condensates", the Fig. 2 caption calls them a "strong indication … result of true BEC", and the Discussion (p. 8) calls phase independence a "necessary feature to prove the formation of a true BEC". But any self-oscillation that builds up from noise breaks the U(1) symmetry and picks a random phase in each realization: a laser or parametric oscillator above threshold, classical-wave (Rayleigh–Jeans) condensation, a kinetic instability transferring magnons directly to the band bottom, four-magnon parametric processes into the k ≈ 0 mode, or Suhl-type instabilities. The paper shows only the 500 MHz IQ band and a spectrum-analyser spectrogram restricted to 3.2–3.9 GHz, said only to "demonstrate the agreement with previous works [41]"; it gives no frequency-resolved occupation compared with a Bose–Einstein distribution, no chemical potential reaching the band bottom, and no critical-density estimate compared with Eq. (1). The BEC interpretation in perpendicularly magnetized YIG has been disputed in Ref. 31 (with Ref. 30), which the authors cite but do not engage with; this matters because the out-of-plane geometry differs from most previous magnon-BEC work. The authors should either supply evidence of thermalized condensation in this sample (time-resolved spectra of the gas near the band bottom across threshold, a critical-density estimate against the injected density) or rely explicitly and quantitatively on the conditions of Ref. 41 (same film, field, pump); discuss Ref. 31 directly; and reframe the phase randomness as consistent with spontaneous symmetry breaking of the k ≈ 0 coherent state rather than as proof of BEC, discussing which observables would differ between BEC, kinetic-instability and laser-like scenarios. Otherwise "confirm all postulated fundamental properties", "proof of randomness" and "true BEC" should be tempered.

4. **The "coherence value" is not calibrated and depends on SNR, chirp and its 100 ns window, so the claims of "no dephasing" and "remanence of coherence" do not follow.** The metric (Sec. 1.3, Sec. 3.2, Figs. 3b,c, 4a, Ext. Data Fig. 6d) is the integral of |autocorrelation| of a "normalized complex signal" in a 100 ns sliding window. It is stated to be zero for "perfect white noise", yet the pre-pump baseline is ~0.17–0.2 and the maximum is ~0.75–0.8, never 1. It is lowered by additive noise, by chirp within the window (which the authors invoke for the dip near 0.9–1.0 µs) and by phase diffusion, and cannot tell these apart; the 100 ns window resolves only ~10 MHz, so any feature narrower than that, including a narrowband incoherent population near the band bottom, scores near perfect coherence. A constant tone or DC offset in I/Q (LO leakage, the 3.77 GHz band, the 3.2–3.3 GHz "shadow") also raises the metric without magnon coherence. The text attributes the late-time decrease to falling SNR (p. 7), while the Fig. 4 caption says coherence "does not depend on the absolute amplitude" and the BEC "doesn't lose its coherent properties during decay", and pp. 7–8 claims it "does not undergo dephasing"; with an SNR-dependent metric these statements cannot be separated from dephasing. The "clearly detectable" coherence at 3.5 µs when the amplitude is "below the noise threshold" rests on ~0.22 versus a baseline of ~0.17–0.18, and averaging 1000 amplitude traces would also detect the signal, so the comparison should be made at equal effective averaging. The authors should give the exact normalization (per point or per window, integration range of Δt, boxcar treatment), whether the reference e^{iω₀t} uses a measured or fixed ω₀, which filtering and artifact removal precede the computation, and how the standard-deviation bands were computed; calibrate the metric with synthetic or measured signals of known linewidth (a pure tone, and a tone with the measured chirp, or a locked tone attenuated to matching amplitudes) plus measured noise as a function of SNR, and place the data on that curve; quantify the 3.5 µs remanence with a significance estimate and show it is not caused by the 3.77 GHz spur or mixer offsets; and explain the finite-window noise floor.

5. **Within-shot dephasing and phase diffusion are not measured, so the coherence time of the condensate is not established.** The "no dephasing" statement (pp. 7–8) rests on a 100 ns sliding-window metric, which cannot test phase stability over microseconds, although the single-shot data allow a direct test. After removing each shot's deterministic frequency evolution (a fitted linear or chirped f(t)), the authors should compute the phase-residual variance ⟨[φ(t+τ) − φ(t)]²⟩ as a function of τ up to the 1–2 µs condensate lifetime, or an SNR-corrected first-order coherence g¹(τ) from the complex traces, or the single-shot linewidth against time from longer windows during free evolution, compared with the Fourier and decay limits. This turns a qualitative statement into a measured coherence time and also bears on concern 1.

6. **The phase statistics are presented only qualitatively, and the informative comparisons are missing.** The uniformity in Fig. 2 rests on three polar histograms "with no apparent clustering", without a statistic or radial scale, and the t = 0.10 µs inset contains only noise, so it is not a control. For 1000 shots the authors should report the mean resultant length |⟨e^{iφ}⟩| (expected ≈ 1/√N ≈ 0.03 for a uniform distribution) and a Rayleigh or Kuiper p-value as a continuous function of time from the first appearance of signal above noise (about 0.55–0.7 µs at 23 and 25 dBm) through pumping, condensation and free evolution, at times earlier than those where a few MHz of jitter could accumulate 2π. This is especially needed during pumping (0–1 µs), when pump-induced locking would be most likely, and a time trace would expose any brief locking near pump-off that three snapshots could miss. Histograms should also be shown at matched times for a below-threshold power (20 dBm) and for the other powers in Fig. 4, alongside the deterministic reference of concern 2.

7. **When condensation occurs is described inconsistently, and this matters for the phase argument.** Page 5 says "After the pump is switched off, condensation occurs", but Fig. 1b, Fig. 3b and Fig. 4a show the band-bottom signal and coherence rising to ~0.5 at 0.6–0.85 µs, while the pump is still on (at 23 and 25 dBm). This is exactly where any pump-imprinted phase would be most visible. The authors should state clearly when the condensate forms, explain the coherence maximum near 0.85 µs and the dip near 1.0 µs, and present phase histograms at the earliest coherent time, not only at 0.90 and 1.25 µs. A shorter pump pulse, so that condensation occurs entirely after pump-off, would separate the pump's influence on the phase more cleanly.

8. **The condensation threshold is not separated from the parametric instability threshold and is weakly defined.** Fig. 4 shows no signal at 20 dBm, a barely visible change at 21 dBm (called "a condensation threshold" in the caption) and condensation at 22 dBm. If the parametric instability threshold lies near 20–21 dBm, Fig. 4 shows the onset of pumping rather than condensation beyond a critical density, which would point to a direct instability-driven process. The coherence also saturates at ~0.75 from 23 to 25 dBm, and it is unclear whether this ceiling is set by noise, chirp or the condensate; the statement that power above 23 dBm raises amplitude but not coherence is not shown quantitatively. The setup includes a microwave detector on the reflected path (Ext. Data Fig. 5), so the parametric threshold should be available. The authors should state it; show the population of parametric magnons at f_p/2 and of the thermalized gas (spectrum analyser or BLS) at 20–25 dBm; add finer power steps between 20 and 23 dBm and define the threshold from a fit; plot peak condensate amplitude and peak and late-time coherence against pump power relative to the parametric threshold; and state where the quoted power is referenced (generator output, after the +50 dB Amp 1, or at the antenna after the stub tuner, circulator and attenuators).

9. **The paper does not say what phase memory of the pump is expected to survive to the k ≈ 0 mode, so it does not state what a pump-locked scenario would look like.** The pump fixes only the sum of the phases of each ±k magnon pair, with a two-fold (φ_p/2, φ_p/2 + π) degeneracy, and the transfer to k ≈ 0 proceeds through four-magnon scattering. Without a prediction of the histogram expected under pump-inherited versus spontaneous scenarios (bimodal, peaked, or uniform), the measured histogram cannot discriminate between them. The authors should add a short theoretical estimate, ideally a minimal model such as a stochastic Gross–Pitaevskii or kinetic simulation, or a noise-seeded growth model, predicting the phase distribution in both scenarios.

10. **The detection is selective in wavevector and frequency, and the band-bottom identification is not supported by a dispersion calculation.** Section 1.2 states that the "detection path is not sensitive to the parametrically introduced magnons, but only to the thermal gas near the bottom". The 100 µm microstrip couples efficiently only to k ≲ 2π/100 µm, and the filters plus 500 MHz IQ bandwidth pass only ~3.2–3.7 GHz. The rise in the coherence value may therefore partly reflect population moving into the narrow detected window, where any single mode looks monochromatic, rather than growth of coherence within a fixed population. The authors should give the antenna's k-space and frequency transfer function; compute the forward-volume dispersion and band-bottom frequency at 281 mT with the film's measured M_s and anisotropy and compare it with the observed 3.33 GHz; and discuss whether the detected mode is the film's uniform precession or a mode confined under or near the antenna.

12. **The physical origin of the frequency drift and of the coherence dip near pump-off is not established.** On p. 7 the ~90 MHz downward shift and the coherence dip near 1 µs are attributed to a nonlinear shift from magnon–magnon interactions, citing Ref. 39, which concerns the Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction formalism; the Fig. 3 caption instead attributes the drift to recovery of the net magnetization. Microwave heating of the 2.1 µm film at 23–25 dBm, which lowers M_s and shifts the band bottom, and its relaxation could also contribute. For the k = 0 forward-volume mode, f ≈ γ(B − μ₀M_s), so the expected shift is easy to estimate. The authors should estimate the shift from the magnon density and from heating and compare with the observed value. This matters for concern 1, since the shift drives shot-to-shot phase accumulation.

13. **The novelty claim does not account for prior phase-sensitive and spontaneous-coherence work.** The abstract ("Until now, the BEC phase was only revealed in phenomena depending on the spatial phase difference"), p. 2 and the Discussion ("For the first time…", "no direct proof of randomness … has yet been provided") overstate the advance. Ref. 35 (Demidov et al., PRL 100, 047205, 2008), titled "Observation of spontaneous coherence in BEC of magnons", reported this via microwave emission but is cited only for the in-plane geometry; Refs. 24 (Snoke & Girvin) and 10 (Rezende) address the onset of phase coherence; in superfluid ³He-B the homogeneously precessing domain and magnon-BEC "time crystal" states are detected inductively with phase relative to a reference (Ref. 19); phase-resolved studies of photon and polariton condensates have addressed phase randomness over repetitions; and the authors' own Ref. 41 treats the same geometry "toward a coherent BEC". The authors should state precisely what is new (single-shot absolute phase against an external reference in a solid-state magnon system, and its shot-to-shot statistics), explain how this goes beyond linewidth and spectral-narrowing evidence, and revise "first", "proof" and "confirm all postulated fundamental properties" accordingly.

14. **The result rests on one sample and one configuration, and the repetition conditions are not given.** All data come from one 2.1 µm YIG film at 281 mT, one pump frequency (7.8 GHz), one 1 µs pulse length and one antenna.
