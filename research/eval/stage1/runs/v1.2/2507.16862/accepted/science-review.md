A. Must address

1. **A uniform phase histogram at a fixed lab time does not show that the phase is chosen spontaneously at condensation; shot-to-shot jitter in onset time or frequency trajectory would produce the same result.** The Fig. 2a insets, Fig. 2b and the "Phase evolution of the BEC" section (p. 1433: "the uniform phase distribution must be an intrinsic property of the BEC") infer spontaneous phase selection from the uniform histogram at t = 1.25 µs. The phase is read against a 3.2-GHz LO about 0.25 µs after pump-off and roughly 0.3–0.5 µs after the signal first appears. Over that interval the condensate frequency chirps by more than 100 MHz, from about 3.42–3.45 GHz to 3.32 GHz (Fig. 3a; Extended Data Fig. 2a,c), through the nonlinear shift set by the magnon number. The measured phase is therefore φ(t) = φ0 + 2π∫f(t′)dt′. A shot-to-shot spread of only δf ≈ 2–4 MHz in the frequency trajectory (from pump-power noise, magnon-number fluctuations, sample temperature), or an onset-time jitter of about 10 ns, gives a phase spread 2π·δf·Δt larger than 2π, so the histogram would be uniform even if φ0 were fixed deterministically by the pump-pair dynamics. Such variation is plausible near threshold, given the large ensemble spread in amplitude in Fig. 4b. The narrow grey pump-phase sectors do not exclude this, because the pump has fixed frequency and no chirp. The 0.10 µs and 0.90 µs histograms add nothing on this point, since the band-bottom signal is noise-dominated then (Fig. 2a left panels; Fig. 4b) and uniformity is trivially expected. The authors should: (a) report the shot-to-shot distribution of instantaneous frequency, amplitude and onset time at onset and at 1.25 µs, and estimate the phase spread jitter alone would produce; (b) for each shot, remove the accumulated phase 2π∫f dt from a common reference point (for example the time the amplitude first crosses a fixed threshold) and show the histogram of the residual phase; (c) plot the circular spread (mean resultant length R) against time from onset (about 0.6–0.8 µs at 23 dBm) through the plateau, with the 1/√N level marked — under deterministic-plus-jitter R starts near 1 and decays, under spontaneous formation it is near 1/√N from the first coherent cycles; (d) plot each shot's phase against its onset time or integrated amplitude, since jitter-set phases would correlate with these; (e) report R and onset-time distributions at each pump power across threshold (Fig. 4 powers), so phase randomness is seen to appear with condensation; and (f) most convincingly, add a positive control: inject a weak coherent seed at the condensate frequency, phase-locked to the reference, during pumping at several seed powers, and show a narrow histogram that broadens as the seed is reduced. This would establish both the method's sensitivity to a locked phase and the threshold where spontaneous selection takes over. Comparison with a stochastic numerical model of the pumped magnon gas (stochastic Landau–Lifshitz or kinetic/Gross–Pitaevskii with thermal noise) predicting how frequency jitter accumulates into phase would also help.

2. **The phase stability of the reference and acquisition chain is asserted but not shown, and the pump-phase reference is unexplained.** The text says "all externally induced factors are stable within a few degrees of phase shift", yet Extended Data Fig. 1 states that the oscilloscope was not locked to the shared 10-MHz reference "due to technical difficulties" and that phase stability "was confirmed in preliminary measurements", which are not shown. The grey "pump phase" sectors in Fig. 2a, against which the BEC phase is called random, look several tens of degrees wide, contradicting "a few degrees"; they appear only in the first two insets; and the paper does not say how the 7.8-GHz pump phase was measured through a chain that removes the pump (5-GHz and <4-GHz low-pass filters F1/F2, diplexer) and lies outside the 3.2-GHz IQ band. It is also not stated whether the pulse generator's gating of SG2 is synchronous with the 7.8-GHz carrier, or what the oscilloscope trigger jitter is relative to the roughly 120-MHz IF (period about 8 ns). If the reference or trigger jitters by a sizeable fraction of that period, the uniformity claim loses its footing. The authors should inject a CW or pulsed tone near 3.3 GHz, derived from the locked synthesizers, through the identical path (antenna, diplexer, filters, amplifier, IQ mixer, unsynchronised oscilloscope) with the same triggering and repetition rate, and show over 1,000 shots a narrow phase histogram at the same delay as the BEC measurement, with its width in degrees. They should also describe the pump-phase measurement in the Methods and give its spread numerically.

B. Should address

3. **There is no direct evidence that the coherent k ≈ 0 state is a thermalised BEC rather than another noise-seeded coherent instability, and a random phase alone does not single out a BEC.** The title, abstract and Discussion state that the random phase "confirms" BEC and was "required ... to prove the formation of a true BEC". But kinetic instability, cascade four-magnon scattering, parametric self-oscillation and laser-like processes also produce a coherent mode at the band bottom whose phase grows from noise and is random from shot to shot; the paper itself describes the kinetic instability (p. 1432) as directly populating the spectral minimum, and such processes may even carry phase correlations with the pump pairs. The signatures offered for BEC (frequency at the band bottom, threshold, narrow linewidth) are shared with these processes. The paper shows only the signal in the antenna's narrow detection window: no magnon distribution over energy or wavevector, no Bose–Einstein distribution with μ reaching the band bottom, and no thermalisation timescale compared with theory. This matters especially in the perpendicular geometry, where ref. 32 questions BEC and spin superfluidity on grounds of the sign of the effective interaction and the stability of the uniform mode; the paper cites but does not discuss it. The authors should either add evidence of thermalisation in this sample and geometry (for example time-resolved, k- and frequency-resolved BLS showing a thermalised distribution feeding the k ≈ 0 peak with a delay consistent with four-magnon scattering rates, or a dependence on pump duration and power showing condensation follows gas density rather than the instantaneous pump), or narrow the claim to the spontaneous phase of a coherent band-bottom state. If refs 27 and 28 establish thermalisation under the same conditions, the matching parameters (film, field, pump frequency and power) should be stated. They should explicitly address ref. 32, including the sign of the nonlinear frequency shift here (the upward shift at high density implied by Fig. 3a is relevant), compare the roughly 0.6-µs delay before signal onset with thermalisation and kinetic-instability timescales, and state that random phase is necessary but not sufficient.

4. **The "coherence value" is uncalibrated: it mixes SNR, chirp, amplitude modulation, spurious tones and noise.** As defined in the Methods, it is the integral of the normalised |autocorrelation| of the boxcar-windowed signal over 100 ns, divided by that of a pure tone. Noise alone gives a baseline of about 0.18, not 0 as the Fig. 3b caption ("perfect white noise") implies. A perfectly phase-coherent signal that chirps by tens of MHz or changes amplitude within the window scores below 1; the authors themselves attribute the dip near 1.0–1.1 µs at pump-off (Figs 3b, 4a) to the nonlinear shift, i.e. chirp, not decoherence. Its dependence on SNR is evident in the paper: the low-amplitude 22-dBm curve saturates at about 0.45 rather than about 0.75, and the Fig. 4 caption states both that coherence "does not depend on the absolute amplitude" and that the 21-dBm increase "is not very pronounced" because of low SNR. These statements need reconciling. The authors should compute the metric for synthetic signals — a pure tone, a tone with the measured chirp, a tone with the measured amplitude envelope, each added to the recorded noise at the measured SNR with the actual sampling rate and window — and for pump-off traces processed identically; derive the calibration curve C_w(SNR); plot measured coherence against measured SNR across all pump powers; and rescale or subtract the noise baseline. This would show whether the plateau value of about 0.75 rather than 1 comes from noise, chirp or finite linewidth, and separate intrinsic coherence changes from detection limits. A complementary standard measure, such as the within-shot phase-noise spectrum or linewidth after chirp removal, or g¹(τ) of the demodulated chirp-corrected signal, should be considered.

5. **The coherence value does not distinguish a coherent condensate from a narrowband incoherent (thermal-like) population.** Being a first-order, within-shot spectral-purity measure over 100 ns, it approaches 1 for any signal whose linewidth is well below 1/(100 ns) = 10 MHz, including a single-mode chaotic field or a thermalised gas piled into a band-bottom mode. The rise in Figs 3b, 3c and 4a may therefore mainly track spectral narrowing (already known from BLS) and the signal emerging from noise. Since the headline is the emergence of a coherent state, the authors should use the single-shot data they already have to test field statistics: over the 1,000 shots at fixed t, give the amplitude or |z|² distribution and the intensity correlation g²(0) = ⟨|z|⁴⟩/⟨|z|²⟩², noise-subtracted, in the pre-condensate, onset and plateau windows. The expected signature of a coherent condensate is a crossover from Rayleigh/exponential intensity (g²(0) ≈ 2) to Rician/Poissonian (g²(0) → 1). They should also compare the measured condensate linewidth with the 10-MHz window resolution so readers see what a high coherence value excludes.

6. **IQ-mixer artifacts lie in the condensate band.** Extended Data Fig. 2c shows "a constant band at 3.77 GHz and a shadow between 3.2 GHz and 3.3 GHz", next to or overlapping the condensate at about 3.32 GHz; the IQ image of a 3.2-GHz LO lies near 3.08 GHz. A stationary spurious tone is itself coherent and would raise the coherence value and could bias phases toward a fixed value; a phase-unstable spur would add phase noise; and IQ gain/phase imbalance can distort phase histograms. The authors should identify the origin of these features, quantify their amplitude relative to the condensate, calibrate and correct the IQ imbalance, state whether the artifacts set the 0.18 baseline, and report the coherence value and phase histograms after notching out the artifacts and for pump-off or sub-threshold (20 dBm) traces in the same windows, showing that the baseline and plateau value do not change.

7. **The claims that the condensate "does not undergo dephasing" and that coherence persists after the signal falls below noise are not established.** The Fig. 4 discussion (p. 1434) concludes the BEC is "phase-stable until the signal vanishes" and that "at t = 3.5 µs ... a clear remanence in coherence can be observed". But the coherence value only probes 100-ns windows, and the steady decrease from about 1.8 µs follows the falling amplitude, as both SNR loss and true dephasing would. At 3.5 µs the 25-dBm curve is within a few hundredths of the 0.18 baseline. The authors should quantify long-time phase coherence directly, for example g¹(Δt) for delays of 0.1–1 µs after removing each shot's deterministic chirp, or the residual phase deviation from a smooth phase model over the whole plateau, and compare the coherence time with the magnon lifetime and the spectrum-analyser linewidth. They should overlay the expected coherence for an ideal tone with the measured amplitude decay on Fig. 4a, and give a significance estimate for the 3.5-µs "remanence" against pump-off traces and the artifact level (concern 6). Otherwise these claims should be removed.

8. **The argument that the parametric magnons carry no phase reference rests on a non-observation the detection chain may be unable to make.** The text says no coherent signal appears at f_p/2 = 3.9 GHz and takes this as evidence of phase randomisation among the pairs. But 3.9 GHz lies at the 4-GHz diplexer crossover and the edge of the "< 4 GHz" F2 low-pass filter (Extended Data Fig. 1); the antenna suppresses 18-µm waves by an estimated factor of 18; and 3.9 GHz mixed with the 3.2-GHz LO gives a 700-MHz IF, which must be checked against the IQ mixer IF and oscilloscope bandwidth. Since pairs are phase-locked to the pump through φ_k + φ_−k = φ_p, this bears on concern 1. The authors should give the measured transfer function of the full receive chain at 3.3 and 3.9 GHz and the expected f_p/2 signal level if the pairs were coherent, turning the non-observation into a quantitative bound, or make a phase-sensitive measurement at f_p/2 synchronous with the pump. The statement on p. 1432 and the dotted line at 3.9 GHz in Fig. 3a should be qualified so readers can tell absence from suppression.

9. **Shot independence, repetition protocol and heating are not documented.** The repetition period of the 1,000 shots, the magnon lifetime against it, and evidence that the system fully relaxes between shots are not given. Residual coherent precession or thermal memory from a previous shot could seed or correlate the next, which bears directly on the phase statistics; heating of the film by 1-µs pulses at 23–25 dBm (about 0.3 W, after +50 dB amplification) could also shift M_s and the condensate frequency and alter thermalisation. The authors should state the repetition rate, show the correlation of phases between consecutive shots (autocorrelation of φ_n against shot index), check that the result survives a change in duty cycle, and give an estimate of the film temperature rise.

10. **The novelty and the quantum framing are overstated.** The introduction says the BEC phase "has only been revealed through phenomena dependent on spatial phase differences" and that observing spontaneous coherence "has been impossible"; the Discussion says "no direct proof of randomness in the established condensate phase has yet been provided". This overlooks interference of independently prepared atomic condensates showing random relative phase (ref. 14), photon-BEC work on temporal phase coherence and phase jumps, polariton condensates showing shot-to-shot spontaneous polarisation build-up, ref. 36 (Demidov et al., spontaneous coherence in magnon BEC), earlier inductive detection of microwave radiation from magnon condensates, and ref. 11 (Snoke and Girvin) on onset of phase coherence. The authors should state precisely what is new (single-shot, pump-referenced phase of a magnon condensate) and temper "first" and "proof" language. Separately, phrases such as "directly probe the coherence of the BEC state", "equivalent to the quantum mechanical phase" and "spontaneous symmetry breaking" describe a classical macrospin precession phase seeded by noise; no quantum (e.g. number–phase) property is tested. The authors should rephrase, and comment on whether the YIG Hamiltonian in this geometry is U(1)-symmetric given dipolar terms and anisotropy, and whether the pump, which breaks it to a discrete symmetry at f_p/2, matters for a condensate at a different frequency.

11. **The paper does not observe the incoherent stage it says it tracks.** The abstract says the measurement reveals "how an initially incoherent magnon gas thermalizes and undergoes a spontaneous transition". But before about 0.6 µs the detected signal (Fig. 2 at t = 0.10 µs, Fig. 3a, Fig. 4b) is indistinguishable from receiver noise; the text acknowledges that parametric magnons lie outside the detection band and are suppressed by the antenna. The low early coherence and the uniform 0.10-µs (and 0.90-µs) histograms are thus the noise baseline, not a measurement of an incoherent magnon gas, and the Fig. 2a caption should not imply a comparison of two physical states. The authors should show the detector can see the parametric or thermalising population (for example by widening the window or tuning the LO near f_p/2), or rephrase the claim as the emergence of a coherent band-bottom signal out of the noise floor.

14. **The explanation of the coherence oscillations is speculative.** The plateau oscillations (period about 0.2–0.3 µs, Figs 3b and 4a, 1.2–2 µs) are attributed to incoherent magnons reflected from the edges of the 9.5 × 2.7 mm film, "still a subject of investigation". Because they sit in the plateau used to argue for full coherence, the authors should report the period at each pump power and compare it with group-velocity round-trip times, or test by moving the antenna or adding edge absorbers; otherwise mark the sentence as a hypothesis. Alternatives include beating between the condensate and a nearby mode or the residual chirp within the 100-ns window, and relaxation oscillations between the pump-fed reservoir and the condensate.
