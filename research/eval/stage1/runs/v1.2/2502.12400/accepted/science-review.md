A. Must address

1. The agreement between the fitted ξ = 586 nm and the dirty-limit coherence length rests on transport parameters that the paper states inconsistently. The coherence-length section uses n = 6×10¹² cm⁻² and ℓ = 130 nm "from magnetotransport data", giving v_F ≈ 3×10⁶ m/s, ξ_BCS ≈ 2.5 µm and √(ξ_BCS ℓ) ≈ 570 nm. The Methods instead report n = 8.14×10¹¹ cm⁻², ℓ_e = 147 nm and a mobility of 9.86×10³ cm²/Vs from van der Pauw measurements on the same wafer. With those values v_F ≈ 1.1×10⁶ m/s, ξ_BCS ≈ 0.9 µm and √(ξ_BCS ℓ) ≈ 370 nm, which no longer "agrees well" with 586 nm. The two densities differ by almost an order of magnitude, and 6×10¹² cm⁻² with v_F = 3×10⁶ m/s is unusually high for an InAs 2DEG. The estimate also uses the bulk Al gap (Δ = 1.764 k_BT_c ≈ 250 µeV) rather than the smaller induced gap Δ* in the proximitized 2DEG, and it does not say which medium's coherence length is meant (the proximitized InAs under the Al, or the 10 nm Al film, which would be much shorter) or what mean free path applies under the Al, which is not measured. Because the quantitative match is the main evidence for the headline claim that I_c(W_sc) reflects the induced coherence length of the Andreev bound states, the authors should reconcile the two parameter sets (for example, density under the Al or a gated value versus a bare-2DEG Hall bar), state which density, mean free path and gap apply to the proximitized region and where each comes from (e.g. gated magnetotransport, tunnelling or MAR for Δ*), justify the choice of gap, and propagate the uncertainties in n, ℓ and Δ into a range for the predicted ξ. A single table of material parameters with their sources, including the Al thickness inferred from the critical field (ref. 37) with its uncertainty, would remove the confusion.

B. Should address

2. Each contact width is represented by a single junction, and device-to-device spread is never characterised, so the W_sc trends in Figs. 3b, 4, 5b and 5c cannot be separated from ordinary sample variation. InAs/Al planar junctions on one chip commonly differ in I_c, R_n, transparency and diode efficiency by 10–30% through local variations in the semiconductor surface, the actual junction length W_n (an 80 nm wet-etched gap to which I_c is very sensitive), interface transparency, local density, Al etch damage or undercut, and the gate dielectric. The data already show non-monotonic behaviour that may be this spread: max η in Fig. 5b is about 10% at W_sc = 0.9 µm but about 8.5% at 1.2 µm (the text concedes it "does not follow a clear exponential trend", and Fig. 5b has no error bars), and B_* at V_g = 0 in Fig. 3c is non-monotonic (about 22, 9, 8, 7 and 13 mT). Yet the abstract states that "both the critical current and the diode efficiency increase with contact width". The Methods mention several cooldowns but not whether I_c, max η, B_η and B_* were reproduced across them. The authors should measure a second junction at some widths, ideally the extremes 0.3 and 1.2 µm, or show reproducibility across thermal cycles; report R_n, I_cR_n, excess current or another transparency measure, and the measured W_n for every device so the I_c trend can be shown not to be a transparency or W_n trend; add error bars to Fig. 5b; and, if the η trend cannot be shown to exceed device scatter, qualify the abstract claim and state plainly that each width is one device.

3. Kinetic inductance and the way current is fed into the narrow Al strips are an untested alternative explanation for both I_c(W_sc) and B_η(W_sc). The contacts are 10 nm thick, 4 µm long strips of width W_sc fed from a T-shaped stem (Fig. 1a). The sheet kinetic inductance of such a film is of order 10 pH/□; for W_sc = 0.3 µm (about 13 squares) this gives a phase drop along the junction of order a radian at I ≈ 2 µA, comparable to Φ₀/I_c, and about four times less at 1.2 µm. A phase gradient along the junction makes the current non-uniform, lowers the measured I_c and shifts the Fraunhofer pattern; the authors themselves attribute B_⊥+ ≠ B_⊥− to "the magnetic field induced by the slightly asymmetric critical current density". Since the gradient scales as 1/W_sc, it could mimic the "coherence-length" suppression of I_c in Eq. 2 and contribute to the 1/W_sc scaling of B_η. The paper says kinetic inductance is "expected to be non-negligible" only in the discussion of the single-mode estimates and never estimates it. The authors should report the normal-state sheet resistance of the Al and estimate the kinetic inductance and phase drop for each device at I_c, state whether the two contacts are fed from the same or opposite ends of the junction, use the Fraunhofer patterns already measured (lobe periods, lifting of minima, the B_⊥+/B_⊥− offset versus W_sc) to bound the current non-uniformity, and compare this alternative functional form with Eq. 2.

4. The extraction of ξ from I_c(W_sc) in Fig. 3b is not transparent and is anchored on a single normalisation. The ordinate 1/ln[I_c^∞/(I_c^∞ − max I_c)] equals ξ/W_sc only if I_c^∞ is known. If I_c^∞ is the measured I_c of the W_sc → ∞ device, the point at the origin is fixed by construction and should not be shown as data, and in practice ξ is set by four points that all depend on that one normalisation. For the wider devices I_c^∞ − I_c is small, so the linearisation strongly amplifies errors in I_c^∞; rough reading of Fig. 3a gives single-device ξ values between about 0.45 and 0.6 µm. Fig. 3b has no error bars and no uncertainty on ξ = 586 nm is given. The authors should state how I_c^∞ was obtained, show error bars in Fig. 3b that include the uncertainty in I_c^∞, quote an uncertainty on ξ, fit Eq. 2 directly to I_c(W_sc) with I_c^∞ free and show that ξ is robust, and compare with at least one alternative functional form (e.g. the kinetic-phase suppression of concern 3).

5. The 1/W_sc scaling of B_η does not single out the orbital mechanism, and no estimate matches the data. Any finite-momentum mechanism has η zeroes set by the resonance condition 2q(2W_sc + W_n) = (2n+1)π/2 that the paper mentions, and since q_M, q_orb and q_RZ are all linear in B_∥, all predict B_η ∝ 1/(2W_sc + W_n). The single-mode estimates (about 13 mT Meissner, 4 mT orbital, 690 mT Rashba–Zeeman) bracket the observed 90–270 mT without matching it. The orbital finite-q estimate of Eq. 6 has no W_sc dependence at all, yet the W_sc dependence in Fig. 5c is attributed to orbital flux. The expected orbital signatures are absent: no Φ₀-periodic oscillations of η, and the weak sign changes at 400–600 mT correspond to several Φ₀ (e.g. about 3.7 Φ₀ for W_sc = 0.9 µm, d = 17 nm, at 0.5 T), not Φ₀. The Conclusion's statement that the W_sc dependence "invites an orbital interpretation" and the claim of consistency with an orbital mechanism are therefore not supported over the alternatives. The authors should fit both the 1/W_sc and 1/(2W_sc + W_n) forms and compare them, ideally compare quantitatively with a multimode BdG or tight-binding calculation of the actual geometry (finite W_sc, 80 nm junction, 4 µm width, Rashba, Zeeman and orbital vector potential) that predicts the scale and slope of B_η(W_sc), and state which mechanisms the data can and cannot exclude; otherwise they should say plainly that the mechanism setting B_η is not identified and soften the abstract and Conclusion. An asymmetric device with one narrow and one wide contact, or junctions with different W_n at fixed W_sc, would separate the resonance condition from the flux area W_sc·d.

6. The linear fit in Fig. 5c does not appear to be Eq. 4, and its parameters are not independent. Eq. 4, B_η = Φ/(W_sc d), is a line through the origin in B_η versus 1/W_sc, but the plotted line, with slope ≈ 74–75 mT·µm matching the legend "Φ/Φ₀ = 0.036 nm⁻¹ × d", has an apparent intercept of about 20–30 mT; a line through the origin with that slope would give about 250 mT at W_sc = 0.3 µm rather than the plotted 270 mT. Only the product Φ/d is fitted, so the quoted Φ/Φ₀ ≈ 0.36–0.61 just restates the assumed d = 10–17 nm and is not an independent test of Φ < Φ₀. The flux area is also taken as W_sc·d without justification, whereas the geometry of Fig. 2 could equally suggest (2W_sc + W_n)·d or a ξ-limited width. The authors should state the fit function and its free parameters, including any offset, explain a finite intercept physically (it may point to the resonance form of concern 5), give the fit parameters with uncertainties, show a fit through the origin and residuals, state the effective d used for the line, explain the legend in the caption, and justify the choice of flux area.

7. The W_sc → ∞ reference device differs from the others in more than W_sc, yet anchors three conclusions. It has unetched leads, so the current distribution, the absence of an S–N interface near the junction, and the screening and vortex environment of the large Al leads all differ, and its data alone went through a separate smoothing procedure described only in the supplement. It sets I_c^∞ in Eq. 2 and Fig. 3b, gives the largest max η (about 16.5%) in Fig. 5b, and is the only evidence for the claimed saturation of B_η "for W_sc ≳ 2ξ ≈ 1.15 µm" in Fig. 5c, since the 1.2 µm point lies on the linear fit. The authors should describe the smoothing in the main text, show that it does not shift the extrema or bias max η and that extracted quantities are insensitive to it, discuss whether the geometry differences could explain its outlying η, and show that the saturation claim survives without this device or soften it.

8. A width-dependent induced gap is an alternative explanation for I_c(W_sc) that is not excluded. A 10 nm Al strip only 0.3 µm wide sits on a large proportion of normal InAs, so inverse proximity or finite-size effects may suppress its gap and superfluid density more than in wide contacts; edge damage from the Al etch could act similarly. Either would lower I_c for a reason other than ABS leakage past the distal Al edge. The authors should report the induced gap of each device, for example from MAR features or from the temperature at which I_c vanishes, to exclude a W_sc-dependent gap, and discuss etch damage.

9. The gate dependence of B_* is attributed to Rashba α and transparency τ through Eq. 3, but neither is measured, so the claim that it "lends credence to a spin-orbit coupling based interpretation" is not established. The right-hand axis of Fig. 4 asserts that α increases and τ decreases with V_g; the α trend is cited from ref. 46 on a different sample, and the τ trend has no supporting data. 10. The sign and the W_sc dependence of B_* are not explained. Eq. 3 contains only positive quantities, yet Figs. 3c and 4 show negative B_* at negative gate voltage (about −5 mT for 1.2 µm, about −2 mT for 0.9 µm). This may be a sign reversal like that of ref. 8, but the paper does not say. The authors should explain what a negative B_* means physically given B_* = argmax I_c+ = −argmin I_c−, and discuss whether the W_sc dependence is consistent with the finite-size and orbital physics invoked for B_η or may reflect self-field or residual out-of-plane effects that scale with lead width.

11. The gate voltages are not matched across devices, and the gate lever arm may depend on W_sc. The "V_g < 0" series in Figs. 3c and 5c mixes roughly −4, −3.5 and −3 V for different devices (Fig. 4), and because I_c pinches off near −5 V (Fig. 1g), small V_g offsets mean large density changes near depletion. The Al contacts screen the gate, so the density change for a given V_g may itself depend on W_sc, and it is not stated which regions the gate covers; if it acts on the bare 2DEG beyond the distal Al edge, it also tunes the Andreev reflection that sets the W_sc dependence. The 0.3 µm device has no V_g = +10 V point. The authors should list the exact V_g for each point, explain why the values differ and why the 0.3 µm point is missing, state the gated area, and present cross-device comparisons against a gate-independent quantity such as density, R_n or normalized I_c(V_g)/I_c(0).

12. The error bars do not capture switching-current statistics. I_c± are single switching events from a dc ramp, and their uncertainty is the RMS residual of a local fit to the Fraunhofer extremum, propagated into η "assuming zero covariance" (Methods). This omits switching stochasticity, premature switching, sweep-to-sweep reproducibility and drift; asymmetric switching histograms can produce or mask a few-percent η. I_c+ and I_c− come from the same sweep and fitting procedure, so their errors are likely correlated and the zero-covariance assumption may misstate the η error. The authors should report the number of sweeps or switching events per point, the switching-distribution width at a representative field, the ramp rate and filtering, and check that η is unchanged when the median of repeated switching events is used.

13. Max η is compared across devices at very different fields, which confounds its W_sc dependence. η peaks at about 90 mT for wide contacts and about 270 mT for W_sc = 0.3 µm, and at higher B_∥ Zeeman and orbital pair breaking suppress the induced gap and I_c (Fig. 3a). The smaller max η of narrow devices may therefore reflect where the peak sits on the field axis rather than an intrinsic geometric reduction of nonreciprocity. The authors should compare η at a fixed B_∥, as a function of B_∥W_sc, or after normalising by I_c(B_∥)/I_c(0), and discuss whether the W_sc dependence of max η survives.
