A. Must address

1. The orbital interpretation of B_η is not supported quantitatively, and it conflicts with the paper's own estimates (Fig. 4c, Section IV, abstract, Conclusion). The paper says the W_sc dependence of B_η "invites an orbital interpretation", based on the flux relation B_∥ W_sc d = Φ (Eq. 3) and the fit in Fig. 4c, and the abstract says B_η follows a linear trend in 1/W_sc that "saturates" at large W_sc. Several problems undercut this. (i) The fit is not Eq. 3: Eq. 3 implies B_η ∝ 1/W_sc through the origin, but the fitted line has a clearly nonzero intercept of about 25 mT at W_sc⁻¹ = 0. (ii) The fit does not test the model: Φ and d enter only as a product, so "Φ/Φ0 ≈ 0.36–0.61 for d = 10–17 nm" follows from the assumed d, not from the data, and no model is given that predicts the flux at which η should peak (an orbital mechanism with Φ0-periodic structure would predict a specific Φ, near Φ0/2 or tied to the first sign change at Φ0). (iii) The orbital picture's own signatures are absent: the text concedes that sign changes at Φ = Φ0 and Φ0-periodic oscillations are expected but not seen; the sign changes occur at 400–600 mT for all devices, whereas for W_sc = 0.9 µm and d = 15 nm Φ0 corresponds to about 150 mT, a factor of about 3 lower; and assigning the missing oscillations to Zeeman collapse of the induced gap is not tested. (iv) The paper's own orbital estimate contradicts the data: q_orb = (e/ħ)B_∥d (Ref. 15) does not depend on W_sc, and with Eq. 5 predicts B^max ≈ 4 mT, 25–70 times below the measured B_η ≈ 90–270 mT, including for the W_sc → ∞ device (B_η ≈ 90–115 mT); the Meissner (13 mT) and Rashba–Zeeman (690 mT) estimates also fail to reproduce the magnitude or the W_sc scaling. The sentence "the above estimates … are a particularly good match to the experimental values observed in Fig. 4(c)" is either a typo (missing "not") or wrong, and the statement "q_orb > q_M > q_RZ … suggesting orbital effects are strong" sits uneasily with these numbers, yet the Conclusion keeps the orbital attribution. (v) 1/W_sc scaling does not single out the orbital mechanism: Meissner screening and vortex entry in the Al strip cross-section (d_sc × W_sc) also give field scales ∝ 1/W_sc. (vi) The saturation for W_sc ≳ 2ξ rests on one point (W_sc → ∞), which is excluded from the fit; the only other point in that regime is W_sc = 1.2 µm, sitting at (2ξ)⁻¹, so the onset of saturation could lie anywhere between 1.2 µm and the real width of the "∞" contact, and the dashed (2ξ)⁻¹ line is untested. The authors should fit B_η with and without a free intercept and report parameter uncertainties; plot the η sign-change field against 1/W_sc for every device (if the flux picture holds it should scale like B_η with a fixed ratio); reconcile the flux picture (Eq. 3) with the finite-momentum picture (Eq. 5); and either (a) give a model (for example a flux-threaded SNS/interferometer picture or a BdG simulation with finite-width contacts) that predicts B_η(W_sc) including its prefactor and intercept and compare it quantitatively with Fig. 4c, or (b) present the 1/W_sc trend as empirical ("B_η decreases with W_sc") and soften the orbital attribution and the saturation claim in the abstract and Conclusion. Plotting the "∞" point at its real width, adding devices between about 1.2 and 5 µm, and a control that separates orbital from Meissner/vortex mechanisms (different Al thickness or 2DEG depth, or rotating the field toward the current) would be needed to keep the stronger statements.

With one device per contact width and no device-level normalization, the cross-device trends, in particular the claim that the diode efficiency increases with W_sc, are not separated from device-to-device variation (Figs. 2–4). Each W_sc is a single junction, so both headline trends, I_c(W_sc) (Fig. 2) and max|η|(W_sc) and B_η(W_sc) (Fig. 4), assume that the 4–20% spread usual in nominally identical planar JJs (from interface transparency, local density, etch quality and effective junction length) is negligible. No normal-state resistance R_n, I_cR_n product, excess current or transparency estimate is reported for any device, although τ enters Eq. 2 and ξ_ABS directly. The fitted ξ is sensitive to this: with I_c^∞ fixed by a single device, a ±5% change in I_c^∞ changes 1/ln(I_c^∞/(I_c^∞ − I_c)) for the 1.2 µm device by roughly ±15–20%. The η claim is especially fragile: Fig. 4b is non-monotonic (about 10% at W_sc = 0.9 µm, about 8.5% at 1.2 µm), the finite-width devices span only about 5–10%, and the trend is carried largely by the W_sc → ∞ device (about 16.5%), whose peak η roughly doubles from 1.2 µm even though I_c is already about 90% saturated there; this jump is unexplained, and Fig. 4b has no error bars. The authors should tabulate R_n, I_cR_n, excess current (or a transparency estimate) for every device and gate voltage and show I_c(W_sc) normalized by 1/R_n; add error bars from switching-current statistics and repeated sweeps to Fig. 4b; ideally measure a second device (or second chip) for at least some widths (for example 0.3 and 1.2 µm) so the spread in I_c, max|η|, B* and B_η can be compared with the claimed trends; state whether all devices were measured in the same cooldown and whether the key quantities survive thermal cycling (T_c is quoted as "measured during multiple cooldowns" but device data are not tied to cooldowns); and otherwise qualify the abstract's statement that "diode efficiency increases with contact width".

B. Should address

3. Identifying the fitted 586 nm with the induced coherence length relies on assumptions the paper does not justify (Eq. 1, Fig. 2b, Section III). (i) Eq. 1 is heuristic: it says the reflection "probability" is proportional to the wavefunction "amplitude" ψ ∝ exp(−W_sc/ξ); a probability would normally go as |ψ|² ∝ exp(−2W_sc/ξ), which would double the extracted length to about 1.2 µm and remove the quoted agreement. Existing finite-contact-width theory (Refs. 31–33) is not compared quantitatively, and a 4 µm wide multimode junction has a distribution of decay lengths rather than one. (ii) The fit returns ξ_ABS = ξ/(√τ |sin(φ/2)|), which reduces to ξ only if τ → 1 and φ_c → π, yet Section III invokes τ < 1 and its gate dependence. The measured I_c ≈ 5 µA for a 4 µm junction at n = 6 × 10¹² cm⁻² is only about 10% of the ballistic estimate (k_F W/π)(eΔ/ħ) ≈ 45 µA, pointing to moderate transparency or a reduced induced gap; if τ < 1, ξ_ABS exceeds ξ and the agreement with 570 nm becomes accidental. (iii) The dirty-limit estimate uses the Al bulk gap 1.764 k_B T_c (which Section III calls the "induced gap") rather than the induced gap, and n and ℓ = 130 nm from bare-2DEG magnetotransport rather than the Al-covered region; with D = v_F ℓ/2, ξ = √(ħD/Δ) gives about 1.25 times the quoted value, so the 3% agreement is well inside an O(1) convention uncertainty. (iv) Fig. 2b is effectively a one-parameter fit to four points, every one of which depends on the single measured I_c^∞; the W_sc → ∞ point at the origin is zero by construction and is not a data point; no uncertainty or residuals are given for ξ. (v) I_c is evaluated at B*, where diode and finite-momentum effects already act, rather than at B_∥ = 0. (vi) Alternative causes of reduced I_c in narrow contacts (gap suppression or inverse proximity in narrow strips, quasiparticle leakage into 2DEG behind the contact, kinetic-inductance phase drops; see items 7 and 8) are not ruled out. The authors should present the 570 nm comparison as order-of-magnitude consistency; justify Eq. 1 (for example from a BdG/tight-binding calculation with finite-width proximitized contacts) and state which length enters and the phase at I_c assumed; estimate τ from I_cR_n or excess current; mark the origin point in Fig. 2b as constructed, fit I_c^∞ as a free parameter or propagate its uncertainty, and quote the uncertainty of ξ including its correlation with I_c^∞; repeat the analysis with I_c(B_∥ = 0); and test the interpretation by fitting ξ at the other gate voltages (data in S.IV already exist; ξ should be largely gate-independent if the gate is screened under the Al, or track √(v_F ℓ/Δ) as density changes) and at several temperatures (ξ should follow Δ(T)).

4. The gate dependence of B* is attributed to spin–orbit coupling without measuring the quantities Eq. 2 depends on (Figs. 2c, 3, Eq. 2, Section III). The rise of B* with V_g is assigned to a larger Rashba α and a smaller τ, but the gate also changes n, k_F (which appears in Eq. 2), v_F, ℓ, g*, the induced gap, E_T and ξ; the Meissner and orbital estimates in Eq. 5 also depend on v_F and Δ, so they are gate-dependent too. Decreasing transparency at more positive gate voltage (higher density) runs against the usual trend and needs evidence. No α(V_g), n(V_g), ℓ(V_g) or τ(V_g) is measured for these samples; the direction of α(V_g) is taken from Ref. 45 without using its numbers, and the right-hand axis of Fig. 3 is schematic. Eq. 2 is never evaluated numerically and compared in magnitude with the measured 0–40 mT values or with the factor of about 2–6 change in Fig. 3. The argument is also not discriminating: gate tunability of B* is said to lend credence to spin–orbit coupling, while the weak gate dependence of B_η is also called "consistent with a spin–orbit mechanism". Finally, Eq. 2 is derived for W_sc → ∞, yet B* is plotted against W_sc in Fig. 2c, including an upturn for W_sc = 0.3 µm, and the dependence is called "nontrivial" and left unexplained. The authors should plot R_n, I_c and an estimate of τ against V_g for each device; measure n(V_g) and α(V_g) (Hall and weak antilocalization on a co-fabricated Hall bar) or use cited values, and τ(V_g) from I_cR_n, excess current or multiple Andreev reflection; evaluate Eq. 2 and show that B* scales with k_so/k_F rather than merely increasing; and state what the W_sc dependence of B* is meant to show.

5. The out-of-plane field compensation and flux focusing could themselves produce W_sc-dependent diode signals (Section II, Section S.III). I_c+ and I_c− are taken as the maximum and minimum of the Fraunhofer central lobe, each at its own out-of-plane field and over a narrow B_z window. This can itself change the apparent asymmetry, and a relative B_z shift between the I_c+ and I_c− lobes is itself a diode signature (from inhomogeneous current or self-field effects) that this procedure removes. Flux focusing by the contacts makes the effective area for B_z, the Fraunhofer period and any misalignment-induced B_z grow with W_sc, so misalignment couples more strongly in wider-contact devices; vortices trapped in the wider Al strips are another W_sc-dependent possibility. The authors should state the misalignment angle and the compensation procedure in the main text; show, for each device, representative raw Fraunhofer maps at several (including large) B_∥, the B_z offset of the I_c± maxima against B_∥, and the Fraunhofer period against W_sc; show that B* and B_η do not change when the B_z window or its centre is varied; and report field-sweep hysteresis and sweep direction.

6. B* is extracted at the few-mT scale without a stated procedure or field-offset calibration (Figs. 2c, 3). B* lies between about −5 and 40 mT and is obtained from "low-field fits to Eq. (1) of Ref. 8", but the fitted function, its free and fixed parameters, the field step, the fit window and the origin of the error bars are not given; since I_c changes over hundreds of mT, B* may depend on the window. A remanent field in the in-plane magnet or trapped flux of a few mT would shift argmax I_c+ and argmin I_c− in opposite directions, and the paper does not say how the zero of B_∥ was calibrated or whether the antisymmetry I_c+(B_∥) = −I_c−(−B_∥) is observed at the few-mT level or imposed by symmetrization. Several V_g < 0 points have negative B* (down to about −5 mT, e.g. for W_sc = 1.2 µm), which Eq. 2 cannot give and which reverses the sign of the effect; this suggests an offset comparable to the W_sc differences being interpreted. The authors should write out the fit function in the paper and show that B* is stable when the window changes; state whether B* is the average of argmax I_c+ and −argmin I_c− (which would cancel an offset) or comes from one branch, and show the two separately with sweep-direction data; document the field-zero calibration and quantify deviations from antisymmetry; and comment on the negative B* values and whether they set a lower bound on the B* uncertainty.

7. The current distribution and phase drop along the narrow Al contact strips are not considered (Fig. 1a, Sections III–IV). Current enters each contact strip from a lead and must spread along the 4 µm junction width through a 10 nm Al strip of width W_sc. A rough estimate of 8 pH per square gives about 100 pH over 4 µm for W_sc = 0.3 µm, comparable to the Josephson inductance Φ0/2πI_c ≈ 66 pH at 5 µA and corresponding to a phase drop of order 1 rad; this scales as 1/W_sc and grows as the gap is suppressed by B_∥. It could reduce I_c and add a geometric, current-direction-dependent asymmetry, giving an alternative explanation for both the I_c(W_sc) and η(W_sc) trends; current crowding where the strips connect to the leads is a related effect. The authors mention kinetic inductance only in passing and neglect it. They should estimate the kinetic inductance from the sheet resistance of their Al at the relevant fields and the resulting phase drop, or simulate the current distribution, and show it is negligible for W_sc = 0.3 µm.

W_sc and the 80 nm separation were confirmed only on "nominally identical proxy devices", not the measured junctions; wet-etch undercut of a 10 nm Al film can shift W_sc by tens of nm, a large fraction of 1/W_sc for the 0.3 µm device and of the 80 nm gap. T-shaped at all?), yet placing it at W_sc⁻¹ = 0 drives both the I_c fit and the η trend. From Fig. 1a it is unclear whether exposed 2DEG or etched mesa lies beyond the outer edge of each Al strip, and how current reaches the strips (along their full length or from one end). This determines whether the rear boundary assumed in Eq. 1 is S/vacuum or an S/N interface into a normal reservoir; in the latter case the I_c suppression would reflect quasiparticle leakage, inverse proximity or a parallel normal path rather than reflection, the decay length need not be the coherence length, and the η analysis is also affected. It also sets the kinetic-inductance phase gradient (item 7). The authors should report measured W_sc and separation (SEM after measurement) with uncertainties, describe the "∞" geometry and plot that point at its real width, and label the mesa regions and current path in Fig. 1a unambiguously.

9. A null control with the in-plane field along the current is missing. For the Rashba–Zeeman, Meissner and flux (Eq. 3) mechanisms alike, the diode effect should vanish or be strongly suppressed when B_∥ ∥ I, while extrinsic contributions (B_z leakage, self-field effects, current crowding in the T-geometry) need not. The flux through W_sc·d and the Rashba–Zeeman finite-momentum term also have distinct angular dependences. Measuring η and I_c±(B_∥) for at least two field angles, for at least the narrowest and widest devices, would show that the W_sc trends in Fig. 4 come from the intended mechanisms and help separate orbital from spin–orbit contributions.
