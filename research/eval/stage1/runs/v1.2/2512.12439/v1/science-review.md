A. Must address

1. Charge transfer and screening are inferred, not shown, and non-electronic origins of the Au effect have not been excluded (pp. 14–16, Fig. 5B). The mechanism ("charge transfer and electrostatic screening … are the likely culprit", p. 15) rests only on the Au versus Al2O3 contrast. No measurement in the paper shows charge transfer: no carrier density, no core-level, work-function or band-edge shift, not even the sign of the doping (Fig. 5B suggests electrons), and no electrostatic estimate of the transferred sheet density from the Au/Ta2NiSe5 work-function offset or of its decay over layers. Without this, "charge transfer" and "screening (mirror charges)" cannot be separated either. Meanwhile the two substrates differ in much more than conductivity. Au is thermally evaporated directly onto the freshly cleaved surface, where hot Au atoms can react with Se (Au–chalcogen bonding is strong), intercalate into the first vdW gap, interdiffuse or create defects; Al2O3 is grown by ALD at 100–150 °C with TMA/H2O and then capped with Au (Methods, pp. 16–17). The interfaces also differ in roughness, conformality to the ripples, adhesion, pressure during cold welding, and thermal-expansion mismatch on cooling from 340 K to 10 K; the wafer under each sample (SiO2/Si or sapphire, p. 16) is not specified, so differential contraction may differ between samples. Ta2NiSe5 Tc is very sensitive to pressure and uniaxial strain (refs. 7, 14–17, including ref. 17 from the same group), and a strain or chemical explanation would have no special connection to excitons. The 4L/Al2O3 control rules out generic oxidation (p. 15) but not damage specific to the Au interface. The authors should: (i) bound strain by comparing absolute Ag phonon frequencies of 4L/Au and 4L/Al2O3 (and of each flake against bulk) at matched temperatures in both phases, using known Grüneisen or strain coefficients; (ii) characterize the Au/Ta2NiSe5 interface chemically (XPS of the Au–Se chemistry, cross-sectional TEM/STEM-EDX), or at least argue quantitatively against reaction and intercalation; (iii) estimate the transferred carrier density and its depth profile, or measure doping (phonon shifts, KPFM, XPS/UPS, reflectance band edge), and compare it with K-dosing (refs. 26–29) and gating (ref. 60) densities; (iv) ideally add a control that separates electrostatics from chemistry and strain, such as Au separated from the flake by monolayer hBN or a few nm of Al2O3 (keeping mechanics, removing charge transfer, retaining screening), Au deposited on top of a flake already on Al2O3, a substrate of different work function (Pt, Ti/Au, graphite), or a Raman temperature series on the 2L/4L ribbons already obtained on non-evaporated Au(111)/mica (Fig. 1B), which would test whether a pristine Au contact gives the same suppression. The authors should also state the wafer for each sample, and state explicitly that the existing Al2O3 samples sit on Au/Cr below 50–100 nm of oxide, which makes them a thick-spacer control. Without at least the strain bound or a spacer control, the abstract's "manifesting the presence of excitonic interactions" does not follow and the charge-transfer mechanism remains one hypothesis among several.

2. The conclusion that Au weakens electron–hole rather than electron–lattice interactions rests on an asymmetric and circular comparison (p. 15–16, Fig. 4). The argument is that the quasi-elastic peak (QEP) at 10 cm⁻¹ is "suppressed beyond detection" in 4L/Au, while the maximum of −1/q drops only ~17%, dismissed as "simple temperature broadening". Several problems: (a) The QEP's microscopic origin is contested (direct excitonic fluctuations, ref. 54, versus exciton-coupled acoustic phonon, ref. 55); the paper uses the Au-induced suppression to "affirm" that the QEP is excitonic and then uses the QEP to conclude that Au suppresses excitonic interactions. (b) Detection limits are not addressed: the 4L/Au spectra are ~50× weaker (×50 scaling; 6L/Au ×25), Fig. 4T is flat at ~3.5 (×50) with scatter comparable to the expected broadened peak, a 15 cm⁻¹ Rayleigh leak was removed by interpolation in the Au samples only (Methods, p. 18), and 10 cm⁻¹ is close to the laser line and filter edge of a single-grating instrument. A QEP broadened over ~100 K and reduced in proportion may simply fall below noise or be swamped by stray light. (c) The same Tc distribution that is said to explain the −1/q reduction would also smear and lower the χ''ac(10 cm⁻¹, T) peak; the 6L/Au data (Fig. 4S) already show a broad plateau rather than a peak. The "broadening" explanation of −1/q is asserted, not shown. (d) In an EI scenario the Fano continuum is the electron–hole continuum itself, so −1/q is not a clean measure of electron–lattice coupling; and a single Fano fit to a superposition of layers in different phases (B2g(o1) evolving into Ag(m2)) has no clear meaning for q. (e) Doping and screening also lower Tc of a hybridization or band-Jahn–Teller instability (band-filling and screened interband Coulomb terms reduce the energy gained from the distortion), so a lowered Tc alone does not identify an excitonic mechanism. (f) The gating study of ref. 60 reaches a different conclusion, but the paper gives this a one-sentence note without saying what that conclusion is or how the results reconcile; the hBN-encapsulated, gated flakes of ref. 60 are a cleaner electrostatic-doping comparison, and the comparison with ref. 45 (5L on SiO2, Tc reduced by ≤9%) should also be made concrete. The authors should convolve the 18L/Au or 7L/Al2O3 (or 4L/Al2O3) curves of −1/q(T) and χ''(10 cm⁻¹, T) with the Tc distribution inferred from Fig. 3 and show quantitatively that broadening explains −1/q but not the QEP loss; give an upper limit on the QEP amplitude in 4L/Au (for example by injecting the scaled 4L/Al2O3 QEP into the 4L/Au noise); show raw low-frequency spectra below 25 cm⁻¹ without interpolation, with filter edge and stray-light background, for all flakes; consider integrating χ'' over a window (e.g. 8–20 cm⁻¹) rather than a single point; explain, ideally with a model estimate, why a lattice-driven scenario would not respond the same way; and engage substantively with refs. 45 and 60. Otherwise "establishes an excitonic component" (p. 16) and "the suppression of excitonic mechanisms lowers the 326 K Tc … by at least 100 K" (p. 15) should be softened.

The headline magnitude ("reduced by over 100 K"; excitonic mechanisms lower Tc "by at least 100 K") uses the wrong reference and is not supported by the paper's own numbers (abstract; pp. 10, 13, 15–16). Both statements take bulk 326 K as the reference. 4. The layer-resolved "Tc gradient" picture is contradicted by the paper's own onset temperatures and ignores the depth weighting of Raman scattering above a metal (pp. 4, 13–14, Fig. 5B inset). The model has only the one or two layers next to Au strongly suppressed while the surface layers keep a Tc "closer to room temperature". The first kink, which marks the highest-Tc layers, should then stay near the 4L/Al2O3 (280 K) or bulk value. Instead it is at 220 K for 4L/Au and 250 K for 6L/Au (Fig. 3S,T): in 6L/Au four or five layers are supposedly unaffected, yet the onset is ~70 K below bulk. This suggests the whole flake is shifted. Interlayer coupling also makes an independent layer-by-layer Tc in a 4L crystal non-obvious. Moreover, the tangential optical field has a node at a metal surface, so at 632.8 nm on Au the layers nearest the metal are sampled least efficiently, not made "detectable in the overall signal" (p. 13); the ~50–100-fold weaker signal of 4L/Au relative to 4L/Al2O3 (colour scales in Figs. 3G–J and 4I,J; ×3, ×25, ×50 scaling for 18L, 6L, 4L on Au) is consistent with this. The statement that the effect is "confined to one or two layers" (p. 14) is a deduction from the persistence of broadening, not a result. The authors should compute layer-resolved Raman weights with a transfer-matrix model for each stack (air/TNS/Au/Cr/SiO2/Si and air/TNS/Al2O3/Au/Cr/…) at 632.8 nm; model the measured response as a weighted sum of bulk-like spectra with an assumed Tc(z) profile; show whether a profile confined to one or two layers reproduces the onset temperatures, kink separation, Ag(m5) intensity and position, and Fano and quasi-elastic evolution in Figs. 3S,T and 4N,O,S,T; and define Tc^avg from that model. They should separately rule out lateral inhomogeneity (amorphous-Au roughness, partial contact, local strain) as the source of broadening, for example with Raman maps across each flake at a few temperatures. If the data require suppression across the whole flake, the interpretation should change.

B. Should address

5. Each condition rests on a single flake, and the thickness series is not matched across substrates. Five flakes are shown, one per thickness/substrate combination (7L/Al2O3, 4L/Al2O3; 18L, 6L, 4L/Au). The central 4L/Au versus 4L/Al2O3 contrast uses one flake of each, and there is no 6L or 18L on Al2O3 and no thinner flake on either substrate, so the thickness effect on Al2O3 cannot be separated from the substrate effect at 6L (the 7L/Al2O3 versus 6L/Au pairing is not matched). Flake-to-flake variation from local strain under the cold-welding press, contact quality with amorphous Au, local damage, folds, laser position or thickness misassignment could shift Tc by tens of kelvin, comparable to the effect. The consistent suppression in both 4L and 6L on Au gives some support to the substrate effect, but the authors should measure two or three further 4L flakes on each substrate (ideally also 6L), preferably from different exfoliation runs, add a 6L/Al2O3 flake, report the spread in Tc and width, state whether results survive thermal cycling, and ideally show a single flake spanning Au and Al2O3 so the comparison is made within one sample.

6. Laser heating and the effective sample temperature are not documented, and heating alone could produce a lowered, broadened transition (Methods, p. 17). About 1 mW at 632.8 nm is used and the text says the Stokes/anti-Stokes ratio was checked, but no ratios, inferred temperatures, power dependences or spot size are given, nor whether the check was made on each flake and at which temperatures. Heating above the stage temperature shifts the transition to a lower nominal temperature, and gradients across the spot and through the stack broaden it — exactly the signatures attributed to the Au interface. Absorption, local field and thermal boundary resistance differ between Au/Cr directly on the wafer and 50–100 nm Al2O3 on Au, and between 18L and 4L, so the 18L/Au reference does not settle this; the effect bears directly on the ~60 K substrate difference. The authors should show Stokes/anti-Stokes temperature against stage temperature for each flake (especially 4L/Au and 4L/Al2O3) near Tc, repeat the 4L/Au (and 4L/Al2O3) series at reduced power (e.g. 0.25 and 0.5 mW) to show Tc and width are unchanged, and state spot size and power density.

7. The definition of Tc^avg and its uncertainty is ad hoc and applied inconsistently (p. 10, Fig. 3S,T). For the sharp flakes Tc is the onset kink (solid triangle); for 6L and 4L on Au the same onset kink (250 K, 220 K) is relabelled Tc^avg and given ±40 K from the excess of the two-kink separation (100 K) over an assumed universal 60 K. In a distribution of local Tc the onset marks the upper end, so the average should lie below it; and an excess separation of 40 K more naturally corresponds to a total spread of ~40 K (±20 K) with the centre below the onset. The second kink (open triangles) is not tied to any physical process — the reason Ag(m4) hardens below it is not explained — so the subtraction is unjustified. Structural and electronic criteria also differ (kink ± half the temperature step versus within 15% of the maximum of χ''), and disagree by more than the quoted error for 18L/Au (320 versus 306 ± 14 K). The authors should apply one criterion to all five flakes, for example the midpoint and width from fits of the integrated Ag(m4)+Ag(m5) intensity (an order-parameter-like quantity) or the Ag(o3)*/Ag(m4) shift versus T with a model including a Tc distribution, and explain or cite the origin of the second kink if it is used quantitatively.

9. Temperature smoothing and the kink-extraction procedure may themselves broaden or shift the transition (Methods, pp. 17–18; Figs. 3F–T, 4F–J, 4P–S). The 2D maps are interpolated onto a 5 K grid and Gaussian-smoothed by 10–15 K in temperature, with raw steps of 10–50 K, and the second-derivative maps (Fig. 3K–O) are computed from these, amplifying noise in the weak 6L and 4L/Au data (colour scales ±0.6 and ±0.3 against ±15). It is unclear whether the peak positions in Fig. 3P–T come from raw or smoothed spectra. The kink criterion relies on a "small but abrupt" shift of the leaked Ag(o3)* mode comparable to the 1.3 cm⁻¹ point spacing, and the 4L/Au kink is "less defined". The electronic Tc uses a "within 15% of maximum" criterion that is arbitrary and directly sets the width. The authors should extract peak positions and intensities from Lorentzian/Fano fits to the raw spectra at each measured temperature with fit uncertainties, state the temperature steps for each flake, present the 2D maps as illustrations only, and justify the 15% criterion or show sensitivity to the threshold.
