# Merged review

## A. Must address

1. **The Monte Carlo collapse of T_C to zero is probably an artefact of how stacking disorder is placed and of which quantity is called T_C.** The stacking-variation curve in Fig. 6b, and the concentration dependence in Fig. S8, drive T_C to zero near 5.6 GPa. Stacking changes, however, happen between whole layers. If each pair of adjacent, fully ferromagnetic layers is coupled uniformly by either J_L(ABC) > 0 or J_L(AA) < 0, the stack is an unfrustrated one-dimensional chain. A gauge transformation (flipping every spin in a layer) maps any such sequence onto a ferromagnet with couplings |J_L|. This is a Mattis-type collinear ground state: its ordering temperature is set by J_1, the anisotropy and |J_L|, not by the signs, and only the net moment is compensated. The authors themselves note (p. 12) that a CrBr3 monolayer, with no interlayer coupling at all, orders at about 70% of the bulk T_C (about 22-27 K), while their DFT still gives J_1 ≈ 1.4-1.7 meV at these pressures and shows only a ~30% fall in J_1 and a factor-of-two fall in the MAE by 8 GPa (Fig. 6a). Interlayer sign disorder alone therefore cannot plausibly bring T_C to ~4 K at 5.6 GPa or to zero above. The collapse in the simulation can come from several artefacts: (i) the text (p. 11) says "atoms from these phases are randomly distributed", and per-atom random assignment of J_L creates in-plane frustration that whole-layer stacking cannot produce; (ii) if T_C is read from the uniform magnetization or uniform susceptibility, a compensated layered order looks like "T_C = 0" although the system is ordered; (iii) the cell has only 10 layers (20×20×1 unit cells) with periodic interlayer boundaries, so an odd number of AFM bonds around the periodic stack frustrates the chain artificially, and only three disorder realizations are averaged; (iv) the paracrystal block length of 10 ± 2 nm (about 16 layers) is longer than the whole simulation cell. The authors should state exactly how "AA" and "ABC" are assigned (per atom, per bond or per layer pair) and which order parameter defines T_C; repeat the simulation with layer-pair-resolved stacking disorder, with block lengths from the paracrystal analysis, in cells thicker than one block, with open boundaries or many more layers and more realizations; and report the layer-resolved (gauge-transformed, staggered) order parameter and the specific heat alongside the net magnetization, with a finite-size check. They should state explicitly whether the predicted state above p_c is ordered (compensated AFM) or disordered. If the layer-resolved model keeps a finite ordering temperature, the claim must change from "collapse of magnetic order" to "loss of net moment through interlayer compensation", and the gap between the measured T_C and the monolayer T_C must be explained by something other than stacking.

2. **The diffraction does not establish AA stacking, yet the magnetic mechanism depends on that specific registry.** The P-3m1 model (Table S3: a = 3.64 Å = a_R-3/√3, one Cr site at 1a with 2/3 occupancy) is the averaged CdI2-type subcell expected when honeycomb layers sit at random among the three lateral positions. The authors concede on p. 6 that they "are unable to distinguish" randomized occupancy from randomized A/B/C stacking. True AA stacking (honeycomb directly above honeycomb) would give an ordered one-layer cell (e.g. P-31m, Cr fully on 2c, 1a empty, c ≈ 6.1 Å) that keeps sharp honeycomb superstructure reflections (h − k ≠ 3n) at integer L; random stacking turns these into diffuse rods along c*. The paper's own observation that the "1/3" reflections fade and vanish by 8.4 GPa (Fig. 3c) fits loss of interlayer registry at least as well as an ordered AA phase. A random A/B/C sequence produces mostly AB-type (rhombohedral-like) and monoclinic-like neighbour pairs, not AA pairs, and bilayer calculations (refs. [25, 49]) give these different signs and magnitudes of J_L. Nevertheless pp. 10-12, the abstract and the Conclusions treat the phase as "AA-stacked", and Fig. 6a and the Monte Carlo use J_L for an ideal AA bilayer, which changes sign above about 2 GPa. The authors should refine an ordered AA model (P-31m or equivalent) against the high-pressure data and compare it with the disordered P-3m1 model; analyse the intensity along the 10L and 11L rods (sharp peaks versus diffuse streaks) at 0, 6.4 and 8.4 GPa, at higher pressures, and after decompression, reporting whether diffuse intensity remains at 8.4-21.5 GPa; and consider the Br positions in the P-3m1 refinement. If ordered AA cannot be shown, they should compute J_L for the registries compatible with the data (random A/B/C shifts, AB, etc.), rebuild the magnetic model with stacking-averaged couplings, and reword "AA-stacked trigonal phase" in the abstract and conclusions.

3. **The decompression result contradicts the proposed structure-magnetism link, and the explanation offered conflicts with the paper's own diffraction.** Fig. S1b shows full recovery of T_C and M_sat after decompression from 10 GPa. Yet Table S4 gives an R-3 fraction of −0.1(7) at 8.4 GPa, the abstract says R-3 is "undetectable above 8.4 GPa", and P-3m1 is retained after release from 21.5 GPa (Fig. 3d) and called metastable. The Conclusions reconcile this by saying P-3m1 "was not yet stabilized at 10 GPa while some volume fraction of R-3 persisted", but no diffraction at or after 10 GPa supports this, and it contradicts the abstract. Moreover, Fig. 6a gives J_L(AA) slightly positive (FM) at ambient pressure, so a recovered P-3m1/AA crystal would be ferromagnetic anyway, and the recovery does not discriminate between scenarios. As it stands, the magnetic collapse may be reversible and unrelated to the irreversible stacking change, leaving hydrostatic compression of the intralayer exchange and anisotropy as a live explanation. The authors should show XRD on a crystal decompressed from about 10 GPa, and measure ambient-pressure M(T) and M(H) (to 5-7 T) on a crystal recovered from 15-21.5 GPa, where the structure is shown to remain P-3m1. If the retained phase carries antiparallel interlayer coupling, the recovered crystal should show a strongly reduced net moment and anomalous M(H); if it is an ordinary ferromagnet, the authors must show how this squares with their mechanism (for example via the sign of J_L(AA) at ambient pressure) rather than treat recovery as support for it. This is the most decisive test within reach of the existing setup.
   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

4. **The quantitative agreement between model and T_C(p) in Fig. 6b is largely built in, and the measured phase fractions do not support it.** The "stacking variation model" assumes an AA fraction rising linearly "up to a critical pressure" (p. 12); its slope and end point are not given, and if they were chosen to reproduce p_c the agreement is by construction. Feeding in the measured fractions undermines the picture: Fig. S8 needs about 73% AA for T_C to vanish, while Table S4 gives about 50-58% P-3m1 at 5.7-6.4 GPa, and since the R-3 fraction is stated to be a lower bound (p. 8) the AA fraction is an upper bound. Page 12 mentions FM being lost at "45% of P-3m1". At the measured fractions the model predicts T_C ≈ 25-30 K, not zero. The crystals also start with 20-40% P-3m1 at ambient pressure (Table S4; Methods) while remaining fully ferromagnetic, but the model starts at 0% AA. The concentration dependence uses exchange parameters fixed at 5 GPa (p. 11), and it is unclear whether Fig. 6b uses pressure-dependent J values; the pure-ABC T_C is 39 K in Fig. S8 but both curves in Fig. 6b start at about 34 K. The authors should list every free parameter and how it was chosen, drive the simulation with the measured fractions of Table S4 (with uncertainties, including the ambient P-3m1 fraction, mapped to the magnetization pressures), use pressure-dependent J_1, J_L and MAE in the mixed-phase simulation, and report the resulting T_C(p) with no adjustable critical pressure.

## B. Should address

5. **The magnetic state above p_c is not characterized, so loss of order cannot be told from loss of net moment.** All DAC field sweeps stop at 1 T (Fig. 1a), "M_sat" is the 1 T value at 2 K, and T_C comes from 0.01 T data (Fig. 1b). "Collapse of ferromagnetism" therefore means only that the 1 T moment and the low-field anomaly vanish. In the proposed scenario, layers keep their moments and couple antiparallel across AA interfaces with J_L(AA) ≈ −0.15 to −0.25 meV per Cr (Fig. 6a); such couplings should be overcome at a spin-flip field of order 1-3 T, so M(H) to 5-7 T at 2 K should show a metamagnetic step and recover most of 3 μB/Cr, and even at 1 T a partially compensated stack should retain a sizeable moment rather than zero. A paramagnetic, unsaturated response would point instead to suppressed intralayer order. There is also a temperature confound: at 5.24 and 5.60 GPa T_C is about 13 K and 4 K, so a moment measured at 2 K is reduced simply by proximity to T_C, and the "identical trend" of T_C and M_sat (p. 3) does not show a reduced Cr moment. Conversely, at 1.92 GPa M_sat is already about 2 μB/f.u. while T_C is nearly unchanged (Fig. 1 inset a), which suggests a calibration or background issue or a non-ferromagnetic fraction the model does not explain. The authors should measure M(H) at 2 K to the maximum available field just below and above p_c (e.g. 5.6 and ≥6.2 GPa); measure M(T) at 0.1-1 T and above the expected spin-flip field above p_c, looking for an AFM cusp and a Curie-Weiss regime (with FC/ZFC data, including re-examining the 6.24 GPa curve in Fig. 1b); compare moments at a fixed T/T_C; and stop calling the 1 T value a saturation moment unless saturation is shown.
   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

6. **Structure and magnetism are measured on different crystals, at different temperatures and pressures, so their correlation is assumed rather than measured.** All XRD and Raman data are at room temperature (Methods, Figs. 2e, 3, 5), while magnetization is at 2-40 K. Cr trihalides show stacking transitions on cooling (CrBr3 near 420 K at ambient, CrI3 near 220 K), and the boundary may shift with pressure, so room-temperature fractions need not match low-temperature ones. Methods say the P-3m1 fraction varies from crystal to crystal and with handling (the XRD crystal, ~80% R-3, was selected "somewhat a matter of chance" from 30+ crystals), but the initial phase content of the magnetization and Raman crystals is not reported. The pressures also do not line up: the 1/3 reflections persist at 6.4 GPa and vanish only by 8.4 GPa, while ferromagnetism is gone at 6.24 GPa in a different cell, medium and crystal. Thus "the T_C decline correlates with the AA fraction" (p. 11) is an assumption. The authors should report XRD on the magnetization and Raman crystals (or sibling pieces) before loading; state whether p_c tracks the initial P-3m1 fraction across runs (which would support the mechanism if it does); add low-temperature diffraction, or at least the 1/3-reflection intensities near T_C, at one or two pressures around p_c, or justify carrying room-temperature stacking over to low temperature; and plot the Table S4 fractions on the same pressure axis as T_C and M_sat in a main-text figure with uncertainties. A microscopic magnetic probe above p_c (neutron diffraction, μSR or XMCD in a DAC) would connect structure and magnetism directly.
   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

7. **The pressure at the measurement point is uncertain, and p_c is quoted more precisely than the runs justify.** Pressure is set and read with ruby at room temperature only (p. 14), and turnbuckle Be-Cu cells can change pressure on cooling. Overpressure during loading reaches several GPa in the first run (Fig. S1a; up to about 14 GPa at the last point), and the horizontal error bars in Fig. 1 are one-sided. If the stacking change is irreversible, the magnetization at each point reflects the maximum pressure reached, not the nominal one. The two runs give p_c values about 1 GPa apart, run 1 still has a finite T_C near 6.3 GPa, and Fig. 1 does not say which run it shows. The authors should state the run and sample for each data set, report p_c for every run and quote it as a range or with an uncertainty covering both (not as 5.8 GPa), and measure or estimate the low-temperature pressure (ruby at low temperature, or a manometer such as the superconducting T_c of Pb or Sn in the cell).
   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **Non-hydrostatic stress may drive the structural change.** The authors show that cutting, bending and mounting alone create P-3m1 (Methods; Fig. 4c-h; pp. 5-6, 14). The methanol-ethanol-water medium is hydrostatic only to about 10.5 GPa (SI p. 9), yet the claim that P-3m1 becomes stable rests on points at 11.8-21.5 GPa and on decompression from 21.5 GPa; the main text describes the medium in the DAC experiments only as "liquid", without naming it for each experiment, and many liquids become non-hydrostatic between 3 and 10 GPa, the range of interest. The growth of the disordered component with pressure, the 10 nm block size, the broadened spots after release from 21.5 GPa (Fig. 3d) and the change of Raman slopes near 10 GPa (Fig. 5; ref. [40] reports 15 GPa for the medium) could all reflect shear rather than intrinsic thermodynamics. The authors should name the medium for each experiment with its hydrostatic limit, report ruby linewidths or pressure gradients at each point, and if possible repeat one diffraction (or Raman) run in He or Ne or show that the onset does not depend on the medium. "Pressure-induced phase transformation" in the title and abstract should be qualified accordingly.
   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

9. **The phase fractions do not support the statement that R-3 is "undetectable above 8.4 GPa", and their derivation is not described.** Table S4 lists R-3 fractions of −0.1(7) at 8.4 GPa, 0.08(3) at 11.8 GPa (non-zero at about 2.7σ), 0.01(1) and −0.03(2) at 21.5 GPa. A value of −0.1 ± 0.7 does not constrain the fraction, and negative values are unphysical. The starting fraction of 0.60(5) out of the DAC conflicts with the roughly 80% R-3 stated in Methods for "the best sample", and the P-3m1 block prints 0.6(5). The fractions from 1/3 intensities are said to be lower bounds (p. 8). The authors should explain how fractions and errors were obtained (which reflections, the DAC absorption correction, the occupancy model), constrain them to physical values, explain the large uncertainty at 8.4 GPa, reconcile the 80% and 60% figures, say how many high-pressure diffraction runs were made and whether the vanishing pressure of R-3 is reproducible, and give the detection limit in the abstract's statement.
   Raised by: r1, r2, r3

10. **The claims of "intrinsic trimorphism" and a "so-far unreported phase" are overstated.** The monoclinic C2/m phase is not observed in this work, so "trimorphism" relies on the literature, yet the abstract and conclusions present it as a finding. Methods report that as-grown crystals were R-3 and P-3m1 appeared after cutting; p. 6 says the fraction is "likely unintentionally modified" by mounting; and the RSM "deformed" state (Figs. 4d, 4g) was produced by an unspecified external force. This points to deformation-induced stacking faults with a P-3m1 average, best described by a paracrystal of ~10 nm blocks (Fig. 4h), rather than an intrinsic thermodynamic polymorph. Similar average structures from stacking disorder are documented for CrCl3, CrI3, VI3 and α-RuCl3, and residual density on the Cr2 site was reported for VI3 and VBr3 [38, 39]. The authors should show data from an undeformed as-grown crystal (e.g. an RSM before any cutting), describe the deformation for Figs. 4d and 4g quantitatively, describe the phase as a stacking-disordered (or AA-type, if concern 2 is resolved) modification, state what is new relative to earlier observations, and cite the stacking-fault literature.
    Raised by: r1, r2, r3

11. **The sign of J_L(AA) and the claim that a single phase cannot explain the data rest on untested DFT choices.** J_L(AA) in Fig. 6a lies between about +0.05 and −0.25 meV and changes sign near 2 GPa, energies of 0.1-0.3 meV per Cr that are within typical functional and U sensitivity. The rise in T_C for pure ABC (Fig. 6b, red) comes from J_L(ABC) increasing from 0.4 to 1.05 meV, outweighing a ~30% drop in J_1; an ambient J_L of 0.4 meV per Cr against J_1 = 2.1 meV implies strong three-dimensional coupling and should be compared with the neutron-scattering interlayer exchange of [47]. Structures were relaxed with PBE+U (U = 2.8 eV) + D3, but exchange was computed with r2SCAN+rVV10; whether U was applied there, on which structure, the supercells and magnetic configurations used for the fit, the energy convergence, the lateral registry used for "AA" relative to ref. [25], and whether the AA interlayer spacing was relaxed at each pressure are not stated. J_2 and J_3 are fixed at ambient experimental values for all pressures. The authors should show J_1, J_L(AA) and J_L(ABC) versus pressure for at least two U values (or with and without U) and one other vdW functional, give the fit configurations and AA geometry, check J_2 and J_3 at one pressure, and propagate the spread into T_C(p) before ruling out a single-phase explanation.
    Raised by: r1, r2, r3

12. **The Raman phase assignment and interpretation have unaddressed alternatives.** (a) For the average P-3m1 structure (Br at 2d, Cr at 1a), factor-group analysis gives only A1g + Eg Raman-active modes, yet three "hard" modes (Ph6-Ph8) are assigned to P-3m1; a disordered average structure has no well-defined zone-centre selection rules anyway. (b) Modes are assigned by slope on the grounds that P-3m1 is "less compressible" with "3D character" (p. 9), but Fig. 2e shows nearly superposed a and c for the two phases, and both share identical layers. (c) The soft "R-3" modes persist to about 10 GPa, while XRD places the disappearance of R-3 between 6.4 and 8.4 GPa. (d) The slope change and hardening near 10 GPa coincide with the hydrostatic limit of the medium. (e) The intensity loss is attributed to "progressive metallization", but the authors' DFT gap is still 1.39 eV at 10 GPa (p. 10, Fig. S6); a shift of the resonance relative to the 514.5 nm line is more likely. The authors should give irreducible representations for both phases (and the local stacking if relevant), support assignments with phonon calculations or polarization-resolved spectra, show fitted peak positions with error bars instead of guide lines, reconcile the pressure scales, remove the metallization explanation, and present the assignment as tentative.
    Raised by: r1, r2, r3

13. **Several features of the magnetization data and their normalization need explanation (Fig. 1).** The loops in inset a are not antisymmetric about M = 0 and show vertical offsets and finite high-field slopes at intermediate pressures, pointing to imperfect background subtraction. The 6.24 GPa loop appears to retain a step of a few tenths of μB/f.u. around zero field while the main panel plots M_sat = 0 there. The pressures of the orange M_sat squares do not match the inset legend (first point near 2.3 GPa vs 1.92 GPa). The conversion of a microgram DAC sample to μB/f.u. (mass determination, possible fragmentation) is not described, and the AC-susceptibility points (blue circles) come from a cell and setup absent from Methods (no frequency or amplitude). The authors should show raw sample signal against the empty-cell background at each pressure, state which background was removed and how M_sat = 0 was decided, give the normalization procedure, show error bars on M_sat including background subtraction, describe the AC method, and state the T_C criterion (dχ/dT minima, inset b).
    Raised by: r1, r2, r3

14. **Some lattice parameters in Table S4 and Fig. 2e are anomalous.** For R-3, c rises from 18.342(2) Å at ambient to 19.0(2) Å at 0.7 GPa (a 3.6% expansion under compression), giving c/3 = 6.33 Å while the P-3m1 refinement of the same data gives 6.10(4) Å, a 4% difference that contradicts the Conclusions' statement that layers are "ideally spaced along the c-axis, irrespective of structure". a drops 1.9% between 0 and 0.7 GPa and then does not change to 3 GPa (6.189 vs 6.1897 Å). The claimed saturation of c above 11.8 GPa (p. 5) rests on non-monotonic values (5.33(7), 5.36(9), 5.21(8) Å) measured beyond the medium's hydrostatic limit. The authors should check the 0.7 GPa refinement, fit an equation of state, compare compressibilities with DFT (Fig. 2e) and published data [12, 40], and drop or qualify the saturation claim.
    Raised by: r1, r2

15. **The Ehrenfest argument is mis-stated and dismissed only qualitatively, and the introduction's framing conflicts with the authors' DFT.** Eq. (1) (p. 3) is an exact thermodynamic identity, not a model that "treats magnetovolume effects in materials with almost isotropic elastic properties"; its standard form for a second-order transition uses volume quantities, or a factor of 3 for linear expansion. The authors note that the negative thermal expansion below T_C [34] implies dT_C/dp < 0 but say the approach "cannot correctly explain" the suppression without evaluating it. They should correct the form, evaluate Eq. (1) with literature Δκ and Δα, and compare with the measured low-pressure slope, which would show whether ordinary magnetoelastic effects account for the region below ~3 GPa so that only the accelerated drop needs the stacking mechanism. The introduction's statement that T_C "is expected to grow" because DFT predicts J_1 rising with pressure conflicts with the authors' own J_1 decrease above about 1 GPa (Fig. 6a) and should be reconciled.

## C. Minor

16. **Paracrystal and occupancy analysis details are missing.** The 10 ± 2 nm block length (p. 8, Fig. S4) comes from a deformed crystal at ambient pressure with Poisson-distributed blocks assumed. The authors should show the fit to Fig. 4h with its free parameters, explain the uncertainty and the Poisson choice, state whether pristine crystals give a different block length, and justify linking this length to the high-pressure state. In the R-3 refinement Cr1 + Cr2 occupancies were constrained to sum to 1; since EDX fixes 1:3 only to about a percent, they should show that an unconstrained model (testing for Cr vacancies or excess) is not preferred.
