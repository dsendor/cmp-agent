A. Must address

1. The measured phase fraction does not track the magnetic suppression, and the ambient state contradicts the proposed mechanism. The mechanism requires Tc and the moment to fall as the P-3m1 / A-A fraction grows. Fig. 5b, however, shows the R-3 fraction essentially flat at about 0.5 to 0.55 from ambient pressure to about 5.7 GPa, dropping only near 6 to 8 GPa. Over that same range Tc falls from 33 K to about 17 to 19 K (Fig. 3) and the 10 K moment at 2 T falls from 2.8 to about 0.85 to 0.9 μB/f.u. (Fig. 2), so most of the magnetic suppression happens with no measured change in phase fraction. Taken at face value, about 45% of the starting sample is already P-3m1, yet at ambient pressure the authors observe Tc = 33 K, a nearly saturated moment of 2.8 μB/f.u. and no antiferromagnetic contribution (p. 2); their own Monte Carlo (Fig. 13b) gives Tc ≈ 28 K, about 11 K below the pure-AB value, for 45% AA. Section 2 / Methods (p. 4) also states that the best sample had about 80% R-3 at the start of the high-pressure experiment, which disagrees with the ~0.55 in Fig. 5b. Finally, Fig. 3 combines several runs, presumably on different crystals whose P-3m1 fractions were never measured, yet they fall on one curve, which suggests Tc is insensitive to the initial stacking-fault content. The authors should reconcile Fig. 5b with the Methods statement and with full ferromagnetism at ambient pressure, determine the initial phase fraction of each crystal used for magnetization, plot Tc and Ms against the measured AA fraction rather than against pressure, and show quantitatively that the A-A concentration rises across 0 to 6 GPa; otherwise the mechanism should be revised or stated as a hypothesis.

2. The P-3m1 subcell does not establish A-A stacking, yet the magnetic mechanism and the DFT exchange rest on that identification. Tables 1 and 3 give a CdI2-type cell (a = 3.64 Å, c = 6.12 Å, Z = 1) with Cr disordered over a single 1a site at 2/3 occupancy. In this average structure the in-plane √3×√3 honeycomb order is averaged away, which is what one expects for random stacking among the three equivalent vacancy registries (or turbostratic shifts), or for in-plane Cr/vacancy disorder. Ordered A-A stacking of complete honeycomb layers would instead keep the a ≈ 6.3 Å in-plane cell, giving a P-31m-type structure with c ≈ 6.1 Å and sharp superstructure reflections at h − k ≠ 3n; random stacking would turn these into diffuse rods along c*. The text on p. 11 describes the model as arising when "the placement of the adjacent layer is randomized along the c-axis", then concludes (pp. 12, 17, Conclusions) that "the only viable option is the A-A type stacking fault"; the Conclusions further give "1/3 occupation of the Cr 1a site", contradicting the 0.667 in Table 3. The reciprocal-space images in Fig. 4 are too small to judge. This matters because JL = −0.35 meV is computed for one specific ordered AA stacking, while random stacking would mix several interlayer registries with different signs of coupling (Ref. 36). The authors should analyse the intensity, including diffuse scattering, along the h − k ≠ 3n rods (plotted along L) in the DAC data at high pressure, in the unloaded crystal and in the high-resolution maps of Fig. 7, stating whether the honeycomb reflections are absent, diffuse or sharp; fit a stacking-fault model (DIFFaX-type, with A-A, ABC and random-shift probabilities as parameters) to decide between ordered A-A, random stacking and in-plane disorder; state which ordered supercell was used in DFT for "the AA phase" and how the fractional-occupancy structure was mapped onto it; and correct the Conclusions to 2/3.

3. There is no direct evidence for the proposed antiferromagnetic high-pressure state or for intact Cr moments. The abstract and Conclusions claim "an antiferromagnetic phase emerging beyond pc" with "the individual Cr ion moments remain[ing] intact", but the evidence is a featureless 0.01 T M(T) at 9.2 GPa (Fig. 1) and a nearly linear M(H) at 10 K only, reaching about 0.5 μB/f.u. at 2 T (Fig. 2). A Cr3+ (S = 3/2) Curie paramagnet at 10 K and 2 T gives about 0.6 to 0.7 μB, so these data are equally consistent with a paramagnet, a spin glass, a state with reduced moments, or a phase with strongly reduced intralayer exchange. The proposed state, with intralayer ferromagnetism intact and only the interlayer coupling reversed, makes specific predictions: an A-type order at a temperature set mainly by the intralayer exchange (tens of K, comparable to the former Tc) with a cusp in M(T); a spin-flip or metamagnetic step at a field set by |JL| = 0.35 meV per Cr (of order a few tesla) that restores the full ~3 μB/Cr; and a Curie-Weiss θ reflecting dominant ferromagnetic intralayer exchange. Weak AFM interlayer coupling between ferromagnetic layers should not remove all ordering down to 2 K, as Fig. 1 at 9.2 GPa appears to show, and none of these signatures is tested. The authors should measure M(H) to the highest available field (7 T) at 2 K and at several temperatures and pressures above pc, measure M(T) at 9.2 GPa in larger fields (for example 0.1, 1 and 5 T) to look for a Néel anomaly, fit the high-temperature susceptibility to Curie-Weiss, estimate the expected spin-flip field, test whether the moment recovers toward 3 μB, and compute the field-dependent magnetization of their spin model for comparison; element-specific probes such as XMCD under pressure would test the intact-moment claim directly. Without this, the "antiferromagnetic phase" statement is not supported.

B. Should address

4. Structure and magnetism are measured at mismatched temperatures, and the three critical pressures are compared as if they were the same quantity. All diffraction (Figs. 4, 5, Tables 1 and S1; Table 1 at 289/296 K) and Raman data (Fig. 10) are at room temperature, while the magnetic suppression occurs at 2 to 60 K. The paper itself notes that CrX3 stacking depends on temperature (including the first-order R-3/monoclinic transition at 420 K), and stacking transitions in CrI3 are known to shift and show hysteresis on cooling, so the stacking energetics and the R-3/P-3m1 boundary may change on cooling. The pressure loading and cooling sequence is not stated for any experiment. The magnetic pc (6.5 GPa at low T), the XRD transition (6.4 to 8.4 GPa at RT) and the Raman pc (~10 GPa at RT) are quoted together without reconciliation, including why the Raman and XRD values differ on the same type of sample at the same temperature. The authors should provide low-temperature structural data under pressure at a few pressures across 3 to 9 GPa (synchrotron single-crystal or powder XRD in the DAC, neutron diffraction, which would also give the magnetic structure, or at minimum low-T Raman), or argue quantitatively why the room-temperature boundary applies at 10 K; state the thermal and pressure history of each magnetization run; and discuss whether the differences among the three critical pressures come from temperature, sample or calibration. If low-T data cannot be obtained, the causal link should be stated as a hypothesis.

5. The atomistic simulations do not reproduce the loss of ferromagnetism they are said to "corroborate". The Monte Carlo (Fig. 13) stops at 45% AA, where Tc = 28 K; it never shows Tc → 0 or an AFM ground state, and the experimental pc and the Tc = 6 K point at 6.4 GPa are not approached. Pure AB gives 39 K against 33 K measured. Fig. 13a shows M/M0 → 1 at low T for every AA fraction, so the model does not account for the large drop of the saturated moment in Fig. 2. AA and AB "atoms" are distributed at random site by site (p. 16), whereas stacking faults are a property of whole layer interfaces, so a site-random sign of the interlayer coupling is not the physical disorder. The intralayer J1 to J3 are fixed at ambient-pressure values from Ref. 73 and JL is taken at zero pressure, although the Introduction (p. 2) cites DFT predicting that intralayer exchange increases under pressure, which would raise Tc. Given that intralayer exchange dominates, the authors should explain physically how AFM interlayer coupling on some interfaces could drive Tc toward zero rather than give a ferrimagnetic or A-type state near the intralayer scale. They should redo the simulations with interface-level disorder, pressure-dependent J1 to J3 and JL from their own DFT at the experimental lattice parameters, extend the AA fraction to 100% and report the resulting ordered state and ordering temperature, and compare simulated Tc(p), M(T) and M(H) at 2 T directly with Figs. 1 to 3 using the measured phase fractions as input.

6. The most direct test of the mechanism, magnetization of a decompressed P-3m1 crystal, is not reported, and reversibility of the magnetism is not tested. Table S1 shows that the crystal stays P-3m1 after unloading (a = 3.6498 Å, no 1/3 satellites). If P-3m1 / A-A stacking carries antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling, a recovered crystal should show no or a strongly reduced spontaneous moment at ambient pressure, which separates the effect of stacking from that of compression without a DAC. The authors should measure M(T) and M(H) at ambient pressure on a pressure-cycled crystal (and, ideally, on deliberately cut or deformed crystals with known P-3m1 fractions, to test whether Tc scales with the fault fraction), and state whether the magnetization in the DAC recovers on releasing pressure, which would also check the moment normalization.

7. The absolute normalization and background subtraction of the DAC magnetization are not described, and the moment trend depends on them. At 1.1 GPa the 2 T moment drops by 25 to 30% (2.8 to about 2.0 to 2.1 μB/f.u., Fig. 2) while Tc changes by only 1 to 2 K (31 K, Fig. 3); at T/Tc ≈ 0.3 thermal reduction cannot explain this, and no homogeneous mechanism easily does, so mass or volume errors, incomplete cell-background subtraction, or partial loss or fracture of the sample are plausible. The sample in a turnbuckle DAC weighs at most micrograms, the background comes from a separate empty-cell run, Fig. 3 combines several runs, and the ambient curve in Fig. 1 (smooth, green) may come from a different crystal measured by the PPMS VSM (Methods mentions both). The 0.01 T M(T) values (~0.08 μB/f.u.) are governed by domains and demagnetization, not by the ordered moment, so their pressure dependence in Fig. 1 does not measure Ms. The authors should state how the sample mass or volume was determined and its uncertainty, whether the ambient reference is the same crystal in the loaded cell, and the size of the cell background relative to the sample signal at 2 T; show raw sample and empty-cell signals for the highest pressures; address demagnetization for the out-of-plane plate geometry; and give error bars on Ms or normalized curves from a single sample before claiming a pressure-induced moment reduction.

8. Pressure calibration and hydrostaticity at low temperature are not established, which matters for a material the authors describe as damaged by "any kind of physical incursions". Ruby was read in the Be-Cu turnbuckle cell, but the paper does not say where or at what temperature; pressure in such cells typically changes on cooling. Methods (p. 4) quote a hydrostatic limit of ~14 GPa for 16:3:1 methanol-ethanol-water, whereas Refs. 45-46 place it near 10 GPa, coinciding with the Raman pc (Fig. 10), and the medium is a glass at all magnetization temperatures. Since the authors' central thesis is that CrBr3 stacking responds to "minute" shear (Section 2, Fig. S1a; cutting creates faults, p. 3), deviatoric stress developing on cooling could create stacking faults independently of pressure and differ between runs, in which case the result describes stress-induced damage rather than a pressure-driven phase. The authors should report each pressure with its temperature of measurement and uncertainty (or the estimated low-T correction), give ruby linewidths or R1-R2 splitting versus pressure, correct the hydrostatic limit, discuss whether runs with different loadings and cooling protocols give the same Tc(p) (Fig. 3 suggests they might, which would be a useful argument), and ideally repeat key points with a different medium (for example Daphne 7575 or a gas) or report a pressure gradient across the culet.

15. The Raman assignment lacks a factor-group analysis and is internally inconsistent.
