# Merged review

## A. Must address

1. **The non-monotonic magnetoconductance is not shown to be a signature unique to tomographic flow.** The Introduction calls the non-monotonic channel magnetoconductance (Fig. 1(c), Eq. (5)) an "unequivocal macroscopic signature" of tomographic flow, and the Abstract and Discussion say it can be used to measure both ℓe and ℓo. But because ℓo ≳ L, the predicted drop at Rc ≈ 3ℓo falls at Rc ~ L. That is the field range of the familiar boundary-scattering magnetoresistance peak of ballistic and quasi-ballistic channels with diffuse edges (the maximum near W/Rc ≈ 0.55; Refs. [12, 36] and the classic 2DEG literature), and of viscous-to-ballistic crossovers and hydrodynamic negative magnetoresistance (Ref. [34]). A conventional single-rate kinetic model (ko = ke) with diffuse walls also gives negative and then positive magnetoresistance in narrow channels. A non-monotonic G_ch(B) with a minimum at Rc ~ L therefore does not by itself distinguish tomographic from conventional transport, and the claimed extraction of both mean free paths is not established. The authors should overlay on Fig. 1(c) the numerical G_ch(1/rc) for the same channel with ko = ke (single-rate electron–electron collisions, matched ℓe and ℓMR), a quasi-ballistic or purely ballistic reference with ℓ comparable to L, and the no-slip hydrodynamic baseline. They should identify which feature is specific to ℓo ≫ ℓe (the position ratio of the two features, the depth scaling as ke·ko, the field position scaling as 1/ko, the lineshape, or the temperature dependence of the minimum implied by ℓe ∝ T⁻², ℓo ∝ T⁻⁴, which a single-rate model would not reproduce), and state whether a fit of Eq. (5) to such a curve could recover ℓe and ℓo without degeneracy with a conventional model.

2. **The main result, Eq. (3), and the equations it is derived from contain sign and coefficient inconsistencies.** Eq. (3) is called "the main result of this work", and readers will use it directly, but as printed it cannot be reproduced. (i) The leading term G²[cosh(2x/G)/cosh(1/G) − 1] is ≤ 0 for |x| ≤ 1/2, whereas Fig. 1(d) plots a positive profile with centre value 1 − 1/cosh 1 ≈ 0.35 at G = 1, and the G → ∞ limit of the printed term, −2(1/4 − x²), integrates to −1/3, opposite in sign to the 1/3 in Eq. (5); the term should be G²[1 − cosh(2x/G)/cosh(1/G)]. (ii) Eq. (15c) sets 𝓘₀ = 0, so Eq. (14) at n = 0 has no driving term and its no-slip solution is identically zero; the electric-field source (2ke v_y in Eq. (2)) must appear, presumably as 𝓘₀ = 1. (iii) Eq. (3) prints the boundary-layer term as +4ke ko 𝒴₁, but Eq. (18a) gives ke(ko/2)∂²u⁽⁰⁾/∂x² 𝒴₁ with ∂²u⁽⁰⁾/∂x² = −4 at the wall for G = 1, i.e. −2ke ko 𝒴₁, different in sign and magnitude. With 𝒴₁(0) ≈ 0.5 from Fig. 2(a), the printed version would give a wall velocity of about 0.6, whereas Fig. 1(d) shows u ≈ 0 at the walls; only the −2ke ko 𝒴₁ version brings the wall value close to zero. (iv) The "Bulk (with slip)" curve in Fig. 1(d) peaks at ≈ 0.50, but evaluating the bulk terms of Eq. (3) at x = 0 for ke = 0.1, ko = 2, G = 1 gives ≈ 0.47 (hydrodynamic plus slip alone give ≈ 0.51), so it is unclear which terms the curve contains. The 𝒴₀ and slip terms do check out against Eqs. (17) and (18a), so these look like transcription errors, but Eqs. (3), (4) and (14)–(18) are the paper's whole technical content. The authors should check every sign and prefactor against the code that produced Fig. 1, state exactly which terms each curve in Fig. 1(d),(e) includes, and state the normalization of 𝒴₀, 𝒴₁, 𝒯₀ and 𝒯₁ explicitly. (The concerns below assume these are typos and that the numerics were computed correctly.)

## B. Should address

3. **The regime of validity of the expansion is not stated, and the parameters shown sit at or beyond its edge.** The expansion is formally in √ke at fixed ko (Eq. (13); "to linear order in ke" above Eq. (3)), but the corrections that carry the physics are O(ke ko): the slip, the rarefaction terms and the ko 𝒴₁ term in Eq. (3), the ke ko term in Eq. (5), the 3ko²/(2G²) term in Eqs. (15b) and (7). The boundary-layer terms scale as ke^{3/2} ko^{1/2}, and the layer width is √(ke ko) (the variable χ in Eq. (16)). The expansion therefore needs ke ko ≪ 1 (ℓo ≪ L²/ℓe), not only ke ≪ 1, and the paper never says so. In Fig. 1(d),(e) (ke = 0.1, ko = 2), √(ke ko) ≈ 0.45 against a half-width of 0.5, so the two wall layers overlap, which conflicts with the bulk-plus-independent-layer construction h = h_B + h_T in Methods. In Fig. 1(c) and the ΔRxy example (ke = 0.025, ko = 5), √(ke ko) ≈ 0.35 and the O(ke ko) slip term (≈ 0.125) is about 40% of the leading 1/3; the zero-field conductance correction is roughly 45%. The headline effects are thus large only where ke ko is not small, and when ke ko ≪ 1 Eq. (5) itself says they are small. Meanwhile the physics invoked in the Discussion (T ≲ 0.1 T_F, ℓo/ℓe ~ (T_F/T)² ≳ 100, the superdiffusive regime of Ref. [16]) is the limit ko ≫ 1, where Eq. (5) grows without bound and the expansion presumably fails. The good agreement with numerics at the two parameter sets shown supports the formulas there, but it is not explained, and "similar agreement for other parameter values" (p. 5) is not documented. The authors should state the distinguished limit (ke → 0 at fixed ko or at fixed ke ko), the formal remainder (O(ke²), O(ke² ko²) or O((ke ko)^{3/2})), the condition for the wall layers not to overlap, and how large ko may be; show the asymptotic-minus-numerical error of Eqs. (3) and (5) against ke at fixed ko and against ko at fixed ke up to ke ko ~ 1, at zero field and at Rc ~ ℓo, confirming the expected residual scaling (for instance as a validity map in the (ke, ko) plane with a 5% error contour, rc as a third parameter, and real materials marked on it); give the window in (T/T_F, L) for a real material where the theory applies; and qualify the Discussion's conclusion that the Stokes–Ohm description is "inapplicable" accordingly.

4. **The two-rate collision model limits the generality of the conclusions.** Eq. (2) relaxes all even modes m ≥ 2 with one length ℓe and all odd modes m ≥ 3 with one length ℓo. The microscopic theory the authors cite (Refs. [14–18]) finds odd-mode rates that grow strongly with m (roughly m⁴, with logarithmic factors), and that m-dependence produces the superdiffusion and scale-dependent viscosity of Ref. [16]. The diffuse-wall boundary layer is populated by high harmonics (the histograms in Fig. 1(d) show weight up to m ≈ 5), exactly the modes the two-rate model misrepresents. The layer functions 𝒴₀,₁, 𝒯₀,₁, the single layer width √(ℓeℓo), the slip coefficients (32/(15π), and the factor 3 in 3ko/rc, which reflects the m = 3 harmonic), and whether the ℓo measured by Eq. (5) is ℓ₃ or some weighted average, could all change quantitatively or qualitatively. Yet the Introduction and Discussion say a "dual relaxation-time description" is "fundamentally limited" and that Eq. (2) is "the appropriate starting point" for degenerate electrons, although Eq. (2) is itself a dual (even/odd) relaxation-time model; Ref. [31], cited as going beyond it, is not available to the reader. The authors should solve at least the numerical Boltzmann benchmark with an m-dependent odd spectrum (e.g. ℓ_m = ℓ₃(3/m)⁴, or γ_m ∝ m⁴ normalized to a given ℓo), show which of the four effects survive, how much G_ch(B) and the velocity profile change and what effective "ko" the magnetoconductance would measure, and then either justify the reduction or narrow the claims to the two-rate model.

5. **The strong-field part of Eq. (5) is not derived within the expansion.** The upturn at Rc ~ 2ℓe comes from a prefactor 1 + (2ke/rc)² that the authors "also include"; it does not appear in Eq. (3) and does not follow from integrating it. At fixed rc it is O(ke²), beyond the order kept, and it matters only when rc ~ ke (1/rc ~ 40 in Fig. 1(c)). In that limit the 1/rc terms in Eq. (2) and in 𝒥₄ (Eq. (15b)) are O(1/ke), the Lorentz term is of the same order as the even-mode collision term, and the expansion, which treats rc only as O(1) through ko/rc, is reordered; the Stokes–Ohm leading order and the first-order (Knudsen-scale) slip coefficient 32/(15π), which carries no field dependence, should then be modified too. Eq. (5) therefore combines results from two orderings, and the claim of validity "for ke ≲ 1 at arbitrary magnetic field strength" (p. 5) is not derived; the agreement with numerics up to 1/rc ≈ 50 is encouraging but needs explaining. Since reading ℓo and ℓe off the two field scales of one curve is the paper's main experimental proposal, the authors should derive a uniformly valid composite expression for rc = O(ke), or state that Eq. (5) is heuristic, say which terms hold where, and give its numerical error across the full 1/rc axis. More numerical points in the sparsely sampled decreasing branch (1/rc ≈ 0.05–1), where the tomographic suppression is the claimed signature, would show whether the Lorentzian form 1/(1 + (3ko/rc)²) is quantitatively right.

6. **The Hall-resistance conclusion rests on one parameter point, a possibly singular weak-field limit and no conventional reference.** The statement that ΔRxy is "dominated by near-boundary kinetic effects", with the opposite sign to Eq. (9), and so "does not provide an accurate avenue" to the Hall viscosity (pp. 6–7), uses only ke = 0.025, ko = 5, 1/rc = 0.02, G → ∞ (ΔRxy = +0.20 asymptotic, +0.19 numerical, against the hydrodynamic −6ke² ≈ −0.004). Eq. (11) scales as ke^{3/2} rc ko^{1/2}; with rc ≈ 50 this nominally higher-order term is O(10⁻¹), about 50 times the Hall-viscosity term, so whether Eq. (11) stays asymptotic as rc → ∞ depends on how 𝒯₀ and 𝒯₁ vanish as ko/rc → 0, which should be shown. The authors should plot ΔRxy against 1/rc for several ko, from Eq. (11) and numerics, alongside Eq. (9), showing where if anywhere the Hall-viscosity term recovers dominance (e.g. small ko or Rc ≲ ℓe); compute the same quantity for ko = ke with the same diffuse walls, to establish whether the O(ke^{3/2}) contribution is specific to tomographic flow or a generic Knudsen-layer effect already present in conventional kinetic theory (their Ref. [23] treats the near-hydrodynamic Hall field; the difference should be stated); give the value of the integral in Eq. (11) at the quoted parameters so +0.20 can be reproduced; and say whether the sign reversal persists at the parameters of the experiments that measured Hall viscosity (Ref. [5] and related graphene work).

7. **Only fully diffuse walls are treated, and the claim of generality to arbitrary geometry is unsupported.** All results assume fully diffuse reflection (Eq. (12)). The tomographic layer, the O(ℓeℓo) slip, the slip coefficient in Eq. (17), the depth of the magnetoconductance minimum and the sign of ΔRxy are boundary-controlled quantities and should depend on the specularity parameter, while real edges (etched or hBN-encapsulated graphene, PdCoO₂ bars) are partly specular (Refs. [5, 7, 26] discuss partial slip). Without this, ℓo inferred from the magnetoconductance is degenerate with the boundary condition. The authors should give Eq. (5) and ΔRxy for a Maxwell-type boundary with specularity p, or at least show numerically how the slip coefficient, the ko term, G_ch and ΔRxy change with p. The statement that the phenomena "persist for an arbitrary device geometry" (p. 3) rests only on unpublished Ref. [31] and should be removed or supported here.

8. **The numerical benchmark, the only independent check of the asymptotics, is not described or quantified.** The manuscript does not say how the linearized Boltzmann equation was solved: angular truncation (number of harmonics), spatial discretization near the walls, how the diffuse condition (Eq. (12)) is imposed, or convergence tests. The functions in Fig. 2 come from "solving the resulting equation numerically" with no method or boundary conditions at χ = 0 and χ → ∞ given. Agreement is shown "by eye" for only two parameter sets, and visible discrepancies in Fig. 1(e) at 1/rc = 1 (the asymptote above the numerics at the channel centre, and in the Hall inset, by several per cent) are neither discussed nor attributed to an omitted order. The authors should add a Methods or Supplement section on both numerical solutions with convergence checks, and quantify the agreement (maximum and integrated deviation per panel, and a table of errors over a grid of (ke, ko, rc, G)).

9. **The plotted curve in Fig. 1(c) needs checking against Eq. (5).** With ke = 0.025, ko = 5, G → ∞, Eq. (5) gives G_ch ≈ 1/3 + 0.034 + 0.125 ≈ 0.49 at zero field and a minimum near 1/3 + 0.034 ≈ 0.37 around 1/rc ~ 1, where the ko term has dropped out and the prefactor is still ≈ 1. Reading the vertical axis as logarithmic (ticks 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2 unevenly spaced), the curve near its minimum appears to lie further below 0.5 than this, possibly below the no-slip value 1/3, which would contradict the statement that tomographic flow enhances the conductance. The solid curve also seems to have a shoulder near 1/rc ≈ 0.1–0.3 that a Lorentzian in 3ko/rc times a monotonic prefactor cannot produce. The authors should mark the scale as logarithmic, confirm that the solid curve is exactly Eq. (5), add the hydrodynamic baseline 1/3 (and its field-dependent version) as a reference line, and consider a linear inset so the weak-field asymptote (≈ 0.49) can be read.

10. **Extracting ℓe and ℓo from G_ch(B) is asserted but not demonstrated.** The Abstract and Discussion say the magnetoconductance "can be used to measure both the even- and odd-mode mean free paths", but no inversion is shown, and Eq. (5) is given only for G → ∞. Real devices have finite ℓMR, a third unknown that also enters the field dependence (Eq. (3) contains G in the slip and rarefaction terms, and momentum relaxation has its own field dependence). The authors should give G_ch for finite G; fit it to synthetic numerical data with realistic noise and unknown G, reporting the uncertainties and correlations of the recovered ℓe and ℓo; state how the dip depth (∝ ke ko) and its field position (∝ 1/ko) jointly constrain ko when ke is free; and state how ℓe and ℓo would be extracted independently of ℓMR and of the boundary condition when fitting real data.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

11. **Experimental feasibility is asserted but not quantified.** The Discussion says the regime ℓe ≪ L ≲ ℓo ≪ ℓMR with G ≳ 1 "is readily accessible" and that the signatures are "readily detectable in present-day experiments", with no material numbers. For at least one platform (hBN-encapsulated graphene at stated density, GaAs or PdCoO₂), the authors should estimate ℓe, ℓo, ℓMR, L and T/T_F at T ≲ 0.1 T_F using the quoted Fermi-liquid scalings and the prefactors of Refs. [17, 18], and give the fields at Rc = 3ℓo and 2ℓe, the fractional conductance dip and the predicted ΔRxy. The condition ℓo ≪ ℓMR, with ℓo ~ (T_F/T)⁴, is demanding when ℓMR in the best graphene devices is tens of µm. Rc = 3ℓo ~ 10 µm corresponds to about 10 mT at n ~ 10¹² cm⁻², which places the feature among weak-localization and residual ballistic magnetoresistance; these competing weak-field effects should be discussed. Dimensional velocity and Hall-field profiles for one realistic device, G_ch(B, T) curves, and even a qualitative comparison with existing channel magnetotransport or imaging data (Refs. [5, 7]) would strengthen the paper considerably.

12. **Novelty relative to closely related work is not stated sharply, and some claims are too strong.** Ref. [20] (shared senior author) already reports magnetic-field suppression of tomographic transport and non-monotonic magneto-response in bulk flow; Refs. [23, 24] develop the near-hydrodynamic matched expansion for channel flow; Ref. [16] contains a tomographic boundary equation of identical structure to Eq. (16) (acknowledged after Eq. (16)); Ref. [31] is said to treat arbitrary geometry. The Introduction should say precisely what is new here: the √ke structure, the O(ke ko) slip, the channel magnetoconductance formula and the ΔRxy result. Statements such as "the conventional ... description ... is fundamentally limited and does not describe electron flows in the degenerate regime" should be toned down, since the authors' own result recovers Stokes–Ohm at leading order with corrections controlled by ke ko.

13. **The boundary-layer structure and the origin of the slip coefficients are not explained.** Eq. (16) is second order in χ, so it cannot by itself satisfy the kinetic boundary condition (Eq. (12)) for every incoming angle. Is there an inner Knudsen layer of thickness ℓe at the wall, and does it contribute at O(ke)? The 32/(15π) coefficient in Eq. (17) and the 64/(15π) term in Eq. (5) look like classical Knudsen-layer slip. The authors should explain the full layer structure (outer bulk, √(ke ko) layer, any inner Knudsen layer) and where each coefficient comes from, for instance in a Supplement.

14. **The scope of the Holder et al. protocol result is narrow and Eq. (7) needs clarification.** The tomographic correction in Eq. (7) is ∝ ke² ko²/G², so it vanishes in clean channels (G → ∞), exactly where tomographic effects are expected to be strongest. The authors should state, with realistic numbers, the range of G and ko over which this correction is comparable to the Hall-viscosity (first) term, and explain why the first term of Eq. (7) lacks the 1/(1 + (2ke/rc)²) factor present in Eq. (9).
