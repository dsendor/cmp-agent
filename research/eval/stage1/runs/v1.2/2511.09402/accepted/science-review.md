# Concerns

## A. Must address

1. **Strain and other extrinsic sources of a rotated l = 2 distortion are not excluded.** The Fermi-surface distortion the paper invokes (Fig. 2e; p. 8) is a two-fold term η cos(2θ + φ) with φ = −π/6, whose director lies 15° away from both the σv and σv' mirror lines. Any in-plane uniaxial or shear strain along a non-high-symmetry direction produces exactly this symmetry class: three unequal |q| values along A–H_I, A–H_II and A–H_III and a cyclic "handedness". Possible sources include cleave-induced strain, differential thermal contraction against the sample plate, a step or terrace gradient, or local strain around Ti clusters. The effect is small (1.4–2.2% of |Q_Bragg|, Supplementary Table 1), so a modest strain acting on the large electronic nematic susceptibility reported for AV3Sb5 could account for it. The global affine transformation that forces the Bragg peaks onto a perfect hexagon (Methods; Figs. 2b, 4d) removes the lattice signature of such strain by construction, but leaves its electronic response, so the analysis discards the very evidence that would identify strain. Domains of opposite handedness (Fig. 2g) are also what strain varying on a ~100 nm scale would give, and the T-matrix argument (Supplementary Figs. 18–20) addresses defects, not strain. The authors should: (a) report for every map the raw, uncorrected Bragg-peak positions, the affine-correction parameters and the lattice strain tensor they imply (magnitude and principal-axis angle); (b) test whether φ and η, and the handedness of the opposite-handedness region in Fig. 2g, track the local strain axis or are independent of it; (c) use DFT to estimate the strain needed to produce the observed η and compare it with the uncorrected lattice distortion; (d) report whether every domain and doping fits the same |φ| — a director pinned at ±π/6 with a ~30° rotation across a domain boundary would favour intrinsic order, while a scattered angle would favour strain; (e) ideally, add maps from several independently mounted crystals with the director angle reported for each, or a strain-tuning experiment.

2. **The data do not yet distinguish a "chiral-nematic" state (all mirrors broken) from an ordinary mirror-preserving nematic, and the model parameters are chosen rather than fitted.** A nematic state that keeps one mirror makes two of the three A–H_m directions equivalent, so the chiral claim requires that all three pairwise differences in q1, q2, q3, d12, d13, d23 and the velocities are significant. These differences are about 0.01–0.03 |Q_Bragg| (Figs. 2c, 3c,d), comparable to the error bars that include the pixel term ε_pix. The line profiles in Fig. 2c,d carry no error bars; in Fig. 3c,d the error bars of the three directions overlap over much of the energy range; the velocities on p. 6 carry no uncertainties. The phase φ = −π/6 appears to be imposed rather than fitted (p. 8: "used for our QPI data analysis"), the value of η used in the simulated QPI (Fig. 2f) is not given, and the agreement is described as "very well captured" without any quantitative comparison of simulated and measured d12, d13, d23 or q_i(θ). The authors should give uncertainties for every q, d and velocity and test pairwise significance against the combined uncertainty from fitting, pixelation and the residual of the affine correction; fit η and φ directly to the extracted q_i(E) along the three directions (or to the full QPI map), stating the number of data points and free parameters, with confidence intervals and residuals; show that φ is many standard deviations from every mirror-preserving value (see concern 6 for which values those are); state whether a pure nematic (φ = 0) plus uniform strain, or a C3-breaking term, fits equally well; and show the same result in independent fields of view and samples.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

3. **The "parent state" claim and T* ≈ 167 K are not measured.** The title and abstract present the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid as the parent of the chiral CDW and of superconductivity, emerging at T* above T_CDW, and Fig. 4f draws a T* line above T_CDW at all doping levels. Yet all normal-state QPI is at 0.4 K, with some data near 4 K, and doping is used as a stand-in for temperature. T* ≈ 167 K is obtained by converting a mean energy splitting of 14.4 meV measured at x = 0.15 directly into a temperature (apparently E/k_B with no prefactor), with no justification for equating a band-splitting scale with a transition temperature; mean-field ratios alone would change the estimate by a factor of order unity, the splitting itself depends on how q-differences are converted to energy through the renormalized velocities, and the comparison with T_CDW ≈ 90 K mixes compositions. The resistivity shows no anomaly at T* (p. 8). The torque anomaly at 130 K (ref. 28) concerns undoped samples and a bulk probe, and is numerically inconsistent with 167 K. Suppression of the CDW by doping does not show that the state seen at x = 0.15 and 0.4 K exists above T_CDW in the undoped compound. The authors should either measure the QPI anisotropy at elevated temperatures (for example 20, 50 and 100 K at x = 0.15, or QPI on x = 0 between T_CDW and the claimed T*), or provide a bulk probe on the same x = 0.15 crystals sensitive to rotation of the principal axes away from the mirrors (polarization-resolved optical birefringence, elastoresistance, polarized Raman mixing of the Eg channels); failing that, they should present "parent state", T* and the Fig. 4f phase diagram as hypotheses and temper statements such as "already present above the CDW transition". In either case they should give the formula and assumptions behind the 14.4 meV → 167 K conversion.

## B. Should address

4. **Instrumental artifacts are not excluded at the level needed for a percent-scale effect.** The signal is a few FT pixels: for the ~70–100 nm field of view of Fig. 1d–g, one pixel is about 0.5–1% of |Q_Bragg|, and the FT pixel size is not stated. A global affine correction removes linear distortion only; it cannot remove non-linear piezo creep, thermal drift along the slow-scan axis, residual non-orthogonality or spatially varying distortion, each of which has a fixed orientation relative to the scan frame and can produce an apparent handedness. A multi-atom or asymmetric tip can redistribute intensity within the QPI arcs and shift apparent peak positions in line cuts (for example the "triangle tilt" of q3). The opposite-handedness region (Fig. 2g) is the most persuasive evidence so far, together with the handedness changes between compositions, but it was taken several hundred nanometres away at a different setpoint (1000 pA, 0.3 mV lock-in against 500 pA, 0.5 mV), and the paper does not say whether it used the same tip, scan angle, session and field-of-view size. The authors should: image the same area at two or three scan-frame rotations (for example 0°, 30°/60° and 90°) and at opposite fast-scan directions, showing that φ stays locked to the lattice; repeat the analysis with a local (Lawler–Fujita-type) drift correction; show that the distortion survives a deliberate tip change, and ideally show opposite handedness with the same tip, ideally both domains and a domain wall in one field of view; calibrate the systematic floor with a quantity that should be symmetric (the inversion-related q1 ring, the uncorrected Bragg-peak shapes, or a reference such as a Au(111) surface-state ring measured with the same scanner and pipeline); and state the field of view, pixel number and q-pixel size for every map (including in the captions of Figs. 1, 2 and 4), giving the distortion in units of q-pixels.

5. **Sample statistics and domain structure are not reported.** The paper says the chirality is reproducible across doping levels, energies and terminations (Discussion; Supplementary Figs. 3–7, 14, 16), but does not state how many crystals, cleaves, tips and fields of view were measured at each x, how often each handedness was seen, whether any region showed no distortion, or how large the domains are. The domain picture rests on one other region (Fig. 2g), at a different setpoint. The authors should give a table listing, for each map, x, sample, termination, tip, scan angle, setpoint, field of view, φ (with uncertainty) or the ordering of d12/d13/d23, and handedness. Large-area or multi-FOV mapping that shows the domain size and the nature of a boundary between opposite-handedness regions would substantially strengthen the claim of spontaneous electronic order.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

6. **The symmetry analysis of the order parameter is incomplete, and the Methods contain errors.** A single two-component l = 2 (E2g) order parameter on a hexagonal lattice has an allowed cubic (Z3) term ∝ cos 3α that pins the director to σv or σv', preserving one mirror. A director at an intermediate angle such as the φ = −π/6 used here (principal axis at 15°, halfway between mirror types) therefore needs either a second order parameter that breaks all vertical mirrors (A2g-like; the authors' intra-unit-cell sublattice charge order, Eqs. 2–5, may play this role) or an external field such as strain (concern 1). The paper should discuss which is assumed, what sets the 15° offset, and what it predicts. In addition, the Methods state that mirror symmetry survives only for φ = 0, ±π, but cos(2θ + φ) with φ = ±π/3 or ±2π/3 is symmetric about a σv' or σv line; with Eqs. (3)–(5), φ = π/3 gives n1 = n3 ≠ n2, so mirror-preserving configurations occur at every φ = nπ/3. The statement that for φ = ±π/2 "the nematic component vanishes and the charge order becomes purely chiral" is inconsistent with n1 = n0 ≠ n2 ≠ n3, which breaks C3: within this parametrization any δn ≠ 0 is nematic, and φ only sets the angle between director and mirrors. The authors should correct these statements and restate the claim as an E2g director at a non-symmetric angle together with whatever additional order parameter is required.
Raised by: r1, r3

7. **Dopant-induced or structural symmetry breaking is not excluded.** The proposed microscopic origin is unequal charge on the three V sublattices (Supplementary Fig. 10b), and Ti substitutes on exactly these sites. Preferential sublattice occupation, short-range Ti ordering, or Ti alignment along a step or growth direction would produce the same symmetry breaking structurally. The T-matrix simulations (Supplementary Figs. 18–20) use "representative" strong-scattering parameters and random point defects, not correlated occupancy, and the statement that no correlated defect population is visible (Supplementary Fig. 13) is qualitative. The authors should tabulate the sublattice assignment of Ti features in the topographs (for example Fig. 1d) for each FOV and test for equal occupancy; quantify the Ti pair-correlation function and orientational distribution; state the range of scattering strengths and sublattice choices explored in the T-matrix study; report whether diffraction on x = 0.15 crystals shows any monoclinic or orthorhombic distortion; give the measured composition, local Ti density per map, and transport or susceptibility confirming the absence of CDW and the Tc at x = 0.15 (the concentrations are currently nominal or cited from ref. 29); and show that the residual Cs arrangement left after sweeping adatoms with the tip (Methods) has no preferred orientation correlated with φ.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **The causal link between the QPI chirality and the CDW chirality is asserted, not demonstrated.** At x = 0.05 mirror-breaking QPI (Fig. 4c–e) coexists with 2a0 CDW peaks of intensity ratio 2.6 : 1.2 : 1.0. For the claim that the CDW-triggered states descend from the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid, the CDW handedness should be locked to the QPI handedness. The authors should show, across several domains of each handedness at x = 0 or 0.05, that the sense of the CDW intensity ratio flips together with the QPI chirality, and that the ratios are robust to setpoint and tip, to which 2a0 peak intensities in AV3Sb5 are known to be sensitive. Without this correlation, coexistence does not establish parentage.
Raised by: r2

9. **The chiral-nematic superconducting state is overstated.** The claim that superconductivity "emerges directly" from the chiral-nematic Fermi liquid with the same handedness rests on subgap Bogoliubov QPI that "closely resembles" the normal-state pattern (Supplementary Figs. 8, 9, 17). This shows that the quasiparticles that pair live on a distorted Fermi surface, which would be expected for any anisotropic band structure; it does not show that the superconducting order parameter breaks mirror symmetry or constrain the pairing symmetry. Chirality is said to be seen "both below (0.4 K) and above (4 K)" Tc = 3.5 K, but 4 K is barely above Tc. The SC gap at x = 0.15 is a fraction of a meV, so the lock-in modulation (0.3–0.5 mV in the main-text maps) and electron temperature (650 mK) should be reported for the B-QPI data and compared with the gap to show that the subgap maps are dominated by Bogoliubov quasiparticles. Convincing evidence would be a same-area, same-tip comparison of subgap B-QPI with normal-state QPI with superconductivity suppressed by a field above Hc2 (11 T is available), with quantitative comparison of the q-vector misalignments; a gap anisotropy Δ(k) along mirror-related directions or a field- or temperature-dependent change in B-QPI distinct from the normal-state QPI; or vortex-core shapes reflecting the same handedness. Otherwise the claim should be phrased as "the normal-state anisotropy persists into the superconducting state".
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

10. **The direction-dependent velocities and mass renormalizations are less robust than presented.** The text and Supplementary Table 2 quote velocities to six significant figures (v1,F = 4583.84, 5107.30, 4121.17 meV/|Q_Bragg|; v_DFT = 6074.72) with no uncertainties, though the chirality argument uses their ordering. This is a ~24% spread for the α pocket and a 1.45–2.33 (≈60%) spread in δ-pocket masses, hard to reconcile with a Pomeranchuk deformation of only ~2% of k_F. The α-band ordering (v^II > v^I > v^III) has the opposite cyclic sense to the δ-band ordering (v^I > v^II > v^III), which is not discussed although both are offered as evidence of one handedness. For the δ pockets, q2 and q3 connect inequivalent pockets not related by inversion, so v_F ≈ 2 v_s holds only "to leading order in the chiral-nematic distortion" (p. 6), while the resulting anisotropy of m*/m (1.53, 1.95, 2.33) is of the same order as the distortion. The DFT reference comes from VCA-DFT without SOC at fixed kz = π, and a Ti-induced chemical-potential shift moves the δ band relative to the van Hove point near L, changing v_DFT. The authors should give the fit windows (to avoid the kinks), uncertainties and the sensitivity of the velocities to the window; test the significance of inter-direction differences; quantify the error of the v_F ≈ 2 v_s approximation, for example by computing dq/dE from the distorted model Fermi surface of Fig. 2e; explain the opposite cyclic orderings; discuss whether a single cos(2θ + φ) deformation can give anisotropies of this size, and if not, stop presenting velocity anisotropy as independent confirmation of the same order parameter; round to meaningful precision and give velocities in physical units (meV·Å).
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

11. **"Chirality" is used inconsistently with the literature the paper links to.** The proposed state preserves inversion (Methods), and with the in-plane mirror σh, which the measurement does not test, the point group is at most C2h, which is achiral in three dimensions; the handedness here is a planar property of the in-plane director. Only the Discussion (p. 8) states that "chirality" means breaking of all vertical mirrors without time-reversal breaking, while the abstract and introduction link the state to the "chiral CDW" (refs. 19, 20, 24), whose handedness is different and is tied to TRS breaking and field-switchable handedness, and to "chiral superconductivity", which most readers take to mean TRSB pairing. The authors should define the term in the abstract or introduction (noting that a nematic director at a non-high-symmetry angle suffices), state the full point group of the proposed state, and explain how it would connect to the 3D chirality and TRSB claimed for the CDW phase. Testing whether the QPI handedness can be trained or switched by an out-of-plane field (the 11 T magnet is available) would bear directly on that link.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

12. **Whether the effect is a surface or bulk property is not addressed.** STM probes the Sb- and Cs-terminated surfaces of a crystal cleaved at 13 K; surface reconstruction, incomplete Cs coverage and subsurface Ti layers can all lower the local symmetry. A bulk "Fermi liquid" phase underlying the bulk CDW therefore needs either a bulk probe at x = 0.15 (torque magnetometry, elastoresistance, polarization-resolved optics, or comparison with the bulk measurements of ref. 28 on doped samples) or an argument that the surface reflects bulk symmetry. The Cs-terminated data (Supplementary Fig. 14) help; the authors should state whether the handedness is the same on both terminations in the same region and discuss surface effects explicitly.
Raised by: r3

13. **The proposed q = 0 sublattice charge order is not looked for directly.** The microscopic picture (n1 ≠ n2 ≠ n3, Eqs. 2–5) is a q = 0 modulation and should be visible as anisotropic Bragg-peak intensities in topography and dI/dV at x = 0.15, or as inequivalent V-site contrast in real-space images. The authors should report Bragg-peak intensity ratios, analogous to the 2.6 : 1.2 : 1.0 CDW ratio at x = 0.05 (Fig. 4c), and correlate their sense with the QPI handedness.
