## A. Must address

1. **Eq. (1) does not reproduce the high-fluence and 190 K data, and a ~70 fs floor rather than a diverging susceptibility may set the minimum at Tc (Eq. 1; Fig. 4d,e; Supplementary Note 9).** The model writes n_tot ∝ F[χ0 + χex(T)] with τ_int = τ0 + A/√n_tot and τ0 = 25 fs. Because n_tot is linear in F, the temperature dependence enters only as a multiplying factor, so the enhancement at Tc should also appear at high fluence, with (τ_int − τ0) scaled by about √(0.2/2.0) ≈ 0.32. At low fluence the variable part falls from about 165 fs (τ_int ≈ 190 fs at 23 K) to about 45 fs (≈ 70 fs at 190 K), which implies n_tot rises roughly 13-fold at Tc. The 2.0 mJ cm⁻² curve should then fall from about 75–77 fs at 23 K to about 40 fs at Tc. The measured red points are instead flat at about 70–80 fs at all temperatures. Likewise, at 190 K the model predicts 1/√F with a smaller prefactor, but the measured τ_int is roughly fluence-independent at about 70–85 fs from 0.2 to 2.3 mJ cm⁻² (Fig. 4c,d). Near Tc, where χex diverges and excitons should be easiest to break, τ_int should approach τ0 = 25 fs, yet it sits at the same ~70 fs that high fluence reaches at every temperature; no point in Fig. 4d at any temperature or fluence falls below about 65 fs. On this reading the "dip" at Tc is the low-fluence curve hitting a common floor, which could come from intrinsic relaxation into the probed Se 4p states, another rate-limiting process outside the model, or an effective resolution limit of the fit. With a floor in place, α and the claim of a diverging χex cannot be determined. The physical meaning of τ0 = 25 fs, shorter than any measured τ_int, is also not explained. The authors should extend the model to include the floor (for example a saturating or additive-rate form) and fit both temperature series and the fluence series of Fig. 4d jointly with one parameter set. They should report τ0, A, χ0, α (and whether it is the same on both sides of Tc) and Tc with uncertainties in the main text, stating which are fixed or fitted; the fit curve in Fig. 4e has its minimum exactly at the measured 190 K point, so say whether Tc was fixed at ~200 K from Fig. 3d,e. They should compare the ~70 fs floor with the IRF width (Fig. 4a, Supplementary Fig. 5), state which features of Fig. 4e the model does and does not capture, and show that the data favour a diverging susceptibility over saturation of a non-divergent quantity.

## B. Should address

2. **The 1/√F scaling is not shown to be specific to excitonic correlations, particularly above Tc (Fig. 4b,d; Results, "Persistent excitonic fluctuations in the normal state", p. 1792).** The claim of short-range excitonic correlations at 300 K rests on τ_int decreasing with fluence as 1/√F plus a constant offset, read as plasmon-limited screening of bound electron–hole pairs. A faster rise of hole population at higher excitation density is generic in a photoexcited semimetal: carrier–carrier scattering and thermalization, screening-limited cascade of hot carriers into states near EF, screening-induced renormalization of core-level or valence bands, Coulomb-driven intraband redistribution and Auger-type processes all speed up with density without any pairing. The plasmon argument also neglects equilibrium carriers, which are substantial in semimetallic TiSe2 and differ strongly between 23 K and 300 K; screening should scale as 1/√(n_eq(T) + cF), and the offset τ0 is introduced without derivation and absorbs the difference. The 23 K and 300 K data fall on the same curve with the same τ0 although the equilibrium states differ qualitatively, which sits uneasily with using the scaling to diagnose ordered versus fluctuating states. Over about one decade of fluence (0.2–3 mJ cm⁻²) and with the scatter in Fig. 4d, 1/√F cannot be distinguished from 1/F, a logarithm, a saturating form or a free power law. The authors should fit alternative forms, including one with n_eq and one with a free exponent (a log–log plot of τ_int − τ0 against F at each temperature), and report exponents, parameters and fit quality. They should add a null reference measured at a matched edge and fluences where excitonic correlations should be absent or suppressed, such as an isostructural non-excitonic compound (1T-TiS2, 1T-ZrSe2), heavily doped or intercalated TiSe2 beyond the CDW dome (e.g. Cu_xTiSe2), or TiSe2 far above Tc (400–500 K), or else a microscopic calculation of density-dependent hole generation without excitonic binding. The alternatives dismissed in Supplementary Note 8 should be summarized quantitatively in the main text. Otherwise "short-range excitonic correlations above Tc" is one possible interpretation rather than a demonstrated one.

3. **The anomaly at Tc does not by itself distinguish an excitonic from a lattice-driven instability, and the phonon exclusion is too thin (Results, "Enhanced excitonic susceptibility at Tc"; Discussion, p. 1793).** In TiSe2 the electronic and lattice order parameters are bilinearly coupled, so any CDW-linked response shows critical behaviour at Tc whatever drives the transition, as the soft-phonon analogy the authors cite (ref. 40) itself shows. Near Tc the CDW amplitude mode and the zone-boundary L-point phonon soften strongly (refs. 40, 44), lattice fluctuations grow and the CDW gap closes; each could change how fast Se 4p holes appear. The Discussion's argument takes a 20% fluence-induced A1g hardening from ref. 28, measured under different conditions, assumes τ_int scales with a phonon period, and addresses only fluence dependence at low temperature, not softening near Tc. Since τ_int (70–190 fs) is a fraction of the A1g period (~167 fs) and the soft mode is strongly temperature dependent, a lattice contribution would not appear as a single-frequency renormalization. The authors should estimate the lattice timescale relevant to the window-(iii) rise from the amplitude-mode and soft-mode frequencies at each temperature and show its fluence and temperature dependence cannot reproduce Fig. 4d,e; state what in τ_int(T) would differ for a phonon-driven transition; and explain why a faster rather than critically slowed response is expected near a continuous transition. Otherwise "consistent with excitonic instability" should be framed as compatible with, not evidence for, an excitonic mechanism.

5. **It is not shown that the window-(iii) signal measures Se 4p hole population (Figs. 2a,e, 3c; Supplementary Note 7).** The interpretation needs the transmission change in window (iii) to be state filling. Fig. 3c shows a derivative-like line shape around 52.7–53.4 eV, with lobes of opposite sign and differences between 25 K and 310 K, the shape expected from an edge shift or broadening caused by core-hole screening or core-exciton changes (refs. 37, 38) or band renormalization, which themselves scale with carrier density. The window [53.04, 53.33] eV sits on the slope of this structure. If the rise is dominated by a screening-induced shift, τ_int tracks screening build-up generally, not exciton dissociation, which feeds directly into concern 2. Supplementary Note 7 tests robustness to the window, which is a different test. The authors should decompose the Se M5 transient at representative delays, temperatures and fluences into amplitude, shift and width components (for example with a model lineshape) and show that τ_int from the amplitude component alone reproduces Fig. 4d,e.

6. **Incident fluence is used as a proxy for carrier density without calibration (Eq. 1; Fig. 4d; plasmon argument, p. 1792).** The 1/√F argument assumes n_tot ∝ F and uniform excitation. The XUV probe is transmitted through the whole sample while the 750 nm pump is absorbed over a finite penetration depth, so the probe averages over a range of densities, smearing and possibly biasing the scaling analysis. Absorbed fluence changes with temperature because the optical constants at 750 nm (1.65 eV) change across Tc; this alone could change n_tot at fixed incident fluence and produce the Tc anomaly and the Fig. 3d,e amplitude changes without any excitonic susceptibility. With a 3.4 fs pump at 2 mJ cm⁻² the peak intensity is about 6 × 10¹¹ W cm⁻², so multiphoton absorption and saturation should be ruled out, for example by checking that the peak ① amplitude is linear in F. The authors should give the sample thickness, pump penetration depth and absorbed fraction, estimate the depth profile of n_tot, normalize to absorbed fluence using measured or literature temperature-dependent optical constants, and check whether the measured τ_int (70–190 fs) is consistent in magnitude with the inverse plasmon frequency for the estimated photoexcited plus equilibrium density with realistic ε∞ and effective masses.

7. **Effective sample temperature, heating and Tc calibration are not established near Tc (Fig. 3d,e; Fig. 4c–e).** The minimum is placed at 190 K with Tc ≈ 200 K and 5 K bins during a continuous ramp. Steady-state pump heating at the repetition rate, particularly at 2.0 mJ cm⁻², and sensor–sample gradients and thermal lag matter at this scale; the 350 K high-fluence point needing reduced fluence suggests heating is not negligible. A tens-of-kelvin offset at high fluence would make the high- versus low-fluence comparison at the same nominal temperature and the location of Tc in each dataset uncertain. The authors should give the repetition rate, the substrate or membrane and its thermal anchoring, and an estimated steady-state temperature rise at each fluence, ideally checked directly (for example from the onset of the Fig. 3e signal measured at high fluence). They should state how Tc was determined independently for these samples (resistivity, or the Fig. 3d,e onset at the same fluence) and give the stoichiometry and Ti self-intercalation level, which shift Tc.
