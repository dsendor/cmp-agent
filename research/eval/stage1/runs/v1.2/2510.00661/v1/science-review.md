A. Must address

1. No quantitative estimate shows that Harper broadening can produce the observed effect at 300–500 T (pp. 8–9, Fig. 4b). The only argument given is that at 300 T, ℓ_B ≈ 14.8 Å ≈ 3.5 a0 and the flux per unit cell is α = φ/φ0 ≈ 0.013 (q ≈ 77, so roughly 77 unit cells share one flux quantum). In the low-flux limit, the Harper/Hofstadter broadening of Landau levels near a tight-binding band edge is controlled by the overlap of neighbouring cyclotron orbits and is exponentially small in 1/α. Cu2O's conduction (Cu 4s) and valence (Cu 3d) bands have eV-scale widths, while ħω_c at 300 T is only about 80 meV for μ* = 0.415. On the 15 Å scale of ℓ_B the carriers should still see a well-averaged potential. The leading lattice correction at ℓ_B/a0 = 3.5 should therefore be a smooth non-parabolicity of order (a0/ℓ_B)², not a threshold; ℓ_B falls smoothly as B^(-1/2), so nothing in the proposed mechanism explains a "sudden" onset at 300 T. The expected lattice-induced width looks negligible next to the excess width reported (about 5–10 meV over the linear trend at 450–500 T, 10–20 meV in total). The description of the mechanism as "the significant increase of the degeneracy of the energy levels" (p. 9) is also backwards: the lattice lifts the Landau-level degeneracy and splits each level into subbands. Finally, the exciton is neutral, so the authors must explain how electron and hole Harper broadening would carry over to a bound 2p0 state. The paper calls the agreement "at least qualitative" (p. 9), but the title rests on it. The authors should compute the Harper bandwidth of the relevant states at α ≈ 0.01–0.02 for B ∥ [001], for example with a tight-binding model of the Cu2O conduction and valence bands with realistic hopping parameters, the real cubic lattice and Peierls phases, or a lattice-corrected magneto-exciton calculation, treating both carriers, and plot the predicted extra FWHM against B on Fig. 4. If the prediction is orders of magnitude too small, the title and abstract must change; if no estimate can be produced, Harper broadening should be presented as one speculative candidate among several.

2. The >300 T anomaly rests on a few points from what appears to be a single EMFC shot, with no reproducibility or artifact controls (Fig. 4a, Fig. 2, Methods D). Both anomalies, the extra width and the energy shortfall relative to the hydrogen model, are carried only by the 350, 400, 450 and 500 T EMFC points; neither NDPM nor STC reaches above about 150–200 T to cross-check them. Several instrumental and sample effects would give exactly this signature. Field inhomogeneity across the 1.8 × 1.8 mm sample and the 800 µm probed spot inside the imploding liner, and the field sweep within one streak-camera exposure (dB/dt grows steeply near peak field), each broaden a line by (dE/dB)·ΔB, and dE/dB of 2p0(Sz=+1) rises above 200 T. Sample heating during the pulse (from 45 K, by eddy currents or shock) would give a red shift through band-gap shrinkage plus phonon broadening, the same pair of signatures. A few-percent error in B moves the points by 10–25 T, comparable to the few-meV energy deviation given the curve's slope. The Methods do not state: the number of EMFC (and STC) shots and whether the upturn appears in more than one shot or in both up- and down-sweep; how B at the sample was measured (pickup coil, Faraday rotation), its calibration and uncertainty at 300–500 T, and how the field-time trace was synchronised with the spectra; the field homogeneity over the probed volume; the streak-camera time window and resolution, and dB/dt during each exposure; the polychromator's spectral resolution compared with the NDPM spectrometer; whether the EMFC detection was polarisation-resolved (unpolarised detection would superpose the rising σ− lines); the sample temperature during the shot and why 45 K was used. The authors should report all of these; show the upturn in at least one more EMFC shot or in the down-sweep; convert field inhomogeneity and in-exposure field change into linewidth contributions using dE/dB from Fig. 2 and subtract or bound them in Fig. 4a; give an estimate or proxy of the sample temperature during the shot (for example from the absorption edge, or a zero-field series of the 2p linewidth and energy at 45–150 K showing what heating would be needed to mimic the data); and add horizontal field error bars to Fig. 2 and Fig. 4.

4. Effective-mass and other intrinsic explanations for both the broadening and the energy shortfall above 300 T are not considered (pp. 6–9, Fig. 2, Fig. 4a, Fig. S3). Each of the following is expected in this field range in Cu2O and would produce excess width and a slower-than-hydrogenic shift without lattice-scale physics. (i) Level crossing and mixing: the paper calls 2p0(Sz=+1) "well separated from other excitonic transitions", but its own Fig. S3(a) shows 2p−1(Sz=0) (grey dashed), 2p−1(Sz=+1), 2p0(Sz=0) and branches descending from 3p approaching or crossing it between 200 and 500 T; the measured points lie between the purple and grey dashed curves, so the line could be an unresolved superposition, an anticrossing, or mixed with field-brightened s/d or dark states. (ii) Valence-band non-parabolicity and yellow (Γ7+)–green (Γ8+) mixing: the spin-orbit splitting is about 130 meV, while cyclotron energies reach several tens of meV at 300 T and about 140 meV at 500 T; as the wavefunction is squeezed to larger k the hole mass grows, the same physics behind the known P-series quantum defect, which naturally gives the reported "increase of reduced effective mass". (iii) Magneto-polaron resonances and screening: internal energy spacings approach the LO-phonon energies (about 19 and 82 meV); the hole cyclotron energy (0.116 meV/T × B/m_h with m_h ≈ 0.58–0.69) crosses the 82 meV Γ4− mode between about 400 and 500 T, and the crossover from static to high-frequency screening would also shift and broaden the level. The authors should, in Fig. 4a and Fig. S3, mark which calculated levels lie within one FWHM of 2p0(Sz=+1) at each field; fit the 350–500 T spectra with and without an additional component; show the 2p0(Sz=+1) energy residuals against B; and estimate each mechanism quantitatively with literature band and phonon parameters (at minimum, the multiband k·p yellow–green correction compared with the observed deviation of about 8–10 meV at 500 T), explaining why the data favour a lattice-periodicity effect.

5. The argument that excludes the 2p±1 assignment uses the wrong orbital Zeeman term (p. 6 point (ii); SI Sec. I, Fig. S2). The paper states that "the exactly ħω_c splitting of the 2p±1 states is imposed by time reversal symmetry", with ω_c = eB/μ, and deduces μ ≈ 2 m_e, "five times too large", if the strong lines were 2p±1. That holds only for an infinitely heavy partner, as in hydrogen. For an exciton at zero centre-of-mass momentum the linear term in the relative-motion Hamiltonian is (eħB/2)(1/m_e − 1/m_h)·m. With m_e ≈ 0.99 and m_h ≈ 0.6–0.7 m_e, the expected 2p+1–2p−1 slope is about 0.05–0.08 meV/T, the same order as the observed 0.11–0.116 meV/T in Fig. S2; the two hypotheses also predict nearly the same slope (they overlap in Fig. S2), so the slope cannot discriminate. The fits of the m ≠ 0 branches in Fig. S3 (Makado–McGill plus spin Zeeman only) also omit this term. Further gaps: the paper says the p excitons arise from an electric-quadrupole transition (p. 5; SI p. 3), whereas in Elliott's classification the yellow np excitons are dipole-allowed (Γ4−) and only 1s is quadrupole-allowed, so the selection-rule reasoning built on this must be redone; the Γ7+ hole is spin-orbit coupled, so the "Sz" labels and the hole g-factor "close to zero" need justification and a literature value with uncertainty for B ∥ [001]; and the SI claim that "σ± couples strongly only with spin-flip transitions regardless of m", used to assign the shoulders to 2p+1(Sz=−1) and 2p−1(Sz=+1), is inferred from fits, not derived, and contradicts the selection rules stated a paragraph earlier. Since the assignment is a headline claim and μ* and the "deviation from the hydrogen model" both depend on it, the authors should redo Fig. S2 and the Fig. S3 fits with the correct (1/m_e − 1/m_h) term and literature masses, state which Hamiltonian was used, derive the σ± selection rules for the Γ5−/Γ4− p states with the Γ7+ hole in Faraday geometry B ∥ k ∥ [001] (O_h → C4h) as a table of which of the nine 2p sublevels appear in σ+ and σ− and with what strength, and discuss explicitly how the assignment differs from Artyukhin et al. 2018, Zielińska-Raczyńska et al. 2019 and Kobayashi et al. 1989 (refs. 18, 11, 27).

B. Should address

6. The extraction of μ* = 0.415 ± 0.01 m_e and its uncertainty are not supported by the fit description (Fig. 2, Table I, p. 6, SI Sec. II). Ry* = 103 meV is "imposed" by the zero-field 2p–3p spacing under a pure 1/n² law, but the Cu2O P series has known non-zero quantum defects (Kazimierczuk et al., ref. 24), and 103 meV is 5% above the accepted 98 meV; this bias propagates into μ*, since the diamagnetic curvature fixes μ* only together with Ry* (equivalently ε). E_g = 2.1756 eV is given without uncertainty and differs from commonly cited values; it is unclear whether it was fixed or fitted, or how g = 2.0 ± 0.1 was obtained. The 3p and 2p0(Sz=−1) branches are followed only to about 130 T, so above that a single branch constrains the fit and "fits very well to the four levels up to 300 T" overstates the constraint. The fit range ends exactly where the deviation is claimed, which is partly circular. The authors should state which parameters were free and which points and field range entered the fit with what weighting; give the covariance of μ*, Ry* and g; show how μ* changes if Ry* is set to 98 meV, a quantum defect is included, or the fit stops at 150, 200 or 250 T; and discuss why μ* lies at or above the top of the literature range (0.35–0.41 m_e), for example polaron corrections, mass anisotropy or central-cell effects. Without this, ±0.01 m_e (2.4%) looks optimistic.

8. The case for a "sudden" upturn rather than smooth superlinear growth is not made statistically, and the linear baseline is unexplained (Fig. 4a, Fig. 4b). 11. The picture of an exciton reaching lattice scale needs numbers. The argument compares the free-carrier ℓ_B with a0, but the relevant object is the bound 2p0 exciton, whose transverse size in strong field is set by the relative-motion magnetic length and whose extent along B stays larger, set by the Coulomb interaction. At 300 T ℓ_B ≈ 15 Å is similar to the 1s Bohr radius (about 7–10 Å for ε ≈ 7.4, μ* = 0.415). The authors should give the in-plane and longitudinal sizes of 2p0 at 300 and 500 T from the hydrogen model, explain why lattice effects should switch on at ℓ_B/a0 = 3.5 rather than another ratio, and justify using the cubic-cell flux α (p. 9) for a six-atom cell whose Cu sublattice has a different spacing.
