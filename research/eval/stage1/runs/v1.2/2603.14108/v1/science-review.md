A. Must address

1. The zero-stress and finite-stress susceptibility curves were measured in different field geometries, so the claimed elevenfold enhancement of 3D superconductivity (Tc,mid from about 3 K to about 35 K) is not established. Section II and Fig. 2b (Methods A) state that the ambient curve was measured before mounting, in a 0.5 mT dc field parallel to the CuO2 planes, "such that the induced shielding currents flow between the layers"; this is what makes it sensitive to 3D superconductivity. The in situ ac excitation under stress is instead applied "predominantly along the c-axis", which drives in-plane shielding currents that 2D superconductivity can also carry. The authors' own resistivity puts 2D superconductivity near 20–25 K at zero stress (Figs. 3e, 4a). Part of the jump from Tc,mid = 3 K at 0 GPa to about 27–28 K at 0.09 GPa may therefore come from switching from an interlayer-sensitive to an in-plane-sensitive measurement, and from mounting strain, rather than from restored 3D coherence. The same problem affects the "3D SC" region of Fig. 4a and the "Tc,3D" line in Fig. 1d. It is also unclear whether every curve in Fig. 2b is normalised to −1, and which curve comes from which measurement. The authors should show in situ ac susceptibility at zero force in the stress cell, with the same coil and excitation, after cooldown with the sample glued, and show that its midpoint matches the dc H ∥ ab value of about 3 K. They should specify the coil geometry and field direction relative to the crystal axes, give the normalisation (for example to the zero-stress low-temperature value with a demagnetisation correction), and justify identifying Tc,mid in this configuration with the 3D transition; ideally they should also measure with the field in the plane under stress, or measure c-axis resistivity under stress.

2. Suppression of the LTT phase and formation of an "LTLO" phase at x = 0.125 are inferred only from the height of a normal-state resistive hump; no structural measurement is made under stress. The abstract and conclusion state that stress suppresses the LTT phase, that the enhanced Tc lives in a "stress-induced LTLO phase", and that "suppression of the LTT phase is essential" (p. 7); Fig. 1d labels the whole 0.23 GPa phase diagram "LTLO". The only evidence is ΔR_LTT (Figs. 3b,e, 4b). This proxy is not clean: in LBCO near 1/8 the LTT transition coincides with charge-stripe ordering, so a smaller hump is equally consistent with weaker charge-order pinning or a smaller charge-ordered volume in a structure that is still LTT, a shift of T_LTT, or a change in transport anisotropy. The "LTT peak" in Fig. 3b sits at about 35–40 K, well below T_LTT (about 55 K), and its height is set both by the resistive upturn and by the superconducting-fluctuation downturn below it; since stress raises the resistive onset to about 46 K, the hump would shrink even with an unchanged LTT fraction. The paper does not show whether the kink at T_LTT itself shifts or vanishes. The data also show a tension with the claim: Tc,mid saturates near 35 K by about 0.15–0.23 GPa (Figs. 2b, 4a), while the hump at x = 0.125 is still 50–70% of its ambient value at those stresses and vanishes only near 0.5 GPa (Fig. 3e), which argues against bulk 3D superconductivity requiring full LTT suppression. The authors should provide direct structural evidence at x = 0.125 under comparable stress (for example the LTT-specific (100)/(010) superlattice reflections by X-ray or neutron diffraction), or compare quantitatively with the stress-dependent XRD at x = 1/8 in Ref. 30; at minimum track the T_LTT anomaly in dR/dT against stress, discuss why Tc,mid saturates while the hump is still large, and otherwise phrase the conclusions in terms of the resistive anomaly.

3. The title claim that x = 1/8 "hosts optimal superconductivity", and the inference that stripe-related interactions enhance pairing, rest on comparisons made at mismatched stresses, with different metrics, apparatus and crystals. The highest x = 0.125 values (Tc,zero = 37 K, onset about 46 K) were reached at 0.56–0.58 GPa in the Razorbill FC100, while x = 0.115 and 0.135 were taken only to 0.23 and 0.29 GPa (Figs. 3d, 3f). At matched stress (about 0.2–0.3 GPa), Fig. 3 gives Tc,zero of about 33–34 K for x = 0.125 against about 31 K and 30 K for x = 0.115 and 0.135, and the x = 0.125 curve is still rising. The susceptibility comparison sets Tc,mid = 35 K here against about 30 K for x = 0.115 and 0.135 from earlier papers (Refs. 6, 19), on different crystals, in a different cell with a different stress calibration and possibly different growth batches. Each doping rests on one sample, and a 2–3 K difference is of the same order as plausible sample-to-sample and mounting spread. Without stripe physics, the LBCO Tc dome would anyway be expected to peak near x ≈ 0.125–0.15. The authors should extend x = 0.115 and 0.135 to 0.5–0.6 GPa in the same cell, or compare all dopings at matched stress with the same Tc definition; base the doping comparison on bulk (susceptibility) Tc; measure further samples per doping and give error bars reflecting sample variation; and present the pairing-enhancement interpretation as a hypothesis.

4. The headline Tc numbers mix 2D and 3D criteria and resistive and magnetic onsets, and the claim of "the highest bulk Tc reported in the 214 family" (p. 7; Fig. 4d) is not supported. The abstract says Tc rises "from 5 K to 37 K under 0.5 GPa": the 5 K is the ambient susceptibility onset (the text gives Tc,mid = 3 K), whereas 37 K is the in-plane zero-resistance temperature at 0.56 GPa, which the authors themselves describe as reflecting the 2D transition (p. 5). The highest bulk Tc,mid actually measured is about 35 K at about 0.23 GPa, because susceptibility was not measured above that stress; that is below optimal LSCO. The 46 K onset at 0.56 GPa has no stated criterion; in Fig. 3b the downturn begins on the shoulder of the residual hump, and ambient LBCO-1/8 already shows a resistive drop near 40 K from 2D correlations, so the onset depends on how the normal-state background is defined. Fig. 4d compares this resistive onset with LSCO data from a single 1989 study (Ref. 32), with no matching of criterion or sample form (single crystal or polycrystal), and the text ignores higher-Tc 214 materials: oxygen-intercalated La2CuO4+δ (about 40–45 K) and compressively strained LSCO films (about 45–50 K). The authors should quote stress-matched numbers with a stated criterion (3D Tc,mid from χ; Tc,zero and onset from ρ, each with its stress) throughout, including the title and abstract; define the onset criterion and apply the same one to the LSCO reference data, stating which stress is plotted for each doping in Fig. 4d; measure χ_AC at about 0.5 GPa if possible; restrict "bulk" to susceptibility (and ideally superfluid-density) results; and soften "highest in the 214 family" and "optimal" with the relevant literature cited.

B. Should address

5. The superconducting volume fraction is never measured, and the percolation picture conflicts with the paper's own data at 0.09 GPa. At 0.09 GPa, V_M at base temperature is about 0.84 (Figs. 2d, 4b), so only about 16% of the sample is non-magnetic, and V_M just below Tc,mid is still about 0.75; yet χ_AC already shows a sharp, complete diamagnetic transition with Tc,mid of about 27–28 K (Fig. 2b), roughly 80% of the final enhancement. The text (p. 7) says that a non-magnetic fraction of about 20% at 0.1 GPa "is likely insufficient for percolation" and that Tc saturates only once 1 − V_M approaches 40%. Full shielding measures a connected superconducting path, not a volume, and a thin percolating network or surface layer can give it. The data therefore cannot distinguish superconductivity confined to the non-magnetic regions (the authors' picture), superconductivity coexisting microscopically with static stripe order, and filamentary superconductivity, which lead to opposite conclusions about competition and bear on the "bulk", "spatially uniform" and "d-wave patches" language. The authors should report the absolute (unnormalised) shielding signal at each stress, measure the superfluid density and superconducting volume fraction with field-cooled TF-μSR above Hc1 at several stresses (for example 0, 0.09 and 0.23 GPa) in the same cell and test whether 1/λ² tracks 1 − V_M, and rewrite the percolation argument to match the 0.09 GPa data.

6. Stress inhomogeneity in the μSR/susceptibility cell is not considered, yet it could produce on its own the partial loss of magnetic volume that the paper interprets as an intrinsic mixed state. The crystal is a 7 × 5.1 × 0.3 mm³ plate glued with Stycast to a Ti holder, with only a 4 × 5.1 mm² free area exposed (Fig. 2a; Methods A), so the free length is shorter than the width. In such a geometry stress differs strongly near the epoxy-bonded ends, the free edges and the centre, and a 0.3 mm plate under compression can bend or buckle. The stress is quoted as force over the nominal 1.53 mm² cross-section (480 N / 1.53 mm² = 0.32 GPa), using a force-sensor calibration ("1 µV ≈ 30 N") from earlier work on different samples (Refs. 6, 41), with no stated uncertainty and no error bars on stress anywhere. Fig. 2d is suggestive: at 0.16–0.23 GPa V_M(T) does not saturate at low temperature and has a long tail to about 35–40 K, as a distribution of local stress would give; regions that lose static order alongside others that keep it with nearly unchanged T_so and B_int (Figs. 2d, 4c) is just what a stress spread would produce. The factor-2.5 drop in V_M, the LTT/LTLO coexistence and the percolation argument (p. 7) could thus reflect high- and low-stress regions rather than an intrinsic state at one stress. The authors should give a finite-element calculation of the stress and strain distribution for this sample, holder and epoxy geometry, weighted by the area the muon beam sees through the hematite masks, quote the fraction of the illuminated volume within, say, 20% of the nominal stress, check for bending, estimate the stress uncertainty, ideally verify with a direct strain measurement (strain gauge or digital image correlation) or a longer, thinner sample, and discuss whether the fractional V_M could reflect a stress distribution.

7. Data from two stress apparatuses and different crystals are placed on one stress axis without cross-calibration, and the sample basis is described inconsistently. The Introduction (pp. 2–3) says μSR, ac susceptibility and resistivity were "performed on the same crystal under uniaxial stress", but the Methods describe a 7 × 5.1 × 0.3 mm³ crystal in the piezoelectric μSR cell and separately mounted crystals, of unstated dimensions, in the Razorbill FC100 in a PPMS. Fig. 4a overlays T_so and Tc,mid (μSR cell) with Tc,zero (FC100), and Fig. 4b plots V_M and the LTT peak height on a common σ axis and calls them "directly correlated". Nominal stress can differ substantially from the stress the sample carries, because of epoxy compliance, different free lengths and aspect ratios, and differential thermal contraction between Ti, epoxy and sample; the apparent tracking in Fig. 4b, the convergence of Tc,zero with Tc,mid near 0.15 GPa in Fig. 4a, and the apparent factor of about 2 between the stress scales of the two cells may partly reflect calibration. The authors should state which crystal was measured in which cell, with dimensions and growth batches; give the resistivity samples' dimensions, epoxy thickness and free length; provide a common reference for the two stress scales (for example Tc,mid or the resistive hump of one crystal measured in both cells, or a direct strain measurement in each); estimate the stress-transmission efficiency, for example by finite-element modelling; and justify plotting both on one axis.

8. How V_M is extracted under stress, in the presence of a large cell background, is not documented, although the 0.97 → 0.38 reduction is a central result. Only about 50% of muons stop in the sample (Methods A); the rest stop in the hematite masks (fast depolarisation, which looks like "magnetic" volume in weak TF) or in the Ti holder and epoxy (non-magnetic, precessing at the applied field). V_M = 1 − P_TF(0,T) (Eq. 5) as written assumes no background, yet V_M reaches about 0.97 at 0 GPa, so a background must have been subtracted. Methods D says "baseline asymmetry shifts were corrected by allowing P_TF(t) to be adjusted for each temperature", which suggests a temperature-dependent free parameter that can move V_M. Any change in background or stopping fraction with stress (sample, mask or epoxy displacement as the cell deforms) would appear as a change in V_M. The authors should give the fit model with the background term, the background fraction at each stress and how it was determined (for example a run above T_so at each stress, or a dummy holder), state which parameters in Eqs. 3–5 were fixed and which free across temperature and stress, show raw (not A0-normalised) weak-TF asymmetries above and below T_so for 0 and 0.23 GPa, cross-check V_M from the ZF fits (Eq. 3) against weak TF, and state whether the 0 GPa μSR point was measured in the cell after mounting.

10. The zero-stress reference states are uncertain and differ from known ambient behaviour. In Figs. 3d–f the "0 GPa" Tc,zero is about 21 K (x = 0.115), about 23 K (x = 0.125) and about 20 K (x = 0.135), and Fig. 4a puts x = 0.125 at about 25 K. The 1/8 sample thus has the highest Tc,zero of the three at nominal zero stress, contradicting its description as the lowest-Tc composition, and the value is well above the in-plane zero-resistance temperatures usually reported for unstressed LBCO-1/8 (about 10–16 K). This points to residual mounting strain from differential thermal contraction against the holder or cell, a slightly different composition, or a different resistance criterion; Fig. 3 already shows small tensile values (−0.03, −0.01 GPa). Any of these affects the comparisons along the stress axis. The authors should explain how zero stress was established after cooldown in each cell and its uncertainty, state the criterion for Tc,zero (threshold or noise floor), and show ρ(T) of each crystal before mounting or free-standing.

11. Reproducibility, reversibility and the stress protocol are not documented. Each result rests on one crystal per doping and per technique, and there is no evidence that Tc, V_M or the resistive hump recover when stress is released, so plastic deformation, cracking (which could also contribute to a resistive drop) or epoxy creep at 0.5–0.6 GPa cannot be ruled out. The paper does not state at which temperature stress was changed (stress applied above T_LTT selects twin and LTLO domains differently from stress applied at base temperature), nor whether crystals were thermally cycled between stress points. The authors should state the number of samples per doping and the stress protocol, show at least one stress-release (return-to-zero) measurement with any hysteresis after the highest stress, and ideally reproduce the key x = 0.125 result on a second crystal.

12. Several interpretive statements go beyond what is measured. The abstract and Discussion (p. 7) state that stress converts static stripes into dynamic ones, that non-magnetic regions host "dynamically fluctuating stripes", that stress "enhances pairing" and acts "primarily at the level of phase coherence" via restored interlayer Josephson coupling, and that the system moves from a "putative PDW-dominated" state to uniform d-wave. None of the measurements is sensitive to dynamic stripes, PDW order, the pairing scale or pairing symmetry; the dynamic-stripe inference rests on absence of a μSR signal, which is equally consistent with the stripes simply melting. "Spin-stripe order remains long range" (abstract, p. 4) is not shown by μSR coherent oscillations, which give a well-defined local field distribution, not a correlation length. The title speaks of the "stripe state" and the introduction promises to track "the evolution of the charge stripe order", but charge order is not measured. The authors should either add discriminating evidence (the stress dependence of λ_L/λ_T or of the relaxation rate of the non-magnetic fraction, or LF-μSR, for slow fluctuations; c-axis resistivity or the ratio of resistive to susceptibility transitions for interlayer frustration; loss of 2D/3D separation as a PDW signature; neutron or X-ray correlation lengths for long-range order), or recast these points as hypotheses and remove "demonstrate" from the abstract.
