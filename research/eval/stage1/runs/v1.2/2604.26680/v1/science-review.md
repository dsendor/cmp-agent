Review outline:

1. Significance and novelty

The authors study a moment-compensated synthetic antiferromagnet (SAF), Ta/[Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]x10, whose two sublattices are chemically different. In a single field sweep they report two families of small (<100 nm) Néel skyrmions with opposite core polarity: conventional-polarity skyrmions near FM saturation (~120 mT) and inverse-polarity skyrmions near the SAF state (~45 mT). They assign all textures to the CoFeB sublattice, with CoB laterally uniform, and explain the field sequence by competition between the applied field and the RKKY bias from CoB. Confining textures to a chosen sublattice, with polarity selected by field, is an attractive idea for three-dimensional spin-texture engineering, and the correlative MFM, resonant SAXS, Lorentz DPC-STEM, element-resolved XMCD, return-point-memory (RPM) and micromagnetic data on one stack are a real strength. However, the central sublattice assignment rests on laterally averaged XMCD loops decomposed with an empirically tuned parameter, the effective-field mechanism is qualitative and not quantitatively consistent with the observed field windows, and the physics is close to known AF/FM phase coexistence in perpendicular layered antiferromagnets (Refs. 30-33, including the authors' own Ref. 33), from which the paper does not yet distinguish itself sharply.

2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A carefully grown, well-characterised compensated SAF: XRR thicknesses with uncertainties, cross-section ADF-STEM (Fig. 1b), SQUID-VSM, and a ~1% remanence plateau (Fig. 1c).
- Real-space (MFM, DPC) and reciprocal-space (SAXS) data at a series of fields consistently show a contrast reversal between the high-field and low-field textures within one branch (Fig. 2).
- Quantitative DPC: the tilt-corrected integrated-induction step of 12.8 ± 0.7 T nm agrees with the 13.6 ± 0.2 T nm expected from SQUID and XRR for a change between local SAF and local FM alignment. This excludes SAF-tube textures and whole-stack reversal.
- The Fe-edge XMCD loop isolates CoFeB without modelling and shows that the gradual high-field reversal is carried by the CoFeB layers (Fig. 3).
- The RPM dataset (Fig. 5) is systematic (four paths, ten repetitions each, cross-path comparisons) and is an uncommon statistical test of pinning: high same-family memory (~0.85) and very low cross-family overlap (~0.04).
- The micromagnetic model reproduces the qualitative sequence FM, conventional skyrmions, maze, inverse skyrmions, SAF.
- The design concept (moment compensation, interlayer exchange, asymmetric K_eff between sublattices) is simple, clearly explained in Fig. 6, and testable.

3. Concerns

A. Must address

1. The headline claim that all textures reside "only in the CoFeB layers", with CoB "laterally uniform", is not directly demonstrated; the abstract and p. 4 nonetheless call the attribution "unequivocal". MFM cannot distinguish the sublattices, and in a compensated SAF with equal sublattice moments (Table 1) a CoB-hosted texture on a uniform CoFeB background gives the same MFM contrast and the same 13.6 T nm DPC step, so the DPC magnitude (Fig. 2n) shows that one sublattice varies but not which one. Moreover, the DPC sign/zero reference is "set using assumptions informed primarily by the XMCD I(H) loops" (Methods), so it is not independent of the XMCD, and DPC is quantified only for the maze state, not the two skyrmion states. The deciding evidence is therefore the isolated CoB loop of Fig. 3d (Eq. 2), obtained as I_Co − a·I_Fe with a = 0.35 chosen "by adjusting a until the reconstructed total loop matched the SQUID-VSM loop" (Methods, p. 17), against a SQUID loop that is itself rescaled (a further scale and offset). Eq. 1 also sums transmitted intensities from two edges with different non-magnetic backgrounds (~4,700 vs ~7,000 counts) and different XMCD magnitudes per unit moment, with an implicit unit weight on the Fe term — a second, unstated free parameter. Because the non-hysteretic slope above 30 mT is present in both I_Co and I_Fe, the flatness and squareness of the reconstructed CoB loop, which is the key evidence, depends directly on a; part of the result is built into the fit. Fig. 3d is also not perfectly flat: the positive-field branch rises from ~2,420 to ~2,500 above 30 mT, ascending and descending branches are offset by ~30 out of 400 a.u. at |μ0H| > 30 mT and do not overlap at +160 mT, and the CoB switching appears near ±10-20 mT rather than "at the edges of the SAF plateau at ±30 mT" (p. 8). This residual hysteresis is either real CoB behaviour in the textured range or beam/detector drift; single-fixed-helicity loops without I0 or opposite-helicity normalisation are vulnerable to drift. Finally, laterally averaged loops cannot exclude CoB textures whose net m_z nearly cancels, or partial CoB perturbations under CoFeB walls, which the authors' own simulations show (p. 10). The authors should: (a) estimate a independently — from the XRR thicknesses and nominal compositions the ratio of Co atoms in CoFeB to CoB is ≈ (0.40 × 6.69)/(0.68 × 8.69) ≈ 0.45, to be combined with tabulated per-atom L3 XMCD cross-sections, or measured on the CoFeB-only reference film at both edges — and compare with 0.35; (b) show Fig. 3d for a range of a (e.g. 0.25-0.45, or ±30%) with an uncertainty on a; (c) turn the residual high-field slope of Fig. 3d into an upper bound on the reversed CoB area fraction at 120 mT and 45 mT and compare it with the core area fractions in Figs. 2b and 2d; (d) add helicity-reversed or I0-normalised loops; (e) provide DPC line-profile quantification for the conventional- and inverse-polarity states (Fig. 2k, m) and state the DPC zero-reference assumptions explicitly; and (f) most directly, provide element-resolved spatial evidence — resonant SAXS at both the Fe and Co L3 edges at the same fields at the COSMIC beamline already used (the Co-edge magnetic scattering should follow the CoFeB-only expectation, reduced by a predictable factor, if CoB is uniform), or element-resolved STXM/ptychography/holography. The photon energy used for Fig. 2f-j is not given and must be stated. Without this, the "unequivocal" wording should be softened.

B. Should address

2. The effective-field mechanism, and the J_RKKY on which it rests, are qualitative and not quantitatively consistent with the observed field windows (Discussion pp. 12-13, Methods p. 16). J_RKKY = −0.034 mJ/m² is obtained by equating the out-of-plane saturation field μ0H_s = 125 mT to an RKKY spin-flip field using the bilayer expression J = μ0H_s·M_s·t/2; interior layers of an [A/B]x10 stack couple to two neighbours, which changes the prefactor. More fundamentally, between ~30 and 125 mT the moment rises through maze domains and skyrmions (the paper's own result), so H_s also contains dipolar (stripe-domain) and DMI-dependent annihilation contributions; Fig. 1c also appears to saturate nearer 150 mT than 125 mT. The resulting J is fed into the simulations (Table 2), which inherit this ambiguity. With 2|J|/(M_s t) ≈ 125 mT on an interior CoFeB layer (M_s·t ≈ 5.4 × 10⁻⁴ A), the simple picture puts the sign change of the effective field near 125 mT, where the FM state is observed, whereas the maze state, which the Discussion associates with near-cancellation, is centred near 80 mT; the two skyrmion families sit roughly symmetrically about 80 mT (120 and 45 mT), and the midpoint of the linear part of Fig. 3b is ~90-95 mT. The stack-averaged FM-state magnetisation (~0.27 MA/m, μ0M ≈ 0.34 T) means demagnetising fields are at least comparable to the RKKY field, so magnetostatics cannot be omitted. The authors should give H_eff = H − H_RKKY (including the stack demagnetising field) for each imaged state and show whether the conventional and inverse windows mirror each other about H_eff = 0; use the simulations to give layer-resolved averages of the RKKY, demagnetising and external fields on the CoFeB layers for each state in Fig. 4; and obtain J independently, e.g. from the field at which CoFeB m_z crosses zero or the CoFeB minor-loop shift in the Fe-edge XMCD, the plateau-edge field, an in-plane loop, FMR, or a Ru-thickness series — or justify the formula used.

3. The skyrmionic character of both families is not shown in the main text. The title ("Beyond conventional skyrmions") and the "inverse-polarity skyrmion" label depend on the low-field objects being homochiral Néel skyrmions with the same chirality as the high-field ones and opposite core polarity, hence opposite topological charge, rather than achiral bubble domains or up-magnetised CoFeB bubbles in an AF background. The text says the Néel nature "was verified experimentally using Lorentz microscopy as shown in Supplementary Note S4", but not whether this holds for both families or with which handedness. With D ≈ 1 mJ/m² (not measured) and appreciable interlayer dipolar fields across 10 repeats, hybrid or thickness-dependent chirality is possible (Refs. 19-21). The authors should bring tilt-series Fresnel or DPC evidence for both families into the main text (or summarise it there), give the sign of the wall chirality for each, and show that chirality is the same and core polarity opposite.

4. Novelty relative to known AF/FM phase coexistence in layered antiferromagnets is not established, and the "beyond conventional" framing is not justified (title, abstract, Introduction, Discussion, Fig. 6). Hellwig et al. (Refs. 30-31) and Hauet et al. (Ref. 32) showed that the field-driven AF-to-FM transition in perpendicular [Co/Pt]/Ru layered antiferromagnets proceeds through domains in which a subset of layers reverses, giving FM-phase domains or bubbles in an AF matrix and the reverse; the authors' own Ref. 33 (PRB 109, 134437, 2024) is titled "Phase coexistence and transitions between antiferromagnetic and ferromagnetic states in a synthetic antiferromagnet". The sequence in Fig. 6 (FM, AF bubbles in FM, maze, FM bubbles in AF, AF) is that same sequence, with the reversing sublattice preselected by its lower K_eff and DMI fixing the wall chirality. In the CoFeB layer's own frame (effectively a ferromagnetic multilayer with an exchange bias from a rigid CoB sublattice), the inverse-polarity texture is an ordinary skyrmion in a background saturated along the local effective field; the Discussion (pp. 12-13) effectively says this, and the polarity is "inverse" only relative to the applied field. The authors should state explicitly what Ref. 33 and Refs. 30-32 observed and what is new here — candidates are sublattice selectivity by K_eff asymmetry, homochiral Néel walls in both families, sub-100 nm size, and opposite topological charge at fixed chirality — and frame the title and abstract around that.

5. The micromagnetic model is described as "parameter-matched" but departs from the experimental structure in ways that bear on the claims it supports (Fig. 4, Methods pp. 19-20, Table 2). (i) The stacking order is inverted: Fig. 4e and p. 10 place CoFeB on top of each repeat and as the topmost magnetic layer ("very top CoFeB layer ... CoB below, vacuum above"), whereas the real stack Ta/[Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]x10/Ru/Pt has CoFeB lowest and CoB topmost; the top-layer deviation result therefore does not describe the real sample, and since MFM is surface-weighted this also bears on Fig. 2a-e. (ii) CoB is modelled with CoFeB-like values — 6 Å and M_s = 0.81 MA/m, with K_u rescaled to 0.63 MJ/m³ — against the measured 8.69 Å, 0.63 MA/m and 0.47 MJ/m³; this changes the RKKY field on CoB (∝ J/M_s t), the interlayer dipolar fields, and how hard CoB is relative to CoFeB, which is exactly what the selectivity argument depends on. The CoFeB cell thickness is given as "6 Å (same as the experimental value)", whereas XRR gives 6.69 Å. (iii) Spacer thicknesses (which set dipolar coupling), lateral boundary conditions, damping, convergence criteria and cell size along z for the 400 nm × 400 nm box are not given; a box this size holds only a few 50-80 nm skyrmions, so box size and edges can set texture density and phase boundaries. (iv) D is a free parameter (1.00 and 1.10 mJ/m²) with no stated fitting observable or uncertainty. (v) States are obtained by zero-temperature field-cooling from random configurations, whereas the experiment is a hysteretic field sweep with strong history dependence (±30 mT switching, path-dependent RPM); the reported convergence of all initial conditions to the same state is surprising given maze/skyrmion metastability. (vi) Several supporting simulations are "not shown": the symmetric-stack test, and the J_RKKY dependence ("reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers, whereas stronger coupling localizes the non-collinear state", pp. 13-14), which is the key mechanistic test. The authors should simulate a quasi-static field sweep in the actual layer order with the measured CoB parameters (or show insensitivity), compare simulated total and sublattice-resolved m_z(H) with Figs. 1c, 3b and 3d and simulated diameters and field windows with experiment, state how D was fixed, give the missing numerical details, and show the "not shown" results, including a phase map in (J_RKKY, field) and/or (J_RKKY, K_eff ratio, D ratio) marking which sublattice hosts the textures.

6. The RPM analysis supports reproducible, family-specific pinning sites, but not "independent nucleation pathways" or the labels "external-field-driven" and "RKKY-driven" (Fig. 5, pp. 10-12, abstract). Cross-family RPM values of 0.03-0.05 are called "negligible"/"uncorrelated", but no chance-level baseline is given; for densities like those in Figs. 2b and 2d and any reasonable matching tolerance, random overlap would probably exceed 0.03-0.05, in which case the result is an anticorrelation, not independence. Anticorrelation is what a shared pinning landscape predicts: a site favouring a down CoFeB core at 120 mT is the last to keep down magnetisation at low field, where it becomes part of the inverse-state background rather than a core. Low cross-overlap could also follow from geometry (paths 2 and 4 both start from the maze; a reproducible maze yields conventional skyrmions from segments of one contrast and inverse skyrmions from the other) or from polarity-dependent local anisotropy/thickness pinning, all with a single nucleation mechanism. The authors should give the matching criterion, detection threshold, number of skyrmions per map, map area, how drift between maps at +120 mT and +40 mT was registered, and what the standard-deviation error bars (±0.005 to ±0.03) are taken over; compute the expected RPM for randomised, shifted or rotated maps at the measured densities and tolerance; report the maze-state RPM; overlay conventional and inverse core positions on the same region and compute their spatial cross-correlation, including with the interstitial positions of the conventional lattice; and either support the mechanism labels (e.g. nucleation statistics versus cycling amplitude, links to specific defects) or remove them from the abstract. RPM also probes pinning reproducibility, not "thermodynamic stability" (p. 10).

7. The "general materials-design principle" and "general and transferable" strategy (pp. 13-14) rest on a single stack composition with no control sample; the sister samples share one deposition on three substrates. A symmetric SAF (CoB/CoB or CoFeB/CoFeB at compensation) should show mirrored textures in both sublattices or sublattice-degenerate FM-phase domains, and a stack with reversed K_eff asymmetry, a different Ru thickness or a different J should move textures to CoB or into both sublattices. The authors should add at least one such control stack, or at minimum show the simulated phase map requested in concern 5, and otherwise present the three design criteria as hypotheses and narrow the generality claims.

8. The DMI argument is not supported by measurement. The Discussion (p. 13) says CoFeB has "potentially reduced intrinsic DMI" offset by its smaller thickness, "yielding comparable D_eff", and calls the "enhanced ratio D_eff/K_eff in CoFeB" the decisive asymmetry. No DMI is measured; in the simulations D is a free parameter (1.0 and 1.1 mJ/m²), so the modelled asymmetry lies almost entirely in K_eff, and the argument is built on fitted rather than measured values. "D_eff = D/t" also mixes conventions: D in Eq. 5 and Table 2 (mJ/m²) is already volume-normalised, and the interfacial quantity is D_s = D·t (pJ/m). The authors should measure D for the reference CoB and CoFeB multilayers (BLS, or domain-period/domain-wall-energy analysis from MFM as in Ref. 48), or present the DMI argument as conjecture, and clarify which quantity the simulations use.

9. Claims about topology and dynamics are made without supporting data (p. 10, abstract). The paper states that the two families carry opposite topological charge, are "topologically protected", and "deflect in opposite transverse directions under current", with skyrmion Hall angles supported by simulations that are "not shown". If chirality is not uniform (see concern 3), the opposite-deflection argument fails. The authors should show the simulated trajectories and Hall angles together with the computed topological charge per layer and for the stack, or present these statements as predictions or remove them.

10. The correlative comparison spans different samples, substrates, field branches, geometries and possibly temperatures, without a demonstration that they match. MFM, SQUID and XRR used Si/SiO2; LTEM used 25 nm Si3N4; XMCD and SAXS used 200 nm Si3N4, and substrate roughness and strain can shift K_u and pinning. The MFM fields are positive (+120, +80, +45 mT) while the DPC images are on the negative branch at −131, −89 and −49 mT, with a fixed 17° tilt that adds an in-plane field of ~30-40 mT at −131 mT; this can cant a compensated SAF and distort Néel textures, and the DPC tilt correction assumes purely out-of-plane domains. The XMCD/SAXS endstation uses a cryo-cooled electromagnet, and the sample temperatures for MFM, XMCD, SAXS and LTEM are not stated, although K_eff and J_RKKY are temperature-dependent. The MFM and DPC diameters differ by ~30% (inverse 81 vs 58 nm; conventional 64 vs 48 nm) and the comparison is deferred to Note S3; MFM transfer-function broadening is one cause, but substrate, field and tilt differences may contribute. The number of samples and regions showing each state is not given. The authors should show m(H) or Fe-edge XMCD loops for each membrane sample against the SQUID loop, report the measurement temperature for each technique, discuss the in-plane field's effect on the DPC states and their quantification, define the diameter in each technique and compare sizes at matched |H| on the same branch (or deconvolve the MFM tip transfer function), and state whether both families were observed in more than one sample, region, or after re-cycling.

4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Add element-resolved spatial evidence — resonant SAXS or STXM/ptychography at both the Fe and Co L3 edges at identical fields in both skyrmion states — so the single-sublattice claim no longer depends on the fitted scaling factor a.
2. Quantify the effective-field picture with an independently determined J_RKKY and layer-resolved field balance, plot the observed states against H − H_RKKY, and measure size and density versus field for both families.
3. Rerun the simulations as quasi-static field sweeps in the real stack order with measured CoB parameters, compare sublattice-resolved m(H), sizes and field windows with experiment, and show the J_RKKY/K_eff phase map and other "not shown" results.
4. Recast the RPM section with a random-overlap baseline and a cross-family anti-correlation analysis, and moderate the mechanism labels.
5. Position the work explicitly against Refs. 30-33, show chirality for both families in the main text, and test the design rule with one control stack (symmetric SAF or reversed K_eff asymmetry).
