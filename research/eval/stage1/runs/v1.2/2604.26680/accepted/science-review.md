B. Should address

1. The claim that the CoB sublattice stays laterally uniform, and hence that "all textures reside solely in the CoFeB layers", rests on an XMCD decomposition with an empirically fitted scaling factor (Fig. 3, Eqs. 1–2, Methods "Soft X-ray techniques"). The CoB loop in Fig. 3d is I_CoB = I_Co − a·I_Fe with a = 0.35, and a was chosen so that the reconstructed total loop (Eq. 1) matches the SQUID-VSM loop with a closed central area. Eq. 1 can be rewritten as I_total = I_Co + (1 − a)·I_Fe, which reproduces the net moment only if diode current converts to moment with the same factor at the Co and Fe edges. Absorption cross-sections, elemental fractions (Co68B32 vs Co40Fe40B20) and transmitted backgrounds differ, so the a that fits the SQUID loop need not equal the ratio r of the CoFeB Co-edge to Fe-edge signal that Eq. 2 needs to isolate CoB. The CoFeB loop (Fig. 3b) has large non-hysteretic slopes beyond ±30 mT, exactly where the textures appear, so any error in a adds or removes part of that slope in the CoB loop; whether the CoB loop is "fully square" is thereby set by the fit. The fitting target is also weak: the SQUID-VSM loop was measured on the sample on oxidized Si, the XMCD on a sister sample on a 200 nm Si3N4 membrane. The other probes do not resolve the question on their own: because Ms·t is equal for both sublattices by design (8.85 × 10⁻⁸ A m² each), the DPC step (12.8 ± 0.7 vs 13.6 ± 0.2 T nm) shows that one sublattice reverses fully but not which one, and the DPC zero reference is itself "set by assumptions informed primarily by the XMCD I(H) loops"; MFM and SAXS are not element-selective as presented. The directly measured Fe-edge loop, which shows CoFeB reversing gradually outside the plateau, combined with the one-sublattice DPC result, does support the assignment, but the strength of "unequivocally" and "solely" depends on bounding the CoB contribution. The authors should: (i) fix a independently, for example by measuring Co- and Fe-edge loops on the CoFeB-only reference multilayer in the same geometry, from saturated XMCD amplitudes with the known equal sublattice moments, or from tabulated cross-sections and Co content; (ii) show the CoB loop for a range of a (for example 0.25–0.45), give an uncertainty on a, and state an explicit upper bound on the fraction of CoB reversed at 45, 80 and 120 mT within the noise of Fig. 3d, including the outer layers (see concern 2); (iii) ideally give element-resolved real- or reciprocal-space evidence, such as resonant SAXS at the Fe L3 vs Co L3 edges normalized by the XMCD amplitudes (the edge and photon energy used for Fig. 2f–j are not stated, so existing data may already allow this; Co-edge scattering should scale with the Co content of CoFeB alone), or STXM/ptychography/holography at both edges for the conventional, maze and inverse states. Without this, the wording should be narrowed to "the net CoB moment remains saturated to within X%".

2. The simulated stacking order is inverted relative to the real sample, and the outer layers are not treated (Fig. 4e vs Fig. 1a and Methods; Results discussion of Fig. 4). Experimentally each repeat is Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB from the bottom up, so the topmost magnetic layer is CoB and the bottommost is CoFeB. Fig. 4e has CoB at the bottom and CoFeB at the top, and the text then discusses the "very top CoFeB layer ... coupled to CoB on one side only, with vacuum above", a configuration the sample does not have. This matters because MFM is weighted towards the near-surface layers, and because the real top CoB layer has a CoFeB neighbour on one side only, so its RKKY field is about half that of interior CoB layers; by the authors' own argument it is the CoB layer most likely to host textures. The bottom CoFeB layer is likewise coupled on one side only. The authors should repeat the simulations with the experimental stacking order (or justify why it is irrelevant), correct the top-layer discussion, and address the outer layers explicitly in the XMCD sensitivity estimate of concern 1; each outer layer contributes 1/10 of its sublattice signal, which should be resolvable.

3. The micromagnetic support for confinement to CoFeB depends on free and rescaled parameters, and the simulations quoted in the Discussion are not shown (Methods "Micromagnetic modelling"; Extended Data Table 2; Discussion). D = 1.10 (CoFeB) and 1.00 (CoB) mJ m⁻² are free parameters, with no statement of how they were chosen or how sensitive the result is to them, and A = 8 pJ m⁻¹ is assumed for both layers. With these values κ = πD/(4√(A·Keff)) is about 0.92 for CoFeB and 0.59 for CoB, placing CoFeB close to the domain-wall instability threshold, so the outcome that textures form only in CoFeB is largely set by this choice. CoB is modelled with the CoFeB thickness and Ms (0.81 instead of the measured 0.63 MA m⁻¹) with Ku rescaled to keep Keff; this removes the thickness and Ms asymmetry that the Discussion credits for the Deff/Keff balance (Deff = D/t), and changes CoB's self-demagnetization and wall energy, so the simulated selectivity tests essentially only the Keff asymmetry. The Discussion states that D_eff is "comparable" in both layers, but D was never measured, and it states that "reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers, whereas stronger coupling localizes the non-collinear state" without showing these simulations. The authors should present the RKKY-variation simulations quoted in the text, add a state diagram of which sublattice hosts textures versus D_CoB, D_CoFeB, Keff asymmetry and J_RKKY, simulate the real CoB Ms and thickness (or show these do not matter), and, if possible, measure D for each sublattice on the reference multilayers, for example by Brillouin light scattering or the domain-period method of ref. 48.

4. The effective-field mechanism (Fig. 6, Discussion) is argued qualitatively but never tested against the measured field windows. The model says the CoFeB layers behave as an ordinary CoFeB skyrmion multilayer in an effective field H − H_RKKY. With the stated parameters (Extended Data Table 1), the RKKY field on an interior CoFeB layer from its two CoB neighbours is 2|J|/(Ms·t) ≈ 125 mT (about 63 mT per neighbour), so the effective field would vanish near 125 mT, not near the observed maze window at about 80 mT. The authors should compute H_eff, including the stack's demagnetizing/stray field, for each observed state (conventional skyrmions at +120 mT, maze at +80 mT, inverse skyrmions at +45 mT) and show that the predicted zero crossing and the boundaries of the two skyrmion windows agree with the data. A natural control is to image the existing CoFeB-only and CoB-only reference multilayers by MFM across a field sweep and plot all three systems against H − H_RKKY: if the CoB-only multilayer also hosts skyrmions, the authors must explain why the RKKY field suppresses them in the SAF; if the CoFeB windows do not map onto the SAF windows after the shift, the simple picture is incomplete (for example, interlayer dipolar coupling between tubes matters).

5. The RKKY coupling is extracted with a formula that does not fit this stack (Methods "Sample fabrication"; Extended Data Tables 1 and 2). J_RKKY = μ0·Hs·Ms·t_FM/2 with μ0Hs = 125 mT is the bilayer spin-flop expression. In a 20-layer stack interior layers couple on both sides, which changes the prefactor by about a factor of two (about /4 for interior layers). More importantly, for perpendicular layers measured with an out-of-plane field, saturation proceeds through domain, maze and skyrmion states (Fig. 1c and Fig. 2, saturation near 130–170 mT), so Hs also contains nucleation, domain-wall, DMI and dipolar energies, not RKKY alone. The same J (−0.03 mJ m⁻²) is fed into the simulations and underlies the mechanism. The authors should justify the formula for a multilayer or determine J independently (in-plane hard-axis loop, minor-loop shifts or plateau-edge field, or a reference bilayer SAF with the same Ru/Pt spacer), propagate its uncertainty, and state how the simulated field windows (−130, −80, −50 mT) change with J.

6. The "general materials-design principle" is claimed from a single stack with no experimental control (Discussion; Outlook). All results come from one composition and thickness set, with sister samples "deposited together"; no second growth run or second stack is shown. The decisive role of the D_eff/K_eff asymmetry and the claim that weaker RKKY restores textures in both layers are not tested on any sample. The authors should measure at least one control under matched conditions and with the same XMCD protocol: a symmetric CoFeB/CoFeB or CoB/CoB SAF with matched moments, a stack with a thicker Ru spacer (weaker RKKY), or a stack with the Keff ordering or thicknesses swapped, which should move the textures into CoB. Failing that, the claim should be narrowed to this stack.

7. The novelty relative to known AF/FM phase coexistence in perpendicular SAFs, and the skyrmion character of the inverse-polarity textures, are not established clearly (Introduction; Discussion "form of phase coexistence"; Fig. 2; Supplementary Note 4). In any SAF a local switch between FM and AF states reverses exactly one sublattice, so AF bubbles in an FM background and FM bubbles in an AF background are both expected on the descending branch; FM-aligned stripe and bubble domains in AF backgrounds and phase coexistence were reported in [Co/Pt]/Ru multilayers by Hellwig et al. (refs 30–32), Hauet et al. (ref. 32), Barker et al. (ref. 33, PRB 2024) and Darwin et al. (ref. 35). The authors should state explicitly what is new beyond these: sublattice selectivity (the same CoFeB sublattice hosting both families), Néel chirality, size scale. Evidence for Néel chirality is only in the SI and not shown for both families; the main text does not show whether the in-plane wall components reverse with the core as a fixed DMI requires, nor that the inverse-polarity tubes have uniform chirality through all ten CoFeB layers without Bloch lines. The authors should show tilt-dependent Lorentz (Fresnel tilt series) or DPC wall-vector maps for both polarities in the main text or Extended Data, give the topological charge per layer from the simulations, and state whether the inverse-polarity textures are chiral skyrmions or achiral bubbles.

8. The return-point-memory analysis does not support the mechanistic conclusion drawn from it (Fig. 5, "Return-point memory" section; Supplementary Note 5). Low cross-family RPM (0.03–0.05, Fig. 5c) shows the two families use different sites, but not that one is "external-field-driven" and the other "RKKY-driven"; defects that favour a down core in an up background need not favour an up core in a down background, and along a continuous descending sweep (path 2 → 4) inverse skyrmions are the last CoFeB regions not yet reversed, so they would naturally sit between former conventional sites and low overlap would follow from geometry alone. The authors should report the chance-level RPM for uncorrelated maps at the measured densities and sizes (for example from rigidly shifted or rotated maps), state how maps with opposite MFM contrast were overlaid, add a spatial cross-correlation (for example nearest-neighbour distance from each inverse skyrmion to the former conventional sites) to distinguish anticorrelation from independence, state how many areas and samples were studied, and soften the mechanistic interpretation unless it is supported separately.

9. The correlative comparison combines different substrates, field geometries, calibrations and samples without checking equivalence (Methods; Figs. 2 and 3). MFM and SQUID-VSM were on Si/SiO2, Lorentz TEM on 25 nm Si3N4 membranes, and XMCD/SAXS on 200 nm Si3N4 membranes; membranes can change strain and anisotropy. Matched states occur at different fields (conventional +120 MFM vs −131 mT DPC; maze +80 vs −89 mT; inverse +45 vs −49 mT). In Lorentz TEM the 17° tilt adds an in-plane field of about 0.3·H_z (about 40 mT at −131 mT), and quoted fields are only the out-of-plane component; in an AF-coupled stack with a soft sublattice (Keff = 0.11 MJ m⁻³) this can cause canting or spin-flop and bias or nucleate textures. The three field calibrations (TEM holder Hall sensor, ALS electromagnet, MFM coil) are not cross-checked, and the sample temperature in the cryocooled XMCD/SAXS electromagnet is not stated. The authors should overlay out-of-plane loops on all three substrates (for example XMCD total loop vs SQUID, and a Lorentz field series) to show plateau and saturation fields match, quantify the in-plane-field effect (comparing tilts or simulating the tilted field), cross-check the field calibrations, state the temperature for every technique, and state whether the result is reproduced on more than one sample.

11. Possible MFM tip influence is not tested (MFM methods; Fig. 2; Fig. 5). The Co-coated, high-Q tip sits about 20 nm above a stack whose soft CoFeB sublattice has low Keff and whose total moment nearly cancels. Tip-induced nucleation or displacement, especially near the maze-to-skyrmion transitions, could affect the imaged densities and the RPM, since the same region is rescanned after each cycle. The authors should show that repeated scans at fixed field, scans in opposite directions, larger lift heights, or a cycle without scanning give the same positions and densities.
