## A. Must address

1. **No quantitative measure of the local anisotropy, and no comparison with what isotropic disorder would produce.** The nematic claim rests on three hand-picked boxes in each of Fig. 3a and 3c (roughly 5–8 nm across), chosen because they "seemingly" show modulation along one direction, with their FTs in Fig. 3b,d; on two zooms in Fig. 4c,d; and on one domain boundary in a ~6–8 nm topograph (Fig. 4a). In a random alloy with 7–14% of Sb sites substituted (x = 0.33–0.68), dopant-induced QPI is intrinsically inhomogeneous. In any window of about 6–8 modulation periods, with ½Q_Bragg FT peaks already ~20% of Q_Bragg wide (p. 4), the three symmetry-equivalent directions will have unequal intensity by chance, even if the underlying electronic state is C6 symmetric. Picking the three windows that look most unidirectional will always yield three "domain types". The paper gives no order parameter, no map, no distribution and no null comparison, so Figs. 3 and 4 are illustrations rather than evidence. The authors should: (i) define a local nematic order parameter, e.g. η(r) = (|A_A| − (|A_B| + |A_C|)/2)/(|A_A| + |A_B| + |A_C|), or the full three-component (Potts) vector, from lock-in/Lawler–Fujita demodulation at the three ½Q (or Bragg) vectors with a stated filter length; (ii) map it over the entire fields of view of Figs. 3a,c and 4a; (iii) report its histogram, spatial autocorrelation (correlation length / domain size), the nematic area fraction, and how these depend on window size; (iv) compare with the same analysis on a null: simulated QPI from randomly placed isotropic point scatterers on the DFT band structure (or a C6-symmetric tight-binding model) at the measured dopant density with no nematic term, phase-randomized, pixel-shuffled or symmetrized surrogates of the data, and the pristine sample away from the CDW wavevector. Only if the anisotropy amplitude and correlation length clearly exceed this null does "short-range electronic nematic domains" follow.

2. **The "parent state" and "pervasive across the phase diagram" claims, and the shading of Fig. 1b, are not supported: all data are at 4.5 K.** Fig. 1b shades an "Electronic nematicity" region across the whole composition range (x = 0 to 1) and up to 100 K, above the CDW transition, including regions with no data (above 4.5 K; x between 0.68 and 1). "Parent state of kagome superconductors" (title, abstract, p. 7) means a state that forms at higher temperature and out of which the CDW and superconductivity emerge, and that exists in pristine CsV3Sb5 above T_CDW. With no temperature dependence, the paper cannot say where the anisotropy sets in or whether it is "parent" to anything, and it shows no link to superconductivity in these samples. The authors should either (a) measure the order parameter of concern 1 at several temperatures (e.g. 4.5, 20–30, 50, 80+ K, including above the pristine T_CDW ≈ 100 K if possible) for at least one CDW-free composition, showing that it vanishes or weakens at higher temperature, or bring bulk probes (elastoresistance, birefringence, nematic susceptibility) on the same crystals; or (b) remove the green region from Fig. 1b and restrict the title, abstract and Discussion to what is shown: short-range, low-temperature local C6 breaking in CDW-free Sn- and Ti-doped samples (e.g. "short-range rotational symmetry breaking persists in CDW-free doped CsV3Sb5").

## B. Should address

3. **Residual short-range 2×2 CDW correlations are not excluded, and no q = 0 signal is shown for the Sn samples.** The Discussion claims "short-range q = 0 electronic nematicity", but all Sn-sample evidence is a unidirectional, locally 1Q modulation at ~½Q_Bragg (Fig. 3b,d). This is also what short-range, locally 1Q 2×2 CDW fluctuations, or disorder- or strain-pinned CDW remnants, would look like, and that alternative would undercut "unrelated to the 2×2 CDW". The three arguments against residual CDW on p. 4 (absence at x ≈ 0.1, appearance at "some energies", "slight dispersion") are qualitative: pinned short-range CDW can have energy-dependent contrast, and the dispersion is not quantified (Extended Data Fig. 2 is not provided). The authors should plot the ½Q peak position against energy with error bars and compare with the dispersion predicted by the DFT autocorrelation; test whether the feature's contrast inverts or shifts across E_F, as expected for QPI but not for a static modulation; and show a q = 0 measure of C6 breaking in the Sn samples (e.g. Bragg-peak amplitude anisotropy or intra-unit-cell signal within each domain, as done for Ti in Fig. 4e,f), spatially correlated with the ½Q orientation.
Raised by: r2, r3

4. **For the Ti-doped sample, an electronic origin is not separated from a structural or instrumental one, and the evidence is very limited.** Fig. 4 rests on one ~6–8 nm constant-current topograph at a single bias (+100 mV, 100 pA), plus one more boundary in Extended Data Fig. 3 (not provided). The "domains" in the Bragg-amplitude maps (Fig. 4e,f) are ~1–3 nm across, comparable to both the unstated Fourier filter width and the Ti–Ti spacing at y = 0.2 (about one Ti per 15 V sites). Unequal Bragg amplitudes can come from local strain or lattice distortion around Ti (clusters), local apparent-height variation, an asymmetric tip, scan-direction and feedback effects, piezo creep, uncorrected drift (Lawler–Fujita), or setpoint effects. The authors should: show the same area at several biases of both polarities and in dI/dV maps (a structural distortion should be largely bias independent; an electronic nematic Fermi surface should give an energy-dependent η, possibly changing sign); show the pattern with rotated scan directions; do a displacement-field (Lawler–Fujita) analysis to extract local lattice constants or strain and test whether domain orientation correlates with uniaxial strain; state the filter width and drift-correction procedure; show larger fields of view and more than one region, tip and cleave; and quantify against the null of concern 1. Because momentum-resolved QPI was not obtained for Ti, the statement that Sn and Ti doping are "consistent" (p. 6) compares two different observables (QPI anisotropy versus Bragg amplitude); spectroscopic maps or energy-resolved Bragg amplitudes for Ti would put both on the same footing. Since the "generic" claim rests on agreement between Sn and Ti, the Ti evidence needs to stand on its own.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

5. **Anisotropic QPI in the Sn samples is not shown to reflect an anisotropic electronic state rather than an anisotropic arrangement of scatterers or local strain.** If the ½Q_Bragg features are scattering between M-point pockets (p. 4; Fig. 2f,i,j), the intensity along each direction in a small window is set by the local arrangement of scatterers (Sn dopants, vacancies, Cs adatoms) and by local strain as much as by the band structure; a dopant cluster aligned along one direction gives unidirectional interference with no electronic nematicity. The paper does not locate the dopants (Sn on the in-plane Sb1 versus out-of-plane Sb2 site should be visible in topographs at suitable bias, as the contrast in Fig. 1d–f suggests), test whether domain orientation and boundaries correlate with dopant geometry or local strain, or show that the q magnitude and its energy dependence differ between A, B and C domains, which would signal a locally distorted Fermi surface rather than anisotropic scattering amplitude. The authors should do one or more of these, for example T-matrix QPI simulations on the Wannier model of the Methods using the measured dopant positions, showing that the simulated pattern is isotropic on the domain scale while the data are not. The paper itself notes (p. 7) that strain [30] must be excluded for the chirality question; the same applies to the nematicity claim.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

6. **Continuity between the doped-sample anisotropy and the nematicity reported in pristine AV3Sb5 is not demonstrated.** The paper's logic treats the CDW-linked nematicity in pristine samples [21, 27, 34, 38] and the anisotropy here as one phenomenon, but the observables differ: long-range uniform C2 anisotropy of CDW-peak or QPI intensities in the pristine compound, versus short-range anisotropy of disorder-broadened ½Q QPI (Sn) or of Bragg amplitudes (Ti) here. At x ≈ 0.1, the composition that connects the two regimes, the paper's own marker (the ½Q_Bragg feature) is absent (p. 4; Extended Data Fig. 1), and the only anisotropy there is the known incommensurate charge stripe, a different phenomenon. This conflicts with "persists in all samples" (abstract). The pristine sample is not analysed with the same metric. The authors should apply one identical anisotropy measure to x = 0, ≈0.1, 0.33 and 0.68 (and, for the Bragg-amplitude measure, to pristine and Ti-doped topographs at matched setpoints), show how magnitude and correlation length evolve with x, and address x ≈ 0.1 explicitly, or narrow the claim.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

7. **Actual compositions and bulk properties of the measured crystals are not given, and nominal values do not match the quoted ones.** For Ti, the Methods give nominal loading Cs20V12Ti3.5Sb120, which corresponds to ≈0.68 Ti per formula unit, yet the sample is labelled y = 0.2. For Sn, the nominal Sb:Sn ratios (80:40 and 60:60) lie far above the quoted x ≈ 0.33 and 0.68, and there is no growth recipe at all for the x ≈ 0.1 crystal. Flux growth makes such differences plausible, but the doping axis of the whole phase diagram then rests on unstated measurements, and composition inhomogeneity is itself a possible source of domains. The authors should give EDS/WDS or diffraction-refined compositions of the measured crystals with their spread, ideally the dopant density counted from topographs, and bulk characterization (resistivity or magnetization showing the absence of the CDW anomaly, and the superconducting Tc, since Fig. 1b implies all compositions are superconducting).
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **Sample statistics and reproducibility are not reported.** The paper does not state how many crystals, cleaves, fields of view and tips were used per composition, how many regions were examined, or what fraction show the anisotropy; for x = 0.68 and y = 0.2 single images are shown. "Persists in all samples" (abstract) and "pervasive" (title) require this, and the "same tip" argument (p. 7) does not establish reproducibility across samples. The authors should give a table listing, per composition, samples, cleaves, fields of view, tips and the area fraction above an anisotropy threshold, and show the analysis of concern 1 for at least two independent regions or cleaves per composition.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

9. **Energy dependence of the domain orientation is not shown.** Figs. 3a and 3c are each at a single energy (−200 mV and +50 mV). Nematicity of the Fermi surface should give the same domain orientation wherever the ½Q feature exists (roughly −300 to 0 meV or +100 meV, per the red ellipse in Fig. 2d); orientation that changes with energy would point to scattering from local defect configurations. The authors should show the same boxes (or the full η map) at several energies within that window and report whether orientations and boundaries are fixed.
Raised by: r1

10. **Setpoint and normalization effects are not addressed.** The dI/dV maps in Figs. 2 and 3 use setpoints of −200 mV/500 pA, 50 mV/200 pA and 0.6 GΩ/1 nA; for x = 0.33 the setpoint bias lies inside the energy window where the ½Q feature appears. In a strongly inhomogeneous doped sample, the setpoint effect can imprint topographic or integrated-DOS anisotropy onto g(r,V). The authors should show the domain pattern in L(r,V) = (dI/dV)/(I/V) or in maps taken at a setpoint outside the relevant window, and confirm that the same regions carry the same orientation.
Raised by: r1, r2

11. **The DFT comparison is semi-quantitative and partly mismatched to the data.** The CECs and autocorrelations (Fig. 2i–l) are computed at x ≈ 0.25 (p. 5) while data are at x = 0.33 and 0.68, using linear interpolation of Wannier hoppings between CsV3Sb5 and CsV3Sb4Sn (effectively a virtual-crystal approach with no disorder broadening or rigid band shift), at kz = 0 only, although the data integrate over kz. The statement that ½Q_Bragg connects "exactly" the M pockets depends on the chemical potential, which Sn hole doping shifts; how the Fermi level and the DFT–experiment energy alignment were set is not stated. Only −100 and −500 meV are calculated, while the measured window (ellipse in Fig. 2d) spans roughly −300 to +100 meV. The authors should compute at the measured compositions, show the predicted ½Q intensity versus energy alongside the measured profile of Fig. 2d, and show robustness to kz and energy offset. The computed CECs are C6 symmetric by construction: they explain the wavevector but not the anisotropy, and the text should say so explicitly.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

12. **The mirror-symmetry statement is made by eye.** The paper states that each domain "breaks two in-plane mirror symmetries but appears to generally preserve" the third (pp. 5–6), from small boxes whose FTs span only a few pixels (Fig. 3b,d). The Discussion builds on this (a possible chiral state at lower temperature, comparison with CsTi3Bi5 [42, 43]). The authors should quantify it, e.g. intensities of FT peaks mirror-related across the preserved axis, or the correlation of each domain image with its mirror image about each of the three axes, with uncertainties from the null of concern 1, for each domain type; or drop the claim.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

13. **Domain size and its dependence on doping are not reported, although disorder is proposed to set it.** On p. 6 the short range is attributed to "increased disorder caused by the dopants". If so, the domain size should decrease from x = 0.33 to 0.68, or scale with mean dopant spacing, and differ between Sn and Ti. The authors should quote a characteristic domain size (from the autocorrelation of concern 1), making "nanoscale" and "short-range" concrete, and report it and the anisotropy amplitude versus composition for Sn and Ti, compared with the average dopant spacing. Absence of a systematic trend would favour disorder-generated anisotropy over disorder-pinned nematicity, so this also helps separate concern 1's null from the claimed state.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

14. **Tip artifacts are only partly excluded.** Domains of different orientation imaged with one tip rule out a uniform tip anisotropy, but not a tip that enhances local anisotropy already present. The authors themselves invoke tip anisotropy [41] as a concern for chirality. Imaging the same area with a second tip, or after a controlled tip change, would strengthen the claim.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

15. **The relation to ref. 44 needs discussion.** Ref. 44 reports parallel STM work on Ti-doped CsV3Sb5 proposing a "chiral-nematic Fermi liquid" parent state, i.e. related conclusions from the same material. The authors should state what is new here and compare directly: domain sizes, wavevectors, mirror symmetry and chirality, and whether the results agree.
