A. Must address

1. The measured energies lie outside the kinematic bound of an intra-band continuum, and the "dispersion" extrapolates to a finite energy at zero momentum. An intra-band continuum of a band with Fermi velocity v_F is bounded by ω ≲ v_F·q (plus a small curvature term) and must collapse to zero energy as q → 0. The paper gives the σ-band Fermi velocity as "roughly 2 eVÅ" (p. 2). The σ sheets are nearly kz-independent cylinders (Fig. 1c), so for them only q_∥ matters, and the c-axis component adds little because the σ bands are nearly flat along c. At q_∥ = 0.016 Å⁻¹ (Fig. 2b) the σ intra-band bound is ~0.03–0.05 eV, yet the feature peaks at ~0.3 eV, an order of magnitude higher. At q_∥ ≈ 0.05–0.055 Å⁻¹ (Fig. 3e) the bound is ~0.1 eV, but the experimental peak is at ~0.40 eV (ω/q ≈ 7 eVÅ). Over the whole range of Fig. 3e the data lie well above v_F·q, and the dashed linear fit extrapolates to a finite intercept of ~0.25–0.3 eV at q_∥ = 0. Even with |q| ≈ 0.15 Å⁻¹, v_F·|q| is only ~0.3 eV. The BSE projection (Fig. 2c) nonetheless assigns the feature to σ states near Γ. The quoted velocities are also internally inconsistent: "≈1.4 eVÅ, around half of the Fermi velocity of the σ bands" does not match "roughly 2 eVÅ" (half would be 1 eVÅ), and the slope read directly from Fig. 3e is closer to ~2–2.2 eVÅ (about 0.43 eV at 0.055 Å⁻¹ to 0.66 eV at 0.155 Å⁻¹). The quoted σ velocity of ~2 eVÅ may itself be low compared with DFT literature values for the MgB2 σ and π sheets (roughly 3–5 eVÅ), and the value matters for this argument. The χ⁰ peak positions in Fig. 3e (triangles) do extrapolate toward zero, but they disagree with the data precisely at the smallest q, where the Lindhard picture is most diagnostic; the text dismisses this as spectral weight being "split between different features". Because the identification ("directly map out the Lindhard continuum") rests on the dispersion, and the dispersion as presented lacks the defining property of the intra-band continuum, the central claim does not yet follow. The authors should: state the fit form, the slope and the intercept with uncertainties; give the in-plane and c-axis velocities of each σ and π Fermi sheet; overlay the per-sheet upper edges ω = v_F⁽ⁱ⁾·q, computed with the full 3D q of each spectrum, on Figs. 3b–e; and explain how a feature at 0.3–0.4 eV at q_∥ ≤ 0.055 Å⁻¹, including the clear 0.3 eV peak at q_∥ = 0.016 Å⁻¹, can be an intra-band σ excitation, or else identify which bands (for example π, with their kz dispersion and larger velocities, at the actual total momentum transfer) could support it.

2. The intra/inter-band decomposition in Fig. 4c, which carries the claim that the analysis "conclusively demonstrates" an intra-band origin, looks unphysical and is ambiguously defined, so it cannot exclude inter-band alternatives. At q_∥ = 0.088 Å⁻¹ the "σσ intra" weight extends to ~0.5–0.6 eV and the "ππ intra" weight to ~1.5 eV, implying ω/q ≈ 6–7 eVÅ and ≈ 17 eVÅ, far above any Fermi velocity in MgB2 (≲ 5–6 eVÅ for π, ~2–3 eVÅ for σ). Possible causes include insufficient k-point sampling relative to q, a mislabelled q (for example a large implicit q_⊥), or an "intra-band" label assigned by band-index ordering that mixes bands across crossings of the 6 σ and 2 π tight-binding bands. SI Sec. F states "an N×N×N grid ... where N=512"; if this means 512 points in total (8×8×8), the in-plane grid spacing (~0.3 Å⁻¹) exceeds the entire measured q range, and intra-band transitions at q ≤ 0.17 Å⁻¹ cannot be resolved with δ = 0.01 eV. It is unclear whether transitions between the two σ bands (light and heavy hole), which are degenerate along Γ–A and split away from it, count as "intra" or "inter"; labelling by energy ordering, orbital character or continuity changes the decomposition. This matters because σ1–σ2 inter-band transitions, and π intra-band excitations driven by the large q_⊥, both produce low-energy, q-dependent weight at finite energy as q → 0, and would naturally explain the finite intercept in Concern 1. If σ-light → σ-heavy transitions carry the weight above v_F·q, the feature is inter-band, which would directly contradict the paper's title and central conclusion. In addition, the Fig. 4 caption says σ–π inter-band excitations are negligible, but the legend shows only σσ-inter and ππ-inter, with no σπ curve. The authors should: clarify the k-mesh and show convergence of χ⁰ with respect to mesh and δ at the measured q; define the decomposition precisely (band index a → b for each of the eight TB bands) and show each Fermi-surface-crossing band pair separately; verify that each "intra" channel vanishes above its max(v_F·q) bound; give the σπ inter-band component; and discuss explicitly how σ1–σ2 inter-band transitions are ruled out.

3. The "dispersion" is measured by rotating the momentum transfer at roughly fixed |q|, so in-plane momentum, out-of-plane momentum, incidence angle and polarization all change together. Fig. 3a labels run from (q_∥, q_⊥) = (0.05, 0.14) through (0.10, 0.08) to (0.16, 0.02) Å⁻¹, i.e. |q| ≈ 0.149, 0.128 and 0.161 Å⁻¹. This is close to constant, as expected for a fixed scattering angle at ~190 eV where the photon momentum is ~0.096 Å⁻¹, but the middle point does not fit a constant |q|, so the geometry is unclear. Rocking θ_in mostly rotates q from near the c axis into the plane while simultaneously changing the polarization projection (from in-plane to out-of-plane orbital sensitivity) and the self-absorption. The authors themselves note that grazing geometry changes the σ/π sensitivity (pp. 3–4), and Fig. 4a shows that orbital weighting alone moves spectral weight between dispersive features. The peak shift from ~0.4 to ~0.65 eV (Fig. 3e) could therefore reflect a changing σ/π matrix-element mixture or the q_⊥ dependence of the 3D π sheets, rather than a true in-plane dispersion; the "nearly linear dispersion" and the ~1.4 eVÅ velocity extracted against q_∥ alone (p. 3) then do not have the meaning given to them. A particle–hole continuum depends on the full 3D q through v_F·q, which in MgB2 is strongly anisotropic. It is also unclear whether the Lindhard and RPA calculations (Figs. 3d, 3e, 4) used the actual (q_∥, q_⊥) of each spectrum or only q_∥ along Γ–M with q_⊥ = 0; the axes suggest the latter. The authors should: give a table of θ_in, 2θ, q_∥, q_⊥ and |q| for every spectrum; recompute χ⁰ and χ along the experimental (q_∥, q_⊥) trajectory; show data that separate momentum from geometry, such as a second scattering angle or a second incident energy within the resonance (different |q| at the same θ_in), spectra at the same q_∥ reached at different θ_in, or σ-polarized incident light at the same q (which changes the p_x/p_y weights of Eq. 32 at unchanged q); and show the Γ–K data mentioned in SI Sec. A but never shown, since near Γ the σ continuum should be nearly isotropic in-plane and Γ–M versus Γ–K is a useful consistency check.

B. Should address

4. The BSE RIXS simulations may not be converged at the relevant momenta and energy scale, so their agreement with the data is weaker support than presented. SI Sec. C uses a 36×36×28 k-mesh, whose in-plane spacing (~0.065 Å⁻¹) is comparable to or larger than the measured q_∥ (0.05–0.16 Å⁻¹, and 0.016 Å⁻¹ in Fig. 2b); a Fermi–Dirac smearing of 0.02 Ry ≈ 0.27 eV, comparable to the feature energy itself and blurring the occupations that define the intra-band window, which alone could generate a low-energy peak at a few hundred meV; only 12 bands, with the first 4 occupied bands removed; and 0.1 eV broadening. It is unclear how sub-mesh momentum transfers are represented, and hence whether the agreement in Figs. 2b, 3c and 3e, and the band projection in Fig. 2c used to say the transition is "localized within the σ bands ... close to the BZ center", are robust. The agreement is also imperfect: the BSE spectrum in Fig. 2b has almost no weight between ~0.6 and 2.5 eV, where the experiment shows a large tail, and its low-energy structure has two sharp peaks (~0.15 and ~0.35 eV) not seen in experiment. SCAN was chosen because it best reproduces the XAS (uncaptioned comparison, SI p. S8); whether the low-energy RIXS result is functional-dependent is not stated. The authors should repeat the calculation at one or more q with a denser (or shifted or interpolated) k-mesh and much smaller smearing, show the low-energy feature and its dispersion are stable, explain the treatment of sub-mesh q, and comment on functional dependence. A more direct test of the intra-band assignment would be a "switch-off" test (removing σ intra-band, or all intra-band, transitions and showing the feature disappears) or a projection of the BSE exciton onto band pairs (same band versus different band), giving an intra/inter decomposition of the measured-geometry signal independent of the tight-binding model.

5. The Raman (valence-excitation) nature of the feature and the role of the intermediate state are not tested. All RIXS data are taken at a single incident energy on the sharp, exciton-like σ-hole pre-edge resonance at ~187 eV (Fig. 2a). In metals, near-threshold RIXS commonly contains a fluorescence-like (non-Raman) component whose apparent energy loss shifts with detuning and absorption geometry, and an asymmetric feature at a few hundred meV that rises from the elastic line with a long high-energy tail (Fig. 3a) is just what threshold fluorescence or intermediate-state relaxation would produce. The unoccupied σ states near Γ/A extend only ~0.5–0.8 eV above E_F (Figs. 2c, S1), the same energy scale as the feature, so a process tied to the σ hole-pocket depth could also produce it. The full-geometry BSE reproduces the feature, which is some evidence, but the link between RIXS and an orbital-projected χ⁰ assumed in Figs. 3d–4 rests on this. The authors should show an incident-energy map across the pre-edge at fixed q demonstrating constant energy loss and resonance behaviour consistent with the BSE; add spectra at the main edge (~192–194 eV) and below the edge, where the predicted orbital selectivity differs; and report whether the BSE feature survives with the core-hole potential switched off, or in an independent-particle RIXS calculation.

6. The predicted acoustic plasmon of MgB2 is not discussed as an alternative. Ref. [12] (Silkin et al., PRB 80, 054521), cited by the authors, predicts a low-energy acoustic plasmon from out-of-phase σ and π carrier oscillations, dispersing roughly linearly with a velocity between the σ and π Fermi velocities, in the energy–momentum window studied here. The introduction names acoustic plasmons, yet this collective mode is not considered, and a linearly dispersing peak rather than a broad continuum is arguably closer to that prediction. The authors should state whether the orbitally resolved RPA (Fig. 4a) contains such a mode, or show that it is absent or overdamped, and discuss it explicitly.

8. The comparison relies on peak positions from symmetric fits to an asymmetric continuum; lineshape, edges and intensity scaling are not tested. The intra-band continuum is a bounded continuum, not a peak: for a 2D cylinder Im χ⁰ ∝ ω/√(v_F²q² − ω²) up to v_F·q, its integrated weight should grow with q at small q, and its physically meaningful quantities are the onset and the upper edge. The positions in Fig. 3e come from Voigt fits (Fig. S2) to a feature described as "markedly asymmetric", alongside an elastic line whose tail overlaps it strongly at the lowest q, with the high-energy tail absorbed into another component; the "peak" therefore depends on the fit model, window, background and elastic-line treatment. Electron–phonon coupling to the E2g mode in the σ bands, and phonon or multiphonon contributions near the elastic line, are not considered in the fit. The error bars in Fig. 3e are not defined, and how ω_BSE and the χ⁰ points were extracted (maximum, first moment or fit) is not stated. The authors should state how error bars were obtained, which parameters were free or fixed, and how the elastic line and the 3 eV feature were constrained; fit with an asymmetric or model-based lineshape (for example the broadened, weighted χ of Fig. 4a convolved with resolution); report the leading and upper edges or first moment as well as the maximum, applied identically to data and theory; compare full lineshapes with resolution-convolved χ⁰/χ at each (q_∥, q_⊥); and show how the integrated low-energy intensity scales with q after normalizing for geometry-dependent cross-section and self-absorption.

11. The novelty claims are overstated and need to be placed in context. The title, abstract and introduction state that the Lindhard continuum "has remained experimentally elusive" and that "no feature resembling the textbook illustration ... has been reported". However, the intra-band single-particle continuum dispersing with v_F·q has long been observed by resonant inelastic light scattering from 2D electron gases in semiconductor heterostructures, relying on the same resonance-enabled escape from Coulomb screening invoked here; non-resonant inelastic x-ray scattering on simple metals (e.g. Li, Na, Al) and momentum-resolved EELS have measured S(q, ω) beyond the plasmon cutoff, where the particle–hole continuum dominates and follows the Lindhard form with known corrections; electronic Raman scattering probes intra-band particle–hole excitations in metals; and particle–hole continua have been compared with Lindhard-type calculations in RIXS and momentum-resolved EELS of metallic oxides, including Ref. 23. Ref. [21] reports monochromated EELS on MgB2 that "distinguish intra-band and inter-band transitions", but is cited only for the inter-band features; the paper should explain what that work saw and why the present result differs. The sum-rule argument (Eq. 1) applies only at small q below the plasmon cutoff, and it shows the continuum carries small, not zero, weight in L(q, ω), so the statement that EELS "cannot see" intra-band excitations should be softened. The claim should be restated more precisely, for example as the first momentum-resolved observation by RIXS of the small-q, below-cutoff intra-band continuum via an orbital-selective probe in a 3D multiband metal, and the relation to this literature discussed.

12. The sum-rule argument is not connected quantitatively to MgB2. The statement that "EELS ... cannot see low-energy intra-band excitations" (p. 5) is derived for a single-component system, whereas MgB2 is a multiband metal with σ and π carriers of different velocities. Out-of-phase σ–π density fluctuations are not fully screened and can carry continuum or acoustic-plasmon-like weight in the total response, as the cited ab initio loss function (Ref. [22]) and Ref. [12] address. The authors should compute the total MgB2 loss function at the experimental q from their RPA or GPAW calculation and quantify the ratio of continuum weight in the total response to that in the orbital-projected response, so that the MgB2-specific claim rests on a number rather than on the toy model of Fig. S4; ideally, compare with EELS on the same film.

13. Sample characterization and reproducibility are not documented. The sample (an HPCVD thin film) is described only by reference to the growth paper [24]. The paper does not give film thickness, substrate, orientation or mosaicity, T_c or residual resistivity, surface preparation, or the number of samples or spots measured, and all data are at a single temperature (77 K). At ~190 eV the probing depth is tens of nm, and MgB2 surfaces readily form Mg and B oxides; the experimental XAS (Figs. 2a, S3) shows a strong peak near 194 eV that exceeds the BSE prediction and lies close to the B₂O₃/borate B π* resonance, and substrate contributions (for example SiC, if used) could also add spectral weight. An oxide would not produce a dispersing metallic continuum, but it could add background under the feature and alter the apparent q dependence through the θ_in-dependent probing depth; angle-dependent self-absorption likewise changes relative intensities across the θ_in series. The authors should report the film details; assess surface oxidation (for example TEY versus TFY, XPS, or the 194 eV excess); state whether self-absorption corrections were applied before comparing intensities with the BSE (Figs. 3b, c); and show the feature on a second sample or spot, or after re-cleaning.
