A. Must address

1. The feature does not extrapolate to zero energy as q → 0, and its energy-momentum location is not shown to lie inside the intraband continuum. For an intraband continuum at small q, Im χ0 is bounded by roughly ω ≤ v_F·q, so its weight (and, for a cylindrical σ sheet, its peak near ω ≈ v_F q) must go to zero linearly as q → 0. The paper's own numbers do not fit this picture. The text gives σ-band Fermi velocities of "roughly 2 eV Å". In Fig. 3e the experimental peak is already at about 0.35–0.42 eV at q_par ≈ 0.05–0.055 Å^-1 (ω/q ≈ 6–7 eV Å, about three times the stated σ velocity, where the σ continuum would extend only to about 0.1 eV), rises to about 0.6 eV at about 0.155 Å^-1, and the dashed linear guide extrapolates to roughly 0.25–0.3 eV at q_par = 0. At q_par = 0.088 Å^-1 the peak is about 0.5 eV (ω/q ≈ 5–6 eV Å), so the quoted 1.4 eV Å "speed" must be a slope with a large offset. In Fig. 2b (q_par = 0.016 Å^-1) the low-energy (dagger) feature still sits at about 0.2–0.3 eV, whereas any in-plane σ intraband response would lie below about 0.03–0.05 eV, inside the 100 meV elastic line. The tight-binding χ0 points in Fig. 3e (blue triangles) do head towards low energy at small q_par, and the text concedes they deviate from experiment there ("split between different features"); only the BSE reproduces the small-q end. BSE agreement alone does not settle the matter, because BSE includes core-hole and matrix-element effects, so it does not show that the feature is the ground-state χ0 continuum. A gapped, linearly dispersing feature with a ~0.3 eV offset fits a collective or interband excitation, or a resolution/elastic-tail artifact, at least as naturally as the Lindhard continuum, and this is the central test of the headline identification. The authors should state explicitly what happens to the feature as q → 0; fit ω(q_par) with a free intercept and report the intercept with its uncertainty (or use a functional form with a physically motivated offset); show the low-q spectra, including the q_par = 0.016 Å^-1 data, with the elastic line and fitted components; overlay the analytic continuum boundaries ω = v_F·q for each band on Figs. 3b–d and 4c; explain the finite-energy offset (out-of-plane momentum, band anisotropy, interactions, interband admixture) or narrow the claim accordingly; and, if possible, add data at smaller total |Q|, for example at smaller 2θ.

2. The apparent in-plane dispersion is confounded with changes in out-of-plane momentum and in polarization/orbital projection, and the momentum labels are hard to reconcile with the geometry. q_par is varied only by rotating θ_in at fixed 2θ. At 2θ = 150° and 187 eV, |Q| ≈ 0.18 Å^-1 is nearly fixed, so rotating θ_in trades q_perp against q_par: the Fig. 3a labels run from (0.05, 0.14) to (0.16, 0.02), so |q| is roughly constant (~0.15–0.16) across the series and q_perp is largest exactly where q_par is smallest (including at q_par = 0.016 Å^-1, relevant to concern 1). The σ sheets are quasi-2D, but the π sheets are 3D, and Fig. 4c assigns a large share of the low-energy weight to ππ intraband transitions, which disperse with q_perp. The same rotation also changes the projection of the incident polarization onto in-plane (σ; p_x, p_y) versus out-of-plane (π; p_z) orbitals, the mechanism the authors themselves use for the intensity suppression and the cos^4/sin^4 RPA weights; and the low-energy peak lies higher in π than in σ polarization (Supplementary Fig. S5), with ππ-intraband weight at higher energy than σσ in Fig. 4c. The peak could therefore shift upward towards grazing incidence through reweighting alone, with no true q dependence. In addition, the labels do not match the geometry: |Q| = 2k sin(θ/2) is about 0.18 Å^-1 at 2θ = 150° and about 0.13 Å^-1 at 2θ = 90°, and neither matches sqrt(q_par² + q_perp²) for both (0.16, 0.02) and (0.05, 0.14); the implied |Q| is not constant as it should be at fixed 2θ; the top label q_par = 0.16 lies outside the Fig. 3e axis; and the units (Å^-1 or r.l.u.; the Methods say r.l.u.) and the 2θ for each dataset are not stated. If the "in-plane dispersion" is really a rotation of a fixed-length Q, the σ-band (2D cylinder) picture and the slope-based velocity comparison must be reworked. The authors should give the full 3D Q, with units, 2θ, beamline and temperature, for every spectrum; state whether the χ0, RPA and BSE calculations (Figs. 3c–e, 4) use the full 3D q of each spectrum or only q_par along Γ–M; state in-plane and out-of-plane Fermi velocities of each band in the TB model; and separate the dependences experimentally, using the BESSY 90° and ALS 150° geometries to compare spectra at the same q_par with different θ_in (and vice versa), and fitting the dispersion separately in σ and π incident polarization.

B. Should address

3. Interband σ1–σ2 transitions are excluded using the wrong response function, so "conclusively demonstrates" (p. 4) is not supported. The paper's central argument is that RIXS measures an orbital-projected response (χ_{px px}, χ_{py py}, χ_{pz pz}; Eq. 5, Methods), yet the intra/interband decomposition said to exclude interband transitions (Fig. 4c) is of the total charge χ0. In the total-density vertex, transitions between the two nearly degenerate σ bands near Γ–A (light and heavy hole) are suppressed at small q by band-orthogonality (coherence) factors; in a p_x- or p_y-projected vertex, which a linearly polarized B 1s → 2p transition selects, they are not. Such σ1 → σ2 transitions near k_F have a finite energy as q → 0 set by the σ-band splitting at the Fermi surface (a few hundred meV), which would naturally produce the finite intercept and weak dispersion of concern 1, and their weight depends on matrix elements the model omits. The BSE projection in Fig. 2c places the weight on the σ bands, which fits either picture. The authors should repeat the Fig. 4c decomposition into σσ-intra, σσ-inter (σ1 ↔ σ2), ππ and σπ parts for the orbital-projected χ0_aa and χRPA_aa, for both polarizations, shown next to the polarization-resolved data of Supplementary Fig. S5; if possible decompose the BSE RIXS spectrum itself into intraband and interband final states; and otherwise temper the language or tie the claim explicitly to experimental tests (polarization dependence, lineshape, q → 0 behaviour).

4. A collective or interaction-renormalized response, in particular the acoustic plasmon, has not been ruled out, and "quantitative agreement with simulations of the non-interacting charge susceptibility" is overstated. MgB2 has σ and π carriers with different Fermi velocities, and the out-of-phase σ–π acoustic plasmon predicted for it (refs. 15, 26) disperses linearly with a velocity between the two, the same phenomenology as the observed feature; moreover the unscreened orbital-projected channel of Eq. 5 is exactly the out-of-phase channel that hosts it. The main text dismisses it only with "expected at a smaller energy range" and a pointer to Supplementary Section F, with no numbers. Interactions clearly matter for this feature: Fig. 3e shows χ0 failing at small q_par (at q_par ≈ 0.055 Å^-1 the χ0 peak is about 0.25 eV, about 0.1 eV below the data, and the χ0 intensity peaks at larger q than in experiment), and the calculation said to reproduce the data is the interacting, orbitally weighted RPA (Fig. 4a), which visibly moves weight to higher energy relative to χ0 (Fig. 4b). The paper does not say whether the RPA intensity maximum is a pole or a reweighted continuum. The authors should plot the RPA peak dispersion in Fig. 3e alongside χ0, BSE and experiment; show the eigenvalues of [1 − Vχ0] (or the loss function) of their RPA model across the measured q range to establish whether any pole lies in the window; state in the main text where the acoustic-plasmon branch falls in the same (q_par, q_perp) coordinates, compared with refs. 15 and 26; and, if the best description is an interaction-dressed orbitally weighted response, say so in the title and abstract, or otherwise back "quantitative agreement" with a lineshape-level comparison.

5. The lineshape and width have not been tested against the Lindhard form; only peak positions from a phenomenological asymmetric Voigt are compared. A continuum differs from a (Landau-damped) mode in its lineshape: a low-ω onset with Im χ0 ∝ ω, weight concentrated just below a sharp upper edge (for a 2D cylindrical sheet Im χ0 ∝ ω/sqrt(v_F²q² − ω²) below ω = v_F q), and a width growing linearly with q. The text mentions only qualitatively that the feature broadens "towards grazing incidence". Relatedly, for a 2D sheet Im χ0 peaks at the upper edge, so a peak tracking about 0.7 v_F needs explaining (hexagonal warping of the σ sheets, q_perp, matrix elements, or convolution with the elastic tail). The evidence for agreement is otherwise mostly visual comparison of colour maps (Figs. 3b–d, 4a,b). The authors should fit the spectra directly with resolution-convolved (100 meV) χ0_aa and χRPA_aa (σ- and π-intraband parts with free amplitudes, plus an elastic line as the only extra component), show lineshape overlays with residuals at several q_par against BSE, χ0 and RPA, report width and integrated intensity versus q compared with χ0 and RPA predictions, compare the direction of asymmetry with Lindhard, and use the onset or upper edge of the continuum, compared with v_F q from the TB model, as the quantitative test rather than the peak maximum.

6. The low-energy feature is not shown to be a Raman-like valence excitation rather than a fluorescence-like or core-hole-induced feature. It is resonantly enhanced at a sharp, excitonic-like pre-edge (Fig. 2a) in a metal. Near threshold, fluorescence-like contributions can give features whose loss energy follows the incident energy, and the core-hole potential produces particle-hole shake-up in the intermediate state (x-ray-edge-singularity physics) that appears as a low-energy asymmetric RIXS tail. All low-q data are at one incident energy, and Supplementary Fig. S2 is cited only for the features above 2 eV. The BSE exciton projection (Eq. 2, Fig. 2c) identifies the states in the absorption step but does not establish that the final state is an intraband σσ pair described by ground-state χ0. The authors should show a detuning series at fixed q across the pre-edge (for example 186.5 to 188 eV in small steps), demonstrating that the 0.3–0.6 eV feature stays at constant loss and giving its resonance profile, with the BSE detuning dependence for comparison; and quantify how much of the low-energy BSE intensity comes from core-hole shake-up versus the bare intraband channel, for example by switching off or reducing the core-hole potential.

7. The quoted Fermi velocities, the measured slope and the energy scales of the calculated continuum do not agree with one another. The abstract says the velocity is "comparable to the Fermi velocity"; the text gives 1.4 eV Å and calls it "around half" of a σ velocity quoted as "roughly 2 eV Å", but 1.4/2 = 0.7; and the slope read from Fig. 3e looks closer to 2 eV Å. In Fig. 4c (q_par = 0.088 Å^-1) the σσ intraband weight peaks near 0.35 eV and extends to about 0.5 eV, and the ππ intraband weight extends to about 1.5 eV, implying edge velocities of about 4–6 and 15–17 eV Å, inconsistent with the quoted σ velocity and with in-plane π velocities expected from DFT. The velocity is also compared only with σ. The authors should state how 1.4 eV Å was obtained (fit range, whether the intercept was free, uncertainty), give the TB Fermi velocity of each sheet along Γ–M and along c, state the broadening and q_perp used in Figs. 3d and 4c, explain why the calculated continuum extends to these energies, and make the abstract and text statements agree.

8. Sample characterization, surface state and reproducibility are not documented. The Methods describe HPCVD growth only qualitatively, with no film thickness, substrate, c-axis orientation, Tc, residual resistivity ratio, XRD or surface preparation, and no statement of how many films were measured or which datasets (ALS at 77 K or BESSY II at 28 K) appear in Figs. 2 and 3. MgB2 surfaces readily form MgO and boron oxides/hydroxides; B2O3 has strong B K-edge (π*) resonances near 194 eV, in the 192–196 eV region where the TFY spectrum in Fig. 2a has structure the BSE does not fully reproduce. 10. The RPA and BSE model choices are unexplained or tuned, and their convergence and sensitivity are not shown. For the BSE, resolving q_par down to 0.05 Å^-1 (Γ–M ≈ 1.18 Å^-1) requires a dense k-mesh near the Fermi surface, but the OCEAN k-mesh, broadening and screening parameters are not given in the main text, and the SCAN wavefunctions may have σ velocities different from the TB model. 12. The novelty claim and the generality of the sum-rule argument are stated too broadly. The intraband continuum has been probed before: non-resonant IXS in simple metals at q of order k_F and beyond the plasmon cutoff, and on MgB2 itself (ref. 29, single-particle versus collective crossover); electronic Raman scattering, which detects intraband continua at q ≈ 0 through the same non-conserved (unscreened) vertex mechanism and has been measured in MgB2; momentum-resolved EELS on correlated metals; and ref. 24, which reports separating intraband from interband transitions in MgB2 by EELS. The statement that the continuum "has remained experimentally elusive" (and that no textbook-like feature has been reported) should be limited to the small-q (q ≪ k_F), plasmon-dominated regime, and these works discussed, with the distinction made explicit (momentum resolution, access to the dispersing edge). Likewise, the Discussion's claim that RIXS "will generically capture the Lindhard continuum" rests on Eqs. 4–5, derived from a two-band toy RPA with orbital-diagonal coupling that treats the RIXS cross-section as an orbital-projected χ and neglects core-hole and intermediate-state effects. The authors should state these assumptions and the conditions under which χ_aa keeps O(1) weight (ratio of orbital susceptibilities, at least two orbital channels), comment on when they fail (single-orbital metals, away from the pre-edge resonance), and check that the full BSE calculation shows the same lack of screening, for example by comparing RIXS-weighted and total-density (EELS-like) responses at the same q.
