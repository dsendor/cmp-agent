B. Should address

1. The graphene stacking sequence is assumed, not measured. The Methods state that the powder data contain only (00l) reflections and the single-crystal data only (hk0)-type reflections ("hkl (l = 0)"). The stacking sequences in Table 1 (A|AB|B for stage 2; AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB for stage 4) are then "concluded" from four assumptions in the Methods, by analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 intercalation compounds. Neither data set fixes the lateral registry of graphene layers across a RuCl3 gallery or within a graphene block, and the c-period multiplicity is consistent with several registries; the two candidate stage-4 sequences in Table 1 already show that the data do not choose. Table 1, Fig. 1c,d (both captioned "stage 4: S4" with different stackings) and the DFT models (ABAB|ABAB, ABCA|ABCA) also refer to different sequences. This matters because the motivation (flat bands of rhombohedral ABC graphene), Fig. 1f, the DFT comparison (Figs. 4c,d and 5d,e) and the Conclusions' "we determined the crystal structure" all rest on the stacking. The authors should show the indexed XRD patterns in the main text with a structure-factor refinement of the (00l) intensities for the staged models, and obtain off-axis (h0l)/(hhl) reflections (for example a rotation or precession dataset) or zone-axis cross-sectional STEM/HRTEM that resolves graphene registry. If that is not possible, they should state plainly which stacking features are measured and which are assumed, remove the implication that rhombohedral blocks are present, treat the DFT stackings as candidate models, and make Table 1, Fig. 1 and the DFT refer to the same candidates with the notation mapping explained once.

2. The agreement between computed and measured quantum-oscillation frequencies is overstated and depends on a free Fermi-level shift. At the stated charge transfer the models give 511 T (A|A), 131/444 T (AB|AB) against a single β = 170 T in stage 2, and 70/296 T (ABAB|ABAB) or 28/311/330 T (ABCA|ABCA) against 91 and 176 T in stage 4; neither stage matches. A rigid 0.1 eV shift then gives 27/183 T and 126/214 T, still 20-40 T off, and only after predicted pockets are pushed below E_F or left unexplained (for example 444 T in stage 2); further shifts are invoked to explain the absence of α in stage 2. No shifted result is given for AB|AB, the model assigned to the stage-2 sample, and the size and sign of the shift are not tied to any independent quantity (Hall density, Bader charge, interlayer distance). With two to three pockets per model, a free shift and a free choice of stacking, the comparison has nearly as many adjustable choices as data points, so the claim that the response is "governed by the stacking sequence" and the description "good agreement" (p. 5) are not supported. The authors should plot every model's frequencies as a continuous function of E_F shift (or transferred charge per C) with the measured stage-2 and stage-4 frequencies overlaid, give the shifted AB|AB result, state whether one shared shift fits both stages and whether ABAB and ABCA need different shifts, explain why unobserved predicted pockets would be invisible up to 9 T, and tone the conclusion down to: the frequencies are consistent with strong hole doping of the graphene layers but do not fix the stacking.

3. The nearly identical β frequency in stage 2 (170 T) and stage 4 (176 T), Fig. 2g,h, is not explained, and the 91 T α frequency is of uncertain origin. The DFT predicts quite different pocket sets for the two stages, yet the dominant frequency is the same within FFT resolution. A natural alternative is that β comes from the graphene layer adjacent to RuCl3, whose doping is set by the interface and is largely independent of stage; Fig. 3b, where depletion concentrates on the interface layers in the four-layer models, supports this. It could also come from a minority phase common to both samples (for example stage-2 regions in the stage-4 crystal), and the "sample-dependent" α peak raises the same question. If so, the oscillation spectrum is not evidence for the assigned stage or stacking. The authors should discuss these scenarios explicitly, compare the interface-layer pocket area across all models, and test them with the effective mass of β in stage 2 versus stage 4 and with angle dependence (concern 6).

4. The level of theory used for the charge transfer is not justified. All band structures and Bader charges are non-spin-polarized GGA without Hubbard U or spin-orbit coupling. In that treatment α-RuCl3 is metallic with t2g states at E_F, whereas it is a spin-orbit-assisted Mott insulator; this also contradicts the text's statements that RuCl3 remains "rather insulating" and that Ru atoms keep "their magnetic nature" (p. 4). Charge transfer at graphene/α-RuCl3 interfaces depends on where the empty Ru t2g (upper Hubbard) states lie relative to the Dirac point, which depends on U, magnetic order and SOC; earlier graphene/α-RuCl3 work (refs. 12 and 34, ref. 12 by some of the same authors) used GGA+U(+SOC) with zigzag order for this reason. The 0.68/0.48 eV graphene shifts for A|A and AB|AB, and hence every computed frequency, rest on this choice, and the later 0.1 eV correction is of the order of plausible U-induced shifts. The authors should repeat at least the A|A or AB|AB case and one four-layer stacking with GGA+U (a range of U) in a zigzag or ferromagnetic configuration, ideally with SOC; report how the Dirac-point shift, transferred charge and pocket areas change; and justify the 0.1 eV shift by this sensitivity rather than by unmodelled "stacking faults".

5. The title, abstract and conclusions overstate what is shown. The title promises a "three-dimensional platform for exotic quantum phases", the abstract claims "comprehensive characterization" and "access to novel correlated and topological states", and the introduction calls RuCl3 "magnetically active" with "possible spin-liquid behavior". No magnetization, susceptibility, specific heat, neutron or muon data are shown, and the calculations are non-magnetic. Whether the intercalated single RuCl3 layers keep local moments, order (bulk α-RuCl3 orders near 7 K) or are quenched by the roughly 0.5 eV hole transfer, which changes the Ru valence substantially, is the key question for the proposed use; the rhombohedral flat-band link depends on the unconfirmed ABCA assignment. The authors should either add at least M(T) and M(H) with field parallel and perpendicular to c on the stage-2 and stage-4 crystals (specific heat if possible), or retitle the paper and rewrite the abstract and conclusions as a synthesis, structure and fermiology study, stating that magnetic and correlated properties remain untested. The Conclusions' caveat ("subject to further spectroscopic and magnetic validation") should be reflected in the abstract.

6. Temperature- and angle-dependent SdH, and hence the dimensionality and origin of the pockets, are missing. Only single-temperature FFTs (1.8 K and 2.5 K) with field along c are shown. There are no Lifshitz-Kosevich effective masses, which would sharply discriminate between Dirac-like, bilayer-like and flat-band-derived pockets (for example a flat-band ABC pocket versus a Dirac-like AB pocket) when compared with the computed cyclotron masses; no Dingle analysis to argue whether unobserved pockets should be visible up to 9 T; and no angular dependence F(θ) to confirm that the β and α orbits are quasi-2D (F ∝ 1/cos θ) graphene-layer sheets rather than a 3D pocket of residual graphite or a disordered phase. Only in-plane ρxx is measured, so the "three-dimensional" label is also untested: with about 9 Å RuCl3 spacers the graphene blocks may be electronically decoupled, which is what the 2D-pocket analysis implicitly assumes. The authors should add T-dependent and rotation-angle SdH on crystals characterised by XRD, compare m* with the unfolded DFT bands, state the kz dependence of the computed extremal areas (the band calculations use a 6x6x2 mesh and stage-4 blocks are coupled along c), and ideally measure c-axis resistivity (ρc/ρab).

7. The structural approximations in the DFT models need quantitative bounds. The 2x2 RuCl3/5x5 graphene cell imposes about 4% tensile strain on RuCl3, whereas the measured a = 29.6 Å implies RuCl3 slightly compressed (5 × 5.92 Å) on a 12 × 12 graphene cell; the strain benchmark against a 5x5/12x12 cell is given only for A|A (SI) and only through the band shift. The graphene-RuCl3 distance is fixed at 3.60 Å as "suggested by X-ray data", although XRD gives only the c repeat; how this follows from c = 25.4 Å (gallery 12.7 − 3.35 = 9.35 Å and its split between RuCl3 thickness and two van der Waals gaps) is not shown. The graphene-graphene distance of 3.54 Å, well above graphite's 3.35 Å, is not justified, and relaxations use the Γ point only. The stage-2 structure is simplified from A|AB|B to AB|AB on the grounds that charge transfer is insensitive to registry, but the bands near K may not be. The unfolded Fermi-pocket boundaries (Fig. 5) are taken from highest-intensity points, with an acknowledged but unquantified "systematic error", and the dhva cross-check covers only A|A. Since the text states that the Fermi surfaces are "highly sensitive to the interlayer distances", the authors should give the change in pocket areas in tesla between strained and unstrained cells and for d(Gr-RuCl3) and d(Gr-Gr) varied by about ±0.1 Å (or use distances from the measured c, or relax them with D3), quantify the unfolding error (for example from the spread between ridge tracing and the dhva code) and extend the cross-check to at least one multilayer stacking, and say whether the 20-40 T residual mismatch is significant in light of these numbers.

8. The Hall, SdH and DFT carrier counts are not shown or cross-checked. A Hall density of about 7.8 × 10^20 cm^-3 (p. 3) is quoted without the sample, temperature, carrier sign, ρxy(B) data or linearity. A single-band reading is questionable for a multicarrier GIC with residual graphite. A Luttinger count for a 170 T pocket with 4-fold degeneracy gives about 1.6 × 10^13 cm^-2 holes per graphene layer (E_F about 0.47 eV); with two layers per 12.7 Å in stage 2 that is about 2.6 × 10^20 cm^-3, roughly a factor of three below the Hall value. The authors should show ρxy(B) for both stages, state the fitting model, and tabulate for each sample the density from Hall, from the sum of SdH pocket areas and from the DFT/Bader charge (Fig. S4, with Bader charges per graphene layer in the main text), discussing any discrepancy (multiband Hall, unresolved pockets, inner layers, residual graphite). This also gives an independent constraint on the Fermi-level shift in concern 2.

9. The chemical composition of the intercalated crystals is not characterised. No stoichiometry is given (for example C_xRuCl3 from mass uptake, EDS/WDS or EELS), and there is no check for co-intercalated Cl2, Ag or AgCl from the photolysis source, reduced Ru species or chlorine-only intercalated regions; the Cl2-anneal control shows that chlorine alone changes ρ(T). Without this it is not shown that the intercalant is stoichiometric RuCl3 rather than RuCl3−x or a mixed chloride. The authors should provide STEM-EDS/EELS maps across the layers with Ru:Cl ratios and absence of Ag, and XPS for the Ru oxidation state if possible.

10. The non-intercalant control stops at resistivity. The Cl2-annealed Kish graphite is shown only as an R(T) curve (Fig. 2b), yet chlorine, acceptor-type surface doping or AgCl/Ag residues could themselves dope graphite and create high-frequency hole pockets. The authors should show magnetoresistance and the SdH/FFT of the Cl2-annealed graphite, ideally also of graphite treated with AgCl alone, measured and analysed identically (temperature, field range, window), to confirm that the 91/170 T orbits require RuCl3.

11. Phase purity, stage mixing and the STEM evidence are not quantified. The text mentions "a small portion of residual pure graphite" without phase fractions, the synthesis section notes mixed staging at longer reactions, the SdH discussion invokes stacking faults, and the STEM (only in the SI) is described as qualitative, with a layer-by-layer pattern "not observed throughout the sample". The c values and stage assignment are central but currently supported only by SI figures. The authors should move the indexed powder (00l) patterns for both stages into the main text, estimate stage and graphite fractions from (00l) intensities and linewidths (for example a Hendricks-Teller-type mixed-stage analysis), and show a representative cross-sectional HAADF image with line profiles giving the measured repeat distance against the expected 12.7 Å and 19.4 Å, with statistics of stage domains and the unintercalated fraction. These fractions matter for deciding which phase gives which oscillation.

12. Sample-to-sample reproducibility is asserted but not shown. The text says a second piece of the stage-2 crystal and "multiple independently prepared samples reproduced the same FFT peak", and that α is "sample-dependent", without sample counts, spread or data; no stage-4 sample lacking α, or stage-2 sample showing it, is presented. Quantum oscillations are dominated by the highest-mobility regions and are insensitive to minority phases, so a common frequency does not prove "uniform intercalation". The authors should state how many crystals of each stage were measured, add a table of all crystals (stage, growth batch, frequencies, RRR, which show α) with overlaid FFTs from at least two independently grown crystals per stage in the SI, and link each transport sample to XRD of the same crystal or batch.

13. The SdH harmonic assignments are inconsistent with the fundamentals and the FFT procedure is not given. In Fig. 2f, ν1 = 4.13 T but 2ν1 is labelled at 9.3 T (not 8.26 T); in Fig. 2g, β = 170 T but 2β is at 359 T (not 340 T); in Fig. 2h, 2β = 347 T against an expected 352 T. A 4-9 T window gives a resolution of about 7 T, so the 19 T offset in stage 2 is not a resolution effect, while for the Kish FFT a 1.5-9 T window gives only about 1.8 T resolution, making 4.13 and 5.16 T hard to resolve. A peak near 360 T could be one of the predicted larger pockets (296-444 T), which would change the comparison in concern 2. The authors should state the field window, background polynomial order and windowing for each FFT, give resolution and uncertainty for every quoted frequency, and either justify the harmonic labels (for example by temperature dependence, since harmonics decay with twice the mass) or reassign these peaks as independent frequencies.

14. The novelty claim needs reconciling with prior work. The Conclusions state that graphite intercalated with RuCl3 was synthesized "for the first time", yet ref. 27 (Postnikov et al., 1977) concerns graphite compounds with ruthenium chlorides, and there is other early Ru-chloride GIC literature. The authors should state precisely what is new: for example the α-RuCl3 phase, stage control, the CVT/AgCl route, crystal quality or the quantum oscillations.

16. The in-plane cell and the XRD Methods text need clarifying.
