A. Must address

1. The chemical identity and composition of the intercalant are not established. The paper calls the guest layer α-RuCl3 throughout and describes the samples as "fully" or "comprehensively" characterized. The only evidence, however, is (00l) and (hk0) diffraction, indexed by analogy with the FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 intercalation compounds (Synthesis and Structure analysis sections, pp. 2-3 and 6-7, assumptions 1-4, Table I, Fig. S1). No composition or phase-identity measurement is shown: no EDS/WDS, no mass uptake or TGA giving the C:Ru:Cl ratio, no XPS of the Ru valence, and no Raman. Growth takes place at 830-900 °C in a Cl2-rich ampoule that contains Ag/AgCl. A chlorine-rich or reduced ruthenium chloride, co-intercalated Cl2, or an Ag-containing species therefore cannot be excluded, and the layer need not keep the honeycomb Ru network that underlies the Kitaev motivation. The authors should:
   - report the composition per stage, from EDS/WDS on cleaved surfaces and cross-sections or from weight gain;
   - give the Ru oxidation state from XPS;
   - show Raman spectra for both stages, since the G-band splitting into bounding and interior modes is the standard check of stage and charge transfer in acceptor GICs, and the RuCl3 phonon modes test the intercalant structure;
   - show that no Ag-containing phase is present.

2. The match between the SdH frequencies and the DFT pockets depends on one adjustable Fermi-level shift, and it does not discriminate between models (Electronic properties section, pp. 5-6; Fig. 2g,h; Fig. 5). Without the shift, the calculations do not reproduce the data:
   - AB|AB (stage-2-like) gives 131 and 444 T, against a single observed 170 T.
   - ABAB|ABAB gives 70 and 296 T, and ABCA|ABCA gives 28, 311 and 330 T, against 91 and 176 T observed in stage 4.

   Agreement is then obtained by shifting E_F by about 0.1 eV, giving 27/183 T and 126/214 T, but only for the stage-4 models. The sign, how the shift was chosen, and its stated justification ("plausible variations in interlayer spacing") are not quantified. No shifted result is given for stage 2, so it stays unexplained why the 444 T pocket is absent and why 131 T does not match 170 T. With two or three predicted pockets per model, one free parameter and one or two measured frequencies, both stage-4 stackings can be brought near the data, so the comparison cannot test the paper's central theory statement that stacking controls the number and size of hole pockets.

   The near-identical main frequency in the two stages (β ≈ 170 T and 176 T) is not discussed, even though DFT predicts quite different pocket sets for them. The simplest reading is a common origin: the RuCl3-adjacent interface graphene layers, which Fig. 3b shows carry most of the depletion in both stages, or a minority or stage-2-like phase common to both crystals. The absence of α (91 T) in stage 2 is called "sample-dependent", but only one sample per stage is shown.

   The experiment allows more discriminating tests, and the authors should:
   - measure temperature-dependent amplitudes (about 1.8-20 K) and fit them with Lifshitz-Kosevich to get the cyclotron mass of each frequency, then compare with the DFT band masses (m* = ħk_F/v_F for near-Dirac bands);
   - measure the angle dependence, to show whether each frequency follows F ∝ 1/cos θ, as a quasi-2D graphene-derived pocket should, rather than coming from a 3D or RuCl3-derived band;
   - give Dingle temperatures, to judge whether a 444 T pocket could have escaped detection below 9 T;
   - make a Luttinger-count comparison: a single fourfold-degenerate 170 T pocket corresponds to about 1.6 × 10^13 cm^-2 holes. Compare this with the DFT doping per layer and with published graphene/α-RuCl3 hole densities (refs 10, 11, 34);
   - measure SdH on more than one crystal per stage;
   - state which DFT pocket each measured frequency is assigned to, at which E_F, and what the shift implies for the transferred charge;
   - say which stacking, if any, the data favour, and give the remaining mismatch.

3. The claim that the compound was synthesized "for the first time" (Conclusions, p. 6) conflicts with the paper's own reference list. Ref. 27 (Postnikov et al., J. Struct. Chem. 18, 819 (1977)) is titled "Synthesis and structure of layer-type graphite compounds with ruthenium, palladium, osmium chlorides", and ref. 30 covers metal-chloride intercalation more broadly. Since this is a central claim of a synthesis paper, it has to be corrected. The authors should discuss what the earlier work reported (staging, c axis, in-plane structure, composition) and state precisely what is new. Candidates are single-crystal growth with staging control, the coincidence-lattice structure, the quantum-oscillation characterization, or identification of an α-phase honeycomb layer.

B. Should address

4. The stage, and especially the graphene stacking, are inferred by analogy rather than measured (Table I, Fig. 1, Fig. S1, assumptions 1-4). Only 00l (powder) and hk0 (single-crystal) reflections are observed; the l ≠ 0 reflections are absent by geometry (Fig. S1 caption).
   - The 00l indices are all even for stage 2 (004, 006, 008, 0012, 0022) and all multiples of 3 for stage 4 (006 ... 0051). The data therefore give identity periods of about 12.7 Å and 19.4 Å, which agree with a roughly 9.35-9.4 Å sandwich plus one or three 3.35 Å graphite gaps. The doubled and tripled cells (25.4 Å and 58.2 Å) and the stacking sequences in Table I come from assumption 4, not from the measurements. For stage 4 the paper itself cannot choose between "AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB".
   - Assumption 1 says the RuCl3 layer is incommensurate with the host. Yet the hk0 reflections of both stages are indexed with a common hexagonal cell, a = 29.62(3) Å and 29.5(4) Å, which implies a commensurate supercell close to 12×12 graphene / 5×5 RuCl3. N, M, n and m are never stated.
   - Residual graphite is visible in Fig. S1, but the graphite fraction and any mixed staging are not quantified. It is also not stated whether the transport specimens themselves were checked by XRD.

   These stackings are used to choose between Bernal and rhombohedral models in the DFT, and the title, abstract and conclusions link the samples to rhombohedral flat-band physics. The authors should:
   - obtain l ≠ 0 reflections (rotation, tilted single-crystal or Laue geometry), or present the stacking as a hypothesis and remove the claims that depend on rhombohedral stacking;
   - state the in-plane supercell explicitly and reconcile it with assumption 1;
   - quantify the phase fractions by fitting the 00l series;
   - confirm the staging of the crystals used for transport.

5. The electronic-structure method makes α-RuCl3 metallic, and charge transfer, the quantity that drives the result, depends on exactly this (Methods, p. 7; Figs. 3-5; Fig. S5b,c). All calculations are non-spin-polarized GGA without U or spin-orbit coupling. At this level the Ru t2g bands sit at the Fermi level, and Fig. S5b,c shows RuCl3-derived bands crossing E_F, with dispersive features near K. The main text nevertheless says the RuCl3 layer remains "rather insulating" (p. 5) and that "only the graphene layers are expected to contribute" to the oscillations. Here the transferred charge, and therefore the Dirac-point shift and every pocket area, is set by aligning the graphene Dirac point with a spuriously metallic Ru manifold. Earlier graphene/α-RuCl3 work, including refs 12 and 35 by some of the same authors, used GGA+U+SOC with magnetic order because the transferred charge depends on these ingredients. The paper's own conclusion that the frequencies are "highly sensitive" to charge transfer means this choice bears directly on concern 2. The authors should:
   - repeat at least the A|A, stage-2 and one stage-4 model in GGA+U(+SOC) with zigzag or ferromagnetic order, or justify why non-magnetic GGA reproduces the charge transfer;
   - report how the Dirac-point shift and the pocket frequencies change with U;
   - state whether any RuCl3-derived Fermi-surface sheets remain that could produce the 91 T or 170 T orbits.

6. The DFT geometries are not tied to the measured c axes, and the interlayer distances quoted in the main text and the SI conflict.
   - The main text fixes d(Gr-RuCl3) = 3.60 Å, "suggested by X-ray data", and d(Gr-Gr) = 3.54 Å, which is larger than graphite's 3.35 Å. The XRD gives only the identity periods, so it is not shown how 3.60 Å was derived from them.
   - The SI uses d(Gr-RuCl3) = 3.765 Å and a graphene-graphene distance of 10.241 Å across the RuCl3 layer. It is not stated whether d(Gr-RuCl3) is measured to the Ru plane or the Cl plane.
   - Fig. S4a marks the "experimental" A|A cell at c ≈ 10.0 Å, whereas the measured sandwich is about 9.35 Å (12.7 − 3.35 ≈ 19.4 − 3 × 3.35).
   - The computed charge transfer changes by about 10% over c = 9.3-10.5 Å, and the SI says it "is strongly affected" by this distance, while the main text describes it as insensitive. Fig. S4a also shows hole doping increasing as c shrinks, so using the experimental spacing would push the computed frequencies further above experiment, against the direction needed for agreement. Since variation in interlayer spacing is the stated reason for the 0.1 eV shift, this bears directly on concern 2.

   The authors should tabulate, for each model, the c-axis repeat against the measured one (25.4/2 Å and 58.2/3 Å). They should use one consistent, figure-defined set of distances, recompute the charge transfer and frequencies at the experimental sandwich thickness, report the sensitivity of the pocket frequencies to d(Gr-RuCl3), and present the Fermi-level shift together with this result.

7. The Hall data were measured (the Methods say transverse resistivities were antisymmetrized) but are not shown. They are the most direct test of the charge-transfer picture. The Hall coefficient, and its field nonlinearity if several pockets are present, gives the carrier sign and density. These can be checked against the Luttinger count of the observed pockets and against the DFT charge transfer (Fig. S4b, about 0.09-0.10 e per RuCl3). As a scale, a single fourfold-degenerate 170 T Dirac-like pocket corresponds to about 1.6 × 10^13 cm^-2 per layer and E_F ≈ 0.45-0.5 eV. Please include ρxy(B) for both stages with this comparison, and consider using the Hall density to fix the Fermi-level shift independently.

8. The framing as a "platform for exotic quantum phases" in the title, abstract and conclusions, with its appeal to Kitaev, spin-liquid, correlated and flat-band physics, goes beyond the evidence.
   - No measurement probes the RuCl3 layers' moments or magnetic order: no susceptibility, magnetization, specific heat, or ρ(T) anomaly near the roughly 7 K ordering of bulk α-RuCl3. The SdH data at 1.8 K are below that temperature.
   - It is not shown whether the layers remain magnetic or Kitaev-like after receiving about 0.1 e/f.u. (Fig. S4b).
   - No flat-band or correlated behaviour of the graphene blocks is measured, and the rhombohedral stacking is not established (concern 4).

   The authors should either add basic magnetic characterization of both stages, compared with pristine α-RuCl3: χ(T) with H ∥ ab and H ∥ c, C(T), and low-field ρ(T) over about 2-15 K. Otherwise they should narrow the title and abstract to what is shown: a new staged intercalation compound with strongly hole-doped graphene layers.

9. The control experiments do not yet rule out doping from chlorine or chloride species, and they are not matched to the intercalated samples (p. 2, Synthesis; p. 3, Transport; Fig. 2b,c).
   - The Cl2-annealed control is shown only in zero-field ρ(T). Its XRD (c axis), magnetoresistance and SdH spectrum at 1.8 K and 9 T are not shown, yet they are the relevant null test that the high-frequency pocket comes from RuCl3 rather than from Cl, AgCl or other chloride exposure.
   - The intercalation host is natural graphite flakes from HQ Graphene, while the transport reference and the annealed control are Kish graphite, so differences in mosaicity, defect density and RRR enter the comparison.
   - The control was annealed at 900 °C "for several days", against 22-48 h for the growth runs.

   Please measure the actual host flakes, pristine and Cl2-annealed with the growth time and temperature profile, including SdH and Hall. Ideally, also measure a sample grown with AgCl but no RuCl3 under the same two-zone conditions.

11. The FFT analysis is not documented, and the harmonic labels do not match the fundamentals (Fig. 2f-h, p. 3).
    - In Fig. 2g, 2β = 359 T, against 2 × 170 = 340 T. In Fig. 2h, 2β = 347 T, against 2 × 176 = 352 T.
    - For a window of about 4-9 T the resolution is about 1/(1/4 − 1/9) ≈ 7 T. The 19 T stage-2 offset is therefore well beyond resolution, and the 359 T peak may be a separate pocket (possibly the second DFT pocket), which matters for concern 2. The same resolution is comparable to the 170 vs 176 T difference between the stages.
    - In Kish graphite, the "2ν1 = 9.3 T" peak coincides with ν1 + ν2 = 9.29 T rather than 2ν1 = 8.26 T. ν1 = 4.13 T and ν2 = 5.16 T are about 1 T apart, close to the roughly 1.8 T resolution of a 1.5-9 T window.
    - α (91 T) has only about 12 periods in the window, and its robustness is not shown.

    Please show the background-subtracted Δρ against 1/B, state the field windows, polynomial order, windowing and padding, give frequency uncertainties and the resolution, show that α and β survive changes in these choices, and revisit the harmonic labels.
