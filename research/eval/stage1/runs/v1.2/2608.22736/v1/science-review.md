A. Must address

1. **The statistics behind the parity "rule" are not given.** The main text (p. 7) states that "all odd-length chains host bound YSR states ... while even-length chains show no sub-gap YSR signal". However, the SI (Figs. S3-S14) shows one chain per length, except N = 11 (two chains, Fig. S11) and N = 8 (a second chain only for the collective modes, Fig. S8c). Fig. 3d has several odd-chain points for some N, so more chains were measured, but the number of chains and termini is never stated, and the caption says "a few cases were found outside the mapped range" without saying how many. The central claim is a deterministic alternation across all lengths, so its strength depends on how often it holds and on whether chains with defects, kinks or ends at particular herringbone sites were excluded. The authors should add a table listing, for each N: the number of chains and termini measured; the number with a resolvable YSR peak, an ST/TS peak, or neither; δE_YSR at each end (or an upper bound for even N); E_ST/E_TS; the chain conformation (kink sequence, e.g. Figs. 2c, S10, S13) and the herringbone site (fcc, hcp, ridge/elbow) of each terminus; and the tip used. They should state the exclusion criteria and report every exception, including any odd chain without a resolvable YSR state and any short even chain (N ≤ 10) with one. Fig. 3d should then be based on this full dataset.

B. Should address

2. **The quantitative test of the two-site model cannot separate the J_K renormalization from scatter in J_K, and the flagship chain lies outside the stated parameters.** In the model, δE_YSR = Δ − (E_i − E_GS) is about 3J_K/4 for a free spin-1/2 and about J_K/2 for an odd chain with |J| ≫ J_K, a factor of 2/3. The input J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV already spreads by about 33%, so the shaded band in Fig. 3d covers roughly 0.1-0.5 meV, nearly the whole range of the odd-chain points (0.15-0.55 meV). The odd-chain scatter, which the authors attribute to site-dependent J_K, is as large as the predicted effect, so the comparison cannot distinguish J_K* = 2J_K/3 from no renormalization, and the model's predicted trend (binding energy rising as N grows and |J| falls) is hidden. Moreover, the N = 11 chain of Fig. 2 and Section S2 has δE_YSR ≈ 0.8 meV (Fig. S11a, "outside the mapped range"). For an odd chain with |J| ~ J_K, this requires J_K of roughly 1.1-1.6 meV, well outside 0.6 ± 0.2 meV. The statements that the mechanism is "independently validated" and that "the model reproduces the experimental evolution well" therefore rest on the presence or absence of YSR states, not on quantitative agreement. The authors' own tool allows a decisive test: measure δE_YSR at one end of several odd chains of different length, dehydrogenate the other end, and measure again. On the same terminus, with the same J_K and adsorption site, the model predicts an increase of about 3/2 for short chains (N = 3, 5, 7) and a ratio approaching 1 for long chains. The same protocol on several even chains (only N = 8 is shown, Fig. S16; N = 14 in Fig. S15 is a different regime) predicts emergence from zero. The before/after pairs, plotted as J_K*/J_K against each chain's measured |J| directly on Fig. 3c, would make the renormalization quantitative. The authors should also state how many chains entered the J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV average ("several chains" are mentioned but only N = 14 with δE = 0.36 meV, implying J_K ≈ 0.48 meV, and N = 8 are shown), the formula used to convert binding energy into J_K, and whether J_K is an energy or a dimensionless product with ν0 absorbed. All chains, including the out-of-range ones, should appear in Fig. 3d.

3. **The "absence" of YSR states in even chains must become a quantitative upper bound, and the text contradicts itself on where YSR reappears.** In the model, even chains do not lose their YSR states; the states move toward the gap edge as J/J_K grows. For N = 10 (|J| ~ 1.0 meV, |J|/J_K ~ 1.7), Fig. 3c gives J_K*/J_K ≈ 0.3-0.4, implying a binding energy of about 0.1-0.2 meV, comparable to the dGSJ linewidth in Figs. S10b and S12. The red model band for N = 10-14 predicts binding energies of about 0.1-0.25 meV, comparable to some odd chains, yet the even-chain points in Fig. 3d sit at δE ≈ 0 without error bars or a fitting procedure. The authors themselves note that many odd-chain YSR states overlap with the dGSJ peaks and need two-Lorentzian fits (Section S2). The dGSJ peaks at even-chain ends in Figs. S6, S8b, S10 and S12 also look lower and reshaped compared with the substrate reference, which could be unresolved YSR weight, as the authors argue for N = 12 and 14 (Fig. S14 inset). The authors should fit every even-N end with the same two-peak (dGSJ + YSR) lineshape and tip-DOS deconvolution used for odd chains (Section S2, Fig. S2), with and without a YSR component, report the largest binding energy consistent with each spectrum, and plot these bounds with error bars against the red band in Fig. 3d. They should also reconcile p. 7 ("already detected for N = 12 (Fig. S12)"), the Fig. 3 caption ("appears only for N ≥ 14 (Fig. S14)") and the Fig. S12 caption ("particle-hole asymmetry ... attributed to incipient emergence").

4. **The single-site BCS model is not matched to the proximitized film, and its parameters are model-dependent.** The two-site model (Eqs. S7-S9) couples each edge spin to a zero-bandwidth BCS site with gap Δ. The substrate is a ballistic proximitized film whose low-energy spectrum is dominated by dGSJ resonances below Δ_Nb (Fig. 1d), for which the authors say a Dynes function fails (Section S2), and binding energies are measured from E_dGSJ. The authors' earlier work (ref. 38) shows that YSR states in such films hybridize with dGSJ states and are pushed away from them near the gap edge, exactly where the small even-chain and long-chain binding energies lie. The model is called "qualitative" yet is used quantitatively in Figs. 3c and 3d, and J_K = 0.6 meV is quoted as if it were a physical exchange although it is an effective parameter of a zero-bandwidth model. The model also assumes J_K1 = J_K2 (footnote/ref. 48), although the two ends of the same chain show clearly different YSR energies (e.g. Figs. S7b, S9b, S11a, S13b). The authors should state which gap plays Δ in the model (Nb gap, E_dGSJ or Δ_sample = 1.2 meV); show how δE_YSR and the even-chain prediction change when J_K1 ≠ J_K2 and say whether the Fig. 3d bands use J_K1 = J_K2; state which conclusions survive if J_K is only an effective parameter; discuss how dGSJ-YSR hybridization affects the extracted δE; and benchmark the J_K*/J_K curves of Fig. 3c against NRG or a finite-bandwidth model with a proximitized normal layer (refs. 27, 28, 38) for the relevant parameters (|J|/J_K ≈ 1-20, J_K/Δ ≈ 0.5).

5. **The weak-coupling regime is assumed outside its stated range and is tested on only one end.** Section S5 and Fig. S15 assume J_K ≪ Δ, but J_K ≈ 0.6 meV against Δ_sample ≈ 1.2 meV is not small, and binding energies in Fig. 3d reach 0.5 meV of about 1.2 meV. The interpretation of all odd chains assumes a free-spin (unscreened) ground state, including for the S = 1 triplet, where two-channel screening and the position of the quantum phase transition differ from the spin-1/2 case. The only evidence is the tip-approach series of Fig. S15c, on a single dehydrogenated N = 14 end (a free spin-1/2). That interpretation assumes the tip reduces J_K rather than changing the junction or adding tip-induced potential scattering, and the YSR state merging into the dGSJ peaks as the tip approaches is also how a YSR state hybridizing with dGSJ states might behave. The authors should repeat the tip-approach test on several intact odd-chain ends at different N (including the 0.8 meV N = 11 end) and on a passivated end with larger binding energy, and show how the J_K* curves of Fig. 3c and the bands of Fig. 3d change for J_K/Δ ~ 0.5, or cite an NRG benchmark at comparable parameters.

6. **The ground-state spins (S = 1 for odd chains, S = 0 for even chains) are inferred, not measured.** The abstract and p. 7 assign these ground states from the YSR pattern, the weak TS peaks (Figs. S7, S9, S11, S13) and the two-site model, which simply assumes J changes sign with parity (Section S5). The spin-1/2 character of each end is likewise inferred from the model. There is no magnetic-field dependence and no normal-state reference in which the TS/ST excitations or the YSR state would show the Zeeman response expected for each multiplicity, or a Kondo signature on odd chains only. The authors should add field-dependent spectra below the Nb critical field, or normal-state spectra (above Tc or at a quenching field), on at least one odd and one even chain; alternatively, show that the full BLBQ calculation (Section S4) of the sign and magnitude of the edge J reproduces the measured TS/ST intensity ratios quantitatively, including J_K*.

7. **The topological and fractionalization claims go beyond the evidence.** The text says the YSR maps "establish the topological origin of the observed YSR states" (p. 5), that the edge states "remain robust in a superconducting environment" (p. 3, p. 9), and that fractionalization "survives". End localization with zigzag-edge shape (Fig. 2f) is what any unpaired spin on a terminal unit would give, and the even-odd alternation of the ground-state spin follows from Lieb-Mattis for any finite antiferromagnetic chain on a bipartite lattice. Neither establishes symmetry-protected topological order. Because J_K ≪ J0 (0.6 meV against 18 meV), survival of the edge states is expected rather than tested, and no perturbation (defect, substrate site, kink) is used to test protection. The spin-1/2 character rests on the ST/TS energies (Fig. 3e) and the model, not on the YSR maps. The authors should soften this wording, or add evidence specific to the Haldane phase: for example, show that a defect inside the chain creates new edge spins with YSR signatures; compare the measured YSR decay length along the chain, and its N-dependence, with the edge-state localization length from the BLBQ model; and measure the natural reference structures, an isolated triangulene (N = 1, S = 1, expected to show YSR) and an N = 2 dimer (singlet with large J, expected to show none), which the 3:1 precursor ratio should produce on the surface.

10. **The attribution of the J_K scatter to the herringbone reconstruction is untested, and chain conformation is not considered.** The text (p. 6, ref. 45) says the scatter in δE_YSR is "probably due to the Au(111) herringbone reconstruction" without showing any correlation. The chains have different cis/trans kink sequences (e.g. Figs. 2c, S10a, S13a), which may change both J (through the end-unit geometry) and J_K. The two ends of the same chain sometimes differ markedly (Figs. S7b, S9b, S11a), and two different N = 11 chains differ too (Fig. S11a,b). Film thickness, local gap variations and the number of film preparations are not quantified. Because this scatter is what limits the quantitative test in concern 2, the authors should correlate δE_YSR with the terminal unit's herringbone site (fcc, hcp, soliton/elbow), the local dGSJ energy and the chain conformation, using the topographs they already have, and report both ends of each chain in Fig. 3d.
