A. Must address

1. **The curvature signature is not shown to be specific to flat bands, and competing explanations for sub- or superlinear ρ(T) are not excluded** (title; abstract; Figs. 3, 4(a), 4(e); conclusion). The model's only input is a steep energy dependence of D(E) within a few k_BT of E_F (Fig. 1(c), Figs. 2–3, Eq. 1). Any transition-metal compound with E_F near a d-band edge has this feature: Pd, Pt, A15 compounds and Heusler alloys show sublinear or superlinear ρ(T) for exactly this reason. That is Mott's s-d scattering, which the authors themselves cite (Refs. 29, 45–47). Several other well-known mechanisms also curve ρ(T) at high temperature: resistivity saturation near the Mott–Ioffe–Regel limit (parallel-resistor behaviour, Fisk–Webb, Wiesmann et al.); thermal expansion, phonon softening and anharmonic or multiphonon scattering, including the constant-pressure versus constant-volume difference up to 862 K; a temperature-dependent chemical potential; spin-fluctuation (paramagnon) scattering, relevant for Ni3In (a strange metal near magnetic order), CaRh2 and the Ni-, Co-, Pd- and Pt-based compounds; and the electron–electron scattering the model explicitly omits (p. 2). Crucially, the two compounds that show sublinearity, Ni3In and CaRh2/Ca(Rh,Ru)2, are also the ones with the largest resistivity (about 100–160 µΩ cm in Fig. 4(a)), exactly where saturation is generic, so the correlation in Fig. 4(a) cannot separate the FB mechanism from saturation. As it stands the agreement shows consistency, not inference. The authors should (i) fit the same data with a parallel-resistor/saturation model (ρ_sat as a parameter), with Eq. 1 plus a saturation term, and with a plain BG-plus-thermal-expansion model, and compare goodness of fit and the physical plausibility of the parameters; (ii) include at least one reference metal with a smooth D(E) near E_F, ideally isostructural to a FB compound (a Cu3Au-type or CoSn-type analog without a FB near E_F), measured with the same setup over the same range, showing that Eq. 1 reduces to BG and ρ(T) is linear above Θ_D; (iii) include a metal with a steep but ordinary d-band edge (Pd/Pt type), or a low-resistivity compound with a FB edge above E_F, or a sublinear compound with no FB near E_F, and state what distinguishes a flat band, i.e. show that sublinearity tracks the FB rather than the magnitude of ρ; and (iv) if (iii) cannot be distinguished, narrow the claim from "detecting flat bands" to "steep DOS features near E_F".

2. **The claim that ρ(T) can locate or track the FB is not demonstrated** (abstract, "one can infer the position of FBs"; p. 3, "derived a fit formula (see Eq. 6 in the SM) to pinpoint the position of the FB"). In Fig. 4(a) the FB position is an input through D_DFT(E), and only the prefactor C is fitted; this tests whether the DFT DOS is compatible with the data, not whether ρ(T) determines E_F − E_edge. The only extraction of a position is the CoSn argument in Fig. 4(e), where a visually chosen departure from linearity near 400 K is equated with Δ_DFT/3. The inverse procedure the abstract and conclusion advertise, SM Eq. 6 with E_edge and m_FB free, is never applied in the main text: no extracted Δ or m_FB, no uncertainties, and no discussion of parameter correlations (Δ vs m_FB vs C, and the choice of n and Θ_D). The authors should apply SM Eq. 6 (or treat a rigid shift of E_F relative to the DFT DOS as a fit parameter) for every compound, reporting E_F − E_edge and m_FB with confidence intervals; show how χ² of the Eq. 1 fit varies with rigid shifts of D_DFT(E) of, say, ±20, ±50 and ±100 meV (if shifts of tens of meV do not change χ² appreciably, the method cannot "pinpoint" the FB); and compare the extracted values, ideally blind, with independent determinations (ARPES for CoSn, Ni3In and CaRh2, Refs. 23, 32, 38, 39; DFT; γ) in a per-compound table. The most convincing test would be a series in which E_F is tuned through the FB edge within one family (electron/hole-doped CoSn such as Co1−xFexSn, In-site substitution or Ni3In/Ni3Sn alloys, the Ca(Rh,Ru)2 series, or pressure), showing the extracted Δ moving as expected and the curvature changing sign.

B. Should address

3. **The model assumptions behind Eq. 1 are not stated or justified, although they control the sign of the effect** (Eq. 1; Figs. 2–3). Eq. 1 is not derived in the main text. (a) It keeps only D_DFT⁻¹(E) inside the thermal average and drops the energy dependence of the conducting carriers' velocity and DOS, which enter σ(E) = e²D_c(E)v_c²(E)τ(E); this holds only in the s-d limit where carriers live entirely in a dispersive band, whereas in the real compounds the FB states also carry current and v²(E) varies strongly in the same window. (b) It uses the total DFT DOS, including the FB states themselves, as the scattering phase space, rather than, for example, the DOS of the flat-orbital character. (c) It assumes an energy- and momentum-independent electron-phonon matrix element for intra- and interband channels alike. (d) Factoring the thermal average of D⁻¹ out as a multiplicative prefactor to the BG integral is an ad hoc separation; in a proper Boltzmann treatment the energy-dependent rate sits inside the transport integral. (e) It is unclear whether μ is fixed or computed with particle number conserved; with a narrow, high-DOS band within 20–100 meV of E_F and k_BT up to about 75 meV, μ(T) shifts by roughly (π²/6)(k_BT)²D′/D, tens of meV at 800 K, which by itself produces sub- or superlinear ρ(T) (Figs. 2(b), 4(b)). (f) The scattering phase space is evaluated quasi-elastically at D(E), although k_BΘ_D ≈ 20–40 meV is comparable to Δ ≈ 20–100 meV, so D(E ± ħω) is relevant. The authors should derive Eq. 1 from the two-band Boltzmann model in the main text or SM, state these assumptions, and show that the conclusions survive a calculation with the energy-dependent rate inside the transport integral, v(E) included, a self-consistent μ(T) and inelastic phase space, recomputing Fig. 3 and the Fig. 4(a) fits accordingly.

4. **The "single free parameter" statement is inaccurate, and fit quality is judged by eye without a null comparison** (p. 3; Eq. 1; Fig. 4(a)). Eq. 1 also involves Θ_D, the exponent n (3 or 5) and, since the data in Fig. 4(a) clearly have ρ0 ≠ 0 (Ni3In and Ca(Rh,Ru)2 start well above zero), a residual resistivity that Eq. 1 does not contain. The text does not say which n was used for which compound, whether Θ_D was fixed (from specific heat?) or fitted, how ρ0 was handled, or the fit range; for polycrystals the geometric-factor uncertainty and grain-boundary contributions further loosen the absolute scale. The six fits are overlaid on a crowded panel, and several (e.g. Pd3Pb above 500 K) visibly deviate. The authors should provide, per compound, a table of every parameter with its source (fixed or free), DOS source and settings, fit range and fitted C (with implied λ_ep or λ_tr); show residuals and a goodness-of-fit metric; and fit plain BG with the same freedom and no DOS factor as a null comparison, so the improvement attributable to D_DFT(E) can be judged. The fitted C could also be checked against independent λ_ep estimates (for example from the low-T T⁵ coefficient in CoSn), turning a free prefactor into a consistency test.

5. **Electron correlations are neglected in the materials where they matter most, especially Ni3In** (p. 1–2, "we do not include electron-electron scattering"; p. 4; Figs. 4(a)–(c)). Ni3In, the main sublinear example, is a strange metal with ρ_ab linear in T down to the lowest temperatures (p. 4, Ref. 23), which BG with n = 3 or 5 cannot reproduce; e-e scattering contributes at all temperatures. The DFT DOS at E_F of about 2.8 states/eV for Ni3In (Fig. 4(b)) gives γ ≈ 6.6 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻², whereas Fig. 4(c) shows γ ≈ 50 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻², a mass enhancement of about 7, far beyond typical electron-phonon values (about 3 for CoSn). Such renormalization narrows the FB and can shift it relative to E_F; ARPES and DMFT place the Ni3In FB differently from GGA-PBE. Using the bare DFT DOS as scattering phase space is therefore questionable. The authors should quantify the enhancement for each compound (stating the per-formula-unit normalization); discuss how a correlation-renormalized FB (DFT+DMFT or the ARPES-measured position) would change the fit; show the Ni3In fit range and the low-T mismatch explicitly and explain why an e-ph-only fit over 1.5–300 K is meaningful; and explain why e-e scattering, which can also give superlinear ρ(T), is negligible at 300–860 K.

6. **The model band structure does not match the FBs in the real materials** (Fig. 1(c) vs Fig. 2 insets vs Fig. 4(b)). The model uses a heavy parabolic band with a single edge, giving a monotonic, step-like σ(E). The schematic in Fig. 1(c) and the DFT DOS in Fig. 4(b) instead show narrow peaks with two sides; with E_F near a peak both sides are thermally sampled, and the clean sub/superlinear dichotomy tied to "E_F above/below the edge" is not obvious. Kagome FBs also touch a dispersive band at a quadratic band-touching point rather than being detached heavy parabolas. The authors should show model results for a peak-shaped DOS (a Lorentzian, or a kagome tight-binding DOS with E_F on either side of the peak) and define operationally what "FB edge" means for the DFT DOS in Fig. 4(b).

7. **The semiclassical treatment is used outside its range of validity** (Fig. 3(c); Fig. 4(a)). For m_FB = 100 m0, Fig. 3(c) gives a resistivity maximum near 500 µΩ cm, well beyond the Ioffe–Regel limit, and Ni3In and Ca(Rh,Ru)2 lie at 100–160 µΩ cm, where the mean free path approaches the lattice spacing and Boltzmann transport with a well-defined τ(E) is doubtful. 8. **The onset criterion T ≈ Δ/3 and its application to CoSn are not robust as presented** (Fig. 3(e); Fig. 4(e); p. 4). The claim that CoSn ρ(T) departs from linearity at about 400 K ≈ Δ_DFT/3 depends on the window used to define the dashed linear reference and on visual judgement, and superlinearity above room temperature is common from thermal expansion. The factor 1/3 was shown in Fig. 3(e) for one parameter set only (m_FB = 10 m0, Δ = 100 meV). The Δ_DFT value used is also unclear: the text says the CoSn FB edge is "more than 0.1 eV below E_F", but the arrow in Fig. 4(b) sits at about −0.09 eV. The authors should state the fit window and give an uncertainty for the 400 K departure point; show dρ/dT versus T, which reveals the onset without a reference line, and compare with d²ρ/dT² predicted by Eq. 1; state the Δ_DFT used; and map the onset T/Δ over a grid of m_FB, FB bandwidth and DOS shape, and n, to establish or qualify the Δ/3 rule.

11. **The high-temperature measurements are thinly documented** (p. 2; Figs. 4(a), 4(e)). Data on CoSn single crystals and Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb polycrystals extend to 862 K, but the text does not describe the apparatus for 1.5–300 K and 300–862 K or how the ranges were joined, the atmosphere, contact geometry and method, heating/cooling protocol, the geometric-factor uncertainty, the number of samples, or the stoichiometry and phase purity (XRD/EDX) of the polycrystals. 12. **Anisotropy and sample form are not accounted for in a scalar-DOS model** (Figs. 4(a), 4(d), 4(e)). CoSn is measured along c only, Ni3In data are in-plane, and Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb are polycrystals. The kagome FB is quasi-two-dimensional, arising from in-plane interference, and interband scattering into it affects c-axis and in-plane conductivity differently through v_z² weighting; polycrystals average over anisotropy and add grain-boundary scattering and porosity effects. The authors should measure in-plane ρ(T) of CoSn to test whether the same curvature and Δ/3 onset appear, or explain why c-axis data test the in-plane FB, and comment on how grain boundaries affect the high-T curvature in the polycrystals.

14. **The relation to prior work and the novelty are not stated clearly** (p. 1, "inspired by Mott's early work"; Refs. 29, 33, 43, 45–48). The mechanism, a scattering rate proportional to the DOS of a heavy band producing ρ(T) curvature set by the band-edge position, is Mott's s-d model, and DOS-driven ρ(T) curvature is classic for A15 compounds, Pd and Pt. The two-parabolic-band interband model and energy-dependent-τ approach appear in the authors' Refs. 45–48, and Refs. 33 and 43 analysed Ni3In and Ni3Ge ρ(T) with first-principles electron-phonon (Phoebe) calculations. The manuscript should state precisely what is new (the application to FB compounds, the T ≈ Δ/3 criterion, the inverse fit procedure, the new high-T data) and compare Eq. 1 quantitatively with the ab initio relaxation-time results for the same compounds.
