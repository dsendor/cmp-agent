B. Should address

1. The fingerprint is not shown to be specific to flat bands, and no null control is presented. The abstract and conclusion present sublinear (or superlinear) high-T ρ(T) as "a clear and universal fingerprint of the proximity of a FB to E_F". By the paper's own model (τ ∝ 1/D(E); Methods, Eqs. 5–7; Fig. S1), however, the only requirement is a steep DOS feature near E_F. That covers a d-band edge (Mott's classic explanation of sublinear ρ(T) in Pd and Pt, ref. [33]; also Ni, V, Nb), van Hove singularities, pseudogaps, A15 compounds such as Nb3Sn and V3Si, Heusler compounds and skutterudites (including the authors' own refs. 53–57). Every material in Figs. 4–5 was chosen because it is already known or predicted to host a flat band, and there is no blind case where the diagnosis was made before the band position was known. The authors should apply the same analysis (curvature criterion, Eq. 1 fit, T ≈ Δ/3 onset) to one or two reference metals with a sharp non-flat-band DOS feature near E_F, and to one with a featureless DOS (a simple sp metal, or ideally an isostructural Cu3Au-type or CoSn-type compound of similar resistivity without a FB; Ni3Sn only partly serves), measured in the same setups. They should state what distinguishes a flat band from a generic DOS edge in their criterion, and otherwise reword "flat band" to "steep DOS feature near E_F" wherever the evidence does not go further.

2. Alternative explanations for the high-T curvature are dismissed qualitatively (one paragraph, p. 6) rather than excluded quantitatively. (i) Resistivity saturation: the paper says the compounds are "good metals (well below the MIR limit)" but gives no numbers. The bending in Ni3In, Ca(Rh,Ru)2 and Ni3Ge sets in at 40–100 µΩ cm; Ni3In reaches about 110–120 µΩ cm at 300 K in Fig. 5a, polycrystalline Ni3In keeps rising to about 75–80 µΩ cm above ρ0 at 860 K (Fig. 4a), and CaRh2 reaches about 160 µΩ cm at 860 K. These are close to typical saturation values of 100–200 µΩ cm, where the parallel-resistor (Wiesmann) form 1/ρ = 1/ρ_ideal + 1/ρ_sat produces exactly this sublinearity. (ii) Thermal expansion and anharmonic phonon softening at constant pressure up to 860 K change Θ_D, the bandwidth and the electron-phonon coupling, and give curvature of a few per cent, comparable to the CoSn deviation in Fig. 5e. (iii) Temperature-dependent shifts of the chemical potential with a large DOS feature near E_F, Debye-Waller effects, thermally activated carriers in semimetals, and Kondo coherence (the Kondo argument with T* ≈ 50 K covers only one case) are not treated. The authors should estimate ρ_sat and the mean free path at the highest temperature for each compound from DFT Fermi velocities and plasma frequencies, comment on thermal-expansion corrections to Θ_D, and fit each data set in Fig. 5a with a conventional BG law plus a saturation term having the same number of free parameters as Eq. 1, reporting χ² or residuals side by side. Until these alternatives are shown to fail where Eq. 1 succeeds, the agreement in Fig. 5a does not single out interband scattering.

3. The paper never demonstrates that ρ(T) alone locates the flat band; the fits take the FB position from DFT. The title and abstract say the method "tracks" and "locates" FBs and gives |E_F − E_edge|, and p. 6 calls it "particularly useful when the DFT-calculated DOS is unavailable or unreliable". Yet every fit in Fig. 5a uses Eq. 1 with the DFT DOS fixed (presumably at the DFT E_F) and only the prefactor C free, which tests consistency with DFT rather than extracting the FB position. Eq. 7, the DOS-free form with C, m₂^{3/2}/D₁ and E_F − E_edge free (plus Θ_D and n), is derived in Methods but never applied to any measured data set, and no extracted E_F − E_edge or uncertainty is reported. This matters because GGA-PBE is known to misplace kagome flat bands by tens of meV, the same scale the method is meant to resolve, and Fig. 5b (the DFT FB positions) anchors the interpretation. The authors should fit Eq. 7, or Eq. 1 with a free rigid shift of D_DFT, to Ni3In1−xSnx, CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb; tabulate E_F − E_edge with confidence intervals and parameter correlations (showing whether E_F − E_edge, the mass ratio and C are separately identifiable from a smooth ρ(T)); show the χ² landscape against the shift; and compare blind with independent determinations: ARPES for CoSn (refs. 29, 30, 45) and the composition at which the edge crosses E_F in Ni3In1−xSnx. Failing that, they should state the expected DFT error in the FB position.

4. The fitting procedure in Fig. 5a is underspecified, so "a single free parameter" and "good agreement with all the FB materials" cannot be judged. Eq. (1) contains C, Θ_D, n and the DOS, and the plotted data are total ρ with non-zero intercepts (e.g. ρ0 ≈ 10 µΩ cm for Ni3In), so ρ0 must also have been set or fitted. Not reported per compound: Θ_D and its source (Methods says only "e.g. from specific heat"), the exponent n (3 or 5) and why, ρ0, the fit temperature window, how the DFT DOS was broadened and which DOS was used, the fitted C, and residuals. The text says n = 5 for intraband and 3 for interband scattering, yet CoSn's low-T exponent is 4.99 (Fig. 5d); which n is used for CoSn and does the choice change the high-T curvature? Several curves (CoSn, Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb, Ni3Ge) are nearly linear, so a one-parameter fit will look good with almost any DOS. The authors should provide a table of all inputs and fitted values with uncertainties, residual plots, fits with n and Θ_D varied within their uncertainties, and a comparison with a standard BG fit (one free parameter at fixed Θ_D, or with Θ_D free) showing that the DOS term is actually needed.

5. The experimental reconstruction of σ(E) in Fig. 4c rests on assumptions that are not stated or justified. (i) Taking (ρ − ρ0)⁻¹ as the intrinsic σ assumes Matthiessen's rule in a concentrated, disordered solid solution, where deviations are large exactly when ρ0 and the DOS vary strongly. (ii) Changing x also changes Θ_D, the phonon spectra and the electron-phonon coupling; at 100 K (k_BT ≈ 9 meV, inside the non-linear BG regime) ρ − ρ0 is very sensitive to Θ_D(x). (iii) The experimental points are a thermal average over roughly ±4k_BT ≈ ±35 meV, but are compared with T = 0 σ(E) curves while the step being resolved is about 50 meV wide; disorder broadening in the alloy also smears the edge. (iv) The y-axis is in arbitrary units and the scaling of the experimental points to the three model curves is not stated, so the visual agreement may include a free scale factor. (v) The x → E mapping uses "alloy-averaged DFT" (ref. 39) without saying whether VCA, CPA or supercells, and without uncertainty. (vi) Geometric-factor errors of 10–20 % for polycrystals (porosity, microcracks, contact spacing, grain boundaries, averaging of anisotropic Ni3In) feed directly into (ρ − ρ0)⁻¹. The authors should state the normalisation and the number of samples per composition, add horizontal and vertical error bars, show Θ_D(x) (e.g. from specific heat) or repeat the reconstruction at other temperatures (200 K, 300 K, or in the linear regime), and compare with model σ convolved with −∂f/∂E. The claim that the data follow D⁻¹ rather than the D⁻² of electron-electron scattering (p. 4) needs a quantitative test, such as log σ_exp against log D_DFT across x, or the ratio of high and low plateaus against the DOS ratio from Fig. 5b, with uncertainties.

6. The most direct test of the high-T fingerprint, ρ(T) to 860 K for the intermediate Ni3In1−xSnx compositions, is not shown. Fig. 4b shows the series only from 2 to 100 K, and Fig. 4a shows 2–860 K data only for the end members x = 0 and x = 1. Using the low-T slope at 100 K as a proxy for σ(E) supports the energy dependence of τ but does not test the predicted high-T curvature or its T ≈ Δ/3 onset (Fig. 3e). The authors should show ρ(T) to 860 K for all x (or at least x = 0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6), show that the curvature changes sign where the edge crosses E_F (x ≈ 0.5), and plot the deviation from BG against T/Δ for all x, with Δ from the alloy-DFT mapping, to test the predicted collapse and onset.

7. Correlation and spin-fluctuation scattering are not separated from electron-phonon scattering. Ni3In is a near-ferromagnetic compound with T-linear, non-Fermi-liquid resistivity at low T attributed to flat-band correlations (ref. 23), and Ni3Ge belongs to the nearly-ferromagnetic Ni3X family. Paramagnon scattering gives a ρ(T) that crosses over from T² through linear to saturating, which resembles the "sublinear" signature; the paper does not mention it. Using (ρ − ρ0)⁻¹ at 100 K as the electron-phonon σ(E) in Fig. 4c also assumes correlation-driven scattering is negligible there. The authors should discuss this mechanism with susceptibility data or an argument based on the spin-fluctuation temperature, discuss how a correlation contribution would distort Fig. 4c near x = 0, and check whether the reconstruction changes at a higher reference temperature.

8. The claim that the effect emerges "independently of microscopic details, such as the electron-phonon coupling strength, as confirmed by ab initio calculations" rests on one compound. First-principles calculations are presented only for Ni3In (Figs. 4c, S2), with Ni3Ge taken from ref. 36. In Fig. 4c the BTE-RTA σ(E) oscillates strongly below and around the edge and its step is less sharp than the 2PB curve; the temperature of the calculation, its sensitivity to smearing and the 75³ mesh, and RTA versus full iterative BTE are not discussed. The DFT is nonmagnetic PBE without stated spin-orbit coupling and with no correlation correction for a strongly correlated compound, so the sensitivity of the FB position to these choices should be stated. The authors should show directly from the Phoebe calculation that τ_km⁻¹ tracks D(E) and that v²(E) and |g|² are smooth across the edge (checking the Methods assumption that intra- and interband matrix elements are comparable), give quantitative step heights and widths from each approach, compare absolute ρ(T) at high T and not only normalised shapes, and either narrow the claim or add a calculation for a structurally different case such as CoSn, where the FB lies below E_F and the predicted curvature has the opposite sign.

9. The high-temperature measurements up to 860 K lack controls for sample changes and for joining two setups. Low-T data (helium bath, spot-welded wires, taken while warming spontaneously) and high-T data (ZEM3, He atmosphere) come from different setups, and it is not stated how the segments were matched (same bar, same contacts, any rescaling or offset near 300 K) or whether the overlap is shown. Heating polycrystalline intermetallics and Sn-flux-grown CoSn to 860 K can cause annealing, ordering changes, oxidation, Sn/Pb loss or contact degradation; CoSn crystals may carry Sn inclusions that melt at 505 K, inside the high-T range. Since the observable is the slope and curvature of ρ(T), the authors should show heating and cooling curves, ρ0 or a repeat low-T measurement after the high-T run, the overlap region, and a check for kinks near 505 K in CoSn.

10. Sample characterisation and reproducibility are not documented. No XRD, metallography/EDX, phase-purity, measured-composition or L1₂-ordering data are given for Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb, CoSn or the Ni3In1−xSnx series; the only check mentioned is mass loss below 0.05 %. Antisite disorder in Cu3Au-type compounds strongly affects both ρ0 and the phonon spectrum. Each composition appears to be one sample, with no sample-to-sample spread or geometric-factor uncertainty, although Fig. 4a compares slopes across compounds. The x = 0.4 and 0.6 samples were measured by a different person (author contributions), possibly in a different setup, yet they straddle the claimed E_F = E_edge crossing at x ≈ 0.5. The authors should give characterisation data, actual compositions, the number of samples per compound and the geometric-factor uncertainties.

11. The inference of a FB about 0.1 eV below E_F in CoSn (Fig. 5e) rests on a small deviation from a hand-drawn linear reference and on a model-specific factor. The fit range of the dashed line (apparently about 200–400 K) and the criterion for "deviation" are not given, and the size of the superlinear excess depends on them; thermal expansion or anharmonicity could produce similar curvature. The factor Δ/3 came from one model setting (m_FB = 10 m0, parabolic bands; Fig. 3e) and, as the finite-bandwidth results in Fig. S1 suggest, depends on m_FB, n_CB and W, so it is not universal; how the "≈1/3" positions in Fig. 3e were determined (e.g. a fixed percentage deviation from BG) is not stated. The authors should define the onset criterion, give an uncertainty on the onset temperature and on the Δ/3 rule across m_FB and W, and hence on Δ, give the onset from an Eq. 7 fit, and compare Δ with the ARPES FB energy for CoSn rather than with Δ_DFT only.

12. The physical approximations in the 2PB model are not justified for the parameter range used. (i) Quasi-elastic scattering: τ(E) ∝ 1/D(E) at the carrier energy ignores the phonon energy; with Θ_D = 400 K (k_BΘ_D ≈ 34 meV) and Δ = 20–100 meV, interband transitions sample D(E ± ħω) and smear the edge on a scale comparable to Δ. (ii) Chemical potential: with a large asymmetric DOS at the edge (m_FB = 100 m0), μ may shift by tens of meV at 1000 K at fixed carrier number; the Methods do not say whether μ or N was held fixed, which matters for the Δ/3 onset and for the non-metallic hump in Fig. 3c. (iii) Factorisation of the T dependence into a BG term times an energy integral (Eqs. 5–7) is exact only if the phonon-occupation factor is independent of electron energy across the window. The authors should quantify each, e.g. by evaluating Eq. 3 with the full inelastic kernel and with N conserved for one parameter set from Fig. 3, and explain how the absolute scale of ρ in Fig. 3 was fixed.

13. The specific-heat support in Fig. 5c needs checking and does not discriminate as presented. The quoted γ ≈ 0.03 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² for Pd3Pb (p. 6) is about two orders of magnitude below typical metals, below a free-electron estimate for a Pd compound and even below Cu per atom, corresponding to a DOS of order 0.01 states eV⁻¹ f.u.⁻¹; the Pd3Pb bar in Fig. 5c also appears larger than 0.03 on the plotted scale. If correct, Pd3Pb is effectively a semimetal, which weakens its use as a "good metal" test and brings thermally activated carriers into play. γ values for compounds with 2, 3 and 4 atoms per formula unit (CoSn, CaRh2, Ni3In) are compared without stating per formula unit or per atom, and they come from different sources. Ni3In's γ ≈ 51 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² includes correlation and spin-fluctuation mass enhancement, so it is not a bare DOS measure. The authors should confirm the Pd3Pb value and units (fit range, Schottky or nuclear contributions), show the C/T versus T² data and fit (Fig. S4), state units per atom, and compare each γ with the DFT D(E_F) (bare γ_band) to separate mass enhancement from DOS.

17. Anisotropy is not addressed. Ni3In and CoSn are quasi-2D kagome systems with strongly anisotropic velocities, but the isotropic 2PB model is compared with polycrystal (Ni3In1−xSnx) and c-axis-only (CoSn) data. CoSn should also be measured in the ab plane, where the kagome FB dispersion is relevant; this would test whether the onset and curvature are direction-independent, as a phase-space mechanism predicts. The authors note that in-plane Ni3In data are strange-metal-like (n = 1).
