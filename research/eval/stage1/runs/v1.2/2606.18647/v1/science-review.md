# Concerns

## A. Must address

1. **The Hall signatures shown are not unique to trions; the authors' own model gives them with excitons plus free carriers alone.** In the negative-trion region the Methods give Rxy ≈ −(B/e)[(1+f)/n_e + f²(1−σX0/σe0)/n_T], with f = σT0/(σT0+σX0). Setting f = 0 (σT = 0: excitons and free electrons only, no trions) leaves Rxy ≈ −B/(e n_e), an electron-like Hall response in the hole layer with no trions at all. This is the familiar perfect-drag mechanism (Refs. 30, 31, ED Fig. 8). With the Mo layer open, the exciton and free-electron channels act in series (ρ_h = ρ_X + ρ_e), so any W-layer current carried by excitons must be returned by free electrons in the Mo layer, and their Hall field is transferred to the W layer because excitons pin E_h = E_e. The same reasoning applies to Rxy(drag) (Fig. 3b, Fig. 4c,d): with current driven in the Mo layer and the W layer open, exciton coupling makes the W-layer transverse field follow the Hall field of the free excess carriers, so the drag Hall signal takes the sign of the majority excess carrier, positive for p > n and negative for n > p. That is exactly the sign change across Vg ≈ 0 presented as the trion signature. On the positive-trion side the authors themselves find Rxy ≈ B/(n_h e), carried only by free holes, so the drop of n_H in Fig. 3d (and the left side of Fig. 3a) shows only that holes leave the free-carrier pool; holes bound into excitons or into trions give the same result, as the text concedes ("loss of free holes to the formation of trions and excitons"). The statements in the abstract and on p. 6 that the negative Rxy "unambiguously demonstrates the presence of a trion Hall effect and trion drags" therefore do not follow from the evidence. The optical evidence for trions (lowered injection threshold, Fig. 2c) is stronger than the transport evidence for a trion-specific Hall effect, and the paper should say which result carries which claim. The authors should: (a) derive Rxx, Rxy, Rxx(drag) and Rxy(drag) explicitly from Eqs. (1)–(5) with and without the trion term; (b) fit all four quantities together along the Vb ≈ 0.88 V cut (red line in Fig. 3a) and the data in Fig. 3d and Fig. 4, with n_e, n_h and n_T constrained by the gate electrostatics and the RC-determined valley occupations and as few free scattering times as possible, and show quantitatively that the σT = 0 model fails and the σT ≠ 0 model succeeds; (c) overlay in Fig. 3d the net excess density |p − n| expected for excitons plus free carriers (each positive trion removes two holes, so the two pictures predict different n_H(Vg)), and use the magnitude of |n_H| on the negative side, which ranges from well below to well above the gate-determined electron density, to show whether the negative Rxy or Hall drag exceeds what free electrons plus exciton drag can give; (d) identify an observable unique to charged composites, for example the ratio Rxy(drag)/Rxy, the B dependence of Rxy(drag), or the Hall angle in the drag channel versus trion density at fixed excess density; (e) use a null case where trions are absent, such as the X region inside the EI triangle or the electron-doped side outside the trion triangle at matched exciton density. Without such a discriminating test, the title and central claim should be restated as a Hall response of a mixed exciton/trion/free-carrier fluid that is consistent with, rather than demonstrating, a trion Hall effect.

2. **The Hall signals are not shown to be free of longitudinal admixture and measurement artifacts, in a regime where Rxx exceeds Rxy by one to three orders of magnitude.** In the trion regions Rxx is 10^4–10^7 Ω (Fig. 1d,e, ED Figs. 6, 7) and Rxx(drag) reaches 10^6 Ω (Fig. 2a, ED Fig. 3a), while the reported Rxy and Rxy(drag) are about 2–15 kΩ (Figs. 3, 4). A Hall-probe misalignment of a fraction of a percent, or an inhomogeneous current path, would mix Rxx or Rxx(drag) into the Hall channels with arbitrary sign and could by itself create the sign change across Vg ≈ 0 and the negative values on the n side. The largest Hall signals sit directly beside the grey "completely insulating" masks (Fig. 3a, Fig. 4a, ED Fig. 6b,c); the text concedes that "Rxy cannot be reliably measured near Vg = 0V due to the diverging Rxx" (p. 5) and that Rxx is much higher on the negative-trion side (p. 4). The ~8 K onset in Fig. 4 closely tracks the rise of Rxx in ED Fig. 7b, which an admixture would also produce. In Fig. 4d both Rxy(drag) curves settle near −0.3 kΩ at 10 K regardless of trion sign, which suggests a field-even offset, whereas the text calls Rxy(drag) negligible above 8 K. The circuit in ED Fig. 2a appears to take the Hall voltage between W-3 and W-4 with W-3 also on the current path, in which case contact resistance adds directly to "Rxy". Further, the 100 MΩ preamplifier input impedance against MΩ-scale source impedances at 7.33 Hz, capacitive and out-of-phase pickup, and interlayer AC coupling in the drag configuration (minimized only by a potentiometer) can all generate kΩ-scale transverse voltages. Neither the Methods nor the captions say that Rxy or Rxy(drag) were antisymmetrized, and Fig. 3c covers only B > 0. The authors should: show Rxy and Rxy(drag) at ±B with the antisymmetrized [R(+B) − R(−B)]/2 results for the maps and line cuts in Figs. 3a–c and 4, together with the symmetric parts, demonstrating that the symmetric part does not drive the sign change; show Rxy(B) through B = 0 at fixed (Vg, Vb) in both trion regions; clarify the Hall-probe geometry (including whether W-3 carries current) and estimate the expected admixture fraction; report the out-of-phase lock-in components, the contact resistances in these regions, and the criterion used for the grey masks; explain or quantify the −0.3 kΩ residual Rxy(drag) at 10 K; and check Onsager reciprocity of the Hall drag, Rxy(drag),Mo→W(B) = Rxy(drag),W→Mo(−B).

## B. Should address

3. **The conductivity-tensor model in Methods appears internally inconsistent, is incompletely specified, and is never compared quantitatively with data.** For a negative trion (two electrons in the Mo layer, one hole in the W layer) moving with velocity v ∝ (−2eE_e + eE_h), the contributions are J_e ∝ (4E_e − 2E_h) and J_h ∝ (−2E_e + E_h), a symmetric block as Onsager reciprocity requires at B = 0. Eq. (1) instead has the asymmetric block [[2, −2], [−1, 1]]σT, and Eq. (2) its transpose for positive trions. It is not stated how the 2×2 field-dependent σT of Eq. (3) enters the 4×4 tensor Σ, or what sign ωc,T takes for each trion type, and the closed-form Rxy and the parameter f depend on these choices. σe0 in the negative-trion Rxy is not defined, and the second term f²(1−σX0/σe0)/n_T can take either sign depending on unknown ratios, so the model does not fix the sign of Rxy without further input. The expressions for Rxx, Rxx(drag) and Rxy(drag), the last underlying the headline Hall-drag claim (Fig. 3b, Fig. 4c,d), are not given. The limits assumed (ωcτ ≪ 1 or not, Landau quantization neglected at 4 T) are not stated, and the drag conductivity σD is neglected "at low temperatures" without saying whether that holds at 8–10 K, where the comparison is made. The authors should correct or justify Eqs. (1)–(2), define all quantities, state the limits used, give explicit Rxx, Rxy, Rxx(drag) and Rxy(drag) for both trion types, and compare them quantitatively with Figs. 3d and 4b,d (this also serves concern 1).

4. **A Hall effect, a response linear in B at fixed particle densities, is not demonstrated; the field sweep mixes phase changes with the Lorentz force.** Fig. 3c scans B at fixed Vb = 0.88 V, where the trion region itself appears near 2 T and disappears near 6 T, while the whole phase diagram shifts upward in Vb with B (ED Fig. 6a–d, the EI apex moving from about 0.875 V to above 0.90 V). The appearance and disappearance of the negative Rxy between 2 and 6 T may therefore partly reflect the fixed cut entering and leaving the triangles geometrically rather than trion stabilization and destabilization, and the paper cannot show that the trion Hall resistance scales linearly with B, the most direct sign of a Lorentz-force origin. The authors should show Rxy and Rxy(drag) (antisymmetrized) versus B at fixed (n, p) points that stay inside the trion regions over a finite field range, for example near the triangle apex, tracking the regions in (Vg, Vb) as they move, or show Rxy maps at several fields with the boundaries marked. Measuring the low-field Hall slope (|B| ≤ 1–2 T), including on the narrow B = 0 trion line along p = 2n (Fig. 1d), would establish the response before Landau quantization and Zeeman polarization set in.

5. **The Hall result is shown in one device, and phase boundaries are transferred between devices.** Rxy and Rxy(drag), including the negative Rxy, the B dependence (Fig. 3c) and the T dependence (Fig. 4), come only from device 2 (Figs. 3, 4, ED Figs. 6, 8). The Rxx phase diagrams and T dependence of Rxx (Fig. 1d,e, ED Fig. 7) come from device 1, and the RC and Rxx(drag) maps (Fig. 2, ED Figs. 3–5) from device 3, which is not shown in ED Fig. 1 or described, measured in a different cryostat. The trion regions sit at very different Vb: about 0.74–0.78 V in device 1, 0.86–0.89 V in device 2 and 0.84–0.90 V in device 3. Nevertheless the dashed boundaries "corresponding to those in Fig. 1f" are overlaid on all of them, so the trion assignment of the Hall features rests on boundaries determined optically in another device. The authors should show Rxy and Rxy(drag), including the negative Rxy and the Hall-drag sign change, in at least one more device; state how the kite-shaped boundaries were determined in each device (fitted to that device's data by hand or by an electrostatic model, or copied and rescaled); overlay RC-derived boundaries on transport measured in the same device; add device 3 to ED Fig. 1, with a summary table of devices, their Vb offsets and which figure comes from which; and state whether results survived thermal cycling.

6. **Interlayer leakage and excitation level are not characterized.** Vb ≈ 0.86–0.90 V is applied across a 1.5–2 nm hBN barrier and the Mo layer is tied to a DC source (AC-grounded) through one contact, yet no interlayer tunnelling current is reported. Leakage would provide a parallel path that contaminates both the "open-circuit" Rxy and the drag signals; any tunnelling into the Mo layer would add an electron contribution to the measured hole-layer Hall voltage, an artifactual route to the negative Rxy. The samples are driven with 1 mV RMS AC voltage; in the insulating regime a substantial fraction may drop across the channel, comparable to or larger than the 0.7–1.2 meV trion binding energy and the cyclotron energy, and how much drops across contacts versus channel is not stated. The authors should report the interlayer leakage current versus Vb and Vg over the range of Fig. 3a, and show that Rxy and Rxy(drag) in the trion regions are in linear response, independent of excitation amplitude and free of heating. Combining excitation-amplitude and temperature dependence (does the signal disappear at an excitation corresponding to the binding energy?) would independently confirm the sub-meV scale.

7. **The semiclassical Drude framework and the reading of n_H as a carrier density are applied deep in an insulating regime.** In the trion regions Rxx is far above h/e² (Fig. 1e, ED Fig. 7c) and the behaviour is insulating (dRxx/dT < 0), yet Eqs. (3) use Drude tensors with well-defined ωcτ for trions, excitons and free carriers, and both n_H = B/(eRxy) and the picture of mobile trions under Lorentz deflection rest on that regime. In hopping or strongly localized transport the Hall coefficient is not simply related to carrier density or sign. The authors should give estimated ωcτ and mobilities for each species at 4 T, discuss whether the large Rxx reflects intrinsic insulating behaviour or the open-circuit drag constraint (the Mo layer unable to carry counterflow), and justify why a semiclassical tensor remains appropriate.
Raised by: r3

8. **The ~8 K scale attributed to trion ionization may be the exciton or excitonic-insulator scale, and the conversion to a binding energy is not justified.** The ~8 K onset of Rxy(drag) and Rxy (Fig. 4) is equated with a trion binding of 0.7 meV (p. 6). But ED Fig. 7b shows the insulating behaviour at Vg = 0 (the EI itself) also emerging below ~8 K at 4 T, and drag from excitons alone would switch on at the exciton-formation scale; the Rxy(drag) onset in Fig. 4c is not separated from the onset of exciton drag. The claim that the binding energy "decreases gradually from ≈ 8 K near charge neutrality to zero near |Vg| ≈ 0.06 V" has no supporting figure. In a Saha-type thermal ionization of a dense or degenerate 2D fluid the crossover temperature lies well below E_b/k_B, so equating 8 K with 0.7 meV underestimates E_b. The authors should plot the extracted crossover temperature against Vg alongside the onset temperature of Rxx(drag) or the drag ratio inside the EI region at Vg = 0 under the same conditions, show that the trion-region scale differs from the EI/exciton scale (or discuss why they coincide), and explain how a crossover temperature maps onto a binding energy.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

9. **The gate calibration behind Fig. 3d is not documented.** The dashed electron and hole densities in Fig. 3d come from "calibrated gate capacitances", but Methods give no hBN thicknesses, capacitances, how Vb enters n and p, the charge-neutrality reference, the calibration procedure (Hall density away from the EI, Landau-level fan, quantum oscillations?) or uncertainty. The text also assumes Δ is proportional to the field because the top and bottom hBN are "nearly the same" thickness. The comparison of n_H with these lines is the key quantitative evidence on the positive-trion side and at 10 K, and concerns 1 and 3 need these densities. The 10 K curve peaks near Vg ≈ 0.01 V at about 2.9 × 10^12 cm^-2, roughly twice the expected hole density; calling this a "weak anomaly" from "remnant electron-hole interactions" understates and does not explain it. The authors should give the electrostatic model and parameters, explain how the calibration was checked, add error bands, and explain the 10 K anomaly.

10. **The evidence for negative trions, on which the headline electron-Hall result rests, is thinner and partly inconsistent.** The negative-trion valley analysis is relegated to ED Fig. 3. Its caption says negative trions lie between the red and black dashed lines "when both valleys are occupied in the Mo-layer and only one valley is occupied in the W-layer", but the panels mark the thresholds in the same order as in Fig. 2 (red = both W valleys, at lower Vb), which corresponds to the positive-trion condition. The line cut (blue dashed line in ED Fig. 3a at Vg ≈ 0.11–0.12 V) appears to lie at or beyond the edge of the (X+X−) triangle. The threshold-shift binding-energy analysis is given only for positive trions. The statement that Rxx is much higher in the (X+X−) region "because holes in negative trions are much more tightly bound" (p. 4) is given without evidence or reference. The authors should correct the caption and threshold labels, show a line cut clearly inside the (X+X−) region with the lowered injection threshold for the Mo K′ valley (the analogue of the red solid line in Fig. 2c), extract a negative-trion binding energy from ED Fig. 3c, and use it to justify or drop the "tightly bound" explanation and to address the different temperature evolution of the two signs in Fig. 4b.

11. **Why the trion regions appear and expand with B is unexplained, and Landau quantization may shape the features.** At B = 0 the trion region is a narrow strip along p = 2n (Fig. 1d); at 2–6 T it becomes two broad triangles (Fig. 1e, Fig. 3c, ED Fig. 6), and above 6 T it vanishes. The vanishing is attributed to valley Zeeman energy exceeding the binding energy, but the onset near 2 T and the expansion are not explained. The authors' own estimate puts the cyclotron energy (≈ 0.8 meV at 4 T) at the binding-energy scale, quantum oscillations are seen inside the trion regions (ED Fig. 8), and Fig. 3c shows vertical stripes, so Landau quantization of the free carriers, rather than trion formation alone, may shape the high-Rxx triangles and the Rxy structure. The authors should explain the field-induced stabilization (for example via valley Zeeman splitting relative to the Fermi energy of the minority species), discuss the role of Landau quantization, and if possible show the Hall response at lower field (2–3 T) where quantization is weaker.
Raised by: r1, r3

12. **The optical binding-energy estimate rests on unexamined choices, and the two estimates disagree.** The optical value (1.2 meV from the 25 mV threshold shift at 6 T) and the transport value (0.7 meV at 4 T) differ by almost a factor of two yet are called "in good agreement". The lever arm C_Q^-1/(C_Q^-1 + C_2L^-1) ≈ 0.05 uses a constant zero-field 2D hole density of states with m_h = 0.45 m0 and g_s g_v = 2, but at 6 T the injection is into a single, initially empty spin-valley species (g_s g_v = 1 would roughly halve C_Q) and the hole cyclotron energy (~1.5 meV) exceeds the claimed binding energy, so the density of states is Landau-quantized. The electron-layer (bilayer MoSe2) quantum capacitance, the exciton compressibility and the negative compressibility expected near the EI are neglected. C_2L uses εr ≈ 3.5 and d = 2 nm although Methods give 1.5–2 nm, and ignores that electrons in bilayer MoSe2 may reside partly in the far layer. The "expected injection threshold without interlayer Coulomb interactions" (red solid line in Fig. 2c, ED Figs. 3–5), the baseline for the 25 mV shift, is not described (how computed, with which g-factors and band offsets). And 6 T is where the trion regions are said to vanish in device 2 (Fig. 3c, ED Fig. 6), so the choice of field needs comment. The authors should describe the red line, include both layers' quantum capacitances at finite field, propagate these uncertainties into an error bar on E_T,b, and compare field and temperature estimates at matched conditions, presenting the agreement among 1.2 meV, 0.7 meV and the valley Zeeman scale with uncertainties and caveats.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

13. **The bilayer MoSe2 electron layer is treated as a monolayer.** Figs. 1b and 2d and the valley analysis of the Mo-layer polarons (ED Fig. 3) treat the electron layer as a single spin-valley-locked valley pair. In 2H bilayer MoSe2 the conduction band carries an additional layer degree of freedom, and the spin-valley ordering differs between the two layers. This affects the spin-singlet negative-trion argument, the identification of σ± polaron features with K/K′ electron occupation, and the effective interlayer separation used in C_2L. The authors should state the layer polarization of the electrons under the applied Δ and justify the monolayer-like valley picture, for example with optical signatures from the two MoSe2 layers.
Raised by: r1

14. **Optical thresholds may be affected by illumination.** RC is measured with white-light illumination of a type-II heterobilayer, in a different cryostat (1.6 K base, 9 T). Photodoping, photoinduced interlayer charge transfer or optical heating can shift ~1 meV threshold features, and the stated intensity limit ("50 nW/μm^−2") is given without evidence that thresholds are intensity-independent. The authors should show a power series demonstrating that the injection thresholds in Fig. 2c and ED Figs. 4–5 do not depend on light intensity.
