A. Must address

1. The sign of the hole-layer Hall resistance does not, on the authors' own model, distinguish trions from perfectly dragged excitons plus free carriers. The main text says the negative Rxy in the (X + X-) region "unambiguously demonstrates the presence of Coulomb drag and the trion Hall effect". But equation (7) gives Rxy ≈ -(B/e)[(1+f)/n_e + f^2(1 - σX0/σe0)/n_T]. With no mobile trions (σT0 → 0, f → 0) this still gives Rxy ≈ -B/(n_e e), and Rxy,drag ≈ -B/(n_e e) does not depend on trions at all. Likewise, in equation (8), f = 0 still gives Rxy,drag ≈ B/(n_h e) in the (X + X+) region. Physically, when the Mo layer is open, perfectly dragged neutral excitons carry the hole-layer current and the free electrons must counterflow, so the transverse field from the free electrons' Hall response is passed to the hole layer through the excitons. That produces an electron-like Hall voltage with no bound trions; trions enter only through f and the f^2/n_T term. The evidence for bound trions currently comes from the insulating Rxx, the p = 2n and n = 2p boundary geometry and the RC valley argument, not from the Hall data, so the title claim is at present a Hall response of a drag-coupled Bose-Fermi mixture that coincides with the trion regions. Equations (7) and (8) contain n_e, n_h, n_T, f and conductivity ratios, none of which is extracted from or fitted to the data, and Fig. 4f shows the Hall density next to the calibrated densities with no model curve. The authors should identify and test an observable that separates the two scenarios, for example: (a) fit Rxy(Vg) and Rxy,drag(Vg) at 1.5 K along the Vb = 0.88 V cut in both regions with equations (7) and (8), using capacitance-derived n and p and a constraint such as n_total = n_free + 2n_T + n_X, with a minimal stated set of free parameters, and report the extracted f and n_T, showing where the f = 0 limit fails; (b) in the (X + X+) region, overlay on Fig. 4f the reference curves p - n (holes bound only in excitons) and p - 2n (maximal trion formation) next to n_H(1.5 K), since equation (8) makes n_H the free-hole density and the deficit below p - n counts holes bound in trions (as read from the figure, n_H falls roughly linearly to zero at Vg = 0, which must be shown inconsistent with p - n); (c) check that Rxy,drag ≈ -B/(n_e e) in the (X + X-) region matches the expected free-electron density, and examine the behaviour as n = 2p is approached (n_e → 0), the size of n_H relative to n - 2p, and the relation between Rxy and Rxy,drag. A reference measurement at matched densities where excitons form but trions do not (for example a device with a thicker barrier) would be a useful null control. Unless such analysis excludes the alternatives, "unambiguously demonstrates" should be replaced by a statement that the Hall response is consistent with mobile trions plus Coulomb drag.

2. The paper does not show that Rxy and Rxy,drag are antisymmetric in field, free of Rxx admixture, or in phase, although the headline result is a sign change measured on a strongly insulating layer. In the trion regions Rxx and Rxx,drag are 10^4-10^7 Ω (Figs. 2a, 3a; Extended Data Fig. 4), while the Hall signals are a few to about 10 kΩ (Fig. 4a-e). A small Hall-probe misalignment, density inhomogeneity between the probes, or an inhomogeneous current path would mix in a longitudinal component of the same size as the signal, of either sign, and strongly dependent on temperature and gate voltage because Rxx is insulating-like there (Fig. 2b,c). Such an admixture changes sign with the gradient of Rxx and could mimic the sign reversal of Rxy below about 4 K (Fig. 4e) and the sign changes near the grey regions and along the lower boundary of the trion regions (blue fringes in Fig. 4a,b and Extended Data Fig. 4). The Methods and captions do not say whether the data are antisymmetrized, and Fig. 4 and Extended Data Fig. 4 (including the 0-12 T sweep in Extended Data Fig. 4e) show positive B only. The authors should show raw Rxy and Rxy,drag at +B and -B, the antisymmetrized results and the symmetric parts at representative points in both trion regions at 1.5 K and 10 K (in particular for the Fig. 4c-e data); state the antisymmetrization procedure and whether the maps are antisymmetrized; report the out-of-phase lock-in component in the high-impedance regions, where the 100 MΩ preamplifier input and line capacitance at 7.33 Hz may matter; and, ideally, check Onsager reciprocity of the drag Hall signal by driving the W layer and measuring the Mo-layer Hall voltage at reversed field.

B. Should address

3. The conductivity tensors in equations (2) and (3) need a derivation, because as written they appear inconsistent with the trion's net charge and with Onsager reciprocity. In Σ^- the trion enters as J_e = 2σT(E_e - E_h) and J_h = -σT(E_e - E_h), so its contribution depends only on E_e - E_h and vanishes for E_e = E_h, which is the response of a neutral exciton. A negative trion (two electrons, one hole) in equal uniform fields feels a net force -eE and should move. Treating the trion as a rigid particle of mobility μ gives contributions proportional to [[4, -2], [-2, 1]], symmetric as reciprocity requires at B = 0, whereas the written [[2, -2], [-1, 1]] is not symmetric; the same applies to Σ^+. These matrices produce equations (7) and (8), the n_T terms in Rxy and Rxy,drag, and the claim that excitons only add "corrections". The authors should derive the tensor from a two-layer force-balance picture, or explain the convention for σT that makes the stated form correct, then recheck the sign and magnitude predictions in both trion regions and whether trions and excitons can be distinguished (concern 1).

4. The assumption of perfect Coulomb drag for excitons and trions, and of no frictional drag between free carriers, is not supported and appears to conflict with the data. Methods (after equation 2) assumes perfect drag for bound states, negligible frictional drag of free carriers, and neglects free electrons in the (X + X+) region and free holes in the (X + X-) region. Extended Data Fig. 3b shows a drag ratio near 1 only in the X region and about 0.2-0.4 inside the trion regions. Part of the deficit may come from free carriers carrying current, but the paper does not show that the measured drag ratio matches the conductivity fractions its model implies. Frictional (unbound) Coulomb drag between free electrons and the hole layer can be strong at d ≈ 2 nm and low density, is known to produce Hall drag with its own temperature dependence, and is set to zero by assumption; it is a second alternative to trions for the negative Rxy. The authors should compare the measured drag ratio in both trion regions with the model prediction for reasonable σ values, and show how relaxing the perfect-drag and no-friction assumptions changes the sign and magnitude of Rxy and Rxy,drag, in particular near the trion dissociation boundary.

6. Interlayer leakage and a.c. coupling in the drag and "open-circuit" configurations are not quantified. The layers are biased at Vb ≈ 0.76-0.9 V across a 1.5-2 nm hBN barrier, near the gap-closing bias, where tunnelling can be significant (the paper cites enhanced tunnelling, ref. 28). Tunnelling current would appear directly as a spurious drag signal and could alter the Hall voltage on the open-circuited layer. In the standard Hall configuration (Extended Data Fig. 2a) the Mo layer is tied to the Vb source at Mo-10, so any a.c. leakage from the W layer returns through that source and the Mo layer is not strictly open-circuit, as equations (4) and (7) assume. The potentiometer and transformer scheme (Extended Data Fig. 2b) reduces a.c. coupling, but the residual is not bounded. The authors should report the d.c. interlayer leakage current versus Vb and Vg across the trion regions, the a.c. leakage and the out-of-phase drag components, the residual drag signal where the layers should be decoupled (high T or outside the bound-state regions), and the dependence of Rxy,drag on excitation amplitude and frequency, which also bears on heating of the highly resistive trion regions.

7. The small-field, semiclassical model is applied at 4 T, where Landau quantization matters. Equation (6) linearizes in ω_c τ ("under a small perpendicular magnetic field B") and the text uses Rxy ≈ B/(n_h e) "for small B fields", yet all Hall data and the n_H of Fig. 4f are at 4 T. There the paper itself says the free-hole cyclotron energy is comparable to the trion binding energy, it exceeds k_B T at 1.5 K, and quantum oscillations are strong in the same regions (Fig. 2a; Extended Data Figs. 3b, 4, 7). Data at 2-3 T, where the trion regions already exist (Extended Data Fig. 4a,b), would strengthen the analysis.

8. The trion binding-energy estimates and the temperature scales are not consistent, and no uncertainties are given. The main text equates the about 8 K crossover (Fig. 4c) with the binding energy ("about 8 K (or 0.7 meV)"), while the Methods estimate about 1.2 meV (about 14 K) from the RC injection threshold in device 2 at 6 T; the "good agreement" is not like-for-like, since the paper says the binding energy depends on B and density. In a Saha-type dissociation equilibrium, half the bound states dissociate at k_B T well below E_b because of the dissociation entropy, so equating the crossover with E_b underestimates it. In Fig. 4e, Rxy in the (X + X-) region turns negative only below about 4 K, while Rxy,drag departs from zero below about 6-8 K, which the paper notes but does not explain; Rxy is positive at high temperature, which the model, omitting free holes in this region, cannot produce. The optical estimate depends on d (quoted as "d ≈ 2 nm" in Methods and 1.5-2 nm in the main text), which changes C_2L and E_T,b by about 30%; on the 25 mV threshold reading; on m_h = 0.45 m0 and g_s g_v = 2, whereas near the injection threshold into the minority valley at 6 T a single-valley compressibility may apply; and on comparing 1.5 K with 10 K data. The authors should model thermal dissociation, for example n_T(T) from a mass-action law together with thermally activated free carriers fed into equations (7) and (8), to reconcile the two transport crossovers and the optical estimate; give error bars on both estimates; compare at matched B in the same device where possible; and soften the agreement claim.

12. The analysis of the resistance oscillations in the (X + X+) region, and the conclusion that they are not trion Landau levels, rest on a fragile fit. The 0.65 m_e mass comes from a Lifshitz-Kosevich fit over only about 1.5-3 K (Extended Data Fig. 7d). The amplitude is normalized to Rxx at 3.3 K, which forces it to zero at the top of the fit range and steepens the apparent decay, and the background is strongly temperature dependent through trion dissociation (Fig. 2c), in an insulating region where Lifshitz-Kosevich theory need not apply. The "expected trion mass (≈1.4-1.6 m_e)" has no stated source. The alternative explanation, recurring transitions between hole quantum Hall states and the trion phase, is plausible but untested. The authors should extract the amplitude after subtracting a smooth background at each temperature, fit over a range not pinned to the reference temperature, give an error bar on the mass and the source of the expected trion mass, and test the proposed scenario, for example by comparing the density period of the stripes and the field dependence of their positions (their slope in the Vb-Vg plane) with the Landau-level filling of free holes; otherwise the interpretation should be stated more tentatively.
