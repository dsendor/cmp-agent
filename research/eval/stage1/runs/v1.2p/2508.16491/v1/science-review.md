A. Must address

1. The curvature signature is not shown to be specific to flat bands, and competing explanations for sub- and superlinear ρ(T) are not excluded (abstract; Figs. 3, 4(a), 4(e); conclusion). The model's only input is a steep energy dependence of D(E) within a few k_BT of E_F (Figs. 1(c), 2, Eq. 1). Any transition-metal compound with E_F near a d-band edge has this feature: Pd, Pt, A15 compounds and Heusler alloys show sublinear or superlinear ρ(T) for exactly this reason, which is Mott's s-d scattering (Ref. 29, and Refs. 45–47 cited by the authors). Several other well-known mechanisms also curve ρ(T) at high temperature and are not discussed: resistivity saturation near the Mott–Ioffe–Regel limit (parallel-resistor behaviour of Fisk–Webb and Wiesmann et al.); thermal expansion, phonon softening, anharmonic or multiphonon scattering, and the constant-pressure versus constant-volume difference up to 862 K; spin-fluctuation (paramagnon) scattering, relevant for Ni3In (a strange metal near magnetic order), CaRh2 and Ni-, Co-, Pd- and Pt-based compounds; a temperature-dependent chemical potential; and electron-electron scattering, which the model explicitly omits (p. 2). The two compounds that show clear sublinearity, Ni3In and CaRh2, are also the ones with the largest resistivity (about 100–160 μΩ cm in Fig. 4(a)), exactly where saturation is generic, so the correlation in Fig. 4(a) cannot tell the FB mechanism apart from saturation; as it stands the agreement shows consistency, not inference. Since the title and abstract claim a method for detecting and tracking flat bands, the authors should: (i) fit the same data with a parallel-resistor (ρ_sat as a parameter) model, with a plain BG-plus-thermal-expansion model, with BG plus a T² electron-electron term, and with Eq. 1 plus a saturation term, using the same number of free parameters, and compare goodness of fit, residuals and the physical plausibility of the parameters; (ii) estimate k_F·l at the highest temperatures for each compound; (iii) include a reference metal with a smooth D(E) near E_F, ideally isostructural to a FB compound (a Cu3Au-type or CoSn-type analogue without a FB near E_F), measured with the same setup over the same range, where Eq. 1 should reduce to BG and give linear ρ(T) above Θ_D; (iv) include a metal with a steep but ordinary (non-flat) d-band edge, or a sublinear compound with no FB near E_F, or a low-resistivity compound whose FB edge lies above E_F, to show that sublinearity tracks the FB rather than the magnitude of ρ, and discuss explicitly whether Pd/Pt-type d-band metals count as "flat-band" positives of this diagnostic; and (v) if a flat band cannot be distinguished from an ordinary steep DOS feature, narrow the claim to "steep DOS features near E_F".

The claim that the method can locate the FB is not validated independently (abstract, "one can infer the position of FBs"; p. 3, "derived a fit formula (see Eq. 6 in the SM) to pinpoint the position of the FB"; conclusion). In Fig. 4(a) the FB position is an input through D_DFT(E) and only the prefactor C is fitted, so the fits test whether the DFT DOS is compatible with the data, not whether ρ(T) determines E_F − E_edge; the only other support is qualitative agreement in sign with γ (Fig. 4(c)). The only position extraction is the CoSn argument in Fig. 4(e), where a visually chosen departure from linearity near 400 K is equated with Δ_DFT/3, and Fig. 3(e) establishes that factor 1/3 for a single parameter set (m_FB = 10 m0, Δ = 100 meV). The authors should: apply SM Eq. 6 (or treat a rigid shift of E_F relative to the DFT DOS as a fit parameter) for every compound and report E_F − E_edge and m_FB with confidence intervals; show how χ² of the Eq. 1 fit varies with rigid shifts of D_DFT(E) by, say, ±20, ±50 and ±100 meV (if shifts of tens of meV do not appreciably change χ², the method cannot pinpoint the FB); compare the extracted values, ideally obtained blind, with independent ARPES (Refs. 23, 32, 38, 39 for Ni3In, CaRh2 and CoSn), DFT and specific-heat determinations, collected in a benchmark table per compound (DFT E_F − E_edge, extracted value, ARPES value, measured versus DFT γ, observed curvature), including at least one non-FB control; show how the "Δ/3" onset criterion depends on m_FB, the FB bandwidth and DOS shape, and n, for example by mapping the onset T/Δ over a grid of m_FB and Δ; and ideally test the method on a series in which E_F is tuned relative to the FB by doping, substitution or pressure (for example electron- or hole-doped CoSn such as Co1−xFexSn, In-site substitution in Ni3In or a Ni3In/Ni3Sn alloy, or the Ca(Rh,Ru)2 series), showing the extracted Δ move and the curvature change sign as E_F crosses the edge, together with the change in γ and in the sign of S. Without this, "one can infer the position of FBs" is an assertion.

B. Should address

3. The model assumptions behind Eq. 1 are not stated or justified, although they control the sign of the effect (Eq. 1; Figs. 2, 3). Eq. 1 is not derived in the main text. It factors the thermal average of D_DFT⁻¹(E) out of the phonon integral as a multiplicative prefactor to the BG integral, an ad hoc separation: in a proper Boltzmann treatment the energy-dependent rate enters inside the transport integral, weighted by D_c(E)v_c²(E) of the conducting band, which enter σ(E) = e²D(E)v²(E)τ(E). Eq. 1 implicitly assumes the carrier velocity and conducting DOS are energy-independent, which holds only if carriers live entirely in the dispersive band (the s-d assumption), whereas the FB states also carry current with small v, and v²(E) varies strongly through the same energy window. It uses the total DFT DOS, which includes the FB states themselves, both as the scattering phase space and implicitly not as a conducting channel, rather than for example the DOS of the flat orbital character. It assumes an energy- and momentum-independent electron-phonon matrix element for intraband and interband channels alike. It evaluates the phase space quasi-elastically at D(E), although for Δ comparable to phonon energies (k_BΘ_D ≈ 20–40 meV against Δ of 20–100 meV) the relevant quantity is D(E ± ħω). Finally, it is not stated whether the chemical potential is fixed or computed self-consistently; with a narrow, high-DOS band within 20–100 meV of E_F and k_BT up to about 75 meV, μ(T) shifts by roughly (π²/6)(k_BT)²D′/D, which can be tens of meV at 800 K for the asymmetric DOS of Figs. 2(b) and 4(b), and that shift alone can produce strong sub- or superlinear ρ(T). The authors should derive Eq. 1 from the two-band Boltzmann model in the main text or SM, state each assumption, and show that the conclusions survive a calculation with the energy-dependent rate inside the transport integral, v(E) included, inelastic D(E ± ħω) phase space, and μ(T) determined self-consistently from the DOS, recomputing Fig. 3 and the Fig. 4(a) fits accordingly.

5. Electron correlations are neglected in exactly the materials where they matter, particularly Ni3In (p. 2, "we do not included electron-electron scattering"; p. 4, Ni3In strange-metal behaviour). The authors note that Ni3In shows T-linear ρ_ab (n = 1) down to the lowest temperatures (Ref. 23), which they attribute to non-Fermi-liquid physics and which BG with n = 3 or 5 cannot reproduce at low T, yet Ni3In serves as the main sublinear example and is fitted with an electron-phonon-only formula across 1.5–300 K. Fig. 4(b) gives a DFT DOS at E_F of about 2.8 states/eV for Ni3In; via γ = (π²/3)k_B²D(E_F) this corresponds to about 6.6 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻², whereas Fig. 4(c) shows γ ≈ 50 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻², a mass enhancement of about 7, far beyond typical electron-phonon values (about 3 for CoSn). Such renormalization narrows the FB and may shift it relative to E_F (ARPES and DMFT place the Ni3In FB differently from GGA), so using the bare GGA-PBE DOS as the scattering phase space is questionable, and electron-electron scattering, which can also give superlinear ρ(T), contributes at all temperatures. The authors should quantify the enhancement for each compound with the per-formula-unit normalization stated, discuss how a correlation-renormalized FB (for example from DFT+DMFT or the ARPES-measured position) would change Eq. 1 and the fits, explain why electron-electron scattering is negligible at 300–860 K, and show the Ni3In fit range and low-temperature mismatch explicitly, explaining why an e-ph-only fit across 1.5–300 K is meaningful.

6. The DFT DOS input is taken as fixed and reliable, and its effect on the fits is not quantified (p. 3; Fig. 4(b)). For CaRh2 the DOS comes from the Materials Project with "very coarse resolution", and this is the worst fit; the authors speculate that a finer grid "would most likely" improve it rather than testing it, even though they performed DFT for other compounds. For CoSn and the other compounds the DFT details (functional, spin-orbit setting, important for Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb, k-mesh, smearing or tetrahedron method) are not given, and smearing directly controls the FB-edge sharpness that drives the effect. The DOS is also used unchanged up to 862 K, ignoring thermal expansion and band broadening. The authors should recompute the CaRh2 DOS on a properly converged fine energy grid, list the DFT settings for all compounds, and show how sensitive the fits are to DOS broadening and small rigid shifts.

7. The semiclassical treatment is used outside its range of validity (Fig. 3(c); Fig. 4(a)). For m_FB = 100 m0, Fig. 3(c) gives a resistivity maximum near 500 μΩ cm, well beyond the Ioffe-Regel limit, and Ni3In and Ca(Rh,Ru)2 lie at 100–160 μΩ cm, where the mean free path approaches the lattice spacing and Boltzmann transport with a well-defined τ(E) is doubtful. 8. The integrity of the high-temperature measurements is not documented (p. 2; Fig. 4(a), 4(e)). 9. Anisotropy and sample form are not accounted for (Fig. 4(a), (d), (e)). CoSn is measured along c only, Ni3In data are in the ab plane, and Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb are polycrystals that average over anisotropy and add grain-boundary scattering. In kagome metals the flat band is quasi-two-dimensional and arises from in-plane interference, so interband scattering into it affects c-axis and in-plane conductivity differently (through v_z² weighting), which a scalar, isotropic DOS model cannot capture. The authors should show CoSn ρ(T) in-plane as well as along c, testing whether the same Δ/3 onset appears, or explain why c-axis curvature tests the in-plane FB, and comment on how grain boundaries and porosity affect the high-T curvature in the polycrystals.

14. The model band structure does not match the FBs in the real materials (Fig. 1(c) vs. Fig. 2 insets vs. Fig. 4(b)). The model uses a heavy parabolic band with a single edge, giving a monotonic, step-like σ(E). The schematic in Fig. 1(c) and the DFT DOS in Fig. 4(b) instead show narrow peaks with two sides; with E_F near a peak both sides are thermally sampled, and the clean sub/superlinear dichotomy tied to "E_F above/below the edge" is not obvious. Kagome FBs also touch a dispersive band at a quadratic band-touching point rather than being detached heavy parabolas. The authors should show model results for a peak-shaped DOS (for example a Lorentzian or a kagome tight-binding DOS) with E_F on either side of the peak, and clarify what "FB edge" means operationally for the DFT DOS in Fig. 4(b).

15. The relation to prior work and the novelty need to be stated clearly (p. 1, "inspired by Mott's early work"; Refs. 33, 43, 45–48). The mechanism, a scattering rate proportional to the DOS of a heavy band producing ρ(T) curvature set by the band-edge position, is Mott's s-d model, and DOS-driven ρ(T) curvature is classic in A15 compounds and Pd and Pt. Energy-dependent scattering phase space producing anomalous ρ(T), including a two-parabolic-band interband framework, appears in the authors' own Refs. 45–48, and the Ni3In and Ni3Ge ρ(T) analyses in Refs. 33 and 43 used first-principles electron-phonon (Phoebe) RTA calculations. The manuscript should put the work in context with this literature and state precisely what is new (the application to FB compounds, the T ≈ Δ/3 criterion, the inverse fit procedure, the new CoSn, Pt3Sn and Pd3Pb data), and explain how Eq. 1 compares quantitatively with the ab initio RTA results for the same compounds.
