# Merged review

## A. Must address

## B. Should address

1. **The signature is not shown to be specific to flat bands, and no null control is presented.** The abstract and conclusion present sublinear (superlinear) high-temperature ρ(T) as "a clear and universal fingerprint of the proximity of a FB to E_F". By the paper's own model (τ⁻¹(E) ∝ D(E); Methods, Eqs. 5–7; Fig. S1), the only requirement is a steep DOS feature near E_F: a d-band edge (Pd, Pt, Ni, V, Nb, the classic Mott/Wilson s-d cases, ref. [33]), a van Hove singularity, or a pseudogap, as in A15 compounds such as Nb3Sn and V3Si, Heusler compounds and skutterudites (refs. 53–57, by the same group). Every material in Figs. 4–5 was chosen because it is already known or predicted to host a flat band, and there is no "blind" case where the diagnosis was made before the band position was known. The authors should apply the same analysis (curvature criterion, Eq. 1 fit, T ≈ Δ/3 onset) to at least one reference metal with a sharp non-flat-band DOS feature near E_F and one with a featureless DOS (e.g. a simple sp metal, or an isostructural Cu3Au-type or CoSn-type compound of similar resistivity without a FB; Ni3Sn only partly serves), measured in the same setups, and state what distinguishes a "flat band" from a generic DOS edge in their criterion. Where the evidence does not go further, the wording should be narrowed to "a steep DOS feature near E_F".
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

2. **The method's stated purpose, locating the FB from ρ(T), is never tested quantitatively against an independent determination.** The title and abstract say the method "tracks" and "locates" FBs and gives |E_F − E_edge|; p. 6 and Methods (Eq. 7) say a ρ(T) fit gives E_F − E_edge and m₂^{3/2}/D₁ without DFT input, which is "particularly useful when the DFT-calculated DOS is unavailable or unreliable". Yet every fit in Fig. 5a uses Eq. (1) with the DFT DOS fixed (presumably at the DFT E_F) and only C free, so the FB position is an input, not an output; these fits test consistency with DFT. Eq. 7 is never applied to any measured dataset, and no extracted E_F − E_edge with uncertainties is reported. This matters because GGA-PBE is known to misplace kagome flat bands by tens of meV, the same scale the method is meant to resolve, and Fig. 5b (DFT DOS) anchors the interpretation. The authors should (i) fit each compound (Ni3In1−xSnx, CoSn, Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb) with Eq. 7 or with Eq. 1 allowing a rigid shift of E_F relative to D_DFT, and tabulate E_F − E_edge with confidence intervals and parameter correlations, showing whether E_F − E_edge, the mass ratio and C are separately identifiable from a smooth ρ(T) curve; (ii) show χ² against the rigid shift to demonstrate how tightly ρ(T) constrains the FB position; (iii) compare with independent determinations, namely ARPES for CoSn (refs. 29, 30, 45) and the crossing composition in the Ni3In1−xSnx series; and (iv) compare the DFT FB positions in Fig. 5b with ARPES or state the expected DFT error.

3. **Alternative explanations for high-temperature curvature are dismissed qualitatively, not excluded quantitatively.** The Discussion (p. 6) treats these in one paragraph. (i) Resistivity saturation: the paper states the compounds are "good metals (well below the MIR limit)" without numbers, but Ni3In reaches about 110–120 µΩ cm at 300 K (Fig. 5a), polycrystalline Ni3In keeps rising to about 75–80 µΩ cm above ρ₀ at 860 K (Fig. 4a), CaRh2 reaches about 160 µΩ cm at 860 K, and the bending in Ni3In, Ca(Rh,Ru)2 and Ni3Ge sets in at 40–100 µΩ cm, all comparable to typical ρ_sat of 100–200 µΩ cm, where the parallel-resistor form 1/ρ = 1/ρ_ideal + 1/ρ_sat produces exactly this sublinearity. (ii) Spin-fluctuation scattering: Ni3In is a near-ferromagnetic strange metal and Ni3Ge belongs to the nearly ferromagnetic Ni3X family; paramagnon scattering gives T² → linear → saturating ρ(T), resembling the sublinear signature, and is not mentioned. (iii) Thermal expansion, anharmonic phonon softening and Debye-Waller effects up to 860 K change Θ_D, bandwidth and coupling and can give curvature of a few per cent, comparable to the CoSn deviation in Fig. 5e. (iv) Temperature-dependent chemical-potential shifts near a large DOS feature, thermally activated carriers in semimetals, and Kondo coherence. The authors should estimate ρ_sat and the mean free path at the highest temperature for each compound (from DFT plasma frequencies/Fermi velocities and interatomic spacing), fit each dataset with a standard BG law with free Θ_D plus a parallel-resistor (Wiesmann) term having the same number of free parameters as Eq. 1 and report χ² or residuals side by side, give susceptibility data or a spin-fluctuation-temperature argument for the Ni3X compounds, and comment on thermal-expansion corrections to Θ_D. Until these alternatives are shown to fail where Eq. 1 succeeds, the agreement in Fig. 5a does not single out interband scattering.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

4. **The Eq. (1) fits in Fig. 5a are underspecified, so the "single free parameter" description and the claimed "good agreement with all the FB materials" cannot be judged.** Eq. (1) contains C, Θ_D, n and the DOS, and the plotted data are total ρ with non-zero intercepts (e.g. ρ₀ ≈ 10 µΩ cm for Ni3In), so ρ₀ must also have been set or fitted. Not reported per compound: Θ_D and its source (Methods says "e.g. from specific heat"), the exponent n (3 or 5) and on what basis, ρ₀, the fitted C, the temperature window, the DFT DOS reference and its broadening, and residuals. The text says n = 5 for intraband and 3 for interband scattering, yet CoSn's low-T exponent is 4.99 (Fig. 5d); which n is used for CoSn, and does the choice change the high-T curvature? Several curves (Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb, Ni3Ge, CoSn) are nearly linear, so a one-parameter fit will look good with almost any DOS. The authors should give a table of all inputs and fitted values with uncertainties, residual plots, fits with n and Θ_D varied within their uncertainties, and a standard BG fit (also one free parameter at fixed Θ_D, or with Θ_D free) showing that the DOS term is actually needed.

5. **The experimental reconstruction of σ(E) in Fig. 4c rests on unstated or unjustified assumptions.** (i) Taking (ρ − ρ₀)⁻¹ as intrinsic σ assumes Matthiessen's rule in a concentrated, disordered solid solution, where deviations are large exactly when ρ₀ and the DOS vary strongly. (ii) Changing x changes Θ_D, phonon spectra and electron-phonon coupling, not only E_F; at 100 K (k_BT ≈ 9 meV, well inside the non-linear BG regime) (ρ − ρ₀) is very sensitive to Θ_D(x). (iii) The data are a thermal average over roughly ±4k_BT ≈ ±35 meV at 100 K but are compared with T = 0 σ(E) curves, while the step resolved is about 50 meV wide; disorder broadening in the alloy also smears the edge. (iv) The y-axis is in arbitrary units and the scaling of the experimental points relative to the three model curves is not stated, so the visual agreement may include a free scale factor. (v) The x → E mapping uses "alloy-averaged DFT" (ref. 39) whose method (VCA, CPA, supercells) and uncertainty are not given. (vi) Geometric factors (porosity, microcracks, contact spacing, 10–20 %), grain-boundary contributions and polycrystalline averaging of anisotropic Ni3In are assumed identical for every x. The authors should state the normalisation, the number of samples per composition and the geometric-factor uncertainty; add horizontal and vertical error bars; show Θ_D(x) (e.g. from specific heat) or repeat the reconstruction at other reference temperatures (200 K, 300 K, or in the linear regime); compare with model σ convolved with −∂f/∂E at 100 K; and test D⁻¹ versus D⁻² (p. 4) quantitatively, e.g. log σ_exp versus log D_DFT across x, or the ratio of high and low σ plateaus against the DOS ratio from Fig. 5b, with uncertainties.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

6. **The high-temperature ρ(T) of the intermediate Ni3In1−xSnx compositions, the most direct test of the proposed fingerprint, is not shown.** The model's main predictions concern the high-T curvature, its onset at T ≈ Δ/3 (Fig. 3e), and how both evolve with |E_F − E_edge|. Fig. 4b shows the series only from 2 to 100 K, and Fig. 4a shows 2–860 K data only for x = 0 and x = 1. The low-T slope at 100 K tests the energy dependence of τ but not the high-T curvature claim. The authors should show ρ(T) to 860 K for all x (or at least x = 0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6), show that the curvature changes sign where the edge crosses E_F (x ≈ 0.5), and plot the deviation from BG against T/Δ for all x to test the predicted collapse and the Δ/3 onset, with Δ from the alloy-DFT mapping. Relatedly, the "rather abrupt change around x = 0.5" in Fig. 4b should be quantified, e.g. as the slope at fixed T against x with uncertainties.
Raised by: r2, r3

7. **The high-temperature measurements to 860 K need controls for sample changes and for joining the two setups.** Low-T data (helium bath, spot-welded wires) and high-T data (ZEM3, He atmosphere) come from different setups, and the key observable is the slope and curvature of ρ(T), compared between compounds in Fig. 4a. It is not stated how the segments were matched (same bar and contact geometry, any rescaling or offset at the overlap near 300 K) or whether the overlap region is shown. Heating polycrystalline intermetallics and Sn-flux-grown CoSn to 860 K can cause annealing, ordering changes, Sn/Pb loss, oxidation or contact degradation; CoSn from Sn flux may carry Sn inclusions that melt at 505 K, inside the high-T range. The authors should show heating and cooling curves, a repeat low-T measurement (ρ₀ before and after) the high-T run, a check for kinks near 505 K in CoSn, and the geometric-factor uncertainties.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. **Sample characterisation and reproducibility are not documented.** No XRD, metallography/EDX, phase-purity, measured-composition or L1₂-ordering data are given for Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb, CoSn or the Ni3In1−xSnx series; the only check mentioned is mass loss below 0.05 %. Antisite disorder in Cu3Au-type compounds strongly affects both ρ₀ and the phonon spectrum. Each composition appears to be a single sample with no sample-to-sample spread. The x = 0.4 and 0.6 samples were measured by a different person (author contributions), possibly in a different setup, yet they straddle the claimed E_F = E_edge crossing at x ≈ 0.5. The authors should provide characterisation, actual compositions for the alloy series, and the number of samples per compound with their spread.

9. **Independence from microscopic details is argued from a single ab initio calculation.** The abstract says the characteristic emerges "independently of microscopic details, such as the electron-phonon coupling strength, as confirmed by comparison with ab initio electron-phonon transport calculations", and the Fig. 4 caption says microscopic details "do not vary significantly". The first-principles calculation is shown only for Ni3In (Fig. 4c, Fig. S2), with Ni3Ge taken from ref. 36. In Fig. 4c the BTE-RTA σ(E) oscillates strongly below the edge and its step is less sharp than the 2PB curve; the temperature of the calculation, its sensitivity to smearing and the 75³ mesh, and RTA versus full iterative BTE are not discussed. The DFT is nonmagnetic PBE without stated spin-orbit coupling or correlation correction for a compound with documented strong correlations. The authors should give a quantitative comparison (step height and width from each approach), show directly from the Phoebe calculation that τ_k⁻¹ tracks D(E) and that v²(E) and |g|² are smooth across the edge (checking the Methods assumption that intra- and interband matrix elements are comparable), compare absolute high-T ρ(T), state the sensitivity of the FB position to the DFT choices, and either narrow the claim or add a BTE-RTA calculation for a structurally different case such as CoSn, where the FB lies below E_F and the predicted curvature is opposite.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

10. **The T ≈ Δ/3 criterion and the CoSn inference of a FB about 0.1 eV below E_F (Fig. 5e) are not quantified.** The onset of deviation from linearity is read against a hand-drawn dashed line whose fit range (apparently about 200–400 K) and deviation criterion (e.g. a residual threshold) are not given; the size of the superlinear excess depends on that range, and thermal expansion or anharmonicity could give similar curvature (concern 3). The factor 1/3 itself (Fig. 3e) comes from one model setting (m_FB = 10 m₀, parabolic bands), it is not defined how the "≈1/3" positions were determined (e.g. a fixed percentage deviation from BG), and Fig. S1 suggests it depends on m_FB, n_CB and W, so it is not universal. The authors should define the onset criterion, give an uncertainty on the onset temperature and hence on Δ, give the uncertainty of the Δ/3 rule across m_FB and W, ideally extract the onset from an Eq. 7 fit, and compare Δ with the ARPES FB energy for CoSn rather than only Δ_DFT.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

11. **The physical approximations in the 2PB model are not justified for the parameter range used.** (i) Quasi-elastic scattering: evaluating τ(E) ∝ 1/D(E) at the carrier energy ignores the phonon energy; with Θ_D = 400 K (k_BΘ_D ≈ 34 meV) and Δ = 20–100 meV (Fig. 3), transitions sample D(E ± ħω) and smear the edge on a scale comparable to Δ. (ii) Chemical potential: with a huge, asymmetric DOS at the edge (m_FB = 100 m₀), μ can shift by tens of meV at 1000 K at fixed carrier number; Methods do not say whether μ or N was held fixed, which matters directly for the Δ/3 onset and the non-metallic hump in Fig. 3c. (iii) Factorisation of the T dependence into a BG term times an energy integral (Eqs. 5–7) is exact only if the phonon-occupation factor is independent of electron energy across the window. The authors should quantify each, e.g. by evaluating Eq. 3 with the full inelastic kernel and with N conserved for one parameter set from Fig. 3.
Raised by: r3

12. **Ni3In's correlated (strange-metal) behaviour is not separated from electron-phonon scattering.** Ni3In shows T-linear, non-Fermi-liquid resistivity at low T attributed to flat-band correlations (ref. 23), and in-plane single crystals show n = 1. Using (ρ − ρ₀)⁻¹ at 100 K as the electron-phonon σ(E) in Fig. 4c assumes correlation-driven scattering is negligible there; the Kondo argument on p. 6 (T* ≈ 50 K) covers only one kind of correlation physics. The authors should discuss how a correlation contribution would distort Fig. 4c near x = 0 and whether the reconstruction changes with a higher reference temperature.
Raised by: r2

13. **The specific-heat support (Fig. 5c) needs checking and does not discriminate as presented.** The value γ ≈ 0.03 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² for Pd3Pb (p. 6) is about two orders of magnitude below typical metals, below Cu per atom and below a free-electron estimate for a Pd-based metal, corresponding to a DOS of order 0.01 states eV⁻¹ f.u.⁻¹; the Pd3Pb bar in Fig. 5c also appears larger than 0.03 on the plotted scale. If correct, Pd3Pb is effectively a semimetal, which weakens its use as a "good metal" test and brings thermally activated carriers into play. γ values are compared for compounds with 2, 3 and 4 atoms per formula unit without stating the normalisation, and Ni3In's γ ≈ 51 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² includes correlation, spin-fluctuation and electron-phonon mass enhancement, so it is not a bare DOS measure. The authors should confirm the Pd3Pb value and units (fit range, Schottky or nuclear contributions), show the C/T versus T² data and fit (Fig. S4), state units per atom, and compare each γ with the DFT bare γ_band / D(E_F) to separate mass renormalisation from DOS.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

14. **Anisotropy and direction mixing are not addressed.** Ni3In and CoSn are quasi-2D kagome systems with strongly anisotropic velocities, and the kagome flat bands disperse mainly in-plane. The isotropic 2PB model is compared with polycrystalline (Ni3In1−xSnx, Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb) and c-axis-only (CoSn) data without discussing the consequences of mixing directions. The authors should discuss this and measure CoSn in the ab plane, which would also test whether the curvature onset is direction-independent, as a phase-space (not velocity) mechanism predicts.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

15. **The Ni3In data are inconsistent between figures, and the fit in Fig. 5a does not use the authors' own wide-range data.** In Fig. 4a (polycrystal, this work) ρ − ρ₀ of Ni3In is about 50 µΩ cm at 300 K and about 75 µΩ cm at 860 K; in Fig. 5a (literature single crystal, Gim et al., ref. 34) ρ at 300 K is about 115 µΩ cm, and the fit stops at about 300 K. The factor-of-two difference (geometry, anisotropy, polycrystal averaging) should be discussed, and Fig. 5a should use, or add, the authors' own Ni3In data to 860 K, where the sublinear curvature is most visible and the test of Eq. 1 is most stringent.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

16. **Novelty relative to Mott/Wilson s-d scattering and to the authors' prior work is not delineated.** The model in Methods (τ ∝ 1/D_tot, Eqs. 2–7) is close to the classic Mott s-d picture (ref. 33) and to the authors' earlier analyses (refs. 39, 53–58); ref. 39 (PRX 15, 021054) already studies Ni3In1−xSnx tuning of the flat band with the same first author. The authors should state explicitly which results are new (data, the Fig. 4c reconstruction, the Δ/3 criterion, Eq. 7), since this determines the significance assessment.
Raised by: r2

17. **Thermopower is available but not used.** The conclusion proposes the Seebeck coefficient as a test of whether the FB lies above or below E_F, and the ZEM3 setup used for the high-T resistivity measures S(T) simultaneously; ref. 39 analysed Ni3In1−xSnx this way. Including S(T) for CoSn, Pt3Sn, Pd3Pb and across the Ni3In1−xSnx series, where the sign of dσ/dE should change at x ≈ 0.5, would be an inexpensive independent check of the σ(E) reconstruction and the FB placement.
