B. Should address

1. **The quantitative test of the two-site renormalization cannot tell it apart from scatter in J_K, and the flagship chain falls outside the model.** The main text (p. 7) says the picture was "validated" and that "the model reproduces the experimental evolution well" (Fig. 3d, Section S5). With J_K = 0.6 meV, the model's binding energy for odd chains with |J| ≫ J_K is about J_K/2 ≈ 0.3 meV, and for a free spin-1/2 it is 3J_K/4 ≈ 0.45 meV. So the distinctive odd-chain prediction is a factor of 2/3, which grows toward 1 as N increases and |J| falls. The ±0.2 meV (about 33%) spread in J_K turns the blue band in Fig. 3d into a region roughly 0.1–0.5 meV wide. That covers almost every odd-chain point (0.15–0.55 meV), whether or not the renormalization operates. The authors themselves attribute the scatter to site-dependent J_K. Also, the N = 11 chain of Fig. 2 and Section S2 has δE_YSR ≈ 0.8 meV and sits "outside the mapped range" (Fig. 3d caption, Fig. S11a). For an odd chain with |J| ≈ 0.8 meV ~ J_K, the model can reach this only if J_K is well above 1 meV (about 1.1 meV even in the J → 0 limit, and more for J ~ J_K), far outside 0.6 ± 0.2 meV. As it stands, the support for the mechanism is the presence or absence of YSR states, not quantitative agreement, and "validated" overstates this. The authors already have the tool for a decisive test. They should measure δE_YSR at one end of several odd chains of different lengths, dehydrogenate the other end, and measure again on the same terminus (same J_K, same adsorption site). The model predicts a rise of up to about 3/2 for short odd chains (N = 3, 5, 7) and a ratio near 1 for long ones. On even chains it predicts emergence from zero (so far only one N = 8 chain, Fig. S16, is shown; the N = 14 chain of Fig. S15 is a different regime). The before/after pairs for all the "several chains" already passivated should be reported and plotted against the curves of Fig. 3c as J_K* versus |J|, chain by chain. All chains should appear in Fig. 3d, including the out-of-range ones, with their number stated. Otherwise the wording should be reduced to qualitative consistency.

2. **The "absence" of YSR states in even chains needs a quantitative upper bound, and the text contradicts itself on where they reappear.** In the model, even-chain YSR states do not vanish; they move toward the gap edge as J/J_K grows. For N = 10 (|J| ≈ 1.0 meV, |J|/J_K ≈ 1.7), Fig. 3c gives J_K*/J_K ≈ 0.3–0.4, which implies δE_YSR ≈ 0.1–0.2 meV, comparable to the dGSJ linewidth in Figs. S10b and S12. The red model band for N = 10–14 in Fig. 3d predicts binding energies of about 0.1–0.25 meV, similar to some odd chains, yet the even-chain experimental points are plotted at δE ≈ 0 without error bars or a stated fitting procedure. The authors themselves say that "many YSR states overlap with the dGSJ peaks" and need two-Lorentzian fits (Section S2). In Figs. S6, S8b, S10 and S12 the dGSJ peaks at even-chain ends look lower and reshaped compared with the substrate, which may be unresolved YSR weight, as the authors argue for N = 12 and 14 (Fig. S14 inset). Separately, p. 7 says even-chain YSR signal is "already detected for N = 12 (Fig. S12)", the Fig. 3d caption says it "appears only for N ≥ 14 (Fig. S14)", and Fig. S12 itself only mentions a "particle-hole asymmetry … attributed to incipient emergence". The authors should fit every even-N end, N = 4 to 14, with the same dGSJ + YSR lineshape and tip-DOS deconvolution used for odd chains (Section S2, Fig. S2), with and without a YSR component, and report either an extracted δE_YSR or the largest value consistent with each spectrum. These should be plotted with error bars against the red band in Fig. 3d, and the N = 12 / N ≥ 14 statements reconciled. Only then does "absent" discriminate the model from a simple "no YSR" picture.

3. **The statistics behind the parity rule are not given.** The main claim (p. 7) is that "all odd-length chains host bound YSR states … while even-length chains show no sub-gap YSR signal". Figs. The reader cannot tell how often the rule holds, whether any odd chain lacked a resolvable YSR state or any short even chain (N ≤ 10) showed one, or whether chains with defects, kinks or particular herringbone end sites were excluded. The authors should add a table listing, for each N: the number of chains and termini measured; how many show a YSR peak, an ST/TS peak, or neither; δE_YSR at each end; E_ST/E_TS; the chain conformation (kink sequence, e.g. Figs. S10, S13) and herringbone domain (fcc/hcp/ridge) of each terminus; and the tip used. They should state the exclusion criteria and report every exception.

4. **The zero-bandwidth single-site BCS model is not matched to a proximitized film, and is used outside its stated range.** The two-site model (Eqs. S7–S9, Section S5) couples each edge spin to one BCS site with gap Δ and assumes J_K ≪ Δ. The substrate is a ballistic proximitized film whose low-energy spectrum is dominated by dGSJ states below Δ_Nb (Fig. 1d); the authors note a Dynes function fails for it (Section S2), and binding energies are measured from E_dGSJ. Their earlier work (ref. 38) shows that YSR states in such films hybridize with dGSJ states and are pushed away from them near the gap edge, which is exactly where the small even-chain and long-chain binding energies lie. J_K = 0.6 meV against Δ_sample ≈ 1.2 meV is not "J_K ≪ Δ" (Fig. S15, Section S5), and the binding energies in Fig. 3d reach 0.5 meV out of about 1.2 meV. J_K is quoted in meV as if it were a physical exchange, but it is an effective parameter of this model, and the model is called "qualitative" yet used quantitatively in Figs. 3c and 3d. The authors should state which gap plays Δ (Nb gap, E_dGSJ, or Δ_sample = 1.2 meV); show how the J_K*/J_K curves of Fig. 3c and the bands of Fig. 3d change at J_K/Δ ≈ 0.5; benchmark the parity effect against NRG or a finite-bandwidth calculation with a proximitized normal layer for |J|/J_K ≈ 1–20 (or cite the NRG benchmarks, SI refs. 27, 28, at comparable parameters); discuss how dGSJ–YSR hybridization affects the extracted δE; and state which conclusions survive if J_K is only an effective parameter.

5. **The topological, fractionalization and robustness claims go beyond the evidence.** The paper says the YSR maps establish "the topological origin of the observed YSR states" (p. 5) and that the fractional S = 1/2 edge spins "remain robust in a superconducting environment" (pp. 3, 9; abstract, conclusion). A YSR state localized on the terminal unit with zigzag-edge shape (Fig. 2f) is what any unpaired spin on a terminal unit would give. End localization and the even-odd alternation of the ground-state spin follow from Lieb-Mattis for any finite antiferromagnetic chain on a bipartite lattice, and are consistent with the AKLT picture, but do not test symmetry protection. The spin-1/2 character of each end comes from the ST/TS energies (Fig. 3e) and the model, not from the YSR maps. Since J_K ≪ J0 (0.6 against 18 meV), survival of the edge states is expected rather than tested, and no perturbation (defect, substrate site, kink) probes protection. The authors should soften this wording, or add evidence specific to the Haldane phase: for example, show that a defect inside a chain creates new edge spins with YSR signatures; compare the measured YSR decay length along the chain, or its N-dependence, with the edge-state localization length of the BLBQ model; and add the natural reference structures if present on the surface (the 3:1 precursor ratio should produce them): an isolated triangulene (N = 1, S = 1, expected to show YSR) and an N = 2 dimer (singlet with large J, expected to show none).

6. **The odd-chain triplet and even-chain singlet ground states are inferred, not measured.** The abstract and p. 7 assign S = 1 to odd chains and S = 0 to even chains. The evidence is the YSR pattern and the weak TS peaks (Figs. S7, S9, S11, S13). The two-site model simply assumes that J changes sign with parity (Section S5: "J is positive … for even chains and negative … for odd chains"). There is no magnetic-field dependence and no normal-state reference in which the TS/ST excitations would show the Zeeman splitting expected for each multiplicity. The authors should add field-dependent spectra below the Nb critical field (Zeeman response of the TS step or YSR state), or normal-state spectra above it showing a Kondo or Zeeman signature on odd chains only, on at least one odd and one even chain. Alternatively, they should show that the full BLBQ calculation of the sign and magnitude of the edge J (Section S4) is consistent with the observed spectra.

7. **The weak-coupling (free-spin) regime is established on only one dehydrogenated end.** The interpretation of all odd-chain YSR peaks as excitations from a free-spin ground state, rather than a screened one, rests on the tip-approach test of Fig. S15c, done on one dehydrogenated N = 14 end (a free spin-1/2). For the S = 1 triplet coupled to two channels, screening and the position of the quantum phase transition differ. The test also assumes the tip reduces J_K rather than changing the junction or adding tip-induced potential scattering, and the YSR state merging into the dGSJ peaks on approach is also how a YSR state hybridizing with dGSJ might behave. The authors should repeat the tip-approach series on several intact odd-chain ends at several N, including the 0.8 meV N = 11 end, and on a passivated end with larger binding energy.

8. **The passivation control that sets J_K needs detail and statistics.** J_K = 0.6 ± 0.2 meV is "averaged from chains with a single edge state", but only the N = 14 chain (δE = 0.36 meV, which implies J_K ≈ 0.48 meV via 3J_K/4; Fig. S15) and the N = 8 chain (Fig. S16) are shown. The main text points to "Section S4" for this, but S4 is the BLBQ model and the data are in S3. The authors should state how many chains entered the average and the spread of J_K, the formula used to convert binding energy into J_K, and whether J_K is an energy or a dimensionless product with ν0 absorbed. Dehydrogenating one site of a terminal S = 1 triangulene leaves at most an S = 1/2 unit, so they should explain why this quenches the fractional edge spin at that end rather than leaving a residual spin. They should also give evidence that σ-radical bonding to Au does not change the adsorption, and hence J_K, of the opposite end, for example by a before/after comparison on a passivated odd chain.

9. **Energies are extracted without uncertainties or a consistent, documented rule.** δE_YSR, E_ST and E_TS in Figs. 3d and 3e come from raw peak separations, justified by a single deconvolution (N = 11, δE ≈ 0.8 meV, error < 3%; Section S2). The NDR-induced shift is likely different for YSR peaks within the dGSJ linewidth. Figs. 3d and 3e have no error bars. For N = 11 and 13, E_TS ≈ 0.5–0.8 meV is read from weak shoulders on the dGSJ negative-differential-resistance dips (Figs. S11, S13), and these |J| values feed the model band in Fig. 3d. Some values are hard to reconcile with the figures: Fig. S13 quotes E_TS ≈ 0.5 meV while its marker sits near 2.9 mV, about 0.8 meV above the dGSJ peak; Fig. 3e has experimental |J| for N = 3 (TS ≈ 12 meV) and N = 14, but Fig. S3 covers only ±5 mV and Fig. S14 shows no ST feature; the N = 5 TS energy cannot be read precisely from Fig. S5c; and N = 12 (E_ST ≈ 0.5 meV) and N = 13 (E_TS ≈ 0.5 meV) are not monotonic as exponential decay would require. The authors should apply the Fig. S2 deconvolution to every spectrum, or at least those with δE < 0.3 meV or E_ST/TS < 1 meV; describe how shoulders were located; tabulate E_ST/E_TS and δE_YSR with uncertainties for every chain, noting whether each comes from peak positions or a fit; add error bars to Figs. 3d and 3e and propagate the |J| uncertainties into the model band; provide the missing spectra; and state explicitly that the BLBQ parameters in Fig. 3e (J0 ≈ 18 meV, β ≈ 0.09) are taken from ref. 13 and not refitted, if so.

10. **The attribution of J_K scatter to the herringbone reconstruction is untested, and chain conformation is not considered.** p. 6 says the scatter is "probably due to the Au(111) herringbone reconstruction" (ref. 45) without showing any correlation. The two ends of the same chain often differ markedly (Figs. S7b, S9b, S11a, S13b), and two different N = 11 chains give clearly different binding energies (Fig. S11a,b). The chains also have different cis/trans kink sequences (Figs. 2c, S10a, S13a), which may change both J (through the end-unit geometry) and J_K. Film thickness and local gap variations across the film, and the number of film preparations, are not given. Since this scatter is what limits the test in concern 1, the authors should correlate δE_YSR with the end unit's herringbone site (fcc, hcp, elbow/soliton), its conformation and orientation, and the local dGSJ energy, using the topographs they already have, and report both ends of each chain in Fig. 3d.

11. **The claim that the collective spin modes are decoupled from the superconductor is not tested.** The abstract and p. 9 state that the CS modes are "decoupled from the superconductor by the much larger Haldane gap" and that their spatial patterns are "perfectly reproduced" by the BLBQ model. No measurement addresses the decoupling, and the comparison in Fig. 4c is visual (overlaid circles, no fit metric). The authors should tabulate measured and calculated CS and ST energies (and linewidths) for each N, give a numerical residual for Figs. 4c,d, and compare them with the same chains on bulk Au(111) (ref. 13), stating whether any mode shows a substrate-induced shift or broadening. Agreement would support decoupling; a systematic shift would measure the substrate's renormalization of the chain. Otherwise the statements should be softened.
