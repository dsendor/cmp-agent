B. Should address

1. The measured feature does not behave as an intraband continuum should as q → 0, and its energy-to-momentum ratio exceeds the quoted Fermi velocity. The intraband continuum is bounded by ω ≤ v_F·q, so its weight (and, for a cylindrical σ sheet, the peak of Im χ0 near ω ≈ v_F·q) must collapse linearly to zero energy as q → 0. The paper's own numbers do not show this. In Fig. 3e the experimental peak is already at about 0.35–0.42 eV at q_par ≈ 0.05–0.055 Å^-1 and rises to about 0.6 eV at 0.155 Å^-1; the dashed linear guide extrapolates to roughly 0.25–0.3 eV at q_par = 0. At q_par ≈ 0.05–0.06 Å^-1, ω/q ≈ 6–7 eV Å, about three times the σ velocity of "roughly 2 eV Å" given in the text; at q_par = 0.088 Å^-1 the peak at ~0.5 eV gives ω/q ≈ 5–6 eV Å, so the quoted 1.4 eV Å "speed" must be a slope with a large offset. In Fig. 2b (q_par = 0.016 Å^-1) the dagger feature still sits at ~0.2–0.3 eV, whereas any in-plane σ intraband response there would lie below ~0.03–0.05 eV, inside the 100 meV elastic line. The tight-binding χ0 points in Fig. 3e head much closer to the origin, and the text concedes they deviate from experiment "at smaller q_par" where the weight is "split between different features"; only the BSE reproduces the offset, and because the BSE includes core-hole and matrix-element effects, its agreement alone does not show the feature is the ground-state χ0 continuum. A gapped, linearly dispersing feature with a ~0.3 eV offset fits a collective or interband excitation, or a resolution/elastic-tail artifact, at least as naturally. The authors should fit ω(q_par) with a free intercept and report it with its uncertainty; show the low-q spectra (including q_par = 0.016 Å^-1) with the elastic line and fitted components; state explicitly what happens to the feature as q → 0 and explain the offset (out-of-plane momentum, band anisotropy, interactions, interband admixture), or narrow the claim accordingly; and, if possible, add data at smaller total |Q|, for example at smaller 2θ.

2. The apparent in-plane dispersion is entangled with changes in out-of-plane momentum and in polarization/orbital projection. q_par is varied only by rotating θ_in at fixed 2θ. At 2θ = 150° and ~187 eV, |Q| ≈ 0.18 Å^-1 is nearly fixed, so the Fig. 3a labels run from (q_par, q_perp) = (0.05, 0.14) to (0.16, 0.02), with |q| nearly constant (~0.15–0.16) across the series; q_perp is largest exactly where q_par is smallest (including at q_par = 0.016 Å^-1), which bears directly on concern 1. The σ sheets are quasi-2D cylinders, but the π sheets are 3D, and Fig. 4c assigns a large share of the low-energy weight to ππ intraband transitions, which disperse with q_perp. The same rotation also changes the projection of the incident polarization onto in-plane (σ, p_x,p_y) versus out-of-plane (π, p_z) orbitals — the mechanism the authors themselves use for the intensity suppression and the cos^4/sin^4 RPA weights — and the low-energy peak lies higher in π than in σ polarization (Supplementary Fig. S5), with π-intraband weight higher in energy than σ-intraband weight in Fig. 4c. The peak could therefore shift upward toward grazing incidence from reweighting alone. The "dispersion" in Figs. 3a, 3b and 3e cannot yet be attributed to in-plane momentum, and the σ-band (2D) picture and the slope-based velocity comparison may need to be reworked. The authors should give the full 3D Q (with units) for every spectrum and state whether the χ0, RPA and BSE calculations in Figs. 3c–e and 4 use the full 3D q of each spectrum or only q_par along Γ–M; use the two available geometries (2θ = 90° at BESSY, 150° at ALS) to compare spectra at the same q_par with different θ_in and q_perp, or at the same θ_in with different q_par; and fit the dispersion separately in σ and π incident polarization.

3. Interband σ–σ transitions are excluded with the wrong response function, so "conclusively demonstrates" (p. 4) is not supported. The paper's central argument is that RIXS measures an orbital-projected response (χ_{px px}, χ_{py py}, χ_{pz pz}; Eq. 5, Methods), not the total density response, yet the intra/inter decomposition in Fig. 4c is of the total charge χ0. In the total-density vertex, transitions between the two nearly degenerate σ bands (light- and heavy-hole) near Γ–A are suppressed at small q by band-orthogonality (coherence) factors; in a p_x- or p_y-projected vertex, which a linearly polarized B 1s → 2p transition selects, they are not. Such σ1 → σ2 transitions near k_F have finite energy as q → 0, set by the σ-band splitting at the Fermi surface (a few hundred meV), and would naturally produce the finite intercept and weak dispersion of concern 1; their weight depends on matrix elements the model leaves out. The BSE projection in Fig. 2c places weight on the σ bands, which fits either picture, and the Fig. 4c split is a property of the TB χ0 rather than an independent test of the measurement. The authors should repeat the Fig. 4c decomposition (σσ-intra, σσ-inter, ππ, σπ) for the orbital-projected χ0_aa and χRPA_aa for both polarizations, show it next to the polarization-resolved data of Supplementary Fig. S5, and if possible decompose the BSE RIXS spectrum itself into intraband and interband final states; otherwise the language should be tempered or tied explicitly to the experimental handles (polarization, lineshape, q → 0 behaviour).

4. The data may show an interaction-renormalized or collective response rather than the non-interacting continuum, and "quantitative agreement with simulations of the non-interacting charge susceptibility" (title/abstract) is overstated. Fig. 3e shows χ0 failing at small q_par: at q_par ≈ 0.055 Å^-1 the χ0 peak (~0.25 eV) is ~0.1 eV below the data, and the text says the χ0 intensity peaks at larger q than in experiment. The calculation said to match the data in energy and momentum is the interacting, orbitally weighted RPA (Fig. 4a), and comparison with Fig. 4b shows that interactions visibly move weight to higher energy — so interactions matter for exactly this feature. In a two-component σ/π metal with Coulomb coupling to total density, the unscreened orbital-projected channel of Eq. 5 is the out-of-phase channel that hosts the acoustic plasmon predicted for MgB2 (refs. 15, 26), which disperses linearly with a velocity between the two Fermi velocities — the same phenomenology as the observed feature. It is dismissed only with "expected at a smaller energy range" and a pointer to Supplementary Section F, with no numbers in the main text, and the paper does not say whether the Fig. 4a maximum is a pole of the RPA response or a reweighted continuum. The authors should plot the RPA peak dispersion in Fig. 3e alongside χ0, BSE and experiment; show the eigenvalues of [1 − Vχ0] (or the loss function) of their RPA model across the measured range and state where the acoustic-plasmon branch falls in the same (q_par, q_perp) coordinates, compared with refs. 15, 26; and either back "quantitative agreement" with a lineshape-level comparison or tone it down. If the best description is an interaction-dressed, orbitally weighted response, the title and abstract should say so.

5. There is no incident-energy (Raman versus fluorescence / intermediate-state) test of the low-energy feature. The feature is resonantly enhanced at a sharp, excitonic-like pre-edge resonance (Fig. 2a) in a metal, and all low-q data are at one incident energy (187 eV). In this regime the core-hole potential produces particle-hole shake-up in the intermediate state (x-ray-edge-singularity physics), which appears as a low-energy asymmetric RIXS tail, and fluorescence-like contributions near threshold give features whose loss energy follows detuning. Supplementary Fig. S2 is cited only for the features above 2 eV, and the BSE exciton-density projection (Eq. 2, Fig. 2c) identifies the states in the absorption step, not that the final state is an intraband σ–σ pair described by the ground-state χ0. The authors should show detuning-dependent spectra or incident-energy maps at fixed q (for example 186.5–188 eV in small steps through the pre-edge peak), demonstrating that the 0.3–0.6 eV feature stays at constant loss and giving its resonance profile, with the BSE detuning dependence for comparison; and they should quantify how much of the low-energy BSE intensity comes from core-hole shake-up versus the bare intraband channel, for example by reducing or switching off the core-hole potential.

6. The lineshape and width are not tested against the Lindhard form; only peak positions are compared. A continuum differs from a mode in its lineshape — for a 2D cylindrical sheet, Im χ0 ∝ ω/sqrt(v_F²q² − ω²) below ω = v_F q, with a low-ω onset ∝ ω, weight concentrated just below a sharp upper edge, and a width that grows linearly with q. The experimental peak positions come from phenomenological asymmetric Voigt fits, and the broadening "towards grazing incidence" is mentioned only qualitatively. The peak of a 2D cylinder's Im χ0 sits at the upper edge, so a peak tracking ~0.7 v_F also needs explaining (warping of the hexagonal σ sheets, q_perp, matrix elements, elastic-tail convolution); the upper edge or onset versus q is a sharper test of the Lindhard bound than the maximum. The authors should fit the spectra directly with resolution-convolved TB χ0_aa (σ- and π-intraband parts with free amplitudes) and χRPA_aa, with the elastic line as the only extra component; show lineshape overlays with residuals at several q_par (data against BSE, convolved χ0 and RPA); report width and integrated intensity versus q, compared with χ0 and RPA predictions; and compare the direction of asymmetry and the upper edge with v_F q from the TB model.

7. The Fermi velocities, the calculated continuum energy scales and the band assignment do not agree with one another. The text gives σ-band velocities of "roughly 2 eV Å", but in Fig. 4c (q_par = 0.088 Å^-1) the σσ intraband weight peaks at ~0.35 eV and extends to ~0.5 eV, and the ππ intraband weight to ~1.5 eV, implying edge velocities of ~4–6 and ~15–17 eV Å, inconsistent with the quoted σ velocity and with DFT/literature in-plane π velocities. The authors should state which band(s) produce the observed peak at each q_par; give the in-plane (Γ–M) and out-of-plane (c-axis) Fermi velocities of each sheet in their TB model; overlay the analytic continuum boundaries ω = v_F·q for each band, computed for the full 3D Q of each spectrum, on Figs. 3b–d and 4c (ideally in one summary figure with the experimental onset, peak and edge, the RPA and BSE peaks and the acoustic-plasmon branch); state the broadening and q_perp used in Figs. 3d and 4c; and explain why the calculated continuum extends to these energies.

8. Sample characterization, surface state and reproducibility are not documented. 9. The peak-extraction model and its uncertainties are under-described. Peak positions come from asymmetric Voigt fits (Supplementary Fig. S4) with error bars from the covariance matrix only. The feature sits just 0.25–0.5 eV above an elastic/quasi-elastic line at ~100 meV resolution and is intrinsically asymmetric, so its "peak position" depends on the elastic-line model, the background (tail of the ~3 eV feature) and the asymmetry parameter. The authors should state which Voigt parameters are free or fixed (including whether the Gaussian width is fixed to the resolution), how the elastic line and high-energy background were modelled and constrained, test the robustness of the low-q peak positions by refitting with alternative background and lineshape models, and include this systematic spread in the Fig. 3e error bars.

10. Several calculation parameters are unstated or ad hoc, and the comparison depends on them. For the BSE, resolving q_par down to 0.05 Å^-1 (Γ–M ≈ 1.18 Å^-1) needs a dense k-mesh near the Fermi surface, but the OCEAN k-mesh, broadening and screening parameters are not given in the main text, and the SCAN wavefunctions may give σ velocities different from the TB model. For the RPA, V(q) = U/q² with U = 2 eV Å is dimensionally inconsistent for a 3D Coulomb kernel; U is calibrated to the ~3 eV feature, which the paper itself describes as a Landau-damped collective excitation and which is anisotropic in a layered material; the cos^4 θ_in / sin^4 θ_in weights ("sinusoidal factors") are stated without derivation and cross-orbital terms are not discussed; and the full RPA intensity (Fig. 4a) is about ten times smaller than χ0 (Fig. 4b) without explanation. The authors should state the k-meshes and show convergence of the low-energy peak position, give the kernel with correct units, derive the polarization weights from the scattering geometry and dipole matrix elements, and show how sensitive the Fig. 4a peak is to U and to the orbital weighting.

11. The novelty claim and the generality of the sum-rule argument are stated too broadly. The intraband continuum has been probed before: non-resonant IXS in simple metals at q of order k_F and beyond the plasmon cutoff; electronic Raman scattering, which detects intraband continua at q ≈ 0 through non-conserved (unscreened) vertices — the same mechanism invoked here — and has been measured in MgB2 itself; momentum-resolved EELS on correlated metals; ref. 24 (EELS separating intra- and interband transitions in MgB2); and ref. 29 (IXS on MgB2, single-particle versus collective). "Has remained experimentally elusive" should be limited to the small-q (q ≪ k_F), plasmon-dominated regime, and the authors should discuss how the present work differs (momentum resolution, access to the dispersing edge). Likewise, the Discussion's claim that RIXS "will generically capture the Lindhard continuum" rests on Eqs. 4–5, a two-band toy RPA with orbital-diagonal coupling that treats the RIXS cross-section as an orbital-projected χ and neglects core-hole and intermediate-state effects. The authors should state these assumptions and when they fail (single-orbital metals, away from the pre-edge resonance, the ratio of orbital susceptibilities needed for O(1) weight), and check that the full BSE RIXS calculation shows the same absence of screening, for example by comparing RIXS-weighted and total-density (EELS-like) BSE or RPA responses at the same q.
