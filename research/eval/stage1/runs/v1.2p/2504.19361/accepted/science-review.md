A. Must address

1. Trapped flux and magnetic history are not excluded as the source of the point-symmetry violation and the zero-field diode (Fig. 2, Figs. S5, S6; Supplementary Note S7). The rings sit between Sr2RuO4 banks several micrometres across and 250–700 nm thick (Figs. 1b, S1, Table S1). Field scans span ±10 mT, and in-plane fields of 10–70 mT are applied in other experiments (Figs. 3, 4, S16, S17). Note S7 states that the remanent field of the superconducting magnet is neither nulled nor measured, so the samples are cooled through Tc in an unknown field; a fraction of a millitesla is enough to trap vortices in banks of this size. A vortex pinned in a bank, neck or arm near a weak link breaks time-reversal symmetry of the device without any TRSB in the order parameter, is a known mechanism for zero-field Josephson diodes, and distorts Ic(H) so that Ic+(H) ≠ |Ic-(-H)|. Its effect is not a uniform field offset, so the H0 symmetrisation does not remove it. Magnetic moments in the Ga-damaged edge layer or the SiOx cap are a second, smaller extrinsic possibility. The manuscript does not record the magnetic history before each Ic(H) dataset (zero-field-cooled or not, maximum field of the preceding sweep, magnet reset), and it never states whether the sign of the diode survives thermal cycling, although it says the bulk configuration is random while the wall is deterministically pinned. The sign of the effect already differs between devices: ΔI_TRSB(0) ≈ +4 µA in Ring 1 (Fig. 2b) and η < 0 in Ring 2 (Fig. S6). Because the headline claim is intrinsic, spontaneous TRSB, this scenario must be ruled out. The authors should (i) null the cooling field independently (on-chip Hall sensor, co-mounted reference SQUID, or a magnet reset or quench protocol); (ii) repeat zero-field cooldowns through Tc several times and report the sign and magnitude of η(H = 0) and ΔI_TRSB for each, across devices: random signs with fixed magnitude would support spontaneous domain formation, whereas an effect that vanishes when the cooling field is nulled, or that follows field history, would point to trapped flux; (iii) field-cool in small controlled ±Hz and show how the sign and size of η depend on the cooling field (sign training would directly link the asymmetry to chirality, as assumed in the toy model of S3 and reported for the Kerr signal, while vortex trapping sets in at a threshold of order Φ0 divided by the bank area); and (iv) show that η is unchanged after a field excursion below Tc.

B. Should address

2. The TRSB comparison is not shown to use time-reversed protocols, and the remanent-field offset H0 is fitted by minimising the very asymmetry being tested (Fig. 2b, 2c; Supplementary Note S7, Eqs. 9–10). The identity Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| holds only for branches taken under protocols related by time reversal; with any history dependence (trapped flux in the banks and leads, fluxoid-state hysteresis, or the mobile domains the authors invoke), the time reverse of an up-sweep at −H is a down-sweep at +H. The paper does not state whether Ic+ and Ic- at each field come from the same IV trace or separate sweeps, nor the sweep direction of each, and it does not show ΔI_TRSB for both sweep directions; the statement that the narrow lobe is independent of sweep direction (p. 8) is not backed by data. H0 is chosen to minimise ∫[Ic+(H) − |Ic-(-H)|]². This removes any uniform shift by construction but cannot remove a sweep-dependent offset, a field-dependent flux-focusing nonlinearity or magnet hysteresis. In Figs. 2b and 2c, ΔI_TRSB has spikes and sawtooth ramps and jumps concentrated at the lobe minima, where |dIc/dH| is largest, which is what a residual field displacement (ΔI ≈ δH·dIc/dH) would produce. The procedure also has two structural consequences: it subtracts by construction any φ0-like phase offset of the domain-wall junctions (Note S5, Fig. S13b), which shifts the pattern for both polarities exactly like H0, so the analysis is blind to the φ0 component the Discussion relies on; and the zero-field diode depends on where H = 0 is placed, since in an asymmetric SQUID mislocating zero turns an ordinary field-induced diode into an apparent zero-field one. The fitted H0 values, their uncertainty and their variation between sweeps, rings and cooldowns are not reported for any Sr2RuO4 dataset. The MoGe/Ag control passing the same pipeline (Figs. S8–S10, S12) shows the procedure does not manufacture asymmetry in a conventional device, but it does not address history dependence in the Sr2RuO4 banks. The authors should state the sweep and acquisition protocol; show ΔI_TRSB for up- and down-sweeps and for the time-reversed pairing (up-sweep Ic+(H) against down-sweep |Ic-(-H)| and vice versa); plot or fit ΔI_TRSB against a·dIc/dH + b plus a field-dependent offset and show the residual; report H0 with its confidence interval for every dataset and whether it agrees between sweeps and between rings in the same cooldown; show the uncorrected data alongside the corrected data; and, ideally, fix H0 independently with a Hall sensor or a TRS-preserving reference SQUID in the same cooldown and field history, analysed identically and shown to give ΔI_TRSB = 0.

3. The error budget for ΔI_TRSB and η(0) counts only voltage noise, and several systematics of the size of the signal are unquantified (Figs. 2b, 2c, S5b, S6; Supplementary Note S2; Eq. 1). ΔI_TRSB is a few µA on Ic of 60–140 µA, and |η| is 2–3.7% from three points without error bars (Fig. S6). In Ring 2 the violation is largely a field-independent vertical offset of about 5 µA. Note S2 states that the bootstrap "only accounts for the uncertainty in measured voltage"; it omits switching-current stochasticity, the uncertainty in H0, the field-axis uncertainty on the steep flanks, temperature drift, and the choice of the 0.1 or 0.2 I0RN threshold, which is selected "depending on the degree of rounding" without saying which value was used for which panel. With RN ≈ 20–100 mΩ this threshold is only about 0.1–1 µV, applied to visibly rounded IV curves (Fig. 1c). Temperature drift alone produced an apparent ΔI of about 1 µA on about 78 µA (≈1.3%) in the control SQUID (Fig. S10), comparable to the Ring 2 diode, whose data are taken up to 1000 mK, close to Tc ≈ 1.35 K, where dIc/dT is large. Two further artifacts are not considered: any voltage even in current (for example a thermoelectric voltage from I² heating of the Ti/Au leads or contacts) survives the near-zero offset subtraction and shifts Ic+ and |Ic-| in opposite directions by a field-independent amount, which is exactly the pattern of Fig. 2c; and if the current runs 0 → +I → 0 → −I, the switching event on one polarity may alter the state before the other is measured. The authors should give repeated sweeps of the same field scan to obtain an empirical run-to-run spread; show ΔI_TRSB(H) and η(0) for both thresholds and for a threshold-free definition (dV/dI peak or a rounded-RSJ fit of each branch); propagate the H0 uncertainty; report thermometer stability during each map; show the even-in-current component [V(I) + V(−I)]/2 below Ic; state the polarity acquisition order and show that reversing it leaves ΔI_TRSB unchanged; and fold the systematic contributions into the error bars of Figs. 2 and S6.

5. The narrow, skewed central lobe has a conventional explanation in flux focusing and vortex entry in the Sr2RuO4 banks that is not excluded, and the paper's own data weaken its link to TRSB (Fig. 2a, Figs. S5, S11). In perpendicular field the Meissner banks focus flux into the loop, enlarging the effective area and narrowing the lobes; once vortices enter the banks, which can happen at a few mT given the small Hc1 and strong demagnetisation of a thin flake, the focusing changes and the period relaxes, and pinned vortices make the pattern history dependent. Ring 3 at 1200 mK is described as showing "normal SQUID behavior" with no significant TRSB (Fig. S5a), yet its central lobe is already narrowed to 0.82 Φ0 against 0.92–0.93 Φ0 for its neighbours, so narrowing occurs without measurable TRSB. The authors should show Ic(H) for both sweep directions and for minor loops, giving the central-lobe width and ΔI_TRSB as a function of the maximum field of the preceding sweep (vortex entry into the banks is hysteretic, whereas domain-wall motion confined by the necks should be largely reversible); tabulate central-lobe width against temperature for each ring together with ΔI_TRSB; and fit a flux-focusing model as an alternative.

6. The half-integer Shapiro steps (Fig. 4, Figs. S15, S16) are not shown to reflect a changed current-phase relation of an individual junction. In a two-junction loop biased near half a flux quantum the first harmonic of the effective current-phase relation is suppressed and half-integer steps appear; Fig. S16 states exactly this for the steps near the Ic(B) minima. The low-Ic state of Fig. 4 may sit closer to such a minimum because of a perpendicular component from misalignment of the in-plane field (1° of 10 mT gives about 0.2 mT, and 1° of 20 mT about 0.35 mT, sizeable fractions of the 4–5 mT period of Ring 1) or because of a different trapped-flux configuration; field alignment accuracy is not stated anywhere. In addition, an in-plane field of 10–20 mT threads about one flux quantum through the arm cross-section (thickness 0.25–0.7 µm times an effective length of about 2λ plus the wall width), so the junctions have a Fraunhofer-like in-plane modulation, with nodes found near 4.5 and 9.9 mT in Fig. S16; Fig. 4 is taken at 10 mT, close to such a node, though at a different angle. The dynamical regime is also not considered: at 144 MHz, hf/2e ≈ 0.3 µV against IcRN ≈ 1.4 µV for Ring 1 gives a reduced frequency of about 0.2, where capacitive or inductive dynamics can generate subharmonic steps even for a sinusoidal current-phase relation. The claim that λJ exceeds the arm width is asserted, not calculated. The steps are not reproducible: after a field reset they disappear from one polarity and an integer step goes missing (Fig. S15). The temperature, frequency and power at the sample for Fig. 4 are not given (only S15 gives 145 MHz and 50 mK). The authors should state the alignment procedure, perpendicular-field calibration and their uncertainty; measure Ic(Hz) at fixed in-plane field for both Ic states to locate each on the SQUID pattern; map the fractional-step amplitude against Hz trim, in-plane field magnitude and angle, power and frequency; compare the integer and half-integer step widths against power with RSJ/RCSJ calculations for a SQUID with sinusoidal current-phase relation, finite βL and the estimated flux bias; show that the half-steps persist where the first harmonic is not suppressed (at SQUID maxima and away from in-plane nodes); give the λJ estimate; and, until then, present "a different current-phase relation between the two Ic states" as one possibility.

7. The bistable, current-switchable Ic states (Fig. 3, Figs. S14, S17) have conventional explanations in vortex motion or fluxoid transitions that the paper does not discuss. A supercritical training current exerts a Lorentz force J × B on in-plane vortices (or, through misalignment, out-of-plane vortices) in the banks and arms; the force reverses with either current or field, which matches the observed rule that the roles of the training polarities swap when Hx is reversed (S14), and different pinned configurations give different Ic. Sr2RuO4 is strongly anisotropic and the crystals are 0.25–0.7 µm thick, so a 10–20 mT in-plane field penetrates substantially; the absence of hysteresis below 10 mT (Fig. S17) is consistent with a vortex-entry threshold. The angular map (S17b) is sparse and speckled because each pixel is a single IV trace, and the optimal angle differs between samples (20° for Ring 1, 55° for Ring 2) with no correspondence to geometry, which fits a misalignment-dependent perpendicular component or pinning better than an order-parameter property. The Josephson-penetration-depth argument (p. 11) addresses only the half-integer steps, not the bistability. Separately, if the ring inductance parameter βL is not small, current-induced transitions between fluxoid states could give two Ic branches; the modulation depth of about 35% in Fig. 2a requires either strong junction asymmetry or a sizeable βL. The authors should quantify the field alignment and out-of-plane component; estimate the in-plane lower critical and entry fields of the banks and arms; report the fitted or estimated βL and junction asymmetry; show the training-current threshold against in-plane field magnitude and temperature; test out-of-plane tilt dependence and whether each state persists when the field is removed and re-applied; map Ic(Hz) in both states to see whether they correspond to different positions on the SQUID pattern (domain-wall reconfiguration and an added vortex should change it differently); test whether bistability survives in the singly connected Ring 2; repeat the S17 map with averaging to establish that the optimal angle is reproducible; and explain why the vortex scenario is excluded.

8. The domain-wall toy model (Supplementary Note S3, Fig. S11) does not account for the headline TRSB, is weakly constrained and internally inconsistent, and has consequences that conflict with other data. As written, φD(H) is odd about π/2 (Eq. 4), so H → −H with chirality exchange is a symmetry of the model: it preserves Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| and produces neither the skew of the central lobe, nor ΔI_TRSB, nor the zero-field diode, even though the paper presents these as having a common origin with the narrow lobe. Eq. 5 gives Φ = Φext + 2Φint^max at H = 0 (φD = π/2), contradicting the statement that the domain contributions cancel at zero field; presumably (φD − π/2)/(π/2) was intended. Eq. 3 couples order-parameter amplitudes linearly to B without a stated Ginzburg–Landau functional. The fit uses φm, a, Φint^max, the RCSJ αI and βL, a Gaussian envelope width and amplitude on about five lobes; the parameters and their posteriors are not listed, Φint^max is poorly determined (0.16 +0.13/−0.06 Φ0), and the lobe heights are visibly missed. Fitted Φint^max of 0.16–0.24 Φ0 through a ~1 µm ring implies mean spontaneous fields of order 1 mT and chiral currents that the scanning-SQUID searches of Sr2RuO4 mesostructures (refs. 28–30) should have detected. The authors should state which rings were fitted and with how many free parameters, report all parameters with uncertainties and correlations, fit both current branches simultaneously, correct Eq. 5, extend the model so that it generates the skew and ΔI_TRSB(H) or state that it does not, and discuss consistency with the scanning-SQUID bounds.

11. The claim that the devices are strain-free, used to reject accidental-degeneracy scenarios and favour dxz + idyz, is not established (Methods; Discussion pp. 14–16). The statement that Tc ≤ 1.5 K "demonstrates the absence of any edge-dislocations or strain" does not follow: absence of the 3K phase only excludes a percolating 3K path, while strains far smaller than those that raise Tc (for example inhomogeneous strain of order 0.1% from differential thermal contraction against the SrTiO3 substrate, the SiOx or Au cap, or Ga-implanted edges) can split the TRSB and superconducting transitions [25] and matter in the cited theories (refs. 61–65). The Tc spread of 1.3–1.5 K across devices, below the bulk value (Table S1), may indicate device-to-device disorder or strain. The paper itself notes that heat-capacity and elastocaloric data conflict with dxz + idyz [59, 60, 66, 67]. The authors should soften the strain claim or support it with estimates or measurements (finite-element thermal-contraction modelling, Raman, X-ray diffraction), discuss whether strain-inhomogeneity models [64, 65] could produce mobile walls, and present dxz + idyz as one possibility among others.
