A. Must address

1. Trapped flux, magnet history and the absence of a training or cooldown test leave an extrinsic origin of the TRSB signal open (Sec. IV, Fig. 2b,c, Fig. S6; Sec. S2, Eq. 1). The identity Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| holds only if the whole device, including any frozen-in flux configuration, is time-reversed together with the field. Several ordinary effects break it without any TRSB of the order parameter: Abrikosov or Pearl vortices pinned in the wide, µm-scale Sr2RuO4 banks, lead arms or Au/Ti contacts near one junction; flux trapped on cooldown in a remanent magnet field; and hysteresis of the superconducting magnet, since +H and -H are reached with different sweep histories. Each acts as an internal magnetization that does not reverse with H and produces an inhomogeneous local phase bias that the single global shift H -> H + H0 of Eq. 1 cannot remove, and that a shift cannot correct for sweep-direction-dependent remanence. Such flux can also give a finite Ic+ - |Ic-| at nominally zero field, i.e. an apparent zero-field diode. Vortex entry is likely: the c-axis Hc1 of Sr2RuO4 is only a few mT, the sweeps in Fig. 2 reach about ±10 mT, and a vortex near a weak link is known to give exactly this kind of offset, asymmetric Ic(H). The paper does not state whether the data were taken after zero-field cooling with the magnet degaussed or nulled, or how the field was swept; Sec. V says field-cooled and zero-field-cooled results agree for the bistable states, but this is not shown for the TRSB data. Conversely, the domain-wall picture makes a testable prediction: a spontaneously chosen domain configuration should have its time-reversed partner, so the sign of ΔIc and of the zero-field diode should vary randomly across zero-field cooldowns (or be set by pinning) and should follow the sign of a small cooling field, as in Kerr training (cited in S3); the paper only notes that earlier macroscopic devices changed on cycling. The authors should report ΔIc(H) and the zero-field diode (a) over several thermal cycles above Tc with zero-field cooling and the remanent field nulled, (b) after field cooling in small +Hz and -Hz, (c) for both field-sweep directions and for different maximum field excursions (minor loops), and (d) after warming to just above Tc and re-cooling in zero field, to check whether the symmetric pattern is recovered. A sign reversal under opposite field cooling would be strong evidence for intrinsic TRSB; a ΔIc that depends on field excursion or sweep history would point to trapped flux; a sign that never changes across cooldowns would point to an extrinsic origin. Without these, TRSB of the order parameter cannot be separated from TRSB of the device's flux configuration.

2. Instrumental offsets and other systematics are not excluded, and the error bars do not cover them (Sec. IV, Fig. 2b,c, Figs. S6, S9; Sec. S2). In Ring 2 (Fig. 2c), ΔIc is a nearly field-independent negative offset of about 5–8 µA at 400, 800 and 1000 mK on Ic of 60–140 µA, with jumps at the lobe minima; this is the same signature the paper describes as a zero-field diode "over the entire interference pattern". A field-independent offset is exactly what simple extrinsic sources give: a dc current offset in the bias source or a ground loop (a shift δI gives ΔIc = 2δI at every field); a polarity-dependent voltage background such as a thermoelectric voltage not removed by subtracting the mean voltage near I = 0; heating after switching at Ic of about 100 µA or the order in which polarities are swept; slow temperature drift, which S2 itself invokes for the apparent diodicity of about 1 µA, larger than its error bars, in the last half-periods of the control SQUID (Fig. S9); and a polarity-dependent Ic criterion, since the threshold is 0.1 or 0.2 I0RN "depending on the degree of rounding", with RN taken from "a representative portion of the dataset", and the paper does not say which threshold was used for each Sr2RuO4 dataset. The quoted error bars come from a parametric bootstrap that, as the text says, "only accounts for the uncertainty in measured voltage": they exclude the stochastic spread of switching currents (telegraph noise near Ic is well documented in Sr2RuO4 junctions, Refs. 31–33), sweep-to-sweep reproducibility and all the systematics above, and the paper does not say how many IV sweeps enter each point. The authors should: show raw IV curves at zero and a few other fields for both polarities, including with the sweep order reversed; take Ic+(H) and Ic-(-H) with interleaved polarity at each field point; repeat the measurement with the current leads swapped and with the voltage leads swapped (an extrinsic offset follows the wiring, an intrinsic asymmetry follows the device); report the measured zero-current voltage and current offsets; show that ΔIc is unchanged for several criteria (for example 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 I0RN, and a dV/dI peak); show switching-current histograms at a few field points and recompute the significance of ΔIc from the measured switching-distribution width and from repeated independent field sweeps; and give a systematic error budget (Ic criterion, offsets, field remanence, temperature drift) next to the statistical one. Without this, the "statistically significant" TRSB of Figs. 2 and S6 is not established at the level the headline claim needs.

3. There is no fabrication- and setup-matched null control (Secs. III–IV, Figs. S7–S11, Fig. S2). The only device shown to obey Ic+(H) = |Ic-(-H)| is a MoGe/Ag SNS SQUID with FIB line-cut junctions. 4. The evidence that the junctions are domain walls rather than FIB- or geometry-defined weak links is indirect (Sec. III, Figs. 1, 2a, Figs. S1, S3, S4). Shapiro steps (Fig. 1c,d, Fig. S3) show that a Josephson-like weak link exists somewhere between the voltage probes, but not where it is or that it is a chiral domain wall; the statement that "Shapiro steps can only originate from Josephson coupling" is too strong, since phase-slip centres and phase-slip lines in narrow strips also lock to microwaves and give steps at hf/2e. Fig. S3 is presented as confirming "the presence of a chiral domain wall" in the single-arm stage, but any FIB-defined constriction, Ga-implanted or amorphised region (TEM limits damage to about 10 nm at the edges, but the arms are only 0.25–0.7 µm thick and implantation is not quantified), local inhomogeneity, or weak link in the contact or bank regions could do the same. The argument that equal retrapping and critical currents exclude constriction junctions is not decisive: constrictions shunted by a large normal channel, or operating at IcRn far below Δ/e, are overdamped. From Fig. S1 (Ic about 45–80 µA, Rn about 19–112 mΩ; e.g. 80 µA × 19 mΩ ≈ 1.5 µV for Ring 1), IcRn is only about 1–8 µV, orders of magnitude below Δ/e of about 0.2–0.3 mV, and Rn is the whole-device resistance, so the junction value is unknown. The argument from the high symmetry of the pattern is qualitative and partly circular, since symmetric FIB-milled necks would naturally give symmetric constriction junctions. Because both the TRSB claim and the order-parameter conclusion rest on the junctions being intrinsic domain walls (extrinsic weak links with magnetic damage or trapped flux could also break time-reversal symmetry), the authors should: discuss the junction IcRn and its temperature dependence (Ambegaokar–Baratoff or KO-type versus phase-slip behaviour); compare rings with different neck widths or asymmetrically milled arms, or re-mill the necks, to show where the phase drop sits; compare quantitatively with the TDGL predictions of Ref. 38; state what each of the four milling stages of Ring 2 (Fig. S2) says about junction location; state how many nominally identical rings did and did not show junctions; and temper the statement that the behaviour "cannot be explained by accidental weak links".

5. The remanent-field correction is ambiguous and may bias the TRSB metric (Sec. S2, Eq. 1). As written, Eq. 1 minimizes ∫[Ic+(H+H0) − Ic−(H+H0)]², i.e. compares the two branches at the same field, which is the diode asymmetry rather than the time-reversal asymmetry Ic+(H) versus |Ic−(−H)|; taken literally it does not implement the symmetrization described, and it is unclear how it interacts with a genuine zero-field diode. Choosing H0 to minimize the very asymmetry later reported as TRSB biases ΔIc towards zero; moreover, a SQUID of two φ0-junctions with different anomalous phases gives a field-shifted pattern, so a shift-based correction partly absorbs exactly the intrinsic signature while leaving extrinsic distortions in place. The authors should correct Eq. 1 and state whether Ic− means |Ic−|, state the sign convention, give the fitted H0 for each dataset and for each sweep direction, compare it with an independent remanent-field calibration (a Hall sensor or a reference superconductor in the same mount) and with the magnet's known remanence, and show ΔIc with and without the H0 correction.

8. "Unstrained" and "strain-free" are asserted rather than shown, yet they are used to reject the leading alternative scenarios (Abstract, Sec. III, Sec. VII; Refs. 52–56). The argument rests on Tc never exceeding 1.5 K and RRR > 100, but these are bulk-averaged, and the device Tc values (1.3–1.5 K, Table S1) lie at or below bulk. Tc rises only gently at small strain, while the 3K phase requires dislocations or large strain. Differential thermal contraction between the 0.25–0.7 µm flake, the SrTiO3 substrate and the Ti/Au and SiOx/Au caps can impose inhomogeneous strain of order 0.1%, enough to split Tc and TRSB, and local strain, Ga implantation or amorphization at the milled sidewalls and necks (where the junctions are proposed to sit) is not excluded; the cited TEM (Ref. 43) is from other devices. The strain-texture and dislocation scenarios are precisely the leading alternative explanation for TRSB at domain walls. The authors should estimate the thermal-mismatch strain for this stack, characterize the neck regions directly if possible (cross-sectional TEM, Raman or local strain mapping on a measured device), or soften the argument and state that the discrimination against Refs. 55 and 56 rests mainly on domain-wall mobility.

9. The pairing-symmetry conclusion goes beyond the data (Abstract, Sec. VII, Conclusion). A transport TRSB signal with mobile domains is consistent with any two-component TRSB state with degenerate time-reversed domains, including accidental degeneracies and non-chiral combinations such as s+id or d+ig; only the toy model and the narrow central lobe invoke chirality and domain flux specifically. The statement that Eg (dxz + idyz) pairing "is favored based on the experiments", the Discussion's "Our experiments confirm the long-standing question" and the abstract's "direct evidence of TRSB" should be softened to consistency with a TRSB two-component state, or supported by an observable that separates chiral from non-chiral domains (for example the sign relation between field-cooling direction and diode sign requested in concern 1). The unresolved conflict with the absence of a second heat-capacity anomaly should be discussed.

12. There is a conceptual tension between reversible domain-wall motion and the observed TRSB (Sec. IV, Fig. 2a). If the domain walls move reversibly with field, as the independence of the central lobe from sweep direction implies, then H -> −H should mirror the domain configuration, which would tend to restore Ic+(H) = |Ic−(−H)| rather than break it. The authors should explain how a reversible, field-driven domain-wall position coexists with the reported TRSB.

13. The in-plane-field bistability has conventional explanations that are not excluded (Sec. V, Fig. 3, Figs. S13, S16). A misalignment of 1–3° of a 10–20 mT in-plane field gives an out-of-plane component of about 0.2–1 mT (1° of 20 mT ≈ 0.35 mT, about 0.08 Φ0 through the ring and a sizeable fraction of the 4–4.7 mT period); the paper gives no alignment procedure or out-of-plane calibration. If βL is not small, the ring can host several fluxoid states that a large bias current switches between. The observed symmetry, in which reversing either the current polarity or the field direction swaps the roles, is that of a J × B force on vortices lying parallel to the ab plane, Josephson-like vortices between RuO2 layers, or vortices trapped at the necks or entering the banks at training currents of about 110–120 µA. A 20 mT in-plane field through an arm cross-section of about 0.5 × 0.5 µm² gives several Φ0, so each junction is strongly field-modulated (Fig. S15), and current-driven switching between Josephson-vortex configurations in a wide junction is a known source of bistable Ic that the Josephson-penetration-depth argument of Sec. VI does not exclude. The optimal angle differs between rings (20° and 55°, Fig. S16) with no link to the crystal axes, and the map in Fig. S16b is irregular, which is more naturally explained by sample-specific pinning. The authors should quantify the field alignment and how it was set, give βL (kinetic plus geometric inductance) for each ring, show the Ic(Hz) pattern of the high- and low-Ic states (a relative shift would identify a fluxoid or flux-offset change), test whether bistability persists below the in-plane vortex entry field and when the out-of-plane component is compensated or varied, show the in-plane-field and temperature dependence of the switching thresholds and of the Ic difference, report the crystal-axis orientation of each ring, and discuss what pins the domain configuration.
