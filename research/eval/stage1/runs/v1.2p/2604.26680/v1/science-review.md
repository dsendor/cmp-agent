B. Should address

1. **Confinement of all textures to CoFeB, with CoB laterally uniform, is inferred rather than directly shown.** The abstract and Introduction (p. 4) say the textures are "unequivocally" attributed to the CoFeB sublattice. The model-free evidence is the Fe-edge XMCD loop (Fig. 3b), which shows that CoFeB carries the gradual reversal above 30 mT, and the DPC step in the maze state (Fig. 2n, 12.8 ± 0.7 T nm against 13.6 ± 0.2 T nm expected), which shows that exactly one sublattice reverses across a domain boundary. In a compensated SAF, however, flipping CoB locally gives the same MFM contrast and the same DPC step as flipping CoFeB. The DPC sign is also set "using assumptions informed primarily by the XMCD I(H) loops" (Methods), so it is not independent. DPC is quantified only for the maze state, not for the two skyrmion states, and SAXS is not reported as element-resolved. The statement that CoB stays uniform therefore rests on the reconstructed CoB loop (Fig. 3d, Eq. 2), I_Co − a·I_Fe, where a = 0.35 is chosen "by adjusting a until the reconstructed total loop matched the SQUID-VSM loop" (Methods), and that SQUID loop is itself rescaled (Fig. 3c). Eq. 1 also gives the Fe-edge term unit weight relative to the Co-edge term, although the two edges have different non-magnetic backgrounds (~4,700 vs ~7,000 counts) and different XMCD magnitudes per unit moment; this is a second, unstated parameter. Because the linear slope above 30 mT is present in both I_Co and I_Fe, whether Fig. 3d comes out flat above 30 mT depends directly on a. Fig. 3d is also not perfectly flat: the positive-field branch rises from ~2,420 to ~2,500, and the ascending and descending branches are offset by ~30 of 400 a.u. at |μ0H| > 30 mT and do not meet at +160 mT. This residual hysteresis is not discussed and may be real CoB behaviour or beam drift; the loops were taken at a single fixed helicity. Loops are spatial averages and cannot exclude CoB textures whose net m_z nearly cancels, or partial CoB perturbations under CoFeB walls, which the authors' own simulations show (p. 10). The authors should: (a) estimate a independently, from composition and XRR thicknesses (Co atoms in CoFeB relative to CoB ≈ (0.40 × 6.69)/(0.68 × 8.69) ≈ 0.45) and tabulated L3 XMCD cross-sections, or from Co- and Fe-edge loops on the CoFeB-only reference multilayer, and compare with 0.35; (b) show Fig. 3d for a range of a (e.g. 0.25–0.45, or ±30% of 0.35) with an uncertainty on a; (c) turn the residual high-field slope of Fig. 3d into an upper bound on the reversed CoB area fraction at 120 mT and 45 mT, and compare it with the core area fraction in Figs. 2b and 2d; (d) add helicity-reversed or I0-normalised loops to remove drift; (e) give DPC line-profile quantification for the conventional- and inverse-polarity states (Fig. 2k, m), not only the maze; and (f) most directly, compare resonant SAXS intensities (or STXM/ptychography/holography images) at the Co and Fe L3 edges at the same fields in both skyrmion states, where the Co-edge magnetic scattering should follow the CoFeB-only expectation if CoB carries no texture. The photon energy used for Fig. 2f–j must be stated. Without such tests, "unequivocally" should be softened.

2. **The skyrmionic, topological character of both families, and the claim that they carry opposite topological charge, are not shown in the main text.** The paper says the Néel nature "was verified experimentally using Lorentz microscopy as shown in Supplementary Note S4", without saying whether this holds for both families or with which handedness. The title ("Beyond conventional skyrmions") and the "inverse-polarity skyrmion" label depend on the low-field objects being homochiral Néel skyrmions with the same chirality as, and opposite core polarity and Q to, the high-field ones, rather than achiral bubble domains. D is not measured, and with D ≈ 1 mJ/m² and appreciable interlayer dipolar fields across 10 repeats, hybrid or thickness-dependent chirality is possible (refs. 19–21); if chirality is not uniform, the topological-charge argument fails. The paper also calls the textures "topologically protected" and states that the two families have opposite skyrmion Hall angles and "deflect in opposite transverse directions under current" (p. 10, abstract), based on simulations that are "not shown". The authors should bring tilt-series Fresnel or DPC evidence for both families into the main text, with the sign of the wall chirality for each, show that chirality is the same and core polarity opposite, and show a size-versus-field dependence for each family. They should either show the current-driven simulations, with trajectories, Hall angles and the computed topological charge per layer and for the stack, or present these statements as predictions or remove them.

3. **Novelty relative to known AF–FM phase coexistence in perpendicular layered antiferromagnets is not established, and the "beyond conventional" framing is not justified.** Hellwig et al. (refs. 30–31) and Hauet et al. (ref. 32) showed that in perpendicular [Co/Pt]/Ru layered antiferromagnets the field-driven AF-to-FM transition proceeds through domains in which a subset of layers reverses, giving FM-phase bubbles in an AF matrix and the reverse. The authors' own ref. 33 (PRB 109, 134437 (2024)) is titled "Phase coexistence and transitions between antiferromagnetic and ferromagnetic states in a synthetic antiferromagnet". The sequence in Fig. 6 (FM, AF bubbles in FM, maze, FM bubbles in AF, AF) is that same sequence, with the reversing sublattice preselected by its lower K_eff and the wall chirality fixed by DMI. In CoFeB's own frame, the inverse-polarity texture is an ordinary skyrmion in a background saturated along the local effective field (H_ext minus the RKKY field, or equivalently an exchange-bias field from a rigid CoB sublattice); the Discussion (pp. 12–13) effectively says this. The authors should state explicitly what ref. 33 observed and what is observed here that was not observed or not possible in refs. 30–33 (candidates: sublattice selectivity set by the K_eff asymmetry, homochiral Néel walls in both families, <100 nm size, opposite topological charge at fixed chirality), and frame the title and abstract around that.

4. **The effective-field mechanism is qualitative, leaves out magnetostatics, and is not quantitatively consistent with the field windows; the J_RKKY it rests on is questionable.** J_RKKY = −0.034 mJ/m² is obtained from setting μ0H_s = 125 mT equal to an RKKY spin-flip field (Methods, p. 16), using the bilayer expression J = μ0H_s·M_s·t/2, although interior layers in a ×10 stack couple to two neighbours. Between ~30 and 125 mT the moment rises through maze domains and skyrmions (the paper's own result), so H_s also contains dipolar (stripe-domain) and DMI-dependent annihilation contributions; Fig. 1c also appears to saturate nearer 150 mT. With M_s·t ≈ 5.4 × 10⁻⁴ A for CoFeB, the RKKY field from two CoB neighbours on an interior CoFeB layer is 2|J|/(M_s t) ≈ 125 mT, so the net field on CoFeB would vanish near 125 mT, where the FM state is observed. Experimentally the maze, which the Discussion (p. 13) associates with near-cancellation, is centred near 80 mT, with the two skyrmion families roughly symmetric about it (120 and 45 mT); the midpoint of the linear part of Fig. 3b is ~90–95 mT. The average FM-state magnetisation (~0.27 MA/m, μ0M ≈ 0.34 T) means demagnetising fields are at least comparable to the RKKY field. J is fed into the simulations, which inherit the ambiguity. The authors should give H_eff for each imaged state, including the stack demagnetising field (e.g. layer-resolved averages of RKKY, demagnetising and external fields on the CoFeB layers from the simulations of Fig. 4), and show whether the conventional and inverse windows are mirror images about H_eff = 0 or explain why not. They should obtain J independently (e.g. from the field where CoFeB m_z crosses zero or the minor-loop shift in the Fe-edge XMCD, the plateau-edge field, an in-plane loop, FMR, or a Ru-thickness series) or justify the formula used, and show how sensitive the simulated field windows are to J and D.

5. **The return-point-memory analysis supports family-specific sites, but not "independent nucleation pathways" or "distinct nucleation mechanisms: external-field-driven … and RKKY-driven" (Fig. 5, pp. 10–12, abstract).** Cross-family RPM values of 0.03–0.05 are called "negligible" or "uncorrelated", but no chance-level baseline is given. For the densities in Figs. 2b, d and any reasonable matching tolerance, random overlap would probably be well above 0.03–0.05, in which case the result is an anticorrelation, not independence. Anticorrelation is what a shared pinning landscape predicts: a site favouring a down CoFeB core at 120 mT is the last to keep down magnetisation at low field, where it becomes inverse-state background rather than an inverse core. Polarity-dependent pinning (local anisotropy or thickness variations favouring up or down cores) would also give family-specific sites with one mechanism. Paths 2 and 4 both start from the maze, so if the maze is reproducible, the two families form from segments of opposite contrast and low cross-overlap follows from geometry. The authors should: give the matching criterion and overlap tolerance, detection threshold, number of skyrmions per map and map area; compute the expected RPM for random or randomly shifted/rotated maps at the measured densities; report whether the cross-family value is below chance; overlay conventional and inverse core positions on the same region and compute their spatial cross-correlation, including with the interstitial sites of the conventional lattice; report the maze-state RPM; explain how drift between maps at +120 and +40 mT was registered; and state what the standard-deviation error bars (±0.005 to ±0.03) are taken over. The mechanism labels should be removed or supported (e.g. nucleation statistics versus field-cycling amplitude, or a link to specific defect sites), and RPM should not be described as probing "thermodynamic stability" (p. 10).

6. **The micromagnetic model is called "parameter-matched" but departs from the experiment in ways that matter for the claims it supports (Fig. 4, Methods, Table 2).** (i) Stack order is inverted: Fig. 4e and p. 10 ("very top CoFeB layer ... CoB below, vacuum above") place CoFeB on top, whereas the real stack Ta/[Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB]×10/Ru/Pt has CoB as the topmost magnetic layer, nearest the MFM tip; the top-layer-deviation result therefore refers to a different structure. (ii) CoB is modelled at 6 Å with M_s = 0.81 MA/m and a rescaled K_u = 0.63 MJ/m³, against measured 8.69 Å, 0.63 MA/m and 0.47 MJ/m³; this changes the RKKY field on CoB (∝ J/M_s t), the interlayer dipolar fields and how hard CoB is relative to CoFeB, which is exactly what the selectivity argument depends on. The CoFeB cell thickness is "6 Å (same as the experimental value)", against 6.69 Å measured. (iii) Spacer thicknesses, lateral boundary conditions for the 400 nm × 400 nm box (which holds only a few 50–80 nm skyrmions), damping and convergence criteria are not given. (iv) D is a free parameter (1.0 and 1.10 mJ/m²) with no stated fitting observable or uncertainty. (v) States are obtained by zero-temperature field-cooling from random configurations, and all initial conditions reportedly converge to the same state, which is surprising given maze/skyrmion metastability; the experiment is a hysteretic sweep with strong history dependence (±30 mT switching, path-dependent RPM). (vi) Simulated sizes in Fig. 4 are never compared with the measured 81/58 nm and 64/48 nm. (vii) Supporting simulations are "not shown": the symmetric-stack test, the current-driven Hall angles and the J_RKKY dependence ("reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers, whereas stronger coupling localizes the non-collinear state", pp. 13–14). The authors should simulate the real stack order and CoB parameters (or show insensitivity), run a quasi-static field sweep and compare simulated total and sublattice-resolved m(H) with Figs. 1c, 3b and 3d, compare simulated and measured diameters and field windows, state how D was fixed and give the missing settings, and include all "not shown" results in the SI.

7. **The "general materials-design principle" ("strategy is general and transferable", pp. 13–14) rests on one stack composition with no control.** The sister samples share one deposition on three substrates. No SAF with identical sublattices (CoB/CoB or CoFeB/CoFeB at compensation, where mirrored textures in both sublattices or sublattice-degenerate FM-phase domains are expected), no stack with the anisotropy asymmetry reversed (textures should move to CoB), and no stack with a different spacer or J is shown. Otherwise the three design criteria should be presented as hypotheses and the claim restricted to this system.

9. **The DMI argument is not supported by measurement.** The Discussion (p. 13) says CoFeB has "potentially reduced intrinsic DMI" offset by its smaller thickness, "yielding comparable D_eff", and calls the "enhanced ratio D_eff/K_eff in CoFeB" the decisive asymmetry. No D is measured; in the simulations D is fitted (1.0 and 1.1 mJ/m²), so the modelled asymmetry lies almost entirely in K_eff. "D_eff = D/t" is also confusing: D in Eq. 5 and Table 2 (mJ/m²) is already volume-normalised, and the interfacial quantity is D_s = D·t (pJ/m). The authors should measure D for the CoB and CoFeB reference multilayers (BLS, or domain-period/domain-wall-energy analysis from MFM as in ref. 48) or present the argument as conjecture, and clarify which quantity the simulations use.
