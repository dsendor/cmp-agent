B. Should address

1. The claim that all textures sit solely in CoFeB while the CoB sublattice "remains laterally uniform" (abstract, Introduction "unequivocally attribute", Fig. 3d, "Element-resolved magnetometry") depends on the scaling factor a = 0.35 in I_CoB = I_Co − a·I_Fe (Eqs. 1–2, Methods "Soft X-ray techniques"). This factor was chosen empirically so that the reconstructed total loop matches the SQUID-VSM loop, so the squareness of the CoB loop partly follows from that choice. The CoFeB loop (Fig. 3b) has large non-hysteretic slopes beyond ±30 mT, exactly where the textures appear, and any error in a adds or removes a fraction of that slope in the reconstructed CoB loop. There are two further reasons to doubt the fit. First, Eq. 1 reproduces the net moment only if diode current converts to moment with the same factor at the Co and Fe edges, and absorption cross-sections, elemental fractions (Co68B32 vs Co40Fe40B20) and transmitted backgrounds differ. The a that matches the SQUID-VSM loop therefore need not equal the ratio r of the CoFeB Co-edge signal to its Fe-edge signal, which is what Eq. 2 needs. Second, the SQUID-VSM loop was measured on Si/SiO2 and the XMCD on a sister sample on a 200-nm Si3N4 membrane, whose switching fields and slopes need not be identical. The other probes cannot settle which sublattice hosts the textures. Ms·t is equal for both sublattices by design (8.85 × 10⁻⁸ A m² each), so the DPC step (12.8 ± 0.7 vs 13.6 ± 0.2 T nm) is identical for CoB-only and CoFeB-only textures. The DPC zero reference is also "set by assumptions informed primarily by the XMCD I(H) loops", so DPC is not independent of the decomposition. MFM and SAXS are not element-selective as presented. The authors should: (i) fix a independently, by measuring Co L3 and Fe L3 loops or XMCD amplitudes on the CoFeB-only reference multilayer in the same geometry, from saturated XMCD amplitudes together with the known equal sublattice moments, or from tabulated cross-sections and Co content; (ii) show I_CoB(H) for a range of a (for example 0.25–0.45), give an uncertainty on a, and state an explicit upper bound on the fraction of CoB reversed at 45, 80 and 120 mT given the noise in Fig. 3d, including whether a single outer CoB layer (1/10 of the signal) would be resolvable; (iii) ideally, provide element-resolved data: resonant SAXS at the Fe vs Co L3 edges, normalized by the XMCD amplitudes (if the textures are only in CoFeB, the Co-edge magnetic scattering should scale with the Co content of CoFeB alone), or STXM/ptychography/holography at both edges for the conventional, maze and inverse states. The SAXS photon energy is not stated, so existing data may already allow this comparison. Without such a check, "unequivocally" and "solely" should be replaced by a bound such as "the net CoB moment remains saturated to within X%".

2. The effective-field mechanism (Discussion, Fig. 6) is argued qualitatively but not tested against the measured field windows. With the stated parameters, the RKKY field on an interior CoFeB layer from its two CoB neighbours is about 2|J|/(Ms·t) ≈ 125 mT (about 63 mT per neighbour), so the CoFeB effective field would vanish near 125 mT, not near the maze state at about 80 mT. The authors should compute H_eff, including the stack's demagnetizing and stray fields, for each observed state (conventional skyrmions at +120 mT, maze at +80 mT, inverse skyrmions at +45 mT) and compare the predicted zero crossing and window boundaries with the data. The CoFeB-only and CoB-only reference multilayers already used for Keff are a natural control: imaging them by MFM across a field sweep and plotting all three systems against H − H_RKKY would test whether the CoFeB windows map onto the SAF windows. If the CoB-only multilayer also hosts skyrmions in some window, the authors should explain why the RKKY field suppresses them in the SAF; if the windows do not map, the simple picture is incomplete (for example, dipolar coupling between tubes matters).

3. The micromagnetic support for confinement to CoFeB rests on a free DMI parameter, and the sensitivity of the result is not shown (Methods "Micromagnetic modelling", Extended Data Table 2, Discussion). D = 1.10 (CoFeB) and 1.00 (CoB) mJ m⁻² were treated as free parameters, with A = 8 pJ m⁻¹ assumed for both layers, and it is not said how D was chosen. With these values κ = πD/(4√(A·Keff)) is about 0.92 for CoFeB and about 0.59 for CoB, placing CoFeB near the domain-wall instability threshold, so the outcome that textures form only in CoFeB is largely set by this choice. The Discussion attributes the confinement to a larger D_eff/K_eff in CoFeB and says D_eff is "comparable" in both layers, but D was never measured. The text also states that "reducing the RKKY strength restores texture formation in both layers, whereas stronger coupling localizes the non-collinear state", but these simulations are not shown. The authors should present a state diagram of which sublattice hosts textures versus D_CoB, D_CoFeB (or the Keff asymmetry) and J_RKKY, show the quoted RKKY-variation simulations, state how the simulated field windows (−130, −80, −50 mT) change with J, and if possible measure D for each sublattice (for example by the method of ref. 48 on the reference multilayers, or by Brillouin light scattering).

4. The simulated stacking order is inverted relative to the sample, and the outer layers are not treated correctly (Fig. 4e vs Fig. 1a and Methods; Results text on the top layer). In the experiment each repeat is Ru/Pt/CoFeB/Ru/Pt/CoB from the bottom up, so the topmost magnetic layer is CoB and the bottommost is CoFeB; Fig. 4e has CoB at the bottom and CoFeB on top. The text's discussion of the "very top CoFeB layer … coupled to CoB on one side only, with vacuum above" therefore describes a configuration the sample does not have; in the real sample that boundary CoFeB layer is on the substrate side. The real topmost CoB layer has a CoFeB neighbour on one side only, so its RKKY pinning is about half that of interior CoB layers, and by the authors' own argument it is the CoB layer most likely to host textures. Because MFM is weighted toward near-surface layers, this bears on both the "solely in CoFeB" claim and the reading of the MFM contrast. The authors should repeat the simulations with the experimental stacking order and real spacer thicknesses (or justify why the order is irrelevant), correct the top-layer discussion, and address the outer layers in the XMCD sensitivity estimate of Concern 1.

5. A "general materials-design principle" (Discussion, Outlook) is claimed from a single stack composition with no experimental control. All results come from one composition and thickness set, with sister samples "deposited together"; reproducibility across growth runs or on a second piece is not shown, and no stack with altered asymmetry or coupling is measured. A symmetric CoFeB/CoFeB or CoB/CoB SAF with matched moments, a stack with a thicker Ru spacer (weaker RKKY), or a stack with the Keff or thickness asymmetry reversed (which should move the textures into CoB), measured with the same XMCD protocol, would show whether the selectivity follows the engineered asymmetry. Failing that, the claim should be narrowed to this stack, and at minimum the simulated state diagrams of Concern 3 should be shown.

6. The RKKY coupling strength is extracted with a formula that does not fit this stack (Methods "Sample fabrication"; Extended Data Table 1). J_RKKY = μ0·Hs·Ms·t_FM/2 with μ0Hs = 125 mT is the bilayer spin-flop/spin-flip expression. In a 20-layer stack interior layers couple on both sides, which changes the prefactor by about a factor of two. For perpendicular layers measured out of plane, saturation also proceeds through domain and skyrmion states (Fig. 1c, saturation near 150–170 mT; Fig. 2 up to about 130 mT), so Hs contains domain-wall, DMI, nucleation and dipolar/demagnetizing energies, not only RKKY. This J (−0.034, rounded to −0.03 mJ m⁻²) feeds the simulations and the effective-field mechanism. The authors should justify the formula for a multilayer or determine J independently, for example from an in-plane hard-axis loop, minor-loop shifts, the plateau-edge field, or a reference bilayer SAF with the same Ru/Pt spacer, and propagate its uncertainty.

7. The novelty relative to known AF/FM phase coexistence in perpendicular SAFs is not stated precisely (Introduction; Discussion "form of phase coexistence"). Field-driven FM bubbles and stripes in an AF background, AF regions in an FM background, and phase coexistence were reported in [Co/Pt]/Ru and related multilayers (Hellwig et al., refs 30–31; Hauet et al., ref. 32; Barker et al., ref. 33; Darwin et al., ref. 35). In any SAF a local switch between the FM and AF states reverses exactly one sublattice, so both "conventional" and "inverse" bubbles are expected along the descending branch. The authors should state explicitly what is new beyond these works, for example that the same chosen sublattice hosts both families, their Néel chirality and their size scale.

9. The return-point-memory analysis does not support the mechanistic conclusion drawn from it (Fig. 5c; "Return-point memory" section; Supplementary Note 5). Low cross-family RPM (0.03–0.05) shows that the two families use different sites, but not that one is "external-field-driven" and the other "RKKY-driven": defects that favour a down core in an up background need not favour an up core in a down background. Along a continuous descending sweep (path 2 → 4), inverse-polarity skyrmions are the last unreversed CoFeB regions and would naturally sit between former conventional sites, so low overlap may follow from geometry alone. No chance baseline is given; if chance-level RPM for the measured densities and sizes is well above 0.03, the families are anti-correlated, which is a different and interesting result. The authors should report the chance baseline (for example from rigidly shifted or rotated maps), state how opposite-contrast maps were overlaid, add a spatial cross-correlation (such as nearest-neighbour distances from inverse skyrmions to former conventional sites), state how many areas and samples were studied, and soften the mechanistic interpretation.

10. The simulations are compared with experiment only qualitatively, using parameters that depart from the measured ones (Fig. 4, Extended Data Table 2). CoB is modelled with the CoFeB thickness and Ms (0.81 instead of the measured 0.63 MA m⁻¹), with Ku rescaled to keep Keff; this removes the thickness and Ms asymmetry that the Discussion credits for the D_eff balance (D_eff = D/t) and changes CoB's self-demagnetization and wall energy, so the simulated selectivity tests essentially only the Keff asymmetry. Each state is relaxed from a random configuration at fixed field and T = 0, whereas the experiments are history-dependent room-temperature field sweeps (Fig. 5a). The simulated inverse-polarity skyrmion in Fig. 4c looks far smaller than the measured 58–81 nm, and no simulated m(H) loop or size-versus-field curve is compared with the data. The authors should simulate the real CoB Ms and thickness (or show they do not matter), simulate a field sweep, and compare simulated total and per-sublattice loops with Figs. 1c, 3b and 3d and simulated diameters with the measured ones versus field.

12. The 17° tilt in Lorentz DPC adds an in-plane field that is not discussed (Methods; Fig. 2). Quoted fields are the out-of-plane component, so an in-plane field of about 0.3·H_z (around 40 mT at −131 mT) is present. In an AF-coupled stack with a soft sublattice (Keff = 0.11 MJ m⁻³), an in-plane field can cause canting or spin-flop and can bias, distort or nucleate textures. The authors should quantify this, for example by comparing images at two tilts or simulating with the tilted field.

13. Possible influence of the MFM tip is not tested (Fig. 2, Fig. 5). The Co-coated, high-Q tip sits about 20 nm above a stack whose soft sublattice has low Keff and whose total moment nearly cancels. Tip-induced nucleation or displacement, especially near the maze-to-skyrmion transitions, could affect the imaged densities and the RPM, since the same region is rescanned after each cycle. The authors should show that repeated scans at fixed field, scan-direction comparisons, larger lift heights, or a cycle without scanning give the same skyrmion positions and densities.
