A. Must address

1. The graphene stacking sequence is assumed, not measured, yet the Conclusions say the crystal structure was "determined" and that the oscillation response is "governed by the stacking sequence". The Methods state that the powder data contain only (00l) reflections ("hkl (h = k = 0)") and the single-crystal data only (hk0) reflections ("hkl (l = 0)"). Neither data set constrains the lateral registry of graphene layers across a RuCl3 gallery or within a graphene block, so AA, AB (Bernal) and ABC (rhombohedral) sequences cannot be told apart. The stackings in Table 1 (A|AB|B for stage 2; AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB for stage 4) are instead "concluded" from four assumptions in the Methods by analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 GICs; the c-period multiplicity (two repeats for stage 2, three for stage 4) is consistent with several registries, and the two candidate stage-4 sequences in Table 1 already show that the data do not choose between them. Fig. 1c,d and the DFT then use different sequences again (ABAB|ABAB, ABCA|ABCA). This matters because the motivation (flat bands of ABC graphene, Fig. 1f), the DFT models (Figs. 3–5) and the interpretation of the SdH frequencies all depend on the stacking. The authors should (a) show the indexed XRD patterns (both stages) in the main text, with a structure-factor refinement of the (00l) intensities for the staged models, not just peak positions; (b) obtain off-axis (h0l)/(hhl)/(hkl, l ≠ 0) reflections, e.g. from a rotation or precession data set at SPring-8, or cross-sectional HRTEM/STEM along a zone axis that resolves graphene registry; (c) make Table 1, Fig. 1 and the DFT models refer to the same candidate stackings; and (d) if the registry cannot be measured, state plainly which stacking features are measured and which assumed, remove the implication that rhombohedral blocks are present, treat the DFT stackings as models, and recast the DFT section as a survey of candidate stackings rather than an identification.

B. Should address

2. The agreement between computed and measured SdH frequencies is overstated and depends on a free Fermi-level shift. At the computed charge transfer, A|A gives 511 T; AB|AB (the stage-2 model) gives 131 and 444 T against a single measured β ≈ 170 T; ABAB|ABAB gives 70/296 T and ABCA|ABCA 28/311/330 T against measured 91 and 176 T in stage 4. Agreement is reached only after a rigid ~0.1 eV shift (27/183 T for ABAB|ABAB, 126/214 T for ABCA|ABCA), which still leaves 20–40 T residuals, is shown only for the stage-4 models, and is supplemented by further shifts that push a predicted pocket below E_F to explain the missing α in stage 2. Predicted pockets that are not observed (e.g. 444 T in stage 2) are not explained by amplitude arguments. With two to three pockets per model, a free shift and a free choice of stacking, the comparison has nearly as many adjustable choices as data points, and the text's own remark that "only a limited range of physically reasonable parameters" reproduces the data undermines the claim that the response is "governed by the stacking sequence" (p. 5, "good agreement"). The authors should give the shifted result for AB|AB; plot the frequencies of every model as a continuous function of E_F shift (or transferred charge per C) in one figure with the measured stage-2 and stage-4 frequencies overlaid; state whether a single shared shift fits both stages and whether the shifts required for ABAB and ABCA differ, i.e. whether the data can discriminate between them; tie the shift's sign and size to an independent quantity (Hall density, Bader charge, interlayer distance) rather than to unmodelled "stacking faults"; and tone the conclusion down to "consistent with strong hole doping of the graphene layers" unless the stacking is pinned down.

3. The near-identical β frequency in stage 2 (170 T) and stage 4 (176 T, Fig. 2g,h), within FFT resolution, is not explained, although the DFT predicts quite different pocket sets for the two stages (131/444 T versus 70/296 T or 28/311/330 T). A natural alternative is that β comes from the graphene layer directly adjacent to RuCl3, whose doping is set by the interface and is largely stage-independent; Fig. 3b, where depletion in the four-layer models concentrates on the interface layers, supports this. Another is a common minority phase, e.g. stage-2 regions inside the stage-4 crystal. If either holds, the oscillation spectrum is not evidence for the assigned stage or stacking. The authors should discuss these scenarios explicitly, compare the interface-layer pocket area across all models, and test them with the effective mass of β in stage 2 versus stage 4 and with angle dependence (concern 4).

4. No temperature- or angle-dependent SdH analysis is presented, so the origin and dimensionality of the pockets are not established experimentally. Only single-temperature FFTs (1.8 K, 2.5 K) with the field along c are shown. The authors should add (a) Lifshitz–Kosevich fits of the temperature-dependent amplitudes for effective masses of α and β, compared with the calculated cyclotron/band masses of each candidate pocket (a flat-band ABC pocket and a Dirac-like AB pocket should differ strongly), which discriminates much more sharply than frequency alone; (b) a Dingle analysis, to judge whether the unobserved predicted pockets should be visible up to 9 T; (c) the angular dependence F(θ), to confirm quasi-2D sheets (F ∝ 1/cos θ) from doped graphene layers rather than a 3D pocket of residual graphite or a disordered phase; and (d) a statement of the kz dependence of the computed extremal areas (bands are computed on a 6×6×2 mesh, and stage-4 blocks are coupled along c). These measurements are also the only way to support the "three-dimensional" label in the title: only in-plane ρxx is measured, and with ~9 Å RuCl3 spacers the graphene blocks may be electronically decoupled, which is what the 2D-pocket analysis implicitly assumes. Angle-dependent SdH and, ideally, c-axis resistivity (ρc/ρab) would show whether the compound is a 3D electronic material or a stack of decoupled doped multilayers. All of this should be done on the same crystals characterised by XRD and STEM.

5. The level of theory used for the charge transfer is not justified. All band structures and Bader charges use non-spin-polarized GGA with no Hubbard U and no spin-orbit coupling. α-RuCl3 is a spin-orbit-assisted Mott insulator, and graphene-to-RuCl3 charge transfer depends on where the empty Ru t2g (upper Hubbard) states lie relative to the Dirac point, which depends on U, magnetic order and SOC; earlier graphene/α-RuCl3 work, including refs. 12 and 34 by some of the same authors, used GGA+U(+SOC) with zigzag order for this reason. In non-magnetic GGA bulk RuCl3 is metallic with t2g states at E_F, which contradicts the text's statement (p. 4) that RuCl3 remains "rather insulating" with Ru atoms "keeping their magnetic nature". The 0.68/0.48 eV graphene band shifts, and hence every computed frequency, rest on this choice, and the later 0.1 eV correction is of the same order as plausible U-induced shifts. The authors should repeat at least A|A or AB|AB and one four-layer stacking (e.g. ABCA|ABCA) with GGA+U over a range of U in a magnetic (zigzag or ferromagnetic) configuration, ideally with SOC; report how the Dirac-point shift, transferred charge and pocket areas change; and judge the 0.1 eV shift against this sensitivity.

6. The title, abstract and conclusions overstate what is shown. The title promises a "three-dimensional platform for exotic quantum phases", the abstract claims "comprehensive characterization" and "access to novel correlated and topological states", and the introduction describes RuCl3 as "magnetically active" with "possible spin-liquid behavior". No magnetization, susceptibility, specific heat, neutron or muon data are shown, and the calculations are non-magnetic. Whether the intercalated single RuCl3 layers keep local moments, order (bulk α-RuCl3 orders near 7 K) or are quenched by the ~0.5 eV hole transfer, which could substantially change the Ru valence, is the key question for the proposed use, and the rhombohedral flat-band link depends on the unconfirmed ABCA assignment (concern 1). The authors should either add at least M(T) and M(H) with H parallel and perpendicular to c on the stage-2 and stage-4 crystals (with low-temperature specific heat if possible), or retitle and rewrite the abstract and conclusions as a synthesis, structural and fermiology study of a new stage-2/stage-4 GIC with heavily hole-doped graphene layers, stating that magnetic and correlated properties remain untested. The Conclusions' closing caveat ("subject to further spectroscopic and magnetic validation") should be reflected in the abstract.

7. The carrier counts from Hall, SdH and DFT are not shown or cross-checked. The Hall density of ~7.8 × 10^20 cm^-3 (p. 3) is quoted without naming the sample or temperature, the carrier sign, the ρxy(B) data or its linearity; a single-band reading is questionable for a multicarrier GIC with residual graphite. A Luttinger count of a 170 T Dirac pocket (degeneracy 4) gives ~1.6 × 10^13 cm^-2 holes per graphene layer (E_F ≈ 0.47 eV); with two layers per 12.7 Å repeat in stage 2 this is ~2.6 × 10^20 cm^-3, a factor of about three below the Hall value. The authors should show ρxy(B) for both stages, and tabulate for each sample the density from Hall (with sign and fitting model), from the sum of SdH pocket areas, and from the DFT/Bader charge (Fig. S4) converted to a volume density, with Bader charges per graphene layer reported in the main text. Any discrepancy (multiband Hall, unresolved pockets, inner graphene layers, residual graphite) should be discussed. This gives an independent constraint on the charge transfer and on the Fermi-level shift in concern 2.

8. The SdH harmonic labels are inconsistent with the fundamentals. In Fig. 2g the peak labelled 2β is at 359 T, whereas 2 × 170 = 340 T; in Fig. 2h 2β = 347 T against an expected 352 T; in Fig. 2f the peak labelled 2ν1 is at 9.3 T rather than 8.26 T. With a 4–9 T window the FFT resolution is ~7 T, so the 19 T offset in stage 2 is not a resolution effect; for Kish graphite a 1.5–9 T window gives only ~1.8 T resolution, which makes ν1 = 4.13 T and ν2 = 5.16 T hard to resolve. A peak near 360 T could be an independent orbit, possibly one of the predicted larger pockets (296–444 T), which would change the comparison in concern 2. The authors should justify the harmonic labels (e.g. by temperature dependence, since harmonics decay with twice the mass) or reassign the peaks, and state for every FFT the field window, background-subtraction order, windowing function, frequency resolution, and error bars on every quoted frequency.

9. The chemical composition of the intercalant and possible side products are not characterised. No stoichiometry is given (e.g. C_xRuCl3 from mass uptake, EDS/WDS or EELS), and there is no check for co-intercalated Cl2/Cl, Ag or AgCl from the photolysis source, or for reduced Ru species. Since the Cl2-anneal control shows that chlorine alone changes ρ(T), chlorine-only intercalated regions could contribute to transport. The authors should provide elemental analysis (STEM-EDS/EELS maps across the layers giving the Ru:Cl ratio, absence of Ag, and the Ru oxidation state, e.g. by XPS) to show that the intercalant is stoichiometric RuCl3 rather than RuCl3-x or a mixed chloride.

10. The null control stops at resistivity. The Cl2-annealed Kish graphite control is shown only as an R(T) curve (Fig. 2b), but chlorine, AgCl/Ag residues or acceptor-type surface doping could themselves dope graphite and create high-frequency hole pockets or minority phases. The authors should show magnetoresistance and the SdH/FFT spectrum of the Cl2-annealed graphite, ideally also of graphite treated with AgCl alone, measured and analysed identically (temperature, field range, FFT window), to confirm that the 91/170 T orbits require RuCl3.

11. Phase purity, stage mixing and the STEM evidence are not quantified. The text mentions "a small portion of residual pure graphite" without phase fractions, gives no (00l) linewidths or Hendricks–Teller-type mixed-stage analysis, and notes that longer reactions give mixed staging while the SdH discussion invokes stacking faults. The STEM evidence is only in the SI, is described as "qualitative", and the text concedes that "a fully resolved layer-by-layer intercalation pattern is not observed throughout the sample". The SdH frequency alone does not prove "uniform intercalation", since quantum oscillations are dominated by the highest-mobility regions and are insensitive to minority phases with short scattering times. The authors should give stage and graphite fractions from the XRD (00l) intensities or linewidths, and bring representative cross-sectional HAADF images into the main text with line profiles of the measured repeat distance compared with the expected 12.7 Å (stage 2) and 19.4 Å (stage 4), statistics of stage domains and the fraction of unintercalated graphite.

13. The structural parameters of the DFT models are not derived from the data and their effect is not bounded. The 2×2 RuCl3/5×5 graphene cell imposes ~4% tensile strain on RuCl3, whereas the measured a = 29.6 Å implies RuCl3 slightly compressed (5 × 5.92 Å) on a 12×12 graphene cell; the benchmark against the unstrained 5×5/12×12 cell is shown only for A|A and only through the band shift. The graphene–RuCl3 distance is fixed at 3.60 Å "suggested by X-ray data", though the XRD gives only the c repeat; how it follows from c (e.g. 12.7 − 3.35 = 9.35 Å gallery split between RuCl3 thickness and two vdW gaps) is not shown. The graphene–graphene distance of 3.54 Å, well above graphite's 3.35 Å, is unjustified, and relaxations use the Γ point only. The stage-2 model is simplified from A|AB|B to AB|AB on the grounds that charge transfer is insensitive to registry, but the bands near K (interlayer coupling across the gallery) may not be. Since the text states that the Fermi surfaces are "highly sensitive to the interlayer distances", the authors should derive the distances from the measured c values (or relax them with D3), report the change in pocket areas (in tesla) between strained and unstrained cells for at least one multilayer stacking and for d(Gr–RuCl3) and d(Gr–Gr) varied by about ±0.1 Å, and say in light of these numbers whether the 20–40 T residual mismatch is significant.
