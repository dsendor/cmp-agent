A. Must address

1. The chemical identity, composition and phase of the intercalant are not established. The paper calls the guest α-RuCl3 throughout and says the samples are "fully characterized" ("comprehensive characterization" in the abstract). The only evidence, however, is the c-axis expansion and (hk0) indexing, interpreted by analogy with FeCl3, CrCl3 and AlCl3 GICs (Synthesis and Structure analysis sections, pp. 2-3 and 6-7; assumptions 1-4; Table I; Fig. S1). No composition or phase-identity measurement is shown: no EDX/EDS/WDS on cleaved surfaces or cross-sections, no mass uptake, weight gain, TGA or other chemical analysis giving the C:Ru:Cl ratio, no XPS of the Ru valence, and no Raman. This matters because growth takes place at 830-900 °C in excess Cl2 in an ampoule that also holds Ag/AgCl. Ru chlorides can sublime and react in several ways under these conditions, so a Cl-rich, reduced or Ag-containing layer, or co-intercalated Cl2, cannot be excluded. The layer between the graphene sheets need not be stoichiometric α-RuCl3, and it need not keep the honeycomb Ru network that underlies the Kitaev motivation. The authors should report the composition (EDS/WDS, or weight gain giving C/RuCl3 per stage) and the Ru oxidation state (XPS). They should add Raman spectra for both stages: the graphite G-band splitting into bounding and interior modes is the standard check of stage and charge transfer in acceptor GICs, and the RuCl3 phonon modes test the intercalant structure. They should also show that no Ag-containing phase is present.

2. The assignment of the SdH frequencies to DFT pockets and stackings rests on one adjustable Fermi-level shift and leaves several mismatches unexplained (Electronic properties section, p. 6; Figs. 2g,h and 5). Without the shift, DFT gives 131 and 444 T for AB|AB (stage 2) against one measured 170 T peak. It gives 70 and 296 T for ABAB|ABAB and 28, 311 and 330 T for ABCA|ABCA (stage 4) against 91 and 176 T. Agreement is then obtained with a rigid shift of about 0.1 eV (27/183 T and 126/214 T), only for the stage-4 models. Its sign, how it was chosen and what it implies for the carrier count are not stated, and its justification ("plausible variations in interlayer spacing") is not quantified. No shifted result is given for stage 2, so it stays unexplained why the 444 T pocket is absent and why 131 T does not match 170 T. With two or three predicted pockets per model and one free parameter, both stage-4 stackings can be brought near the data, so the comparison does not discriminate between models. The main frequency is also nearly the same in both stages (β ≈ 170 T and 176 T), even though DFT predicts quite different pocket sets for them. The simplest reading is a common, stage-independent origin: either the RuCl3-adjacent interface graphene layers, which the authors' own Fig. 3b shows carry most of the depletion in both stages, or S2-like domains or a minority phase present in both samples. The paper should address this directly. The absence of α (91 T) in stage 2 is called "sample-dependent", but only one sample per stage is shown. The experiment has more discriminating observables than frequency alone, and the authors should use them:
   - SdH amplitudes from 1.8 to about 20 K with Lifshitz-Kosevich fits, giving the cyclotron mass of each frequency for comparison with the DFT band mass of each pocket (m* = ħk_F/v_F for near-Dirac bands).
   - Dingle temperatures, to judge whether a 444 T pocket could have escaped detection below 9 T.
   - Field-angle dependence, to show whether each frequency scales as 1/cos θ, as expected for a quasi-2D graphene-derived pocket, rather than coming from a 3D pocket or a RuCl3-derived band.
   - SdH on more than one crystal per stage.
   - A Luttinger-count comparison. With fourfold spin and valley degeneracy, F = 170 T corresponds to about 1.6 × 10^13 cm^-2 holes per pocket (E_F ≈ 0.45-0.5 eV for a Dirac-like band). This should be compared with the DFT doping per layer and with published graphene/α-RuCl3 hole densities (refs. 10, 11, 34).

   The authors should then state which DFT pocket each measured frequency is assigned to, at which E_F, and which stacking, if any, the data favour, with the remaining mismatch.

3. The claim that the compound was synthesized "for the first time" (Conclusions; Introduction) conflicts with the authors' own reference list. Ref. 27 (Postnikov et al., J. Struct. Chem. 18, 819 (1977)) is titled "Synthesis and structure of layer-type graphite compounds with ruthenium, palladium, osmium chlorides", and ref. 30 covers intercalation of metal chlorides more broadly. The novelty claim is therefore likely wrong as stated. The authors should discuss what that and related earlier work reported (staging, c axis, composition, in-plane structure) and how the present compound differs. If earlier RuCl3-graphite compounds exist, the novelty should be restated around what is new here, for example single-crystal staging control, the coincidence-lattice structure, the quantum-oscillation characterization, or identification of the α-phase honeycomb layer.

B. Should address

4. The electronic-structure method makes the RuCl3 layer metallic, which affects both the size of the charge transfer and the premise that only graphene states produce the oscillations (Methods, p. 7; Figs. 3-5; Fig. S5b,c). All calculations are non-spin-polarized GGA without U and without spin-orbit coupling. At that level α-RuCl3 is metallic: Fig. S5b,c shows RuCl3-derived bands crossing E_F, with dispersive features near K. The main text nevertheless says α-RuCl3 remains "rather insulating" and that "only the graphene layers are expected to contribute" to the oscillations (p. 5). In this setup the transferred charge, which sets the Dirac-point shift and so every pocket area, is fixed by aligning the graphene Dirac point with a spurious metallic Ru t2g manifold. The position of those states is known to depend on U, SOC and magnetic order; earlier graphene/α-RuCl3 work, including ref. 12 by some of the same authors (and ref. 35), used GGA+U+SOC with magnetic order for this reason. Since the paper itself concludes that the frequencies are "highly sensitive" to the charge transfer, this choice directly affects the comparison in concern 2. The authors should repeat at least the A|A, AB|AB and one stage-4 model with GGA+U(+SOC) in a zigzag or ferromagnetic configuration, at XRD-consistent spacings (concern 7), or justify why non-magnetic GGA reproduces the charge transfer. They should show how the Dirac-point shift and pocket frequencies change with U, which would turn the 0.1 eV shift into a calculated quantity or show that it cannot be one. They should also state whether any RuCl3-derived Fermi-surface sheets remain that could account for the 91 T or 170 T orbits.

5. The graphene stacking and the in-plane structure are inferred by analogy rather than determined by the data (Table I; Fig. 1; Fig. S1; assumptions 1-4). Only 00l (powder) and hk0 (single crystal) reflections are observed. The 00l indices in Fig. S1 are all even for stage 2 (004, 006, 008, 0012, 0022) and all multiples of 3 for stage 4 (006, 0012, 0015, 0018, 0033, 0036, 0051). The data therefore establish identity periods of about 12.7 Å and 19.4 Å, which fit a roughly 9.35-9.4 Å intercalant sandwich plus one or three 3.35 Å graphite gaps. The doubled and tripled cells (25.4 Å, 58.2 Å) and the stacking sequences in Table I (A|AB|B; "AB|BCAB|BC or AB|BABA|AB") follow from assumption 4, not from measurement; for stage 4 the paper itself cannot tell Bernal from rhombohedral blocks. These stackings are then used to choose between Bernal and rhombohedral models in the DFT, and the paper's central theory result is that "the stacking sequence controls the number and size of Fermi hole pockets", so without measured stacking the SdH comparison cannot test that statement. The in-plane description is also internally inconsistent: assumption 1 says RuCl3 is "incommensurate with the graphite host", yet the hk0 reflections of both stages are indexed with one hexagonal cell, a = 29.62(3) Å and 29.5(4) Å, which implies a commensurate supercell close to 12×12 graphene / 5×5 RuCl3 (29.62/2.46 ≈ 12.0; 29.62/5.97 ≈ 4.96); N, M, n and m are never stated. Residual graphite is visible in Fig. S1, but the graphite and mixed-stage fractions are not quantified, and it is not stated whether the transport crystals were themselves characterized by XRD. The authors should state the in-plane supercell and reconcile it with assumption 1; quantify phase fractions by fitting the 00l series; check the staging of the transport specimens; and either obtain l ≠ 0 reflections (rotation, rocking or tilted single-crystal geometry) to fix the stacking, or present the stacking as a hypothesis and remove claims that depend on rhombohedral stacking.

6. The Hall data were measured but are not shown. The Methods say transverse resistivities were antisymmetrized (p. 7), so ρxy(B) exists for both stages. It is the most direct test of the charge-transfer picture: it gives the carrier sign and density (and, through any field nonlinearity, evidence for several pockets), which can be checked against the Luttinger count of the observed SdH pockets (about 1.6 × 10^13 cm^-2 per layer for a single fourfold-degenerate 170 T pocket) and against the DFT charge transfer (Fig. S4b, about 0.09-0.10 e per RuCl3). The authors should show ρxy(B) for both stages with this comparison, and could use the Hall density to fix the Fermi-level shift of concern 2 independently.

7. The DFT geometries are not tied to the measured c axes, and the stated distances conflict between the main text and the Supplement. The main text fixes d_Gr-RuCl3 = 3.60 Å "suggested by X-ray data" and d_Gr-Gr = 3.54 Å (Methods, p. 7), but the XRD gives only the identity periods (about 12.7 Å and 19.4 Å), and 3.54 Å is larger than graphite's 3.35 Å. The SI uses d_Gr-RuCl3 = 3.765 Å and a graphene-graphene distance of 10.241 Å across the RuCl3 layer, and it is not stated whether d_Gr-RuCl3 is measured from the Ru or the Cl plane. Fig. S4a marks the reference A|A cell at c ≈ 10.0 Å, while the measured sandwich thickness is about 9.35 Å (12.7 − 3.35 ≈ 19.4 − 3 × 3.35). Over c = 9.3-10.5 Å the computed charge transfer changes by about 10%, and the SI says the charge transfer "is strongly affected" by this distance while the main text calls it insensitive. Fig. S4a also shows hole doping increasing as c shrinks, so using the experimental spacing would push the computed frequencies further above experiment, against the direction needed for agreement. The authors should tabulate the c-axis repeat of each model against 25.4/2 Å and 58.2/3 Å, use one consistent and clearly defined set of distances (shown in a figure), recompute charge transfer and frequencies at the experimental sandwich thickness, and give the sensitivity of the pocket frequencies to d_Gr-RuCl3 directly, since it is the stated reason for the Fermi-level shift.

8. The controls do not rule out doping by chlorine or chlorine/AgCl-derived species and are not matched to the intercalated samples (Synthesis, p. 2; Transport, p. 3; Fig. 2b,c). The Cl2-annealed Kish graphite is shown only in zero-field ρ(T); its magnetoresistance and SdH spectrum, which are the relevant null control for the claim that the high-frequency pocket comes from RuCl3 intercalation, are not shown. The control also differs from the samples: the intercalation host is natural graphite flakes from HQ Graphene, whereas the reference and control are Kish graphite, so differences in mosaicity, defect density and RRR enter the comparison; and the control was annealed at 900 °C "for several days", whereas growths lasted 22-48 h. The authors should show XRD (c axis), SdH and Hall at 1.8 K and 9 T for the actual host flakes, both pristine and Cl2-annealed under the growth time and temperature profile, and ideally a sample grown with AgCl but no RuCl3 under the same two-zone conditions.

9. Sample statistics and reproducibility are not reported (Fig. 2b-e). Fig. 2 shows one sample per stage, and stage 4 is missing from Fig. 2b. The zero-field resistivity at 1.8 K differs by about 15× between stage 2 (about 10 µΩ cm, Fig. 2d) and stage 4 (about 150 µΩ cm, Fig. 2e), and the magnetoresistance shape differs (near-linear versus quadratic); this is not discussed beyond geometric "uncertainties", and the text acknowledges that cutting changes the RRR. The time-to-stage mapping (22 h → stage 4, 48 h → stage 2) is given without saying how often it reproduces. Metal-chloride GICs can deintercalate. The authors should state how many growths and samples per stage were measured, whether the 91 T and 170-176 T frequencies recur across samples and batches (with their spread), the air and thermal-cycling stability of the compounds, and ρ(T) for stage 4.

10. The FFT analysis is under-specified and the harmonic labels do not match the fundamentals (Fig. 2f-h; p. 3). In Fig. 2g the peak labelled 2β is at 359 T, while 2 × 170 T = 340 T; in Fig. 2h 2β = 347 T, while 2 × 176 T = 352 T. For a 4-9 T window the FFT resolution is about 1/(1/4 − 1/9) ≈ 7 T, so the 19 T offset in stage 2 is well beyond resolution and the 359 T peak may be a separate pocket (for example the second DFT pocket), which matters for concern 2. The same resolution is comparable to the stage-2/stage-4 difference in β (170 vs 176 T). In graphite, the "2ν1 = 9.3 T" peak coincides with ν1 + ν2 = 9.29 T rather than 2ν1 = 8.26 T, and ν1 = 4.13 T and ν2 = 5.16 T are about 1 T apart, comparable to the roughly 1.8 T resolution of a 1.5-9 T window. α (91 T) gives only about 12 periods in the window. The authors should show the background-subtracted Δρ versus 1/B, state the field windows, polynomial order, windowing and padding, give frequency uncertainties and the resolution, show that α and β are robust to these choices, and revisit the harmonic labels.

11. The title, abstract and conclusions present the material as a platform for "exotic quantum phases", invoking Kitaev, spin-liquid, correlated and flat-band physics, but none of these is measured. No susceptibility, magnetization, specific heat or transport anomaly near the roughly 7 K ordering temperature of bulk α-RuCl3 is shown, although the SdH data at 1.8 K are below it. The paper does not establish whether the intercalated layers keep local moments or order magnetically after receiving about 0.1 e/f.u. (Fig. S4b), and no flat-band or correlated behaviour of the graphene blocks is probed; the rhombohedral stacking is also not established (concern 5). The authors should add χ(T) (H ∥ ab and H ∥ c) and/or C(T) for both stages, compared with pristine α-RuCl3, together with low-field ρ(T) over about 2-15 K, or narrow the title, abstract and conclusions to the demonstrated result: a new staged intercalation compound with strongly hole-doped graphene layers.

12. The charge-transfer and strain numbers are not mutually consistent. Fig. S4a gives about −0.0143 to −0.0144 e per C for A|A in the strained 2×2 RuCl3 / 5×5 graphene cell, about 0.72 e per 50 C, i.e. about 0.18 e per RuCl3 formula unit; Fig. S4b gives 0.089 e/f.u. on RuCl3 for the same geometry. Unless the normalizations differ, charge neutrality is not satisfied. The strain is quoted as about 4% tensile on RuCl3 with graphene fixed at 1.42 Å in the main text (p. 4), about 1% with a stretched C-C distance of 1.434 Å in the SI, while the arithmetic for the 2×2/5×5 cell gives about 3%. The authors should reconcile these, state the normalization of each figure and which layer is strained in each model and by how much, and compare the Bader charge with the hole density implied by the computed pocket areas (Luttinger count).

13. The computed quantum-oscillation frequencies have no error bars (Methods; Fig. 5). Fermi contours are drawn by joining the "highest intensity points" of broadened unfolded spectra, and the resulting "small systematic error" is not quantified. The pockets in Fig. 5c-e overlap within the broadening, and for the small pockets (28 T, 70 T) and the closely spaced 311/330 T pair this error could change the pocket assignment. Only A|A is cross-checked with the dhva code. The authors should quantify the uncertainty of each computed frequency (by varying broadening, k-mesh and contour choice), apply the dhva/tricubic method to all structures, especially stage 2, state at which k_z the extremal areas are taken, and report any k_z dispersion.
