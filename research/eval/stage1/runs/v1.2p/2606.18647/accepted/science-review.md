A. Must address

1. The sign of the hole-layer Hall resistance does not, on the authors' own model, single out trions from a mixture of excitons and free electrons. The main text says the negative Rxy in the (X + X−) region "unambiguously demonstrates the presence of Coulomb drag and the trion Hall effect". But equation (7) gives Rxy ≈ −(B/e)[(1+f)/n_e + f²(1 − σX0/σe0)/n_T]. With no mobile trions (σT0 → 0, so f = 0) this reduces to Rxy ≈ −B/(n_e e), still negative, and Rxy,drag ≈ −B/(n_e e) is unchanged. Likewise in equation (8), f = 0 still gives Rxy,drag ≈ B/(n_h e) in the (X + X+) region. Physically, perfectly dragged neutral excitons carry the hole-layer current, the free electrons in the open-circuited electron layer must counterflow, and their Hall field is passed to the hole layer through the excitons; this alone makes the hole layer's Hall response electron-like. Trions enter only through f and the f²/n_T term. The trion assignment therefore currently rests on the coincidence of the sign-reversed region with the boundaries from Rxx and the RC valley argument, not on the Hall effect itself, while the title claim is that trions, as charged composites, show a Hall effect. The authors should show explicitly what Rxy and Rxy,drag the exciton-plus-free-electron scenario (σT = 0, σX0 ≠ 0) predicts, and then identify and test an observable that separates it from the trion scenario. Concretely: (a) fit Rxy(Vg) and Rxy,drag(Vg) at 1.5 K along the Vb = 0.88 V cut in both regions with equations (7) and (8), using capacitance-derived n and p and the constraint that free, exciton-bound and trion-bound carriers add up to the total densities, with a minimal stated set of free parameters, and report the extracted f and n_T, showing where the trion term is needed and the f = 0 limit fails; (b) in the (X + X+) region, plot the reference curves p − n (holes bound only in excitons) and p − 2n (maximal trion formation) on Fig. 4f alongside n_H(1.5 K), since equation (8) makes n_H the free-hole density and the deficit below p − n directly counts holes bound in trions (n_H(1.5 K) appears to fall roughly linearly to zero at Vg = 0, which needs to be shown inconsistent with p − n); (c) check that Rxy,drag ≈ −B/(n_e e) in the (X + X−) region matches the expected free-electron density, and examine further discriminating features such as the magnitude of n_H relative to n − 2p, the behaviour approaching the n = 2p boundary (where n_e → 0) and the relation between Rxy and Rxy,drag. A reference measurement in a device or regime where excitons form but trions do not (for example a thicker barrier) at matched densities would also help. If the alternatives cannot be excluded, the text should replace "unambiguously demonstrates" with a statement that the Hall response is consistent with mobile trions plus Coulomb drag.

2. The Hall signals are measured on a strongly insulating layer, and the paper does not show that they are antisymmetric in field or free of longitudinal admixture. In the trion regions Rxx and Rxx,drag on the W layer are 10^4–10^7 Ω (Figs. 2a,b, 3a; Extended Data Fig. 4), while the Hall resistances of interest are a few to about 10 kΩ (Fig. 4a–e). A small Hall-probe misalignment, density inhomogeneity between the probes, or a contact-dependent offset would mix in a longitudinal component of the same size, which can take either sign, changes with the gradient of Rxx, and depends strongly on temperature and gate voltage because Rxx is insulating-like here (Fig. 2b,c). The sign reversal of Rxy below about 4 K (Fig. 4e) and the blue fringes and sign changes near the lower boundary and the grey regions in Fig. 4a,b and Extended Data Fig. 4 could arise this way. All Hall data are shown only at positive B (2, 3, 4, 6, 8 T, and 0–12 T in Extended Data Fig. 4e), and the Methods (Electrical measurements) do not say whether Rxy and Rxy,drag were antisymmetrized under B → −B. Because the headline result is a sign, the authors should show raw Rxy(±B) and Rxy,drag(±B) and the antisymmetrized values at representative points in both trion regions, at 1.5 K and 10 K and for the Fig. 4c–e data; state the antisymmetrization procedure and whether the maps are antisymmetrized; show the symmetric component, to demonstrate it is small or removed; and report the out-of-phase lock-in component in the high-impedance regions, where the 100 MΩ preamplifier input and line capacitance at 7.33 Hz may matter. An Onsager reciprocity check of the drag Hall signal (drive the W layer, measure the Hall voltage on the Mo layer, compare with Rxy,drag at reversed field) would further show that the drag Hall signals are intrinsic.

B. Should address

3. The conductivity tensors in equations (2) and (3) need a derivation, because as written they appear inconsistent with the net charge of the trion and with Onsager reciprocity. In Σ− the trion enters as J_e = 2σT(E_e − E_h) and J_h = −σT(E_e − E_h), so the trion contribution depends only on E_e − E_h and vanishes for E_e = E_h, as for a neutral exciton. A negative trion (two electrons, one hole) in uniform equal fields feels a net force −eE and should move. Treating the trion as a rigid particle with mobility μ gives contributions proportional to [[4, −2], [−2, 1]], which is symmetric as Onsager reciprocity requires at B = 0; the written [[2, −2], [−1, 1]] is not symmetric. The same applies to Σ+. These matrices produce equations (7) and (8), the n_T terms in Rxy and Rxy,drag, and the statement that excitons only add "corrections" without changing the picture. The authors should derive the tensor from a two-layer force-balance picture, or explain the convention for σT that makes the stated form correct, then check whether the sign and magnitude predictions in the (X + X−) region still hold and whether trions and excitons can be distinguished (concern 1).

4. The assumptions of perfect Coulomb drag for excitons and trions and of no frictional drag for free carriers are unsupported and appear to conflict with the measured drag ratio. Methods (after equation 2) assumes perfect drag for bound states, negligible frictional drag of free carriers, and neglects free electrons in the (X + X+) region and free holes in the (X + X−) region. Extended Data Fig. 3b shows a drag ratio near 1 only in the X region and about 0.2–0.4 inside the trion regions. Part of the deficit may come from free carriers carrying current, but the paper does not show that the measured drag ratio agrees with the conductivity fractions its model implies. Frictional (unbound) Coulomb drag between free electrons and the hole layer can be strong at d ≈ 2 nm and low density, is known to produce Hall drag with its own temperature dependence, and is set to zero by assumption; near the trion dissociation boundary it is not bounded. The authors should compare the measured drag ratio in both trion regions with the model prediction for reasonable σ values, and assess how relaxing the perfect-drag and no-friction assumptions changes the sign and magnitude of Rxy and Rxy,drag, showing the frictional-drag scenario's predictions alongside the trion model.

6. Interlayer leakage and a.c. coupling in the drag and "open-circuit" configurations are not quantified. The layers are biased by Vb ≈ 0.76–0.9 V across a 1.5–2 nm hBN barrier, close to the gap-closing bias, where tunnelling can be significant (the paper cites enhanced tunnelling, ref. 28). A tunnelling current would appear directly as a spurious drag signal and could alter the Hall voltage on the open-circuited layer. In the standard Hall configuration (Extended Data Fig. 2a) the Mo layer is tied to the Vb source at Mo-10, so any a.c. leakage from the W layer returns through that source and the Mo layer is not strictly open-circuit, as equations (4) and (7) assume. The potentiometer and transformer scheme of Extended Data Fig. 2b reduces a.c. coupling, but the residual capacitive or tunnelling pickup is not bounded. The authors should report the d.c. and a.c. interlayer leakage current as a function of Vb and Vg over the trion regions; the residual a.c. interlayer signal at a point where the layers should be decoupled (high T, or outside the bound-state regions); the out-of-phase drag components; and the dependence of Rxy,drag on excitation amplitude and frequency, showing the drag signal is linear, in phase, and not set by tunnelling. Linearity also bears on heating of the highly resistive trion regions.

7. The small-field, semiclassical model is applied at 4 T, where Landau quantization is clearly important. Equations (6)–(8) linearize in ω_c τ ("under a small perpendicular magnetic field B") and the text uses Rxy ≈ B/(n_h e) "for small B fields", yet all Hall data and the n_H analysis of Fig. 4f are at 4 T. There, by the paper's own account, the free-hole cyclotron energy is comparable to the trion binding energy and larger than k_BT at 1.5 K, and quantum oscillations are strong in the same regions (Fig. 2a; Extended Data Figs. 3b, 4, 7). The authors should give the hole and electron mobilities, or ω_c τ for each species, at the measurement field, and discuss whether Rxy ≈ B/(n_h e) and equations (7) and (8) can be used quantitatively. They should show Rxy and Rxy,drag versus B at fixed (Vg, Vb) in both trion regions, with both signs of B and down to fields well below 1 T, to test linearity and locate where the sign reverses; Rxy,drag versus B is missing from Extended Data Fig. 4e. Extended Data Fig. 4e shows negative Rxy only between about 2 and 6 T, with the trion region opening only above about 2 T; the authors should discuss whether the low-field onset reflects the trion region opening up rather than a Hall response linear in B. Data at 2–3 T, where the trion regions already exist (Extended Data Fig. 4a,b), would strengthen the analysis.

8. The temperature scales and the trion binding-energy estimates are not consistent and lack uncertainties. The main text equates the about 8 K crossover (Fig. 4c, device 3, 4 T) with the binding energy ("about 8 K (or 0.7 meV)"), while the Methods estimate about 1.2 meV (about 14 K) for positive trions from the RC injection threshold (device 2, 6 T); since the paper says the binding energy depends on B and density, calling this "good agreement" is not a like-for-like comparison. In a Saha-type dissociation equilibrium, ionization occurs at k_BT well below E_b because of the dissociation entropy, so equating the crossover temperature with E_b underestimates the binding energy. Within the (X + X−) region, Rxy,drag departs from zero below about 6–8 K while Rxy turns negative only below about 4 K (Fig. 4e); the paper notes this without explaining it. The model omits free holes in the (X + X−) region even though Rxy there is positive at high temperature. The optical estimate depends on the 25 mV threshold reading, on d (quoted as "≈2 nm" in Methods and 1.5–2 nm in the main text, changing C_2L and E_T,b by about 30%), on m_h = 0.45 m0 and g_s g_v = 2 (near the injection threshold into the minority valley at 6 T the relevant compressibility may be that of a single valley), and on comparing 1.5 K with 10 K data. The authors should model thermal dissociation, for example n_T(T) from a mass-action law with thermally activated free carriers fed into equations (7) and (8), and use it to explain the different onset temperatures of Rxy and Rxy,drag and to reconcile the transport and optical estimates; give error bars on both estimates; compare at matched B in the same device where possible; and soften the agreement claim.

12. The analysis of the resistance oscillations in the (X + X+) region, and the conclusion that they are not trion Landau levels, rests on a fragile fit. The 0.65 m_e mass comes from a Lifshitz–Kosevich fit over only about 1.5–3 K (Extended Data Fig. 7d). The amplitude is normalized to Rxx at 3.3 K, which forces it to zero at the top of the fit range by construction, steepens the apparent decay and biases the extracted mass; and the background in this insulating region is strongly temperature-dependent through trion dissociation (Fig. 2c), so the Lifshitz–Kosevich form need not apply. The "expected trion mass (≈1.4–1.6 m_e)" has no stated source or derivation. The alternative, "recurring transitions between quantum Hall states and the trion phase", is plausible but untested. The authors should extract the amplitude after subtracting a smooth background at each temperature, fit over a range not pinned to the reference temperature, give an error bar on the mass and a source for the expected trion mass, and test the proposed scenario, for example by showing that the oscillation period in density and the slope of the stripes in the Vb–Vg plane track the Landau-level degeneracy and filling of free holes and their B dependence; otherwise present the interpretation more tentatively.
