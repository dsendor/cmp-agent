**A. Must address**

1. **A uniform phase histogram at a fixed delay does not show that the initial condensate phase is random; shot-to-shot frequency and onset-time jitter has not been excluded.** The paper's evidence for spontaneity is the set of polar histograms in the Fig. 2 insets (t = 0.10, 0.90 and 1.25 µs, 1000 shots, 23 dBm), from which Sec. 1.2 (pp. 5–6) and the Discussion conclude that "the uniform phase distribution must be an intrinsic property of the BEC" and that the initial phase "must be independent of the external pumping". But the phase measured at a fixed laboratory time is φ(t) = φ₀ + 2π∫[f(t′) − f_LO] dt′ from the moment of formation. The condensate frequency chirps from about 3.42 GHz to 3.33 GHz (an 80–100 MHz shift between about 0.8 and 1.3 µs) after pump-off (Fig. 3a, Ext. Data Fig. 6a,c), which the text attributes to the density-dependent nonlinear shift. The amplitude, and hence the density, varies shot to shot by a factor of 2–3 (compare the three traces in Fig. 2a at 1.25 µs). A spread of only about 1 MHz to a few MHz in the frequency trajectory (roughly 1–3% density fluctuation given the ~90 MHz total shift), accumulated over the 0.3–1 µs between formation and the histogram time, gives several radians of phase spread and fills the circle even if φ₀ were rigidly set by the pump. Jitter in the condensation onset time, which itself depends on pump power and the initial thermal population, has the same effect. The 0.10 µs histogram contains only noise and is trivially uniform, so it is not a control. A uniform histogram is therefore expected under both the spontaneous and the pump-locked hypotheses and does not discriminate between them. The paper also does not discuss what pump-phase memory could survive to the k ≈ 0 mode: the pump fixes only the sum of the phases of each ±k magnon pair, with a two-fold (φ_p/2, φ_p/2 + π) degeneracy, and the transfer through four-magnon scattering is a theoretical question. The authors should: (a) extract the phase where it is set, for example at the first time each shot's amplitude crosses a fixed threshold, or by fitting each shot's phase trajectory in the free-evolution window (frequency and chirp free) and back-extrapolating to the onset of coherence, and show the histogram of these phases against the fit uncertainty; (b) show the shot-to-shot spread of the instantaneous frequency, onset time and accumulated phase ∫Δf dt, and compare the phase spread it implies with the observed one; (c) condition the histogram on amplitude, or show phase against amplitude and frequency, to test for hidden correlations with the pump; (d) most importantly, measure a deterministic control under the same chirp conditions through the identical chain and analysis, such as a pulsed direct (linear or weakly nonlinear) FMR excitation near 3.3–3.4 GHz strong enough to give a comparable nonlinear shift, a weak coherent seed at the band bottom during parametric pumping, or the parametric magnons at f_p/2 = 3.9 GHz (with the LO retuned, since the mixer bandwidth is 500 MHz), whose phase should be locked to the pump modulo π and give a bimodal histogram; and (e) give a short theoretical estimate, or a minimal noise-seeded growth, kinetic or stochastic Gross–Pitaevskii model, predicting the histogram under the pump-inherited and spontaneous scenarios. Without one of these, the central claim of a spontaneous, pump-independent phase does not follow.

2. **The phase stability of the detection chain is asserted but not shown, and the main text contradicts the Extended Data on phase locking.** Sec. 1.2 (pp. 5–6) states that "all generation and detection equipment in the experimental setup is phase-locked" and that "all externally induced factors are stable within a few degrees of phase shift"; this is the quantitative basis for attributing the uniform histogram to the condensate. Yet the Ext. Data Fig. 5 caption says "The oscilloscope was excluded due to technical difficulties, but phase stability of the setup as a whole was confirmed in preliminary measurements", and those measurements are not shown. With the oscilloscope off the shared 10 MHz reference, the phase at a fixed time depends on trigger and time-base jitter relative to the 100–130 MHz IF: 1 ns of jitter is about 0.7 rad at 110 MHz. Further risks are the start phase of the pulsed 7.8 GHz SG2 relative to the CW 3.2 GHz LO, the relation between the pump-pulse onset, the DG645 trigger and the LO, and drift over the 1000-shot acquisition. The inference "uniform histogram → phase not set externally" holds only if a signal whose phase is set by the drive gives a narrow histogram under identical acquisition (same trigger chain, oscilloscope, analysis and delay). The authors should show the single-shot phase histogram and its circular width for at least one deterministic reference recorded this way: a weak pulsed ~3.3 GHz tone from a synthesizer locked to the same 10 MHz and triggered by the same pulse generator, sent through the antenna or a bypass and the diplexer, amplifier and mixer chain; the constant 3.77 GHz band in Ext. Data Fig. 6c within the same shots, if it derives from a locked source; or the linear FMR response at 3.3 GHz driven below the parametric threshold. They should report its circular standard deviation next to the condensate histogram, resolve the contradiction between the main text and the Ext. Data Fig. 5 caption, and state the trigger jitter and the pump–LO phase relation at pulse onset. Without this control, a uniform histogram cannot be distinguished from a timing or synchronization artifact.

**B. Should address**

3. **Random phase and narrow linewidth are necessary for a BEC but not sufficient, and the paper gives no evidence of thermalization in this geometry, yet presents the result as proof of a "true BEC".** The abstract says the findings "confirm all postulated fundamental properties of quasiparticle condensates"; the Fig. 2 caption calls the result "a strong indication … of true BEC"; the Discussion calls the independence of the phase from external sources a "necessary feature to prove the formation of a true BEC". But any instability that grows from thermal or quantum noise picks a random phase in each realization: a laser or parametric oscillator above threshold, classical-wave (Rayleigh–Jeans) condensation, a kinetic instability transferring magnons directly to the bottom of the spectrum, four-magnon parametric processes into the k ≈ 0 mode, Suhl-type instabilities, or any self-oscillation. What separates BEC from these is evidence that the magnon gas thermalizes to a quasi-equilibrium distribution with the chemical potential reaching the band bottom. The paper shows only the 500 MHz IQ band and a spectrum-analyser spectrogram over 3.2–3.9 GHz, said only to "demonstrate the agreement with previous works [41]"; it gives no frequency-resolved occupation, no comparison with a Bose–Einstein distribution, and no critical density compared with Eq. (1) for this film's dispersion. Refs. [30, 31] show that BEC in perpendicularly magnetized YIG is disputed, and the out-of-plane geometry differs from most previous magnon-BEC work, but the manuscript does not engage with the comment [31]. The authors should either supply evidence of thermalized condensation in this sample (time-resolved spectra of the gas near the band bottom across threshold, the occupation compared with a thermal distribution, an estimate of the critical density against the injected density), or rely explicitly on, and quantitatively match, the conditions of Ref. [41]; discuss [31] directly; and otherwise reframe the result as spontaneous phase selection of the k ≈ 0 coherent state, discussing which observables would differ between BEC and kinetic-instability or laser-like scenarios, and temper "confirm all postulated fundamental properties", "proof of randomness" and "true BEC".

4. **The phase statistics are only qualitative, and the informative comparisons are missing.** Uniformity rests on three polar histograms "with no apparent clustering" (Fig. 2 insets), with no statistic. For 1000 shots the authors should report a quantitative test (Rayleigh or Kuiper test p-value, mean resultant length R = |⟨e^{iφ}⟩| against its ≈1/√N ≈ 0.03 floor for a uniform distribution) as a continuous function of time from the very first signal above noise (about 0.55–0.7 µs at 23 and 25 dBm), across pumping, condensation and free evolution. This is most needed during pumping (0–1 µs), where pump-induced locking is most likely and where three snapshots could miss brief locking near pump-off. They should also show histograms at matched times for a below-threshold power (20 dBm) and for the different powers of Fig. 4, alongside the deterministic reference of concern 2.

5. **The "coherence value" is not calibrated, depends on SNR, chirp and its 100 ns window, and the text contradicts the figures; the claims of "no dephasing" and "remanence of coherence" therefore do not follow.** Sec. 1.3 and the Fig. 3 caption state that "zero denotes perfect white noise", but the baseline in Figs. 3b, 4a and Ext. Data Fig. 6d is about 0.17–0.2, and the maximum is about 0.75–0.8, never 1. The metric (integral of |autocorrelation| of the normalized complex signal in a 100 ns window, Secs. 1.3, 3.2) is lowered by additive noise, by chirp within the window (invoked for the dip near 0.9–1.0 µs) and by phase diffusion, and cannot tell these apart; the 100 ns window resolves only about 10 MHz, so any narrower spectral feature, including a narrowband incoherent population near the band bottom, scores close to perfect coherence; and a constant tone or DC offset in I/Q raises it without any magnon coherence. The text attributes the late-time decline to falling SNR (p. 7), yet the Fig. 4 caption says coherence "does not depend on the absolute amplitude" and that the BEC "doesn't lose its coherent properties during decay", and p. 7–8 says the BEC "does not undergo dephasing". The "clear remanence" at 3.5 µs, when the amplitude is below noise, rests on about 0.22 versus a 0.17–0.18 baseline, and averaging 1000 amplitude traces would also detect the signal there, so the comparison should be at equal effective averaging. It is not stated how the signal is normalized (per point, per window, or globally), whether ω₀ in f(t) = e^{iω₀t} is measured or fixed, the integration range in Δt, the treatment of the boxcar envelope, how the standard-deviation bands are computed, or whether DC offsets, LO leakage, the 3.2–3.3 GHz "shadow" and the 3.77 GHz band are removed first. The authors should compute the metric for synthetic signals (a pure tone and a tone with the measured chirp, or a locked tone attenuated to the measured amplitudes) plus measured noise as a function of SNR and linewidth, place the data on that calibration curve, explain the finite-window noise floor, quantify the 3.5 µs remanence with a significance estimate and show it is not caused by the 3.77 GHz spur or mixer offsets, give the full definition and filtering, and correct the statements that are not supported.

6. **Within-shot dephasing and phase diffusion are not measured, so "no dephasing" is not established.** The claim that "the BEC does not undergo dephasing" (pp. 7–8) rests on a 100 ns sliding-window metric that cannot test phase stability over microseconds. The single-shot data allow a direct test: after removing each shot's deterministic frequency evolution (a fitted f(t) with chirp), compute the phase-residual variance ⟨[φ(t+τ) − φ(t)]²⟩ against τ up to the 1–2 µs condensate lifetime, or the single-shot linewidth against time, or an SNR-corrected first-order coherence g¹(τ) from the complex traces, compared with the Fourier and decay limits. This would turn a qualitative statement into a measured coherence time and also bears on concern 1.

9. **The novelty claim does not account for prior work on spontaneous and phase-referenced coherence.** The abstract ("Until now, the BEC phase was only revealed in phenomena depending on the spatial phase difference"), p. 2 and the Discussion ("For the first time…", "no direct proof of randomness … has yet been provided") overstate novelty. Demidov et al. (Ref. [35], PRL 100, 047205, "Observation of spontaneous coherence in BEC of magnons") reported spontaneous coherence via microwave emission but is cited only for in-plane geometry; Snoke & Girvin [24] and Rezende [10] address the onset of phase coherence; in superfluid ³He-B the homogeneously precessing domain and magnon-BEC "time crystal" states are detected inductively with phase relative to a reference ([19]); phase-resolved studies of photon and polariton condensates have addressed spontaneous symmetry breaking and phase randomness over repetitions; and the authors' own Ref. [41] treats the same out-of-plane geometry "toward a coherent BEC". The authors should state precisely what is new (single-shot absolute phase against an external reference in a solid-state magnon system, and the shot-to-shot phase distribution), explain how this goes beyond linewidth and spectral-narrowing evidence, and revise "first", "proof" and "confirm all postulated fundamental properties" accordingly.
