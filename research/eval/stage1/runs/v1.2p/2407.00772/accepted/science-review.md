A. Must address

1. The susceptibility model of Eq. (1) is not consistent with the high-fluence data, the 190 K fluence series, or the ~70 fs floor, so the claim of a diverging excitonic susceptibility at Tc is not supported by the model as stated (Eq. (1); Fig. 4d,e; Supplementary Note 9). Eq. (1) writes n_tot ∝ F[χ0 + χex(T)], and the paper uses τ_int = τ0 + A/√n_tot with τ0 = 25 fs (Fig. 4d). Because n_tot is linear in F, the temperature dependence enters only as a multiplicative factor, so the model makes three predictions the data contradict. (i) High fluence: (τ_int − τ0) at 2.0 mJ cm⁻² should follow the low-fluence temperature dependence scaled by √(0.2/2.0) ≈ 0.32. The low-fluence variable part falls from ~165 fs (190 fs at 23 K) to ~45 fs (~70 fs at 190 K), implying n_tot rises ~13-fold at Tc; applied to the high-fluence data (~75 fs at 23 K), this predicts τ_int falling to ~40 fs at Tc. The measured red points in Fig. 4e are flat at ~70–80 fs at all temperatures. (ii) 190 K: the model predicts a 1/√F dependence with a smaller prefactor, but the measured τ_int is roughly fluence-independent at ~70–85 fs from 0.2 to 2.3 mJ cm⁻² (Fig. 4c,d). (iii) Offset: τ0 is attributed to the time to create mobile holes without excitonic interactions, yet at Tc, where excitons should be easiest to break, τ_int does not approach 25 fs; it sits at the same ~70 fs reached by the high-fluence data at every temperature, and no point in Fig. 4d at any temperature or fluence falls below ~65 fs. The data are better described by a temperature-independent floor near 70 fs, which could come from a rate-limiting process outside the model (for example intrinsic carrier relaxation into the probed Se 4p states near EF) or from an effective resolution limit of the fitting. On that reading the "dip" at Tc is the low-fluence τ_int reaching the same floor as high fluence, a saturation effect, and with a floor in place α and the divergence of χex cannot be determined. The low-fluence fit also has at least three to four free parameters (χ0, α, prefactor, possibly Tc and τ0) for about eight points, and its minimum falls exactly on the measured 190 K point. The authors should extend the model to include the floor (for example a saturating or additive-rate form) and fit both temperature series and the fluence series of Fig. 4d jointly with one parameter set; report τ0, A, α, χ0 (and the χ0/χex ratio) and Tc with uncertainties in the main text, state which are fixed (in particular whether Tc is fixed at ~200 K from Fig. 3d,e or fitted, and whether α is the same on both sides of Tc), and comment on α relative to known CDW critical exponents of TiSe2; explain the physical meaning of τ0 = 25 fs and why it is shorter than any measured τ_int; explain the origin of the ~70 fs floor and compare it with the IRF width (green curve in Fig. 4a, Supplementary Fig. 5); state explicitly which features of Fig. 4e the model does and does not capture; and show that the data favour a diverging susceptibility over saturation of a non-divergent quantity.

B. Should address

2. The 1/√F scaling has not been shown to be specific to excitonic correlations, which is what the claim of persistent short-range excitonic correlations at 300 K rests on (Fig. 4b,d; Results, "Persistent excitonic fluctuations in the normal state"; Supplementary Note 8). The argument reads τ_int ∝ 1/ω_pl ∝ 1/√n_tot as plasmon-limited screening of bound electron–hole pairs. But a faster rise of a hole population at higher excitation density is generic in a photoexcited semimetal: carrier–carrier scattering and thermalization, the cascade of hot carriers into states near EF, screening-induced renormalization of the core-level or valence bands, Coulomb-driven intraband redistribution, and Auger-type processes all speed up with density without pre-existing electron–hole pairing. In addition, the 23 K and 300 K data fall on the same curve with the same τ0 (Fig. 4d) even though the equilibrium states differ qualitatively, which sits uneasily with using the scaling to diagnose the ordered versus the fluctuating state. The paper states that Supplementary Note 8 excludes alternatives, but the main text gives no quantitative argument. The authors should add a null reference measured at the same edge under matched fluence and spectral conditions, where excitonic correlations should be absent or suppressed: an isostructural non-excitonic compound (for example 1T-TiS2 or 1T-ZrSe2, or another dichalcogenide with a comparable chalcogen 4p reporter state), heavily doped or intercalated TiSe2 beyond the CDW dome (such as CuxTiSe2), or TiSe2 at much higher temperature (400–500 K); alternatively, a microscopic calculation of the density-dependent hole-generation time without excitonic binding, compared directly with Fig. 4d. They should also move the mechanisms considered in Supplementary Note 8 into the main text and treat at least one non-excitonic screening mechanism quantitatively. Otherwise the statement in the abstract and Fig. 4g should be framed as one possible interpretation.

3. The temperature anomaly at Tc does not by itself distinguish an excitonic from a lattice-driven instability, and the argument excluding a phonon contribution is too thin (Results, "Enhanced excitonic susceptibility at Tc"; Discussion, p. 1793). In TiSe2 the electronic and lattice order parameters are bilinearly coupled, so the electronic susceptibility at the ordering wavevector is enhanced near Tc whichever degree of freedom drives the transition, as the diffuse-scattering phonon-softening analogy the authors invoke (ref. 40) itself shows. Near Tc the CDW amplitude mode and the zone-boundary L-point soft mode soften strongly (refs. 40, 44), lattice fluctuations grow, and the CDW gap closes; each could change how fast Se 4p holes are created. The Discussion's exclusion of phonons takes a 20% phonon frequency renormalization from ref. 28, measured under different conditions, and assumes τ_int scales with a single phonon period; it addresses only fluence-induced hardening at low temperature, not softening near Tc, and τ_int (70–190 fs) is a fraction of the A1g period (~167 fs). The authors should give a quantitative estimate of the lattice timescale relevant to the window-(iii) rise (from the amplitude-mode and soft-mode frequencies at each temperature) and show that its fluence and temperature dependence cannot reproduce Fig. 4d,e; state what feature of τ_int(T) would differ if the transition were primarily phonon-driven; and explain why a faster, rather than critically slowed, response is expected near a continuous transition. Otherwise "consistent with excitonic instability" should be framed as compatible with, not evidence for, an excitonic mechanism.

4. It is not shown that the window-(iii) signal measures Se 4p hole population rather than an edge shift or broadening (Figs. 2a,e and 3c; Supplementary Note 7). The interpretation requires the transmission decrease in window (iii) to be state filling. The 25 K and 310 K spectra in Fig. 3c are derivative-like, with opposite-sign lobes on either side of ~53 eV and differences between the two temperatures, and the integration window [53.04, 53.33] eV sits on the slope of this structure. That is the shape expected from a transient edge shift or broadening, for example from core-hole screening or band renormalization (refs. 37, 38), which themselves scale with carrier density and can have different time and fluence dependence from hole filling. If the rise is dominated by such a shift, τ_int tracks screening build-up in general, not exciton dissociation, and this feeds directly into concern 2. The authors should decompose the Se M5 transient at peak ③ into amplitude (state-filling), shift and width components as a function of delay, fluence and temperature, and show that τ_int from the amplitude component alone reproduces Fig. 4d,e. The window-robustness test in Supplementary Note 7 is a different test and does not replace this.

6. Incident fluence is used as a proxy for carrier density without calibration, and equilibrium carriers are neglected (Eq. (1); Fig. 4d,e; plasmon argument, p. 1792). The 1/√F argument assumes n_tot ∝ F and uniform excitation. The XUV probe is transmitted through the whole sample, while the 750 nm pump is absorbed over a finite penetration depth, so the probe averages over a range of excitation densities, smearing and possibly biasing a density-scaling analysis. The optical constants at 750 nm (1.65 eV) change across the CDW transition, so the absorbed dose changes with temperature at fixed incident fluence; this alone could change n_tot near Tc and affects both the Fig. 3d,e amplitudes and the low-fluence τ_int(T). With a 3.4 fs pump at 2 mJ cm⁻² the peak intensity is ~6 × 10¹¹ W cm⁻², so multiphoton absorption and saturation should be ruled out, for example by showing the peak ① amplitude is linear in F. The equilibrium carrier density in semimetallic TiSe2 is substantial and differs strongly between 23 K and 300 K (as the resistivity anomaly near 150–200 K shows), so screening should scale as 1/√(n_eq(T) + cF), not 1/√F + τ0. The plasmon argument also assumes a single carrier species with constant effective mass, whereas TiSe2 has electron and hole pockets with different masses and a band reconstruction across Tc. The authors should give the sample thickness, pump penetration depth and absorbed fraction and estimate the depth profile of n_tot; show the temperature dependence of 750 nm absorption is small or normalize to absorbed fluence; rule out nonlinear absorption; state which carriers, effective masses and dielectric background enter ω_pl and whether the gapped low-temperature phase changes the n_tot–F proportionality; and estimate whether 70–190 fs is consistent with the inverse plasmon frequency for the estimated photoexcited plus equilibrium density with realistic ε∞.
