A. Must address

1. **The dispersion law used to extract J^orb is the next-nearest-neighbour form, so it cannot support the nearest-neighbour conclusion for PrNiO2, and the fitted J^orb does not reproduce the bandwidths marked in Fig. 5.** Eq. (1), ε_k = 2J^orb cos(k_x)cos(k_y), is taken from Ref. 43, where it describes diagonal (NNN) orbiton hopping in CaCuO2: it is the tight-binding form for hopping along the square diagonals (cos(k_x+k_y) + cos(k_x−k_y) = 2cos k_x cos k_y), and it gives equal energies at Γ and M. NN propagation instead gives a dispersion ∝ (cos k_x + cos k_y), with its extremum at M opposite to Γ. The paper fits both compounds with Eq. (1), reads the positive J^orb = +14 ± 4 meV in PrNiO2 as NN orbital superexchange (pp. 6–7), and sets it against the calculated J^orb_NN = 14.5 meV in Table III. Flipping the sign of the coefficient of cos k_x cos k_y does not turn an NNN process into an NN process; the two differ in the shape of the dispersion along [H,H] relative to [H,0], not in sign. The forms make different predictions along the measured paths: the NN form gives equal bandwidths of 4J from Γ to (0.5,0) and from Γ to (0.25,0.25), with the energy still changing towards (0.34,0.34), while the NNN form gives 4J along [H,0] but only about 1.4J from Γ to (0.34,0.34). The quoted numbers also do not match the fit: Eq. (1) with J^orb = 14 meV gives about 56 meV from Γ to (0.475,0) and about 20 meV from Γ to (0.34,0.34), whereas Fig. 5(a) marks 90 meV and 62 meV (the [H,H] branch is off by a factor of about 3); for CaCuO2, J^orb = −11 meV gives 44 and 22 meV against 56 and 34 meV marked in Fig. 5(c). Eq. (1) also has no constant on-site term, although the data sit at about 1.3 and 1.65 eV, and the Fig. 5(a) caption calls the continuous line a "charge transfer model fitting" while the text says it is the Eq. (1) fit. The authors should write the full dispersion, ε0 + 2J_NN(cos k_x + cos k_y) + 4J_NNN cos k_x cos k_y; fit the [H,0] and [H,H] branches of both compounds jointly with the NN, NNN and NN+NNN forms, with J_NN and J_NNN free; report parameters, uncertainties, their correlation, χ² and the [H,0]/[H,H] bandwidth ratios for each; overlay all fitted curves on the data along both cuts; state which form is used for which compound; state the marked bandwidths as differences between fitted points or of the fitted curve, with uncertainties, and reconcile them with 4J^orb; and correct the caption. The central claim, NN propagation in the nickelate against NNN in the cuprate, should rest on that comparison.

2. **The PrNiO2 d_xy dispersion (60–90 meV on a peak about 0.5 eV wide) has not been shown to be robust against fitting and geometry artifacts.** In PrNiO2 the d_xy feature (Fig. 2(c)) is a Gaussian several hundred meV wide that overlaps the 0.7 eV "RE peak", the d_xz/yz peak at 1.93 eV, the d_z² component and an unpolarized charge continuum (Fig. 4(a–c)); its fitted position depends on how these components are constrained. At fixed 2θ = 149.5° the incidence angle and L change continuously along each in-plane cut (grazing incidence on one side, grazing emission on the other), and Fig. 4 and the text on p. 6 show that the relative weights and polarization character of d_xy, d_xz/yz and the continuum change strongly with geometry and with the sign of H. Self-absorption through the STO cap and film, which can distort Ni L3 line shapes, also varies with geometry. With free widths and intensities, a centroid shift of tens of meV can arise purely from changing component weights, so the sign relative to CaCuO2, where the peaks are narrow and well separated (Figs. 2(d), 5(d)), could differ for instrumental rather than physical reasons. It is also notable that the d_xy energy maximum in Fig. 5(a) coincides with the specular point, where the elastic and low-energy intensity is largest (Fig. 1(e)), and that Fig. 5(b), with only fixed vertical guide lines, shows no shift visible by eye. The authors should state which fit parameters (widths, positions and amplitudes of neighbouring peaks, the RE-peak position and weight, the continuum shape) were free, fixed or shared across Q; show that the dispersion survives alternative constraints (fixed versus free widths, with and without the 0.7 eV peak and the continuum, constrained versus free d_xz/yz positions, a different continuum model, and a model-independent first moment over a fixed window); compare the ±H branches at equal |H|, which an intrinsic dispersion must make symmetric; show σ- versus π-incidence cuts as an independent check; give the d_xy linewidth and its Q dependence; estimate, from the single-ion calculation, how large an apparent shift the polarization and geometry factors produce for overlapping peaks of these widths; discuss geometry-dependent self-absorption; and overlay raw, normalized spectra at Γ and at the zone boundary so the shift can be seen without a fit.

B. Should address

3. **J⊥ and the claim of "three-dimensional antiferromagnetic order" rest on the magnon energy near Γ measured at an uncontrolled, varying L.** The abstract and p. 4 conclude that both materials "support a three-dimensional antiferromagnetic order", and J⊥ = 5.9 ± 1.1 meV for PrNiO2 (Fig. 3(a)) is justified only by the 60–80 meV magnon energy near the in-plane zone centre "at non-integer L". The Methods state that L is not fixed during H scans and that "this is not an issue, since the in-plane Js are still dominating", which contradicts using the same data to extract J⊥. With 2θ = 149.5° at 852.4 eV and c = 3.28 Å, L varies from roughly 0.44 r.l.u. at Γ to roughly 0.18 r.l.u. at H = 0.475 (similarly for CaCuO2), so J⊥ is constrained essentially by a handful of near-Γ points. The L value of each point is not given, nor whether SpinW used the actual (H,K,L). Near Γ the magnon lies close to the phonon (placed at about 80 meV in the text), to the 40 meV resolution and within about 1.5–2 resolution widths of a very strong specular elastic line (Figs. 1(e), 2(a)), and the Γ-point error bars in Fig. 3(a) are the largest of the dataset. Alternatives to a J⊥ dispersion are not ruled out: an exchange or single-ion anisotropy gap (easy-plane/XY, Ref. 59, mentioned and set aside), an artefact of the elastic/phonon subtraction, finite film thickness (15–20 unit cells), the paramagnon character of a system without long-range order, or Pr 4f–Ni coupling. The LSW model presupposes a specific 3D Néel order (stacking vector), which is not stated, and no evidence of order is shown (no magnetic Bragg peak, no temperature dependence), while the introduction notes that infinite-layer nickelates show no long-range order and spin-glass behaviour (Refs. 13, 19, 20). The authors should report L for every point in Fig. 3(a) and include it in the SpinW fit; show the Γ-point spectra, their decomposition and the sensitivity of J⊥ to those fits; compare fits with J⊥ = 0 plus an anisotropy gap against fits with J⊥ ≠ 0 and no anisotropy, with goodness of fit; ideally measure the magnon at fixed in-plane momentum near Γ (and at (0.5,0)) while varying L, by changing 2θ or the incident geometry, to separate a J⊥ dispersion from an L-independent gap; and, short of that, replace "3D antiferromagnetic order" with "three-dimensional (interlayer) spin correlations".

4. **The orbital argument for PrNiO2 contradicts the magnetic argument, and the magnetic string effect that decides NN versus NNN is asserted, not computed.** The orbital section argues that the string effect, which suppresses NN orbiton hopping in CaCuO2, is "absent or much weaker" in PrNiO2 because "the nickelate … does not present evidence of long-range AFM order" (p. 6). Yet the spin section reports a sharp, weakly damped magnon with J1 ≈ 46 meV, a sizeable fraction of the CaCuO2 value, and argues for 3D AFM order (concern 3). The string effect depends on antiferromagnetic correlations over a few lattice spacings on the timescale of orbiton hopping, not on static long-range order. The charge-transfer model in Table III contains no magnon coupling: for CaCuO2 it gives J_NN = 22 meV > |J_NNN| = 15 meV, and the authors attribute the observed NNN behaviour to a string-effect suppression that is outside the model and not quantified. The authors should give a quantitative estimate, for each compound, of the suppression of NN hopping by spin–orbital coupling, using the measured J1 and the spin correlation length (for example the ratio J^orb_NN/J1 that controls the string effect in the Wohlfeld et al. framework), giving a criterion for when NN propagation is suppressed in one compound but not the other; otherwise they should present the NN/NNN interpretation as a hypothesis and make the abstract consistent with it.

5. **The charge-transfer-model values in Tables II–III are partly tuned, so the agreement between J^orb_NN = 14.5 meV and the measured 14 ± 4 meV is not independent support.** For PrNiO2 the hoppings are set to 80% of the CaCuO2 values, and Δ = 8.00 eV was "chosen sufficiently larger than U also providing reasonable results" (p. 7), which reads as tuning against the outcome; literature estimates of Δ for infinite-layer nickelates are commonly a few eV, substantially below 8 eV. U = 3.2 eV for Ni, taken from Refs. 5 and 25, is far below the 8 eV used for Cu and below the 5–7 eV usually assumed for nickelates; those references give effective one-band U values, not the on-site multiplet Hubbard U a multiband charge-transfer model requires. The model equations and the definition of orbital superexchange are given only by reference to Ref. 43. The authors should list all parameters with their sources; show J_NN and J_NNN as functions of Δ (for example 3–10 eV), U (for example 3–7 eV) and the hopping scaling, and state whether the sign of the NN–NNN competition survives across those ranges; justify the PrNiO2 parameter set against literature estimates; check that the on-site parameters (ε_xy = 0.70 eV, ε_πσ = −1.00 eV) are consistent with the measured dd energies in Table I; and give the model equations in the Supplement.

6. **The magnon damping comparison does not isolate self-doping, so it does not support "self-doping has a much milder impact on spin order than chemical doping".** The claim (p. 4, Fig. 3(b)) rests on the ~100 meV damping in PrNiO2 being smaller than the ≳200 meV in UD15K Bi2201. The self-doped hole concentration of the film is never estimated, so the samples are compared at unknown relative doping. The comparison uses absolute damping between a system with J1 ≈ 46 meV and Bi2201 with J of order 150 meV; in units of the magnon energy, PrNiO2's damping (about 100 meV against magnon energies of 100–200 meV) is not obviously smaller than underdoped Bi2201's (about 250 meV against roughly 300 meV). The Bi2201 values come from a different study (Ref. 49) with different resolution and possibly a different line-shape and background model. The PrNiO2 damping depends on the fit model: no continuum is included (Fig. 2(a); phonon and magnon overtones omitted), near Γ the magnon overlaps the phonon, and near (0.5,0) the paper itself notes that a single-peak fit overestimates damping because of the multimagnon continuum, so the flat ~100 meV may partly reflect the fit. Damping in a 5 nm topotactically reduced film can also come from inhomogeneous broadening (partial reduction, residual or excess oxygen, stacking faults or c-axis defects, strain relaxation) absent in single crystals. The authors should refit the Bi2201 spectra (or a representative subset) with the present procedure and resolution convolution, or demonstrate equivalence; normalize the damping (for example γ/ω(q) or γ/J1); show how sensitive the PrNiO2 damping is to adding a continuum; estimate the self-doped hole density (Ni L3 or O K XAS, or Hall data on the measured film) and place it on the Bi2201 damping-versus-doping plot; compare with Sr-doped infinite-layer nickelates (Ref. 12 and the authors' Ref. 46) or literature NdNiO2/LaNiO2 damping; or otherwise rephrase the conclusion as a hypothesis.

7. **The LSW exchange constants are weakly constrained, their uncertainties are not credible as stated, and they are not compared quantitatively with the literature.** Five parameters (J1–J4, J⊥) are fitted to a single dispersion per compound along one path that does not reach X (it stops at H = 0.475), yet the Fig. 3(a) table quotes J1 = 45.8 ± 0.3 meV and similarly tight values for the others, despite the large error bars in Fig. 3(a). J1, J2 and J3 are expected to be strongly correlated when fitted only along M–Γ–X–M, and systematic errors (L variation, elastic/phonon subtraction, the quantum renormalization factor Z_c, whose use is not stated) are not included. The PrNiO2 [H,H] maximum lies at (0.2,0.2) rather than (0.25,0.25), with elastic intensity rising beyond the zone boundary (p. 5, Fig. 1(e)); if this reflects stripe-like or incommensurate order, a Néel-based J1–J4 model may absorb that physics into J2–J4, and it is not clear whether the fit was forced through these points. "In agreement with the literature [12, 24, 46]" needs numbers: Ref. 12 reported J1 ≈ 64 meV for NdNiO2 with a different, 2D parameter set. The authors should report the fit covariance or correlation matrix and uncertainties that include systematic contributions, state whether Z_c was applied, test whether a reduced model (J1, J2, J⊥, or J1, J2, J3 with J3 = J2/2 as in a t–t′–U mapping, plus J⊥) fits equally well, show how the J values change when the [H,H] points beyond (0.2,0.2) are excluded, tabulate literature values and explain the difference from 46 meV (rare-earth or strain dependence, inclusion of J⊥, different longer-range terms), and compare model-independent quantities, namely the zone-boundary energies at (0.5,0) and (0.25,0.25), across compounds and references.

8. **The characterization of the PrNiO2 film actually measured, and the reproducibility of the results, are not shown.** Several conclusions depend on this specific film being fully reduced, single-phase and superconducting: the superconductivity framing in the title and abstract, the 0.7 eV "RE peak" and the unpolarized continuum. It is not stated whether the RIXS film is the one whose transport was measured, how long it was exposed between reduction and measurement, how many PrNiO2 and CaCuO2 films were measured, or whether the key results (J values, damping, the d_xy sign) were reproduced on a second film. 9. **The two films sit on different substrates, and the effect of strain on J1, the orbiton hopping and the d_xy energy is not addressed.** PrNiO2 is on SrTiO3 and CaCuO2 on NdGaO3(110), with in-plane lattice constants 3.94 and 3.86 Å. The PrNiO2 lattice parameter a = 3.94 Å is larger than that of the STO substrate (3.905 Å), so it is unclear whether the film is relaxed. The d_xy energy difference is partly attributed to the different substrates, but strain could also change the hoppings and hence part of the factor of about two in J1 (as Ref. 57 suggests) and the orbiton hopping J^orb. The authors should state the strain state of each film and discuss how a substrate-induced change in hopping would affect the J1 ratio, J^orb and the d_xy energy comparison.
