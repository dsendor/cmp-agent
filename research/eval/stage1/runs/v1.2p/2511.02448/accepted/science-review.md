A. Must address

1. The PNO dxy orbital dispersion (Fig. 5a,b) has not been shown to be intrinsic rather than an artifact of fitting or cross-section. It is the paper's most novel result and the basis for the "opposite dispersion" headline and the orbital-superexchange analysis. The claimed dispersion is a centroid shift of 62–90 meV in a peak with a width of about 0.4 eV (FWHM in the single-ion model, SM G). That peak sits between the 0.7 eV "RE" feature and the broad, unresolved dxz/yz plus dz² tail. The overlapping components have strongly cross-correlated fit parameters (SM Figs. 7–8), and the quoted error bars are fit confidence intervals that ignore those correlations. Several things change along the cut and could move the fitted centroid by tens of meV without any real propagation:
   - the 0.7 eV feature, which by the authors' own account (p. 3) grows toward the zone edge;
   - the incidence angle, which varies along each cut at fixed 2θ = 149.5°, so the incident and scattered polarization projections and the relative dxz/yz weight change with Q (Fig. 4);
   - the relative weight of the spin-flip and non-spin-flip components of the dd excitation, which differ in energy by an amount of order the exchange energy (tens of meV for J1 ≈ 46 meV);
   - self-absorption, which differs between grazing incidence and grazing emission;
   - L itself.
   Fig. 4 and p. 6 already show that the polarization character of the 1.29 eV peak differs between ±H and from theory. Fig. 5b, with vertical lines fixed at the minimum, is not enough to judge a shift of a few tens of meV on a 0.4 eV-wide peak. Three hoppings plus an offset are fitted to this dispersion (t1 = 12.5 ± 3.8, t2 = 0.2 ± 4.8, t3 = 5.4 ± 1.7 meV), so robustness matters. The authors should: (i) compare the dxy position at +H and −H at the same |Q| (grazing incidence against grazing emission), for example with the spectra already taken at H = ±0.4 (Fig. 4a,b); an intrinsic dispersion is even in Q, while cross-section artifacts are generally not. (ii) Show that the trend survives changes to the fit model: two versus three dd peaks (as in Fig. 7), with and without the RE peak, the RE peak free versus fixed, the dxz/yz and dz² widths free versus fixed across Q. (iii) Report a model-independent estimator, such as the first moment over a fixed window around 1.3 eV. (iv) Use single-ion cross sections along the actual Q path, including the sf/nsf energy offset, to simulate the apparent centroid shift from geometry alone. (v) Plot the fitted dxy width and amplitude against Q next to Fig. 5a, and show the dxy energy from σ-polarized maps if these exist. If possible, repeat one cut at a different scattering angle. The same tests on CCO would show whether the sign reversal between the two materials is robust. If only one sign of dispersion survives every test, the claim stands; otherwise it should be presented as tentative.

B. Should address

2. The quantitative agreement between orbiton theory and experiment (Table III: t1^orb = 13.7 against 12.5 ± 3.8 meV) depends on chosen parameters, and the theory section has internal inconsistencies. The calculated hopping is t1^orb = (jHS − jLS)/2 · J̄1^orb with jHS = 1/(1 − 3J_H/U). With J_H = 1.2 eV and U = 6.5 eV, 3J_H/U = 0.55, so the result sits close to a divergence. Taking J_H = 1.0 eV (the CCO value) at the same U lowers (jHS − jLS)/2 from about 0.5 to about 0.34, roughly a 30% change, before J̄1^orb changes at all. The CCO value (t1^orb = 6.7 meV) uses J_H = 1.0 eV and U = 8 eV, so the contrast between the two materials depends largely on these choices. The inputs are loosely justified. J_H is argued from the atomic value through a nephelauxetic argument. The hoppings are set by hand to 80% of the CCO values "to account for the larger d-p splitting". Δ = 4.4 eV comes from DFT. U is said in the Methods to be taken from trilayer La4Ni3O8 (ref. 29), but SM H says "our estimate of the Mott energy U value is exclusively based on the orbital dispersion". If U was tuned to the dispersion, the Table III agreement is a fit, not a prediction. There is also an arithmetic problem. SM I gives J̄1^orb = U·J1^orb/(U − 2J_H) ≈ 27 meV, but with J1^orb = 21.6 meV, U = 6.5 eV and J_H = 1.2 eV this formula gives about 34 meV; 27 meV corresponds to U/(U − J_H). The value t1^orb = 13.7 meV uses 27 meV. The authors should state unambiguously how U was obtained, correct the formula or the number, and give a sensitivity analysis of t1^orb, J1^orb and J2^orb over plausible ranges of U, J_H, Δ and the hopping scaling for both PNO and CCO. They should show Table III against that band, check whether the sign and NN dominance survive, and present the agreement as consistency rather than validation.

3. The claim that PNO has three-dimensional antiferromagnetic correlations "similar to" CCO, with comparable J⊥ (abstract; p. 5; Conclusions, "clearly confers a certain degree of tridimensionality"), is not constrained by the data. For PNO, J⊥ = 5.9 meV is fixed, not fitted (Fig. 3a table, "fix"). SM Fig. 13 shows that it is constrained only by the few points within about 0.05–0.125 r.l.u. of Γ. There the magnon merges with a strong specular elastic line (SM Fig. 9), the error bars span roughly ±30–50 meV (about 75 meV near Q = 0.05), and the phonon was removed by hand (SM D). Other effects could raise the energy near Γ in the same way and are not excluded: an XY or easy-plane anisotropy gap (ref. 65 is cited for this), a finite in-plane correlation length in a film without established long-range order (a spin-glass-like state is cited in the Introduction), and the bias from fitting a ~100 meV-wide damped mode with a single DHO next to a strong elastic tail. Because 2θ is fixed, L changes along every cut (Methods), yet the L of each spectrum is not given, and it is not stated whether the LSW model was evaluated at the actual (H, K, L) of each point. No L dependence was measured at fixed in-plane Q. The authors should tabulate or plot L for each spectrum (this also bears on the dd intensities) and state whether the fit used it. They should fit J⊥ freely, or with an anisotropy gap as the alternative, and report the confidence interval, the correlations with J1–J4 and a χ² comparison. Ideally, they should measure the near-Γ magnon energy at two or more L for the same in-plane Q (for example at H = K = 0.05–0.1 with a different incidence angle or scattering angle), which directly tests interlayer dispersion. Otherwise J⊥ should be quoted as an upper bound (0–10 meV, as in SM F) wherever it is used, and J⊥/J1 = 13% as a range (0–22%). The statement that the results "give a proof of the fully occurred topotactic reduction of the PNO samples and the absence of interstitial oxygen atoms" (p. 5) does not follow from a fixed J⊥ and should be removed or supported by structural data.

4. The damping comparison with Bi2201 (Fig. 3b; p. 4) does not support the claims that self-doping has "a much milder impact on spin order than chemical doping" and offers "a non-disruptive way to achieve superconductivity". Fig. 3b compares absolute damping: about 80–100 meV for PNO against about 200–500 meV for Bi2201. The PNO magnon bandwidth is roughly half that of the cuprates (J1 = 46 against 82 meV). Relative to the mode energy (about 90/150–190 meV, γ/ω ≈ 0.5–0.6), PNO damping is close to that of underdoped Bi2201 at comparable momenta, so on that measure PNO looks like an underdoped cuprate rather than a nearly undamped system. Several other contributions to the PNO width are not separated from intrinsic damping: disorder and inhomogeneous or partial reduction in a 5 nm capped film, residual apical oxygen, the capping interface, the impurity phases the authors invoke for the 0.7 eV peak, L integration, and the phonon overtones and multi-magnon/bimagnon continua that were left out of the PNO fit (SM B) but included for CCO. The single-peak DHO fit near X also overestimates damping, as the authors admit for CCO. The Bi2201 widths come from a different sample family, experiment and fitting chain (ref. 54). The self-doped hole content of the measured film is not determined, so "self-doping" and "chemical doping" cannot be put on a common axis. The authors should plot damping normalized by magnon energy or by J1 for all samples; fit PNO with a model that includes the phonon and a multi-magnon component and show how much width remains; confirm that the Bi2201 values were obtained with the same DHO definition and resolution treatment, ideally refitting PNO and Bi2201 with the same model; estimate the doped-hole density of the RIXS sample (for example from the Ni L3 XAS lineshape, the O K pre-edge, or Hall data in ref. 42); discuss extrinsic sources of damping; and ideally compare with a non-superconducting or differently reduced PNO film.

5. The CCO and PNO orbiton dispersions are fitted with different models, and the fitted parameters do not match the quoted bandwidths. CCO is fitted with a pure NNN term (Eq. 2, with no constant offset written), while PNO is fitted with NN, NNN and third-neighbour terms (Eq. 1). The "opposite dispersion" and "NN against NNN" contrast therefore partly reflects the choice of model, even though SM I itself gives a finite NN hopping for CCO (t1^orb = 6.7 meV). Eq. 2 with t2 = −13.6 meV gives bandwidths of about 54 meV along Γ-X and 27 meV along Γ-(0.25,0.25), whereas Fig. 5c quotes 70 and 61 meV, and the white curve at (0.25,0.25) appears close to the maximum rather than midway. For PNO, Eq. 1 with the quoted t1, t2 and t3 gives about 50 meV along Γ-(0.475,0) and about 100 meV toward (0.34,0.34), the reverse of the 90 and 62 meV quoted in Fig. 5a. The authors should fit both data sets with the same Eq. 1 form, with t1 and t2 free for CCO to test whether the data are compatible with 6.7 meV, report the parameters with covariances, check that the plotted curves correspond to the quoted parameters, and reconcile the bandwidths with the arrows in Fig. 5.

6. The free-orbiton approximation drops terms larger than the one it keeps (SM Eqs. 15–19), and this undercuts the mechanism the main text relies on. The kept free hopping has prefactor (jHS − jLS)/2 ≈ 0.5, while the neglected orbiton–magnon terms (Eqs. 16–17) have prefactors (jHS + jLS)/2 ≈ 1.7 and jHS ≈ 2.2. The main text (p. 8) stresses that string-type scattering "strongly" suppresses NN orbiton motion, so the argument relies on the very terms that are neglected; dropping them because they produce mostly incoherent weight does not show that the coherent band is unrenormalized. The model also assumes a long-range antiferromagnetic, ferro-orbital ground state, while PNO is a self-doped metal without reported long-range order. The fitted t3 = 5.4 meV, about half of t1, lies outside the model. The authors should estimate the renormalization of the coherent orbiton band (for example with the self-consistent Born or linear-string treatment used for CCO in ref. 45), or at least give a bound; state the expected quasiparticle weight; relate the large PNO dxy width (about 0.4 eV FWHM) to the incoherent part; and explain why neglecting t3 does not undermine the comparison.

7. The "coexistence of superconductivity and sharp spin excitations" (pp. 2–4; abstract; Conclusions) was not measured as stated. All RIXS data were taken at 20 K (Methods), above both the onset (just below 11 K) and zero resistance (below 4 K). RIXS also averages over the whole film, so a superconducting fraction and a fraction with magnetically ordered-like correlations could be spatially separate, and the broad resistive transition hints at inhomogeneity. Describing the PNO magnon as "sharp" (p. 2) is also hard to reconcile with the roughly 100 meV damping in Fig. 3b, which is far from resolution-limited. The authors should restate the claim as normal-state spectra of a film that becomes superconducting below a given temperature, with unknown bulk superconducting fraction, unless data below Tc or a Meissner fraction can be provided, and reconcile the word "sharp" with Fig. 3b.

8. Characterization of the measured PNO film is missing, although several conclusions depend on it. Superconductivity in nominally undoped PrNiO2 is itself unusual and is the basis of the paper's framing, yet it is supported only by reference to ref. 42 ("the resulting samples show a complete superconducting behavior"); no ρ(T), XRD or XAS from the RIXS sample is shown. Without Ni L3 or O K XAS, a residual Ni²⁺ (incompletely reduced) fraction is not excluded; it would add dd weight near 1–2 eV and could produce the mixed polarization of the 1.29 eV peak and the unpolarized continuum (Fig. 4). The authors should show ρ(T) (including after the beamtime), XRD (c-axis parameter, absence of secondary phases) and Ni L3 and O K XAS for the measured film, state whether the same film was used for transport and RIXS, and temper the "full topotactic reduction" claim accordingly.

10. The four-neighbour linear spin-wave fit for PNO (Fig. 3a) is under-constrained, and its comparison with the literature is not quantitative. Five parameters (J1–J4 and J⊥) are fitted to a single dispersion along M-Γ-X-M that stops at H = 0.475, has about 45 meV of zone-boundary dispersion, large error bars near Γ, and a single-peak fit near X that the authors call approximate. J2–J4 are likely strongly correlated with each other and with J1, yet no covariances are given, and the quoted uncertainties (J1 = 45.8 ± 0.3 meV, J4 = 8.0 ± 0.4 meV) look implausibly small for a magnon about 100 meV wide. Along [H,H] the dispersion peaks near (0.2,0.2), which no J1–J4 Heisenberg model with this sign structure reproduces, and the data near (0.25,0.25) lie about 20 meV below the fit (Figs. 3a, 13). It is not stated whether the S = 1/2 quantum renormalization factor (Zc ≈ 1.18) is included, which affects J1 directly, and the sign convention for J2 (negative) is not defined. "In agreement with the literature [12, 24, 51]" is not quantified: earlier studies mostly used J1–J2 models with or without quantum renormalization, and ref. 12 reported J1 ≈ 64 meV for NdNiO2, well above 46 meV; ref. 47 reports values for PrNiO2 films. The authors should report the parameter covariance matrix and how the 95% intervals were obtained; show fits with fewer couplings (J1–J2, J1–J2–J3, or t/U with ring exchange), for both compounds, with model-selection statistics; show how J1 changes when the [H,H] points beyond about 0.2 are excluded; state Zc and the J2 convention; tabulate published nickelate J values with the model used for each; and express the PNO/CCO comparison also through model-independent quantities such as E(0.5,0), E(0.25,0.25) and their difference.

11. The polarimetric analysis needs its procedure and a quantitative comparison before the mixed polarization of the 1.29 eV dxy peak is attributed to Pr 5d (or interstitial s) hybridization (Fig. 4, Fig. 12; p. 6; SM G). The paper does not describe how the crossed and non-crossed components are extracted (polarimeter efficiency, multilayer reflectivity ratio, normalization), and the PNO components are noisy enough to need 15-point smoothing (Fig. 12). The single-ion calculations take the Table I peak energies as input, with per-peak widths chosen to match, so "well reproduced within a single-ion model" tests only relative polarization weights. Alternatives to hybridization are not addressed: overlap with the broad dxz/yz tail and the 0.7 eV peak, both partly non-crossed; the unpolarized continuum the authors attribute to self-doping; a residual Ni²⁺ fraction; and the polarimeter calibration uncertainty. The authors should describe the calibration; fit crossed and non-crossed spectra simultaneously with the same component set and report the crossed fraction of each peak, including dxy, with uncertainties propagated from the calibration; show how the PNO predictions change for other spin orientations or a spin-averaged paramagnetic case; state the expected fraction under each scenario; and present hybridization as one of several possibilities.

12. The three-band parameter set used for the orbital superexchange is not checked against the measured spin exchange.
