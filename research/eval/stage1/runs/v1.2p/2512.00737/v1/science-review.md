## A. Must address

1. The data do not separate "bi-altermagnetism" from what the known magnetic structure already implies (abstract; introduction, p. 1; final paragraph of "CD in RIXS and bi-altermagnetism", p. 4). The headline claim is that each sublattice, Oh alone and Td alone, is separately altermagnetic and that this is a new form of altermagnetism. The paper gives the relativistic magnetic point group (6'mm') but no spin-group (non-relativistic) analysis, although altermagnetism is defined through spin groups. It does not give the Wyckoff positions of each sublattice or the operation (6_3 screw or c-glide, not translation or inversion) that relates the up and down sites within each sublattice. The experimental logic is the larger problem. Both sublattices order at one T_N and are locked together by strong Oh–Td exchange, so the crystal has a single magnetic point group. Once 6'mm' is realised, symmetry allows CD on every excitation of every site, including a site that is only polarised by its neighbour. The authors' own model describes the Td site as Van Vleck-type: SOC gives a singlet and the moment is induced by the Weiss field (p. 5; SI p. 7), so its staggered moment is slaved to the exchange field and is not an independent order parameter. In the single-ion model, a moment along c on any site breaks the m mirrors whether that sublattice is compensated or not, and the paper never shows that the CD would be absent or different if a sublattice were ferromagnetically aligned, as in the field-induced or Zn-doped ferrimagnetic phases of this family (Refs. 35, 36). The up- and down-sublattice contributions are summed (Methods; SI Sec. 2) but never shown separately, so it is unclear whether they add or cancel, and therefore whether the observable is sensitive to the compensated arrangement that defines an altermagnet. As written, any altermagnet with two inequivalent magnetic Wyckoff sites would qualify as "bi-altermagnetic", and the claim reduces to a new label for a structure neutron diffraction has already established. The authors should: (i) give the spin-group analysis of each sublattice, with Wyckoff positions and the relating operation; (ii) state an operational definition of bi-altermagnetism that differs from "an altermagnet with two inequivalent magnetic sites"; (iii) compute the CD for each sublattice separately and for alternative ordering patterns (parallel spins within the Oh sublattice, only one site ordered, L in the ab plane), and state which observed features rule out each; (iv) identify an observable that would differ between a bi-altermagnet and an altermagnet whose second site is merely polarised (for example a CD ratio between sites that depends on separate sublattice order parameters, distinct temperature dependences of the CD at A and B, or independently controllable sublattice orders) and say whether the data test it; and (v) explain what the RIXS-CD shows that neutron diffraction has not. If the CD cannot make these distinctions, the claim should be reframed as sublattice-resolved detection of mirror-symmetry breaking by the Néel order in a material with two magnetic sites, with bi-altermagnetism inferred from the known structure rather than "unveiled".

## B. Should address

2. The Oh-site assignment of the CD at feature A rests on the model, in a crowded energy region (pp. 3 and 5; Fig. 4; Figs. S4, S6). The Oh half of the bi-altermagnet claim depends on feature A, which sits on the tail of the elastic line at 45 meV total resolution. The fits in Fig. S4 place components at about 35 and 70 meV; the Td spin-orbital and exchange-split levels are computed to extend to about 20–45 meV for Weiss fields of 3–5 meV (Fig. S6a); magnons (10–15 meV, Ref. 44) and phonons also lie in this range. Feature A is defined as a "broad structure centered at about 70 meV", but the CD in Figs. 2a, 3a and S2a spans roughly 0.03–0.15 eV and overlaps all of these. The statement (p. 3) that the Td spin-orbit excitation "lies well below 50 meV and does not yield significant CD" is a calculated statement, and the Td simulations in Fig. S6 are not discussed in this low-energy window. The paper itself notes (p. 2) that the Td and Oh resonances occur at different incident energies, which gives a direct experimental test. The authors should: measure a CD map (RCP − LCP) over 703–710 eV matching the RCP map of Fig. 4b,c and compare it with computed Oh and Td CD maps, to show that the dichroic weight of A resonates at the Oh energy; show the calculated Td-site CD explicitly in the 0–0.15 eV window; give the energy-integrated CD of A in a window that excludes the region below 50 meV, alongside the calculated Td CD in the same window; fit the RCP and LCP spectra separately with the Fig. S4 model to show which component carries the dichroism; and state the elastic-line CD, which differs visibly between helicities in Figs. S1 and S2g.

3. The magnetic domain state is not characterised (RIXS measurements, Methods; Figs. 2, 3). The m operation maps L onto −L and RCP onto LCP in this geometry, so the two antiferromagnetic domains give CD of opposite sign (the basis of the MnTe domain imaging in Refs. 40, 41). A beam footprint with equal domain populations would show no net CD, so the observed CD implies a domain imbalance, yet the paper does not say how one was obtained: zero-field cooling, field cooling, magnetoelectric cooling (Fe2Mo3O8 is a polar magnetoelectric), strain, or natural domain size compared with spot size. Because the compound is polar, the CD sign may also depend on the relative orientation of P and L, so the polar domain state matters too. Without this, the sign agreement between experiment and simulation in Fig. 2b,c has no meaning (the simulated sign depends on the chosen L), and the magnitude comparison is uncontrolled. The authors should report the cooling protocol, beam size and the number of thermal cycles; show the CD sign and magnitude at several sample positions and after repeated cooling through T_N; and ideally reverse L deliberately (magnetoelectric cooling with opposite E·H, or field cooling through the ferrimagnetic phase) and show that the CD at A and at B reverses sign together. A common sign flip would be the strongest evidence that both site signals come from a single Néel order parameter.

4. The CD is not quantified and the normalisation is not described (Figs. 2a, 3, S1–S3). Every CD statement rests on visual comparison of overlaid RCP and LCP curves, with no difference spectra, no normalised asymmetry (I_R − I_L)/(I_R + I_L), no error bars and no counting statistics. Key statements are "vanished within the experimental uncertainty" above T_N (p. 3) and "vanishes within the present experimental accuracy" in the m-plane geometry (p. 4), but the uncertainty is never given. At 70 K there are visible RCP/LCP differences at the elastic line (Fig. 3d, Fig. S2g). The normalisation (incident flux, elastic line, or high-energy dd weight) is not stated; normalising to a feature that itself carries CD would bias the result. Absolute intensities also change between temperatures (feature B is about 20 at 32 K and about 14 at 80 K; all dd features at 80 K in Fig. S3h are about 25–30% weaker than at 70 K), and the shape of the feature-A shoulder changes across T_N. Fig. 2a and Fig. 3 use different vertical scales for the same feature, and no CD magnitude (for example a percentage at A and B) is quoted, which prevents comparison with MnTe and CrSb. The authors should show RCP − LCP difference spectra or normalised CD with statistical error bars per energy bin; state the normalisation procedure within and across temperatures and separate changes in total spectral weight from changes in CD; give the degree of circular polarisation for each helicity and beam-position stability on helicity switching; and quote the CD magnitude at A and B.

5. The comparison with theory is qualitative, and the one comparison that does not depend on the domain state is not shown (Fig. 2a vs 2b,c; Figs. S6, S7; Methods). Measured and calculated difference spectra are never plotted, and the Oh and Td contributions are shown separately, never as a weighted sum convolved with the 45 meV resolution and overlaid on the data. The relative sign and magnitude of the CD at A (Oh) and B (Td) are fixed by the antiparallel Oh and Td moments within a layer, so they are a domain-independent prediction. In the data (Fig. 2a, Fig. S1), LCP exceeds RCP at both A (about 0.07 eV) and B (about 0.43 eV), but at the plotted scale one cannot tell whether the model gives the same relative sign. The paper also does not say how the Oh-up/Td-down pairing of the real Néel structure, the orientation of local axes for the up and down sublattices (related by the 6_3 screw, which does not preserve the scattering geometry), the incident and scattered angles and the Q of Fig. 2 enter the simulation. The relative CD magnitudes and line shapes (sign changes within feature B) are not compared. Without this, "the simulations reproduce the experimentally observed CD" amounts to "both show some CD", which symmetry alone guarantees. The authors should overlay measured and calculated difference spectra for A, B and C on the same axes; present a combined, resolution-convolved simulation for each geometry, including the m-plane geometry, where the model should predict zero; state the relative A/B sign in theory and experiment with the sublattice orientation given explicitly; and give the ratio CD(A)/CD(B).

6. The m-plane null control is narrow and not quantified (Fig. 2a inset; Fig. S1 lower panel; p. 4). It was measured at one temperature (32 K) and one Q. In the m-plane geometry the elastic peaks for RCP and LCP differ by about 8% (about 95 vs 88 in Fig. S1), and small differences remain at B and near 1 eV, while in the m' data the elastic intensities match. The Q given for this geometry, (0.05, 0, 0.65), is the same as for the a*c* geometry, although a momentum transfer in the ac plane cannot have indices (H, 0, L) in hexagonal reciprocal-lattice units; the sample azimuth (which needs a 30° rotation) is not given. The authors should give the correct Q and azimuth in the hexagonal basis, state whether the same crystal and beam spot were used, give the residual m-plane CD as a number with its uncertainty next to the m'-plane CD, and if possible measure the m-geometry CD at a second temperature below T_N to show it stays zero while the m'-geometry CD is finite.

7. The temperature dependence of the CD is not quantified (Fig. 3; Figs. S2, S3). The claim that the CD tracks the altermagnetic order rests on overlaid spectra judged by eye, and the CD is already nearly gone at 55 K (Figs. S2d, S3d), below T_N = 60 K. The authors should plot integrated |CD| at A and at B against T with error bars from counting statistics and repeat scans, and compare it with the sublattice magnetisation from neutron diffraction or susceptibility (or (1 − T/T_N)^(2β)), since RIXS-CD is expected to be odd and to first order linear in L. They should say whether the Oh and Td signals have the same temperature dependence, as a single-order-parameter picture requires; separate temperature dependences would also test the site-resolved claim.

8. The Weiss field is chosen after the fact and the model parameters are not tested for their effect on the CD (SI pp. 6–7; Fig. S6). The CD is reproduced for an exchange field of "around 3–5 meV, which appears to be a reasonable value", not tied to the known exchange constants, the magnon bandwidth (Ref. 44) or T_N; mean-field estimates from T_N = 60 K and the magnon bandwidth suggest exchange fields well above this. The size and line shape of the calculated CD and the induced Td moment depend strongly on this parameter. The Weiss field acts only on spin, although the paper stresses an unquenched orbital moment, and the lifetime broadening Γ = 0.3 eV is fixed; the SI says only that the "presence or absence" of CD is insensitive to ζ. The authors should justify the field from a mean-field estimate using published exchange constants or magnon data; compare the resulting ordered moments, for example (S_c, L_c) = (1.67, 0.35) at Td for 3 meV, with the Oh and Td moments from neutron diffraction; and show how the CD magnitudes and line shapes at A and B depend on Weiss field, Γ and ζ within the allowed range, comparing the integrated CD with the measured magnitude.

9. The explanation for the missing CD at feature C is ad hoc (p. 4, "Note that feature C …"; Fig. 2b; Figs. S6f–h). The Oh simulation predicts visible CD at the ~1 eV Oh feature, and the experiment shows none. The authors attribute this to "large lifetime broadening", but the final-state broadening used in the simulation is not given (only Γ = 0.3 eV for the intermediate state is quoted) and the explanation is not tested in a calculation. Since C belongs to the same Oh ion as feature A, this discrepancy bears on the site-resolved interpretation. The authors should compute the CD with a physically justified final-state broadening for the 5E and 3T1 states and check whether the CD at C then falls below the experimental noise.

10. The 2:3 energy-ratio claim does not match the paper's own level scheme and fits (p. 5, left column; Fig. 4a; Fig. S4d). The text says the two low-energy components have "energy ratio close to 2:3—consistent with the prediction of the zero-order approximation". In the first-order scheme of Fig. 4a the intervals are ζ/2 and 3ζ/4 (2:3), but transitions from the J_eff = 1 ground state lie at ζ/2 and 5ζ/4 (2:5). The Fig. 4 caption gives quintet levels at 23.7–25.6 meV and septet levels at 58.1–64.8 meV (ratio about 0.4, consistent with 2:5). The fitted peaks in Fig. S4d (about 31–38 and 68–73 meV) give an energy ratio of about 1:2, and intervals of about 1:1. Neither is 2:3. The text says "zero-order" while Fig. 4a shows first- and second-order levels. The main text uses ζ = 52 meV (Fig. 4) while the simulations use ζ = 65 meV (SI criterion C2). The fit itself (a free Voigt elastic peak plus two Lorentzians at 45 meV resolution) has many free parameters for a structureless shoulder, gives no parameter uncertainties, and places the ~35 meV component within one resolution width of the elastic line; peak 1 also disperses by about 7 meV between L = 0.7 and 0.9 (Fig. S4d), although the SI calls this "minimal momentum dependence". The authors should correct the ratio statement and terminology, give the fitted energies with uncertainties and constraints, compare them with the full calculation including the Weiss field and trigonal distortion (Fig. S7a), use one SOC value throughout and say which is compatible with the data, state how robust the two-peak decomposition is, and avoid calling the agreement quantitative.

11. A ferrimagnetic or net-moment contribution is not excluded for the measured crystal (Methods, crystal growth). Fe2Mo3O8 lies close to a ferrimagnetic phase induced by modest c-axis fields or small composition changes (Refs. 35, 36), with a net moment and an ordering temperature similar to T_N; in that phase conventional XMCD-type dichroism would also appear below about 60 K. The m-geometry null argues against this, since in a ferrimagnetic 6m'm' state all vertical mirrors become primed and CD would appear in both geometries, but that control exists only at 32 K and one Q and is not quantified (concern 6). The authors should show M(T) and M(H) for the measured crystal confirming a compensated ground state, give the Fe:Mo stoichiometry from EDX or refinement, and ideally show circularly polarised XAS in the same geometry, where 6'mm' requires zero XMCD.

12. No geometric sign-reversal test separates the CD from helicity-dependent instrumental artifacts (Figs. 2a, 3). The temperature dependence alone cannot exclude helicity-dependent flux or beam-position artifacts, because the paramagnetic and ordered measurements also differ in time and thermal history. Measuring the mirror-conjugate geometry (for example ± angle about the specular condition, or rotating the sample 180° about c) should reverse the CD in the ordered phase. The authors should perform and show such a test.

14. A site-substitution control would make the sublattice assignment experimental rather than model-based. Zn in Fe2Mo3O8 substitutes preferentially on the Td site (Ref. 36 and related work). In a (Fe,Zn)2Mo3O8 or Zn-rich sample the CD at B should be suppressed while the CD at A remains, provided the Oh sublattice stays compensated. The authors should consider this control, or the field-induced ferrimagnetic phase (concern 1), as a direct test of site specificity.

15. The momentum and geometry dependence of the CD is not measured (pp. 4–5). The authors state that the CD "arises predominantly from the wave-vector dependence of the polarization (dipole) operators rather than momentum dependence of the final-state wave functions", but all CD data are taken at a single, nearly specular Q, (0.05, 0, 0.65) or (0.03, 0, 0.66). 16. The "active orbital magnetism" claim is overstated and not tested by the data (p. 5, end of results). The paper calls Fe2Mo3O8 "a rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism", but rutile-type FeF2 and CoF2, widely discussed as altermagnets, host Fe2+/Co2+ ions with unquenched orbital moments. The ~20% orbital-to-spin ratio comes only from the ionic model with an assumed Weiss field (SI p. 7) and is quoted in the SI for the Td site, while the main text discusses it in the Oh context; no measurement here isolates the orbital moment. The authors should temper the claim, state explicitly which site each statement and the 20% figure refer to, and either check the model moments against independent evidence (g-factors from ESR or THz, ordered moments from neutrons, susceptibility anisotropy, XMCD sum rules in the field-induced ferrimagnetic phase) or present the statement as a model inference.

The authors should redo the derivation consistently, citing a standard treatment (for example Abragam–Bleaney).
