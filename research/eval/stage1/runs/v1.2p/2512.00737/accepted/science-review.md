A. Must address

1. The CD, and the "null" results the main claim rests on, are never quantified, and the m-plane null is not clean in the data shown. The paper says the CD "vanished within the experimental uncertainty" above T_N (p. 4) and vanishes "within the present experimental accuracy" in the m (glide-plane) geometry. It also says that this m'/m contrast, "rather than the mere onset of CD below T_N, identifies the observed signal as a signature of bi-altermagnetism". No uncertainty is ever defined. Every CD figure (Figs. 2, 3, S2, S3, S4) only overlays RCP and LCP spectra: there are no difference spectra I_RCP − I_LCP, no counting-statistics error bars, no repeat scans and no integrated CD values. In the lower panel of Fig. S2 (m plane, 32 K), the elastic/quasi-elastic peak differs by about 8% between LCP (~95 counts) and RCP (~88), and there are visible RCP/LCP differences near 0.45–0.55 eV and at 1.6–2.0 eV. These are comparable to the m'-plane CD on feature A (Figs. 2a, 3a) and on feature B. The Fig. 2a inset, described as the corresponding m-plane spectrum, shows essentially overlapping elastic peaks; it is not clear whether it is the same data as Fig. S2 or processed differently. The m-plane null is shown only at 32 K and at a Q that is not stated. This matters because the m-plane null is the main evidence separating the proposed state from a ferrimagnetic state, which the authors' own simulations predict has CD allowed in the m geometry and larger (Fig. S9); if the "null" contains differences of the same size as the signal, that distinction is lost, and the link between the CD and the order parameter is currently made only by eye. The authors should: plot difference spectra with statistical error bars (Poisson statistics plus repeat-to-repeat scatter, for example from RCP/LCP/RCP alternation) for both geometries at the same Q, at 32 K and above T_N; report the integrated CD or asymmetry (I_R − I_L)/(I_R + I_L) of features A and B with uncertainties for both geometries; plot the integrated CD against temperature for all eight temperatures in Figs. S3 and S4, against an order-parameter form, the neutron sublattice moment or the T_N of the measured crystal; measure the m-plane CD at more than one temperature and give a quantitative upper bound; show that the 5–7 eV normalization window carries no CD; and explain the elastic-line asymmetry in the m geometry.

B. Should address

2. The Néel-domain state, and hence the sign and size of the CD, is not controlled, characterized or discussed, and reproducibility is not shown. In 6'mm' with L ∥ c, the two Néel domains (L and −L) are related by time reversal (equivalently by the 6_3 operation combined with time reversal) and must give CD of opposite sign in the m' geometry, as the authors' co-authored MnTe work (Ref. 40) states explicitly. Equal populations within the ~386 × 30 µm² beam footprint (SI Sec. 2) would cancel the CD, so the finite CD in Figs. 2a and 3 requires a domain imbalance whose origin is not stated. The paper does not give the cooling protocol (zero field, magnetic field, magnetoelectric cooling), the number of crystals, cool-downs or beam positions, or whether the CD sign and size repeat after cycling through T_N. Figs. 2a and 3a are both at 32 K but at different Q and θ, and it is not said whether they come from the same cool-down. All data appear to come from one crystal (Fig. S1), and since the m and m' geometries require an azimuthal rotation, they may probe different spots and domain populations. Without this, the CD magnitude and its temperature dependence (Fig. 3) could reflect domain fractions rather than the order parameter, the sign agreement with the simulation (Fig. 2a versus 2b,c) carries no information, and there is no test that the dichroism is odd in the Néel vector rather than instrumental. The authors should state the cooling protocol and the number of crystals, cool-downs and positions; show repeat measurements of the 32 K m'-plane CD after thermal cycling and at two or more spots, and ideally the m'/m contrast on a second crystal or mounting; discuss how the polar axis (±c) combines with the Néel domain to set the CD sign; quote the relative sign of the CD on features A and B, which the model ties to the antiparallel Oh and Td moments and which does not depend on the domain, and compare that with the model; and, if possible, select a single domain by magnetoelectric field cooling (parallel E and H along c, below the metamagnetic field, exploiting the large linear magnetoelectric effect of Refs. 9, 35, 36) and show that the CD on A and B reverses together for the opposite cooling sign. If this cannot be done, the sign comparison with theory should be removed and domain averaging discussed.

3. The evidence for symmetry breaking on the Oh sublattice rests on feature A, the least clean feature, while the one well-separated Oh excitation (feature C) disagrees with the model. Feature A (~70 meV) sits on the tail of the elastic line at 45 meV resolution, in a region that also contains electromagnons (4–10 meV), magnons (11–14 meV), phonons (SI Sec. 3.2) and the Td spin-orbital levels, which Fig. S7a places at ~5–40 meV for Weiss fields of 3–5 meV, overlapping the fitted peak_1 at 30–38 meV (Fig. S5). Feature C at ~1 eV, the clean Oh crystal-field excitation, shows no measurable CD (p. 4), yet the Oh calculation gives visibly different RCP and LCP intensities there (Fig. 2b; Figs. S7f–h, S9h). The explanation offered, "large lifetime broadening and the character of the corresponding wave functions", is not demonstrated, and the model does not include that broadening. The paper thus has one ambiguous Oh feature where model and data agree and one clean Oh feature where they do not, which weakens the claim that both sublattices are seen to break the symmetry. The authors should fit the RCP and LCP spectra of feature A with the same constrained decomposition as in Fig. S5, with the elastic line fixed from the carbon-tape reference, and show which component carries the CD; show the model's Td contribution to the CD in the 0–100 meV window to exclude a Td origin of the A-region CD; and recompute the Oh CD at feature C with a final-state broadening matched to the measured width of C, showing whether the predicted CD falls below the noise.

4. Ferrimagnetic contamination of the probed volume is not excluded, because the measured crystal is not magnetically characterized. SI Sec. 1 gives only powder XRD and Laue diffraction; T_N = 60 K is taken from the literature, and no magnetization, susceptibility or specific-heat data are shown for this crystal. Fe2Mo3O8 sits close to a ferrimagnetic phase stabilized by small Zn substitution, off-stoichiometry or modest fields (Refs. 35, 36), and soft-X-ray RIXS probes roughly the top 100 nm of an ex-situ, HCl-cleaned surface, where off-stoichiometry or strain could locally favour ferrimagnetism. Since the ferrimagnetic CD is predicted to be "significantly stronger" than the altermagnetic one (Fig. S9), a small ferrimagnetic volume fraction could produce a sizable part of the m'-plane CD while giving m-plane CD below the present sensitivity. The authors should show M(T) at low field and M(H) along c for the measured crystal, bounding any net moment and confirming T_N; measure Fe L-edge XAS-XMCD in the RIXS geometry at 32 K, which is forbidden for 6'mm' but allowed for the ferrimagnet and so directly tests for a net moment at the relevant depth (or explain why it was not taken); combine this with the quantitative m-plane bound of concern 1 to put an upper limit on the ferrimagnetic fraction; and, if available, measure a ferrimagnetic reference (Zn-substituted (Fe,Zn)2Mo3O8 or the field-induced phase) under matched conditions to test the predicted appearance of m-plane CD.

5. It is not stated what observation distinguishes "bi-altermagnetism" from an ordinary altermagnet with two magnetic Wyckoff sites, or what the RIXS data add to the known structure. The m/m' selection rules are properties of the global magnetic point group 6'mm' and would hold for any compensated order with this group. In this non-centrosymmetric crystal, a compensated pair of moments related by 6_3 cannot be related by inversion, so each compensated Wyckoff orbit is altermagnetic by construction; "bi-altermagnetism" then seems to follow from the neutron magnetic structure and the space group, not from the RIXS measurement. The authors' own model also makes the Td moment a Van Vleck moment induced entirely by the Weiss field (p. 5, SI p. 6), so in what sense the Td sublattice is "independently" altermagnetic is unclear. Since the title and abstract rest on this concept, the authors should define bi-altermagnetism in spin-group terms; state which alternative ordered states the site-resolved data exclude (for example, Td moments disordered, Td moments parallel within a unit while Oh moments are antiparallel, or only one sublattice compensated by a rotation) and show with the model that these would give a measurably different CD pattern at A and B in the two geometries; soften "identifies the observed signal as a signature of bi-altermagnetism", since the response is consistent with 6'mm', which also follows from earlier neutron results; rephrase "the realization of bi-altermagnetism" toward the defensible claim of site-resolved detection of time-reversal symmetry breaking at both Fe sites; and add a short paragraph on what new physics bi-altermagnetism predicts (for example separate spin-split bands or anomalous responses from each sublattice), so the term has a role beyond a classification label.

6. The incident-energy dependence of the CD is not shown, although the site assignment relies on resonance energy. The text argues that Td and Oh excitations resonate at different incident energies (p. 3; Fig. 1e,f; Fig. S6), yet all CD data are taken at a single incident energy of 706 eV, and the energy maps (Figs. 1e, 4b,c) are RCP only. A CD map against incident energy would make the site attribution independent of the energy-loss assignment, would rule out CD on a nominal Td feature that actually carries Oh weight (or the reverse), would show whether the CD in the A region follows the Oh or the Td resonance, and would help separate a local multiplet CD from a collective (magnon) contribution, since the two should resonate differently. The authors should measure RCP − LCP maps across the L3 edge for features A and B and compare them with the calculated CD maps for each site.

7. The assignment of feature A to Oh spin-orbital (J_eff) excitations, and the quoted 2:3 level spacing, do not match the numbers given, and collective modes are not convincingly excluded. The main text (p. 5) says the two-component fit gives "an energy spacing [that] follows a 2:3 ratio (see Fig. S5)", consistent with ζ/2 and 3ζ/4. But Fig. S5d gives peak_1 at ~31–39 meV and peak_2 at ~68–73 meV, so the successive spacings (ground state to peak_1, peak_1 to peak_2) are ~34 and ~36 meV, close to 1:1; read as absolute positions the ratio is ~1:2. The ratio is near 2:3 only if the first spacing is measured from the elastic line assuming an unsplit J_eff = 1 ground level. The SOC constant is used inconsistently: the Fig. 4a caption uses ζ = 52 meV, giving quintet levels at 23.7–25.6 meV and septet levels at 58–65 meV, neither matching 35 and 70 meV, while the simulations use ξ = 65 meV (SI C2), for which the first-order levels would sit near 33 and 81 meV. The fit (a Voigt, two Lorentzians with free widths and a free linear background, on a shoulder only ~1.5 resolution widths from the elastic line) is called a "constrained minimal decomposition" by the authors and can hardly decide between level schemes. The trigonal distortion (Δ_tri = ±30 meV) is comparable to the SOC splitting, so the J_eff picture is only approximate. The argument that collective modes are "unlikely" (p. 5) rests on dispersion measured only along (0,0,L), with RCP only and error bars of ~±10 meV (Fig. S5d), while the CD was measured at in-plane components of only 0.03–0.05 r.l.u. This matters because chiral magnons in altermagnets also carry RIXS-CD (Refs. 26, 27, 45), and the paper notes CD differences at the quasi-elastic peak (Fig. S3a,b). The authors should correct or explain the 2:3 statement, including the effect of the Weiss field and trigonal distortion on the J_eff levels (Fig. S8a); state which ζ applies where; report the fit parameters, which were fixed, their widths and correlations, or compare one-, two- and three-component models; address whether magnons or electromagnons could contribute CD to the shoulder through the resolution tail; and either measure in-plane dispersion (or CD at larger in-plane Q) or soften the claim.

8. The comparison with the Weiss-field model is described as quantitative but is only qualitative, and the Weiss field and lifetime are free choices. Page 5 says "the measured CD is quantitatively reproduced by an atomic (impurity) model", but the Weiss field is set to "3–5 meV, which appears to be a reasonable value" (SI p. 6) without connection to exchange constants, the 10–15 meV magnon energies, the neutron ordered moments or T_N; the value used in Figs. 2b,c is not stated; it is restricted to a spin-only form along c although the authors note it could be more general (SI p. 5); and Γ = 0.3 eV is fixed although the CD arises from the iΓ term (p. 3) and its size should depend on Γ. The CD amplitude depends on the Weiss field (Fig. S7), no CD magnitudes or A:B ratio are compared, and no calculated and measured difference spectra are overlaid. The ferrimagnetic-versus-altermagnetic discrimination (Fig. S9) uses the same 3 meV field, so the statement that the ferrimagnetic CD is "significantly stronger" depends on that choice. The authors should fix or bound the Weiss field independently (for example from mean-field exchange consistent with T_N = 60 K or neutron-derived J values, Ref. 47); compare the model's ordered Oh and Td moments with neutron values; give measured and calculated asymmetries (I_R − I_L)/(I_R + I_L) for A, B and C side by side and overlay the difference spectra on a common broadened scale; show the calculated CD of A, B and C as a function of Weiss field and Γ; show that the m-geometry CD expected for a ferrimagnetic state exceeds the experimental upper bound for every Weiss field in the plausible range; and otherwise replace "quantitatively" with "qualitatively".

9. Only two scattering geometries were measured; the stronger azimuthal symmetry test is missing. In 6'mm', a 60° rotation about c maps one m' plane onto another through the primed six-fold (containing time reversal), so the CD of features A and B should reverse sign under a 60° rotation and be unchanged under 120°; no such reversal is expected for the ferrimagnetic group 6m'm'. This test is specific to the altermagnetic order, largely insensitive to domain and normalization artifacts, constrains the domain population, and would strengthen the "symmetry-dependent response" argument far beyond a single m'/m pair. The authors should measure the CD of A and B at φ and φ + 60° (and φ + 120°) within one domain region, ideally a full azimuthal scan between neighbouring m' and m planes, and compare with the model; grazing-incidence versus grazing-emission measurements would add a further test of the 6'mm' selection rules.

10. Polarization-switching artifacts are not bounded. The CD is obtained by switching the EPU between helicities, with a quoted phase error of ~3.5°, polarization degree above 98% and an LCP/RCP flux ratio of 1.1 that is normalized away. A residual linear component that differs between the two modes, multiplied by magnetic linear dichroism (which switches on at T_N and could be strong in this hexagonal polar crystal), would produce a temperature-dependent "CD" that the paramagnetic null cannot exclude; a change in beam position or footprint between modes could do the same. The authors should measure LH and LV RIXS at 32 K and above T_N in the m' geometry and estimate the leakage into RCP − LCP, and record fluorescence-yield XAS with both helicities at 32 K in the same geometry: a vanishing XMCD, required by 6'mm' (p. 4), would validate the helicity switching as well as exclude a net moment.

11. The claims of a Van Vleck Td moment and of "active orbital magnetism" come from the model alone. The statements that the Td moment is Van Vleck-type, that the orbital moment is "about 20% of the spin moment", and that Fe2Mo3O8 is "a rare example among transition-metal altermagnets that retain active orbital magnetism" (pp. 5–6) rest on (S_c, L_c) values from the ionic model with an assumed, spin-only Weiss field; in the SI these vary from (1.29, 0.30) to (1.80, 0.35) between 1 and 5 meV. No measurement in the paper constrains the orbital moment, and the CD was not fitted to obtain it. The authors should compare the model's total Td and Oh moments with published neutron ordered moments and either give independent support (for example XMCD sum rules in the field-induced ferrimagnetic phase, published g-tensor or neutron form-factor data) or present these statements as model predictions.

12. The normalization between polarizations and between temperatures needs justification. Spectra are normalized to the 5–7 eV loss region (SI Sec. 3.1), which in the ordered phase could itself carry dichroism (for example through fluorescence or charge-transfer tails). Intensities shift between temperatures by more than the CD itself: the elastic peak is ~80 at 50 K (Fig. S3c) against ~90–97 elsewhere, and feature B drops from ~21 at 70 K to ~15 at 80 K (Fig. S4g–h), with the dd intensities at 80 K ~30% lower than at other temperatures, suggesting changes in normalization, alignment or sample position. The authors should show that the 5–7 eV region is free of polarization- and temperature-dependent weight, show raw I0-normalized spectra, confirm that the CD is unchanged when normalizing to the dd intensity or I0, and explain the intensity changes, in particular the 80 K data.

17. The derivation in SI Sec. 5 is heuristic and partly inconsistent. The SOC expectation value is computed for a single Slater determinant, mixing real orbital labels (xy, yz, zx) with an m_l basis; the configuration is written as xy-down while the down electron is labelled m_l = −1; only t2g electrons are kept ("ignore the e_g electrons" is not justified); the sign change l_eff = −l on projection onto t2g (T–P equivalence) is not included; and the sentence "or the eg level, both orbitals are filled..." is a fragment. As a result the stated J_eff = 1 moment (5/2 µB, attributed to an "anti-parallel" orbital moment, without the orbital reduction factor) is doubtful; for Fe²⁺ the orbital moment is expected parallel to the spin. The final ζ/2 and 3ζ/4 intervals agree with the standard result (λ = −ζ/4 for high-spin d⁶), so a citation (for example Abragam and Bleaney) and a brief correct derivation, checked against the multiplet results of Fig. 4a, would suffice.
