1. The main result, Eq. (3), and the equations it is derived from contain sign and coefficient errors that stop a reader from reproducing or using it. (i) The O(ke⁰) term is printed as G²[cosh(2x/G)/cosh(1/G) − 1], which is ≤ 0 for |x| ≤ 1/2. The Poiseuille-type profile plotted in Fig. 1(d) (centre value ≈ 0.35 at G = 1, i.e. 1 − 1/cosh 1) and the hydrodynamic conductance 1/3 in Eq. (5) both correspond to G²[1 − cosh(2x/G)/cosh(1/G)]; for G → ∞ the printed term tends to −2(1/4 − x²), whose integral −1/3 has the opposite sign to Eq. (5). (ii) Eq. (15c) sets 𝓘₀ = 0, so Eq. (14) at n = 0 is a homogeneous no-slip problem whose only solution is u = 0; the electric-field source (the 2ke v_y term in Eq. (2)) must appear, presumably as 𝓘₀ = 1. (iii) Eq. (3) prints the layer term as +4ke ko 𝒴₁, whereas Eq. (18a) gives ke(ko/2)∂²u⁽⁰⁾/∂x² 𝒴₁ and ∂²u⁽⁰⁾/∂x² = −4 at the wall for G = 1, so the coefficient should be −2ke ko 𝒴₁, differing in sign and magnitude. With 𝒴₁(0) ≈ 0.5 from Fig. 2(a), the printed version gives a wall velocity of about 0.6, while Fig. 1(d) shows u ≈ 0 at the walls; only the −2ke ko 𝒴₁ version brings the wall value close to zero. The 𝒴₀ and slip terms are consistent with Eqs. (17)–(18a), so these look like transcription errors. (iv) The "Bulk (with slip)" curve in Fig. 1(d) peaks at ≈ 0.50, whereas the bulk terms of Eq. (3) (hydrodynamic + slip + rarefaction) at x = 0 for ke = 0.1, ko = 2, G = 1 give ≈ 0.47 and the hydrodynamic plus slip terms alone give ≈ 0.51, so it is unclear which terms the curve contains. Because Eq. (3) is called "the main result of this work" and readers will use it directly, the authors must check every sign and prefactor in Eqs. (3), (4) and (14)–(18) against the code that produced Fig. 1, state the normalization of 𝒴₀, 𝒴₁, 𝒯₀ and 𝒯₁ explicitly, and say exactly which terms each curve in Fig. 1(d),(e) includes.

2. The non-monotonic magnetoconductance is not shown to be a unique signature of tomographic flow, so the claims that it is an "unequivocal macroscopic signature" (Introduction) and that it "can be used to measure both the even- and odd-mode mean free paths" (Abstract, Discussion) are unsupported. Because ℓo ≳ L, the predicted drop at Rc ≈ 3ℓo falls at Rc ~ L, the same field range as the familiar boundary-scattering magnetoresistance of ballistic or quasi-ballistic channels with diffuse edges (the peak near W/Rc ≈ 0.55; Refs. [12, 36] and the classic 2DEG literature) and as the viscous or viscous-to-ballistic negative magnetoresistance of Ref. [34]. A conventional single-rate kinetic model with diffuse walls also gives negative and then positive magnetoresistance in narrow channels. A non-monotonic G_ch(B) with a minimum at Rc ~ L therefore does not by itself distinguish tomographic from conventional transport. The authors should overlay on Fig. 1(c) the numerical G_ch(1/rc) for ko = ke (single-rate electron–electron collisions, same diffuse boundaries, matched ℓe and ℓMR) and a quasi-ballistic reference with ℓ comparable to L, and identify which feature is specific to ℓo ≫ ℓe: the ratio of the two field positions, the depth scaling as ke·ko, the ko dependence or a lineshape. They should also state whether a fit of Eq. (5) to such a curve could recover ℓe and ℓo without degeneracy with a conventional model.

B. Should address

3. The regime of validity of the expansion is not stated, and the parameters used to demonstrate it sit at or beyond its edge. The expansion is in √ke at fixed ko (Eq. (13), "to linear order in ke" above Eq. (3)), but the corrections that carry the physics are O(ke ko): the slip, the rarefaction terms and the 𝒴₁ term in Eq. (3), the ke ko term in Eq. (5), and the 3ko²/(2G²) term in Eqs. (15b) and (7), with boundary-layer contributions of order ke^{3/2} ko^{1/2}; the layer width is √(ke ko). The expansion therefore needs ke ko ≪ 1 (ℓo ≪ L²/ℓe), not only ke ≪ 1. In Fig. 1(d),(e) (ke = 0.1, ko = 2), √(ke ko) ≈ 0.45 against a half-width of 0.5, so the two layers span the channel and overlap, which the construction h = h_B + h_T with independent wall layers (Methods) assumes they do not. In Fig. 1(c) and the ΔRxy example (ke = 0.025, ko = 5), √(ke ko) ≈ 0.35 and the O(ke ko) slip term (≈ 0.125) is about 40% of the leading 1/3. The headline effects are large only when ke ko is not small (the zero-field conductance correction in Fig. 1(c) is ≈ 45%; when ke ko ≪ 1, Eq. (5) itself says the corrections are small). Meanwhile the motivating physics is the limit ko ≫ 1: the Discussion places the tomographic regime at T ≲ 0.1 T_F, where ℓo/ℓe ~ (T_F/T)² ≳ 100, and as ko → ∞ (the superdiffusive regime of Ref. [16]) Eq. (5) grows without bound. The good agreement with numerics is encouraging but unexplained, and "similar agreement for other parameter values" (p. 5) is not documented. The authors should state the distinguished limit (ke → 0 at fixed ko or at fixed ke ko), the no-overlap condition and the formal remainder (O(ke²), O(ke² ko²) or O((ke ko)^{3/2})); show the asymptotic-minus-numerical error of Eqs. (3) and (5) against ke at fixed ko and against ko at fixed ke up to ke ko ~ 1, at zero field and at Rc ~ ℓo, confirming the expected scaling of the residual; show where the expansion fails as ko → ∞; give the window in (T/T_F, L) for a real material; and qualify accordingly the Discussion's statements that the Stokes–Ohm description is "inapplicable" and that non-equilibrium effects "penetrate significantly deeper".

4. The collision model is a two-rate model, but the conclusions are stated for degenerate Fermi liquids in general. Eq. (2) relaxes all even modes m ≥ 2 with one length ℓe and all odd modes m ≥ 3 with one length ℓo. The microscopic and exact-diagonalization studies cited (Refs. [14–18]) find odd-mode rates that grow strongly with m (roughly m⁴, with logarithmic factors), and that m-dependence produces the scale-dependent viscosity and superdiffusion of Ref. [16]. The diffuse-wall boundary layer is populated by high harmonics (the histograms in Fig. 1(d) show weight up to m ≈ 5), exactly the modes the two-rate model misrepresents. The layer width √(ℓeℓo), the functions 𝒴₀,₁ and 𝒯₀,₁, the slip coefficient in Eq. (17), and the factor 3 in 3ko/rc (which reflects the m = 3 harmonic) may change quantitatively or qualitatively; it is also unclear whether ℓo in Eq. (5) would be ℓ₃ or a weighted average. The Introduction and Discussion say a "dual relaxation-time description" is "fundamentally limited" and that Eq. (2) is "the appropriate starting point", yet Eq. (2) is itself a dual (even/odd) relaxation-time model, and Ref. [31], cited for going "beyond the dual-relaxation time approximation", is not available to the reader. The authors should solve at least the numerical benchmark with an m-dependent odd spectrum (e.g. ℓ_m = ℓ₃(3/m)⁴ or γ_m ∝ m⁴ for odd m ≥ 3, normalized to a given ℓo), show which of the four effects survive, how G_ch(B) and the velocity profile change and what "ko" the magnetoconductance would then measure, and either justify the two-rate reduction (e.g. as an effective ℓo for the slowest odd mode) or narrow the claims to the two-rate model.

8. All results assume fully diffuse walls, Eq. (12). The tomographic layer, the O(ℓeℓo) slip, the layer functions, the depth of the magnetoconductance minimum and the sign of ΔRxy are boundary-controlled quantities whose size and sign should depend on specularity, and real edges (etched or hBN-encapsulated graphene, PdCoO₂ channels; Refs.
