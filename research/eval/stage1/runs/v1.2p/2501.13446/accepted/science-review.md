A. Must address

1. The diffraction does not establish AA stacking, yet the magnetic mechanism depends on it. The P-3m1 model (Table S3: one Cr site at 1a with occupancy 2/3, a = 3.64 Å = a_R-3/√3) is the averaged CdI2-type subcell expected when honeycomb layers sit at random among the three lateral positions, so that the √3 in-plane honeycomb order is lost on averaging. The authors state on p. 6 that they "are unable to distinguish" randomized Cr occupancies from a randomized A/B/C stacking sequence. Yet pp. 10–12, Fig. 2d, the abstract ("AA-stacked trigonal phase") and the Conclusions treat the high-pressure phase as AA-stacked, and the Monte Carlo model uses the DFT J_L for an ideal AA bilayer (Fig. 6a), which changes sign above about 2 GPa. A random or turbostratic A/B/C sequence produces mostly AB-type (rhombohedral-like) and monoclinic-like neighbour pairs, not AA pairs; according to the bilayer calculations in refs. [25, 49] these have different signs and magnitudes of J_L, so they would give a broad distribution of interlayer couplings rather than the AA value. The two cases predict different diffraction: true AA stacking (honeycomb over honeycomb, an ordered one-layer cell such as P-31m with Cr fully occupying 2c and 1a empty, c ≈ 6.1 Å) keeps sharp honeycomb superstructure reflections (h − k ≠ 3n) at integer L, whereas random stacking turns them into diffuse rods along c*. The paper's own observation that the "1/3" reflections fade and vanish by 8.4 GPa (Fig. 3c, histograms) fits loss of interlayer registry at least as well as an ordered AA phase. The authors should (i) refine an ordered AA model (P-31m or equivalent) against the high-pressure data and compare it with the disordered P-3m1 model; (ii) analyse the intensity along the 10L and 11L rods (diffuse streaks versus sharp peaks at integer L) at 0, 6.4 and 8.4 GPa, at 8.4–21.5 GPa, and after decompression, and report whether diffuse intensity remains, or use specific (h0l) intensities or the Br positions in the P-3m1 refinement; (iii) if ordered AA cannot be shown, compute J_L for the registries actually compatible with the data (random A/B/C shifts, AB and so on), rebuild the magnetic model on stacking-averaged interlayer couplings, and reword the abstract, Fig. 2 and the Conclusions to drop the AA label.

2. The Monte Carlo collapse of T_C to zero may be an artefact of how the stacking disorder is placed and of what quantity is called T_C. Stacking faults act between whole layers. If each pair of adjacent layers is coupled uniformly by J_L(ABC) > 0 or J_L(AA) < 0 and each layer is ferromagnetic, the stack is an unfrustrated one-dimensional chain of layers: a gauge transformation (flipping every spin in a layer) maps it onto a ferromagnet with couplings |J_L| (a Mattis-type collinear ground state). The ordering temperature is then set by J1, the anisotropy and |J_L|, not by the signs; only the net moment is compensated. The model text instead says (p. 11) that "atoms from these phases are randomly distributed"; per-atom random assignment of J_L creates in-plane frustration that whole-layer stacking cannot produce. Further artefacts are possible: (a) if T_C in Fig. 6b and Fig. S8 is taken from the uniform magnetization or uniform susceptibility (the text says "determined from the susceptibility"), a compensated layered order looks like "T_C = 0" even though the system is ordered; (b) the cell is only 10 layers (20×20×1 unit cells) with periodic interlayer boundaries, so an odd number of AFM bonds around the periodic stack frustrates the chain artificially, and with only three disorder realizations this can lower the computed T_C; (c) the paracrystal block length of 10 ± 2 nm (about 16 layers) is longer than the whole simulation cell. The authors' own remark (p. 12) that a CrBr3 monolayer orders at about 70% of bulk T_C (22–27 K) makes the point sharply: layers decoupled or coupled antiparallel by a weak J_L should still order near that temperature while J1 is still about 1.4–1.7 meV and the DFT MAE falls only by a factor of two by 8 GPa, so interlayer sign changes alone cannot plausibly drive T_C from 33 K to about 4 K at 5.6 GPa and then to zero; this gap must be discussed. The authors should state exactly how "AA" and "ABC" are assigned in the simulation (per atom, per bond or per layer pair) and which order parameter defines T_C; repeat the simulation with layer-wise stacking disorder, using block lengths from the paracrystal analysis, in cells thicker than one block with open boundaries or many more layers, averaged over more realizations, and with a finite-size check; and report the layer-resolved (gauge-transformed, staggered) order parameter and the specific heat alongside the uniform magnetization. They should state explicitly whether the predicted state above p_c is ordered (a compensated AFM) or disordered. If the layer-resolved model keeps a finite ordering temperature, the claim should change from "collapse of magnetic order" to "loss of net moment through interlayer compensation".

B. Should address

3. The magnetic state above p_c is not characterized, although the proposed state would have a measurable signature, and the present data partly conflict with it. All DAC field sweeps stop at 1 T (Fig. 1a), T_C comes from 0.01 T (Fig. 1b), and "M_sat" is defined as M at 1 T and 2 K, so "collapse of ferromagnetism" here means only that the 1 T moment and the low-field anomaly vanish. In the proposed scenario, layers keep their moments and couple antiparallel across AA interfaces with J_L(AA) ≈ −0.15 to −0.25 meV per Cr (Fig. 6a). Such couplings should be overcome at a spin-flip field of order 1–3 T; in a mixture where only some interfaces are AFM, 1 T along the easy axis should already flip much of the compensated structure, and even a fully A-type arrangement would show a large linear susceptibility and a metamagnetic step at a few tesla. M(1 T) should therefore remain a sizeable fraction of 3 μB/Cr, not zero as at 6.24 GPa, while a genuine loss of moment or a glassy state would give an unsaturated paramagnetic response. There is also a temperature confound: at 5.24 and 5.60 GPa T_C is about 13 K and 4 K, so the moment at 2 K is reduced simply because the measurement is close to T_C, and the "identical trend" of T_C and M_sat (p. 3) does not show a reduced Cr moment or a growing antiparallel fraction. The authors should measure M(H) at 2 K to the highest available field (5–7 T) at pressures just below and above p_c (for example 5.6 and 6.2 GPa or higher); measure M(T) at several fields (0.1–1 T and above the expected spin-flip field) above p_c to look for a Néel-type cusp and a Curie–Weiss regime confirming local moments, with field-cooled and zero-field-cooled data; examine the 6.24 GPa M(T) (Fig. 1b, green) for a cusp or kink; compare moments at a fixed reduced temperature T/T_C; and stop calling the 1 T value a saturation moment near p_c unless saturation is shown.

4. The decompression result contradicts the proposed structure–magnetism link as presented, and the stated explanation conflicts with the diffraction. Fig. S1b shows full recovery of T_C and M_sat after decompression from 10 GPa. Table S4, however, gives an R-3 fraction of −0.1(7) at 8.4 GPa, the abstract says R-3 is "undetectable above 8.4 GPa", the 1/3 reflections are extinct at 8.4 GPa (Fig. 3c), and P-3m1 is called metastable on release from 21.5 GPa (Fig. 3d). The Conclusions resolve this by saying P-3m1 "was not yet stabilized at 10 GPa while some volume fraction of R-3 persisted", but no structural data at or after 10 GPa support this. Moreover, Fig. 6a gives J_L(AA) slightly positive (FM) at ambient pressure, so a recovered P-3m1/AA crystal would be ferromagnetic anyway and recovery does not discriminate between scenarios; conversely, without a test, the magnetic collapse may be reversible and unrelated to the irreversible stacking change, leaving compression of the intralayer exchange and anisotropy as a live explanation. The authors should measure XRD on a crystal decompressed from about 10 GPa, and measure ambient-pressure M(T) and M(H) (to 5–7 T) on a crystal recovered from 21.5 GPa (single-phase P-3m1 per Table S4) or from above 15–20 GPa. If the AA phase carries antiparallel interlayer coupling, the recovered crystal should show strongly reduced net moment or anomalous M(H); if it is a normal ferromagnet, the authors should discuss what this means for the scenario.

6. Structure and magnetism are measured on different crystals, at different temperatures, so the correlation between them is assumed rather than measured. All XRD and Raman data are at room temperature (Methods, Fig. 2e, Fig. 3, Fig. 5), while the magnetism is at 2–40 K. Cr trihalides have stacking transitions on cooling (CrBr3 near 420 K at ambient, CrI3 near 220 K), the R-3 phase is reached through a first-order transition, and the boundary may move with pressure, so room-temperature phase fractions need not match low-temperature ones. Methods say the P-3m1 fraction varies from crystal to crystal and with handling ("somewhat a matter of chance" among 30+ crystals; the XRD crystal began at about 80% R-3), but the initial phase fraction of the magnetization and Raman crystals is not given. The pressures also do not line up: the 1/3 reflections are still visible at 6.4 GPa and gone by 8.4 GPa, while ferromagnetism is already gone at 6.24 GPa, measured in a different cell, medium and crystal. Fig. S1a shows two magnetization runs with p_c about 1 GPa apart, but only 5.8 GPa is quoted; if p_c tracks the initial P-3m1 fraction that would support the mechanism, and if not it argues against it. The authors should report XRD on the magnetization and Raman crystals (or pieces cut from the same crystal) before loading; report p_c for every run and discuss whether it tracks the initial fraction; ideally add low-temperature diffraction, or at least 1/3-reflection intensities near T_C, at one or two pressures around p_c, or justify why room-temperature stacking carries over; and plot the Table S4 phase fractions, with uncertainties, on the same pressure axis as T_C and M_sat in a main-text figure. Without these, "the T_C decline correlates with the AA fraction" (p. 11) is an assumption.

7. The pressure at the magnetization measurement point is uncertain, and overshoot matters for a metastable transformation. Pressure is set and read with ruby at room temperature only (p. 14), and turnbuckle Be-Cu cells can change pressure on cooling, so the low-temperature pressure is unknown; the horizontal error bars in Fig. 1 are one-sided overpressure during loading, reaching about 3 GPa in the first run (Fig. S1a; up to about 14 GPa at the last point). If the stacking change is irreversible, the magnetization at each point reflects the maximum pressure reached rather than the nominal pressure. The two runs give p_c about 1 GPa apart, and run 1 has a finite T_C near 6.3 GPa; Fig. 1 does not say which run it shows. The authors should state the run and sample for each data set, estimate or measure the low-temperature pressure (ruby at low temperature, or a manometer such as the superconducting transition of Pb or Sn in the cell), and quote p_c as a range with an uncertainty covering both runs rather than as 5.8 GPa.

8. A non-hydrostatic, shear-induced origin of the structural change is not excluded. The authors find that cutting, bending and mounting alone produce the P-3m1 component (pp. 5–6, 14; Fig. 4c–h). The methanol–ethanol–water medium is hydrostatic only to about 10.5 GPa (SI p. 9), yet the claim that P-3m1 becomes the stable phase rests on points at 11.8–21.5 GPa and on decompression from 21.5 GPa; the main text describes the media only as "liquid" and does not state the medium and its hydrostatic limit for each experiment. Non-hydrostatic stress is therefore a plausible driver of the growth of P-3m1, the 10 nm block size, its retention on decompression and the broadened spots after release from 21.5 GPa (Fig. 3d). The change of Raman slopes "around 10 GPa" (Fig. 5) coincides with the solidification of the medium, while Fig. 6 of [40] reports 15 GPa. The authors should name the medium for each experiment, report ruby linewidths or pressure gradients at each point, and if possible run at least one diffraction (or Raman) experiment in He or Ne, or show that the onset does not depend on the medium. "Pressure-induced phase transformation" in the title and abstract should be qualified accordingly.

16. Eq. (1) is misdescribed. The Ehrenfest relation is an exact thermodynamic identity, not a model that "treats magnetovolume effects in materials with almost isotropic elastic properties", and it cannot fail to apply to van der Waals magnets; it only connects quantities.
