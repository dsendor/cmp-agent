## B. Should address

1. **The non-thermal magnon state has not been distinguished from a heated, quasi-equilibrium magnet with critical spin fluctuations.** All tr-RDS data are taken at 190 K, only ~23 K below T_N1 = 213 K, with the incommensurate phase just above (213-230 K) (Methods, Fig. S5). A rough estimate for 3 mJ/cm² absorbed within the ~450 nm penetration depth gives an energy density of order 50-70 J/cm³; with a CuO heat capacity of roughly 3 J/(cm³·K) this is a near-surface temperature rise of order 15-20 K once spins and lattice equilibrate, bringing the probed ~18 nm layer to or near T_N1. Near T_N, thermally populated spin fluctuations produce critical diffuse scattering around the magnetic Bragg peak that would be resonant, π-enhanced, symmetric about q_RXD, rise as the order parameter falls and recover as the order recovers. The fluence dependence in Fig. 2f (onset with demagnetization, then a decrease at high fluence that the text itself attributes to approaching T_N) fits this picture as well. The paper interprets I_RDS through Eqs. (1)-(2) as f_μ(q,t) and claims both "non-thermal magnons" and a "quasi-thermalization fulfilling Bose-Einstein statistics", but shows no equilibrium diffuse intensity around (1/2 0 -1/2) as a function of temperature in the same geometry. Such a series (for example 150 K to T_N1 and through the incommensurate phase) would test that the diffuse tail scales with the thermal magnon population as Eq. (2) predicts, calibrate an effective magnon temperature at each delay, and show whether the state at 4 ps (and at 4.5 ps in Fig. 2f) is simply a hotter magnet. The authors should compare transient q-profiles with static ones at matched RXD suppression and show that the transient q-dependence (Fig. 3e,f) differs from the equilibrium critical lineshape at the same order-parameter reduction. They should fit the extracted f_μ(q, t ≥ 2 ps) to a Bose-Einstein form with free temperature and chemical potential and report the result (a magnon-number-conserving process should give a non-zero chemical potential). The main text should state the estimated spin and lattice temperatures from Supplementary Note 3 at 4.5 ps and at 1-10 ns, and say explicitly whether any part of the excited layer crosses T_N1 at 3 mJ/cm². Measurements at a lower base temperature or fluence, where heating stays well below T_N, would test whether the upturn and its picosecond dynamics persist away from criticality.

2. **A transient change in absorption or in the resonant scattering factor has not been ruled out as a contributor to the "RDS" upturn.** The upturn is strongest at about 931.5-932 eV, near the XAS maximum, exactly where the static RXD shows a self-absorption dip and away from the RXD maximum near ~930.5 eV (Fig. 2e). The authors attribute this to a near-surface depth profile of photo-created magnons reducing self-absorption. Photoexcitation of a charge-transfer insulator is also expected to change the Cu L3 absorption itself (doublon/holon creation, edge shifts, bleaching, broadening), and heating can shift the resonance and change the refractive index. A pump-induced reduction of self-absorption, or a change in the real part of the scattering factor, would raise the scattered intensity across the peak profile at fixed photon energy without any change in spin correlations; away from q = 0, where the multiplicative RXD loss is small in absolute terms, this could look like an upturn. The amplitude profile A_RDS(q) in Fig. 3f closely follows the Bragg-tail shape, as a multiplicative q-independent change would, and the π/σ dependence (Fig. 2g) does not separate the two pictures because the Bragg tail has the same polarization dependence. The authors should show (i) the ratio (I_ON - I_OFF)/I_OFF versus q at fixed delays (for example 0.5, 1.5 and 4 ps), which is q-independent for an absorption or form-factor change but not for magnon redistribution; (ii) time-resolved XAS (or fluorescence yield) at 3 mJ/cm², or the full energy dependence of the RXD and RDS signals at several delays; (iii) diffuse intensity at a position where magnetic diffuse scattering should be absent but the same absorption path applies (a charge Bragg tail at the same energy, or a non-resonant/below-edge control in the same geometry); and (iv) a model of the expected energy dependence of a purely magnetic upturn including the depth profile, quantifying how much of the upturn could come from self-absorption change or resonance shift.

3. **The separation of RDS from the Bragg-tail reduction is model-dependent and weakest at the small-|q| points that carry the linear 1/τ_RDS ∝ |q| claim.** A_RDS and τ_RDS (Fig. 3a,d,f) come from a two-exponential fit to each trace (Supplementary Note 2). Near q_RXD the tail dominates: the q = 0.010 trace drops to about -5 a.u. while the upturn is of order +0.2 a.u. (Fig. 3a inset); the Bragg top is saturated (Fig. 2c) and the central region is excluded (hatched, Fig. 3c-f). The points at |q| = 0.010-0.020 r.l.u. are only 2-4 times the stated 5×10⁻³ r.l.u. resolution, have the largest tail contribution, and give the slowest τ_RDS (~3-4 ps), comparable to the 4 ps delay window of Fig. 3a, and the near-zero rate in Fig. 3i. τ_RXD is about 50-100 fs, comparable to the 60 fs time resolution (Fig. 3d), so a misestimated fast component leaks into the slow amplitude. The positive/negative q asymmetry in Fig. 3a is attributed to "a small error in the definition of the origin"; an error of the order of the resolution is half the smallest |q| used and directly affects the small-q rates. The authors should state whether the fit is convolved with the instrument response; show full residuals and the amplitude-τ covariance for the inner q points; test an alternative decomposition, for example modelling the RXD contribution from the measured, unsaturated static Bragg lineshape scaled by the RXD time trace; extend the delay range at small |q| to several time constants; treat the q origin as a fit parameter, give its value and uncertainty, and show that the linear scaling survives; and state whether the line in Fig. 3i is a fit (through the origin or not) and what functional form the QBE predicts for 1/τ(q).

4. **The data and the quantum Boltzmann simulation are compared only qualitatively, the free parameters are not identified, and the momentum grid looks coarse for the measured window.** Fig. 3g (simulation, 0-800, 0-6 ps) and Fig. 3e (data, a.u., 0-4 ps) are compared by eye on different colour scales and time ranges; the lines in Fig. 3i are "guides to the eye", and there is no overlay of simulated and measured τ_RDS(q) or A_RDS(q). The initial doublon density and "excitation parameters were chosen to be consistent with the experiment" (Methods) without saying which observable they were tuned to, and J_AFM, J_FM, J_out and D are "representative" values. The QBE uses a 32×48×32 k-grid in the magnetic Brillouin zone, a spacing of order 0.02-0.03 r.l.u. along b*, so the measured |q| = 0.010-0.080 r.l.u. window covers only a few grid points, yet Fig. 3g,h are smooth maps. The 30% order-parameter suppression at 3 mJ/cm² (Fig. 2d) implies a large magnon density, and it is not shown that leading-order scattering beyond linear spin-wave theory remains valid there. Claims such as "fully quantum-mechanical calculations provide the first direct visualization" and "transcends phenomenological models" depend on a quantitative comparison. The authors should overlay simulated and measured τ_RDS(q) and A_RDS(q) on common axes (Fig. 3f,i); give a parameter table separating fixed from fitted parameters (doublon density, ramp time, exchange constants, anisotropy, couplings); state how Fig. 3g,h are obtained on this grid (interpolation, local refinement near Γ, smoothing) and show convergence of τ(q) with grid density near Γ; and show how the predicted rates and the |q|-linear behaviour depend on doublon density, coupling constants and the anisotropy gap that sets the accumulation point.

5. **The nanosecond recovery is attributed to anomalous magnon-phonon scattering without excluding heat transport or lattice cooling, and the coupling that supports it is fitted to the same timescale.** The ~7 ns recovery of RXD and RDS (Fig. 4d) is read as an intrinsically inefficient anomalous process, and the effective coupling of ~59 µeV is "estimated from the magnon relaxation timescale". That makes the coupling an output fitted to one number, so the model's agreement with ~7 ns is not an independent test of the mechanism. Heat leaving the excited layer into the cold bulk takes roughly d²/D; for a ~45 nm layer (r3's figure) and a diffusivity of order 10⁻⁶ m²/s this is a few nanoseconds, the same order as the recovery, while the ~450 nm penetration depth quoted in r1 and r2 would give a much longer time, so the estimate needs to be done with the actual excitation profile. The RXD in Fig. 4d recovers only partially by 12 ns, and the residual, possibly part of the 7 ns component, could reflect near-surface lattice temperature and heat redistribution rather than intrinsic magnon annihilation; Supplementary Note 11 treats the microsecond phonon timescale but not the nanosecond component. The authors should give an explicit thermal-diffusion estimate (or a depth-resolved model combining diffusion with the QBE magnon-phonon channel) using literature CuO thermal conductivity at 190 K, show the lattice temperature in the 18 nm probe depth from 0.1 to 20 ns, state which parameter is fitted, and add a test that changes the predicted bottleneck, for example the fluence, probe-depth (incidence angle or energy) or base-temperature dependence of the recovery, since diffusion and an intrinsic bottleneck predict different scalings. Without this, neither the 59 µeV coupling nor the "design principle" for lifetimes follows.

6. **A transient incommensurate (AF2) state or a transient change of the Bragg profile is not quantitatively excluded.** A photo-induced collinear-to-spiral transition in CuO has been reported (ref. 23), and the AF2 propagation vector (0.506, 0, -0.483) lies within a few hundredths of r.l.u. of the commensurate peak, the same scale as the q range of Fig. 3. The RDS detector sits at (0.49, 0.01, -0.51) and Fig. 4d uses (0.47, 0, -0.53), which also deviate in h and l. Transient incommensurate satellites, short-range AF2 correlations, or a peak shift (from AF2 or from lattice expansion/strain) or lineshape change would give a growing, resonant, π-enhanced diffuse signal consistent with Figs. 2e-g without magnon redistribution. The exclusion rests on the statement that the peak width "remains nearly constant" (Fig. S4) and on Supplementary Note 4, but the peak top is saturated in the shown profiles (Fig. 2c). The authors should state how width and centre were extracted despite saturation (attenuated data, the Jungfrau detector), give upper bounds on transient changes in width and peak position, show full transient q-scans through the Bragg peak at several delays with position, width and integrated intensity, explain in the main text why the measured q points and their time dependence rule out AF2 satellites, and show quantitatively that the measured symmetry (Fig. 3f) excludes a shift toward the incommensurate wavevector by a fraction of the resolution.

7. **It is unclear whether Figs. 3j-n are measured or simulated, and either reading raises problems.** The caption says the panels are "derived from tr-RDS data using Eq. (2)", while the main text says "our simulations reveal ... (Figs. 3j-n and S8)". Fig. 3h and Figs. 3j-n also share a colour scale ending at 13 without units or a stated normalization. The authors should state clearly which panels are simulated and which are extracted, and for any extracted distribution show the inversion, its assumptions and uncertainties.

8. **The tr-NRDS evidence for phonon creation comes from conditions that do not match the tr-RDS experiment, and the timescales do not obviously match.** The nanosecond picture rests on matching the tr-NRDS enhancement at (0 0 -2), measured at ESRF, to the RXD/RDS recovery measured at SwissFEL (Fig. 4d). The tr-NRDS probe depth (~1.5 µm) is about three times the pump penetration depth (~500 nm at 88° incidence), while tr-RDS probes ~18 nm, so NRDS averages over a strongly inhomogeneous profile including unexcited bulk. The pump pulses differ (35 fs vs picosecond), the repetition rates differ (100 Hz vs 1 kHz, so steady-state heating may differ), and a quoted "3 mJ cm⁻²" may correspond to very different absorbed energy densities. The text says the NRDS enhancement appears "around 2 ns, a similar timescale to RDS signals", but the RXD/RDS recovery constant is ~7 ns, and the NRDS data are sparse with large error bars. The PM reference temperature is not given (the cryostream range is 190-300 K); if it is near 300 K, the equilibrium phonon population, heat capacity and diffuse background differ from 190 K. The authors should report absorbed energy density per unit volume for both setups, a depth-weighted estimate of the excited fraction seen by NRDS, the average heating at 1 kHz, the fitted NRDS rise and decay constants with uncertainties and why ~2 ns and ~7 ns count as matching, and the exact temperatures used for the AFM and PM traces in Fig. 4d and Fig. S11, explaining why the AFM/PM difference is not a temperature-dependent background effect. A PM reference just above T_N2 (about 235-240 K) would be the cleaner null control.

9. **All momentum-resolved results come from one crystal, one base temperature (190 K) and essentially one fluence (3 mJ/cm²).** Both proposed mechanisms have characteristic dependences: magnon-magnon thermalization rates depend on the initial magnon density and on the gap and thermal background, and the anomalous magnon-phonon process depends on thermal phonon occupation. Measuring τ_RDS(q) and the RXD recovery at a second fluence and a lower base temperature (for example 100-150 K) and comparing with the QBE prediction there would turn a consistency argument into a test. The authors should also state how many samples and beamtimes contributed, whether the key tr-RDS traces were reproduced on another crystal, sample spot or beamtime, and whether the signal changed after cumulative FEL and pump exposure (surface degradation).

10. **The ~7 ns recovery time is poorly constrained and has no error bar.** It comes from roughly 8-10 delay points out to 12 ns, at which point the RXD still shows about 30% of its maximum suppression (Fig. 4d); the "AFM RXD fit" visibly departs from the data between 2 and 8 ns. Because the 59 µeV coupling is derived from this timescale, its uncertainty carries directly into the coupling. The authors should state the functional form, the fitted time constants with uncertainties, and if possible add data out to about 50 ns.

12. **The ~0.5 eV energy integration has not been shown to leave only magnetic contributions.** The integration window includes elastic charge-disorder scattering, phonons coupled through resonant processes, and possibly bimagnon and multimagnon weight. The phase-factor argument at the half-integer position (Eq. 1) suppresses ordinary phonon diffuse scattering but not obviously pump-induced magnetoelastic or orbital/charge disorder that carries the magnetic periodicity. The authors should discuss and bound these contributions.

13. **The magnetic and phonon model behind the bottleneck argument is not shown in enough detail.** The simplified orthorhombic cell with one Cu per layer (J_AFM = 120 meV, J_FM = -15 meV, J_out = -18 meV, D = 0.025 meV) is said to reproduce the neutron dispersion (Fig. S1c), but it is not shown that it contains both the acoustic (~1.3 meV) and optical (~23.2 meV) magnon branches used in the text to rule out optical-magnon contributions, nor is the quoted 1.3 meV acoustic gap reconciled with the gap produced by D = 0.025 meV. The phase-space argument (Fig. 4a-c,e,f) depends on where the acoustic magnon and lowest optical phonon dispersions cross; the text names the "lowest-energy optical phonons" as the partner in the anomalous process, while magnons quasi-thermalized near the ~1.3 meV gap would need phonons of only a few meV. The authors should plot magnon and phonon dispersions together along the relevant directions with crossing points marked, give the phonon branch energies and the momentum region where energy and momentum are conserved, state whether the neglected monoclinic distortion changes these crossings, and give the magnon-phonon QBE parameters (phonon branches, coupling vertex) in the main Methods rather than only in Supplementary Note 10, since the 59 µeV coupling is a headline number.

14. **The generality and priority claims go beyond the evidence.** The abstract and Discussion state that the framework "generalizes across antiferromagnetic insulators", extends to "any gapped magnetic insulators, including ferromagnets", establishes "design principles", and that materials with overlapping dispersions "will exhibit faster recovery", on the basis of one material, one sample and one temperature. Statements such as "has not yet been experimentally demonstrated" and "first direct visualization" should be placed against earlier time-resolved resonant scattering of transient magnetic correlations and diffuse magnetic scattering in pumped magnets, and against tr-RIXS of transient magnons (e.g., ref. 40). The authors should present these as hypotheses or narrow them, or support them with simulations for at least one other gapped magnet with different magnon-phonon overlap.

17. **The self-similarity exponent is offered as validation with thin support.** The exponent 2.28 ± 0.08 (Fig. S10b) is compared with 2.33 from a theory of two-dimensional Heisenberg antiferromagnets (ref. 43), whereas CuO is quasi-one-dimensional, three-dimensionally ordered, gapped (~1.3 meV), anisotropic and here pumped with a finite pulse.
