# Merged scientific review

## A. Must address

1. **It is not shown that the "RDS" upturn is diffuse magnon scattering rather than a pump-induced change in the shape of the magnetic Bragg peak.** All tr-RDS points lie 0.01–0.08 r.l.u. from the (1/2 0 -1/2) peak, whose top saturates the detector (Fig. 2C caption; hatched region in Fig. 3). The main text (p. 4) rules out a change of correlation length because "the (1/2 0 -1/2) RXD peak width remains nearly constant while the tail intensity rises (Fig. 2C)". With the top of the profile missing, neither width, position nor integrated intensity can be judged from that figure. Several lineshape effects would raise the tail intensity with a delayed, q-dependent rise. (i) Transient thermal or magnetoelastic strain: the X-ray probe depth is ~18 nm and the pump depth ~450 nm, so strain fronts cross the probed layer in a few picoseconds, the timescale of the upturn (τ_RDS up to ~4 ps, Fig. 3F). Inhomogeneous strain broadens the peak symmetrically in ±q, reproducing the symmetric amplitude profile (Fig. 3F) and a rise that is faster far from q_RXD. Because the surface normal (1 0 -1) is close to q_RXD, a strain-induced shift matters most along [h 0 h]. That path is measured only on the negative-q side (Fig. S9A), so the symmetry test is missing exactly where it matters, and Fig. S9 shows the largest upturns, which grow almost linearly up to 4 ps. (ii) A change in the magnetic correlation length, mosaic or domain structure. (iii) A small shift of the magnetic propagation vector. The resonance (Fig. 2E), π > σ dependence (Fig. 2G) and fluence threshold (Fig. 2F) show that the signal is magnetic, not that it is diffuse rather than a magnetic Bragg tail; the paper itself notes that the polarization dependence mirrors the RXD (Fig. S5). Because the APDs integrate over energy, quasi-elastic scattering from static short-range magnetic disorder and domain walls is also collected. The authors should provide unsaturated (attenuated) θ–2θ and rocking profiles, or 2D maps with the 0.5M Jungfrau already used for Fig. S7, at 3 mJ/cm² and several delays (e.g. 0, 1, 4, 20, 100 ps, 1 ns); fit peak position, width and integrated intensity versus delay; test whether any combination of broadening and shift reproduces the tail increase and show that the upturn survives after subtracting the fitted peak; measure both signs of q along [h 0 h]; compare the measured q-profile of the upturn with that predicted by Eqs. 1–2 for magnon populations (e.g. the weighting by A_μ(q)/ω(q)); and add a temperature null control at the same detuned momentum just above T_N2 and well below T_N1, to test whether the upturn and its q-dependent time constant follow the magnon population. Without these, the central identification of the signal as magnon scattering is not secure.

2. **The thermal versus non-thermal interpretation lacks an equilibrium temperature calibration, and the probed layer may sit close to T_N1.** The sample is at 190 K and the authors' own estimate of heating is up to ~20 K (p. S7), an average over the pumped volume; the 18 nm probed surface layer, where absorbed energy density is highest, could therefore reach ~210 K or more, within a few kelvin of T_N1 (213 K in the text, 214.5 K in Fig. S3). Near T_N, equilibrium critical magnetic diffuse scattering at small |q| grows steeply with temperature, and a delayed rise peaked at small |q| with a q-dependent rise time is what one would expect if the probed layer were simply heating towards T_N1. This also bears on the ns-scale RDS signal of Fig. 4D: at 12 ns the RXD has recovered to about −10%, after the energy should have passed to the lattice, and the probed layer stays hot for ≫10 ns, so thermal recovery of the order is an alternative to the proposed magnon-phonon channel. The authors should measure the static (unpumped) RDS intensity at the q points of Fig. 3A and the (1/2 0 -1/2) RXD intensity versus temperature from ~150 K through T_N1 and T_N2 on this crystal; use them to convert the pumped values at the 4 ps plateau, at 4–100 ps and at 10–12 ns into effective temperatures; test whether RDS and RXD at late delays match a single equilibrium temperature (thermal magnons) or not (non-thermal distribution); show that these temperatures agree with the stated heating; and redo the heating estimate for the 18 nm probed layer. This is the central test of the "non-thermal → quasi-thermal" claim; without it the ps dynamics could equally be read as fast local heating plus critical fluctuations.

## B. Should address

3. **The agreement between the computed anomalous magnon-phonon rate and the ~7 ns recovery is set by an assumed coupling constant, so the "dispersion-mismatch bottleneck" is inferred rather than tested.** On p. S17 the coupling is fixed at c^x = 1 eV/Å for both the normal and anomalous processes, without justification. The rate scales as |c^x|², so the absolute rates in Fig. 4E/F and Fig. S17 (~0.2 ns⁻¹) do not independently test the measured recovery; only the relative phase-space structure is a prediction. The "effective magnon-phonon coupling ~59 μeV" (p. 9) is back-derived from the measured time constant with no stated formula or inputs, and its comparison with the 6.2 meV spin-phonon coupling from Raman (ref. 44) mixes quantities with different definitions. The claim that the effective coupling is small because of phase-space restriction rather than a small microscopic matrix element is therefore not demonstrated, and the conclusion that anomalous scattering dominates depends on the assumed equality of the anomalous and normal couplings. Only the initial rate Γ(q) at t = 0 is computed. The authors should fix the vertex independently (e.g. ∇J from frozen-phonon DFT+U displacements of the lowest optical modes, which they already run, the measured spin-phonon shifts of ref. 44, or magnetostriction); show that it gives nanosecond rates in Eq. S27; give the derivation of the 59 μeV value; show the sensitivity of "anomalous scattering dominates" to the ratio c^anomalous/c^normal; and compute the full time-evolved QBE recovery curve for comparison with the RXD and RDS traces of Fig. 4D.

4. **Long-lived photocarriers are not excluded, either as a magnon-scattering channel on picosecond timescales or as the cause of the persistent demagnetization and its ns recovery.** The paper excludes magnon-electron scattering (p. 6) and assumes doublons decay within ~200 fs using refs. 30, 31, which concern other copper oxides, not CuO; no measurement on CuO is shown. In a ~1.4–1.5 eV charge-transfer insulator, photo-holes and electrons can localize as polarons or trapped carriers and survive for ps to ns. Through the spin-charge coupling of Eq. S5 they could redistribute magnons, keep the order suppressed, and set the 7 ns recovery through recombination; the authors themselves invoke defect-related electronic thermal conductivity in this sample (p. S10). Without excluding this, step (3) of the conclusions, recovery via anomalous magnon-phonon scattering, is not uniquely supported. The authors should measure the photocarrier lifetime on this crystal at the same fluence, for example by transient optical reflectivity/absorption or time-resolved Cu L3 / O K XAS (edge shift or bleach) across ps–ns, which the SwissFEL setup can do, and show that carriers have decayed well before the RXD recovers.

5. **The comparison between the magnon-magnon kinetic theory and the momentum-resolved data is only visual.** The evidence for picosecond quasi-thermalization is the side-by-side comparison of Fig. 3E (data, 0–4 ps, arbitrary units) and Fig. 3G (simulation, 0–6 ps, colour scale 0–800), plus the statement that 1/τ_RDS is "proportional to momentum". The simulated rise times are never extracted with the same two-exponential procedure and overlaid on Fig. 3F/3I, and simulated and measured traces are never compared on a common scale with a single overall factor. The authors should fit the simulated traces exactly as the data are fitted and overlay τ_RDS(q) and A_RDS(q) from theory and experiment on Fig. 3F/3I, along both b* and [h 0 h]; show simulated and measured time traces at matched q with one scale factor; and include the instrument momentum resolution in the simulation.

6. **The excitation strength in the simulation does not match the measured demagnetization.** B₀Δn(t) was tuned "to reproduce the experimentally observed reduction of the AFM order by approximately 50%" (p. S13). Fig. 2D at the same 3 mJ/cm² shows a ~28% drop in RXD intensity; since intensity scales as M², this is roughly a 15% reduction of the sublattice moment. If the simulation is overdriven by a factor of ~3, the magnon density, and with it the quartic magnon-magnon scattering rate, is overestimated, which bears directly on the claimed picosecond quasi-thermalization. The authors should reconcile the numbers and rerun the QBE with the excitation calibrated to the measured RXD drop.

7. **The two-exponential decomposition and the extracted τ_RDS(q), the key observable, are not robust as presented.** Eq. S3, A·exp(−t/τ), decays to zero and cannot describe a step-like drop or the rising curves of Figs. S6C/F; the actual fitted function, including any (1 − e^(−t/τ)) form, convolution with the ~60 fs instrument response and time-zero handling, must be stated. The fit window is 4 ps (Fig. 3A, Fig. S9A), while τ_RDS reaches ~4 ps near q = 0.01 (Fig. 3F) and ~13 ps with A_RDS ≈ 18 along [h 0 h] (Fig. S9E); where τ approaches or exceeds the window, amplitude and time constant are strongly correlated and the fit error bars may understate the uncertainty. These near-Γ points carry the linear 1/τ ∝ q claim (Fig. 3I), whose lines are only "guide to the eyes". Fig. S8 shows that the upturn is non-monotonic (maximum ~5 ps, plateau after ~10 ps), so a single exponential may bias τ. The decomposition also assumes the magnon contribution is zero just after excitation, whereas the authors' own model (Fig. S14B, Fig. 3J) creates magnons across the zone instantaneously; a prompt positive RDS step would be absorbed into the negative "RXD" term. The authors should report the A–τ covariances; use longer delay scans (as in Fig. S8) at several q, especially small |q|; test whether τ_RDS(q) survives a global fit with the q-independent RXD time constant (Fig. 3D) shared across q and a fit that allows a prompt RDS step; and either fit a linear model to 1/τ_RDS(q) with uncertainties or soften "proportional to momentum".

8. **The polarization test does not distinguish transverse magnon fluctuations from longitudinal (Bragg-like) magnetic scattering.** The text argues that the π/σ dependence of the upturn (Fig. 2G) is "similar to the magnetic RXD profile (Fig. S5)" and takes this as proof of magnetic origin. The measured π/σ ratio at 4 ps (~6) is close to the static RXD ratio in Fig. S5B (~3.1/0.5). Magnons are fluctuations transverse to the b-axis moments, so Eq. 1 generally predicts a ratio different from that of the ordered moment; as it stands, agreement with the RXD ratio arguably favours a Bragg-tail origin. Using the geometric tensor F (Eq. S37), the authors should compute the expected σ and π RDS intensities from transverse S^xx and S^zz correlations and the expected Bragg ratio from S^yy, and compare both with Figs. 2G and S5B.
Raised by: r1, r3

9. **The tr-NRDS evidence for phonon creation by magnon annihilation comes from an experiment not matched to the RXD/RDS conditions, with an unmatched paramagnetic control.** The NRDS data (Fig. 4D top) were taken at a different facility with a picosecond 1 kHz pump instead of 35 fs at 100 Hz (with possible average heating), ~100 ps X-ray pulses, pump incidence near 88°, a probe depth of ~1.5 μm against ~500 nm pump penetration (versus 18 nm for RXD/RDS), and no stated fluence definition. The "similar timescale" of the NRDS and RDS signals is therefore a comparison between different depth-averaged volumes and excitation densities. The PM control is at 300 K, 110 K warmer, where phonon populations, heat capacity, Debye-Waller factor, absorption, thermal conductivity and thermal expansion all differ. The authors should add a PM reference just above T_N2 (~235–250 K) and an AF2 reference, quantify the deposited energy at each temperature and the steady-state heating at 1 kHz, and state the fluence definition and ESRF pump parameters.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

10. **The NRDS kinetics do not obviously match the proposed energy flow.** If phonons were created at the magnon annihilation rate (~7 ns) and dissipated over ~0.7 μs (Fig. S11), the phonon population should accumulate over ~10 ns; instead the NRDS peaks at ~1–2 ns and then decays. Moreover, NRDS within 0.1 r.l.u. of (0 0 −2) is dominated by low-energy acoustic phonons (intensity ∝ n/ω²), whereas the anomalous process is said to emit the lowest optical phonons, so the observable is at best indirect. The authors should fit RXD, RDS and NRDS jointly with a coupled rate-equation model (with rates taken from the QBE) that includes magnon → phonon transfer and phonon redistribution, and check the energy budget: magnon energy lost against phonon population gained.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

11. **The NRDS analysis does not exclude lattice artefacts, and its key features are weakly constrained.** The region of interest between the ellipses in Fig. S10C was chosen by hand to avoid a Bragg-peak shift, yet panels 2 and 4 of Fig. S10 (0.15 and 0.6 ns) show clear dipolar, shift-like patterns. CuO has strong magnetoelastic coupling, so thermal expansion and magnetostriction differ between the AFM and PM phases; strain from the recovering order could broaden (0 0 −2) inhomogeneously and raise intensity in the annular ROI without a peak shift. The AFM enhancement at ~2 ns rests on a handful of delays with large error bars, and the ~160 ps feature, read as the "phonon quasi-thermalization timescale", sits at the 100 ps resolution limit. The authors should show radial and azimuthal (transverse) profiles versus delay, the fitted Bragg-peak position versus delay in both phases, the sensitivity of the NRDS trace to the ROI choice, and fit uncertainties for the 2 ns and 160 ps time constants.

12. **The fluence scale is inconsistent between figures.** Fig. 2D shows ΔI/I ≈ −0.28 at 3 mJ/cm² (log₁₀ ≈ 3.48 in μJ/cm²), but the Fig. 2F axis ends near log f ≈ 3.3–3.4 (~2–2.5 mJ/cm²), where ΔI/I ≈ −0.5 is already reached at a similar delay (4.5 ps vs ~4 ps). All momentum-resolved and ns data were taken at 3 mJ/cm², outside the plotted scan, and the RDS amplitude peaks at log f ≈ 2.7–3 and falls above it, so the main data may sit on the falling side where the authors themselves invoke approach to T_N. The authors should state the log base and units; mark 3 mJ/cm² on Fig. 2F; define the fluence (incident or absorbed, pump and probe spot sizes, footprint correction at 63.5° incidence, reflectivity); explain the difference between datasets (different runs, footprint, sample position); reconcile these numbers with each other and with the ~50% assumed in the simulations (concern 6); and give the fluence used for the high-excitation data in Fig. S7.

13. **Momentum-resolved data exist at only one temperature (190 K) and one fluence (3 mJ/cm²), so the proposed scattering hierarchy is not tested through its predicted dependences.** Magnon-magnon rates scale with magnon density, so τ_RDS(q) should shorten with fluence. The anomalous magnon-phonon rate depends on the occupation of the lowest optical phonons and thermal magnons, so τ_RDS(q) and the ~7 ns recovery should depend on temperature in a calculable way; the Bose factor in Eq. 2 also makes the equilibrium RDS baseline temperature dependent. The dispersion-overlap principle is not checked even within CuO, e.g. by temperature tuning of phonon populations. The authors should measure τ_RDS(q) at two or three fluences, and tr-RDS and long-delay RXD at one or two lower base temperatures (e.g. 100 K and 150 K, which also moves away from T_N1; concern 2), and compare each with QBE predictions for the same conditions.

14. **The energy dependence of the RDS upturn follows the absorption maximum, not the magnetic RXD resonance (Fig. 2E).** The upturn peaks at ~931.7 eV, coinciding with the XAS maximum, whereas the RXD peaks near 930 eV and dips at 933 eV from self-absorption. Magnon-related resonant scattering is governed by the same dipole matrix elements as the magnetic Bragg intensity, so the difference needs an explanation, for example a calculated resonance profile for the inelastic channel or self-absorption corrections for each geometry. It also needs a test excluding a pump-induced change in absorption, fluorescence or self-absorption that would scale with the local tail intensity, e.g. time-resolved XAS in fluorescence yield at the same fluence.

15. **Detector linearity is not demonstrated where it matters most.** The largest RDS amplitudes and longest τ_RDS occur closest to q_RXD (Fig. 3F; Fig. S9E), in the APD channels nearest the saturated, hatched region of Fig. 3. Single-shot XFEL pulses can drive avalanche photodiodes non-linear. The authors should provide a linearity calibration (an attenuator series at those q) and describe the saturation handling in Methods.

17. **The simulated momentum dependence near Γ may be set by grid spacing and broadening.** The QBE is solved on a 32 × 48 × 32 grid over the magnetic zone, a spacing of ~0.02–0.03 r.l.u., while the experimental points are at q = 0.010, 0.015, 0.020 and 0.025 r.l.u. The δ-function is broadened with a 3.0 meV kernel (p. S15), comparable to the acoustic magnon energies at these q (spin gap ~1.3 meV, Fig. S13C). The simulated near-Γ dependence in Fig. 3G/H and Fig. S16 is therefore largely interpolation and broadening. The authors should show convergence with grid density and kernel width.

18. **The model's magnon branch structure cannot test the acoustic-branch assignment.** The simplified orthorhombic model has two degenerate magnon branches (p. 21. **Twin and antiferromagnetic domains, and their walls, are not quantified as a source of diffuse intensity.** The polarized-microscope image (Fig. S4) shows a dense set of twin lamellae across the face, at odds with "a minimal volume of twin domains"; magnetic domains are not characterised. Domain walls and finite domain size give static, quasi-elastic diffuse intensity near q_RXD that can change on photoexcitation. The authors should give the twin volume fraction, the X-ray footprint relative to the domain size, where the twin-domain magnetic reflections and domain-wall scattering fall relative to the detuned positions, the pump-off diffuse intensity at the measured q, and an argument that pump-induced domain-wall dynamics do not contribute.
