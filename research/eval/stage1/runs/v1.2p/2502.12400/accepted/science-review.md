A. Must address

1. The agreement between the fitted ξ = 586 nm and the dirty-limit coherence length (≈570 nm) rests on transport parameters that the paper states inconsistently. The coherence-length section uses n = 6×10¹² cm⁻² and ℓ = 130 nm "from magnetotransport data", which give v_F ≈ 3×10⁶ m/s, ξ_BCS ≈ 2.5 µm and √(ξ_BCS ℓ) ≈ 570 nm. The Methods instead report n = 8.14×10¹¹ cm⁻², μ = 9.86×10³ cm²/Vs and ℓ_e = 147 nm from van der Pauw measurements on the same wafer. Those give v_F ≈ 1.1×10⁶ m/s, ξ_BCS ≈ 0.9 µm and √(ξ_BCS ℓ) ≈ 370 nm, well below the fitted 586 nm. A density of 6×10¹² cm⁻² and v_F = 3×10⁶ m/s are also unusually high for an InAs 2DEG. Further, it is not stated which medium's coherence length is relevant (the proximitised 2DEG under the Al, or the 10 nm Al film itself, which would be much shorter), and the estimate uses the bulk Al gap Δ = 1.764 k_BT_c ≈ 250 µeV rather than the smaller induced gap Δ*, which changes ξ substantially; the mean free path in the InAs under the Al is not measured. Since the quantitative match is the main evidence for the headline conclusion that I_c(W_sc) reflects the induced coherence length of the Andreev bound states, the authors should reconcile the two sets of parameters (for example, density under the Al versus a bare-2DEG or gated sample, or a different wafer), state which density, mobility, mean free path and gap apply under the contacts and where each comes from (e.g. gated magnetotransport, tunnelling or MAR for Δ*), justify the choice of gap, and propagate the uncertainties in n, ℓ and Δ into a range for the predicted ξ. A single table of material parameters with their sources would avoid this confusion.

B. Should address

2. Each contact width is represented by a single junction, and device-to-device spread is never characterised (Figs. 3, 4, 5). The I_c(W_sc) fit in Fig. 3b has four non-trivial points plus a reference point, so the conclusions that I_c follows Eq. (2), that max η increases with W_sc, and that B_η saturates are exposed to ordinary device-to-device variation. InAs/Al planar junctions on one chip commonly differ in I_c, R_n, transparency and diode efficiency by 10–30%, from the actual junction length W_n (an 80 nm wet-etched gap to which I_c is very sensitive), interface transparency, local density, Al etch undercut or damage, or disorder at the S/N interface. The data already show non-monotonic behaviour that may be this spread: in Fig. 5b max η is about 10% at W_sc = 0.9 µm but about 8.5% at 1.2 µm (the text concedes it "does not follow a clear exponential trend"), and the V_g = 0 values of B_* in Fig. 3c are non-monotonic (about 22, 9, 8, 7 and 13 mT). Yet the abstract states that "both the critical current and the diode efficiency increase with contact width". The Methods mention multiple cooldowns but do not say whether I_c, max η, B_η and B_* were reproduced across them. The authors should measure at least a second junction for some widths (ideally the extremes, 0.3 and 1.2 µm) or show reproducibility across thermal cycles; report R_n, I_cR_n, the excess current or another transparency measure, and the measured W_n for every device, so the W_sc trend can be separated from transparency or W_n variation; and, if the η trend cannot be shown to exceed device scatter, qualify the abstract and conclusions and state plainly that each width is one device.

3. Kinetic inductance and current feeding along the narrow Al strips are an untested alternative explanation for both I_c(W_sc) and B_η(W_sc). The contacts are 10 nm thick, 4 µm long strips of width W_sc fed from a T-shaped stem (Fig. 1a). The sheet kinetic inductance of such a film is of order 10 pH/□; for W_sc = 0.3 µm (about 13 squares) the kinetic inductance can be comparable to Φ₀/I_c, giving a phase drop along the junction of order a radian at I ≈ 2 µA, about four times smaller for 1.2 µm. A phase gradient along the junction makes the current non-uniform, lowers the measured I_c and shifts the Fraunhofer pattern; the authors themselves attribute B_⊥+ ≠ B_⊥− to a slightly asymmetric J_c(x). Because the gradient scales as 1/W_sc it could mimic the "coherence-length" suppression of Eq. (2) and contribute to the 1/W_sc scaling of B_η. The paper says kinetic inductance is "expected to be non-negligible" only in the discussion of the single-mode estimates and does not estimate it. The authors should report the Al normal-state sheet resistance and estimate the sheet kinetic inductance (from R_□ and T_c) and the phase drop along each lead at I_c; state whether the two contacts are fed from the same or opposite ends of the junction; and use the Fraunhofer data already measured (lobe periods, lifting of minima, the B_⊥+/B_⊥− offset versus W_sc) to bound the current non-uniformity.

4. A width-dependent induced gap is a further untested explanation for the I_c(W_sc) trend. A 10 nm Al strip only 0.3 µm wide sits on a large proportion of normal InAs, so inverse proximity or finite-size effects may suppress its gap and superfluid density more than in wide contacts; edge damage from the Al etch could act similarly. Either would lower I_c for reasons other than Andreev-bound-state leakage past the distal contact edge. The authors should report the induced gap of each device, for example from multiple Andreev reflection features or from the temperature at which I_c vanishes, to exclude a width-dependent gap, and discuss etch damage. A BdG or tight-binding model of the T-geometry with a bare 2DEG beyond the contacts would strengthen the link between Eq. (2) and ξ.

5. The orbital interpretation of B_η's 1/W_sc dependence is not supported over the alternatives, and none of the quantitative estimates match the data (Fig. 5c, Eqs. 4 and 6). The authors note that any finite-momentum mechanism has zeroes of η set by the resonance condition 2q(2W_sc + W_n) = (2n+1)π/2; since q_M, q_orb and q_RZ are each linear in B_∥, all three predict B_η ∝ 1/(2W_sc + W_n). The flux area could likewise be (2W_sc + W_n)·d or a ξ-limited width rather than W_sc·d, which sets the inferred Φ/Φ₀. The orbital finite-q estimate of Eq. (6) gives B_η ≈ 4 mT with no W_sc dependence, yet the W_sc dependence is attributed to orbital flux; the three single-mode estimates (≈13 mT Meissner, ≈4 mT orbital, ≈690 mT Rashba–Zeeman) bracket but do not match the observed 90–270 mT. The orbital picture also predicts a sign change at Φ = Φ₀ and Φ₀-periodic structure, which are not seen; the observed sign changes at 400–600 mT correspond to several Φ₀ (e.g. ≈3.7 Φ₀ for W_sc = 0.9 µm, d = 17 nm, at 0.5 T). The authors should fit both the 1/W_sc and 1/(2W_sc + W_n) forms and compare their quality, justify the flux area, and ideally compare quantitatively with a multimode BdG or tight-binding calculation of the actual geometry (finite W_sc, W_n = 80 nm, 4 µm width, Rashba, Zeeman and orbital vector potential) that reproduces both the scale and slope of B_η. Otherwise they should state plainly that the mechanism setting B_η is not identified and temper the abstract and Conclusion statement that the W_sc-dependence "invites an orbital interpretation".

6. The analysis of I_c(W_sc) in Fig. 3b needs more transparency. The ordinate 1/ln[I_c^∞/(I_c^∞ − max I_c)] equals W_sc⁻¹·ξ only if I_c^∞ is known; it is taken from the W_sc → ∞ device, so that point sits at the origin by construction and should not be shown as data, and ξ is in practice set by four points that all depend on this single normalisation. For the wider devices I_c^∞ − I_c is small, so the linearisation strongly amplifies errors in I_c^∞; a rough reading of Fig. 3a gives single-device ξ values between about 0.45 and 0.6 µm. Fig. 3b has no error bars and no uncertainty is quoted on ξ = 586 nm. The authors should state how I_c^∞ was obtained, show error bars including the uncertainty of I_c^∞, fit Eq. (2) directly to I_c(W_sc) with I_c^∞ free and show that ξ is robust, quote ξ with its uncertainty, and compare with at least one alternative functional form (for example the kinetic-phase suppression of concern 3).

9. The gate dependence of B_* is attributed to Rashba α and transparency τ via Eq. (3), but neither is measured (Figs. 3c and 4). The right-hand axis of Fig. 4 asserts that α increases and τ decreases with V_g; the α trend is cited from ref. 46 on a different sample and the τ trend has no supporting data. A gate also changes k_F, E_T and possibly g*, and strongly changes I_c (Fig. 1g); since B_* ∝ k_so/k_F = αm*/(ħ²k_F), it falls with rising density unless α rises faster. The statement that the gate dependence "lends credence to a spin-orbit coupling based interpretation" is therefore not established. The authors should provide gate-dependent density and τ (or E_T) for each device, e.g. from R_n, I_cR_n, excess current or MAR versus V_g, and ideally α(V_g) from weak antilocalisation on the same wafer and gate stack, then compare B_*(V_g) quantitatively with Eq. (3) for at least one device. The fit to "Eq. (1) of ref. 8" that yields B_* should be written out in the supplement with its free and fixed parameters.

10. Max η is compared at very different fields, which confounds its W_sc-dependence. η peaks at about 90 mT for wide contacts and about 270 mT for W_sc = 0.3 µm; at higher B_∥, Zeeman and orbital pair breaking suppress the induced gap and I_c (Fig. 3a). The smaller max η of narrow devices may therefore reflect where the peak sits on the field axis rather than an intrinsic geometric reduction of nonreciprocity. The authors should compare η at fixed B_∥, as a function of B_∥W_sc, or after normalising by I_c(B_∥)/I_c(0), and discuss whether the W_sc-dependence of max η survives.

12. The gate voltages are not matched across devices, and the gate's effect may depend on W_sc (Figs. 3c, 4, 5c). The "V_g < 0" series mixes different voltages (roughly −4, −3.5 and −3 V in Fig. 4), and near pinch-off (≈ −5 V in Fig. 1g) small offsets in V_g mean large changes in density. The Al contacts screen the gate, so the density change for a given V_g may differ between widths; and if the gate acts on the bare 2DEG beyond the distal edge of the Al strips it also tunes the Andreev reflection that sets the W_sc-dependence. The 0.3 µm device has no V_g = +10 V point. The authors should list the exact V_g for each point, explain why the values differ, state which regions the gate covers, convert V_g to density or to normalised I_c(V_g)/I_c(0) (or compare against R_n or Hall density) for each device, and explain the missing 10 V point.

13. The switching-current statistics and the error bars are under-specified (Methods, Fig. 1h, Figs. 3 and 5). The error bars are the RMS residual of a local fit to the Fraunhofer extremum, propagated "assuming zero covariance". This does not capture switching-current stochasticity, premature switching, sweep-to-sweep reproducibility or drift; asymmetric switching histograms can produce or mask a few-percent η. I_c+ and I_c− come from the same sweeps and the same fitting procedure, so their errors are likely correlated, and assuming zero covariance may misstate the error on η. The authors should state the number of switching events or sweeps per point, the ramp rate and filtering, report the switching-distribution width at a representative field, and check that η is unchanged when the median of repeated switching events is used.

15. Temperature dependence is missing. All data are at about 30 mK base temperature, and the electron temperature is not given. ξ in Eq. (2) depends on temperature through the induced gap (ξ ∝ √(ħD/Δ)), and the mechanisms the paper weighs (Meissner, orbital finite-q, Rashba–Zeeman finite-q) predict different temperature dependences of η and B_η, while a kinetic-inductance mechanism would not follow ξ(T). Measuring I_c(W_sc), and ideally η(B_∥) and B_η(W_sc), at two or three temperatures (e.g. 0.5–1 K) would test whether the extracted length behaves like a coherence length and help separate the mechanisms.
