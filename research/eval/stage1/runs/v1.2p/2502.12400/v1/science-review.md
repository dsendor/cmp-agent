A. Must address

1. The cross-device trends rest on one junction per contact width, with no device-level characterization, so they are not separated from device-to-device variation (Figs. 2, 3, 4). Every conclusion of the paper (I_c rising with W_sc, max|η| rising with W_sc, the W_sc dependence of B_η and B_*) is a trend across five devices, each with a different W_sc, and no geometry is repeated. In nominally identical planar Al/InAs junctions, I_c typically varies by 4–20% from device to device, because of interface transparency, local density, 2DEG disorder, etch quality and the effective junction length after the Al wet etch. No normal-state resistance R_n, I_cR_n product, excess current or transparency estimate is reported for any device, so these variations cannot be normalized out, even though τ enters Eq. (2) and ξ_ABS directly. The danger is visible in the data. Fig. 4b is non-monotonic: max|η| is about 10% at W_sc = 0.9 µm but about 8.5% at 1.2 µm. Apart from the W_sc → ∞ device (about 16.5%), max|η| ranges only from about 5% to 10%, so the "general trend" and the abstract's statement that diode efficiency increases with contact width rest largely on one device. Peak η also roughly doubles between 1.2 µm and W_sc → ∞, although I_c is already about 90% saturated at 1.2 µm; this jump is not explained. The fitted ξ is also sensitive to device scatter: with I_c^∞ fixed by a single device, a ±5% change in I_c^∞ changes 1/ln(I_c^∞/(I_c^∞ − I_c)) for the 1.2 µm device by roughly ±15–20%. The authors should tabulate R_n, I_cR_n, the excess current (or a transparency estimate) for every device, show I_c(W_sc) normalized by 1/R_n, and ideally add at least a second device for some widths (for example 0.3 µm and 1.2 µm, or on a second chip) so that the spread in I_c, max|η|, B_* and B_η can be compared with the size of the claimed trends. They should state whether all devices were measured in the same cooldown and whether the key quantities survive thermal cycling (T_c is quoted as "measured during multiple cooldowns", but the device data are not tied to specific cooldowns). Otherwise the abstract's claim about η should be qualified.

2. The orbital interpretation of B_η, and the claimed saturation of B_η for W_sc ≳ 2ξ, are not supported by Fig. 4c and conflict with the paper's own estimates (Section IV, Conclusion, abstract). Section IV and the Conclusion say the W_sc dependence of B_η "invites an orbital interpretation", based on the flux relation B_∥W_sc d = Φ (Eq. 3) and the fit in Fig. 4c. Several problems undermine this. (i) The fit is not Eq. (3): Eq. (3) gives B_η = Φ/(W_sc d), a line through the origin in W_sc⁻¹, but the fitted line has a free intercept of about 25 mT at W_sc⁻¹ = 0, which a pure flux picture does not allow. (ii) The fit does not test the model: Φ and d are both free, so the fit fixes only Φ/d, and the quoted Φ/Φ0 ≈ 0.36–0.61 follows entirely from the assumed d = 10–17 nm. No model is given that predicts the flux at which η should peak. (iii) The orbital picture's own signatures are absent: the paper concedes that the η sign changes do not occur at Φ = Φ0 and that no Φ0-periodic oscillation is seen. The sign changes occur at 400–600 mT for all devices; for W_sc = 0.9 µm and d = 15 nm, Φ0 corresponds to about 150 mT, roughly a factor of 3 below the observed crossings. Attributing the missing oscillations to Zeeman collapse of the induced gap is not tested, and the paper does not report the crossing fields against W_sc. (iv) The paper's quantitative orbital estimate contradicts the data: q_orb = (e/ħ)B_∥d does not depend on W_sc, and with Eq. (5) it predicts B^max ≈ 4 mT, 25–70 times smaller than the measured B_η ≈ 90–270 mT, and it should apply to the W_sc → ∞ device, which has B_η ≈ 90–115 mT. The Meissner (13 mT) and Rashba–Zeeman (690 mT) estimates also fail to reproduce the magnitude or the W_sc scaling. The text admits that none of the estimates is a good match, yet the statement that q_orb > q_M > q_RZ suggests "orbital effects are strong", and the Conclusion keeps the orbital attribution; the sentence comparing the estimates with Fig. 4c must at least be checked, since if it reads as claiming a good match it is incorrect, and it is the only quantitative link between model and data in the section. (v) 1/W_sc scaling does not single out the orbital mechanism: Meissner screening and vortex entry in the Al strip cross-section (d_sc × W_sc) also give field scales that go as 1/W_sc for an in-plane field along the strip. (vi) The saturation claim rests on one point: the W_sc → ∞ point is excluded from the fit, there are no data between W_sc = 1.2 µm and "∞", and the 1.2 µm point sits at about 1/(2ξ), so the onset of saturation could lie anywhere between 1.2 µm and the real width of the "∞" contact; the dashed (2ξ)⁻¹ line is not tested. The authors should either (a) give a model, for example a flux-threaded SNS/interferometer picture or a BdG/tight-binding simulation with finite-width contacts, that predicts B_η(W_sc) including its prefactor and intercept and the magnitude of B_η, and reconcile the flux picture (Eq. 3) with the finite-momentum picture (Eq. 5); or (b) present the 1/W_sc trend as empirical ("B_η decreases with W_sc") and soften the orbital attribution and the word "saturates" in the abstract and Conclusion. In either case they should fit B_η with and without a free intercept and report parameter uncertainties; plot the η sign-change field against 1/W_sc for every device, which should scale like B_η with a fixed ratio if the flux picture holds; plot the "∞" point at the real contact width; add devices with W_sc between about 1.2 and 5 µm; and add a control that separates orbital from Meissner and vortex mechanisms, such as a different Al thickness or 2DEG depth, or rotating the in-plane field toward the current direction (see concern 8).

B. Should address

3. Identifying the fitted 586 nm with the induced coherence length rests on a heuristic model and unjustified assumptions, and alternatives are not excluded (Section III, Eq. 1, Fig. 2b). (i) Eq. (1) is not derived: it is motivated by a reflection "probability" proportional to the wavefunction "amplitude" ψ ∝ exp(−W_sc/ξ). A probability would normally go as |ψ|² ∝ exp(−2W_sc/ξ), which would double the extracted length to about 1.2 µm and remove the quoted agreement. Existing finite-contact-width theory (Refs. 31–33) is not compared quantitatively. (ii) The fitted length is ξ_ABS = ξ/(√τ|sin(φ/2)|), which reduces to ξ only for τ → 1 and φ_c → π, yet Section III invokes τ < 1 and gate-dependent τ. The measured I_c ≈ 5 µA for a 4 µm wide junction at n = 6 × 10¹² cm⁻² is only about 10% of the ballistic estimate (k_F W/π)(eΔ/ħ) ≈ 45 µA, pointing to moderate transparency or a reduced induced gap; if τ < 1, ξ_ABS exceeds ξ and the agreement with 570 nm becomes accidental. (iii) The dirty-limit estimate uses the Al bulk gap 1.764 k_BT_c rather than the induced gap, and the density, v_F and ℓ = 130 nm of the bare 2DEG rather than of the Al-covered region; it also drops order-unity prefactors (with D = v_Fℓ/2, ξ = √(ħD/Δ) gives about 1.25 times the quoted value), so the 3% agreement is well inside the uncertainty of the estimate. (iv) A 4 µm wide junction carries many transverse modes with different longitudinal velocities, hence a distribution of decay lengths, not one. (v) Eq. (1) assumes a reflecting rear boundary; if the 2DEG continues behind the contacts (Fig. 1a appears to show semiconductor beyond the Al strips), the rear boundary is an S/N interface into a normal reservoir, and the suppression of I_c would reflect quasiparticle leakage, a parallel normal path or inverse proximity, with a decay length that need not be the coherence length. Gap suppression in narrow Al strips, current crowding where the strips meet the leads, and kinetic-inductance phase drops (concern 4) would also reduce I_c for narrow contacts. (vi) The fit is effectively one free parameter on four points: the W_sc → ∞ point sits at the origin by construction and is not a data point, every point depends on the single measured I_c^∞, and no uncertainty or residuals are given for ξ. (vii) I_c is taken at the field extremum B_*, where diode and finite-momentum effects already act, rather than at B_∥ = 0. The authors should state the mesa layout behind the contacts; estimate τ from I_cR_n or the excess current; fit I_c^∞ as a free parameter (or propagate its uncertainty) and quote the uncertainty of ξ including its correlation with I_c^∞; mark the W_sc → ∞ point as not a data point; repeat the analysis with I_c(B_∥ = 0); justify Eq. (1) and the length that enters it, for example with a BdG/tight-binding calculation of a junction with finite-width proximitized contacts; fit ξ at the other gate voltages (the data in S.IV exist), where ξ should be largely gate-independent if the gate is screened under the Al, or should track √(v_Fℓ/Δ) as density changes; measure I_c(W_sc) at a second temperature to check that the length scales with Δ(T); and present the 570 nm comparison as order-of-magnitude consistency.

4. The current distribution and phase drop along the narrow contact strips in the T-geometry are not considered (Fig. 1a, Section IV). Current enters each contact strip from a central lead and must spread along the 4 µm junction width through a 10 nm thick Al strip only 0.3–1.2 µm wide. The strip's kinetic inductance and resistance can produce a phase gradient along the junction: a rough estimate of 8 pH per square gives about 100 pH over 4 µm for W_sc = 0.3 µm, comparable to the Josephson inductance Φ0/2πI_c ≈ 66 pH at 5 µA and corresponding to a phase drop of order 1 rad. This scales as 1/W_sc and grows as the gap is suppressed by B_∥. It could reduce I_c and add a geometric, current-direction-dependent asymmetry, which would be an alternative explanation for both the I_c(W_sc) and η(W_sc) trends. The authors themselves note that kinetic inductance is non-negligible and neglected (Section IV, Conclusion). They should estimate the phase drop along the strip from the measured sheet resistance or sheet kinetic inductance of their Al at the relevant fields, or simulate the current distribution, and show that it is negligible for W_sc = 0.3 µm.

5. The spin–orbit attribution of the gate dependence of B_* is not tested against other gate effects and is not quantitative (Section III, Figs. 2c, 3, Eq. 2). The paper attributes the rise of B_* with V_g to a larger Rashba α and a smaller τ through Eq. (2). However, the gate also changes the density and with it k_F (which appears in Eq. 2), v_F, ℓ, the induced gap, E_T, g* and ξ; the Meissner and orbital estimates in Eq. (5) also depend on v_F and Δ, so they are gate-dependent too. Decreasing transparency at higher density runs against the usual trend in these heterostructures and needs evidence. None of α(V_g), n(V_g), ℓ(V_g) or τ(V_g) is measured for these samples; the direction of α is taken from Ref. 45 without using its numbers, and the right-hand axis of Fig. 3 is schematic. Eq. (2) is never evaluated numerically and compared in magnitude with the measured 0–40 mT values or with the factor of about 2–6 change in Fig. 3. The argument is also not discriminating: strong gate dependence of B_* and weak gate dependence of B_η are both presented as consistent with spin–orbit coupling. Eq. (2) was derived for W_sc → ∞, yet B_* is plotted against W_sc in Fig. 2c and its dependence, including the upturn at W_sc = 0.3 µm, is called "nontrivial" and left unexplained. The "V_g < 0" series in Fig. 2c is taken at a different negative gate voltage for each device (about −2.5 to −5 V in Fig. 3), so its W_sc dependence mixes in gate dependence. The authors should measure or cite n(V_g) and α(V_g) (for example Hall and weak-antilocalization measurements on a co-fabricated Hall bar) and τ(V_g) from R_n, I_cR_n, the excess current or multiple Andreev reflection; plot R_n, I_c and τ against V_g for each device; evaluate Eq. (2) and show that B_* scales with k_so/k_F as predicted rather than merely increasing; compare devices at a common negative gate voltage or matched density; and state what the W_sc dependence of B_* in Fig. 2c is meant to show.

6. The extraction of B_* is not described and is sensitive to field offsets at the few-mT scale (Figs. 2c, 3). B_* lies between about −5 and 40 mT and is obtained from "low-field fits to Eq. (1) of Ref. 8", but the main text does not give the fit function, which parameters are free or fixed, the field step, the fit window, or how the error bars were obtained. Because I_c changes over hundreds of mT, the result may depend on the window. A remanent field in the in-plane magnet or trapped flux of a few mT, comparable to typical vector-magnet offsets and hysteresis, shifts argmax I_c+ and argmin I_c− in opposite directions. Negative B_* values (down to about −5 mT, for example for 1.2 µm at V_g < 0) cannot come from Eq. (2), reverse the sign of the effect, and suggest a residual offset or misalignment component comparable to the W_sc differences being interpreted. The authors should write out the fit function and its parameters in the main text; show that B_* is stable when the fit window is changed; state how the zero of B_∥ was calibrated; state whether B_* is the average of argmax I_c+ and −argmin I_c− (which would cancel an offset) or comes from one branch, and show the two separately together with sweep-direction (hysteresis) data; and comment on whether the negative B_* values set a lower bound on the B_* uncertainty.

7. Out-of-plane field and the B_z compensation procedure could themselves produce W_sc-dependent signals (Section II, Section S.III). I_c+ and I_c− are taken as the maximum and minimum of the Fraunhofer pattern, each at its own out-of-plane field within a narrow window. This removes Fraunhofer shifts, but it can also change the apparent asymmetry, and a relative B_z shift between the I_c+ and I_c− lobes is itself a diode signature (from inhomogeneous current or self-field effects) that the procedure discards. Flux focusing by the contacts makes the effective area for B_z, the Fraunhofer period and any misalignment-induced B_z grow with W_sc, so misalignment couples more strongly in wider-contact devices. Vortices trapped in the wider Al strips are a further W_sc-dependent possibility. The authors should state the misalignment angle and the compensation procedure in the main text; show, for each device, representative raw Fraunhofer maps at several B_∥ including large B_∥, the B_z offset of the I_c± maxima against B_∥, and the Fraunhofer period against W_sc; show that B_* and B_η do not change when the B_z window or its centre is varied; and report field-sweep hysteresis and sweep direction.

8. A null control with the in-plane field along the current is missing. For the Rashba–Zeeman, Meissner and flux (Eq. 3) mechanisms alike, the diode effect should vanish or be strongly suppressed when B_∥ is parallel to I, whereas extrinsic contributions (B_z leakage, self-field effects, current crowding in the T-geometry) need not vanish in the same way; the flux through W_sc·d and the Rashba–Zeeman term also have distinct angular dependences. Measuring η and I_c±(B_∥) for at least two field angles, for at least the narrowest and widest devices, would show that the W_sc trends in Fig. 4 come from the intended mechanisms and help separate orbital from spin–orbit contributions.
