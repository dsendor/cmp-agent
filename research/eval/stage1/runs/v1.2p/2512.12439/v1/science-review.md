A. Must address

1. Charge transfer and screening are inferred, not shown, and non-electronic differences between the two interfaces have not been excluded (pp. 14–15, Fig. 5B; Methods pp. 16–17). The conclusion that "charge transfer and electrostatic screening ... are the likely culprit" rests only on the Au versus Al2O3 contrast. The paper has no measurement of charge transfer: no carrier density, no core-level, work-function or band-edge data, not even the sign of the doping (Fig. 5B suggests electrons), and no electrostatic estimate of the transferred sheet density or its decay over layers from the Au/Ta2NiSe5 work-function offset. Without this, "charge transfer" and "screening (mirror charges)" cannot be told apart, and the excitonic interpretation is not tied to any quantity. The two substrates also differ in much more than conductivity. Au is thermally evaporated directly onto the freshly cleaved Ta2NiSe5, where hot Au adatoms can react with Se (Au–chalcogen bonding is strong), intercalate into the first vdW gap, interdiffuse, or create defects and local strain. Al2O3 is grown by ALD at 100–150 °C with TMA/H2O and then capped with Au. The interfaces also differ in roughness, conformality to the ripples and adhesion, and the mechanical boundary conditions differ (the cold-welding press, and different thermal-expansion mismatch on cooling from about 340 K down to 10 K; the wafer under each sample, SiO2/Si or sapphire, is not stated). Ta2NiSe5 Tc and excitonic fluctuations are known to be very sensitive to pressure and uniaxial strain (refs. 7, 14–17, including ref. 17 from the same group), and a strain or chemical explanation would have no special link to excitons. The 4L/Al2O3 control excludes generic oxidation (p. 15), but not damage specific to the Au interface. The authors should: (i) compare absolute Ag phonon frequencies of 4L/Au and 4L/Al2O3 (and of each flake against bulk) at matched temperatures in the orthorhombic and monoclinic phases, and use known mode Grüneisen or strain coefficients to bound the strain; (ii) characterize the Au/Ta2NiSe5 interface chemically, for example by XPS of the Au–Se chemistry or cross-sectional STEM/EDX, or at least argue quantitatively against Au–Se reaction and intercalation; (iii) estimate the transferred carrier density and its decay, and ideally measure doping directly (phonon shifts, KPFM, XPS/UPS core-level or work-function shifts, reflectance band edge); (iv) add a control that separates electrostatics from chemistry and strain, such as a monolayer hBN or a few-nm Al2O3 spacer between Au and the flake, graphite, Au deposited on top of a flake already on Al2O3, or a substrate of different work function (for example Pt or Ti/Au); a Raman temperature series on the 2L/4L ribbons already obtained on non-evaporated Au(111)/mica (Fig. 1B) would test whether a pristine Au contact without evaporation gives the same suppression; and (v) state explicitly that the existing "Al2O3" samples sit on Au/Cr below 50–100 nm of oxide, which makes them a thick-spacer control, and state the wafer for each sample. Without at least a strain bound or a spacer control, the mechanism remains one hypothesis among several and "manifesting the presence of excitonic interactions" (abstract) does not follow.

2. The conclusion that Au weakens electron–hole rather than electron–lattice interactions rests on an asymmetric and circular comparison (p. 15–16, Fig. 4). The argument is that the quasi-elastic peak (QEP) at 10 cm⁻¹ is "suppressed beyond detection" in 4L/Au, while the maximum of −1/q drops only 17% (0.38 versus 0.46 for 18L/Au), which is said to be "explained by a simple temperature broadening". Several problems follow. First, the reasoning is circular: the QEP's microscopic origin is contested (direct excitonic fluctuations, ref. 54, versus an exciton-coupled acoustic phonon, ref. 55); the paper uses the Au-induced suppression to "affirm" that the QEP is excitonic, then uses the QEP to conclude that Au suppresses excitonic interactions. Second, the QEP may simply be undetectable: the 4L/Au spectra are scaled ×50 and 6L/Au ×25, a Rayleigh leak at 15 cm⁻¹ was removed and interpolated only for the Au samples (Methods p. 18), 10 cm⁻¹ is close to the cutoff of a single-grating spectrometer, and Fig. 4T is flat at about 3.5 (×50) with scatter comparable to the peak expected after broadening. A peak broadened over about 100 K and reduced in proportion would plausibly fall below the noise, and the 6L/Au data (Fig. 4S) already show a broad plateau. Third, the same broadening must be applied to both observables: a distribution of local Tc would also smear and lower χ''ac(10 cm⁻¹, T), so the claim that broadening explains −1/q but not the QEP is not demonstrated. Fourth, the Fano asymmetry is not a clean measure of electron–lattice coupling: in an excitonic scenario the continuum is the electron–hole continuum itself, and a single Fano fit to a superposition of layers in different phases has no clear meaning for q. Fifth, carrier doping and screening also suppress a hybridization or band-Jahn–Teller instability (band-filling changes and screened interband Coulomb interaction both reduce the energy gained from the distortion), so a lowered Tc alone does not show the mechanism is excitonic. The authors should: convolve the 18L/Au or 7L/Al2O3 curves of −1/q(T) and χ''(10 cm⁻¹, T) with the Tc distribution inferred from Fig. 3 and show quantitatively that broadening accounts for one and not the other; give a detection limit or upper bound for the QEP amplitude in 4L/Au, for example by injecting the scaled 4L/Al2O3 QEP into the 4L/Au noise; show raw low-frequency spectra below 25 cm⁻¹ without interpolation, with the filter edge and stray-light background, for all flakes; consider integrating χ'' over a window (for example 8–20 cm⁻¹) rather than a single point; and explain, ideally with a model estimate, why a lattice-driven scenario would not respond the same way. Unless such discriminating evidence is added, "establishes an excitonic component" (p. 16) and "Our results confirm the existence of excitonic effects" should be softened.

3. The headline magnitude ("reduced by over 100 K", "lowers the 326 K Tc ... by at least 100 K") uses the wrong reference and is not supported by the quoted uncertainty (abstract; pp. 13, 15). The reduction is measured from bulk 326 K, but the same-thickness control 4L/Al2O3 already sits at 280–287 K (about 15% below bulk), so part of the 4L/Au reduction is a thickness, encapsulation or fabrication effect unrelated to the conducting substrate. Against the 4L/Al2O3 control the substrate-induced shift is about 60 ± 40 K; even relative to bulk it is 106 ± 40 K. Neither supports a lower bound of 100 K, and the ±40 K uncertainty alone makes "at least" untenable. "At least" also assumes that Au removes excitonic correlations completely in the measured layers, which is not shown. The paper itself calls Tc^avg a "crude estimate" (p. 13) yet draws a lower bound from it. The authors should quote the substrate effect as a shift at fixed thickness relative to 4L/Al2O3 with propagated uncertainties, state clearly which baseline is used, and revise the abstract, p. 13, and the excitonic-share estimate on pp. 15–16 accordingly, framing the excitonic share as an estimate whose bounds depend on the interface model's assumptions.

B. Should address

4. Each condition rests on a single flake, and the thickness series is not matched across substrates (Figs. 3–5). Five flakes are shown in total, one per combination (7L and 4L on Al2O3; 18L, 6L and 4L on Au). The central contrast, 4L/Au versus 4L/Al2O3, uses one flake of each; there is no 6L or 18L on Al2O3 and no thinner flake on either substrate, so the thickness effect on Al2O3 (4L already 15% lowered) cannot be separated from the substrate effect at 6L (the present comparison pairs 7L/Al2O3 with 6L/Au). Flake-to-flake variation from local strain from the cold-welding press, contact quality with amorphous Au, local damage, folds, laser position or thickness misassignment could produce Tc shifts of tens of kelvin, comparable to the claimed effect. The authors should measure two or three further 4L flakes (and ideally 6L) on each substrate, preferably from different exfoliation runs, add a matched 6L/Al2O3 flake, report the spread of Tc and of the transition width, state whether results survive thermal cycling, and ideally show one flake crossing from Au onto Al2O3 so the comparison is made within one sample.

5. The layer-resolved "Tc gradient" picture is not tested quantitatively and appears inconsistent with the paper's own onset temperatures and with the depth weighting of Raman scattering above a metal (pp. 4, 13–14, Fig. 5B inset). The model has the one or two layers next to Au suppressed by over 100 K while layers farther away keep a Tc "closer to room temperature". The first (onset) kink should then mark the highest-Tc layers and stay near the 4L/Al2O3 (280 K) or bulk value; instead it is at 220 K for 4L/Au and 250 K for 6L/Au (Fig. 3S,T). In 6L/Au four or five layers are supposedly unaffected, yet the onset is about 70 K below bulk, suggesting the whole flake is shifted. An independent layer-by-layer Tc is also not obvious in a coupled 4L crystal. Further, the tangential optical field at 632.8 nm has a node at the metal surface, so the layers nearest Au are the ones Raman samples least efficiently, not the ones that become "detectable in the overall signal" (pp. 13–14); the roughly 50–100-fold weaker 4L/Au signal compared with 4L/Al2O3 (colour scales in Figs. 3G,I,J and 4I,J; ×3, ×25, ×50 factors for 18L, 6L, 4L on Au) is consistent with this. The statement that the effect is "confined to one or two layers" (p. 14) is a deduction from the persistence of broadening, not a result. The authors should compute layer-resolved Raman weights by a transfer-matrix calculation for each stack (air/TNS/Au/Cr/SiO2/Si and air/TNS/Al2O3/Au/Cr/...), model the response as a weighted sum of bulk-like spectra with a Tc(z) profile, and show that a profile confined to one or two layers reproduces the onset temperatures, kink separation, and Fano and quasi-elastic evolution for 4L and 6L on Au (Figs. 3S,T, 4N,O,S,T); if a whole-flake suppression is needed, the interpretation should change. They should also rule out lateral inhomogeneity (amorphous-Au roughness, partial contact, local strain) as the source of broadening, for example with Raman maps across each flake at a few temperatures.

6. Laser heating and the effective sample temperature are not documented, and heating alone could produce a lowered, broadened transition (Methods p. 17). About 1 mW at 632.8 nm is used and the Stokes/anti-Stokes ratio was "checked", but no ratios, inferred temperatures, spot size, power density or power dependence are shown, and it is not said whether the check was made on each flake and at which temperatures. Heating above the stage temperature makes the transition appear at a lower nominal temperature, and a gradient across the spot and through the stack broadens it — the very signatures attributed to the Au interface. Absorption, local field and heat sinking differ between the Au/Cr stack and the stack with an extra 50–100 nm of Al2O3, and between 18L and 4L flakes, so the 18L/Au reference does not settle this; the effect bears directly on the 60 K substrate difference. The authors should tabulate the Stokes/anti-Stokes temperature against stage temperature for each flake and temperature region, especially 4L/Au and 4L/Al2O3, repeat the 4L/Au (and 4L/Al2O3) series at reduced power (for example 0.25 and 0.5 mW) to show that Tc and width do not change, and state the spot size and power density.

7. The definition of Tc^avg and its ±40 K uncertainty is ad hoc and inconsistent with the criterion used for the sharp flakes (p. 10, Fig. 3S,T, Fig. 5B). For sharp flakes Tc is the onset kink; for 6L and 4L on Au the same onset kink (250 K and 220 K) is relabelled Tc^avg and given ±40 K from the gap between the first and second kinks (100 K versus an assumed universal 60 K). In a distribution of local Tc the onset marks the upper end, so the average should lie below it; and a 40 K excess separation more naturally corresponds to a total spread of about 40 K (±20 K) with the centre below the onset. The assumption that the second kink sits a fixed 60 K below Tc, and that the extra gap measures symmetric broadening, is not tied to any physical process. The structural and electronic criteria also differ and disagree by more than the quoted error for 18L/Au (320 versus 306 ± 14 K). The authors should use one criterion for all five samples — for example the midpoint and width from fits of the integrated Ag(m4)+Ag(m5) intensity (an order-parameter-like quantity) or of the Ag(o3)*/Ag(m4) shift, with a model that includes a distribution of Tc — and define Tc^avg from that model rather than from the onset kink.

8. Temperature interpolation and smoothing may artificially broaden the transition, and the kink extraction is subjective (Methods pp. 17–18; Figs. 3F–T, 4F–J). The 2D maps are resampled on a 5 K grid and Gaussian-smoothed by 10–15 K in temperature, with raw steps of 10–50 K, and the second-derivative maps (Fig. 3K–O), from which the peak positions in Fig. 3P–T are taken, are built from these smoothed maps; this smoothing is comparable to the claimed broadening and uncertainties, and the second derivative amplifies noise in the weak 6L and 4L/Au data (colour scales ±0.6 and ±0.3 against ±15). The kink criterion relies on a "small but abrupt" shift of the leaked Ag(o3)* mode comparable to the 1.3 cm⁻¹ point spacing, and the 4L/Au kink is "less defined". The authors should state explicitly whether peak positions come from smoothed maps, extract positions and intensities from Lorentzian/Fano fits to the raw spectra at each measured temperature with fit uncertainties, state the temperature steps for each flake, and use the 2D maps only as illustrations.

9. The 4L/Al2O3 result is described inconsistently and its 15% reduction is not explained (abstract; pp. 13, 15). The abstract calls it "nearly bulk-like" and the Discussion claims "the absence of any notable interface effect from Al2O3" and uses it as the clean control, yet Tc drops to 280–287 K, more than the up to 9% reported for 5L on SiO2 (ref. 45). If Al2O3 has no interface effect, the reduction must come from dimensionality, strain or ALD processing, and the same contribution must also be present in 4L/Au. Moreover, a sharp transition in an ALD-capped flake does not exclude degradation specific to the Au interface, since the process histories differ. The authors should explain the 40 K reduction and align the wording of abstract and text.
