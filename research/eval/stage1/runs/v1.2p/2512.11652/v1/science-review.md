# Merged review

## A. Must address

1. **The claim that the nuclear spin is driven "irrespective of hybridization" is not supported, and conflicts with standard hyperfine enhancement and with the paper's own reasoning.** The Abstract ("the nuclear spin can be driven efficiently irrespective of its hybridization with the electron spin"), the Introduction ("we unambiguously demonstrate NMR driving independent of electron-nuclear spin hybridization") and the Conclusion ("hybridization ... is not necessary to drive NMR") rest on one observation: ENDOR features I–IV persist from 200 to 1400 mT, where the flip-flop admixture |⟨↓,−3/2|ψ⟩|² is strongly reduced (SFig. 1b, about 10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵). This shows that the readout no longer needs hybridization; it does not show that the drive matrix element is independent of it. The single-tone features in Fig. 1d vanish above about 100 mT because readout there needs Δm_s ≠ 0, which says nothing about drive efficiency. In ENDOR the nuclear transition moment is normally dominated by hyperfine enhancement. The transverse hyperfine term mixes electron character into each nuclear state with amplitude ε ~ A_⊥/(2 f_ESR), roughly 10⁻³–10⁻² for A_⊥ of tens of MHz and f_ESR = 2–11.5 GHz. The electron moment exceeds the ⁴⁷Ti nuclear moment by g_e μ_B/(g_N μ_N) ~ 10³–10⁴ (g_e ≈ 0.56, g_N ≈ 0.315). So an oscillating field, or an effective field from tip-height modulation in the strongly tilted tip field, acting on the electron gives a Δm_I = ±1, Δm_s = 0 matrix element of order (g_e μ_B/g_N μ_N)(A/h f_ESR) g_N μ_N B1. Across the whole field range this is plausibly 10–100 times larger than direct coupling to the bare nucleus, and it falls as about 1/B, the same scaling as the hybridization coefficient in SFig. 1b. There is also an internal inconsistency. Supplementary Note 1 states that the tip field is mostly exchange and "cannot couple directly to the nuclear spin". Yet the Discussion proposes a drive through an "effective oscillating magnetic field" of the ESR-STM type (piezoelectric displacement in the tip field, barrier modulation, spin torque). All of these act on the electron spin, so a nuclear drive produced by them must pass through the hyperfine admixture, which is the hybridization the paper says is not needed. Finally, the paper does not show that drive efficiency is independent of B: the ENDOR amplitude varies non-monotonically with field, and the authors attribute this to V_ESR transmission (Supplementary Note 2), which leaves open whether the NMR Rabi rate falls with B as hyperfine-mediated driving predicts. The authors should: (i) compute the Δm_I = ±1 transition matrix elements (Rabi rates) of the Eq. 1 eigenstates as a function of B for each candidate drive term (a transverse field on the bare nucleus only; a transverse field on electron and nucleus together; modulation of B_tip or exchange on the electron; modulation of the anisotropic hyperfine and quadrupole terms), including the anisotropic A, the tip-field tilt (φ ≈ 5°) and the measured g-tensor, and give the predicted field and m_s dependence of each; (ii) measure the drive strength independently of the ESR-readout calibration, through the V_NMR dependence of ENDOR depth and linewidth (saturation, power broadening) or an NMR Rabi-rate estimate, at two or three fields with matched V_ESR (200–450 mT, where V_ESR = 20 mV is available, suffices; alternatively normalize each ENDOR dip to the corresponding ESR peak height in SFig. 4a), ideally also below 200 mT where the admixture is large; (iii) compare the intensities of the ↑ and ↓ transitions (I vs II, III vs IV), which have opposite hyperfine-enhancement factors, with the predictions. If the drive turns out to be electron-mediated, the result still stands, but the headline must be narrowed to what the data show, for example "readout independent of hybridization" or "driving does not rely on electron-nuclear flip-flop (Δm_s ≠ 0) transitions", with drive persisting to 1.4 T. The wording in the Abstract, Introduction and Conclusion must follow whatever the analysis shows.

## B. Should address

2. **The arguments that exclude hyperfine and quadrupole modulation as the drive are incomplete, so the conclusion that the drive is "dominantly an effective oscillating magnetic field" is not established (Discussion; Supplementary Note 3; SFig. 8; Fig. 2b).** Hyperfine modulation is ruled out because it "would drive flip-flop transitions ... in the GHz range". That holds only for the isotropic or S_x I_x + S_y I_y part. With an anisotropic or tilted hyperfine tensor, and an electron quantization axis tilted by the tip field (φ ≈ 5°, Supplementary Note 1), modulation of off-diagonal terms such as A_zx S_z I_x drives Δm_I = ±1, Δm_s = 0 transitions at exactly the observed MHz frequencies, with an m_s-dependent sign. This is the hyperfine-Stark mechanism of electrically driven NMR in donors and single-molecule magnets (the authors' refs. 7, 16, 17), and a piezoelectric displacement of the atom or a tip-induced change of the adsorption geometry makes it plausible here. Quadrupole modulation is dismissed on two grounds, neither decisive. First, the "slight signal" at the −1/2 ↔ +1/2 transitions (V/VI, f_ESR ≈ 4.0–4.1 GHz in Fig. 2b) is only said to "may be discerned"; its significance is not quantified, yet it is used in the mechanism argument. Second, the absence of Δm_I = ±2 lines rests on a single noisy sweep (SFig. 8, noise about ±3 fA, in which even I and III are barely above noise), and whether Δm_I = ±2 lines appear, and how strongly relative to ±1 lines ({I_z, I_x} versus I_x² − I_y² terms), depends on which electric-field-gradient components are modulated. The authors should write the modulated terms explicitly with selection rules and expected relative intensities for I–VI and the Δm_I = ±2 lines; state the amplitude ratio needed to see Δm_I = ±2 at the SNR of SFig. 8, or repeat the wide sweep with enough averaging for a meaningful bound; show line cuts and Lorentzian fits with significance for V/VI, ideally from a dedicated measurement with f_ESR on the m_I = −1/2 peak, or treat V/VI as not resolved; and use the m_s dependence of line amplitudes (I/III versus II/IV) as a discriminant. Otherwise the mechanism should be presented as an open question, and the sentence that a drive "not dependent on system specific constants A or Q" implies applicability to other nuclear spin systems should be removed.

3. **The contrast between single-tone (Fig. 1d) and ENDOR (Fig. 3a) detection is not a matched comparison.** Fig. 1d was taken with a different microtip (Methods), so tip fields and spin polarizations differ, and at different setpoints (I_set = 2.5–3 pA and V_bias = 70, 75, 90 mV at the three fields, versus 4 pA and 80 mV for the ENDOR data). The two datasets overlap only at 200 mT, no ENDOR data are shown at 60 or 100 mT, and no single-tone data are shown with the ENDOR tip. Since single-tone mode cannot distinguish "no driving" from "no readout" at 200 mT, the comparison does not show that the hybridization-dependent features and the ENDOR features come from different drive processes, yet the contrast between "vanishes beyond 100 mT" and "persists to 1.4 T" is a main pillar of the narrative. With one tip, one atom and one setpoint and V_NMR, the authors should measure single-tone and ENDOR spectra at a low field (60–100 mT) and at a high field (≥ 200 mT), show whether the low-field ENDOR resonances coincide with the single-tone features, and check how their amplitudes scale relative to SFig. 1b.

4. **Reproducibility across atoms and tips is not shown.** All main-text ENDOR data (Figs. 1e, 2, 3, 4) come from one microtip and apparently one ⁴⁷Ti atom, so the fitted A_z, Q and g_N, the transition assignments and the persistence of ENDOR at high field are each demonstrated once. The ENDOR amplitudes depend on tip polarization and height (as the ~10 fA offset argument for Fig. 2 shows), and the authors themselves attribute the deviation of Q from the literature to a tip-dependent electric field gradient, which is a claim of tip dependence. The authors should state how many ⁴⁷Ti atoms and tips showed ENDOR features, show the ENDOR spectrum (at least transitions I and II, e.g. the 450 mT spectrum of Fig. 3a) on a second ⁴⁷Ti atom and preferably with a second tip, and report the spread of line positions, A_z and Q. Measuring Q at two tip heights on one atom would test the tip-EFG explanation directly.

5. **The nuclear g-factor extraction neglects second-order hyperfine (pseudo-nuclear Zeeman) shifts (SFig. 5).** g_N = 0.37 ± 0.04 comes from a linear fit of transition I against B_z, compared with 0.315 in bulk (about 17%, 1.4σ). The transition frequencies also contain field-dependent second-order terms of order A_⊥²/(4 h f_ESR), about 1–2 MHz at 200 mT and falling roughly as 1/B, which change by a MHz or more between 200 mT (f_ESR ≈ 2 GHz) and 1.4 T (f_ESR ≈ 11.5 GHz). That is comparable to the ~3 MHz total shift in SFig. 5, whose first four points (200–450 mT) look flat and steeper above, which a straight line does not describe. A linear fit therefore biases g_N and probably explains the discrepancy, which argues against presenting it as a measured deviation from the bulk value. In addition, the tip field (B_tip ≈ 67 mT) is included in the electron Zeeman term but not in the nuclear Zeeman term, and Supplementary Note 1 then fixes g_N at the literature value "given the discrepancy", which is circular if the discrepancy comes from the linear fit. The sign convention for g_N (negative for ⁴⁷Ti) should be stated. The authors should fit all four transitions at all fields with the full Hamiltonian, g_N free and A_z, A_⊥ and Q shared, report whether any discrepancy with 0.315 remains, use the resulting g_N consistently, and state whether the grey lines in Fig. 3b include the pseudo-nuclear Zeeman term.

6. **The Hamiltonian fit is under-specified and its uncertainties are inconsistent (Supplementary Note 1, main text p. 4, Fig. 3b).** κ* = −56.7 ± 0.8 MHz with Q = κ/[2I(2I−1)] = κ/20 gives Q = −2.84 ± 0.04 MHz, not ± 0.8 MHz. With the quoted ±0.8 MHz (about 30%), the stated "~5% deviation" from refs. 32 and 33 cannot be resolved, so attributing it to a tip-dependent field gradient is speculative; either the uncertainty or the comparison must be corrected. The fixed values of A_x = A_y and g_e,x/y are not given, although they control the second-order shifts and the mixing in SFig. 1. The paper does not say which transitions and fields enter the fit (so the data-to-parameter ratio cannot be judged), how the ±0.4 and ±0.8 MHz uncertainties were obtained (covariance only, or including tip-field uncertainty), or how A_z and Q change when B_tip, φ, g_e,z and g_N vary within their uncertainties. B_tip,z = 65.64 ± 0.02 mT from a seven-point linear fit seems implausibly precise. η = 0 is assumed from the four-fold site symmetry, while the authors attribute the change in Q to the tip, and a tilted tip (φ ≈ 5°) breaks that symmetry. The Q convention differs from the common 3e²qQ/[4I(2I−1)h], and with Eq. S2 the observed splittings correspond to an effective axial coefficient of 1.5Q; this must be stated for comparison with refs. 32 and 33. In Fig. 3b, transition IV at 1070 mT (~72 MHz) lies several MHz below the calculated line and II at 1400 mT (~90 MHz) lies above it, beyond the "MHz precision" claimed; the reliability of four-Lorentzian fits at these low-SNR fields should be discussed and fit uncertainties given for all points. The authors should provide a table of fixed parameters (with sources) and free parameters (with uncertainties), and a residuals table of measured against calculated frequencies for every transition and field.

7. **No population model accounts for the sign, amplitude and assignment of the ENDOR features.** The text says population is driven out and in, seen as "dips (blue) and peaks (red)" in Fig. 2a, but the red m_I = −3/2 trace shows no clear peaks at 50 or 85 MHz, and in Fig. 2b the −3/2 column (~3.9 GHz) mainly shows decreases, at III/IV; if population is pumped from −5/2 to −3/2, that column should show increases at I/II. The column-mean background subtraction in Fig. 2b could also shift apparent signs. The assignment of III/IV as features in the m_I = −5/2 readout relies on a qualitative spin-pumping cascade (Fig. 3c). The field dependence of the amplitudes (III/IV "less pronounced" at higher field through reduced flip-flop rates, although they remain clearly visible at 660 and 1070 mT; ↑ features weakening at higher field) is interpreted without a model and is confounded with the V_ESR changes above 500 mT (Supplementary Note 2). The authors should show fitted amplitudes with uncertainties for both Fig. 2a traces, and provide a rate-equation (or Lindblad) model with spin-polarized scattering, flip-flop rates weighted by SFig. 1b, ESR drive and NMR drive, stating which parameters are fitted and which fixed. It should reproduce the sign and relative size of each feature I–IV in each m_I readout column of Figs. 2a/b and 3a and turn the field-dependence argument into a prediction. If peaks are absent where the model predicts them, this matters for the population-transfer picture.

8. **Reading the I/II amplitude ratio as the electron-spin population ratio (Fig. 4) needs justification.** P↑/P↓ is taken from the normalized amplitudes of transitions I and II. This equals the m_s population ratio only if both transitions are driven equally strongly and read out with equal efficiency. If the drive is hyperfine-enhanced (concern 1), the effective nuclear matrix elements differ between the ↓ and ↑ manifolds (enhancement of about 1 ± A/2ν_n in opposite senses), and so do the saturation levels. Continuous ESR driving of |↓,−5/2⟩ ↔ |↑,−5/2⟩ also mixes exactly the two states being compared. The trend rests on three V_ESR values with large error bars, and the normalization is not defined. The authors should state the normalization; correct for or bound the matrix-element ratio using the model from concern 1; add more V_ESR values if possible; and compare the 6 mV ratio (about 0.63) with the Boltzmann ratio at 0.4 K and 450 mT (exp(−h·4 GHz/k_B·0.4 K) ≈ 0.62) as a consistency check. Since the ↑/↓ assignment is already supported by the opposite field slopes of I and II in Fig. 3b, the authors could lean on that rather than saying Fig. 4 "removes the ambiguity". The same caveat applies to the explanation of the field dependence of II and IV in Fig. 3a.

9. **The MHz drive amplitude at the junction is not calibrated, and drive-related artifacts and heating are not characterized.** All NMR data use V_NMR = 20 mV, but the MHz transfer function over 20–170 MHz is not shown; Supplementary Note 2 says transfer functions exist for the "full frequency range", but SFig. 6 covers only the GHz tone. Since features are 5–10 MHz wide and the I and II amplitudes at different f_NMR are compared quantitatively (Fig. 3 discussion, Fig. 4), a frequency-dependent V_NMR would bias those comparisons and the attribution of the weaker high-field signal to reduced V_ESR. The two tones are combined passively and may mix in the nonlinear junction. Supplementary Note 2 also mentions heating at high RF power. The claim that the equalized ESR peaks in Fig. 1e imply the drive "saturate[s] the two nuclear spin populations" rests on one spectrum, and "driven efficiently" is not backed by any measure of drive efficiency. The authors should show the calibrated V_NMR(f_NMR) at the junction (at least constant between 45 and 90 MHz), state whether adding V_NMR changes the DC current, tip height or ESR linewidth, and show the V_NMR dependence of dip depth and width for transition I, including at least one spectrum at reduced V_NMR (e.g. 10 mV) confirming that line positions do not shift with drive amplitude.

10. **NMR linewidths are not analysed, and the coherence outlook is unsupported.** The ENDOR lines in Fig. 3a and Fig. 4 are several MHz wide, far broader than any nuclear dephasing compatible with the seconds-scale T1 invoked in the Conclusion, yet their origin is not discussed. Candidate causes are power broadening by V_NMR = 20 mV; electron-spin flips from tunnelling and continuous ESR drive, which switch the nuclear frequency by ~A_z = 132 MHz (so the m_s-resolved lines have a lifetime set by the electron T1), including mixing of |↓,−5/2⟩ and |↑,−5/2⟩ by the ESR drive; and tip-height or tip-field noise. Under tunnelling and ESR drive the electron flips at kHz–MHz rates and would dephase the nucleus far faster than T1. The authors should report the fitted widths against field, V_NMR, V_ESR (already available in Fig. 4) and I_set, discuss what sets them, and justify or soften the Conclusion's statement that coherence times could be "in the same order of magnitude" as T1.

11. **Possible instrumental structure in the 2D ENDOR map (Fig. 2b, SFig. 3, Supplementary Note 2).** In Fig. 2b, feature II near 83 MHz extends along f_ESR over roughly 3.72–3.82 GHz, wider than the ESR linewidth in Fig. 1b. In the raw map (SFig. 3), dark horizontal lines near 48 and 83 MHz appear to run across a wide range of f_ESR, including between ESR peaks. Column-average background subtraction can both create and suppress such structure. The off-resonance trace in Fig. 2a and the I = 0 control are each taken at a single f_ESR, so they do not exclude it. The authors should show line cuts of the raw data at f_ESR values between ESR peaks and confirm that the feature width along f_ESR matches the ESR linewidth.
