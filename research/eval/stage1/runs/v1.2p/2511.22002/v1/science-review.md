A. Must address

1. No quantitative measure of the local anisotropy, and no comparison with what isotropic disorder alone would produce. For the Sn-doped samples the nematic claim rests on three hand-picked boxes in each of Fig. 3a and 3c (roughly 5–8 nm across), each chosen because its modulations "seemingly" run along one direction, and their FTs (Fig. 3b,d). For the Ti-doped sample it rests on two zooms (Fig. 4c,d) and one domain boundary in a ~6–8 nm topograph (Fig. 4a,e,f). In a random alloy with 7–14% of Sb sites substituted, dopant-induced QPI is intrinsically inhomogeneous: any window of only 6–8 modulation periods, with FT peaks already ~20% of Q_Bragg wide (p. 4), will show unequal intensity among the three ½Q_Bragg (or Bragg) directions purely by chance, even if the underlying electronic state is C6 symmetric. Picking the three most unidirectional windows will always yield three "domain types". The paper gives no order parameter, no map of it, no distribution and no null comparison, so Figs. 3 and 4 are at present illustrations rather than evidence for "short-range ordered electronic nematic domains". The authors should (i) define a local nematic order parameter, e.g. η(r) = (|A_A| − (|A_B| + |A_C|)/2)/(|A_A| + |A_B| + |A_C|) or the full three-component (Potts) vector, from lock-in/Lawler–Fujita demodulation at the three ½Q (or Bragg) vectors with a stated filter length; (ii) map it over the entire fields of view of Figs. 3a,c and 4a; (iii) report its histogram, its spatial autocorrelation and correlation length, the area fraction above an anisotropy threshold, and how these depend on window size; and (iv) compare them with the same analysis on a null: simulated QPI from randomly placed isotropic point scatterers at the measured dopant density on the DFT (or a C6-symmetric tight-binding) band structure with no nematic term, phase-randomized, pixel-shuffled, rotated or symmetrized versions of the data, and the pristine sample away from the CDW wave vector. Only an anisotropy amplitude or correlation length clearly above that null supports the nematic interpretation.

2. The "parent state", "pervasive", "in all samples" and phase-diagram claims go beyond what was measured. All data are at 4.5 K, yet Fig. 1b shades "Electronic nematicity" across the whole composition range (x = 0 to 1, including x between 0.68 and 1 where no sample was measured) and up to the 100 K top of the axis, above the pristine CDW transition. Without temperature dependence the paper cannot say where the anisotropy sets in, that it exists above the CDW or superconducting transitions, or that it is "parent" to them (title, abstract, pp. 3, 7). "Parent state" also implies that the same order exists in pristine CsV3Sb5 above T_CDW and gives rise to the CDW and superconductivity, but nothing presented connects the short-range domains in heavily doped alloys to the pristine compound, and the superconducting Tc of the measured crystals is not given even though Fig. 1b implies all compositions are superconducting. Moreover, at x ≈ 0.1 the paper's own marker, the ½Q_Bragg feature, is absent (p. 4, Extended Data Fig. 1); the only anisotropy there is the previously reported incommensurate charge stripe, a different phenomenon, so nematic domains are not demonstrated at x ≈ 0.1, which conflicts with "persists in all samples" in the abstract. The authors should either (a) measure the anisotropy order parameter of concern 1 at several temperatures (e.g. 4.5, 20–30, 50, 80+ K, including above the pristine T_CDW) on at least one CDW-free composition, or bring in bulk nematic probes (elastoresistance, birefringence, nematic susceptibility) on the same crystals, and address x ≈ 0.1 explicitly; or (b) remove the green region from Fig. 1b and restrict the title, abstract and Discussion to what is shown: short-range local C6 breaking at 4.5 K in CDW-free Sn- and Ti-doped samples (x = 0.33, 0.68, y = 0.2). Tc of the measured crystals should be reported in either case.

B. Should address

3. Anisotropic QPI in the Sn samples is not shown to reflect an anisotropic electronic state rather than an anisotropic arrangement of scatterers or local strain. On p. 4 the ½Q_Bragg features are attributed to scattering between M-point pockets (Fig. 2f,i,j). If so, the intensity along each direction in a small window is set by the local configuration of scatterers (Sn dopants, vacancies, Cs adatoms) and by local dopant-induced lattice distortion as much as by the band structure; a cluster of dopants aligned along one direction, or a locally anisotropic strain field, gives unidirectional interference with domains of all three orientations and no electronic instability. The paper does not locate the dopants (Sn on the in-plane Sb1 versus out-of-plane Sb2 site; Sn appears as distinct contrast in Fig. 1d–f), does not test whether domain orientation or boundaries correlate with dopant geometry or with a local strain map extracted from the same data, and does not compare the wave vector or its dispersion between the A, B and C domains, which would distinguish a locally distorted Fermi surface from an anisotropic scattering amplitude. The authors should do one or more of these, for example by comparing q-magnitude and its energy dependence within each domain type, or by simulating QPI (T-matrix on the Wannier model of the Methods) with the measured dopant positions and showing that the simulated pattern is isotropic on the domain scale while the data are not. The text itself notes (p. 7) that strain [30] must be excluded for the chirality question; the same applies to the nematicity claim.

4. For the Ti-doped sample, an electronic and intrinsic origin of the anisotropy is not established and the evidence is thin. Fig. 4 rests on one ~6–8 nm constant-current topograph at a single bias (+100 mV, 100 pA), plus one more boundary in Extended Data Fig. 3; the "domains" in the amplitude maps (Fig. 4e,f) are ~1–3 nm, comparable to the Ti–Ti spacing at y = 0.2 (about one Ti per 15 V sites). Unequal atomic Bragg-peak amplitudes can arise from local lattice distortion or strain around Ti clusters, local apparent-height variations, an asymmetric tip, scan-direction and feedback effects, piezo creep or uncorrected drift (Lawler–Fujita), and setpoint effects. The same-tip argument (p. 7) excludes only a uniform tip anisotropy. The authors should show the same areas at several biases of both polarities and in dI/dV maps (an electronic nematic should change in sign or magnitude with energy, a structural distortion should be largely bias independent); show the pattern is unchanged under rotated scan directions; perform a displacement-field (Lawler–Fujita) analysis to test for local uniaxial strain and its correlation with domain orientation; show larger fields of view and more than one region, tip and cleave; and quantify against the null of concern 1. Because momentum-resolved QPI was not obtained in this sample, the "consistent with" statement for Sn and Ti (p. 6) compares two different observables, QPI anisotropy and Bragg-peak amplitude; since the "generic" claim rests on agreement between the two dopants, the Ti evidence needs to stand on its own, ideally with spectroscopic (QPI or energy-resolved Bragg-amplitude) maps.

5. Finite-q unidirectional modulations are presented as "q = 0 nematicity", and residual short-range 2×2 correlations are not fully excluded. The Discussion calls the state "short-range q = 0 electronic nematicity", but all Sn-sample evidence is a locally unidirectional modulation at ~½Q_Bragg (Fig. 3b,d), which is also what locally 1Q, disorder- or strain-pinned remnants of the 2×2 CDW would look like. The paper gives three arguments against residual CDW on p. 4 (absence at x ≈ 0.1, appearance only at some energies, "slight dispersion"), but disorder-pinned short-range CDW correlations can also have energy-dependent contrast, and the dispersion is not quantified (Extended Data Fig. 2 is cited but not provided). Fig. 3b,d also label the domain wave vectors Q^A_CDW, Q^B_CDW, Q^C_CDW, contradicting the argument that they are not CDW. The authors should plot the ½Q peak position (and width) against energy with error bars and compare it with the DFT autocorrelation prediction; test whether the contrast inverts or shifts across E_F as expected for QPI but not for a static modulation; show a q = 0 measure of C6 breaking in the Sn samples (e.g. the three Bragg-peak amplitudes within each domain, as done for Ti in Fig. 4e,f) and show it is spatially correlated with the ½Q orientation; state explicitly how a q = 0 nematic order parameter is inferred from finite-q modulations; and relabel Fig. 3 with a neutral symbol (e.g. q_½).

6. Continuity between the doped-sample anisotropy and the nematicity reported in pristine AV3Sb5 is not demonstrated. The argument that the two are the same phenomenon, "unrelated to the 2×2 CDW" and present "across the AV3Sb5 phase diagram", compares different observables: a long-range, uniform C2 anisotropy of CDW-peak or QPI intensities in the pristine compound [21, 27, 34, 38] versus short-range anisotropy of disorder-broadened ½Q QPI (Sn) or Bragg amplitudes (Ti) here. The pristine sample is not analysed with the same metric, and at x ≈ 0.1, the composition that bridges the two regimes, the paper shows only unidirectional stripe correlations (p. 4; Extended Data Fig. 1). The authors should apply one identical anisotropy measure to x = 0, x ≈ 0.1, x = 0.33 and x = 0.68 (and the Bragg-amplitude measure to pristine and Ti-doped topographs at matched setpoints) and show how its magnitude and correlation length evolve with composition.

7. Sample statistics and reproducibility are not reported. The paper does not say, per composition, how many crystals, cleaves, fields of view and tips were used, how many regions were examined, or in what fraction the anisotropy was seen; for x = 0.68 and y = 0.2 single images are shown. "Persists in all samples" (abstract) and "pervasive" (title) require this, and the same-tip argument (p. 7) does not establish reproducibility across samples or that the domains shown are representative. The authors should give a table with these numbers and the area fraction above an anisotropy threshold, and show the analysis of concern 1 for at least two independent regions or cleaves per composition, including a second crystal or cleave for at least one Sn composition.
Raised by: r1, r2, r3

8. Setpoint and normalization effects are not addressed. The dI/dV maps in Figs. 2 and 3 use different setpoints (−200 mV/500 pA, 50 mV/200 pA, 0.6 GΩ/1 nA), and for x = 0.33 the setpoint bias (−200 mV) lies inside the energy window where the ½Q feature appears. In a strongly inhomogeneous doped sample the setpoint effect can imprint topographic or integrated-DOS anisotropy onto g(r,V). The authors should show the domain pattern in L(r,V) = (dI/dV)/(I/V) or in maps taken at a setpoint bias outside the relevant window, and confirm that the same regions carry the same orientation.

9. The energy dependence of the domain orientation is not shown. QPI-based nematicity should give the same domain orientation wherever the ½Q feature exists (roughly −300 to 0 meV or up to +100 meV, according to the red ellipse in Fig. 2d), whereas Figs. 3a and 3c are each a single energy (−200 mV and +50 mV). The authors should show the same boxes at several energies within that window and report whether orientations and boundaries are fixed or change with energy; orientation that changes with energy would point to scattering from local defect configurations rather than a nematic Fermi surface.

10. Actual compositions are not measured, and nominal values do not match the quoted ones. For Sn, the nominal Sb:Sn ratios (80:40 and 60:60) lie far above the quoted x ≈ 0.33 and 0.68, and no growth recipe is given for the x ≈ 0.1 crystal; for Ti, the nominal load Cs20V12Ti3.5Sb120 corresponds to ~0.68 Ti per formula unit, yet the sample is labelled y = 0.2. Flux growth makes such differences plausible, but the doping axis of the whole phase diagram then rests on unstated measurements, and composition inhomogeneity is itself an alternative source of domains. The authors should give EDS/WDS or diffraction-refined compositions of the crystals actually measured, with spread and uncertainty; the dopant density counted from topographs, identifying Sn (Sb1 vs Sb2) and Ti sites; and bulk transport or magnetization showing the absence of the CDW anomaly and the Tc of the measured batches.

11. The mirror-symmetry statement is not substantiated. The paper states that each domain "breaks two in-plane mirror symmetries but appears to generally preserve" the third (pp. 5–6), judged by eye from small boxes whose FTs span only a few pixels (Fig. 3b,d). The Discussion builds on this (a possible further transition to a chiral state, p. 7, and the comparison with CsTi3Bi5 [42, 43]). The authors should quantify the intensities of FT peaks mirror-related across the preserved axis, or the correlation between each domain image and its mirror image about each of the three axes, with uncertainties from the null model of concern 1, for each domain type; otherwise they should drop the claim.

12. Domain size and its dependence on composition are not reported, although the Discussion (p. 6) attributes the short range to dopant disorder. "Nanoscale" and "short-range" should be made concrete with a characteristic domain size (e.g. from the autocorrelation of concern 1), compared with the mean dopant spacing. If disorder limits the correlation length, the domain size should decrease from x = 0.33 to 0.68 or scale with dopant spacing, and should be compared between Sn and Ti doping. Reporting the correlation length and anisotropy amplitude versus composition would test this; no systematic change would weaken a disorder-pinned nematic picture relative to disorder-generated anisotropy.

13. The DFT comparison is semi-quantitative and partly mismatched to the data. The CECs and autocorrelations are computed for x ≈ 0.25 (p. 5) while the data are at x = 0.33 and 0.68; they use linear interpolation of Wannier hoppings between CsV3Sb5 and CsV3Sb4Sn (effectively virtual-crystal, with no disorder broadening), and only kz = 0, although the data integrate over kz. The statement that ½Q_Bragg connects "exactly" the M pockets depends on the chemical potential, which Sn hole doping shifts, and it is unclear how the Fermi level was set or whether a rigid shift was needed. The Methods also state both that "all first-principles calculations were performed on the pristine phase" and that a CsV3Sb4Sn Hamiltonian was built, and describe Sn as "within the kagome plane", which differs from the Sb-site wording on p. 3; which structures were relaxed and with what settings, the 300 eV cutoff (or a convergence check), the Wannier-fit energy window and the kz handling should be stated. Most importantly, the authors should compute at the measured compositions and compare the predicted energy window and intensity of the ½Q feature with the measured profile in Fig. 2d (the red ellipse spans roughly −300 to +100 meV, while only −100 and −500 meV are calculated), and test the robustness of the M-pocket nesting to kz and an energy offset. They should also state explicitly that the C6-symmetric calculation explains the wave vector but not the anisotropy.

Imaging the same area with a second tip, or after a controlled tip change, would strengthen the claim; the authors themselves invoke tip anisotropy [41] as a concern for chirality.
