Review of "Trapping e/4 quasiparticles in bilayer graphene" (Di Luca, Hajigeorgiou et al., arXiv:2608.27444v1)

SUMMARY OF THE CLAIMS

The authors add a suspended air-bridge gate (ADG) to a gate-defined antidot (AD) in Bernal bilayer graphene so they can sweep the AD potential without pushing the narrow even-denominator plateaus out of their stability window. The claims:
1. With weak AD-to-edge coupling, the ADG period normalized to the nu = -1 reference (10 T) is Delta V/Delta V1 = 0.27, 0.24 and 0.25 (each +/- 0.03) at nu = -5/2, -1/2 and 3/2, which they read as charge e/4 added to the AD. At nu = 2/3 the ratio is 0.32 +/- 0.03, read as e/3.
2. Making the side gates (SG) couple more strongly drives a crossover to roughly twice the gate period at the three even-denominator states and at 2/3. They see no doubling at -4/3 and -1/3.
3. They weigh two readings of the doubling, bunching (2e*) or a move toward an Aharonov-Bohm (AB) or mixed regime, and say neither explains all the data.

The device work is solid, and the main result (resolved quarter-period oscillations at three even-denominator fillings in BLG) is timely and interesting. The paper is mostly careful about not overclaiming, but there are several internal inconsistencies in the numbers, and one of them sits in the headline result. Details follow, most important first.

MAJOR ISSUES

1. The quoted ADG periods for the e/4 states contradict the quoted ratios (main text, p. 4). The text says "we extract ADG voltage periods in the range 112 mV to 126 mV, giving respective normalized periods of Delta V/Delta V1 = 0.27, 0.24, 0.25." With Delta V1 = 233 +/- 14.3 mV (Supp. F), 112 to 126 mV gives ratios of 0.48 to 0.54, which is the e/2 (or doubled-regime) value, not e/4. The ratios as quoted need periods of about 56 to 63 mV (0.24 x 233 = 56 mV, 0.27 x 233 = 63 mV). The rest of the paper supports the smaller numbers. Fig. S8 says the nu = -5/2 period has a conservative uncertainty of 5.6 mV, and 5.6 mV on 63 mV, combined with the 6% error on Delta V1, gives exactly the quoted +/- 0.03. Errors of 5.6 mV on 112 to 126 mV would give about +/- 0.04 on a ratio near 0.5. So the "112 to 126 mV" range looks like an error, probably a factor of two that came from the doubled-regime data. It must be fixed: as written, the raw periods in the text support the authors' alternative (e/2) reading rather than their e/4 claim. Please also put the absolute Delta V for every state and regime in a table (Fig. 3p shows them, but the text should quote them consistently).

2. The nu = 3/2 magnetic-field data in the doubled regime are described inconsistently.
   - Results (p. 6): for Delta B_double/Delta B_minimal, "nu = 3/2 approaches close to 2 within uncertainty."
   - Discussion (p. 7): in the doubled regime at 3/2, "the magnetic-field periodicity corresponds to p ~ 1", and later 3/2 is one of the states that "fall close to Delta B1/Delta B = 1, as expected in the AB regime."
   These cannot both be true. The minimal regime gives Delta B_3/2 = 5.3 +/- 1.4 mT, so p ~ 1 means Delta B_double ~ Delta B1 ~ 40 mT, a ratio near 7.6, not 2. A ratio of 2 would mean Delta B_double ~ 11 mT and p ~ 3.6 (the bunching value). Whichever is correct, the 3/2 data then supports a different interpretation (bunching versus AB), so this needs resolving and the actual Delta B_double with its error should be quoted.

3. The claim that the doubled gate ratios "do not match the corresponding filling factors" (p. 7) is only half right, and the part it skips matters. In the AB limit the paper cites, Delta V/Delta V1 = |nu|. The doubled-regime ratios are about 0.5 at nu = -1/2 and about 0.64 (2 x 0.32) at nu = 2/3. Those do equal |nu|. Only -5/2 and 3/2 clearly fail. These same two states (-1/2 and 2/3) are the ones whose doubled oscillations survive to 120 mK, while the -5/2 and 3/2 doubled oscillations die near 50 to 60 mK with the e/4 ones (Supp. L). At 2/3 the doubled B period is also near Delta B1 (p/2 = 1 and AB both give 1 there). So the data allow a simpler picture than the text offers: an AB-like or mixed crossover at -1/2 and 2/3, and something tied to the e/4 physics at -5/2 and 3/2. The authors should say this directly instead of dismissing the AB reading for all states based on -5/2 and 3/2. The anomalous p ~ 1/3 at -1/2 is still unexplained either way (see point 5).

4. Two incompatible statements about the temperature scale.
   - Main text (p. 8): the e/4 oscillations at -5/2 and 3/2 disappear "at approximately 60 mK."
   - Supp. L, first part: e/4 oscillations at all three even-denominator states are "suppressed between 50 mK and 70 mK."
   - Supp. L, last part: "All the e/4 oscillations ... are suppressed in the same range of 30 mK to 50 mK, which places an upper bound on the electron temperature."
   The last statement disagrees with the first two and with the temperature steps (16, 30, 50, 70 mK, where the oscillations are listed as surviving at 50 mK). It also feeds the electron-temperature claim of 30 to 50 mK, which the paper uses (p. 5) to explain why it resolves e/3 at 2/3 where Ref. [30] saw 2e/3. Please settle on one number. Also note that the bath temperature at which oscillations vanish bounds the electron temperature at base only loosely. An independent thermometer (for example the width of Coulomb peaks in the integer regime, or noise thermometry) would make the "lower temperature explains e/3 at 2/3" argument much stronger.

5. The B-period values in the doubled regime at -1/2 are too weak to carry an interpretation. Fig. S9 says the doubled-regime map does not contain even one full B period, and the period comes from a graphical estimate of half a period. With a field step of 1 mT and about 101 points, the scan window is about 100 mT, while p ~ 1/3 implies Delta B ~ 120 mT. The "anomalously large" period, the "factor of ten" area argument on p. 8, and the conclusion that -1/2 "remains anomalous within both pictures" all rest on that half-period estimate. Please take a wider B sweep, or at least show the estimate and its uncertainty clearly in Fig. 3r,s and Fig. 4a and soften the text. Also, the "factor of ten" compares Delta B_double with Delta B_minimal directly. Against what bunching predicts (2 x 13 = 26 mT) the area would have to shrink about 4.6 times, and against the AB value (Delta B1) about 3 times. Please state the comparison you mean.

6. The minimal-regime B periods support p only weakly, and at -1/2 there is mild tension. Using Delta B1 = 40.3 +/- 6.7 mT:
   - -5/2: 40.3/5.9 = 6.8 +/- 3.3 (expected 10)
   - -1/2: 40.3/13 = 3.1 +/- 0.65 (expected 2, about 1.7 sigma off, even including the 8% area term)
   - 3/2: 40.3/5.3 = 7.6 +/- 2.3 (expected 6)
   The text says these "correspond approximately to p = 10, 2, and 6." That is fair for 3/2, loose for -5/2 (where the uncertainty is about 50% and cannot rule out p = 5 or less), and a stretch for -1/2. The B periods are short compared with the 1 mT step (5 to 6 points per period at -5/2 and 3/2). The two-point moving average along B also slightly suppresses those short periods. The B data therefore add little beyond the gate ratios. The paper should say so, and should not present Fig. 4a as independent confirmation of the e/4 picture at -5/2. The citations [2, 33] for these p values also look misplaced: p = |nu|/(q/e) comes from the antidot literature ([25, 26, 30]).

7. The calibration chain needs more justification.
   - The reference (nu = -1) is taken at 10 T and applied at 14 T. The only check is nu = -4/3, giving 0.37 +/- 0.04 against 1/3, which is 11% high. If the lever arm shifts by a similar amount between the reference and the even-denominator fillings, it would move 0.27 at -5/2 toward 0.3, between e/4 and e/3. A reference at 14 T with ADG sweeps (Fig. S3 says the 14 T nu = -1 data are noisy, but they still give a value), or a 14 T integer check at nu = -2, would help.
   - The ratio method assumes the ADG-to-AD charge lever arm does not depend on filling. Since the authors argue the ADG makes area oscillations (Supp. E), and the AD-bound orbit moves by about a magnetic length per edge mode (Methods), please estimate how much the lever arm can change between nu = -1 and nu = -5/2 (two more filled levels) and add that to the gate-ratio error, as was done for Delta B.
   - Supp. E: the series-capacitor estimate depends strongly on the poorly known bridge height (160 nm, "can vary significantly"). It uses 50 nm of hBN where 46 nm was measured, and predicts 330 mV against 235 mV measured, about 40% off. Calling that "agreement" that "confirms that the ADG oscillations are area oscillations rather than density oscillations" goes too far, because no density-oscillation prediction is given to compare with. Either state what a density mechanism would predict or soften the conclusion.

8. The null results at the odd-denominator states do not test comparable coupling. The idea that doubling depends on edge structure (perhaps neutral modes) depends on showing no doubling at -4/3 and -1/3 at a coupling similar to where doubling shows up at the even-denominator and 2/3 states. But -1/3 was measured at only two SG settings (-2 V and 0 V). For -4/3 the paper gives SG voltages (up to 5 V) but no measure of coupling. A voltage is not a coupling: the same SG voltage gives a different constriction density at different fillings. Please report a common coupling measure for every state at the crossover (constriction filling from Rd, background transmission or Rt, or peak-to-valley ratio), so that readers can see whether -1/3 and -4/3 really reached the regime where the other states double.

9. The nu = 2/3 instability makes the e/3 claim weaker than presented. Supp. K blames the hysteresis on two nearly degenerate configurations that "favour the localisation of either e/3 or 2e/3 quasiparticles on the AD." If the system can switch between e/3 and 2e/3 localization, then the "doubled period" at 2/3, and possibly parts of the minimal-regime map, could reflect that switching rather than an SG-driven crossover. The statement that the instability "cannot be related to quasiparticles entering and leaving the AD-bulk" is given without evidence. Please justify it or remove it, and discuss how the switching picture fits with presenting 2/3 as a clean e/3 measurement. Also, only 20 repeats were taken, and the first 6 are transient. Please say how many repeats per field went into the histograms, and whether the transient sweeps were dropped.

10. Reproducibility. All the data appear to come from one device and, as far as I can tell, one cooldown. Please say so explicitly. If a second device or a thermal cycle exists, even with less complete data, include it, especially for the e/4 ratio at one filling. Releasing the data publicly instead of "upon reasonable request" would help, given how much depends on fit choices (Gaussian fits to 2D-FFT line cuts, switching to histogram errors where the FFT fit is "not considered reliable").

MINOR ISSUES

- Abstract and conclusion: "compatible with a non-Abelian ground state" is true but weak, since e/4 is also the minimal charge of the Abelian 331 and K = 8 states, as the introduction says. The abstract should say just as clearly that the charge measurement does not tell these candidates apart.
- p. 7: "magnetic field periodicities at nu = -2/3 and nu = 3/2" should read nu = 2/3. No -2/3 data appear in the paper.
- p. 6, Discussion: "As shown in Fig. 3d,e for nu = 2/3, and similarly for nu = -1/2 in Fig. 3g,i" has the panels swapped. Per the caption and the earlier text, d-f is -1/2 and g-i is 2/3.
- Methods C: "an additional 8% uncertainty ... when taking ratios of gate voltage periods to determine Delta B1/Delta B" should say magnetic-field periods.
- Methods C: the 8% area figure is 2 x 6.9/180, about 7.7%. That is fine, but it assumes one magnetic length per extra edge mode. At -5/2 there are two more filled levels than at -1, so the shift may be larger. Please state the assumption per filling.
- p. 3: the AD diameter (361 +/- 30 nm) comes from Delta B1 at 10 T and is applied at 14 T. Please also say that the effective diameter may depend on field and filling. "Consistent with the lithographic diameter of approximately 210 nm" needs a sentence on the expected electrostatic broadening (about 150 nm here, roughly 3 hBN thicknesses).
- p. 3: the symbol D is used for both the displacement field and the AD diameter.
- Supp. L: model selection by R^2 of linearized fits over T < 2 T0 cannot tell exponential from power-law decay, as the authors admit. That part could be shortened, or a log-likelihood or AIC comparison added.
- Supp. L defines shorthand "e/2" and "2e/3" for the doubled oscillations and stresses that these are labels only. Please also use "doubled-period" in the main-text temperature paragraph, so that readers do not see "e/2" and assume it is a measured charge.
- Fig. S16 caption: steps are 16, 30, 50, 70, 95, 120 mK except panel a (12.7 to 70 mK). If the -5/2 e/4 data stop at 70 mK, say whether the oscillations are actually gone by 70 mK or the run simply ended there.
- Typos and wording: "FHQ" (Fig. 2 caption); "shwon" and "sweeped" (Supp. E, Fig. S2); "we have measured reported oscillation periods" (Conclusion); "a transition toward an Aharonov-Bohm-dominated." (missing noun, p. 6); "interferometers experiments has reported" (p. 6); "a a factor" (p. 8); "find Delta Vtg..." missing "we" (Supp. G); "Using the more precise nu = -1 data at 10" missing "T" (Supp. F); "closer to the measured value" (Supp. E, closer than what?); "Table resuming" should be "Table summarizing" (Fig. S18, and the caption title is repeated).

ASSESSMENT

The experiment is valuable and the e/4 gate-period ratios at three fillings, if confirmed with corrected numbers, are a worthwhile result. Before the paper can be judged I would need: (i) the Delta V values in the e/4 paragraph corrected (as written they imply about e/2), (ii) the nu = 3/2 doubled-regime B period made consistent, (iii) the temperature numbers made consistent, and (iv) a fairer treatment of the AB reading at -1/2 and 2/3, plus a common coupling measure behind the "no doubling at odd-denominator states" claim. With those changes, plus the calibration and reproducibility points, I would support publication. The crossover discussion should stay framed as open, as it mostly is now.

TL;DR: An interesting, mostly careful paper, but its numbers are internally inconsistent. Most important: the text says the e/4 periods are 112 to 126 mV, but with Delta V1 = 233 mV that gives ratios near 0.5 (e/2), not the quoted 0.24 to 0.27. The quoted errors point to about 56 to 63 mV, so the paragraph appears to be off by a factor of two. The nu = 3/2 doubled B period is also described two incompatible ways (ratio near 2, and p ~ 1), and the e/4 suppression temperature is given as both 50 to 70 mK and 30 to 50 mK. The doubled gate ratios at -1/2 and 2/3 do match the AB value |nu|, which the paper says they do not. The -1/2 B period comes from half a period. The null tests at -1/3 and -4/3 lack a common coupling measure. My verdict is major revision.
