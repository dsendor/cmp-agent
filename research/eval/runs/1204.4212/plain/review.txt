Referee report

Manuscript: "Observation of the fractional a.c. Josephson effect and the signature of Majorana particles"
L. P. Rokhinson, X. Liu, J. K. Furdyna (arXiv:1204.4212v2)


1. Summary of the claims

The authors fabricate Nb/InSb/Nb weak links in which the InSb quantum well is etched into a
nanowire self-aligned to a 120 nm wide Nb strip. They study Shapiro steps under 2 to 4 GHz
irradiation. At B = 0 they see steps of height hf0/2e (6 uV at 3 GHz), with step widths that
follow Bessel functions at high rf power. With an in-plane field parallel to the current, the
first step (6 uV) disappears above about 2 T while steps at 12 and 18 uV remain up to about
3 T, after which all steps vanish. They interpret the missing first step as a doubling of the
fundamental step to hf0/e, i.e. the 4pi-periodic fractional ac Josephson effect, and call it
"a unique signature of Majorana fermions" and "a hallmark of topological superconductivity".

The motivation is good. The ac Josephson effect is in principle a cleaner probe than a zero
bias conductance peak, since parity relaxation that masks 4pi periodicity in dc measurements
can be outrun at finite frequency, and a missing odd step is not obviously mimicked by Kondo
or 0.7 physics. A clear demonstration would be important. However, as presented, the data
establish a field-induced suppression of the n = 1 step in a single device over a roughly
1 T window. They do not establish that this suppression is caused by a 4pi-periodic
current-phase relation, and the paper does not rule out conventional mechanisms that are
known to suppress low-order Shapiro steps. The abstract and conclusions substantially
overstate what is shown. I recommend major revision.


2. Major concerns

2.1 Only the n = 1 step disappears; the n = 3 step survives.
A 4pi-periodic supercurrent produces steps at multiples of hf0/e, so in the 2e counting all
odd steps (6, 18, 30 uV at 3 GHz) should be suppressed, not just the first. In Fig. 3 and
Fig. 4 the 18 uV step is present, and even described as "prominent", for B > 2 T. The
explanation offered ("enhanced mixing between gapless and gapped modes" at higher voltage) is
qualitative and is introduced after the fact. The cited theory (Dominguez et al., ref. 43)
predicts suppression of odd steps in general. The authors need either to show that the n = 3
step is quantitatively reduced relative to expectation at B > 2 T, or to give a model that
predicts the survival of n = 3 while n = 1 vanishes, with parameters fixed independently.
As it stands, the selective loss of only the first step points toward a low-power, low-
frequency dynamical effect rather than a change in periodicity.

2.2 Conventional mechanisms for a missing first step are not excluded.
The junction is, by the authors' own description, in an intermediate damping, hysteretic
regime (IR ~ Ic), strongly shunted by substrate leakage (Rs < 1 kOhm), and driven at a
frequency far below its characteristic frequency: hf0/2e = 6 uV compared with IcRN ~ 1 mV
(or VR ~ 60 uV on the measured, shunted curve). In the RCSJ/RCLSJ model with finite
capacitance, in this low-frequency, hysteretic regime the low-order steps are known to be
narrow, distorted, or absent at low drive, and step visibility depends sensitively on Ic, on
the damping, and on the bias-dependent resistance. Applying a field reduces Ic and changes
the effective damping and shunt, so a field-driven loss of the n = 1 step at low Vrf is a
natural expectation even with a purely 2pi current-phase relation. The observation that the
6 uV step "re-appears at high Vrf > 10 mV for B = 2.2 T" is more consistent with this kind of
regime effect than with a topological change, which should remove the step at all powers.
The manuscript needs:
  (a) RCSJ (with capacitance and the measured shunt) simulations using the measured Ic(B),
      IR(B), and Rn, showing that a 2pi junction would keep the n = 1 step in the B > 2 T
      regime at the measured drive levels;
  (b) the dimensionless frequency Omega = h f0 / (2e Ic RN) (equivalently f0/fc) and the
      Stewart-McCumber parameter as a function of B, so readers can see whether the
      junction moves into a regime where n = 1 is expected to be lost anyway.

2.3 The decisive frequency dependence at high field is missing.
Frequency dependence (Fig. S10) is shown only at B = 0, where it merely confirms hf0/2e.
The key test is f0 dependence at B > 2 T. A 4pi contribution competing with 2pi current and
with parity relaxation should become more visible at lower frequency (or show a crossover
frequency tied to the gap and poisoning rates), while dynamical artifacts from the low-
frequency regime have a different, and testable, trend. Showing the missing step at several
frequencies with a consistent crossover would greatly strengthen the claim. Without it, the
B > 2 T data at a single frequency (3 GHz) cannot distinguish the interpretations.

2.4 There is no independent evidence that the wire is in the topological regime.
  - The devices have no gate, so EF cannot be tuned. A phase transition that moves with
    chemical potential is the standard control.
  - The electron density is not measured. In Section I of the SI the authors "assume that at
    B = 2 Tesla (observed topological phase transition) EZ = EF" and then use this to infer k,
    ESO, and Delta(B). This is circular: the parameter estimate that is later said to
    "satisfy the requirements for observation of Majorana fermions" is derived by assuming
    the transition occurs where the step disappears.
  - Field orientation controls are absent. The topological phase requires B perpendicular to
    Bso. The authors identify Bso as in-plane and perpendicular to the wire. Rotating B in
    plane to lie along Bso (perpendicular to I), and applying B out of plane, should
    respectively destroy and drastically alter the effect. These measurements are cheap
    relative to their diagnostic value and are the most direct control available in an
    ungated device.
  - The dc SQUID measurement said to show only 2pi periodicity is mentioned but not shown,
    and the field at which it was taken is not given.

2.5 Reproducibility and statistics.
All high-field Shapiro data come from a single junction (JJ8), and lithographically JJ8 is a
two-junction SQUID with an asymmetry r > 10. The paper should state how many devices were
measured under rf in field, how many showed the missing first step, and how many did not.
Data from JJ7 (r = 7.3), if available, should be shown. Reproducibility after thermal
cycling and on field sweeps up and down (Nb vortex trapping is noted in SI Section V) should
be reported.

2.6 Proximity gap and adiabaticity are marginal by the authors' own numbers.
The estimated induced gap at 2 T is about 30 ueV and at 3 T about 20 ueV, compared with
h f0 = 12 ueV (the energy of the doubled step) at 3 GHz. Under these conditions Landau-Zener
transitions into the continuum and quasiparticle poisoning are expected to restore 2pi
behaviour, not reveal 4pi behaviour. The authors should discuss the expected poisoning and
diabatic rates, and explain why 4pi periodicity would survive. Relatedly, Delta is inferred
from the resistive Tc of the junction (Delta = 1.76 kB Tc), which is a weak link transition,
not a spectroscopic measure of the induced gap. A tunneling or multiple Andreev reflection
measurement of Delta would be valuable.

2.7 The IcRN product is inconsistent with the stated gap.
The SI concludes the junction is in the clean limit because IcRN ~ 1 mV exceeds 320 Tc and is
"almost twice as large" as the dirty limit. But the clean (KO-2) limit for Delta = 180 ueV is
eIcRN = pi*Delta ~ 0.57 meV, and 480 Tc[K] = 0.56 mV for Tc = 1.17 K. A measured value of
~1 mV exceeds even the clean-limit bound by nearly a factor of two. This means either RN is
overestimated (it is inferred, not measured, through a parallel-shunt model with an assumed
Rs and an assumed factor of 2 for the second arm) or the relevant gap is larger than 180 ueV.
Either way, the conclusion "JJ8 is in the clean regime" does not follow, and the estimates
of the number of channels and of Delta that feed into Section I become uncertain.

2.8 Bessel-function fits use field-dependent free parameters.
The identification of the 12 uV step as the new n = 1 step rests on one minimum of its width
near Vrf ~ 7 mV coinciding with a zero of J1. But the conversion beta changes between fits
(0.84 vs 1.04 vs 0.89 mV^-1 in Fig. 4 and Fig. S11), and the text notes that the low field
attenuation "does not fit" the n = 0 plateau and that evolution at B > 2 T is "poorly
described by Bessel functions". With beta free, matching a single minimum is weak evidence.
The rf coupling at the sample should be field independent; a fit with beta fixed from B = 0,
and a joint fit of all steps using a 2pi plus 4pi current-phase relation (as in RSJ
simulations with Ic sin(phi) + IM sin(phi/2)), would be much more convincing and would
yield IM/Ic quantitatively.

2.9 Role of the Nb wetting layer and the SQUID geometry in field.
The 2 to 4 nm Nb layer that fills the gap is argued to be non-conducting based on test
devices on GaAs. On InSb, with a different surface and with strong proximity coupling, this
is not guaranteed, and a thin disordered Nb film in the gap could itself form a weak link with
non-trivial behaviour in field. In addition, the device is a SQUID; a small misalignment of
the in-plane field threads flux through the loop (1 degree at 2 T is about 35 mT). Please
state the alignment procedure and accuracy, and show Ic(B) for the specific field range
2 to 3 T.


3. Presentation and smaller issues

- Abstract and conclusions: "unique signature of Majorana fermions" and "unique signature of
  a topological quantum phase transition" are not supported (see 2.1 to 2.4). A wording such
  as "consistent with" a 4pi contribution, with the alternatives discussed, is appropriate.
- Abstract: "supercurrent is carried by charge-e quasiparticles" is loose. The 4pi effect
  reflects fermion-parity-conserving single-electron transfer through a Majorana-coupled
  level, not free charge-e quasiparticles.
- Wire width is given as "<~290 nm" in the main text and "~170 nm" in the SI; Nb thickness
  as 45 nm and 40 nm; Tc1 as 6.4 K, 6.3 K and 7.5 K (different substrates). Please reconcile
  and state which values apply to JJ8.
- Zeeman energy is defined as g muB B / 2 in the main text and g* muB B in the SI.
- The condition EZ > sqrt(Delta^2 + EF^2) is described in the SI as "lifting of the Kramers
  degeneracy". Any field lifts Kramers degeneracy; this is the topological criterion, with
  EF measured from the band bottom (i.e. the chemical potential mu). Also "Kramers", not
  "Kramer's".
- "Vrf ~ 7 meV" should be mV; "Vc = IR RN ~ 65 ueV" should be uV; "Omega_J = 2e/hbar V"
  should be omega_J = 2eV/hbar. The vrf attenuation is given as 5e-3 Vrf (main) and 5.4e-3
  Vrf (SI).
- The small-signal step width formula Delta I1 = 2 Ic I_omega / I-bar is used without
  defining I-bar.
- Fig. 2: the measured hysteresis, IR and Ic should be quoted numerically, along with their
  field dependence, since they matter for 2.2.
- Fig. 3 right: label the Vrf of each trace and mark expected step positions for both 2e and
  e periodicity.
- The SI refers to Fig. S12 for detailed field dependence, but the analysis of step width
  versus B (as opposed to versus Vrf) is not presented. A colour map of dV/dI versus I and B
  at fixed Vrf, and step widths versus B for n = 1, 2, 3, would show whether n = 1 vanishes
  abruptly (transition) or fades smoothly with Ic (dynamics).
- Intro: Majorana's paper is from 1937, so "ca. 80 years ago" is about 75 years.
- Ref. 33 is "Refer to the Supplementary Information" and can be removed in favour of
  in-text pointers.


4. Requested additions (in rough order of importance)

  1. RCSJ simulations with measured parameters showing that a purely 2pi junction would
     retain the n = 1 step at B > 2 T (concern 2.2).
  2. Frequency dependence of the missing step at B > 2 T (2.3).
  3. In-plane rotation of B toward Bso and out-of-plane field controls (2.4).
  4. Data from additional devices and a count of devices showing and not showing the effect
     (2.5).
  5. Joint fits with fixed rf coupling and a two-component current-phase relation (2.8).
  6. Treatment of the n = 3 step (2.1).
  7. Corrected or justified IcRN and RN estimates (2.7).


5. Assessment

The experiment addresses a central question, uses a probe that is in principle more
discriminating than zero bias peaks, and the B = 0 characterisation (frequency scaling and
Bessel dependence) is carefully done. The central high-field observation is interesting and
worth publishing in some form. But the evidence that the missing step reflects a 4pi-periodic
supercurrent is indirect: one device, one frequency, no gate, no orientation control, a
surviving odd step, free fit parameters, and a junction regime where conventional dynamics
can suppress the first step. I recommend major revision, with the claims reframed as
"consistent with a fractional ac Josephson effect" unless the controls above are added.


Note for the editor (context, not part of the scientific assessment above; from memory, not
re-checked): this preprint was later published (Nature Physics, 2012). Subsequent work has
shown that missing first Shapiro steps can arise in topologically trivial junctions, for
example in InAs quantum well junctions (Dartiailh et al., PRL 2021) and in models of
bias-dependent resistance (Mudi and Frolov). That later literature reinforces concern 2.2.
