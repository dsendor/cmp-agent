Review of "Trapping e/4 quasiparticles in bilayer graphene" (Di Luca, Hajigeorgiou, Ma, Waldherr, Watanabe, Taniguchi, Banerjee; arXiv:2608.27444)

SUMMARY

The authors build a gate-defined quantum Hall antidot (AD) in Bernal bilayer graphene and add a suspended air-bridge gate (ADG) that couples mainly to the AD potential. That lets them drive AD oscillations without pushing the bulk off the narrow even-denominator plateaus. In what they call the weak-coupling, Coulomb-dominated (CD) regime they report normalized ADG periods dV/dV1 = 0.27 +/- 0.03, 0.24 +/- 0.03 and 0.25 +/- 0.03 at nu = -5/2, -1/2 and 3/2. They read these as the localized AD charge changing in steps of e/4. At nu = 2/3 they find 0.32 +/- 0.03, read as e/3. When the side gates push the extended edges toward the AD, the gate period doubles at all four of these states but not at nu = -4/3 or -1/3. The authors discuss two explanations, quasiparticle bunching (2e*) and a crossover toward an Aharonov-Bohm (AB) or mixed regime, and say they cannot decide between them.

The air-bridge gate is a real technical advance. It is the right tool for states whose plateaus are only a few tens of mV wide in top-gate voltage. The paper is unusually frank about the limits of its doubled-period interpretation. The central e/4 result is plausible and would be an important addition to the BLG even-denominator literature, which so far rests mostly on interferometry. But the e/4 assignment depends on a single lever-arm calibration carried across magnetic field and carrier sign. The magnetic-field periods that should independently confirm the CD regime have large uncertainties and scatter. The doubled-period section has a hidden degeneracy that the authors do not point out. Finally, the abstract's link to non-Abelian physics claims more than the data can carry. I recommend major revision.

MAJOR ISSUES

1. The charge assignment depends on transferring one lever arm across field, filling and carrier sign.

Every charge in the paper comes from q/e = dV/dV1, with dV1 = 233 +/- 14 mV measured at nu = -1 and B = 10 T. This is valid only if the ADG-to-AD coupling (in the CD regime, the gate-to-localized-charge capacitance) is the same for the reference and for each state. Several things vary between them:

(a) Field. The reference is at 10 T and all the fractional data are at 14 T. The only check at 14 T is nu = -4/3, which gives 0.37 +/- 0.04 against the expected 1/3. That is about 11% high. If it reflects a real 10 T to 14 T shift in lever arm, it is as large as the full error bar on the e/4 values. Please discuss whether this offset should be applied as a correction or folded into the systematic error.

(b) Carrier sign. All four calibration points in Fig. S3 (nu = -1 at 10 T and 14 T, nu = -2, nu = -4/3) are on the hole side. Two of the headline states, nu = 3/2 and 2/3, are on the electron side. There the AD potential has the opposite sign relative to the carriers, the side gates have opposite polarity, and V_Si is flipped from +40 V to -40 V. Nothing in the paper shows that the hole-side dV1 applies on the electron side. An electron-side integer reference (nu = +1 or +2, ideally at 14 T) is needed before the e/4 at 3/2 and e/3 at 2/3 can be taken at face value.

(c) Orbital and edge position. The even-denominator states sit in the N = 1 orbital, and their AD-bound orbits lie several edge channels inside the nu = -1 orbit. The ADG-to-orbit coupling of an air-bridge gate about 160 nm above the hBN is probably not very sensitive to a shift of a few l_B. Still, a short estimate of how C_adg changes with orbit radius would help.

A related internal tension: Supplementary Section E concludes that "the ADG oscillations are area oscillations rather than density oscillations." That conclusion is used to rule out the slope method. But if the ADG were literally an area gate at fixed density, then in the CD regime one would expect dV/dV1 to scale as (q/e)/|nu| (charge nu*e*B*dA/phi0 per area step), with an extra factor B_ref/B between the 10 T reference and 14 T. For nu = -4/3 that predicts about 0.18, not the 0.37 observed. The data instead fit a purely capacitive picture, dV = q/C. The capacitance model in Section E also misses the measured dV1 by about 40% (330 vs 235 mV), uses an air-bridge height that "can vary significantly," and uses a 50 nm hBN thickness where Methods says 46 nm. Calling this "agreement" is generous. Please give one consistent electrostatic picture of what the ADG couples to, and use it for both the CD expectation (q/e) and the AB-limit expectation (the paper quotes dV/dV1 = nu from Ref. 48, which itself presupposes a gate model).

2. The magnetic-field periods only weakly confirm the CD regime, and they scatter in ways the text does not acknowledge.

The B-field ratios are the only independent check that the system is in the CD regime, and therefore that dV/dV1 measures charge. They are weak checks:

- nu = -5/2: 40.3/5.9 = 6.8, about +/- 3.3 once the 44% error on dB, the 17% error on dB1 and the 8% area term are combined. The expected p = 10 is within 1 sigma, but so is p = 5 (the e/2 value).
- nu = -1/2: 40.3/13 = 3.1 +/- 0.6 against an expected p = 2. That is about 1.7 sigma high, in the direction away from both e/4 and e/2.
- nu = 3/2: 7.6 +/- 2.4 against an expected 6.
- nu = 2/3: 2.32 +/- 0.93, presented as consistent with p = 2 but equally consistent with p = 1 or 3.
- Calibration at nu = -4/3: 3.47 +/- 0.79 against an expected 4.

The field step is 1 mT over about 100 mT, so the shortest periods (5 to 6 mT) get only 5 to 6 samples per period. The two-point moving average along B is a low-pass filter (|cos(pi f dB)|, about 0.85 at these frequencies), so it also slightly distorts both amplitude and peak position near the short-period end. Please (i) give the full error budget for each ratio in a table, (ii) apply the area correction per state (two or more extra edges for -5/2 should give more than the single-l_B, 8% figure, and l_B differs between 10 T and 14 T), and (iii) where you can, take finer or longer B sweeps for -5/2 and 3/2. Also, the formula p = |nu|/(q/e) used for the expected values should be stated and derived or cited from the antidot literature. Refs. [2, 33] are not appropriate sources for it.

3. In the doubled-period regime, the gate period cannot tell bunching from AB, and the state selectivity does not favor bunching.

The two scenarios predict the same gate ratios at every state studied. Bunching gives 2e*/e = 1/2 at -5/2, -1/2 and 3/2, and 2/3 at nu = 2/3. The nu_LL-AB phenomenology the authors invoke (Refs. 22-24) also gives 1/2, 1/2, 1/2 and 2/3. The paper notes this "striking correspondence" but does not say plainly that the gate data cannot discriminate.

The same holds for the controls. With nu_LL defined from |nu| (the paper should state its convention; nu - floor(nu) gives 2/3 for -1/3 and -4/3, while |nu| - floor(|nu|) gives 1/3), the nu_LL-AB picture predicts no change in gate period at -4/3 and -1/3, exactly as bunching does. So the selective appearance of doubling, which the paper twice offers as evidence against an AB origin ("an AB crossover alone does not naturally explain why the doubled periodicity appears selectively"), is predicted equally by the AB phenomenology.

The discrimination therefore rests entirely on the magnetic-field periods, which are the least precise quantities in the paper (point 2):

- At nu = 2/3, bunching (p/2 = 1) and AB (p = 1) predict the same B period. The 2/3 data cannot discriminate at all, yet the text counts 2/3 as "consistent with" bunching.
- At nu = -1/3, CD and AB predict the same gate ratio (1/3) and the same p (1). This state is uninformative as a control for an AB crossover. Only nu = -4/3, where p stays at 4 up to V_sg = 5 V, rules out AB, and only for that state.
- Taking the doubled-regime values at face value, -5/2 looks like bunching, 3/2 looks like AB, and -1/2 looks like neither.

The paper's eventual conclusion ("intermediate or mixed regime") may be right, but the argument leading to it should be rewritten around this degeneracy. The cleanest regime discriminator in Fabry-Perot work is the sign and slope of the constant-phase lines in the gate-B plane. That information is already in the 2D-FFT (the signed positions of the f_V and f_B peaks). The slope method was set aside because the ADG is not a pure density gate, but the stripe orientation can still be compared between the minimal and doubled regimes at the same gate settings. If the orientation changes across the crossover, that is direct evidence of a regime change. If it does not, the bunching interpretation gains a great deal. Please report it for every map in Figs. 2 and 3.

4. Peak pairing, a known non-Abelian Coulomb-blockade signature, is a third candidate that the paper does not consider.

For a Moore-Read-type state in a Coulomb-blockaded geometry, theory predicts peak spacings that alternate with quasiparticle-number parity, and peaks that pair into e/2-like doublets when the neutral (Majorana) edge level spacing is comparable to the charging energy (Ilan, Grosfeld and Stern; Stern, Rosenow, Ilan and Halperin; Ref. [47] is cited but this point is not discussed). Unequal alternating spacings s1, s2 with s1 + s2 = 2*dV produce Fourier weight at both 1/dV and 1/(2dV). That is exactly the "intermediate regime where both periodicities are superimposed" in Fig. 3e,h. Increasing the AD-edge coupling shifts the energy balance and could turn alternation into full pairing.

The doubling at the Abelian 2/3 state shows that pairing cannot be the whole story. Still, the even-denominator data should be checked directly: sort consecutive peak spacings into odd and even, look for bimodality and alternation in the spacing sequence (not just the pooled histogram), and check whether peak amplitudes alternate. The intermediate-regime datasets are the most valuable ones for this test. A positive result would greatly strengthen the paper. A negative result would usefully constrain the neutral-mode velocity.

The reverse concern applies to the e/4 claim itself. Two interleaved series of e/2 resonances, for example from the AD and a nearby localized state in the constriction, would produce an apparent e/4 period. A unimodal distribution of consecutive spacings at e/4, with no alternation in amplitude or B-slope, would rule this out. Please show it explicitly for -5/2, -1/2 and 3/2.

5. The constriction filling factor changes in exactly the regime under discussion.

The text says "as the absolute value of the constriction filling factor is reduced, the minimal-excitation oscillations evolve into an intermediate regime." So the doubled regime coincides with the constriction leaving the bulk filling. The fractional fluid near the AD may then be a different state, or a lower-density region may form between the side gates and the AD (the paper raises this possibility for -1/2 and cites Ref. [51]). The diagonal resistance R_d is said to be measured simultaneously to verify the constriction filling, but no R_d values are shown for any dataset. Please report R_d (and so the constriction filling) and R_xx for each minimal and doubled dataset in Figs. 2, 3 and S8-S12.

Relatedly, coupling should be compared by a measured quantity, such as the transmitted resistance background or the backscattered fraction, not by V_sg. That is what it takes to show that the -4/3 and -1/3 controls reached couplings comparable to those at which the even-denominator and 2/3 states doubled. Right now -1/3 is shown only at V_sg = -2 V and 0 V.

6. Excitation amplitude relative to the relevant energy scales.

The AC current is 0.2 to 1 nA. In the AD geometry, the edges passing on either side of the AD differ in potential by roughly I*h/(nu*e^2). At nu = 1/2 that is about 10 to 50 uV, compared with k_B*T of about 4 uV at 50 mK and with even-denominator gaps of about 1 K (about 85 ueV). For the DC-bias maps, the 10 uV AC excitation is also several times k_B*T_e. Please give the actual excitation used for each dataset, the corresponding voltage across the AD, and the charging energy from the diamonds, and show that the oscillation periods do not depend on excitation in the range used. This also matters for the temperature argument (point 7) and for the claim that a lower T_e explains why e/3 appears here where earlier work found 2e/3.

7. Electron temperature and the temperature analysis.

The paper quotes T_e of "30 to 50 mK," says the e/4 oscillations disappear at "approximately 60 mK" (main text), "between 50 mK and 70 mK" (Supp. L, paragraph 2) and "in the same range of 30 mK to 50 mK" (Supp. L, end), and treats the last of these as an upper bound on T_e. These numbers need to be reconciled. The reasoning that oscillation suppression bounds T_e is also loose: the disappearance temperature depends on the coupling-limited linewidth, the excitation and the level spacing, not only on T_e. An independent thermometer (for example, a Coulomb peak width in a known regime, or noise thermometry) would make the comparison with the 150 mK of Ref. [30] meaningful.

The exponential-versus-power-law comparison by R^2 of linearized fits is not a proper model comparison, as the authors concede (T at most about 2*T0). The A0*T^-alpha form also diverges as T goes to 0 and holds only for k_B*T well above the level spacing. I suggest presenting this section as non-diagnostic and shortening it.

The argument that -5/2 and 3/2 doubled oscillations "originate from the same underlying e/4 quasiparticles" because they vanish at a similar temperature is weak. Two oscillation families with different coupling can easily share a disappearance temperature by coincidence.

8. The abstract and conclusion claim more than the results support.

The introduction correctly says that e/4 is expected for both Abelian (for example 331 and 113) and non-Abelian candidates, so observing it is necessary but not sufficient. The abstract then says the results, "together with previous observations of the daughter states, show that the even-denominator FQH states in bilayer graphene are compatible with a non-Abelian ground state." The e/4 measurement adds essentially nothing to that statement beyond what the daughter-state work already established. I suggest rephrasing so the claim is what the data show: the localized AD charge changes in steps of e/4, the expected minimal charge for all leading paired candidates. Likewise, "a necessary ingredient for topological quantum computation" should be tied to what was shown (sequential e/4 changes in the AD charge in a many-quasiparticle CD regime), not to deterministic single-quasiparticle control, which the conclusion correctly defers to smaller antidots.

The novelty statement should also be sharpened against Refs. [22-24]. The introduction itself notes that in those interferometers the enclosed localized excitations carry e/4. The real advance here is a direct conductance-based charge readout in an AD geometry, and the paper should say so.

MINOR ISSUES

- Fig. 3 panel references are swapped in the Discussion. The caption assigns d-f to nu = -1/2 and g-i to nu = 2/3, but the text says "Fig. 3d,e for nu = 2/3, and similarly for nu = -1/2 in Fig. 3g,i."
- "the magnetic field periodicities at nu = -2/3 and nu = 3/2 fall close to dB1/dB = 1": presumably +2/3.
- At nu = 2/3 the histogram values (19.6 +/- 4 mT minimal, 30.4 +/- 10 mT doubled) give dB_double/dB_min of about 1.55 +/- 0.6. Please confirm this is what Fig. 3s shows. The text says 2/3 is "consistent with a ratio of 2," which is true but equally true of 1.
- The -1/2 doubled-regime B period is estimated "graphically" from less than one full period (Fig. S9). It should be reported as a bound, not a value, and the "p of about 1/3" statement in the main text should be qualified to match.
- The -5/2 minimal-regime gate uncertainty was doubled by hand (2.8 to 5.6 mV). That is conservative and fine, but the same rule should be applied to every state, not only one.
- For nu = 2/3 (Supp. K), give the fraction of spacings in each Gaussian population at each field, so readers can judge how much of the "e/3 regime" is mixed with 2e/3 configurations. The first six of the 20 repetitions show strong hysteresis. Were they excluded?
- AD diameter: 361 +/- 30 nm from dB1, against a 210 nm lithographic hole plus 46 nm hBN. "Consistent" needs a sentence of electrostatics (where the nu = -1 edge sits relative to the hole edge at 10 T).
- Ref. [50] (e/4 at nu = 1/2 in GaAs) is cited for the edge structure of nu = -1/2 in BLG near charge neutrality. Please cite BLG-specific work, or explain the relevance.
- Only one device and, apparently, one cooldown. Please state this explicitly, and say whether the e/4 periods were reproduced at more than one (V_tg, V_bg) point within each plateau and at more than one field.
- The developing states at +1/2 and +5/2 are mentioned but not measured. One sentence on why would help.
- Data availability "upon reasonable request": for a charge-assignment paper resting on FFT and histogram analysis, depositing the raw maps and analysis code would materially help readers check the lever-arm and error budgets.
- Typos and wording: "FHQ" (Fig. 2 caption); "transition toward an Aharonov-Bohm-dominated." (missing noun); "we have measured reported oscillation periods"; "interferometers experiments has reported"; "close to a a factor"; "shwon"; "sweeped"; "Table resuming" (twice, Fig. S18); "at 10 as the reference" (missing T); "Supp. F" refers to Fig. S3 for the -4/3 analysis that appears to be in the same figure as the integer points. Please check the cross-references. The Fig. S18 table is referred to but its contents are not described in the text. Since it is the comparison of scenarios, consider moving it to the main text.

ASSESSMENT

The device and the e/4 gate-period observation are worth publishing. The e/4 assignment at -5/2 and -1/2 is reasonably supported, subject to a clearer lever-arm error budget. The assignments at 3/2 and 2/3 need an electron-side calibration. The doubled-period section should be reorganized around the fact that the gate data do not distinguish bunching from the nu_LL-AB phenomenology, so any discrimination must come from B periods and stripe slopes. A peak-pairing and alternation analysis of the intermediate regime should be added, and the non-Abelian framing in the abstract toned down. With these changes, the paper would be a solid and useful contribution.

Recommendation: major revision.
