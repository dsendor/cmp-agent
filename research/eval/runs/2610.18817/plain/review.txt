Referee report

"Single anyons and anyon binding in a quantum Hall dot" (arXiv:2610.18817v1)

SUMMARY

The authors build a monolayer graphene QPC with a 90 nm hole in a patterned graphite top gate and an aluminum finger gate over the hole. This gives a gate-defined dot inside the QPC saddle, with separately tunable dot occupation and edge-to-dot coupling. At nu = 1 they see Coulomb blockade of backscattering, a quantum Hall ferromagnet spin blockade that separates the quasihole and quasielectron sides, magnon-assisted lifting of that blockade at high bias, excited states at 0.3 to 0.8 meV, and very low-frequency charge noise. At nu = 1/3 and 2/3 the gate period is about three times shorter, which they read as e/3 tunneling. After an electrostatic calibration of N_qp = 0 (tripling B from nu = 1 at 4.5 T), they claim to reach the N_qp = 1 to 5 regime and compare irregular addition spacings with DMRG on the Haldane sphere. At |N_qp| of about 10 to 20 they report a particle-hole-symmetric split: 1/3 quasiholes and 2/3 quasiparticles give regular spectra, while 1/3 quasiparticles and 2/3 quasiholes give irregular ones. Lowering V_T makes alternate charge sectors vanish, leaving a 2e/3 period, which they interpret as anyon binding with E_B > E_C.

The device is impressive. A tunable dot this small and this clean in the FQH regime is a real step, and the integer benchmarks (spin blockade, magnon-assisted transport, the DMRG-matched gap width) are convincing. The particle-hole comparison and the continuous merging of charge-degeneracy lines in Fig. 4f are the most striking data. Several central claims, though, rest on calibrations and assumptions that need tightening before the headline statements ("single anyon," "anyon binding energy at least as large as the charging energy") are fully supported. My main concerns follow, roughly in order of importance.

MAJOR COMMENTS

1. What vacuum the fractional dot sits in. The main text says the nu = 1/3 data in Fig. 1h use nu_edge ~ nu_bulk = nu = 1/3. Table I, however, labels Fig. 1h "nu_bulk = 2/5, nu_dot = 1/3". The same gate configuration (V_T = 0.080 V, V_qpc = -0.40 V, V_edge = -1.25 V) is used for the 1/3 quasihole data in Fig. 1j and Fig. 4a. Extended Data Fig. 7f states explicitly that this configuration puts the bulk at nu_bulk = 2/5. So the "regular 1/3 quasihole" spectrum, a central ingredient of both the e/3 assignment and the particle-hole symmetry argument, appears to come from a dot in a 2/5 environment, not a 1/3 vacuum. The irregular 1/3 quasiparticle data (Fig. 4c, V_T = 0.05 V) come from a different configuration. The authors should:
   (a) state clearly, for every main-text dataset, the bulk filling, the QPC filling and the inferred dot filling;
   (b) explain why the tunneling charge is e/3 and not e/5 when the bulk is at 2/5. The text says e* is "determined by the surrounding bulk," which would predict e/5;
   (c) say whether the particle-hole mapping (1/3 QH <-> 2/3 QP) still holds when the two sides sit in different surrounding fillings.
   As written, the reader cannot tell which comparisons are like-for-like.

2. Gate-configuration confound in Fig. 4a-d. Per Table I, both regular datasets (4a, 4b) use V_edge = -1.25 V, and both irregular datasets (4c, 4d) use V_edge = -4.0 V, with different V_T as well. The regular/irregular split therefore lines up with the edge-gate setting in the main figure. Extended Data Figs. 9 and 10 partly address this. For 2/3 quasiparticles the spectrum stays regular over V_edge from about -3 to 0 V, and for 1/3 quasiparticles it stays irregular over about -4 to -1 V. That overlap is valuable and should be stated in the main text. The 1/3 quasihole sweep, however, covers only V_edge ~ -1.5 to -1.2 V. I would like at least one pair of quasihole and quasiparticle spectra at the same filling taken at identical V_edge, V_qpc and V_T, differing only in V_dot. Positively, the regular and irregular regimes each occur at both extremes of V_dot (around -1 to 0 V and around 3.5 to 4.5 V). This argues against a trivial V_dot-dependent cause and deserves to be said explicitly.

3. Basis of the e/3 assignment. The charge assignment rests on delta = e*/(alpha C_d), under the assumption that alpha and C_d are unchanged between nu = 1 (mostly 5 T) and nu = 1/3 (13.5 T). Fig. 1j shows ratios from about 2.5 (250/100) to about 3.75 (300/80). Both the dielectric screening of the dot gate (Extended Data Fig. 4a and 4c show clearly different RPA screening at 5 T and 13.5 T) and the compressible/incompressible structure around the dot depend on B and nu, so the assumption is not innocuous. Suggestions:
   (a) Fig. 1j already contains nu = 1 quasihole data at 13.4 T. Quote the nu = 1 (13.4 T) to nu = 1/3 (13.5 T) period ratio explicitly, since this comparison largely removes the field dependence.
   (b) Get e* without assuming C_d. The diamond slopes give alpha, and the temperature dependence of the resonance width in V_dot scales as kT/(e* alpha). The CLL lineshape complicates this, but Extended Data Fig. 3 already contains the temperature series.
   (c) In Extended Data Fig. 3, e* is fixed to e/3 and g comes out near 1/3. Report a fit with e* free, or with e* = 2e/3, to show how strongly the data constrain the charge.
   (d) The "contrast" with 2e/3 at nu = 2/3 in earlier work should be restricted to the regime measured (2/3 quasiparticles, regular spectrum). Your own Fig. 4 finds a 2e/3 period in other regimes, which arguably connects to those earlier reports rather than contradicting them.

4. Charging-energy extraction in Fig. 1g and 1i. The Fig. 1i caption gives E_C = 3e DeltaV_DC ~ 300 ueV. For charge e* transferred across a bias V, the energy is e* V = (e/3)V, so I would expect E_C = (e/3) DeltaV_DC. A factor of 3 relative to e DeltaV_DC is a factor of 9 relative to the expected expression. Please justify the factor, for example by explaining how V relates to the voltage across the dot in the diagonal four-terminal geometry, or correct it. Also compare the two diamond heights: for fixed C, the bias-height ratio between nu = 1 and nu = 1/3 should be 3, and the E_C ratio 9. The quoted 2 meV versus 300 ueV is about 6.7. Read correctly, the diamond heights give an independent consistency check on e*, and the paper does not currently use it.

5. Absolute calibration of N_qp = 0 at 1/3. This calibration underpins the "single anyon" claim, so it needs more support.
   (a) The B-tripling argument maps density, so it fixes the center of the nu = 1 N_qp = 0 plateau. The center of that plateau sits midway between quasihole and quasielectron addition. At nu = 1 that midpoint is shifted by the Zeeman/exchange asymmetry and by the particle-hole asymmetry the authors attribute to the trench work function. At 1/3 the quasihole and quasiparticle creation energies also differ. So the two plateau centers need not coincide even with zero quantum capacitance. The quoted error bar (less than one e/3 period) covers only quantum capacitance. Please estimate the error from plateau-center asymmetry as well, for example from the DMRG model, which already includes both.
   (b) The few-quasihole side could not be reached in weak backscattering: the QPC pinches off or the coupling becomes too weak to resolve. The large diamond at V_dot = 1.44 V therefore sits at the edge of the visible window. Please show that its low-V_dot boundary is a true charge-degeneracy point (a closing diamond with normal finite-bias edges) and not the point where resonances stop being visible. Otherwise several sectors with unresolved resonances could look like one wide incompressible diamond.
   (c) The main text says the work-function step is fixed from the N_qp = 0 position at nu = 1. Methods says Delta V = -2.02 V is fixed "once at nu = 1/3." Please reconcile these. If it is fixed at 1/3, the model's agreement with the 1/3 plateau position is input, not a test; only the width is a prediction.

6. The missing-charge-sector argument for binding. A vanishing sector implies E_add < 0 only if the sector is truly absent. It could instead be (i) narrower than the resonance linewidth, or (ii) present but with a transition too weakly coupled to the leads to show up.
   (a) Give a quantitative resolution bound: the minimum sector width that would be resolved, given T_e of about 110 mK (Extended Data Fig. 3k), the lifetime and CLL broadening, and the V_dot step. The stated bound E_B > E_C then becomes E_B > E_C - (resolution), and the size of that correction matters for the abstract's claim.
   (b) Fig. 4f is taken at V_DC = 55 uV, where each resonance is split into diamond edges. Please show full bias spectroscopy at several V_T across the merging, not only at the two endpoints (Fig. 4e and 4g). Then check that in the merged regime (i) the diamond height scales as expected for 2e*/C and (ii) no excited-state lines remain inside the merged diamond from a hidden odd transition. Invisible odd transitions caused by weak coupling would leave such lines.
   (c) The continuous merging of lines in Fig. 4f is the most convincing evidence. A plot of sector width against V_T, extrapolated through zero, would make the transition from even-odd to paired explicit and give a quantitative estimate of E_B - E_C.

7. What "anyon binding" means at |N_qp| of 10 to 20. A rough count: a hole of radius 45 nm at 13.5 T threads about 21 flux quanta, so a 1/3 droplet of that area holds about 7 electrons. Adding 10 to 20 e/3 charges then changes the dot's electron number by 50 to 100 percent. Unless the effective dot area is much larger than the hole, the interior at these occupations is far from a dilute gas of anyons in a 1/3 vacuum. This fits the authors' own discussion of 2/5 puddles, 2/3 droplets and wedding-cake structure, and it suggests a simple reading of the particle-hole split. 1/3 quasiholes and 2/3 quasiparticles push the interior toward an integer filling (0 or 1), with no Jain states in between. 1/3 quasiparticles and 2/3 quasiholes push it into the interval between 1/3 and 2/3, where the Jain states and Lambda-level structure live. Please give the electrostatic or DMRG estimate of the effective dot area and the local filling at these N_qp. Also consider tempering the abstract and title wording. What is observed is a non-convex addition spectrum (pair addition) of a small FQH droplet. Its relation to intrinsic anyon binding, which the paper says it does not establish at 1/3, is open, and the abstract should say so as plainly as the main text does.

8. Absolute N_qp at large occupation. Assigning |N_qp| ~ 10 to 20 requires counting resonances from N_qp = 0 over V_dot ranges where, by the authors' own account (Extended Data Fig. 10 caption), the dip-finding algorithm can miss resonances. Please give the uncertainty in N_qp for the Fig. 4 datasets.

9. Robustness of the DMRG comparison in Fig. 3f. The agreement covers about five spacings, which form essentially one alternating pattern. The pattern depends on near-degeneracies (Lambda_1(2)/Lambda_2(0) and Lambda_2(1)/Lambda_1(3)) that are inherently sensitive to the model. Meanwhile the model (i) assumes axial symmetry, so that L_z is conserved, although the dot sits in a QPC saddle with N/S gates at -3 V; (ii) neglects fringe fields from the QPC and edge gates, although Extended Data Fig. 2 gives an edge-gate lever arm of about 1/15 that of V_dot, and at V_edge = -4 V this is not small; (iii) uses small systems (N_e = 8 to 18 with up to 6 quasiparticles) extrapolated linearly in 1/N_e; and (iv) depends on a 2 V work-function step and a chosen delta mu. Please show how the predicted spacing sequence changes with dot radius, Delta V, delta mu and a modest quadrupolar distortion, and show the raw finite-size data behind the extrapolation. If the even-odd pattern survives these variations, the comparison is strong. If it does not, it should be presented as illustrative. Separately, the text calls the even-odd pattern at N_qp <= 5 "accidental" and orbital, while in Fig. 4 a similar pattern at large N_qp is taken as a precursor of binding. Please state what distinguishes the two cases experimentally. The authors' own Methods note that even-odd widths alone do not imply binding.

10. Spin blockade at nu = 1. The spin-blockade picture rests on the empty sublevel above nu = +1 in the graphene zero Landau level having the opposite spin to the nu = 1 edge. Given the spin/valley ordering of the zero Landau level, this should be justified with references. The blockade is lifted for |V_DC| >~ 1 mV, while E_Z = 0.58 meV at 5 T. Please explain this factor of about 2, and if possible show the threshold against B (or in-plane field). Scaling with g mu_B B would confirm a magnon (spin) mechanism over, for example, a valley excitation.

11. Charge-noise comparison. The spectrum covers 1e-4 to 1e-2 Hz from a 15.5 h trace, so the lowest-frequency point has only a few periods of statistics. The exponent alpha differs noticeably between transitions: from Table III, about 1.4 for -3 -> -2 and about 0.8 for -1 -> 0. Semiconductor benchmarks are usually quoted at 1 Hz. The claim "among the best reported in a solid state system" therefore relies on an unstated extrapolation of two to four decades. Please either measure near 1 Hz or state the extrapolation and its exponent, and quote S_E (ueV/sqrt(Hz)) at a common frequency next to the cited values. The sentence on aluminum SETs ("similar noise at frequencies two orders of magnitude higher") is easy to misread; please give both numbers at the same frequency. Note too that the noise was measured only on the integer dot. The text should not imply that the fractional dot has the same stability, which matters for the topological-qubit discussion.

12. Novelty and positioning. The introduction says earlier experiments probed many-anyon ensembles. Refs. [15, 16] (STM imaging and spectroscopy of single quasiparticles bound to traps), [25] (trapping e/4 quasiparticles in bilayer graphene) and [56] (controlled localization of anyons in a graphene interferometer) are close in spirit. Please state explicitly what this work adds beyond each, for example gate-controlled occupation down to N_qp = 1 with direct fractional-charge tunneling spectroscopy, rather than grouping them with the many-anyon experiments.

MINOR COMMENTS

- Fig. 3f plots delta in mV (0 to 300), while the text cites a "100-200 ueV scale of the charge stability sectors." Please fix the units.
- Methods (Stack preparation) puts the "aligned top gates" 32 nm above the graphene and the "top graphite gate" 35 nm above those. Extended Data Fig. 1 shows the patterned graphite at 32 nm and the metal gates on the 35 nm hBN. The device model also places the metal gates "32 nm above the top graphite layer," which contradicts the 35 nm hBN. Please make these consistent.
- Alignment-mark etch depth: about 5 nm in Methods, about 10 nm in the Extended Data Fig. 1i caption.
- Lock-in frequencies: 13.333 and 17.777 Hz in Methods, 13.77 and 17.77 Hz in Table I.
- Notation: V_B (Fig. 1a) versus V_BG (Table I, Extended Data Figs. 9 and 10).
- Extended Data Fig. 2a caption: the bracketed "[V_T = 0.04 V]" is unexplained.
- Extended Data Fig. 9d: "n = V_T + alpha V_BG" is called the electron density, but it is a gate-voltage combination proportional to density. Please define it, and clarify which quantity is held fixed.
- "Fig. Extended Data Fig. 8" (Methods); "T he addition energy" (Methods); "Free Enegy" (Fig. 4g).
- Affiliations 2 and 7 are both UC Berkeley Physics and could be merged.
- Fig. 2i: please mark how the excited-state energies are read off (the dashed lines), and say whether any could come from lead or other localized states rather than dot levels.
- Fig. 3b: the bracket's meaning (ν = 1 plateau span versus the expected 1/3 N_qp = 0 center with error bar) is hard to follow; one sentence of explanation would help.
- Table II: say whether the 2/5 quasiparticle pair binding (88 ueV) depends on the gate distances and RPA screening used. It is the one positive binding result, and the discussion leans on it.

ASSESSMENT

This is an important experiment on an exceptional device, and the integer-regime results and the qualitative fractional phenomenology are convincing. Before the claims of single-anyon occupation and of a lower bound on an anyon binding energy are fully supported, the authors need to: (1) clarify the bulk and dot fillings of each dataset, especially the 2/5 environment of the "1/3 quasihole" data; (2) back up the e/3 and N_qp = 0 assignments with checks that do not depend on a fixed lever arm and capacitance; (3) show that the missing charge sectors are truly absent, not unresolved or uncoupled; and (4) temper the "anyon binding" language to match the main text's own caveats about the high-occupation dot. I recommend major revision. I expect most of these points can be addressed with existing data and additional analysis.
