Review of "Chiral Majorana edge state in a quantum anomalous Hall insulator-superconductor structure" (He, Pan, et al., arXiv:1606.05712)

SUMMARY OF THE CLAIM

The authors deposit a Nb/Au bar across the middle of a 6 QL (Cr0.12Bi0.26Sb0.62)2Te3 quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) film and measure a four-terminal "longitudinal" conductance sigma_12 across the bar while sweeping a perpendicular field. Near the magnetization reversals they see shoulders at about 0.5 e^2/h (0.48 to 0.52 e^2/h across three devices), flanked by about 1 e^2/h at saturation and about 0.05 e^2/h dips in the zero Hall plateau regime. They interpret the 0.5 e^2/h feature as the transport signature of an N = +/-1 chiral topological superconductor phase, i.e. a single chiral Majorana edge mode, following the proposals of Chung et al. (2011) and Wang et al. (2015). The features narrow with temperature and vanish near 400 mK. The paper presents this as the first observation of a one-dimensional chiral Majorana mode.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT

The experiment is a natural and important test of a clean theoretical proposal, and the data are reproducible across three devices and many sweeps. However, the central inference does not follow from the evidence shown. A conductance of 0.5 e^2/h in this geometry is not unique to a chiral Majorana mode: it is the generic result for two C = 1 QAH segments in series connected through any region that equilibrates the edge channels, superconducting or not. The paper contains no control that distinguishes the Majorana scenario from this trivial one, does not establish that the film under the Nb is proximitized at all, and does not test any property that is specific to superconductivity. In its present form I cannot recommend publication of the Majorana claim. The measurements could support a more modest paper about transport in QAH/Nb hybrids, but the Majorana interpretation needs the controls listed below.

MAJOR ISSUES

1. The half plateau has a non-Majorana explanation that the paper does not address.
   If the region beneath the Nb (or the Nb itself, through a transparent Au/Nb contact) acts as a metallic reservoir that equilibrates the incoming chiral edge, the device is two QAH segments in series, each contributing h/e^2, giving sigma_12 = 0.5 e^2/h. This happens with a normal metal and requires no pairing. It is especially plausible near the coercive field, where the film is multidomain and the region under the bar can reverse at a slightly different field than the uncovered film (the Nb/Au overlayer can change strain, carrier density and local coercivity). In that window, a disordered, metallic, equilibrating middle region between two C = 1 segments yields exactly the observed shoulder, plus its narrowing with temperature as bulk conduction grows. The authors must rule this out explicitly. The current Barkhausen discussion addresses a different, weaker alternative and does not cover it.

2. No control isolates superconductivity.
   The only control is a bare QAH Hall bar, which tests nothing about the bar. The needed controls are:
   - an identical device with a normal metal bar (e.g. Au or Ti/Au of the same footprint), measured under the same sweeps;
   - suppression of superconductivity in the TSC device while the QAH state survives. Hc2 of Nb is too large to reach easily, but in-plane field, a thinner or lower-Tc superconductor (Al), or a device with a thin insulating spacer would each help;
   - dependence on bar width along the current path and on interface transparency.
   If the 0.5 e^2/h feature survives with a normal metal, the Majorana interpretation fails.

3. There is no evidence that the TI surface under the Nb is proximitized or gapped.
   Fig. 2a shows Hc2 of the Nb film, which says the Nb is superconducting, not that a pairing gap is induced in the magnetic TI surface with Delta_1 != Delta_2, which the N = 1 phase requires. The text also says (Fig. 2c caption) that superconductivity is induced "on the top surface", while the main text relies on asymmetric pairing on both surfaces. Needed: Andreev or tunneling spectroscopy showing an induced gap in the covered region, and dc bias dependence of sigma_12. A topological superconductor plateau should be robust only for bias below the induced gap and should disappear above it; an equilibration plateau should not care. This test is straightforward with the existing devices and is the single most informative missing measurement.

4. The temperature scale points to the QAH state, not superconductivity.
   The plateaus vanish near 400 mK, where the QAH quantization itself degrades, while Nb remains superconducting to several kelvin. This is consistent with the trivial mechanism and gives no positive support for the Majorana one. The attribution to "thermally activated bulk carriers" is asserted, not shown (no Arrhenius analysis, no comparison with the control bar's sigma_xx at the same temperatures).

5. The field windows are not connected quantitatively to the theory.
   In the proposal, the N = 1 window lies between the QAH and TSC transitions and its width is set by the induced gaps. The paper gives no estimate of Delta_1, Delta_2, lambda or m0, so there is no prediction of where or how wide the plateau should be. The plateaus sit at +/-80 mT in Fig. 2b, while the control bar has HC = +/-150 mT; the reason for this offset, and how it relates to the TSC versus QAH transitions, is not discussed. The asymmetry between low-field plateaus and high-field "kinks" is explained by an unquantified "interfacial exchange coupling" that is speculative. Only device I shows the high-field feature at 350 mK (Fig. 2b caption), which weakens the claim that the plateau appears at every reversal.

6. Device geometry and measurement configuration are unclear, and they matter.
   - Dimensions are millimeter scale (2 mm x 1 mm QAH bar, 8 mm x 0.6 mm Nb bar). A 0.6 mm wide proximitized region is many orders of magnitude longer than any superconducting coherence length and any plausible edge coherence length; the paper should explain why a coherent N = 1 phase over this length is expected, or why it does not matter.
   - It is not stated whether the Nb is floating or grounded. This changes the Landauer-Buttiker analysis completely and determines what sigma_12 = 0.5 e^2/h means.
   - The positions of the voltage probes 1 and 2 relative to the edges and the current path need to be shown, with the full Landauer-Buttiker expressions for sigma_12 in each scenario (N = 2, N = 1, NI, and the equilibrating normal-metal case). The statement that sigma_12 = e^2/h at saturation requires a specific probe configuration that should be made explicit.
   - Excitation current, frequency, and checks for heating are missing.

7. The plateaus are narrow and their flatness is not quantified.
   Several features are described as kinks and are identified by the derivative (Fig. 2c). The stated uncertainties (+/-0.05 to +/-0.07 e^2/h) are comparable to the deviation one would need to exclude other values. The paper should give the field width of each plateau, the number of sweeps, sweep rate and direction, the field step, and the spread across sweeps, and show raw traces from many sweeps rather than representative ones.

8. Alternatives should be treated in full.
   Beyond Barkhausen jumps, the discussion should cover: series equilibration (issue 1); percolation through domain networks during reversal, which in QAH films produces intermediate conductance values; different coercivities of covered and uncovered regions; and parallel conduction through the Nb/Au through pinholes or edges. The statement that "only when backscattering is owing to the TSC N = +/-1 phase does it involve such a half-quantized conductance" is too strong given issue 1.

9. Predictions specific to Majorana transport are not tested.
   Chung et al. predict characteristic shot noise in the N = 1 phase. Nonlocal measurements, dependence on the length of the TSC region, and the bias test of issue 3 would each discriminate. At least one such test is needed for a claim of this weight.

MINOR ISSUES

- "This has never been probed directly or indirectly in any experiments" and "for the first time" overstate; temper.
- "0.5 h/e2" should be 0.5 e^2/h. "QSHI regions" should be QAHI. "QHAI" and "Majoranana" are typos.
- The Hamiltonians are garbled in this version; check the typesetting of H_0(k), H_BdG and Delta_k.
- The sentence on structural asymmetry says "When structural asymmetry is preserved ... Delta_1 = Delta_2"; this should read inversion symmetry.
- Fig. 2a: "zero-resistance temperature" defines Hc2 using a strict criterion; state it, and note that the linear fit says nothing about the induced gap.
- Fig. 3 caption: "10-mK steps" and the offset description are hard to follow; give offsets in e^2/h.
- The abstract's "promising evidence" and the conclusion's "concrete evidence" are inconsistent; the abstract wording is closer to what the data support.
- Report the Hall bar and TSC device data from the same cooldown and the same field sweep rate.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY ASSESSMENT

A normal metal bar control that shows no 0.5 e^2/h feature, plus a bias dependence that collapses the plateau above an induced gap measured independently, would make the Majorana interpretation credible. Without those, the data are consistent with a trivial series-equilibration mechanism.

CONTEXT OUTSIDE THE MANUSCRIPT

This preprint was published in Science in 2017. Theoretical follow-ups (Ji and Wen, PRL 2018; Huang, Setiawan and Sau, PRB 2018) showed that a 0.5 e^2/h plateau arises without chiral Majorana modes when the superconductor provides good electrical contact. Experiments by Kayyalha et al. (Science 2020) on many QAH/Nb devices found the half plateau tied to the Nb shorting the QAH film rather than to Majorana physics. Science retracted the paper in 2022. These later developments are consistent with major issues 1 to 3 above, which can be raised from the manuscript alone.
