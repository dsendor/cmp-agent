Referee report

"Symmetry Origins of the Field-Free Superconducting Diode Effect in the Kagome Superconductor CsV3Sb5" (Liu, Sun, Song, Peng et al.)


1. Summary of the submission

The authors measure dV/dI versus DC bias in exfoliated CsV3Sb5 flakes and report the following sequence:
(i) ten hBN-encapsulated "pristine" devices show no zero-field superconducting diode effect (SDE), with |eta| below about 0.5% (Table S1);
(ii) after the hBN is removed and the flake is exposed to air (oxidation), or after the flake is etched into an irregular or zig-zag shape, a zero-field asymmetry between |Ic+| and |Ic-| appears, reaching eta = 83.6% in one sweep of Device 11;
(iii) in these degraded or etched devices, the sign and size of eta change from sweep to sweep at fixed conditions;
(iv) out-of-plane fields of 0.1 to 0.2 T (and an in-plane field of 0.3 T, Fig. S10) make the sign and then the size reproducible;
(v) Ti-doped CsV3-xTixSb5 (x = 0.24), where long-range CDW is suppressed, shows no zero-field SDE after the same oxidation or etching (two devices, Figs. S13 and S14).

From this they conclude that the zero-field SDE needs extrinsic inversion breaking, that the superconducting state hosts metastable TRS-breaking domains, and that this TRS breaking is inherited from the CDW.

The question matters. Whether the TRS breaking reported in the superconducting state of AV3Sb5 belongs to the condensate or to the CDW is open. A careful diode study that pairs an encapsulated baseline with a CDW-free control would be a real contribution, and the encapsulated baseline across ten devices is useful in itself, given the earlier zero-field SDE report in this material (Le et al., ref. 17). As submitted, though, the central interpretive claims (TRS-breaking domains, field selection of domains, CDW origin) do not follow from the data. The main problem is that the stochastic, field-stabilized behavior has a simpler and well-known explanation that the manuscript does not rule out: sweep-to-sweep scatter of the switching current in an inhomogeneous, weak-link-like device, together with extrinsic TRS breaking from trapped or residual flux and a conventional field-induced diode. Below I set out the specific problems and the measurements that would settle them.


2. Major issues

2.1 A random-sign eta with roughly zero mean is what you see when there is no diode at all.

If |Ic+| and |Ic-| are drawn independently from the same broad switching distribution, a single pair of sweeps gives an eta of random sign whose typical size is about sigma/<Ic>. Oxidation is what produces the multi-peak dV/dI and the lower, smeared Ic (Fig. 2b, Fig. S3), which is exactly when the switching distribution should widen. The numbers fit this null hypothesis. In the SI device with eta = +17.07%, -30.86% and -14.07%, the other three of six sweeps show no asymmetry, and the extracted Ic values (16.8 to 36 uA) vary by about a factor of two from sweep to sweep. That spread is enough by itself to produce |eta| of 30%. In Device 11, the 83.6% headline value comes from one of three sweeps in the second oxidation stage; the other two show no SDE, and the third oxidation stage shows none in three sweeps (Supplementary Text). Neither the abstract nor the main text says this. The 83.6% should not be presented as the diode efficiency of the device.

The authors call the signal a "mesoscopic statistical quantity". If so, it has to be analyzed statistically, and six sweeps per temperature (Fig. 3) or nine per field (Fig. 4) is far too few. What is needed:
- Switching-current histograms for each polarity separately, from at least a few hundred sweeps, at B = 0 and at each training field. Report the mean and width of Ic+ and Ic- with uncertainties.
- A test of whether <|Ic+|> and <|Ic-|> differ at B = 0, and whether Ic+ and Ic- within one forward/backward pair are correlated. A TRS-broken domain state that is fixed for the duration of a pair predicts anticorrelated deviations; independent switching noise predicts none.
- The same statistics for the pristine encapsulated devices. Table S1 gives a single eta per device. For "no SDE in pristine devices" to be a meaningful baseline, it needs the sweep-to-sweep width of Ic as well.

Until this is done, the data do not show that the zero-field state of the degraded devices has any nonreciprocity beyond switching noise.

2.2 Extrinsic TRS breaking from trapped or residual flux is not excluded.

The authors' own argument holds that without TRS breaking, geometry alone cannot give |Ic+| not equal to |Ic-|. So whatever produces the zero-field signal has to break TRS. Trapped flux does that in an obvious way, and it predicts the reported phenomenology:
- Every sweep drives the device normal (to +/-300 uA) and back. As the current falls through retrapping, the device re-enters the superconducting state in the presence of the residual magnet field and the self-field of the transport current. In a multiply connected, inhomogeneous network, the trapped vortex or fluxoid configuration can differ each time, so eta changes sign and magnitude from sweep to sweep. Diodes with memory set by trapped Abrikosov vortices are established in the literature.
- After a field excursion, the superconducting magnets in a PPMS and in a Triton keep remanent fields of order mT to tens of mT unless they are reset or quenched. The field-cooling protocol (1 T, then field removed; Figs. S8 and S9) makes this a particular concern for every "zero-field" sweep taken afterward.
- An applied out-of-plane field of 0.1 to 0.2 T is a substantial fraction of the out-of-plane Hc2 quoted for Device 14 (about 0.85 T, Fig. S10). In a thin flake with demagnetization, the effective out-of-plane Hc1 is tiny, so vortices are certainly present. A deterministic, field-odd diode in an asymmetric (etched, zig-zag) strip is the standard edge-barrier or vortex-ratchet SDE (for example ref. 8 and the work it builds on). Once that deterministic contribution exceeds the switching noise, "the polarity is fixed, then the amplitude is fixed" follows. It does not need preexisting domains.

The Hc1 estimate in Fig. S10 addresses only the in-plane 0.3 T case. It says nothing about the out-of-plane training that the abstract and Fig. 4 rely on.

Needed:
- State how the zero-field condition was established: magnet reset or quench, and the residual field measured with a Hall probe or from the shift of a field-odd quantity.
- Show eta(B) over a fine grid through zero, for both field polarities, with full statistics. Is the stabilized eta odd in B? Is there a finite zero-field intercept of the mean once the field-odd part is fitted? The present Fig. 4 shows that the field fixes "the" polarity but not that +B and -B fix opposite polarities.
- Run the same field sweep on the Ti-doped devices (Devices 15 and 16). If they show the same field-induced SDE at 0.1 to 0.2 T, the field-training data say nothing about CDW-specific domains.

2.3 Domains inherited from the CDW sit uneasily with what is observed.

The proposed picture has each current sweep at 1.8 K or below "repopulating" TRS-breaking domains derived from the CDW. The CDW orders near 94 K, and the measurements stay deep inside the CDW phase. Joule heating during the normal excursion of these few-ohm devices at a few hundred uA is far too small to take the lattice near T_CDW. It is therefore unclear why CDW-derived domains would reconfigure on every sweep at 0.25 to 1.8 K. At the same time, a 1 T field applied during cooling (Figs. S8 and S9) does not leave a stable diode after removal, even though fields of 0.1 to 0.2 T are said to select the domains. A metastable domain state that a 0.1 T field selects but a 1 T field-cool does not set is hard to reconcile with either a CDW-locked texture or the normal-state trainability reported in ref. 39.

The manuscript should state the starting temperature of the field-cooling (above Tc only, or above T_CDW) and confront ref. 39 directly. If the relevant degree of freedom resets on the scale of a switching event, it is more naturally a property of the superconducting state, or of trapped flux, than of the CDW. That runs against the paper's main conclusion.

A discriminating experiment that the authors are well placed to run: thermal cycling at zero field to a temperature above Tc but below T_CDW (for example 10 to 20 K), compared with cycling above T_CDW (for example 120 K), with the eta distribution measured after each. Trapped flux and superconducting-state domains reset on any cycle above Tc. A CDW-locked texture should reset only on cycles above T_CDW. The same comparison with field-cooling from each temperature would test trainability in each regime.

2.4 The symmetry argument for oxidized devices appears to forbid the proposed mechanism.

The authors use eta proportional to (P x B_eff) . I. For surface oxidation of a flake, the natural polar axis P is along the surface normal (c axis). The orbital or loop-current moments proposed for the CDW of AV3Sb5 point along c, and out-of-plane training would select exactly that component. With P along z and B_eff along z, P x B_eff = 0, so this configuration gives no diode for in-plane current. For the etched devices, an in-plane polar axis together with B_eff along z can work, provided P is perpendicular to I. For the oxidized devices, the mechanism needs either an in-plane B_eff perpendicular to I or a laterally non-uniform oxidation that produces in-plane polar components. Neither is shown.

The paper also does not report the orientation of the in-plane field in Fig. S10 relative to the current, or the orientation of the etched asymmetry relative to I. These are needed to check the selection rule. A 0.3 T in-plane field perpendicular to I, with a c-axis polar axis, gives the textbook magnetochiral SDE (refs. 1 and 3). A deterministic diode at that field is expected and is not evidence for domains. The theoretical contribution listed in the author contributions should be made explicit: which point group results after oxidation and after etching, which components of B_eff are allowed, and what Eq. (2) predicts for each device geometry. Eq. (2) as written is a parametrization with free weights w_j and signs. It accommodates any outcome and so constrains nothing.

2.5 The Ti-doped control does not isolate the CDW.

Ti substitution at x = 0.24 changes much more than the presence of long-range CDW: disorder and scattering, carrier density, Tc, Ic, coherence length, penetration depth, pinning, and the superconducting state itself (doped CsV3Sb5 has its own phase diagram). The control rests on two devices. The manuscript gives no comparison of their Tc, Ic, thickness, critical fields, or the degree of inhomogeneity induced by oxidation. Without that, the absence of an SDE in Ti-doped devices could reflect any of the following:
- a narrower switching distribution;
- different flux pinning, so that trapped-flux configurations differ (relevant to 2.2);
- oxidation or etching that did not produce the same degree of inversion breaking or inhomogeneity.

To make the control meaningful:
- Show that the oxidized Ti-doped devices develop the same multi-peak dV/dI and a comparable Ic reduction as the undoped ones (Figs. S13 and S14 should state these numbers), so the "same protocol" produced the same extrinsic inversion breaking.
- Give the full statistical comparison (2.1) for doped and undoped devices at comparable Ic/Ic(pristine) and comparable T/Tc.
- Show the field-induced SDE in doped devices (2.2).
- Ideally, add an intermediate doping level at which the CDW survives (the caption of Fig. S13 implies a threshold near x = 0.12). The SDE should then track the CDW across that threshold rather than simply disappear at large disorder.
- The evidence that the CDW is suppressed is the absence of a kink in R(T) plus ref. 55. That reference's title ("Competitive charge density waves") suggests that doped samples may host other or short-range charge order. Clarify what is and is not suppressed at x = 0.24.

Even with all of this, a CDW-free control cannot distinguish "TRS breaking inherited from the CDW" from "a TRS-broken superconducting order parameter that exists only in the superconducting phase that coexists with the CDW". The abstract's final sentence, and the Discussion's statement that the TRS breaking "is rooted in the CDW-related normal-state order", should be softened accordingly.

2.6 The Fig. S12 fluctuation control does not address the relevant alternative.

Fixed-bias time traces show no telegraph switching over 20 minutes. That excludes slow two-level fluctuations at fixed bias. It does not exclude stochastic switching during a ramp (thermal or quantum activation, phase slips), which is what switching-current statistics measure. Nor does it exclude a state that is reset by each excursion to the normal state, which is the authors' own picture and also the trapped-flux picture. The claim that this control "rules out" ordinary fluctuations should be limited to what it tests.

2.7 Measurement details that bear directly on Ic.

Fig. S3 states an AC excitation of 10 uA, against critical currents of about 17 to 36 uA in the degraded devices. An AC modulation of that size (and is it rms or peak?) smears the onset over a large fraction of Ic and can shift the extracted onset asymmetrically if the transition shape differs between branches. The step size also changes between sweeps (0.2 uA versus 0.5 uA in Fig. S3d,e), and the sweep rate is given only for Device 14. Please report the following for every dataset:
- AC amplitude and frequency, DC step, dwell time and sweep rate;
- the voltage or dV/dI threshold that defines "onset" (this matters with multi-peak structures);
- whether forward and backward sweeps alternate in order, and the wait time between them;
- temperature stability.
Repeat key zero-field statistics with a pure DC voltage criterion and a much smaller AC excitation.

2.8 Characterization of the inversion-breaking treatments.

"Surface oxidation" is inferred from air exposure and AFM roughness (Fig. S5). There is no chemical evidence of an oxide (Raman, XPS, EDS on the oxidized flake) and no estimate of oxide thickness relative to the flake thickness. The etching method (ion milling, RIE, gas, mask) is not described. Etching also damages edges and can create weak links, which matters for 2.1 and 2.2. The thicknesses of Devices 11 to 16 are not given.

2.9 Relation to prior work.

The Discussion says that "any claim of an intrinsic superconducting diode effect should be approached with caution". This is directed, implicitly, at ref. 17 (Le et al.), and the closest prior work is ref. 39 (Ge et al., field-trainable nonreciprocal Ic in CsV3Sb5), which is cited only within a list of CDW papers. Both need a direct, specific comparison: device geometry, encapsulation, thickness, field history, and how the present null result and stochastic result relate to theirs.

Ref. 34 (Saykin et al., high-resolution polar Kerr) is cited as implicating normal-state TRS breaking. My reading of that work is that it reports no resolvable Kerr signal below the charge-order transition, that is, an upper bound. Please check this citation. The TRS-breaking evidence in this material is contested, and the Introduction should say so rather than treating it as settled.


3. Minor issues and presentation

- Device numbering is inconsistent. The main text says Device 11 is the oxidized device in Fig. 2, but Fig. S3 is captioned "Device 12 (Fig. 2 in the main text)". The main text attributes eta between -30.86% and 17.07% to Device 12 (Figs. S3 to S5), while the SI attributes these values to Device 13 (Fig. S4). The main text calls Device 13 etched, but Fig. S4 is titled "after successive oxidation stages". Fig. S5 is AFM, not diode data. Please reconcile all labels; as written the reader cannot tell which treatment produced which data.
- Fig. S1 and S2 captions refer to "Table 1"; it is Table S1. Fig. 1F versus 1f; Fig. 2C versus 2c.
- Table S1: say whether eta is from one sweep or an average, and give its uncertainty. Zero entries should be upper bounds set by the resolution.
- Fig. 3d,e: "the average magnitude decreases". Specify whether this is <|eta|> or <eta>, with error bars. Give Tc of the etched device, so that the 0.25 to 1.5 K range can be read as T/Tc.
- Fig. S10: the Hc1 formula and the ratio d/xi of about 2 put the film in a regime where the thin-film expression is marginal, and the caption's "(rather than Hc2)" is confusing. More importantly, the argument does not apply to the out-of-plane case (2.2).
- Wording: "should generally preclude" (abstract, duplicated word); "Oxidation or etching can break the inversion symmetry breaking"; "Only by extrinsic inversion symmetry breaking ... then a field-free diode response could be observed"; "would stabilizes" (Fig. S11 title); "points to TRS breaking nature ... probably inherited" (end of Introduction). The Discussion sentence "The emergence of a finite response after oxidation ... shows that the time-reversal symmetry is still broken" overstates what a zero-mean, random-sign signal shows.
- The pristine devices sit on a substrate with hBN on top and electrodes on one side, so inversion is already weakly broken at the interfaces. "Centrosymmetric baseline" is an approximation that depends on thickness (21 to 76 nm). A short comment on why thicker flakes are expected to show negligible SDE would help.
- Data availability "upon reasonable request" is weak for a claim that rests on sweep statistics. Please deposit the raw sweep data.


4. Assessment

The encapsulated-device null result (|eta| below about 0.5% in ten devices) is the most solid finding, and it is worth reporting given ref. 17. The observation that degraded devices give irreproducible zero-field asymmetry is a useful caution for the field. But the headline claims, namely transport evidence for TRS-breaking domains in the superconducting state and their CDW origin, are not supported at present. The stochastic signal has not been separated from switching-current noise. Trapped or residual flux and the conventional field-induced diode have not been excluded. The Ti-doped control changes too many things at once. And the proposed mechanism appears to conflict with the authors' own selection rule for the oxidized geometry.

I recommend major revision. The key additions are:
(a) switching-current statistics for each polarity, with hundreds of sweeps;
(b) a controlled zero field, and eta(B) through zero with field-odd analysis, for both doped and undoped devices;
(c) thermal cycling to temperatures above Tc but below T_CDW, compared with cycling above T_CDW;
(d) a matched characterization of the Ti-doped control, ideally with an intermediate doping level;
(e) an explicit symmetry analysis for each device geometry.

If (a) to (c) support the domain picture, the paper would be a significant result. If they do not, a reframed paper on the encapsulated null result and on extrinsic, irreproducible diode signals in degraded kagome flakes would still be worth publishing.
