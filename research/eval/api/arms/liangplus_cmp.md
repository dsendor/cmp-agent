Your task is to review the attached submission (the PDF above) for a top-tier physics journal.

Work through these steps first. They are for you; the review follows them.

1. Claims. List the paper's main claims and the evidence it offers for each.
2. Required evidence. For each claim, describe the experiments, controls, analyses and calculations you would want to see before accepting it, as if you had run the study yourself: the setup, the comparison, and what would rule out the leading other explanations.
3. Gaps. Compare that list with what the paper actually shows. Each gap that matters is a candidate concern. Do not ask for anything the paper already contains.
4. Hidden assumptions. For each claim, name the assumptions it rests on (about the sample, the measurement and the model) and check whether the paper justifies them.
5. Figures against text. Check each figure and table against what the text says about it: values, trends, error bars, fits, panel labels, and numbers quoted elsewhere in the paper.

6. Condensed matter checklist. Go through the items that apply to this paper's techniques and claims. Raise an item only when you can point to the specific gap in this paper.
   - Samples and devices: how many were measured, whether the key result appears in more than one, whether device-to-device spread is shown, and whether results survive thermal cycling.
   - Null and reference controls: a sample, device or geometry where the effect should be absent (another orientation, a reference resonator, a bare device without the extra layer, a nonmagnetic tip, a material with the opposite sign of the coupling), measured under matched conditions.
   - Apparatus artifacts: contacts, leads, filters, bias lines, illumination of contacts, laser or probe heating, tip changes or tip convolution, trapped flux, switching-current fluctuations, surface degradation, frequency-dependent extrinsic responses.
   - Heating and temperature: Joule or optical heating, electron temperature against lattice temperature, phonon contributions, and whether the effective sample temperature is known.
   - Calibration and conversions: gate voltage to carrier density and filling, twist or alignment angle and its uncertainty, field alignment, beam power, fluence and spot size, sample thickness, stoichiometry.
   - Dependences: temperature, field, power, frequency or angle dependence where the claim predicts one; a result shown at a single temperature or field; comparisons made at mismatched temperatures.
   - Fits and extraction: which parameters are free and which fixed, extrapolations (for example to zero temperature), resolution limits, how error bars were obtained, data-to-parameter ratios, normalizations across conditions.
   - Inhomogeneity: disorder, strain, domains, twist-angle variation, interface or surface states, terminations and reconstructions, competing phases.
   - Direct evidence for the headline property: zero resistance and Meissner response for superconductivity, spin resolution for spin splitting, protection or symmetry tests for topological claims, and whether a signature is unique to the proposed state.
   - Theory and calculations: whether model assumptions match the real structure and symmetry, how parameters were chosen (fitted or independent), functional, interaction and spin-orbit settings, structural relaxation and convergence, and whether the comparison with data is quantitative.
   - Consistency: between figures, between the text and the figures, with the authors' own earlier data, and with accepted literature values.

Then write the review. Start with "Review outline:" and give:

1. Significance and novelty, in two to four sentences.
2. Potential reasons for acceptance.
3. Concerns, in priority order, under three headings:
   A. Must address: the main conclusion does not follow without this.
   B. Should address: a claim is weaker, narrower or less certain without this.
   C. Minor.
   Number the concerns 1, 2, 3 and so on across all three headings, most important first. Give each concern what is missing or wrong, where in the paper, why it matters for the claim, and what the authors should do. Be specific: name the measurement, control, parameter or figure. A concern that would fit any paper is not useful. Raise every concern that matters; there is no set number.
4. Suggestions for improvement, up to four.

Be thoughtful and constructive.
