**Review outline:**

### 1. Significance and novelty

This manuscript reports point-contact spectroscopy (PCS) on the (001) surface of UTe₂ under hydrostatic pressure up to 1.4 GPa, crossing the SC1, SC2 and SC3 regions of the phase diagram. Pressure is a regime where symmetry-sensitive probes of UTe₂ are scarce, so spectroscopic data there would be valuable. The central claims are:
- a zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP) that develops side dips above about 0.3 GPa;
- an extended-BTK analysis that favours B₂u/B₃u;
- a single parameter, ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩, that distinguishes SC1–SC3 and has a minimum at the SC2–SC3 boundary.

As presented, the symmetry and phase assignments rest on fit parameters whose physical meaning and uniqueness have not been established. The extrinsic origins of the key spectral features (the side dips) have not been excluded under pressure.

### 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Technically demanding measurement: PCS in a piston-clamp cell down to 0.3 K, on high-RRR molten-salt-flux crystals. The resistive phase diagram (Fig. 2b) broadly matches the literature.
- At ambient pressure the ZBCP is seen in three samples (Fig. 2d–f) and evolves smoothly with temperature and field (Fig. 2c, S2a).
- The qualitative change in spectral shape with pressure (Fig. 3, S3) is clear and potentially important.
- The extended BTK formalism for unitary triplet states is laid out in the SI. The simulations for each irreducible representation (Fig. S5, S6) are a useful reference.
- The pressure-dependent evolution is compared, at least qualitatively, with the SC2–SC3 boundary from independent thermodynamic data.

### 3. Concerns

#### A. Must address

**1. The fitted "gaps" are far too large to be superconducting gaps, which undermines the ratio analysis built on them.**
- At ambient pressure the fits give Δx(y) ≈ 1.15–1.18 meV for T_c⁰ = 2.1 K (Table S1, Fig. S7, S9a). That is 2Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 13, roughly 3.7 times the BCS line the authors draw at about 0.32 meV in Fig. S2c.
- At 1.2 GPa, Δ¹z reaches about 1.6 meV (Fig. S9c) for T_c ≈ 3 K.
- These values are also well above the gap scales reported by tunnelling and STM on UTe₂.
- The text presents the deviation from BCS as evidence of unconventional superconductivity (p. 8, Fig. S2 caption). A gap ratio of this size is not a signature of unconventional pairing. It is more naturally a sign that the fitted Δ absorbs other energy scales, such as:
  - critical-current or Maxwell-term structure;
  - spreading resistance shifting the voltage scale;
  - the broad Dynes term (Γ ≈ 0.4 meV at ambient).

If the absolute gaps are not physical, the meaning of Δz/Δx(y) is unclear. The authors should:
- compare fitted Δ(T→0) with T_c and with literature gaps;
- show that the voltage scale of the features does not depend on the normal-state resistance R_N of the junction (see concern 3);
- correct for any series resistance and explain why the fitted Δ is a gap.

Table S1 also lists Δz = 1.18 meV and Δx(y) = 0.72 meV for S1, while Fig. S7a and S9a give the opposite assignment. The quoted ratio 0.57(5) is only consistent with Fig. S7, so the Table S1 columns appear to be swapped.

**2. The px- versus pz-weight inference rests on peak heights, which non-ideal contacts strongly affect, and the fit parameters are degenerate.**
- The key ambient-pressure argument (p. 8) is that G_NS(0) < 1.2 "suggests that the px(y) pairing component plays a more dominant role."
- In soft Au-epoxy contacts, reduced peak amplitudes commonly arise from several effects: parallel multi-contacts, non-ballistic transport, spreading resistance, a partially non-superconducting surface layer, or a non-flat normal-state background.
- The fits treat Z, Γ, Δx(y) and Δz as free, and with a spherical Fermi surface Z, Γ and Δz/Δx are strongly correlated.
- The changes in Z (1.67 → 1.2) and Γ (0.39 → 0.10 meV) between 0 and 0.8 GPa show that the junction changed. A change in the ratio could therefore reflect junction evolution rather than order-parameter evolution.

The authors should:
- report R_N for every pressure and temperature;
- estimate contact size against the mean free path (Wexler/Sharvin analysis);
- provide parameter-correlation matrices or χ² profiles in the ratio. For example, show explicitly that a fit with Δz/Δx fixed at 0.57 cannot reproduce the 0.6–0.8 GPa spectra for any Z and Γ, and conversely that a ratio near 1 cannot reproduce the ambient spectra;
- explain how the error bars were obtained. Values of ±0.01 meV in Fig. S8 are implausible given these correlations.

**3. The side dips that drive the pressure analysis could come from the critical-current or thermal regime, and this is not excluded under pressure.**
- The tests against heating (p. 5: T-dependence and field dependence of the peak) are performed only at ambient pressure, where no side dips exist.
- Symmetric conductance dips flanking a zero-bias peak are the hallmark of the critical-current regime described in refs. 42–44, which the authors cite.
- The 1.4 GPa spectrum makes this sharper: a broad flat-topped peak with sharp dips at about ±1.7 mV (Fig. 3d). It closely resembles a supercurrent or critical-current spectrum.
- Near the magnetically ordered phases the sample resistivity also changes, which alters the Maxwell contribution.

The authors should show, at 0.6–1.4 GPa:
- the field dependence of the dip positions (critical-current dips move rapidly to lower bias with field);
- whether dip position scales with R_N across junctions with different resistances;
- whether dip position tracks Δ(T) or I_c(T) as T → T_c.

Without this, the claimed shift towards pz dominance, which comes from fitting the dips, is not established.

**4. All pressure data come from a single junction in a single pressure run, with no test of reproducibility or reversibility.**
- S2 and S3 were measured only at ambient pressure.
- The double-dip structure at 1.0–1.2 GPa is attributed to "pressure inhomogeneity" or multiple domains, which itself shows that the junction is not well characterised.

The authors should:
- report a second pressurised junction, ideally on a different crystal;
- show the ambient-pressure spectrum after releasing pressure, to confirm the contact was not irreversibly modified by compression of the epoxy or by cycling;
- describe the pressure calibration (manometer, Pb/Sn T_c, and whether calibration was done at low temperature). This is absent from Methods.

**5. The model's Fermi surface and the exclusion of A_u are not adequate for the symmetry conclusions.**
- The calculation assumes a single spherical Fermi surface (k_z² + k_∥² ≈ k_F², SI p. 6). UTe₂ has quasi-2D cylindrical sheets along k_z, as in ref. 39, which is co-authored by members of this author team.
- For a (001) junction, the distribution of k_z and v_z on such a Fermi surface controls both transmission and the weight of the sign-changing pz component. The extracted Δz/Δx(y) is therefore strongly model-dependent.
- A_u is excluded (p. 8) using only the isotropic choice Δx = Δy = Δz with Γ = 0.01 meV (Fig. S6a). The authors acknowledge (p. 9–10) that an anisotropic A_u could mimic B₂u/B₃u, and with Γ ≈ 0.4 meV any peak splitting would be smeared.
- The statement that ZABS are "unambiguously suggesting the presence of pz component" (p. 4) is therefore inconsistent with the later concession that A_u cannot be excluded.

The authors should:
- fit A_u and B₁u with free, anisotropic amplitudes and compare goodness of fit quantitatively with B₂u/B₃u;
- at least test the ratio's robustness with a cylindrical or tight-binding Fermi surface;
- align the wording of the abstract and main text with the actual strength of the exclusion.

#### B. Should address

**6. The evidence for an SC2–SC3 phase transition from the ratio's temperature minimum is weak.**
- The minimum is defined by a handful of points (Fig. 5f, S9e,f).
- Near T_c the ratio of two shrinking, thermally smeared gaps becomes ill-conditioned; the growing error bars show this. A rise towards T_c may therefore be generic to the fit rather than to the physics.
- At 0.8 GPa the fitted Δx(y) increases with temperature from 0.87 meV (0.5 K) to 1.00 meV (1.1 K) (Fig. S8). This is unusual and suggests trade-off between parameters.
- The SC2–SC3 boundary used for comparison comes from other samples (refs. 27, 30), and the boundary location is known to depend on sample quality.

The authors should:
- present model-independent indicators from the raw spectra, such as G(0,T), dip depth or position versus T, and a derivative analysis showing a kink at T_SC2–SC3;
- determine the boundary on the same crystal, for example by in-situ ac susceptibility in the cell.

**7. The proposed B₂u (SC2) → B₃u (SC3) change is not supported by PCS and is thermodynamically questionable.**
- The authors themselves show that B₂u and B₃u are indistinguishable for a (001) junction, so the PCS data cannot distinguish them.
- A second, lower-temperature transition seen in calorimetry normally signals onset of an additional component, such as B₂u + B₃u with some relative phase, as discussed in ref. 30, rather than replacement of one irreducible representation by another.
- Within the fits, the ratio is larger in SC2 near T_c than in SC3 at 0.6 and 1.2 GPa (Fig. S9e,f). This sits awkwardly with describing SC3 as the "pz-dominant" phase.

The discussion (p. 10) and summary should be reframed accordingly.

**8. The multi-channel superposition (Eqs. 2–3) is under-constrained.**
- At 1.0–1.2 GPa the two-channel fits carry at least eight parameters: two Δx, two Δz, weights, Z and Γ.
- The weighted ratio in Eq. 3 is one of several possible averages.
- The SC1 → SC3 increase in the inset of Fig. 5a partly coincides with switching from one-channel to two-channel models.

The authors should:
- list all parameters with uncertainties for each pressure;
- show residuals;
- state which parameters were shared between channels;
- demonstrate that the conclusion is insensitive to the averaging definition and to the number of channels;
- consider alternatives for the double dips, such as multiband effects or a second critical-current feature.

**9. The sample and surface state probed by the contact are not established.**
- The resistive transition spans T_c⁰ = 2.1 K to T_c^on = 2.6 K (Fig. S1a). This is broad for ultraclean molten-salt-flux crystals, which usually show sharp transitions near 2.1 K.
- The ZBCP is already visible at 2.3–2.5 K (Fig. 2c), above bulk zero resistance, while the fitted gaps vanish near 2.05 K (Fig. S2c).
- The (001) surface was mechanically polished, and the epoxy was cured at 423 K for 30 minutes. Either could create a damaged, off-stoichiometric or strained surface layer. Such a layer could suppress specular reflection, which the ABS argument requires, or locally raise T_c.

The authors should:
- provide bulk thermodynamic T_c (specific heat or susceptibility) for S1;
- give post-polish orientation and roughness characterisation;
- discuss which region the contact probes.

The SC1/SC3 boundary at 0.3 GPa (hatched in Fig. 5a) is also assumed rather than measured. With data only at 0.2, 0.3 and 0.6 GPa, the "abrupt" increase in the Fig. 5a inset is not resolved.

**10. Extrinsic zero-bias anomalies near the magnetic phases are not considered.**
- Magnetic-scattering zero-bias anomalies are dismissed (p. 5) because UTe₂ is non-magnetic at ambient pressure.
- At 1.2–1.4 GPa the sample is next to MO1 and MO2, so Kondo-like anomalies or a strongly T-dependent normal-state background are plausible there.

The authors should show the normal-state spectra just above T_c and T_m at each pressure, and the field dependence at 1.4 GPa.

**11. Normalisation across pressures is not described, yet the peak heights (1.15, 1.70, 2.08) are compared quantitatively.**
The authors should state:
- how G_NS was normalised at each pressure (reference temperature and treatment of background);
- whether the spectra were symmetrised;
- how the model conductance is normalised to the normal-state transmission. Eq. S14 as written tends to 1 − |b|² rather than 1 at high bias.

### 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. Measure junctions on (100) and (010) faces, or the (0 −1 1) face, ideally under pressure. These geometries separate B₂u from B₃u, which would directly test the proposed SC2→SC3 change.
2. Use field-orientation-dependent PCS as a symmetry test. A field parallel to the (001) surface along a versus b gives Doppler-shift splitting of a ZABS peak, and the Zeeman response of the surface states depends on the d-vector orientation. Neither mechanism applies to critical-current dips.
3. Replace the spherical-Fermi-surface BTK with a calculation using a realistic quasi-2D (tight-binding) Fermi surface. Report how Δz/Δx(y) and the A_u/B₂u discrimination change.
4. Measure ac susceptibility on the same crystal inside the pressure cell. This would pin the SC2–SC3 boundary for the actual sample and allow a direct comparison with the minima in ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩.
