**Review outline:**

## 1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports point-contact spectroscopy (PCS) on the (001) surface of molten-salt-flux UTe₂ under hydrostatic pressure up to 1.4 GPa. Spectra are analyzed with an extended spin-triplet BTK model. The authors argue that:
- a zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP) implies a pz component, consistent with B₂u/B₃u;
- the ratio ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ rises from ≈0.57 (SC1) to ≈1.3 (SC3);
- a minimum in this ratio versus T tracks the SC2/SC3 boundary.

Symmetry-sensitive probes of the pressure-induced phases of UTe₂ are scarce, so spectroscopic data across SC1–SC3 would be valuable. However, the central quantitative claims rest on one junction under pressure and a heavily parameterized model. Neither has been sufficiently validated against extrinsic explanations.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **New data.** These appear to be the first PCS data on pressurized UTe₂ spanning SC1, SC2/SC3 and the edge of the magnetically ordered phases. Spectra are shown over full temperature ranges (Fig. 3, Fig. S3).
- **Clear pressure dependence.** The spectral shape changes qualitatively with pressure: a featureless ZBCP below 0.3 GPa; side dips at 0.6–0.8 GPa; double dips at 1.0–1.2 GPa; a broad flat-top peak at 1.4 GPa.
- **Ambient-pressure checks.** A ZBCP is reproduced in three ambient-pressure samples (Fig. 2d–f). The ambient-pressure ratio Δz/Δx(y) ≈ 0.5–0.6 is consistent across them.
- **Transparent model.** The BdG/BTK formalism is written out (SI IV). Simulations for each irreducible representation (IR) are provided (Figs. 4, S5, S6), so the qualitative screening logic can be followed.
- **Suggestive correlation.** The temperature of the ratio minimum (Fig. 5a) falls near the literature SC2/SC3 line.

## 3. Concerns

### A. Must address

**1. Critical-current and thermal-regime artifacts are not excluded for the pressure-induced features.**
- *What the paper does.* The heating argument (p. 5) is made only for the ambient-pressure ZBCP. It is also only qualitative: a gradual T dependence and a weak effect of fields below 2 T.
- *Why it matters.* The key pressure signatures are symmetric side dips that sharpen on cooling (0.6–1.2 GPa), "double dips" (1.0–1.2 GPa), and a flat-topped peak with sharp dips at ≈±1.5–2 mV (1.4 GPa, Fig. 3d). These are textbook signatures of contacts in the thermal/Maxwell regime, where the local current exceeds Ic (Sheet et al., ref. 42, which the authors cite). Several parallel nano-contacts with different Ic, as expected for an Au-epoxy soft contact, would naturally produce multiple dips.
- *What is missing.* The manuscript gives no normal-state junction resistance R_N at any pressure, no estimate of contact radius, and no comparison with the electronic mean free path. For RRR ≈ 410 the mean free path should be long, but the series spreading resistance of UTe₂ (ρ changes strongly with p and T, Fig. S1) also rescales the voltage axis.
- *What the authors should do.*
  - Report R_N(p, T) for each spectrum and a Wexler-type estimate of the regime.
  - Show how dip position V_dip evolves with T and with magnetic field at 0.6–1.4 GPa, and compare with Ic(T,B) ∝ Δ(T) or Hc2(T).
  - Show that the dip positions do not scale with the series resistance.

  Dips from the critical current typically shift strongly with field and with R_N. Andreev bound state (ABS) depletion dips should stay pinned near |Δ|.

**2. The evolution with pressure is measured on a single junction whose barrier parameters change at the same time.**
- *What the paper does.* All pressure data come from one contact on S1.
- *The confound.* The fitted Z and Γ change markedly between ambient pressure (Z = 1.67, Γ = 0.39 meV; Table S1/Fig. S7) and 0.8 GPa (Z ≈ 1.20, Γ ≈ 0.10 meV; Fig. S8). The authors' own simulations (Fig. S6c,d, p. 11 of SI) show that side dips appear only when Z ≳ 0.8 and Γ is small. Ambient-pressure sample S3 (Γ = 0.16 meV) already shows satellite structure (Fig. 2f) while lying in SC1 with Δz/Δx(y) ≈ 0.52. The appearance of side dips above 0.3 GPa therefore cannot be attributed to the order parameter rather than to the junction. Pressure cells are clamped at room temperature, and the medium is frozen at low T, so contact evolution under load is plausible.
- *What the authors should do.*
  - Measure at least one more junction (ideally on S2/S3) under pressure.
  - Measure a spectrum after pressure release, which ideally recovers the ambient-pressure spectrum and Z/Γ.
  - Fit with Z and Γ constrained across pressures, or show that unconstrained fits do not trade Γ against Δz/Δx(y).

**3. The fitted gap magnitudes are unphysical, the parameters are degenerate, and the supplementary table labels appear swapped.**
- *Magnitudes.*
  - At ambient pressure S1 gives Δ ≈ 1.18 meV with Tc⁰ = 2.1 K, i.e. 2Δ/kBTc ≈ 13. The authors' own BCS curve (Fig. S2c) is ≈0.33 meV, and STM and thermodynamic estimates for UTe₂ are a few tenths of a meV.
  - At 0.8–1.2 GPa, Δ ≈ 1–1.6 meV (Figs. S8, S9c).
  - Δx(y) at 0.8 GPa rises from 0.87 to 1.00 meV between 0.5 and 1.1 K (Fig. S8).
  - The authors read these values as "strongly violating BCS". The more likely reading is that the peak width is set by something other than the gap, such as spreading resistance, thermal broadening of the contact, or inhomogeneity. In that case Δx and Δz are not physical gap amplitudes, and neither is their ratio.
- *Swapped labels.* Table S1 lists S1 as Δz = 1.18(7), Δx(y) = 0.72(4). Fig. S7a and Fig. S2c list the opposite assignment, which is the one consistent with the quoted Δz/Δx(y) ≈ 0.57(5). The S2 and S3 rows appear similarly swapped. Since the paper's main parameter is this ratio, this must be corrected.
- *Degeneracy and what is fixed.* The ambient S1 spectrum is essentially a single Gaussian-like peak of ~15% height, fitted with four parameters. Z and Γ are nearly constant across temperatures in Figs. S7 and S8, but the paper never states whether they were fixed. The authors should:
  - state which parameters are free and which fixed at each T and p;
  - explain how the error bars were obtained;
  - show χ² maps or the correlation matrix in the (Δz/Δx(y), Z, Γ) space.

**4. Peak height is used as the measure of pz versus px(y) weight, but generic suppressions of Andreev signal are ignored.**
- *What the paper does.* It infers px(y) dominance at ambient pressure from a peak height below 1.2 (p. 8).
- *Why that inference is weak.* Small Andreev enhancements are generic in heavy-fermion point contacts. Causes include Fermi-velocity mismatch, the two-fluid/Kondo-lattice character of tunneling, parallel normal channels, a degraded surface layer, and series spreading resistance. These suppress the normalized conductance independently of the gap symmetry, and the effect would masquerade as a small Δz/Δx(y).
- *What the authors should do.*
  - Show that the ratio is robust when a phenomenological parallel/background channel or spreading resistance is included in the fit.
  - Discuss how the normalization (G0, normal-state reference spectrum) is defined at each pressure.

**5. The model does not reflect UTe₂'s Fermi surface or the candidate multicomponent states, and the competing IRs are excluded too narrowly.**
- *Fermi surface.* The calculation (SI IV) assumes a single spherical Fermi surface with linear p-wave basis functions. UTe₂ has quasi-2D cylindrical sheets along kc (ref. 39, which includes a co-author). For tunneling into (001), the c-axis velocity and kz-dispersion are small and strongly k-dependent. Both the weighting of the k∥ sum and the meaning of a "Δz" amplitude are therefore normalization-dependent. The ratio Δz/Δx(y) has no basis-independent meaning unless defined on a realistic Fermi surface.
- *Self-consistency.* The step-function gap (Θ(z)) neglects pair breaking at a (001) surface for the sign-changing pz component.
- *Unitarity assumption.* The assumption d × d* = 0 excludes, by construction, the non-unitary and multicomponent states proposed for UTe₂. Examples are B₂u + iB₃u (refs. 16, 24) and, specifically for the pressurized dual-transition region, the multicomponent scenario of ref. 30.
- *Au exclusion.* Au is excluded from one simulation with Δx = Δy = Δz and Γ = 0.01 meV (Fig. S6a). With realistic Γ ≈ 0.1–0.4 meV and unequal components, the splitting may be unresolved.
- *What the authors should do.*
  - Fit Au (with free Δx, Δy, Δz) and at least one non-unitary combination to the same data, and compare goodness-of-fit quantitatively.
  - Discuss how a cylindrical Fermi surface changes the conclusions.

**6. The SC2→SC3 transition and the B₂u→B₃u assignment are not established by the PCS data.**
- *Size of the anomaly.* The minimum in ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ (Fig. 5f, Fig. S9e,f) is a shallow variation of about 0.98–1.3. Above the minimum the ratio is formed from two vanishing quantities, with large and growing error bars. The authors should show that the minimum is statistically significant once correlated fit uncertainties are propagated.
- *Indistinguishable IRs.* B₂u and B₃u are indistinguishable for this geometry (Fig. S6). The proposed change of representation therefore rests entirely on the NMR result of ref. 31.
- *Symmetry of the transition.* A transition inside the superconducting state between two different one-dimensional IRs of D₂h is generically either first order or leads to a mixed (e.g. B₂u + iB₃u) state, not a pure B₃u phase. This should be discussed.
- *Independent boundary.* The SC2/SC3 boundary is taken from literature crystals. Resistivity on S1 cannot see it.
- *What the authors should do.* Detect the SC2/SC3 transition on the same sample in the same cell (e.g. ac susceptibility coil or ac calorimetry). Otherwise, temper the claims.

### B. Should address

**7. The superposition model and Eq. (3) are under-constrained, and one result points to contact inhomogeneity.**
- *Parameter count.* Above 0.8 GPa the fit uses two channels with weights, roughly doubling the free parameters, and the paper does not report them.
- *What Fig. S9c shows.* At 1.2 GPa both channels have nearly the same ratio (Δz¹/Δx¹ ≈ 1.6/1.25 and Δz²/Δx² ≈ 0.95/0.75, both ≈1.27). They differ mainly in overall scale. This is what one expects from inhomogeneous parallel contacts, or from the issue in concern 1, rather than from distinct order parameters.
- *What the ratio means.* If the channels are domains of different phases, ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ is not a property of a single phase.
- *What the authors should do.* Report all fitted parameters and the weights wᵢ. Explain why the weighted-average ratio in Eq. (3) is meaningful.

**8. Surface preparation and specular reflection are not verified.**
- *Why it matters.* The ZABS → pz inference requires specular reflection from a well-defined (001) facet.
- *The preparation.* The surface was mechanically polished (UTe₂ cleaves along (011)) and then heated to 423 K during epoxy curing, which risks damage, Te loss and oxidation.
- *Evidence of a modified surface.* The spectral hump appears at 2.5 K, above bulk Tc⁰ = 2.1 K (Fig. 2c). The resistive transition is broad (2.1–2.6 K) for an RRR ≈ 410 crystal. Both suggest the near-contact region may be strained or modified.
- *What the authors should do.*
  - Show the surface orientation after polishing (Laue/XRD) and an estimate of roughness.
  - Discuss contributions from other facets: on a rough surface, (011)-like facets would carry different ABS selection rules.
  - Discuss why the local Tc differs from the bulk.

**9. Field dependence and other ZBCP origins under pressure are not examined.**
- *What exists.* Field data exist only at ambient pressure (Fig. S2a). The statement that a field below 2 T "only weakly affects" the width is not compared with Hc2∥a.
- *What is missing.* Under pressure, especially at 1.4 GPa next to MO1/MO2, the ZBCP could also arise from magnetic or Kondo-like scattering. The absence of long-range order at ambient pressure does not exclude this.
- *Useful tests.*
  - Show that all features, including the side dips, vanish at the pressure-dependent Hc2, and show the spectra above Tc at each pressure.
  - An ABS should show characteristic Doppler-shift splitting or broadening in fields applied parallel to the surface. This would be a direct test of the bound-state interpretation.

**10. Pressure determination is not described.** The Methods do not say how pressure was measured (manometer, at which temperature). The Fig. 5a comparison with literature boundaries, and the 0.3 GPa SC1 threshold, depend on this. Crystals of different quality show shifted phase boundaries, so the uncertainty in p should be stated.

**11. Several statements are stronger than the evidence.** The abstract says "strongly suggests" but the main text (p. 4) says "unambiguously suggesting". "SC1–3 are mutually independent phases" (p. 10) is not defined and not demonstrated. The ZBCP is argued to be triplet-specific, yet a ZBCP also arises for sign-changing singlet states (as the authors note for d-wave). The claims should be aligned with what PCS on one facet can establish.

### C. Minor

**12. Normalization in Eq. (1)/(S12).** The conductance as written is not normalized by the normal-state transmission Σ|tₙ|². State the normalization explicitly, and give the weighting used in the k∥ sum (velocity or angular factor).

**13. Table I.** The "Spin component" column (ẑ, ŷ, x̂) apparently denotes the d-vector component that is absent. Label it clearly. Use "Table I" consistently rather than "Table 1".

**14. SI V cross-reference.** The text cites Fig. S5(a) for the Z = 1 zero-bias dip of px(y); this should be Fig. S5(b).

**15. Fig. 1 caption.** It describes "reflection of Cooper pairs"; it should be reflection of quasiparticles. "Differentiate conductance" should be "differential conductance".

**16. References.** Ref. 24 has the wrong year (2002 → 2020). Ref. 1 contains typos ("Pep. Rrog. Phys.").

**17. Typos.** These include "spits" (splits), "Supplementray", "constraints on" (constrains), and "presentation" (representation).

**18. Data availability.** Fitted parameters are given only graphically, and raw spectra are not provided. Please deposit the raw dI/dV data and the fit code.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Strengthen the core contrast.** Add a second pressurized junction (ideally on S2/S3) plus a pressure-release spectrum. Report R_N(p, T) with a regime analysis. Together these would show that the SC1→SC3 contrast is not a junction effect.
2. **Add field dependence under pressure.** Measure field dependence at 0.8 and 1.2 GPa. This discriminates critical-current dips from ABS-depletion dips and tests the bound-state origin via in-plane field splitting.
3. **Run a more realistic model and compare quantitatively.** Re-run the analysis with a quasi-2D cylindrical Fermi surface and a parallel-channel or spreading-resistance term. Compare χ² across B₂u/B₃u, a generic Au state, and a non-unitary B₂u + iB₃u state.
4. **Measure the SC2/SC3 transition on the same sample.** Use, for example, an ac susceptibility coil in the same cell to independently confirm the boundary that the ratio minimum is claimed to track.
