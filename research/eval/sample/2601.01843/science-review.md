## A. Must address

**1. Critical-current and thermal-regime artifacts are not excluded for the pressure-induced side dips.** The side dips that appear above about 0.3 GPa (Fig. 3b–d, Fig. S3) are the main evidence for pz dominance in SC2/SC3 and for excluding B₁u: symmetric side dips that sharpen on cooling at 0.6–1.2 GPa, "double dips" at 1.0–1.2 GPa, and at 1.4 GPa a broad flat-topped peak with sharp dips at about ±1.5–2 mV (≈±1.7 mV; Fig. 3d) that closely resembles a supercurrent or critical-current spectrum. Symmetric dips flanking a zero-bias peak are the textbook signature of contacts in the thermal/Maxwell or critical-current regime, where the local current exceeds I_c (Sheet et al., ref. 42, and refs. 42–44, which the authors cite). Several parallel nano-contacts with different I_c, as expected for an Au-epoxy soft contact, would naturally produce multiple dips. The heating argument (p. 5) is qualitative (gradual T dependence, weak effect of fields below 2 T, not compared with Hc2∥a) and is made only at ambient pressure (Fig. S2a), where no dips exist; no test is applied to the pressurized spectra. The manuscript gives no normal-state junction resistance R_N at any pressure, no contact-radius estimate (Sharvin/Wexler), no comparison with the elastic and inelastic mean free paths (which should be long for RRR ≈ 410), and no description of the dI/dV scheme (lock-in excitation amplitude and frequency or numerical derivative, bias range, two-probe or pseudo-four-probe). The series spreading resistance of UTe₂, whose ρ changes strongly with p and T (Fig. S1) and near the magnetically ordered phases, also rescales the voltage axis and alters the Maxwell contribution. The authors should report R_N(p, T) for each spectrum with a Wexler-type regime analysis; show how the dip position V_dip evolves with T and with magnetic field at 0.6–1.4 GPa and compare with I_c(T,B), Δ(T) or Hc2(T) (critical-current dips move rapidly to lower bias with field and shift with R_N, whereas ABS-depletion dips stay pinned near |Δ|); show whether V_dip scales with R_N across contacts (V_dip = I_c R_N) or with the series resistance, using additional contacts or samples under pressure; and state explicitly whether the 1.4 GPa spectrum is in the thermal regime. Without this, the attribution of the dips to bulk-continuum depletion by surface ABS, and the shift towards pz dominance obtained by fitting them, is not established.

**2. The fitted gap magnitudes are too large to be superconducting gaps, which undermines the ratio built on them.** At ambient pressure S1 gives Δ ≈ 1.15–1.18 meV with Tc⁰ = 2.1 K (Table S1, Fig. S7, Fig. S9a), i.e. 2Δ/k_BTc ≈ 13, roughly 3.7 to 4 times the BCS line the authors draw at about 0.32–0.33 meV in Fig. S2c. At 0.8–1.2 GPa Δ ≈ 1–1.6 meV (Figs. S8, S9c), with Δz¹ ≈ 1.6 meV at 1.2 GPa for Tc ≈ 3 K (2Δ/k_BTc ≈ 12). These values are well above the gap scales of a few tenths of a meV reported by STM, tunnelling and thermodynamic measurements on UTe₂. The text (p. 8, Fig. S2 caption) reads the deviation as "strongly violating BCS" and as evidence of unconventional superconductivity, but a gap ratio of this size is not a signature of unconventional pairing. It more naturally indicates that the fitted Δ absorbs other energy scales: critical-current or Maxwell-term structure, spreading resistance shifting the voltage scale, thermal broadening of the contact, inhomogeneity or multiple parallel contacts, or the broad Dynes term (Γ ≈ 0.4 meV at ambient). In that case Δx and Δz are not physical gap amplitudes, and their ratio is not obviously a measure of the pz/px(y) pairing weights. The authors should compare the fitted Δ(T→0) with Tc, with literature gaps and with the observed ZBCP widths; correct for any series resistance and show that the voltage scale of the features does not depend on R_N (see concern 1); explain why the fitted Δ is a gap; and show whether the extracted ratio survives when the absolute gap is constrained to physically reasonable values.

**3. All pressure data come from a single junction in a single pressure run whose barrier parameters change at the same time.** All pressure spectra come from one Au-epoxy contact on S1; S2 and S3 were measured only at ambient pressure. The fitted Z and Γ change markedly between ambient pressure (Z = 1.67, Γ = 0.39 meV; Table S1, Fig. S7) and 0.8 GPa (Z ≈ 1.20, Γ ≈ 0.10 meV; Fig. S8), which shows the junction itself evolved; the contact is compressed under load, clamped at room temperature, with the medium frozen at low T, and thermally cycled between pressures. The authors' own simulations (Fig. S6c,d, SI p. 11) show that side dips appear only when Z ≳ 0.8 and Γ is small, and ambient-pressure sample S3 (Γ = 0.16 meV) already shows satellite structure (Fig. 2f) while lying in SC1 with Δz/Δx(y) ≈ 0.52. The appearance of side dips above 0.3 GPa, and the change in the ratio, therefore cannot be attributed to the order parameter rather than to the junction. The attribution of the double dips at 1.0–1.2 GPa to "pressure inhomogeneity" or multiple domains itself shows the junction is not well characterised. The authors should reproduce the main pressure trends (onset of side dips above 0.3 GPa, the ratio minimum, the double dips) on at least one more pressurized junction, ideally on a different crystal such as S2 or S3; show the spectrum after pressure release, which ideally recovers the ambient-pressure spectrum, SC1-type behavior and Z/Γ; fit with Z and Γ constrained across pressures, or show that unconstrained fits do not trade Γ against Δz/Δx(y); and state whether each pressure point required warming to room temperature, the number of thermal cycles, and how the contact changed after cycling.

**4. The fit parameters are degenerate and the fitting procedure is not reported.** Each spectrum is fit with at least four free parameters (Δx(y), Δz, Z, Γ), and with a spherical Fermi surface Z, Γ and Δz/Δx(y) are strongly correlated. Several spectra, notably S1 and S2 at ambient pressure (Fig. 2d,e, Fig. S7), are a single featureless, Gaussian-like peak only 5–15% above background, which cannot constrain both a gap ratio and Z and Γ. Z and Γ are nearly constant across temperatures in Figs. S7 and S8 (in Fig. S8, Γ = 0.10 meV and Z ≈ 1.20 at all T), suggesting they were fixed, but the paper never says so. The quoted error bars (for example ±0.01 meV in Fig. S8) are implausible given these correlations, and at 0.8 GPa the fitted Δx(y) rises with temperature from 0.87 meV (0.5 K) to 1.00 meV (1.1 K) (Fig. S8), which suggests parameter trade-off. The authors should state which parameters are free and which fixed at each T and p; explain how the error bars were obtained; show χ² maps, χ² profiles in the ratio or the parameter correlation matrix in the (Δz/Δx(y), Z, Γ) space; demonstrate explicitly that a fit with Δz/Δx(y) fixed at 0.57 cannot reproduce the 0.6–0.8 GPa spectra for any Z and Γ, and conversely that a ratio near 1 cannot reproduce the ambient spectra; and discuss the non-monotonic Δx(y)(T) at 0.8 GPa.

**5. Peak height is used to infer px(y) versus pz weight, but generic suppressions of the Andreev signal are ignored.** The key ambient-pressure argument (p. 8) is that G_NS(0) < 1.2 "suggests that the px(y) pairing component plays a more dominant role." Small Andreev enhancements are generic in heavy-fermion point contacts: conductance enhancements of only a few to about 15% are typical even for clean contacts on CeCoIn₅ and similar materials. Causes include the large Fermi-velocity/mass mismatch between Au and heavy-fermion UTe₂, the two-fluid/Kondo-lattice character of tunneling, parallel normal channels or multi-contacts, non-ballistic transport, series spreading resistance, a partially non-superconducting or degraded surface layer, and a non-flat normal-state background. These suppress the normalized conductance independently of gap symmetry and would masquerade as a small Δz/Δx(y). The ambient-pressure conclusion currently rests heavily on this amplitude argument plus the satellite structure of S3 alone, and the SC1 value of the ratio anchors the SC1-to-SC3 contrast. The authors should show that the ratio is robust when a phenomenological parallel/background channel or spreading resistance is included in the fit, or otherwise weaken the claim or support it independently.

Raised by: r1, r2, r3

**6. The exclusion of other irreducible representations rests on simulations rather than fits, and non-unitary states are excluded by assumption.** A_u is excluded (p. 8) using only the isotropic choice Δx = Δy = Δz with Γ = 0.01 meV (Fig. S6a). With realistic Γ ≈ 0.1–0.4 meV, which at ambient pressure exceeds the simulated gap, and with unequal components, the peak splitting used to exclude A_u would be washed out; the authors themselves acknowledge (p. 9–10) that an anisotropic A_u could mimic B₂u/B₃u. B₁u is excluded only by the absence of side dips, yet S1 and S2 at ambient pressure also show no side dips. The assumption d × d* = 0 excludes by construction the non-unitary, chiral and multicomponent states proposed for UTe₂, such as B₂u + iB₃u (refs. 16, 24), B₃u + iA_u (refs. 16, 24–25) and, for the pressurized dual-transition region specifically, the multicomponent scenario of ref. 30. For each representative spectrum (ambient S1–S3; 0.2, 0.6, 0.8, 1.2 GPa) the authors should provide best fits with A_u and B₁u (with free, anisotropic Δx, Δy, Δz), B₂u/B₃u, at least one non-unitary combination, and an s-wave BTK plus broadening baseline, all with the same parameter freedom, and compare them quantitatively with χ² or an information criterion.

Raised by: r1, r2, r3

**7. The model's spherical Fermi surface does not reflect UTe₂, so the extracted ratio has no model-independent meaning.** The calculation (SI IV, SI p. 6) assumes a single spherical Fermi surface (k_z² + k_∥² ≈ k_F²) with linear-in-k p-wave basis functions, a unitary d-vector and specular reflection. UTe₂ has quasi-2D cylindrical Fermi sheets along k_z (ref. 39, which includes co-authors of this paper). For tunneling into (001) these sheets carry small and strongly k-dependent v_z and k_z dispersion, and the k_z-odd gap on a cylinder is a lattice harmonic (for example sin k_z c), not k_z. The distribution of k_z and v_z then controls both transmission and the weight of the sign-changing pz component, so the weighting of channels in the k∥ sum of Eq. (1), and hence Δz/Δx(y), is normalization- and basis-dependent; the ratio has no basis-independent meaning unless defined on a realistic Fermi surface. The step-function gap Θ(z) also neglects pair breaking at a (001) surface for the sign-changing pz component. The authors should re-run the analysis with a quasi-2D cylindrical or tight-binding Fermi surface, or at minimum discuss how it would alter the simulated spectra, report how Δz/Δx(y) and the A_u/B₂u discrimination change, and discuss the effect of non-self-consistent gap treatment.

Raised by: r1, r2, r3

**8. The SC2/SC3 transition inferred from the minimum of the ratio is not established.** The minimum in ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ (Fig. 5f, Fig. S9e,f) is a shallow variation of about 0.98–1.3 defined by a handful of points. Near Tc the ratio is formed from two shrinking, thermally smeared quantities and becomes ill-conditioned, with large and growing error bars (Fig. S9e, at about 2.5 K), so a rise towards Tc may be generic to the fit rather than to the physics; the non-monotonic Δx(y)(T) at 0.8 GPa (concern 4) points the same way. The SC2/SC3 boundary used for comparison comes from other crystals (refs. 27, 30), its location is known to depend on sample quality, and resistivity on S1 cannot see it. The authors should show that the minimum is statistically significant once correlated fit uncertainties are propagated, and that it cannot arise from fit-parameter trade-offs; present model-independent indicators from the raw spectra, such as G(0,T), dip depth or position versus T, and a derivative analysis showing a kink at T_SC2–SC3; and determine the boundary on the same sample in the same cell, for example by in-situ ac susceptibility or ac calorimetry. Otherwise the claims should be tempered.

## B. Should address

**9. A change in the ratio does not by itself show that SC1 and SC3 are distinct phases, and the 0.3 GPa threshold is not resolved.** Within a single irreducible representation Δz/Δx(y) is not fixed by symmetry and can vary continuously with pressure, so a change from about 0.6 (SC1) to about 1.3 (SC3) is not, on its own, evidence of distinct phases. This matters especially because the Discussion itself suggests that SC1 (by comparison with STM, ref. 34) and SC3 may both be B₃u, and the statement that "SC1–3 are mutually independent phases" (p. 10) is neither defined nor demonstrated. The "abrupt increase" in the Fig. 5a inset rests on about seven pressures from one junction while Z and Γ also change, and the SC1/SC3 boundary at 0.3 GPa (hatched in Fig. 5a) is assumed rather than measured; with data only at 0.2, 0.3 and 0.6 GPa the abruptness is not resolved. The authors should define what makes the phases distinct, add pressure points near 0.3 GPa or reproduce the step on a second pressurized junction, and otherwise describe the change as a continuous evolution.

Raised by: r1, r2, r3

**10. The proposed B₂u (SC2) to B₃u (SC3) change is not supported by the PCS data and is thermodynamically non-generic.** The authors' own simulations show B₂u and B₃u give identical (001) spectra in zero field (Fig. S6), so the proposed change of representation rests entirely on the NMR result of ref. 31, and it is unclear why the onset of a B₃u component should produce a minimum in the ratio at all. A transition inside the superconducting state between two different one-dimensional irreducible representations of D₂h is generically either first order or produces a mixed state (B₂u + B₃u with some relative phase, or B₂u + iB₃u, as discussed in ref. 30), not replacement by a pure B₃u phase. Within the fits, the ratio is also larger in SC2 near Tc than in SC3 at 0.6 and 1.2 GPa (Fig. S9e,f), which sits awkwardly with describing SC3 as the "pz-dominant" phase. The authors should simulate a two-IR state whose second component grows below T_SC3 and show whether it reproduces the minimum in Fig. 5f and Fig. S9e,f, discuss the order of the transition, and reframe the discussion (p. 10) and summary accordingly.

Raised by: r1, r2, r3

**11. The multi-channel superposition model (Eqs. 2–3) is under-constrained and its interpretation is ambiguous.** Above 0.8 GPa, and at 1.0–1.2 GPa in particular, the two-channel fits carry at least eight parameters (two Δx, two Δz, weights w_i, Z, Γ), and the paper does not report them; the Fig. S9 caption does not define Δ¹, Δ² or the weights used at 1.2 GPa. At 1.2 GPa both channels have nearly the same ratio (Δz¹/Δx¹ ≈ 1.6/1.25 and Δz²/Δx² ≈ 0.95/0.75, both ≈ 1.27; Fig. S9c) and differ mainly in overall scale, which is what one expects from inhomogeneous parallel contacts or from the critical-current issue of concern 1 rather than from distinct order parameters. The double dips are attributed either to multiple gaps or to pressure inhomogeneity, two physically very different interpretations that are not distinguished; if the channels are domains of different phases, ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ is not a property of a single phase, and the weighted ratio of Eq. (3) is one of several possible averages. The SC1-to-SC3 increase in the Fig. 5a inset also partly coincides with switching from one-channel to two-channel models. The authors should list all fitted parameters and weights with uncertainties for each pressure, show residuals, state which parameters were shared between channels, explain why Eq. (3) is meaningful, demonstrate that the conclusion is insensitive to the averaging definition and the number of channels, and consider alternatives for the double dips such as multiband effects or a second critical-current feature.

**12. Extrinsic origins of the zero-bias peak near the magnetic phases, and the 1.4 GPa spectrum, are not addressed.** Magnetic-scattering zero-bias anomalies are dismissed (p. 5) because UTe₂ is non-magnetic at ambient pressure, but at 1.2–1.4 GPa the sample is next to MO1 and MO2, where Kondo-like or magnetic-scattering anomalies and a strongly T-dependent normal-state background are plausible. The 1.4 GPa spectrum is shown but neither fit nor discussed. Field data exist only at ambient pressure (Fig. S2a), and the statement that fields below 2 T "only weakly affect" the width is not compared with Hc2∥a. The authors should show the normal-state spectra just above Tc and T_m at each pressure, show the field dependence under pressure (in particular at 1.4 GPa) and that all features, including the side dips, vanish at the pressure-dependent Hc2, and address the 1.4 GPa spectrum explicitly.

**13. The surface and sample state probed by the contact are not established.** The ZABS-to-pz inference requires specular reflection from a well-defined (001) facet, but the surface was mechanically polished (UTe₂ cleaves along (011)) and then heated to 423 K for 30 minutes during epoxy curing, which risks damage, strain, Te loss, oxidation or an off-stoichiometric layer; possible interfacial reactions with Au should also be discussed, since gold tellurides exist and avoiding Ag does not remove all Te-reaction concerns. On a rough surface, (011)-like facets would carry different ABS selection rules. The resistive transition is broad for an RRR ≈ 410 molten-salt-flux crystal (onset 2.6 K, zero resistance 2.1 K; Fig. S1a), and the ZBCP is already visible at 2.3–2.5 K (Fig. 2c), tracking the onset rather than bulk Tc, while the fitted gaps vanish near 2.05 K (Fig. S2c). **16. Field dependence is not exploited as a direct test of the bound-state origin and of B₂u versus B₃u.** An ABS should show characteristic Doppler-shift splitting or broadening in fields applied parallel to the surface, which would directly test the bound-state interpretation and does not apply to critical-current dips. The statement that B₂u and B₃u are indistinguishable on (001) holds only in zero field: with H ∥ a, the ABS from the k_z-odd component carries d ∥ x̂ for B₂u and d ∥ ŷ for B₃u, so a Zeeman field should split or shift the ABS differently in the two cases.
