**A. Must address**

1. **No evidence that the probed surface layer is superconducting and intrinsic.** The null result (no gap, no flat band) is equally consistent with a non-superconducting, degraded or heavily overdoped surface layer. The manuscript does not exclude this.
   - **SQUID tests only the bulk.** The SQUID check before and after milling is bulk-sensitive. Even the "thin slab at the top" is dominated by unmilled material. It says nothing about the top ~5 Å probed by VUV ARPES at the cleave plane.
   - **No surface characterization.** There are no XPS/core-level data (Cu 2p satellites, O 1s, La/Sr ratio, implanted Ga/Xe from (P)FIB), no in situ LEED/RHEED, and no cross-sectional TEM/STEM/EDX near the notch. Several extrinsic mechanisms therefore remain open:
     - FIB-induced amorphization or a dead layer;
     - ion implantation;
     - oxygen loss or Sr segregation in UHV;
     - surface polarity or reconstruction of the (110) termination;
     - cleave-induced strain or microcracking.
   - **The doping estimate does not secure the surface.** The surface doping from Fermi-surface fitting spans p ≈ 0.20–0.30. The authors themselves describe these fits as poor and distorted. The upper end lies beyond the LSCO superconducting dome (~0.27). In addition, the tight-binding chemical potential was tuned to the nodal kF, which is only weakly doping-dependent. The claims that the surface is "unlikely to undergo a reconstruction", is bulk-like in doping, and is "pristine" are therefore not established.
   - **The EDCs show no temperature dependence.** The EDCs at 6, 21 and 35 K (Fig. 4g), and up to 55 K for the second sample (Supp. Fig. 11), are essentially identical across Tc (~27 K). No leading-edge shift, coherence peak or symmetrized/Fermi–Dirac-divided analysis is shown for the (110) data.
   - **Requested analyses.**
     - Perform leading-edge-midpoint analysis relative to gold across Tc. Use the same space-charge and flux-extrapolation protocol applied to the (001) reference in Supp. E.
     - Show symmetrized and Fermi–Dirac-divided nodal and antinodal EDCs with error bars at all temperatures.
     - Search for any other temperature-dependent signature, such as off-nodal spectral changes or the nodal kink.
     - Provide the surface-sensitive characterization listed above.
     - Compare nodal quasiparticle linewidths with those from (001) cleaves.
     - Give an error-bounded surface-doping estimate, for example by constraining fits with the nodal kF and the antinodal Lifshitz topology.

2. **Missing discriminating controls on surfaces of the same crystal and preparation route.** Without a control, the absence of a gap and flat band cannot be attributed to (110)-specific physics rather than to generic degradation of side-cleaved surfaces.
   - **(100)/(010) side surface.** The decisive control is a FIB-cleaved (100)/(010) side surface of the same x = 0.22 crystal under identical conditions. There, the d_{x²−y²} order parameter is not pair-broken and no flat band is expected, so an antinodal gap should be visible.
   - **(001) reference.** The existing (001) reference (Supp. E) is not an adequate substitute. It comes from a different doping (x = 0.19), uses a different photon energy and resolution, and does not test the FIB/side-cleave procedure. A (001) surface of the same x = 0.22 crystal, measured in the same beamtime, is also needed.
   - **Larger gap.** Repeating at lower doping (e.g. optimally doped LSCO, x ≈ 0.15, with larger gap and Tc) would enlarge the expected contrast.

3. **The expected gap scale is internally inconsistent, so both the "surprising" absence and the simulations are miscalibrated.**
   - **Expected versus measured gap.** The text expects a gap of "approximately 20 meV" at x = 0.22. The BdG pairing is tuned to ~24 meV at the antinode. Yet the authors' own (001) reference at x = 0.19 yields only ~7 meV, which undermines rather than supports preservation of surface superconductivity at the expected strength. At the more overdoped x = 0.22 the gap should be smaller still.
   - **The predicted structure may be unresolvable.** At ~5–7 meV, the predicted dip–peak structure would be compressed to a scale comparable to:
     - the 3–4 meV resolution;
     - thermal broadening at 6 K;
     - the large intrinsic antinodal lifetime broadening of overdoped LSCO near the van Hove singularity and Lifshitz transition (tens of meV).
     The simulations include only 3 meV broadening and no self-energy.
   - **The quantitative conclusions depend on Δ.** A smaller Δ implies a longer coherence length and a more delocalized edge state. That state would have less weight within the probing depth, more kz smearing, and greater fragility to disorder. Both conclusions — that AFM roughness is "insufficient" and that ~100 meV disorder is required — may change qualitatively.
   - **Requested recalculation.** Recalibrate the BdG pairing to the measured gap (~5–7 meV). Include realistic antinodal self-energy broadening. Reassess whether the clean-limit signature would be resolvable at all.

4. **The non-observation is not quantified, so "suppressed beyond detectability" cannot be evaluated.**
   - **Processing could hide weak features.** The key evidence (Fig. 4g) consists of EDCs processed in several ways that could suppress a broad or weak near-EF enhancement or narrow peak, or create spurious structure:
     - empirical Shirley/Matt-type background subtraction (Ref. 77);
     - normalization to total intensity;
     - integration over broad k sectors defined from the simulations.
     These EDCs are compared by eye with gold.
   - **Agreement is not quantified.** The "visual agreement" between Fig. 4d (right) and Fig. 4f is not quantified, for example by χ².
   - **What is missing.** There are no raw EDCs, no error bars and no detection-limit analysis.
   - **Requested analysis.**
     - Show raw spectra before background subtraction and normalization.
     - Forward-model the clean-surface prediction through the measured background, resolution, 6 K thermal broadening, analyzer response, measured S/N, a consistent kz broadening, and identical integration windows.
     - Alternatively, fit the k-resolved EDCs with a Fermi–Dirac function, Gaussian resolution, a lifetime-broadened bulk spectral function and an optional zero-energy surface peak.
     - Either way, extract a quantitative upper bound on flat-band weight and width relative to the bulk.

5. **The disorder strength is ad hoc, not independently constrained, and made consistent with a bulk gap only by construction.**
   - **The disorder is not "moderate".** The Introduction calls the disorder "moderate" and "comparable to the superconducting gap". In fact σ = 100 meV Gaussian on-site disorder on every site is ~4–14× the gap and roughly half the nearest-neighbour hopping.
   - **It should be strongly pair-breaking.** A Born estimate gives Γ ≈ πN(0)σ² ~ 10–20 meV, i.e. Γ ~ Δ. For a d-wave superconductor this should strongly suppress Tc (Abrikosov–Gor'kov-type) and produce large nodal and antinodal linewidths.
   - **The pairing was retuned to compensate.** The authors raised V from 0.153 to 0.21555 (units given as "meV", which is implausible for a ~24 meV gap; presumably eV) to restore the bulk gap. Why this is physically justified, rather than a symptom of excessive disorder, is not discussed. The disordered and clean calculations therefore do not describe the same physical system.
   - **Methodological details are missing.** Only one disorder strength is shown, apparently for a single realization. There is:
     - no σ scan;
     - no statement of disorder averaging;
     - no finite-size check (280 × 200 sites relative to ξ and the disorder correlation length);
     - no criterion for when the peak becomes undetectable at experimental resolution.
     The surviving bulk gap (Supp. Fig. 12) is shown only qualitatively.
   - **Requested analysis.**
     - Scan σ (e.g. 10–150 meV) without retuning V. Report flat-band peak height and width, bulk gap and Tc, averaged over realizations, with finite-size checks.
     - Map σ onto the implied normal-state scattering rate, residual resistivity, nodal MDC widths and Tc suppression.
     - Compare these with the measured nodal linewidths (Fig. 3d), the authors' (001) data, and transport and superfluid-density data for x ≈ 0.22 LSCO.

6. **The disorder type chosen is the least representative of LSCO and possibly the most favourable to the conclusion.** The literature the authors cite (Özdemir, Lee-Hone, Hirschfeld) argues that disorder in overdoped LSCO is predominantly weak, smooth, forward-scattering out-of-plane Sr/O dopant disorder. By the authors' own chirality argument, inter-chirality hybridization requires large momentum transfer, so such disorder should be much less effective at destroying the flat band. Uncorrelated on-site Gaussian disorder is therefore not a representative test of the mechanism invoked. The authors should compare at least:
   - spatially correlated (smooth) out-of-plane dopant potentials;
   - dilute strong (unitary) scatterers;
   - hopping and pairing disorder;
   - surface-localized versus bulk disorder.
   They should show explicitly whether realistic forward-scattering disorder can suppress the flat band at the experimental resolution.

7. **The central conclusion establishes sufficiency, not necessity; alternative explanations are not excluded and no discriminating test is offered.**
   - **What the evidence supports.** The abstract states that disorder is "the key factor currently preventing" flat bands. The evidence only shows that one disorder model *can* reproduce featureless spectra.
   - **Alternatives that also give featureless spectra.**
     - a non-superconducting surface (concern 1);
     - a small gap combined with lifetime broadening (concern 3);
     - displacement of the states below the topmost layer, which the authors themselves invoke;
     - sub-resolution micro-faceting;
     - spontaneous surface symmetry breaking that splits or gaps the flat band below resolution: TRSB d+is, phase crystals, edge magnetism.
   - **Symmetry breaking is excluded by construction.** The last alternative is the very physics motivating the study, yet the self-consistent calculation rules it out by restricting to a single d-wave channel.
   - **Requested changes.**
     - Include subdominant s- or d_xy-wave and magnetic channels in the self-consistency.
     - Identify and test a prediction specific to the disorder scenario, such as the predicted increase of trivial-sector weight near EF or a particular k-space redistribution.
     - Discuss the competing explanations quantitatively, and state which measurements would discriminate among them.

**B. Should address**

8. **The geometric-roughness conclusion only applies to roughness the AFM can resolve, and the stated bound is logically reversed.**
   - **Resolution limits.** The authors concede that flat-band suppression requires roughness at the single-unit-cell lateral scale. AFM lateral resolution (a few nm) cannot resolve this. Only rms height (1.8 Å) is given, with no lateral correlation length or step density.
   - **Sampling mismatch.** The AFM covers ~1 µm², whereas the beam spot is ~40–50 µm on a ~150 µm cleave.
   - **The bound is reversed.** The claimed "upper bound on the disorder autocorrelation length" is reversed or ill-defined. AFM can exclude long-wavelength roughness but cannot bound the short-wavelength part that matters.
   - **Unclear measurement conditions.** It is not stated whether AFM was done in situ or after air exposure, or on the same cleave as the ARPES. Ex situ topography may not represent the measured surface.
   - **Requested changes.**
     - Rephrase the bound correctly, and state the lateral-resolution caveat prominently in the abstract and conclusions.
     - Provide same-cleave, preferably in situ, higher-lateral-resolution topography over larger areas (nc-AFM, high-resolution tips, or STM on larger FIB-defined facets).

9. **The roughness model omits c-axis variation, misorientation and faceting.** The AFM height histogram is mapped onto site removal in a single-orbital 2D model at a/√2 discretization. This ignores roughness along c: in the body-centred stack, each CuO₂ plane may terminate at a different position, and ARPES integrates over the full (110)–[001] surface.
   - **Misorientation and faceting are not quantified.** Macroscopic misorientation and facet coexistence within the spot are not quantified, although the SEM shows non-trivial topography. The miscut tolerance of the Laue-based alignment is also not stated. A vicinal surface would contain (100)/(010) micro-facets with no flat band, and misorientation is known to redistribute and weaken flat-band weight.
   - **Requested changes.** Quantify misorientation by SEM/EBSD or from the ARPES photon-energy and angle maps. Extend the simulations to include interlayer edge-position variation and small misorientation angles.

10. **The kz broadening is reported inconsistently, and other kinematic factors affecting visibility are not discussed.** Flat-band visibility at the antinodal kz depends critically on kz broadening, so these points matter for the predictions.
    - **Conflicting values.** Methods give Δkz ≈ 0.4 Å⁻¹ (FWHM = 2/λ, λ ≈ 4.7 Å). The Fig. 3 caption states that all calculations use Δk₍₁₁₀₎ = 0.2 Å⁻¹ with λ ≈ 5 Å. The text further says the calculations overestimate kz broadening, attributing this to aliasing and spectral leakage of the slab.
    - **Kinematics.** The choice of hν = 95 eV for the antinodal kz (and 79 eV for the nodal) relies on a free-electron final state with an unstated inner potential, while Supp. F reports noticeable Fermi-surface distortions.
    - **Matrix elements.** Matrix-element and polarization effects in this unusual geometry are not discussed.
    - **Requested changes.** Resolve the broadening discrepancy and use a single justified value. State the inner potential, and discuss matrix-element and polarization effects.

11. **The disorder theory is insufficiently placed in the context of existing work, and some terminology is confusing.** The disorder sensitivity of (110) Andreev bound states is well established in the quasiclassical and BdG literature on rough or diffuse surfaces and impurity broadening of ZBCPs (Kalenkov–Fogelström–Barash, including their two regimes; Poenicke et al.; Tanaka–Kashiwaya; Asano–Tanaka; Golubov; Queiroz–Schnyder). These are cited only briefly or not at all. The mechanism itself is familiar: chiral-symmetric disorder couples opposite-chirality sectors whose net index vanishes once k∥ is not conserved.
    - **Requested changes.** Benchmark the predicted bandwidth "proportional to disorder strength" against these results, and state the novelty explicitly relative to them; as presented, it appears incremental.
    - **Terminology.** The disorder Vᵢ = vᵢσ_z in the Nambu basis (σ_x in the chiral basis) is an ordinary non-magnetic potential. Calling it a "mass term" is confusing.

12. **The title, abstract and conclusions overstate what was shown.**
    - **Title.** "Suppression of Spectral Gap and Flat Bands" implies that an intrinsic suppression was demonstrated. What is shown is non-observation.
    - **Abstract and conclusions.**
      - "Reveal disorder as the key factor" is a model inference not discriminated from alternatives.
      - "Pristine" is asserted from rms roughness alone.
      - "Moderate" and "comparable to the gap" misdescribe 100 meV disorder.
    - **Requested changes.** Recast the paper around its well-supported contributions, the technique and the null result. For example, use "absence of resolvable gap and flat-band signatures" and "bulk disorder of this type is a sufficient explanation". Present disorder as a plausible hypothesis, not an established cause.

13. **Reproducibility and sample statistics are not reported.**
    - The number of samples and cleaves measured, and how many succeeded, is unclear.
    - It is not stated where the beam spot sat relative to cleave features, or whether it included steps or other facets.
    - The temperature-dependent intensity changes in the main sample were not reproduced in the second sample and are dismissed as extrinsic drift.
    The authors should report these statistics and discuss the irreproducible changes.

14. **The spatial-averaging hypothesis in the Discussion is not tested.** The authors argue that flat-band weight may survive on isolated nanoscale segments, which would reconcile STM observations with area-averaged ARPES. This is testable with nano-ARPES (sub-µm spot) or by spatially scanning the existing beam. If the hypothesis holds, a smaller spot should reveal spatially varying near-EF weight. The authors should perform or propose this test.

**C. Minor**

15. **Errors and questionable choices in the theory section.**
    - "dk: S¹ → ℝ\{0}" should read ℝ²\{0}; this also appears as R\{0} in the supplement.
    - ℝ²\{0} is wrongly called a "group".
    - The trigonometric expansion yields a circular Fermi surface, which does not represent the antinodal van Hove regime of LSCO at x = 0.22. Its use for antinodal conclusions should be justified.

16. **TPP-2M input.** The 20 meV superconducting gap is used as the "band gap" input to TPP-2M. This is not the intended input; the insulating gap enters at the eV scale. The numerical effect is small, but it should be corrected.

17. **Units, typos and missing elements.**
    - **Units:**
      - V = 0.153/0.21555 "meV";
      - "kF = −0.4605 Å" should be Å⁻¹;
      - the Fig. 4b axis "r[11] (Å⁻¹)" should be Å.
    - **Typos:**
      - "2 amt" should be atm;
      - "40˘200 eV" is garbled.
    - **Notation:** doping is labelled inconsistently as "p" and "x".
    - **Figures:** integration ranges are not fully labelled in the figures.
    - **Data availability:** the section is empty and should be completed.

18. **Speculative explanation of tunnel-junction ZBCPs.** The Discussion explains tunnel-junction ZBCPs by disorder pushing the states below the top layer, citing a Bi₂Se₃ sputtering study. This is not supported by the present calculations. It should be labelled as speculation or backed by calculation.

19. **Outlook could be more concrete.** The outlook would be strengthened by specific routes to a positive observation:
    - cleaner or near-stoichiometric cuprates (e.g. detwinned YBCO, Tl2201, Hg1201);
    - optimally doped LSCO;
    - laser or low-photon-energy ARPES for better resolution;
    - photon energies with larger probing depth (low-energy laser or soft X-ray) to test the proposed sub-surface displacement of the states;
    - nano-ARPES to address the spatial-averaging argument.
