1. ### 1. The null result needs a quantitative detection-sensitivity analysis

The main experimental finding is an *absence*: no gap and no flat-band peak. To be meaningful, the authors must show that the predicted signal would have been detectable under the actual measurement conditions. The paper estimates kz broadening and shows simulated spectra in visual comparison with the data. However:

- **The kz broadening is internally inconsistent.** It is 0.4 Å⁻¹ (TPP-2M, λ = 4.74 Å) in the text but 0.2 Å⁻¹ (IMFP ~5 Å) in the Fig. 3 caption.
  - The Methods also admit that aliasing and finite-slab spectral leakage may overestimate the effective kz broadening.
  - The edge-state decay width in k⊥ (~1/ξ from the analytic model) is never compared numerically with this kz window. That comparison decides whether a surface state is resolvable in a photon-energy scan at all.
- **Requested forward model.** The authors should construct a full forward model of the clean-edge prediction and present the resulting EDCs/MDCs at nodal (79 eV) and antinodal (95 eV) kz. The model should include:
  - the exponential depth weighting;
  - the kz window (for both 0.2 and 0.4 Å⁻¹);
  - energy and momentum resolution;
  - the Fermi–Dirac cutoff at the actual temperatures;
  - beam-spot averaging.
- **Detection limit.** From this, they should state the minimum flat-band spectral weight (or peak amplitude relative to background) and the minimum gap that would have been detected, given the measured signal-to-noise. An explicit upper bound on any gap (e.g., Δ < X meV at 95% confidence) and on the zero-energy weight should be reported.
- **Consequence.** Without this, one cannot tell whether the absence of the dip/peak reflects physics (disorder) or simply that the signal is unresolvable. The latter would make the disorder explanation unnecessary.

2. ### 2. The missing gap is expected even in the clean case, so the two explanations are not discriminated

- The paper itself states that the d-wave order parameter is suppressed at a (110) surface over a length comparable to the coherence length ("a few nanometers"). This is larger than the ARPES probing depth, so "one in general does not expect the superconducting gap to be observable at a (110) surface."
- The Supp. App. J / Fig. 13 calculation also shows that surface gap suppression has only a minimal effect on the surface-state spectral function. This is shown for the clean case only.
- Consequently, the absence of the ~20 meV gap carries little information. The abstract and intro should not present it as a co-equal finding alongside the flat-band null result.
- Moreover, the paper leaves two explanations for the missing gap open: surface pair-breaking versus broadening of the surface state. It provides no observable that discriminates between them.
- The authors should:
  - (i) reframe the gap null result as expected or consistent;
  - (ii) repeat the self-consistent versus non-self-consistent comparison (Fig. 13) *with* disorder present, both geometric and Anderson;
  - (iii) propose and evaluate a discriminating observable. Candidates are the k∥ window of E_F weight enhancement between projected nodes, the temperature dependence of E_F weight across Tc, and any residual gap outside the projected-node window.

3. ### 3. The claim "disorder is the key factor" rests on one value of σ, with no sweep, no ensemble statistics and no quantitative fit

The Anderson-disorder conclusion is supported by:
- a single σ = 100 meV;
- an unstated number of disorder realizations ("different disorder realizations" in Fig. 4e appears to refer to different disorder *types*: C, GE_AFM, GE0.5, GE_AFM+BA);
- "visual agreement" with the data.

This is insufficient to establish a causal "key factor". The authors should:

- **Sweep σ.** Cover roughly 0–150 meV in 10–20 meV steps (finer near any threshold), with ≥20–50 realizations per value. Report the ensemble mean and spread.
- **Define quantitative metrics:**
  - for the flat band, the edge LDOS integrated within ±2 meV of E_F over the projected-node k∥ window, normalized to the clean case, plus the zero-energy peak width;
  - for the bulk, the self-consistent Δ in the slab centre.
  - From these, identify the threshold σ_c at which the flat band broadens beyond the ~4 meV resolution.
- **Compare with the data statistically.** Pass each σ through the forward model of Comment 1. Compute χ² or a likelihood against the measured EDCs/MDCs. Report the allowed σ range and whether the clean case is statistically excluded.
- **If the clean and disordered predictions become indistinguishable after kz broadening,** the conclusion must be downgraded from "the key factor" to "consistent with".

4. ### 4. The bulk-gap/disorder coexistence argument is circular and not physically constrained

- The claim that σ = 100 meV disorder lifts the flat bands yet is "not inconsistent" with a bulk gap rests solely on Fig. 12, a single clean versus GE+BA DOS comparison.
- However, the Methods state that the pairing coupling was re-tuned when disorder was added (V: 0.153 → 0.21555, and β: 0.8 → 0.7) to keep the antinodal gap at ~24 meV. The bulk gap's survival is therefore imposed by construction, not demonstrated.
- In reality, a disorder strength of σ ≈ 4–5Δ throughout the bulk would be expected to cause substantial d-wave pair breaking (Abrikosov–Gor'kov) and suppress Tc.
- The authors should:
  - (i) show results at *fixed* V as σ increases, to reveal whether a window σ_c < σ < σ_bulk-kill exists;
  - (ii) convert σ to a Born scattering rate and mean free path ℓ, and compare ℓ with ξ and with experimental bulk constraints (residual resistivity of overdoped LSCO, nodal MDC widths from their own (001) data);
  - (iii) simulate disorder confined to the outermost N rows (N = 1 … ξ/a). This is physically more plausible for PFIB/cleave damage and avoids the bulk-Tc problem.
  - (iv) place σ within the two surface-disorder regimes of Kalenkov et al. (ref. [52]).
- **Wording inconsistency.** The intro describes the disorder as having "a magnitude comparable to the superconducting gap", but σ = 100 meV is 4–5 times the 20–24 meV gap. This statement should be corrected, and σ also expressed in units of t.

5. ### 5. The geometric-disorder conclusions are overstated and internally contradictory

- **Overstated.** The paper claims an "upper bound on the disorder autocorrelation length" and that "for all disorder resolvable by our AFM, the surface states remain intact". These rest on one AFM-derived edge profile and one probabilistic roughness model (GE0.5, which is not clearly defined in the main text).
  - AFM lateral resolution is a few nm.
  - Atomic-scale faceting (sub-nm), which is known to strongly suppress zero-energy states (Matsumoto–Shiba; Fogelström et al.), is therefore unconstrained by the measurement.
- **Requested phase diagram.** The authors should map the flat-band metric over roughness amplitude and lateral correlation length, with ensemble averaging. They should mark the AFM-accessible region versus the region below AFM resolution.
  - If the damaging regime lies below AFM resolution, the conclusion should be "unconstrained" rather than "remain intact".
- **Contradictory.** The intro states that the results "reveal the importance of both geometric and Anderson-type disorder". The results section concludes that geometric roughness is "too weak to eliminate these modes". The authors must reconcile these statements and support one position.

6. ### 6. Alternative mechanisms for the absent flat band are not considered

Several well-established mechanisms could remove or obscure the zero-energy peak, independently of Anderson disorder. The paper should either exclude each or list it as an open possibility:

- **(a) Spontaneous time-reversal-symmetry-breaking surface order (d+is, d+id_xy).** This splits the zero-energy peak (Fogelström–Rainer–Sauls; Covington et al.).
  - The authors could add a subdominant channel to the BdG model and check whether a splitting below 4 meV would fill in like the data.
- **(b) Surface currents / Doppler shift.**
- **(c) A non-superconducting or over-doped surface layer** from cleave/PFIB damage. This predicts the absence of both gap and flat band.
  - The authors could simulate Δ = 0 in the outermost n rows.
  - Note that the surface-doping uncertainty (Comment 7) does not exclude p ≈ 0.27–0.30.
- **(d) Misorientation or faceting with (100) admixture.** The (100) orientation has no zero-energy states.
- **(e) Matrix-element suppression of the antinodal surface weight** at 95 eV for the chosen polarization.
  - The authors could compare LH/LV polarization and additional photon energies near the antinodal kz.
- **(f) Chiral symmetry and disorder (App. C).** The scattering argument treats only chiral-preserving disorder.
  - The authors should explain which protection is actually lost when onsite disorder breaks translation along the edge, since the flat band is protected by a k∥-resolved winding number.
  - For completeness, they should also test a chiral-breaking channel.

7. ### 7. The evidence for a "pristine", bulk-like, unreconstructed surface is insufficient

- **Surface doping.** The value p ≈ 0.2271 is quoted to four digits. Yet it comes from fits over a 0.2–0.3 range, described as the "worst fits", with no error bars.
  - The authors should propagate uncertainties from the inner potential V₀, the kz broadening (0.2 vs 0.4 Å⁻¹), the k_F extraction and the fit range, and report p ± δp.
- **Reconstruction.** A bulk-like doping value does not demonstrate absence of reconstruction. The authors should report a search for folded or replica bands, or LEED.
- **Surface chemistry.** No core-level/XPS data are shown to check for implanted or redeposited ions from PFIB processing. Xe 4d/3d should be checked if a Xe plasma FIB was used.
  - The ion species, beam current and final polish conditions should be stated.
- **Possible O₂ anneal.** The process schematic (Fig. 2a) indicates a 600 °C / 1 h / 1 bar O₂ anneal. Its order relative to FIB and its possible effect on near-surface oxygen content should be discussed.
- **Orientation and homogeneity.** The beam spot (40–50 µm) covers a substantial fraction of the ~150×150 µm² face. Spatial maps across the face are needed, including:
  - spectra at several positions;
  - verification of the (110) orientation (e.g., from Fermi-surface symmetry or Laue after cleaving).
- **Timing of AFM.** The AFM timing and location relative to ARPES (ex situ? after measurement?) and the scan size should be stated.
- **Wording.** Without such evidence, "pristine" should be softened.

8. ### 8. The bulk superconductivity, Tc, temperature analysis and reference sample are not adequately controlled

- **SQUID comparison.** The claim that FIB processing "does not affect bulk superconductivity" rests on qualitative SQUID data from the rod versus the milled slab, measured at different fields (5 vs 2 Oe).
  - Matched-field measurements are needed, together with the onset Tc and transition width for both.
- **Tc is never stated numerically.** For x ≈ 0.22 one expects Tc ≈ 25–28 K. That would mean 35 K is in the normal state and 21 K is near Tc. This must be stated explicitly.
- **Most sensitive gap test.** Given Tc, the most sensitive test is a direct comparison at identical k of Fermi–Dirac-divided or symmetrized EDCs at 6 K versus 35 K, including the difference spectrum. The authors should report an upper bound, with uncertainty, on any temperature-dependent change in weight within ±10 meV of E_F.
- **Simulation temperature.** The simulations are at 0 K (the temperature is not stated in the Methods). At least one finite-temperature calculation should be added for comparison with the 6–35 K data.
- **(001) reference sample.**
  - It has a different doping (x = 0.19, not 0.22) and a different photon energy and resolution (65 eV, 4–5 meV).
  - Its gap is quoted as ~7 meV from symmetrized EDCs, with no uncertainty. The main text elsewhere cites 10–20 meV, and the literature value used for the expectation is ~20 meV. The simulations use 20 meV (TPP estimate) and 24 meV (BdG tuning).
  - These values must be reconciled.
  - A same-doping (x = 0.22) (001) positive control, measured with the same setup and resolution, is needed to establish that a gap is detectable in this experimental configuration. With 4–5 meV resolution, a 7 meV gap is marginal, so the extraction method and confidence should be described.
  - The authors should show that their conclusions are insensitive to Δ across the 7–24 meV range.

9. ### 9. Reproducibility across samples and cleaves is unclear

- Only one additional sample is shown (Fig. 11, App. H). Its caption gives no temperature, beamline, doping or orientation, and no statement of whether it reproduces the null result.
- The text admits that the temperature dependence "was not reproduced in another sample", but does not say what differed.
- The authors should report:
  - the number of crystals, cleaves and spots measured;
  - which of them showed the null result;
  - which data underlie each claim.
- If essentially one cleave supports the main conclusions, the claims should be qualified accordingly.
- The Data Availability section is empty, and any additional datasets (e.g., from other beamlines) should be documented or explicitly excluded.

10. ### 10. The result must be reconciled with tunneling experiments

- Zero-bias conductance peaks are robustly observed on (110) cuprate junctions and surfaces that are far from atomically clean. If σ ≈ 100 meV surface disorder destroys the flat band in ARPES, the authors must explain why tunneling still sees it. Possible reasons include different surface quality, local versus spatially/kz-averaged probing, or domain dilution.
- The domain-size argument is also misstated. ~16 nm ZBCP domains against a ~50 µm spot is a linear factor of ~3×10³ (and ~10⁷ in area), not "two orders of magnitude".
- The authors could use literature ZBCP widths as an independent constraint on edge broadening and compare them with the broadening implied by σ = 100 meV.

11. ### 11. Model realism at x ≈ 0.22

- The numerical slab includes hopping up to third neighbour. However, the analytic edge-state model (Supp. VI.B), used to discuss decay lengths and the projected-node window, is nearest-neighbour only and uses a small-k expansion.
- This is not controlled near the antinodal van Hove point, which lies close to the LSCO Lifshitz transition at x ≈ 0.22.
- The decay length and flat-band k∥ extent should be recomputed with the measured-Fermi-surface tight-binding model.
- Finite-size convergence of the 280×200 slab should also be shown, in particular edge length relative to ξ and to the roughness correlation lengths.

12. ### 12. The novelty and scope claims are overstated

- Ref. [53] (Hunter et al., RSI 2024) already demonstrated FIB-enforced cleaving perpendicular to the layers with ARPES on Sr₂RuO₄, which is itself a quasi-2D layered system.
- The claims of "applicability of the technique to quasi-2D systems" and "lays essential groundwork" should therefore be qualified. The novelty should be stated precisely as the first cuprate and the first side surface in the superconducting state.

13. The coupling is quoted as V = 0.153 (and 0.21555) "meV". The unit is implausible; it is presumably eV or units of t.

14. Define GE_AFM, GE0.5 and BA in the main text, not only in the Fig. 4b caption. Describe Fig. 4e as showing disorder *types* if that is what is meant.

15. State the number of disorder realizations and the averaging protocol for every disorder figure.

16. State the photon polarization for each dataset, the Fermi-edge reference and the energy calibration.

17. Give the V₀ used for the kz conversion.

18. **1. The "first" claims need scoping, and the significance should rest on the technique.**
- The FIB notch-cleave method was published earlier, in Hunter et al. [53] (Rev. Sci. Instrum. 95, 2024). The present contributions are:
  - applying it to a high-Tc cuprate;
  - working on a side surface;
  - measuring in the superconducting state.
- These should be stated precisely.
- The physics outcome is a non-observation. Its significance therefore depends on demonstrating that the clean-case signal would have been detectable (see B1). Without that, the main scientific claim is not established.

19. **2. The headline disorder result is largely generic, and the paper does not say so.**
- The winding invariant is defined only at fixed k∥. The zero modes at +k∥ and −k∥ have opposite chirality (Supp. Eqs. 23/29), so the net chirality of the d-wave (110) edge is zero.
- The authors' own App. C states that their onsite disorder (σz in the Nambu basis, σx in the chiral basis) scatters only between states of opposite chirality.
- Pairwise annihilation of the flat band is therefore expected for essentially any short-range disorder that mixes k∥. It is a known consequence of the topology (cf. Sato et al. [19]), not a new finding.
- The paper should state this explicitly and temper the framing of "topological flat bands" in light of it.
- It should also explain what the simulations add beyond this expectation, for example a quantitative threshold or bandwidth.

20. **3. The relevant theory is under-discussed.**
- Kalenkov, Fogelström & Barash [52], "Two regimes for effects of surface disorder on the ZBCP…", directly addresses this question. It is cited only in the Intro, as one of several proposed reconciliations of tunneling data, and is never compared with the present results.
- The authors should state which of the two regimes applies here.
- The broader literature on roughness and impurity broadening of d-wave zero-energy Andreev states is absent. This includes work by Svidzinsky, Poenicke et al., and Tanaka/Kashiwaya/Asano.
- The cited analogies to topological-insulator and class DIII surfaces under strong disorder ([84], [85]) concern systems with a nonzero global invariant, where surface states survive. They do not transfer to a zero-net-winding d-wave edge.

21. **4. The comparison with tunneling literature crosses materials.**
- All cited zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP) and STM evidence comes from YBCO, Bi2212 and Tl2212. None comes from LSCO.
- The "reconciliation" with STM and planar junctions therefore compares different materials, and the mechanisms invoked (subsurface states displaced by dilute disorder [76], domain interfaces [50]) are speculative.
- Please acknowledge this, cite any LSCO (110) tunneling or STM work, and state that no STM was performed on these samples.

22. **5. The stated motivation for ARPES is never exercised.**
- The Intro argues that ARPES is needed because topological states have "a characteristic momentum-space distribution not expected for disorder-induced in-gap states."
- The paper never uses this discriminating power, since no in-gap weight is observed.
- The authors also acknowledge that the coherence length ξ (a few nm) greatly exceeds the probing depth λ ≈ 4.7 Å. The probed fraction of edge-state weight therefore scales roughly as λ/ξ.
- This raises a general question: is surface-sensitive ARPES intrinsically ill-suited to small-gap nodal superconductors? It deserves explicit discussion, since it bears on the claim that this work lays "groundwork" for ARPES on topological superconductors.

23. **6. The forward-looking recommendation is vague.**
- "Low-disorder, near-stoichiometric materials" are recommended, but no candidate is named.
- Feasibility of the FIB-cleave method for such materials is not discussed. This would strengthen the paper's significance.

24. **1. Detectability of the clean-case signal is not established. This is the central issue.** The predicted clean "dip and peak" is computed under several favourable conditions:
- a 24 meV gap, whereas the paper's own (001) reference shows ~7 meV;
- 0 K, with no Fermi–Dirac cutoff, while the data are taken at 6–35 K;
- a 3 meV Lorentzian lifetime broadening in place of a Gaussian convolution for the 3–4 meV instrumental resolution;
- no background processing matching the data. The Fig. 4 caption applies background subtraction "for the ARPES data" only, following [77], with no stated model or window. [77], Matt et al., is primarily an orbital-hybridisation paper, and it may describe a soft-X-ray procedure.

In addition, no contrast-versus-noise estimate, error bars, or raw unsubtracted energy distribution curves (EDCs) are shown.

*Request:* a forward-modelled clean spectrum overlaid on both raw and processed data with error bars. It should use a realistic gap (5–10 meV), the Fermi–Dirac factor at the measured temperature, Gaussian resolution, and identical background treatment. The authors should also give quantitative upper bounds on the flat-band spectral weight and on the leading-edge gap at the surface. If the clean signal would not have been resolvable, the disorder explanation is unnecessary.

25. **2. The disorder strength σ = 100 meV is chosen post hoc and not validated.**
- Only one value is simulated: there is no σ scan, no threshold, and no stated number of disorder realizations or ensemble averaging.
- The Abstract and Intro call the disorder "moderate" and "comparable to the superconducting gap." Yet σ is about 4× the model gap and over 10× the measured gap.
- The supporting citations do not match the disorder class:
  - [73] and [74] concern weak, out-of-plane, forward-scattering dopant potentials. These are least effective at the large momentum transfer needed to couple ±k∥.
  - [75] concerns pairing disorder.

26. **3. Retuning the pairing V masks a consistency check.**
- To keep the bulk gap at ~24 meV under disorder, V was raised from 0.153 to 0.21555. This is quoted in "meV", which is likely a units error.
- The preserved bulk gap is therefore imposed by construction.
- A simple Born estimate (a reviewer estimate, not from the paper) suggests σ = 100 meV implies a normal-state scattering rate of order tens of meV, well above k_B·Tc. This is potentially incompatible with Tc ≈ 25 K in a d-wave superconductor, where impurities are pair-breaking.
- *Request:* compute the implied scattering rate and mean free path and compare them with:
  - LSCO residual resistivity;
  - measured nodal momentum distribution curve (MDC) widths and EDC linewidths on both the (001) and (110) surfaces;
  - the Tc-suppression results the authors themselves cite ([57]–[59]).

27. **4. Only one disorder class is tested.**
- Hopping, dilute, spatially correlated, long-range and edge-only disorder are listed in the Discussion as "one could consider…" but were not simulated.
- Since the conclusion is "bulk disorder is the key factor," at least a minimal survey of disorder classes and strengths is needed.
- The meaning of the "upper bound on the disorder autocorrelation length" should be clarified and quantified; at present it carries no number.

28. **5. Competing explanations are not excluded.**
- **Symmetry-broken edge phases.** The Intro presents time-reversal-symmetry-breaking s+id or d+ip states, phase crystals and edge magnetism as *expected* consequences of the flat-band density of states. A few-meV splitting could be hidden by the resolution, yet the paper never tests or excludes this before invoking disorder.
- **Unreconciled reference [26].** Chakraborty et al. [26], the paper from which the authors' own self-consistent BdG scheme is taken, reports a *disorder-robust* phase crystal. This should be reconciled with the disorder explanation.
- **Over-overdoped, non-superconducting surface layer.** The surface doping estimate spans p ≈ 0.2–0.3; 0.3 is at or beyond the end of the superconducting dome.
  - The tight-binding μ is fixed by a nodal k_F, which the authors admit is weakly doping-sensitive.
  - The van Hove position is unresolved.
  - Polar-surface self-doping [63] is cited but not discussed for (110).
  - Bulk SQUID cannot rule out a non-superconducting surface layer.
- **Micro-faceting or miscut.** AFM (lateral resolution of a few nm) cannot exclude atomic-scale (100)/(010) faceting. Such facets carry no flat band and would dilute the signal. No angular tolerance of the cleaved normal is given.
- **Preparation and measurement conditions.**
  - FIB milling occurs after the final 600 °C anneal, so milled regions are never re-annealed.
  - The 40–50 µm beam spot on a ~150 µm face makes spot placement relative to the milled edges relevant, but it is not stated.
  - Surface aging and oxygen loss over the measurement time are not reported.
  - Cleave-induced strain is not discussed.

29. **6. The data–model comparison is qualitative and the hypothesis is not falsified.**
- Agreement is described only as "visual," using hand-drawn sector integration windows (Fig. 10) with no sensitivity analysis.
- The disorder model makes a testable prediction: weight transfers into the trivial sector. The authors set this aside as "experimentally challenging." As presented, the disorder hypothesis is effectively unfalsifiable.
- A (110) vs (001) linewidth comparison would directly test whether the effective disorder is bulk or surface-specific, but it is absent.

30. **7. The "suppressed gap" finding is not discriminating.**
- The absence of a gap is presented as a finding, yet two facts make it uninformative:
  - the authors themselves expect the order parameter to be suppressed over ξ ≫ λ;
  - a gap of a few meV would be at or below the resolution.
- The title's "Suppression of Spectral Gap and Flat Bands" and the Intro's "collapse" therefore overstate a non-observation. A title reflecting non-observation would be more accurate.
- The temperature-independence argument (6, 21 and 35 K (110) EDCs compared with gold) is given without quantitative differences or error bars.
- A temperature-dependent feature seen on one sample did not reproduce on a second (Fig. 11). Attributing this to "minor thermal drift or variations in surface conditions" needs substantiation.

31. **1. The (001) reference gap control is weak.**
- It uses a different doping (x = 0.19 vs 0.22) and a different face.
- It is unclear whether this sample was FIB-shaped and notch-cleaved. The main text says "comparably prepared," the supplement "prepared under the same conditions," and Methods describe only one rod and one growth.
- The ~7 meV gap is quoted without a temperature. Elsewhere the paper quotes 10–20, 15–25, ~20 and 24 meV.
- App. E argues consistency with literature but admits strong sensitivity to small E_F shifts.
- Please clarify the preparation, harmonize the quoted gap values, and justify using 24 meV in the model.

32. **2. The depth weighting and λ need care.**
- The TPP-2M estimate uses the 20 meV superconducting gap as the "band gap" input, which is meaningless for a metal; it should be zero.
- λ is quoted to five significant figures from an empirical formula.
- A single λ is used across photon energies from 40 to 200 eV, and the kinetic energy assumed is not stated.
- It is ambiguous whether exp(−x/λ) weights the amplitude or the intensity.
- The authors acknowledge aliasing and spectral leakage that overestimate the k_z broadening, which makes the predicted peak height uncertain.
- There is no sensitivity analysis on λ.
- The k_z width is given as 0.4 Å⁻¹ in the text and 0.2 Å⁻¹ in the Fig. 3 caption, likely the full width (2/λ) versus 1/λ. The convention should be defined.

33. **3. Model simplifications should be justified.**
- The model is single-orbital mean-field, and the authors themselves cite [77] on d_z² hybridisation in LSCO.
- Two different tight-binding parameter sets are used ([62] for the 3D Fermi surface, [83] for the slab) without a consistency check.
- The cited coherence-length references [67]–[69] are not specific to overdoped LSCO.
- Fig. 13, the self-consistent gap-suppression test, omits its gap, V, temperature and resolution parameters, and covers only the clean case.

34. **1. Surface characterization is incomplete.** The paper lacks:
- LEED, RHEED or other diffraction of the cleaved (110) surface;
- a post-cleave orientation check or miscut estimate;
- the structural phase (LTO or HTT) at 6 K, which matters because orthorhombic and tetragonal (110) refer to different bond directions;
- the photon polarization and geometry for the (110) data, and any check with both polarizations;
- characterization of ion implantation (the PFIB ion species is not stated in Methods) or of oxygen loss;
- a temperature calibration on the pillar itself; the mounting of the gold reference relative to the pillar is not stated.

35. **2. Reproducibility is limited.**
- The Data Availability section is empty, and no code is provided.
- The numbers of samples, cleaves and the cleave success rate are not stated.
- The number of disorder realizations is not stated.
- Convergence criteria for the self-consistency solver are not given.
- The edge-state selection criteria (eigenvalue and participation-ratio cutoffs) are not given.
- Growth conditions for the x = 0.19 crystal are missing.
- The Acknowledgments cite a SOLARIS URANOS beamtime that is not mentioned in Methods; please explain whether those data are used.

36. Fig. 1 swaps the k⊥ and k∥ axes between panels c and d/e, and the "[11] edge" notation is ambiguous.

37. The label "chiral-symmetric mass term"/"Anderson disorder" describes what is ordinary non-magnetic onsite potential disorder (τz). Stating this plainly would aid readers.

38. GE0.5 ("probabilistic roughness") is never defined.

39. The pairing V is given in "meV."

40. Surface doping is written interchangeably as p and x (Sec. II C), with "only occasionally" in the main text versus "worst fits" in the supplement for the p ≈ 0.3 values.

41. Methods should explicitly describe the notch, the cleave procedure, and the order of all preparation steps (Au coating and anneal, mounting, milling).

42. Generalizations to "quasi-2D systems" in the Discussion rest on simulation alone and should be qualified.

43. **Map the photon energies to momentum explicitly.** On the (110) face, node vs antinode is chosen by photon energy, i.e. by k[110] along the surface normal. However, the inner potential V₀ and the work function are never given. The k[110] and k[001] values of the 79 eV ("nodal") and 95 eV ("antinodal") cuts are also never stated. The central claim ("by selecting appropriate photon energies, we can resolve … nodal and antinodal regions") therefore cannot be checked. Please provide:
   - V₀ and the work function;
   - the hν→k⊥ conversion;
   - the k⊥ and k[001] value of each cut;
   - a schematic of the measurement plane in the CuO₂ Brillouin zone (beyond Fig. 3a).

44. **Show that the cuts cross the edge-state weight.** The supplement places the edge-state weight at k⊥ = I(κ) (Eq. 30). This is k⊥ = 0 for k∥* ≤ |k∥| ≤ kF, and it shifts elsewhere for |k∥| < k∥*. In the small-gap limit it moves to the normal-state Fermi surface k⊥^F(k∥) (Eqs. 31–33). The paper never shows that the 95 eV cut passes through this k⊥. Nor does it state which k⊥ is used for the simulated EDCs (the "red line" in Fig. 4a); data and simulation are matched only by both being labelled "antinodal".
   - Please give numerical k⊥ values for both.
   - Please discuss, as an alternative explanation for the missing zero-energy peak, that the cut may miss the edge-state weight.

45. **kz broadening: resolve the inconsistency and discuss its consequences.**
   - The value is Δk ≈ 0.4 Å⁻¹ in the text and Methods (FWHM = 2/λ) but 0.2 Å⁻¹ in the Fig. 3 caption (presumably the HWHM); please state FWHM or HWHM consistently.
   - A window of ~0.4 Å⁻¹ is comparable to the Γ–node distance (~0.59 Å⁻¹). The cuts may therefore integrate over gapped and nodal regions, which directly affects the "no gap" conclusion; this should be discussed.
