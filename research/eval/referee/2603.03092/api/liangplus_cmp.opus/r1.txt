Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors use FIB-notch-controlled cleaving to expose (110) side surfaces of overdoped LSCO (x = 0.22). They report what they describe as the first ARPES measurement of a cuprate side surface in the superconducting state.

- They find no resolvable spectral gap and no zero-energy flat-band peak.
- Self-consistent BdG slab calculations are used to argue that the AFM-measured roughness cannot remove the flat band, but that ~100 meV Gaussian on-site disorder can.

The technique is a clear advance: side surfaces of layered superconductors have been inaccessible to ARPES. However, the physics conclusion ("disorder as the key factor") currently rests on a null result whose interpretation has several open alternatives.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Method.** This is the first application of FIB-defined cleaving to a cuprate. It yields surfaces of apparently good quality: sharp nodal dispersions (Fig. 3d), a recognisable (001)-plane Fermi surface from photon-energy maps (Fig. 3b), and a reported 1.8 Å RMS roughness (Fig. 2e).
- **Bulk superconductivity.** Susceptibility before and after milling (Fig. 2b) shows that the preparation does not degrade bulk superconductivity.
- **Theory.** The slab calculations are momentum-resolved, self-consistent, include ARPES depth weighting and kz broadening, and are directly compared with sector-resolved EDCs. This is a useful framework for future side-surface ARPES.
- **Value of the null result.** The measurement constrains interpretations of ZBCP experiments and motivates cleaner materials.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **No evidence that the probed surface layer is superconducting; the "dead/normal surface layer" alternative is not excluded.**
   - The main observables are all null: no gap, no flat-band peak, and no temperature dependence between 6, 21 and 35 K (Fig. 4g; Fig. 11, where differences are attributed to "extrinsic effects").
   - All of these are also what one expects if the top few Å (ARPES probing depth ~5 Å) is non-superconducting. Plausible causes include:
     - oxygen out-diffusion from an ab-plane-exposing surface in UHV;
     - broken Cu–O bonds at the (110) termination and the resulting charge redistribution;
     - cleave-induced strain;
     - FIB-related damage or implantation near the notch.
   - The surface-doping estimate (Supp. F) cannot exclude this. It spans x ≈ 0.2–0.3, the fit is acknowledged to be imperfect, and a normal metallic dead layer would show essentially the same Fermi surface.
   - Bulk susceptibility (Fig. 2b) does not probe the surface.
   - Without positive evidence of surface superconductivity, the absence of flat bands cannot be attributed to disorder broadening of a topological state.
   - Requested:
     - (i) A quantitative leading-edge-midpoint (LEM) analysis versus gold, with error bars, at node and antinode on the (110) surface, below and above Tc. Even a 1–2 meV antinodal LEM shift, or a temperature-dependent sharpening of the nodal quasiparticle peak, would establish a superconducting surface.
     - (ii) Core-level spectra (O 1s, Cu 2p, La 4d/Sr 3d, and Ga or Xe depending on the FIB source) from the cleaved (110) surface, compared with a (001) cleave, to check stoichiometry and contamination.
     - (iii) A statement of the cleave temperature, the time between cleave and measurement, and a time series checking for surface ageing.
     - (iv) If feasible, any structural probe (LEED/RHEED) of the termination.

2. **The superconducting gap used in the argument and the model is inconsistent with the authors' own reference measurement.**
   - The main text gives the expected gap in three ways:
     - "approximately 20 meV" (p. 9);
     - "15–25 meV" (p. 3);
     - "10–20 meV … consistent with our (001)-surface reference measurement" (p. 9).
   - Supp. E reports ~7 meV from symmetrized EDCs on a (001) x = 0.19 sample, with 4–5 meV resolution. At x = 0.22 one would expect a smaller value still.
   - Yet the BdG pairing V is tuned to give 24 meV at the antinode (Methods F).
   - This affects both headline claims:
     - (a) **"Gap suppression."** With a realistic gap of ≲5–7 meV, 3–4 meV resolution and ~0.2–0.4 Å⁻¹ kz broadening that mixes momenta along the FS, the absence of a resolvable gap on the (110) surface may simply be a resolution effect. It need not be pair breaking.
     - (b) **Flat-band detectability.** The coherence length scales as v_F/Δ. A 3–4× smaller gap pushes the edge-state weight deeper into the bulk, beyond the ~5 Å probing depth, and narrows the dip/peak features of Fig. 4e. In the Δ→0 limit, Supp. B shows the edge weight evolves into the normal-state Fermi-surface weight.
   - The clean-case simulation may therefore overstate how visible the flat band would be.
   - Requested:
     - Measure the (001) gap on the same x = 0.22 crystal, with the same preparation, beamline and resolution as the (110) data.
     - Redo the clean and disordered simulations with Δ matched to that value.
     - Include the 6 K Fermi function and the actual instrumental resolution.
     - Show whether the clean flat-band peak would still be detectable.
     - Correct the text claiming consistency of the reference with 10–20 meV.

3. **The comparison between data and simulation is qualitative; no detection limit is given.**
   - The data/simulation comparison (Fig. 4e–g) rests on visual agreement.
   - The data are background-subtracted following Ref. [77], normalized to total intensity, and averaged within integration windows (Fig. 10). Each of these steps can suppress or create weak features near E_F.
   - A zero-energy peak is also truncated by the Fermi function. At 3–4 meV resolution it would appear mainly as an enhanced, slightly shifted step, not as a clear peak.
   - Requested:
     - Show raw, unsubtracted EDCs for each sector, with statistical error bands.
     - Fit them with a model of the form [background + Lorentzian at E_F] × Fermi–Dirac ⊗ Gaussian resolution.
     - Quote an upper limit on the flat-band spectral weight relative to the clean-model prediction. This should account for the fraction of the ~50 µm spot that may host intact edge segments.
     - Note that Fig. 4g ("Band" panel) and Fig. 11 show a small hump just below E_F at 6 K. Its significance should be assessed quantitatively rather than dismissed.
   - Without such a bound, "suppressed" cannot be distinguished from "present but below sensitivity."

4. **The disorder model that carries the main conclusion is not justified against LSCO, and only one disorder type is tested.**
   - Uncorrelated Gaussian on-site disorder with σ = 100 meV on every site is strong short-range disorder.
   - It is pair-breaking for a d-wave state: V had to be raised by ~40% (0.153 → 0.2156) to keep the gap. In a real material, a scattering rate of this size would also suppress Tc.
   - The paper itself argues that destroying the flat band requires large-momentum-transfer, inter-chirality scattering. However, the dominant disorder in LSCO, from out-of-plane Sr dopants, is generally considered smooth and forward-scattering (cf. Refs. [73,74]), which by the same argument should be relatively ineffective.
   - The conclusion that disorder is "the key factor" therefore depends on choosing the most harmful disorder type.
   - Requested:
     - (i) A disorder-strength scan, showing peak height or width in the topological sector versus σ, with ensemble averages over realizations. It is currently unclear whether Fig. 4d–e is a single realization.
     - (ii) A comparison of the implied normal-state scattering rate with independent constraints: the nodal MDC widths in the authors' own 79 eV data (Fig. 3d) versus simulated widths, and the residual resistivity or mean free path of x ≈ 0.22 LSCO.
     - (iii) At least one alternative, more realistic model: dilute strong scatterers, or spatially correlated or smooth potentials.
     - (iv) Whether the same disorder is compatible with the bulk Tc and superfluid density.
   - If realistic disorder does not remove the flat band, the conclusion must be revised.

**B. Should address**

5. **Surface topography characterization does not match the ARPES footprint, and the roughness simulation is partly circular.**
   - AFM is shown over ~1 µm² (Fig. 2d–e), while the beam spot is 40–50 µm FWHM. The SEM image (Fig. 2c) shows structure on the 10–100 µm scale.
   - Please clarify:
     - whether AFM was performed on the same cleave that was measured by ARPES;
     - whether it was done ex situ, and after how long in air.
   - Please provide:
     - large-area scans covering a spot-sized region;
     - the distribution of local surface normals, to assess faceting or miscut toward (100)-type facets, which would host no flat band;
     - an estimate of the miscut from the photon-energy map.
   - The roughness simulation:
     - Resampling a nm-resolution AFM profile onto an a/√2 lattice necessarily produces long clean segments. The conclusion that "AFM-resolvable roughness is benign" therefore follows largely from the AFM resolution.
     - The phrase "upper bound on the disorder autocorrelation length" should be stated more precisely.
     - Describe how the 2D AFM map is reduced to the 1D edge of a 200-site periodic slab. That edge spans only ~50–75 nm, so long-wavelength structure is not represented.
     - Define "GE0.5"/"Edge 0.5"; it appears only in the Fig. 4 caption.

6. **Alternative physical explanations for the missing zero-energy peak are not discussed.**
   - The self-consistent model allows only the d-wave channel. Yet the introduction cites predictions of surface states with:
     - d+is or d+ip character;
     - phase crystals, which Ref. [26] finds robust to disorder;
     - edge magnetism.
   - These shift or split the flat band by a few meV and redistribute weight. With 3–4 meV resolution and the Fermi cutoff, this could reduce the visible peak.
   - LSCO at x = 0.22 lies close to the LTO/HTT boundary. Orthorhombic twin domains, whose axes lie along tetragonal [110], could also introduce interfaces of the type discussed in Ref. [50].
   - Matrix-element effects on the side surface should be considered: polarization and geometry are not stated for the (110) measurements.
   - Please discuss these possibilities and, where possible, test them in the model (e.g., by allowing a subdominant s or p channel) or exclude them with the data.

7. **Reproducibility and sample statistics are unclear.**
   - Please state:
     - how many pillars were cleaved and how many gave usable (110) surfaces;
     - whether the key null result (no gap, no peak) appears on every surface.
   - The second sample (Fig. 11) shows a temperature dependence that differs from Fig. 4g. It extends to 55 K, whereas Methods lists 6–35 K.
   - Show the thermal-cycling history and sample-to-sample spread.
   - Quote Tc and the shielding fraction from Fig. 2b explicitly.

8. **The photon-energy-to-kz conversion and kz broadening need to be stated and made consistent.**
   - The inner potential and the assignment of 79 eV to the node and 95 eV to the antinode are not given.
   - The broadening value is inconsistent across the paper:
     - Fig. 3 caption: Δk[110] = 0.2 Å⁻¹ from λ ≈ 5 Å;
     - Methods D and main text: Δkz ≈ 0.4 Å⁻¹ (FWHM = 2/λ).
   - Feeding the superconducting gap (20 meV) into TPP-2M as a "band gap" is not meaningful; for a metal Eg should be zero. The numerical effect is likely negligible, but it should be corrected.
   - The authors note that the simulation overestimates kz broadening, which they attribute to aliasing and finite-size leakage. This matters because kz mixing is part of why the bulk gap is "filled."
   - Please:
     - use a consistent value;
     - show the sensitivity of Fig. 4e to it;
     - discuss whether the strong intensity between the antinodal bands (Fig. 3e) constrains in-gap weight.

9. **The claims are stronger than the evidence, and prior theory should be engaged.**
   - The abstract states that the results "reveal disorder as the key factor." Given concerns 1–4, this should be reframed as a hypothesis consistent with the data.
   - Broadening of Andreev bound states by surface roughness and by bulk impurities has a substantial prior literature (e.g., Matsumoto–Shiba; Poenicke et al.; Tanaka/Asano/Golubov; Barash; Ref. [52]). The present results should be placed relative to it.
   - There is an internal tension that should be addressed. Bulk disorder strong enough to erase the flat band everywhere seems at odds with the ZBCPs seen in large-area tunnel junctions. The proposed resolution, displacement of the states away from the top layer (Ref. [76]), is not demonstrated in the authors' own model, where it could be checked directly via the depth profile of the edge states with disorder.

**C. Minor**

10. **Units and typos.**
    - Fig. 4b: the x-axis is labelled r[11] (Å⁻¹); it should be Å.
    - Methods C: k_F = −0.4605 "Å" should be Å⁻¹.
    - Methods F: V = 0.153 meV is implausible for producing a 24 meV gap; please check the units.
    - "2 amt" should be "2 atm."
    - "40˘200 eV" should be "40–200 eV."

11. **Supplementary A.**
    - It states d_k: S¹ → ℝ\{0} and π₁(ℝ\{0}) = ℤ. This should be ℝ²\{0}, as written in Fig. 1b/Fig. 5.
    - α in the text after Eq. 26 is undefined.
    - Eq. 11 and Fig. 1 use δ = a√2 inconsistently in formatting.

12. **Notation consistency.**
    - The Fig. 4 caption uses C/GE_AFM/GE0.5/BA, while the legend uses Clean/Edge AFM/Edge 0.5/Mixed.
    - The doping is quoted as x in some places and p in others (0.22 vs 0.2271), and "x = 0.2–0.25" is used for a surface p-estimate.

13. **Figure 4b.** Please state that ⟨|Δ|⟩ is a bond amplitude. Its ~6 meV bulk value versus the "24 meV antinodal gap" will otherwise confuse readers.

14. **Figure 4g.** Explain how the gold DOS is normalized relative to the sample EDCs, and why it sits at a different level.

15. **Supp. E.** Clarify whether the x = 0.19 (001) sample was gold-coated, annealed or FIB-prepared, i.e., what "prepared under the same conditions" means. Supp. I is cited for the "(001) surface" claim, but it shows a [11]-edge slab DOS; please clarify how it supports the statement.

16. **Data availability.** The section is empty.

17. **Discussion.** The STM argument (16 nm range vs 50 µm spot) would benefit from a suggestion of nano-ARPES as a direct test.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Establish surface superconductivity directly on the (110) surface. Use LEM shifts versus temperature, with gold referencing and the space-charge corrections already used in Supp. E, plus a matched (001) cleave of the same x = 0.22 crystal at identical resolution. This single addition would turn the null result into an interpretable one.
2. Re-run the simulations with the measured gap and then produce a quantitative sensitivity curve: expected flat-band peak weight versus disorder strength and type, compared with an experimental upper bound from fits to raw EDCs.
3. Calibrate the disorder model against the authors' own nodal linewidths and known transport scattering rates, and include a smooth, dopant-like disorder model.
4. Consider spatially resolved (nano-/µ-ARPES) measurements or a smaller spot on the best AFM-characterized regions. The authors' own picture predicts that edge states survive on isolated clean segments, so spatial resolution would test it directly.
