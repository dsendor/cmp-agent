**Review outline:**

## 1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports three findings for back-gated AlOx/KTaO3(111) 2DEGs:

- Electrostatic gate cycling at cryogenic temperature progressively raises T_c (about 1.02 → 1.09 K at V_g = 0, Supp. Fig. 1e) and R_s, then settles into a stable hysteresis loop.
- Sub-gap red illumination (630 nm) at 10 K drives R_s back to a baseline and lowers T_c.
- Both effects correlate with two thermally activated dielectric-loss processes, assigned to polar-nanoregion (PNR) reorientation (E_a ≈ 76 meV) and oxygen-vacancy (OV) ionization (E_a ≈ 236 meV).

Optical resetting of a gate-written superconducting state at an oxide interface would be new and of interest. Gate-cycling enhancement of T_c itself has been reported (Ref. 30), so the novelty rests on the optical erasure and the proposed microscopic link to lattice excitations. In its current form that link is correlational, and several simpler explanations are not excluded.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- A clear, repeatable write/erase protocol for R_s at 10 K (Fig. 2c, Supp. Fig. 4), with sequence and amplitude dependence.
- T_c tuned in both directions by two different stimuli (Figs. 1c–e, 2h–i). The states survive thermal cycling between 10 K and below T_c (Fig. 2h).
- Combined use of transport, Hall, gate current, frequency-dependent dielectric loss and dark-relaxation kinetics on the same platform.
- Practical relevance: a non-volatile, optically resettable superconducting parameter is attractive for cryogenic memory concepts.

## 3. Concerns

### A. Must address

**1. Optical heating is not excluded as the origin of "erasure."**
- The LED sits 1 cm above the sample. Fig. 2b already shows heating-induced resistance jumps at 2 K.
- No sample-temperature trace during illumination is shown.
- The photo-response is absent at 2 K but strong at 10–15 K. This is unexpected for direct photo-ionization of deep in-gap states, which should be largely temperature-independent. It is what one would expect from a thermally assisted or heating-driven process.
- Methods state that the LED I–V curve is non-monotonic in temperature. The LED is operated at fixed V_L, but the optical power at the sample is never given.
- Requested:
  - A thermometer reading at, or calibrated to, the sample stage during illumination.
  - A dark control: warm the gate-cycled sample to the maximum temperature reached under illumination, return to 10 K, and compare R_s, n, μ and T_c with the illuminated state.
  - Dependence on optical power and dose.
  - Ideally a wavelength dependence (e.g., near-IR below the relevant in-gap thresholds) to show the effect is photonic rather than thermal.

**2. "Complete erasure" (title, abstract, conclusion) is not demonstrated against the pristine state.**
- Fig. 2i shows T_c decreasing from L0 to L4. It does not compare L4 with the pre-gating transition (dashed curve in Fig. 1d, or T_c ≈ 1.017 K at the origin in Supp. Fig. 1e).
- The illuminated baseline R_s = 795 Ω at 10 K is never compared with the pristine R_s at 10 K.
- Supp. Fig. 1 shows the gate-imprinted state has higher n and lower μ than pristine. Figs. 2d–g show illumination raises both n and μ. The illuminated state is therefore not obviously the pristine one.
- Requested: overlay pristine, gate-cycled and fully illuminated T_c(R) curves and (n, μ, R_s) values from the same cooldown. Either demonstrate full recovery or soften the claim to "partial reset."

**3. The T_c memory may follow the known T_c–mobility relation of KTO(111), so the PNR-pairing interpretation may be unnecessary.**
- The Discussion calls the reduction of T_c despite higher μ "counterintuitive." In back-gated KTO(111) interfaces, however, T_c is known to anticorrelate with mobility (Ref. 17, by an overlapping author group; Ref. 18). This is usually attributed to changes in confinement or disorder.
- In this dataset, every operation that lowers μ raises T_c, and vice versa:
  - gate sweeps (negative V_g: lower μ, higher T_c; Supp. Fig. 1a, d);
  - cycling history (Supp. Fig. 1e, h);
  - illumination (Figs. 2d, i).
- The simplest reading is that T_c tracks μ (or confinement) along a single curve, with no separate PNR-mediated pairing channel.
- Requested: plot T_c against μ, n and R_s for all states (pristine, each gate cycle, each illumination step L0–L4, Type A vs B). If the points collapse onto the literature or gating curve, the claim that "PNR alignment strengthens superconductivity" is not supported independently. If they do not collapse, that would be the strongest evidence for the authors' picture.

**4. The PNR/OV mechanism is inferred only from temperature coincidences. The time scales and temperatures do not obviously match.**

*No direct measurement of the proposed states.*
- PNR alignment or randomization and OV charge state are never measured after gating or illumination.
- The dielectric data (Fig. 3b) appear to come from one sample state and probe the entire 0.5 mm substrate, not the interfacial region.
- Requested: ε and tan δ, especially Peak 1 amplitude and position, in the pristine, gate-cycled and illuminated states. Also thermally stimulated depolarization current or pyroelectric current after gate cycling versus after illumination, to show a remanent polarization is written by gating and removed by light.

*Time-scale mismatch.*
- The 35 K and 95 K transport features are equated with T_p1 and T_p2. These peak temperatures depend on frequency (Fig. 3c spans roughly 29–43 K and 87–118 K over 0.1–4000 Hz).
- The dark relaxation has τ ≈ 10³ s (Fig. 4c), i.e., rates of about 10⁻³ s⁻¹.
- Extrapolating the authors' Arrhenius fits from the 0.1 Hz peak positions to that rate gives roughly 25 K and 75–80 K, not 35 K and 95 K.
- Please state which frequency is appropriate for each comparison and show the extrapolation explicitly.

*Activation at the gating temperature.*
- The T_c memory in Fig. 1 is written at, or near, 1.4 K. At that temperature, Arrhenius processes with 76 and 236 meV barriers are completely frozen.
- The mechanism must therefore be field-driven (tunnelling or nucleation), not the thermally activated processes identified in Fig. 3.
- The Discussion should address this explicitly.

*Optical activation argument.*
- The statement that both E_a values are "much smaller than the photon energy … and therefore can be activated by red light" (p. 4) is not a valid argument. A thermal reorientation barrier lower than the photon energy does not imply an optical coupling channel exists. Please remove it or provide a mechanism.

**5. Hall-derived carrier density changes are inconsistent with electrostatics, and the extraction is fragile.**

*Magnitude and sign.*
- Supp. Fig. 1c shows n rising from about 1.04 to 1.085 × 10¹⁴ cm⁻² as V_g goes from +50 to −50 V. Negative voltage on a back gate should deplete the 2DEG, so the sign is opposite to field-effect doping.
- The magnitude (about 4–5 × 10¹² cm⁻²) also exceeds the maximum capacitive change. With ε ≈ 2000 (Fig. 3a), d = 0.5 mm and a 100 V swing, that maximum is about 2 × 10¹² cm⁻², and less if ε is suppressed by the DC field.
- If charge exchange with traps dominates, quantitative charge accounting is needed. Requested: integrate I_g over each sweep (Supp. Fig. 3), compare with the Hall Δn, and state the sweep rate.

*Two-point Hall extraction.*
- n is taken from only B = ±6 T in a van der Pauw geometry, implicitly assuming a single band.
- KTO(111) 2DEGs are multiband. Changes in the Hall coefficient could reflect redistribution of mobility between bands rather than a change in total n.
- Requested: full Hall sweeps (linearity check) in representative states, and two-band fits where warranted.

*Separate runs for each field.*
- n and μ come from three separate runs (0, +6 T, −6 T) "following exactly the same procedures."
- For a strongly history-dependent system, run-to-run reproducibility must be demonstrated, for example by overlaying R_s(t) from the three runs. It should also be shown that a 6 T field does not itself alter the trap or PNR dynamics.

*Internal inconsistency.*
- Supp. Fig. 1 and p. 3 report that gate cycling increases n.
- Figs. 2f–g and p. 4 report that gate cycles decrease n.
- The different starting states (pristine vs illuminated) may explain this, but it needs to be stated and reconciled.

**6. The dielectric measurement may be dominated by extrinsic relaxations.**

*Space-charge relaxation.*
- Thermally activated loss peaks with E_a ≈ 0.2–0.3 eV are a classic signature of Maxwell–Wagner or electrode (space-charge) relaxation in partially reduced perovskites, with OV-related conduction in a depletion layer.
- The substrate was annealed at 500 °C in UHV, which may reduce all surfaces, not only the top.
- Peak 2 could therefore be extrinsic rather than an "OV ionization" mode coupled to the 2DEG.

*Stray capacitance.*
- The sample capacitance is about 0.2 nF. Cryostat wiring capacitance is of the same order and is explicitly neglected (Methods, Eq. 1).

*Absolute ε.*
- The low-temperature ε (about 1600–2000, with a maximum near 10 K) is well below the roughly 4000–4500 commonly reported for KTO. This suggests a series dead layer or a calibration problem.

*Requested:*
- An open/short calibration of the wiring.
- A reference measurement on an unreduced KTO substrate with metal electrodes on both faces, and ideally an annealed substrate without the 2DEG.
- Excitation-amplitude dependence. 5 V is large for a field-tunable dielectric.
- Peak-position extraction with background subtraction and error bars for Fig. 3c. The 0.1 Hz trace in Fig. 3b is very noisy.

### B. Should address

**7. Figure 4 appears inconsistent with Supplementary Fig. 5 and is statistically fragile.**
- In Fig. 4a, the 35 K trace rises by ΔR/R₁ ≈ 0.14 after the LED is turned off. The drop during illumination in the same trace is only about 0.03 R₁, so R_s would end well above its pre-illumination value.
- The same holds at 95 K (about 0.06 rise vs about 0.03 drop).
- In Supp. Fig. 5a, normalized to ΔR₀, the 35 K and 95 K traces look unremarkable and recover only a fraction of ΔR₀.
- The highlighted 35 K and 95 K traces are also visibly noisier, which raises the possibility of temperature-control instability.
- Requested:
  - Clarify whether Fig. 4 and Supp. Fig. 5 are the same dataset.
  - Show the thermometer traces.
  - Repeat the measurement with finer temperature steps around 35 K and 95 K. Each maximum in Fig. 4b is a single data point.
- On the fits:
  - τ is reported at temperatures where ΔR/R₁ ≈ 0 (12–30 K, 85–90 K, >110 K), where it is essentially unconstrained.
  - τ (0.7–1.4 × 10³ s) is comparable to the observation window (about 2.2 × 10³ s).
  - Single-exponential fits are questionable for the glassy dynamics expected in KTO (Ref. 43).
  - Requested: fit uncertainties, longer windows, and a comparison with stretched-exponential or logarithmic forms.

**8. The Δ_L ∝ ε correlation (Fig. 3d) is weak evidence on its own.**
- Both quantities are monotonic in T, so an approximately linear relation over ε ≈ 300–800 is not specific.
- Requested:
  - Show that Δ_L does not correlate equally well with other T-dependent quantities (R_s, μ, 1/T).
  - Specify whether the sample was reset (e.g., by warming) between temperatures in Supp. Fig. 2, since accumulated history could create the trend.
  - Measure the sweep-rate dependence of Δ_L. A relaxation-based mechanism predicts a characteristic rate dependence near 35 K and 95 K.

**9. Sample statistics and controls are missing.**
- The number of samples and cooldowns is not stated. All data appear to come from a single device.
- Requested:
  - Reproducibility of the T_c memory and its optical erasure in at least one more sample, with device-to-device spread.
  - Reset behaviour after warming to room temperature (cf. Ref. 30).
  - A control in which the proposed ingredients are varied, for example different Al thickness or reduction conditions (OV density), or a KTO(001) interface. Such a control would show whether the 35 K and 95 K features and the optical reset scale with OV content.

**10. Gate-current magnitude and protocol inconsistencies.**
- The 10 nA I_g transients (Supp. Fig. 3) are orders of magnitude above the capacitive current expected for about 0.2 nF at typical sweep rates. This points to leakage or charge injection, possibly through reduced side faces of the unpatterned substrate. Please report the sweep rate and the DC leakage, and discuss whether leakage current contributes to the memory.
- The main text (p. 3) uses Supp. Fig. 3 to explain the pristine drift with negative-first sweeps (Fig. 1). Supp. Note 3, however, describes data taken after illumination with positive-first sweeps.
- In Supp. Fig. 4, sweep amplitude (±40 V vs ±50 V) is compared using positive-first cycles. In Fig. 2c, the Type A vs B comparison uses ±50 V.
- Please make the protocols consistent or explicitly comparable.

**11. Van der Pauw geometry and contact effects.**
- R_s is obtained from a single resistance via FEM. KTO(111) interfaces show anisotropic transport (Ref. 16).
- A change in anisotropy, for example from domain or PNR alignment, would masquerade as a change in R_s in a single configuration.
- Requested:
  - Both orthogonal vdP configurations (or reciprocity checks) in the gate-cycled and illuminated states.
  - A statement of whether the contacts and wire bonds are illuminated.
  - Exclusion of parallel photoconduction in the bulk substrate under top illumination, for example from Hall linearity or a masked-illumination test.

**12. Positioning relative to prior work and alternative trap models.**
- Hysteretic, history-dependent back-gating of oxide 2DEGs is well documented in LaAlO3/SrTiO3 and attributed to electron trapping beyond the electrostatic limit (e.g., Biscaras et al., PRL 2014, and related work). Ref. 30 reports gate-cycling T_c enhancement.
- The manuscript should discuss these explicitly. It should also identify which observation distinguishes the PNR picture from a pure interfacial-trap picture; a remanent-polarization measurement (concern 4) would do this.
- "Previously unrecognized interplay" should be justified against this literature.

**13. Illumination parameters are under-specified.**
- Optical power or flux at the sample, spot uniformity, and dose dependence are not given.
- Methods are internally inconsistent: the LED is driven by "dc forward current I_L," but the text states "we kept the same V_L."
- Since Supp. Note 4 presents illumination period and flux as tuning knobs, a quantitative dose–response curve, e.g., ΔR_s and ΔT_c versus photon dose, is needed.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Single T_c-versus-μ (and n) master plot** combining all gate-cycled, illuminated and pristine states. This one figure would show whether a mechanism beyond the established T_c–μ relation is required.

2. **State-resolved dielectric and polarization measurements** at 10 K in the pristine, gate-cycled and illuminated states:
   - ε and tan δ;
   - thermally stimulated depolarization or pyroelectric current;
   - ideally a local probe of near-interface polarization, such as second-harmonic generation.
   These would turn the PNR picture from a temperature coincidence into direct evidence.

3. **A thermal-excursion control and a wavelength/power series** for the illumination, with on-stage thermometry, to establish that the erasure is optical.

4. **Quantitative charge budget.** Integrated gate charge, Hall Δn from full field sweeps, and capacitive estimates using the measured ε, presented together and reproduced on a second sample.
