Review outline:

## 1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports three findings for AlOₓ/KTaO₃(111) 2DEGs:

- Back-gate cycling at cryogenic temperature progressively raises both T_c (≈1.02 K to ≈1.08–1.09 K at V_g = 0) and R_s, which then settle into a stable hysteresis loop.
- Red-light (1.97 eV) illumination at 10 K resets R_s to a baseline and lowers T_c stepwise.
- Two thermally activated dielectric-loss peaks (E_a ≈ 76 and 236 meV) are assigned to polar-nanoregion (PNR) reorientation and oxygen-vacancy (OV) charge-state switching. Their temperatures (~35 K, ~95 K) are argued to coincide with features in the R_s hysteresis and in the dark relaxation.

Optical reset of a gate-written superconducting state at an oxide interface is new and potentially useful. However, gate-cycling enhancement of T_c at oxide interfaces has already been reported (Ref. 30), and that overlap should be addressed explicitly. The mechanistic attribution to PNRs and OVs currently rests on temperature coincidences rather than direct measurements.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **Clear and reproducible protocols.** Repeated illumination/gate-cycle sequences (Fig. 2c, Supp. Fig. 4) show reversible, multilevel, tunable switching of R_s at 10 K. The effect depends on cycle order (Type A vs B) and amplitude (±40 vs ±50 V).
- **Optical counter-control of T_c.** Stepwise T_c reduction is shown through intermediate states L₁–L₄, with R_s stable across thermal cycles to below T_c (Fig. 2h,i).
- **Multiple probes on one device.** Transport, Hall, gate current and dielectric spectroscopy on the same device give a rich data set across 1–180 K.
- **Interesting timing of the R_s and I_g signatures.**
  - The sharp n drop beyond V_g ≈ −17 V after illumination (Fig. 2g) coincides with the onset of large gate current beyond ≈ −15 V (Supp. Fig. 3a).
  - The asymmetry in I_g weakens and the loop becomes nearly symmetric near 95 K (Supp. Fig. 3b).
- **Broad relevance.** The topic matters to communities working on quantum paraelectrics, oxide interfaces and cryogenic memory devices.

## 3. Concerns

### A. Must address

**1. The T_c "memory" is not shown to be distinct from the normal-state R_s/μ memory, and the "counterintuitive" T_c drop under illumination may follow the known gating trend.**

- **What the data show.** In Fig. 1e,f and Supp. Fig. 1a,b, T_c(V_g) and R_s(V_g) have essentially identical trajectories and loops. T_c rises whenever μ falls: at negative V_g, and along the initial drift where μ drops from ≈80 to ≈75 cm²/Vs.
- **Why the illumination result may not be counterintuitive.** Illumination raises μ (Fig. 2d) and lowers T_c. This is the same sign of T_c–μ correlation as electrostatic gating in LAO/KTO(111) (Ref. 17, where T_c increases as mobility decreases at nearly fixed n). The Discussion nevertheless calls the illumination result "apparently contradicting the expected trend" and uses it as support for soft-mode/PNR-assisted pairing.
- **Why it matters.** If gate-swept, cycled and illuminated states fall on a single T_c(R_s) or T_c(μ) curve, the superconducting memory is simply inherited from the normal-state disorder/mobility memory. Neither PNR alignment nor polar-fluctuation pairing would then be needed to explain T_c.
- **Requested.** A single plot of T_c versus R_s(10 K or 2 K), μ and n for every state measured (pristine, each gate step, cycled loops, L₀–L₄, illuminated-then-recycled). State whether the points collapse. Only deviations from such a collapse would support a lattice-specific contribution to pairing.

**2. PNR alignment and remnant polarization, central to the mechanism (Fig. 3e), are never measured directly.**

- **What is missing.**
  - No P–E loop, ε(V_g) "butterfly", pyroelectric/thermally stimulated depolarization current or optical (SHG) signature is shown.
  - The I_g traces (Supp. Fig. 3) show no switching currents on the positive branch, and nearly flat responses after the first cycle.
  - The statement that "PNR alignment [is] suppressed by illumination" has no measurement behind it at all.
- **Requested.**
  - **Hysteresis in charge.** Integrate I_g(V_g) to obtain Q(V_g) loops at 10, 25, 50 and 100 K and look for remnant charge after the leakage contribution is removed.
  - **ε in each state.** Measure ε(V_g) and tan δ at a fixed frequency in the pristine, gate-cycled and illuminated states at 10 K. A light-induced change in dielectric response would directly test the "randomized PNRs" claim.
  - **Charge balance.** Compare the integrated I_g charge in the −15 to −50 V branch with the Hall Δn (~2–4% of 1.06 × 10¹⁴ cm⁻²) to check whether the "tunneling back to deep donors" picture balances.

**3. The dielectric data probe the bulk 0.5 mm substrate, and their link to the interfacial 2DEG is not established.**

- **The bulk-versus-interface problem.** The capacitor in the Methods is the full substrate thickness between the back Au electrode and the 2DEG. The reduced, OV-rich layer is a thin near-surface region. Peak 2 (236 meV) is assigned to "OVs' ionization" adjacent to the 2DEG, yet the measurement is dominated by the bulk.
- **Alternative explanation.** Activated loss peaks of 0.2–0.4 eV in perovskites are frequently Maxwell–Wagner or space-charge relaxations at electrode or depletion-layer interfaces.
- **Requested controls.**
  - Measure an unreduced KTO(111) substrate from the same batch with metal electrodes on both faces, and if possible a sample of different thickness.
  - Show whether Peak 1 and Peak 2 are intrinsic to the substrate (e.g., impurity-driven PNRs, as in Refs. 37, 38, 42) or tied to the reduced layer.
  - Report the Arrhenius prefactors ω₀ with uncertainties on E_a. My rough reading of Fig. 3c gives f₀ ~ 10¹¹ Hz for Peak 1; for Peak 2 the apparent slope is hard to reconcile with 236 meV and should be shown with error bars.
  - Describe the phase calibration (open/short compensation, equivalent circuit including the 2DEG and contact resistances).
- **ε calibration.** The absolute ε ≈ 1500 with a maximum near 10 K is well below bulk KTO values (~4000 or more) and saturates rather than peaking in the bulk. This suggests series dead-layer or contact capacitance or geometric error, and should be discussed.

**4. The Δ_L–ε linearity (Fig. 3d) does not demonstrate coupling to lattice excitations.**

- **The linearity is generic.** For fixed gate voltage, induced charge scales as εε₀V/d. Any charge-trapping hysteresis proportional to induced charge would therefore give Δ_L ∝ ε. This is in fact the reasoning in the Discussion ("a large ε enables a stronger displacement field").
- **Correlation is weak evidence.** Two quantities that both decrease monotonically with T will look linear over a limited range.
- **The deviation below 35 K may be an artifact.** ε was measured with 5 V ac (~10⁴ V/m), but the loops are taken at ±50 V (~10⁵ V/m). KTO's ε is field-tunable most strongly at low T. A mismatch between small-signal ε and the effective large-signal susceptibility could produce the deviation from linearity without any PNR physics.
- **Requested.**
  - Plot Δ_L against the actual induced charge, i.e., ∫ε(E)dE using ε measured under dc bias.
  - Give ε∞ and the fit parameters.
  - State which frequency's ε is used.

**5. "Complete erasure" is not demonstrated against the pristine state, and a thermal origin is not excluded.**

- **No pristine reference.** Fig. 2i shows L₀→L₄ shifting downward but no virgin (pre-cycling) transition for comparison. The illuminated baseline also has higher n and μ than the cycled state (Fig. 2d,e), so it is a photo-defined reset state, not necessarily the original state.
- **Heating.** The LED is ~1 cm from the sample and produces visible heating jumps at 2 K (Fig. 2b).
- **Requested.**
  - Overlay pristine, cycled and L₄ curves, and give R_s, n and μ at 10 K for each.
  - Run a thermal control: a temperature excursion matching the LED-induced heating, in the dark, then return to 10 K and re-measure T_c and R_s.
  - Soften "complete erasure" in the abstract if T_c does not return to the pristine value.

### B. Should address

**6. Fig. 4c contradicts the text, and the dark-relaxation analysis is under-constrained.**

- **Text–figure mismatch.** The text claims "two minima in τ" at 35 K and 95 K. In Fig. 4c, τ is minimal at ≈30–35 K, but at 95 K τ is a local maximum (≈1.38 × 10³ s); the second minimum is at ≈85–90 K (≈0.78 × 10³ s). The ΔR/R₁ peak at 95 K thus coincides with a τ maximum, which is not what the "accelerated relaxation" narrative implies.
- **Single-point peaks.** The ΔR/R₁ maxima (Fig. 4b) are each defined essentially by one data point (30→35→40 K; 90→95→100 K).
- **Fit degeneracy.** The fitting window (~2.2 × 10³ s) is only ~2τ, and the 35 K curve is still rising at the end (Fig. 4a), so ΔR and τ are strongly covariant.
- **Uncontrolled excitation.** The LED flux varies with temperature (Methods: same V_L, temperature-dependent I–V), so the photo-excited population differs between temperatures.
- **Requested.**
  - Denser temperature sampling around 30–40 K and 85–100 K.
  - Longer dark windows.
  - Fit uncertainties, and a comparison with stretched-exponential forms commonly used for persistent photoconductivity (PPC).
  - A flux-calibrated LED drive (photodiode or fixed I_L with in situ calibration).
  - Confirmation that the initial state (four gate cycles) was re-prepared identically at each temperature.

**7. The temperature coincidences between dielectric peaks and transport features ignore frequency dispersion.**

- **The problem.** The loss-peak temperatures shift with frequency (Fig. 3b), but they are compared with quasi-static processes (τ ~ 10³ s; gate cycles of several hundred seconds).
- **What the paper's own parameters imply.** Extrapolating the Arrhenius laws to f ~ 1/τ ~ 10⁻³ Hz, my estimate is T_p ≈ 27 K for Peak 1 and ≈ 80 K for Peak 2. These are not 35 K and 95 K.
- **Requested.** Perform this extrapolation explicitly and state whether the coincidence survives.

**8. The carrier-density and mobility analysis needs validation.**

- **Sign and magnitude of Δn are anomalous.**
  - A capacitive estimate with ε ≈ 1500 and d = 0.5 mm gives Δn ≈ 8 × 10¹¹ cm⁻² per 50 V.
  - Supp. Fig. 1c shows ~2 × 10¹² cm⁻² per 50 V.
  - n is higher at −50 V than at +50 V, the opposite of field-effect expectations.
- **Internal inconsistency.** Supp. Note 1 says cycling increases n from the pristine state, while the main text says gate cycles decrease n (Fig. 2g, from the illuminated state).
- **Likely cause.** These features suggest single-band Hall analysis is inadequate for a multiband KTO(111) 2DEG; mobility-weighted Hall density can move opposite to the true density.
- **Run-to-run reproducibility.** n and μ come from three separate runs (0 T and ±6 T) of a history-dependent protocol. The 1–4% changes reported are comparable to plausible run-to-run variation.
- **Requested.**
  - R_xy(B) traces with linearity checks or two-band fits.
  - Demonstrated reproducibility of R_s trajectories between the three runs.
  - Reconciliation of the two n trends.

**9. Reproducibility and statistics are not reported.**

- The number of devices measured is not stated.
- No error bars are given on T_c, E_a or Δ_L.
- T_c is defined at 50% of a normal-state value over a broad transition (0.9–1.3 K), with shifts of only tens of mK.
- **Requested.**
  - Show that the shifts persist with onset and zero-resistance criteria, and state how the normal-state value is defined.
  - R_s "at 1.4 K" (Fig. 1f) lies within the fluctuation regime (Fig. 1c). Use R_s at 2–5 K to separate normal-state changes from paraconductivity.
  - Report results from at least one additional sample.

**10. The operating window and non-volatility claims for device applications need qualification and data.**

- **Erasure fails at 2 K.** PPC is absent at 2 K (Fig. 2b), so writing can occur near T_c but erasure requires warming to ≥10 K. This limits the "superconducting neuromorphic" framing and should be stated explicitly.
- **Retention is untested.**
  - Retention is shown only over ~10³ s plateaus.
  - Hours-long retention at 10 K and below T_c should be shown.
  - The written T_c enhancement should be checked after warming to 50, 100 and 300 K.
- **Prior work.** The relation to Ref. 30's gate-cycling T_c enhancement should be stated: what is new beyond optical erasure?

**11. The photo-excitation argument is physically loose.**

- **The flawed step.** The statement that E_a1 and E_a2 "are much smaller than the photon energy … and therefore can be activated by red light" conflates thermal activation barriers with optical transitions. Photons do not directly drive PNR reorientation; any effect must be photocarrier-mediated.
- **Requested.**
  - Wavelength dependence (threshold photon energy identifying the deep donor level).
  - Intensity/fluence dependence, with LED power at the sample given.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **A unified "state map".** Plot T_c against R_s, μ and n for every gate and illumination state, as the central figure. It would directly answer whether superconductivity carries memory beyond the normal state.
2. **Direct, state-resolved lattice probes.** Measure dielectric spectra and Q(V_g) loops at fixed temperature in the pristine, cycled and illuminated states. Add a bare-substrate dielectric control to localize Peaks 1 and 2.
3. **Quantitative charge bookkeeping.** Integrate I_g and compare with two-band Hall Δn. Use field-dependent ε when relating Δ_L to induced charge.
4. **Device-relevant benchmarking.** Report retention versus time and temperature, the number of distinguishable T_c levels, cycle endurance, and the minimum temperature at which optical reset works. This would substantiate the memory and neuromorphic framing.
