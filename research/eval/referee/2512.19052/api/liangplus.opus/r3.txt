**Review outline:**

### 1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports three findings for a single AlOx/KTaO3(111) 2DEG:
- Back-gate cycling at cryogenic temperature progressively raises T_c (about 1.02 → 1.09 K at V_g = 0) and R_s, which then settle into a stable hysteresis loop.
- Red-light (1.97 eV) illumination at 10 K resets R_s to a reproducible baseline and lowers T_c.
- Two dielectric-loss peaks (E_a ≈ 76 and 236 meV), assigned to polar-nanoregion (PNR) reorientation and oxygen-vacancy (OV) ionization, coincide with features in the gate hysteresis and the dark relaxation.

Optical erasure of a gate-imprinted superconducting state, combined with dielectric spectroscopy of the host lattice, is a new and potentially useful addition to the KTO interface literature. However, gate-cycling enhancement of T_c at oxide interfaces has already been reported (Ref. 30). The mechanistic attribution to PNRs and OVs, which carries much of the claimed novelty, currently rests mainly on temperature coincidences.

### 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **Clean switching protocol.** Illumination/gate-cycle switching of R_s is reproducible (Fig. 2c, Supp. Fig. 4). It is tunable by illumination time and gate amplitude, and sensitive to sweep order (Type A vs B).
- **Effect reaches the superconducting state.** The memory appears in T_c itself (Figs. 1c–e, 2i), not only in the normal state. T_c is stepped down by successive illuminations, and R_s survives thermal cycling (Fig. 2h).
- **Combined dataset.** Transport, Hall, gate-current and frequency-dependent dielectric data on the same device form a coherent dataset.
- **Timely topic.** The question of how quantum-paraelectric lattice degrees of freedom couple to interfacial superconductivity is of current interest.

### 3. Concerns

#### A. Must address

**1. A simpler, established explanation for the T_c changes is not excluded.**
- Across every manipulation in the paper, T_c moves opposite to the mobility:
  - gate cycling from the virgin state: T_c↑, μ↓, R_s↑ (Supp. Fig. 1);
  - negative branch: T_c↑, R_s↑;
  - illumination: T_c↓, μ↑, R_s↓ (Figs. 2d,e,h,i).
- An anticorrelation between T_c and μ in gated KTO(111) is already documented in Refs. 17 and 18. There it is attributed to the spatial distribution and confinement of carriers relative to the soft-mode-active interface region.
- The data therefore look consistent with a single "hidden" variable: the trapped-charge or confinement state of the well, set by gate history and light.
- Calling the light-induced T_c reduction "counterintuitive" (p. 6) is therefore unwarranted, and invoking PNR alignment is not yet required.
- Requested actions:
  - Plot T_c against μ (and against R_s and n) for all states: virgin, every gate-loop point, L0–L4, and Type A vs Type B end states.
  - If all states collapse onto one curve, the gate/light memory is a memory of the electrostatic and trapped-charge configuration. The PNR contribution to T_c would then be unproven.
  - If they do not collapse, show the deviation explicitly; that would be the strongest evidence for an additional lattice ingredient.
  - Discuss the established mechanism from STO back-gating: irreversible escape of electrons over the well barrier into substrate traps on first sweeps (e.g., Biscaras et al.), and how it differs from the proposed picture.

**2. The dielectric measurement probes the 0.5 mm bulk substrate, not the interface.**
- The ε and tan δ data (Fig. 3a–c) come from a capacitor formed by the back electrode and the 2DEG across the entire crystal. The reduced, OV-rich layer created by Al deposition is nanometres thick.
- Loss peaks near 30–50 K are known in nominally pure KTO from impurity dipoles (Refs. 37, 38, 42). The 236 meV process could equally be a bulk impurity or defect relaxation.
- Assigning Peak 2 to the OVs that donate the 2DEG therefore relies only on E_a falling in a 200–300 meV range taken from the literature.
- Requested actions:
  - Measure a bare, unreduced KTO(111) substrate from the same batch, with metal electrodes on both faces.
  - Measure samples with different OV densities (Al thickness, deposition temperature, post-anneal).
  - If both peaks persist unchanged in the bare substrate, they are bulk features, and linking them to the interfacial OVs needs a different argument.
- Ideally, also measure ε and tan δ (i) in the virgin state, (ii) after gate cycling and (iii) after illumination. If PNR alignment and randomization drive the memory, the dielectric response should change between these states. At present it is measured in only one state.

**3. The 35 K / 95 K coincidences are not quantitatively consistent with the stated activation energies, and the τ(T) data look fit-limited.**
- *Arrhenius extrapolation.* The peaks in Fig. 3b,c are measured at 0.1 Hz–4 kHz. Gate sweeps and dark relaxation occur on ~10³ s timescales (f ≈ 10⁻³–10⁻⁴ Hz). Extrapolating the Fig. 3c fits to these frequencies gives characteristic temperatures noticeably below 35 K and 95 K (my rough estimate is about 25 K and 70–75 K). The authors should report ω₀ with uncertainties and do this extrapolation explicitly.
- *τ is nearly flat.* In Fig. 4c, τ stays within about 0.7–1.4 × 10³ s from 10 to 180 K. For a 76 meV activated process, τ should change by many orders of magnitude between 15 and 30 K. The observed near-constancy is incompatible with relaxation limited by thermally activated PNR or OV processes.
- *Window artifact.* The fitted τ is comparable to the ~2 × 10³ s observation window (Fig. 4a). This suggests the single-exponential fits are constrained by the window rather than by the physics.
- *Single-point peaks.* The ΔR/R₁ maxima at 35 K and 95 K (Fig. 4b) each rest on one data point. Neighbouring points at 30/40 K and 90/100 K are small. The 35 K and 95 K curves in Fig. 4a are also visibly noisier and differ in shape during illumination.
- Requested actions:
  - Repeat these temperatures, with finer steps (e.g., 2 K) around 35 K and 95 K.
  - Use longer dark windows.
  - Show residuals, or test stretched-exponential or logarithmic fits.
  - Report the temperature stability during each run.

**4. Whether the light effect is photonic, and whether erasure is "complete", is not established.**
- *Heating.* The 2 K trace shows heating jumps (Fig. 2b). The hysteresis is thermally annealed on warming (Fig. 1g, Supp. Fig. 2). A heating control is therefore essential: warm the sample in the dark to the maximum temperature reached under the LED, return to 10 K, and compare R_s and T_c.
- *Missing light characterization.* Please report:
  - LED optical power and photon flux at the sample;
  - dependence on flux (I_L);
  - ideally, a wavelength dependence;
  - optical absorption of reduced KTO at 630 nm.
- *Contradiction at 2 K.* Fig. 2b shows no illumination effect at 2 K, and PPC appears only at ≥10 K. This contradicts the statement (p. 4) that the processes "can be activated by red light illumination even at extremely low temperatures". It points instead to a thermally assisted process.
- *Practical consequence.* Fig. 2h requires warming from the superconducting state to 10 K for each erasure. Erasure therefore does not operate in or near the superconducting regime.
- *Completeness.* "Complete erasure" (title, abstract) requires overlaying the virgin T_c curve and R_s on L4 (Fig. 2i) and on the 795 Ω baseline. As shown, L0–L4 are compared only with each other.
- *Recovery at higher temperature.* The authors note the system does not fully recover even at 180 K. This also needs reconciling with "complete" erasure.

#### B. Should address

**5. Carrier-density and mobility changes are at the percent level and extracted with a single-band Hall model.**
- The mechanism relies on Δn/n of about 1–2% (Figs. 2e,g; Supp. Fig. 1c). KTO(111) 2DEGs are multiband.
- If mobility ratios between subbands change, a single-band Hall n from ±6 T data can shift by this much with no change in total density.
- Requested actions:
  - Show full ρ_xy(B) curves and test for nonlinearity.
  - Apply a two-band analysis where justified.
  - State error bars on n and μ.
- The sign of dn/dV_g also needs comment. In Supp. Fig. 1c, n is *higher* at −50 V than at +50 V, opposite to capacitive induction. The trapping picture may account for this, but it should be stated explicitly, together with the sign convention for V_g.
- *Separate runs.* n and μ come from three separate runs, at B = 0 and ±6 T, with the state re-prepared each time (Methods).
  - Please demonstrate run-to-run reproducibility of the prepared state.
  - Please show that 6 T does not itself alter the OV/PNR dynamics.
  - The two runs at ±6 T should give consistent R_s.

**6. The gate current is far larger than displacement current and needs quantification.**
- The first-cycle spike of about −10 nA near −50 V (Supp. Fig. 3a) is orders of magnitude above C·dV/dt. My estimate is C ≈ 0.2 nF for ε ≈ 2000, area 6.25 mm² and d = 0.5 mm, giving tens of pA at typical sweep rates.
- This implies real leakage or charge transfer through the substrate.
- Requested actions:
  - Report the sweep rate.
  - Integrate I_g to obtain the transferred charge per cycle, and compare it with the Δn inferred from Hall.
  - Discuss whether the spike is DC leakage, which would also accompany the initial T_c drift.
- An integrated Q(V_g) loop would also directly test the claimed remnant polarization at V_g = 0 below 35 K.
- The −15 V onset in Supp. Note 3 and the −17 V threshold in Fig. 2g should be reconciled.

**7. There is no direct evidence of PNR alignment or remnant polarization.**
- The PNR picture in Fig. 3e is a schematic. The only support is the tan δ peak and the 35 K kink.
- Requested evidence:
  - P–E/Q–V loops, including a remnant-charge measurement at V_g = 0 after Type A vs Type B;
  - the change in ε after poling;
  - optical probes (SHG or Raman) if available.
- Please measure T_c after Type A versus Type B end states. If PNR alignment strengthens pairing, as claimed on p. 6, the paper should state whether the effect depends on the polarization direction or only on its magnitude. Fig. 1e suggests sign dependence, which looks more like internal gating than "alignment strengthens pairing".

**8. The ΔL ∝ ε correlation (Fig. 3d) is weak evidence for the specific mechanism.**
- Both ΔL and ε are monotonic in T, so they will correlate whatever the mechanism.
- Proportionality to ε is also expected generically: the gate-induced charge, and hence any constant-fraction trapped charge, scales with ε.
- The linear range spans only ε ≈ 200–800, and the 35 K "kink" is drawn by eye.
- Requested actions:
  - Provide error bars on ΔL.
  - State which frequency's ε is used, given that the process is quasi-static.
  - Fit both regimes with uncertainties.
  - Show that a generic trap-filling model with the measured ε(T) fails to reproduce ΔL(T).
- "ε∞ as the high-temperature limit" is non-standard notation; please define its value.

**9. Novelty relative to Ref. 30, sample statistics and retention.**
- Ref. 30 reports universal T_c enhancement by cryogenic gate cycles, recovery at room temperature, and coexisting ferroelectricity. The abstract's "previously unrecognized interplay" needs explicit positioning against it.
- All data appear to come from one device. Please show reproducibility on at least one or two additional samples, ideally with different OV content.
- Please add error bars on T_c; the shifts are only about 0.02–0.07 K.
- Please quantify retention of the gate-imprinted state at 1.4 K and 10 K over hours. State the temperature at which the memory is fully reset by warming.

**10. Physical arguments in the illumination model are loose.**
- That E_a is much smaller than ħω does not imply photons drive PNR reorientation or OV ionization (p. 4). This requires an optical transition with appreciable cross-section, not merely sufficient energy.
- The "deep donor states" are not identified.
- Photo-ionizing deep donors leaves positively charged centres that should scatter more strongly. Explain why μ nonetheless increases.
- State whether these centres lie within the 2DEG's scattering range.
- The claim that excess electrons "tunnel back into deep donor states" beyond −17 V (p. 6) has no independent support.

**11. The application claims are overstated relative to the data.**
- T_c tunability is about 7%.
- Erasure takes 10²–10⁴ s and only works at T ≥ 10 K, roughly 10 × T_c.
- The neuromorphic and quantum-memristor framing in the abstract and conclusion should be tempered, or supported with figures of merit.

#### C. Minor

**12. Text and labelling errors.**
- A sentence on dielectric loss is duplicated in the first Discussion paragraph.
- "laser emitting diode" should be "light-emitting diode".
- Typos: "illlumination", "memrisitive", "Suppelementary", "the it is", "would facilitates".
- Supp. Note 4, item 2, labels 856 Ω as R_s^b; it should be the elevated value R_s^a.
- "Extended Figure 5" should read Supplementary Figure 5.
- Units mix "Ω" and "Ω·sq⁻¹".
- p. 6 cites "Fig. 3c" for loss peaks shown in Fig. 3b.

**13. Inconsistent stated reasons for the 35 K line.**
- The Fig. 3 caption says the 35 K line marks the onset of TO-mode softening.
- The text says 35 K corresponds to T_p1 of the PNR peak.
- The Fig. 3d caption says ΔL "deviates above"; the text says "steeper below".
- Peak 1 is described as "near 30 K" but compared with 35 K.

**14. Dielectric method details.**
- State where I_g is sensed. If it is sensed on the gate line, coax and stray capacitance add directly and matter at high T, where C_sample is only tens of pF.
- The low-T ε of about 1500–2000 is well below the bulk KTO literature value of about 4000. Comment on geometry, fringing or electrode effects, and on the slight downturn of ε below about 10 K at 0.1 Hz.

**15. Methods consistency.**
- The rationale "to minimise fringe effect in dielectric measurements, transport … in vdP" is unclear.
- Specify the FEM correction magnitude.
- Supp. Note 1 says loops stabilize in "3~4" cycles; the main text says four. Harmonize.

### 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Universal T_c plot.** Add a single figure plotting T_c against μ (and n) for every state: virgin, gated, Type A/B, and L0–L4. This is the most direct way to show whether a lattice contribution beyond carrier redistribution exists.
2. **Control set.** Add the following controls:
   - bare-substrate dielectric spectroscopy;
   - samples with varied OV density;
   - a dark thermal excursion mimicking LED heating;
   - flux/wavelength dependence of erasure;
   - the virgin-state reference in Fig. 2i.
3. **Direct polarization evidence.** Add integrated-charge (Q–V) loops and ε/tan δ measured before and after gate cycling and illumination, to show remnant polarization directly.
4. **Redo the relaxation analysis.** Use longer dark windows, finer temperature steps and repeat runs. Report fit uncertainties. Compare τ(T) quantitatively with the Arrhenius parameters from Fig. 3c, including ω₀, rather than relying on temperature coincidences.
