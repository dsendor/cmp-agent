**A. Must address**

**1. No direct evidence for PNR alignment, remnant polarization or OV charge-state switching.** The central mechanism has four parts:
- gate cycling aligns polar nanoregions and leaves remnant polarization at V_g = 0;
- illumination randomizes the PNRs;
- OV charge states switch;
- "superconductivity and PNR alignment are strengthened by gate cycling and suppressed by illumination."

None of these is measured. The paper contains no P–E loops, bias-dependent C–V (butterfly ε(V_g)) hysteresis, PUND, pyroelectric or thermally stimulated depolarization current, PFM, SHG, or Raman/IR soft-mode or structural probe. The Fig. 3e cartoon ("PNRs pointing outwards/inwards") is therefore a hypothesis, not a result.

The dielectric spectra were also measured in a single, unspecified state of the sample. The paper never shows that ε, tan δ or the relaxation strengths of Peaks 1 and 2 differ between gate-cycled and illuminated states. That comparison is the minimal test that these excitations carry the memory.

The claim that 1.97 eV photons "randomize PNR orientations owing to the large photon energy" has no stated microscopic pathway. Photon energy is not a natural variable for dipole reorientation, so local heating or photo-screening must be considered.

The authors should:
- measure ε(V_g) and tan δ(V_g) loops, and ε and tan δ under dc bias, at 5–50 K in each protocol state: virgin, gate-cycled Type A, gate-cycled Type B, and illuminated;
- show that the relaxation strengths of the two peaks track the memory state;
- perform TSDC after poling at ±50 V, where release features would be expected near 35 K and 95 K if the model is right;
- where feasible, add an interface-sensitive polarization probe (low-T SHG, PFM through thin AlOₓ, Raman of the TO mode under gate) and an OV charge-state probe (photoluminescence, EPR, thermally stimulated current).

**2. A simpler disorder/mobility explanation of the T_c changes is not tested.** In every state shown, higher T_c accompanies higher R_s and lower μ. Gate cycling raises R_s, lowers μ and raises T_c; illumination raises n and μ, lowers R_s and lowers T_c (Fig. 1e,f; Supp. Fig. 1; Fig. 2). Figs. 1e/f suggest T_c and R_s track almost linearly.

Prior KTO(111) gating work (Refs. 17, 18, cited in the Introduction) found that gating mainly modulates mobility. It also found that T_c anticorrelates with mobility/disorder, with electrons confined closer to the interface, rather than tracking density. Calling the illumination-induced T_c drop "counterintuitive", or saying it "apparently contradicts the expected trend", presupposes a density-controlled T_c that the authors' own Introduction contradicts.

If all gate- and light-prepared states collapse onto a single T_c(μ), T_c(R_s) or T_c(k_F·l) curve, then changes in carrier distribution, confinement and disorder explain the data. OV charge-state changes alone, a well-established trap-charging picture, would explain both effects, and PNR-mediated "coherent polar fluctuations" become unnecessary.

The authors should:
- plot T_c against μ, n, R_s and k_F·l for all prepared states: gate-swept, cycled, partially illuminated (L₁–L₄) and dark-relaxed;
- prepare states with matched R_s, n and μ by different routes (light vs. gate). If their T_c values differ, the PNR hypothesis is strengthened; if not, it should be withdrawn or downgraded;
- exploit the proposed thresholds through selective-erasure experiments. Cycle at 10 K, warm to ~50 K (above T_p1, below T_p2), recool, and determine which part of the T_c/R_s memory survives; repeat with warming to ~120 K. This would be far more compelling than the current parametric correlations.

**3. The dielectric measurement is not interface-specific, and Maxwell–Wagner and other artefacts are not excluded.** The capacitance is measured across the full 0.5 mm KTO slab, between the Au back electrode and the grounded 2DEG. It therefore reflects a bulk-averaged response plus any interfacial layers. The 2DEG, however, couples only to the near-interface region (nanometres to tens of nanometres).

A conducting, OV-rich surface layer on an insulating bulk is a textbook Maxwell–Wagner (interfacial-polarization) configuration. Thermally activated, frequency-shifting loss peaks are its classic signature, and their apparent activation energy reflects the conductivity of the defect-rich layer, not dipole reorientation. Other candidate sources are:
- the Au/KTO Schottky contact;
- the depletion layer under the 2DEG;
- the AlOₓ;
- hopping among defects.

The analysis also neglects the finite 2DEG sheet resistance (~800 Ω) and the wiring.

The extracted ε (~1500–2000 at low T, ~1600 quoted) is well below bulk KTO values (~4000–4500). This points to:
- series dead layers or depletion regions, which would directly distort tan δ;
- geometric or fringe errors;
- an effect of the 5 V ac excitation amplitude;
- a contribution from the reduced region.

In addition, ε was measured only at zero dc bias, although ε(E) in KTO is strongly field-dependent within the ±50 V range used.

The authors should:
- present an equivalent-circuit or full impedance (Cole–Cole) analysis including the 2DEG resistance, contact/Schottky layers and any series dead layer;
- explain the low ε;
- measure a reference bare, unreduced KTO(111) substrate with symmetric/identical electrodes, plus samples of different 2DEG sheet conductance and a strongly reduced or non-superconducting sample, to determine whether Peaks 1 and 2 are intrinsic to the lattice or interface-related;
- measure ε and tan δ under the dc biases actually used.

**4. The established back-gate trap-escape mechanism is neither discussed nor excluded.** Several observations are hallmarks of known back-gate physics in LAO/STO, in which electrons escape over the edge of the confining potential into bulk or interfacial traps above the Fermi level:
- first-cycle irreversibility only on the negative branch;
- a threshold-like drop in n beyond about −17 V (Fig. 2g);
- suppression of the effect in later cycles.

Combined with the field-dependent permittivity of the quantum paraelectric, which reshapes the confining potential, this picture can account for the persistent R_s/μ changes without invoking PNRs. The relevant literature (Biscaras et al. and related work on STO back-gate forming, hysteresis and optical reset) is not cited. The statement that electrons "tunnel back into deep donor states" beyond −17 V is asserted, not tested.

The authors should discuss this alternative explicitly and test it. Useful tests are the sweep-rate, hold-time and temperature dependence of the first-cycle irreversibility and of the −17 V threshold.

**5. The charge-transfer model is inconsistent with the transport data and lacks charge bookkeeping.** There are four problems.

- **Sign of Δn.** Supp. Fig. 1c shows the Hall density higher at V_g = −50 V (≈1.08–1.085×10¹⁴ cm⁻²) than at +50 V (≈1.035×10¹⁴ cm⁻²). This is opposite to the naive field-effect expectation and is not discussed.
- **Magnitude of Δn.** Δn ≈ 4–5×10¹² cm⁻² over the sweep, several times the capacitive estimate ε₀εV/d. With ε ≈ 1500 and d = 0.5 mm, that estimate is ~10¹² cm⁻² for ±50 V.
- **Contradictory statements.**
  - Supp. Note 1 says n increases over the initial cycles, while Fig. 2f–g and the main text say gate cycles decrease n. These may refer to different starting states (virgin vs. illuminated), but this is not stated.
  - The Discussion says that on the negative branch "electrons hop from the OVs back into the 2DEG", which should increase n. Yet Fig. 2g shows n dropping rapidly beyond −17 V, attributed to electrons tunnelling into deep donors. The same branch is invoked for opposite charge flows.
- **Missing bookkeeping.** The ~10 nA gate-current transient decaying over minutes (Supp. Fig. 3a) plausibly integrates to ≳10¹³ cm⁻² over the 6.25 mm² sample, far more than the Hall Δn. Leakage through the substrate and contacts is not separated from displacement current. It is therefore impossible to tell where the displaced charge resides: deep traps, shallow OVs, the AlOₓ or its interface, or leakage.

The authors should:
- integrate I_g over each sweep and hold, subtract the measured leakage, and compare the result with Δn and with the capacitive estimate;
- state where each charge population resides;
- reconcile the sign of Δn (Supp. Fig. 1c vs. Fig. 2g) and the contradictory electron-flow statements;
- develop at least a minimal quantitative model that reproduces the sign and magnitude of ΔR_s, Δn and Δμ, for example rate equations for trap/OV occupancy coupled to a Schrödinger–Poisson confinement profile with field-dependent ε.

**6. The T_c shifts are small relative to the transition width and rest on a single, normalization-sensitive criterion.** The gate-induced T_c enhancement is ~50–80 mK out of ~1.0–1.1 K (≈5–7%; Fig. 1e; Supp. Fig. 1a,e). The illumination-induced shifts are ~30–70 mK. Both are a fraction of the ~0.2–0.3 K transition width (Fig. 1c,d; Fig. 2i).

T_c is defined at 50% of R_n, while R_n itself shifts by ~5–8% across gate and illumination states. Part of the apparent shift may therefore reflect normal-state normalization, or changes in inhomogeneity and percolation of a broad transition, rather than a change in pairing strength. No onset, 10/90%, zero-resistance, normalized R/R_n, transition-width, BKT or H_c2 analysis is given, and there are no error bars.

The authors should:
- report T_c with several criteria plus normalized curves and widths, with error estimates;
- add BKT analysis and perpendicular/parallel H_c2(T), from which the coherence length and any change in effective 2D thickness can be extracted. This would distinguish pairing changes from confinement or disorder changes.

**7. "Complete erasure" is not demonstrated.** The manuscript never shows that illumination returns T_c, or R_s at the same temperature, to the pristine, pre-gating value.
- The pristine R_s (Fig. 1f origin, ~0.745 kΩ) is quoted at 1.4 K, whereas the illuminated baseline (795 Ω) is quoted at 10 K. The two cannot be compared.
- Fig. 2i shows L₄ only relative to L₀, an already-cycled state.
- The Discussion states that the system "does not fully recover to the pre-illumination state even at 180 K". Warming to room temperature remains the established full reset.

The relation between erasure by light and reset by thermal cycling is therefore unclear. The authors should compare L₄ and the fully illuminated state with the virgin and room-temperature-reset states at identical temperature and measurement conditions, showing full R(T) curves. Otherwise they should replace "complete erasure" with a quantitatively supported statement.

**B. Should address**

**8. Assignment of the loss peaks rests on literature analogy, and the fits are under-documented.**
- **Peak 1 (76 meV, ~30 K).** It is assigned to PNRs by comparison with Li- or impurity-doped KTO studies. In nominally pure KTO, low-temperature loss peaks have also been attributed to off-centre impurities (Li, Fe, Nb) and polaronic hopping. The impurity content of the substrates (Li, Fe, Na, etc.) is not characterized.
- **Peak 2 (236 meV).** It is assigned to OV ionization solely because it falls in a 200–300 meV literature window. Alternatives are not excluded: defect-dipole complexes (e.g., Fe³⁺–V_O), hopping conduction in the reduced near-surface layer, and Maxwell–Wagner relaxation.
- **Fitting.** Peak positions in Fig. 3b, especially Peak 2, are marked on weak, sloped, partly noisy curves. The Arrhenius fits (Fig. 3c) span a narrow temperature window. No uncertainties are given for T_p, E_a or the attempt frequencies ω₁, ω₂, and the peak-fitting procedure is not described. The prefactors would help discriminate between mechanisms.

The authors should characterize impurities (SIMS/ICP), describe the peak-fitting procedure, and report ω₁, ω₂, E_a and T_p with uncertainties.

**9. The Δ_L–ε correlation and the 35 K/95 K coincidences are weak, non-discriminating evidence.**
- **Δ_L vs. ε.** Both are monotonic functions of temperature, so the parametric plot (Fig. 3d) will be smooth and near-linear for almost any mechanism. Only ~10–15 points define the "linear" (95–35 K) and "steeper" (<35 K) regimes, with no fit statistics or error bars. Δ_L ≈ 0 near 95 K, so the "onset" there and the "deviation" at 35 K depend on a handful of points. No comparison is made with Δ_L plotted against other monotonic variables (e.g., 1/T or thermally activated rates). The steeper slope below 35 K could equally reflect stronger trap freezing or the strongly field-dependent ε(E) at low T.
- **Physical model.** The proposed Δ_L ∝ (ε − ε_∞), on the grounds that "larger ε gives a stronger displacement field", is not derived. For fixed V_g across a 0.5 mm slab, the induced charge scales as εV_g/d. This is a statement about gate efficiency, not about OV charge exchange specifically.
- **Timescales.** The dielectric peaks shift strongly with frequency over the measured band: Peak 1 spans ~25–45 K and Peak 2 ~80–140 K. Gate sweeps and dark relaxation (τ ~ 10³ s) probe effective frequencies of order 10⁻³ Hz. Extrapolating the Arrhenius laws with plausible prefactors to these frequencies gives roughly 25 K and 80 K, not 35 K and 95 K.

The authors should:
- derive the expected Δ_L–ε relation;
- measure Δ_L as a function of sweep rate and hold time, which is essential for a kinetic interpretation;
- extrapolate the fitted Arrhenius laws (with the fitted ω₀) to the effective frequencies of the gate sweeps and dark relaxation;
- compare the dielectric relaxation times τ = 1/ω(T) from Fig. 3c quantitatively with the transport kinetics, rather than relying on coincident temperatures.

**10. The dark-relaxation analysis is internally inconsistent and statistically thin.**
- **τ(T) description.** The text claims "two minima in τ" at 35 K and 95 K (Fig. 4c). The plotted data show minima near ~30–35 K and ~85–90 K, while 95 K is a sharp local maximum (~1.38×10³ s). Adjacent points jump by ~50% (e.g., 90 K → 95 K), suggesting scatter or fit instability.
- **ΔR/R₁ maxima.** Each maximum (Fig. 4b, 35 K and 95 K) rests on essentially one point, with coarse temperature sampling and no repeat runs.
- **Data quality.** The 95 K trace in Fig. 4a is visibly noisier and non-monotonic during illumination, raising the possibility of temperature instability or heating.
- **Fit model.** A single-exponential fit to likely stretched or multi-component (glassy PPC) relaxation makes τ and ΔR fit-model dependent. The non-monotonic τ(T) could instead arise from overlapping relaxations with different amplitudes.
- **Interpretation.** The "energetic preference for partially aligned PNRs and ionized OVs" in an "interfacial potential trap" is invoked without a model.

The authors should:
- correct the τ(T) description;
- add fit uncertainties and compare single-exponential, stretched-exponential and two-component fits;
- sample temperature more densely around 30–40 K and 85–100 K, with repeated runs;
- repeat the series at constant, calibrated photon dose (see concern 11).

**11. Illumination dose, heating and spectral dependence are uncontrolled.** The LED is driven at fixed V_L, and its I–V characteristic is acknowledged in the Methods to be non-monotonically temperature dependent. Photon flux, and hence the starting state, therefore varies with temperature, and the flux at the sample is unknown. Heating is visible at 2 K (resistance jumps in Fig. 2b), but the sample-temperature excursion at 10 K is not quantified. This matters because both PPC and the claimed activated processes are strongly T-dependent, and because "optical" erasure could partly be thermal annealing. Only one wavelength (630 nm) is used, so no spectral threshold is available to identify the defect levels involved.

The authors should:
- use a calibrated, temperature-independent source, for example fiber-coupled from room temperature;
- report the optical power at the sample and quantify the temperature rise during illumination;
- measure the power, dose and wavelength dependence of erasure using several LEDs from near-IR to blue;
- include a control in which the sample is heated in the dark to the LED-induced temperature.

**12. The Hall-derived n and μ may be unreliable at the percent level the mechanism relies on.** n and μ come from single-band analysis (n = 1/eR_H) at ±6 T in a KTO(111) 2DEG at n ~ 10¹⁴ cm⁻², which is expected to be multi-band. This can conflate changes in band occupation and mobility ratio with changes in total density. The reported changes are only Δn/n ~ 1–2% and Δμ/μ ~ 4% (1–5% overall).

The data come from three separate protocol repetitions (B = 0, +6 T, −6 T) "following the same procedure". Given the history dependence the paper itself emphasizes, run-to-run reproducibility of the starting state must be shown, but no error bars are given. The van der Pauw-to-R_s conversion via FEM, and the possible anisotropy of KTO(111) transport (Ref. 16), are not documented.

The authors should:
- perform two-carrier fits of field-dependent Hall and magnetoresistance data;
- give error bars and demonstrate cross-run reproducibility;
- document the R_s conversion and any anisotropy.

**13. All data appear to come from a single device, without controls.** The number of samples, the AlOₓ (Al) thickness, batch-to-batch variation, and dependence on reduction conditions, capping layer or orientation are not stated. Intrinsic 2DEG mechanisms cannot be distinguished from substrate-bulk mechanisms without controls such as:
- a non-superconducting or differently reduced KTO 2DEG;
- a different orientation, e.g., (001), which is non-superconducting in this temperature range.

The authors should show the key effects in at least two or three independent devices, including at least one with a different AlOₓ thickness, reduction condition or orientation.

**14. The memristive, neuromorphic and quantum-memristor framing is not supported by device-level data.** Writing requires ±50 V across a 0.5 mm substrate. Erasing requires tens to thousands of seconds of illumination at ~10 K, i.e., out of the superconducting state, because light cannot be applied at 2 K without heating (Fig. 2b).

None of the following is given:
- retention time at base temperature;
- endurance over many write/erase cycles;
- cycle-to-cycle variance;
- number of distinguishable levels with error margins;
- switching speed or energy per operation;
- minimal gate amplitude or dose needed to write and erase;
- any figure of merit relevant to superconducting circuits.

The authors should provide these metrics or tone down the abstract and conclusions.

**15. Novelty relative to prior work is overstated.** Ref. 30 (Zhang et al., arXiv 2506.16298) already reports gate-cycling-enhanced superconductivity as "universal" at STO- and KTO-based interfaces, recovered only at room temperature and linked to ferroelectric-like behaviour. The manuscript cites it only in passing. Other relevant prior work is also not discussed or clearly differentiated:
- hysteretic back-gating of KTO(111) (Refs. 17–18);
- LAO/STO back-gate forming, trapping and optical-reset literature;
- PPC and light-tuned transport in reduced KTO 2DEGs (Refs. 31, 32, 45);
- glassy PNR-related electron dynamics in electron-doped KTO (Ref. 43).

The new elements appear to be the optical reset of a gate-imprinted T_c and the attempt to map the memory onto two dielectric relaxations. The claim of a "previously unrecognized interplay" should be recalibrated accordingly.

**16. The Type A/B sequence dependence may be trivially branch-dependent.** The final R_s depends on cycle order, which is presented as an additional "sensitivity to cycling sequence" and a directional fingerprint of an internal state variable. It may instead simply reflect which branch of the known hysteresis loop the sweep ends on, since Type B ends from −50 V, where R_s^{−0} > R_s^{+0}. The authors should show that the Type A/B difference goes beyond branch-dependent readout, or reframe it as such.

- which frequency's ε is overlaid with Δ_L in Fig. 3a.
