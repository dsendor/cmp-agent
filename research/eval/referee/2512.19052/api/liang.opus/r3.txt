**Review outline:**

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic.** The manuscript reports two effects in back-gated AlOₓ/KTaO₃(111) 2DEGs:
  - Cryogenic back-gate cycling (±50 V) progressively and persistently raises T_c (≈1.02 → ≈1.09 K) and R_s. The system then settles into a reproducible hysteresis loop after about four cycles.
  - Red (630 nm, sub-gap) illumination at 10 K lowers R_s back to a baseline, raises n and μ, and shifts T_c downward. The authors call this an "erasure" of the gate-imprinted memory.
- **Proposed mechanism.** Two thermally activated dielectric-loss peaks are identified: ~30 K (E_a ≈ 76 meV) and ~100 K (E_a ≈ 236 meV). They are assigned to polar-nanoregion (PNR) reorientation and oxygen-vacancy (OV) ionization, respectively. These assignments are linked to:
  - the linear-then-steeper dependence of the loop size Δ_L on ε (with breaks near 95 K and 35 K);
  - extrema near the same two temperatures in the dark-relaxation magnitude and time constant.
- **Potential significance.** Optically resettable, electrically programmable T_c in an oxide interface superconductor would interest:
  - the oxide-interface community;
  - researchers on superconductivity in quantum paraelectrics;
  - device-oriented readers working on cryogenic memristive and neuromorphic elements.
- **Novelty is partial.**
  - Gate-history enhancement of T_c recoverable only near room temperature has recently been reported as "universal" in STO- and KTO-based interfaces (Ref. 30).
  - Irreversible first-cycle back-gate hysteresis is well documented in LAO/STO (electron escape from the well into trap states).
  - Persistent photoconductivity (PPC) in reduced KTO and light-tuned transport in KTO 2DEGs are known (Refs. 31, 32, 45).
  - The genuinely new elements are:
    - the optical reset of the gate-imprinted T_c;
    - the attempt to map transport hysteresis and relaxation onto two dielectric-loss excitations.
  - The novelty claim ("previously unrecognized interplay") should be calibrated accordingly.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Reconfigurable superconductivity.** The work demonstrates bidirectional, non-volatile tuning of T_c in a 2D interface superconductor, controlled by two orthogonal knobs (electrostatic gating and light). This is conceptually attractive and relevant to cryogenic memory concepts.
- **Breadth of data on a single platform.**
  - Superconducting transitions; normal-state R_s; Hall n and μ.
  - Gate leakage/displacement current I_g.
  - Multi-frequency dielectric constant and loss (0.1–4001 Hz).
  - Temperature-resolved dark relaxation (10–180 K).
- **Reproducible protocols.** Illumination/gate-cycle sequences (Fig. 2c, Supp. Fig. 4) show:
  - reproducible switching between the baseline and elevated R_s;
  - grading by illumination time and gate amplitude.
  This supports "multi-level" programmability.
- **Coincident characteristic temperatures.** Two temperatures (~35 K and ~95 K) recur across independent observables: the loss peaks, the Δ_L–ε kink, the ΔR/R₁ maxima and the τ minima. This gives the phenomenology internal coherence.
- **Useful counterintuitive result.** Illumination raises n and μ yet lowers T_c, while gating lowers μ and raises T_c. This is informative about the pairing mechanism in KTO interfaces, and relevant to the debate on soft-TO-phonon and Rashba-polar pairing (Refs. 18, 44, 47–49).

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**Reason 1 — The microscopic mechanism (PNR alignment + OV charge switching) is inferred from correlations, not demonstrated, and simpler established explanations are not excluded.**

- **No direct evidence of PNR alignment or remanent polarization.**
  - The central claim is that gate cycling aligns PNRs, leaves remnant polarization at V_g = 0, and that illumination randomizes them.
  - None of the following are measured:
    - C–V or P–E hysteresis (butterfly ε(V_g), PUND, pyroelectric or thermally stimulated depolarization current);
    - SHG or other polarization-sensitive probes;
    - dielectric response before versus after illumination or gate cycling.
  - The cartoon in Fig. 3e ("PNRs pointing outwards/inwards") is therefore a hypothesis, not a result.
  - The claim that 1.97 eV photons "randomize PNR orientations owing to the large photon energy" has no stated microscopic pathway. Photon energy is not a natural variable for dipole reorientation, so local heating or photo-screening must be considered.
- **The OV/PNR assignment of the loss peaks rests on literature analogy.**
  - Peak 1 (76 meV, ~30 K) is assigned to PNRs by comparison with Li- or impurity-doped KTO studies. The impurity content (Li, Fe, Na, etc.) of the substrates is not characterized.
  - Peak 2 (236 meV) is assigned to OV ionization because it falls within a 200–300 meV literature window. Other thermally activated processes with similar energies have not been excluded:
    - defect-dipole complexes (e.g., Fe³⁺–V_O);
    - hopping conduction in the reduced near-surface layer;
    - Maxwell–Wagner interfacial relaxation between the conducting 2DEG/reduced layer and the insulating bulk.
  - The fitted prefactors ω₁, ω₂ (attempt frequencies) are not reported, although they would help discriminate between mechanisms.
- **A competing, well-established explanation is not addressed.**
  - First-cycle irreversibility only on the negative branch, a threshold-like drop in n beyond about −17 V (Fig. 2g), and suppression in later cycles are hallmarks of known back-gate physics in LAO/STO: electrons escape over the confining-potential edge into bulk or interfacial traps.
  - This picture, combined with the field-dependent permittivity of the quantum paraelectric, can reshape the confining potential. It can account for the persistent R_s/μ changes without invoking PNRs.
  - The relevant literature (e.g., Biscaras et al. and related works on STO back-gate hysteresis) is neither cited nor discussed.
- **"Correlation implies coupling" is weakly justified.**
  - Δ_L and ε are both monotonic functions of temperature, so a linear Δ_L–ε relation between 95 and 35 K (roughly ten points) does not by itself establish causation.
  - The steeper slope below 35 K could equally reflect stronger trap freezing or the strongly field-dependent ε(E) at low T.

**Reason 2 — The key superconducting claims ("progressive enhancement" and "complete erasure") are modest in magnitude and incompletely substantiated.**

- **"Complete erasure" is not demonstrated against the pristine state.**
  - Fig. 2i shows T_c moving from L₀ (post-gating) down to L₄, but never compares L₄ to the virgin, pre-gating transition at the same temperature and measurement conditions.
  - Pristine R_s is quoted at 1.4 K (~745 Ω), whereas the illuminated baseline is quoted at 10 K (795 Ω). The two cannot be compared.
  - The manuscript itself states that the system "does not fully recover to the pre-illumination state even at 180 K." The relation between "erasure by light" and "reset by thermal cycling to room temperature" is therefore unclear.
- **The effect size is small and robustness is not quantified.**
  - The T_c changes are about 0.05–0.07 K (≈5–7%) and are defined at the 50% R_n criterion, while R_n itself changes in the same protocols.
  - No alternative criteria (onset, 10/90%, zero resistance) or analyses of transition width, BKT temperature or H_c2 are provided. These are needed to show that the shift reflects pairing strength rather than changes in inhomogeneity or percolation of a broad transition.
  - Error bars and sample-to-sample statistics are absent.
- **The physical significance of the illumination-induced T_c reduction is under-explored.**
  - The paper presents "T_c decreases while μ and n increase" as counterintuitive. In KTO(111) interfaces, however, gating studies (Refs. 17, 18) report that T_c anticorrelates with mobility, with electrons confined closer to the interface.
  - The authors do not test whether T_c is simply a single-valued function of R_s or μ across both gating and illumination. Figs. 1e/f suggest T_c and R_s track almost linearly.
  - If T_c is single-valued in R_s or μ, the data are explained by changes in carrier distribution/confinement and disorder. The specific role of PNR "coherent polar fluctuations" would then be unnecessary.

**Reason 3 — Experimental controls and quantitative rigor are insufficient for a top-tier journal.**

- **Reproducibility and controls.**
  - All data appear to come from a single device. There is no statement on the number of samples, batch-to-batch variation or AlOₓ thickness.
  - Two controls would distinguish 2DEG-intrinsic from substrate-bulk mechanisms:
    - a non-superconducting or differently reduced KTO 2DEG;
    - a different orientation, e.g., (001), which is non-superconducting in this T range.
  - No retention-time measurements at base temperature and no endurance data over many cycles are given. Both are essential for the "non-volatile memory" claim.
- **Illumination conditions are poorly specified.**
  - Photon flux and intensity at the sample are unknown; the LED is driven at fixed V_L, while its I–V curve is acknowledged to be non-monotonic in T.
  - Heating is visible at 2 K (resistance jumps), but the sample-temperature excursion at 10 K is not quantified. This matters because both PPC and the claimed activation processes are strongly T-dependent.
  - Only one wavelength is used, so no spectral threshold is available to identify the defect levels involved.
- **Hall and transport analysis.**
  - n and μ come from single-band Hall analysis at ±6 T in separate runs, with nominally identical protocols repeated three times. The fractional changes are small (Δn/n ~1–2%, Δμ/μ ~4%), but run-to-run reproducibility and uncertainties are not reported.
  - Multiband conduction is expected for KTO(111) 2DEGs at n ~10¹⁴ cm⁻². It could make the extracted Δn and Δμ partly artefactual.
  - The van der Pauw-to-R_s conversion via FEM, and the possible anisotropy of KTO(111) transport (Ref. 16), should be documented.
- **Dielectric quantification and internal consistency.**
  - The measured ε ≈ 1600 at low T is well below typical bulk KTO values. The source of the discrepancy is not discussed (e.g., the 5 V ac excitation amplitude, geometry and fringe corrections, a dead layer, or the reduced region).
  - The capacitive charge expected from ε, the 0.5 mm thickness and the ±50 V sweep (of order 10¹² cm⁻² for ε ~1500) should be compared quantitatively with the Hall Δn. If there is a discrepancy, it is itself informative about trapping.
  - ε was measured only at zero dc bias, although ε(E) in KTO is strongly field-dependent within the ±50 V range used.

**Reason 4 — Overreaching framing and presentation issues weaken the manuscript.**

- **The neuromorphic/quantum-memristor framing is not supported by device-level metrics.**
  - Missing metrics include switching speed, energy per operation, number of distinguishable levels with error margins, retention, endurance and any superconducting-circuit relevant figure of merit.
  - Programming requires ±50 V on a 0.5 mm substrate and tens to thousands of seconds of illumination, which is far from practical operation.
- **Mechanistic narrative contains inconsistencies and loosely connected steps.**
  - **Type A/B dependence.** It is presented as an additional "sensitivity to cycling sequence." It appears simply to reflect which branch of the known hysteresis loop the sweep ends on, since Type B ends from −50 V, where R_s^{−0} > R_s^{+0}.
  - **Dark relaxation (35 K and 95 K).**
    - The account invokes "energetic preference for partially aligned PNRs and ionized OVs" in an "interfacial potential trap" without a model.
    - The non-monotonic τ(T) could arise from overlapping relaxations with different amplitudes. A two-component fit would test this.
  - **"Electrons tunnel back to deep donors" beyond −17 V.** This is asserted rather than tested, for example by its temperature or sweep-rate dependence.
- **Writing and figure errors.**
  - A duplicated sentence appears in the Discussion ("The interactions are further reinforced… Dielectric loss signatures further reinforce…").
  - Supp. Note 4 labels the elevated value as "R_s^b = 856 Ω" when R_s^a is meant.
  - "Extended Figure 5" is used versus "Supplementary Figure 5".
  - Fig. 3 caption ("deviates above") versus main text ("steeper dependence").
  - Misused terms: "variance" for "variation"; "illlumination", "memrisitive", "the it is", "would facilitates".
  - Relevant prior back-gate hysteresis literature is missing.
  - A clearer statement of how the present results go beyond Ref. 30 is needed.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Directly probe the proposed lattice degrees of freedom.**
   - Measure ε(V_g) and tan δ(V_g) loops (C–V butterfly) at 5–50 K, before and after gate cycling and after illumination. This would test for remnant polarization and whether light erases it.
   - Complement with pyroelectric or thermally stimulated depolarization current, or SHG/Raman where feasible.
   - Characterize substrate impurities (SIMS/ICP for Li, Fe, Na).
   - Measure dielectric response on an identical, un-reduced KTO substrate to separate bulk-substrate relaxations from those tied to the reduced interfacial layer.
   - Report the attempt frequencies.
   - Explicitly discuss and test the Maxwell–Wagner and trap-escape (Biscaras-type) alternatives, e.g., through sweep-rate, holding-time and temperature dependence of the first-cycle irreversibility.
2. **Strengthen the superconducting evidence.**
   - Compare L₄ (and the fully illuminated state) with the virgin and room-temperature-reset states at identical T, showing full R(T) curves.
   - Use several T_c criteria, transition widths, and H_c2(T) or BKT analysis.
   - Plot T_c against R_s, μ and n for all states generated by both gating and illumination. If T_c collapses onto a single curve, discuss what this implies for the PNR-based pairing interpretation. If it does not, that would genuinely support an additional lattice contribution.
3. **Improve experimental rigor and reproducibility.**
   - Show the key results on at least two or three devices, ideally including a different AlOₓ/reduction condition or crystal orientation as control.
   - Quantify incident photon flux, sample-temperature rise during illumination, and wavelength dependence (e.g., several LEDs spanning the near-IR to blue). This would identify the defect levels responsible for erasure.
   - Provide error bars for the Hall-derived n and μ, a two-band analysis if the Hall response is nonlinear in B, and cross-run reproducibility.
   - Reconcile the measured ε and the capacitive charge estimate with the observed Δn.
4. **Recalibrate claims and polish the manuscript.**
   - Clearly delineate novelty relative to Ref. 30 and prior back-gate hysteresis and PPC work in STO/KTO.
   - Replace "complete erasure" with a quantitatively supported statement.
   - Reframe the Type A/B result as branch-dependent readout of the hysteresis loop.
   - Either add retention, endurance and multi-level statistics or tone down the neuromorphic and quantum-memristor framing.
   - Develop at least a minimal quantitative model (e.g., rate equations for trap/OV occupancy coupled to a Schrödinger–Poisson confinement profile with field-dependent ε) that reproduces the sign and magnitude of the ΔR_s, Δn and Δμ changes.
   - Correct the textual, notational and caption inconsistencies listed above.
