**Review outline:**

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic.** The manuscript reports two coupled phenomena in back-gated AlOₓ/KTaO₃(111) 2DEGs:
  - repeated ±50 V back-gate cycling at cryogenic temperature progressively raises T_c (≈1.02 K → ≈1.09 K at V_g = 0) and R_s (≈5%), converging after 3–4 cycles to a stable hysteresis loop;
  - sub-gap red illumination (630 nm, 1.97 eV) at 10 K lowers R_s back to a baseline and reduces T_c, which the authors call an "erasure" of the gate-imprinted memory.
- **Proposed mechanism.** Two dielectric-loss processes are attributed to distinct excitations and are said to govern the memory:
  - Peak 1 (~30 K, E_a ≈ 76 meV): polar-nanoregion (PNR) reorientation;
  - Peak 2 (~100 K, E_a ≈ 236 meV): oxygen-vacancy (OV) ionization.
- **Supporting correlations.**
  - The loop size Δ_L scales linearly with ε between 95 K and 35 K and more steeply below 35 K.
  - Dark-relaxation amplitude and time constant show anomalies at 35 K and 95 K.
- **Potential significance.** Non-volatile, optically resettable and electrically writable control of an interfacial superconductor is attractive. Linking the quantum-paraelectric lattice (PNRs, soft TO mode) to the interfacial pairing would be of broad interest for KTO/STO superconductivity.
- **Novelty assessment: incremental.**
  - Gate-cycling enhancement of T_c that is recovered only at room temperature has recently been reported as "universal" at oxide interfaces (Ref. 30).
  - Forming and hysteresis effects under back-gating of oxide 2DEGs are well documented in LaAlO₃/SrTiO₃, typically attributed to electron trapping and defect migration.
  - Persistent photoconductivity in reduced KTO and STO is known.
  - Glassy, PNR-related electron dynamics in electron-doped KTO were reported in Ref. 43.
  - The genuinely new elements are the optical reset of the gate-induced T_c enhancement and the attempt to assign the two memory channels to specific dielectric relaxations. The latter is currently supported mainly by temperature coincidences rather than direct evidence.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Clear, reproducible phenomenology at 10 K.**
  - Illumination and gate-cycle sequences switch R_s between well-defined levels (795 / 840 / 856 Ω).
  - These levels are tunable by illumination duration and gate amplitude (Supp. Note 4).
  - They are stable through thermal excursions to the superconducting state (Fig. 2h).
- **Effect extends into the superconducting state.** The work goes beyond normal-state transport:
  - stepwise illumination produces stepwise T_c reductions (Fig. 2i);
  - this gives an all-in-situ, two-knob (electrical write, optical erase) control of T_c, appealing for oxide superconducting devices.
- **Multi-probe dataset.** The study combines:
  - R_s, Hall n and μ under identical protocols;
  - gate-current transients;
  - frequency-dependent dielectric spectroscopy (0.1 Hz – 4 kHz);
  - temperature-resolved dark relaxation (10–180 K).
  This breadth is uncommon for KTO interfaces and could constrain models.
- **Directionality of the memory.** The observation that the final R_s depends on cycle order (Type A vs Type B) is non-trivial. It suggests the memory carries directional (polarity-dependent) information, not just a cumulative "forming" effect.
- **Physically interesting hypothesis.** The proposal that coherent polar fluctuations, rather than carrier density or mobility, control T_c is a worthwhile hypothesis that the community is actively debating (Refs. 44, 48, 49).

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**Reason 1: The microscopic attribution to PNRs and OV charge states is inferential, and plausible alternative explanations are not excluded.**
- **No direct evidence of remnant polarization or PNR alignment.**
  - No polarization–field loop, bias-dependent capacitance (C–V) hysteresis, thermally stimulated depolarization current, or structural/optical probe of polarity (SHG, Raman) is presented. The "remnant polarization as V_g crosses zero" remains a cartoon (Fig. 3e).
  - The dielectric spectra are measured only in a single (unspecified) state of the sample. The authors never show that tan δ peaks, ε, or the relaxation strength change between gate-cycled and illuminated states. That comparison would be the minimal test that these excitations carry the memory.
- **The dielectric measurement does not isolate the interface.**
  - It probes the full 0.5 mm KTO slab between the back gate and the grounded 2DEG, whereas the 2DEG couples only to the near-interface region (nanometres to tens of nanometres).
  - A conducting, OV-rich surface layer on an insulating bulk is a textbook Maxwell–Wagner (interfacial polarization) configuration. This produces thermally activated loss peaks whose apparent activation energy reflects the conductivity of the defect-rich layer, not dipole reorientation.
  - Assigning Peak 1 to PNRs "as known from the literature" is insufficient. In nominally pure KTO, low-temperature loss peaks have also been attributed to off-centre impurities (Li, Fe, Nb) and polaronic hopping.
  - Attempt frequencies ω₁, ω₂, their uncertainties, and the peak-fitting procedure are not reported. The Peak 2 arrows in Fig. 3b sit on weak, noisy features, and the Arrhenius fit spans a narrow temperature window.
- **A simpler explanation of the T_c changes is not tested.**
  - In all data shown, higher T_c accompanies higher R_s and lower μ: gate cycling raises R_s, lowers μ, and raises T_c; illumination does the reverse.
  - A T_c–μ (or T_c–disorder) anticorrelation has already been reported for gated KTO(111) interfaces (Refs. 17, 18).
  - If all gate- and light-prepared states fall on a single T_c(μ) or T_c(R_s) curve, then invoking PNR-mediated pairing is unnecessary. OV charge-state changes alone, a well-established trap-charging picture, would explain both effects.
  - The statement that the T_c reduction "apparently contradicts the expected trend" presupposes that higher n or μ should raise T_c, which is not established for this system.

**Reason 2: The key correlations (Δ_L vs ε; 35 K / 95 K coincidences) are weak evidence and partly internally inconsistent.**
- **The Δ_L–ε linearity is not discriminating.**
  - Both Δ_L and ε are monotonic functions of temperature, so a parametric plot of one against the other will be smooth for almost any mechanism. Only about 15 points define the "linear" and "steeper" regimes.
  - No fit statistics or error bars are shown, and no comparison is made with Δ_L plotted against other monotonic variables (e.g., 1/T or thermally activated rates).
  - The physical model behind Δ_L ∝ (ε − ε_∞) — "larger ε gives a stronger displacement field" — is not derived. For fixed V_g across a 0.5 mm slab, the induced interface charge scales as εV_g/d. This is a statement about gate efficiency, not about OV charge exchange specifically.
- **The "coincidence" temperatures are frequency-dependent but treated as fixed.**
  - The dielectric peaks shift strongly with frequency: Peak 1 spans roughly 25–45 K and Peak 2 roughly 80–140 K over the measured band.
  - Gate sweeps and dark relaxation (τ ~ 10³ s) probe effective frequencies of order 10⁻³ Hz.
  - Extrapolating the reported Arrhenius laws with plausible prefactors to such frequencies gives roughly 25 K and 80 K, not 35 K and 95 K. The authors must show quantitatively, with the fitted ω₀, that the transport anomalies match the relaxation rates at the relevant timescales.
- **The dark-relaxation anomalies rest on single data points with uncontrolled conditions.**
  - The maxima in ΔR/R₁ (Fig. 4b) at 35 K and 95 K are each defined by essentially one point, with coarse temperature sampling and no repeat runs.
  - The Methods state that the LED I–V characteristic is non-monotonically temperature dependent and the photon flux is not constant across temperature. The illumination dose, and hence the starting state, therefore differs between temperatures.
  - The 95 K trace in Fig. 4a is visibly noisier and non-monotonic during illumination, raising the possibility of temperature instability or heating artefacts.
  - A single-exponential fit to what are likely stretched or multi-component relaxations makes τ and ΔR fit-model dependent.

**Reason 3: The charge-transfer model is internally inconsistent with the transport data and lacks quantitative charge accounting.**
- **Contradictory statements on carrier density and gating direction.**
  - Supp. Fig. 1c shows the Hall density *higher* at V_g = −50 V (≈1.085×10¹⁴ cm⁻²) than at +50 V (≈1.035×10¹⁴ cm⁻²). This is opposite to the naive field-effect expectation for a back gate and is not discussed.
  - Supp. Note 1 states that n *increases* over the initial cycles, while Fig. 2f–g and the main text state that gate cycles *decrease* n. These may correspond to different starting states (virgin vs illuminated), but this is not made explicit.
  - The Discussion says that in the negative branch "electrons hop from the OVs back into the 2DEG", which should increase n. Yet Fig. 2g shows n dropping rapidly beyond −17 V, attributed to electrons "tunnelling back into deep donor states". The same branch is thus invoked for opposite charge flows.
- **No charge bookkeeping.**
  - The gate-current transient of ~10 nA decaying over minutes (Supp. Fig. 3a) corresponds to an integrated charge that is plausibly of order 10¹³ cm⁻² or more over the 6.25 mm² sample. The Hall-measured change in n is only a few ×10¹² cm⁻².
  - Without integrating I_g and comparing it with Δn, it is impossible to tell where the displaced charge resides: deep traps, shallow OVs, the AlOₓ layer or its interface, or leakage.
  - Leakage through the substrate and contacts is not separated from displacement current.
- **Hall analysis is oversimplified and runs are not concurrent.**
  - KTO(111) 2DEGs are multi-band, so a single-band n = 1/(eR_H) at ±6 T can conflate changes in band occupation and mobility ratio with changes in total density. The percent-level Δn and Δμ on which the mechanism relies may be artefacts of this conversion.
  - n and μ come from separate runs (B = 0, +6 T, −6 T) "following the same procedure". Given the history dependence the paper itself emphasizes, run-to-run reproducibility of the starting state must be demonstrated.

**Reason 4: Novelty, magnitude and generality are overstated relative to the evidence.**
- **Overlap with prior work is insufficiently differentiated.**
  - Ref. 30 already reports gate-cycling-enhanced superconductivity, recovered only at room temperature, and links it to ferroelectricity at oxide interfaces, including KTO. The manuscript cites it only in passing and does not explain what is mechanistically new.
  - Extensive LAO/STO literature on irreversible back-gate forming, electron trapping above the Fermi level, and optical reset is not discussed.
  - The claim of a "previously unrecognized interplay" is therefore not justified as written.
- **"Complete erasure" is not demonstrated.**
  - Fig. 2i shows a progressive T_c decrease from L₀ to L₄, but never compares L₄ with the pristine (pre-gating) transition.
  - The authors themselves note that the system "does not fully recover to the pre-illumination state" in dark relaxation, and that warming to room temperature is the established full reset.
  - Rapid T_c changes of ~30–70 mK and R_s changes of ~5% are modest, and the T_c definition (50% of R_N) is not complemented by BKT analysis or critical-field data.
- **Device and neuromorphic claims are not supported by the data.**
  - Writing requires ±50 V across a 0.5 mm substrate, and erasing requires illumination at about 10 K. Light cannot be applied in the superconducting state because of heating (Fig. 2b, 2 K).
  - Retention, endurance and cycle-to-cycle statistics are not given.
  - Only one sample appears to be measured; the AlOₓ thickness, sample count and reproducibility are not stated.
  - "Superconducting neuromorphic elements" and "quantum memristors" are therefore aspirational, not demonstrated.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Directly test the PNR and OV assignments.**
   - Measure ε and tan δ (including DC-bias C–V hysteresis) *in situ* after each protocol: virgin, gate-cycled Type A, gate-cycled Type B, and illuminated. Show that the relaxation strengths of Peaks 1 and 2 track the memory states.
   - Perform thermally stimulated depolarization current measurements after poling at ±50 V. These would directly reveal remnant polarization and its release temperatures, expected near 35 K and 95 K if the model is right.
   - Model or exclude Maxwell–Wagner contributions, e.g., by comparing with an unreduced KTO(111) substrate with identical electrodes and with samples of different 2DEG sheet conductance. Report ω₀ and uncertainties for both Arrhenius fits.
   - Measure the wavelength and power dependence of the erasure. Include a control in which the sample is heated to the LED-induced temperature in the dark, to separate optical excitation from thermal annealing.
   - If possible, add an interface-sensitive polar probe such as optical SHG or Raman of the TO mode under gate.

2. **Disentangle "disorder/mobility" from "polar fluctuation" control of T_c.**
   - Plot T_c against μ, n and R_s for *all* prepared states: gate-swept, cycled, partially illuminated (L₁–L₄) and dark-relaxed. Check whether a single curve describes them.
   - Prepare states with matched R_s, n and μ by different routes (light vs gate). If their T_c values differ, the PNR hypothesis is strengthened; if not, it should be withdrawn or downgraded.
   - Exploit the proposed 35 K / 95 K thresholds:
     - cycle at 10 K, warm to about 50 K (above T_p1, below T_p2), recool, and measure which part of the T_c and R_s memory survives;
     - repeat with warming to about 120 K.
     These selective-erasure experiments would be far more compelling than the current parametric correlations.
   - Include the pristine transition in Fig. 2i, and add H_c2 or BKT analysis to substantiate the T_c shifts.

3. **Make the model quantitative and internally consistent.**
   - Integrate I_g over each sweep and hold period, subtract measured leakage, and compare the displaced charge with Δn from Hall data. Specify where each charge population resides.
   - Reconcile the sign of Δn under negative gating (Supp. Fig. 1c vs Fig. 2g) and the contradictory electron-flow statements in the Discussion.
   - Use multi-band (two-carrier) fits of field-dependent Hall data rather than single-field n and μ.
   - Extrapolate the dielectric Arrhenius laws to the effective frequencies of gate sweeps and dark relaxation, and compare the predicted temperatures with the observed 35 K and 95 K anomalies.
   - Derive the expected relation between Δ_L and ε explicitly, rather than asserting proportionality.
   - Fit the relaxations with stretched-exponential or two-component forms and report parameter uncertainties.
   - Repeat the temperature-dependent relaxation series with constant, calibrated photon dose, denser temperature sampling around 35 K and 95 K, and repeated runs.

4. **Strengthen reproducibility, context and presentation.**
   - State the number of samples, the AlOₓ (Al) thickness, the LED optical power at the sample, and the dielectric-measurement geometry (2DEG area vs gate area, fringe corrections). Show the key effects in at least one additional device.
   - Provide retention-time, endurance and cycle-to-cycle variance data, plus the minimal gate amplitude or dose needed to write and erase.
   - Expand the comparison with Ref. 30, LAO/STO back-gate trapping studies, PPC in reduced KTO, and Ref. 43. Clearly delineate what is new.
   - Temper the claims of "complete erasure", "previously unrecognized" and "neuromorphic" functionality.
   - Fix inconsistencies and errors, including:
     - the R_s^b = 856 Ω mislabel in Supp. Note 4 item 2 (it should be the elevated value);
     - the duplicated sentence in the first Discussion paragraph;
     - "Extended Figure 5" vs "Supplementary Figure 5";
     - the "deviates above" wording in the Fig. 3d caption;
     - typos ("memrisitive", "illlumination", "Suppelementary", "would facilitates").
   - Clarify the Fig. 3a overlay (which frequency ε is plotted against Δ_L).
