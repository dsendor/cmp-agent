Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic.** The manuscript addresses non-volatile, reconfigurable control of interfacial superconductivity in AlOₓ/KTaO₃(111). This is timely because KTO interfaces combine strong spin–orbit coupling, a ~1–2 K superconducting 2DEG and a quantum-paraelectric host.
- **Three main claims:**
  - Back-gate cycling (±50 V) progressively raises T_c (from ~1.02 K to ~1.09 K) and R_s, after which both settle into stable hysteretic loops.
  - Sub-gap red illumination (1.97 eV) at 10 K resets R_s to a baseline and lowers T_c. The authors describe this as "complete erasure."
  - Two dielectric-loss relaxations (E_a ≈ 76 meV near 30 K; E_a ≈ 236 meV near 100 K) are assigned to polar-nanoregion (PNR) reorientation and oxygen-vacancy (OV) ionization. They are argued to correlate with the R_s hysteresis amplitude Δ_L and with dark-relaxation kinetics.
- **Novelty relative to prior work:**
  - Gate-cycling-induced T_c enhancement at cryogenic temperatures is already reported as "universal" in Ref. 30 (Zhang et al., arXiv 2506.16298), together with ferroelectric-like behaviour.
  - Hysteretic back-gating of KTO(111) appears in Refs. 17–18.
  - Persistent photoconductivity and light-induced transport changes in KTO 2DEGs are known (Refs. 31, 32, 45).
  - The genuinely new elements are therefore:
    - the optical reset of a gate-imprinted T_c state;
    - the attempt to link both to specific lattice excitations through dielectric spectroscopy measured on the same device.
- **Overall assessment.** The phenomenology is interesting and potentially useful for defect- and polarization-engineered oxide superconductivity. However:
  - the modulation of T_c is small (~5–7%, smaller than the transition width);
  - the microscopic attribution is largely inferential.
- **Broader framing.** The framing toward "superconducting neuromorphic elements" is speculative at present.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Clear, reproducible phenomenology.**
  - The illumination/gate-cycle switching of R_s is repeated many times (Fig. 2c; Supp. Fig. 4).
  - The switching is controllable by illumination time and gate amplitude.
  - It depends on sweep order (Type A vs. Type B), which is a nontrivial fingerprint of an internal state variable.
- **Optical reset of T_c.**
  - Fig. 2h,i shows stepwise, non-volatile reductions of T_c that survive thermal cycling between 10 K and below 1 K.
  - This demonstrates two independent, opposing control knobs (electrostatic vs. optical) acting on the superconducting state.
- **Multi-probe approach on one device.** Transport, Hall, gate-current displacement and dielectric loss are all measured on the same sample. This provides internally consistent temperature scales: ~35 K and ~95 K appear in tan δ, in Δ_L vs. ε, in the I_g asymmetry and in the dark-relaxation parameters.
- **Physically plausible scenario.**
  - The proposed picture of OV charge-state switching (scattering) plus PNR alignment (internal gating) is relevant to the broader debate on soft-mode/polar-fluctuation-mediated pairing in SrTiO₃ and KTaO₃ (Refs. 44, 48, 49).
  - Experimental constraints of this kind are valuable to that debate.
- **Stability of the photo-reset state.** The states are stable over hours at 10 K and are only partially recovered even at 180 K. This is a practical point for device-oriented studies.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**(i) Overstated claims and limited magnitude of the effect.**
- **Size of the T_c change.**
  - The T_c enhancement is ~50–80 mK out of ~1.0–1.1 K (Fig. 1e; Supp. Fig. 1a,e).
  - This is a fraction of the ~0.2–0.3 K transition width (Fig. 1c,d; Fig. 2i).
  - T_c is defined at 50% of R_n, while R_n itself shifts by ~5–8% across gate/illumination states. Part of the apparent shift may therefore reflect normal-state normalization rather than a change in pairing.
  - Onset, zero-resistance and normalized-curve analyses are not presented.
- **"Complete erasure" is not demonstrated.**
  - The manuscript never shows that illumination returns T_c (or R_s at the same temperature) to the pristine, pre-gating value.
  - Fig. 1f's origin (~0.745 kΩ at 1.4 K) and the 795 Ω baseline at 10 K are measured at different temperatures and cannot be compared.
  - In Fig. 2i, the L₄ curve is only shown relative to L₀, an already-cycled state.
  - The Discussion itself states that the system "does not fully recover" even at 180 K.
- **Device relevance is not demonstrated.**
  - Erasure requires illumination at 10 K, i.e. warming out of the superconducting state.
  - The gate operates through a 0.5 mm substrate at ±50 V.
  - Neither "memristive" nor "neuromorphic" functionality (analog weights, endurance, retention statistics, switching energy) is demonstrated in the superconducting state.
  - The abstract and conclusions thus extrapolate well beyond the data.
- **Single device.**
  - All data appear to come from one sample.
  - Sample-to-sample reproducibility, dependence on AlOₓ thickness or reduction conditions, and comparison with other orientations or capping layers are absent.

**(ii) The microscopic attribution to PNRs and OVs is not directly established; simpler explanations are not excluded.**
- **No direct evidence of PNR alignment or remnant polarization.**
  - There are no P–E loops, PFM/SHG, Raman/IR soft-mode or structural probes.
  - The key statement that "superconductivity and PNR alignment are strengthened by gate cycling and suppressed by illumination" is asserted rather than measured.
- **A simpler empirical correlation is not tested.**
  - Across all states, higher T_c accompanies higher R_s and lower μ (Fig. 1e,f; Supp. Fig. 1; Fig. 2).
  - Prior KTO(111) gating work (Refs. 17, 18, cited in the Introduction) found that gating mainly modulates mobility and that T_c tracks mobility/disorder rather than density.
  - If T_c vs. μ (or vs. R_s, k_F·l) collapses onto a single curve for both gate-cycled and illuminated states, the PNR-mediated pairing picture is unnecessary.
  - Calling the illumination-induced T_c drop "counterintuitive" presupposes a density-controlled T_c that the authors' own Introduction contradicts.
- **The density response has the opposite sign to a conventional field effect and is not discussed.**
  - In Supp. Fig. 1c, n is higher at V_g = −50 V (~1.08×10¹⁴ cm⁻²) than at +50 V (~1.035×10¹⁴ cm⁻²).
  - Δn ≈ 4–5×10¹² cm⁻² over the sweep, which is also several times larger than the capacitive estimate ε₀εV/d. With ε ≈ 1500 and d = 0.5 mm, this gives ~10¹² cm⁻² for ±50 V.
  - Fig. 2g, by contrast, shows n dropping beyond −17 V after illumination.
  - These sign and magnitude inconsistencies are central to any charge-transfer model and need explicit treatment.
- **Hall analysis may be unreliable.**
  - Single-band Hall analysis at ±6 T in a multi-band KTO(111) 2DEG may not separate n and μ correctly when relative band occupations change.
  - The reported changes are only ~1–5%.
  - The data come from three separate protocol repetitions (0, ±6 T), without error bars or a demonstrated run-to-run reproducibility level.

**(iii) The dielectric analysis and its correlation with transport are not robust enough to carry the mechanism.**
- **The measurement averages the whole substrate.**
  - The capacitance is taken across the 0.5 mm substrate between a Au back electrode and the 2DEG. It therefore reflects a bulk-averaged response plus any interfacial layers, not the near-interface region where PNRs/OVs couple to the 2DEG.
  - The extracted ε (~1500–2000 at low T) is well below bulk KTO values (~4000–4500). This suggests series dead layers or depletion regions and/or geometric/fringe errors.
  - Series layers of this kind would directly distort tan δ.
- **Maxwell–Wagner artefacts are not excluded.**
  - Thermally activated, frequency-shifting loss peaks are the classic signature of space-charge/interfacial (Maxwell–Wagner) relaxation. Possible sources include the Au/KTO Schottky contact, the depletion layer under the 2DEG, the AlOₓ, and hopping of carriers among defects.
  - These alternatives are not ruled out; the analysis also neglects the finite 2DEG sheet resistance (~800 Ω) and the wiring.
- **Peak identification and fitting are weak.**
  - Peak positions in Fig. 3b (especially Peak 2) are marked on weak, sloped, partly noisy curves.
  - The Arrhenius fits (Fig. 3c) lack uncertainties for T_p, E_a and the attempt frequencies.
  - The assignment of Peak 2 to OV ionization rests solely on a numerical match to literature (200–300 meV), and Peak 1 likewise on literature assignment.
- **The Δ_L–ε correlation is weak evidence.**
  - Δ_L and ε are both monotonic functions of temperature, so a near-linear parametric plot (Fig. 3d) is weak evidence of causal coupling.
  - The identification of a "deviation" at 35 K and an "onset" at 95 K depends on a handful of points, where Δ_L ≈ 0 near 95 K.
  - The sweep-rate dependence of Δ_L, essential for a kinetic interpretation, is not provided.

**(iv) Internal inconsistencies in the dark-relaxation analysis and in the presentation.**
- **The τ data contradict the text.**
  - The text claims "two minima in τ" at 35 K and 95 K (Fig. 4c).
  - The plotted data show τ minima near ~30–35 K and ~85–90 K, while 95 K is a sharp local *maximum* (~1.38×10³ s).
  - Adjacent points jump by ~50% (e.g. 90 K → 95 K), suggesting that scatter or fit instability dominates.
  - No error bars are given, and the single-exponential form is not justified against stretched-exponential alternatives typical of glassy PPC.
- **The ΔR/R₁ maxima are poorly resolved.** The maxima at 35 K and 95 K (Fig. 4b) each rest on essentially a single data point.
- **Illumination dose and heating are uncontrolled.**
  - The Methods acknowledge that LED flux is not constant with temperature (constant V_L, non-monotonic I–V).
  - The temperature dependence of relaxation magnitude and kinetics may therefore partly reflect varying illumination dose and LED heating (visible at 2 K in Fig. 2b).
- **Presentation errors:**
  - A duplicated sentence in the Discussion.
  - In Supp. Note 4 (group 2), the elevated value is mislabelled R_s^b = 856 Ω (it should be the gate-elevated value).
  - The Fig. 3d caption ("deviates above") is inconsistent with the main text.
  - The Fig. 3 caption places TO softening below 35 K, whereas ε rises down to ~10 K.
  - "Extended Figure 5" is used versus "Supplementary Figure 5."
  - Typos: "memrisitive," "illlumination," "Suppelementary."
  - Sweep rates, hold times and gate-leakage levels are missing from the main-text protocols.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Establish the magnitude, completeness and reproducibility of the effect.**
   - Report T_c using onset, midpoint and zero-resistance criteria, and normalized R/R_n curves, with error estimates.
   - Show explicitly whether optical reset returns the system to the pristine, pre-gating state, measured at the same temperature.
   - Provide data from at least 2–3 independent devices, including at least one with a different AlOₓ thickness or reduction condition.
   - Quantify retention time, number of write/erase cycles and dose (photon fluence) dependence with a calibrated, temperature-independent light source, e.g. fiber-coupled from room temperature.
   - Tone down neuromorphic/memristor claims unless corresponding metrics are demonstrated.

2. **Test simpler phenomenological explanations before invoking PNR-mediated pairing.**
   - Plot T_c against μ, R_s, n and k_F·l for *all* gate-cycled and illuminated states together. Discuss whether a single disorder/mobility scaling, as suggested by Refs. 17, 18, accounts for the data.
   - Perform multi-band (two-carrier) fits of field-dependent Hall and magnetoresistance data rather than single-field Hall.
   - Explicitly address the anomalous sign and magnitude of Δn(V_g) in Supp. Fig. 1c relative to the capacitive estimate, and reconcile it with Fig. 2g.
   - Add perpendicular/parallel H_c2 data, from which coherence length and possible changes in 2D thickness can be extracted. This would help distinguish pairing changes from confinement or disorder changes.

3. **Strengthen the dielectric evidence and exclude artefacts.**
   - Present an equivalent-circuit or full impedance (Cole–Cole) analysis that includes the 2DEG resistance, contact/Schottky layers and any series dead layer. Explain why the extracted ε is below bulk KTO.
   - Measure a reference bare KTO(111) substrate (no 2DEG, symmetric metal electrodes) and a strongly reduced/non-superconducting sample. This would determine whether Peaks 1 and 2 are intrinsic to the lattice or interface-related.
   - Measure tan δ and ε under dc gate bias and after illumination. If the peaks reflect PNRs and OVs relevant to the memory, they should change with the imprinted state.
   - Where feasible, add a direct polarization probe (low-T SHG, PFM through thin AlOₓ, or remnant-polarization/pyroelectric current) and an OV charge-state probe (photoluminescence, EPR, or thermally stimulated current), to substantiate the assignments.
   - Provide uncertainties for all Arrhenius parameters.

4. **Make the kinetic analysis quantitative and internally consistent.**
   - Correct the τ(T) description to match Fig. 4c, add fit uncertainties, and compare single- versus stretched-exponential fits.
   - Increase temperature sampling around 30–40 K and 85–100 K so that the claimed features rest on more than single points.
   - Measure Δ_L as a function of sweep rate and hold time. Measure dark relaxation after a controlled, equal photon dose at each temperature.
   - Correlate the kinetics with the dielectric relaxation times τ = 1/ω(T) extracted from Fig. 3c, so that common microscopic timescales are compared quantitatively rather than only by coincident temperatures.
   - Fix the presentation issues listed in 3(iv): the duplicated sentence, mislabelled quantities, caption inconsistencies and typos.
   - Report all sweep rates, hold times and gate-leakage levels, and position the work more carefully relative to Ref. 30 and prior hysteretic gating studies on KTO.
