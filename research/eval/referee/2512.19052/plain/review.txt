Referee report

Manuscript: "Gate-imprinted memory and light-induced erasure of superconductivity at KTaO3-based interfaces"

1. Summary of the submission

The authors study a superconducting 2DEG at an AlOx/KTaO3(111) interface made by Al-induced surface reduction. A single back-gated 2.5 x 2.5 x 0.5 mm^3 van der Pauw device is examined. They report:
(a) at low temperature, repeated back-gate cycling between +/-50 V makes Tc (50% criterion) and the normal-state Rs drift upward over the first three to four cycles before they settle into closed hysteresis loops. Tc rises from about 1.02 K to about 1.09 K at Vg = 0, and the loop amplitude Delta_L falls with temperature;
(b) red (630 nm, 1.97 eV) LED illumination at 10 K produces persistent photoconductivity that lowers Rs, raises the Hall density and mobility, and moves the superconducting transition back down. The authors call this "complete erasure" of the gate-imprinted state. Alternating illumination and gate cycles switches Rs between roughly 795 and 840 to 856 Ohm/sq;
(c) gate-current dielectric spectroscopy of the substrate shows two Arrhenius loss peaks, at about 30 K (76 meV) and about 100 K (236 meV), assigned to polar-nanoregion (PNR) reorientation and oxygen-vacancy (OV) charge-state change;
(d) Delta_L scales linearly with epsilon between about 95 K and 35 K and rises more steeply below 35 K;
(e) after illumination, the dark relaxation of Rs shows maxima in amplitude and minima in time constant near 35 K and 95 K.
From these observations the authors build a microscopic picture: PNR alignment and OV ionization near the interface control the 2DEG, and superconductivity is strengthened by PNR alignment. They then motivate superconducting memristive and neuromorphic applications.

2. Overall assessment

The phenomenology is interesting and largely new. An optical reset of a gate-history state that includes Tc, in a KTaO3 interface superconductor, is worth reporting, and the data in Figs. 1, 2 and Supplementary Fig. 4 look clean. My main concern is the interpretation. The microscopic attribution to PNRs and OV charge states rests on correlations in temperature between a bulk dielectric measurement and transport quantities, and these correlations do not survive simple quantitative checks (see M2 to M4). A simpler explanation, based on the Tc-mobility relation already established in KTaO3(111) and on ordinary charge trapping near the interface, is not considered (M1). Several headline words ("complete erasure", "non-volatile superconductivity", "previously unrecognized") go beyond what the data show (M5, M6). All results come from what appears to be one device, with no control samples (M7). I recommend major revision. The phenomenology could support a solid paper, but the mechanism section needs to be substantially reworked or presented as a hypothesis.

3. Major issues

M1. Tc tracks mobility throughout, which suggests a more economical explanation.
In every protocol in the paper, Tc moves opposite to mobility:
- within a stable gate loop, Tc is highest at -50 V, where mu is lowest and Rs highest (Fig. 1e,f; Supp. Fig. 1a,d);
- over the history drift, Tc rises as mu falls from about 79 to about 75 cm^2/Vs (Supp. Fig. 1e,h);
- under illumination, mu rises and Tc falls (Figs. 2d, 2i).
The Discussion calls the illumination result "counterintuitive", but an inverse Tc-mobility relation is what earlier KTaO3(111) gating work has reported (the authors' refs. 17 and 18). There it is usually linked to how closely the electron distribution is confined at the interface and to its coupling with the soft TO mode. On that reading, gate history and light both act by moving trapped charge near the interface, which changes confinement and therefore mu and Tc together, with no need for PNR alignment. I ask the authors to plot Tc against mu (and against Rs) for every state in the paper: pristine, every loop branch, L0 to L4, and the post-illumination baselines. If these fall on one curve, the "superconducting memory" is a mobility or confinement memory, and the paper should say so. If they do not, the deviation is the real evidence for an extra lattice contribution and should be the centerpiece.

M2. The time scales of the claimed dielectric processes do not match the transport time scales.
Using the reported activation energies and the peak positions in Fig. 3c, the attempt frequencies are of order 1e11 to 1e12 Hz for both peaks (the authors should report the fitted omega_1 and omega_2). With these parameters, a single process with Ea = 76 meV has a relaxation time of roughly 1e-2 to 1e-1 s at 35 K. A process with Ea = 236 meV has roughly 1 s at 95 K. The dark relaxation times in Fig. 4c are about 1e3 s, three to five orders of magnitude longer. Gate sweeps take hundreds of seconds or more, corresponding to an effective frequency near 1e-3 Hz. Extrapolating the Arrhenius laws to that frequency puts the loss maxima near 26 K and 80 K rather than 35 K and 95 K. So the "coincidence" of the transport features with Tp1 and Tp2 depends on comparing quantities measured at very different frequencies, and the processes probed in transport are not quantitatively the ones seen in tan delta. The authors should either show a model in which the dielectric relaxations set the transport time scale, or weaken the identification.

M3. The dielectric measurement probes the bulk substrate, and alternative origins of the loss peaks are not excluded.
- The capacitor is the full 0.5 mm substrate between the 2DEG and the back electrode, so epsilon and tan delta are dominated by bulk KTaO3, not the interfacial region the 2DEG samples. That limits how far the results can be used to describe interfacial PNRs or OVs.
- In KTaO3, loss peaks with Ea near 80 meV are classically associated with off-center impurity dipoles such as Li+ (refs. 37, 42). Peaks in the 0.2 to 0.3 eV range in electrode/substrate structures are also commonly Maxwell-Wagner or space-charge relaxations. Here one electrode is a 2DEG whose depletion region and sheet resistance depend on temperature and history. A control with the same substrate batch, evaporated metal electrodes and no 2DEG, and ideally a thickness dependence, would distinguish bulk-defect, interfacial and Maxwell-Wagner origins.
- A 5 V excitation across 0.5 mm is about 100 V/cm. That is not obviously in the linear-response regime for KTaO3 at low temperature, and it is not small compared with the gate fields that produce the memory. Please show that epsilon and tan delta do not depend on amplitude, and that the measurement itself does not disturb the imprinted state.
- epsilon saturates near 2300 at low temperature, well below the usual bulk KTaO3 values. This suggests the effective area or thickness differs from the nominal values, for example through fringing or a 2DEG electrode smaller than the gate. Please comment.
- The loss "peaks" in Fig. 3b are weak features on a background, especially Peak 1 and the noisy 0.1 Hz trace. Peak temperatures need uncertainties, and the Arrhenius fits need error bars.
- The 2DEG series resistance (a few kOhm) and C (about 0.2 nF) give omega*R*C of about 1e-2 at the highest frequency. That is comparable to the measured tan delta, so the correction should be estimated or removed.

M4. The Delta_L versus epsilon linearity is close to automatic.
At fixed gate voltage, the induced charge is C*Vg, which is proportional to epsilon. Any hysteresis that scales with transferred charge will therefore scale with epsilon. The authors effectively say this themselves when they write that "a large epsilon enables a stronger displacement field". The linear regime in Fig. 3d then shows that the hysteresis is proportional to the induced charge, not that OV dynamics have been identified. The steeper slope below 35 K, where epsilon begins to saturate, is also expected from almost any mechanism that keeps growing as temperature falls. A more discriminating test would compare loops at fixed induced charge, scaling Vg by 1/epsilon(T), or plot Delta_L against the integrated gate charge. Without this, observation (3) in the Discussion does not add independent support for the model.

M5. "Complete erasure" and "non-volatile superconductivity" are not established.
- No figure compares the post-illumination state directly with the pristine (never-gated) state in the same cooldown, either for the Tc curve or for Rs at the same temperature. The available numbers point to differences. The pristine state takes three to four cycles to reach a stable loop (Fig. 1e,f), yet after illumination a single cycle restores the full 840 Ohm (Fig. 2c). Supp. Note 4 shows 300 s exposures leave Rs at 808 Ohm. The text also states that the system "does not fully recover to the pre-illumination state" even at 180 K. Please show pristine Tc and Rs against L4 directly and tone down "complete".
- "Non-volatile" requires retention data: Rs and Tc of an imprinted state held at Vg = 0 in the dark for long times (hours to days) at the operating temperature, and the temperature at which the imprint is lost on warming without light. Fig. 2h gives some evidence across cooling to the superconducting state. A dedicated retention measurement is needed.
- Tc changes by about 5 to 7%, and the transition is about 0.3 K wide. To support "reconfigurable superconductivity", demonstrate switching at a fixed temperature inside the transition, for example near 1.05 K, between a zero-resistance and a resistive state by gate cycle and by light, repeated over many cycles. Also report onset and zero-resistance temperatures and the transition width for each state, not only the 50% point.

M6. Light-erasure mechanism and heating.
The statement that Ea1 and Ea2 are "much smaller than the photon energy and therefore can be activated by red light" is not a valid argument. Being energetically allowed does not mean a 1.97 eV photon couples to a 76 meV dipole-reorientation coordinate. How light "randomizes" PNR alignment is not specified. The authors acknowledge heating at 2 K and say the LED output varies with temperature. Needed controls:
(i) a pure thermal excursion: does warming the gate-imprinted sample in the dark to the temperature reached during illumination, or to 35 K or 100 K, erase the imprint the same way?
(ii) dependence on photon flux and wavelength, to separate photo-ionization of specific in-gap levels from heating;
(iii) a calibrated estimate of the incident flux or power.
Because the flux is not constant across temperature, the "fully illuminated" state used in Fig. 4 must be shown to be saturated at each temperature. Otherwise Delta_R/R1(T) mixes dose with intrinsic behavior.

M7. Reproducibility and controls.
The paper appears to rest on one device. Please state how many samples were measured and show at least the key effects (history drift of Tc, optical reset, the two loss peaks) on a second device. The sharp single-point maxima at exactly 35 K and 95 K in Fig. 4b,c, with qualitatively different traces from their neighbors at 40 K and 90 K in Fig. 4a, need repeat measurements at finer temperature steps to rule out run-to-run or temperature-control artifacts. The non-monotonic tau(T) is itself unusual for thermally activated processes. With a fitting window of only about 2*tau, Delta_R and tau are strongly correlated, so the fit uncertainties should be shown. A non-superconducting reference interface, for example on (001) KTaO3 with the same process, would show which effects are specific to the superconducting (111) interface.

M8. Hall analysis and charge bookkeeping.
- KTaO3(111) 2DEGs have several occupied subbands, so single-carrier n and mu extracted from +/-6 T data are model-dependent. Please show the Hall curves and test for nonlinearity, or justify a single band.
- The parallel-plate estimate for this geometry (epsilon about 2000, A = 6.25 mm^2, d = 0.5 mm) gives about 1e12 cm^-2 per 50 V. The Hall density changes by about 4e12 cm^-2 across the loop (Supp. Fig. 1c), and with the opposite sign to simple field-effect doping: n is higher at negative Vg. The authors' OV hopping picture can give that sign, but the numbers should be checked. Integrate the anomalous gate-current transient in Supp. Fig. 3a to get the transferred charge and compare it with the Hall Delta n and with the mobility change expected from the corresponding number of ionized scatterers.
- Separate real leakage from displacement current during the 10-minute holds at -50 V.
- Supp. Fig. 3a was measured after illumination, whereas the main text uses it to explain the drift of the pristine state in Fig. 1. Please show the gate current for the first-ever sweep, or clarify.

M9. Positioning relative to prior work.
The paper describes the interplay as "previously unrecognized", but its own ref. 30 reports gate-cycle enhancement of Tc at cryogenic temperatures, recoverable only at room temperature, in oxide interfaces with coexisting ferroelectricity. Irreversible first-sweep back-gating caused by charge trapping, and visible-light modification of gating and persistent photoconductivity, are well documented in SrTiO3-based 2DEGs. The manuscript should state clearly what is new relative to these (mainly, I think, the optical reset of a gate-imprinted Tc in KTaO3) and discuss the charge-trapping explanations used there.

M10. Internal logic of the PNR picture.
In the stable loop, the PNR model assigns polarization "outwards" after positive cycling and "inwards" after negative cycling (Fig. 3e). Both are aligned states of similar magnitude. Yet the Discussion attributes the Tc enhancement to PNR alignment and its suppression to randomization by light. That does not explain why Tc differs strongly between the two loop branches, by about 0.1 K between -50 V and +50 V. Please explain how Tc depends on the sign of the remnant polarization, as opposed to its magnitude. More generally, the paper presents no direct probe of interfacial PNRs (for example SHG, Raman, or local piezoresponse), so Fig. 3e is a schematic hypothesis and should be labeled as one.

4. Minor issues

m1. The Discussion repeats the same sentence twice ("The interactions are further reinforced by the dielectric loss..." and "Dielectric loss signatures further reinforce this picture...").
m2. "Remarkably, these two temperatures coincide with the dielectric loss peaks ... (Fig. 3c)" should probably cite Fig. 3b. Also, Tp shifts by tens of kelvin across the measured frequency range, so specify which frequency is meant.
m3. The Fig. 3d caption says Delta_L "deviates above this dependence at lower temperature", while the main text says "a steeper dependence below 35 K". The Fig. 3 caption also places the onset of "significant TO phonon softening" at 35 K, although epsilon(T) in Fig. 3a rises continuously from about 150 K. Please make these consistent.
m4. Notation is inconsistent. The main text uses R_s^0, R_s^A and R_s^B. Supp. Note 4 uses R_s^b and R_s^a, and in item 2 labels the elevated 856 Ohm value R_s^b, the symbol used for the baseline.
m5. Type B gives a higher Rs than Type A (856 vs 840 Ohm) simply because the two protocols end on opposite branches of the hysteresis loop. Please say this explicitly, and explain why the difference (about 1.9%) is about twice Delta_L at 10 K (about 0.8 to 0.9%, Fig. 3a,d).
m6. Units: "795 Ohm" in one place and Ohm/sq elsewhere. "red laser emitting diode (LED)" should be "light-emitting diode". Supp. Note 5's figure is labeled "Extended Figure 5". "Suppelementary", "illlumination", "memrisitive", "would facilitates", "in consistence with", "It is worth to note", "Simultaneous measurement ... is not plausible" (should be "not possible").
m7. Rs and n differ across figures that are presumably the same device: Rs about 0.745 to 0.79 kOhm at 1.4 K in Supp. Fig. 1, about 0.86 kOhm at L0 in Fig. 2h, and n about 1.06e14 cm^-2 in Supp. Fig. 1 against about 1.28e14 cm^-2 at 50 K in Supp. Fig. 5. Please state the cooldown and history of each data set.
m8. Give the gate sweep rate, the dwell times, and the time between protocol steps. These are essential for comparing with the dielectric and relaxation time scales (M2).
m9. In the Methods, define the sign convention of Vg (gate relative to the 2DEG).
m10. The definition l0 = v_TO/omega_TO in the Results is introduced and never used. Either use it quantitatively (estimate l0 against the 2DEG thickness) or remove it.
m11. The Methods do not report the AlOx thickness, the 2DEG thickness, or any structural characterization.
m12. The neuromorphic and quantum-memristor framing in the Abstract, Introduction and conclusion is far ahead of the demonstrated device behavior. A switching ratio of about 6% in Rs, at about 10 K, with switching times of 1e2 to 1e3 s, should be stated plainly next to these claims.

5. Recommendation

Major revision. The central experimental observation, an optical reset at 10 K of a gate-history state that includes a shift in Tc, is of interest to the oxide-interface community. The microscopic interpretation in terms of PNRs and OV charge states is not yet supported. The quantitative checks in M1 to M4 (Tc against mu collapse, time-scale consistency, a dielectric control sample, and normalizing Delta_L by induced charge), the retention and fixed-temperature switching tests in M5, the heating controls in M6, and a second device (M7) would be needed before the stated conclusions can be accepted.
