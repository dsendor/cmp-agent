Referee report

Manuscript: "Quantum Hall Antidot as a Fractional Coulombmeter" (arXiv:2509.04209v1)

SUMMARY

The authors build a fully gate-defined antidot (AD) in hBN-encapsulated bilayer graphene: a ~190 nm hole etched in a graphite top gate, with two side gates that bring the extended edges close to the AD-bound states. They measure resonant backscattering ("diagonal conductance" Gd) as a function of top-gate voltage and magnetic field. In the integer regime (nu = 1 to 4, device AD #1 at 5 T, and AD #2) the oscillations are of the Coulomb-dominated type: the stripes follow lines of constant bulk filling, the field period scales as phi0/(N A) with N = nu_int, and Coulomb diamonds appear in bias. From the gate period, the field period and the geometric gate capacitance they recover a tunneling charge of about 1.05e. In the fractional regime (AD #2, mainly 13.5 T) they report oscillations at nu = 3/5, 2/3, 4/3, 5/3, 7/3 and 8/3. Assuming a filling-independent AD area, they infer N from the ratio of field periods to that at nu = 2, and q from the ratio of gate periods to the single-electron gate period. They find q close to e/3 at 4/3, 5/3, 7/3 and for a weaker, half-period oscillation at 8/3; close to 2e/3 at 2/3 and for the dominant 8/3 oscillation; and close to 3e/5 at 3/5. The headline claims are (i) that the gate period and oscillation slope "directly reveal" the tunneling charge, giving a simple charge meter for van der Waals systems, and (ii) the first measurement of e/3 charge in a graphene device.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT

This is a technically good experiment. A tunable, fully electrostatic AD in bilayer graphene with clean Coulomb-dominated oscillations at several fractional fillings above nu = 1 is new and useful. The integer-regime characterization is careful, the numbers are internally consistent (I checked: with C_tg = 549 uF/m^2 and D_AD = 300 nm, e/(C A) = 4.1 mV and phi0/A = 58 mT, matching Fig. 4a), and the extension of AD charge measurements to higher-Landau-level fractions such as 4/3 and 5/3 goes beyond the GaAs AD literature. In its present form, though, the paper overstates how independent and direct the charge determination is. One supplementary dataset appears to be used twice under two different filling factors. The most exotic interpretive claims (even/odd tunneling at 8/3, the orbital-number argument) rest on thin evidence. I recommend major revision. The points below are listed roughly in order of importance.

MAJOR COMMENTS

1. The charge extraction rests on a single assumption (constant area), and the "cross-check" is not independent of it.

In the Coulomb-dominated regime the stripes follow lines of constant bulk density, so Delta V / Delta B = nu e / (C phi0). The authors show this in Fig. 5. Substituting it into Eq. (1) gives q/e = nu/N identically (Eq. 2). Once the slope is fixed by nu, Eq. (1), the field-period ratio of Fig. 4b and the gate-period ratio of Fig. 4d carry the same information. Delta V_nu / Delta V_2 = (nu Delta B_nu)/(2 Delta B_2) = nu/N follows algebraically from the slope condition plus the N assignment. The agreement between Fig. 4d and Fig. 4e is therefore a restatement of Fig. 5, not a test of the constant-area assumption. The text says this agreement "confirm[s] that the assumption of a nearly constant area across filling factors is reasonable", and that claim does not hold.

What the experiment actually measures is the product N A for each state (from Delta B) and q/A (from Delta V), with N q = nu e enforced by the Coulomb-dominated slope. Any change of the interfering-edge area by a factor k between nu = 2 and the fractional state maps q to q/k and N to kN without changing any plotted quantity. Distinguishing e/3 from 2e/3 therefore requires knowing the area at each fractional filling to better than a factor of 2, independently of the periods. The abstract's statement that "the gate-voltage period and the oscillation slope directly reveal the charge" should be revised.

This matters most for nu = 2/3. The alternative assignment (q = e/3, N = 2) implies D_AD of about 300/sqrt(2), roughly 212 nm. That is close to the 195 nm lithographic diameter and plausible for an edge sitting at lower density, i.e. closer to the potential hill, than the inner nu = 2 edge. The constant-area argument does not exclude it. For 4/3 the authors argue the alternative N = 2 would give a diameter "far larger than" the ~450 nm side-gate separation. By my estimate it gives about 300 x sqrt(2), roughly 425 nm, which is below 450 nm, not far above it. Please state the number explicitly and justify the exclusion.

I ask the authors to:
 (a) give an independent estimate of the edge radius at each filling, for example electrostatic modeling of the AD potential through the 45 nm hBN with the actual gate voltages, showing where the 2/3, 4/3, 5/3, 8/3 and nu = 2 inner edges sit;
 (b) where possible, compare states on the same plateau or the same edge at different bulk filling, or use an Aharonov-Bohm-regime period (phi0/A) to calibrate the area at the fractional fillings;
 (c) reframe Fig. 4d/4e and the text so it is clear that only one independent quantity per state is measured, and that the constant-area assumption carries the conclusion;
 (d) use an independent observable that scales differently with q. One example is the charging energy from the Coulomb diamonds, which for a fixed geometry should scale as q^2: the 2/3 and large-8/3 oscillations would then show about four times the charging energy of the 4/3 and 5/3 oscillations. The SM reports "on the order of 50 ueV" for all fractional states. If that holds across the e/3 and 2e/3 groups, it needs discussion. Shot noise on the same device would of course be the most convincing complement.

2. Apparent duplication of a dataset between nu = 3/5 and nu = 2/3.

Fig. S13a, c, e, g (labeled nu = 3/5) and Fig. S14b, e, h, m (labeled nu = 2/3) appear to show the same measurement. Both are at V_bg = -0.08 V, D = -0.12 V/nm, with the same V_tg window (0.656 to 0.667 V) and visibly identical stripe patterns, the same bias-map features and the same line cuts with identical axis limits (R up to 12.64 kOhm, G from 0.45 to 0.61 e^2/h). The extracted periods are also the same: Delta V = 2.5 +/- 0.4 mV in both, and Delta B = 64 vs 65 +/- 12 mT. Main-text Fig. 3b (3/5) uses the same V_tg window. The Gxy trace near 0.6 e^2/h suggests the data belong to 3/5. Please clarify which filling these data belong to, whether this dataset entered the 2/3 points in Figs. 4 and 5, and recompute them if so. Relatedly, the 3/5 and 2/3 periods are indistinguishable within error (Delta V of 2.5 to 2.7 mV, Delta B of 56 to 65 mT for both, Fig. 4d values of about 0.68 for both). Given the roughly 5 to 10 percent gate-dependent systematics the authors themselves identify (Sec. E, G), the experiment cannot distinguish q = 3e/5 from 2e/3. The statement that 3/5 "appears near q/e = 3/5" should be softened accordingly. Since N = 1 is assigned in both cases, q = nu e follows by construction.

3. The half-period oscillation at 8/3 needs to be distinguished from a second harmonic.

The claimed e/3 signal at 8/3 is a weaker FFT peak (Fig. S18c, Delta V = 1.3 mV, Delta B = 7 mT) at almost exactly twice the frequency of the dominant peak (2.3 mV, 14 mT), along the same line through the origin. That is exactly where the second harmonic of a non-sinusoidal resonance would appear, and Coulomb-blockade dips are strongly non-sinusoidal. The text says the feature appears "only in certain regions" and that both points were "retained". Please:
 (a) show real-space evidence (line cuts) that the extra dips sit midway between the main dips with an amplitude not explained by harmonic content;
 (b) compare the 2f/f spectral weight at 8/3 with that of other states whose dips have similar shape (e.g. 4/3 or nu = 2), to show that 8/3 is anomalous;
 (c) quantify where in (V_tg, B, V_sg) the half-period appears and whether it depends on coupling strength or temperature.
Also, the second 8/3 dataset (Fig. S18b, D = -0.24 V/nm) gives Delta V = 1.8 +/- 0.4 mV and Delta B = 10 +/- 2 mT. That corresponds to q of about e/2 and N of about 5.4, matching neither assignment, and it does not appear to be shown in Fig. 4. All datasets should be reported and the selection criteria stated.

4. Dataset selection, averaging, and use of integer N.

Several states have multiple datasets with visibly different periods. For 5/3 (Fig. S16), Delta B ranges from 10 to 13 mT across displacement fields, i.e. N from about 4.2 to 5.8 using phi0/A of about 55 to 58 mT. Yet the SM says there is no "detectable variation" with D. For 7/3 there is a single dataset with Delta B = 10 +/- 4 mT. Please provide a table of every dataset used: filling, B, V_bg, D, Delta V, Delta B, and the reference Delta V_e and Delta B_2 used for normalization. State how the points in Fig. 4 are combined (averaged, or representative).

Please also clarify whether q in Fig. 4e is computed with the integer N assigned from Fig. 4b or with the measured non-integer ratio. If integer N is imposed, Fig. 4e is close to nu/N by construction. Errors should then be propagated from the measured ratio, so the reader can see how strongly, for example, N = 5 is preferred over N = 4 or 6 at 5/3. As far as I can see, the e/3 conclusions at 4/3 and 5/3 are the best supported part of the paper: at 5/3 a 2e/3 charge would need a non-integer N = 2.5, and at 4/3 N is about 3.8. This should be stated clearly, with the 7/3 and small-8/3 points presented as weaker.

5. The reference states and the Coulomb-dominated assumption.

All fractional charges are normalized to nu = 2 (or nu = 1 at 8 T) in AD #2. The SM notes that at 13.5 T the nu = 1 and 4 oscillations "may not be operating in the single-level hopping regime" and are quasiperiodic. Please show that the nu = 2 reference at 13.5 T is clean, and give its Delta V_2 and Delta B_2 values with errors. The Fig. S7 caption says the AD #2 integer oscillations were taken versus BG voltage, while the text says TG; if the reference was taken with the BG, the capacitance used matters.

The SM also reports that the 4/3 bias dependence shows a peak-to-dip transition "consistent with an Aharonov-Bohm-like mechanism rather than a purely Coulomb-dominated regime". Eq. (1) and the N assignment assume the Coulomb-dominated regime. Please reconcile these two statements in the main text: either show that the slope criterion (Fig. 5) suffices to establish the regime for each fractional dataset, or discuss how a partial AB contribution would bias the extracted charge.

6. Theoretical basis for N at fractional fillings above nu = 1.

The interpretation of N as "the number of quasiparticles transferred per flux quantum", with N = 4 at 4/3 and N = 5 at 5/3, amounts to N = nu e / q. It implicitly assumes that the whole filled Landau level(s) inside the interfering ring contribute to the charge imbalance compensated by tunneling through the fractional edge. This should be derived or referenced explicitly. Coulomb-dominated interferometer theory (e.g. Halperin, Stern, Neder and Rosenow, PRB 83, 155440 (2011)) is not cited, and in the Fabry-Perot context the field period is set by the fully transmitted channels rather than by the total filling. A short model section stating the assumptions for the antidot geometry (which edges encircle the AD, which are coupled, what the bulk inside the ring is) would make the N assignments much more convincing. Please also state which edge is partitioned at each fractional filling: the constriction filling nu_c from Gd, and, for 2/3 and 8/3, the role of the counter-propagating modes.

7. The even/odd and orbital-number interpretation is speculative.

The proposal that 2/3 and 8/3 show 2e/3 because one parity of AD occupation dominates rests on the 8/3 half-period feature (see point 3) and on the observation that 2/3 and 8/3 share orbital index sigma = 0 while 5/3 has sigma = 1. For this argument to carry weight, the authors should tabulate the valley/orbital character of every state measured, including 4/3 and 7/3, at the displacement fields used. If 4/3 or 7/3 shares sigma = 0 with 2/3 yet shows e/3, the argument fails. Temperature dependence of the 2/3 and 8/3 periods would directly test both the bunching and the parity scenarios, since shot-noise experiments in GaAs see 2e/3 crossing over to e/3 with temperature. It seems feasible with the existing devices. Without it, this section should be explicitly labeled as a hypothesis and kept out of the abstract-level conclusions.

8. Priority claim.

"The first measurement of the e/3 fractional charge in a graphene-based device" needs qualification. Ref. [37] (Mills, Averin and Du, PRL 2020) reports fractional quasiparticles localized on graphene antidots. The graphene Fabry-Perot works [11-13] also report data interpreted in terms of e/3 quasiparticles. The authors should explain precisely in what sense their measurement is a first (e.g. the first charge determination from Coulomb-dominated AD periodicities at fractions above nu = 1, or in bilayer graphene), and should note in the abstract that the most-studied lowest-LL states measured here (2/3, 3/5) do not yield the elementary charge.

MINOR COMMENTS

9. Sample quality statements conflict. The main text says device quality is "confirmed from the appearance of many well-developed fractional Hall states at B = 8 T" (Fig. 1c). The SM says "the absence of fractional quantum Hall states at a field of B = 8 T is due to the relatively high density of charge impurities". Please state which device Fig. 1c shows and make the statements consistent.

10. Sec. G: a gate period about nine times larger in the area-sweep implies roughly an 11 percent correction (one level per nine electrons), not 5 percent as stated. Please clarify the arithmetic. Also, TG oscillations overestimate the charge by about 5 percent and BG oscillations underestimate it by about 5 percent. Is this systematic corrected in Fig. 4, or does it cancel only in the ratios?

11. SM Sec. I: "the reduced quasiparticle charge e* in fractional states increases the spacing" and "delta epsilon ~ -1/(e* B)" are unclear. The charging energy of a fixed geometry scales as q^2/C, so a smaller q reduces it. Please separate the single-particle level spacing from the charging energy and give the correct scaling with q and B, which also bears on point 1(d).

12. Resolution claims. With dB = 1 mT steps, a 7 mT (small 8/3) or 5 mT (nu = 11/3) period still gives 5 to 7 samples per period. The statement that these periods approach "the resolution limit of our instrumentation" needs justification, e.g. magnet ramp hysteresis, field stability, or the limited plateau width. A ~200 mT plateau would contain ~30 periods of 7 mT, which also seems inconsistent with "no more than about 10 oscillations can be resolved".

13. Electron temperature is not stated. Please give it, together with how the excitation (3 uV, about 35 mK) compares with the fractional charging energies.

14. Figure/text inconsistencies:
 - Main text, Fig. 4a: "gate periods (red) and field periods (blue)"; the caption and axis colors say Delta V blue, Delta B red.
 - "Figure 2g shows the diagonal conductance oscillations as a function of the TG voltage and the dc bias": this is Fig. 2f.
 - Fig. S1 caption uses "g" twice (AFM and resistance map); the SM text cites "Fig. S1b" for the mobility fit, which is the AFM panel.
 - Fig. S9 caption labels "d-f, 2D-FFT" twice; the SM text says the fractional BG data were taken on AD #1 while the caption says AD #2.
 - Fig. S12 caption has two panels "b".
 - Fig. S7 caption says BG voltage; the text says TG.
 - Mobility units "cm^-2 V/s" should be cm^2/(V s).
 - The supplementary sections start at "C"; please relabel or explain.
 - Level spacing is given as hbar v / D_AD in the main text and 2 hbar v / D_AD in the SM.
 - "pijama plot" should be "pajama plot"; "consistent with the correctly estimation of N" needs rewording.

15. References: the statement on Fano factor 3/5 at low temperature for nu = 3/5 cites [17] (half-integer thermal Hall conductance), which does not appear to contain that result. Please check. Ref. [49] is a PhD thesis; if the auto/cross-correlation result has been published, cite the publication.

16. Gd is defined as I_t/V_d = nu_c e^2/h. Please show that the fractional plateaus of Gd and Gxy are quantized to the expected values after the f = 1.02 series correction (which is calibrated on nu = 2 only), since the fractional fillings are identified from these traces.

17. Please describe how the side gates are set for each fractional dataset (transmission, symmetric point). For 5/3 the authors fall back on the nu = 1 symmetric diagonal because of an impurity near one gate. Please show that the extracted periods do not depend on the side-gate setting.

18. Data availability "upon reasonable request": given that the conclusions rest on period extraction from many 2D maps, depositing the raw maps and FFT analysis code in a public repository would help readers check the results.

RECOMMENDATION

Major revision. The device and the higher-LL fractional AD oscillations are a worthwhile contribution, and the e/3 assignments at 4/3 and 5/3 look reasonably robust if area constancy can be supported independently. The paper needs to: present the charge extraction honestly as resting on one independent quantity plus a constant-area assumption, and add an independent check of that assumption; resolve the apparent 3/5 to 2/3 dataset duplication; rule out a harmonic origin for the 8/3 half-period oscillation and report all 8/3 datasets; tabulate all datasets and their combination; and qualify the priority claim and the even/odd interpretation.
