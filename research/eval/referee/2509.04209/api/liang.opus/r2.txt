Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **What the paper does.** It reports a fully electrostatically defined antidot (AD) in hBN-encapsulated bilayer graphene. The AD is a hole etched in a graphite top gate, with a graphite bottom gate and two graphite side gates. This gives independent control of the AD potential, the bulk filling and the edge–AD coupling, which earlier etched or STM-defined graphene antidots lacked.
- **Main measurements.**
  - Diagonal-conductance oscillations versus top-gate voltage and magnetic field are reported at integer fillings ν = 1–4 (two devices) and fractional fillings ν = 3/5, 2/3, 4/3, 5/3, 7/3 and 8/3.
  - Coulomb-diamond-like bias spectroscopy accompanies the oscillations.
  - The tunnelling charge is extracted from q = Cφ₀ΔV/(NΔB), with N inferred from field-period ratios referenced to ν = 2.
- **Central claims.**
  - e/3 tunnelling is reported at ν = 4/3, 5/3, 7/3 and in a subset of 8/3 data.
  - 2e/3 is reported at ν = 2/3 and in the dominant 8/3 oscillation; 3e/5 is reported at ν = 3/5.
  - The paper claims the "first measurement of e/3 fractional charge in a graphene-based device."
- **Potential importance.**
  - A gate-tunable AD in a van der Waals platform would be valuable for charge metrology in FQH states with ν > 1, including the even-denominator states of bilayer graphene.
  - It could serve as a building block for controlled anyon localization and braiding.
  - It could be transferable to rhombohedral graphene and moiré FCI systems.
- **Assessment of novelty.**
  - The device architecture is a genuine technical advance.
  - The physical novelty, i.e. a reliable fractional-charge determination, depends on whether charge and area can be disentangled. As argued below, the present analysis does not achieve this.
  - The priority claim needs qualification relative to Mills et al. (PRL 2020, ref. 37), which localized fractional quasiparticles on graphene antidots, and relative to graphene Fabry–Pérot interferometer (FPI) work that inferred e* from period ratios.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Fabrication.**
  - The all-graphite-gated AD with independent side gates is clean and reproducible across two devices.
  - Transmission of the inner edge is tunable from full transmission to full reflection (Fig. 1d).
  - The fan diagrams show high device quality, with well-developed FQH states and even-denominator states at 13.5 T.
- **Breadth of data.**
  - Oscillations are observed over many integer and fractional fillings, several magnetic fields and displacement fields, and both electron and hole sides (ν = −2 with reversed slope).
  - Both top-gate and bottom-gate sweeps are shown, with consistent trends (Fig. S10).
- **Integer regime.** The ΔB ∝ 1/ν scaling and the bias-spectroscopy diamonds agree reasonably with the GaAs AD literature, which supports the claim that the device behaves as a quantum Hall AD.
- **Higher Landau levels.** The paper explicitly addresses FQH states with ν > 1, where several edges encircle the AD. It recognizes that N (charges transferred per flux quantum) need not equal the number of edges, which is an underexplored question.
- **Methodological caution.** The authors note that the slope ΔV/ΔB is fixed by ν (Eq. 2), so q from Eq. 1 can be misread. This awareness is useful for the community.
- **Supplement.** Line cuts of R_t, R_xx, G_d and G_xy confirm the bulk stays on the plateau. The supplement also details FFT and windowing procedures and error estimates.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**Reason 1 — Charge and area are not independently determined; the "cross-checks" are not independent and partly circular.**
- **Only q/A is measured.**
  - Fig. 5 shows stripes of slope ΔV/ΔB = νe/(Cφ₀). Given this, the stripe family is set by one number, which both ΔV = q/(CA) and ΔB = (q/e)φ₀/(νA) encode: the ratio q/A.
  - N = νe/q is therefore not an independent observable. Assigning q requires an independent area.
  - The area is never measured independently. It is calibrated by assuming the ν = 2 (or ν = 1) area equals the fractional-state area.
- **Fig. 4d and Fig. 4e carry the same information.**
  - With N ≡ 2ΔB₂/ΔB, Eq. 1 gives q = Cφ₀ΔV/(2ΔB₂) = (ΔV/ΔV₂)·q₂.
  - So Fig. 4e is Fig. 4d rescaled by the ν = 2 charge (≈1). Their "agreement" is algebraic, not a validation of the constant-area assumption, as the text claims.
- **Fig. 4c is circular by construction.** D_AD is computed using N obtained by rounding the ratio 2ΔB₂/ΔB to the nearest integer. The diameters therefore cluster near D(ν = 2) to within rounding error. Their clustering cannot confirm that N was "correctly estimated."
- **The constant-area assumption is contradicted by the authors' own data.**
  - Integer AD #1 data at fixed B (Fig. 2h) show D rising from about 280 to 350 nm between ν = 1 and 4, i.e. an area change of about 50%.
  - ν = 4 deviates from N = 4, which the authors attribute to area change.
  - In AD #2 the ν = 2 reference at 13.5 T was taken at V_bg = −3 V. The fractional data were taken at V_bg ≈ −1.6 to +0.5 V.
  - Sec. G states that the bottom gate primarily controls the AD potential and area.
  - A factor-of-two area difference is exactly what separates q = e/3 from 2e/3 at fixed q/A, and is not excluded.
- **The argument against N = 2 at ν = 4/3 is numerically weak.** N = 2 would imply D ≈ 300 nm × √2 ≈ 425 nm. This is below, not "far larger" than, the roughly 450 nm side-gate separation, especially for a smooth potential under 45 nm of hBN.

**Reason 2 — Ambiguity in identifying the fundamental period (harmonics) and limited statistics.**
- **The small 8/3 period may be a harmonic.**
  - The secondary 8/3 peak in Fig. S18c (ΔV ≈ 1.3 mV, ΔB ≈ 7 mT) lies at almost exactly twice the frequency of the main peak (2.3 mV, 14 mT), along the same direction in Fourier space. That is precisely where the second harmonic of a non-sinusoidal resonance appears.
  - Coulomb-blockade-like lineshapes, with sharp dips on a smooth background, naturally generate harmonics.
  - The e/3 claim at 8/3 needs to rule this out. Possible tests are lineshape analysis, phase relations between components, and the dependence of relative amplitude on coupling or temperature.
  - The claim is also currently based on a feature present "only in certain regions" that the authors chose to retain.
- **Several fractional FFTs show a ladder of peaks.**
  - At 4/3 and 5/3 (e.g. Figs. S15e,h; S16f,i) the FFTs show several peaks along the same diagonal.
  - Choosing which one is the fundamental, sometimes by consulting the real-space "pijama plot," directly sets q by integer factors.
  - No objective criterion is given. The reported uncertainties, sometimes ±50% (e.g. ΔV = 1.6 ± 0.7 mV), do not reflect this discrete ambiguity.
- **Resolution and statistics are marginal.**
  - Fractional windows contain fewer than about 10 periods, as the authors acknowledge.
  - The 8/3 small period (~7 mT) is sampled with 1-mT field steps, close to the stated magnet resolution limit.
  - Several states (7/3, 3/5) rest on one or two scans. There is no assessment of reproducibility across thermal cycles or gate configurations.

**Reason 3 — The transport regime and the physical model for N are not established; the interpretation of anomalous charges is post hoc.**
- **Regime evidence is mixed.**
  - The paper calls the regime Coulomb-dominated. Yet Sec. S-E argues the integer level spacing is explained "without the need for electron-electron interactions."
  - ν = 1 and 4 in AD #2 are said to be in multilevel hopping without resolvable diamonds.
  - The 4/3 bias map (Fig. S15l) shows a peak-to-dip transition that the authors themselves describe as Aharonov–Bohm-like.
  - Fractional "diamonds" (~50 µeV) are weak and in places inverted.
  - The validity of Eq. 1 and the meaning of N depend critically on the regime, but no systematic criterion is applied. Candidate criteria include:
    - stripe slope versus coupling;
    - temperature dependence of amplitude;
    - the ratio of charging energy to level spacing.
- **N is not predicted by any model.**
  - N = 4, 5 and 8 are inferred, not predicted, for multiple edges (integer and fractional) encircling the AD.
  - A quantitative model is needed, for example an extension of the Halperin–Stern–Rosenow–Neder K_I/K_L/K_IL framework or the Kataoka-type compressible-ring picture to ν > 1 fractional antidots. It should specify which edge tunnels, how bound charge redistributes between rings, and why N = νe/e*.
- **The anomalous charges are explained only by speculation.**
  - ν = 2/3, the large 8/3 and 3/5 all give q ≈ νe. Equivalently N = 1, a flux period of φ₀ through A.
  - This could equally indicate electron-like or AB-type resonances, or a different area, rather than "bunching" or an even–odd tunnelling asymmetry.
  - The even–odd hypothesis is not supported by evidence such as alternating peak amplitudes or spacings in line cuts.
  - The orbital-number argument (σ = 0 versus σ = 1) is a correlation on two or three states without a mechanism.
  - Selectively accepting e/3 where it appears and invoking ad hoc mechanisms elsewhere weakens the claim that the AD is a reliable "Coulombmeter."

**Reason 4 — Insufficient control experiments, ambiguous origin of resonances, and an overstated priority claim.**
- **Resonance location is not established.**
  - The side gates are asymmetric, attributed to "localized impurities near one gate" at 5/3.
  - Resonances could therefore arise from impurity-bound states in the constriction rather than the AD. These would have a different, unknown area and would invalidate the area calibration.
  - Needed controls include:
    - showing oscillations disappear when the AD potential is removed (AD region filled to the bulk ν);
    - showing period invariance under asymmetric side-gate tuning;
    - comparing the two ADs scaled by area.
- **The line-cut text is confusing.** The supplement states oscillations in "R_xx and G_d are fully correlated, confirming that they originate from the AD." Oscillations in R_xx measured outside the AD would argue the opposite. This appears to mean R_t, but it must be clarified.
- **The absolute charge in Eq. 1 relies on poorly justified capacitances.**
  - The top-gate capacitance per unit area is extracted from bulk Hall data. The lever arm on edge states at the rim of a top-gate hole under 45–56 nm hBN is likely reduced by fringing fields.
  - The quantum capacitance in gapped states is also neglected.
  - The authors already see a systematic 5% offset. Fractional results rest on ratios precisely because absolute calibration is uncertain, which reinforces Reason 1.
- **The priority claim needs qualification.**
  - "First measurement of e/3 in a graphene-based device" should be reconciled with Mills et al. (PRL 2020) on graphene antidots.
  - It should also be reconciled with graphene FPI studies (refs. 11–13, 16), which inferred e* from gate/field period ratios and phase jumps.
  - The novelty should be stated more precisely, for example as the first gate-defined bilayer-graphene AD measuring fractional tunnelling charge at ν > 1.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Independently calibrate the AD area.**
   - Measure integer oscillations (ν = 1 or 2) at the same V_bg, displacement field and side-gate settings, and at magnetic fields adjacent to each fractional measurement. Alternatively, use the constant-density area sweeps of Sec. G per state to map A(V_bg, D, ν).
   - Add electrostatic simulations of edge position versus ν for the actual stack.
   - Most convincingly, compare ADs of different lithographic diameters. q must stay invariant while ΔV and ΔB scale as 1/A.
   - Remove or reframe the circular presentation in Figs. 4c–e. Present a single quantity, q/A or ΔB·ν, with the area assumption explicit.
2. **Address the harmonic and fundamental-frequency ambiguity rigorously.**
   - Fit real-space lineshapes with a Coulomb-resonance model and report harmonic content.
   - Show full FFTs with all peaks labelled, and state an objective rule for choosing the fundamental.
   - For 8/3, show the e/3 component persists over extended regions with amplitude and phase inconsistent with a second harmonic.
   - Use finer field control, e.g. a sweep coil, or flux control via gate-induced area, to resolve ~7 mT periods.
   - Report reproducibility across cooldowns and gate configurations, with statistics.
3. **Establish the regime and provide a predictive model.**
   - Carry out systematic temperature dependence (amplitude and period), bias spectroscopy and coupling (side-gate) dependence for each fractional state, to classify AB versus Coulomb-dominated behaviour.
   - Develop or adapt a quantitative model that predicts N and the stripe slope for multiple encircling edges at ν > 1.
   - Test the bunching hypothesis at 2/3 and 8/3 via temperature dependence (2e/3 → e/3, as in GaAs shot noise).
   - Test the even–odd hypothesis via peak-resolved amplitude and spacing statistics.
   - Extend to 7/3 with better statistics, to −2/3 and −5/3 (opposite orbital index), and to 11/3, as proposed.
4. **Add independent cross-checks, controls and presentation corrections.**
   - Where feasible, measure shot noise through the same constriction in the weak-backscattering regime for a charge determination independent of A.
   - Add controls ruling out constriction impurities, e.g. no AD potential, or asymmetric side-gate settings.
   - Discuss local lever arms and quantum capacitance.
   - Temper and contextualize the priority claim.
   - Fix inconsistencies:
     - "Fig. 2g" should read 2f for the bias map.
     - The Fig. 4a and Fig. 2g colour descriptions of ΔV/ΔB are swapped between text and caption.
     - The Fig. S7 caption refers to the bottom gate while the axes show the top gate.
     - Sec. F says "AD #1" for the fractional data, which are from AD #2.
     - The mobility units are wrong (cm²/V·s).
     - Ref. [17] is cited for 3/5 shot noise, but it is a thermal Hall paper.
     - The R_xx versus R_t correlation statement needs clarification.
     - The sign in δε ∼ −1/(e*B) is incorrect.
     - "pijama" is a typo.
     - The statements about FQH states at 8 T in Fig. 1c conflict with the supplement.
   - Deposit raw data and analysis code in a public repository.
