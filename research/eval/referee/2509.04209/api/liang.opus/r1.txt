Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Platform advance.** The manuscript reports a fully electrostatically defined quantum Hall antidot (AD) in hBN-encapsulated bilayer graphene (BLG).
  - The AD is formed by an etched hole in a graphite top gate, with the bottom gate pushing the interior towards ν = 0, plus two side gates that tune edge–AD coupling.
  - Earlier graphene ADs were etched (Mills et al. 2019, 2020) or probed locally (STM, SGM), and lacked in-situ control of the coupling. This is a real fabrication and tunability step forward for vdW antidots.
- **Physics target.** Resonant tunneling through AD-bound states in the Coulomb-dominated (CD) regime is used as a "Coulombmeter".
  - The charge is obtained from the gate period ΔV = q/(CA) and the field period ΔB = φ0/(NA), combined as q = Cφ0ΔV/(NΔB).
  - The method is applied to integer states (ν = −2 to 4) and fractional states (3/5, 2/3, 4/3, 5/3, 7/3, 8/3), including higher-Landau-level fractions not accessed in GaAs ADs (Kou et al. 2012).
- **Headline claims.**
  - q ≈ e/3 at ν = 4/3, 5/3, 7/3, and for a weaker "small" ν = 8/3 oscillation.
  - q ≈ 2e/3 at ν = 2/3 and for the dominant 8/3 oscillation.
  - q ≈ 3e/5 at ν = 3/5.
  - The authors claim the "first measurement of e/3 fractional charge in a graphene-based device".
  - They also argue that N (oscillations per flux quantum) can exceed the number of interacting edges (N = 4, 5, 8).
- **Assessment of novelty.**
  - The device concept and the higher-LL extension are novel and of interest to the FQH and fractional Chern insulator (FCI) communities.
  - The "first e/3 in graphene" claim must be reconciled with:
    - Mills, Averin & Du, PRL 125, 227701 (2020), which localized fractional quasiparticles on graphene ADs;
    - graphene Fabry–Pérot interferometer (FPI) works (Werkmeister 2025; Samuelson 2024; Kim 2024), where e* enters the period and phase-jump analyses.
  - The conceptual novelty of the charge-extraction method is limited, since it follows the Goldman–Su and Kou et al. protocol.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Device engineering.** A clean, reproducible, multi-gate BLG antidot is demonstrated in two devices.
  - Mobilities are ~2×10⁵ cm²/Vs.
  - Well-developed FQH states, including even-denominator 1/2, 3/2 and 5/2, appear in the Rxx(ν, D) map.
  - The side gates selectively partition edges (Fig. 1d, S4), showing genuine tunability absent in prior graphene ADs.
- **Breadth of data.**
  - Integer oscillations are shown in two devices and at 5, 8 and 13.5 T.
  - Several modes are demonstrated: top-gate, bottom-gate, area (TG+BG) and outer-edge oscillations.
  - Coulomb-diamond bias spectroscopy is provided.
  - The integer analysis gives a self-consistent picture: N = ν, q ≈ e within ~5%, and D_AD ≈ 280–360 nm, consistent with lithography plus hBN fringing.
- **Robust phenomenology.**
  - The B–V stripe slope tracks ΔV/ΔB ∝ ν across integer, fractional, electron and hole sides (Fig. 5, S10).
  - This is a clean, model-light signature of CD-regime antidot charging.
  - It is a useful benchmark for future vdW AD and FPI work.
- **Relevance.**
  - The approach can in principle be transplanted to rhombohedral graphene and twisted TMDs, where shot-noise or interferometry are hard.
  - The higher-LL observations (N > 1 per φ0 at 4/3, 5/3, 8/3) and the 8/3 period doubling raise interesting questions about quasiparticle pairing and even/odd tunneling amplitudes.
  - These questions connect to current debates on bunching at 2/3.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

- **R1. The charge extraction is largely circular; the "independent" cross-checks are not independent.**
  - **Eq. 2 removes the information.** Eq. 2 shows that once the stripe slope equals ν (Fig. 5), q/e = ν/N identically.
    - The charge is therefore not "measured" by Eq. 1. It is fixed by the assignment of N, which comes from the ΔB ratio to ν = 2 under a constant-area assumption.
    - Fig. 4b (N), 4c (D_AD), 4d (ΔV/ΔVe) and 4e (q) are algebraically linked by ν = qN and a single assumed area. Their mutual agreement is a tautology, not a confirmation.
  - **The 2D-FFT does not give independent periods.** A single peak of a stripe pattern yields (f_V, f_B), whose ratio is the slope.
    - With ~101 points at 1 mT steps (~100 mT window), the field resolution is ~0.01 mT⁻¹. This is comparable to f_B itself for ΔB ≈ 55–65 mT (ν = 2/3, 3/5, ν = 1 at 13.5 T, where ΔB = 62 ± 27 mT).
    - ΔB is thus effectively inferred from ΔV and the slope. The ΔB-based N and the ΔV-ratio-based q are not two independent observables.
  - **The argument against edge counting is numerically incorrect.** The text says that N = 2 at ν = 4/3 would give a diameter "far larger" than the ~450 nm side-gate separation.
    - Using ΔB ≈ 15 mT and D = 2√(φ0/(πNΔB)), N = 4 gives ≈ 296 nm, while N = 2 gives ≈ 420 nm. This is below 450 nm, not far above it.
    - The main argument excluding q = 2e/3 (N = 2) at 4/3 therefore rests only on the constant-area assumption.
  - **No unified theory is given for N.** The manuscript alternates between "N = filling of the interacting edge" and "N = number of quasiparticles per φ0".
    - In the standard CD antidot picture (e.g., Halperin–Stern–Neder–Rosenow-type models; Kou et al.), N = ν_bulk/e* follows from flux-induced charge redistribution.
    - The manuscript should derive this, state its assumptions (which compressible region sets A, role of fully transmitted edges), and show what it predicts a priori rather than fitting N post hoc.

- **R2. The constant-area / common-reference assumption is not justified across the gate configurations actually used.**
  - **Reference and fractional data come from very different electrostatics.**
    - The ν = 2 reference at 13.5 T (Fig. S7) is at V_bg = −3 V and V_tg ≈ 6.06 V; ν = 1 is at V_bg = −1.58 V.
    - Most fractional data are at V_bg ≈ −0.08 to +0.5 V, V_tg ≈ 0.6–3.8 V, with D from −0.03 to −0.46 V/nm.
    - The authors themselves state that the AD potential and side-gate trenches are "primarily controlled by the BG" (Sec. F/G). Equal areas at the AD for the reference and the fractional data are therefore not expected a priori.
    - The statement that D is "kept close to zero" contradicts the D values listed in Sec. I.
  - **Areas measured in this work already vary strongly.**
    - In AD #1 the fitted diameter changes from ~280 to ~360 nm between ν = 1 and 4, i.e., ~65% in area (Fig. 2h).
    - ν = 4 gives N > 4, which the authors attribute to area change.
    - Distinguishing e/3 from 2e/3 requires a factor-2 area accuracy, and the 3/5 vs 1/5 distinction requires factor 3. With ±30% area excursions and ±20–40% period errors, the discrimination margin is uncomfortably small for some points.
  - **The capacitance is not the one relevant at the AD.**
    - C is the bulk geometric TG capacitance from Hall data. The relevant quantity is the lever arm of the TG on the compressible AD ring under a hole in the gate.
    - Fringe fields (50 nm hBN versus a 190 nm hole) and quantum capacitance in the fractional regime can change it substantially.
    - The ~5% overestimation (TG) and underestimation (BG) for integer states are only phenomenologically attributed. Electrostatic modeling of the lever arm and area versus (V_tg, V_bg, D, ν) is missing.

- **R3. Evidence for e/3 is not decisive, and the framework accommodates "unexpected" charges ad hoc.**
  - **The 8/3 small oscillation is fragile.**
    - ΔB ≈ 7 mT, while the magnet step is 1 mT and the window ~100 mT, so it is near the stated instrumental limit.
    - It appears only "in certain regions" and as a weaker FFT peak lying along the same direction at ~twice the main frequency.
    - This is exactly the signature of a second harmonic of a non-sinusoidal 2e/3 Coulomb-blockade lineshape. No harmonic analysis, lineshape fitting, phase analysis or region-selection criterion is presented.
  - **The regime is ambiguous for key states.**
    - For ν = 4/3, Sec. I reports a zero-bias peak-to-dip transition and states this is "consistent with an Aharonov–Bohm-like mechanism rather than purely Coulomb-dominated".
    - If parts of the fractional data are AB-like or intermediate, Eq. 1 and the N assignment do not apply. In an AB-dominated regime the field period at fixed bulk quasiparticle number is not φ0/N.
    - The headline e/3 states (4/3, 5/3) are exactly where this ambiguity is admitted.
  - **The interpretive flexibility is too large.** Three explanatory routes are used depending on the outcome:
    - e/3 for 4/3, 5/3, 7/3;
    - 2e/3 for 2/3 and 8/3, explained by an untested even/odd tunneling-amplitude asymmetry tied to orbital index σ;
    - 3e/5 for 3/5, versus the expected e/5, explained by analogy to low-temperature shot-noise Fano factors.
    - The 3/5 Fano-factor argument cites Ref. [17], which is the half-integer thermal Hall paper rather than a 3/5 shot-noise study.
    - The resulting framework has little discriminating power: almost any outcome can be rationalized.
    - No temperature dependence, which the bunching and dissociation literature predicts, is shown to support any of these explanations.
  - **The novelty claim is overstated.** The "first e/3 in graphene" statement is not reconciled with Mills et al. 2020 (graphene AD fractional quasiparticles) or with e* inferred in graphene FPIs.
    - Given the caveats above, the claim should at minimum be qualified as "first AD-based estimate in a gate-defined BLG device".

- **R4. Data-integrity, filling-assignment and error-analysis concerns.**
  - **The same dataset appears with two filling labels.**
    - Fig. S13a/c/e/g (labelled ν = 3/5) and Fig. S14b/e/h/m (labelled ν = 2/3) appear to be the same dataset. They share V_bg = −0.08 V, D = −0.12 V/nm and V_tg = 0.656–0.667 V.
    - The FFT values are essentially identical (ΔV = 2.5 ± 0.4 mV; ΔB = 64 ± 12 vs 65 ± 12 mT).
    - The line-cut axes are identical: R up to 12.64 kΩ and G from 0.45 to 0.61 e²/h.
    - The line cut shows G_xy ≈ 0.6 e²/h. It must be clarified which points in Fig. 4 derive from this dataset and how the filling was assigned; this bears directly on the 2/3 and 3/5 charge assignments.
  - **The constriction filling is undetermined.**
    - For the 2/3 data, G_d ≈ 0.33–0.59 e²/h is far below 2/3, so the constriction may sit at a different fraction (e.g., ~1/2 or 3/5).
    - The identity of the tunneling quasiparticle depends on which incompressible regions separate the edges from the AD.
    - ν_c, transmission and G_xy are not tabulated per dataset, although they are needed to interpret q.
  - **The error analysis is incomplete.**
    - Error bars combine FFT bin resolution with a subjective "peak-identification" term, and their statistical meaning is unclear.
    - ≤10 oscillations per plateau limits precision. Some reported uncertainties (e.g., ±12 mT on ~65 mT from a ~100 mT window) look optimistic relative to the stated resolution.
    - The electron temperature is not reported, although the CD vs multilevel-hopping regime (k_BT vs Δε) depends on it.
    - The level-spacing discussion is inconsistent ("δε ∼ −1/(e*B)", "reduced e* increases the spacing").
  - **Numerous textual and figure inconsistencies reduce confidence in the analysis.**
    - Coulomb diamonds are referenced as Fig. 2g instead of 2f.
    - Fig. 4a colours are swapped between text and caption.
    - Sec. F says "AD #1 for fractional filling factors" where the data are from AD #2.
    - The Fig. S7 caption says "BG voltage" while the axes show TG.
    - Ref. [13] is cited for the Rxx fit; mobility units are wrong.
    - The SM states that FQH states are absent at 8 T, contradicting the main text.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

- **S1. Make the area and lever-arm calibration independent and configuration-matched.**
  - Measure integer references (ν = 1, 2) at the same V_bg and D, and as close as possible in V_tg, as each fractional dataset. Alternatively, track ΔB and ΔV continuously from an integer plateau into the adjacent fractional plateau at fixed BG.
  - Provide self-consistent electrostatic or Thomas–Fermi simulations of the AD potential, compressible-ring radius and TG/BG lever arms versus (V_tg, V_bg, D, ν).
  - Use dense 1D B-sweeps with finer field resolution (e.g., sweep a small auxiliary coil, or use longer windows within wide plateaus at different B). This gives ΔB truly independently of ΔV.
  - Quantify how large an area change would be needed to convert each claimed e/3 into 2e/3, and show it is excluded.
- **S2. Strengthen the e/3 evidence with discriminating experiments.**
  - For ν = 8/3 (and 2/3), measure the period versus temperature and dc bias. Bunching or even/odd-amplitude scenarios predict a crossover from 2e/3 to e/3 with T, as in shot-noise studies.
  - Perform harmonic and lineshape analysis to exclude second-harmonic artifacts, and state objective criteria for the regions where the small oscillation appears.
  - Measure additional states (11/3, −2/3, −5/3, and ideally 1/3 via a different BG/contact scheme) to test the orbital-index (σ) hypothesis predictively.
  - Where feasible, add an independent shot-noise measurement through the same constriction at 4/3 or 5/3.
- **S3. Provide a coherent theoretical framework and clean regime identification.**
  - Derive N = ν/e* (or its appropriate generalization with fully transmitted edges) within a CD antidot model, cite Halperin et al. and Kou et al., and state which region defines A.
  - Use this to make a priori predictions for each state, including hole-conjugate states and 3/5 with its multiple edge modes.
  - Classify each dataset as CD, AB or intermediate using bias spectroscopy, side-gate coupling dependence and slope sign. Restrict charge extraction to unambiguously CD data, especially at 4/3.
  - Quantitatively formulate the even/odd-amplitude hypothesis (e.g., parity-dependent tunneling matrix elements) and explain why it should depend on σ.
  - Correct the edge-counting diameter argument.
- **S4. Resolve data and presentation issues and calibrate the claims.**
  - Clarify or correct the apparent S13/S14 duplicate and re-derive the 2/3 and 3/5 points accordingly.
  - Add a master table listing, for every dataset, B, V_bg, V_tg, D, ν_bulk, ν_c, G_xy, ΔV, ΔB, N, q, D_AD, and which reference was used.
  - Report the electron temperature and a transparent, reproducible error-propagation procedure.
  - Fix the citation errors (Ref. [17] for 3/5 Fano factors, Ref. [13] for the Rxx fit), figure cross-references, captions and units.
  - Reconcile the 8 T FQH statements.
  - Reword the novelty claim relative to Mills et al. 2020 and graphene FPI results.
  - Consider depositing the data in a public repository rather than "upon request".
