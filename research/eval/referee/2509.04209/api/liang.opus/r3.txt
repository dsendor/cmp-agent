Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **What the paper does.** It reports a fully gate-defined antidot (AD) in hBN-encapsulated bilayer graphene. A hole etched in the top graphite gate sets the AD, two etched side gates form the constriction, and the bottom gate holds the AD region near ν = 0.
- **What it measures.** Resonant-tunneling (diagonal-conductance) oscillations versus top-gate voltage and magnetic field, at integer fillings (ν = −2, 1–4) and fractional fillings (3/5, 2/3, 4/3, 5/3, 7/3, 8/3).
- **How charge is extracted.** From the gate period ΔV, the field period ΔB and an integer N, via q = Cφ₀ΔV/(NΔB). The authors also use gate-period ratios ΔV/ΔV_e referenced to integer fillings.
- **Headline claims.**
  - Quasiparticle charge ≈ e/3 at 4/3, 5/3, 7/3 and at a weak "small-period" 8/3 oscillation.
  - Charge ≈ 2e/3 at 2/3 and the dominant 8/3 oscillation; ≈ 3e/5 at 3/5.
  - "First measurement of e/3 fractional charge in a graphene-based device."
  - N should be reinterpreted as "quasiparticles transferred per flux quantum" (e.g., N = 4, 5 at 4/3, 5/3), rather than the number of edges.
- **Potential significance.**
  - A tunable, all-electrostatic AD in a van der Waals system is a genuine technical advance. Prior graphene ADs (Refs. 36–37) were lithographically etched and lacked tunable coupling.
  - It could become a platform for charge detection and, eventually, controlled anyon localization and braiding in graphene, including in higher Landau levels and even-denominator states.
  - The extension to ν > 1 fractional states (4/3, 5/3, 7/3, 8/3) has not been explored in GaAs ADs (Kou et al., Ref. 27) and is potentially interesting.
- **Novelty caveats.**
  - The priority claim needs qualification. Ref. 37 (Mills et al., PRL 2020) already reported localizing fractional quasiparticles on graphene ADs and, as far as I recall, analyzed their charge. Graphene Fabry–Pérot works (Refs. 11–13) infer e* = e/3 from AB/phase-jump periodicities.
  - The device concept is novel. The physical conclusions, however, depend heavily on an interpretive framework (choice of N, constant-area assumption) that is not independently validated (see Section 3).

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Device engineering.**
  - Fully electrostatic, four-gate control gives independent tuning of bulk filling, constriction transmission and AD potential.
  - The two devices show high quality: many fractional states and strong even-denominator states at 13.5 T.
- **Breadth of data.**
  - Integer and fractional oscillations in two devices, at three magnetic fields, with both top-gate and bottom-gate sweeps (Supp. F).
  - Area-sweep oscillations (Supp. G), outer-edge oscillations (Supp. H), and dc-bias maps for nearly every state.
  - Supplementary line cuts show oscillations taken where R_xx ≈ 0 and G_xy is quantized. This credibly localizes the oscillations to the AD.
- **Internal consistency.**
  - The integer results reproduce expected behavior: q/e ≈ 1, flux period φ₀/ν_int, Coulomb diamonds with charging energies of 300–600 µeV consistent with ħv/D_AD.
  - The slope analysis (Fig. 5) shows that oscillations follow constant-filling lines to within ±10%. This is a useful, transparent diagnostic of area versus density contributions.
- **Timeliness and relevance.**
  - The work addresses the charge-identification gap in recent graphene interferometry and the bunching debates at hole-conjugate states (2/3, 8/3, 3/5).
  - It offers a conductance-only charge probe, simpler than shot noise, that could be transferred to rhombohedral graphene and twisted TMD fractional Chern insulators.
- **Candid reporting.** The authors openly report anomalous values (3e/5 at 3/5; 2e/3 at 2/3 and 8/3) rather than forcing them into expectation.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**(i) The charge extraction is largely circular; the two "independent" checks are not independent.**

- **The charge reduces to a choice of N.**
  - Fig. 5 / Eq. (2) establish that CΔV/ΔB = νe/φ₀ for essentially all data. Hence q/e = ν/N identically.
  - The charge therefore carries no information beyond the integer N. N is itself assigned by rounding 2ΔB₂/ΔB to a nearby integer under a constant-area assumption.
  - Consequently, assigning N = 4 at 4/3 or N = 5 at 5/3 is equivalent to assuming e* = e/3. The authors' own admission that Eq. (2) "may lead to a misinterpretation" applies to their main result.
- **The cross-check ΔV/ΔV_e is not independent of Eq. (1).**
  - Using the fixed slope, ΔV_ν/ΔV₂ = (ν/2)(ΔB_ν/ΔB₂) = ν/N, the same quantity as Eq. (1).
  - The agreement between Fig. 4d and Fig. 4e is therefore a mathematical identity (up to the capacitance calibration), not a confirmation that "the constant-area assumption is reasonable".
  - The genuinely independent information is ΔB_ν/ΔB_ref at fixed area. This is exactly what is in question.
- **The pattern of results is what one expects if the method returns ν/N.**
  - For all ν < 1 the method returns N = 1 and hence q = ν (3/5 → 3/5; 2/3 → 2/3).
  - This raises the possibility that, for these states, the method measures the bulk filling (the total charge added per flux quantum in a Coulomb-dominated region) rather than a tunneling quasiparticle charge.
  - The 3/5 result, which contradicts the expected e/5, should be treated as a warning about the method, not explained away by analogy to shot-noise bunching.

**(ii) The constant-area assumption is untested and likely violated across the operating points.**

- **Reference and fractional data are taken at very different bottom-gate settings.**
  - The AD potential is stated to be "primarily controlled by the BG" (Supp. F, G).
  - Yet the ν = 2 reference at 13.5 T was taken at V_bg = −3 V (Fig. S7) and ν = 1 at −1.58 V. The fractional oscillations were taken at V_bg ≈ −0.08 to +0.5 V, with one 2/3 point at −1.58 V, and at displacement fields from −0.03 to −0.46 V/nm.
  - The AD radius and edge-state positions should change appreciably over this range. Yet ratios of ΔB taken at these different settings are used to infer N.
- **The diameter itself varies by more than the margin needed to pin N.**
  - Extracted diameters span ~270–350 nm across states (Fig. 4c) and ~280–360 nm across integer states in AD #1 (Fig. 2h). The paper itself says "the measured diameter increases with the filling factor".
  - A 15% change in diameter changes the area by ~30%. This is enough to move 2ΔB₂/ΔB between neighboring integers, e.g., 3 versus 4 at 4/3, or 6–7 versus 8 for the small 8/3 oscillation.
  - Area variation is invoked ad hoc to explain the ν = 4 discrepancy, but is assumed negligible for the fractional states that carry the central claim.
- **The argument against edge counting rests on a crude estimate.**
  - The effective diameter (~300 nm) comes from "approximately 50 nm hBN" with no electrostatic simulation.
  - The upper bound (~450 nm between side gates) is not a strict constraint on where the relevant edge resides.
  - Self-consistent electrostatics (or Thomas–Fermi modeling of compressible/incompressible strips) is needed to place the interfering edge.

**(iii) The evidence for e/3 is statistically marginal, and alternative explanations are not excluded.**

- **Limited resolution.**
  - Only ≲10 periods fit within the fractional plateaus (~200 mT, ~50 mV).
  - Field steps are 1 mT for ΔB ≈ 10–15 mT, and ≈ 7 mT for the small 8/3 oscillation, which is near the magnet resolution limit.
  - With ~101 points over ~100 mT, the FFT frequency resolution is ~10–15% of the peak frequency. Several ΔB, ΔV values carry 30–50% uncertainties (e.g., ΔV = 1.6 ± 0.7 mV at 7/3 and at 4/3/8 T).
  - Where the FFT peak "is not objectively distinguishable", periods are picked by eye from the real-space data and then matched to FFT peaks. This introduces selection bias.
- **The small 8/3 oscillation may be an artifact.**
  - It appears "only in certain regions". Its FFT peak (ΔV ≈ 1.3 mV, ΔB ≈ 7 mT) lies at almost exactly twice the main frequency (2.3 mV, 14 mT) and on the same slope.
  - This is precisely where the second harmonic of a non-sinusoidal (Coulomb-blockade-shaped) resonance would appear.
  - Without a harmonic analysis, lineshape fitting, or demonstration that the e/3 component dominates somewhere, it cannot be distinguished from:
    - a harmonic;
    - a second AD-bound level or impurity;
    - a switching/telegraph event.
- **The interpretation of the 2e/3 results is ad hoc and untested.**
  - The even/odd tunneling-amplitude explanation for 2/3 and 8/3 (and, by extension, possibly for 3/5) is proposed without a model, temperature dependence, or bias dependence.
  - The state-specific argument (σ = 0 versus σ = 1 orbital character) is suggestive but relies on states not measured (11/3, −2/3, −5/3).
- **Statistics and reproducibility are thin.**
  - The e/3 conclusion rests on effectively one device (AD #2) and a handful of (B, V_bg, D) points per state.
  - There is no reproducibility across thermal cycles and no blind or automated period extraction.

**(iv) The transport regime and the physical model are insufficiently established.**

- **The Coulomb-dominated label is asserted rather than demonstrated for the fractional data.**
  - The Supplement notes that at 4/3 the zero-bias oscillation shows a peak-to-dip transition and "inverted diamonds", "consistent with an Aharonov–Bohm–like mechanism".
  - For integer ν = 1 and 4 in AD #2, diamonds are unresolved and multilevel hopping is suspected.
  - The formulas used, the meaning of N and the identification of ΔV with one quasiparticle all depend on the regime. No systematic regime diagnosis is given, e.g.:
    - B-dependence of ΔV;
    - slope sign versus AB expectations;
    - dependence on constriction transmission;
    - charging energy versus e*.
- **No theoretical framework connects N to the edge structure.**
  - Redefining N as "quasiparticles per φ₀" is phenomenological. In a Coulomb-dominated picture it is natural that N = ν/e*, but this presupposes e*.
  - Several questions are not addressed:
    - which mode tunnels, e.g., the 1/3 mode atop ν = 1 at 4/3, or the counter-propagating structure of hole-conjugate 2/3, 5/3, 8/3;
    - how upstream neutral modes at 2/3, 5/3 and 8/3 affect AD resonances;
    - how the result relates to Kou et al.'s GaAs analysis.
  - This is especially important because the introduction motivates ADs as avoiding bulk–edge complications, yet the Coulomb-dominated regime is itself a manifestation of strong bulk–edge coupling.
- **Calibration and systematics are underspecified.**
  - **Gate capacitance.** C_tg is extracted far from the AD, but the AD sits under a hole in the top gate, where fringe fields reduce and spatially vary the coupling.
  - **Lever-arm correction.** The 5% over-estimate in integer charge is attributed to a top-gate lever arm on the AD potential, but no correction is applied to the fractional data.
  - **Conductance correction.** A conductance correction factor f = 1.02 is fixed by the ν = 2 plateau.
  - **Electron temperature.** It is not reported, although fractional charging energies (~50 µeV) are only modestly above plausible electron temperatures.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Make the analysis non-circular and propagate uncertainties honestly.**
   - State explicitly that, given Fig. 5, q/e = ν/N and that Fig. 4d and Fig. 4e are equivalent. Identify which measured ratio carries the independent information.
   - For each fractional point, report the raw 2ΔB_ref/ΔB_ν with full error propagation, and show the likelihood of competing integer N assignments (e.g., N = 2 versus 4 at 4/3; 6 versus 7 versus 8 for small 8/3).
   - Determine the area independently at the same V_bg, D and constriction settings as each fractional measurement. Options include:
     - integer reference oscillations at matched gate settings;
     - outer-edge or AB-regime oscillations;
     - self-consistent electrostatic simulations of the gate geometry.
   - Quantify dA/dV_tg and dA/dV_bg from the area-sweep data (Supp. G) and apply the lever-arm correction consistently to all states.

2. **Establish the transport regime and provide a theoretical model.**
   - Present a quantitative model of a Coulomb-dominated AD in higher (and hole-conjugate) Landau levels. Its predictions for ΔB, ΔV and charging energy versus ν and e* should be compared with competing hypotheses (edge counting, AB regime, bunching).
   - Diagnose the regime systematically for each fractional state:
     - ΔV and ΔB versus B across the plateau;
     - dependence on constriction transmission;
     - temperature dependence of amplitude;
     - charging energy scaling with e*.
   - Explicitly identify which edge mode tunnels, including the role of counter-propagating and neutral modes at 2/3, 5/3 and 8/3.

3. **Strengthen the e/3 evidence and test the 2e/3 interpretation directly.**
   - Improve resolution with finer field control (persistent-mode magnet with a small trim coil, or gate-only sweeps at closely spaced fixed B) and longer acquisitions at plateau centers.
   - Perform harmonic and lineshape analysis to show the small-period 8/3 component is not a second harmonic. Map where it appears in (V_tg, B, V_sg) and test whether it can be made dominant.
   - Measure temperature and bias dependence at 2/3, 8/3 and 3/5 to test the even/odd amplitude hypothesis. It predicts that e/3 or e/5 periodicity should emerge as the amplitude asymmetry is reduced.
   - Measure the proposed discriminating states (−2/3, −5/3; 11/3 if resolution allows). Also measure ν = 1/3 and 2/5, e.g., by modifying the contact or charge-neutrality-point strategy.
   - Demonstrate reproducibility across devices and cooldowns. Ideally, cross-validate charge with shot noise on the same constriction.

4. **Calibrate carefully, temper the claims and fix presentation issues.**
   - **Capacitance.** Calibrate the effective top-gate and bottom-gate capacitance at the AD, e.g., through electrostatic simulations including the gate hole, or via integer oscillations at matched settings. Report the electron temperature.
   - **Priority claim.** Qualify "first e/3 measurement in graphene" relative to Refs. 11–13 and especially Ref. 37. Frame the result as the first in a gate-defined AD and at ν > 1 fractions, if that is accurate.
   - **Editorial errors to correct:**
     - Text cites Fig. 2g for Coulomb diamonds (should be 2f).
     - Main text describes Fig. 4a with gate/field colors swapped.
     - Fig. 4d caption describes the inverse ratio of what is plotted.
     - Fig. S7 caption says BG while the text says TG.
     - Fig. S9/S10 device labels are inconsistent (AD #1 versus AD #2).
     - Mobility units are wrong ("cm⁻² V/s").
     - The SM statement on the absence of FQH states at 8 T contradicts the main text's claim of well-developed fractions at 8 T.
     - The sign and form of "δε ∼ −1/(e*B)" need correcting, as does "pijama plot".
   - **Data availability.** Deposit raw data and analysis code (FFT windowing, peak selection) publicly, given the analysis-sensitive nature of the claims.
