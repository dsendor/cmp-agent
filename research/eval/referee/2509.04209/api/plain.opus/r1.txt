# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** *Quantum Hall Antidot as a Fractional Coulombmeter* (Di Luca et al., arXiv:2509.04209)

## Summary

The authors build a fully gate-defined antidot (AD) in hBN-encapsulated bilayer graphene. A hole etched in the graphite top gate defines the AD. The bottom gate depletes the AD region toward ν = 0, and two side gates tune the coupling between the extended edges and the AD-bound states.

- **Integer regime (ν = 1–4).** They observe resonant-tunneling oscillations of the diagonal conductance in the (V_tg, B) plane and Coulomb-diamond bias spectroscopy. Using q = Cφ₀ΔV/(NΔB) with N = ν_int, they recover q ≈ 1.05e.
- **Fractional regime (ν = 3/5, 2/3, 4/3, 5/3, 7/3, 8/3; second device, 13.5 T).** They infer N from the ratio of field periods relative to ν = 2 and q from the ratio of gate periods relative to integer states. They report:
  - q ≈ e/3 at 4/3, 5/3, 7/3, and a secondary "small" oscillation at 8/3;
  - q ≈ 2e/3 at 2/3 and the dominant 8/3 oscillation;
  - q ≈ 3e/5 at 3/5.
- **Slope.** The oscillation slope ΔV/ΔB, normalized by Cφ₀/e, equals ν.
- **Headline claim.** This is presented as the first measurement of e/3 in a graphene device.

The device engineering is impressive. A tunable, electrostatically defined AD in a van der Waals heterostructure is a valuable platform, and the data quality (many well-developed FQH states, clean 2D oscillation maps, bias spectroscopy) is good. However, the charge determination is less independent and less model-free than the presentation suggests. Several interpretive claims, including the headline one, need either additional evidence or more careful framing.

## Major comments

### 1. The charge extraction reduces to a single assumption (constant area), and the "cross-check" is not independent

The authors' own Eq. (2) and Fig. 5 show that ΔV/ΔB · Cφ₀/e = ν for every state. This is the expected Coulomb-dominated behavior: the stripes run parallel to lines of constant bulk filling. Given this identity:

- The three quantities in Fig. 4b, 4d and 4e (N from 2ΔB₂/ΔB, q from ΔV/ΔV_e, and q from Eq. 1) are not independent. Once the slope equals ν, ΔV/ΔV_e = q and N = ν/q follow automatically from each other.
- The agreement between Fig. 4d and 4e therefore does not "confirm that the assumption of a nearly constant area is reasonable." It is guaranteed by Fig. 5.

The only independent experimental input to the charge is one period, field or gate, combined with an assumed AD area. In practice that area is borrowed from ν = 2 at the same field.

**Example, ν = 4/3.** From the quoted values (ΔV ≈ 1.4 mV, ΔB ≈ 14.5 mT, C_tg = 549 µF/m²), Cφ₀ΔV/ΔB ≈ 1.37e ≈ (4/3)e, so q = (4/3)e/N.
- N = 4 (q = e/3) gives D_AD ≈ 300 nm.
- N = 2 (q = 2e/3) gives D_AD ≈ 425 nm.

The same factor-of-√2 discrimination applies at 2/3, where N = 1 versus N = 2 gives D ≈ 296 nm versus ≈ 420 nm.

The diameters in Fig. 4c are computed *after* choosing N, so their clustering near 300 nm is a consistency condition the authors imposed, not an independent check. The paper's own integer data undercut the constant-area premise:
- In AD #1 (Fig. 2h) D_AD varies from ~285 to ~350 nm between ν = 1 and 4, i.e. an area change of ~1.5×.
- At ν = 4, N is overestimated, which the authors attribute to an area change.

Distinguishing e/3 from 2e/3 requires excluding an area change of 2×. The observed integer spread is not far from that.

**Requested:**
- State plainly that the fractional charge assignment rests on the AD area at fractional filling being equal (within a stated tolerance) to that at ν = 2.
- Provide an explicit error budget for this assumption, including the ν-dependent area drift seen in the integer data.
- Discuss why the interfering (innermost) fractional edge should enclose the same area as the inner ν = 2 edge. In a smooth AD potential, the incompressible strip associated with, e.g., the 1/3 sub-edge at ν = 4/3 need not sit at the same radius.
- An independent area calibration at the fractional fields would greatly strengthen the claim. Possible routes include integer oscillations at a field where the relevant edge sits at comparable density, or the area/BG oscillations of Sec. G applied systematically.

### 2. The "first measurement of e/3 in a graphene device" claim needs qualification

Ref. [37] (Mills, Averin & Du, PRL 125, 227701, 2020), which the authors cite, reports localized fractional quasiparticles on graphene QH antidots. To my reading it also interprets its oscillation periods in terms of e/3 charge. Recent graphene FQH interferometers ([11–13]) also infer e* from period analysis in combination with phase jumps.

The authors should explain precisely how their result differs from these. Likely differences include:
- a gate-tunable, non-etched AD;
- simultaneous gate- and field-period analysis;
- states in higher LL sublevels.

Otherwise the "first" claim should be softened.

### 3. Origin of the oscillations: AD versus incidental localized states

Stripes parallel to constant-ν lines are a generic signature of any Coulomb-blockaded localized region capacitively tied to the bulk. This includes impurity-bound islands in the constriction. The manuscript itself notes such localized impurities near one side gate at ν = 5/3 (Sec. D, Fig. S4e).

The extracted diameter (~300 nm) and the area-sweep oscillations (Sec. G) are supportive. However, a more direct demonstration would help, such as:
- dependence of the periods on the AD-defining (bottom) gate at fixed bulk density, across more fractional states;
- a control constriction without an AD;
- systematic side-gate asymmetry tests at the fractional states.

This is especially relevant for the states that set the key conclusions, namely 4/3, 5/3 and 8/3.

### 4. Coulomb-dominated versus Aharonov–Bohm regime at fractional filling

The whole framework (Eq. 1, the meaning of N) assumes the Coulomb-dominated (CD) regime. Yet Sec. I reports features that the authors themselves describe as "consistent with an Aharonov–Bohm–like mechanism rather than a purely Coulomb-dominated regime." These are inverted diamonds and a peak-to-dip transition at zero bias (ν = 4/3, Fig. S15l).

If some fractional data are not in the CD regime, the interpretation of the periods changes. The authors should:
- reconcile this with Fig. 5, where the slope equals ν, a CD signature;
- state clearly which datasets they consider unambiguously CD;
- check whether the conclusions survive restricting to those datasets.

### 5. The ν = 8/3 "small" oscillation may be a second harmonic

The secondary FFT peak in Fig. S18c lies at ΔV ≈ 1.3 mV, ΔB ≈ 7 mT. This is essentially exactly half the main peak's periods (2.3 mV, 14 mT) in *both* directions. That is precisely where the second harmonic of a non-sinusoidal (e.g., sharply peaked Coulomb-resonance) lineshape would appear.

Because this feature is a central piece of evidence for both the e/3 claim and the even/odd tunneling-amplitude explanation of the 2e/3 doubling, the authors need to rule out the harmonic interpretation. Useful evidence would be:
- line cuts showing alternating peak amplitudes;
- the spatial extent in (V, B) of the regions where the doubled frequency appears, compared with the main oscillation;
- comparable harmonic analysis for integer states with similarly sharp lineshapes.

The ~7 mT period with 1 mT field steps (~7 points per period) also deserves explicit discussion of the resulting resolution.

### 6. Interpretation of the 2e/3 and 3e/5 results

- **Even/odd mechanism.** This explanation for 2/3 and 8/3 is plausible but speculative. The orbital-index argument (σ = 0 for 2/3 and 8/3 versus σ = 1 for 5/3) is offered on limited evidence.
  - Please clarify the LL assignment. The text states that the ν = 1/2 even-denominator state belongs to the "first orbital LL." This seems hard to reconcile with ν = 2/3 in the same 0 < ν < 1 range being assigned σ = 0, unless the displacement field changes the ordering.
- **3e/5 result.** The justification cites Ref. [17] (Banerjee et al., half-integer thermal Hall conductance) for low-temperature shot-noise Fano factors at 3/5. This appears to be an incorrect citation.
- **Temperature dependence.** Both bunching and even/odd scenarios are invoked by analogy to temperature-dependent shot-noise results [15, 48]. The most direct test available within this platform would be the temperature (and perhaps bias) dependence of the periods at 2/3, 8/3 and 3/5: does the e/3 or e/5 periodicity emerge at higher T? This would considerably strengthen the discussion.
- **Pattern for N = 1.** For all N = 1 cases (3/5, 2/3, 1), Eq. 2 gives q = ν. The measurement therefore cannot discriminate "tunneling charge νe" from "one flux quantum per period with no further information about e*." This deserves an explicit comment.

### 7. Systematic error from top-gate leverage on the AD potential

The integer charge is ~5% high. The authors attribute this to the TG altering the AD potential, and support it with the area oscillations (ΔV ≈ 39 mV, "almost nine times" the density period).

- One level shift per ~9 electrons corresponds to an ~11% correction, not 5%. Please clarify the argument.
- This systematic should be propagated into the fractional charge error bars, rather than only being discussed for integers.

### 8. Internal inconsistencies in reported periods and energy scales

**Periods.** The ΔV values in Fig. 2g appear to differ from those in the 2D-FFT panels of Fig. S6:
- ν = 1: ~5.7 mV in Fig. 2g versus 5.0 ± 0.3 mV in S6e;
- ν = 2: ~4.9 mV versus 5.3 ± 0.4 mV.

Using the S6 values with C = 451 µF/m² gives q ≈ 1.10e at ν = 2, not ≈ 1.03e as plotted in Fig. 2h. Please confirm which datasets underlie each figure.

**Energy scales.**
- Sec. I states that the reduced quasiparticle charge *increases* the level spacing (δε ∼ 1/(e*B)). This conflicts with the usual e*²/C scaling of the charging energy and needs justification.
- The claim that integer oscillations at 13.5 T are in the multilevel regime (k_BT ≳ Δε), while fractional ones at the same field have ~50 µeV charging energies, should be reconciled.

## Minor comments

1. **Fractional states at 8 T.** The main text says Fig. 1c shows "many well-developed fractional Hall states at B = 8 T." The SM states that the absence of FQH states at 8 T (AD #1) is due to charge impurities. Please clarify which device each statement refers to.
2. **Rxx in Sec. I.** The text says oscillations occur where R_xx = 0 but that "the oscillations in R_xx and G_d are fully correlated." Presumably R_t is meant.
3. **Effective capacitance.** The AD sits under a hole in the top gate, so the local TG lever arm near the bound states differs from the bulk geometric C_tg. The ratio method with integer calibration partially absorbs this; a sentence acknowledging it would help.
4. **Area estimate.** The ~300 nm electrostatic diameter estimate from the 50 nm hBN thickness should be supported by an electrostatic simulation, given its central role.
5. **Mobility units.** These should be cm²/(V·s), not cm⁻²V/s.
6. **Raw periods.** Please provide a table of all raw ΔV, ΔB, N, q and D_AD values with uncertainties, field, device and gate configuration. Fig. 4 is dense, and mixed markers across devices and fields make it hard to read.
7. **Data availability.** "Upon reasonable request" is weak for a measurement-method paper. Depositing the 2D maps and FFT code would be valuable.

## Recommendation

The device platform is novel and the measurements are carefully executed, so I believe the work merits publication in some form. In its current form, however, the central claim of a direct e/3 measurement rests on a constant-area assumption whose uncertainty is not quantified and is comparable to the margin needed to distinguish e/3 from 2e/3. The claimed internal cross-check is also not independent.

I recommend **major revision**. The required changes are:

1. Reframe the charge analysis around its actual assumptions and provide an explicit area error budget.
2. Rule out the second-harmonic interpretation of the 8/3 small oscillation.
3. Clarify the CD versus AB regime for the fractional datasets.
4. Reconcile the "first" claim with Ref. [37] and graphene interferometer work.
5. Ideally, add temperature-dependent data at 2/3 and 8/3 to test the proposed charge-doubling mechanism.
