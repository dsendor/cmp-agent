# Review: "Quantum Hall Antidot as a Fractional Coulombmeter" (Di Luca et al., arXiv:2509.04209)

## Summary

The authors build a fully gate-defined antidot (AD) in hBN-encapsulated bilayer graphene. A hole etched in the top graphite gate, combined with a bottom graphite gate tuned near ν = 0, creates the potential hill. Two side gates control how strongly the extended edges couple to the AD-bound states.

In the Coulomb-dominated (CD) regime they measure diagonal-conductance oscillations versus top-gate voltage and magnetic field at:
- integer fillings ν = −2, 1–4, in two devices and at several fields;
- fractional fillings ν = 3/5, 2/3, 4/3, 5/3, 7/3 and 8/3, in device AD #2.

From the 2D-FFT periods ΔV and ΔB they extract:
- the number of charges transferred per flux quantum, N, from field-period ratios referenced to ν = 2;
- the effective AD diameter (~300 nm);
- the tunneling charge q, both from gate-period ratios and from q = CΦ₀ΔV/(NΔB).

Their reported charges are:
- q ≈ e/3 at ν = 4/3, 5/3, 7/3, and for a weaker short-period oscillation at 8/3;
- q ≈ 2e/3 at ν = 2/3 and for the dominant oscillation at 8/3;
- q ≈ 3e/5 at ν = 3/5.

They claim the first measurement of e/3 in a graphene device. They also note, usefully, that the oscillation slope ΔV/ΔB is pinned to the bulk ν, so that q/e = ν/N identically.

## Overall assessment

The device is a genuine technical advance. A tunable, fully electrostatically defined AD in a van der Waals heterostructure, with independent control of edge–AD coupling, is valuable. The integer-regime data are clean, internally consistent across two devices, and well characterized (Coulomb diamonds, area oscillations, back-gate versus top-gate comparison, outer-edge data). The platform is likely to be useful well beyond this paper, for example for fractional Chern insulators in rhombohedral graphene.

The fractional-charge conclusions, however, rest on an analysis with less independent content than the presentation suggests. The headline "first e/3 in graphene" claim also needs qualification. I detail these points below.

## Major concerns

### 1. The two "independent" charge determinations are not independent

The authors show (Eq. 2, Fig. 5) that ΔV/ΔB = νe/(CΦ₀) holds within ~10%. Once this is true:
- Eq. 1 reduces to q = νe/N;
- the gate-period ratio ΔV_ν/ΔV_e is fixed by the field-period ratio, so Fig. 4d contains no information beyond Fig. 4b.

The agreement between Figs. 4d and 4e, presented as a cross-check "supporting the validity of this approach," is therefore guaranteed by the slope condition.

The entire charge assignment thus reduces to one quantity: the integer N inferred from 2ΔB₂/ΔB_ν, under the assumption that the AD area is identical at ν and at ν = 2. The authors should:
- state this explicitly;
- remove or reframe the cross-check language;
- discuss the extraction as a single measurement with a single key assumption.

### 2. The constant-area assumption is not well supported by the authors' own data

In AD #1 (Fig. 2h), the extracted diameter rises from ~280 nm (ν = 1) to ~350 nm (ν = 4), which is roughly a 50% change in area across integer fillings. Fig. 5b also shows growing deviations at higher ν, attributed to gate-induced area changes.

Distinguishing N = 4 from N = 2 at ν = 4/3 requires only a factor of two, so the 4/3 and 5/3 assignments are probably robust to this. Finer distinctions are not:
- N ≈ 8 versus N ≈ 4 at 8/3 depends on a single weak feature (see concern 4);
- the overall ±10% claims are optimistic given area changes of this size.

The authors should give an independent estimate of how the area varies with ν and B. For example, they could use the electrostatics of the AD potential, or ΔB measured at several integer fillings at 13.5 T within AD #2.

### 3. A simpler pattern fits most of the data, which makes ν = 5/3 the decisive case

Since q = ν/N with N = 1 for every ν < 1 state, the method returns q = ν for all such states (2/3 → 2/3, 3/5 → 3/5). This matches the long-standing concern with the Goldman–Su-type analysis: in the CD regime the gate-period ratio can track the partial filling rather than e*.

More broadly, the results at 4/3, 7/3, 8/3 (large period), 2/3 and 3/5 all coincide with q = (ν mod 1)·e, the filling of the partially occupied sublevel. These are exactly the cases where the authors invoke an extra mechanism (pairing, even/odd tunneling) for disagreement with e*.

The one main data set that departs from this pattern, and favors e* over the partial filling, is ν = 5/3:
- the partial-filling rule predicts 2ΔB₂/ΔB ≈ 2.5, i.e. ΔB ≈ 21 mT;
- the measured ΔB is 10–13 mT across several displacement fields (Fig. S16).

This makes 5/3 the strongest evidence in the paper, but it is not presented that way. The authors should:
- explicitly confront the "partial-filling" alternative;
- foreground 5/3 as the discriminating case;
- discuss what the CD theory of an antidot (e.g., Halperin–Stern–Rosenow-type models extended to AD geometries and multi-component edges) actually predicts for the charge per period at each state.

The current explanation for 2/3 and 8/3 is ad hoc and untested. It relies on even/odd tunneling-amplitude asymmetry, with the 5/3 difference attributed to orbital index σ. Possible tests include temperature dependence, the ν = −2/3 and −5/3 states the authors themselves suggest, and coupling dependence via the side gates.

### 4. The e/3 signal at ν = 8/3 may be a second harmonic

The secondary FFT peak (Fig. S18c) sits at almost exactly twice the main frequency in both f_B and f_V, so it lies on the same ray through the origin. That is precisely where a second harmonic of a non-sinusoidal main oscillation would appear.

The expected ΔB ≈ 7 mT is also close to the 1 mT field step and the FFT resolution, a limitation the authors acknowledge.

To claim N = 8 and q = e/3 here, the authors need to rule out the harmonic interpretation. Options include:
- lineshape analysis of the raw traces;
- showing regions where the short-period component exists without the long one, or has an independent phase;
- comparing harmonic content at other fillings, such as integer data with similar lineshapes.

Without this, the 8/3 short-period point should not be used as support for the even/odd interpretation.

### 5. Is the regime actually Coulomb-dominated at all fractional fillings?

The authors note that the bias dependence at ν = 4/3 (Fig. S15l) shows a zero-bias peak-to-dip transition, which they describe as "consistent with an Aharonov–Bohm–like mechanism." This is the very state underpinning the main claim.

The CD formulas are applied throughout, so the evidence that each fractional data set is in the CD regime needs to be made explicit. Relevant evidence would be:
- the 1/ν scaling of ΔB;
- positive slopes along constant-ν lines;
- Coulomb-diamond structure.

Mixed or intermediate regimes would change the interpretation of ΔB.

### 6. The novelty claim needs qualification

"First measurement of the e/3 fractional charge in a graphene-based device" sits uneasily with prior work, including work the paper cites:
- Mills, Averin & Du, PRL 125, 227701 (2020) [37], reported localizing fractional quasiparticles on graphene antidots and, as I understand it, inferred fractional charge;
- graphene Fabry–Pérot interferometers [11–13] extracted e/3 in the course of their analyses.

The authors should state precisely what is new. Possible distinctions are:
- the first gate-defined, tunable AD;
- the first e/3 at ν > 1 in BLG;
- the first such measurement in bilayer graphene.

They should also explain how their result differs from [37].

### 7. The geometric argument against N = 2 is overstated

The discussion claims that edge counting (N = 2 at 4/3) would give a diameter "far larger than" the ~450 nm side-gate spacing. Using ΔB ≈ 14.5 mT and N = 2 gives D ≈ 425 nm, which is below 450 nm. At 5/3 (ΔB ≈ 12 mT) it gives ≈ 470 nm, which is only marginally above.

The real discriminator is consistency with the ν = 2 area, not the lithographic limit. The text should say so.

## Further requests and checks

- **Robustness.** Report how many independent data sets, cooldowns and devices support each fractional assignment. The fractional data come from one device. A reproducibility statement, or fractional data from a second device, would strengthen the work.
- **Origin of the resonances.** Strengthen the case that the oscillations come from the AD rather than from impurity-bound states in the constrictions. The side-gate asymmetry at 5/3, attributed to localized impurities, raises this possibility. The consistent ~300 nm diameter helps, but explicit discussion would be valuable.
- **5% integer charge offset.** The argument that the AD potential shifts by one level every ~9 electrons (Sec. G) is qualitative. Please quantify it, for example with a simple capacitive model of the AD potential's dependence on V_tg and V_bg. Also say whether the correction would alter any fractional assignments.
- **Error analysis.** Many fractional scans cover only ~10 periods with 101 points. Please state the uncertainty from the FFT resolution floor separately from the added "peak-identification" uncertainty, and say how the latter was chosen.
- **Temperature dependence.** Measuring 2/3 and 8/3 (where q = 2e/3 is reported) at elevated temperature would directly test the bunching scenario against the even/odd scenario. The comparison to shot-noise results in [15, 48] invites exactly this test.

## Minor issues and errors

1. **Wrong figure panel.** Main text, integer section: "Figure 2g shows the diagonal conductance … as a function of the TG voltage and the dc bias" should be Fig. 2f.
2. **Fig. 4 colors.** The text describes "gate periods (red) and field periods (blue)" for Fig. 4a, but the caption and axes give ΔV in blue and ΔB in red.
3. **Fig. 4d caption.** It describes the ratio of ΔV_e to ΔV, but the axis plots ΔV/ΔV_e.
4. **Contradictory statements about 8 T.** Main text: "high quality … confirmed from … many well-developed fractional Hall states at B = 8 T." Supplement: "absence of fractional quantum Hall states at B = 8 T is due to the relatively high density of charge impurities." Clarify which device each statement refers to.
5. **Level-spacing prefactor.** The main text gives δε ~ ħv/D; the supplement uses δε = 2ħv/D.
6. **Fig. S7 caption.** It says "as a function of the BG voltage," but the axes show V_tg.
7. **Fig. S9 inconsistencies.**
   - Panel labels "d-f" are repeated for the fractional FFTs (should be m–p).
   - The text says the fractional BG data are from AD #1, while the caption says AD #2.
8. **Mobility units.** "cm⁻²V/s" should be cm²/(V·s).
9. **Fit panel.** The fit referenced as "Fig. S1b" appears to be Fig. S1d,i.
10. **Correlated quantities.** Sec. I states that oscillations "in R_xx and G_d are fully correlated," but R_xx is measured outside the AD and should be featureless. Presumably R_t is meant.
11. **References.**
    - Ref. [17] (half-integer thermal Hall conductance) is cited for shot-noise Fano factors at ν = 3/5; this appears to be the wrong citation.
    - Ref. [13] is cited for the R_xx(n) fitting procedure; please check.
    - Relying on a PhD thesis [49] to "rule out" bunching is weak sourcing for a key argument; cite peer-reviewed cross-correlation work if available.
12. **Typos.**
    - "pijama plot" should be "pajama plot."
    - "correctly estimation" should be "correct estimation."
    - "in function of" should be "as a function of."
    - The panel label "f" is duplicated in Fig. S16.
13. **Current correction.** The 1.02 correction factor calibrated to the ν = 2 plateau should be stated as applied, or not, to the fractional G_d values as well.

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The device platform and integer-regime results are solid and of clear interest. The fractional analysis needs to be reframed for three reasons:
- the charge extraction reduces to determining N under a constant-area assumption;
- a simpler "partial-filling" pattern explains most data points, which makes ν = 5/3 the crucial evidence;
- the 8/3 e/3 signal may be a harmonic.

The novelty claim also needs to be reconciled with prior graphene antidot and interferometer work. With these points addressed, and ideally with temperature-dependent or hole-side data to test the even/odd hypothesis, the paper would make a valuable contribution.
