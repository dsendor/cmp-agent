# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** "Quantum Hall Antidot as a Fractional Coulombmeter" (Di Luca et al., arXiv:2509.04209)

## Summary

The authors build a fully gate-defined antidot (AD) in hBN-encapsulated bilayer graphene. The AD is defined by an etched hole in a graphite top gate, combined with a graphite bottom gate and two side gates that tune the coupling between the extended edges and the AD-bound states.

- **Integer regime:** Gate- and field-swept conductance oscillations in the diagonal conductance G_d are seen at ν = 1–4 (and ν = −2). Their periods are consistent with tunneling of charge ≈ e and an effective AD diameter of ≈ 300 nm. Coulomb diamonds are observed.
- **Fractional regime:** A second device shows oscillations at ν = 3/5, 2/3, 4/3, 5/3, 7/3 and 8/3. The quasiparticle charge is extracted from Eq. 1, q = Cφ₀ΔV/(NΔB). Here N is inferred from the ratio of field periods to the ν = 2 reference, assuming the AD area is constant.
- **Main results:** The authors report q ≈ e/3 at ν = 4/3, 5/3, 7/3 and for a weaker "small" oscillation at 8/3. They report q ≈ 2e/3 at ν = 2/3 and for the dominant 8/3 oscillation, and q ≈ 3e/5 at ν = 3/5. They claim the first measurement of e/3 charge in a graphene-based device.

The device platform is a real technical advance. A fully electrostatically defined, tunable AD in a van der Waals heterostructure is valuable, and the integer-regime characterization is careful and convincing. My concerns are with the fractional-charge claims. In my view these are not yet established at the level the title and abstract suggest.

## Major Comments

### 1. The two "independent" charge determinations are not independent

The paper presents Fig. 4d (gate-period ratio ΔV/ΔV_e) and Fig. 4e (Eq. 1 with N from Fig. 4b) as a cross-check. Fig. 5 is shown separately as a consistency observation. However, these three figures are algebraically linked:

- Fig. 5 shows that the slope satisfies CΔV/ΔB ≈ νe/φ₀ for every state.
- Given this, ΔV_ν/ΔV_2 = (ν ΔB_ν)/(2ΔB_2) = ν/N, where N ≡ 2ΔB_2/ΔB_ν.
- This is exactly Eq. 2, i.e. the quantity in Fig. 4e.

So once the slope equals ν, Fig. 4d and Fig. 4e contain the same information, and their agreement is guaranteed rather than confirmatory. The authors partly acknowledge this around Eq. 2 but still call Fig. 4d a cross-check. The paper should state plainly that:

- there is effectively one measured quantity per state (the flux period relative to the integer reference, or equivalently the gate period);
- the fractional charge rests entirely on the constant-area assumption.

### 2. The constant-area assumption is the crux, and the paper's own data partly contradict it

Every fractional charge assignment depends on the AD area being the same at ν = 2 (or ν = 1) and at the fractional state of interest. Several observations cast doubt on this:

- **ν = 4 gives N ≈ 5, not 4.** The authors attribute this to an area change, i.e. a ~25% deviation in exactly the quantity used to assign N.
- **ν = 5/3 gives N ≈ 5.4**, which is closer to 5.5 than 5, with the error bar shown.
- **The integer gate periods vary by ~30%** (AD #1: ΔV ≈ 5.6, 4.9, 5.4, 4.0 mV for ν = 1–4). The authors attribute this to "the increasing number of edge states encircling it." That is itself an area or lever-arm effect.
- **Systematic offsets of ~5% from the gate used** (TG gives +5%, BG gives −5%, Supp. F/G) show that gate-dependent AD potentials matter at the level of the claimed precision.
- **The ν = 1 reference at 13.5 T is poorly determined.** Fig. S7 gives ΔB = 62 ± 27 mT (≈ 45% error), and the authors state that ν = 1 and 4 at this field may be in a multilevel-hopping regime with "quasiperiodic" oscillations.

The authors should:

- (a) propagate the reference-state uncertainty explicitly into N;
- (b) show how robust the integer assignments of N are, e.g. by explicitly testing N = 3 vs 4 vs 5 at ν = 4/3 against the data;
- (c) ideally, provide an electrostatic simulation of the AD potential and effective area versus filling factor and gate configuration.

### 3. ν = 7/3: N is assumed, not measured

Fig. 4b omits AD #2 at 8 T because no ν = 2 reference was measured. Yet ν = 7/3 (measured at 8 T) appears in Figs. 4c and 4e, which require N.

- It appears N = 7 was assumed, which makes the 7/3 entries in Figs. 4c and 4e circular.
- The only non-circular datum for 7/3 is the gate-period ratio to ν = 1 (Fig. 4d), which has a large error bar. The FFT peak in Fig. S17b is also not clearly distinguishable.

This should be stated explicitly, and 7/3 should not be counted as independent support for e/3.

### 4. The "small" 8/3 oscillation may be a higher harmonic

The small 8/3 oscillation is the only case where e/3 is inferred in a state that otherwise shows 2e/3, so it carries much of the paper's interpretive weight. In Fig. S18c the two FFT peaks are:

| Peak | ΔV | ΔB |
|---|---|---|
| Main | 2.3 ± 0.5 mV | 14 ± 3 mT |
| Secondary | 1.3 ± 0.2 mV | 7 ± 2 mT |

The secondary peak sits at ≈ 2× the main frequency along the same slope. This is exactly where the second harmonic of a non-sinusoidal periodic signal would appear, and Coulomb-blockade line shapes are generically non-sinusoidal. In addition:

- ΔB ≈ 7 mT is sampled with 1 mT steps, i.e. ~7 points per period. The authors note this is at the limit of their magnet supply.
- The feature appears "only in certain regions."

To support the interpretation, the authors should:

- show that the secondary peak cannot be reproduced by the harmonic content of the main oscillation (e.g. fit the line shape and compare the expected harmonic amplitude);
- show real-space line cuts where the half-period features are clearly resolved and alternate in amplitude, as the even/odd picture would predict;
- acquire finer-B data, e.g. by sweeping the gate at fixed B and stepping B more finely, or by using a superconducting-magnet persistent-mode approach.

### 5. Pattern for ν < 1 states and the limits of the "Coulombmeter" concept

For every state with N = 1 (ν = 3/5, 2/3), the extracted "charge" is simply q = νe. This follows from Eq. 2 whenever the flux period equals φ₀. Consequences:

- The method returns 3e/5 at ν = 3/5, not the expected e/5.
- It returns 2e/3 at ν = 2/3, not e/3.
- In 3 of the ~6 fractional cases (2/3, dominant 8/3, 3/5), the measured "charge" is not the fundamental quasiparticle charge.

This substantially weakens the framing of the AD as a "Coulombmeter" that "directly reveal[s] the charge of tunneling quasiparticles." The proposed explanations do not yet resolve this:

- **Bunching** is acknowledged as disfavored, with the counter-argument resting on a PhD thesis [49].
- **Even/odd amplitude asymmetry** is offered without a model, without a prediction for which states should show it, and without a prediction for how it should evolve with temperature, coupling or bias.

I recommend:

- (a) temperature dependence of the 2/3 and 8/3 oscillations. Shot-noise analogues show 2e/3 → e/3 with increasing T, so a crossover in period would be very compelling;
- (b) dependence on side-gate coupling;
- (c) toning down the abstract and title claims accordingly.

### 6. Theoretical framework for N in higher Landau levels

The statement that N "should be understood as the number of quasiparticles transferred per quantum flux" is a relabeling rather than a derivation.

- In a Coulomb-dominated (CD) picture à la Halperin–Stern–Rosenow, the expected flux period is ΔΦ = (e*/νe)φ₀, with ν the bulk filling. This immediately gives N = νe/e* and the observed slope = ν.
- Writing the model down explicitly would clarify which quantities are measured and which are inferred.
- It would also clarify why the total ν, rather than the fractional part of the interacting edge (e.g. 1/3 for 4/3, 2/3 for 5/3), sets N.
- The paper's terminology is inconsistent: "N = ν_int, the filling factor of the interacting edge" is used for the inner ν = 2 edge (ν_int = 2), while ν_int = ν_b − 1 is used for the outer edge in Supp. H.

**Alternative mechanisms for a reduced flux period.** Kataoka et al. [47] showed that h/2e periodicity at ν = 2 arises from Coulomb blockade of two compressible rings. At ν = 4/3, 5/3, 8/3 several spin/valley-split rings (integer plus fractional) surround the AD. The authors should argue why period subdivision from multiple coupled rings cannot produce φ₀/4 or φ₀/5 independently of e*.

**Regime identification.** Supp. I reports a peak-to-dip transition at zero bias for ν = 4/3, described as "consistent with an Aharonov–Bohm–like mechanism." This directly undercuts the CD assumption on which N is based for the state that is the strongest e/3 evidence. This tension should be discussed in the main text, not only in the SI.

### 7. Novelty claim: "first measurement of e/3 in a graphene-based device"

This claim needs careful qualification:

- Mills, Averin & Du (PRL 125, 227701, 2020; ref. [37] here) studied fractional quasiparticles localized on graphene ADs.
- Several graphene Fabry–Pérot interferometer works [11–13] infer e/3 from gate/field period ratios or phase-jump analysis.
- Shot-noise-based fractional charge measurements in graphene may also exist.

The authors should state precisely what is new. For example, it may be the first AD-based e/3 determination in bilayer graphene, or the first in higher-LL fractional states. They should also discuss how their result differs from these prior works.

### 8. Ruling out spurious resonances

Device AD #2 shows strongly asymmetric side-gate behavior at ν = 5/3, which the authors attribute to localized impurities near one gate. Impurity-bound states in the constriction would also produce Coulomb resonances. The consistent ~300 nm diameter is partial evidence for AD origin, but further checks would help:

- show the dependence of the fractional oscillations on independent tuning of each side gate (an AD resonance should be insensitive to which gate pinches, an impurity resonance should not);
- if available, include a control constriction without an AD hole.

## Minor Comments

**Text and figure inconsistencies**

1. Page 4: "Figure 2g shows the diagonal conductance oscillations as a function of the TG voltage and the dc bias" should refer to Fig. 2f.
2. Page 4 says "gate periods (red) and field periods (blue)," while the Fig. 4 caption says ΔV blue and ΔB red. Please make these consistent.
3. Supp. F text says fractional BG oscillations were measured on AD #1, but the Fig. S9 caption says AD #2. The S9 caption also reuses "d-f" for panels m–p.
4. Fig. S7 caption says "as a function of the BG voltage," but the axes show V_tg.
5. Supp. C has panel references that do not match the figure: the fit is in S1d,i, not S1b, and the TG capacitance slope panel for AD #2 is S1h. Mobility units should be cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹, not cm⁻² V/s.
6. Supp. H: "Since the oscillations are gate-induced, we cannot directly extract the interfering charge" is unclear, since these are area-type (TG+BG) sweeps. Please clarify. The caption also labels two panels "b."

**Parameters and expressions**

7. The main text uses "top hBN ≈ 50 nm," while the Methods give 56 nm (AD #1) and 45 nm (AD #2). Use the device-specific values.
8. l_B ≈ 25.7 nm/√B[T], not 24 nm.
9. Supp. I: δε ∼ −1/(e*B) has an unexplained sign. The accompanying argument, that reduced e* "increases the spacing," is not obviously consistent with δε ∼ ħv/D_AD. Please clarify.

**References**

10. Ref. [17] (Banerjee et al., thermal Hall at 5/2) is cited for the claim that shot noise at ν = 3/5 gives Fano factor 3/5 at low T and e/5 at higher T. This appears to be the wrong reference (cf. Chung, Heiblum et al., PRL 91, 216804, 2003). Also check whether ref. [13] is the intended source for the R_xx fitting procedure.

**Experimental details and data**

11. Please report the electron temperature. With charging energies of ~50 µeV (~0.6 K) in the FQH regime, and the 3 µV excitation, T_e is directly relevant to the single- vs multilevel regime discussion.
12. The absolute capacitance in Eq. 1 is taken from Hall measurements far from the AD. Fringing near the etched hole may change the local lever arm, which is relevant to the 5% offset. Ratio-based estimates avoid this, but Eq. 1 does not.
13. The FFT error analysis (Supp. E) should be summarized in the main text. The main limitation, ≲10 oscillations per plateau, directly bounds how well integer values of N can be distinguished.
14. "Pijama plot" should be "pajama plot"; "consistent with the correctly estimation of N" is ungrammatical; "in function of" should be "as a function of" (several places).
15. Please consider depositing the data and analysis code in a public repository rather than "upon reasonable request."

## Assessment

**Strengths**

- A novel, highly tunable, fully gate-defined BLG antidot with clear integer-regime Coulomb oscillations and diamonds.
- Systematic measurements across many integer and fractional states, two devices and several fields, with both TG and BG sweeps.
- A useful, honest observation (Eq. 2 and Fig. 5) that the oscillation slope is fixed by ν, which warns against naive charge extraction.

**Weaknesses**

- The fractional-charge determination reduces to a single flux-period ratio that depends critically on a constant-area assumption the data only partly support.
- The headline e/3 evidence rests on:
  - 4/3 and 5/3, where the CD regime is not firmly established;
  - 7/3, where N appears to be assumed;
  - a possible second harmonic at 8/3.
- In half the fractional states the method does not return the fundamental charge, and the proposed explanation is speculative.
- The novelty claim needs qualification relative to prior graphene AD and interferometer work.

**Recommendation: Major revision.**

The device and dataset merit publication. The conclusions should be either:

- (i) supported by additional measurements: temperature dependence, finer-B data at 8/3, harmonic analysis, a ring-subdivision control, and an explicit CD model; or
- (ii) reframed more cautiously, presenting the AD as a platform that measures flux and gate periodicities consistent with e/3 in some higher-LL states, while openly discussing the cases where it does not yield the fundamental charge.
