**A. Must address**

**1. The threshold-field kink has no error analysis or statistical support, and the extrapolated κ⁰/T values carry no uncertainties.**
The central evidence is a kink at H*/H_c2^a ≈ 0.015 (≈0.18 T) for H ∥ a in Fig. 4b,c. It sits at the level of (κ⁰/T)/(κ⁰/T)_N ≈ 0.003, with differences Δ(κ̃⁰/T)^DS of only ~0.001–0.002.

- Only ~3–5 field points lie in the range 0–0.03 H_c2^a (below ~0.35 T).
- Each point is a polynomial extrapolation to T → 0 from T ≥ 50 mK (Fig. 3), shown without error bars.
- The extrapolation uncertainty (polynomial order, fitting window, weight of the lowest-T points) may be comparable to the kink itself.
- The same applies to the zero-field residual bound, κ_b⁰/T ≤ 0.0017 W/K²m. This is only ~0.1% of the normal-state κ_N/T ≈ 1.4 W/K²m expected from L₀/ρ₀.
- No statistical comparison is made between a two-slope fit and a smooth convex form (e.g., H ln H or H^{1/2}).
- Fig. 3 shows only curves at ≥1 T, so the raw low-field data underlying the kink are not visible.
- It is unclear whether the sub-1 T points come from temperature sweeps or from field sweeps at base temperature.

The authors should:

- report extrapolation uncertainties for all κ⁰/T values, including their dependence on polynomial order and fitting range;
- show the raw low-field T-sweeps;
- acquire dense, continuous field sweeps (Δμ₀H ≲ 20 mT between 0 and 0.5 T) at fixed lowest temperatures (e.g., 50, 80, 120 mK), with error bars;
- state the measurement protocol;
- statistically test the kink against smooth alternatives.

**2. Extrinsic and alternative origins of the low-field kink are not excluded.**
Several mechanisms can produce a kink or slope change in κ(H) near 0.1–0.3 T without any gap minimum.

- **Flux entry and history:**
  - flux entry near H_c1 (tens of mT in UTe₂);
  - demagnetization effects, which differ strongly between H ∥ a (in-plane, plate thickness along c) and H ∥ b;
  - flux pinning, irreversibility and trapped-flux history (field-cooled vs. zero-field-cooled; sweep direction).
- **Vortex physics:**
  - vortex-lattice structural transitions;
  - the crossover from vortex scattering of quasiparticles to vortex-induced excitation, well known in clean superconductors as a minimum or plateau in κ(H). In these ultraclean crystals (ρ₀ ≈ 1.7 µΩcm) ℓ is plausibly microns, while the intervortex spacing at 0.18 T is ~100 nm, so the field at which the intervortex distance becomes comparable to ℓ is a natural kink scale. The authors themselves note strong vortex-induced suppression at 0.3 K.
- **Impurity-band crossover:** the crossover between impurity-band-dominated and Doppler-dominated regimes (E_H ~ γ) produces a field scale and slope change even for true nodes (Kübert–Hirschfeld; Vekhter–Houghton).

None of these is discussed. H_c1, demagnetization corrections, field protocol and hysteresis are not reported.

The authors should:

- measure under both FC and ZFC/field-swept protocols and check for hysteresis;
- characterize H_c1 and the irreversibility field by magnetization and apply demagnetization corrections;
- track the temperature dependence of H*, which should smear out as T approaches Δ_min ~ 0.4 K;
- measure additional samples, including one of lower RRR, to test whether H* tracks Δ_min, ℓ, or the impurity bandwidth γ.

**3. The "isotropic below H*, anisotropic above" comparison relies on cross-sample differences.**
The field orientations were not all measured on the same crystal:

- H ∥ b was measured only on #B, and H ∥ c only on #A.
- H ∥ a was measured on both, so a same-sample comparison exists only for H ∥ a vs. H ∥ b on #B.
- The pink symbols in Fig. 4c explicitly subtract #B (H ∥ b) from #A (H ∥ a).

The two crystals have different RRR, and κ_b differs measurably between them (Fig. 2a,b; ~30% at 0.2–0.3 K). Sample-dependent impurity scattering, geometric factors and residual phonon terms can easily produce differences at the 10⁻³ level. Cross-sample subtraction of such sub-percent effects is therefore fragile. The authors should:

- perform all orientation comparisons on the same crystal and mounting, ideally on both crystals and ideally by in-situ field rotation;
- avoid cross-sample subtractions in the key figure.

**4. The semiclassical series-resistor model likely fails in exactly the regime where the threshold is claimed.**
The Kübert–Hirschfeld series-resistor picture assumes local transport coefficients. The authors concede (p. 6) that it breaks down when ℓ exceeds the width of the "forbidden" gapped ring, and they use this to explain the finite, roughly linear, orientation-independent rise of κ below H*, which the model (predicting κ ≈ 0) does not reproduce.

However, in these ultraclean crystals ℓ is plausibly ≳ 1 µm (ℓ ≫ ξ₀), whereas the intervortex spacing at ~0.1–0.18 T is ~100 nm. The condition ℓ ≫ R therefore likely holds at and below H*. The same caveat that explains the sub-threshold rise would then also wash out the threshold itself, and the existence and position of a kink are not robust predictions of the model.

The authors should:

- estimate ℓ and compare it with the intervortex spacing and with the width of the gapped ring (R − R*) as a function of H;
- show with a nonlocal treatment that a sharp threshold survives. Options are quasiclassical Eilenberger calculations (e.g., Adachi et al. [36]), Brandt–Pesch–Tewordt-type approaches, or at least a nonlocal average over ℓ.

**5. The absence of a low-T peak does not exclude true point nodes.**
The zero-field discriminator is the low-T peak in κ/T predicted for point nodes with j_Q ∥ nodes and finite c = cot δ (Fig. 2c, Γ/Δ₀ = 0.002, c = 0.15).

- **Location relative to the data.** In Fig. 2c the Δ_min = 0 curve:
  - peaks at T/T_c ≈ 0.01 (≈20 mK);
  - reaches a minimum near T/T_c ≈ 0.04 (≈80 mK);
  - rises monotonically thereafter.

  The base temperature is ~50 mK (T/T_c ≈ 0.024–0.025). Above ~80 mK the true- and pseudo-node curves are qualitatively indistinguishable, so "no indication of a peak" rests on a handful of points in the 50–80 mK window.
- **Sensitivity to parameters.**
  - The peak position and height depend strongly on Γ and c, on the nodal dispersion (linear vs. quadratic), and on the number of Fermi sheets crossing the node direction.
  - A slightly cleaner sample or a different c pushes the peak further below the measured window.
  - The authors acknowledge that the unitary limit (c → 0) gives no peak. They also state that very clean unitary samples are consistent with the vanishing residual term.

  The "generic behavior" claim in the Supplement is not demonstrated against the experimental window.

The authors should:

- constrain Γ independently (e.g., from ρ₀) and constrain the phase shift;
- map the (Γ, c) region, including strong-scattering phase shifts suggested by other UTe₂ studies, in which a nodal peak or upturn would be resolvable within 50–300 mK;
- test whether any nodal parameter set reproduces the data;
- extend measurements to ~15–20 mK if possible.

**6. The phonon contribution is not separated from the quasiparticle κ at the temperatures where conclusions are drawn.**
Boundary-limited phonons give κ_ph ∝ T³, i.e., κ_ph/T ∝ T². This is precisely the "nearly T²" dependence highlighted in Fig. 2b. Quasiparticle dominance is inferred only from the 1 T field suppression at 0.3 K, which does not establish the quasiparticle fraction at 50–150 mK.

- For a ~110 × 370 µm cross-section, with possible specular reflection, the phonon term could be comparable to the measured κ/T (~0.005–0.02 W/K²m) below 0.15 K.
- At that level it would obscure both a small nodal peak and activated behavior, and it would bias the polynomial extrapolation of κ⁰/T (which is ≲ 0.003 of the normal state).
- Phonon–vortex and phonon–electron scattering in field are not discussed.
- The ~30% sample dependence between #A and #B is not analyzed in terms of quasiparticle vs. phonon content or disorder sensitivity.

The authors should:

- include an explicit phonon term κ_ph = βT³, with β bounded from sample dimensions, sound velocities and surface roughness;
- cross-check with a reference above H_c2, or with a deliberately disordered or smaller sample;
- discuss field-dependent phonon scattering.

**7. The zero-field κ_b(T) is not fitted with the pseudo-node model, and the expected activated behavior is not seen or tested.**
- **Expected behavior.** For Δ₀ ≈ 4 K and Δ_min ≈ 0.1Δ₀ ≈ 0.4 K, the quasiparticle κ/T at 50–150 mK (T/Δ_min ≈ 0.12–0.4) should be exponentially suppressed, ~exp(−Δ_min/T) ~ e⁻⁸ at 50 mK. This would give visible downward curvature on a T² plot below ~100 mK. The calculated pseudo-node curve in Fig. 2c indeed shows a near-zero plateau up to T/T_c ≈ 0.08 (~170 mK).
- **Observed behavior.** The data instead show a smooth power law, with appreciable κ_b/T ≈ 0.01 W/K²m at ~0.1 K. The inset of Fig. 2b shows no curvature.
- **Ambiguity of the T² law.** κ/T ∝ T² is also the canonical clean-limit expectation for axial point nodes with j_Q along the node direction.
- **Missing comparison.** The Fig. 2c curves are in reduced units and are not overlaid on the data.

The authors should:

- fit κ_b/T(T) for both samples with competing forms: anisotropic activated gap with Δ_min free, clean point-node power law, and impurity-band forms, each plus a phonon term;
- report residuals, fit statistics, and the best-fit Δ_min with its confidence interval;
- overlay theory in absolute units, using measured N₀ and v_F.

**B. Should address**

**8. The conclusions are overstated relative to the evidence.**
The abstract claims the data "definitively resolve the gap anisotropy" and "exclude non-unitary mixing." Given concerns 1–7, the data are at most consistent with a small gap minimum along b. Two further points narrow the claim:

- Experimentally, a "pseudo point node" with Δ_min ~ 0.1Δ₀ is an anisotropic full gap. Its distinction from a true node at the measured temperatures (T_min ≈ 0.12Δ_min) is marginal.
- The data do not distinguish A_u from B₁u, since c-axis nodes are inaccessible on quasi-2D sheets. The topological conclusions (flat-band vs. dispersive Majorana modes) therefore remain undetermined by this work.

The authors should:

- replace "definitively" and "excluding non-unitary mixing" with language commensurate with the evidence;
- state explicitly that A_u and B₁u remain indistinguishable;
- state that the topological implications are conditional.

**9. The near-square sample geometry compromises the directional selectivity of κ_b.**
The crystals are ~360 µm long (∥ b) and ~370 µm wide (∥ a), an aspect ratio of ~1, with spot-welded contacts. In such a geometry the heat current is not uniform. Given κ_a ≠ κ_b, the measured "κ_b" may contain an admixture of a-axis transport.

This directly undermines the j_Q ∥ b selectivity on which the paper's novelty rests. It also affects the geometric factor and the absolute values compared with κ₀₀/T. The Wiedemann–Franz check validates internal consistency but not current uniformity. The authors should either:

- use, or justify, a geometry with a larger length-to-width ratio along b; or
- provide finite-element modeling of the heat flow, with an assessment of current-spreading and geometric-factor uncertainties.

**10. The H ∥ c threshold and the angular dependence of H* are not tested.**
For H ∥ c, the field is also perpendicular to the b-axis gap minima. The proposed mechanism therefore predicts a threshold at H*/H_c2^c ≈ a⁻²(Δ_min/Δ₀)² with the same Δ_min. Yet Fig. 4b omits H ∥ c, and Fig. 4a has only coarse low-field resolution. This is arguably the most direct internal consistency test of the b-axis attribution, and its absence weakens it.

The authors should:

- measure low-field H ∥ c data at matching resolution and test whether H* scales as predicted;
- perform continuous field-angle rotations in the ab- and bc-planes at fixed T and H just below and above H*. The pseudo-node scenario predicts a characteristic angular onset that true nodes or multiband gaps would not reproduce.

**11. The low-field isotropy and the Δ_min extraction depend on normalizing by measured H_c2.**
The claimed isotropy below H* relies on normalizing H ∥ a by H_c2^a = 11.7 T and H ∥ b by H_c2^b ≈ 23 T. In absolute field units, the H ∥ a and H ∥ b curves would not coincide below 0.18 T.

The Doppler scale is set by the orbital H_c2 and the anisotropic coherence lengths. In UTe₂, however, H_c2 is strongly affected by paramagnetic limiting and by field reinforcement and complex phases, especially for H ∥ b. Comparing H ∥ a and H ∥ b at equal H/H_c2 is therefore not a like-for-like Doppler comparison. The same issue enters the use of H_c2^a to set the vortex-lattice scale in Δ_min.

The authors should:

- present all field data in absolute units alongside the normalized plots;
- show alternative scalings, such as by the H_c2 slope near T_c or by orbital H_c2/coherence length;
- discuss which scale is appropriate.

**12. Alternative gap scenarios that also yield a threshold field are not considered.**
Other scenarios would also produce a threshold, and no discriminating test is offered:

- **Multiband small gap.** A small but isotropic or modestly anisotropic gap on one of the two quasi-2D sheets (MgB₂-like) would give H* ≈ H_c2(Δ_small/Δ_large)² with a rapid rise above it.
- **Field-induced change of order parameter** (cf. Lee et al.).
- **True nodes with finite Γ** (impurity-band crossover).

It is not explained why the data favor a momentum-localized minimum along b over a small gap on one sheet. The authors should compute these scenarios on equal footing with the pseudo-node model, normalized to the data. They should identify discriminating observables, such as the angular dependence of H*, the H ∥ b vs. H ∥ c response, and the H ∥ a vs. H ∥ b contrast.

**13. The theory is not compared with the data in common units.**
Fig. 4d is plotted as κΔ₀/N₀v_F²T, whereas the experiment is κ/κ_N.

- **Model.** The true-node curve reaches ~7–12 at H/H_c2 ≈ 0.01 and ~10 by ~0.05, while the pseudo-node curve is ~0 below threshold.
- **Experiment.** The data show a gentle, nonzero rise below H* and only ~0.3–0.4% of κ_N at comparable reduced fields.

Without a common normalization and without vortex scattering, it is unclear whether either model describes the magnitude, rather than merely the existence, of a threshold. The high-field anisotropy between H ∥ a and H ∥ c is also not modeled. The authors should:

- plot the theoretical κ/κ_N on the same axes as the data, including vortex scattering;
- quantify the expected below-threshold slope;
- model the H ∥ a vs. H ∥ c high-field anisotropy.

**14. The model Fermi surface is unrealistic and artificially enhances the true-node vs. pseudo-node contrast.**
The calculations use a single Fermi surface with a square cross-section. The authors attribute the anomalously steep rise for Δ_min = 0 to this artificial shape. This weakens the true-node vs. pseudo-node contrast precisely where the claim is made.

UTe₂ has two quasi-2D sheets (electron and hole) with rounded-rectangular cross-sections and different velocities. It is not tested whether true nodes on a realistic Fermi surface also produce a kink-like crossover, for example from vortex scattering or nodal-region phase space. The authors should repeat the calculations with the quantum-oscillation-derived two-cylinder Fermi surface and realistic v_F anisotropy.

**15. Δ_min is weakly constrained, and the H*–Δ_min arithmetic is inconsistent.**
With a = 0.72, H*/H_c2 = a⁻²(Δ_min/Δ₀)² gives ≈0.019 for Δ_min = 0.1Δ₀, not the stated ~0.015. The observed 0.015 instead implies Δ_min/Δ₀ ≈ 0.088–0.09.

More importantly, Δ_min/Δ₀ = a√(H*/H_c2) depends linearly on the poorly defined O(1) constant a. Values of order 1–2 would change Δ_min by a factor ~2. Further uncertainty comes from:

- the choice Δ₀ ~ 2T_c ~ 4 K;
- the choice of H_c2 (concern 11);
- the Fermi-surface shape.

The authors should correct the arithmetic, propagate these dependencies explicitly, and present Δ_min/Δ₀ as an order-of-magnitude estimate with a stated range.

**16. The exclusion of non-unitary states is asserted without derivation.**
In non-unitary triplet states the two spin-branch gaps, Δ±² = |d|² ± |d × d*|, generally differ. On multiple Fermi sheets, nodes may be shifted, gapped on one branch, or retained on another.

- **Unproven key statement.** The statement that B₂u + iαA_u "merely shifts the node positions" depends on the specific d-vector components and is not demonstrated.
- **Quantitative, not categorical.** A finite residual DOS on one spin branch would strongly affect κ₀/T, which makes this a quantitative constraint rather than a categorical exclusion.
- **Limited novelty.** TRS breaking is already disfavored by Kerr and μSR [26, 27], so this constraint is less novel than presented.

The authors should give explicit d-vector and gap expressions on each Fermi sheet. These should show why non-unitary B₂u + iαA_u retains nodes while unitary B₂u + αA_u does not.

**17. The favored unitary B₂u + αA_u admixture conflicts with the single-component evidence the authors cite.**
Unitary mixing with a real relative phase combines two different irreducible representations of D₂h. This breaks the point-group symmetry and generically requires either:

- two transitions; or
- an explicit symmetry-lowering field (strain or nematic component).

That is in tension with the single sharp T_c and with the single-component evidence the authors rely on (ultrasound, Theuss et al.; strain). It is also not discussed at zero field. The alternative scenario of "quasi-protected nodes lifted by weak perturbations" is not specified: which perturbation, of which symmetry, and of what magnitude. The authors should reconcile the admixture with the single-component evidence, or present an A_u state with deep, symmetry-allowed minima along b as the minimal scenario.

**18. The argument that an accidental gap minimum is "highly improbable" is not compelling.**
The paper argues that a deep gap minimum within a single irrep is anomalous because the Fermi surfaces are smooth. This conflates Fermi-surface corrugation with gap anisotropy.

A strongly anisotropic |d(k)| with a deep minimum is generic within A_u. The d-vector components ∝ k_a, k_b, k_c carry independent, interaction-dependent coefficients. Strong momentum dependence of the pairing interaction (e.g., spin-fluctuation-mediated A_u) is symmetry-allowed rather than fine-tuned. The authors should show explicitly how a deep minimum along b arises within A_u. They should also discuss microscopic routes, such as spin–orbit coupling and inter-orbital or interband effects, as a natural alternative to "accidental" pseudo-nodes.

**19. Conflicting prior thermal-conductivity results, especially Hayes et al. [30], are not reconciled.**
Hayes et al. (PRX 2025) report robust nodal behavior in κ_a, including its field dependence, on comparable-quality crystals. The present work adopts their parameter a = 0.72 but does not explain concretely why κ_a data would appear nodal if the gap is finite. Possible reasons include:

- sample growth and quality;
- measured axes and field ranges;
- temperature range;
- extrapolation procedure;
- phonon treatment;
- interpretation of the field dependence.

Suetsugu et al. [29] is likewise not compared quantitatively. The authors should add a dedicated quantitative comparison and explain why the b-axis data resolve the disagreement. Ideally, they should also measure κ_a on a crystal from the same batch to place both directions on equal footing.

**20. The consistency checks with specific heat and penetration depth are relegated to the Supplement and are only qualitative.**
The claim that pseudo nodes explain C ∝ T³ above ~0.3 K and the anisotropic field dependence of C (Lee et al.; Metz et al.) is given in Supplementary Fig. 6 without data overlays. Penetration-depth power laws (Ishihara et al.; Bae; Iguchi) are dismissed as surface-affected without a quantitative argument.

Because the discriminating regime (T, H ≪ Δ_min, H*) is then inaccessible to all other probes, the burden rests entirely on the thin low-field κ data. The authors should move these checks into the main text, with quantitative curves for Δ_min/Δ₀ ≈ 0.1 overlaid on the published data.

**21. The vortex-core explanation for the weak H ∥ b response is not secure.**
The weakest field response, for H ∥ b (j_Q ∥ H), is attributed to negligible vortex-core conduction because of discrete Caroli–de Gennes–Matricon levels. However, the estimated level spacing (~0.13 K) is comparable to the measurement temperatures (50–300 mK). The authors should justify the negligible vortex-core contribution at these temperatures, for example by estimating thermal population and broadening of the CdGM levels.

**C. Minor**

**22. Field alignment accuracy is not reported.**
A small misalignment of H ∥ b introduces a perpendicular field component that would Doppler-shift b-axis quasiparticles and contaminate the H ∥ a vs. H ∥ b contrast. The authors should report the alignment accuracy and any angle-resolved checks.

**23. The quasi-2D Fermi-surface assumption is not tested.**
The assumption that renders B₁u (c-axis) nodes "inaccessible" is a key step in narrowing the irreducible representations. It is not tested in the data, for example by checking whether residual 3D warping affects the H ∥ c response. The authors should discuss this, or test it.

**24. Concrete complementary tests are not identified.**
The manuscript would benefit from naming feasible experiments that could independently test the pseudo-node scenario and distinguish A_u from B₁u. Examples include:

- ultra-low-T specific heat with Schottky subtraction;
- low-T penetration depth along b;
- field-angle-resolved κ;
- STM and edge-mode spectroscopy.
