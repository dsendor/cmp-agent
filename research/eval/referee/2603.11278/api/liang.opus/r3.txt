# Review outline

**Manuscript:** "Pseudo Point Nodal Superconducting Gap in Spin-Triplet UTe₂" (Hosoi et al.)

---

## 1. Significance and novelty

**Topic and context**
- The superconducting gap structure of UTe₂ is a central open question. The point-node (B₂ᵤ/B₃ᵤ) versus full-gap (Aᵤ) debate bears directly on its pairing symmetry and possible topological crystalline superconductivity.
- Bulk probes disagree:
  - specific heat shows C ∝ T³ (Metz, Lee);
  - penetration depth gives conflicting results (Ishihara, Bae, Iguchi);
  - a-axis thermal conductivity studies reach opposite conclusions (Suetsugu [29]: fully gapped; Hayes [30]: robust nodal).

**Claimed advances**
- **First b-axis data.** This is the first measurement of κ_b (j_Q ∥ b) on ultraclean molten-salt crystals (RRR ≈ 350–410) down to ~50 mK. This is the configuration most sensitive to putative b-axis point nodes, and it was previously inaccessible because crystals were too thin along b.
- **Zero field.** κ_b⁰/T is negligible (≤ 0.0017 W/K²m, far below the line-node universal value of ~0.054). There is no low-temperature non-monotonic peak of the kind predicted for clean point nodes with a non-unitary impurity phase shift.
- **Field response.**
  - κ_b grows only weakly with field.
  - A threshold kink appears at H*/H_c2^a ≈ 0.015 for H ∥ a.
  - Below H*, the H ∥ a and H ∥ b responses are nearly identical; above it, they become clearly anisotropic.
- **Interpretation.** The authors propose a "pseudo point node": a finite gap minimum Δ_min/Δ₀ ~ 0.1 along b. This is supported by a semiclassical Doppler-shift / series-resistor model (Kübert–Hirschfeld) in which a fully gapped outer ring of the vortex unit cell blocks transverse heat flow until E_H exceeds Δ_min.

**Conceptual claims beyond the data**
- Non-unitary B₂ᵤ + iαAᵤ mixing is excluded.
- The pairing state is restricted to Aᵤ or B₁ᵤ within a quasi-2D Fermi surface.
- The results are reconciled with specific heat and Knight-shift data.

**Overall assessment of novelty**
- The b-axis dataset is genuinely new and valuable.
- The "pseudo point node" concept, an intermediate between a nodal and an anisotropic full gap, is interesting and potentially unifying.
- However, the decisive discriminating evidence is a small kink resolved at the ~10⁻³ level of the normal-state κ/T. Whether the data support the strong claims ("definitively resolve") is the key issue for a top-tier journal.

---

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

**Experimental quality and uniqueness**
- The b-axis heat-current geometry directly tests the most debated (B₂ᵤ) node direction, closing a gap in the existing κ_a literature.
- Samples are of very high quality: RRR up to 410, ρ₀ ≈ 1.7 µΩcm, Fermi-liquid ρ ∝ T² maintained in field, and Wiedemann–Franz law verified. This supports the reliability of the absolute κ values.
- Field-dependence data along all three crystallographic axes, normalized by the respective H_c2, give a systematic anisotropy picture.

**Physically motivated analysis**
- The authors correctly emphasize that κ selects quasiparticles with v_F ∥ j_Q and that the Doppler shift selects v_F ∥ p_s (⊥ H). The resulting a/b/c field-orientation logic is sound and pedagogically clear (Fig. 1d,e).
- The zero-field theoretical argument (Fig. 2c) links the residual term and low-T peak to the impurity phase shift (Hirschfeld–Wölfle–Einzel; Mishra et al.). This is a nontrivial, falsifiable criterion.
- The threshold-field concept, H*/H_c2 = a⁻²(Δ_min/Δ₀)², gives a quantitative route to Δ_min. The observed kink is supported in two samples, and the difference plot (Fig. 4c) is consistent across sample combinations.

**Potential impact**
- If correct, a pseudo-node gap would reconcile:
  - power-law specific heat above ~0.3 K;
  - the anisotropic field response of C (Lee et al.);
  - the fully gapped T → 0 behavior in κ.
- It would also constrain theory, for example by excluding time-reversal-breaking non-unitary admixtures and favoring Aᵤ/B₁ᵤ. This would interest the broad unconventional-superconductivity and topological-materials communities.
- The manuscript is concise, well organized, and candid about some limitations of the Doppler-shift approach, such as the sub-threshold growth of κ.

## 3. Potential reasons for rejection

### 3.1 The zero-field evidence does not discriminate pseudo point nodes from true point nodes within the measured window

**(a) The predicted point-node peak lies largely below the measurement range.**
- In Fig. 2c (Γ/Δ₀ = 0.002, c = 0.15), the Δ_min = 0 curve:
  - peaks at T/T_c ≈ 0.01 (≈ 20 mK);
  - reaches a minimum near T/T_c ≈ 0.04 (≈ 80 mK);
  - rises monotonically thereafter.
- The lowest data are at ~50 mK (T/T_c ≈ 0.025). Above ~80 mK, both curves rise monotonically and are qualitatively indistinguishable.
- The statement that "no indication of a peak is observed" therefore relies on a handful of points in the narrow 50–80 mK window.
- The peak position and height depend strongly on Γ and c, so a slightly cleaner sample or different c shifts the peak further down. The manuscript does not show the parameter space in which the peak would fall inside 50–300 mK and remain resolvable.
- The claim that this is "generic behavior" (Supplementary) needs to be shown explicitly in terms of the experimental temperature window.

**(b) The observed T² dependence of κ_b/T is natural for point nodes and for phonons, not for a gap of ~0.4 K.**
- For axial point nodes with j_Q ∥ node direction in the clean limit, κ/T ∝ T² is the canonical expectation.
- Boundary-limited phonons also give κ_ph/T ∝ T².
- With Δ₀ ~ 4 K and Δ_min ~ 0.1Δ₀ ~ 0.4 K, the quasiparticle κ/T should show activated suppression of order exp(−Δ_min/T) ~ e⁻⁸ at 50 mK. This implies visible downward curvature on a T² plot below ~100 mK, which is not evident in Fig. 2b's inset.
- The manuscript provides:
  - no activated or two-component fit;
  - no direct quantitative comparison of the measured κ_b/T(T) with the Δ_min = 0.1Δ₀ calculation (Fig. 2c is in reduced units with no overlay on data);
  - no phonon estimate.

**(c) The phonon contribution is not separated.**
- The 1 T suppression at 0.3 K shows that quasiparticles dominate at 0.3 K. It does not establish what fraction remains at 50–100 mK, where the gap argument is made.
- Boundary-limited phonon conductivity for a ~110 × 370 µm cross-section, with possible specular reflection, could be comparable to the measured κ/T (~0.005–0.02 W/K²m) below 0.15 K. This would obscure both a small point-node peak and activated behavior.
- The sample dependence between #A and #B (~30% difference at 0.2–0.3 K) is not analyzed in terms of quasiparticle versus phonon content or disorder sensitivity.

### 3.2 The central field-threshold signature is weak, sparsely sampled, and lacks error analysis

**(a) The kink has a small magnitude and no stated uncertainties.**
- The feature in Fig. 4b,c sits at the level of Δ(κ⁰/T)/(κ⁰/T)_N ~ 0.001–0.002, defined by only ~3–5 points below H*.
- No error bars are given, and the extrapolation uncertainty is not quantified.
- κ⁰/T is obtained by polynomial extrapolation from T ≥ 50 mK. The extrapolated intercept depends on the polynomial order and fitting range, and that uncertainty may be comparable to the kink itself.
- It is unclear whether the low-field points (< 1 T) come from temperature sweeps (Fig. 3 shows only 0, 1, 2, … T) or from field sweeps at base temperature. The protocol needs to be specified.

**(b) The comparison mixes samples and orientations.**
- Fig. 4b,c compares H ∥ a in #A or #B with H ∥ b in #B only. Fig. 4c's "consistency check" subtracts #B (H ∥ b) from #A (H ∥ a).
- Given the measurable sample-to-sample differences in κ_b (Fig. 2a,b), inter-sample subtraction at the 10⁻³ level is fragile.
- A same-sample, same-mounting comparison (ideally by in-situ field rotation) is needed to establish "isotropic below H*, anisotropic above."
- The H ∥ c low-field response, where a b-axis gap minimum should also produce a threshold of comparable reduced magnitude, is not shown at matching resolution. The absence of a c-axis threshold test weakens the b-axis-specific attribution.

**(c) Extrinsic and alternative low-field origins are not excluded.** A feature near μ₀H ≈ 0.18 T (for H ∥ a) could arise from:
- the flux-entry regime near H_c1 (tens of mT in UTe₂), together with pinning and irreversibility, without field-cooled versus field-swept protocols;
- a vortex-lattice structural transition or a change in vortex-scattering regime;
- the crossover between impurity-band-dominated and Doppler-dominated regimes, E_H ~ γ, which is known to produce a field scale and a change of slope in κ(H) even for true nodes (Kübert–Hirschfeld; Vekhter–Houghton).

No hysteresis tests, temperature dependence of H*, or H_c1 and magnetization characterization are presented.

### 3.3 The theoretical modeling is only qualitatively connected to the data, and the extracted Δ_min is weakly constrained

**(a) Model and data are not quantitatively compared.**
- Fig. 4d is in units of κΔ₀/N₀v_F²T and reaches ~10 at H/H_c2 ~ 0.05. The experiment shows only ~0.4% of the normal-state value at the same reduced field.
- No common normalization is given, so the reader cannot judge whether the predicted magnitude above H* is compatible with the measured weak enhancement.
- The Δ_min = 0 curve in the same model rises "very rapidly" because of the assumed square Fermi-surface cross-section. This model-specific artifact undermines its use as a discriminator.
- Fig. 4d also does not compare the two scenarios under identical normalization with the experimental curve.

**(b) Internal inconsistency and parameter sensitivity of the H*–Δ_min relation.**
- With a = 0.72, H*/H_c2 = a⁻²(Δ_min/Δ₀)² gives 0.019 for Δ_min = 0.1Δ₀, not the stated ~0.015. Equivalently, the measured H* implies Δ_min/Δ₀ ≈ 0.09.
- More importantly, a is uncertain by a factor of order 2, so Δ_min/Δ₀ is uncertain by a comparable factor.
- The Δ₀ ~ 2T_c ~ 4 K choice, and the use of H_c2^a (which is Pauli- and anisotropy-affected) to set the vortex-lattice scale, add further uncertainty. The manuscript should present Δ_min as an order-of-magnitude estimate with a stated range.

**(c) The model's prediction fails below H*, and the explanation is ad hoc.**
- The series-resistor Doppler model predicts κ ≈ 0 below H*. The data instead show a finite, roughly linear, orientation-independent rise.
- The authors invoke non-local transport (mean free path ≳ width of the forbidden region). In these ultraclean samples that condition is likely satisfied, since ℓ ≫ ξ₀ and the inter-vortex spacing at 0.1 T is ~100 nm. This raises the question of whether a sharp threshold should survive at all.
- A more appropriate treatment is needed to show that the kink is robust. Options include quasiclassical Eilenberger calculations (e.g., Adachi et al. [36]), Brandt–Pesch–Tewordt-type approaches, or at least a non-local Doppler average.

**(d) Alternative gap scenarios are not tested.**
- A multiband scenario, in which one quasi-2D sheet has a small but isotropic or modestly anisotropic gap (MgB₂-like), would also give a threshold scale set by the small gap.
- It is not explained why the data favor a momentum-localized minimum along b over a small gap on one sheet. The b-axis specificity and the H ∥ a versus H ∥ b contrast should be modeled for both scenarios.

### 3.4 Overstated conclusions and insufficient reconciliation with conflicting literature

**(a) The claims of definitiveness exceed the evidence.**
- The abstract states that the data "definitively resolve the gap anisotropy" and "exclude non-unitary mixing." Given points 3.1–3.3, the evidence is suggestive rather than definitive.
- Experimentally, a "pseudo point node" with Δ_min ~ 0.1Δ₀ is an anisotropic full gap. Its distinction from a true node at the measured temperatures (T_min ≈ 0.12Δ_min) is marginal.

**(b) The exclusion of non-unitary states is asserted without derivation.**
- For triplet non-unitary states, the two spin-branch gaps |d|² ± |d × d*| generally differ. Whether B₂ᵤ + iαAᵤ "merely shifts" nodes, or leaves one branch nodal and the other gapped, depends on the d-vector components.
- No explicit gap expressions are given.
- Time-reversal-symmetry breaking is already disfavored by Kerr and μSR [26, 27], so the new constraint is less novel than presented.
- Unitary mixing B₂ᵤ + αAᵤ of different irreducible representations requires symmetry breaking (e.g., strain or a field). This is not discussed in zero field.

**(c) There is no direct confrontation with Hayes et al. [30].**
- Hayes et al. concluded "robust nodal behavior" from κ_a on comparable-quality samples. The present work cites it, and even adopts its a = 0.72, but does not explain the discrepancy. Possible factors include sample growth, field orientation, extrapolation method, and interpretation of the field dependence.
- Penetration-depth evidence for nodal excitations is dismissed as surface-affected without quantitative argument.
- The claimed consistency with specific heat (C/T ∝ T² above 0.3 K) is relegated to the supplement without showing data overlays.

**(d) Additional interpretive assertions are weakly supported.**
- **Vortex-core argument.** The suppression of vortex-core conduction is attributed to discrete CdGM levels. Yet the estimated level spacing (~0.13 K) is comparable to the measurement temperatures, which weakens the argument.
- **Quasi-2D Fermi surface.** The quasi-2D assumption used to render B₁ᵤ nodes "inaccessible" is a key step in narrowing the irreducible representations. It is not tested in the data, for example by checking whether residual 3D warping affects the H ∥ c response.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

### 4.1 Strengthen the zero-field analysis quantitatively
- Extend measurements to ~15–20 mK where possible, so that the temperature range in which the point-node peak or minimum is predicted is actually covered.
- Fit κ_b/T(T) with competing forms, presenting residuals and fit statistics:
  - activated (anisotropic gap with Δ_min as a free parameter);
  - clean point-node power law;
  - impurity-band forms;
  - each plus an explicit phonon term (κ_ph = βT³, with β estimated from sample dimensions, sound velocities, and surface roughness).
- Overlay theory curves on the data in physical units using measured N₀ and v_F, and show the region of (Γ, c) space in which a point-node peak would be detectable above 50 mK.

### 4.2 Make the threshold-field signature robust
- Acquire dense low-field data (Δμ₀H ≲ 20 mT between 0 and 0.5 T) at several fixed temperatures, with error bars from extrapolation and fitting-range variation.
- Demonstrate the temperature evolution of H*, which should smear as T → Δ_min.
- Perform all orientation comparisons on the same sample, ideally by in-situ field rotation within the ab and bc planes. Use both field-cooled and field-swept protocols, and include magnetization to locate H_c1 and the irreversibility field.
- Show whether an analogous threshold exists for H ∥ c, as expected for a b-axis gap minimum. Test disorder dependence (samples with lower RRR) to see whether H* tracks Δ_min, as the model implies, or the impurity bandwidth γ.

### 4.3 Upgrade the theoretical modeling and quantify uncertainties
- Go beyond the local series-resistor Doppler model by using quasiclassical Eilenberger calculations, or at least a non-local average over the quasiparticle mean free path.
- Use a realistic quasi-2D Fermi surface rather than a square cross-section.
- Compare true nodes with finite Γ (impurity-band crossover E_H ~ γ), pseudo-nodes, and a multiband small-gap model on equal footing, normalized to the data.
- Correct or clarify the H*–Δ_min arithmetic for a = 0.72. Report Δ_min/Δ₀ as a range reflecting the uncertainties in a, Δ₀, and the choice of H_c2.
- Explain why a sharp kink should survive in ultraclean samples where ℓ exceeds the vortex spacing.

### 4.4 Temper the claims and broaden the context
- Replace "definitively resolve" and "excluding non-unitary mixing" with language proportionate to the evidence.
- Provide explicit d-vector and gap expressions for the B₂ᵤ + iαAᵤ and B₂ᵤ + αAᵤ scenarios, and discuss the symmetry requirements for unitary inter-irreducible-representation mixing at zero field.
- Add a dedicated discussion reconciling these results with Hayes et al. [30] and with the penetration-depth data. Move the specific-heat comparison (Supplementary Fig. 6) into the main text with data overlays.
- Discuss microscopic routes to a symmetry-allowed strongly anisotropic Aᵤ gap (spin–orbit coupling, inter-orbital or interband effects) as a natural alternative to "accidental" pseudo-nodes. Clarify the testable predictions for STM and edge-mode spectroscopy that distinguish Aᵤ from B₁ᵤ.
