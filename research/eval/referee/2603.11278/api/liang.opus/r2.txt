Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Open problem addressed.** The gap structure of UTe₂ is among the most contested questions in unconventional superconductivity. Bulk thermal-transport studies disagree on whether point nodes exist along b: Suetsugu et al. report a full gap, while Hayes et al. report "robust nodal behavior."
- **New measurement.** The manuscript reports the first b-axis thermal conductivity (j_Q ∥ b) on ultraclean molten-salt-grown crystals (RRR ≈ 350–410), measured down to ~50 mK. This is the configuration that should be maximally sensitive to the putative B₂u point nodes. It addresses a gap left by earlier κ_a-only studies, which were limited by crystal dimensions.
- **Central new observation.** For H ∥ a, κ₀_b/T shows a kink at a low threshold field, H*/H_c2^a ≈ 0.015 (≈0.18 T).
  - Below H*, the response is nearly isotropic between H ∥ a and H ∥ b.
  - Above H*, a pronounced anisotropy develops.
- **Interpretation.** The authors read the threshold as the field at which the minimal Doppler shift at the vortex-cell boundary exceeds a finite gap minimum. This gives Δ_min/Δ₀ ~ 0.1, i.e., "pseudo point nodes" along b.
- **Symmetry claims.** The authors further claim to exclude B₂u (and B₃u) symmetry and non-unitary (TRS-breaking) mixed states, favoring A_u or B₁u on a quasi-2D Fermi surface.
- **Assessment.** If robust, the result would be of broad interest, because it would:
  - reconcile apparently nodal power laws (C ∝ T³) with fully gapped transport;
  - constrain the topological classification;
  - introduce a transport signature (threshold field) for small gap minima that could apply to other anisotropic superconductors.
- **Caveat on novelty.** Novelty rests heavily on (i) the low-field kink and (ii) model-dependent zero-field arguments. Both need substantially stronger support before the strong conclusions ("definitively resolve," "excluding non-unitary mixing") are justified.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Experimental advance.** b-axis κ on high-quality crystals, down to ~50 mK, with:
  - in-situ resistivity on the same contacts;
  - a reported Wiedemann–Franz check;
  - two samples with different RRR showing qualitatively consistent behavior.
- **Comprehensive data set.** Field-dependent data along all three crystallographic axes, normalized by the respective H_c2. The data show:
  - a clear hierarchy of field response (H ∥ a > H ∥ c > H ∥ b);
  - strong field suppression of κ_b at 0.3 K, supporting a dominant quasiparticle contribution.
- **Residual term.** The residual κ₀_b/T (≤ 0.0017 W/K²m) is far below the universal line-node value (≈0.054 W/K²m). This credibly rules out symmetry-imposed line nodes in the b direction.
- **Physically transparent mechanism.** The threshold-field picture is simple: Doppler shift versus Δ_min, with a "thermally insulating" gapped ring in the vortex unit cell for transport normal to the vortices. It yields a falsifiable relation, H*/H_c2 = a⁻²(Δ_min/Δ₀)².
- **Unifying potential.** A small-but-finite gap could reconcile conflicting literature (specific heat, penetration depth, κ_a studies). The manuscript makes a reasonable attempt to discuss NMR, specific heat, quantum-oscillation-derived Fermi-surface topology, and topological consequences.
- **Theory support.** Theory by a co-author (I. Vekhter) is included. It provides calculations for both zero-field κ(T) and field-dependent κ(H) for true versus pseudo nodes, rather than a purely qualitative argument.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**3.1 The zero-field argument against true point nodes is not decisive and may be untestable in the measured temperature window.**

- **The predicted peak lies below base temperature.**
  - The key discriminator is the low-temperature peak in κ/T predicted for point nodes with j_Q ∥ nodes and finite c = cot δ (Fig. 2c, Γ/Δ₀ = 0.002, c = 0.15).
  - In Fig. 2c this peak appears at T/T_c ≲ 0.01, i.e., ≲ 20 mK. That is below the ~50 mK (T/T_c ≈ 0.024) base temperature.
  - Absence of the peak in the data is therefore not evidence against nodes unless the authors show that, for physically plausible (Γ, c), the peak or its high-T flank should fall within 50–300 mK.
  - The robustness claim is deferred to the SI. The peak position and amplitude depend strongly on Γ and c, which are not independently constrained. For example, Γ could be estimated from ρ₀ via scattering rate, and phase shift from the normal-state residual.
- **The observed T² dependence does not discriminate.**
  - The observed T² dependence of κ_b/T (Fig. 2b inset) is precisely what is expected for clean point nodes with heat current along the nodal direction above any impurity band.
  - It is also the boundary-limited phonon law, κ_ph/T ∝ T².
  - For Δ_min ≈ 0.1Δ₀ ≈ 0.4 K, activated behavior would only become apparent at T ≪ 0.4 K. The manuscript gives no quantitative fit showing that a pseudo-node model fits κ_b(T) better than a nodal one between 50 and 300 mK.
- **The phonon contribution is not separated.**
  - The claim of quasiparticle dominance rests on field suppression at 0.3 K.
  - At the lowest temperatures, where the extrapolated κ₀/T is ≲ 0.003 of the normal state, even a small phonon term can dominate the fit and bias the polynomial extrapolation.
  - No phonon estimate is given, for example from sample dimensions and sound velocities, or from data above H_c2.
- **The extrapolated residual is tiny compared with the normal state.**
  - The normal-state κ_N/T expected from L₀/ρ₀ is ~1.4 W/K²m. The quoted upper bound, 0.0017 W/K²m, is ~0.1% of κ_N/T.
  - Error bars on the polynomial extrapolation are not shown, nor is the sensitivity to the fitting functional form or range.

**3.2 The central low-field "kink" is weakly established experimentally and susceptible to alternative explanations.**

- **Statistical weakness.**
  - The kink in Fig. 4b at H/H_c2^a ≈ 0.015 is defined by only a few data points in the range 0–0.03 H_c2^a, i.e., below ~0.35 T.
  - Each point is a polynomial extrapolation (Fig. 3) to T → 0, with no error bars.
  - The changes involved are ~0.001–0.002 in normalized units. That is comparable to the zero-field extrapolation uncertainty itself.
  - A two-slope fit versus a smooth convex curve (e.g., H ln H or H^{1/2}) is not statistically compared.
- **Normalization dependence of the "isotropy" below H*.**
  - Isotropy relies on normalizing H ∥ a by H_c2^a = 11.7 T and H ∥ b by H_c2^b = 23 T. These are high-field values for a material with field-reinforced and complex phases for H ∥ b.
  - In absolute field units, the H ∥ a and H ∥ b curves would not coincide below 0.18 T.
  - The scaling choice therefore materially affects the claimed qualitative change. A plot in absolute field units and in alternative scalings (e.g., by H_c2 slope near T_c, or by coherence length) is needed.
- **Mixing samples in the difference plot.**
  - Fig. 4c compares H ∥ a and H ∥ b using #B for both, and also #A (H ∥ a) minus #B (H ∥ b).
  - Sample-to-sample differences in RRR, geometric factor, and extrapolation make cross-sample subtraction of sub-percent effects questionable.
- **Vortex scattering is a strong competing explanation.**
  - In ultraclean crystals (ρ₀ ≈ 1.7 μΩcm), the quasiparticle mean free path is likely microns. The intervortex spacing at 0.18 T is ~100 nm.
  - The authors themselves note strong vortex-induced suppression at 0.3 K.
  - A crossover between (i) Doppler enhancement and (ii) vortex scattering, or the field at which the intervortex distance becomes comparable to ℓ, could readily produce a kink or a change in slope without any gap minimum.
  - Other effects can also introduce low-field structure: H_c1 and demagnetization (especially relevant given the plate-like geometry), flux pinning and trapped-flux history, and vortex-lattice structural transitions.
  - None of these are discussed or excluded. FC versus ZFC protocols, field-sweep direction, and hysteresis are not reported.
- **Missing H ∥ c low-field data.**
  - For H ∥ c, the field is also perpendicular to the b-axis gap minima. The proposed mechanism therefore predicts a threshold at H*/H_c2^c ≈ a⁻²(Δ_min/Δ₀)² with the same Δ_min.
  - Fig. 4b omits H ∥ c, and Fig. 4a has coarse low-field resolution.
  - This is a missing internal consistency test, and arguably the most direct check of the interpretation.

**3.3 The theoretical modeling is not quantitatively connected to the data, and its own caveats undermine the threshold argument.**

- **Incompatible units.**
  - Fig. 4d is plotted as κΔ₀/N₀v_F²T, while the experiment is κ/κ_N.
  - In the model, the true-node curve rises to values ~7–12 at H/H_c2 ≈ 0.01, while the pseudo-node curve is ~0 below threshold.
  - The experiment shows a gentle, nonzero rise below H* and very small absolute values (~0.3% of κ_N).
  - Without conversion to the same units and inclusion of vortex scattering, it is unclear whether either model describes the magnitude, rather than merely the existence, of a threshold.
- **Arithmetic inconsistency in Δ_min.**
  - With a = 0.72, H*/H_c2 = a⁻²(Δ_min/Δ₀)² gives ≈ 0.019 for Δ_min = 0.1Δ₀, not 0.015. The observed 0.015 implies Δ_min/Δ₀ ≈ 0.088.
  - This is a minor difference, but it highlights that Δ_min is set by an O(1) parameter a, so the extracted "~0.1" carries a factor ~2 uncertainty.
  - The dependence on a, Fermi-surface shape, and Δ₀ (taken as ~2T_c ~ 4 K) should be propagated explicitly.
- **Unrealistic Fermi surface in the model.**
  - A square-cross-section Fermi surface with nodes or minima along b is used.
  - The authors attribute the very steep true-node rise to this artificial shape, which weakens the true-node versus pseudo-node contrast precisely where the claim is made.
  - The model should use the two quasi-2D cylinders determined by quantum oscillations, with realistic v_F anisotropy.
- **The authors' own caveat undercuts the mechanism.**
  - The authors acknowledge that the semiclassical "series-resistor" (Kübert–Hirschfeld) picture fails when ℓ exceeds the width of the "forbidden" ring. They offer this to explain why κ/T rises below H*.
  - In these ultraclean crystals that condition is likely satisfied at all relevant fields. The same caveat would then wash out the threshold itself.
  - An estimate of ℓ versus R − R* as a function of field is needed to show the threshold should survive.
- **Unexplained field ordering.**
  - The observed weakest response for H ∥ b (j_Q ∥ H) is attributed to negligible vortex-core conduction via CdGM level spacing (~0.13 K).
  - That spacing is comparable to the measurement temperatures (50–300 mK), so this explanation is not secure.
  - The anisotropy between H ∥ a and H ∥ c at high fields is also not modeled.

**3.4 Symmetry and mechanism conclusions are overstated and partly in tension with cited evidence.**

- **Non-unitary states are excluded too strongly.**
  - The abstract states that the results exclude non-unitary mixing. In non-unitary triplet states, the two spin-split gaps Δ±² = |d|² ± |d × d*| generally differ. On multiple Fermi sheets, nodes may be shifted, gapped on one branch, or retained on another.
  - The paper's statement that B₂u + iαA_u "merely shifts the node positions" requires an explicit d-vector analysis.
  - Furthermore, a finite residual DOS on one spin branch would strongly affect κ₀/T. That is a quantitative point, not a categorical exclusion.
- **The favored unitary mixing conflicts with the single-component evidence the authors rely on.**
  - The proposed alternative, unitary mixing B₂u + αA_u with a real relative phase, couples two different irreducible representations. This requires either:
    - a second transition, or
    - explicit lowering of the orthorhombic symmetry (a nematic/strain component).
  - The authors cite ultrasound (Theuss et al.) and strain data as evidence for a single-component order parameter. They should reconcile this.
  - The alternative "quasi-protected nodes lifted by weak perturbations" scenario is not specified concretely: which perturbation, which symmetry, and what magnitude.
- **The "anomalous/improbable" argument is not compelling.** Arguing that a pseudo node is "anomalous" because the Fermi surfaces are smooth conflates Fermi-surface corrugation with gap anisotropy. Strong momentum dependence of the pairing interaction (e.g., spin-fluctuation-mediated A_u with deep minima) is symmetry-allowed and generic, not fine-tuned.
- **Literature reconciliation is incomplete.**
  - Hayes et al. reported field-dependent κ_a interpreted as nodal. The present paper adopts their parameter a = 0.72 but does not explain why their data should be reinterpreted.
  - Penetration-depth power laws and specific-heat anisotropy are addressed only in the SI and with qualitative arguments.
  - The claim that pseudo nodes explain C ∝ T³ above 0.3 K is plausible, but it implies that the essential discriminating regime (T, H ≪ Δ_min, H*) is inaccessible to all other probes. The burden therefore rests entirely on the low-field κ data, which, as argued above, are thin.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

**4.1 Strengthen the zero-field analysis quantitatively.**
- Extend measurements below 50 mK if possible, or show explicitly, over a realistic (Γ, c) parameter range constrained by ρ₀, that the predicted nodal peak or upturn would lie within the measured window.
- Fit κ_b(T)/T for both samples with full pseudo-node and true-node models, including an explicit phonon term (boundary-limited, using the measured sample dimensions). Report the best-fit Δ_min and its confidence interval.
- Show error bars and the sensitivity of κ₀/T to the extrapolation range and functional form.
- Clarify the sample geometry: length ≈ width ≈ 360–370 μm, an aspect ratio near unity. Assess current-spreading and geometric-factor uncertainties, given the in-plane anisotropy between κ_a and κ_b.

**4.2 Make the threshold field experimentally unambiguous.**
- Take dense, continuous field sweeps at fixed lowest temperatures (e.g., 50, 80, 120 mK), with error bars and both FC and ZFC protocols. This would exclude flux-history, H_c1, and demagnetization artifacts.
- Measure low-field H ∥ c data and test whether H* scales as predicted with H_c2^c and the same Δ_min.
- Add fine angular rotations in the ab and bc planes to map how H* evolves with field angle relative to the b-axis minima.
- Track the temperature dependence of the kink, which should smear out for T ≳ Δ_min ~ 0.4 K.
- Present all field data in absolute units alongside the H_c2-normalized plots, and avoid cross-sample subtractions in the key figure.
- Additional samples, including one of lower RRR, would test whether H* is intrinsic or controlled by the mean free path or disorder.

**4.3 Upgrade the theory and make it directly comparable to experiment.**
- Use the quantum-oscillation-derived two-cylinder Fermi surface with realistic v_F.
- Include vortex scattering and finite-ℓ nonlocality, or at least estimate ℓ versus the intervortex spacing and versus the width of the gapped ring as a function of H.
- Plot the theoretical κ/κ_N on the same axes as the data.
- Propagate the dependence of Δ_min on a and Δ₀, and correct the H*–Δ_min arithmetic. Explicitly quantify the expected below-threshold slope and the high-field anisotropy between H ∥ a and H ∥ c.
- Justify the negligible vortex-core contribution for H ∥ b at T comparable to the CdGM spacing.

**4.4 Temper and sharpen the symmetry and mechanism discussion, and reconcile it with the literature.**
- Replace "definitively resolve" and "excluding non-unitary mixing" with conclusions proportionate to the evidence.
- Provide explicit d-vector examples for the non-unitary versus unitary mixed states, including spin-split gaps on each Fermi sheet.
- Address how B₂u + αA_u (real) is compatible with single-component thermodynamic and ultrasound evidence. Alternatively, present an A_u state with deep, symmetry-allowed minima along b as the minimal scenario.
- Directly compare with Hayes et al. and Suetsugu et al.: sample quality, measured axes, field ranges, and why the present b-axis data resolve the disagreement.
- Move the specific-heat and penetration-depth consistency checks from the SI into the main text, with quantitative curves for Δ_min/Δ₀ ≈ 0.1.
- Identify a concrete, feasible complementary test, such as ultra-low-T specific heat with Schottky subtraction, low-T penetration depth along b, or field-angle-resolved κ.
