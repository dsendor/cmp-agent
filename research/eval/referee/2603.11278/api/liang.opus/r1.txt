# Review outline

## 1. Significance and novelty

- **Open question addressed.** The submission tackles one of the most contested issues in UTe₂: whether point nodes exist along the b-axis. Answering it constrains the pairing irreducible representation (A_u, B₁u, B₂u, B₃u) and the predicted topological surface states.
- **First b-axis bulk measurement.** The main experimental novelty is the first b-axis thermal conductivity, κ_b, with j_Q ∥ b.
  - It was enabled by large molten-salt-grown crystals (RRR ≈ 350–410).
  - It reaches ~50 mK.
  - It uses field orientations along all three crystallographic axes.
  - Previous κ studies (Suetsugu et al.; Hayes et al.) measured only κ_a. As the authors correctly argue, κ_a is weakly sensitive to quasiparticles near putative b-axis nodes.
- **Conceptual claim.** The authors propose a fully gapped state with "pseudo point nodes" (Δ_min/Δ₀ ≈ 0.1 along b). The main evidence is a low-field threshold H*/H_c2^a ≈ 0.015 for H ∥ a.
  - Below H*, κ_b is isotropic with respect to field direction.
  - Above H*, a strong anisotropy between H ⊥ j_Q and H ∥ j_Q develops.
  - The proposed mechanism is a semiclassical "thermally insulating" Doppler-shift barrier in the vortex unit cell. This is a potentially useful new diagnostic for small gap minima, distinct from true nodes.
- **Potential impact.** If correct, the result would:
  - reconcile apparently conflicting thermal transport, specific heat and penetration depth reports;
  - favor A_u (or effectively B₁u) pairing;
  - bear directly on the predicted Majorana edge modes.
- **Assessment.** Novelty is genuine. However, the strength of the central claim rests on a small-amplitude, low-field feature and on a highly simplified model. Whether the evidence reaches "definitive" status is therefore the crux of the evaluation.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **Directionally appropriate probe.**
  - κ_b with j_Q ∥ b is the configuration that should be maximally sensitive to b-axis nodal quasiparticles.
  - It fills a clear gap in the literature, where conclusions were drawn from κ_a.
- **High-quality, cross-checked data.**
  - Two crystals with high RRR are studied.
  - The Wiedemann–Franz law is verified in the normal state.
  - In-situ resistivity is measured in the same setup.
  - Measurements extend to ~50 mK, below the Schottky-dominated range that limits specific heat.
  - The pronounced quasiparticle peak below T_c and the strong field suppression at 0.3 K support quasiparticle dominance at intermediate temperatures.
- **Small residual term.**
  - The residual term (κ_b⁰/T ≤ 0.0017 W/K²m) is far below the universal line-node value of ~0.054 W/K²m.
  - This convincingly rules out line nodes in the ab-plane, consistent with κ_a.
- **Multi-orientation field study.**
  - Data are taken for H ∥ a, b, c and normalized by the respective H_c2.
  - This provides a rich, internally comparable dataset.
  - The weak growth for H ∥ b despite j_Q ∥ H is an informative observation on vortex-core transport. The Caroli–de Gennes–Matricon level-spacing argument (~0.13 K) is plausible.
- **Falsifiable theoretical framework.**
  - The zero-field calculation (Hirschfeld–Wölfle–Einzel-type, finite phase shift) predicts a low-T peak for true nodes with j_Q ∥ nodes.
  - The field calculation (Kübert–Hirschfeld series-resistor) predicts a threshold field H* = a⁻²(Δ_min/Δ₀)² H_c2.
  - Together they give the community concrete, testable predictions.
- **Broader consistency.**
  - The paper discusses how the pseudo-node scenario could reconcile C ∝ T³ and the anisotropic field dependence of specific heat.
  - It also argues why NMR Knight-shift data cannot uniquely fix the irrep.
  - Both are useful contributions to the debate.

## 3. Potential reasons for rejection

### R1. The zero-field evidence against b-axis nodes is not decisive and partly internally inconsistent.

- **Phonon contribution not separated.**
  - Boundary-limited phonons give κ_ph ∝ T³, i.e., κ_ph/T ∝ T². This is precisely the "nearly T²" dependence highlighted in Fig. 2b.
  - Quasiparticle dominance is inferred from field suppression at 0.3 K. That argument does not establish the quasiparticle fraction at 50–150 mK, where the conclusions are drawn.
  - The ratio of crystal size (~110–370 µm) to phonon mean free path, and any phonon–vortex or phonon–electron scattering in field, are not discussed.
  - Without a quantitative separation (e.g., a fit κ/T = a + bT^α with physical bounds on b, or comparison with field-induced changes), the T-dependence cannot be attributed to the gap structure.
- **Power law instead of activation.**
  - The pseudo-node scenario implies Δ_min ≈ 0.1Δ₀ ≈ 0.4 K for Δ₀ ≈ 4 K.
  - At 50–150 mK (T/Δ_min ≈ 0.12–0.4), the quasiparticle κ/T should then be exponentially suppressed. Fig. 2c indeed shows a near-zero plateau up to T/T_c ≈ 0.08 (~170 mK).
  - The data instead show a smooth power law with appreciable κ_b/T ≈ 0.01 W/K²m at ~0.1 K.
  - No direct fit of the measured κ_b(T) with the pseudo-node model is presented. The calculated curves in Fig. 2c are not overlaid on the data, nor converted to absolute units.
- **"Absence of a low-T peak" is fine-tuned.**
  - The argument relies on specific scattering parameters (Γ/Δ₀ = 0.002, c = 0.15). The authors themselves acknowledge that the unitary limit (c → 0) does not produce the peak. They also state that very clean unitary samples are consistent with the vanishing residual term.
  - The peak height and position depend sensitively on Γ, c and on the nodal dispersion (linear vs. quadratic point nodes, number of Fermi sheets crossing the node direction).
  - The Supplementary "generic behavior" claim needs to be demonstrated across the physically relevant parameter space, including strong-scattering phase shifts suggested by other UTe₂ studies.
  - Otherwise "no peak" cannot exclude true nodes.
- **Extrapolation uncertainty unreported.** The residual-term bound (0.0017 W/K²m) and all κ⁰/T values in Fig. 4 come from polynomial extrapolation. No uncertainty analysis is given, e.g., dependence on polynomial order or fitting window, or the effect of the lowest-T points.

### R2. The key experimental signature (the threshold-field kink) is weakly established and possibly extrinsic.

- **Small effect, no error bars.**
  - The kink at H*/H_c2^a ≈ 0.015 (~0.18 T) sits at extremely small normalized values. In Fig. 4b,c, (κ⁰/T)/(κ⁰/T)_N ≈ 0.003, and the difference Δ(κ̃⁰/T)^DS is ~0.001.
  - These are values extrapolated to T → 0.
  - Only a handful of field points lie below and around H*. Error bars, extrapolation uncertainties and reproducibility under repeated sweeps are not shown.
  - Fig. 3 displays only ≥1 T curves, so the raw low-field temperature sweeps underlying the kink are not visible.
- **Cross-sample comparisons.**
  - H ∥ b was measured only on #B and H ∥ c only on #A. H ∥ a was measured on both, so a same-sample comparison exists only for H ∥ a vs. H ∥ b on #B.
  - The "isotropy below H*" and the "anisotropy above H*" partly rely on cross-sample differences between crystals with different RRR, which the authors show affects κ.
  - The pink symbols in Fig. 4c explicitly subtract #B from #A.
  - Sample-dependent impurity scattering, geometry factors and residual phonon terms can easily produce differences at the 10⁻³ level.
- **Low-field extrinsic effects not addressed.** At 0.1–0.3 T several effects can produce kinks or slope changes in κ(H):
  - H_c1 and entry into the mixed state;
  - geometry-dependent demagnetization, which differs strongly between H ∥ a (in-plane, plate thickness along c) and H ∥ b;
  - flux pinning and field-history effects (field-cooled vs. zero-field-cooled);
  - vortex-lattice structural transitions;
  - the crossover from vortex scattering of quasiparticles to vortex-induced excitation, well known in clean superconductors as a minimum or plateau in κ(H).

  The field protocol, demagnetization corrections and H_c1 values are not reported.
- **Field alignment.** Small misalignment of H ∥ b introduces a perpendicular component that would Doppler-shift b-axis quasiparticles. The alignment accuracy and any angle-resolved checks are not given.
- **Sample geometry.**
  - The crystals are ~360 µm long (∥ b) and ~370 µm wide (∥ a), an aspect ratio of ~1, with spot-welded contacts.
  - In such a geometry the heat current is not uniform. Given κ_a ≠ κ_b, the measured "κ_b" may contain an admixture of a-axis transport.
  - This directly undermines the directional selectivity on which the j_Q ∥ b argument rests.
  - It also affects the absolute values compared with κ₀₀/T. The Wiedemann–Franz check validates internal consistency but not current uniformity.

### R3. The theoretical model is too simplified to extract Δ_min, and alternative explanations are not excluded.

- **Semiclassical "insulating barrier" picture.**
  - The Kübert–Hirschfeld series-resistor approach assumes local transport coefficients. The authors concede (p. 6) that it fails when the mean free path ℓ exceeds the width of the "forbidden" ring.
  - For ρ₀ ≈ 1.7 µΩcm in a heavy-fermion metal, ℓ is plausibly of order a micron or more. The intervortex spacing at H* ≈ 0.18 T is ~100 nm.
  - Hence ℓ ≫ R at and below H*, the regime where the barrier picture should break down.
  - The observed rise of κ below H*, which the model does not reproduce, is consistent with this breakdown.
  - Without an estimate of ℓ and a nonlocal treatment (quasiclassical Eilenberger or Brandt–Pesch–Tewordt-type calculations, as in Adachi et al.), the existence and position of a kink are not robust predictions.
- **Unrealistic Fermi surface.**
  - The model uses a single Fermi surface with a square cross-section. The authors acknowledge that this produces the anomalously rapid rise for Δ_min = 0.
  - UTe₂ has two quasi-2D sheets (electron and hole) with rounded-rectangular cross-sections and different velocities.
  - Whether true nodes on a realistic Fermi surface also produce an apparent "kink-like" crossover (for example from vortex scattering or nodal-region phase space) is not tested.
- **Δ_min is weakly constrained.**
  - Δ_min/Δ₀ = a√(H*/H_c2) depends linearly on the poorly defined constant a (0.72 here). Values of order 1–2 would change Δ_min by a factor of ~2.
  - The normalization by measured H_c2 is questionable: the Doppler scale is set by the orbital H_c2 and anisotropic coherence lengths.
  - H_c2 in UTe₂ is strongly affected by paramagnetic and field-reinforcement effects, especially H_c2^b ≈ 23 T.
  - Comparing H ∥ a and H ∥ b at equal H/H_c2 is therefore not a like-for-like Doppler comparison.
- **Multiband alternative not considered.**
  - A small but isotropic gap on one of the two Fermi sheets, as in MgB₂, would also give a threshold field H* ≈ H_c2(Δ_small/Δ_large)² with a rapid rise above it.
  - Other alternatives are not considered either: a field-induced change of order parameter (cf. Lee et al.), or a vortex-scattering crossover.
  - No discriminating test against the single-band anisotropic "pseudo-node" scenario is offered.

### R4. The conclusions on symmetry and topology are overstated and partly inconsistent; reconciliation with prior work is insufficient.

- **Overstated language.**
  - The abstract claims the data "definitively resolve" the gap anisotropy and "exclude non-unitary mixing".
  - Given R1–R3, the data are at most consistent with a small gap minimum along b. A demonstration would require same-sample, angle-resolved, error-quantified evidence.
- **Internally inconsistent symmetry discussion.**
  - The paper argues that an accidental gap minimum within a single irrep is "highly improbable". Yet a strongly anisotropic |d(k)| with a deep minimum is generic within A_u, since the d-vector components ∝ k_a, k_b, k_c carry independent, interaction-dependent coefficients.
  - Conversely, the proposed unitary B₂u + αA_u admixture combines two different irreps of D₂h. This breaks the point-group symmetry and generically requires two transitions or an external symmetry-breaking field. That is in tension with the single sharp T_c and with the single-component evidence the authors cite (ultrasound, strain).
  - The exclusion of non-unitary states rests on the claim that B₂u + iαA_u "merely shifts" the nodes. This depends on the specific d-vector forms and should be demonstrated explicitly.
- **B₁u is not excluded.** The data do not distinguish A_u from B₁u (c-axis nodes inaccessible on quasi-2D sheets). The topological conclusions (flat-band vs. dispersive Majorana modes) therefore remain undetermined by this work.
- **Insufficient engagement with conflicting results.**
  - Hayes et al. (PRX 2025) report robust nodal behavior in κ_a, including field dependence, on comparable-quality crystals.
  - The paper does not explain concretely why κ_a data could appear nodal if the gap is finite. Possible reasons include differing sample quality, temperature range, extrapolation procedure or phonon treatment.
  - Penetration-depth power laws (Ishihara et al.) and specific heat (Metz, Lee) are addressed only qualitatively or relegated to the Supplement.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Quantify and model the zero-field data directly.**
   - Separate phonon and quasiparticle contributions, e.g., by:
     - fitting κ/T = a + bT^α with bounds on the phonon term from crystal dimensions;
     - comparing with a high-field reference;
     - measuring a deliberately disordered or smaller sample.
   - Overlay calculated κ_b(T) for true nodes and pseudo-nodes, in absolute units with realistic N₀ and v_F, on the measured data.
   - Show the full (Γ, c) parameter space for which a low-T peak is expected and test whether any nodal parameter set reproduces the data.
   - Report extrapolation uncertainties for all κ⁰/T values (polynomial order, window), and explicitly test for activated behavior consistent with Δ_min ≈ 0.4 K.
2. **Strengthen the threshold-field evidence experimentally.**
   - Measure all three field orientations on the same crystal, ideally on both crystals.
   - Densify field points below ~0.5 T and show the raw low-field T-sweeps and isothermal field sweeps at the lowest T.
   - Report field history (field-cooled/zero-field-cooled), H_c1, demagnetization corrections and alignment accuracy.
   - Add continuous field-angle rotation in the ab-plane (and bc-plane) at fixed T and H just below and above H*. The pseudo-node scenario predicts a characteristic angular onset that true nodes or multiband gaps would not reproduce.
   - Use, or at least justify, a sample geometry with a larger length-to-width ratio along b. Alternatively, provide finite-element modeling of the heat flow for the near-square crystal.
3. **Upgrade the theory and test alternatives.**
   - Estimate the quasiparticle mean free path and compare it with the intervortex spacing at H*.
   - Go beyond the local series-resistor approximation (quasiclassical Eilenberger, Brandt–Pesch–Tewordt, or the Adachi et al. approach).
   - Include vortex scattering and a realistic two-sheet UTe₂ Fermi surface with appropriate velocities.
   - Quantify the sensitivity of Δ_min to the constant a and to the choice of orbital vs. measured H_c2.
   - Explicitly compute the multiband small-isotropic-gap scenario and identify observables that discriminate it from a single-band anisotropic minimum, such as angular dependence and H ∥ b vs. H ∥ c response.
4. **Recalibrate claims and sharpen the symmetry discussion.**
   - Replace "definitively" with language commensurate with the evidence.
   - Clarify that A_u and B₁u remain indistinguishable here, and that topological implications are conditional.
   - Present explicit d-vector forms showing:
     - how a deep minimum along b arises within A_u, and whether it is natural rather than "accidental";
     - why non-unitary B₂u + iαA_u retains nodes while B₂u + αA_u does not;
     - how a multi-irrep admixture is compatible with a single T_c and single-component evidence.
   - Add a dedicated, quantitative comparison with Hayes et al. and Suetsugu et al. covering sample quality, temperature range, extrapolation and phonon treatment. Ideally, also measure κ_a on a crystal from the same batch to place both directions on equal footing.
   - Move the specific-heat consistency check (Supplementary Fig. 6) into the main text with direct data comparison.
