# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** "Pseudo Point Nodal Superconducting Gap in Spin-Triplet UTe₂" (Hosoi et al., arXiv:2603.11278)

---

## 1. Summary of the submission

The authors report b-axis thermal conductivity κ_b on two molten-salt-grown UTe₂ crystals (RRR ≈ 410 and 350) down to ~50 mK. Measurements were taken in zero field and with fields along all three crystallographic axes. Their main findings are:

1. **Negligible residual term.** The upper bound is κ⁰_b/T ≲ 0.0017 W/K²m, far below the universal line-node value (~0.054 W/K²m). κ_b/T follows roughly T² with no low-temperature peak. They argue such a peak would be expected for point nodes along the b axis in the clean limit with non-unitary scattering.
2. **A weak kink near H*/H^a_c2 ≈ 0.015 for H ∥ a.** Below the kink, κ⁰_b/T is nearly isotropic between H ∥ a and H ∥ b; above it, the response is strongly anisotropic.
3. **A model interpretation.** Using a Kübert–Hirschfeld "series-resistor" Doppler-shift model, the kink is read as the field at which the Doppler shift at the vortex-cell boundary exceeds a finite gap minimum. This gives Δ_min/Δ₀ ≈ 0.1 along b.

They conclude that UTe₂ is fully gapped with "pseudo point nodes" along b. They argue this excludes non-unitary B₂u + iA_u-type mixing and favours A_u (or B₁u, with c-axis nodes inaccessible on quasi-2D Fermi sheets).

**Significance.** The question is important and the measurement is new. κ_b on ultraclean crystals is the configuration most sensitive to putative b-axis nodes, and it has been missing from the κ_a-based debate (Suetsugu et al. vs. Hayes et al.). The data quality appears high, and the field-orientation strategy is well motivated.

**Main problem.** The central conclusion rests on two pieces of evidence that are, in my view, not yet decisive:
- the absence of a zero-field peak predicted to lie at or below the base temperature;
- a subtle kink defined by a handful of points without error bars.

The abstract's language ("definitively resolve") is not supported at the present level of analysis.

---

## 2. Major issues

### 2.1 The zero-field temperature dependence does not discriminate nodes from pseudo-nodes in the accessible range

- **T² is the textbook point-node result.** For point nodes with heat current along the nodal direction, κ/T ∝ T² is the standard clean-limit result (N(E) ∝ E²). A boundary-limited phonon contribution also gives κ_ph/T ∝ T². The abstract states the T-dependence is "consistent with a small superconducting energy gap," but the observed T² is at least equally consistent with true point nodes.
- **The decisive feature lies below the base temperature.** In Fig. 2c the low-T peak for Δ_min = 0 sits at T/T_c ≈ 0.005–0.01, i.e. ≈10–20 mK. The lowest measured point is ~50 mK (T/T_c ≈ 0.024). The model also gives zero residual term for *both* Δ_min = 0 and Δ_min = 0.1Δ₀. The residual term therefore cannot distinguish the scenarios, and the discriminating peak is outside the data.
  - The authors should state explicitly where, in T, the point-node and pseudo-node curves first differ by more than the experimental resolution.
  - They should show the model curves overlaid on the data in physical units over 50–300 mK.
- **The parameters (Γ/Δ₀ = 0.002, c = 0.15) need justification.** The peak position and height depend strongly on both. Γ can be estimated from ρ⁰_b and band parameters; this should be done and compared with the assumed value. The authors also concede that a unitary-limit (c = 0), very clean sample would show neither a residual term nor a peak. Since nothing independent constrains c in UTe₂, this concession seriously weakens the zero-field argument.
- **An activated contribution should be visible but is not tested.** With Δ_min ≈ 0.1Δ₀ ≈ 0.4 K, the quasiparticle contribution should be close to activated (∝ e^(–Δ_min/T)) below ~100 mK. The data look power-law down to 50 mK. The authors should:
  - fit the data with a pseudo-node model plus phonons, and with a point-node model plus phonons;
  - show that the former is statistically preferred.

### 2.2 The phonon contribution at the lowest temperatures is not quantified

The 1 T suppression at 0.3 K shows that quasiparticles dominate at 0.3 K. It does not establish this at 50–100 mK, where the zero-field signal is ~1% of the normal state.

- An estimate of the boundary-limited phonon conductivity for the ~110 µm thickness should be given, using the known sound velocities and Debye temperature.
- If phonons account for a substantial fraction of κ_b/T below ~0.15 K, both the "T²" argument and the "no peak" argument need revisiting.

### 2.3 The statistical significance of the kink at H* is not established

The kink in Fig. 4b,c is the key new observation, but:

- **No error bars.** Fig. 4 shows no uncertainties. κ⁰_b/T comes from polynomial extrapolation (Fig. 3), and the extrapolated values at low field are ~0.002–0.004 of the normal state. The fit-form and fit-range uncertainty is plausibly comparable to the anisotropy differences in Fig. 4c (~3–7 × 10⁻⁴). Please give error bars and show robustness to the extrapolation procedure, including the choice of polynomial order and lower cutoff.
- **Few points below the kink.** Only ~3–4 field values lie below H*. Denser field steps near 0–0.4 T (H*_a ≈ 0.18 T) would greatly strengthen the claim.
- **Cross-sample comparisons.** H ∥ b was measured only on #B, H ∥ c only on #A, and H ∥ a on both. The "isotropic below H*" statement compares different crystals with different RRR. The agreement in Fig. 4c is encouraging, but measuring all orientations on one crystal (by rotation or remounting) would remove this ambiguity.
- **Field history.** Field-cooled vs. zero-field-cooled protocols, flux pinning, and proximity to H_c1 and demagnetization effects for in-plane fields on a platelet should be stated and ruled out as sources of low-field structure.

### 2.4 The H ∥ c low-field data are missing and overstated

The abstract states that below the threshold κ_b/T "remains isotropic for all field orientations." Fig. 4b, however, shows only H ∥ a and H ∥ b at low field.

H ∥ c is also perpendicular to j_Q, so it is a natural consistency test. If Δ_min is a property of the gap along b, a threshold should also appear for H ∥ c at H*_c/H^c_c2 ≈ a⁻²(Δ_min/Δ₀)², up to anisotropy corrections. The authors should:
- show the low-field H ∥ c data, or temper the abstract;
- ideally, show that a threshold appears where expected.

### 2.5 The H_c2 normalization is not obviously appropriate for a Doppler-shift comparison

The comparison of H ∥ a and H ∥ b uses H/H_c2 with μ₀H^a_c2 = 11.7 T and μ₀H^b_c2 = 23 T. The Doppler-shift scale E_H ∝ Δ₀√(H/H_c2^orb) involves the *orbital* critical field, i.e. the coherence lengths in the plane perpendicular to H.

- For H ∥ b, the 23 T figure is affected by field-reinforced superconductivity and the complex high-field phase diagram.
- H^a_c2 may be partly paramagnetically limited for some d-vector orientations.

The "isotropy below H*" therefore depends on a normalization whose physical basis is uncertain. Please:
- show the same comparison in absolute field and with orbital fields estimated from the low-field H_c2 slopes;
- discuss whether the conclusions survive.

### 2.6 Alternative explanations for the low-field structure are not considered

A rapid low-field change of κ(H), followed by a change of slope, is well known in multiband superconductors with one small-gap band (e.g. MgB₂). There the characteristic field is set by the small gap's own coherence length. UTe₂ has distinct electron and hole cylinders (as the authors emphasize), so a small but more isotropic gap on one sheet is a natural alternative to a pseudo-node on one sheet.

The authors should discuss how their data distinguish:
- (i) a deep minimum along b on one sheet;
- (ii) a small gap on one Fermi-surface sheet;
- (iii) a combination of the two.

The directional anisotropy above H* would help here only if it is quantitatively tied to b-directed velocities.

### 2.7 The theoretical model is applied in a regime where the authors acknowledge it fails, and the comparison is only qualitative

- **The measured increase below H* is unexplained.** The series-resistor model predicts an essentially zero κ below H* and a sharp onset. The data instead show a finite, comparable increase in both orientations below H*. That increase is then attributed to an unspecified mechanism, but it is half of the phenomenon being interpreted.
- **The mean free path may invalidate the local picture.** With ρ₀ ≈ 1.7 µΩcm, the quasiparticle mean free path in the superconducting state is likely much larger than the intervortex spacing near 0.2 T (~100 nm). This is the regime in which the local, series-connected transport coefficient is least justified, as the authors themselves note.
- **The model Fermi surface affects the Δ_min = 0 curve.** The square cross-section is chosen, and the very rapid Δ_min = 0 rise is attributed to it. This affects precisely the comparison used to exclude true nodes.
- **No direct theory–data comparison is given.** Please put theory and data on a common normalization, convert κΔ₀/N₀v_F²T to κ/κ_N, and show them on the same plot. Discuss whether the *magnitude* of the post-threshold slope is reproduced.
- **The extracted gap carries several model dependences.** The value Δ_min/Δ₀ ≈ 0.09 follows from H*/H_c2 = 0.015 with a = 0.72. It therefore depends on a, on the H_c2 normalization (see 2.5), and on the model. The quoted "~0.1" should carry a realistic uncertainty range.

### 2.8 The discussion of pairing symmetry and mechanism needs revision

- **Unitary mixing conflicts with the authors' own evidence.** The proposed unitary mixed state B₂u + αA_u (real relative phase) mixes two irreducible representations. It therefore lowers the crystal symmetry and generically requires two transitions or accidental degeneracy. This sits uneasily with the single-component evidence the authors cite (ultrasound [25], strain [24]). The tension should be addressed explicitly.
- **The non-unitary claim needs support.** The statement that B₂u + iαA_u "merely shifts the node positions while preserving the nodes" should be demonstrated or referenced. In non-unitary states the two spin branches have gaps |d|² ± |d × d*|, and the behaviour of each branch should be specified. The abstract's claim of "excluding non-unitary mixing" is stronger than the main-text argument supports.
- **The "fine-tuning" argument looks overstated.** In a strong-SOC A_u state, d = (c₁k_a, c₂k_b, c₃k_c) gives |Δ| ≈ c₂k_F along k_b on a cylinder. A deep minimum along b therefore arises whenever c₂ ≪ c₁, e.g. near-degeneracy with B₂u. No Fermi-surface corrugation fine-tuning is needed. The claim that the pseudo-node is "anomalous from the perspective of conventional pairing mechanisms" should be reconsidered in this light.

### 2.9 The disagreement with Hayes et al. [30] deserves direct discussion

Ref. [30] reports "robust nodal behaviour" in κ_a on high-quality crystals. The present paper cites it mainly for the parameter a and the absence of a peak. A direct comparison would clarify whether the disagreement lies in data or interpretation. This should cover:
- sample quality and RRR;
- residual terms;
- field dependence;
- whether the present pseudo-node model reproduces their κ_a(H).

## 3. Minor issues

1. **Fig. 1 caption inconsistency.** It states "d,e: … H applied perpendicular and parallel to point nodes, respectively," but panel d is labelled H ∥ nodes and panel e H ⊥ nodes, as is the later caption text.
2. **Normal-state normalization.** Please state how (κ_b/T)_N is obtained, e.g. L₀/ρ⁰_b or measured above H_c2. Also state the conditions (field, temperature) under which the Wiedemann–Franz law was verified.
3. **Geometric-factor uncertainty.** The sample is nearly square in the ab plane (~360 × 370 µm) with spot-welded contacts, so the absolute κ may carry a sizeable geometric uncertainty. The comparison with κ₀₀/T relies on absolute values, so this uncertainty should be quoted.
4. **Field alignment.** Give the alignment accuracy. A small misalignment of H ∥ b toward a would introduce Doppler contributions for b-moving quasiparticles and affect the "reference" curve.
5. **Notation.** The notation alternates between κ⁰_b, κ^b_0 and κ_b0; please unify it.
6. **Specific-heat formula.** In the expression C/T ∝ −(H/H_c2) ln[H/H_c2 + (Δ_min/aΔ₀)²], the bracket placement is garbled. "H ≪ H⋆" also uses a different star symbol than elsewhere.
7. **Typographical errors.** "Moreover, The Fermi"; "the the excitations"; "quasipartcles"; "sample#A".
8. **Fig. 2b inset.** Label the gray dashed line directly.
9. **Fig. 3.** Show the extrapolation fits with uncertainty bands. Please also comment on the curve crossing for H ∥ b (field suppression at 0.3 K but enhancement at low T).
10. **Vortex-core argument.** The core level spacing Δ₀²/ε_F ≈ 0.13 K is comparable to the measurement temperatures. The argument that core conduction is negligible should therefore be phrased as T-dependent, and its consistency with the H ∥ b data across 50–300 mK checked.
11. **Supplementary claims.** The specific-heat consistency (Supplementary Fig. 6) is an important supporting claim; a summary figure in the main text would help.
12. **Title and terminology.** "Pseudo point node" is, operationally, a strongly anisotropic full gap. Please define the term carefully and avoid implying a symmetry-based protection that is not established.

---

## 4. Recommendation

**Major revision.** The data set is valuable and the κ_b geometry is the right one for this question. However, the claimed exclusion of b-axis point nodes currently rests on:
- a zero-field signature predicted below the measured temperature range;
- a low-field kink of unquantified significance, interpreted with a model the authors acknowledge is only qualitative in this regime.

To support the conclusions, I would expect at minimum:
- error bars and robustness tests for κ⁰_b/T(H);
- denser low-field data;
- the H ∥ c low-field test;
- a phonon estimate;
- direct quantitative theory–data comparisons for both the T and H dependences;
- a discussion of the multiband alternative and of the normalization choice.

The abstract and conclusions should then be calibrated to what these analyses support. Phrases such as "definitively resolve" and "excluding non-unitary mixing" should be softened unless the additional analysis substantiates them.
