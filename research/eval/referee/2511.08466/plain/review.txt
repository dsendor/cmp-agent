Referee report

Manuscript: "Kinetic Inductance of Few-Layer NbSe2 in the Two-Dimensional Limit" (arXiv:2511.08466v1)

1. Summary

The authors terminate quarter-wave Al CPW resonators with hBN-encapsulated, edge-contacted NbSe2 flakes. They compare each resonator with an Al-terminated control and extract the added inductance with a transmission-line model (SI Eq. 12). Twelve devices from about 2 to 52 nm (AFM) give Lk,sq from 4 pH up to 1.2 nH per square. The thinnest device is identified as a monolayer. The power dependence gives self-Kerr coefficients of -0.006 to -14.7 Hz/photon, and the DC-bias dependence of device D6 gives I* = 0.4 mA. The thickness dependence is analysed with a "clean plus dirty" BCS/GL interpolation, Lk,sq = meff/(n0 e^2 d) + hbar Rs/(1.764 kB Tc) (Eq. 2). From this the authors claim a crossover from clean-limit to dirty-limit behaviour as thickness decreases. They attribute it to surface scattering, multiband effects and Pearl-regime confinement. They also argue that NbSe2 suits compact, nearly linear superinductors such as fluxonium shunts.

The measurement is a sensible and useful one. It extends the resonator-termination technique this group used for twisted graphene to an exfoliated crystalline superconductor. It reaches the 1 to 2 layer limit, which amorphous films cannot reach. The raw extraction is internally consistent: I recomputed Lk from fr,Al and fr,Al-NbSe2 with SI Eq. 12 for D1 and D6 (1.198 nH and 0.271 nH, against 1.195 and 0.274 in Table 1). The Lk,sq = Lk W/L conversion also checks for all twelve rows. The data set has value. However, I do not think the central physical claim (the clean-to-dirty crossover) follows from the data, and the application claims are stated more strongly than the reported loss and inductance values support. Details follow.

2. Major issues

2.1 The dirty-limit model does not describe the data once its coefficient is fixed by theory.
Eq. 2 has no free coefficient on the dirty term. It is hbar Rs/(pi Delta0). SI Eq. 23 carries the pi, but it is dropped between Eq. 23 and Eq. 24 and in main-text Eq. 2. With Delta0 = 1.764 kB Tc this coefficient is hbar/(pi 1.764 kB) = 1.38 pH K/Ohm, or 4.33 pH K/Ohm if the pi is (incorrectly) omitted. The fitted slope in Fig. 4c is 16.3, and SI Fig. S5 gives a second, different slope of roughly 10.5. That is 12x (or about 4x) larger than the theory the fit is meant to test, and the text does not mention it. Put concretely, the monolayer point has Rs/Tc = 68.6 Ohm/K (Khestanova values). The dirty term then predicts about 94 pH, while the device measures 1195 pH. The 4-layer device (Rs/Tc = 12.5) gets a dirty term of about 17 pH against a measured 282 pH. With the physical coefficient, the dirty term is a few percent of Lk for every multilayer device and under 10% for the thinnest one. So the data, read through the authors' own model, point to a strongly suppressed superfluid stiffness (the "clean" term), not to dirty-limit physics. The crossover conclusion holds only if the slope is treated as a free parameter, and then Eq. 2 is no longer being tested.

2.2 The inferred mean free path contradicts the films' own sheet resistance.
SI Eq. 26 with the Table S3 ratio gives xi0/l of about 14 for the thinnest device, so l is about 0.6 nm if xi0 is about 8 nm. A 2D Drude estimate from the same Rs = 240 Ohm gives kF l = (h/e^2)/Rs, about 100. That corresponds to l of order 10 to 20 nm, in line with the literature values the authors cite (15 to 17 nm). A sub-nanometre mean free path in a crystalline film with RRR 5 to 10 is not plausible. This is another sign that the excess inductance is not impurity-scattering physics as encoded in Eq. 2.

2.3 The measured Lk is an order of magnitude above both BCS terms. This needs to be confronted directly.
With the paper's own lambda = 230 nm, the London term mu0 lambda^2/d is 44 pH at 1.5 nm, 7.5 pH at 8.8 nm and 1.3 pH at 52 nm. Adding the dirty term with the correct coefficient, the model underestimates Lk by about 27x (D1), 11x (D6) and 3x (D12). Equivalently, the effective penetration depth implied by Lk,sq d/mu0 is about 0.4 um (D12), 0.9 um (D6) and 1.2 um (D1), against the 230 nm used in the Pearl-length section. The intercept of SI Fig. S5a (A of about 720 pH nm) implies lambda_L of about 750 nm. That is also inconsistent with the 230 nm used two sections later. The authors should state plainly that the measured inductance is far above BCS expectations at all thicknesses. They should then discuss the candidates: suppressed n_s/m* in thin layers, extrinsic series contributions (section 2.5), thickness or geometry errors (section 2.6), and degraded edges. It would also help to compare with the direct few-layer Pearl-length measurements of their Ref. 66. The Pearl length follows directly from their data as Lambda = 2 Lk,sq/mu0, which is about 1.9 mm for D1 and 0.2 mm for D6, not 10 to 70 um.

2.4 The crossover metric depends on how the clean term is normalized, and the fit rests on a single literature point.
- Table S3 divides every Lk,sq by a single constant, 80 pH (the Fig. 4c intercept). The clean term in Eq. 2 scales as 1/d, so this normalization is inconsistent with the model. The parenthetical "the 1/d dependence for the clean-limit term can be considered" understates the effect. With the SI's own A/d (A of about 720 pH nm), the ratio runs from about 0.8 to 2.5, not 0.7 to 15. A ratio below 1 (the 10-layer point, 56 pH, below its own clean estimate) makes xi0/l negative in Eq. 26, which is unphysical. This should be addressed rather than reported as the start of a monotonic trend.
- The Fig. 4c line passes almost exactly through the monolayer point ((1195 - 80)/68.57 = 16.3) and the 4-layer point. Both the slope and the "dirty-limit" interpretation therefore rest on one point whose Rs and Tc come from a different sample in the literature (Ref. 38). Its layer number is also uncertain (section 2.6). The remaining five points span Rs/Tc of only 1.4 to 5.3 Ohm/K and scatter around the line.
- For the points labelled "this work", Rs and Tc come from separate DC flakes (Table S1), not from the microwave devices. Table S1 itself shows sizeable flake-to-flake spread at similar thickness, for example Rs = 45 vs 60 Ohm for 5 vs 4 nm and 29 vs 31 Ohm at 6 nm. This must be stated in the main text, with uncertainties propagated. Better still, measure Rs(T) on the microwave devices themselves. The DC bias line through the NbSe2 to ground should allow a two-terminal R(T) above the Al Tc, with a resistance step at the NbSe2 Tc.

2.5 Series contributions from the contacts and leads are not bounded.
The extraction attributes the whole added inductance to the NbSe2 sheet. Each device has two 1D edge contacts with a cross-section of only W x d (a few um x 1 to 2 nm for the thinnest flakes). Any interfacial kinetic or Josephson inductance scales inversely with that area, and so grows as d shrinks. That would produce exactly a steeper-than-1/d trend and could also add nonlinearity. The contact verification (Table S1, Fig. S1) was done on separate DC devices. It shows zero resistance, but it constrains neither contact inductance nor contact critical current. Also, the claimed noise floor of 0.01 Ohm is not consistent with the +/-0.3 Ohm scatter visible in Fig. S1c. The device set allows a partial test that the authors should perform and report: fit Lk = L_contact + Lk,sq (L/W) across devices of similar thickness but different aspect ratio. My rough check on D6/D7 and D4/D5 suggests L_contact of order +/-25 pH near 7 to 9 nm. That is small there, but it is comparable to or larger than Lk for D8 to D12, and it is untested for D1 (L/W = 1). A dedicated length series at fixed thickness would settle this. Similarly, the geometric inductance of the NbSe2 strip and of the evaporated Al electrodes differs from the control. For D10 to D12 (total Lk of 8 to 14 pH) this is plausibly of the same order as the signal.

2.6 Thickness and layer-number assignment.
- The SI states "1 layer = 1 nm" and the top axis of Fig. 4a uses it. The 2H-NbSe2 layer spacing is about 0.63 nm (c/2). Either the AFM thicknesses overestimate the NbSe2 by up to about 60%, or the layer assignments are off. Either way d enters Lk,sq d, rho = Rs d, A and lambda directly. The authors should reconcile AFM and Raman explicitly and use one consistent thickness with error bars.
- The "monolayer" headline (abstract, conclusions, Fig. 4) rests on D1. AFM gives 1.99 nm. Raman gives a weak feature at 19.04 cm^-1 that the authors fit as N = 1.5. A monolayer has no shear mode. Using their own relation, w_s = 29.39 cos(pi/2N), 19.04 cm^-1 corresponds to N of about 1.8, and a bilayer is expected near 20.8 cm^-1. The evidence is at least as consistent with a bilayer. A non-integer N cannot be used as a thickness of "1.5 nm" for a sample called a monolayer. If D1 is a bilayer, the monolayer claim in the abstract, the use of monolayer Rs/Tc from Ref. 38, and the slope of Fig. 4c all change. Please provide stronger layer identification (optical contrast, high-frequency Raman, or a cleaner shear or breathing-mode spectrum, ideally after encapsulation) or soften the claim.
- The shear-mode frequency saturates for N of 6 or more (Fig. S7a: 28.6 to 29.2 cm^-1 for N = 6 to 9), so Raman cannot resolve neighbouring layer numbers there. The assignment uncertainty should be propagated.

2.7 "Approximate 1/d dependence" does not describe the data, and the fit form is not stated.
Lk,sq d from Table 1 is not constant. It is about 1.1 nH nm for 4 to 9 nm, 0.56 nH nm at 10 to 12 nm, and 0.15 to 0.2 nH nm at 25 to 52 nm, with about 1.8 nH nm for D1. Between D7 (8.9 nm, 107 pH) and D8 (10.1 nm, 56 pH) Lk,sq halves for one extra layer. The dashed "1/d fit" in Fig. 4a cannot be a pure A/d: a curve through about 1370 pH at 1.2 nm would give about 140 pH at 13 nm, while the plotted curve is near 20 pH there. Please state the functional form and the fitted parameters, show a log-log plot with a fitted exponent, and explain why D10 to D12 are omitted from Fig. 4a (the caption says nine devices; Table 1 lists twelve). The abstract's "scales inversely with the number of layers" and the later "steeper-than-expected inverse thickness scaling" contradict each other. Pick the description the data support.

2.8 Control-resonator reference and uncertainties.
fr,Al varies from 5.21 to 5.62 GHz across devices. By the paper's own simulation (200 MHz per 0.1 nH), a spread of that size is equivalent to about 0.2 nH, which is more than Lk for nine of the twelve devices. Please state where the control for each device sits (same chip or a different chip). Report the reproducibility of nominally identical Al-terminated resonators, and propagate that systematic into Lk. No uncertainties are given anywhere in Table 1. Fig. 4 shows error bars of roughly +/-50 pH without explaining their origin. Kerr coefficients, I*, d, Rs and Tc also come without uncertainties.

2.9 Superinductor and application claims.
- Loss: correcting Qi for the inductive participation p = Lk/L0 (L0 = 2.627 nH from the SI) and the Al-control Qi of 1e5, my estimate of the effective quality factor of the NbSe2 element plus contacts is only about 3e3 (D1) to 4e3 (D6). That is far below what fluxonium shunts or high-impedance resonators need. The paper attributes the reduced Qi to contacts and polymer residue but does not separate these from intrinsic NbSe2 loss. Fig. S4c shows Qi rising with power, which suggests TLS loss. A participation-ratio analysis versus thickness and length, and an honest statement of current loss levels, are needed before the abstract and conclusions call the material "well suited" to fluxonium.
- Magnitude: 1.2 nH/sq at 5 GHz is a reactance of about 38 Ohm per square. Reaching R_Q = 6.4 kOhm needs about 170 squares of the thinnest material, with the loss above. The Introduction's superinductor framing should be quantified against this.
- Kerr comparison: absolute K values from devices with very different inductive participation, volume and mode frequency (grAl at 4.5 MHz, JJ arrays at 8 MHz) are not a like-for-like comparison. A material figure of merit would be fairer, such as I* or j* = I*/(W d) at a given Lk,sq, or K normalized to participation and to the number of squares. By that measure D6 gives j* of about 1.1e10 A/m^2. Please compare it with the cited NbN, NbTiN and grAl values.
- Fig. 4b: at matched thickness (3 to 10 nm) the NbSe2 points in Fig. 4b lie at or below the TiN, NbTiN and grAl points in the same panel. "One of the highest sheet kinetic inductances at comparable thicknesses" is true only at 1 to 2 layers, where no comparison points exist. The real advantage, superconductivity persisting in crystalline films at 1 to 2 layers, should be stated that way. The comparison also omits the highest-Lk disordered films (InOx, high-resistivity grAl, WSi, NbSi) cited in the Introduction.

2.10 Pearl-regime discussion.
SI Eq. 27 reads 2 lambda/d. It should be 2 lambda^2/d, as in the main text. With lambda = 230 nm, Lambda ranges from about 2 um (52 nm) to about 70 um (1.5 nm), not 10 to 70 um. For D10 to D12, Lambda (2 to 4 um) is comparable to the widths (2.3 to 4.1 um), so "width much smaller than the Pearl length" does not hold for all devices. More fundamentally, for W << Lambda standard thin-film electrodynamics gives a uniform current density and Lk = Lk,sq L/W with no extra enhancement. A mechanism by which "geometric confinement" raises Lk,sq needs to be stated and estimated quantitatively, or the paragraph should be removed. As written it adds a third untested explanation to the conclusions.

3. Further technical points

3.1 Kerr derivation (SI Eqs. 6 to 8). For L(I) = Lk0 (1 + I^2/I*^2), the quartic energy term is (1/4) Lk0 I^4/I*^2 if L is the differential inductance, which is what the DC-bias resonator measurement probes. It is (3/4) if L is the secant inductance. It is not 1/2. Eq. 8 gives a positive K, while the measured K is negative; fix the sign convention. With the stated inputs (fr = 4.79 GHz, Lk = 0.274 nH, L0 = 2.627 nH, I* = 0.4 mA), I obtain K/2pi of about 5.7 Hz from Eq. 8 as written, not 3.8 Hz. Please give the exact inputs used. "Good agreement" with -1.7 Hz is a factor of 2 to 3. The photon-number calibration (nominal 70 dB attenuation, no cable-loss calibration) probably carries an uncertainty of that size, which should be quoted. Also define L0 (which lumped-element mapping of the lambda/4 mode, and current at which node).

3.2 I* and Ic. The fitted I* = 0.4 mA for D6 equals the 0.4 mA critical current of a different 8 nm DC flake of unstated width (Fig. S1e). In GL or Usadel treatments, I* in Eq. 1 is larger than the depairing current by a factor of order a few. Please explain whether I* was fitted freely and how well a bias range of at most 15 to 30 uA ((I/I*)^2 below 0.006) constrains it.

3.3 Pairing-symmetry inference. A quadratic Lk(I) at small bias is generic for any fully gapped superconductor and is measured here over a tiny range. Presenting it as "consistent with anisotropic nodeless pairing" is over-interpretation unless the authors test for a linear-in-|I| term at the level a nodal state would produce.

3.4 Temperature dependence is missing. Lk(T) below the Al Tc, plus the NbSe2 Tc of the actual microwave devices, would directly test the two-gap and gap-suppression arguments now invoked only qualitatively. It would also tie Rs/Tc to the same samples.

3.5 The Kerr coefficients of D10 to D12 (-0.006 to -0.008 Hz/photon) are tiny. Please show that they exceed the nonlinearity of the Al-terminated control, for which only "no observable shift" up to -60 dBm is stated, and give their uncertainties.

3.6 The factor a in Eq. 25 is 0.75 in the SI but set to 1 in the main text. Either use 0.75 or justify setting it to 1, since it changes the coefficient by 25%.

4. Minor issues and internal inconsistencies

- Text: fr,Al - fr,Al-NbSe2 = 0.45 GHz for D6. Table 1 gives 5.34 - 4.79 = 0.55 GHz.
- Onset of the power shift: text says -90 dBm (green arrow); the Fig. 3b caption says -100 dBm (green arrow).
- DC bias "up to 30 uA" in the text; Figs. 3e and 3f extend to about 15 uA.
- Qi for D6: 2.8e4 in the text, 2e4 in the Fig. 3a inset. Baseline fr: 4.79 GHz in the text, 4.785 GHz in the caption, and the resonance in Fig. 3a is near 4.7825 GHz.
- Fig. 3c: the axis label "-1.15" should presumably be "-0.15". Delta f is defined as f(1) - f(nr), which is positive for a downshift, yet it is plotted as negative.
- Conclusions: "K/2pi = -0.008 to 14.7 Hz/photon" drops the sign. The Table 1 column unit should be Hz/photon.
- Table S3: 135/80 = 1.69, not 1.79 (or 1.75).
- SI Eq. 19: the leading mu0 is a typo (dimensions). SI Eq. 27: lambda should be lambda^2.
- Fig. S3 reports Qi = 5.985e6 for the spectator, with Qc = 1e5. Fig. S4c shows the spectator at about 5e5 to 2e6. With Qi/Qc of about 60, Qi is poorly constrained by the circle fit; quote an uncertainty or drop the number.
- Attenuation: the text gives 20/20/30 dB at 4 K, still and MXC; Fig. S2 shows -20, -10, -20, -20. The totals agree, but the stages do not.
- Table S1: the unit "Ohm/um" for Rc and entries such as "1,80" and "0.02,2" need explanation. The Fig. S1a caption describes a two-terminal measurement while the text calls it pseudo-4-probe.
- Figs. 2d to 2g draw a Si/SiO2 substrate; the text says high-resistivity Si.
- Fig. S7 caption says N = 4 to 8, but the legend includes N = 9 and 1.5.
- Introduction: n0 is described as "number of Cooper pairs per unit volume per unit thickness". With pairs, the expression needs 2e and 2m, or n0 should be the electron density. Fix the definition.
- Bulk 2H-NbSe2 is centrosymmetric; broken inversion and Ising SOC apply to odd-layer (especially monolayer) films. Please make the symmetry statement layer-specific.
- The DC transport literature contrast ("microwave response may be sensitive to the multi-band nature") is speculative. Given sections 2.1 to 2.5, the simpler explanations (extrinsic contributions, a free-slope fit, Rs and Tc from other samples) should be ruled out first.
- Data availability "upon reasonable request with the cognizance of US Government sponsors" is restrictive for a materials data set of this kind. Please deposit the data underlying the figures and Table 1.

5. Requested additions, in priority order

(a) Refit Fig. 4c and Fig. S5 with the dirty-limit coefficient fixed at hbar/(pi Delta0), using a 1/d clean term with a thickness you can defend. Report what fraction of Lk each term explains, and revise the crossover claim accordingly.
(b) Bound contact and lead contributions with an aspect-ratio or length series at fixed thickness.
(c) Give uncertainties for Lk (including control-resonator reproducibility), d, Rs, Tc, K and I*, and show Fig. 4a on log-log axes with all twelve devices.
(d) Strengthen or soften the monolayer assignment of D1.
(e) Measure Rs and Tc, and ideally Lk(T), on the microwave devices themselves.
(f) Add a participation-corrected loss analysis, and rewrite the application claims against it.

6. Recommendation

Major revision. The microwave kinetic-inductance data on encapsulated NbSe2 down to one or two layers are new and worth publishing. The extraction of Lk from resonator shifts is internally consistent, and the Kerr and DC-bias measurements are a useful start. The physical interpretation is not supported as written. With the coefficient its own theory requires, Eq. 2 explains only a small fraction of the measured inductance. The "0.7 to 15" crossover depends on an inconsistent normalization and on a single literature data point. Thickness, layer number and series-inductance systematics are not controlled. If the interpretation is rebuilt along the lines above, and the application claims are restated against the measured loss, the paper would make a solid contribution.
