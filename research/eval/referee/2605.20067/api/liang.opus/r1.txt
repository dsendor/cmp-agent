**Review outline:**

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Scientific question.** The manuscript addresses coherent, mode-resolved nonlinear coupling between antiferromagnetic magnons and optical phonons. This is a timely topic connecting ultrafast spintronics, nonlinear THz spectroscopy and proposals for hybrid magnon–phonon quantum states.
- **Material and method.** Applying double-THz-pump / optical-probe 2D coherent spectroscopy to FePS₃ is a natural and attractive choice. The material is a van der Waals zigzag antiferromagnet with:
  - a 3.7 THz magnon close to zone-folded Raman phonons (2.64, 2.83, 3.25 THz);
  - an established linear magnon-polaron hybridization;
  - strong linear birefringence (LB) readout of the order parameter.
- **Claimed novelty.** The authors report:
  - the first coherent, spectrally resolved sum- and difference-frequency (SFG/DFG) peaks between a magnon and a specific phonon (Ph₃);
  - a full set of 2nd- and 3rd-order magnonic 2D features (rectification, SHG, 2Q, NR, PP, R);
  - Zeeman bifurcation of the DFG peaks at 9 T into Mag±–Ph₃ components;
  - a Lindblad/Liouville-space model attributing the response mainly to a two-magnon–one-phonon coupling (X_a²X_b).
- **Potential impact.** If the nonlinearity is shown to be intrinsic to the material rather than to the readout, the work would:
  - establish a quantitative spectroscopic handle on cubic spin–lattice couplings in 2D magnets;
  - complement prior work on photon-mediated magnon–phonon coupling (CoF₂), magnon harmonics (orthoferrites) and THz-driven magnon polarons in FePS₃.
- **Limits of novelty.** Novelty relative to refs. 9, 34, 35, 46 (THz-driven magnon polarons and metastable magnetization in FePS₃; nonlinear magnon–phonon coupling in CoF₂) needs sharper delineation. The squeezing and entanglement discussion is speculative and numerically marginal.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **High-quality experimental data.**
  - Long-lived coherences, exceeding 100 ps (Ext. Data Fig. 2).
  - Clean nonlinear-channel extraction (S_NL = S_AB − S_A − S_B).
  - Several independent cross-checks:
    - temperature tracking of the 0.45 THz mode;
    - polarization dependence;
    - field-strength scaling;
    - magnetic-field splitting.
- **The 9 T data are the most compelling evidence.** The DFG peaks split into (0.22, 3.45) / (0.65, 3.88) and (0.22, −3.23) / (0.65, −3.23). These positions match ω_Mag± − ω_Ph3 from static magneto-THz data, which strongly supports a genuine Mag–Ph₃ mixing product (as opposed to an unrelated low-frequency mode).
- **Systematic 2D spectral assignment.**
  - Rephasing, non-rephasing, pump-probe, 2Q, DFG and SFG quadrants are clearly organized.
  - Frequency-vector diagrams (Fig. 3d, Ext. Data Fig. 4) make the pathways transparent to a broad readership.
- **Theoretical framework.**
  - A Liouville-space Lindblad framework with explicit order-by-order expansion is a sound and general tool.
  - Comparing the X_a²X_b and X_b²X_a channels is an appealing attempt to discriminate microscopic coupling mechanisms.
- **Broad interest.** The topic is relevant to nonlinear THz spectroscopy, 2D magnetism, magnonics and quantum-hybrid communities. The data could become a benchmark for first-principles spin–phonon nonlinear coefficients.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

- **(i) The intrinsic (material) origin of the nonlinear signals is not established against readout (detection) nonlinearities.**
  - **The LB readout is not a linear function of the mode amplitudes.**
    - Magnetic LB in a collinear AFM is even in the order parameter (∝ L²). Expanding about the ground state gives δLB ∝ 2L₀·δL + δL².
    - A magnetoelastic optical term ∝ δL·Q_Ph3 also generically exists.
    - Such terms generate peaks at 0 and 2ω_Mag (rectification/SHG) and at ω_Mag ± ω_Ph3 (DFG/SFG), purely from linear mode amplitudes.
  - **None of the presented controls separates readout mixing from material mixing.**
    - Linear fluence scaling of the fundamentals does not exclude this, because a product of two linear amplitudes scales quadratically, exactly as observed in Fig. 1g.
    - The 2D peak coordinates are identical for both scenarios. In a pump-A/pump-B experiment, a magnon coherence created by A (phase ∝ e^{iω_Mag τ}) multiplied in the readout by a Ph₃ coherence created by B yields a peak precisely at (ω_Mag − ω_Ph3, ω_Mag).
    - The S_AB − S_A − S_B subtraction removes only terms linear in each pulse. It does not remove detection cross-terms.
  - **Missing discriminating tests.** The manuscript does not provide:
    - probe-wavelength or probe-polarization dependence of the DFG/fundamental amplitude ratio (an intrinsic mode should be probe-independent up to its own optical coupling);
    - an estimate of the size of ΔLB relative to static LB, and of the polarization-rotation linearity of the balanced detector;
    - phase relations, or a lifetime analysis showing whether the DFG coherence decays at γ_Mag + γ_Ph3 (product) or at a distinct rate. Ext. Data Fig. 2 shows the DFG vanishing within about 30 ps while both parents persist beyond 100 ps, which requires quantitative explanation;
    - a quantitative check that the DFG polarization pattern (Ext. Data Fig. 1c) differs from the product of the Mag and Ph₃ patterns.
  - **The headline claim depends on this point.** The claim of "definitive" coherent TWM between collective excitations hinges entirely on this distinction. As it stands, the 1D and 2D data cannot discriminate the two scenarios.

- **(ii) The interpretation of the purely magnonic 2nd-order signals rests on a questionable symmetry argument.**
  - **Second-order responses arise without DMI or phonons.**
    - The authors argue that, without DMI, z-axis rotational symmetry of the precession suppresses 2nd-order magnonic responses, so magnon–phonon coupling must be invoked.
    - However, the longitudinal component of the Néel vector, L_z ≈ L₀(1 − L_⊥²/2), contains 0 and 2ω components for any finite-amplitude precession.
    - In FePS₃ the zigzag order and C2/m symmetry make the precession elliptical in general, so U(1) symmetry is not exact.
    - The Holstein–Primakoff expansion itself generates such terms.
  - **Consequences for the paper's logic.**
    - The observation of magnon rectification and SHG does not by itself imply a symmetry-breaking magnon–phonon mechanism.
    - The paper's argument that cascaded X_a²X_b terms produce these features is not unique.
  - **The single-mode treatment of the magnon doublet is inconsistent with the data.**
    - The authors state that the analysis is "insensitive to the internal structure of the degeneracy".
    - Yet the 9 T data show a Mag₊–Mag₋ DFG feature, i.e., intra-doublet nonlinear mixing.
    - This indicates that the doublet structure (and the selection rules for which branch couples to Ph₃) matters and should enter the model.
  - **Unresolved selection rule.** No symmetry (irrep) analysis explains why Ph₃, but not Ph₁, Ph₂ or Ph₅, participates in TWM, even though Ph₁ and Ph₂ are also magnon-polaron partners. This is a central selection-rule question left unaddressed.

- **(iii) The field-scaling evidence for the assigned nonlinear orders is statistically weak and internally inconsistent.**
  - **Fits are visually guided rather than quantitative.**
    - Figs. 1g, 2d,e and Ext. Data Fig. 5h show dotted H² or H³ curves.
    - There are no log–log plots, no free-exponent fits and no uncertainties.
    - In Ext. Data Fig. 5 the field range spans only 0.58–0.93 MV/cm (a factor of about 1.6, plus an origin point). Over that range, n = 2 and n = 3, or a threshold behaviour, are hardly distinguishable; the authors themselves note the "limited amount of data".
    - In Fig. 1g the lowest-field points sit at zero, consistent with a noise floor or threshold rather than a clean power law.
  - **Unexplained signal strengths and field calibration.**
    - At 9 T the in-magnet field is ≤ 0.2 MV/cm, versus 1.3 MV/cm at 0 T.
    - Quadratic scaling would predict a DFG reduction of roughly 40×, and SFG is said to vanish for this reason.
    - Yet the Mag±–Ph₃ DFG peaks in Fig. 3c appear with prominence comparable to the 0 T data. This needs quantitative normalization, signal-to-noise numbers and an explanation (e.g., field-induced resonance enhancement).
    - The driving field is H_THz, but E_THz is measured and normalized with different references (2.5 vs 1.1 vs 1.3 MV/cm) across figures. The conversion to an in-sample H field (Fresnel factors, absorption in a 20 µm flake) is not given, so cross-figure comparison of orders is unreliable.
  - **Third-order assignments are under-supported.**
    - NR, PP and R signals in a two-pump scheme can arise from AAB/ABB pathways or from cascaded second-order processes. The model explicitly invokes cascades without a Kerr term.
    - The manuscript does not separate the cascaded and intrinsic contributions, or show how they would scale differently (e.g., with sample thickness or detuning).
  - **Inconsistent mode labels.** The text states that "the linear field dependence of the infrared-active modes" rules out carrier effects, while elsewhere all observed modes are described as Raman-active, with Mag and Ph₃ driven magnetically. The logic of this exclusion argument should be corrected.

- **(iv) The theoretical modelling is under-specified, dimensionally inconsistent and only weakly discriminating; the quantum-application claims are overstated.**
  - **Parameters and units are unclear.**
    - β^b_aa = 17 meV/Å and β^a_bb = 0.017 meV/Å² are quoted, yet the Hamiltonian uses dimensionless quadratures X_b = Q/ℓ. The conversion via ℓ (mode mass, frequency) is not given.
    - The estimate β^a_bb ≈ 0.001 β^b_aa compares quantities with different units, and the extra "Q_b/l" factor is double-counted in a dimensionless formulation.
    - The values of g, μ_a, μ_det, κ, γ_φ, the pulse waveforms, the Fock-space truncation and the initial (thermal) state are not reported.
    - The simulated maps differ in absolute scale by about 10⁶ (×10⁻⁵ vs ×10⁻¹¹), and no quantitative comparison of peak ratios with experiment is attempted.
  - **The agreement with experiment is weakly constraining.**
    - Peak positions are kinematically fixed by ω_Mag and ω_Ph3, so any cubic coupling reproduces them.
    - The main discriminator is the absence of Ph₃ rectification. That conclusion depends on the assumed μ_det, which is "magnon-only", whereas experimentally Ph₃ is clearly detected through LB.
    - Ph₃ SHG, which the X_b²X_a channel predicts, is observed experimentally but is attributed post hoc to other phonon–phonon nonlinearities.
    - Quartic magnon terms (which the authors say naturally arise in Holstein–Primakoff theory) and readout nonlinearities are omitted. Hence the conclusion that "X_a²X_b is the dominant TWM mechanism" is not robustly established.
  - **The squeezing and entanglement claims are overstated.**
    - The computed effects are about −0.01 to −0.02 dB and E_N ≈ 0.006 (Ext. Data Fig. 7), oscillating about zero, with no thermal noise and an idealized pulse.
    - The experiment measures classical coherent amplitudes and provides no access to quantum fluctuations.
    - Abstract and conclusion phrases such as "entangle magnetic and vibrational excitations" and "hybrid quantum technologies", along with the cavity-magnomechanics analogy ("cooperativity of 15"), go well beyond what is demonstrated.
    - The novelty claim of a "first direct observation" also needs careful positioning against refs. 34, 35, 46 and 18.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

- **Rule out readout nonlinearity explicitly.**
  - Derive the LB response to second order in δL and Q (including L² and δL·Q magneto-optical terms) and estimate their contribution relative to the measured DFG/SFG amplitudes.
  - Perform probe-wavelength (and probe-polarization/azimuth) dependent measurements of the DFG-to-parent amplitude ratio, and calibrate the linearity of the balanced-detection signal versus ΔLB.
  - Analyze DFG coherence lifetimes and phases relative to γ_Mag + γ_Ph3. Compare the measured DFG polarization pattern with the product of the parent patterns.
  - If possible, use an independent probe (e.g., Faraday/Kerr at a different wavelength, or THz emission) to confirm that the 0.45 THz coherence exists in the material.
- **Strengthen the quantitative field-scaling and calibration analysis.**
  - Provide log–log plots with free-exponent fits and error bars for all assigned 2nd- and 3rd-order peaks, over the widest achievable dynamic range (at least one decade where possible).
  - Report the in-sample H_THz (Fresnel and absorption corrected) and use a single normalization across figures.
  - Quantify the 9 T signal levels against the 0 T data at matched fields, and explain the apparent strength of the DFG at 0.2 MV/cm.
  - Correct the "infrared-active" wording and clarify how Raman-active Ph₃ is driven (via hybridization).
- **Make the theory complete, reproducible and discriminating.**
  - Give all parameters with consistent units, including the dimensionless β obtained via ℓ. Specify the truncation, the initial state and the detection operator, and include Ph₃ in μ_det consistent with the experiment.
  - Include the magnon doublet (to capture Mag₊–Mag₋ DFG and the field dependence), quartic magnon terms and the readout nonlinearity as separate channels.
  - Compare relative peak amplitudes (DFG/Rect, SFG/DFG, DFG I/DFG II) quantitatively with experiment.
  - Add a group-theoretical analysis explaining why only Ph₃ shows TWM, and why Ph₁ and Ph₂ do not.
  - Replace the U(1) symmetry argument with a correct discussion of longitudinal and elliptical-precession contributions to 2nd-order magnonic signals.
- **Recalibrate the claims and positioning.**
  - Tone down "definitive" and the quantum-technology language. Present the squeezing and entanglement calculation as an outlook, with realistic thermal and decoherence estimates and the required parameter regime (e.g., narrowband drive amplitude needed for 1 dB of squeezing).
  - Explicitly contrast the present results with photon-mediated coupling in CoF₂ (refs. 34, 35), magnon-pair DFG in YFeO₃ (ref. 18) and THz-driven magnon-polaron control in FePS₃ (refs. 9, 46).
  - Provide a quantitative plot of DFG frequency versus ω_Mag − ω_Ph3 across temperature (Fig. 1d).
  - Include the complete peak-assignment table (Table S2) and the processing details (windowing, resolution) in the main or extended material.
