# Review of "Interlayer coupling enhanced superconductivity near 100 K in La₃₋ₓNdₓNi₂O₇" (arXiv:2510.12359)

## Summary

The authors synthesize polycrystalline La₃₋ₓNdₓNi₂O₇ (0 ≤ x ≤ 2.4) by a sol-gel route. They report four main findings:

1. **Structure and magnetism at ambient pressure.** Nd substitution contracts *a* and especially *c*, and the resistive density-wave anomaly rises from 138 to 161 K.
2. **Structural transition.** The Amam→I4/mmm transition pressure rises from ~13 GPa (x = 0.9) to ~26 GPa (x = 2.1).
3. **Superconductivity.** Resistive onset T_c reaches 93 K (x = 2.1, 34.9 GPa) and 92 K (x = 2.4). Radio-frequency (RF) transmission anomalies appear at 97–98 K.
4. **Mechanism.** Compiling their data with literature bulk and thin-film results, they find a roughly linear T_c–*c* relation (≈ −28 K/Å). They argue this demonstrates that interlayer magnetic exchange is the dominant pairing mechanism.

Extending rare-earth substitution this far and applying an RF technique to nickelates is worthwhile, and the doping-dependent structural transition pressure is a useful result. However, the headline claims rest on evidence that is not yet strong enough: "near 100 K" superconductivity, a "record" T_c, and especially a "demonstration" of the pairing mechanism. Several internal inconsistencies also need resolving.

---

## Major issues

### 1. Sample characterization is insufficient for the doping-dependent conclusions

**Actual composition is not measured.** The ED Fig. 1 caption states that the compositions are *nominal*. No EDS/WDS, ICP, or STEM data are given. This matters for three reasons:
- The *c* parameter changes by only 0.017 Å from x = 2.1 to 2.4, compared with ~0.05–0.07 Å per step at lower x. This suggests saturation, i.e., that the real Nd content of the x = 2.4 sample may be well below nominal.
- The authors themselves say x = 2.4 "may approach the upper limit of thermodynamic stability."
- Later they attribute the RF/transport discrepancy to "inhomogeneity of the Nd doping levels." This concedes that the composition is not well controlled, yet the composition is the variable on which the whole paper is built.

**Oxygen stoichiometry is not determined.** T_c in La₃Ni₂O₇₋δ is known to be very sensitive to δ. TGA or iodometric titration should be reported for each x. The annealing atmosphere should also be specified.

**Phase purity is asserted from laboratory XRD only.** Laboratory powder XRD cannot reliably exclude the alternatives that are most relevant here:
- 1313-type stacking;
- La₄Ni₃O₁₀/La₂NiO₄ intergrowths;
- Nd-rich secondary phases.

The authors invoke "other intergrowth RP phases" to explain the low-temperature anomaly in x = 2.4 (Fig. 2d,e), which contradicts the claim of pure-phase samples. STEM, or at minimum synchrotron powder data with refinement statistics, is needed. R_wp/χ² values are also not reported for any refinement in ED Fig. 1, and the x = 2.1 and 2.4 refinements have visibly larger uncertainties and residuals.

### 2. The magnetism argument is weakly supported

**Susceptibility.** The "systematically enhanced" susceptibility (ED Fig. 2b) is almost certainly dominated by the Curie–Weiss paramagnetism of Nd³⁺ (~3.6 μ_B per ion). It says nothing about Ni magnetism unless the Nd contribution is subtracted. The text implies otherwise.

**Identifying T_SDW.** T_SDW is assigned from features in dρ/dT (ED Fig. 3). There are three problems:
- For x = 2.1 and 2.4, the feature is a dip rather than the kink seen at low x, so it may not be the same transition.
- Consecutive points in Fig. 1c differ by 1–3 K, comparable to the stated ±3 K error.
- The figure labels it T_SDW, but ED Fig. 3 calls it T_DW. In La₃Ni₂O₇, both SDW and CDW-like anomalies have been reported, so the assignment should be justified (μSR, NMR, or at least magnetization anomalies).

**Conflicting literature.** Ref. 34 reports an *unaltered* density-wave transition in Nd-doped La₃Ni₂O₇. This should be discussed.

**From T_SDW to J⊥.** Even if T_SDW does increase, inferring a stronger *interlayer* exchange from it is not straightforward. T_SDW also depends on in-plane exchange, nesting, and disorder.

### 3. The radio-frequency evidence for 97–98 K superconductivity is not convincing as presented

This is the basis for the "near 100 K" in the title. I list the problems in order of importance.

**a. Direction of the discrepancy with transport.**
- Resistive onset is percolative and normally gives the *highest* T_c, because it detects the first filament. Inductive or RF methods typically give equal or lower values.
- Here RF gives a T_c ~5 K above the resistive onset.
- At comparable pressure the gap is worse: x = 2.4 at 40.3 GPa has a resistive onset of ~85 K (Fig. 2e), while RF at 41 GPa gives 98 K, a ~13 K difference.
- The proposed explanation (current-path redistribution among granules) is qualitative. It does not explain why RF would see a *higher* T_c than any resistive measurement.

**b. Missing controls.** None of the following are shown:
- The same cell measured below the superconducting pressure threshold (e.g., <20 GPa), where no anomaly should appear.
- Field dependence: a DC field should shift the anomaly to lower temperature. This is the standard discriminator.
- Dependence on modulation amplitude.
- A background run with the PTM, gasket, and Lenz lens but no sample.

The BSCCO validation was done at 0 GPa only, not in a loaded high-pressure cell.

**c. Signal quality and lineshape.**
- The anomalies are small fractions of the total signal (e.g., ~0.02 mV on 2.46 mV in ED Fig. 5d).
- The lineshape is not reproducible: steps on cooling, dips on warming, and single-point spikes (25 MHz, Fig. 3d/ED Fig. 5c).
- Some features appear in Re U and others in Im U, depending on the run.
- Attributing these differences to "intrinsic properties of the RF method" with a self-citation to a preprint is insufficient. A model of the expected response is needed.

**d. Pressure determination.** The Methods do not state how pressure was determined in the RF cells. The "36 ± 4 GPa" uncertainty is large relative to the pressure dependence of T_c.

**e. Inconsistent descriptions of the same data.**
- The Fig. 3e caption says S1 was measured at 25 MHz on warming, while the figure and ED Fig. 5f show 20 MHz.
- A stray caption fragment on p. 15 describes x = 2.1 "at 40 GPa, measured at 25 MHz," whereas ED Fig. 5c says 36 GPa.
- The Fig. 3d caption assigns 20 MHz to the real component and 25/60 MHz to the imaginary component, but ED Fig. 5 shows the relevant features in both components.

Until these points are addressed, the RF data should be described as possible signatures, and "near 100 K" should not appear in the title.

### 4. Transport data and the "record" claim

**Size of the resistive drop.** No zero resistance is reached. The drop below onset is only ~10–20% of R(150 K), so the superconducting fraction may be small. The onset is taken at a resistance maximum on a semiconducting background. Such a maximum can shift for reasons unrelated to superconductivity, so the onset criterion should be stated explicitly and applied uniformly.

**"Highest among all nickelate superconductors."** The 93 K value is compared with 92 K for Sm-doped crystals (Ref. 36). A 1 K difference is well within the uncertainty of onset determination and of pressure (ruby versus diamond-Raman calibrations differ by 5–10%, i.e., 2–3 GPa near 35 GPa). The authors should also check recent reports on Sm-doped La₃Ni₂O₇ under pressure, which may already exceed this value.

**Upper critical field.** The GL fits (Fig. 2f) extrapolate from ~6 points spanning <10 K below T_c to ~100 T, which is not a reliable estimate. The statement that "μ₀H_c2(0) is lowered by doping" is unsupported for two reasons:
- No undoped reference value is given in the paper.
- The more heavily doped x = 2.4 has the *larger* fitted H_c2(0).

**Anomalous zero-field curve.** In Fig. 2e, the 0 T curve has a qualitatively different shape from all finite-field curves: it is metallic down to 2 K with no low-temperature upturn. This suggests it was taken under different conditions and needs explanation.

**Limited sampling.** Only one run per composition appears to be shown, and x = 0.3 and 0.6 are absent from the high-pressure study. Reproducibility across samples should be documented.

### 5. Structural transition determination

**Space group assignment.** The transition is assigned from merging of the (020)/(200) peaks. This depends on resolution, and it cannot distinguish Amam from Fmmm, an intermediate phase reported in several La₃Ni₂O₇ studies. Assigning the space group properly requires tracking the tilt-related superlattice reflections. In Fig. 1d, *a* and *b* are already nearly degenerate at 20–25 GPa, so the 26 GPa value is uncertain.

**Different conditions across experiments.** XRD was done at room temperature in Ne, transport at low temperature in KBr, and RF in NH₃BH₃. The statement that the superconducting onset "approximates" the structural transition pressure compares transitions measured under different temperatures and hydrostaticity. Low-temperature XRD, or at least a discussion of this caveat, is needed.

**Oxygen positions.** "Nd doping enhances the interapical oxygen distortion" is asserted, but no refined oxygen positions or Ni–O_ap–Ni angles are reported. Such parameters would be poorly determined from high-pressure powder data in any case.

### 6. The T_c–*c* correlation does not demonstrate the mechanism

This is the central conceptual claim, and it is overstated in the abstract and conclusion ("demonstrating," "fundamental mechanism").

**a. Pressure dependence within a compound contradicts it.** Within a single compound, T_c falls at high pressure while *c* keeps shrinking:
- pristine La₃Ni₂O₇ to 100 GPa (Ref. 13);
- the authors' own x = 2.4 data, where T_c drops from 92 K at 33.8 GPa to ~85 K at 40 GPa.

The "linear relation" is obtained only by selecting "T_c measured near the onset pressure and at the maximum." This selection criterion should be stated prominently, and the full T_c(*c*) data, including the overdoped side, should be shown.

**b. Lattice parameters at the measurement conditions are unclear.**
- It is not stated how *c* was obtained for x = 1.5, which was not measured by high-pressure XRD.
- The values are room-temperature parameters in a different PTM, not low-temperature values.
- x = 2.4, the composition with the highest RF T_c, is absent from Fig. 5.

**c. The "universality" largely reflects two clusters.** Bulk samples at high pressure have short *c* and high T_c; strained films at ambient pressure have long *c* and lower T_c. Within the thin-film cluster, *c* and *a*_p are coupled through the Poisson ratio, and many other variables differ (dimensionality, interface, oxygenation, T_c criterion). The NGO-substrate film is excluded as an outlier without a physical justification. No fit uncertainty, R², or treatment of the data from different groups and T_c definitions is given.

**d. *c* is a poor proxy for J⊥.** The *c* axis includes the rock-salt spacer layers, not just the Ni–O_ap–Ni bond that controls J⊥. Compressing *c* also changes the d_z² bandwidth, the γ-pocket, and the effective carrier density. A correlation with *c* is therefore consistent with several mechanisms. Supporting the magnetic interpretation would require either:
- the apical Ni–O distance and bond angle versus x, or
- a direct J⊥ measurement (RIXS or INS) versus doping.

I recommend rephrasing to "consistent with" and discussing the alternatives.

## Minor issues

- **Introduction:** RP nickelates (octahedral NiO₆) are grouped under "square-planar nickelates," which is incorrect.
- **Resistivity trend:** The main text says the compounds become "more insulating" with doping, but the ED Fig. 2a caption says the temperature dependence of ρ is non-monotonic (increasing up to x = 1.8, then decreasing). Reconcile these.
- **Unpublished reference:** The Sm comparison data (Fig. 1a,b) come from Ref. 42, "paper in preparation." Please provide these data or cite an accessible source.
- **Fig. 1c:** The label T_SDW is inconsistent with T_DW in ED Fig. 3.
- **Fig. 4:** The superconducting region for x = 2.1 is drawn at pressures below 27 GPa, where no data are shown. Mark measured points versus extrapolation.
- **Fig. 5 caption:** It lists x = 0.9, 1.5, 2.1, while Fig. 4 uses 1.5, 2.1, 2.4. Explain the choice of compositions.
- **Typos:**
  - "adoped" → "adopted";
  - "Rigabu" → "Rigaku";
  - "Identical XRD patterns" (ED Fig. 4c) → "Synchrotron XRD patterns";
  - "The temperature dependence of χ(T) increases" is awkward;
  - remove the orphaned ED Fig. 5 caption text on p. 15–16.
- **Pressure error bars:** Give them for all transport points, reflecting the 5–10% calibration discrepancy.
- **Extended Data captions:** Several are missing (e.g., no ED Fig. 5 legend for panel-specific protocols). Check consistency throughout.

---

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The synthesis and the doping-dependent structural transition pressure are a useful contribution. The paper would be publishable in a solid form if:

1. composition, oxygen content, and phase purity are quantified;
2. the RF results are supported by field-dependent and below-threshold control measurements, or else downgraded to tentative signatures and removed from the title;
3. the "record" T_c claim is tempered given the uncertainties;
4. the T_c–*c* analysis is presented transparently, including the pressure-overdoped data that contradict monotonic behavior, and its mechanistic interpretation is framed as consistent with interlayer exchange rather than a demonstration of it.
