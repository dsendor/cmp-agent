# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** "Interlayer coupling enhanced superconductivity near 100 K in La₃₋ₓNdₓNi₂O₇" (arXiv:2510.12359v1)

---

## 1. Summary of the submission

The authors synthesize polycrystalline La₃₋ₓNdₓNi₂O₇ (0 ≤ x ≤ 2.4) by a sol-gel route and report the following:

1. **Structure and ambient properties.** Nd substitution contracts the *a* and *c* lattice parameters, leaves *b* nearly unchanged, and raises the resistive anomaly attributed to the SDW from 138 to 161 K.
2. **Structural transition.** Nd doping raises the Amam → I4/mmm transition pressure from about 13 GPa (x = 0.9) to 26 GPa (x = 2.1).
3. **Transport under pressure.** Resistive superconducting onsets reach 93 K (x = 2.1, 34.9 GPa) and 92 K (x = 2.4).
4. **Radio-frequency detection.** RF transmission, newly applied to nickelates, shows anomalies at 93/97 K (x = 2.1) and 98 ± 2 K (x = 2.4).
5. **Structural descriptor.** Across bulk and thin-film data, Tc correlates linearly with the *c*-axis parameter at about −28 K/Å. The authors interpret this as evidence that interlayer magnetic exchange is the dominant pairing mechanism.

The synthesis of a high-Nd series is valuable, and introducing a contactless probe for pressurized nickelates is a worthwhile methodological step. However, I have three main concerns:

- The headline claims ("near 100 K", "record Tc", "universal" *c*-axis relation, "demonstrating" the pairing mechanism) go considerably beyond what the data support.
- Several key analyses rest on selective data or untested assumptions.
- The manuscript contains numerous internal inconsistencies.

I recommend **major revision**.

---

## 2. Strengths

- **Synthesis.** The doping series reaches x = 2.4, with Rietveld-refined lattice parameters for nine compositions (ED Fig. 1). The *c*-axis contraction is smooth and monotonic.
- **Combined probes.** Structure under pressure (synchrotron XRD), transport, and RF were measured on the same material family. This is the right approach for linking structure to Tc.
- **Structural transition result.** The shift of the orthorhombic–tetragonal transition to higher pressure with Nd doping is clearly shown (Fig. 1d, ED Fig. 4). It is a useful result in itself.
- **Methodological novelty.** The RF transmission technique with Lenz lenses could become a valuable complement to resistance measurements, which in this field are plagued by filamentary, non-zero-resistance transitions. The BSCCO calibration (ED Fig. 5a) is a good practice.
- **Useful compilation.** Fig. 5 attempts to unify bulk and thin-film data, which is a worthwhile exercise even if the conclusions drawn are too strong.

---

## 3. Major issues

### 3.1 The "universal linear relationship" between Tc and *c* is not established

This is the central claim of the paper, and several problems undermine it.

**(a) Data selection.** The authors state they "selected the Tc measured near the onset pressure and at the maximum transition temperatures."

- For pressurized La₃Ni₂O₇, Tc is non-monotonic in pressure: it rises, then declines at higher pressure (the authors' own Ref. 13, up to 100 GPa).
- In that high-pressure regime, *c* keeps shrinking while Tc falls.
- Including all pressures would therefore give a non-monotonic Tc(*c*), not a line.
- Restricting the dataset to the rising branch largely builds in the correlation. The authors should show all available (Tc, *c*) pairs, including the high-pressure decline, and justify any exclusions.

**(b) Obvious counterexamples at ambient pressure.**

- Bulk La₀.₆Nd₂.₄Ni₂O₇ at ambient pressure has *c* = 20.13 Å. The fitted line predicts a Tc of roughly 70–80 K, yet the material is not superconducting.
- Bulk La₃Ni₂O₇ at ambient pressure (*c* = 20.52 Å) is also non-superconducting.
- Meanwhile, thin films with *larger* *c* (about 20.6–20.8 Å) superconduct at about 40 K.
- *c* is therefore not a sufficient descriptor even within the authors' own samples. The authors acknowledge that *a*ₚ is a "critical prerequisite," but this makes the relationship conditional rather than universal. The abstract and conclusion should be revised accordingly.

**(c) Ad hoc exclusion.** The NdGaO₃-substrate film is removed as "overstretched" without an independent criterion. Please state an objective rule, or include the point and report the effect on the fit.

**(d) No statistics.** No uncertainty on the slope, correlation coefficient, or residual analysis is given. Fig. 5a also shows a gray band with a declining trend vs *a*ₚ for the bulk data. The argument that *c*, rather than *a*ₚ, is the relevant parameter rests almost entirely on a handful of thin-film points. A quantitative comparison (e.g., fit quality vs *c*, vs *a*ₚ, vs *c*/*a*ₚ) is needed.

**(e) Mismatched thermodynamic conditions.**

- Bulk lattice parameters are measured at room temperature in Ne.
- Tc values come from low-temperature measurements in KBr (transport) or NH₃BH₃ (RF), with different hydrostaticity.
- Thin-film *c* values are at ambient pressure and are known to be sensitive to oxygen stoichiometry and Poisson-ratio effects.

These systematic differences should be discussed and, where possible, estimated.

**(f) Missing provenance of plotted points.**

- High-pressure XRD is reported only for x = 0.9 and 2.1. How were *c* values obtained for the x = 1.5 points in Fig. 5? Interpolation?
- Why is x = 2.4, the highest-Tc composition by RF, absent from Fig. 5?
- The Sm-doped data come from a "paper in preparation" (Ref. 42), which the reader cannot verify.

### 3.2 The mechanistic conclusion is not supported

The abstract states that the correlation "demonstrat[es] that enhanced interlayer magnetic exchange coupling is the dominant mechanism for superconducting pairing." A structural correlation cannot demonstrate a pairing mechanism. Specifically:

**(a) *c* is not the intra-bilayer distance.** The *c* parameter includes the rock-salt (La/Nd)–O spacer between bilayers as well as the intra-bilayer Ni–O_apical–Ni distance. The interlayer exchange J⊥ invoked by theory (Refs. 49–58) depends on the latter, and on the Ni–O–Ni bond angle. The authors should:

- extract the apical Ni–O distances and Ni–O–Ni angle from refinements, or
- at minimum, discuss what fraction of the *c* contraction is intra- vs inter-bilayer.

Lab Cu-Kα XRD on La/Nd-rich compounds is poorly suited to refining oxygen positions. Neutron diffraction would be needed to substantiate the claim that "Nd doping is found to enhance the interapical oxygen distortion," which is stated without supporting data.

**(b) T_SDW is a weak proxy for J⊥.**

- The rise of T_SDW from 138 to 161 K is inferred from features in dρ/dT (ED Fig. 3).
- For x = 2.1 and 2.4 the derivative changes qualitatively, from a step to a dip, so the identification is ambiguous.
- Ref. 34 reported an *unaltered* density-wave transition in Nd-doped La₃Ni₂O₇. This should be discussed.
- The SDW is an ambient-pressure, orthorhombic-phase property, while superconductivity appears at above 26 GPa where it is suppressed. A link to the pairing interaction at high pressure requires argument.
- Direct probes (μSR, NMR, RIXS, or INS as in Ref. 60) would be far more convincing.

**(c) Susceptibility is dominated by Nd moments.** The "systematically enhanced" susceptibility (ED Fig. 2b) is almost certainly dominated by Nd³⁺ local moments (μ_eff ≈ 3.6 μB), not by Ni magnetism. It should not be cited as evidence of enhanced Ni interlayer exchange. A Curie–Weiss analysis separating the Nd contribution is needed. The possible influence of Nd 4f moments on superconductivity (e.g., pair breaking, the Hc2 behavior) should also be discussed.

**(d) Chemical vs physical pressure.** Nd doping contracts the lattice but *increases* the pressure needed for superconductivity. Chemical and physical pressure are thus not equivalent, and the octahedral tilt/orthorhombicity matters. This complicates any single-parameter (*c*) picture and deserves explicit discussion.

### 3.3 The RF evidence for Tc ≈ 98 K is not yet convincing

The "near 100 K" claim in the title rests entirely on RF data. These require substantially more support.

**(a) Small, irregular signals.**

- Signal changes are very small, e.g., about 0.01 mV on a 2.46 mV background in ED Fig. 5d.
- The anomalies change form between frequencies and thermal cycles: steps, dips, peaks, and, at 25 MHz (ED Fig. 5c), what appears to be a sharp one- or two-point spike at 97.5 K that resembles an artifact.
- Attributing these differences to "intrinsic properties of the RF method" is too vague. A physical model of the expected Re/Im response (shielding onset vs dissipation peak) would let readers judge whether each anomaly is consistent with superconductivity.

**(b) Missing field dependence.** This is the essential control. A superconducting anomaly should shift to lower temperature and broaden under an applied DC field, at a rate consistent with the transport-derived Hc2 slope (about −1 to −2 T/K near Tc). Without this, the anomalies cannot be distinguished from:

- background effects from the Lenz lens (Ta/Au), W/Ta₂O₅ gasket, or NH₃BH₃;
- temperature-dependent drifts in the RF circuit;
- impurity phases.

**(c) Missing backgrounds.** Please show:

- an empty-cell or non-superconducting reference measured under identical conditions, and
- data from the same sample at a pressure where it is not superconducting.

**(d) RF Tc exceeds the resistive onset, which is unusual.**

- Resistive onset is usually the *most* sensitive indicator of the first superconducting filaments. Inductive and magnetic probes typically yield equal or lower Tc.
- An RF Tc about 5 K above the resistive onset therefore requires a stronger explanation than the current-path argument given.
- The manuscript also offers a second, different explanation ("inhomogeneity of the Nd doping levels," Phase-diagram section). These two explanations should be reconciled.
- If inhomogeneity is responsible, it is unclear which composition actually carries Tc ≈ 98 K.

**(e) Missing methodological details.**

- How was pressure determined in the RF cells, and with what uncertainty (36 ± 4 GPa vs 41 GPa)?
- Where is the thermometer relative to the sample, and how large is the cooling/warming thermal lag?
- The Methods state that the second harmonic is detected, but the figures appear to show the fundamental Re/Im components. Please clarify what is plotted.

Until these points are addressed, I suggest the RF results be presented as "signatures consistent with" superconductivity onset near 97–98 K. They should not be described as "pushing the Tc frontier," and "near 100 K" should be removed from the title.

### 3.4 The "record Tc" claim is marginal

- **Margin.** The resistive onset of 93 K exceeds the Sm-doped record (92 K, Refs. 36, 42) by 1 K.
- **Undefined criterion.** The onset criterion is never stated. In Fig. 2 the "onset" coincides with a resistance maximum superimposed on a semiconducting background, so its position depends on that background.
- **Pressure uncertainty.** The authors note a 5–10% discrepancy between the ruby and diamond-Raman scales, and the relevant pressures (about 27–45 GPa) straddle the switch-over at 30 GPa.
- **Inconsistent values for x = 2.4.** The abstract gives about 93 K, Fig. 2a appears to label 93 K, the text gives 92 K, and Fig. 2e shows 85 K at 40.3 GPa.

Please define the onset criterion, give uncertainties, and moderate the "highest among all nickelate superconductors" statement.

### 3.5 Sample characterization is insufficient for the claims made

**(a) Oxygen stoichiometry.** It is not measured (no TGA or iodometric titration), although it is known to be critical for Tc and phase stability in La₃Ni₂O₇. The authors themselves note that oxygen stoichiometry has hampered systematic studies.

**(b) Actual Nd content.** Only nominal compositions are given (ED Fig. 1). EDS/ICP analysis is needed, especially since the authors invoke Nd inhomogeneity to explain the RF/transport discrepancy.

**(c) Phase purity.**

- The text attributes low-temperature anomalies to "other intergrowth RP phases," which contradicts the claim of a "pure bilayer phase."
- Lab XRD cannot readily exclude the 1313 polymorph, La₄Ni₃O₁₀/La₂NiO₄ intergrowths, or Nd-rich secondary phases at x ≥ 2.1.
- Synchrotron XRD at ambient pressure and/or STEM on the high-x samples would strengthen the work considerably.

**(d) Resistivity trend.** ED Fig. 2a's caption states that the temperature dependence of ρ(T) increases up to x = 1.8 and then decreases. This conflicts with the main-text statement that samples become "more insulating" with Nd doping, and may signal stoichiometry or microstructure variation at high x.

### 3.6 Superconducting volume fraction and filamentary nature

- Zero resistance is never reached.
- The resistance drops are small (about 10–15% of R₁₅₀K in Fig. 2b–c).
- The field suppression up to 14 T shifts the onset by only a few kelvin.

These are standard features in this field, but they mean the superconducting fraction is unknown. This weakens any quantitative structure–Tc mapping. Some discussion, or a lower-bound estimate from the RF shielding signal if possible, would help.

### 3.7 Link between structural and superconducting transitions

The claim that superconductivity and the structural transition "occur coincidentally" is tested only for x = 2.1:

- For x = 2.1, superconductivity appears at about 28.9 GPa vs a structural transition at 26 GPa, measured in Ne at room temperature.
- For x = 1.5 and 2.4, the transition pressure is not measured. Yet Fig. 2d suggests superconductivity in x = 2.4 already at 30 GPa.
- For x = 0.9, where the transition pressure *was* measured (13 GPa), the onset pressure of superconductivity is not shown in Fig. 4.

Recent work (Ref. 16) argues that the tetragonal structure is not the prerequisite. This debate should be acknowledged. The phase diagram (Fig. 4) also needs clearer definition of the shaded regions, particularly the dark-green region below the data points.

## 4. Minor issues and inconsistencies

1. **Hc2 statement contradicts the numbers.** The text states "µ₀Hc2(0) is lowered by doping although the Tc is enhanced." Yet the reported values are 98.5 T (x = 2.1) and 111.4 T (x = 2.4), and x = 2.4 has the *lower* Tc at the field-measurement pressure (85 K vs 93 K). Please clarify what is being compared. In addition:
   - A GL extrapolation from 0–14 T to about 100 T is highly uncertain. Report the initial slope dHc2/dT at Tc, compare with WHH, and compare with the Pauli limit (about 1.84 Tc ≈ 170 T).
2. **RF frequencies inconsistent.** For sample S1 warming, the main text and Fig. 3e caption say 25 MHz, but Fig. 3e and ED Fig. 5f say 20 MHz.
3. **Fig. 3d caption.** It labels the 25 MHz trace as an imaginary component, but ED Fig. 5c shows the corresponding feature in Re U.
4. **Orphaned caption text.** On pp. 15–16, caption fragments ("e Real component... at 40 GPa, measured at 25 MHz. f,g, ...") do not match ED Fig. 5 and cite 40 GPa vs 36 ± 4 GPa elsewhere. Extended Data captions appear to be incompletely merged.
5. **Fig. 5 caption.** It lists bulk x = 0.9, 1.5, 2.1 "from this work," while the legend and the RF claims also involve x = 2.4. Please reconcile.
6. **Doping coverage.** "Superconductivity is observed across all doping levels," but transport under pressure is shown only for x = 0, 0.9, 1.2, 1.5, 1.8, 2.1, 2.4, not 0.3 or 0.6.
7. **Ionic radii.** Specify coordination number (presumably 9-fold) and source.
8. **Fig. 1c.** Error bars appear inconsistent with the stated "<3 K" uncertainty in ED Fig. 3. Please harmonize.
9. **ED Fig. 4c caption.** "Identical XRD patterns" should be "Similar" or "Analogous."
10. **Literature.** Refs. 34 and 35, on Nd and chemical-pressure effects, should be engaged substantively in the discussion, not only cited.
11. **Typos and errors:**
    - "Rigabu" → Rigaku
    - "adoped" → adopted
    - "Fullprof/FullProf" (inconsistent capitalization)
    - unbalanced parenthesis in the infinite-layer formula in the Introduction
    - Ref. 3 is missing the journal name
    - "PILATUS R CdTe" formatting
    - "1.5 Mbar" vs GPa units: use one consistently
12. **Data availability.** The statement "Source data are provided with this paper" should be fulfilled, including raw RF traces with backgrounds.

## 5. Suggested additional work (in rough order of priority)

1. **RF under DC field.** Measure RF transmission under several DC magnetic fields to show the anomaly shifts as expected for a superconductor. Add background and empty-cell controls.
2. **Full Tc(*c*) dataset.** Re-plot Fig. 5 with all available (Tc, *c*) pairs across the full pressure range, with stated fit statistics and objective inclusion criteria. Tone down "universal."
3. **Bond-level structure.** Determine intra-bilayer apical Ni–O distances and Ni–O–Ni angles (ideally neutron diffraction at ambient pressure; at least high-resolution synchrotron data). Correlate Tc with these rather than with *c* alone.
4. **Sample chemistry.** Quantify oxygen content and actual Nd content, and assess phase purity at high x (synchrotron XRD/STEM).
5. **Susceptibility analysis.** Separate the Nd³⁺ contribution from Ni magnetism, and justify, or drop, the susceptibility-based argument for enhanced exchange.
6. **Structural transition pressures.** Measure them for x = 1.5 and 2.4, ideally under conditions closer to the transport PTM, to test the claimed coincidence with superconductivity.
7. **Tc definitions.** Define Tc criteria (onset, midpoint) consistently and provide pressure and temperature uncertainties throughout.

---

## 6. Recommendation

**Major revision.** The high-Nd-doped series, the doping dependence of the structural transition pressure, and the first RF measurements on pressurized nickelates are worthwhile contributions. However, three of the paper's central claims are not adequately supported:

- the RF-based Tc near 100 K;
- the "universal" linear Tc–*c* relationship;
- the assertion that this relationship demonstrates interlayer magnetic exchange as the pairing mechanism.

In addition, there are numerous inconsistencies between text, figures, and captions. With the controls and analyses outlined above, and with claims recast as correlations and signatures rather than demonstrations, the manuscript could become a solid contribution.
