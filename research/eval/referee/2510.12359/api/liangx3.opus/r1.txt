# Merged review

## A. Must address

**1. Bulk superconductivity in the majority bilayer phase is not demonstrated.**
In every composition the resistive drop below the onset is only ~10–25% of R(150 K). R/R₁₅₀K falls from ~1.05 to only ~0.8–0.9 (Fig. 2a–e). Zero resistance is never reached, and the "onset" is a broad maximum or kink on a semiconducting background. No diamagnetic signal, AC susceptibility or shielding/volume-fraction estimate is given for the transport-pressure runs.

Such small drops are compatible with filamentary or minority-phase superconductivity. This possibility is sharpened by the authors' own appeal to "other intergrowth RP phases" to explain the low-temperature "abnormal transition" in x = 2.4. That attribution is made without evidence and leaves open which phase hosts the 92–98 K signal.

The authors should:
- pursue zero resistance with a more hydrostatic medium (e.g., NaCl or Ne in the transport cell), smaller pressed grains, or single crystals;
- add a magnetic probe that yields a shielding fraction, such as DAC AC susceptibility or NV-centre magnetometry (as in Refs. 14–15).

**2. The RF "~98 K" anomalies lack the controls needed to identify them as superconductivity.**
The abstract's claim of "pushing the Tc frontier" to near 100 K rests on these data alone.

The lineshapes are heterogeneous across frequency, sample, channel and sweep direction:
- a step at 20 MHz (ED Fig. 5e,g);
- a sharp single-point-like spike at 97.5 K at 25 MHz (Fig. 3d / ED Fig. 5c);
- dips on S1 and S2 warming (ED Fig. 5f,h);
- a step of opposite sign on S2 cooling;
- a gradual drift (ED Fig. 5d);
- opposite-sign Re/Im changes across panels.

The relative changes are often only ~0.5–1% of the background (e.g., ~0.01 mV on 2.45–2.47 mV in ED Fig. 5d). No physical model explains why a superconducting transition should produce such varied responses in this geometry. Appeal to "intrinsic properties of the RF method" (Ref. 63, a preprint) is not sufficient. The BSCCO reference, by contrast, shows a clean step. Moreover, that validation was done at 0 GPa on a film, not in a loaded high-pressure cell, and its cited 94 K Tc was not checked against an independently measured Tc of the same film.

The Methods call the second harmonic the "distinct signature," yet the figures show Re/Im of the transmitted signal. It is unclear whether the plotted quantity is the second harmonic or the fundamental.

Without discriminating controls, extrinsic origins remain plausible:
- density-wave remnants;
- Nd³⁺ paramagnetism or Nd-related magnetism;
- thermal or magnetic anomalies in the Lenz-lens/Ta₂O₅/gasket assembly or the NH₃BH₃ medium;
- structural anomalies in the sample;
- thermometry artefacts.

The authors should:
- show the anomaly shifting to lower temperature and weakening in static DC fields of a few tesla, and its dependence on modulation-field amplitude;
- show its pressure dependence, including absence in the low-pressure, non-superconducting Amam state of the same cell and pellet;
- provide empty-cell and reference-gasket/background (Lenz lens, gasket, NH₃BH₃) temperature sweeps;
- plot the second-harmonic signal explicitly, with each channel clearly defined;
- present all thermal cycles;
- validate the method on a known superconductor inside a loaded DAC with NH₃BH₃.

Unless these controls are added, 98 K should be described as a "possible signature" in the title and abstract, not a new Tc frontier.

**3. The RF Tc exceeding the transport onset by ~5 K is unexplained, and the measurement conditions are not comparable.**
The RF Tc (97–98 K) exceeds the resistive onset (92–93 K). Resistive onset normally detects percolating filaments first and is the most generous criterion, so the opposite ordering needs quantitative justification. Instead, two mutually inconsistent qualitative explanations are offered: granules altering induction-current paths (RF section) and "inhomogeneity of the Nd doping levels" (phase-diagram section).

Several factors make the comparison unreliable:
- **Pressure medium.** RF used NH₃BH₃, transport KBr and XRD Ne. Possible chemical reaction of ammonia borane or its decomposition products (hydrogen/nitrogen) with an oxygen-labile nickelate, including hydrogen uptake, is not addressed.
- **Pressure.** The RF pressures (36 ± 4 GPa; 41 GPa) are uncertain and differ from the transport pressure (34.9 GPa).
- **Batches.** Sample batches may differ.
- **Thermometry.** Sensor–sample thermal lag in the RF DAC could account for several kelvin, and the cooling/warming asymmetry suggests exactly this. Thermometer placement and sweep rates are not described.

The authors should:
- give a quantitative model or simulation for the ordering;
- calibrate sensor–sample thermal lag and quantify the hysteresis;
- perform RF and transport on the same batch at matched pressure and medium;
- check post-decompression XRD/Raman of the recovered RF sample for reaction with the medium.

**4. The "highest Tc among nickelates" claim is not established.**
The transport onset of ~93 K (x = 2.1) versus 92 K for Sm-doped samples (Refs. 36, 42) is an increment of ~1 K. This lies within the uncertainties:
- **Undefined onset criterion.** Arrow placement at a broad maximum or kink, with no stated method (deviation point, derivative, intersecting tangents).
- **Pressure calibration.** The calibration switches from ruby fluorescence to diamond Raman at exactly 30 GPa, with a stated 5–10% discrepancy. This corresponds to ±2–3 GPa in the critical 27–35 GPa window.
- **Reproducibility.** Data appear to come from one run per composition (Run 1: x = 2.1; Run 2: x = 2.4), with no Tc error bars or statistics across cells or samples.

The authors should:
- define Tc uniformly with several criteria (onset as deviation from normal-state extrapolation, 90%/50% of normal-state resistance, zero resistance) and give error bars that include pressure-calibration errors;
- use a single, cross-checked pressure scale through 25–45 GPa;
- show data from multiple cells/samples per composition and tabulate Tc, pressure and run for each sample;
- cite and compare all competing >90 K reports;
- rephrase "highest among all nickelates" as "comparable to Sm-doped samples" unless the margin exceeds the uncertainty.

**5. The "universal" linear Tc(c) relation (slope ≈ −28 K/Å, Fig. 5) is not supported by the analysis.**
The relation fails on four counts.

- **Selective point inclusion contradicts the known dome.** Tc values were "selected near the onset pressure and at the maximum." Within any single bulk sample, Tc saturates or falls with further compression while c keeps shrinking. This is seen in pristine La₃Ni₂O₇ above ~18 GPa (Ref. 13) and in the authors' own Fig. 4 for x = 2.1 and 2.4 above ~35 GPa. A monotonic Tc(c) therefore cannot be universal. The inconvenient La₃Ni₂O₇/NdGaO₃ film point is excluded post hoc.
- **c and Tc come from mismatched conditions.** Bulk c values come from room-temperature, Ne-medium synchrotron XRD, only for x = 0.9 and 2.1. They are interpolated to pressures determined in KBr cells and paired with low-temperature Tc. Thermal contraction and medium-dependent stress can shift c by amounts comparable to the spread in Fig. 5b. The x = 1.5 and 2.4 points and the Sm data have no high-pressure XRD of their own, and the procedure for obtaining c for literature data is not described.
- **The compilation mixes unlike samples.** Thin films differ in substrate, strain, tetragonality, Sr/Pr substitution, oxygen treatment and Tc criterion. Some key Sm data come from an unpublished "paper in preparation" (Ref. 42) that cannot be verified.
- **The slope is fixed by two disjoint clusters.** No slope uncertainty, R², correlation coefficient, residual analysis or treatment of errors in c or Tc is given. The grey band is ~15–20 K wide at fixed c. The slope is visually set by bulk samples (c ≈ 19.1–19.6 Å, Tc ≈ 75–93 K) and films (c ≈ 20.0–20.8 Å, Tc ≈ 40–60 K). These clusters also differ in strain, hydrostaticity, in-plane parameter, oxygen stoichiometry and dimensionality, and within the bulk cluster the correlation is weak.

The authors should:
- tabulate every point with its source, pressure, temperature of the lattice measurement, Tc criterion and medium;
- plot full Tc(P) trajectories of each sample in Tc–c space and state where the relation breaks down;
- measure c at low temperature under pressure, in the transport medium, for every composition used (at least x = 1.5, 2.1, 2.4);
- provide weighted fits with slope uncertainty and correlation statistics, for bulk and films separately and with and without the NGO point and Ref. 42;
- supply or remove the unpublished Ref. 42 data;
- recast the relation as a hypothesis-generating correlation.

**6. The conclusion that interlayer magnetic exchange J⊥ is "demonstrated" to be the "dominant"/"fundamental" pairing mechanism does not follow from a phenomenological correlation.**
The c axis is a poor proxy for J⊥:
- It includes the rock-salt La/Nd–O spacer thickness, not just the intra-bilayer Ni–O(apical)–Ni distance and angle that control t⊥ and J⊥.
- c co-varies with in-plane parameters, octahedral tilts, apical-oxygen geometry, t⊥ and the d_z² bonding–antibonding splitting.

No apical-oxygen positions, bond lengths or Ni–O–Ni angles are refined, and no independent estimate of J⊥ (DFT/DMFT, RIXS, INS, μSR) is provided. A correlation with c therefore cannot single out J⊥ over alternatives:
- electronic bandwidth, interlayer hopping or d_z² bonding–antibonding splitting;
- γ-band metallization;
- disorder or cation-size variance;
- strain;
- orthorhombicity;
- 4f–3d coupling from magnetic Nd/Sm/Pr;
- doping-dependent carrier concentration.

The authors should:
- test competing descriptors on the same dataset (intra-bilayer Ni–Ni distance, Ni–O_apical–Ni angle, c/a_p ratio, orthorhombicity, unit-cell volume, DFT t⊥ and d_z² splitting);
- support the J⊥ interpretation with DFT/DMFT estimates of t⊥ and J⊥ versus doping and pressure, or with existing RIXS/INS data;
- moderate the language to "consistent with" an interlayer-exchange scenario.

**7. Composition, oxygen stoichiometry and phase purity are insufficiently characterized for the doping-dependent conclusions.**
Only nominal compositions are reported; there is no EDS/WDS, ICP, STEM or site-occupancy refinement. The lattice parameters nearly saturate between x = 2.1 and 2.4 (c = 20.148 vs 20.132 Å; a = 5.348 vs 5.341 Å; b essentially equal within error, ED Fig. 1), and the authors themselves suggest a "thermodynamic stability limit." The real Nd content of "x = 2.4," which carries the ~98 K RF claim, may therefore be lower than nominal or inhomogeneous. This undermines the "record-level substitution" claim and the distinction between x = 2.1 and 2.4. It is all the more critical because Nd inhomogeneity is invoked to explain the RF/transport discrepancy.

Phase purity is asserted rather than shown:
- Rietveld refinements of lab Cu-Kα XRD report no agreement factors (R_wp, χ²), site occupancies (including La/Nd distribution between perovskite and rock-salt sites) or impurity fractions.
- Lab powder XRD cannot reliably exclude 1313/2222/4310 polytypes, La₄Ni₃O₁₀/La₂NiO₄ intergrowths or stacking faults. These are known to affect superconducting volume and Tc.
- The invocation of intergrowths for x = 2.4 contradicts the "pure bilayer phase" claim.

Oxygen content δ, which strongly affects both the density wave and superconductivity in La₃Ni₂O₇₋δ, is not measured. The ambient-pressure resistivity (ED Fig. 2a) is non-monotonic in x: it increases to x = 1.8 and then decreases, contrary to the statement that samples become monotonically "more insulating." The resistivity may also reflect oxygen or disorder changes rather than intrinsic doping effects. Cation disorder can affect octahedral rotations and Tc independently of c.

The authors should:
- report measured cation ratios (ICP, EDS/WDS mapping) and oxygen content (TGA or iodometry) for every x, especially x = 2.1 and 2.4;
- assess intergrowths, refine site occupancies and refine apical-oxygen positions using STEM or high-resolution synchrotron/neutron powder diffraction;
- report Rietveld reliability factors;
- discuss the solubility limit near x ≈ 2.1–2.4.

## B. Should address

**8. The coincidence of the structural (Amam→I4/mmm) and superconducting boundaries is inferred, not demonstrated.**
The transition pressures (≈13 GPa for x = 0.9; ≈26 GPa for x = 2.1) come from room-temperature XRD in Ne. Superconductivity (onset ~29 GPa for x = 2.1) is measured below 100 K in KBr, which has different hydrostaticity and a pressure scale carrying a 5–10% calibration uncertainty. The correspondence is therefore bridged across ~200 K and different media at a precision the data do not support. High-pressure XRD is absent for x = 1.5 and 2.4, yet both appear in the phase diagram (Fig. 4) as if the same correspondence applied.

The authors should perform low-temperature high-pressure XRD in the transport medium on x = 2.1 and 2.4, and measure the transition pressure for x = 1.5 and 2.4.

**9. The ambient-pressure magnetic evidence does not bear on Ni interlayer exchange.**
Interpreting the susceptibility rise of roughly two orders of magnitude (ED Fig. 2b) as "systematically enhanced" magnetism is misleading. The rise is presumably dominated by the Nd³⁺ 4f Curie contribution and says nothing about Ni exchange without subtraction.

T_SDW (138 → 161 K) is inferred only from weak dρ/dT kinks in increasingly insulating ceramics. For x = 2.1 and 2.4 the features change shape to dips (ED Fig. 3), and errors of ±3 K are assigned. There is no μSR, NMR, neutron or χ-anomaly confirmation. The trend also contradicts Ref. 34, which reports an "unaltered density wave transition" in Nd-doped La₃Ni₂O₇; this conflict is not discussed. Finally, an increased ambient-pressure T_SDW does not equate to an increased J⊥ in the pressurized tetragonal phase, where the SDW is suppressed.

The authors should:
- separate the Nd³⁺ and Ni contributions with Curie–Weiss fits;
- confirm the density wave with a microscopic probe where possible;
- discuss Ref. 34.

**10. The upper-critical-field analysis is unreliable and its stated conclusion contradicts the numbers.**
μ₀Hc2(0) values of 98.5 T (x = 2.1) and 111.4 T (x = 2.4) come from a single-band GL extrapolation. The fit uses onset points within ~5–8 K below Tc, measured up to 14 T, which shifts Tc by only a few K. The onset criterion inflates Hc2 and is sensitive to background curvature. No error bars are given.

The text states that "μ₀Hc2(0) is lowered by doping although Tc is enhanced." Yet the more heavily doped x = 2.4 sample gives the larger value at a lower Tc (85 K at 40.3 GPa). No x = 0 reference measured the same way is provided.

The authors should:
- limit claims to the measured range, or use WHH, initial-slope or two-band fits with midpoint criteria and fit uncertainties;
- add a reference measurement;
- correct the doping-trend statement.

**11. Tc values are quoted inconsistently, and some transport data raise stability and coverage questions.**
- The abstract gives "~93 K for x = 2.1 and 2.4."
- The text and Fig. 2d give 92 K for x = 2.4.
- The Fig. 2a caption assigns the orange 93 K arrow to x = 2.4, whereas the text assigns 93 K to x = 2.1.
- Fig. 2e gives 85 K at 40.3 GPa.

In the Fig. 2e field series, the 0 T curve is qualitatively different from the in-field curves (metallic down to low T versus upturns). This raises questions about sample or thermal-cycle stability during the run. The claim that superconductivity is observed "across all doping levels" is not supported for x = 0.3 and 0.6, which are absent from Fig. 2a.

The authors should harmonize Tc values across abstract, text and captions, explain the 0 T curve, and show data for all claimed compositions.

## C. Minor

**12. RF frequency, pressure and channel labels are inconsistent.**
- The Fig. 3e text/caption gives S1 warming at 25 MHz, while the panel and ED Fig. 5f give 20 MHz.
- The ED Fig. 5 caption fragment refers to the x = 2.1 sample at 40 GPa/25 MHz, versus 36 GPa elsewhere.
- The Fig. 3d caption labels 20 MHz as "real" and 25/60 MHz as "imaginary," but ED Fig. 5c shows the 97.5 K feature in Re U.

These should be harmonized across Fig. 3, ED Fig. 5 and the captions.

**13. Several figure and caption errors need correcting.**
- Parts of the ED Fig. 5 caption are misplaced in the reference list.
- The Fig. 5 legend's composition list is not consistent with its caption.
- Figs. 4 and 5 use different composition subsets (Fig. 4: x = 1.5, 2.1, 2.4; Fig. 5: x = 0.9, 1.5, 2.1); a consistent set should be used or the choice explained.

These lapses reduce confidence in the data handling.

**14. Several smaller wording and data-presentation points need correcting.**
- The cited ionic radii (La³⁺ 1.106, Nd³⁺ 0.995, Sm³⁺ 0.964 Å) appear to mix coordination numbers and should be taken consistently from Shannon tables for the relevant coordination.
- The phrasing "Sm-doped analogue (x = 1.5)" should be clarified.
- Typographical errors should be fixed: "Rigabu" → "Rigaku" and "adoped" → "adopted."
