Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

*What the paper claims*
- Polycrystalline La₃₋ₓNdₓNi₂O₇ samples with 0 ≤ x ≤ 2.4, made by sol-gel synthesis. This is the highest rare-earth substitution reported for a bilayer Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) nickelate.
- A systematic study combining:
  - ambient-pressure structure, resistivity and susceptibility;
  - high-pressure synchrotron XRD for x = 0.9 and 2.1;
  - high-pressure transport for x = 0 to 2.4;
  - radio-frequency (RF) transmission under pressure for x = 2.1 and 2.4.
- Headline results:
  - Transport onset Tc ≈ 93 K for x = 2.1 (~92–93 K for x = 2.4).
  - RF anomalies at 97–98 ± 2 K.
  - Nd doping raises the Amam → I4/mmm transition pressure from about 13 to 26 GPa.
  - A claimed universal linear relation, Tc ≈ −28 K/Å × c, spanning pressurized bulk samples and ambient-pressure thin films.

*Potential significance*
- If substantiated, near-100 K superconductivity would set a new record for nickelates and narrow the gap to the cuprates.
- A single structural descriptor (the c-axis length) that unifies bulk and film data would be a valuable design principle.
- The RF transmission method is new to nickelates. It could provide a contactless complement to transport in diamond anvil cells (DACs), where magnetic probes are scarce.

*Novelty caveats*
- Nd doping of La₃Ni₂O₇ was reported previously (Ref. 34), at lower x.
- Sm-doped bilayer crystals already reach a 92 K onset (Ref. 36). Some of the key Sm comparison data come from an unpublished manuscript (Ref. 42).
- The transport "record" is therefore an increment of about 1 K. This lies within the uncertainty of the onset criterion and of the pressure calibration.
- The step beyond the state of the art rests mainly on the RF data and the Tc–c correlation. These must therefore carry the burden of proof.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Materials achievement.** Phase-pure-looking bilayer samples over a very wide doping range. The evolution of a and c (with b nearly constant) is smooth and consistent with the Nd³⁺ ionic radius, and is benchmarked against Sm doping.
- **Coherent structure–pressure picture.**
  - Chemical pressure stabilizes the orthorhombic Amam phase and roughly doubles the transition pressure.
  - The superconducting onset pressure in x = 2.1 (~29 GPa) tracks the structural transition (26 GPa).
  - This supports the emerging view that a near-tetragonal, straight Ni–O–Ni interlayer bond is a prerequisite for superconductivity.
- **Systematic doping trends.**
  - Tc onset rises monotonically from 82 K to 93 K across many compositions measured with a common protocol.
  - The density-wave temperature T_DW rises monotonically from 138 K to 161 K.
- **Methodological contribution.**
  - The RF/Lenz-lens technique is described in reasonable technical detail.
  - It was validated on BSCCO-2212 at ambient pressure.
  - It offers a route to flux-sensitive detection in powders at tens of GPa.
- **Broad, testable hypothesis.** The compilation of bulk and thin-film Tc against lattice parameters is useful in its own right. It poses a falsifiable hypothesis (the c axis, not the in-plane parameter a_p, controls Tc) of wide interest to theory.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**(1) Transport evidence for superconductivity, and for the "record" Tc, is weak and internally inconsistent**

- *Small, incomplete transitions.*
  - No sample shows zero resistance. R/R₁₅₀K falls only from ~1.05 to ~0.8–0.9 (Fig. 2a,b).
  - The "onset" is a broad maximum or kink on a semiconducting background.
  - Without zero resistance, a diamagnetic signal or a volume-fraction estimate, filamentary or minority-phase superconductivity cannot be excluded.
  - This matters because the authors themselves invoke intergrowth RP phases to explain a "low-temperature abnormal transition" in x = 2.4.
- *Inconsistencies in the headline numbers.*
  - The x = 2.4 maximum onset Tc is given as 92 K in the text and Fig. 2d, but marked as 93 K in Fig. 2a.
  - In the field series at 40.3 GPa (Fig. 2e) the zero-field onset is 85 K, and the 0 T curve is qualitatively different from the in-field curves (metallic down to low T versus upturns). This raises questions about sample or thermal-cycle stability during the run.
- *Pressure uncertainty.*
  - The method switches from ruby fluorescence to diamond Raman calibration at exactly 30 GPa, with a stated 5–10% discrepancy. This corresponds to ±2–3 GPa in the critical 27–35 GPa window.
  - The solid KBr medium is used for transport, whereas Ne is used for XRD. The claimed coincidence between the superconducting onset and the 26 GPa structural transition is therefore not established at the precision implied.
- *Upper critical field analysis.*
  - μ₀H_c2(0) of 98.5 T and 111.4 T is extrapolated with a single-band GL form from data up to 14 T, spanning only a few kelvin below Tc.
  - The onset criterion inflates H_c2 and is sensitive to background curvature.
  - The conclusion that "μ₀H_c2(0) is lowered by doping" contradicts the numbers quoted, since x = 2.4 > x = 2.1. It is also unsupported without an x = 0 reference measured the same way.
- *The "highest among all nickelates" claim* (93 K versus 92 K in Sm-doped samples) is not statistically meaningful given these uncertainties.

**(2) The RF "~98 K" signatures are not yet convincing evidence of superconductivity**

- *Inconsistent lineshapes.*
  - Depending on frequency, sample, real versus imaginary channel, and warming versus cooling, the anomalies appear as steps, dips, peaks or a single-point spike. Examples:
    - the sharp spike at 97.5 K at 25 MHz (Fig. 3d, ED Fig. 5c);
    - opposite-sign Re/Im changes across panels;
    - steps on cooling versus dips on warming (ED Fig. 5e–h).
  - No model explains why a superconducting transition should give such varied responses. "Intrinsic properties of the RF method" (Ref. 63, a preprint) is not a sufficient justification.
  - Signal changes are often below 1% of the background (e.g., ED Fig. 5d, ~0.01 mV on 2.46 mV).
- *Missing discriminating controls.*
  - No field dependence of the RF anomaly (it should shift down in temperature under a static field).
  - No pressure dependence. There is no low-pressure Amam (non-superconducting) run on the same cell showing that the anomaly is absent.
  - No empty-cell or reference-gasket background.
  - No second-harmonic data, even though the Methods state the second harmonic is the "distinct signature". The figures show Re/Im of the transmitted signal, and it is unclear which quantity is plotted.
  - Other mechanisms that could produce permeability or impedance anomalies near 100 K are not excluded. These include density-wave remnants, Nd-related magnetism, thermal anomalies in the Lenz-lens/Ta₂O₅ assembly, and the NH₃BH₃ medium.
- *Unreconciled discrepancy with transport.*
  - The RF Tc (97–98 K) exceeds the transport onset (92–93 K), even though transport onset is usually the most generous criterion.
  - Two mutually inconsistent explanations are offered: granular current paths (RF section) and Nd inhomogeneity (phase-diagram section).
  - The RF pressures (36 ± 4 GPa; 41 GPa) and pressure medium (NH₃BH₃) differ from the transport runs. A possible chemical reaction of NH₃BH₃ with the nickelate, or hydrogen uptake, is not discussed.
- *Overstated framing.* The abstract's "pushing the Tc frontier further" rests on these RF data alone. As presented they are suggestive, not conclusive.

**(3) The "universal" Tc–c correlation and the mechanistic conclusion are not adequately supported**

- *Mismatched conditions for c and Tc.*
  - The c values for bulk samples come from room-temperature synchrotron XRD with a Ne medium, for x = 0.9 and 2.1 only. They are paired with low-temperature Tc measured with a KBr medium and a different pressure scale.
  - The x = 1.5 and 2.4 points, and the Sm data, have no high-pressure XRD of their own.
  - Thermal contraction and medium-dependent stress can shift c by amounts comparable to the spread in Fig. 5b.
- *Point selection and a known counterexample.*
  - The authors chose Tc "near the onset pressure and at the maximum". This is a post-selection that can manufacture a trend.
  - Within a single sample, c keeps shrinking with pressure while Tc saturates or falls: for pristine La₃Ni₂O₇ above ~18 GPa (Ref. 13), and in Fig. 4 above ~35 GPa for x = 2.1. This directly contradicts a universal monotonic Tc(c).
  - The NGO-film outlier is excluded ad hoc.
  - The "fit" is shown as a grey band, with no uncertainties on the slope, no correlation coefficient, and no treatment of errors in c or Tc.
- *Not like-for-like comparison.*
  - Thin films are tetragonal, strained, and partly Sr- or Pr-substituted, with different oxygen content.
  - Bulk samples under pressure differ in octahedral tilts and apical Ni–O–Ni geometry.
  - The c axis also co-varies with in-plane parameters, apical oxygen distance, interlayer hopping t⊥, and d_z² bonding–antibonding splitting.
  - A correlation with c therefore cannot single out interlayer magnetic exchange J⊥ over hopping or band-structure mechanisms.
- *The magnetic link is indirect.*
  - T_DW is extracted from dρ/dT kinks at ambient pressure in increasingly insulating samples, where the lineshape changes for x = 2.1 and 2.4 (ED Fig. 3).
  - The spin density wave is suppressed under pressure, so its ambient-pressure scale is only a proxy for J⊥ in the superconducting phase.
  - The "systematically enhanced susceptibility" is largely the expected Curie contribution of Nd³⁺ 4f moments and says nothing about Ni exchange.
  - No measurement of J⊥ is presented (RIXS, INS or μSR).
- *Overstated conclusion.* "Demonstrating that interlayer exchange is the dominant mechanism" is far stronger than the data allow.

**(4) Sample characterization is insufficient for the doping-dependent conclusions**

- *Composition and oxygen content are not established.*
  - Only nominal compositions are reported. There is no EDS, WDS or ICP analysis.
  - Oxygen stoichiometry δ, known to control superconductivity in La₃Ni₂O₇₋δ, is not determined (no TGA, iodometry or neutron refinement).
  - The c parameter nearly saturates between x = 2.1 and 2.4 (20.148 Å versus 20.132 Å). The authors themselves suggest a stability limit, so the actual Nd content of the "x = 2.4" sample is uncertain.
- *Phase purity is asserted rather than demonstrated.*
  - Evidence is limited to lab Cu-Kα XRD Rietveld fits, with no reliability factors reported.
  - Bilayer, trilayer, 1313 and 214 intergrowths are notoriously hard to distinguish by lab powder XRD. STEM, high-resolution synchrotron or neutron diffraction would be needed.
  - The authors invoke intergrowths to explain anomalous transport, which undermines the "pure-phase" claim.
- *Site distribution and disorder.*
  - La/Nd distribution between the perovskite and rock-salt sites, and the associated disorder, can affect octahedral rotations and Tc independently of c. This is not addressed.
- *Structural transition pressure for x = 2.4.*
  - It is not measured, yet x = 2.4 appears in the phase diagram (Fig. 4) as if the same structure–superconductivity correspondence applies.
- *Inconsistent sample sets.*
  - Different figures use different subsets: Fig. 4 uses x = 1.5, 2.1, 2.4; Fig. 5 uses x = 0.9, 1.5, 2.1; Fig. 5's legend is not fully consistent with its caption.
  - Parts of the Extended Data Fig. 5 caption appear misplaced in the reference list.
  - These lapses reduce confidence in the data handling.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

**(1) Strengthen and quantify the bulk superconductivity evidence**
- Pursue zero resistance using a more hydrostatic medium (e.g., NaCl or Ne in the transport cell), smaller pressed grains, or single crystals.
- Add a magnetic probe that yields a shielding fraction: DAC ac susceptibility, or NV-center magnetometry as in Refs. 14–15.
- Define Tc with several criteria (onset, 90%/50% of normal-state resistance, zero resistance) and give error bars.
- Use a single, cross-checked pressure scale through 25–45 GPa.
- Re-analyze H_c2 with WHH or two-band fits using midpoint criteria. Correct the doping statement on H_c2, reconcile the 92/93 K values, and explain the anomalous 0 T curve in Fig. 2e.
- Moderate the "record" claim to "comparable to Sm-doped samples".

**(2) Make the RF measurements decisive**
- Show the anomaly shifting to lower temperature, and weakening, in static fields of a few tesla.
- Show that it disappears at low pressure in the Amam phase, using the same cell and pellet.
- Provide empty-cell and reference backgrounds, and plot the second-harmonic signal explicitly with a clear definition of each channel.
- Present all thermal cycles and justify the warming/cooling hysteresis with a model.
- Perform at least one RF run in the same medium and at the same pressure as a transport run, to reconcile the 93 K versus 98 K discrepancy with one consistent explanation.
- Discuss the chemical compatibility of NH₃BH₃.
- Until these controls exist, describe the 98 K result as a "possible signature" in the title and abstract.

**(3) Put the Tc–c analysis on a rigorous footing and temper the mechanistic claim**
- Measure c at low temperature under pressure for every composition used (at least x = 1.5, 2.1, 2.4), in the same medium as transport.
- Plot the full Tc(P) trajectories of each sample in Tc–c space rather than selected points, and explicitly address where Tc decreases at high pressure.
- Provide a weighted fit with slope uncertainty and correlation statistics, with and without the NGO film and the unpublished Sm data.
- Test competing descriptors on the same dataset: apical Ni–O distance, Ni–O–Ni angle, c/a_p ratio, and, from DFT, t⊥ and the d_z² bonding–antibonding splitting.
- Reframe "dominant mechanism" as "consistent with" interlayer coupling, unless direct measurements of J⊥ can be added.
- Separate the Nd 4f Curie contribution from the susceptibility before drawing magnetic conclusions.

**(4) Complete the materials characterization and fix the presentation**
- Report measured cation ratios (ICP, EDS or WDS) and oxygen content (TGA or iodometry) for every x.
- Assess intergrowths via STEM or high-resolution synchrotron XRD.
- Give Rietveld reliability factors and refined site occupancies (La/Nd distribution), and comment on the solubility limit near x ≈ 2.1–2.4.
- Measure the structural transition pressure for x = 1.5 and 2.4.
- Use a consistent composition set across Figs. 4–5.
- Correct figure and caption issues: Fig. 2a versus text Tc values; real versus imaginary labels in Fig. 3d versus ED Fig. 5; the misplaced ED Fig. 5 caption text.
- Make the Sm data of Ref. 42 available, or remove dependence on them.
