# Review outline

## 1. Significance and novelty

**Scope of the work**
- The manuscript reports sol-gel synthesis of polycrystalline La₃₋ₓNdₓNi₂O₇ across a very wide nominal doping range (0 ≤ x ≤ 2.4).
- Characterization includes:
  - ambient-pressure XRD, resistivity and susceptibility;
  - room-temperature high-pressure synchrotron XRD for x = 0.9 and 2.1;
  - high-pressure transport to 45 GPa;
  - radio-frequency (RF) transmission measurements in a DAC.

**Headline claims**
- A resistive onset Tc of ~93 K for x = 2.1 (and ~92 K for x = 2.4), described as the highest among nickelates.
- RF "signatures" of superconductivity at 98 ± 2 K for x = 2.4.
- A "universal" linear relation Tc(c) with slope ≈ −28 K/Å, spanning pressurized bulk samples and ambient-pressure films.
- The conclusion that interlayer magnetic exchange is "the dominant mechanism" for pairing.

**Novelty**
- The doping range is the main synthetic advance, if the compositions are real.
- RF transmission (Lenz-lens DAC) has not previously been applied to nickelates and is a useful methodological addition.
- The Tc increase over Sm-doped analogues (onset ≈ 92 K, refs. 36 and 42) is ~1 K, which is within the stated pressure (5–10%) and onset-definition uncertainties. Incrementally, the transport result is therefore modest.
- The potentially high-impact elements are the near-100 K RF signal and the structural descriptor/mechanism claim. In their present form, both rest on the weakest evidence in the paper.

**Overall assessment**
- The topic is timely and of broad interest.
- The significance hinges on claims that are currently overstated relative to the data.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

**Systematic, large data set**
- Nine compositions with Rietveld-refined lattice parameters (ED Fig. 1).
- Monotonic contraction of a and c with nearly constant b, consistent with the ionic-radius trend and with the Sm series.

**Useful structural phase-boundary information**
- Doping raises the Amam → I4/mmm transition pressure (≈13 GPa for x = 0.9; ≈26 GPa for x = 2.1).
- The superconducting onset in x = 2.1 appears close to this boundary, extending the reported structure–superconductivity correlation in La₃Ni₂O₇ to a new chemical axis.

**Reproducible resistive anomalies in heavily doped samples**
- Anomalies near 90 K appear in heavily doped samples.
- They are field-suppressed (Fig. 2c,e) and pressure-tuned (Fig. 2b,d), consistent with the emerging picture that chemical pressure can raise Tc in bilayer nickelates.

**Methodological contribution**
- RF transmission is introduced to nickelate research and validated on BSCCO.
- If made robust, a contactless probe in a DAC is valuable for granular, non-zero-resistance samples typical of this field.

**Cross-platform comparison**
- Bulk and thin-film data are compared on a common structural axis.
- Even if not "universal," this comparison is a stimulating hypothesis for the community.

## 3. Potential reasons for rejection

### 3.1 The evidence for superconductivity, and for the "record" Tc, is weak and internally inconsistent

**Small, non-zero-resistance drops**
- In all compositions, the drops below the onset are only ~10–25% of R(150 K) (Fig. 2a–e). No zero resistance is reached, and the normal state is semiconducting.
- The x = 2.4 sample shows an additional "abnormal transition" at low temperature, attributed without evidence to intergrowth phases.
- Such small drops are compatible with filamentary or minority-phase superconductivity. The bulk nature of superconductivity in the majority bilayer phase is not demonstrated: there is no diamagnetic shielding fraction and no AC susceptibility.

**The "record" is not established**
- 93 K vs the Sm-doped 92 K is not meaningful, given:
  - the stated 5–10% discrepancy between ruby and diamond-Raman pressure scales above 30 GPa;
  - the arbitrary onset definition (arrow placement at a broad maximum or kink).
- Tc is also quoted inconsistently:
  - The abstract says "~93 K for x = 2.1 and 2.4."
  - Fig. 2d gives 92 K for x = 2.4, and Fig. 2e gives 85 K at 40.3 GPa.
  - The Fig. 2a caption assigns the orange 93 K arrow to x = 2.4, whereas the text assigns 93 K to x = 2.1.

**Upper-critical-field analysis**
- The GL fits extrapolate from data within a ~5–8 K window just below Tc onset (14 T shifts Tc by only a few K) to ~100 T. Such long extrapolations are unreliable.
- The text states that "μ₀Hc2(0) is lowered by doping," yet the x = 2.4 value (111.4 T) exceeds the x = 2.1 value (98.5 T). The conclusion contradicts the numbers.

**Reproducibility**
- Transport data appear to come from one run per composition (Run 1: x = 2.1; Run 2: x = 2.4).
- No error bars on Tc or statistics across cells or samples are given.

### 3.2 The RF "~98 K" signatures do not yet constitute evidence of superconductivity

**Inconsistent signal morphology**
- Across frequencies and channels, the anomaly appears as:
  - a step (20 MHz);
  - a sharp single-point-like spike (25 MHz, Fig. 3d / ED Fig. 5c);
  - a dip (S1 warming, S2 warming);
  - a step of opposite sign (S2 cooling).
- Some anomalies are in the real component and others in the imaginary component.
- There is no consistent physical model of what a superconducting transition should produce in this geometry. The BSCCO reference shows a clean step, unlike most nickelate traces.

**Missing standard controls**
- No dependence of the anomaly on the DC/modulation field (a superconducting signature must shift to lower T with field).
- No pressure dependence, and no measurement in the non-superconducting low-pressure state of the same cell.
- No empty-cell or background (Lenz lens, gasket, NH₃BH₃) temperature sweeps.
- No discussion of thermometry offsets between sensor and sample during sweeps, which is critical when the claimed Tc differs from transport by only ~5 K and cooling and warming differ.
- The validation was done at 0 GPa on a film, not in a loaded high-pressure cell.

**Unconvincing explanation of the transport discrepancy**
- RF Tc exceeds the resistive onset by ~5 K. The current-path argument (granules changing induction-current trajectories) is qualitative.
- It also conflicts with the later attribution to "inhomogeneity of the Nd doping levels."
- If a minority region with Tc ≈ 98 K existed, the resistive onset should normally detect percolating filaments first. The opposite ordering requires quantitative justification.

**Different pressure-transmitting media**
- RF used NH₃BH₃, transport used KBr, and XRD used Ne.
- Possible chemical interaction of ammonia borane, or its decomposition products (hydrogen/nitrogen), with an oxygen-labile nickelate is not addressed.
- The RF pressure is also uncertain (36 ± 4 GPa).

**Documentation inconsistencies**
- Fig. 3 caption: S1 warming at 25 MHz; ED Fig. 5f: 20 MHz.
- Fig. 3d caption assigns 25 MHz to an imaginary component, while ED Fig. 5c shows the 97.5 K feature in Re U.
- Parts of the ED Fig. 5 caption are misplaced in the reference list.

**Effect on the abstract**
- "Pushing the Tc frontier" to 98 K is not supported at the level required for a top-tier journal.

### 3.3 Composition, phase purity, oxygen stoichiometry and the magnetic interpretation are insufficiently characterized

**Nominal compositions only**
- No EDS/WDS, ICP, or local probes (STEM) are reported.
- Lattice parameters of x = 2.1 and x = 2.4 are nearly identical (c = 20.148 vs 20.132 Å; a and b essentially equal within error), and the authors themselves suggest a solubility limit.
- This undermines the "record-level substitution" claim and the distinction between x = 2.1 and x = 2.4 samples.
- Rietveld refinements report no agreement factors (R_wp, χ²), site occupancies, or impurity-phase fractions.

**Polytypes and oxygen content not addressed**
- Lab powder XRD cannot reliably exclude 1313/2222 polymorphs, La₄Ni₃O₁₀/La₂NiO₄ intergrowths, or stacking faults.
- These are known to matter for superconducting volume and Tc.
- Oxygen content (TGA or iodometry) is not measured, although δ strongly affects both the density wave and superconductivity. The increasingly insulating behaviour with x (ED Fig. 2a, with non-monotonic ρ) could reflect oxygen or disorder changes rather than intrinsic doping effects.

**Magnetic interpretation is unsupported**
- The "systematically enhanced" susceptibility is presumably dominated by the Nd³⁺ 4f Curie contribution. It says nothing about Ni interlayer exchange unless the 4f contribution is subtracted (e.g., by Curie–Weiss fits).
- T_SDW is inferred only from weak dρ/dT kinks in increasingly insulating samples; the x = 2.1 and 2.4 traces show differently shaped features (dips), with assigned errors of ±3 K. There is no μSR, NMR or neutron confirmation.
- Ref. 34 reports an "unaltered density wave transition" in Nd-doped La₃Ni₂O₇; this contradiction is not discussed.
- Increasing T_SDW at ambient pressure does not equate to increasing J⊥ in the pressurized tetragonal phase where superconductivity occurs.

**Structural data under pressure are limited**
- Collected only at room temperature, and only for x = 0.9 and 2.1, whereas superconductivity is measured at < 100 K.
- Coincidence of the structural and superconducting boundaries is therefore inferred across ~200 K of temperature difference and different media (Ne vs KBr).

### 3.4 The "universal" Tc–c relation and the mechanistic conclusion are overstated

**Mixed provenance of the data**
- Fig. 5 combines:
  - c from room-temperature, Ne-medium XRD interpolated to pressures determined in KBr cells;
  - literature bulk data, including an unpublished "paper in preparation" (ref. 42) that cannot be verified;
  - thin films with different substrates, strain states, oxygen treatments and Tc criteria.
- Selection rules for which Tc to plot ("near onset pressure and at maximum") are ad hoc.
- An inconvenient point (La₃Ni₂O₇ on NdGaO₃) is excluded post hoc.
- No fit uncertainty, correlation coefficient or residual analysis is given. The grey band is broad (~15–20 K at fixed c), and the thin-film cluster is separated from the bulk data, so the slope is largely set by two disjoint groups.

**Monotonic trend contradicts the known Tc(P) dome**
- In La₃Ni₂O₇ and in the authors' own data (Fig. 4, x = 2.4 at higher P), Tc decreases with further compression while c keeps shrinking.
- A single linear Tc(c) cannot be universal. Plotting only selected points hides this.

**c is a poor proxy for J⊥**
- c includes the rock-salt La/Nd–O spacer thickness, not just the intra-bilayer Ni–O(apical)–Ni distance and angle that control t⊥ and J⊥.
- No refinement of apical oxygen positions (bond lengths or angles) is provided.
- No independent measure of J⊥ (RIXS, INS) or calculation is given.

**Alternative explanations not excluded**
- Bandwidth or d_z² bonding–antibonding splitting (electronic rather than magnetic) effects.
- Disorder or size-variance effects.
- 4f–3d coupling from magnetic Nd/Sm/Pr.
- Orthorhombicity.
- Doping-dependent carrier concentration.

**Overreach**
- Statements that the data "demonstrate" magnetic exchange is "the dominant mechanism" or the "fundamental mechanism" are not justified by a phenomenological correlation.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

**1. Strengthen and reconcile the superconductivity evidence.**
- Provide data on multiple cells and samples per composition, with Tc error bars and an explicit, uniform onset criterion (e.g., deviation from normal-state extrapolation).
- Attempt more hydrostatic media or single crystals to approach zero resistance.
- Add AC susceptibility or NV-magnetometry to estimate the shielding fraction.
- Limit Hc2 claims to the measured range (or use WHH/initial slope); correct the contradictory doping statement.
- Harmonize the Tc values in the abstract, text and figure captions.
- Recast "record" claims in light of pressure-scale uncertainty, and cite and compare all competing >90 K reports.

**2. Make the RF result rigorous or soften it.**
- Show the field dependence of the anomaly (Tc shifting with applied DC field) and the pressure dependence, including a non-superconducting reference pressure in the same cell.
- Provide empty-cell and background sweeps, sensor–sample thermal-lag calibration, and the second-harmonic signal versus modulation amplitude.
- Validate the method on a known superconductor inside a loaded DAC with NH₃BH₃.
- Address possible PTM–sample chemistry, e.g., post-decompression XRD/Raman of the recovered sample.
- Give a quantitative model (or simulation) explaining why the RF Tc exceeds the resistive onset.
- Fix the frequency/channel inconsistencies between Fig. 3 and ED Fig. 5.
- Unless these controls are added, describe 98 K as a "possible signature," not a new Tc frontier.

**3. Provide quantitative composition, phase and magnetic characterization.**
- EDS/WDS or ICP for actual Nd content and homogeneity (especially x = 2.1 vs 2.4).
- TGA or iodometric oxygen content; Rietveld agreement factors and impurity quantification.
- STEM or synchrotron/neutron powder diffraction to exclude 1313/intergrowth polytypes and to refine apical-oxygen positions.
- Separate the Nd³⁺ Curie contribution from the Ni susceptibility.
- Confirm the density-wave transition with a microscopic probe (μSR/NMR) where possible, and discuss the conflict with ref. 34.
- Ideally, perform low-temperature high-pressure XRD on the superconducting compositions, in the same medium used for transport.

**4. Reframe the structure–Tc analysis as a hypothesis with transparent methodology.**
- Tabulate every point in Fig. 5 with its source, pressure, temperature of the lattice measurement, Tc criterion and medium.
- Include full Tc(P) trajectories rather than selected points, explicitly addressing the dome.
- Report fit statistics with and without the thin films and the excluded NGO point.
- Test alternative descriptors (intra-bilayer Ni–O–Ni distance and angle, a_p/c, orthorhombicity, unit-cell volume).
- Support the J⊥ interpretation with DFT/DMFT estimates of t⊥ and J⊥ versus doping and pressure, or with RIXS/INS data.
- Remove reliance on unpublished data (ref. 42) or supply it.
- Moderate "demonstrates/dominant/fundamental mechanism" language accordingly.
- Correct presentation errors, including:
  - mislabelled figure legends (Fig. 5 composition list vs caption);
  - "Sm-doped analogue (x = 1.5)" phrasing;
  - misplaced ED Fig. 5 caption text;
  - "Rigabu" → "Rigaku";
  - "adoped" → "adopted."
