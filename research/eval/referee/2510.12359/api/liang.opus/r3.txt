Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Context.** The authors report sol-gel synthesis of bilayer La3−xNdxNi2O7 polycrystals over 0 ≤ x ≤ 2.4. The upper end corresponds to a Nd-majority composition, well beyond previously reported Nd/Pr/Sm substitution.
- **Measurements.** They combine:
  - ambient-pressure XRD, resistivity and susceptibility;
  - high-pressure synchrotron XRD (x = 0.9, 2.1);
  - high-pressure transport up to ~45 GPa;
  - radio-frequency (RF) transmission in a DAC, applied to nickelates for the first time.
- **Headline claims.**
  - Transport onset Tc reaches ~93 K (x = 2.1) and ~92 K (x = 2.4), marginally above the Sm-doped record of 92 K.
  - RF anomalies at 97–98 ± 2 K suggest superconductivity "near 100 K".
  - Nd doping raises the Amam→I4/mmm transition pressure (13 → 26 GPa) and an apparent T_SDW (138 → 161 K).
  - A "universal" linear relation Tc(c) with slope ≈ −28 K/Å spans pressurized bulk samples and ambient-pressure thin films. The authors interpret it as evidence that interlayer magnetic exchange J⊥ is the dominant pairing mechanism.
- **Potential significance.** If substantiated, a simple structural descriptor (the c axis) unifying bulk and film data would be valuable for materials design. Pushing Tc toward 100 K would also be a notable milestone for nickelates.
- **Novelty assessment.**
  - The chemical-pressure route (rare-earth substitution raising Tc) is incremental relative to La2PrNi2O7 and La3−xSmxNi2O7.
  - The transport Tc increase over the Sm record (93 vs 92 K) is within experimental uncertainty.
  - The genuinely new elements are the extreme Nd substitution, the RF evidence, and the proposed Tc–c correlation. Each currently rests on limited or indirect evidence.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Synthesis advance.** A systematic, extended doping series is achieved with smooth lattice-parameter evolution (Ext. Data Fig. 1). Side-by-side comparison with Sm-doped samples grown under identical conditions is a useful control for chemical pressure.
- **Multi-probe dataset.** Structure under pressure, transport with field dependence, and RF measurements are combined, giving a reasonably complete T–P phase diagram for heavily doped samples.
- **Useful structural trend.** The observation that the structural transition pressure, and the pressure at which superconductivity onsets, both shift upward with doping is informative. It reinforces the empirical link between the tetragonal (or near-tetragonal) structure and superconductivity.
- **Methodological novelty.** The RF transmission technique is contact-free and validated on BSCCO. Its introduction to the nickelate field could help address the persistent low-superconducting-volume-fraction question in these materials.
- **Testable hypothesis.** The Tc–c correlation bridges bulk and film literature and connects to bilayer t–J–J⊥ / interlayer-pairing theories. It could stimulate targeted experimental and theoretical work.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**3.1 Insufficient characterization of composition, oxygen stoichiometry and phase purity, undermining the doping-dependent conclusions**

- **Nominal compositions only.**
  - No EDX/WDS, ICP, or site-occupancy refinement is presented.
  - Ext. Data Fig. 1 shows the lattice parameters nearly saturating between x = 2.1 and x = 2.4 (c = 20.148 vs 20.132 Å; a = 5.348 vs 5.341 Å). The authors themselves suggest a "thermodynamic stability limit".
  - The real Nd content of the x = 2.4 sample, which carries the ~98 K RF claim, may therefore be lower than nominal or inhomogeneous. The authors also invoke "inhomogeneity of the Nd doping levels" to explain the RF/transport discrepancy, which further makes this critical.
- **Oxygen and intergrowths not quantified.**
  - Oxygen content (TGA/iodometry) is not given, although apical-oxygen vacancies are known to strongly affect Tc and T_DW in La3Ni2O7−δ.
  - Lab Cu-Kα XRD cannot reliably exclude 1313/2222/4310 intergrowth polytypes.
  - The authors attribute the low-temperature resistance anomaly in x = 2.4 to "other intergrowth RP phases". This contradicts the "pure bilayer phase" claim and leaves open which phase hosts the 92–98 K signal.
- **Over-interpretation of ambient-pressure data.**
  - The susceptibility rise of roughly two orders of magnitude (Ext. Data Fig. 2b) is presumably dominated by Nd3+ local-moment paramagnetism. Describing it as "systematically enhanced" magnetism, in the context of Ni interlayer exchange, is misleading without Curie–Weiss analysis or subtraction.
  - The resistivity is non-monotonic in x (increasing to x = 1.8, then decreasing), contrary to the statement that samples become monotonically "more insulating".
  - The cited ionic radii (La3+ 1.106, Nd3+ 0.995, Sm3+ 0.964 Å) appear to mix coordination numbers. They should be taken consistently from Shannon tables for the relevant coordination.

**3.2 The transport evidence does not establish a new Tc record or bulk superconductivity**

- **Modest, non-bulk transitions with marginal margin over prior work.**
  - Resistance drops are only ~10–20% of R(150 K), and zero resistance is never reached.
  - No diamagnetic or volume-fraction evidence is given from transport-pressure runs.
  - The 93 K vs 92 K (Sm) "record" is within the uncertainty of onset determination. The onset criterion is not defined (deviation point? derivative? intersecting tangents?). Above 30 GPa the pressure calibration differs by 5–10% between ruby and diamond Raman.
  - The claim that superconductivity is observed "across all doping levels" is not supported for x = 0.3 and 0.6, which are absent from Fig. 2a.
- **Internal inconsistencies in the Hc2 analysis.**
  - The GL extrapolation from ≤14 T to ~100–111 T uses onset points spanning only a few kelvin below Tc, so the extrapolated Hc2(0) has very large uncertainty. No error bars or alternative (WHH) fits are given.
  - The text states that "µ0Hc2(0) is lowered by doping although Tc is enhanced". Yet the higher-doped x = 2.4 sample yields the larger value (111.4 T vs 98.5 T) at a lower Tc (85 K at 40.3 GPa). This is the opposite of what is stated.
- **The "coincidence" of structural and superconducting transitions is not demonstrated.**
  - The structural transition pressure comes from room-temperature XRD in Ne. Superconductivity is measured at low temperature in a KBr medium, with different hydrostaticity and pressure calibration.
  - No low-temperature structural data are given. High-pressure XRD is absent for x = 2.4 and x = 1.5, both of which appear in Fig. 4.

**3.3 The RF evidence for superconductivity at 97–98 K is not conclusive**

- **Weak, heterogeneous signatures without positive controls.**
  - The anomalies differ in form between runs and frequencies: a step (Ext. Data Fig. 5e,g), a dip (Fig. 5f,h), a sharp spike (5c), and a gradual drift (5d).
  - The relative changes are often ~0.5–1% of the signal (e.g., 2.45–2.47 mV in 5d).
  - Crucial discriminating tests are missing:
    - dependence on modulation-field amplitude and on DC field (shift or suppression of the anomaly);
    - pressure dependence (absence at low pressure, below the superconducting onset);
    - the same cell measured empty or with a non-superconducting reference.
  - Without these, extrinsic origins remain plausible: Lenz-lens/gasket effects, Nd3+ paramagnetism, magnetic or structural anomalies in the sample, or thermometry artifacts.
- **Physically questionable RF > transport Tc, explained only vaguely.**
  - Resistance onset normally captures the earliest percolating filaments, so a 5 K higher RF Tc requires a quantitative explanation. Several factors make the comparison difficult:
    - different pressure media (NH3BH3 vs KBr);
    - different pressures (36 ± 4 or 41 GPa vs 34.9 GPa);
    - possibly different sample batches.
  - Thermal lag between sensor and sample in the RF DAC could account for several kelvin, and the cooling/warming asymmetry suggests exactly this. The thermometer placement and sweep rates are not described.
  - The "granular current path" argument is speculative.
  - For the BSCCO validation, the cited Tc of 94 K should be compared with an independently measured Tc of the same film.
- **Inconsistent reporting of conditions.**
  - Frequencies differ between Fig. 3e text/caption (S1: 25 MHz warming) and the panel/Ext. Data Fig. 5f (20 MHz).
  - The Ext. Data caption fragment refers to the x = 2.1 sample at 40 GPa/25 MHz, versus 36 GPa elsewhere.
  - Fig. 3d labels 20 MHz as "real" and 25/60 MHz as "imaginary" components, which does not match the Ext. Data panels.
  - It is unclear whether the plotted quantity is the second-harmonic signal described in Methods or the fundamental.

**3.4 The "universal" Tc–c relation and the mechanistic conclusion are not supported by the analysis**

- **Selective data inclusion and ill-defined variables.**
  - Tc values were "selected near the onset pressure and at the maximum." Within any single bulk sample, Tc decreases at higher pressure while c continues to shrink (the well-known dome in La3Ni2O7). Including all pressures would violate the monotonic relation, so it is not universal.
  - The exclusion of the NGO film is ad hoc.
  - The c values for bulk samples are taken from room-temperature, Ne-medium XRD (only for x = 0.9 and 2.1), yet are paired with low-temperature Tc from different PTMs. The procedure for obtaining c for literature data is not described.
  - No fit statistics or uncertainties are given (error bars, R², confidence interval on −28 K/Å).
- **Leverage and the jump to mechanism.**
  - Visually, the slope is set by two clusters: bulk samples at c ≈ 19.1–19.6 Å with Tc ≈ 75–93 K, and films at c ≈ 20.0–20.8 Å with Tc ≈ 40–60 K.
  - These clusters also differ in strain state, hydrostaticity, in-plane parameter, oxygen stoichiometry and dimensionality. Within the bulk cluster the correlation is weak.
  - The c parameter includes the rock-salt La–O spacer and is not a direct proxy for the intra-bilayer Ni–O_apical–Ni distance or for J⊥.
  - No bond lengths, Ni–O–Ni angles, or J⊥ estimates (DFT, RIXS/INS references) are provided.
  - The T_SDW increase is measured at ambient pressure, whereas superconductivity appears only where the SDW is suppressed. It also relies on subtle dρ/dT features in increasingly insulating ceramics, whose shapes change at x ≥ 2.1 (dips rather than kinks).
  - This contradicts Ref. [34] (unaltered density-wave transition in Nd-doped La3Ni2O7), which is not discussed.
  - The claim that J⊥ is "demonstrated" to be the "dominant mechanism" is therefore an overstatement of a correlation.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Strengthen sample characterization.**
   - Provide quantitative composition (EDX/WDS mapping or ICP) and oxygen content (TGA) for each x, especially x = 2.1 and 2.4.
   - Provide higher-resolution synchrotron or neutron powder diffraction to quantify intergrowth fractions, La/Nd site distribution, and Ni–O_apical bond lengths.
   - Analyze χ(T) with a Curie–Weiss model to separate Nd3+ and Ni contributions.
   - Confirm the SDW with a local or magnetic probe (µSR, NMR, or at least a χ anomaly) rather than dρ/dT alone.
   - Discuss the conflict with Ref. [34].
2. **Make the transport analysis rigorous and temper record claims.**
   - Define the Tc^onset criterion explicitly and give uncertainties that include pressure-calibration errors.
   - Show data for all claimed compositions, and tabulate Tc, pressure and run for each sample.
   - Report Hc2 with fit uncertainties and alternative models, and correct the contradictory doping-trend statement.
   - Where possible, add an ac-susceptibility or NV-magnetometry estimate of superconducting volume fraction.
   - Perform low-temperature high-pressure XRD (ideally in the same PTM) on x = 2.1 and 2.4, so the claimed structural/superconducting coincidence can be tested directly.
   - Rephrase "highest among all nickelates" as "comparable to" unless the margin exceeds the uncertainty.
3. **Make the RF evidence discriminating.**
   - Show the anomaly's dependence on modulation amplitude and DC magnetic field, and its evolution with pressure (including a low-pressure, non-superconducting state of the same loading).
   - Show background or empty-cell runs.
   - Describe thermometry (sensor position, sweep rates) and quantify thermal hysteresis.
   - Present the second-harmonic signal consistently and harmonize frequencies, pressures and Re/Im labels across Fig. 3, Ext. Data Fig. 5 and the captions.
   - Ideally, perform transport and RF on samples from the same batch at matched pressure and PTM, to address the 5 K discrepancy quantitatively.
4. **Recast the Tc–c analysis as a hypothesis-generating correlation and test it properly.**
   - Include all pressure points for each bulk sample, not only selected ones, and state explicitly where the relation breaks down (e.g., the overpressured side of the dome).
   - Provide fit statistics and a table of the sources and conditions for all c values.
   - Fit bulk and film data separately.
   - Test whether the intra-bilayer Ni–Ni distance, the Ni–O_apical–Ni angle, or the c/a ratio is a better descriptor.
   - Support the J⊥ interpretation with DFT-derived interlayer hopping or exchange versus doping and pressure, or with existing RIXS/INS data.
   - Moderate the language from "demonstrating … dominant mechanism" to "consistent with" an interlayer-exchange scenario, and discuss alternatives: d_z² bonding–antibonding splitting, γ-band metallization, disorder and strain.
