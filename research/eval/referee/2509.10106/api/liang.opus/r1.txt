Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic.** The manuscript reports ARPES (41–205 eV) and DFT/surface Green's function results on a non-natural, cut-and-cleaved (103) facet of the noncentrosymmetric Weyl semimetal NdAlSi.
- **Claims.** The authors make three claims:
  - Disorder "filtering" (flat vs. uneven or adsorbate-covered regions) separates four classes of states: surface states (SS), surface-bulk projected states (SBPS), bulk states (BS) and surface-bulk resonance states (SBRS).
  - A "bulk-boundary correspondence (BBC) paradox" on low-symmetry facets is resolved by summing projections over several bulk Brillouin zones (BZs), via an "LCM criterion" (N_min = 3 for (103)), interpreted as a momentum-space moiré.
  - Fermi arcs from "different bulk BZs" hybridize into closed surface Fermi arc loops (SFALs).
- **Where the value lies.** ARPES on a deliberately prepared high-index facet of a Weyl semimetal is rare and technically demanding. The photon-energy-dependent data set (Figs. 3 and S10) and the termination-resolved Green's function calculations (Fig. S8) are a useful resource.
- **Concerns about the conceptual novelty.** The central conceptual claims appear to rest on a misidentification of the surface reciprocal lattice:
  - It is a standard result of surface crystallography that the reciprocal lattice of a rational lattice plane equals the projection of the full bulk reciprocal lattice onto that plane.
  - Weyl-point projections are therefore automatically SBZ-periodic once k∥ is taken modulo the correct surface reciprocal lattice.
  - The "paradox" thus arises from choosing g1 = Pb1, g2 = Pb2 as the SBZ basis and from drawing only one bulk BZ. It is not a physical tension in the BBC.
- **Overall assessment.** The experimental work is a solid contribution. The claimed conceptual advance (paradox, LCM criterion, moiré, SFALs, quasicrystal link) does not, in its present form, reach the novelty or correctness bar of a top-tier journal.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Experimental difficulty and originality.**
  - A (103) facet of a bct RAlSi compound was prepared by XRD-guided wire-saw cutting plus a pre-notch for in situ cleavage.
  - Surface-state Fermi surfaces were obtained with apparent SBZ periodicity, at two synchrotrons, with several polarizations and dichroism (Fig. S3).
- **Theory–experiment agreement.**
  - The Si-terminated semi-infinite Green's function calculation (Fig. 2b; Fig. S8b) reproduces the main surface-state contours.
  - The DFT k⊥ slices (Figs. 3j–m, r–u) track the photon-energy evolution reasonably well, supporting the bulk assignment.
- **Broad interest of the question.** How topological surface spectra organize on high-index facets is relevant to:
  - real devices, nanostructures and faceted crystals;
  - catalysis on topological materials;
  - facet-dependent responses.
  A correct, general treatment would be welcome.
- **A usable practical message.** Semi-infinite Green's function approaches, rather than very thin slabs, are needed to capture resonance and projected-continuum features. This is useful for ARPES practitioners, if properly benchmarked.
- **Extensive supplementary data.** Termination survey, polarization data, a 25-energy hν series and impurity-adsorption tests show substantial effort.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**(i) The "BBC paradox" and its resolution reflect an incorrect choice of surface reciprocal lattice, not new physics.**
- **The basis g1 = Pb1, g2 = Pb2 is generally not primitive.**
  - The surface reciprocal lattice is PΛ*, generated by Pb1, Pb2 and Pb3 together.
  - The authors' own result Pb3 = −(1/3)Pb1 means the true primitive vector along g1 is Pb1/3.
  - The "new SBZ" whose period is one third of the "theoretical SBZ" (Fig. 4d, green vs. purple) is therefore simply the correct SBZ. No moiré, hybridization or "infinite projection limit" is required to explain it.
  - The purple "theoretical SBZ" is a 3× supercell in reciprocal space.
- **The projection is already well defined on a single BZ.**
  - The map k → Pk mod Λ_SBZ is well defined on the bulk 3-torus because PΛ* = Λ_SBZ.
  - Each Weyl point therefore has a unique image in the SBZ.
  - "Including second- and third-order BZs" is just the unfolding needed because the projected first-BZ polygon is not a fundamental domain of the SBZ.
  - This contradicts statements that "exact reconstruction requires infinitely many bulk BZs" and that "no finite cutoff can capture all projected Weyl points" (SI §6, "Two notions of commensurability").
  - Likewise, M_min = 2N_min is an artifact of truncating a sum over lattice translations rather than working modulo the lattice.
- **The "LCM criterion" is trivial, and its claimed metric dependence is wrong.**
  - Since PG = 0 with G = hb1 + kb2 + lb3, one always has Pb3 = −(h/l)Pb1 − (k/l)Pb2.
  - This holds for any lattice, orthogonal or not. The coefficients a_i are therefore always rational for integer Miller indices.
  - The claims that non-orthogonal lattices can give irrational a_i and N_min = ∞ ("Remarks on non-orthogonal lattices"), and the extension to quasicrystals as the "limiting case", are incorrect for crystalline facets.
  - N_min = 3 for (103) is simply l = 3.
- **The bct structure is not handled consistently.**
  - SI §6 calls NdAlSi "body-centered cubic" with an "fcc" reciprocal lattice. NdAlSi is body-centered tetragonal (I4₁md); the reflection condition is h + k + l even.
  - The SI also applies the orthogonal-lattice formula to a centered cell, then introduces ad hoc redefinitions (g1 ≡ 2g′1, b1 ≡ 2b′1, "half a primitive unit cell" in the Fig. 4 caption) to recover "three-to-one".
  - The real-space 2D Bravais lattice of the (103) plane should be derived directly. For example, in conventional coordinates [010] and (3/2, 1/2, −1/2) lie in the plane.
  - The SBZ should then be computed from that lattice and compared with the experimental green cell.

**(ii) The Fermi-arc "hybridization across bulk BZs" and SFAL claims are conceptually problematic and experimentally unsupported.**
- **"Arcs from different bulk BZs" are not distinct states.**
  - In a semi-infinite crystal, surface states are labelled by k∥ in the SBZ. Replicas from bulk zones related by a reciprocal-lattice vector are the same eigenstates and cannot hybridize with one another.
  - Genuine physics arises only when inequivalent Weyl points (different k⊥, same or nearby k∥) project close together. In that case the standard rule applies: the net projected chirality fixes the minimum number of chiral arc crossings, and arcs can reconnect pairwise.
  - This is well established (projected chiral charge and arc counting, Belopolski et al.) and is not specific to low-symmetry facets.
- **The claim that the loop-Chern-number equality is "relaxed" is unjustified.**
  - SI §7 states that the equality between the loop Chern number and the net enclosed projected chirality is "relaxed".
  - The Chern number on a closed k∥ loop (a cylinder in the bulk BZ) equals the net enclosed chirality by Gauss's law. The signed count of chiral surface modes crossing that loop at E_F is fixed accordingly.
  - Relaxing this would itself violate the BBC. A closed surface contour enclosing zero net chirality is topologically trivial and need not be "Fermi-arc derived" at all.
- **The experimental evidence for SFALs is weak.**
  - The loop cuts L1–L4 (Fig. S15g–j) are shown without the required analysis: a signed count of E_F crossings with Fermi-velocity directions, and the Weyl-point positions/chiralities inside each loop.
  - The labelled "SFAL" (Fig. 4e) lies in a region of overlapping SBPS/SBRS intensity.
  - The authors themselves state that "unambiguous demonstration remains open". Yet SFALs feature prominently in the abstract, Fig. 4 and the conclusions, together with extensive speculation (dHvA/SdH, Andreev, Josephson, QPI, CDW).
  - Fig. 4g is explicitly "not the actual connectivity". A schematic should not carry a headline claim.

**(iii) The disorder-based "filtering" methodology and the SS/SBPS/SBRS/BS taxonomy are not quantitatively established.**
- **The surface conditions are not characterized.**
  - "Flat", "uneven", "mildly" and "strongly disordered", and "impurity-adsorbed" regions are defined only qualitatively.
  - There is no LEED, STM/AFM, roughness metric, adsorbate species or dose, or spatial map, and the probe spot sizes differ (50 µm vs. 10×12 µm).
  - XPS (Fig. S2) shows an extra Si 2p shoulder and Al 2p broadening on uneven regions. This indicates chemical modification, which weakens the claim that the termination is unchanged.
- **The physical distinction between SBPS and BS is unclear.**
  - Any ARPES measurement of bulk bands includes k⊥ integration set by the final-state lifetime and escape depth. "SBPS" vs. "BS" therefore appears to be a matter of degree of k⊥ broadening.
  - No mechanism is given for why increasing disorder would convert projected continua into k⊥-resolved bulk states. The opposite, increased k-broadening, is more intuitive.
  - The weight function W(k∥, k⊥) in the SI formula is never computed or connected to data.
- **SBRS identification relies on qualitative criteria.**
  - The criteria are hν-invariance and "broader linewidth".
  - Hν-invariance only shows 2D character, which is equally consistent with SS.
  - Needed but absent: linewidth/MDC analysis, layer-resolved spectral weight from the Green's function along the arrowed features, and adsorption-dose dependence of intensity versus linewidth.
  - "Direct and unambiguous identification of SBRSs" is therefore overstated.

**(iv) Computational and experimental details are insufficient, and some comparisons are not fair tests.**
- **Thin-slab comparison.**
  - The six-layer slab (Fig. S9) is compared with a semi-infinite Green's function to conclude that slabs "fail" on low-symmetry facets because of incomplete bulk projection.
  - This conflates finite-thickness convergence, which applies on any facet, with a facet-specific effect.
  - A thickness-convergence series (e.g., 6, 12, 24 and more layers, or a larger number of (103) units) is needed. The same caveat would apply to (001), where a 36-layer slab was used.
- **Missing DFT and Wannier details.**
  - The Wannier/tight-binding model is not described: projections, number of Wannier functions, disentanglement window, fit quality.
  - Weyl-point coordinates, energies and chiralities are not tabulated (how many W1/W2/W3, and their positions relative to E_F).
  - "SOC as a second-order perturbation" in VASP is unusual and needs clarification.
  - The 6×6×6 SCF grid is coarse for a semimetal.
  - The 4f-in-core treatment of Nd should be justified at 8–18 K: T_N ≈ 7.2 K, so 8 K is only marginally paramagnetic, and short-range correlations may be present.
- **Missing ARPES and orientation details.**
  - The inner potential and the k⊥(hν) conversion are not given. This is essential for Fig. 3, whose stated coverage (125–205 eV) differs from the 85–205 eV data in Fig. S10.
  - No sample-orientation tolerance is given: XRD rocking curve, miscut, terrace effects on the SBZ.
- **Inconsistencies in termination and figure definitions.**
  - The termination is described variously as the "Si-Al plane" (Methods), "Si-terminated cleaved at the Si-Al layer" (Fig. 2), and "Al-Si layer" (SI §4).
  - The definitions of the pink, purple and green zones change between Figs. 1, 2 and 4 and the SI.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Rebuild the geometric framework on the correct 2D Bravais lattice.**
   - Derive the real-space in-plane primitive vectors of the bct (103) plane and the resulting SBZ, using PΛ* with a primitive bulk basis.
   - Show explicitly whether this equals the experimentally observed (green) cell, which we expect.
   - Present Weyl-point projections modulo Λ_SBZ in a single SBZ, with a table of coordinates and chiralities.
   - Reframe or remove the "paradox", "LCM criterion", "moiré", "infinite-projection limit" and quasicrystal claims accordingly. Correct the bcc/fcc and non-orthogonal-lattice statements.
   - If a genuine 3× surface reconstruction is instead suspected, test it directly with LEED or STM.
2. **Put the arc and loop analysis on rigorous topological footing.**
   - For each loop L1–L4, show the enclosed projected Weyl points with net chirality.
   - Give a signed count of E_F crossings with dispersion directions, from both ARPES (MDC fits) and the SS-only Green's function spectrum (Fig. S16c).
   - Distinguish arc reconnection between inequivalent Weyl projections from trivial closed contours.
   - Remove the claim that the loop-Chern equality is "relaxed".
   - Tone down SFAL statements in the abstract and conclusions, and shorten the speculative applications.
3. **Quantify the disorder-filtering protocol.**
   - Characterize the surfaces: LEED, STM/AFM or micro-ARPES spatial maps, adsorbate identity and dose series.
   - Track SS intensity and linewidth versus dose.
   - Compute layer-resolved spectral weight W(k∥, k⊥) and bulk/surface-resolved Green's functions along the SBRS features.
   - Give operational, quantitative criteria (linewidth, hν dependence, dose dependence) separating SS, SBRS, SBPS and BS. Present SBPS vs. BS as a k⊥-broadening/escape-depth effect if that is what it is.
4. **Strengthen computational and experimental reproducibility.**
   - Provide slab thickness convergence for (103) and (001) to support or revise the "slabs fail on low-symmetry facets" statement.
   - Give full Wannier/Green's function details and the Weyl-point list.
   - Specify the inner potential and k⊥ mapping, and the orientation accuracy.
   - Unify the termination nomenclature and the zone-colour conventions across figures.
   - Fix typos ("BCC paradox", "never explored") and moderate rhetoric ("inaugurate a new frontier", "universal criterion") so that claims match the evidence.
