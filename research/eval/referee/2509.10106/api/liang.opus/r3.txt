Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Scope.** The manuscript reports ARPES and DFT (slab and semi-infinite Green's function) results on a low-symmetry, non-natural (103) facet of the non-centrosymmetric Weyl semimetal NdAlSi. Such facets are rarely studied experimentally. Preparing a (103) surface by saw cutting with a controlled pre-cut is technically non-trivial, and the data set is valuable in its own right.
- **Claimed contributions.**
  - (i) A "disorder filter" that separates surface states (SSs), surface-bulk projected states (SBPSs), surface-bulk resonance states (SBRSs) and bulk states (BSs).
  - (ii) Resolution of an apparent "bulk-boundary correspondence (BBC) paradox": projected Weyl points seemingly do not follow surface Brillouin zone (SBZ) periodicity.
  - (iii) A "universal" least-common-multiple (LCM) criterion (N_min) for arbitrary facets, framed as a momentum-space moiré.
  - (iv) Possible closed surface Fermi-arc loops (SFALs) formed by "hybridization of arcs from different bulk BZs."
- **Assessment of novelty.**
  - The experimental exploration of a low-symmetry Weyl facet is genuinely new.
  - The conceptual core, however, is largely standard surface crystallography. The projection of the bulk reciprocal lattice onto a rational lattice plane is itself the 2D surface reciprocal lattice. A bulk band cut at a fixed k⊥ is generally not SBZ-periodic, whereas the full k⊥-integrated projection always is.
  - The "paradox" therefore appears to stem from a non-primitive choice of surface basis rather than from new physics.
  - The SFAL proposal is potentially interesting, but as formulated it is in tension with the exact topological counting rule.
- **Overall.** The data are interesting, but the advertised conceptual breakthrough is overstated and partly incorrect. In its current form the manuscript does not meet the conceptual bar of a top-tier journal.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **First ARPES study of the (103) facet.** It covers a wide photon-energy range (41–205 eV), polarization and dichroism (LH/LV/PC/NC, LD/CD), and spatially resolved flat versus disordered regions. It also includes XPS evidence that the termination chemistry is unchanged between these regions.
- **Reasonable theory–experiment agreement.** Semi-infinite Green's-function surface spectra (Si-terminated, Al–Si cleavage) reproduce many measured surface features. Bulk k⊥ slices reproduce the photon-energy-dependent maps (Figs. 3, S10–S12).
- **Useful practical lesson.** Fixed-hν ARPES on low-symmetry facets samples sheared bulk slices. Very thin slabs (six layers) are inadequate there, while semi-infinite Green's functions perform well. This is pedagogically useful for the ARPES community.
- **Systematic phenomenology.** Comparing pristine, adsorbate-disordered and rough regions offers a pragmatic way to enhance bulk-projected spectral weight relative to coherent SSs.
- **Forward-looking proposals.** Facet engineering of Fermi-arc geometry, with consequences for quantum oscillations, nesting and nonlinear responses, could stimulate further work if placed on firmer footing.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**(1) The "BBC paradox" and its resolution rest on an incorrect or non-primitive definition of the SBZ; the LCM framework is a restatement of elementary lattice projection.**
- **The "theoretical SBZ" is not the SBZ.**
  - The purple SBZ is built from g1 = Pb1 and g2 = Pb2. The true surface reciprocal lattice is P applied to the entire bulk reciprocal lattice, i.e., it is generated by {Pb1, Pb2, Pb3}.
  - For (103), the authors' own result Pb3 = −g1/3 immediately implies that the true surface reciprocal lattice is generated by g1/3 and g2.
  - The "emergent new SBZ" with one third of the period (green, matching experiment) is therefore simply the correct SBZ, dual to the in-plane real-space translations of the (103) plane. It is not a moiré superlattice, an emergent periodicity, or a "hybridization of three nonequivalent surface unit cells differing by a phase 2π/3."
  - Once the SBZ is defined correctly, the projected Weyl points obey SBZ periodicity by construction, and no paradox exists.
- **The general coefficient formula is mis-stated, and the irrationality claim is false.**
  - Supplementary Sec. 6 presents a1 = −h/l, a2 = −k/l as special to orthogonal lattices. In fact it follows for any lattice from PG = 0, since h·Pb1 + k·Pb2 + l·Pb3 = 0 identically.
  - Hence a_i is always rational for integer Miller indices, irrespective of metric. The claim that hexagonal, rhombohedral or monoclinic facets can give irrational a_i and N_min = ∞ is incorrect.
  - The "universal criterion" N_min = lcm(den(a1), den(a2)) reduces to the well-known statement that the index of the sublattice ⟨Pb1, Pb2⟩ in the full projected lattice divides l. Its "independence of a and c" is trivial.
- **Crystallographic misstatements undermine the derivation.**
  - The Supplement states that NdAlSi is "body-centered cubic (bcc) in real space" with an fcc reciprocal lattice. NdAlSi is body-centered tetragonal (I4₁md), as stated elsewhere in the paper.
  - The "half primitive unit cell" definition of the purple SBZ and the redefinitions g1 ≡ 2g1′, b1 ≡ 2b1′, b3 ≡ 2b3′ are ad hoc. They mix conventional and primitive bases and obscure rather than clarify the geometry.

**(2) The claim that exact reconstruction requires "infinitely many bulk BZs," and the finite-cutoff (M = 2N_min) argument, are mathematically incorrect.**
- **The full projection requires only a finite k⊥ integration window.**
  - For a rational facet, the line k∥ + k⊥n is periodic in k⊥ with period |G_min|, the shortest reciprocal-lattice vector along the (103) normal.
  - Integrating A_bulk over one such period yields the complete, exactly SBZ-periodic projection. This window traverses a finite set of BZs, not infinitely many.
  - For NdAlSi (103) (a = 4.204 Å, c = 14.519 Å), |G_103| ≈ 2π·√(1/a² + 9/c²) ≈ 1.98 Å⁻¹. The integration windows in Fig. S14 (k⊥ = 0–0.5 Å⁻¹; k∥ = 0–1.5 Å⁻¹) are arbitrary and incomplete.
  - The "progressive restoration" seen in Fig. S14 is therefore an artifact of partial integration, not evidence for an infinite-projection principle.
- **The finite-cutoff lemma is invalid.**
  - The argument claims that the smallest finite index set Ω invariant under m → m + N_min is {1,…,2N_min}. No nonempty finite subset of ℤ is invariant under a nonzero translation, so the derivation of M_min^(adj) = 2N_min does not hold.
  - The related statements about "multiplicities and degeneracies" of second- versus third-order neighbours (Fig. S13) conflate BZ-counting conventions with physics. Each Weyl node and its images k + G are the same physical state, and weights in A_proj come from W(k∥, k⊥), not from counting zones.
- **The quasicrystal extension inherits the same misconception.**
  - The analogy (a "fully incommensurate limit") has no rigorous basis for periodic crystals with rational facets.
  - It should be removed or substantially reformulated.

**(3) The SFAL / "hybridization of arcs from different bulk BZs" concept conflicts with the exact bulk-boundary correspondence and is not supported by the data.**
- **The theoretical construct is ill-defined.**
  - Surface states of a semi-infinite crystal are eigenstates of a single surface problem labelled by k∥ in the (true) SBZ. There are no independent "arc replicas from successive bulk BZs" that can overlap and hybridize. Images of a Weyl point under G are the same node, and its projections are related by surface reciprocal vectors.
  - The statement that "the strict equality between the Chern number enclosed by a surface loop and the net projected chiral charge is relaxed" contradicts the theorem the paper claims to restore. For any closed contour in the SBZ, the signed number of chiral surface-state crossings equals the net chirality of Weyl nodes projecting inside. This is exact, not "collective" or "broadened."
  - A closed Fermi contour formed by surface states is either topologically trivial (net enclosed chirality zero) or it is the ordinary arc–Weyl configuration. The paper must state which, and on what basis.
  - When opposite-chirality nodes project onto the same k∥, the net charge cancels and arcs need not exist. This standard scenario should be distinguished from the claimed new topology.
- **The experimental evidence is inconclusive.**
  - Figs. S15g–j show dispersions along loops L1–L4, but no explicit counting of Fermi-level crossings and their Fermi-velocity signs, following the Belopolski et al. criteria, is presented. Nor is the enclosed chirality computed from the DFT Weyl-node list.
  - The Fig. 4g schematic is explicitly "not the actual connectivity." Fig. 4e labels an "SFAL" on a broad intensity feature without any connectivity analysis.
  - Subtracting SBPS spectral weight from the total Green's-function spectrum (Fig. S16c) is not a well-defined way to isolate pure SSs near projected Weyl points, where SS and bulk weight merge.
  - The authors themselves concede that unambiguous demonstration "remains open." It is therefore inappropriate for SFALs to feature in the abstract and conclusions as a key result.

**(4) The experimental methodology for surface identification and state classification is insufficiently controlled.**
- **The surface structure is not characterized.**
  - XRD confirms only the bulk orientation of the cut crystal. There is no LEED, STM or other surface-diffraction evidence of the (103) surface lattice, possible reconstruction, miscut, or faceting into natural cleavage planes after in-situ cleavage.
  - The "disordered/uneven" regions, central to Fig. 1f and the "disorder filter," could expose differently oriented facets or step bunches. Such facets alone would change the apparent periodicity and the bulk/surface weight.
  - The "experimental SBZ" (green lines) is drawn from Fermi-surface patterns, not from an independent structural measurement.
- **The disorder filter and the SS/SBPS/SBRS/BS classification are qualitative.**
  - Disorder is uncontrolled: different regions, deliberate but uncharacterized "impurity adsorption," and "mild" versus "strong" disorder are not quantified by adsorbate species, coverage, doping-induced band shifts or core-level changes.
  - Assignments rely on visual inspection of intensity maps. There are no quantitative linewidth or EDC/MDC analyses, no k⊥-dependence metrics, and no surface-weight comparison with calculated layer-resolved spectral functions.
  - Using photon-energy invariance of an SBPS as a criterion is ambiguous, because SBPSs are by definition k⊥-integrated. It cannot cleanly separate SBPSs from SBRSs or SSs.
  - Similar disorder-based suppression was already reported by the same group (Ref. 15). This weakens the claim of a "new experimental strategy."
- **Termination and k⊥ calibration details are inconsistent or missing.**
  - The termination is described as "Si-Al plane" in Methods but as "Si-terminated, cleaved at Al-Si" elsewhere, with "four planes / eight terminations." The selection relies on visual comparison only.
  - No inner potential or k⊥ conversion is given for Fig. 3e and Figs. 3f–q. Measurements at ~8 K lie close to the magnetic ordering temperature of NdAlSi (~7 K), yet "non-magnetic" calculations are assumed without discussion.
- **DFT details are sparse or unusual.**
  - Reported settings include a 6×6×6 k-mesh, SOC "as a second-order perturbation," and 4f states in the core.
  - The Wannier/tight-binding construction required for the Green's-function calculations is not described.
  - Weyl-node positions, energies and chirality are never tabulated.
  - Cited Quantum ESPRESSO and DFPT references (Refs. 48–49) appear unused.
  - The slab comparison is unbalanced: six layers for (103) versus 36 for (001). The conclusion that low-symmetry facets "intrinsically require" Green's functions is therefore not established; a converged thick slab would also work.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Reformulate the geometry rigorously.**
   - Derive the (103) surface Bravais lattice directly from the in-plane real-space translations of the body-centered tetragonal lattice, and define its dual SBZ. Show explicitly that this equals the observed (green) SBZ, generated by g1/3 and g2.
   - Correct the general relation Pb3 = −(h/l)Pb1 − (k/l)Pb2, valid for all lattices, and drop the irrationality and quasicrystal claims.
   - Fix the bcc/bct error and the "half primitive cell" construction.
   - Recast the paper's message accurately: on low-symmetry facets, a fixed-hν ARPES slice of the bulk is not SBZ-periodic, while the complete projection (one period |G_min| ≈ 1.98 Å⁻¹ along the normal) is exactly SBZ-periodic. Replace Fig. S14 with integration over exactly one such period.
2. **Put the Fermi-arc topology on a quantitative footing.**
   - Tabulate all Weyl nodes (k, E, chirality) and their projections onto the true SBZ.
   - Compute the net projected chirality enclosed by each experimental loop (L1–L4), and perform signed crossing counts from MDC fits along those loops.
   - Compute the surface-state Chern number on closed SBZ contours from the Green's function.
   - Either demonstrate a nontrivial closed contour consistent with the exact counting rule, or remove the SFAL and "relaxed equality" claims. In any case, drop SFALs from the abstract unless proven.
   - Spin-resolved ARPES, or quantum-oscillation data on (103) faces, would substantially strengthen this part.
3. **Characterize the surface and quantify the "disorder filter."**
   - Provide LEED or STM on cleaved (103) surfaces, including miscut and facet analysis for "uneven" regions, and spatial maps linking surface morphology to spectra.
   - Quantify adsorbate coverage and its effects (core levels, rigid shifts).
   - Support the SS/SBPS/SBRS/BS assignments with linewidth and EDC/MDC analysis and with layer-resolved Green's-function spectral weights.
   - Report the inner potential and k⊥ conversion, and discuss the proximity of the measurement temperatures to the magnetic transition.
4. **Strengthen the computational section and temper the claims.**
   - Give full Wannier/Green's-function details and convergence checks, including k-mesh, the SOC treatment and the full eight-termination comparison with a quantitative metric.
   - Compare against a converged thick (103) slab rather than six layers, so that the slab-versus-Green's-function conclusion is fair.
   - Remove unused references.
   - Moderate the language ("universal criterion," "inaugurate a new frontier," "moiré"), correct typos (e.g., "BCC paradox"), and state clearly which elements are new (the experimental (103) data and practical guidance) versus established surface-projection theory.
