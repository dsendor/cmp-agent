Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic and claimed contributions.** The manuscript reports ARPES and DFT (semi-infinite Green's function and slab) results on the (103) facet of the noncentrosymmetric Weyl semimetal NdAlSi. This facet is not a natural cleavage plane. The authors claim four advances:
  - (i) a "paradox" of the bulk–boundary correspondence (BBC) on low-symmetry facets, in which projected Weyl points follow the bulk periodicity while Fermi arcs follow the surface Brillouin zone (SBZ);
  - (ii) a resolution of this paradox through multi-BZ projection and an LCM criterion (N_min = 3 for (103));
  - (iii) an "emergent" SBZ one third the size of the "theoretical" SBZ, interpreted as a momentum-space moiré;
  - (iv) a disorder-filtering route to identify surface–bulk resonance states (SBRSs), plus a proposal of closed surface Fermi-arc loops (SFALs).
- **Genuine value.** Systematic ARPES on deliberately prepared high-index facets of topological semimetals is scarce and experimentally demanding. A careful dataset on NdAlSi (103), with termination-resolved Green's-function comparison, would be a useful contribution.
- **Conceptual novelty is doubtful.** It is a standard result of surface crystallography that, for any rational (hkl) plane, the orthogonal projection of the full 3D reciprocal lattice equals the reciprocal lattice of the 2D in-plane real-space sublattice. Bulk states at k and k+G are the same states, so projected Weyl points are automatically periodic with the true SBZ. The "paradox" appears to arise from two choices:
  - drawing the projected Wigner–Seitz cell of a single bulk BZ (which is not a fundamental domain of the surface lattice);
  - using a non-primitive surface reciprocal basis.
- **Consequence for the headline claims.** The "resolution", the LCM criterion, and the "new SBZ" may therefore be restatements of known kinematics rather than new physics.
- **Disorder-filtering approach.** This draws heavily on the authors' prior work (Ref. 15). Its incremental novelty and generality should be weighed accordingly.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Experimental effort.** Preparing, cleaving, and measuring a non-natural (103) facet of a magnetic Weyl semimetal at two synchrotrons is a nontrivial achievement. It provides rare data on a low-symmetry surface:
  - photon-energy series from 41 to 205 eV;
  - polarization and dichroism maps;
  - flat versus uneven regions.
- **Theory–experiment comparison.** Semi-infinite Green's-function calculations were run for eight terminations, and the Si-terminated (Al–Si cleavage) surface reproduces the main surface-state contours reasonably well. This is useful for future work on this material family.
- **Pedagogical value.** The explicit comparison of Green's-function and thin-slab results illustrates the pitfalls of thin-slab modeling for high-index facets, which practitioners may find useful.
- **Interesting experimental phenomenology.** The redistribution of spectral weight between sharp surface states, diffuse projected bulk continua, and photon-energy-dispersive bulk states with increasing disorder could be valuable if quantified more rigorously.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**3.1. The central "BBC paradox" and its "resolution" rest on a misconstructed SBZ, not on new physics.**
- **The theorem that removes the paradox.** For a lattice plane with normal G = h b1 + k b2 + l b3, the set {P G′ : G′ ∈ Λ\*} is exactly the 2D surface reciprocal lattice. Projected Weyl points therefore have SBZ periodicity by construction. No "higher-order BZ" must be "added": the first BZ already contains all inequivalent bulk states. The pink "projected first bulk BZ" polygon is simply not a primitive cell of the surface lattice.
- **The "new SBZ" is the true SBZ.** Because P G = 0, one has P b3 = −(h/l) P b1 − (k/l) P b2. For (103) this gives P b3 = −(1/3) P b1. The authors' basis g1 = P b1, g2 = P b2 is therefore not primitive; the primitive surface reciprocal basis is (P b3, P b2).
  - The "emergent" SBZ, one third of the "theoretical" SBZ along k∥ (green lines, Fig. 4d), is just the correct SBZ.
  - The purple "theoretical SBZ" is too large by a factor of three.
  - The ad hoc redefinitions in the Supplementary Material ("cross section of half a primitive unit cell", "identify 2SBZ′ as the redefined SBZ") appear to be patches for this error.
  - The momentum-space "moiré" interpretation, with nonequivalent surface cells differing by a 2π/3 phase that "hybridize", is therefore unnecessary and potentially misleading.
- **Fig. 1f is not evidence of incommensurability.** At fixed photon energy, ARPES samples a (curved) constant-k⊥ cut through the 3D BZ. Such a cut is periodic only under bulk reciprocal vectors lying in the plane, a sublattice of the projected lattice. Its non-SBZ periodicity is fully expected for any material, topological or not, and poses no challenge to the BBC.

**3.2. The mathematical framework (Supplementary Section 6) contains errors and spurious conclusions.**
- **The closed-form result is not restricted to orthogonal lattices.** The relation a1 = −h/l, a2 = −k/l follows identically from P G = 0, so it holds for any lattice, not only orthogonal ones. The claim that for hexagonal, rhombohedral, or monoclinic lattices a1 or a2 "may be irrational, leading to N_min = ∞" is incorrect for rational Miller indices. This undermines both the "universal criterion" and the quasicrystal analogy built on it.
- **The finite-cutoff arguments are artifacts of plotting truncated sets.** The claims that "exact spectral reconstruction is achieved only in the infinite-projection limit", that "no finite cutoff can fully capture all projected Weyl points", and that M_min = 2N_min = 6 describe drawing truncated sets of repeated zones, not physics. The set of Weyl points per bulk BZ is finite. Their projection modulo the surface reciprocal lattice is a finite set on the surface torus, obtainable from the first BZ alone.
- **Factual and logical slips:**
  - NdAlSi is described as "body-centered cubic (bcc) in real space" with an "fcc" reciprocal lattice. It is body-centered tetragonal (I4₁md).
  - The bookkeeping of the "fourth bulk BZ" returning to −g1 is off by one.
  - The "position coincidence but different multiplicity" discussion of second- versus third-order neighbors has no physical content, since equivalent k-points carry identical spectral weight.
- **The slab-versus-Green's-function contrast is misattributed.** The authors attribute it to "infinitely many bulk BZs". The Green's-function method includes the continuum of k⊥ within one BZ. A six-layer slab fails mainly because of finite-size effects (top–bottom hybridization and coarse k⊥ quantization) and, possibly, an incorrect in-plane cell. Neither reflects a fundamental requirement of low-symmetry facets.

**3.3. The SFAL claims, and the statement that the Chern-number–chirality equality is "relaxed", conflict with established topology and lack evidence.**
- **"Arcs from different bulk BZs" is ill-defined.** Surface states are labeled by k∥ within one SBZ. There is a single set of arcs terminating at the projected Weyl points; multiple bulk nodes projecting to the same k∥ simply add their chiralities.
- **The Chern-number statement contradicts a theorem.** The Chern number on any closed surface-momentum loop, computed on the corresponding bulk cylinder, equals the net chirality of projected nodes enclosed. The net chiral crossings of surface states along that loop must match it. Asserting that hybridization "relaxes" this equality contradicts the BBC the paper claims to restore.
- **Closed loops are not topological by default.** A closed contour of surface states enclosing zero net chirality is topologically trivial and indistinguishable from an ordinary surface pocket. The authors themselves concede that "unambiguous demonstration remains open".
- **Insufficient experimental support.** The SFAL claims rest on:
  - visual superposition features;
  - dichroic contrast (Fig. S3);
  - loop cuts L1–L4 (Fig. S15g–j) presented without any crossing count, chirality bookkeeping, or Weyl-node coordinates.

  This is insufficient for claims in the abstract and the conclusions. The figure captions themselves state that the arc connectivity in Fig. 4g is schematic.

**3.4. The experimental characterization and state classification are too qualitative to support the claims.**
- **The surface is not characterized structurally.** No LEED, STM, or surface-sensitive diffraction is shown. XRD verifies only the bulk orientation of the cut. Two problems follow:
  - Given the authors' own report of surface-selective reconstruction in NdAlSi (Ref. 14), the factor-of-three periodicity could alternatively reflect a reconstruction. This must be excluded independently of the projection argument.
  - The "uneven/disordered" regions may contain facets of differing orientation. Facet mixing would smear k∥ and could mimic "bulk-like" or "projected" features.
- **The SS/SBPS/BS/SBRS taxonomy relies mainly on visual comparison.** There is no quantitative lineshape or linewidth analysis, no controlled dosing series with known coverage, and no surface-aging or alkali-dosing test. The distinction between "SBPS" and "BS" is conceptually unclear: both are bulk states, sampled with different effective k⊥ broadening. The claim of an "unambiguous identification of SBRSs" is therefore not established.
- **The k⊥ analysis lacks essential parameters.** Neither the inner potential nor the k⊥ resolution is given, so the mapping of photon energy to k⊥ (Fig. 3, S10–S12) is not specified. The caption's assurance that "denser sampling would reveal exact repetitions" is unsupported.
- **Physical conditions are incompletely addressed:**
  - Measurements at about 8 K are very close to the magnetic ordering temperature of NdAlSi (about 7 K), yet the paramagnetic, 4f-in-core DFT is assumed. Possible chemical-potential offsets and the effect of near-T_N fluctuations on node positions are not discussed.
  - Weyl-node energies, coordinates, and counts are never stated, so neither the projections in Fig. 4 nor the arc assignments can be checked.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Reframe the central concept using standard surface crystallography.**
   - Derive the (103) surface unit cell from the in-plane real-space sublattice of the body-centered tetragonal lattice.
   - Show explicitly that its reciprocal lattice equals the projection of the full bulk reciprocal lattice.
   - Demonstrate that this coincides with the experimentally observed (green) SBZ.
   - Remove or substantially revise the "paradox", "LCM criterion", and "moiré" language unless a genuinely new effect beyond this theorem can be isolated.
   - Discuss prior ARPES and theory on high-index or side surfaces of topological insulators and semimetals.
2. **Correct and condense the formalism in Supplementary Section 6.**
   - State the general identity P b3 = −(h/l) P b1 − (k/l) P b2 for all lattices and drop the irrational/N_min = ∞ claim.
   - Fix the lattice-type description (body-centered tetragonal, not bcc).
   - Remove the finite-cutoff (2N_min) and "infinite BZ" arguments.
   - Tabulate all Weyl-node coordinates, energies, and chiralities. Give their projections modulo the correct surface reciprocal lattice, with the net chirality at each projected point.
3. **Either rigorously substantiate or substantially downgrade the SFAL claims.**
   - Perform chirality counting along closed loops in both ARPES and Green's-function spectra, at E_F and at the node energies, following established criteria (e.g., Belopolski et al.).
   - Show the surface-only spectral function with arc connectivity traced explicitly.
   - Delete the statement that the Chern-number–chirality equality is "relaxed".
   - Move speculative implications (transport, superconductivity, quasicrystals) to a brief outlook.
4. **Strengthen the experimental basis.** Add:
   - LEED and/or STM of the cleaved (103) surface, to establish surface periodicity and exclude reconstruction or facet mixing;
   - a controlled, quantified disorder or adsorption series with linewidth and intensity analysis, to support the SS/SBPS/SBRS classification;
   - the inner potential, k⊥ resolution, and photon-energy-to-k⊥ mapping, with denser k⊥ sampling in DFT;
   - temperature-dependent data across the magnetic transition, or an explicit argument that the paramagnetic description holds;
   - slab-thickness convergence tests demonstrating why thin slabs fail;
   - a sensitivity check of the 4f treatment (open core versus DFT+U) on node positions and surface-state contours.

   Also tone down overstated wording ("never explored", "inaugurate a new frontier", "unambiguous") and correct typos (e.g., "BCC paradox", "SraAl4").
