# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** "Resolving the Bulk-Boundary Correspondence Paradox on Low-Symmetry Surfaces of Weyl Semimetals" (Li, Yang et al., arXiv:2509.10106)

---

## 1. Summary of the submission

The authors report ARPES measurements on a sawn-and-cleaved (103) surface of the non-centrosymmetric Weyl semimetal NdAlSi, complemented by DFT (VASP) and semi-infinite surface Green's function calculations. The main claims are:

1. **A "BBC paradox."** On the (103) surface, the periodicity of projected bulk Weyl points (inherited from the bulk BZ) does not match the surface Brillouin zone (SBZ) periodicity obeyed by the surface states. The authors present this as an apparent violation of the bulk-boundary correspondence.
2. **A resolution via an "LCM criterion."** Including projections from successive bulk BZs produces laterally shifted replicas that close after N_min = lcm[den(a₁), den(a₂)] steps (N_min = 3 for (103)). This restores commensurability with the SBZ. The resulting "new SBZ," one-third the size of the "theoretical SBZ," is interpreted as a momentum-space moiré pattern.
3. **A disorder-filtering method.** Surface disorder suppresses coherent surface states, which the authors use to separate surface states (SSs), "surface-bulk projected states" (SBPSs), bulk states (BSs), and surface-bulk resonance states (SBRSs).
4. **A methodological claim.** Finite slabs fail for low-symmetry facets, so semi-infinite Green's function methods are "indispensable."
5. **Closed Fermi arc loops.** Arcs "from different bulk BZs" may hybridize into closed surface Fermi arc loops (SFALs), with an accompanying relaxation of the Chern-number/chiral-charge equality.

The experimental effort is considerable. ARPES on a non-natural cleavage plane of a RAlSi compound is difficult, and the data set (photon-energy scans, polarization dependence, comparison of eight terminations) has value in its own right.

However, I believe the central theoretical claim rests on an incorrect definition of the surface Brillouin zone. Once the SBZ is constructed correctly, the "paradox," the "emergent new SBZ," and the "moiré" interpretation all disappear. Several further claims about topology and computation are also incorrect or unsupported. Detailed comments follow.

---

## 2. Major issues

### 2.1 The "theoretical SBZ" is defined incorrectly; the "new SBZ" is simply the correct SBZ

The authors take g₁ = Pb₁ and g₂ = Pb₂ as the primitive basis of the SBZ (SI Sec. 6). For a general (hkl) facet, these are not primitive vectors of the surface reciprocal lattice.

The surface reciprocal lattice is the projection of the full bulk reciprocal lattice, P(Λ*), generated by Pb₁, Pb₂ **and** Pb₃. Because PG_hkl = 0:

  h·Pb₁ + k·Pb₂ + l·Pb₃ = 0  ⇒  Pb₃ = −(h/l)·Pb₁ − (k/l)·Pb₂.

For (103), the authors themselves obtain Pb₃ = −g₁/3. Therefore g₁/3 is a vector of the projected lattice, and the true SBZ is spanned by (g₁/3, g₂).

This can be checked directly in real space for the primitive-tetragonal model the authors use:
- The shortest in-plane lattice vectors of the (103) mesh are [010] and [30‾1], the latter of length √(9a² + c²).
- The corresponding surface reciprocal vector has magnitude 2π/√(9a² + c²).
- A short calculation gives |Pb₁| = 3 × 2π/√(9a² + c²).

So the purple "theoretical SBZ" is three times too large along g₁. The green "new SBZ," which agrees with experiment, is just the correct SBZ of the (103) surface mesh.

Consequences:
- There is no emergent moiré superlattice.
- There is no "phase of 2π/3 between three nonequivalent surface unit cells."
- There is no mismatch to resolve.

The ad hoc definition in the Fig. 4 caption ("cross section of half a primitive unit cell") and the "redefined SBZ, g₁ ≡ 2g′₁" in the SI's fcc-indexing remark look like attempts to patch this inconsistency. The SBZ should instead be computed from the 2D Bravais lattice of the (103) surface, including the body-centring of I4₁md.

### 2.2 The "paradox" is standard surface projection, not a BBC problem

Even setting aside the SBZ definition, the phenomenology described is textbook surface crystallography.

**(i) Projected Weyl points are automatically SBZ-periodic.** The set {P(k_W + G) : G ∈ Λ*} is invariant under P(Λ*), the surface reciprocal lattice. Weyl points related by a bulk G are the same Weyl point. Drawing the projected 3D Wigner–Seitz cell (pink lines) and noting that it does not tile the SBZ is a statement about a choice of unit cell, not about physics. Nothing in the BBC requires the projected 3D WS cell to coincide with the SBZ.

**(ii) Fixed-hν maps of bulk states are not expected to have SBZ periodicity.** In Fig. 1f and Fig. 3, the data sample bulk states at (approximately) fixed k⊥. Such states are periodic only under bulk G vectors lying in the surface plane (G·n̂ = 0). These form a sublattice of P(Λ*) of index equal to the authors' N_min. The k⊥-integrated projection, by contrast, has the full SBZ periodicity.

This is the complete and well-known explanation of the observation in Fig. 1e/f. The "LCM criterion" is a restatement of this sublattice index, which equals |l| for orthogonal cases and is generally fixed by the Miller indices. The manuscript should engage with the standard literature on high-index and vicinal surface projection rather than presenting this as a new framework.

### 2.3 Internal contradictions in the "infinite projection" argument (SI Sec. 6)

**Exact closure versus "infinitely many BZs."** For any rational (hkl), the line {k∥ + t·n̂} in the bulk reciprocal space is periodic with period |G_hkl|_prim. A single period, which traverses a finite number of bulk BZs, determines the projected spectral function exactly. The claims that "exact spectral reconstruction is achieved only in the infinite-projection limit" and that "no finite cutoff can fully capture all projected Weyl points" contradict the authors' own N_min = 3 closure.

**The "M_min = 2N_min" construction is mathematically false.** The claim that "the smallest nonempty finite set with this property [Ω = Ω + N_min] is two consecutive periods" fails because no nonempty finite set of integers is invariant under a nonzero translation. The "spectral commensurability" notion that depends on this argument, including the claim that Weyl-point distributions "do not preserve the full symmetry of the BZ," needs to be removed or rigorously reformulated. A Bloch spectrum is exactly periodic under every bulk G.

**The non-orthogonal-lattice remark is incorrect.** Because PG_hkl = 0 holds for any lattice, the relation a₁ = −h/l, a₂ = −k/l (with the Pb₁, Pb₂ basis) is not restricted to orthogonal lattices. For any crystallographic (rational) plane, the coefficients are rational. Irrational coefficients and N_min = ∞ arise only for non-lattice ("irrational") planes, which do not have a 2D periodic surface at all.

**The crystallography is mis-stated.** NdAlSi is body-centred tetragonal (I4₁md), not "body-centred cubic." The reflection condition for an I-centred lattice is h + k + l even, not the fcc "all-even/all-odd" rule. Since h + k + l = 4 for (103), G₁₀₃ is an allowed lattice vector. The surface mesh is a centred rectangular lattice. Its correct SBZ should be derived and shown explicitly.

### 2.4 Topological claims about Fermi arc loops

**The "relaxed" Chern-number equality is not physical.** The SI states that "the strict equality between the Chern number enclosed by a surface loop and the net projected chiral charge is relaxed." This is not correct. For any closed loop in the SBZ that avoids projected bulk states, the net signed number of chiral surface-state crossings equals the Chern number on the corresponding bulk cylinder. That number in turn equals the net chirality of the Weyl points projected inside the loop. This holds for any surface orientation. Hybridization and reconnection of surface states can change arc connectivity, but they cannot change this count.

**"Arcs from different bulk BZs" is not physically meaningful.** There is one set of surface eigenstates defined on the SBZ. Weyl points that differ by a bulk G are identical, so arcs cannot "originate" from distinct bulk zones and then hybridize. Closed Fermi contours on the surface can certainly arise from arc reconnection with trivial states. However, a closed contour enclosing zero net chirality is topologically trivial and is not a new "boundary topology."

**The experimental evidence for SFALs is insufficient.** The authors acknowledge that SFALs are not demonstrated, yet they remain a headline claim in the abstract and conclusion. For the loop cuts L1–L4 (Fig. S15g–j), the manuscript asserts that the loops "indicate nonzero Chern number." To support this, the authors need to:
- enumerate the Fermi-level crossings along each loop;
- determine the sign of each crossing's Fermi velocity;
- show the net count, as in Belopolski et al., PRL 116, 066802;
- compare it with the net chirality of the projected Weyl points enclosed.

Note that a nonzero Chern number on a loop is evidence for open arcs terminating inside the loop, which sits uneasily with the claim that the arcs form closed loops. Fig. 4e labels a vertical streak at k_y = 0 as an "SFAL" without dispersion analysis. The Weyl points in question are separated by distances comparable to the experimental linewidths.

### 2.5 The disorder "filter" and the state taxonomy

**SBPSs are not a distinct class of states.** By the authors' own definition, the k⊥-integrated projection A_proj is a representation of bulk states with finite k⊥ broadening, not a class of states. The distinction between "SBPS-dominated" and "BS-dominated" spectra is therefore one of effective k⊥ resolution. That resolution depends on photon energy (escape depth) and surface quality.

**The proposed ordering is not explained physically.** The manuscript claims that moderate disorder yields k⊥-integrated spectra while strong disorder or roughness yields k⊥-resolved bulk spectra. This is counter-intuitive and needs a physical mechanism. For example, roughness smears k∥ through local variations in the surface normal and would not be expected to sharpen k⊥.

**The disordered surfaces are poorly characterized.** The adsorbed impurities are not identified. There is no LEED, STM, or AFM characterization of the "flat," "disordered," and "uneven" regions. The XPS in Fig. S2 shows an additional Si 2p shoulder and visible Al 2p broadening on the uneven surface. This sits uneasily with the conclusion that the chemical termination is unchanged.

**The SBRS evidence is weak.** SBRSs are identified mainly by visual persistence across a narrow photon-energy range (41–53 eV, Fig. S7). A quantitative analysis would be much more convincing, for example:
- momentum-distribution-curve (MDC) linewidths versus binding energy;
- the evolution of linewidth upon entering the projected continuum;
- spectral weight versus hν.

The claim of a "new experimental strategy" for identifying SBRSs needs this kind of support.

### 2.6 Slab versus Green's function comparison

A slab has, by construction, exactly the 2D periodicity of the surface mesh. The claim that slabs fail because they cannot "restore the surface periodicity" is therefore incorrect.

The poorer agreement of the six-layer (103) slab most likely reflects ordinary thin-slab artefacts:
- strong quantization of k⊥;
- hybridization between the top and bottom surfaces, which have different terminations for a polar, non-centrosymmetric stacking;
- an insufficient bulk-like interior.

The (001) comparison used 36 layers, so the comparison is not like-for-like. A convergence test in slab thickness for (103) is needed before any general conclusion about computational schemes is drawn.

### 2.7 Missing methodological information

**Green's function calculations.**
- How was the tight-binding Hamiltonian obtained (Wannier90 projections, energy window, spread)?
- Which code was used (WannierTools or other)?
- What were the number of principal layers, the broadening, and the k-mesh?
- How were the SBPS and SS contributions separated in Fig. S16? "Subtracting" the bulk spectral function from the surface one is not a standard decomposition and needs to be defined.

**DFT.**
- "SOC included as a second-order perturbation" is unusual for VASP and needs clarification.
- The 6×6×6 self-consistent grid is coarse for a semimetal.
- The Weyl-point positions and energies relative to E_F (for each Weyl family) should be tabulated, since they underpin Figs. 1c and 4.

**Magnetism.** NdAlSi orders near 7.2–7.3 K. Measurements at about 8 K at I05 are only marginally above the transition, and beam heating and short-range correlations are possible. The authors' own previous work reports magnetism-induced Weyl fermions in this compound (Ref. 13). Please justify the non-magnetic calculation for the 8 K data, or restrict the quantitative comparison to the 18 K data.

**k⊥ calibration.** Converting photon energy to k⊥ (Fig. 3, Fig. S10) requires an inner potential, which is not stated. The k⊥ axis for the (103) geometry, including its tilt relative to [001], should be shown explicitly.

**Surface quality.** The θ–2θ XRD in Fig. 1d confirms bulk orientation, not surface order. LEED (or equivalent) is needed to exclude faceting into natural low-index planes after sawing and cleaving. Faceting would itself produce superposed periodicities.

## 3. Minor issues

- "BCC paradox" should read "BBC paradox" (p. 6).
- The phrase "which is never explored" on p. 4 needs rewording. Prior ARPES and theory on non-natural and side facets of Weyl and Dirac semimetals (e.g., TaAs-family side surfaces, Na₃Bi, MoTe₂) and theoretical work on arc connectivity versus surface orientation should be cited and discussed.
- References: Ref. 29 (PRB 94, 161401) is dated 2015 but should be 2016. "SraAl₄" in Ref. 21 should be "SrAl₄." Refs. 48–49 (Baroni; Quantum ESPRESSO) are not cited in the text.
- The main text describes the termination as "Si-terminated surface cleavage at the Si-Al layer," while the Methods say "the Si-Al plane termination." Please harmonize.
- The Fig. 3 caption states that "denser sampling would reveal exact repetitions." This should be demonstrated or removed.
- In Fig. S16, red/green denote chirality, while elsewhere red/blue are used. Please use consistent conventions.
- For the LD/CD colour scales (Fig. S3), please state the normalization and any symmetrization with respect to experimental geometry. On a low-symmetry surface, geometric dichroism can dominate.
- The quasicrystal and moiré discussions (SI Sec. 8, main-text conclusion) are speculative and, given §2.1, not supported by the results. I recommend removing them or substantially condensing them.
- Language such as "universal criterion," "inaugurate a new frontier," and "resolves the paradox" should be moderated.

---

## 4. Assessment and recommendation

**Experiment.** The data constitute, as far as I know, the first ARPES study of the (103) facet of a RAlSi Weyl semimetal. The termination analysis is useful, and the agreement between Green's-function calculations and experiment for the surface states is encouraging. This is publishable material.

**Theory.** The conceptual framing does not hold up. The apparent mismatch arises from using Pb₁, Pb₂ as the SBZ basis, which is not primitive for (103). The correctly constructed SBZ coincides with the observed "new SBZ." The bulk-boundary correspondence for rational facets is already fully consistent with standard surface projection. Several supporting statements are mathematically or physically incorrect:
- the finite shift-invariant set;
- irrational coefficients for non-orthogonal lattices;
- the "relaxed" Chern-number equality;
- slabs being unable to restore surface periodicity;
- the "bcc/fcc" crystallography.

The SFAL claim lacks the crossing-count analysis needed to establish topology.

**Recommendation: major revision, with substantial reframing.** I would encourage the authors to:

1. Recompute the (103) SBZ from the 2D surface lattice of I4₁md and redraw Figs. 1c and 4 accordingly.
2. Reframe the paper as an experimental study of a low-symmetry facet. The interesting points then become:
   - how bulk states measured at fixed hν exhibit the in-plane bulk sublattice periodicity, while surface states and k⊥-integrated features exhibit the SBZ periodicity;
   - how arcs connect on this facet.
3. Provide a rigorous Fermi-arc identification: chirality-resolved Weyl-point projections onto the correct SBZ, together with signed crossing counts along closed loops.
4. Strengthen the surface characterization (LEED; identity of the adsorbates) and the SBRS linewidth analysis.
5. Remove or correct the statements listed in §2.3, §2.4 and §2.6.

With these changes, the work could become a solid and useful contribution on Fermi-arc phenomenology on high-index facets. In its present form, its central claim is not supported.
