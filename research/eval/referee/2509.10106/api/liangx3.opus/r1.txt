**A. Must address**

**1. The "BBC paradox" comes from a non-primitive surface reciprocal basis, and the "new SBZ" is simply the correct SBZ.**
The purple "theoretical SBZ" (Figs. 1, 2, 4d; SI §6) is built from g1 = Pb1, g2 = Pb2. The surface reciprocal lattice of a rational lattice plane is the projection PΛ* of the whole bulk reciprocal lattice, so it is generated by {Pb1, Pb2, Pb3}. This is a standard result of surface crystallography: PΛ* equals the reciprocal lattice of the in-plane real-space sublattice. The authors' own result Pb3 = −(1/3)Pb1 therefore means the primitive surface reciprocal basis is (Pb3, Pb2), i.e. g1/3 and g2. The purple cell is a 3× supercell in reciprocal space. The "emergent" SBZ with one-third period (green, matching experiment) is just the correct SBZ, dual to the in-plane translations of the (103) plane.

Once k∥ is taken modulo this lattice, the map k → Pk mod Λ_SBZ is well defined on the bulk 3-torus. Every Weyl point then has a unique SBZ image, and projected Weyl points are SBZ-periodic by construction. The pink "projected first bulk BZ" polygon is not a fundamental domain of the surface lattice, and "adding second- and third-order BZs" only unfolds it. No moiré, no "hybridization of three nonequivalent surface cells differing by a 2π/3 phase", and no paradox is needed.

Fig. 1f is not evidence of incommensurability either. At fixed hν, ARPES samples a curved constant-k⊥ cut, which is periodic only under bulk reciprocal vectors lying in the plane, a sublattice of PΛ*. Non-SBZ periodicity of such a cut is expected for any material.

The construction is also crystallographically inconsistent. SI §6 calls NdAlSi "body-centered cubic" with an "fcc" reciprocal lattice, but it is body-centered tetragonal (I4₁md, reflection condition h + k + l even). It applies the orthogonal-lattice formula to a centered cell. It then patches the result with ad hoc redefinitions that mix conventional and primitive bases: g1 ≡ 2g′1, b1 ≡ 2b′1, b3 ≡ 2b′3, "half a primitive unit cell" in the Fig. 4 caption, and "identify 2SBZ′ as the redefined SBZ".

The authors should:
- derive the real-space 2D Bravais lattice of the bct (103) plane directly (e.g. [010] and (3/2, 1/2, −1/2) in conventional coordinates lie in the plane), together with its dual SBZ;
- show explicitly that this SBZ equals PΛ* computed with a primitive bulk basis and coincides with the green cell;
- present all Weyl-point projections modulo Λ_SBZ in a single SBZ;
- remove or substantially reframe the "paradox", "resolution", "moiré" and "emergent SBZ" language unless an effect beyond this theorem can be isolated.

The accurate message is narrower: on low-symmetry facets a fixed-hν bulk slice is not SBZ-periodic, whereas the full projection always is.

**2. The "LCM criterion" is a trivial identity, and its claimed metric dependence and quasicrystal extension are wrong.**
In SI §6 ("Remarks on non-orthogonal lattices"), a1 = −h/l and a2 = −k/l are presented as specific to orthogonal lattices. In fact PG = 0 for G = hb1 + kb2 + lb3 gives Pb3 = −(h/l)Pb1 − (k/l)Pb2 identically for any lattice. The a_i are therefore always rational for integer Miller indices. The claim that hexagonal, rhombohedral or monoclinic facets can give irrational a_i and N_min = ∞ is incorrect. The "universal criterion" N_min = lcm(den(a1), den(a2)) reduces to the known statement that the index of ⟨Pb1, Pb2⟩ in PΛ* divides l. Its "independence of a and c" is trivial, and N_min = 3 for (103) is simply l = 3. The quasicrystal analogy ("fully incommensurate limit") has no basis for rational facets of periodic crystals. The authors should state the general identity for all lattices, drop the irrationality, N_min = ∞ and quasicrystal claims, and stop presenting the LCM rule as a new universal criterion.

**3. The "infinite-projection limit" and finite-cutoff (M_min = 2N_min) arguments are mathematically incorrect.**
SI §6 ("Two notions of commensurability") claims that "exact spectral reconstruction is achieved only in the infinite-projection limit" and that "no finite cutoff can capture all projected Weyl points". Both are wrong. For a rational facet, the line k∥ + k⊥n is periodic in k⊥ with period |G_min|, the shortest reciprocal vector along the normal. For NdAlSi (103), with a = 4.204 Å and c = 14.519 Å, |G_103| ≈ 2π√(1/a² + 9/c²) ≈ 1.98 Å⁻¹. Integrating A_bulk over one such period gives the complete, exactly SBZ-periodic projection, crossing a finite set of BZs. The set of Weyl points per BZ is finite, and so is its projection on the surface torus.

The integration windows in Fig. S14 (k⊥ = 0–0.5 Å⁻¹; k∥ = 0–1.5 Å⁻¹) are arbitrary and incomplete. Their "progressive restoration" is therefore an artifact of partial integration.

The lemma that the smallest finite index set Ω invariant under m → m + N_min is {1,…,2N_min} fails, because no nonempty finite subset of ℤ is invariant under a nonzero translation. M_min = 2N_min = 6 is thus an artifact of truncating a sum over lattice translations instead of working modulo the lattice.

The discussion of "position coincidence but different multiplicity/degeneracy" for second- versus third-order neighbours (Fig. S13) has no physical content. A node and its images k + G are the same state, and weights in A_proj come from W(k∥, k⊥), not from zone counting. The bookkeeping of the "fourth bulk BZ" returning to −g1 is also off by one.

The authors should remove these arguments and replace Fig. S14 with integration over exactly one period |G_min| along the normal.

**4. "Hybridization of Fermi arcs from different bulk BZs" is ill-defined, and the claim that the loop Chern-number–chirality equality is "relaxed" contradicts the bulk–boundary correspondence.**
In a semi-infinite crystal, surface states are eigenstates of a single surface problem labelled by k∥ in the true SBZ. "Arc replicas from successive bulk BZs" related by reciprocal vectors are the same eigenstates and cannot hybridize with one another. Genuine physics arises only when inequivalent Weyl nodes (different k⊥) project to the same or nearby k∥. Their chiralities then add, arcs may reconnect pairwise, and if opposite chiralities cancel, no arc is required. This is standard (projected chiral charge and arc counting, Belopolski et al.) and not specific to low-symmetry facets.

SI §7 states that "the strict equality between the Chern number enclosed by a surface loop and the net projected chiral charge is relaxed". This is wrong. The Chern number on a closed k∥ loop (a cylinder in the bulk BZ) equals the net enclosed chirality by Gauss's law, and the signed count of chiral surface-state crossings along the loop must match it exactly. Relaxing this equality would itself violate the BBC the paper claims to restore. A closed surface contour enclosing zero net chirality is topologically trivial, indistinguishable from an ordinary surface pocket, and need not be "Fermi-arc derived". Otherwise it is the ordinary arc–Weyl configuration.

The authors should delete the "relaxed equality" statement. They should state for each proposed SFAL which of the two cases applies, and on what basis, and distinguish arc reconnection between inequivalent Weyl projections from trivial closed contours.

**5. Experimental evidence for surface Fermi arc loops (SFALs) is insufficient for their prominence in the abstract, Fig. 4 and the conclusions.**
The loop cuts L1–L4 (Fig. S15g–j) are shown without:
- a signed count of E_F crossings with Fermi-velocity directions (Belopolski et al. criteria);
- the positions and chiralities of Weyl points enclosed by each loop.

The "SFAL" labelled in Fig. 4e lies on a broad feature in a region of overlapping SBPS/SBRS intensity, with no connectivity analysis. Fig. 4g is explicitly "not the actual connectivity", so a schematic is carrying a headline claim. Dichroic contrast (Fig. S3) and visual superposition do not establish loop topology. Obtaining "pure SS" by subtracting SBPS weight from the total Green's-function spectrum (Fig. S16c) is not well defined near projected Weyl points, where surface and bulk weight merge. The authors themselves concede that "unambiguous demonstration remains open", yet the manuscript speculates extensively about dHvA/SdH, Andreev, Josephson, QPI, CDW and quasicrystal consequences.

The authors should either substantiate or downgrade the claim. Substantiation requires:
- the net projected chirality enclosed by each of L1–L4;
- signed crossing counts from MDC fits in ARPES and in a properly defined surface-only Green's-function spectrum, at E_F and at the node energies;
- the surface-state Chern number on closed SBZ contours, computed from the Green's function;
- arc connectivity traced explicitly.

Spin-resolved ARPES or quantum oscillations on (103) faces would help. Unless this is done, SFALs should be removed from the abstract and conclusions, and the speculative applications condensed into a brief outlook.

**B. Should address**

**6. The structure of the cleaved (103) surface is not characterized, so reconstruction, miscut and facet mixing are not excluded.**
XRD confirms only the bulk orientation of the cut crystal. No LEED, STM/AFM or surface-diffraction data are shown, and no orientation tolerance is given (XRD rocking curve, miscut, terrace effects on the SBZ). The "experimental SBZ" (green lines) is inferred from Fermi-surface patterns rather than measured independently. The authors have reported surface-selective reconstruction in NdAlSi (Ref. 14), so the factor-of-three periodicity could alternatively reflect a reconstruction; this should be excluded independently of the projection argument. The "uneven/disordered" regions, central to Fig. 1f and the disorder filter, may expose step bunches or facets of different orientation, including natural cleavage planes. Such facets would smear k∥ and change apparent periodicity and bulk/surface weight, mimicking "bulk-like" or "projected" features. The authors should provide LEED and/or STM on cleaved (103) surfaces, a miscut and facet analysis of the uneven regions, and spatial maps (e.g. micro-ARPES) linking morphology to spectra.

**7. The disorder-filtering protocol is uncontrolled and unquantified.**
"Flat", "uneven", "mildly" and "strongly disordered" and "impurity-adsorbed" regions are defined only qualitatively. Adsorbate species, coverage, dose, and resulting rigid shifts or core-level changes are not reported. The probe spot sizes also differ between beamlines (50 µm vs. 10×12 µm), which affects spatial averaging. XPS (Fig. S2) shows an extra Si 2p shoulder and Al 2p broadening on uneven regions. This indicates chemical modification, contrary to the statement that termination chemistry is unchanged. Similar disorder-based suppression was already reported by the same group (Ref. 15), which weakens the claim of a new experimental strategy. The authors should:
- perform a controlled, quantified adsorption or disorder series (dose, coverage, alkali dosing or surface ageing);
- track surface-state intensity and linewidth against dose;
- address the XPS changes explicitly;
- state clearly what is new relative to Ref. 15.

**8. The SS/SBPS/SBRS/BS taxonomy lacks physical justification and quantitative criteria, so "unambiguous identification of SBRSs" is not established.**
Assignments rest on visual comparison of intensity maps. The SBPS–BS distinction is conceptually unclear. Every ARPES measurement of bulk bands integrates over k⊥ according to final-state lifetime and escape depth, so "SBPS" versus "BS" appears to be a matter of degree of k⊥ broadening. No mechanism is given for why more disorder would convert projected continua into k⊥-resolved bulk states; increased k-broadening would be the more intuitive outcome. The weight function W(k∥, k⊥) in the SI formula is never computed or connected to data. SBRS identification relies on hν-invariance and "broader linewidth". Hν-invariance only shows 2D character, which is equally consistent with SS. Because SBPS are k⊥-integrated by definition, hν-invariance also cannot cleanly separate SBPS from SBRS or SS. The authors should:
- provide linewidth and EDC/MDC analysis and k⊥-dependence metrics;
- compute W(k∥, k⊥) and layer-resolved, bulk- and surface-resolved Green's-function spectral weight along the arrowed SBRS features;
- add dose dependence of intensity versus linewidth;
- give operational criteria separating the four classes, presenting SBPS vs. BS as a k⊥-broadening/escape-depth effect if that is what it is.

**9. The slab-versus-Green's-function comparison does not support the claim that low-symmetry facets "intrinsically require" Green's functions.**
A six-layer (103) slab (Fig. S9) is compared with a semi-infinite Green's function, while the (001) comparison uses 36 layers. The discrepancy is attributed to "incomplete bulk projection" over "infinitely many bulk BZs". However, the Green's function simply includes the k⊥ continuum within one BZ. A six-layer slab fails mainly through finite-size effects (top–bottom hybridization, coarse k⊥ quantization) and possibly an incorrect in-plane cell, problems that apply on any facet. The authors should present a thickness-convergence series for (103) (e.g. 6, 12, 24 and more layers or (103) units) and the corresponding check for (001), compare against a converged thick slab, and revise the conclusion accordingly.

**10. Weyl-node positions, energies, counts and chiralities are never tabulated.**
Without these data, the projections in Fig. 4, the arc assignments and any loop chirality bookkeeping cannot be checked. It is not stated how many W1/W2/W3 nodes exist or where they lie relative to E_F. The authors should tabulate all nodes (k, E, chirality) and their projections modulo the correct surface reciprocal lattice, with the net chirality at each projected point.

**11. The inner potential, k⊥ resolution and photon-energy-to-k⊥ mapping are missing.**
These are essential for Fig. 3 (including Fig. 3e and Figs. 3f–q) and Figs. S10–S12. Fig. 3's stated coverage (125–205 eV) also differs from the 85–205 eV data in Fig. S10. The caption's assurance that "denser sampling would reveal exact repetitions" is unsupported. The authors should report the inner potential, the k⊥ conversion and resolution, and denser k⊥ sampling in the DFT comparison.

**12. Measurements near the magnetic transition are interpreted with a paramagnetic, 4f-in-core model without justification.**
Data at about 8 K (up to 18 K) lie just above T_N ≈ 7.2 K, so short-range magnetic correlations may be present. Yet non-magnetic DFT with Nd 4f in the core is assumed throughout. Possible chemical-potential offsets and the effect of near-T_N fluctuations on node positions are not discussed. The authors should either justify the paramagnetic description or show temperature-dependent data across the transition. They should also test the sensitivity of node positions and surface-state contours to the 4f treatment (open core versus DFT+U).

**13. DFT, Wannier and termination-selection details are insufficient for reproducibility.**
The Wannier/tight-binding model behind the Green's-function calculations is not described: projections, number of Wannier functions, disentanglement window, fit quality. Treating SOC "as a second-order perturbation" in VASP is unusual and needs clarification. The 6×6×6 SCF grid is coarse for a semimetal, and no convergence checks are reported. The Si-terminated surface was selected from eight terminations (four planes) by visual comparison only. The authors should give full computational details and convergence tests, and compare all terminations against the data with a quantitative metric.

**14. Context relative to prior work is missing.**
The manuscript does not discuss earlier ARPES and theory on high-index or side surfaces of topological insulators and semimetals, or the standard surface-projection literature its geometric framework restates. The authors should add this context and state clearly what is new: the experimental (103) data and practical guidance for fixed-hν ARPES and slab modelling on low-symmetry facets. They should also state what is established theory.

**C. Minor**

**15. The termination nomenclature is inconsistent.**
The surface is called the "Si-Al plane" (Methods), "Si-terminated, cleaved at the Si-Al/Al-Si layer" (Fig. 2) and "Al-Si layer" (SI §4). The authors should use one terminology throughout.

**16. The zone-colour conventions change between figures.**
The definitions of the pink, purple and green zones differ between Figs. 1, 2 and 4 and the SI. The authors should unify them and define each colour once.

**17. Some references are unused.**
The Quantum ESPRESSO and DFPT references (Refs. 48–49) appear unused and should be removed.

**18. The language overstates the results, and there are typos.**
Overstated wording includes "never explored", "inaugurate a new frontier", "universal criterion", "unambiguous" and "moiré"; it should be moderated to match the evidence. Typos to correct include "BCC paradox" and "SraAl4".
