# Review outline

**Submission:** "Correlated insulating states in slow Dirac fermions on a honeycomb moiré superlattice" (twisted MoSe₂ homobilayer, Γ-valley moiré bands)

---

## 1. Significance and novelty

**Scientific question.** The paper addresses a long-standing goal: realizing the interaction-driven semimetal-to-Mott-insulator physics of the half-filled honeycomb Hubbard model, which pristine graphene cannot reach.

**Platform.** The authors use a twisted MoSe₂ homobilayer (2.9°–4.0°). Its Γ-valley-derived moiré bands form a spin-degenerate (SU(2)) honeycomb lattice on the MX/XM sites. The estimated hopping is t ≈ 5 meV, so the Fermi velocity is about 30× smaller than in graphene.

**Main claims.**
- **Compressibility probe.** Rydberg (2s) exciton sensing in a WS₂ layer 1 nm away reveals incompressible states at ν = −1, −2 and at six fractional fillings between 0 and −1.
- **Melting temperatures.** For ν = −2, Tₘ ≈ 65 K in D1 and ≈ 105–110 K in D2. Fractional states melt at 13–25 K.
- **Mott versus CDW at ν = −2.** The doping dependence of two distinct moiré trions is used to argue for a Mott state (one hole per site) rather than a sublattice CDW. These are a "charge-transfer" trion X₁⁺ and a "tightly bound" trion X₂⁺.
- **Magnetism.**
  - MCD at ν = −1 gives a small positive Θ_CW, interpreted as weak ferromagnetism.
  - The absence of MCD at ν = −2 is interpreted as an antiferromagnetic or spin-liquid ground state.

**Novelty assessment.**
- **What is new.** Combining Γ-valley honeycomb moiré bands, a 2s-exciton compressibility sensor, and trions with distinct spatial character is a genuinely new optical toolkit. The high-temperature ν = −2 insulator in a Γ-valley TMD homobilayer is notable.
- **What limits it.**
  - The central claim (a correlated gap at the moiré Dirac point) overlaps substantially with arXiv:2412.07150 (Ma et al., Cornell), which the authors acknowledge. That work reportedly tunes through the relativistic Mott transition, which this manuscript does not.
  - Correlated insulators in Γ-valley moiré bands have been reported before (e.g., Foutty et al., Nat. Mater. 2023).
  - Generalized Wigner crystals detected by 2s sensing are well established in heterobilayers (Xu et al. 2020).
- **Bottom line.** The incremental novelty lies mainly in the platform, the trion-based charge-distribution argument, and the magnetic characterization. The latter two are therefore where the evidentiary bar must be highest.

---

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **Timely and important target physics.** The honeycomb Hubbard model at strong coupling is a canonical problem: Gross–Neveu criticality, chiral symmetry breaking, and candidate exotic magnetism. A clean SU(2) solid-state realization with large U/t would be of broad interest.
- **High-quality optical data and device engineering.**
  - The data quality is high: narrow WS₂ 1s/2s linewidths, a dual-gate geometry with a contact gate, and multiple devices (D1–D3) and positions (P1, P2).
  - The robustness of ν = −1 and −2 to temperature and electric field supports Γ-valley, layer-hybridized carriers.
- **New spectroscopic handle on charge distribution.**
  - The correlated doping dependence of X₁⁺ (maximal near ν = −1) and X₂⁺ (maximal near ν = −2) is striking and reproduced in two devices.
  - If substantiated, it gives an optical route to distinguish site-resolved charge configurations in honeycomb moiré lattices.
- **Large energy scales.** Insulating behaviour persists to ~100 K at ν = −2 in the smallest-angle device. The trend of Tₘ(ν = −2) with twist angle is qualitatively consistent with the expected scaling of U₀/t.
- **Theory alongside experiment.** Hartree–Fock phase diagrams, DFT band alignment, and perturbative exchange calculations accompany the data, giving a coherent (if not yet fully quantitative) picture.

---

## 3. Potential reasons for rejection

### 3.1 The filling-factor calibration is not independently established, which undermines the assignment of all states

**The linearity claim is contradicted by the authors' own voltages.**
- The text states that fillings are assigned "based on the equal voltage spacing between the two integer fillings and the charge-neutral condition."
- In D1 the reported voltages give unequal intervals:
  - charge neutrality ≈ −1.0 V, ν = −1 at −2.36 V, ν = −2 at −3.48 V;
  - interval 0 → −1 is ≈ 1.36 V, interval −1 → −2 is ≈ 1.12 V (~20% mismatch).
- D2 shows a similar asymmetry:
  - ν = 0 at ≈ −1 V, ν = −1 at ≈ −3.7 V, ν = −2 at ≈ −6.1 V;
  - intervals ≈ 2.7 V versus ≈ 2.4 V.
- The authors also invoke nonlinear gating from contact resistance (at low density) and from gate-dielectric leakage near breakdown (near ν = −2). Both weaken the assumption of a linear V–n relation exactly where the anchors sit.
- With a 20% calibration uncertainty, nearby fractions (−1/6, −1/4, −1/3) and (−2/3, −5/6) cannot be robustly distinguished. Several fractional assignments therefore rest on assumption rather than measurement.

**There is no independent density benchmark.**
- The two most prominent features are assumed to be ν = −1 and −2. They are not cross-checked, for example by:
  - Landau-level or quantum-oscillation features in magneto-reflectance of the sensor or sample;
  - identification of ν = −4 (full filling of the two-band manifold);
  - a geometric-capacitance estimate of the absolute density from charge neutrality.
- The capacitance model uses ε_hBN ≈ 3, lower than the commonly used out-of-plane value of ~3–3.8. This choice directly sets n_M and hence the inferred twist angle (3.8° versus the 3.6° target).
- The agreement with the target angle is therefore not a strong validation.
- Alternative assignments should be explicitly excluded, for example:
  - the two strong features being ν = −2 and −4;
  - the ν = −1 feature being a strong fractional state.

**Fractional states are not shown to be reproducible.**
- The fractional set differs between positions P1 and P2 (Ext. Fig. 5b versus Fig. 2b).
- No discussion explains why −1/6 and −5/6 should be stable on a honeycomb lattice with two sites per cell, i.e., at 1/12 and 5/12 site filling.
- Real-space charge configurations or classical-energy arguments for each fraction are absent.

### 3.2 Quantitative claims about the Mott gap, U₀/t, and the "Dirac-point Mott insulator" are not supported at the level stated

**The step from Tₘ to U₀ is methodologically weak.**
- Tₘ is defined operationally, as the temperature where the 2s spectral-weight contrast drops to 10% of its maximum. The authors themselves say it is a lower bound affected by loss of sensor sensitivity and Lindemann-type melting.
- Converting it to U₀ via U₀ ≈ 4.75 k_B Tₘ (from finite-T QMC of Tang et al.) equates an optical sensing threshold with a specific thermodynamic crossover of the Hubbard model. Which crossover, and at which U/t, is not specified.
- The resulting U₀/t ≈ 5 for D1 sits close to the critical value (~3.8–4). Uncertainties are not propagated, including:
  - t from an idealized BM model that ignores relaxation;
  - the unknown prefactor;
  - Tₘ error bars.
- The conclusion that D1 lies on the insulating side is therefore not statistically meaningful as presented.

**There are internal inconsistencies in the interaction scale.**
- The introduction quotes U₀ = 100–200 meV, whereas the extracted value is ~25 meV.
- To reconcile these, the authors infer ε_eff ≈ 27–30. This is implausibly large for hBN-encapsulated TMDs (ε ≈ 4–5) even with nearby gates. It suggests either that Tₘ grossly underestimates the gap or that the model is miscalibrated.
- Logically, a lower bound on U₀ implies an upper bound on ε, not a determination of ε. The subsequent statement that "HF agrees with the twist-angle trend" is thus circular.

**"Mott gap at the Dirac point" is asserted rather than demonstrated.**
- The data show an incompressible state at the filling assigned as ν = −2. No measurement accesses the Dirac dispersion, chiral-symmetry breaking, or proximity to the Gross–Neveu transition.
- No control device on the semimetallic side is presented, nor any tuning (angle, displacement field, screening) across the transition. Only the competing work claims this.
- Other quantitative statements lack direct support or are internally inconsistent:
  - **Fermi velocity:** the abstract claims a reduction "by nearly two orders of magnitude," but the main text says "over thirty times" (consistent with t ≈ 5 meV, a_M ≈ 5 nm).
  - **Temperature scale:** the abstract's "persists up to 110 K" relies on a faint feature in D2 (Ext. Fig. 6h) with no quantitative Tₘ analysis shown for that device beyond Fig. 3d.
- Twist-angle-dependent atomic reconstruction, strain, and disorder could equally explain the D2 versus D1 difference in Tₘ(ν = −2), given only three devices and two of them nearly identical.

### 3.3 The trion-based argument for Mott (rather than CDW) order at ν = −2 is indirect and partially internally inconsistent

**The trion assignments themselves are insufficiently justified.**
- The spatial characters (X₂⁺ tightly bound on-site, X₁⁺ charge-transfer to a neighbouring site) are attributed to "DFT calculations." Standard DFT does not yield excitonic or trionic bound states; GW-BSE or a model calculation would be needed, and none is shown.
- The manuscript states: "Since the exciton-hole distance is larger in X₂⁺, the Coulomb attraction and binding energy become smaller." This contradicts the assignment that X₁⁺ (smaller binding energy) is the spatially separated one. The core logic needs to be stated consistently.
- It is not shown that K-valley intralayer excitons localize at the same MX/XM sites as the Γ-valley layer-hybridized holes. The exciton moiré potential minima in twisted homobilayers generally differ from the hole minima.
- The ~17 versus ~30 meV binding energies should be compared with expected intervalley Γ–K trion energies in a monolayer or bilayer.

**The doping dependence admits non-ordering explanations.**
- In doped TMDs the attractive-polaron oscillator strength generically grows, saturates, and then declines as the Fermi sea grows (Huang et al., PRX 2023). A maximum near some density does not by itself imply site-resolved charge order.
- The sharpness of the X₁⁺ peak at ν = −1 could reflect reduced screening at an incompressible state, as the authors themselves invoke for X₂⁺ in D2, rather than geometric hole–exciton adjacency.
- The CDW alternative is not quantitatively ruled out. In a CDW at ν = −2, half the sites are doubly occupied and half empty. This is also expected to produce strong X₁⁺ (exciton on an empty site next to a filled site) and suppressed on-site X₂⁺ due to Pauli blocking or double occupancy.
- A simple counting model is needed for both scenarios, giving the expected relative X₁⁺/X₂⁺ weights versus ν. It should be compared with the data in Fig. 4b before the conclusion "strongly suggest[s]" Mott order.
- The relevant ratio U₀/U₁ (which determines Mott versus CDW) is never estimated from experiment. The HF phase diagram (Ext. Fig. 4) does not show where the CDW phase would appear.

**There are presentation inconsistencies.**
- In Fig. 4b, the caption says the insets show ν = −2 (left) and ν = −1 (right), while the panels are D1 (left) and D2 (right). This confuses which configuration is being argued for.

### 3.4 The magnetic conclusions are over-interpreted relative to the data's statistical power and the probe's sensitivity

**The "weak ferromagnetic coupling" at ν = −1 is not statistically established.**
- Θ_CW = +0.20 ± 0.20 K is consistent with zero at 1σ. The second position gives +1.10 ± 0.40 K. Neither excludes Θ_CW = 0 or small negative values at 2–3σ.
- The fit is based on ~6–7 points between 1.6 and ~7 K. The 8 K point visibly deviates, and the authors acknowledge that MCD loses spin sensitivity above ~7 K. The fitting window is therefore barely larger than the extracted |Θ_CW|.
- MCD is used as a proxy for magnetization without calibration (no g-factor, no Brillouin-function fit). The sharp saturation at |B| = 3.8 T at 1.6 K should be compared with the free-spin Brillouin expectation, where g μ_B B/k_BT ≈ 3 at 3.8 T, 1.6 K for g ≈ 2.
- There is a methodological ambiguity about where MCD is integrated:
  - Methods and text: near the repulsive polaron (RP) at 1.620 eV;
  - Fig. 5c caption: near X₂⁺.
- The theoretical J (inset of Fig. 5d) depends on unspecified parameters, and the Supplementary Information needed to evaluate it is not provided.

**The ν = −2 "zero susceptibility" claim and the spin-liquid suggestion are problematic.**
- For a Heisenberg antiferromagnet with J ≈ 4t²/U₀ ≈ 4 meV (using the authors' own t and U₀), χ_zz at T ≪ J is reduced but finite (χ ~ 1/(zJ)), not zero.
- A null MCD therefore requires a sensitivity estimate showing that the expected signal lies below the noise floor; otherwise the null result is not informative.
- The MCD mechanism itself (trion exchange) may be suppressed at ν = −2 for non-magnetic reasons. These include changed trion character, reduced RP weight, and every site being occupied, which alters the exciton–hole configuration.
- The spin-liquid scenario cites Meng et al. (Nature 2010). Subsequent large-scale QMC (Sorella, Otsuka & Yunoki 2012; Assaad & Herbut 2013; Otsuka et al. 2016) found no spin-liquid phase in the half-filled honeycomb Hubbard model. Instead there is a direct semimetal-to-AFM transition at U/t ≈ 3.8. Invoking this scenario without acknowledging the later literature is misleading.
- **The Conclusion contradicts the main text.**
  - The Conclusion asserts "a Mott insulator with antiferromagnetic coupling" and an "unconventional magnetic ground state."
  - The main text states the scenarios cannot be distinguished.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Establish the density calibration independently and quantify assignment uncertainty.**
   - Provide an absolute density calibration, for example:
     - magneto-optical Landau-level or Shubnikov–de Haas-type features of the 2s sensor or attractive polaron;
     - identification of ν = −4 or ν = +1/+2 features;
     - geometric-capacitance consistency with measured hBN dielectric constants.
   - Explicitly address the unequal voltage spacings, either by modelling the nonlinear gating with the circuit model of SI S4 or by using a piecewise calibration with error bars.
   - Report the filling uncertainty for each fractional state.
   - Show reproducibility of the fractional states across positions and devices.
   - Give real-space configurations or classical-energy arguments for each fraction on the honeycomb lattice, especially −1/6 and −5/6.

2. **Put the gap and U₀/t analysis on firmer quantitative footing, and align the claims with what is measured.**
   - Measure the charge gap more directly. Options include temperature-dependent 2s energy shifts with an activation analysis, or comparison of chemical-potential jumps from exciton-sensing calibration.
   - Specify which Hubbard-model crossover the 4.75 k_BTₘ relation refers to, and propagate uncertainties in t (including relaxation effects in the BM model) and Tₘ into U₀/t.
   - Reconcile U₀ ≈ 25 meV and ε ≈ 27–30 with the 100–200 meV estimate. Treat ε as bounded rather than determined.
   - Present the Tₘ analysis for D2 explicitly.
   - Tone down "Mott gap at the Dirac point" and "nearly two orders of magnitude" unless additional evidence is provided. Ideally include a larger-angle or more strongly screened device showing the semimetallic side or a closing gap.
   - Clearly delineate what is new relative to arXiv:2412.07150 and Foutty et al.

3. **Strengthen the Mott-versus-CDW argument with explicit modelling and additional controls.**
   - Provide the actual exciton/trion calculation (GW-BSE or a continuum few-body model) that supports the spatial assignment of X₁⁺ and X₂⁺, including:
     - exciton localization relative to the hole sites;
     - intervalley exchange.
   - Correct the X₁⁺/X₂⁺ inconsistency in the text and the Fig. 4b caption.
   - Construct a site-occupation model predicting X₁⁺ and X₂⁺ oscillator strengths versus ν for (i) the Mott/AFM state and (ii) the sublattice CDW, and fit both to Fig. 4b.
   - Include a control showing that the trion maxima do not simply follow a generic polaron oscillator-strength curve. Examples: displacement-field or temperature dependence across Tₘ, or the behaviour at the fractional fillings.
   - Estimate U₀/U₁ from the data and the model, and show where the CDW phase lies in the HF phase diagram.

4. **Make the magnetic analysis statistically and physically rigorous, and moderate the conclusions.**
   - Extend the MCD temperature range downward (dilution refrigerator) or use a more sensitive probe, such as Kerr rotation or the RP with calibrated g-factor. This would bring the uncertainty on Θ_CW well below its magnitude.
   - Fit full M(B,T) curves with Brillouin or mean-field models rather than only the near-zero slope.
   - Clarify the spectral integration window (RP versus X₂⁺).
   - For ν = −2, provide a quantitative sensitivity estimate: the MCD expected for an AFM with J ~ 4t²/U₀ versus the measured noise floor. Also discuss non-magnetic reasons for suppression of the probe.
   - Update the spin-liquid discussion in light of the post-2012 QMC literature.
   - Make the Conclusion consistent with the main text, stating that the magnetic ground state at ν = −2 is undetermined.
   - Provide the Supplementary Information (HF, perturbative J, circuit model) with all parameters so the theory–experiment comparison can be reproduced.

**Minor issues to fix.**
- The acknowledgment lists "M.Z.," who is not an author.
- The circularity in using HF calculations calibrated with ε to "confirm" the twist-angle trend in Tₘ.
- Inconsistent naming (Bistritzer–MacDonald versus MacDonald–Bistritzer).
