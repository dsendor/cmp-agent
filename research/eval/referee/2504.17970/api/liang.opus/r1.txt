# Review outline

**Manuscript:** "Correlated insulating states in slow Dirac fermions on a honeycomb moiré superlattice" (twisted MoSe₂ homobilayer, Γ-valley moiré bands, Rydberg-exciton sensing, moiré trions and MCD)

---

## 1. Significance and novelty

- **Scientific target.** The paper addresses a long-standing goal: realizing the strongly interacting honeycomb Hubbard model, in which Coulomb repulsion gaps the Dirac point into a Mott insulator. Pristine graphene cannot reach this regime (U/t too small). An SU(2)-symmetric, spin-degenerate Γ-valley honeycomb moiré band with tunable U/t is a conceptually attractive simulator.
- **Platform novelty.**
  - Γ-valley t-MoSe₂ is predicted to host graphene-like bands (Angeli & MacDonald; Zhang, Liu & Fu).
  - Experimental demonstrations of correlated states in Γ-valley honeycomb moiré systems remain scarce.
  - The work combines a dual-gated geometry, a contact gate, and a WS₂ 2s-exciton sensor separated by a 1-nm spacer. It reports insulating states at ν = −1, −2 and at several fractional fillings (−1/6 … −5/6, −3/2).
- **Methodological novelty.**
  - The authors correlate two spatially distinct moiré trions in the sample (X₁⁺ "charge-transfer" and X₂⁺ "tightly bound") with integer fillings to infer the real-space charge configuration (Mott vs. CDW at ν = −2).
  - They use trion-based MCD to probe spin–spin coupling.
  - If validated, this is a useful all-optical toolkit for Γ-valley carriers, which couple weakly to the usual K-valley valley-selective optical probes.
- **Context that limits novelty.**
  - A closely related concurrent study (Ma et al., arXiv:2412.07150, "Relativistic Mott transition in strongly correlated artificial graphene") reports Mott physics in the same material system. The authors acknowledge it only in a one-line note.
  - Γ-valley correlated insulators and their spin/valley excitations have been reported before (e.g., Foutty et al., Nat. Mater. 2023).
  - Distinct moiré trions in twisted homobilayers have been reported (Liu et al., arXiv:2407.17025).
  - The genuinely new elements are therefore:
    - the trion-based charge-configuration argument at ν = −2;
    - the high melting temperature (~105–110 K, in D2 only);
    - the ν = −1 Curie–Weiss analysis.

---

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **Important physics question.** The Mott insulator at the Dirac point of a honeycomb lattice with full SU(2) symmetry is of broad interest. It connects to Gross–Neveu criticality, spin-liquid debates, and nonlocal-interaction-driven magnetism. A clean experimental platform would be valuable to a wide readership.
- **Rich and largely reproducible phenomenology.**
  - Integer-filling insulating states appear in three devices (D1, D2, D3) and two positions in D1.
  - Signatures persist to high temperature, with the ν = −2 feature visible at ~100–110 K in D2.
  - A hierarchy of melting temperatures (integer ≫ fractional) is consistent with expectations for Mott versus generalized Wigner crystal states.
- **Sound experimental design.**
  - The trilayer-hBN spacer suppresses charge and energy transfer while keeping the 2s exciton sensitive; the narrow WS₂ linewidths support this.
  - The contact gate mitigates contact-resistance artifacts.
  - The authors explicitly analyze nonlinear gating (circuit model in SI S4).
  - Weak electric-field dependence of R₂ₛ and indirect-exciton PL support Γ-valley hole doping.
- **Complementary probes.**
  - Sensor-layer compressibility (2s) is combined with sample-intrinsic spatially resolved trions and with MCD.
  - The anticorrelated doping dependence of X₁⁺ and X₂⁺ peaking near ν = −1 and −2 is striking and reproduced in D2.
- **Theory–experiment interplay.**
  - Unrestricted Hartree–Fock gives the ν = −2 gap versus θ and ε.
  - Fourth-order perturbative exchange gives J at ν = −1.
  - Both provide a framework for interpretation, and the twist-angle trend of T_m at ν = −2 qualitatively matches the HF trend.

## 3. Potential reasons for rejection

### Reason 1 — The filling-factor and twist-angle calibration is self-referential and internally inconsistent, which undermines all filling-specific claims.

- **Circular assignment.**
  - The two strongest 2s blueshifts are *assumed* to be ν = −1 and ν = −2 because they are the largest and most temperature-robust.
  - n_M (and hence θ ≈ 3.8°) is then derived from their voltage separation.
  - The fractional fillings are assigned by linear interpolation from these same anchors.
  - No independent density calibration is provided: no Landau-level or quantum-oscillation feature in the sensor or sample, no Hall or capacitance measurement, no ν = −4 band-insulator check, no structural twist-angle measurement (e.g., PFM, TEM, STM).
  - Alternative assignments cannot be excluded on the evidence shown, for example:
    - ν = −2 and ν = −4;
    - a sublattice-polarized ν = −1 together with a sensor-specific artifact.
  - The "targeted 3.6°" versus "calibrated 3.8°" agreement is not an independent check, given typical twist-angle relaxation and inhomogeneity.
- **Unequal voltage spacings despite the stated linearity.**
  - The text states fillings are assigned "based on the equal voltage spacing between the two integer fillings and the charge-neutral condition." The quoted voltages are not equally spaced:
    - charge neutrality ≈ −1.0 V → ν = −1 at −2.36 V (ΔV ≈ 1.36 V);
    - ν = −1 → ν = −2 at −3.48 V (ΔV ≈ 1.12 V).
  - This ~20% discrepancy, together with the acknowledged contact- and gate-leakage nonlinearities in exactly the ν ≤ −1/3 and ν ≈ −2 ranges, makes assignment of closely spaced fractions (−1/6, −1/4, −1/3) at the ±0.05 level unreliable.
  - Several of these fractions disperse with E for extrinsic reasons, per the authors' own account.
- **Mismatch between Fig. 2 and Fig. 4.**
  - The text says X₁⁺ peaks at V_tg ≈ (−)2.67 V and X₂⁺ at ≈ (−)3.50 V, "well matched" to ν = −1 and −2.
  - In Fig. 2, however, ν = −1 sits at −2.36 V. The ~0.3 V offset corresponds to Δν ≈ 0.25–0.3, which is not a match.
  - If Fig. 4 data come from a different cooldown, position or temperature (1.6 K vs. 7 K), the filling axis must be recalibrated and stated. Otherwise the central X₁⁺/ν = −1 correlation is weakened.
  - Also unexplained: the sign convention (positive voltages quoted in text, negative on the figure axes) and the lower nominal density but larger voltage spans in D2.
- **Theory inputs propagate the uncertainty.** U₀/t, the T_m-versus-θ trend (Fig. 3d) and the HF comparison (Ext. Fig. 4) all inherit any error in θ.
  - With only three angles (2.9°, 3.8°, 4.0°), two of them nearly equal, the claimed "T_m(ν = −2) nearly doubles as θ decreases" rests effectively on a single device (D2).

### Reason 2 — The identification of a correlation-driven (Mott) gap at the Dirac point is not established against single-particle and CDW alternatives.

- **A single-particle sublattice mass is not excluded.**
  - In Γ-valley t-TMDs, MX and XM sites carry opposite layer weight, so any layer asymmetry acts as a staggered sublattice potential that gaps the Dirac point at ν = −2 without interactions.
  - The device is intrinsically asymmetric: the WS₂ sensor and 1-nm spacer lie on one side only, and the top and bottom hBN thicknesses differ (6.6 vs. 9.5 nm).
  - The authors find that R₂ₛ at ν = −2 is nearly E-independent up to |E| ≈ 50 mV/nm. Yet across ~0.65 nm interlayer separation, a partially layer-polarized orbital should still acquire a sublattice bias of order ~10 meV, comparable to the inferred gap (~25 meV).
  - A Mott-to-sublattice-polarized (band/CDW) insulator crossover would therefore be expected under E. The *absence* of any E response therefore needs a quantitative explanation, with the layer polarization of the Wannier orbitals estimated.
  - At minimum, the authors should estimate the asymmetry-induced mass and show it is ≪ the observed gap.
- **The trion-based Mott-versus-CDW argument is indirect and model-dependent.**
  - The argument assumes:
    - K-valley MoSe₂ excitons are localized at MX/XM sites (supported only by an unspecified DFT result);
    - X₁⁺ and X₂⁺ oscillator strengths scale with the probability of an exciton finding a hole on a neighboring versus the same site.
  - A monotonic rise of X₂⁺ followed by decline beyond ν = −2 could arise from generic phase-space filling or from the trion-to-polaron crossover at high density (n ≈ 10¹³ cm⁻²), independent of the charge configuration.
  - In a sublattice-polarized CDW, excitons on doubly occupied sites would also form same-site trions while those on empty sites form charge-transfer trions. Quantitative predicted oscillator-strength ratios for Mott versus CDW are needed, not a qualitative argument.
  - The text also contradicts itself: it states "the exciton–hole distance is larger in X₂⁺, the … binding energy become[s] smaller," which is the opposite of the assignment (X₁⁺ is the charge-transfer trion).
- **Quantitative energy-scale inferences are fragile and inconsistent.**
  - U₀ ≈ 4.75 k_B T_m borrows a relation from finite-T DQMC of the honeycomb Hubbard model (Tang et al.). T_m here is an instrument-dependent threshold (10% of maximum 2s spectral-weight contrast), not a thermodynamic crossover, so the mapping is not justified.
  - The resulting U₀/t ≈ 5 for D1 is only marginally above U_c/t ≈ 3.8–4. The claim "Mott insulator" versus "near-critical" therefore depends on t ≈ 5 meV from an ideal unrelaxed continuum model, with no uncertainty given.
  - The extracted ε ≈ 27–30 is unusually large for hBN encapsulation (ε ≈ 4–5, even including gate screening). It also contradicts the introduction's U₀ = 100–200 meV.
  - If ε ≈ 30 is needed, the band-projected Hubbard description and the HF calculations should be re-examined. U ≈ 25 meV is comparable to the gap between the top two bands and remote bands (Fig. 1d), so the two-band projection is questionable.
- **Overstated framing.**
  - The abstract claims a Fermi velocity "reduced by nearly two orders of magnitude." With t ≈ 5 meV and a site spacing a_M/√3 ≈ 2.9 nm, v_F = 3ta/2ħ ≈ 3 × 10⁴ m/s, i.e., ~30× smaller than graphene, as the main text itself states.
  - The Gross–Neveu framing implies tuning across a semimetal–insulator transition, but no semimetallic regime is observed in any device. The experiment only shows an insulating state at ν = −2.

### Reason 3 — The magnetic conclusions at ν = −1 and ν = −2 are not supported by the data at the stated level.

- **Ferromagnetism at ν = −1 is statistically and phenomenologically weak.**
  - Θ_CW = +0.20 ± 0.20 K is consistent with zero within 1σ. The second position gives +1.10 ± 0.40 K. Together these do not establish a sign of J.
  - The fit uses only a handful of points between 1.6 and ~7 K, with the 8 K point excluded as an outlier.
  - The authors concede that the MCD loses spin sensitivity above ~7 K, so the fit window and the sensor linearity are entangled.
  - The M(B) shape is in tension with the claim:
    - at 1.6 K the MCD is *linear* up to a sharp saturation at 3.8 ± 0.2 T;
    - for a free S = 1/2 moment with g ≈ 2, gμ_B B at 3.8 T ≈ 0.44 meV ≈ 5 K, so a Brillouin curve, not a kink, is expected;
    - linear-then-saturating magnetization is the classic hallmark of an *antiferromagnetically* coupled system (saturation field ∝ zJ), whereas FM coupling would give faster-than-Brillouin saturation.
  - A full Brillouin/mean-field fit of M(B,T) is required.
- **The MCD-to-magnetization mapping is not calibrated.**
  - The MCD integration window is described inconsistently:
    - "around 1.620 eV" (main text);
    - "near the Fermi-polaron (RP) resonance" (Methods);
    - "near the resonance of X₂⁺" (Fig. 5c caption).
  - Because X₂⁺ and RP carry opposite signs, the choice of window matters.
  - The reference spectrum is taken at "heavily hole-doped" conditions, which may themselves be field-dependent.
  - No check is shown that MCD ∝ M, for example at a dilute or Fermi-liquid filling where Pauli or Curie behavior is known. The Γ-valley hole g-factor is not discussed.
- **The null MCD at ν = −2 is overinterpreted.**
  - The absence of MCD up to 8 T is taken to indicate zero susceptibility, and the conclusion asserts a "Mott insulator with antiferromagnetic coupling."
  - The abstract and discussion simultaneously invoke a possible gapped spin liquid.
  - There is no control showing that the sensor is spin-sensitive at and near ν = −2, for example nonzero MCD at ν = −1.8 or −2.2, where X₂⁺ is also strong.
  - A spin-singlet-favoring trion configuration at full site occupancy (each exciton binding to a site already hosting one hole) could suppress MCD for reasons unrelated to the magnetic ground state.
  - An AFM interpretation also requires J ≫ k_B T at 1.6 K, i.e., J ≳ 1 meV. This should be compared with 4t²/U₀ ≈ 4 meV for D1 and the expected susceptibility of a 2D Heisenberg AFM, which is not zero at T ≪ J.
- **Theory support for FM J is not tied to the experimental parameters.**
  - The inset of Fig. 5d shows J(ε) negative over 0–40. However, the Heisenberg parameters and the ε that reproduces the observed T_m (ε ≈ 27–30) are not tied to a predicted Θ_CW.
  - The authors should state the predicted Θ_CW (≈ zJ/4k_B) at ε ≈ 28 and compare it with +0.2 K.

### Reason 4 — The advance over concurrent and prior work is unclear, and the thermal-melting analysis is over-interpreted.

- **Positioning relative to closely related work is insufficient.**
  - The Cornell work (Ma et al., arXiv:2412.07150) reports a Mott transition in the same Γ-valley honeycomb system. The present manuscript does not compare twist angles, gaps, critical U/t, or magnetic findings.
  - Without such a comparison, it is not clear which conclusions are new or whether they agree. Specifically:
    - the trion-based Mott-versus-CDW discrimination;
    - FM coupling at ν = −1;
    - the T_m(θ) trend.
  - Prior Γ-valley correlated-insulator work (Foutty et al.; Pan, Kim & Jian) and the moiré-trion work (Liu et al.) further reduce the novelty of the individual ingredients.
- **The melting temperature is ill-defined and sensor-dependent.**
  - T_m is defined at 10% of maximum 2s spectral-weight contrast, while the 2s linewidth, background and polaron splitting all change with T.
  - The ν = −1 non-monotonic contrast (peak at T* ≈ 15 K) is likened to the Pomeranchuk effect. No supporting evidence is shown, such as filling shifts with T or entropy arguments, and a temperature-dependent sensor-response or background artifact is not excluded.
  - Since the Pomeranchuk effect implies a spin-entropy-driven stabilization, it should be reconciled with the weak FM coupling (|J| ≪ 15 K) claimed in the same section.
- **Mechanistic conclusions from T_m(θ) are underdetermined.**
  - Three devices, two nearly degenerate in angle and each with ±5–10 K error bars, cannot discriminate "gap-limited" from "Lindemann-limited" melting.
  - Nor can they support the statement that ν = −1 melting "depends on the local moiré potential rather than the moiré period."
  - The sublattice-polarized crystal at ν = −1 attributed to U₁ should have a gap scaling with 1/a_M, so a θ-independent T_m is, if anything, unexpected and requires discussion.
- **Headline claims rest on limited data.**
  - The "persists up to 110 K" claim in the abstract relies on a single device (D2), where the feature at 110 K is barely discernible in Ext. Fig. 6h.
  - The fractional states (including −1/6, −5/6, −3/2) are identified from weak R₂ₛ modulations at a single position and temperature, with no lattice-commensuration analysis for the honeycomb geometry.
  - The P2 dataset (Ext. Fig. 5b) shows a visibly different set of features.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Provide an independent, quantitative density and twist-angle calibration, and reconcile inconsistencies.**
   - Extend gating, if possible, to the ν = −4 filled-band gap. Alternatively, use Landau-level or quantum-oscillation features in the 2s or 1s response under field, capacitance or Hall measurements, or structural imaging (PFM/TEM) to fix n_M independently of the assumed ν = −1/−2 anchors.
   - Report gate capacitances including quantum capacitance, and the full voltage-to-ν mapping with uncertainties.
   - Explain the unequal 0→−1 and −1→−2 voltage spacings.
   - Reconcile the X₁⁺/X₂⁺ peak voltages in Fig. 4 with the Fig. 2 calibration, including temperature, position, cooldown and voltage sign conventions.
   - Present all key datasets (2s, trions, MCD) on a common calibrated ν axis.

2. **Directly test the Mott versus single-particle/CDW nature of the ν = −2 gap.**
   - Perform systematic displacement-field sweeps at larger |E| (or with a symmetric control device without a sensor-side asymmetry) and compare with HF predictions that include a sublattice bias from the measured layer polarization of the Γ-valley Wannier orbitals.
   - Estimate the built-in staggered potential from the asymmetric dielectric stack.
   - Quantify the gap by a thermally activated analysis (e.g., 2s energy shift or contrast versus T with a screening model) rather than the 4.75 k_B T_m conversion.
   - Provide uncertainty bands on t from a relaxation-corrected continuum model.
   - Compute predicted X₁⁺/X₂⁺ oscillator-strength ratios versus ν for the Mott and CDW configurations, including exciton localization from DFT with details in the SI.
   - Reconcile ε ≈ 27–30 with the stated U₀ = 100–200 meV and with the validity of the two-band projection.

3. **Strengthen and calibrate the magnetic measurements, and temper the conclusions accordingly.**
   - Fit the full M(B,T) at ν = −1 with Brillouin and mean-field (FM and AFM) models. Address explicitly why the linear-then-saturating M(B) at 1.6 K is consistent with FM rather than AFM coupling.
   - Add more temperatures below 7 K and, if possible, lower base temperature.
   - Calibrate MCD ∝ M at a reference filling, and unify the definition of the integration window (X₂⁺ vs. RP vs. 1.620 eV).
   - Show filling-resolved MCD (ν from −0.5 to −2.5) to demonstrate sensor spin-sensitivity adjacent to ν = −2, so the null result there is meaningful.
   - Compare the predicted Θ_CW from the computed J at the experimentally inferred ε with the measurement.
   - Rephrase the conclusion: "antiferromagnetic coupling" at ν = −2 and "weak ferromagnetic coupling" at ν = −1 are hypotheses consistent with the data, not established results.

4. **Clarify novelty and positioning, and correct presentation errors.**
   - Add a substantive comparison with Ma et al. (arXiv:2412.07150), Foutty et al., and Liu et al. (moiré trions) covering angles, gaps, critical U/t and magnetic results, and state precisely what is new here.
   - Correct the "nearly two orders of magnitude" versus "over thirty times" Fermi-velocity statement.
   - Fix the X₁⁺/X₂⁺ binding-energy sentence.
   - Fix the T_m-related colour and legend descriptions, and the sign of the quoted V_tg values.
   - Give a robust, sensor-independent definition of T_m (e.g., multiple thresholds, or fits to a gap model), with sensitivity analysis.
   - Support or remove the Pomeranchuk analogy and the "local potential versus moiré period" melting statement.
   - Moderate the "110 K" abstract claim to reflect that it comes from one device.
   - Provide a lattice-commensuration analysis for the reported fractional fillings on the honeycomb lattice, with E-field and multi-position reproducibility.
   - Expand the SI with HF parameters, the DFT trion-localization method, and the circuit-model fits to the nonlinear gating.
