Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Scientific question.** The paper targets a long-standing problem: the interaction-driven semimetal-to-Mott-insulator (Gross–Neveu-type) instability of Dirac fermions on a honeycomb lattice. This instability is inaccessible in graphene because the Fermi velocity is large compared with the Coulomb scale.
- **Platform.** Γ-valley-derived moiré bands in small-angle twisted MoSe₂ (2–4°) are an attractive simulator:
  - The MX/XM potential minima form a honeycomb lattice.
  - The bands are spin-degenerate, with SU(2) symmetry and negligible spin–orbit coupling.
  - The Dirac velocity is strongly reduced. With t ≈ 5 meV and a_M ≈ 5 nm, v ≈ 3×10⁴ m/s, about 30× slower than graphene.
- **Claimed advances:**
  - Optical (2s Rydberg-exciton) detection of incompressible states at ν = −1, −2 and at six fractional fillings in 0 > ν > −1.
  - An insulating state at ν = −2 (the single-particle Dirac point) persisting to ~65 K (D1) and ~105–110 K (D2).
  - Use of two moiré trions with distinct spatial character (on-site X₂⁺ vs. charge-transfer X₁⁺) to infer one-hole-per-site occupancy at ν = −2, i.e., Mott rather than CDW.
  - MCD-based magnetometry giving a small positive Curie–Weiss temperature at ν = −1 and no detectable MCD at ν = −2.
- **Novelty relative to concurrent work.** Novelty must be weighed against Ma et al. (arXiv:2412.07150, Cornell), which reports a relativistic Mott transition in the same material system. The distinct contributions here are mainly:
  - the optical sensing methodology;
  - trion-based charge-distribution analysis;
  - magnetic measurements;
  - fractional-filling states.
  
  The paper does not tune across the semimetal–insulator transition, so it does not directly address the Gross–Neveu physics invoked in the abstract and introduction.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Timely, important platform.** The work provides an independent experimental confirmation of correlated insulators in Γ-valley honeycomb moiré bands, a system of broad interest for Hubbard-model simulation with SU(2) spin symmetry.
- **Rich and multi-modal data set:**
  - 2s sensing versus doping, electric field, and temperature (1.6–110 K).
  - 1s trion spectroscopy.
  - MCD versus field and temperature.
  - Three devices spanning 2.9–4.0°, plus two positions in D1.
- **Robust ν = −2 state.** The insulating state at ν = −2 is reproduced across devices, with high melting temperatures. Its twist-angle trend (higher Tm at smaller θ) is qualitatively consistent with increasing U₀/t.
- **Creative trion-based probe.** Correlating the charge-transfer and on-site trions with integer fillings is a potentially general optical probe of sublattice occupancy in honeycomb moiré systems.
- **Supporting theory.** The theory spans continuum-model bands, unrestricted Hartree–Fock, DFT for trion spatial character, and perturbative exchange. This provides a coherent, if model-dependent, interpretive framework.
- **Clear device engineering.** The contact gate and 1-nm hBN spacer are well designed, and the narrow WS₂ exciton linewidths confirm that sensor–sample charge and energy transfer is suppressed.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**Reason 1: Filling-factor assignment and electrostatic calibration are not independently established, yet all central conclusions rest on them.**

- **Circular integer-filling assignment.** ν = −1 and −2 are assigned to the two strongest blueshifts (Vtg = −2.36 V and −3.48 V), and ν is then interpolated linearly from charge neutrality.
  - The authors themselves document strong nonlinear gating: contact-resistance-induced dispersion at ν ≤ −1/3, and gate-dielectric leakage near breakdown around ν = −2.
  - Linearity of n(V) is therefore precisely what is in doubt.
  - Alternative assignments are not excluded, e.g., (−2, −4), or a ν = −1 feature shifted by nonlinear gating.
  - No independent density calibration is provided, such as Landau-level fan diagrams, Hall density, or STM/TEM/PFM twist-angle measurement.
- **Sensitivity of the extracted twist angle.** The twist angle (3.8° vs. target 3.6°) depends on assumed ε_hBN ≈ 3 and ε_WS₂ ≈ 7.5 and on AFM thicknesses.
  - Since θ ∝ √n_M, a 10–15% capacitance uncertainty gives a ~0.2° error.
  - Error bars on n_M and θ, and on ν for each feature, should be reported.
- **Internal inconsistency in trion peak positions.**
  - X₁⁺ is said to peak at Vtg ≈ 2.67 V (sign apparently dropped) versus the 2s-assigned ν = −1 at −2.36 V.
  - The 0.31 V offset is ~28% of the ν = −1 to −2 spacing (1.12 V), placing the X₁⁺ maximum near ν ≈ −1.3, not ν = −1.
  - This undermines the claim that the X₁⁺ maximum "matches well" ν = −1, while X₂⁺ (3.50 vs. 3.48 V) does match ν = −2.
  - The authors should explain whether the 1s and 2s data were taken under identical gating conditions and positions.
- **Weak support for the fractional states.** The six fractional states (−1/6, −1/4, −1/3, −1/2, −2/3, −5/6), plus −3/2 in Fig. 3c, appear as modest R₂s modulations.
  - Several lie in the regime where E-field dispersion from contact resistance is admitted.
  - There is no assessment of signal-to-noise, reproducibility across sweeps and positions, or the expected generalized Wigner crystal structures on the honeycomb lattice for each fraction.
  - Without this, listing six fractions risks over-interpretation.
  - P2 (Ext. Fig. 5b) shows a visibly different set of features.

**Reason 2: The interpretation of ν = −2 as a correlation-driven Mott insulator "at the Dirac point" is not uniquely supported; single-particle and CDW alternatives remain open.**

- **Possible single-particle (Semenoff) gap.** MX and XM are related by a symmetry (C₂y-type) that any layer asymmetry breaks, opening a Semenoff mass at the Dirac point. The device is intrinsically layer-asymmetric:
  - The WS₂ sensor and the 1-nm spacer sit on one side only.
  - The top and bottom hBN thicknesses differ (6.6 vs. 9.5 nm).
  - Lattice relaxation and heterostrain also contribute.
  
  The authors should:
  - estimate the resulting sublattice offset and compare it with the observed gap scale;
  - explain why the near-E-independence of the ν = −2 feature (Fig. 2d) excludes a field-tunable Semenoff gap, given the claimed hundreds-of-meV interlayer hybridization;
  - show whether there is any trend of R₂s at ν = −2 with E beyond the leakage region.
- **The trion argument for Mott vs. CDW is indirect and model-dependent.**
  - It relies on DFT assignment of X₁⁺ as a charge-transfer trion and X₂⁺ as an on-site trion. Binding energies (17 vs. 30 meV) are not computed quantitatively, and the main-text sentence explaining them is self-contradictory: it says the exciton–hole distance is larger in X₂⁺ when the argument requires X₁⁺.
  - A CDW with doubly occupied sites on one sublattice also places holes on-site with excitons. It is unclear why X₂⁺ would not also be maximal there.
  - At these densities (~10¹³ cm⁻²), the attractive/repulsive polaron description, screening, and phase-space filling all modulate oscillator strength. The amplitude maxima may track compressibility minima rather than site occupancy (cf. the authors' own explanation of X₂⁺ enhancement at ν = −1 in D2).
  - A quantitative model of trion oscillator strength versus occupancy configuration is needed.
- **Overstated persistence claim.** The abstract's claim that the Mott gap "persists up to 110 K" relies on D2 data (Ext. Fig. 6g,h) where the ν = −2 feature is barely visible. D1 gives ~65 K, so the headline number should be qualified and quantified with the same spectral-weight-contrast analysis used for D1.

**Reason 3: Quantitative extraction of U₀, U₀/t, and the dielectric constant is weakly grounded and partly circular.**

- **Unjustified mapping from Tm to U₀.**
  - U₀ ≈ 4.75 k_B·Tm is taken from finite-T QMC/NLCE results (Tang et al.) for a specific thermodynamic feature.
  - Tm here is operationally defined as 10% of the maximum 2s spectral-weight contrast, a sensor-dependent quantity that the authors acknowledge is also affected by loss of sensitivity and Lindemann-type melting.
  - Mapping one onto the other is not justified. The resulting U₀/t ≈ 5 for D1 is only marginally above U_c/t ≈ 3.8–3.9.
  - With t ≈ 5 meV taken from a continuum model with unquoted parameter uncertainty, the conclusion that D1 lies in the insulating phase is not quantitatively robust.
  - The same applies to U₀/t ≥ 17 for D2.
- **Circular dielectric constant.**
  - The effective ε ≈ 27–30 is obtained by fitting HF to the U₀ lower bound, then used to "confirm" the twist-angle trend.
  - ε ≈ 27–30 is far above the hBN environment (~3–5). It likely absorbs HF's known overestimation of gaps and screening by the WS₂ sensor and gates.
  - This should be stated explicitly rather than presented as a retrieved material parameter.
- **Unexplained ν = −1 melting behavior.**
  - In D1, ν = −1 and ν = −2 have essentially identical Tm (~65 K), and ν = −1 is twist-angle independent across devices.
  - A U₁-driven crystal and a U₀-driven Mott state would naively have different energy scales. The explanation offered ("local moiré potential rather than moiré period") is vague.
  - The non-monotonic T dependence at ν = −1, with T* ≈ 15 K, is labeled Pomeranchuk-like. However, the reported |Θ_CW| ≲ 1 K implies the spin entropy is already nearly saturated by 1.6–7 K, so a spin-entropy mechanism at 15 K is inconsistent with the authors' own exchange scale.
  - An alternative explanation is needed, for example competing charge orders, sensor effects, or T-dependent linewidth artifacts in the spectral-weight metric.

**Reason 4: The magnetic conclusions are statistically and methodologically weak, and the conclusion overreaches.**

- **The ferromagnetism claim is statistically marginal.**
  - Θ_CW = +0.20 ± 0.20 K at P1 is consistent with zero, from a fit over ~1.6–7 K with few points. Above 7 K the authors admit the MCD no longer tracks magnetization.
  - P2 gives +1.1 ± 0.4 K, so the two values differ beyond the errors at roughly 2σ.
  - Over such a narrow window, systematic effects can easily shift Θ_CW by ~1 K: a T-independent background term, the choice of |B| ≤ 1.2 T, or T-dependent trion linewidth.
  - "Weak ferromagnetic coupling", stated in the abstract, is therefore not established.
  - The MCD integration window is described inconsistently: near the RP resonance (Methods and main text, 1.620 eV) versus near X₂⁺ (Fig. 5c caption).
- **Absence of MCD at ν = −2 is over-interpreted.**
  - The conclusion states "a Mott insulator with antiferromagnetic coupling", yet the text concedes the data cannot distinguish AFM correlations, a spin gap, or other possibilities.
  - The cited spin-liquid scenario (Meng et al., 2010) was subsequently overturned by larger-scale QMC (Sorella et al., 2012; Assaad & Herbut, 2013), which found a direct semimetal–AFM transition. Invoking it without this caveat is misleading.
  - Mermin–Wagner (SU(2)) precludes finite-T order. A Heisenberg AFM with J = 4t²/U₀ ≈ 4 meV would still give a finite, measurable χ at 1.6 K.
  - The authors should estimate the expected MCD magnitude and compare it with their noise floor.
  - A sensor-failure scenario is not excluded: at ν = −2 every exciton binds on-site, the RP/trion contributions change, and opposite-sign contributions may cancel.
  - The authors need a control showing the MCD probe remains spin-sensitive near ν = −2, e.g., at ν = −1.5 or −2.5 or slightly away from commensurability.
- **Charge order at ν = −1 is untested.** The HF prediction of a sublattice-polarized crystal at ν = −1 is untested experimentally. The trion data are equally compatible with other quarter-filling arrangements, such as stripes or mixed-sublattice order.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Independently calibrate density and twist angle, and tighten the filling assignment.**
   - Provide magneto-optical Landau-level fans or Hall/capacitance data to verify n(V) linearity, or STM/TEM/PFM twist-angle measurement.
   - Report error bars on n_M, θ, and each ν.
   - Reconcile the X₁⁺ peak position (2.67 V) with the ν = −1 2s feature (2.36 V).
   - Show reproducibility (multiple sweeps and positions) and a noise analysis for each fractional state.
   - Discuss the candidate charge configurations of the fractional states on the honeycomb lattice.
2. **Discriminate the ν = −2 Mott state from single-particle and CDW alternatives.**
   - Estimate the built-in sublattice asymmetry from the asymmetric stack and show that the gap scale exceeds it.
   - Present the ν = −2 contrast versus E over a wider range, with a model of the expected Semenoff-gap response.
   - Develop a quantitative model of X₁⁺/X₂⁺ oscillator strengths for Mott versus CDW configurations, including polaron and screening effects.
   - Fix the contradictory statement on exciton–hole distance.
   - Report Tm for D2 using the same spectral-weight-contrast metric as D1, and qualify the "110 K" claim accordingly.
3. **Reframe the quantitative energetics with uncertainties and without circularity.**
   - Justify or replace the U₀ ≈ 4.75 k_B·Tm mapping, e.g., by modeling the 2s response versus T for a given gap, or by using thermally activated fits.
   - Propagate uncertainties in t from continuum-model parameters.
   - Present ε ≈ 27–30 explicitly as an HF fitting parameter.
   - Offer a consistent explanation of the ν = −1 non-monotonic T dependence and twist-independent Tm that does not conflict with the sub-kelvin exchange scale.
   - Moderate the language linking the results to the Gross–Neveu transition, since the transition itself is not traversed.
   - Clearly delineate what is new relative to arXiv:2412.07150.
4. **Strengthen the magnetic analysis and align the conclusions with the evidence.**
   - Extend measurements below 1.6 K or to more temperature points.
   - Fit with and without a constant background, and report sensitivity of Θ_CW to the field window.
   - Report results from more positions and devices.
   - Unify the description of the MCD integration window.
   - Add control measurements demonstrating spin sensitivity of the probe near ν = −2, and give a quantitative estimate of the expected MCD for an AFM Mott state with J ≈ 4t²/U₀.
   - Remove or heavily caveat the spin-liquid suggestion, citing the later QMC literature.
   - Replace "Mott insulator with antiferromagnetic coupling" and "weak ferromagnetic coupling" in the abstract and conclusion with statements matching the actual statistical significance.
   - Correct the inconsistent velocity-reduction claims ("nearly two orders of magnitude" vs. "over thirty times").
