Referee report

Manuscript: "Unveiling the landscape of Mottness and its proximity to superconductivity in 4Hb-TaS2"

1. Summary of the submission

The authors use STM/STS on cleaved 4Hb-TaS1.99Se0.01 to study the 1T (Star of David, SoD) termination. Most clusters show a nearly empty narrow band just above EF (about 0.2 e per cluster, W0 about 80 meV). Roughly 12% of clusters show a sharp zero-bias peak flanked by two broad peaks, and there is a continuum of intermediate spectra. The authors attribute the variation to cluster-by-cluster differences in interlayer charge transfer, set by Se substitution (with supporting experiments on Se content and uniaxial strain in the SI). Sorting about 100 spectra by the energy of a broad hump near -0.5 eV, they present the evolution as a filling-controlled Mott transition: Hubbard bands emerge with a filling-independent U of about 190 meV, and the central peak sharpens and loses weight. A comparison with single-band DMFT is qualitative. They argue the central peak is a Brinkman-Rice quasiparticle peak and not a Kondo resonance screened by 1H electrons. The arguments are that a TK estimated from the Anderson model (about 40 K) predicts a much wider peak than observed, and that the observed HWHM falls linearly with T from 4.2 to 0.3 K. Finally they report a proximity-type superconducting gap on the 1T surface (up to 0.35 meV). The gap shrinks with increasing local filling and is absent on the correlated clusters, which instead show a Zeeman-split zero-bias peak (g about 2). They speculate that these paramagnetic clusters are linked to the pi-shifted Little-Parks results and the magnetic memory effect.

The dataset is interesting. A single surface that offers a quasi-continuous set of local fillings in a well-defined narrow band is a useful platform. The 1T-surface superconducting gap and its anticorrelation with local correlation strength is a potentially valuable observation. However, the central claims are stated far more strongly than the evidence supports ("unambiguously demonstrate", "quantitatively describe", "unsurpassed energy resolution", "first full characteristics", "seminal system"). The two most important ones need substantially more work: that the filling is controlled and calibrated up to half filling, and that the central peak is a doped-Mott coherent peak rather than a Kondo resonance. My recommendation is major revision.

2. Major issues

2.1 The filling axis is not calibrated, and "toward half filling" is asserted, not measured.

The only quantitative filling determination is the 0.2 e per cluster obtained for the depleted narrow band, from the fraction of narrow-band weight below EF. The bottom of Fig. 2d is labelled "1.0 e", but I find no measurement that supports it. The sorting variable is the energy of the -0.5 eV hump (Fig. 2c to 2e), and from Fig. 3 onward the variable is the LHB position epsilon (-100 to -40 meV). Neither is converted to an electron count. Please:
(a) Apply the same integration used for the 0.2 e estimate to every spectrum in Fig. 2d, and plot n against hump position and against epsilon, with uncertainties. In the single-band picture the fraction of the +/-0.25 eV weight lying below EF should grow from about 0.1 to 0.5. That is a direct, falsifiable check, and Fig. 2e (constant total weight) already provides the denominator.
(b) Explain the conversion from hump energy to filling. A rigid shift of the -0.5 eV band by about 70 to 80 meV (Fig. 2c, 2e) is equally consistent with a local electrostatic potential (for example from the Se substituent, local strain, or the screened charge of neighbouring clusters) that does not change the narrow-band occupation one-to-one. Please show why the shift tracks occupation rather than local potential.
(c) Note that epsilon is defined only once a resolved LHB exists. Figs. 3c to 3f therefore cover only the upper part of the filling range. The low-filling half of the "continuous" evolution is shown only in the colormap, not quantified.

2.2 Sorting by a single fitted parameter can manufacture apparent continuity.

Fig. 2e shows most hump positions within about 40 meV (roughly -490 to -450 meV). The fit uncertainty of a broad hump at -0.5 eV is not given, and it may be comparable to the spacing between neighbouring rows of Fig. 2d. Sorting noisy spectra by one parameter always produces a monotonic-looking colormap in that parameter. The physically meaningful statement is the correlation between the high-energy and low-energy features. Please (i) give fit uncertainties for the hump position, (ii) report the correlation (with scatter) between hump position and independent low-energy observables (epsilon, central-peak weight, LHB/UHB weight ratio), and (iii) show the colormap with the spectra sorted by a low-energy parameter instead, to demonstrate that the ordering is robust.

2.3 A spatially dilute set of half-filled clusters is not a homogeneous doped Mott lattice.

The comparison with lattice DMFT and the Brinkman-Rice picture assumes a translationally invariant system at filling n. Here the high-filling clusters are about 12% of the surface, embedded in a matrix of 0.2 e clusters (with some short-range order, SI Fig. 11). A coherent Brinkman-Rice quasiparticle band requires intercluster hopping among sites of similar filling. An isolated near-half-filled cluster surrounded by depleted clusters and coupled to the 1H layer is, by construction, an Anderson impurity. Its zero-bias peak is a Kondo-type resonance whose bath is some combination of the neighbouring 1T clusters and the 1H layer. The relevant question is then which bath does the screening, not "Kondo versus Mott". Please:
(a) Show whether the low-energy spectrum of a given cluster depends on the fillings of its neighbours (isolated correlated clusters versus clusters inside correlated patches). This is the single most direct test of lattice coherence that the data permit.
(b) Reframe the "ensemble of doped Mott insulators" language accordingly, or justify why each cluster can be treated as a homogeneous system at its own filling.

2.4 The argument against Kondo screening by the 1H layer does not hold as presented.

(a) The TK estimate uses the Haldane/Anderson formula with W0, the 1T narrow-band width (80 meV), in place of the hybridization width Gamma between the cluster orbital and the 1H conduction electrons. These are different quantities. Gamma is not measured and is presumably much smaller than W0. Because TK depends exponentially on Gamma, TK can be anywhere from tens of K down to well below 1 K for plausible Gamma (for example, Gamma about 20 meV with the stated epsilon and U gives TK of order 0.1 K). The conclusion that a 40 K Kondo scale is incompatible with the observed width therefore rules out only one arbitrary parameter choice. Please treat TK, or Gamma, as a free parameter.
(b) The linear decrease of the HWHM from 4.2 K to 0.3 K (Fig. 3h) does not discriminate between scenarios. The slope read from Fig. 3h is about 0.28 meV/K. Thermal broadening of the tip and sample Fermi functions alone gives a FWHM of about 3.5 kBT (about 0.3 meV/K), and the Kondo expression the authors quote contributes alpha*kBT/2 at T above TK. For a Kondo resonance with TK of a few K, T-linear broadening in this window is exactly what is expected. The extrapolated zero-T HWHM (about 0.5 to 0.6 meV) corresponds to kBTK of the same order. Please deconvolve thermal and lock-in broadening (Vmod 0.05 to 0.2 meV below 4 K, and 2 meV for the wide spectra) and fit the T dependence with TK free. The phrase "suggests an essential mechanism at zero temperature" should be explained or removed.
(c) Fig. 3f states that the FWHM was obtained from Fano fits, while the text says Lorentzian fits were used. If Fano fits were used, please report the q parameter and its evolution. A Fano line shape is itself a hallmark of interference between a localized level and a conduction channel, and is directly relevant to the Kondo question.
(d) The Zeeman splitting with g about 2 (SI Fig. 10), and the description of these clusters as "paramagnetic local moments", fit more naturally with a weakly screened local moment than with a coherent heavy quasiparticle band. Please state the field at which the splitting becomes resolved and compare it with kBTK/(g muB) for the TK implied by the width.
(e) A potentially stronger argument is not made. At 0.3 K the 1H layer is superconducting (Delta about 0.5 meV). A resonance produced by screening from 1H electrons should be strongly modified when kBTK is not much larger than Delta: it should be gapped or replaced by Yu-Shiba-Rusinov states. The persistence of a peak at EF with HWHM comparable to Delta, together with the absence of YSR states, could constrain the 1H coupling. The authors should analyse this explicitly. They should include spectra across Tc and Hc2 on the correlated clusters, and a search for YSR states on the 1T surface itself, not only on the 1H termination.

2.5 Internal consistency of the model parameters.

The text states U/W0 about 2, but U = 190 meV and W0 = 80 meV give about 2.4, and the DMFT uses U = 1.67 W. W0 is taken as the FWHM of a DOS peak, which is not the full bandwidth used in the Hubbard model. Please (i) give the DFT width of the isolated band, (ii) state clearly which W enters U/W, and (iii) justify placing the system "near the border of the Mott criterion" against the critical U/W of the single-band triangular-lattice model in the same DMFT scheme. The LHB-UHB separation also equals U only approximately at intermediate coupling, and this should be stated. A simple self-consistency check is available from the data: in a local-self-energy picture the quasiparticle weight Z and width (about Z times W) should scale together. Please compare Fig. 3e and 3f on the same set of clusters at the same temperature.

2.6 DMFT comparison.

The calculation is a paramagnetic single-band Hubbard model for the monolayer 1T narrow band. It has no 1H layer, no disorder and no intersite correlations, and uses maximum-entropy continuation, which cannot resolve millielectronvolt peak widths. The paper itself notes that it "deviates from the tunneling peaks". The comparison therefore supports only the qualitative statement that spectral weight transfers from the coherent peak to Hubbard bands on doping, a generic feature of many models, including impurity models. The language that the evolution is "well captured" and that the data "illustrate the predictive power of the Hubbard model" should be toned down. Alternatively, the authors could add a calculation that includes the 1H bath and the dilution (for example cluster DMFT or an impurity model with a realistic hybridization function) to discriminate the scenarios.

2.7 Origin of the filling variation.

(a) The 13% estimate (one Se per SoD, 26 S per cluster, 0.5% Se) agrees with the 12% zero-bias-peak fraction. That agreement leaves no room for the "series of intermediate states". Please report the fractions of each spectral class and compare their sum with the Se statistics, allowing also for Se in the adjacent 1H layer.
(b) A direct test is available. Se in the top S plane is normally visible in STM topography, so please correlate the positions of top-layer Se with the high-filling clusters.
(c) The claim of short-range order among the local-moment clusters (redistribution by nonlocal Coulomb interaction) is in tension with random Se substitution being the cause. Please quantify the order (pair correlation compared with a random model at the same density) and reconcile the two statements.
(d) Isoelectronic Se substitution "inhibiting charge transfer" needs at least a sentence of mechanism, and the cited calculation should be summarized. For the strain experiment, give the strain magnitude, how it was applied and measured at low T, and why in-plane tensile strain is expected to reduce the interlayer separation by an amount relevant to the charge transfer.

2.8 Surface versus bulk.

The probed 1T layer is the top layer and has a 1H neighbour on one side only. A bulk 1T layer is sandwiched between two 1H layers, so its charge transfer, and hence its filling, differ from the surface layer. The extrapolations to bulk phenomena (pi-shifted Little-Parks oscillations, magnetic memory, chiral superconductivity) should carry this caveat explicitly. The two STMs also used different cleave temperatures (30 K and 90 K). Please state which datasets come from which instrument, and confirm that the cluster statistics and spectral classes agree between the two.

2.9 Superconductivity on the 1T surface.

(a) The anticorrelation between the 1T gap and local filling is interesting. But a simpler reading is that, as the sharp zero-bias peak grows, it dominates the local DOS near EF and fills in or masks a small proximity gap. Please show the gap evolution with fits (for example a Dynes-type form plus the quasiparticle peak) that separate gap size from in-gap spectral weight. Please also show that a gap of 0.35 meV with a V-shaped bottom is resolved given the 0.3 K thermal broadening and the modulation used.
(b) The mechanism given (that interlayer coupling is renormalized together with the quasiparticle hopping) is qualitative. Note also that a suppressed gap at a local moment is the generic expectation for pair breaking by a magnetic impurity. Please discuss how the two pictures can be distinguished.
(c) The link to negative Josephson coupling and to the Little-Parks pi shifts should be clearly labelled as speculation.

3. Minor issues

- Panel references: the text cites the spectral weight of the central peak as Fig. 3g, but the caption gives it as Fig. 3e (3g is DMFT). Check all panel references.
- Fig. 2 caption: "Vmod = 2" lacks a unit. The caption says "one hundred clusters" while the text says "more than one hundred". The setpoint in Fig. 1d (-800 mV) differs from Fig. 2 (-1000 mV). State the measurement temperature for every panel.
- Constant-current setpoint normalization (here -1 V, 2.5 nA) distorts relative LDOS when spectra differ strongly between clusters. Describe precisely how the "normalized dI/dV" was obtained from the high-energy weights, and show that the conclusions are insensitive to the choice.
- Give the fitting model for Fig. 3b in full (background, constraints, number of free parameters) and show residuals for representative fits.
- "Unsurpassed energy resolution": the wide-range spectra were taken with Vmod = 2 meV, and no resolution figure is given. Remove or substantiate.
- Wording: "unambiguously", "seminal", "first full characteristics", "quantitatively describe" should be moderated in line with the issues above.
- The novelty relative to Refs. 28 and 49, which already discuss zero-bias-peak clusters and correlated flat-band electrons in 4Hb-TaS2 and 4Hb-TaSexS2-x, should be stated explicitly. The same applies to the claim that a 1T-surface superconducting gap "has not been reported before".
- Typos and language: "large a Mott gap", "first principal calculation", "This saturation is unique" (situation?), "Insert" (inset), "Colormap maps", "sorted in according to", "the the National". The double hyphens around "characterized by the Coulomb repulsion U" should be proper punctuation.
- References: Ref. 44 volume and page appear swapped (Rev. Mod. Phys. 68, 13). Ref. 38's article number (95138) looks truncated.
- Specify how the ~4000 clusters in Fig. 2a were classified (automated criteria or by eye), and give the classification thresholds.
- Data availability: please deposit the spectra used in Figs. 2 to 4, including the full sorted set, in a public repository rather than providing them on request.

4. Assessment

The experimental observations are of interest to the community working on 4Hb-TaS2 and on correlated vdW heterostructures. These are: a quasi-continuous variation of local filling across SoD clusters, a filling-independent splitting of about 190 meV between the emerging peaks, a narrowing and weakening zero-bias peak, and a cluster-resolved proximity gap on the 1T surface. As written, the paper does not establish its headline conclusions. The filling calibration up to half filling is missing. The Kondo alternative is dismissed with an inappropriate parameter choice and a temperature dependence that does not discriminate. And a dilute set of correlated clusters is compared with homogeneous lattice models without addressing intercluster coherence. With a calibrated filling axis, a robustness analysis of the sorting, a proper treatment of the Kondo scenario (free TK or Gamma, thermal deconvolution, the superconducting-bath argument), neighbour-dependence analysis, and moderated language, the work could become a solid contribution. Recommendation: major revision.
