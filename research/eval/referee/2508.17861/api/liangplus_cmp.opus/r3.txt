Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript uses STM/STS on the 1T termination of lightly Se-doped 4Hb-TaS₂ to follow the Star-of-David (SoD) narrow band cluster by cluster. Its central claims are:
- Clusters span a continuum of fillings, from about 0.2 e per SoD toward half filling.
- Upper and lower Hubbard bands emerge with a constant U ≈ 190 meV.
- The central peak sharpens and vanishes in a Brinkman–Rice-like way, which the authors read as a doped Mott state rather than Kondo screening by the 1H layer.
- A previously unreported proximity-induced gap on the 1T layer is suppressed on the most correlated clusters, leaving paramagnetic puddles.

If the Mott-versus-Kondo distinction and the filling calibration hold, this would be a useful, data-rich contribution. It bears on an active debate (Refs. 18, 28, 34–37) and on the unusual superconducting phenomenology of 4Hb-TaS₂.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The dataset is large and systematic: about 100 sorted spectra over ±1 eV (Fig. 2d). The ±0.25 eV window resolves the LHB, the UHB and a sub-meV-wide central peak on the same clusters.
- Using the −0.5 eV hump as a filling indicator that is independent of the low-energy fits is a sensible idea. It also allows spectra from different clusters to be compared.
- A constant U across the dataset (Fig. 3c) is a clean, falsifiable observation.
- The 1T-layer superconducting gap and its cluster-by-cluster variation (Fig. 4d,e) are new. A linecut that contains gapped and non-gapped clusters measured with the same tip rules out tip artefacts for that contrast.
- The topic is timely: there are competing Kondo-lattice and doped-Mott interpretations of 1T/1H heterostructures, and the unconventional superconductivity of 4Hb-TaS₂ is under active study.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

**1. The observed phenomenology is not unique to a lattice (Brinkman–Rice) Mott transition; a single-impurity Anderson model (SIAM) predicts the same trends.**
- As the level energy ε moves from the mixed-valence regime toward the particle–hole-symmetric point ε = −U/2, the SIAM gives the same features the paper reports:
  - Hubbard-like satellites at ε and ε+U;
  - transfer of weight from the central resonance into these satellites;
  - LHB and UHB weights equalising at the symmetric point (Fig. 3d);
  - a central peak whose width and weight shrink exponentially (Figs. 3e,f).
- Here ε = −U/2 ≈ −95 meV, which is essentially the left end of the data in Figs. 3c–f.
- The doping is also inhomogeneous at the single-cluster scale. Fig. 4d shows the gap and the spectral type changing from one SoD to the next, and about 88% of clusters sit near 0.2 e.
- A near-half-filled cluster surrounded by nearly empty clusters is therefore closer to an Anderson impurity coupled to a bath (the 1H layer plus neighbouring 1T clusters) than to a uniformly doped Hubbard lattice. Brinkman–Rice and lattice DMFT both presuppose uniform filling.
- The claim "unambiguously demonstrate the Mott physics at play" therefore does not yet follow. The authors should:
  - fit the FWHM and spectral weight of the central peak against ε (Fig. 3e,f) with both forms: the exponential SIAM/Kondo dependence (NRG, with the hybridization Γ as a free parameter) and the doped-lattice dependence (Z ∝ δ in Gutzwiller/DMFT, Refs. 45–46);
  - report which form describes the data and with what Γ;
  - analyse the neighbours of near-half-filled clusters, including their filling, their central-peak width and any correlation of these, to establish whether lattice coherence among correlated clusters exists at all;
  - discuss explicitly how a "doped Mott state" is defined when filling varies cluster to cluster.

**2. The exclusion of the Kondo scenario rests on an unjustified choice of hybridization and an incomplete width analysis.**

(a) Choice of hybridization.
- The T_K ≈ 40 K estimate inserts the 1T narrow-band width W₀ = 80 meV into the place of the hybridization width Γ of the Anderson impurity model.
- The relevant quantity for Kondo screening by the 1H electrons is the 1T–1H hybridization. The authors themselves argue this is weak, describing the 1H layer as "primarily a charge reservoir."
- T_K is exponentially sensitive to this parameter. Using the paper's own ε = −76 meV and U = 190 meV:
  - Γ = 80 meV gives T_K ≈ 45–50 K;
  - Γ ≈ 30 meV gives T_K ≈ 1–2 K.
- A Kondo scale of a few kelvin is fully compatible with the observed HWHM of 0.6–1.7 meV.
- The authors need an independent estimate of the 1T–1H hybridization, for example from DFT of a 1T/1H bilayer or from Ref. 37.

(b) Width analysis.
- Fig. 3h shows HWHM values a factor of about 1.5–4 below the grey "Kondo" line. The text says "generally an order of magnitude smaller"; this should be corrected.
- The grey line sits at about 2.5 meV, whereas Γ = k_B T_K for T_K = 40 K would be about 3.4 meV. This should be reconciled.
- Thermal broadening of the tip Fermi function is not deconvolved. Its FWHM is about 3.5 k_B T, roughly 1.3 meV at 4.2 K, so its HWHM contribution grows by about 0.15 meV/K. That is a large fraction of the measured slope of about 0.27 meV/K.
- The lock-in modulation (0.05–0.2 meV) and the effective electron temperature also contribute.
- The linear extrapolation in Fig. 3h has a finite intercept of about 0.5 meV at T = 0. This does not support "an essential mechanism at zero temperature."
- A Kondo resonance with T_K of a few kelvin would also be T-dependent between 0.3 and 4.2 K.
- The authors should:
  - deconvolve the instrumental broadening;
  - fit the full Kondo expression Γ(T) with T_K free;
  - repeat this for several ε values;
  - note that a DMFT quasiparticle peak is also T-dependent near its coherence scale, so linear T dependence by itself does not discriminate.

(c) Field dependence.
- The Zeeman splitting with g ≈ 2 (Supp. Fig. 10) is a hallmark of both a Kondo resonance and a free moment.
- It becomes discriminating only through the threshold field relative to k_B T_K. Splitting at a few tesla would be incompatible with T_K = 40 K, but compatible with a few-kelvin T_K.
- The field values, the splitting versus field, and the comparison with SIAM expectations should be shown in the main text.

**3. The filling scale is not independently calibrated, yet all central conclusions are plotted against it.**
- The 0.2 e figure comes from integrating dI/dV below E_F as a fraction of the narrow-band weight. dI/dV depends on:
  - the setpoint normalisation (V_s = −800 mV or −1 V, which forces the integral to the setpoint to be constant);
  - an energy-dependent tunnelling matrix element;
  - the tip density of states.
- The 1.0 e endpoint in Fig. 2d is assumed, not measured.
- Figs. 3c–f use ε from the fits as the doping coordinate. It is not stated how ε maps onto n, or onto the DMFT fillings n = 0.1–1.0 in Fig. 3g.
- The spectral-weight comparisons (Figs. 2e, 3d, 3e) depend on the normalisation by high-energy weight, which itself shifts with filling.
- The authors should:
  - show how robust these quantities are to setpoint bias and to the normalisation window, including raw spectra;
  - give an explicit n(ε) calibration with uncertainties;
  - use internal consistency checks, for instance whether equal LHB/UHB weight and ε ≈ −U/2 coincide, and whether integrated occupation and ε agree;
  - explain what the clusters with ε ≲ −95 meV represent, since they would lie beyond half filling.

**B. Should address**

**4. The Se origin of the doping is plausible but not directly demonstrated.**
- The 13% estimate counts only the 26 S atoms of a 1T SoD. Se in the adjacent 1H layer, or in the bottom versus top S sheet, could also alter local charge transfer.
- Only the zero-bias-peak (ZBP) clusters (about 12%) are compared with this estimate. If intermediate-filling clusters are added, the fraction of perturbed clusters may exceed 13%. Please give the full filling histogram.
- Supp. Fig. 11 reports short-range order of the moment-bearing clusters. This is in tension with random isovalent substitution, and invoking nonlocal Coulomb redistribution needs support.
- No mechanism is given for how one isovalent Se in 26 S raises filling from 0.2 e to about 1 e, or for the continuum of intermediate fillings.
- The authors should:
  - image Se substituents in the topography (or with bias-dependent contrast) and correlate their positions with cluster filling;
  - show DFT supercell results for Se at inequivalent sites;
  - quantify the higher-Se and strain controls (Supp. Note 4). This includes strain magnitude, how it was applied, the number of clusters counted, and whether the CDW is unchanged.
- The Methods state a nominal Se of 1% of S, but the text states 0.5% substitution. Please reconcile, and state the EDX uncertainty at this level.

**5. The surface 1T layer is not equivalent to the bulk.**
- A surface 1T layer has one 1H neighbour; a bulk 1T layer is sandwiched between two.
- Interlayer charge transfer, and hence the baseline filling, the fraction of correlated clusters and the paramagnetic puddles, may therefore differ in the bulk. Ref. 37 shows a strong dependence on interlayer distance, and surface relaxation could matter here.
- This qualifies the links drawn to bulk phenomena (π-shifts, magnetic memory).
- The authors should discuss this, and ideally compare with DFT for a surface versus a bulk 1T layer.

**6. The 1T-layer superconducting gap and its suppression need firmer quantification.**
- Gap extraction:
  - Δ₁T ≈ 0.35 meV is comparable to the combined resolution of modulation and effective electron temperature. The effective temperature of USM1300-type systems is often about 0.5 K, not 0.3 K.
  - The authors should calibrate the effective resolution on a reference superconductor.
  - They should state how Δ is extracted (Dynes or other fits) and give uncertainties.
- Temperature and field dependence:
  - Fig. 4b shows only 0.3 K, 3.0 K and 2 T, on a ±10 meV axis that hides the gap.
  - A full Δ₁T(T) and Δ₁T(B) series compared with Δ₁H is needed to support "proximity-induced."
- Alternative explanation:
  - On clusters with a strong 1T density of states at E_F, tunnelling may be dominated by non-superconducting 1T states.
  - A ZBP about 1 meV wide could fill in or mask a 0.35 meV gap, giving an apparent closure without any change in pairing.
  - The authors should show whether the coherence-peak energy shrinks continuously with filling (Fig. 4e), or only the gap depth.
  - They should state how filling was assigned to the roughly 30 clusters in Fig. 4e.

**7. The paramagnetic-puddle picture should be reconciled with the absence of Yu-Shiba-Rusinov (YSR) states.**
- If about 12% of 1T clusters host nearly free moments exchange-coupled to superconducting 1H layers, YSR states would be expected:
  - in the 1T spectra of those clusters;
  - in 1H-termination spectra above buried moment-bearing 1T clusters.
- None are reported (Fig. 4a,c).
- This bears directly on the size of the 1T–1H coupling (concern 2) and on the competition between Kondo screening and Δ. It should be discussed quantitatively.

**8. Fitting procedures and uncertainties are under-specified.**
- Fig. 3b uses Lorentzians, while Fig. 3f uses "Fano fitting." A finite Fano asymmetry is itself characteristic of Kondo-type interference, so the q values should be reported and discussed.
- The free and fixed parameters, the background model and the treatment of the strong LHB/central-peak overlap should be specified.
- Figs. 3c–f have no error bars, and the origin of ±10 meV on U is unstated.
- It is unclear whether the 190 meV value depends on the Lorentzian line-shape assumption for asymmetric Hubbard bands.

**9. The theoretical modelling is too schematic to place the system "near the border of the Mott criterion."**
- The model parameters are inconsistent:
  - U/W₀ is quoted as about 2, but 190/80 ≈ 2.4;
  - the DMFT uses U/W = 1.67;
  - W₀ is defined as the half-height width of a local STS peak, which includes lifetime and disorder broadening and is not the lattice bandwidth.
- The DMFT is single-site, paramagnetic and single-band. It omits the 1H bath, which is the central issue for Mott versus Kondo, and nonlocal correlations on the triangular lattice. The simulation temperature is not given.
- Maximum-entropy continuation of CT-HYB data cannot resolve meV-scale quasiparticle widths, so Fig. 3g cannot be compared with Fig. 3f.
- The DFT band structure (Fig. 1e) is for a monolayer. It does not mention spin–orbit coupling, relaxation or the heterostructure, and it does not address charge transfer.
- The authors should:
  - define W consistently;
  - include a 1T/1H hybridization in the impurity or lattice model, as in Ref. 37;
  - use NRG (Supp. Fig. 8) for quantitative width comparisons;
  - state the critical U/W for their lattice so that "near the border" has a meaning.
- They should also note that Brinkman–Rice describes a bandwidth-controlled transition at half filling. The filling-controlled case corresponds to Refs. 45–46.

**10. Reproducibility and statistics.**
- Please state:
  - the number of samples, cleaves, fields of view and tips;
  - whether the roughly 12% ZBP fraction and the U value are reproduced across samples;
  - whether the CreaTec data (above 4 K, 2 meV modulation) and the Unisoku data (sub-kelvin) were combined in Figs. 2–3.
- The 2 meV modulation used in Fig. 2d is comparable to the central-peak width and would broaden it. It should be clear which dataset feeds Figs. 3e,f.

**11. The bulk-superconductivity implications are speculative.**
- The proposed links to the π-shift Little–Parks effect (Ref. 47) and to the magnetic memory effect rely on puddle densities and couplings that are not established here, and are compounded by the surface-versus-bulk issue (concern 5).
- These should be framed clearly as hypotheses.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Quantitative impurity-versus-lattice test.** Fit the central-peak width and weight versus ε (Figs. 3e,f) with an NRG SIAM, with hybridization to the 1H layer as a free parameter, and with doped-lattice DMFT including a 1H bath. Report which description fits, with what Γ, and whether that Γ is consistent with DFT for a 1T/1H bilayer. This single analysis would settle the main claim far more convincingly than the T_K estimate.
2. **Combined temperature and field series at several fillings.** Measure the central peak and the 1T gap at three or more ε values, from 0.3 K to above Tc and from 0 to above Hc2. Deconvolve the calibrated instrumental resolution, and analyse the onset field for Zeeman splitting relative to k_B T_K.
3. **Direct origin of the doping.** Map Se positions (topography or bias-dependent contrast) onto the cluster-filling map over the same 100 nm field of view. Add DFT supercells for Se at inequivalent 1T and 1H sites, and report the full filling histogram and the nearest-neighbour filling correlations.
4. **Robustness of the filling axis and spectral weights.** Show raw and normalised spectra at two or more setpoints. Give an explicit n(ε) calibration with uncertainties, and check it against the particle–hole-symmetric point ε = −U/2.
