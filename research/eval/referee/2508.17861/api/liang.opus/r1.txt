# Review outline: Wu et al., "Unveiling the landscape of Mottness and its proximity to superconductivity in 4Hb-TaS2"

## 1. Significance and novelty

**Topic.** The manuscript uses STM/STS on lightly Se-substituted 4Hb-TaS2 (TaS1.99Se0.01). It reports an ensemble of Star-of-David (SoD) clusters in the 1T layer whose spectra evolve continuously:
- At one end, a depleted, nearly empty narrow band (about 0.2 e/cluster, W0 ≈ 80 meV).
- At the other end, a three-peak spectrum: lower and upper Hubbard bands (LHB/UHB, separated by U ≈ 190 meV) plus a sharp central peak that narrows and loses weight.

**Main claims.**
- The authors interpret this sequence as a filling-controlled Mott transition of the Brinkman–Rice (BR) type.
- On this basis they argue against the widely used Kondo/heavy-fermion picture for 1T/1H heterostructures. The 1H layer is recast as a charge reservoir, siding with the doped-Mott proposal of Crippa et al.
- The second main result is a proximity-induced superconducting gap on the 1T surface (Δ ≈ 0.35 meV), which they say has not been reported before. This gap is said to shrink cluster-by-cluster with increasing correlation and to vanish on clusters hosting a Zeeman-splitting zero-bias peak (ZBP). The authors connect these "paramagnetic puddles" to the π-shift Little–Parks and magnetic-memory phenomena.

**Why it matters.**
- The question is timely and important. Whether the 1T electrons in 4Hb-TaS2 and in 1T/1H bilayers are a Kondo lattice or a doped Mott system is actively debated.
- A real-space, cluster-resolved map of correlation versus pairing would be valuable to both the moiré/vdW correlated-matter and unconventional-superconductivity communities.

**Assessment of novelty.**
- The observation of inhomogeneous ZBP clusters is not itself new (cf. Nayak et al., PNAS 2023; Geng et al., PRB 2024).
- The novelty rests on three elements:
  - the systematic sorting of more than 100 spectra into a "filling axis";
  - the quantitative extraction of (ε, U, widths, weights);
  - the correlation with the local superconducting gap.
- The headline interpretive claim ("unambiguously demonstrate the Mott physics", "lattice BR narrowing") is, in my view, not yet established. The reasons are given in Section 3.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **Rich, high-quality dataset.**
  - Thousands of clusters are surveyed in a single field of view.
  - More than 100 wide-energy spectra are normalized and sorted.
  - Sub-kelvin, low-modulation spectra resolve features at the 0.1 meV scale.
  - Together these provide an unusually complete phenomenological map of how a narrow band acquires correlation features cluster-by-cluster.
- **Quantitative parameter extraction.**
  - The near-constancy of the extracted U (190 ± 10 meV) across the ensemble is a useful benchmark.
  - So is its consistency with AC-stacked bulk 1T-TaS2.
  - The equalization of LHB/UHB weights toward the particle–hole-symmetric point is likewise informative and will be useful for modeling.
- **Plausible, testable doping mechanism.**
  - The ~12% fraction of correlated clusters is consistent with the binomial estimate 1 − (0.995)^26 ≈ 12% for Se occupancy per cluster.
  - The trends with higher Se content and with uniaxial strain (Supplementary Note 4) make the "local suppression of charge transfer" picture credible.
- **New superconducting observation.**
  - The authors observe a superconducting gap on the 1T surface that is suppressed by temperature and field.
  - The gap is cluster-specific and anticorrelated with local correlation strength.
  - This is a new and potentially important input for theories of chirality, π-shifts and magnetic memory in 4Hb-TaS2.
- **Theory–experiment combination.**
  - DMFT (CT-HYB and NRG) is combined with DFT-derived band input.
  - This frames the data within a recognizable theoretical language.

## 3. Potential reasons for rejection

### 3.1 The data do not discriminate a lattice (Brinkman–Rice) Mott transition from single-impurity Anderson physics; the latter is arguably the more natural description of the geometry

**Geometry argument.**
- Only about 12% of clusters are correlated. They are embedded in a matrix of nearly empty (≈0.2 e) clusters and sit atop a metallic 1H layer.
- A BR quasiparticle peak is a lattice-coherence phenomenon. It requires the neighbouring sites to share the same (near-half) filling so that renormalized hopping t·Z sets the coherent bandwidth.
- An isolated, locally gated cluster surrounded by depleted neighbours is, almost by construction, an Anderson impurity coupled to a bath. That bath consists of 1H states and/or the empty 1T band.
- The authors' own picture supports this. Doping is a local potential (Se) that pulls the cluster level below EF, i.e. it tunes ε at fixed U.

**Every highlighted trend is also the textbook evolution of the single-impurity Anderson model (SIAM) as ε is swept from the empty-orbital regime, through mixed valence, to the symmetric point ε = −U/2:**
- A single resonance first sharpens and becomes pinned near EF.
- Charge peaks then develop at ε and ε + U, with constant separation U.
- The LHB/UHB weight asymmetry vanishes at particle–hole symmetry (Fig. 3d).
- The central peak narrows and loses weight because T_K ∝ exp(−π|ε||ε+U|/ΓU) is minimal at the symmetric point (Figs. 3e, f).
- Hence Figs. 3c–f cannot by themselves distinguish "divergent lattice effective mass" from "decreasing T_K of an impurity".
- The single-site DMFT comparison (Fig. 3g) does not resolve this. In DMFT the lattice quasiparticle peak is itself a self-consistent Kondo resonance. The physical distinction lies in the bath (other 1T clusters versus 1H electrons), and that is precisely what is not tested.

**Some of the authors' own observations point toward local-moment/impurity physics:**
- The central-peak FWHM in Fig. 3f is extracted by Fano fitting. A Fano line shape implies interference between tunnelling into a localized level and into a continuum, which is a hallmark of impurity-bath coupling. The paper does not report the q-values or discuss what they imply.
- The ZBP splits linearly into two peaks with g ≈ 2 (Supplementary Fig. 10). Splitting of a zero-bias resonance at ±gμBB is the canonical signature of a field-split Kondo resonance or a localized spin. The authors themselves then call these clusters "paramagnetic impurities", which is in tension with the lattice-BR narrative.
- No test is presented of whether the central-peak width depends on the local environment. For example, does it differ between isolated correlated clusters and those with correlated neighbours (cf. the short-range order in Supplementary Fig. 11)? Such a test is exactly what would separate lattice coherence from impurity physics.

### 3.2 The argument used to rule out the Kondo scenario is not quantitatively sound

**Hybridization scale.**
- The T_K ≈ 40 K estimate inserts W0 (≈80 meV, the intra-1T narrow-band width at 0.2 filling) into the Haldane/Anderson formula.
- In the Kondo-with-1H scenario, the relevant energy is the hybridization width Γ between the cluster orbital and the 1H continuum. Γ is neither measured nor estimated, and it is generically much smaller than W0 for a vdW-coupled layer.
- Because T_K depends exponentially on 1/Γ, a factor of 2–3 smaller Γ easily brings T_K down to the ~1–10 K range. The HWHM-based argument (Fig. 3h, "an order of magnitude smaller than Kondo") then collapses.

**Temperature dependence.**
- The HWHM in Fig. 3h rises from ~0.6 meV (0.3 K) to ~1.7 meV (4.2 K). The slope is ≈0.28 meV/K ≈ 3.3 k_B.
- This is close to the intrinsic thermal broadening of STS: a Fermi–Dirac derivative of FWHM ≈ 3.5 k_BT, convolved with the lock-in modulation of 0.05–0.2 meV.
- It is also close to the α ≈ 5.4 term of the Kondo expression itself.
- Without explicit deconvolution of tip-side thermal and modulation broadening, the linear T dependence may be dominated by instrumental smearing. It therefore cannot establish "an essential mechanism at zero temperature".
- The extrapolated T = 0 intercept (~0.5 meV) corresponds to k_BT_K ≈ 6 K. In that case, saturation would only be expected below ~1 K, which is close to the measured range.
- A BR coherent peak also has a finite coherence temperature (∼ZW) and should broaden and disappear with T. So linear-in-T broadening is not uniquely diagnostic of either scenario.

**What would be needed instead.**
- Measurements up to and beyond the putative T_K (10–40 K) to track peak disappearance.
- Field-dependent splitting thresholds: a Kondo peak splits only for gμBB ≳ k_BT_K, whereas a free spin or narrow band splits immediately.
- Direct comparison of the extracted width with Z·W predicted from DMFT at the inferred filling.

### 3.3 The "filling axis" and its quantitative calibration (0.2 e → 1.0 e) are not adequately justified and are partly circular

**Circularity of the doping axis.**
- Spectra are ordered by the energy of the −0.5 eV hump, and the doping axis in Figs. 3c–f is the LHB position ε.
- ε is simultaneously one of the fitted model parameters. Plotting U(ε), weights(ε) and width(ε) and then interpreting them as functions of filling n assumes a monotonic, model-independent ε(n) mapping.
- That mapping is never established. In a SIAM-like or locally gated system, a rigid electrostatic shift (the hump moves by ~80 meV while ε moves by ~70 meV, cf. Supplementary Fig. 6) is not the same as a known occupancy.

**Calibration of the colour bar.**
- The labels "0.2 e" and "1.0 e" on Fig. 2d are asserted, not measured for each spectrum. The 0.2 e value derives from integrating dI/dV below EF, relative to the total narrow-band weight, for the depleted spectra.
- This procedure is sensitive to several factors:
  - setpoint normalization (a −0.8 to −1 V setpoint biases the integrated weight);
  - energy-dependent tunnelling matrix elements and tip DOS;
  - background from other bands and from the 1H layer;
  - the choice of integration window.
- No uncertainty is quoted. It is not shown that the same procedure yields ≈1.0 e for the three-peak spectra.
- The statement that the integrated weight within ±0.25 eV is constant (Fig. 2e) is partly enforced by the normalization to high-energy weight. It does not by itself demonstrate filling.

**Heterogeneity of the ensemble.**
- The ensemble is heterogeneous in more than filling. Se may sit in the top or bottom S sublayer and at inequivalent positions within the SoD.
- It can therefore modify local W, the 1T–1H coupling Γ, the CDW amplitude and the local potential, not just n.
- The "continuous" sequence may thus be a multi-parameter scatter projected onto one axis.
- Se atoms should be identifiable in topography (by contrast at selected biases). Yet no direct spectrum-versus-Se-site correlation is shown.

**Surface versus bulk.**
- The surface 1T layer has a 1H neighbour on only one side. Its charge transfer, and hence its filling, differs from that of bulk 1T layers.
- The extrapolation to bulk phenomena (π-shifts, magnetic memory) therefore requires caution and is not addressed.

### 3.4 Theoretical modelling and the superconductivity analysis are qualitative and leave obvious alternatives untested

**Inconsistent parameters and absent quantitative comparison.**
- Experimentally U/W0 ≈ 190/80 ≈ 2.4. The text states "~2", while DMFT uses U/W = 1.67.
- For a single-band Hubbard model in DMFT, Uc2 is ≈1.5W. U/W ≈ 2–2.4 would then place half filling in the insulating phase, not "near the border". The claim that the system sits "near the border line of the Mott criterion" therefore needs a precise definition and a consistent parameter set.
- W0 is taken from the FWHM of the depleted-band peak at 0.2 filling, which is not obviously the bare bandwidth of the DFT band in Fig. 1e.
- The UHB–LHB separation equals U only in the large-U limit.
- No quantitative DMFT-versus-experiment comparison is shown (e.g., Z(n), peak width(n), Hubbard-band weights(n)). The authors themselves concede that the model "deviates from the tunnelling peaks".
- The three-Lorentzian decomposition of strongly overlapping features (Fig. 3b) is likely non-unique. No fit-parameter correlations or uncertainties are reported, and Fig. 3f has only ~7 points.

**The superconductivity conclusion conflates several effects.**
- On correlated clusters the ZBP (FWHM ≈ 1.2–3 meV) is wider than the 1T gap (2Δ ≈ 0.7 meV). The apparent "gap collapse" may partly be spectral filling by the overlapping resonance rather than a true absence of pairing. Explicit modelling (gap convolved with a resonance) is needed.
- A local moment is expected to be pair-breaking (Yu–Shiba–Rusinov physics) regardless of BR renormalization. The data do not distinguish "reduced 1T–1H coupling via Z" from "magnetic pair breaking", and no search for in-gap YSR states on correlated clusters is shown.
- If ~12% of the underlying 1T clusters carry free moments, YSR states or gap suppression would be expected on the 1H termination above them. The reported homogeneous, YSR-free 1H gap needs reconciling: it suggests either screened moments or different filling of subsurface 1T layers.

**Overstated links and technical gaps in the superconductivity data.**
- The connections to π-shift Little–Parks and magnetic memory are speculative. The Fischer–Lee–Ruhman mechanism requires specific moment density, orientation and coupling, none of which is quantified here.
- Fig. 4b shows the 1T gap "disappearing" at 3.0 K, which is at, not above, Tc = 3.0 K.
- No Δ_1T(T) curve is shown to demonstrate proximity-type temperature scaling with the 1H gap.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Directly confront the impurity (SIAM) alternative and test lattice coherence.**
   - Perform NRG calculations of the SIAM as a function of ε at fixed U, with Γ treated as a fitting parameter (or estimated from DFT for the 1T–1H coupling). Show which observables (peak width versus ε, weight ratios, Fano q, field splitting) differ quantitatively from lattice DMFT.
   - Experimentally, compare central-peak width and weight for correlated clusters with zero, one or several correlated nearest neighbours. Use the short-range order of Supplementary Fig. 11 to do so.
   - Report the Fano q-factors and discuss them.
   - Determine the onset field of ZBP splitting quantitatively and relate it to k_BT_K.
2. **Make the temperature and width analysis rigorous.**
   - Deconvolve thermal (tip) and lock-in broadening, or use a superconducting tip, before extracting intrinsic HWHM.
   - Extend the temperature range to ≥ 20–40 K to see whether, and at what scale, the central peak disappears.
   - Replace W0 by an independent estimate of the hybridization Γ when computing T_K, and give the uncertainty band arising from the exponential sensitivity.
3. **Establish the filling calibration independently and reduce circularity.**
   - Correlate each spectrum with the identified Se position (top versus bottom sublayer; site within the SoD) via bias-dependent imaging.
   - Perform DFT supercell calculations with Se substitution to obtain the charge per cluster for each configuration.
   - Provide error bars on n from the integration procedure, including setpoint-independence checks (constant-height or multiple setpoints).
   - Show that the same procedure yields n ≈ 1 for three-peak spectra.
   - Plot results versus an observable that is not a fit parameter (e.g., the −0.5 eV hump position), in addition to ε.
   - Use a consistent U/W in theory and experiment and define precisely what "near the Mott border" means.
4. **Strengthen the superconductivity section and calibrate the claims.**
   - Model the 1T low-energy spectra as gap ⊗ resonance to separate gap suppression from spectral filling.
   - Search for YSR states on correlated clusters, ideally with superconducting tips.
   - Map the 1H gap above subsurface correlated clusters.
   - Show Δ_1T(T) and Δ_1T(B) together with Δ_1H.
   - Discuss surface versus bulk charge transfer before linking to π-shifts and magnetic memory, and frame these links as hypotheses.
   - Soften "unambiguously", "unsurpassed energy resolution" and "first full characteristics".
   - Fix editorial issues:
     - "large a Mott gap";
     - "This saturation is unique" (presumably "situation");
     - "first principal" (should be "first-principles");
     - Fig. 3g cited for both experimental weight and DMFT;
     - the Rev. Mod. Phys. citation for Georges et al. (should be vol. 68, p. 13);
     - the Se fraction stated as 1% (Methods) versus 0.5% (main text).
