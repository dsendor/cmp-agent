Review outline:

**Manuscript:** "Unveiling the landscape of Mottness and its proximity to superconductivity in 4Hb-TaS₂" (Wu, Wang, Zhang, Chen et al.)

---

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic and importance.** The manuscript tackles a central open question in 4Hb-TaS₂ and related 1T/1H heterostructures. Are the 1T-layer electrons Kondo-screened local moments coupled to the 1H metal (heavy-fermion picture), or a doped Mott system in which the 1H layers act mainly as a charge reservoir (doped-Mott picture, Crippa et al.)? Settling this matters for interpreting the unusual superconductivity of 4Hb-TaS₂: time-reversal-symmetry-breaking signatures, π-shifted Little–Parks oscillations, and the magnetic memory effect.

- **Key conceptual idea.** Nanoscale variations in interlayer charge transfer, attributed to sparse Se substitution, create an "ensemble" of Star-of-David (SoD) clusters with different local fillings. This could turn one cleaved surface into a filling-controlled Hubbard-model experiment. If the filling calibration holds, the idea is elegant.

- **Claimed advances.**
  - Continuous tracking of the 1T spectral function, from a depleted narrow band (~0.2 e/SoD) toward half filling.
  - Emergence of LHB and UHB with an approximately filling-independent U ≈ 190 meV.
  - Narrowing and vanishing of a central quasiparticle peak, interpreted in Brinkman–Rice terms.
  - A previously unreported, cluster-resolved proximity superconducting gap on the 1T surface (Δ ≤ 0.35 meV) that collapses on correlated, paramagnetic clusters.

- **Novelty relative to prior work.** Novelty is partly incremental and needs sharper positioning against:
  - zero-bias-peak/Hubbard-band clusters in 4Hb-TaS₂ already reported by Nayak et al. (PNAS 2023);
  - flat-band correlation studies in 4Hb-TaSeₓS₂₋ₓ (Geng et al., PRB 2024);
  - "artificial heavy fermion" interpretations in 1T/1H bilayers (Vaňo et al., Nature 2021);
  - doped-Mott phases with charge correlations in 1T-NbSe₂ (Huang et al., PRL 2025).

  The genuinely new elements are the systematic filling-sorted dataset, the T-dependence of the central peak width, and the cluster-resolved 1T superconducting gap. The broad claims ("first full characteristics", "unsurpassed energy resolution", "unambiguously demonstrate") are not yet commensurate with the evidence.

---

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Large, systematic dataset.** More than 100 cluster-centered spectra over a ~4000-cluster field of view are sorted by a high-energy spectral marker. This yields a visually compelling evolution: a depleted narrow band turns into a three-peak structure, followed by suppression of the central peak (Figs. 2d, 3a).

- **Quantitative phenomenology extracted from data.** The analysis yields U, ε, the LHB/UHB weight asymmetry, the central-peak weight and width, and their filling dependence. These are directly comparable with Hubbard-model and DMFT expectations. The LHB > UHB weight asymmetry that decreases with filling (Fig. 3d) is qualitatively what a hole-doped Hubbard system should show.

- **Discriminating experiment between Kondo and Mott scenarios.** The temperature dependence of the central-peak width (0.3–4.2 K, Fig. 3h) and its Zeeman splitting with g ≈ 2 are in principle the right experiments, if analyzed correctly (see below).

- **Cluster-resolved proximity superconductivity.** The cluster-by-cluster superconducting gap on the 1T layer, and its anticorrelation with correlation strength (Fig. 4d, e), is new. It directly links local correlations to the superconducting landscape. It also has potential relevance for π-phase Little–Parks and magnetic-memory phenomena.

- **Supporting controls.** The Se-concentration and uniaxial-strain controls (Supplementary Note 4) give at least qualitative support for a charge-transfer origin of the inhomogeneity.

---

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**Reason 1 – The filling axis, which underpins every central claim, is not quantitatively established.**

- **The 0.2 e/SoD baseline is fragile.** It comes from integrating dI/dV below E_F relative to the total narrow-band weight. dI/dV is not the LDOS:
  - setpoint (normalization) effects at V_s = −800 mV or −1000 mV;
  - bias-dependent tunneling matrix elements and transmission;
  - tip DOS;
  - subtraction of the 1H background and other bands.

  All of these can strongly bias such an integral, and no error bar or sensitivity analysis is given. The Fig. 2d colorbar ("0.2 e → 1.0 e") implies a calibrated filling scale that is never derived. Clusters are only rank-ordered by the energy of the −0.5 eV hump or by ε.

- **The proxy may track local potential rather than filling.**
  - The hump shifts by only ~60–80 meV (≈ −440 to −520 meV). Converting this to Δn ≈ 0.8 requires a rigid-band or compressibility assumption that is incompatible with the strongly correlated physics claimed. In a Mott regime, band positions do not shift rigidly with filling.
  - The shift could equally reflect a local electrostatic potential from a Se substituent or defect (local gating), or tip-induced band bending in a weakly screened layer, without the claimed change in occupancy.
  - Using ε as the filling coordinate (Fig. 3c–f) is partly circular: U, ε and the peak weights are all extracted from the same fits.

- **Internal evidence suggests the system may not approach n = 1.**
  - In the atomic or Hubbard-I limit for n < 1, the expected weight ratio is LHB : UHB ≈ (1 − n/2) : n/2, which becomes 1 : 1 only at half filling.
  - Fig. 3d shows UHB ≈ 20–40% even at the "most filled" clusters, which would imply n well below 1 (≈ 0.5–0.8).
  - The headline "toward a Mott transition at half filling" and the "divergence of the effective mass" interpretation are therefore not demonstrated. The authors should reconcile these two filling estimates.

**Reason 2 – The Kondo-vs-Mott discrimination is not convincing as presented.**

- **The Kondo-temperature estimate is conceptually problematic.**
  - The Haldane/Anderson-model expression for T_K involves the hybridization width Γ between the 1T orbital and the conduction bath (here, 1H electrons). The manuscript instead uses W₀, the intrinsic 1T narrow-band width.
  - Because T_K depends exponentially on |ε||ε+U|/(ΓU), a realistic Γ ≪ W₀ would reduce T_K from ~40 K to the sub-kelvin or few-kelvin range.
  - A peak with HWHM ~0.5–1.7 meV is then not obviously inconsistent with Kondo physics. The "breakdown of the Kondo explanation" thus rests on an unjustified parameter choice.

- **The temperature dependence is equally consistent with a Kondo resonance with T_K below the measurement range.**
  - The HWHM grows linearly from ~0.65 meV (0.3 K) to ~1.7 meV (4.2 K), a slope of ~0.27 meV/K ≈ 3 k_B. This is close to the α k_B T term (α ≈ π–2π) of the very Kondo formula the authors quote, i.e., the regime T ≳ T_K.
  - It is also comparable to the expected tip Fermi–Dirac thermal broadening (~3.5 k_B T FWHM) plus lock-in broadening. The manuscript does not deconvolve these contributions.
  - The finite zero-temperature intercept (~0.5 meV) is not discussed.
  - Zeeman splitting with g ≈ 2 and pair-breaking of superconductivity are expected for Kondo/local-moment scenarios too, so they do not discriminate.

- **The picture of an isolated correlated cluster is ambiguous between scenarios.**
  - Only ~12% of clusters show the three-peak structure, embedded among depleted clusters (n ≈ 0.2). The "doped Mott lattice" assumed in homogeneous DMFT does not apply locally. An isolated half-filled cluster coupled to a metallic bath is effectively an Anderson impurity problem.
  - The real question is which bath screens the moment (neighboring 1T clusters vs. 1H electrons) and what the coherence scale is, not "Mott vs. Kondo".
  - The Brinkman–Rice interpretation, a lattice concept with Z → 0, needs to be justified for this spatially dilute situation, for instance with inhomogeneous or real-space DMFT, or cluster-embedded calculations.

**Reason 3 – Identification of the Hubbard bands and consistency of model parameters need more support.**

- **Parameters are inconsistent across the paper.**
  - U ≈ 190 meV and W₀ ≈ 80 meV give U/W₀ ≈ 2.4.
  - The text quotes "~2", while DMFT uses U/W = 1.67.
  - For a single-band Hubbard model with this bandwidth, the critical U_c in DMFT (~1.2–1.5 W depending on lattice and DOS) is exceeded in all cases. The system is then not "near the border line" but well inside the Mott regime at n = 1.
  - W₀ itself is taken from the half-height width of a peak that includes lifetime, thermal (~2 meV modulation) and possibly hybridization broadening. It should be compared with the DFT narrow-band width of Fig. 1e.

- **Alternative origins of the two "sub-bands" at ±~100 meV are not excluded.**
  - Hybridization gap with the 1H band.
  - Local 1T–1T dimerization or stacking faults near defects, which produce a band-insulator-like gap in bulk 1T-TaS₂.
  - Se-induced local CDW distortion.
  - Inelastic or vibronic features.

  The comparison with AC-stacked bulk 1T-TaS₂ (Supplementary Fig. 7) is itself ambiguous, because that termination's gap is often attributed to dimerization rather than Mottness.

- **The fitting procedure is inconsistent and its constraints are unclear.**
  - Lorentzians are used for the weights, but Fano fits for the central-peak FWHM (Fig. 3f caption).
  - Background treatment is not specified.
  - Normalization uses high-energy features that themselves shift with "filling".
  - The DMFT comparison (Fig. 3g) is only qualitative, with acknowledged deviations. It does not show the predicted narrowing of the quasiparticle peak on the meV scale that the experiment emphasizes.

**Reason 4 – The superconductivity part is suggestive but under-characterized, and its broader implications are overstated.**

- **The abrupt cluster-to-cluster gap variation is unusual for a proximity effect.**
  - The 1T gap is reported as constant within each SoD cluster and changing abruptly between neighbors (~1.2 nm). A proximity-induced gap is set by coupling to an extended 1H condensate whose coherence length is tens of nm.
  - Such abrupt variations require explicit discussion: a local tunneling rate Γ_i, local pair breaking, or quasiparticle-peak renormalization.
  - The proposed mechanism (renormalized 1T–1H coupling) is not tested quantitatively, for example via a McMillan-type model with a cluster-dependent Γ_i and Z_i.

- **Expected magnetic-impurity signatures are absent and unexplained.**
  - If the correlated clusters carry free local moments (g ≈ 2 splitting), Yu–Shiba–Rusinov states are expected in the 1H gap beneath them and in the 1T proximity gap nearby.
  - Their complete absence (Fig. 4a, d) is puzzling and unexplained. It bears directly on the Kondo/Mott question and on the claimed "paramagnetic puddles".
  - The temperature, field and energy-resolution conditions (0.3 K, modulation 0.05–0.2 mV, gap ~0.35 meV) should be shown to resolve such states.

- **Surface relevance and the bulk-superconductivity implications are speculative.**
  - The STM probes a surface 1T layer bounded by a 1H layer on only one side. Its charge transfer, and hence the ~12% fraction, may differ from bulk 1T layers sandwiched between two 1H layers.
  - The links to π-shifted Little–Parks oscillations via negative Josephson coupling, and to the magnetic memory effect, are not supported by any quantitative estimate (e.g., the required density of moment clusters, coupling sign or magnitude).
  - The claimed "short-range order" of moment clusters (Supplementary Fig. 11) also conflicts with the hypothesis that their positions are set by randomly distributed Se, which weakens the doping-origin argument unless reconciled.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Establish an independent, quantitative filling calibration.**
   - Directly identify Se substituents in topography (top and bottom S sublattices; atomic-resolution, bias-dependent imaging) and correlate their positions with cluster spectra.
   - Use the LHB/UHB weight ratio, corrected for the central peak, as an internal filling estimator. Compare it with the integrated-weight method under several setpoints.
   - Perform setpoint-, tip- and normalization-dependence checks, and report error bars on n.
   - Show the hump-shift-to-Δn conversion explicitly, together with an estimate of local electrostatic gating and tip-induced band bending, e.g., via tip-height dependence.
   - Moderate "toward half filling" if n < 1 is indicated.

2. **Strengthen the Kondo-vs-Mott analysis.**
   - Re-derive T_K with an estimated 1T–1H hybridization Γ, e.g., from DFT interlayer hopping, from the LHB width, or from the cluster-resolved proximity gap. Show the T_K range over plausible Γ.
   - Deconvolve thermal and lock-in broadening in Fig. 3h, ideally with a superconducting tip or with mK data. Fit the full Kondo T-dependence, including the α k_B T term, as a competing hypothesis.
   - Examine the field dependence of the splitting below and above the putative T_K, and the spatial extent of the resonance onto neighboring 1H/1T sites.
   - Support the Brinkman–Rice claim for dilute correlated clusters with inhomogeneous, real-space or cluster-embedded DMFT (half-filled clusters embedded in a depleted 1T lattice plus a 1H bath), using parameters consistent with experiment (U/W₀ ≈ 2.4).

3. **Clarify the Hubbard-band identification and harmonize parameters.**
   - Use one consistent U/W value throughout, and compare W₀ with the DFT narrow-band width.
   - Discuss Mott criteria appropriate to the triangular-lattice single-band model.
   - Rule out alternative gap origins: hybridization with 1H; local dimerization/stacking; Se-induced CDW distortion; inelastic features (via d²I/dV² and setpoint symmetry).
   - Present all fits with a consistent functional form, show residuals and background treatment, and provide the full fit tables in the Supplement.
   - Add a direct experiment–theory overlay of peak widths and weights as functions of filling.

4. **Deepen and temper the superconductivity analysis and the overall claims.**
   - Model the cluster-resolved 1T gap with a proximity model using cluster-dependent coupling and quasiparticle renormalization.
   - Provide high-resolution 1H-side spectra beneath or near moment clusters (e.g., at 1T/1H step edges) to search for YSR states, and discuss their absence.
   - Measure gap temperature and field dependence cluster by cluster.
   - Address surface-vs-bulk differences in charge transfer.
   - Present the Little–Parks and magnetic-memory connections as hypotheses with order-of-magnitude estimates.
   - Reconcile the short-range order of moment clusters with the Se-origin hypothesis.
   - Soften overclaims: "unambiguously", "unsurpassed energy resolution" (wide spectra used 2 meV modulation), "first full characteristics".
   - Correct the following errors:
     - Se content: 0.5% in the text vs. 1% nominal in Methods.
     - Notation: "first principal" should read "first-principles".
     - Ref. 44 volume and page are swapped (Rev. Mod. Phys. 68, 13).
     - Fig. 3 panel references: spectral weight is in Fig. 3e, not 3g.
     - "this saturation is unique" should presumably read "situation".
