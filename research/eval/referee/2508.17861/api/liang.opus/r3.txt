# Review outline

## 1. Significance and novelty

- **Scientific question.** The manuscript addresses a central, unresolved question for 1T/1H TaS₂ heterostructures. Is the zero-bias resonance on 1T Star-of-David (SoD) clusters a Kondo resonance, screened by the itinerant 1H electrons? Or is it the remnant coherent quasiparticle peak of a doped Mott system, with the 1H layer acting mainly as a charge reservoir? The second view was proposed theoretically by Crippa et al. (ref. 37).
- **Experimental approach.** The authors exploit natural cluster-to-cluster variation in interlayer charge transfer, attributed to dilute Se substitution, as a "local filling knob." This yields an ensemble of spectra, sorted by an energetic proxy (the −0.5 eV hump and the LHB position ε), that nominally spans about 0.2 to 1 electron per SoD.
- **Advance (1): systematic Hubbard-model phenomenology.** The study offers a systematic extraction of Hubbard-model parameters from a single platform:
  - U ≈ 190 meV, roughly filling-independent;
  - W₀ ≈ 80 meV;
  - LHB/UHB spectral-weight transfer;
  - narrowing and fading of the central peak;
  - a sub-kelvin temperature dependence of the central-peak width.
- **Advance (2): new superconductivity observation.** The authors report a proximity-type superconducting gap on the 1T termination (Δ ≈ 0.35 meV), not reported before. Its local suppression on the correlated, near-half-filled clusters is new and potentially relevant to the unconventional superconductivity reported in 4Hb-TaS₂ (π-shifts, magnetic memory, TRSB).
- **Qualifications on novelty.**
  - Coexisting "depleted" and "Kondo-like" clusters in 4Hb-TaS₂ have been reported (ref. 28, Kumar Nayak et al., PNAS 2023; ref. 49, Geng et al., PRB 2024).
  - Doped-Mott versus heavy-fermion scenarios have been discussed for 1T/1H bilayers (refs. 16, 35–37) and for 1T-NbSe₂ (ref. 48).
  - Filling- or Se-controlled Mottness in 1T-TaS₂-related systems has also been studied by STM/ARPES.
  - The genuinely new elements are therefore:
    - the quantitative, filling-sorted parameter extraction;
    - the T-dependence argument against Kondo;
    - the correlation with the 1T proximity gap.
  - Claims such as "unambiguously demonstrate," "unsurpassed energy resolution," and "first full characteristics" are overstated relative to this context and the evidence presented.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **Large, high-quality dataset.**
  - More than 100 cluster-resolved spectra over ±1 eV, plus sub-kelvin low-bias spectroscopy.
  - The authors correctly anchor the analysis to high-energy features (the −0.5 eV hump) rather than relying only on low-energy line shapes. This is methodologically sound and allows cross-cluster normalization.
- **Coherent phenomenological picture.**
  - A roughly constant U across the ensemble.
  - Approximately symmetric LHB/UHB weights near the endpoint.
  - Systematic narrowing and loss of central-peak weight.
  - Together these are qualitatively consistent with doped-Hubbard expectations and with the authors' DMFT (CT-HYB and NRG) spectra.
- **Superconductivity on the 1T surface.**
  - The gap is identified by its disappearance above T_c and at 2 T.
  - Its cluster-by-cluster variation correlates with local correlation strength.
  - This is a new, experimentally clean observation that links correlation physics to the bulk superconducting phenomenology of 4Hb-TaS₂.
- **Supporting control experiments.**
  - Higher Se content increases the zero-bias-peak (ZBP) fraction.
  - Tensile strain decreases it.
  - Both lend plausibility to the charge-transfer origin of the inhomogeneous filling.
  - The Zeeman splitting (g ≈ 2) demonstrates a local spin degree of freedom on these clusters.
- **Broad interest.** The topic connects Mott physics, vdW heterostructure engineering, and unconventional superconductivity. A well-substantiated version would interest a wide readership.

## 3. Potential reasons for rejection

### Reason 1: The Kondo-versus-doped-Mott discrimination, the paper's central claim, is not established

- **Misuse of the Haldane/Anderson formula.**
  - The T_K estimate (≈40 K) uses the 1T narrow-band width W₀ ≈ 80 meV in place of the impurity–conduction hybridization width Δ (or Γ).
  - In the Kondo scenario, the relevant scale is the hybridization between the SoD orbital and the 1H (and neighbouring 1T) electrons. This is unknown and plausibly much smaller than W₀.
  - Because T_K depends exponentially on this parameter, the estimate is unreliable:
    - With ε = −76 meV and U = 190 meV, W₀ = 80 meV gives k_BT_K ≈ 4 meV.
    - Δ ≈ 20 meV instead gives T_K well below 1 K.
    - Intermediate Δ gives T_K of a few kelvin, which is exactly the range compatible with the observed HWHM (0.6–1.7 meV).
  - The "breakdown of the Kondo explanation" therefore follows from a parameter choice, not from data.
- **Overstated mismatch and incomplete width analysis.**
  - The claimed "order of magnitude smaller" width is not supported by Fig. 3h. The data (≈0.6–1.7 meV) lie only a factor of about 1.5–4 below the grey Kondo line (≈2.5 meV), well within the uncertainty of the T_K estimate.
  - The quasi-linear growth of HWHM with T (slope ≈0.28 meV/K ≈ 3.3 k_B) is what the cited expression itself predicts when T is comparable to T_K, with α ≈ 2π-ish.
  - No deconvolution of tip Fermi–Dirac broadening (~3.5 k_BT FWHM) or lock-in modulation is reported, although these contribute a significant fraction of the measured width at 2–4 K.
  - Without a fit over a wider T range (e.g., 0.3–20 K) and proper deconvolution, neither saturation nor its absence can be claimed.
- **The other signatures are generic and do not discriminate.**
  - A three-peak structure at ε and ε+U with a central resonance is exactly the single-impurity Anderson model spectrum.
  - As ε approaches the particle–hole-symmetric point −U/2, the Anderson Kondo peak also narrows and loses weight (T_K is minimal there).
  - Symmetrization of LHB/UHB weights toward ε ≈ −U/2 is likewise expected in the impurity model.
  - The Fano fitting used for the FWHM (Fig. 3f) implies interference between tunnelling channels, a hallmark of Kondo impurities in STM.
  - Zeeman splitting with g ≈ 2 is the standard Kondo-impurity response. The authors themselves describe these clusters as "paramagnetic local moments," which sits uneasily with a Brinkman–Rice coherent-quasiparticle interpretation.
  - The field scale at which splitting appears, relative to the peak width, is not analysed; it would directly bound T_K.

### Reason 2: Conceptual mismatch between local, cluster-by-cluster filling and lattice Mott/Brinkman–Rice physics

- **The near-half-filled clusters are not a lattice.**
  - Only about 12% of clusters are near half filling, embedded in a matrix of n ≈ 0.2 clusters.
  - A filling-controlled Mott transition and the Brinkman–Rice divergence of m* are lattice phenomena. They require coherent hopping among sites at the same average filling.
  - An isolated (or short-range-correlated, Supp. Fig. 11) half-filled SoD surrounded by depleted clusters is, physically, closer to an impurity hybridizing with its environment.
  - Mapping each cluster's local DOS onto a homogeneous Hubbard model "at filling n" needs explicit justification. Examples would be inhomogeneous or real-space DMFT, or a demonstration that spectra depend only on local filling and not on the neighbourhood.
- **The DMFT comparison is qualitative and internally inconsistent.**
  - The model uses U/W = 1.67, whereas the experimental parameters give U/W₀ ≈ 190/80 ≈ 2.4. The text instead quotes "~2."
  - The non-interacting band is taken from monolayer 1T-TaS₂ DFT, omitting 1T–1H hybridization. That hybridization is precisely the ingredient that decides between the two scenarios.
  - DMFT spectra are shown only on an E/U axis. No quantitative comparison is given of:
    - quasiparticle weight Z(n);
    - peak width;
    - Hubbard-band positions and weights versus ε.
  - The authors acknowledge that the model "deviates from the tunneling peaks." As it stands, the theory cannot adjudicate the central claim.
- **W₀ and the Mott criterion are loosely defined.** W₀ is taken as the FWHM of the depleted-band STS peak. That peak width:
  - is not the bandwidth of the triangular-lattice band (whose DOS is asymmetric, with a van Hove singularity);
  - may itself be broadened by hybridization with 1H.
  
  The statement that the system sits "near the border line of the Mott criterion" thus rests on an ill-defined ratio.

### Reason 3: Filling calibration, data selection, and the doping mechanism are insufficiently quantified

- **The filling axis is a proxy, not a measurement.**
  - The −0.5 eV hump shift (≈80 meV) and ε are used as filling indicators.
  - A local substitution could also shift all bands electrostatically, through a local potential or gating, independently of the narrow-band occupation.
  - Only two numbers, n ≈ 0.2 and n → 1, are assigned. Even these rely on integrating tunnelling DOS below E_F, which is affected by matrix elements, tip DOS, background subtraction, and the constant-current setpoint normalization (V_s = −1 V fixes the integrated LDOS over [−1 V, 0]).
  - Intermediate fillings are never quantified. A sum-rule check (e.g., UHB weight ≈ n/2 per spin) is available from Fig. 3d but is not used.
  - The colour-bar labels "0.2 e → 1.0 e" in Fig. 2d are therefore not substantiated.
- **Selection bias and fitting robustness.**
  - About 100 spectra were selected from about 4000 clusters, but the selection criteria are not specified.
  - No histogram of ε (or filling) across the full FOV is shown.
  - Without this, it is unclear whether a continuous distribution exists or a bimodal one interpolated by a few intermediate cases.
  - The three-Lorentzian decomposition within ±250 meV is under-constrained when the LHB overlaps the central peak. Fit uncertainties, error bars on U, ε, and the weights, and the robustness of the constant-U conclusion to the line-shape model are not reported.
  - Using Lorentzians for weights but Fano fits for widths is inconsistent.
- **The doping mechanism is plausible but not directly demonstrated.**
  - The 13% estimate assumes all 26 S sites per SoD are equivalent. It ignores:
    - top versus bottom (1H-facing) chalcogen layers;
    - central versus peripheral positions;
    - the difference between nominal growth Se (1% of S) and actual composition (0.5%, near the EDX detection limit).
  - It is unexplained why an isovalent substitution would block charge transfer strongly enough to move n from 0.2 to about 1, and how a nearly binary perturbation (Se present or absent) yields a continuum of fillings.
  - No direct real-space correlation between identified Se sites and ZBP clusters is shown.
  - The strain experiment is not quantified (strain magnitude, sign of the c-axis change). Strain also modifies W₀ and the CDW, confounding the interpretation.

### Reason 4: The superconductivity section is suggestive but under-analysed, and its implications are overextended

- **Alternative explanations for gap suppression are not excluded.**
  - Several standard mechanisms would also suppress the 1T proximity gap on near-half-filled clusters:
    - pair breaking by a local moment;
    - Kondo–superconductivity competition (T_K versus Δ);
    - reduced 1T–1H tunnelling because of the altered orbital energy.
  - None of these requires Brinkman–Rice renormalization.
  - The absence of Yu–Shiba–Rusinov states on clusters with local moments needs explanation in either scenario. Neither case is addressed quantitatively.
  - Statements about the "pairing potential" are not justified, since STS measures the LDOS gap.
- **Quantitative analysis is missing.**
  - There is no plot of Δ₁T versus ε or filling with error bars.
  - No fitting model is specified (Dynes, McMillan proximity, anisotropic or nodal), and the sample is only about 30 clusters.
  - The V-shaped 1T gap, versus the U-shaped 1H gap, is noted but not explained; it could indicate nodal structure, broadening, or a proximity-induced energy-dependent gap.
  - The temperature dependence of Δ₁T is not shown beyond a single spectrum at 3 K.
- **Overextension to bulk phenomena.**
  - "Nonsuperconducting puddles" are single SoD clusters (~1 nm), far below the superconducting coherence length. The term "puddle" is misleading for a local LDOS feature.
  - The surface 1T layer has a 1H neighbour on only one side, so its charge transfer and proximity coupling differ from the bulk.
  - The links to π-shifts in Little–Parks experiments and to the magnetic-memory effect are therefore speculative. They should not be presented as ramifications of the data.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Decisive Kondo-versus-Mott tests.**
   - Measure the central-peak width from 0.3 K to at least 15–20 K, with explicit deconvolution of tip thermal and lock-in broadening. Fit both the Kondo form and a Fermi-liquid/BR-type form with T_K (or Z) as a free parameter.
   - Map the onset field and evolution of the Zeeman splitting relative to the zero-field width. Extract g and the critical field, compared with ~0.5 k_BT_K/gμ_B.
   - Estimate the actual 1T–1H hybridization Δ from DFT of a 4Hb supercell and recompute T_K with realistic Δ. Show NRG Anderson-impurity fits to the same spectra.
   - Test the lattice-coherence premise directly. Compare central-peak width and weight for isolated near-half-filled clusters versus those with near-half-filled neighbours (using Supp. Fig. 11). A BR quasiparticle should depend on the local neighbourhood; a Kondo impurity should not, to first order.

2. **Strengthen theory–experiment correspondence.**
   - Use the band from a 4Hb (1T/1H) DFT calculation, including interlayer hybridization.
   - Adopt a U/W consistent with the measured values.
   - Perform inhomogeneous or real-space DMFT, or embed half-filled clusters in a depleted host.
   - Present direct quantitative overlays of Z(n), peak width, and Hubbard-band positions and weights against Fig. 3c–f, on the same energy axis.
   - Clarify how W₀ relates to the model bandwidth W.

3. **Quantify filling and the doping origin, and report data transparently.**
   - Provide a filling calibration independent of the hump position, e.g., spectral-weight sum rules (UHB weight versus n) checked against the hump shift.
   - Show setpoint-dependence tests to exclude normalization and tip-gating artifacts.
   - Include a histogram of ε over the full ~4000-cluster FOV, explicit selection criteria for the ~100 spectra, and fit uncertainties for all extracted parameters.
   - Attempt a direct correlation between Se sites (from bias-dependent topography or Se-rich samples) and ZBP clusters, distinguishing top and bottom chalcogen layers.
   - Quantify the strain experiment (magnitude, c-axis response).

4. **Tighten the superconductivity analysis and calibrate claims.**
   - Plot Δ₁T versus ε with fits (Dynes or McMillan proximity model) and show T-dependent Δ₁T.
   - Search for YSR or in-gap states on intermediate-filling clusters at the highest resolution.
   - Discuss pair breaking, Kondo–superconductivity competition, and reduced tunnelling as alternatives.
   - Address surface-versus-bulk differences, and present the π-shift and magnetic-memory connections explicitly as speculation.
   - Moderate the language throughout ("unambiguously," "unsurpassed," "first full characteristics," "puddles") and situate the work more fully relative to refs. 28, 37, 48, 49 and related STM/ARPES studies of filling-controlled 1T-TaS₂/TaSe₂/NbSe₂.
   - Fix editorial issues:
     - Fig. 3g is cited for both spectral weight and DMFT (the former should be Fig. 3e);
     - Supp. Fig. 9 is cited for both the T-dependence and wide-energy spectra;
     - "V_mod = 2" lacks units;
     - "first principal" should be "first-principles";
     - "large a Mott gap" should be "a large Mott gap";
     - "This saturation is unique" should be "situation";
     - setpoint units are given as meV versus mV.
