Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript uses STM/STS on the 1T termination of 4Hb‑TaS₁.₉₉Se₀.₀₁. Cluster‑to‑cluster variation in interlayer charge transfer serves as an "ensemble" of fillings, which the authors use to follow how a depleted narrow band (~0.2 e/SoD) evolves into a three‑peak spectrum with Hubbard bands and a narrowing central peak. They also report two new observations: a proximity‑type superconducting gap on the 1T layer, and its local suppression on clusters that show Hubbard bands.

If the doped‑Mott (Brinkman–Rice) interpretation holds, this would be a valuable real‑space picture of filling‑controlled Mott physics. It would also bear directly on the debate over 1T/1H heterostructures: heavy‑fermion/Kondo (refs. 16, 34–36) versus doped Mott (ref. 37). At present, however, the key discriminating arguments are not yet conclusive.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The dataset is large and systematic (~4000 clusters imaged; >100 sorted spectra), spanning hundreds of meV down to sub‑meV scales on the same system.
- Sorting by a high‑energy spectral feature (the −0.5 eV hump) is a sensible, model‑light proxy for local filling. Normalization by high‑energy weight makes the low‑energy comparisons more meaningful.
- The extracted U is roughly constant across clusters (Fig. 3c), and the LHB/UHB weight redistribution (Fig. 3d) is qualitatively consistent with Hubbard‑model expectations.
- The superconducting gap on the 1T termination, and its anticorrelation with the correlated features (Fig. 4d,e), is new. It is relevant to the unconventional superconductivity reported in 4Hb‑TaS₂.
- The control experiments (higher Se content; uniaxial strain) support the charge‑transfer origin of the inhomogeneity.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

**1. The Kondo scenario is not ruled out by the arguments given (pp. 5, Fig. 3h).** The central claim is that the zero‑bias peak is a Brinkman–Rice quasiparticle and not a Kondo resonance. The case rests on three arguments, each of which has a problem.

- **(a) The T_K estimate uses the wrong energy scale.** T_K is computed with the 1T narrow‑band width W₀ where the Anderson model requires the hybridization width Γ. In the Kondo scenario, Γ is the coupling of the SoD orbital to the 1H conduction electrons.
  - With W₀ = 80 meV, U = 190 meV and ε ≈ −95 meV, the formula indeed gives ~45 K.
  - Γ for 1T–1H coupling is unknown and could easily be several times smaller, which would put T_K in the few‑K range.
  - The "~40 K" benchmark, and the gray line in Fig. 3h, therefore do not test the Kondo picture.
- **(b) The temperature dependence is not deconvolved and is compatible with a small‑T_K Kondo peak.** The HWHM rises from ~0.6 meV at 0.3 K to ~1.7 meV at 4.2 K.
  - The slope, ~0.28 meV/K ≈ 3.3 k_B, is of the order of the extrinsic broadening from the tip Fermi function (FWHM ≈ 3.5 k_BT) plus intrinsic thermal broadening.
  - The linear trend extrapolates to a finite intercept of ~0.5 meV at T = 0. That is what a Kondo resonance with k_BT_K ~ 0.5 meV (T_K ~ 5–6 K) would show.
  - The statement that the width "does not saturate" and implies "an essential mechanism at zero temperature" is therefore not supported.
  - A Fermi‑liquid quasiparticle with ZW ~ 1 meV would not, by itself, give an intrinsically T‑linear width either.
- **(c) The remaining discriminators do not discriminate.** The three‑peak structure, the peak's connection to the LHB, and Zeeman splitting with g ≈ 2 are all generic to the Anderson/Kondo problem as well.

What the authors should do:
- Fit the HWHM(T) data, after convolving with the tip Fermi–Dirac function and the lock‑in modulation, to the Kondo form with T_K (and α) as free parameters. Report whether a small‑T_K fit is excluded.
- Present the field dependence quantitatively (concern 7).
- Ideally, find an observable that differs between the two pictures, for example:
  - width versus local hybridization: clusters above 1H versus 1H′ stacking, near steps or defects in the underlying 1H layer, or 1T‑on‑1T regions;
  - dependence on whether neighbouring clusters are also near half filling (concern 2).
- In the T_K estimate, replace W₀ with an independently estimated Γ (for example from DFT for the 1T/1H heterostructure). Alternatively, explicitly discuss the range of Γ for which the Kondo picture survives.

**2. The lattice (DMFT/Brinkman–Rice) description is applied to clusters that are, by the paper's own account, dilute and isolated (pp. 3–6).**
- Only ~12% of clusters show the zero‑bias peak, and their filling is attributed to individual Se substitutions. A near‑half‑filled cluster is therefore typically surrounded by clusters at ~0.2 e.
- In that situation the local spectral function is essentially that of an Anderson impurity. It hybridizes with the 1T neighbours and with 1H; it is not the k‑averaged spectral function of a homogeneous doped Hubbard lattice at filling n.
- The Brinkman–Rice divergence of m* is a property of coherent hopping among sites at the same filling. It is unclear what coherent band an isolated half‑filled cluster belongs to.
- Ironically, an impurity hybridizing with the 1T band of width ~W₀ may be the setting in which the authors' T_K formula is meaningful. That would blur the dichotomy the paper draws.

What the authors should do:
- Use the large‑FOV dataset to correlate the central‑peak width and weight with the local environment, such as the number and filling of nearest‑neighbour clusters. Supplementary Fig. 11 suggests short‑range correlation, so this analysis is feasible.
- Replace or supplement the homogeneous single‑band DMFT with a model matching the experimental geometry, for example:
  - an inhomogeneous or real‑space DMFT with a dilute distribution of doped sites; or
  - an impurity embedded in the 1T band plus a 1H bath.
- Clarify in what sense "filling‑controlled Mott transition" applies to an ensemble of spatially separated clusters.

**3. The filling axis is not quantitatively established (pp. 3–5, Figs. 2d, 3c–f).**
- The only quantitative filling is ~0.2 e/SoD, obtained from the fraction of narrow‑band weight below E_F.
- The colour bar in Fig. 2d (0.2 e → 1.0 e) and statements such as "toward half filling" are not supported by an electron count for the individual spectra. Figs. 3c–f use ε as the abscissa.
- The 0.2 e estimate itself assumes dI/dV ∝ LDOS with a constant setpoint normalization (V_s = −800 to −1000 mV). In weakly screened 1T‑TaS₂ the setpoint effect and possible tip‑induced gating of individual clusters are known issues. Both would distort integrated weights and could locally change the filling.
- The proposed Se mechanism is binary (a cluster contains a Se or not). This does not obviously produce the observed continuum of intermediate fillings.
- The counting also looks inconsistent: the 13% estimate would have to cover both the ~12% zero‑bias‑peak clusters and the "series of intermediate states."
- The Methods give 1% nominal Se, while the text gives 0.5% substitution. Se in the 1H layers or in the lower S plane may also contribute, and Se may locally modify W or the CDW, not only the filling.

What the authors should do:
- Provide an n estimate for each spectrum, for example from integrated weight within ±250 meV below versus above E_F, and propagate its uncertainty.
- Show setpoint‑ and tip‑height‑dependent spectra on representative clusters to exclude tip gating and normalization artefacts.
- Directly image Se sites in the top S plane at the same location and correlate them with the cluster spectra. Specify which substitution sites (top or bottom S plane, centre or rim of the SoD, 1H layer) give which filling.
- Reconcile the Se statistics with the observed distribution of fillings.

**B. Should address**

**4. The interaction and bandwidth parameters are inconsistent and loosely defined (pp. 3, 5, 14; Fig. 3g).**
- W₀ ≈ 80 meV is taken as the FWHM of the dI/dV peak at 0.2 e. A DOS FWHM is not the bare bandwidth. At 0.2 e it already includes correlation narrowing and instrumental and lifetime broadening.
- The text then quotes U/W₀ ~ 2, but 190/80 ≈ 2.4. The DMFT uses U/W = 1.67.
- At n = 1 the DMFT spectrum (Fig. 3g) is gapped, so the chosen U is above U_c for that lattice. Whether the system is "near the border of the Mott criterion" therefore depends on these choices.
- What the authors should do:
  - Compare W₀ with the DFT width of the isolated band (Fig. 1e; ideally computed for the 1T/1H heterostructure, not a monolayer).
  - Justify the U/W used in DMFT.
  - Report the DMFT temperature.
  - Quantitatively compare DMFT Z(n) and quasiparticle width versus n with Figs. 3e,f, rather than only qualitatively.
  - Discuss the reliability of maximum‑entropy continuation for meV‑scale features.
- The phrase "deviates from the tunneling peaks due to the solver and approximations" needs specifics.

**5. Fitting procedure and error bars (Figs. 3b–f; Supplementary Fig. 5).**
- U = 190 ± 10 meV is quoted, but the scatter and shaded band in Fig. 3c appear to span roughly 165–215 meV.
- Decomposing the spectrum into three Lorentzians is not unique once the LHB overlaps the central peak, and the background treatment is not described.
- The central‑peak FWHM is obtained by a Fano fit (Fig. 3f caption). A Fano lineshape implies interference between tunnelling channels and is the standard Kondo analysis, so the authors should report q and its evolution with filling.
- What the authors should do:
  - Give fit uncertainties and the constraints used.
  - Show robustness to the choice of lineshape and background.
  - Explain why the Fano and Lorentzian fits of the same peak (Figs. 3b, e, f) are mutually consistent.
- The FWHM data in Fig. 3f stop at ε ≈ −76 meV, while the spectral weight data in Fig. 3e extend to −100 meV. The claimed "vanishing" width near half filling is therefore not directly shown.

**6. The mechanism for suppression of the 1T superconducting gap is not isolated (pp. 5–6, Fig. 4).**
- The anticorrelation of Δ_SC^1T with filling is attributed to Brinkman–Rice renormalization of 1T–1H coupling. Alternatives are not excluded:
  - pair breaking by the local moments the authors themselves invoke;
  - a different local DOS at E_F;
  - a modified 1T–1H coupling at Se sites independent of correlations.
- A sharp peak at E_F coexisting with a superconducting 1H layer at 0.3 K, with no YSR states and no gap, needs explanation. Its HWHM (~0.6 meV) is comparable to Δ_1H ≈ 0.5 meV.
- What the authors should do:
  - Fit Δ_SC^1T with a Dynes or proximity (McMillan) model and report Δ versus n with error bars.
  - Show a full temperature series rather than a single spectrum at 3.0 K ≈ T_c.
  - Track the zero‑bias peak across T_c and H_c2 to see whether it is affected by the onset of superconductivity.
  - Discuss the expected YSR or Kondo–superconductivity competition (k_BT_K versus Δ).

**7. The "paramagnetic puddle" claim needs quantitative field data (p. 6; Supplementary Fig. 10).**
- For g = 2, the splitting is 0.116 meV/T. Resolving it against a 1.3–3.5 meV FWHM therefore requires fields of order 10 T.
- Please give:
  - the field range;
  - splitting versus B with a fit;
  - any threshold field, which is expected for a Kondo resonance at B ~ k_BT_K/gμ_B;
  - whether the split peaks are spin‑asymmetric.
- Zeeman splitting with g ~ 2 is expected for a Kondo resonance, a free moment, or a narrow quasiparticle band alike. It supports "magnetic" but does not support "paramagnetic impurity formed by Mott correlations" specifically.

**8. Energy resolution and measurement conditions (Figs. 2–3; Methods).**
- The temperature, modulation voltage and instrument used for Figs. 2d and 3a–f are not stated.
- Fig. 2 lists V_mod = 2 mV, which is comparable to or larger than the 1.3–3.5 meV central‑peak widths in Fig. 3f.
- What the authors should do:
  - State the conditions for each panel.
  - Show that the low‑energy widths and spectral weights are not resolution‑limited.
  - Moderate the repeated claim of "unsurpassed energy resolution."

**9. Novelty relative to prior STM work on the same and related systems.**
- Ref. 28 (Nayak et al., PNAS 2023) reported zero‑bias‑peak clusters coexisting with depleted‑band clusters in 4Hb‑TaS₂.
- Ref. 49 (Geng et al., PRB 2024) studied correlated flat‑band electrons in 4Hb‑TaSeₓS₂₋ₓ.
- Ref. 48 reported doped‑Mott physics in 1T‑NbSe₂.
- What is new here (the systematic filling series, U extraction, and 1T superconducting gap) should be stated explicitly against these works. Statements such as "first full characteristics" should be calibrated accordingly.

**10. The implications for the π‑shift and magnetic memory are speculative (p. 6).**
- The link to the Little–Parks π‑shift (ref. 47) requires the moment‑bearing clusters to couple to both adjacent 1H and 1H′ layers, at a sufficient density. Please estimate whether ~12% coverage on the 1T layer suffices within the Fischer–Lee–Ruhman mechanism.
- Otherwise, present this as a hypothesis.

**C. Minor**

    - Supplementary Fig. 9 is cited both for the HWHM temperature dependence and for the wide‑energy spectra of Fig. 4e.
    - The Fig. 3c caption says the filling axis is "according to the position of the LHB," but ε is defined as the LHB position, which is circular. Clarify.
12. The Fig. 3h gray line sits at ~2.5 meV. For T_K = 40 K the stated formula gives HWHM = k_BT_K ≈ 3.4 meV at T → 0. Please check.
    - The text says the observed width is "an order of magnitude smaller." The ratio appears to be ~4–5.
13. Fig. 3d: the LHB/UHB difference at the highest filling (~55% versus ~40%) does not "almost disappear." Please temper the text.
14. Units are missing or wrong:
    - "V_mod = 2" (Fig. 2b,c);
    - "V_s = −10 meV / −500 meV" should be mV.
    - Fig. 2 caption says "one hundred clusters"; the text says "more than one hundred."
15. Methods give "Se (1% of the S amount)"; the text gives 0.5% substitution. Distinguish nominal from measured composition.
16. The strain control (Supplementary Note 4) needs the strain magnitude and an estimate of the resulting change in interlayer spacing.
17. Fig. 1e is a monolayer 1T‑TaS₂ calculation with a half‑filled band. A heterostructure calculation showing the charge transfer would better support the "shifted above E_F" statement.
18. Typos and wording: "This saturation is unique" (likely "situation"); "first principal"; "large a Mott gap"; "Insert" → "Inset"; "electrons in" spacing; "shifts to negative binding energies" (clarify direction).
19. References:
    - Ref. 44 volume/page are swapped (Rev. Mod. Phys. 68, 13).
    - Ref. 38 article number appears truncated.
    - Ref. 16 is cited for 1T/1H bilayer Kondo spectra; check that it refers to the correct system.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Build the Kondo‑versus‑doped‑Mott discrimination around observables that genuinely differ between the two pictures: environment dependence, hybridization dependence, a properly deconvolved T‑dependence fit, and the field threshold. Present both fits side by side.
2. Exploit the 4000‑cluster dataset statistically. Correlate the spectra with the positions of imaged Se defects and with nearest‑neighbour filling. This would simultaneously address the origin of the doping and the applicability of a lattice picture.
3. Put the filling axis on a quantitative footing, with n per spectrum and error bars, setpoint and tip‑height checks, and a heterostructure DFT estimate of charge transfer. Then compare DMFT (or an inhomogeneous/impurity calculation) quantitatively with Z(n) and the quasiparticle width.
4. Strengthen the superconductivity section with temperature and field series of both Δ_SC^1T and the zero‑bias peak across T_c and H_c2, and with Dynes/proximity fits. Discuss why no YSR states appear next to the moment‑bearing clusters.
