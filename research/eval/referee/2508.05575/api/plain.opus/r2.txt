# Review of "Negative differential conductance in triangular molecular assemblies" (Li, Pokorný, Hapala et al., arXiv:2508.05575)

## Summary

The authors use STM lateral manipulation to build a C₃-symmetric trimer of TBTAP radicals on Pb(111). In STS they observe tip-gated discharging rings that expand with bias, overlap into "flower-like" patterns, and develop a chiral central region of negative dI/dV between about 0.7 and 0.9 V.

They model the system as a three-impurity Anderson model with inter-site capacitive coupling (W ≈ 50 meV), no direct hopping, and an electrostatic tip model, solved with a Pauli master equation (PME). Their interpretation has three parts:

- Many dI/dV peaks reflect intra-cluster charge *rearrangement* (via the substrate) at fixed total charge, not changes in total charge.
- The NDC arises from a non-equilibrium mixture of singly occupied states. In this mixture the empty site is often far from the tip, which reduces tunneling.
- Orbital nodal structure produces the chiral pattern.

They also present an NRG analysis of the subgap YSR states, and data on a hexamer whose inner molecules are neutral and show charging rings with NDC at negative bias.

## Overall assessment

The experiments are of high quality. The trimer is a well-chosen, tractable system, and the experiment–simulation correspondence across voltages (Figs. 2, S7, S8) is visually impressive. The insight that sharp dI/dV features can be intra-cluster charge rearrangements at fixed total charge is valuable and likely applies to other Coulomb-ring studies.

However, the central mechanistic claim rests on a transport regime the authors themselves state is inconsistent with the system. Several parameter identifications are internally inconsistent. The hexamer section and the device/application framing overreach. I consider the work publishable after substantial revision.

## Strengths

- **Atomically precise, reproducible construction.** The trimer is stable and the hexamer can be moved as a unit (Fig. S13).
- **Rich, high-quality data.** The bias-dependent dI/dV and current maps are reported alongside each other, and the current traces (Fig. 1D) show that the NDC is genuine and not a lock-in artifact.
- **Fully enumerable model.** The many-body decomposition of the current (Fig. 3A–B) is pedagogically clear.
- **Fixed-charge rearrangement.** The demonstration that ring crossings need not change the total charge is an important, generalizable point.
- **Code availability**, with cross-validation against QmeQ.

## Major concerns

### 1. The NDC mechanism is derived in a regime the system is not in

The PME uses Γ_s ≈ 0.01 meV ≪ k_BT = 0.22 meV, while the authors' own YSR fit gives Γ_s = 10–20 meV, about 100× k_BT and comparable to W. The justification offered is that ring *size* is set by electrostatics. That may be true for ring positions, but the NDC is attributed specifically to a **non-equilibrium population** of the |100⟩, |010⟩, |001⟩ states. Those states are long-lived only because no first-order process connects them.

At Γ_s ≈ 20 meV, two processes would act much faster than the tip:

- substrate-mediated inelastic cotunneling (rates of order Γ_s²/ΔE), and
- the substrate-induced effective inter-site exchange of charge (ζΓ_s with ζ = 1 in the NRG).

Both would relax these configurations far faster than tip events, at roughly 10⁹ s⁻¹ for ~100 pA. This would likely destroy the population mixing that produces the NDC.

The ratio Γ_t/Γ_s also differs enormously between simulation (≲1) and experiment (~10⁻⁴ given the measured currents). The authors should:

- (a) show how the simulated NDC depends on Γ_t/Γ_s over several orders of magnitude;
- (b) include at least the leading cotunneling relaxation, or use a method valid for Γ ≳ k_BT (e.g., real-time diagrammatics or a second-order master equation);
- (c) reconcile Γ_s = 10–20 meV with the observed sharpness of the discharge peaks. With the model's lever arm, a 20 meV level broadening should translate into a voltage width that can be checked directly against the roughly 10–20 mV peak widths in Fig. 1C. If the peaks are too sharp, the effective Γ_s in the high-bias regime is far smaller than the YSR-derived value, and that should be discussed.

As it stands, the NDC explanation is plausible but not established.

### 2. Inconsistent identification of the bare level ε_d

ε_d = −90 meV is taken from the peak at −90 mV in Fig. S2. Within the authors' own model, however, the zero-bias ground state is doubly occupied, so the removal energy is E(1e) − E(2e) = −ε_d − W. Removal should therefore appear near −40 meV (plus a small tip-induced shift), not at ε_d.

The parameters also drift between methods:

- the PME uses ε_d = −90 meV;
- the NRG YSR fits use ε = −104 or −106 meV, close to the 2↔3 transition at −111.5 meV;
- the main text states "−90 meV" throughout.

Please clarify what the −90 mV peak actually corresponds to. Please also show that the charge assignments (n = 2 → 1) and the NDC survive across the range of ε_d consistent with all the data.

### 3. Spin degeneracy is dropped

The statement that U → ∞ reduces the space "from 4³ = 64 to 2³ = 8 states" describes a *spinless* model. A spinful model with U → ∞ has 3³ = 27 states. Spin degeneracy changes the relative in/out rates (the well-known factor-of-2 asymmetry), and hence the steady-state populations on which the NDC argument relies.

Please state whether the PME is spinless. If it is, please demonstrate that the NDC region and its magnitude are unchanged in the spinful case. Fig. S6 (QmeQ, U = 200 meV) could serve this purpose if it was spinful, but the text does not say so.

### 4. Alternative NDC mechanism not ruled out for the trimer

For the hexamer, the authors invoke the TCNQ-type mechanism of Ref. 18: a localized molecular charge raises the barrier for the dominant non-resonant tip–substrate current. Fig. 1D shows this background current is of the same order as the total current. In the trimer's "mix" region the time-averaged charge distribution under the tip also changes, so the same electrostatic barrier modulation could contribute to the NDC.

The PME accounts only for resonant current through the molecular levels and reports dI/dV in arbitrary units. Please:

- estimate the background-modulation contribution for the trimer, and
- compare simulated and measured NDC *magnitudes* (e.g., peak-to-valley ratios), not just spatial patterns.

### 5. The hexamer section does not support the stated conclusions

The conclusions claim that the hexamer "further confirms the robustness of these effects." The hexamer NDC, however, is attributed to a different mechanism (barrier modulation upon *charging*, at negative bias) and is not modeled. It therefore does not confirm the non-equilibrium-occupancy mechanism. Either model the hexamer or reword these claims.

The proposed reason the outer-molecule discharging rings are absent (discharge voltage possibly > 1.2 V) is speculative and should be supported, for example with an electrostatic estimate from the fitted model.

### 6. Fitted parameters, uniqueness, and "almost perfect match"

The electrostatic model has many free parameters:

- r_tip, z_tip, z_C, z_S, Q_xx, β, W and ε_d.

The mirror plane z_S = −0.09 nm differs substantially from the physical ~0.3 nm. Fig. S5 also requires rescaling the molecules along one axis, attributed to piezo calibration. Please:

- provide a quantitative goodness-of-fit metric;
- discuss parameter correlations, especially whether W is uniquely determined or trades off against z_S and ε_d;
- state whether the maps were recorded at constant height or constant current. With constant current the tip–sample distance varies across the map, which the fixed z_tip = 0.6 nm model does not capture.

Setpoints and lock-in modulation amplitudes are missing from most captions, even though the Methods promise them.

### 7. Chirality and reproducibility

The chirality is attributed to incommensurability with Pb(111). However, a planar trimer held together by directional H- and halogen bonds in a pinwheel arrangement is 2D-chiral irrespective of the substrate.

The decisive test is the opposite enantiomer, which should show a mirrored NDC/YSR pattern. Please report:

- how many trimers were measured,
- whether both handednesses were observed, and
- whether the results hold across different tips.

### 8. Superconductivity independence is asserted, not tested

Given the energy scales (≈0.7 V ≫ Δ), the claim that the NDC is independent of superconductivity is very plausible. It is nonetheless stated as a demonstrated result. A measurement in a small magnetic field above H_c of Pb (~80 mT) would settle it cheaply.

### 9. Framing and novelty claims

- **"Device" and "molecular Gunn diode."** These are misleading. The NDC exists only for specific tip positions and requires the STM tip as both gate and electrode. The Gunn effect is a bulk transferred-electron instability unrelated to this mechanism.
- **"First observation of NDC in a molecular system engineered using lateral manipulation."** This needs a careful literature check, or softening.
- **Operating conditions.** The claims about programmable nanoelectronics should acknowledge the energy scale (W ≈ 2k_BT at room temperature), the small peak-to-valley ratio, and the absence of a non-tip-based contact scheme.

## Minor and technical points

- **YSR model.**
  - The NRG uses ζ = 1, meaning fully correlated substrate hybridization, which generates substrate-mediated coherence. This is inconsistent with the coherence-free PME and should be discussed. Ref. 12 shows dark-state physics can arise in exactly this situation.
  - The doublet ground state at ⟨N⟩ ≈ 2 is non-intuitive and deserves a sentence of explanation (presumably partial YSR screening).
  - The proposed tip-induced Γ₁ asymmetry predicts that the YSR splitting should vary with tip height and position; this is testable.
- **DFT.**
  - B3LYP with def2-SV(P) on a three-layer Pb cluster in a molecular code is unusual for a metal surface.
  - Charging the system by 3e contradicts the model's −2e ground state.
  - Please justify these choices, or note the limitations for the SOMO shapes used in the tunneling matrix elements.
- **Eq. (S7).** As written, with f(x) = (eˣ + 1)⁻¹ and x_{a,b} = (E_a − E_b − μ)/k_BT, both Fermi-factor signs appear inverted relative to the standard electron-addition and removal rates. Please check the convention. Notation is also inconsistent: V_αi vs. t_αi, and "P_α" where P_a or P_b is meant.
- **Image charges.** "Mirrored across the substrate at z = −2z_S": an image across the plane z = z_S lies at z = 2z_S, not −2z_S. Please check the sign.
- **YSR map bias.** The text says ±1.8 mV, while the Fig. S10 caption says 1.9 mV and the panel label reads "1.9 V".

## Typos and presentation

- Main text: "Vs = 790 meV" should be mV; "an molecular system"; "a flower-like patterns."
- Hexamer section: "intersect at Vs = 0.45 V" is missing a minus sign.
- Fig. 2 caption uses "NDR" while the rest of the paper uses "NDC"; please unify.
- Fig. 3B axis: "Current cmponent."
- Fig. 4 caption: "with decreasing negative voltage" is ambiguous; say "increasingly negative."
- Fig. S5 caption: double period.

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The experimental observations are solid and interesting, and the fixed-charge rearrangement insight is valuable. To support the central claim that the NDC "emerges purely from electron correlations" through non-equilibrium occupancy, the authors need to:

- demonstrate robustness in the experimentally relevant coupling regime (or reconcile the Γ_s discrepancy),
- fix the ε_d inconsistency,
- address spin degeneracy, and
- exclude the electrostatic barrier-modulation alternative.

The hexamer conclusions and the device framing should also be toned down.
