Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Scope.** The manuscript reports dc SQUIDs built from a twisted Bi₂Sr₂CaCu₂O₈₊δ (BSCCO) c-axis interface. Devices with twist angles of 0°, 40°, 42°, 44° and 45° are made by cryogenic exfoliation and stacking. The loop is formed afterwards by cutting the overlap region with a tapered fibre scalpel.
- **Central claim.** Wollman-type extrapolation of dV/dI minima to zero bias gives an anomalous inter-arm phase φ₁₂ that switches between ≈0 and ≈π as a small in-plane field (±10–20 µT) is swept. The authors interpret this as the two arms realizing interfacial d+id and d−id orders of opposite chirality, as predicted by Can et al. for ~45° twisted cuprates.
- **Supporting claims.**
  - Time-reversal symmetry breaking at B = 0, from a finite η′ ≈ 4% (45°) and ≈ 8% (40°).
  - Second-harmonic content in the switching-current modulation, attributed to Cooper-pair co-tunnelling (4e).
  - An "unusual" temperature dependence of the interfacial Josephson inductance and superfluid stiffness.
  - A modulation period ~7× smaller than expected from the geometric loop area.
  - Flux-noise sensitivity of ~1.5 µΦ₀/√Hz at 60 K, said to be state of the art.
- **Why it matters.**
  - Single-junction experiments (Zhao et al. Science 2023; Ghosh et al. Nat. Mater. 2024) cannot access relative phases.
  - An interferometric, phase-sensitive probe of TRSB interfacial order would therefore be a genuine step beyond the existing transport evidence.
  - Such evidence is contested; Yuan et al. propose inhomogeneity-induced TRSB as an alternative.
  - Phase-sensitive SQUID experiments historically settled the d-wave debate, so the conceptual motivation is strong and timely.
- **Technical novelty.**
  - Fibre-scalpel cutting of a pre-assembled twisted stack to make a high-Tc twisted SQUID is new and simple.
  - It allows deliberate twist-angle control, unlike grain-boundary YBCO SQUIDs.
- **Overstatements.**
  - "First-of-its-kind c-axis SQUID" needs qualification against prior twisted-vdW SQUIDs (Farrar et al., Nano Lett. 2021) and earlier intrinsic-junction BSCCO interferometers.
  - "Close to 77 K" operation is inconsistent with a junction Tc of ~75 K.
- **Overall.** The novelty hinges almost entirely on whether φ₁₂ ≈ π can be robustly attributed to opposite chirality rather than to flux-offset or vortex artefacts. In its present form that is not yet established.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **High-interest problem.** The paper offers a phase-sensitive interferometric test of chiral (d ± id) interfacial superconductivity in twisted cuprates, a question at the frontier of both twistronics and unconventional superconductivity.
- **Fabrication advance.**
  - Cryogenic stacking combined with post-hoc fibre-scalpel loop definition is a simple, potentially general route to SQUIDs from any vdW superconducting interface.
  - SI 2 indicates the cut does not damage or fold the stack.
- **Clear SQUID signatures.**
  - Interference is visible in switching current, fixed-bias voltage and dV/dI.
  - It is seen across several twist angles and devices.
  - Junction parameters (Jc ≈ 0.013 kA/cm² at 60 K, Tc ≈ 75 K) are consistent with prior twisted-BSCCO reports.
- **Multiple complementary observables in one platform.** The authors measure:
  - the anomalous phase,
  - zero-field η′,
  - harmonic content and its temperature and angle dependence (SI 12),
  - Josephson inductance.
- **Controls.** A nominally 0° device without a zero-field diode effect is a useful control. Data from an additional device (SI 14) points toward reproducibility.
- **Practical value.** A high-Tc SQUID with µΦ₀/√Hz-level flux noise at tens of kelvin is of applied interest, and twist-angle-engineered junctions may connect to proposed cuprate qubits (d-mon, twisted-cuprate qubits).

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**Reason 1 — The anomalous-phase (φ₁₂ ≈ π) measurement is not protected against trivial flux offsets, so the central claim is not established.**

- **Flux zero is not independently known.**
  - The Wollman extrapolation yields φ₁₂ only relative to the true zero of applied flux.
  - The period is 3.3 µT for the 45° device, so a residual perpendicular field of only ~1.6 µT shifts the intercept by exactly half a period.
  - Plausible sources of such a residual field include:
    - imperfect cryoperm shielding of the ~50 µT Earth field;
    - remanence of the copper-coil system or nearby magnetic parts;
    - trapped Abrikosov/pancake vortices in the large-area flakes;
    - the 7× flux-focusing enhancement, which amplifies any residual field.
  - The manuscript gives no calibration of absolute zero field, no field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled comparison, and no residual-field measurement.
- **The in-plane sweep is itself a likely source of offset.**
  - The φ₁₂ switching is induced by in-plane fields of only 10–20 µT, i.e. several perpendicular periods.
  - A misalignment of a few degrees between the "in-plane" coil axis and the flake plane gives a perpendicular component of ~0.5–1 µT, a sizeable fraction of a period.
  - This adds directly to Φ_ext in Eq. (2) and shifts the intercept.
  - An in-plane field-dependent intercept is therefore the generic expectation even with no chiral physics.
  - The authors must show the misalignment-induced offset is negligible, and explain why the intercept clusters near 0 and π rather than drifting continuously with B_par.
- **Hysteresis points to vortex rearrangement rather than chirality selection.**
  - Extended Data Fig. 2 shows the forward and reverse sweeps giving different φ₁₂ at the same B_par (−10, 0 and 10 µT).
  - This history dependence is equally consistent with vortex trapping or depinning in the flakes or electrodes.
  - A vortex pinned near one arm, or a Josephson vortex in one AJJ, imprints an arbitrary phase offset on that arm.
  - The suggested mechanism (in-plane-field-induced Josephson/Abrikosov vortex coupling at µT fields) is admitted to be "not well understood" and is not quantified.
- **Statistics are thin.**
  - There are 5 field values × 2 sweeps for one device at 20 K, plus one additional device in SI.
  - The "half-integer" points range roughly from 0.7π to 0.95π, and the "integer" points from 0.1π to 0.2π.
  - No error bars are given on the intercepts, though they come from a linear extrapolation over a large lever arm: data at I_bias = 22–59 µA are extrapolated to I = 0.
  - Assigning which minimum belongs to which order ("modulo period") is a further source of ambiguity.
  - Given these error bars, a binary 0/π classification needs to be justified statistically, e.g. with a histogram over many thermal or field cycles.
- **Missing decisive control.**
  - φ₁₂ is not reported for the 0° SQUID, where no chiral order is expected, under the same in-plane sweep protocol.
  - If the 0° device also shows 0/π switching, the chirality interpretation fails.
  - The 0° control currently exists only for the diode effect.

**Reason 2 — The theoretical mapping from "opposite chirality" to a π-shifted SQUID response is not internally consistent with the measured harmonic content.**

- **Chirality is not a fixed phase offset.**
  - In the Can et al. framework the junction phase α is the gauge-invariant phase across the junction, the very quantity constrained by fluxoid quantization.
  - A TRSB junction with CPR I₁ sin φ + I₂ sin 2φ (I₂ < 0) has degenerate minima at φ = ±φ₀, with φ₀ = arccos(I₁ / 2|I₂|).
  - This is not equivalent to a junction with a fixed intrinsic offset sin(φ + φ₀), which is the situation Wollman's extrapolation was designed for.
  - "Opposite chirality" in the two arms therefore corresponds to the SQUID sitting on different metastable branches of a multivalued Is(Φ). It is not obvious that this gives a uniform half-period shift of the dV/dI minima at all bias currents.
- **The required harmonic content is not what is measured.**
  - The relative phase between the two chiral states is 2φ₀, which equals π only in the pure-sin 2φ limit (I₁ → 0).
  - In that limit, however, the SQUID periodicity is Φ₀/2, and a π shift equals one full period — indistinguishable from zero.
  - The data instead show predominantly h/2e periodicity, with A₂/A₁ ≈ 0.2–0.37 in switching-current FFTs.
  - Such a ratio, if it reflected the CPR, is below the |I₂| > I₁/2 threshold needed for TRSB minima at all.
  - The manuscript needs to reconcile how φ₁₂ ≈ π (rather than some intermediate 2φ₀) arises with a first-harmonic-dominated CPR.
- **The theoretical support is not shown.**
  - The SI 10 free-energy analysis is invoked as "consistent", but the main text shows no simulated dV/dI(Φ, I_bias) maps or extracted intercepts for the opposite-chirality configuration.
  - It is also not shown how such a simulation compares with alternative states that also arise in a β ≈ 1 SQUID with a second harmonic.
  - Examples of such states include different fluxoid winding states and branch hopping near switching.

**Reason 3 — The TRSB (η′ ≠ 0 at B = 0) and second-harmonic/co-tunnelling claims have unaddressed mundane explanations in an inductive, asymmetric SQUID.**

- **η′(0) is equally sensitive to a field offset.**
  - Onsager symmetry Is⁺(B) = |Is⁻(−B)| holds only about the true zero of total flux.
  - With steep modulation (period 2.2–3.3 µT; slopes visible in Fig. 2d inset), an offset of ~0.1 µT can produce several percent η′.
  - The same residual-field or trapped-flux issue as in Reason 1 therefore undermines the TRSB evidence.
  - The authors should test whether the asymmetry is removed by a shift B → B − B₀, i.e. whether Is⁺(B) = |Is⁻(2B₀ − B)| for some B₀.
  - Other needed checks:
    - sweep-direction dependence;
    - heating or retrapping effects in the counter-based protocol, where I⁺ and I⁻ are measured alternately;
    - reproducibility after thermal cycling above Tc.
- **The slow envelope is unexplained and may itself break symmetry.**
  - Fig. 4a shows large envelope differences between Is⁺(+B) and |Is⁻(−B)| on a ~100 µT scale.
  - In a c-axis junction the perpendicular field is parallel to the current and does not produce a Fraunhofer pattern of the AJJ.
  - The origin of the "outer envelope" attributed to the large arm is therefore unclear. Meissner currents or pancake-vortex entry in the flakes generate inhomogeneous phase gradients that can break the ±B symmetry without intrinsic TRSB.
- **Harmonics in Is(Φ) do not imply harmonics in the CPR.**
  - With β ≈ 1 and 3:1 asymmetry, Is(Φ) is strongly skewed, sawtooth-like, even for a purely sinusoidal CPR.
  - Its FFT therefore contains a substantial 2Φ₀⁻¹ component.
  - The A₂/A₁ ratio and its non-monotonic temperature dependence (only three points: 60, 66, 74 K) thus mostly track β(T) = 2πLIc/Φ₀, which changes strongly near Tc.
  - These data cannot be taken as evidence for 4e co-tunnelling without a quantitative fit of the full Is(Φ) using Lgeo from SI 8.
  - Such a fit would need to compare the pure-sin CPR model with a sin + sin 2φ model and report fitted I₂/I₁ with uncertainties.
- **The angle trend and inhomogeneity alternative are not addressed.**
  - A clearly dominant first harmonic in a nominal 45° device itself requires explanation: twist error (±0.5° quoted), local strain or misorientation from the cut, or the interfacial inhomogeneity of Yuan et al.
  - The inhomogeneity scenario is also an alternative explanation for TRSB, which the paper claims to rule out but does not test.

**Reason 4 — Device geometry, inductance analysis and sensor benchmarks contain inconsistencies and over-interpretation.**

- **The loop geometry is ill-defined.**
  - In a c-axis SQUID the superconducting loop runs top flake → JJ1 → bottom flake → JJ2.
  - It is not clear from Fig. 1b/c and the Fig. 2a inset how this loop encloses an in-plane area of 81–131 µm² threaded by a perpendicular field.
  - Nor is it clear what loop area the in-plane field threads: the vertical cross-section of ~(flake thickness) × (arm separation).
  - The ~7× period reduction is called "surprising and unusual". Yet washer-type screening, with A_eff ≈ hole size × outer dimension, routinely gives such enhancements for flakes extending tens of µm.
  - It should be quantified with the actual flake outline rather than offered qualitatively. The effective area also matters for the field-noise figure.
- **The inductance analysis is loose.**
  - L_J,1 = (Φ₀/2π)·φ₁/Is₁ is not the differential Josephson inductance.
  - The slope relation δIs₁/δΦ = 1/(L_J,1 + Lgeo,1) holds only on specific branches, with sinusoidal-CPR assumptions.
  - Lgeo values and their uncertainties from the AWR simulation are not stated in the main text. Since L_J is obtained by subtraction and β ≈ 1, the error may dominate.
  - The comparison of the "superfluid stiffness" 1/L_J (an interlayer Josephson quantity ∝ Ic(T)) with bulk in-plane λ_ab⁻²(T) ∝ T² behaviour (Osborn thesis) is not like-for-like. It does not by itself indicate an "unusual interfacial superconductor".
  - A comparison of Ic(T) with Ambegaokar–Baratoff or d-wave tunnelling forms would be more meaningful.
  - The green trace is non-monotonic, with large error bars at low T.
- **The noise benchmark is internally inconsistent.**
  - The text quotes 1.5 µΦ₀/√Hz at 60 K, but the Extended Data table lists 1.41 µΦ₀/√Hz at 77 K. The abstract claims "close to 77 K".
  - The junction Tc is ~75 K (Fig. 1d), so 77 K operation appears impossible.
  - The spectrum in Fig. 5b remains 1/f-like up to 10 kHz with no white-noise floor, and the frequency at which 1.5 µΦ₀/√Hz is quoted is unspecified.
  - Also missing:
    - the transfer coefficient V_Φ;
    - the preamplifier noise floor;
    - the bias stability.
  - Comparing a bare 81 µm² loop (34 pT/√Hz) with fT/√Hz pickup-coil magnetometers in the same table is not a fair "state-of-the-art" comparison. Flux noise is reasonably competitive with high-Tc devices, but the claim should be toned down.
- **The dV/dI measurement regime is unclear.**
  - dV/dI is said to be measured "below the switching current", yet Vac shows large peaks at I_bias values comparable to Is.
  - The data are taken at 20 K, whereas all other characterisation is at 60–74 K.
  - The phase-diffusion or retrapping regime affects the location of minima and hence the extrapolation.
  - It is also unclear why Is at 20 K is not much larger, given L_J(T).

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Establish the absolute flux zero and rule out offset or vortex artefacts for both φ₁₂ and η′.**
   - Measure and report the residual field inside the shield.
   - Perform field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled runs, and repeated thermal cycling above Tc with statistics on the φ₁₂ intercept.
   - Rotate or invert the in-plane coil to calibrate its misalignment-induced perpendicular component.
   - Re-analyse η′ with a fitted offset B₀ to check whether Onsager symmetry is restored about a shifted zero.
   - Provide histograms of φ₁₂ over many cycles, with intercept error bars, showing clustering at 0 and π rather than a continuum.
2. **Add phase-sensitive controls and an explicit model of the SQUID response.**
   - Repeat the in-plane-field φ₁₂ protocol on the 0° SQUID and on additional 40–45° devices. Ideally include a reference SQUID with one twisted and one untwisted arm, which would give a much cleaner phase reference.
   - Present simulated dV/dI(Φ, I_bias) maps and Wollman intercepts from a full RCSJ model with Lgeo, the 3:1 asymmetry, and I₁ sin φ + I₂ sin 2φ CPRs in same- and opposite-chirality states.
   - Show explicitly for which I₂/I₁ the intercept equals π, versus 2φ₀ or zero, and how this relates to the fitted harmonic content.
3. **Quantitatively extract the CPR rather than relying on FFT ratios.**
   - Fit the complete Is⁺(Φ) and Is⁻(Φ) curves with the inductive asymmetric-SQUID model, including Lgeo from simulation, to obtain I₂/I₁ and its sign versus temperature and twist angle.
   - Compare against a pure-sin CPR fit to demonstrate that a second harmonic is genuinely required.
   - Add more temperature points, especially at low T where β changes less.
   - Discuss twist-angle uncertainty and the inhomogeneity scenario of Yuan et al. as an alternative source of TRSB and sin 2φ, and identify which observable discriminates between them.
4. **Clarify geometry, inductance and sensor metrics, and temper the claims.**
   - Provide a 3D schematic of the current paths and the loop (which flake segments close the loop, and what areas the perpendicular and in-plane fields thread).
   - Quantify the 7× area enhancement with the measured flake outline; tabulate simulated Lgeo,1, Lgeo,2 and β with uncertainties.
   - Present Ic(T) directly alongside L_J(T), with an appropriate theoretical comparison.
   - Correct the operating temperature and flux-noise inconsistencies (60 K vs 77 K; 1.5 vs 1.41 µΦ₀/√Hz).
   - Specify the frequency, V_Φ and amplifier floor for the noise figure, and restrict benchmarking to comparable bare-loop high-Tc SQUIDs.
   - Revise "first-of-its-kind", "unique evidence" and "confirm" toward "consistent with", unless the controls above are added.
