# Review outline

**Manuscript:** "Quantum interference in a twisted high‑Tc SQUID senses emergent interfacial order" (Basu et al.)

---

## 1. Significance and novelty

**Scientific context**
- Whether time‑reversal symmetry is broken in twisted Bi‑2212 (BSCCO) c‑axis junctions near 45°, and how, is an open and contested question in cuprate twistronics.
  - Chiral d ± id order has been proposed (Can et al.; Zhao et al.).
  - Competing explanations include inhomogeneity‑induced time‑reversal breaking (Yuan et al.) and s‑wave‑like coupling (Zhu et al.).
- Existing evidence comes almost entirely from single‑junction probes: Shapiro steps, Fraunhofer patterns, diode asymmetry and switching hysteresis. These cannot access the junction phase directly.

**Claimed advance**
- The authors build a phase‑sensitive interferometer, in the spirit of Wollman et al., from a single twisted stack. The loop is cut with a fibre scalpel, producing two parallel twisted‑interface junctions.
- They use it to report three things:
  1. an "anomalous" phase difference φ₁₂ ≈ π between the arms, interpreted as opposite chiralities (d + id vs d − id);
  2. non‑zero η′ at B = 0, interpreted as time‑reversal symmetry breaking;
  3. second‑harmonic content in Is(Φ), interpreted as Cooper‑pair co‑tunnelling.
- A secondary result is a c‑axis high‑Tc SQUID with a quoted flux noise of ~1.5 µΦ₀/√Hz at 60 K.

**Novelty assessment**
- Fabricating a twist‑angle‑controlled, c‑axis, cuprate van der Waals SQUID by post‑hoc cutting is genuinely new and technically attractive.
- Applying a Wollman‑type intercept analysis to a twisted interface is a conceptually appropriate idea.
- The headline physics claim would be high impact if established: direct phase‑sensitive evidence of spontaneously chosen, opposite chiralities in two arms.
- At present, however, the central inference rests on an extrapolated intercept, a single device, a single temperature and roughly ten data points.

---

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **Device innovation.**
  - The cryogenic re‑exfoliation plus stencil contacts plus fibre‑scalpel cut is a simple, generalizable route to interferometers in twisted vdW superconductors.
  - It could become a standard platform, for example for d‑mon or twisted‑cuprate qubit proposals.
- **Clear SQUID modulation at high temperature.**
  - Well‑resolved oscillations are shown in both the voltage V(B) and the switching current Is(B), for 40° and 45° devices at 60–74 K.
  - Positive and negative bias are both measured, together with field‑tunable diode asymmetry.
- **Relevant observables.**
  - The paper addresses the right questions for the field: phase offset, harmonic content, Josephson inductance versus temperature, and time‑reversal symmetry.
  - Several complementary measurement modes are used: counter‑based switching statistics, dV/dI versus bias, and an in‑plane field knob.
- **Supporting infrastructure.**
  - Electromagnetic simulations of L_geo, a free‑energy model (SI 10) and additional devices (SI 9, 14) are provided.
  - A nominally 0° control device shows no zero‑field diode effect.
- **Practical sensing figure of merit.**
  - If the noise claims are substantiated, a µm‑scale high‑Tc sensor operating at tens of kelvin with µΦ₀/√Hz noise is of applied interest.

---

## 3. Potential reasons for rejection

### R1. The φ₁₂ ≈ π observation does not uniquely establish opposite chiralities; extrinsic flux offsets are not excluded

- **No absolute flux reference.**
  - The intercept of Φ_min versus I is measured relative to the nominal applied‑field zero. Any static flux offset produces exactly the same signature as φ₁₂.
  - Possible sources include:
    - remanent field inside the cryoperm shield;
    - magnet hysteresis;
    - pinned Abrikosov or pancake vortices in the flakes or electrodes, which couple fractional flux into the loop via the strong flux focusing the authors themselves invoke (period ~7× smaller than geometric);
    - offsets that change after in‑plane field excursions.
  - Wollman et al. controlled this carefully, with field‑reversal and reference measurements. Nothing comparable is reported here. Fig. 3(a) and 3(b) have visually similar slopes and differ mainly by a constant intercept, which is precisely what a static offset would give.
- **In‑plane field misalignment.**
  - The φ₁₂ toggling is produced by 10 µT steps of in‑plane field, while the perpendicular period is only ~3.3 µT.
  - A coil tilt of only ~3–5° projects ~0.5–1.7 µT onto the c‑axis, i.e. a sizeable fraction of a half period at 20 µT.
  - The misalignment angle and the coil cross‑talk are not quantified.
- **History dependence suggests flux trapping.**
  - In Extended Data Fig. 2, the same in‑plane field value (0 and ±10 µT) yields both "0" and "π" depending on sweep direction.
  - Fig. 3(a), at 10 µT, gives 0, while Extended Data Fig. 2 at 10 µT shows both states.
  - Such hysteretic, sweep‑dependent switching is characteristic of vortex entry and exit or pinning.
- **Extrapolation and statistics are weak.**
  - In Fig. 3(a), the lowest bias is 41 µA. The intercept requires linear extrapolation over more than 40 µA to I = 0.
  - With β ~ 1 and asymmetric arms, the linear relation in Eq. 2 is itself only approximate.
  - No error bars are given on the intercepts. The "π" values scatter from roughly 0.8π to π and the "0" values from roughly 0.05π to 0.13π.
  - The dataset is about ten points, from one device, at one temperature (20 K), in one cooldown. This is insufficient to distinguish a binary chiral state from a broad offset distribution.
- **Missing control with identical protocol.**
  - The 0° device is used only as a zero‑field diode control.
  - It is not reported to show φ₁₂ ≡ 0 under identical in‑plane sweeps. That is the decisive control for R1.

### R2. Conceptual tension between the d ± id interpretation and how a SQUID's Is(Φ) is actually determined

- **Ground‑state minimum versus current–phase relation (CPR).**
  - In Can et al., α is the gauge‑invariant phase across the junction itself. The "chirality" is therefore the choice of minimum (±α₀) of a CPR that is identical in both arms.
  - The SQUID critical current Ic(Φ) is set by maximizing I₁(φ₁) + I₂(φ₂) under the fluxoid constraint. It depends on the CPRs, not on which zero‑current minimum each junction occupied.
  - This differs from Wollman et al., where the π shift is intrinsic to the CPR (a sign change of Ic between crystal faces).
  - The authors must explain how a ground‑state chirality is encoded as a persistent offset in the switching or dV/dI pattern. Such an effect would arise only via metastable fluxoid or branch states, which must then be modelled explicitly.
- **Memory through switching.**
  - In both the counter‑based and dV/dI measurements, the junction is repeatedly driven into the running‑phase (resistive) state.
  - It is unclear why a spontaneously chosen chirality would survive repeated phase slips and retrapping, rather than being re‑randomized at each switching event.
- **Harmonic content versus a clean π shift.**
  - For F(α) = −E₁cos α − E₂cos 2α, the time‑reversal‑breaking minima sit at cos α₀ = E₁/(4|E₂|). This requires a dominant second harmonic, and φ₁₂ = 2α₀ equals π only in the limit E₁ → 0.
  - In that limit, however, the SQUID period halves to Φ₀/2, so a "half‑period" shift becomes a full‑period shift and is unobservable.
  - The data show Φ₀‑periodic, first‑harmonic‑dominated patterns, with A₂/A₁ of only 0.2–0.37. A clean π offset is therefore hard to reconcile with the stated mechanism.
  - The free‑energy calculation in SI 10 should address this quantitatively.
- **Simpler alternative near 45°: a 0–π junction pair.**
  - The first‑harmonic coupling changes sign at 45° (∝ cos 2θ).
  - Sub‑degree twist inhomogeneity, local strain, or partial relaxation along the cut could give the two arms opposite signs of E₁. That would be a 0‑junction and a π‑junction, producing φ₁₂ = π without any chirality.
  - Twist‑angle homogeneity across the two arms (±0.5° stated accuracy) is not characterized.
- **40° device inconsistent with the invoked theory.**
  - Theory (and Zhao et al.) places the time‑reversal‑breaking phase within a few degrees of 45°.
  - Yet the 40° device shows a larger η′(B = 0) (~8%) than the 45° device (~4%).
  - This undermines the claim that η′ tracks the chiral order. It is not discussed.
- **Unexplained mechanism for toggling.**
  - The proposed role of the in‑plane field (coupling of Josephson and Abrikosov vortices) is speculative and untested.
  - It is unclear why ~10 µT, which is below Earth's field, should select chiralities differently in two arms only a few µm apart.

### R3. The time‑reversal‑breaking and second‑harmonic evidence is not robust to known SQUID artefacts

- **η′ at B = 0 is offset‑sensitive.**
  - With a 2–3 µT period and steep Is(B), a residual field of ~0.1 µT can produce η′ of several percent.
  - In Fig. 4(a), the envelopes of I_s⁺(+B) and |I_s⁻(−B)| appear mutually shifted. This suggests an offset field or sweep hysteresis, not intrinsic time‑reversal breaking.
  - The essential test is missing: does a single constant B₀ restore I_s⁺(B) = |I_s⁻(2B₀ − B)| over the whole sweep?
  - Sweep‑direction dependence and repeated cooldowns are also not shown.
- **Asymmetric, finite‑L SQUIDs produce diodes and harmonics on their own.**
  - A SQUID with β ~ 1, 3:1 asymmetry and inductance asymmetry produces skewed, non‑sinusoidal Is(Φ) and a diode effect even with purely sinusoidal CPRs. This limitation is discussed in the authors' own ref. 31 (Babich et al.).
  - FFT harmonics of Is(Φ) therefore cannot be equated with CPR harmonics without a full fit.
- **Weak support for co‑tunnelling.**
  - Only three temperatures are used, giving A₂/A₁ = 0.20, 0.37 and 0.27.
  - The background subtraction procedure is not specified and may itself generate harmonics.
  - The non‑monotonic trend could equally reflect β(T) changing through Ic(T).
  - The "4e tunnelling" label in Fig. 4(c) is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- **Fluctuations near Tc.**
  - Measurements at 60–74 K lie close to the junction Tc (~75 K for the 45° device).
  - Thermally activated premature switching distorts Is(Φ) and polarity asymmetries.
  - Switching‑current distributions (widths) are not reported, so fluctuation effects cannot be assessed.

### R4. Quantitative inconsistencies in device characterization, and overstated sensor claims

- **Field period ~7× smaller than geometric for both devices.**
  - The identical factor for different loop geometries (81 versus 131 µm²) is suspicious. A coil‑calibration error should be excluded with an independent Hall‑probe or reference measurement.
  - The effective area (~570 µm²) is comparable to the whole flake. The "loop" and the flux‑focusing geometry should be defined quantitatively.
  - This also affects the quoted field noise (34 pT/√Hz) and the identification of what flux the arms actually see.
- **Josephson inductance and superfluid‑stiffness analysis.**
  - The extraction assumes Is2 ≫ Is1 with φ₂ constant, yet the areal ratio is only 3:1 and β ~ 1.
  - It relies on simulated L_geo and on switching rather than critical currents.
  - L_J ≈ 30 pH is roughly what Φ₀/(2πIc) gives for Ic ~ 10 µA. The extracted L_J(T) may simply reflect Ic(T) of a tunnel junction.
  - Comparing it with the T² dependence of bulk in‑plane superfluid stiffness is not like‑for‑like. The claimed "unusual nature" is unsupported.
  - The non‑monotonic low‑T stiffness (rising from 20 to 30 K) is unexplained.
- **Operating temperature inconsistencies.**
  - The abstract says "close to 77 K", the text says 60 K, and Extended Data Fig. 1 lists 77 K. But the 45° device has Tc ≈ 75 K, so it cannot operate at 77 K.
  - The noise value differs between 1.5 and 1.41 µΦ₀/√Hz in different places.
  - The frequency at which the noise is quoted is unclear: Fig. 5(b) is 1/f‑dominated, falling from ~10⁻⁵ to ~4 × 10⁻⁷ Φ₀/√Hz.
  - Missing information includes the amplifier noise floor, the transfer function V_Φ used, and the measurement bandwidth convention.
- **Overstated claims.**
  - "First‑of‑its‑kind c‑axis SQUID" needs qualification. Twisted vdW SQUIDs (ref. 16) and BSCCO intrinsic‑junction interferometers predate this work.
  - "State‑of‑the‑art" is compared against mm²‑pickup magnetometers and a 4.5 K Nb nanoSQUID, which is not an appropriate comparison set.
- **Possible interface degradation from cutting.**
  - The cut is made under unspecified temperature and atmosphere conditions after contact fabrication. Twisted BSCCO interfaces are known to degrade on warming.
  - Tc and Jc before versus after cutting, edge damage, and oxygen loss at the cut faces are not documented.
  - Arm‑dependent edge damage could itself produce arm‑dependent behaviour, linking back to R1 and R2.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Establish an absolute phase and flux reference and exclude extrinsic offsets.**
   - Measure, under the identical in‑plane sweep protocol, either an on‑chip reference loop (0° twisted, or a conventional superconductor) or the 0° SQUID.
   - Quantify coil misalignment by measuring the shift of the perpendicular pattern versus in‑plane field.
   - Perform repeated zero‑field‑cooled and field‑cooled thermal cycles above Tc. Report φ₁₂ histograms over many cooldowns: an intrinsic binary chiral choice should give a bimodal 0/π distribution with stable values, whereas trapped flux should give a broad or continuous distribution.
   - Give error bars on every intercept, and extend dV/dI data to lower bias currents to shorten the extrapolation.
   - Apply the B₀‑symmetrization test to η′.

2. **Provide a quantitative model connecting chirality to the observables.**
   - Fit Is±(Φ) and the dV/dI minima with a full two‑junction model that includes L_geo,1, L_geo,2, arm asymmetry, a CPR with E₁ and E₂ (including sign), and thermal fluctuations.
   - From the fit, extract the actual E₂/E₁ and state whether the time‑reversal‑breaking minima exist. Predict φ₁₂ = 2α₀ and explain how a Φ₀‑periodic pattern displays a π offset.
   - Explicitly address metastable fluxoid branches, and the survival of chirality through switching and phase slips.
   - Discriminate quantitatively against the 0–π (sign of E₁) alternative, for example via the temperature dependence of φ₁₂: chiral order should vanish above T_TRSB < Tc, while a 0–π configuration should persist up to Tc.

3. **Broaden statistics across twist angle and temperature.**
   - Report φ₁₂, η′(B = 0) and A₂/A₁ versus temperature for several devices, including angles just off 45° (43°–47°) and symmetric‑arm control loops.
   - Explain why the 40° device shows the largest η′.
   - Characterize twist homogeneity in both arms after cutting (SHG, polarized optics, or STEM), together with Tc and Jc before and after the cut.
   - Present switching‑current distributions to quantify fluctuation effects.

4. **Tighten the device and sensor claims.**
   - Calibrate the magnet independently and define the effective area quantitatively.
   - Recast the L_J(T) discussion as Ic(T) of the interfacial junction, compared with Ambegaokar–Baratoff‑type or d‑wave tunnelling predictions, rather than with bulk stiffness.
   - Correct the operating temperature (60 K, below the 75 K Tc).
   - Quote white‑noise and 1/f values at stated frequencies, with the amplifier floor and V_Φ.
   - Compare against appropriate µm‑scale high‑Tc and nanoSQUID benchmarks.
   - Moderate the "first‑of‑its‑kind" and "state‑of‑the‑art" language, and temper the abstract's assertion that π "reflects chiral order" until the controls above are in place.
