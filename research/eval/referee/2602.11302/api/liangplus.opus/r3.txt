Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript applies operando Bragg XPCS to VO₂ two-terminal devices. It tracks speckle decorrelation for hours under four conditions: no current, sub-threshold current, constant above-threshold current, and ~1800 synchronized on/off cycles. The central claims are:

- slow (~10³ s) current-induced domain reconfiguration;
- a "jamming transition," read from the stretching exponent β rising from 1 to 1.5;
- cycle-to-cycle speckle correlation that rises over ~1000 cycles, interpreted as a transition from stochastic to deterministic filament formation that sub-threshold currents can erase.

Using stroboscopic coherent diffraction to quantify the microscopic reproducibility of resistive switching is a genuinely new idea, and it bears directly on neuromorphic devices. However, the headline conclusions (deterministic switching, erasability, jamming) currently rest on small decorrelations of a ~2% contrast signal, interpreted without electrical, structural or beam-effect controls.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The stroboscopic XPCS protocol (Fig. 1c, Fig. 3c,i) is original. It could become a general tool for assessing the microscopic cycle-to-cycle reproducibility of phase-change and resistive-switching devices.
- The measurements span long times (hours) on operating devices. This addresses a real gap between fast ensemble electrical measurements and slow real-space nano-imaging.
- The qualitative contrast between sub-threshold current (delayed decorrelation, Fig. 3a) and above-threshold current (immediate decorrelation followed by a stable new state, Fig. 3b) is interesting. If substantiated, it is relevant to the debate on persistent metallic domains and memory in VO₂ (refs. 9–11, 45, 46).
- The topic suits a broad physics readership, and the experimental details (beamline, flux, coherence, detector geometry) are largely reported.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

**1. "Deterministic switching" is inferred only from speckle correlation; no switching statistics are shown.**
- The abstract, Fig. 1e and the conclusions state that switching "becomes deterministic" and that the device "learns." The only evidence is ΔG(n,n+1) in Fig. 3i.
- Switching stochasticity is conventionally defined electrically: cycle-to-cycle spread of threshold voltage/current, switching delay, and On/Off resistance.
- The authors should show, for the same 1800-cycle run:
  - the On- and Off-state resistance for every cycle;
  - the threshold or delay distribution versus cycle number, e.g. in blocks of 100 cycles.
- They should demonstrate that its spread narrows over the same ~200–1000-cycle window over which ΔG rises.
- Without this, Fig. 3i shows only that the On-state diffraction pattern becomes reproducible. That is necessary but not sufficient for the claim.

**2. Leading alternative explanations for the rise in ΔG(n,n+1) are not excluded.**
With a 3 s period (and slow VO₂ relaxation; the text itself cites relaxation "up to hours," ref. 22), the following could each produce rising correlation between successive On images without any "learning" of the switching process:
- incomplete relaxation to the insulating state between pulses, so the filament or metallic patches simply persist;
- a cumulative rise of the device/substrate temperature toward a periodic steady state (~8 mW at 4 mA, ~2 V, Fig. 5b);
- irreversible electroforming (oxygen-vacancy migration or electromigration under repeated 4 mA pulses, enhanced in the Ga-irradiated region);
- cumulative X-ray dose.

The Off state is never imaged, so persistence cannot be distinguished from reproducible re-formation. The authors should:
- record diffraction during the Off phase as well, and report G(On_n, Off_n) and G(Off_n, Off_n+1);
- show the Off-state resistance per cycle, to prove each cycle fully returns to the insulating state;
- report I–V and R(T) before and after training, to test for irreversible forming;
- repeat with varied Off duration (e.g. 3 s vs 30 s), since relaxation-limited persistence should depend strongly on it.

**3. The erasure and reset claims are not demonstrated.**
- The abstract ("sub-threshold currents erase the short-term memory... recovering stochastic switching") and the conclusion ("ability to train and reset") imply a sequence of training, sub-threshold erasure, then stochastic switching again.
- No such experiment is shown. Fig. 3a applies sub-threshold current to an as-prepared (untrained) state.
- The required experiment is: train (as in Fig. 3c), apply 0.26 mA for ≥ several τ, then re-cycle. Ideally repeat this loop more than once.
- The authors should show that ΔG returns to its low initial value and that electrical switching statistics broaden again.
- Without this, "in-device learning" and "reset" remain speculation.

**4. The β and τ values, and hence the jamming transition, are not robustly determined.**
- The fitted τ values lie far beyond the measurement windows:
  - 81,000 s from data to ~10⁴ s (Fig. 2c);
  - 35,000 s from data to ~3×10³ s (Fig. 3g);
  - 160,000 s from data to ~3×10³ s (Fig. 3h).
- For t ≪ τ, |F|² ≈ 1 − 2(t/τ)^β. β and τ are then almost perfectly degenerate and are set by a decay of a few ×10⁻³ in g₂.
- A single τ is imposed for all t_age (Methods, Eq. 3), while β is apparently allowed to vary with t_age (Fig. 4). Any genuine aging of τ (non-stationarity, which the two-time maps show) would therefore be absorbed into β. A drift of β from 1 to 1.5 could be a fitting artefact of this constraint.
- The treatment of the contrast A and the baseline (fixed at 1 or free) is not stated.
- The authors should:
  - report fits with τ and β both free per t_age, with the full covariance or confidence contours;
  - show fits with A and baseline fixed from an independent measurement (e.g. static-sample contrast);
  - demonstrate that β = 1.5 vs 1.0 is statistically distinguishable for individual curves;
  - consider model-independent measures (e.g. the curvature of g₂ on log axes, or time-to-decorrelate thresholds).
- Calling this a jamming transition also needs more than β:
  - the Q-dependence of τ, since τ ∝ Q⁻¹ (ballistic) vs Q⁻² (diffusive) is the standard discriminator (Cipelletti et al.);
  - two-time-map dynamic heterogeneity (variance of the fluctuations, χ-type analysis).
- The "domain speeds" mentioned in the Methods-referenced text are never reported; doing so would help.

**5. Measurement artefacts that can decorrelate speckle are not controlled.**

*(a) Normalization.*
- Eq. 1 normalizes I(t₁)I(t₂) by a global ⟨I²⟩_t. The standard is pixel-wise products normalized by ⟨I(t₁)⟩_p⟨I(t₂)⟩_p, averaged over an ROI.
- As written, G is sensitive to changes in total intensity, such as M1→R phase-fraction changes, Debye–Waller effects, or beam decay/top-up.
- The decrease along the diagonal in Fig. 2b suggests exactly this contamination.
- The authors should give the exact ROI-based formula and the ROI (Q range, which peak), and show integrated intensity versus time for every run.

*(b) Thermal and positional drift.*
- Joule heating and temperature setpoints change the lattice parameter and sample position. Both shift the Bragg peak and speckle on the detector and mimic decorrelation.
- The authors should show peak centroid (2θ, ω) and width versus time for each run, and correct or reject drift-affected intervals.

*(c) X-ray beam effects.*
- 2×10¹⁰ ph/s focused to ~100 µm² for hours delivers a large dose. X-ray-induced persistent conductivity and structural changes in VO₂ are documented in the literature.
- A dose-dependence control is needed: attenuated flux, beam-off intervals, or a fresh spot. This should show that the ~1000 s delays and the Fig. 3c evolution are not beam-driven.

*(d) Illuminated area.*
- The beam (7.4 × 14.8 µm²), and its footprint, which is elongated by the incidence geometry at 2θ ≈ 50°, is larger than the 5 × 5 µm² gap. It covers film under or outside the electrodes that never switches, and the filament occupies a small fraction of it.
- The authors should quantify the scattering-volume fraction from the gap and the filament. They should discuss how this dilutes or biases the correlation changes attributed to filament reproducibility.

**6. Joule heating versus current-specific effects is not resolved.**
- The Discussion attributes the dynamics to Joule heating, yet interprets the ~1000 s delay under 0.26 mA (Fig. 3a) as "short-term memory" being erased.
- A ~1000 s incubation is also what one expects from slow thermal equilibration of stage, substrate and probe station after a ~0.6 mW step.
- The authors should:
  - measure device resistance (as a local thermometer) and stage temperature versus time during each constant-current run;
  - perform a control in which the stage temperature is stepped to reproduce the same device resistance without current;
  - test whether the incubation and β evolution are reproduced.
- Without this, "memory" and "erasure" cannot be separated from thermal transients.

**B. Should address**

**7. The Arrhenius analysis and melting-point argument are weak.**
- The Fig. 2d inset appears to contain very few temperatures. Ea follows essentially from τ = 81,000 s (298 K) and 24,000 s (340 K): ln(81000/24000)/(1/298 − 1/340) ≈ 2900 K.
- The 298 K value is stated to be the "experimental stability limit," so it is not a material relaxation time.
- At 340 K the system is non-stationary, mid-MIT (Fig. 2b), so a stationary activated rate is ill-defined there.
- More temperatures are needed, all below the transition onset and with stationary two-time maps.
- The comparison Ea/k_B ≈ T_melt (2240 K), and the inference that "melting occurs when all domains become freely mobile," lack a physical basis. Activation barriers for domain-wall motion are not generally tied to the melting temperature. This should be removed or justified.

**8. The current dependence of t_jam and the shear-thickening analogy are not supported.**
- The t_jam values (1100 ± 600, 1200 ± 500, 4000 ± 3000 s) are not statistically distinct.
- For 4 mA, β never reaches 1.5 within the 7000 s window (Fig. 4, black), so "for all currents a transition from β = 1 to β = 1.5 is observed" is not shown.
- The 0.26 mA run is sub-threshold, with no filament (Fig. 3a text). Its inclusion contradicts "Electrical switching induces a jamming transition."
- 1.7 mA appears to lie at or below I₁ in Fig. 5b. The authors should state whether it switches. If it does not, the β evolution is not switching-specific.
- The 1.7 mA two-time data are not shown anywhere and should be included.
- The shear-thickening analogy should be removed, or supported by a defined "driving rate" and more currents.

**9. The role of Ga irradiation and device-to-device reproducibility are unclear.**
- Irradiation is introduced precisely as a way to localize the filament (refs. 31–34). Defect pinning is therefore an obvious, conventional explanation for increasingly reproducible filament formation.
- The pristine-device data, said to give "equivalent results including the stochastic-to-deterministic transition," are only in the SI. They should appear in the main text alongside Fig. 3i, with the same analysis.
- Only one training run on one device is shown. The authors should report how many devices and runs were measured, and the spread in the "learning" cycle number.

**10. Experimental history and protocol are not reported.**
- Given the emphasis on memory, the order of the runs on the irradiated device matters (Fig. 2 temperature series, Fig. 3a, 3b, 4 at 1.7 mA, 3c). Any intervening reset (thermal cycling above T_MIT?) should also be stated.
- For example, if the cycling run followed a long 4 mA hold, the initial stochastic state and its later evolution are history-dependent.
- The cycling duty cycle is ambiguous: "turned on and off for 3 s," with 1 s acquisition. The top axis of Fig. 3c implies ~3 s per full cycle. The authors should state the On and Off durations, and when within the On phase the 1 s exposure starts.
- The definition and normalization of ΔG(n,n+1) must be given explicitly. If it is min–max scaled to [0,1], a value near 1 does not mean "deterministic." The absolute G(n,n+1) should be compared with the static-sample correlation level, which is the ceiling for identical configurations.

**11. Phase specificity and domain size are not established.**
- The text speaks of 200_M1 and 011_R domains, but it is not stated whether both reflections are on the detector, are separated, or are analysed separately.
- If only one peak is in the ROI, the measurement cannot distinguish metallic from insulating rearrangement. Statements about "metallic domains" moving toward the filament then remain speculative.
- R_d = 2π/Q_FWHM ≈ 20 nm is a structural coherence length that includes strain and size broadening along one direction (unspecified). It is not necessarily the metallic/insulating domain size. The authors should state the direction and caveat the interpretation.

**12. The novelty framing should be tempered.**
- "Remarkably... here it is observed in hard matter" overlooks extensive prior XPCS reports of compressed-exponential and aging dynamics in hard condensed matter. Examples include metallic glasses, antiferromagnetic and charge-density-wave domains (including work from this group), and LSCO (ref. 43).
- The claim should be positioned relative to these.

**C. Minor**

13. Numerical inconsistencies:
- the room-temperature stability limit is "8000 ± 3000 s" in the text but τ = 81,000 ± 3000 s in Fig. 2c;
- the constant 4 mA τ is "≈150,000 ± 50,000 s" in the text but 160,000 ± 50,000 s in Fig. 3h;
- the sub-threshold current is 0.27 mA in the text but 0.26 mA in Figs. 3 and 4;
- τ values at 4 mA exceed the stated stability limit and should be described as lower bounds.

14. The text refers to "the inset (orange data)" for ΔG, but there is no inset; this is Fig. 3i. "Stabilizes for ≈1000 cycles" should read "after ≈1000 cycles." The text also links "t < 1000 s" to "cycle number < 200," but at ~3 s/cycle 200 cycles ≈ 600 s.

15. Irradiation geometry is inconsistent. The main text says "a central 5 × 5 µm region irradiated," while Methods says "devices were globally irradiated," even though one device is pristine.

16. The aging-time construction is misdefined. Lines starting at t₁ = t₂ = t_age cannot satisfy t₁ + t₂ = t_age (that would be 2t_age). Please define it precisely, e.g. t₁ = t_age, t₂ = t_age + t.

17. Eq. 3 is described as "a single exponential decay function"; it is a stretched/compressed (KWW) exponential.

18. Fig. 1 panels are labelled c, d, e, but the caption describes them as top, center and bottom of panel c.

19. The Fig. 5 caption title "Resistance vs temperature and current" does not match panel b, which plots voltage. Please also state the measurement temperature for Fig. 5b and the ramp rate.

20. Please supply a table of all fit parameters (A, τ, β, baseline) per t_age and condition, and the number of temperatures in the Arrhenius inset with their 1/T values.

21. Specify the Bragg reflection indices relative to the R-cut substrate and the incidence angle, from which the beam footprint can be evaluated.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Electrical-plus-stroboscopic experiment.** Combine the stroboscopic XPCS with per-cycle electrical switching statistics and Off-phase imaging, in a single train, erase (sub-threshold) and retrain sequence. Repeat it on both the irradiated and the pristine device. This one experiment would directly test the paper's central claims and exclude the persistence and forming explanations.
2. **Reanalysis of the correlation functions.** Use standard pixel-normalized G(t₁,t₂) with explicit ROIs, and free τ and β per t_age with confidence contours. Add a Q-resolved analysis of τ (ROIs at different |Q − Q_Bragg|) to discriminate diffusive from ballistic dynamics before invoking jamming.
3. **Beam and thermal controls.** Run a flux-attenuated or intermittent-exposure control, and track peak centroid, width and integrated intensity together with device resistance throughout each run. Run a temperature-step control matched to the Joule-heated state. Together these separate intrinsic, current-specific dynamics from beam and thermal transients.
4. **Real-space anchoring of the filament.** Where possible, image the filament location for one trained and one untrained state (e.g. nano-diffraction, dark-field X-ray microscopy, or post-mortem s-SNOM). Speckle reproducibility could then be tied to a reproducible filament location rather than inferred. Also report the fraction of the illuminated volume occupied by the switched region.
