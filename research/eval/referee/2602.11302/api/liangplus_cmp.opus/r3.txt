Review outline:

## 1. Significance and novelty

The submission applies operando Bragg XPCS to micron-scale VO₂ two-terminal devices during electrical switching. It tracks nano-domain configurations over hours, and it introduces a stroboscopic protocol that correlates successive On states across about 1800 cycles. Four results are claimed:

- slow, current-dependent erasure of the initial domain configuration;
- an electrically driven "jamming" transition, read from the stretching exponent β changing from 1 to 1.5;
- a progressive change from stochastic to deterministic filament formation with repeated switching;
- by implication, a train/erase protocol for neuromorphic use.

The methodology is new for this material class and the questions are important. However, the central physical interpretations (jamming, learning, deterministic switching) currently rest on fits to partial correlation decays, on single runs, and on structural correlations with no electrical counterpart.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **Technique.** Bragg XPCS under bias over hours, with 1 s resolution, is a capability that real-space nano-imaging cannot easily match. The stroboscopic, cycle-synchronized correlation scheme (Fig. 1c–e, Fig. 3c,f,i) is novel and could become a standard tool for testing device reproducibility.
- **Clear qualitative contrast in Fig. 3a vs 3b.** Under a 0.26 mA sub-threshold current, the pre-current configuration persists for about 1000 s before decorrelating. Under 4 mA, the post-switch configuration stays highly correlated for thousands of seconds. These observations deserve publication if artifacts are excluded.
- **Relevance.** Cycle-dependent growth of On-to-On correlation (Fig. 3i) bears directly on the stochasticity question in Mott neuromorphic devices. It is in line with reports of persistent metallic domains (refs. 10, 11, 45, 46).

## 3. Concerns

### A. Must address

**1. The jamming claim rests on β values that the fitting procedure may produce artificially.**

- *What is wrong.* Per Methods and the Fig. 2/3 captions, a single τ is fit for all g₂^tage(t) in a measurement, while β is evidently allowed to vary with t_age (Fig. 4).
- *Why it matters.* If the true relaxation time ages, which is likely after a current step, forcing τ to be constant obliges β to absorb the change in curve shape. A drift from β≈1 to β≈1.5 can then appear with no change in the physics.
- *Partial decays.* The decays are tiny fractions of the speckle contrast. In Fig. 2c, g₂ falls from about 1.019 to 1.017 within a 10⁴ s window, against τ = 81 000 s. In Fig. 3h, τ = 160 000 s is extracted from data extending to about 3×10³ s. When τ is 10–50× longer than the observation window, τ and β are strongly covariant and β is barely constrained. The quoted uncertainties (±3000 s at 298 K, ±100 s at 340 K) look implausibly small for such extrapolations.
- *What to do.*
  - Fit τ(t_age) and β(t_age) jointly as free parameters, and show the τ–β covariance.
  - State whether the contrast A and the baseline were fixed, and to what. A could be fixed from the short-time limit or a static reference.
  - Report β with fixed A, with τ free per t_age, and with τ fixed.
  - Show that β = 1.5 is distinguishable from β = 1 at the data's signal-to-noise. For example, compare residuals or an information criterion for the two models on representative curves.
  - If β cannot be determined robustly when τ greatly exceeds the window, the jamming interpretation should be withdrawn or heavily qualified.

**2. Trivial decorrelation sources under current are not excluded.**

- *Possible artifacts.* Joule heating at 4 mA is roughly 8 mW into a 5×5 µm channel, taking V ≈ 2 V from Fig. 5b. This, together with the M1→R lattice change, will shift and broaden the Bragg peak, thermally expand the sample and stage, and can translate the illuminated footprint. Any of these decorrelates speckle without any rearrangement of nano-domains.
- *Ballistic drift looks like jamming.* Uniform ballistic drift gives compressed exponentials (β > 1), the same signature the paper assigns to jamming.
- *Normalization.* Eq. (1), G = I(t₁)I(t₂)/⟨I²⟩_t, does not state a pixel/ROI average or normalization by ⟨I(t₁)⟩⟨I(t₂)⟩. Changes in integrated intensity, such as a growing R fraction or a peak moving out of the ROI, would then change G directly. The diagonal decrease at 340 K (Fig. 2b) and the "immediate erasure" at 4 mA (Fig. 3b) are both open to this reading.
- *What to do.*
  - State the exact G definition, ROI, and normalization.
  - Show the time traces of Bragg peak centroid (2θ, ω), FWHM, and integrated intensity for the M1 and R peaks across each run.
  - Show that decorrelation persists after recentring images on the peak centroid. Cross-correlation shift analysis of speckle patterns would directly test for rigid translation.
  - Report any sample-position or fiducial monitoring during heating and current steps.

**3. "Deterministic switching" and "learning" are inferred only from structural correlations, with no electrical counterpart and no verification of the Off state.**

- *No electrical evidence.* The abstract and conclusion claim that switching "becomes deterministic," yet Fig. 3c/i shows only ΔG(n,n+1). Cycle-by-cycle electrical data are absent:
  - threshold current or voltage,
  - switching delay,
  - On-state and Off-state resistance,
  - their variance versus cycle number.

  These must be shown and correlated with ΔG.
- *Off-state relaxation.* With off-times of a few seconds, and the paper's own note that relaxation "may take up to hours for large switching currents," the device may never fully return to the insulating state. Cumulative substrate heating at about 50% duty could have the same effect.
  - The rise in On-to-On correlation could then simply reflect persistent metallic domains or a thermally drifting steady state accumulating.
  - That is not necessarily a jamming-mediated plastic memory.
  - Please show Off-state resistance and Off-state diffraction (correlating Off images between cycles) versus cycle number, and estimate the device temperature over the duty cycle.
- *ΔG normalization.* State how ΔG(n,n+1) is normalized to [0, 1] (Fig. 3i) and what "1" means. A value of 0.8 on an arbitrary normalized scale does not establish determinism. Compare it with the absolute correlation of two consecutive frames under constant 4 mA (Fig. 3b), which serves as the "perfectly reproducible" reference.

**4. Train, retain and reset are claimed but never demonstrated.**

- *The gap.* The abstract and conclusion propose that sub-threshold currents erase memory and restore stochastic switching after training, giving "in-device learning." No experiment applies a sub-threshold current to a trained device. Retention of the learned state after cycling stops is also not measured.
- *What to do.*
  - Run a train (N cycles) → wait → test protocol, to measure retention.
  - Run a train → 0.26 mA erase → retrain protocol, showing ΔG and the electrical statistics return to the initial stochastic level.
  - Without these, the neuromorphic training/erasure statements should be presented as speculation.

**5. It is not shown that the diffraction is sensitive to the filament and channel rather than to unbiased film.**

- *Beam size.* The beam is 7.4×14.8 µm² and the device is 5×5 µm². At θ ≈ 25° incidence the footprint is further elongated by about 1/sinθ ≈ 2.4. A large part of the illuminated VO₂ therefore sits outside the biased channel and under or around the Au/Ti electrodes.
- *Filament volume.* The filament is likely sub-micron wide and occupies a small fraction of the scattering volume.
- *Coherence.* The 1.7 µm coherence length is smaller than the beam, so the low speckle contrast (A ≈ 0.02) arises from many partially coherent patches.
- *Why it matters.* Statements about "stochastic vs deterministic filament formation" need evidence that the speckle signal changes appreciably when the filament changes location.
- *What to do.*
  - Quantify the fraction of scattering from the channel.
  - Show a control with the beam on unbiased film adjacent to the device, under the same thermal history.
  - Ideally simulate the expected change in ΔG for filament relocation versus rearrangement of surrounding domains, for this geometry and coherence.

### B. Should address

**6. A null control for the jamming transition is missing, and the current dependence of t_jam is not statistically supported.**

- *Null control.* Fig. 4 has no zero-current curve at 313 K. The β(t_age) evolution could be generic aging after the temperature step or after beam exposure began.
  - Show β(t_age) for I = 0 at 313 K over the same duration.
  - Show β for t_age < 0 in the current runs.
- *Trend not supported.* The t_jam values are 1100±600 s (0.26 mA), 1200±500 s (1.7 mA) and 4000±3000 s (4 mA). The first two are indistinguishable. The 4 mA β never clearly reaches 1.5, rising from about 0.9 to 1.3 over 7000 s, so its sigmoid fit is poorly constrained. The statements "the larger the current, the slower the transition" and the shear-thickening analogy are not supported.
- *Link to switching is undermined.* Jamming occurs at 0.26 mA, which forms no filament. From Fig. 5b, 1.7 mA also appears to be below I₁, so please specify its regime explicitly. This weakens the claimed causal link between jamming driven by above-threshold currents and the learning seen under cycling.
- *β not measured under cycling.* β is not reported for the cycling experiment itself. Report it, and determine whether the rise in ΔG coincides with a change in dynamics.

**7. The activation energy is effectively a two-point estimate, and it conflicts with the Joule-heating interpretation.**

- *Two points.* The Arrhenius inset of Fig. 2d appears to rest on 298 K and 340 K. The two τ values (81 000 and 24 000 s) give E_a/k ≈ 2900 K, essentially the quoted value.
- *Neither point is clean.*
  - The 298 K value is described in the text as possibly the "experimental stability limit," which cannot serve as a data point.
  - At 340 K the system is non-stationary (Fig. 2b), yet a single τ is fit across t_age.
- *Contradiction.* Fig. 3h gives τ ≈ 160 000 s in the 4 mA On state at 313 K. That is slower than at 298 K with no current, despite substantial Joule heating. This contradicts the discussion's suggestion that current-induced dynamics are Joule-activated and "comparable to thermally activated timescales."
- *What to do.*
  - Provide at least four or five temperatures below the transition, measured at stationary conditions, with beam-stability controls.
  - Reconcile the 4 mA τ with the heating picture, or revise the discussion.
- *Melting-point comparison.* Comparing E_a/k_B to the melting temperature (2240 K) is not physically meaningful; a barrier for domain-wall motion is unrelated to bulk melting. It should be removed or recast, for example against known domain-wall or strain barriers in VO₂.

**8. X-ray beam effects are not controlled.**

- *Known effect.* X-ray illumination is known to induce defects and persistent changes in VO₂.
- *Exposure.* Here a 2×10¹⁰ ph/s focused beam sits on the film for hours, and on the contacts, which are within the footprint.
- *Why it matters.* Beam-induced dynamics could set the "stability limit," contribute to aging/β evolution, or progressively modify the channel during the 5000 s cycling run, which would mimic "learning."
- *What to do.* Provide flux-attenuated and intermittent-exposure controls, and compare pre- and post-beam R(T) of the measured devices.

**9. Sample statistics are limited.**

- The main text appears to show one run per protocol on one irradiated device. The pristine device is only mentioned as "equivalent" (SI).
- Because the irradiation is designed to pin the filament (ref. 32), the pristine-device ΔG(n,n+1) curve belongs in the main text. A comparison of cycles-to-saturation between devices is needed.
- Please also state whether each protocol was repeated after thermal cycling through T_MIT, which should reset the domain configuration, and how reproducible the onset at about 200 cycles and saturation at about 1000 cycles are.
- Methods says devices were "globally irradiated," whereas the main text says a "central 5×5 µm region" was irradiated. Please clarify.

**10. Phase identification and the domain-packing picture are not substantiated.**

- *No measured detector images.* Fig. 1's diffraction patterns are simulated. Show measured detector images before and after switching, the ROI used, and whether 200_M1 and 011_R are resolved on the detector. Address the possibility of M2 or T phases under strain and current.
- *No metallic fraction.* The jamming interpretation invokes an increasing metallic fraction that reduces free space. The R-peak integrated intensity versus time after switching would test this directly.
- *Domain size.* R_d = 2π/Q_FWHM = 20 nm needs specification:
  - which peak and which direction (in-plane or out-of-plane), given the 45 nm film thickness;
  - whether instrumental resolution, strain broadening and partial coherence were deconvolved.

**11. The effective temperature under current is unknown.**

- At 4 mA, local filament temperatures may exceed T_MIT while the stage reads 313 K.
- Comparisons between current-driven and thermal dynamics require at least an estimate: a thermal model, or a temperature read from the M1 Bragg peak shift.

**12. Interpretive overstatement.**

- "Remarkably… observed in hard matter" overlooks strain-glass and martensitic-glass literature in ferroelastics and shape-memory alloys. It also overlooks compressed-exponential relaxation from internal stress, reported widely in XPCS of hard materials. These should be discussed and cited.
- β = 1.5 is one of several known signatures. Compressed exponentials are typically attributed to ballistic, stress-driven rearrangements, not uniquely to jamming. Alternatives should be weighed.

### C. Minor

13. **Numerical inconsistencies between text and figures.**
    - Room-temperature decay: "8000±3000 s" in the text vs τ = 81 000±3000 s in Fig. 2c.
    - On state: "τ ≈ 150000±50000 s" in the text vs 160 000±50 000 s in Fig. 3h.
    - Lowest current: "0.27 mA" in the text vs 0.26 mA in Fig. 4 and Fig. 3.
14. **Cycle timing is ambiguous.** The text says the device is on and off "for 3 s." The caption says switching occurs "every three seconds." The top axis of Fig. 3c appears to place about 1800 cycles in about 5000 s, roughly 2.8 s per cycle. The text also places the low-correlation region at "t < 1000 s and cycle number < 200," which does not match that axis. Please state on-time, off-time and detector window precisely.
15. The text refers to "the inset (orange data)" for Fig. 3i, but there is no inset.
16. The Fig. 1 caption labels panel c as both protocol and pulse sequences; panels c–e should be described separately.
17. **Notation in the equations.**
    - Methods/Fig. 2: perpendicular cuts from t₁ = t₂ = t_age satisfy t₁ + t₂ = 2t_age, not t_age.
    - Eq. (2) uses τ as the dummy time variable, which clashes with the relaxation time τ.
    - Eq. (3) is a stretched or compressed exponential, not a "single exponential."
18. "Decay rates of 8000 s" should read "relaxation times."
19. The Fig. 2d inset axes need units and labelled tick values. Please list the temperatures used.
20. Define "sub-threshold" quantitatively using Fig. 5b, with I₁, I₂, I_On and I_Off values in mA, and place 1.7 mA relative to them.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Closed-loop experiment on more than one device, including the pristine one.** Run train → retain → sub-threshold erase → retrain, recording ΔG(n,n+1), Off-state correlations, β, and cycle-by-cycle electrical statistics (V_th, delay, R_on, R_off) at the same time. This single dataset would test the learning, erasure and determinism claims together.
2. **Robust and transparent XPCS analysis.** Use per-t_age free τ and β with fixed, independently measured contrast. Add drift and peak-centroid tracking with recentred correlations, and state the G normalization explicitly. Consider sub-ROI or q-resolved analysis to separate the M1 and R contributions and to test for ballistic versus diffusive q-dependence of τ. The expected scalings are τ ∝ 1/q for ballistic motion and τ ∝ 1/q² for diffusive motion, which would directly test the jamming interpretation.
3. **Spatial attribution.** Use a smaller beam, scanned across channel, electrode edges and off-device film. Alternatively, do post-mortem nano-diffraction or s-SNOM on trained versus erased devices to tie ΔG changes to filament localization. Pair this with speckle simulations of filament relocation in the actual geometry.
4. **Improved thermal series and heating estimate.** Measure at five or more stationary temperatures between 298 and 335 K with beam-dose controls. Add an estimate of filament temperature under 4 mA, so that the comparison between current-driven and thermally activated dynamics can be made quantitatively.
