Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript applies operando Bragg XPCS to 5×5 µm VO₂ two-terminal devices. It reports four findings:
- domain reconfiguration is slow (10³–10⁵ s) and thermally activated;
- a sub-threshold current slowly decorrelates the initial domain configuration;
- under constant current, the stretching exponent evolves from β≈1 to β≈1.5, interpreted as a jamming transition;
- repeated above-threshold switching makes successive On-state speckle patterns increasingly correlated, interpreted as a transition from stochastic to deterministic filament formation ("learning").

Using coherent X-ray speckle to follow cycle-to-cycle reproducibility of the nanoscale phase distribution during device operation is new and potentially valuable for resistive-switching and neuromorphic research. In its current form, however, the headline claims (learning, erasure/reset, jamming, control of stochasticity) rest on small changes in a ~2% speckle contrast. Those changes are not tied to any electrical measure of stochasticity, and they are not separated from thermal, drift or beam-induced artifacts.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The synchronized, cycle-resolved XPCS protocol (Fig. 1c–d, Fig. 3c,i) is a creative way to measure the reproducibility of the nanoscale configuration over thousands of switching events. It could become a useful method.
- Operando, hour-long measurements on actual devices at 1 s resolution fill a real gap between fast ensemble electrical probes and slow real-space nano-imaging.
- The qualitative observation that the On-state configuration is stable for ≫10³ s, while a sub-threshold current produces delayed decorrelation, is interesting. It connects to ramp-reversal memory and persistent metallic domains (refs. 9–11, 45, 46).
- The conceptual framing of plastic training and electrical reset is attractive for the neuromorphic community, if it can be substantiated.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

**1. The "stochastic → deterministic switching" claim has no electrical evidence, and incomplete relaxation is not excluded (Fig. 3c,f,i; Abstract; Discussion).**
- *What is shown.* Stochasticity of switching is a device property (threshold voltage or current, delay time, filament location). The paper infers it solely from increasing speckle correlation between adjacent On-state frames. No per-cycle electrical data are shown: no V(t), no On/Off resistance per cycle, no threshold distribution versus cycle number.
- *Competing explanation from the paper's own statements.* The introduction notes that relaxation to the insulating state "may take up to hours for large switching currents" (ref. 22), and Fig. 3b shows the 4 mA On-state configuration is stable for >10⁴ s. With Off periods of ~1.5–3 s (the duty cycle is ambiguous; see item 17), the device may never return to its insulating configuration. The device may also progressively heat to a higher time-averaged temperature. Either effect would raise On-to-On correlation without any "learning."
- *What the authors should do:*
  - Report per-cycle Off-state resistance and switching voltage, and threshold or delay statistics versus cycle number.
  - Record speckle during the Off phase, and show the On–Off correlation versus cycle number.
  - Repeat the cycling with Off times long compared with the relaxation time, or verify full electrical recovery between pulses.
  - Show that the correlation increase coincides with reduced electrical cycle-to-cycle variance.

  Without this, "deterministic switching" is not established.

**2. Erasure of a trained state and recovery of stochastic switching are claimed but not demonstrated (Abstract; Conclusions).**
- The abstract states that sub-threshold currents "erase the short-term memory … recovering stochastic switching," and the conclusions propose train/reset operation.
- The only erasure experiment (Fig. 3a,g) acts on the pristine pre-switching configuration, not on a state trained by cycling. It also does not test switching stochasticity afterwards.
- A train → sub-threshold erase → retrain sequence on the same device is required, showing ΔG(n,n+1) returning to low values and rising again. If that is not possible, the claims should be restricted to what is shown.

**3. The jamming transition rests on a β parameter that is poorly constrained by the data (Fig. 4; Eq. 3; Figs. 2c,d, 3g,h).**
- *Degenerate fit.* The fitted τ values (35,000 s; 81,000 s; 160,000 s) are 1–2 orders of magnitude longer than the time windows over which g₂ is fitted (≲10³–10⁴ s). For t ≪ τ the decay is only a small fraction of its total amplitude: g₂ drops by ~0.002–0.005 out of a contrast of ~0.02. In this regime β and τ are almost completely degenerate.
- *Constraint transfers into β.* A single τ is imposed for all t_age within a measurement while β varies freely with t_age. Any real aging of τ would therefore be forced into β. A rising β could thus simply be a slowing or speeding of the relaxation.
- *Error bars.* The 4 mA β values scatter by ±0.3. The sigmoid yields t_jam = 4000 ± 3000 s and does not saturate within the window.
- *What the authors should show:*
  - fits with τ and β both free per t_age, with the full covariance or confidence contours;
  - fits with β fixed at 1 and τ(t_age) free, with a model comparison;
  - how the error bars in Fig. 4 were obtained;
  - whether the β evolution survives these tests.
- *Unsupported current dependence.* The ordering "larger current → slower transition" and the shear-thickening analogy rest on three currents. Two of them (1100 ± 600 s and 1200 ± 500 s) are indistinguishable, and the third has a 75% error. This should be removed or substantiated.
- *Sub-threshold case.* Fig. 4 includes 0.26 mA, where by the paper's own description no filament forms. The statement "electrical switching induces a jamming transition" is therefore inconsistent.

**4. Measurement artifacts that mimic decorrelation are not excluded (Methods; Figs. 2–3).** Speckle decorrelation can arise from causes other than domain rearrangement:
- *Bragg peak motion from thermal expansion.* This is expected under Joule heating at 0.26–4 mA and during the 340 K run. The same applies to changes in the M1/R phase fraction.
- *Sample/beam drift.* With a 1.7 µm transverse coherence length, drift of the beam relative to the sample at the sub-micron level will decorrelate speckle.
- *Intensity changes interacting with the normalization.* Eq. 1 normalizes by ⟨I²⟩ over the whole acquisition, rather than by the standard per-frame ⟨I(t₁)⟩⟨I(t₂)⟩ over pixels.
- *X-ray-induced modification of VO₂.* X-ray-induced persistent metallization and defect creation in VO₂ have been reported. Here 2×10¹⁰ ph/s is focused on ~100 µm² for hours.

The authors should report, versus time and cycle number:
- integrated Bragg intensity;
- peak center-of-mass (2θ, ω) and peak width;
- the ROI and pixel count used;
- any drift correction.

They should also add:
- a matched no-current baseline at 313 K over the same duration (Fig. 2 provides only 298 and 340 K);
- a dose-dependence test (attenuated beam, or beam-off intervals) to rule out beam-induced dynamics.

**5. The effective sample temperature under current is unknown, so field and Joule-heating effects cannot be separated (Figs. 3–4; Discussion).**
- *Expected dissipation.* From Fig. 5b, the dissipated power is ~0.5 mW at 0.26 mA and ~8 mW at 4 mA in a 5 µm device. Substantial, inhomogeneous heating is expected, likely through T_c within the filament.
- *The paper's own interpretation.* The Discussion attributes the dynamics to Joule heating, and the sub-threshold "erasure" could simply be thermalization at a higher temperature.
- *Inconsistency.* The 313 K, 0.26 mA τ (35,000 s) lies between the 298 K and 340 K no-current values, consistent with heating. The 1000 s delay could also reflect stage or device thermal equilibration after the current or setpoint change.
- *What the authors should do:*
  - Extract the lattice temperature from the Bragg peak shift during each current run; the data already exist.
  - Provide stage temperature logs.
  - Perform a heating-only control that reproduces the same lattice temperature without current. This will show whether any of the effects are specific to electrical driving.

**6. The extracted timescales and the activation energy are not robust (Fig. 2; text).**
- *Stability limit.* The text gives the 298 K "experimental stability limit" as 8000 ± 3000 s, while Fig. 2c reports τ = 81,000 ± 3000 s. The 4 mA On-state τ (150,000 or 160,000 s) exceeds either value. A τ longer than the instrument stability limit cannot be attributed to the sample.
- *Arrhenius fit.* The fit appears to use only a few temperatures. It includes the 298 K point, which the authors call the stability limit, and it spans the MIT, where the 340 K data are explicitly non-stationary (decaying diagonal in Fig. 2b) and therefore unsuitable for a single τ.
- *Comparison with melting.* Comparing E_a/k_B ≈ 2700 K to the melting temperature of VO₂ has no physical basis: a domain-wall or nucleation barrier is not k_B·T_m.
- *What the authors should do:*
  - State the number of temperatures used.
  - Restrict the Arrhenius analysis to stationary data on one side of the transition.
  - Quote τ only where the decay is resolved within the window, or report lower bounds otherwise.
  - Remove or justify the melting-point argument.

**B. Should address**

**7. Number of devices and reproducibility.**
- All electrical-switching data come from a single irradiated device. The pristine device is relegated to unseen Supplemental Information.
- The central result (Fig. 3c,i) should be shown on at least two devices, with device-to-device spread. Repeated training runs on the same device (including after thermal cycling through T_c) would show whether the "learned" state is reproducible or history-dependent.
- The irradiation description is inconsistent: "central 5×5 µm region irradiated" versus "devices were globally irradiated" in Methods. The role of irradiation should be clarified, given that ref. 7 reports that irradiation enhances stochasticity.

**8. Phase specificity and the ROI.**
- The text says the diffraction comes from the 200_M1 and 011_R reflections. These have very similar d-spacings (~2.42 Å), but they separate as the phase fraction and strain change.
- Please specify which reflection(s) fall within the analyzed ROI at 313 K and under 4 mA, and show the full detector images.
- Au(111) (d ≈ 2.355 Å, 2θ ≈ 52° at 6 keV) lies within the detector's ~±2.5° angular acceptance at 861 mm. With a beam of 7.4 × 14.8 µm² that is larger than the 5 µm gap, the current-carrying Ti/Au electrodes are illuminated and heated. Confirm that no electrode scattering, or electrode thermal expansion, contributes to the ROI.

**9. Spatial origin of the signal.**
- The beam (7.4 × 14.8 µm²) is larger than the device. It therefore includes film under and beside the electrodes that carries little or no current, as well as unirradiated film.
- The coherence length (1.7 µm) is much smaller than the beam, giving partial coherence and ~2% contrast.
- A filament plausibly occupies a small fraction of the illuminated area. Estimate what fraction of scattering volume is affected by the filament and its surroundings, and how this dilutes or biases the correlations.
- A spatially resolved check would greatly strengthen the link between speckle correlation and filament location: a smaller beam scanned across the gap, or Bragg CDI/ptychography on a subset of states.

**10. Definition and normalization of ΔG(n,n+1) (Fig. 3i).**
- The authors note that absolute correlation values are setup-dependent and that only timescales are meaningful. Yet the learning claim relies on a normalized ΔG rising from 0 to 1, and the normalization is not defined.
- Adjacent-cycle correlation alone cannot distinguish convergence to a fixed configuration from a slowly drifting one. Please define the normalization and give the raw G values with noise floor and error bars.
- Also show G(n, n+k) for several k, and correlation against a fixed late reference cycle.
- The text refers to an "inset (orange data)" that does not exist.

**11. Missing data and an inconsistent current list.**
- The 1.7 mA data used in Fig. 4 are not shown anywhere: no two-time map, no g₂, no electrical state. From Fig. 5b, 1.7 mA is near I_on, so its switching state should be stated.
- The current is listed as 0.26 mA in Figs. 3–4 but 0.27 mA in the text.
- Fig. 5b is not labeled with its temperature. The XPCS runs are at 313 K, so it should be stated whether the thresholds were measured there.

**12. Interpretation of β.**
- β > 1 (compressed exponential) in XPCS is generally attributed to ballistic, stress-driven relaxation (e.g., Cipelletti and co-workers). The assignment "β = 1 liquid-like, β = 1.5 jammed" is not universal and needs a physical model for domains in VO₂.
- A transient drift in the strain or thermal field around the filament would produce ballistic-like dynamics without any jamming.
- The authors should discuss these alternatives. They should also explain why the mechanisms offered in the Discussion (increasing metallic fraction versus directed motion toward the filament) cannot be distinguished, since the M1/R peak intensities would directly test the first.

**13. Positioning relative to prior memory effects in VO₂.**
- Ramp-reversal memory, phase-boundary scarring and persistent metallic domains (refs. 9–11, 45, 46) already describe history-dependent nucleation.
- The paper should state clearly what is new beyond confirming such memory with XPCS. It should also say whether the "learning" observed is distinguishable from these known non-volatile metallic remnants.

**14. Domain-size estimate.** R_d = 2π/Q_FWHM = 20 nm does not account for broadening from the 45 nm film thickness, instrumental resolution, strain or mosaicity, and the direction of Q_FWHM is not stated. Please give the direction, the temperature and current at which it was measured, and the resolution correction.

**C. Minor**

15. Fig. 2 caption and Methods: the line perpendicular to the diagonal through t₁ = t₂ = t_age satisfies t₁ + t₂ = 2t_age, not t_age.

16. Eq. 3 is called a "single exponential" but is a stretched/compressed exponential. In Eq. 2, τ is used both as the averaging variable and, in Eq. 3, as the relaxation time; rename one. Eq. 1 should state the pixel and ROI averaging explicitly.

17. Cycling protocol: "turned on and off for 3 s" is inconsistent with the top axis of Fig. 3c (~1800 cycles in ~5000 s). State the On time, Off time, period, and the position of the 1 s exposure within the On phase.

18. Text quotes τ ≈ 150,000 ± 50,000 s while Fig. 3h gives 160,000 ± 50,000 s.

19. Text says "Bragg angle ∼50°" while Methods says 2θ = 50°.

20. The Discussion cites "lack of correlation between data at positive and negative times in Fig. 3b," but Fig. 3b contains almost no pre-switching data. Show this cross-block quantitatively.

21. The Fig. 1 caption describes panel c as covering the pulse sequences and the bottom panel. Label panels d and e separately. Also note that Fig. 1e is schematic, not data.

22. Report photon counts per pixel per frame, the number of ROI pixels, and how g₂ error bars are computed, given the low contrast.

23. Fig. 2d inset: label the axes with units and state the number of temperatures.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Combine the cycling XPCS with simultaneous per-cycle electrical metrics.** Use threshold voltage, delay and Off-state resistance, and vary the Off time systematically. This would directly test whether speckle correlation predicts electrical determinism, and whether the effect is learning or incomplete relaxation.
2. **Run a closed-loop train → sub-threshold erase → retrain protocol on at least two devices**, irradiated and pristine, with heating-only and no-current baselines at matched lattice temperature. Determine the lattice temperature from the Bragg peak shift.
3. **Revise the dynamical analysis.**
   - Use longer acquisitions or restrict claims to resolved decays.
   - Fit τ and β jointly per t_age and report confidence regions.
   - Track M1 and R intensities separately to test the "increasing metallic fraction" jamming mechanism.
4. **Add spatial resolution on a subset of states**, for example a scanned sub-micron beam or Bragg ptychography of the On state early and late in training. This would show that the correlated configuration is localized at the filament and not in electrodes or unbiased film.
