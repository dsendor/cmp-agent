1. ### 1. The abstract's "train-and-reset" claim is never demonstrated

The abstract and conclusion state that a sub-threshold current erases the trained state and "recovers stochastic switching," offering a route to in-device learning. The data do not test this:
- The only sub-threshold experiment (Fig. 3a/d/g, 0.26 mA at 313 K) is applied to the initial, untrained configuration.
- No measurement applies a sub-threshold current after the ~1800-cycle training and then resumes cycling.
- The Discussion itself uses conditional language: sub-threshold erasure "has the potential to increase the stochasticity … in particular if applied between two above-threshold current pulses." The claim is built by combining two separate experiments.

**Requested experiment:**
- Run train (4 mA, ~1000+ cycles) → sub-threshold erase (0.26 mA, ≥1000 s) → retrain.
- Show that ΔG(n,n+1) returns to the low, stochastic level after the erase step.
- Show whether retraining follows a similar or faster curve.
- Repeat the loop several times.
- Also test persistence: rest at zero bias for 10³–10⁴ s after training, then resume switching.

Without these, the abstract and conclusion should be rephrased as a hypothesis.

2. ### 2. "Learning" / deterministic filament formation is not distinguished from forming, degradation, heating, or beam effects

The rise and plateau of ΔG(n,n+1) after about 200–1000 cycles is the only evidence. Several alternative explanations fit it equally well:
- **(a) Electroforming or irreversible change.** Examples are oxygen-vacancy accumulation, Ga redistribution, electromigration, or a permanently metallic channel.
- **(b) Thermal steady state.** The device and substrate gradually heat up under 3 s-period cycling.
- **(c) X-ray dose accumulation.** Beam exposure could stabilize a configuration.
- **(d) Loss of switching contrast.** If the probed region stops fully switching, consecutive On patterns become trivially similar.

**Requested controls:**
- **Per-cycle electrical data across all ~1800 cycles.** Report V_th, hold voltage, R_on, R_off, and switching delay. Stochastic switching is fundamentally an electrical observable. Showing that the cycle-to-cycle variance of V_th decreases together with ΔG is the most important missing link. Currently Fig. 3d–f only illustrates the applied current waveform. Drift in R_off would reveal forming.
- **Off-state analysis.** Report the Off-state correlation ΔG_off(n,n+1) and the On-vs-Off cross-correlation, plus speckle contrast and integrated intensity per cycle. This rules out (d).
- **Beam-off training.** Keep the shutter closed except for brief snapshots, to address (c).
- **Protocol variation.** Vary the period (e.g., 1/3/10 s) and amplitude (just above threshold vs 4 mA). This tests whether convergence scales with event number, total charge, or heating time.
- **Timing information.** Give frame/pulse synchronization: with 1 s frames and 3 s cycles, does an "On" frame contain any Off-state time?
- **Definitions and labels.** Define the normalization of ΔG(n,n+1). Fix the inconsistent labeling between text and caption (the "inset (orange data)" vs panel i, and C(n,n+1) vs ΔG(n,n+1)).

Until these are addressed, "learning" should be softened, e.g., to "convergence of the filament configuration."

3. ### 3. Irradiation is a confound for deterministic filament formation; generality rests on one device

All switching and current-driven data come from a single Ga-irradiated device, and each condition appears to have been measured once.
- The authors' own citation [32] (Ghazikhanian et al., APL 2023) shows that focused-ion-beam irradiation is used to localize resistive switching.
- The convergence to a fixed filament may therefore reflect irradiation-defined pinning rather than intrinsic current-driven jamming.
- The pristine data are described as "equivalent" but appear only in the SI.
- Methods states devices were "globally irradiated" after the as-grown sample was characterized. It is unclear whether the pristine data come from a separate device or from the same device before irradiation.

**Requests:**
- Bring the pristine switching data, including repetitive-switching ΔG, into the main text, side by side with the irradiated data.
- Clarify the device history.
- Measure at least 2–3 devices per type and repeat key conditions (repetitive switching, 0.26 mA erasure).
- Ideally include an irradiation-dose series.

The title ("jamming-controlled") and the abstract's general statement that the initial domain distribution "controls stochasticity" are causal claims. They cannot be established from N = 1.

4. ### 4. "Initial domain distribution controls stochasticity" lacks a causal test

The claim implies that changing the initial domain configuration changes switching stochasticity. Only one initial state per device was examined.

**Requested experiment:** Prepare deliberately different initial configurations and show that cycle-1 ΔG and V_th variance change accordingly. Possible routes:
- slow cooling vs quenching through the MIT,
- pre-conditioning at different temperatures,
- different irradiation doses,
- pristine vs irradiated devices.

5. ### 5. Relaxation times far exceed the measurement window; fit uncertainties and instrument limits are not credible as reported

**Scale of the problem.** The window is about 10⁴ s. The reported τ values are 24,000 s (340 K), 35,000 s (0.26 mA), 81,000 s (298 K), and 150,000–160,000 s (4 mA). Only a small fraction of the g₂ decay is observed in each case. As a result:
- τ and β are strongly degenerate.
- An uncertainty such as 24,000±100 s almost certainly reflects fit covariance, not real uncertainty.

**The stability limit undermines several results.**
- The authors describe the 298 K value as possibly "the experimental stability limit."
- If so, the "stable" new state after 4 mA (τ ≈ 150k s, slower than that limit) is indistinguishable from instrument drift.
- The 0.26 mA τ (35k s) is within a factor of about 2 of it.

**Requests:**
- **Static reference.** Measure τ_instrument(T) under identical stage/temperature conditions over the same duration, e.g., from a substrate Bragg peak or a non-transforming reflection. Demonstrate that the claimed τ values are clearly faster.
- **Realistic uncertainties.** Report the fraction of decay observed and give confidence intervals from bootstrap/block resampling of G(t₁,t₂).
- **Model checks.** Show τ–β joint confidence contours, and compare β-fixed vs β-free fits (AIC/BIC).
- **Model-free metrics.** Add metrics such as 1 − g₂ at a fixed lag, or the initial slope of the two-time correlation, compared across conditions.
- **Longer runs.** Perform at least one longer acquisition (≈5×10⁴ s) to directly observe decay.
- **Two-time maps.** Show the full G(t₁,t₂) for every condition, including zero current at 313 K.

6. ### 6. The Arrhenius analysis (Ea = 230±70 meV) is weakly supported and internally inconsistent

**Data points and the 298 K value.**
- Only 298 K and 340 K are shown, and the number of temperatures in the Fig. 2d inset is not stated.
- A two-point estimate from τ = 81,000 s and 24,000 s gives about 250 meV, consistent with the quoted value. The fit therefore seems dominated by these two points.
- One of them is the possible instrument-limited point.
- The text gives τ(298 K) = 8000±3000 s while the figure gives 81,000 s. The text value would imply a negative activation energy.

**Physical justification.**
- 340 K essentially coincides with the VO₂ MIT (~341 K). A single activation law across phase coexistence is not obviously justified.
- Comparison to the melting temperature (2240 K vs 2700±800 K) is not a meaningful benchmark. Comparisons to domain-wall pinning, twin-boundary motion, or oxygen-vacancy migration energies would be more relevant.

**Requests:**
- Correct the 298 K value.
- State which temperatures were fitted.
- Exclude or justify the drift-limited point.
- Add at least 4–5 temperatures well below the MIT, with repeats.

The statement that this "confirms" XPCS sensitivity to domain dynamics should be softened accordingly.

7. ### 7. The jamming / shear-thickening interpretation is not statistically or physically supported

**The trend is not significant.** t_jam = 1100±600 s (0.26 mA), 1200±500 s (1.7 mA), and 4000±3000 s (4 mA) overlap within about 1σ. Three points, one dominated by its error bar, do not establish that larger currents slow the transition. The analogy to shear-thickening is therefore unsupported.

**β > 1 is not diagnostic of jamming.** Compressed exponentials also arise from:
- ordinary aging,
- stress relaxation in hard and soft glasses,
- slow drift or collective translation, including instrumental drift.

**The fitting procedure may create the effect.** Fitting a single shared τ while β varies with t_age forces any change in decay shape into β.

**Requests:**
- **Zero-current baseline.** Show β(t_age) with no current at 313 K. If β also rises to ~1.5, the effect is aging rather than current-induced jamming.
- **q-dependence of τ.** Bin the detector ROI around the Bragg peak to distinguish ballistic (τ ∝ 1/q), diffusive (τ ∝ 1/q²), and drift-like dynamics.
- **Dynamic heterogeneity.** Report a χ₄-like measure, e.g., the variance of the instantaneous correlation along the diagonal of G(t₁,t₂), which should peak near t_jam if the transition is real.
- **Current series.** Measure more currents (e.g., 0, 0.1, 0.26, 0.5, 1, 1.7, 2.5, 4 mA) with repeats.
- **Fit robustness.** Fit τ and β jointly per t_age window, and show t_jam is robust to window length.

8. ### 8. Joule heating is inferred only from a coincidence of timescales; beam effects are not controlled

The claim that current-driven timescales comparable to thermal ones are "suggesting Joule heating" lacks any temperature measurement. This matters because the authors also cite work arguing for non-thermal and electronic switching mechanisms (refs. [42], [44]) and thermal analyses (refs. [27]–[29]).

The XPCS data already contain an internal thermometer: the Bragg peak position, via thermal expansion and M1/R peak position.

**Requests:**
- **Device temperature.** Report peak position vs time and current (0.26, 1.7, 4 mA), calibrated against the zero-field temperature series. Optionally complement this with finite-element thermal modeling or Raman/thermoreflectance thermometry.
- **Beam effects.** Measure τ at 2–3 attenuator settings and report the dose per measurement, to exclude beam-driven dynamics or heating.
- **Sub-threshold literature.** Relate the sub-threshold results to prior sub-threshold work (ref. [45], del Valle et al., Nature 2019).

9. ### 9. Filament localization and stochasticity are inferred without real-space evidence; key measurement details are missing

**Signal dilution and contrast.**
- The 7.4×14.8 µm² beam overfills the 5×5 µm device, so the speckle includes electrodes and non-switching film. This dilutes the switching signal by an unknown amount.
- The 1.7 µm coherence length is much smaller than the device, implying low contrast.

**Missing details from Methods:**
- the speckle contrast A,
- which Bragg reflection was used (inferred d ≈ 2.44 Å, not stated),
- the ROI and q-range,
- pixel averaging in Eq. 1,
- exposure time, frame count, and drift correction,
- the threshold currents I₁/I₂ in Fig. 5b (no numbers given),
- the estimated volume fraction of the illuminated region that actually switches.

Without these, the interpretation of On-state correlation as filament position cannot be evaluated.

**Real-space corroboration.** On at least one device, check whether filament location is random early and fixed late, e.g., with nano-focused Bragg mapping or ptychography, operando/post-mortem s-SNOM, C-AFM, or thermoreflectance. The authors cite comparable approaches (refs. [22], [39], [40], [46]).

10. ### 10. Novelty framing should be qualified

The paper presents XPCS tracking of domain reconfiguration as a "novel approach." Prior dynamic structural and optical imaging of VO₂ filament formation exists (refs. [22], [39], [40], [46]), as does XPCS/aging work on domain boundaries (ref. [41]). The authors should clearly state what the XPCS approach provides beyond these, such as access to long-time correlations and cycle-resolved statistics.

12. **τ after 4 mA switching:** 150,000±50,000 s (text) vs 160,000 s (figure).

13. **Sub-threshold current:** 0.26 mA (text) vs 0.27 mA (Fig. 4 legend).

16. **Language:** soften the claims until the above is addressed: "jamming-controlled" → "consistent with jamming/aging-like dynamics," "learning" → "convergence," "confirms" → "suggests."

17. **1. Novelty relative to prior work is not stated.**
- The paper never contrasts its findings with prior X-ray imaging of filaments and domains in VO2 and V2O3 (nanodiffraction, dark-field and full-field X-ray microscopy: refs [20–22, 36–40]). The only contrast offered is that those methods are "typically slower."
- The memory claims are largely anticipated by refs [9–11]:
  - ramp-reversal memory;
  - persistent metallic domains;
  - phase-boundary scarring.
- They are also anticipated by:
  - [22], on volatile and non-volatile switching in VO2 imaged by X-rays;
  - [45], where persistent metallic domains "act as long lived memory and reduce switching stochasticity."
- [45] and [46] are cited as "corroboration," but they report essentially the same conclusion. The authors should state explicitly what is new beyond them.
- My reading is that the defensible novelty is methodological: second-to-hours tracking of configurational memory and convergence in a working device. The paper should be reframed around this.

18. **2. Prior work on compressed exponentials (β > 1) is missing, so the "jamming in hard matter" claim lacks context.**
- The bibliography contains no compressed-exponential XPCS literature:
  - Cipelletti et al. on colloidal gels;
  - Ruta et al. on metallic glasses;
  - hard-matter domain dynamics, e.g. antiferromagnetic domains in Cr.
- In that literature, β > 1 is generally attributed to stress-driven or ballistic relaxation and aging, not specifically to jamming.
- The criterion "β = 1.5 means glassy/jammed" is supported only by a general XPCS review (ref [12]).
- Closer hard-matter precedents in the authors' own list, [41] (domain-boundary dynamics) and [43] (heterogeneous dynamics in an oxide, a self-citation preprint), are not discussed as such.
- The statement "while jamming is expected in soft matter… here it is observed in hard matter" should be qualified or removed.

19. **3. The paper relies heavily on unrefereed preprints from the same group.**
- Key supporting references are [34], [43] and [46].
- Ref [7], also from the same group, reports *enhanced* stochasticity in irradiated VO2 oscillators. This sits in tension with the claimed convergence to deterministic switching and should be discussed.

20. **4. The motivation is inconsistent: is stochasticity a problem or a resource?**
- The introduction treats stochasticity as a problem to be suppressed.
- The abstract says that "recovering stochastic switching" offers "a path for in-device learning," but this is never explained.
- Stochasticity as a computational resource (random number generation, stochastic or probabilistic computing) is not discussed.
- The significance for neuromorphic hardware therefore remains vague.

21. **5. The jamming signature may be a fitting artefact.**
- Fits use a single τ for all aging times t_age, with only β allowed to vary, as both figure captions state.
- The fitted τ (tens of thousands of seconds, up to ~160,000 s) far exceeds the measurement window. Under these conditions, ordinary aging or slowly varying dynamics would be forced to appear as a change in β.
- The Methods define g2 and G normalized by ⟨I²⟩ rather than ⟨I⟩². This is inconsistent with the relation g2 = 1 + A|F|² used to extract |F|, so either a misprint or an error that affects β.
- Speckle contrast, noise floor and fit uncertainties or covariances are not reported. Low contrast is likely, given a speckle size of ~1.4 pixels and a 1.7 µm transverse coherence length against a 7.4×14.8 µm beam.
- The Methods also call the fit a "single exponential decay."
- **Requested:**
  - free (τ, β) fits with joint confidence regions and residuals;
  - a scaling collapse of the correlation versus t/τ;
  - corrected normalization formulas;
  - reported contrast.

22. **6. The account of what drives jamming contradicts the data.**
- The abstract and Discussion attribute jamming to "repetitive above-threshold currents" and to "electrical switching."
- Yet β rises at a sub-threshold current of 0.26 mA, where no filament forms.
- At 0.26 mA, the "memory erasure" onset (~1000 s, Fig. 3) and t_jam = 1100 ± 600 s (Fig. 4) coincide. They may be the same aging event described twice.

23. **7. The claimed current dependence and shear-thickening analogy are not supported.**
- The t_jam values overlap within error: 1100 ± 600, 1200 ± 500 and 4000 ± 3000 s.
- The 1.7 mA point has no g2 or two-time map shown, and the paper does not say whether it is above threshold.
- The statements "the larger the current, the slower the transition, reminiscent of shear-thickening" and "granular-like dynamics" are therefore not supported. More currents, repeats and a significance analysis are needed.

24. **8. The "learning / stochastic-to-deterministic" claim lacks independent corroboration.**
- Determinism is inferred only from correlations between successive On-state speckle patterns.
- No electrical switching statistics are shown versus cycle number: no threshold-voltage or delay distributions, and no On/Off resistance drift.
- The authors' own framing makes stochasticity an electrical-device property, so these data are essential.
- The quantitative metric ΔG(n, n+1) compares successive cycles only, and its normalization is undefined. A fixed-reference curve (correlation of each cycle with the final state) extracted from Fig. 3c would be more informative.

25. **9. Alternative explanations for the rising On–On correlation are not excluded.**
- **Irradiation pinning.** By the authors' own rationale and ref [32], irradiation defects guide filament formation.
- **Defect conditioning.** Ref [34] reports electrically induced persistent states in irradiated oxides.
- **Other candidates:** electroforming, oxygen-vacancy migration or electromigration, gradual device degradation, and simple persistent metallic patches ([45, 46]).
- **Uncontrolled history.** The device was thermally cycled and electrically switched up to ~4 mA *before* the "initial" state was measured, and no reset protocol is described.
- **Requested controls:**
  - quantify the pristine device in the main text rather than only in the Supplementary Information;
  - an erase step (heating above the transition) followed by retraining, testing whether the same or a different final configuration is reached;
  - a different cycling protocol;
  - Off-state frames. None are recorded, so filament changes cannot be separated from reconfiguration of the whole film.

26. **10. Spatial interpretation: what part of the device is probed is unclear.**
- The reflections are named: insulating 200 of M1 and metallic 011 of R (where M1 and R denote the insulating and metallic VO2 phases).
- At ~2θ = 50° and 6 keV, however, these peaks nearly coincide (d ≈ 2.42 vs 2.41 Å). The paper does not say whether they are resolved on the detector, nor does it separate phase-specific dynamics.
- The 20 nm domain size comes from "the measured Bragg peak" without saying which phase. It ignores strain and instrumental broadening and is not compared with grain size.
- There is no Bragg peak position or centre-of-mass tracking, and thermal expansion or Joule-induced peak shifts are not discussed.
- The beam is larger than the device, and the filament location is never determined.
  - "In and around the conductive filament" is therefore asserted rather than shown.
  - The filament's volume fraction of the illuminated region is unknown.
  - Matrix aging could thus dominate the On–On correlation.
- The conclusion that "the initial distribution controls the stochasticity of the filament formation" asserts a causal link that is not measured.
- The text says "Bragg angle ∼50°," which, as reported, must mean 2θ.

27. **11. The Arrhenius and Joule-heating arguments are weak, and the melting-point comparison should be removed.**
- **Two-point fit.** Ea = 230 ± 70 meV comes from only two temperatures.
  - One (298 K) is described as the "experimental stability limit," so it is effectively a bound.
  - The other (340 K) is described as non-stationary.
  - Both τ values exceed the measurement window.
- **Unphysical prefactor.** Using the figure values (81,000 s and 24,000 s), the implied attempt time is τ₀ ≈ 5 s, about 12 orders of magnitude slower than phonon timescales. The fitted Ea is therefore not a microscopic barrier, and the comparison with the melting temperature (2240 K) has no physical basis.
- **Erasure not explained.** With τ₀ ≈ 5 s, thermally activated reconfiguration cannot produce the <1 s erasure at 4 mA. That erasure is more plausibly the first-order transition itself, so the single "Joule heating" narrative needs two distinct mechanisms.
- **Heating argument is only an analogy.**
  - "Joule heating" is supported only by "comparable timescales," with no estimate of dissipated power or local temperature.
  - Extrapolating the authors' Arrhenius law gives τ(313 K) ≈ 51,000 s, against 35,000 s measured at 0.26 mA. This would imply only ~13 K of heating, a testable figure against I²R and a thermal model.
- **Conflicting citation.** The authors cite [42] (Kalcheim et al., "Non-thermal resistive switching in Mott insulator nanowires") in support of thermal switching. They should explain how they reconcile this.

28. **12. Beam effects are not addressed.**
- The flux is 2×10¹⁰ ph/s on ~110 µm², giving an estimated dose of ~10²⁰ ph/cm² over 10⁴ s. Neither the dose nor any attenuation is reported.
- Beam damage, beam heating and beam–current synergy are not discussed. Examples of synergy include photo-assisted vacancy motion and photoconduction.
- The stable pre-current baseline is a partial beam-only control and should be presented explicitly as such. Repeats at attenuated flux would strengthen the case.

29. **13. Statistics are limited.**
- Results come from essentially one irradiated device at one dose, plus one pristine device shown only in the Supplementary Information.
- No repeats and no device-to-device variability are reported.
- Generalizing to "VO2 neuromorphic devices" requires more devices and doses.

30. **14. Biological terms are undefined or misapplied.**
- "Short-term memory" refers to states lasting ~10³–10⁴ s.
- "Plasticity" conflates mechanical (plastic deformation) and synaptic meanings.
- "This demonstrates that the material learns" is an overclaim based on rising correlation alone.
- "Synapses" are proposed without any conductance or weight readout.
- The authors should define these terms operationally or tone them down.

31. **15. The timescale mismatch is unacknowledged.**
- Filament nucleation occurs on ps–µs timescales, while XPCS resolution is 1 s, so each frame integrates over a fully formed filament.
- The link between the slow domain configuration and fast switching statistics is assumed rather than demonstrated. This should be discussed as a limitation.

32. **16. Several numbers and notations are inconsistent or wrong.**
- **Values that disagree:**
  - τ(298 K) is 8000 s in the text but 81,000 s in Fig. 2;
  - τ is 150,000 s in the text but 160,000 s in Fig. 3h;
  - the current is 0.27 mA in the text but 0.26 mA in the figures.
- **Notation and terminology errors:**
  - a time in seconds is called a "decay rate";
  - "t₁ + t₂ = t_age" (should be 2t_age) appears in the main text, the Fig. 2 caption and the Methods, alongside the conflicting definition t_age = t₁ = t₂;
  - the fit is called a "single exponential decay";
  - the setup is described as a "four-probe station" although the devices are two-terminal.
- **Overstated or unclear statements:**
  - "Stable for tens of thousands of seconds" extrapolates beyond the ~7000 s axis of Fig. 3b.
  - The cycle timing ("on and off for 3 s") and where the 1 s frame falls within the On state are ambiguous.

33. **1. The probed volume is not defined relative to the device.**
- The focused beam (7.4 × 14.8 µm²) is larger than the "5 × 5 µm²" channel.
- The Methods give 2θ = 50°, so θ ≈ 25°. For a symmetric reflection, the footprint along the beam is about 18–35 µm.
- Much of the speckle signal may therefore come from VO2 outside the channel or near the Ti/Au electrodes. Only a small fraction would come from the filament.
- The paper says only that "the X-ray beam is kept as large as the measured devices." Please state:
  - the scattering geometry and actual incidence angle;
  - which beam axis lies in the scattering plane;
  - the footprint on the sample;
  - how the beam was aligned to the channel;
  - an estimate of the fraction of signal from the channel and from the filament.
- Without this, changes in the speckle cannot be attributed to filament formation.

34. **2. How the Bragg reflections are assigned to phases is not explained.**
- The M1 (200) and rutile (011) reflections nearly coincide (d ≈ 2.42 Å, 2θ ≈ 50.5° at 6 keV). Please explain:
  - how the two reflections were resolved and assigned;
  - which reflection was used for Figs. 3 and 4.
- The main text calls 2θ the "Bragg angle ∼50°", which conflicts with the Methods (2θ = 50°). Please correct the main text.
- Please state what the ~20 nm "nanodomains" are: M1 domains, metallic domains, or M1 twin/rotational variants.
- Please give the epitaxial relationship on R-cut sapphire and the expected variants.

35. **3. Speckle quality and coherence are not demonstrated.**
- The estimated speckle size (≈12–24 µm) is smaller than the 75 µm pixel, so the pattern is strongly undersampled.
- The beam is several transverse coherence lengths (1.7 µm) wide.
- Please report:
  - the contrast A, which is defined but never given;
  - the oversampling;
  - photons per pixel per frame;
  - the noise floor of G.
- Raw G spans only about 1.01–1.03. Please show that the variations exceed noise, and include representative detector images.

36. **4. The claim that the speckle "encode[s] the full real-space distribution" is overstated.**
- Phase information is lost in an intensity measurement.
- Please rephrase, or explain how the real-space configuration could be recovered.

37. **5. The domain size Rd = 2π/Q_FWHM = 20 ± 4 nm needs more detail.** Please state:
- the direction along which the Q-width is measured;
- which reflection was used;
- whether resolution and strain-broadening corrections were applied;
- whether the size is lateral or limited by the 45 nm film thickness.

38. **6. Equations 1 and 2 are incomplete and internally inconsistent.**
- Normalizing by ⟨I²⟩ gives g2(0) = 1, which conflicts with g2 = 1 + A|F|². Please give the correct normalization, presumably ⟨I⟩².
- Please specify:
  - ROI and pixel averaging, including ROI size and position;
  - background subtraction;
  - flux normalization;
  - whether the ROI tracked peak shifts.
- τ is used both as the dummy variable in Eq. 2 and as the relaxation time in Eq. 3. Please use separate symbols.

39. **7. The two-time geometry is defined inconsistently.**
- The diagonal is defined as t1 = t2 = t_age, so the anti-diagonal cut should be t1 + t2 = 2t_age, not "t1 + t2 = t_age".
- Please define t_age = 0 for every dataset. It is undefined for Fig. 2.

40. **8. The fitting of Eq. 3 is unclear.**
- Eq. 3, exp[−(t/τ)^β], is a stretched/compressed exponential; with β = 1.5 it is compressed. The Methods call it "a single exponential decay function". Please describe it accurately.
- The captions and Methods state that "a single τ value" is used for all t_age. Please clarify:
  - whether τ is fitted globally while β is free for each cut;
  - the fit range in t;
  - how uncertainties are obtained.
- Please discuss the τ–β covariance and the bias that fixing τ introduces into β(t_age).

41. **9. Relaxation times are extrapolated beyond the measurement windows without saying so.**
- All reported τ values (about 24,000–160,000 s) far exceed the measurement windows of at most about 10⁴ s.
- Please state that these are extrapolations, assess their reliability, and report the actual measurement durations.

42. **10. ∆G(n,n+1) has no formula.**
- Please give its definition, normalization, ROI and noise floor, and the values that correspond to "uncorrelated" and "identical" configurations.
- The paper says only timescales, not absolute correlation values, are meaningful in this setup. Yet the learning claim rests on ∆G levels. Please reconcile these.

43. **11. The sigmoid used to extract t_jam is not specified.** Please give its functional form, the fit parameters, and how the error bars were obtained.

44. **12. Decorrelation not caused by the sample is not addressed.**
- Several mechanisms could mimic "erasure", "training" or "jamming":
  - stage or beam drift over hours to days;
  - Bragg-peak shifts or tilts from Joule heating or strain at 1.7 and 4 mA;
  - X-ray dose effects (2 × 10¹⁰ ph/s over 10⁴–10⁵ s);
  - irreversible device changes such as electromigration, oxygen-vacancy redistribution or Ga-defect evolution.
- Please report controls:
  - beam-off or intermittent exposure;
  - dose dependence;
  - tracking of peak position and intensity;
  - resistance stability.
- The paper says the 298 K data "provide the slowest observable dynamics in our material or the experimental stability limit." Please quantify the stability limit, for example with a static reference sample, so the two cases can be told apart.

45. **13. The switching-cycle protocol is ambiguous.**
- The text says "on and off for 3 s"; the caption says "every three seconds". Please give the On and Off durations explicitly.
- Please describe how the 1 s exposure was synchronized with the On state, including triggering and settling delay.
- Please use consistent cycle and time axes. The cycle period cannot be reliably inferred from the paper.
- The introduction says relaxation after large currents can take hours. Please discuss whether the short Off period allows meaningful relaxation between cycles.

46. **14. Current levels are not placed relative to the switching thresholds.**
- Please give numeric values of the switching currents I1 and I2.
- Please state whether a filament forms at 1.7 mA. This current appears only in the jamming analysis (Fig. 4).
- Please give the voltage and power at each current. Fig. 5b suggests about 5–10 mW at 4 mA.
- Joule heating is used to explain the jamming trend, so please estimate the local temperature rise.

47. **15. Electrical and environmental details are missing.**
- Please give the temperature-control hardware, sensor location, and stability at 313 K and 340 K.
- Please give the compliance voltage of the Keithley 2612B constant-current source.

52. **20. The jamming interpretation is not supported as stated.**
- **Overlapping t_jam values.**
  - The t_jam values appear only in the Fig. 4 legend, not in the main text. They are 1100 ± 600, 1200 ± 500 and 4000 ± 3000 s, read as 0.26, 1.7 and 4 mA respectively.
  - The 4 mA ↔ 4000 s pairing is the most plausible reading of the legend and matches the text's ordering. Please state these values explicitly in the text.
  - The values overlap within errors, so "the larger the current, the slower the transition" is not established.
- **Tension with the training claim.**
  - The Discussion attributes the 4 mA training (deterministic switching after t ≈ 1000 s, cycle ≈ 200) to the "jamming transition that occurs on … ∼1000 s". However, the central t_jam for 4 mA is 4000 ± 3000 s, and the paper's own trend makes 4 mA the slowest.
  - The large error bar does not formally exclude ~1000 s. The comparison also crosses protocols: t_jam comes from continuous current, while the training (Fig. 3c,i) uses on/off cycling. Please state:
    - whether t_jam under continuous current is expected to apply to cycled operation;
    - whether "time" in the training means wall-clock time or cumulative On-time. The duty cycle is not stated (see item 13). If the current is On for only part of each cycle (e.g., ~1/2 for 3 s on/3 s off), cumulative On-time is substantially shorter than 1000 s.
  - The paper notes that jamming and sub-threshold erasure share timescales of thousands of seconds, but does not discuss whether this coincidence is causal.
- **Weak support for the β criteria.**
  - The β = 1.5 "jammed/glassy" criterion is cited only to ref 12, a general XPCS review (Shpyrko, J. Synchrotron Radiat. 2014).
  - The only other support for the glassy interpretation is ref 43. This is an unreviewed arXiv preprint on a different material system (a perovskite-to-brownmillerite topotactic transformation), and it shares the first author with this paper.
  - The β = 1 "liquid-like" assignment is uncited.
  - In the XPCS literature, compressed exponentials (β > 1) are usually linked to ballistic, stress-relaxation-driven dynamics in jammed systems. Glassy dynamics typically give stretched exponentials (β < 1).
  - The "jammed/glassy" terminology therefore conflates two regimes. It appears in the abstract, and the introduction uses equivalent wording ("jamming transition from liquid-like to glassy").
  - With a single Q measured, the τ(Q) scaling needed to distinguish diffusive ("liquid-like") from ballistic dynamics cannot be checked.
  - Please explain the physical basis of both criteria and cite primary sources. Candidates are the jamming/glass references already in the paper (refs 13–17) and specific XPCS studies of compressed relaxation.
- **Analogies.** The shear-thickening and granular-matter analogies are asserted rather than argued.
