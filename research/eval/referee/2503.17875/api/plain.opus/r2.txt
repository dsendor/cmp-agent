# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** "Heavy fermions, mass renormalization and local moments in magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene via planar tunneling spectroscopy" (Zhang, Wu, *et al.*)

---

## 1. Summary of the submission

The authors use a planar tunnel junction to probe MATBG near 1.06–1.08°. The junction consists of a graphite ribbon, ~2 nm of hBN, and the MATBG sample, with an Au top gate. The tunnel capacitance is large (C_b ≈ 1.3 µF/cm²), so the bias electrode also acts as a gate. The authors exploit this: by measuring both dI/dV_b and dI/dV_g they extract the inverse compressibility dµ/dn. From dµ/dn they derive three further quantities:

- **Chemical potential µ(n).** This gives a ~10 meV gap at the charge neutrality point (CNP), a ~1.3 meV correlated gap at ν = 2 with an anomalous g-factor of 5.4, and Landau level (LL) and Chern insulator (CI) fans.
- **"Cyclotron effective mass" m*(n).** Obtained via m* = 2πħ²/(g′ dµ/dn), it swings between ~0.6 mₑ just below positive integer fillings and ~0.06 mₑ just above. The authors interpret this as the f-like vs c-like character of charge-one excitations in the topological heavy-fermion (THF) model.
- **Entropy S(ν, T).** Obtained via the Maxwell relation, it shows two plateaus near 10 K and 20 K. These are matched to kB ln C(4,|ν|) and kB ln C(8, ν+4) counting of free isospin local moments. A strained atomic-limit THF calculation is invoked to explain the 4→8 crossover.

The central new claims are:
1. Planar tunneling spectroscopy (PTS) gives quantitative access to dµ/dn.
2. The mass asymmetry across integer fillings supports the heavy-fermion picture.
3. Entropy plateaus provide evidence for 4- and 8-fold degenerate local-moment regimes split by heterostrain.

## 2. Overall assessment

The experimental platform is attractive. Measuring both dI/dV_b and dI/dV_g in an ultra-stable planar junction and combining them to obtain a thermodynamic quantity is a genuinely useful idea. The low-temperature data are of good quality, in particular:
- the dµ/dn peak at ν = 2,
- the Landau fans, and
- the correspondence between dark lines in dI/dV_b and dµ/dn maxima (Fig. 2f,g).

These largely reproduce known SET/transport phenomenology in a new measurement geometry, which is itself a validation of the method.

However, the manuscript's main physics claims (heavy-fermion mass renormalization and entropic evidence for local moments) rest on a chain of approximations. Several of these are not justified or tested, and some internal features of the data argue against the interpretation as presented. I detail these below. In its current form I cannot recommend publication. I would be happy to see a revised version that addresses the points under "Major issues."

---

## 3. Major issues

### 3.1 Validity and robustness of the dµ/dn extraction

All downstream results (µ, m*, S) depend on dµ/dn, so this deserves much more scrutiny.

**(a) Neglect of ρ(E_F) in Eq. (3).** The exact result derived in the Methods (Eq. 7) is

dµ/dn = e (dI_n/dV_g) / [I₀ C_g (ρ(µ+eV_b) − ρ(µ))].

The main-text Eq. (3) follows from it only if ρ(µ) is neglected relative to ρ(µ+eV_b). Within a flat band this is the opposite of the natural regime: ρ(E_F) is large and strongly filling-dependent, and it is closely tied to the compressibility being measured. Neglecting it introduces a filling-dependent multiplicative error in dµ/dn. That error propagates directly into m* (which is ∝ 1/(dµ/dn)) and into S. The authors should:
- justify the approximation quantitatively, or
- use the full expression with an independent estimate of ρ(E_F), and show how much the results change.

It is also unclear how the phonon gap enters. The gap suppresses the *tunneling* signal near E_F, not the sample DOS that appears in the formula. If the argument is that inelastic thresholds shift the integration limits, Eq. (1) should be rewritten for phonon-assisted tunneling, and the derivation redone.

**(b) Dependence on the chosen bias.** dµ/dn is a property of the sample and should not depend on V_b. It is extracted at a single bias, V_b = ±42 mV. A key consistency check is missing: extracting dµ/dn at several biases (e.g., 35, 42, 50, 60 mV) and showing that the curves collapse. Without this, it is hard to separate genuine compressibility features from spectral features of ρ(µ+eV_b), such as the flat-band peaks that sit near ±40 mV.

**(c) Transmission normalization.** The empirical background T(n) ∝ exp(0.258 n/n₀) enters dI_n/dV_g through T′(n)/T(n), which is not small compared with the signal of interest. Please show:
- how the exponent was determined,
- its uncertainty, and
- the sensitivity of dµ/dn, m* and S to it.

This is especially important at elevated temperature, where any temperature dependence of T(n) feeds directly into ∂µ/∂T.

**(d) Bias-induced doping.** Because C_b ≫ C_g, the probe gates the sample very strongly: C_b · 60 mV / e ≈ 5×10¹¹ cm⁻² ≈ 0.7 n₀. Consequences:
- A dI/dV_b spectrum at fixed V_g traverses a large fraction of a filling interval.
- The region under the 1 µm² probe is doped differently from the surrounding sample, so lateral density gradients exist at the probe perimeter.
- The energy axis is µ(n(V_b)) + eV_b, not eV_b, which matters for the LL mass fits.

The shearing in Fig. 1e handles the first point only partially. Please discuss the edge-region contribution and the energy-axis correction explicitly.

**(e) Filling correction at ν = ±4.** Before correction the band-edge peaks appear ~0.2 away from ±4. They are then moved to ±4 by a quantum-capacitance correction described only in the SI. Three things are needed:
- The density calibration (n = 0.30×10¹² V_g cm⁻²) is itself taken from LL fans, which already includes an average quantum capacitance. Please clarify why an additional correction is needed and how it is applied without double-counting.
- Please also clarify how the procedure avoids simply forcing the peaks onto the expected values.
- Show that the bias-doping term (d) is not the origin of the 0.2 offset.

### 3.2 The "effective mass" analysis

**(a) Compressibility is not the quasiparticle DOS in an interacting system.** In a Fermi liquid, dµ/dn = (1 + F₀ˢ)/N*(0), where F₀ˢ is the symmetric Landau parameter. Strong exchange contributions are known to make dµ/dn negative in MATBG, and the authors observe this themselves. Eq. (4) therefore does not measure a cyclotron mass in this regime. Concretely:
- The "heavy" values (≥0.6 mₑ) correspond to dµ/dn ≈ 2×10⁻¹² meV·cm², i.e., to the zero crossings of dµ/dn.
- Where dµ/dn < 0, Eq. (4) gives negative or divergent masses.

The "factor of 10 renormalization" is thus largely a re-plotting of dµ/dn near its zeros. It cannot be taken as an independent measurement of quasiparticle mass.

**(b) Degeneracy assumption.** g′ = 4 is assumed at all fillings. Yet the cascade and Dirac-revival picture, which the authors invoke, implies that the effective flavor degeneracy changes across integer fillings. A change of g′ from 4 to, say, 1 changes m* by a factor of 4, which is comparable to the claimed effect.

**(c) Discriminating power.** The manuscript states that the observed asymmetry is "consistent with both symmetry-broken and symmetric phases." The dip-below/peak-above dµ/dn sequence was already reported and explained by flavor-polarization cascades (Zondiner et al., Wong et al., Saito et al.). It is therefore unclear what the present data add as *specific* support for the THF picture. Possible approaches:
- A quantitative comparison with THF predictions for dµ/dν (not just at ν = 2, Fig. 3c), including the hole side.
- Evidence beyond what cascade models already capture.

Absent this, I suggest reframing the claims as "consistent with" rather than evidence for heavy fermions.

**(d) Hole side.** THF predicts a mirrored structure at negative fillings, yet the hole-side behavior is barely discussed. Fig. 2a shows weaker features there. A side-by-side analysis would strengthen or constrain the interpretation.

**(e) Comparison with bilayer graphene masses.** Comparing ~0.06 mₑ to bilayer graphene (0.03–0.05 mₑ) is misleading. The low-mass points coincide with dµ/dn peaks, which the authors elsewhere associate with incipient gaps. A large inverse compressibility near a gap does not imply a light band.

### 3.3 Entropy and the local-moment claim

This is the headline result, and it needs the most work.

**(a) Non-monotonic S(T).** In Fig. 4c and Extended Fig. 4c the extracted entropy *decreases* with increasing temperature over several intervals, beyond the error bars:
- ν = 2: from ≈4.3 k_B at ~17 K to ≈3.6 k_B at ~22 K;
- ν = 3: from ≈1.9 k_B at ~6 K to ≈1.3 k_B at ~8 K;
- ν = 0 and ν = 1 show similar behavior near 20–24 K.

dS/dT < 0 means a negative specific heat at fixed ν, which violates thermodynamic stability. This strongly suggests that systematic errors are present at the level of the claimed "plateaus." Candidate sources include:
- temperature-dependent tunneling normalization;
- thermal activation of the 20 meV phonon channel (Bose factor at T ≳ 20 K);
- thermal smearing not included in the T = 0 Eq. (1);
- the three-point derivative scheme.

The authors must address this explicitly. The plateau structure should only be claimed if it survives once these artifacts are understood or removed.

**(b) Reference choice and closure test.** S is obtained by integrating from ν = 4 with S(4) = 0, after fixing µ(CNP) = 0 at every temperature. Fixing µ(CNP) = 0 is equivalent to assuming ∂µ/∂T = 0, i.e., ∂S/∂ν = 0, at the CNP. That is a particle-hole symmetry assumption, which is not justified in a strongly e–h asymmetric system. Any T-dependent offset c(T) adds −(ν − 4) c′(T) to S(ν, T). Two checks are needed:
- Integrate across to ν = −4 and verify that S(−4) ≈ 0 at each temperature (a closure test).
- Show hole-side S(ν) data. Currently Fig. 4b shows only model curves on the hole side.

**(c) Model comparison is overstated.** The text states that the strained atomic-limit calculation (Fig. 4d) "agrees well with the two observed entropy plateaus." However, the calculated S(T) at ν = 1, 2, 3 in Fig. 4d is a smooth, nearly linear increase from ~5 K to 40 K, with no visible plateau near 20 K. In addition:
- U₁ = 23 meV and ε = 0.001 were tuned to match the data.
- The strain was not independently determined.
- The model is acknowledged to fail below ~6 K and to neglect f–c hybridization and inter-site correlations, precisely the ingredients that determine whether the moments are "free."

Please either quantify the agreement (e.g., overlay the data and model on the same axes) or soften the claim.

**(d) Counting-model consistency.** At the 10 K plateau the measured value at ν = 2 (~1.3 k_B) lies below ln 6 ≈ 1.79. At ~20 K the ν = 3 value (~2.8–3 k_B) lies well above ln 8 ≈ 2.08. Please tabulate measured vs. predicted plateau values with uncertainties for all fillings.

**(e) Sharpness of the crossover.** If a strain-induced splitting Δ separates two groups of four flavors, the 4→8 crossover should be a smooth, Schottky-like increase spread over a temperature range ~Δ/k_B. The observed rise is ~2.5 k_B between ~12 K and ~16 K, flanked by flat regions. Please estimate Δ from the strain parameters and show that it is compatible with this sharpness.

**(f) Relation to prior work.** Rozen et al. and Saito et al. already reported ~1 k_B per moiré cell near ν ≈ 1 at ~10 K and interpreted it as free isospin moments (Pomeranchuk effect). The novelty here is the second (8-fold) regime. That regime is exactly where the methodological concerns (a)–(b) are most acute, so it requires correspondingly stronger evidence.

### 3.4 Phonon-assisted tunneling and the energy axis

- **Fermi level vs. phonon threshold.** The statement "E_F = µ(n) ~ ±22 mV" conflates the inelastic threshold with the chemical potential. The phonon threshold shifts the spectral features by ħω_ph; it does not move E_F. Please clarify the energy-axis definition used throughout. Fig. 1f uses "eV_b ± ħω_ph," but the text and figures elsewhere use eV_b.
- **Misalignment angle.** Determining the probe-sample misalignment (18°) from AFM of flake edges is ambiguous: zigzag vs. armchair edges give 18° vs. 12° modulo 30°. The phonon energy also depends on this angle. Please explain how the ambiguity was resolved and how the ZA phonon energy at Δk ≈ 0.53 Å⁻¹ was determined.
- **Energy resolution.** The "high energy resolution" claim should be quantified, including phonon linewidth/dispersion broadening and the 0.2–1 mV modulation. This is relevant for the flat-band LL analysis, where ħω_c ≈ 0.7 meV at 1 T for m* = 0.174 mₑ.

### 3.5 Reproducibility

Only one device appears to be reported. Given the reliance on empirical normalizations and corrections, data from a second device, ideally with a different misalignment angle and therefore a different phonon threshold, would greatly strengthen the claims. At minimum, data at a second probe location or barrier thickness would help.

## 4. Additional physics comments

1. **g-factor at ν = 2.** g = 5.4 ± 0.2 is much larger than the bare spin value. The authors interpret the linear decrease as indicating a spin-unpolarized ground state, but do not discuss why g is ~2.7× too large. Spin-skyrmion excitations (their Ref. 20) or orbital/valley Zeeman contributions are natural candidates and should be discussed. The constant gap below 1.5 T should also be explained.
2. **CNP gap.** The 10 meV CNP gap is large compared with typical SET values. Please comment on possible contributions from hBN alignment or from the probe region, and on whether the value is bias-dependent.
3. **LL terminology.** E_N − E₀ = ħω_c√(N(N−1)) with spacing linear in B is the *massive chiral (bilayer-like)* spectrum, not that of "massive Dirac fermions." A gapped Dirac spectrum has √B dependence at large N. The x-axis of Extended Fig. 2e is labeled √(N(N+1))B in the text and √(N(N−1))B in the caption.
4. **Fig. 4e.** The non-monotonic T-dependence near ν = 1 and its "HFL" and "LM" labels are not discussed in the main text. Either develop this or remove the labels.

## 5. Minor issues and inconsistencies

**Twist angle**
- The angle is given as 1.06 ± 0.02° (main text), 1.08° (Fig. 1 caption), and 1.07° (Methods). Please reconcile.
- The Methods formula θ = a(2/(√3 n₀))^{1/2} is dimensionally incorrect. It should be θ = a(√3 n₀/2)^{1/2}, which with n₀ = 0.67×10¹² cm⁻² indeed gives 1.07°.

**Temperature claims**
- The persistence of mass renormalization is quoted as "~50 K" (text, attributed to Fig. 3b), "~77 K" (Fig. 3d caption), and "~70 K" (elsewhere).
- Fig. 3b shows only 0.3 K and 4.2 K data; the text appears to mean Fig. 3d.

**Internal contradictions**
- dµ/dn is said to peak "just below the next integer filling," while m* (∝ 1/(dµ/dn)) is said to be minimal "just above integer fillings."
- LL degeneracy lifting is quoted as "above 3.5 T" (main text) vs. "above 1.5 T" (Methods).

**Equations**
- Methods equation numbering is inconsistent: "(1)," "(5)," "solving equations (4) and (5)," then (11)–(13).
- Main-text Eq. (3) differs from Methods Eq. (7) without the approximation being stated.

**Figure captions**
- Extended Fig. 2 caption: "high compressibility of gaps between LLs" should read *low* compressibility (high inverse compressibility).
- The entropy expressions k_B ln(8 over ν+4) and k_B ln(4 over |ν|) are typeset as fractions rather than binomial coefficients.

**References**
- Duplicates: Ref. 1 = Ref. 30; Ref. 28 = Ref. 60; Ref. 23 ≈ Ref. 57.
- Ref. 4 has a garbled author list.
- Ref. 27 is cited in the Methods for tunneling-model assumptions but is a Kondo-lattice theory paper.
- The statement that "the entropy at the CNP saturates to zero below 12 K, as also observed in SET measurements" needs a citation.

**Typos and names**
- "Wanatabe" → Watanabe; "Seeback" → Seebeck; "filings" → fillings; "valance" → valence; "hightemperature," "zerohybridization" (spacing).

**Fig. 1**
- The band separation is quoted as both 16 mV and ~15 mV; clarify whether these refer to different quantities.
- Panel d labels should match the text's ZA-phonon assignment.

**Capacitance**
- The extracted C_g ≈ 0.048 µF/cm² is about half the geometric estimate for 30 nm hBN (~0.1 µF/cm²). Please comment, e.g., on actual thickness or quantum-capacitance effects.

## 6. Requested additions (summary)

1. dµ/dn extracted at multiple V_b values, showing collapse; full Eq. (7) analysis including ρ(E_F).
2. Sensitivity analysis of dµ/dn, m*, and S to the T(n) normalization and the quantum-capacitance correction.
3. Hole-side dµ/dn, m*, and entropy, with a closure test S(−4) ≈ 0 at each temperature.
4. A discussion, and ideally removal, of the regions with dS/dT < 0; finite-temperature treatment of Eq. (1) and of the phonon-assisted channel.
5. A quantitative overlay of the measured S(T) with the model, a table of plateau values, and an independent estimate of the heterostrain.
6. Reframing of the m* results in terms of compressibility, acknowledging Landau-parameter and degeneracy effects, or a justification for interpreting them as quasiparticle masses.
7. Data from a second device or probe location.

---

## 7. Recommendation

**Major revision.** The planar-tunneling route to dµ/dn is promising, and the low-temperature results are valuable as a demonstration of the technique. However, the heavy-fermion mass and local-moment entropy conclusions are not yet supported at the level claimed. The extraction relies on untested approximations, the effective mass is not separable from interaction contributions to the compressibility, and the entropy curves contain thermodynamically inconsistent features in the same temperature window as the key plateaus. Addressing these points could make this a strong contribution.
