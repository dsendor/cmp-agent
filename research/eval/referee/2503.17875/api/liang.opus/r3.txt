**Review outline:**

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Scope of the work.** The manuscript applies planar tunneling spectroscopy (PTS) with a graphite probe through a ~2 nm hBN barrier to magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene (MATBG, θ ≈ 1.06–1.08°). Combining dI/dV_b with dI/dV_g, the authors extract:
  - the inverse compressibility dμ/dn;
  - the chemical potential μ(n);
  - a "cyclotron" effective mass m*(n);
  - the entropy S(n,T), via the Maxwell relation.
- **Three central claims.**
  - (i) A strong heavy-to-light mass asymmetry across positive integer fillings, interpreted as f-like holes and c-like electrons in the topological heavy-fermion (THF) model.
  - (ii) A correlated gap at ν = 2 (Δ ≈ 1.3 meV, g ≈ 5.4).
  - (iii) Two entropy plateaus near 10 K and 20 K. These are interpreted as 4-fold and 8-fold degenerate isospin local-moment manifolds, split by heterostrain (ε ≈ 0.001).
- **Methodological novelty.** Using a large-area tunnel junction, whose large C_b makes the compressibility term in dI/dV_b non-negligible, is an elegant twist on PTS. In principle it gives spectroscopy and thermodynamics from one device. The gate-modulation scheme (Eq. 3) extends SET-style analysis by including the bias-electrode charging.
- **Physical novelty.** The second entropy plateau and its attribution to strain-split 8-fold f-moments would be new if established.
  - Much of the remaining phenomenology is already documented: cascades, sawtooth dμ/dn, negative compressibility, heavy/light asymmetry, and the Pomeranchuk-like entropy of ~1–2 k_B per moiré cell.
  - These were reported by STM (Wong et al.; Nuckolls et al.), SET (Zondiner et al.; Rozen et al.; Saito et al.) and thermoelectric transport (Merino et al.).
  - The incremental advance on those points is mainly the probe technique and the THF framing.
- **Overall assessment.** The topic is timely and of broad interest. However, the headline claims rest on derived quantities (second derivatives and temperature derivatives of integrated tunneling signals) whose systematic uncertainties are not yet adequately controlled.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Technically ambitious platform.** PTS on MATBG with gate-modulated lock-in detection offers high stability and access to 0.3 K and 8 T. It yields Landau fans, Chern insulators (half-fans from ν = 1, 2, 3) and the ν = 2 gap, all consistent with transport. This is good evidence that the device is of reasonable quality and that the dμ/dn extraction captures real features.
- **Unified dataset.** Spectroscopy (band positions, 15–16 mV flat-band splitting, phonon-assisted channels), thermodynamics (dμ/dn, μ, S) and magnetic-field response all come from one sample. This allows cross-checks that are not possible when combining different devices and techniques.
- **Theory–experiment collaboration.** The THF model with heterostrain gives concrete, falsifiable predictions:
  - the f/c-character asymmetry of charge-one excitations;
  - the degeneracy doubling from 4 to 8 as thermal energy overcomes strain splitting.
  
  Engaging the experiment at this level is valuable for the community.
- **Potential new thermodynamic signature.** If robust, a two-step entropy evolution would be an important constraint on finite-temperature theories of MATBG (local-moment regime, Kondo lattice, "melting" of moments). It would go beyond the single Pomeranchuk-type jump reported earlier.
- **Clear electrostatic insight.** Including C_b explicitly (n e = C_g V_g − C_b V_b; C_g/C_b ≈ 1/27) is a useful methodological point for the growing community using tunnel-junction and twisting-microscope probes.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**3.1 The dμ/dn extraction is insufficiently validated, and its systematic errors may be comparable to the features interpreted**

- **Several uncontrolled assumptions feed Eqs. (2), (3) and (7):**
  - an energy-independent transmission T(n);
  - a constant probe DOS;
  - a phenomenological background T(n) ∝ exp(0.258 n/n₀), divided out before differentiation with respect to V_g.
  
  Any error in the exponent, or any residual filling dependence of the barrier, enters dI_n/dV_g directly. It therefore enters dμ/dn linearly.
  - The authors should show how dμ/dn, the sawtooth amplitude and especially the negative-compressibility dips change when the background exponent is varied within its uncertainty.
  - The procedure for obtaining 0.258 (fit range, which fillings) needs to be stated in the main text.
- **Eq. (7) is numerically unstable where the DOS difference vanishes.** The denominator ρ(μ + eV_b) − ρ(μ) can approach zero, which can produce spurious peaks or divergences. The data are shown only at V_b = ±42 mV. A thermodynamic quantity must be independent of the probe bias.
  - Maps of extracted dμ/dn versus V_b (e.g., 30–60 mV, both polarities) are needed to show this invariance.
  - Electron- and hole-side extractions at opposite bias should agree in the overlapping filling ranges.
- **The bias itself shifts the sample density substantially.** With C_b ≈ 1.3 µF/cm², V_b = 42 mV changes n by C_b·V_b/e ≈ 3.4 × 10¹¹ cm⁻² ≈ 0.5 n₀.
  - It must be clarified whether "filling" in Figs. 2–4 is the equilibrium filling or the filling under bias.
  - It must also be clarified whether finite-bias charging and the out-of-equilibrium electrostatic configuration (sample potential set partly by the probe) bias μ(n).
  - The ~0.2 shift of the ν = ±4 peaks, corrected by a quantum-capacitance argument relegated to the SI, is of the same order as the "δ" offsets of the dμ/dn peaks from integer filling. That shift is a key part of the heavy/light interpretation.
- **Absolute calibration is not benchmarked.**
  - The 26 meV bandwidth, 10 meV CNP gap and 1.3 meV ν = 2 gap should be compared quantitatively with SET/capacitance results at similar angles.
  - The CNP gap in particular appears large relative to typical SET values.
  - The robustness of the CNP-gap estimate to the integration constant and to the V_b-dependent filling correction should be shown.

**3.2 The "effective mass" analysis is not a measurement of quasiparticle mass, and the heavy-fermion interpretation is not uniquely supported**

- **Eq. (4) is not a cyclotron mass here.** Eq. (4), m* = 2πħ²/(g′ dμ/dn) with fixed g′ = 4, converts a thermodynamic DOS into a mass. In the cascade regime, flavor polarization resets the number of active flavors (4 → 3 → 2 → 1) on each integer crossing. That reset alone produces factor-of-several jumps in the extracted m*.
  - The claimed tenfold "renormalization" could therefore be largely a degeneracy-counting artifact.
  - Where dμ/dn < 0 (explicitly observed), m* is negative or divergent and has no quasiparticle meaning. It is unclear how these points are treated in Fig. 3a and 3d (log scale).
  - Calling the result "cyclotron effective mass" is misleading unless it is confirmed by, for example, Lifshitz–Kosevich analysis of quantum oscillations at ν = n + δ.
- **The heavy-below/light-above asymmetry has a known competing explanation.** The same asymmetry is the established phenomenology of the cascade and "Dirac revival" picture (Zondiner et al.; Wong et al.). There, Hartree-Fock band renormalization and flavor resetting produce a sawtooth dμ/dn without invoking f/c hybridization.
  - The manuscript states the data are "consistent with" THF but does not identify an observable that discriminates THF from these alternatives.
  - Candidate discriminating observables include the temperature scale of asymmetry survival compared with predicted hybridization/Kondo scales, the magnitude of the light-mass branch, or spectroscopic f/c spectral-weight transfer in dI/dV_b.
  - The ~0.06 m_e "light" mass is quoted as comparable to bilayer graphene, but this is not quantitatively tied to a THF c-electron velocity.
- **Theory and experiment are compared at different conditions, and the text is internally inconsistent.**
  - The theory curve in Fig. 3c is computed at T = 20 K, yet it is compared with data at 0.3 K and 4 K (Fig. 3b). The text nonetheless claims agreement "at the lowest temperatures" with the gapped IKS state.
  - The text states that Fig. 3b shows persistence to ~50 K, but Fig. 3b contains only 0.3 K and 4 K. Fig. 3d shows 20–77 K, and its caption says ~77 K whereas the text says ~50 K and ~70 K elsewhere.
  - The m* = 0.174 m_e near CNP from LL fits, and the use of √(N(N−1)) for "massive Dirac" fermions (a chiral-bilayer-type formula), are not reconciled with the Fig. 3a values near ν = 0. The Extended Fig. 2e axis label also differs between text (√(N(N+1))) and caption.
- **The tunneling channel is momentum-selective, which matters for an f/c interpretation.** Tunneling is phonon-assisted with a fixed momentum transfer Δk ≈ 0.53 Å⁻¹ set by the 18° probe misalignment. The f-orbitals (spread over the moiré BZ) and c-orbitals (concentrated near Γ_M) may then have very different tunneling matrix elements.
  - The assumption that the measured ρ is the total DOS needs justification.
  - This is critical, because the central physics claim concerns f versus c character.

**3.3 The entropy plateaus, the main novel claim, are not thermodynamically robust as presented**

- **The reported S(T) is non-monotonic, which equilibrium thermodynamics forbids.** Fig. 4c and Extended Fig. 4c show the extracted entropy decreasing with T in several intervals, e.g., after a peak at ~6–7 K and after ~18 K for ν = 1, 2, 3. Since ∂S/∂T = C/T ≥ 0, these dips must be artifacts.
  - Likely sources are noise, temperature-dependent background or calibration drift, or the three-point local fitting.
  - The "plateaus" are bracketed by exactly these unphysical features, so it is not established that they are intrinsic rather than artifacts of the differentiation procedure.
  - A global, monotonicity-constrained analysis with full error propagation is required.
- **The reference assumptions and error accumulation are not addressed.**
  - Integration starts at ν = 4, assuming S(ν = 4) = 0 up to 36–40 K, so errors accumulate toward lower fillings.
  - μ(CNP) ≡ 0 is fixed at all T, yet the CNP has a gap and particle–hole asymmetry. That convention can itself introduce a T-dependent offset into ∂μ/∂T.
  - The transmission background T(n) and the quantum-capacitance filling correction may themselves be temperature dependent. Any T-dependence of these normalizations appears directly as spurious entropy.
  - The temperature grid (roughly 2–4 K steps), the number of independent datasets, and thermalization of the sample at each T are not described in enough detail to judge whether two plateaus can be resolved.
- **The degeneracy assignment is under-constrained.**
  - The plateau values are compared with k_B ln C(4,|ν|) and k_B ln C(8, ν+4). These assume completely random local moments, with no c-electron entropy, no phonon contribution, and no inter-site correlations.
  - The authors themselves note that the theory curve (Fig. 4d) is in the atomic limit and keeps rising with T without saturating at the combinatorial values.
  - U₁ = 23 meV and ε = 0.001 are tuned to match the data, and ε is not independently measured. Agreement on the location of the plateau is therefore partly by construction.
  - The c-electron Dirac-like sector alone contributes appreciable entropy at 20 K. Its omission from the experimental comparison needs justification.
- **The decisive test of local-moment physics is missing.** The entropy of free isospin moments should be suppressed by an in-plane (Zeeman) magnetic field, as done by Rozen et al. and Saito et al.
  - The abstract and introduction state that local-moment states emerge "at finite fields and temperatures", yet no field-dependent entropy is shown.
  - Without this test, the plateaus cannot be distinguished from other sources of temperature-dependent μ. Examples include band-structure renormalization with T, thermal smearing across the strain-split flat bands, or phonon-related effects, which the authors themselves mention.

**3.4 Several interpretive statements are overreaching or inconsistent, and the presentation does not yet meet top-tier standards**

- **The ν = 2 gap analysis is internally inconsistent.**
  - A linear decrease with g ≈ 5.4 is interpreted as a "spin-unpolarized" intervalley-coherent spin-singlet state. However, a bare spin-flip excitation would give g ≈ 2.
  - g ≈ 5.4 points to multi-spin-flip (skyrmion) excitations or orbital contributions (cf. Yu et al., Ref. 20). This should be discussed rather than treated as simple confirmation.
  - The low-field plateau of Δ(B) below ~1.5 T is unexplained.
  - The ν = 2 gap appears only at 0.3 K, whereas the IKS comparison is made with theory at 20 K.
- **The narrative of moments "emerging" is ambiguous.**
  - Moments said to emerge "at 10 K and 20 K" in a "melting local moment scenario" is directionally unclear: degeneracy-8 moments should become thermally accessible upon heating, not "emerge".
  - The Pomeranchuk-like non-monotonic dμ/dn near ν = 1 (Fig. 4e, with HFL/LM shading) is labeled in the figure but never discussed in the text.
  - The claim of "direct evidence of isospin local moments" goes beyond what the data and analysis support.
- **Numerous internal inconsistencies erode confidence in the analysis.**
  - The twist angle is given as 1.06° ± 0.02° (text), 1.07° (Methods) and 1.08° (Fig. 1b).
  - Methods equations are misnumbered: "solving equations (4) and (5)" refers to Eqs. (1) and (5), and Eqs. 11–13 follow Eq. 7.
  - Statements about "4 K" versus "below 4 K" for the ν = 2 gap conflict.
  - Figure references are mismatched (Fig. 3b/3d; Extended Fig. 3 versus 4 for the entropy extraction).
  - There are typographical errors ("Seeback", "Wanatabe") and a duplicated reference (1 = 30; 28 = 60).
- **Essential content is relegated to the SI.**
  - Key methodological elements sit in the SI: the T(n) background determination, the quantum-capacitance filling correction, and the zero-hybridization model.
  - Only one device is reported. Reproducibility across devices or twist angles, and the effect of twist-angle inhomogeneity within the ~1 µm² junction, are not addressed.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Rigorously validate the thermodynamic extraction.**
   - Demonstrate that dμ/dn is independent of V_b (maps over a range of positive and negative biases) and of the T(n) background parameters.
   - Explicitly correct, or quantify, the ~0.5 n₀ bias-induced doping and its effect on the filling axis.
   - Benchmark against an independent thermodynamic measurement on the same device. Examples are a direct penetration-field or capacitance measurement between probe and sample, which the large C_b makes feasible, or comparison with published SET data at matched angle.
   - Report the CNP and ν = 2 gaps with uncertainties that include these systematics.

2. **Reformulate and strengthen the mass analysis.**
   - Present the result as a thermodynamic DOS rather than a "cyclotron mass".
   - Either use a flavor-resolved degeneracy g′(ν) consistent with the cascade, or show explicitly how much of the swing survives such a correction.
   - Exclude or clearly flag regions with dμ/dn ≤ 0.
   - Where possible, obtain genuine cyclotron masses from temperature-dependent Landau-level or quantum-oscillation amplitudes on either side of ν = 1, 2, 3.
   - Compare with THF calculations at the same temperatures as the data, and with a Hartree-Fock/cascade model, to identify which features discriminate THF from the alternatives.
   - Discuss how the momentum-selective, phonon-assisted tunneling channel weights f- versus c-states.

3. **Make the entropy result thermodynamically sound and directly test the local-moment hypothesis.**
   - Acquire a denser T grid with documented thermalization.
   - Use a global, monotonicity-constrained fit of the integrated μ(ν,T), with full error propagation including the ν = 4 and CNP reference assumptions.
   - Show that the plateaus survive these steps.
   - Measure S(ν,T) under in-plane magnetic field to show Zeeman suppression consistent with 4-fold and 8-fold isospin moments.
   - Include c-electron, f–c hybridization and phonon contributions in the theoretical comparison, e.g., finite-T DMFT/Kondo-lattice results (Rai et al.; Hu et al.), to establish that two plateaus are a robust prediction rather than an artifact of the atomic limit.
   - Constrain the heterostrain ε independently, e.g., from spectroscopic splittings, Landau-level degeneracy lifting, or transport anisotropy, rather than fitting it.

4. **Sharpen the narrative, situate it in prior work, and fix the presentation.**
   - Clearly delineate what is new relative to the STM, SET, entropy and thermoelectric literature.
   - Revise the ν = 2 g-factor interpretation, considering skyrmion or orbital contributions.
   - Discuss the Pomeranchuk-like feature in Fig. 4e in the main text.
   - Temper "direct evidence" claims to match the evidence.
   - Harmonize the twist angle, temperatures, figure references, equation numbering and duplicate references.
   - Move the T(n) background and quantum-capacitance correction derivations into the Methods.
   - Add data from at least one additional device, or a discussion of reproducibility and twist-angle inhomogeneity within the junction area.
