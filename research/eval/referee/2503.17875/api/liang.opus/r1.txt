Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **What the paper does.** It applies planar tunneling spectroscopy (PTS), using a graphite probe behind a ~2 nm hBN barrier, to magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene (MATBG, θ ≈ 1.06–1.08°). From the bias and gate derivatives of the current (dI/dV_b and dI/dV_g), it extracts:
  - the inverse compressibility dμ/dn;
  - the chemical potential μ(n);
  - a compressibility-derived "effective mass" m*(n);
  - the entropy S(n,T), via a Maxwell relation.
- **Claimed advances.**
  - **(i) Method.** A route to thermodynamic quantities (dμ/dn, entropy) from the same junction that gives spectroscopy. It exploits the large probe–sample capacitance (C_b ≈ 1.3 μF/cm²) and a stable junction that allows dI/dV_g.
  - **(ii) Mass asymmetry.** A strong heavy-below / light-above asymmetry of carriers around positive integer fillings, persisting to ~50–77 K. This is interpreted as f- vs c-character charge-one excitations of the topological heavy-fermion (THF) model.
  - **(iii) Entropy plateaus.** Two plateaus near 10 K and 20 K, interpreted as 4-fold and 8-fold degenerate isospin local-moment manifolds, split by heterostrain.
  - **(iv) Gap at ν = 2.** A correlated gap of ~1.3 meV at ν = 2 with g ≈ 5.4, plus Landau-level (LL) and Chern-insulator fans seen in dμ/dn.
- **Relevance.** The topic is timely. Direct thermodynamic evidence for f-electron local moments, and for a change in their degeneracy, would be a meaningful test of Kondo-lattice/THF descriptions. The proposed simultaneous spectroscopic-plus-thermodynamic probe would also interest the moiré community.
- **Novelty relative to prior work is partial.**
  - The cascade, negative compressibility and Dirac revivals are well established by SET, STM and compressibility work (Zondiner, Wong, Choi, Yu).
  - Isospin-Pomeranchuk entropy of ~1 k_B per moiré cell is established (Rozen, Saito).
  - The heavy/light asymmetry has been reported in thermopower (Merino et al.).
  - The genuinely new claims are the two-plateau entropy structure, its degeneracy interpretation, and the PTS-based dμ/dn methodology.
  - These are exactly the parts whose robustness most needs establishing.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Technical capability.**
  - A stable planar junction allowing both dI/dV_b and dI/dV_g, with sub-meV features resolved at 0.3 K and up to 8 T.
  - A sensible accounting of the probe-bias contribution to doping (ne = C_g V_g − C_b V_b), which is usually neglected.
  - The sheared maps make the incompressible lines vertical.
- **Internal consistency with established phenomenology.**
  - The extracted dμ/dn reproduces known features: band-edge peaks at ±4, a CNP gap (~10 meV), the sawtooth cascade (stronger on the electron side), and a sharp ν = 2 gap at the lowest temperature.
  - It also shows LL fans (ν = ±4, ±8, ±12) and Chern-insulator half-fans from s = 1, 2, 3 that match transport on similar devices.
- **Self-consistent phonon-gap explanation.**
  - The ~22 mV onset is consistent with ZA-phonon bridging of the Δk ≈ 2|K|sin(9°) ≈ 0.53 Å⁻¹ mismatch set by the 18° probe–sample misorientation.
  - The arithmetic checks out, giving confidence in the junction model.
- **Strong theory collaboration.**
  - Explicit THF-model calculations (band structure with strain, dμ/dν and m* near ν = 2, atomic-limit entropy) provide a concrete framework for interpretation.
  - The connection to the IKS state at ν = 2 is a useful, falsifiable link.
- **Potentially high-impact outcome.**
  - If substantiated, the observation of temperature-tuned changes in local-moment degeneracy would be a qualitatively new thermodynamic signature of the f-electron sector.
  - It would interest a broad readership in correlated and heavy-fermion physics.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**3.1 The extraction of dμ/dn rests on a tunneling model whose key assumptions are neither justified nor tested, and the extracted quantity may not be the thermodynamic inverse compressibility.**

- **Elastic-tunneling assumptions vs. the actual tunneling mechanism.**
  - Eqs. (1)–(3) assume elastic tunneling, a constant probe DOS, an energy-independent transmission and a smooth T(n).
  - Yet the paper states that tunneling here is phonon-assisted and inelastic, bridging a 0.53 Å⁻¹ mismatch. The measured signal is then a convolution of the TBG spectral function with the ZA-phonon spectral weight and a momentum-dependent matrix element. It is not ρ(E_F + eV_b).
  - Phonon-assisted, momentum-selective tunneling may also weight f-like states (localized at AA sites, spread over many plane waves) and c-like states differently.
  - This matters directly for the central claim: an apparent heavy/light asymmetry could partly reflect orbital-selective tunneling matrix elements rather than thermodynamics.
- **Fragility of Eq. (3).**
  - Eq. (3) divides dI_n/dV_g by a combination involving dI_n/dV_b. Equivalently (Methods Eq. 7), the denominator contains ρ(E_F + eV_b) − ρ(E_F), which can become small or change sign.
  - The procedure is performed at a single bias, V_b = ±42 mV. A thermodynamic dμ/dn must be independent of V_b. No demonstration of V_b-independence, nor error propagation, is given.
  - The empirical background T(n) ∝ exp(0.258 n/n₀) enters dI_n/dV_g directly through dT/dV_g. A modest error in this fitted exponent produces a smooth additive error in dμ/dn and hence in μ(n) and S(n). Its uncertainty is not reported.
- **Large and unspecified filling correction.**
  - A quantum-capacitance correction shifts the band-edge dμ/dn peaks by ~0.2 in ν; the procedure is relegated to the SI.
  - The correction is asserted to be "negligible" away from band edges. However, the physics claims depend on whether features sit just below or just above integer ν, at the δ ≲ 0.1–0.2 level.
  - The correction is also presumably temperature-dependent, which feeds into the entropy analysis.
- **Inconsistent device and calibration parameters.**
  - C_g = e × 0.30×10¹² cm⁻²/V ≈ 0.048 μF/cm² implies a gate dielectric of ~65 nm (ε ≈ 3.5). This contradicts the stated 30 nm top hBN, which would give ~0.1 μF/cm².
  - The twist angle is quoted as 1.06 ± 0.02°, 1.07° and 1.08° in different places.
  - The Methods formula θ = a(2/√3n₀)^{1/2} is dimensionally inverted. It should be θ = a(√3n₀/2)^{1/2}, which with n₀ = 0.67×10¹² cm⁻² does give ≈1.07°.
  - These issues undermine confidence in the absolute density and capacitance scales on which all quantitative results depend.
- **Absolute scale not benchmarked.** The absolute scale of dμ/dn is not compared against independent SET/compressibility data on the same or similar devices, so a systematic multiplicative error cannot be excluded.

**3.2 The "effective mass" and "mass renormalization" analysis is not physically well-founded.**

- **Eq. (4) does not give a quasiparticle mass in this regime.**
  - Eq. (4), m* = 2πħ²/[g′(dμ/dn)], converts a thermodynamic compressibility into a non-interacting DOS mass. In a strongly interacting system, dμ/dn includes Hartree and exchange contributions. Fermi-liquid compressibility scales as m*/(1 + F₀ˢ), and dμ/dn turns negative, which Eq. (4) cannot accommodate.
  - The "heavy" regions just below integer ν coincide with near-zero or negative dμ/dn, where Eq. (4) diverges or is undefined. The "heavy" masses therefore primarily reflect negative-compressibility physics, not band mass.
- **Fixed degeneracy g′ = 4 contradicts the cascade picture.**
  - Using g′ = 4 throughout contradicts the cascade/reset picture invoked elsewhere, in which carriers above integer ν occupy a reduced number of flavors (e.g. 4 − ν).
  - Correcting g′ would change the extracted "light" masses by factors of up to 4. This directly affects the claimed "factor of 10 renormalization" and the comparison with bilayer graphene masses.
- **Numerical conversion needs checking.**
  - My estimate with ħ²/m_e ≈ 7.6×10⁻¹³ meV·cm² and g′ = 4: dμ/dn ≈ 80×10⁻¹² meV·cm² gives m* ≈ 0.015 m_e, and ≈ 20×10⁻¹² gives ≈ 0.06 m_e.
  - It is unclear whether the plotted minima (~0.05–0.06 m_e at the dμ/dn peaks) are consistent with g′ = 4.
- **Thermal smearing of a sawtooth.**
  - Peaks in dμ/dn at finite temperature can arise from thermally smeared jumps in μ, i.e. incipient gaps or resets, rather than from light carriers.
  - The temperature dependence of "m*" (Fig. 3d) is therefore at least partly a thermal-broadening effect and cannot be read as mass renormalization surviving to 77 K.
- **The LL-derived masses are internally inconsistent.**
  - The text calls the B-linear LL spacing "characteristic of massive Dirac fermions". Yet the formula used, E_N − E₀ = ħω_c√(N(N−1)), is that of chiral parabolic (bilayer-graphene-like) bands.
  - Extended Fig. 2e is plotted versus √(N(N−1))B in one place and √(N(N+1))B in another.
  - The lifting of the zeroth-LL degeneracy is quoted at >3.5 T in the main text and >1.5 T in Methods.
  - The remote-band fit appears to have a large non-zero intercept (~60 mV), which is not discussed.
- **The asymmetry is not uniquely diagnostic.**
  - It is also expected in Hartree–Fock cascade or flavor-reset descriptions and was previously reported (SET Dirac revivals, STM cascades, thermopower).
  - The manuscript itself states it is consistent with symmetric and symmetry-broken phases alike. The claim that it specifically "verifies" heavy f- and light c-electrons is therefore overstated.

**3.3 The entropy analysis, the paper's main novel claim, has uncontrolled systematic errors, and some extracted features are thermodynamically implausible.**

- **Arbitrary reference at the CNP.**
  - μ is obtained by integrating dμ/dn from the CNP, with μ(CNP, T) ≡ 0 at all temperatures. The true μ_CNP(T) is unknown and generically T-dependent in a particle-hole-asymmetric system.
  - With S(ν = 4) = 0 imposed, an unknown dμ_CNP/dT adds an error ΔS = −(ν − 4)·dμ_CNP/dT.
    - This is linear in ν and largest at the CNP (4·dμ_CNP/dT).
    - A drift of only ~0.1 meV/K produces errors of order several k_B per moiré cell, comparable to the plateau structure itself.
  - Any T-dependence of the Cq-based filling correction or of the T(n) normalization enters in the same way.
- **Implausible or under-supported features in S(T).**
  - In Extended Fig. 4c and Fig. 4c, S(T) at ν = 2 and 3 decreases with increasing T between ~18 and ~24 K, by roughly 0.5–1 k_B. This would imply negative specific heat (C = T∂S/∂T < 0), which is thermodynamically forbidden.
  - This non-monotonic segment defines the "20 K plateau", which strongly suggests that the plateau structure is at least partly an artifact.
  - Entropies with error bars extending below zero appear in Fig. 4b.
  - The plateaus rest on a three-point numerical derivative over a sparse temperature grid, which amplifies noise.
- **Contradiction with the reported CNP gap.**
  - At the CNP, the 8-fold count gives k_B ln C(8,4) = k_B ln 70 ≈ 4.25 k_B, which the data are said to approach near 20 K. Yet the same paper reports a ~10 meV CNP gap (≈115 K).
  - Thermal activation across such a gap at k_BT ≈ 1.7 meV would give negligible entropy.
  - Either the gap, the entropy reference, or the "random local moment" counting is inconsistent. The resolution is not discussed.
- **Weak evidence for the degeneracy assignment.**
  - The comparison with k_B ln C(8, ν + 4) and k_B ln C(4, |ν|) is made only at a few integer points connected by lines, and only on the electron side.
  - No independent test is presented, such as Zeeman suppression of spin entropy in parallel field, which Pomeranchuk studies have used.
  - The claim that the CNP entropy "saturates to zero below 12 K" is essentially guaranteed by the CNP gap and the chosen reference, so it is not independent support.

**3.4 The theory comparison is under-constrained, and the interpretation does not address alternative explanations or internal inconsistencies.**

- **Questionable atomic limit.**
  - The entropy theory uses the zero-hybridization (atomic) limit. In the THF model, the f–c hybridization γ is of order tens of meV, far larger than k_BT ≈ 1–2 meV at 10–20 K.
  - Why f–c decoupling should hold in exactly the temperature window of the plateaus is not justified. Finite-hybridization or DMFT-type calculations (e.g. Rai et al., Hu et al.) are cited but not used.
  - The model also gives non-zero entropy as T → 0, acknowledged as unphysical, so agreement at the low-T plateau is not a stringent test.
- **Fitted parameters.**
  - U₁ = 23 meV and heterostrain ε = 0.001 are "set to match the experimental data". Strain is neither measured independently (e.g. by STM, Raman, or band splitting in the spectra) nor varied.
  - With two free parameters chosen to produce a crossover near 15 K, reproducing two plateaus is weak evidence, especially given the systematic concerns in 3.3.
- **Mismatched temperatures in the ν = 2 comparison.**
  - The IKS/THF comparison of m* near ν = 2 (Fig. 3c) is computed at T = 20 K but compared with 0.3 K and 4 K data.
  - The 0.3 K gap at ν = 2 is attributed to the IKS state, yet its field dependence (g ≈ 5.4) is interpreted as a spin-unpolarized intervalley-coherent singlet.
  - A slope well above the spin value g = 2 implies orbital or valley magnetic moments or multi-flavor excitations. The microscopic origin is not discussed, nor is its compatibility with IKS.
  - Δ = 1.3 meV from the μ jump versus Δ₀ = 1.75 meV from the linear fit, and the flat region below 1.5 T, also need reconciliation.
- **Alternative explanations not ruled out.**
  - The temperature-dependent cascade, isospin Pomeranchuk physics, and phonon or low-energy charge excitations (which the authors themselves mention) are alternatives.
  - The hole side, where the THF model predicts a related structure for negative fillings, is not analyzed for entropy or mass. The weaker hole-side asymmetry is not reconciled with theory.
- **Single device and inconsistent statements.**
  - All data appear to come from a single device with a ~1 μm² probe area, so twist-angle and strain inhomogeneity and reproducibility are unaddressed.
  - The persistence temperature of the cascade or mass asymmetry is variously stated as ~50 K, ~70 K and ~77 K.
  - Equation numbering in Methods is inconsistent ("solving equations (4) and (5)" refers to an equation labelled (1)).

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

**4.1 Validate the dμ/dn extraction independently and quantify its uncertainties.**
- Show that dμ/dn is independent of V_b over a range (both polarities, several biases), and present full error propagation through Eq. (3)/(7), including the uncertainty of the T(n) exponent.
- Explicitly present the Cq correction and its temperature dependence.
- Benchmark the absolute scale against SET or capacitance data, or against the known CNP and band-edge gaps.
- Discuss how phonon-assisted, momentum-selective tunneling and orbital-dependent (f vs c) matrix elements affect the "DOS" term. Ideally, compare devices with different probe misorientation angles.
- Resolve the capacitance, thickness and twist-angle inconsistencies, and correct the θ(n₀) formula.
- Show reproducibility in at least one additional device.

**4.2 Reformulate the mass analysis so it reflects what is actually measured.**
- Present the thermodynamic DOS (dn/dμ) as the primary quantity, rather than a "cyclotron mass" from Eq. (4) with fixed g′ = 4.
- If masses are quoted, use flavor-resolved degeneracies consistent with the reset picture, and exclude regions of negative compressibility.
- Separate thermal-smearing effects by simulating a thermally broadened sawtooth μ(n).
- Where possible, obtain genuine cyclotron masses from LL spacings or Lifshitz–Kosevich temperature dependence of the Chern-insulator and LL features on either side of integer fillings. This would give a direct test of heavy vs light carriers.
- Fix the LL formula and terminology (chiral parabolic vs massive Dirac; √(N(N−1)) vs √(N(N+1))), and explain the remote-band intercept.
- Compute dμ/dν within the THF model at the measured temperatures, including Hartree/exchange, for a like-for-like comparison.

**4.3 Make the entropy result robust and thermodynamically consistent.**
- Determine or bound dμ_CNP/dT, e.g. using the absolute Fermi-level position from the phonon-onset or band-peak energies versus T. Alternatively, use a reference choice whose uncertainty can be propagated, and show the resulting systematic band in S.
- Use a denser temperature grid and a global smooth fit of μ(ν,T) instead of three-point derivatives. Explicitly verify ∂S/∂T ≥ 0, and address the non-monotonic segments.
- Reconcile the large entropy near the CNP at ~20 K with the 10 meV CNP gap.
- Provide discriminating tests of the local-moment interpretation:
  - suppression or shift of the plateaus in an in-plane (Zeeman-only) field;
  - the full S(ν) on both electron and hole sides at non-integer fillings, compared with model curves rather than only integer points.

**4.4 Strengthen the theory–experiment connection and calibrate the claims.**
- Go beyond the atomic limit: e.g. finite-hybridization, DMFT or strong-coupling calculations at 5–30 K. Show how sensitive the plateau positions are to U₁ and ε, and justify why f–c decoupling applies at k_BT ≪ γ.
- Constrain strain independently, e.g. from observed band splittings in the spectra or complementary STM/Raman measurements.
- Discuss the g ≈ 5.4 slope at ν = 2 in terms of orbital/valley moments and its compatibility with the IKS state.
- Address alternative interpretations (cascade thermal evolution, Pomeranchuk physics, phonon or charge-excitation entropy) and the hole-side behavior.
- Harmonize the quoted temperature scales (50/70/77 K) and equation numbering.
- Temper wording such as "direct evidence" and "experimental verification" to reflect the model dependence of the extracted quantities.
