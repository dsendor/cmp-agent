Referee report

Manuscript: "Probing electric field tunable multiband superconductivity in alternating twisted quadralayer graphene"

1. Summary of the submission

The authors report transport on one dual-gated alternating twisted quadralayer graphene (ATQG) device at theta = 1.58 degrees. Their main claims are:
(i) Robust superconductivity for v_tot between about +/-2 and +/-4 and |D| < ~0.5 V/nm, with Tc up to ~2 K (40% criterion), a maximum T_BKT of 1.6 K, and an in-plane critical field of ~7 T, about twice the Clogston value.
(ii) Landau fan analysis separates Dirac-band and flat-band densities. It finds an effective flat-band width of ~50 meV against ~10 meV in the continuum model, and puts flat-band half filling at v_tot ~ 2.36.
(iii) A coherence-length analysis (xi x k_F) points to a BCS to BEC crossover as a function of doping at D = 0, and to D-tunable strong coupling at v_tot = 2.31.
(iv) A "high" normal-state critical current gives a flat-band velocity v_n ~ 1700 m/s, from which Delta/k_B Tc ~ 0.2 is estimated. The superconducting critical current gives a superfluid stiffness of ~4 x 10^7 H^-1, about ten times a "conventional" estimate. Both are attributed to the quantum metric.

The data look good. The phase diagram, the D-tuned Tc, the Pauli-limit violation and the Landau fans add usefully to the ATMG literature, and ATQG at this angle is less studied than the trilayer. However, the headline interpretive claims (BEC-like pairing, "vanishing Fermi velocity", a large stiffness beyond conventional, and the quantum metric as their cause) rest on a chain of estimates. Each link has a large or unquantified uncertainty, and several links contradict one another. My main concerns follow.

2. Major issues

2.1 The pairing density n* is a modelling choice that drives the BEC conclusion, and it contradicts the paper's own multiband picture.
The authors set n* from v* = |v_flat - 2| and g = 2, so only flat-band carriers counted from half filling are taken to pair. Three problems follow.
(a) By construction, k_F goes to 0 at v_tot = 2.36. The dip of xi x k_F in Fig. 3c toward ~2 is therefore built in by the definition, not measured. Any finite xi gives the same dip.
(b) v_tot = 2.31, the point used for all the D-dependent analysis (Fig. 4, Fig. 5), sits only about 0.05 in filling from the assigned half filling. That is n* ~ 7 x 10^10 cm^-2. The location of v_flat = 2 comes from a chemical-potential plateau edge measured at T = 1.7 K and finite B (Fig. 2d,e) and from Hall data. Its uncertainty is plausibly comparable to 0.05, as is the density inhomogeneity of a typical moire device. A shift of 0.05 changes n* by order 100%, so k_F changes by a factor of about 1.4 or more. No error bars on v_flat, n*, k_F or xi x k_F are given.
(c) Most importantly, Section B states that at v_flat ~ 2 the Hall density equals the Dirac-band density (~0.5 x 10^12 cm^-2), and the paper argues that superconductivity "arises with the multiband involvement". If the Dirac carriers take part in pairing, n* is about ten times larger. xi x k_F at v_tot = 2.31 then rises to about 10 or more, and the BEC proximity disappears. The authors' caveat that they "overestimate the product since not all the carriers are paired" points the wrong way: leaving out the Dirac carriers makes the product smaller. The authors should either justify why only flat-band carriers counted from v_flat = 2 form pairs, or present xi x k_F for the alternative choices (all carriers, flat band from its own band edge, Hall density) with uncertainties, and moderate the claims to match.

2.2 Using xi_GL x k_F as a BCS-BEC indicator needs justification in this system.
xi is the GL coherence length from Bc0 = Phi_0/(2 pi xi^2), with Bc0 linearly extrapolated from data between ~0.6 and 1.25 K (Supp. Fig. 2b). The Bc criterion is not stated. In the dirty limit xi_GL ~ sqrt(xi_0 l) is not the pair size, and no mean free path is given. More fundamentally, in an isolated flat band the coherence length is set by the quantum metric rather than by hbar v_F/Delta or 1/k_F. Ref. 45, with a co-author of this manuscript, makes this point. The paper then attributes its results to quantum geometry while interpreting xi x k_F with criteria from parabolic-band crossover theory (Ref. 38). The authors should state the limits of the criterion, give an estimate of l, and explain why a small xi x k_F means BEC-like pairing rather than a quantum-geometric coherence length.

2.3 The estimate Delta/k_B Tc ~ 0.2 contradicts the strong-coupling claim.
Delta ~ hbar v_n / xi is a BCS relation (xi_0 = hbar v_F / (pi Delta)). It assumes the regime the authors argue against, and in a flat band it fails for the reason given in 2.2. The result is also inconsistent with the rest of the paper. In the BEC or strong-coupling regime, Delta/k_B Tc should exceed the BCS value of 1.76, not fall ten times below it. A gap ten times smaller than k_B Tc is hard to reconcile with T*/T_BKT ~ 2.2 (Supp. Fig. 5) or with B_par ~ 7 T: with Delta/k_B Tc = 0.2, the Pauli field would be ~0.2 T, a violation by more than 30 times. I recommend removing this estimate or reframing it as a demonstration that the BCS relation fails, rather than as evidence of unconventional pairing.

2.4 Extracting the flat-band velocity from the high critical current j_n is not well supported.
(a) Parallel conduction: at v_tot ~ 2.3 the Dirac bands carry ~0.5 x 10^12 cm^-2 with v_F ~ 6 x 10^5 m/s. Why would a drift-velocity saturation or Zener-Klein threshold of the flat band set a sharp dV/dI peak while the Dirac channels conduct in parallel? Please discuss the two-channel problem explicitly.
(b) Density reference: v_n = (1/e) dj_n/dn needs j_n to scale with the carrier density measured from the correlated gap. In Fig. 5b, as far as I can read it, the linear fits to j_n cross zero near v_tot ~ 2.25 to 2.3, and |j_n| grows on approaching 2.35. That is not obviously consistent with a correlated gap at v_tot = 2.36 (v_flat = 2). Please state which density the slope refers to and why.
(c) The channel width, which is needed to convert I to j, is not given anywhere. The width and the geometry of the measured segment must be stated.
(d) v_n is measured only at D = 0.26 V/nm. The j_n feature is clearly D-dependent (Supp. Fig. 7c shows it mainly for D > ~0.2 V/nm). Yet a single v_n is used for the "conventional" stiffness at all D (Fig. 5d, flat red dashed line). Either measure v_n(D) or limit the comparison to D = 0.26 V/nm.
(e) Joule heating at I ~ 300 to 600 nA with dV/dI ~ 1 to 2 kOhm (sub-nW to ~1 nW at 30 mK) can produce critical-current-like peaks. Please give a heating estimate or show the T dependence of the j_n boundary.
The phrase "vanishing Fermi velocity" in the abstract and conclusions overstates a single model-dependent number of 1700 m/s. Please tone it down.

2.5 The superfluid stiffness comparison depends on the choices above, and the "conventional" baseline is not unique.
(a) D_s = 2 pi j_s xi / Phi_0 treats the measured switching current as the depairing current. The GL depairing current carries a different numerical prefactor (in 2D, D_s = 3 sqrt(3) pi j_c xi / Phi_0, about 2.6 times larger), so please state the exact relation used. More importantly, the switching current in moire devices is often limited by inhomogeneity, weak links or vortex entry, which makes D_s a lower bound. The "weak interference pattern" in the inset of Fig. 1e itself suggests weak-link or junction-like inhomogeneity.
(b) The baseline e^2 n*/m*, with m* = hbar k_F / v_n, again uses only the tiny n* from 2.1. If the Dirac carriers pair, their conventional stiffness is e^2 n_D/m_D with m_D = hbar k_F,D / v_F. A rough estimate (n_D ~ 5 x 10^15 m^-2, v_F ~ 6 x 10^5 m/s) gives ~10^10 H^-1. That is more than two orders of magnitude above the measured D_s. Under that assumption, the measured stiffness would be anomalously small, not anomalously large. The conclusion that D_s exceeds the conventional value therefore depends entirely on the assumption in 2.1. The authors should discuss this alternative explicitly.
(c) Please give uncertainties on D_s, which combine those of j_s, xi and the width.

2.6 The quantum metric attribution is qualitative.
The quantum metric is invoked to explain the strong coupling, the large D_s and the growth of T*/T_BKT with D, but nothing in the paper is computed for this system. The continuum model is already in hand (Fig. 1b,c), and the theory co-authors work on exactly this question. A calculation of the integrated quantum metric of the flat band, or of the geometric bound on D_s (Refs. 43 to 46), as a function of U at theta = 1.58 degrees, compared with Fig. 5d, would turn a suggestion into a test. Without it, "attributed to substantial quantum metric contributions" in the abstract should become a possibility consistent with the data. Likewise, a rising T*/T_BKT with T* defined as "the onset of non-linear I-V curves" (Supp. Fig. 5) does not validate quantum-geometry-induced strong coupling. Nonlinearity can come from heating, inhomogeneity or fluctuations, and Supp. Fig. 5 shows only the 0.15 K curve and a single line labelled T*, not the temperature series from which T* was read.

2.7 Mapping fan origins to flat-band fillings is inconsistent.
Section B identifies fans from v_tot ~ 2, ~3.3 and 4 with interaction-induced gaps at flat-band fillings 2, 3 and 10/3. Yet the paper's central point is that v_tot differs from v_flat (v_flat = 2 at v_tot ~ 2.36). Gaps at flat-band fillings should be indexed in v_flat, and the fan origins should be converted using n_Dirac at those densities. As written, the v_tot ~ 2 fan is assigned to v_flat = 2 while v_flat = 2 is placed at v_tot = 2.36. The text also gives the origins as "3 and 3.3" in one sentence and "~3.3" in another. The CDW assignment at 10/3 rests on a single fan origin and is speculative. It also depends on n0 calibration at the 1% level (3.3 versus 3.33). I suggest presenting it as a possibility.

2.8 Extracting the bandwidth from the Dirac LL trajectories assumes linear dispersion up to ~50 meV.
In ATQG the dispersive sector is a TBG-like band at an effective angle of theta/lambda ~ 2.6 degrees (lambda = 2 cos(2 pi/5)). Its dispersion and velocity are not linear over tens of meV, and the velocity itself may be renormalized by interactions. Using the continuum-model v_F = 6.1 x 10^5 m/s in mu_N - mu_0 = v_F sqrt(2 e hbar N B) and comparing the result with the same model's 10 meV bandwidth is partly circular. Please show the continuum-model Dirac-sector dispersion over the relevant energy window, give the uncertainty in mu from the v_F assumption, and say how the ~50 meV figure changes if the LL spectrum of the actual dispersive sector is used.

2.9 The "insulating normal state" (Fig. 4f) is not supported.
Fig. 4f shows dV/dI at a fixed Idc = 420 nA, on the tail of the critical-current peak (Fig. 4e). There the response is a nonequilibrium one, set by how the peak shrinks and shifts with T. It is not a normal-state transport coefficient. Fig. 1f at the same D = 0.26 V/nm and v_tot = 2.31 shows zero-bias Rxx rising with T above Tc, which is metallic. The claim of a pseudogap of pre-paired electrons should be removed or supported with zero-bias Rxx(T) with superconductivity suppressed by B_perp.

2.10 Other interpretive overreach.
(a) "Higher critical current at finite D, suggesting a stronger coupling superconductivity closing to the limit of BEC regime" (Fig. 4e). A larger Ic follows from a larger stiffness or Tc and says nothing specific about BEC.
(b) The divergence of n_H near |D| ~ 0.35 V/nm (Fig. 4d) is presented as a large DOS near a VHS, with falling Tc called inconsistent with BCS. A diverging Hall density near a Lifshitz transition reflects Rxy passing through zero, which is not directly a DOS measure. Falling Tc near a VHS is also commonly attributed to competing orders. "Inconsistent with the conventional BCS prediction" is too strong.
(c) "The observation of maximized Tc at v_tot = 2.3 is unexpected" and "collaboration instead of competition". Tc maxima adjacent to |v| = 2 are seen in MATTG and other ATMG devices (Refs. 7, 8, 10, 11), so please compare explicitly. There is also an internal tension: in a BEC picture Tc scales with the phase stiffness, which in a conventional band scales with n*. Yet Tc is maximal where the paper's n* goes to 0.

2.11 Only one device, with limited evidence of homogeneity.
All results come from one device at one angle. Supp. Fig. 1b shows normal-state resistances that differ by factors of 3 to 4 between contact pairs. That may be geometric, but it should be normalized. A per-segment n0 (twist angle) should be extracted to support "uniform angle distribution". The text should say which pair is used for each figure. The asymmetry in D (Tc of ~1.5 K at D ~ -0.2 versus ~2 K at +0.25 V/nm in Fig. 4b; ~4.5 T versus ~7 T in Supp. Fig. 4a) is notable for a nominally mirror-symmetric stack and suggests angle differences between interfaces or another source of broken mirror symmetry. The same goes for the electron-hole asymmetric fans at D = 0, which the authors attribute to hybridization at D = 0. These points should be discussed, because they bear directly on the band-hybridization narrative. A second device, even with less complete data, would greatly strengthen the reproducibility of the D-tuned results.

3. Minor issues

- Abstract: the maximum T_BKT of 1.6 K appears only as a label in Supp. Fig. 5b. Please show the V-I power-law analysis (exponent versus T) at D = 0.26 V/nm, not just at D = 0 (Fig. 1g).
- The BKT identification should show the exponent alpha(T) crossing 3, not one curve with an I^3 guide. Fig. 1g does not state the D value in the caption (the panel says D = 0).
- Tc criterion: 40% of the intersection value R* is unusual. Please justify it and check that the conclusions do not change for 50% or 90% of R_N. Supp. Fig. 2a also shows a residual of about 25 Ohm at base temperature, so please comment.
- Fig. 3b: the "double dome" with maxima at 2.3 and 2.8 is hard to see; the blue trace looks like one dome with a shoulder. The text gives the xi maximum as ~100 nm, and the plot looks closer to 90 nm. Fig. 4b: the xi minimum looks closer to 35 to 40 nm than 30 nm, and the xi x k_F minimum in Fig. 4c is near D ~ 0.3 V/nm and ~2.3, not 2 at 0.25 V/nm. Please make the text and figures agree, and add error bars to the derived quantities.
- Fig. 2f and Fig. 4d: state the field and temperature at which n_H was taken. In a two-carrier system n_H = (sum n mu)^2 / sum n mu^2, so the statement that n_H equals n_Dirac implies a negligible free flat-band density only under strong assumptions. Please state them.
- Section B: n_Dirac = 2 N_LL x 4eB/h. Please state explicitly that this counts two Dirac cones with fourfold degeneracy each at the LL centre.
- Valence-side LL indices of -5, -14 and -24: an odd index (-5) needs comment.
- Methods: the main text says "(Methods)" and "(details in Methods)", but the Methods sit in the Supplementary. The channel width, segment length, Bc criterion and the maximum current used should be given there.
- The Supplementary has two notes numbered 6. Supp. Note 6 says "D = 0", but the data are at D = -0.03 V/nm.
- Author contributions: "K.L." does not match any author initials (K.T.L.?).
- Typos: "quatralayer" and "quadralayer" (the established term is "quadrilayer", Ref. 31), "AQTG", "valance", "vesus", "verus", "Fermi vector" (should be wave vector), "coherent length" (should be coherence length).
- Data availability "upon reasonable request": please consider depositing the data behind the figures.
- In the introduction, "The number of dispersive bands is N - 2" is accurate only if "bands" means Dirac cones per valley and spin. For N = 4 the dispersive sector is a TBG-like band at a larger effective angle. Please phrase this precisely.

4. Assessment and recommendation

The measurements are of good quality and the phenomenology (D-tunable Tc reaching 2 K, Pauli-limit violation, Landau-fan evidence for coexisting Dirac and flat bands, two critical-current scales) is worth reporting. The quantitative interpretation is not yet convincing. The BEC-proximity claim depends on a pairing density that is ill-determined and that conflicts with the paper's own multiband argument. The gap estimate contradicts the strong-coupling claim. The flat-band velocity and the stiffness enhancement rest on an unstated width, a single D value and an assumed depairing-limited critical current. The quantum metric explanation is not tested against any calculation. I recommend major revision. A revised version should (1) give uncertainties for v_flat, n*, xi x k_F, v_n and D_s; (2) show how the conclusions change if the Dirac carriers take part in pairing; (3) remove or reframe the Delta/k_B Tc and "insulating normal state" arguments; (4) add a continuum-model estimate of the quantum-geometric stiffness against D; and (5) provide the device geometry and homogeneity details, ideally with a second device. With these changes the paper could be suitable for publication. The abstract and conclusions should then be scaled to what the data support.
