# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** "Probing electric field tunable multiband superconductivity in alternating twisted quadralayer graphene" (Liu et al.)

## Summary

The authors report transport on a single dual-gated alternating-twist quadrilayer graphene (ATQG) device with θ = 1.58°. The main results are:

1. Robust superconductivity for 2 ≲ |ν_tot| ≲ 4 and |D| ≲ 0.5 V/nm, with T_BKT up to 1.6 K and in-plane critical fields about twice the Pauli limit.
2. A Landau-fan "Dirac spectroscopy" analysis that separates the Dirac-band and flat-band carrier densities. It shows a chemical-potential plateau for 1.64 < ν_tot < 2.36 and places ν_flat = 2 at ν_tot ≈ 2.36.
3. A doping- and D-dependent ξ_GL·k_F, which the authors interpret as tuning from BCS-like toward BEC-like pairing near half filling of the flat band.
4. Two critical-current boundaries. The higher one is used to extract a flat-band velocity v_n ≈ 1700 m/s. The lower one yields a superfluid stiffness D_s about 10 times larger than the "conventional" estimate, which is attributed to quantum-metric contributions.

**Strengths.** The device appears to be of good quality. The data set is broad, combining Landau fans, Hall data, R(T), V–I, dV/dI versus I_dc, B_⊥, B_∥ and D. The Dirac-spectroscopy analysis of the conduction-band side is informative. The topic is timely.

**Main weakness.** The central quantitative claims rest on chains of estimates whose assumptions are not justified, are partly circular, or are mutually inconsistent. These claims are BCS–BEC crossover, "vanishing Fermi velocity," D_s greatly exceeding the conventional value, and quantum-metric enhancement. I detail these below.

I recommend **major revision**.

---

## Major comments

### 1. The ξ·k_F minimum near half filling is largely fixed by the definition of n*

The pairing density is defined as n* ∝ ν* = |ν_flat − 2|, so k_F → 0 and ξk_F → 0 by construction as ν_flat → 2. The dip in Fig. 3c at ν_tot ≈ 2.36, and the choice of ν_tot = 2.31 for all D-dependent studies, therefore sit exactly where this quantity is forced to be small.

Back-calculating from ξ = 30 nm and ξk_F = 2 at D = 0.26 V/nm gives:
- k_F ≈ 0.067 nm⁻¹
- n* ≈ 7×10¹⁰ cm⁻², i.e. ν* ≈ 0.05

This is consistent with the stated ν_flat ≈ 1.96 at ν_tot ≈ 2.3. However, an uncertainty of only ~0.05 in ν_flat (≈0.07×10¹² cm⁻² in n_Dirac, well within the scatter in Fig. 2e) changes n* by ~100%. Error bars propagated from the Landau-fan extraction are essential.

The approach also has conceptual problems:
- **Dirac carriers are excluded.** n_Dirac ≈ 0.4×10¹² cm⁻² at ν_tot ≈ 2.3, which is several times larger than n*. Yet the paper's own thesis is multiband involvement. Please show ξk_F with alternative definitions (total density, Dirac density, flat-band density counted from charge neutrality) and discuss which is appropriate.
- **ξ_GL is not the pair size.** The ξ_GL obtained from B_c2 is the GL (phase-healing) length. In the BEC regime it does not track the pair size, and in the dirty limit ξ_GL ~ √(ξ₀ℓ) is reduced by disorder independently of the coupling strength. The mean free path ℓ should be estimated from the normal-state resistivity and the Dirac/flat-band parameters.
- **The D dependence comes from ξ alone.** Fig. 4d shows n_H constant for |D| < 0.3 V/nm. Please state explicitly how n* is determined at finite D. If n* is taken as constant, the D dependence of ξk_F simply reflects that of B_c2. B_c2 depends on many factors besides coupling, including orbital effects of D on Fermi velocity and disorder.

### 2. Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 0.2 contradicts the strong-coupling interpretation

Using Δ ≈ ħv_n/ξ with v_n = 1700 m/s and ξ = 30 nm gives Δ ≈ 37 μeV and Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 0.2 (I reproduce this number). Three problems follow:
- **The gap is unphysically small.** A gap five times smaller than k_BT_c cannot sustain the condensate at T_c. Strong coupling and BEC-like pairing should give Δ/k_BT_c *larger* than the BCS value, not an order of magnitude smaller. This directly contradicts Sections C–D and the T*/T_BKT argument.
- **The formula is the wrong one to use.** The BCS expression is ξ₀ = ħv_F/(πΔ), so the stated relation also misses a factor of π. More fundamentally, using a BCS relation to argue that the system is non-BCS is circular.
- **A more consistent reading exists.** The result signals that ξ is not set by ħv_F/Δ, as expected for flat-band superconductors where the coherence length is controlled by the quantum-metric length (Ref. 45, co-authored by K.T. Law).

I suggest reframing this result in that way and dropping the Δ estimate, unless Δ can be obtained independently (e.g., from tunneling).

### 3. Interpreting the higher critical current j_n as Zener–Klein breakdown of a correlated gap is not established

**The normal state is not insulating.** At ν_tot ≈ 2.3, the Dirac bands provide a metallic parallel channel. R_xx ≈ 0.5–1 kΩ, and zero-bias R(T) above T_c is metallic (Fig. 1f). It is unclear how breakdown of a flat-band gap produces a sharp dV/dI peak when the gap is shunted by Dirac carriers with v_F ~ 6×10⁵ m/s.

**Alternative explanations must be excluded:**
- inhomogeneous superconductivity or multiple domains with different I_c;
- residual superconductivity at B_⊥ = 0.2 T (a faint feature remains in Fig. 5a, bottom);
- Joule heating (I²R ~ 1 nW at 600 nA and ~2 kΩ).

Also report whether the peaks are hysteretic in sweep direction.

**The relation v_n = (1/e)dj_n/dn needs justification.** The fitted lines in Fig. 5b give j_n → 0 near ν_tot ≈ 2.1 and growing toward ν_tot ≈ 2.36. This is not obviously j_n = e·n·v_d with n counted from the gap at ν_flat = 2. Please specify:
- the reference density;
- the device width (needed to convert I to j; from the numbers I infer W ≈ 0.5 μm);
- the number of points in the fit.

**The extracted "Fermi velocity" conflicts with the authors' own spectroscopy.** A ~50 meV flat-band bandwidth from Fig. 2d, spread over k_θ ≈ 0.47 nm⁻¹, implies band velocities ~10⁵ m/s. Even the 10 meV continuum-model width implies ~10⁴ m/s. A velocity of 1700 m/s therefore requires explanation, e.g. a strongly renormalized band edge at the correlated gap. Calling it the "flatband Fermi velocity" and concluding a "vanishing Fermi velocity" (abstract, conclusions) is an overstatement.

### 4. The superfluid stiffness comparison depends on excluding the Dirac bands

**Prefactor.** The GL depairing current gives D_s = 3√3π j_c ξ/Φ₀, about 2.6 times the 2π used here. Please justify the prefactor.

**The switching current is only a lower bound.** In a Hall bar, the switching current is generally below the depairing current because of vortex entry, inhomogeneity and heating. D_s is then a lower bound, which would actually strengthen the comparison.

**The conventional baseline is the critical problem.** It uses only n* (~7×10¹⁰ cm⁻²) and m* = ħk_F/v_n, with v_n from the contested analysis in point 3. I reproduce the red line (~4×10⁶ H⁻¹). However, the conventional (Drude) weight of the Dirac carriers is much larger:
- with n_D ≈ 4×10¹¹ cm⁻², v_F ≈ 6×10⁵ m/s and g = 8, e²n_D v_F/(ħk_F,D) ~ 10⁹–10¹⁰ H⁻¹;
- this is more than two orders of magnitude above the measured D_s.

So either the Dirac carriers are essentially not part of the condensate, which conflicts with the "multiband superconductivity" narrative, or the measured I_c is far below depairing and D_s is not reliably extracted.

The statement "one order larger than conventional" is therefore not meaningful without a proper multiband treatment. A direct kinetic-inductance measurement (cf. Refs. 48–49) would be far more convincing.

### 5. The quantum-metric attribution is qualitative

The paper attributes enhanced coupling and stiffness to "quantum metric driven by D when the Dirac bands touch the flat band," but provides no calculation. Given a theory co-author, I would expect:
- continuum-model band structures vs U showing where flat–Dirac touchings occur, mapped onto the experimental D (does it coincide with D ≈ 0.25 V/nm?);
- the integrated quantum metric of the flat band(s) vs U;
- an estimate of the geometric D_s to compare with the measured ~4×10⁷ H⁻¹. A rough estimate, (e²Δ/ħ²) × O(1) geometric factor, does fall in the 10⁷ H⁻¹ range for Δ of tens of μeV, so a quantitative check is feasible and would be valuable.

Two further points need addressing:
- **D asymmetry.** T_c, ξ and D_s are clearly asymmetric in D (T_c ≈ 1.5 K at −0.2 vs 2 K at +0.25 V/nm; D_s peaks of ~3.3 vs 4.7×10⁷ H⁻¹). Ideal ATQG is symmetric under D → −D. This suggests unequal interfacial twist angles, heterostrain or hBN alignment, and the band-touching picture must accommodate it.
- **"Inconsistent with BCS" near the VHS.** The argument that T_c decreasing while n_H diverges near the VHS contradicts BCS is weak. D simultaneously changes hybridization, band topology and competing orders, so DOS is not the only variable.

### 6. Separation of Dirac and flat-band carriers

**Hall density in a multiband system.** n_H is mobility-weighted, n_H = (Σn_iμ_i)²/Σn_iμ_i². If Dirac mobility greatly exceeds flat-band mobility, n_H ≈ n_Dirac is expected regardless of the flat-band density. Thus n_H ≈ n_Dirac at ν_tot ≈ 2.3 does not demonstrate that flat-band carriers are negligible.

**Nature of the dispersive bands.** In ATQG the dispersive sector is not graphene-like, as it is in ATTG. In the Khalaf et al. decomposition it is a TBG-like sector at effective angle θ/(2cos(2π/5)) ≈ 2.6°. Please justify:
- the use of a linear-Dirac Landau-level formula with v_F = 6.1×10⁵ m/s up to μ ≈ 50 meV;
- whether this sector's own band curvature or VHS lies in the relevant energy range.

The description "two Dirac bands" in the Introduction and Fig. 1 should be made precise.

**Internal inconsistency at ν_tot = 4.** n_Dirac ≈ 0.96×10¹² cm⁻² at ν_tot = 4 (Fig. 2e) implies ν_flat ≈ 3.3 there, i.e. the flat band is not full. Yet the text states that carriers for ν_tot > 4 come exclusively from the Dirac bands. The red line in Fig. 2f also appears to extrapolate to zero near ν_tot ≈ 4. Please define ν₀ and reconcile these statements, including the definition of n₀ "at U ≠ 0."

### 7. B_c2 and ξ consistency

ξ = 30 nm at D = 0.26 V/nm implies B_c0 = Φ₀/(2πξ²) ≈ 0.37 T. Yet Supp. Fig. 7c (T = 100 mK, B = 0.2 T, ν = 2.31) shows superconductivity fully suppressed over the whole D range, and Fig. 5a likewise shows near-complete suppression at 0.2 T. Please reconcile.

Please also show B_c(T) for several D values (not only ν = 2.87, D = 0) and state the resistance criterion used for B_c.

### 8. "Pseudogap" and T*

**Fig. 4f does not establish insulating behavior.** It shows dV/dI at fixed I_dc above I_c. Because I_c and the dV/dI peak shift with T, and heating is relevant, a decreasing dV/dI(T) at fixed bias does not imply insulating normal-state behavior. It also contrasts with the metallic zero-bias R(T) in Fig. 1f at the same D. The non-monotonic jump at ~4 K reinforces this concern.

**T* is not unambiguously a pairing temperature.** It is defined as the onset of nonlinear I–V. Nonlinearity can also come from fluctuations, heating or nonlinear normal-state transport. The T*/T_BKT ratio should not be presented as validating quantum-geometric strong coupling without further support.

### 9. 2D superconductivity, homogeneity, and reproducibility

- **BKT analysis.** Show the full exponent α(T) from V ∝ I^α and the crossing α = 3, rather than a single fitted curve.
- **The "interference pattern."** In the Fig. 1e inset this is cited as evidence of phase coherence. Fraunhofer-like modulation in a nominally uniform film usually signals inhomogeneity or junction-like regions, which bears directly on the critical-current analysis.
- **Device homogeneity.** Supp. Fig. 1b shows normal-state resistances differing by a factor of 2–3 between segments. Please give n₀ (twist angle) for each segment, specify which contacts are used for each figure, and show D-dependent data from at least one other segment.
- **Single device.** Data from a second device would substantially strengthen the claims.

### 10. Novelty and context

Superconductivity in ATQG, its D tunability and Pauli-limit violation have been reported (Park et al., Nat. Mater. 2022; Zhang et al., Science 2022; with correlations in Burg et al., 2022). The Dirac-spectroscopy approach follows Shen et al. (ATTG) and Park et al. (MATTG). The critical-current/stiffness methodology follows Tian et al. (TBG). The manuscript should state clearly what is new relative to these works, and compare T_c, B_c∥ and the phase diagram with prior ATQG data.

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The experimental data are rich and potentially valuable. However, the headline interpretive claims (BEC-like pairing, vanishing Fermi velocity, D_s ≫ conventional, quantum-metric origin) currently rely on:
- (i) a pairing density defined to vanish at the point of interest;
- (ii) BCS formulas whose output contradicts the strong-coupling narrative;
- (iii) an unestablished interpretation of the higher critical current;
- (iv) a "conventional" baseline that omits the Dirac carriers, whose contribution would exceed the measured stiffness by orders of magnitude.

Addressing points 1–5 with error analysis, alternative definitions, quantitative theory and, ideally, an independent stiffness measurement or a second device would be needed before the conclusions can be considered supported.
