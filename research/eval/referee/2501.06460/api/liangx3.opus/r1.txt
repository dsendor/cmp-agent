**A. Must address**

**1. The flat/Dirac carrier-density decomposition rests on unverified, model-dependent assumptions.**
All later quantitative claims (ν_flat, n*, ξ·k_F, the conventional D_s) depend on this decomposition.

- **Assumed velocity.** The relation μ_N − μ_0 = v_F√(2eħN_LL·B) uses a single continuum-model velocity, v_F = 6.1×10⁵ m/s, which is never measured.
  - The ~50 meV "flat-band bandwidth" scales linearly with this parameter.
  - In ATQG the "Dirac" bands belong to the dispersive TBG-like sector at an effective angle of θ/(2cos 2π/5) ≈ 2.6°. That dispersion is not linear over a ~50 meV window.
  - Its velocity can also be renormalized by Hartree/exchange effects from the filling flat band, or by hybridization with it.
  - The ~5× broadening (50 vs ~10 meV) may therefore partly reflect band non-linearity or velocity renormalization rather than flat-band broadening.
- **Span versus bandwidth.** The ~50 meV figure is a chemical-potential span that includes cascade/Hartree jumps and correlated gaps. It is not a single-particle bandwidth.
- **Model-comparison mismatch.** The statement that the Dirac density at ν_tot = 4 is "one order larger than simulation" compares an interacting quantity with a rigid-band calculation at a different T and B, without an independent check.
- **Degeneracy and decoupling assumptions.** n_Dirac = 8N eB/h assumes unbroken eightfold Dirac-LL degeneracy and complete Dirac–flat decoupling at D = 0. The authors' own valence-side data contradict this:
  - the ν_LL = −5, −14, −24 sequences and the C = −2 gap deviate from the two-Dirac-band picture;
  - the authors themselves invoke hybridization at D = 0;
  - it is unexplained why hybridization would be absent for ν > 0, where the key ν_flat assignments are made;
  - lattice relaxation at 1.58° is mentioned but not modelled.
- **Limited conditions.** The decomposition is obtained only at D = 0, T = 1.7 K and B up to ~8 T. Superconductivity is analysed at 30 mK, at B → 0 and at finite D.
  - At finite D, flat–Dirac hybridization (Fig. 1c) invalidates a clean separation.
  - Strong B can reorganize the flat-band ground state (Chern states, cascades), so extrapolation to B → 0 is non-trivial.
  - Fig. 4c nevertheless computes ξ·k_F versus D with no explanation of how n* or ν_flat is obtained at D ≠ 0. It implicitly assumes n* is D-independent, even though the stated mechanism is D-driven redistribution between ν_flat and ν_Dirac.
- **No error budget.** No uncertainty is propagated.

The authors should:
- determine the Dirac-sector velocity experimentally, e.g. from LL activation gaps, SdH temperature damping, or μ ∝ √B scaling across several N_LL;
- alternatively, fit the LL crossings with the full (ideally Hartree-corrected) continuum spectrum rather than a single linear v_F;
- propagate the uncertainty into μ, n_Dirac and ν_flat;
- explain the valence-side anomalies;
- extend the decomposition to finite D and low T, or explicitly justify the n* assignment in Fig. 4c;
- cross-check with an independent thermodynamic probe (capacitance/compressibility) where possible;
- show that ν_flat = 2 at ν_tot ≈ 2.36 is robust to these assumptions.

**2. The filling-factor calibration is internally inconsistent at ν_tot = 4.**
- **The flat band would not be full at ν_tot = 4.** n_0, and hence θ = 1.58°, is fixed from the full-filling peak at U ≠ 0, assuming hybridization makes ν_tot = ν_flat there. Yet at D = 0 the paper reports n_Dirac ≈ 0.96×10¹² cm⁻² at ν_tot = 4, i.e. ν_Dirac ≈ 0.66 and ν_flat ≈ 3.34. This conflicts with three statements:
  - that Landau fans emanate from ν_tot = 4 as full filling;
  - that superconductivity "vanishes at ν_tot = 4";
  - that for ν_tot > 4 the linear fit n_H = (ν_tot − ν_0)n_0/4 (Fig. 2f, "only Dirac" line) reflects Dirac carriers only.
- **The "only Dirac" line should not extrapolate to zero at ν_tot = 4.** That line extrapolates to ~0 near ν_tot ≈ 4. If the Dirac bands already hold ~10¹² cm⁻² there, it should not.
- **ν_0 is undefined.** The meaning and fitted value of ν_0 are not given.

The authors should:
- revise the n_Dirac extraction, the n_0 calibration, or the interpretation of ν_0, and state ν_0 explicitly;
- resolve the Fig. 2e–f inconsistencies;
- calibrate the twist angle independently of the D ≠ 0 full-filling assumption (fan convergence points, Brown–Zak or Hofstadter features), for several contact pairs.

**3. The BCS–BEC crossover conclusion from ξ·k_F is largely built into the definition of the pairing density and excludes known carriers.**
- **The dip is guaranteed by construction.** The pairing density n* = |ν_flat − 2|·n_0/4, with k_F = √(4πn*/g) and g = 2, vanishes at ν_flat = 2. The sharp minimum of ξ·k_F to ~2 at ν_tot ≈ 2.36 (Fig. 3c) is therefore guaranteed regardless of the actual pair size.
  - At the working point ν_tot = 2.31 (ν_flat ≈ 1.96): ν* ≈ 0.04, n* ≈ 6×10¹⁰ cm⁻², k_F ≈ 6×10⁷ m⁻¹ and, with ξ = 30 nm, ξ·k_F ≈ 1.8. This reproduces the reported value simply from the definition.
- **The reference point is uncertain at the 100% level.** The location of ν_flat = 2 is itself known only to ~±0.05; the text gives both 2.30 and 2.36. Density inhomogeneity is likely ≳10¹⁰–10¹¹ cm⁻² (concern 13), so n* could plausibly be zero or double its quoted value. No error propagation is given.
- **Degeneracy and definition problems.**
  - g = 2 is applied on both sides of ν_flat = 2, although cascade physics is asymmetric.
  - Superconductivity also exists from ν_tot = 2 (ν_flat < 2), where the meaning of ν* is unclear.
- **Dirac carriers are excluded.** The ~0.45×10¹² cm⁻² Dirac carriers present at ν_tot ≈ 2.3 (where n_H ≈ n_Dirac) are left out of n* and k_F. This contradicts the paper's own "multiband superconductivity" framing.
  - Including them (g = 4–8, ξ = 50–100 nm) gives ξ·k_F ≈ 8–12, i.e. BCS-like.
  - Using the total density, as in Park et al. 2021, also moves ξ·k_F well away from the quoted BEC threshold of ~2.2.
- **The stated bias is in the wrong direction.** The claim that the product is "overestimated since not all carriers are paired" is inconsistent with deliberately excluding a known carrier population. As constructed, the estimate is if anything biased low.
- **The benchmark is undefined.** The "BEC onset ~2.2" from Chen et al. RMP 2024 is applied without specifying which theoretical definition of pair size it corresponds to.

The authors should:
- present ξ·k_F for several bracketing choices: flat band relative to ν_flat = 2; flat band counted from ν_flat = 0 or 4 with g = 4; Dirac only; total density; Dirac carriers included; a D-dependent partition;
- propagate error bars on ν_flat(ν_tot) into n* and ξ·k_F;
- state which conclusions survive, and tone down the BCS–BEC claim otherwise;
- complement the analysis with T_c/T_F, using T_F from the measured density and velocity.

**4. The "conventional" stiffness baseline is not discriminating, so the "one order larger than conventional" claim is an artefact of the benchmark choice.**
- **The baseline inherits the construction of n*.** The red dashed line in Fig. 5d uses e²n*/m*, with the tiny by-construction n* ≈ 6×10¹⁰ cm⁻² and m* = ħk_F/v_n ≈ 4m_e. This gives ~4×10⁶ H⁻¹, artificially suppressed near half filling.
- **Including the Dirac carriers reverses the conclusion.** The Dirac carriers have n ≈ 0.45×10¹² cm⁻², v_F ≈ 6×10⁵ m/s and k_F ~10⁸ m⁻¹. Their conventional contribution alone would be ~10⁹–10¹⁰ H⁻¹, two orders of magnitude (~200×) above the measured value.
- **Either reading undermines a claim.** The data then imply either that the Dirac carriers are not condensed, contradicting the multiband narrative, or that j_s does not measure D_s. Neither is discussed.
- **A multiband alternative is not considered.** Dirac-band-mediated (Dirac-assisted) stiffness could itself enhance D_s over a flat-band-only estimate.

The authors should:
- include the Dirac carriers in the conventional benchmark;
- discuss why their apparent stiffness contribution is so small;
- treat multiband conventional stiffness explicitly as an alternative explanation.

**5. The critical-current-derived D_s is inconsistent with the paper's own BKT temperatures and omits the Ginzburg–Landau prefactor.**
- **BKT cross-check fails.** The Nelson–Kosterlitz relation requires D_s(T_BKT) ≥ 8e²k_BT_BKT/(πħ²). This gives ≈1.3×10⁸ H⁻¹ for T_BKT = 1.6 K and ≈0.9×10⁸ H⁻¹ for 1.1 K.
  - The extracted zero-temperature D_s of 2.6–4×10⁷ H⁻¹ is ~3× below these bounds. It would cap T_BKT at ≲0.5 K, contradicting the observed 1.6 K at the same D.
  - Since D_s(T_BKT) < D_s(0), the discrepancy is even larger.
  - The "conventional" value (~0.4×10⁷ H⁻¹) would correspond to T_BKT ~ 50 mK.
- **Missing prefactor.** D_s = 2πj_sξ/Φ_0 omits the GL depairing prefactor. For a 2D sheet, j_dp = Φ_0D_s/(3√3πξ), giving D_s = 3√3πj_sξ/Φ_0, larger by ~2.6. Even with the prefactor, D_s ≈ 1×10⁸ H⁻¹ remains below the BKT bound.
- **Consequence.** The extracted stiffness is not "large"; it is too small to be consistent with the paper's own data, pointing to an underestimate.

The authors should:
- include the correct prefactor;
- compare D_s(0) with the BKT universal bound at each D, and discuss any shortfall as a limitation of the method;
- corroborate D_s with a direct probe where possible (kinetic inductance, mutual inductance or microwave resonators, as in Banerjee 2024 and Tanaka 2024, refs. 48–49), or at least compare with those results.

**6. The measured I_c is not shown to be an intrinsic depairing current.**
- **Alternative limiting mechanisms.** Using I_c in D_s = 2πj_sξ/Φ_0 assumes a uniform, intrinsic GL depairing current. In a few-µm Hall bar near the BKT regime, switching currents are typically set by:
  - vortex entry or edge barriers;
  - phase slips;
  - weak links or current crowding at contacts and inhomogeneous regions;
  - self-heating.

  Such currents give at best a lower bound of uncertain relation to D_s.
- **Missing information and tests.**
  - The channel width needed to convert I to j is not reported, so the numbers cannot be reproduced.
  - Current distribution and contact geometry are not discussed.
  - No I_c(T) dependence or hysteresis check is shown.
- **Alternative reading of the peak.** The sharp D_s peak at D ≈ 0.26 V/nm (Fig. 5d) could simply track the T_c maximum and ξ minimum through I_c, rather than signal a geometric contribution.

The authors should:
- give the Hall-bar width and the j_s normalization;
- measure I_c(T) and test scaling with the GL form (1 − T/T_c)^{3/2};
- test for hysteresis, sweep-rate dependence and heating (pulsed I–V, thermal estimates), and for contact- or edge-limited behaviour.

**7. The quantum-metric attribution is asserted rather than demonstrated, and the proposed D-driven mechanism is internally unclear.**
- **No calculation of the relevant quantities.** There is no continuum-model or Hartree–Fock calculation, for ATQG at θ = 1.58° as a function of U/D, of:
  - the quantum metric or minimal quantum metric;
  - the geometric superfluid weight;
  - a geometric D_s lower bound, e.g. the Chern/Wilson-loop bound of Xie et al. 2020, to compare with the measured ~4×10⁷ H⁻¹.

  This is despite theory co-authors.
- **The "band touching" picture conflicts with the paper's own band structure.** The Conclusions say the metric is enhanced "when the Dirac bands touch the flat band" under D. Fig. 1b–c and the text say the bands are decoupled and touching at U = 0, and that finite U hybridizes them and opens a gap at the CNP. Which band touching, at which filling, and in which interaction-renormalized (flavor-polarized, ν_flat ≈ 2) band structure enhances the metric is not specified.
- **Predicted trends are never tested.** The predicted D-dependence (non-monotonic D_s peaking at |D| ≈ 0.2–0.26 V/nm and collapsing near the VHS) is never compared with data.
- **Alternatives are not excluded:**
  - D-dependent disorder or inhomogeneity;
  - changes of the flavor-polarized parent state;
  - competing orders near the VHS;
  - heating;
  - multiband conventional or Dirac-assisted stiffness.

The authors should:
- compute band-resolved quantum metric and minimal-metric/Chern bounds versus U, including the Dirac sector and ideally interaction-renormalized bands consistent with the ~50 meV broadening;
- evaluate conventional and geometric superfluid weights in a simple pairing model;
- test whether this reproduces the magnitude and D-dependence of D_s, T_c(D) and T*/T_BKT(D);
- clarify the band-touching picture against Fig. 1c;
- state which observations discriminate between the alternatives;
- otherwise, rephrase the attribution as a hypothesis.

**8. The gap estimate Δ ≈ ħv_n/ξ contradicts the paper's strong-coupling and Pauli-violation claims and uses a relation invalid in the regime the paper invokes.**
- **Implausible gap ratio.** With v_n ≈ 1700 m/s and ξ = 30 nm, Δ ≈ 37 µeV and Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 0.2.
  - In any strong-coupling or BEC-like scenario one expects Δ/k_BT_c ≳ 1.76.
  - A gap about five times smaller than k_BT_c cannot sustain phase coherence up to T_c.
- **Conflict with the in-plane data.** A Pauli field Δ/(√2μ_B) from this Δ is ~0.45 T, yet superconductivity survives to ~7 T in-plane.
  - The paper cannot simultaneously use B_p = 1.86 T/K × T_c (which assumes Δ = 1.76 k_BT_c, giving B_p ≈ 3.7 T) and claim Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 0.2.
  - Either Δ is wrong by more than an order of magnitude, or a pairing state immune to Zeeman pair-breaking must be invoked; neither is discussed.
- **Inapplicable relation.** ξ = ħv_F/(πΔ) is a BCS dispersive-band (Pippard) relation. In the flat-band/quantum-metric regime the authors argue for, ξ is set by the quantum-metric length (e.g. Chen & Law PRL 2024, ref. 45, by co-authors). Using it to "measure" Δ is self-contradictory. The natural reading is that the estimate is invalid, not that the superconductivity is unconventional.

The authors should:
- remove the estimate, or reconcile it explicitly with the in-plane critical field and the strong-coupling claims;
- add a direct gap probe (tunnelling or nonlinear-transport spectroscopy) if a gap is to be quoted.

**B. Should address**

**9. The single-carrier Hall density is used as a carrier count in a two-carrier system.**
- **The quantity is not a carrier count.** n_H = B/(eR_xy) is taken as the "real carrier density". With coexisting flat-band and Dirac carriers of different mobilities, n_H is mobility-weighted and is not the sum of the densities.
- **It is unreliable in this system.** It is also non-monotonic (Fig. 2f) and unreliable near Fermi-surface reconstructions.
- **The key observation is ambiguous.** The observation n_H ≈ n_Dirac at ν_tot ≈ 2.3 is equally consistent with a non-negligible, low-mobility flat-band population. It does not establish that free flat-band carriers are negligible, which the ν_flat = 2 assignment and the exclusion of carriers from n* rely on.

The authors should perform two-carrier fits of ρ_xx(B) and ρ_xy(B) (or R_xx and R_xy), possibly temperature-dependent, to bound the flat-band carrier contribution near ν_flat = 2.

**10. ξ_GL is poorly controlled and may not represent the pair size; there is an apparent inconsistency in B_c0.**
- **Extraction is weak.** ξ = √(Φ_0/2πB_c0) comes from a linear extrapolation of a few B_c(T) points measured only between ~0.6 K and T_c (Supplementary Fig. 2b). The resistance criterion for B_c is not stated, and there is no error analysis.
- **ξ_GL is not the pair size.** In the dirty limit ξ_GL ≈ √(ξ_0ℓ), and no mean free path is estimated. In flat-band/quantum-geometric or BEC regimes, ξ_GL is set by the quantum metric and disorder rather than the BCS pair size. The clean-limit assumption behind comparing ξ_GL·k_F to crossover thresholds is therefore unchecked.
- **Internal inconsistency.** ξ ≈ 30 nm at D = 0.26 V/nm implies B_c0 ≈ 0.37 T. Yet Supplementary Fig. 7c (100 mK, B⊥ = 0.2 T) appears to show superconductivity fully suppressed at all D, and the text says 0.2 T "kills" superconductivity in Fig. 5a. This suggests B_c0 ≲ 0.2 T and ξ ≳ 40 nm.

The authors should:
- state the B_c criterion;
- measure B_c(T) down to base temperature with fits and error bars;
- estimate ℓ from the normal-state resistance and discuss clean versus dirty limits;
- reconcile ξ with the 0.2 T suppression.

**11. The interpretation of the high-current boundary j_n and its conversion to a flat-band "Fermi velocity" are ambiguous.**
- **Two incompatible pictures.** j_n is attributed to Zener–Klein breakdown of a correlated gap, but is then converted to a drift velocity v_n = (1/e)dj_n/dn.
  - A tunnelling threshold is governed by the gap and field-dependent tunnelling rates, not by a saturation current n·e·v_F.
  - The observed linear growth of j_n with doping away from ν_tot ≈ 2.27 (Fig. 5b) looks more like velocity saturation of carriers doped out of a gap (j = n_eff·e·v_sat).
  - The two pictures predict different relations between dj/dn and the band velocity.
- **Unspecified inputs and missing tests.** The density entering dn (total, flat or n*) and the width used to form j are unspecified. No dependence on T, B, D or channel length, and no comparison with heating models, is shown.
- **Dirac carriers are ignored.** Mobile Dirac carriers (v_F ~10⁵–10⁶ m/s) coexist at the same density and should carry substantial current, so assigning v_n to the flat band alone is unexplained.
- **Overstatement.** 1700 m/s is not a "vanishing" velocity. Because v_n enters m*, Δ and the conventional D_s, these ambiguities propagate into concerns 4 and 8.

The authors should:
- state and justify the physical model;
- specify which density enters dn;
- explain why Dirac carriers do not set j_n;
- present the T-, B-, D- and length-dependence of j_n, ruling out heating.

**12. The ancillary "unconventional" signatures are weakly supported and have unaddressed alternatives.**
- **T*/T_BKT.** The rise of T*/T_BKT from 1.45 to 2.19 with D rests on T* defined as the onset of I–V nonlinearity. This is not a pairing-onset temperature. Nonlinearity can arise from:
  - Joule heating;
  - fluctuation conductivity;
  - inhomogeneous T_c or superconducting puddles;
  - non-ohmic normal-state transport, which the authors themselves report.
- **"Pseudogap of pre-paired electrons" (Fig. 4f).** The evidence is a ~12% decrease of dV/dI with T, measured at I_dc = 265–420 nA beyond the switching peak.
  - This is far from equilibrium, where self-heating and Zener-type breakdown matter.
  - Meanwhile the zero-bias R_xx(T) at the same D is metallic above T_c (Fig. 1f).
- **"Inconsistent with BCS" near the VHS.** This assumes a diverging n_H implies a large DOS that should enhance T_c. A diverging Hall density signals a Lifshitz transition or Hall sign change, not directly the DOS. Competing flavor-polarized orders, flavor reconstruction or Lifshitz transitions routinely suppress T_c near a VHS.

The authors should:
- define T* with a heating-robust criterion (onset of excess conductance/paraconductivity, Aslamazov–Larkin or Nernst-type analysis);
- quantify heating for all high-bias data;
- support the pseudogap with zero- or low-bias data or drop it;
- remove or qualify the VHS argument.

**13. All results come from a single device with evident inhomogeneity.**
- **Evidence of inhomogeneity.** Supplementary Fig. 1b shows normal-state resistances differing by factors of ~2–4 between contact pairs, and visibly different onsets and edges of the superconducting windows. This indicates twist-angle or strain inhomogeneity and is inconsistent with the claim of a "uniform angle distribution".
- **Why it matters.** This directly affects n_0, the ν_tot axis, the single "ν_tot ≈ 2.36 = ν_flat = 2" calibration, critical currents and ξ. It is a first-order concern for quantities that depend on n* at the 10¹⁰ cm⁻² level.
- **Missing data and generality.** No contact-pair-resolved Landau fans or twist-angle map are shown. The off-magic angle (1.58° vs ~1.75°) makes generalization to ATMG superconductivity uncertain.

The authors should:
- report twist-angle maps and contact-pair-resolved T_c, ξ, j_s and fans;
- restrict quantitative analysis to the most homogeneous segment;
- ideally reproduce the key trends in at least one more device, preferably both closer to and farther from the magic angle.

**14. The ±D asymmetry is unexplained.**
- **Observation.** T_c, ξ, critical currents and D_s are clearly asymmetric between +D and −D. For example, T_c ≈ 0.6 K at −0.3 V/nm versus ~2 K at +0.26 V/nm, and the D_s peaks in Fig. 5d are asymmetric.
- **Why it matters.** Ideal ATQG should be symmetric under D → −D. The asymmetry points to structural effects (relaxation, hBN alignment, layer-dependent strain) that could also alter the band-hybridization and quantum-metric picture.

The authors should discuss these possibilities and their implications.

**15. The novelty is overstated relative to prior work.**
Each methodological ingredient and several qualitative findings have precedents:
- ATMG/ATQG superconductivity, D-tunability and ~2× Pauli violation: Park et al. Nature 2021 and Nat. Mater. 2022; Hao et al. Science 2021; Zhang et al. Science 2022; Cao et al. Nature 2021; Burg et al. Nat. Mater. 2022;
- ξ·k_F as a BCS–BEC proxy and D-tunable strong coupling: Park 2021; Kim et al. Nature 2022;
- Dirac-LL spectroscopy: Park 2021; Shen et al. Nat. Mater. 2023;
- the two-boundary critical-current method and D_s formula: Tian et al. Nature 2023;
- more direct kinetic-inductance D_s measurements: Banerjee 2024; Tanaka 2024.

Superconductivity peaking just beyond the ν = ±2 cascade is also documented in MATTG/ATMG, so the "unexpected" T_c maximum at ν_flat ≈ 2 is overstated. Without a new probe or a quantitative theory–experiment comparison, the advance is mainly the combination of these tools on one ATQG device. The authors should state precisely what is new relative to these works.

**C. Minor**

**16. Several ancillary claims are speculative.**
The following are asserted without supporting measurements:
- the CDW at ν = 10/3;
- the C = −2 gap at ν_flat = −2;
- "lattice reconstruction" as the cause of the valence-side asymmetry;
- the "collaboration" between the correlated state and superconductivity.

These should be substantiated or softened.

**17. T_c and T_BKT definitions and reporting are inconsistent.**
- **Non-standard T_c.** T_c is defined at 40% of an extrapolated R* from a two-line construction, and the error bars reflect only the 30–50% criterion range.
- **Inconsistent values.**
  - T_c ≈ 2 K at D = 0.26 V/nm (text) versus a "maximum BKT 1.6 K" (abstract) versus T_BKT = 1.1 K at D = 0 is not reconciled.
  - B_p is computed from the 40%-criterion T_c rather than T_BKT. Given concern 8, the comparison with 1.86 T/K is not meaningful as stated.
- **Weak BKT assignment.** The BKT assignment relies only on the V ∝ I³ exponent, without Halperin–Nelson R(T) fits or finite-size/inhomogeneity checks.
- **Labelling.** "D = 0" data are actually at D = −0.03 V/nm, and the U–D mapping is undefined.

The authors should use standard definitions alongside the current one, report them consistently, label the actual D values and define the U–D mapping.

**18. Wording should match what the data support.**
- Use "crossover" rather than "BCS to BEC transitions".
- Remove "vanishing Fermi velocity" and "unexpected".
- Moderate "large superfluid stiffness".
