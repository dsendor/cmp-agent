**Review outline:**

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic.** The manuscript reports transport on a single alternating-twist quadralayer graphene (ATQG) device at θ ≈ 1.58°, slightly below the ATQG magic angle (~1.75°). It aims to:
  - separate flat-band and Dirac-band carrier densities using Dirac-band Landau-level (LL) spectroscopy and Hall data;
  - map the doping- and displacement-field (D) dependence of Tc, the GL coherence length ξ, and ξ·kF as a coupling-strength proxy;
  - extract a flat-band "Fermi velocity" from a high-current breakdown boundary;
  - estimate the superfluid stiffness Ds from the superconducting critical current;
  - attribute the large Ds and strong coupling to quantum-metric contributions.
- **Potentially new elements:**
  - Dirac-band LL spectroscopy in ATQG, giving an apparent interaction-broadened flat-band width of ~50 meV versus ~10 meV in the continuum model.
  - The observation that Tc is maximal near vflat = 2. This contrasts with magic-angle TBG, where superconductivity is suppressed by the correlated insulator at half filling.
  - D-tunable Ds that appears to exceed a "conventional" estimate. This is combined with Pauli-limit violation (~7 T versus a Bp of ~3.7 T) and a T*/TBKT ratio that grows with D.
- **Relation to prior work.** The conceptual framework is largely established:
  - ATMG superconductivity and its D-tunability: Park et al. Nature 2021 and Nat. Mater. 2022; Hao et al. Science 2021; Zhang et al. Science 2022; Burg et al. Nat. Mater. 2022.
  - ξ·kF as a BCS–BEC proxy: Park et al. 2021.
  - Dirac-band spectroscopy: Shen et al. Nat. Mater. 2023.
  - Extracting vn and Ds from two critical-current boundaries: Tian et al. Nature 2023.
  - Quantum-metric contributions to Ds in ATMG/TBG: Banerjee et al. and Tanaka et al., 2024.
- **Overall assessment.** The novelty lies mainly in combining these established methods on ATQG, not in a new probe or a qualitatively new phenomenon. The central mechanistic claim (a quantum-metric-driven, D-tunable BEC-like state) is asserted rather than demonstrated.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Data quality.** The data are of good quality and cover a broad phase space:
  - robust zero-resistance states over 2 ≲ |vtot| ≲ 4 and |D| ≲ 0.4–0.5 V/nm;
  - BKT-type V ∝ I³ behavior;
  - Fraunhofer-like modulation in dV/dI(B⊥);
  - in-plane critical fields well above the weak-coupling Pauli estimate.
- **Multiband accounting.** The paper recognizes that dispersive bands contribute non-negligibly to the carrier density. It attempts a quantitative decomposition, which is often neglected in ATMG analyses where vtot is naively equated with vflat.
- **Tc at half filling.** The Tc maximum near vflat ≈ 2 (vtot ≈ 2.3–2.36) is coincident with a Hall-density reset (cascade). It is an interesting, experimentally clear observation that bears on whether correlated states and superconductivity compete or cooperate.
- **Multiple quantities in one device.** Tc, ξ, critical currents, nH, T*/TBKT and Ds are measured on the same device and plotted versus D. This allows correlated trends to be compared, e.g., the Tc maximum and ξ minimum near D ≈ 0.25 V/nm, and the drop of Ds and Tc as nH diverges near the putative VHS.
- **Possible extension.** With substantial revision, the work could usefully extend critical-current-based stiffness estimates from TBG to a tunable multiband system.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**3.1. The carrier-density decomposition, which underpins all subsequent quantitative claims, rests on unverified assumptions and contains internal inconsistencies.**

- **Assumed Dirac velocity.** μN − μ0 = vF√(2eħN_LL·B) uses vF = 6.1×10⁵ m/s from the continuum model; it is not measured.
  - The ~50 meV "flat-band bandwidth" scales linearly with this assumed vF.
  - The paper simultaneously claims strong interaction renormalization of the flat band. The Dirac (large-effective-angle TBG-sector) velocity may also be renormalized, or hybridized with the flat band, as the authors themselves argue for the valence side at D = 0.
  - No independent determination is given, e.g., from LL gap activation, SdH temperature dependence, or the μ ∝ √B scaling. No uncertainty is propagated.
- **Measurement conditions.** The decomposition is obtained only at D = 0, T = 1.7 K and B up to ~8 T.
  - Superconductivity is analyzed at 30 mK, at B → 0 and, crucially, at finite D. There, Dirac–flat hybridization (Fig. 1c) invalidates a clean separation.
  - Yet Fig. 4c computes ξ·kF versus D without explaining how n* (or vflat) is obtained at D ≠ 0.
  - Strong B can also reorganize the flat-band ground state (Chern states, cascades), so B → 0 extrapolation is non-trivial.
- **Numerical inconsistencies.**
  1. nDirac ≈ 0.96×10¹² cm⁻² at vtot = 4 implies vDirac ≈ 0.66 and hence vflat ≈ 3.34 at vtot = 4. The flat band would then not be full at nominal full filling, yet superconductivity "vanishes at vtot = 4" and n0 is defined from the full-filling peak.
  2. Fig. 2f's "only Dirac" line n = (vtot − v0)n0/4 extrapolates to ~0 near vtot ≈ 4. This contradicts nDirac(vtot = 4) ≈ 0.96×10¹² cm⁻² if, beyond full filling, only Dirac carriers contribute. The meaning and fitted value of v0 are not given.
  3. The claim of "one order larger than simulation" compares an interacting quantity to a rigid-band calculation at a different T and B.
- **Hall-density interpretation.** The single-carrier Hall density nH = B/(eRxy) is used as the "real carrier density".
  - In a two-carrier system, nH is mobility-weighted. The observation nH ≈ nDirac at vtot ≈ 2.3 is therefore equally consistent with a low-mobility, non-negligible flat-band carrier population. It does not establish that the flat-band free carrier density is negligible.
  - No two-carrier fits of ρxx(B) and ρxy(B) are presented.
- **Device homogeneity.** Supplementary Fig. 1b shows segment-dependent resistances differing by factors of ~2–4, and visibly different onsets of the superconducting windows. This suggests twist-angle or strain inhomogeneity.
  - This directly affects n0, the vtot axis, and the meaning of a single "vtot ≈ 2.36 = vflat = 2" calibration.
  - Only one device is reported.

**3.2. The BCS–BEC crossover claims based on ξ·kF are largely a consequence of definitions rather than an independent measurement.**

- **Dip by construction.** kF = √(4πn*/g) with n* defined from v* = |vflat − 2|.
  - kF → 0 by construction at vflat = 2. The sharp minimum of ξ·kF at vtot ≈ 2.36 (Fig. 3c) is therefore guaranteed, regardless of the actual pair size.
  - The authors' own analysis shows Dirac carriers (~0.4×10¹² cm⁻² at vtot ≈ 2.3) are present, yet they are excluded from kF.
  - Including them, or using the total density as in Park et al., would move ξ·kF well away from the quoted BEC threshold (~2.2).
  - The statement that the product is "overestimated since not all carriers are paired" is inconsistent with the choice to exclude a known carrier population. As constructed, the estimate is, if anything, biased low.
- **ξ_GL is not the pair size.** ξ_GL from Bc0 = Φ0/(2πξ²) is not equivalent to the Cooper-pair size entering crossover criteria.
  - In flat-band and quantum-geometric superconductors, ξ_GL is set by the quantum metric and disorder (dirty-limit ξ ~ √(ξ0·ℓ)), not by the BCS pair size.
  - Bc0 is obtained by linear extrapolation of a few points (Supplementary Fig. 2b), with no error analysis.
  - The degeneracy g = 2 (motivated by symmetry breaking) is applied on both sides of vflat = 2, even though cascade physics is asymmetric. Superconductivity also exists from vtot = 2 (vflat < 2), where the definition of v* is unclear.
- **Supporting evidence is ambiguous.**
  - *T*/TBKT.* T* is defined as the onset of I–V nonlinearity, which is not a pairing-onset temperature. Nonlinearity can arise from:
    - heating;
    - normal-state non-ohmicity, which the authors themselves report (insulating-like dV/dI in Fig. 4f);
    - inhomogeneous superconducting puddles.
  - *Pseudogap.* The "pseudogap of pre-paired electrons" is inferred from dV/dI(T) at a large bias (420 nA), where Joule heating and Zener-type breakdown are relevant.
  - *VHS argument.* "Tc decreases near the VHS, inconsistent with BCS" is not a valid argument against BCS. Competing flavor-polarized orders or Lifshitz transitions near a VHS routinely suppress Tc.

**3.3. The quantitative extraction of vn, Δ and Ds is internally inconsistent and, by simple cross-checks, contradicts the paper's own other observations.**

- **Gap ratio versus Pauli violation.** Δ ≈ ħvn/ξ with vn ≈ 1700 m/s and ξ = 30 nm gives Δ ≈ 37 μeV and Δ/kBTc ≈ 0.2.
  - A gap ~8× smaller than in BCS contradicts the claim of strong-coupling or BEC-like superconductivity, where Δ/kBTc should exceed the BCS value.
  - It also contradicts the in-plane data. A paramagnetic limit Bp ≈ Δ/(√2·μB) with this Δ is ~0.45 T, yet superconductivity survives to ~7 T. Either Δ is wrong by more than an order of magnitude, or a pairing state immune to Zeeman pair-breaking must be invoked; neither is discussed.
  - Moreover, ξ = ħvF/(πΔ) is a BCS dispersive-band relation. It is inapplicable precisely in the quantum-metric-dominated regime the paper argues for, where ξ is set by the quantum metric rather than vF.
- **BKT cross-check on Ds.** The 2D relation kB·TBKT = πħ²Ds(TBKT)/(8e²) is a standard sanity check.
  - Ds(0) ≈ 4×10⁷ H⁻¹ gives TBKT ≲ ~0.5 K, below the reported TBKT = 1.6 K at the same D. Since Ds(TBKT) < Ds(0), the discrepancy is even larger.
  - This suggests the critical-current-based Ds is underestimated, possibly by the omitted GL prefactor (~3√3/2) or by non-intrinsic, vortex- or inhomogeneity-limited Ic.
  - The "conventional" Ds (red line, ~0.4×10⁷ H⁻¹) would correspond to TBKT ~ 50 mK. The comparison is therefore not self-consistent, and the "one order larger than conventional" conclusion is not robust.
- **Ic is not demonstrably a depairing current.** Using measured Ic in Ds = 2π·js·ξ/Φ0 assumes a uniform, intrinsic GL depairing current. In a few-μm Hall bar, Ic is typically set by:
  - vortex entry and edge barriers;
  - phase slips;
  - current crowding at contacts or inhomogeneous regions;
  - self-heating.
  
  No sample width, current-distribution analysis, Ic(T) dependence or hysteresis check is given. The sharp peak of Ds at D ≈ 0.26 V/nm (Fig. 5d) could simply track the Tc maximum and ξ minimum through Ic, rather than signaling a geometric contribution.
- **vn is not the flat-band Fermi velocity.** vn = (1/e)·djn/dn from the high-current boundary is a breakdown drift velocity.
  - Its identification with the flat-band Fermi velocity, via a Zener–Klein mechanism, is asserted without tests such as dependence on B, T, D or channel length, or comparison with heating models.
  - The density entering dn (total, flat, or n*?) is unspecified.
  - 1700 m/s is described as "vanishing", which is a semantic overstatement.
  - The conventional estimate e²n*/m* with m* = ħkF/vn inherits the by-construction n* → 0 issue from 3.2, so the conventional baseline is artificially suppressed near half filling.

**3.4. The mechanistic attribution to quantum metric and the overall novelty are insufficiently supported.**

- **No quantum-metric calculation.** No calculation of the quantum metric (or its minimal value) for ATQG at 1.58° as a function of interlayer potential U is presented, despite a theory co-author.
  - The claim that D "drives band touching" and thereby enhances the metric conflicts with Fig. 1c, where finite U hybridizes the bands and opens a gap at CNP.
  - The geometric Ds bound, and its predicted D-dependence (maximum near 0.25 V/nm, collapse near the VHS), are never compared with data.
  - The attribution is therefore post hoc. Alternatives are not excluded, including:
    - D-dependent disorder or inhomogeneity;
    - changes of the flavor-polarized parent state;
    - Dirac-band-mediated (multiband) stiffness, which in a multiband system could itself enhance Ds over the flat-band-only "conventional" estimate.
- **Incremental relative to prior work.**
  - ATQG superconductivity, D-tunability, Pauli violation and ξ·kF-based BCS–BEC arguments are established for ATMG (Park 2021/2022; Zhang 2022; Cao 2021).
  - The two-boundary critical-current method and Ds formula are adopted from Tian et al. 2023.
  - Kinetic-inductance studies (Banerjee, Tanaka 2024) already provide more direct Ds measurements in the same material family.
  - Without a decisive new probe or a quantitative theory–experiment comparison, the advance over the literature is modest for a top-tier venue.
- **Speculative ancillary claims, single device, presentation problems.**
  - *Speculative claims:* the CDW at 10/3, the C = −2 gap at vflat = −2, "lattice reconstruction" for the valence asymmetry, and the "collaboration" between correlated state and superconductivity are asserted without supporting measurements.
  - *Single device:* the results come from one device with evident segment-to-segment variations.
  - *Inconsistent Tc and TBKT values:* Tc ≈ 2 K at D = 0.26 V/nm in the text, versus a "maximum BKT 1.6 K" in the abstract, versus TBKT = 1.1 K at D = 0. Bp is computed from the 40%-criterion Tc rather than TBKT.
  - *Display errors in equations,* e.g., the square root in ξ and in kF.
  - *Typos:* "quatralayer", "AQTG", "vesus/verus", "coherent length", and a duplicated "Supplementary Note 6".

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

**4.1. Make the carrier-density decomposition independent, self-consistent and error-bounded.**
- Determine the Dirac-band velocity experimentally, e.g., from LL activation gaps or SdH temperature damping, or from the μ ∝ √B scaling across several N_LL. Propagate the uncertainty into the bandwidth and nDirac.
- Perform two-carrier fits of ρxx(B) and ρxy(B) to bound the flat-band carrier contribution near vflat = 2.
- Extend the decomposition to finite D and lower T, or explicitly justify how n* is assigned in Fig. 4c.
- Resolve the vtot = 4 and v0 inconsistencies in Fig. 2e–f.
- Report twist-angle maps for all contact pairs, restrict quantitative analysis to the most homogeneous segment, and ideally reproduce key results in a second device.

**4.2. Recast the coupling-strength analysis so that it does not depend on a by-construction definition.**
- Present ξ·kF for several bracketing choices: total density, flat-band density relative to vflat = 2, and the inclusion of Dirac carriers. Show which conclusions survive.
- Report Bc2(T) over the full temperature range with fits and error bars, and discuss the clean versus dirty limit (estimate the mean free path ℓ from the normal-state resistance).
- Complement with Tc/TF, using TF from the measured density and velocity.
- Define T* by a criterion robust to heating, e.g., the onset of excess conductance or paraconductivity, or Aslamazov–Larkin/Nernst-type analysis. Quantify heating at high-bias dV/dI before invoking a pseudogap.

**4.3. Put the stiffness and gap estimates on a quantitatively consistent footing.**
- Cross-check Ds against TBKT using the BKT universal relation, and include the GL depairing prefactor.
- Give the channel width and the js normalization. Measure Ic(T) and check scaling with the GL form (1 − T/Tc)^{3/2}. Test for hysteresis or heating, and for contact or edge-limited behavior.
- Remove the Δ ≈ ħvn/ξ estimate, or reconcile it explicitly with the in-plane critical field and the strong-coupling claims.
- Test the Zener–Klein interpretation of jn through its dependence on T, B, D and segment length, and specify which density enters dn.
- Where possible, compare with a direct Ds probe, e.g., kinetic inductance, mutual inductance or microwave resonators, as in Banerjee and Tanaka.

**4.4. Provide theory support, sharpen the novelty and tighten presentation.**
- Compute the band-resolved quantum metric and the minimal-metric Ds bound for ATQG at 1.58° versus U, ideally with interaction-renormalized bands consistent with the ~50 meV broadening.
- Compare the predicted D-dependence with Fig. 5d, and clarify the "band touching" picture against Fig. 1c.
- Explicitly discuss multiband (Dirac-assisted) stiffness and disorder as alternative explanations.
- State clearly what is new relative to Park 2021/2022, Zhang 2022, Tian 2023 and the 2024 kinetic-inductance studies.
- Soften or substantiate the speculative claims (CDW at 10/3, C = −2, "vanishing" velocity, pseudogap).
- Unify the Tc and TBKT definitions used throughout, and use the appropriate Tc for the Pauli estimate.
- Correct the equations and typographical errors.
