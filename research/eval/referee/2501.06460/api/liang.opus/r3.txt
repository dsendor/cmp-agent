# Review outline: "Probing electric field tunable multiband superconductivity in alternating twisted quadralayer graphene"

## 1. Significance and novelty

**What the manuscript reports.** It presents transport on a single dual-gated alternating-twist quadralayer graphene (ATQG) device at θ ≈ 1.58°, which is below the ATQG magic angle of ~1.75°. The main claims are:
- **Robust superconductivity:** zero resistance over 2 ≲ |ν_tot| ≲ 4 and |D| ≲ 0.4–0.5 V/nm, with a D-tunable dome (T_c from ~1.2 K at D = 0 to ~2 K at D ≈ 0.26 V/nm). Supporting evidence includes V ∝ I³ BKT behaviour (T_BKT = 1.1–1.6 K) and in-plane critical fields (~7 T) roughly twice the Pauli limit.
- **Separation of band contributions:** Dirac-band "spectroscopy" from Landau fans separates Dirac and flat-band filling. It yields an apparent flat-band chemical-potential span of ~50 meV (vs. ~10 meV from the continuum model) and assigns ν_flat = 2 to ν_tot ≈ 2.36.
- **BCS–BEC crossover:** the product ξ_GL·k_F, computed with a pairing density counted from ν_flat = 2, is used to argue for a doping-tuned and D-tuned crossover.
- **Critical-current analysis:**
  - a flat-band "drift velocity" v_n ≈ 1700 m/s from the high-current boundary;
  - a pairing gap estimate Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 0.2;
  - a superfluid stiffness D_s ≈ 4×10⁷ H⁻¹, about 10× the "conventional" value.
  These are attributed to quantum-metric contributions enhanced by D.

**Assessment of significance.**
- **Topic:** the role of dispersive versus flat bands, and of band geometry, in moiré superconductivity is timely and of broad interest.
- **Potentially useful contributions:**
  - an off-magic-angle ATQG device that still superconducts robustly;
  - a quantitative attempt to partition carrier density between Dirac and flat sectors;
  - a D-resolved D_s(D) curve.

**Assessment of novelty.** Novelty is moderate to incremental. Each methodological ingredient has been introduced before:
- superconductivity in ATQG/ATMG: Park et al. Nat. Mater. 2022; Zhang et al. Science 2022; Burg et al. 2022 for correlations;
- Dirac-LL spectroscopy of the flat band: Park 2021; Shen et al. 2023;
- ξ·k_F as a BCS–BEC diagnostic and D-tunable strong coupling: Park et al. Nature 2021; Kim et al. 2022;
- D_s from critical current and drift velocity from the "correlated insulator" critical current: Tian et al. Nature 2023;
- quantum-metric superfluid stiffness via kinetic inductance: Banerjee 2024; Tanaka 2024.

The new element is mainly combining these tools in one ATQG device. The headline physical conclusions (BEC-like pairing, "vanishing Fermi velocity", quantum-metric-dominated stiffness) depend critically on quantitative extractions whose validity is questionable (Section 3).

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **Clear, multi-faceted superconducting data set** on a less-studied member of the ATMG family:
  - zero resistance over a wide (ν, D) range;
  - V ∝ I³ BKT signature;
  - in-plane fields beyond the Pauli limit;
  - D-dependent T_c dome and critical currents.
- **Systematic effort to disentangle multiband contributions.**
  - Landau-fan identification of the ν_LL = +4, +12, +20 … sequences tied to the two Dirac cones.
  - The extracted Dirac density (~0.96×10¹² cm⁻² at ν_tot = 4) is internally consistent with a ~50 meV chemical-potential span and a Dirac velocity of 6.1×10⁵ m/s. My check: n = g·k_F²/4π with g = 8 at μ ≈ 50 meV gives ≈ 1×10¹² cm⁻².
  - This quantitatively illustrates the interaction-driven "non-rigid" flat band in a multilayer setting.
- **Interesting phenomenology near ν_flat ≈ 2.**
  - The T_c maximum lies near the cascade point rather than being suppressed by a correlated insulator, unlike TBG.
  - Superconductivity is enhanced at intermediate D and collapses as the system approaches a Hall-density divergence (Lifshitz/VHS-like) at |D| > 0.3 V/nm.
- **D-resolved critical-current maps** (Fig. 5c, SI Fig. 7) and the extracted D_s(D) give a concrete, testable target for theories of geometric superfluid weight in multiband moiré systems.
- **Generally well-organized presentation.** Supplementary material covers Hall/cascade data, the T_c/B_c0 definitions, the in-plane-field response and T*/T_BKT.

## 3. Potential reasons for rejection

### Reason 1: The carrier-density bookkeeping is internally inconsistent, and the BCS–BEC conclusion is largely built into the definition of the pairing density

- **Circular definition of n\*.**
  - The pairing density is defined as n\* = |ν_flat − 2|·n₀/4, which vanishes by construction at ν_flat = 2. The dip of ξ·k_F to ~2 near ν_tot ≈ 2.36 (Fig. 3c) is therefore largely a consequence of this definition, not an independent measurement of pair size.
  - At the working point ν_tot = 2.31 (ν_flat ≈ 1.96), ν\* ≈ 0.04 and n\* ≈ 6×10¹⁰ cm⁻². With k_F = √(4πn\*/2) ≈ 6×10⁷ m⁻¹ and ξ = 30 nm, ξ·k_F ≈ 1.8, which reproduces the reported value of ~2.
  - The onset of ν_flat = 2 is itself only located to within ~±0.05 (the text gives both 2.30 and 2.36). Twist-angle and density inhomogeneity is likely ≳10¹⁰–10¹¹ cm⁻² (SI Fig. 1b shows contact-dependent normal-state resistances differing by several times). n\* is therefore uncertain by ≥100%, and could be zero or double.
  - No error propagation is provided.
  - The D-dependence of ξ·k_F (Fig. 4c) assumes n\* is independent of D, even though the stated mechanism is D-driven flat–Dirac hybridization, which redistributes ν_flat vs ν_Dirac.
- **Dirac carriers are excluded from the pairing density, contradicting the multiband narrative.**
  - The paper stresses a "non-negligible contribution of dispersive bands" and multiband superconductivity. Yet the ~0.45×10¹² cm⁻² Dirac carriers present at ν_tot ≈ 2.3 are omitted from n\* and k_F.
  - Including them would raise k_F by roughly an order of magnitude and push ξ·k_F deep into the BCS regime.
  - The conclusions thus hinge on an unstated assumption that only flat-band carriers counted from ν_flat = 2 pair, with degeneracy g = 2. This needs to be justified, or alternative scenarios shown.
- **Filling factors are inconsistent.**
  - n₀ (and hence θ = 1.58°) is fixed from the full-filling peak at U ≠ 0, under the assumption that hybridization makes ν_tot = ν_flat there.
  - At D = 0 the authors report n_Dirac ≈ 0.96×10¹² cm⁻² (ν_Dirac ≈ 0.66) at ν_tot = 4. That implies ν_flat ≈ 3.34 at ν_tot = 4, so the flat band would not be full there.
  - This conflicts with three other statements: that Landau fans emanate from ν_tot = 4 as full filling, that superconductivity "vanishes at ν_tot = 4", and that above ν_tot > 4 the linear fit n_H = (ν_tot − ν₀)n₀/4 (with ν₀ apparently ≈ 4 in Fig. 2f) reflects Dirac carriers only.
  - If Dirac bands already hold ~10¹² cm⁻² at ν_tot = 4, n_H should not extrapolate to zero there. Either the n_Dirac extraction, the n₀ calibration, or the interpretation of ν₀ must be revised, and ν₀ should be stated explicitly.
- **The Hall density is used as a carrier count in a two-carrier system.**
  - n_H = B/(eR_xy) is not the sum of carrier densities when two carrier types with different mobilities coexist (flat band and Dirac band). The "minimal assumption" n_H ≈ n_real, and the conclusion n_H ≈ n_Dirac at ν_tot ≈ 2.3, are not justified.
  - The ~50 meV "bandwidth" is a chemical-potential span that includes cascade/Hartree jumps and correlated gaps. It is not a single-particle bandwidth.
  - It also depends on a model-dependent Dirac velocity (6.1×10⁵ m/s from the continuum model, with no interaction renormalization).

### Reason 2: The quantitative extractions of v_n, Δ and D_s rely on formulas used outside their validity and are mutually inconsistent with other data in the paper

- **Δ ≈ ħv_n/ξ contradicts the paper's own framework and data.**
  - The Pippard/BCS relation ξ ≈ ħv_F/Δ applies to dispersive BCS superconductors. In the flat-band/quantum-metric regime the authors invoke, ξ is set by the quantum-metric length, not ħv/Δ (e.g., Chen & Law PRL 2024, ref. 45, by co-authors of this paper). Using it to "measure" Δ is therefore self-contradictory.
  - The result, Δ ≈ 37 μeV ≈ 0.2 k_BT_c at T_c = 2 K, is physically implausible: a gap five times smaller than k_BT_c cannot sustain phase coherence up to T_c.
  - It is also inconsistent with the Pauli-limit claim. A Pauli field estimated from this Δ, Δ/(√2 μ_B), would be ~0.45 T, yet superconductivity survives to ~7 T in-plane.
  - The paper cannot simultaneously use Bp = 1.86 T/K × T_c (which assumes Δ = 1.76 k_BT_c) and claim Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 0.2. The more natural reading is that the estimate is invalid, not that the superconductivity is "unconventional".
- **D_s from the switching current conflicts with the BKT temperature reported in the same paper.**
  - The Nelson–Kosterlitz universal jump requires D_s(T_BKT) ≥ 8e²k_BT_BKT/(πħ²) ≈ 1.3×10⁸ H⁻¹ for T_BKT = 1.6 K.
  - The reported zero-temperature D_s ≈ 4×10⁷ H⁻¹ is about 3× smaller. It would bound T_BKT ≲ 0.5 K, contradicting the observed 1.6 K at the same D.
  - The formula D_s = 2πj_sξ/Φ₀ also omits the Ginzburg–Landau depairing prefactor (3√3/2 ≈ 2.6). Even with this prefactor, D_s ≈ 1×10⁸ H⁻¹ remains below the BKT bound.
  - Measured switching currents in 2D, possibly inhomogeneous, current-biased samples are typically limited by vortex entry, phase slips, weak links or heating, not intrinsic depairing. They are lower bounds of uncertain relation to D_s.
  - The device width needed to convert I to j is not reported, so the numbers cannot be reproduced.
- **The "conventional" stiffness baseline is not discriminating.**
  - The red dashed line uses e²n\*/m\* with the tiny n\* ≈ 6×10¹⁰ cm⁻² and m\* = ħk_F/v_n ≈ 4m_e. This reproduces ~4×10⁶ H⁻¹, so the "order-of-magnitude enhancement" inherits the arbitrariness of n\* from Reason 1.
  - If the Dirac carriers (~4.5×10¹¹ cm⁻², v_F ≈ 6×10⁵ m/s) participated in the condensate, their conventional contribution alone would be ~10¹⁰ H⁻¹ (my estimate), ~200× larger than measured.
  - The data thus imply either that Dirac carriers are not condensed, contradicting the multiband narrative, or that j_s does not measure D_s. Neither possibility is discussed.
- **The interpretation of v_n is ambiguous.**
  - The higher boundary j_n is described as Zener–Klein breakdown of a correlated gap. However, j_n grows linearly with doping away from ν_tot ≈ 2.27 (Fig. 5b), which looks more like velocity saturation of carriers doped out of a gap (j = n_eff·e·v_sat). These two pictures predict different relations between dj/dn and the band velocity.
  - Highly mobile Dirac carriers are present at the same density and should carry substantial current. Why the breakdown current would reflect the flat-band velocity alone is unexplained.
  - Calling 1700 m/s a "vanishing Fermi velocity" overstates the result.

### Reason 3: The attribution to quantum metric is asserted, not demonstrated, and no theory for ATQG is provided

- **No geometric quantity is calculated.**
  - The manuscript has theorists among its co-authors but contains no continuum-model or Hartree–Fock calculation of the quantum metric, minimal quantum metric, or geometric superfluid weight of ATQG as a function of U/D at θ = 1.58°. It also gives no estimate of the geometric D_s lower bound (e.g., the Chern/Wilson-loop bound of Xie et al. 2020) to compare with the measured ~4×10⁷ H⁻¹.
  - Without such a comparison, "attributed to substantial quantum metric contributions" is a hypothesis, not a result.
- **The proposed D-driven mechanism is internally unclear.**
  - The Conclusions state that the quantum metric is enhanced "when the Dirac bands touch the flat band" under D. Yet Fig. 1b–c and the text say the Dirac and flat bands are decoupled and touching at U = 0, and that D hybridizes them and opens a gap at the CNP.
  - Which band touching, at which filling, and in which interaction-renormalized (flavor-polarized, ν_flat ≈ 2) band structure enhances the metric must be specified.
  - The non-monotonic D_s(D) peaks at ±0.2–0.26 V/nm should be reproduced at least qualitatively.
- **Other "unconventional" signatures are weakly supported.**
  - **T*/T_BKT (1.45 → 2.19) as quantum-geometry evidence:** T* is defined as the onset of nonlinear I–V, which can arise from fluctuation conductivity, Joule heating, inhomogeneous T_c, or nonlinear normal-state transport. It is not a measurement of a pairing temperature.
  - **"Insulating" normal state / pseudogap (Fig. 4f):** this rests on a ~12% decrease of dV/dI with T measured at I_dc = 420 nA beyond the switching peak, where self-heating is significant. Meanwhile the zero-bias R_xx(T) at the same D is metallic above T_c (Fig. 1f).
  - **"Inconsistent with BCS" near the VHS:** this assumes that divergent n_H implies large DOS and should enhance T_c. A diverging Hall density signals a Lifshitz transition or Hall sign change, not directly the DOS. Competing orders or flavor reconstruction near the VHS could also suppress superconductivity.

### Reason 4: Limited robustness and generality of the experimental evidence (single device, possible inhomogeneity, uncontrolled artefacts)

- **Single device, single angle.**
  - All conclusions come from one device at one twist angle.
  - SI Fig. 1b shows contact-pair-dependent normal-state resistances (differing by several times) and different superconducting-window edges. This is inconsistent with the claim of a "uniform angle distribution".
  - No contact-pair-resolved Landau fans or twist-angle map are shown. For quantities that depend on n\* at the 10¹⁰ cm⁻² level, on ξ from B_c, and on j_s, inhomogeneity is a first-order concern.
- **Unexplained ±D asymmetry.**
  - T_c, ξ, critical currents and D_s are clearly asymmetric between +D and −D (e.g., T_c ≈ 0.6 K at −0.3 V/nm vs ~2 K at +0.26 V/nm; asymmetric D_s peaks in Fig. 5d). An ideal ATQG should be symmetric under D → −D.
  - This points to structural asymmetry (relaxation, hBN alignment, layer-dependent strain) that is not discussed. Any of these could also affect the band-hybridization and quantum-metric story.
- **Coherence-length and critical-temperature definitions.**
  - ξ_GL is obtained from a linear extrapolation of B_c(T) to T = 0 with an unstated B_c criterion. The clean-limit assumption implicit in comparing ξ_GL·k_F to BCS–BEC thresholds is not checked; in the dirty limit, ξ_GL ≈ √(ξ₀ℓ).
  - In the BEC regime, ξ_GL is not the pair size.
  - T_c is defined at 40% of an extrapolated R\*, which is non-standard, and the error bars reflect only the 30–50% criterion range.
  - The BKT assignment relies only on the V ∝ I³ exponent, without Halperin–Nelson R(T) fits or a check for finite-size/inhomogeneity effects.
- **Claims relative to prior work are overstated.**
  - "Unexpected" maximum T_c at ν_flat ≈ 2: superconductivity peaking just beyond the ν = ±2 cascade is well documented in MATTG/ATMG.
  - The Pauli-limit violation by ~2× has also been reported in MATTG/ATMG.
  - "BCS to BEC transitions" should be "crossover".
  - "Vanishing Fermi velocity" is not supported by 1700 m/s.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Make the carrier bookkeeping rigorous and propagate its uncertainty.**
   - Resolve the ν_tot = 4 / ν₀ / n₀ inconsistency explicitly, and state ν₀.
   - Calibrate the twist angle independently of the D ≠ 0 full-filling assumption (fan convergence points, Brown–Zak or Hofstadter features), and do so for several contact pairs.
   - Replace single-carrier n_H with two-carrier fits of R_xx(B) and R_xy(B) (or ρ_xx and ρ_xy) to separate flat and Dirac contributions.
   - Quote error bars on ν_flat(ν_tot) and propagate them into n\*, ξ·k_F and D_s^conv.
   - Show how the BCS–BEC conclusions change under alternative pairing-density assumptions: Dirac carriers included; n\* counted from ν_flat = 0 or 4 with g = 4; D-dependent partition.

2. **Re-derive Δ and D_s with appropriate formulas and independent cross-checks.**
   - Drop or heavily qualify Δ ≈ ħv_n/ξ, and reconcile any gap estimate with the in-plane critical field.
   - Report device dimensions and include the correct depairing prefactor.
   - Explicitly compare D_s(0) with the BKT universal bound at each D.
   - Use additional methods where possible: Halperin–Nelson fits, Fraunhofer or SQUID-type measurements, and ideally kinetic inductance or microwave resonator measurements as in refs. 48–49. Tunnelling spectroscopy would address Δ.
   - For the high-current boundary, state the physical model (Zener–Klein vs velocity saturation), justify why Dirac carriers do not set it, and present its T- and B-dependence.

3. **Provide a theoretical calculation for ATQG at θ = 1.58°.**
   - Compute band structures and quantum metric (and minimal quantum metric / Chern bounds) versus U, including the Dirac sector and, ideally, Hartree or Hartree–Fock renormalization at ν_flat ≈ 2.
   - Evaluate the conventional and geometric superfluid weights within a simple pairing model.
   - Show whether this reproduces the magnitude of D_s, its D-dependence (peaks at |D| ≈ 0.2–0.26 V/nm, collapse near the VHS), and the role of the Dirac bands.
   - Clarify which "band touching" is responsible.
   - Without this, rephrase the quantum-metric attribution as a hypothesis.

4. **Strengthen experimental robustness and temper the claims.**
   - Add at least one more device, preferably closer to and farther from the magic angle, or contact-pair-resolved data on the present device for T_c, ξ, j_s and the fans.
   - Discuss the ±D asymmetry and possible hBN alignment or relaxation.
   - Rule out heating for all high-bias quantities (j_n, Fig. 4f, T\*), using pulsed I–V and thermal estimates, and support the "pseudogap" claim with zero-bias or low-bias data or drop it.
   - Specify the B_c criterion, justify the clean-limit use of ξ_GL, and use a standard T_c definition alongside the current one.
   - Revise wording: "crossover" not "transition"; remove "vanishing Fermi velocity" and "unexpected". Place the work more precisely relative to Park 2021/2022, Zhang 2022, Burg 2022, Shen 2023 and Tian 2023.
   - Fix editorial issues:
     - typos: "AQTG", "quatralayer", "valance", "coherent length", "vesus/verus";
     - formatting: the duplicated "Supplementary Note 6" and the garbled v_n formula;
     - missing information: gate dielectric thicknesses and Hall-bar width.
