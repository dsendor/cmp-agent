Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Scope of the work.** The manuscript reports transport on one dual-gated alternating-twist quadralayer graphene (ATQG) device at θ ≈ 1.58°, slightly below the ATQG magic angle of ~1.75°. The reported observations are:
  - superconductivity spanning |ν_tot| ≈ 2–4 for |D| < ~0.5 V/nm;
  - T_c up to ~2 K and T_BKT up to 1.6 K;
  - in-plane critical fields of ~7 T, about twice the BCS Pauli limit.
- **Claimed advances.**
  - **Band disentanglement.** The authors separate flat-band and Dirac-band carrier densities by "Dirac spectroscopy" of Landau-level crossings at D = 0. They argue that ν_flat = 2 occurs at ν_tot ≈ 2.36 and that the flat band is strongly broadened (~50 meV versus ~10 meV in the continuum model).
  - **T_c at half filling.** A local T_c maximum sits at flat-band half filling (ν_flat ≈ 2), unlike magic-angle TBG.
  - **BCS–BEC tuning.** Doping and D tune the system from BCS-like to near-BEC behaviour, judged by the ratio ξ·k_F.
  - **Critical-current quantities.** From two critical-current boundaries they extract a flat-band "Fermi velocity" (~1700 m/s), a pairing gap (Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 0.2) and a superfluid stiffness (~4×10⁷ H⁻¹). The stiffness is claimed to exceed the conventional contribution by an order of magnitude and is attributed to the quantum metric.
- **Novelty in context.** The topic is timely: multiband moiré superconductivity, the role of quantum geometry, and the BCS–BEC crossover. However, most methods and many qualitative findings have direct precedents:
  - robust and Pauli-violating superconductivity across the ATMG family, including ATQG (Park et al. Nat. Mater. 2022; Zhang et al. Science 2022; Cao et al. Nature 2021);
  - ξ·k_F-based strong-coupling arguments and D-tunable coupling in alternating-twist trilayer graphene (Park et al. Nature 2021; Kim et al. Nature 2022);
  - Dirac spectroscopy in alternating-twist trilayer graphene (Shen et al. Nat. Mater. 2023);
  - critical-current-based Fermi velocity and superfluid stiffness with quantum-metric interpretation in TBG (Tian et al. Nature 2023);
  - kinetic-inductance stiffness in ATMG and TBG (Banerjee 2024; Tanaka 2024).
- **What is genuinely new.**
  - Application of these methods to ATQG at an off-magic angle.
  - The T_c maximum at ν_flat ≈ 2.
  - The D-dependence of the extracted stiffness.
- **Overall assessment of significance.** The added value depends almost entirely on whether the quantitative extractions (n*, ξ·k_F, v_n, Δ, D_s) are reliable and internally consistent. As argued below, they currently are not, which limits the significance for a top-tier journal.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Data quality.** The data on a challenging four-layer moiré system appear to be of high quality.
  - There are clear zero-resistance regions.
  - V ∝ I³ BKT behaviour is observed.
  - Fraunhofer-like interference is weakly visible.
  - Different contact pairs give comparable superconducting windows.
  - Sizable critical currents allow multiple complementary measurements on the same device.
- **Multi-probe dataset.** The study combines R_xx(ν, D, T), Landau fans, Hall density, dI/dV critical-current maps, and parallel-field response. This is a broad dataset for one system.
- **Half-filling observation.** The T_c maximum near ν_flat ≈ 2 and the apparent cooperation between the correlated cascade and superconductivity are interesting. If substantiated, they distinguish ATQG from TBG.
- **Conceptual reach.** The attempt to apportion carriers between flat and dispersive bands, and then link superconducting properties to them, addresses an important open question in ATMG. The D-tunable non-monotonic stiffness, peaking near D ≈ 0.26 V/nm, could be useful input for theories of geometric superfluid weight in multiband systems.
- **Supporting observations.**
  - The fractional 10/3 feature, interpreted as a CDW-like state.
  - Chern-number assignments.
  - Pauli-limit violation.

  These add phenomenology that the moiré community may find useful.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**Reason 1 – The flat/Dirac band disentanglement is model-dependent and partially contradicted by the authors' own data.**
- **Fixed Dirac velocity.** The chemical potential μ_N − μ_0 = v_F√(2eħN_LL B) is computed with a single continuum-model velocity v_F ≈ 6.1×10⁵ m/s.
  - In ATQG the "Dirac bands" belong to the dispersive TBG-like sector, with an effective angle of θ/(2cos 2π/5) ≈ 2.6°. That dispersion is not linear over the ~50 meV window, and its velocity can be renormalized by Hartree/exchange effects from the filling flat band.
  - The inferred flat-band "bandwidth" (~50 meV, 5× the model value) therefore scales directly with an assumed parameter. It may partly reflect band-shape non-linearity or velocity renormalization rather than flat-band broadening.
  - No error budget or sensitivity analysis is given.
- **Degeneracy and decoupling assumptions.** The relation n_Dirac = 8N eB/h assumes:
  - an unbroken eightfold degeneracy of the Dirac Landau levels;
  - complete decoupling of the Dirac and flat bands at D = 0.

  Yet the authors' own valence-side data contradict these assumptions. The ν_LL = −5, −14, −24 sequences and the C = −2 gap deviate from the two-Dirac-band picture, and the authors themselves invoke hybridization at D = 0. If hybridization is present for ν < 0, it is unclear why it is absent for ν > 0, where the key ν_flat assignments are made.
  - Lattice relaxation at 1.58° is mentioned but not modelled.
- **Unresolved density discrepancies.**
  - The conversion ν_flat = ν_tot − 4n_Dirac/n_0 leaves ν_flat ≈ 3.3 at ν_tot = 4, whereas n_0 is calibrated from a full-filling peak "at U ≠ 0".
  - The Dirac-band density at ν_tot = 4 is "one order larger than simulation". This is attributed to broadening without an independent check.
  - The Hall density n_H is used as "an approximation for the real carrier density". Yet it is non-monotonic in Fig. 2f and is known to be unreliable in multi-carrier systems with Fermi-surface reconstructions.
- **Consequence.** The central quantity used later, n* = |ν_flat − 2|·n_0/4, inherits all of these uncertainties.

**Reason 2 – The BCS–BEC crossover / strong-coupling claim is largely circular and relies on an ill-suited coherence length.**
- **Definition of n* forces the minimum.** n* is defined to vanish at ν_flat = 2, so k_F = √(4πn*/g) → 0 and ξ·k_F → 0 there by construction. The "dip to ~2" in Fig. 3c is therefore set by the chosen reference point and by g = 2, not by a measured pair size.
- **Dirac carriers are excluded.**
  - At ν_tot ≈ 2.3, n_H ≈ n_Dirac ≈ 0.45×10¹² cm⁻². The free carriers present are thus predominantly Dirac carriers.
  - Using this density (g = 4–8) with ξ = 50–100 nm gives, by my estimate, ξ·k_F ≈ 8–12, i.e. BCS-like.
  - Excluding these carriers is inconsistent with the paper's own "multiband superconductivity" framing.
- **ξ is not the pair size.** The coherence length is the GL length ξ = √(Φ₀/2πB_c0), obtained by linear extrapolation of B_c(T) measured only between ~0.6 K and T_c.
  - In the dirty limit, ξ_GL ≈ √(ξ₀ℓ) and is not the Cooper-pair size; no mean free path is estimated.
  - The B_c criterion (resistance fraction) is not stated.
  - The paper's own caveat ("we overestimate the product since not all carriers are paired") shows the metric is not quantitatively controlled.
- **Possible internal inconsistency in B_c.**
  - ξ ≈ 30 nm at D = 0.26 V/nm implies B_c0 ≈ 0.37 T.
  - However, Supplementary Fig. 7c (T = 100 mK, B⊥ = 0.2 T) appears to show superconductivity fully suppressed at all D. The text also states that superconductivity is "killed" by 0.2 T in Fig. 5a.
  - This suggests B_c0 ≲ 0.2 T, i.e. ξ ≳ 40 nm.
- **Undefined comparison benchmark.** The "BEC onset ~2.2" value from Chen et al. RMP 2024 is applied without specifying the theoretical definition of pair size it corresponds to.

**Reason 3 – The critical-current analysis (v_n, Δ, D_s) is physically and quantitatively inconsistent.**
- **Interpretation of j_n.** The upper boundary j_n is attributed to Zener–Klein tunnelling across a correlated gap. Yet the same boundary is then converted to a drift/Fermi velocity, v_n = (1/e) dj_n/dn.
  - A tunnelling threshold is governed by the gap and field-dependent tunnelling rates, not by the saturation current n·e·v_F.
  - The two interpretations are mutually incompatible.
  - The relevant density n (total, flat-band or n*) and the channel width used to form j are not specified.
  - No temperature or magnetic-field dependence of j_n is shown to support either mechanism.
  - Assigning v_n to the flat band ignores the coexisting Dirac carriers (v_F ~10⁵–10⁶ m/s), which should dominate current flow in the normal state. The claim of "vanishing Fermi velocity" is therefore overstated.
- **Δ contradicts the strong-coupling claim.** Using v_n gives Δ ≈ ħv_n/ξ ≈ 37 µeV, so Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 0.2.
  - In any strong-coupling or BEC-like scenario one expects Δ/k_BT_c ≫ 1.76. A gap well below k_BT_c is unphysical for a superconductor.
  - This result most likely shows that ξ = ħv/Δ does not hold here, or that v_n is not the relevant velocity. It cannot serve both as support for unconventionality and as a quantitative gap estimate.
- **Prefactor and depairing assumption.** D_s = 2πj_sξ/Φ₀ omits the GL depairing prefactor. For a 2D sheet, j_dp = Φ₀D_s/(3√3πξ), giving D_s = 3√3πj_sξ/Φ₀, a factor ~2.6 larger.
  - More fundamentally, transport critical currents in 2D near-BKT films are usually set by vortex entry, phase slips, inhomogeneity or heating. They therefore give at best a lower bound on depairing.
  - Device width, current uniformity and contact geometry are not discussed.
- **Conflict with the BKT bound.** The Nelson–Kosterlitz relation gives D_s(T_BKT) = 8e²k_BT_BKT/(πħ²). By my estimate this is ≈ 1.3×10⁸ H⁻¹ for T_BKT = 1.6 K and ≈ 0.9×10⁸ H⁻¹ for 1.1 K.
  - The extracted zero-temperature D_s (2.6–4×10⁷ H⁻¹) is about 3× below these lower bounds.
  - The extracted stiffness is thus not "large". It is too small to be consistent with the authors' own BKT temperatures, pointing to an underestimate.
- **Selective "conventional" benchmark.** The conventional benchmark e²n*/m* with m* = ħk_F/v_n is small only because n* → 0 and v_n is tiny.
  - Including the Dirac carriers (n ≈ 0.45×10¹² cm⁻², v_F ≈ 6×10⁵ m/s, k_F ~10⁸ m⁻¹) gives, by my estimate, a conventional contribution of ~10⁹–10¹⁰ H⁻¹. That is two orders of magnitude above the measured value.
  - The "one order larger than conventional" claim is therefore an artefact of the benchmark choice. The opposite question — why the Dirac carriers contribute so little stiffness — is not addressed.

**Reason 4 – The "unconventional / quantum-metric" interpretation is not substantiated, and the incremental novelty does not meet top-tier standards.**
- **No theory calculation.** Despite a theory co-author group, no quantum metric, minimal metric or geometric superfluid weight is computed for ATQG versus D and compared with the data.
  - The key claim — that D enhances quantum-metric contributions "when Dirac bands touch the flat band" — remains a qualitative assertion.
  - The D-dependence of D_s is not shown to track any calculated geometric quantity.
- **Supporting arguments are weak or have alternatives.**
  - **T*/T_BKT.** The ratio rising from 1.45 to 2.19 rests on T* defined as the "onset of non-linear I–V". That onset can arise from Joule heating, inhomogeneous T_c or fluctuation conductivity.
  - **Near the VHS.** The claim that T_c decreasing near the van Hove singularity is "inconsistent with BCS" ignores competing orders, Fermi-surface reconstruction, and the fact that a diverging n_H marks a Lifshitz-type change rather than directly a DOS peak.
  - **"Pseudogap".** The insulating dV/dI(T) is measured at I_dc = 265–420 nA, i.e. far from equilibrium, and does not by itself establish pre-pairing.
- **Pauli violation is expected.** In ATMG this is already established. Moreover, if Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 0.2 were correct, the relevant Pauli field would be even lower, so the comparison with 1.86 T/K is not meaningful as stated.
- **Single device and inhomogeneity.**
  - All results come from one device.
  - Supplementary Fig. 1b shows normal-state resistances differing by factors of ~2–3 between contact pairs, implying twist-angle or strain inhomogeneity. This directly affects critical currents and ξ.
  - The off-magic angle (1.58° versus ~1.75°) makes generalization to "ATMG superconductivity" uncertain.
- **Presentation inconsistencies.**
  - T_c is defined at 40% of R* from a two-line construction, which is non-standard.
  - "D = 0" data are actually taken at D = −0.03 V/nm.
  - T_c ≈ 2 K versus T_BKT = 1.6 K at the same D is not reconciled.
  - Supplementary Note 6 appears twice.
  - There are typos ("AQTG", "quatralayer", "valance") and an undefined U–D mapping.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Put the band disentanglement on firmer footing.**
   - Fit the Landau-level crossings with the full continuum-model (ideally Hartree-corrected) dispersive-sector spectrum, not a single linear v_F, and propagate uncertainties into μ, n_Dirac and ν_flat.
   - Extend the analysis to finite D, and explain the valence-side anomalies, including the role of lattice relaxation.
   - Cross-check with an independent thermodynamic probe if possible (e.g., capacitance/compressibility, or temperature-dependent Hall measurements with a two-carrier model).
   - Show explicitly that ν_flat = 2 at ν_tot ≈ 2.36 is robust to these assumptions.
2. **Reformulate the coupling-strength analysis so it is not circular.**
   - Report ξ·k_F for several physically motivated choices of n* and g: flat band only, Dirac only, and total. The BCS–BEC claim should survive all of them or be toned down.
   - Estimate the mean free path, and discuss clean versus dirty limits for ξ_GL.
   - State the B_c criterion, and measure B_c(T) down to base temperature rather than extrapolating linearly.
   - Reconcile ξ ≈ 30 nm with the apparent full suppression at B⊥ = 0.2 T in Supplementary Fig. 7c and Fig. 5a.
   - Add direct pairing-gap probes where feasible (e.g., tunnelling or nonlinear-transport spectroscopy), especially given the Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 0.2 contradiction.
3. **Redo the critical-current and stiffness analysis quantitatively and consistently.**
   - Specify device width and current distribution, and use the correct GL depairing prefactor.
   - Compare D_s(0) against the Nelson–Kosterlitz bound from the measured T_BKT. Any shortfall should be discussed as a limitation of the critical-current method.
   - Include the Dirac-band carriers in the "conventional" benchmark, and discuss why their apparent contribution is small.
   - Provide T- and B-dependences of j_n to establish its mechanism (Zener–Klein versus drift saturation) before converting it into a Fermi velocity.
   - Ideally, corroborate D_s with kinetic-inductance or microwave measurements, or at least compare with Banerjee et al. 2024.
   - Rule out heating through pulsed measurements or by comparing V–I hysteresis with sweep rate.
4. **Provide theory and broader evidence for the quantum-metric interpretation.**
   - Compute the band-resolved quantum metric, minimal metric and geometric superfluid weight of ATQG versus D (including band touchings) at 1.58°. Compare their trends with the measured D_s(D), T_c(D) and T*/T_BKT(D).
   - Discuss alternative explanations (competing orders near the VHS, inhomogeneity, heating, multiband conventional pairing) and state which observations discriminate between them.
   - Reproduce the key trends on at least one additional device, or several contact pairs with a mapped twist-angle distribution.
   - Revise the presentation:
     - use standard T_c/T_BKT definitions and report them consistently;
     - label the actual D values;
     - fix the duplicated Supplementary Note numbering and the typos;
     - moderate claims such as "vanishing Fermi velocity" and "large superfluid stiffness" to match what the data support.
