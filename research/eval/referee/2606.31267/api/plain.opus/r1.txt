# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** "Orientation-tunable correlated Chern insulating states in chiral twisted double bilayer graphene proximitized by WSe₂"

---

## 1. Summary of claims

The authors study AB-BA ("chiral") twisted double bilayer graphene (cTDBG, θ ≈ 0.8–0.93°) with monolayer WSe₂ on one side. They make four claims:

1. **Theory.** In a continuum model with λ_I(Φ) = λ_I,0 cos 3Φ (λ_I,0 = 2 meV) and constant λ_R = 0.5 meV, the first valence band in valley K carries C_K = +2 at Φ = 0° (two spin-split subbands with C = +1 each). It carries C_K = 0 at Φ = 15°, at 30°, and without WSe₂ (all at D = 0.5 V/nm). The predicted phase boundary is Φ_c ≈ 9°.
2. **Zero-field transport.** Φ ≈ 0° devices show a correlated insulator at ν = −1, with an activation gap Δ ≈ 0.78 meV in D2. Bare cTDBG controls do not.
3. **Magnetotransport, Φ ≈ 0°.** A Streda-slope C = +1 fan emanates from ν = −1 above ~0.3 T, with R_xy reaching 97.6% of h/e² at 3 T (D1).
4. **Magnetotransport, Φ ≈ 15°.** In D3, the ν = −1 resistive feature does not disperse with B (C = 0). The authors conclude that Φ "deterministically" selects the topology of the correlated state.

The question is timely and well-motivated. Twist-angle control of proximity SOC is an active area (Refs. 42–46). Combining it with the intrinsic valley Chern bands of AB-BA TDBG is a natural and interesting idea. The Φ ≈ 0° magnetotransport data appear to be of good quality.

However, the central causal claim — that the TMDC alignment angle switches the correlated state between C = ±1 and C = 0 — currently rests on:

- an alignment determination with a potential 30° ambiguity;
- essentially one device per "large-Φ" class;
- finite-field signatures only;
- a theoretical model whose physical narrative is not consistent with its own parameters and appears to be finely tuned.

I detail these below. I consider the manuscript potentially publishable after a **major revision**.

---

## 2. Major issues

### 2.1 Determination of Φ: possible zigzag/armchair (30°) ambiguity, and circular validation

Φ is set by aligning straight edges of WSe₂ and graphene under an optical microscope. Same-family edges are identified using 60°/120° inter-edge angles. The procedure has three problems:

- **Edge type is not identified.** Exfoliated graphene (and TMDC) edges can be zigzag or armchair, and these differ by 30°. Inter-edge angles of 60°/120° confirm that two edges belong to the *same* family, but not *which* family. If one flake's straight edge is zigzag and the other's is armchair, a nominal "Φ ≈ 0°" device is actually Φ ≈ 30°, and vice versa. This is exactly the distinction on which the paper hinges.
- **The ±2° uncertainty is an estimate, not a measurement.** It is inferred from the facet statistics of Ref. 63 and the argument that long, straight edges were selected. This does not address the family ambiguity.
- **The validation is circular.** The Methods state that "the consistency of the measured Chern numbers across all aligned devices then provides an a posteriori confirmation of the alignment assignment." The Chern numbers are the dependent variable; they cannot be used to validate the independent variable.

**Request.** An independent determination of Φ for every device, at minimum for D1–D5. Options include:

- SHG polarimetry on the WSe₂ flake, which gives its armchair axis directly and is routine;
- Raman/SHG or lateral-force/atomic-resolution AFM on the graphene flake or its remaining parent piece;
- the graphene–hBN or graphene–WSe₂ moiré, where accessible.

Without this, the 0° vs. 15°/30° comparison cannot be considered established.

### 2.2 Statistics and controls for the large-Φ conclusion

The "trivial" side of the comparison rests on one Φ ≈ 15° device (D3) and one Φ ≈ 30° device (D5, Supplementary only). Yet the abstract refers to "Φ ≈ 15° devices" in the plural and calls the control "deterministic." The D2/D5 pairing (same θ = 0.80°) is helpful. But devices with the same nominal θ still differ in twist-angle disorder, strain, contact quality, the accessible D range, and the sign of D relative to the WSe₂ side.

**Request.**

- Additional large-Φ devices, or at least a frank toning-down of "deterministic."
- Ideally, devices near the predicted boundary (Φ ≈ 5°, 10°). The authors themselves note these would be decisive.
- Twist-angle homogeneity data (multiple contact pairs, ν = ±4 positions) for D1–D5.

### 2.3 The C = 0 assignment in D3 is too thin

The trivial assignment is based on a single R_xx Landau fan at a single displacement field (D = 0.6 V/nm).

**Request.**

- R_xy maps for D3 and D5.
- Fans at several D values, including both signs of D (see 2.7). This is important because in the Φ ≈ 0° devices the ν = −1 state itself is strongly non-monotonic in D (Fig. 3e).
- An explicit check that no C = ±1 trajectory emerges from ν = −1 at other fields or densities.

A non-dispersing feature at one D does not exclude a Chern state elsewhere in the (n, D) plane.

### 2.4 Finite-field Chern insulators do not establish zero-field topology

The title and abstract describe "correlated Chern insulating states." The evidence, however, is a Streda-slope C = +1 state that becomes well developed above ~0.3 T.

- **Field-induced Chern states are common.** In non-hBN-aligned MATBG and other moiré systems, symmetry-broken Chern insulators routinely emanate from integer fillings at finite B, even when the zero-field state is not a Chern insulator (e.g., Refs. 9, 10, 12, 22). Field-induced flavor selection and Hofstadter-band reconstruction can produce such states from bands that are trivial or only marginally topological at B = 0.
- **No zero-field evidence is shown.** The paper presents no anomalous Hall signal, no hysteresis in R_xy(B), no field-training behaviour, and no low-field R_xy data.

**Request.**

- Low-field R_xy sweeps in both directions at ν = −1 for D1/D2, with symmetrized R_xx and antisymmetrized R_xy, and a statement of the procedure used.
- Data at both field polarities. The sign of C should follow the sign of B for a flavor-polarized state; please show this.

If no anomalous Hall effect is found, the claims should be reframed as concerning the Chern character of the field-stabilized ν = −1 state.

### 2.5 The ν = −2 fans are incompatible with the proposed simple picture

The authors' model has the four valence flavors carrying C = +1, +1 (K) and −1, −1 (K′). Integer fillings of these bands then give:

- at ν = −1 (one hole flavor): C = ±1, as claimed;
- at ν = −2 (two empty flavors): C ∈ {0, ±2}.

A (ν, C) = (−2, +1) state therefore cannot arise from filling these bands. The manuscript calls it "consistent with sequential filling of Chern bands," which it is not without further reconstruction. Either the band topology in the interacting state differs from the single-particle picture, or the fans have another origin. In either case, this bears directly on how much weight the single-particle Chern numbers can carry.

**Request.** Please address this explicitly.

### 2.6 Internal consistency and robustness of the theoretical model

This is the most serious conceptual concern.

**(a) "Rashba dominates at larger Φ" contradicts the model parameters.**

| Φ | λ_I = 2.0 cos 3Φ (meV) | λ_I / λ_R (λ_R = 0.5 meV) |
|---|---|---|
| 0° | 2.00 | 4.0 |
| 9° (Φ_c) | ≈ 1.78 | ≈ 3.6 |
| 15° | ≈ 1.41 | ≈ 2.8 |
| ≈ 25° | ≈ 0.5 | ≈ 1 |

λ_I exceeds λ_R up to Φ ≈ 25°. Ising therefore still dominates at 15°, and indeed at the predicted transition. Furthermore, the no-WSe₂ reference (no SOC at all) is also trivial. The natural reading of the authors' own calculation is that a sufficiently large layer-asymmetric Ising term drives the transition, not that Rashba "renders the bands trivial." The abstract, main text, and Discussion should be revised accordingly.

**(b) The transition is triggered by a ~11% reduction of λ_I, implying fine tuning.** The trivial-to-nontrivial change occurs between λ_I ≈ 1.78 and 2.0 meV. Published estimates of λ_I for graphene/WSe₂ and BLG/WSe₂ (DFT and experiment, Refs. 33, 35, 37, 38, 44, 57) span roughly 1–3 meV and depend on interlayer distance, strain, and gating. A prediction that hinges on λ_I being above ~1.8 meV is not robust. The DFT checks in Supplementary Figs. 9–10 show only that λ_I > λ_R is preserved, which is not the relevant criterion.

*Request:* Chern-number phase diagrams in (λ_I, D), (λ_I, λ_R), and for the layer-decay factor (0.35), with the experimental parameter ranges indicated.

**(c) The mechanism needs clarification.**

- Within a fixed valley and spin, H_I = (λ_I/2) τ_z s_z is a constant. If applied uniformly to all layers, it cannot change any Chern number.
- In the model it acts only through its layer profile (L4, and 0.35 × on L3). For each spin sector it is therefore equivalent to a spin-dependent ±1 meV-scale correction to the layer potential. This is small compared with U_d at D = 0.5 V/nm, which by the Methods formula is ≈ 170 meV across the four layers.
- Changing C from 0 to +1 requires a gap closing with an adjacent band. Yet the text says Ising "splits bands into spin-resolved subbands without strongly perturbing the overall gap structure, preserving robust Berry curvature." That statement is inconsistent with the C_K = 0 result for the no-WSe₂ reference.
- If the ±spin sectors see opposite effective layer-potential shifts, it is unclear why *both* subbands acquire C = +1. Getting the same change for opposite-sign shifts would normally require D = 0.5 V/nm to be a special point.

*Request:* Please show the gap closings (location in the mBZ, the gap vs. λ_I, and which bands are involved), and explain the mechanism in these terms. The Introduction's statement that "Ising SOC opens a proximity-induced gap at each valley that carries finite Berry curvature, whereas Rashba SOC alone does not" should also be revisited. In graphene, valley-Zeeman coupling alone does not open a gap; gaps arise from its combination with Rashba, staggered potentials, or layer asymmetry.

**(d) Subband-resolved Chern numbers at 15° and 30° are needed.** Experimentally, the ν = −1 state empties a single flavor, so the relevant quantity is the Chern number of an individual subband. C_K = 0 for the composite band at Φ = 15° does not exclude subband Chern numbers of +1 and −1. If so, the theory would predict |C| = 1 at ν = −1 at 15°, as at 0°.

*Request:* Please report subband-resolved Chern numbers, and the direct gaps between the two subbands, for all Φ. Please also confirm that the two Φ = 0° subbands are isolated from each other throughout the mBZ, so that individual C = +1 values are well defined.

**(e) The angular dependence model is oversimplified relative to the literature it cites.**

- The text states that λ_I "decreases almost linearly" toward 30°, while the model uses cos 3Φ.
- Refs. 42–44 and 57 find non-trivial Φ dependences for WSe₂, including a substantially Φ-dependent Rashba magnitude and a Φ-dependent Rashba phase angle, neither of which is included.
- Since Φ_c is the key quantitative prediction, please justify the functional forms or test sensitivity to the angular dependences reported in Refs. 42–44.

**(f) Use of the same D as experiment.** Theory is shown at D = 0.5 V/nm, while the key experiments are at D = 0.6 V/nm and the correlated state spans a range of D. Please show C vs. D for each Φ.

**(g) Electrostatics.** The model applies a linear, unscreened potential drop with ε = 3 attributed to "the h-BN substrate." In a four-layer system, interlayer screening substantially reduces the potential on inner layers. Because the SOC is layer-localized and the effect is meV-fine-tuned, a self-consistent Hartree treatment, or at least a reduced U_d, could change the conclusions. Please comment and test.

### 2.7 Sign of the displacement field relative to the WSe₂ side

The SOC acts only on the top layers, so the theory makes a clear prediction: the topological character should depend on whether valence-band holes are polarized toward or away from the WSe₂. This is one of the most direct experimental handles on a proximity-SOC mechanism.

**Request.**

- Fans and R_xy at both signs of D for the Φ ≈ 0° devices.
- The theoretical C(D) for both signs.
- An explicit statement of the sign convention linking D > 0 to the WSe₂ side in both experiment and model.

### 2.8 Origin of the valence-band correlated state

The authors attribute the appearance of the ν = −1 insulator, absent in bare cTDBG (D6, D7), to "SOC-induced band flattening and enhanced Berry curvature." Two observations argue against this:

- **D5 (Φ ≈ 30°)** has λ_I ≈ 0 and C = 0 in theory, yet is reported to show a correlated state, and D3 (15°) shows one clearly. The ν = −1 state therefore does not track the Ising SOC or the Berry curvature.
- **No band flattening is demonstrated** in the calculations. Bandwidths with and without SOC are not compared.

Alternative explanations should be discussed:

- the dielectric and screening environment introduced by WSe₂;
- WSe₂-induced strain or relaxation;
- charge transfer or an effective built-in displacement field;
- differences in θ and disorder between the WSe₂ and bare devices.

**Request.** The twist angles of D6 and D7 should be given in the main text. "Exclusively in the conduction band" for previous TDBG studies should be checked against the AB-BA literature (e.g., Refs. 55, 56).

### 2.9 Single-particle topology vs. interacting ground state

The paper's central inference is that "the topological response of the correlated state is governed by the single-particle band topology." All calculations are non-interacting, while interaction scales in these flat bands are tens of meV. Since the single-particle topology changes on a ~0.2 meV change in λ_I (2.6b), the interacting bands may well reorganize. Hartree–Fock is known to modify Chern numbers in TDBG and TBG.

**Request.** At minimum, a Hartree–Fock calculation at ν = −1 for Φ = 0° and 15°. Failing that, a substantially more cautious statement of the inference.

### 2.10 Activation gap

The Arrhenius fit for D2 uses the regime below T* ≈ 2.7 K. From Fig. 3f, this appears to include only ~2–3 temperatures (1.5–2.7 K), spanning less than a factor of two in T, while the fitted gap corresponds to ~9 K. In addition, the 20 mK peak in Fig. 3e (~60 kΩ at D = 0.6) is much larger than the 1.5 K peak in Fig. 3f (~38 kΩ), indicating continued evolution well below the fit range.

**Request.**

- Please show the Arrhenius plot in the main text or the response, with the number of points and the fit range.
- Please consider temperatures between 20 mK and 1.5 K. The dilution refrigerator should allow this.
- Please clarify what "a characteristic minimum at T*" refers to.

## 3. Minor issues

1. **Φ vs. θ terminology.** "Twist angle" is used for both. For example, "Rashba SOC is relatively insensitive to the twist angle" and "This twist-angle dependence provides a tuning mechanism" refer to Φ, while "twist-angle engineering" in the Introduction is ambiguous. Please reserve "twist angle" for θ and use "alignment angle" for Φ throughout.

2. **Fig. 4c labels.** The schematic labels "(1, −2)", "(1, −1)", "(2, −2)" appear to be (C, ν) or (t, s), while the caption uses (ν, C). Please unify the notation, and state the Streda/Diophantine convention and the sign convention linking C = +1 to R_xy ≈ −h/e².

3. **Hole-language description.** At ν = −1, three valence flavors are filled and one is empty. The measured C is minus the Chern number of the empty subband. "Occupation of a single spin-split subband within one valley" should be rephrased in hole language to avoid confusion about signs.

4. **"Near-quantized" R_xy.** The 97.6% value occurs at a single B point along a trajectory where R_xy varies between ~80% and ~98% (Fig. 4d), and R_xx is small but finite. Please report the R_xx minimum value and the resistivity tensor (σ_xy), and describe the geometry and symmetrization.

5. **0° vs. 60° alignment.** Edge alignment cannot distinguish Φ = 0° from 60°, which in the cos 3Φ model flips the sign of λ_I. Please comment on whether the sign of λ_I affects the predicted subband Chern numbers.

6. **Fig. 1j–l colouring.** The Φ = 30° bands are plotted in grey without ⟨S_z⟩ colouring. Please explain, or colour them consistently.

7. **Fig. 2a–i relevance.** The non-interacting quarter-filling Fermi surfaces are of limited relevance to a correlated insulating state. Consider moving them to the Supplement, or clarify what they are meant to establish beyond "flat bands are sensitive to meV perturbations."

8. **Supplementary Fig. 11.** The comparison of SOC-only, sublattice-asymmetry-only, and combined cases is important to the argument. It should be summarized quantitatively in the main text, including what sublattice potential is assumed and why.

9. **Methods details.**
   - Please justify γ₁ = 400 meV (commonly ~340–380 meV), and give the values of v₃, v₄ and the 0.35 layer-decay factor, with sensitivity to each.
   - "Effective dielectric constant of the h-BN substrate" is not the appropriate quantity for interlayer potentials.
   - Please give the D range and step used in the theory.

10. **"Seven devices."** Only D1–D3 appear in the main text. A main-text table summarizing θ, Φ (with method and uncertainty), D range, presence of the ν = −1 insulator, and C for all seven devices would help readers assess the claim.

11. **Wording.**
    - "Deterministically controls" and "establishes" are stronger than the data support.
    - "Φ ≈ 15° devices" should be "a Φ ≈ 15° device."
    - The Introduction's "This Berry curvature arises from broken inversion symmetry" should more precisely refer to broken C₂zT, or C₂z, as appropriate.

12. **Typos.** "Then, the first half of the bilayer graphene was then picked up" (duplicated "then"); "intra-valley" hyphenation; inconsistent use of "C = 0" vs. "C= 0."

---

## 4. Assessment and recommendation

The idea of using TMDC alignment to switch the topology of a correlated moiré state is attractive. The Φ ≈ 0° data show a clean C = +1 trajectory from ν = −1, which would be a useful observation on its own.

However, the comparison that gives the paper its significance (0° vs. 15°/30°) is not yet convincing, for three reasons:

1. **The alignment angles are not independently measured**, and zigzag/armchair ambiguity could interchange the categories (2.1).
2. **The trivial category rests on minimal data**: one device at one D, without Hall data (2.2, 2.3).
3. **The theory does not support the stated mechanism.** Its narrative (Rashba dominance) contradicts its parameters, the predicted topology is fine-tuned to an ~10% change in an uncertain λ_I, the subband-resolved Chern numbers needed for comparison with ν = −1 are missing, and interactions are not considered (2.6, 2.9).

In addition, the title's "Chern insulating states" is supported only by finite-field Streda analysis, and the (−2, +1) state is unexplained within the proposed picture.

**Recommendation: major revision.** Most essential for reconsideration:

- independent (SHG/Raman/AFM) Φ determination for all devices;
- R_xy, multi-D, and ±D data for the large-Φ device(s);
- low-field hysteresis/anomalous Hall measurements at ν = −1;
- a revised theoretical section with subband-resolved Chern numbers, parameter-robustness phase diagrams, a correct description of the mechanism, and at least a mean-field treatment of interactions at ν = −1.
