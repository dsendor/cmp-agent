# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** "Orientation-tunable correlated Chern insulating states in chiral twisted double bilayer graphene proximitized by WSe₂"

## Summary of claims

The authors study AB-BA (chiral) twisted double bilayer graphene (cTDBG, θ ≈ 0.8–0.93°) with monolayer WSe₂ on one side. They make two main claims:

1. **Theory.** A continuum model with Φ-dependent proximity SOC, using λ_I(Φ) = λ_I,0 cos 3Φ with λ_I,0 = 2 meV and constant λ_R = 0.5 meV, predicts the following at D = 0.5 V/nm:
   - At Φ = 0° the first valence band carries C_K = +2.
   - At Φ = 15°, at Φ = 30°, and without WSe₂, it is trivial.
   - The phase boundary is Φ_c ≈ 9°.
2. **Experiment.**
   - Three Φ ≈ 0° devices show a ν = −1 correlated insulator, with a C = +1 Landau-fan trajectory and R_xy reaching 97.6% of h/e² at ~3 T.
   - A Φ ≈ 15° device (D3) and a Φ ≈ 30° device (D5) show a ν = −1 insulator with C = 0.
   - Bare cTDBG devices show no ν = −1 insulator.

The authors conclude that the WSe₂ alignment angle deterministically selects the topology of the correlated state.

## Overall assessment

The question is timely, and the experimental contrast between aligned and misaligned devices is potentially interesting. However, I have three central concerns:

- **Theory.** The theoretical narrative is not internally consistent with the parameters used. The predicted topological transition appears to be extremely fine-tuned.
- **Alignment.** The experimental determination of Φ has an unaddressed 30° ambiguity.
- **Chern assignment.** The Chern-insulator assignment rests on finite-field fan slopes, without zero-field evidence of time-reversal breaking.

Together, these leave the central causal claim, that Φ controls topology via Ising/Rashba competition, insufficiently supported. I recommend **major revision**.

## Major concerns

### 1. The "Ising vs. Rashba competition" narrative is inconsistent with the model's own parameters

With λ_I(Φ) = 2.0 cos 3Φ meV and λ_R = 0.5 meV:

| Φ | λ_I | Ising vs. Rashba |
|---|---|---|
| 15° | 2.0 × cos 45° ≈ 1.41 meV | Ising ≈ 3× larger |
| 9° (claimed Φ_c) | ≈ 1.78 meV | Ising ≈ 3.6× larger |
| ≈ 25° | ≈ 0.5 meV | Rashba first equals Ising |

Rashba therefore does not "dominate" at 15°. The abstract's statement that "Rashba coupling dominates at larger Φ" does not hold, and neither do similar statements on pp. 6, 10 and 11.

What the model actually implies is a threshold effect: C_K changes from +2 to 0 when λ_I drops from 2.0 to ~1.8 meV, a ~10% change in a ~2 meV term. Rashba competition plays no evident role. The authors should:

- Present a phase diagram of C_K in the (λ_I, λ_R) plane, and in (Φ, D) and (Φ, θ).
- Show the direct gaps above and below the relevant band as functions of λ_I.

This would let readers judge how close the Φ = 0° point is to a gap closing. It would also show whether the conclusion survives plausible uncertainties in λ_I,0, which literature values place anywhere from ~1 to ~3 meV.

### 2. Physical plausibility of the Ising-driven C = 0 → +2 transition

The authors should explain more carefully why a ~2 meV Ising term changes the topology at all. Three observations raise doubts.

**The Ising term acts as a small spin-dependent layer potential.** With λ_R → 0 and negligible λ_KM, s_z is conserved. In that limit, H_I = (λ_I/2)τ_z s_z, applied with weights (0.35, 1) on L3 and L4, is a sublattice-independent, layer-dependent scalar potential of opposite sign for the two spins. Each spin sector is therefore bare cTDBG with a slightly different effective interlayer potential.

**That potential is tiny compared with the displacement-field potential.** At D = 0.5 V/nm, Eq. (7) gives U_d = eD·d_t/ε ≈ 0.5 × 1.005 / 3 ≈ 170 meV across the stack. The Ising perturbation is ±1 meV on L4 and ±0.35 meV on L3.

**Opposite-sign perturbations should not both produce C = +1.** Fig. 2j/m and the caption state that the bare band has C = 0 and that *both* spin-split subbands acquire C = +1. Yet the two subbands are subject to perturbations of *opposite* sign. Near a generic single gap closing, opposite-sign perturbations of the mass give opposite Chern changes, not identical ones.

Either additional ingredients are essential, or the Φ = 0° point sits at a non-generic band touching. Possible additional ingredients include Rashba-induced spin mixing, inter-subband touchings, or the way "first valence band" is defined when subbands overlap.

To resolve this, please provide:

- Berry-curvature maps for each subband.
- Explicit confirmation that each subband is isolated over the whole mBZ. The Kubo formula of Eq. (14) is ill-defined at touchings.
- A gauge-invariant lattice computation of C, e.g. Fukui–Hatsugai–Suzuki.
- Convergence with respect to the 6k_θ cutoff and k-mesh.
- Results for −λ_I, since cos 3Φ changes sign at Φ = 60°; see point 4.

Relatedly, the statement in the Introduction that "the Ising SOC opens a proximity-induced gap at each valley that carries finite Berry curvature" should be stated more precisely. A sublattice-independent Ising term does not by itself gap a Dirac point. In bilayer systems it acts through layer asymmetry.

### 3. The Φ dependence of the SOC parameters is oversimplified relative to the cited literature

Eqs. (12–13) assume a pure cos 3Φ Ising dependence and an angle-independent Rashba term, attributed to Ref. 44.

- Several of the cited works (Refs. 42–44, 57) report non-monotonic angular dependences for WSe₂, notably of the Rashba term, at intermediate angles. The constant-λ_R assumption therefore needs explicit justification.
- Ref. 46 reports an experimental Φ dependence of the proximity SOC in bilayer graphene, which is directly relevant. A quantitative comparison with Ref. 46 is needed.

Because the predicted transition is so sensitive to λ_I (point 1), the angular form matters at the ~10% level. The authors should show that Φ_c is robust to the functional form adopted.

### 4. Determination of Φ: 30° (and 60°) ambiguity, and circular validation

Φ is set by aligning straight edges of WSe₂ and graphene under an optical microscope, and the authors claim ±2° accuracy. Two problems arise.

**Zigzag vs. armchair ambiguity.** Inter-edge angles of 60°/120° identify the edge *family* but cannot distinguish zigzag from armchair edges, which differ by 30°. Both edge types occur in exfoliated graphene and TMDs. Edge matching therefore cannot by itself distinguish Φ ≈ 0° from Φ ≈ 30°, which are exactly the two cases being contrasted.

**60° ambiguity.** WSe₂ lacks inversion symmetry, so 0° and 60° alignments are inequivalent. In the authors' model, cos 3Φ = −1 at 60°, which flips the sign of λ_I. The paper should state what the model predicts for negative λ_I, and whether the experiment can tell the two cases apart.

**Circular validation.** The statement that "consistency of the measured Chern numbers … provides an a posteriori confirmation of the alignment assignment" is circular. The Chern number is the quantity whose Φ dependence is being tested.

Requested evidence:

- An independent crystallographic determination, e.g. second-harmonic generation on WSe₂ and graphene edge-type identification by Raman D-peak polarization, or post-measurement imaging.
- Ideally, an in-device measurement of the proximity SOC, e.g. Landau-level crossings near CNP or weak antilocalization as in Refs. 37–38, showing that λ_I is indeed larger in the "0°" devices than in the "15°/30°" devices.

Without this, the experimental Φ axis is not established.

### 5. The Chern-insulator assignment is based only on finite-field data

A trajectory of slope C = +1 emanating from ν = −1 is consistent with a Chern insulator. However, it is also what one expects from Landau levels of a *trivial* flavor-polarized insulator: once fewer flavours are active, singly-degenerate pockets doped away from ν = −1 produce LLs with slopes ±1, ±2, …. The state becomes well developed only above ~0.3 T, and Fig. 4d shows R_xx minima of order 10² Ω rather than vanishing values.

To support the claim of an intrinsic C = +1 state with spontaneous isospin polarization, as stated in the abstract, the authors should:

- Report low-field R_xy at ν = −1, including any anomalous Hall signal and hysteresis under B sweeps, as done in Refs. 4, 14, 30, 55.
- Show the ±B behaviour, including whether a C = −1 counterpart appears.
- Compare quantitatively with the expected LL sequence of a trivial insulator.
- State the sign convention linking C = +1 to R_xy = −h/e² on the hole side, and describe the (anti)symmetrization procedure.

If no zero-field signature exists, the language should be tempered to "field-stabilized C = +1 state". The conclusion that the correlated state "tracks single-particle topology" should then be weakened accordingly.

### 6. Insufficient statistics and an overstated "controlled comparison"

The trivial side rests on one device at 15° (D3) and one at 30° (D5), with the 30° data only in the Supplement. The statement that D2 and D5 "differ only in Φ" is an overstatement. Devices sharing a nominal θ still differ in:

- twist-angle inhomogeneity and heterostrain;
- disorder;
- hBN alignment;
- the sign convention of D relative to the WSe₂ side.

Please:

- Report hBN–graphene alignment angles for all devices. Accidental hBN alignment is a well-known alternative source of Chern bands, and Supplementary Section XI shows that sublattice asymmetry can contribute.
- Report twist-angle homogeneity, e.g. from multiple contact pairs.
- Show Landau fans for D3/D5 at more than one D. Theory says topology is D-dependent, and a C = 0 assignment at a single D = 0.6 V/nm is not conclusive.

### 7. The role of WSe₂ in creating the ν = −1 insulator is unexplained

The bare cTDBG devices D6 and D7 show no ν = −1 insulator, but the Φ ≈ 30° device does. Yet in the model the 30° band structure, with λ_I = 0 and λ_R = 0.5 meV, is nearly identical to the bare one (compare Fig. 1j–l with Fig. 2m).

This undermines the attribution of the valence-band correlated state to "SOC-induced band flattening and enhanced Berry curvature" (p. 8). It points instead to non-SOC effects of the WSe₂, such as dielectric environment, strain, or disorder. The authors should address this directly.

Relatedly, the claim that previous TDBG studies observed correlated states "exclusively in the conduction band" should be qualified. Most such studies concern AB-AB stacking. The relevant comparison is the authors' own cTDBG controls at matched θ and D.

### 8. Theory–experiment parameter mismatch and the role of D

Several mismatches and omissions should be addressed:

- **Parameter mismatch.** Chern numbers are reported only at D = 0.5 V/nm and θ = 0.9°. The experiments are at D = 0.6 V/nm and θ = 0.80–0.93°. Please compute C at the experimental parameters.
- **D dependence.** Show C(D) for each Φ, and compare with the D range over which the C = +1 fan is observed.
- **Sign of D.** Proximity SOC acts only on the WSe₂-adjacent layers. Whether valence-band holes are polarized toward or away from WSe₂ depends on the sign of D, so the theory should predict a ±D asymmetry. Is the C = +1 state present at both signs of D, and is the observed asymmetry consistent with the model? Fig. 3 suggests the ν = −1 feature is stronger on one side of D; this should be discussed explicitly.
- **Screening.** Eq. (7) uses an unscreened linear potential, which likely overestimates the interlayer asymmetry. Given the sensitivity discussed in point 2, the effect of layer screening (e.g. self-consistent Hartree) should be checked.

### 9. Interaction effects are not modelled

The conclusion that "the topological response of the correlated state is governed by the single-particle band topology" is inferred, not computed. Hartree–Fock renormalization in flat bands can be comparable to, or larger than, the ~meV SOC scale and can reshuffle Berry curvature.

At minimum, a Hartree–Fock calculation of the flavor-polarized ν = −1 state at Φ = 0° and 15° would substantially strengthen the link between the single-particle Chern numbers and the measured C. If this is not feasible, the limitation should be stated clearly.

## Minor comments

1. **Terminology.** "Twist angle" is used for both θ and Φ, e.g. "Rashba SOC is relatively insensitive to the twist angle" on p. 3 and "twist-angle engineering" on p. 4. Please use "alignment angle" consistently for Φ.
2. **"Deterministically."** Given one or two devices per trivial-side angle and the open alignment ambiguity, this wording is overstated in the abstract, introduction and discussion.
3. **Intrinsic topology.** The Introduction emphasizes that cTDBG has nontrivial valley Chern numbers "even at zero displacement field", yet the bare-cTDBG reference (Fig. 2m) is trivial at D = 0.5 V/nm. Please show the bare C(D) so readers can reconcile these statements.
4. **Activation gap.** The Arrhenius fit "below T* ≈ 2.7 K" appears to use very few temperature points (Fig. 3f shows 1.5, 2.0 and 3.0 K in this regime), spanning less than a factor of 2 in T. Please show the fit and the data points explicitly. Dilution-fridge data down to lower T would give a far more reliable gap.
5. **Error bars in Fig. 4d.** These are said to "indicate deviation from −h/e²". Error bars should represent measurement uncertainty; deviation from quantization is better shown as a separate quantity.
6. **Fig. 2a–i.** The relevance of non-interacting Fermi surfaces at ν = −1 to the topological argument is unclear, since the non-interacting system is metallic there. Please clarify what "lower pair of flat valence bands" refers to.
7. **Missing model parameters.** v₃ and v₄ are not given numerically. Please list all continuum-model parameters, and specify whether the 0.35 L3 scaling applies equally to all SOC terms.
8. **Sign conventions.**
   - Eq. (9) uses σ_x and σ_y, while the kinetic term uses σ = (−σ_x, σ_y). Please confirm the Rashba term is written consistently with this convention.
   - Clarify the layer ordering (L1…L4) relative to the WSe₂ side and the sign of D.
9. **Phase boundary.** Φ_c ≈ 9° appears only in the Discussion, Methods and Supplement. The corresponding phase diagram (Supplementary Fig. 11a) belongs in the main text, since it is central to the claim.
10. **Prior work.** Ref. 50 (TDBG/WSe₂) and Ref. 46 (Φ-programmable SOC in BLG) deserve a more concrete comparison of the observed phenomenology.
11. **Fig. 4c.** The labels "(1, −1)" and "(1, −2)" appear to be (C, ν), whereas the caption uses (ν, C). Please make them consistent.

| Priority | Request |
|---|---|
| Essential | Independent crystallographic determination of Φ, resolving the zigzag/armchair (30°) and 0°/60° ambiguities |
| Essential | In-device estimate of λ_I (LL crossings or WAL) for aligned vs. misaligned devices |
| Essential | Low-field Hall/hysteresis at ν = −1; ±B data |
| Essential | Chern-number phase diagrams in (λ_I, λ_R), (Φ, D), (Φ, θ); gap sizes; Berry-curvature maps; gauge-invariant C calculation |
| Important | Correction of the "Rashba dominates" narrative, or a demonstration that Rashba matters |
| Important | Landau fans for D3/D5 at several D; hBN alignment for all devices |
| Important | Explanation of why WSe₂ at Φ ≈ 30° induces the ν = −1 insulator while bare cTDBG does not |
| Desirable | Hartree–Fock analysis of the ν = −1 state; screening-corrected D potential |
| Desirable | Additional devices on the trivial side and at intermediate Φ |

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The experimental observation of a field-stabilized C = +1 trajectory in some WSe₂/cTDBG devices but not others is potentially valuable. As presented, however, the theoretical mechanism is internally inconsistent and appears fine-tuned. The Φ assignment is not independently verified, and the Chern-insulator claim lacks zero-field evidence. Addressing points 1, 2, 4 and 5 is necessary before the central conclusion can be considered established.
