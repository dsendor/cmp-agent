1. ### 1. The central BMS result is not established statistically or experimentally

All the key claims rest on a single flake, a single cool-down (cooling only, 300 → 80 K), a single wavevector and a single propagation direction (q ∥ b).

Fig. 4 is missing the information needed to judge whether the ~5.5% change is robust and reversible:
- error bars on the peak frequencies;
- the uncertainties of the peak fits and the fit model used;
- the number of temperature points and the step size near T_N.

The BMS Methods are missing the information needed to judge the measurement itself:
- laser power at the sample and spot size;
- acquisition time;
- the cryostat and vacuum level used;
- how the sample temperature was calibrated, including laser heating.

The text also does not define how the 5.5% is computed. It could be 300 K vs. 80 K, or the ~200 K maximum vs. 80 K. It is also unclear whether an anharmonic background was subtracted. This matters because the frequency first rises down to ~200 K and the stated values are inconsistent (5.5% vs. ~6%).

**Requested:**
- Repeat the measurement on at least 2–3 flakes and at least 2 thermal cycles, recording both heating and cooling sweeps.
- Use dense temperature steps (≤5 K) across 130–180 K.
- Report error bars and fit uncertainties.
- Run a laser-power series at 80 K and near T_N to quantify heating.
- Define Δv/v explicitly, for example relative to an anharmonic (Varshni/Klemens-type) background fitted above ~200 K.

2. ### 2. Plausible artefacts that could mimic the effect are not excluded

At θ = 35°, d ln q/dθ = cot θ ≈ 2.5% per degree. A ~2° change in effective incidence angle would therefore reproduce the entire claimed effect. Such a change could come from cryostat contraction, sample tilt or a window misalignment. Other temperature-dependent artefacts are also possible:
- adsorbate or ice condensation, which mass-loads the surface;
- strain release or partial delamination of the flake from SiO₂.

**Requested:**
- Measure the full angular dispersion (30–50°) at 80 K and compare it with RT. A true stiffness change gives a q-independent Δf/f, or the specific q-dependence predicted by FEM. An angle error scales differently.
- Track an internal, angle-insensitive reference where possible, such as a bulk Si/SiO₂ mode or a bulk NiPS₃ LA/TA peak.
- Re-measure at 300 K after the thermal cycle to test reversibility.
- Report the chamber pressure.

3. ### 3. The attribution to zigzag AFM order is not demonstrated for this flake

The only evidence for magnetic origin is the coincidence of a drop at ~150 K with the literature T_N. Several observations weaken this link:
- **The Raman data do not show the known order signatures.** The authors' own Raman measurements fail to reproduce the 176 cm⁻¹ mode splitting associated with zigzag order. They show only tiny anomalies (~0.3 cm⁻¹ in FWHM at 255 cm⁻¹). So there is no independent confirmation that this flake orders at ~155 K on the same temperature axis.
- **The onset is non-monotonic.** The frequency rises down to ~200 K, well above T_N, and this is attributed to "reduced anharmonicity" without any fit.

**Requested controls (several of these are feasible with the existing setup):**
- Measure q ∥ a vs. q ∥ b. Exchange striction in a zigzag state is strongly anisotropic. Crystal axes are currently assigned only from cleavage edges, so verify them by polarized Raman, SHG or XRD.
- Run a thickness series.
- Measure an isostructural MPS₃ with a different T_N (FePS₃ at ~118 K, MnPS₃ at ~78 K), where the anomaly should track that material's own T_N.
- Optionally, measure a nonmagnetic analogue or apply a magnetic field.
- Report the BMS linewidth versus temperature. Critical attenuation peaking at T_N would be strong evidence of magnetic origin.
- Determine T_N independently on the same flake or temperature stage, for example by Raman two-magnon or the 176 cm⁻¹ splitting with improved polarization geometry.

4. ### 4. The claim that DFT and FEM "reproduce" the shift contradicts the paper's own numbers

There are two quantitative problems:
- **The predicted shift is ~10× too small.** The FEM shift between the NM and AFM tensors is 4.603 → 4.578 GHz, i.e., −0.54%, against ~5.5% measured.
- **The baseline frequency is off by more than the effect.** The RT frequency (4.6 vs. 4.2 GHz) differs by ~10%, about twice the effect being explained, yet it is described as "close agreement."

The sensitivity analysis undercuts the DFT interpretation further:
- The Rayleigh mode is most sensitive to c33 and c44. DFT gives c44 unchanged (9.7 → 9.7 GPa) and c33 down only ~8%.
- The large DFT changes (c66 +17%, c12 −22%) barely affect the mode, which is exactly why the predicted shift is small.
- The conclusion of a "sizable reduction in shear/out-of-plane constants" is therefore inferred from the data and is inconsistent with DFT, not corroborated by it.

**Requested:**
- Rephrase the abstract and conclusions ("reproduces," "corroborate") to state the discrepancy honestly.
- Report linear sensitivity coefficients ∂ln f/∂ln c_ij, including the film participation fraction.
- Perform an inverse fit giving the Δc33 and/or Δc44 required to obtain 5.5%, and compare it explicitly with the DFT values.
- Plot the computed dispersion over the full 30–50° range against the measured data, not a single q point.

5. ### 5. The DFT methodology cannot reliably predict the sign or size of an order-induced elastic change

- **No Hubbard U.** Ni²⁺ (d⁸) is correlated. PBE without U misestimates the moment, the gap and the exchange constants, and hence ∂J/∂ε, which is the very quantity that controls exchange striction. Please perform a U scan (e.g., 0–6 eV). For each U, report the moment, gap and lattice constants against experiment, and show how Δc_ij(AFM − reference) depends on U.
- **The nonmagnetic state is not a paramagnet.** Local Ni moments (~2 μB) persist above T_N. NM-vs-AFM therefore measures moment formation, not ordering. An ordering-specific comparison is needed:
  - zigzag vs. FM, Néel and stripy configurations at fixed moment, and/or
  - a disordered-local-moment or special-quasirandom-structure paramagnet.
- **Convergence and completeness are not shown.** A 3×2×3 k-mesh on a 1×4×2 supercell, with no tests, is insufficient for elastic constants. Please:
  - show k-point and cutoff convergence;
  - state the strain amplitude used;
  - state whether the lattice is relaxed separately for each magnetic state;
  - report all 13 C2/m constants and check Born stability;
  - compare with available experimental elastic data or neutron sound velocities, especially for c33 and c44, which are sensitive to the D3 correction.

6. ### 6. FEM validity and reproducibility

- **Boundary conditions.** The Si substrate has finite thickness, a fixed bottom and no perfectly matched or low-reflecting layer. This can introduce spurious plate modes and shift eigenfrequencies. Please show convergence with Si thickness and mesh, and identify the Rayleigh mode by its displacement profile.
- **Missing parameters.** The paper should state:
  - the NiPS₃ density;
  - the SiO₂ and Si elastic parameters, Si anisotropy and wafer orientation;
  - how the monoclinic tensor is rotated relative to q ∥ b.
- **Temperature effects are omitted from the model.** These include the temperature dependence of the substrate (SiO₂ has anomalous elasticity on cooling), the density change, and clamped thermal-mismatch strain. All of these affect the frequency at the percent level and must be included or bounded before the full 5.5% is assigned to magnetism.

7. ### 7. The magnetoelastic analysis is not quantitative

The model c_eff = c0 − λ²χ is introduced, but λ and χ are never estimated. Δc/c ≈ 2Δv/v is used, but it only holds for a homogeneous medium where the mode depends on a single elastic constant. For a layered, substrate-loaded Rayleigh mode, Δf/f = ½Σ S_ij Δc_ij/c_ij with participation-weighted sensitivities S_ij < 1. The required film Δc would therefore exceed 11%, and density and thermal-strain terms must be added.

**Requested:**
- Estimate λ from DFT ∂J/∂ε, or from the thermal-expansion or magnetostriction anomaly at T_N.
- Take χ from susceptibility data.
- Perform the standard Landau/Ehrenfest consistency check, Δc ≈ −(dT_N/dε)² ΔC_p/(T_N V_m), using the known NiPS₃ specific-heat jump and the pressure dependence of T_N.

Without these, "quantitatively links" and "the same exchange-striction mechanism as optical phonons" are unsupported, since no coupling constant is extracted from either the Raman or the BMS data. Either compute and compare the two, or downgrade the wording to "qualitatively consistent with."

8. ### 8. The "uniquely sensitive probe" comparison with Raman is not a fair comparison

- **Different observables.** The paper compares a fractional acoustic-velocity change with fractional optical-phonon shifts. Fractional magnitude is not a sensitivity metric. The relevant figure of merit is anomaly divided by measurement uncertainty, measured on the same flake on the same calibrated temperature axis. It cannot be computed here because BMS uncertainties are not reported.
- **The weak Raman response is more likely a measurement problem.** The Raman fails to reproduce the established 176 cm⁻¹ splitting. Moreover, the cited Ref. 49 (Liu 2025, NiPS₃ nanosheets) reports clear Raman spin–phonon signatures. This suggests polarization geometry, axis misassignment, heating or sample quality rather than intrinsic insensitivity of optical phonons.
- **"Uniquely" ignores other techniques.** Ultrasound, resonant ultrasound and dilatometry are also highly sensitive to elastic anomalies at T_N.

**Requested:** Revisit the Raman polarization configuration, then either reconcile the result with the literature or temper the claim. The Raman temperature range also needs reconciling: 77–300 K in Methods vs. 90–290 K in Fig. 2.

9. ### 9. The "order-of-magnitude larger than FePS₃/CrBr₃/Cr₂Ge₂Te₆" statement is unsupported and misattributed

- **No quantity is defined.** The sentence names no quantity, gives no units and gives no NiPS₃ value.
- **The attribution is wrong.** It is attributed to "a recent theoretical work" (Refs. 49–52), but Refs. 49–51 are experimental, and Ref. 52 is theory on Cr₂Ge₂Te₆, not NiPS₃.
- **The comparison is likely incommensurable.** It appears to compare optical spin–phonon constants (in cm⁻¹) with an acoustic elastic renormalization.

**Requested:** Remove the statement, or replace it with a table comparing one defined dimensionless metric (Δc/c or Δv/v at T_N) across materials, with explicit sources. This should include bulk ultrasound data on antiferromagnets, where anomalies of ~0.1–1% are typical and several-percent values are known.

10. ### 10. Overclaiming and novelty positioning

**Overclaimed language:**
- "2D" in the title and throughout, for a ~220 nm (bulk-like) film; "van der Waals antiferromagnet" would be accurate.
- "Giant" and "rarely reported in 2D magnets," without the quantitative comparison requested in comment 9.
- "Benchmark platform" and "establish the continuum limit of spin–phonon interactions," from one sweep on one flake.
- "Direct measurement of how the elastic constants evolve." A single-q Rayleigh velocity is a weighted combination of several c_ij and of the substrate.

**Novelty relative to prior work:** Ref. 36 (Wright et al., APL 2024, "Acoustic and optical phonons in quasi-two-dimensional MPS₃"), from overlapping authors, is cited only for Raman peak positions. Meanwhile, the intro calls acoustic phonons in vdW magnets "largely unexplored." The authors should state what Ref. 36 already measured (e.g., RT BMS acoustic modes in NiPS₃) and position the temperature-dependent result relative to it.

11. **Thickness and spot.** Give the flake thickness at the BMS spot, its uniformity ("films" plural are mentioned), and the lateral flake size relative to the laser spot.

12. **Velocity scale.** Discuss why the measured RT velocity (1.92 km/s) exceeds the DFT-based film shear velocity √(c44/ρ) ≈ 1.75 km/s. This could indicate substrate loading, an underestimated c44, or a mode-assignment issue.

13. **Fig. 4 wording.** The caption places the "pronounced drop at 150 K" while citing T_N ≈ 155 K. Tie the anomaly position to a defined criterion, such as the inflection point from a fit.

14. **Raman uncertainties.** Report Raman fit uncertainties alongside the peak positions and FWHM values.

15. **The novelty relative to the authors' own prior work is not stated.**
   - Wright et al., APL 2024 (ref. 36), reports Brillouin–Mandelstam spectroscopy of acoustic and optical phonons in quasi-2D MPS3 antiferromagnets. D. Wright is a co-author here, so this is very likely the same group.
   - In the main text, ref. 36 is cited only as a consistency check for Raman peak positions. This conflicts with the claim that acoustic phonons in vdW magnets are "largely unexplored".
   - The authors should state what was already measured in ref. 36, including whether NiPS3 was measured versus temperature, and what is new here.
   - Liu 2025 (spin–phonon coupling in 2D NiPS3) and Jiang 2020 (exchange magnetostriction in 2D antiferromagnets) also need an explicit comparison.

16. **The "2D" framing is not supported by the samples.**
   - The flakes are ~220 nm thick (hundreds of layers), and the Raman analysis uses the bulk C2h assignment.
   - Yet the title, the abstract, the Mermin–Wagner / "strict 2D limit" motivation, "rarely reported in 2D magnets" and the 2D-device outlook all rely on two-dimensionality.
   - The authors should either reframe the work as thin-film or bulk-like, or add a thickness series showing behaviour that is specific to the 2D or few-layer limit.

17. **The argument against bulk techniques is weak.**
   - The paper motivates BMS by saying exfoliated flakes are too small for neutron or ultrasound methods.
   - At 220 nm thickness the physics is bulk-like, and the authors used commercial bulk crystals.
   - Ultrasound, inelastic neutron or X-ray scattering, bulk Brillouin scattering, picosecond acoustics, and thermal-expansion or magnetostriction measurements on bulk NiPS3 are all feasible. No bulk elastic or magnetostriction data on MPS3 are cited or compared.
   - The paper should explain what the thin-flake geometry adds.

18. **"Giant" and "rarely reported" are not benchmarked.**
   - Acoustic and elastic anomalies at magnetic transitions in bulk materials (refs. 21, 22, 24: FeRh, U2Rh2Sn, BiFeO3) are cited only to say that softening "reflects magnetoelastic renormalization".
   - Velocity anomalies of several percent are common at magnetic transitions; classic examples include Cr, MnF2, UO2 and Invar.
   - A quantitative comparison table (Δv/v and Δc/c at T_N across materials) is needed to justify "giant".

19. **The claimed agreement with refs. 49–52 compares different quantities.**
   - The paper says NiPS3 spin–phonon coupling is ~10× larger than in FePS3, CrBr3 and Cr2Ge2Te6, and that this agrees with its result.
   - From their titles, refs. 49–52 address optical-phonon or computed spin–phonon coupling and thermal expansion, not acoustic-mode or elastic-constant renormalization.
   - The comparison is qualitative only and should be made quantitative or removed.

20. **The central dichotomy is asserted rather than demonstrated.** The claim is that optical phonons probe local ⟨Si·Sj⟩ while acoustic phonons probe long-range order.
   - Macroscopic moduli also depend on ⟨Si·Sj⟩ through exchange striction, the very mechanism the paper invokes.
   - The Rayleigh frequency appears to start changing near 200 K, ~50 K above T_N. That would be consistent with short-range-order effects, but the 200→150 K interval is never discussed.
   - The data shown do not establish that acoustic modes are "uniquely" sensitive to long-range order.

21. **The motivation that "Raman is not sufficiently sensitive" is unconvincing.**
   - It rests on the authors' own null result: they fail to reproduce the ~176 cm⁻¹ splitting reported in prior work.
   - No spectral resolution, grating, signal-to-noise ratio or spectral range is given. The only explanation offered is that "higher spectral sensitivity" would be needed, which points to an instrumental limitation rather than a physical one.
   - The authors themselves observe weak anomalies in the 255 cm⁻¹ mode (flattening, a ~0.3 cm⁻¹ FWHM deviation, Fano asymmetry).
   - Established low-energy magnon and two-magnon Raman in NiPS3 (refs. 28, 29) was not used.
   - The authors should discuss whether ref. 25 observed the splitting in bulk or few-layer samples. If so, it should be visible in 220 nm flakes.
   - The splitting is attributed to refs. 16/30 in the introduction but to ref. 25 in the results; this should be made consistent.

22. **The significance and application claims overreach.**
   - "Uniquely sensitive probe", "benchmark platform", and phononic, magnonic and strain-spintronic applications rest on one material.
   - The data appear to come from one flake, one wavevector, one propagation direction and one temperature run.
   - There are no comparison MPS3 compounds, and no argument for why a fixed elastic step at 150 K enables devices.
   - These claims should be toned down or substantiated.

23. **The link to magnetism rests only on a temperature coincidence with literature T_N (~155 K).**
   - T_N is not measured on these flakes (no magnetometry, no magnon Raman).
   - BMS laser power, spot size and local heating are not reported. BMS typically uses much higher power than the 0.024 mW Raman power, on a poorly heat-sunk flake on SiO2.
   - Cryostat type, temperature calibration and stabilization are not reported.
   - An independent check of T_N and an estimate of laser heating are needed.

24. **There are no controls against extrinsic causes.**
    - The bare SiO2/Si substrate was measured only at room temperature, and only to assign the mode.
    - Amorphous SiO2 has anomalous temperature-dependent elastic moduli, and the Rayleigh wave (λ ≈ 465 nm ≈ 2.1× the film thickness) penetrates the substrate.
    - The following are not mentioned: thermal-mismatch strain, interface or adhesion changes, adsorbate or ice condensation, vacuum conditions, and warming-versus-cooling hysteresis.
    - A temperature-dependent substrate measurement (80–300 K) is essential.

25. **Statistics and reproducibility are missing.**
    - The number of flakes and the number of temperature points near T_N are not stated.
    - There are no error bars, fit uncertainties or instrumental frequency resolution, although the effect is only ~0.2 GHz.
    - The v(T) curve is "a guide to the eye" with no model fit.

26. **Discriminating measurements are absent.**
    - The dispersion v(q) was measured only at room temperature; the temperature scan used a single q. Dispersion above and below T_N, and ideally a thickness series, would separate a film-modulus change from a substrate or interface effect.
    - BMS linewidth, intensity and attenuation versus temperature are not analysed. An attenuation peak at T_N would be stronger evidence than a frequency step.
    - Alternative mechanisms are not discussed: zigzag domain formation, domain-wall or ΔE-type magnetostrictive relaxation, and laser heating.
    - Crystal axes were assigned only from cleavage edges, with no verification and no angular uncertainty. This matters because a and b are inequivalent under zigzag order.

27. **The abstract overclaims what the calculations achieve.** It states that DFT + FEM "reproduce both the Rayleigh-wave dispersion and its magnetic-order–induced shift", and the Conclusions say the calculations "corroborate" the result. In fact:
    - The FEM shift is −0.54% (softening), against a measured −5.5%, about 10× smaller.
    - The absolute FEM frequency (4.6 vs ~4.2 GHz) is off by ~10%, which is larger than the effect being explained.
    - The wording should reflect a qualitative agreement in sign only.

28. **The explanation for the 10× gap is not justified and likely points the wrong way.**
    - The paper invokes 0 K DFT and neglected spin fluctuations, with no argument about which direction these corrections go.
    - Comparing AFM with a fully relaxed non-magnetic state removes local moments entirely and includes the volume change. If anything, this should overestimate the magnetic contribution.
    - Short-range order above T_N would further reduce the AFM–paramagnet difference.

29. **The non-magnetic state is an unrealistic proxy for paramagnetic Ni²⁺ (S = 1).**
    - No Hubbard U is used or justified.
    - No band gap, moment per Ni, AFM–NM energy difference or NM lattice parameters (volume change) are reported, so the NM state may be metallic.
    - Standard alternatives exist and should be used or discussed: DFT+U, disordered-local-moment or SQS paramagnets, and comparison of several magnetic configurations (FM, Néel, stripy, zigzag).
    - The supercell is labelled "1×4×2", but its dimensions correspond to 2×2×2.
    - DFT convergence tests (k-mesh, cutoff, strain amplitude) are not reported.

30. **The elastic constants emphasized are not the ones the mode depends on.**
    - The text emphasizes c66 because it shows the largest change (+17%). For a Rayleigh wave propagating along b in C2/m, the mode depends mainly on c22, c33, c23 and c44; c66 enters only weakly via c46.
    - The paper's own sensitivity analysis says the velocity is most sensitive to c33 and c44.
    - The DFT gives c44 essentially unchanged and c33 only −8%. Yet the text attributes the drop to "a sizable reduction in shear- and out-of-plane coefficients", contradicting its own calculation.
    - The "slow-on-fast" criterion is defined via in-plane shear velocity, but √(c66/ρ) ≈ 3.9 km/s is not slow relative to SiO2; it is c44 that makes the film slow.
    - The sensitivity numbers (Supplementary Table 2) should be summarized in the main text and explicitly connected to the DFT changes.

31. **The inverse analysis is invalid.**
    - "Δc_eff/c_eff ≈ 2Δv/v ≈ 11%" applies half-space scaling to a 220 nm film on SiO2/Si. It ignores substrate participation and density changes, and c_eff is undefined.
    - The authors should solve the inverse problem: what change in NiPS3 c33 or c44 reproduces −5.5% in the layered stack?
    - Given the FEM sensitivity (−0.54% for the DFT changes), the required change is likely several tens of percent. The authors should state this and discuss whether it is physically plausible.

32. **The phenomenological model is asserted, not derived, and is inconsistent with the data and the DFT.** The model is c_eff = c0 − λ²χ_m.
    - It is not derived, λ is defined only as "∝ B", and the strain and spin indices are conflated. λ is also used for the laser wavelength.
    - No coupling constants are extracted or fit.
    - It is not stated whether χ is the uniform or staggered susceptibility. For exchange striction, the relevant response is closer to exchange-energy fluctuations (a specific-heat-like quantity) than to χ.
    - With χ nonzero above T_N, the form predicts a cusp or dip near T_N, not the observed step followed by a plateau. A step is the textbook Landau signature of strain coupling to the square of the order parameter, which is not mentioned.
    - The form can only soften the moduli, yet the DFT shows c66 stiffening.
    - "Continuum limit of spin–phonon coupling" is never defined.
    - Gold (1956) and Callen & Callen (1963) treat cubic crystals; no adaptation to monoclinic symmetry is shown.

33. **The FEM model is underdocumented.**
    - The SiO2, Si and NiPS3 elastic constants and densities are not given, nor are their sources or temperature dependence.
    - The Si layer has finite thickness and a fixed bottom boundary, with no absorbing layer and no mesh or thickness convergence test. Spurious plate or standing modes are therefore possible.
    - Sezawa and Love modes of the film are not discussed. The Rayleigh assignment rests on a single FEM eigenmode and a loosely stated selection rule.

34. **The velocity drop is reported inconsistently.** It is "~6%" in the discussion and FEM comparison but "~5.5%" in the abstract, conclusions and 11% derivation.

35. **The room-temperature velocity exceeds the stated paramagnetic maximum.**
    - The room-temperature value of ~4.2 GHz at q = 0.0135 nm⁻¹ corresponds to ~1955 m/s.
    - The stated paramagnetic maximum is ~1920 m/s, although the frequency reportedly rises on cooling.
    - The data points defining 1920 → 1815 m/s should be identified.

36. **Other inconsistencies.**
    - The Raman temperature range differs between sections.
    - "Electrodynamic" appears where "elastodynamic" is meant.

37. **1. The size of the Rayleigh-wave (RW) softening is reported inconsistently, and its reference point is undefined.**
- The velocity drop is given as "~5.5%" (para 36, Fig. 4 caption, para 59, Conclusions) and as "~6%" (paras 40, 62). At fixed q, frequency and velocity drops must be identical, so a single value should be used throughout.
- The room-temperature frequency of ~4.2 GHz at q = 0.0135 nm⁻¹ (para 32) gives v = 2πf/q ≈ 1950 m/s. This conflicts with the quoted "~1920 m/s in the PM phase."
  - Measured from 1950 m/s, the drop to ~1815 m/s is ≈6.8%, not 5.5%.
- The data show the frequency rising from 300 K to ~200 K before dropping near ~150 K. The magnetic contribution should therefore be defined relative to an extrapolated non-magnetic (anharmonic) baseline, as the authors already do for the Raman data.
- **Requested:**
  - one clearly defined reference point and baseline model;
  - the resulting Δf (≈0.23 GHz) and Δv/v, with uncertainties.

38. **2. Error bars and statistics are absent.** Please report:
- the uncertainty on each fitted BMS frequency and velocity, from the fit and from repeat measurements;
- the number of flakes measured;
- the number of thermal cycles;
- whether data were taken on cooling only or also on warming, and whether hysteresis was checked;
- the number of temperature points and their spacing near T_N.

Without these, the reader cannot judge whether the ~0.23 GHz shift exceeds run-to-run scatter, or how sharp the anomaly is.

39. **3. Dispersion should also be measured below T_N.** The θ-dependent dispersion (θ = 30–50°) was measured only at room temperature. Repeating it below T_N would show that the softening is a property of the mode across q, not an artefact of a single angle.

40. **4. The substrate is not excluded as a source of the temperature dependence.**
- The RW wavelength (2π/q ≈ 465 nm) is about twice the film thickness (~220 nm), so a large fraction of the elastic energy resides in the SiO2/Si substrate.
- Amorphous SiO2 has well-known anomalous temperature dependence of its elastic constants.
- The bare SiO2/Si reference was measured only at room temperature.
- **Requested:**
  - a temperature-dependent reference measurement on bare SiO2/Si, or an FEM estimate of the substrate's contribution;
  - the fraction of modal elastic energy in the film versus the substrate.

41. **5. T_N was not verified on the measured flakes.**
- T_N ≈ 155 K is taken from the literature, but the BMS drop appears at ~150 K. The difference is unexplained.
- Local laser heating is a plausible cause, since 532 nm lies above the NiPS3 gap.
- **Requested:**
  - the BMS laser power on the sample;
  - a heating check, e.g. the apparent transition temperature versus laser power;
  - ideally, an independent on-sample signature of magnetic order, such as two-magnon or quasi-elastic Raman scattering.

The unassigned ~450 cm⁻¹ feature (marked with an asterisk) should be discussed. Its temperature dependence should be shown, and a possible magnetic origin considered.

42. **6. The conversion Δc/c ≈ 2Δv/v ≈ 11% is not justified.**
- "c_eff" is not identified with any specific c_ij.
- The relation treats the RW as if it propagated in bulk NiPS3. It ignores:
  - substrate participation, which would make the intrinsic NiPS3 change larger;
  - possible density changes from magnetostriction or thermal contraction.
- Para 62's statement that the drop "implies a sizable reduction in the shear- and out-of-plane-related elastic coefficients" is qualitative only.
- **Requested:** an inverse FEM fit giving the change in c33 and/or c44 needed to reproduce the measured shift, with a statement about non-uniqueness.

43. **7. The ~10× model–experiment gap is not addressed, and agreement is overclaimed.**
- The FEM magnetic shift is −0.54% (4.603 → 4.578 GHz, NM → AFM). By √c scaling this corresponds to only a ~1.1% change in effective modulus, about 10× smaller than the inferred 11%.
- The room-temperature discrepancy (4.6 vs 4.2 GHz, ~10%) is itself larger than the magnetic effect being explained.
- Despite this:
  - the abstract states that the model "reproduce[s] both the dispersion and its magnetic-order-induced shift";
  - the text calls 4.6 vs 4.2 GHz "close agreement";
  - the Conclusions say DFT and FEM "corroborate" the effect.

These statements should be revised, and the gap discussed explicitly. The authors attribute it to 0 K DFT and the NM≠PM approximation; this should be quantified or at least argued in more detail.

44. **8. The discussion of the DFT elastic constants does not match what the RW probes.**
- The text highlights c66 (47.4 → 55.4 GPa, +17%, called "the most notable variation") and c12 (−22%). For a sagittal RW propagating along b, these in-plane constants contribute only weakly; c66 enters only through c46 mixing.
- The paper's own sensitivity analysis ranks c33 and c44 as most important, followed by c22, c23 and c46. Of these:
  - c44 is **unchanged** in DFT (9.7 → 9.7 GPa);
  - c33 decreases by 7.8%;
  - c23 decreases by 24%;
  - c22 increases by 1.9%.
- These changes partly offset, which presumably explains the small 0.54% shift. The paper says only that the shift arises from a "complex combination of elastic coefficients" and "boundary constraints and elastic mismatch."
- **Requested:**
  - a breakdown of Δf for each c_ij using the actual AFM−NM differences;
  - explicit acknowledgment that DFT predicts no magnetic change in c44, the most sensitive constant;
  - discussion of what mechanism could soften c44 or c33 beyond the DFT prediction.

45. **9. The magnetoelastic model is underspecified.**
- **Notation:** the coupling B_αβ is written with two indices, and strain indices are reused as spin indices. A symmetry-adapted rank-4 tensor appropriate for C2/m should be used.
- **Derivation:** c_eff = c0 − λ²χ_m is stated without derivation or reference.
- **Undefined quantities:**
  - whether χ_m is the uniform, staggered, or order-parameter susceptibility;
  - the definition, units and magnitude of λ and B.
- **Expected anomaly shape:** linear-quadratic ε·M² coupling gives a step below T_N, whereas λ²χ coupling gives a cusp or dip at T_N. The paper should state which it expects and compare with the step-like data.
- **Cited theory:** Callen & Callen and Gold were developed for cubic crystals. This should be acknowledged, and its applicability to monoclinic NiPS3 discussed.
- **Thin-film effects:** substrate clamping, thermal-mismatch strain, and spontaneous magnetostriction of a clamped film should be considered (ref. 13 is cited but not applied).
- **Bonding:** perfect film/substrate bonding is implicitly assumed and should be stated.

46. **10. The "local vs long-range order" argument is asserted rather than justified.**
- The paper argues that Raman probes local spin correlations, while acoustic phonons probe long-range order. However, the q→0 magnetoelastic response also involves short-range correlations, and precursor or critical elastic softening above T_N is common in antiferromagnets.
- The 300 → 200 K behaviour should be discussed in this light.
- The classic ultrasound literature on antiferromagnets (e.g., MnF2, NiO, Lüthi's work) should be cited.
- The paper should explain more concretely why the Ni-involving 255 cm⁻¹ Raman mode shows only a ~0.3 cm⁻¹ anomaly while the RW softens by ~5–6%.

47. **11. Mode assignment is incomplete.**
- For qh ≈ 3, guided (Sezawa) modes are generally expected in a film-on-substrate system. The FEM itself yields "several surface and guided branches."
- The paper should explain why only one film mode is observed. The cited literature on BMS of transparent supported films (refs. 40, 41) could inform this discussion.
- Loudon's surface-ripple selection rule is stated as displacement "perpendicular to propagation." This literally includes shear-horizontal motion; it should read "surface-normal displacement."

48. **12. BMS methods.** Please specify:
- **Instrument:**
  - laser power on the sample;
  - objective and NA;
  - spot size, including its elongation at oblique incidence;
  - free spectral range and mirror spacing;
  - spectral resolution and instrumental linewidth;
  - frequency calibration procedure;
  - acquisition time per spectrum;
  - polarization configuration.
- **Geometry:**
  - how θ was set and calibrated with the sample inside the cryostat, including window refraction;
  - whether the geometry is strictly 180° backscattering.
- **Cryostat:** model, temperature sensor location, temperature stability, cooling rate.
- **Fitting:** the fitting procedure and fitted linewidths.
- **Spot vs flake:** confirmation that the (elongated) spot remains on the flake during the temperature series, given the lateral flake size.

49. **13. Raman methods.**
- **Power rationale:** "A low laser power of 0.024 mW was used to ensure a high signal-to-noise ratio while minimizing laser-induced heating" is illogical, since lower power reduces SNR. Please correct the sentence and confirm the power value.
- **Missing parameters:** objective, grating, spectral resolution, acquisition time, number of accumulations, temperature steps.
- **Temperature range:** the Methods state 77–300 K, but the data shown cover ~90–290 K.
- **Anharmonic model:** specify the model (e.g., Balkanski/Klemens) and the fit range used for the 255 cm⁻¹ mode.
- **Line shape:** quantify the reported "slight Fano asymmetry," or justify using Lorentzian fits despite it.
- **Mode count:** the claim that "all Raman-active modes are observed" (Γ = 8Ag + 7Bg = 15) should be supported explicitly by the mode table, given the near-degenerate Ag/Bg pairs.
- **Selection rules:** check the stated rules (Ag+Bg in parallel, Bg only in crossed polarization) against the C2/m Raman tensors. State how the lab x/y axes relate to the crystal a/b axes.
- **Missing number:** para 19 reads "the higher-frequency modes above … originate from (P2S6)⁴⁻ units," but the threshold frequency after "above" is missing.
- **Discrepancies with prior work:** the non-observation of the reported 176 cm⁻¹ splitting and of magnon or excitonic features deserves more than the generic "requires higher spectral sensitivity." Discuss sample thickness, heating, resolution and sample quality.

50. **14. Crystal orientation.**
- The a/b assignment relies solely on optical microscopy of cleavage edges at 60°/120°. In a quasi-hexagonal layer, edges cannot distinguish the true a-axis from pseudo-equivalent directions rotated by 60°, so "q ∥ b" is not verified.
- This matters because the sensitivity ranking and the relevant c_ij depend on propagation direction.
- **Requested:**
  - independent verification, e.g. angle-resolved polarized Raman (Ag/Bg intensity vs polarization angle), or azimuth-dependent BMS;
  - the angular uncertainty;
  - how alignment was maintained in the cryostat.
- The term "zigzag" is used both for the lattice-edge geometry (para 18) and for the magnetic order (para 17). These usages should be distinguished.

51. **15. DFT methods.**
- **Missing settings:**
  - whether a Hubbard U was used, and its value (important for Ni²⁺ d⁸);
  - whether spin–orbit coupling was included;
  - whether elastic constants are relaxed-ion or clamped-ion;
  - convergence tests for the 3×2×3 k-mesh and the 600 eV cutoff.
- **Implied but not stated:** PAW pseudopotentials and D3 with Becke–Johnson damping are implied only through citations (refs. 55, 57) and should be stated explicitly.
- **Supercell:** it is labelled "1×4×2," but the stated dimensions (11.499 × 19.914 × 12.308 Å) correspond roughly to 2a × 2b × 2c of the conventional cell. Clarify the base cell and the multiplication.
- **Strain description:** "a strain amplitude of 0.015, and four displacements" is undefined. Is 0.015 a fraction or a percentage? Is it a strain or a displacement step (e.g., VASP POTIM/NFREE)? What do the "four displacements" refer to?
- **Magnetic state:**
  - specify the AFM configuration used (presumably zigzag; the word does not appear in the Methods);
  - give the Ni moment magnitude;
  - say whether moments were free or constrained under strain.
- **Cartesian frame:** state the frame for c_ij. Readers must currently infer y ∥ b from the nonzero c15/c25/c35/c46 entries. Given the monoclinic angle β ≈ 107°, state whether z is perpendicular to the layers.
- **Numerical precision:**
  - some small constants change sign between states (c35: −0.5 → +0.4 GPa), suggesting a precision of roughly ±0.5–1 GPa;
  - report uncertainties and avoid interpreting differences of this size (e.g., c13, c15, c55).
- **NM as a paramagnetic proxy:**
  - a non-spin-polarized (NM) calculation removes the Ni local moments entirely, whereas these moments persist above T_N in the real paramagnet;
  - the AFM−NM difference therefore mixes moment-formation effects with magnetic-ordering effects;
  - discuss this limitation more fully, and ideally compare with a spin-disordered (SQS) or ferromagnetic reference;
  - report the relaxed NM lattice parameters.
- **Exchange-striction claim:** the Conclusions claim the modelling links the effect "to exchange-striction at the microscopic level." No exchange constants or their strain derivatives are computed, so this should be supported or removed.

52. **16. FEM methods.**
- **Geometry and mesh:**
  - unit-cell dimensions (Lx, Ly);
  - Si thickness used to represent the half-space;
  - mesh size and mesh-convergence test;
  - how spurious modes from the fixed bottom boundary are identified or suppressed (e.g., PML or low-reflecting boundary).
- **Floquet wavevector:** Floquet periodicity is applied along both x and y, but the wavevector components are not specified ("k = q"). State, for example, k_x = 0 and k_y = q, and confirm that propagation is along b.
- **Material inputs:**
  - NiPS3 density, and whether it is the DFT or experimental value;
  - SiO2 and Si elastic constants and densities, with sources;
  - Si wafer orientation and propagation direction;
  - how the NiPS3 stiffness tensor is rotated into the model frame;
  - whether temperature-dependent substrate constants were used.
- **Thickness sensitivity:** analyse sensitivity to film thickness, given that thickness is reported only as an "average ~220 nm" without spread.
- **Sensitivity analysis:** summarise the method (+20% change of each c_ij individually; Supp. Table 2) in the main text, and confirm that the perturbed tensors remain positive definite.
- **Wording errors:**
  - "linear electrodynamic equation" should be "elastodynamic";
  - the periodic-boundary-condition sentences are unclear and should be rewritten.

54. **18. Novelty relative to prior work is not stated.**
- Ref. 36 (Wright, D. et al., APL 124, 161903 (2024), "Acoustic and optical phonons in quasi-two-dimensional MPS3 antiferromagnetic semiconductors") appears to come from the same group: D. Wright is a co-author here, and the corresponding authors' prior BMS work is also cited (refs. 38, 43).
- Ref. 36 is cited only as a Raman benchmark.
- The paper should state explicitly what is new relative to it, e.g. temperature dependence through T_N, or observation of the RW.
- The statements that acoustic phonons in vdW magnets "remain largely unexplored" and that the acoustic response of NiPS3 "remains unknown" should be tempered accordingly.
- "Rarely reported in 2D magnets" (para 62) needs supporting citations.

55. **19. Superlatives are undefined.**
- "Giant," "anomalous," and "continuum limit … established" are not quantified.
- A comparison with elastic or acoustic anomalies at magnetic transitions in other materials would give these terms meaning. Ultrasound studies of classic antiferromagnets, and refs. 21 (FeRh), 22 (U2Rh2Sn) and 24 (BiFeO3), which are already cited, could serve.
- Otherwise the language should be toned down.

56. **20. The "order of magnitude larger coupling" comparison (refs. 49–52) needs qualification.**
- It mixes:
  - theory with experiment;
  - monolayer/nanosheet calculations with bulk measurements;
  - ferromagnets (CrBr3, Cr2Ge2Te6) with an antiferromagnet;
  - Raman spin–phonon coupling constants with the magnetoelastic coupling relevant to acoustic waves.
- It is not related quantitatively to the measured RW shift, and no coupling constant B is extracted from the data.
- Please qualify the comparison, or extract B from the data and compare like with like.
