**Review outline:**

## 1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript uses angle-resolved Brillouin–Mandelstam spectroscopy (BMS) to follow the Rayleigh surface acoustic wave of a ~220 nm exfoliated NiPS₃ flake on SiO₂/Si through the Néel temperature. It reports a ~5.5% drop in phase velocity on entering the zigzag AFM phase. In the same material, optical phonons (Raman) show only marginal anomalies.

Accessing long-wavelength elastic renormalization in micron-scale exfoliated vdW magnets is a worthwhile goal, and BMS is a sensible tool for it. If the anomaly is intrinsic, it would be a notable result. In its present form, however, the headline effect rests on:

- a small number of temperature points;
- a single flake and a single propagation direction;
- no extrinsic controls;
- a first-principles model that reproduces only ~1/10 of the measured shift.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **Technique.** The approach is experimentally relevant and fairly novel for 2D magnets. Surface acoustic phonons in a supported exfoliated flake are a direct handle on the macroscopic elastic tensor, which ultrasound and inelastic neutron scattering cannot easily reach.
- **Mode assignment.** The RT assignment is reasonable:
  - the dispersion follows q = (4π/λ)sinθ (e.g., 0.0135 nm⁻¹ at 35°);
  - the phase velocity decreases with q, as expected for slow-on-fast;
  - a bare-substrate spectrum is shown (Supp. Fig. 5);
  - FEM with DFT elastic constants yields a surface-localized mode of similar frequency.
- **Elastic tensor.** The full 13-component monoclinic elastic tensor is given for both AFM and NM states (Table 1), along with a sensitivity analysis (Supp. Table 2). These are useful to the community independent of the anomaly.
- **Raman context.** Raman data are presented alongside, which sets the acoustic result against the more common optical-phonon approach.

## 3. Concerns

### A. Must address

**1. The temperature dependence of the anomaly is under-sampled, single-sample, and partly inconsistent with the paper's own RT data (Fig. 4b; Fig. 3b).**

- *Sparse sampling.* Fig. 4b has only seven points (90, 120, 130, 150, 200, 250, 300 K), and none between 150 and 200 K.
  - The "drop at T_N" is therefore not resolved; it could occur anywhere in a 50 K window.
  - The dash-dot curve, with its maximum near 210 K, is not constrained by data.
- *Reference value.* The 5.5% figure is computed from a single point at 200 K, which is also the maximum of the curve. Relative to 300 K (~1865 m/s), the AFM value (~1815 m/s) is only ~2.7% lower.
- *Internal inconsistency.* At the same θ = 35° (q ≈ 0.0135 nm⁻¹), Fig. 3b gives f ≈ 4.2 GHz and v ≈ 1945 m/s at RT. Fig. 4a/b give ≈4.05 GHz and ≈1865 m/s at 300 K. This ~4% run-to-run (or spot-to-spot) discrepancy is comparable to the claimed effect.
- *Unknown sample count and history.* The number of flakes measured, thermal cycling, and cooling/warming hysteresis are not stated.

The authors should provide:
- dense sampling across 130–200 K (≤5 K steps);
- repeated measurements at each temperature and both cooling and warming runs;
- data on at least two or three independent flakes, ideally of different thickness;
- an explanation of the 300 K vs RT discrepancy.

**2. Peak-position extraction may be biased by the low-frequency background (Fig. 4a).**

- *Fitting procedure.* The text states that "each spectrum was fit using a single Lorentzian function." Yet the low-T spectra in Fig. 4a sit on a steeply rising quasi-elastic tail, and the Rayleigh peak becomes weak and broad below 150 K.
  - Part of this weakening is expected from the ~T scaling of the Bose factor.
  - Fitting a single Lorentzian without a background term on such a slope systematically pulls the centroid toward lower frequency.
  - That bias grows as the peak-to-background ratio falls, which is precisely what happens on cooling. It could mimic, or inflate, a softening.
- *Error bars.* Their origin is not stated (fit covariance or repeats?).
- *Calibration.* Frequency calibration and drift of the tandem Fabry–Pérot interferometer are not described.

Please:
- show raw spectra with fits that include an explicit background model;
- fit Stokes and anti-Stokes peaks together and average them, to cancel zero-offset drift;
- report how the extracted frequency depends on fit window and background choice;
- state how error bars were obtained.

**3. No null or reference controls exclude extrinsic origins of a velocity change near 150 K.**

At q ≈ 0.0135 nm⁻¹ the acoustic wavelength is ~465 nm. This exceeds the film thickness, so the mode samples the 300 nm SiO₂ substantially (Supp. Fig. 7). Several non-magnetic mechanisms could therefore produce softening on cooling:

- *(a) Substrate elasticity.* Amorphous SiO₂ has anomalous elasticity: its moduli decrease on cooling below RT. The bare SiO₂/Si substrate modes (Supp. Fig. 5) should be measured versus temperature under identical conditions.
- *(b) Adsorbate mass loading.* Residual water condenses and desorbs in vacuum cryostats at roughly 140–170 K. Mass loading by an adsorbed ice layer lowers surface-wave frequency and would appear as a step close to T_N.
  - The vacuum level should be reported.
  - Warming-cycle data are needed.
  - The q dependence of the shift should be examined: mass loading and modulus changes scale differently with q.
- *(c) Interfacial and mismatch effects.* Thermal-expansion-mismatch strain or changes in interfacial contact stiffness of the exfoliated flake on cooling can also shift the Rayleigh velocity.

The most convincing control is a non-magnetic or differently ordered MPS₃ flake of similar thickness, measured on the same substrate under matched conditions:
- ZnPS₃ or CdPS₃ should show no step;
- FePS₃ (T_N ~118 K) or MnPS₃ (~78 K) should show a step that tracks their own T_N.

**4. The effective sample temperature and the T_N of the measured flake are not established.**

- *Unreported laser conditions.* The BMS laser power and spot size are not given. Raman used 0.024 mW, but BMS normally needs milliwatts to tens of milliwatts.
- *Heating risk.* NiPS₃ absorbs at 532 nm and sits on a thermally insulating 300 nm oxide, so optical heating of tens of kelvin is plausible. This would shift the apparent transition temperature and distort the temperature scale of Fig. 4b.
- *No independent T_N marker.* Nothing confirms T_N in this specific flake under BMS illumination. Options include:
  - the sharp ~1.47 eV spin-entangled exciton in photoluminescence;
  - the low-energy magnon Raman modes (refs. 28, 29);
  - Raman-based thermometry (Stokes/anti-Stokes ratio or peak shift) at the BMS power.

Please report the laser power, show a power-dependence test at a fixed set temperature, and correlate the acoustic anomaly with an independent order-parameter marker measured on the same flake.

**5. The calculations do not reproduce the magnitude of the effect, yet the abstract states they do.**

- *Magnitude.* The abstract and conclusions say DFT+FEM "reproduce both the Rayleigh-wave dispersion and its magnetic-order-induced shift." The FEM gives a 0.54% decrease (4.603 → 4.578 GHz) against a ~5.5% measured change, a factor of ~10.
- *Inconsistency with the sensitivity analysis.*
  - The sensitivity analysis identifies c₃₃ and c₄₄ as dominant.
  - The DFT c₄₄ is unchanged between phases (9.7 GPa in both).
  - c₃₃ falls ~8%, and c₂₂ actually increases.
  - The DFT therefore does not identify the mechanism; it argues against a large intrinsic effect at 0 K.

The authors should:
- revise the claims to reflect this;
- invert the problem: use the sensitivity coefficients, or better a fit of the full dispersion at, e.g., 90 K and 200 K, to determine what changes in c₄₄ and c₃₃ the data require (likely tens of percent for the interlayer shear constant);
- check whether such changes are thermodynamically plausible. For a continuous transition, the Landau/Testardi relation Δc ≈ (∂T_N/∂ε)²ΔC_p/(VT_N) links the elastic step to the specific-heat jump and the strain or pressure dependence of T_N. Both are available or estimable for NiPS₃.

### B. Should address

**6. DFT methodology is insufficiently specified and likely inadequate for the PM reference (Methods; Table 1).**

- *Missing correlation and SOC settings.* No Hubbard U (or hybrid functional) is mentioned for Ni 3d. Plain PBE underestimates the gap and moments of NiPS₃ and may misplace the magnetic energy scale that drives magnetoelastic changes. SOC is omitted, although the XY anisotropy is invoked throughout.
- *NM is a poor PM proxy.* For local-moment Ni²⁺ (S = 1), the NM state quenches the moments entirely, which changes the volume and bonding. Disordered-local-moment or SQS-type paramagnetic models, or at least an average over several collinear configurations (FM, Néel, stripy, zigzag), would be far more meaningful.
- *Cell description.* The supercell is called 1×4×2, but the quoted lattice dimensions (11.5 × 19.9 × 12.3 Å) correspond to ~2×2×2 of the conventional C2/m cell. Please clarify.
- *Further items to specify:*
  - whether elastic constants are relaxed-ion (essential in C2/m);
  - the meaning of "four displacements";
  - convergence with respect to the 3×2×3 k-mesh;
  - the Cartesian frame of the Voigt tensor relative to a, b and the monoclinic c*;
  - whether the FEM density was updated for each phase's relaxed volume.

**7. The phenomenological model in the "atomistic perspective" section is not appropriate as written.**

- *Wrong susceptibility.* The expression c_eff = c⁽⁰⁾ − λ²χ_m uses the uniform magnetic susceptibility. For an antiferromagnet, coupling to the staggered order parameter L has the form ε L². This gives a Landau step proportional to λ²/(quartic coefficient) below T_N, plus a fluctuation term near T_N. A step below T_N with saturation is in fact what the data suggest, and the authors should derive and use that form.
- *Modulus conversion.* Δc/c ≈ 2Δv/v ≈ 11% assumes the Rayleigh velocity scales as the square root of a single film modulus. In a bilayer with substantial substrate participation, the required change in the NiPS₃ constants is larger. A participation-weighted estimate from the FEM should be given.
- *Sign.* The DFT shows c₆₆ hardening on ordering, which is inconsistent with a universally softening λ²χ term.

**8. The absolute theory–experiment offset exceeds the effect under discussion (Fig. 3b vs 3d).**

- At q = 0.0135 nm⁻¹ the FEM gives 4.6 GHz against 4.2 GHz measured (~10%), and the simulated velocities (≈1985–2210 m/s) are systematically above the measured ones (≈1700–1960 m/s). "Close agreement" overstates this.
- Please quantify the sensitivity to the inputs: film thickness and its uniformity under the spot, NiPS₃ density, SiO₂/Si constants and Si orientation.
- Show that the fixed-bottom, finite-Si FEM boundary (rather than a PML) does not perturb the mode.
- Consider fitting thickness and c₄₄ to the RT dispersion before interpreting temperature-dependent shifts.

**9. Only one propagation direction is measured, and the crystallographic assignment rests on cleavage edges alone (Supp. Fig. 1).**

- The zigzag order breaks the pseudo-hexagonal in-plane symmetry, so magnetostriction should be anisotropic. Measuring q ∥ a as well as q ∥ b would be a strong intrinsic test, because extrinsic effects should be nearly isotropic.
- Cleavage edges at 60° do not by themselves distinguish a from b, and monoclinic stacking twins may be present. Angle-resolved polarized Raman (or SHG/XRD) should confirm the axes and domain state under the BMS spot.

**10. The Raman analysis and the "optical phonons are insensitive" narrative need tightening (Fig. 2; p. 8).**

- *Fit range.* The anharmonic fits appear to be straight lines; please state the fit range. Fitting above T_N only and extrapolating is standard, and including low-T data hides deviations.
- *Missing curves.* Fig. 2d,e show no fitted curves despite the caption.
- *FWHM jump.* The 255 cm⁻¹ FWHM in Fig. 2d shows a visible jump around 150 K that looks larger than the quoted ~0.3 cm⁻¹ deviation.
- *Conflicting literature.* The non-observation of the 176 cm⁻¹ splitting reported in ref. 25 should be discussed in terms of spectral resolution, laser power and SNR at 0.024 mW, and temperature step.
- *Internal tension.* The authors invoke ref. 49 (exceptionally large spin–phonon coupling in NiPS₃) as support. Yet their own Raman data show weak optical-phonon coupling, and the DFT gives a small acoustic effect. This should be reconciled.

**11. The "giant" and "rarely reported" framing needs quantitative context.**

Please compare the inferred ~11% modulus change with:
- ultrasonic elastic anomalies at T_N in classic antiferromagnets;
- reported lattice-parameter and thermal-expansion anomalies of bulk NiPS₃ at T_N;
- magnetostriction in MPS₃ (ref. 13).

An effect this large should leave a correspondingly large signature in thermal expansion, and consistency with existing structural data would strengthen the claim.

### C. Minor

**12. Figure and text inconsistencies.**
- The softening is quoted as 5.5% in some places and ~6% in others (pp. 15, 17).
- The 80 K spectrum appears in Fig. 4a but not in Fig. 4b.
- The Raman range is 77–300 K in Methods but 90–290 K in Fig. 2.
- Fig. 2a axis labels are wrong: the x-axis should be "Raman shift" and the y-axis "Intensity."
- The text says "red spheres" while the caption says "red circles."
- Typos: "Temperaure" in Fig. 4b; "REFRENCES."
- "Linear electrodynamic equation" should read "elastodynamic."
- "Intermolecular vibrations of (P₂S₆)⁴⁻" should read "intramolecular."

**13. Scattering mechanism.**
- The statement of Loudon's selection rule is imprecise: the surface-ripple mechanism requires a displacement component normal to the surface.
- For a semi-transparent 220 nm film, the elasto-optic contribution and interference effects (ref. 40) should be discussed.
- The authors should explain why no Sezawa modes of the film are observed at qh ≈ 3.

**14. Missing experimental details.**
- BMS: laser power, spot size, collection NA, acquisition time, FSR and mirror spacing.
- Cryostat: type, vacuum level, and thermometer location.
- FEM: mesh, material constants and Si orientation.
- Thickness: the distribution under the laser spot, not just the average.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Full dispersion at several temperatures.** Measure the complete dispersion (θ = 30–50°) at representative temperatures (e.g., 90, 140, 170, 200, 300 K) and invert it with the FEM to extract c₄₄(T) and c₃₃(T) with uncertainties. This separates film-modulus changes from thickness, interface and mass-loading effects, which have distinct q dependences.
2. **Tie the anomaly to magnetic order.** Run a family comparison on the same substrate: ZnPS₃/CdPS₃ as nulls, and FePS₃ and MnPS₃ as materials with different T_N. Also measure a thickness series down to few layers, where the order is known to be suppressed (ref. 25).
3. **Simultaneous order-parameter marker.** Acquire a temperature-resolved marker of magnetic order (exciton PL or magnon Raman) on the same flake at the BMS laser power. Plot the velocity change against the order-parameter squared to test the ε L² Landau form.
4. **Stronger paramagnetic reference in the calculations.** Upgrade the DFT to include DFT+U (with U justified), SOC where relevant, and a disordered-moment PM model. State clearly what fraction of the measured effect the 0 K calculation captures.
