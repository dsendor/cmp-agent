Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic.** The manuscript uses angle-resolved Brillouin–Mandelstam scattering (BMS) to track a Rayleigh-type surface acoustic wave in exfoliated NiPS3 (~220 nm) on SiO2/Si across T_N (~155 K). It reports a ~5.5% drop in phase velocity, interpreted as a "giant" magnetoelastic renormalization of the long-wavelength elastic tensor.
- **Supporting work.** The authors add:
  - Room-temperature and temperature-dependent Raman data, which they argue show only weak optical-phonon signatures of ordering.
  - DFT (PBE-D3) elastic tensors for antiferromagnetic (AFM) and non-magnetic (NM) states.
  - COMSOL finite-element modelling of the heterostructure.
- **Why the question matters.** How long-range magnetic order renormalizes macroscopic elasticity in vdW magnets is timely and relevant to magnetoelastic, magnonic and strain-spintronic concepts. Contrasting short-range (optical-phonon) and continuum (acoustic) channels is a conceptually appealing framing.
- **Methodological novelty.** Probing surface acoustic phonons of micron-scale exfoliated antiferromagnets across T_N is non-trivial. Conventional ultrasound and inelastic neutron scattering are impractical at these sample sizes.
- **Novelty is overstated, however.**
  - Magnetic-transition signatures in the mechanical response of exfoliated MPS3 (including NiPS3) have already been reported with nanomechanical resonators (e.g., Šiškins et al., Nat. Commun. 2020).
  - Elastic anomalies at T_N in bulk antiferromagnets are textbook ultrasound phenomena.
  - The authors' own earlier BMS work on MPS3 acoustic phonons (Ref. 36) is not clearly differentiated.
- **Bottom line on significance.** The central quantitative claim (a ~5.5% velocity, i.e. ~11% modulus, step) would be striking if robust. As presented, however, it rests on sparse data and is not reproduced by the authors' own theory.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Experimental access.** The work demonstrates that a surface acoustic mode can be resolved in a thin exfoliated vdW antiferromagnet with a cryogenic micro-BMS setup. This opens a route to elastic spectroscopy of 2D magnets that is inaccessible to conventional techniques.
- **Mode assignment is reasonably supported.**
  - The dispersion follows q = (4π/λ)sinθ with physically sensible velocities.
  - The bare-substrate reference spectrum rules out a pure SiO2/Si origin.
  - The FEM eigenmode analysis reproduces the slow-on-fast trend of decreasing phase velocity with increasing q.
- **The full monoclinic elastic tensor (13 constants) in two magnetic configurations is useful.** It is rarely reported for MPS3 and could serve as a community reference. The 20% sensitivity analysis (Supplementary Table 2) identifies which constants the Rayleigh mode actually probes.
- **Clear conceptual framing.** The bond-scale (∂J/∂u) versus uniform-strain (∂J/∂ε) distinction is pedagogically clear and could motivate further work on length-scale-dependent spin–lattice coupling.
- **Characterization is broadly consistent with the literature.**
  - Polarized Raman symmetry assignment of A_g and B_g modes.
  - AFM thickness measurement.
  - Crystallographic orientation from cleavage edges.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**3.1 The experimental evidence for the anomaly is sparse, and its magnitude is not robust.**

- **Temperature sampling is too coarse.**
  - Fig. 4 contains only ~7–8 temperatures (300, 250, 200, 150, 130, 120, 90/80 K) at a single q, with no data between 150 and 200 K, i.e., precisely around T_N.
  - The onset, width and shape of the anomaly (step, cusp, or gradual fluctuation-driven softening beginning above T_N, as expected for a quasi-2D magnet with short-range correlations persisting to ~2T_N) are therefore not resolved.
  - The statement that the drop occurs "at around T_N" is an interpolation, not a measurement.
- **The 5.5% figure depends on the chosen reference point.**
  - It is computed from the single 200 K maximum (~1925 m/s) to ~1815 m/s.
  - Relative to the 300 K value (~1865 m/s), the AFM-phase velocities (1805–1835 m/s) are only ~1.5–3% lower.
  - The unusually large ~3% stiffening between 300 and 200 K is far larger than typical anharmonic trends and is not explained. A single outlier at 200 K could account for much of the claimed magnitude.
  - The AFM-phase points are non-monotonic (1835 m/s at 130 K vs 1805 m/s at 90 K) with error bars of ±20–25 m/s. This undercuts the claim that the elastic constants "stabilize" below T_N.
- **Room-temperature values are not internally reproducible.**
  - At θ = 35° (q ≈ 0.0135 nm⁻¹), Fig. 3(b) gives ~4.2 GHz and ~1945 m/s.
  - Fig. 4(b) gives ~1865 m/s at 300 K for the same nominal q.
  - This ~4% discrepancy is comparable to the claimed magnetic effect and is not discussed (different flake? cryostat window? angle calibration? focus?).
- **Spectral quality degrades exactly where the conclusion matters.**
  - In Fig. 4(a), the Rayleigh peak intensity collapses at 80–120 K while a quasi-elastic background rises.
  - The peaks are broad (~1 GHz).
  - Each spectrum is fitted with a single Lorentzian, without reported linewidths, fitting windows or background treatment.
  - A shift of ~0.2 GHz extracted from weak, broad features on a sloping background requires much more careful uncertainty analysis.
- **No repeatability or hysteresis checks are reported.**
  - There is no warming run, no repeat cooling, and no second flake or thickness.

**3.2 Non-magnetic and extrinsic explanations are not excluded.**

- **Substrate participation is substantial and its temperature dependence is ignored.**
  - At q = 0.0135 nm⁻¹ the acoustic wavelength (~465 nm) exceeds the film thickness (220 nm), and the authors themselves state that the mode penetrates into SiO2.
  - Amorphous SiO2 has an anomalous temperature dependence of its elastic moduli: they decrease on cooling over this range.
  - Neither a temperature-dependent bare-substrate BMS measurement nor temperature-dependent SiO2/Si constants in the FEM are presented.
- **Static strain and interface effects are not considered.**
  - Thermal-expansion mismatch between NiPS3 (anisotropic, with known lattice anomalies at T_N) and SiO2/Si induces biaxial strain on cooling.
  - A vdW-bonded exfoliated flake may partially slip or change interfacial stiffness with temperature.
  - Either could shift a hybrid film–substrate mode without any change in intrinsic c_ij.
  - The FEM assumes a perfectly bonded interface. No interfacial-spring sensitivity test is given.
- **Measurement artefacts are not ruled out.**
  - *Adsorbates.* The Rayleigh mode is surface-localized, so condensation of water or other adsorbates on cooling would mass-load it and lower its frequency. The concurrent loss of BMS intensity in Fig. 4(a) is compatible with this. No vacuum level, cold-finger configuration or warm-up recovery data are given.
  - *Laser heating.* The BMS laser power is not stated, although the Raman power (0.024 mW) is. BMS typically requires mW-level powers, and heating of a thin flake on 300 nm SiO2 could offset the true sample temperature by tens of kelvin. That would shift the apparent anomaly temperature and distort the curve near T_N.
- **No control experiments link the anomaly to magnetic order.** Possible controls include:
  - a non-magnetic isostructural analogue (e.g., CdPS3 or ZnPS3);
  - an MPS3 with a different T_N (FePS3, ~118 K; MnPS3, ~78 K), to show that the anomaly tracks T_N;
  - thickness dependence, which changes film versus substrate participation;
  - measurements along a versus b.

**3.3 The theory does not support the quantitative claims and is methodologically questionable.**

- **An order-of-magnitude discrepancy is presented as confirmation.**
  - The FEM with DFT tensors gives a 0.54% frequency decrease (4.603 → 4.578 GHz) versus the ~5.5–6% measured.
  - Stating that "the trend confirms" the softening is not justified when the computed shift is within plausible DFT/FEM uncertainties.
  - The NM-based model already misses the room-temperature data by ~10% in frequency (4.6 vs 4.2 GHz) and in velocity (~2000–2200 vs ~1700–1950 m/s). This calibration error exceeds the magnetic effect.
- **The NM state is a poor proxy for the paramagnetic phase of Ni²⁺ (d⁸, S = 1).**
  - The NM calculation quenches local moments, altering bonding, lattice parameters and elastic constants. It therefore compares "moment formation" rather than "long-range ordering."
  - The experiment, by contrast, compares a disordered local-moment paramagnet with the ordered zigzag state.
  - No Hubbard U is mentioned, despite its known importance for NiPS3 band gap, moments and structure.
  - Disordered-local-moment or special-quasirandom-structure calculations, or at least energy/elastic comparisons among several magnetic orders (FM, Néel, zigzag, stripy), are needed.
- **Computational details raise further concerns.**
  - The stated "1×4×2 supercell" with dimensions 11.499 × 19.914 × 12.308 Å does not obviously match the conventional C2/m cell (a ≈ 5.8, b ≈ 10.1, c ≈ 6.6 Å, β ≈ 107°).
  - A 1.5% strain amplitude is large for the soft interlayer constants (c33, c44, c55 ≈ 10–45 GPa).
  - The k-mesh convergence of the soft constants is not shown.
- **The DFT table is internally inconsistent with the interpretation.**
  - The sensitivity analysis shows the Rayleigh velocity is most sensitive to c33 and c44.
  - In the DFT table, c44 is unchanged (9.7 → 9.7 GPa) and c33 drops by ~8%, while c22 and c66 increase.
  - The authors nonetheless claim the data "imply a sizable reduction in the shear- and out-of-plane-related elastic coefficients," without quantifying how large a change in c44 and/or c33 would reproduce 5.5%. For a mode partly in the substrate, this would likely be well above 11% in the film.
  - Attributing the gap to "0 K versus finite temperature" is speculative and not tested (e.g., with quasi-harmonic or spin-fluctuation corrections).

**3.4 The physical interpretation and the "giant coupling" claim are insufficiently grounded.**

- **The formula c_eff = c0 − λ²χ_m is not appropriate as applied.**
  - For an antiferromagnet with linear-in-strain, quadratic-in-order-parameter coupling (λεL²), Landau theory gives a step-like softening below T_N, Δc ≈ λ²/(2b), set by the quartic coefficient. It is not set by the uniform susceptibility, which merely cusps at T_N.
  - The written magnetoelastic energy E_me = −Σ B_αβ ε_αβ S_α S_β is a single-ion/anisotropic form, not the exchange-striction form the text invokes.
  - Converting Δv/v ≈ 5.5% into "Δc/c ≈ 11%" assumes a single effective constant, which contradicts the authors' own statement that the Rayleigh velocity in this heterostructure depends on a complex combination of c_ij and substrate participation.
- **No thermodynamic consistency check is made.**
  - An ~11% (or larger) step in film moduli should be consistent, via Ehrenfest-type relations, with the specific-heat anomaly, the strain dependence of T_N (dT_N/dε), and measured spontaneous magnetostriction or thermal-expansion anomalies of NiPS3.
  - NiPS3's specific-heat anomaly is broad and weak, owing to quasi-2D fluctuations, which makes a giant elastic step surprising. This must be reconciled quantitatively.
  - The cited "order-of-magnitude larger spin–phonon coupling constant" (Ref. 49) refers to a different (optical-phonon, theoretical) quantity and does not validate this magnitude.
- **Optical and acoustic probes are not compared like for like.**
  - Comparing fractional shifts of optical frequencies with the velocity of a hybrid surface wave is not an equal-footing comparison of coupling strength.
  - The weak Raman response reported here contrasts with prior reports of clear spin–phonon signatures in NiPS3 (mode splitting near 176 cm⁻¹, Fano lineshapes, magnon-related features; Refs. 16, 25, 30).
  - The discrepancy is attributed to sensitivity without analysis. The sparse Raman temperature grid, and spectra that are possibly not resolved enough, may explain it.
- **Magnetic domains and anisotropy are not addressed.**
  - Zigzag order in NiPS3 forms three 120°-rotated domains, so the probed region likely averages over domains.
  - Only q ∥ b was measured, although the DFT predicts anisotropic changes (e.g., c66 increases, c12 decreases).
  - Measuring the anomaly's anisotropy (q ∥ a vs q ∥ b) would be a strong test of the magnetoelastic origin, but is absent.
  - Cleavage-edge orientation assignment has a 60°/30° ambiguity (zigzag vs armchair), which should be confirmed by polarized Raman or SHG.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Strengthen and densify the temperature-dependent BMS data set.**
   - Measure at 5–10 K steps between ~100 and ~250 K, on both cooling and warming, at several q (θ = 30–50°). This gives the full AFM-phase dispersion, not one point.
   - Use at least two flakes of different thickness.
   - Report the BMS laser power and spot size, and calibrate local heating (e.g., Raman thermometry or a power-series extrapolation).
   - Report the cryostat vacuum, and demonstrate warm-up recovery to exclude adsorbate loading.
   - Provide linewidths, fitting procedures, background models and realistic uncertainties.
   - Reconcile the ~4% room-temperature discrepancy between Fig. 3(b) and Fig. 4(b).
   - Define the anomaly magnitude relative to an extrapolated paramagnetic baseline rather than a single 200 K point.

2. **Add control measurements that isolate the magnetic contribution.**
   - Bare SiO2/Si surface-wave velocities versus temperature, fed into the FEM.
   - Measurements along a and b to test anisotropy.
   - A non-magnetic or differently ordering MPS3 analogue (CdPS3/ZnPS3, FePS3, MnPS3) to show that the anomaly tracks T_N.
   - Thickness dependence to separate film and substrate participation.
   - Optionally, an independent probe such as a suspended-membrane resonance or field-dependent BMS for the same flake.
   - Include thermal-mismatch strain and an interfacial-stiffness parameter in the FEM to quantify extrinsic shifts.

3. **Upgrade the first-principles and continuum modelling to a quantitatively meaningful comparison.**
   - Use DFT+U (with U convergence) and model the paramagnet via disordered local moments or special quasirandom structures, or average over several magnetic configurations, instead of the NM state.
   - Clarify the supercell definition and check convergence of the soft constants with strain amplitude and k-mesh.
   - Perform an inverse fit: determine which changes in c33, c44 (and others) reproduce the measured multi-q dispersions in the AFM and paramagnetic phases.
   - Test whether these changes are physically plausible against the DFT and against literature thermal-expansion/magnetostriction data.
   - Present the 0.54% result honestly as a disagreement, and discuss candidate missing ingredients (spin fluctuations, quasi-harmonic effects) with estimates.

4. **Recast the theoretical interpretation and calibrate the claims.**
   - Replace the c0 − λ²χ_m expression with a Landau free-energy treatment of strain–order-parameter coupling, including the fluctuation contribution above T_N. Extract λ and compare with dT_N/dε and the specific-heat anomaly via Ehrenfest relations.
   - Discuss domain averaging for zigzag order.
   - Moderate the "giant" and "rarely reported" language, and situate the work against nanomechanical-resonator studies of MPS3, classic ultrasonic studies of antiferromagnets, the authors' own prior BMS on MPS3, and Raman reports of strong spin–phonon effects in NiPS3.
   - Fix inconsistencies and errors:
     - 5.5% vs "~6%".
     - 80 K in Fig. 4(a) vs 90 K in Fig. 4(b).
     - Fig. 2(a) axes: the x-axis is labelled "Temperature (K)" but is Raman shift; the y-axis is labelled "Raman Shift" but is intensity.
     - "Temperaure" typo in Fig. 4(b); "REFRENCES" heading.
     - "electrodynamic" should be "elastodynamic" in Methods.
     - The Loudon surface-ripple selection rule concerns displacement normal to the surface, not "perpendicular to the propagation direction."
