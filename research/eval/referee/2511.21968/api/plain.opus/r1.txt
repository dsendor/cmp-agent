# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** "Anomalous spin–lattice coupling in a 2D antiferromagnetic semiconductor revealed by surface acoustic Rayleigh waves"

## Summary of the submission

The authors use angle-resolved Brillouin–Mandelstam spectroscopy (BMS) to study a Rayleigh-type surface acoustic wave in a ~220 nm exfoliated NiPS₃ flake on 300 nm SiO₂/Si. The main experimental claim is that, at fixed q = 0.0135 nm⁻¹ along the b-axis, the Rayleigh phase velocity drops by ~5.5% (from ~1920 to ~1815 m/s) on cooling through T_N ≈ 155 K. They interpret this as a "giant" magnetoelastic renormalization of the long-wavelength elastic tensor.

Supporting work includes:
- temperature-dependent Raman spectroscopy, which shows only weak optical-phonon anomalies;
- DFT (PBE-D3) elastic tensors for AFM and non-magnetic (NM) states;
- COMSOL modelling of the heterostructure, which reproduces the dispersion trend but predicts only a 0.54% softening.

## Overall assessment

The topic is timely. Using surface BMS to access long-wavelength elastic anomalies in exfoliated vdW magnets is attractive and potentially valuable. However, the central claim rests on a sparse temperature data set with no controls. Several plausible non-magnetic contributions (substrate, strain, laser heating, fitting artefacts) are not excluded. The theoretical support is considerably weaker than the abstract implies. I cannot recommend publication in its current form. I consider the work potentially publishable after **major revision**, provided the experimental concerns below are addressed.

## Major issues

### 1. Temperature sampling around the transition is insufficient

Fig. 4(b) contains only seven temperatures, and there are **no data between 150 and 200 K**, exactly where the transition occurs.
- The "~1920 m/s PM value" is a single point at 200 K, about 45 K above T_N.
- The "drop at T_N" is defined by the gap between 200 K and 150 K.
- The dash-dotted guide in Fig. 4(b) peaks near 210 K and implies a smooth curve that the data do not constrain.

With these data one cannot tell whether the softening:
- is step-like at T_N (as expected for Landau coupling of strain to L²);
- has a critical dip; or
- begins well above T_N, which the 200 K maximum hints at and would point to short-range correlations.

**Requested:** dense sampling (≤5 K steps) from ~110 to ~200 K, both cooling and heating runs, and repeated measurements to establish reproducibility.

### 2. Internal inconsistency in the room-temperature velocity

At θ = 35° (q ≈ 0.0135 nm⁻¹), the two figures disagree:
- Fig. 3(b) and the text give f ≈ 4.2 GHz, i.e. v ≈ 1945–1955 m/s.
- Fig. 4(b) gives v ≈ 1865 m/s at 300 K for the same q.

This ~80 m/s (~4%) discrepancy is comparable to the claimed magnetic effect. The authors should explain it: different flake, cryostat window or refraction effects, angle calibration, or fitting procedure. Until this is resolved, the systematic uncertainty of the method appears comparable to the signal.

### 3. Missing control measurements

At this q, the Rayleigh mode samples the substrate substantially; the authors' own slow-on-fast argument depends on this. The substrate is therefore part of the measured response, and two controls are essential.

- **Bare-substrate temperature dependence.** Amorphous SiO₂ has anomalous elasticity: its moduli *decrease* on cooling below room temperature. This could contribute to the velocity decrease below 200 K. A temperature-dependent BMS measurement of the bare SiO₂/Si substrate in the same geometry is essential; Supp. Fig. 5 is at RT only.
- **A non-magnetic or differently ordered reference.** One option is an isostructural non-magnetic analogue (e.g., ZnPS₃ or CdPS₃) on the same substrate. Another is a different MPS₃ compound (FePS₃, T_N ≈ 118 K; MnPS₃, T_N ≈ 78 K). Either would show whether the anomaly tracks T_N rather than a generic temperature-dependent artefact.
- **Thermal-mismatch and adhesion effects.** Differential thermal contraction between NiPS₃ and Si/SiO₂, possibly with interfacial slip, should be discussed.

### 4. Laser heating and sample-temperature calibration in BMS

The BMS laser power is not reported, although the Raman power (0.024 mW) is. BMS typically requires mW-level power, and 532 nm is above the NiPS₃ gap, so local heating could be significant.
- If the local temperature exceeds the nominal temperature by tens of K, assigning the anomaly to T_N becomes uncertain.
- **Requested:** report the BMS power and spot size, and estimate or measure the local temperature (e.g., Stokes/anti-Stokes ratio, power-dependence tests). Ideally, independently verify T_N on the same flake under BMS conditions, for example via the Raman-active magnon or two-magnon features.

### 5. Spectral quality and fitting at low temperature

In Fig. 4(a), the Rayleigh peak:
- broadens and loses most of its intensity below ~130 K, becoming a weak shoulder at 80–90 K;
- sits on a strongly temperature-dependent sloping quasi-elastic background.

Fitting a single Lorentzian without an explicit background model to such spectra can bias the peak position by amounts comparable to the reported shift.

Please also address the following:
- Describe the background treatment, the origin of the error bars (fit uncertainty only?), acquisition times, and the number of spectra per temperature.
- Show the fits and residuals for the low-T spectra in the SI.
- Explain the dramatic intensity loss. Possible causes include focus or cryostat drift, condensation, or a physical origin such as AFM domain formation or changed optical absorption. If physical, it is itself interesting and should be discussed.

### 6. The DFT/FEM results do not support the magnitude of the effect

The abstract states that the calculations "reproduce both the Rayleigh-wave dispersion and its magnetic-order–induced shift." This is not accurate.

- **Magnitude.** The calculated shift (0.54%) is an order of magnitude smaller than the measurement (5.5%).
- **Mechanism.** The sensitivity analysis shows the mode is governed mainly by c₃₃ and c₄₄. DFT gives **no change in c₄₄** (9.7 GPa in both phases) and an ~8% reduction in c₃₃. The large change highlighted in the text, c₆₆ increasing by 17%, is essentially irrelevant to a sagittal-plane (b–c) Rayleigh wave. The calculations therefore do not explain the mechanism either.
- **Absolute agreement.** The calculated frequency (4.6 vs 4.2 GHz) and velocities (~2000–2200 vs ~1700–1950 m/s) are ~10% off. Calling this "close agreement" is generous, since the error exceeds the effect being studied.

The appeal to "finite-temperature effects" for the factor-of-10 discrepancy is not substantiated. The abstract and conclusions should be revised so the DFT is described as qualitatively consistent in sign only.

### 7. The NM state is a poor proxy for the paramagnet

Ni²⁺ (d⁸) carries a robust local moment of ~2 μB well above T_N. A non-spin-polarized calculation therefore conflates local-moment formation (a large Hund's-coupling energy scale) with long-range ordering. The AFM–NM elastic difference does not represent the ordered-vs-disordered difference probed experimentally.

**Suggestions:**
- Use a disordered-local-moment or special-quasirandom-structure approach.
- At minimum, compare several collinear magnetic configurations (FM, Néel, zigzag, stripy) to bracket the sensitivity of c_ij to spin arrangement.

Further methodological points:
- Was a Hubbard U used? PBE without +U is known to poorly describe NiPS₃'s gap and moments.
- Was spin–orbit coupling included? It is relevant to XY anisotropy and anisotropic magnetostriction.
- Report the relaxed lattice parameters against experiment. The stated supercell (1×4×2; 11.499 × 19.914 × 12.308 Å) does not obviously correspond to multiples of the conventional C2/m cell (a ≈ 5.81, b ≈ 10.07, c ≈ 6.63 Å, β ≈ 107°), and the c-direction value looks compressed.
- Clarify "four displacements" in the finite-difference strain scheme. Comment on whether ±1.5% strain is within the linear regime for the soft interlayer constants.
- Check whether the NM-state phonon dispersions (Supp. Fig. 9) show instabilities.

### 8. The phenomenological model is heuristic and partly inconsistent

- **Mixed coupling forms.** The magnetoelastic energy E_me = −Σ B_αβ ε_αβ S_α S_β is a single-ion (anisotropic) magnetostriction form. Yet the text attributes the effect to exchange-striction, which involves ⟨Sᵢ·Sⱼ⟩.
- **Wrong susceptibility.** The relation c_eff = c⁽⁰⁾ − λ²χ_m with χ_m the *magnetic susceptibility* is not the standard result for an antiferromagnet. In Landau theory with ε·L² coupling, the ordered phase shows a step Δc = −λ²/(2b), where b is the quartic coefficient, plus fluctuation terms. The relevant response is not the uniform susceptibility.
- **Underestimated film softening.** The estimate Δc/c ≈ 2Δv/v ≈ 11% treats the Rayleigh velocity as set by a single film modulus. Because a substantial fraction of the mode energy resides in the substrate, the required change in the NiPS₃ moduli must be larger than 11%. The authors have the sensitivity coefficients (Supp. Table 2) and should invert them to state explicitly how much c₄₄ and/or c₃₃ must change.
- **An independent check is available.** A thermodynamic (Testardi/Ehrenfest-type) estimate of the expected elastic step would be valuable. It can be built from the known specific-heat anomaly at T_N and the strain or pressure dependence of T_N in NiPS₃. Such an estimate would test whether "giant" is warranted.

### 9. The optical-vs-acoustic argument is overstated

The introduction and discussion argue that optical phonons probe short-range ⟨Sᵢ·Sⱼ⟩, whereas acoustic phonons probe long-range order. However, the exchange-striction contribution to the uniform elastic constants is also governed by Σ (∂J/∂ε)⟨Sᵢ·Sⱼ⟩, i.e. by the same short-range correlations, summed coherently. In a quasi-2D magnet such as NiPS₃, these correlations persist well above T_N (cf. Ref. 30).

The velocity maximum at 200 K and decrease starting above T_N may actually reflect this. The claim that the Rayleigh wave isolates *long-range* order should be softened or supported with data.

### 10. Missing prior work, and the "rarely reported" / "giant" framing

The statement that acoustic and elastic responses of vdW magnets are "largely unexplored" overlooks directly relevant work. Most notably, Šiškins et al. (Nat. Commun. 11, 2698, 2020) detected T_N in FePS₃, MnPS₃ and NiPS₃ membranes through nanomechanical resonance anomalies and related them to specific heat and thermal expansion. The authors' own BMS work on MPS₃ (Ref. 36) and thermal-expansion and magnetostriction studies of NiPS₃ should also be discussed.

Elastic anomalies of a few percent at magnetic transitions are well documented in ultrasonic studies of bulk antiferromagnets. "Giant" and "anomalous" should be justified quantitatively against such benchmarks or toned down.

### 11. Single sample, single direction, single wavevector

All temperature-dependent data come from one flake, one propagation direction, and one q. Three extensions would considerably strengthen the conclusions:

- **Propagation along a as well as b.** Zigzag order breaks in-plane symmetry, so an a/b anisotropy of the anomaly is a natural, falsifiable prediction. Note also that three zigzag domain orientations at 120° may coexist within the laser spot.
- **Full dispersion above and below T_N.** Different q weight film and substrate differently, which helps separate film from substrate contributions.
- **Several flake thicknesses.** The anomaly should scale with the film participation ratio if it is intrinsic to NiPS₃.

Crystallographic axis assignment from cleavage edges alone is ambiguous in a nearly hexagonal lattice. Please confirm it independently, for example with angle-resolved polarized Raman.

## Minor issues

1. The magnitude is quoted inconsistently: ~5.5% in the abstract and conclusions, ~6% elsewhere.
2. The "2D" framing in the title is questionable. A 220 nm flake is ~340 layers, is bulk-like, and has bulk T_N. "Layered" or "quasi-2D vdW" would be more accurate.
3. The Loudon selection rule is misstated. Ripple scattering couples to the surface-normal displacement u_z, not generically to "components perpendicular to the propagation direction"; SH modes satisfy the latter but do not scatter via ripple.
4. Raman (Section 2):
   - Kim et al. (Ref. 25) reported splitting of the ~176 cm⁻¹ mode. Your failure to observe it deserves discussion (spectral resolution, thickness, strain).
   - The "anharmonic model" in Fig. 2(b, c) appears linear; specify the functional form (e.g., Balkanski) and parameters.
5. Fig. 2(a) has its axes mislabeled: the x-axis should be Raman shift and the y-axis intensity.
6. Typos:
   - "Temperaure" in Fig. 4(b) should be "Temperature".
   - "REFRENCES" should be "REFERENCES".
   - In Methods, "electrodynamic equation" should be "elastodynamic equation".
   - Several hyphenation artefacts ("lowdimensional", "angleresolved", "outof-plane").
7. The NiPS₃ density used in COMSOL, and the elastic constants and densities used for SiO₂ and Si, should be stated.
8. Specify the Si thickness and fixed-bottom boundary condition used to approximate the half-space, and demonstrate convergence with respect to depth and mesh.
9. Report the thickness uncertainty from AFM and its effect on the calculated dispersion.
10. The Methods state the Raman temperature range as 77–300 K, but Fig. 2 shows 90–290 K. Please reconcile.
11. Indicate which data in Fig. 4(b) were taken on cooling vs. heating, and the thermal equilibration time at each temperature.

---

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The core observation could be interesting and important, but in its present form it is not established with the rigor required. The essential additions are:
- dense temperature sampling through T_N;
- a resolution of the RT-velocity inconsistency between Figs. 3 and 4;
- a temperature-dependent bare-substrate control;
- laser-heating characterization;
- a candid reframing of the DFT results.

A control material, a second propagation direction, and a physically sounder paramagnetic DFT reference would substantially strengthen the paper.
