Referee report

Manuscript: "Experimental Realization of the Topologically Nontrivial Phase in Monolayer Si2Te2" (arXiv:2508.07351v1)

1. Summary of the submission

The authors screen the C2DB database for a substrate on which monolayer Si2Te2 can be grown with small strain, select HfTe2, and use DFT (HSE06 + SOC) to argue that ML-Si2Te2 on ML-HfTe2 keeps its band inversion at Gamma and Z2 = 1, with a direct gap enlarged to 429 meV and an effective gap of about 319 meV once the HfTe2 valence band is included. Experimentally they grow ML-Si2Te2 islands on cleaved HfTe2 by MBE and study them with LT-STM/STS at 5.8 K. They report: a step height of 710 pm; an in-plane lattice constant of 390 pm (DFT 389 pm), from which they infer a "strain-free (1x1)" film; two broad dI/dV peaks about 1.25 eV apart that match DFT after a 0.25 eV rigid shift; a low-conductance region they identify as a gap of about 300 meV (VBM about -10 meV, CBM about 290 meV); and a dI/dV enhancement near EF at island edges, about 2 nm wide and seen as a partial ring in a map taken at +7 mV. From this they claim "the first experimental realization of the QSH phase in ML-Si2Te2".

The materials problem is worth pursuing. The substrate screening is a sensible strategy, and if the claim held it would be of broad interest. As presented, though, the experimental evidence does not support the central claim. The edge-state signature sits in an energy window that does not correspond to the claimed bulk gap. The islands are too small for bulk and edge to be told apart. Basic controls are missing. On a metallic substrate the system is not a QSH insulator in any case. My main concerns follow.

2. Major issues

2.1 The edge feature is not shown to lie inside the bulk gap, and its energy profile does not look like a helical edge state.
The paper places the bulk gap between VBM of about -10 meV and CBM of about +290 meV (Fig. 4a inset). The "edge state" is a narrow peak at EF, roughly within +-10 to 20 mV of zero bias (Fig. 5b, 5c), which is within about 10 meV of the claimed VBM. All edge spectroscopy covers only -100 to +100 mV, which takes in the valence band edge and only the lowest third of the gap. Two further observations in Fig. 5b work against the interpretation:
 (a) Across most of the window from 0 to +100 mV, which by the authors' own assignment is deep inside the gap, the edge spectrum is lower than the center spectrum and falls toward zero. A gapless 1D Dirac edge mode crossing the gap should give a roughly energy-independent, enhanced LDOS across the whole gap, not a suppression.
 (b) At negative bias the edge spectrum rises well above the center spectrum. Together with (a), this looks more like the valence band edge being shifted or bent at the boundary than like an in-gap state.
A sharp peak pinned exactly at EF is also not what one expects from a linearly dispersing helical mode. It is what one expects from a zero-bias anomaly, a Kondo-like or impurity resonance, a Coulomb-blockade or charging effect in a small island, or a flat-band or dangling-bond edge state. Fig. 2b shows the calculated edge-state dispersion. The authors should state where its Dirac point and its gap traversal fall in energy, and compare that with the experiment. What I need to see: spectra and dI/dV maps over the full claimed gap, at for example +50, +100, +150, +200 and +250 mV, and at energies inside the bands as references. These should show an edge enhancement that persists through the gap and disappears or weakens in the bands. Spectra taken with a setpoint outside the gap (for example +0.5 V and -0.5 V) would show whether the result depends on the setpoint.

2.2 Center and edge spectra disagree with the bulk-gap spectrum.
In Fig. 5b the "center" spectrum has a V-shaped dip of about +-50 mV centered on EF, with a minimum at about +-10 mV (Fig. 5c), and a dI/dV that is not small at +100 mV. In Fig. 4a the bulk spectrum is described as a gap from -10 to +290 meV. These two descriptions cannot both apply to the bulk of the same film. A gap whose lower edge is at -10 meV should produce a step-like rise at negative bias, not a dip symmetric about EF. The small peak inside the +-10 mV minimum is attributed to "substrate-induced states" without evidence. The authors need to reconcile the low-bias structure (Fig. 5) with the wide-range gap (Fig. 4) and explain what the symmetric V-shaped dip at EF is. If it is a zero-bias anomaly, the edge "peak" has to be analyzed relative to it, for example through its temperature and setpoint dependence.

2.3 Island size versus edge-state width.
The islands in Figs. 3b, 5a and 5d are only about 6 to 9 nm across. The authors report an edge-state width of about 2 nm, with the peak appearing 2 to 3 nm from the edge. In islands this size there is hardly any "center" that is more than one decay length from every edge. Confinement of bulk states, hybridization of opposite edges (the DFT ribbon decay length is 1.35 nm), and charging effects should all matter. The "bulk" gap measured in such islands is itself suspect. Please (a) state the island-size distribution and the size of every island used for gap and edge spectroscopy; (b) show how the center spectrum and edge feature depend on island size, ideally on islands of 20 nm or more; and (c) address whether the 300 meV "gap" and the zero-bias structure could come from quantum confinement or Coulomb blockade in nanometer-scale islands that are weakly coupled through a vdW gap.

2.4 Missing controls.
The authors are well placed to run the most persuasive control there is. Their own earlier work (Refs. 29, 30) produced strained, topologically trivial ML-Si2Te2 on Sb2Te3. Edge spectroscopy on those islands, under identical tip and acquisition conditions, would show whether the edge feature reported here comes from topology or is a generic property of Si2Te2 edges or step-edge geometry. Equivalent measurements at bare HfTe2 step edges (and at HfTe2 surface defects), and at Si2Te2 island edges with different terminations, are also needed. Without these, the claim that the feature "rules out trivial edge models" is not supported.

2.5 Arguments used to exclude trivial edge states.
The text argues that trivial edge states from defects or dangling bonds are "typically confined to one or two atomic rows", so a 2 to 3 nm extent indicates topology. That is not a general rule. Trivial edge or Tamm states, band bending at boundaries, and image-potential or electrostatic effects at a 7 Å step can all extend over nanometers, especially once a tip a few nanometers wide is convolved with the signal. Spatial extent alone cannot distinguish the cases. Likewise "independent of step geometry" is asserted from Fig. S11, but the main text never identifies the edge terminations (zigzag, armchair, Te- or Si-terminated) at atomic resolution. Please show atomically resolved edges and the spectra measured at each.

2.6 dI/dV map artifacts.
The map in Fig. 5e was taken in constant-current mode (Vstab = 0.2 V). At a 7 Å step the tip-sample distance changes abruptly, and a dI/dV "ring" at island boundaries is a well-known setpoint artifact. The map is also coarsely pixelated, and the ring is visibly incomplete along more than "a small segment" of the boundary. Please provide (a) maps at several energies, inside and outside the gap, taken with the same setpoint; (b) constant-height or normalized maps (for example (dI/dV)/(I/V), or a different setpoint); and (c) the simultaneously recorded topography and current channels. The attribution of the missing segment to "residual tip-related effects" needs evidence.

2.7 "QSH phase" on a metallic substrate.
In the authors' own DFT, the HfTe2 VBM lies 110 meV above the Si2Te2 VBM. The Fermi level is said to sit below the Si2Te2 VBM, and the HfTe2 substrate is metallic or semimetallic. The heterostructure therefore has no global gap. Any edge mode of Si2Te2 is degenerate with substrate continuum states, and backscattering through the substrate is not forbidden. The authors themselves say the band edges are "smeared due to the metallic HfTe2 substrate". In this setting, "realization of the QSH phase" overstates what is shown. At most the data could show that the band inversion survives on HfTe2 and that edge-localized states consistent with it exist. The title, abstract and conclusions should be toned down to match, and the abstract's mention of "room-temperature" QSH should not be read as supported by 5.8 K STM on a metallic substrate. Relatedly, the Z2 calculation from Wannier charge centers needs a gap between the occupied and unoccupied manifolds at every k. Please state precisely which bands were included for the metallic heterostructure, and how the HfTe2 bands that cross EF were handled.

2.8 "Strain-free" versus "(1x1) epitaxial, hollow-site registry".
The manuscript claims both that the Si2Te2 lattice constant (390 pm) equals the free-standing value (389 pm), so the film is strain-free, and that the islands are (1x1) and registered to the HfTe2 substrate in a hollow-site configuration. HfTe2 has an in-plane lattice constant noticeably larger than 389 pm (the screening admits a nonzero mismatch, and HfTe2 is commonly reported near 3.95 to 3.96 A). A truly commensurate (1x1) overlayer would then be strained to the substrate lattice. An unstrained overlayer would be incommensurate and should show a moire pattern with a period of order 20 nm, which the small islands may not be able to resolve. Please:
 (a) report the HfTe2 lattice constant measured in the same image (Fig. 3b shows both lattices) and use it as an internal calibration;
 (b) give uncertainties, since a 1 pm agreement is well below the usual accuracy of STM piezo calibration;
 (c) state the HfTe2 lattice constant used in the DFT supercell. If the heterostructure was relaxed to 389 pm, the ML-HfTe2 was strained by roughly 1.5 to 2%, which changes its bands and work function and so also the claimed 110 meV VBM offset and the doping direction.
A monolayer of HfTe2 strained to the overlayer is also a poor stand-in for the bulk semimetallic substrate. Fig. 4b already mixes "ML-HfTe2" and "bulk HfTe2". The key conclusions should be checked with a thicker HfTe2 slab at the experimental substrate lattice constant.

2.9 Comparison between DFT and the measured spectra.
The two broad peaks agree only after a rigid shift of about 0.25 eV, attributed to doping "not fully captured" by DFT. Yet the gap edges are then compared without any shift (300 meV measured versus 319 meV calculated, plus the placement of EF). If a 0.25 eV shift is needed for the peaks, the same shift should apply to the band edges, and the positions of VBM and CBM relative to EF would then disagree with DFT. In addition, DFT puts EF below the Si2Te2 VBM (p-type), while the measured VBM is at -10 meV, with EF inside the gap. Please treat the energy alignment consistently, state which functional was used for Fig. 4b (HSE06 or PBE), and explain how the band edges were extracted (for example log-scale fits or a threshold criterion). The gap "corroborated by systematic measurements on 20 islands" should be given as a distribution with mean and spread in the main text, not only in the SM.

2.10 Identification and chemistry of the islands.
The film is identified as Si2Te2 only by STM lattice constant and step height. The step height (710 pm) differs from the calculated 790 pm by 10%, which the text calls "reasonable agreement". Apparent heights are bias dependent, and the bias dependence should be shown. Growth needed annealing at 385 C under Si and Te fluxes 20 times the nominal rate, with "significant reduction in coverage above 320 C". This raises the possibility of other phases: elemental Te, SiTe2, Hf-Si-Te alloying, or intercalation. Chemical or diffraction evidence (XPS, LEED or RHEED, Raman, or at least a comparison with the Si2Te2 grown on Sb2Te3 in Refs. 29 and 30) would make the identification secure. The sentence "a significant reduction in coverage is observed above 320 C, well below the crystallization threshold of ML-Si2Te2" is confusing. Please clarify what the crystallization threshold is and how it relates to the 385 C annealing temperature.

3. Minor issues

- The screening funnel is inconsistent. The text says the mismatch criterion yields 76 candidates; Fig. 1(a) says 65 TMDs.
- The DFT gap of free-standing ML-Si2Te2 is given as 220 meV in the introduction, yet the substrate is said to "enhance" it to 429 meV, almost a factor of two. This is a strong substrate effect for a "typical vdW" interaction of -0.296 eV per unit cell, and it deserves explanation (which band moves and why) and a check with a thicker substrate.
- The AIMD runs (10 ps at 300 K, 4x4 supercell) are short, and they are only marginally relevant to a 5.8 K experiment. They could move to the SM.
- Ref. 35 is described as containing "image processing procedures and large area scans showing the coexistence of three stripe orientations". This appears to be left over from another manuscript and should be corrected.
- Ref. 39 (Giustino and Pasquarello, mixed Wannier-Bloch functions) is not the standard citation for computing Z2 from Wannier charge center evolution (for example Soluyanov and Vanderbilt, PRB 83, 235401 (2011); Yu et al., PRB 84, 075119 (2011)). The tool used (for example Z2Pack or WannierTools) should be named. Ref. 40 (Te on graphene) is an odd source for the Si2Te2 interlayer spacing.
- The edge-state width (about 2 nm measured versus 1.35 nm calculated, a 50% difference) is described as "good agreement". The finite tip resolution should be discussed, and the decay length extracted with an explicit fit.
- Please state the lock-in modulation and the temperature broadening relative to the width of the zero-bias peak (Vmod = 2 mV at 5.8 K), and show the peak's dependence on Vmod and setpoint.
- The calculation in Fig. 2(b) should be described in the main text: semi-infinite Green's function from a Wannier Hamiltonian, and for which system (free-standing ribbon or heterostructure).
- Language: "remains challenge", "deriving the system into a trivial semiconducting phase" (driving), "valance band", "doping effect that are". There is also a date mismatch between the header (September 22, 2025) and the arXiv stamp (10 Aug 2025).
- Words such as "confirm", "direct evidence", "rules out" and "strong evidence" are used repeatedly where the data are suggestive at best. Please calibrate the language to the evidence.

4. Assessment and recommendation

The DFT screening and the growth of a well-ordered Si2Te2 monolayer on HfTe2 are a useful materials result. The topological claim, however, rests on a small zero-bias peak at the edges of 6 to 9 nm islands on a metallic substrate. That peak sits at the bottom edge of the claimed gap and is not followed across the gap. Its energy dependence argues against a helical Dirac mode, and no trivial-edge control is shown. Taken together with the internal inconsistencies (strain-free versus commensurate, the alignment between DFT and STS, the center spectra in Figs. 4 and 5), the evidence does not yet support "experimental realization of the QSH phase". I cannot recommend publication in its current form. A substantially revised version could be reconsidered if it includes: energy-resolved edge spectroscopy and maps across the full gap; measurements on larger islands; control measurements on trivial Si2Te2/Sb2Te3 edges and bare HfTe2 steps; a consistent structural and DFT model with a realistic substrate; and claims rescoped to what STM on a metallic substrate can establish.

Recommendation: major revision (reject in current form). If the requested measurements cannot be supplied, the manuscript would suit a more specialized venue as a growth-and-electronic-structure study, without the QSH claim.
