Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Scientific context.** Monolayer Si₂Te₂ has been predicted to be a large-gap (~220 meV, free-standing) quantum spin Hall (QSH) insulator. It has no bulk van der Waals parent, so it can only be made by epitaxy. The authors' earlier growth on Sb₂Te₃ produced a strained, topologically trivial semiconductor. Realizing the nontrivial phase on a better-matched substrate is a meaningful goal for the field, which still has few large-gap QSH systems.
- **Claimed advances.**
  - A database-driven ("material-by-design") route to choosing a substrate: C2DB screening for symmetry, lattice mismatch, prior synthesis, phase stability and ease of exfoliation, which selects HfTe₂.
  - MBE growth of (1×1) ML-Si₂Te₂ islands that the authors describe as strain-free.
  - STS evidence of a ~300 meV bulk gap.
  - In-gap edge states that extend ~2–3 nm into the island and appear as a continuous ring in dI/dV maps.
- **Assessment of novelty.**
  - The idea of tuning Si₂Te₂ topology by choosing the substrate is natural and somewhat incremental relative to the authors' own Sb₂Te₃ work.
  - If the topological identification is solid, it would be the first experimental nontrivial Si₂Te₂ and a new member of the small family of large-gap 2D topological insulators, which would be significant.
  - The impact therefore depends almost entirely on how convincingly the evidence supports the "QSH phase" claim. In its present form, that evidence is mostly circumstantial: DFT plus an STS edge peak.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Coherent strategy combining theory and experiment.** High-throughput screening, DFT and AIMD stability checks, HSE06+SOC band structures, Z₂ calculations, MBE growth and low-temperature STM/STS are integrated into a clear narrative.
- **High-quality STM growth data.**
  - Atomically resolved (1×1) lattice with a measured a = 390 pm, close to the DFT value of 389 pm.
  - A reproducible epitaxial relationship (hollow-site registry) across samples.
  - A well-documented, non-trivial growth recipe (flux-compensated annealing) that others could reproduce.
- **Spectroscopic consistency with DFT.**
  - The two main dI/dV peaks (~1.25 eV apart) are reproduced by DFT, albeit with a rigid shift.
  - The measured gap (~300 meV) agrees with the predicted 319 meV.
  - The gap is reportedly checked on 20 islands.
- **Edge-state phenomenology resembles accepted QSH signatures.**
  - Enhanced in-gap dI/dV at island edges.
  - A spatial extent of a few nm rather than one or two atomic rows.
  - Continuity around the island perimeter in the dI/dV map.
  - Apparent independence of edge geometry.
- **Potential community interest.** A room-temperature-scale QSH candidate on a cleavable vdW substrate would be attractive for future spin-resolved, ARPES and device studies.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**Reason 1: The edge-state evidence is insufficient to establish topological (helical) edge states, and the reported phenomenology is partly at odds with QSH expectations.**
- **Energy window inconsistent with a gap-spanning helical mode.**
  - The bulk gap is claimed to run from VBM ≈ −10 meV to CBM ≈ +290 meV.
  - Yet the edge feature is a narrow peak at E_F, essentially at the VBM, confined to roughly ±50 mV. A helical 1D Dirac mode traversing the whole gap should raise the edge LDOS over most of the 300 meV window.
  - For a linearly dispersing 1D mode, the DOS is nearly energy-independent. A sharp peak therefore needs explanation; deferring this to the Supplement ("expected to manifest as a dI/dV peak") is not sufficient.
  - A peak pinned right at the valence-band edge is equally consistent with trivial mechanisms: band bending or a shifted VBM at the island boundary, edge-localized valence-band states, or Si/Te dangling-bond states. The authors' own Ref. 30 documents strong interfacial band bending in Si₂Te₂ lateral heterostructures.
- **Only one map energy and no out-of-gap reference.**
  - Only a single dI/dV map is shown (+7 mV, Fig. 5e).
  - There are no maps at several energies inside the gap (e.g., 50, 100, 200 meV) and none inside the bulk bands, where edge contrast should vanish or invert.
  - Without these, the claim that the ring of enhanced intensity reflects an in-gap topological mode cannot be distinguished from topographic or setpoint-induced contrast at a 7 Å step.
- **Measurement artifacts not excluded.**
  - All spectra and the map are taken in constant-current stabilization (V_stab = −0.1 V or +0.2 V). Near an edge, the integrated LDOS and tip–sample geometry change abruptly, so the setpoint effect can create apparent edge peaks.
  - No setpoint-dependence tests, constant-height spectroscopy, or normalized (dI/dV)/(I/V) analysis are given.
  - The acknowledged "signal degradation … likely due to residual tip-related effects" on part of the edge further underlines the sensitivity to tip artifacts.
- **Spatial extent used ambiguously and over-interpreted.**
  - The extent is quoted as "2.0 to 3.0 nm" in one place and "≈2 nm" in another, and is compared with a DFT value of 1.35 nm as "good agreement." This is a factor of 1.5–2.
  - Tip convolution at a 7 Å step can easily broaden a signal by ~1 nm.
  - An extended decay alone does not prove topology. Trivial edge states and band-bending regions can also extend over several nm.
- **Inconsistent and unexplained bulk-interior spectra.**
  - The bulk-interior dip is described as "V-shaped between ±50 mV" in one paragraph and as a minimum "between ±10 mV" in another.
  - A small central peak is attributed to "substrate-induced states" without evidence.
  - A V-shaped zero-bias suppression at E_F in an island on a metal could be a zero-bias anomaly (Coulomb/dynamical Coulomb blockade or tunneling into a disordered system). It is not obviously a band-structure feature. The edge "peak" may partly be the absence of this suppression rather than an added state.

**Reason 2: The structural claims ("strain-free (1×1)", commensurate hollow-site registry) are internally inconsistent and insufficiently characterized.**
- **Strain-free and commensurate cannot both hold.**
  - Bulk HfTe₂ has a ≈ 395–396 pm, which is ~1.5% larger than 389–390 pm.
  - If ML-Si₂Te₂ is truly strain-free, it cannot be commensurate (1×1) with HfTe₂ in a uniform hollow-site registry. A moiré pattern with a period of order 25 nm should then appear on larger islands.
  - If it is commensurate, it must be tensile-strained by ~1.5%. That would push it toward the edge of the stated topological window (−3% to +2%).
  - The manuscript asserts both a strain-free state and a well-defined (1×1) hollow-site epitaxy, without reconciling them or reporting the measured HfTe₂ lattice constant.
- **DFT model lattice choice not stated.**
  - The heterostructure model gives a = 389 pm, "identical to free-standing." This implies that either HfTe₂ was compressed in the supercell or the cell was relaxed jointly.
  - The lattice parameter actually used for HfTe₂, and the strain it implies for each layer, must be stated. Otherwise the "strain-free" conclusion is partly an artifact of the computational setup.
- **Lattice-constant precision not established.**
  - A value of 390 pm from a ~2 nm line profile with typical piezo calibration uncertainties of 1–3% cannot distinguish 389 from 396 pm.
  - A simultaneous FFT comparison of substrate and overlayer in one image (Fig. 3b shows both) is needed, with stated error bars.
- **Height and chemical identification are weak.**
  - The measured step height (710 pm) differs from the calculated interlayer spacing (790 pm) by 0.8 Å. This is called "reasonable agreement" without discussing bias-dependent apparent heights.
  - No chemical or structural probe is provided (XPS, Raman, LEED/RHEED, or cross-sectional TEM).
  - Under 20× excess Si/Te flux at 385 °C, which is above the 320 °C onset of decomposition, Te adlayers, SiTeₓ phases, Hf–Si–Te interdiffusion or Te-rich edge reconstructions are plausible and should be excluded.

**Reason 3: The theoretical support for topology in the actual heterostructure is incomplete, and several DFT-based arguments are inconsistent.**
- **Heterostructure is metallic, so the Z₂ claim needs justification.**
  - ML-HfTe₂ is (semi)metallic, and its VBM lies 110 meV above the Si₂Te₂ VBM. The combined system is therefore not a global insulator.
  - The authors must explain how a Z₂ invariant from Wannier charge centers is defined for the heterostructure, for instance using a curved chemical potential or Si₂Te₂-projected bands with a direct gap throughout the BZ.
  - It is also not demonstrated that the helical edge modes survive hybridization with a metallic substrate continuum at the same energies. This is the central physical question for a QSH system on a metal.
- **Band inversion asserted, not shown.**
  - SOC-induced band inversion at Γ is claimed but not shown explicitly, e.g., orbital characters without and with SOC, or Si-s/Te-p ordering versus strain.
  - Given the strain sensitivity, the authors should show the topological phase diagram versus biaxial strain for the actual heterostructure and indicate where the experimental lattice sits.
- **Gap-mapping logic and model geometry are unclear.**
  - The "expected experimental gap ≈319 meV" is derived by subtracting the HfTe₂ VBM offset. Yet STS on Si₂Te₂ measures the local Si₂Te₂-dominated LDOS, so the argument for which gap STS should see needs to be made rigorously.
  - The ~0.25 eV rigid shift between STS and DFT is attributed to "substrate-induced doping not captured in DFT," even though the substrate is explicitly included. This contradicts the use of the same model elsewhere, e.g., to place E_F below the VBM.
  - A monolayer HfTe₂ slab is not representative of a bulk cleaved crystal: band alignment, work function and charge transfer can all differ.
- **Edge calculation insufficiently specified.**
  - The edge spectral function (Fig. 2b) does not state whether it is for a free-standing ribbon or a semi-infinite heterostructure.
  - The edge termination is not given: zigzag versus armchair, Si- or Te-terminated, passivated or not.
  - The energy of the edge Dirac point relative to E_F and to the VBM is not stated, so the predicted edge-state energy distribution cannot be compared directly with the narrow STS peak.
  - The 1.35 nm decay length is quoted for a free-standing nanoribbon, not the supported system.

**Reason 4: The central claim ("experimental realization of the QSH phase") is overstated relative to the evidence, and the manuscript has numerous inconsistencies.**
- **Claim exceeds the evidence.**
  - No momentum-resolved (ARPES), spin-resolved, magnetic-field-dependent, quasiparticle-interference or transport evidence is provided.
  - Topology is inferred from DFT plus an STS edge feature. That combination has repeatedly proven ambiguous in other 2D systems (e.g., debates on Bi bilayers and stanene edges).
  - Because the substrate is metallic, a QSH effect in the transport sense cannot be realized in this geometry at all. The title and abstract should be tempered to "evidence for topological edge states" or similar.
- **Missing decisive controls.**
  - The authors possess trivial ML-Si₂Te₂ on Sb₂Te₃. STS across its edges under identical conditions would directly test whether the edge peak is topology-specific.
  - Edges of bare HfTe₂ steps should also be measured, to exclude substrate-step contributions.
  - Statistics across many islands, edge orientations and terminations are referenced only qualitatively ("Fig. S11").
- **Numerical and textual inconsistencies.**
  - The text gives 76 candidates after the mismatch filter, while Fig. 1a shows 65.
  - The Supplemental Material description in Ref. 35 ("image processing procedures and … three stripe orientations") appears copied from an unrelated paper.
  - Ref. 39 (mixed Wannier–Bloch functions for electron–phonon coupling) is not the appropriate citation for Wannier-charge-center Z₂ evaluation; Soluyanov–Vanderbilt and Yu et al. would be appropriate.
  - Typographical errors include "deriving the system," "remains challenge" and "valance."
  - The two descriptions of the interior dI/dV minimum (±50 mV vs. ±10 mV) conflict.
- **Thermal stability tested at the wrong temperature.**
  - AIMD is performed at 300 K for 10 ps, whereas growth and annealing occur at 385 °C (~658 K), where decomposition is observed experimentally.
  - Stability at the growth temperature, or a discussion of kinetic stabilization, is missing.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Substantially strengthen and de-risk the edge-state identification.**
   - Provide dI/dV maps at multiple energies spanning the full gap (−10 to +290 meV) and within the bulk bands.
   - Repeat spectra and maps at several setpoints, include constant-height data, and use normalized conductance.
   - Show quantitative decay profiles with fits, compared against tip-convolved DFT LDOS profiles.
   - Analyze statistics versus edge orientation and termination.
   - Perform control measurements on edges of trivial Si₂Te₂/Sb₂Te₃ and bare HfTe₂ step edges.
   - If possible, add:
     - edge QPI, showing suppressed backscattering compared with trivial edges;
     - magnetic-field dependence;
     - spin-polarized STM;
     - a demonstration that the edge state persists along edges containing defects or kinks.
   - Explicitly address the alternatives: band bending, dangling bonds, and zero-bias anomalies.

2. **Resolve the strain and registry question and confirm the chemical identity.**
   - Report HfTe₂ and Si₂Te₂ lattice constants from the same image (FFT, with error bars).
   - Search for and discuss any moiré pattern.
   - State explicitly the lattice constants used in the DFT supercell and the strain they imply in each layer.
   - Recompute the band topology at the experimentally determined Si₂Te₂ lattice parameter.
   - Add complementary characterization (LEED/RHEED, XPS, Raman, or ARPES) to confirm phase purity, rule out Te adlayers or alloying, and ideally to image the Si₂Te₂ bands and inversion directly.

3. **Make the theory directly comparable with the experiment.**
   - Use a multilayer or semi-infinite HfTe₂ substrate.
   - Show the band inversion with and without SOC, and the strain-dependent phase diagram.
   - Justify how the Z₂ invariant is defined in the metallic heterostructure.
   - Compute the edge spectral function for the supported system with realistic, specified terminations. Report the energy of the edge Dirac point relative to E_F and the VBM, and whether the edge modes hybridize with HfTe₂ states.
   - Explain why the STS edge signal is a narrow peak at E_F rather than an enhancement across the whole gap.
   - Reconcile the 0.25 eV rigid shift with the substrate-included model.
   - Extend the AIMD to the growth temperature.

4. **Calibrate claims and improve presentation and rigor.**
   - Temper the title and abstract to reflect STM-based evidence rather than a demonstrated "QSH phase."
   - Explicitly discuss that the metallic substrate precludes transport-based QSH, and outline a route forward (e.g., transfer, intercalation, or an insulating buffer).
   - Present the gap determination transparently: log-scale dI/dV, the criteria used for band-edge identification, and the statistical distribution with uncertainty over the 20 islands.
   - Correct the inconsistencies: 65 vs. 76 candidates, ±10 vs. ±50 mV, the Ref. 35 description, Ref. 39, and the typographical errors.
   - Move key supplementary evidence (the gap statistics in Fig. S9 and the multi-island edge data in Fig. S11) into the main text or a more prominent figure.
