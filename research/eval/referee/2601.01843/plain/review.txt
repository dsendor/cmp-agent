Referee report

Manuscript: "Multiple nodal superconducting phases and order-parameter evolution in pressurized UTe2" (arXiv:2601.01843v1)

1. Summary of the submission

The authors report soft (Au-epoxy) point-contact spectroscopy (PCS) on the (001) surface of molten-salt-flux-grown UTe2 (RRR about 410) at ambient pressure (three samples, S1-S3) and under hydrostatic pressure up to 1.4 GPa (one junction, on S1). At ambient pressure they see a zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP) of modest height (G/GN below 1.2). Above about 0.3 GPa the ZBCP grows (up to about 2.1), and side dips appear. At 1.0 to 1.2 GPa an additional dip-peak structure appears. At 1.4 GPa the spectrum becomes a broad, flat-topped peak with deep side dips. The authors simulate the spectra with a spin-triplet extension of the BTK model, using linear-in-k d-vectors for the four odd-parity D2h representations and a spherical Fermi surface. They argue that Au and B1u are disfavoured and that B2u or B3u (which this geometry cannot tell apart) describe all the data. Fits give a ratio Delta_z/Delta_x(y) of about 0.57 at ambient pressure, rising to about 1.0 to 1.3 in the SC3 region. The ratio has a minimum as a function of temperature that the authors place on the literature SC2-SC3 boundary. From this they conclude that SC1, SC2 and SC3 are distinct nodal phases, that the pz weight grows strongly with pressure, and (together with published NMR) that SC2 is B2u and SC3 is B3u.

The question matters. Symmetry-sensitive probes of the pressure-induced phases of UTe2 are scarce, and PCS under pressure in a clean, well-characterised crystal is technically demanding. The raw data in Fig. 3 and Fig. S3 show a clear, systematic evolution with pressure that deserves to be reported. My concern is with how much the analysis can carry. The headline claims are the nodal character, the IR assignment, the "single parameter" separating SC1 to SC3, and the B2u to B3u transition. They rest on one under-pressure junction and on a highly parameterised, simplified model. The paper also lacks several standard PCS controls. In its present form I do not think the conclusions follow from the evidence. I recommend major revision, detailed below.

2. Major issues

2.1 The junction regime is not established, and the side dips have a well-known extrinsic explanation.
The manuscript gives no normal-state junction resistance R_N for any contact or pressure. It gives no estimate of contact size (Sharvin/Wexler) and no comparison with the electronic mean free path, which should be long in RRR 410 crystals. It also gives no discussion of whether the contact is ballistic, diffusive or thermal. This matters most for the central spectroscopic feature. Side dips flanking a conductance peak are the textbook signature of the contact region reaching its critical current, as in Sheet, Mukhopadhyay and Raychaudhuri (ref. 42), which the authors themselves cite. The manuscript instead treats the appearance of the side dips as evidence that the ZBCP is intrinsic (p. 5, "the appearance of these side dips suggests that these ZBCPs are intrinsic"). That reasoning is circular unless the critical-current origin is excluded. The deep, sharp dips at about +/-2 mV at 1.4 GPa (Fig. 3d) on a flat-topped broad peak are particularly reminiscent of critical-current behaviour. The heating test on p. 4 is applied only to the ambient-pressure ZBCP, uses a nonstandard criterion ("amplitude nearly temperature independent"), and is not applied to the pressurised junction where the dips appear.
Requested: R_N for every spectrum, and contact-size and mean-free-path estimates. Also requested: the dip positions as functions of T and H compared with the expected I_c(T, H), and the dip positions expressed as current (V_dip/R_N) and compared across pressures. A discussion of the spreading or series resistance of the sample near the contact is also needed (e.g., Chen, Tesanovic and Chien, PRB 2010 and related work), because this both rescales the voltage axis and creates dips.

2.2 All pressure-dependent conclusions rest on a single junction whose parameters change with pressure.
Only sample S1 was measured under pressure, and apparently with only one contact. The fitted junction parameters change considerably across exactly the pressure range where the spectral change is attributed to the order parameter. Z goes from 1.67 to about 1.2 and Gamma from 0.39 meV to about 0.10 meV between ambient and 0.8 GPa (Fig. S7 vs Fig. S8). A fourfold reduction of the Dynes broadening under pressure is not expected for an intrinsic property, and it suggests the junction itself (an epoxy contact, compressed in oil) evolved. The ambient-pressure spectra of S1, S2 and S3 already differ among themselves by about as much as some pressure-induced changes do. S3 shows satellite structure at about +/-0.8 mV at p = 0 (Fig. 2f), which illustrates how sensitive the line shape is to the junction. The fitted ratio Delta_z/Delta_x is strongly coupled to Z and Gamma in this model (compare Figs. 4, S5 and S6). So a change in the junction can masquerade as a change in Delta_z/Delta_x.
Requested: Z and Gamma for every pressure and temperature, R_N versus pressure, and at least one more independent junction measured under pressure. A depressurisation check would also help: return to ambient or 0.2 GPa after the high-pressure runs and show that the original spectrum, and the ratio near 0.6, are recovered. Without such controls the pressure evolution cannot be attributed unambiguously to the order parameter.

2.3 The fitted gap magnitudes are implausibly large and inconsistent with the authors' own simulations.
At ambient pressure the fits give gaps of 1.18 and 0.72 meV for Tc0 = 2.1 K (kB Tc = 0.18 meV). That is 2Delta/kBTc of about 13 for the larger component and about 8 for the smaller. The weak-coupling BCS curve drawn in Fig. S2c has Delta(0) of about 0.3 meV, roughly four times smaller than the fitted values. Under pressure the fits give about 1.1 meV at 0.8 GPa and about 1.6 meV at 1.2 GPa (Fig. S9c), for Tc of about 3 K, i.e. 2Delta/kBTc of about 8 to 12. The model simulations in Fig. S6 use Delta = 0.25 meV, a value much closer to the energy scale expected for UTe2, but the fits need about 1 meV. The manuscript presents the departure from BCS behaviour in Fig. S2c as evidence of unconventional superconductivity. A factor-of-four excess in gap magnitude, however, is more naturally read as a sign that the voltage scale is not set by the gap alone: series or spreading resistance, heating, or a non-ballistic contact (see 2.1). The authors should address this directly. If they believe the values are intrinsic, they should reconcile them with thermodynamic data (specific-heat jump, condensation energy), with STM gap values, and with the gap scale used in their own simulations.

2.4 Exclusion of Au, and hence the "nodal" conclusion, is not established.
The Au case is simulated only for Delta_x = Delta_y = Delta_z = 0.25 meV with Gamma = 0.01 meV (Fig. S6a). There the peak splits at finite Z. The experimental fits, however, require Gamma up to 0.39 meV (Gamma/Delta of about 0.3), which would wash out a small splitting of that kind. Moreover, the Delta_z kz z-hat component of Au also changes sign under kz -> -kz. For an anisotropic Au state with Delta_z larger than Delta_x and Delta_y, which is precisely the regime the B2u/B3u fits suggest, near-normal-incidence quasiparticles see a sign change with only a small non-sign-changing admixture. Low-energy surface states and a single ZBCP are then expected. The authors acknowledge this in remark (1) ("Au representation may blur into a quasi-Bnu representation, while PCS lacks the necessary resolution to tell them apart"). That admission undercuts the claims of a "nodal" character, made in the title and in the summary, and of B2u/B3u as "the most likely representation". Independent bulk evidence for a full gap at ambient pressure (ref. 48) makes this more pressing. Requested: fits with a general anisotropic Au state (independent Delta_x, Delta_y, Delta_z) using the same Gamma freedom as the B2u fits, with a quantitative goodness-of-fit comparison (chi^2, information criterion) between Au, B1u and B2u/B3u for each pressure. If Au cannot be excluded, the title and abstract should be revised.

2.5 The model is too idealised for the specific symmetry argument being made.
(a) Fermi surface. The calculation assumes a single spherical Fermi surface (kz^2 + k_par^2 = kF^2) and linear-in-k basis functions. The Fermi surface of UTe2 is predominantly quasi-2D cylinders along c, as ref. 39 (whose first author is a co-author here) shows. For c-axis injection into a quasi-2D Fermi surface, the weight of states with large kz velocity is small, and a "pz" component of the form Delta_z kz is not the natural lowest harmonic. Both the ABS formation argument (Fig. 1) and the meaning of Delta_z/Delta_x(y) depend on this geometry. The authors should at least discuss how a cylindrical or realistic Fermi surface changes the distinction between pz-dominant and px(y)-dominant spectra, and ideally show a simulation for it.
(b) Nature of the surface states. For B2u with d = (Delta_z kz, 0, Delta_x kx), only one component changes sign on reflection. The resulting surface Andreev states disperse with k_par: they are zero-energy only on a line or point of the surface Brillouin zone, not a flat zero-energy band as in d-wave (110) junctions. The terminology "ZABS" and the yes/no column of Table I should be justified by showing the surface-state dispersion or the surface density of states for each IR. The column labelled "Spin component" in Table I appears to list the spin axis absent from d. Please define it.
(c) Heavy-fermion PCS. Small Andreev enhancements (about 10 to 20%) are generic in heavy-fermion point contacts and are usually attributed to the heavy-electron and Kondo-lattice physics of the junction rather than to pairing symmetry. The inference on p. 7 that a peak below 1.2 implies "the px(y) pairing component plays a more dominant role than the pz pairing" therefore needs justification beyond the simple BTK model. Fermi-velocity mismatch between Au and UTe2 should also be discussed.
(d) Unitarity. The formalism assumes unitary d (d x d* = 0). This excludes the non-unitary and multicomponent states widely discussed for UTe2 (refs. 16, 24, 30, 31), exactly the kind of state expected in a low-temperature phase that appears through a second transition.

2.6 The "single parameter" and the SC2-SC3 transition claims are over-interpreted.
(a) For p >= 0.6 GPa all 0.3 K data lie in SC3 by the authors' own phase diagram. The inset of Fig. 5a therefore compares SC1 with SC3 only, and SC2 is never characterised by a distinct value of the ratio. In SC2 the ratio rises again toward or above its SC3 values (Figs. 5f, S9e, S9f). The statement that the phases are "distinguished by a single parameter" is therefore not supported for SC2.
(b) The temperature minimum arises from a modest decrease (about 15 to 25%) at low T, combined with a rise near Tc. Near Tc both gaps go to zero, and the ratio of two vanishing quantities has large and growing error bars (Fig. S9e at 2.5 K). The quoted uncertainties (e.g., 0.87(1) meV) are clearly statistical, conditional on the other parameters. They do not reflect parameter correlations. In Fig. S8, Delta_x(y) increases from 0.87 to 1.00 meV between 0.5 and 1.1 K, i.e. a gap that grows with temperature. This points to parameter trade-offs rather than to physics. Please state whether Z and Gamma were fixed in the temperature series (their values barely change in Figs. S7 and S8). Please also give correlation matrices or chi^2 maps, and show that the minimum survives when Z and Gamma are allowed to vary freely or are fixed at different plausible values.
(c) The minima are compared with SC2-SC3 boundaries taken from other groups' samples (refs. 27, 30), which have different Tc and pressure calibrations. Resistivity cannot detect this boundary in the authors' own sample. A bulk thermodynamic or susceptibility measurement on S1, or at least a careful discussion of the pressure calibration offset, is needed before claiming that they "match well".
(d) The two-channel superposition (Eqs. 2 and 3) at 1.0 to 1.2 GPa adds at least four more parameters. It is motivated by either multiple gaps or pressure inhomogeneity, two physically different scenarios that the analysis does not distinguish. The weighted ratio <Delta_z>/<Delta_x(y)> then has no clear physical meaning, and it is this value (about 1.3) that anchors the high-pressure end of the inset in Fig. 5a. Its uncertainty should reflect the non-uniqueness of the decomposition.
(e) The PCS model gives identical spectra for B2u and B3u, so the proposed B2u (SC2) to B3u (SC3) assignment comes entirely from the NMR of ref. 31. If the IR changes at the boundary, "Delta_x(y)" refers to Delta_x on one side and Delta_y on the other, and a continuous ratio across the boundary is not a meaningful order parameter for the transition. Moreover, a transition between two different single-IR states along a second-order line is not generic. The lower-temperature phase would more naturally be a mixed state (e.g., B2u + iB3u or similar), which the unitary single-IR model cannot describe. The abstract and summary should make clear that the IR assignment under pressure is a conjecture based on other measurements.

2.7 Alternative origins of the ZBCP are dismissed too quickly.
The magnetic-scattering explanation is dismissed because UTe2 does not order at ambient pressure. A polished uranium-telluride surface, however, can host local moments regardless of bulk order. Above 1.4 GPa the bulk orders magnetically, and the 1.4 GPa spectrum changes qualitatively. Better arguments are available and should be shown explicitly: onset of the ZBCP at Tc; complete suppression at Hc2 with recovery of a flat normal-state spectrum; scaling of the width with gap rather than with T or H as for Kondo or Appelbaum anomalies. Fig. S2a goes to 8 T, but the text (p. 4) discusses only fields below 2 T. Please show the field dependence up to the suppression of superconductivity, with the field direction relative to Hc2. Also, at ambient pressure the ZBCP appears at 2.5 K (Fig. 2c), while the fitted gaps essentially vanish by about 2 K (Fig. S2c). Please comment.

2.8 Sample and pressure characterisation.
(a) The resistive transition of S1 at ambient pressure is broad and appears two-step (onset 2.6 K, zero 2.1 K; Fig. S1a inset). That is unusual for an RRR 410 molten-salt-flux crystal, where transitions are typically sharp. It raises the question of inhomogeneity near the contact. Please comment, and give specific-heat or susceptibility Tc for the same crystal if available.
(b) The pressure determination (manometer, low-temperature calibration) is not described, nor is the pressure inhomogeneity. Inhomogeneity is invoked as one explanation for the double dips, so it should be quantified.
(c) The cured Au epoxy on the sample surface will contract differently from UTe2 under pressure and on cooling. This can impose local non-hydrostatic stress at exactly the probed spot, and UTe2 is known to be very sensitive to uniaxial stress. This should be discussed as a possible origin of junction-specific phase behaviour.
(d) The surface is "fine-polished" before contact. Polishing damage and possible Te loss or oxidation at the surface are known issues for UTe2. Some characterisation of the surface, or evidence that the results do not depend on surface preparation, would strengthen the work.

3. Minor issues

(1) Table S1 contradicts Fig. S7 and the main text. Table S1 lists Delta_z = 1.18, 1.15, 1.15 meV and Delta_x(y) = 0.72, 0.62, 0.60 meV. Fig. S7a gives Delta_x(y) = 1.18(7) and Delta_z = 0.72(4) meV for the same spectrum, and the main text quotes Delta_z/Delta_x(y) of about 0.57, i.e. Delta_z < Delta_x(y). The column headers of Table S1 appear to be swapped. Since this ratio is the central quantity of the paper, please correct it and check all other reported values.
(2) The main text quotes the minima at 0.6, 0.8 and 1.2 GPa (1.6, 1.5 and 0.7 K), but Fig. 5a appears to show four red circles. Please make these consistent and list all values.
(3) The 1.4 GPa data are described but not fitted, and the ratio is not reported there. Since this pressure borders the MO phases, please state why, and say what was observed at higher pressure (the cell reaches about 1.8 GPa).
(4) Wording such as "unambiguously suggesting the presence of pz component" (p. 3) is inconsistent with "strongly suggests" in the abstract and with the caveats in remark (1). Please tone it down throughout.
(5) Eq. (1): the derivative should be with respect to eV (or carry the appropriate sign and factor), and G0 should be defined explicitly as the normalisation that makes G_NS = 1 in the normal state (with Delta = 0, Eq. S12 gives G0 times the sum over k_par of (1 - |r_n|^2), not G0).
(6) Fig. 1b-d: the role of the internodal vector q_N versus the reflection kz -> -kz is confusing. The ABS condition depends on the sign change between incoming and reflected wavevectors, not on q_N as such. Please clarify, and state which IR is drawn.
(7) Fig. S6 caption ("a zero-bias dip appears for all Z > 0") and SI text ("the peak gradually splits into two peaks") describe the Au result differently. On p. 9 "Fig. S5(a)" should read S5(b).
(8) Ref. 32: please check that this reference (titled for the a-axis Knight shift) supports the statement that the Knight shift decreases for fields along all principal axes. Please also discuss the relation of the present ambient-pressure results to ref. 47 in more detail: same surface, a different conclusion on the dominant component?
(9) Typos and references: "Pep. Rrog. Phys." (ref. 1); ref. 24 year "(2002)" should be 2020; "Supplementray"; "spits" for "splits"; "presentation" for "representation" (SI p. 10); "differentiate conductance" (Fig. 1 caption); "constraints on their physical properties" (p. 1); "seeing Fig." for "see Fig." in several places.
(10) Data availability: "all source data ... are available within the paper" is not sufficient for a fitting-heavy analysis. Please deposit the raw dI/dV(V, T, H, p) data and the BTK fitting code.

4. Assessment of the conclusions

What the data support: a reproducible ZBCP on the (001) surface at ambient pressure in three samples, and a clear, systematic change of the point-contact spectra with pressure in one junction. That change correlates with the known SC1/SC2/SC3 phase diagram. That is a useful observation.

What is not yet supported:
(i) the nodal character, which is model-dependent, with Au not excluded;
(ii) B2u/B3u as the IR, which depends on a spherical Fermi surface, a unitary single-IR model and a restricted Au comparison;
(iii) the claim that <Delta_z>/<Delta_x(y)> distinguishes SC1 to SC3 as a single parameter;
(iv) the SC2-SC3 transition being a change from B2u to B3u.
Controls that would separate order-parameter effects from junction effects are also missing: R_N, contact regime, critical-current analysis, a second junction under pressure, and reversibility. Without them, the pressure evolution cannot be attributed unambiguously to the order parameter.

5. Recommendation

Major revision. The experiment is worthwhile and the data are potentially important. Before the symmetry conclusions can be accepted, the authors need to: (a) establish the junction regime and exclude critical-current and spreading-resistance origins of the side dips; (b) demonstrate reproducibility and reversibility under pressure with independent junctions; (c) reconcile the gap magnitudes; (d) perform a fair, quantitative model comparison that includes anisotropic Au; and (e) temper the title, abstract and summary to match what the evidence supports.
