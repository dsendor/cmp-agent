**Review outline: "Multiple nodal superconducting phases and order-parameter evolution in pressurized UTe2" (Zou et al.)**

---

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **The question is important.** The superconducting order parameter of UTe2, and especially of its pressure-induced phases SC1–SC3, is a central open problem in spin-triplet superconductivity.
  - Symmetry-sensitive probes under pressure are scarce. Before this work, the main evidence came from NMR Knight shift (Kinjo et al.), plus thermodynamic and AC susceptibility phase boundaries.
  - A spectroscopic probe that tracks the order parameter across the pressure phase diagram would be a valuable addition.
- **The experiment is novel.** To my knowledge, this is the first point-contact spectroscopy (PCS) study of UTe2 under hydrostatic pressure (0–1.4 GPa).
  - It uses ultraclean molten-salt-flux crystals (RRR ≈ 410).
  - Data span temperature, pressure and (at ambient pressure) magnetic field.
- **The conceptual claims are significant, if correct.**
  - (i) The zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP) on the (001) surface signals a sign-changing pz component, favouring B2u/B3u over Au/B1u.
  - (ii) A single scalar, ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩, distinguishes SC1, SC2 and SC3.
  - (iii) The non-monotonic temperature dependence of this ratio marks the SC2–SC3 boundary, possibly a B2u → B3u change.
- **The incremental relation to prior work needs sharper positioning.**
  - Ambient-pressure PCS on (001) has already been reported (Yoon et al., npj QM 2024).
  - A B3u assignment at ambient pressure has been made by STM (Gu et al., Science 2025).
  - The novelty therefore rests mainly on the pressure evolution. That part of the analysis is the most model-dependent.

---

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Unique and difficult data set.**
  - Spectra are reproducible in temperature on a single junction across five or more pressures.
  - The spectral evolution is qualitatively striking: a simple ZBCP (SC1), then a ZBCP with side dips (0.6–0.8 GPa), then double dips (1.0–1.2 GPa), then a flat-topped broad peak (1.4 GPa).
  - This evolution correlates with the known phase boundaries.
- **Sample quality and basic characterisation are good.**
  - Resistivity on the same crystal reproduces the published p–T phase diagram, including Tc(p), the MO1/MO2 anomalies and the critical pressures.
  - This lends credibility to the pressure calibration and to the sample's intrinsic behaviour.
- **The theoretical framework is transparent.**
  - The SI derives the extended BTK formalism for unitary triplet states.
  - Simulations systematically compare Au, B1u and B2u/B3u, and pure px(y) versus pz.
  - They give clear qualitative discriminants: a split peak for Au, no side dips for B1u, a robust ZBCP plus side dips for B2u/B3u.
- **Some consistency checks are encouraging.**
  - Three ambient-pressure samples, with different Z and Γ, give a similar gap ratio (~0.55).
  - The temperature-defined ratio minima at 0.6, 0.8 and 1.2 GPa fall near the literature SC2/SC3 boundary.
- **The paper could stimulate further work.**
  - It offers a concrete, falsifiable hypothesis (SC2 = B2u, SC3 = B3u).
  - That hypothesis can be tested by PCS on other facets, by STM, or by NMR.

---

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**3.1 The junction regime is not established, and extrinsic origins of the spectral features are not excluded.**
- **Unexplained gap magnitudes.** The fitted gaps (Δx(y) ≈ 1.2 meV at ambient pressure with Tc ≈ 2.1 K; Δ ≈ 1.0–1.1 meV at 0.8 GPa) imply 2Δ/kBTc ≈ 10–13.
  - This is far above weak-coupling values for nodal p-wave states.
  - It is also several times larger than STM gap scales reported for UTe2 (~0.25 meV).
  - Fig. S2(c) shows that the "BCS violation" is mostly a magnitude discrepancy: the plotted BCS curve has Δ(0) ≈ 0.33 meV.
  - Inflated gaps are a classic symptom of non-ballistic (diffusive or thermal) contacts or series/spreading resistance. In that case, energy scales in V are not spectroscopic and BTK fits are invalid.
  - The manuscript reports no junction resistances, no estimate of contact size versus mean free path (Wexler formula), and no discussion of spreading resistance in a material with ρ0 of order μΩ·cm.
- **Side dips are not tested against the critical-current mechanism.** Side dips flanking a peak are the hallmark of the critical-current/heating mechanism (Sheet et al., PRB 2004, cited as ref. 42).
  - The heating test in the text is applied only to the ZBCP at ambient pressure, where no dips exist.
  - It is not applied to the pressurized spectra, where the dips are the key evidence for "increased pz weight".
  - The authors should show the following for the side dips: dip-position scaling with temperature (the dip should follow Ic(T) rather than Δ(T)); field dependence under pressure; and dependence on junction resistance.
  - The 1.4 GPa spectrum (flat-topped broad peak with sharp dips at about ±2 mV) looks very much like critical-current spectra.
- **Other ZBCP origins are not excluded.**
  - At ambient pressure the ZBCP is only about 15% of G0.
  - A ZBCP of this size can also arise from reflectionless tunnelling, multiple parallel contacts (soft Au-epoxy contacts are inherently multi-contact), or proximity effects at a polished surface.
  - The argument against magnetic scattering ("no ordering at ambient") is insufficient. UTe2 has strong magnetic fluctuations, and the near-surface stoichiometry after polishing is unknown.
- **The ZBCP survives fields comparable to or above the bulk upper critical field.** Fig. S2(a) shows the ZBCP still visible at 8 T along a at 0.5 K.
  - This is near or above Hc2∥a for this Tc, but is not discussed.
  - A peak surviving above the bulk Hc2 would argue against a purely superconducting ABS origin, or would point to a surface region with different properties.

**3.2 The fits are over-parametrised, so the central observable ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ is not robustly determined.**
- **Parameter degeneracy.** Each single-channel fit has four free parameters (Δx(y), Δz, Z, Γ). The multi-channel superposition (Eq. 2) used at 1.0–1.2 GPa roughly doubles this, adding weights wi.
  - The SI itself shows that side dips appear or vanish depending on Γ and Z (Fig. S6c,d), not only on Δz/Δx.
  - The statement that dips "can only be reproduced by increasing the weight of pz" is therefore not demonstrated.
  - Missing are parameter-correlation analyses, χ² landscapes, or fits with Δz/Δx constrained to the ambient value while Z and Γ vary freely.
- **Junction parameters change with pressure on the same contact.** Z changes from 1.67 to ~1.2, and Γ drops from 0.39 to 0.10 meV between 0 and 0.8 GPa.
  - The side dips are, by the authors' own statement, smeared by large Γ.
  - So the qualitative change from SC1 to SC2/SC3 spectra may be at least partly a junction-evolution effect, such as epoxy compression or a changing contact geometry under pressure.
  - This alternative is not addressed. There is also no pressure-release (depressurisation) cycle to show that the ambient-like spectrum is recovered.
- **Error bars appear unrealistically small.**
  - Quoted uncertainties of ±0.01 meV on gaps (Fig. S8, Table S1) are smaller than Γ, the thermal smearing (~25 μeV at 0.3 K) and any plausible systematic error.
  - They most likely reflect statistical fit errors, not true confidence intervals. The "minimum" in the ratio and its identification with a phase transition depend critically on these error bars.
- **Internal inconsistencies undermine confidence in the fitting.**
  - Table S1 assigns Δz = 1.18 meV and Δx(y) = 0.72 meV to S1, whereas Fig. S7(a), Fig. S2(c) and the main-text ratio Δz/Δx(y) ≈ 0.57 imply the reverse.
  - Table S1 therefore appears to have its columns swapped, which needs correction and a consistency check of all tabulated values.
  - The ratio is called "≈ 0.57(5)", but the individual samples give approximately 0.61, 0.54 and 0.52.

**3.3 The theoretical model is too simplified to support symmetry-specific conclusions for UTe2.**
- **The Fermi-surface geometry is unrealistic for this material.** The extended BTK uses an isotropic (spherical) Fermi surface with linear-in-k basis functions.
  - UTe2 has quasi-2D cylindrical Fermi sheets with weak kz dispersion (Eaton et al., ref. 39, by co-authors).
  - For c-axis injection, the available kz, the Fermi-velocity projection, and hence both the ABS spectrum and the meaning of "Δz versus Δx" differ qualitatively from the spherical model.
  - The ratio of basis-function coefficients is not a physical observable unless it is weighted over the real Fermi surface. It is therefore unclear what a statement such as "pz-dominant SC3" means physically.
- **The symmetry discrimination is model-dependent.** The exclusion of Au relies on peak splitting at "any finite Z" for a specific choice Δx = Δy = Δz.
  - Anisotropic Au states, which the authors themselves invoke in Remark (1) as "quasi-Bnu", or states including higher-order terms, can produce near-zero-energy ABS on (001).
  - Thus the claims in the abstract and conclusions ("unambiguously suggesting the presence of pz", "B2u or B3u as the most likely representation") are stronger than the analysis supports.
  - Similarly, B1u is excluded only within the linear-k model.
- **The physics is incomplete.**
  - Non-unitary or time-reversal-symmetry-breaking combinations (e.g. B3u + iB2u, relevant to the chirality and multicomponent proposals the paper cites) are not considered.
  - Yet a two-component state is exactly what would be expected within SC3 if SC2 and SC3 belong to different representations.
  - The B2u → B3u proposal for SC2 → SC3 is inconsistent with considering only single-representation fits in the coexistence region.

**3.4 The evidence for distinct phases and for the SC2–SC3 transition is indirect and partially circular.**
- **The SC2/SC3 boundary is taken from the literature.** The boundary in Fig. 2(b) and Fig. 5(a) is reproduced from other samples and other groups, not measured on S1.
  - The "match" between the ratio minima and this boundary rests on three points with sizeable error bars (Fig. S9e,f).
  - Near Tc the gaps are small and the ratio becomes ill-determined, as the error bars at 0.6 GPa above 2 K show.
  - The rise of the ratio towards Tc could equally be a fitting artefact as the gaps collapse.
- **A minimum is not by itself evidence of a phase transition.** A smooth temperature-dependent redistribution between components, or between bands in a multigap scenario, would also produce a minimum.
  - The claim that it is "more likely a phase transition than a crossover" is not backed by any kink analysis, coherence-peak anomaly, or independent thermodynamic measurement on the same sample.
  - At 0.6 GPa the ratio is essentially flat (~1.05) below 1.6 K, so the "minimum" is marginal.
- **The interpretation of the double dips is ad hoc.** The double-dip structure at 1.0–1.2 GPa is explained either by multiple gaps or by pressure inhomogeneity across domains, and both are fitted by the same superposition.
  - These two scenarios have very different physical implications.
  - The resulting ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ of about 1.3 at 1.2 GPa is a weighted average of two channels with different Z or Γ, and is not a property of a single order parameter.
- **The symmetry assignment depends on another experiment.** The assignment SC2 = B2u, SC3 = B3u rests mainly on an interpretation of the NMR data (Kinjo et al.).
  - The PCS here, by the authors' own statement, cannot distinguish B2u from B3u on (001).
  - The PCS evidence therefore does not independently support the representation change highlighted in the abstract and summary.
- **The robustness basis is too narrow.**
  - All pressure data come from a single junction on a single sample (S1).
  - No pressure-dependent field data are shown, even though SC2 and SC3 are known to have very different Hc2 anisotropies and field responses. Such data would be a powerful discriminator.

---

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Establish the spectroscopic (ballistic or near-ballistic) regime and rule out heating artefacts explicitly.**
   - Report the junction resistance R_N at each pressure, and estimate contact size versus mean free path.
   - Show raw I–V and dI/dV over larger bias ranges.
   - Track the position of the side dips versus temperature and field, and test whether it follows Δ(T) or a critical-current scaling.
   - Measure field dependence up to Hc2 under pressure, along several field orientations if possible.
   - Add spectra from additional junctions and samples under pressure, and a depressurisation run to demonstrate reversibility.
2. **Make the fitting statistically rigorous and internally consistent.**
   - Correct Table S1 and cross-check all parameter tables against the figures.
   - Provide χ² maps and parameter correlations (especially Δz/Δx versus Γ and Z), plus realistic confidence intervals including systematic errors.
   - Show that fits with the ratio held at its SC1 value, letting Z and Γ vary freely, fail at 0.6–1.2 GPa.
   - Justify the number of channels in Eq. (2), and test alternative models (two-band B2u, Au with anisotropic gaps, B3u + iB2u) on equal footing using an information criterion.
3. **Extend the theory towards UTe2's actual electronic structure.**
   - Compute conductance spectra with a quasi-2D (cylindrical) Fermi surface or a tight-binding model consistent with dHvA/ARPES, including Fermi-velocity mismatch for a heavy-fermion/Au interface.
   - Assess how the ABS signatures and the meaning of ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ change.
   - Reconcile the very large 2Δ/kBTc with STM and thermodynamic gap estimates, or discuss how the gap scale might be renormalised by the junction.
   - Tone down "unambiguous" claims accordingly.
4. **Strengthen the phase-transition argument and broaden the symmetry test.**
   - Measure AC susceptibility or AC calorimetry on the same sample and in the same cell, to place the SC2/SC3 boundary independently.
   - Analyse the temperature dependence of the gaps themselves (kinks or slope changes), not only their ratio.
   - Perform PCS on (100) and (010) facets, where B2u and B3u give different ABS signatures, to test the proposed B2u → B3u change directly.
   - Fix minor issues:
     - Reference typos: ref. 24 year should be 2020; ref. 1 "Pep. Rrog."
     - Inconsistent symbol labels in Figs. S2(c) and S9.
     - The pressure-calibration method (manometer at low temperature) is not described.
     - The data-availability statement should be expanded to include raw spectra and fitting code.
