# Review outline

**Manuscript:** "Multiple nodal superconducting phases and order-parameter evolution in pressurized UTe₂" (point-contact spectroscopy on the (001) surface under hydrostatic pressure)

---

## 1. Significance and novelty

**Scientific context**
- Identifying the pairing symmetry of UTe₂ is a central open problem in unconventional and possibly topological superconductivity.
- The pressure-induced SC1/SC2/SC3 phases are firmly established in thermodynamic data (ac calorimetry, ac susceptibility, NMR).
- Their order parameters, however, are essentially unknown, because symmetry-sensitive probes rarely work inside a pressure cell.

**Claimed novelty**
- This is, to my knowledge, the first point-contact spectroscopy (PCS) study of UTe₂ under hydrostatic pressure (up to ~1.4 GPa), on the (001) facet.
- A zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP) is reported at all pressures, interpreted as a zero-energy Andreev bound state (ZABS) from a k_z-odd (p_z) gap component.
- Pressure-induced side dips and double dips are reported above ~0.3 GPa.
- An extended spin-triplet BTK analysis is used to:
  - favour B₂u/B₃u over A_u and B₁u;
  - introduce ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩ as a single parameter distinguishing SC1 (p_x(y)-dominant, ratio ≈0.6) from SC3 (p_z-dominant, ratio ≈1.0–1.3).
- A non-monotonic temperature dependence of this ratio is claimed to track the SC2–SC3 boundary.
- Combined with Kinjo et al.'s NMR, the authors propose SC2 = B₂u and SC3 = B₃u.

**Potential impact**
- If the spectral features are intrinsic and the model is adequate, this would be a valuable spectroscopic counterpart to the thermodynamic pressure phase diagram.
- It would also be among the first k-space-sensitive constraints on the pressure-induced phases.

**Caveat on novelty**
- The decisive symmetry assignment (B₂u vs B₃u) cannot be made from (001) data alone. The authors acknowledge that B₂u and B₃u are indistinguishable in their geometry.
- The most striking conclusion (an SC2→SC3 change of irreducible representation) therefore rests on external NMR input rather than on the present measurement.

---

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

**Experimentally challenging and timely**
- Soft point contacts surviving pressure cycling to 1.4 GPa inside a piston-clamp cell, with simultaneous resistivity on the same crystal, is technically non-trivial.
- The (p, T) dataset (Figs. 3, S3) is rich and systematic.

**Clear qualitative pressure evolution in a single contact**
- At p ≤ 0.2–0.3 GPa: a ZBCP of modest amplitude.
- At 0.6–0.8 GPa: a strongly enhanced ZBCP with symmetric side dips.
- At 1.0–1.2 GPa: double dips.
- At 1.4 GPa: a flat-topped broad peak.
- The onset of the qualitative change near ~0.3 GPa roughly coincides with the SC1/SC2–SC3 boundary from the literature.

**Coherent theoretical framework**
- The extended BTK formalism for unitary triplet pairing (SI IV) is laid out explicitly.
- Simulations for pure p_x(y), pure p_z and the four D₂h odd-parity irreducible representations (Figs. 4, S5, S6) provide a useful qualitative screening tool.

**Ambient-pressure reproducibility and consistency with prior work**
- Three samples (S1–S3) show ZBCPs at ambient pressure.
- The claimed p_y/B₃u-leaning conclusion is qualitatively consistent with Yoon et al. (PCS) and Gu et al. (STM, (0-11) facet).

**Bridges probes**
- Linking a spectroscopic quantity to thermodynamically defined phase boundaries (Fig. 5a, red circles) is an attractive idea that could guide future theory and experiment.

## 3. Potential reasons for rejection

### Reason 1 — The intrinsic (ZABS) origin of the ZBCP and, especially, the side dips is not established; non-ballistic/critical-current artifacts are not excluded.

**Side dips are a classic critical-current signature.**
- Symmetric conductance dips flanking a central enhancement are the textbook fingerprint of point contacts in the intermediate/thermal regime. In that regime the local current density exceeds the critical current (Sheet et al., PRB 69, 134507, which the authors themselves cite).
- The 1.4 GPa spectrum (Fig. 3d) is particularly telling: a broad, flat-topped peak bounded by sharp dips that move inward with temperature is precisely the critical-current pattern.
- The double dips at 1.0–1.2 GPa are naturally explained by several parallel micro-contacts with different critical currents. Soft Au-epoxy contacts made from colloidal Au particles almost certainly comprise many parallel channels.
- The authors themselves invoke "multiple domains" (Eq. 2) to fit these spectra, which strengthens rather than weakens this concern.

**The heating-artifact rebuttal (p. 5) is qualitative and incomplete.**
- It relies only on:
  - a gradual temperature degradation of the peak;
  - weak field dependence below 2 T at ambient pressure (Fig. S2a).
- No contact resistance R_N, no estimate of contact radius (Sharvin/Wexler) versus the electronic mean free path, and no I–V characteristics are given.
- No analysis is made of dip position versus temperature compared with an expected I_c(T) or Δ(T).
- The critical-current test is not performed at all under pressure, which is precisely where the dips appear.

**The ZBCP persists to unusually high bias, temperature and field.**
- ZBCP widths are ~1–2 mV, and the feature is visible up to ~2.5 K at ambient pressure, i.e. above the bulk zero-resistance T_c⁰ = 2.1 K.
- A ZBCP is still present at 8 T with H ∥ a (Fig. S2a).
- These observations call for discussion of:
  - bulk versus surface/filamentary superconductivity;
  - the conventional Zeeman/Doppler splitting expected for a surface ABS;
  - why no splitting is seen.

**Contact stability across pressure is not demonstrated.**
- The same contact on S1 is cycled through pressure, but the fitted junction parameters change substantially:
  - Z ≈ 1.67 → ≈1.2;
  - Γ ≈ 0.39 → ≈0.10 meV.
- The normalized ZBCP amplitude jumps from ~1.15 to ~2.1.
- This suggests that the junction itself (area, number of channels, regime) changes under pressure. The spectral "evolution" may then partially reflect junction evolution rather than order-parameter evolution.

### Reason 2 — The extracted gap parameters are internally inconsistent and physically implausible, undermining the central quantitative claim (⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩).

**Direct internal contradictions in the fitted values.**
- Table S1 lists Δ_z ≈ 1.15–1.18 meV and Δ_x(y) ≈ 0.60–0.72 meV for S1–S3. That would imply Δ_z/Δ_x(y) ≈ 1.6–1.9.
- By contrast, the main text (p. 8), Fig. S7a and Fig. S9a give Δ_x(y) = 1.18 and Δ_z = 0.72 meV for S1, and Δ_z/Δ_x(y) ≈ 0.57.
- Either the Table S1 columns are swapped or the conclusions are reversed. Since the paper's key argument is precisely the relative weight of p_z versus p_x(y), this must be resolved unambiguously.

**Gap magnitudes are far outside any reasonable range.**
- At ambient pressure, Δ_x(y)(0.3 K) ≈ 1.18 meV with T_c ≈ 2.1 K gives 2Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 13. The BCS value is Δ ≈ 0.32 meV, which the authors plot as the dashed line in Fig. S2c.
- At 1.2 GPa, Δ_z¹ ≈ 1.6 meV with T_c ≈ 3 K again gives 2Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 12.
- STM, thermal conductivity and penetration-depth studies on UTe₂ indicate gaps of order ~0.2–0.4 meV.
- The authors interpret the "strong violation of BCS" (Fig. S2c) as evidence of unconventional SC. A factor of ~4 excess is far more naturally a symptom of fitting artifacts: spreading resistance, series resistance, or non-ballistic broadening that inflates the voltage scale.

**Parameter degeneracy and unconstrained models.**
- The ambient fits have 4 free parameters (Δ_z, Δ_x(y), Z, Γ) for a nearly featureless Gaussian-like peak of ~15% height (Figs. 2d, S7). Δ_z/Δ_x(y) is very likely strongly correlated with Z and Γ.
- Neither correlation matrices nor χ² landscapes are provided.
- The superposition model (Eq. 2), with several channels each having its own (Δ_z, Δ_x, Z, Γ) plus weights w_i, has enough freedom to fit almost any multi-dip spectrum. Its success in Fig. 5e is therefore not evidence for a particular pairing state.
- Eq. 3, a weighted ratio of weighted sums, mixes junction-channel weights with order-parameter properties. Its physical meaning in the "pressure-inhomogeneity" interpretation (different domains at different effective pressures) is unclear.

**Suspicious uniformity of fit uncertainties and parameters.**
- At 0.8 GPa, Γ = 0.10(1) meV and Z ≈ 1.20(1–2) are essentially constant across 0.5–2.1 K (Fig. S8).
- Several gaps carry ±0.01 meV uncertainties, implausibly small given the spectral broadening.
- It should be stated which parameters were fixed and how uncertainties were obtained.

### Reason 3 — The theoretical model is too idealized for UTe₂ to support symmetry discrimination among irreducible representations.

**Fermi-surface geometry and mismatch are ignored.**
- The extended BTK uses a single spherical Fermi surface with equal masses and Fermi velocities on both sides (Eqs. S2–S9).
- UTe₂ has quasi-2D cylindrical Fermi sheets along c, with small v_z, plus a possible 3D pocket, and a very large Au/heavy-fermion Fermi-velocity mismatch.
- For (001) injection the transmission is dominated by the small v_z portions of the Fermi surface. Both the k_∥-sum weighting and the effective Z will differ qualitatively from the spherical model.
- The robustness of the main conclusions to this assumption — "ZBCP height <2 ⇒ p_x(y)-dominant" and "side dips ⇒ larger p_z weight" — is untested.

**Exclusion of A_u and B₁u is not robust.**
- A_u is dismissed using an isotropic simulation (Δ_x = Δ_y = Δ_z) with Γ = 0.01 meV (Fig. S6a). This is very different from the Γ ≈ 0.1–0.4 meV used in the experimental fits.
- An anisotropic A_u state (strongly unequal Δ_i), as suggested by NMR and thermal conductivity, together with realistic Γ, may well reproduce a single broadened ZBCP. The authors concede in Remark (1) that A_u cannot be excluded.
- The B₁u exclusion is based solely on the absence of side dips, which are themselves of uncertain origin (Reason 1).

**The SC2/SC3 representation assignment is not derived from the data.**
- B₂u and B₃u give identical (001) spectra. The proposed B₂u → B₃u change at the SC2–SC3 boundary is inferred from NMR alone.
- Multicomponent states (e.g. B₂u + iB₃u, or mixtures with A_u) are not modelled, even though a cited work (Wu et al., PRL 2025) argues SC3 may be multicomponent.
- Such states could plausibly also produce non-monotonic temperature dependence of an effective ratio. Only unitary states are considered (d × d* = 0), although time-reversal-symmetry breaking has been discussed for UTe₂.

**The surface condition is assumed specular.**
- ZABS formation assumes specular reflection at a clean (001) interface.
- The surface was mechanically "fine-polished" and contacted with Au epoxy cured at 423 K. Roughness, a disordered layer and Te loss could strongly modify or suppress bound states.
- None of this is characterized or modelled (for example, a rough-interface or diffusive-surface treatment).

### Reason 4 — The evidence for distinct phases and for a phase transition at the SC2–SC3 boundary is statistically and experimentally thin, and the claims are overstated.

**All pressure data come from a single sample and a single contact.**
- There is no reproducibility under pressure on a second sample or contact.
- The pressure determination (manometer type, e.g. Pb T_c or manganin; pressure gradients; loss of pressure on cooling) is not described.
- Daphne 7373 solidifies at ~2 GPa at room temperature and well below that at low temperature; non-hydrostaticity is invoked only ad hoc, to explain the double dips.

**The "minimum of ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩" criterion is weak.**
- In Figs. 5f and S9e–f, the minima are shallow, comparable to the error bars, and located where both gaps shrink and their ratio becomes ill-conditioned. Error bars grow dramatically above the minimum.
- The agreement with literature SC2–SC3 boundaries (red circles in Fig. 5a) involves only three pressures, with substantial scatter. A minimum in a ratio of two fit parameters does not imply a thermodynamic transition; a smooth crossover in fit weights or a junction-regime change can produce it.

**The pressure dependence of the ratio has too few points to claim a "drastic" change across SC1→SC3.**
- The inset of Fig. 5a has ~7 points.
- The SC1 → SC3 increase coincides with the very pressures at which the junction parameters (Z, Γ) change and side dips appear.
- SC2 and SC3 are not distinguished by the ratio value itself (both ≈1–1.3). This contradicts the abstract's claim that the phases "can be distinguished by a single parameter."

**Bulk sample characterization raises additional questions.**
- The ambient-pressure resistive transition is broad (T_c^on = 2.6 K vs T_c⁰ = 2.1 K). This is unusual for MSF-grown "ultraclean" crystals with RRR ≈ 410, which typically show sharp transitions at ~2.1 K.
- It hints at inhomogeneity or a surface/strain layer, which matters directly for a surface-sensitive probe.
- No specific heat or susceptibility on the measured crystal is shown.

**Overstatement of conclusions.**
- Phrases such as "unambiguously suggesting the presence of p_z component" and "spectroscopic evidence for the existence of multiple SC phases" go well beyond what single-facet, single-contact BTK fits can support.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

### Suggestion 1 — Provide full junction characterization and decisive artifact tests.
- Report the following for every contact and pressure:
  - R_N;
  - raw I–V curves and un-normalized dI/dV;
  - the normalization procedure and background;
  - contact-size estimates (Sharvin/Wexler) compared with the mean free path.
- Plot side-dip positions versus T and H and compare with the expected I_c(T) and Δ(T). Show whether the dips scale with R_N across contacts, as critical-current dips do.
- Repeat the pressure series on at least one more sample or contact, ideally including a deliberately higher-R_N (closer to tunneling) junction.
- Use a manometer (e.g. Pb or Sn) and state the pressure uncertainty.
- Present quantitative field dependence (peak height, width, any splitting) under pressure and along several field directions. Discuss the expected Zeeman/Doppler response of a triplet ABS for the relevant d-vector orientation.

### Suggestion 2 — Correct inconsistencies and put the fitting on a rigorous statistical footing.
- Correct Table S1 against Fig. S7, Fig. S9 and the main text, and state clearly which component is larger in SC1.
- Explain how gap values ~4× the BCS value (2Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 12–13) can be physical, or account for spreading or series resistance, which typically inflates the apparent gap.
- Provide χ²/AIC comparisons across all candidate states fitted under identical conditions:
  - pure B₁u;
  - B₂u/B₃u;
  - anisotropic A_u with realistic Γ;
  - multicomponent B₂u + iB₃u.
- Give parameter correlation matrices and uncertainty estimates (e.g. bootstrap), and state explicitly which parameters (Z, Γ) are fixed versus free in temperature series.
- For the superposition model, limit the number of channels, justify w_i physically, and show that single-channel fits truly fail.
- Reconsider or justify the definition in Eq. 3.

### Suggestion 3 — Use a more realistic theoretical model.
- Extend the BTK calculation to include:
  - Fermi-velocity/effective-mass mismatch;
  - a quasi-2D (cylindrical) UTe₂ Fermi surface, or a tight-binding fit to quantum-oscillation and ARPES data;
  - possibly interface roughness.
- Then test whether the key discriminators ("peak <2 ⇒ p_x(y)-dominant", "side dips ⇒ p_z weight", "A_u ⇒ split peak") survive.
- Calculate the spectra expected on the (100), (010) and (011) surfaces, where B₂u and B₃u become distinguishable. This provides a falsifiable prediction for the proposed SC2 = B₂u, SC3 = B₃u scenario.
- Discuss non-unitary or time-reversal-breaking states and higher-order basis functions (e.g. k_x k_y k_z terms) that are allowed by symmetry.

### Suggestion 4 — Strengthen the link to bulk phases and moderate the claims.
- Measure bulk T_c (ac susceptibility or calorimetry) in situ on the same crystal and pressure cell, so that the SC2–SC3 line used in Fig. 5a is from the same sample rather than reproduced from the literature.
- Use a denser pressure grid near 0.2–0.4 GPa and 1.2–1.5 GPa.
- Present the temperature dependence of the ratio with honest uncertainty bands. Test whether a smooth crossover model is statistically distinguishable from the claimed minimum.
- Where feasible, measure PCS on (100) or (010) facets, at least at ambient pressure and one pressure in SC3, to actually distinguish B₂u from B₃u.
- Rephrase the abstract and conclusions accordingly, e.g. "consistent with" rather than "unambiguously," and acknowledge that SC2 and SC3 are not separated by the ratio's value.
- Discuss relevant theory and experiment on pressure-induced multicomponent pairing in more depth.
- Fix editorial errors:
  - "Pep. Rrog. Phys." in Ref. 1;
  - the year of Ref. 24 (2020, not 2002);
  - "Supplementray", "spits", "differentiate conductance";
  - the "seeing Fig." phrasing;
  - the column labels in Table S1.
