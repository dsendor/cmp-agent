Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Problem addressed.** The superconducting order parameter of UTe₂ is unresolved, especially for the pressure-induced phases SC1, SC2 and SC3. These phases are known mainly from thermodynamic and magnetic phase boundaries (AC calorimetry, AC susceptibility, NMR). Symmetry-sensitive probes under hydrostatic pressure are almost absent. A spectroscopic probe that tracks gap structure across this phase diagram would be valuable.
- **Main claims.**
  - Point-contact spectroscopy (PCS) on the (001) surface of ultraclean molten-salt-flux crystals (RRR ≈ 410), from ambient pressure to 1.4 GPa, shows a zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP) at all pressures.
  - Above about 0.3 GPa, symmetric side dips and later double side dips appear.
  - An extended spin-triplet BTK model attributes these features to a zero-energy Andreev bound state (ZABS) from a k_z-odd ("p_z") gap component, favouring B₂u/B₃u.
  - The single ratio ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩ is proposed to distinguish SC1 (~0.6), SC2 and SC3 (>1, reaching ~1.3 at 1.2 GPa).
  - A non-monotonic temperature dependence of this ratio is said to reproduce the SC2–SC3 boundary.
- **Novelty.**
  - This would be the first pressure-dependent PCS study of UTe₂.
  - It would be the first attempt to assign symmetry-resolved gap content to SC2 and SC3.
  - The proposed B₂u (SC2) → B₃u (SC3) scenario, combined with prior NMR, is provocative and of broad interest.
- **Overall assessment.** The concept and dataset are interesting. However, the leap from spectral line shapes to an irreducible-representation (IR) assignment and a "single-parameter" phase discrimination rests on a minimal model and a single pressurized contact. Novelty is high; the robustness of the central inference is currently insufficient for a top-tier journal.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Experimental advance.**
  - Andreev/tunnelling-type spectroscopy inside a piston-clamp cell down to 0.3 K is technically demanding.
  - It gives systematic T-dependent data at several pressures spanning SC1, SC2/SC3 and the edge of the magnetically ordered (MO) phases.
- **Sample quality and phase diagram.**
  - High-quality crystals are used, with resistivity measured on the same sample.
  - The p–T phase diagram reproduces the literature (Tc dome, Tm1 and Tm2), anchoring the PCS data to known phases.
- **Clear qualitative evolution.** The spectral changes with pressure are striking:
  - ZBCP only, then ZBCP with side dips, then double dips, then a broad flat-topped peak.
  - These are reproducible over thermal cycles within the pressurized contact.
- **Reproducibility at ambient pressure.** The ZBCP is seen in three samples, and the fitted gap ratio Δ_z/Δ_x(y) ≈ 0.57 is similar across contacts with different Z and Γ.
- **Theory framework and context.**
  - The generalized BTK formalism for unitary triplet states is laid out in the supplementary information (SI), with simulations for all four D₂h odd-parity IRs.
  - This provides a useful comparative template for the community.
- **Physical interest.** The suggested correlation between the minimum of ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩(T) and the SC2–SC3 boundary from bulk probes would, if robust, be a genuinely new spectroscopic handle on multicomponent superconductivity.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**(i) The intrinsic, spectroscopic (ballistic/ABS) origin of the ZBCP and especially the side dips is not established; extrinsic origins are not excluded.**
- **Side dips have a well-known extrinsic explanation.**
  - Symmetric conductance dips flanking a ZBCP are the textbook signature of the thermal/diffusive regime: the contact reaches its local critical current (Sheet et al., ref. 42, which the authors cite only for the ZBCP).
  - The appearance of dips only under pressure, their deepening on cooling, and the broad flat-topped peak with sharp dips at 1.4 GPa are all qualitatively consistent with critical-current physics in a soft Au-epoxy contact whose geometry evolves with pressure.
  - The critical-current test is applied only to the ambient-pressure ZBCP, and only weakly ("small field <2 T weakly affects width"). It is never applied to the dips, which carry the central claim.
  - Needed but absent:
    - dip position vs. temperature compared with Δ(T) and with I_c(T) scaling;
    - dip position vs. normal-state contact resistance R_N;
    - dip position vs. magnetic field;
    - plots against bias current rather than voltage.
- **The contact regime is not characterized.**
  - No R_N values, contact-area estimate (Wexler/Sharvin), or comparison with the elastic and inelastic mean free paths are given, although RRR ≈ 410 implies long ℓ_el.
  - A drop of Au epoxy almost certainly forms many parallel nanocontacts of varying transparency. This undermines the single-channel, specular-interface assumption of the planar BTK model.
  - It also undermines the use of absolute peak heights (G/G₀ < 1.2 vs. > 2) as a quantitative discriminator.
  - Normalization procedures, background subtraction and spreading-resistance corrections are not described.
- **The ZBCP onset and the fitted gap closure are inconsistent.**
  - At ambient pressure the ZBCP appears at 2.5 K, near T_c^on, i.e., above the bulk zero-resistance T_c⁰ = 2.1 K.
  - Yet the fitted gaps (Fig. S2c) close at ~2.1 K.
  - The resistive transition is 0.5 K wide, unusually broad for RRR 410 ultraclean crystals. This raises the question of surface or strained regions (polishing, epoxy curing at 423 K) with a different T_c contributing to the signal.

**(ii) The fitted parameters are physically implausible and strongly degenerate, so the key quantity ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩ is not reliably determined.**
- **Gap magnitudes are unphysically large.**
  - At ambient pressure Δ_x(y) ≈ 1.18 meV with T_c ≈ 2.1 K gives 2Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 13, nearly four times the weak-coupling value.
  - The authors present the deviation from the BCS curve in Fig. S2c (whose dashed line, ~0.32 meV, is indeed weak-coupling BCS) as proof of unconventionality.
  - Such values instead usually signal that the BTK fit is absorbing extrinsic broadening (spreading resistance, heating) into Δ.
  - This should be compared with existing UTe₂ STM and specific-heat gap estimates.
- **Γ and Z change with pressure in a way that can by itself explain the new features.**
  - Γ falls from ~0.39 meV (ambient, S1) to ~0.10 meV (0.8 GPa), and Z changes from 1.67 to ~1.2.
  - The authors' own simulations (Fig. S6d, "the shoulder deepens into satellite peaks as Γ is reduced") show that lowering Γ alone generates side dips and satellites.
  - The emergence of dips, attributed to "increasing p_z weight", may thus reflect a change in junction broadening. No analysis disentangles Δ_z/Δ_x from Γ and Z.
  - No parameter-correlation matrix, χ² landscape or confidence regions are provided.
- **Bookkeeping errors undermine confidence in the headline ratio.**
  - Table S1 lists Δ_z = 1.18, 1.15, 1.15 meV and Δ_x(y) = 0.72, 0.62, 0.60 meV. Fig. S7a gives the opposite labelling for the same S1 fit (Δ_x(y) = 1.18, Δ_z = 0.72).
  - Taken literally, Table S1 implies Δ_z/Δ_x(y) ≈ 1.8, i.e., p_z-dominant at ambient pressure. This contradicts the main-text claim of ≈ 0.57.
- **The superposition model adds unconstrained freedom.**
  - Eq. (2) is introduced exactly where the single-channel fit fails (≥1.0 GPa).
  - It roughly doubles the number of free parameters (two Δ sets plus weights w_i), without independent justification (multiband vs. pressure inhomogeneity).
  - The weighted ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩ then mixes physically distinct channels, so its continuity with the single-channel ratio at lower pressure is not meaningful.

**(iii) The theoretical model is too oversimplified to support an IR assignment, and the IR discrimination is incomplete or biased.**
- **The Fermi-surface and interface model is inappropriate for UTe₂.**
  - The model assumes a single isotropic 3D spherical Fermi surface (k_z² + k_∥² = k_F²), linear-in-k basis functions, and no Fermi-velocity mismatch between Au and a heavy-fermion metal.
  - UTe₂ has quasi-2D cylindrical sheets along c (ref. 39, co-authored by the team), so tunnelling along c samples a small and peculiar portion of k-space.
  - Whether a k_z-odd component produces a robust (001) ZABS on cylindrical sheets, possibly with a weak 3D pocket, is non-trivial and could change the qualitative conclusions.
  - The B₂u/B₃u nodal positions are themselves Fermi-surface dependent; Fig. 1's spherical/ellipsoidal sketches do not represent UTe₂.
- **The IR exclusion arguments use unequal conditions.**
  - Aᵤ is simulated with Γ = 0.01 meV and only isotropic Δ_x = Δ_y = Δ_z. B₂u/B₃u use Γ = 0.05 meV, and the actual fits use Γ = 0.1–0.4 meV.
  - At realistic Γ, the Aᵤ splitting (a feature smaller than Δ) is likely smeared into a single peak. Strongly anisotropic Aᵤ (as NMR suggests) is not explored. The claim that "splitting is never observed" therefore does not exclude Aᵤ.
  - B₁ᵤ also gives a ZBCP at small Z (Fig. S6b), and the ambient-pressure S1/S2 spectra have no side dips. Ambient-pressure data alone thus cannot distinguish B₁ᵤ from B₂u/B₃u, contrary to the abstract's "for both ambient and pressurized UTe₂".
- **The SC2 = B₂u / SC3 = B₃u assignment cannot come from these data.**
  - The authors acknowledge that B₂u and B₃u are indistinguishable on (001), so this assignment rests entirely on external NMR.
  - A transition between two different IRs within the superconducting state at a continuous phase line implies either a first-order transition or an intermediate mixed (e.g., B₂u + iB₃u, TRS-breaking) state.
  - Neither possibility is modelled. A two-component state was not fitted, although it is the natural low-temperature state below the second transition.
  - The "p_z weight" ratio is also not a symmetry-defining quantity. Δ_z/Δ_x within a single IR is a material parameter that can vary continuously with pressure, so its change does not by itself establish distinct phases.

**(iv) The experimental basis for the pressure claims is thin, and the phase-transition inference is statistically weak.**
- **There is no independent control under pressure.**
  - All pressure data come from a single contact on a single sample (S1). Pressure changes the epoxy, the contact area and the barrier, and possibly sample strain, at every step.
  - No pressure-release check is shown: does the ambient spectrum return?
  - No second pressurized contact or sample, and no variation of R_N at fixed pressure, is presented to separate intrinsic from junction-induced evolution.
- **The ratio minimum is shallow and poorly constrained.**
  - The "minimum" in ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩(T) is about 1.25 → 1.0 → 1.3 at 0.8 GPa. The upturn is defined by points near T_c, where both gaps are small, error bars grow strongly (Fig. S9e,f), and the ratio becomes ill-conditioned.
  - The 1.2 GPa minimum is at 0.7 K, within the region requiring the two-channel model.
  - No independent signature at the SC2–SC3 boundary is identified directly in the raw spectra (kink in peak height, dip position or zero-bias conductance vs. T).
  - With fit uncertainty properly propagated (including correlations with Γ and Z), the agreement with bulk boundaries may not be significant. The language "more likely a phase transition than a crossover" and "unambiguously suggesting p_z" is overstated.
- **Discriminating tests and the near-MO regime are left untouched.**
  - Magnetic-field dependence under pressure is absent, despite an 8 T magnet and field ∥ a. Field-induced ZBCP splitting (Doppler shift) or anisotropic suppression would be a key test of ABS vs. critical-current origin and of d-vector orientation.
  - At 1.4 GPa, close to MO1/MO2, the authors' own argument against magnetic (Kondo/Appelbaum-type) zero-bias anomalies no longer holds, yet this is not addressed.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

**(1) Establish the spectroscopic regime and exclude extrinsic dips.**
- Report R_N, an estimate of contact size and the ballistic/diffusive/thermal classification at every pressure.
- Present dip positions vs. T, B and R_N. Replot the data vs. bias current to test I_c scaling.
- Perform a pressure-release measurement and add at least one more independent pressurized contact or sample, ideally with different R_N, to show the side-dip evolution is intrinsic.
- Clarify normalization and any background subtraction, and discuss the ZBCP onset above T_c⁰ together with the broad resistive transition.

**(2) Make the fitting quantitatively trustworthy.**
- Correct the Δ_z/Δ_x(y) labelling inconsistency between Table S1, Fig. S7 and the main text.
- Provide χ² maps or Bayesian posteriors showing the covariance of Δ_z/Δ_x(y) with Γ and Z. Test whether a fixed ratio with varying Γ and Z can reproduce the 0.6–0.8 GPa spectra; this is the critical null test.
- Address the 2Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 8–13 problem, e.g., by including a spreading-resistance or series-resistance correction. Compare with STM and thermodynamic gap scales.
- Justify the number of channels in Eq. (2), or replace the weighted ratio with channel-resolved quantities.

**(3) Upgrade the theory to UTe₂-specific conditions.**
- Use realistic quasi-2D (plus possible 3D pocket) Fermi surfaces and Fermi-velocity mismatch, and include higher-harmonic basis functions.
- Compare all IRs at identical, experimentally relevant Γ, Z and anisotropy, including strongly anisotropic Aᵤ (as NMR suggests) and B₁ᵤ for the ambient-pressure data.
- Explicitly model a two-component state (e.g., B₂u + B₃u, real and complex mixtures) for the low-temperature SC3 region.
- Discuss the thermodynamic nature of an inter-IR transition at the SC2–SC3 line.

**(4) Add discriminating measurements and temper the claims.**
- Measure the field dependence (several directions where feasible) under pressure to test ABS splitting and d-vector orientation.
- Measure on other facets ((100), (010) or (0−11)) to separate B₂u from B₃u, which the authors themselves note is required.
- Identify model-independent raw-spectrum signatures at the SC2–SC3 boundary.
- Recast the conclusions accordingly: "consistent with" rather than "unambiguously"; frame the IR assignment for SC2/SC3 as a hypothesis combined with NMR.
- Fix reference errors, e.g., ref. 24 year (2020, not 2002) and the ref. 1 journal abbreviation.
