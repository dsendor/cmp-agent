Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports point-contact spectroscopy (PCS) on the (0 0 1) surface of ultraclean UTe2 under hydrostatic pressure up to 1.4 GPa. It is, to my knowledge, the first pressure-dependent, symmetry-sensitive spectroscopy covering the SC1–SC3 regions of the phase diagram. The authors interpret a zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP), and side dips that appear above about 0.3 GPa, as zero-energy Andreev bound states (ZABS). This would imply a pz-type (kz-odd) component of the gap function. They then use an extended spin-triplet BTK model to argue that:

- the pairing belongs to B2u or B3u;
- the three SC phases differ in the weight ratio ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩;
- the SC2–SC3 boundary is a genuine phase transition, possibly B2u → B3u.

If the spectra are intrinsic, the data set would be valuable: it addresses a question (the pairing of SC2 and SC3) for which almost no spectroscopic information exists. As currently presented, however, the quantitative conclusions rest on a phenomenological fit whose uniqueness and physical grounding are not yet established.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The experiment is technically demanding: PCS inside a piston-clamp cell down to 0.3 K, on high-RRR molten-salt-flux crystals. The resistivity on the same sample reproduces the known p–T phase diagram (Fig. 2b, Fig. S1).
- The spectra change qualitatively with pressure: a simple ZBCP, then a ZBCP with side dips, then double dips, then a broad flat-topped peak (Fig. 3, Fig. S3). In each case the ZBCP disappears near the resistive Tc, which ties the features to superconductivity.
- At ambient pressure a ZBCP appears in three samples with different junction parameters (Fig. 2d–f).
- The temperatures at which the fitted ratio has a minimum (0.6, 0.8 and 1.2 GPa) fall close to the SC2–SC3 line from earlier calorimetry and susceptibility (Fig. 5a). This is a suggestive, independently checkable correlation.
- The SI derivation of the spin-triplet BTK conductance is clear. Simulations across the four D2h odd-parity irreducible representations (Fig. S6) are a useful reference.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

**1. Junction regime and extrinsic origin of the side dips are not established.**

- *What is missing.* The pressure-induced side dips (Fig. 3b–d, Fig. S3) are central: they are the main reason given for pz-dominance in SC2/SC3 and for excluding B1u. Yet conductance dips outside the gap are the textbook signature of the thermal/critical-current regime (Sheet et al., the authors' own Ref. 42). The heating test in the text rests on ambient-pressure field data (Fig. S2a), and that spectrum has no dips. No test is applied to the pressurized spectra, where the dips actually appear.
- *Junction characterization is absent.* The manuscript does not report:
  - the normal-state junction resistance R_N at each pressure;
  - an estimate of contact radius from Sharvin/Wexler formulas;
  - a comparison with the elastic and inelastic mean free paths of UTe2;
  - the dI/dV measurement scheme (lock-in excitation, or numerical derivative; two-probe or pseudo-four-probe).
  These are needed to establish whether the series bulk resistance of the sample contributes.
- *Single junction across pressures.* All pressure data come from one Au-epoxy junction on S1. The junction is compressed and thermally cycled between pressures. The fitted Z (1.67 → ~1.2) and Γ (0.39 → 0.10 meV) change substantially (Fig. S7 vs Fig. S8), which indicates that the junction itself evolved.
- *Requested.* Please:
  - report R_N(p) and the normal-state background;
  - show whether the dip bias scales with R_N (expected if V_dip = I_c R_N) or stays at fixed voltage (expected if the dips are spectroscopic), using additional contacts or samples under pressure;
  - give field dependence of the dip positions under pressure;
  - state explicitly whether the 1.4 GPa spectrum (flat-topped peak, sharp dips near ±2 mV, Fig. 3d) is in the thermal regime.
  Without this, the attribution of the dips to bulk-continuum depletion by surface ABS is not secure.

**2. Fit uniqueness and model selection are not demonstrated.**

- *Free parameters versus spectral content.* Each spectrum is fit with at least four free parameters (Δx(y), Δz, Z, Γ). In the superposition model (Eq. 2) this rises to roughly double plus the weights w_i. Several spectra, notably S1 and S2 at ambient pressure (Fig. 2d,e) and Fig. S7, are a single featureless peak 5–15% above background. Such a shape cannot constrain both a gap ratio and Z and Γ.
- *IR exclusion relies on simulations, not fits.*
  - The Au simulation (Fig. S6a) uses Γ = 0.01 meV. The fitted Γ ≈ 0.4 meV at ambient pressure exceeds the simulated gap, and would wash out the peak splitting that is used to exclude Au.
  - B1u is excluded only by the absence of side dips, but S1 and S2 at ambient pressure show no side dips.
- *Requested.* For each representative spectrum (ambient S1–S3; 0.2, 0.6, 0.8, 1.2 GPa), please provide:
  - best fits with Au, B1u, B2u/B3u, and an s-wave/BTK plus broadening baseline, all with the same parameter freedom, together with χ² or an information criterion;
  - parameter covariances or χ² maps in the (Δz/Δx, Z, Γ) space;
  - a statement of which parameters were fixed. In Fig. S8, Γ = 0.10 meV and Z ≈ 1.20 at all temperatures, which suggests they were constrained.
  - for the superposition fits, the number of components, the w_i values, and the sensitivity of ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ to the number of components.

**3. The fitted gap magnitudes are inconsistent with a physical gap.**

- *The values.* At ambient pressure Δx(y) ≈ 1.15–1.18 meV, with Tc = 2.1 K (Table S1, Fig. S2c). This gives 2Δ/k_BTc ≈ 13. At 1.2 GPa, Δz¹ ≈ 1.6 meV (Fig. S9c), giving 2Δ/k_BTc ≈ 12.
- *Comparison.* These values are roughly four times the weak-coupling value shown as the dashed line in Fig. S2c. They are also well above gap scales from STM and other spectroscopies on UTe2 (a few tenths of a meV).
- *Why it matters.* A fitted "Δ" this large most likely absorbs non-spectroscopic broadening, such as heating, spreading resistance or multiple parallel contacts. If so, the ratio of two such fitted quantities is not obviously a measure of the pz/px(y) pairing weights.
- *Requested.* Please reconcile these magnitudes with literature values and with the observed ZBCP widths. Then show whether the extracted ratio survives when the absolute gap is constrained to physically reasonable values.

**4. The logic linking the ratio to distinct phases needs strengthening.**

- *A ratio within one IR is not a symmetry label.* Δz/Δx(y) within a single representation is not fixed by symmetry and can vary continuously with pressure. A change from about 0.6 (SC1) to about 1.3 (SC3) is therefore not, by itself, evidence that SC1 and SC3 are distinct phases. This is especially so because the Discussion itself suggests SC1 (by comparison with STM, Ref. 34) and SC3 may both be B3u.
- *A sharp change needs a sharp data set.* The "abrupt increase" in the Fig. 5a inset rests on about seven pressures from one junction, with Z and Γ also changing. More points near 0.3 GPa, or reproduction in a second pressurized junction, are needed.
- *The proposed B2u → B3u transition is thermodynamically non-generic.* A second-order transition below an existing B2u state into a different IR normally produces a mixed state (B2u + B3u or B2u + iB3u), not a replacement. Moreover, in the authors' own model B2u and B3u give identical (0 0 1) spectra in zero field. It is then unclear why the onset of a B3u component should produce a minimum in the ratio.
- *Requested.*
  - Simulate a two-IR state whose second component grows below T_SC3. Show that it reproduces the minimum in Fig. 5f and Fig. S9e,f.
  - Alternatively, show that the minimum cannot arise from fit-parameter trade-offs. Note that near Tc both gaps are small and the error bars are very large (Fig. S9e, at about 2.5 K).
  - Note the non-monotonic Δx(y)(T) at 0.8 GPa: it rises from 0.87 to 1.00 meV between 0.5 and 1.1 K (Fig. S8). This should be discussed.

**B. Should address**

**5. Model assumptions do not match UTe2's electronic structure.**

- *Fermi surface.* The BTK model assumes a single spherical Fermi surface, linear-in-k basis functions, a unitary d-vector, and specular reflection.
  - UTe2 has quasi-2D Fermi cylinders along c (Ref. 39, which includes co-authors). For (0 0 1) tunneling these carry small v_z.
  - The kz-odd gap on a cylinder is a lattice harmonic (for example sin kz c), not kz.
  - These differences directly affect the weighting of channels in the k∥ sum of Eq. (1), and hence the extracted Δz/Δx(y).
- *Other effects not considered.*
  - The large Fermi-velocity/mass mismatch between Au and heavy-fermion UTe2.
  - The polished (non-cleaved) (0 0 1) surface, where roughness breaks specular reflection.
  - Non-unitary or chiral combinations proposed in Refs. 16 and 24–25 (for example B3u + iAu), which are excluded by assumption.
- *Requested.* At minimum, discuss how a cylindrical Fermi surface would alter the simulated spectra and the meaning of the ratio. Ideally, test it.

**6. Using peak amplitude to infer px(y) dominance at ambient pressure is not robust.**

- The statement that "G_NS < 1.2 implies px(y) dominance" neglects the strong suppression of the Andreev signal generic to heavy-fermion point contacts. Conductance enhancements of only a few to about 15% are typical even for clean contacts on CeCoIn5 and similar materials.
- It also neglects parallel normal channels and the normalization choice. The normalization of G_NS to G_N, and the background used at each pressure, should be stated.
- The ambient-pressure conclusion currently leans heavily on this amplitude argument, plus the satellite structure of S3 alone. Please weaken the claim or support it independently.

**7. Field dependence could discriminate B2u from B3u but is not exploited.**

- The paper states that B2u and B3u are indistinguishable on (0 0 1). That holds only in zero field.
- With H ∥ a, the ABS from the kz-odd component carries d ∥ x̂ for B2u and d ∥ ŷ for B3u. A Zeeman field should split or shift the ABS differently in the two cases.
- The existing 8 T, H ∥ a data at ambient pressure (Fig. S2a) show no splitting. This should be analyzed within an extended model including Zeeman coupling.
- Measurements under pressure with H ∥ a, and ideally H ∥ b, in SC2 and SC3 would directly test the proposed SC2 = B2u, SC3 = B3u assignment. Field-driven crossings of phase boundaries at fixed T would also test whether the ratio changes at those boundaries.

**8. The superposition model and pressure characterization need more detail.**

- *The two-channel interpretation.* The double-dip spectra are attributed either to multiple gaps or to pressure inhomogeneity (Eq. 2). These two interpretations are physically very different and are not distinguished.
- *Pressure calibration is missing.* The Methods give no:
  - manometer (Pb or Sn Tc, or ruby);
  - pressure at low temperature versus at loading;
  - estimate of the pressure gradient.
  These determine whether domain superposition is plausible.
- *1.4 GPa is unexplained.* The 1.4 GPa spectrum, near the MO boundary, is shown but neither fit nor discussed. Magnetic-scattering zero-bias anomalies, dismissed at ambient pressure because there is no order, become relevant near the MO1/MO2 phases. They need to be addressed explicitly there.

**9. The resistive transition and the onset of PCS features should be compared.**

- The ambient resistive transition is unusually broad for an RRR ≈ 410 crystal: the onset is 2.6 K but zero resistance is reached at 2.1 K (Fig. S1a inset).
- The ZBCP appears at about 2.5 K (Fig. 2c), i.e., it tracks the onset rather than bulk Tc.
- Please:
  - comment on the origin of the 2.6 K onset (strain, inhomogeneity, surface);
  - consider whether the polished and then annealed surface (epoxy cured at 423 K) probes a region with different Tc or pairing;
  - plot the PCS onset temperature versus pressure against T_c^0(p) and T_c^on(p).
- Possible interfacial reactions should also be discussed, since gold tellurides exist; avoiding Ag does not remove all Te-reaction concerns.

**10. Reproducibility under pressure.**

- Only one contact on one sample was measured under pressure.
- The main pressure trends need to be reproduced at least once: the onset of side dips above 0.3 GPa, the minimum in the ratio, and the double dips. This could be a second contact, a second sample, or a pressure-release run returning to SC1 behavior.
- Please also state whether each pressure point required warming to room temperature, and how the contact changed after thermal cycling.

**C. Minor**

13. "Unambiguously suggesting the presence of pz component" is overstated given concerns 1–3 and the authors' own caveat about Au.
14. Fig. S2c: "strongly violate the BCS prediction" refers mainly to magnitude, not functional form. Please compare the normalized Δ(T)/Δ(0) shapes.
15. Fig. S9 caption should define Δ¹ and Δ² and the weights used at 1.2 GPa.

    - the Fermi function exponent lacks parentheses;
    - Eq. (1) and Eq. (S12) should make explicit the normalization by the normal-state conductance at the same Z.
17. Reference and typographical errors:
    - Ref. 1 journal name ("Pep. Rrog. Phys.");
    - Ref. 24 year (should be 2020);
    - "Supplementray", "spits", "differentiate conductance".

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a junction-characterization section: R_N(p), contact-size estimate, the dI/dV method, and a direct test separating spectroscopic dips from critical-current dips (scaling with R_N across contacts, and field dependence under pressure).
2. Replace the qualitative IR screening with a quantitative model comparison. Fit all IRs and a conventional baseline to every key spectrum with equal freedom. Report χ² values and parameter covariances, and constrain gap magnitudes to physically reasonable values.
3. Extend the theory with a Zeeman term, and with two-IR mixed states (B2u + B3u, B2u + iB3u), on a quasi-2D Fermi surface. Use the existing and new H ∥ a / H ∥ b data to discriminate B2u from B3u directly, rather than relying only on the NMR argument.
4. Reproduce the pressure trends on at least one additional junction or sample, with denser pressure points near 0.3 GPa. Include a pressure-release check that recovers SC1-type spectra.
