# Review of "Multiple nodal superconducting phases and order-parameter evolution in pressurized UTe₂"

## Summary of the submission

The authors report point-contact spectroscopy (PCS) on the (001) surface of molten-salt-flux-grown UTe₂ (RRR ≈ 410), at ambient pressure and under hydrostatic pressure up to 1.4 GPa.

**Ambient-pressure results.** They observe a zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP) in three samples. They interpret it as a zero-energy Andreev bound state (ZABS), which requires a p_z (sign-changing under k_z → −k_z) component of the gap.

**Pressure results (one sample, S1).**
- Above ~0.3 GPa the ZBCP grows and develops symmetric side dips.
- At ≥1.0 GPa a "double-dip" structure appears.

**Analysis.**
- The authors use an extended BTK model for unitary triplet states with a spherical Fermi surface.
- They argue that B₂u/B₃u best reproduce the spectra, while A_u and B₁u are disfavoured.
- They propose the ratio ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩ as a single parameter distinguishing SC1 (~0.6) from SC3 (~1.0–1.3).
- At fixed pressure the ratio has a minimum in temperature that tracks the literature SC2–SC3 boundary.
- Combining this with ¹²⁵Te NMR [31], they suggest SC2 = B₂u and SC3 = B₃u.

## Overall assessment

Symmetry-sensitive spectroscopy inside the pressure-induced multiphase region of UTe₂ is genuinely missing from the literature, and the data set is valuable. Particular strengths:
- The low-temperature spectra are clean.
- The ambient-pressure ZBCP is reproduced in three samples.
- The pressure-induced change in spectral line shape is striking.
- The theoretical framework is laid out in reasonable detail in the SI.

However, the step from line shapes to order-parameter symmetry, and especially to the quantitative ratio ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩, rests on a highly simplified model and a multi-parameter fit whose uniqueness is not demonstrated. Several well-known extrinsic PCS mechanisms are not adequately excluded, and there are internal inconsistencies. In my view the central claims are currently stronger than the evidence supports. **I would recommend major revision.**

## Major concerns

### 1. Side dips are a classic signature of critical-current/thermal-regime artefacts

The paper's key pressure-dependent feature is the pair of side dips. However, symmetric conductance dips flanking a central peak are the textbook signature of contacts outside the ballistic (Sharvin) regime, where the local current density exceeds the critical current (Sheet et al., PRB 69, 134507, ref. [42] in the manuscript itself).

The 1.4 GPa spectrum (Fig. 3d) looks especially suspicious: a broad flat-topped peak with sharp dips at ±2 mV. The authors' exclusion of heating effects is directed only at the ambient-pressure ZBCP, not at the side dips. It is also weak in its own right:
- The argument that "a small field (<2 T) only weakly affects its width" is not discriminating. With H ∥ a, H_c2 is several times larger, so 2 T barely suppresses the critical current.
- No contact resistances, estimated contact diameters, or comparison with the mean free path (Wexler formula) are given anywhere. These are essential to establish the transport regime.

The authors should:
- report R_N for every junction and pressure;
- show whether the dip bias scales with R_N or with an I_c(T)-like temperature dependence (a dip at fixed current rather than fixed voltage);
- ideally show spectra from contacts of different resistance under pressure.

Without this, the side dips cannot be assigned to ABS-induced depletion of the bulk continuum.

### 2. The junction itself is likely changing with pressure

All pressure data come from a single contact on a single sample. The fitted junction parameters change substantially:

| Condition | Z | Γ (meV) |
|---|---|---|
| Ambient | ≈ 1.67 | ≈ 0.39 |
| 0.8 GPa | ≈ 1.20 | ≈ 0.10 |

A four-fold reduction in Dynes broadening is hard to attribute to the bulk superconductor. It more plausibly reflects mechanical reconfiguration of the Au-epoxy contact under pressure. Fig. S6d shows that the side dips themselves appear or disappear largely as a function of Γ. It is therefore possible that the "emergence" of side dips above 0.3 GPa is driven by the junction getting cleaner, rather than by an increase in Δ_z/Δ_x.

Related points:
- The thermal-expansion and compressibility mismatch between cured Au epoxy and UTe₂ will create local non-hydrostatic stress at the contact. UTe₂ is known to be highly sensitive to uniaxial stress.
- The authors themselves invoke "pressure inhomogeneity" to explain the double dips, which concedes this point.

I would ask for:
- a second sample/contact measured under pressure;
- a decompression run showing recovery of the ambient spectrum;
- a description of how pressure was calibrated (manometer, low-temperature value).

### 3. Uniqueness and robustness of the fits

The extracted ratio is the paper's central quantitative result. Yet each spectrum is fitted with Δ_x, Δ_z, Z and Γ, and with several channels plus weights wᵢ at ≥1.0 GPa. Strong correlations between Δ_z/Δ_x, Z and Γ are expected; ZBCP height and side-dip depth are both controlled by all of them.

The quoted uncertainties, typically ±0.01 meV in Fig. S8, look like statistical errors from the minimiser and almost certainly underestimate the true uncertainty. Please provide:
- χ² maps or confidence regions in the (Δ_z/Δ_x, Γ) and (Δ_z/Δ_x, Z) planes;
- fits at fixed Γ and Z across pressures, to test whether the pressure evolution survives;
- a statement of how many channels are used at each pressure, and how the number was chosen (e.g. an information criterion).

The averaged ratio defined in Eq. (3) is ad hoc. If the double dips arise from distinct domains, a weight-averaged ratio is not obviously a physical order-parameter property.

### 4. Implausibly large gap magnitudes

The fitted gaps imply very large coupling ratios:

| Condition | Gap | Coupling ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Ambient | Δ_x(y) ≈ 1.18 meV, T_c = 2.1 K | 2Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 13 |
| 1.2 GPa | Δ_z ≈ 1.6 meV | similarly large |

Fig. S2c makes the scale explicit: the BCS reference curve starts at ~0.32 meV, a factor ~3.7 below the fitted value. This is far beyond what specific heat implies for UTe₂. It also disagrees by roughly a factor of 3–5 with STM gap scales (~0.25–0.4 meV). In addition, the gaps fall almost linearly from 0.3 K.

At 0.8 GPa (Fig. S8), Δ_x(y) is non-monotonic in temperature, rising from 0.87 meV at 0.5 K to 1.00 meV at 1.1 K. This is unusual within a single phase and deserves comment.

Together these suggest that the fitted "Δ" may be absorbing contact-related broadening or spreading-resistance effects rather than measuring the bulk gap. This must be reconciled with thermodynamic and STM data before the extracted parameters can be regarded as physical.

### 5. Model simplifications undermine the quantitative Δ_z/Δ_x extraction

**Fermi surface.** The BTK calculation assumes a spherical Fermi surface (k_z² + k_∥² ≈ k_F²). UTe₂ has quasi-2D, cylinder-like Fermi sheets extended along k_z, as shown by ref. [39], whose author is on this paper. For (001) tunnelling:
- the distribution of k_z, of Fermi velocities normal to the interface, and of the nodal geometry on the actual Fermi surface differ qualitatively from the sphere;
- the c-axis velocity is small;
- the ZABS weight relative to the continuum will depend strongly on Fermi-surface geometry.

Since the paper's main parameter is a relative weight of k_z- vs k_x(y)-linear components, the absence of a realistic Fermi surface is a serious limitation. At minimum, the authors should show that the qualitative trends survive a cylindrical or tight-binding Fermi surface.

**Other model limitations.**
- Only linear-in-k basis functions are kept. Higher harmonics can change nodal structure and therefore ABS spectra.
- Spin–orbit coupling and multiband effects are absent.
- Fermi-velocity mismatch between Au and heavy-fermion UTe₂, expected to be enormous, is absorbed into Z without discussion.

### 6. The "peak height < 2 ⇒ p_x(y)-dominant" inference is not robust

Reduced ZBCP amplitudes in PCS are ubiquitous. Typical causes include:
- spreading resistance;
- parallel non-superconducting channels;
- inelastic broadening;
- finite temperature.

The fit attributes the low ambient peak height (~1.15) primarily to Δ_z < Δ_x, but large Γ (Γ/Δ ~ 0.3–0.6) plays an equally important role. The reproducibility of Δ_z/Δ_x across S1–S3 is encouraging, but the same model degeneracies apply to all three.

### 7. Phase assignment: the side dips do not cleanly separate SC2 from SC3

According to the authors, side dips and a large ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x⟩ characterise SC3. However, the data show otherwise:
- At 0.8 and 1.0 GPa, side dips persist up to ~2.9 K (Figs. 3c, S3b, S8), well within SC2.
- The fitted ratio in SC2 is ≥1 and rises toward T_c, reaching ~1.9 at 0.6 GPa (Fig. S9e). This makes SC2 at least as p_z-weighted as SC3.

The claimed "minimum" in the ratio rests on modest variations. At 0.8 GPa, computing from Fig. S8, the ratio goes 1.26 → 1.18 → 1.08 → 0.98 → 1.03 → 1.14. The upturn near T_c occurs where both gaps are small and the ratio is poorly constrained, as the large error bars in Fig. S9e–f show.

Agreement of the minimum with the literature SC2–SC3 line is suggestive but not compelling. I recommend that the authors:
- tone down the statement that this "is more likely indicative of an underlying phase transition"; or
- provide an independent signature at the same contact, such as a kink in the individual Δ_α(T) or a change in the field response.

### 8. The B₂u → B₃u scenario needs theoretical justification

The authors acknowledge that B₂u and B₃u are indistinguishable on (001). The SC2 = B₂u / SC3 = B₃u assignment therefore comes entirely from the NMR results of ref. [31], not from PCS.

More importantly, a lower-temperature transition below a single-IR phase generically yields a mixed state (e.g. B₂u + iB₃u or B₂u + B₃u) rather than a switch from one pure IR to another. This multicomponent picture is advocated in ref. [30]. The authors should discuss mixed-IR states and whether their model could distinguish them; for example, B₂u + iB₃u breaks time-reversal symmetry and modifies the ABS spectrum.

### 9. Alternative origins of the ZBCP are only partially addressed

**Magnetic scattering.** This is dismissed because UTe₂ does not order at ambient pressure. However, Kondo/Appelbaum-type zero-bias anomalies arise from local moments or magnetic impurities without long-range order. This explanation also becomes more relevant near the MO phases under pressure.

The strongest argument against an extrinsic origin is that the ZBCP onset coincides with superconductivity. Even this has a caveat: at ambient pressure the hump appears at 2.5 K, above the bulk zero-resistance T_c⁰ = 2.1 K and close to the unusually high T_c^on = 2.6 K.

**Unusual resistive transition.** A 2.1–2.6 K transition width is unusual for ultraclean molten-salt-flux crystals, which typically show sharp transitions near 2.1 K. Two possibilities should be discussed:
- a strained or modified surface layer from fine polishing, or from epoxy curing at 423 K;
- filamentary superconductivity.

**Other mechanisms.** Proximity-induced reflectionless tunnelling and intergranular Josephson effects in multi-particle Au-epoxy contacts should also be mentioned and excluded.

### 10. A missed opportunity: field-orientation dependence

ABS in triplet superconductors respond to a Zeeman field in a way that depends on the orientation of **d**. In principle this is exactly what could separate B₂u (d ⊥ ŷ) from B₃u (d ⊥ x̂) on a (001) contact.

Only H ∥ a is measured, and only at ambient pressure (Fig. S2a). A quantitative analysis of the ZBCP evolution in field, ideally along a and b and under pressure, would be far more symmetry-discriminating than the zero-field line shape.

## Internal inconsistencies and errors

1. **Table S1 column labels appear swapped.**
   - The table lists S1 with Δ_z = 1.18 meV and Δ_x(y) = 0.72 meV.
   - Fig. S7a, Fig. S2c, Fig. S9a and the main-text conclusion (Δ_z/Δ_x ≈ 0.57, p_x(y)-dominant) all require the opposite assignment.
   - The same swap applies to S2 and S3.
2. **Fig. 1b vs Fig. 1c.** The nodal gap drawn in Fig. 1b, with nodes at the poles connected by a vertical q_N, does not obviously correspond to a p_z gap, which has an equatorial line node. The relation between I, q_N and the ZABS condition should be clarified.
3. **Table I, "Spin component" column.** The column lists the axis missing from **d** (e.g. x̂ for B₃u, whose d = Δ_z k_z ŷ + Δ_y k_y ẑ). This should be labelled explicitly, e.g. "d ⊥" or "spin polarisation of equal-spin pairs", because the NMR argument in remark (2) depends on it.
4. **Heating argument.** The sentence "G_NS decreases by forming a peak centering about V = 0" is confusing and should be rephrased.
5. **Overstated wording.** "Unambiguously suggesting the presence of p_z component" is both internally contradictory and too strong given the concerns above.
6. **Ref. [24].** The year should be 2020, not 2002.
7. **Ref. [1].** "Pep. Rrog. Phys." should be "Rep. Prog. Phys."
8. **Typos.**
   - "Supplementray"
   - "spits into two peaks"
   - "Albeit of"
   - "constraints on" (should be "constrains")
   - "differentiate conductance" (Fig. 1 caption)
   - "presentation" → "representation" (SI V)
9. **Lost punctuation.** Dashes are missing in "point-contact spectroscopy (PCS) a technique well-suited…".
10. **Eq. (S10).** Parentheses are missing in the exponent of the Fermi function.
11. **Data availability.** "All source data … available within the paper" is inadequate for fitted spectra. Please deposit raw G(V,T,p) data and fitting code.

## Suggestions to strengthen the work

- Report R_N, contact size and transport regime for all contacts. Add at least one more contact under pressure, plus a decompression check.
- Demonstrate fit uniqueness with confidence maps. Test whether the pressure trend in Δ_z/Δ_x survives with Z and Γ held fixed.
- Repeat key simulations with a realistic quasi-2D Fermi surface. Include Fermi-velocity mismatch and a mixed B₂u + iB₃u state.
- Reconcile gap magnitudes with STM and specific-heat data, or reinterpret the fitted energy scale.
- Add field-dependent spectra (H ∥ a, b, c) under pressure, as a direct d-vector probe.
- Moderate the conclusions about SC1–SC3 being "mutually independent" and about a B₂u → B₃u switch. Present these as hypotheses consistent with, but not established by, the PCS data.

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The experimental observation of a pressure-tuned evolution of the (001) PCS spectrum in UTe₂ is interesting and potentially important. In its current form, however, the paper does not exclude extrinsic origins of the side-dip features or junction changes under pressure. Nor does it establish that the extracted ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩ is a robust, model-independent quantity. Addressing points 1–5 in particular is necessary before the symmetry conclusions can be considered supported.
