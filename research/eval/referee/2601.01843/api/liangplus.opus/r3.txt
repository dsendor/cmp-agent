Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports point-contact spectroscopy (PCS) on the (001) surface of ultraclean UTe₂ under hydrostatic pressure up to 1.4 GPa. A zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP) evolves with pressure and develops side dips and, at higher pressure, double-dip structures. The authors fit these spectra with an extended BTK model and argue for B₂u/B₃u pairing. They propose that the ratio ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ tracks the SC1, SC2 and SC3 phases. A symmetry-sensitive probe of the pressure-induced superconducting phases of UTe₂ would be valuable, since almost no such data exist (only NMR at 1.2 GPa). The dataset itself is novel. However, the chain from spectral shape, to model parameters, to irreducible representation and phase identity currently has several weak links, set out below.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- This is, to my knowledge, the first PCS dataset on UTe₂ across the SC1, SC2 and SC3 regions, taken on a high-quality crystal (RRR ≈ 410) with resistivity measured on the same sample.
- The qualitative pressure evolution is clear and visually striking (Fig. 3, Fig. S3). A modest ZBCP becomes a ZBCP with sub-unity side dips, then a double-dip structure. Zero-bias conductance exceeds 2 at 0.8 GPa (Fig. 5d).
- The ambient-pressure ZBCP is reproduced on three samples with different contact parameters (Fig. 2d–f). The fitted ratio Δz/Δx(y) ≈ 0.57 is reasonably consistent across them.
- A full extended-BTK formalism for unitary triplet states is given in the SI. The qualitative distinctions between px(y)-like and pz-like spectra (Fig. 4, Fig. S5) are pedagogically useful.
- The temperature of the ratio minimum appears to coincide with the literature SC2–SC3 boundary (Fig. 5a). If it proves robust, this is an intriguing observation.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **Pressure evolution may reflect contact evolution rather than order-parameter evolution.**
   - *What is missing.* All pressure data come from a single soft contact on sample S1. The fitted contact parameters change substantially between ambient and 0.8 GPa:
     - Z falls from 1.67 to about 1.20.
     - Γ falls from 0.39 to 0.10 meV (Table S1, Fig. S7, Fig. S8).
   - *Why it matters.* The authors' own simulations show that lowering Γ at a fixed ratio Δz/Δx = 0.55 produces pronounced side features (Fig. S6d). Raising Z at ratio 1 produces sub-unity side dips (Fig. S6c). The statement that the 0.6 GPa dips "can only be reproduced by increasing the weight of pz component" (p. 8) is not demonstrated. The central claim (a pz weight that rises with pressure and distinguishes phases) then rests on an unverified separation of contact and intrinsic parameters.
   - *Requested analyses.*
     - Fit the 0.6 and 0.8 GPa spectra with the ratio fixed at the ambient value (≈0.57–0.64), leaving Z, Γ and the overall gap scale free. Report χ² relative to the free fit.
     - Provide χ² maps or confidence regions in the (Δz/Δx, Γ, Z) space, to expose parameter correlations. Error bars of ±0.01 meV in Fig. S8 seem implausibly small for such a correlated four-parameter fit.
     - State whether any parameters were held fixed versus temperature.
   - *Requested experiments.* Show the spectrum after pressure release, to test whether the contact returns to its ambient form. Ideally, measure at least one additional contact or sample under pressure.

2. **Extrinsic (critical-current or thermal-regime) origins of the ZBCP and especially of the side dips are not excluded.**
   - *Why the concern arises.*
     - Sub-unity dips at finite bias that deepen on cooling are a well-known signature of the junction or local current reaching the critical current (Sheet et al., ref. 42).
     - So is a broad flat-topped peak flanked by sharp dips, as at 1.4 GPa (Fig. 3d).
     - The text's rule-out of heating (p. 5) addresses only the ambient ZBCP. It is qualitative ("<2 T only weakly affects its width", with no width-vs-field plot), and no field dependence is shown under pressure at all.
   - *What is missing.* The manuscript does not report:
     - the normal-state contact resistance of any contact;
     - an estimate of contact size (e.g., via a Wexler analysis using the mean free path at each pressure);
     - which transport regime the contact is in.
   - *Further warning signs.* The fitted gaps are anomalously large. At ambient pressure Δx(y) ≈ 1.18 meV for T_c ≈ 2.1 K, i.e., Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 6.5, roughly 3.7 times the BCS value plotted in Fig. S2c. At 0.8 GPa Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 3.7. A bias scale stretched by series or spreading resistance or by heating would produce exactly this, and would also distort the line shape that the IR analysis relies on.
   - *Requested data.*
     - Contact resistances for all contacts.
     - Dip position versus T and versus H at 0.6–1.4 GPa, to test whether dips track a gap-like or a critical-current-like dependence.
     - Spectra from contacts of different resistance at a fixed pressure.
     - A direct comparison of feature energies with T_c at each pressure.

3. **The discrimination among irreducible representations is not quantitative and depends on model truncations.**
   - *Au.* Au is excluded mainly by one simulation with isotropic Δx = Δy = Δz = 0.25 meV and Γ = 0.01 meV (Fig. S6a). The fitted Γ values (0.1–0.4 meV, i.e., Γ/Δ up to ~0.5) would wash out the predicted splitting. An anisotropic Au state, which the authors themselves invoke on p. 10, is never fitted.
   - *B₁u.* B₁u is excluded because it "lacks a component that changes sign upon kz → −kz". That is only true after dropping the symmetry-allowed kx·ky·kz ẑ term (the paper acknowledges dropping higher-order terms in Table I and on SI p. 10). Including this term would allow dispersive Andreev bound states on (001).
   - *Fermi surface.* The model uses a single spherical Fermi surface. UTe₂ has quasi-2D cylindrical sheets along c (ref. 39, which shares co-authors). For such sheets, c-axis tunneling and the effective kz-odd gap weight differ qualitatively, so the quantitative Δz/Δx ratio is model-dependent.
   - *Requested analyses.* Fit the ambient and pressurized spectra with Au (anisotropic), B₁u (including the cubic term) and B₂u/B₃u, all on the same footing, and report goodness of fit. Assess at least qualitatively how a quasi-2D Fermi surface modifies the conclusions. Until then, "unambiguously suggesting the presence of pz component" (p. 4) should be softened.

4. **The SC2→SC3 interpretation exceeds what the data and model can support.**
   - *(a) PCS cannot detect the proposed change.* By the authors' own statement, B₂u and B₃u are indistinguishable for an (001) junction. The proposed B₂u → B₃u change therefore rests entirely on NMR (ref. 31). For that inference, the field direction of the NMR measurement and the field dependence of the SC2/SC3 boundary should be stated explicitly.
   - *(b) A wholesale IR switch conflicts with Landau theory.* At a second-order transition (bulk calorimetry shows a second anomaly) between phases of different IRs, the low-T phase generically retains the high-T component. The expected low-T state is a two-component state such as B₂u + iB₃u, which may be non-unitary. It would not replace {pz, px} with {pz, py}. The BTK formalism explicitly assumes d × d* = 0 (SI p. 6), so the model used to fit SC3 cannot represent the most natural SC3 candidate. This also bears on ref. 30 (shared authors), which reports multicomponent pressure-induced superconductivity.
   - *(c) The minimum is weak and the high-T upturn may be a fitting artifact.* The ratio minimum at 0.6 and 1.2 GPa is ~0.1 deep, comparable to the error bars (Fig. S9e,f). The upturn above the minimum occurs where Δ approaches Γ and k_BT, the error bars grow, and the fits become weakly constrained. At 0.8 GPa the minimum is driven by Δx(y) *increasing* with temperature from 0.87 to 1.00 meV (Fig. S8a–c). Such non-monotonic gap behaviour needs scrutiny before it can be attributed to a phase transition.
   - *(d) The boundary comparison uses other samples.* The comparison is to boundaries measured on other samples (refs. 27, 30), and the authors' own resistivity cannot see the SC2/SC3 line.
   - *Requested work.* Model a two-component state. Quantify the significance of the minimum, for example by a fit with fixed Z and Γ and bootstrap errors. Ideally, obtain an in-situ bulk ac-susceptibility or calorimetry signature of the boundary on the same crystal and in the same cell.

**B. Should address**

5. **SC1 versus SC3 distinctness is not established.**
   - *Literature.* In several published phase diagrams (refs. 27, 29, and Braithwaite et al.), the low-T phase above ~0.3 GPa connects continuously to the ambient phase. The dashed SC2/SC3 line in Fig. 2b indeed looks like a continuation of the SC1 T_c.
   - *What the data show.* The paper's evidence is a ratio that rises from 0.64 (0.2 GPa) through ~0.75 (0.3 GPa) to ~1.03 (0.6 GPa), sampled at only four pressures below 0.6 GPa (Fig. 5a inset). This does not distinguish a phase boundary from a continuous evolution of a single phase.
   - *Requested work.* Use a denser pressure grid around 0.2–0.5 GPa. Look for a discontinuity, or coexistence of both spectral types, or temperature-dependent switching at 0.3 GPa, where Fig. S3a already shows additional structure.

6. **The superposition model (Eqs. 2–3) is under-specified.**
   - *Missing information.* Weights w_i are not reported. The number of channels and the per-channel free parameters (apparently Δx, Δz, Z, Γ and w per channel, about 9 parameters at 1.2 GPa) are not stated. Fits at 1.0 GPa (part of the inset of Fig. 5a) and at 1.4 GPa are not shown.
   - *Interpretation.* If the cause is pressure inhomogeneity across domains, then ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ is an average over different local states, and its physical meaning is unclear. The weighted-average-of-gaps definition is also not equivalent to a weighted average of ratios.
   - *Requested additions.* Report all parameters with uncertainties, show the residuals, and justify the number of channels, for example with an information criterion.

7. **Gap magnitudes and the BCS comparison are misinterpreted.**
   - *The comparison.* Fig. S2c compares fitted Δ(T) with an absolute BCS curve of ~0.33 meV and concludes the data "strongly violate BCS … demonstrates the unconventional nature" (p. 8). A gap 3–4 times BCS is not evidence for unconventional pairing. As noted in concern 2, it more likely signals that the fit parameters are not literal gap energies.
   - *The temperature dependence.* The near-linear Δ(T) from 0.3 K is also unusual. So is the observation that the ZBCP persists to 2.5 K while the fitted gaps extrapolate to zero near T_c⁰ = 2.1 K.
   - *Requested changes.* Compare normalized Δ(T)/Δ(0) curves. Discuss the magnitudes against independent spectroscopic gap estimates (e.g., STM, refs. 26 and 34). Remove or qualify the conclusion drawn from the BCS comparison.

8. **Surface condition and local T_c are uncontrolled.**
   - *Surface preparation.* The (001) face is not a natural cleave and was mechanically polished, then covered with epoxy cured at 423 K. Polishing damage, surface oxidation, multiple micro-facets (which could admit Andreev bound states from other orientations), and strain from differential thermal contraction can all alter surface pairing. This matters particularly because UTe₂'s T_c and phase diagram are strain-sensitive.
   - *Sample transition.* The resistive transition is unusually broad for molten-salt-flux crystals (T_c^on = 2.6 K, T_c⁰ = 2.1 K; Fig. S1a), and the ZBCP appears at 2.5 K, above T_c⁰.
   - *Requested additions.* Report specific heat (or equivalent bulk T_c) on S1. Characterize the polished surface (e.g., roughness and orientation by XRD or Laue). Discuss whether the PCS probes a strained or damaged surface layer.

9. **Pressure determination and hydrostaticity are not described.**
   - *What is missing.* The Methods do not state how pressure was calibrated (manometer type, low-T versus room-T values) or whether pressures in Fig. 3 and Fig. 5 are low-temperature values. Daphne 7373 is solid at low temperature, so the stated "quasi-hydrostatic" conditions imply some anisotropic stress.
   - *Why it matters.* Pressure gradients are already invoked to explain the double dips, so they should be quantified, for example through the width of the manometer transition.

10. **ZBCP height is used as a criterion for px(y) dominance.** The argument that peak values below 1.2 imply px(y) dominance at ambient pressure (p. 8) depends on normalization and on contact effects. Parallel non-superconducting channels, spreading resistance, and non-ballistic transport all reduce amplitudes. S2, with Z = 0.76, and S1, with Z = 1.67, give similar ratios, which is encouraging. Even so, the authors should state how G₀ is determined and discuss how robust the height criterion is.

11. **Several claims are overstated.**
    - "Unambiguously suggesting" (p. 4) conflicts with the later admission that Au cannot be excluded (p. 9).
    - "The multiple SC phases can be distinguished by a single parameter" is not borne out for SC2 versus SC3. These are distinguished only by the temperature dependence of the ratio, and the ratio values in SC2 (1.0–1.9) overlap those in SC3.
    - The ambient-pressure magnetic-scattering rule-out ("UTe₂ does not exhibit magnetic ordering") is weak, because Kondo-like zero-bias anomalies need local moments, not order. The disappearance of the ZBCP near T_c is the better argument and should be stated as such.

**C. Minor**

12. Table S1 has the Δz and Δx(y) column headers swapped relative to Fig. S7a and Fig. S9a, where Δx(y) = 1.18 meV and Δz = 0.72 meV. As printed, the table implies a ratio greater than 1, contradicting the 0.57(5) quoted in the text.
13. On p. 8 the 0.6 GPa ratio is quoted as ≈1.03, while Fig. S9e shows ≈1.1 at 0.3 K. Clarify which temperature and fit are meant. Also state the 0.8 GPa value at 0.3 K explicitly in the text.
14. The Methods should specify:
    - the lock-in modulation amplitude (relevant to broadening);
    - the two- versus four-probe configuration;
    - how G₀ is normalized;
    - which fit parameters are free or fixed;
    - how uncertainties are estimated.
    The data-availability statement should provide the raw spectra.
15. Typographical and formula errors:
    - Eq. (3) is garbled as typeset.
    - SI p. 6 has k_F² = √(2mε_F/ħ²); it should read k_F = √(2mε_F)/ħ.
    - The symbol ξ is used for both the kinetic energy operator and √(E² − |d|²).
    - Fig. S9c has a garbled legend.
    - Spelling: "spits" should be "splits", and "Supplementray" should be "Supplementary".
16. Fig. 1a caption: it is quasiparticles, not Cooper pairs, that are reflected. Also note that for B₂u/B₃u the Andreev bound states are generally dispersive in k∥ (zero energy only for a subset of k∥) because the two d-components lie along different spin axes. Calling them a ZABS should be qualified.
17. The "spin component" column in Table I should be defined, e.g., as the spin direction of the equal-spin pairs, i.e., the direction perpendicular to d.
18. References: ref. 1 journal name ("Pep. Rrog."); ref. 24 year (2002 → 2020); "Table 1" versus "Table I"; "seeing Fig." → "see Fig.".
19. Fig. S2a shows fields up to 8 T along a. Relate this to H_c2∥a of these crystals, and quantify ZBCP width and height versus field rather than describing them qualitatively.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Use field orientation to separate B₂u from B₃u.** Measure the ZBCP under fields along a, b and c on the (001) contact. Zeeman coupling to the d-vector splits or shifts the Andreev bound states differently depending on whether d lies in the ac plane (B₂u) or the bc plane (B₃u). Under pressure, this would directly test the proposed SC2 (B₂u) versus SC3 (B₃u) assignment without relying on NMR.
2. **Add a second junction orientation.** Measure on (100), (010) or the natural (011) cleave, at least at one pressure in SC2/SC3. As the authors acknowledge, this is needed for the B₂u/B₃u assignment and would also test whether Au or B₁u are excluded.
3. **Strengthen the pressure measurements experimentally.** Include an in-situ bulk probe (ac susceptibility coil or ac calorimetry) in the same cell as the point contact. Use two or more contacts of different resistance at each pressure. Record spectra on pressure release. Together these would separate intrinsic from contact-driven changes and pin the SC2/SC3 boundary on the same crystal.
4. **Make the model more realistic.** Extend it to a quasi-2D multiband Fermi surface with lattice harmonics such as sin(k_z c), include symmetry-allowed higher-order terms, and add non-unitary two-component states (e.g., B₂u + iB₃u). Present the IR comparison as a quantitative model selection, reporting χ² and parameter correlations, rather than as visual agreement.
