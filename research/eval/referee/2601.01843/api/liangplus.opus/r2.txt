**Review outline:**

## 1. Significance and novelty

The paper reports point-contact spectroscopy (PCS) on the (0 0 1) surface of UTe₂ under hydrostatic pressure up to 1.4 GPa, a regime where almost no symmetry-sensitive probes exist. It interprets a zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP) and pressure-induced side dips with an extended spin-triplet BTK model. From this it argues for a B₂ᵤ/B₃ᵤ order parameter and proposes the ratio ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩ as a single parameter distinguishing SC1, SC2 and SC3. If the spectra are intrinsic and the model is adequate, this would be the first spectroscopic evidence that the pressure-induced phases differ in order-parameter structure, which would be significant.

The central inferences, however, rest on:
- one junction measured under pressure;
- a spherical-Fermi-surface BTK model applied to a quasi-2D Fermi surface;
- fitted gap values that look anomalously large;
- side-dip features that have well-known extrinsic origins.

None of these is yet adequately addressed.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- PCS data under pressure are technically difficult to obtain, and the phase diagram covers SC1, SC2, SC3 and the edge of MO1/MO2 in one sample (Figs. 2b, 3, S3).
- The ZBCP at ambient pressure is seen in three samples (Fig. 2d–f).
- A clear, systematic change in spectral shape with pressure is visible in the raw data (Fig. 3 contour plots).
- The extended BTK formalism for unitary triplet states with spin-resolved Andreev channels is documented in useful detail (SI IV).
- The simulations of the four D₂h representations (Fig. S6) are a helpful screening tool.
- The observation that the fitted ratio has a minimum near the literature SC2–SC3 boundary (Fig. 5a, f; Fig. S9) is intriguing and testable.

## 3. Concerns

### A. Must address

**1. The junction regime is not established, and the side dips (the key evidence for increased p_z weight) have a standard extrinsic explanation.**
- *What is missing.* The paper gives no junction resistance R_N for any contact, no estimate of contact diameter versus mean free path (Sharvin/Wexler), and no discussion of whether the Au-epoxy "soft" contact is a single ballistic contact or many parallel contacts.
- *Why the heating rebuttal (p. 5) is insufficient.* Conductance dips flanking a ZBCP are the textbook signature of the critical-current/thermal regime (Sheet et al., ref. 42). The argument offered — gradual change with T and weak field dependence below 2 T — does not test this. The diagnostic is whether the dip voltages track I_c(T, H) and scale with R_N, rather than tracking Δ(T).
- *Fig. 3d (1.4 GPa).* Deep dips at about ±2 mV with a flat-topped central feature look very much like critical-current dips. The dip bias there lies well above the fitted gaps of about 1 meV.
- *Why it matters.* The authors state that the dips "can only be reproduced by increasing the weight of p_z" (p. 8). This is the evidential core of the SC1→SC3 claim. If the dips are extrinsic, the inferred ratio evolution does not follow.
- *What to do.*
  - Report R_N for each spectrum and each pressure.
  - Plot dip position versus T and versus H at fixed pressure, and compare it with Δ(T) from the fits and with an I_c(T)-type dependence.
  - Show spectra from junctions of different R_N on the same pressurized sample.
  - Estimate the contact size against the mean free path (RRR 410 implies a long bulk ℓ, but ℓ at a polished surface may be much shorter).

**2. Spectral changes under pressure are not separated from changes of the junction itself; the fit parameters are likely strongly correlated.**
- *Single junction.* All pressure data come from one junction on sample S1.
- *The junction parameters change a lot.* Between ambient pressure (Table S1/Fig. S7: Z ≈ 1.67, Γ ≈ 0.39 meV) and 0.8 GPa (Fig. S8: Z ≈ 1.2, Γ ≈ 0.10 meV), Γ drops by a factor of four and Z changes substantially. In a piston-clamp cell the epoxy contact is mechanically compressed, so junction evolution with pressure is expected.
- *Γ alone can produce satellites.* The authors' own Fig. S6d shows that lowering Γ at fixed Δ_z/Δ_x = 0.55 generates satellite peaks and sharpens the ZBCP.
- *Why it matters.* The growth of the ZBCP from ~1.15 to ~2.1 and the appearance of structure could therefore be driven largely by the junction (Z, Γ, effective normal-channel dilution) rather than by the order parameter.
- *What to do.*
  - Refit the 0.6 and 0.8 GPa spectra with Δ_z/Δ_x fixed at the ambient value (~0.57) while Z, Γ and the overall gap scale are free. Report χ², and show χ²(Δ_z/Δ_x) profiles or parameter correlation matrices.
  - Show reversibility: return to low pressure and recover the SC1-type spectrum with the same junction.
  - Ideally, repeat the pressure series with a second sample/junction.
  - Explain how G₀ normalization was done at each pressure.

**3. The model assumptions behind both the representation screening and the meaning of Δ_z/Δ_x are not justified for UTe₂.**
- *Fermi surface.* The BTK formulation (SI, p. 6) assumes a single isotropic band with k_z² + |k_∥|² ≈ k_F². UTe₂ has quasi-2D cylindrical Fermi sheets along c (ref. 39, co-authored by members of this team). For c-axis transport, the range of k_z/k_F, the Fermi velocity along c, and the angular weighting of channels all differ qualitatively from the spherical case.
- *Physical meaning of the ratio.* The quantity Δ_z/Δ_x is a ratio of coefficients of linear basis functions. On a cylinder, the actual gap from the k_z term is set by the k_z extent of the Fermi surface. The ratio is therefore model-dependent and cannot be compared with other probes.
- *B₂ᵤ vs B₃ᵤ degeneracy.* The equivalence of B₂ᵤ and B₃ᵤ on (0 0 1) (Fig. S6 caption) is an artifact of the rotational symmetry of the spherical model.
- *Exclusion of Aᵤ.* This rests on one simulation with Δ_x = Δ_y = Δ_z (Fig. S6a). The authors themselves note (p. 10) that an anisotropic Aᵤ can "blur into" quasi-B_nu. Aᵤ is therefore not excluded in any meaningful region of parameter space.
- *What to do.*
  - Repeat the key simulations and at least the 0 and 0.8 GPa fits with a realistic quasi-2D (tight-binding) Fermi surface, or justify why the spherical model is quantitatively adequate.
  - Map the Aᵤ parameter space (e.g., varying Δ_y/Δ_x and Δ_z/Δ_x) to show which anisotropic Aᵤ states are excluded.
  - State the ignored higher-order terms (e.g., k_xk_yk_z for B₁ᵤ) and show that they cannot generate side dips.

**4. The fitted gap magnitudes are implausibly large and internally inconsistent across the paper.**
- *Size.* At ambient pressure, Δ_x(y) ≈ 1.18 meV with T_c⁰ = 2.1 K gives 2Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 13. Fig. S2c itself shows a weak-coupling BCS curve at ~0.33 meV, a factor of ~3.5 below the data. STM gap features in UTe₂ are typically a few hundred μeV, and the authors' own simulations in Fig. S6a–c use Δ = 0.25 meV.
- *Likely interpretation.* A factor of this size usually points to a voltage-scale problem — series/spreading resistance in a non-ballistic or multi-contact junction, or a sample-bulk contribution — rather than to strong coupling. This links directly to Concern 1.
- *Internal inconsistency.* Table S1 lists Δ_z = 1.18(7) meV and Δ_x(y) = 0.72(4) meV for S1. Fig. S7a and Fig. S9a list the reverse, and the text quotes Δ_z/Δ_x(y) ≈ 0.57. The table columns appear swapped; read literally, the table would imply p_z dominance at ambient pressure, the opposite of the paper's claim.
- *Physically odd trends.* At 0.8 GPa, Δ_x(y) increases from 0.87 meV (0.5 K) to 1.00 meV (1.1 K) (Fig. S8). This non-monotonic single-gap temperature dependence is suggestive of fit degeneracy.
- *What to do.*
  - Address the gap magnitude explicitly, for example by correcting for lead/spreading resistance and showing the effect on Δ.
  - Correct Table S1.
  - Discuss the Δ_x(T) behavior.
  - The statement that the gaps "strongly violate BCS" should rest on the shape of Δ(T), not on comparison with a weak-coupling magnitude.

### B. Should address

**5. The claim of a phase transition at the SC2–SC3 boundary, drawn from the minimum of ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩(T), is weakly supported.**
- *Sparse sampling.* At 0.8 GPa there are about six temperature points (Fig. S8). The "minimum" at 1.5 K is defined by essentially one point (ratio ≈ 0.98 vs ≈ 1.03 at 1.8 K).
- *Near-T_c behavior.* The upturn toward T_c is a ratio of two shrinking, poorly constrained gaps, and the error bars grow accordingly (Fig. S9e, f).
- *Smoothness.* The variation is smooth. Nothing in the data distinguishes a transition from a crossover or a fitting artifact, despite the statement on p. 9.
- *Boundary from other samples.* The SC2–SC3 boundary in Fig. 5a is taken from other samples and studies (refs. 27, 30), with possibly different pressure calibration and T_c.
- *Theoretical consistency.* A second-order B₂ᵤ→B₃ᵤ transition would generically produce a B₂ᵤ + (i)B₃ᵤ two-component state in SC3 (cf. ref. 30, "multicomponent"). The single-IR fitting model used on both sides cannot describe this.
- *What to do.*
  - Take denser temperature scans at 0.6, 0.8 and 1.2 GPa.
  - Determine the SC2–SC3 boundary in the same cell, e.g., with an AC susceptibility coil.
  - Fit SC3 with a two-component (B₂ᵤ + B₃ᵤ) order parameter as an alternative to Eq. (2).

**6. The multi-channel superposition at ≥1.0 GPa is under-constrained, and the averaged ratio is not well defined physically.**
- *Overfitting.* Eq. (2) with two channels adds roughly nine free parameters (two sets of Δ_x, Δ_z, Z, Γ plus a weight). Fitting a "double-dip" shape with this freedom is close to guaranteed.
- *Eq. (3).* The weighted average of gaps has no clear meaning in a multigap scenario.
- *Pressure inhomogeneity argument.* This is not convincing for Daphne 7373 at 1.2 GPa, which remains liquid at room temperature in this range.
- *Unexplored behavior in Fig. S9c.* The two channels at 1.2 GPa have nearly the same Δ_z/Δ_x but differ by an overall scale factor of ~0.6. This deserves comment: it suggests a scale (inhomogeneity or contact-geometry) effect rather than distinct gaps.
- *Missing pressure point.* The 1.4 GPa spectra (Fig. 3d) are not fitted at all.
- *What to do.* Report χ² per degree of freedom, show single-channel fits for comparison, and discuss 1.4 GPa.

**7. The B₂ᵤ (SC2) / B₃ᵤ (SC3) assignment is not supported by the PCS data, and a field-orientation test is available.**
- *Current basis.* PCS on (0 0 1) cannot distinguish B₂ᵤ from B₃ᵤ in this model, so the assignment (p. 10) rests entirely on the NMR of ref. 31.
- *A possible discriminant.* Surface Andreev bound states of triplet superconductors generally respond anisotropically to Zeeman fields depending on d-vector orientation. Field along **a** versus **b** could therefore discriminate between the representations.
- *Data already in hand.* Fig. S2a shows the ZBCP at ambient pressure with no splitting up to 8 T along **a**.
- *What to do.*
  - Discuss what the model predicts for **H** ∥ a in B₂ᵤ vs B₃ᵤ, including Zeeman and Doppler (screening-current) effects.
  - Present field-dependent spectra in SC2 and SC3 under pressure, ideally for two in-plane field directions.
  - Until then, soften the assignment in the abstract and summary.

**8. Surface preparation and sample homogeneity under the contact are not characterized.**
- *Surface.* The (0 0 1) face was "fine-polished" (Methods). UTe₂ does not naturally cleave on (0 0 1), and polishing an air-sensitive heavy-fermion compound may leave a damaged or strained layer. This undermines the specular-reflection assumption (Fig. 1a), which is essential for ABS formation and for the absence of diffuse-scattering artifacts.
- *Onset temperature.* The resistive T_c^on = 2.6 K is well above T_c⁰ = 2.1 K and above the T_c typical of ultraclean molten-salt crystals. The PCS hump already appears at 2.5 K (Fig. 2c), i.e., above bulk T_c⁰. This suggests the contact may probe a strained or epoxy-stressed region.
- *What to do.* Comment on the origin of the 2.6 K onset, specify whether the transport contacts were also Au epoxy, and give any characterization of the polished surface.

**9. Non-superconducting zero-bias anomalies near the magnetic phases are not ruled out under pressure.**
- *Scope of the current argument.* The magnetic-scattering argument (p. 5) applies only at ambient pressure.
- *Why it matters at high pressure.* At 1.2–1.4 GPa the system is adjacent to MO1/MO2 with strong magnetic fluctuations. Kondo/Fano-type zero-bias anomalies are common in heavy-fermion point contacts.
- *What to do.* Show normal-state spectra just above T_c at each pressure and field-suppression data under pressure. Note that 8 T may be insufficient given the enhanced H_c2 under pressure.

**10. Pressure determination is not described.**
- *What is missing.* The Methods give no manometer (Pb/Sn T_c, ruby, etc.), no statement of whether pressures are low-temperature values, and no uncertainty.
- *Why it matters.* The SC1/SC3 boundary at 0.3 GPa and the overlay with literature boundaries in Fig. 5a depend directly on this.
- *What to do.* Report the manometer, the temperature at which pressure was determined, and the uncertainty.

**11. Several statements overstate the evidence.**
- "Unambiguously suggesting the presence of p_z component" (p. 4) is not justified given Concerns 1–3.
- A zero-energy ABS indicates a sign change on reflection, not nodes per se (p. 6, "high probability of nodes").
- The "abrupt increase" from SC1 to SC3 (Fig. 5a inset) rests on very few pressures between 0.2 and 0.6 GPa. Finer pressure steps across 0.3 GPa are needed to support a discontinuity.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Reproducibility and reversibility under pressure.** Perform a second pressure run with several junctions of different R_N, including a depressurization step, and report R_N and contact-size estimates throughout. This single addition would address Concerns 1, 2 and 4 most directly.
2. **Field-orientation dependence.** Measure field dependence along **a** and **b** in SC1, SC2 and SC3, together with the corresponding model predictions for B₂ᵤ vs B₃ᵤ bound states. This could turn the representation assignment from an NMR-based inference into a PCS result.
3. **Realistic Fermi surface and uncertainty analysis.** Re-do the BTK analysis with a quasi-2D tight-binding Fermi surface, report parameter correlations and χ² profiles for Δ_z/Δ_x, and include a fixed-ratio null-hypothesis fit.
4. **Boundary in the same cell, and two-component fits.** Locate the SC2–SC3 boundary in the same cell (AC susceptibility) with dense temperature scans, and test a two-component B₂ᵤ + B₃ᵤ order parameter in SC3 as an alternative to the domain-superposition model of Eq. (2).
