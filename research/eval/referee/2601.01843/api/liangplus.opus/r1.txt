Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports point-contact spectroscopy (PCS) on the (001) face of UTe2 under hydrostatic pressure up to 1.4 GPa. It is, to my knowledge, the first symmetry-sensitive spectroscopic probe applied across the SC1/SC2/SC3 regions of the pressure phase diagram.

The authors observe:
- a zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP) at all pressures;
- side dips above about 0.3 GPa;
- double dips at 1.0–1.2 GPa.

They interpret these as a zero-energy Andreev bound state (ZABS) from a k_z-odd gap component. Using an extended BTK model, they argue for B2u/B3u pairing and claim that the ratio ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ distinguishes the three SC phases, including a minimum in its temperature dependence that tracks the bulk SC2–SC3 boundary.

If the spectra are intrinsic, this would be a valuable constraint on the order parameter and its pressure evolution. At present, however, the inference chain from raw spectra to "multiple nodal phases of distinct symmetry" has several weak links.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The experiment addresses a real gap: very few symmetry-sensitive probes exist for pressurized UTe2, and PCS in a clamp cell is technically demanding.
- The crystals are high quality (RRR ≈ 410), and the resistive phase diagram (Fig. 2b, Fig. S1) reproduces the literature, including MO1/MO2.
- The spectra change systematically with pressure (Fig. 3, Fig. S3). A ZBCP is reproduced in three samples at ambient pressure (Fig. 2d–f).
- The extended BTK formalism for unitary triplet states is laid out in reasonable detail (SI IV). Simulations for all four D2h odd-parity irreducible representations (IRs) are provided (Fig. S6).
- Most intriguing is the apparent correspondence between the minima of ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩(T) and the bulk SC2–SC3 line (Fig. 5a). If it survives scrutiny, this would be an interesting result.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

**1. The junction regime is not established, and the side dips have a well-known extrinsic explanation.**
- The central new spectral feature is the pair of side dips above 0.3 GPa (Fig. 3b–d, Fig. S3, Fig. 5c–e). The paper attributes them to bulk-continuum depletion by surface bound states.
- Sharp symmetric dips flanking a zero-bias peak are, however, the textbook signature of contacts leaving the ballistic regime. In that case the local current exceeds the critical current of the contact region (Sheet et al., PRB 69, 134507, the paper's own Ref. 42).
- The 1.4 GPa spectrum strengthens this worry: a broad, flat-topped peak bounded by sharp dips at about ±1.8 mV (Fig. 3d). The double dips at 1.0–1.2 GPa would also arise naturally from several parallel contact channels with different critical currents.
- The paper does not report:
  - the normal-state contact resistance R_N at any pressure;
  - an estimate of contact size (Sharvin/Wexler) versus the elastic mean free path, which should be long in RRR ≈ 410 crystals;
  - the effect of spreading resistance.
- The heating argument (p. 5) rests on the assumption that a thermal-regime peak would have a temperature-independent amplitude. This is not generally true, because the critical current itself is strongly T-dependent.
- The authors should:
  - (i) tabulate R_N versus pressure;
  - (ii) plot dip voltage V_dip(T) against both the fitted Δ(T) and an Ic(T)-like form;
  - (iii) show whether V_dip scales with R_N across different contacts or pressures;
  - (iv) give the field dependence of the dip positions at a fixed pressure in the SC2/SC3 regime. Critical-current dips move strongly and monotonically with field; ABS-related features should follow the gap.
- Without this, the side dips cannot be used to raise the p_z weight, and the claim that SC2/SC3 is p_z-dominant does not follow.

**2. Pressure-induced junction changes are not separated from order-parameter changes.**
- All pressure data come from a single contact on sample S1. The fitted junction parameters change substantially between ambient pressure and 0.8 GPa:
  - Z falls from 1.67 to about 1.2;
  - Γ falls from 0.39 meV to about 0.10 meV (Table S1, Fig. S8).
- The paper's own simulations (Fig. S6d) show that reducing Γ alone, at fixed Δz/Δx = 0.55, turns a featureless ZBCP into one with side features and satellite peaks. Sample S3 at ambient pressure, with Γ = 0.16 meV, already shows side humps (Fig. 2f).
- The qualitative change from SC1 to SC2/SC3 spectra may therefore reflect a junction that sharpened under pressure, with Δz/Δx simply re-parameterising this.
- The authors should:
  - fit the high-pressure spectra with Δz/Δx constrained to the ambient value (about 0.57), allowing Z and Γ to float, and report the change in χ²;
  - report a decompression or pressure-cycling check, i.e. whether the SC1-type spectrum returns at low pressure;
  - ideally, repeat the pressure series on at least one more contact or sample.

**3. The fits do not, as presented, discriminate between IRs or pin down Δz/Δx.**
- The exclusion of A_u rests on Fig. S6a, which uses Γ = 0.01 meV and Δx = Δy = Δz. The experimental fits require Γ ≈ 0.1–0.4 meV, at which the A_u peak splitting would plausibly be washed out.
- B1u produces a single ZBCP at small Z (Fig. S6b), which is all that the ambient S1/S2 spectra show.
- At ambient pressure, a single structureless peak about 15% high (Fig. 2d, Fig. S7) is fitted with four free parameters (Δx, Δz, Z, Γ). This is likely highly degenerate, yet uncertainties as small as ±0.01 meV are quoted.
- At 1.0–1.2 GPa, the two-channel superposition (Eq. 2) roughly doubles the parameter count and adds weights w_i.
- The authors should:
  - fit the same datasets with A_u (unequal Δi allowed), B1u and B2u at equal parameter count and report χ² for each;
  - show parameter correlation matrices or χ² maps in the (Δx, Δz) plane at fixed Z and Γ;
  - derive the error bars on ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ (Fig. 5a inset, Fig. 5f, Fig. S9d–f) from these, rather than from the local fit covariance.

**4. Model assumptions bias the extracted ratio.**
- The BTK model uses a single spherical Fermi surface, linear-in-k basis functions, and no Fermi-velocity mismatch.
- UTe2 has quasi-2D cylindrical Fermi sheets (Ref. 39, by co-authors). Tunnelling along c from such sheets samples k_z and v_z very differently from a sphere, and "k_z" should be a lattice-periodic function of k_z.
- The heavy-electron/Au velocity mismatch is known to strongly suppress Andreev signals in heavy-fermion PCS; amplitudes of 10–20% are typical.
- The argument that peak heights below 1.2 imply p_x(y) dominance (p. 8) is therefore not secure. The fitted ratio is a model-dependent quantity whose physical meaning (relative gap amplitude on which sheet?) is unclear.
- The normalisation in Eq. (1)/(S12) is also unclear. As written, the normal-state conductance for Z > 0 is not unity unless the result is divided by the angle-integrated normal-state transmission.
- The authors should:
  - state the normalisation explicitly;
  - include a velocity-mismatch parameter or justify omitting it;
  - at minimum, test how Δz/Δx changes for a cylindrical-Fermi-surface geometry.

**5. The phase identification from ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ is over-interpreted.**
- A ratio of basis-function amplitudes within one IR is not symmetry-fixed and can vary continuously with pressure. A change in the ratio between SC1 and SC2 therefore does not by itself establish distinct phases.
- The ratio minima are shallow compared with the error bars:
  - at 0.6 GPa the values run 1.10 → 0.97 → up to 1.9 with very large uncertainties (Fig. S9e);
  - at 1.2 GPa they run 1.30 → 1.18 → 1.58 (Fig. S9f).
- The upturn near Tc, where both gaps vanish, may simply reflect an ill-defined ratio as Δ → 0.
- The gaps themselves behave oddly. At 0.8 GPa, Δx(y) rises from 0.87 meV (0.5 K) to 1.00 meV (1.1 K) (Fig. S8), which needs comment.
- The (001) junction cannot distinguish B2u from B3u (Fig. S6). The PCS data therefore cannot support a B2u → B3u change at the SC2–SC3 boundary.
- A complete change of IR within a superconducting state is generically first order. A second, lower-T transition more naturally signals the onset of an additional component, e.g. B2u + iB3u or B2u + B3u, consistent with the multicomponent picture of Ref. 30. Such states are not simulated, and non-unitary states are excluded by assumption (d × d* = 0).
- The authors should:
  - quantify the significance of each minimum, e.g. via a fit with and without a kink;
  - simulate two-component states;
  - restate the conclusion as consistency with bulk boundaries rather than evidence of IR change.

**B. Should address**

**6. The gap magnitudes are implausibly large.**
- At ambient pressure, Δx(y) ≈ 1.18 meV with Tc0 = 2.1 K gives Δ/k_BTc ≈ 6.5, about four times weak-coupling BCS. The BCS curve in Fig. S2c sits near 0.3 meV.
- The authors should compare with STM gap scales on UTe2 (Refs. 26, 34) and with Ref. 47.
- Large apparent gaps together with large Γ are a common symptom of non-ballistic contacts or series resistance, which ties back to concern 1.

**7. Tables and figures disagree with each other.**
- Table S1 lists Δz = 1.18 meV and Δx(y) = 0.72 meV for S1. Fig. S7a and Fig. S9a give Δx(y) = 1.18 and Δz = 0.72, and the text quotes Δz/Δx(y) ≈ 0.57. The Table S1 column headers appear swapped for all three samples.
- The Fig. S1 caption assigns triangles to Tm1 and diamonds to Tm2, opposite to the labels in Fig. S1c and Fig. 2b.
- The text mentions minima at 0.6, 0.8 and 1.2 GPa. Fig. 5a shows an additional red circle near 1.0 GPa that is not discussed.
- No fits are shown for 0.3, 1.0 or 1.4 GPa, although 0.3 GPa defines the SC1/SC3 step in the Fig. 5a inset.

**8. The ambient-pressure ZBCP needs stronger controls.**
- The hump appears at 2.5 K, above Tc0 = 2.1 K, and the resistive transition is unusually broad (2.1–2.6 K) for ultraclean crystals. This suggests surface or strain inhomogeneity, possibly from fine polishing of the (001) face.
- The field data (Fig. S2a, H ∥ a, up to 8 T) should be analysed quantitatively: ZBCP height and width versus H/Hc2. Any splitting or Doppler shift expected for a ZABS with field in-plane should be discussed.
- A characterisation of the polished surface (e.g. X-ray or AFM roughness) would help.

**9. Pressure calibration and homogeneity are not described.**
- The manometer (Pb or Sn Tc?) and the pressure gradient are not stated.
- Pressure inhomogeneity is invoked to justify Eq. (2), so an estimate of the pressure distribution is needed.
- Denser pressure points around 0.3 GPa would test whether the SC1 → SC3 change in the ratio is abrupt, as the inset suggests.

**10. The NMR-based assignment needs explicit logic.**
- The SC2 = B2u / SC3 = B3u proposal (p. 10) depends on which Knight-shift component Ref. 31 measured and how K_i relates to d_i. Please state the field axis and the reasoning explicitly.
- Please also tone down wording such as "unambiguously suggesting" (p. 4) and "mutually independent phases" (p. 10), given concerns 3–5.

**C. Minor**

11. Eq. (3) is mistypeset (misplaced subscripts and superscripts in the denominator). In Eq. (1), the derivative should be with respect to eV with the correct sign, or written as −∂f/∂E.
12. On p. 6 of the SI, "k_z² + |k∥|² ≈ k_F² = √(2mε_F/ħ²)" should read k_F = √(2mε_F)/ħ.
13. Typos: "Supplementray", "spits" (splits), "differentiate conductance" (Fig. 1). Ref. 1 journal name is garbled. Ref. 2 should be Annu. Rev. Condens. Matter Phys. Ref. 24 year should be 2020.
14. Fig. 1: please clarify the relation between q_N, the node positions and the tunnelling direction for each IR. The current schematic (point nodes at the poles) does not obviously correspond to the B2u/B3u node locations for a (001) junction.
15. The data availability statement should point to a repository for raw spectra and fit code, given the central role of fitting.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a junction-characterisation section covering R_N versus pressure, contact-size versus mean-free-path estimates, V_dip(T,H) analysis, and at least one additional pressurised contact. This would convert the side dips from ambiguous to diagnostic.
2. Replace the qualitative IR screening with quantitative model comparison (χ² for A_u, B1u, B2u, and two-component states at realistic Γ). Use a more realistic Fermi-surface geometry and include velocity mismatch.
3. Present ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ as a phenomenological fit parameter with honest uncertainties. Frame the correspondence with the SC2–SC3 line as suggestive rather than as evidence of an IR change, unless concern 5 is resolved.
4. Measure PCS on a second facet, e.g. (100) or (010), at least at one pressure in SC2/SC3. This is what would actually separate B2u from B3u, as the authors themselves note.
