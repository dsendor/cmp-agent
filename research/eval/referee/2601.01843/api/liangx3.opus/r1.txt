# Merged review: "Multiple nodal superconducting phases and order-parameter evolution in pressurized UTe₂"

## A. Must address

**1. The spectroscopic regime is not established, and the side dips have not been tested against the critical-current/heating mechanism.**

The ZBCP and side dips are the paper's key evidence. Symmetric conductance dips flanking a central enhancement are the textbook signature of contacts in the intermediate/thermal regime, where the local current density reaches the critical current (Sheet et al., PRB 69, 134507, ref. 42; the authors cite it only for the ZBCP).

Several features of the data fit this picture:
- The dips appear only under pressure and deepen on cooling.
- At 1.4 GPa (Fig. 3d) there is a broad, flat-topped peak bounded by sharp dips at about ±2 mV that move inward with temperature. This is the characteristic critical-current pattern.
- The double dips at 1.0–1.2 GPa are naturally explained by several parallel micro-contacts with different critical currents. A soft Au-epoxy contact made from colloidal Au particles almost certainly comprises many such channels.
- The authors' own invocation of "multiple domains" (Eq. 2) to fit these spectra strengthens this concern.

The heating rebuttal on p. 5 is qualitative and incomplete. It rests on the gradual temperature degradation of the peak and a weak field effect below 2 T, and it is applied only to the ambient-pressure ZBCP, where no dips exist. It is never applied under pressure, where the dips appear.

The manuscript also lacks basic junction characterization:
- contact resistances R_N;
- raw I–V curves and un-normalized dI/dV over a wide bias range;
- the normalization and background-subtraction procedure;
- any spreading-resistance or series-resistance correction (relevant given ρ₀ of order μΩ·cm);
- a contact-size estimate (Sharvin/Wexler) compared with the elastic and inelastic mean free paths, which are long given RRR ≈ 410.

A multi-channel contact also undermines the single-channel, specular BTK model and the use of absolute peak heights (G/G₀ < 1.2 vs. > 2) as a quantitative discriminator.

The authors should:
- report R_N, a contact-size estimate and a ballistic/diffusive/thermal classification for every contact and pressure;
- plot side-dip positions versus T, H and R_N, and test whether they follow Δ(T) or an I_c(T) scaling;
- replot the spectra against bias current to test I_c scaling;
- include, ideally, a deliberately higher-R_N (closer to tunnelling) junction.

**2. The fitted gap magnitudes are implausibly large, which suggests the voltage scale is not spectroscopic.**

At ambient pressure, Δ_x(y)(0.3 K) ≈ 1.18 meV with T_c ≈ 2.1 K gives 2Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 13. At 0.8 GPa, Δ ≈ 1.0–1.1 meV, and at 1.2 GPa, Δ_z¹ ≈ 1.6 meV with T_c ≈ 3 K, both giving 2Δ/k_BT_c ≈ 10–12. These are roughly four times the weak-coupling values. They are also several times the gap scales from STM (~0.25 meV), thermal conductivity and penetration depth (~0.2–0.4 meV) in UTe₂.

Fig. S2c shows that the claimed "strong violation of BCS" is mostly a magnitude discrepancy, since the dashed BCS curve has Δ(0) ≈ 0.32–0.33 meV. The authors present this violation as evidence of unconventional superconductivity. Inflated gaps are far more naturally a symptom of non-ballistic broadening, heating, or spreading/series resistance absorbed into Δ by the BTK fit. In that case the extracted energy scales, and the ratio built from them, are not spectroscopic.

The authors should either justify these values physically, or apply spreading/series-resistance corrections and reconcile the gap scale with STM and thermodynamic estimates.

**3. Pressure-induced changes in Z and Γ may alone explain the new spectral features; ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩ is not shown to be independently determined.**

On the same contact, Z changes from ≈1.67 to ≈1.2 and Γ falls from ≈0.39 to ≈0.10 meV between 0 and 0.8 GPa, while the normalized ZBCP amplitude jumps from ~1.15 to ~2.1. The authors' own simulations show that side dips and satellites appear or vanish depending on Γ and Z, not only on Δ_z/Δ_x (Fig. S6c,d: "the shoulder deepens into satellite peaks as Γ is reduced"). The authors also state that dips are smeared by large Γ.

The emergence of dips, attributed to "increasing p_z weight", may therefore reflect a change in junction broadening, for example from epoxy compression or contact geometry evolving under pressure. The claim that the dips "can only be reproduced by increasing the weight of p_z" is not demonstrated.

The degeneracy is already severe at ambient pressure. There, four free parameters (Δ_z, Δ_x(y), Z, Γ) are fitted to a nearly featureless, Gaussian-like peak of ~15% height (Figs. 2d, S7). No correlation matrices, χ² landscapes, confidence regions or Bayesian posteriors are given.

The authors should:
- provide these analyses, especially the covariance of Δ_z/Δ_x(y) with Γ and Z;
- perform the critical null test: hold the ratio at its SC1 value, let Z and Γ vary freely, and show whether the 0.6–1.2 GPa spectra can or cannot be reproduced.

**4. All pressure data come from a single contact on a single sample, with no reversibility check.**

Every pressure spectrum is from one junction on S1. Pressure can change the epoxy, contact area, barrier and sample strain at each step, so intrinsic and junction-induced evolution cannot be separated. No depressurization run shows that the ambient-like spectrum returns. No second pressurized contact or sample is presented, and there is no variation of R_N at fixed pressure.

The authors should repeat the pressure series on at least one more sample or contact, preferably with different R_N, and include a pressure-release measurement.

**5. Table S1 contradicts the main text on which gap component is larger.**

Table S1 lists Δ_z = 1.18, 1.15, 1.15 meV and Δ_x(y) = 0.72, 0.62, 0.60 meV for S1–S3. Taken literally, this gives Δ_z/Δ_x(y) ≈ 1.6–1.9, i.e. p_z-dominant at ambient pressure. The main text (p. 8), Fig. S2c, Fig. S7a and Fig. S9a instead give Δ_x(y) = 1.18 and Δ_z = 0.72 meV for S1, with Δ_z/Δ_x(y) ≈ 0.57.

The paper's central argument is precisely the relative weight of p_z versus p_x(y), so this must be resolved unambiguously. The authors should correct Table S1, state clearly which component dominates in SC1, and cross-check all tabulated parameters against the figures.

**6. The BTK model's Fermi-surface and interface assumptions are too idealized for UTe₂ to support symmetry discrimination or give physical meaning to the ratio.**

The extended BTK (Eqs. S2–S9) assumes:
- a single isotropic spherical Fermi surface (k_z² + k_∥² = k_F²);
- linear-in-k basis functions;
- equal masses and Fermi velocities on both sides of the interface.

UTe₂ instead has quasi-2D cylindrical sheets along c with small v_z (Eaton et al., ref. 39, by co-authors), possibly a 3D pocket, and a very large Au/heavy-fermion Fermi-velocity mismatch. For (001) injection, transmission is dominated by the small-v_z portions of the Fermi surface. Both the k_∥-sum weighting and the effective Z therefore differ qualitatively from the spherical model.

Whether a k_z-odd component produces a robust (001) ZABS on cylindrical sheets is non-trivial. The B₂u/B₃u nodal positions are themselves Fermi-surface dependent, and the spherical/ellipsoidal sketches in Fig. 1 do not represent UTe₂. The ratio of basis-function coefficients is not a physical observable unless weighted over the real Fermi surface, so "p_z-dominant SC3" has no clear physical meaning.

The authors should recompute the spectra with a quasi-2D Fermi surface or a tight-binding model consistent with dHvA/ARPES, including velocity mismatch and higher-harmonic basis functions (e.g. k_x k_y k_z terms). They should then test whether the key discriminators survive: "ZBCP < 2 ⇒ p_x(y)-dominant", "side dips ⇒ larger p_z weight", and "A_u ⇒ split peak".

**7. The evidence that the ratio minimum marks the SC2–SC3 phase transition is statistically weak and partly circular.**

The SC2–SC3 boundary in Figs. 2b and 5a is reproduced from other samples and groups, not measured on S1. The claimed match with the ratio minima rests on three pressures with substantial scatter.

The minima themselves are poorly constrained:
- At 0.8 GPa the ratio goes roughly 1.25 → 1.0 → 1.3.
- At 0.6 GPa it is essentially flat (~1.05) below 1.6 K.
- The upturns are defined by points near T_c, where both gaps are small, the ratio is ill-conditioned and error bars grow dramatically (Fig. S9e,f).
- The 1.2 GPa minimum, at 0.7 K, lies in the region requiring the two-channel model.

A minimum in a ratio of two fit parameters does not imply a thermodynamic transition. A smooth redistribution between components or bands, or a junction-regime change, would also produce one. The statement that it is "more likely a phase transition than a crossover" is not supported by any kink analysis, raw-spectrum signature, or independent measurement on the same sample.

The authors should:
- measure ac susceptibility or calorimetry in situ on the same crystal and cell;
- look for model-independent signatures at the boundary in the raw spectra (kinks in peak height, dip position or zero-bias conductance versus T);
- analyse kinks or slope changes in the individual gaps;
- present the ratio with honest uncertainty bands, and test statistically whether a smooth crossover is distinguishable from the claimed minimum.

## B. Should address

**8. The exclusion of A_u and B₁u uses unequal and unrealistic simulation conditions.**

A_u is dismissed using an isotropic simulation (Δ_x = Δ_y = Δ_z) with Γ = 0.01 meV (Fig. S6a). The B₂u/B₃u simulations use Γ = 0.05 meV, and the experimental fits use Γ ≈ 0.1–0.4 meV. At realistic Γ, the A_u splitting (smaller than Δ) is likely smeared into a single peak. A strongly anisotropic A_u state, as suggested by NMR and thermal conductivity, is not explored. The authors themselves invoke "quasi-B_nu" anisotropic A_u in Remark (1) and concede it cannot be excluded.

B₁u is excluded only within the linear-k model and only by the absence of side dips, which are themselves of uncertain origin (concern 1). Moreover, B₁u also gives a ZBCP at small Z (Fig. S6b), and the ambient-pressure S1/S2 spectra show no side dips. Ambient-pressure data alone therefore cannot distinguish B₁u from B₂u/B₃u, contrary to the abstract's "for both ambient and pressurized UTe₂".

The authors should compare all candidate states under identical, experimentally relevant Γ, Z and anisotropy, using χ²/AIC. The candidates should include pure B₁u, B₂u/B₃u, anisotropic A_u with realistic Γ, and multicomponent states.

**9. The SC2 = B₂u / SC3 = B₃u assignment is not derived from the present data.**

The authors acknowledge that B₂u and B₃u give identical (001) spectra. The B₂u → B₃u change highlighted in the abstract and summary therefore rests entirely on Kinjo et al.'s NMR. The PCS data do not independently support a representation change.

The authors should:
- calculate the spectra expected on the (100), (010) and (0−11)/(011) surfaces, where B₂u and B₃u become distinguishable, as a falsifiable prediction;
- where feasible, measure on such facets, at least at ambient pressure and at one pressure in SC3;
- reframe the SC2/SC3 assignment as a hypothesis combined with NMR.

**10. Multicomponent, non-unitary and time-reversal-symmetry-breaking states are not modelled, and the nature of an inter-representation transition is not discussed.**

A transition between two different irreducible representations within the superconducting state implies either a first-order transition or an intermediate mixed state, for example a TRS-breaking B₂u + iB₃u state. Such a two-component state is the natural expectation within SC3 if SC2 and SC3 belong to different representations, and a cited work (Wu et al., PRL 2025) argues SC3 may be multicomponent. Yet only single-representation unitary states (d × d* = 0) are fitted, including in the coexistence region. This is internally inconsistent with the proposal.

Such states could also produce a non-monotonic temperature dependence of an effective ratio. The authors should explicitly model real and complex B₂u/B₃u mixtures (and mixtures with A_u) for the low-temperature SC3 region. They should discuss the thermodynamic nature of the SC2–SC3 line and engage more deeply with the theory and experiment on pressure-induced multicomponent pairing.

**11. The superposition model (Eqs. 2–3) adds unconstrained freedom, and its weighted ratio is not a property of a single order parameter.**

Eq. 2 is introduced exactly where single-channel fits fail (≥1.0 GPa). It roughly doubles the number of free parameters: several sets of (Δ_z, Δ_x, Z, Γ) plus weights w_i. With this freedom it can fit almost any multi-dip spectrum, so its success in Fig. 5e is not evidence for a particular pairing state.

The double dips are attributed either to multiple gaps or to pressure inhomogeneity across domains. These scenarios have very different physical implications but are fitted identically. Eq. 3, a weighted ratio of weighted sums, mixes junction-channel weights with order-parameter properties. The resulting value of ~1.3 at 1.2 GPa averages channels with different Z or Γ, and its continuity with the single-channel ratio at lower pressure is not meaningful.

The authors should limit and justify the number of channels and the w_i physically, show that single-channel fits truly fail, and either justify Eq. 3 or replace it with channel-resolved quantities.

**12. The ratio does not by itself distinguish SC2 from SC3, and its pressure dependence is sparsely sampled.**

The abstract claims the phases "can be distinguished by a single parameter". However, SC2 and SC3 both have ⟨Δ_z⟩/⟨Δ_x(y)⟩ ≈ 1–1.3. Within a single representation, Δ_z/Δ_x is a material parameter that can vary continuously with pressure, so its change does not by itself establish distinct phases.

The claimed "drastic" SC1 → SC3 change rests on ~7 points (Fig. 5a inset). It coincides with the pressures at which Z and Γ change and side dips appear.

The authors should acknowledge that the ratio's value does not separate SC2 from SC3, and add a denser pressure grid near 0.2–0.4 GPa and 1.2–1.5 GPa.

**13. Fit uncertainties appear unrealistically small, and the fixed/free status of parameters is not stated.**

Several gaps carry ±0.01 meV uncertainties (Fig. S8, Table S1). These are smaller than Γ, the thermal smearing (~25 μeV at 0.3 K) and any plausible systematic error, so they likely reflect statistical fit errors only. At 0.8 GPa, Γ = 0.10(1) meV and Z ≈ 1.20(1–2) are essentially constant over 0.5–2.1 K, suggesting they may have been fixed.

The ambient ratio is quoted as ≈0.57(5), while the individual samples give ≈0.61, 0.54 and 0.52. Because the claimed ratio minimum and its identification with a transition depend on these error bars, the authors should:
- state which parameters (Z, Γ) are fixed or free in each temperature series;
- explain how uncertainties were obtained;
- provide realistic confidence intervals (e.g. bootstrap, including systematic errors).

**14. The magnetic-field response is not analysed, and no field data are taken under pressure.**

Fig. S2a shows the ZBCP still visible at 8 T with H ∥ a at 0.5 K. This is near or above H_c2∥a for this T_c, and no Zeeman or Doppler splitting is seen. Neither observation is discussed. A peak surviving above the bulk H_c2 would argue against a purely superconducting ABS origin, or point to a surface region with different properties.

Under pressure no field data are shown, despite an 8 T magnet being available. SC2 and SC3 have very different H_c2 anisotropies and field responses, so such data would be a powerful discriminator. Field-induced ZBCP splitting or anisotropic suppression would also test ABS versus critical-current origin and the d-vector orientation.

The authors should present quantitative field dependence (peak height, width, any splitting) up to H_c2 under pressure and along several field directions. They should discuss the expected Zeeman/Doppler response of a triplet ABS for the relevant d-vector.

**15. A ZBCP above the bulk T_c and a broad resistive transition suggest a surface or strained layer with different properties.**

At ambient pressure the ZBCP appears at ~2.5 K, near T_c^on = 2.6 K and above the bulk zero-resistance T_c⁰ = 2.1 K. Yet the fitted gaps (Fig. S2c) close at ~2.1 K. The 0.5 K-wide resistive transition is unusual for MSF-grown "ultraclean" crystals with RRR ≈ 410, which typically show sharp transitions at ~2.1 K. This hints at inhomogeneity, or at a surface/strained layer produced by polishing or by epoxy curing at 423 K, with a different T_c contributing to the signal.

No specific heat or susceptibility is shown for the measured crystal. The authors should discuss the ZBCP onset together with the broad transition, distinguish bulk from surface or filamentary superconductivity, and characterize the crystal thermodynamically.

**16. The interface is assumed to be clean and specular, but this is not characterized.**

ZABS formation in the model requires specular reflection at a clean (001) interface. The surface was mechanically "fine-polished" and contacted with Au epoxy cured at 423 K. Roughness, a disordered layer or Te loss could strongly modify or suppress bound states.

The authors should characterize the surface. They should also assess the effect with a rough-interface or diffusive-surface treatment.

**17. Alternative origins of the ZBCP are not excluded.**

At ambient pressure the ZBCP is only ~15% of G₀. A peak of this size can also arise from reflectionless tunnelling, from multiple parallel contacts, or from proximity effects at a polished surface.

The argument against magnetic (Kondo/Appelbaum-type) zero-bias anomalies, namely "no ordering at ambient pressure", is insufficient for two reasons. UTe₂ has strong magnetic fluctuations and unknown near-surface stoichiometry after polishing. At 1.4 GPa, close to MO1/MO2, the argument no longer holds at all.

The authors should address each of these alternatives explicitly.

**18. The pressure calibration and hydrostaticity are not described.**

The pressure determination is not described: manometer type (e.g. Pb or Sn T_c, or manganin), calibration at low temperature, pressure gradients, and loss of pressure on cooling. Daphne 7373 solidifies at ~2 GPa at room temperature and at lower pressure at low temperature. Yet non-hydrostaticity is invoked only ad hoc, to explain the double dips.

The authors should describe the calibration, state the pressure uncertainty, and discuss hydrostaticity consistently.

**19. The conclusions are overstated.**

Phrases such as "unambiguously suggesting the presence of p_z component", "B₂u or B₃u as the most likely representation" and "spectroscopic evidence for the existence of multiple SC phases" go beyond what single-facet, single-contact BTK fits with a spherical-Fermi-surface model can support.

The abstract and conclusions should be rephrased, for example "consistent with" rather than "unambiguously". The text should acknowledge the limitations raised above.

## C. Minor

**20. Positioning relative to prior work should be sharper.**

Ambient-pressure (001) PCS has already been reported (Yoon et al., npj QM 2024), and a B₃u assignment at ambient pressure has been made by STM on the (0−11) facet (Gu et al., Science 2025). The novelty therefore rests mainly on the pressure evolution, which is the most model-dependent part of the analysis. The introduction and discussion should make this explicit.

**21. Editorial, labelling and data-availability issues.**

The following should be corrected:
- Ref. 1: "Pep. Rrog. Phys."
- Ref. 24: the year should be 2020, not 2002.
- Typos: "Supplementray", "spits", "differentiate conductance", and the "seeing Fig." phrasing.
- Column labels in Table S1.
- Inconsistent symbol labels in Figs. S2(c) and S9.

The data-availability statement should be expanded to include the raw spectra and the fitting code.
