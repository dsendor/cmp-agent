1. 1. The spectroscopic (ballistic/diffusive) nature of the contacts is not demonstrated

This underpins all claims. Heating and Kondo origins of the ZBCP are excluded only qualitatively, using the temperature dependence, weak field dependence below 2 T, and the absence of magnetic order at ambient. Several observations call for quantitative checks:

- **Missing contact data.** No normal-state contact resistance R_N is reported for any S1 spectrum. There is no Wexler/Sharvin estimate of the contact radius and no a/l ratio. The values 2.3E-07, 4.7E-07 and 6.0E-07 in Fig. 2 are unexplained. If they are contact areas or resistances, they should be defined and given for S1 at every pressure.
- **Gap-to-Tc ratio.** Gaps of about 1.2 meV with Tc0 = 2.1 K give 2Δ/kBTc ≈ 13. This is far above specific-heat and STM estimates and is not discussed. Such a value is a typical sign of spreading or series resistance stretching the bias axis in partially thermal or diffusive contacts.
- **Peak amplitude.** The measured G_NS at ambient is ≤1.2. The paper's own Fig. 1c says the ABS peak "readily exceeds 2" at large Z. The suppression is attributed implicitly to a large Dynes Γ (0.16–0.40 meV) without checking contact-regime effects.

**Requested.** The authors should:
- report R_N for each spectrum;
- compute the Wexler radius, R_N = 4ρl/(3πa²) + ρ/(2a), using ρ(T, P) measured on S1 and a stated estimate of the mean free path l;
- either correct the bias axis for spreading resistance or show that the extracted gap energies are independent of R_N across several contacts;
- show that all spectral structure vanishes at the bulk Tc(P) of S1;
- show raw, un-normalized G(V) above Tc at each pressure.

**Magnetic order under pressure.** The "no magnetic order" argument applies only at ambient. The paper's own resistivity on S1 (Fig. S1b,c) marks Tm1 and Tm2 of magnetically ordered phases MO1 and MO2 under pressure. The authors must state which PCS pressures, if any, lie within or near MO phases, and exclude magnetic-scattering ZBCPs there.

**Field data.** Fig. S2a appears to contain spectra up to about 8 T, yet the text cites only "<2 T." The full field dependence should be shown and compared with the anisotropic bulk Hc2(T). An ABS-derived ZBCP should disappear at Hc2. A Kondo-type peak should Zeeman-split and persist above Hc2. Ideally this should be tested with fields along more than the a axis and at more than ambient pressure.

2. 2. Side dips cannot be used as evidence of nodal character without excluding critical-current artifacts

The paper cites Sheet et al. (Ref. [42]), who show that dips in PCAR spectra commonly arise from the contact reaching the critical current. Yet it interprets side dips as intrinsic, nodal features (claim 5 and the title). Two further problems arise:

- **Γ dependence of the simulations.** In the paper's own simulations (Fig. S6c,d), B2u side dips at Γ = 0.40 meV are only "shallow, shoulder-like." They become pronounced only at small Γ. The experimental fits use Γ = 0.16–0.40 meV, so deep measured dips at these Γ values are more consistent with a non-spectroscopic origin.
- **Inconsistent onset.** The pressure at which dips appear is stated inconsistently. The summary says "above 0.3 GPa." The body describes 0.2 GPa as ambient-like, with dips "emerging" at 0.6 GPa.

**Requested.** Measure 3–5 contacts with R_N spanning about a factor of 5–10, at ambient and at two or more pressures (e.g. 0.6 and 1.2 GPa). Plot the dip bias against R_N, T and H. Dips whose position scales with R_N or with Jc(T, H) are artifacts. If this test cannot be done, side dips should be removed as evidence of nodes.

3. 3. All pressure conclusions rest on one sample, possibly one contact, with no reproducibility or stability checks

The pressure data come only from S1. The text does not state how many contacts were made, or whether the same contact survived all pressures. UTe2's pressure phase diagram depends strongly on the sample (growth method, Tc of 1.6–2.1 K). Over the pressure range, the peak height rises from ≤1.2 at ambient to 2.08 at 0.8 GPa. This is just as consistent with a change in contact transparency or contact reconstruction under load (the pressure medium solidifies, the clamp is reloaded) as with an intrinsic change in order parameter.

**Requested.**
- Repeat the pressure series on at least one more crystal, ideally with 2–3 contacts per pressure.
- Report Z, Γ and R_N as functions of pressure.
- Perform a pressure-release check: return to ambient and verify that the ratio returns to about 0.57.

**Pressure calibration.** The Methods give no in-situ manometer, such as the Tc of Pb or Sn. Without one, the pressures used to match literature phase boundaries are uncalibrated at low temperature. The 0.2 GPa (main text) versus 0.3 GPa (Fig. S3) labelling must also be resolved.

4. 4. The phase boundaries are not measured on the sample studied, so the agreement in claims 3 and 4 is partly circular

The SC1/SC2/SC3 boundaries in Figs. 2b and 5a are taken from other groups' AC calorimetry and susceptibility on different samples [27, 29, 30]. The authors' own resistivity on S1 shows only a single Tc dome. Nothing in the paper therefore establishes independently that S1 has multiple superconducting phases at the stated pressures. The ratio-minimum "critical temperatures" are then overlaid on a literature line called "a guide to eyes."

**Requested.** Measure AC calorimetry or AC susceptibility on S1, or on a piece from the same crystal, in the same cell. At minimum, rescale the pressure axis by matching Tc(P) of S1 to the literature samples, and state explicitly that the correspondence is circumstantial.

5. 5. The ratio minima are not shown to be statistically significant or robust, and "phase transition, not a crossover" is overstated

The minima at 0.6, 0.8 and 1.2 GPa underpin claim 4, but:

- **Too few points.** The temperature dependence at 0.8 GPa (Fig. S8) uses only six temperatures (0.5–2.1 K). Any minimum is therefore a dip of 0.05–0.1 in the ratio, defined by 1–3 points.
- **No uncertainties.** Neither Fig. 5 nor Fig. S9 defines error bars on the ratio or on the derived "critical temperatures."
- **Fit-pipeline artifact.** As T → Tc, Δ/Γ decreases and the two gaps become poorly constrained. Non-monotonic fitted ratios could therefore be an artifact of the fitting procedure.
- **Missing pressure.** Fig. S9 shows only 0, 0.6 and 1.2 GPa. The 0.8 GPa temperature dependence appears only in Fig. 5f, and the panel temperatures in Fig. S8 (panel e: 1.5 K vs 1.8 K) are inconsistent between text and caption.

**Requested.**
- **Error bars.** Show error bars on all ratio and critical-temperature points.
- **Dense sampling.** Measure densely through each minimum, e.g. 5–8 points within ±0.2 K.
- **Synthetic-data test.** Generate spectra from a smooth, monotonic ratio(T), using the fitted Z and Γ, the experimental noise and the experimental temperature grid. Refit them with the same pipeline and show that spurious minima do not appear.
- **Field tuning.** Show that the minimum follows the known field dependence of the SC2/SC3 boundary, e.g. at 0, 0.5 and 1 T at 1.2 GPa.

Even then, PCS alone does not distinguish a thermodynamic phase transition from a crossover. The wording should be softened unless thermodynamic evidence on the same sample is added.

6. 6. Irrep assignment lacks quantitative model comparison, and the parameters' identifiability and uncertainties are not established

The exclusions of competing irreps are visual:

- **Au.** It is excluded because it gives peak splitting or a zero-bias dip, yet the text later concedes Au cannot be excluded. The descriptions of the Au lineshape in the Fig. 1f caption and in the main text should also be made consistent.
- **B1u.** It is excluded because its ZBCP evolves into a zero-bias dip at large Z (per Fig. S6b). The main text should state this criterion consistently.
- **Other states.** No s-wave, non-unitary or chiral state (e.g. B3u+iB2u, relevant given Refs. [16, 24, 30]) and no two-band model is fitted. The unitary assumption d × d* = 0 is therefore untested.

**Requested.** Fit the same spectra with all these models, using the same normalization and the same treatment of Z and Γ. Report reduced χ², AIC/BIC and residuals.

**Identifiability.** With Γ/Δ ≈ 0.15–0.6, the parameters Z, Γ, Δz and Δx(y) are strongly correlated. Quoted uncertainties as small as ±0.01 meV have no stated method. The authors should provide:
- a correlation matrix;
- profile-likelihood or MCMC/bootstrap contours for (Δz, Δx(y)), (ratio, Γ) and (Z, Γ);
- systematic errors from the choice of normalization background and fitting window.

**Model assumptions.** The model assumes a single band, a spherical Fermi surface and specular reflection. UTe2's main Fermi-surface sheets are quasi-2D cylinders, which change the angular weighting of ABS for (001) injection substantially. The authors should refit with a cylindrical Fermi surface, partially diffuse reflection and a tunnelling-cone parameter. They should then show whether the pressure trend in the ratio survives. If only the trend is robust, the claims should be framed that way.

7. 7. The superposition model used above 0.8 GPa is underspecified, which weakens claim 3

Eq. (2), G = Σ w_i G_i, is introduced without stating:
- the number of channels;
- whether Z and Γ are shared between channels;
- whether Σ w_i = 1 is enforced;
- any criterion showing that a single-channel fit is statistically rejected.

The physical meaning of w_i (coexisting phases, pressure inhomogeneity or multiple bands) is left open, and the authors say "the exact origin of the double side dips remains elusive." The weighted ratio Σ w_iΔz^i / Σ w_iΔx(y)^i may therefore depend on the definition and be degenerate with respect to the w_i.

The notation ⟨·⟩ is also used at 0 GPa in Fig. S9d. It is unclear whether 0.6 GPa uses the single- or multi-channel model. The 1.4 GPa spectrum is shown in Fig. 3 but never fitted, even though it lies within the claimed SC3 region.

**Requested.**
- Specify the model fully.
- Fit 0.8 GPa with both models to demonstrate continuity.
- Report ΔBIC at 1.0 and 1.2 GPa.
- Show that the ratio is stable across the allowed w_i.
- Fit the 1.4 GPa spectrum or explain why it is excluded.

8. 8. The proposed SC2 = B2u → SC3 = B3u sequence is not supported by the PCS data

The paper states that B2u and B3u are indistinguishable on (001). The proposed sequence therefore relies entirely on external NMR. Two options would make this a PCS result:

- **Directional PCS** on (100), (010) or the natural (011) cleave surface, where ABS signatures depend on the node direction relative to the surface normal.
- **In-plane field orientation** on (001), with the field along a versus along b. The Doppler-shift response of the ABS depends on the node direction.

Otherwise, the assignment should be presented as speculative, consistent with the abstract's own hedged wording.

9. 9. "Nodal" (title and claim 5) is largely an input of the model, not a result

The linear-k B2u/B3u basis has point nodes by construction. The side dips are compromised (Comment 2). Independent evidence is needed:
- quantitative comparison with fully gapped alternatives (Au, s-wave, gapped non-unitary states);
- the low-temperature dependence of the zero-bias or sub-gap conductance (power-law versus activated) down to ≤Tc/10.

The paper should also address conflicting reports of a fully gapped ambient state (Suetsugu et al. 2024, Ref. [48]) and of single-component order (Theuss et al. 2024, Ref. [33]).

10. 10. The BCS-violation argument (claim 6) does not establish unconventional pairing

Deviation of the fitted Δ(T) from a single BCS curve (Fig. S2c) is common in multigap conventional superconductors. It is also sensitive to how Γ is treated; Γ is fixed at about 0.10 meV at 0.8 GPa but varies elsewhere.

**Requested.**
- Fit Δ(T) with Γ fixed and with Γ free.
- Include error bars.
- Compare with two-gap α-model and strong-coupling forms.
- Reconcile the result with 2Δ/kBTc ≈ 13 (see Comment 1).

11. 11. Internal inconsistencies that affect the central quantitative claim

- **Table S1.** It lists Δz = 1.18 meV and Δx(y) = 0.72 meV for S1, a ratio of 1.64. Fig. S7 and the main-text value of 0.57(5) require the opposite assignment. The columns appear to be swapped, and this affects the key ambient number.
- **Strength of claim 1.** The text describes the ABS/pz conclusion as unambiguous, while the abstract says "strongly suggests." The wording should be made consistent, and the conclusion should be hedged given Comments 1 and 6.
- **Pressure points.** The pressures shown (Fig. 3: 0.2, 0.6, 1.0, 1.4 GPa; Fig. S3: 0.3, 0.8, 1.2 GPa) differ from those fitted (0.2, 0.6, 0.8, 1.2 GPa). A table of all measured pressures, temperatures, R_N and fit parameters (Z, Γ, Δz, Δx(y), w_i) with uncertainties should be provided.
- **Figure labels.** Figs. S5(a)/(b) appear mislabelled. The panel temperatures in Fig. S8 are inconsistent between text and caption.

12. 12. Novelty should be positioned more precisely

Point-contact spectroscopy of UTe2 has been reported before (Yoon et al., npj Quantum Mater. 9, 91 (2024), Ref. [47]). Zero-energy surface states have already been used to constrain the pairing symmetry (Gu et al., Science 388, 938 (2025), Ref. [34]). The claim of novelty should be restricted explicitly to measurements under pressure. The paper should also discuss how its ambient results (ratio, irrep, nodal versus gapped) compare with those works.

13. **1. State the novelty explicitly and engage substantively with prior spectroscopy.**
- The main new element appears to be PCS under pressure. The paper should say so directly. It should also explain what PCS can determine that bulk probes under pressure (calorimetry, AC susceptibility, NMR) cannot.
- Prior ambient-pressure PCS by Yoon et al. (2024) on the same (001) facet (and on (0.4 0.6 0.7)) reported py-dominant pairing.
- Here, (001) gives "px(y)-dominant, with B2u and B3u indistinguishable." The authors call the two results "qualitatively consistent" in a single sentence without reconciling them.
- STM by Gu et al. (2025) on (0−11) favoured B3u. How this relates to the present ambient-pressure result also needs discussion.
- A dedicated comparison is needed. It should cover the gap magnitudes, the ratio values, and the surface preparation (polished here versus prepared differently in earlier work). It should state what is genuinely new.

14. **2. The independent contribution is narrower than the title and abstract imply.** By the authors' own account:
- B2u and B3u are indistinguishable on (001).
- The SC1/SC2/SC3 boundaries are taken from calorimetry and susceptibility (refs. 27, 29, 30). The Fig. 2b caption itself calls these "three possible phases."
- The SC2/SC3 irrep labels rely on ¹²⁵Te NMR by others (ref. 31, at 1.2 GPa).

What PCS independently provides is a model-dependent fitted gap ratio that changes with p and T, measured on one contact. The title ("multiple nodal superconducting phases") and the SC2 = B2u / SC3 = B3u assignment should be reframed so that the paper clearly separates conclusions drawn from its own data from conclusions imported from other work.

15. **3. The motivation is not connected to the findings.**
- The Introduction motivates the work through spin-triplet topology and Majorana modes. The Results never return to this.
- Please explain what B2u, B3u or a two-component SC3 state would imply. Examples include surface Majorana states on specific facets, Weyl nodes, or chirality and time-reversal symmetry breaking (TRSB).
- Please also explain why pressure-tuning between these phases matters for that question. Without this, the significance beyond cataloguing phases is unclear.

16. **4. Overstated and inconsistent claim strength.**
- The Introduction hedges ("one assumes … can be distinguished, in part"). A few paragraphs later it concludes that the ZBCP is "unambiguously suggesting the presence of pz component." The abstract says "strongly suggests."
- "Nodal" in the title rests only on the ZBCP within one model. The authors' own Fig. 1 caption states that a zero-energy Andreev bound state (ZABS) arises when the gap changes sign under kz → −kz. That is a sign-change condition, not a nodal one, and the caption also notes that nodal gaps need not produce a ZABS.
- The text itself says only "a high probability of nodes." Wording such as "sign change of the gap along kz" would be more accurate, unless independent evidence for nodes is presented.

17. **5. The ZBCP diagnostic is not uniquely tied to triplet B2u/B3u states.**
- Triplet pairing is assumed from the outset (Table I lists "the four possible spin-triplet pairings"). Even-parity states that change sign under kz → −kz (B2g, B3g, e.g. kxkz) could also produce a ZBCP on (001). They are never considered.
- Au is excluded only within a single-band, isotropic, unitary, linear-k model. The authors state that they "have ignored the high-order (kx ky kz) terms."
- Non-unitary or two-component states and realistic Fermi surfaces are untested. The authors themselves concede that Au cannot be excluded at ambient pressure, given NMR and thermal conductivity data (ref. 48).
- The criterion is quantitative (a ZBCP above 2 at large Z), yet the G(V) normalization is never described and the axes read "arb. unit."
  - Temperature series extend to about 3.0–3.3 K, so normal-state spectra appear to exist. It is not stated whether they were used.
  - Normalizing near Tc, where a residual hump persists up to 2.5 K, would distort the peak heights.
  - The headline peak heights (≈1.2 at ambient, ≈2.08 at 0.8 GPa) therefore cannot be verified.

18. **6. Within the authors' model, the SC2→SC3 signature has no mechanism.**
- In the isotropic model, B2u and B3u map onto each other under x↔y and give identical (001) spectra. The model thus predicts no spectral change at a B2u→B3u boundary, yet a minimum in the ratio versus T is interpreted as exactly that transition.
- A phase emerging below SC2 through a continuous transition would generically be two-component (e.g. B2u + iB3u, possibly non-unitary). The unitary (d × d* = 0) model cannot describe such a state, and the order of the transition is not discussed.
- The authors should either give a physical reason why Δz/Δx(y) should be extremal at the boundary, or present the minimum as an empirical anomaly that coincides with literature boundaries.

19. **7. The model is mismatched to UTe2's electronic structure.**
- UTe2 has quasi-2D Fermi-surface cylinders along kz, as shown in works cited by the authors, including a co-author's.
- The model nonetheless uses a spherical single-band Fermi surface.
- For (001) injection, both the effective barrier Z/(kz/kF) and the weight of the kz component depend directly on Fermi-surface geometry. The extracted ratio is therefore model-dependent.
- A robustness check with a cylindrical or multi-pocket Fermi surface is needed before the ratio is interpreted physically.

20. **8. It is unclear whether the fitted parameters are intrinsic gaps.**
- The fitted values imply 2Δ/kBTc ≈ 10–13 for the larger gap and ≈ 6–8 for the smaller. Both are far beyond weak coupling, and neither is discussed or compared with STM or specific-heat gap estimates.
- Γ/Δ ranges from about 0.14 to 0.65 at ambient pressure. It is large for S1 and S2 and smaller for S3.
- At 0.8 GPa, gaps of about 1 meV persist at 2.1 K, and the fitted Δ(T) is non-monotonic. The Fig. S2c caption says it "deviates from BCS."
- The ambient-pressure ZBCP reaches only G_NS ≈ 1.2, against an ideal value of 2.
- Taken together, these raise the possibility of a non-ballistic (thermal or diffusive) contact.
- The lock-in excitation amplitude and instrumental resolution are not reported. Modulation broadening of about 0.1 mV and thermal broadening (~0.1 meV at 0.3 K) are comparable to the fitted Γ at high pressure, so Γ cannot be interpreted without this information.

21. **9. The contact regime and artifacts are not addressed.**
- No contact resistances or contact sizes are given in the text or captions we examined, beyond the statement "various contact sizes and resistances" for S2/S3. There is also no Wexler or Sharvin estimate and no comparison with the mean free path. Please report R_N values explicitly.
- Side dips are a well-known critical-current signature (Sheet et al., ref. 42, cited by the authors). Here they are used as positive evidence for a ZABS.
- The heating and critical-current dismissal relies on two observations: the ZBCP is insensitive to fields below 2 T, and the peak shape degrades gradually with T. Neither discriminates well in a material whose Hc2 is tens of tesla.
- Spectra up to 8 T (H ∥ a) exist but are not analysed. The authors should show whether:
  - the dip positions track Jc(H, T);
  - the dips scale with R_N;
  - the ZBCP splits or depends on field direction. Because the response depends on the d-vector, this could even help distinguish B2u from B3u on (001).
- The Kondo/magnetic-scattering dismissal ("UTe2 does not exhibit magnetic ordering at ambient pressure") is insufficient. Zero-bias anomalies require only local moments, and UTe2 is a Kondo lattice. The argument also does not address spectra near MO1 (~1.4 GPa).

22. **10. The pressure evidence comes from one contact whose properties change, at uncertain low-temperature pressures.**
- All pressure data come from S1. For the same contact, Z falls from 1.67 to 1.20 and Γ from 0.39 to 0.10 meV, with no explanation.
- The authors themselves note that large Γ smears the side dips. The emergence of side dips and the rise of the ZBCP may therefore reflect reduced broadening rather than increased pz weight. The Fig. 4 simulations use Γ/Δ = 0.2, well below most fitted values.
- Comparing like with like, S1 at 0 GPa (~0.61) and S1 at 0.2 GPa (0.64 ± 0.02) are indistinguishable. The quoted 0.57(5) appears to be a three-sample average, which should be stated.
- The "abrupt" increase therefore rests on a few higher-pressure values reported without error bars. No spectra exist between 0.2 and 0.6 GPa, so the onset near 0.3 GPa is inferred from the literature rather than resolved.
- No manometer is described, and nothing says how or at what temperature pressure was determined.
  - Piston-clamp cells typically lose about 0.1–0.3 GPa on cooling. The pressure labels differ across the text (0.2/0.6/1.2 GPa), Fig. S3 (0.3/0.8/1.2) and Fig. S9 (0/0.6/1.2) by about this amount.
  - Please state whether the quoted pressures are room-temperature or low-temperature values. With an uncertainty of ±0.1–0.2 GPa, it is unclear whether the 0.2 GPa point lies in SC1.
- Freezing of the Daphne 7373 medium on each cooldown is a plausible cause of the changes in contact parameters. Any resulting non-hydrostatic strain also bears on the authors' "inhomogeneity/domains" explanation for the double side dips.
- Measurements on additional contacts and samples under pressure are essential.

23. **11. The superposition fit is overparameterized and under-reported.**
- Eq. (3) defines ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ as a ratio of weighted sums. However, several fit details are not given:
  - the number of channels (the Fig. S9 legends imply at least two, i.e. at least four gaps plus weights);
  - whether the weights are normalized;
  - whether Z and Γ were fixed or free in the T-series. Their near-constancy suggests they were fixed, which would force all thermal evolution into the gap ratio.
- No uniqueness, χ² or covariance analysis is given, and most error bars are missing.
- If the channels represent domains, pressure gradients or multiple gaps, the physical meaning of a single averaged ratio is unclear.
- The simulations treat Γ inconsistently: Fig. S5 fixes Γ/Δ = 0.2, while Fig. S6 uses absolute Γ (0.00–0.40 meV). This makes it hard to compare the criteria with each other and with the fits.

24. **12. Selective reporting and missing complementary measurements.**
- The raw main-text temperature series (Fig. 3: 0.2/0.6/1.0/1.4 GPa) and the fitted pressures (Fig. 5: 0.2/0.6/0.8/1.2 GPa) only partly overlap.
- The 1.0 and 1.4 GPa data are never fitted. The 1.4 GPa spectra, at the MO1 onset, are described only qualitatively, and no reason is given for not fitting them.
- Measurements on other surfaces ((100), (010), (011)) are needed to support any irrep assignment.

25. **13. The phase boundaries are not tied to the authors' own data.**
- Resistivity on S1 under pressure exists (Fig. S1b,c), but per-pressure Tc values are not reported.
- These values are not used to normalize temperature (Δ vs T/Tc) or to cross-check the locations of the ratio minima.
- The comparison is made only with boundaries measured on other samples.

26. **14. Missing methods details.**
- The G(V) normalization procedure is not given (see item 5).
- The dI/dV technique is not described: lock-in frequency, excitation amplitude and energy resolution are all missing.
- Pressure determination and the manometer are not described (see item 10).
- The polishing method is not described, and there is no etching or orientation check. No evidence supports the claim that curing the Au epoxy at 423 K "preserves intrinsic surface integrity."
- RRR is given only for S1.
- Sample quality: for crystals with RRR ≈ 410, a broad transition (onset 2.6 K, zero resistance 2.1 K) is unusual. Could this indicate filamentary or strained, possibly surface-near, regions with enhanced Tc? This bears on the 2.5 K hump and on whether PCS probes bulk-representative material.
- Raw data and fitting code are not available ("data within the paper").

27. **15. Internal inconsistencies that bear on the central claim.**
- The Δz and Δx(y) labels in Table S1 appear swapped relative to Fig. S7 and to the quoted ratio below 1. Since the claim that "pz weight increases" depends on the direction of this ratio, the labels must be corrected and made consistent throughout.
- The derivation of 0.57(5) (apparently a three-sample mean) should be stated explicitly.
- Pressure labels are inconsistent across the main text, Fig. S3 and Fig. S9. In the main text, 0.6 and 0.8 GPa appear as distinct datasets (Fig. 5c,d), but the correspondence with the SI labels should be clarified (see item 10).
- The SI text cites Fig. S5(a) where (b) is meant.
- Ref. 24 is misdated (2002 should be 2020).
- The descriptions of the Au signature in the Fig. 1 caption, the main text and the SI are physically equivalent but worded differently. Please harmonize them.

28. **1. The point-contact regime is not established.**
- No contact resistances are given for S1–S3 or for any pressure. The text says only "various contact sizes and resistances".
- There is no mean-free-path or Sharvin/Wexler estimate, so ballistic transport is assumed rather than shown. Spectroscopic interpretation depends on that assumption.
- Please give R_N for every contact and pressure, and an estimate of contact size relative to the mean free path.

29. **2. Side dips are not excluded as critical-current or heating artifacts.**
- Conductance side dips are a well-known artifact of non-ballistic contacts, arising from local critical current or heating (e.g., Sheet et al., PRB 2004).
- The paper never discusses this. Instead it uses the dips as evidence that the ZBCP is intrinsic.
- Please show:
  - how dip positions scale with temperature and field relative to Δ(T);
  - how they depend on contact resistance;
  - why a critical-current origin is excluded.

30. **3. Surface preparation and orientation are under-documented.**
- The (001) face was "fine-polished" in an Ar glove box. There is no Laue or orientation check, and no estimate of misorientation.
- The paper does not discuss:
  - polishing damage;
  - UTe2's natural (011) cleavage;
  - air exposure during pressure-cell loading.
- This matters because the paper's own model (Fig. 1) makes Andreev bound state (ABS) formation depend on the current direction relative to the crystal axes.

31. **4. Contact fabrication and continuity across pressures are unclear.**
- Au epoxy is cured at 423 K for 30 min. There is no check of local Tc or surface degradation after curing.
- It is only implied that the same S1 contact is used at all pressures. Please state this explicitly.
- If it is the same contact, explain why Z falls from about 1.66 to 1.20 and Γ from about 0.38 to 0.10 meV between 0 and 0.8 GPa. This suggests the junction changed, which undermines direct comparison of fitted ratios across pressures. At present this is mentioned only as "junction variation".

32. **5. Measurement and data-processing details are missing.**
- Lock-in frequency and excitation amplitude, and whether dI/dV is measured directly or obtained by numerical differentiation.
- How G_NS is normalized: by a normal-state spectrum above Tc, by a polynomial background, or otherwise. Also whether spectra are symmetrized.
- Figures use "arb. unit" with vertical offsets, yet fits are performed on absolute G_NS values. The normalization must be specified.
- Pressure calibration: the manometer used (e.g., Pb or Sn Tc), the low-temperature pressure values, and uncertainties. The paper should also discuss hydrostaticity of Daphne 7373 in this range.
- Resistivity: the Tc criterion used for the ρ(p,T) map, and whether resistivity and PCS were measured in the same cooldown.

33. **6. The field-dependent data are incompletely described.**
- The main text gives H ∥ a, but the Fig. S2a caption omits the field direction, and its label is partly unreadable.
- Fig. S2a appears to extend to about 8 T, but the text discusses only fields below 2 T ("only weakly affects its width"). The authors' heating criterion is that the width "shall shrink rapidly with magnetic field". Please discuss the full field range.

34. **7. Heating and Kondo or magnetic scattering are excluded only qualitatively.**
- The argument that "UTe2 does not exhibit magnetic ordering at ambient pressure" is not revisited at 1.2–1.4 GPa, near the MO1/MO2 phases.
- The unexplained flat-topped peak appears at 1.4 GPa, so this is precisely where the argument matters.

35. **8. The window between Tc0 = 2.1 K and the onset Tc = 2.6 K is not discussed.**
- A hump is reported at 2.5 K. Please explain how spectral features in this window should be interpreted.
- State why the ambient fits stop at 1.85 K while the 0.8 GPa fits extend to 2.1 K.

36. **9. Model assumptions should be stated and justified in the main text.**
- The SI uses a single spherical Fermi surface, identical m and kF on both sides with no Fermi-velocity mismatch, and a step-function (non-self-consistent) gap.
- UTe2 has quasi-2D cylindrical Fermi sheets, so please justify this model for c-axis transport.
- Please also discuss which Fermi-surface states a (001) contact actually probes.

37. **10. Several quantities needed to reproduce the fits are undefined.**
- G0 is described only as "the conductance in the normal state", and I0 in Eq. S10 is undefined.
- The weighting and grid of the k∥ sum are not given (e.g., cos θ or transmission weighting).
- It is not stated whether the d-vector components use k/kF. This determines whether Δz/Δx(y) is a meaningful physical ratio.
- "Δ" in Fig. S5 is undefined.
- It would help to state explicitly that Γ enters only through E → E + iΓ in the reflection coefficients (Eq. S14) and not through the Fermi function.

38. **11. The axis convention is missing.**
- The mapping x, y, z ↔ a, b, c is never stated, yet the B2u and B3u labels and the field direction depend on it.

39. **12. The zero-energy ABS criterion needs generalizing.**
- It is stated only for a scalar gap, Δ(kz) vs Δ(−kz). Please generalize it to multicomponent d-vectors.
- Explain why Au, whose ẑ-component also changes sign under kz → −kz, gives a split peak while B2u gives a zero-bias peak.

40. **13. The B2u/B3u "indistinguishable" statement needs qualification.**
- This appears to follow from the model's in-plane isotropy. Please state this explicitly.
- Please show a B3u simulation alongside the B2u one.

41. **14. The pairing assumptions are restrictive.**
- Unitary pairing and a spin-inactive barrier are assumed.
- Non-unitary states (e.g., B2u + iB3u) are not considered. These are widely discussed for UTe2 and are directly relevant where SC2 and SC3 coexist.

42. **15. "Nodal character" is not supported by the paper's own simulations.**
- The fully gapped Au (Balian–Werthamer-like) simulation also produces side dips at Z > 1.
- The model text attributes dips to ABS depleting the continuum, not to nodes.
- Please remove the claim that side dips indicate nodes, or justify it properly.

43. **16. The fitting protocol is not described.** Please specify:
- the optimizer and cost function;
- the voltage window and initial values;
- which parameters (Z, Γ, Δz, Δx(y), weights) are free or fixed at each temperature and pressure.

44. **17. The uncertainties look unrealistic.**
- Quoted errors such as ±0.01 meV appear to be fit-covariance errors only. They ignore strong correlations among Z, Γ, Δz and Δx(y), and systematic effects.
- The key ratios at 0.6 GPa (1.03) and 1.2 GPa (1.30) have no uncertainties, and Fig. 5 has no error bars.

45. **18. Some fitted parameter values are physically implausible and undiscussed.**
- At ambient pressure, Δx(y) = 1.18 meV with Tc ≈ 2.1 K gives 2Δ/kBTc ≈ 13, far above weak coupling.
- The fitted gaps do not close near Tc: about 0.4/0.24 meV at 1.85 K (0 GPa) and 0.7–0.8 meV at 2.1 K (0.8 GPa). The Fig. S2c caption says only that they "apparently deviate from the conventional BCS behavior".
- Γ/Δ ≈ 0.3–0.5 is large.
- These suggest parameter degeneracy, or that the fitted "gaps" are not the superconducting gap. Please discuss.

46. **19. The ZBCP amplitude is inconsistent with the model.**
- The Fig. 1c caption states that the model ZBCP "readily exceeds 2 in the large-Z limit", yet the ambient fits give Z ≈ 1.66 with a peak of only about 1.1.
- The text attributes this qualitatively to a dominant px(y) component, but gives no quantitative breakdown.
- No scaling factor, spreading resistance or parallel channel is stated. Given the arbitrary units, please state whether any vertical rescaling or normalization was applied before fitting. Such rescaling would weaken the constraint on Z.

47. **20. The symmetry exclusion is not quantitative.**
- Au and B1u are only simulated, never fitted to the data. The simulations use small fixed Γ (about 0.01–0.05 meV) and Δ = 0.25 meV, whereas the fitted values are Γ ≈ 0.38 meV and Δ ≈ 1 meV.
- Au is excluded because its peak "typically splits at any finite Z". With broadening as large as fitted, a split peak may merge into a single ZBCP.
- Please fit Au and B1u to the data with Γ free, and give χ² or residual comparisons. Otherwise the admission that Au "cannot entirely be excluded" undermines the central symmetry claim.

48. **21. Sample and pressure bookkeeping is inconsistent.**
- Ambient ratios at 0.3 K are 0.61 (S1), 0.54 (S2) and 0.52 (S3), while the main text quotes 0.57(5). State whether this is an average and over what.
- Main-text Fig. 3a and Fig. 5b show 0.2 GPa, while Fig. S3 shows unfitted 0.3 GPa spectra. Clarify whether these are distinct datasets. 0.3 GPa lies at the SC1/SC2 boundary, so the label matters.
- The spectra at 1.0 and 1.4 GPa are never fitted, and the 1.4 GPa flat-topped peak is left uninterpreted.

49. **22. The superposition model is underspecified.** Please state:
- the irrep of each component;
- whether the weights are normalized (Σwi = 1) and whether they vary with temperature;
- the criterion for choosing the number of components.
The parameter count grows by about 5 per component, so an information criterion or similar is needed to control overfitting.

50. **23. The switch to superposition is not justified.**
- 0.8 GPa is fitted with one component and 1.2 GPa with two, justified only by the statement that one component "can hardly capture" the double-dip feature.
- The "pressure inhomogeneity" explanation is offered without evidence. For example, the paper does not show broadening of the resistive transition with pressure.

51. **24. The weighted ratio has no clear interpretation.**
- ⟨Δz⟩/⟨Δx(y)⟩ = Σwi Δz^i / Σwi Δx^i has no clear physical meaning if the components represent different gaps or phases.
- If the components are B2u and B3u, coexistence is already assumed, which makes the later SC3 = B3u inference partly circular.
- The ⟨⟩ notation is also used for single-component pressures (0 and 0.6 GPa in Fig. S9) without saying that it then reduces to the plain ratio.

52. **25. The SC2–SC3 transition inferred from the ratio minimum is statistically unsupported.**
- The minima (about 1.6 K at 0.6 GPa, about 0.7 K at 1.2 GPa, and at 0.8 GPa "near 1.5 K" in the text but about 1.8 K in Fig. S8) are comparable to the scatter at 0 GPa (0.55–0.67). The authors call that 0 GPa behaviour "nearly temperature-independent".
- There is no statistical test, and no second contact is measured under pressure.
- Only 0.8 GPa is shown as a ratio-versus-temperature curve in the main text, and Fig. S9 omits 0.8 GPa.
- No numerical literature transition temperatures are compared, so "matches the literature" is not demonstrated.

53. **26. The B2u (SC2) → B3u (SC3) assignment is imported from NMR.**
- It rests entirely on Ref. [31], since PCS cannot distinguish B2u from B3u in this model.
- The paper should clearly separate PCS-derived conclusions from imported ones.
- It should discuss that the fitted quantity "Δx(y)" changes meaning across the transition (Δx → Δy).
- It should justify the reading of Δz as "pz-wave weight" (Fig. 5a caption).
- The hedging should be made consistent across the abstract, the main text and the Remarks.

54. **27. The use of literature phase boundaries needs more care.**
- The SC2/SC3 lines are copied from calorimetry and susceptibility on other samples, with no discussion of sample comparability or pressure-scale offsets.
- Citations differ between the text ([27, 30]) and the figures ([27, 29, 30]).
- Fig. S9 marks SC1, SC2 and SC3 regions with no source. Please also check whether SC2/SC3 labels appear on the p = 0 panel.

55. **28. Conflicting prior work is not addressed.**
- Only the agreeing PCS study (Ref. [47]) is discussed.
- The tension with Ref. [48] (Suetsugu 2024, fully gapped) should be addressed.

56. **29. Table S1 appears to have Δz and Δx(y) swapped.**
- Table S1 lists Δz > Δx(y), implying a ratio of about 1.6.
- The main text (0.57) and Fig. S7 (Δx(y) = 1.18 meV, Δz = 0.72 meV) imply a ratio of about 0.6.

57. **30. Minor errors.**
- Figure cross-reference errors (e.g., S5a should be S5b) and unit inconsistencies.
- Ref. [24] (Jiao et al., Nature 579, 523) is dated 2002; it should be 2020.
- The summary says side dips appear "above 0.3 GPa", but they are first shown at 0.6 GPa. Please align the wording.

58. **31. Data and code availability are inadequate.**
- The statement that data are "available within the paper" is insufficient, since only shifted, arbitrary-unit plots are provided.
- Custom fitting code exists (per the Author Contributions) but is not released.
- The SI link is a placeholder ("********").
- Please deposit raw dI/dV spectra (with R_N) and the BTK and superposition fitting code.
