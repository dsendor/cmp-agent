# Referee report

**"Resolving Abrikosov vortex entry in superconducting nano-string resonators via displacement-noise spectroscopy in cavity-optomechanics"** (Luschmann, Parvini, Niekamp, Marx, Gross, Huebl)

## Summary

The authors embed a suspended Al nanostring (30 × 0.2 × 0.11 µm³) in the loop of a dc SQUID that terminates a λ/4 CPW resonator. For each in-plane field B_ip (resolution 0.25 mT), they sweep the out-of-plane flux bias. They fit the dome-shaped Ω_m(Φ_b) with a Lorentz-force spring model (Eq. 3) to extract a "bare" frequency Ω₀(B_ip).

They report two findings:

1. **A smooth background.** Ω₀² rises as B_ip^k with k = 1.81 ± 0.05. They interpret this as Campbell-regime pinning stiffness and extract α_L ~ 10¹⁴ N m⁻⁴.
2. **Discrete steps.** Steps of ΔΩ/2π ≈ 10–50 Hz are interpreted as single-vortex entry events. From these they derive pinning stiffnesses k_p ≈ 14–70 N m⁻², pinning energies of 0.09–0.44 eV, and "attonewton-scale" force steps.

**Strengths**
- Using an integrated, low-backaction electromechanical readout to probe vortex physics in a chip-relevant mesoscopic geometry is attractive and timely.
- The frequency resolution is impressive, the data are openly available, and the estimates in the text are arithmetically reproducible.

**Main weakness.** In its present form, the vortex interpretation is not adequately supported. Several parts of the physical framework appear internally inconsistent or inconsistent with the cited vibrating-reed literature. Key control experiments are also missing.

## Major concerns

### 1. Can this cross-section host Abrikosov vortices in a parallel field?

Using the authors' own estimates (ξ ≈ 108 nm, λ ≈ 0.84 µm):

- **Thickness relative to ξ.** The thickness d = 110 nm ≈ ξ, and the width w = 200 nm ≈ 2ξ. In Ginzburg–Landau theory, films in a parallel field with d ≲ ~1.8–2ξ do not form vortices. Instead, the order parameter is suppressed homogeneously and the film undergoes a second-order transition at H_c∥.
- **Field range relative to H_c2.** The bulk-like upper critical field is B_c2 = Φ₀/(2πξ²) ≈ 28 mT, essentially equal to the quoted ZFC entry field Φ₀/(2πξd) = 27.7 mT, since d ≈ ξ. The measurements extend to 35 mT, above this value. Superconductivity survives there only because of the thin-film confinement, and that is precisely the regime in which vortices are not expected.
- **Parallel critical field.** The thin-film parallel critical field is H_c∥ ≈ √3 Φ₀/(πξd) ≈ 95 mT. The whole measured range therefore lies in the regime of quadratic, vortex-free pair-breaking.
- **Mis-applied formulas.** The entry-field and vortex-density formulas used (Likharev; Stan et al.; Bronson et al., Eqs. 6–7) were derived for thin strips in a *perpendicular* field, with the Pearl length ≫ w. Their transfer to a parallel-field geometry with d ≈ ξ needs justification.

The authors should address this directly, ideally with a GL simulation of the actual cross-section (d × w with B_ip along w). Without it, "Abrikosov vortex" in the title is not established.

### 2. The physical model for pinning-induced stiffening (Eq. 8) is questionable

Eq. 8 adds α_L/ρ directly to Ω², treating pinning wells as springs anchored to the laboratory frame. However, pinning centers are attached to the lattice and move with the string. A rigid translation of the string, with its vortices pinned, in a uniform applied field costs no pinning energy.

In the vibrating-reed theory the authors cite (Brandt, Esquinazi, Neckel 1986; Esquinazi 1991), the restoring force comes from the *tilt* of the flux-line lattice relative to the fixed external field, i.e., a magnetic line-tension term ∝ B²/μ₀. α_L enters only through the Campbell length relative to sample dimensions. In the strong-pinning limit, the shift saturates at a value that is independent of α_L and scales as ≈ B².

Consequences:
- An exponent k ≈ 2 is therefore not specific evidence for a Campbell-regime α_L ∝ B^1.81.
- The extracted α_L values, and everything derived from them (⟨k_p⟩, U), depend on a model the cited literature does not support.

The authors should derive the frequency shift for their geometry: a string mode with out-of-plane displacement, B_ip in-plane and perpendicular to the string, and λ ≫ d, w so that the internal field barely differs from the applied field. They should then show whether α_L is even recoverable from Δω².

### 3. Simpler explanations for the smooth background are not ruled out

The near-quadratic background is dismissed on the basis of the jumps. But the jumps say nothing about the origin of the background. At least three B²-type mechanisms need to be excluded quantitatively:

- **(a) Junction asymmetry.** For any asymmetry α ≠ 0, the Josephson inductance stays finite at Φ_b = Φ₀/2. A residual Lorentz-spring term ∝ B_ip² then survives in "Ω₀". The fits fix α = 0 by default.
- **(b) Field-dependent kinetic inductance.** Parallel-field pair-breaking gives |ψ|² ∝ 1 − (B/B_c∥)². This changes the string's (and loop's) kinetic inductance, and hence L_l in Eq. 3, roughly ∝ B². If L_l is held fixed in the fits, this aliases into Ω₀.
- **(c) Other effects.** Field-dependent stress or tension changes and magnetomotive effects.

Please state which parameters (I_c, L_l, η, α, φ_off) are free in each fit. Plot their fitted values versus B_ip. Show fits with α free.

### 4. The steps: assignment and internal consistency

- **Extraction artefacts.** Ω₀ is not measured directly but obtained from a multi-parameter fit of a full flux sweep. Vortices entering *other* parts of the circuit (wide CPW, SQUID arms, junction leads) would shift φ_off, I_c, or the effective loop inductance. Such shifts could appear as steps in fitted Ω₀. Please show:
  - the raw Ω_m at Φ_b = Φ₀/2, which the authors themselves note directly gives Ω₀;
  - the raw Ω_m at Φ_b = 0;
  - the fitted I_c(B_ip) and φ_off(B_ip), checking whether the steps coincide with changes in those parameters.
- **Smooth rise between steps.** If the steps are vortex entries, the vortex number is constant between them. Yet Ω₀² keeps rising smoothly between steps at roughly the background slope. What mechanism produces α_L growth at fixed N_v? The background and step interpretations appear mutually inconsistent.
- **Counting.** Eq. 7 predicts ΔB ≈ 0.6–1.25 mT per vortex, i.e., roughly 15–30 entries between 12 and 30 mT. Only 4–5 steps are observed, with spacings of 1.5–5 mT. The authors invoke "one or a few flux quanta" per step, but then assume exactly one vortex per step when extracting k_p and U. If steps contain several vortices, the single-vortex values are overestimates.
- **Magnitude check.** With ~56 vortices at 35 mT, each contributing k_p ≈ 14–70 N m⁻², the total would be α_L ≈ 0.2–1.2 × 10¹⁵ N m⁻⁴. This exceeds the extracted 2 × 10¹⁴ N m⁻⁴. Moreover, the steps alone account for a large fraction of the total shift, which suggests double counting between "background" and "jumps".
- **Mode shape.** The fundamental string mode is sinusoidal, not uniform. A vortex entering near a clamp contributes much less than one at the antinode. The single-vortex analysis should include the mode-shape weighting, or at least discuss the resulting spread.

### 5. Stochasticity and history dependence are asserted rather than demonstrated

- In Fig. 3(a), datasets 1 and 2 cover non-overlapping field ranges (~12–17 mT and ~19–30 mT). They therefore cannot show that step positions vary between runs.
- It is unclear how dataset 2 is referenced to Ω₀(20 mT) when it does not reach 20 mT. An arbitrary offset could create or hide the step at 20 mT.
- Comparing Fig. 3(a) with 3(b), steps appear at 22.5 mT in both and at 20.0 vs. 20.5 mT. This hints at reproducible rather than stochastic features.

The following controls are, in my view, essential for the claim:
- repeated up-sweeps within one cooldown over identical ranges;
- down-sweeps, to test hysteresis and vortex exit;
- reversed B_ip polarity;
- field-cooled vs. zero-field-cooled comparison;
- temperature dependence toward T_c.

### 6. The force-sensitivity argument is conceptually loose

ΔF = Δk·x_rms is not a force that the measurement detects. What is detected is a frequency shift, whose resolution is set by frequency noise or Allan deviation and by the fit uncertainty of Ω₀. Comparing ΔF with S_FF^th integrated over "1–10 Hz" does not correspond to the actual measurement protocol, which is a quasi-static sweep with a full flux scan and fit per point.

- The meaningful figures of merit are the uncertainty of Ω₀ per field point and the step amplitude relative to it. Steps of 10–50 Hz correspond to 0.5–2.5 Γ_m.
- The mode temperature is assumed to equal 85 mK; this should be verified.
- The claims of "attonewton-scale forces" and "real-time" capture of spontaneous flux dynamics (Sec. V) are therefore overstated.
- The claim of in-situ determination of B_c2 is not demonstrated.

### 7. Effective density in Eq. 8

ρ_eff = m_eff/V_geom ≈ 910 kg m⁻³, whereas Al has 2700 kg m⁻³. For a spatially uniform α_L, the modal projection gives Δω² = α_L/ρ, with the *physical* density: the mode-shape factors cancel between modal stiffness and modal mass. Using m_eff/V_geom mixes modal and geometric quantities and appears to bias α_L by a factor of ~2–3. This is secondary to point 2 but should be corrected.

### 8. Empirical B_c1 and the functional form

B_c1 ≈ 5 mT is taken from where "the trend sets in". However:
- Fig. 2(d) has no data below ~6 mT.
- The fitted function A₀ + C·B^k has no threshold.

If stiffening starts at B_c1, a form such as (B − B_c1)^k, or Eq. 7 inserted into α_L, should be fitted and compared. Please also state whether k = 1.81 ± 0.05 comes from independent fits to each dataset or from a joint fit, and whether the jumps were excluded.

## Minor issues

- **Mixed analysis models.** Fig. 4 combines older data analyzed with Eq. A1 (neglecting L_l) with new data analyzed with Eq. 3. Please reanalyze the old data with Eq. 3, or show that the difference is negligible.
- **VNA protocol.** Explain how Ω_m is obtained by VNA near the flux points where the sideband signal vanishes (e.g., OMIT?), and whether this introduces systematic offsets relative to the noise spectroscopy.
- **Magnetoelastic estimate.** Give a short derivation of ΔΩ₀/Ω₀ ~ 10⁻⁸ for the tensile string.
- **Figure captions.**
  - Fig. 2(d) caption says "Ω₀(B_ip)", but Ω₀² − Ω₀²(35 mT) is plotted.
  - Fig. 4 caption refers to "blue circles" (they are green) and "black squares", while the text says "black filled circles".
  - Fig. 1(a) caption mentions κ_ext and κ_in labels that are hard to identify.
- **Symbol overloading.** α denotes surface scattering (1.33), junction asymmetry, and α_L. κ denotes cavity decay and κ_GL. Please disambiguate.
- **Terminology.** "Cavity optomechanics, also known as nano-electromechanics" is imprecise; electromechanics is a microwave implementation of cavity optomechanics.
- **Hedging.** Wording is inconsistent: "suggest" in the introduction and conclusion vs. "attributable"/"directly quantify" in the abstract and Sec. V. The abstract should reflect the level of evidence.
- **References.**
  - Duplicates: 34/70, 42/82, and 36/69/79. Ref. 79 is listed as Phys. Rev. B 19, 044073, which should be Phys. Rev. Applied.
  - Refs. 9 and 12 lack years.
  - "Gorodetksy" in Ref. 86 should be "Gorodetsky".
- **Unexplained fit parameter.** Define S₀(Φ_b) in Eq. A1.

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The platform and data are interesting and potentially valuable. However, the central claim, resolving single Abrikosov vortex entry and extracting single-vortex pinning energies, currently rests on:
- a vortex picture that is doubtful for a d ≈ ξ cross-section in a parallel field;
- a stiffening model (Eq. 8) at odds with established vibrating-reed theory;
- step features that have not been separated from fit-parameter artefacts or shown to be stochastic and hysteretic.

The manuscript should either:
- **(i)** add the controls and modelling requested above (raw Φ₀/2 data, fit-parameter traces, up/down and FC/ZFC sweeps, temperature dependence, a GL treatment of the actual geometry); or
- **(ii)** substantially soften the claims, presenting the steps as discrete flux-related events of undetermined microscopic origin and the background as phenomenological.
