# Review outline

## 1. Significance and novelty

**What the paper does**
- An Al nanostring (30 × 0.2 × 0.11 µm³) forms one arm of a dc SQUID that terminates a λ/4 CPW resonator.
- The authors track the "intrinsic" mechanical frequency Ω₀(B_ip) with 0.25 mT resolution in-plane field steps. Ω₀ is obtained by fitting flux-bias sweeps of Ω_m with a Lorentz-force spring model (Eq. 3).
- They report two features:
  - a smooth background, Ω₀² − Ω²₀,bare ∝ B_ip^1.81, interpreted as Campbell-regime pinning elasticity (α_L ≈ 10¹⁴ N m⁻⁴);
  - discrete upward steps of 10–50 Hz (ΔΩ/Ω ≈ 10⁻⁵), interpreted as single-vortex entry events. From these they derive single-vortex pinning stiffness (14–70 N m⁻²), pinning energies (0.1–0.4 eV) and "attonewton-scale" forces.

**Novelty**
- Using a cavity-read nanomechanical element embedded in a working superconducting circuit as an in situ probe of vortex matter is attractive and, to my knowledge, new at this length scale.
- It extends vibrating-reed methods (Esquinazi, Brandt, Kober) and the micromechanical-oscillator work of Bolle et al. (Nature 1999) to a sub-micron cross-section. It also bears directly on flux-noise and decoherence questions and on the design of inductively coupled optomechanics in large in-plane fields.

**Significance relative to evidence**
- The potential significance is clear.
- The central claim, resolution of individual Abrikosov vortex entry, is only weakly supported. It rests on:
  - roughly 9 visually identified steps across three datasets;
  - a model-dependent extraction of Ω₀;
  - an interpretive framework borrowed from bulk and perpendicular-field strip geometries that may not apply to a film whose thickness is comparable to ξ.
- In its present form the work reads as an intriguing observation rather than a demonstration. Novelty is moderate-to-high; the strength of the conclusion is currently low.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **Timely, cross-disciplinary platform.** It connects cavity electromechanics, mesoscopic superconductivity and quantum-circuit materials science. Mechanical sensing integrated into a device-relevant geometry could become a useful diagnostic for flux trapping in qubits and resonators.
- **Improved data quality over prior work (Ref. 46).**
  - In-plane field resolution is 0.25 mT.
  - The geometric loop inductance L_l is now included in the spring model.
  - A third dataset after a thermal cycle allows a first look at history dependence.
- **Consistent background scaling.** The background exponent (k = 1.81 ± 0.05) agrees between low- and high-resolution data and between cooldowns, suggesting a robust underlying field-dependent stiffening.
- **Plausible orders of magnitude.**
  - The extracted α_L agrees with vibrating-reed values for disordered superconductors.
  - The pinning energies (0.1–0.4 eV) lie below the condensation-energy bound, which I estimate at ≈1–2 eV for πξ²w with B_c ≈ 10 mT.
  - The magnetoelastic (volume-effect) mechanism is reasonably excluded on magnitude grounds (10⁻⁸ vs 10⁻⁵).
- **Good practice and readability.**
  - Data are openly available on Zenodo.
  - The measurement scheme (thermal anti-Stokes sideband plus VNA) is clearly explained.
  - The fitting model is stated explicitly.
  - The manuscript is concise and accessible.

## 3. Potential reasons for rejection

### 3.1 Applicability of the Abrikosov-vortex/mixed-state picture to this geometry is not established

- **Dimensions are comparable to or smaller than the vortex core.**
  - By the authors' own estimates, ξ ≈ 108 nm ≈ d (110 nm) ≈ w/2, and λ ≈ 840 nm ≫ d, w.
  - If B_ip is in-plane and perpendicular to the string (required for a Lorentz force on J), vortex cores must sit within the 110 nm thickness. A vortex core has diameter ≈ 2ξ ≈ 216 nm.
  - Standard thin-film theory (Tinkham; Saint-James–de Gennes) predicts that for d ≲ 1.8ξ (some treatments give ≲ 4ξ) a parallel-field film does not form vortices at all. The order parameter is instead suppressed uniformly, with a continuous transition at H_c∥ ∝ Φ₀/(ξd).
  - With λ ≫ d the field penetrates almost uniformly, so there is little screening current to drive vortex nucleation. "B_c1" and Bean–Livingston-barrier concepts have no straightforward meaning here.
  - The κ_GL ≈ 7.8 argument for type-II behaviour therefore does not by itself justify a vortex mixed state in this cross-section. The authors need to show, for example by GL/TDGL simulations of a 110 × 200 nm cross-section, that stable vortex states exist in the relevant field range and with what entry fields.
- **Formulas are imported from perpendicular-field strip theory.**
  - Eq. 6 (Stan/Field/Martinis; Likharev) and Eq. 7 (Bronson, Gelfand & Field 2006) describe Pearl vortices in narrow strips in perpendicular field, where the relevant length is w, not d.
  - Transferring them to parallel-field entry by substituting d and ξ is not justified.
  - The statement that field cooling "enhances" the Bean–Livingston barrier, giving 282 mT, is unusual and unexplained.
- **The input material parameters are guesses.**
  - The mean free path is taken equal to a "~10 nm grain size" without transport data (no RRR, ρ_n, T_c or H_c∥ given).
  - λ and ξ in Eq. 5 scale as l_f^(−1/2) and l_f^(1/2) respectively, so a realistic l_f of 20–50 nm for 110 nm Al would change ξ and the d/ξ ratio appreciably.
  - The conclusion "type-II, vortex-hosting" therefore depends on an unverified number.
- **The empirical entry field is circular, and the vortex counting is inconsistent with the number of observed jumps.**
  - B_c1 ≈ 5 mT is defined as where the near-quadratic trend "sets in". However, the data begin at ≈6 mT and a pure B^1.8 background has no onset.
  - With B_c1 = 5 mT, Eq. 7 predicts ≈35 vortices by 35 mT, with expected step spacings of 0.6–1.25 mT.
  - Only ~4–5 steps are observed per dataset, spaced 1.5–5 mT apart. The paper does not reconcile "each jump = one vortex" with the expected number of vortices.
  - If most entries are invisible, the authors should explain why the visible ones are special.

### 3.2 The mechanism coupling pinned vortices to string motion, and the Campbell/Labusch analysis, are not derived and contain internal inconsistencies

- **No restoring force from the geometry has been shown.**
  - In vibrating-reed experiments the Campbell restoring force arises because reed tilt changes the orientation of the sample relative to the fixed external field. Pinned flux lines then exert a torque.
  - Here the displacement δx is out-of-plane (Lorentz force J × B_ip). The local tilt of the string is a rotation about the width axis, which is parallel to B_ip and to the putative vortex axis.
  - To first order this rotation leaves the field in the sample frame unchanged. Uniform translation in a uniform field displaces nothing relative to the pins.
  - The authors must explain what displaces vortices from their pinning wells during mechanical motion. One possibility is the motion-induced circulating current J acting on vortices; that would be an inductive (Campbell-inductance) effect entering through L_l in Eq. 3, not an additive mechanical stiffness in Ω₀. Field misalignment is another possibility.
  - Eq. 8 is currently asserted by analogy, not derived.
- **The effective density is used inconsistently.**
  - Eq. 8 uses ρ_eff = m_eff/V ≈ 910 kg m⁻³, i.e. m_eff divided by the physical volume (0.6 pg / 6.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ m³).
  - For a spatially uniform spring density acting on a mode, the mode-shape integrals cancel. The correct ratio is α_L/ρ with the material density, ≈2700 kg m⁻³ for Al.
  - This introduces a factor-of-~3 underestimate of α_L, Δα_L, k_p and U.
  - Separately, m_eff = 0.6 pg is inconsistent with half the string mass (≈0.9 pg) and should be justified.
- **The power-law interpretation contradicts the paper's own uniform-pinning formula.**
  - The uniform-pinning formula α_L ≃ (B/Φ₀)⟨k_p⟩ predicts α_L ∝ B, i.e. k ≈ 1. With Eq. 7 densities, the predicted dependence is even sub-linear near threshold.
  - The observed k = 1.81 therefore implies a field-dependent ⟨k_p⟩ ∝ B^0.8. This contradicts the quoted roughly constant ⟨k_p⟩ ≈ 10–20 N m⁻².
  - No collective-pinning theory is cited that predicts k ≈ 1.8 specifically. "Consistent with the Campbell regime" is asserted rather than shown.
  - An exponent close to 2 is precisely what generic B²-type electromagnetic or kinetic-inductance effects would produce (see 3.3).
- **The single-vortex quantities rest on simplifications that are not justified.**
  - The jump-size analysis ignores mode shape. A vortex entering near a clamp versus at mid-span contributes ∝ φ²(x₀), so jump sizes should vary by large factors with position. This should be modelled and could itself serve as a test.
  - The pinning energy U = ½k_p r_p² L assumes a harmonic well extending to r_p ~ ξ with L ~ w. This is an order-of-magnitude extrapolation with at least factor-of-several uncertainty, not a "quantitative extraction".

### 3.3 Alternative explanations for both the background and the jumps are not excluded

- **The background may be a systematic of the spring-model fit.** Ω₀ is not measured directly; it is a fit parameter of Eq. 3. The Lorentz spring term itself scales as B_ip², and several effects would leave a near-quadratic residual in the extracted Ω₀:
  - **Junction asymmetry.** It is set to α = 0 by default. With α ≠ 0 the Josephson inductance stays finite at half flux, leaving a residual ∝ B_ip² at the dome minimum.
  - **Field suppression of the junction critical current.** In-plane field causes Fraunhofer-type suppression of I_c in the JJs.
  - **Field-dependent kinetic inductance.** Pair-breaking in the 110 nm film in parallel field increases L_l ∝ B².
  - **Flux offset and misalignment.**

  The scale makes this concern serious. By my estimate with the stated parameters, the Lorentz spring amplitude at 35 mT is of order kHz (Fig. 2c shows ≈1.5 kHz). The total "vortex" background shift is ≈0.5 kHz. A ~30% model error would therefore account for the entire effect. The paper does not show which parameters were free per field, their fitted values versus B_ip, or the fit residuals.
- **The jumps may originate outside the nanostring.** The 110 nm-thick, 200 nm-wide string is arguably the least likely place for flux entry in the device. More likely candidates include:
  - vortex entry or rearrangement in the wider SQUID leads, junction electrodes, ground planes or CPW;
  - perpendicular vortices from field misalignment. A 1° misalignment at 35 mT gives ≈0.6 mT out-of-plane, which is ample for wide films.

  Such events would shift I_c (vortices near JJs), L_l or the flux offset. Through the fit, these would appear as steps in Ω₀. The step-wise character alone does not localize the event to the string.
- **Other sources of frequency discontinuity are not discussed.**
  - Two-level-system or defect switching in the string, mechanical frequency drifts and telegraph noise are not addressed.
  - Thermal effects of field ramps (eddy-current heating) and changes in tensile stress are likewise not considered.
  - These can produce 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁵ frequency jumps in nanostrings even without field.
  - Zero-field (or normal-state, T > T_c) stability data at the same 0.25 mT-equivalent time intervals are needed as a baseline.
- **The probe itself may contribute.**
  - Each Ω₀ point requires sweeping B_oop over a flux period, and the VNA and sideband probes drive circulating currents.
  - Whether these sweeps themselves trigger flux entry or rearrangement, i.e. whether the measurement is truly "passive", is not examined.

### 3.4 Statistical evidence for discrete single-vortex events is insufficient, and the sensitivity argument is misframed

- **Jump identification is subjective and weakly significant.**
  - Steps are marked by arrows without a stated detection criterion, such as a change-point or step-fit algorithm, a significance threshold or a false-positive estimate.
  - Several steps (e.g. 12.0 and 15.5 mT in Fig. 3a; 18.0 and 20.5 mT in Fig. 3b) appear comparable to the error bars and the point-to-point scatter.
  - The 10–50 Hz steps are of the same order as the mechanical linewidth (Γ_m/2π = 20 Hz).
  - With ~9 events in total, no distribution of jump sizes or spacings can be compared with predictions.
- **Key experimental signatures of vortex physics are missing.** The data contain no:
  - down-sweeps or hysteresis loops, the hallmark of pinning and trapped flux;
  - field-cooled vs zero-field-cooled comparison;
  - temperature dependence (e.g. disappearance of steps and background near T_c, or thermally activated depinning consistent with U ≈ 0.1–0.4 eV);
  - field-angle dependence;
  - real-time monitoring at fixed field to observe switching or telegraph behaviour.

  Without these, the "history dependence" rests on a single thermal-cycle comparison. Moreover, one step (22.5 mT) appears in both cooldowns, which, if anything, suggests deterministic rather than stochastic features. Datasets 1 and 2 in Fig. 3a cover non-overlapping field ranges, so within-cooldown reproducibility is untested.
- **The "attonewton" sensitivity claim conflates force detection with frequency-shift detection.**
  - The experiment detects a stiffness change Δk. Multiplying Δk by the thermal rms displacement to obtain "ΔF = 3–17 aN" and comparing it with the thermal force noise is not a meaningful resolvability criterion.
  - The relevant figure of merit is frequency stability, i.e. the Allan deviation of Ω_m and the uncertainty of the fitted Ω₀ over the measurement time. Neither is reported.
  - The abstract's statement of "attonewton-scale forces" is therefore misleading and should be reframed.
- **The fit-uncertainty chain is opaque.**
  - The error bars in Figs. 3–4 are not explained. It is unclear whether they come from the Eq. 3 fit covariance, include parameter correlations with I_c, L_l and η, or reflect the scatter of repeated measurements.
  - Fig. 3a and 3b use different reference fields, which hampers direct comparison.
  - The power-law fit's offset A₀ and Ω₀,bare are not independently determined, and the fit quality and correlation of k with A₀ are not reported.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Add decisive control experiments that test the vortex hypothesis directly.**
   - Field up/down sweeps showing hysteresis, and FC vs ZFC protocols.
   - Temperature series up to T_c, showing suppression of background and jumps, and thermally activated changes in jump statistics.
   - Rotation of the in-plane field angle relative to the string axis.
   - Fixed-field time traces (Allan deviation; search for telegraph switching).
   - A zero-field or normal-state baseline of frequency stability.
   - Ideally, comparison devices: a thicker or wider string (d ≫ ξ), a non-superconducting string, or a device without the SQUID arm. These would localize the events and test the predicted dimensional thresholds.
   - More cooldowns, enough to establish whether step positions are stochastic or pinned to specific defects.

2. **Make the Ω₀ extraction fully transparent and robust against systematics.**
   - Report, for every B_ip, the fitted I_c, L_l, η, junction asymmetry and flux offset, together with residuals and covariances.
   - Allow I_c(B_ip) (Fraunhofer suppression), L_l(B_ip) (kinetic inductance) and asymmetry to vary.
   - Show whether the jumps in Ω₀ coincide with jumps in I_c, offset or resonator ω_c. Coincidence would point to flux entry elsewhere.
   - Cross-check Ω₀ with direct measurements of Ω_m near half flux, where the spring term is minimal.
   - Show raw Ω_m(Φ_b) sweeps immediately before and after each step.
   - Introduce an objective step-detection procedure with significance levels and false-positive estimates. State clearly what the error bars represent.

3. **Provide a self-consistent theoretical treatment for the actual geometry.**
   - Derive how pinned vortices couple to out-of-plane motion when B_ip is parallel to the rotation axis. Distinguish direct mechanical stiffening from vortex-induced (Campbell) inductance entering Eq. 3.
   - Use the correct density (or explicit mode-shape integrals) in Eqs. 8–9, and justify m_eff.
   - Model the position dependence of jump amplitudes ∝ φ²(x₀).
   - Reconcile k = 1.81 with α_L ∝ n_v⟨k_p⟩, or identify an alternative origin of the background.
   - Perform GL/TDGL calculations for a ~110 × 200 nm Al cross-section in parallel field. These should establish whether vortices exist (d/ξ thresholds), at which entry fields, and with what expected spacing and number of vortices.
   - Replace the perpendicular-strip formulas (Eqs. 6–7) with expressions appropriate to parallel-field entry.

4. **Ground the material parameters and recalibrate the claims and presentation.**
   - Measure ρ_n/RRR, T_c, the parallel critical field and, if possible, the perpendicular B_c2 on co-fabricated test structures to obtain l_f, ξ and λ experimentally. Propagate the uncertainties into κ_GL, d/ξ, the entry fields and U.
   - Reword "attonewton force" claims in terms of frequency or stiffness resolution.
   - Soften "single vortex" and "quantitative extraction" language unless the controls in suggestions 1–3 support them.
   - Compare explicitly with Bolle et al. 1999 and with vibrating-reed literature values, noting what is genuinely new.
   - Use a common reference field across Fig. 3 panels, overlap the field ranges of datasets within a cooldown, and show the full low-field data of the new runs.
   - Clarify the field geometry, i.e. the direction of B_ip relative to the string, in Fig. 1.
   - Fix minor issues:
     - the symbol α is used both for surface scattering and for junction asymmetry;
     - Ref. 79 is listed with an incorrect journal;
     - Refs. 34/70 and 42/82 are duplicates;
     - the Appendix states initial values but not which parameters remain fixed.
