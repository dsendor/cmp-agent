1. The Campbell-regime interpretation of the smooth stiffening (Ω0² ∝ Bip^k, k = 1.81 ± 0.05) rests on only six Bip values between 6.2 and 35 mT. Each Ω0 is an indirect output of a nonlinear least-squares fit of Eq. (3) to an out-of-plane flux sweep, and no per-point error bars or residuals are shown. With six points, a power law with k ≈ 1.8 is hard to distinguish from a pure B² dependence, a sum of terms, or a saturating form. The authors should (i) measure Ω0 at many more in-plane fields, especially below the expected vortex-entry field and near the upper end; (ii) show Ω0 uncertainties from the Eq. (3) fits, including parameter correlations; and (iii) compare the Campbell model quantitatively against competing models using goodness-of-fit or information criteria.

2. Plausible non-vortex mechanisms for the field-induced stiffening are not excluded experimentally. These include Meißner screening currents and kinetic-inductance or Lorentz-force tension in the thin Al string, field-dependent changes of the superconducting gap that affect the elastic modulus, magnetostatic or demagnetization forces, and drifts of the SQUID operating point as Bip changes. Key controls are missing:
(a) the same Ω0(Bip) measurement with the string in the normal state (T > Tc or Bip above the in-plane critical field);
(b) a field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled comparison, since the vortex configuration and pinning response should differ;
(c) up- versus down-sweep hysteresis, which is a hallmark of pinned vortices;
(d) the temperature dependence of α_L, which for Campbell physics should follow the temperature dependence of the pinning landscape.
Without these, attributing the stiffening to vortex pinning is not established.

3. The Labusch parameter α_L ~ 10^14 N/m^4 is described as an 'ensemble-averaged collective-pinning value'. However, the conversion from the frequency shift to α_L requires the number and distribution of vortices in the string, the string volume, and the effective coupling between vortex lattice and mechanical displacement. The paper should state explicitly every assumption entering this conversion (vortex density versus field, the values used for ξ and λ, the film-thickness geometry) and propagate their uncertainties. It should also compare the resulting α_L with values from Campbell penetration-depth measurements or Labusch-theory estimates for Al or similar films. Otherwise the number cannot be judged as physically reasonable.

4. The central claim that discrete frequency steps (~9 steps of 10–50 Hz between ~12 and 27 mT) correspond to the entry of single Abrikosov vortices is not adequately supported. The authors should:
(i) show raw Ω0 time and field traces with the frequency noise floor (e.g. Allan deviation) so the step significance can be assessed;
(ii) show that ωc and the SQUID flux operating point were tracked simultaneously, and that the steps are not accompanied by flux jumps in the SQUID loop, junctions, or cavity film;
(iii) report up- and down-sweeps and whether steps reverse, which is expected for vortex exit;
(iv) give statistics of step sizes and entry fields over many cooldowns.
The paper states that entry fields 'vary stochastically between thermal cycles' but does not say how many cycles or devices were measured. Without these, trapped-flux or SQUID-related artifacts cannot be ruled out, and single-vortex resolution cannot be claimed.

5. The step sizes and entry fields should be checked against expectations for single vortices in this geometry. Estimate the first-entry field for a thin Al string of the given width and thickness, using the stated ξ and λ and accounting for edge and geometric barriers. Then check whether ~9 entries between 12 and 27 mT agree with the expected change in vortex number for that field range and the string's cross-section. Also show that the magnitude of each 10–50 Hz step is consistent with a single flux quantum rather than flux bundles. This quantitative consistency check is currently missing and is necessary for the 'single vortex' interpretation.

6. The derived pinning energies (0.1–0.4 eV) and force changes (3–17 aN) depend strongly on unmeasured quantities: the position of each vortex along the string relative to the mode shape (a vortex near a clamp couples very differently from one at the antinode), the pinning length scale, and the assumed effective mass and mode profile. The derivation should be given step by step, with all assumed parameters listed and uncertainties propagated. The large spread of plausible vortex positions likely translates into order-of-magnitude uncertainties. The resulting numbers should be presented as ranges with error bars, not point values.

7. The 'attonewton force sensitivity' claim should be put in context with the actual measurement noise. Using the stated m_eff = 0.6 pg, Γm/2π = 20 Hz and T ≈ 85 mK (plus any excess mode temperature), the authors should compute the thermal force noise spectral density √(4 k_B T m_eff Γm) and the resulting minimum detectable force for their integration time. They should also compare it with the inferred 3–17 aN shifts. A frequency shift is a force-gradient measurement, not a direct force measurement, and the claim should be phrased precisely.

8. Several modeling assumptions in extracting Ω0 from the flux sweeps (Eq. (3), extending Luschmann et al. 2022 by adding the loop inductance Ll; Eq. (A1) adapted from Shevchuk et al. 2017) are not tested:
- symmetric junctions, with the asymmetry parameter apparently fixed at zero;
- negligible Meißner field enhancement at the string;
- fixed initial values (I0 = 1.3 µA, Ll = 136 pH, η = 0.9).
The authors should report fitted parameter values and uncertainties at each Bip, and show how Ω0 changes if the asymmetry is freed or field focusing is included. If these choices shift Ω0 by amounts comparable to the 10–50 Hz steps or to the smooth trend, the conclusions would change.

9. Optomechanical backaction and heating should be excluded explicitly. Because Ω0 is extracted through the cavity response, a dynamical optical-spring shift or probe-induced heating could produce field-dependent frequency shifts via the field-dependent SQUID responsivity. The authors state a probe power below 2 fW and C < 1, but they should show Ω0 versus intracavity photon number at several Bip values and verify that the extrapolated zero-photon frequency reproduces both the smooth trend and the steps.

10. Field alignment is critical but not characterized. A small misalignment of the nominally in-plane field produces an out-of-plane component, which threads flux through the SQUID loop and can nucleate vortices perpendicular to the film. Both effects could mimic or mask the reported behavior. The authors should report the alignment procedure and the residual misalignment angle, and ideally show measurements at deliberate small tilt angles to demonstrate that the smooth stiffening and the steps follow the in-plane component.

11. Reproducibility is not documented. The paper does not state how many devices, cooldowns, or field sweeps underlie the reported α_L, step statistics and pinning energies. For claims of a new sensing modality and single-vortex detection, results from at least a few cooldowns, and preferably more than one device or string geometry, should be shown, with the variability quantified.

12. The claims are stated with inconsistent strength across the paper. The abstract and introduction present single-vortex detection, pinning-energy extraction and attonewton force sensitivity as established results. The conclusion instead refers to 'signatures suggesting' vortex entry. The authors should either provide the additional controls listed above or tone down the abstract and introduction to match the evidence, and clearly separate demonstrated results from interpretation.

13. Novelty relative to the authors' own prior work is not clearly stated. The device platform (a nanomechanical string embedded in an Al dc-SQUID, flux-mediated optomechanics) appears to come from ref. 46 (Sci. Rep. 2022). The fitting model (Eq. 3, including loop inductance L_l) is cited to ref. 43. Fig. 4 overlays low-resolution data from ref. 46 with the new data. The Introduction should state explicitly what is new: the vortex interpretation, the higher-resolution field sweeps, or the inclusion of L_l. It should also say whether the earlier data contained the same features. The old model (Eq. A1) and Eq. 3 should be compared quantitatively, to show whether including L_l materially changes the extracted bare frequency Ω0(B_ip). The relation to the same group's later preprint (ref. 52) should also be clarified.

14. Positioning within the vortex-physics literature is thin. Vibrating-reed and Campbell-regime studies (e.g. Brandt, Esquinazi, Kober) are mentioned largely 'historically'. Hall probes, scanning SQUIDs and MFM are mentioned only qualitatively. The paper should explain what this approach can measure that these established techniques cannot: single-vortex resolution in a nanoscale strip, compatibility with operating circuits, or MHz-frequency pinning response. It should include a quantitative comparison table (force or flux sensitivity, spatial information, bandwidth, invasiveness, operating fields). Without this, the claim that optomechanics is a 'highly sensitive' or advantageous probe of vortex matter is not established.

15. The motivation (vortex-induced loss and decoherence in superconducting quantum circuits) is only loosely connected to the experiment. The device is a mechanical SQUID with a suspended Al beam under deliberately applied in-plane fields of tens of mT. The paper should explain how this method could inform or be transferred to qubit or resonator circuits, which have no mechanical element and operate near zero field. Otherwise the significance for quantum technology is overstated.

16. Whether a vortex (mixed) state can exist in the beam is not adequately justified. The dirty-limit estimate (Eq. 5) gives ξ ≈ 108 nm, λ ≈ 0.84 µm and κ ≈ 7.8. However, the vortices are parallel to a film only 110 nm thick and 200 nm wide. That is below the usual ~1.8ξ (~195 nm) thickness criterion for nucleating parallel vortices, and both dimensions are far smaller than λ. The authors should show that vortex states in this geometry are energetically allowed at the relevant fields, with references, or discuss alternatives such as surface or edge states and flux penetration into wider parts of the SQUID. The uncertainty in grain size and mean free path behind the dirty-limit renormalization should also be stated, since ξ and κ depend on it.

17. The interpretation of the smooth background Ω0² − Ω0²(B_ref) ∝ B^k (k = 1.81 ± 0.05) as Campbell-regime vortex pinning is not well supported. No theoretical expectation for the exponent is given. The value is close to the B² scaling expected for magnetomotive, Lorentz or screening-current springs. The text concedes these mechanisms may contribute, yet still calls vortex pinning the 'principal' or 'dominant' contribution. The arguments used to exclude alternatives (they are smooth and cannot produce jumps) apply only to the discrete jumps, not to the background. Also, the Eq. 3 fit subtracts a B²-scaling Lorentz spring with the asymmetry fixed at α = 0. Any misfit there could leak a near-B² trend into Ω0. The authors should test the vortex interpretation against the alternatives quantitatively, for example with field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled data, hysteresis in up/down sweeps, temperature dependence approaching T_c, or angle dependence.

18. The Labusch parameter α_L ~ 10^14 N m^-4 is derived from that same contested background. If non-vortex terms contribute, α_L is overestimated or meaningless. The value should also be compared with literature values for Al or similar thin films, or with the authors' own independent estimate from the jumps. The per-vortex stiffness implied by the background (~10–20 N/m²) and by the jumps (~14–70 N/m²) do not agree, and this discrepancy should be discussed.

19. The single-vortex attribution of the frequency jumps is stated with inconsistent confidence. The Abstract says 'attributable to individual vortex entry', while the Intro and Conclusion only say 'suggest'. The text also allows 'one or a few flux quanta' per jump, yet the pinning-energy estimate assumes exactly one. The expected field spacing per vortex is quoted as ~1.25 mT near entry and ~0.63 mT at high field; Φ0/(l·d) gives ~0.63 mT, and the factor of two in the first value is unexplained. The observed spacings are irregular, 1.5–5 mT. The paper should give a histogram or statistical analysis of jump sizes (in Hz and in field) to test quantization. It should compare the cumulative number of jumps with the expected vortex count. It should also explicitly rule out multi-vortex avalanches, flux entry into the SQUID arms, leads or pads (which would alter flux bias or critical current), TLS-like switching, and changes in the junctions.

20. The empirical entry field (~5 mT) lies far below the theoretical bounds quoted (~27.7 mT for zero-field cooling, ~282 mT for field cooling). This large gap is not explained. Edge defects, field focusing by the wider film, or misalignment of the in-plane field could explain it, but each would also affect the interpretation of later jumps. The choice of reference fields is also unexplained and inconsistent: 35 mT in Fig. 2(d) versus 20 mT in Fig. 3(a).

21. The statistical basis is too thin for the claims. Only three field sweeps were made: two runs in one cooldown and one after thermal cycling to room temperature, so effectively one comparison between thermal cycles. This cannot support the claim that entry fields are 'stochastic' between thermal cycles. It certainly cannot support the Conclusion's claim of 'directly mapping the disorder landscape', since the measurement has no spatial resolution. More cooldowns, up/down sweeps showing hysteresis, field-cooled data and temperature dependence would substantially strengthen the work.

22. The attonewton claims need careful qualification. The 'attonewton-scale forces' associated with jumps are defined as the spring-constant change times the thermal rms displacement (~1 pm). This is a constructed quantity, not a directly measured force. The quoted force noise (S_FF^1/2 ≈ 0.42 aN/√Hz, F_rms ≈ 0.4–1.3 aN over 1–10 Hz) is computed from the thermal formula assuming the 85 mK bath temperature and the measured linewidth. It is not an experimentally measured noise floor; mode temperature, readout noise and frequency noise are not demonstrated. The comparison with vibrating reeds and scanning probes ('pN') is qualitative. The paper should distinguish clearly between the resolved frequency shift, the inferred force, and the thermal-limit sensitivity, and benchmark measured frequency stability (e.g. Allan deviation) against the jump sizes.

23. The single-vortex pinning energies (0.1–0.4 eV) rest on U = ½ k_p r_p² L with r_p ≈ ξ and L ≈ beam width. Because U scales with r_p², the result is very sensitive to the assumed pinning range. The paper should justify these choices, propagate the uncertainties (in ξ, the jump stiffness and the number of vortices per jump), and compare with known pinning energies in Al or other low-κ films.

24. The dynamics of the vortex–mechanics coupling are not discussed. Why do pinned vortices add stiffness rather than damping at 5.8 MHz? How does the mechanical frequency compare with the depinning (Campbell) frequency and with creep rates? Linewidth or damping versus field is not reported; only a single ~20 Hz linewidth is given. Field-dependent damping would be a natural independent check of the pinning interpretation and of possible dissipation from vortex motion.

25. Key modelling assumptions need justification or testing. Junction asymmetry is fixed at α = 0 rather than fitted. Meißner-screening field enhancement is dismissed in a footnote (fn. 51). The cavity optomechanical spring is not modelled. Heating from the probe tone and in-plane field misalignment (which would add an out-of-plane flux component) are not analysed; only backaction is addressed, via cooperativity < 1 and < 2 fW probe power. The authors should show that these assumptions do not bias Ω0(B_ip).

26. The fitting methodology in Appendix A is under-documented. Only initial values are listed. The paper should state which parameters (I_c, L_l, η, α, Ω0, etc.) are free or fixed at each field. It should report parameter uncertainties, goodness of fit and correlations (e.g. between I_c and L_l), and show residuals. Since Ω0 at each field underlies every derived quantity (k, α_L, jump sizes, forces, energies), this information is essential to judge the significance of the results.

27. The Conclusion lists no limitations and overstates scope. 'Minimally invasive' is not justified, given the applied tens-of-mT fields, the microwave drive and the mechanical motion, which could themselves influence vortex entry and pinning. 'Mapping the disorder landscape' is not supported by spatially unresolved data from a few sweeps. A balanced discussion should cover the limitations (geometry, sample statistics, alternative mechanisms, model dependence) and concrete next steps.

28. Minor issues. The symbol α is used both for junction asymmetry and for the Labusch parameter (α_L); use distinct symbols. The reference list contains duplicates (e.g. 34/70, 42/82, 36/69/79), and ref. 79 appears to contain a citation error. The paper should state explicitly that the device in Fig. 4 is the same as in ref. 46, if so, and say whether the data come from different cooldowns and resolutions.

29. Justify the change of model and compare the two. The paper replaces the Shevchuk et al. model (Eq. A1, used in the authors' earlier work, which neglects the loop inductance Ll) with Eq. (3), which includes Ll. Please explain why this was needed. Please also show how much including Ll changes the extracted quantities: Ω0(Bip), the stiffening exponent and the Labusch parameter. A side-by-side fit or a short sensitivity analysis would be sufficient.

30. The fitting procedure in Appendix A is not reproducible. Please state: (i) which parameters among I0/Ic, Ll, η, Ω0, α and ϕoff are free and which are fixed; (ii) whether they are fitted separately at each Bip or globally across all fields; (iii) the parameter bounds, weighting, optimizer and software; (iv) goodness-of-fit measures; and (v) how parameter uncertainties are obtained. Please also say where the initial values (Ll = 136 pH, I0 = 1.3 µA, η = 0.9) come from, e.g. design values, earlier measurements or simulation. Finally, discuss possible correlations between I0 and Ll in the fit and whether they are separately identifiable.

31. The fitted values are not reported. Please give the final Ic/I0, Ll, η, α and ϕoff, with uncertainties, either for each Bip or as a table or plot in the appendix. Readers cannot currently check whether these parameters stay constant, as expected, while Ω0 changes with field.

32. Notation is inconsistent. (a) α denotes both the junction asymmetry (Eq. A1) and, as αL, the Labusch parameter; please use distinct symbols. (b) Footnote 55 says the junctions are symmetric, yet Eq. (A1) contains α and Appendix A appears to allow it to be fitted, while Fig. 1(b) draws separate LJ1 and LJ2. Please state clearly whether α is fixed at 0. (c) The critical current is called Ic in Eq. (3) but I0 in Appendix A. (d) The Fig. 1 caption reads 'L1/2' where 'Ll/2' appears intended.

33. The flux variables are easy to confuse. ϕ = Φb/Φ0 and φb = πΦb/Φ0 differ by a factor of π; please use a single convention or flag the difference clearly. Please also explain why the flux offset ϕoff appears only in the Josephson-inductance term and not elsewhere in the model.

34. Several symbols in Eq. (A1) are not defined in the appendix: EJ, l, meff, S0(Φb), and the role of the Bip² prefactor. Please define every symbol where it first appears, or add a symbol table.

35. The Fig. 2(d) caption and fit description are inconsistent. The caption says the fit is to Ω0(Bip), but the vertical axis shows Ω0² − Ω0²(35 mT). The fit form is written once as A0 + C·Bip^k and elsewhere as Ω0,bare² + ϑBip^k. Please give one unambiguous expression and state which quantity is actually fitted. Please also give the field range used, the reason for choosing 35 mT as the reference, and the uncertainty on the exponent (1.81).

36. Make the extraction of the Labusch parameter (αL ~ 10^14 N m^-4) from the Ω0²(Bip) stiffening explicit. Please write out each step, the assumptions of the Campbell-regime model, the vortex density and geometry used, and the uncertainty. Please also justify the claim that the system is in the Campbell (linear, small-displacement) regime, for example by comparing the oscillation amplitude with the pinning-well size.

37. The Meißner field-enhancement assumption in Footnote 51 needs justification. It sets the effective field at the string, so it directly affects the stiffening exponent, αL and the inferred vortex numbers. Please justify it or show how the results depend on it.

38. The attribution of the discrete frequency jumps to single Abrikosov vortices is worded tentatively ('signatures suggesting'). Please state clearly what evidence supports single-vortex entry rather than multiple vortices, flux bundles, or changes in the SQUID or junction flux. Please also explain how jumps are detected: the threshold and criteria, and how they are separated from noise or flux-bias drift.

39. The headline single-vortex numbers cannot currently be reproduced. Please give the formulas, input values and uncertainties used to obtain the attonewton force sensitivity and the 0.1–0.4 eV single-vortex pinning energies, so that readers can recompute them from the reported data.

40. The claim that stochastic entry fields 'map the disorder landscape' needs support. Please report how many thermal cycles were performed, how many jumps were recorded, the statistical analysis of entry fields between cycles (e.g. distributions), and why this variation reflects pinning disorder rather than other effects.

41. The device description is incomplete. Missing items:
- the tensile stress, the superconducting Tc, and how λ ≈ 0.84 µm and ξ ≈ 108 nm were obtained (measured, estimated, or taken from the literature);
- fabrication details, which are deferred entirely to Ref. 44 and should be summarized at least briefly;
- the orientation of Bip relative to the string axis, which is never stated explicitly;
- how the in-plane field is aligned and the out-of-plane misalignment checked or compensated;
- the field sweep rate and direction, and whether hysteresis was examined;
- the zero-field-cooling protocol between cycles.

42. The measurement chain and backaction are not specified well enough to reproduce the measurement. Please report the input attenuation, the power at the device (rather than only '<2 fW'), the intracavity photon number, the cooperativity and the thermal occupation. Please also justify quantitatively why backaction is negligible (C < 1) at all field values, since g0 depends on Bip.

43. Ω0 is measured by thermal-noise sideband spectroscopy at some fields and by a VNA at others. Please mark which data points use which method, state how the switch was decided, and show that the two methods agree in an overlapping region. Please also explain how Γm (20 Hz) was determined and whether it changes with field.

44. The data are on Zenodo, but no analysis code is provided for the SQUID-model fits, PSD fits or jump detection. Please release the code, or describe the algorithms in enough detail to reproduce them.

45. Please add brief background on the Campbell regime, the Labusch parameter and vortex pinning in thin films, and on how vortex dynamics relates to flux noise in superconducting qubits. This would make the paper accessible to readers from optomechanics who do not specialize in vortex physics.

46. The reference list needs cleaning:
- there are duplicate entries (e.g. 34/70, 42/82, 36/69/79);
- ref. 79 appears to give the wrong journal;
- several entries lack years or volumes (e.g. refs. 5, 9, 12, 36, 70);
- there is a typo, 'Gorodetksy'.
The back matter (acknowledgments and the Appendix heading) also appears out of order.
