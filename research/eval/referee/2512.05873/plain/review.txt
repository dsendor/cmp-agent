Referee report

Manuscript: "Resolving Abrikosov vortex entry in superconducting nano-string resonators via displacement-noise spectroscopy in cavity-optomechanics" (arXiv:2512.05873v1)

1. Summary

The authors embed a suspended Al nanostring (l, w, d) = (30, 0.2, 0.11) um in the loop of a dc SQUID that terminates a lambda/4 CPW resonator, and read out the string's thermal motion through motional sidebands. At each in-plane field Bip they sweep the out-of-plane flux bias, fit the dome-shaped Omega_m(Phi_b) with a Lorentz-force spring model (Eq. 3), and so extract a flux-independent baseline Omega_0(Bip). Two features are reported: (i) a smooth offset power law, Omega_0^2 = Omega_0,bare^2 + C Bip^k with k = 1.81 +/- 0.05, which they attribute to collective Campbell/Labusch pinning elasticity; and (ii) a handful of abrupt steps of 10 to 50 Hz (Delta Omega/Omega ~ 1e-5), irregularly spaced by 1.5 to 5 mT, with positions that differ after a thermal cycle. They attribute these to the entry of single vortices or small bundles, and from them extract single-vortex pinning stiffnesses (14 to 70 N/m^2), pinning energies (0.09 to 0.44 eV) and "attonewton-scale" force steps.

The platform is interesting. A mechanical probe built into a device, sensitive to the superconducting state of a sub-micron element under in-plane field, would be useful to both the vortex physics and the superconducting circuits communities. The data quality of the Omega_0(Bip) curves is good, and the paper builds sensibly on the authors' earlier work (Ref. 46). However, I do not think the manuscript yet establishes its central claim, that the steps resolve individual Abrikosov vortex entry and that the smooth background is Campbell-regime pinning elasticity. There are problems with whether vortices can exist in this geometry at all, with the mechanism that would make pinned vortices stiffen a translating string, with the internal consistency of "smooth progressive penetration" plus "discrete single-vortex steps", and with ruling out fit and measurement artifacts. I recommend major revision.

2. Major issues

2.1 Can this cross-section host Abrikosov vortices at all?
The paper's own estimates give xi ~ 108 nm and lambda ~ 0.84 um, with d = 110 nm and w = 200 nm. From the area used (A = l d) and the vortex length used (L_line ~ w), the field and the vortices run along w, so the confining dimension perpendicular to the vortex axis is d ~ xi. A vortex core has diameter ~2 xi ~ 220 nm, twice the film thickness. For a slab in parallel field, standard GL results (Saint-James and de Gennes; Tinkham, Ref. 32, ch. 4) give no vortex state for d below roughly 1.8 xi. Such a film instead goes normal through a second-order transition at an enhanced parallel critical field, H_c,par ~ 2 sqrt(6) H_c lambda/d, without vortices entering. Since d is also much smaller than lambda, screening of Bip is weak, and the kappa_GL = 7.8 argument (Section IV) does not by itself imply vortex entry in this geometry. The authors should:
 (a) state the field orientation relative to the string explicitly. Fig. 1(c) appears to draw the flux lines along the string's long axis, which conflicts with A = l d and L_line ~ w;
 (b) discuss the d ~ xi, d << lambda regime and explain why vortices, rather than a smooth pair-breaking suppression of the order parameter, are expected;
 (c) clarify the layer structure of the string. Double-angle shadow evaporation often leaves a bilayer with an AlOx interlayer in suspended elements. If the string is two thinner Al layers, the relevant d is smaller still and the physics changes (Josephson-coupled layers).
Measured Tc, normal-state resistivity (giving l_f directly, rather than equating it to grain size), and the parallel critical field of the string or a co-deposited film would constrain xi and lambda far better than the bulk-based estimates of Eq. (5).

2.2 What makes pinned vortices stiffen a translating string?
Eq. (8) adds the Labusch constant alpha_L as a uniform spring density. But a Labusch restoring force acts on the displacement of a vortex relative to its pinning site. When the string undergoes a small transverse displacement in a spatially uniform static field, the pinned vortices simply move with the lattice. Nothing in the lab frame holds them back, so the pinning springs are not stretched. In vibrating-reed experiments (Refs. 38 to 42, 85), the stiffening comes from the tilt between the moving sample and the fixed external field. It is set by flux-line tension and magnetic pressure (~B^2/mu_0, with pinning entering through the Campbell length), not by alpha_L acting as a local spring density. For a string whose displacement is out-of-plane with B along w, bending rotates the string axis but not the w direction, so even that tilt mechanism appears to vanish at first order. The authors need to identify the force that displaces the vortices relative to the pins when the string moves. Possible candidates are field gradients, the oscillating circulating current J in the SQUID loop (which exerts a Lorentz force on vortices in the string), and tilt. They also need to derive the resulting stiffness, including its geometric factors. If the relevant drive is the SQUID current J, the effect should depend on Phi_b and would partly sit in the term the fit removes, which then changes what Omega_0 means.

2.3 Smooth background and discrete steps are not mutually consistent as presented
By the authors' own estimate (Eq. 7 and the following text), entry spacings are 0.6 to 1.25 mT per vortex, so roughly 20 vortices enter between 5 and 30 mT. If alpha_L ~ n_v <k_p> and vortex number changes only at entry events, Omega_0^2 should be a staircase. Between entries it should be flat, apart from whatever field dependence <k_p> has. In Fig. 3(a), the smooth rise between 12 and 15.5 mT is comparable to the step at 15.5 mT, and most of the total change over each dataset is smooth. Either most vortices enter unresolved, so the identified steps are not special and the "single vortex" calibration of Section V is not justified, or the smooth part is not vortex-number driven. Further points:
 - The uniform-pinning form alpha_L ~ (B/Phi_0)<k_p> with constant <k_p> predicts k = 1. The observed k = 1.81 requires <k_p> ~ B^0.81, which is not discussed, yet <k_p> = alpha_L Phi_0/Bip is then quoted as a single number (10 to 20 N/m^2).
 - The empirical entry field Bc1 ~ 5 mT is said to be where the trend "sets in" (Fig. 2(d)). But the fitted form A0 + C B^k has no threshold, the Fig. 4 inset shows alpha_L rising continuously from B = 0, and the data simply begin near 5 mT. Please fit a threshold form (for example Eq. 7 with Bc1 free) and report whether the data actually require a finite onset field.
 - The text calls the k = 1.81 exponent "reproduced consistently" across datasets, but Figs. 3(a), 3(b) and 4 all appear to show the same fit line. Please report k fitted independently for each run and cooldown, with and without the step regions, and state the fit range.
 - The authors themselves list magnetomotive stiffening, in-plane screening and higher-order Lorentz-force corrections as possible origins of the near-quadratic background (Section III). Any B^2 mechanism (an imperfectly subtracted Lorentz term, field-induced pair breaking changing kinetic inductance, Fraunhofer suppression of junction Ic by the in-plane field) would give k near 2. These need to be quantitatively excluded before the background is assigned to Campbell elasticity, and a k of 1.81 versus 2 is not by itself discriminating.

2.4 Are the steps intrinsic to the string, and are they robust?
 - Omega_0 is a fitted parameter of Eq. (3), not a directly measured frequency. A vortex entering the SQUID arms, the junction electrodes or the CPW (all thin Al films in the same Bip) would abruptly change the flux offset, Ic or the loop inductance. Depending on which parameters are free, that could appear as a step in Omega_0. Please (i) state which parameters are free at each Bip; (ii) plot the fitted Ic, Ll, eta and phi_off versus Bip alongside Omega_0, with their covariances; (iii) show the raw Omega_m(Phi_b) curves just below and just above one or two steps; and (iv) give a model-independent check, for example Omega_m measured directly near Phi_b = Phi_0/2, where the Lorentz term is minimal.
 - In Fig. 3(a), the step marked at 20.0 mT coincides with the boundary between Dataset 2 and Dataset 1 and with the reference field Omega_0(20 mT). It may be a stitching offset between runs rather than an event. Please clarify, or remove it from the event list.
 - The smallest quoted steps (10 Hz, about 1e8 Hz^2 in Omega_0^2) are comparable to the error bars in Figs. 3 and 4 and to half the mechanical linewidth (Gamma_m/2pi = 20 Hz). A quantitative step-detection criterion (change-point analysis, significance relative to the point-to-point scatter) is needed, together with the distribution of step sizes.
 - Nanomechanical resonators show spontaneous frequency jumps and drifts from two-level systems, adsorbates and temperature fluctuations, of the same order (tens of Hz) over hours. Please show a control: frequency versus time at fixed Bip, and a zero-field or below-onset run of comparable duration, to show that steps occur only when Bip is changed.
 - Vortex entry should be hysteretic. Please give the sweep protocol (ramp direction, waiting time, whether the field returned to zero between datasets) and show a down-sweep. Within one cooldown, the two datasets cover non-overlapping field ranges, so the within-cooldown reproducibility of step positions is not tested. A repeated up-sweep over the same range, after cycling the field to zero, would distinguish history-dependent vortex entry from fixed-field artifacts.

2.5 Quantitative extraction
 - rho_eff = 910 kg/m^3 equals m_eff divided by the geometric volume l w d (6e-16 kg / 6.6e-19 m^3), mixing a modal mass with a geometric volume. For a uniform spring density acting on a mode of uniform material density, the mode shape cancels and Delta Omega^2 = alpha_L / rho, with rho ~ 2700 kg/m^3 for Al. Using 910 changes every alpha_L, Delta alpha_L, k_p and U value by a factor of about 3. Please define V_eff and justify the choice. Relatedly, m_eff = 0.6 pg is about a third of the geometric mass (about 1.8 pg), whereas a string's fundamental mode has m_eff = m/2. Please reconcile.
 - The single-vortex k_p and U assume one vortex per step. The text itself allows "one or a few flux quanta" per step, so these are upper bounds per vortex and should be stated as such, including in the abstract and conclusion (0.1 to 0.4 eV).
 - U = (1/2) k_p r_p^2 L_line with r_p ~ xi extends a harmonic approximation to the full well width and takes L_line = w. It is an order-of-magnitude estimate and should be labelled so. A comparison with the core condensation energy (Bc^2/2mu_0) pi xi^2 w, which is of order 1 eV for Al, would help readers judge plausibility.

2.6 The "attonewton force" framing
What is measured is a change of spring constant, detected as a frequency shift. The product Delta k x_rms is not a force that is applied or detected, and comparing it with the thermal force noise integrated over a chosen 1 to 10 Hz bandwidth does not establish resolvability. The relevant comparison is the frequency step against the frequency-estimation precision (Allan deviation, or the fit uncertainty on Omega_m at the averaging time actually used). I suggest restating the sensitivity in those terms and removing "attonewton-scale forces" from the abstract, or justifying it rigorously. The noise estimate also assumes the mode is thermalized at 85 mK. Please state the measured mode occupation or temperature, and the convention used for S_FF.

3. Minor issues

 - The field-cooled threshold B = 1.65 Phi_0/d^2 (Stan, Field and Martinis, Refs. 34/70) is the maximum cooling field below which no vortices are trapped in a narrow strip. It is not an enhanced Bean-Livingston barrier that "delays penetration". It is also attributed to Ref. 72. Please correct the description and the citation. The ZFC formula, Eq. (6), is the Likharev/Stan expression for a strip in perpendicular field; using d as the strip width for a parallel-field geometry should be justified.
 - The discussion of jump spacing invokes "local thickness decrease at rough edges" to explain spacings larger than expected. This is qualitative, and it should be reconciled with point 2.3.
 - The conclusion and Section V claim in situ determination of Bc1 and Bc2. Bc2 is not measured, and Bc1 is assigned, not determined. Please soften.
 - Omega_0(Bip) is called the "intrinsic" frequency, yet it is field dependent. Consider "flux-independent baseline frequency".
 - Notation: Eq. (4) uses Omega_0,bare and theta, while the figures use A0 + C Bip^k. alpha is used both for surface scattering (Eq. 5) and junction asymmetry (Appendix A). Ic and I0 are both used for the critical current. Ll and L1 both appear in the Fig. 1 caption. E_J and S_0 in Eq. (A1) are undefined. The sign convention for the Lorentz spring term should be stated.
 - Fig. 4 caption: the data are described as "blue circles", but they appear green. The text says "black filled circles" for the Ref. 46 data, while the caption says "black squares". Please give the y-axis reference and the meaning of the error bars consistently across Figs. 2 to 4.
 - "Cavity optomechanics, also known as nano-electromechanics": these are not synonyms. Please rephrase.
 - References: 42 and 82 are identical. 34 and 70 are identical. 36, 69 and 79 are the same paper, and 79 is given as Phys. Rev. B instead of Phys. Rev. Applied. Refs. 9, 12 and 70 lack years. Please also check that Ref. 56 (Bolle et al.) is an appropriate precedent: that experiment probed magnetization jumps of mesoscopic samples mounted on oscillators, which is a different coupling mechanism.
 - The measurement time per field point and the total duration of each sweep should be given. They are needed to assess drift (point 2.4).
 - Data are deposited on Zenodo, which is welcome. Including the raw Omega_m(Phi_b) sweeps and the fit outputs per field would make points 2.4 and 2.5 checkable.

4. Assessment

The experimental technique is promising, and the observation of irregular, cooldown-dependent steps in the string's baseline frequency under in-plane field is potentially interesting. As written, however, the vortex interpretation rests on estimates that place the string in a regime (d ~ xi << lambda) where vortex states are not generically expected. It applies a Labusch spring-density model whose coupling to rigid string motion is not derived. It combines a smooth "progressive penetration" background with discrete "single vortex" steps in a way that is internally inconsistent for a sample hosting only about 20 vortices. And it does not yet exclude fit-parameter, junction or SQUID-arm, stitching, and drift artifacts as sources of the steps. The quantitative pinning energies also depend on a density choice that shifts them by a factor of about 3, and on the one-vortex-per-step assumption. I would be glad to see a revised version that addresses Section 2, in particular the controls in 2.4 and a mechanism and geometry discussion for 2.1 to 2.3. With those, the paper could be suitable for publication. At present the claims in the title and abstract go beyond what the data support.

Recommendation: major revision.
