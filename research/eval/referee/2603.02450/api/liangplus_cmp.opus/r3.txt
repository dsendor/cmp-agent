Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports a reproducible, history-dependent critical current in bulk UTe₂ single crystals at 50 mK. An abrupt drop of a large dc current leaves the sample in a state with higher J_c. A smooth ramp-down restores the lower-J_c state. The effect appears only in a window of field and angle (B ∥ b̂ ± ~20°, roughly 7–20 T, depending on sample) that the authors associate with SC1/SC2 coexistence.

If the effect can be shown to arise from the multiphase order parameter, it would be a notable addition to UTe₂ vortex physics. However, current-switchable metastable vortex states with enhanced J_c are well documented in conventional weakly pinned superconductors. The paper's central novelty claims are therefore that the effect is "intrinsic", "rooted in the superconducting order itself", and a first electrically controllable superconducting memory. These claims are not yet established against that background or against a thermal-quench write mechanism.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The phenomenology is clear and reproducible within a run. Pump/erase protocols in Figs. 1–2 give consistent, saturating dependences on amplitude, duration and turn-off rate (ED Fig. 1).
- The effect is observed in two samples (S1, S2) and in both current polarities (ED Fig. 4).
- Built-in null regions are measured with the same contacts and protocol:
  - low field (3–5 T, Fig. 4c; ED Fig. 8);
  - the SC2 phase at 29–30 T at all angles (ED Fig. 10);
  - tilt angles beyond ~20° (ED Fig. 7).
- The B–θ mapping is extensive and is correlated with features in equilibrium V(B) and V(θ) (Fig. 4b, g).
- The measurement code is to be supplied, which aids reproducibility.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

**1. Relation to known metastable vortex states in conventional superconductors is not discussed or tested.**
- The paper states that intrinsic, electrically controllable superconducting memory "has remained elusive" (pp. 2–4) and attributes the effect to two competing vortex species (p. 13). The observed phenomenology is closely analogous to well-studied cases:
  - disordered, high-J_c metastable vortex states in 2H-NbSe₂ near the peak effect, which are created or annealed by current pulses and depend on pulse amplitude, duration and rise/fall time (e.g. Henderson et al., PRL 1996; Xiao, Andrei & Higgins, PRL 1999);
  - edge-contamination injection of disordered vortex matter (Paltiel et al., Nature 2000);
  - current-"shaking" memory in YBCO (Valenzuela & Bekeris, PRL 2000).
- In those systems the effect needs only a soft, weakly pinned vortex lattice near an order–disorder crossover. No second order parameter is involved.
- The paper's own data point the same way. The memory window coincides with the region of anomalously low J_c and enhanced flux-flow voltage (Fig. 4b hump; ED Fig. 9; ref. 46). Bulk J_c here is only ~1–2 A cm⁻², which is extraordinarily weak pinning for a bulk crystal.
- The authors should:
  - discuss this literature explicitly;
  - state which observation distinguishes their mechanism from generic order–disorder metastability;
  - compare field-cooled and zero-field-cooled states at fixed B, and the state immediately after a field ramp. In the generic picture, FC typically yields the disordered high-J_c state;
  - ideally run the identical protocol, contacts and setup on a conventional reference superconductor in its peak-effect or low-J_c regime.
- Without this, "intrinsic", "rooted in the superconducting order itself" and the link to p-wave vortex matter do not follow from the data.

**2. A thermal-quench write mechanism is not excluded, and the heating budget is not quantified.**
- The arguments against heating (pp. 12–13) mainly target heating as an artifact that would mimic hysteresis. They do not address heating as the write mechanism.
- A current pulse that warms the sample or contacts, followed by fast cool-down when the current is switched off abruptly, is a temperature quench. It would freeze in a disordered vortex configuration.
- On that reading:
  - faster turn-off rates α give more memory (Fig. 2c; ED Fig. 1c) because cooling is faster, not because less energy was deposited;
  - memory being absent outside the window only means no metastable configurations exist there;
  - the disappearance above ~0.6 K (Fig. 3) is a natural scale for the quench target.
- Spot-welded contacts carrying 20–40 mA (reset from 40 mA, Methods) could dissipate microwatts to milliwatts at 50 mK. This is far above typical mixing-chamber cooling power. Contact resistances, sample dimensions and cross-sections, and thermometer location are not reported. The Supplementary Information with T(t) is referenced but was not available for review, and a mixing-chamber thermometer would not resolve the sample's electron temperature on ms timescales in any case.
- The authors should:
  - report contact resistances and the dissipated power for each protocol;
  - attempt a write using a heater or thermal pulse at J = 0 (or below J_c) with matched temperature excursion and quench rate;
  - decouple J from J²Δt, e.g. pulses of equal energy but different amplitude, and pulses below J_c that heat the contacts but drive no flux flow;
  - if possible, use an on-sample thermometer or a second "witness" sample.

**3. Retention, the basis of the "non-volatile" claim, is asserted but not shown.**
- The text states the memory state "persists over a timescale of at least several hours" (pp. 5, 7) and "does not appear to be transiently metastable". No figure shows J_c or ΔJ against waiting time.
- The Methods describe the same state as "a long-lived transient (metastable) state", which contradicts that wording.
- Please add retention curves: ΔJ against waiting time at zero current, at several temperatures (e.g. 50, 200 and 400 mK). Decay with temperature would give an activation scale. That scale is directly relevant to the claim of a low-energy intrinsic phenomenon and to the comparison with flux creep.
- Please also state whether waiting at the probe current (read-disturb) or at zero current changes the state.

**4. The current waveforms actually reaching the sample are not established, and the protocols are ambiguous.**
- All V(J) data use the Keithley 6221/2182A pulse-delta mode. In that mode each data point is itself a current pulse, with a low level between pulses, at fast rise and fall times.
- If the low level was zero, every probe point is an abrupt J → 0 step, i.e. a "pump". The "smooth" erase (40 mA to 0 in 2 mA steps over 20 s) then consists of discrete 2 mA jumps. These may be comparable to the write threshold of ~3–4 A cm⁻² in ED Fig. 1a, depending on cross-section.
- In Fig. 1d,e the erase also appears as a rectangular step of lower amplitude, rather than a smooth ramp. This differs from the narrative on p. 13 that erasure requires gradual ramp-down.
- The nominal timescales also need checking against the wiring. Fig. 2b uses 10 μs pulses, α reaches 500 A cm⁻² ms⁻¹, and the arbitrary waveform is defined by 100 points. Dilution-fridge lines normally include RC or π filters with kHz-range cutoffs, and the 6221 has range-dependent rise times. The ~1 ms saturation in ED Fig. 1b and the α dependence could partly reflect filtering.
- Please specify for every protocol:
  - pulse width, low level, repetition interval and duty cycle;
  - filtering on current lines;
  - measured current waveforms at the sample end (e.g. via a shunt or voltage across a reference resistor at base temperature).
- Please also explain why pulse-delta probe points do not themselves write.

**5. The identification of the memory region with SC1/SC2 coexistence is not established for the samples studied.**
- In S2 (RRR 44) the memory starts at ~7 T, peaks at ~10 T and is essentially gone by 15–16 T at 0° (Fig. 4e; ED Fig. 8). This lies largely below the ~20 T thermodynamic SC1/SC2 boundary (ref. 9) and below the 15–20 T coexistence window of refs. 10 and 46.
- In S1 the window sits near 20 T (Fig. 1b; ED Fig. 9). The text attributes this sample dependence to "crystalline quality", but that is not demonstrated.
- The angular cutoff at θ ≈ 20° is matched to the SC2 angular extent from ref. 33 for high-quality crystals, not measured in S2.
- No independent determination of the SC1/SC2 boundary is presented for S1 or S2 (e.g. ac susceptibility, specific heat, T_c(B) kinks, or the SC2 onset field and angle in these crystals).
- The only correlates offered are features in equilibrium V(B) and V(θ) (Fig. 4b, g). These track J_c, which is consistent with Concern 1 rather than evidence for two phases.
- Please measure the phase boundary (B and θ) in the same samples and overlay it on ΔJ(B, θ). Please also explain how a coexistence region can extend down to 7 T in S2.

**B. Should address**

**6. The temperature-dependence argument is weaker than presented.**
- Faster thermal relaxation of metastable vortex configurations at higher T (creep, annealing) generically limits observable memory to low T. A low onset temperature therefore does not by itself indicate "an intrinsic low-energy phenomenon distinct from superconductivity" (p. 9).
- The analogy with the intrinsic superconducting diode effect (ref. 49) is not apt: that argument concerns symmetry breaking of the condensate, not vortex metastability.
- In Fig. 3a (S1, 20 T) the zero-voltage plateau has nearly vanished by 650 mK–1 K. The loss of hysteresis may therefore simply track J_c → 0.
- Please plot ΔJ/J_c(T) and relate the vanishing temperature to the retention-derived activation scale (Concern 3).

**7. Reproducibility and quantitative consistency across figures.**
- Memory protocols are reported on only two samples. The highest-quality crystal, S3 (RRR 395), appears only in ac R(B) (Fig. 4h) and was apparently not tested for memory. Testing it would directly probe the quality dependence claimed on p. 9.
- No data are shown after thermal cycling above T_c or across cooldowns.
- Quoted magnitudes for S2 at 14 T and 50 mK differ substantially between figures:
  - ED Fig. 1a saturates at ΔJ ≈ 0.55 A cm⁻² (amplitude tuning, evaluated at V = 1 μV);
  - the Fig. 3b heatmap peaks at ≈0.25 A cm⁻²;
  - Fig. 4e at 0° and ~14 T shows ≈0.1–0.2 A cm⁻².
- The text says ΔJ "peaks just above 0.2 A cm⁻² at around 10 T", whereas Fig. 4e points appear to reach ~0.3.
- Please reconcile these values. Please also define ΔJ's reference curve and voltage criterion uniformly, state the J_c criterion, and add error bars from repeated identical runs to Fig. 4e,f and ED Fig. 1. This is needed to define what "ΔJ reaches zero within resolution" means.
- Heatmap ΔJ values near V → 0 are noise-sensitive and should be treated with care.

**8. Switching reliability is shown only anecdotally.**
- Fig. 1d shows five write/erase cycles with a read signal of ~0.5 μV against zero.
- For a memory claim, please report:
  - write and erase success probabilities over many (≥10²) cycles;
  - the distribution of ΔJ;
  - the write threshold and its spread;
  - read-disturb, i.e. whether reading in the flux-flow state of the "0" bit drifts the state.

**9. The RSJ model is phenomenological, and its connection to the proposed physics is not demonstrated.**
- A bulk mm-scale crystal is represented by a single phase variable with a hand-built potential (Eq. 1) and a damping profile chosen to make the φ = π minimum "sticky" (β = 40, δ = 20, γ₀ = γ_π = 1). The parameter g is never specified.
- The memory behaviour is therefore largely built in, and the comparison with data is qualitative. The simulated curves in Fig. 3c show step structures absent in the data.
- The physical meaning of φ = π at zero current in a bulk crystal is unclear. The inference "inversion unbroken → minimum must be at π" relies on this single-variable picture.
- The Methods sentence "treat γ as a proxy to the injected current J" is unclear.
- The model and text make a checkable claim that the memory state has larger damping, i.e. lower flux-flow resistance. This should be tested on the measured dV/dJ above J_c.
- The model contains no B, θ or T, so it cannot account for the window. Please present it explicitly as a minimal phenomenology. Alternatively, derive testable predictions, e.g. the α threshold or the Δt saturation, and compare them quantitatively with ED Fig. 1.

**10. The Bean-model argument and self-field remanence are mis-stated.**
- The statement that a Bean-model or Bean–Livingston process "should be insensitive to how the current is turned off, depending only on the total energy imparted" (p. 13) conflates two things. The critical-state model is rate-independent but not energy-based, and flux creep makes real critical states rate-dependent.
- Transport current through a bulk sample leaves a remanent self-field current distribution that depends on history, including the sign and manner of the last excursion. This is the classic origin of transport hysteresis (ref. 25).
- Please estimate self-field against J_c × thickness for the actual geometry and discuss whether remanent self-field states can account for part of ΔJ.

**11. Bulk versus surface or contact origin.**
- Current is injected through spot-welded contacts on the (001) face, J ∥ a, so vortex motion is along c.
- Edge and surface barriers can inject disordered vortex matter (Paltiel mechanism). Non-uniform current injection near the contacts could also host the switching region.
- Measurements using multiple voltage-contact pairs at different distances from the current contacts, or on a FIB-defined bar with uniform current, would test whether the effect is bulk and spatially uniform.
- Please give the sample dimensions and the contact geometry.

**12. Field-angle calibration.**
- The angular cutoff (θ ≈ 19–20°) is a key piece of evidence. Please state how θ was calibrated, its uncertainty, and the residual misalignment toward â.
- SC2 and J_c in UTe₂ are sensitive to small b→a tilts.

**C. Minor**

**13. Technological claims need qualification.**
- The ~1 nW read power excludes contact dissipation, the write energy (flux-flow pulses of up to 20 A cm⁻²), cryogenic overhead at 50 mK, and the 7–20 T bias field.
- "Both read and write operations can be performed while superconducting" sits uneasily with reading in, and writing via, the dissipative flux-flow state.
- Ref. 59 (intrinsic carrier density in silicon) does not support the power comparison.

**14. Speculative statements should be clearly labelled as such.**
- These include the link to triplet/p-wave vortex matter, ³He A-phase cores, Majorana modes and topological quantum computing. Nothing in the data depends on the triplet character of the pairing.

**15. Labels, units and wording.**
- ED Fig. 1a: x-axis reads "J_ex (ms)".
- ED Fig. 5b: α given in A cm⁻² s⁻¹, versus ms⁻¹ elsewhere.
- Fig. 3b: "J_ext" versus J_ex.
- Methods: "global minimum at π = 0".
- Refs. 26 and 57 are duplicates.
- "Minimise entropy" should probably be free energy.
- "Pinning forces between vortices" should be vortex–defect pinning or collective-pinning correlation volume.
- Fig. 1a: check that the schematic purple region's temperature extent is consistent with the measured <0.6 K closure.
- Fig. 4h (S3, ac, NHMFL): clarify its relevance, since an ac excitation continuously shakes vortices and no memory protocol was run.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Decisive mechanism controls.** Run thermal-pulse writes at J = 0, FC versus ZFC preparation, equal-energy pulses of different amplitude, and the identical protocol on a conventional reference superconductor. Together these separate "multiphase-intrinsic" from generic vortex order–disorder metastability and from thermal quenching.
2. **Same-sample phase mapping.** Determine the SC1/SC2 boundary in B and θ (ac susceptibility or specific heat) in S1, S2 and the high-RRR S3. Overlay ΔJ(B, θ) and J_c(B, θ) to test whether the memory tracks phase coexistence or simply weak pinning.
3. **Direct probes of the proposed glassy state.** Use broadband voltage-noise spectroscopy, local magnetometry (Hall or scanning-SQUID array, magneto-optics) or SANS before and after a pump. These would directly test the claim of a quenched, more disordered vortex configuration and the "two vortex species" hypothesis.
4. **Device-relevant metrics with uncertainties.** Report retention against T (with the extracted barrier), cycle statistics, write threshold, read-disturb, and measured on-sample waveforms. Re-express the model's predictions quantitatively against these.
