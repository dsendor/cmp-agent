**Review outline:**

## 1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports that, in bulk UTe₂ crystals with B near the b axis and T ≲ 0.5 K, the transport critical current density is history-dependent and current-controlled:
- An abrupt turn-off of a large current ("pump") raises J_c by roughly 10–150%, depending on sample and field.
- A slow ramp-down ("erase") restores the lower value.
- The size of the effect depends on pulse amplitude, duration and turn-off rate.
- The effect is confined to a field–angle window below the SC2 phase.

If the effect is intrinsic and specific to the SC1/SC2 interplay, it would be an interesting new handle on multicomponent vortex matter. The device framing would be secondary. As written, however, the paper does not separate this effect from two alternatives: pulse-induced heating followed by a thermal quench, and the well-documented metastability and memory of vortex matter in conventional type-II superconductors. The novelty claims ("intrinsic", "rooted in the superconducting order itself", "remained elusive") are therefore not yet supported.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **Clear, repeatable bistability.** Fig. 1d shows five consecutive write/erase cycles with a binary readout at fixed J.
- **Systematic parameter dependence.** Three independent pulse parameters are tuned (Fig. 2), with saturation behaviour shown (ED Fig. 1).
- **Internal null regions.** The effect is absent at 3 T, absent in SC2 at 29–30 T over all angles (Fig. 4c, ED Fig. 10), and absent for θ ≳ 19° at 15 T (ED Fig. 7). This argues against a trivial instrumental artifact.
- **Polarity symmetry.** The memory state appears for both probe polarities after a single-polarity pump (ED Fig. 4). This argues against a remanent self-field (Bean-type) offset, which would shift the two polarities oppositely.
- **More than one sample.** The effect is seen in two samples (S1, S2) and survives across a range of temperatures, fields and angles.

## 3. Concerns

### A. Must address

**1. Heating and thermal quench are not excluded, and the two main counter-arguments do not exclude them.**
- *Where:* p. 12–13 and the Supplementary Information. The SI is cited for the heating analysis and Figs. S2 and S7 but is not part of the submission I could assess.
- *Scale of the problem:* Pulses reach 20 A cm⁻². Methods state that the reset starts at 40 mA. The current runs through spot-welded gold contacts in a dilution refrigerator. Even a contact resistance of ~1 Ω gives I²R ≈ 1.6 mW at 40 mA. That is well above typical mixing-chamber cooling power at 50–100 mK. The sample's own flux-flow dissipation at J_ex (V ~ 10²–10³ µV) adds a further ~10 µW.
- *Turn-off-rate argument:* Faster turn-off is said to "anticorrelate" with deposited energy. But a heat-then-quench mechanism predicts exactly this sensitivity. A briefly warmed vortex system (above the ~0.6 K closing temperature) that cools quickly freezes a disordered, higher-J_c configuration. Slow cooling anneals it. This is the standard field-cooled versus annealed phenomenology.
- *Temperature argument:* The effect is strongest at the lowest base temperature. This is also expected from a thermal quench, because the quench depth is largest there and vanishes once base T exceeds the ordering or irreversibility temperature.
- *Timescale:* The characteristic times from ED Fig. 1b,c (saturation at Δt ≈ 1 ms; decay of ΔJ for J/α ≈ 0.3 ms) are suggestive of a thermal time constant of a mm crystal coupled through gold wires. They are long compared with typical vortex relaxation times.
- *Requested:*
  - Two- and four-terminal contact resistances for each sample.
  - The energy dissipated per pump, separated into contact and sample contributions, for each protocol and field.
  - A thermometer or bare-chip thermistor mounted on the sample (or a resistive-thermometry proxy) with ms time resolution during pulses.
  - An estimate of the sample thermal time constant.
  - The decisive controls:
    - a heater pulse with no transport current, at matched energy and duration;
    - a pure temperature quench (warm to ~0.7–1 K at fixed field, then cool fast versus slow) followed by an identical probe sweep.

   If a thermal quench reproduces the high-J_c state, the effect is still interesting. The "electrical" and "intrinsic" framing and the mechanism would then need to change.

**2. The relation to known vortex-matter metastability and memory effects is not addressed.**
- *Missing literature:* History-dependent J_c, in which a disordered metastable vortex state with higher J_c is written by quenching and erased by current-driven annealing, is well established in conventional superconductors near the peak effect. Examples include 2H-NbSe₂ (Henderson et al., PRL 1996; Xiao, Andrei, Higgins, PRL 1999; Paltiel et al., Nature 403, 398 (2000), "Dynamic instabilities and memory effects in vortex matter"), CeRu₂, V₃Si and twinned YBCO (Valenzuela and Bekeris). None of these is cited.
- *Why it matters here:* The authors' own data show a peak-effect-like anomaly where the memory appears. Fig. 4b shows a nonmonotonic V(B) at fixed J over 7–14 T (S2). ED Fig. 9 shows J_c minimal near 20 T and higher at 28 T than at 9 T (S1). This is the generic setting in which single-order-parameter superconductors show such memory.
- *Bean-model argument:* The claim (p. 13) that Bean-type pinning "should be insensitive to how the current is turned off" does not address these dynamic-ordering and edge-contamination scenarios.
- *Requested:*
  - Discuss this literature explicitly.
  - Identify which observations, if any, require two vortex species or SC1/SC2 coexistence rather than a generic order–disorder vortex transition near a J_c anomaly.
  - Add discriminating tests, for example:
    - whether J_ex at threshold scales with J_c(B);
    - dependence on edge and contact geometry (Paltiel's edge-contamination mechanism);
    - comparison with a conventional peak-effect material measured with the same protocols and wiring.

**3. The identification of the memory region with SC1/SC2 coexistence ("SC1.5") is not established by the data.**
- *Field range in S2 versus the cited phase boundaries:* In S2 the effect appears at ~7 T (Fig. 1c), peaks near 10 T (Fig. 4e) and is essentially absent from 16 to 20 T (ED Fig. 8a–c). This is well below the ~20 T thermodynamic SC1/SC2 boundary (ref. 9). It is also below the 15–20 T coexistence window of refs. 10 and 46 that motivates the "SC1.5" label.
- *Text versus figures:* p. 9 states that the effect persists until the SC1→SC2 boundary, which "for S2 is just below 20 T". This is inconsistent with ED Fig. 8 and Fig. 4e.
- *Sample dependence:* In S1 the effect is strong at 20 T (Fig. 1b). The difference between samples is attributed to crystalline quality (RRR 105 versus 44). If the field window is set by disorder, that itself points to a pinning-controlled phenomenon.
- *Phase boundaries not measured:* Figs. 1a and 4a are schematics (Fig. 1a is adapted from ref. 33). No independent determination of the SC1/SC2 boundary is given for S1 or S2, for example from resistive T_c(B), ac susceptibility or specific heat.
- *Angular argument:* At 15 T, rotating toward c changes the reduced field B/B_c2(θ) of SC1 and the irreversibility field. The disappearance of the effect near 19–20° (ED Fig. 7, Fig. 4f,g) could reflect this rather than leaving the region "below SC2".
- *Requested:*
  - Measure the SC1/SC2 boundary and T_c(B, θ) on the same samples.
  - Field-sweep the protocol at θ ≈ 25–30° from low field up to B_c2 to test whether memory appears anywhere at comparable reduced field or comparable J_c.
  - Either justify extending "SC1.5" down to 7 T with independent evidence, or soften the claim.
  - Correct the text on p. 9.

**4. The delivered current waveform is not characterised, which undermines the timescale-tuning results.**
- *Pulse-delta mode:* Methods state that sweeps used the 6221/2182A pulse-delta mode. In that mode the source alternates between a pulse level and a low level, often zero, with finite pulse widths. A "smooth" sweep may therefore itself contain many abrupt turn-offs. This conflicts with the statement that "all excitations were applied as direct currents" and blurs the distinction between pump and erase.
- *Erase step size:* The erase steps of 2 mA (~1 A cm⁻²) are abrupt steps comparable to the J_ex threshold seen in ED Fig. 1a.
- *Bandwidth:* Fig. 2 reports Δt down to 10 µs and α up to 500 A cm⁻² ms⁻¹ (20 A cm⁻² in 40 µs), using 100-point arbitrary waveforms. These are delivered through dilution-fridge wiring that normally includes RC or π filters with kHz-scale cutoffs.
- *Fig. 1d/e protocol:* The erase step is drawn as a rectangular moderate-amplitude block ending abruptly. This contradicts the main-text definition of erase as a slow ramp.
- *Requested:*
  - Specify pulse width, low level, duty cycle and repetition rate for all pulse-delta sweeps.
  - Give the filter configuration of the measurement lines.
  - Show oscilloscope traces of the current actually delivered, for example across a series reference resistor at the cold stage, for the shortest Δt and largest α.
  - Show whether a truly continuous dc ramp gives the same equilibrium and erase behaviour.

### B. Should address

**5. The non-volatility and long-retention claim has no data behind it.**
- *What is claimed:* The text states persistence "over at least several hours" (p. 5 and p. 7).
- *Wording is inconsistent:*
  - the Abstract calls the state "metastable";
  - p. 7 says it "does not appear to be transiently metastable";
  - Methods call it a "long-lived transient (metastable) state".
- *Requested:*
  - Retention data: V at a fixed probe J versus time over hours.
  - The effect of repeated reads.
  - The effect of small field or temperature excursions.
  - Whether the state survives thermal cycling to ~1 K or above T_c.
  - Note that a full V(J) read that exceeds J_c and returns slowly erases the state (Fig. 1b), so readout is destructive unless restricted to sub-critical J.

**6. Sample statistics are thin and ΔJ is quantified inconsistently between figures.**
- *Samples:* The memory effect is shown only in S1 and S2. S3 (RRR 395, the cleanest) appears only in ac data at 400 mK (Fig. 4h). Was the protocol applied to S3? Its result is directly relevant to the disorder-dependence question in concern 3. Results after thermal cycling are not shown.
- *Uncertainty:* No error bars are given anywhere (Figs. 2, 4e,f; ED Fig. 1). The run-to-run scatter of the equilibrium V(J) should be compared with ΔJ.
- *Inconsistent magnitudes at nominally similar conditions (S2, 14 T, 50 mK, amplitude tuning):*
  - ED Fig. 1a saturates at ΔJ ≈ 0.55 A cm⁻²;
  - the Fig. 3b colour scale tops out at ~0.25 A cm⁻²;
  - Fig. 4e (J_ex = 10 A cm⁻²) gives a few tenths at most;
  - the text quotes a peak "just above 0.2 A cm⁻²".
- *Requested:*
  - Define ΔJ uniquely: reference curve, voltage criterion (1 µV in ED Fig. 1, but unspecified elsewhere), averaging and noise floor.
  - Give the cross-section dimensions, their uncertainty, and whether current flow is uniform between spot-welded contacts.

**7. The temperature scale is compared with the wrong T_c.**
- *Problem:* The argument that a ~0.6 K closing temperature is "surprising" given T_c = 2.1 K (p. 9) uses the zero-field T_c. At 14–20 T along b, T_c(B) is substantially lower. Fig. 3a at 1.0 K and 20 T already shows a nearly ohmic V(J).
- *Requested:* Compare the closing temperature with T_c(B) and with the irreversibility or vortex-solid boundary at the same field.
- *Diode analogy:* The analogy to the intrinsic diode effect (ref. 49) does not discriminate between mechanisms and should be toned down.

**8. The RSJ model is under-specified, internally inconsistent and largely post hoc.**
- *Missing and inconsistent parameters:*
  - g in Eq. 1 is not given;
  - the factor 0.6, β = 40 and δ = 20 are chosen by hand;
  - the noise amplitude is unstated;
  - temperature does not enter the model;
  - Methods give γ₀ = γ_π = 1, so γ(φ) ranges over 1–2, yet the ED Fig. 2a colour bar spans 0.3–0.6;
  - the statement that γ is treated "as a proxy to the injected current J" is unclear.
- *Physical mapping:* Mapping a mm-scale bulk crystal with flux flow onto a single macroscopic phase is not justified. φ is described as both an applied phase difference and a Cooper-pair momentum. A phase-modulated state Δ ∝ e^{iφr/L} is not obviously a "grid of domain walls".
- *Outcome built in:* The key result, that a quench lands in φ = π, is engineered by placing large damping at π.
- *Untested prediction:* The model links higher damping to lower flux-flow resistance in the memory state. The measured curves instead merge at high J (Fig. 1c; ED Fig. 1).
- *Requested:*
  - Give all parameters.
  - State which features are inputs and which are outputs.
  - Add temperature-dependent noise to test the T dependence.
  - Make at least one quantitative, falsifiable comparison, for example the J_ex threshold relative to J_c, or the characteristic turn-off time in ED Fig. 1c, which has an unexplained red fit curve.

**9. There is no direct evidence that the vortex configuration changes.**
- *Problem:* The "memory state" is defined solely by a ~10–30% J_c shift in two-terminal-pair transport.
- *Requested, any of:*
  - local versus distant voltage-contact pairs, to show whether the state is bulk or localised near the current contacts where heating and injection occur;
  - a force-free geometry (J ∥ B, e.g. J ∥ b with B ∥ b), where a vortex-motion mechanism predicts suppression;
  - ac susceptibility or Campbell-regime response before and after pumping;
  - broadband voltage-noise spectroscopy, which distinguishes plastic from elastic flow in the NbSe₂ literature.

**10. The technological claims are overstated.**
- *Operating conditions:* The effect requires 7–20 T and T ≲ 0.4 K. This should be stated plainly in the Abstract and Outlook.
- *Energy budget:* The write energy (flux flow at 20 A cm⁻² for ms, plus contact dissipation) is not quantified, while read power is quoted.
- *Scaling argument:* The pW scaling argument ignores contacts and assumes the effect persists in micron-scale devices.
- *Citation:* Ref. 59 (intrinsic carrier density in Si) does not support the comparison made.
- *Framing:* The AI-energy framing in the introduction is disproportionate to a bulk, high-field, millikelvin observation.

### C. Minor

11. **Units.** ED Fig. 1a x-axis reads "J_ex (ms)"; it should be A cm⁻². ED Fig. 5b gives α in A cm⁻² s⁻¹ while Fig. 2 and ED Figs. 1, 4 and 6 use ms⁻¹. Fig. 3b uses "J_ext" where J_ex is meant elsewhere.
12. **Methods typos and references.** "global minimum at π = 0" should read φ = 0. The text cites "Extended Data Fig. 2d,e", but the time-evolution panels are c and d.
13. **Fig. 4e description.** The 15° profile is called "very similar" to 0°, yet it terminates near ~12 T rather than ~18 T. Comment on this.
14. **Terminology.** "minimise entropy" (p. 13) should be free energy. "Pinning forces between vortices" should be vortex–defect pinning. Explain why an effect explained by enhanced disorder pinning is called "intrinsic".
15. **References.** Refs. 26 and 57 are duplicates.
16. **Supplementary material.** Move the heating analysis and the cited Figs. S2 and S7 into ED figures, since they bear on the central claim.
17. **Fig. 4h.** Clarify the meaning of "effective resistivity" for nonlinear ac data. State whether the ac excitation amplitude could itself act as a write or erase stimulus.
18. **Field history.** State the field history and cooling protocol (field-cooled or not) before each dataset, and whether results depend on it.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Add a thermal-quench control.** At fixed B in the memory region, compare three preparations:
   - (a) a current pump;
   - (b) a heater-only pulse of matched energy and duration;
   - (c) a fast versus slow cool from ~0.8 K.

   Read each with the identical probe. This single figure would decide between the electrical-intrinsic and thermal-quench pictures.
2. **Map the effect against independently measured phase boundaries, in normalised variables.**
   - Measure T_c(B, θ) and the SC1/SC2 boundary on S1, S2 and S3.
   - Replot the memory region against these boundaries, against B/B_c2(θ), and against the J_c(B) anomaly.
   - Include S3 to test disorder dependence directly.
3. **Benchmark against conventional vortex matter.** Run the same pulse protocols, with the same wiring, on a conventional superconductor with a peak effect, such as NbSe₂ or Nb. Show which features (angular cutoff, polarity symmetry, timescales) are unique to UTe₂.
4. **Strengthen the evidential base.**
   - Characterise the delivered waveforms by oscilloscope.
   - Provide retention-versus-time data and error bars.
   - Reconcile the ΔJ definitions across figures.
   - Make the model quantitatively predictive, for example of the threshold J_ex/J_c and the ~0.3 ms turn-off timescale, rather than illustrative only.
