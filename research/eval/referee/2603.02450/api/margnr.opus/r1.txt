1. ### 1. Joule heating and thermal-quench artefacts are not excluded quantitatively

The "intrinsic" claim rests on three qualitative arguments:

- the effect is stronger for faster ramp-downs, which deposit less energy;
- it exists only in a narrow B–θ window;
- it is strongest at the lowest temperature.

None of these excludes a thermal origin.

- An abrupt current cut-off is also the fastest *cooling* protocol. A thermal quench of the vortex system from a transiently heated state would therefore produce exactly the observed rate dependence.
- Heat capacity is smallest at the lowest temperature, so the same dissipated energy gives the largest temperature excursion there.
- Pumps reach up to 20 A/cm² through spot-welded contacts in flux flow. The only heating evidence is a mention of temperature-vs-time traces in the SI, with no stated thermometer location or magnitude of the temperature rise.

The authors should:

- report contact resistances and the dissipated energy per pump (E_write) for each protocol;
- measure, or bound with a thermal model, the actual sample temperature during and after each pulse, for example with an on-sample thermometer or the sample's own R(T) as a thermometer;
- re-plot all ΔJ data against E_write, peak temperature and estimated cooling rate. If ΔJ collapses onto a single function of cooling rate, the effect is thermal-quench driven.
- run a direct control: a heat pulse with no transport current (heater pulse) followed by an abrupt vs a slow cool, at the same B and θ. Check whether the high-Jc state is reproduced.

2. ### 2. No comparison with conventional history-dependent vortex states

Current- and history-dependent metastable high-Jc vortex states are well established in conventional type-II superconductors, notably:

- the peak-effect region of 2H-NbSe₂ (Henderson, Andrei, Higgins and Bhattacharya, Paltiel et al.);
- similar effects in CeRu₂ and in Nb.

In these systems a disordered, supercooled vortex configuration has a higher Jc and is annealed by current. That phenomenology closely matches the "pump/erase" behaviour reported here.

The paper dismisses Bean-type pinning as "insensitive to how the current is turned off". However, it neither engages with this literature nor tests the alternative. Extended Data Fig. 9 even shows a non-monotonic Jc(B) with the memory window near a Jc minimum and a higher Jc at 28 T than at 9 T, which is suggestive of a peak-effect-like order–disorder crossover.

The authors should:

- discuss the peak-effect / order–disorder literature explicitly;
- plot ΔJ(B) against Jc(B) and dJc/dB on the same sample;
- compare field-cooled (FC) and zero-field-cooled (ZFC) entry into the window, and test field-sweep history dependence;
- ideally, apply the identical pump/erase protocol to a benchmark superconductor (e.g. NbSe₂ in its peak-effect regime, or Nb). This would show which features, if any, are unique to UTe₂.

Without this, the claim of a novel, multicomponent-order-parameter memory effect is not established.

3. ### 3. The SC1.5 / two-phase-coexistence attribution contradicts the authors' own data

The text defines the coexistence region (SC1.5) as ~15–20 T, based only on literature. Yet:

- memory appears at ~7 T (Fig. 1c), with no data point between 5 and 7 T;
- it peaks near 10 T;
- one passage says it persists "until the phase boundary out of SC1 into SC2", i.e. within SC1.

The memory window and its strongest effect therefore sit deep in nominal SC1, where the paper also states SC1 shows no anomaly. In addition:

- The angular cutoff at ~19–20° is said to "track" SC2, but the SC2 angular range is taken from other groups' samples.
- The S1 vs S2 shift of the window is attributed to "crystal quality" without analysis. S2, which provides most of the data, has the lowest RRR.

The authors should measure the phase boundaries in situ on the same crystals:

- the SC1→SC2 transition via R(B), Jc(B) kinks, ac susceptibility/TDO or the magnetocaloric effect;
- the SC2 angular extent.

These boundaries should then be overlaid directly on the memory window. The authors must either provide independent evidence that SC1/SC2 coexistence extends down to ~7 T in these crystals, or substantially weaken the statement that memory "requires" coexistence.

An orthogonal rotation (b→a) would be a useful discriminating control. If memory persists where SC2 is absent, the "tracking SC2" claim fails.

4. ### 4. Field, angle and temperature mapping is too sparse for the boundary claims

The boundary claims rest on the following data:

| Measurement | Coverage |
|---|---|
| ΔJ vs B | 2 angles (0°, 15°) |
| ΔJ vs θ | 2 fields |
| ΔJ vs T | 5 bath temperatures (50, 100, 300, 430, 600 mK) at a single field (14 T) |
| SC2 null result | 1 sample (S2), 1 field (29 T) |

The claim that memory is "closed by ~650 mK" has no data between 430 and 600 mK.

The authors should provide:

- dense ΔJ(B, θ) maps: ΔB ≲ 1 T including the 5–7 T onset; Δθ ≲ 1–2°, and ≤0.5° near the cutoff; with the angle-calibration and misalignment uncertainty stated;
- ΔJ(T) at ≥10 temperatures with fine steps (≤25 mK) between 400 and 700 mK, at several fields (e.g. 7, 10, 14, 18 T), so that a T*(B) line can be compared with the phase diagram.

**The SC2 null result is not conclusive.** Jc is higher at 28–29 T, so a fixed pump amplitude may simply fail to exceed Jc. The null should be repeated with the pump scaled to J_ex/Jc(B), at several fields between 22 and 30 T and on more than one sample.

5. ### 5. No statistics, error bars or sample-to-sample reproducibility for a small effect

The memory signal is small: ΔJ peaks at ~0.2 A/cm². The agents' rough estimates from axis ranges put this at somewhere between a few percent and ~10–20% of Jc. It is read out as sub-µV voltage differences. Yet:

- no figure has error bars;
- no repeat counts are given;
- there is no null distribution of Jc scatter between repeated erase→probe measurements;
- most data come from one crystal (S2). S1 appears only at 20 T, and the role of S3 is unclear (it appears only in a figure label).

The authors should:

- perform ≥10 independent pump→probe repeats per data point and report mean ± SD or confidence intervals;
- construct the erase→probe vs erase→probe distribution of Jc differences, and show that ΔJ clearly exceeds it;
- randomize or interleave protocol order to rule out drift in field, temperature or contacts;
- reproduce the core results on ≥3 crystals, ideally from different growth batches and spanning RRR, and report any failures. Core results are ΔJ(B) at θ = 0, ΔJ(T), the angular cutoff and the rate dependence;
- explicitly state ΔJ/Jc;
- report sample dimensions, contact geometry and resistances, the voltmeter noise floor and thermoelectric offsets.

Sample dimensions are not given anywhere, so current densities and the power estimates cannot be checked.

6. ### 6. ΔJ metric is inconsistently defined and its robustness is untested

Three different definitions or metrics appear:

- Extended Data Fig. 1 defines ΔJ "at a nominal fixed value of V = 1 µV";
- the Fig. 2 caption defines it "at fixed values of V";
- Fig. 4 appears to use a different metric, V at fixed J = 2.5 A/cm².

It is unclear which metric underlies each quantitative statement, e.g. "peaks just above 0.2 A cm⁻² at around 10 T" and the onset and cutoff fields.

Because V(J) near threshold depends on the flux-creep n-value, the authors should:

- state the metric used in every figure;
- show that the conclusions are robust to the voltage criterion, by reporting ΔJ at e.g. 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2 and 5 µV;
- show full V(J) curves in both states.

7. ### 7. Tuning experiments are confounded by changing conditions

The amplitude, duration and relaxation-rate studies, the temperature study and the B/θ maps each use different fields (9 T, 14 T, 7 T, 20 T), temperatures, samples and fixed stimulus parameters. For example:

- the temperature study (Extended Data Fig. 6) uses relaxation rates of 0.04–36 A cm⁻² ms⁻¹;
- the rotation and field maps (Extended Data Figs. 7–8) use 20–500 A cm⁻² ms⁻¹.

These data sets cannot be compared directly, and the claimed saturation behaviour cannot be separated from the change in conditions.

The authors should:

- run a factorial (or at least fully crossed) grid of amplitude × duration × fall rate at one (B, T, θ) on one sample, with repeats, and reproduce a reduced grid on a second sample;
- normalize pump amplitude to Jc(B, T) to determine whether a threshold at J_ex ≈ Jc (entry into flux flow) exists. Such a threshold would point to vortex depinning and reorganization.

8. ### 8. Retention / "non-volatile" claims are unsupported and internally contradictory

The introduction claims the state persists "over a timescale of at least several hours" and "does not appear transiently metastable". However:

- no retention data are shown; the only time trace is ~400 s of pump/probe/erase cycling (Fig. 1d,e);
- Methods describes the memory state as a "long-lived transient (metastable) state", contradicting the main text.

The authors should:

- measure ΔJ(t_w) after a single pump at logarithmically spaced wait times, from 1 s to ≥10⁴ s, using a fresh pump for each t_w;
- fit logarithmic (creep-like) vs exponential decay;
- repeat the measurement at several temperatures to extract an activation barrier. This would also give a quantitative basis for the "low-energy scale" argument.

**Read disturbance.** The read biases the sample into the dissipative flux-flow regime (V ≈ 1 µV), so moving vortices could anneal the state. The read waveform (pulse width, duty cycle, low level) is not specified. Read-disturb should be tested by measuring ΔJ after N = 1–10⁴ consecutive reads.

**Endurance and bit discrimination.** For a memory claim, the authors should run ≥10³ pump/erase cycles and report:

- histograms of V_read for the 1 and 0 states;
- the bit-error rate;
- drift of the erase-state Jc over the cycling.

The term "non-volatile" should be qualified: the state requires continuously held fields above ~7 T and temperatures of ~mK.

9. ### 9. The phenomenological model does not provide evidence for the proposed mechanism

The modified RSJ model has a double-well potential, based on a cos 2φ term following Goldobin et al. Key ingredients are imposed by hand:

- the metastable minimum at φ = π;
- its "stickiness" via the damping profile γ(φ) with δ = 20.

Further problems:

- The noise amplitude is not reported, and Eq. 1's coefficients are unclear.
- Time is in arbitrary units, and the current is represented by using γ as "a proxy to the injected current J".
- Capacitance is neglected, so hysteresis arises solely from the imposed potential and damping.
- It is not stated what φ is the phase across in a bulk crystal without a junction.
- The agreement with data (Extended Data Fig. 2b) is only qualitative. Qualitative reproduction of hysteresis and rate dependence is therefore largely built in.

The authors should:

- report all parameters, including the noise amplitude, integrator, time step and initial conditions, and release the code;
- scan sensitivity over β, δ, γπ/γ₀, barrier depth and noise, to show which features are robust;
- compare against null models that lack two vortex species: (i) a single-well model with history-dependent pinning; (ii) a thermal-quench model using the measured T(t);
- calibrate time to a physical scale, so that the model makes falsifiable predictions for the critical fall rate and the escape rate vs temperature. These can then be compared with measured retention and the ~0.6 K cutoff.

**Inversion symmetry.** The choice of φ = π rests on the statement that "inversion symmetry appears unbroken". Only one pump/probe polarity combination was checked. The full 2×2 polarity matrix and a direct test of |Jc⁺| vs |Jc⁻| non-reciprocity in both states should be reported.

10. ### 10. Direct evidence for "two vortex species" and "glassy" pinning is absent

The central mechanistic claim is that two vortex species quench into a disordered configuration with stronger pinning. It is supported only by transport data and the phenomenological model.

Measurements that would substantially strengthen it, on the same crystals in the pump vs erase states, include:

- small-angle neutron scattering of the vortex lattice: Bragg-peak broadening or loss of order, and possible distinct form factors from two species;
- Campbell penetration depth (linear ac susceptibility or TDO), to directly measure pinning-well curvature;
- glass signatures: aging and wait-time dependence, creep exponents, and broadband or telegraph noise in flux flow.

If these are beyond scope, the mechanism should be presented clearly as a hypothesis. It should not appear in the abstract and conclusions as an explanation.

11. ### 11. Application and power claims are not quantitatively supported

The paper claims:

- "ultralow-power switching";
- ~1 nW read power for mm-sized crystals, projected to ≲1 pW at 1 µm²;
- relevance to neuromorphic and quantum hardware;
- the ability to "encode and process information".

None of these is backed by the data shown. Specifically:

- The read-power estimate gives no current, voltage or dimensions.
- The scaling assumption (area vs cross-section, constant J) is not stated.
- Write and erase energy are never quantified, even though writes require pumps up to 20 A/cm² through resistive contacts.
- The comparison with silicon and cryotrons ignores the ≥7 T magnet and dilution-refrigerator overhead.
- A "1" read at V ≠ 0 is itself dissipative. This undercuts the claim that read and write are performed "while superconducting".

The authors should provide E_read and E_write per operation with the underlying numbers, and state the operating-condition overhead. Otherwise they should substantially tone down these claims in the abstract and conclusions.

12. 1. Novelty is not established: the closest prior art is uncited

The reported write/erase phenomenology closely matches the well-known current-driven metastable vortex states in the peak-effect regime of conventional superconductors. In that phenomenology:
- a fast current quench or field-cooling freezes a disordered, more strongly pinned, higher-Jc vortex state that persists;
- slow current ramping anneals it back to the ordered, low-Jc state;
- the behavior depends on ramp rate.

None of this literature is cited. Missing references include:
- Henderson, Andrei, Higgins, Bhattacharya, PRL 1996;
- Xiao, Andrei and co-workers;
- Paltiel et al., Nature 2000;
- Banerjee, Grover and co-workers on NbSe2 and CeRu2;
- field-cooled vs zero-field-cooled Jc history effects.

The authors' own ED Fig. 9 points directly at this setting:
- "Jc is minimal around 20 T, and is actually higher at 28 T than it is at 9 T."
- "anomalous non-monotonic behaviour… in the memory region."

These are hallmarks of a peak-effect / order–disorder regime.

The only conventional mechanism the paper explicitly rejects is the Bean / Bean–Livingston one, on the grounds that it is insensitive to how the current is turned off. This is not the relevant alternative. The relevant alternative is itself rate-sensitive.

The cited hysteresis and trapped-flux works (Qian 1989; Semenok 2024) are dismissed collectively as "extrinsic" without individual discussion.

Requested:
- A dedicated discussion of this literature.
- An explicit statement of which observation in UTe2 a single-species order–disorder metastability cannot explain.

Unless such a distinguishing feature is shown, the "Discovery" framing in the title and abstract should be tempered, for example to "history-dependent critical current in UTe2."

13. 2. The "intrinsic" claim is undefined and internally inconsistent

"Intrinsic" is never defined. It is supported in two ways:

(i) By contrast with geometric or field-modulated flux trapping. Under that definition, bulk NbSe2 metastability and YBCO transport hysteresis (ref. 25) are equally "intrinsic," so the term does not distinguish this work.

(ii) By the low temperature scale (≲0.6 K vs Tc = 2.1 K) and an analogy with the intrinsic diode effect. This argument has two problems:
- It is heuristic. Strong low-temperature enhancement and loss of memory at higher T are also expected for pinned metastable vortex states, which relax faster through thermal creep.
- It contradicts the abstract. The text says this points to a phenomenon "distinct from the superconductivity itself," while the abstract says the effect is "rooted in the superconducting order itself."

The proposed mechanism is itself defect-controlled. It involves a "glassy" vortex state with stronger pinning, and the authors attribute the sample-dependent field window to "differences in crystalline quality (and hence vortex pinning forces)" (ED9).

The paper also offers a second, unrelated mechanism in the Methods: finite-momentum pairing Δ(r) ∼ cos(qr), linked to the diode effect. No test distinguishes it from the vortex-glass picture.

Requested:
- A precise definition of "intrinsic."
- A consistent statement of the proposed mechanism.
- At least one discriminating test, for example the RRR dependence of ΔJ, or nonreciprocity of ΔJ for the finite-q scenario.

14. 3. The link to SC1/SC2 coexistence ("SC1.5") is circular and contradicted by the paper's own data

Two definitions of SC1.5. The introduction defines SC1.5 as the low-Jc coexistence region "at around 15–20 T" (from Tokiwa et al. 2023). It also states that "when the material is entirely in either the SC1 or SC2 state, we observe forms of V(J) that are typical for a type-II superconductor." However, the Fig. 4a caption redefines SC1.5 as "the memory region… identified by nonzero ΔJ." The argument therefore becomes circular.

The data lie outside the stated region.
- Memory in S2 "quickly rises for B > 7 T" and "peaks… at around 10 T."
- The main-figure example in Fig. 1c is at 7 T.
- By the paper's own definition, 7 T lies in SC1.
- The 7 T onset is not tied to any measured phase boundary, and no independent evidence for SC2 vortices at 7–10 T is given.
- The 7–20 T window is described as "narrow."

An unaddressed coincidence. The introduction notes that anomalous SC1 vortex structures (doublets, stripes) "persist up to at least 8 T." Whether the memory onset is related to these, rather than to SC1/SC2 coexistence, is not discussed.

The window depends on the sample. S1's memory window lies at higher B than S2's, which the authors attribute to pinning. A window that shifts with sample quality is hard to reconcile with a fixed thermodynamic coexistence region. It is natural in a pinning or peak-effect picture.

The angular evidence is partial.
- Fig. 4f,g at 15 T on S2 shows ΔJ vanishing at a jump in V(θ) near 20°. This is a direct measurement on the crystal.
- However, interpreting that jump as the SC2 edge relies on the SC2 angular extent from ref. 33. SC2 itself (e.g., Bc2(θ)) is not re-measured on these crystals.
- The field of the ED7 angle data is not stated.

Missing controls.
- (a) SC1 at larger angles at a matched reduced field H/Hc2. Without this, a "SC2-required" mechanism cannot be distinguished from a pinning window within SC1.
- (b) Current along other crystal axes; all data use J ∥ a.
- (c) The identical pump/erase protocol on a conventional type-II superconductor.
- (d) A field-cooled vs zero-field-cooled comparison.

Possible self-citation dependence. The phase diagram and the SC1.5 Jc anomaly come from prior works that may share authors (refs 33, 35, 37, 39, 46). The independent evidence for coexistence should be summarized, and the field range in which it applies should be clarified.

15. 4. Heating and dissipative quenching are not excluded

The write is dissipative by the authors' own account. "Current densities above Jc are applied, inducing a flux-flow regime… when J drops back below Jc vortex flow suddenly halts." Pumps reach roughly 10× Jc. Memory increases with pump amplitude and duration, which both mean more deposited energy, until it saturates. It also increases with faster fall rate. Write vs erase thus behaves as a continuum of fall rate, which is the signature of a quench/anneal process, whether thermal or dynamical.

The arguments against heating are qualitative. The "less energy yet more memory" argument holds only along the fall-rate axis.

Missing information:
- Thermometer location and time constant, relative to the 10 µs–20 ms pumps.
- Contact resistance of the spot-welded leads.
- Dissipated power and energy per pump.
- Verification of the current waveform at the sample. The pumps are 100-point arbitrary waveforms, so a 10 µs pulse has about 0.1 µs per point, likely near the source and wiring limits.
- Parameters of the pulse-delta read: pulse width, duty cycle and low level.

Requested controls:
- An abrupt drop from below Jc, a non-dissipative case.
- Energy-matched pumps at fixed fall rate.
- Evidence that the slow-α limit reaches ΔJ = 0. The ED1 caption states saturation only for amplitude and duration, not for α.
- An erase-rate scan. The erase is a stepwise 20 s sweep, a protocol different from a slow pump, and its rate is never varied.

The fall-rate ranges also differ greatly between figures (about 0.04–500 A cm⁻² ms⁻¹), so they should be reconciled.

16. 5. Significance as a memory is overstated; key figures of merit are absent

The operation is not "while superconducting." The outlook claims "both read and write operations can be performed while superconducting." This is inaccurate: the write requires flux flow, and the "0" bit is read as a finite voltage (µV, about 1 nW).

Energy comparisons are incomplete.
- No write energy is reported.
- Energy is compared only with silicon and cryotrons. There is no comparison with SFQ/Josephson or other cryogenic memories.
- The overhead of a ≤0.6 K cryostat and a 7–20 T magnet is ignored.

The motivation applies a double standard. The introduction criticizes competing approaches for requiring "external magnetic field" modulation or "high drive currents." The UTe2 effect requires a static multi-tesla field and super-critical drive currents.

The scaling projection is unsupported. The ≲1 pW figure at 1 µm² is extrapolated without device data. At 10 T, such an area contains only a few thousand vortices, so collective-pinning behavior may change.

Memory metrics are missing:
- read margin and bit-error rate, and switching statistics;
- endurance (only a few cycles over about 400 s are shown);
- read-disturb tests;
- behavior when the field is removed.

Retention is overinterpreted. It is supported by one sentence ("at least several hours"), with no decay curves. From this the authors conclude the state "does not appear to be transiently metastable in character," promising non-volatility. Hours-long persistence at 50 mK is fully consistent with a metastable pinned vortex state undergoing slow creep. Retention curves, for example on a log-time scale, would be both a figure of merit and a mechanistic test.

ΔJ should be stated quantitatively relative to Jc. It is about 0.2 A cm⁻² at a 1 µV criterion. From the figure axes this appears to be of order 10–20% of Jc, but the paper never states Jc or ΔJ/Jc explicitly.

The Majorana / half-quantum-vortex link is speculative. The authors themselves write "we speculate." It should not be part of the significance claims.

17. 6. Reproducibility and sample dependence

Effectively two samples show memory (S1 at 20 T; S2 from 7 T upward), and their field windows differ. The cleanest sample, S3 (RRR 395), appears only in an equilibrium ac measurement (Fig. 4h). Whether S3 shows memory is unknown.

This is a key omission. The RRR dependence of ΔJ is the most direct test of a pinning-controlled vs intrinsic origin.

Statistics are absent. There are no error bars, repeat runs or thermal-cycle reproducibility data.

Data and code availability. The code is provided; the data are promised for the Apollo repository but are not yet available.

18. 7. The model is an illustrative cartoon and should be presented as such.
- Imposed features. The 0/π double well is justified only by inversion symmetry. The higher Jc at π is set by choosing β = 40 "such that" it holds. The damping peaked at π (δ = 20) and the cos φ/cos 2φ mixing with a hand-set factor of 0.6 are all imposed.
- What the variables represent. φ is described as the TDGL pair momentum in a bulk sample with no junction, while γ is interpreted as vortex-flow damping. These are not reconciled. γ is also used as a "proxy to the injected current J," so the same symbol has two roles.
- Reversed physical picture. Equating higher damping with pinning, through "R ∼ 1/γ" and then "higher Jc," conflates flux-flow resistance with pinning strength. This reverses the usual RSJ meaning of damping.
- Missing simulation details. The noise amplitude and the quench and anneal amplitudes and timescales are not stated. All units are arbitrary, there are no T, B or timescale predictions, and the TDGL derivation is claimed only "in principle."
- Physics errors to correct.
  - Vortices do not order "to minimise entropy."
  - Pinning is not a force "between vortices."
  - "Global minimum at π = 0" should read φ = 0.

19. 8. Loose analogies. The Fig. 1d comparison to "supercurrent rectification by the superconducting diode effect" is promotional. A history-dependent switch is not nonreciprocal transport.

20. 9. Protocol and text inconsistencies.
- The abstract says "pulses push the material in and out," but the erase is a smooth sweep.
- Erase polarity is unsigned in the Methods but "from a large negative value" in the Fig. 1b caption.
- "All excitations were applied as direct currents" conflicts with the pulse-delta reads and the ac lock-in data in Fig. 4h.
- The temperature dependence of the V(J) shape (markedly different at 1 K) is not analyzed.

21. 10. Measurement conditions are missing or vary between figures.
- Main figures use 9, 14 and 15 T.
- No field or temperature is stated for ED4 or ED7, and the Fig. 4h conditions are unclear.
- Jc is defined only implicitly through the 1 µV ΔJ criterion.
- Current non-uniformity from top-surface contacts on mm-sized crystals is not considered.

22. 11. Editorial issues.
- ED2 panel letters do not match the Methods (d,e vs c,d).

- Several Fig. 4 panel labels are garbled.

23. 1. The write/erase/probe protocol is undefined, and the measurement method may itself act as a "pump"

- **Pulse-delta settings are missing.** All V(J) data use Keithley 6221/2182A pulse-delta mode. The Methods give only the start current, end current, number of steps, and "pulses defined by 100 points". Please state:
  - pulse width, low (off) level, and sampling delay;
  - whether the polarity alternates, and the repetition period;
  - how the 100-point arbitrary waveforms were combined with pulse-delta acquisition.
- **Self-pumping is not discussed.** In pulse-delta mode each data point returns abruptly to a low level, which is the paper's own definition of a pump. The authors should explain why ordinary sweeps (and the reset staircase) do not write the memory state. Otherwise the statement that "smooth sweeps give no hysteresis" cannot be interpreted.
- **No threshold separates erase from pump.** The reset is itself a staircase from 40 mA to 0 in 2 mA steps over 20 s. Please state the thresholds in relaxation rate α, step size, duration and amplitude Jex that separate erase from write.
- **The delivered waveform is not verified.** α and Δt appear to be programmed values. Pulses as short as 10 µs defined by 100 points need about 0.1 µs per point. Please state the instrument's actual timing limits and show the current waveform measured at the sample (oscilloscope or shunt resistor). Please also describe the line filtering and cabling bandwidth.
- **The erase polarity is inconsistent.**
  - Fig. 1 describes the erase as a sweep from "a large negative value"; the Methods reset runs from +40 mA.
  - ED Fig. 4 uses the same polarity for erase and pump but the opposite polarity for the probe, without explanation.
  - Please clarify these, show same-polarity probing, and state whether a smooth sweep of either sign erases.
- **The probe is not specified.** Please give the probe current value and state whether reading is destructive.

24. 2. Heating is not quantitatively excluded

A heating pulse followed by rapid cooling is phenomenologically equivalent to the proposed "quench" of a disordered vortex state, so this must be addressed carefully.

- Jex reaches about 20 A cm⁻² against equilibrium Jc of about 10 A cm⁻². The voltage and dissipated power during the pump are not reported (Fig. 1b suggests flux flow at tens of µV), and no estimate of the temperature rise is given.
- Please add control experiments:
  - a heater pulse at zero transport current;
  - field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled comparisons;
  - pumps at fixed deposited energy but different α.
- Please state where the thermometer is, whether the sample sits in vacuum or in the mixture, the contact resistances, and whether the quoted T is the mixing-chamber or sample temperature.
- The temperature-versus-time traces are only in the Supplementary Information. They should be moved to Extended Data and analysed quantitatively.
- The current arguments against heating are qualitative: faster α deposits less energy, the effect is confined to the SC2 region, and it is strongest at the lowest T. They should be made quantitative.

25. 3. Missing comparison with known metastable vortex states in conventional superconductors

- History-dependent, metastable disordered vortex states with enhanced Jc are well documented, especially near the peak effect (e.g. 2H-NbSe₂: Henderson/Andrei, Bhattacharya/Higgins, Paltiel et al.). In those systems a fast current or field change traps a high-Jc disordered state and slow ramps anneal it. None of this literature is cited or discussed.
- The paper's own data show peak-effect-like features that are not discussed as such:
  - the kink in V(B) at the edge of the memory region (Fig. 4b);
  - the "complex flux-flow behaviour" in Fig. 4h;
  - the non-monotonic Jc(B) of S1 (ED Fig. 9).
- The main workhorse sample, S2, has the lowest RRR (44). The paper does not discuss whether the memory scales with disorder or pinning.
- Without this discussion, the claim that the effect is intrinsic and specific to the SC1/SC2 competition is under-supported.

26. 4. Memory metrics are inconsistently defined and lack statistics

- **No Jc criterion is given**, e.g. a voltage or electric-field threshold.
- **The ΔJ definition varies between figures:**
  - "at fixed V", with V unspecified, in Fig. 2;
  - at V = 1 µV in ED Fig. 1;
  - unspecified for the Fig. 4e,f maps. The J = 2.5 A cm⁻² value applies only to the equilibrium V(θ) trace in Fig. 4g.
  - Please use a single stated definition throughout.
- **There are no error bars or repeat counts.** The "resolution of the measurement" is invoked but not quantified. The peak ΔJ of about 0.2 A cm⁻² is only about 2% of J, so run-to-run scatter of equilibrium sweeps must be shown.
- **The parameter grids are coarse and differ between figures.**
  - Field: 5, 7, 8, …, 20 T, with no point between 5 and 7 T.
  - Angle: −1, 2, 4, …, 27°.
  - The Fig. 3b heatmaps use only five temperatures (50–600 mK). Fig. 3a includes loops at 650 mK and 1.0 K, but the heatmaps do not resolve the claimed vanishing at about 0.6–0.65 K.
  - The stimulus sets differ between the temperature study (ED Figs. 5–6) and the field/angle studies (ED Figs. 7–8). For example, Δt is 20/1/0.1/0.01 ms versus 20/5/0.5 ms, and α is 36/1/0.04 versus 500/100/20 A cm⁻² ms⁻¹. Please explain why.

27. 5. The attribution to "SC1.5" and the phase boundaries is circular or unsupported

- **SC1.5 has two definitions.** The introduction defines it as the region of anomalously low Jc near 15–20 T, from prior work. The Fig. 4a caption defines it as the region where memory occurs ("We refer to this as SC1.5"). The second definition makes the attribution of the memory to SC1.5 circular.
- **The 7 T onset is unreconciled.** The onset lies far below 15–20 T and is never reconciled with that range. Figs. 1c, 2 and 3 are at 7, 9 and 14 T.
- **The boundaries are taken from the literature.** The SC1/SC2 boundary (about 20 T) and the angular boundary (θ ≈ 20°) come from other crystals and are not measured on these samples (Fig. 1a is reproduced). Please measure them on these samples or justify the transfer, including any sample dependence.
- **The Jc tension is not addressed.** Prior work reports reduced Jc in SC1.5, whereas the memory state has enhanced Jc.
- **The 29 T trace in Fig. 4f is unexplained.** It lies above S2's stated memory window (to just under 20 T). Please state its sample and what it shows.

28. 6. Device-relevant claims are unquantified and internally inconsistent

- **Retention.** "At least several hours" is given without the hold conditions (J, B, T), the read interval or a decay curve.
- **The nature of the state is described inconsistently.**
  - The abstract and Methods call it "metastable" and a "long-lived transient (metastable) state".
  - The main text says it "does not appear to be transiently metastable".
- **Switching statistics are missing.** The Fig. 1d demonstration covers a few cycles over about 400 s. Please report cycle counts, error rate and switching probability versus Jex, Δt and α.
- **The power estimate is unsupported.** The figure of about 1 nW, scaling to ≲1 pW at 1 µm², gives no J or V and no scaling assumptions (e.g. size-independent Jc). It also excludes the write and erase energy.
- **Practical requirements are not acknowledged.** Operation needs a 7–20 T static field and about 50 mK, yet the introduction criticises competing schemes for requiring external fields.

29. 7. The phenomenological model is ill-defined and not reproducible

- **φ is defined inconsistently.** The main text calls it the superconducting phase (φ̇ = 2eV/ħ, L = domain size); the Methods call it the Cooper-pair momentum. Please give one definition, and explain how two vortex species map onto a single coordinate.
- **Eq. 1 and the damping γ(φ) are typeset ambiguously.** The parameters g and the constant 0.6 are undefined; only β = 40 is described. Please give clean equations and explain each parameter.
- **The π minimum is not guaranteed by symmetry.** Inversion symmetry only makes φ = π a stationary point. Whether it is a minimum depends on the parameters, so please state the condition. The cited φ-junction analogy has minima at ±φ₀, not at π.
- **The localised viscosity is ad hoc.** The choices γ₀ = γ_π = 1 and δ = 20 are not justified, and "viscous minimum ⇒ higher Jc" is asserted rather than derived. Please provide a sensitivity analysis. The text says δ sets "which minimum is viscous"; in the formula, δ sets the localisation instead.
- **Numerical details are missing:**
  - the integrator and time step;
  - the noise amplitude and its relation to temperature (only ⟨J_n J_n⟩ ∝ δ(t−t′) is given);
  - the pulse and ramp parameters used for Fig. 3c and ED Fig. 2;
  - the random seeds;
  - how V (asymptotic ⟨dφ/dt⟩?), Jc and ΔJ are extracted from the simulations;
  - the meaning of "γ as a proxy to the injected current J".
  - Results averaged over 60 noise realisations are described deterministically.
- **Neglecting capacitance is justified circularly** ("no hysteresis is seen without a pump").
- **There is no mapping to experiment.** Simulations show only "qualitative agreement", with no units or parameter mapping to the data.
- **No simulation code is provided.** The TDGL justification is given only "in principle".
- **The alternative finite-momentum (cos qr) explanation** in the Methods is not connected to the data.

30. 8. Sample geometry and characterisation are missing

- Sample dimensions, cross-section and voltage-contact spacing are not given. As a result, the mA values in the Methods cannot be converted to the A cm⁻² values in the figures, and the current densities cannot be reproduced.
