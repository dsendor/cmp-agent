**A. Must address**

**1. The effect is not distinguished from conventional metastable vortex-matter phenomena, so the "intrinsic, order-parameter-rooted" attribution is not demonstrated.**

The proposed mechanism is the textbook picture of metastable vortex states near the peak effect / order–disorder transition: a quench yields a glassy, disordered, more strongly pinned vortex configuration with higher J_c, and a slow current ramp anneals it back to an ordered lattice with low J_c. This is documented in 2H-NbSe₂, CeRu₂, V₃Si, MgB₂, low-T_c alloys and cuprates. Key works are neither cited nor discussed:
- Henderson, Andrei, Higgins and Bhattacharya, PRL 1996;
- Xiao, Andrei, Ling/Higgins and Banerjee, PRL/Nature 1999;
- Paltiel et al., Nature 2000 (dynamic instabilities, edge contamination, memory effects);
- Ravikumar/Banerjee, Grover, Ramakrishnan et al. on CeRu₂ and NbSe₂;
- the broader Bragg-glass/vortex-glass and FC/ZFC literature.

In those systems, current pulses of specific amplitude, duration and shape reversibly switch J_c. The switching depends on pulse width and ramp rate, saturates, is erased by slow driving or ac shaking, disappears on warming through thermal annealing, and the states persist for hours at low T. This is the phenomenology reported here.

The UTe₂ data even show peak-effect hallmarks:
- the non-monotonic J_c(B) and ∂V/∂J (Ext. Data Fig. 9: J_c minimal near 20 T and higher at 28 T than at 9 T);
- the kink in V(B) at the window edge (Fig. 4b);
- strong sample dependence of the window.

A single-species order–disorder transition, driven by UTe₂'s strongly field-dependent pinning and elastic moduli in this range (cf. the low-J_c region of Tokiwa et al.), could reproduce every observation. "Intrinsic" is also used ambiguously: bulk vortex order–disorder metastability is intrinsic to vortex matter, but it is not evidence for triplet or multiphase order.

The low-temperature argument is not physical. Its analogy to the intrinsic diode effect (ref. 49) does not hold, because metastable disordered phases are also most robust at low T, where creep is suppressed. Vanishing at ~0.6 K is likewise expected for thermally activated annealing.

The authors should:
- cite and directly compare with this literature, including the dynamics in NbSe₂ and CeRu₂;
- perform FC versus ZFC and field-sweep-history comparisons in and out of the window, and test whether FC states reproduce the high-J_c state;
- test whether ac current or ac field "shaking" erases the state;
- measure J_c(B) at high resolution to establish whether the window coincides with a peak-effect-like anomaly;
- run identical protocols in pure SC1 at matched reduced field and temperature, and on a conventional reference superconductor (e.g., NbSe₂ near its peak effect, or Nb) in the same setup and contact geometry;
- state explicitly what in the response is specifically two-component.

**2. The field window does not coincide with the SC1/SC2 coexistence region, and the phase boundaries are not measured on the same samples.**

In S2 (Fig. 4e, Ext. Data Fig. 8) the effect sets in near 7 T, peaks near 10 T and is essentially gone by ~16 T. The thermodynamic coexistence region (Sakai, Rosuel; refs. 10, 46) lies at ~15–20 T from specific heat and susceptibility. The text's placement of the S2 SC1→SC2 boundary and memory-region end "just below 20 T" conflicts with Ext. Data Fig. 8. A window starting at 7 T lies deep in SC1 by specific-heat criteria, and no evidence is offered that SC2 components exist at 7–10 T.

The phase diagrams in Figs. 1a and 4a are schematic and taken from other works (refs. 9, 33). The purple "memory region" is defined by the transport effect itself, which makes the argument circular. The shift of the window between S1 (~20 T, RRR 105) and S2 (7–15 T, RRR 44) is attributed to "crystalline quality" without evidence. It points to pinning and disorder rather than a sharp thermodynamic boundary.

"SC1.5 coexistence" and "two distinct vortex species" are adopted as premises rather than shown. The authors should:
- measure the SC1/SC2 boundary on the same crystals versus B and θ, by specific heat, ac susceptibility, magnetization (including ZFC/FC hysteresis), magnetostriction or torque, with particular attention to 7–12 T;
- overlay these boundaries on ΔJ(B, θ, T) maps with error bars;
- explain why ΔJ peaks ~10 T below the boundary in S2;
- plot the temperature dependence relative to T_c(B) at each field;
- correlate ΔJ quantitatively with the equilibrium J_c(B, θ) minimum and the V(θ) jump, to show whether the memory tracks an order-parameter boundary or a pinning/elasticity crossover.

**3. Heating is not excluded, and the arguments offered may favour a thermal-quench mechanism.**

The pump drives the sample deep into flux flow at up to 20 A cm⁻² for up to 20 ms at 50 mK, through spot-welded contacts on a mm-scale crystal, in 20 T fields. The erase starts from 40 mA. A transient rise of local, contact or vortex temperature followed by rapid cooling is precisely a thermal quench, the canonical way to freeze a disordered vortex state (analogous to field cooling). Faster turn-off implies faster effective cooling. This would naturally give "stronger memory for faster turn-off, saturating with amplitude and duration" even though less energy is deposited. The relaxation-rate argument is therefore consistent with heating, not evidence against it.

The temperature-dependence argument is circular. Glass and supercooling physics is itself strongest at low T, and the frozen disorder need only survive at base temperature.

The thermometry is inadequate:
- The temperature traces are in a Supplementary Information that was not provided.
- The thermometer is at the mixing chamber, not on the sample, and cannot resolve local heating on 10 µs–ms timescales.
- The sample–bath time constant at 50 mK is likely longer than the pulses.
- Contact resistances in field, sample dimensions and cross-sections, dissipated power and energy per pump and erase (bulk plus contact), sample heat capacity and thermal relaxation times are not reported.

The authors should report these quantities. They should also:
- use on-sample (or on-contact) fast thermometry or a resistive sensor on the crystal;
- perform heater-only (or laser/heat-pulse) "thermal pumps" of matched energy and timescale at zero transport current, to test whether heat plus fast cooldown writes the same high-J_c state;
- apply equal-energy pulses of different shapes.

**4. The pulse-delta read-out makes the operational definition of the "written" versus "equilibrium" state ambiguous.**

The Keithley 6221/2182A pulse-delta mode applies discrete current pulses that return abruptly to a low level between readings. The "smooth" probe sweeps and the erase (40→0 mA in 2 mA steps over 20 s) may therefore consist of repeated abrupt on/off transitions. Fig. 2b shows that even ~10 µs pulses write the memory state. It is then unclear why the probe and erase sweeps do not themselves act as pumps, and which settings differentiate "pump" from "probe." Without this, the distinction between "equilibrium" and "out-of-equilibrium" is undefined.

The authors should:
- fully specify the pulse width, duty cycle, low/off level, polarity alternation, repetition rate and step timing for every sweep;
- show explicitly that probe and erase sweeps do not write the state;
- repeat key results with true continuous dc ramps from a low-noise source.

**B. Should address**

**5. The angular cutoff is not specific evidence for SC2.**

The ~19–20° cutoff (Ext. Data Fig. 7) is measured at 15 T, where S2 at θ = 0 is, by the authors' own statement, still below SC2. Rotating B toward c at fixed field strongly reduces B_c2 of SC1 and changes the reduced field b = B/B_c2(θ), the H_c2 anisotropy, the vortex-lattice orientation relative to pinning, and the pinning landscape. The disappearance of the effect and the V(θ) jump near 20° (Fig. 4f,g) may therefore reflect a change of pinning regime or proximity to a peak-effect/B_c2 regime, not loss of SC2. No independent probe on the same sample locates SC2 versus angle.

The authors should:
- state the field used in Ext. Data Fig. 7;
- perform angle sweeps at several fields, plotted versus B/B_c2(θ), or at constant reduced field;
- locate SC2 versus angle thermodynamically on the same crystal.

**6. Sample coverage is thin, and the dependence on disorder is untested.**

Detailed tuning data come essentially from S2 alone, the lowest-quality crystal (RRR 44). S1 (RRR 105) contributes only a few traces near 20 T. The cleanest crystal, S3 (RRR 395), was measured only by ac lock-in resistivity (Fig. 4h), with no pump/erase protocol. Strong dependence on disorder is exactly what peak-effect-type metastability predicts. If the effect is weaker or absent in cleaner crystals, an extrinsic pinning origin is favoured over an order-parameter origin.

The authors should apply the full dc memory protocol to S3 and present systematic results on several crystals spanning a range of RRR, including RRR ~400. This would separate disorder/pinning effects from phase-boundary effects and establish reproducibility across crystals.

**7. The effect is small, and its statistical significance is not established.**

ΔJ is only ~0.2–0.5 A cm⁻² on J_c ~1–2 A cm⁻². It is defined at a nominal 1 µV threshold on curves showing µV-level noise and step-like structure. No error bars, noise floor, cycle-to-cycle distributions or trace-to-trace reproducibility of the equilibrium curve are shown for the heat maps (Figs. 2, 3b) or for Ext. Data Fig. 1. The heat maps appear partly noise-textured.

The authors should:
- show repeated equilibrium sweeps under identical conditions;
- add error bars and noise floors to all ΔJ maps;
- report ΔJ normalised to J_c;
- demonstrate the robustness of ΔJ and the heat maps to the choice of voltage criterion.

**8. Contact geometry, current distribution and edge/surface effects are unexplored.**

The crystals are bulk mm-scale with point-like spot-welded contacts on the (001) surface of a thick crystal. This gives inhomogeneous current injection, current crowding, local self-heating, self-fields and inhomogeneous vortex flow. Edge and surface-barrier contamination (Paltiel et al.) is a known source of pulse-dependent metastable high-J_c states.

The authors should vary contact configuration and resistance, sample cross-section and thickness, and the placement and spacing of current contacts relative to voltage contacts. Bulk magnetization or local Hall-probe measurements of pump-induced J_c changes would test whether the effect is bulk or edge/contact-related.

**9. Critical-state remanence is not quantitatively excluded.**

The argument that a Bean-model process "should be insensitive to how the current is turned off" is not generally correct. Flux creep, the E–J relation and induced electric fields make remanent current distributions rate dependent. Remanent transport-current distributions in mm-sized crystals can shift the apparent voltage onset.

The polarity symmetry of the enhancement (Fig. 1b orange curve, Ext. Data Fig. 4) is a useful counter-argument but is left implicit. The authors should develop it quantitatively and add polarity-resolved writing tests: abrupt drop from −J versus +J, and slow erase from the opposite polarity.

**10. The current waveform actually delivered to the sample is unverified.**

The 6221 arbitrary-waveform output is limited in point rate and bandwidth. A 10 µs "duration" defined by 100 points, or α = 500 A cm⁻² ms⁻¹, may not be realised as specified. In addition, the sample sits at the end of long twisted-pair wiring in a 30 T magnet, which filters and distorts fast edges. Because the claimed dose–response in duration and turn-off rate depends on these parameters, the effective waveform at the sample should be measured, e.g., by oscilloscope on an in-situ dummy load.

**11. Direct evidence for distinct vortex species or quenched disorder, and for the relevant energy scale, is absent.**

No local or bulk probe shows that the two states differ in vortex structure. The ~0.5–0.6 K scale is not connected to any independently measured energy scale of SC1/SC2 competition.

Where feasible, the authors should probe vortex order before and after pump/erase using:
- small-angle neutron scattering for lattice order;
- scanning Hall or SQUID microscopy;
- voltage-noise spectroscopy to distinguish plastic from elastic flow;
- magnetization relaxation;
- NMR linewidth.

They should also report the field dependence of the 0.6 K scale and relate it to an independent measurement.

**12. The theoretical model encodes the result and makes no testable predictions.**

The overdamped RSJ-type model uses a hand-crafted double-well U(φ) with minima at 0 and π and several tuned parameters: g, the 0.6 prefactor, β = 40 (higher critical current of the π minimum), δ = 20 (sharply peaked damping at φ = π), and γ₀ = γ_π = 1. The phase-dependent damping is introduced by hand to make φ "stick" at π, so the model reproduces the binary outcome (quench → π, anneal → 0) by construction. It does not reproduce:
- the graded, saturating dependence on amplitude and duration (Ext. Data Fig. 1);
- the ms-scale saturation of the duration dependence;
- the temperature dependence and the ~0.6 K scale;
- the field and angle window.

There is no microscopic link to SC1/SC2, for example through coherence lengths, domain-wall energy, pinning, or a two-component Ginzburg–Landau free energy. The simulated curves show discrete jumps while the measured curves are smooth. The Josephson timescales implied by φ̇ = 2eV/ħ are not reconciled with the observed ms dynamics. Without distinguishing predictions, the model cannot discriminate the proposed mechanism from conventional metastability.

The authors should either:
- ground U(φ) and γ(φ) in a two-component (time-dependent) Ginzburg–Landau or vortex-dynamics description, such as overdamped Langevin simulations of two interacting vortex species with pinning, that yields the metastable state and its B, θ and T dependence; or
- present the model explicitly as an illustration.

In either case they should reduce or justify the free parameters and derive at least one quantitative, falsifiable prediction to compare with data. Candidates include scaling of the threshold amplitude J_ex or turn-off rate with T or B, a characteristic time constant, ΔJ-versus-duration/rate scaling and saturation, the temperature cutoff, noise signatures, and the presence or absence of nonreciprocity.

**13. The model description is physically ambiguous and internally inconsistent.**

The following points need correcting:
- **Meaning of φ.** φ is described variously as a single-junction phase difference, as "Cooper pair momentum in the TDGL description," and through Δ(r) ~ e^{iφr/L} as a domain-wall/vortex configuration variable. The Methods additionally invoke a cos(qr) pair-density-wave state. These are different objects, and the coarse-graining from a junction-free mm-scale crystal to a single phase variable is not justified.
- **The π minimum.** The claim that inversion symmetry forces the second minimum to φ = π is not demonstrated for an inhomogeneous state. A uniform π winding over L is a finite-momentum state; calling it "inhomogeneous domain walls" changes the physics.
- **Current-to-γ mapping.** Treating "γ as a proxy to the injected current J" when constructing J–V curves is unclear and appears inconsistent with Eq. (2).
- **J_c versus damping.** In RSJ, J_c is set by max U′, not by damping. The text instead equates larger flux-flow damping / "lower resistance from domain walls" with stronger pinning and higher J_c, conflating flux-flow viscosity with the pinning force.
- **Two unreconciled pictures.** A "glassy vortex state with higher disorder" and a "grid of SC1/SC2 domain walls" are both offered without reconciliation.
- **Terminology.** "Minimise entropy" and "stronger pinning forces between vortices" are inaccurate. Pinning is vortex–defect, and the enhanced J_c of a disordered lattice follows from Larkin–Ovchinnikov-type arguments.
- **Transient wording.** The Methods call the state "long-lived transient," whereas the main text says it is "not transiently metastable."
- **Untested nonreciprocity.** The finite-q and diode scenarios raised in the Methods would generally predict nonreciprocity, which is not tested.

**14. Memory performance metrics are asserted rather than demonstrated.**

The following are missing:
- **Retention.** "Non-volatile" and "persists for at least several hours" are stated without retention data (ΔJ or V versus waiting time), without temperature dependence of the decay, and without documentation of field and temperature stability during the wait.
- **Endurance and statistics.** Fig. 1d shows only ~5 write/erase cycles. Endurance, read margin, switching thresholds and their distributions, stochasticity and error rates are not quantified.
- **Read disturb.** Readout is dissipative: the "0" state in Fig. 1d is read at ~0.5 µV, i.e., in flux flow, and the read contrast is ~0.5 µV versus 0. Whether repeated probing near J_c anneals the stored state is not tested. This is central both to the physics, since flux flow should anneal a quenched glass, and to any memory claim.

The authors should provide retention curves over hours, many-cycle endurance with switching statistics, and read-disturb tests. They should also report write and erase energies and the ON/OFF voltage ratio versus field and temperature.

**15. Novelty and technological claims are overstated.**

Several statements need correcting or substantiating:
- **Priority claims.** "First" electrically controlled superconducting memory, and "electrically-controllable memory ... has remained elusive," are inaccurate. Current-controlled vortex memories exist (Golod & Krasnov, refs. 23–24, which are cited), as do kinetic-inductance and nanowire memories. These are the relevant benchmark.
- **"Controlled solely by electrical stimuli."** The effect requires a 7–20 T bias field, T ≲ 0.4–0.6 K and mm-scale crystals.
- **"Ultralow-power," "non-dissipative," "read and write while superconducting."** Writing requires driving into flux flow, and one read state is resistive.
- **Power scaling.** The ~1 nW → ≲1 pW estimate ignores magnet, cryogenic and write overheads. It also assumes the bulk vortex-glass physics survives at ~1 µm², where edge and surface pinning dominate.
- **Silicon comparison.** The comparison to "nanometre-sized silicon elements" rests on a reference on intrinsic carrier concentration in Si (ref. 59), which does not support it.
- **Speculative outlook.** The AI energy-consumption framing and the Majorana/topological quantum computation outlook are not supported by the data.

The authors should tone down "first," "intrinsic," "non-volatile" and "ultralow-power." They should quantify the write-pulse energy, account for field and cryogenic requirements, remove or substantiate the pW-scaling and Si comparisons, and condense the speculative outlook.
