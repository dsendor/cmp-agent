Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Core claim.** Bulk single crystals of UTe₂ (B ∥ b, 50 mK, roughly 7–20 T) are said to show a history-dependent critical current.
  - An abrupt drop of a large dc current to zero (the "pump") leaves the sample in a state with higher J_c.
  - A smooth ramp-down from a large current (the "erase") restores the original, non-hysteretic V(J).
  - The high-J_c state is reported to persist for hours.
  - It can be tuned by pulse amplitude, duration and turn-off rate, and saturates for strong perturbations.
- **Proposed origin.** The authors place the effect in an "SC1.5" regime where SC1 and SC2 coexist.
  - The angular extent of the effect (θ ≲ 20° from b toward c) tracks the angular range of SC2.
  - They attribute it to two competing vortex species quenched into a disordered, more strongly pinned configuration.
  - They support this with a phenomenological RSJ-type model whose potential has minima at φ = 0 and φ = π and whose damping depends on phase.
- **Potential significance.**
  - If the effect is shown to be intrinsic to a multicomponent (possibly spin-triplet) order parameter, it would be a new transport probe of the SC1/SC2 interplay in UTe₂.
  - It would also be a new handle on unconventional vortex matter.
  - It connects to active topics: multiphase superconductivity, nonsingular or half-quantum vortices, and superconducting diode and memory effects.
- **Novelty is weaker than presented.** History-dependent J_c, metastable disordered vortex states with enhanced pinning, and current-driven switching between ordered and disordered vortex phases are long-established phenomena.
  - Examples include 2H-NbSe₂ and CeRu₂ near the peak effect: Henderson, Andrei et al.; Xiao, Andrei and Higgins; Paltiel et al., *Nature* (2000); Banerjee, Grover, Ramakrishnan et al.
  - In those systems, J_c depends on pulse amplitude, duration and ramp rate, and the "anneal" and "quench" phenomenology is the same as here.
  - Electrically addressable vortex memories already exist (Golod and Krasnov, refs. 23–24, which the authors cite).
  - The genuinely new element would be the specific link to the SC1/SC2 coexistence region. In its present form, that link is suggestive rather than demonstrated.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **High-quality, systematic dataset in a demanding environment.**
  - Data span dilution-fridge temperatures, fields up to 30 T and angular rotation.
  - Several crystals were measured, and three independent tuning knobs (amplitude, duration, relaxation rate) were explored.
  - Saturation behaviour (ED Fig. 1), temperature evolution (Fig. 3, ED Figs. 5–6) and field/angle maps (Fig. 4, ED Figs. 7–10) are documented.
- **Clear experimental phenomenology.**
  - Pump, erase and probe protocols are reproducible.
  - Switching between zero and finite voltage at a fixed bias is demonstrated (Fig. 1d).
  - The effect appears for both current polarities (ED Fig. 4).
  - Controls in pure SC2 (29 T, all angles, ED Fig. 10) and in low-field SC1 show no effect.
- **Informative internal trends.**
  - The effect grows with faster turn-off rate, even though this deposits less energy.
  - It disappears at a temperature scale (~0.6 K) well below T_c.
  - Its abrupt angular cutoff near ~20° coincides with a jump in equilibrium V(θ).
  - These features are non-trivial constraints for any explanation.
- **Topical relevance and reproducibility aids.**
  - The work is relevant to the large UTe₂ community and to efforts on superconducting logic and memory.
  - The measurement-control code is provided.
  - A theoretical framework is offered that could be refined into testable predictions.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

- **(i) Insufficient differentiation from well-known metastability of vortex matter; the "intrinsic, order-parameter-rooted" claim is not established.**
  - **Missing literature.** The proposed mechanism (a quenched, disordered vortex glass with stronger pinning, annealed by a slow current ramp) is essentially the textbook picture of metastable vortex states near the peak effect. Key works are not cited:
    - Henderson et al., PRL 1996;
    - Xiao et al., *Nature* 1999;
    - Paltiel et al., *Nature* 2000;
    - Ravikumar/Banerjee et al. on CeRu₂ and NbSe₂.
  - **Same phenomenology in conventional systems.** Those studies already show:
    - J_c enhancement after rapid current changes;
    - dependence on pulse width and ramp rate;
    - saturation behaviour;
    - erasure by slow driving;
    - disappearance on warming through thermal annealing.

    A conventional single-species vortex lattice with a nearby order–disorder transition could therefore reproduce every observation. The manuscript must show what is specifically two-component about UTe₂'s response.
  - **Anomalous V(B) is reminiscent of a peak effect.** The non-monotonic J_c(B) and dV/dJ features (Fig. 4b, ED Fig. 9: J_c minimal near 20 T and higher at 28 T than at 9 T) are classic peak-effect signatures. The memory window may simply coincide with this anomalous pinning regime rather than with order-parameter coexistence.
  - **Energy-scale argument is weak.** The effect vanishes below T_c, which is also expected for thermally activated creep annealing metastable disorder. It does not by itself point to an "intrinsic low-energy phenomenon." The analogy to the temperature dependence of the intrinsic diode effect (ref. 49) is loose and not physically motivated.
  - **Possible dependence on disorder.** Memory is shown only on S1 and S2 (RRR = 105 and 44). The cleanest crystal, S3 (RRR = 395), was measured only with ac lock-in and contributes no memory protocol. If the effect is weaker or absent in cleaner crystals, a disorder or pinning origin is favoured over an order-parameter origin.

- **(ii) Field and angle mapping does not convincingly tie the effect to an SC1/SC2 coexistence region.**
  - **Mismatch in field window.** In S2 (Fig. 4e, ED Fig. 8):
    - the effect onsets near 7 T and peaks near 10 T;
    - it is essentially gone by 16–20 T.

    The coexistence region cited (refs. 10, 46) is around 15–20 T. The text's statement that the memory region in S2 ends "just below 20 T" also conflicts with ED Fig. 8. The authors must explain why the effect is strongest about 10 T below the SC1→SC2 boundary, where SC2 is not thermodynamically present.
  - **Phase boundaries not measured on the same samples.** The phase diagram (Figs. 1a, 4a) is schematic and taken from other works (refs. 9, 33).
    - No thermodynamic measurement (specific heat, magnetization, ac susceptibility) of the SC1/SC2 boundary is presented on S1 or S2.
    - The sample dependence of the window (S1 near 20 T versus S2 at 7–15 T) is attributed to "crystalline quality" without evidence.
  - **Angular cutoff has a simpler explanation.** Rotating B toward c at fixed 15 T strongly reduces B_c2 of SC1 and changes the reduced field b = B/B_c2(θ) and the pinning landscape.
    - Disappearance of the effect and the V jump near 20° (Fig. 4g) may reflect approach to a peak-effect or upper-critical-field regime, not loss of SC2.
    - A comparison at constant reduced field, or at several fields versus angle, is needed.
    - The field used in ED Fig. 7 should be stated explicitly.
  - **Coexistence and two species are assumed, not shown.** "SC1.5 coexistence" and "two distinct vortex species" are adopted as premises.
    - No local or bulk probe (vortex imaging, SANS, magnetization, NMR) demonstrates distinct vortex species or domain structure.
    - The 0.6 K temperature scale is not connected to any independently measured energy scale of SC1/SC2 competition.

- **(iii) Extrinsic explanations (heating, contacts, critical-state/self-field effects, measurement-mode artefacts) are not adequately excluded.**
  - **Heating plus fast cooling is a thermal quench.** The heating counter-argument rests on the claim that faster turn-off deposits less energy. But heating followed by rapid cooling is precisely a thermal quench, which is known to produce disordered, high-J_c vortex states, analogous to field-cooled states.
    - The pump drives the sample (or the regions near spot-welded contacts) deep into flux flow at up to 20 A cm⁻², and the erase starts from 40 mA.
    - At 50 mK this may raise the local temperature momentarily, with the turn-off rate setting the effective cooling rate.
    - The rate dependence therefore does not discriminate against heating. The Supplementary temperature traces from a remote thermometer cannot resolve millisecond, local heating of the crystal or contacts.
  - **Missing quantitative inputs.**
    - Contact resistances in field, dissipated power during pump and erase, sample dimensions and cross-sections, and thermal time constants are not reported.
    - Without these, the Joule-heating discussion cannot be evaluated.
    - A heater-only thermal-pulse control at zero transport current is needed: does a heat pulse plus fast cooldown also "write" the memory state?
  - **Critical-state remanence not quantitatively excluded.** The argument that a Bean-model process "should be insensitive to how the current is turned off" is not generally correct.
    - Flux creep, the E–J relation and induced electric fields make remanent current distributions rate dependent.
    - Remanent transport-current distributions in mm-sized crystals can shift the apparent voltage onset.
    - The polarity symmetry of the enhancement (Fig. 1b orange curve, ED Fig. 4) is a useful argument against simple remanence and should be developed explicitly and quantitatively, rather than left implicit.
  - **Internal inconsistency with pulse-delta mode.** The Keithley 6221/2182A pulse-delta mode applies discrete current pulses that return to a low level between readings.
    - The "smooth" ramps and the erase (40→0 mA in 2 mA steps over 20 s) may therefore consist of repeated abrupt on/off transitions.
    - Fig. 2b shows that even ~10 µs pulses write the memory state.
    - It must be clarified why the steps in pulse-delta sweeps do not themselves act as pumps. Pulse widths, low-level values and duty cycles must be specified.
  - **Small effect size and missing uncertainties.**
    - ΔJ is only ~0.2–0.5 A cm⁻² at J_c ~ 1–2 A cm⁻², using a ~1 µV criterion.
    - No error bars, noise floor, or trace-to-trace reproducibility of the equilibrium curve are given.
    - Repeated equilibrium sweeps under identical conditions are needed to establish the significance of the heatmap features (Figs. 2, 3b).

- **(iv) The theoretical model is under-constrained and partly inconsistent; the device and energy claims are overstated.**
  - **Model is phenomenological and heavily tuned.** U(φ) is hand-crafted with several tuned parameters (g, the 0.6 prefactor, β = 40, δ = 20, γ₀, γ_π).
    - A single macroscopic phase variable with a junction-type Josephson relation is used to describe flux flow in a mm-sized, junction-free crystal, with no justification of the coarse-graining.
    - The model reproduces "quench → φ = π, anneal → φ = 0" essentially by construction. It does not predict the measured dependence on amplitude, duration, saturation, temperature (~0.6 K) or field window.
  - **Conceptual inconsistencies in the model description.**
    - φ is described in turn as a junction phase difference, as "Cooper pair momentum in the TDGL description," and as linked to Δ(r) ~ e^{iφr/L}. These are different objects.
    - The argument that inversion symmetry forces the second minimum to φ = π is not explained.
    - "Treat γ as a proxy to the injected current J" (Methods) is unclear.
    - The statement that larger damping implies "enhanced pinning" conflates the flux-flow viscosity with the pinning force that sets J_c.
    - The connection of the φ = π minimum to domain walls, "two vortex species," or a finite-q state is speculative. The finite-q and diode possibilities raised in Methods would generally predict nonreciprocity, which is not tested.
  - **Unsupported application claims.**
    - Operation requires 7–20 T and T ≲ 0.4 K.
    - Both writing (flux flow above J_c) and reading the "1" state (finite voltage) are dissipative.
    - The ~1 nW read power excludes magnet, cryogenic and write overheads.
    - Extrapolation to ≲1 pW at 1 µm² is not justified. Finite-size and edge-barrier effects may destroy the bulk vortex-glass physics.
    - The comparison to silicon cites a paper on intrinsic carrier concentration (ref. 59), which does not support the claim.
    - Statements such as "electrically-controllable memory ... has remained elusive" are inaccurate given refs. 23–24.
  - **Non-volatility and switching reliability not documented.** "Persists at least several hours" is asserted without data, and the pump/probe cycling (Fig. 1d) shows only ~5 cycles. Missing:
    - retention curves;
    - read-disturb tests (does repeated probing near J_c erase the state?);
    - endurance;
    - switching error rates and stochasticity.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Place the work within the vortex-metastability literature and add discriminating experiments.**
   - Cite and directly compare with the NbSe₂/CeRu₂ peak-effect metastability and memory work.
   - Run field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled and field-history protocols. Test whether FC states reproduce the high-J_c "memory" state.
   - Test whether shaking (ac drive) erases it, as in conventional vortex glasses.
   - Measure the same protocols in pure SC1 at matched reduced field and reduced temperature, and ideally in a conventional reference superconductor with the same contact geometry.
   - Measure the highest-RRR crystal (S3) with the dc memory protocols to establish how the effect depends on disorder.
2. **Rigorously exclude heating, contact and measurement-mode artefacts.**
   - Report sample dimensions, contact resistances in field, dissipated energy per pump and erase, and estimated thermal time constants.
   - Use on-sample (or on-contact) fast thermometry.
   - Perform heater-only quench controls without transport current, and equal-energy pulses with different shapes.
   - Vary contact geometry (e.g., separate current and voltage contact spacing) to test for contact-local effects.
   - Fully specify pulse-delta parameters (pulse width, low level, repetition rate). Repeat key results with true continuous dc ramps from a low-noise source.
   - Add error bars, noise floors and repeat-sweep reproducibility to all ΔJ maps.
   - Quantify the polarity-symmetry argument against remanent critical-state currents.
3. **Establish the microscopic link to SC1/SC2 coexistence.**
   - On the same crystals, measure the thermodynamic SC1/SC2 boundary (specific heat, magnetization or ac susceptibility, magnetic torque) versus B and θ. Overlay these boundaries on the ΔJ maps.
   - Explain why ΔJ peaks near 10 T in S2.
   - Perform angle sweeps at several fields, plotted versus reduced field B/B_c2(θ), to separate an "SC2 presence" from a "proximity to B_c2 or peak effect" explanation.
   - Probe vortex order before and after pump/erase where feasible, using magnetization relaxation, local Hall-probe or SQUID imaging, SANS or NMR linewidth. This would directly support quenched disorder and distinct vortex species.
   - Report the field dependence of the 0.6 K scale and relate it to an independently measured energy scale.
4. **Strengthen the theory and temper the application claims.**
   - Ground the RSJ potential in a coarse-grained or TDGL picture of a two-component order parameter, or replace or supplement it with overdamped Langevin simulations of two interacting vortex species with pinning.
   - Derive testable predictions:
     - ΔJ versus pulse duration and turn-off rate scaling;
     - saturation;
     - temperature cutoff;
     - absence or presence of nonreciprocity, in particular for the finite-q scenario.
   - Reduce or justify the free parameters and clarify the physical meaning of φ and γ.
   - On the device side, provide retention-time data, read-disturb and endurance tests, and switching statistics.
   - Report write and read energies including dissipation, and note the requirement for 7–20 T and sub-0.5 K operation.
   - Remove or substantiate the pW-scaling and silicon comparisons, and correct the novelty statements regarding prior electrically controlled vortex memories.
