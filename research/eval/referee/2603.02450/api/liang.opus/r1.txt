Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Central claim.** Bulk flux-grown UTe₂ crystals with B ∥ b (and tilts up to ~20° toward c) show a history-dependent critical current in a field window between the SC1 and SC2 phases (the "SC1.5" region).
  - The "write" step is an abrupt drop of a large dc current to zero. It produces a state with enhanced J_c.
  - ΔJ is ~0.2–0.5 A cm⁻², on a J_c of ~1–2 A cm⁻².
  - The "erase" step is a smooth ramp down from large current. It restores the low-J_c state.
  - The state is reported to persist for hours.
- **Tunability.** The effect is controlled by three stimulus parameters: amplitude, duration and turn-off rate. It vanishes above ~0.6 K, beyond θ ≈ 20°, and in SC2 (29–30 T).
- **Interpretation.** The authors attribute the effect to competition between SC1- and SC2-derived vortex species. In this picture, a quench yields a glassy, more strongly pinned configuration and a slow ramp anneals it.
  - The phenomenology is captured with an overdamped RSJ-type model. It has a double-well U(φ) (minima at 0 and π) and phase-dependent damping.
- **Potential significance.**
  - It would be an intrinsic, electrically addressable bistability of vortex matter tied to multiphase (possibly triplet) superconductivity.
  - This would add a new transport fingerprint of the SC1/SC2 interplay in UTe₂, alongside the reported low-J_c anomaly, vortex doublets and stripes.
  - It may also bear on non-singular or half-quantum vortex physics.
- **Novelty caveat.** History-dependent J_c, metastable disordered vortex phases, current-pulse writing/annealing and "memory effects" in vortex matter are long established. Examples include 2H-NbSe₂, CeRu₂ and low-T_c alloys near the peak effect, and high-T_c cuprates.
  - Novelty therefore rests entirely on demonstrating that the effect is specific to, and caused by, the SC1/SC2 multiphase order. The manuscript does not yet establish this convincingly.
  - The technological framing (AI energy consumption, non-volatile cryogenic memory) is presently far ahead of the evidence. It requires 7–20 T fields, 50 mK and mm-scale crystals.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Systematic, multi-axis phenomenology.**
  - Three independent stimulus parameters are explored, each showing saturating dose–response curves (Ext. Data Fig. 1).
  - The effect appears under both current polarities (Ext. Data Fig. 4).
  - It has a well-defined temperature cutoff (Fig. 3, Ext. Data Figs. 5–6).
- **Clear negative controls.** The effect is absent at low field (≤5 T), in SC2 at 29–30 T across angles (Ext. Data Fig. 10), and beyond θ ≈ 19–20° (Ext. Data Fig. 7).
  - This makes a purely generic contact or instrumentation artefact less likely.
- **Correlation with known UTe₂ anomalies.** The memory window coincides with:
  - the anomalous low-J_c region;
  - non-monotonic ∂V/∂J (Ext. Data Fig. 9);
  - kinks in V(B) (Fig. 4b) and a jump in V(θ) (Fig. 4g).
  - Together these suggest a genuine change in vortex dynamics in this regime.
- **Relaxation-rate dependence.** The effect is stronger for faster turn-off, i.e., for less integrated energy. This is a non-trivial observation that constrains simple dose-type explanations.
- **Broad interest and reproducibility.** The topic is timely, UTe₂ is a high-profile material, and the measurement code is shared.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**(i) The effect is not distinguished from well-known metastable vortex-matter phenomena; the "intrinsic, order-parameter-rooted" attribution is not demonstrated.**
- **Peak-effect phenomenology is uncited and unaddressed.** Ordered (low-J_c) versus supercooled disordered (high-J_c) vortex states near the peak effect are a textbook case of history-dependent J_c. Relevant work includes:
  - Henderson, Andrei, Higgins and Bhattacharya on 2H-NbSe₂;
  - Paltiel et al. (Nature 2000) on dynamic instabilities, edge contamination and memory effects;
  - Xiao, Andrei, Ling and Banerjee on CeRu₂;
  - field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled J_c and current-pulse switching.
  - In those systems, current pulses of specific amplitude, duration and shape reversibly switch J_c, and states persist for hours at low T. This matches what is reported here.
  - The UTe₂ data even show the hallmarks: a J_c minimum/maximum structure versus B (Ext. Data Fig. 9), a kink in V(B) at the window edge (Fig. 4b), and strong sample dependence of the window.
- **The field window does not coincide with the SC1/SC2 coexistence region.**
  - For S2 the memory region spans ~7–15 T at θ = 0 (Ext. Data Fig. 8; Fig. 4e peaks at ~10 T and vanishes by ~16 T).
  - Yet the text places the S2 SC1→SC2 boundary "just below 20 T".
  - The thermodynamic coexistence region reported by Sakai and Rosuel lies ~15–20 T.
  - For S1 the effect is at ~20 T.
  - A window starting at 7 T sits deep in SC1 by specific-heat criteria. The schematic phase diagrams in Figs. 1a and 4a appear drawn to fit the transport data rather than independently determined.
  - The window shifts by several tesla between samples with different RRR (S1: 105; S2: 44). This points to pinning and disorder as the controlling factor rather than a sharp thermodynamic boundary.
- **The angular cutoff is not specific evidence for SC2.**
  - The ~20° cutoff is measured at 15 T, where S2 at θ = 0 is (by the authors' own statement) still below SC2.
  - Angle-dependent changes in J_c, H_c2 anisotropy and vortex-lattice orientation relative to pinning could produce a similar cutoff.
  - No independent probe on the same sample (specific heat, ac susceptibility, magnetostriction) locates SC2 versus angle.
- **"Intrinsic" is used ambiguously.** Bulk vortex-lattice order–disorder metastability is intrinsic to vortex matter, but it is not evidence of triplet or multiphase order.
  - The analogy to the intrinsic superconducting diode effect, based only on enhancement at low T, is not a physical argument.
  - Metastable disordered vortex phases in conventional superconductors are also most robust at low T, where thermal creep is suppressed.

**(ii) Thermal and instrumental artefacts are not adequately excluded; key arguments against heating may actually favour a thermal-quench mechanism.**
- **The relaxation-rate argument does not separate thermal from non-thermal mechanisms.** Fast turn-off delivers less energy, but a rapid quench from a locally heated state is the canonical way to freeze a disordered vortex configuration.
  - Local heating at spot-welded contacts during the pump, followed by fast cooling, would naturally give "stronger memory for faster turn-off, saturating with amplitude and duration".
  - Heat deposited at contacts at 20 A cm⁻² in 20 T fields at 50 mK is plausibly significant. The mixing-chamber thermometry described in the SI cannot resolve sample temperature on 10 µs–ms timescales.
- **The low-T enhancement argument is circular.** It does not exclude heating, because the vortex-glass and supercooling physics itself is strongest at low T. A decisive control is missing: pump with a deliberate heater pulse (or a laser/heat pulse) at zero transport current and test whether the same high-J_c state results.
- **The waveform delivered to the sample is unverified.**
  - The 6221 arbitrary-waveform output is limited in point rate and bandwidth. A 10 µs "duration" pulse defined by 100 points, or α = 500 A cm⁻² ms⁻¹, may not be realised as specified at the sample.
  - The sample sits at the end of long twisted-pair wiring in a 30 T magnet, which filters and distorts fast edges.
  - The effective shape at the sample should be measured, e.g., by oscilloscope on a dummy load in situ.
- **The pulse-delta read-out itself consists of abrupt pulses to and from a baseline.**
  - It is unclear why the "smooth" probe and erase sweeps, which are discrete 2 mA steps over 20 s, do not themselves constitute pumps.
  - It is also unclear which instrument settings (pulse width, low level, polarity alternation) differentiate "pump" from "probe".
  - Without this, the operational definition of "equilibrium" versus "out-of-equilibrium" is ambiguous.
- **Geometry and current-distribution effects are unexplored.**
  - The crystals are bulk mm-scale with point-like spot-welded contacts, giving inhomogeneous current injection and self-fields.
  - Edge and surface barrier contamination (cf. Paltiel et al.) is a known source of pulse-dependent metastability.
  - These effects are not explored by varying contact geometry, sample thickness, or voltage-contact placement.

**(iii) The data quality, statistics and memory metrics do not support the strength of the claims ("non-volatile", "read/writeable memory", technological viability).**
- **The effect is small and defined at a low threshold.**
  - ΔJ is ~0.2–0.5 A cm⁻², defined at a nominal V = 1 µV threshold on curves showing µV-level noise and step-like structure.
  - No error bars, cycle-to-cycle distributions or repeat measurements are shown for the heat maps or Ext. Data Fig. 1. The heat maps appear partly noise-textured.
  - Robustness of ΔJ to the choice of V criterion is not shown.
- **Persistence is asserted but not shown.**
  - "At least several hours" retention is stated without any retention-time data (ΔJ versus waiting time) or temperature dependence of decay.
  - Field and temperature stability during the wait are not documented.
  - Fig. 1d shows only ~5 write/erase cycles. Endurance, read margin and error rates are not quantified.
- **Readout is dissipative and potentially destructive.**
  - In Fig. 1d the "0" state is read at finite voltage (~0.5 µV), i.e., in flux flow.
  - Whether repeated reads anneal the memory state (read disturb) is not tested. This is central both to the physics (flux flow should anneal a quenched glass) and to any memory claim.
- **Sample coverage is thin.**
  - Detailed tuning data come essentially from S2 alone, the lowest-RRR sample (44).
  - S1 provides a few traces at 20 T, and S3 only ac resistivity without pump/erase tests.
  - Reproducibility across crystals, including the highest-quality ones, is therefore not established.
- **Application claims are overstated.**
  - Power estimates (~1 nW → ≲1 pW) ignore the 7–20 T bias field and dilution-refrigerator temperatures.
  - "Ultralow-power switching" is not quantified for the write pulse.
  - The comparison to "nanometre-sized silicon elements" via a reference on intrinsic carrier concentration in Si is not meaningful.
  - The statement that the effect is "controlled solely by electrical stimuli" ignores the requirement of a large applied field.
  - Existing zero-field vortex memories (e.g., Golod & Krasnov) are a more relevant benchmark.

**(iv) The theoretical model is phenomenological, internally inconsistent with the proposed microscopic picture, and makes no testable predictions.**
- **The mapping from a bulk crystal to a single-phase RSJ variable is unjustified.**
  - φ is described variously as a phase difference, a Cooper-pair momentum, and a domain-wall/vortex configuration variable.
  - The claim that inversion symmetry forces the second minimum to be at φ = π is not demonstrated for an inhomogeneous state.
  - Treating "γ as a proxy to the injected current J" when constructing J–V curves is confusing and appears inconsistent with Eq. (2).
- **The key ingredients are put in by hand.** The extra minimum, its higher critical current (β = 40) and the sharply peaked damping at φ = π (δ = 20) are chosen to obtain the result.
  - As a consequence, the model captures only the binary outcome (quench → π, anneal → 0).
  - It does not reproduce the graded, saturating dependence on amplitude and duration (Ext. Data Fig. 1), the T dependence, or the field window.
- **Physical inconsistencies need resolution.**
  - The text equates larger flux-flow damping (lower flux-flow resistance) with stronger pinning and higher J_c. These are distinct quantities.
  - Two different pictures are offered without reconciliation: a "glassy vortex state with higher disorder" and a "grid of SC1/SC2 domain walls with Δ ∼ e^{iφr/L}".
  - Methods contains errors, e.g., "global minimum at π = 0".
- **The model is not falsifiable and is not tied to UTe₂ energy scales.**
  - No connection is made to UTe₂ parameters (coherence lengths of SC1/SC2, domain-wall energy, pinning).
  - There is no explanation of why the relevant energy scale is ~0.5 K.
  - No distinguishing predictions are offered (e.g., threshold J_ex versus T or B, a characteristic time constant, noise signatures). The model therefore cannot discriminate the proposed mechanism from conventional vortex-lattice metastability.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Benchmark directly against conventional vortex-metastability phenomenology and cite that literature.**
   - Perform field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled and field-sweep-history comparisons of J_c in and out of the window.
   - Test "shaking" annealing with small ac currents or ac fields.
   - Measure the magnetic-field dependence of J_c with high resolution to establish whether the window coincides with a peak-effect-like anomaly.
   - Compare the dynamics with NbSe₂ and CeRu₂ results.
   - Bulk magnetization (or local Hall-probe) measurements of pump-induced J_c changes would test whether the effect is bulk or edge/contact-related.

2. **Rule out thermal and instrumental origins decisively.**
   - Use an on-sample fast thermometer or a resistive sensor on the crystal.
   - Apply heater-only "thermal pumps" of matched energy and timescale with no transport current.
   - Verify the delivered waveforms at the sample with a scope.
   - Vary contact configuration and resistance, sample cross-section and thickness, and the location of the current contacts relative to the voltage contacts.
   - Fully document the pulse-delta parameters and show that probe and erase sweeps do not themselves act as pumps.

3. **Establish the link to SC1/SC2 coexistence with independent thermodynamic data on the same crystals.**
   - Measure ac susceptibility, specific heat or magnetostriction versus B and θ, and overlay these on maps of ΔJ(B, θ, T) with error bars.
   - Plot temperature dependence relative to T_c(B) at each field.
   - Present systematic results on several crystals spanning a range of RRR, including the cleanest (RRR ~400), to separate disorder/pinning effects from phase-boundary effects.
   - Explain the 7–15 T window in S2 versus ~20 T in S1 and versus the published coexistence region.

4. **Quantify memory performance and recalibrate claims; strengthen or reframe the theory.**
   - Show retention curves over hours, many-cycle endurance with statistics, read-disturb tests, and threshold/switching-energy estimates for the write pulse.
   - Show robustness of ΔJ to the voltage criterion.
   - Substantially moderate the AI and energy framing and account for magnet and cryogenic overheads.
   - For the model, either derive U(φ) and γ(φ) from a two-component Ginzburg–Landau or vortex description of SC1/SC2, or present it clearly as illustrative.
   - Reconcile the vortex-glass and domain-wall pictures, and provide at least one quantitative, falsifiable prediction (e.g., T or B scaling of the threshold amplitude or turn-off rate) to compare with the data.
   - Correct the noted errors: Ext. Data Fig. 1 axis labels "J_ex (ms)" and "J/α (ms)", inconsistent α units in Ext. Data Fig. 5, the "π = 0" typo, and the Si power-comparison reference.
