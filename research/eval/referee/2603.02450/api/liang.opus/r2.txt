Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Claim.** The manuscript reports a history-dependent, electrically switchable critical current in bulk UTe₂ single crystals (B ∥ b̂, T ≲ 0.6 K, roughly 7–20 T).
  - An abrupt drop of a large dc current to zero ("pump") leaves the sample in a state with an enhanced J_c.
  - A slow ramp-down ("erase") restores the original, non-hysteretic V(J).
  - The authors present this as an *intrinsic* superconducting memory effect. They tie it to the proposed SC1/SC2 coexistence ("SC1.5") region and to competition between two vortex species.
- **Why the topic matters.**
  - It addresses a timely question: how a multicomponent, putatively spin-triplet order parameter affects vortex matter.
  - Electrically addressable vortex states are of genuine interest for cryogenic electronics.
- **Novel elements.**
  - Systematic three-axis control of the effect (pulse amplitude, duration, relaxation rate), each showing saturation.
  - An angular study in which the memory region appears truncated near θ ≈ 20°, close to the known angular extent of SC2.
  - Absence of the effect deep in SC2 (29–30 T).
- **Novelty is weaker than presented.** Current-driven switching between a metastable disordered vortex state (high J_c) and an annealed ordered state (low J_c) is well documented in conventional superconductors. Examples include 2H-NbSe₂ and CeRu₂ near the peak effect: Henderson, Andrei, Higgins et al. (PRL 1996); Xiao et al. (PRL 1999); Paltiel et al. (Nature 2000); and the broader Bragg-glass/vortex-glass metastability literature.
  - The protocol described here (quench from flux flow versus slow annealing through J_c) closely mirrors those studies.
  - None of this literature is cited or discussed.
  - The genuinely new content therefore hinges on showing that the UTe₂ effect is *qualitatively* tied to multiphase superconductivity rather than being a generic vortex order–disorder metastability.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Robust, reproducible phenomenology.**
  - The effect appears in two samples (S1, S2) and under three independent stimulus parameters.
  - It shows saturation behaviour (Ext. Data Fig. 1) and is consistent for both probe polarities (Ext. Data Fig. 4).
- **Extensive parameter-space mapping.**
  - Field (5–30 T), angle (−1° to 31°) and temperature (50 mK–1 K) are covered.
  - The raw curves are shown (Ext. Data Figs. 6–8, 10), which allows independent assessment.
- **Suggestive link to the SC2 phase.**
  - The memory region appears confined to the B–θ range lying beneath SC2.
  - Its disappearance coincides with a jump in equilibrium V(θ) near θ ≈ 20° (Fig. 4f,g) and with anomalies in ∂V/∂J (Ext. Data Fig. 9).
  - This hints at a real connection between vortex dynamics and the multiphase structure.
- **Informative relaxation-rate dependence.** A stronger effect for *faster* current turn-off (at lower deposited energy) is non-trivial and constrains simple critical-state explanations.
- **Broad interest.**
  - The work engages the large UTe₂ community.
  - It points to a new experimental handle (transport history protocols) on vortex physics in candidate triplet superconductors.
  - Measurement code is offered as a supplement, aiding reproducibility.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

- **Reason 1: Conventional vortex-matter metastability is not excluded, so the "intrinsic, order-parameter-rooted" claim is unsupported.**
  - **The proposed mechanism is the textbook picture.** The authors' own explanation (a quenched glassy, disordered vortex configuration with higher J_c, annealed back to an ordered lattice by slow current reduction) is essentially that of metastable disordered vortex phases near the peak effect / order–disorder transition in NbSe₂, CeRu₂, V₃Si, MgB₂ and others.
    - In those systems, current pulses, FC/ZFC histories and ac "shaking" switch J_c between two states with long-lived memory.
    - The same systems show strong dependence on pulse amplitude, duration and ramp rate.
    - Without addressing this literature, nothing in the data distinguishes "two vortex species / two order parameters" from a single-species order–disorder transition driven by the strongly field-dependent pinning and elastic moduli that UTe₂ is known to have in this field range (cf. the low-J_c region of Tokiwa et al., and Ext. Data Fig. 9, where J_c is minimal near 20 T).
  - **The link to SC1/SC2 coexistence is weaker than stated.**
    - The memory appears from ~7 T and ΔJ peaks near ~10 T (Fig. 4e). This is far below the thermodynamic SC1→SC2 boundary (~15–20 T from specific heat and susceptibility).
    - No thermodynamic or microscopic evidence is offered that SC2 components exist at 7–10 T. The purple "memory region" in Figs. 1a and 4a is defined by the transport effect itself, which makes the argument circular.
    - The angular cutoff (~19–20°) could equally reflect the angle at which the vortex lattice/pinning regime changes, e.g. the V(θ) jump in Fig. 4g. It need not reflect a change of order parameter.
  - **Sample dependence points toward pinning-related (extrinsic) physics.**
    - S2 (RRR 44) shows memory from 7 to ~19 T, whereas S1 (RRR 105) shows it near ~20 T.
    - The cleanest crystal, S3 (RRR 395), apparently was never tested with the pulse protocol; only ac ρ(B) is shown in Fig. 4h.
    - A strong dependence on disorder level is exactly what is expected for peak-effect-type metastability. The authors attribute it to "crystalline quality" without testing this.

- **Reason 2: Heating and measurement artefacts are insufficiently excluded, and the stated counter-arguments are not conclusive.**
  - **The relaxation-rate argument does not rule out heating.**
    - Pulses up to 20 A cm⁻² for up to 20 ms drive the sample deep into flux flow at 50 mK, through spot-welded contacts on a mm-sized crystal.
    - A transient rise of sample or vortex temperature followed by rapid cooling is precisely a thermal *quench*. It is a classic way to freeze in a disordered vortex state, analogous to field cooling versus zero-field cooling.
    - Faster current turn-off implies faster cooling and hence a more disordered frozen state. This would reproduce the observed α dependence even though less total energy is deposited.
    - The relaxation-rate argument is therefore consistent with a heating-induced quench rather than evidence against it.
  - **The temperature-dependence argument is not decisive.** The effect vanishing above 0.6 K does not exclude heating, since the frozen-in disorder only needs to survive at base temperature.
  - **The thermometry and power estimates are not quantitative.**
    - Temperature-versus-time traces are said to be in the Supplementary Information, which is not provided here.
    - The thermometer is presumably not on the sample. The sample–bath thermal time constant at 50 mK is likely longer than the ms pulses.
    - The following estimates are missing: dissipated power per pulse (bulk plus contact resistance), sample heat capacity, thermal relaxation time.
  - **The pulse-delta protocol raises a consistency question.**
    - The Keithley 6221 pulse-delta mode applies short current pulses with abrupt off-periods.
    - If an abrupt drop from high J to zero "writes" the memory state, why do the probe and erase sweeps, which are themselves sequences of abrupt pulses (and a stepwise 2 mA-step ramp), not write it?
    - The pulse width, duty cycle and off-level used in each sweep must be specified. The authors should show that the probe does not disturb the stored state (read disturb).
  - **Non-uniform current injection is not discussed.** Spot-welded contacts on the (001) surface of a thick crystal can produce current crowding, local self-heating and inhomogeneous vortex flow. Edge/surface contamination effects (Paltiel et al.) are known to produce exactly this type of metastable high-J_c state.

- **Reason 3: The theoretical model is ad hoc, physically ambiguous for a bulk sample, and largely encodes the result.**
  - **It is unclear what the phase variable means.**
    - The RSJ description treats φ as the phase drop across a single junction. For a mm-scale bulk crystal in the flux-flow regime, the physical meaning of φ, the second minimum at φ = π, and Δ(r) ~ e^{iφr/L} are not derived.
    - The inversion-symmetry argument for placing the minimum at φ = π is not convincing. A uniform phase winding of π over a length L is a finite-momentum state, and calling it "inhomogeneous domain walls" changes the physics.
    - The Methods also invoke a cos(qr) pair-density-wave state, which is a different object.
  - **The parameters are tuned to give the answer.**
    - The potential contains several arbitrary choices: g, the factor 0.6, β = 40, δ = 20, γ₀ = γ_π = 1.
    - The key ingredient is a phase-dependent damping γ(φ) sharply peaked at π. This is introduced by hand to make φ "stick" at π after a quench, so the simulation demonstrates the assumption rather than explaining the data.
    - The model has no microscopic link to SC1/SC2 (e.g. a two-component Ginzburg–Landau free energy).
    - It does not predict the field, angle or temperature range, the ~0.6 K scale, or the ms-scale saturation of the duration dependence.
  - **There are internal inconsistencies and confusing statements.**
    - In RSJ, J_c is set by max U′, not by damping. Yet the text attributes higher J_c to larger damping and "lower resistance from domain walls."
    - The Methods state that γ is treated "as a proxy to the injected current J", which is unclear.
    - The Methods describe the state as "long-lived transient", whereas the main text says it is "not transiently metastable".
    - The simulated curves show discrete jumps; the measured curves are smooth.
    - Phrases such as "minimise entropy" and "stronger pinning forces between vortices" are conceptually inaccurate. Pinning is vortex–defect, and the enhanced J_c of a disordered lattice follows from Larkin–Ovchinnikov-type arguments.
    - The Josephson timescales implied by φ̇ = 2eV/ħ are not reconciled with the observed ms-scale dynamics.

- **Reason 4: Significance and technological claims are overstated relative to the evidence.**
  - **The claim of first electrically controlled superconducting memory is too strong.**
    - Current-controlled vortex-based memories already exist (e.g. Golod & Krasnov, refs. 23–24), as do kinetic-inductance and nanowire memories.
    - The effect requires 7–20 T static fields and T < 0.6 K, with ΔJ ≈ 0.2–0.5 A cm⁻² on J_c ≈ 1.5–2 A cm⁻² and read contrast of ~0.5 µV versus 0.
  - **The power and practicality claims do not hold up as stated.**
    - Writing requires driving the sample into the dissipative flux-flow state.
    - Reading by voltage at a bias inside the hysteresis window means one logic state is resistive.
    - Hence the claims of "ultralow-power", "non-dissipative" and "both read and write while superconducting" need qualification.
    - The 1 nW → 1 pW scaling estimate is naive. It ignores the magnet, cryogenic overhead and whether the effect survives in micron-scale devices, where edge and surface pinning dominate.
  - **Key memory metrics are asserted rather than demonstrated.**
    - "Non-volatile" and "persists for at least several hours" are stated without retention data (V or ΔJ versus waiting time).
    - Fig. 1d shows only a handful of cycles. There are no switching-error statistics, threshold distributions or endurance data.
  - **Peripheral discussion is speculative.**
    - The Majorana and topological quantum computation outlook, the analogy to the intrinsic diode effect's temperature dependence, and the AI-energy framing are not supported by the data.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

- **Discriminate against conventional vortex metastability and heating with decisive control experiments.**
  - Repeat the identical protocol on a conventional reference superconductor (e.g. 2H-NbSe₂ near its peak effect, or Nb) in the same setup.
  - Perform FC versus ZFC and field-shaking (small ac field) tests to see whether the "memory" state is equivalent to a supercooled disordered vortex phase.
  - Use an on-sample thermometer or a fast-response thermometer, and give quantitative estimates of pulse energy, sample heat capacity and thermal time constants.
  - Vary contact geometry and sample thickness to test edge and surface contamination effects.
  - Test polarity-resolved writing (abrupt drop from −J versus +J; slow erase from +J) to rule out critical-state remanence.
  - Measure the cleanest sample, S3, with the full pulse protocol, and compare samples of systematically different RRR.
- **Establish an independent, thermodynamic or microscopic link between the memory region and SC1/SC2 coexistence.**
  - On the same crystals, measure specific heat, ac susceptibility or magnetisation (including ZFC/FC hysteresis) to locate the SC1–SC2 boundary and any coexistence regime, especially at 7–12 T where ΔJ peaks.
  - Where feasible, use direct vortex-structure probes in the two states: small-angle neutron scattering (lattice order), scanning Hall/SQUID microscopy, or voltage-noise spectroscopy (plastic versus elastic flow).
  - Correlate ΔJ quantitatively with the equilibrium J_c(B, θ) minimum and with the V(θ) jump. This would show whether the memory tracks the order-parameter boundary rather than a pinning/elasticity crossover.
- **Quantify memory performance and clarify protocols.**
  - Provide retention curves over hours, many-cycle switching statistics (write/erase thresholds and error rates), and read-disturb tests.
  - Report energy per write/erase and ON/OFF voltage ratio versus field and temperature.
  - Fully specify the pulse-delta parameters (pulse width, duty cycle, off-level, step timing) for probe and erase sweeps. Explain why these do not write the memory state.
  - Report ΔJ normalised to J_c, with error bars and the sensitivity of the heatmaps to the chosen V threshold.
  - Fix the inconsistencies:
    - Ext. Data Fig. 1a x-axis labelled "J_ex (ms)".
    - α units given as A cm⁻² s⁻¹ in Ext. Data Fig. 5 but A cm⁻² ms⁻¹ elsewhere.
    - "π = 0" typo in the Methods.
    - Duplicate refs. 26/57.
    - The "transient" versus "not transient" wording.
- **Strengthen or moderate the theory, and recalibrate the claims.**
  - Either ground the model in a two-component time-dependent Ginzburg–Landau / vortex-dynamics simulation, with explicit SC1 and SC2 order parameters, that yields the metastable state and its field, angle and temperature dependence; or present the RSJ potential explicitly as a phenomenological illustration.
  - Justify γ(φ) physically, define φ for the bulk geometry, and correct the J_c-versus-damping reasoning and the entropy/pinning terminology.
  - Explicitly cite and contrast prior work on current-driven metastable vortex states and current-controlled vortex memories.
  - Tone down "first", "intrinsic", "non-volatile" and "ultralow-power", and condense the speculative Majorana/AI outlook so the paper's conclusions match what the data establish.
