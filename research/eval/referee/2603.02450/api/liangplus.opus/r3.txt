Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The authors report that, in bulk UTe₂ crystals with B ∥ b̂ (7–20 T, T ≲ 0.5 K), the dc critical current can be switched between a low-J_c "equilibrium" state and a high-J_c "memory" state. Switching uses current pulses: an abrupt turn-off writes the high-J_c state, and a slow ramp-down erases it. The effect is confined in field-angle space to the region beneath SC2 and vanishes in pure SC2. The authors attribute it to competition between SC1 and SC2 vortex species and model it with a two-minimum RSJ potential.

If the effect is intrinsic to the multiphase order parameter, it would be a notable addition to the phenomenology of UTe₂ vortex matter. As presented, however, the phenomenology closely resembles current-driven metastability of vortex matter already documented in single-order-parameter superconductors. The evidence does not yet separate the proposed mechanism from that, or from heat-pulse quenching.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The switching protocol is clear and repeatable (Fig. 1b,d), with three independent control knobs (amplitude, duration, turn-off rate) that each saturate (Fig. 2, ED Fig. 1).
- Systematic field and angle mapping on S2 includes useful negative controls:
  - no effect at 3–5 T;
  - no effect at 29–30 T at any angle (Fig. 4c,f; ED Fig. 10);
  - an angular cut-off near θ ≈ 19–20° at 15 T that coincides with an anomaly in the equilibrium V(θ) (Fig. 4g; ED Fig. 7).
- Temperature dependence is reported for all three tuning modes (Fig. 3, ED Figs. 5–6).
- Both current polarities are tested (ED Fig. 4).
- The measurement code is to be supplied.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **Prior work on current-driven vortex metastability and memory is not discussed or ruled out.**
   - *Prior phenomenology.* Pulse-amplitude- and pulse-duration-dependent switching between a disordered high-J_c and an ordered low-J_c vortex state, persisting for hours and erasable by slow current ramps or shaking, is well established. Examples include 2H-NbSe₂ and CeRu₂ near the peak effect (Henderson et al., PRL 1996; Xiao, Andrei & Higgins, PRL 1999; Paltiel et al., Nature 2000; Banerjee et al.). In those systems the mechanism involves a single order parameter: a supercooled disordered vortex phase, plus edge contamination and annealing by the drive.
   - *This paper's own data look similar.* The equilibrium data contain the signatures associated with those effects. Fig. 4b shows a non-monotonic V(B) dome at 8–14 T, and ED Fig. 9 shows J_c minimal near 20 T but larger at 28 T than at 9 T. These are peak-effect-like non-monotonicities of J_c, coinciding with the memory region.
   - *Why it matters.* The introduction's statement that electrically controllable superconducting memory "has remained elusive", and the conclusion that the effect is "rooted in the superconducting order itself", do not follow until this explanation is excluded.
   - *What to do.* Discuss this literature and perform discriminating tests, for example:
     - field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled initial states;
     - annealing by a small ac field or ac current ("shaking") instead of the dc erase;
     - dependence on contact geometry and on the relative positions of current and voltage leads (edge contamination predicts strong geometry and position dependence);
     - broadband voltage-noise measurements in the two states;
     - whether the memory region tracks the J_c non-monotonicity or the thermodynamic SC1/SC2 boundary when the two are separated (e.g., by angle or sample).

2. **A heat-pulse ("thermal quench") mechanism is not excluded, and the main counter-arguments do not exclude it.**
   - *Size of the heating.* The pumps reach ~20 A cm⁻² (the reset starts at 40 mA). They last ms to tens of seconds (Fig. 1d pumps appear to be ~20 s long) and drive the sample into flux flow. Current passes through spot-welded contacts at 50 mK. Contact dissipation at 40 mA could reach the µW–mW range, far above typical dilution-fridge cooling power at the sample.
   - *Turn-off rate (α) argument.* The authors argue that the strong dependence on turn-off rate excludes heating because faster α deposits less energy. But if the current heats the sample, a fast current turn-off is also a fast thermal quench and a slow ramp is a slow anneal. The α-dependence (Fig. 2c, ED Fig. 1c) is therefore exactly what a heat-pulse-then-quench mechanism predicts.
   - *Temperature-dependence argument.* An effect strongest at low T and vanishing by ~0.6 K is likewise expected for any quenched metastable vortex state that thermal creep anneals at higher T. It argues against a spurious voltage offset, not against heat-assisted quenching.
   - *Missing heating analysis.* The heating analysis is deferred to a Supplementary Information that is not part of the submission. Fridge-thermometer readings cannot resolve ms-scale transients at the sample.
   - *What to provide.*
     - Contact resistances.
     - Sample dimensions and an energy budget per pump.
     - Time-resolved on-sample thermometry (e.g., a bare RuO₂ chip glued to the crystal).
     - Most decisively, a control in which a heater pulse of comparable energy and time profile is applied with no transport current, or with a sub-threshold current, to test whether it writes the memory state.

3. **The measurement waveforms appear to contradict the protocol narrative, and the actual waveform at the sample is unknown.**
   - *Pulse-delta mode.* Methods state that V(J) sweeps, the reset (40→0 mA in 2 mA steps over 20 s) and the Fig. 1d cycling all use the 6221/2182A pulse-delta mode. In that mode each point is a short pulse with abrupt returns to a low level, often zero. As described, the "smooth" erase and the probes would themselves consist of abrupt drops from up to 40 mA, which the paper identifies as the write operation.
   - *What to clarify.*
     - Pulse width, low level, repetition rate and delay for every protocol.
     - Why these steps do not write.
     - Whether the probe sweep, which enters flux flow at up to ~8 A cm⁻² in Fig. 1b, is destructive. The text says the state persists "upon subsequent measurements", but the erase is itself a ramp down from high current.
   - *Filtering.* Cryostat wiring usually includes RC/π filters. The 10 µs pulses and the α values up to 500 A cm⁻² ms⁻¹ are therefore nominal unless the current at the sample was measured. Please show measured I(t) for representative pumps, for example across a series shunt at the cold stage.

4. **The identification of the memory region with SC1–SC2 coexistence ("SC1.5") is not established independently.**
   - *Onset field.* The memory turns on at ~7 T (ED Fig. 8; Fig. 4e). This is well below the thermodynamic SC1/SC2 boundary near 15–20 T (refs. 9, 10) and below the 15–20 T coexistence region of ref. 46.
   - *Upper edge in S2.* In S2 the memory appears to vanish at ~16–17 T (ED Fig. 8). This conflicts with the statement that the boundary in S2 is "just below 20 T".
   - *Source of the phase diagrams.* Figs. 1a and 4a are schematics drawn from ref. 33, which concerns ultraclean crystals, whereas S2 has RRR 44.
   - *Angular cut-off.* The cut-off at θ ≈ 20° is shown only at 15 T. At that field SC2 is not itself present at θ = 0, so the coincidence with SC2's angular extent at higher fields is suggestive rather than conclusive.
   - *What to do.* Locate the SC1/SC2 boundary on the same crystals with a thermodynamic or bulk probe (ac susceptibility, specific heat, magnetostriction, or the J_c anomaly at several angles). Map the angular cut-off at several fields, e.g., 10, 15 and 20 T, to show that it tracks the SC2 boundary rather than the J_c non-monotonicity.

**B. Should address**

5. **Retention is asserted, not shown, and its description is self-contradictory.**
   - "Persists over … at least several hours" appears with no data.
   - The abstract and Methods call the state metastable and "long-lived transient", while p. 7 says it "does not appear to be transiently metastable".
   - Show ΔJ against waiting time at zero current from seconds to hours, at several temperatures (e.g., 50, 200, 400 mK). Fit to logarithmic or creep-type relaxation; this would also give the energy barrier underlying the ~0.5 K scale.
   - Test whether small field sweeps or brief warming erase the state.

6. **Quantification, resolution and internal consistency of ΔJ.**
   - *Missing information.* ΔJ is "the change in J at fixed V", but the reference curve, the voltage level used, the noise floor and the repeatability are not specified. ED Fig. 1 and Fig. 4e,f have no error bars.
   - *Inconsistent magnitudes.* For S2 at 14 T and 50 mK:
     - ED Fig. 1a shows a saturated ΔJ ≈ 0.55 A cm⁻² at V = 1 µV;
     - the Fig. 3b heatmap for nominally the same amplitude tuning tops out at ≈0.25 A cm⁻²;
     - the Fig. 2 heatmaps at 9 T extend to 0.8;
     - the text says ΔJ peaks "just above 0.2 A cm⁻² at around 10 T".
   - *Switching statistics.* Fig. 1d shows five cycles. Report switching probability and error rate over many cycles, plus repeat-to-repeat scatter, since these are central to the memory claim.

7. **Sample dependence and the role of disorder.**
   - Memory is shown for S1 (RRR 105) and S2 (RRR 44). S3 (RRR 395) is used only for ac ρ(B) in Fig. 4h.
   - A vortex-pinning-based effect should depend strongly on disorder, while an order-parameter-intrinsic one should be robust.
   - Report the memory protocol on S3 or another clean crystal, together with sample dimensions and contact layouts. Discuss how the memory field window scales with RRR (it shifts between S1 and S2, per the ED Fig. 9 caption).

8. **The RSJ model is phenomenological and does not support the specific mechanism.**
   - *Built-in result.* The two-minimum potential (Eq. 1, with g, β = 40, the 0.6 factor and δ = 20) and the enhanced damping at φ = π are chosen to produce the result. Nothing in the model requires two order parameters, so the statement that coexistence "is crucial for realising a non-trivial minimum" is not demonstrated.
   - *Binary versus graded response.* The model gives binary switching, whereas the data show graded, saturating ΔJ against amplitude, duration and α, plus temperature dependence. Show whether the model, with noise strength as a temperature proxy, reproduces these trends.
   - *Physical meaning of φ.* The meaning of a single phase φ "across" a mm-scale bulk crystal needs justification.
   - *Symmetry argument.* The argument that inversion symmetry forces the second minimum to φ = π needs justification.
   - *Inconsistencies.*
     - ED Fig. 2a's γ colour bar spans 0.3–0.6, but Methods sets γ₀ = γ_π = 1.
     - Methods says γ is treated "as a proxy to the injected current J", which is unclear.
     - Methods contains the typo "global minimum at π = 0".
   - *Pinning language.* "Higher disorder implies stronger pinning forces between vortices" conflates vortex–vortex interactions with vortex–defect pinning; in collective pinning, disorder increases J_c by better accommodation to pinning centres. Similarly, larger damping is not the same as a larger J_c in the overdamped model.

9. **Technological claims are overstated.**
   - Writing requires driving the sample into flux flow, which is dissipative, so it is not accurate that "both read and write operations can be performed while superconducting". In the Fig. 1d read scheme one state is resistive.
   - Operation requires 7–20 T and T ≲ 0.4 K. This should be stated alongside the AI-energy framing.
   - Ref. 59 (intrinsic carrier density in Si) does not support the comparison with silicon elements.
   - The pW scaling estimate assumes that the vortex physics survives at µm scale.
   - The analogy to the superconducting diode effect (Fig. 1d caption; p. 9) is misleading, since no nonreciprocity is shown. Its use as evidence for an "intrinsic" mechanism, based on temperature trends, is not a valid inference.

**C. Minor**

10. Axis, unit and label errors:
    - ED Fig. 1a x-axis is labelled "J_ex (ms)".
    - ED Fig. 5b gives α in A cm⁻² s⁻¹, whereas ms⁻¹ is used elsewhere.
    - Fig. 3b uses "J_ext".
11. The Fig. 1b caption describes the erase as starting from a large negative current, whereas Fig. 2 uses positive. Please state the polarity convention for each protocol.
12. "Minimise entropy and energetic costs" should read "minimise free energy".
13. Refs. 26 and 57 are duplicates.
14. Give sample dimensions and cross-sections used to convert I to J, and the I–J conversion factor (40 mA ↔ ? A cm⁻²).
15. Supplementary Information (Figs. S2, S7, heating analysis) should be provided for review. Key heating data belong in Extended Data.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Run a compact set of discriminating controls:
   - heater pulse with no transport current;
   - FC versus ZFC initial states;
   - ac-field or ac-current shaking as the erase;
   - contact-geometry variation.

   Together these would separate an intrinsic two-order-parameter mechanism from peak-effect-type metastability and from thermal quenching.
2. Probe the two states non-destructively and independently of dc J_c, for example via the Campbell penetration depth or ac susceptibility, voltage-noise spectra, or (if feasible) SANS or local imaging. This would show that the vortex configurations really differ as proposed.
3. Map ΔJ on a finer B–θ–T grid on each sample, overlaid with same-sample thermodynamic SC1/SC2 boundaries and the J_c(B) non-monotonicity, to show which feature the memory region tracks.
4. A FIB-patterned microdevice with on-chip thermometry would test the scaling claims, reduce contact heating, and allow cleaner waveform control in a single experiment.
