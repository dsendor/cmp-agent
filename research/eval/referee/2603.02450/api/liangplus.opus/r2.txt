Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

The manuscript reports that bulk UTe₂ crystals, with B near the b-axis and fields roughly 7–20 T at T ≲ 0.6 K, show a history-dependent critical current. An abrupt turn-off of a large dc current leaves the sample in a higher-J_c state. A smooth ramp-down restores the lower-J_c state. The authors attribute this to a quenched, disordered configuration of two competing vortex species associated with SC1/SC2 coexistence.

If the effect is tied to the multiphase order parameter, it would be a new probe of the unusual vortex matter of UTe₂, and potentially a new kind of switchable element. However, current-driven metastable disordered vortex states with enhanced J_c and electrical "write/erase" behaviour are well established in conventional superconductors. The manuscript does not engage with that literature. The link to SC1/SC2 coexistence, rather than to generic vortex-matter metastability, is not yet demonstrated.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- The effect is reproducible across two samples (S1, S2) and is mapped systematically in amplitude, duration and turn-off rate, with saturation behaviour (Fig. 2, ED Fig. 1).
- The parameter-space mapping is extensive: field (ED Fig. 8), angle (Fig. 4d,f, ED Figs. 7, 10) and temperature (Fig. 3, ED Figs. 5, 6). The effect is absent at low field, absent in SC2 at 29 T, and absent beyond θ ≈ 20°.
- The cyclic write/read/erase demonstration (Fig. 1d) is a clear phenomenological illustration of bistability.
- The effect occurs in a material of intense current interest. Electrically resolved vortex-matter metastability near a superconducting phase boundary could provide a valuable transport handle on SC1/SC2 physics.
- The measurement code is supplied.

**3. Concerns**

**A. Must address**

1. **No engagement with the established literature on current-driven metastable vortex states; novelty and mechanism are overstated.**
   - History-dependent, electrically writable and erasable high-J_c states are a well-documented phenomenon in vortex matter, most prominently near the peak effect in 2H-NbSe₂ and CeRu₂. Examples include:
     - Henderson, Andrei, Higgins & Bhattacharya (PRL 1996), on metastability of a driven flux-line lattice;
     - Xiao, Andrei & Higgins (PRL 1999), on current-driven switching between ordered and disordered vortex phases;
     - Paltiel et al., *Nature* 403, 398 (2000), "Dynamic instabilities and memory effects in vortex matter";
     - work by Banerjee, Grover, Ravikumar and others on supercooled/superheated disordered vortex phases.
   - In those systems:
     - a disordered vortex state with higher J_c is quenched in by current pulses or field-cooling;
     - it persists for hours at low T;
     - it is annealed by a slowly ramped current;
     - the response depends on pulse amplitude, duration and ramp rate.
   - This matches Figs. 1–2 closely. Statements in the abstract and introduction that electrically controllable superconducting memory "has remained elusive", and that the effect is "rooted in the superconducting order itself", must be reassessed against this body of work.
   - The authors should explain what in their data distinguishes the UTe₂ effect from a generic order–disorder metastability of a single vortex species. This includes the edge-contamination mechanism of Paltiel et al., in which disordered vortices injected at sample edges through surface barriers are carried into the bulk.
   - Discriminating tests:
     - field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled J_c, i.e. whether thermal quenching alone produces the high-J_c state;
     - dependence on sample geometry and contact layout, i.e. edge versus bulk;
     - ac "shaking" annealing;
     - comparison of the memory-onset line with any peak-effect or J_c non-monotonicity in SC1 away from the SC2 boundary.

2. **The correspondence between the memory region and SC1/SC2 coexistence is not established, and some data argue against it.**
   - The introduction identifies SC1.5 with the 15–20 T low-J_c region of ref. 46. In S2, however, the memory appears at ~7 T, peaks at ~10 T and is essentially gone by 15–16 T (Fig. 4e; ED Fig. 8, 16–20 T panels). This is below the claimed boundary.
   - The text's statement that the effect persists "until the phase boundary out of SC1 into SC2 is crossed", "just below 20 T" for S2, is not what ED Fig. 8 shows.
   - In S1 the effect is centred near 20 T (Figs. 1b, 3a). The field windows in S1 and S2 therefore differ by roughly a factor of two. This is attributed to crystal quality, with no independent determination of the SC1/SC2 boundary in either crystal. RRR = 105 for S1 and 44 for S2.
   - Fig. 4e shows essentially the same field window at θ = 0° and 15°. The SC1→SC2 boundary is expected to move with θ, so this does not support "tracking" of the boundary. The angular argument rests on a single field (15 T, Fig. 4f) and a single equilibrium V(θ) trace (Fig. 4g).
   - At 29 T and θ ≳ 20° (ED Fig. 10), J_c is nearly zero and V(J) is close to ohmic. Absence of memory there says little.
   - Requested:
     - a thermodynamic or susceptibility determination of the SC1/SC2 boundary on S1 and S2 themselves (specific heat, ac susceptibility, magnetisation);
     - a plot of memory onset/offset fields versus θ against that boundary at several fields;
     - replacing the schematic phase diagrams (Figs. 1a, 4a) with data points.
   - Fig. 1a currently colours the memory region up to ~1 K, whereas the data show it gone by 0.6 K.

3. **Joule heating, and specifically a thermal-quench mechanism, is not excluded. The relaxation-rate argument is consistent with heating rather than against it.**
   - The pulses reach 20 A cm⁻². From Methods, 40 mA corresponds to ~20 A cm⁻², so the cross-section is ~0.2 mm². They are passed through spot-welded contacts in a dilution refrigerator at 50 mK.
   - Contact resistances of even 0.1–1 Ω give I²R ≈ 0.2–2 mW at 40 mA. This is far above typical mixing-chamber cooling power. A mixing-chamber thermometer (presumably what the SI reports) cannot detect millisecond transients at the sample.
   - In a heat-then-quench scenario, faster turn-off means faster cooling and therefore more frozen-in disorder. The observed dependence on α (Fig. 2c, ED Fig. 1c) is exactly what thermal quenching would predict. The total-energy argument on pp. 12–13 therefore does not discriminate.
   - The argument that heating would appear everywhere fails if the consequence of heating, a metastable vortex state, exists only where vortex matter is metastable.
   - The low-T enhancement is also expected for thermally annealable glassy states.
   - Requested:
     - report contact resistances and dissipated power during pump, probe and erase;
     - provide a time-resolved estimate of sample temperature, e.g. a thermometer bonded to the sample, or the sample's own flux-flow V at fixed J used as a thermometer immediately after the pulse;
     - estimate the thermal time constants;
     - most decisively, perform a control in which a heater pulse with no transport current through the sample, of comparable energy and fall time, is applied. Show whether it produces the high-J_c state. Combine this with FC/ZFC comparisons.
   - If heat pulses reproduce the effect, the mechanism and the "electrically controllable" framing change substantially.

4. **The "abrupt" versus "smooth" protocols, the core of the effect, are not adequately specified, and the actual waveforms are unverified.**
   - The text states that all excitations are dc, but Methods states that the Keithley 6221/2182A pulse-delta mode was used for sweeps, the erase and the cycling in Fig. 1d. In pulse-delta mode each data point is a short current pulse, alternating with a low level.
   - If the low level is zero, every "smooth" sweep consists of many abrupt drops to zero. The distinction between pump and erase is then not abrupt versus smooth turn-off, but something else, such as the envelope of successive amplitudes.
   - In Fig. 1d,e the erase is drawn as a moderate-amplitude rectangular block followed by an abrupt step to the probe level. This conflicts with the statement that erasure requires smooth ramp-down.
   - The duration study extends to 10 µs, and α up to 500 A cm⁻² ms⁻¹ implies a 40 µs fall. These are comparable to the output settling of the 6221 and to the time resolution of a 100-point arbitrary waveform. Lead inductance and capacitance may also distort the current at the sample.
   - Requested:
     - pulse widths, low levels, duty cycles and inter-pulse intervals for every protocol;
     - oscilloscope traces (e.g. a series resistor) of the actual current for the shortest pulses and fastest ramps;
     - a precise statement of which feature of the waveform writes and which erases.

5. **The memory claim, including "at least several hours" and "non-volatile", is asserted but not shown, and read-disturb and endurance are not characterised.**
   - No retention data appear. The longest time series is ~400 s (Fig. 1d).
   - Wording is inconsistent: the abstract and Methods say "metastable" or "long-lived transient", while p. 7 says "does not appear to be transiently metastable".
   - Requested:
     - V(t) at a fixed probe J, or repeated V(J) curves, over hours, with the time-dependence of ΔJ (e.g. logarithmic creep);
     - many more write/erase cycles than the five in Fig. 1d, with the distribution of the two read-out levels (the "1" state differs from "0" by only ~0.5 µV);
     - a test of whether repeated probing alters the state. Reading the "0" state requires J above the equilibrium J_c, so a probe ending abruptly could itself act as a pump.

**B. Should address**

6. **Quantitative inconsistency and lack of uncertainties in ΔJ.**
   - For the same sample, field and temperature (S2, 14 T, 50 mK), the saturated ΔJ is:
     - ≈0.55 A cm⁻² (amplitude, ED Fig. 1a);
     - ≈0.29 (duration, ED Fig. 1b);
     - ≈0.2 (relaxation, ED Fig. 1c);
     - ≈0.25 (Fig. 3b);
     - ~0.05–0.1 near 14–15 T in Fig. 4e.
   - The text quotes a peak "just above 0.2 A cm⁻²" near 10 T.
   - Whether these differences reflect different V thresholds, J_ex values, cooldowns or run-to-run scatter is not stated. No error bars are shown in any ΔJ plot.
   - Requested:
     - a uniform ΔJ definition (threshold V, reference curve);
     - repeated trials at selected points with standard deviations;
     - the field-ramp history before each dataset.
   - That different tuning knobs saturate at different ΔJ is itself informative. It suggests a continuum of states rather than the two minima of the model, and deserves discussion.

7. **The RSJ model is underdetermined and is not tested against the data's distinctive features.**
   - The potential in Eq. (1) has several hand-chosen parameters: g (whose value is not given), the factor 0.6, β = 40, δ = 20, and γ₀ = γ_π = 1. These are chosen so that the π minimum has the higher J_c and the higher viscosity. Reproducing a hysteretic loop (Fig. 3c, ED Fig. 2) is therefore largely by construction.
   - The statement that γ is treated "as a proxy to the injected current J" when plotting J–V needs explanation.
   - The model is binary: the pump always lands at φ = π. The experiment shows a continuous, saturating dependence of ΔJ on amplitude, duration and α, with an amplitude threshold near 3 A cm⁻² (ED Fig. 1a).
   - The text conflates flux-flow damping, or resistance, with J_c: "larger damping … lower resistance … therefore … higher J_c". In an RSJ model J_c is set by max U′, not by γ. Experimentally, the memory and equilibrium curves converge at high J (Fig. 1b), i.e. they show no obvious change in flux-flow slope.
   - The claim that unbroken inversion symmetry forces the second minimum to φ = π, identified with SC1/SC2 domain walls, needs justification. The mapping of a single phase variable onto a mm-scale bulk vortex ensemble should be argued rather than asserted.
   - The authors should do one of two things:
     - show that the model, without retuning, reproduces the α, Δt, amplitude-threshold and temperature (noise) dependences;
     - or present it explicitly as an illustrative toy model and soften "we confirm".

8. **Temperature dependence and the "intrinsic" claim.**
   - ΔJ is not normalised by J_c(T). A decrease of ΔJ with T may simply track the decrease of J_c. Please show ΔJ/J_c.
   - Warming from 50 mK to 0.6 K at 14 T may also move the sample relative to the SC1/SC2 boundary.
   - The analogy with intrinsic versus extrinsic diode effects (p. 9) is not compelling. Glassy pinning metastability is generically strongest at low T, where thermal activation cannot anneal.
   - "Intrinsic" sits uneasily with the proposed mechanism, which is pinning-dependent, and with the strong sample dependence (S1 vs S2).
   - Please define "intrinsic" precisely. Ideally, add samples spanning RRR, including measuring S3 (RRR 395) with the dc protocol, and different current directions relative to the vortex stripes.
   - J is quoted as nominal for spot-welded contacts on the (001) surface. Report sample dimensions and comment on current-distribution non-uniformity.

9. **Polarity and Bean-type remanence.**
   - ED Fig. 4 is described as "consistent" for both probe polarities, but the effect is not quantified. At α ≤ 3 A cm⁻² ms⁻¹ the signal is in the weak regime of Fig. 2c, and in panel b it is not visually obvious that the fast-α curves have the larger |J_c|.
   - Please give ΔJ for both polarities, using the strong-α regime.
   - A demonstrated symmetric enhancement would argue against a simple directional remanent critical state. This would be a useful argument the authors do not currently make.
   - The Bean-model statement on p. 13 ("depending only on the total energy imparted") mischaracterises the critical-state model and should be revised.

10. **Angular analysis.**
    - Fig. 4f at 15 T shows ΔJ falling gradually from ~0.1 at 0–5° to ~0 at 20°, not "abruptly". The equilibrium J_c itself decreases with θ (ED Fig. 7).
    - Please show ΔJ/J_c versus θ, add angular data at fields other than 15 T, and temper "abruptly".

11. **Application claims omit the dominant costs and constraints.**
    - The ~1 nW figure refers only to reading. Reading the "0" state is resistive (flux flow).
    - The write pulse dissipates roughly µW for ms, and the erase is a 20 s ramp from 40 mA. Contact dissipation is excluded.
    - Operation requires 7–20 T and T < 0.6 K.
    - The scaling to ≲1 pW assumes the same vortex physics in µm-scale devices, where edge effects would dominate.
    - Ref. 59, on intrinsic carrier concentration in silicon, does not support the comparison with silicon memory power.
    - Please give write/erase energy and time and acknowledge these constraints.

**C. Minor**

12. Typos and units:
    - Methods: "global minimum at π = 0" should read φ = 0.
    - ED Fig. 1a: x-axis "J_ex (ms)" should be A cm⁻².
    - ED Fig. 5b: α is in A cm⁻² s⁻¹, but ms⁻¹ elsewhere.
    - Fig. 3b: "J_ext" should be J_ex.
    - p. 9: "timescale- and duration-tuning" should presumably read "duration- and relaxation-tuning".
13. Physics wording:
    - "minimise entropy" (p. 13) should be "free energy".
    - "stronger pinning forces between vortices" conflates vortex–vortex interactions with vortex–defect pinning.
    - There is no direct structural evidence that the equilibrium state is "highly ordered".
14. The Fig. 1b caption describes the erase as a sweep from a large negative value, whereas Methods describes 40 → 0 mA. State the polarity for each dataset.
15. The role of Fig. 4h (S3, ac, 400 mK, resistive magnet) in the argument is unclear, because no memory protocol was applied. Either connect it to the memory region or move it to the SI.
16. The text says Fig. 3 covers 50 mK–1.0 K, but the heatmaps stop at 0.6 K and the 1.0 K trace is from S1 at 20 T. Clarify which sample and field are shown in each panel.
17. Refs. 26 and 57 are duplicates. Check the page numbers in ref. 31.
18. The phrase "akin to supercurrent rectification by the superconducting diode effect" (Fig. 1d caption) is a loose analogy. Consider removing it.
19. Datasets "will be uploaded … prior to publication". Please make them available for review.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. Add a direct comparison with the NbSe₂/CeRu₂ peak-effect metastability literature. Include FC vs ZFC and heater-pulse controls to establish what, if anything, is specific to UTe₂ and to SC1/SC2 coexistence.
2. Determine the SC1/SC2 boundary thermodynamically on the measured crystals and overlay quantitative memory onset/offset points in the B–θ–T space, with uncertainties.
3. Provide verified current waveforms, retention-versus-time data and many-cycle endurance statistics. Together these would turn the phenomenological "memory" into a quantified one.
4. Seek a direct probe of the proposed quenched vortex disorder in the two states, such as broadband flux-flow noise, SANS, or scanning Hall/SQUID on a polished face. Use it to discriminate the two-vortex-species picture from single-species order–disorder metastability.
