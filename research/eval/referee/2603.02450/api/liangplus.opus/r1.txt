Review outline:

## 1. Significance and novelty

The manuscript reports history-dependent critical currents in bulk UTe₂ crystals at 50 mK for B ∥ b̂ between about 7 and 20 T.

- An abrupt drop of a large dc current to zero ("pump") produces a state with Jc raised by about 0.2–0.5 A cm⁻².
- A slow ramp-down ("erase") restores the original state.
- The effect depends on pulse amplitude, duration and turn-off rate, vanishes above about 0.6 K, and disappears in SC2 and for θ ≳ 20°.

The authors attribute this to competing SC1/SC2 vortex species and present it as an intrinsic, electrically controllable superconducting memory. If the link to the multiphase order parameter were established, the result would be interesting both for UTe₂ vortex physics and conceptually for cryogenic memory.

However, the phenomenology closely resembles well-documented current-pulse-switchable metastable vortex states in conventional superconductors. Neither the novelty nor the specific link to SC1/SC2 coexistence is yet demonstrated.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- The switching phenomenology is clear and reproducible within a given run (Fig. 1b,d). It is characterised systematically along three independent pulse parameters, with saturation behaviour (Fig. 2, ED Fig. 1).
- The parameter-space mapping is extensive:
  - field up to 30 T,
  - tilt angle,
  - temperature (Figs. 3–4, ED Figs. 5–10),
  - an explicit negative result in SC2 (ED Fig. 10).
- The turn-off-rate dependence is a useful experimental handle. It shows that the final state depends on the dynamics of the quench, not just the maximum current.
- There is a phenomenological model that reproduces the qualitative shape of the two J–V branches (Fig. 3c, ED Fig. 2).
- The measurement code is to be provided.

## 3. Concerns

### A. Must address

**1. The leading alternative explanation, quench-disordered metastable vortex matter in a single superconducting phase, is not considered, and the novelty claim depends on excluding it.**

- **Prior work.** History-dependent Jc that is switched by current pulses has been studied extensively. Examples include:
  - 2H-NbSe₂ (Henderson et al., PRL 77, 2077 (1996); Xiao, Andrei, Higgins and co-workers; Paltiel et al., Nature 403, 398 (2000));
  - CeRu₂;
  - other peak-effect systems.
- **Matching phenomenology.** In that literature:
  - a disordered vortex state, prepared by field-cooling or by a sudden current change, has a higher Jc;
  - it persists for long times at low T;
  - it is annealed into the ordered low-Jc state by driving above Jc and ramping down slowly;
  - it requires a field window near an order–disorder (peak-effect) crossover;
  - it disappears on warming as thermal relaxation becomes fast.

  Every element of the present data (Figs. 1–3, ED Fig. 1) matches this picture qualitatively, and the authors' own interpretation (p. 13, "glassy vortex state quenched") is essentially this mechanism.
- **Peak-effect signature in the data.** Fig. 4b shows a non-monotonic V(B) at fixed J, with a hump near 7–12 T where the memory sits. This is the textbook transport signature of a peak effect.
- **Why it matters.** The claim that "an experimental demonstration of electrically-controllable memory functionality, exclusively using superconducting elements, has remained elusive" (p. 3) is therefore not accurate. The claim that the effect is "rooted in the superconducting order itself" requires showing what is *not* explained by generic vortex-lattice metastability.
- **Requested actions:**
  - Cite and discuss this literature.
  - Perform the standard discriminating tests:
    - field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled Jc;
    - field-history protocols at fixed current;
    - dependence on field ramp rate.
  - Determine whether the 7–20 T window coincides with the Jc(B) peak or trough on the same sample.
  - State explicitly which observation, if any, requires two order parameters.

**2. The identification of the memory region with SC1/SC2 coexistence ("SC1.5") is not established by the data.**

- **Field range.** The text places the thermodynamic SC1/SC2 boundary near 20 T and the reported coexistence region at 15–20 T (pp. 4–5). Yet in S2 the memory:
  - begins at about 7 T,
  - peaks near 10 T (Fig. 4e, p. 12),
  - has essentially vanished by about 17 T (Fig. 4e, ED Fig. 8).

  Its strongest part therefore lies well inside what the cited thermodynamic data call SC1.
- **Sample-to-sample differences.** S1 shows the memory at 20 T (Fig. 1b), but its full field dependence is not shown. It is therefore unknown whether S1 has a 7–20 T window or a narrow window near 20 T. The text calls the sample difference "slight", but the windows differ substantially.
- **Phase diagrams are borrowed.** The phase diagrams in Figs. 1a and 4a come from ref. 33, which concerns ultraclean crystals. They are not measured on S1 or S2. S2 has RRR = 44, much lower than the crystals in ref. 33.
- **Angular argument.** The argument is made at 15 T, where SC2 is not adjacent even at θ = 0. The coincidence of the 20° cutoff with the angular extent of SC2 at high field therefore does not show that the two are linked at 15 T.
- **Requested actions:**
  - Map the SC1/SC2 boundary and Hc2(θ) on the same samples, for example by ac susceptibility, the resistive Tc(B) kink or specific heat.
  - Show the full B-dependence of ΔJ for S1.
  - Report whether S3 (RRR 395) shows the effect.
  - Explain why an effect attributed to coexistence peaks about 10 T below the phase boundary.

**3. Joule and contact heating is not excluded, and the arguments offered do not exclude it.**

- **Missing numbers.** Pumps reach about 20 A cm⁻², and the erase protocol starts at 40 mA. The paper gives no:
  - sample dimensions,
  - contact resistance for the spot-welded Au leads,
  - dissipated power during the pump.

  At 40 mA, even about 1 Ω of contact resistance gives about 1.6 mW. That is orders of magnitude above typical mixing-chamber cooling power at 50 mK.
- **Why the three arguments on pp. 12–13 fail.**
  - (i) The turn-off-rate dependence is exactly what a *thermal* quench predicts. A heat pulse followed by faster cooling freezes in more vortex disorder, as in field-cooled NbSe₂.
  - (ii) Selectivity in B–θ does not exclude heating as the trigger. It only shows that a metastable state exists in that window, as in the peak-effect scenario.
  - (iii) The disappearance of the memory at higher T concerns the stability of the memory state, not what writes it.
- **Requested actions:**
  - Quote sample dimensions and contact resistances, and estimate the energy per pump.
  - Report on-sample thermometry with sub-ms resolution, or at least a thermal time constant.
  - Perform a control in which a heat pulse from a heater on the sample, with no transport current, is applied and quenched at different rates.
  - Compare pump protocols at equal dissipated energy but different J.
- **Note on the SI.** The Supplementary Information mentioned in the text (temperature versus time) was not available to this reviewer. The thermometer location and time resolution should be stated in Methods. A fridge thermometer cannot resolve millisecond excursions of the sample.

**4. The current waveform actually delivered to the sample, and hence the distinction between "pump", "erase" and "probe", is not defined.**

- **Pulse-delta mode.** Methods states that J–V sweeps and the erase ramp use the Keithley pulse-delta mode. In this mode each data point is a pulse with return to a low level. A nominally "smooth" ramp is therefore a train of abrupt current steps.
- **Erase steps.** The erase ramp is "40 mA to 0 mA with 2 mA steps". Each step is itself an abrupt drop.
- **Probe sweeps.** These go deep into flux flow (V up to 80 µV in Fig. 1b). Under the authors' own picture they should re-pump or erase the state.
- **Filtering.** Dilution-refrigerator lines are normally RC-filtered. The 10 µs pulses (Fig. 2b) and α = 500 A cm⁻² ms⁻¹ ramps may be strongly distorted at the sample.
- **Why it matters.** Without these details the central contrast between abrupt and smooth cannot be interpreted.
- **Requested actions:**
  - Specify pulse width, low level, duty cycle and inter-point delay for every protocol.
  - Show J(t) traces measured at the sample, for example across a calibrated shunt at the probe end.
  - Explain why the probe sweep neither writes nor erases.
  - Characterise the erase threshold as a function of amplitude and ramp rate.

**5. The retention and readout claims central to "memory" and "non-volatile" are unsupported by shown data.**

- **Retention.** "Persists over a timescale of at least several hours" (pp. 5 and 7) is stated without any data.
- **Endurance.** Fig. 1d shows only about five write/erase cycles over 400 s, with no statistics.
- **Readout.** Reading the low-Jc state involves finite voltage, i.e. vortex motion. Whether reading is non-destructive is not shown.
- **Requested actions:**
  - Show retention curves (V at fixed probe J, or ΔJ, versus waiting time) at several temperatures. These would also give the activation barrier relevant to the 0.6 K scale.
  - Show many-cycle endurance statistics with the spread in ΔJ and V.
  - Test repeated-read stability in both states.

### B. Should address

**6. The ΔJ metric is inconsistently defined and lacks uncertainties.**

- The Fig. 2 heatmaps show ΔJ up to about 0.8 A cm⁻² at 9 T, concentrated at V ≲ 1 µV. There, at fixed V, ΔJ is dominated by noise near the V = 0 plateau.
- ED Fig. 1 uses V = 1 µV and gives saturation at about 0.55 A cm⁻² at 14 T.
- Fig. 3b at 14 T peaks near 0.25 A cm⁻².
- Fig. 4e reports a peak of "just above 0.2 A cm⁻²".
- **Requested action:** adopt one voltage criterion above the noise floor. Give error bars from repeated cycles in Figs. 4e,f and ED Fig. 1, and state the voltage noise floor.

**7. The model is constructed to reproduce the result rather than test it.**

- **Ad hoc parameters.** The potential (Eq. 1) contains several, and g is never specified.
- **Built-in outcome.** The damping γ(φ) is chosen to peak at φ = π precisely so that the phase "sticks" there.
- **Unclear mapping.** The physical meaning of a single Josephson phase obeying φ̇ = 2eV/ħ in a mm-sized junction-free crystal is not explained. The statement that γ is treated "as a proxy to the injected current J" is unclear.
- **Symmetry argument.** The statement that inversion symmetry "has to" place the second minimum at φ = π is incorrect. Inversion maps φ → −φ and allows any symmetric pair ±φ₀.
- **Requested actions:**
  - Give all parameters.
  - Test whether the model reproduces:
    - the amplitude threshold and saturation (ED Fig. 1a),
    - the logarithmic duration dependence (ED Fig. 1b),
    - the rate dependence (ED Fig. 1c),
    - the temperature scale, with noise amplitude ∝ T.
  - Check the model's implication of a lower flux-flow resistance in the memory state against the data. In Fig. 1b the branches merge at high J.
  - Otherwise, present the model explicitly as an illustrative cartoon.

**8. The energy-scale argument uses the wrong reference.**

- The memory dies at about 0.6 K, which is compared with the zero-field Tc = 2.1 K (p. 9).
- The relevant comparison is Tc(B, θ) of the given sample at 14 T, which is substantially lower, especially for RRR 44.
- The analogy with the intrinsic diode effect (ref. 49) does not discriminate between mechanisms. Extrinsic metastable vortex states also anneal on warming.
- **Requested action:** quote Tc(B) for S2 at 14 T and S1 at 20 T, and extract a barrier from retention versus T (see concern 5).

**9. It is untested whether the memory is a bulk effect or a local one.**

- With current along [100] injected from leads on the (001) surface, the current distribution is non-uniform. The memory could reside in regions near the contacts or edges, consistent with the edge-contamination picture of Paltiel et al.
- **Requested actions:**
  - Compare voltage-contact pairs at different distances from the current leads.
  - Compare different contact geometries.
  - Ideally use a FIB-defined bar, which the authors note is feasible.

**10. The role of disorder is not exploited.**

- The three samples differ strongly in RRR (44, 105 and 395).
- An intrinsic order-parameter mechanism and a pinning-driven metastability make different predictions for how ΔJ and the field window scale with disorder.
- **Requested action:** report ΔJ(B) for all three samples at a common protocol.

**11. The technology claims are overstated.**

- **Field and temperature.** The effect requires 7–20 T and T < 0.6 K. This sits uneasily with the introduction's criticism of competing schemes that "rely on external magnetic field modulations", and with "controlled solely by electrical stimuli".
- **Write and erase energy.** The pumps (about 20 A cm⁻² for ms) and the 20 s, 40 mA erase are not costed. Quoting only the about 1 nW read power is misleading.
- **Scaling.** The scaling to ≲1 pW assumes the effect survives miniaturisation, which is untested.
- **Irrelevant citation.** Ref. 59, on the intrinsic carrier concentration in Si, does not support the power comparison.
- **Prior all-superconducting memories.** Refs. 22–24 already demonstrate electrically operated memories of this kind and should be contrasted fairly.
- **Requested action:** temper the abstract and Outlook accordingly.

### C. Minor

12. **Wording on metastability is internally inconsistent.** The abstract says "metastable state". p. 7 says "does not appear to be transiently metastable". Methods says "long-lived metastable … state" and "long-lived transient (metastable) state".
13. **Text versus figure on the S2 field window.** p. 9 says the S2 window extends to "just below 20 T", but Fig. 4e and ED Fig. 8 show ΔJ vanishing by about 17 T.
14. **Temperature range quoted for Fig. 3.** p. 8 says Fig. 3 covers 50 mK–1.0 K for amplitude tuning. Fig. 3b spans 0.05–0.6 K. The 1.0 K data (Fig. 3a) are from S1 at 20 T with a different protocol.
15. **Units and labels in figures:**
    - ED Fig. 1a x-axis "J_ex (ms)" should be A cm⁻².
    - ED Fig. 5b gives α in A cm⁻² s⁻¹; elsewhere it is A cm⁻² ms⁻¹.
    - Fig. 3b uses "J_ext" while the text uses J_ex.
    - The α ranges differ between Fig. 2c (2–500), ED Fig. 6 (0.04–36) and ED Fig. 7 (20–500). The colour bars and the definition of "J/α" in ED Fig. 1c should be clarified.
16. **Typo in Methods.** "The global minimum at π = 0" should read φ = 0.
17. **Physics wording:**
    - "Minimise entropy" (p. 13) should be "free energy".
    - "Stronger pinning forces between vortices" conflates intervortex interactions with pinning.
    - The Bean critical-state model is rate-independent but not determined by "total energy imparted".
18. **Missing sample information.** Sample dimensions, cross-sections, contact separations and the J-conversion factor should be given for each sample.
19. **Fig. 1b clarity.** The protocol insets are hard to read. Explicit J(t) traces for each panel would help, particularly whether the down-sweep after each red/orange probe was abrupt.
20. **Duplicate reference.** Refs. 26 and 57 are identical (Blatter et al.).
21. **Fig. 4h relevance.** Fig. 4h (S3, ac, 400 mK) is not connected to any memory measurement on S3. Its relevance should be stated or the panel moved.
22. **Missing analysis files.** The simulation code and raw-data processing scripts used for the ΔJ heatmaps should be provided alongside the measurement code.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Vortex-history diagnostics.** Add the standard tests from the metastable-vortex-matter literature:
   - FC versus ZFC Jc;
   - field-ramp history;
   - small ac "shaking" currents to anneal.

   Compare the 7–20 T window directly with Jc(B) (peak effect) on the same sample. Then identify any feature that requires two order parameters.
2. **Same-sample phase determination and decisive heating control.** Pair a same-sample determination of the SC1/SC2 boundary (e.g. ac susceptibility versus B and θ) with a current-free heater-quench control. These two measurements would address concerns 2 and 3 most efficiently.
3. **Retention and endurance.** Present retention-versus-time curves at several temperatures and a long write/erase endurance test. An Arrhenius analysis would give a barrier to compare with SC1/SC2 domain-wall or vortex-pinning energies.
4. **Microstructured devices or local imaging.** Consider FIB-defined microbars, or local probes such as scanning Hall/SQUID microscopy or Campbell-penetration-depth measurements. These would test whether the memory is bulk, reduce heating, and directly probe the claim that miniaturised devices would work.
