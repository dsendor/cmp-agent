# Referee Report: "Discovery of an electrically-controllable superconducting memory effect" (Wu *et al.*, arXiv:2603.02450)

## Summary of claims

The authors report that bulk UTe₂ crystals, with field along *b̂* at 50 mK and in a regime they call "SC1.5", show a history-dependent critical current. Abrupt current turn-offs ("pump") raise J_c into a long-lived state. Slow ramps down ("erase") restore the lower J_c. The size of the effect depends on pulse amplitude, duration and turn-off rate, and it vanishes above about 0.6 K. The authors attribute it to competition between two vortex species and model it with an RSJ-type double-well potential.

The data are interesting and the protocol dependence is carefully documented. However, I have serious concerns in five areas:

- novelty relative to the large literature on metastable vortex states;
- whether the memory region really coincides with SC1/SC2 coexistence;
- the measurement methodology, especially pulse-delta mode and pulse fidelity;
- the strength of the arguments excluding heating;
- the physical content of the model.

The technological claims are substantially overstated. Concerns are numbered below, grouped by theme.

---

## A. Novelty and relation to prior work

1. **Prior art on current-driven metastable vortex states is essentially omitted.** Current-switchable metastable vortex states with enhanced J_c and long-lived memory are well established in NbSe₂, CeRu₂, 2H-NbSe₂ near the peak effect, and other low-pinning crystals. Examples include Henderson, Andrei, Higgins & Bhattacharya (PRL 1996), Xiao, Andrei & Higgins (PRL 1999), and Paltiel *et al.* (*Nature* 2000, titled "Dynamic instabilities and memory effects in vortex matter"). In those systems:
   - a disordered state with higher J_c is quenched in by rapid current or field changes;
   - it is annealed into an ordered low-J_c state by slow driving;
   - it persists indefinitely at low T.

   That phenomenology is almost exactly what is reported here, yet none of these works is cited or discussed.

2. **The headline claims need re-evaluation in light of point 1.** These are "Discovery", "novel electrically-controllable superconducting memory effect", and "has remained elusive". The paper must explain what is qualitatively new beyond observing a known class of vortex-matter metastability in a new material.

3. **The peak-effect / order–disorder framework is never discussed.** In that framework, a single vortex species shows ordered (weakly pinned) and disordered (strongly pinned) phases with supercooling and superheating. This is the simplest alternative to the "two vortex species" proposal and must be addressed explicitly.

4. **The edge-contamination mechanism of Paltiel *et al.*** is not considered. Here, a disordered vortex phase injected at sample edges is transported into the bulk by current. It naturally produces dependence on pulse amplitude, duration and rate, and it is strongly geometry- and contact-dependent.

5. **Prior electrically operated vortex memories are dismissed too quickly.** Golod & Krasnov (refs. 23, 24) are electrically operated superconducting vortex memories. Stating that electrical control "exclusively using superconducting elements has remained elusive" mischaracterises them.

6. **The historical claim in the opening is questionable.** It states that efforts toward superconducting computers "yielded" STM and high-T_c superconductivity. Neither ref. 1 nor ref. 2 supports this causal claim. It should be removed or properly sourced.

7. **The AI-energy motivation is disproportionate.** The opening paragraph on AI energy use does not connect to a device requiring 7–20 T and 50 mK. It reads as rhetorical and should be shortened.

## B. Identification of the "memory region" with SC1/SC2 coexistence

8. **The memory region starts far below the cited coexistence regime.** ED Figs. 8 and 4e show memory onset at ≈7 T, with ΔJ peaking near 10 T for S2. The SC1.5 coexistence regime cited (refs. 10, 46) is ≈15–20 T, and the bulk thermodynamic SC1→SC2 transition (ref. 9) is ≈20 T. For S2 the effect occurs predominantly well inside what is conventionally SC1, so calling it SC1.5 is not justified by independent evidence.

9. **The memory is absent where coexistence is supposed to be strongest.** ED Fig. 8 shows essentially no memory at 16–20 T for S2, which is where coexistence should be most pronounced. This undercuts the central mechanistic narrative.

10. **The argument is circular.** The memory region is defined by nonzero ΔJ (Fig. 4a caption: "identified by nonzero ΔJ") and then labelled SC1.5. The paper then argues that the effect is tied to SC1.5. An independent marker of coexistence (thermodynamic, magnetic or spectroscopic) is needed at 7–15 T.

11. **The phase diagrams are schematic, not measured.** Fig. 1a and Fig. 4a are labelled schematic. The purple region in Fig. 1a appears to extend to roughly 1 K, inconsistent with hysteresis closing by about 0.6 K (Fig. 3). Please replace them with diagrams built from actual measured ΔJ(B, T, θ) points for each sample.

12. **The sample dependence is explained inconsistently.** S1 shows memory near 20 T; S2 shows it at 7–15 T. The authors attribute this to crystalline quality, but S1 (RRR 105) and S2 (RRR 44) differ in the direction opposite to what would be expected if the SC2 boundary were simply shifting. A full ΔJ(B) map for S1 is needed.

13. **The cleanest sample was not tested for memory.** S3 (RRR 395) was used only for ac transport (Fig. 4h). Testing memory in the cleanest sample is essential to distinguish intrinsic order-parameter physics from disorder-dependent pinning physics.

14. **Sample quality is described inconsistently.** The text calls the crystals "high quality salt flux-grown", but S2 has RRR 44, which is modest for MSF-grown UTe₂ (state-of-the-art is several hundred or more). The main memory dataset comes from S2.

15. **The angular argument is correlation only.** Memory vanishes near θ ≈ 20°, where SC2 ceases (Fig. 4f). But at 15 T the equilibrium J_c, pinning landscape and flux-flow behaviour all change with angle. Please show ΔJ/J_c rather than absolute ΔJ, and show that J_c itself does not change in a way that trivially suppresses the hysteresis.

16. **Fig. 4g shows a slope change, not a jump.** The data look like a change of slope in V(θ), with sparse points and a dashed guide, rather than the "sudden jump" claimed. Denser angular data and uncertainties are needed.

17. **Only the *b̂*→*ĉ* rotation was studied.** The SC2 angular extent is very different for *b̂*→*â* rotation. Testing whether the memory region follows SC2 in that plane would be a much stronger discriminating test.

18. **The 29 T rotation test (ED Fig. 10) partly probes the normal state.** At 27–31°, V(J) is linear through zero. "No memory in SC2 at any θ" is therefore partly a statement about the normal state, and the caption should be qualified.

19. **Bulk and susceptibility evidence on coexistence conflict.** Bulk specific heat (ref. 9) shows a reasonably sharp transition, while ref. 10 suggests coexistence. If SC1→SC2 is first order, the natural explanation would be current-driven nucleation of metastable domains. Field-sweep hysteresis of J_c (up versus down) should be shown to test this.

20. **Vortex structures cited from surface probes were measured in different geometries.** The vortex doublets and stripes (refs. 41–44) were obtained by STM or SQUID with fields generally perpendicular to the cleaved surface, not along *b̂*, and below 8 T. Their relevance to bulk vortices with B ∥ *b̂* at 7–20 T is not established.

21. **The coherence-length argument is questionable.** The claim that the higher B_c2 of SC2 "implies a shorter coherence length" assumes orbital limiting. For B ∥ *b̂* in UTe₂, the field-reinforced superconductivity is not straightforwardly orbital.

## C. Measurement methodology

22. **Pulse-delta mode contradicts the "dc" description.** On the Keithley 6221/2182A, pulse-delta mode applies discrete current pulses that return to a low level (typically zero) between pulses. The paper states that "all excitations were applied as direct currents".

23. **More importantly, every pulse is itself an abrupt turn-off.** The authors' own mechanism says abrupt turn-off from above J_c quenches the memory state. Yet "smooth" pulse-delta sweeps through flux flow reportedly produce no hysteresis. This apparent contradiction is central and must be resolved. Please report:
    - pulse width;
    - pulse low level;
    - duty cycle;
    - the effective turn-off rate of each pulse.

24. **The erase protocol is a staircase, not a ramp.** Methods describe it as 40 mA → 0 in 2 mA steps over 20 s. The distinction between "smooth" and "abrupt" then reduces to step size and dwell time. Please characterise ΔJ versus step size to define the threshold quantitatively.

25. **Pulse fidelity at the sample is not shown.** Durations as short as 10 µs (Fig. 2b) and rates up to 500 A cm⁻² ms⁻¹ (20 A cm⁻² decaying in ~40 µs) are claimed, using a 100-point arbitrary waveform. These pass through dilution-refrigerator wiring, which normally includes RC/π filtering and has significant capacitance. The actual current waveform at the sample must be measured (e.g., with a calibrated shunt or pick-up). Otherwise the duration and rate axes of Fig. 2 and ED Fig. 1 may be instrument- or wiring-limited.

26. **Source specifications need to be reported.** These are the 6221 output rise/fall time, its update rate in ARB mode, and the compliance settings for the relevant range and load.

27. **Sample geometry is missing.** Cross-sections, voltage-contact separation and dimensions are not given. Converting currents in mA to J in A cm⁻² cannot be checked. Inferring from 40 mA ↔ ~20 A cm⁻² gives a cross-section of ~0.2 mm²; please confirm.

28. **Contact resistances are not reported.** For spot-welded gold contacts at 50 mK they can be ≳1 Ω. At 40 mA this would dissipate ≳1 mW, far exceeding typical dilution-refrigerator cooling power at 50 mK.

29. **Current distribution is likely inhomogeneous.** Current is injected through top-surface (001) contacts into a mm-thick bulk crystal. The current distribution, and hence the local J, is probably far from I/A, especially near contacts, where vortex disorder may be injected (see point 4). Please discuss, ideally with a geometry variation or a second contact configuration.

30. **The criterion for J_c is never defined.** No voltage or electric-field criterion is given, and ΔJ is evaluated at different V values in different figures (heat maps versus 1 µV in ED Fig. 1). Please define a standard metric and use it consistently.

31. **The reference curve for ΔJ is unclear.** Is it an equilibrium curve measured immediately before each pump, a single curve for the whole dataset, or something else? Drift of the equilibrium curve between cycles would contaminate ΔJ.

32. **No uncertainties are given.** None of the ΔJ plots (Figs. 2, 3b, 4e,f; ED Figs. 1, 5) has error bars, repeat statistics or a noise floor. The heat maps show striations that may be noise. The number of repeats per condition should be reported.

33. **The readout sweep rate is not controlled for.** Since J_c in weakly pinned vortex systems depends on sweep rate (creep), please state the probe sweep rate and show that the reported ΔJ does not depend on it.

34. **Delay between pump and probe is not varied systematically.** This is essential both for claims of retention and for the physics (relaxation dynamics).

35. **Retention is asserted but not shown.** "Persists over a timescale of at least several hours" is not supported by any figure. Please show V or ΔJ versus delay time, with intermittent reads.

36. **Read-disturb is not addressed.** In the low-J_c state the read current is above J_c (V ≠ 0 in Fig. 1d), i.e., vortices are flowing during the read. Is the state disturbed by repeated reads? How many reads are possible before state loss?

37. **Endurance is not shown.** Fig. 1d shows only about five write/erase cycles. Statistics over many cycles, error rates and the distributions of the high and low states are needed before any memory claim.

38. **Field history and magnet behaviour are unspecified.** Zero-field-cooled versus field-cooled, field sweep direction, persistent-mode drift and magnet remanence may all affect the equilibrium vortex state. Please describe the field history before each dataset.

39. **Thermal environment and thermometry are not described.** Is the sample in vacuum or in the mixture? Where is the thermometer relative to the sample, and what is its time constant?

40. **The supplementary information is missing.** The heating analysis and Fig. S7 are referenced but were not provided. A key part of the argument (heating) therefore cannot be assessed.

41. **Data and simulation code are unavailable.** Data are promised only "prior to publication". Measurement code is said to be supplied, but simulation code is not mentioned.

42. **No control material was measured.** Running the same pulse protocols on a conventional type-II superconductor in the same setup (e.g., Nb, or a weakly pinned crystal such as NbSe₂ at a comparable reduced field) would establish which features are instrument- or protocol-generic and which are specific to UTe₂.

43. **Fig. 4h mixes sample, technique and temperature.** It uses S3, ac lock-in at 400 mK, in a resistive magnet. Its relation to the dc memory data is unclear, and continuous ac excitation may itself anneal vortices. The ac frequency is not given.

## D. Heating and the arguments against artifacts

44. **The relaxation-rate argument does not exclude heating.** If a pulse locally heats the sample (or contact regions), a fast turn-off is a fast thermal quench and a slow turn-off is a slow cool. Thermal quenches of vortex matter are a classic route to disordered high-J_c states. The anticorrelation between memory and deposited energy is therefore fully consistent with a heating-plus-thermal-quench mechanism.

45. **The temperature-dependence argument is a non sequitur.** It is called "perhaps the strongest argument against heating". But a metastable disordered vortex state will generically be erased by thermally activated creep at higher T, regardless of whether heating writes it. Suppression at higher bath T says nothing about whether pulses heat the sample.

46. **The B–θ localisation argument is also weak.** Heating may occur everywhere but only matter where a metastable vortex configuration exists. This does not exclude heating as the writing mechanism.

47. **Dissipation per write should be estimated explicitly.** This includes sample flux-flow dissipation (≈ I·V during the pump) and contact dissipation (≈ I²R_contact). These should be compared with the heat capacity of UTe₂ and its thermal link at 50 mK, giving an estimated transient temperature rise.

48. **Flux-flow voltage during the pump is not reported.** Measuring it would directly quantify in-sample dissipation.

49. **The Bean-model statement is incorrect.** The paper says a Bean or Bean–Livingston process "should be insensitive to how the current is turned off, depending only on the total energy imparted". The Bean critical state is history-dependent, not energy-dependent, and flux creep and surface barriers are inherently rate-dependent. Please correct and expand.

50. **Surface-barrier tests are absent.** Changing the surface condition or geometry would test the Bean–Livingston scenario, which the paper raises but dismisses.

## E. Physical interpretation

51. **There is no direct evidence for two vortex species.** No imaging, magnetisation, noise or spectroscopic data distinguish one disordered species from two competing species. The interpretation should be framed as a hypothesis.

52. **The pinning statement is wrong.** "Higher disorder implies stronger pinning forces between vortices" is incorrect: pinning is between vortices and material defects. In collective pinning theory a disordered lattice adapts better to the pinning landscape. Please correct.

53. **The entropy statement is wrong.** "Vortices arrange themselves in a highly ordered configuration to minimise entropy" is incorrect. The system minimises free energy, and the ordered state has lower entropy as a consequence, not as a goal.

54. **The temperature-scale argument is internally contradictory.** The low temperature scale is said to point to a phenomenon "distinct from the superconductivity itself". Yet the abstract claims the effect is "rooted in the superconducting order itself". Please reconcile.

55. **The wrong T_c is used for comparison.** The ~0.6 K closure is compared with T_c = 2.1 K (zero field). The relevant scale is T_c(B) at 14–20 T for B ∥ *b̂*, which is substantially lower. Using T_c(B) would change the "surprisingly low" argument.

56. **The diode-effect analogy is unsupported.** Intrinsic diode effects strengthen at low T (ref. 49). But the memory here is non-polar (a positive pump affects both current polarities, Fig. 1b and ED Fig. 4), so it lacks the symmetry breaking that defines intrinsic diodes.

57. **The "supercurrent rectification" analogy for Fig. 1d is misleading.** Switching between two J_c values in time is not rectification.

58. **The physical meaning of the saturation behaviour is not discussed.** Key quantitative features of ED Fig. 1 receive no physical interpretation:
    - amplitude onset near 3–4 A cm⁻² and saturation near 8 A cm⁻², compared with J_c ≈ 1.5 A cm⁻²;
    - the roughly logarithmic duration dependence between 10 µs and 1 ms;
    - the ~0.2 ms characteristic time in the relaxation-rate data.

   These are the most informative numbers in the paper and should be compared with vortex relaxation or transit times.

59. **Flux-flow resistance in the two states is not compared.** The memory state alters V(J) well into flux flow (Fig. 1b, up to ~80 µV) before rejoining. The model predicts lower flux-flow resistance (larger γ) in the memory state. Please compare dV/dJ between the states quantitatively; visually the memory branch is steeper, which seems to contradict the model.

60. **The equilibrium V(J) curves show multiple kinks.** This is visible in Fig. 1b and ED Fig. 9. Such structure can indicate sample inhomogeneity, such as cracks, inclusions or multiple current paths. Sample homogeneity should be demonstrated, e.g., with multiple voltage pairs.

61. **The Fig. 4b kink lies inside the memory region, not at its boundary.** The "anomalous non-monotonic kink in V(B)" is a broad hump spanning roughly 7–14 T, contrary to the caption's description of a boundary feature.

62. **No independent probe of the two states is presented.** AC susceptibility, magnetisation, broadband noise or Hall measurements could each test whether the high- and low-J_c states differ in vortex order.

63. **Response to field perturbations is not tested.** Small ac or dc field "shaking" is the standard way to anneal vortex lattices. Its effect is relevant both mechanistically and for device robustness.

64. **The Majorana and topological-protection speculation is not connected to the data.** It is also inconsistent with the paper's own proposed mechanism of a disordered glassy vortex state. It should be removed or clearly marked as speculation.

## F. The RSJ-type model

65. **The model is phenomenological and tuned.** A single global phase variable for a mm-sized bulk crystal in the mixed state, with a double-well potential, has no derivation. The result is built in through several choices:
    - a hand-chosen potential;
    - the coefficient 0.6 and β = 40;
    - a damping γ(φ) sharply peaked (δ = 20) at the metastable minimum.

   As presented, the model demonstrates consistency rather than explanation.

66. **Simulation parameters are incomplete.** The parameter g in Eq. (1) is never specified. Also missing are the noise amplitude, time units, pulse shapes and pulse durations.

67. **Variable roles are confused.** The Methods state "treat γ as a proxy to the injected current J", yet γ is the damping and J is the drive. Please clarify what is actually plotted on the J axis of Fig. 3c and ED Fig. 2b.

68. **Damping is conflated with pinning.** The model text says vortices are "pinned at infinite damping". But in the RSJ analogue γ is a shunt conductance (γ ∼ 1/R). Pinning corresponds to the barriers of U(φ), not to γ.

69. **The inversion-symmetry inference is unsupported.** "As inversion symmetry appears unbroken in the metastable state, this second minimum has to occur at φ = π" is not backed by any measurement. In addition, Δ(r) ∼ e^{iφr/L} with φ = π is itself a finite-momentum state that breaks inversion. The later appeal to cos(qr) states is a different construction. This section needs a coherent formulation.

70. **The model is bistable but the data are graded.** The model gives a binary outcome: the quench always lands at π and the anneal at 0, "regardless of initial condition". The experiment shows continuous, saturating dependence of ΔJ on amplitude, duration and rate (ED Fig. 1). Please show whether the model reproduces these graded dependences and thresholds.

71. **The model is not tested against temperature.** Noise strength naturally plays the role of temperature. The model should predict a closure temperature, which can be compared with Fig. 3b.

72. **The model does not explain why the effect is confined to the "memory region".** The non-trivial minimum is simply assumed. The connection to SC1/SC2 coexistence is asserted rather than derived.

73. **The domain length L is never estimated.** There is no experimental handle on L, so the domain-wall picture cannot currently be falsified.

74. **A more appropriate model class is available.** Langevin vortex dynamics or TDGL simulations with two vortex species, or simply an order–disorder single-species model, would discriminate between hypotheses and connect more directly to the known vortex-matter literature (e.g., the authors' own ref. 58).

75. **Fig. 3c duplicates ED Fig. 2b.** They appear to be the same simulation in different colours; one should be removed.

## G. Technological claims

76. **The "ultralow-power" claim ignores the operating conditions.** The effect requires 7–20 T static fields and T ≲ 0.4 K. The energy and infrastructure costs dominate, and must be acknowledged when comparing with silicon or other cryogenic memories.

77. **Writing is dissipative.** The pump requires J > J_c (resistive flux flow), and reading in the low-J_c state produces a finite voltage. The claim that "both read and write operations can be performed while superconducting" is inaccurate. The paper's own criticism of dissipative magnetic elements in heterostructures applies equally here.

78. **Write energy and speed are not quantified, and the speed is slow.** A rough estimate (~40 mA × ~100 µV × ~1 ms) gives nJ-scale writes per bit, excluding contact losses. ΔJ grows only logarithmically from 10 µs to 1 ms. This is orders of magnitude slower than SFQ logic and undercuts the claim of fast "purely electronic switching".

79. **The scaling argument is unsupported.** It claims ≲1 pW at 1 µm². The readout signal is already only ~0.5 µV in a mm-sized crystal (Fig. 1d), and V scales with contact separation. Signal-to-noise, vortex-number statistics (only thousands of vortices in 1 µm² at 10 T) and finite-size effects on collective pinning are not addressed. The memory effect may not survive miniaturisation.

80. **Ref. 59 does not support the silicon comparison.** It is a paper on intrinsic carrier concentration in silicon, not on the power consumption of memory elements.

81. **The neuromorphic claim lacks evidence.** Claiming "inherent plasticity" for neuromorphic processing would require reproducible, addressable multi-level states with low variance, which are not shown.

82. **"Non-volatile" is used inconsistently.** The state is called "metastable" in the abstract and model, but "does not appear to be transiently metastable" in the text. Persistence also requires maintained field and temperature. Please define terms and avoid "non-volatile" without qualification.

83. **"Controlled solely by electrical stimuli" needs a caveat.** It is used to contrast with field-modulated systems, which is fair. But the abstract and introduction should state clearly that a large static magnetic field is required.

## H. Figures, notation, internal consistency and references

84. **ΔJ magnitudes vary across figures without explanation.** Values include up to 0.8 A cm⁻² (Fig. 2, 9 T), 0.55/0.3/0.2 (ED Fig. 1, 14 T), ~0.25 (Fig. 3b, 14 T) and 0.2–0.4 (Fig. 4e). The text says ΔJ "peaks just above 0.2 A cm⁻²" at ~10 T, while the plotted θ = 0 peak in Fig. 4e appears near 0.3. Please harmonise the definitions and explain the differences.

85. **Fig. 1c and Fig. 4e disagree at 7 T.** Fig. 1c (S2, 7 T) shows a large hysteresis of order 1 A cm⁻², whereas Fig. 4e indicates ΔJ is near zero at the 7 T onset.

86. **There are unit and label errors:**
    - ED Fig. 1a x-axis "J_ex (ms)";
    - ED Fig. 1c "J/α (ms)" does not define which J;
    - ED Fig. 5b gives α in A cm⁻² s⁻¹ but Fig. 2 uses A cm⁻² ms⁻¹;
    - Fig. 3b uses "J_ext" whereas the text uses J_ex.

87. **Parameter ranges are inconsistent across datasets.** For α these are 2–500 (Fig. 2), 0.04–36 (ED Fig. 6) and 20–500 (ED Fig. 7). For Δt the colourbar extends to 20 ms but the heat-map axis stops at ~10 ms. This complicates comparison across fields and temperatures.

88. **The Fig. 4e protocol is not stated.** J_ex is given for Fig. 4f but not for 4e.

89. **Some figures are hard to read.** The Fig. 1b protocol insets are very hard to interpret, and the logic of which operations write, read or erase is confusing. For example, the red probe sweep goes to large J, which might itself anneal, yet the subsequent orange branch still shows memory. A table of the exact sequence for each panel is needed. The 3D waterfall heat maps in Fig. 3b and ED Fig. 5 obscure quantitative comparison; side-by-side 2D maps or line cuts would be better.

90. **There are typos and reference errors:**
    - Methods: "The global minimum at π = 0" should read φ = 0.
    - Refs. 26 and 57 are the same paper (Blatter *et al.*, RMP 1994), cited twice.
    - Ref. 31's page/article number appears garbled.
    - Ref. 48 is a general review, not a source for a "nonsingular vortex state" in UTe₂.

---

## Overall recommendation

The observation of protocol-dependent, long-lived J_c switching in UTe₂ near the SC1/SC2 regime is potentially interesting. However, I cannot recommend publication in its present form. Before the claims of a "discovery" of an "intrinsic" effect "rooted in the superconducting order" can be supported, the authors need to:

- position the work against the extensive literature on metastable vortex states and memory in vortex matter;
- resolve the pulse-delta and pulse-fidelity issues;
- provide quantitative heating analysis and control experiments;
- establish independently that the 7–15 T regime involves SC1/SC2 coexistence;
- substantially temper the technological claims.
