Referee report

Manuscript: "Discovery of an electrically-controllable superconducting memory effect" (arXiv:2603.02450v1), Wu et al.

1. Summary of the submission

The authors measure dc current-voltage characteristics of bulk, flux-grown UTe2 single crystals (three samples; RRR 105, 44 and 395) at dilution-refrigerator temperatures, in magnetic fields up to 30 T applied along or near the b axis. Over a range of fields (roughly 7 T up to the SC1/SC2 boundary near 20 T for B || b, and out to about 20 degrees of tilt towards c), they find that the V(J) curve depends on how the current was last brought to zero. An abrupt drop from a large current ("pump") leaves a state with a higher critical current. A slow ramp down ("erase") brings back the lower-Jc curve. The size of the shift, Delta J (typically 0.1 to 0.3 A cm^-2 against a Jc of order 1 to 2 A cm^-2), grows with pulse amplitude, pulse duration and turn-off rate, and saturates at large values of each. It vanishes above about 0.6 K, and it is absent at 30 T (SC2) and at low field. The authors say the high-Jc state lasts for at least several hours. They attribute the effect to two competing vortex species from SC1 and SC2 being quenched into a disordered, strongly pinned configuration, and they support this with a phenomenological RSJ-type model whose potential has a second, metastable minimum at phi = pi. They then argue for applications in low-power cryogenic memory, neuromorphic computing and topological quantum computing.

The data are interesting and look carefully taken. Systematic maps of history-dependent Jc against pulse amplitude, duration, turn-off rate, field, angle and temperature in a multiphase spin-triplet candidate are worth publishing in some form. In its current form, though, the manuscript does not support its three headline claims: (i) that this is a new class of superconducting memory and the first electrically controlled one, (ii) that the effect is intrinsic and rooted in the SC1/SC2 order parameters, and (iii) that it is technologically relevant. The phenomenology closely resembles metastable vortex-matter states that have been known for over 25 years and are not cited. The field range of the effect does not line up well with the coexistence region it is attributed to. Several simpler explanations (thermal quench, surface or edge contamination, ordinary thermally activated relaxation of a disordered vortex state) have not been excluded. Details follow.

2. Major issues

2.1 Missing prior work on metastable vortex states and history-dependent Jc; the novelty claims are overstated.

The central observation is that a vortex system driven above Jc and then quenched quickly freezes into a disordered state with higher Jc, while slow ramping anneals it into a more ordered, lower-Jc state. This is a well-established part of vortex physics. In 2H-NbSe2, CeRu2 and related low-pinning crystals near the peak effect, work from the mid-1990s onward showed all of the following:
- metastable disordered vortex states with enhanced Jc;
- switching between them by current pulses, with dependence on pulse amplitude, duration and ramp rate;
- long-lived memory of the last drive;
- annealing back to the ordered state by slow drives or ac shaking;
- an "edge contamination" mechanism in which disordered vortices enter through surface barriers and are carried into the bulk.
Representative papers include Henderson, Andrei, Higgins and Bhattacharya (PRL 1996), Xiao, Andrei and Higgins (PRL 1999), Paltiel et al., "Dynamic instabilities and memory effects in vortex matter" (Nature 2000), Banerjee et al. on history dependence in the peak-effect regime, and Valenzuela and Bekeris on ac-induced ordering. None of these is cited. Field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled history effects in the same systems are also directly relevant.

The manuscript has to place its results against this literature and state clearly what is qualitatively new in UTe2. It also needs to drop or substantially soften these statements:
- "an experimental demonstration of electrically-controllable memory functionality, exclusively using superconducting elements, has remained elusive". The paper's own refs. 23 and 24 (Golod and Krasnov) describe electrically operated all-superconducting vortex memories, and Josephson and kinetic-inductance memories exist as well.
- "Discovery" and "a new class of superconducting memory elements" in the title and abstract.
- "intrinsic ... rooted in the superconducting order itself" (see 2.2 and 2.3).

2.2 The field and angle range of the effect does not establish a link to SC1/SC2 coexistence.

The interpretation rests on the effect occurring where SC1 and SC2 coexist ("SC1.5"). The data shown do not support this well.
(a) For B || b the effect starts at about 7 T (Fig. 1c shows a clear effect at 7 T on S2). In Fig. 4e, Delta J peaks at about 10 T and falls towards zero as the SC1/SC2 boundary near 18 to 20 T is approached. The coexistence region in the cited literature (refs. 10 and 46) sits at roughly 15 to 20 T and coincides with the low-Jc anomaly. In Fig. 4b that anomaly appears as the V(B) peak near 20 T, above the dashed "memory region" dome at about 7 to 15 T, not inside it. Bulk thermodynamics (ref. 9) puts the sample in pure SC1 at 7 to 12 T. The authors need to either (i) give independent evidence that SC2 domains or SC2-type vortices exist down to 7 T at 50 mK, or (ii) relabel the purple region in Figs. 1a and 4a as an empirical "memory region" rather than "SC1.5" or a coexistence phase. As drawn, those panels look like measured phase diagrams but are schematics.
(b) The angle argument (the effect vanishes beyond about 20 degrees, where SC2 disappears) does not discriminate. Tilting B from b towards c also lowers the SC1 upper critical field and changes the reduced field B/Bc2 and the vortex-lattice anisotropy. The authors should show where (15 T, 20 to 27 degrees) lies relative to Bc2 of SC1 at 50 mK for these samples. The sharp jump in equilibrium V(theta) at about 20 degrees (Fig. 4g) could just as well be the approach to the SC1 resistive transition or to a peak-effect regime.
(c) The decisive control is missing. Is any memory effect seen for B || a or B || c, where only SC1 exists, at comparable reduced fields B/Bc2 and comparable Jc? If a similar effect appears there, the multiphase mechanism is not needed. If it is absent at matched B/Bc2, the case for an SC1/SC2 origin becomes much stronger. Similarly, the absence of memory at 29 to 30 T (SC2) should be tested at comparable reduced fields within SC2, including closer to its own Bc2.
(d) Please reconcile Fig. 4e and Fig. 4f. At 15 degrees, Fig. 4e appears to show Delta J = 0 from about 13 T upwards. At 15 T, Fig. 4f appears to show a finite Delta J near 15 degrees. If the difference comes from a different J_ex or a different V criterion, say so, and use one consistent definition of Delta J across the maps.
(e) Sample dependence is mentioned but not shown. S1 (RRR 105) and S2 (RRR 44) are used for the memory data. The cleanest crystal, S3 (RRR 395), appears only in the ac measurement of Fig. 4h. Was the memory effect measured on S3, and if so, how does Delta J/Jc scale with RRR? The Extended Data Fig. 9 caption attributes the sample-to-sample shift of the memory region to "crystalline quality (and hence vortex pinning forces)". That points to a pinning-controlled effect rather than one fixed by the SC1/SC2 boundary. Also, RRR 44 is low for the salt-flux crystals described as "high quality ... ultraclean"; please comment.

2.3 Heating, and a thermally driven quench, are not excluded.

The heating discussion relies on the Supplementary Information, which is not included in this submission, so I cannot assess the thermometry. The arguments in the main text are not convincing.
(a) The turn-off-rate argument cuts the other way. If a pulse at 20 A cm^-2 in the flux-flow state warms the sample or its contacts above some local order-disorder or depinning scale, a fast turn-off is a fast thermal quench and would freeze in disorder. A slow ramp lets the vortex system anneal while it cools. That is exactly the observed dependence on alpha, amplitude and duration. The rate dependence therefore does not distinguish a current-driven quench from a thermally driven one. In addition, in Fig. 2c the energy difference between alpha = 2 and alpha = 500 A cm^-2 ms^-1 is set by a ramp-down of up to about 10 ms following a plateau of several ms. That is not a large lever arm on deposited energy.
(b) The statement that a Bean or Bean-Livingston process "should be insensitive to how the current is turned off" is not correct in general. Flux creep, surface-barrier dynamics and the edge-contamination mechanism mentioned above are all rate dependent.
(c) Mixing-chamber or probe thermometry will not see millisecond temperature transients at the sample. Please give: the contact resistances of the spot-welded gold contacts at low temperature and high field; the dissipated power and energy per pump pulse (flux-flow voltage times current, plus contact heating); an estimate of the sample's thermal time constant at 50 mK from its heat capacity and the thermal link through the leads and mount; and, ideally, a direct test such as a thermometer on the sample, or a deliberate heat pulse with no transport current followed by a fast or slow cool, to see whether it also writes the memory state.
(d) "Heating would be present throughout SC1 and SC2" does not follow. A thermal quench only produces a memory effect where the vortex system has metastable disordered states, which is itself field and angle dependent.

2.4 The temperature-dependence argument is flawed.

The authors compare the 0.6 K disappearance of the effect with the zero-field Tc of 2.1 K and infer "some intrinsic low energy phenomenon, distinct from the superconductivity itself". The relevant comparison is with Tc(B) at the field in question (14 T for Fig. 3b, 20 T for Fig. 3a), which for B || b is well below 2.1 K in this field range, and with the temperature dependence of Jc itself. In Fig. 3a (S1, 20 T), the zero-voltage plateau has essentially gone by 650 mK and V(J) is close to ohmic at 1.0 K. The disappearance of hysteresis therefore coincides with the near-disappearance of measurable pinning in the probed current range. Ordinary thermally activated relaxation of a metastable disordered vortex state also naturally gives memory only at low T. Please plot Delta J/Jc(T), give Tc(B) for each sample and field used, and remove or substantially qualify the inference of a separate low-energy scale. The analogy with intrinsic versus extrinsic superconducting diode effects is not relevant to this question and should be dropped.

2.5 The measurement protocol needs clarifying; the probe may itself act as a pump.

The Methods say the data were taken in the Keithley 6221/2182A pulse-delta sweep mode, with the reset also done in pulse-delta mode. In pulse-delta mode every reading is a current pulse switched on and off (to zero or to a low level) on a timescale of tens of microseconds to milliseconds. That is exactly the kind of abrupt current change the paper identifies as a "pump". At the same time, the text says "all excitations were applied as direct currents" and describes probe sweeps as "smooth". Please state precisely:
- the pulse width, the low level, the repetition rate and the step size used for probe sweeps and for the reset;
- why probe readings above Jc do not themselves write the memory state;
- whether the "smooth" erase is really a staircase of pulses.
Also show the current waveform actually delivered to the sample, for the 10 microsecond pulses and the fast turn-off rates in particular. Cryostat wiring in a dilution refrigerator is usually filtered and has significant inductance and capacitance, so the nominal and real waveforms may differ substantially. Finally, describe the magnetic-field and thermal history before each data set (field ramp direction, waiting time, field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled), since it is known to set the initial vortex state. Calling the annealed state "equilibrium" needs justification. A field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled comparison would show whether the low-Jc state is the true equilibrium or one particular metastable state.

2.6 The memory performance claims are not quantified.

"Persists over a timescale of at least several hours" is central to the non-volatility claim, but no data are shown. Please provide:
- V at a fixed probe current against time over hours, for both states;
- the read margin and its distribution (in Fig. 1d the "low" state is about 0.5 microvolts against about 0 for the "high" state, with visible scatter);
- write and erase success rates over many cycles (Fig. 1d shows only a handful);
- read disturb, that is, whether repeated probing near Jc degrades the memory state;
- error bars on Delta J, the voltage noise floor, and how sensitive Delta J is to the 1 microvolt criterion in Extended Data Fig. 1.

2.7 The model is underdetermined and only loosely connected to the physics.

The RSJ-type model puts in by hand a potential with a second minimum at phi = pi (with an ad hoc exponent beta = 40 and coefficient 0.6) and a phase-dependent damping sharply peaked at pi (delta = 20). Given that construction, getting quench-to-pi and anneal-to-0 is close to automatic. The "good qualitative agreement" therefore tests very little. Specific problems:
(a) The meaning of phi is inconsistent. The main text calls it the phase across the sample, obeying the Josephson relation. The Methods call it the Cooper-pair momentum in a time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau description. The main text also writes Delta(r) ~ exp(i phi r/L) for the second minimum, which reads as a finite-momentum state, and then cites a cos(qr) pair-density-wave scenario. Using a single phase variable for a millimetre-sized bulk crystal in the flux-flow regime needs justification.
(b) The model gives a binary outcome. The data show a graded, saturating Delta J against amplitude, duration and turn-off rate. A distribution of domains or barriers would be needed, and with one the model is no longer distinctive.
(c) The model has no temperature, field or angle dependence, so it cannot address the evidence offered for the SC1/SC2 mechanism.
(d) The text links larger damping in the memory state to a "lower resistance in the vortex flow state". That is a testable prediction: the flux-flow slope at high J should differ between the two states. Do the data show it? In Fig. 1c and Fig. 2 the curves appear mainly shifted in J rather than changed in slope.
(e) Treating "gamma as a proxy to the injected current J" in the Methods is unclear and should be explained. The noise amplitude, the integration scheme and the pulse parameters used in the simulations should be given.
(f) The phrase "higher disorder implies stronger pinning forces between vortices" is not correct as written. Pinning is between vortices and material defects; a disordered vortex structure has higher Jc because smaller correlated (Larkin) domains accommodate the pinning landscape better. Likewise, "minimise entropy and energetic costs" should read "minimise the free energy".
I recommend presenting the model explicitly as an illustrative phenomenology and toning down the microscopic claims built on it (domain-wall grids, half-quantum or nonsingular vortices, Majorana modes), which the data do not address.

2.8 The technological framing is overstated.

The introduction and outlook frame the result in terms of AI energy consumption, replacements for hard disks and solid-state drives, neuromorphic hardware and topological quantum computing. The effect needs fields of about 7 to 20 T applied within about 20 degrees of a specific crystal axis and temperatures below about 0.5 K. The estimated read power of about 1 nW (about 1 pW when scaled down) ignores the cost of providing those conditions, the write energy (flux-flow dissipation during millisecond pulses at 20 A cm^-2, which should be estimated), and the switching speed. The comparison with "nanometre-sized silicon elements" cites a paper on intrinsic carrier concentration in silicon (ref. 59), which does not support a power comparison. The opening claim that historical superconducting-computing research led to scanning tunnelling microscopy and high-Tc superconductivity is tenuous and unnecessary. The point that UTe2 "avoids magnetic interfaces" also needs balancing against the large applied field it requires. I suggest cutting this framing back substantially and leading with the vortex-matter physics.

3. Minor issues

- Methods: "The global minimum at pi = 0" should read phi = 0.
- Extended Data Fig. 1a: the x-axis is labelled "J_ex (ms)"; the unit should be A cm^-2. Extended Data Fig. 1c: please define the plotted quantity "J/alpha (ms)".
- The text says Extended Data Fig. 5 shows "timescale- and duration-tuning", but its caption says duration and relaxation-rate. Use one name ("relaxation rate" or "turn-off rate") consistently, and avoid "timescale" as a third name.
- The Fig. 1b caption describes the erase as "smoothly sweeping the current from a large negative value to zero", while the Methods describe the reset as a sweep from 40 mA to 0. Please make these consistent, and give the sample cross-sections so that currents in mA can be converted to current densities.
- Refs. 26 and 57 are duplicates.
- Fig. 3a axis label "J (A/cm^-2)" should be "J (A cm^-2)". Unit formatting is inconsistent across the Extended Data figures.
- Several statements rely on Supplementary Figs. S2 and S7 and on the SI heating discussion, none of which were provided with this submission.
- Give the rotation stage, the angular resolution, and how theta = 0 was determined. The SC2 boundary is very sensitive to misalignment.
- State the contact geometry and the voltage-contact spacing relative to the current contacts. In a millimetre-sized anisotropic crystal with all leads on the (001) face, current flow may be non-uniform through the thickness, which matters for a bulk interpretation of Jc.
- For B in the b-c plane and current along a, the Lorentz force geometry stays fixed throughout the rotation. That is a strength of the design and worth stating explicitly.
- Fig. 1d is compared to "supercurrent rectification by the superconducting diode effect". The analogy is superficial and could be removed.
- Fig. 4h was measured on a different sample (S3), at a different temperature (400 mK), in a different magnet and with ac rather than dc. Its role in the argument should be stated or the panel moved to the Extended Data.
- The data-availability statement is acceptable for a preprint, but the data and acquisition code should be deposited before publication, as promised.

4. Requested additional analysis or measurements (in priority order)

1. A discussion of the NbSe2/CeRu2 metastable-vortex and memory-effect literature, with a clear statement of what is new here.
2. Control measurements for B || a and B || c (pure SC1) and within SC2 at matched reduced fields B/Bc2.
3. A direct test for heating: sample-mounted thermometry or a heat-pulse write test, contact resistances, and the energy per pulse.
4. An exact description of the pulse-delta protocol, and the measured waveforms at the sample.
5. Retention against time, cycling statistics, read disturb, and error bars on Delta J.
6. Delta J/Jc against T, together with Tc(B) for the fields used.
7. Field-cooled versus zero-field-cooled initial states.
8. Memory data on S3 (the highest-RRR sample) and the dependence on RRR.

5. Recommendation

Major revision. The measurements are systematic and potentially valuable as a study of metastable vortex matter in a multiphase superconductor. As written, however, the manuscript presents a phenomenon with close, uncited precedents as a discovery, links it to SC1/SC2 coexistence on evidence the field and angle data do not clearly support, and has not ruled out more conventional explanations. I would be glad to see a revised version that addresses points 2.1 to 2.6 and recasts the interpretation and the outlook accordingly.
