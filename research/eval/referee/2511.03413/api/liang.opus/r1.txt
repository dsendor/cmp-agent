Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Scope of the work.** The manuscript uses magnetic exchange force microscopy (MExFM) with a Co-functionalized tip on a qPlus-type length-extension resonator. With it, the authors:
  - read the two magnetic states of individual Ho adatoms on 3 ML MgO/Ag(001) at near-zero bias (Δf spectroscopy, Fig. 3; constant-height imaging, Fig. 5);
  - switch them by mechanical tip approach beyond point contact (Figs. 2 and 4, S9).
- **Proposed mechanism.** Tip-induced partial lateral displacement of Ho from the C4v O-top site toward the C2v bridge site. This would lower the symmetry, mix the Jz = ±8 states and open a direct relaxation channel. It is supported by a point-charge Stevens-operator model (SI Sec. 3, Fig. S3).
- **Why it matters.**
  - Ho/MgO is the benchmark single-atom magnet.
  - To date it has been read and written mainly by spin-polarized tunneling current, ESR of a nearby sensor, or XMCD (Natterer 2017; Donati 2016).
  - A force-based, nominally current-free read/write channel would be conceptually new. It would broaden MExFM from imaging static spin textures (Kaiser 2007; Schmidt 2011; Hauptmann 2017/2020) to manipulating a bistable quantum magnet.
- **Assessment of novelty.**
  - Detecting a Ho spin-state contrast in Δf is, to my knowledge, new.
  - Controlling a 4f moment through adsorption-site symmetry via mechanical contact is an appealing concept.
  - However, the novelty of the *mechanism* is less established than claimed (see Section 3).
  - The work should be positioned more carefully against:
    - theoretical proposals for exchange-force switching (Wieser 2013; Schmidt 2012);
    - tip-exchange-bias control of quantum magnets (e.g., Yan et al., Nat. Nanotechnol. 2015);
    - AFM-based single-spin detection (Sellies 2023).

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Technically demanding experiment.** Combined AFM/STM at 4.5 K and 3 T with a Co-cluster tip. Tip preparation and spin sensitivity are verified by current-induced telegraph noise (Fig. S5), and switching is cross-checked against SP-STM conductance contrast before and after the write sequence (Figs. S6, S9b).
- **Bidirectional state changes with a consistent Δf sign.**
  - Δf contrast is opposite for ↓→↑ (Fig. 2) and ↑→↓ (Fig. S9), and appears as two-level telegraph noise in Δf at constant height (Fig. 4a,b).
  - This makes a purely irreversible structural change unlikely as the origin of each event.
- **Spin-resolved Δf imaging with an internal reference.** In Fig. 5, the right adatom remains ↑ and serves as a built-in control while the left adatom changes contrast.
- **Spectroscopic observation of an FM-to-AFM crossover.** The distance-dependent sign change of the spin-dependent force (Fig. 3b inset) is potentially interesting in its own right for 4f–3d exchange across a contact.
- **Comprehensive SI.** Tip pick-up protocol, tip bookkeeping (Table 1), dissipation data, adatom-site identification, atom-manipulation events (Fig. S11) and a tip-dependence histogram (Fig. S13).
- **Broad interest.** The topic is timely for atomic-scale spintronics and quantum-coherent surface spins.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**(R1) Attribution of the Δf contrast to the Ho spin state (and to exchange force) is not unambiguously established.**
- **The contrast is small and lies in a regime dominated by contact mechanics.**
  - The spin-dependent difference is ~0.1–0.6 Hz on a ~5–17 Hz background. This corresponds to ≤ ~50–100 pN in force (Fig. 3b inset), noise-dominated over much of the range, with no error bars or repeat statistics.
  - It appears only at z ≲ +20 pm, i.e., at or beyond point contact. There the Ho adatom is shown (Fig. S8) to relax laterally under the tip.
  - Any spin-state-dependent difference in adsorption geometry, bond length or lateral relaxation would produce a Δf difference indistinguishable from an "exchange force". The authors' own mechanism (partial displacement toward the bridge site) makes this concern acute.
  - Specifically, the system may end in a slightly different configuration after a switch. The near-identity of the ↑ and ↓ curves away from z ≈ 0 is reassuring but not conclusive.
- **The Sader–Jarvis inversion assumptions are questionable here.**
  - The inversion of Δf to F (Fig. 3b) assumes a single-valued, conservative, z-only force. That is questionable when the adatom moves laterally with z, as evidenced by the double-minimum Δf(z) in Fig. S8.
  - The amplitude (65 pm) is comparable to the length scale of the features being inverted.
  - The sign changes in F_MExFM between z = 0 and −0.1 nm are therefore susceptible to inversion artifacts. The "FM→AFM transition" claim rests on a few tens of pN extracted from a difference of two inverted curves.
- **Decisive controls are absent.**
  - (i) Δf on Ho with a non-magnetic tip, showing no bistability and no contrast.
  - (ii) Reversal of the tip magnetization (B → −3 T), showing inversion of the readout contrast.
  - (iii) A Δf readout for an adatom in the same state measured repeatedly after many switching cycles, showing reproducible state-specific values.
- **The state labels rely on unverified assumptions.**
  - The ↑/↓ assignment rests on the assumption that the Co-cluster tip is superparamagnetic and aligned with +B, and on the sign of the TMR.
  - Neither is independently verified; for example, the field dependence of the contrast and the population asymmetry under current pumping are not shown.
  - The sign interpretation (FM coupling at z = 0) therefore lacks an independent anchor.

**(R2) The proposed writing mechanism is speculative and appears energetically inconsistent with the data.**
- **The dwell times are incompatible with equilibrium relaxation.**
  - The model states that the ↑ and ↓ states are split by Zeeman energy ΔE ≈ 3.47 meV at 3 T (Table 5), while k_BT ≈ 0.39 meV.
  - Any switching channel that merely *opens* a relaxation pathway (symmetry lowering plus spin–phonon or electron scattering with a 4.5 K bath) should drive the system into the ground state. The expected population ratio is ~exp(−ΔE/k_BT) ≈ 10⁻⁴.
  - Yet the telegraph traces in Fig. 4a,b show comparable dwell times in both states (seconds each), at V = 1 mV, i.e., below ΔE.
  - The data therefore require a source of energy or a level shift that the authors do not discuss. Candidates are:
    - (a) a tip-induced exchange bias that partially cancels the Zeeman splitting. The reported FM→AFM crossover near z ≈ −0.08 nm would naturally produce a near-degeneracy at some z;
    - (b) non-adiabatic (Landau–Zener-type) driving by the 1 MHz tip oscillation;
    - (c) multi-electron or hot-electron processes in contact.
- **Bidirectional switching does not rule out exchange-related mechanisms.**
  - The claim that "bidirectional spin switching rules out exchange forces as the driving mechanism" is not justified. Scenario (a) is an exchange-mediated mechanism that is fully compatible with bidirectional switching.
  - Negligible dissipation (Fig. S7e) also cannot exclude mechanical driving. Flipping one spin per second at ~3.5 meV is many orders of magnitude below the dissipation detection limit.
- **The crystal-field modelling is too crude to support the mechanism.**
  - It is a bare point-charge model with formal ±2e charges and an unrelaxed geometry.
  - The predicted splitting (~280 meV for Ho_top in Fig. 1b; ~190 meV for Ho_bridge in Fig. S3b) is not benchmarked against experimentally constrained crystal-field parameters for Ho/MgO (XAS/XMCD multiplet fits, refs 6, 10, 12).
  - The linear interpolation αH_C2v + (1−α)H_C4v interpolates Hamiltonians, not geometries. α is never connected to an actual displacement, a tip height, or a DFT-relaxed structure.
  - The metric I₀₁ (Eq. S6) ignores energy-dependent spectral densities, Boltzmann factors, phonon DOS and the hyperfine interaction known to matter for Ho (ref. 14).
  - Fig. S3c shows an unexplained minimum near α ≈ 0.1 and a kink near α ≈ 0.3, suggesting numerical or level-crossing artifacts. Its "approximately exponential" dependence is not mapped onto the measured exponential z-dependence of Fig. 4c.
- **The tip's own contribution is dismissed without calculation.**
  - The authors argue in SI Sec. 14 that the tip's symmetry-breaking contribution is smaller "because the Co tip is metallic and neutral".
  - This is qualitative. At point contact, hybridization with the tip can strongly modify the 5d/6s shell, the crystal field and the exchange field, so it should be estimated, e.g., with DFT or a hybridization model.

**(R3) "Controlled writing" is not demonstrated; the protocol is stochastic, tip-dependent and close to destructive.**
- **Writing is stochastic and requires read-verify feedback.**
  - Switching in Fig. 4 is telegraphic in both directions, with similar occupancies. Figs. 2 and S9 therefore show a stochastic event that happened within a 10 s window, followed by retraction.
  - Writing a *chosen* state thus requires read-verify-retract feedback. No data quantify the probability of ending in a target state, the write error rate, or the dependence of the ↑:↓ occupancy ratio on z.
  - The claim that "spin orientation… can be controlled by adjusting the tip–sample distance" is not supported. Only the rate, not the orientation, depends on z in the data shown.
- **The operating window is narrow and poorly quantified.**
  - The switching distance varies from ~0 to −140 pm between tips (Fig. S13; the axis is labeled "nm", presumably pm).
  - A slightly closer approach irreversibly converts Ho_top to Ho_bridge, a short-lived magnet that "rarely returns". The working window between "no switching" and "bit destroyed" is never quantified for any tip.
  - Neither the fraction of attempts that ended in displacement nor how many adatoms and switching cycles were tested is reported.
- **The rate data are statistically weak.**
  - In Fig. 4c, the force-switching fits for tips 4 and 5 rest on ~3–5 points spanning ~10 pm. They are compared with current-induced switching measured with a different tip at a different bias (150 mV) and ~200 pm farther away.
  - The conclusion of "more spatially localized control" refers only to vertical decay; lateral localization (essential for bit density) is never measured.
  - Switching-rate maps versus lateral tip position, or tests on closely spaced adatoms, are absent.

**(R4) Key claims about current-free, low-heat and nondissipative operation, and about technological relevance, are overstated.**
- **Tunneling current is not negligible at the writing distances.**
  - At z ≤ 0, the conductance is ~10⁻²G₀ (Fig. S7d, S12b). At V = 1 mV (Fig. 4) this implies currents of order ~1 nA; at 200 µV, ~0.1–0.2 nA. These are comparable to or larger than currents used in SP-STM work.
  - The method is therefore not "current-free". The roles of current and bias, including at exactly V = 0 and with reversed polarity, are not disentangled.
- **The energy-efficiency claims are unsupported.**
  - No estimate is given of the energy per write: mechanical work in contact, resonator drive power, or Joule heating at the reported currents.
  - There is no comparison with current-pulse writing or ESR protocols.
  - The forward-looking statements about long coherence times and quantum information are not supported by any coherence-relevant measurement. Repeated point contact with a metal tip would, if anything, be expected to degrade T₁/T₂.
- **Definitions and presentation undermine confidence.**
  - z = 0 is defined as the point of "point conductance G₀", yet the measured conductance at z = 0 is ~10⁻²G₀. This calls into question what "point contact" means across tips and figures.
  - Residual non-English annotations appear in Fig. 4 and Fig. S1.
  - Ag(001) and Ag(100) are used interchangeably.
  - It is not stated how the left adatom in Fig. 5 was switched (by force or by current), which is critical for the "force-based" narrative.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Establish the magnetic origin of the readout with explicit controls and statistics.**
   - Measure Δf(z) on Ho with a non-magnetic (W/Ag) tip and show the absence of bistable contrast.
   - Reverse the field (±3 T), or deliberately use tips of opposite polarization, and demonstrate contrast inversion.
   - Cycle the same adatom many times (↓→↑→↓…) and report the state-resolved Δf with error bars across several adatoms and tips.
   - Show the full z-dependence of the contrast with the same tip used for switching.
   - Quantify the uncertainty of F_MExFM, and test the inversion's robustness against amplitude (e.g., smaller A) and lateral relaxation, for instance by comparing with direct Δf differences.
2. **Determine the switching mechanism experimentally rather than by assumption.**
   - Report up and down rates and the occupancy ratio separately as functions of z.
   - Vary B: an exchange-bias or level-crossing mechanism predicts a field-dependent resonance distance, whereas a symmetry-lowering relaxation channel predicts relaxation toward the ground state.
   - Vary temperature and oscillation amplitude, including quasi-static approach or a very small A.
   - Vary bias down to 0 V and with both polarities.
   - Explicitly model the tip exchange field together with the Zeeman term. Replace the point-charge/α-interpolation model with DFT-relaxed geometries of Ho under a Co-cluster tip, or at least with literature-benchmarked crystal-field parameters, and connect the displacement to z.
   - Explain quantitatively how near-equal populations arise when ΔE ≫ k_BT.
3. **Demonstrate genuine, quantified writing control.**
   - Define a deterministic write protocol (e.g., an approach–retract pulse of fixed depth and duration, or read-verify feedback), and report success probability, error rate and number of attempts.
   - Quantify the window between the onset of switching and Ho_top→Ho_bridge conversion for each tip, including the fraction of destructive events.
   - Map switching rate versus lateral tip position, and test closely spaced adatoms, to substantiate the spatial-localization and bit-density claims.
   - Discuss strategies to reduce tip-to-tip variability (Fig. S13), such as a standardized tip-apex characterization.
4. **Temper claims, contextualize the work and fix presentation issues.**
   - Replace "current-free" and "nondissipative" with accurate statements that report the actual currents during read and write. Provide an order-of-magnitude energy-per-operation estimate compared with current-pulse writing.
   - Remove or substantially qualify the coherence and quantum-information claims.
   - Cite and discuss tip-exchange-bias and exchange-force-switching literature, and revise the statement that bidirectional switching rules out exchange.
   - Clarify the z = 0 definition, given that the conductance at z = 0 is ~10⁻²G₀, and state whether the static or the deconvolved conductance is used.
   - Correct units (Fig. S13 axis), remove residual non-English annotations (Fig. 4, Fig. S1), and unify Ag(001)/Ag(100).
   - State how the Fig. 5 switch was performed, and explain the unexplained features of Fig. S3c.
