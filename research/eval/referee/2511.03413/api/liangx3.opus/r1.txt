# Merged review

## A. Must address

**1. Comparable dwell times in both states contradict detailed balance, and the proposed mechanism cannot explain this.**
The calculated Ho↑–Ho↓ splitting at 3 T is 3.47 meV (Table 5), while k_BT ≈ 0.39 meV at 4.5 K. Thermal equilibrium therefore predicts an occupation ratio of order exp(−9) ≈ 10⁻⁴.

The two mechanisms proposed in SI Sec. 14 (symmetry lowering with spin–phonon relaxation, and scattering of thermal electrons) only open a relaxation pathway to a 4.5 K bath. Either one should drive the adatom into the ground state. Quantum tunnelling of magnetization alone would also relax it to the ground state.

Yet the telegraph traces in Fig. 4a,b show comparable dwell times of seconds in each state (ratio ~1–2), measured at V = 1 mV, below ΔE. This implies an effective splitting of ≲0.3 meV. The data therefore require one of two things:
- **(i) A large level shift under the tip.** For example, a tip exchange or ligand field could nearly cancel the Zeeman splitting. The reported FM→AFM crossover near z ≈ −0.08 nm would naturally produce a near-degeneracy at some z.
- **(ii) A non-thermal energy source.** Candidates are:
  - non-adiabatic (Landau–Zener-type) driving by the 1 MHz, 130 pm peak-to-peak mechanical loading cycle;
  - contact-induced vibrations;
  - tunnelling, multi-electron or hot-electron processes at ~1 mV and ~nA.

Neither possibility is discussed. As written, the mechanism is inconsistent with the authors' own data.

The authors should:
- report Γ↑→↓ and Γ↓→↑ separately, the occupancy ratio, and dwell-time histograms as functions of z, and compare them with detailed balance;
- explain quantitatively how near-equal populations arise when ΔE ≫ k_BT.

**2. Alternative writing mechanisms are dismissed without the discriminating experiments.**
The manuscript states that bidirectional switching "rules out exchange forces as the driving mechanism" (citing Ref. 25). This is not justified. A tip exchange field of a few meV would bring the ±8 states near degeneracy, and any transverse perturbation would then produce bidirectional, near-symmetric telegraph switching. The measured F_MExFM of ~0.1 nN acting over ~10 pm corresponds to ~6 meV. Possible transverse perturbations include:
- a canted tip moment;
- an asymmetric Co cluster (as Fig. S12 indicates);
- the hyperfine interaction;
- small distortions.

Several other channels are also neither excluded nor tested:
- In contact, the Co tip exerts ~1.5 nN and is chemically bonded to Ho. Tip-induced ligand fields and Co 3d–Ho 5d/6s hybridization could modify the 4f anisotropy.
- Tunnelling electrons are present. G reaches ~10⁻² G₀ (Fig. S7d, S12b), giving roughly 0.1–1 nA (≈0.8 nA) at V = 1 mV in Fig. 4. At 200 µV the time-averaged current is still tens of pA (0.04–0.08 nA, Fig. S7c). These currents permit inelastic transitions, local heating and spin–spin scattering, which the authors themselves list as a possible mechanism.
- The 1 MHz mechanical modulation could drive transitions.
- Co could transfer to or from the tip during contact.

The absence of a dissipation signal (Fig. S7e) cannot exclude mechanical driving. Its noise floor is ~±0.01 eV/cycle, whereas flipping one spin per second at ~3.5 meV is spread over 10⁶ cycles.

An exchange-field or level-crossing scenario makes testable predictions: a field-dependent and possibly resonance-like (rather than monotonic) dependence of switching distance and rate. The authors should measure switching rates and the ↑/↓ dwell-time ratio as functions of:
- B magnitude (e.g., 1–8 T), direction and polarity;
- bias magnitude down to a compensated V ≈ 0, for both polarities, with the current recorded (Fig. 4 uses 1 mV, other figures 200 µV, and no V-dependence is given);
- temperature;
- oscillation amplitude, including very small A and quasi-static, non-oscillating contact.

They should also model the tip exchange field explicitly together with the Zeeman term. Finally, the statement that bidirectional switching rules out exchange should be revised.

**3. The Δf readout is not shown to arise from the Ho spin state or from exchange.**
The ↑/↓ Δf difference is small: ~0.1–0.6 Hz on a −5 to −30 Hz background (Fig. 3a; ~0.15 Hz in Fig. 4; ~0.5 Hz only at the minimum in Fig. 5c). This corresponds to ≤ ~50–100 pN (Fig. 3b inset). The force difference is noise-dominated over much of the range and is shown without error bars or repeat statistics.

The contrast appears only within roughly ±30 pm of point contact (z ≲ +20 pm). This is the regime in which the authors themselves argue that Ho relaxes laterally under the tip (Fig. S8). F_MExFM is dominated by a narrow ~−0.09 nN spike near z ≈ 0.01 nm, which coincides with the conductance jump (jump-to-contact) in Fig. S7d.

Any spin-state-dependent difference in adsorption geometry, bond length, lateral relaxation or bonding would produce an indistinguishable Δf difference. Examples include the system ending in a slightly different configuration after a switch, or a partial displacement toward the bridge site as the authors' own mechanism proposes. The near-identity of the ↑ and ↓ curves away from z ≈ 0 is reassuring but not conclusive.

The standard magnetic controls are missing. The authors should provide:
- Δf(z) on Ho with a non-magnetic (W/Ag) tip, showing no bistable contrast (Fig. S5 tests this only in current mode);
- field reversal to −3 T, or tips of deliberately opposite polarization, showing inversion of the readout contrast;
- field-magnitude and temperature dependence of the contrast;
- the same adatom cycled many times (↓→↑→↓…), with state-resolved Δf values and error bars across several adatoms and tips;
- the full z-dependence of the contrast measured with the same tip used for switching.

**4. The partial lateral displacement toward the bridge site is inferred, not observed.**
The central mechanistic claim is that switching occurs "when Ho_top moves toward Ho_bridge but does not fully reach it". It rests on three indirect observations:
- the double-minimum Δf(z) in Fig. S8;
- the irreversible top→bridge hop at somewhat larger push (Fig. S11);
- switching always occurring at z < 0 (Fig. S13).

Each is also compatible with other explanations: tip-apex relaxation, Co-cluster reconfiguration, multi-atom-tip artifacts, or vertical compression of the Ho–O bond. None establishes a lateral displacement at the switching height, nor its magnitude or direction. The argument that relaxation "begins after point contact" (S13) is also circular, because point contact is defined from conductance, which the same relaxation strongly affects.

The authors should provide direct evidence:
- switching-rate maps versus lateral tip offset in several directions relative to the MgO lattice, and versus height (2D/3D rate and force maps);
- correlation of the rate with lateral force or relaxation extracted from 3D Δf;
- DFT relaxations of a Co-cluster/Ho/MgO/Ag junction giving the real Ho displacement as a function of z.

**5. "Controlled writing" is not demonstrated; switching is stochastic.**
At the write distance the adatom telegraphs in both directions with similar occupancies (Fig. 4). The final states in Figs. 2 and S9 are therefore whichever state happened to be occupied when the tip was retracted within the ~10 s window.

Writing a chosen state requires a read-verify-retract loop, but its fidelity, latency and success probability are not quantified. The claim that "spin orientation… can be controlled by adjusting the tip–sample distance" is unsupported: in the data shown, only the rate depends on z, not the orientation. It is also not shown that the written state is retained with the native Ho_top lifetime after retraction.

The authors should:
- define and demonstrate a deterministic protocol, for example a fixed-depth/duration approach–retract pulse, real-time Δf feedback that retracts on detecting the target state, or B/tip-field biasing that makes the rates asymmetric;
- report write-attempt statistics: success probability, error rate, time-to-switch distributions, final-state distribution after retraction, and number of attempts;
- verify the post-write lifetime with the tip retracted.

## B. Should address

**6. The crystal-field model is too crude to support the mechanism.**
The model has several weaknesses:
- **Unbenchmarked parameters.** It is a bare point-charge model with Q_M = +3e, nominal ±2e ligand charges and an unrelaxed geometry. It ignores screening by Ag, covalency and 5d/6s polarization. The predicted splittings (~280 meV for Ho_top, Fig. 1b; ~190 meV for Ho_bridge, Fig. S3b) are not benchmarked against crystal-field parameters constrained by XAS/XMCD multiplet fits (refs 6, 10, 12; Donati et al.).
- **Questionable geometry.** The geometry in Table 3 (O at d⊥ = 173 pm vs. Mg at 248 pm) needs justification.
- **Easy-axis labelling at the bridge site.** There, |B₂²| = 1323 µeV exceeds |B₂⁰| = 563 µeV, so the easy axis is no longer along z. Labelling the eigenstates as ⟨J_z⟩ "Ho↑/Ho↓" is then questionable.
- **Unphysical interpolation parameter.** The interpolation H = αH_C2v + (1−α)H_C4v interpolates Hamiltonians, not geometries. α is never connected to a displacement in pm, a tip height or a relaxed structure, so Fig. S3c cannot be compared with the exponential z-dependence in Fig. 4c.
- **Unexplained features in Fig. S3c.** It shows a minimum near α ≈ 0.1 and a kink near α ≈ 0.3, suggesting level crossings or numerical artifacts.
- **Oversimplified transition metric.** I₀₁ (Eq. S6) assumes a fixed bath. It ignores energy-dependent spectral densities, Boltzmann factors and the phonon DOS, so "relative transition probability" cannot be compared with absolute rates.
- **Missing interactions.** The model omits the ¹⁶⁵Ho hyperfine interaction (I = 7/2), which is central to QTM and stability in Ho/MgO (Donati 2016; Forrester 2019, ref. 14). It also omits the Co-tip exchange field, which acts exactly where switching occurs.
- **Tip contribution dismissed qualitatively.** SI §14 argues that the "metallic and neutral" tip perturbs the crystal field less than the ionic substrate. This ignores the screening, image charges and hybridization that change precisely at contact.

The authors should replace the point-charge/α model with DFT-relaxed geometries and ab initio or multiplet crystal-field parameters along the actual displacement path, or at least with literature-benchmarked parameters. The model should include the tip ligand field, the exchange field and the hyperfine interaction, and the authors should explain the features in Fig. S3c.

**7. The ↑/↓ assignment lacks an independent anchor, and the contrast sign is tip-dependent.**
The labels rest on two unverified assumptions: that the Co-cluster tip is superparamagnetic and aligned with +B, and that the TMR has a particular sign, which depends on the unknown spin polarization of the cluster. Neither the field dependence of the contrast nor the population asymmetry under current pumping is shown. The interpretation of FM coupling at z = 0 therefore has no independent anchor.

The sign problem is visible in the data:
- In Fig. 2 (tip 1), ↓→↑ at −0.13 nm produces an upward Δf jump.
- In Fig. S9 (tip 12), ↑→↓ at −0.03 nm also produces an upward jump.

Consistency thus relies entirely on a tip- and distance-dependent sign change, which must be shown explicitly for each tip.

The TMR confirmations of state changes are also marginal. Figs. S6 and S9b inset show 1.10 vs. ~1.07 nA, a difference of a few percent after a ~70 s excursion into contact. Drift and creep correction are not described, although conclusions rest on ~pm-scale and ~0.1 Hz-scale differences between approach and retraction curves (Figs. 2, S9).

The authors should provide an independent calibration, such as the B-polarity dependence of the ground-state population or a known reference. They should also add drift and tip-change controls for the TMR cross-checks and state the drift-handling procedure.

**8. The Sader–Jarvis force inversion and the FM→AFM crossover are not robust.**
The inversion in Fig. 3b assumes a smooth, conservative, single-valued, z-only force. Three features of the data violate this:
- Ho moves laterally with z, as the double-minimum Δf(z) in Fig. S8 shows.
- Δf(z) has abrupt steps near z ≈ 0.02 nm and −0.05 nm (Figs. 3a, S8a, S12a), which the authors attribute to junction rearrangement.
- The amplitude (65 pm) is comparable to the feature size, so discontinuities are smeared into neighbouring z.

The claimed sign change of F_MExFM between z = 0 and −0.1 nm rests on a few tens of pN extracted from the difference of two inverted curves, and is therefore susceptible to inversion artifacts. At z ≈ −0.1 nm, Ho sits beneath secondary tip atoms (Fig. S8c), so the two heights do not compare the same tip-atom pair. Analogy to Fe–Ta theory (Ref. 24) is not evidence, and no Co–Ho exchange calculation is given. It is also not shown that the adatom stayed in its assigned state throughout each Δf(z) curve down to −0.11 nm, which is the range where other tips switch.

The authors should:
- quantify the uncertainty of F_MExFM;
- test robustness against smaller A and against direct Δf differences;
- confirm that no switch occurred during each spectrum;
- estimate the exchange energy by integrating F_MExFM and compare it with known Ho–Co 4f–3d exchange (Singha et al., PRL 2018);
- revisit the FM→AFM interpretation in light of the lateral relaxation.

The crossover is potentially interesting in its own right, but only if it is established.

**9. Readout fidelity, statistics and back-action are not quantified, and the near-contact regime is not acknowledged.**
The readout evidence is thin:
- Fig. 3 uses a single tip.
- Fig. 5 is a single image pair, and the profile of the right (reference) atom also changes slightly between images, so tip-height drift cannot be excluded.
- The numbers of atoms and tips showing readout contrast are not stated.

For a memory-context "reading" claim, the authors need:
- read fidelity: Δf histograms per state, SNR versus bandwidth and z, and error rates;
- read back-action: the switching probability per read at the imaging height, ~20 pm above contact;
- approach/retract control cycles that stay above the write threshold and give identical minima, to rule out drift or hysteresis.

The readout is essentially at contact, not exchange across a vacuum gap as in earlier MExFM work. This regime should be stated explicitly and its implications for non-invasiveness discussed. Ideally, the authors would demonstrate readout at a height without relaxation, or else state clearly that near-contact is required.

**10. The definition of z = 0 is inconsistent with the measured conductance.**
The SI defines z = 0 by the "point conductance" G₀ = 7.748×10⁻⁵ S, yet the conductance at z = 0 is only ~10⁻² G₀ (Fig. S7d, S12b). The reference is also taken from the point-contact conductance of Ho↓, which is itself spin-, tip- and relaxation-dependent.

Every switching threshold and the "always at z < 0" argument are quoted relative to this reference. The ambiguity therefore directly affects the mechanistic argument and the comparison between tips and figures. The authors should fix and justify the definition, and state whether the static or the deconvolved conductance is used.

**11. The "current-free" claim is overstated.**
All force experiments run at finite bias (200 µV–1 mV), with conductances up to ~10⁻² G₀. The resulting currents of tens of pA to ~1 nA are comparable to or larger than those used in SP-STM work, and they carry spin-scattering channels. The roles of current and bias are not disentangled.

"Current-free" should be replaced with an accurate description (low-bias, sub-threshold current), with the actual currents during read and write reported. Alternatively, the authors should demonstrate operation at compensated zero bias.

**12. The energy-efficiency, "nondissipative" and quantum-coherence claims are unsupported.**
The dissipation noise floor (~0.01 eV/cycle, Fig. S7e) is three orders of magnitude above the meV-scale spin energies, so it cannot support "nondissipative" or "minimal energy loss". The following are not considered:
- mechanical work in contact;
- resonator drive power and the stored LER energy (~keV-scale);
- Joule heating at the reported currents;
- the plastic risk of irreversible bridge hopping (Fig. S11).

No energy-per-operation estimate or comparison with current-pulse writing (Natterer et al. 2017) or ESR protocols is given.

The closing statements about long coherence times and quantum information are not backed by any coherence-relevant measurement. Repeated contact with a metallic tip at ~1.5 nN would, if anything, be expected to degrade T₁/T₂. The authors should provide an order-of-magnitude energy budget and remove or substantially qualify these claims.

**13. The operating window between switching and destruction is not quantified.**
The switching distance varies from ~0 to −140 pm between tips (Fig. S13). A slightly deeper approach irreversibly converts Ho_top into Ho_bridge, a short-lived magnet that "rarely returns" (Fig. S11). The following are not reported:
- the window between onset of switching and destructive conversion, for any tip;
- the fraction of attempts ending in displacement or tip changes;
- the numbers of atoms, tips, attempts and switching cycles;
- endurance, i.e., cycles before irreversible hopping or tip change.

The authors should report these as functions of z for each tip. They should also discuss how to reduce tip-to-tip variability, for example through standardized tip-apex characterization.

**14. Fig. 4c rate data are statistically weak, and the locality claim is unsupported.**
For tips 4 and 5, the force-switching fits rest on only ~2–5 points spanning ~10–15 pm, with no error bars. The fits are nearly unconstrained and their slopes differ substantially. They are compared with current-induced switching measured with a different tip, at a different bias (150 mV) and at z = 160–290 pm, roughly 200 pm farther away.

The conclusion of "more spatially localized control" rests only on vertical decay. A steeper vertical slope is expected for any contact-mechanical process and says nothing about lateral localization or crosstalk, which are what matter for bit density.

The authors should:
- provide denser data with error bars and fit details;
- map switching rate versus lateral tip position;
- test writes near neighbouring Ho adatoms at various separations.

**15. The work is insufficiently positioned against prior literature.**
Novelty of the mechanism and of tip-proximity control should be weighed against:
- MExFM imaging and exchange-force spectroscopy (Kaiser 2007; Schmidt 2011; Pielmeier & Giessibl 2013; Hauptmann 2017, 2020);
- exchange-force switching proposals (Schmidt 2012; Wieser 2013);
- tip exchange-bias and anisotropy control (Yan et al., Nat. Nanotechnol. 2015; Oberg et al., Nat. Nanotechnol. 2014);
- mechanical control of molecular spin states (Parks et al., Science 2010);
- AFM-based single-spin detection (Sellies 2023).

These works should be cited and discussed.

**16. The method used to switch the left adatom in Fig. 5 is not stated.**
The manuscript does not say whether the left adatom in Fig. 5 was switched by force or by current. This is critical for the "force-based" narrative, because the figure is presented as a bit-addressing demonstration with the right adatom as an internal reference. The authors should state the protocol.

## C. Minor

**17. The spin/moment sign convention is confusing.**
The ground state with ⟨J_z⟩ = −8 is called "Ho↑", presumably because the moment is antiparallel to J. In Table 5, ψ₀ is listed at 0.00 meV for both sites, and the text refers inconsistently to "ground state and metastable state (Ho↑ and Ho↓)". The authors should state the moment-vs-J convention explicitly and identify the thermodynamic ground state with the tip present.

**18. There are presentation errors.**
- The Fig. S13 abscissa is labelled "nm" but presumably means pm.
- Leftover Japanese annotations appear in Fig. 4 ("傾きが のほうが強いので電子だまりより", "が安定") and Fig. S1 ("３．０").
- Ag(001) and Ag(100) are used interchangeably and should be unified.
- Error bars and fit details are missing in Fig. 4c and elsewhere.

**19. Tip dependence is hidden in the SI.**
Tip bookkeeping (Table 1) and the tip dependence of sign and threshold should be consolidated into the main text, so readers can see the variability.

**20. Data and citation sourcing should be improved.**
Several key crystal-field results are cited only to a PhD thesis (Refs. 11/16). Raw data are said to be "included in the ESI" but are not provided as data files. The authors should supply dwell-time histograms, rates for both switching directions and raw data files.
