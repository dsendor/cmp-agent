Referee report

Manuscript: "Imaging of Gate-Controlled Suppression of Superconductivity via the Meissner Effect"
(arXiv:2510.09044v1)

SUMMARY

The authors use a scanning NV magnetometer in a dilution refrigerator to image the stray field
above ~20 nm Nb/Ti islands on SiO2/Si next to side gates that draw a leakage current of tens to
hundreds of nA. When a power P = IL*VG of order 0.1 to 5 uW is dissipated, the Meissner-screened
area of the island shrinks on the gate-facing side. The suppressed region is modeled as a disk of
radius r0 centered at the gate apex. In device D3, r0 is found to depend on P only, not on bath
temperature (0.4 K vs 5.6 K), gate polarity, or VG. r0(P) is fitted with a Lambert-W form derived
from a 3D steady-state diffusion equation with a point source and a linear decay term, giving a
diffusion length rd ~ 0.6 um. Gate-geometry variations (extended gate, corner gate, gate-to-gate
bias with a floating island) are shown qualitatively. The authors also report that trapped vortices
leave the island at low gate power. They conclude that the gate-controlled supercurrent (GCS) is a
"genuine feature of superconductivity not restricted to transport", that Joule heating and a direct
electric field effect are excluded, and that the data support a non-equilibrium hot-spot picture
with a quasiparticle lifetime of ~1e-9 s.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT

This is the first real-space image of leakage-driven suppression of superconductivity in a gated
metallic superconductor, and the technique is well suited to the question. The power scaling, the
polarity and VG independence, and the floating-island geometry are useful facts for the GCS debate.
The experimental work appears careful, and the figures are clear.

However, the mechanistic conclusions are stated more strongly than the data allow. The exclusion
of Joule (thermal) heating rests on a single comparison of two temperatures, with no estimate of
the expected temperature rise. The fitted model does not discriminate between mechanisms. The
lifetime quoted in the abstract depends on a choice of diffusion channel that the authors
themselves say they cannot make. The link between this high-power regime (uW, hundreds of nA) and
the transport GCS (pW to nW) is assumed rather than shown, although a device built for that purpose
(D1) exists. I think the paper can become a solid contribution after a major revision that either
adds the missing analysis or tones the claims down to what the data show.

Recommendation: major revision.

MAJOR COMMENTS

1. Exclusion of Joule heating is not established.
   (a) The only argument given is that r0(P) is the same at 0.4 K and 5.6 K. This holds only if
   the local hot-spot temperature is close to Tc. If the dissipated power drives the local
   temperature well above Tc, the radius where T(r) = Tc depends only weakly on T_bath, especially
   at these low temperatures where the thermal conductivities of SiO2, of the Si/SiO2 interface
   (Kapitza) and of the Nb film depend strongly on temperature. A simple order-of-magnitude
   estimate gives a temperature rise that is not small: for a point source dissipating P = 1 uW
   into amorphous SiO2 with kappa ~ 0.05 to 0.1 W/(m K) at a few K, dT ~ P/(2 pi kappa r) is of
   order 1 to 3 K at r = 1 um, and larger at 0.4 K where kappa is smaller. The authors should
   provide a quantitative thermal model (oxide on Si, with temperature-dependent kappa and boundary
   resistance) and show that it fails to reproduce r0(P) and its temperature independence. Without
   this, "excluded" should become "not required" or similar.
   (b) The temperature independence is equally puzzling for the authors' own model. Within Eq. (3),
   Pd = 4 pi e rd^3 rho_c/(alpha tau). Both the critical quasiparticle density rho_c and the
   relaxation/recombination time tau should depend strongly on temperature between 0.4 K and
   5.6 K, especially if 5.6 K is a sizable fraction of Tc for a 20 nm Nb/Ti film. Please state Tc
   of the islands in the main text (Table S1 is cited but the value matters for this argument),
   and discuss why Pd and rd come out temperature independent. As it stands, the key observation
   used to exclude heating is not explained by the proposed alternative either.
   (c) The distinction between "Joule heating" and "non-equilibrium particles generated by the
   leakage current" needs a sharper definition. The leakage power is Joule dissipation in either
   case. The physical question is whether the energy reaches the island as a thermalized
   (local-equilibrium) phonon and electron distribution or as athermal excitations. Please frame
   the exclusion in those terms and state what observable would separate the two in this
   experiment. Time-resolved measurements are mentioned as future work. If no current observable
   separates them, the abstract and the "second key result" paragraph should say so.

2. The diffusion model and the Lambert-W fit do not discriminate between mechanisms.
   (a) Eq. (1) is a 3D isotropic diffusion equation. For quasiparticles in a 20 nm film the
   problem is 2D, and the Green's function is K0(r/rd), not exp(-r/rd)/r. For heat or phonons in
   a 300 nm oxide on Si, the geometry is layered and the healing length is set by the oxide
   thickness and the interface conductance. The functional form used therefore presupposes a
   channel the authors say they cannot identify. Please show fits with the 2D form and with a
   layered thermal model, and report the fitted parameters with uncertainties and residuals.
   (b) Any monotonic, sublinear two-parameter form will fit Fig. 3a well. "Excellent agreement"
   is therefore weak support for the hot-spot picture specifically. A stronger test would be a
   parameter-free prediction. Examples: the reduced quench area for the corner and floating
   geometries in Fig. 4d,e, using rd and Pd from Fig. 3a; or the G2/G3 extended-gate result
   using a line source. The text refers to the SM for the 0.8 um offset, but this prediction
   should be in the main text with a number.
   (c) Fig. 3a fits r0 from the gate apex, with r0 -> 0 as P -> 0. The gate apex sits ~80 nm
   from the island edge, so the model implies a finite threshold power before any suppression
   appears. How is r0 defined (from the apex or from the island edge)? Is the zero-power point
   included in the fit?

3. The lifetime estimate in the abstract is not supported.
   The abstract states "the estimated quasiparticle lifetime ~1e-9 s", but the main text states
   that it is not possible to determine whether rd reflects quasiparticle diffusion in Nb or NEP
   diffusion in the substrate. The substrate channel is dismissed because tau ~ 350 ns "appears
   unreasonably long". That estimate uses the room-temperature thermal diffusivity of glass
   (1e-6 m^2/s, Ref. [29]). This is not the relevant quantity at sub-K temperatures, where the
   heat capacity of SiO2 is much smaller and the diffusivity correspondingly different. It is
   also not relevant for athermal phonons, which propagate quasi-ballistically with mean free
   paths that can exceed microns. The comparison of the 0.7 ns lifetime with a hot-spot formation
   time from Ref. [13] is also not like-for-like. Moreover, the floating-island result (Fig. 4e)
   shows that suppression occurs with no current through the island, so energy must reach the
   island through the substrate (or through vacuum). This argues for a substrate contribution and
   is in tension with dismissing that channel. Please remove the lifetime from the abstract, or
   qualify it as conditional on the quasiparticle channel, and redo the substrate estimate with
   appropriate low-temperature or athermal-phonon parameters.

4. The connection to the transport GCS needs to be demonstrated, not assumed.
   The required powers (~uW) are several orders of magnitude above those in transport GCS
   experiments, and the leakage currents (hundreds of nA) are far above the nA or less typical of
   the literature. The onset voltages drift between sweeps, consistent with stress-induced leakage
   or partial breakdown of the oxide. This is a different regime, and it is not obvious that it
   is the same phenomenon as the GCS in nanowires at much lower dissipation. The conclusions
   speculate about "differences in the GCS in transport versus Meissner screening". A simpler
   explanation is that complete suppression of screening over ~1 um^2 requires much more energy
   than suppressing Ic in a ~100 nm wide constriction, where partial gap suppression suffices.
   Device D1 was fabricated to mimic transport GCS devices, yet no gate-dependent transport data
   from D1 (or from the same film) appear in the main text. Please show Ic(VG) and Ic(P) for D1
   in the same setup, ideally with simultaneous imaging. This would give the calibration that
   ties the imaging result to the GCS literature. Without it, the claim that the GCS is
   "established as a genuine feature of superconductivity not restricted to transport" is too
   strong. What is shown is that leakage-current dissipation suppresses the superconducting state
   locally, which most current mechanistic work on GCS would already predict.

5. Where is the power dissipated?
   The model assumes a point source of NEPs at the gate apex. Intrinsic Si freezes out at these
   temperatures, so the leakage path (oxide surface, through the oxide into the Si and back, or
   a filament) determines where the energy is released. That path could lie partly under the
   island. For field-emission-like transport, energy would be released mainly at the anode, which
   would predict a polarity asymmetry. The authors observe none and use this to rule out vacuum
   injection. Please discuss the leakage path explicitly (for example, from IL(VG) for the
   floating G4-G5 configuration, or by comparing devices) and what fraction of P is dissipated
   near the apex. Because P includes the 60 GOhm parasitic path, please also confirm that its
   contribution is negligible at the relevant VG (it appears to be <1 nA, but this should be
   stated).

6. Vortex history and what the images measure.
   A 3 um x 3 um island in B0 = 1 mT encloses B0*A/Phi0 ~ 4.4 flux quanta. The four vortices in
   the first panel of Fig. 2c are exactly what one expects after field cooling. Please state the
   cooling protocol (field-cooled or zero-field-cooled) for each dataset. Under field cooling, the
   reduced screening in the first panel and the much stronger screening in later panels are
   largely due to vortex expulsion, not to the GCS itself. This matters for (i) the qualitative
   comparison with the vortex-free simulations in Fig. 2d, and (ii) the Bx-step method, which
   uses a VG = 0 reference map that may contain vortices (Fig. 4 caption). Also, the expulsion of
   all vortices at small P, when only a small fraction of the island is suppressed, is what one
   would expect from global warming of the island that reduces pinning. Please compare with a
   control in which the bath temperature is raised with no gate power, and check whether
   vortices leave at a comparable bath temperature. The non-monotonic temperature dependence of
   the escape power (0.45 K, 3.5 K, 5.8 K) should be stated with the device and cool-down for
   each, since field history can dominate.

7. "Complete suppression" and the precision of r0.
   With lambda ~ 240 nm and d ~ 20 nm, the effective penetration depth lambda^2/d is ~2.9 um,
   comparable to the island size. Screening is therefore nonlocal, and the stray-field edge is
   broad. A region of strongly reduced, but nonzero, superfluid density would look nearly
   unscreened. A gradual suppression profile would also shift the apparent edge relative to a
   sharp disk model. Please (i) state "complete" only if a model with a smooth suppression profile
   (for example, lambda(r) diverging at r0) can be excluded, and (ii) show how sensitive the
   extracted r0 is to the assumed profile. Please also show line cuts of the measured Bz with
   model overlays: Fig. 2b suggests screening of several hundred uT, while the color scale in
   Fig. 2c spans +/-50 uT. Is the scale saturated, and what is the measured screening amplitude
   compared with the model?

8. Reproducibility across devices.
   The text states that the suppression "is reproducible given the same applied power P for all
   our devices", but the fitted Pd differs by almost a factor of three between D3 (0.95 uW) and
   D2b (0.36 uW), while rd is similar. Please reconcile this statement with the fits and discuss
   the origin of the difference (gap size, gate shape, oxide condition, cool-down). Please also
   give the uncertainties of rd and Pd.

9. Power drift during scans.
   For D2a/D2b, the scans used constant-current mode, and VG drifts over hours (Fig. S2). Only D3
   used the software constant-power loop. Please state the duration of a scan and how much P
   varied during each image. Also state whether the uncertainties in the Fig. 2c labels (e.g.,
   1.74(14) uW) are the spread over the scan.

MINOR COMMENTS

10. The abstract and introduction speak of a "nanoscale spatial region", while the suppressed
    region exceeds 1 um. Consider "micron-scale region imaged with nanoscale resolution".
11. The outlook says the technique works "over a temperature range from well below the
    superconducting gap to above the critical temperature of Nb". Temperature and gap energy are
    mixed here, and no above-Tc data appear in the main text. Please rephrase, and give the
    actual Tc of the films.
12. Fig. 3b: please state which temperature each color is, and whether the two branches
    correspond to different sweeps with different V_G^ON. The point that r0 tracks P rather than
    VG would be clearer with a plot of pairs of points at equal VG and different P.
13. Fig. 4c: the extended-gate argument ("current density lower for the same power") is
    plausible but qualitative. The E-field FEM maps in the top row show field, not current
    density. Please state what determines the current distribution in your model of leakage.
14. The "corner effect" (Fig. 4d) is attributed to "the relatively long penetration depth that
    smoothens sharp features". Please expand. In a thin film the relevant length is the Pearl
    length, and the island edge also changes the boundary condition for diffusion.
15. Ref. [11] (Kivelson and Rokhsar, spinons and spin-charge decoupling) is an unusual citation
    for "quasiparticles formed from broken Cooper pairs". A textbook reference would be more
    appropriate.
16. Ref. [13]: the DOI (s41567-023-...) and the year (2021) are inconsistent. Ref. [18] lacks a
    year. Ref. [21] should be updated if published.
17. Typos: "microcopy" (scanning tunneling microscopy), "enviroment", "the measured current
    include". In the acknowledgments, the naming of thanked colleagues is inconsistent.
18. Fig. 2c labels: "0.08(0) uW" is odd notation for an uncertainty below the last digit.
19. The Methods describe the SMU current as including wiring leakage. Please state at which VG
    the device leakage dominates for each device.
20. Data availability "upon reasonable request": deposition of the raw scans and fit scripts in
    a public repository would help, given that the mechanistic conclusions rest on model fits.

SUMMARY OF REQUESTS

Essential: a quantitative thermal-model comparison (1a), a discussion of the temperature
independence within the authors' own model (1b), alternative-geometry fits and at least one
parameter-free prediction (2), removal or qualification of the lifetime claim (3), transport GCS
data from D1 or the same film to connect the regimes (4), the cooling protocol and a
vortex/bath-temperature control (6), and a reconciliation of the device-to-device Pd difference (8).
With these, or with correspondingly moderated claims, the paper would be a useful contribution.
