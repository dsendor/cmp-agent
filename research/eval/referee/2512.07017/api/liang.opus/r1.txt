**Review outline**

---

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic and claim.** The manuscript studies second-harmonic (2ω) longitudinal and Hall voltages in MBE-grown V-doped/undoped/Cr-doped (Bi,Sb)₂Te₃ sandwiches tuned between C = ±1 QAH and axion-insulator (antiparallel) configurations. The central claim is that the large 2ω response near the quantized regimes is dominated by boundary modes rather than bulk quantum geometry. This addresses a timely open question: whether edge, surface or hinge channels contribute to nonlinear transport in nearly bulk-insulating topological materials.
- **Key observations.**
  - V²ω_xx,L and V²ω_xx,R have opposite signs, i.e., the signal depends on which edge is probed.
  - V²ω_yx ≈ V²ω_xx,R.
  - The signal reverses sign with carrier type and with Axion-I vs Axion-II alignment.
  - The signal nearly vanishes on the well-quantized QAH plateau and at the charge-neutral point.
- **Conceptual contribution.** The authors recast the symmetry analysis in terms of the electrode-indexed second-order conductance G⁽²⁾_ijk, rather than the bulk conductivity σ⁽²⁾_αβγ. Combined with C₂z geometry symmetry, this yields V²ω_xx,L = −V²ω_xx,R = −V²ω_yx. This is a useful framing that is rarely made explicit in the nonlinear-transport literature. It is complemented by nonlinear (Christen–Büttiker) Landauer–Büttiker simulations of a two-surface Dirac model.
- **Novelty relative to prior work is incremental and needs sharper delineation.**
  - Edge-mediated nonreciprocal transport in QAH magnetic TI heterostructures was already reported (Yasuda et al., Nat. Nanotechnol. 2020, Ref. 33).
  - Analogous results exist for quantum Hall states in TIs (Li et al., Nat. Mater. 2024, Ref. 43) and for MnBi₂Te₄ (Refs. 34, 35).
  - The genuinely new elements appear to be:
    - extension to the axion-insulator configuration;
    - the explicit left/right electrode sign-reversal test;
    - the G⁽²⁾-based symmetry argument.
  - Whether these elements suffice for a top-tier journal depends on how convincingly the boundary origin is separated from extrinsic mechanisms (see Section 3).

---

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **High-quality material platform.**
  - Well-quantized QAH: |σ_xy(0)| ≈ 1.001 e²/h, σ_xx(0) ≈ 4×10⁻⁴ e²/h.
  - Clear axion-insulator regime: σ_xx ≈ 0.22 e²/h, σ_xy ≈ 0.
  - Gate tunability across p- and n-type regimes in a single device.
  - Measurements span 25 mK to ~40 K, with dilution and PPMS data on multiple samples (S1–S4).
- **Systematic and internally consistent dataset.**
  - Opposite-sign left/right signals are observed at both 25 mK and 1.7 K, and in at least two samples (Extended Data Figs. 2, 9, 10).
  - The signal is frequency independent (≈7–107 Hz) and quadratic in current.
  - The signal disappears near T_C ≈ 20 K.
  - The V²ω_yx ≈ V²ω_xx,R correspondence is reproduced in several gate regimes.
- **Conceptually useful theoretical message.**
  - Second-order responses measured in a multi-terminal device are not determined by σ⁽²⁾ alone; the electrode configuration and sample geometry enter explicitly.
  - Consequently, a vanishing in-plane σ⁽²⁾ (e.g., under C₂z) does not imply a vanishing 2ω voltage.
  - This point is relevant well beyond magnetic TIs, for example for nonlinear Hall studies in other quantum materials.
- **Potential practical value.**
  - Electrode-resolved measurement (symmetric vs antisymmetric decomposition) could serve as a diagnostic for distinguishing boundary from bulk contributions.
  - The sign dependence on magnetic configuration offers an electrical readout of the antiparallel magnetic state.

---

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**3.1 Extrinsic mechanisms with the same phenomenology are not convincingly excluded**

- **Gate-voltage modulation / self-gating via the local Hall potential.**
  - The device uses an SrTiO₃ back gate, whose dielectric constant is very large at low temperature, and the channel carries AC potentials of order I·ρ.
  - In the dissipative regimes, ρ_yx is a sizable fraction of h/e² (e.g., ≈ −0.3 h/e² in the antiparallel window at 1.7 K). The two edges of the Hall bar therefore sit at different AC potentials (tens of mV at 2–5 µA). This modulates the local carrier density with opposite sign on the two edges.
  - Mixing this density modulation with the first-harmonic current yields 2ω longitudinal voltages that are:
    - opposite on the left and right edges;
    - odd in magnetization;
    - sign-reversed between p- and n-type (∝ ∂ρ/∂n);
    - maximal near the gap edges and vanishing at the charge-neutral point.
  - This is precisely the reported phenomenology.
  - The control in Extended Data Fig. 3 (grounding electrode 1 vs 4) mainly shifts the common-mode potential and does not test this edge-differential mechanism. The V²ω_yx curves in Extended Data Fig. 3f also visibly differ between the two grounding configurations.
  - A quantitative estimate is required, using measured ∂ρ_xx/∂V_g, ∂ρ_yx/∂V_g and the gate capacitance, together with experimental tests (see Section 4).
  - The Christen–Büttiker "characteristic potential" term ∂_eV_k T_ij, which the authors emphasize, is itself an electrostatic-response effect. The line between "intrinsic nonlinear boundary transport" and "gate-electrostatic modulation" must therefore be drawn explicitly.
- **Thermoelectric contributions from Joule heating.**
  - In QAH and axion regimes, dissipation concentrates at "hot spots" at diagonally opposite current-contact corners, set by edge chirality. Such hot spots are related by C₂z and flip with magnetization.
  - Combined with Nernst/anomalous-Nernst or Seebeck voltages at the probe contacts, they produce 2ω signals that are:
    - quadratic in I;
    - frequency independent at tens of Hz;
    - odd in M;
    - consistent with the C₂z relation.
  - Frequency independence and I² scaling therefore do not discriminate against heating.
  - The heating budget is non-negligible. At 25 mK with I = 2 µA and ρ_xx ≈ 4 h/e² (~100 kΩ), the dissipated power is ~0.4 µW, which can substantially raise the electron temperature in a dilution refrigerator. The statement that 2 µA "minimizes heating" needs to be substantiated.
- **Contact nonlinearity.**
  - Ohmic contacts are hand-pressed indium dots on uncapped ~10 nm films. Non-ohmic or asymmetric contact I–V characteristics can rectify.
  - In chiral-edge transport, the contact's role in edge equilibration is chirality dependent, so contact artifacts would also inherit M-odd and edge-dependent signs.
  - No DC I–V characteristics, two-terminal contact resistances, or DC rectification tests (±I_DC) are reported.

**3.2 The symmetry argument is generic and does not by itself establish a "boundary" origin**

- **The relation is mechanism-agnostic.**
  - V²ω_xx,L = −V²ω_xx,R = −V²ω_yx follows from the 2ω potential being C₂z invariant whenever the device (sample + contacts + gate) is C₂z symmetric and the drive is C₂z odd.
  - It holds for any C₂z-respecting nonlinear mechanism, including the thermoelectric and electrostatic ones in 3.1.
  - Verifying the relation thus confirms device symmetry, not boundary dominance. The claim that it "allows us to distinguish nonlinear boundary transport from bulk contributions" is overstated.
- **C₂z is not a crystalline symmetry of the system.**
  - (Bi,Sb)₂Te₃ is D₃d. The sandwich, with distinct Cr- and V-doped layers, breaks inversion, leaving at most C₃v-type symmetry, which does not contain C₂z.
  - Hexagonal warping and other C₃-symmetric terms allow nonzero in-plane bulk σ⁽²⁾ (e.g., σ_xxx = −σ_xyy). This is the mechanism behind bilinear magnetoresistance in TI surface states, which the authors themselves cite (Ref. 58).
  - The "quasi-symmetry" argument (Ref. 64) is asserted rather than quantified. The authors should:
    - estimate the warping-induced bulk/surface σ⁽²⁾ for realistic parameters;
    - compare that estimate with the measured magnitudes;
    - specify the Hall-bar orientation relative to the crystal axes;
    - discuss twin domains on SrTiO₃(111), which could restore an effective sixfold, and hence C₂z, symmetry on average. If so, this should be stated as the actual justification.
- **Internal inconsistency in interpreting V²ω_xx,S.**
  - Under the authors' own C₂z analysis, V²ω_xx,S = (V²ω_xx,R + V²ω_xx,L)/2 must vanish identically. A nonzero V²ω_xx,S therefore measures C₂z breaking, from device asymmetry (contact placement, hand-scratched geometry, inhomogeneity) or from the crystalline C₃ terms above.
  - Attributing V²ω_xx,S to quantum-metric bulk transport "consistent with MnBi₂Te₄" is not justified without independent evidence.
  - The sign reversal of V²ω_xx,S between Axion-I and Axion-II is automatic for any T-odd quantity and, moreover, is imposed by the field antisymmetrization (see 3.4).

**3.3 The theory–experiment connection is qualitative, and several microscopic claims lack experimental support**

- **The simulation regime is far from the experiment.**
  - Simulations use a 100 × 20-site ballistic tight-binding model, with arbitrary units and large exchange parameters (M_t = 1.2, M_b = 0.6 vs m₀ = 0.1).
  - No disorder, dephasing or dissipation is stated, whereas the experiment uses mm-scale, strongly diffusive devices (σ_xx ~ 0.2 e²/h in the axion state).
  - Inside the axion gap the simulated resistance diverges because transmission vanishes (Extended Data Fig. 8b). The simulated nonlinear voltages inside the gap (Fig. 4f,g, near-linear crossing) are therefore of unclear meaning.
  - There is no mapping between n_e and V_g, and no comparison of absolute magnitudes or line shapes.
  - The asymmetric p/n behavior requires adding ad hoc parabolic valence bands (Fig. S10).
- **The self-consistent potential term is under-specified.**
  - Equation (8) includes ∂_eV_k T_ij, which requires characteristic potentials and hence an electrostatic model (screening, gate capacitance, Lindhard/Thomas–Fermi response).
  - The main text and Methods do not explain how this term is computed, or whether the result is sensitive to the gate electrostatics. This bears directly on the self-gating concern in 3.1.
- **Microscopic attributions are asserted rather than demonstrated.**
  - "In-band boundary states buried in the metallic surface states" and "hinge currents" are invoked mainly because |V²ω| is larger in the slightly doped than in the highly doped regime.
  - That trend is generic for any response ∝ ∂σ/∂μ near a gap edge, so it does not single out boundary states.
  - Similarly, "requires assistance from bulk carriers" and "boundary–bulk interplay" are not formulated as falsifiable predictions, such as specific scalings with device width, length or edge length.
  - Spatially resolved current maps from the simulations, and separation of hinge vs surface vs bulk contributions in the computed G⁽²⁾, are relegated to the SI and not tied to measurable quantities.

**3.4 Data processing and quantitative claims need clarification**

- **Field (anti)symmetrization may manufacture key features.**
  - The Methods state that all magneto-transport data are symmetrized or antisymmetrized in field. For hysteretic, M-odd 2ω signals this procedure (pairing up and down sweeps) enforces V(H↑) = −V(−H↓).
  - As a result, the Axion-I/Axion-II sign reversal, presented as evidence that the response is "intrinsic to the axion state," and the V²ω_xx,S sign reversal are partly built in.
  - Any M-even 2ω component, which could reveal artifacts or thermal contributions, is discarded.
  - Raw, unsymmetrized data and the exact protocol are needed. A sign reversal under T (flipping all magnetizations) is in any case expected for any nonreciprocal T-odd response, so it does not by itself identify a topological origin.
- **The nonreciprocal coefficient γ and the "giant" comparisons are ill-defined.**
  - γ = √2 V²ω_xx,A/(R₀ i²) normalizes by a bulk current density, which is inconsistent with the claim that transport is boundary dominated: edge transport does not scale with cross-section.
  - The choice of R₀ (which resistance, which electrode pair), the thickness used for i, and uncertainties are not specified.
  - Comparisons of γ (1.22×10⁻⁹ m² A⁻¹) with WTe₂, MnBi₂Te₄ and HM/FM bilayers, claimed to be one and four orders of magnitude larger, conflate very different geometries and mechanisms. These comparisons, and "giant", should be either rigorously justified or toned down.
- **Reproducibility statistics and terminology are incomplete.**
  - Samples S3 and S4 appear only in the SI.
  - There is no statement on whether the sign relation (L vs R, p vs n) is the same in all devices, nor on the device-to-device magnitude spread.
  - The deviation from V²ω_yx = V²ω_xx,R at (V_g − V_g⁰) = −15 V (Fig. 2f) is not discussed.
  - "Néel order" is used for an uncompensated synthetic heterostructure, with different dopants and concentrations (Cr 0.26 vs V 0.11) and net magnetization. This terminology, and the implied analogy to compensated antiferromagnets such as even-layer MnBi₂Te₄, should be justified or revised.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Add decisive geometry-dependence experiments that discriminate boundary from bulk and from extrinsic effects.**
   - Fabricate lithographically defined Hall bars with deposited contacts and systematically varied width, length and contact spacing. Test whether V²ω scales with edge length rather than area.
   - Measure a Corbino (edgeless) geometry, where boundary-mediated 2ω should be suppressed.
   - Use nonlocal configurations.
   - Use deliberately C₂z-broken electrode layouts, to test the predicted violation of V_xx,L = −V_xx,R quantitatively.
   - Rotate the current direction relative to the crystal axes to bound C₃-symmetric (warping) bulk contributions.

2. **Quantitatively exclude the extrinsic mechanisms.**
   - *Self-gating:* estimate the self-gating contribution from the measured ∂ρ/∂V_g and the gate capacitance. Measure with the gate floating vs grounded through different impedances, at different gate-leakage and capacitance conditions, and with different SrTiO₃ thicknesses or a top gate.
   - *Thermal effects:* report both in-phase and out-of-phase lock-in components of the 2ω signal, since thermal signals carry a characteristic phase. Provide heating estimates and electron-temperature calibration from the first-harmonic response at 25 mK. Show current-dependence data at 25 mK, not only at 1.7 K.
   - *Contacts:* provide DC I–V curves and DC rectification (±I_DC) of each contact pair, and compare with V²ω.

3. **Make the data treatment transparent and the quantitative claims well-defined.**
   - Show raw, unsymmetrized up- and down-sweep data. State the (anti)symmetrization protocol and report the M-even 2ω component.
   - Provide error bars and multi-device statistics, moving S3 and S4 into the Extended Data.
   - Redefine or supplement γ with a normalization appropriate for boundary transport, for example V²ω/(I² R) per unit edge length, and justify or soften the literature comparisons.
   - Reinterpret V²ω_xx,S as a measure of symmetry breaking unless independent evidence supports a quantum-metric origin.
   - Revise the "Néel order" terminology for the uncompensated heterostructure.

4. **Strengthen the theory so it makes quantitative, falsifiable contact with experiment.**
   - Include disorder and dephasing (e.g., Büttiker probes) and use larger systems, so that the model approaches the diffusive, dissipative experimental regime.
   - Explain in the main text how the characteristic potentials and gate electrostatics enter ∂_eV_k T_ij, and test the sensitivity of the results to them.
   - Map n_e to V_g and compare absolute magnitudes and line widths with the data.
   - Compute the bulk/surface σ⁽²⁾ including hexagonal warping, to bound non-boundary contributions.
   - Present real-space current and potential maps, or channel-resolved decompositions, that isolate hinge vs surface vs bulk contributions to G⁽²⁾. Derive at least one geometry-scaling prediction that distinguishes the proposed "boundary–bulk interplay" from generic ∂σ/∂μ-type nonlinearities.
   - Reframe the C₂z relation as a necessary consistency check (device symmetry) rather than a sufficient discriminator of boundary transport.
