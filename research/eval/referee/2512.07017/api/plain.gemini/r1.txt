Here is a comprehensive review of the manuscript **"Boundary-Bulk Interplay in Nonlinear Topological Transport"** by D. Zhuo *et al.*

---

### **1. Summary of the Work**
In this manuscript, Zhuo *et al.* investigate second-harmonic nonlinear electrical transport ($V_{xx}^{2\omega}$ and $V_{yx}^{2\omega}$) in molecular beam epitaxy (MBE)-grown magnetic topological insulator (TI) sandwich heterostructures—specifically, V-doped $\text{(Bi,Sb)}_2\text{Te}_3$ / undoped $\text{(Bi,Sb)}_2\text{Te}_3$ / Cr-doped $\text{(Bi,Sb)}_2\text{Te}_3$. By tuning the external magnetic field and gate voltage, the authors navigate between well-quantized Quantum Anomalous Hall (QAH, $C = \pm 1$) states and Axion Insulator states (Axion-I and Axion-II, corresponding to opposite antiparallel magnetization alignments).

While recent literature on nonlinear topological transport has heavily focused on bulk quantum geometry (i.e., Berry curvature dipole and quantum metric dipole), the authors demonstrate that in nearly bulk-insulating regimes, **boundary modes dominate the nonlinear response**. Key findings include:
1. **Boundary-Bulk Interplay:** The nonlinear signals vanish inside the strict magnetic exchange gap ($V_g = V_g^0$) where bulk carriers are absent, but reach giant maxima when the chemical potential is tuned slightly into the bulk/surface bands. 
2. **Electrode Geometry Dependence:** The longitudinal second-harmonic voltages measured on opposite edges of the Hall bar have opposite signs ($V_{xx,L}^{2\omega} \approx -V_{xx,R}^{2\omega}$) and match the magnitude of the nonlinear Hall voltage ($V_{yx}^{2\omega} \approx V_{xx,R}^{2\omega}$).
3. **Symmetry & Theoretical Framework:** Because a 2D bulk conductivity tensor $\sigma_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{(2)}$ vanishes under two-fold rotation ($C_{2z}$), standard bulk theories cannot explain these results. By applying Neumann’s principle to the *multi-terminal conductance tensor* $G_{ijk}^{(2)}$ of a $C_{2z}$-symmetric 6-terminal Hall bar, the authors derive the universal relation $V_{xx,L}^{2\omega} = -V_{xx,R}^{2\omega} = -V_{yx}^{2\omega}$. This is further supported by numerical simulations using the nonlinear Landauer-Büttiker formalism.
4. **Readout of Néel Order & Giant Nonreciprocity:** The nonlinear signals flip sign between the Axion-I and Axion-II states (which are otherwise indistinguishable in linear transport) and achieve a massive nonreciprocal coefficient of $\gamma \sim 1.22 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}^2\text{A}^{-1}$.

---

### **2. Strengths and Novelty**

* **A Needed Paradigm Shift:** The field of nonlinear transport has recently been dominated by bulk band-structure interpretations (quantum metric and Berry curvature dipoles). This paper provides a crucial course correction by showing that in topological materials with nearly insulating bulks, boundary modes and **electrode geometry** play a primary role—an effect entirely absent in nonlinear optics.
* **Exceptional Material Quality and Experimental Rigor:** The MBE-grown sandwiches exhibit textbook quantization in the linear regime ($\sigma_{xy} \approx 1.001 e^2/h$, $\sigma_{xx} \approx 0.0004 e^2/h$ at $25\text{ mK}$). Furthermore, the authors are commendably thorough in ruling out experimental artifacts that often plague second-harmonic measurements, such as Joule heating, capacitive gate oscillations, and grounding asymmetries (Extended Data Figs. 3–5).
* **Elegant Theoretical Insight:** Shifting the symmetry analysis from the local conductivity tensor $\sigma_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{(2)}$ to the global, electrode-dependent conductance matrix $G_{ijk}^{(2)}$ is a major highlight of the paper. The derivation of $V_{xx,L}^{2\omega} = -V_{xx,R}^{2\omega} = -V_{yx}^{2\omega}$ is model-independent and relies solely on the $C_{2z}$ symmetry of the device geometry and the low-energy Hamiltonian. This provides a simple, universal tool for future experiments to separate boundary-dominated nonlinear transport from bulk contributions (via symmetrization $V_{xx,S}^{2\omega}$ and antisymmetrization $V_{xx,A}^{2\omega}$).
* **Practical Applications:** Demonstrating that second-harmonic transport can distinguish between outward (Axion-I) and inward (Axion-II) antiparallel magnetizations provides a direct electrical readout for antiferromagnetic Néel order in axion insulators.

---

### **3. Critique and Questions for the Authors**

While the manuscript is of very high quality and suitable for a top-tier journal (such as *Nature* or *Nature Physics*), addressing the following points would further strengthen the clarity and impact of the work:

1. **Clarifying "2D Surface Bulk" vs. "3D Bulk" in the Interplay:**
   In a 3D magnetic TI thin film, there are 1D boundary modes (chiral edge or helical hinge states), 2D gapped surface states, and 3D bulk valence/conduction bands. 
   * In the Landauer-Büttiker simulations (Eq. 5, Fig. 4), a "two-surface-state model" is used. Here, the "bulk" that assists the 1D boundary transport is actually the 2D gapped Dirac surface states. 
   * However, on pages 9 and 12, the strong asymmetry between the $p$-doped and $n$-doped regimes is attributed to the 3D bulk valence band maximum lying closer to the magnetic exchange gap. 
   * *Suggestion:* It would be helpful for the general reader if the authors explicitly distinguish between the role of the *2D surface states* (which drive the symmetric peaks at the gap edges in the minimal model) and the *3D bulk bands* (which introduce the $p/n$ asymmetry).

2. **Physical Intuition Behind the Nonlinear Landauer-Büttiker Results:**
   On page 12, the authors note that the nonlinear signals in the axion insulator regime are much larger than in the QAH regime *"due to a larger transmission probability derivative, e.g., $\partial_E T_{ij}$ and $\partial_{V_k} T_{ij}$."* While mathematically true within the Christen-Büttiker formalism, this is somewhat opaque physically. 
   * *Suggestion:* Can the authors provide a brief, intuitive physical picture in the main text for *why* $\partial_E T_{ij}$ is much sharper in the Axion state than in the QAH state? For instance, does it relate to the onset of strong backscattering between the counter-propagating helical hinge/in-band boundary modes and the surface states, as opposed to the topologically protected chiral edge state in the QAH regime (similar to the mechanism discussed by Yasuda *et al.*, Ref. 33)?

3. **Origin of the Symmetric Component ($V_{xx,S}^{2\omega}$) and Quasi-Symmetry:**
   In the Methods (page 16), the authors rightly note that $C_{2z}$ is a *quasi-symmetry* of the low-energy $\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{p}$ Dirac Hamiltonian, whereas the actual crystal structure of $\text{(Bi,Sb)}_2\text{Te}_3$ has three-fold rotational symmetry ($C_{3z}$). 
   * In Fig. 2b and 2d, a small but finite electrode-independent signal ($V_{xx,S}^{2\omega}$) is observed and attributed to the quantum metric dipole (QMD) of the bulk. 
   * *Suggestion:* It would tie the theoretical narrative together nicely to explicitly state in the main text that while the dominant $V_{xx,A}^{2\omega}$ is governed by the approximate $C_{2z}$ symmetry of the low-energy boundary/surface modes, the residual $V_{xx,S}^{2\omega}$ is allowed precisely because the underlying crystal lattice breaks $C_{2z}$ down to $C_{3z}$ (via trigonal warping at higher energies) alongside the broken $P$ and $PT$ symmetries of the antiparallel sandwich.

4. **Divergence in Extended Data Fig. 8b:**
   In Extended Data Fig. 8b, the calculated linear resistances $R_{xx}^\omega$ and $R_{yx}^\omega$ diverge inside the gap for the axion insulator state, yet the calculated second-order voltages in Fig. 4f,g smoothly go to zero at $n_e = 0$ (even though Eq. 10 relies on inverting $\tilde{G}^{(1)}$ to get $\tilde{R}^{(1)}$, which diverges). 
   * *Suggestion:* A brief comment in the Methods or caption explaining how the numerical regularization in the zero-transmission limit of the Axion gap was handled to yield $V^{2\omega} = 0$ would be useful for readers looking to reproduce the KWANT simulations.

---

### **4. Conclusion and Recommendation**
This is an outstanding manuscript that combines state-of-the-art MBE material synthesis, ultra-low-temperature magnetotransport, and a clever, generalizable symmetry analysis. By proving that boundary modes and electrode configurations dictate the giant nonlinear responses in slightly doped QAH and Axion insulators, Zhuo *et al.* resolve open questions regarding the role of topological edges/hinges in nonlinear transport and establish a robust methodology for future studies. 

**Recommendation:** The paper is a strong candidate for publication in a high-impact journal following minor revisions to clarify the physical intuition and terminology as outlined above.
