Referee report

Manuscript: "Boundary-Bulk Interplay in Nonlinear Topological Transport"
(MBE-grown V-doped (Bi,Sb)2Te3 / (Bi,Sb)2Te3 / Cr-doped (Bi,Sb)2Te3 sandwiches)


1. Summary of the claims

The authors measure second-harmonic longitudinal and Hall voltages in six-terminal,
millimetre-scale Hall bars of magnetic TI sandwiches that show a well-quantized C = +/-1
QAH state and an antiparallel ("axion insulator") state. They report:

(a) The 2-omega longitudinal voltages on the two edges, V_xx,L = V6 - V5 and
    V_xx,R = V2 - V3, have opposite signs. Their signs also depend on carrier type and
    on the magnetization configuration (Axion-I vs Axion-II, C = +1 vs -1).
(b) The signals vanish at charge neutrality in both the QAH and axion states. They are
    largest slightly away from it, and they vanish near T_C ~ 20 K.
(c) A symmetry argument at the level of terminal conductances G(1)_ij and G(2)_ijk. For a
    C2z-symmetric six-terminal device driven 1 -> 4 it gives V_xx,L = -V_xx,R = -V_yx.
    The authors call this a "universal relation" that separates boundary from bulk
    nonlinear transport.
(d) Nonlinear Landauer-Buttiker (Christen-Buttiker) simulations of a two-surface Dirac
    model in KWANT reproduce the relation and some qualitative trends.
(e) A nonreciprocal coefficient gamma ~ 1.2e-9 m^2/A. The authors call this an order of
    magnitude larger than in MnBi2Te4 and WTe2 flakes and four orders larger than in
    HM/FM bilayers.

The headline conclusion is that "boundary mode transport" is the dominant source of the
"giant" nonlinear response in nearly bulk-insulating topological materials.

The samples are good. The zero-field QAH state is well quantized (sigma_xy(0) ~ 1.001
e^2/h, sigma_xx(0) ~ 4e-4 e^2/h). The measurements were made down to 25 mK on more than one
sample, and the basic controls are present: frequency independence, quadratic current
scaling, a swap of the ground electrode, and temperature dependence. The data are deposited
on Zenodo. Second-order transport in the antiparallel magnetic TI configuration is a timely
topic. The observation that the 2-omega signal reverses between opposite edges, and that
the 2-omega Hall and longitudinal voltages track each other, is interesting and worth
publishing in some form.

My main concern is with the interpretation. The central argument shows that the response is
even under C2z, and therefore antisymmetric across the bar. It does not show that the
response comes from topological boundary modes. Several mundane mechanisms satisfy the same
"universal relation", and the paper does not exclude them. The "giant" comparison also rests
on a normalization that is dimensionally questionable and inconsistent with the paper's own
picture. Details follow.


2. Major issues

2.1 The C2z relation does not tell boundary-mode transport apart from other C2z-even
mechanisms.

The derivation in Methods (Eqs. 1-4) is correct and simple. Any C2z-symmetric device driven
by a C2z-odd current has a C2z-even 2-omega voltage vector. Together with
V_xx,L = V6 - V5, V_xx,R = V2 - V3 and V_yx = V2 - V6, this gives the stated identities.
But the result is a statement about the symmetry of the whole device. It does not depend on
the microscopic origin, which the authors themselves stress ("does not rely on the
microscopic model or whether the transport is diffusive or ballistic"). The relation
therefore holds for any second-order source that respects C2z, whether or not it is
localized at edges or hinges. Three examples follow.

(i) Thermoelectric response to Joule heating. Joule power is proportional to I^2, so its
    thermal response appears at 2-omega. Contact heating at the two current electrodes
    (pressed indium dots, with possibly large contact resistance at mK) produces a
    temperature profile that is even under C2z. Its gradient dT/dx is odd in x. Through
    the anomalous Nernst effect, E_y = N m_z dT/dx, this gives a transverse 2-omega
    voltage that is odd in x. A short calculation shows that such a profile gives exactly
    V_xx,R = -V_xx,L = V_yx with V_xx,S = 0, which is the pattern in Figs. 2 and 4. The
    phenomenology also fits. Through the Mott relation, an anomalous Nernst signal goes as
    d(sigma_xy)/d(mu). That quantity vanishes on a quantized plateau, changes sign between
    n- and p-type doping, is odd in magnetization, and is largest just off the plateau.
    These are the observations listed in (b) and (a). The thermal mechanism is also
    compatible with frequency independence from 6 to 107 Hz and with I^2 scaling, so
    those two checks do not exclude it. The text says the 2 uA current was chosen "to
    enhance the signal-to-noise ratio and minimize heating effects". That is contradictory,
    since a larger current increases heating. The authors should estimate the anomalous
    Nernst and Nernst contributions quantitatively, or exclude them experimentally.
    Possible tests: a frequency sweep high enough to reach the thermal roll-off of the
    film/STO system; dc current-reversal measurements; an independent measurement of the
    thermoelectric coefficients with an on-chip heater; and current injection through
    different contact pairs, where the thermal and boundary-mode pictures predict
    different patterns.

(ii) Self-gating through the STO back gate. At 25 mK the four-terminal voltages in the
    axion regime are a sizable fraction of a volt (rho_xx ~ 4.5 h/e^2 at 2 uA). The
    conductance changes on a scale of a few volts of gate bias (Figs. 1c, 1d, 3). The
    internal potential drop along and across the bar therefore modulates the local carrier
    density in proportion to I. A rough estimate (d ln sigma / dV_g ~ 0.2 V^-1, internal
    potential variation ~0.1 V) gives a second-order voltage of order 1e-2 times V^omega,
    which is at or above the observed 1e-4 to 1e-3 level. The part of this effect that
    depends on the spatial profile, rather than on the common-mode potential, respects C2z
    and satisfies the same relation. The ground-swap test in Extended Data Fig. 3 removes
    only the common-mode part. Even so, it shows a visible (~20%) change in V_yx between
    grounding electrode 1 and grounding electrode 4. It was also done only at 1.7 K and one
    gate voltage, where the resistances and the STO permittivity differ from the 25 mK
    axion regime where the main claims are made. In the Landauer-Buttiker language this
    effect is the d_{eV_k} T_ij term of Eq. 8. Its size depends on the electrostatic
    environment, including the gate, which is a seventh terminal that the C2z analysis
    leaves out. The paper should (1) say how the gate enters the symmetry analysis and the
    simulation, (2) repeat the ground-swap test at 25 mK in the Axion-II state, and
    (3) ideally compare with a device that has a thin, low-permittivity gate dielectric or a
    floating gate.

(iii) Multidomain states and domain-wall channels. In Fig. 1b and Extended Data Fig. 1a the
    "axion" window has no flat zero-Hall plateau. rho_xx peaks at ~4.4 h/e^2, and sigma_xy
    rises gradually from ~0 at 0.25 T to 1 at ~0.75 T. The second layer is therefore
    switching gradually through a multidomain state over most of the field range where
    V^2omega is large (Figs. 1e, 2b, 2d). Domain-wall chiral channels are boundary modes of
    a different kind, and they can produce edge-asymmetric nonreciprocity. The paper argues
    that random domains give zero net response. That argument does not apply when the
    domain configuration is set by a field history, and the same mechanism is invoked later
    to explain the spikes. To exclude domains, the authors should show V^2omega in the
    remanent antiparallel state at mu0H = 0 after minor-loop preparation, its time
    stability, and its insensitivity to small field excursions. The phrase "robust against
    external magnetic field perturbations" needs these data behind it.

All three mechanisms are compatible with "electrode-configuration dependence". The
statements that the data "establish boundary mode transport as the dominant contributor" and
that the relation "allows us to distinguish nonlinear boundary transport from bulk
contributions" are not justified as written. What the data establish is a C2z-even
response that is antisymmetric across the bar. A more direct test of boundary dominance
would vary the geometry. Examples: Hall bars of different widths and aspect ratios (edge
and bulk contributions scale differently); a Corbino or edge-free geometry; nonlocal
configurations. As far as I can tell, all four samples are millimetre-scale scratched bars
of similar geometry.

2.2 C2z is not a crystal symmetry. Its use to suppress the bulk needs justification.

The (Bi,Sb)2Te3 family has a threefold axis along z. With out-of-plane magnetization and
the structural asymmetry of the sandwich (V vs Cr layers, substrate vs vacuum), the
relevant point group contains C3z but not C2z. C3z allows an in-plane second-order
conductivity, for example the trigonal sigma_xxx = -sigma_xyy = -sigma_yxy from hexagonal
warping. Its value depends on the angle between the current and the crystal axes. The
Methods argue that C2z is a quasi-symmetry of the k.p model near the gap. That may hold
deep in the gap, but the signals are largest away from it, where warping grows. Ref. 58,
cited to support suppression of the bulk by "P or C2z", is a hexagonal-warping-driven
nonlinear effect, which if anything points the other way. The authors should:

- state the orientation of the Hall bar relative to the crystal axes;
- say whether the films have rotational twin domains, which would average the trigonal
  term out and could restore an effective C2z (a plausible, testable argument they could
  make);
- estimate the warping-induced bulk sigma(2) at the relevant Fermi energies and compare it
  with the measured V_S.

2.3 The interpretation of V_xx,S as a quantum-metric bulk response is not supported, and it
is in tension with the main argument.

In a C2z-symmetric device V_xx,S vanishes identically. A nonzero V_S therefore measures C2z
breaking, from the crystal or from the device. With pressed indium contacts on a scratched
bar, the probe positions are almost certainly not C2z images of one another at the 10%
level. Probe misalignment mixes the large V_A (and Hall-like) signal into V_S. In Fig. 2b,
Fig. 2d and Extended Data Figs. 7b and 7d, V_S has the same field and gate shape as V_A at a
fraction of its amplitude, which is what geometric mixing would produce. The sign reversal
of V_S between Axion-I and Axion-II is also not specific to the quantum metric. Axion-I and
Axion-II are related by time reversal, so any magnetization-odd contribution reverses sign.
In addition, the PT symmetry that underlies the quantum-metric interpretation in MnBi2Te4
(Ref. 28) is absent in this structurally asymmetric sandwich. I would ask the authors to
estimate the geometric mixing (for example by correlating V_S with V_A, and with the
linear-response Hall pickup in the longitudinal channels) before assigning V_S to
quantum-metric bulk transport. If V_S is a genuine bulk sigma(2), then C2z is broken and the
clean assignment of V_A to boundary modes has to be revisited.

Related point: under C2z, V_yx and V_xx,R are the same quantity. The "nonlinear Hall" and
"nonreciprocal longitudinal" responses are therefore not independent observations here, and
Figs. 2f-j test C2z rather than add a second phenomenon. Where the relation fails, it fails
substantially. At (V_g - V_g0) = -15 V, V_yx(0.25 T) = -70.8 uV while V_xx,A(0.25 T) =
-36.3 uV, a factor of about two (Figs. 2a, 2f). The authors should quantify the deviation
across gate voltage and say what it implies for the boundary assignment in those regimes.

2.4 The gamma comparison and the "giant" claim.

(i) As printed, gamma = sqrt(2) V_A / (R_0 i^2), with i a current density, has units of
    m^4/A (if R_0 is a resistance and i is in A/m^2), not the stated m^2/A. Taking the
    film thickness as 10 nm and the width as 0.5 mm, I can reproduce the quoted 1.2e-9
    m^2/A only with the form sqrt(2) V_A / (R_0 I i) and R_0 ~ 1e5 ohm. Please give the
    exact formula, the values of R_0, I, i, the cross-section used, and which probe pair
    R_0 refers to.
(ii) More fundamentally, normalizing by a bulk current density assumes uniform bulk
    current. That contradicts the paper's own picture of edge- and hinge-dominated
    transport. With that normalization gamma scales with the cross-sectional area. A
    millimetre-wide film is roughly 100 times wider than the micrometre flakes in Refs. 28
    and 48, so the "order of magnitude larger" comparison may be largely geometric. The
    comparison with HM/FM metallic bilayers, whose resistivities differ by many orders of
    magnitude, is not informative. I recommend either dropping the cross-material gamma
    comparison or replacing it with a geometry-independent figure of merit, for example
    V^2omega / V^omega at a fixed V^omega, or a per-edge-channel quantity. Without that,
    "giant" should be removed from the abstract and conclusions.

2.5 Temperature dependence does not point to topological boundary modes.

The signal persists up to about T_C ~ 20 K (Extended Data Fig. 6). By then the QAH state
is far from quantized: at 1.7 K, rho_yx is already ~0.8 h/e^2 with rho_xx ~0.4 h/e^2, and by
10 K rho_yx is a small fraction of h/e^2. Yet once the I^2 scaling is taken into account,
the signal at 1.7 K is only about 2.5 times smaller than at 25 mK. At (V_g - V_g0) = -5 V,
V_xx,R is about 250 uV at 5 uA and 1.7 K, or about 40 uV scaled to 2 uA, against about
100 uV at 2 uA and 25 mK. Over the same range, quantization degrades sharply. A response
that follows the magnetization up to T_C, rather than the
exchange gap or the quantization, fits a magnetization-odd transport or thermoelectric
mechanism better than gapped chiral-edge or hinge modes. Please plot normalized
|V_A|/(I^2) against T together with rho_yx(T), rho_xx(T) and, if available, the activation
gap. Please also discuss why a boundary-mode mechanism should survive into a regime where
no boundary mode is well defined.

2.6 Simulations are qualitative and miss key ingredients.

- The KWANT system is 100 x 20 sites with exchange terms (M_t = 1.2, M_b = 0.6) an order
  of magnitude larger than the hybridization (m_0 = 0.1). It appears to be clean and
  phase-coherent, which is why the axion-state resistance diverges in the gap (Extended
  Data Fig. 8b). The experiment is a millimetre-scale diffusive film with
  sigma_xx ~ 0.22 e^2/h in the axion state. Please state whether disorder is included and
  how the conclusions depend on system size and disorder.
- The self-consistent potential U(r) that enters d_{eV_k} T_ij, which the authors say is
  needed for gauge invariance, is not specified: neither the screening model nor the gate
  coupling. As argued in 2.1(ii), this term may dominate, so its treatment is central.
  Please also show the two terms of Eq. 8 separately.
- The comparison is in arbitrary units against carrier density in e/a^2. A nontrivial
  check would be an explicit sign comparison for each state and carrier type (for example,
  does the simulated V_xx,R in Axion-II on the p side have the measured sign?). Please add
  a table of the predicted and measured signs.
- The statement that hinge currents "play a substantial role" is deferred to the SI.
  Please put the supporting quantity (for example the fraction of the 2-omega response
  carried by hinge versus surface states) in the main text.

2.7 Novelty relative to prior edge-state nonreciprocity work.

The introduction says the boundary contribution "has remained elusive" and is "largely
unexplored". Yet Refs. 33-35 and 43 report nonreciprocal transport mediated by QAH chiral
edge states and by quantum Hall states. Please state clearly what is new here: the
antiparallel/axion configuration, the terminal-level symmetry relation, the edge-resolved
sign reversal. Please also say whether the earlier works measured both edges. The statement
that MnBi2Te4 flakes do not show an L/R sign reversal needs a reference showing that both
edges were measured there.


3. Minor issues

- Eq. 5 / Methods: M_minus is defined as (M_t + M_b)/2, the same as M_plus. Presumably
  M_minus = (M_t - M_b)/2.
- The data processing should be stated explicitly. The Methods say that "all
  magneto-transport results are symmetrized or anti-symmetrized". Please specify which
  operation is applied to each 2-omega quantity, and how it is applied to hysteretic data
  (pairing up-sweep and down-sweep branches). Antisymmetrization with respect to (H, M)
  builds the magnetization-odd sign reversal into the data. Raw, unsymmetrized traces for
  at least one panel of Figs. 1e and 2 should be in the SI.
- "Neel order" for a bilayer of two different ferromagnets with different moments and
  coercivities is loose terminology. The net moment is not zero. "Antiparallel
  configuration" would be more accurate. Likewise, the remark in the introduction that the
  antiparallel alignment breaks P is misleading, since the V/Cr sandwich on STO breaks P
  structurally in every configuration.
- The axion state here has no zero-Hall plateau: rho_xx ~ 4.5 h/e^2 and
  sigma_xx ~ 0.22 e^2/h. "Nearly bulk-insulating axion insulator" overstates it. Please
  qualify it, or show a better-developed axion state in another sample.
- Current-dependence data (Extended Data Fig. 5) are shown only at 1.7 K. Please show the
  25 mK current dependence, the third-harmonic signal (as a heating diagnostic), and the
  QAH breakdown current of these devices relative to the 2 uA used.
- The statement that domain scattering "cannot" explain the signal (p. 6) and the later
  attribution of the spikes to domain scattering (p. 8) should be reconciled.
- Please give the probe positions and contact sizes, at least approximately, so that
  readers can judge how close the devices are to C2z.
- Add a table of all samples (S1-S4) with V_g0, quantization quality, maximum V_A and
  V_yx, the temperatures measured, and whether the L/R relation holds.
- The "wireless rectifier" outlook does not follow from a millikelvin, millimetre-scale,
  microvolt effect and could be shortened.


4. Assessment

The measurements are careful and the edge-antisymmetric second-harmonic response in QAH
and antiparallel magnetic TI sandwiches is a useful observation. The terminal-level
symmetry relation is a clean organizing principle for experiments. However, the central
interpretive claims do not yet follow from the evidence. These are that boundary modes
dominate, that the relation separates boundary from bulk contributions, that V_S is a
quantum-metric bulk response, and that the response is "giant". The symmetry argument cannot
tell boundary-mode transport apart from other C2z-even mechanisms, in particular anomalous
Nernst responses to contact Joule heating, gate self-gating, and multidomain/domain-wall
transport. The gamma comparison is ill-posed. I recommend major revision. The authors
should add discriminating experiments (geometry and width dependence, thermal controls, the
25 mK ground-swap test, remanent-state measurements) and reframe the conclusions around what
the symmetry analysis actually establishes.
