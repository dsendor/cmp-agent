Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Problem addressed.** The paper tackles a long-standing ambiguity in twisted bilayer graphene (TBG): whether the ρ ∼ T^α behaviour (α between 1 and 2) comes from electron–phonon (e-ph) scattering, umklapp electron–electron (e-e) scattering, strange-metal physics, or renormalized Fermi-liquid physics.
- **Core experimental idea.** CW 0.14 THz irradiation is used as a "hot-electron knob." It raises the electron temperature T_e while the lattice temperature T_L stays near 2 K. A positive photoresistance Δρ_THz then flags a resistivity that depends on T_e rather than T_L.
- **Headline claims:**
  - (i) A giant positive photoresistance, up to ~70% in magic-angle TBG (MATBG), persists even in the linear-in-T (Planckian-slope) regime. The authors argue against phonon-limited interpretations.
  - (ii) In a clean 1.58° device, T² resistivity survives at |ν| ≈ 0.1 and ≈ 3.8. These fillings lie outside the kinematic window for intervalley umklapp, and Baber/electron–hole channels are argued to be absent.
  - (iii) This T² is attributed to normal intervalley ("interpocket") collisions between trigonally warped, non-Galilean-invariant Dirac pockets. The magnitude is estimated via an adaptation of the Sharma–Principi–Maslov theory (Eq. 1, Λ = 0.01–1).
- **Broader relevance.** If substantiated, the work would:
  - provide a model-light discriminator between e-e and e-ph contributions in moiré systems;
  - link TBG to the puzzle of umklapp-free T² resistivity in dilute metals (SrTiO₃, Bi₂O₂Se, HgTe);
  - be of broad interest.
- **Novelty relative to prior work.** Novelty should be weighed against the authors' own prior THz photoconductivity work (refs. 49, 50) and against Hubmann et al. (refs. 36, 37), who already reported THz/IR photoresistance in TBG near the magic angle with bolometric interpretations. The genuinely new elements are:
  - the use of the photoresponse as a diagnostic of the scattering mechanism;
  - the umklapp-free T² analysis.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Simple and general experimental concept.** Decoupling T_e from T_L is conceptually transparent and, in principle, applicable to many low-density 2D materials. The dual-modulation lock-in scheme is a clean way to extract Δρ directly.
- **Rich phenomenology across twist angles.** The data span 1.04°, 1.05°, 1.58°, ~2° and up to 3° devices.
  - The sign structure of Δρ_THz is clear and informative: negative at the charge neutrality point (CNP), at ν = ±4 and at ν ≈ 2; positive in metallic regions.
  - This makes Δρ_THz a useful map of insulating-like versus metallic-like regions.
- **High-quality 1.58° device.** Metallic conduction persists down to ~10¹¹ cm⁻², which allows a careful test of umklapp kinematic thresholds. It is a regime rarely accessed in TBG.
- **Quantitative theory component.** The interpocket e-e mechanism is concrete and grounded in established non-Galilean Fermi-liquid theory. It is accompanied by explicit expressions (Eqs. 1–6) and an order-of-magnitude comparison with the measured T² prefactor (~0.1 Ω/K²).
- **Timely and potentially high-impact message.** The result would reframe the linear-in-T debate in MATBG and connect to a broader class of "mysterious" T² metals.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**Reason 1 — Positive photoresistance is not, by itself, proof of current-relaxing e-e scattering.** The central inference "Δρ_THz > 0 ⇒ interaction-limited conductivity" is not logically closed.
- **Other T_e-dependent mechanisms are not excluded:**
  - Temperature-dependent screening of charged impurities (Das Sarma–Hwang type). In dilute 2D systems this yields a substantial, often linear-in-T resistivity controlled by T_e/T_F, with no e-e momentum relaxation.
  - Sommerfeld smearing of energy-dependent impurity scattering near van Hove singularities and flat-band edges.
  - Thermal smearing of the density of states, which shifts the chemical potential at fixed n.
  - In MATBG specifically, the T_e-driven isospin Pomeranchuk effect and cascade physics near ν ≈ 1.7–2 (Saito et al.; Rozen et al.). These change ρ through entropy-driven isospin reordering rather than through scattering.
  - Hydrodynamic/viscous corrections in clean channels, demonstrated in the authors' own ref. 50. These can give Δρ of either sign.
  - None of these is discussed. All would produce Δρ_THz ≠ 0 at fixed T_L.
- **The "negligible e-ph" argument rests on an inadequate control:**
  - In monolayer graphene (MLG) at 2–17 K, ρ(T) is already nearly flat. Absence of photoresistance therefore says nothing about how a T_e-dependent e-ph channel behaves in TBG.
  - TBG has much smaller v_F and k_F, and hence a Bloch–Grüneisen temperature T_BG of only a few to ~20 K at the relevant densities. For example, at ν = 0.1 for 1.58°, T_BG ≈ 2ħk_F s/k_B is ~20 K.
  - TBG also has enhanced, umklapp-assisted e-ph coupling (ref. 34) and possible phason/gauge-phonon channels.
  - In the regime T_L < T_BG < T_e, spontaneous phonon emission by hot electrons yields a momentum-relaxation rate that depends explicitly on T_e. A quantitative estimate of ρ_eph(T_e, T_L = 2 K) for TBG is required before the e-ph contribution can be called "negligible."
- **No quantitative decomposition is shown.** The abstract promises a "direct separation" of e-e and e-ph contributions, but the data are qualitative.
  - There is no plot comparing Δρ_THz(n) with ρ(T*) − ρ(2 K), where T* is the inferred T_e.
  - Only such a comparison would show what fraction of the equilibrium T-dependence is T_e-controlled.
  - The "70%" figure is Δρ/ρ. That is not the same as showing that 100% of dρ/dT is electronic.

**Reason 2 — Electron and lattice temperature calibration is insufficient and partly circular.**
- **Circular T_e estimate:**
  - T_e (up to ~15 K above T_L) is partly inferred by matching Δρ_THz to equilibrium ρ(T). This presupposes that ρ depends only on T_e, which is exactly the hypothesis under test.
  - The independent SdHO-damping estimate is relegated to the SI. It is unclear whether it was performed on the same TBG devices, at the same fillings, and in the same power range. Fig. 2f shows SdHO only for MLG.
  - Independent thermometry is needed on the TBG devices themselves, for example Johnson-noise thermometry, SdHO in TBG, or the critical temperature/current of superconducting MATBG as an internal thermometer.
- **"Cold lattice" is asserted, not demonstrated:**
  - CW illumination at 2 K deposits power into the TBG and also into the graphite back gate, the Ti/Au contacts, the bow-tie/triangular antenna, and the hBN.
  - Graphite gates and metal antennas absorb THz efficiently. Heating of the local phonon bath and substrate, i.e., a rise in T_L, would mimic an e-ph-driven Δρ.
  - Two observations deserve analysis: the sub-linear power dependence with saturation (Fig. 2d), and the fact that P_max and the absorbed power are not given in absolute units.
  - Without an energy-balance estimate (absorbed power, e-ph cooling power, and T_L rise through the hBN/graphite stack), the central "T_L fixed" premise is unverified.
- **Spatial inhomogeneity is ignored:**
  - Antenna coupling produces strongly non-uniform near fields, concentrated near contacts.
  - Contact cooling over the cooling length makes T_e non-uniform along the channel.
  - The quantity extracted is therefore a weighted average over an inhomogeneous T_e profile. This complicates any quantitative comparison with ρ(T).

**Reason 3 — The "umklapp-free T²" evidence at |ν| ≈ 0.1 and 3.8 is not yet compelling.**
- **The exponent extraction is fragile:**
  - Fig. 3b,c covers only about one decade (≈10–100 K) after subtracting a chosen ρ₀. The apparent exponent is highly sensitive to the choice of ρ₀.
  - A crossover from Bloch–Grüneisen e-ph behaviour (T⁴ or higher below T_BG ≈ 10–20 K) to equipartition behaviour (∝ T) naturally produces an apparent T² over exactly this window.
  - The fits extend to T ≈ T_F/2, where Fermi-liquid T² theory, including Eq. 1, which is explicitly a low-T limit, is not expected to hold.
  - Local exponents d ln Δρ / d ln T versus T, and fits with alternative functional forms, should be shown.
- **The photoresistance diagnostic is not applied where it matters most:**
  - Δρ_THz is shown for 2° and MATBG devices, but not at |ν| ≈ 0.1 or ≈ 3.8 in the 1.58° device.
  - These are precisely the fillings where the anomalous T² is claimed to be electronic. This is a key missing link between the two halves of the paper.
  - The inset of Fig. 2a even shows negative Δρ near the CNP of the 2° device. This raises the question of what the sign is at ν = 0.1.
- **Alternative channels near the CNP and band edge are dismissed too quickly:**
  - With E_F ≈ 20 meV (~230 K) and T up to 100 K, thermally activated minority carriers (∼e^(−E_F/T) ≈ 0.1) are not negligible. Electron–hole friction or drag (refs. 41, 42) could contribute T-dependent resistivity.
  - Charge-puddle inhomogeneity (δn ~ 10¹⁰ cm⁻²) at n ~ 10¹¹ cm⁻² could likewise matter.
  - Thermally smeared umklapp just below threshold gives activated contributions, roughly exp(−Δ/T) with Δ ~ 20 meV. These can masquerade as a power law over one decade.
  - Near ν = 3.8, scattering into remote bands (Baber-like inter-band processes involving the second miniband or γ-point pockets) is not addressed.
- **The kinematic window is internally inconsistent:**
  - The umklapp range quoted for the hole-side band (−3.5 < ν < 0.5) extends to positive ν. This appears to be an error; the window should be roughly symmetric to the electron side (0.45 < ν < 3.45).
  - The thresholds k_F > g/(4√3) and k_F > g/4 assume circular pockets. With strong trigonal warping, as in Fig. 3e,f, the actual threshold shifts.
  - Hall density (Fig. 3d) is used as a proxy for k_F, but n_H departs from n near van Hove singularities.

**Reason 4 — The theoretical explanation is only weakly constrained and of uncertain applicability.**
- **The agreement is not a sharp test:**
  - Λ spans two orders of magnitude (0.01–1), so A spans 0.005–0.5 Ω/K².
  - The "best correspondence" value Λ ≈ 0.2–0.3 is effectively a fit parameter.
  - Agreement "within the theoretical range" therefore does not discriminate this mechanism from others, such as umklapp tails or two-phonon processes, which give comparable orders of magnitude.
- **The model's applicability is questionable:**
  - The trigonal-warping expansion is controlled by k_F a, with a the moiré period. At ν = 0.1 for 1.58°, k_F a ≈ 0.6, so the perturbative warping treatment (Eq. 4) is marginal.
  - Near ν = 3.8 the pockets sit at γ and are not Dirac-like. It is unclear that the "Dirac non-Galilean" framework applies there at all.
  - The model also neglects the band's quantum geometry and Wannier form factors. The authors themselves invoke these at the end of the discussion to explain the weak T-dependence above |ν| = 4, which undercuts the specificity of Eq. 1.
- **Testable predictions are not confronted with data:**
  - Eq. 1 implies A ∝ a²/v_F² · α_e² ∝ v_F⁻⁴ (up to Λ(k_F)), i.e., a nearly density-independent T² coefficient with a steep twist-angle dependence.
  - The paper has devices from 1.58° to 3°, offering a v_F variation of a factor of several. Yet no A(θ) or A(n) comparison is presented.
  - Without this, the mechanism remains a plausibility argument rather than an identification.
  - The link to the MATBG linear-in-T regime ("unifies the anomalous transport") is also asserted, not derived. Eq. 1 gives T², so the paper offers no mechanism for e-e-limited linear-in-T resistivity.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

**Suggestion 1 — Make the e-e/e-ph separation quantitative and close the logical gaps.**
- For each filling, plot Δρ_THz against ρ(T_L = T_e*) − ρ(2 K), using an independently calibrated T_e*. Alternatively, plot Δρ_THz/ΔT_e against dρ/dT. This shows what fraction of the equilibrium T-dependence is T_e-controlled; a ratio of ~1 across n would strongly support the claim.
- Provide a TBG-specific calculation of ρ_eph(T_e, T_L) in the T_L < T_BG < T_e regime, including umklapp-assisted and gauge/phason channels, rather than relying on the MLG control.
- Explicitly estimate or exclude:
  - T_e-dependent impurity screening (e.g., compare with Das Sarma-type ρ(T/T_F) expressions);
  - Pomeranchuk/isospin effects near ν ≈ 1.7;
  - viscous/Gurzhi contributions.

**Suggestion 2 — Strengthen thermometry and demonstrate the cold-lattice condition.**
- Use independent T_e thermometry on the TBG devices:
  - SdHO damping or Johnson noise at the same fillings and powers;
  - in MATBG, the superconducting transition or critical current as a thermometer.
- Report P_max in absolute units, the estimated absorbed power, and an energy-balance estimate of the lattice and graphite-gate temperature rise. Include a control showing that T_L-sensitive quantities do not shift under illumination.
- Add the following checks:
  - frequency and polarization dependence, to exclude plasmonic, rectification and photogalvanic contributions;
  - linearity of V_dm in I and its sign reversal with I, to exclude bias-heating cross terms;
  - a discussion or simulation of the spatial T_e profile given the antenna geometry and contact cooling.

**Suggestion 3 — Directly test the umklapp-free T² regime with the THz probe and more robust analysis.**
- Measure Δρ_THz at ν ≈ ±0.1 and ≈ 3.8 in the 1.58° device and in the other high-angle devices, ideally with its power and T_L dependence. Show that the inferred T_e-dependence reproduces the T² coefficient.
- Present local exponents versus T, the sensitivity of the fits to the choice of ρ₀, and the full A(ν) map across the umklapp thresholds (currently in the SI). Compare against activated-umklapp and Bloch–Grüneisen-crossover fits.
- Quantify the thermally excited minority-carrier density and the residual disorder density δn (e.g., from the CNP width), and show that e-h friction or drag is negligible.
- Correct the hole-side umklapp window. Recompute the thresholds using realistic trigonally warped Fermi contours from a continuum model, rather than circular pockets.

**Suggestion 4 — Sharpen the theory–experiment comparison and the narrative.**
- Exploit the twist-angle series (1.58°–3°) and the density dependence to test the scaling A ∝ v_F⁻⁴ Λ(k_F) predicted by Eq. 1, using the device-specific hBN thickness in the gate-screened model. Identify where k_F a ≪ 1 holds and where the expansion fails.
- Either justify applying the Dirac-pocket model at ν ≈ 3.8 (γ-point pockets) or treat that regime separately.
- Temper or justify the claims about MATBG linear-in-T resistivity being "interaction-dominated" and "unified" with the higher-angle T². Provide at least a qualitative mechanism, or reframe the claim as "T_e-dominated." Discuss explicitly how the results bear on the phonon theories (refs. 17–25) and on Jaoui et al. (ref. 11).
- Clarify the novelty relative to refs. 36, 37, 49 and 50.
- Fix presentation issues:
  - figure references in the text (e.g., "Fig. 1a/1b" should be Fig. 1c/1d);
  - the missing word "antenna" in the Methods;
  - typos ("previosuly");
  - the undefined normalization behind the 70% figure;
  - absent device statistics, i.e., how many devices of each angle show each behaviour.
