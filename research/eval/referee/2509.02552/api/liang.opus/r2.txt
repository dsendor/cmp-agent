Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Problem addressed.** The paper tackles a long-standing and important question: which microscopic scattering processes set the metallic resistivity of twisted bilayer graphene (TBG)?
  - Equilibrium ρ ∼ T^α data cannot cleanly separate electron–electron (e-e) from electron–phonon (e-ph) contributions.
  - Competing interpretations include phonons, umklapp, strange/Planckian metallicity and heavy-fermion renormalization.
- **Methodological novelty.** The core idea is to use sub-gap continuous-wave THz radiation (0.14 THz, 0.6 meV) to raise the electron temperature T_e while the lattice stays cold.
  - A finite ∂ρ/∂T_e at fixed T_L is then used to diagnose interaction-limited conductivity.
  - Hot-electron bolometric photoresistance is well established in graphene. Its systematic use as a scattering-mechanism discriminator across twist angles, including near the magic angle, is new and potentially broadly applicable.
- **Headline results.**
  - Large positive photoresistance (Δρ_THz/ρ up to ~70%) in magic-angle TBG (MATBG), including at fillings where ρ is linear in T (dρ/dT ≈ 92 Ω/K at ν = 1.7).
  - Sign reversal at the charge neutrality point (CNP), at ν = ±4 and near ν ≈ 2.
  - A vanishing response in doped monolayer graphene at B = 0.
  - In a 1.58° device, T² resistivity at ν ≈ ±0.1 and ν ≈ 3.8, which the authors place outside the kinematic window for umklapp scattering.
  - An attribution of this T² term to inter-pocket ("inter-valley") normal e-e collisions enabled by broken Galilean invariance and trigonal warping (Eq. 1).
- **Broader relevance.** The work connects TBG to the puzzle of T² resistivity in dilute single-pocket metals (SrTiO₃, Bi₂O₂Se, HgTe), which gives it interest beyond the moiré community.
- **Main caveat on novelty.** Photoresistance can at most separate "T_e-controlled" from "T_L-controlled" contributions. It cannot by itself discriminate among the competing *electronic* scenarios (Planckian/strange metal, umklapp Fermi liquid, heavy fermion, isospin-fluctuation scattering). The claim of "resolving" the debate is therefore partial, and should be framed as ruling out (or bounding) phonons rather than identifying the mechanism.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Simple, general experimental concept.** A cold-lattice/hot-electron protocol with dual-modulation lock-in detection is transferable to other low-density quantum materials. It could become a standard diagnostic.
- **Striking, clean phenomenology.**
  - Positive Δρ_THz across the metallic fillings of several twist angles (≈1.04°, 1.05°, 1.58°, 2°, and others in the 1.6–3° range).
  - Clear negative features at insulating fillings, which makes Δρ_THz a useful local probe of insulating versus metallic character.
  - Monotonic power dependence, and suppression with increasing T_L as expected for hot-electron physics.
- **Direct experimental input to a lively debate.** The data bear on whether linear-in-T resistivity in MATBG is phonon-dominated. A credible bound on the phonon contribution at low T would be of high interest to both experimentalists and theorists.
- **Careful identification of an "umklapp-free" T² regime.** The 1.58° device has low disorder, metallic behaviour down to ~10¹¹ cm⁻², Hall-density characterization, and explicit Fermi-surface constructions. It adds a well-defined moiré entry to the growing list of anomalous dilute T² metals.
- **Concrete, falsifiable theoretical proposal.**
  - Eq. 1 ties the T² prefactor to the moiré period, the Fermi velocity and a screening-dependent parameter Λ.
  - The argument that the T² form is robust to interaction details (given U(0) finite and U(∞) = 0) is useful.
  - The estimated order of magnitude is compatible with experiment.
- **Multi-author expertise.** The collaboration combines THz experiment and Fermi-liquid transport theory, and the manuscript makes an effort to rule out alternative channels (interband photoconductivity, electron–hole friction/drag).

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**(i) The central inference — positive ∂ρ/∂T_e at fixed T_L ⇒ e-e scattering limits the conductivity — is not established. Several non-e-e mechanisms also produce a T_e-dependent resistivity.**

- **Hot-electron e-ph scattering is not quantitatively excluded for TBG itself.**
  - At T_L = 2 K, hot electrons with T_e up to ~17 K can relax momentum by spontaneous phonon emission. In the Bloch–Grüneisen (BG) regime, this e-ph resistivity depends on T_e, not only on T_L.
  - TBG has strongly enhanced e-ph coupling, including umklapp-assisted e-ph processes. The authors themselves cite fast umklapp-assisted e-ph cooling in MATBG (Ref. 34).
  - Monolayer graphene (MLG) is therefore an inadequate control. Its Fermi velocity is ~3–10× larger, its BG temperature and coupling are entirely different, and at 2 K its resistivity is disorder-dominated with negligible dρ/dT. A small Δρ_THz in MLG follows from small dρ/dT regardless of mechanism.
  - The required control is a calculation (or a measurement in a non-interacting-dominated regime of TBG) of the hot-electron e-ph photoresistance with TBG's actual phonon coupling, sound velocities and k_F, for the same T_e and T_L.
- **Other T_e-dependent, non-collisional or single-particle effects are not addressed.**
  - *Thermal smearing of a strongly energy-dependent conductivity σ(E).* Near the van Hove singularities and flat-band edges, Sommerfeld-type corrections to ρ scale with T_e. The flat-band structure makes this significant even for purely elastic (impurity) scattering.
  - *Temperature-dependent screening of charged impurities.* This produces linear-in-T or T² corrections in 2D Dirac and massive systems. It is a T_e effect that is not "interaction-limited conductivity" in the paper's sense.
  - *T_e-driven changes of isospin order or entropy near correlated states.* Examples are Pomeranchuk-like physics and the cascade transitions around integer ν.
  - The sign change at ν ≈ 2 shows that such correlated-state physics is active in the very devices studied. Each of these mechanisms gives Δρ_THz > 0 without any e-e momentum/current relaxation.

**(ii) The claim that interactions dominate the linear-in-T regime is extrapolated beyond the temperature window actually probed, and the analysis is not quantitative.**

- **Mismatch of temperature windows.**
  - The photoresistance is measured essentially only at T_L ≈ 2 K. Δρ_THz vanishes by T_L ~ 40–60 K (Fig. 2e), whereas the linear-in-T regime and the phonon-based theories (Refs. 10, 17–25) concern T up to ~50 K and above.
  - Phonon theories predict that the linear-T phonon resistivity emerges above an equipartition/BG scale. If that scale is comparable to the probed T_e range, the experiment tests a regime where the phonon contribution is expected to be small anyway.
  - The data therefore cannot support statements that the linear-T resistivity "often misattributed to e-ph scattering" is interaction-dominated over its full range.
- **No decomposition of ρ(T) into T_e- and T_L-controlled parts.**
  - If ρ(T_e, T_L) = ρ_0 + ρ_e(T_e) + ρ_ph(T_L), then Δρ_THz = ρ_e(T_e) − ρ_e(T_L). With an independent calibration of T_e, one could quantify the fraction of dρ/dT that is electronic, as a function of n and θ.
  - Instead, T_e ("up to 15 K") is estimated partly by comparing Δρ_THz with equilibrium ρ(T). This is circular, because it presupposes ρ depends only on T_e.
  - The independent shot via SdHO damping is performed in a magnetic field, likely at a different density and possibly a different absorption regime.
  - Without an n-resolved T_e map, the absorbed power and absorption vary with σ(n) and the cooling power varies with n and θ. As a result, even the sign-resolved maps (Figs. 2a,b,e) cannot be converted into ∂ρ/∂T_e.
- **Unexplained power dependence and possible non-thermal contributions.**
  - The saturation of Δρ_THz toward P_max (Fig. 2d) is not explained. Possible origins include nonlinear cooling (e.g. supercollision or umklapp-assisted), absorption bleaching, or a genuine saturation of ρ_e(T_e).
  - The modulation frequency (44.4 Hz) is slow enough that partial heating of the lattice, the hBN or the graphite gate cannot be excluded without a modulation-frequency or time-resolved check.
  - Non-bolometric effects are not quantitatively ruled out: dynamic (ac) heating with ωτ not ≪ 1, rectification or photogalvanic currents at the antenna-coupled contacts, and contact or Schottky-like photoresponses.

**(iii) The identification of an "umklapp-free" T² regime at |ν| ≈ 0.1 and ν ≈ 3.8 is not robust.**

- **Kinematic thresholds rest on idealized Fermi surfaces, and the T range is not in the degenerate limit.**
  - The thresholds k_F > g/(4√3) and k_F > g/4 assume circular pockets. The 1.58° pockets are strongly trigonally warped, and the conversion to ν windows depends on the band model, which should be stated and tested against the measured Hall density.
  - At ν = 0.1 (n ~ 1.5×10¹¹ cm⁻², E_F ~ 15–20 meV, T_F ~ 150–230 K), the T² fit in Fig. 3b extends to ~100 K ≈ T_F/2 (the figure itself marks T_F/2). This is far from the T ≪ T_F limit assumed by Fermi-liquid theory and Eq. 1. In this regime:
    - thermally smeared Fermi surfaces can access umklapp processes in an activated fashion that mimics a power law over one decade;
    - the residual-subtraction choice ρ − ρ₀ strongly affects the apparent exponent.
- **Two-carrier and inhomogeneity effects are dismissed too quickly.**
  - At ν = 0.1 and T ~ 50–100 K (k_BT ≈ 4–9 meV versus E_F ~ 20 meV), thermally excited minority carriers in a Dirac spectrum are not negligible.
  - Residual charge puddles of ~10¹⁰–10¹¹ cm⁻² are comparable to n.
  - At ν = 3.8, the system is ~0.2 carriers per moiré cell from a band insulator whose superlattice gap at 1.58° is likely tens of meV. Thermal activation into the remote band then creates a genuinely two-band (Baber-type) and electron–hole-friction situation, which are exactly the mechanisms the paper claims are inoperative.
  - The statement "the electron system remains unipolar at all measured T" needs a quantitative estimate of minority-carrier density, or Hall data versus T at these fillings.
- **The THz diagnostic is not applied to the key device and fillings.**
  - Fig. 3 contains only equilibrium ρ(T); no Δρ_THz is shown for the 1.58° device at ν = ±0.1 or 3.8.
  - The link "T² below the umklapp threshold" ⇒ "T_e-governed e-e process" is therefore asserted rather than demonstrated, even though it is the paper's main bridge between Figs. 2 and 3.

**(iv) The theoretical explanation (Eq. 1) is applied outside its regime of validity and offers little discriminating power.**

- **Regime of validity.**
  - *Small-warping expansion.* The derivation follows Sharma et al. for a weakly trigonally warped Dirac–Fermi liquid, expanding in k_F·a. At ν = 0.1 with a ≈ 9 nm, k_F·a ≈ 0.6, so the expansion is marginal.
  - *Wrong starting point at ν = 3.8.* The relevant pockets sit near γ in the flat band (Fig. 3f) and are not Dirac-like at all, so Eq. 1 does not apply there. The paper nonetheless uses ν = 3.8 as evidence for the same mechanism.
  - *Strong coupling.* With v_F reduced at 1.58°, α_e = e²/(4πε₀ħv_F) is of order 5–10 without dielectric screening. This makes a Born/second-order collision integral questionable and the prefactor uncontrolled. It is also unclear whether hBN dielectric screening is included in α_e, which changes the estimate by ~ε².
  - *Disorder regime.* Sharma et al. computed an *optical* conductivity. In the dc limit with momentum-conserving collisions in a non-Galilean-invariant system, the e-e contribution to ρ depends on the relation between the e-e and impurity rates: the current component parallel to momentum is protected, and the result changes between the τ_ee ≫ τ_imp and τ_ee ≪ τ_imp regimes. The authors should state which regime applies, give τ_ee and τ_imp estimates, and explain how the predicted "saturation" enters the observed temperature window.
- **Weak quantitative test and unclear physical picture.**
  - Λ spans two orders of magnitude (0.01–1), and ρ_ee ∝ a²Λ/v_F⁴ depends on the fourth power of a poorly known v_F. "Falling within the range" is consequently a weak consistency check, and the "best" Λ ≈ 0.2–0.3 is effectively a fit parameter.
  - More discriminating predictions of Eq. 1 are not tested:
    - the n-dependence of A, which is predicted to be weak since (k_F a)²/n is n-independent, apart from Λ(n);
    - its θ-dependence across the 1.6–3° devices;
    - its absence or smallness in MLG, where (k_F a)² with the atomic a is tiny.
  - The terminology is also unclear. The text alternates between "inter-valley", "inter-pocket", and "valley and layer inequivalence". It is not explained why time-reversal-related K/K′ pockets, whose warping is mirror-related, give a finite current-relaxing contribution in TBG, or how the moiré κ/κ′ pockets map onto the original K/K′ valleys.
  - The final remark that flat-band quantum geometry or Wannier localization is "responsible" for the scaling, based on the weak T-dependence above |ν| = 4, sits uneasily with Eq. 1, which contains no such ingredients.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Calibrate T_e independently and convert Δρ_THz into ∂ρ/∂T_e versus n, θ and T_L.**
   - Use Johnson-noise thermometry, or SdHO/weak-localization damping at the same densities.
   - Measure absorbed power, or at least n-resolved absorption estimates.
   - Then present a quantitative decomposition: the fraction of equilibrium dρ/dT that is T_e-controlled at each filling, especially at ν = 1.7 in MATBG.
   - Repeat at several T_L (e.g. 5, 10, 20, 30 K) with a small ΔT_e, to test the "interaction-dominated linear-T" claim within the regime where linear-T resistivity is actually observed.
   - Moderate the abstract and conclusion statements to the temperature range actually probed.

2. **Provide quantitative controls against alternative T_e-dependent mechanisms.**
   - *Hot-electron e-ph:* calculate the phonon-emission photoresistance for TBG, using realistic coupling (including umklapp-assisted processes), sound velocities and k_F.
   - *Thermal smearing of σ(E):* estimate it from the measured low-T ρ(n) via a Sommerfeld/convolution analysis.
   - *Temperature-dependent impurity screening:* estimate this contribution as well.
   - *Non-thermal or lattice heating:* show modulation-frequency dependence, linear-response checks of I-V at fixed P, and polarization and antenna-coupling dependence. Present data on a contact-free region if available.
   - Replace or supplement the MLG control with a regime of TBG (or Bernal bilayer) whose dρ/dT is comparable but known to be phonon-dominated, so that the comparison is between equal-magnitude dρ/dT.

3. **Strengthen the umklapp-free T² claim in the 1.58° device.**
   - Show Δρ_THz at ν = ±0.1 and 3.8 on this same device.
   - Restrict T² fits to T ≪ T_F, report fitted exponents with uncertainties and the sensitivity to ρ₀ subtraction, and show dρ/d(T²) versus T.
   - Use realistic, trigonally warped band-structure Fermi contours, reconciled with the Hall data, to define the umklapp windows. Include thermal smearing of the threshold.
   - Quantify minority-carrier and remote-band populations versus T, for example from n_H(T) and the activation gap at ν = 4.
   - Display the A(ν) map (now in the SI) in the main text, with its enhancement inside the umklapp windows, and add A(θ) across all 1.6–3° devices.

4. **Tighten the theory and the presentation.**
   - Clarify the physical picture of inter-pocket current relaxation in TBG, including the role of the time-reversal partner pockets.
   - State the dc-limit regime (τ_ee versus τ_imp) and the treatment of screening in α_e (hBN dielectric constant).
   - Give an explicit expression and numbers for A at ν = 0.1, and discuss strong-coupling corrections.
   - Either justify the use of the Dirac-cone model at ν = 3.8 or provide a separate calculation for the γ-point pockets.
   - Test the predicted (weak) n-dependence and the θ-scaling (∝ a²/v_F⁴) against data.
   - Presentation fixes:
     - correct the figure call-outs (the text cites Fig. 1a/1b where Fig. 1c/1d are meant);
     - move the 70% Δρ/ρ data and the T_e calibration into the main text;
     - list all devices with twist angles, angle homogeneity, mobility and contact geometry;
     - report absolute incident and estimated absorbed THz power rather than only P/P_max;
     - explain the colour scale and sign structure in Fig. 2e, and the origin of the negative Δρ_THz near ν ≈ 2.
